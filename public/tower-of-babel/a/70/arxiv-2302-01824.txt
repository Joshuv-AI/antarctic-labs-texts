Upper bounds for eigenvalue multiplicities of almost cyclic elements in irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups
A.E. Zalesski

Abstract
We study the rational irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups in which some non-central semisimple element has at most one eigenvalue of multiplicity greater than 1 1 . We bound the multiplicity of this eigenvalue in terms of the rank of the group. 1 1 1 2020 Mathematics subject classification: 20G05, 15A18 2 2 2 Keywords: simple algebraic groups, representations, regular elements, eigenvalue multiplicities.

Introduction

In this paper we study a special case of a general problem on eigenvalue multiplicities of semisimple elements in irreducible representations of algebraic groups. All representations of algebraic groups and their modules are assumed to be rational. This problem is connected with a similar problem on weight multiplicities.
In
[
23
]
we determine the irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups
G
whose all non-zero weights are of multiplicity 1, and compute the zero weight multiplicity in each case. Note that weights are one-dimensional representations of a maximal torus
T
, say, of
G
,
and the multiplicity of a weight
\omega
, say, in a representation
\rho
of
G
is the multiplicity of
\omega
as a constituent of
\rho(T)
.
Turning to individual non-central semisimple elements
g\in G
, an analog of this result could be (i) obtaining a list of the irreducible representations
\rho
of
G
such that at most one eigenvalue multiplicity of
\rho(g)
is greater than 1, and then (ii) determining the multiplicity of the exceptional eigenvalue. An essential step of understanding problem (i) is made in
[
25
]
, where it is proved that all non-zero weights of
\rho
must be of multiplicity 1. This does not mean however that the eigenvalue multiplicities of
\rho(g)

are the same as the weight multiplicities. This can be seen on examples with
G
to be of classical type and
\rho
the natural representation of
G
. Moreover, in these examples
m_{g}(\rho)
, the maximum eigenvalue multiplicity of
\rho(g)
, is almost arbitrary (subject to the obvious condition
m_{g}(\rho)\leq\dim\rho
),
whereas all weight multiplicities of
\rho
are equal to 1. However, it is shown below that
m_{g}(\rho)
can be bounded in terms of the rank of
G
.
An initial step in this direction is made in
[
25
]
, where, for a non-central non-regular semisimple element
g\in G
, it is shown that the natural representation of a classical group
G
is essentially the only one in which at most one eigenvalue multiplicity of
\rho(g)
is greater than 1; that result is quoted below as Theorem
3.17
. To be more precise,
\rho
is a twist of the natural representation. (We call a representation
\rho^{\prime}
a twist of
\rho
if
\rho^{\prime}(g)=\rho(\sigma(g))
for
g\in G
and a surjective homomorphism
\sigma:G\rightarrow G
.) Therefore,
the current paper mainly deals with regular semisimple elements.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a simple algebraic group of rank n and
V
an irreducible
G
-module. Let
s\in G
be a regular semisimple element and let
m_{s}(V)
be the maximum of the eigenvalue multiplicities of
s
on V. Suppose that at most one eigenvalue multiplicity of
s
on V exceeds
1
. Then
m_{s}(V)\leq n+1.

In fact, we can give more details as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a simple algebraic group of rank n and
V
an irreducible
G
-module. Let
s\in G
be a regular semisimple element and let

be the maximum of the eigenvalue multiplicities of
s
on V. Suppose that at most one eigenvalue multiplicity of

on V exceeds

or one of following holds.

(1)

and
m_{s}(V)\leq n+1;

(2)

1
and

equals the weight zero multiplicity of V recorded there; in particular

The bound in item (1) of Theorem

In this example for

(1) is obvious. (2) A Frobenius twist, as it is defined in Section 2, arises from a so called standard Frobenius mapping

4
Eigenvalue multiplicities: a proof of Theorem

In this section we bound the eigenvalue multiplicities of a regular semisimple element

on a

-module

4.1
A uniform bound

Lemma 4.1
.

Let G be a simple algebraic group in characteristic

or

, and let

. If

,
suppose that
\omega

\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{+}
.
Let

and

. Then there is
\alpha\in\pm\Pi
such that
\lambda-\alpha\in\Omega(V)
.

Proof.

If

then the result is true by
[
4
, Ch. VIII, §7, Prop. 5]
. If
p>e(G)
and
\omega
is
p
-restricted then the result follows from that for
p=0
and Lemma
3.11
. Suppose that
\omega
is not
p
-restricted. Then
V=M\otimes V^{\prime}
, where

are irreducible
G
-modules, and

is
p
-restricted. Every weight
\lambda
of

is of shape
\mu+\nu
with

and
\nu\in\Omega(V^{\prime})
. As

is

-restricted, we have
\mu-\alpha\in\Omega(V)
for some

by the above. Then

\lambda-\alpha=(\mu-\alpha)+\nu\in\Omega(V)
, as required.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let G be a simple algebraic group of rank n in characteristic
p
. Assume
p=0
or
p>e(G)
. Let

be a subgroup such that
C_{G}(S)=T
, and

G
-module with

-restricted
highest weight
\omega\neq 0
. Assume in addition that some homogeneous component U of

is of dimension greater than

and all other homogeneous component of

are
1

(1)
if
V^{T}\neq 0
, then
V^{S}=V^{T}
and
\dim V^{S}\leq n+1;

(2)

\dim U\leq 2n
.

Proof.

By
[
25
, Theorem 5]
, all non-zero weights of
V
are of multiplicity 1. Therefore,
\omega
appears in Tables 1,2, and in particular
\dim V^{T}\leq n+1
.
By Lemma
3.8
we have
\alpha(S)\neq 1
for every
\alpha\in\Phi(G)
.

(1) Here we have

. Suppose the contrary, that
V^{T}\neq V^{S}
, so
\dim V^{S}>1
. Note that

is a sum of weight spaces of
V
, so

\mu(S)=1
for some non-zero weight of
V
.

Suppose first that
\omega
is a root. Then all non-zero weights of

are also roots. (This is well known.)
Since
\alpha(S)\neq 1
for every
\alpha\in\Phi(G)
, we conclude that

, a contradiction.

Next suppose that
\omega
is not a root. By the above,
\mu
is not a root. By replacing

by a conjugate if necessary, we may assume that
\mu
is dominant. By Lemma
4.1
,
\mu+\beta
is a weight of
V

for some
\beta\in\pm\Pi
. Then
\mu+\beta\neq\beta
, while
\mu+\beta

and
\beta
restricts to the same (non-zero) character of

. If
\beta
is not a weight of

then

implies
\beta(S)=1
, a contradiction. So
\beta
is not a weight of
V
.
But
0\prec\omega
so Lemma
3.11
(3)
implies that some root is a weight of
V
. We deduce that
\Phi(G)
has two root
lengths and either the long roots or the short roots are not weights of
V
. As

if

, by Lemma
3.14
, either
\omega
is a short root, a contradiction,
or

and
\omega=\omega_{i}
with

even. In the latter case
\omega_{j}\in\Omega(V)
for
i-j>0
even, and these are the only dominant weights of
\Omega(V)
. So
\mu=\omega_{r}
with
i-r>0
even. Here
r>2
as
\omega_{2}
is a root. Then
r-2\neq 0
and
\omega_{r}-\omega_{r-2}=\varepsilon_{r}+\varepsilon_{r-1}\in\Phi(G)
.
Set
\beta=-\varepsilon_{r}-\varepsilon_{r-1}
. As
\beta
is a short root, it lies in the

-orbit of
\omega_{2}=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}
, and hence
\beta\in\Omega(V)
. However, by the above,
we have a contradiction.

(2)
In view of (1) we can assume that
\dim V^{T}\leq 1
, so all weights of

are of multiplicity 1.
If

then the claim follows from (1). So we now
assume that

hence

is the sum of

-weight
spaces which are of dimension

3.9
. Assume for a contradiction that there exist distinct
T
-weights

\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{2n+1}
such that

of
S
and all

. For each

\gamma_{j}\in\pm\Pi
such that

is a weight of

. Then there exists

such that

. But then

, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Lemma 4.14
.

\mu_{i}
,

, be distinct weights of

, there are distinct

(ii) Suppose that

is not trivial. By Lemma
3.2
(3),

is cyclic on

and

.
Then

for

,

Proposition 4.27
.

For
G=A_{n}
let
M\in\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits(G)
be p-restricted and

s\in T_{reg}
. Suppose that s is almost cyclic on M. Then

.

Proof.

Suppose first that the weight zero multiplicity of

is greater than
1
. Suppose the contrary; as
s
is almost cyclic, only the eigenvalue 1 multiplicity is greater than 1. Then the result follows from Theorem
4.2
(1) and Table 1.

Suppose that the weight zero multiplicity of

is at most
1
. By Theorem
3.9
, all weight multiplicities of
M
equal
1
. By Table 2, the highest weight
\omega
of

is in the following list:

(1\leq i\leq n)
,
a\omega_{1},

a\omega_{n}
,
a\omega_{i}+(p-1-a)\omega_{i+1}
, where
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
1\leq a<p
.

Note that we assume
\omega\neq 0,\omega_{1},\omega_{n}
as these cases are trivial.
Since the list of the eigenvalue multiplicities of
M
is the same as for the dual of
M
, it suffices to deal with
\omega_{i},a\omega_{1},a\omega_{i}+(p-1-a)\omega_{i+1}
for

.

Suppose the contrary. Let
M_{1}=V_{\omega_{1}}
and

M_{2}=\begin{cases}V_{\omega_{i-1}}&if\,\,\omega=\omega_{i},i>1;\cr V_{(a-1)\omega_{1}}&if\,\,\omega=a\omega_{1},a>1;\cr V_{\omega_{i-1}+(a-1)\omega_{i}+(p-1-a)\omega_{i+1}}&if\,\,\omega=a\omega_{i}+(p-1-a)\omega_{i+1}.\end{cases}

(If

By Lemmas

and

separates the weights of

Note that

is a constituent of

. Indeed,

and

Furthermore,
\Omega(M)\subseteq\Omega(M_{1})+\Omega(M_{2})
(see Lemma
4.26
). As all eigenvalue multiplicities of

M

distinct weights

of

.
Then

.
As

for some

.

separates the weights of

. ∎

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
G=A_{n}=SL_{n+1}(F)
and

. If

then

.

Proof.

By
[
26
, Theorem 1.2]
, if
s
is almost cycic on
V_{2\omega_{1}}
then
s
is strictly regular. Suppose that
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}\in\Omega(V_{2\omega_{1}})
be distinct weights such that
\mu_{1}(s)=\cdots=\mu_{l}(s)=e
. The weights of
V
are
2\varepsilon_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,n+1
) and
\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}

(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\},i<j)
; this can be recorded as

\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}

(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\},i\leq j)

Suppose first that
\mu_{i},\mu_{j},\mu_{k}\in\{2\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,2\varepsilon_{n+1}\}
for some distinct

. By reordering of
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{l}
we can assume that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\in\{2\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,2\varepsilon_{n+1}\}
,
and let

. Therefore, at most two

\mu_{i}

(1\leq i\leq l)

Note that
\Omega(V_{\omega_{2}})=\Omega(V_{2\omega_{1}})\setminus\{2\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,2\varepsilon_{n}\}

.

, we have

, and we can assume that

do not lie in the support of any

Proposition

We next prove it for tensor-decomposable modules.

Lemma 4.30
.

[
25
, Lemma 7]
Let
s\in T
be a
non-central element. Let
\omega
be a dominant weight which is not
p
-restricted and
not of the form
p^{k}\mu
for
\mu
a
p
-restricted dominant weight. If
s
is
almost cyclic on
V_{\omega}
then all weights of
V_{\omega}
are of multiplicity
1
.

For a
G
-module

and

denote by
\deg_{V}(s)
the degree of the minimum polynomial of
\rho(s)
,
where
\rho
is the representation afforded by
V
. If
s
is semisimple then
\deg_{V}(s)=|E_{V}(s)|

is the number of distinct eigenvalues of
s
on
V
. Note that
s
is cyclic on

if and only if
\deg_{V}(s)=\dim V
.

For
A,B\subset F^{\times}
we set
AB=\{ab:a\in A,b\in B\}
.

Lemma 4.31
.

Let
M,M_{1},M_{2},V
be non-trivial G-modules and
s\in T
.
Suppose that s is not scalar on both
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
,
E_{M}(s)=E_{M_{1}}(s)E_{M_{2}}(s)
and

s
is almost cyclic on

.

(1)

\deg_{M_{i}\otimes V}(s)=\deg_{M_{i}}(s)\cdot\dim V
for

.

(2)
the eigenvalue multiplicities of s on

do not exceed
d=\dim M_{1}
.

Proof.

(1) We can assume
i=1
. By Lemma
3.2
,
s
is cyclic on
M
and
V
. Then

\dim V=\deg_{V}(s)
.
Suppose the contrary, that
\deg_{M_{1}\otimes V}(s)<\deg_{M_{1}}(s)\cdot\dim V
. Then there are distinct eigenvalues
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in E_{M_{1}}(s)
and
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in E_{V}(s)
such that

. Then
\mu\lambda\nu=\mu^{\prime}\lambda\nu^{\prime}\in E_{M\otimes V}(s)

. So

is not almost cyclic on

unless

is scalar on

, which is not the case by assumption.

(2) This follows from Lemma

(1) as the eigenvalue multiplicities of

equal 1.
∎

To illustrate the use of Lemma

, we state the following:

.

,
\omega=a\omega_{1}
or

. In addition, here and in Lemmas

Lemma 4.33
.

Let
M,M_{1},M_{2},V
be non-trivial G-modules. Let
s\in T\setminus Z(G)
. Suppose that

(i)
for every
f,f^{\prime}\in E_{M_{2}}(s)
there exist distinct eigenvalues
e,e^{\prime}\in E_{M_{1}}(s)
such that

(ii)

E_{M}(s)\subseteq E_{M_{1}}(s)\cdot E_{M_{2}}(s);

(iii)

is almost cyclic on

.

Then
\deg_{M_{2}\otimes V}(s)=\deg_{M_{2}}(s)\cdot\dim V
, and

.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
s
is cyclic on
V
, so
\dim V=\deg_{V}(s)
. To prove the first statement of the lemma, suppose (the contrary), that
\deg_{M_{2}\otimes V}(s)<\deg_{M_{2}}(s)\cdot\dim V
. Then there are distinct eigenvalues

and eigenvalues

such that

.

By
(i)
, there are distinct eigenvalues

such that

and hence

and

on

, each of multiplicity at least 2.
As

is almost cyclic on

, we must have

, whence

, a contradiction.

Turn to the second statement. Set

Suppose the contrary, that the multiplicity of some eigenvalue

of

on

is greater than

. By Lemma
3.2
(3),

is cyclic on

and

, so there are distinct eigenvalues

of

and distinct eigenvalues

of

for

. Furthermore, by

,

for

and some eigenvalues

for some

Then

.

contradicting the first statement of the lemma. ∎

Let

We can assume (by replacing

Set

.

Suppose first that

. Set

by Lemma

.

Suppose that
M=V_{a\omega_{i}+(p-1-a)\omega_{i+1}}
. Then we set

.
The result follows from Lemma

To prove

, let

. Then there are weights
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Omega(M_{2})
such that
\lambda(s)=f,\lambda^{\prime}(s)=f^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
4.26
, there are weights

5
An application to a recognition problem for linear groups over finite fields

There is a problem in the theory of linear groups over finite fields aimed to determining the subgroups

of

containing a matrix
\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits_{m},M)
, where

[
8
]
, where the authors solve this problem for

. The result of
[
8
]
can be also interpreted as a classification of subgroups of
GL_{n}(q)
containing a cyclic Sylow

GL_{n}(q)
for some prime divisor
\ell
of
GL_{n}(q)
, see
[
1
]
.
Observe that the case where

is irreducible in

is the bulk of the problem.

In this section we contribute to the problem in the case where
H
is a quasi-simple group of Lie type in defining characteristic
p>0
for
p|q
.
Our purpose is to consider the complementary case with
m\geq n/2
. Observe that the case with

is considered in
[
7
]
.

Note that specializing the main theorem of
[
8
]
to the case where

is a quasi-simple group of Lie type
leads one to Examples 2.1, 2.4 and 2.9 in
[

]

5.3
Non-regular semisimple elements in representations of quasi-simple finite groups of Lie type

In order to deal uniformly with
p=2
and
p>2
we denote by
SO_{8}(F)
the connected component of the orthogonal group
O_{8}(F)
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
s\in{\mathbf{G}}=Spin_{8}(F)
be a non-central semisimple element,
and V the natural
G
-module. Suppose that s is almost cyclic on V.

(1)
If
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of s on V of multiplicity
m_{s}(V)>1
then
\lambda\in\{1,-1\}
and either
1
or
-1
is not an eigenvalue of
s
.

(2)
Let
G\cong{}^{3}D_{4}(q)
and
s\in G
.
Then s is regular.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{H}=SO_{8}(F)
and let
h:=\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}

be the representation of
\mathbf{G}
with highest weight
\omega_{1}
.

(1) By Lemma
3.1
,

\lambda^{-1}
is an eigenvalue of
s
on
V
of multiplicity equal to that of
\lambda.
As
s
is almost cyclic on
V
, we have

\lambda\in\{1,-1\}
. The
1
-eigenspace and the
-1
-eigenspace
are well known to be non-degenerate and of even dimension. As
s
is almost cyclic on
V
, both
1,-1
cannot be eigenvalues of
s
. It follows that
C_{\mathbf{H}}(s)

is connected, see
[
28
, Lemma 2.2]
.

(2) Let
V_{1}\subset V
be the eigenspace of dimension
m_{s}(V)
. We have
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
for some
s
-stable subspace
V_{2}
of
V
, and
s
is cyclic on
V_{2}
. Then
C_{\mathbf{H}}(h(s))
contains the subgroup
\mathbf{Y}_{1}:=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(SO(V_{1}),\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits)
.

Suppose the contrary, that
s
is not regular.
Then
C_{G}(s)
and hence
C_{\mathbf{H}}(h(s))
contains a unipotent element
u\neq 1
.
If
\dim V_{1}=2
then
u

is a transvection, however,
\mathbf{H}
contains no transvection. So
\dim V_{1}\in\{6,4\}
.

It is known that
C_{\mathbf{G}}(s)
is a reductive group and contains a maximal torus
T
of
\mathbf{G}

[
20
, Theorem 5.8]
.

Note that
\mathbf{G}=Spin_{8}(F)
is the universal group of type
D_{4}
. Let
\sigma
be the Steinberg endomorphism of
\mathbf{G}
such that
G={\mathbf{G}}^{\sigma}:=\{g\in\mathbf{G}:\sigma(g)=g\}
. In particular,
\sigma(s)=s
and hence
\sigma(C_{\mathbf{G}}(s))=C_{\mathbf{G}}(s)
. Then
C_{G}(s)=C_{\mathbf{G}}(s)^{\sigma}
is a group of maximal rank in
G
in terms of
[
13
]
. Maximal subgroups
M
of maximal rank in
G
are determined in
[
13
]
, and those containing a non-trivial unipotent element are
listed in
[
13
, Table 5.1]
. This tells us that
M
contains a normal subgroup
M_{1}
isomorphic either to
SL_{2}(q)\circ SL_{2}(q^{3})
or to
SL_{3}(q)
or to
SU_{3}(q)
, and the quotient
M/M_{1}
is abelian.

Let
\mathbf{Y}
be the semisimple part of
C_{\mathbf{G}}(s).
Then
h(\mathbf{Y})=\mathbf{Y}_{1}
.

Suppose first that
\dim V_{1}=6
.
Then
\mathbf{Y}\cong D_{3}\cong A_{3}\cong SL_{4}(F)
.

Obviously, the restriction of
\sigma
to
\mathbf{Y}
is a Steinberg endomorphism of
\mathbf{Y}
.
Steinberg endomorphisms of simple algebraic groups are classified, see
[
16
, Theorem 22.5 and Table 22.1]
. In particular,
C_{\mathbf{G}}(s)^{\sigma}
contains
\mathbf{Y}^{\sigma}=SL_{4}(q)
or
SU_{4}(q)
. However, none of these groups is contained in
M
.

Suppose that
\dim V_{1}=4
. Set
\mathbf{X}=C_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{Y})^{\circ}

so
\mathbf{X}V_{i}=V_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
\mathbf{X}
acts trivially on
V_{1}
. Clearly,

\sigma(\mathbf{X})=\mathbf{X}
. Set
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Y}
; then
\sigma(\mathbf{K})=\mathbf{K}
and the rank of
\mathbf{K}
equals 4. Therefore,
K:=\mathbf{K}^{\sigma}
is a subgroup of maximal rank in
G
and hence
K\subseteq M
by the above.

Observe that
\mathbf{X}\cong\mathbf{Y}\cong Spin_{4}(F)\cong\mathbf{X}_{1}\circ\mathbf{X}_{2}
, where

\mathbf{X}_{1}\cong\mathbf{X}_{2}\cong SL_{2}(F)
.
As
\sigma(\mathbf{X})=\mathbf{X}
, we have either (i)
\sigma(\mathbf{X}_{i})=\mathbf{X}_{i}
for
i=1,2
or (ii)
\sigma(\mathbf{X}_{1})=\mathbf{X}_{2}
and
\sigma(\mathbf{X}_{2})=\mathbf{X}_{1}
. We show that (ii) does not hold.

Indeed, by Steinberg’s famous theorem
[
16
, Theorem 21.5]
, a surjective endomorphism of
\mathbf{G}
is either
Steinberg or an automorphism. It follows that
\sigma^{i}
is Steinberg for every
i>0
.
In particular,
\sigma^{2}
stabilizes
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
.
Moreover,
\sigma^{3}
acts on maximal tori
T
of
\mathbf{H}
and of
\mathbf{K}
by sending
t\in T
to
t^{q^{3}}
, and hence stabilizes
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
too. For a moment view
\sigma
as the abstract group isomorphism
\mathbf{H}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}
. Then
\sigma=\sigma^{3}\sigma^{-2}
must stabililze
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
.

So (i) holds.
Similarly,
\mathbf{Y}\cong Spin_{4}(F)\cong\mathbf{Y}_{1}\circ\mathbf{Y}_{2}
, where

\mathbf{Y}_{1}\cong\mathbf{Y}_{2}\cong SL_{2}(F)
and
\sigma

stabililzes both
\mathbf{Y}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Y}_{2}
. Therefore, each group

\mathbf{X}^{\sigma}_{1}
,
\mathbf{X}^{\sigma}_{2}
,
\mathbf{Y}^{\sigma}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Y}^{\sigma}_{2}
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
SL_{2}(p)
, and hence
K
contains a subgroup isomorphic to some central product
of four copies of
SL_{2}(p)
. However, one easily observes that
M

contains no subgroup of this kind. This contradiction completes the proof. ∎

The following result extends Theorem
3.17
to finite groups of Lie type. Observe that we use isomorphims
SL_{4}(q)\cong Spin^{+}_{6}(q)
and

SU_{4}(q)\cong Spin^{-}_{6}(q)
to interpret an irreducible 6-dimensional module for
the first group as a twist of the natural module for the second one. Similarly, we use isomorphims
Sp_{4}(q)\cong SO_{5}(q)
to interprete
the irreducible module with highest weight
\omega_{2}
for one of these groups as the natural module for another one.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let G be a universal quasi-simple group of Lie type and

\phi
a non-trivial irreducible F-representation of G. Let
s\in G
be a non-regular non-central semisimple element. Suppose that
\phi(g)
is almost cyclic. Then
G\in\{SL_{n+1}(q),n>1,SU_{n+1}(q),n>1
,
Spin_{2n+1}(q),n\geq 3
,
q
odd,
Sp_{2n}(q),n\geq 2
,
Spin^{\pm}_{2n}(q),n\geq 3\}

and
\dim\phi
is
equal to
n+1,n+1
,
2n+1
,
2n
,
2n
, respectively.

Proof.

It is well known that
\phi
extends to a representation of a simply connected simple algebraic group

{\mathbf{G}}
such that
G={\mathbf{G}}^{\sigma}
for a suitable Steinberg endomorphism of
{\mathbf{G}}
. We have
s\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
By Theorem
3.17
,
{\mathbf{G}}\in\{SL_{n+1}(F),

Spin_{2n+1}(F),n\geq 3
,
p>2
,
Sp_{2n}(F),n\geq 2
,
Spin_{2n}(F),n\geq 3\}
and
\dim\phi=n+1
,
2n+1
,
2n
,
2n
, respectively.
By the classification theorem of Steinberg endomorphisms of simple algebraic groups,
G={\mathbf{G}}^{\sigma}\in\{SL_{n+1}(q),SU_{n+1}(q)
,

Spin_{2n+1}(q),n>3
,
q
odd,
Sp_{2n}(q),n\geq 2
,
Spin^{\pm}_{2n}(q),n\geq 3,{}^{3}D_{4}(q),{}^{2}C_{2}(q)\}
. As
s\in G
is non-regular,
we exclude from this list the groups
SL_{2}(q)\cong SU_{2}(q)
and
{}^{2}C_{2}(q)
, as they have no non-regular non-central element. The group

{}^{3}D_{4}(q)
must be dropped in view Lemma
5.10
. Indeed,
every irreducible representation of
G={}^{3}D_{4}(q)
of degree 8 is a twist of the natural
representation of
G
with
an automorphism
\tau
, say, of
G
. Obviously, if
\tau(s)=t
and
\rho(t)
is almost cyclic then
so is
\rho^{\tau}(s)=\rho(\tau(s))
. So Lemma
5.10
(2) extends to every irreducible representation of
G
of degree 8.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

If
\rho
is viewed as a representation into
GL_{n}(F)
then
\rho=\rho_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\rho_{k}
, where

\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}
are Galois conjugate irreducible representations of
G
into
GL_{n/k}(F)
, see
[
11
, Lemma 2.10.2]
. Each
\rho_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, extends to a representation
\tau_{i}:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow GL_{n/k}(F)
, where
\mathbf{G}
is a simple simply connected algebraic group such that
G=\mathbf{G}^{\sigma}

for a Steinberg endomorphism
\sigma
of
\mathbf{G}
(
[
9
, §2.11]
).
Obviously,
\tau_{i}(g)
is almost cyclic for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then
the result follows from the definition of a nearly natural representation of
G
and Theorem
5.11
. ∎

The intersection form on the homology of a surface acted on by a finite group.
Jean Barge, Julien Marché

Abstract




1
Introduction

The purpose of this note is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on a compact oriented surface
S
by homeomorphisms preserving the orientation.
There exists a
G
-invariant Lagrangian subspace in
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
.

A Lagrangian subspace is a free abelian subgroup
L
of
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
of rank
g
, where
g
is the genus of
S
satisfying the following extra assumptions. It is a free summand, meaning that there exists
M\subset H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
such that
L\oplus M=H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, and it is isotropic, meaning that
x\cdot y=0
for and
x,y\in L
where
\cdot
is the intersection pairing on
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
.

The topological meaning of this theorem is that
G
can act in a unique way on the rational homology of a surface, together with its intersection form.

Corollary 1
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on two compact oriented surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of the same genus by homeomorphisms preserving the orientation.
There exists a
G
-equivariant linear map
H_{1}(S_{1},\mathbb{Q})\to H_{1}(S_{2},\mathbb{Q})
preserving the intersection form.

In this way, it is a symplectic straightening of a celebrated theorem of Chevalley and Weil stating that for any free action of
G
on
S
one has
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[G]^{2g-2}
as
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules (see
[
2
,
5
]
).
When
G
is abelian, a complete classification of
G
-actions on surfaces has been obtained by Edmonds in
[
3
]
.
This problem seems to have not been addressed before. Its proof uses a mixture of arguments coming from various fields, all of which are commonly used in surgery theory. Let us give an overview of its steps, which gives also the plan of the note.

1.

We define a Witt group
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
for symplectic
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with an action of
G
: the theorem states that the class of
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})
vanishes in
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
whatever be the group
G
and its action on
S
.

2.

A bordism argument: the action of
G
on
S
defines a fundamental class
[S/G]
in
H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and the class of
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})
in
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
only depends on
[S/G]
. At this point we hoped to find an interesting map
H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
. The sequel consists in proving that this map vanishes.

3.

We decompose
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})
into pieces corresponding the the irreducible rational representations of
G
.

4.

From the fact that
H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is a torsion group and that
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
only has
2
-primary torsion, we reduce the proof to the case of
2
-groups.

5.

We invoke results on rational representations of
2
-group, precisely a theorem of Fontaine extending results of Roquette and Witt, to reduce the result to four families of
2
-groups for which the result is easily proved.

The question arose when studying representations of mapping class groups of surfaces on the homology of their finite covers.
Consider
G
as a subgroup of
\Mod(S)
, the mapping class group of
S
, and let
\Gamma
be its normalizer. Elements of
\Gamma
act on the quotient
S/G
, giving a map
\Gamma\to\Mod(S/G)
whose kernel is
G
and whose image has finite index. The problem of identifying the image of the symplectic representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\Sp(H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z}))
has deserved a lot of attention, see for instance
[
7
,
5
]
.

A modest consequence of our result is that one can describe in any case the target group of
\rho
as the group
\Sp_{G}(L\oplus L^{*})
where
L=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[G]^{g-1}
. The question whether the image of
\rho
is commensurable to the sugroup of integral points stays a major one in the theory of representations of the mapping class group, implying in particular the Ivanov conjecture, see
[
9
]
.

To end this introduction, we would like to raise an analogy, pointed out by Mathieu Florence. Given a finite Galois extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
with group
G
, one may ask if one can find a normal basis (i.e. of the form
(gx)_{g\in G}
for some
x\in K
) which is orthonormal for the trace form
\tr_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
. A theorem of Bayer and Lenstra shows that it holds if
G
has odd order, see
[
1
]
. Both the statement and the proof show a deep analogy with the main result of this note.

2
The Witt group
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})

As the notation suggest, we could replace
\mathbb{Q}
by any field whose caracteristic does not divide twice the cardinality of
G
. We refrain from doing this for the sake of brevity.

Definition 1
.

A symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V
, endowed with a symplectic form and an action of
G
which preserves the form.

It is called metabolic if there exists a
G
-invariant Lagrangian
L\subset V
.

We say that two symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules
V
and
W
are Witt-equivalent if one can find two metabolic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules
E
and
F
such that
V\oplus E
is isomorphic to
W\oplus F
. We will write
V\sim W
.

For instance, denote by
-V
the vector space
V
with the opposite symplectic form and the same action. One check that
V\oplus(-V)
is metabolic, which shows that the set of Witt-equivalence classes of symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules form a group with respect to the direct sum : we denote it by
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Remark 1
.

We can alternatively define a symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module as a
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module
V
with a map
\omega_{G}:V\times V\to\mathbb{Q}[G]
satisfying
\omega_{g}(gx,hy)=g\omega(x,y)h^{-1}
and
\omega_{G}(y,x)=-\overline{\omega(x,y)}
where we have set
\overline{g}=g^{-1}
. The form
\omega_{G}
is constructed from a
G
-invariant symplectic form
\omega
by putting

\omega_{G}(x,y)=\sum_{g\in G}\omega(x,gy)g.

In this form, the Witt group
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to the standard skew-symmetric Witt group of the ring with involution
\mathbb{Q}[G]
, usually denoted by
W^{-}(\mathbb{Q}[G])
.

The following lemma, adapted to our purposes, is the heart of the Witt cancellation theorem.

Lemma 1
.

Let
V
be a symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module and
I,J
be two
G
-invariant coisotropic subspaces, meaning that
I\subset I^{\perp}
and
J\subset J^{\perp}
.
Then
I^{\perp}/I\oplus-(J^{\perp}/J)
is metabolic.

Proof.

The proof consists in showing that the image
\Delta
of the diagonal map
I^{\perp}\cap J^{\perp}\to(I^{\perp}/I)\oplus-(J^{\perp}/J)
is a
G
-invariant Lagrangian. It is left to the reader.
∎

As a consequence of this lemma, a symplectic module
V
vanishes in
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
if and only if it is metabolic. Indeed, suppose that
V\oplus W
contains a Lagrangian
L
and
W
contains a Lagrangian
\Lambda
.
Take
I=V\oplus\Lambda
and
J=L
so that
I^{\perp}/I=V
and
J^{\perp}/J=0
. Lemma
1
claims that
V
is metabolic.

Suppose now that
V=L\oplus L^{\prime}
contains two
G
-invariant transverse Lagrangians.
The symplectic form gives an isomorphism
L^{\prime}\simeq L^{*}
which has to be
G
-invariant. We say that the symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module is hyperbolic. The following lemma states that any metabolic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module is hyperbolic.

Lemma 2
.

If
L
is a
G
-invariant Lagrangian in a symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module
V
, there exists a
G
-invariant Lagrangian transverse to
L
.

Proof.

Forgetting the
G
-action, it is known that the set of Lagrangians transverse to
L
is naturally an affine vector space directed by
S^{2}L
. The stabilizer of
L
in the symplectic group acts on this space by affine transformations. A
G
-invariant transverse Lagrangian is obtained by considering the barycenter of the
G
-orbit of any Lagrangian transverse to
L
.
∎

3
A bordism argument

Suppose that
G
acts on the right and freely on
S
: the quotient map
S\to S/G
is a principal
G
-bundle, hence classified by a map
f:S/G\to BG
. Its fundamental class is the class
f_{*}([S/G])\in H_{2}(BG,\mathbb{Z})=H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

Let us rephrase this using covering spaces, supposing that the quotient
S/G
is connected. It forces us to make use of the following more suggestive notation.
Let
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
be Galois covering with group
G
where
S
is a connected compact oriented surface. Its monodromy is a morphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
which determines the covering completely.

As
H_{2}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathbb{Z})=H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is generated by the fundamental class
[S]
we define the fundamental class of the covering to be the element
\rho_{*}([S])\in H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition 1
.

Given a finite group
G
, there exists a unique map

\Phi_{G}:H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})

such that for any Galois covering
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
with monodromy
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
, the class of
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})
in
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
is equal to
\Phi_{G}(\rho^{*}([S]))
.

Proof.

Given any group
G
, we denote by
BG
a
K(G,1)
-space and consider the bordism group
\Omega^{2}(BG)
. It is generated by pairs
(S,f)
where
S
is a compact oriented surface and
f:S\to BG
is a continuous map, considered up to homotopy. Recall that if
S
is connected, it is equivalent to give
f
or to give a representation
\rho=f_{*}:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
up to conjugation.

We add the relation
(S_{1}\amalg S_{2},f_{1}\amalg f_{2})\sim(S_{1},f_{1})+(S_{2},f_{2})
and for any compact oriented
3
-manifold
M
with boundary and map
f:M\to BG
,

(\partial M,f|_{\partial M})\sim 0.

It is well-known that the map
\Omega^{2}(BG)\to H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
given by
(S,f)\mapsto f_{*}([S])
is an isomorphism.
This can be proved by hand or as a by-product of the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence.

Using this point of view, the proposition reduces to showing that the map sending
(S,\rho)
to the Witt class of
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})
induces a linear map

\Phi_{G}:\Omega_{2}(BG)\to W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
.

If
S
is not connected, we define
\Phi_{G}(S,f)
to be the sum of the image of its connected components. In this way, the first relation in
\Omega^{2}(BG)
is trivially satisfied. We only need to show that if
M
is a connected
3
-manifold with boundary and
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\to G
is a morphism, then
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})
is metabolic. For that, we consider the
G
-covering
\widetilde{M}\to M
corresponding to
\rho
.

The natural map
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})\to H_{1}(\widetilde{M},\mathbb{Q})
is
G
-equivariant and its kernel is Lagrangian by Poincaré duality. Hence
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})
is metabolic as claimed.
∎

4
Decomposition of the homology of the cover

4.1
Homology with twisted coefficients

Let
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
be a galois
G
-cover, where
G
acts on the right. Given any left
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module
E
, we define
H_{*}(S,E)
to be the homology of the complex
C_{*}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}E
.

Given two left
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules
E
and
F
, there is an intersection product
H_{1}(S,E)\times H_{1}(S,F)\to H_{0}(S,E\otimes F)
where
E\otimes F
is the usual tensor product over
\mathbb{Q}
endowed with the diagonal action of
G
. It is defined as follows: suppose that
\alpha,\beta
are two 1-simplices in
\widetilde{S}
whose projections in
S
are transversal. Denoting by
\epsilon_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
the sign of the intersection of
\alpha
and
\beta
at
p
, we have:

\alpha\otimes e\cdot\beta\otimes f=\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{p\in\alpha\cap\beta g}\epsilon_{p}(\alpha,\beta g)\,\,p\otimes e\otimes g^{-1}f.

(1)

Our main example will be
\mathbb{Q}[G]
, viewed as a
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-bimodule: it gives the isomorphism of right
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules
H_{1}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Q})=H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])
. Composing the above product with the pairing
\mathbb{Q}[G]\times\mathbb{Q}[G]\to\mathbb{Q}
given by
\langle g,h\rangle=1
if
g=h
and
0
else, gives back the intersection form

H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])\times H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])\to H_{0}(S,\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}.

4.2
Decomposition into irreducible representations

Fix a finite group
G
and consider a family
(\rho_{i}:G\to\GL(V_{i}))_{i\in I}
representing every isomorphism class of irreducible rational finite dimensional representation.

By the Schur Lemma, the space of endomorphisms of
V_{i}
commuting with
\rho_{i}(G)
is a division algebra, noted
D_{i}
(setting
\phi\psi=\psi\circ\phi
for
\phi,\psi\in D_{i})
. This endows
V_{i}
with the structure of right
D_{i}
-module.
Averaging a scalar product, we find that each
V_{i}
has an invariant scalar product, giving a
G
-isomorphism
\phi_{i}:V_{i}\to V_{i}^{*}
. The dual space
V_{i}^{*}
has a structure of left
D_{i}
-module hence, there exists an involution on
D_{i}
such that
\phi_{i}(x\lambda)=\overline{\lambda}\phi_{i}(x)
. This data is better encoded into a Hermitian form
h_{i}:V_{i}\times V_{i}\to D_{i}
satisfying for all
x,y\in V_{i}
and
\lambda,\mu\in D_{i}
:

h_{i}(x\lambda,y\mu)=\overline{\lambda}h_{i}(x,y)\mu\text{ and }h_{i}(y,x)=\overline{h_{i}(x,y)}.

Let
S
be a surface with a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
: we may consider as in Section
4.1
the homology
H_{1}(S,V_{i})
. It has a structure of right
D_{i}
-module and the composition of the intersection form and the Hermitian form gives an skew-Hermitian form

H_{1}(S,V_{i})\times H_{1}(S,V_{i})\to D_{i}.

These forms may be viewed as elements of a Witt group
W^{-}(D_{i})
whose definition is analogous to the definition of
W^{-}_{G}(\mathbb{Q})
. As before, we can define a map
\Phi^{i}_{G}:H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to W^{-}(D_{i})
such that for any
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
one has
\Phi_{G}^{i}(\rho_{*}[S])=H_{1}(S,V_{i})
.

In this section, we prove the following reduction.

Lemma 3
.

If the maps
\Phi^{i}_{G}:H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\to W^{-}(D_{i})
vanish for all rational irreducible
G
-modules
V_{i}
with Hermitian forms
h_{i}:V_{i}\times V_{i}\to D_{i}
, then
\Phi_{G}=0
.

Proof.

Recall the Artin-Wedderburn decomposition

\mathbb{Q}[G]=\prod_{i\in I}\End_{D_{i}}(V_{i})=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\otimes_{D_{i}}V_{i}^{*}.

We write it in two ways to insist that it is both an isomorphism of algebras and of
\mathbb{Q}[G]-\mathbb{Q}[G]
-bimodules.

The pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{Q}[G]
is
G
-invariant, hence it induces a
G
-invariant map
\mathbb{Q}[G]\to\mathbb{Q}[G]^{*}
. As all the
V_{i}
s are self-dual, this map must preserve the decomposition. In other terms, the decomposition is orthogonal. Moreover, a computation shows that the pairing in restriction to
\End_{D_{i}}(V_{i})
has to be

\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{n_{i}}{|G|}\tr(fg^{*})\quad\forall f,g\in\End_{D_{i}}(V_{i})

(2)

where
g^{*}
is the adjonction with respect to the Hermitian form
h_{i}
,
n_{i}=\dim_{D_{i}}V_{i}
and
V_{i}
has to be though of as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space when taking the trace.

Consider now a morphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to G
. We can write

H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])=\bigoplus_{i\in I}H_{1}(S,\End(V_{i}))=\bigoplus_{i\in I}H_{1}(S,V_{i})\otimes_{D_{i}}V_{i}^{*}.

In the last formula, we applied the universal coefficients theorem to write
H_{1}(S,V_{i}\otimes_{D_{i}}V_{i}^{*})=H_{1}(S,V_{i})\otimes_{D_{i}}V_{i}^{*}
, noting that the homology with coefficients only depends on the left
G
-action.

By assumption,
H_{1}(S,V_{i})
vanishes in
W^{-}(D_{i})
hence there exist Lagrangians
L_{i}\subset H_{1}(S,V_{i})
for all
i\in I
. One can check from Equation
2
that the following space

L=\bigoplus_{i\in I}L_{i}\otimes_{D_{i}}V_{i}^{*}

is a
G
-invariant Lagrangian in
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])
, proving the lemma.
∎

5
Reduction to
2
-groups

5.1
Induction

Suppose that
H
is a subgroup of
G
and we have a morphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to H
. We may compare
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[H])
and
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])
as symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-modules.

To this aim, we observe that for any symplectic
\mathbb{Q}[H]
-module
V
, the induced module
\Ind V=V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}\mathbb{Q}[G]
has a natural symplectic structure when viewed as a direct sum of copies of
V
indexed by representatives of
H\backslash G
. This construction gives a morphism
\Ind:W_{H}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})\to W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
and satisfies the following lemma, whose proof is left to the reader.

Lemma 4
.

If
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to H
is a morphism and
H
is a subgroup of
G
, then

H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G])=\Ind H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[H])\in W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q}).

5.2
Witt groups have 2-primary torsion

We will need the following result:

Theorem 2
.

For any finite group
G
, the Witt group
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
has only 2-primary torsion.

This statement seems well-known to specialists although we couldn’t find a proof of it in the literature.
By decomposing into irreducible pieces as in Section
4.2
, it reduces to the same statement for all
W^{-}(D_{i})
where the
D_{i}
’s are involved in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
. As such, it is proved by Wall in
[
11
]
: we follow the formulation of Ranicki (Proposition 22.11 in
[
10
]
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
D
be a division ring with involution such that
M_{n}(D)
is a simple factor of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
for some finite group
G
and some integer
n
, then
W^{-}(D)
is a countable abelian group of finite rank with countable
2
-primary torsion only.

Proof.

The proof consists in reducing the statement to the case of the standard Witt group of a field which is classical, see for instance
[
8
]
. For the interested reader, we sketch the steps of the proof.

First case:
If
D
is a field with trivial involution, then
W^{-}(D)=0
as any symplectic form over a field has a Lagrangian.

Second case:
If
D
is a field with a non trivial involution, pick an element
i\in D
such that
\overline{i}=-i
. The map
h\mapsto ih
induces an isomorphism from
W^{-}(D)
to
W^{+}(D)
. Let
K
denote the subfield of
D
preserved by the involution. Jacobson theorem tells that the map
h(x,y)\mapsto h(x,x)
gives an embedding
W^{+}(D)\to W^{+}(K)
, hence the result follows from the case of
W^{+}(K)
.

Third case:
If
D
is not commutative, it is a quaternion algebra over its center (mainly because
D
is isomorphic to its opposite thanks to the involution, see for instance
[
11
]
). The proof reduces to the previous case by applying Jacobson theorem, see
[
6
]
.
∎

Given a finite group
G
and a
2
-sylow
H
, we have the following commutative square:

\textstyle{H_{2}(H,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{H}}
\textstyle{H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{G}}
\scriptstyle{T}
\textstyle{W^{-}_{H}(\mathbb{Q})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Ind}
\textstyle{W^{-}_{G}(\mathbb{Q})}

Here
\alpha
is the map induced by the inclusion
H\subset G
and
T
is the transfer map. Let
2d+1
be the index of
H
in
G
: we have
\alpha\circ T=(2d+1)\id
.

Suppose that
\Phi_{H}
is trivial. Given
x\in H_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, the equation above and the commutativity of the diagram gives
(2d+1)\Phi_{G}(x)=0
. By Theorem
2
, multiplying by an odd number is injective in
W_{G}^{-}(\mathbb{Q})
, hence
\Phi_{G}(x)=0
.
This shows that if
\Phi_{G}
is trivial for all
2
-groups
G
, then it is trivial for any finite group.

6
The case of
2
-groups

6.1
Special 2-groups

We consider the following list of
2
-groups together with their unique rational irreducible and faithful representation
E
. They represent all possible extensions of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}/2^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}
.

6.1.1
Cyclic group

The group
C2^{n}=\langle x|x^{2^{n}}=1\rangle
(
n\geq 1
) acts on
E=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
by
xu=\zeta u
where
\zeta
has order
2^{n}
. The action commutes with the involution defined by
\overline{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1}
.

6.1.2
Dihedral group

In the remaining examples, we choose a root
\zeta
of order
2^{n-1}
. The group
D2^{n}=\langle x,y|x^{2^{n-1}}=y^{2}=1,yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}\rangle
(
n\geq 2
) acts on
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
by
xu=\zeta u
and
yu=\overline{u}
. Viewing
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
as a 2-dimensional vector space
E
over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})
gives the unique faithful rational representation of
D2^{n}
. It preserves a quadratic form over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})
for which
1,i
is an orthogonal basis.

6.1.3
Semi-dihedral group

The group
SD2^{n}=\langle x,y|x^{2^{n-1}}=y^{2}=1,yxy^{-1}=x^{2^{n-2}-1}\rangle
(
n\geq 4
) acts on
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)^{2}
by
x(u,v)=(\zeta u,-\zeta^{-1}v)
and
y(u,v)=(v,u)
, preserving the standard Hermitian form. Setting
E
to be the
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})
-vector space generated by the
SD2^{n}
orbit of
(1,1)
, we get a faithful representation defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})
preserving the standard Hermitian form over that field.

6.1.4
Generalized quaternion group

The group
Q2^{n}=\langle x,y|x^{2^{n-2}}=y^{2}=(xy)^{2}\rangle
(
n\geq 3
) acts on
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)^{2}
by
x(u,v)=(\zeta u,\zeta^{-1}v)
and
y(u,v)=(-v,u)

Let us recall the following theorem due to Fontaine, see

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a
2
-group and
V
be an irreducible
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module. Then there exists subgroups
K\lhd H
of
G
such that
H/K
is isomorphic to one of the 4 families of groups of Section
6.1
and
V=\mathbb{Q}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}E
where

-module through the quotient map

.

Let us prove now that
\Phi_{G}=0
for any finite
2
-group
G
. From Lemma
3
, it is sufficient to prove that
H_{1}(S,V)=0
in
W^{-}(D)
for any map
\pi_{1}(S)\to G

Let
H,K,E

D

E

H_{1}(S,V)=H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}E)=H_{1}(S^{\prime},E)

H_{1}(S^{\prime},E)
can defined directly through the composition

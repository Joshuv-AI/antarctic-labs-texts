Cube sum problem for integers having exactly two distinct prime factors
Dipramit Majumdar and Pratiksha Shingavekar

Abstract
. Given an integer n > 1 n>1 , it is a classical Diophantine problem that whether n n can be written as a sum of two rational cubes. The study of this problem, considering several special cases of n n , has a copious history that can be traced back to the works of Sylvester, Satgé, Selmer etc. and up to the recent works of Alpöge-Bhargava-Shnidman. In this article, we consider the cube sum problem for cube-free integers n n which has two distinct prime factors none of which is 3 3 .

Introduction

It is a long been asked Diophantine question: which cube-free integers
n>1
can be expressed as a sum of cubes of two rational numbers? If
n>2
is a cube-free integer and
\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
denotes the Mordell-Weil rank of the elliptic curve
E_{-432n^{2}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-432n^{2}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, then it is a well-known fact that
n
can be written as a sum of two rational cubes if and only if
\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})>0
.
For a prime
\ell\geq 5
,
a conjecture classically attributed to Sylvester (see for example
[
DV
]
) predicts that

\text{rk }E_{-432\ell^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 2,5(\text{mod }9),\\
1,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 4,7,8(\text{mod }9),\\
0\text{ or }2,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 1(\text{mod }9),\end{cases}\kern 4.62497pt\text{rk }E_{-432\ell^{4}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 2,5(\text{mod }9),\\
1,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 4,7,8(\text{mod }9),\\
0\text{ or }2,&\text{if }\ell\equiv 1(\text{mod }9).\end{cases}

One can reformulate the conjecture above as follows:

Sylvester’s Conjecture
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime and either
n=\ell
or
n=\ell^{2}
.
We have

(1)

If
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then

\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\ell\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
1,&\text{ if }\ell\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

(2)

If
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then

\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\ell\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
\{0,2\},&\text{ if }\ell\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

Several studies of the cube sum problem considering different particular cases for
n
have been done by many mathematicians including Sylvester
[
Sy
]
, Satgé
[
Sa
]
, Selmer
[
Se
]
and many others. For example, the case when
n=\ell
is a prime such that
\ell\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}
,
then the conjecture above was proved by Sylvester himself. If
\ell\equiv 4\text{ or }7\pmod{9}
, then
[
DV
]
proved that
\ell
and
\ell^{2}
are cube sums under the hypothesis that
3
is not a cube modulo
\ell
.

In
[
MS
]
, the authors showed that there are infinitely many primes congruent to
\pm 1\pmod{9}
which are sum of two rational cubes. Many other authors have contributed towards the Sylvester’s conjecture and the cube sum problem, for specific congruence classes of primes modulo
9

[
SY
]
,
[
Yi
]
. However, the problem is still open in full generality. In
[
JMS
]
, we proved Sylvester’s Conjecture under the hypothesis that the Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})

satisfies
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even.

For a general cube-free integer
n
,
recently,
[
ABS
]
announced a proof of the fact that a positive proportion of integers are cube sums and a positive proportion are not. Apart from this, very little is known about this problem in general. If
n
has two distinct prime factors, we know some partial results. Satgé
[
Sa
]
proved that
n=2\ell
(resp.
n=2\ell^{2}
) is a cube sum when
\ell\equiv 2\pmod{9}
(resp.
\ell\equiv 5\pmod{9}
);
[
CST
]
and
[
KL
]
also study this situation. In
[
JMS
]
, we have also examined the integers of type

2\ell
and
2\ell^{2}
for their solubility as a cube sum, where
\ell\neq 3
is a prime. In this article, we generalize the situation to consider the cube sum problem for cube-free integers
n
which are co-prime to
3
and have exactly two distinct prime factors, that is
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
,
where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are two distinct primes, none of which is equal to
3
.

We start by stating a conjecture which is slightly more general in nature.
By the work of Birch-Stephens
[
BS
, §4]
, we know that for a cube-free
n
,
the global root number of
E_{-432n^{2}}
is given by
-\omega_{3}\prod_{p\neq 3}\omega_{p}
.
In particular, when
n
is a cube-free integer co-prime to
3
, this formula for the global root number simplifies to
(-1)^{k_{2}}
(resp. to
(-1)^{1+k_{2}}
) if
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
(resp. if
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
); here
k_{2}
is the number of distinct primes
p
which divide
n
and are congruent to
2
modulo
3
. Looking at the sign of the functional equation of
E_{-432n^{2}}
,
we predict:

Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture
.

Let
n
be a cube-free integer co-prime to
3
. Assume that the set of prime divisors of
n
is given by
\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\},
where
\ell_{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv\ell_{k_{1}}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{k_{1}+1}\equiv\cdots\equiv\ell_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
Let
r_{n}:=\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
.
We have

(1)

If
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then
r_{n}\leq 2k_{1}+k_{2}-1
and
r_{n}\equiv k_{2}-1\pmod{2}
.

(2)

If
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then
r_{n}\leq 2k_{1}+k_{2}
and
r_{n}\equiv k_{2}\pmod{2}
.

A heuristic for the Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture:

Consider the elliptic curves
E_{16n^{2}}:y^{2}=x^{3}+16n^{2}
and
E_{-432n^{2}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-432n^{2}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
It is a well-known fact that there exists a rational
3
-isogeny
\phi_{n}:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{-432n^{2}}
given by
(x,y)\mapsto\big(\frac{x^{3}+64n^{2}}{x^{2}},\frac{y(x^{3}-128n^{2})}{x^{3}}\big)
.
This implies that
\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
.
Hence, for determining
r_{n}
, it is enough to obtain
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
.

Let
K:={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}:=1-\zeta\in K
,
where
\zeta=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
denotes the primitive third root of unity. Observe that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{6}=-27
and so, it follows that
E_{16n^{2}}
and
E_{-432n^{2}}
are isomorphic over
K
.
Composing
\phi_{n}
with this isomorphism, we obtain a
3
-isogeny
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
defined over
K
given by

\phi(x,y)=\Big(\frac{x^{3}+64n^{2}}{{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}x^{2}},\frac{y(x^{3}-128n^{2})}{{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}x^{3}}\Big).

(1)

Further, let
\Sigma_{K}
be the set of all finite primes of
K
and
S_{n}:=\{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Sigma_{K}\text{ }\big|\hskip 9.24994pt{\mathfrak{q}}\mid n\}.
Note that
|S_{n}|=2k_{1}+k_{2}
.
It follows from Corollary
1.7
that
r_{n}\leq 2k_{1}+k_{2}-1
(resp.
r_{n}\leq 2k_{1}+k_{2}
) if
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
(resp. if
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
). From these upper bounds on the Mordell-Weil rank of
E_{-432n^{2}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
along with the global root number, the parity conjecture implies Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture.

Sylvester’s Conjecture is a special case of the Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture for
k_{1}+k_{2}=1
.
In this paper, we study the case
k_{1}+k_{2}=2
,
that is, when
n
has exactly two distinct prime divisors both different from
3
. In this special case, the Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture can be restated as follows:

Conjecture 0.1
.

Let
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be two distinct primes both different from
3
. Assume that
n
is of the form either
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
.
We have

(1)

If
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then

\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}\{0,2\},&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
1,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
\{1,3\},&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

(2)

If
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then

\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}\{1,3\},&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
\{0,2\},&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3},\\
\{0,2,4\},&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

To prove Conjecture
0.1
, we study the
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-dimension of the
\phi
-Selmer group of
E_{16n^{2}}
over
K
and prove that it has the same parity as
k_{2}
(resp.
k_{2}+1
) provided
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
(resp.
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
). Now a simple application of Lemma
1.8
proves Conjecture
0.1
(assuming
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even). We remark that the Tate-Shafarevich conjecture predicts that
\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
is finite, which in turn implies that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even (see
[
JMS
, Remark 5.13]
for a more detailed discussion).

To compute
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
explicitly in all possible scenarios, we broadly divide them in three cases, namely

k_{1}=0
,
k_{1}=1

and

k_{1}=2
.

The computations for each of these cases are quite involved and challenging. From these computations we observe that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
depends on equivalence classes of
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n
modulo
9
, as well as on certain cubic residues (if
k_{1}>0
). See §
2
for precise statements. Finally, Lemma
1.8
provides us with bounds on
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
and we show that our bounds on
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
are sharp. See §
3
for precise statements.

One of the consequences of our explicit computation of the Selmer groups is that it leads us to obtain the following unconditional result:

Theorem
.

Let
n
be a cube-free integer co-prime to
3
having exactly two distinct prime factors
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
.
We have
\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=0
i.e.
n
is not a rational cube sum in the following cases:

(1)

n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
with
\ell_{1}\equiv 2\pmod{9}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 5\pmod{9}
,

(2)

n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
with
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2,5\pmod{9}
,

(3)

n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
with
n\equiv 2,5\pmod{9}
where
\ell_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3},\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}
is not a cube in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell_{1}}
,

(4)

n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}^{2}
with
n\equiv 4,7\pmod{9}
where
\ell_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3},\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}
is not a cube in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell_{1}}
.

Under the assumption
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even, we also provide a criterion for natural numbers
n\in\{\ell_{1}\ell_{2},\thinspace\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2},\thinspace\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2},\thinspace\ell_{1}\ell_{2}^{2}\}
for which
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=1
(cf. Theorem
3.2
) in terms of congruences modulo
9
and cubic residues.

The article is structured as follows: In §
1
, we discuss the basic set-up, recall some definitions and results from
[
JMS
]
which are used subsequently. Further, we explore the relationship between dimension of
\phi
-Selmer group over
K
and rank of elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
This is done in Lemma
1.8
, which is one of the key components of the paper. In §
2
, we restrict ourselves to cube-free natural numbers co-prime to
3
with exactly two distinct prime divisors and give an explicit description of the
\phi
-Selmer group of
E_{16n^{2}}/K
through computing its generators. Finally in §
3
, using Lemma
1.8
, we relate
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
with
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
,
thereby comment about the solubility of
n
as a sum of two rational cubes.

Lemma 1.5

(2) Now if

.
Hence, there exists
\alpha\in 1+3{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}\subset U^{2}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
such that
16n^{2}-1=-3\alpha^{2}
.
Thus, the point
P=(-1,{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\alpha)=(-1,\sqrt{16n^{2}-1})\in E_{16n^{2}}(K_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
Observe that
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}(P)=\big(\overline{\sqrt{16n^{2}-1}-4n},\overline{\sqrt{16n^{2}-1}+4n}\big)=\big(\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\alpha-4n},\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\alpha+4n}\big).

Now

{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\alpha+4n\equiv{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\beta)\pm 4\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{4}}
as
\alpha=1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\beta
for some
\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
.
Consequently,

{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\alpha+4n\equiv\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta\pm 4\equiv\sqrt{-3}\pm 4\equiv\pm\big((1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})\zeta^{2}\big)^{\pm 1}\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{4}},&\text{if }\beta\equiv 0\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}},\\
{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\pm 4\equiv-2\sqrt{-3}\pm 4\equiv\pm\big((1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})^{2}\zeta^{2}\big)^{\pm 1}\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{4}},&\text{if }\beta\equiv 1\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}},\\
{\mathfrak{p}}\zeta(1-{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\pm 4\equiv\pm\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-3}\equiv\pm\big(\zeta^{2})^{\pm 1}\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}^{4}},&\text{if }\beta\equiv-1\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}}.\end{cases}

As
\frac{V_{3}}{A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*3}}=\big\langle\big(\overline{(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})^{2}},\overline{(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})}\big)\big\rangle\subset\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
\big|\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\big|=9
by Lemma
1.4
, we see that
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\big\langle\big(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta}\big),\big(\overline{(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})^{2}},\overline{(1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3})}\big)\big\rangle
.

Note that
\zeta\notin U^{2}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
;
so
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap f\big(\frac{U^{2}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}{U^{4}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\big)\cong\frac{U^{3}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}{U^{4}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
.

∎

For the isogeny

\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}

, we define the
\phi
-
Selmer group of
E_{16n^{2}}/K
as a subgroup of

H^{1}(G_{K},E_{16n^{2}}[\phi])\cong\big(K^{*}/K^{*3}\times K^{*}/K^{*3}\big)_{N=1}

, which we denote by
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
,
as follows (cf.
[
JMS
, Eq. (6)]
for details):

{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K):=\{(\overline{x}_{1},\overline{x}_{2})\in\big(K^{*}/K^{*3}\times K^{*}/K^{*3}\big)_{N=1}\mid(\overline{x}_{1},\overline{x}_{2})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{q}}\in\Sigma_{K}\}.

(3)

Using the following result, one obtains a trivial bound on
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)

[
JMS
, Theorem 3.15]
:

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
E_{16n^{2}}:y^{2}=x^{3}+16n^{2}
be an elliptic curve and
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
be the
3
-isogeny over
K
in (
1
). Then
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\subset\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}
. As a consequence we obtain that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{K,S_{n}}^{*}}{{\mathcal{O}}_{K,S_{n}}^{*3}}=|S_{n}|+1
.

Following results relate

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
with

\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
:

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
n
be a cube-free natural number co-prime to
3
and
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\}
be the set of all distinct primes dividing
n
.
Further, assume that
\ell_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
for
i\leq k_{1}
and
\ell_{j}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
for
k_{1}+1\leq j\leq k_{1}+k_{2}
.
Then

r_{n}:=\text{rk }E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq\begin{cases}2k_{1}+k_{2}-1,&\text{ if }n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9},\\
2k_{1}+k_{2},&\text{ if }n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Notice that the the primes
\ell_{j}
for
k_{1}+1\leq j\leq k_{1}+k_{2}
remain inert in
K
,
whereas the primes
\ell_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k_{1}
split in
K
,
say
\ell_{i}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=\pi_{\ell_{i}}\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{i}}
.
(One can make an explicit choice of
\pi_{\ell_{i}}
(resp.
\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{i}}
) as follows: Write
\ell_{i}=\frac{1}{4}(L_{i}^{2}+27M_{i}^{2})
and define
\pi_{\ell_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{i}+\sqrt{-27}M_{i})
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{i}-\sqrt{-27}M_{i})
.
) Therefore,

S_{n}=\{\pi_{\ell_{1}},\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{1}},\ldots,\pi_{\ell_{k_{1}}},\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{k_{1}}},\ell_{k_{1}+1},\ldots,\ell_{k_{1}+k_{2}}\}

and hence
|S_{n}|=2k_{1}+k_{2}
.
So, it follows from Lemma
1.6
that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq|S_{n}|+1=2k_{1}+k_{2}+1
.

Now recall from
[
JMS
, Corollary 3.26]
that

\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}E_{16n^{2}}(K)[\phi]-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/K)[\phi]

(4)

Thus,
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}E_{16n^{2}}(K)[\phi]
.
Note that
|E_{16n^{2}}(K)[\phi]|=3
, so it follows that

r_{n}=\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)-1\leq|S_{n}|=2k_{1}+k_{2}
.

In particular, if
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then we claim that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq|S_{n}|=2k_{1}+k_{2}
and hence
r_{n}\leq 2k_{1}+k_{2}-1
in this situation. Observe that the group

\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}:=\{(\overline{x}^{2},\overline{x})\mid\overline{x}\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\}
,
where
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{n}}^{*}
denotes the set of
S_{n}
-units in
K
,
has
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-
dimension
|S_{n}|+1
by Dirichlet’s
S
-units theorem. In particular,
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\in\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}
.

On the other hand, note from Lemma
1.5
that
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
,
whenever
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
.
Consequently,
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
, so
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\subsetneq\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}
.
Thus, one obtains
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\lneq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\big({A_{S_{n}}^{*}}/{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}=2k_{1}+k_{2}+1
.

∎

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
E_{16n^{2}}:y^{2}=x^{3}+16n^{2}
be an elliptic curve and
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
the
3
-isogeny over
K
defined in (
1
). If

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=t
,
then

\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq t-1
.

Moreover, if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even, then

\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\equiv t-1\pmod{2}
.

Proof.

We get
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)-1
from (
4
). So if
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=t
,
then
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\leq t-1
.

Recall that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{3}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})=\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[3]+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]=\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]+1
,
since

|E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[3]|=3
.

On the other hand, consider the rational
3
-isogeny
\phi_{n}:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{-432n^{2}}
defined in the Introduction and its dual isogeny
\widehat{\phi}_{n}:E_{-432n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
.
Note that
|E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[\widehat{\phi}_{n}]|=1
.
Set
R:=\frac{\Sh(E_{-432n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[\widehat{\phi}_{n}]}{\phi_{n}(\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3])}
. Then by
[
BES
, Prop. 49]
we have the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[\widehat{\phi}_{n}]}{{\phi}_{n}({E}_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[3])}\rightarrow{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow{\rm Sel}^{3}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow{\rm Sel}^{\widehat{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{-432n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow R\rightarrow 0.

So,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{3}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\widehat{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{-432n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\frac{E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[\widehat{\phi}_{n}]}{{\phi}_{n}({E}_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})[3])}

-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\widehat{\phi}_{n}}({E}_{-432n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R
,
since
E_{-432n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
has no non-trivial
3
-torsion points. Now, we have
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\cong{\rm Sel}^{\phi_{n}}(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus{\rm Sel}^{\widehat{\phi}_{n}}(E_{-432n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
by
[
JMS
, Lemma 3.20]
. Therefore, we obtain

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{3}({E}_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})=

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/K)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R=

t-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R
.

Then the two expressions for
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{3}(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
composedly imply

\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/K)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R-1.

We know that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}R
is even by
[
BES
, Prop. 49]
. Now if we assume
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})[3]
is even, then
\text{rk }E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})\equiv\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/K)-1=t-1\pmod{2}.

∎

2.
Explicit computation of
3
-rank of
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)

Henceforth, we restrict our attention to cube-free natural numbers
n
that are co-prime to
3
and have exactly two distinct prime factors, that is
n
is of the form
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
or
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
,
where
\ell_{1}\neq 3
and
\ell_{2}\neq 3
are two distinct primes. In the language of the Generalised Sylvester’s Conjecture, we are studying the case
k_{1}+k_{2}=2
.

We consider the cases
k_{1}=0
,
k_{1}=1
and
k_{1}=2
separately in three different subsections. In each of the three subsections, we obtain the
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-dimensions of
{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
for integers
n\in\{\ell_{1}\ell_{2},\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2},\ell_{1}\ell_{2}^{2},\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}\}
,

where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are two distinct primes both different from
3
. However, we furnish the proofs only for
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
to avoid redundancy of literature, as the proofs for other types of
n
are similar in nature.

2.1.
The Case
k_{1}=0
, i.e.,
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}

In this subsection, we assume that
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
We have the following results regarding
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{{\phi}}({E}_{16n^{2}}/K)
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
be distinct primes and
n
is either
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
. Let
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
be the
3
-isogeny over
K
given in (
1
). Then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv 2\pmod{9}\text{ and }\ell_{2}\equiv 5\pmod{9},\\
3,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9},\\
2,&\text{ if }n\equiv 4,7\pmod{9}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the result for
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
.
Observe that
S_{n}=\{\ell_{1}{\mathcal{O}}_{K},\ell_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\}
.
By Lemma
1.6
, we have

{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\subset\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq|S_{n}|+1=3
.

The group of
S_{n}
-units in
K
is
{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,S_{n}}=\langle\pm\zeta,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\rangle=\langle\pm\zeta,\ell_{1},n\rangle
.
Hence,

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}=\big\langle\big(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta}\big),\big(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}}\big),\big(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n}\big)\big\rangle
.

Note that
(0,4n)\in E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
and
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(0,4n)=\big(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n}\big)
for all
{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Hence,
\big(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n}\big)\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
Thus,
1\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq 3
.
We now examine the remaining generators of
\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}
for their containment in

{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.

First, we discuss the generator
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})
. For
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9},
we know that
\zeta_{9}\in K_{\ell_{1}}
and
\zeta_{9}\in K_{\ell_{1}}
. Then it follows that
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})=(\overline{1},\overline{1})\in\big(A_{\ell_{i}}^{*}/A_{\ell_{i}}^{*3}\big)_{N=1}
for
i=1,2
.
Hence,
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})
belongs to both
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{1}}}
and
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}
by Lemma
1.4
. Also
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
by Lemma
1.5
(2) in this situation. For primes
{\mathfrak{q}}\notin S_{n}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
,
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})\in\big({A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{*}}/{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}=\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
by Lemma
1.4
. So
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
, when
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9}
.
On the other hand, if
\ell_{1}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}
,
then we know that
\zeta_{9}\notin K_{\ell_{1}}
and
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{1}}}\cap\big(A_{\ell_{1}}^{*}/A_{\ell_{1}}^{*3}\big)_{N=1}=\{(\overline{1},\overline{1})\}
by Lemma
1.4
. Hence,
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{1}}}
and so
(\overline{\zeta}^{2},\overline{\zeta})\notin{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
when
\ell_{1}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}
.

We now investigate the remaining generator
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})
of

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}
.
Note that for primes
{\mathfrak{q}}\notin S_{n}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
,
one has
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
by Lemma
1.4
. Thus, we only need to check whether
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
for
{\mathfrak{q}}\in S_{n}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
.
Note that the cubic residue
\big(\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{2}}\big)_{3}=1
,
as both
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are primes congruent to
2\pmod{3}.
Hence,
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})=(\overline{1},\overline{1})\in\big({A_{\ell_{2}}^{*}}/{A_{\ell_{2}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}
and so
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}
.
Similarly using
\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\ell_{1}}\big)_{3}=1
,
we get
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})=(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{1}}}
.
Thus, it reduces to check whether
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Let us first take
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9}
.
In this case,
\ell_{1}^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{9}
i.e.
\ell_{1}^{2}\in U^{4}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
Since
\frac{U^{3}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}{U^{4}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\cong\frac{V_{3}}{A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*3}}\subset\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
by Lemmas
1.3
and
1.4
, it follows that
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})=(\overline{1},\overline{1})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
Hence,
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=3
when
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9}
.

Next, let
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{9}
and
notice that
\ell_{1}^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
i.e.

\ell_{1}^{2}\in U^{2}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Hence by Lemma
1.5
(1),
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and it follows that
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
So,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=2
whenever
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{9}
.

Finally for
\ell_{1}\equiv 2\pmod{9}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 5\pmod{9}
,

observe that
\ell_{1}^{2}\in U^{2}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\setminus U^{3}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
This implies by Lemma
1.5
(2) that
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and hence
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}})\notin{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
There are four subgroups of

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}\Big/\big\langle(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n})\big\rangle
of order
3
corresponding to the elements
\ell_{1}
,
\zeta
,
\ell_{1}\zeta
and
\ell_{1}\zeta^{2}
.
To conclude that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=1
,
we need to show that neither

\big\langle(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}\zeta^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}\zeta})\big\rangle
nor
\big\langle(\overline{\ell_{1}\zeta^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}\zeta})\big\rangle
are contained in
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
Recall that
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}\cap\big({A_{\ell_{2}}^{*}}/{A_{\ell_{2}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}=\{(\overline{1},\overline{1})\}
by Lemma
1.4
. Using
\big(\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{2}}\big)_{3}=1
,
we see that
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}\zeta^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}\zeta})=(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\in\big({A_{\ell_{2}}^{*}}/{A_{\ell_{2}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}
.

Since
\zeta_{9}\notin{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{\ell_{2}}}^{*}
,
we have
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}\zeta^{2}},\overline{\ell_{1}\zeta})=(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}
and so it is not in
{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
A similar argument works for
(\overline{\ell_{1}^{2}\zeta},\overline{\ell_{1}\zeta^{2}})
.
This shows that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=1
,
when
\ell_{1}\equiv 2\pmod{9}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 5\pmod{9}
.

This completes the proof for
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
be distinct primes and
n=\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
. For the
3
-isogeny
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
over

given in (

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9},\\
3,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9},\\
2,&\text{ if }n\equiv 2,5\pmod{9}.\hskip 56.9055pt\qed\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

The results in this subsection easily generalize to any cube-free integer
n
,
co-prime to
3
, all of whose prime divisors
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
are congruent to
2
modulo
3
. In this situation we have,

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=\begin{cases}k,&\text{ if }n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9},\\
k+1,&\text{ if }\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv\cdots\equiv\ell_{k}\equiv 8\pmod{9},\\
k-1,&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

To see this, observe that if
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
,
then
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
,
whereas
(\overline{\ell_{i}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{i}})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
for each
1\leq i\leq k
.
If
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv\cdots\equiv\ell_{k}\equiv 8\pmod{9}
,
then
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
and for every
1\leq i\leq k
,
(\overline{\ell_{i}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{i}})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.
Finally, if
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
and if some of the
\ell_{i}\not\equiv 8\pmod{9}
,
writing
m=\zeta^{a_{0}}\ell_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\ell_{k}^{a_{k}}
,
one checks that
(\overline{m}^{2},\overline{m})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
if and only if
a_{0}=0
and
m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
.
An easy combinatorial argument shows that there are
3^{k-1}
such elements.

2.2.
The Case
k_{1}=1
, that is,
\ell_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}

In this subsection, we study the situation when one of the prime divisors of

is congruent to
1\pmod{3}
,
while its other prime divisor is congruent to
2\pmod{3}
.
For simplicity of notation, we assume that
\ell_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
Note that
\ell_{1}
splits as
\ell_{1}=\pi_{\ell_{1}}\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{1}}
in
K
, while
\ell_{2}
remains inert in
K
.
We denote by
\big(\frac{\cdot}{\cdot}\big)_{3}
the cubic residue symbol. Note that
\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}\big)_{3}=1
if and only if
\ell_{2}
is a cube in

.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
n
be an integer of the form either
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}^{2}
or
\ell_{1}^{2}\ell_{2}^{2}
, where
\ell_{1}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
are primes such that
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
.
For the
3
-isogeny
\phi:E_{16n^{2}}\to E_{16n^{2}}
defined in (
1
), we have

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)=\begin{cases}3,&\text{ if }\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}\big)_{3}=1,\\
1,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the result for
n=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
and mention that the proof in other cases for
n
given above can be obtained via a similar computation. We have
S_{n}=\{\pi_{\ell_{1}},\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{1}},\ell_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\}
and by Lemma
1.6
,

{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\subset\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq|S_{n}|+1=4
.

The group of
S_{n}
-units in
K
is
{\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{K,S_{n}}=\langle\pm\zeta,\pi_{\ell_{1}},\pi^{\prime}_{\ell_{1}},\ell_{2}\rangle=\langle\pm\zeta,\pi_{\ell_{1}},\ell_{2},n\rangle
.
Hence,

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}=\big\langle(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta}),(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}}),(\overline{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}^{2},\overline{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}),(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n})\big\rangle
.

Note that
(0,4n)\in E_{16n^{2}}({\mathbb{Q}})
and
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(0,4n)=(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n}),

\forall{\mathfrak{q}}
by the Definition (
2
) of the local Kummer maps. Hence,
(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n})\in{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
.

If
\ell_{2}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}
,
then we know that
\zeta_{9}\notin K_{\ell_{2}}
and
\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}\cap\big(A_{\ell_{2}}^{*}/A_{\ell_{2}}^{*3}\big)_{N=1}=\{(\overline{1},\overline{1})\}
by Lemma
1.4
. Hence,
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\ell_{2}}}
.
On the other hand, if
\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9},
then
\ell_{1}\equiv 4\text{ or }7\pmod{9}
.
So by a similar argument
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}}
.
Thus in either cases, we get that
(\overline{\zeta^{2}},\overline{\zeta})\notin{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)
and so
1\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq 3
.

We now investigate the remaining generators
(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}})
and
(\overline{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}^{2},\overline{\pi_{\ell_{1}}})
case by case. Recall that one can write
\ell_{1}=\frac{1}{4}(L^{2}+27M^{2})
and
\pi_{\ell_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}(L+3\sqrt{-3}M)
.

(1)

Let us first consider the case
\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}\big)_{3}=1
and
investigate the generator
(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}})
of

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}
.
Note that for primes
{\mathfrak{q}}\notin S_{n}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
,
(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
due to Lemma
1.4
. Thus, we only need to check whether or not
(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}})\in\text{Im }\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
for
{\mathfrak{q}}\in S_{n}\cup\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}
.
Notice that the cubic residue
\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}\big)_{3}=1

(\overline{\ell_{2}^{2}},\overline{\ell_{2}})=(\overline{1},\overline{1})\in\big({A_{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}^{*}}/{A_{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}^{*3}}\big)_{N=1}

So, it is in

Similarly,

Now using

Finally,

Next, we draw our attention to the generator

\Big(\frac{A_{S_{n}}^{*}}{A_{S_{n}}^{*3}}\Big)_{N=1}

Proposition 2.5

n

\ell_{1}\ell_{2}

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}{\rm Sel}^{\phi}(E_{16n^{2}}/K)\leq|S_{n}|+1=4

2.4

\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(0,4n)=(\overline{n}^{2},\overline{n})

(1)

\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9}

Note that
\zeta_{9}

1.5

Now if
\big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{\pi_{\ell_{1}}}\big)_{3}=1

Remark 3.6
.

For a prime
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{9}
,
Sylvester’s conjecture is unable to pinpoint which integers
n
of the form
\ell
or
\ell^{2}
are rational cube sum. This question was studied by
[
RZ
]
. The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts that

L(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}},1)=*C_{n},

where
*
is an non-zero number (depending on
n
) and
C_{n}=0
if and only if
n
is a rational cube sum (note that if
n
is not a rational cube sum, then one expects that
C_{n}\neq 0
,
and
C_{n}=|\Sh(E_{16n^{2}}/{\mathbb{Q}})|
which is expected to be a square integer).
[
RZ
]
provided efficient methods to numerically test for which of these integers
n
of the form
\ell
or
\ell^{2}
with
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{9}
,
C_{n}=0.

In a similar fashion, one can ask:
Let
n
be a cube-free integer co-prime to
3
with exactly two distinct prime factors
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
.
Can you provide an efficient method to numerically test which of the following integers
n
are rational cube sum?

•

n\equiv 1\pmod{9}
with
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,

•

n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
with
\ell_{1}\equiv\ell_{2}\equiv 8\pmod{9}
,

•

n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
with
\ell_{1}\equiv-\ell_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
\ell_{2}
a cube in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell_{1}}
.

Heisenberg formulation of adiabatic elimination for open quantum systems with two time-scales
François-Marie Le Régent Pierre Rouchon

Abstract
Consider an open quantum system governed by a Gorini–Kossakowski –Sudarshan–Lindblad master equation with two times-scales: a fast one, exponentially converging towards a linear subspace of quasi-equilibria; a slow one resulting small decoherence and Hamiltonian dynamics. Usually adiabatic elimination is performed in the Schrödinger picture. We propose here an Heisenberg formulation where the invariant operators attached to the fast decay dynamics towards the quasi-equilibria subspace play a key role. Based on geometric singular perturbations, asymptotic expansions of the Heisenberg slow dynamics and of the fast invariant linear subspaces are proposed. They exploit Carr’s approximation lemma from center-manifold and bifurcation theory. Second-order expansions are detailed and shown to ensure preservation, up to second-order terms, of the complete positivity for the slow propagator on a slow time-scale. Such expansions can be exploited numerically to derive reduced-order dynamical models.

Introduction

In the quantum physics community, adiabatic elimination is widely used to analyze the dynamics of open and dissipative quantum systems (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,

[

In this context, adiabatic elimination produces low dimensional dynamical models via the derivation from the original slow/fast differential equations of the slow differential equations governing the evolution on the invariant slow manifold. For open quantum system governed by the deterministic Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) master equation, adiabatic elimination is usually performed in the Schrödinger picture.

We perform here adiabatic elimination in the Heisenberg picture where the invariant operators associated to the fast dynamics play a crutial role: they are used to describe the slow dynamics but also to define the equations characterizing the fast invariant linear subspace. As far a we know, such Heisenberg point of view has not been considered in such a systematic and general way, despite the fact that, for stochastic quantum systems, the Heisenberg stochastic evolutions play a central role (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
10
]

In particular, our derivation relies on very general two time-scale assumptions: we only assume an exponentially fast convergence towards a linear subspace of quasi-equilibria those structure does not necessarily correspond to a decohence free subspace; the slow dynamics can result from arbitrary perturbations either Hamiltonian or Lindbladian; we do not assume a tensor-product structure where the fast decay is due to local decoherence fast dynamics of some sub-systems.

Combining two asymptotic expansions, a first one for the slow dynamics and a complementary one providing the set of linear equations characterizing the exponentially fast decaying sub-space, we show how to approximate up to exponentially small corrections the propagator over a slow time-scale (see lemma

2
Slow/fast dynamics

2.1
Singular perturbations for finite dimensional, linear and time-invariant systems

Take a linear time-invariant system of finite dimension

\frac{d}{dt}\xi=(A_{0}+\epsilon A_{1})\xi

where
\xi
is a real vector of finite dimension
\bar{D}
,
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are
\bar{D}\times\bar{D}
matrices with real entries and
\epsilon
is a small parameter. Assuming a slow/fast structure means that
A_{0}
can be block diagonalized in two blocks:

A_{0}=P_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&\Gamma_{0}\\
\end{array}\right)P_{0}^{-1}

where
P_{0}
is invertible and
\Gamma_{0}
is an Hurwitz (stable) matrix of dimension
\bar{D}-\bar{d}>0
, the dimension of the fast dynamics and where
\bar{d}>0
is the dimension of the slow dynamics.
Standard perturbation theory (see
[
14
]
) ensures that, for
\epsilon
small enough, one has a similar block decomposition:

A_{0}+\epsilon A_{1}=P(\epsilon)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta(\epsilon)&0\\
0&\Gamma(\epsilon)\\
\end{array}\right)P^{-1}(\epsilon)

where the matrices
P(\epsilon)
,
\Delta(\epsilon)
and
\Gamma(\epsilon)
are analytic versus
\epsilon
with

P(0)=P_{0}
,
\Delta(0)=0
and
\Gamma(0)=\Gamma_{0}
. Geometrically, exists, for
\epsilon
small enough, two invariant linear subspaces:

•

the slow one, of dimension
\bar{d}
, corresponding to the slow evolution governed by the propagator
e^{t\Delta(\epsilon)}

•

the fast one, of dimension
\bar{D}-\bar{d}
, corresponding to the fast and exponentially stable evolution governed by the propagator

e^{t\Gamma(\epsilon)}
.

2.2
Slow/fast GKSL quantum dynamics

All the developments below combine the above dynamics structure with non commutative computations with operators used to describe the decoherence dynamics of open-quantum systems.

Consider the time-varying density operator
\rho_{t}
on underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of finite dimension obeying to the following dynamics

\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho_{t})+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{t})

(1)

where
\epsilon
is a small positive parameter and where the GKSL linear super-operators
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
read (
\sigma=0,1
)

\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\rho)=-i[\widehat{H}_{\sigma},\rho]+\sum_{\nu}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\rho\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\rho+\rho\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\Big)

with
\widehat{H}_{\sigma}
Hermitian operator and
\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}
any operator not necessarily Hermitian.

Assume that for
\epsilon=0
and any initial condition
\rho_{0}
,
\rho_{t}
converges exponentially towards a steady state depending a priori on
\rho_{0}
. This means that we have a TPCP map
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
such that for any
\rho_{0}
:

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\rho_{0})\triangleq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho_{0})

(2)

The range of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{0}
, the linear space of equilibria for
\mathcal{L}_{0}
corresponding to its kernel. Denote by
\bar{d}
the dimension of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and consider an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
made of
\bar{d}
Hermitian operators
\widehat{S}_{1}
, …,
\widehat{S}_{\bar{d}}
such that
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
.
To each
\widehat{S}_{d}
is associated an invariant operator
\widehat{J}_{d}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{d})
being a steady-state of the adjoint dynamics (according to the Frobenius Hermitian product)
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{J}=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J})
where
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}
is the adjoint of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). For any solution
\rho_{t}
of (
1
) with
\epsilon=0
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{t}\right)
is constant since
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})=0
implies that

\frac{d}{dt}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{t}\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho_{t})\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})\rho_{t}\right)=0.

Thus one has:

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{0}\right)\widehat{S}_{d}\triangleq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho_{0}).

(3)

Moreover
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
since for any
t>0

\rm{Tr}\left(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{d})~\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}~e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}})\right)

and
e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}})=\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}
.
Notice that the operator sub-space of co-dimension
\bar{d}
defined by

\Big\{\rho~\big|~\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},~\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho\right)=0\Big\}

corresponds to the set of trajectories exponentially converging to
0
, i.e. the fast invariant sub-space when
\epsilon=0
.

3
Asymptotic expansion of the slow dynamics

For
\epsilon>0
and small, (
1
) admits also a
\bar{d}
dimensional linear invariant subspace denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
and close to
\mathcal{D}_{0}
(see
[
14
]
for a mathematical justification). This means that the set of real variables
x_{1}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{1}\rho\right)
, …,
x_{\bar{d}}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{\bar{d}}\rho\right)
can be chosen as local coordinates on
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
with the perturbed operator basis
\widehat{S}_{1}(\epsilon)
, …
\widehat{S}_{\bar{d}}(\epsilon)
.
If at some time
t
, the solution
\rho_{t}
of the perturbed system (
1
), belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
, it remains on
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
at any time:

\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{t}=(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})(\rho_{t})
where
\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}(t)\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)
.
Thus for any
(x_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{\bar{d}}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}}
, this invariance property reads

\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\frac{dx_{d}}{dt}~\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\right).

(4)

Thus for any
d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}
,
\frac{dx_{d}}{dt}
depends linearly on
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\bar{d}})
, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}x_{d}=\sum_{d^{\prime}}F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)x_{d^{\prime}}

where
F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)
are real coefficients to be chosen in order to satisfy the
invariance conditions:

\sum_{d^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime},d}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})(\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon))

for all
d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}
.
With the asymptotic expansion

F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)},\quad\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}

one can compute recursively
F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}
from
F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(m)}
and
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(m)}
with
m<n
. The recurrence relationships is based on the identification of terms with same orders versus
\epsilon
in the following equations:
\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}

\sum_{d^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}\right)\left(\sum_{n^{\prime}\geq 0}\epsilon^{n^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(n^{\prime})}\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right).

The zero-order term is satisfied with

F^{(0)}_{d,d^{\prime}}=0,\quad\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{d}.

First-order conditions read:
\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}

\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}).

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and taking the trace yields

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}{{}^{(0)}})\right)

(5)

since
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}\right)=\delta_{d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime}}
and
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{W})\right)=0
for any operator
\widehat{W}
because
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})=0
.
Thus
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}
is a solution
\widehat{X}
of the following equation:

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{X})=\sum_{d^{\prime}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}^{(0)}_{d})\right)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})

where the quantum channel
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is defined in (
2
). Following
[
4
]
, the general solution
\widehat{X}
is given by the absolutely converging integral,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)\right)~ds+\widehat{W}

where
\widehat{W}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{0}
the kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. We consider here the solution with
\widehat{W}=0
and thus

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)\right)~ds

where for all
d^{\prime}
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right)=0
.
The super-operator
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
defined for any operator
\widehat{W}
by

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\widehat{W})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\widehat{W}-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\widehat{W}\big)\right)~ds

(6)

provides thus the unique solution
\widehat{X}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\widehat{W})
of

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{X})=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\widehat{W})-\widehat{W}

such that for all
d
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\widehat{X}\right)=0
. To summarize the above calculation:

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})\big).

(7)

Take
n\geq 2
and assume that we have computed all the terms
F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(r)}
and
\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(r)}
of order
r<n
with

\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(r)}\right)=0
for all
d
and
d^{\prime}
. Invariance conditions of order
n
read:
\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}

\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)}).

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and taking the trace yields

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})\right).

(8)

Then
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}
is as follows

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}\right)

(9)

and satisfies for all
d^{\prime}
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right)=0
.

With such asymptotic expansion, we get an order
n
approximation of the dynamics on the invariant slow-manifold
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
.

Lemma 1
.

Take the slow-fast dynamics (
1
). For
\epsilon
small enough, it admits a unique invariant slow manifold of dimension
\bar{d}
with local real coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\bar{d}})
and analytic versus
\epsilon

\rho=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right)

Moreover, the evolution on this invariant slow linear subspace is governed by the following linear system, analytic versus
\epsilon

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\epsilon^{n}F^{(n)}\right)x(t)

(10)

where
F^{(n)}
is the matrix of real entries
F^{(n)}_{d,d^{\prime}}
.
Here
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}
and
F^{(n)}_{d,d^{\prime}}
are given recursively by (
8
) and (
9
) starting with
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{d}
and

F^{(0)}_{d,d^{\prime}}=0
.

The precise proof of this lemma is based on the above calculations and on the approximation theorem 5, page 32 of
[
15
]
. Uniqueness and analyticity versus
\epsilon
is automatically guaranteed since (
1
) is linear, time-invariant and of finite dimension.

4
Asymptotic expansion of invariant fast manifold

Section
3
was devoted to the invariant slow subspace of (
1
).
To compute the equation of the invariant fast subspace, it is in fact enough to search for its invariant operators equations, i.e.,
\bar{d}
independent linear scalar equations on
\rho
.
For
\epsilon=0
, the fast subspace corresponds to the solutions
\rho_{t}
of
\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho_{t})
converging to
0
. They are characterized by the following
\bar{d}
linearly independent equations:

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho\right)=0.

Thus for
\epsilon>0
, we are looking for the following set of
\bar{d}
equations,

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)\rho\right)=0

where
\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{J}_{d}^{(n)}
with
\widehat{J}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{J}_{d}
.
Invariance conditions mean that for all
\rho
such that
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\rho\right)=0
for all
d^{\prime}
, we have,

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\frac{d}{dt}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)(\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho)+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho)\right)=0.

Thus exists a square matrix of entries
G_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)
depending analytically on
\epsilon
such that,
\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}
,

\big(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}\big)(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon))=\sum_{d^{\prime}}G_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)

Setting
G_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}G_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and identifying
terms of the same power
n
versus
\epsilon
give:

•

for
n=0
,
G_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(0)}=0
since
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})=0
.

•

for
n=1
:

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d}^{(1)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})=\sum_{d^{\prime}}G_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(1)}\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}};

taking the trace with
\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}
gives
G_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(1)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})\right)
and

\widehat{J}_{d}^{(1)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d})-\sum_{d^{\prime}}G_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(1)}\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\right)

where

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{W})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}\left(\widehat{W}-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{*}\big(\widehat{W}\big)\right)~ds

(11)

and
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{*}=\lim_{s\mapsto+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}
.

•

for
n\geq 2
, we have

G_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d}^{(n-1)})\right).

(12)

and

\widehat{J}_{d}^{(n)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d}^{(n-1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}G_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}\right).

(13)

We have thus proved the following lemma

Lemma 2
.

For the slow/fast system (
1
) and
\epsilon
small enough, exists
\bar{d}
linearly independent Hermitian operators
\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)
, analytic versus
\epsilon
such that the linear subset of Hermitian operators

\Big\{\rho~\big|~\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)\rho\right)=0\Big\}

is invariant. Any trajectory of (
1
) starting in this subset converges exponentially to zero with a strictly positive rate independent of
\epsilon
. Moreover by construction, for all
\forall d,d^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
.

5
Slow propagator and TPCP maps

Take
T>0
. Then the linear map
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon,T}
on
\mathcal{D}_{0}
:

\mathcal{D}_{0}\ni\rho_{0}\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon,T}(\rho_{0})\triangleq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\Big(e^{T(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})}(\rho_{0})\Big)\in\mathcal{D}_{0}

is TPCP. Take
\epsilon
small enough and consider the
\bar{d}\times\bar{d}
matrix
E(\epsilon)
of entries

E_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\right)

where
\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(n)}
with
\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}=\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(0)}
and
\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{J}_{d}^{(n)}
with
\widehat{J}_{d}=\widehat{J}_{d}^{(0)}
are defined in lemma
1
and lemma
2
respectively.
Set
\rho_{0}=\sum_{d}x_{d}(0)\widehat{S}_{d}
with
x(0)=(x_{1}(0),\ldots,x_{\bar{d}}(0))\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}}
and consider
the solution

z_{\epsilon}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}}
of the linear system

E(\epsilon)z_{\epsilon}(0)=x(0).

From
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
one has

E(\epsilon)=I_{\bar{d}}+\epsilon^{2}C(\epsilon)

where
I_{\bar{d}}
is the identity matrix of size
\bar{d}
and the matrix
C(\epsilon)
is analytic versus
\epsilon
. Thus
E(\epsilon)
is invertible for
\epsilon
small with

E^{-1}(\epsilon)=I_{\bar{d}}+O(\epsilon^{2})
.
Then we have the following lemma

Lemma 3
.

For
\epsilon
small enough, exist
\gamma>0
and
M>0
such that for any
T>0
and any
x(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}}
we have

\sum_{d}\left|\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon,T}(\rho_{0})\right)-z_{\epsilon,d}(T)\right|\leq Me^{-\gamma T}\sqrt{\rm{Tr}\left(\rho^{2}_{0}\right)}

where
z_{\epsilon}(T)=e^{TF(\epsilon)}E^{-1}(\epsilon)x(0)
and
\rho_{0}=\sum_{d}x_{d}(0)\widehat{S}_{d}

Concluding remarks

For infinite dimensional systems, mathematical justifications of such asymptotic expansions are not straightforward (existence, uniqueness and convergence of the series). For infinite dimensional systems constructed with mainly bounded operators, precise mathematical guaranties are available (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
18
]
). One cannot use these available results when the dynamics rely on unbounded operators parameterizing the GKSL dynamical model (
1
).
The mathematical justification of such infinite dimensional extension requires precise functional analysis investigations and assumptions. They will be addressed in future developments.

These asymptotic expansion can be exploited numerically to simulate on a classical computer, composite open quantum systems encountered in quantum error correction (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
for preliminary results with cat-qubit systems).

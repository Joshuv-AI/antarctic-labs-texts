Linear isomorphism testing of Boolean functions with small approximate spectral norm
Arijit Ghosh, Chandrima Kayal, Manaswi Paraashar, Manmatha Roy

Abstract
Two Boolean functions f , g : 𝔽 2 n → { − 1 , 1 } f,\,g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\} are called linearly isomorphic if there exists an invertible matrix n × n n\times n matrix A A with entries from the the field 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} such that f ∘ A = g f\circ A=g , that is, for all x ∈ 𝔽 2 n x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} we have f ⁡ ( A ​ x ) = g ⁡ ( x ) f\left(Ax\right)=g(x) . Testing linear isomorphism is a generalization of, now classical in the context of property testing, isomorphism testing between Boolean functions. Linear-invariance of Boolean functions has also been extensively studied in other areas like coding theory and cryptography, and mathematics in general. In this paper, we will study the following two variants of this problem: Communication complexity: Assume that Boolean functions f f and g g on 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} are given to Alice and Bob respectively, and the goal is to test linear isomorphism between f f and g g by exchanging a minimum amount of communication, measured in bits, between Alice and Bob. Our main result is an efficient two-party communication protocol for testing linear isomorphism in terms of the approximate spectral norm of the functions. We will crucially exploit the connection between approximate spectral norm and sign-approximating polynomials. Query complexity: If f : 𝔽 2 n → { − 1 , 1 } f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\} is a known function and g : 𝔽 2 n → { − 1 , 1 } g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\} be an unknown function with a query access. We study the query complexity of testing linear isomorphism between f f and g g in terms of the approximate spectral norm of f f . As in the case of communication complexity, we will use properties of the approximate spectral norm.

1.1
Our results

Our first result is in the deterministic two-party communication model, which was first defined by Yao
[
35
]

In this model, there are two parties, Alice and Bob, and they are allowed to send messages (that is, bits) to each other. In linear isomorphism testing in

theorem 1.1
.

Let

with
t=\min\left\{\|f\|_{1,1/3},\|g\|_{1,1/3}\right\}
, and
\epsilon,\omega>0
. The two-party communication complexity of deciding whether
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)\leq\epsilon
or

is upper bounded by
O\left(t^{4}\log^{2}(1/\omega)\right)
.

Directly adapting the result of Parnas, Ron and Rubinfeld
[
31
, Claim 2]
about
distance estimation using testing
we get the following result.

theorem 1.2
.

Let

, and

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)-\epsilon\leq\Delta\leq\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)+\epsilon.

Since Alice and Bob have to decide the isomorphism distance between

To use these estimates, following

To construct

We will now recall some definitions and results from Fourier analysis of Boolean functions. For more details see
[
30
]
. For every
S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
parity functions

\chi_{S}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
are defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{S}(x)=(-1)^{\langle S,x\rangle},\;\forall x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.

Every function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
has a unique representation as a linear combination of the parity functions of the following form

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\widehat{f}(S)\chi_{S}(x),

where
\widehat{f}(S)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
are called the Fourier coefficients of
f
. The
spectral norm
of
f
, denoted by
\|f\|_{1}
, is defined as
\|f\|_{1}=\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(S)|
.

theorem 2.1

(Parseval’s identity)
.

For any function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[f(x)^{2}\right]=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\widehat{f}(S)^{2}.

In particular, when
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\left\{-1,1\right\}
we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(x)^{2}]=1
.

Parseval’s inequality implies that the absolute value of any Fourier coefficient of a Boolean function is at most
1
.

We need the following notion of an
\alpha
-approximation of a Boolean function.

Definition 2.2

(
\alpha
-Approximating function)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. A function
p:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to
\alpha
-approximate
f
if for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
|f(x)-p(x)|\leq\alpha
.

Definition 2.3

(
\alpha
-Approximate spectral norm)
.

The
\alpha
-approximate-spectral norm of
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
\|f\|_{1,\alpha}
, is defined as follows

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,\alpha}=\inf\{\|p\|_{1}:p\text{ }\alpha\text{-approximates }f\}.

We also need the notion of fractional Hamming distance between (the truth tables of) two functions.

Definition 2.4

(Fractional Hamming Distance)
.

For
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
and
g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, the fractional Hamming distance between
f
and
g
is denoted by
\delta(f,g)
and defined as

\displaystyle\delta(f,g):=\Pr_{x\sim\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}[f(x)\neq g(x)]=\frac{\left|x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\;:\;f(x)\neq g(x)\right|}{2^{n}}.

It is well known that fractional Hamming distance satisfies the triangle inequality, i.e. for all
f,g,h:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
,
\delta(f,g)\leq\delta(f,h)+\delta(h,g)
.
The fractional Hamming distance between two functions has a nice connection with their Fourier coefficients, which we describe in the following observation.

Observation 2.5
.

For any two Boolean function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
and
g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
,
\delta(f,g)\leq\epsilon
if and only if
\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\widehat{f}(S)\widehat{g}(S)\geq(1-2\epsilon)
.

Proof.

For any
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(x)g(x)]=\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\widehat{f}(S)\widehat{g}(S).

see
[
30
, Section 1.4]

Now for
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
and
g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(x)g(x)]=\Pr_{x}[f(x)=g(x)]-\Pr_{x}[f(x)\neq g(x)]=1-2\delta(f,g).

Hence,
\delta(f,g)\leq\epsilon
if and only if
\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\widehat{f}(S)\widehat{g}(S)\geq(1-2\epsilon)
.
∎

As discussed in the introduction, we focus on linear transformations of Boolean functions by an invertible matrix, which is defined below.

Definition 2.6

(Linear Transformation and Linear Isomorphism)
.

For
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
and
M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, the linear transformation of
f
with respect to
M
, denoted by
f\circ M
, is defined as follows:

\displaystyle f\circ M(x)=\sum_{S\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\widehat{f}(S)\chi_{S}(Mx).

Two functions
f,g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
are said to be linearly isomorphic if there exists
M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
such that
f=g\circ M
.

Definition 2.7

(Linear Isomorphism Distance)
.

The linear distance between two Boolean function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
and
g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
is defined as

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)=\min_{M\in GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)}\delta(f\circ M,g).

Therefore, if

f,g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
satisfy
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)\leq\delta
then there exists
M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
such that
\delta(f\circ M,g)\leq\delta
, and if
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f\circ M,g)\geq\delta
then for all
M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,

\delta(f\circ M,g)\geq\delta
.

Lemma 3.1

(Haviv and Regev
[

)
.

Let
p:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. For all
\delta\in(0,1/2]

\mathcal{S}

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and collection of signs
\left\{a_{S}\in\{-1,1\}\;\mid\;S\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

\displaystyle\left|p(x)-\frac{\|p\|_{1}}{|\mathcal{S}|}\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right|<\epsilon

Note that the above result was already implicitly present in a paper of Bruck Smolensky
[

[

Our communication protocol will be using the approximate small spectral norm variant of the above lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(Approximate spectral variant of
Lemma
3.1
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
be a Boolean function and
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Then there exists a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and Boolean function
F:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
such that the following are satisfied

1.

|\mathcal{S}|=O\left(\|f\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}\cdot 1/\right(1-\alpha\left){}^{2}\cdot\log(1/\delta)\right)
,

2.

F
is of the form
\text{sign}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right)
where
a_{S}\in\{-1,1\}
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
, and

3.

\delta(f,F)\leq\delta
.

Proof.

By our hypothesis there exists a function
p:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle|p(x)-f(x)|\leq\alpha,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

(1)

and
\|p\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1,\alpha}
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there exists a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and a collection of signs

\displaystyle\left\{a_{S}\in\{-1,1\}\mid S\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}|=O\left(\|f\|_{1,\alpha}^{2}\cdot 1/\right(1-\alpha\left){}^{2}\cdot\log(1/\delta)\right),

(2)

and for at least
1-\delta
fraction of the points in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\left|p(x)-\frac{\|p\|_{1}}{|\mathcal{S}|}\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right|<1-\alpha.

(3)

Now consider the Boolean function

\displaystyle F=\text{sign}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{F}_{p}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right).

If Equation (
3
) is satisfied for some

, then
F(x)=f(x)
. To see this observe that, if Equation (
3
) holds for some
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
then, from Equation (
1
), we also have

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-\frac{\|p\|_{1}}{|\mathcal{S}|}\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right|\leq\left|f(x)-p(x)\right|+\left|p(x)-\frac{\|p\|_{1}}{|\mathcal{S}|}\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}a_{S}\chi_{S}(x)\right|<1.

Therefore,
\delta(f,F)\leq\delta
.
∎

We will also need the following triangle inequality for linear distance.

Lemma 3.3

(Triangle inequality for linear distance)
.

Let
f,g,h:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)+\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(g,h)\geq\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,h).

Proof.

Let
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\in GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{F}_{2}\right)
such that
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g)=\delta(f\circ M_{1},g)
,
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,h)=\delta(f\circ M_{2},h)
and
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(h,g)=\delta(h,g\circ M_{3})
. Observe that by triangle inequality for fractional Hamming distance,

\displaystyle\delta(f\circ M_{2},h)+\delta(h,g\circ M_{3})\geq\delta(f\circ M_{2},g\circ M_{3})=\delta(f\circ M,g)\geq\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(f,g),

where
M=M_{2}\cdot M_{3}^{-1}
and the last inequality follows from the definition of linear distance.
∎

Input.

In particular, let

Thus the probability of bad events happening for Algorithm

5
Many functions with small approximate spectral norm but large spectral norm

For
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
let
|x|
denote the number of
1
’s in
x
, i.e. the Hamming weight of
x
. The Hamming ball of radius
1
centered at
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is the set
B_{1,a}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:|x+a|\leq 1\}
. Let
\mathbb{I}_{1,a}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
be the indicator function of the Hamming ball of radius
1
centered at
a
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1,a}(x)=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ $|x+a|\leq 1$}\\
1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

In
[
11
]
it was shown that the indicator function of the Hamming ball of radius
1
centered at
0^{n}
has small approximate spectral norm and large spectral norm.

theorem 5.1

(Cheung, Hatami, Zhao, and Zilberstein
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{I}_{1,0^{n}}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
be the indicator function of the Hamming ball of radius
1
centered at
0^{n}
. Then

\|\mathbb{I}_{1,0^{n}}\|_{1}=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
and for all
\alpha\in(0.1/2]
,

\|\mathbb{I}_{1,0^{n}}\|_{1,\alpha}=O(\log\alpha)

The following is a simple corollary of the above theorem.

Corollary 5.2
.

There are
2^{\Omega(n^{2})}
Boolean functions
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
that satisfy
\|f\|_{1}=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
and for all
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, and
\|f\|_{1,\alpha}=O(\log\alpha)
.

Proof.

Let
p:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be any function, then observe that the spectral norm of
p
is equal to the spectral norm of
p\circ M
, where
p\circ M(x)=p(Mx)
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. This is because the Fourier spectrum of
p\circ M
is a permutation of the Fourier spectrum of
p
.

We also have
\|p\|_{1,\alpha}=\|p\circ M\|_{1,\alpha}
for all
p:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
(we are interested in Boolean valued functions in this paper but the notion of approximating functions extends naturally to real valued functions). If not then without loss of generality assume
\|p\|_{1,\alpha}<\|p\circ M\|_{1,\alpha}
and let
\widetilde{p}
be an
\alpha
-approximating function for
p
. Observe that
\widetilde{p}\circ M
is an approximating function for
p\circ M
which is a contradiction.

The corollary follows since there are
|GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|=2^{\Omega(n^{2})}
and for any
M_{1}\neq M_{2}\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\mathbb{I}_{1,0^{n}}\circ M_{1}\neq\mathbb{I}_{1,0^{n}}\circ M_{2}
.
∎

6
Conclusion

In Theorem
1.3
we require that
\alpha\leq\omega/4
. Because of this reason, we cannot use Theorem
1.3
together with distance estimation via testing framework of Parnas et al.
[
31
]
to additively approximate distance to linear isomorphism. We want to remove this dependence between
\alpha
and
\omega
. Note that our communication result (Theorem
1.1
) does not have this dependence.

Recall that two Boolean functions
f,g:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
are called affinely isomorphic if there exist a
\exists M\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
f(Ax+a)=g(x)
. Our results (Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
) can be extended to test affine isomorphism between functions.

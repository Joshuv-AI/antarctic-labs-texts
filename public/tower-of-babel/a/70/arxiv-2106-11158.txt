The purpose of the present article is to show several refined and generalized Bohr-type inequality. These results are based on recent investigation and various

[
4
,
5
,
18
,
19
]
). For a detailed account of
investigation on this topic, one may refer to the articles by Abu-Muhanna et al.
[
3
]
, Defant and Prengel
[
12
]
,
Garcia et al.

1.1.
Classical Bohr inequality and some recent developments

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of all analytic functions defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|<1\}
.
The present article mainly considers the following two subclasses of
\mathcal{A}
, namely,

\mathcal{B}=\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}:\,\mbox{ $|f(z)|<1$ in $\mathbb{D}$}\}~\mbox{ and }~\mathcal{P}=\{f\in{\mathcal{A}}:\,\mbox{ ${\rm Re\,}f(z)<1$ in $\mathbb{D}$}\}.

While defining the class
\mathcal{B}
, sometimes we consider
|f(z)|\leq 1
(instead of
|f(z)|<1
) which is nothing more than admitting unimodular
constant functions into the set

\mathcal{B}
. The classical power series inequality for the unit disk due to Harald Bohr in 1914
[
10
]
is the following:

Theorem A.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1~\mbox{ for }~|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the constant
1/3
is sharp.

We observe that
M_{f}(r)
is rotationally invariant, i.e.,
M_{f}(r)=M_{f_{\theta}}(r)
, where
f_{\theta}(z)=e^{-i\theta}f(z)
with
\theta=\arg a_{0}.

Thus, it suffices to assume in Theorem A that
a_{0}\in[0,1).

At first Bohr obtained this inequality only for
r\leq 1/6
, but later Wiener, Riesz and Schur independently proved that it holds for
r\leq 1/3
(cf.
[
29
,
37
,
38
]
). The number
1/3
is called the Bohr radius for
\mathcal{B}
. Other proofs were given in
[
30
,
31
]
. Indeed as pointed out in
[
34
]
, in terms of subordination,
f\in\mathcal{B}
is equivalent to
writing

f(z)\prec\varphi_{a_{0}}(z),\quad\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}=a-(1-|a|^{2})\frac{z}{1-\overline{a}z},

and thus, from
[
36
]
, it follows that
|a_{n}|\leq|\varphi_{a_{0}}^{\prime}(0)|=1-|a_{0}|^{2}
for
n\geq 1
which clearly shows that

M_{f}(r)\leq|a_{0}|+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\frac{r}{1-r}\leq|a_{0}|+(1-|a_{0}|)\frac{2r}{1-r}\leq 1~\mbox{ for $r\leq 1/3$}.

The number
1/3
is optimal because for
a\in(0,1)
, we have

M_{\varphi_{a}}(r)=1+\frac{1-a}{1-ar}\left(r(1+2a)-1\right)>1~\Longleftrightarrow~r>\frac{1}{1+2a},

which by allowing
a\rightarrow 1^{-}
shows that the Bohr radius for
\mathcal{B}
cannot be bigger than
1/3
.

Remark 1
.

We would like to point out that a similar approach as above clearly shows that Theorem Acontinues to hold even
if we replace
f\in\mathcal{B}
by
f\in\mathcal{P}
such that
f(0)\in[0,1)
.
However, an earlier proof of this result may be obtained from
[
29
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
3
]
.

Determination of Bohr radius is being extensively considered nowadays in different settings, which includes the discussion on the Bohr radius for holomorphic functions of several complex variables (cf.
[
2
,
9
,
13
,
16
,
27
]
) or Bloch spaces (cf. Kayumov et al.
[
22
, Section 4]
, Liu and Ponnusamy
[
25
]
), Hardy spaces (cf.
[
6
,
13
]
) and moreover the majorant series comparison of two analytic functions satisfying the subordination relation (cf.

[
1
,
28
,
35
]
and the references therein). Kayumov et al.
[
18
,
20
]
considered the problem of determining Bohr radius for the classes of odd analytic functions, and for locally univalent functions, and for derivatives of analytic functions, we refer to
[
7
,
8
]
. Recently, it has become very common to apply Bohr radius to harmonic function
[
14
,
19
,
22
]
and the Bohr radius of harmonic functions is almost parallel to that of the case of analytic functions.

A version of Bohr’s inequality for the family
\mathcal{P}
was discussed by Ponnusamy et al. in
[
32
]
. Therefore in Section
3
, we mainly extend the Bohr radius
for the family
\mathcal{P}
. Moreover, the properties of
M_{f}(r)
are extended to the alternating series
A_{f}(r)
, which was originally investigated in
[
4
]
by Ali et al. In fact they have shown that if
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then
|A_{f}(r)|<1
holds for
r\leq 1/\sqrt{3}
. In addition, according to Liu et al. in
[
26
]
, the Bohr radius can be generalized by using the property of
A_{f}(r)
. Surprisingly, in recent papers, the authors in
[
17
,
24
,
33
,
34
]
refined the Bohr inequality in improved form by replacing
M_{f}(r)
by
M_{f}(r)+(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r})\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}
and more recently, the authors in
[
23
,
35
]
use nonnegative continuous functions
\{\zeta_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
[0,1)
instead of
\{r^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}.
Refer to Majorant series
M_{f}(r)
. In Sections
3
and
4
of this paper, refined forms are also used to promote the Bohr phenomenon.

1.2.
Basic notation

Before we continue the discussion, we fix some notations. Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
,
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
f_{0}(z)=f(z)-f(0)
. Then we define respectively the majorant series and the alternating series of
f
as follow:

M_{f}(r)=|a_{0}|+M_{f_{0}}(r)~\mbox{ and }~A_{f}(r)=|a_{0}|+A_{f_{0}}(r).

where
M_{f_{0}}(r)
and
A_{f_{0}}(r)
are the majorant series and the alternating series of
f_{0}
given by

M_{f_{0}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}~\mbox{ and }~A_{f_{0}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}|a_{n}|r^{n}.

We let for convenience,

\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}~\mbox{ and }~\|f\|_{r}^{2}=|a_{0}|^{2}+\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}.

In addition, if
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{np+m}z^{np+m}
for some
m\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, we define

A_{f_{m}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{np+m}a_{np+m}r^{np+m}.

In order to develop the Bohr phenomenon proposed by Kayumov et al.
[
23
]
and developed
further by Ponnusamy et al.
[
35
]
, we need to introduce some new notation. Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all sequences
\zeta=\{\zeta_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of nonnegative continuous functions in
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(r)
converges locally uniformly with respect to
r\in[0,1)
. For convenience, we let
\Phi_{N}(r)=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(r)
whenever
\zeta\in\mathcal{F}
, and in what follows we let

B_{N}(f,\zeta,r):=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\zeta_{n}(r)\ \mbox{for}\ N\geq 0;\quad G(f_{0},\zeta,r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2n}(r)}{1+|a_{0}|}+\Phi_{2n+1}(r)\right)

for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.
The reason for introducing these quantities are clear from the recent development on this topic (cf.
[
23
,
35
]
). In the classical case
\zeta=\{r^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
G(f_{0},\zeta,r)
reduces to
\left(\frac{r}{1-r}+\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}.

1.3.
Known important theorems

Recently, Kayumov and Ponnusamy et al.
[
21
]
(see also
[
32
]
) refined Theorem A in the following form whereas, Liu et al.
[
26
]
proved the estimate of the alternating series.

Theorem B.

(
[
21
]
)
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},
and
S_{r}
denotes the area of the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under the mapping
f
. Then

M_{f}(r)+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}\right)\leq 1\quad for\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the constants
1/3
and
16/9
cannot be improved. Moreover

M_{f}(r)+|f(z)-a_{0}|^{2}\leq 1\quad for\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the constant
1/3
cannot be improved.

In
[
23
]
, a natural generalization of Theorem A was established. We now recall this result. Further development of this result
for the classes of subordinations and quasi-subordinations may be obtained from
[
35
]
.

Theorem C.

(
[
23
]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
p\in(0,2]
. If
\zeta_{0}(r)>\frac{2}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(r)
for
r\in[0,R)
, where
R
is the minimal positive root of the equation
\zeta_{0}(x)=\frac{2}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(x)
, then the following sharp inequality holds:

B_{f}(\zeta,p,r):=|a_{0}|^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\zeta_{n}(r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)\ \mbox{ for all $r\leq R$.}

In the case when
\zeta_{0}(x)<\frac{2}{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(x)
in some interval
(R,R+\varepsilon)
, the number
R
cannot be improved.

Theorem D.

(
[
32
, Theorem 1]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{P}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
a_{0}\in[0,1)
. Then

(1.1)

M_{f}(r)+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}\leq 1

holds for all
r\leq r_{*}
, where
r_{*}\approx 0.24683
is the unique root of the equation
3r^{3}-5r^{2}-3r+1=0
in the interval
[0,1)
.
Moreover, for any
a_{0}\in(0,1)
there exists a uniquely defined
r_{0}=r_{0}(a_{0})\in\left(r_{*},\frac{1}{3}\right)
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a_{0}}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}\leq 1

for
r\in[0,r_{0}]
. The radius
r_{0}=r_{0}(a_{0})
can be calculated as the solution of the equation

\Phi(\lambda,r)=4r^{3}\lambda^{2}-(7r^{3}+3r^{2}-3r+1)\lambda+6r^{3}-2r^{2}-6r+2=0,

where
\lambda=1-a_{0}.
The result is sharp.

At this place, it is worth pointing out that (
1.1
) holds for
r\leq 1/3
(cf.
[
33
, Theorem 2]
) if the assumption
f\in\mathcal{P}
is replaced by
f\in\mathcal{B}
.
As mentioned in the introduction, this is in contrast to the case of classical Bohr inequality (refer to Theorem A and Remark
1
).

Theorem E.

(
[
26
]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

\left|\ |f(z)|+A_{f_{0}}(r)\right|\leq 1~~\mbox{ for }~~|z|=r\leq\sqrt{2}-1.

Can we establish an analog of Theorems B and C
for functions

In Section

1
and present other promotion concerning functions

.
Moreover, in Section

A_{f}(r)
and
improve some conclusions of
[
4
]
and
[
26
]
. Throughout the article for
a\in[0,1),
we let

(1.2)

\varphi_{a}(z)=\dfrac{a-z}{1-az}~\mbox{ and }~\psi_{a}(z)=a-2(1-a)\frac{z}{1-z},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that
\varphi_{a}\in\mathcal{B}
and

2.
Key lemmas and their Proofs

In order to establish our main results, we need the following lemmas and some important remarks. Two of these lemmas are well-known and the remaining are
new and are key in our discussion.

Lemma F.
(
[
3
]
)

If
p\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}
such that
{\rm Re}\,p(z)>0
in
\mathbb{D}
, then we have the sharp inequality

|p_{n}|\leq 2{\rm Re}\,p_{0}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Following the notation of Section 1.2, we have

Lemma G.
(
[
35
]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{B}
,
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
p\in(0,2]
. If
\zeta=\{\zeta_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
, then the following sharp inequality holds:

|a_{0}|^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+B_{1}(f,\zeta,r)+G(f_{0},\zeta,r)\leq|a_{0}|^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\Phi_{1}(r)

for all
|z|=r\in[0,1)
. The result is sharp for
\varphi_{a}(z)
given by (
1.2
).

Remark 2
.

As a motivation for our discussion, we mention now few useful remarks concerning some special cases of Lemma G for
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
.
Proof of each item below can be easily derived.

(i)

Set
p=1
,
\zeta_{n}(r)=r^{n}
for
n=km
for some fixed natural number
k\geq 1
and
0
, otherwise. Then
Lemma G gives following sharp inequality for
f\in\mathcal{B}
:

(2.1)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a_{km}|r^{km}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r^{k}}{1-r^{k}}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}\leq|a_{0}|+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\frac{r^{k}}{1-r^{k}}

for
r\in[0,1)
. Equality is attained for
f(z)=\varphi_{a}(z^{k})
, where
\varphi_{a}
is given by (
1.2
).
As

|a_{0}|+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\frac{r^{k}}{(1-r^{k})}\leq|a_{0}|+(1-|a_{0}|)\frac{2r^{k}}{1-r^{k}}\leq 1

for
r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt[k]{3}}
, the inequality (
2.1
) contains a refined version of
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
.

(ii)

For the case
p=1
,
\zeta_{2n-1}(r)=r^{2n-1}~(n\geq 1)
and
\zeta_{2n}(r)=0~(n\geq 0)
, we can obtain the following the sharp inequality

(2.2)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2n-1}|r^{2n-1}\leq\frac{r}{1-r^{2}}(1-\|f\|_{r}^{2})\ \mbox{ for $r\in[0,1)$.}

Equality is attained for
f=\varphi_{a}
, where
\varphi_{a}(z)
is given by (
1.2
).

This formula (
2.2
) can also be written in another form which establishes the relationship between
M_{f}(r)
and
A_{f}(r)
:

M_{f}(r)\leq\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}}(1-\|f\|_{r}^{2})+A_{f}(r)\ \mbox{ for $r\in[0,1)$.}

(iii)

Adding (
2.1
) with
k=2
and (
2.2
), we can easily obtain the following known inequality (which is (
2.1
) with
k=1
):

M_{f_{0}}(r)+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}\leq(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\frac{r}{1-r}\ \mbox{ for $r\in[0,1)$.}

Equality is attained for
f(z)=\varphi_{a}(z)
, where
\varphi_{a}(z)
is given by (
1.2
).

Lemma 1
.

If
f\in\mathcal{P}
,
a_{0}=f(0)\in[0,1)
, and
S_{r}(f)
denotes the area of the image of the subdisk
|z|<r
under the mapping
f
, then the following sharp inequality holds

(2.3)

\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi}\leq 4(1-a_{0})^{2}\frac{r^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}\quad for\quad|z|=r<1.

Proof.
Let
f\in\mathcal{P}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, where
a_{0}\in[0,1).
Applying Lemma F to
p(z)=1-f(z)
, we easily have

|a_{n}|\leq 2(1-a_{0})
for
n\geq 1
and thus,

\frac{S_{r}(f)}{\pi}=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dxdy=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 4(1-a_{0})^{2}\frac{r^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}

and the inequality (
2.3
) follows. It is easy to see that the inequality (
2.3
) becomes an equality for the
function
f(z)=\psi_{a}(z)
, where
\psi_{a}(z)
is given by (
1.2
). This is because for this function,
a_{0}=a
and
a_{n}=-2(1-a)
for
n\geq 1
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{np+m}z^{np+m}
, where
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq m<p
. Then

(2.4)

|f(z)|+r^{m}A(r)\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq r_{p,m}
, where
r_{p,m}
is the minimal positive root of the equation

r^{3p-m}-2r^{3p}-3r^{2p}-r^{p-m}+1=0,

and

A(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2np+m}|r^{2np}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{m}|}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}.

The radius
r_{p,m}
is best possible.

Proof.
We write
f
as
f(z)=z^{m}g(z^{p})
, where
p\geq 1
and
m>0
are fixed and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
with
b_{n}=a_{np+m}\ (n\geq 0)
. Schwarz-Pick Lemma
applied to
g
gives

|f(z)|\leq r^{m}\left(\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|}\right)\ \mbox{for all $z=re^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{D}}$}.

This inequality and Lemma
4
(i) (with
\zeta_{2n}(r)=r^{2n}
,
n\geq 0
) proved later give, for
r\in[0,1)
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle|f(z)|+r^{m}A(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{m}\left[\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|}+(1-|a_{m}|^{2})\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\dfrac{P_{r}(|a_{m}|)}{(1-r^{2p})(1+r^{p}|a_{m}|)}

where

\displaystyle P_{r}(x)=(xr^{3p}-x^{3}r^{3p+m}+xr^{3p+m}-r^{3p+m})

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+(r^{2p+m}+r^{2p}-xr^{2p+m}-x^{2}r^{2p+m})+(r^{p+m}-xr^{p})+(xr^{m}-1).

Thus, to prove that the left hand side of (
2.5
) is smaller than or equal to
1
, it suffices to show that
P_{r}(x)\leq 0
for
x\in[0,1)
. Note that

P_{r}(1)=(1-r^{m})(r^{3p}+r^{2p}-r^{p}-1)=(1-r^{m})(1+r^{p})(r^{2p}-1)\leq 0.

We claim that
P_{r}(x)
is an increasing function of
x
for
x\in[0,1]
. Now a direct computation shows that

P_{r}^{\prime}(x)=r^{3p}-3x^{2}r^{3p+m}+r^{3p+m}-r^{2p+m}-2xr^{2p+m}-r^{p}+r^{m},

and

P_{r}^{\prime\prime}(x)=-6xr^{3p+m}-2r^{2p+m}\leq 0,

for all
x\in[0,1]
. Thus,
P_{r}^{\prime}
is a decreasing function of
x\in[0,1].
Consequently, by the condition of the theorem, we have

P_{r}^{\prime}(x)\geq P_{r}^{\prime}(1)=r^{m}[r^{3p-m}-2r^{3p}-3r^{2p}-r^{p-m}+1]\geq 0~\mbox{ for $r\leq r_{p,m}$.}

Thus for each
r\leq r_{p,m}
,
P_{r}(x)
is an increasing function of
x
on
[0,1]
, which implies that
P_{r}(x)\leq P_{r}(1)<0
for all

x\in[0,1]
and
r\leq r_{p,m}
.

To show that the radius
r_{p,m}
is best possible, we let
a\in[0,1)
and consider the function

\displaystyle f(z)=z^{m}\left(\frac{a+z^{p}}{1+az^{p}}\right)=az^{m}+(1-a^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}a^{n-1}z^{np+m},\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

A simple computation gives

A(r)=(1-a^{2})\frac{ar^{2p}}{1-a^{2}r^{2p}}+\left(\frac{1}{1+a}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)(1-a^{2})^{2}\frac{r^{2p}}{1-a^{2}r^{2p}}=(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}

and thus, we find that

\displaystyle|f(r)|+r^{m}A(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{m}\left[\frac{a+r^{p}}{1+ar^{p}}+(1-a^{2})\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right].

Comparison of this expression with the right hand side of the inequality in (
2.5
) delivers the asserted sharpness.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For
p>0
, let

T(x)=\frac{1-x^{p}}{1-x}~\mbox{ for $x\in[0,1)$.}

Then

(i)

for each
p\in(0,1],
we have
T(x)\geq p
for all
x\in[0,1)
.

(ii)

for each
p>1,
we have
1=T(0)\leq T(x)\leq\lim_{x\to 1^{-}}T(x)=p
.

Proof.
For
y\in[0,1]
and
0<p\leq 1,
the Binomial theorem quickly gives
(1-y)^{p}\leq 1-py
so that (i) follows if we replace
y
by
1-x.
This fact and the second part also follow from the fact that

f(y)=(1-y)^{p}-(1-py)

is decreasing function of
y,
when
0<p\leq 1
and is an increasing function of
y,
when
p>1.

∎

3.
Bohr type inequalities of analytic functions
f
with
{\operatorname{Re}\,}f(z)<1

We first present Bohr-type radius for
f\in\mathcal{P}
instead of
f\in\mathcal{B}
and cover a more general Bohr-type sum.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{P}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
with
a_{0}\in[0,1)
. Then we have the following:

(i)

If
p\in(0,1]
and
\{\zeta_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies

(3.1)

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r)\geq\frac{2}{p}\Phi_{1}(r)

for
r\in[0,R_{1}]
, where
\Phi_{1}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(r)
and
R_{1}
is the minimal positive root of the equation

(3.2)

\zeta_{0}(r)=\frac{2}{p}\Phi_{1}(r)

then the following sharp inequality holds:

(3.3)

B_{f}(\zeta,p,r):=a_{0}^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\zeta_{n}(r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)\ \mbox{for all}\ r\leq R_{1}.

In the case when
\zeta_{0}(r)<\frac{2}{p}\Phi_{1}(r)
in some interval
(R_{1},R_{1}+\varepsilon)
, the number
R_{1}
cannot be improved.

(ii)

If
p>1
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
and

\zeta_{0}(r)\geq\frac{2}{1+a_{0}+\cdots+a_{0}^{p-1}}\Phi_{1}(r)

for
r\in[0,R_{p}]
, where
R_{p}
is the minimal positive root of the equation

\zeta_{0}(r)=\frac{2}{1+a_{0}+\cdots+a_{0}^{p-1}}\Phi_{1}(r),

then we have the sharp inequality

B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)\quad for\quad all\quad r\leq R_{p}.

In particular, when
p=2
,
B_{f}(\zeta,2,r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)
for all
r\leq R_{2}
, where
R_{2}
is the root of
\zeta_{0}(r)=\frac{2}{1+a_{0}}\Phi_{1}(r)

Proof.
(i) As
f\in\mathcal{P},
we have
{\operatorname{Re}\,}(1-f(z))>0
in
\mathbb{D}
and so by Lemma F, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 2(1-a_{0})
for all
n\geq 1
and thus, we see that

(3.4)

\displaystyle B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{0}^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}(r)=a_{0}^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\Phi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\Bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\frac{1-a_{0}^{p}}{2(1-a_{0})}\zeta_{0}(r)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\Bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\frac{p}{2}\zeta_{0}(r)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r),\ \mbox{by Lemma \ref{HLP-lem6}(i) and \eqref{thm1-eqn1}, }

for all
r\leq R_{1}
. This proves the inequality (
3.3
).

Now, let us prove that
R_{1}
is an optimal number. For the function
f(z)=\psi_{a}(z)
, where
\psi_{a}
is defined by
(
1.2
), we easily see that

(3.5)

B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)=\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a)\Bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\left(\frac{1-a^{p}}{2(1-a)}\right)\zeta_{0}(r)\Bigg],

which is bigger than
\zeta_{0}(r)
if

\Phi_{1}(r)-\left(\frac{1-a^{p}}{2(1-a)}\right)\zeta_{0}(r)>0.

In fact, for
r>R_{1}
and
a
close to
1
, we see that

\lim_{a\rightarrow 1^{-}}\bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\left(\frac{1-a^{p}}{2(1-a)}\right)\zeta_{0}(r)\bigg]=\Phi_{1}(r)-\frac{p}{2}\zeta_{0}(r)\geq 0,

showing that the number
R_{1}
is best possible.

(ii) When
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, we easily have by (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\frac{1+a_{0}+\cdots+a_{0}^{p-1}}{2}\zeta_{0}(r)\bigg]

so that
B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)
, only when
\zeta_{0}(r)\geq\frac{2}{1+a_{0}+\cdots+a_{0}^{p-1}}\Phi_{1}(r)
.

To prove that
R_{p}
is an optimal number, we consider the function
f(z)=\psi_{a}(z)
, where
\psi_{a}
is defined by
(
1.2
). For this function, by (
3.5
) we get that

B_{f}(\zeta,p,r)=\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a)\bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)-\left(\frac{1+a+\cdots+a^{p-1}}{2}\right)\zeta_{0}(r)\bigg],

and the sharpness follows as with the previous arguments.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{P}

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

a_{0}\in[0,1)

,

,

\{\zeta_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}

Then we have the following:

(i)

If
p\in(0,1]
and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}(r)\geq\frac{2}{p}\bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)+2^{q-1}\frac{r^{mq}}{(1-r^{m})^{q}}\bigg]

(3.7)

\zeta_{0}(r)=\frac{2}{p}\bigg[\Phi_{1}(r)+2^{q-1}\frac{r^{mq}}{(1-r^{m})^{q}}\bigg],

(3.8)

E_{f}(\zeta,p,r)\leq\zeta_{0}(r)\ \mbox{for all}\ r\leq R_{m,q}^{p}.

When
p=1

and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, when
q=1

.

(ii)

If

\zeta_{0}(r)=\psi(r)

Proof.
(i) Let
0<p\leq 1.
Then the triangle inequality and assumption that

gives

and thus, Lemma F yields that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{0}^{p}\zeta_{0}(r)+2(1-a_{0})\Phi_{1}(r)+2^{q}(1-a_{0})^{q}\frac{r^{mq}}{(1-r^{m})^{q}}

3

As

and

and thus,

Theorem 4

Theorem 5
.

Assume that
\zeta^{*}=\{\zeta^{*}_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
belonging to
\mathcal{F}
is a decreasing sequence of nonnegative functions,
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=z^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{np+m}(z^{p})^{n}
, where
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq m\leq p
. For convenience, we let

A^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r),\quad B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\tau_{np+m}}|a_{np+m}|\zeta^{*}_{n}(r^{p}),

C^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\bigg[B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)+(-1)^{\tau_{(2n-1)p+m}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2k+1}(r^{p})\bigg],~\mbox{ and }

D^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\bigg[B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)+(-1)^{\tau_{np+m}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\left(\frac{\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})}{1+|a_{m}|}+\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{k}(r^{p})\right)\bigg].

We have the following:

(I)

If the degree
\tau_{2np+m}
of
\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
is even (resp. odd) and the degree
\tau_{(2n-1)p+m}
of
\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
is odd (resp. even), then the sharp inequality

(4.5)

\bigg|C^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)\bigg|\leq 1~\mbox{ for $|z|=r\leq r_{*}$}

holds, where
r_{*}
is the root of the equation
\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})=1
.

(II)

If the degrees of
\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
and
\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
are both even
(resp. both odd), then the sharp inequality

(4.6)

\bigg|D^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)\bigg|\leq 1~\mbox{ for $|z|=r\leq R_{*}$}

holds, where
R_{*}
is the root of the equation
\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{n}(r^{p})=1
.

Proof.
(I) Assume first that the degree of
\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
is even and the degree of
\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)
is odd, both for all
n\geq 1
. Then, we may write

A^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2np+m}|\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{(2n-1)p+m}|\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})\bigg].

Because all
\zeta_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}~(n\geq 0)
, by Lemma
4
(ii) and the definition of
B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r),
we have

\displaystyle B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2k+1}(r^{p})

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{(2n-1)p+m}|\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2k+1}(r^{p})

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p}).

Again by Lemma
4
(i), since
\{\zeta^{*}_{n}(r)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence, we have
\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})\leq\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})
for all

and thus,

\displaystyle B^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2k+1}(r^{p})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2np+m}|\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})

\displaystyle\leq(1-|a_{m}|^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\bigg(\frac{\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})}{1+|a_{m}|}+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2(k+1)}(r^{p})\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p}).

which by, combining with the inequality (

) gives

\displaystyle\bigg|C^{*}_{f_{m}}(r)\bigg|\leq\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})=h_{1}(r)+1,

which is less than or equal to
1
, provided
h_{1}(r)\leq 0
, where

h_{1}(r):=\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})-1.

Note that

and

for

Since the degree of
\zeta^{*}_{2n-1}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)

According to the intermediate value theorem, we deduce that

for

.

which is bigger than 1 for

Next we may suppose that the degree

(II) Assume that the degrees of

(resp. both odd), we have

(4.9)

\bigg|A^{*}_{f}(r)+(-1)^{\tau_{np+m}}\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}\bigg(\frac{\zeta^{*}_{2n}(r^{p})}{1+|a_{m}|}+\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{k}(r^{p})\bigg)\bigg|\leq 1

holds for
|z|=r\leq\tilde{r}
, which is the root of
\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{n}(r^{p})[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta^{*}_{n}(r^{p})\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)-1]=0
. This result is sharp for
f(z)=z^{m}\frac{z^{p}-a}{1-az^{p}}
with
0<a<1
.

Proof.
With the help of Corollary
4
, the proof is similar as in
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

Example 2
.

Let
\zeta^{*}_{n}(r^{p})=r^{np}~(n\geq 0)
and
\zeta^{*}_{m}(r)=r^{m}
in Corollary
5
. Then we obtain the
following two examples, which can be regarded as the promotion of
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, Lemma 2.2]
.

(i)

Set
m=0
and
p=2
, so that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n}z^{2n}\in\mathcal{B}
. By Corollary
5
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{2n}|r^{2n}+\bigg(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\bigg)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2n}|^{2}r^{4n}\leq 1~\mbox{ for $\displaystyle|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.}

This result is sharp for
f(z)=\frac{z^{2}-a}{1-az^{2}}
with
0<a<1
.

(ii)

Set
m=1
and
p=2
, so
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n+1}z^{2n+1}\in\mathcal{B}
. By Corollary
5
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{2n+1}|r^{2n+1}+\bigg(\frac{r}{1+|a_{1}|}+\frac{r^{3}}{1-r^{2}}\bigg)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2n+1}|^{2}r^{4n}\leq 1~\mbox{$|z|=r\leq\tilde{r_{2}}$,}

where
\tilde{r_{2}}\approx 0.731348
is the root of
5r^{4}+4r^{3}-2r^{2}-4r+1=0
. This result is sharp for
f(z)=z\frac{z^{2}-a}{1-az^{2}}
with
0<a<1
.

Finally, we present an improved version of Theorem E. In this case, we only need to discuss the case of
\zeta_{n}(r)=r^{n}
. For convenience, let

E_{f_{m}}(r)=r^{m}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{np+m}|a_{np+m}|r^{np}+(-1)^{m}\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{m}|}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}\bigg].

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=z^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{np+m}z^{np}
, where
0\leq m\leq p
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
is even and
p\in\mathbb{N}
is odd. Then

(4.10)

\left|\ |f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)\ \right|\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq r_{p,m}
, where
r_{p,m}
is the minimal positive root of
r^{3p-m}-2r^{3p}-3r^{2p}-r^{p-m}+1=0
, and
E_{f_{m}}(r)
is defined as above.

Proof.
By assumption, each
2np+m
(
n\geq 0
) is even and each
(2n-1)p+m
(
n\geq 1
) is odd, as
m
is even and
p
is odd. Thus, we write

A_{f_{m}}(r)=r^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{np+m}|a_{np+m}|r^{np}=r^{m}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2np+m}|r^{2np}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{(2n-1)p+m}|r^{(2n-1)p}\bigg].

Next by Lemma
4
(i) with
\zeta_{2n}(r^{p})=r^{2np}(n\geq 0)
and
m
is even, then

\displaystyle E_{f_{m}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{m}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{np+m}|a_{np+m}|r^{np}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{m}|}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{m}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{2np+m}|r^{2np}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{m}|}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|a_{m}|^{2})\frac{r^{2p+m}}{1-r^{2p}},

so that

(4.11)

|f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)\leq r^{m}\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|}+(1-|a_{m}|^{2})\frac{r^{2p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}.

To find a lower bound for
|f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)
, by Lemma
4
(ii) with
\zeta_{2n-1}(r^{p})=r^{(2n-1)p}~(n\geq 1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-r^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{(2n-1)p+m}|r^{(2n-1)p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}+\frac{r^{p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}(1-|a_{m}|^{2})=-\frac{r^{2p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}(1-|a_{m}|^{2})-r^{m}\cdot\frac{r^{p}(1-|a_{m}|^{2})}{1+r^{p}},

where the third inequality above, we have used the identity (for
x=r^{p}
)

\frac{1}{1-x^{2}}=\frac{x}{1-x^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+x}\ \mbox{for $x\in[0,1)$},

and because

\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|}-\frac{r^{p}(1-|a_{m}|^{2})}{1+r^{p}}=\frac{(1-|a_{m}|+|a_{m}|^{3})r^{2p}+|a_{m}|[(1+|a_{m}|)r^{p}+1]}{(1+r^{p}|a_{m}|)(1+r^{p})}\geq 0,

we get that

(4.12)

|f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)\geq-\frac{r^{2p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}(1-|a_{m}|^{2})-r^{m}\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|},

and thus, combining (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) gives

(4.13)

\left||f(z)|+E_{f_{m}}(r)\right|\leq r^{m}\frac{r^{p}+|a_{m}|}{1+r^{p}|a_{m}|}+(1-|a_{m}|^{2})\frac{r^{2p+m}}{1-r^{2p}}.

According to Lemma
2
, we find that the right hand side of (
4.13
) is smaller than or equal to
1
for
|z|=r\leq r_{p,m}
, where
r_{p,m}
is as in the statement. This completes the proof of inequality (
4.10
).
∎

Corollary 6
.

According to Theorem
6
, we can present an improved version of Theorem E. Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

\left||f(z)|+A_{f_{0}}(r)+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\right|\leq 1

holds for
|z|=r\leq\sqrt{2}-1.

It is worth pointing out that we have not proved that the number
r=\sqrt{2}-1
is the best possible in Theorem
6
and therefore,
the following problem remains open.

Problem 2
.

Find the largest radius
r_{0}
for the class
\mathcal{B}
of analytic functions
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\bigg||f(z)|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right)\|f_{0}\|_{r}^{2}\bigg|\leq 1~~\mbox{ for }~~|z|=r\leq r_{0}.

Corollary 7
.

Suppose that
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{np+m}z^{np+m}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is even and
p\in\mathbb{N}
is odd. Then

\left|\frac{r^{m+p}}{1+r^{p}}+A_{f_{m}}(r)+r^{m}\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{m}|}+\frac{r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{np+m}|^{2}r^{2np}\right|\leq 1

for
|z|=r\leq R_{p,m}
, where
R_{p,m}
is the root of
r^{2p}+r^{p+m}-1=0
. The result is sharp for
f(z)=z^{2p+m}
.

Proof.
Although Theorem
6
cannot provide an accurate result, the result becomes accurate if we replace the term
|f(z)|
by
\frac{r^{m+p}}{1+r^{p}}
. The method of proof of Corollary
7
is exactly the same as Theorem
6
, so it will not be repeated. Note that condition
\frac{r^{m+p}}{1+r^{p}}
in Corollary
7
is necessary, otherwise the scaling of the lower bound will not hold.
∎

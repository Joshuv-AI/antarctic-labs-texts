Abstract
The design of cell-free massive MIMO systems requires accurate, measurement-based channel models. This paper provides the first results from the by far most extensive outdoor measurement campaign for cell-free massive MIMO channels in an urban environment. We measured impulse responses between over 20,000 20,000 potential access point locations and 80 80 user equipments at 3.5 3.5 GHz with 350 350 MHz bandwidth. Measurements use a “virtual array” approach at the access point and a hybrid switched/virtual approach at the user equipment. This paper describes the sounder design, measurement environment, data processing, and sample results, particularly the evolution of the power-delay profiles as a function of the access point locations, and its relation to the propagation environment.

Sounder Setup

The basic principle of the sounder combines a virtual array at the access point with a hybrid switched/virtual array at the user equipment. Specifically, the transmitter is placed on a cherry picker that is moved along a trajectory covering the potential access point locations. V
Conclusions

We have conducted the to-date most extensive outdoor cell-free massive MIMO channel sounding measurement campaign with over
20,000
access point locations and
80
user equipment locations, using a combination of virtual array and switched-array technique. We presented the evolution of the clusters as a function of changing access points locations. The sample results show the validity of our measurement data, by relating the dominant MPCs to the surrounding environment geometry. The sample results further show the various important propagation processes, including line-of-sight, over the rooftop propagation, building reflections, and vegetation attenuation. The results presented here, and the statistics from the campaign that will be presented in
[
17
]
, provide important insights into the impact of environments and deployment density on the achievable performance of CF-mMIMO systems.

Collet–Eckmann maps in the unicritical family
Magnus Aspenberg, Mats Bylund, Weiwei Cui

Abstract
In this paper we study perturbations of complex unicritical polynomials satisfying the Collet–Eckmann condition. We show that Collet–Eckmann parameters are Lebesgue density points of the complement of the Mandelbrot set (i.e. the connectedness locus).



1
Introduction

We consider the unicritical family

f_{c}(z)=z^{d}+c,

where
d\geq 2
is an integer and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

A non-hyperbolic unicritical map
f_{c}
satisfies the
Collet–Eckmann
condition (CE) if there exist
C>0
and
\gamma>0
such that

|Df_{c}^{n}(c)|\geq Ce^{\gamma n}~\,~\text{for all}~\,n>0.

(1.1)

Recall that a map
f
in this family is hyperbolic if
\omega(0)\cap{\mathcal{J}}(f)=\emptyset
, where
{\mathcal{J}}(f)
is the Julia set of
f
. For
d\geq 2
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is defined as the set of parameters
c
such that the Julia set
{\mathcal{J}}(f_{c})
of
f_{c}
is connected (or equivalently, the critical orbit
\{f^{n}_{c}(0)\}
is bounded). When
d=2
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{2}={\mathcal{M}}
is the well known
Mandelbrot set
(but we also call
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
a Mandelbrot set).

Our paper is about perturbations of CE-maps in the unicritical family. It has a quite long history. In
[
24
]
, J. Rivera-Letelier proved that Misiurewicz maps (i.e. critically non-recurrent maps) are Lebesgue density points of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. It was generalised by the first author in
[
1
]
to rational maps. However, even if Misiurewicz maps behave quite nicely under perturbations, they are rare in the parameter space (
[
2
]
).

In
[
6
]
, A. Avila, M. Lyubich and W. Shen proved that the Lebesgue density of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
at at most finitely renormalizable maps in the unicritical family, is strictly less than one (Theorem 1.4 and Remark 6.1 in
[
6
]
). Since Collet–Eckmann maps are at most finitely renormalizable, the result applies to them and consequently CE-maps cannot be density points of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
.

In a series of quite recent papers
[
12
,
15
,
16
]
, J. Graczyk and G. Świa̧tek made careful estimates of the geometry of the Julia set around typical Collet–Eckmann maps with respect to harmonic measure on
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. These maps are typically critically slowly recurrent, i.e. not Misiurewicz maps, but the speed of recurrence is slow (see
[
14
]
). Similar to the Misiurewicz case (which is easier), the goal is to get some kind of similarity between phase and parameter space. One cannot expect a complete (conformal) similarity between them since the Mandelbrot set contains an interior and the Julia set for a CE-map is a dendrite. However, at small scales this similarity becomes asymptotically conformal. The result is obtained by introducing a powerful tool called the similarity map, which transfers the geometry around the critical value
c
between the Julia set and the Mandelbrot set
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
in an intriguing way. In particular it follows that typical Collet–Eckmann maps, with respect to harmonic measure, are density points of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. The slow recurrence condition is also imposed on the Collet–Eckmann maps in
[
4
]
, where we proved a corresponding result for rational maps. We also mention an unpublished work by M. Benedicks and J. Graczyk, where they prove corresponding results for one-dimensional families (with some regularity) around typical CE-maps with respect to harmonic measure in
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
. For more general CE-maps, the dynamical constants can be drastically different. This yields quite wild dynamics, and crucial parts of the traditional methods break down.

In
[
13
]
it is shown that at most finitely renormalizable maps are weak density points of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
(again, this applies to CE-maps). The combinatorics for such maps seem to allow for the existence of deep central returns in the puzzle of the induced map at infinitely many small scales. Seemingly, this implies that this weak density cannot be replaced by strong density. We claim that this is not the case. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

In the unicritical family, Collet–Eckmann maps are Lebesgue density points of
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
.

Remark 1.2
.

One should compare the above theorem with a corresponding result in the real setting. M. Tsujii proved that (slowly recurrent) CE-maps with all periodic cycles repelling are Lebesgue density points of CE-maps
[
25
]
in families of real polynomials, including the unicritical family, restricted to
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}\cap{\mathbb{R}}
; see also
[
10
]
.

It will be quite clear from the proof that the same methods used in this paper can be applied to get the same conclusion as in Theorem
1.1
for rational CE-maps with precisely one critical point in the Julia set.

Recall that a rational map satisfies the Collet–Eckmann condition if the map has no parabolic cycles and (
1.1
) is satisfied for each critical point in the Julia set (usually one requires the map to be non-hyperbolic). Let
\mathcal{R}_{d}
be the space of rational maps of degree
d\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Any rational Collet–Eckmann map of degree
d
at least two with only simple critical points and with precisely one critical point in the Julia set is a Lebesgue density point of hyperbolic maps in
\mathcal{R}_{d}
.

This also partially improves our previous result in
[
4
]
where we imposed the additional assumption of slow recurrence of the critical points in the Julia set.

Remark 1.4
.

In case that rational maps have critical points of higher multiplicities, one can also get a corresponding result but in a subspace of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
. This subspace was defined by G. Levin
[
19
]
.

Ideas of the proof.

We trace the critical orbits for all parameters close to a Collet–Eckmann map and use the parameter exclusion technique developed by M. Benedicks and L. Carleson in the seminal works
[
7
,
8
]
. However, since we start with arbitrary Collet–Eckmann maps, the dynamical constants, e.g. the recurrence rate, can be quite wild in general (deep central returns) and traditional methods break down. This difficulty will be overcome by introducing the idea of
promotion
. Roughly speaking, promotion means that almost all parameters will reach some kind of slow recurrence condition.

2
Preliminaries

We collect some preliminary notions and notations in this section. For some general notions such as the Fatou and Julia sets, and other related notions in complex dynamics, we refer to
[
9
,
22
]
. We will always use the Euclidean metric in this paper and thus distances and diameters are measured with respect to this metric. The notation
\operatorname{dist}(A,B)
means the Hausdorff distance between two sets
A
and
B
, and
\operatorname{diam}(A)
denotes the diameter of
A
. Also,
m(A)
is the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure (i.e., area) of the set
A
.

Throughout the paper we fix
d\geq 2
. Let
f_{c_{0}}
be a Collet–Eckmann unicritical polynomial of degree
d
. The only critical point is
0
with the corresponding critical value being
c_{0}
. Let
{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{Q}}(c_{0},\varepsilon)
be a square of side length
\varepsilon
in the parameter plane centred at the parameter
c_{0}
with
\varepsilon>0
to be sufficiently small (cf. Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.8
). For a parameter
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
the corresponding map is denoted, as usual, by
f_{c}
. We study the evolution of the critical orbit for nearby parameters. Let

\xi_{n}(c)=f_{c}^{n}(0)\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0.

We will need to fix some neighbourhood for the critical point and study recurrence of
\xi_{n}(c)
to this neighbourhood for
c
close to
c_{0}
(cf. Lemma
2.8
). Let
0<\Delta^{\prime}<\Delta
be large integers to be determined later and put

U=D(0,e^{-\Delta})\quad\text{and}\quad U^{\prime}=D(0,e^{-\Delta^{\prime}}).

(2.1)

In the following, we also put
\delta=e^{-\Delta}
and
\delta^{\prime}=e^{-\Delta^{\prime}}
.

Starting with a Collet–Eckmann map, we have exponential growth of derivative along the critical orbit. This expansion will be inherited by nearby parameters with possibly slightly slower growth at the initial piece of their critical orbits. This is ensured by the transversality at Collet–Eckmann parameters (see Lemma
2.9
). To gain growth for later orbits, we divide the orbit into pieces:
bound periods
and
free periods
. During the bound periods, the orbits shadow the initial iterates of its critical orbit and expansion is ensured (see Section 3.1). During free periods, the orbits stay away from the critical point and hence gain exponential growth of derivatives (Lemma
2.8
) similar as the classical Mañé theorem (Lemma
2.7
).

In the rest of this section we present necessary notions which are crucial in our later analysis and also some other related results concerning recurrence rates of Collet–Eckmann maps, expansions during bound periods and finally the transversality result.

2.1
Recurrence, bound periods and partitions

With the critical neighbourhoods defined in (
2.1
), we say that
\xi_{n}(c)
is a
return
if
\xi_{n}(c)\in U^{\prime}
. It is a
pseudo-return
if
\xi_{n}(c)\in U^{\prime}\setminus U
. Sometimes we specify and say that, for instance,
\xi_{n}(c)
is a return into
U
. If
\xi_{n}(c)
is a return, the following orbit will be shadowed by its early piece of the critical orbit of
f_{c}
until they separate in the following sense. The number
n
in this case is called a return time.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\beta>0
. Let
\xi_{n}(c)
be a return to
U^{\prime}
. The bound period for this return is defined as the indices
j>0
such that

|\xi_{n+j}(c)-\xi_{j}(c)|\leq e^{-\beta j}|\xi_{j}(c)|.

The largest number
p
with the above inequality is called the length of the bound period (for this return).

The number
\beta>0
should be thought of as a comparatively small number. There will be some conditions imposed on
\beta
, e.g. in Section
5.1
.

After the bound period ends, the two critical orbits will then separate and stay away from critical points until the next return happens. A return might be deep in the sense that
\xi_{n}(c)
is quite close to
0
. To achieve our goal, we will need to disregard parameters with very deep returns. For later purpose we will define
\alpha_{n}(c)
for a parameter
c
as follows:

|\xi_{n}(c)|=e^{-\alpha_{n}(c)n}\quad\text{for all}\quad n.

It is clear that if one is considering a Misiurewicz or semi-hyperbolic parameter, then
|\xi_{n}(c)|
has a uniform lower bound. For general Collet–Eckmann parameters, this need not be true. The map
f_{c}
is (critically)
slowly recurrent
(see e.g.
[
21
]
) if

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}(c)=0.

Recall that the lower and upper Lyapunov exponents at the critical value
c
are defined respectively as

\underline{\gamma}(c):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df_{c}^{n}(c)|\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\gamma}(c):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df_{c}^{n}(c)|

The Collet–Eckmann condition simply means that
\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})>0
and that
f
is not hyperbolic. Let
\gamma_{n}(c)
be defined such that

|Df_{c}^{n}(c)|=e^{\gamma_{n}(c)n}\quad\text{for all~}~n,

where
\gamma_{n}(c)
depends on the parameter
c
. Note also that
|Df_{c}^{n}(z)|
grows at most exponentially in
n
for any
c
and any
z
. So
\overline{\gamma}(c)
indeed has an upper bound. Both
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j}(c)
and
\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{j}(c)
depend on the parameter, but we usually suppress it.
Suppose that the starting map
f=f_{c_{0}}
satisfies, for some
C_{0}>0
and
\gamma>0
,

|Df_{c_{0}}^{n}(c_{0})|\geq C_{0}e^{\gamma n}\quad\text{ for all ~}\,n>0.

In other words,
0<\gamma\leq\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})
. We now make the following definition. Let
\tilde{N}>0
be a number which satisfies
\gamma_{k}(c_{0})=\frac{1}{k}\log|Df_{c_{0}}^{k}(c_{0})|\geq(9/10)\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})>0
, for all
k\geq\tilde{N}
, and that
\gamma_{\tilde{N}}(c)=\frac{1}{\tilde{N}}\log|Df_{c}^{\tilde{N}}(c)|>(9/10)\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})
for all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Moreover, we also require that all bound periods for return times
n
considered in the paper are larger than
\tilde{N}
(so
n
is typically much larger than
\tilde{N}
). This can be achieved by choosing
\delta^{\prime}
small enough (see the Definition
2.1
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
. We say that
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\gamma)
if
|Df_{c}^{k}(c)|\geq e^{\gamma k}
for all
\tilde{N}\leq k\leq n
.

Note that we drop the constant
C_{0}
in front, which appears in the starting function
f_{c_{0}}
. So in principle,
c_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})-\varepsilon)
, for very small
\varepsilon>0
.
In our analysis later, we will specify the constant
\gamma
that suits our needs.

Using the chain rule

\displaystyle\left|Df_{c}^{n}(c)\right|=\left|Df_{c}^{n-1}(c)\right|\,\left|Df_{c}(f_{c}^{n-1}(c))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|Df_{c}^{n-1}(c)\right|\left|Df_{c}(\xi_{n}(c))\right|

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\left|Df_{c}^{n-1}(c)\right|d|\xi_{n}(c)|^{d-1},

we get that

e^{\gamma_{n}n}=e^{\gamma_{n-1}(n-1)}\,d\,e^{-\alpha_{n}n(d-1)}.

(2.3)

The following simple inequalities, for
n
large enough,

\gamma_{n}\geq\gamma_{n-1}-(d-1)\alpha_{n}-\frac{C}{n}

(2.4)

and

\gamma_{n-1}\geq\gamma_{n}+(d-1)\alpha_{n}-\frac{C}{n}

(2.5)

will be used throughout the paper. Here
C
can be chosen to be
\log d
.

Our first observation is that one still has some control over the recurrence of the critical point for a Collet–Eckmann map.

Lemma 2.3

(Initial recurrence rate)
.

Let
f_{c}
be a Collet–Eckmann map. Then there exist
K>0
and
\alpha>0
such that

\left|\xi_{n}(c)\right|\geq Ke^{-\alpha n}~\,~\text{for all}~\,n\geq 0.

Proof.

From the definition of the upper and lower Lyapunov exponents, given any
\epsilon>0
we can find
N\geq 0
such that, with
\underline{\gamma}=\underline{\gamma}(c)
and
\overline{\gamma}=\overline{\gamma}(c)
,

e^{(\underline{\gamma}-\epsilon)n}\leq|Df_{c}^{n}(c)|\leq e^{(\overline{\gamma}+\epsilon)n}\quad(n\geq N).

By (
2.2
), we have

d|\xi_{n}(c)|^{d-1}=\frac{\left|Df_{c}^{n}(c)\right|}{\left|Df_{c}^{n-1}(c)\right|}\geq\frac{e^{(\underline{\gamma}-\epsilon)n}}{e^{(\overline{\gamma}+\epsilon)(n-1)}}=e^{\overline{\gamma}+\epsilon}e^{-(\overline{\gamma}-\underline{\gamma}+2\epsilon)n}\quad(n\geq N).

Therefore, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

|\xi_{n}(c)|\geq Ke^{-\alpha n}\quad(n\geq 1),

where
\alpha=(\overline{\gamma}-\underline{\gamma}+2\epsilon)/(d-1)
.
∎

By Lemma
2.3
, for a Collet–Eckmann map the numbers
\alpha_{n}
cannot tend to
\infty
, so the critical orbits cannot return too close to the critical point.

Remark 2.4
.

We notice from the above lemma that if the Lyapunov exponent exists, i.e. if
\underline{\gamma}=\overline{\gamma}
, then the map satisfies the so-called slow recurrence condition. In this situation Theorem
1.1
follows from
[
4
]
. (Compare also with
[
15
,
16
]
.) In what follows, we therefore assume that
\underline{\gamma}<\overline{\gamma}
.

Actually, we take the upper exponent
\overline{\gamma}
to be a trivial upper bound for all exponents in the cube
{\mathcal{Q}}
at all times; i.e.

\overline{\gamma}=\sup\limits_{|z|\leq 2,c\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\log|Df_{c}(z)|,

so that we have
\gamma_{n}(c)\leq\overline{\gamma}
, for all
n>0
and all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Also, in order to have some space to play with when we change the parameter, we define

\underline{\gamma}=\frac{9}{10}\underline{\gamma}(c_{0}).

The number
\underline{\gamma}
will stand as a kind of lower Lyapunov exponent, at least up to the start-up time
N
, described in Lemma
3.9
. Up until this time, we have
\alpha_{n}(c)<\overline{\gamma}-\underline{\gamma}
, (
\tilde{N}\leq n\leq N
), for
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Although a Collet–Eckmann map has controlled recurrence rates in the sense of Lemma
2.3
, nearby parameters may return very deep due to the expansion. As mentioned before, we will delete these parameters in our analysis. For this purpose we need the notion of partition elements.

Definition 2.5

(Partition elements)
.

Let
S>0
be given. A square
A\subset{\mathcal{Q}}
is called a partition element at time
n
if the following holds for all
k\leq n
:

\operatorname{diam}\xi_{k}(A)\leq\begin{cases}~\,~\dfrac{\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{k}(A),0)}{(\log\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{k}(A),0))^{2}}~&\text{if}\,~\xi_{k}(A)\cap U\neq\emptyset,\\
\quad~\,~S~&\text{if}\,~\xi_{k}(A)\cap U=\emptyset.\end{cases}

(2.6)

For clarification, in the rest of the paper we will write
A_{n}
for a partition element at time
n
. More specifically, we use
A_{n}(c)
for a partition element at time
n
that contains the parameter
c
. It is useful to have in mind that partitions are getting finer and finer as time proceeds; in other words,
A_{n+1}(c)\subset A_{n}(c)
for all
n
.

The term
S
is referred as
large scale
in this paper. It is always a good situation if a partition element reaches the large scale. However, this is not clear for all partition elements. Our main effort is to show that almost all partition elements will reach the large scale. Clearly, the obstruction to reach the large scale is caused by the frequent passages near the critical point.

For a partition element, we can also give a notion of returns. Let
A
be a partition element at time
k-1
. Then
\xi_{k}(A)
is called a
return
to
U
if
\xi_{k}(A)\cap U\neq\emptyset
and a
return
to
U^{\prime}
if
\xi_{k}(A)\cap U^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. It also may happen that
\xi_{k}(A)\cap U^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
but
\xi_{k}(A)\cap U=\emptyset
. In this case we say that
\xi_{k}(A)
is a
pseudo-return
. Now bound periods can also be defined similarly if a return occurs for a partition element.

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose that
A
is a partition element at time
n
. The bound period for
A
is the indices
j>0
such that for any
c_{1},c_{2}\in A
and for any
z\in\xi_{n}(A)
the following holds:

|f_{c_{1}}^{j}(z)-\xi_{j}(c_{2})|\leq e^{-\beta j}|\xi_{j}(c_{2})|.

The length of this bound period, denoted by
p
, is the largest number such that the above relation holds.

After bound periods end, the next return will be called a
free return
. At this point, there are two possibilities: essential and inessential returns. Let
A_{n-1}
be a partition element. A return
\xi_{n}(A_{n-1})
to
U
or
U^{\prime}
is
essential
if

\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n}(A_{n-1}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n-1}),0)}{(\log\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n-1}),0))^{2}}.

Otherwise the return is said to be
inessential
.

Suppose that
\xi_{n}(A_{n-1})
is an essential return to
U
. Then partition at time
n
is made from
A_{n-1}
as follows. If
A_{n-1}
is a partition element according to Definition
2.5
then we do not partition further, i.e. we put
A_{n}=A_{n-1}
. If not, we partition
A_{n-1}
into four squares of equal size. If all of them are partition elements, then we are done. If not, we continue with each square not being a partition element, and partition it into four new squares of equal size. Continuing in this way we obtain a partition of
A_{n-1}
into sub squares
A_{n}^{j}
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0)}{(\log\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0))^{2}}\leq\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}))\leq\dfrac{\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0)}{(\log\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0))^{2}}.

(2.7)

Thus each
A_{n}^{j}
is a partition element at time
n
.

Now we make another definition. After the partition is made as above, for each set
\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j})
put

\tilde{r}=-\log(\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0))
. Define
r=\lceil\tilde{r}-\frac{1}{2}\rceil
. Then we have

e^{-r-1/2}\leq\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0)<e^{-r+1/2}.

If this is the case we also write
\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n}(A_{n}^{j}),0)\sim e^{-r}
. In general we write
A\sim B
if there is a non-dynamical constant
C
such that

\frac{1}{C}A\leq B\leq CA.

2.2
Free periods

After the end of a bound period, we enter into a
free period
before the next return happens. During this time window, the orbit is away from critical points which gives growth of derivatives similarly as the classical Mañé theorem. The following is a Mañé type result which says that a Collet–Eckmann map is expanding away from critical points. A similar lemma was proved in
[
4
]
.

In the following, we state and prove the result for rational maps satisfying the so-called
exponential shrinking property
. A rational map
f
is said to satisfy this property if there exist
\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}>1
and
r>0
such that for every
z\in{\mathcal{J}}(f)
, each
n>0
and every component
W
of
f^{-n}(D(z,r))
one has
\operatorname{diam}W\leq\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}^{-n}
. This property is equivalent to the
topological Collet–Eckmann condition
and is, in general, weaker than the Collet–Eckmann condition; see
[
23
]
. For the setting considered in this paper, i.e. maps with exactly one critical point in the Julia set, these conditions are all equivalent. Let
\operatorname{Crit}(f)
be the set of critical points of
f
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f
be rational satisfying the exponential shrinking property. Then there exist a neighbourhood
U^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{Crit}(f)\cap{\mathcal{J}}(f)
,
C>0
and
\gamma>0
such that the following holds: Let
z\in{\mathcal{J}}(f)
and if
f^{j}(z)\not\in U^{\prime}
for
0\leq j\leq n-1
, then

|Df^{n}(z)|\geq Ce^{\gamma n}.

Proof.

Put
z_{n}=f^{n}(z)
and
z_{0}=z
.
Since
f
satisfies the exponential shrinking property, per definition one can find
\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}>1
and
r>0
such that preimage components shrink exponentially. Take any
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,r]
and put

W^{\prime}_{k}:=f^{-k}(D(z_{n},\delta^{\prime})),

the component which contains
z_{n-k}
. Thus one has

\operatorname{diam}W^{\prime}_{k}\leq\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}^{-k}

for all
k\leq n-1.
So there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\operatorname{diam}W^{\prime}_{k}\leq\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}^{-k}<2\delta^{\prime}

for all
k\geq\ell
. Since
f^{j}(z)\not\in U^{\prime}
for
j<n
which means that
|f^{j}(z)-c|>\delta^{\prime}
for all
j<n
and all
c\in\operatorname{Crit}(f)\cap{\mathcal{J}}(f)
, we see that

W^{\prime}_{k}\cap(\operatorname{Crit}(f)\cap{\mathcal{J}}(f))=\emptyset

for all
k\geq\ell
. This implies that there exists
0<\delta^{\prime\prime}:=\delta^{\prime\prime}(\delta^{\prime})<\delta^{\prime}
such that if
W^{\prime\prime}_{k}\subset W^{\prime}_{k}
is the component of
f^{-k}(D(z_{n},\delta^{\prime\prime}))
we have

f^{k}:W^{\prime\prime}_{k}\to D(z_{n},\delta^{\prime\prime})

is conformal and onto for all
k\leq n
. By the above choice of
\delta^{\prime\prime}
and the Koebe one-quarter theorem,

\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}^{-n}>\operatorname{diam}W^{\prime\prime}_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{\prime\prime}}{|Df^{n}(z_{0})|}.

This gives

|Df^{n}(z)|\geq\frac{\delta^{\prime\prime}}{2}\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}^{n}=:Ce^{\gamma n}.

Here
C=\delta^{\prime\prime}/2
and
\gamma=\log\lambda_{\operatorname{Exp}}
.

So the lemma holds with
U^{\prime}=\bigcup_{c\in\operatorname{Crit}(f)\cap{\mathcal{J}}(f)}D(c,\delta^{\prime})
.
∎

The following lemma follows from the above and is proved in
[
4
, Lemma 3.10]
. To state the result, let
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
be a neighbourhood of the Julia set
{\mathcal{J}}(f_{c_{0}})
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is chosen in a way such that
U^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
. That is,

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{0}}=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,\operatorname{dist}(z,{\mathcal{J}}(f_{c_{0}}))<\varepsilon_{0}\right\}.

(2.8)

Now we return to Collet–Eckmann maps in the unicritical family. Recall that
U,U^{\prime},\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are defined in (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f_{c_{0}}
be a Collet–Eckmann map. Then there exist
\Delta^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large,
C>0
and
\gamma_{H}>0
such that the following holds. For any
\Delta>\Delta^{\prime}
there exist
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and
C_{U}>0
(dependent on
U
) such that for all
n\geq 1
and for all
c\in\mathcal{Q}
, if
z,f_{c}(z),\dots f_{c}^{n-1}(z)\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{0}}\setminus U
, then

|Df_{c}^{n}(z)|\geq C_{U}e^{\gamma_{H}n}.

(2.9)

If, in addition,
f_{c}^{n}(z)\in U
, then

|Df_{c}^{n}(z)|\geq Ce^{\gamma_{H}n}.

(2.10)

2.3
Transversality

One of the ingredients used in the proof of the theorem is a transversality result at Collet–Eckmann parameters. For the unicritical family, this was proved by A. Avila
[
5
]
and G. Levin
[
18
]
. (For general rational Collet–Eckmann maps, this was due to G. Levin
[
20
]
.) This result says that for a Collet–Eckmann unicritical polynomial
f_{c_{0}}
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\xi^{\prime}_{n}(c_{0})}{Df^{n-1}_{c_{0}}(c_{0})}=L\,~(\neq 0,\,\infty).

A direct consequence of this is the following lemma proved in
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f_{c_{0}}
be a unicritical polynomial satisfying the Collet–Eckmann condition with
\gamma_{0}=\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})
. Then for any
q\in(0,1)
and any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0})
there exists
N_{L}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\left|\frac{\xi^{\prime}_{n}(c)}{Df^{n-1}_{c}(c)}-L\right|\leq q|L|

(2.11)

provided that
f_{c}
satisfies the Collet–Eckmann condition up to time
n\geq N_{L}
with exponent
\gamma
for all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

It follows from this lemma that, with some a priori specified
\gamma<\gamma_{0}
, all parameters from
{\mathcal{Q}}
will have exponential growth along the critical orbit up to time
N_{L}
with this exponent. Moreover, the function
\xi_{n}
has bounded distortion for all
n\leq N_{L}
.

2.4
The weak parameter dependence property

We state here the basic and crucial fact, that, given some lower exponent
\gamma_{Q}\in(0,\underline{\gamma})
, and
Q>1
(typically so that
\log Q
is much smaller than both
\underline{\gamma}
and
\gamma_{Q}
), there is
\varepsilon>0
(the size of the square
{\mathcal{Q}}
) such that, if the derivative
|Df_{c}^{k}(c)|\geq e^{\gamma_{Q}k}
for all
N_{L}\leq k\leq n
, then

Q^{-(n-k)}|Df_{c}^{n-k}(\xi_{k}(c))||\xi_{k}(a)-\xi_{k}(b)|\\
\leq|\xi_{n}(a)-\xi_{n}(b)|\\
\leq Q^{n-k}|Df_{c}^{n-k}(\xi_{k}(c))||\xi_{k}(a)-\xi_{k}(b)|,

(2.12)

for all
a,b,c
belonging to the same partition element
A_{n}\subset{\mathcal{Q}}
. For a detailed proof see Lemma 3.2 in
[
4
]
.
The point is that this result in connection to the Main Distortion Lemma
3.8
gives a strong version of (
2.12
) which we present after Lemma
3.8
.

3
Bound periods, main distortion and start-up

This section is devoted to proving distortion results. This relies on the expansion during bound periods which in turn depends on estimating the growth during this period (Lemma
3.3
). The expansion during bound periods depends on the length (Lemma
3.2
) and distortion estimates (Lemma
3.1
) of bound periods.

3.1
Distortion, length and expansion during bound periods

We first prove that we have good distortion during the bound period (see Definition
2.6
) and that, given a return at time
n
, this forces the bound period to never succeed
n
in length. With these in hand it will be clear that one has some definite expansion when the bound periods end.

Distortion during bound periods.

The following lemma gives distortion estimate in the bound periods.

Lemma 3.1

(Distortion during the bound period)
.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be given. Then if
\delta^{\prime}=e^{-\Delta^{\prime}}
is sufficiently small the following holds. Let
n
be the index of a return,
A
a partition element at time
n
, and let
p
be the associated bound period. Then, for all
c\in A
and all
z\in\xi_{n}(A)
,

\left|\frac{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(f_{c}(z))}{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(c)}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\qquad(j=1,2,\dots,p).

Proof.

By the chain rule and a standard estimate we have

\left|\frac{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(f_{c}(z))}{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(c)}-1\right|\leq\exp\left\{\sum_{\nu=0}^{j-1}\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))^{d-1}-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)^{d-1}|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|^{d-1}}\right\}-1.

Using the identity

z^{d-1}-w^{d-1}=(z-w)\sum_{k=0}^{d-2}z^{k}w^{d-2-k},

we find that

|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))^{d-1}-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)^{d-1}|\leq|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|\sum_{k=0}^{d-2}|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))|^{k}|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|^{d-2-k}.

During the bound period the following holds,

\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}\leq\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}+1\leq e^{-\beta j}+1<2,

and hence,

|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))^{d-1}-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)^{d-1}|\leq|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)||f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|^{d-2}(2^{d-1}-1).

We conclude that

\left|\frac{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(f_{c}(z))}{(f_{c}^{j})^{\prime}(c)}-1\right|\leq\exp\left\{(2^{d-1}-1)\sum_{\nu=0}^{j-1}\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}\right\}-1.

Suppose that
\operatorname{dist}(0,\xi_{n}(A))\sim e^{-r}
. Then for all
z\in\xi_{n}(A)
we have
|f_{c}(z)-c|\sim e^{-dr}
. We divide the sum into two parts
[0,j-1]=[0,J]\cup[J+1,j-1]
, where
J=\lceil r/(\alpha+\overline{\gamma})\rceil
and where
\alpha
is the upper bound of
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j}(c)
for
c\in A
, satisfying
\alpha<\overline{\gamma}-\underline{\gamma}
. Then the first sum becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{J}

\displaystyle\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}\leq C\sum_{\nu=1}^{J}\frac{|Df_{c}^{\nu}(c)||f_{c}(z)-c|}{e^{-\alpha\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\nu=0}^{J}e^{\overline{\gamma}\nu}|f_{c}(z)-c|e^{\alpha\nu}\leq C\sum_{\nu=1}^{J}e^{(\overline{\gamma}+\alpha)\nu-dr}\leq C^{\prime}e^{r-dr}=C^{\prime}e^{-(d-1)\Delta^{\prime}},

which can be made arbitrarily small if
\Delta^{\prime}
is large enough. For the estimate of the second sum we use the binding condition directly:

\sum_{\nu=J+1}^{j-1}\frac{|f_{c}^{\nu}(f_{c}(z))-f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}{|f_{c}^{\nu}(c)|}\leq\sum_{\nu=J+1}^{j-1}e^{-\beta\nu}\leq Ce^{-\beta(J+1)}\leq C\exp\left\{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\overline{\gamma}}\Delta^{\prime}\right\}.

Adding these two estimates, making
\delta^{\prime}
sufficiently small, completes the proof.
∎

Length of bound periods.

The bounded distortion above ensures that the bound period will never exceed its return time, as shown in the following result. Recall that
N_{L}
was defined in Section 2.3 and
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\gamma)
was defined in Definition
2.2
.

Lemma 3.2

(Length of the bound period)
.

Let
n\geq N_{L}
and
\xi_{n}(c)
be a return where
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\gamma)
for some
\gamma>0
. Suppose that
p
is the associated bound period for this return. Then
p<n
.

Proof.

Assume, on the contrary, that
p\geq n
. This means that
2n
belongs to the bound period of
\xi_{n}(c)
. By Definition
2.1
, we have

|\xi_{2n}(c)-\xi_{n}(c)|\leq e^{-\beta n}|\xi_{n}(c)|.

With
z=\xi_{n}(c)
in Lemma
3.1
, we have the following lower bound estimate for the left hand side of the above inequality:

\displaystyle|\xi_{2n}(c)-\xi_{n}(c)|

\displaystyle=|f_{c}^{n}(\xi_{n}(c))-f_{c}^{n}(0)|

\displaystyle=|f_{c}^{n-1}(\xi_{n+1}(c))-f_{c}^{n-1}(c)|

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime}|(f_{c}^{n-1})^{\prime}(c)||\xi_{n+1}(c)-c|

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}|(f_{c}^{n-1})^{\prime}(c)||\xi_{n}(c)|^{d},

where
C^{\prime}>0
is some constant which is arbitrarily close to
1
if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. So we have that

C^{\prime}e^{\gamma_{n-1}(n-1)-\alpha_{n}nd}\leq e^{-\beta n}e^{-\alpha_{n}n},

which means that

C^{\prime}e^{\gamma_{n-1}(n-1)-\alpha_{n}n(d-1)}\leq e^{-\beta n}.

By (
2.3
), one can see that

\frac{1}{d}C^{\prime}e^{\gamma_{n}n}\leq e^{-\beta n},

which is impossible since
\gamma_{n}\geq\gamma>0
and
n\geq N_{L}
and
N_{L}
can be taken sufficiently large by making
\varepsilon
small. This completes the proof.
∎

We will assume that
\tilde{N}\leq N_{L}
(in fact the difference should be very large).

Expansion during bound periods.

We have the following lemma and its corollary ensuring the derivative growth of the critical orbit during bound periods.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\xi_{n}(c)\in U^{\prime}
and
|\xi_{n}(c)|\sim e^{-r}
and let
p
be the associated bound period. Then for any fixed
\eta>0
the following hold if
\delta^{\prime}>0
is sufficiently small:

\frac{(1-\eta)dr}{\gamma_{p}+\alpha_{p+1}+\beta}\leq p\leq\frac{(1+\eta)dr}{\gamma_{p-1}+\alpha_{p}+\beta},

(3.1)

and

\left|Df_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))\right|\geq Ce^{(1-\eta)\frac{\gamma_{p}-(d-1)(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)}{\gamma_{p}+\alpha_{p+1}+\beta}r}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Since
p
is the bound period for the return
\xi_{n}(c)
, we have, by Definition
2.1
, that

\left|f_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))-\xi_{p+1}(c)\right|\geq e^{-\beta(p+1)}|\xi_{p+1}(c)|.

(3.3)

Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
there exists some constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

|Df_{c}^{p}(c)||\xi_{n+1}(c)-c|\geq C^{\prime}e^{-\beta(p+1)}|\xi_{p+1}(c)|,

which gives, since
|Df_{c}^{p}(c)|=e^{\gamma_{p}p}
and
|\xi_{p+1}(c)|=e^{-\alpha_{p+1}(p+1)}
,

e^{\gamma_{p}p}e^{-dr}\geq C^{\prime\prime}e^{-(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)(p+1)},

(3.4)

where
C^{\prime\prime}=C^{\prime}e^{-1/2}
. By taking
\delta>0
sufficiently small, so that
r
is large enough, (and thus
p
sufficiently large) we have the first inequality of (
3.1
). To see that the second inequality in (
3.1
) also holds, we use Definition
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
again at the time
p
. This gives similar estimate as above but with reversed inequalities. We leave details for interested readers.

To obtain (
3.2
), first we note that, by (
3.4
),

e^{-r}\geq C^{\prime\prime 1/d}e^{-\frac{1}{d}(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)(p+1)}e^{-\frac{1}{d}\gamma_{p}p},

which implies

e^{-(d-1)r}\geq C^{\prime\prime(d-1)/d}e^{-\frac{d-1}{d}(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)(p+1)-\frac{d-1}{d}\gamma_{p}p}.

(3.5)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|Df_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))\right|

\displaystyle=|Df_{c}^{p}(\xi_{n+1}(c))||Df_{c}(\xi_{n}(c))|

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime\prime}|Df_{c}^{p}(c)|e^{-(d-1)r}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime\prime\frac{2d-1}{d}}e^{\gamma_{p}p-\frac{d-1}{d}(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)(p+1)-\frac{d-1}{d}\gamma_{p}p}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime\prime 2}e^{-2\overline{\gamma}}e^{\frac{1}{d}(\gamma_{p}-(d-1)(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta))p}.

(3.6)

Combining (
3.6
) with the first inequality of (
3.1
), we get

|Df_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))|\geq Ce^{(1-\eta)\frac{\gamma_{p}-(d-1)(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)}{\gamma_{p}+\alpha_{p+1}+\beta}r},

where
C>0
is a (non-dynamical) constant.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

By the definition of the bound period, one can see that
p
cannot be a return time. This means, for example, that
\alpha_{p}\approx 0
.

Notice that (
2.5
) gives us

\gamma_{k}\geq\gamma_{k+1}+(d-1)\alpha_{k+1}-\frac{C}{k}.

If we assume that
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\gamma)
then, since
p<n
by Lemma
3.2
,

\gamma_{p}\geq\gamma+(d-1)\alpha_{p+1}-\frac{C}{p+1}.

Therefore, assuming
\beta<\gamma/(4d)
,

\gamma_{p}-(d-1)(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)\geq\gamma-\beta(d-1)-\frac{C}{p+1}\geq\frac{3\gamma}{4},

(3.7)

provided
p
is large enough (which is the case if
\delta^{\prime}
is small enough). Now we assume that
\gamma\geq\underline{\gamma}
, and
\alpha_{p+1}<\overline{\gamma}-\underline{\gamma}\leq\overline{\gamma}
(see Remark
2.4
).
Choosing
\eta
sufficiently small in Lemma
3.3
we get

(1-\eta)\frac{\gamma_{p}-(d-1)(\alpha_{p+1}+\beta)}{\gamma_{p}+\alpha_{p+1}+\beta}\geq(1-\eta)\frac{3\gamma}{\left(8+\frac{1}{d}\right)\overline{\gamma}}\geq\frac{\underline{\gamma}}{3\overline{\gamma}}.

We can get rid of the constant in front of (
3.2
), such that

|Df_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))|\geq e^{\frac{\underline{\gamma}}{4\overline{\gamma}}r},

given that
\delta^{\prime}
is sufficiently small.

Now, put

\kappa=1-\frac{\underline{\gamma}}{4\overline{\gamma}}.

We arrive at the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose that
\xi_{n}(c)\in U^{\prime}
,
|\xi_{n}(c)|\sim e^{-r}
and
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\gamma)
for some
\gamma\geq\underline{\gamma}
. Moreover, suppose that
\beta<\gamma/(4d)
. Then

|Df_{c}^{p+1}(\xi_{n}(c))|\geq e^{(1-\kappa)r}.

We finish this section with the following two useful lemmas.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
\xi_{n_{1}}(c)
and
\xi_{n_{2}}(c)
are two returns,
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, and that
|\xi_{n_{1}}(c)|\sim e^{-r_{1}}
and

|\xi_{n_{2}}(c)|\sim e^{-r_{2}}
, where
r_{1}\leq r_{2}-1
. Then the corresponding bound periods
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
satisfy
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
.

Proof.

By bounded distortion, Lemma
3.1
(note that we can choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}
as small as we like, therefore
C
below is very close to
1
), we have, for
j\leq\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
,

|\xi_{n_{1}+j}(c)-\xi_{j}(c)|\geq Ce^{-2r_{1}}|Df_{c}^{j}(c)|\geq Ce^{2}e^{-2r_{2}}|Df_{c}^{j}(c)|\geq|\xi_{n_{2}+j}(c)-\xi_{j}(c)|.

So the bound period for the second return will be longer than (or at least as long as) the first one.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
\xi_{n_{1}}(A)
and
\xi_{n_{2}}(A)
are two consecutive returns,
n_{2}>n_{1}
, where
A
is a partition element at time
n_{2}
and that
A\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{n_{1}}(\gamma)
for some
\gamma>0
, and
\beta<\gamma/(4d)
. Then
|Df_{c}^{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\xi_{n_{1}}(c))|\geq e^{\gamma_{2}(n_{2}-n_{1})},
for some
\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma/(3d)
and for
c\in A
.
In particular,

\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n_{2}}(A))\geq 2\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n_{1}}(A)).

Moreover,
|Df_{c}^{n_{2}}(c)|\geq e^{(\gamma/3)n_{2}}
if
\gamma\leq\gamma_{H}
.

Proof.

We use Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
2.8
and equations (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) to conclude, with
p_{1}
the bound period after
n_{1}
, and
\ell_{1}
the following free period,

|Df_{c}^{p_{1}+1}(\xi_{n_{1}}(c))|\geq e^{\frac{3\gamma_{p_{1}}}{4d}p_{1}}.

and therefore, since
\gamma_{p_{1}}\geq\gamma
,

|Df_{c}^{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\xi_{n_{1}}(c))|\geq e^{\frac{3\gamma}{4d}p_{1}}Ce^{\gamma_{H}\ell_{1}}\geq e^{\gamma_{2}(n_{2}-n_{1})},

for some
\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma/(3d)
. Moreover, by the weak parameter dependence property,

\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n_{1}+p_{1}+\ell_{1}}(A))\geq CQ^{-(p_{1}+\ell_{1})}e^{\gamma_{H}\ell_{1}}e^{(1-\kappa)r}\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n_{1}}(A))\geq 2\operatorname{diam}(\xi_{n_{1}}(A)),

since
r
is large, and
Q>1
is comparatively small (
\log Q<\min\{\gamma_{H},\gamma/(3d)\}
). Since
p_{1}<n_{1}
by Lemma
3.2
, using that
n_{1}
is large,

|Df_{c}^{n_{2}}(c)|\geq Ce^{\gamma n_{1}+\frac{3\gamma}{4d}p_{1}+\gamma_{H}\ell_{1}}\geq e^{(\gamma/3)n_{2}}.

∎

3.2
Main Distortion Lemma

Let us first state the main distortion lemma, see Lemma 3.14,
[
4
]
. It can be used in our situation as well, even if the map is not slowly recurrent. The constant
\tilde{\gamma}
in the following lemma can be chosen to be arbitrarily small, but we may have to adjust
{\mathcal{Q}}
, i.e.
\varepsilon
to it. We want to be able to use the lemma related to a small “critical”
\gamma_{C}>0
defined later.

Lemma 3.8

(Main distortion lemma)
.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\tilde{\gamma}>0
. Then there exists
N
large enough such that the following holds: If
A\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\nu}(\tilde{\gamma})
is a partition element and
\nu\geq N
is a return time or does not belong to the bound period, and
\nu^{\prime}
is the next free return, then we have

\left|\frac{Df_{c_{1}}^{n}(c_{1})}{Df_{c_{2}}^{n}(c_{2})}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}

(3.8)

for
c_{1},c_{2}\in A
and for
\nu\leq n\leq\nu^{\prime}
, provided that
A
is still a partition element at the time
n
.

For consistency we include the proof of this important lemma, although its proof is almost the same, verbatim, as the proof of Lemma 3.14 in
[
4
]
(alternatively see
[
3
, Lemma 7.3]
).

Proof.

The exponent cannot drop too much from
\nu
until
\nu^{\prime}
due to Lemma
3.7
. In fact, the exponent
\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}(c)
at
n
, satisfies
\gamma_{n}\geq\tilde{\gamma}/3
. Note that the weak parameter dependence property holds as soon as
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\tilde{\gamma}/3)
(if we choose
\gamma=\gamma_{Q}>0
small enough in Lemma
2.9
), so we can use Lemmas 3.2 and 3.4 in
[
4
]
. The lemma then reduces to check whether the following sum can be made arbitrarily small:

\Upsilon:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}.

(3.9)

We divide the sum into three parts as follows. Let
(\nu_{k})
be the free returns before time
n
, where
k\leq s
. In other words,
\nu=\nu_{s}
and
\nu^{\prime}=\nu_{s+1}
. Let also
p_{k}
be the length of the associated bound period of the return
\nu_{k}
. The estimate of
\Upsilon
is then divided into the following parts:

\displaystyle\Upsilon=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j,l}(b)|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k}-1}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=\nu_{s}}^{n-1}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

\displaystyle=:\Upsilon_{B}+\Upsilon_{F}+\Upsilon_{T}.

Here
\Upsilon_{B}
denotes the contribution from bound periods, while
\Upsilon_{F}
the contribution from free periods, and
\Upsilon_{T}
the contribution from the last return
\nu_{s}
up until time
n
.

For the bound periods, we use the definition of bound period, distortion, to estimate each sum in the sums in
\Upsilon_{B}
. First we note that, by the weak parameter dependence property, if
|\xi_{\nu_{k-1}}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k-1}}(b)|\sim e^{-r_{k-1}}/r_{k-1}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b)|

\displaystyle\sim e^{-r_{k-1}(d-1)}|\xi_{\nu_{k-1}}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k-1}}(b)|

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{e^{-r_{k-1}d}}{r_{k-1}^{2}}\sim\frac{|\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b)-\xi_{1}(b)|}{r_{k-1}^{2}}.

(3.11)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{B}^{k}=\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|Df^{j-\nu_{k-1}-1}(\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b))||\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|Df^{j-\nu_{k-1}-1}(\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b))|e^{-r_{k-1}d}}{r_{k-1}^{2}|\xi_{j}(b)|}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|Df^{j-\nu_{k-1}-1}(\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b))||\xi_{\nu_{k-1}+1}(b)-\xi_{1}(b)|}{r_{k-1}^{2}|\xi_{j}(b)|}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{|\xi_{j}(b)-\xi_{j-\nu_{k-1}}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{k-1}^{2}}+\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}}\frac{e^{-\beta(j-\nu_{k-1})}}{r_{k-1}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{r_{k-1}^{2}}.

(3.17)

Given
r\geq\Delta
, let
K(r)
be the set of indices
k
such that
|\xi_{\nu_{k}}(A)|\sim e^{-r}
, and let
\hat{k}(r)
be the largest index contained in
K(r)
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.7
that

\Upsilon_{B}=\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}\Upsilon_{B}^{k}\leq\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\sum_{k\in K(r)}\Upsilon_{B}^{k}\lesssim\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\Upsilon_{B}^{\hat{k}(r)}\lesssim\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta},

(3.18)

To estimate the contribution from the corresponding free period, we use the weak parameter dependence and Lemma
2.8
to obtain

\displaystyle|\xi_{\nu_{k}}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k}}(b)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{Q^{\nu_{k}-j}}|Df_{a}^{\nu_{k}-j}(\xi_{j}(a))|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\frac{e^{\gamma_{H}}}{Q}\right)^{\nu_{k}-j}|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|

for
\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}+1\leq j\leq\nu_{k}-1
. Since
Q
is small compared to
e^{\gamma_{H}}
we get the each sum
\Upsilon_{F}^{k}
in
\Upsilon_{F}
can be estimated by

\Upsilon_{F}^{k}\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{\nu_{k}}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k}}(b)|}{e^{-r}}\sum_{j=\nu_{k-1}+p_{k-1}+1}^{\nu_{k}-1}\left(\frac{Q}{e^{\gamma_{H}}}\right)^{\nu_{k}-j}\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{\nu_{k}}(a)-\xi_{\nu_{k}}(b)|}{e^{-r}}.

Using the same argument as in the estimate of the contribution from the bound periods, we find that

\Upsilon_{F}=\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}\Upsilon_{F}^{k}\leq\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\sum_{k\in K(r)}\Upsilon_{F}^{k}\lesssim\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\Upsilon_{F}^{\hat{k}(r)}\lesssim\sum_{r\geq\Delta}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta}.

We now consider the tail
\Upsilon_{T}
. It is easy to see that we may assume that
\nu_{s}+p_{s}+1\leq n<\nu_{s+1}
, otherwise
n
belongs to a bound period or is simply a free return which we already have considered. For this purpose, we consider pseudo-returns, and we let
\nu_{s}+p_{s}+1\leq q_{1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{t}\leq n
be the indices of these returns. By definition,
\xi_{q_{k}}(a)\cap U^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
\xi_{q_{k}}(a)\cap U=\emptyset
. For pseudo-returns, bound periods and free periods are defined in a similar way. As in the previous estimates, the contribution to the distortion between any two pseudo-returns of index
q_{k}
and
q_{k+1}
is a constant times

\frac{|\xi_{q_{k}}(a)-\xi_{q_{k}}(b)|}{e^{-r_{k}}}+\frac{|\xi_{q_{k+1}}(a)-\xi_{q_{k+1}}(b)|}{e^{-r_{k+1}}},

where
r_{k}
and
r_{k+1}
such that
|\xi_{q_{k}}(b)|\sim e^{-r_{k}}
and
|\xi_{q_{k+1}}(b)|\sim e^{-r_{k+1}}
. The difference here is that, at a pseudo-return, the only thing we know about the length of our interval is that
|\xi_{q_{k}}(a)-\xi_{q_{k}}(b)|\leq S
, where
S=\varepsilon_{1}\delta
is the large scale. With similar methods and notation used for estimating the bound and free contributions, we have

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=\nu_{s}}^{q_{1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\sum_{j=q_{k}}^{q_{k+1}-1}+\sum_{j=q_{t}}^{n-1}\right)\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{s}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\frac{|\xi_{q_{k}}(a)-\xi_{q_{k}}(b)|}{|\xi_{q_{k}}(b)|}+\sum_{j=q_{t}}^{n-1}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}+\sum_{r=\Delta^{\prime}}^{\Delta}\frac{|\xi_{q_{\hat{k}(r)}}(a)-\xi_{q_{\hat{k}(r)}}(b)|}{|\xi_{q_{\hat{k}(r)}}(b)|}+\frac{S}{\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}+\varepsilon_{1}\sum_{r=\Delta^{\prime}}^{\Delta}e^{r-\Delta}+\varepsilon_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}+\varepsilon_{1},

where we in the sum from
q_{t}
to
n-1
used Lemma
2.8
(the first inequality, now with respect to
U^{\prime}
) and that
|\xi_{j}(b)|>\delta^{\prime}>\delta
during this time.

Combining all these estimate above we arrive at

\Upsilon=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{|\xi_{j}(a)-\xi_{j}(b)|}{|\xi_{j}(b)|}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Delta}+\varepsilon_{1},

and if
\delta
and
\varepsilon_{1}
are small enough, we reach the desired conclusion of strong distortion.

∎

(3.19)

for all

3.3
Start-up

Next we prove the following lemma that provides us with a suitable initial parameter square. (Compare with Lemma 3.15 in
[
4
]
.)

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f_{c_{0}}
be a Collet–Eckmann unicritical map. Let
N_{L}
be as in Lemma
2.9
, let
\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon_{1}>0
be sufficiently small. Then there is a neighbourhood
U
of the critical point such that, with
S=\varepsilon_{1}\delta
and for each sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
there is
N,\tilde{N}^{\prime}
such that
\tilde{N}^{\prime}\leq N_{L}\leq N
and such that for all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
we have the following:

(i)

For some
\gamma\geq\underline{\gamma}(c_{0})(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})
, one has for all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}

\left|Df_{c}^{k}(c)\right|\geq e^{\gamma k}\quad(\tilde{N}^{\prime}\leq k\leq N-1).

(ii)

\mathcal{Q}
is a partition element at time
N-1
, and
\xi_{N}(Q)
either is an essential return or reaches the large scale
S
.

(iii)

For all
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\mathcal{Q}}
one has

\left|\frac{Df_{c_{1}}^{k}(c_{1})}{Df_{c_{2}}^{k}(c_{2})}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\quad(k=0,1,\dots N).

(iv)

There exists a constant
K=K(\delta,\varepsilon_{1})>1
such that for all
c\in\mathcal{Q}
and
0\leq k\leq N-1

K^{-1}|\xi_{k}(c)|\leq|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|\leq K|\xi_{k}(c)|.

We will use this lemma where
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq 1/10
, so that
\tilde{N}^{\prime}
can be set equal to
\tilde{N}
in Definition
2.2
.

Proof.

For any complex
c_{1},c_{2},z,w
we have

\left|\frac{Df_{c_{1}}(z)}{Df_{c_{2}}(w)}-1\right|\leq\left|\frac{z-w}{w^{d-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-2}z^{k}w^{d-2-k}\right|\leq\left|\frac{z-w}{w}\right|\sum_{k=0}^{d-2}\left(\left|\frac{z-w}{w}\right|+1\right)^{k}.

Assuming that
z
and
w
belong to a partition element, we have that

\left|\frac{z-w}{w}\right|\leq\begin{cases}\,\Delta^{-2}\ &\mbox{if}\ z\in U\ \mbox{or}\ w\in U,\\
\,\varepsilon_{1}\ &\mbox{if}\ z,w\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus U.\end{cases}

With
\eta=\min(\Delta^{-2},\varepsilon_{1})
we therefore have

\left|\frac{Df_{c_{1}}(z)}{Df_{c_{2}}(w)}-1\right|\leq(1+\eta)^{d-1}-1.

In particular,

\left|\frac{Df_{c_{1}}(z)}{Df_{c_{2}}(w)}\right|\leq C,

with
C>1
arbitrarily close to
1
if
\delta
and
\varepsilon_{1}
are sufficiently small. Assuming that
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a partition element at time
n
, and that
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, we get from the above distortion that

|Df^{k}_{c}(c)|\geq C^{-k}|Df_{c_{0}}(c_{0})|\geq C^{-k}C_{0}e^{\gamma_{0}k}\geq C_{0}e^{\gamma k}.

This tells us that as long as
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a partition element
f_{c}
, with
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, will satisfy a Collet–Eckmann condition. We may thus apply Lemma
2.9
to conclude that our interval will expand:

\operatorname{diam}\left(\xi_{k}({\mathcal{Q}})\right)\sim|\xi_{k}^{\prime}(c^{\prime})|\operatorname{diam}\left({\mathcal{Q}}\right)\sim e^{\gamma k}\operatorname{diam}\left({\mathcal{Q}}\right).

In particular we can make the perturbation small enough to find the time
N
such that (i) and (ii) are valid.

By the choice of
N_{L}
, we can choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that
{\mathcal{Q}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{N_{L}}(\gamma)
for some
\gamma
arbitrarily close to
\gamma_{0}
.

By the definition of a partition element we can use Lemma
3.8
repeatedly starting from the time
N_{L}
up to
N-1
to get the distortion claimed in (iii).

To prove (iv) suppose first that
\xi_{k}({\mathcal{Q}})
is a return to
U
, and let
r
be the integer satisfying

e^{-r}\leq\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{k}(\mathcal{Q}),0)\leq e^{-r+1}.

Then
\operatorname{diam}\left(\xi_{k}(\mathcal{Q})\right)\leq e^{-r+1}/r^{2}
, hence for every
c\in\mathcal{Q}
we have that

\displaystyle|\xi_{k}(c)|

\displaystyle\geq|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|-\operatorname{diam}\xi_{k}(\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{e^{-r+1}}{|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|r^{2}}\right)|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|.

Since
|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|\geq\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{k}(\mathcal{Q}),0)\geq e^{-r}
and
r\geq\Delta
, we conclude that

|\xi_{k}(c)|\geq\left(1-\frac{e}{\Delta^{2}}\right)|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|.

On the other hand, if we are outside of
U
, then

|\xi_{k}(c)|\geq\left(1-\frac{S}{\delta}\right)|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|=(1-\varepsilon_{1})|\xi_{k}(c_{0})|.

This proves one of the inequalities, and the other one follows from exchanging
c_{0}
and
c
in the above.
∎

4
Promotion

In this part we introduce the key idea of
promotion
which means that most of the parameters in
{\mathcal{Q}}
will reach a slow recurrence condition after several essential returns.

By Lemma
3.9
, all critical orbits for parameters in
{\mathcal{Q}}
will follow the starting critical orbit up to time
N
with bounded distortion and exponential growth of derivatives. If
{\mathcal{Q}}
has grown to the large scale under
\xi_{N}
, then
\xi_{N}({\mathcal{Q}})
covers mostly the Fatou set of
f_{0}
, and by a compactness argument, we can conclude that most parameters in
{\mathcal{Q}}
will land in the Fatou set of the corresponding map under
\xi_{n}
; see next section for more details. Hence a very large portion of parameters are hyperbolic in
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Therefore, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\xi_{N}({\mathcal{Q}})
has not reached the large scale yet and thus is an essential return.

At this time we may have to delete parameters that returns too deep at time
N
in comparison to the starting map. Let
C_{1}>1
be given and consider those
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
such that

\frac{\alpha_{N}(c)}{\alpha_{N}(c_{0})}\leq C_{1}.

(4.1)

This means that we delete parameters of measure

m\left(\{c\in{\mathcal{Q}}:c\text{ does not satisfy \eqref{appr2-ess} }\}\right)\leq m({\mathcal{Q}})\frac{e^{-2C_{1}\alpha_{N}(c_{0})N}}{e^{-2\alpha_{N}(c_{0})N}}=\colon\delta_{0}m({\mathcal{Q}}),

which can be made arbitrarily small if
N
is large enough. We make the deletion so that the smallest collection of whole partition elements are deleted (this means that we may delete a little more than necessary). The remaining parameters are partitioned into partition elements as above. Each of these partition elements, containing
c
, is denoted by
A_{N}(c)
. Sometimes we just write
A_{N}
. However, note that
A_{N-1}={\mathcal{Q}}
(there is no partition before time
N
).

After this time
N
we lose this kind of dependence on the starting orbit. So we need to control recurrence rates so that we do not lose a lot of derivative growth.

Let
N=\nu_{0}<\nu_{1}<\cdots
be the free returns after time
N
. Suppose that
A
is a partition element at time
\nu_{j}
. We will introduce two sequences
\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j}}(A)\}
and
\{\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}}(A)\}
inductively which capture two key features for later analysis. Firstly, parameters
c\in A
for which
\xi_{\nu_{j}}(c)
are at distance
e^{-\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j}}(A)\nu_{j}}
away from the critical point will be kept (and thus has controlled approach rate to the critical point), and secondly, we do not lose a lot of the derivative growth between two free returns.

We start with the time
\nu_{0}
. By Lemma
3.9
(iv),
\xi_{k}(c)
stays close to
\xi_{k}(c_{0})
and thus
\alpha_{k}(c)
is comparable to
\alpha_{k}(c_{0})
for all
k\leq N
and all
c\in A_{N}
.

Definition of
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{0}}(A)
.

Put

\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{0}}(A_{N})=\sup_{c\in A_{N}}\alpha_{\nu_{0}}(c).

Let
p_{0}
and
\ell_{0}
be the length of bound and free periods for the return time
\nu_{0}
, respectively. The following lemma is a consequence of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
|\xi_{\nu_{0}}(c)|\sim e^{-r}
, for some
c\in A_{N}
, where
A_{N}\subset{\mathcal{Q}}
is a partition element satisfying (
4.1
). Then
|Df_{c}^{p_{0}+1}(\xi_{\nu_{0}}(c))|\geq e^{(1-\kappa)r}
.

Proof.

We can make
C_{1}
as close to
1
as we want so that
c\in A_{N}
implies
c\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(\underline{\gamma})
. The lemma now follows from Corollary
3.5
.
∎

Note that by the definition of
\kappa
, Lemma
4.1
says that
\operatorname{diam}\left(\xi_{\nu_{0}+p_{0}}(A_{N})\right)\geq e^{-\kappa r}
, where
\operatorname{diam}\left(\xi_{\nu_{0}}(A_{N})\right)\sim e^{-r}
. Now we fix some
\kappa^{\prime}
such that
\kappa<\kappa^{\prime}<1
. In the following, we shall define
\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j}}(A)\}
and
\{\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}}(A)\}
, where
A
is a partition element at the free return time
\nu_{j}
.

Assume that
\operatorname{dist}(0,\xi_{\nu_{j}}(A))\sim e^{-r_{j}}\sim e^{-\alpha_{\nu_{j}}\nu_{j}}
, and
A=A_{\nu_{j}}
is a partition element. The lengths of the bound and free periods for this return are
p_{j}
and
\ell_{j}
, respectively. Define

\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j+1}}(A)=\kappa^{\prime}\frac{\nu_{j}}{\nu_{j+1}}\sup\limits_{c\in A}{\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)}\quad\left(\leq\kappa^{\prime}\sup\limits_{c\in A}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)\right).

(4.2)

We do this at each return (including inessential returns).

Note that since
\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)
is almost constant in

\tilde{\kappa}
arbitrarily close to

\tilde{\kappa}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c^{\prime})\leq\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\kappa}}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c^{\prime}).

This, together with (

\tilde{\kappa}\kappa^{\prime}\frac{\nu_{j}}{\nu_{j+1}}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j+1}}(A)\leq\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{\tilde{\kappa}}\frac{\nu_{j}}{\nu_{j+1}}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)

for all

At the next return we are going to delete from
A
(which is a partition element at time
\nu_{j}

In other words, if

\alpha_{\nu_{j+1}}(c)>\tilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j+1}}(A).

A

, let us also define

\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(A)=\sup\limits_{c\in A}\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c).

So we have a procedure; given

\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{\nu_{j}}(A),0)\sim e^{-\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(A)\nu_{j}}

at the next return, i.e. we have a mapping

\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(A_{\nu_{j}})\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j+1}}(A_{\nu_{j}}),

so that

\alpha_{\nu_{j+1}}(A_{\nu_{j+1}})\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{\nu_{j+1}}(A_{\nu_{j}})

Let

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A

\operatorname{diam}\xi_{\nu_{j+1}}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{-r_{j+1}}}{r_{j+1}^{2}}.

By the above Lemma

.

Recall that
A=A_{\nu_{j}}
is a partition element at time

\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}}

We define

\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{0}}(A_{\nu_{0}})=\gamma_{\nu_{0}-1}(A_{\nu_{0}})-(d-1)\alpha_{\nu_{0}}(A_{\nu_{0}})-\frac{C}{\nu_{0}},

so that
\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{0}}(A_{\nu_{0}}(c_{0}))=\underline{\gamma}_{N}
is at least

from Lemma

). For a partition element

(4.6)

and put

Remark 4.4
.

Note that
\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}}

are respectively partition elements at time

and
\nu_{j+1}

Suppose
\underline{\gamma}_{k}(A)
is defined for
\nu_{0}\leq k\leq\nu_{j}
, that
\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}}(A)=\underline{\gamma}_{\nu_{j}-1}(A)
, that (for

at returns.

If we follow a partition element
A

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu_{j+1}}(c)\geq\gamma_{\nu_{j+1}-1}(c)-(d-1)\alpha_{\nu_{j+1}}(c)-\frac{C}{\nu_{j+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\gamma_{\nu_{j}-1}(c)(\nu_{j}-1)+(1-\kappa)\alpha_{\nu_{j}}(c)\nu_{j}+\gamma_{H}\ell_{j}\right)\frac{1}{\nu_{j+1}-1}

(4.9)

.

Suppose that
\nu_{J}=m_{0}<m_{1}<\ldots<m_{s}<(1+\iota)m_{0}
are consecutive essential returns for a parameter
a\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let
A_{j}
be a partition element for an essential return
\xi_{m_{j}}(A_{j})\subset U
where
\operatorname{dist}(0,\xi_{m_{j}}(A_{j}))\sim e^{-r_{j}}
. Then, by Lemma
3.3
,

\frac{m(A_{j+1})}{m(A_{j})}\leq C\frac{e^{-2r_{j+1}}}{e^{-2\hat{\kappa}r_{j}}}.

Consequently,

\frac{m(A_{s})}{m(A_{0})}\leq C^{s}\frac{e^{2\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}-r_{j+1}}}{e^{-2\hat{\kappa}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}r_{j}}}\leq C^{s}e^{2\hat{\kappa}r_{0}-2(1-\hat{\kappa})\sum_{j=1}^{s}r_{j}}.

Putting
R=r_{1}+\ldots+r_{s}
we have, by the q-Lemma,

E(m_{0},c)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{s}\tilde{M}r_{j}\leq\tilde{M}r_{0}+\tilde{M}R.

Following the old traditions (the ideas go back to the works
[
7
,
8
]
), we now compute the number of combinations of returns, i.e. the number of combinations of
R=r_{1}+\ldots+r_{s}
where
R
is fixed. This can be estimated with Stirling’s formula: If
\Delta
is sufficiently large, then

\binom{R+s-1}{s-1}\leq e^{R(1-\hat{\kappa})/3}(1+\eta(\Delta))^{R},

where
\eta(\Delta)\rightarrow 0
as
\Delta\rightarrow\infty
. Let
A_{s,R}
be the set of parameters in
A_{0}
that have exactly
s
free essential returns in the time interval
[m_{0},(1+\iota)m_{0}]
before escape, where
R=r_{1}+\ldots+r_{s}
. Let
\hat{A}
be the largest of all squares in
A_{s,R}
. Then,

m(A_{s,R})\leq m(\hat{A})e^{R(1-\hat{\kappa})/3}(1+\eta(\Delta))^{R}.

So,

\displaystyle m\left(\left\{c\in A_{0}:E(m_{0},c)=t\right\}\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{R\geq\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r,s\leq R/\Delta}m(A_{s,R})

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{R\geq\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r}\sum_{s=1}^{R/\Delta}m(\hat{A})e^{R(1-\hat{\kappa})/3}(1+\eta(\Delta))^{R}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq m(A_{0})\sum_{R\geq\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r}C^{\prime}e^{R(1-\hat{\kappa})/3}(1+\eta(\Delta))^{R}C^{R/\Delta}e^{2\hat{\kappa}r-2(1-\hat{\kappa})R}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq Cm(A_{0})\sum_{R\geq\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r}e^{R(1-\hat{\kappa})/3+R\ln(1+\eta(\Delta))+(R/\Delta)\log C+2\hat{\kappa}r-2(1-\hat{\kappa})R}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq Cm(A_{0})\sum_{R\geq\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r}e^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\hat{\kappa})R+2\hat{\kappa}r}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq Cm(A_{0})e^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\hat{\kappa})(\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}-r)+2\hat{\kappa}r}

(5.15)

\displaystyle=Cm(A_{0})e^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\hat{\kappa})\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}+\frac{r}{2}(3+\hat{\kappa})}.

(5.16)

So if
t\geq 2\tilde{M}\frac{3+\hat{\kappa}}{1-\hat{\kappa}}r
then we have an estimate of the measure for parameters with long escape times (recall
r=\alpha m_{0}
):

m\left(\{c\in A_{0}:E(m_{0},c)=t\}\right)\leq Cm(A_{0})e^{-2(3+\hat{\kappa})r}=Cm(A_{0})e^{-2(3+\hat{\kappa})\alpha m_{0}},

or

m(\{c\in A_{0}:E(m_{0},c)=t\})\leq Cm(A_{0})e^{-(1-\hat{\kappa})\frac{t}{\tilde{M}}}.

This means that a big portion of
A_{0}
will reach the large scale within the time period
[m_{0},(1+\iota)m_{0}]
, where
\iota=10\tilde{M}\frac{3+\hat{\kappa}}{1-\hat{\kappa}}\alpha\leq 1
(see the condition on
\alpha
in (
5.3
)). Let this portion be
1-\delta_{2}
, where
0<\delta_{2}<1
, is small. Again note that
\delta_{2}
can be chosen as close to zero as we want.

Since this is true for every such
A_{0}\subset{\mathcal{Q}}
, we have that the measure of parameters that reach the large scale within time
(1+\iota)m_{0}
is at least

m({\mathcal{Q}})(1-\delta_{0})(1-\delta_{1})(1-\delta_{2}).

From
m_{0}
to
m_{0}(1+\iota)
, we do not loose too much either, since the Lyapunov exponent during this time cannot drop below
\gamma_{I}/(2(1+\iota))\geq\gamma_{C}
. Hence we have good distortion control for all parameters satisfying the basic approach rate assumption until
(1+\iota)m_{0}
.

5.2
Hyperbolic maps

Now to prove our theorems, we can argue similarly as in
[
4
]
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}(f_{c_{0}})
be the Fatou set for the unperturbed map
f_{c_{0}}
which is Collet–Eckmann, and let
{\mathcal{N}}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
be an
\varepsilon_{0}
-neighbourhood of
{\mathcal{J}}(f_{c_{0}})
as defined in (
2.8
). Here,
\varepsilon_{0}
can be chosen arbitrarily close to zero since the Hausdorff dimension of the Julia set of
f_{c_{0}}
is strictly less than two and therefore has zero area
[
11
, Corollary 2]
. Then since the Fatou set of
f_{c_{0}}
consists only of the attracting basin of
\infty
, any compact subset of the Fatou set is stable under perturbation. So
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus{\mathcal{N}}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is still contained the Fatou set
{\mathcal{F}}(f_{c})
for all
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
, provided that
{\mathcal{Q}}
is chosen small enough. By bounded distortion

m\left(\{c\in{\mathcal{Q}}:c\in{\mathcal{F}}(f_{c})\}\right)\geq(1-\delta_{0})(1-\delta_{1})(1-\delta_{2})(1-\delta_{3})m({\mathcal{Q}}),

(5.17)

where
\delta_{3}
is a constant depending on the distortion of
\xi_{n}(c)
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Since also
0<\delta_{3}<1
can be arbitrarily small, it follows that the Lebesgue density of hyperbolic maps at
c=c_{0}
is equal to
1
, by making the diameter of
{\mathcal{Q}}
tending to zero.

To see that Theorem
1.1
actually holds, we note that for all parameter
c\in{\mathcal{Q}}
satisfying
c\in{\mathcal{F}}(f_{c})
, the critical point is attracted by the super-attracting fixed point at
\infty
. Therefore, (
5.17
) actually gives an estimate of parameters which lie outside of
{\mathcal{M}}_{d}
.

Remark 5.5
.

For Collet–Eckmann rational maps with precisely one critical point in the Julia set, our proof works completely analogously. In this case, the parameter space is higher dimensional. Instead of using the transversality result by Avila and also Levin mentioned before, we shall need a more general result by Levin
[
20
]
. To get Lebesgue density of hyperbolic maps, we consider one-dimensional slices in the parameter space and get the same estimate as above.

Remark 5.6
.

In the setting of rational maps with precisely one critical point in the Julia set, the Collet–Eckmann condition is equivalent to several other well known non-uniform hyperbolicity conditions, e.g., the topological Collet–Eckmann condition, the second Collet–Eckmann condition, uniform hyperbolicity on periodic orbits, etc, see
[
23
]
. So our result applies to these maps as well.

Remark 5.7
.

The methods of promotion and the following parameter exclusion are likely to generalise to all rational CE-maps. Since the recurrence exponent
\alpha>0
after the promotion period can be chosen arbitrarily small (with possibly diminishing the diameter of
{\mathcal{Q}}
), taking into consideration that there may be several critical points in the Julia set (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
), one could prove that every rational CE-map for which
J(f)=\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is a Lebesgue density point of slowly recurrent CE-maps, even for real-analytic families of maps containing the starting CE-map (cf.
[
17
]
where complex one-dimensional families were considered).

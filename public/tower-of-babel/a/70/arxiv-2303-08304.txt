Universal Law of Coiling for a Short Elastic Strip Contacting Within a Tube
Jeng Yi Lee, 1,∗ Hao-Yu Lu, 2 and Ray-Kuang Lee 2,3,4,5

Abstract
We find that there exists a universal law of coiling not only for a long elastic strip contacting within a tube but also for a short one. Here the elastic strip we consider has the ratio of 2 < L / R ≤ 2 ​ π 2<L/R\leq 2\pi for its length L L to the tube radius R R . By varying the ratio of L / R L/R , we identify four types of deformation for such a short elastic strip, namely, two point-contact, three point-contact, continuous-contact, and self-contact. With theoretical formulas in closed forms and experimental demonstration, these four types are verified for any elastic strips contacting within a tube, irrespective of elastic properties, strip lengths, and tube radius. Our results on coiling can be readily applied to a variety of physical systems, including thin flexible electronic devices, van der Waals materials in scroll shape, and DNA packaging into viral capsids.

Conclusion.
—In addition to the universal law of coiling for a long stripe, we find theoretically and experimentally that a short elastic strip contacting within a tube, with the length ratio
2<L/R\leq 2\pi
, also follows universal behavior.
Four different types of deformation: two point-contact, three point-contact, continuous-contact, and self-contact, are identified in theory and verified in experiments.
Theoretically, the boundaries between two adjunct regions of deformation are characterized by elastic Kirchhoff’s equations; while experimentally three sets of material parameters, with a series of different lengths, are investigated, resulting in good agreement with our theoretical analysis.
Our results show the existence of a universal law even for a short strip, irrespective of elastic properties, strip lengths, and tube radii.
The results in this work can be readily applied to many practical applications, ranging, e.g., from flexible electronic devices, medical fibre imaging, to DNA packaging.

We remark that in this two point-contact region, a crucial geometric constraint is that the position of the middle part of the strip projected to x-axis is bound by

\displaystyle 0\leqslant

\displaystyle x\leqslant R\quad\Rightarrow\quad 1-\frac{1}{4}[\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}d\phi]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sin\phi_{0}\leq 1.

(21)

Here, we already apply
P_{0}
from Eq. (

.
By solving above condition, we then obtain

0.358\leq\phi_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}.

(22)

Next, combined with Eq. (
19
), the strip length in this two point-contact region is bounded by

L/R\in[2,3.033),

(23)

which is valid for any elastic materials.

Once we determine
\phi_{0}
and
P_{0}
, the shape of elastic strip can be numerically calculated through

\displaystyle x(\phi^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{\cos\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi=\sqrt{\frac{2B}{P_{0}}[\sin\phi^{{}^{\prime}}-\sin\phi_{0}]},

(24)

\displaystyle y(\phi^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi,

(25)

where
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}=[\phi_{0},\pi-\phi_{0}]
. An example of two point-contact deformation for

II
Three point-contact region

When
L/R>3.033
, the strip encounters three external forces, denoted as
\vec{P}_{0}
,
\vec{P}_{1}
, and
\vec{P}_{2}
.
The corresponding tangential angles of the strip with respect to X-axis are denoted as
\phi_{0}
,
\phi_{1}
, and
\phi_{2}
, respectively.
We note that in this stage the curvature of the strip at the middle part is larger than
1/R
, corresponding to a point contact.
On the other hand, from the force balance, it leads to
\vec{P}_{0}+\vec{P}_{1}+\vec{P}_{2}=0
.
Now, we introduce another angle coordinate
\beta
, with respect to
-
Y axis, as shown in Fig.
3
(b), as an auxiliary coordinate.
Thus, the coordinates of these contact-points can be written as

\vec{R}(\beta)=R\sin\beta\hat{x}+(R-R\cos\beta)\hat{y},

(26)

with
\beta=[\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}]
.
With the auxiliary coordinate, we have
\beta_{1}=\phi_{1}
, but
\phi_{0}\neq\beta_{0}
and
\phi_{2}\neq\beta_{2}
.
As a result, the corresponding point-contact forces are

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{0}\hat{y},

(27)

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1}(-\sin\beta_{1}\hat{x}+\cos\beta_{1}\hat{y}),

(28)

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{0}(-\sin\beta_{2}\hat{x}+\cos\beta_{2}\hat{y}).

(29)

With mirror symmetry, we have
|\vec{P}_{0}|=|\vec{P}_{2}|=P_{0}
. Then, by force balance, one obtain
\vec{P}_{0}+\vec{P}_{1}+\vec{P}_{2}=0
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\hat{x}:-P_{1}\sin\beta_{1}=P_{0}\sin\beta_{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\hat{y}:P_{0}+P_{1}\cos\beta_{1}+P_{0}\cos\beta_{2}=0.

(31)

Then, we find

\displaystyle\tan\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\beta_{2}}{1+\cos\beta_{2}},

(32)

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{P_{0}\sin\beta_{2}}{\sin\beta_{1}}.

(33)

Once we determine
\beta_{1}
(
\phi_{1}
) and
P_{0}
,
\beta_{2}
and
P_{1}
can be obtained.

Further more, we divide the detached strips into two regions, denoted as
(1)
and
(2)
, marked by blue and green curves in Fig.
3
(b).
From the moment equation, a curvature equation for the region (1) can be obtained, i.e.,

\displaystyle\phi^{(1)}_{s}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}.

(34)

Here we also apply a zero moment at
s=0
, corresponding to
\phi_{s}(0)=0
.
Complemented by the geometry constraint for the middle part of the strip, we have the following conditions,

\displaystyle R\sin\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}},

(35)

\displaystyle R-R\cos\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}.

(36)

Again, by eliminating the unknown
P_{0}
, we have

\frac{1-\cos\phi_{1}}{\sin\phi_{1}}=\frac{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}}{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}},

(37)

with two unknowns involved, i.e.,
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
. For this segment of the strip, the corresponding length is

\frac{L}{2}=\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}.

(38)

With Eqs. (
35
) and (
38
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}=\frac{L}{R}\frac{\sqrt{\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0}}}{\sin\phi_{1}}.

(39)

Then, by considering a set of Eqs. (
37
) and (
39
):

\displaystyle\frac{1-\cos\phi_{1}}{\sin\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}}{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}},

(40)

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{R}\frac{\sqrt{\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0}}}{\sin\phi_{1}},

(41)

one can simultaneously solve
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
(
\beta_{1}
) when
L/R
is given.

The corresponding dimensionless force
\frac{P_{0}R^{2}}{B}
for the three point-contact case has the form:

\frac{P_{0}R^{2}}{B}=\frac{2(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})}{\sin^{2}\phi_{1}},

(42)

with an implicit dependence of
L/R
.

Regarding the other region (2), i.e.,
s=[\frac{L}{2},L]
, we have
\vec{F}=\vec{P}_{2}=P_{0}(-\sin\beta_{2}\hat{x}+\cos\beta_{2}\hat{y})
, where
\beta_{2}
can be calculated by using Eq. (
32
). Again, by using moment equation, the curvature equation for the region (2) is

\displaystyle\phi^{(2)}_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}\sin(\beta_{2}-\phi)+\frac{2B(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})^{2}}{R^{2}\sin^{2}\phi_{1}}-P_{0}\sin(\beta_{2}-\phi_{1})]},

(43)

where we apply the condition of a continuous curvature at
\phi_{1}
, i.e., between the region (1) and the region (2).
The curvature at
s=L/2
is

\phi_{s}(\frac{L}{2})=\frac{2(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})}{R\sin\phi_{1}}.

(44)

For this detached region (2), the corresponding length is defined by

\frac{L}{2}=\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\phi_{2}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}\sin(\beta_{2}-\phi)+\frac{2B(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})^{2}}{R^{2}\sin^{2}\phi_{1}}-P_{0}\sin(\beta_{2}-\phi_{1})]}},

(45)

which can be used to determine
\phi_{2}
, i.e., tangential angle at
s=L
.

The three-point contact region will be terminated when the curvature at the middle plate meets
\phi_{s}(\frac{L}{2})=\frac{2(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})}{R\sin\phi_{1}}=\frac{1}{R}
, resulting in the continuous-contact region.
Consequently, the strip length for three-point contact situation is bounded by

L/R\in[3.033R,4.176),

(46)

which is again valid for any elastic materials.
In Fig.
3
(b), we illustrate such a three point-contact deformation from the numerical calculation with

.

III
Continuous-contact region

When
L/R>4.176
, we enter the continuous-contact region, as illustrated in Fig.
3
(c).
Here, the boundaries of the continuous-contact are defined as
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, with its tangential angles as
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

The corresponding force analysis diagram is also shown in Fig.
3

Now, there are two point-contact forces
\vec{P}_{0}
and
\vec{P}_{3}
and a continuous-contact pressure
\vec{K}

Here,
\vec{K}
is pressure defined as force per unit length.
The tangential angles of the strip at
\vec{P}_{0}
and
\vec{P}_{3}
with respect to X-axis are denoted as
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{3}
, respectively.

By considering mirror symmetry for the strip, we have
|\vec{P}_{0}|=|\vec{P}_{3}|=P_{0}
and the detached shapes, denoted as (1) and (3), are expected to be identical.
It is noted that within this continuous-contact region, its curvature remains
1/R

Bu a properly chosen coordinate, we also have
\phi_{1}=\beta_{1}
and
\phi_{2}=\beta_{2}
.
Now, for the region (1), the stress force is constant, i.e.,
\vec{F}=-\vec{P}_{0}=-P_{0}\hat{y}

\phi_{s}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\phi_{0})},

(47)

where a zero moment at
s=0
, corresponding to
\phi_{s}(0)=0
is used.
As the curvature at
C_{1}
is
\frac{1}{R}
, one can have

\frac{1}{R}=\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi_{1}-\phi_{0})}.

(48)

Since
C_{1}
is confined by the tube, we therefore have the geometric constraints:

\displaystyle R\sin\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi=\sqrt{\frac{2B}{P_{0}}(\sin\phi_{1}-\sin\phi_{0})},

\displaystyle R-R\cos\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi.

(50)

By eliminating
P_{0}
, we reach the following equation

\frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{1-\cos\phi_{1}}=\frac{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi}{\sqrt{(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi}{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt{(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi}.

(51)

By taking Eq. (
48
) into the first term of Eq. (
49
), we obtain

2\sin\phi_{0}=\sin\phi_{1}.

(52)

Consequently, we have a set of Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
),

\displaystyle 2\sin\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\phi_{1},

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{1-\cos\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi}{\sqrt{(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi}{\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt{(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}d\phi},

(54)

where two unknowns, i.e.,
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
are involved, which can be sufficiently determined, independent from the elastic bending moment
B
and tube radius
R
.
Numerically, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=0.421=24.1^{\circ},

(55)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=2.185=125.2^{\circ}.

(56)

The results reveal that the tangential angle of the free end is
\phi_{0}=0.421=24.12^{\circ}
; while the opening angle is
\phi_{1}=\beta_{1}=2.185=125.2^{\circ}
, valid for any elastic material and tube size, referring to universal law of coiling.

The corresponding dimensionless force

in this continuous contact region is

\frac{P_{0}R^{2}}{B}=\frac{1}{\sin\phi_{1}}=1.22.

(57)

Moreover, we formulate the detached length of the region (1), defined as

\displaystyle\frac{L^{{}^{\prime}}}{R}=\frac{1}{R}\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{2}}\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}}}=2.088.

(58)

Here, we already apply Eq. (
57
) with
\phi_{0}=0.421
and
\phi_{1}=2.185
.
This result defines the minimum length for the emergence of the continuous contact, i.e.,

.
On the other hand, the maximum length before the self-contact situation occurs with

2\frac{L^{{}^{\prime}}}{R}+(2\pi-2\beta_{1}).

(59)

Here,
2L^{{}^{\prime}}/R
denotes the total length of detached region; while
(2\pi-2\beta_{1})
denotes the length of the continuous-contact region.
As a result the strip length for this continuous-contact is bounded by

L\in[4.176R,6.091R],

(60)

which is valid for any elastic materials.

For the region (3), due to mirror symmetry of the strip,
\beta_{2}
(here
\beta_{2}=\phi_{2}
) can be found by

\beta_{2}=\frac{L-2L^{{}^{\prime}}}{R}+\beta_{1}.

(61)

By geometry, we have

\beta_{3}=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}.

(62)

We note that the tangential angle at
s=L

\phi_{3}
, is not the same as
\beta_{3}
.
By using the detached length condition, we can have

by

\frac{L^{{}^{\prime}}}{R}=\frac{1}{R}\int_{\phi_{2}}^{\phi_{3}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}(\sin[\phi_{3}-\beta_{3}]-\sin[\phi-\beta_{3}])}}=2.088.

(63)

The corresponding curvature equation for this detached region (3) has the form:

\phi_{s}^{(3)}=\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{3}-\beta_{3})-\sin(\phi-\beta_{3})]}.

(64)

The stress force at this detached region is
\vec{F}^{(3)}=q_{0}(-\sin\beta_{3}\hat{x}+\cos\beta_{3}\hat{y})
.
An example of this continuous contact region is illustrated in

.

IV
Self-contact region

The deformation of self-contact occurs at

L/R>6.091.

Due to the interaction among different segments of the strip emerges, the self-contact problem is nonlocal and highly nonlinear.

(d), we divide the strip into four segments, denoted as (1), (2), (3), and (4), with the corresponding length

,
L_{2}

First of all, we have

Here, only the region (3) is subject to the continuous contact.
We also denote
\phi_{0}
,
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}

\phi_{3}

\phi_{4}

and

Accordingly, a geometric relation for

\displaystyle\pi-\beta_{2}=\beta_{3}-\pi\quad\Rightarrow\quad\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}

Then, in the region (2), i.e.,

and

\vec{F}^{(4)}=P_{1}(-\sin\phi_{1}\hat{x}+\cos\phi_{1}\hat{y})

\vec{P}_{1}=-\vec{P}_{4}

s=L
, i.e.,
\phi_{s}(L)=0

s=L_{1}
meets that at
s=L
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}

\displaystyle R\sin(\beta_{3})+\int_{\beta_{3}}^{\phi_{4}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{1}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{4}-\phi_{1})-\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}

\displaystyle R-R\cos(\beta_{3})+\int_{\beta_{3}}^{\phi_{4}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{1}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{4}-\phi_{1})-\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}}.

(69)

In addition, we also have a position condition at
s=L_{1}+L_{2}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle R\sin\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

(70)

\displaystyle R-R\cos\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}}.

(71)

We also have the total strip length

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}}

\displaystyle+

(73)

With the conservation relation, one can obtain

\frac{B}{2R^{2}}-P_{0}\sin\phi_{2}+P_{1}\sin(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})=-P_{0}\sin\phi_{0}.

(74)

Finally, we summarize a set of six equations for these six unknowns:

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}=R\sin(\beta_{3})+\int_{\beta_{3}}^{\phi_{4}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{1}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{4}-\phi_{1})-\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

(76)

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}=R-R\cos(\beta_{3})+\int_{\beta_{3}}^{\phi_{4}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{1}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{4}-\phi_{1})-\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

\displaystyle R\sin\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{\cos\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

(77)

\displaystyle R-R\cos\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{\sin\phi d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

(78)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\phi_{0}}^{\phi_{1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{0}}{B}[\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0}]}}+\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\beta_{2}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{B}[P_{0}(\sin\phi-\sin\phi_{0})-P_{1}\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}}

(81)

\displaystyle+R(\beta_{3}-\beta_{2})+\int_{\beta_{3}}^{\phi_{4}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\frac{2P_{1}}{B}[\sin(\phi_{4}-\phi_{1})-\sin(\phi-\phi_{1})]}},

\displaystyle\frac{B}{2R^{2}}-P_{0}\sin\phi_{2}+P_{1}\sin(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})=-P_{0}\sin\phi_{0},

where
\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=2\pi

As example, Fig.

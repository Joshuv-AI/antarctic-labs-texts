Introduction

For C*- or W*-dynamical systems
(\mathcal{M},\tau,\mathbb{R})
, KMS states have been studied extensively, for instance in
[
BR96
]

(G,\alpha,\mathbb{R})
and
\alpha
-covariant representations
\pi

\mathcal{M}=\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
and the one-parameter group of *-automorphisms uniquely determined by
\tau_{t}(\pi(g))=\pi(\alpha_{t}(g))

\text{W}(\text{E},\sigma)
which is generated by the image of the Schrödinger representation of the Heisenberg group
\text{Heis}(\text{E},\sigma)

is a symplectic vector space (cf.
[
BR96

(\tau,\beta)
-KMS condition of a state

proves that the corresponding state
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C},\;g\mapsto\omega(\pi(g))
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS in the sense of the following definition.

Definition

If one conversely starts with an

If an

Structure of this paper
:

This paper is divided into two sections. What motivated this paper is mostly contained in Section
2

Geometric and representation theoretic aspects of Gibbs states

Definition

We call a subalgebra

Under the assumption that

\displaystyle\Delta:=\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\subseteq i\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}\quad\text{such that}\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}

(ii)

(iii)

1.1
Ellipticity of Gibbs elements

The goal of this subsection is to prove that, if
\pi
is a unitary representation of a finite-dimensional Lie group
G
with discrete kernel and
X\in\mathfrak{g}:=\textbf{L}(G)
such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class, then
X\in\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}
. This is equivalent to
\mathfrak{g}^{X}:=\text{ker}(\text{ad}(X))\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
being compactly embedded by
[
Ne00
, Lemma VII.1.7(c)]
.

The first crucial observation is that in this case, the eigenspaces of
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
are all finite-dimensional and invariant under
G^{X}:=\langle\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{X})\rangle
. Since
\pi
has discrete kernel, this implies that
\mathfrak{g}^{X}
is a compact Lie algebra. In order to prove that it actually is compactly embedded, we consider the smallest ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}\trianglelefteq\mathfrak{g}
, for a fixed
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
, such that

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}_{Y}}:\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}_{Y}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}_{Y},\;x+\mathfrak{n}_{Y}\mapsto[Y,x]+\mathfrak{n}_{Y}

is an elliptic endomorphism. For
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{X}
, we prove that
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}=\{0\}
holds, which is equivalent to
Y
being elliptic. To do this, we proceed by first showing that
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
is abelian and then use this to prove that
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
is an at most one-dimensional central ideal.

Lemma 1.1.1
.

Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of
G
with discrete kernel such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class. The ellipticity ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
is abelian, for every
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{X}

Proof.

Consider the unitary representation of
G

\displaystyle\rho:G\to U(B_{2}(\mathcal{H})),\quad\rho(g)(A)=\pi(g)A\pi(g)^{\ast}.

The eigenspace projections of

have finite rank and thus are Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Since
\pi(\exp(\mathbb{R}Y))
leaves the eigenspaces of

\rho(\exp(\mathbb{R}Y))
fix the corresponding projections. Now Moore’s Theorem
[
Mo80

implies that the normal subgroup

also fixes the projections onto the eigenspaces, which is equivalent to

leaving the eigenspaces invariant. Hence the representation
\pi|_{N_{Y}}
decomposes into finite-dimensional subrepresentations and has discrete kernel. Therefore the finite-dimensional unitary representations of
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
separate the points which implies that
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
is a compact Lie algebra. As a compact Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}
is in particular reductive and decomposes as
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{n}_{Y}}\oplus[\mathfrak{n}_{Y},\mathfrak{n}_{Y}]

\text{ad}(y)_{n}
were zero, then
\text{ad}(y)=\text{ad}(y)_{s}
would be elliptic since
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{C}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{0}
. This proves that there exists
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{0}
such that
\text{ad}(y)(x)=\text{ad}(y)_{n}(x)\in\mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. If we write
x=x_{0}+ix_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
, then
[x,y]\in\mathfrak{z}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
implies that either
[x_{0},y]\in\mathfrak{z}\setminus\{0\}
or
[x_{1},y]\in\mathfrak{z}\setminus\{0\}
hold. This proves the assertion.
∎

Remark 1.1.4
.

Consider the three-dimensional Heisenberg group defined as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\quad\text{with multiplication}\quad(a,b,c)(x,y,z):=(a+x,b+y,c+z+ay),

for
a,b,c,x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
. For irreducible unitary representations of
\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the center
Z(\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cong\mathbb{R}
acts by scalars and thus, if the center acts non-trivially, defines a central character. The Stone-von-Neumann Theorem (cf.
[
DE09
, Thm. 10.2.1]
) implies that this central character
\lambda:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T},\;x\mapsto e^{i\lambda x}
uniquely determines the unitary representation and that the unitary representation is unitarily equivalent to the representation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda}(a,b,c)f(x):=e^{i\lambda(bx+c)}f(x+a)\quad\text{for}\quad a,b,c,x\in\mathbb{R},\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Consider the automorphism
\Phi:\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;(a,b,c)\mapsto(b,-a,c-ab)
, which fixes the center pointwise. We note that by the Stone-von-Neumann theorem
\pi_{\lambda}\cong\pi_{\lambda}\circ\Phi
and
\Phi(0,t,0)=(t,0,0)
holds, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This proves that the unitary one-parameter groups defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda}(t,0,0)f(x)=f(x+t)\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{\lambda}(0,t,0)f(x)=e^{it\lambda x}f(x),\quad\text{for}\quad x,t\in\mathbb{R},\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

are conjugate via
U(\mathcal{H})
. In particular,
\pi_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R},0,0)
and
\pi_{\lambda}(0,\mathbb{R},0)
do not have compact closure in
U(\mathcal{H})
since the identical representation of both groups on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
clearly does not decompose into irreducible subrepresentations and the groups are conjugate.

We now come to our first main result:

Theorem 1.1.5
.

Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of
G
with discrete kernel such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class. Then
\pi
is a direct sum of irreducible representations and
X
is contained in
\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Since
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}\in\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a positive operator of trace class, it follows that
\pi(G)^{\prime}
leaves the finite-dimensional eigenspaces invariant. As finite dimensional C*-algebras are isomorphic to a direct sum of matrix algebras, this implies that there exists a maximal family of pairwise disjoint minimal projections in
\pi(G)^{\prime}
summing to the idenitfy in the strong operator topology. Therefore
\pi
is a direct sum of irreducible representations
\pi_{j}
with discrete kernel satisfying
\text{tr}(e^{i\partial\pi_{j}(X)})<\infty
. Suppose now that
\pi
is irreducible and let
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{X}
. It follows from Lemma
1.1.2
that
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and thus
Y
is either elliptic or
0\neq Y+\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is elliptic in
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. In the latter case, Lemma
1.1.3
implies that there exists
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
[Y,x_{0}]=z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\setminus\{0\}
and thus the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{Y,x_{0},z\}
is isomorphic to the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
\textbf{L}(\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
via the isomorphism defined by

\displaystyle Y\mapsto(1,0,0),\;x_{0}\mapsto(0,1,0),z\mapsto(0,0,1).

The simply connected Heisenberg group
\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the universal covering group of
\langle\exp(\mathfrak{h})\rangle
and thus the representation
\pi|_{\langle\exp(\mathfrak{h})\rangle}
lifts to a representation
\rho
of
\text{Heis}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. As
\pi
is irreducible,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
acts by scalars, i.e. there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\partial\rho(0,0,1)=\partial\pi(z)=i\lambda\mathds{1}.

One has
\lambda\neq 0
, because
\partial\pi(z)=0
would imply
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\text{ker}(\partial\pi)=\{0\}
. The Stone-von-Neumann Theorem (cf.
[
Ne00
, Thm. X.3.1]
) implies that
\rho
is a multiple of
\pi_{\lambda}
, where
\pi_{\lambda}
is the unique irreducible unitary representation of the Heisenberg group with central character
\lambda:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T},\;x\mapsto e^{i\lambda x}
. It follows from Remark
1.1.4
, that the closure of

\displaystyle\rho(\exp(\mathbb{R}(1,0,0)))=\pi(\exp(\mathbb{R}Y))

is not compact. This is a contradiction since a closed subgroup of
U(\mathcal{H})
leaving the finite-dimensional eigenspaces of
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
invariant is compact. Hence
\mathfrak{n}_{Y}=\{0\}
follows, which proves that
Y
is elliptic. As
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{X}
was arbitrary, we have shown that
\mathfrak{g}^{X}
is contained in
\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})
, which together with
[
Ne00
, Lemma VII.1.5(b)]
yields that
\mathfrak{g}^{X}
is compactly embedded. From
[
Ne00
, Lemma VII.1.7(c)]
, one obtains that
X\in\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}
.
∎

1.2
Characterization of representations of Gibbs type

The goal of this subsection is to summarize results from
[
Ne00
]
to prove that the irreducible unitary representation
\rho
of Gibbs type with discrete kernel coincide with faithful unitary highest weight representations of a finite-dimensional connected Lie group
G
. The Lie algebras, for which such representations exist, are precisely the admissible Lie algebras, i.e. those containing a generating invariant closed convex subset containing no affine line. The Lie algebraic and representation theoretic concepts used in this subsection are introduced in Subsection
A.1
and Subsection
A.2
.

The convex momentum set
I_{\pi}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
of a unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of a finite-dimensional Lie group
G
(cf. Definition
A.2.3
) connects convex geometry and representation theory. Its relevance for our study of representations of Gibbs type can be seen from the following result (cf.
[
Ne00
, Lemma X.I.6]
):

\displaystyle B(I_{\pi}):=\{Y\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\langle I_{\pi},Y\rangle>-\infty\}=\{Y\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\sup\text{spec}(i\partial\pi(Y))<\infty\}.

If
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
is an irreducible unitary representation of
G
with discrete kernel for which there exists
X\in\mathfrak{g}:=\textbf{L}(G)
such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class, then the spectrum of
i\partial\pi(X)
is bounded from above and Theorem
1.1.5
implies
X\in B(I_{\pi})\cap\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}
. Therefore
[
Ne00
, Thm. X.3.8]
is applicable and we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite-dimensional Lie group and
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible unitary representation of
G
with discrete kernel for which there exists
X\in\mathfrak{g}:=\textbf{L}(G)
such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class. Then there exist a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
containing
X
and a positive system of roots
\Delta^{+}\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
, which is adapted
(cf. Definition
A.1.3
),
such that
\pi
is a unitary highest weight representation
(cf. Definition
A.2.9
)
with respect to
\Delta^{+}
and
X
is contained in the interior of the cone

\displaystyle C_{\text{max}}:=C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})=\{X\in\mathfrak{t}\;|\;i\alpha(X)\geq 0\;\text{for all}\;\alpha\in\Delta_{p}^{+}\}.

For an element
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, the condition that there exists an adapted positive system
\Delta^{+}
such that
X\in C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}
is not sufficient for
X
to be a Gibbs element. To ensure this, we need further restrictions on
\Delta^{+}
. The Gelfand-Rai’kov Theorem for Unitary Highest Weight Representations
[
Ne00
, Thm. X.4.8]
states sufficient conditions for a positive root system
\Delta^{+}
of a simply connected admissible Lie group such that unitary highest weight representations exist. In this theorem, one considers the convex cone

\displaystyle C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}):=\text{cone}(\{i[Z_{\alpha},Z_{\alpha}^{\ast}]\;|\;Z_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha},\;\alpha\in\Delta_{p}^{+}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{t},

where
\ast:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\;x+iy\mapsto-x+iy
denotes the involution on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
discussed in Remark
A.1.2
. The condition stated in the following definition then ensures that the unitary highest weight representations separate the points.

Definition 1.2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra. We call an adapted positive system of roots
\Delta^{+}\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
is pointed and contained in
C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})

admissible
.

Remark 1.2.3
.

The term admissible for a positive system of roots is not used in
[
Ne00
]
. We introduce it to state the conditions for positive systems of roots arising in our context more concisely.

The condition of admissibility of a positive system arises quite naturally in the theory of unitary highest weight representations. In the Gelfand-Raik’kov theorem, it is stated as a sufficient condition for the existence of unitary highest weight representation of admissible Lie algebras. The following theorem, which is stated implicitly in
[
Ne00
]
, shows that for faithful unitary highest weight representations the condition is also necessary:

Theorem 1.2.4
.

(
[
Ne00
, Thm. IX.2.17]
)

Let
G
be a finite dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible unitary highest representation of
G
with respect to a positive system
\Delta^{+}
and suppose
\pi
has discrete kernel. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is admissible and
\Delta^{+}
is an admissible positive system.

Proof.

We consider the space of smooth vectors
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
which is a faithful, unitary highest weight module of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. It follows from
[
Ne00
, Prop. IX.1.14]
(iii) that, for the highest weight
\lambda\in i\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}
of
\pi
the
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
is isomorphic to
L(\lambda,\Delta^{+})
, which is introduced there. Using
[
Ne00
, Thm. IX.2.17]
, which is applicable to
L(\lambda,\Delta^{+})
, one obtains that
\mathfrak{g}
is admissible,
\Delta^{+}
is adapted and
-i\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}
is contained in the interior of

\displaystyle C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\star}:=\{f\in\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}\;|\;f(C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}))\subseteq[0,\infty)\}\subseteq B(C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})).

Then
[
Ne00
, Prop. V.1.15]
implies that the convex cone
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
is pointed since
B(C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}))
has inner points. To prove that
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
holds, we apply
[
Ne00
, Prop. VIII.1.11]
which is applicable if there exists
f\in C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\star}
such that its orbit
\mathcal{O}_{f}
under the coadjoint action is closed, the convex hull of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
contains no affine lines and
\mathcal{O}_{f}
spans
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

If one applies
[
Ne00
, Thm. VIII.1.19]
to the highest weight
\lambda\in iC_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\star}\subseteq i\mathfrak{t}^{\ast}
of the unitary highest weight representation
\pi
with discrete kernel, one obtains that
-i\lambda
is admissible, i.e. its orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-i\lambda}
under the coadjoint action is closed and its convex hull contains no affine lines. Using
[
Ne00
, Thm. X.4.1]
which states that the momentum set of
\pi
is given by
I_{\pi}=\text{conv}(\mathcal{O}_{-i\lambda})
and
(I_{\pi})^{\bot}=\text{ker}(d\pi)=\{0\}
(cf.
[
Ne00
, Lemma X.1.6]
), one obtains that
I_{\pi}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is generating and

\displaystyle-i\lambda\in C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\star}\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{O}_{-i\lambda}=\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

Hence
[
Ne00
, Prop. VIII.1.11]
is applicable which yields
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
and thus the positive system
\Delta^{+}
is admissible.
∎

Remark 1.2.5
.

Note that
[
Ne00
, VIII.1.11]
implies that the positive system
\Delta^{+}
in the theorem above is uniquely determined by the condition that
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
is an irreducible unitary highest representation with respect to
\Delta^{+}
.

In
[
Ne00
]
, the highest weight modules
L(\lambda,\Delta^{+})
are studied, for involutive Lie algebras with root decomposition (cf.
[
Ne00
, Subsection IX.2]
). For those modules, one has the following result:

Theorem 1.2.6
.

(
[
Ne00
, Thm. IX.4.6]
)

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an admissible Lie algebra,
\Delta^{+}
an adapted positive system and
L(\lambda,\Delta^{+})
a unitary highest weight module with respect to
\Delta^{+}
. If
X\in C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}

and
\partial\pi
denotes the representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
L(\lambda,\Delta^{+})
, then
\text{tr}(e^{i\partial\pi(X)})<\infty
.

Remark 1.2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an admissible Lie algebra. Then
[
Ne00
, Thm. VII.3.10]
implies that there exists a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
and an admissible positive system of roots
\Delta^{+}
. Then
[
Ne00
, Prop. VIII.3.7]
associates to each admissible positive system
\Delta^{+}
a closed generating invariant cone
W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
such that
W_{\text{max}}\cap\mathfrak{t}=C_{\text{max}}
. In the proof of
[
Ne00
, Thm. VIII.3.10]
it is also shown that

\displaystyle W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}=\text{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}).C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}\subseteq\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is non-compact, the open invariant convex cone
W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}
is proper since
\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}
.

We are now able to present our second main result:

Theorem 1.2.8
.

Let
G
be a finite-dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible unitary highest representation of
G
with respect to a positive system
\Delta^{+}
. Then one has
\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\text{tr}(e^{i\partial\pi(X)})<\infty\}=W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Corollary
1.2.1
, Theorem
1.2.4
, and Remark
1.2.5
.
∎

As the Gelfand-Rai’kov Theorem (cf.
[
Ne00
, Thm. X.4.8]
) applies to simply connected admissible Lie groups, we may combine these results to obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2.9
.

Let
G
be a simply connected finite-dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between

(i)

irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of
G
such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class,

(ii)

irreducible unitary highest weight representations of admissible quotients
q:\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with respect to an admissible positive system
\Delta_{1}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that
q(X)\in W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{1,p}^{+})^{\circ}
.

We will now prove that an element
X
is contained in
\text{Gibbs}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if it is contained in an open proper invariant convex subset of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 1.2.10
.

Geometric characterization of Gibbs elements

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. Then an element
X
is a Gibbs element if and only if it is contained in an open proper invariant convex subset of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Suppose
X\in\text{Gibbs}(\mathfrak{g})
and
G
is a simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then there exists a unitary representation
\pi
of
G
such that
e^{i\partial\pi(X)}
is of trace class. Then Corollary
1.2.1
and Theorem
1.2.4
imply that
\mathfrak{g}/\text{ker}(d\pi)
is admissible and there exists an admissible positive system
\Delta^{+}
such that

\displaystyle q(X)\in C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}\subseteq W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ},

where
q:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\text{ker}(d\pi)
denotes the quotient map. If
\mathfrak{g}/\text{ker}(d\pi)
is non-compact, the subset

\displaystyle q^{-1}(W_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ})\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}

is an open proper invariant convex subset containing
X
. If
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{g}/\text{ker}(d\pi)
is compact, choose an
\text{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
-invariant metric on
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and an open ball
B\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
with respect to this metric containing
q(X)
. Then
q^{-1}(B)\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}
is an open proper invariant convex subset containing
X
.

Suppose conversely that
X
is contained in an open proper invariant convex subset
C\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}
. We adopt elements of the proof of
[
Ne00
, Prop. VII.3.4]
. We consider the set

\displaystyle H(C):=\{v\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;v+C=C\}.

H(C)
is a convex additive subgroup of the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
which implies that
H(C)
a linear subspace and the invariance of
C
yields that
H(C)
is an ideal. Consider the quotient map
q:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{g}/H(C)
and observe that
q(C)
is an invariant open generating convex subset. It satisfies

\displaystyle H_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(q(C))=\{w\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\;|\;w+q(C)=q(C)\}=\{q(v)\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\;|\;v\in\mathfrak{g},\;q(v+C)=q(C)\}=\{0\}

since
q(v+C)=q(C)
is equivalent to
(v+C)+H(C)=C+H(C)
, and then
C+H(C)=C
implies
v\in H(C)
. Therefore the closure of
q(C)
is an invariant generating closed convex subset containing no affine line and thus
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is admissible. To apply Theorem
1.2.6
and Corollary
1.2.9
, we show that there exists an admissible positive system
\Delta^{+}
such that
q(X)\in C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}
.

The set
q(C)
is contained in the interior of its closure
\overline{q(C)}
whose interior consists of elliptic elements (cf.
[
Ne00
, Prop. VII.3.4(b)]
) and thus
q(X)
is contained in the open invariant elliptic convex set
q(C)
.

For a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, one has
\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=\text{Inn}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}).\mathfrak{t}
(cf.
[
Ne00
, Thm. VII.1.8]
), we may assume
q(X)\in\mathfrak{t}
. Then the Sandwich Theorem (cf. Theorem
A.1.6
) implies that there exists an adapted positive system
\Delta^{+}
with respect to
\mathfrak{t}
such that

\displaystyle\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t}\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq\lim(\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t}):=\{v\in\mathfrak{t}\;|\;v+\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t}\}.

As
\overline{q(C)}
contains no affine line, the cone
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
is pointed and since
C_{\text{max}}
is a cone and
\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t}\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
, it follows that

\displaystyle C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq\lim(\overline{q(C)}\cap\mathfrak{t})\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}).

This proves that
\Delta^{+}
is admissible and that
q(X)\in C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}
. Hence the assertion follows.
∎

Example 1.2.11
.

Let
(V,\Omega)
be a symplectic vector space,
\mathfrak{h}(V,\Omega)=\mathbb{R}\oplus V
be the corresponding Heisenberg algebra and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega):=\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)\;|\;\Omega(v,Aw)+\Omega(Av,w)=0\;\text{for all}\;v,w\in V\}

be the corresponding symplectic Lie algebra. The prototypical example of an admissible Lie algebra which is not semi-simple, is given by the Jacobi algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{hsp}(V,\Omega):=\mathfrak{h}(V,\Omega)\rtimes\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega)
. The map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathfrak{hsp}(V,\Omega)\to C^{\infty}(V),\quad\Phi(s,v,A)(w):=\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega^{A}(w)+\Omega(v,w)+s,

for
s\in\mathbb{R},\;v,w\in V,\;A\in\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega)
, defines an injective homomorphism of Lie algebras if one equips
C^{\infty}(V)
with the Poisson-bracket corresponding to
\Omega
(cf.
[
Ne00
, Prop. A.IV.15]
). The image of
\Phi
coincides with the polynomials of degree
\leq 2
and it maps
\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega)
to polynomials of homogeneous degree
2
. Continuing onwards we identify
\mathfrak{hsp}(V,\Omega)\cong\text{im}(\Phi)
. Consider a symplectic basis

\displaystyle\{p_{j},q_{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq n:=\tfrac{1}{2}\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}V\}\quad\text{and define}\quad\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{l}}:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{p_{i}^{2}+q_{i}^{2}\;|\;1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{l}}
is a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}:=\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega)
. If one computes the root system
\Delta:=\Delta(\mathfrak{l}_{\mathbb{C}},(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{l}})_{\mathbb{C}})
as in
[
Ne00
, Ex. VII.2.30]
and chooses the up to a sign unique positive admissible system
\Delta^{+}
, then one has

\displaystyle C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})=\text{cone}(\{p_{i}^{2}+q_{i}^{2}\;|\;1\leq i\leq n\})=\{\Omega^{A}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{l}}\;|\;\Omega^{A}\geq 0\}.

Since there is up to a sign only one admissible positive system of non-compact roots
\Delta_{p}^{+}
in the simple hermitian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(V,\Omega)
(cf.
[
Ne00
, Lemma VII.2.16(iv)]
) and
\Omega^{A}>0
if and only if
A
is elliptic and all its eigenvalues are contained in
i\mathbb{R}^{+}
, this proves that the set of Gibbs elements consists of all elliptic symplectic endomorphisms for which the eigenvalues are contained in either
i\mathbb{R}^{+}
or
i\mathbb{R}^{-}
.

Then
[
Ne00
, Ex. VII.2.30]
) implies that
\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{\mathfrak{l}}
is a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{hsp}(V,\Omega)
for the corresponding admissible positive systems of roots
\Delta_{0}^{\pm}\subseteq\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
defined by extending the elements of

by zero on

which implies

which gets mapped to the set of strictly convex polynomials of degree

by

2
KMS states of topological groups

In this section, we discuss how results for KMS states of C*-algebras and W*-algebras can be transferred to topological groups. For general topological groups
G
, there exists a universal group W*-algebra
W^{\ast}(G)
. It has the property that, for a strongly continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)
, one obtains a
\sigma
-weakly continuous one-parameter group of *-automorphisms
\tau:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(W^{\ast}(G))
such that a state
\varphi
of
G
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state if and only if its corresponding state
\omega_{\varphi}
of
W^{\ast}(G)
is a
(\tau,\beta)
-KMS state (cf. Proposition
2.1.6
). As is the case for W*-dynamical systems, one does not have good control over the set of KMS states in general. For locally compact groups this is not the case, as the availability of the group C*-algebra
C^{\ast}(G)
has the consequence that the set
K_{\beta}
of
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS states of a locally compact topological group is a weak*-compact convex subset of
L^{\infty}(G)\cong L^{1}(G)^{\prime}
(cf. Theorem
2.2.4
). Therefore the Krein-Millman Theorem implies that
K_{\beta}
is the closed convex hull of its extremal points
\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})
. As a KMS state is extremal if and only if it is a factor state, it suffices to study factorial KMS states. For general topological groups
G
, we characterize semi-finite KMS states as generalized Gibbs states. This allows us to apply the results from Section
1
to factorial type I KMS states of finite-dimensional Lie groups
G
for one-parameter groups of inner automorphisms generated by
X\in\textbf{L}(G)
, which correspond to irreducible unitary representations
\rho
such that
\text{tr}(e^{i\beta\partial\rho(X)})<\infty
.

2.2
C*-methods for locally compact groups

Let
G
be a locally compact group. The
group
C^{*}
-algebra
of
G
is defined as the universal enveloping
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G):=C^{*}(L^{1}(G))
of the convolution algebra
L^{1}(G)
, i.e. the completion of
L^{1}(G)
with respect to the maximal
C^{*}
-norm given on
L^{1}(G)
by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{*}}:=\sup\{\|\pi(f)\|\;|\;\pi:L^{1}(G)\to B(\mathcal{H})\;\text{non-degenerate *-representation}\}.

Every non-degenerate *-representation
\pi:L^{1}(G)\to B(\mathcal{H})
extends uniquely to a continuous *-representation of
C^{\ast}(G)
and
\|\pi(f)\|\leq\|f\|_{C^{\ast}}
holds trivially for all
f\in L^{1}(G)
. We will usually also denote this extension by
\pi
. Note that non-degenerate *-representation are in one-to-one correspondence with unitary representations of
G
(cf.
[
DE09
, Prop. 6.2.3]
). This one-to-one correspondence yields the following one-to-one correspondence of states as they are uniquely determined by their cyclic GNS-representation and a fixed cyclic vector.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence of states
\varphi
of
G
and states
\omega
of
C^{*}(G)
established by
\Phi:\mathcal{S}(G)\to\mathcal{S}(C^{\ast}(G)),\;\varphi\mapsto\omega_{\varphi}
, where
\omega_{\varphi}
is uniquely determined by

\displaystyle\omega_{\varphi}(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x)dx,\quad\text{for}\quad f\in L^{1}(G).

If one equips
\mathcal{S}(G)
with the weak*-subspace topology from
\mathcal{S}(G)\subseteq L^{\infty}(G)\cong L^{1}(G)^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}(C^{\ast}(G))
with the weak*-topology of
C^{\ast}(G)^{\prime}
, then
\Phi
is affine linear and a weak*-continuous homeomorphism.

In order to get a one-to-one correspondence between KMS states of
C^{*}(G)
and KMS states of
G
, one needs to define a strongly continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms

\displaystyle\tau:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(C^{*}(G))\quad\text{corresponding to}\quad\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)

such that
\omega_{\varphi}
is a
(\tau,\beta)
-KMS state of
C^{\ast}(G)
if and only if
\varphi
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state of
G
. To do this, we note that the pullback
(\alpha_{t})_{*}\mu_{G}
of the Haar measure
\mu_{G}
of
G
is a left-invariant Radon measure and thus there exists a unique scalar
\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mu_{G}\circ\alpha_{-t}=:(\alpha_{t})_{*}\mu_{G}=\lambda_{t}\cdot\mu_{G}\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

It is an easy exercise to show that
\tau:\mathbb{R}\times L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G),\;\tau_{t}(f):=\lambda_{t}\cdot(f\circ\alpha_{-t})
extends to a strongly continuous one-parameter group of *-automorphisms of
C^{\ast}(G)
which we also denote by
\tau
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{1}(G)
, one has

\displaystyle\pi(\tau_{t}(f))=\int_{G}\lambda_{t}\cdot f(\alpha_{-t}(x))\pi(x)dx=\int_{G}f(x)\pi(\alpha_{t}(x))dx=(\pi\circ\alpha_{t})(f).

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact topological group and
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)

be a strongly continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms. For a state
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
, one has

\displaystyle\omega_{\varphi}(f\ast\tau_{t}(g))=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y)\,g(x)\,\varphi(y\alpha_{t}(x))dxdy,\quad\text{for}\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\;f,g\in L^{1}(G).

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\pi,\Omega)
be the GNS-representation of
\varphi
and
\omega_{\varphi}
. Using
\varphi(x)=\langle\Omega,\pi(x)\Omega\rangle
, we calculate
\omega_{\varphi}(f\ast\tau_{t}(g))
which is given by

\displaystyle\int_{G}(f\ast\tau_{t}(g))(x)\langle\Omega,\pi(x)\Omega\rangle dx=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y)\,\lambda_{t}\,g(\alpha_{-t}(y^{-1}x))\,\varphi(x)dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y)\,\lambda_{t}\,g(\alpha_{-t}(x))\,\varphi(yx)dxdy=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y)\,g(x)\,\varphi(y\alpha_{t}(x))dxdy.\qed

A state

BR87
, Subsection 4.1]
provides a decomposition theory. For a given Radon probability measure
\mu
on
K_{\beta}
,
[
BR87
, Prop. 4.1.1]
proves that there exists a unique point
b(\mu)=\int_{K}\omega\,d\mu(\omega)\in K_{\beta}
called the
barycenter
of
\mu
, such that

\displaystyle f(b(\mu))=\mu(f):=\int_{K}f(\omega)d\mu(\omega),

for all affine linear continuous functions
f:K_{\beta}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then
[
BR87
, Thm. 4.1.15]
establishes the importance of
K_{\beta}
being a simplex since it implies that every
\omega\in K
is the barycenter of a unique normalized Radon measure
\mu_{\omega}
maximal with respect to the partial order
\succ
defined by

\displaystyle\mu\succ\nu\quad:\Leftrightarrow\quad\mu(f)\geq\nu(f),\quad\text{for all}\;f:K\to\mathbb{R}\;\text{continuous and convex.}

For separable locally compact topological groups
G
the convolution algebra
L^{1}(G)
is separable which shows that
C^{\ast}(G)
is a separable C*-algebra. In particular,
K_{\beta}
is metrizable and thus
[
BR87
, Thm. 4.1.11]
implies that
\mu_{\omega}
is supported on the extremal points of
K_{\beta}
, i.e.
\mu_{\omega}(\text{Ext}(K_{\beta}))=1
. We thus obtain for each
\omega\in K_{\beta}
a unique decomposition

\displaystyle\omega=\int_{\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})}\omega^{\prime}d\mu_{\omega}(\omega^{\prime}).

For
f\in L^{1}(G)
, the function
\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})\times G\ni(\omega,x)\mapsto f(x)\varphi_{\omega}(x)
is clearly integrable, the measure space
\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})\times G
is
\sigma
-finite and thus from Fubini’s theorem, one obtains, for all
\omega_{0}\in K_{\beta}
, that

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(f)=\int_{\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})}\int_{G}f(x)\varphi_{\omega}(x)dxd\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\omega)=\int_{G}f(x)\int_{\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})}\varphi_{\omega}(x)d\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\omega)dx.

This implies that the state
\varphi_{\omega_{0}}
of
G
corresponding to
\omega_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\omega_{0}}(x)=\int_{\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})}\varphi_{\omega}(x)d\mu_{\omega_{0}}(\omega),\quad\text{for}\quad x\in G.

Since
L^{1}(G)\subseteq C^{\ast}(G)
is dense, it follows the evaluation functionals

\displaystyle\delta_{f}:K_{\beta}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\omega\mapsto\omega(f),\quad\text{for}\quad f\in L^{1}(G),

separate the points and thus the algebra generated by these functionals is dense in
C(K_{\beta})
with respect to the maximum norm by the Theorem of Stone-Weierstrass. This proves that
\mu_{\omega_{0}}
is already uniquely determined by these functionals. As the set
K_{\beta}
is convex and weak-
\ast
-closed every such measure defines an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state. Hence we have proven the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2.6
.

Let
G
be a separable locally compact topological group,
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)

be a strongly continuous one-parameter group and
\beta\neq 0
. For each
\varphi_{0}\in K_{\beta}
, there exists a Radon probability measure
\mu
on

\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})

uniquely determined by

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(x)=\int_{\text{Ext}(K_{\beta})}\varphi(x)d\mu(\varphi),\quad for\;all\quad x\in G.

Conversely every such measure defines an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state of
G
.

2.3
Semi-finite KMS states of topological groups

Consider an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state
\varphi
of a topological group
G
. If
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra, then there exists a unique faithful semi-finite normal trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
(cf.
[
Tak02
, Thm. 2.15]
). Since the faithful normal state
\omega_{\varphi}
is invariant under the modular automorphism group
\sigma_{t}^{\tau}=\text{id}_{\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}}
of the trace
\tau
, it follows from the Radon-Nikodym Theorem
[
Tak03
, Cor. 3.6]
that there exists a unique non-singular positive self-adjoint operator
h
affiliated to
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
such that
\omega_{\varphi}(A)=\tau(hA)
, for
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
. Then
[
Tak03
, Thm. 2.11]
implies that the modular automorphism group of
\omega_{\varphi}
is given by

\displaystyle\text{Ad}(h^{-it/\beta})=\text{Ad}(e^{itH}),\quad\text{for}\quad H=-\tfrac{1}{\beta}\log(h).

Furthermore one has
\tau(h)=\tau(e^{-\beta H})=\omega_{\varphi}(\mathds{1})=1
and
\varphi
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi(g)=\frac{\tau(\pi_{\varphi}(g)e^{-\beta H})}{\tau(e^{-\beta H})},\quad\text{for}\quad g\in G.

Suppose conversely that
\pi
is an
\alpha
-covariant cyclic unitary representation such that
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
is semi-finite and for which there exists a non-singular implementing operator
H
of
\alpha
affiliated to
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
which satisfies
\tau(e^{-\beta H})=1
, for the unique faithful semi-finite normal trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}=\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
. We want to show that this defines an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state of
G
by constructing a
(\sigma,\beta)
-KMS state, where
\sigma_{t}(A):=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}
for
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
. To do this, observe that
[
Tak02
, Thm. 2.22]
implies that the semi-finite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}^{\text{op}}
acts on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi},\tau)
, which is defined as the completion of the complex pre-Hilbert space

\displaystyle\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\;|\;\tau(|A|^{2})<\infty\}

with respect to the scalar product
\langle A,B\rangle:=\tau(A^{\ast}B)
, by left and right multiplication. If one denotes

\displaystyle\lambda:\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\to B(L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi},\tau)),\quad

\displaystyle\lambda(a)x=ax,\quad\text{for}\quad\tau(|x|^{2})<\infty

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\rho:\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}^{\text{op}}\to B(L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi},\tau)),\quad

\displaystyle\rho(a)x=xa,\quad\text{for}\quad\tau(|x|^{2})<\infty,

one has that
\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})^{\prime}=\rho(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})^{\prime\prime}
and
\rho(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})^{\prime}=\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})^{\prime\prime}
(cf.
[
Tak02
, Thm. 2.22]
).

In particular if
\Omega=e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta H}
, then
\Omega\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi},\tau)
is non-singular and self-adjoint. This implies that the vector
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
because by the Range-Kernel Lemma one has
\overline{\mathcal{R}(\Omega)}=\text{ker}(\Omega^{\ast})=\text{ker}(\Omega)=\penalty\ \{0\}
and therefore
A\Omega=0
implies
A=0
and
\Omega B=0
implies
(\Omega B)^{\ast}=B^{\ast}\Omega=0
, i.e.
B^{\ast}=0
and thus
B=0
, for
A,B\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
.

The corresponding modular objects
(\Delta,J)
are given on
\lambda(\mathcal{M})\Omega
by
J(A\Omega)=\Omega A^{\ast}
and
\Delta(A\Omega)=\Omega A
, for
A\in\Omega
and thus the corresponding modular automorphism group is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M},\quad A\mapsto e^{itH}Ae^{-itH},\quad\text{for}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

This implies that the state

\displaystyle\omega:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C},\quad A\mapsto\frac{\tau(Ae^{-\beta H})}{\tau(e^{-\beta H})}=\frac{\langle\Omega,A\Omega\rangle}{\|\Omega\|^{2}}

is a
(\sigma,\beta)
-KMS state. This proves that
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C},\;g\mapsto\omega(\pi(g))
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state. Hence one has the following proposition:

Proposition 2.3.1
.

Let
G
be a topological group,

\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)
be a strongly continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms. There is a one-to-one correspondence between
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS states
\varphi
such that
\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a semi-finite von Neumann algebras and
\alpha
-covariant cyclic unitary representation
\pi
such that
\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a semi-finite for which there exists an implementing operator
H
of
\alpha
affiliated to
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
which satisfies
\tau(e^{-\beta H})=1
, for the unique faithful semi-finite normal trace
\tau
on
\pi(G)^{\prime\prime}
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

If
\varphi
is a factorial type I
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state, then
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a type I von Neumann algebra and therefore there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and an isomorphism of von Neumann algebras
\Phi:\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}\to B(\mathcal{H})
. The unitary representation
\rho:=\Phi\circ\pi_{\varphi}
is irreducible since the
\sigma
-weak closure of
\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\rho(G))
is given by
\rho(G)^{\prime\prime}=\Phi(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi})=B(\mathcal{H})
. This proves that there exists an operator
H
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle e^{itH}\rho(g)e^{-itH}=\rho(\alpha_{t}(g))\quad\text{and}\quad\text{tr}(e^{-\beta H})<\infty

since the GNS-representation of
\varphi
is
\alpha
-covariant for the modular automorphism group, i.e.
\Delta^{it}\pi_{\varphi}(g)\Delta^{-it}=\pi_{\varphi}(\alpha_{t}(g))
and
\Phi\circ\pi_{\varphi}=\rho
. In particular

\displaystyle\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C},\quad\frac{\text{tr}(\rho(g)e^{-\beta H})}{\text{tr}(e^{-\beta H})},\quad\text{for}\quad g\in G,

is a Gibbs state corresponding to the irreducible unitary representation
\rho
of
G
. Since the representation
\rho
is unique, the implementing operator
H
is unique up to the addition of a real scalar.

Remark 2.3.3
.

By
[
Tak03
, Thm. 3.14]
a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is semi-finite if and only if for every faithful semi-finite normal weight
\psi
, the corresponding modular automorphism group
\sigma_{t}^{\psi}
is inner in the sense that there exists a self-adjoint operator
H
affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
such that
\sigma_{t}^{\psi}=\text{Ad}(e^{itH})
as automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
. For KMS states of topological groups, this innerness of the modular automorphism group translates to the extension of the factorial KMS state to the larger group
G^{\flat}:=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
. Let
\varphi
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state such that
\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
is a semi-finite von Neumann algebra and
H
the corresponding implementing operator affiliated to
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}:=\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
satisfying
\tau(e^{-\beta H})<\infty
. It follows that the unitary representation

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi}^{\flat}:G^{\flat}=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}\to U(\mathcal{H}),\quad(g,t)\mapsto\pi_{\varphi}(g)e^{itH}

is a semi-finite representation since
e^{itH}\in\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
implies
\pi_{\varphi}^{\flat}(G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R})^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\varphi}
. In particular

\displaystyle\varphi^{\flat}:G^{\flat}=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C},\quad(g,t)\mapsto\frac{\tau(\pi_{\varphi}^{\flat}(g,t)e^{-\beta H})}{\tau(e^{-\beta H})}

is an
(\alpha^{\flat},\beta)
-KMS state of
G^{\flat}=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha^{\flat}
is the one-parameter group of inner automorphisms of
G^{\flat}
defined by conjugation with
(e,t)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha^{\flat}(g,s)=(e,t)(g,s)(e,t)^{-1}=(\alpha_{t}(g),s),\quad\text{for}\quad g\in G,\;s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that the modular automorphism group which acts on
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}\cong L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\varphi},\tau)
by conjugation with
e^{itH}
and thus leaves the cyclic vector
\Omega=\frac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta H}}{\sqrt{\text{tr}(e^{-\beta H})}}
invariant, but the left multiplication by
e^{itH}
does not.

Theorem 2.3.4
.

Let
\beta>0
and suppose
G
is a finite dimensional Lie group and
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)

is given by
\alpha_{t}(g)=\exp(tX)g\exp(-tX)
, for some fixed

X\in\mathfrak{g}:=\textbf{L}(G)
and
g\in G
. Then the extremal type I
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS states are the extremal Gibbs states

\displaystyle\varphi(g)=\frac{\text{tr}(\rho(g)e^{i\beta\partial\rho(X)})}{\text{tr}(e^{i\beta\partial\rho(X)})},

where
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
G
such that
e^{i\beta\partial\rho(X)}
is of trace class. In particular, if
G
is simply connected, then there is a one-to-one correspondence between

(i)

extremal type I
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS states

(ii)

equivalence classes of irreducible unitary highest weight representations of admissible quotients
q:\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with respect to an admissible positive system
\Delta_{1}^{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that
q(X)
is conjugate to
C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{1,p}^{+})^{\circ}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Remark
2.3.2
since
\rho(\exp(tX))=e^{t\partial\rho(X)}=e^{it(-i\partial\rho(X))}
implies that the corresponding implementing operator
H
, which is unique up to addition by a real scalar, is w.l.o.g. given by
-i\partial\rho(X)
. Therefore
e^{i\beta\partial\rho(X)}
is of trace class and Corollary
1.2.9
applies if
G
is simply connected.
∎

Remark 2.3.5
.

If
G
is a finite dimensional Lie group and the action
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(G)
is not inner, then one considers
G^{\flat}=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\flat}:=\textbf{L}(G^{\flat})
. In order to classify all representations of Gibbs type, it suffices to classify all admissible quotients
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\flat}
and check if the image of
(0,1)\in\mathfrak{g}^{\flat}=\mathfrak{g}\rtimes\mathbb{R}
under the quotient map
q:\mathfrak{g}^{\flat}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
lies in the interior of
C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\circ}
, for an admissible positive system
\Delta^{+}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. If one then classifies the unitary highest weight representations of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
with respect to
\Delta^{+}
, one obtains in the same manner as above a representation of Gibbs type of
G
, and all representations of Gibbs type are obtained in this manner.

3
Perspectives and open problems

3.1
Extensions of KMS states of
G
to KMS states of
G^{\flat}

To completely understand how the extension of a topological group
G
to
G^{\flat}
affects the study of KMS states, it would be interesting to know if every
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state of
G
extends to an
(\alpha^{\flat},\beta)
-KMS state of
G^{\flat}=G\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
and if every KMS state of
G^{\flat}
arises in this manner. We have seen in Remark
2.3.3
that semi-finite
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS states of
G
extend to
(\alpha^{\flat},\beta)
-KMS states of
G^{\flat}
. For a not necessarily semi-finite
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state
\varphi
of
G
with GNS-representation
(\pi_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\Omega_{\varphi})
and
\mathcal{M}:=\pi_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime\prime}
, it is not clear if such KMS extensions always exist. It might even be possible that this extension property already implies that the state is semi-finite.

We considered the crossed product
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R}
, where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is the modular automorphism group of the associated state
\omega_{\varphi}
of
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
(cf.
[
Tak03
, Section X.1]
). This crossed product is defined as the von Neumann algebra generated by the sets of bounded linear operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
given by

\displaystyle(\pi_{\sigma}(A)f)(t):=\sigma_{-t}(A)f(t)\quad\text{and}\quad(\lambda(s)f)(t):=f(t-s),

for
A\in\mathcal{M},\;s,t\in\mathbb{R},\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
. Then one can consider the associated dual weight
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varphi}
of
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R}
and
[
Tak03
, Thm. X.1.17(ii)]
implies that the modular automorphism group
\widetilde{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}\to\text{Aut}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R})
of the dual weight
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varphi}
satisfies

\widetilde{\sigma}_{t}(\pi_{\sigma}(A))=\pi_{\sigma}(\sigma_{t}(A)),\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\sigma}_{t}(\lambda(s))=\lambda(s),\quad\text{for}\quad A\in\mathcal{M},\;s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

For the unitary representation
\rho
of
G^{\flat}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
defined as
\rho(g,t):=\pi_{\sigma}(\pi_{\varphi}(g))\lambda(t)
, the relation
\pi_{\varphi}(\alpha_{t}(g)))=\sigma_{t}(\pi_{\varphi}(g))
and (
2
) imply

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha_{t}^{\flat}(g,s))=\rho(\alpha_{t}(g),s)=\widetilde{\sigma}_{t}\left(\rho(g,s)\right),\quad\text{for}\quad g\in G,\;s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

But dual weight
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varphi}
of a state does not define a state of the crossed product and thus one cannot naively define the extension of
\varphi
as
\varphi^{\flat}(g,t):=\widetilde{\omega}_{\varphi}(\rho(g,t))
for
g\in G,\;t\in\mathbb{R}
. This does not work as the dual weight does not define a state. To see that, observe that
[
Tak03
, Thm. X.1.17(i)]
implies that, for a compactly supported
\mathcal{M}
-valued continuous function
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
and for the representation
\widetilde{\pi}_{\sigma}
of
C_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}_{\sigma}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda(s)\pi_{\sigma}(f(s))ds,\quad\text{one has}\quad\widetilde{\omega}_{\varphi}(\widetilde{\pi}_{\sigma}(f))=\omega_{\varphi}(f(e))=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},f(e)\Omega_{\varphi}\rangle.

As
\widetilde{\pi}_{\sigma}(C_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M}))
generates
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
Tak03
, Lemma X.1.10(iii)]
), this implies that the dual weight does not define a state of the cross product
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R}
since delta distributions do not define states as the passage from
C_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
suggests. Therefore this attempt does not seem to work but it illustrates a few key structures.

3.2
Factorial Type II KMS states of Lie groups

We are not aware of how type II factor representations
\pi
of finite dimensional Lie groups
G
look like. For us, the more natural context of type II factor representation is given by inductive limits of Lie groups which then act for example an the hyperfinite
\text{II}_{1}
factor defined as a infinite product of two by two matrix algebras (cf.
[
Po67
]
). For KMS states of the inductive limit group
U(\infty)=\varinjlim_{n}U(n)
this has been done in
[
St78
]
whereas general factor representations of
U(\infty)
are studied in
[
SV75
]
. A survey of these results is given in
[
SV82
]
. Nonetheless it would still be interesting to know what conditions does
\tau(e^{i\beta\partial\pi(X)})<\infty
implies for finite dimensional Lie groups
G
, representation
\pi
and generator
X\in\mathfrak{g}
? Clearly, the spectrum of
H=-i\partial\pi(X)
does not need to be semi-bounded anymore as in type II factors there exists a sequence of non-zero mutually orthogonal projections
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
0<\tau(p_{n})\to 0
. Instead of the semi-boundedness of
\text{spec}(H)
on has that the spectrum decreases exponentially fast in the sense that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\beta x}d\tau(P_{H}(x))<\infty
holds, i.e.
\tau(P_{H}((-\infty,t_{0}]))\leq e^{\beta t_{0}}\tau(e^{-\beta H})
goes to
0
exponentially as
t\to-\infty
.

Appendix A
Appendix

A.1
Compactly embedded Cartan subalgebras and admissible Lie algebras

Definition A.1.1
.

We call a subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}

compactly embedded
if
\overline{\langle e^{\text{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{a})}\rangle}\subseteq\text{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
is compact. We call an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
elliptic if the subalgebra
\mathbb{R}\cdot x\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is compactly embedded and denote with
\text{comp}(\mathfrak{g})
the subset of elliptic elements in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark A.1.2
.

(cf.
[
Ne00
, Thm. VII.2.2]
):

If
\mathfrak{t}
is a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra, then it is nilpotent and compact, i.e. abelian. Therefore the subset
\text{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
is an abelian subalgebra consisting of diagonalizable elements since the compact group
\text{INN}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t})
is a compact subgroup of the compact subgroup O
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
of
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
for some scalar product on
\mathfrak{g}
. Hence one obtains a root decomposition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;[t,X]=\alpha(t)X,\;\text{for all}\;t\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}\}
and
\Delta:=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}\;|\;\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}
. On the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
we define the involutive antihomomorphism

\displaystyle(X+iY)^{\ast}:=-X+iY\quad\text{for}\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}

and observe that
\mathfrak{g}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;X^{\ast}=-X\}
. It follows from
[
Ne00
, Thm. VII.2.2]
that for
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
, one has
Z^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{-\alpha}
and
[Z,Z^{\ast}]\in i\mathfrak{t}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}(Z):=\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Z,Z^{\ast},[Z,Z^{\ast}]\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}(Z):=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}(Z)\cap\mathfrak{g}

For roots
\alpha\in\Delta
, one now distinguishes between the following cases:

(NS)

There exists an element
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
such that
\alpha([Z,Z^{\ast}])<0
(the non-compact simple type). Then
\mathfrak{g}(Z)\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

(CS)

There exists an element
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
such that
\alpha([Z,Z^{\ast}])>0
(the compact simple type). Then
\mathfrak{g}(Z)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)
.

(N)

\alpha([Z,Z^{\ast}])=0
for all
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
and there exists
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
such that
[Z,Z^{\ast}]\neq 0
(the nilpotent type). Then
\mathfrak{g}(Z)
is isomorphic to the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.

(A)

[Z,Z^{\ast}]=0
for all
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
(the abelian type). Then
\mathfrak{g}(Z)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

That only these four cases occur follows from
[
Ne00
, Lemma VII.2.3]
which implies
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}=1
, for all
\alpha\in\Delta
of simple type, and thus conditions (NS) and (CS) hold for all
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
if they hold for one.

Definition A.1.3
.

(cf.
[
Ne00
, Def. VII.2.4]
and
[
Ne00
, Def. VII.2.6]
):

Let
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra.

(i)

We call a root
\alpha\in\Delta

semisimple
if it is of simple type and denote the set of semisimple roots by
\Delta_{s}
. The set of
solvable roots
is denoted by
\Delta_{r}:=\Delta\setminus\Delta_{k}
.

(ii)

We call a root
\alpha\in\Delta

compact
if there exists
Z\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
such that
\alpha([Z,Z^{\ast}])<0
. All other roots are called
non-compact
and the set of all non-compact roots is denoted with
\Delta_{p}
and the set of compact roots as
\Delta_{k}
. We also define the non-compact semisimple roots
\Delta_{p,s}:=\Delta_{p}\cap\Delta_{s}
.

(iii)

A subset
\Delta^{+}\subseteq\Delta
is called a
positive system
if there exists a regular element
X_{0}\in i\mathfrak{t}
such that

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}=\{\alpha\in\Delta\;|\;\alpha(X_{0})>0\}.

If
\Delta^{+}
is such a system of positive roots we define
\Delta_{k}^{+},\Delta_{p}^{+},\Delta_{p,s}^{+}
and
\Delta_{r}^{+}
as the intersection of the respective subset with
\Delta^{+}
.

(iv)

We call a positive system
\Delta^{+}

adapted
if for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{k}
and
\beta\in\Delta_{p}^{+}
one has

\displaystyle\beta(X_{0})>\alpha(X_{0}),

for some
X_{0}
defining
\Delta^{+}
.

Definition A.1.4
.

(cf.
[
Ne00
, Def. VIII.2.6]
):

Let
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra and
\Delta^{+}
be a corresponding system of positive roots. We associate to the positive system of non-compact roots
\Delta_{p}^{+}
the convex cones

\displaystyle C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})

\displaystyle:=\text{cone}(\{i[Z_{\alpha},Z_{\alpha}^{\ast}]\;|\;Z_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha},\;\alpha\in\Delta_{p}^{+}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})

\displaystyle:=(i\Delta_{p}^{+})^{\star}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{t}\;|\;i\alpha(X)\geq 0\;\text{for all}\;\alpha\in\Delta_{p}^{+}\}.

If it is clear which system of positive non-compact roots
\Delta_{p}^{+}
is considered, then we simply write
C_{\text{min}}
, resp.
C_{\text{max}}
, for
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
, resp.
C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})
.

Definition A.1.5
.

We call a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

admissible
if
\mathfrak{g}
contains an invariant closed generating convex subset
C
which does not contain any affine line.

Theorem A.1.6
.

(
Sandwich Theorem

[
Ne00
, Thm. VII.3.8]
)

Let
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra and
\emptyset\neq C\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
a closed generating invariant elliptic convex subset. Then there exists a uniquely determined adapted positive system
\Delta_{p}^{+}
of non-compact roots such that
C\cap\mathfrak{t}\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}).

Moreover, we also have
C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq\lim(C\cap\mathfrak{t})
.
If, in addition,
C
is a cone, then this means that

\displaystyle C_{\text{min}}(\Delta_{p}^{+})\subseteq C\cap\mathfrak{t}\subseteq C_{\text{max}}(\Delta_{p}^{+}).

A.2
Convex geometric and representation-theoretic concepts

Definition A.2.1
.

(cf.
[
Ne00
, Def. V.1.1]
and
[
Ne00
, Prop. V.1.6]
):

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space and
W\subseteq V
be a closed convex cone.

(i)

For a subset
E\subseteq V
, we put

\displaystyle B(E):=\{\omega\in V^{\ast}\;|\;\inf\omega(E)>-\infty\}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{\star}:=\{\omega\in V^{\ast}\;|\;\omega(E)\subseteq[0,\infty)\}.

(ii)

We define, for a convex subset
C\subseteq V
, the
recession cone

\lim(C):=\{v\in V\;|\;v+C\subseteq C\}
.

(iii)

For
I\subseteq V^{\ast}
, we define
I^{\bot}:=\{v\in V\;|\;\omega(v)=0\;\text{for all}\;\omega\in I\}
.

(iv)

For a subset
E\subseteq V
, we define
\text{algint}(E)
as the interior of
E
with respect to the affine subspace spanned by
E
.

Remark A.2.2
.

We remark the following:

(i)

E^{\star}
is always closed but
B(E)
is not closed in general.

(ii)

One has
B\left(\overline{\text{conv}(E)}\right)=B(E)
.

(iii)

B(E)^{\star}=\lim(E)
for
E
convex (cf.
[
Ne00
, Prop. V.1.14]
)

Definition A.2.3
.

(cf.
[
Ne00
, Def. X.1.2]
):

Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of a Lie group
G
with Lie algebra
\textbf{L}(G)=\mathfrak{g}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
be the dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
of smooth vectors on which
\mathfrak{g}
acts as
d\pi(X)(v):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\pi(\exp_{G}(tX))v
for
X\in\mathfrak{g},\quad v\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. Then we define the
momentum map
of the unitary representation
\pi
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{\pi}:\mathds{P}(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}):=(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}\setminus\{0\})/{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\quad\Phi_{\pi}([v])(X):=-i\frac{\langle v,d\pi(X)v\rangle}{\langle v,v\rangle}

and the
momentum set

I_{\pi}
as the closed convex hull of the image of
\Phi_{\pi}
.

Remark A.2.4
.

If
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
is a unitary representation of a Lie group
G
we consider the cone

\displaystyle B(I_{\pi})=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\inf\langle I_{\pi},X\rangle>-\infty\}.

This cone is invariant since
I_{\pi}
is invariant under the coadjoint action (cf.
[
Ne00
, Lemma X.1.3]
) and
[
Ne00
, Lemma X.1.6]
implies that

\displaystyle B(I_{\pi})=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\sup(\text{spec}(i\cdot d\pi(X)))<\infty\}.

Definition A.2.5
.

We call a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\ast)
an
involutive Lie algebra
if
\mathfrak{g}
is a complex Lie algebra and
\ast:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an antilinear antiautomorphism, i.e.
[x^{\ast},y^{\ast}]=-[x,y]^{\ast}
, holds for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition A.2.6
.

We say that an involutive Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\ast)
has a
root decomposition
if there exists a
\ast
-invariant subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and a subset
\Delta\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
such that
\alpha(h^{\ast})=\overline{\alpha(h)}
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha},

for
\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\;|\;\text{ad}(h)(X)=\alpha(h)X\;\text{for all}\;h\in\mathfrak{h}\}\neq\{0\}
.

Definition A.2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\ast)
be an involutive Lie algebra. We call a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V

unitary
if there exists a positive definite hermitian form
h:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
that is also contravariant, i.e. it satisfies
h(X.v,w)=h(v,X^{\ast}.w)
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v,w\in V
.

Definition A.2.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\ast)
be an involutive Lie algebra with root decomposition and
\Delta^{+}\subseteq\Delta
be a positive system. We call a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
a
highest weight module
with respect to
\Delta^{+}
if there exists a linear functional

\displaystyle\lambda:\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}\to\mathbb{C}

and a cyclic vector
v\in V
such that
X.v=\lambda(X)v
holds for all
X\in\mathfrak{b}
. We call
\lambda
the
highest weight
of
V
and
v
a
highest weight vector.

Definition A.2.9
.

An irreducible unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of a connected Lie group
G
is called a
unitary highest weight representation
if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
contains a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and if
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is the unique maximal compactly embedded subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{t}
(cf.
[
Ne00
, Prop. VII.25]
) and
K:=\exp(\mathfrak{k})
, then the space
\mathcal{H}^{K,\infty}
of smooth
K
-finite vectors is a highest weight module for the involutive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
with involution
(X+iY)^{\ast}=-X+iY
, for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

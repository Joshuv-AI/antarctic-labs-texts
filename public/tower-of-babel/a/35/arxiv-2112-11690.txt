\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left\|xu(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma+2b)}{\sigma+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma+2}dx.}

(5.2)

In order to recall the localized virial estimates, we introduce a function
\theta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

\theta(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{r^{2},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;0\leq r\leq 1,}\\
{\textrm{0},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;r\geq 2,}\end{array}\right.\textrm{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \theta^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq 2\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for}\penalty\ \penalty\ r\geq 0.

(5.3)

For
R>1
, we define the radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\varphi_{R}(x)=\varphi_{R}(r):=R^{2}\theta(r/R),\;r=|x|.

(5.4)

The authors in
[
17
]
proved the following localized virial estimate for
\sigma_{0}<\sigma<\sigma_{1}
, but the the proof and so the result are still valid for
0<\sigma\leq\sigma_{1}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Localized Virial Estimate,
[
17
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
0<b<2
,
R>1
,
0<\sigma\leq\sigma_{1}
and
\varphi_{R}
be as in (
5.4
). Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a solution to the focusing INLS equation (
1.14
). Then for any
t\in I
,

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq 8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma+2b)}{\sigma+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma+2}dx}+CR^{-2}+CR^{-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\sigma+2}.

(5.5)

Next, we recall the sharp Hardy-Sobolev embedding inequality associated with the focusing energy-critical INLS equation (
1.14
). For example, see
[
35
]
or Theorem 15.2.2 in
[
36
]
.

Lemma 5.3

(Sharp Hardy-Sobolev embedding inequality)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
0<b<2
and
\sigma_{1}=\frac{4-2b}{n-2}
. Then we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{|x|^{-b}\left|f\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}+2}}\leq C_{HS}\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

(5.6)

for all
f\in\dot{H}^{1}
, where the sharp Hardy-Sobolev constant
C_{HS}
defined by

C_{HS}(b,c)=\inf_{f\in\dot{H}^{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}{\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{\left(\int{|x|^{-b}\left|f\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}+2}}}}.

(5.7)

is attained by function
W_{b}(x)
given in (
1.16
).

Lemma 2.2 in
[
35
]
also shows that
W_{b}
solves the equation

\Delta W_{b}+|x|^{-b}\left|W_{b}\right|^{\sigma_{1}}W_{b}=0,

and satisfies

\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}=\int{|x|^{-b}W_{b}^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}.

(5.8)

Hence, we have

\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}=\int{|x|^{-b}W_{b}^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}=[C_{HS}]^{-\frac{2(n-b)}{2-b}},\penalty\ \left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}=[C_{HS}]^{-(\sigma_{1}+2)},

(5.9)

E\left(W_{b}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|W_{b}\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}=\frac{2-b}{2(n-b)}[C_{HS}]^{-\frac{2(n-b)}{2-b}}.

(5.10)

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.13
.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

We divide the study in two cases:
E(u_{0})<0
and
E(u_{0})\geq 0

Case 1.
We consider the case
E(u_{0})<0
.

Let us prove the first part. If
T_{*},\penalty\ T^{*}<\infty
, then we are done. If
T^{*}=\infty
, then we prove that there exists
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\left\|u(t_{n})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Assume by contradiction that it doesn’t hold, i.e.
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\leq M_{0}
for some
M_{0}>0
. By the conservation of mass, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}}\leq M_{1},

for some
M_{1}>0
. Using the localized virial estimate (
5.5
) and the conservation of energy, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq&8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}+CR^{-2}+CR^{-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\sigma_{1}+2}\\
=&4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u_{0}\right)-2(n\sigma_{1}-4+2b)\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+CR^{-2}+CR^{-b}M_{1}^{\sigma+2},\end{split}

for any
t\in[0,\infty)
. Since
n\sigma_{1}-4+2b>0
, we take
R>1
large enough to have that

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq 2(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u_{0}\right)<0,

for any
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Integrating this estimate, there exists
t_{0}>0
sufficiently large such that
V_{\varphi_{R}}(t_{0})<0
which is impossible. Similarly, if
T_{*}=\infty
, then we can prove that there exists
t_{n}\to-\infty
such that
\left\|u(t_{n})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. This completes the proof of the first part.

Next, we prove the second part.

\cdot

Finite-variance data.
Let us assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}
. Applying the standard virial identity (
5.2
) and the conservation of energy, we have for any
t\in(-T_{*},\;T^{*})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left\|xu(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\\
&=4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u(t)\right)-2(n\sigma_{1}-4+2b)\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}<0,\end{split}

where we used the fact
n\sigma_{1}-4+2b>0
. By the classical argument of Glassey
[
37
]
, it follows that the solution
u
blows up in finite time.

\cdot

Radially symmetric data.
Let us assume in addition that
u_{0}
is radially symmetric.
Using Lemma 3.4 of
[
15
]
and the conservation of energy, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
t
in the existence time,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq&8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\\
&+O\left(R^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}}R^{-{\frac{2\left[(n-1)\sigma_{1}+2b\right]}{4-\sigma_{1}}}}+\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\\
=&4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u_{0}\right)-2(n\sigma_{1}-4+2b)\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\\
&+O\left(R^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}}R^{-{\frac{2\left[(n-1)\sigma_{1}+2b\right]}{4-\sigma_{1}}}}+\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

Since
n\sigma_{1}-4+2b>0
, we take
\varepsilon>0
small enough and
R>1
large enough depending on
\varepsilon
to have that

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq 2(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u_{0}\right)<0,

for any
t\in(-T_{*},\;T^{*})
. This shows that the solution
u
must blow up in finite time.

\cdot

Cylindrically symmetric data.
Let us assume in addition that
b\geq 4-n
and
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{n}
. We define the radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
:

\psi_{R}(y)=\psi_{R}(r):=R^{2}\theta(r/R),\;r=|y|,

(5.11)

where
R>1
and
\theta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is given in (
5.3
).
We define the function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\phi_{R}(x)=\phi_{R}(y,x_{n}):=\psi_{R}(y)+x_{n}^{2}.

(5.12)

It follows from (4.12) of
[
17
]
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\phi_{R}}(t)\leq 4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)&E\left(u(t)\right)-2(n\sigma_{1}-4+2b)\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\\
&+CR^{-2}+\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{CR^{-\frac{n-2}{2}-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;\sigma_{1}=2,}\\
{CR^{-\frac{(n-2)\sigma_{1}}{4}-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+CR^{-\frac{(n-2)\sigma_{1}}{4}-b},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;\sigma_{1}<2,}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

for any
t\in(-T_{*},\;T^{*})
. Note that
\sigma_{1}\leq 2
is equivalent to
b\geq 4-n
.
Since
n\sigma_{1}-4+2b>0
, we take
R>1
large enough to have that

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\phi_{R}}(t)\leq 2(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u_{0}\right)<0,

for any
t\in(-T_{*},\;T^{*})
. This shows that the solution
u
blows up in finite time.

Case 2.
We consider the case
E(u_{0})\geq 0
. Let us assume that
E(u_{0})<E(W_{b})
and
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}>\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.
By definition of the energy and the sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}E\left(u(t)\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{[C_{HS}]^{\sigma_{1}+2}}{\sigma_{1}+2}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\sigma_{1}+2}=:g\left(\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right),\end{split}

where

g(y)=\frac{1}{2}y^{2}-\frac{[C_{HS}]^{\sigma_{1}+2}}{\sigma_{1}+2}y^{\sigma_{1}+2}.

(5.13)

Lemma
5.3
shows that

g\left(\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)=E(W_{b}).

By the conservation of energy and the assumption
E(u_{0})<E(W_{b})
, we can see that

g\left(\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\leq E\left(u(t)\right)=E\left(u_{0}\right)<E(W_{b}).

By the assumption
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}>\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
and the continuity argument, we have

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}>\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

(5.14)

for any
t
as long as the solution exists. We next improve (
5.14
) as follows. Pick
\delta>0
small enough such that

E(u_{0})\leq(1-\delta)E(W_{b}),

(5.15)

which implies that

g\left(\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\leq(1-\delta)E(W_{b}).

(5.16)

Using (
5.9
), (
5.10
) and (
5.16
), we have

\frac{n-b}{2-b}\left(\frac{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)^{2}-\frac{n-2}{2-b}\left(\frac{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\right)^{\sigma_{1}+2}\leq 1-\delta.

The continuity argument shows that there exits
\delta^{\prime}>0
depending on
\delta
such that

\frac{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}{\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}}\geq 1+\delta^{\prime}.

(5.17)

Then we can take
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that

8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}+\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq-c<0,

(5.18)

for any
t
in the existence time. Indeed, by the definition of energy, we have

\textrm{LHS\eqref{GrindEQ__5_18_}}=4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E\left(u(t)\right)+(8+\varepsilon-2n\sigma_{1}-4b)\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Using the conservation of energy, (
5.9
), (
5.10
), (
5.15
) and (
5.17
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textrm{LHS\eqref{GrindEQ__5_18_}}&\leq 4(1-\delta)(n\sigma_{1}+2b)E(W_{b})+(8+\varepsilon-2n\sigma_{1}-4b)(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\\
&=\left\|W_{b}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left[\frac{8(2-b)}{n-2}\left(1-\delta-(1+\delta^{\prime})^{2}\right)+\varepsilon(1+\delta^{\prime})^{2}\right].\end{split}

Hence, by taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we can get (
5.18
).

Let us prove the first part. Let us consider only positive times. The one for negative times is similar. If
T^{*}<\infty
, then we are done. If
T^{*}=\infty
, then we prove that there exists
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\left\|u(t_{n})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Assume by contradiction that it doesn’t hold. Then, as in Case 1, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}{\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}}\leq M_{1}

for some
M_{1}>0
. Using the localized virial estimate (
5.5
) and the conservation of energy, we have

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq 8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}+CR^{-2}+CR^{-b}M_{1}^{\sigma_{1}+2},

for any
t\in[0,\infty)
. Taking
R>1
large enough, (
5.18
) implies

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq-c/2<0,

for any
t\in[0,\infty)
, which is impossible. This completes the proof of the first part.

Next, we prove the second part.

\cdot

Finite-variance data.

Let us assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}
. Using the standard virial identity (
5.2
) and (
5.18
), we have

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left\|xu(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\leq-c<0,

which implies that the solution blows up in finite time.

\cdot

Radially symmetric data.
Let us assume in addition that
u_{0}
is radially symmetric.
Using Lemma 3.4 of
[
15
]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq&8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\\
&+O\left(R^{-2}+\epsilon^{-\frac{\sigma}{4-\sigma}}R^{-{\frac{2\left[(n-1)\sigma+2b\right]}{4-\sigma}}}+\epsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

for any
\epsilon>0
and any
t
in the existence time. Taking
\epsilon>0
small enough and
R>1
large enough depending on
\epsilon
, it follows from (
5.18
) that

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\varphi_{R}}(t)\leq-c/2<0.

This shows that the solution must blow up in finite time.

\cdot

Cylindrically symmetric data.
Let us assume in addition that
b\geq 4-n
and
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{n}
.
It follows from (4.12) of
[
17
]
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\phi_{R}}(t)\leq 8\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}&-\frac{4(n\sigma_{1}+2b)}{\sigma_{1}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}\left|u(t,x)\right|^{\sigma_{1}+2}dx}\\
&+CR^{-2}+\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{CR^{-\frac{n-2}{2}-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;\sigma_{1}=2,}\\
{CR^{-\frac{(n-2)\sigma_{1}}{4}-b}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+CR^{-\frac{(n-2)\sigma_{1}}{4}-b},\penalty\ \textrm{if}\;\sigma_{1}<2,}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

for any

. Taking
R>1
large enough, (
5.18
) shows that

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{\phi_{R}}(t)\leq-c/2<0,

which implies that the solution blows up in finite time.
This completes the proof.

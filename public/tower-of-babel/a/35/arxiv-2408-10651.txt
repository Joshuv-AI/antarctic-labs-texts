Towards an edge-coloured Corrádi–Hajnal theorem
Allan Lo, Ella Williams

Abstract
A classical result of Corrádi and Hajnal states that every graph G G on n n vertices with n ∈ 3 ​ ℕ n\in~3\mathbb{N} and δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ n / 3 \delta(G)\geq 2n/3 contains a perfect triangle-tiling, i.e., a spanning set of vertex-disjoint triangles. We explore a generalisation of this result to edge-coloured graphs. Let G G be an edge-coloured graph on n n vertices. The minimum colour degree δ c ​ ( G ) \delta^{c}(G) of G G is the largest integer k k such that, for every vertex v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , there are at least k k distinct colours on edges incident to v v . We show that if δ c ​ ( G ) ≥ ( 5 / 6 + ε ) ​ n \delta^{c}(G)\geq(5/6+\varepsilon)n , then G G has a spanning set of vertex-disjoint rainbow triangles. On the other hand, we find an example showing the bound should be at least 5 ​ n / 7 5n/7 . We also discuss a related tiling problems on digraphs, which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

Given graphs

The following proposition allows us to convert edge-coloured graphs to digraphs, which was introduced by Czygrinow, Molla and Nagle

A system of equations

A

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in e\in E(\mathcal{F})}\omega^{*}(e)

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in e}\omega(v)

\displaystyle\geq 1\textrm{ for all $e\in E(\mathcal{F})$,}

\displaystyle\min_{v\in V(\mathcal{F})}\omega(v)

\displaystyle=0,

Let

be the adjacency matrix of

with rows and columns representing

(6.1)

Remarks

We would like to know the minimum colour degree threshold for rainbow-
K_{r}
-tilings.
We conjecture that the construction given by Proposition
1.4
is sharp.

Conjecture 8.1
.

Let
1/n\ll 1/r
with
n\equiv 0\pmod{r}
.
Let
G
be an edge-coloured graph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{c}(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r^{2}-2}\right)n
.
Then
G
contains a perfect rainbow-

-tiling.

When
r=3
we can prove that an absorption lemma already exists when the minimum colour degree is at least
(2/3+\gamma)n
, which is much less than the conjectured bound.
See Lemma
A.1
in Appendix.
Therefore in order to prove Conjecture
8.1
for
r=3
, it suffices to find an almost perfect rainbow-
K_{3}
-tiling (or a perfect fractional
(3,1)
-tiling in the digraph setting). Note that our proof of
Lemma

6.7
still works with
\delta^{c}(G)\geq(0.832+\gamma)n
, a minor improvement on
5/6
.
However we believe this can be lowered further, but new ideas would be needed.

For
r\geq 5
, we are unable to obtain any ‘reasonable’ upper bound for the existence of a rainbow-
K_{r}
-tiling.
One reason is that we are unaware of a density version of the Hajnal–Szemeredi theorem, that is, the Turán number for
t
vertex-disjoint
K_{r-1}
.

In this paper, we only focus on rainbow-
K_{r}
-tilings.

-tiling is a properly coloured

-tiling, so one could ask the minimum colour degree threshold of a properly coloured-

-tiling instead.
However, we feel that it should be the same asymptotically as the one for rainbow-
K_{r}
-tilings.
Using Proposition

, one can consider the corresponding digraph problem.
By the regularity lemma and Corollary
5.2
, finding a properly coloured

by Lemma

Generalising

Let

What is the minimum out-degree threshold that guarantees a perfect

Lemma A.4
.

Let
1/s\ll 1/n
.
Let
G
be a digraph on
n

\delta^{+}(G)>2n/3
.
Then
G
is weakly
s

Note that

is a function of

However, we will not track

This is because we will apply Lemma

to the reduced graph which has a constant number of vertices.
By Fact

, weakly connected is an “equivalent relationship”.
We first show that if a vertex

has large in-degree, then its neighbourhood is weakly connected. We will denote by

and

the neighbourhood and degree of

in

Proposition A.5
.

Let

be a digraph on

\delta^{+}(G)=\delta n
.
Let

Proof.

First we show that
N^{+}(x)
is weakly connected.
For

\displaystyle|N^{+}(y)\cap N^{+}(y^{\prime})\cap N^{-}(x)|\geq d^{+}(y)+d^{+}(y^{\prime})+d^{-}(x)-2n>0.

Let
z\in N^{+}(y)\cap N^{+}(y^{\prime})\cap N^{-}(x)
.
Each of
G[xyz]
and
G[xy^{\prime}z]
contains a copy of
C_{3}
, implying that
y
and
y^{\prime}
are weakly connected.
Thus
N^{+}(x)
is weakly connected.

Note that
d^{\pm}(x)>d^{+}(x)+d^{-}(x)-n>(1-\delta)n
.
For all
z\in N^{-}(x)
, we have
N^{+}(z)\cap N^{\pm}(x)\neq\emptyset
.
Given any two

, there exist
y,y^{\prime}\in N^{\pm}(x)
such that

and

span a copy of

.
Recall that
y
and
y^{\prime}
is weakly connected (as

), so

and
z^{\prime}
is weakly connected by Fact
A.3
(ii)
.
Hence
N^{-}(x)
is weakly connected.
Since
N^{-}(x)\cap N^{+}(x)=N^{\pm}(x)\neq\emptyset

A.3
(i)
.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

Let
G
be a digraph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{+}(G)=\delta n
.

is weakly connected and

Consider any

Note that

\displaystyle|N^{+}(y)\cap N^{-}(x)\cap X|\geq\delta^{+}(G)+d^{-}(x)+|X|-2n>0.

For each

forms a

. Fact

(iii)

is weakly connected. Since this holds for all

A.3
(i)
.
∎

Proposition A.7

Let
G
be a digraph on
n
vertices with
\delta^{+}(G)=\delta n
.
Suppose that
X\subseteq V(G)
is weakly connected and
\mathcal{K}_{3,1}(G[X])\neq\emptyset

Let

Let

Hence

(iii)
.
∎

(A.2)

\displaystyle=e(G)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d^{-}(v)\leq|X||T|+2(1-\delta)n|Z|+((1-\delta)n+|Y|)|W|+(1-\delta)n|Y|

\displaystyle=(n-|Z\cup W\cup Y|)(n-|Y|)+2(1-\delta)n|Z|+((1-\delta)n+|Y|)|W|+(1-\delta)n|Y|

\displaystyle|Y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta(2-3\delta)n^{2}<0,

(A.4)

Consider

\displaystyle d^{-}(z,V(G)\setminus T)

(A.5)

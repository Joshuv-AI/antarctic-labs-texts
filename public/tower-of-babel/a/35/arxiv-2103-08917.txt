REFLECTED BSDEs AND DOUBLY REFLECTED BSDEs DRIVEN BY RCLL MARTINGALES
Tianyang Nie, Marek Rutkowski

Abstract
We prove some new results on reflected BSDEs and doubly reflected BSDEs driven by a multi-dimensional RCLL martingale. The goal is to develop a general multi-asset framework encompassing a wide spectrum of nonlinear financial models, including as particular cases the setups studied by Peng and Xu [ 55 ] and Dumitrescu et al. [ 13 ] who dealt with BSDEs driven by a one-dimensional Brownian motion and a purely discontinuous martingale with a single jump. Our results are not covered by existing literature on reflected and doubly reflected BSDEs driven by a Brownian motion and a Poisson random measure. Keywords: backward stochastic differential equation, RCLL martingale, reflected BSDE AMS Subject Classification: 60H10, 60H30, 91G30, 91G40

2
Preliminaries

We start by describing the setup and assumptions introduced in
[
52
]
where BSDEs driven by RCLL martingales are studied. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions of right-continuity and completeness. The initial
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is assumed to be trivial. For any two processes, say
X
and
Y
, we denote the equality
X=Y
as
\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=Y_{t},\,\forall\,t\in[0,T])=1
. For any process
X
, we set
X_{0-}=0
by convention, and
\Delta X_{0}:=X_{0}-X_{0-}=X_{0}
is the jump of
X
at time 0. Moreover, the integral
\int_{s}^{t}X_{u}\,dY_{u}
is interpreted as
\int_{]s,t]}X_{u}\,dY_{u}
where
]s,t]=\{u\in[0,T]:s<u\leq t\}
.

We assume that
M=((M^{1}_{t},M^{2}_{t},\ldots,M^{n}_{t})^{\ast},\,t\in[0,T])
is an
n
-dimensional, square-integrable martingale on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
. Since the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is right-continuous, an RCLL modification of any
\mathbb{F}
-martingale is known to exist and thus it is assumed throughout that
M
is an RCLL process. We denote by
\langle M\rangle
(resp.
[M]
) the predictable covariation process (resp. the covariation process ) of
M
. As in
[
8
,
18
,
51
,
52
]
, we work under the following assumption regarding the martingale
M
.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists an
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
m
and an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, continuous, nondecreasing process
Q
with
Q_{0}=0
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast}\,dQ_{u}.

(2.1)

In addition, we assume that
Q
is bounded i.e.
\exists
a constant
C_{Q}
s.t.
0\leq Q_{t}\leq C_{Q}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumption
2.1
holds naturally under some suitable assumptions, see
[
52
]
for more details. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the process
m
appearing in (
2.1
) takes values in the space of symmetric matrices so that
m_{u}=m^{\ast}_{u}=(m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, see e.g.
[
51
,
52
]
.

Now we introduce some spaces which will be used in the sequel of the paper. As usual,
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
stands for the space of all real-valued,
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random variables
\eta
such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{2})<\infty
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
be the space of all
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes
Z
with the pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{t}Z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty,

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any fixed
\beta\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)
the class of all

\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes
Z
with the pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}\|m_{t}Z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

Hence
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)=\mathcal{L}^{2}_{0}(M)
and the pseudo-norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}
are equivalent for every
\beta>0
since the nondecreasing process
Q
is nonnegative and bounded.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
stand for the space of all real-valued, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
X
with the norm

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X^{2}_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

Similarly, we denote by
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}
the space of all real-valued, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
X
with the norm

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}X^{2}_{t}\big)\bigg]<\infty.

It is clear that
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2}
and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}
are equivalent for every
\beta>0
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
the space of all real-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
X
with the pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

Similarly, we denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)
the space of all real-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
X
with the pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}X^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

We observe that
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
and, for brevity, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}
the product space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
with the following pseudo-norm,
for every
(X,Z)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle\|(X,Z)\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

Similarly, we introduce the space
\mathbb{H}^{2}:={\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
endowed with the natural pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|(X,Z)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

For a fixed
\beta\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
the Banach space
({\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M),\|\cdot\|_{\beta})
with the pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|(X,Z)\|^{2}_{\beta}:=\|Z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|Z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}

and we note that
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\mathbb{S}^{2}_{0}
. In view of Assumption
2.1
, the set
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
can be formally identified with
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

We introduce the following martingale representation assumption, which provides a natural underpinning for the existence of a solution of the following BSDE (
2.2
).

Assumption 2.2
.

The martingale
M
has the predictable representation property under
\mathbb{P}
with respect to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
, i.e. for any real-valued, square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
N
with
N_{0}=0
there exists a process
Z\in{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
s.t.
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}
. Moreover, the uniqueness of
Z
in
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
holds, that is, if
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}
, then
\|Z-\widetilde{Z}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}=0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Assumption
2.2
is easily to be satisfied, the readers are referred to
[
52
]
for more comments on Assumption
2.2
. We just mention here that, similar to Remark 2.1 in
[
52
]
, Assumption
2.2
can be relaxed to the case that the generalized martingale representation property of
M
is valid, in the sense that any real-valued, square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
N
can be represented as
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}+L_{t}
where
L
is a square-integrable,
\mathbb{F}
-martingale orthogonal to
M
. Then the results of the existence and uniqueness of solutions in this paper still hold, provided that an additional orthogonal martingale term is introduced in the definition of a solution to a reflected or doubly reflected BSDE.

For a fixed horizon date
T>0
, we consider the following BSDE on
[0,T]
with the data
(g,\eta,D)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})\,dQ_{t}+Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t}+dD_{t},\\
Y_{T}=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
\eta
is a given the random variable in
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, and
D
is a given processes in
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
s.t.
D_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
. Equation (
2.2
) can be written more explicitly in the following manner, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Y_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}-(D_{T}-D_{t}).

(2.3)

Note that here
D
is not necessary to be of finite variation.
The next definition states the measurability properties of the
generator

g
in (
2.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

A
generator

g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable function such that the process
g(\cdot,\cdot,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, for every
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let us define
\mu_{Q}(A):={\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{A}(\omega,t)\,dQ_{t}(\omega)\big]
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
, which is the Doléans measure of the process
Q
on the space
\Omega\times[0,T]
endowed with the product
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
. We first recall the uniform Lipschitz condition for the generator, which is frequently employed in the study of classical BSDEs, reflected BSDEs and doubly reflected BSDEs driven by a Brownian motion (also with jumps generated by a Poisson random measure).

Definition 2.2
.

A generator
g
is
uniformly Lipschitz continuous
if there exists a constant
L
such that,
for
\mu_{Q}
-almost every
(\omega,t)
, for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq L\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\big).

(2.4)

We emphasis that condition (
2.4
) is no longer suitable when dealing with BSDEs, reflected BSDEs and doubly reflected BSDEs with a single jump, which may be used to represent the occurrence of some extraneous event
E
, and thus condition (
2.4
) has been modified to explicitly account for the
intensity process

\lambda
of
E
. For instance, in
[
13
,
15
,
16
,
55
,
56
]
, the authors postulate that a generator satisfies the following condition

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1},k_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2},k_{2})|\leq L\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|+\sqrt{\lambda_{t}}|k_{1}-k_{2}|\big)

(2.5)

where the variables
k_{1},k_{2}
correspond to the compensated martingale of the indicator process of the event
E
. More generally, in existing papers on BSDEs driven by a multi-dimensional martingale (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
18
,
51
]
), a generator is typically postulated to satisfy some kind of the
m
-Lipschitz condition with
m
being the process from Assumption
2.1
. We refer the reader to
[
51
]
for further references and comments on alternative Lipschitz-type conditions, which can be used when dealing with some particular classes of generators appearing in financial applications.
In the present work, we focus on the generator satisfying the following regularity condition.

Definition 2.3
.

A generator
g
is
uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous
if there exists a constant
\widehat{L}>0
such that, for
\mu_{Q}
-almost every
(\omega,t)
,
and all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq\widehat{L}\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|m_{t}(z_{1}-z_{2})\|\big).

(2.6)

Remark 2.3
.

We note that condition (
2.5
) employed in Peng and Xu
[
55
,
56
]
and Dumitrescu et al.
[
13
,
16
]
, can be seen as a special case of (
2.6
) (for more details, see Remark 2.2 in
[
52
]
). The readers are also referred to
[
52
]
for the existence and uniqueness theorem for solutions to the BSDE (
2.2
) with generator satisfying the uniform
m
-Lipschitz condition.

3
Reflected BSDEs Driven by RCLL Martingales

Our aim in this section is to establish the existence and uniqueness result for the
reflected backward stochastic differential equation
(RBSDE) driven by RCLL martingales. Due to various kinds of the so-called
minimality conditions
, which are also referred to as the
Skorokhod conditions
,
governing the behavior of a solution when it hits the obstacle, solutions to RBSDEs studied in various papers are not always directly related to the nonlinear optimal stopping problem associated with the obstacle process playing the role of reward process. Specifically, the component
Y
of a solution to the RBSDE is not necessarily equal to the nonlinear Snell envelope of the obstacle process, although, obviously, this is indeed the case when the proof of the existence of a solution to the RBSDE hinges on the properties of the classical Snell envelope for the standard optimal stopping problem under the linear expectation, which was studied, in particular, by El Karoui
[
17
]
, Kobylanski and Quenez
[
44
]
, and Maingueneau
[
50
]
.

In view of our further applications to the study of American options in nonlinear markets (see
[
39
]
), the equivalence between the component
Y
in a solution
(Y,Z,K)
to the RBSDE and the value process of the corresponding nonlinear optimal stopping problem is a highly desirable feature and thus it will be ensured by a particular choice of minimality conditions in (
3.1
).

To be more specific, in this section we consider the following RBSDE on
[0,T]
with data
(g,\eta,D,\xi)
(which satisfies the following Assumption
3.1
)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})\,dQ_{t}+Z^{\ast}_{t}\,dM_{t}+dD_{t}-dK_{t},\ Y_{T}=\eta,\\
Y_{t}\geq\xi_{t},\ \forall\,t\in[0,T],\\
\int_{0}^{T}(Y_{t}-\xi_{t})\,dK^{c}_{t}=0\ \mbox{\rm and}\ \Delta K^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta K^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{(Y-D)_{\tau-}=(\xi-D)_{\tau-}\}},\ \forall\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{p},\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{T}_{p}
is the class of
\mathbb{F}
-predictable stopping times taking values in
[0,T]
and
K
is a nondecreasing, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process with
K_{0}=0
. The continuous and discontinuous components of sample paths of
K
are denoted by
K^{c}
and
K^{d}
, respectively.

Assumption 3.1
.

The quadruplet
(g,\eta,D,\xi)
is such that: (i) the generator
g
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous and the process
g(\cdot,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, (ii) the process
D
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
and the random variable
\eta-D_{T}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, (iii) the process
\xi-D
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and
\xi_{T}\leq\eta
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
be the class of nondecreasing, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes such that
A_{0}=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}(A^{2}_{T})<+\infty

and let
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}:=\mathbb{H}^{2}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}={\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}:=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}={\mathcal{S}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
solution
to the RBSDE (
3.1
) with data
(g,\eta,D,\xi)
is a triplet of stochastic processes
(Y,Z,K)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
such that conditions
(
3.1
) are satisfied in the following sense

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Y_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}-(D_{T}-D_{t})+(K_{T}-K_{t}),\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]\bigg)=1,

\mathbb{P}(Y_{t}\geq\xi_{t},\,\forall\,t\in[0,T])=1
and the Skorokhod (minimality) conditions hold:
\int_{0}^{T}(Y_{t}-\xi_{t})\,dK^{c}_{t}=0
and

(3.4)

\displaystyle+e^{\beta Q_{t}}\left[dN_{t}+\|m_{t}Z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\right].

(3.5)

) from

&\leq 2\lambda^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u}^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u},\end{split}

which yields

\begin{split}e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}&\leq\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\\
&\leq\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}w_{t}^{2}\Big)+\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}\|m_{t}v_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\right)-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}.\end{split}

(3.13)

We denote
\widetilde{y}_{u}=y_{u}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}Q_{u}}
and
\widetilde{M}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}Q_{u}}z^{\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}
and we apply the Davis inequality (see, e.g., Theorem 10.24 in He et al.
[
33
]

or Theorem 11.5.5 in Cohen and Elliott
[
9
]
with
p=1
)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{y}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{M}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq 2\sqrt{6}\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{y}_{t-}|^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]\leq 6\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{y}_{t-}|^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\right].

Consequently, using also the elementary inequalities

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{y}_{t-}|^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{t}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{y}_{t}|\,[\widetilde{M}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{48}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+12\,{\mathbb{E}}\big([\widetilde{M}]_{T}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+12\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle\widetilde{M}\rangle_{T}\big),

we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{y}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{M}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{8}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+72\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle\widetilde{M}\rangle_{T}\big),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{8}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\Big)\bigg]+72\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,\|m_{t}z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg].

Thus, by using (
3.12
), we get

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}\Big)\bigg]+144\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,\|m_{t}z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}\Big)\bigg]+144\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).\end{split}

(3.14)

From (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we obtain

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\Big)\bigg]&\leq\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big)+2\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\bigg]\\
&\leq 289\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\Big)\bigg],\end{split}

which gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\Big)\bigg]\leq 578\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).

(3.15)

By combining (
3.12
) and (
3.15
), we find that (recall that
\lambda=2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}
)

\displaystyle\|y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\leq 1158\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big),

which ends the proof.

\Box

3.2
Existence and Uniqueness Theorem for Reflected BSDEs

Our next goal is to study the existence and uniqueness of the solution to RBSDE driven by RCLL martingales. From Lemma
3.1
, we know that once we obtain the existence and uniqueness of the solution to RBSDE (
3.3
), we also have similar results for RBSDE (
3.1
). We henceforth assume that the obstacle
\xi
in (
3.3
) (or the modified obstacle
\xi-D
in (
3.1
)) belongs to the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
. For the reader’s convenience, we give the detailed demonstration of Lemma
3.2
, which is adapted from the proof of Lemma 2.5 in Quenez and Sulem
[
59
]
who in turn use, in particular, Proposition B.11 from Kobylanski and Quenez
[
44
]
(see also
[
45
]
) where the case of the classical optimal stopping problem was studied.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
3.1
be satisfied. If the generator
g
does not depend on
(y,z)
so that the process
g_{t}:=g(t,y,z)=g(t,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, then the RBSDE (
3.3
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z,K)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Proof.
To alleviate the notation, we assume in the proof of Lemma
3.2
that
\xi_{T}=\eta
. Otherwise, it suffices to introduce an auxiliary process

\xi_{T}:=\xi_{t}\mathds{1}_{\{t<T\}}+\eta\mathds{1}_{\{t=T\}}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}
the class of all stopping times
\sigma^{\prime}
such that
\sigma\leq\sigma^{\prime}\leq T
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}[\,\cdot\,]={\mathbb{E}}[\,\cdot\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}]
and define the stochastic system
\overline{Y}
by setting, for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle\overline{Y}(\sigma):=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\Big[\xi_{\tau}+\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}g_{t}\,dQ_{t}\Big]=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[\xi_{\tau}+G_{\tau}-G_{\sigma}\big]

where
G_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}g_{s}\,dQ_{s}
. From the classical aggregation theorems for stochastic systems due to
Dellacherie and Lenglart
[
12
]
(see also El Karoui
[
17
]
, Kobylanski and Quenez
[
44
]
and the references therein), there exists an RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process
\overline{Y}
such that the equality
\overline{Y}_{\sigma}=\overline{Y}(\sigma)
holds for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
. Furthermore, the process
V:=\overline{Y}+G
is a supermartingale of class (D) so it has the Doob-Meyer decomposition

V=V_{0}+\overline{M}-D
where
\overline{M}
is a square-integrable martingale and
D
is a nondecreasing, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process from
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
. Let
D=D^{c}+D^{d}
be the pathwise decomposition of
D
into its continuous and discontinuous components.

By Assumption
2.2
, the
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
M
has the predictable representation property and thus there exists an
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
\overline{Z}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
such that
\overline{M}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\overline{Z}_{s}\,dM_{s}
. Moreover, the process
\overline{Z}
is unique in the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
. By an application of Proposition B.11 in
[
44
]
, we obtain the equality
\int_{0}^{T}(\overline{Y}_{t}-\xi_{t})\,dD^{c}_{t}=0
. In addition, we have, for every predictable stopping time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{p}
,

\displaystyle\Delta D^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta D^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{Y}_{\tau-}=\widetilde{\xi}_{\tau-}\}}.

If we set
\overline{K}:=D^{c}+D^{d}
, then it is easy to check that
(\overline{Y},\overline{Z},\overline{K})
is a solution to the RBSDE (
3.2
).
We also note that the process
\overline{Y}
is RCLL and, in view of Proposition
3.1
, the processes
\overline{Y}
and
\overline{K}
belong to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
. This completes the proof of the existence of a solution
(Y,Z,K)
to the RBSDE (
3.3
) with the desired properties.

To prove the uniqueness of a solution, we assume that
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime},K^{\prime})
is an arbitrary solution to the RBSDE (
3.2
). We will show that
Y^{\prime}=\overline{Y}
, up to indistinguishability of stochastic processes. Inspecting the RBSDE (
3.3
) with the fixed driver
g_{t}
, we see that

Y^{\prime}+G
is a supermartingale and thus, since
Y^{\prime}\geq\xi
, we obtain, for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
and
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}
,

\displaystyle Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[Y^{\prime}_{\tau}+G_{\tau}-G_{\sigma}\big]\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[\xi_{\tau}+G_{\tau}-G_{\sigma}\big].

Since
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}
is arbitrary, this in turn yields

\displaystyle Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}\geq\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[\xi_{\tau}+G_{\tau}-G_{\sigma}\big]=\overline{Y}_{\sigma}.

We will now show that
Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}\leq\overline{Y}_{\sigma}
. To this end, we fix
\varepsilon>0
and we set
\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}:=\inf\{t\geq\sigma\,|\,Y^{\prime}_{t}\leq\xi_{t}+\varepsilon\}
so that
Y^{\prime}_{t}>\xi_{t}+\varepsilon
on
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon})
and thus the process
K^{c}
is constant on
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}]
. The process
K^{d}
is also manifestly constant on
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon})
and, in addition,
Y^{\prime}_{t}>\xi_{t}+\varepsilon
on
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon})
gives

\displaystyle Y^{\prime}_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}-}\geq\xi_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}-}+\varepsilon>\xi_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}-},

which implies that
\Delta K^{d}_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}=0
so that
K^{d}
is constant on
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}]
.
Therefore, the process
Y^{\prime}+G
is a martingale on the stochastic interval
[\sigma,\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}]
.
Furthermore, from the definition of
\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}
and the right-continuity of
Y^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\xi}
, we have
Y^{\prime}_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}\leq\xi_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon
and thus

\displaystyle Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}={\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[Y^{\prime}_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}+G_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}-G_{\sigma}\big]\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\sigma}\big[\xi_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}+G_{\tau_{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}}-G_{\sigma}\big]+\varepsilon\leq\overline{Y}_{\sigma}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that
Y^{\prime}_{\sigma}\leq\overline{Y}_{\sigma}
. The uniqueness of
Z
and
K
follows from the uniqueness of the Doob-Meyer decomposition and the postulated uniqueness in the predictable representation property of
M
(see Assumption
2.2
).

\Box

We now proceed to show the existence and uniqueness result for the solution of the RBSDE (
3.3
).
Recall that for a fixed
\beta\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
the Banach space
({\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M),\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\beta})
where the norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\beta}

is given by

\displaystyle\|(w,v)\|^{2}_{\beta}:=\|w\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}.

To set up the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we need to define some mappings. We define the mapping
\Psi:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
as follows: for
(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
we set
\Psi(w,v)=(Y^{w,v},Z^{w,v},K^{w,v})
where
(Y^{w,v},Z^{w,v},K^{w,v})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is the unique solution of the RBSDE (
3.3
) associated with generator
g_{t}(\omega):=g(\omega,t,w_{t}(\omega),v_{t}(\omega))
. In view of Lemma
3.2
, the mapping
\Psi
is well-defined. In addition, we define the projection
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, which maps
(w,v,l)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
to
\pi(w,v,l):=(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
. Finally, we define the mapping
S:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
by
S:=\pi\circ\Psi
. The following lemma shows that
S
is a contraction on
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, provided that
\beta
is sufficiently large.

Lemma 3.3
.

If Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
3.1
hold, then
S:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
is a contraction mapping for some
\beta\geq 0
and thus
S
has a unique fixed point, which is denoted as
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})
.

Proof.
We consider two pairs
(w^{1},v^{1})
and
(w^{2},v^{2})
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
and, for brevity, we denote their respective images through
S
by
(Y^{1},Z^{1})
and
(Y^{2},Z^{2})
. Using the
a priori
estimate from Proposition
3.3
and the fact that the generator
g
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous with a constant
\widehat{L}
, we obtain for arbitrary
\beta\geq 0
and
\gamma>0

\displaystyle\|Y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|Z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\leq 1158\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w^{1}-w^{2}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|v^{1}-v^{2}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).

If we set
\beta=2\times 1158\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)>0
for
\beta\geq 0
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\|S(w^{1},v^{1})-S(w^{2},v^{2})\|_{\beta}^{2}=\|(Y^{1},Z^{1})-(Y^{2},Z^{2})\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(w^{1},v^{1})-(w^{2},v^{2})\|_{\beta}^{2}.

By the Banach theorem, the mapping
S:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
has a unique fixed point
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})
.

\Box

The next result establishes the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the RBSDE (
3.3
). Let the mapping
T:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}

be defined by
T=\Psi\circ\pi
so that
T(w,v,l)=(Y^{w,v},Z^{w,v},K^{w,v})
for all
(w,v,l)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
3.1
are satisfied, then the reflected BSDE (
3.3
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z,K)
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.
Moreover, the triplet
(Y,Z,K)
belongs to
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
and the process
K
is in
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
.

Proof.
From the definitions of
S
and
T
, it is clear that, for every
(w,v,l)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
,

\displaystyle T(w,v,l)=(S(w,v),K^{w,v}).

(3.16)

From Lemma
3.3
, we know that the mapping
S
has a unique fixed point
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
.
We claim that
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})
where
\widehat{K}:=K^{\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
is a unique fixed point of
T
.
The equality
T(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})=(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})
follows from the equality
S(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})=(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})

and (
3.16
) with
l=\widehat{K}
. Furthermore, in view of Lemma
3.2
, the process
\widehat{K}
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
, since
the triplet
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})
can also be interpreted as a unique solution to the RBSDE with a fixed generator

g_{t}=g(t,\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})
.

To check that the fixed point of
T
is unique, it suffices to recall that the fixed point of
S
is unique and use again (
3.16
).
We have thus shown that the RBSDE (
3.3
) has a unique solution
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})
where
\widehat{K}=K^{\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}}
.
It is also easy to verify that
(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z},\widehat{K})
is also a unique fixed point of the mapping
T^{k}
for every
k\geq 2
. To this end,
we notice that the equality
T^{k}(w,v,l)=(S^{k}(w,v),K^{S^{k-1}(w,v)})
holds for every
k\geq 2
and, by Lemma
3.3
,

S^{k}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})=S^{k-1}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})=(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})
. The second assertion is now an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.1
.

\Box

3.3
Picard’s Iterations for Solutions to Reflected BSDEs

In this section, we work under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. Our next goal is to construct Picard’s sequence and to show that it converges to the unique solution
(Y,Z,K)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
to the RBSDE (
3.3
). For this purpose, we take the sequence
\{(Y^{k},Z^{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
where
(Y^{k},Z^{k})=S^{k}(w,v)
for some
(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and we define the sequence
\{K^{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
by postulating that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the triplet
(Y^{k},Z^{k},K^{k})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is the unique solution to the RBSDE with generator
g^{k}_{t}:=g(t,Y_{t}^{k-1},Z_{t}^{k-1})
(see Lemma
3.2
).
Hence we have that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

K^{k}_{t}=-\int_{0}^{t}g(u,Y_{u}^{k-1},Z_{u}^{k-1})\,dQ_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}Z^{k,\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}-Y_{t}^{k}+Y_{0}^{k},

Y^{k}_{t}\geq\xi_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
, and the continuous and discontinuous components
K^{k,c}
and
K^{k,d}
of
K^{k}
satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(Y^{k}_{t}-\xi_{t})\,dK_{t}^{k,c}=0,\quad\Delta K_{\tau}^{k,d}=\Delta K_{\tau}^{k,d}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{\tau-}=\widetilde{\xi}_{\tau-}\}}.

From Lemma
3.3
, we obtain the convergence
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(Y^{k},Z^{k})-(Y,Z)\|_{\beta}^{2}=0

where
(Y,Z,K)
is the unique solution to the RBSDE (
3.3
). The following result establishes the convergence of Picard’s iterations

(Y^{k},Z^{k},K^{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
to
(Y,Z,K)
in the Banach space
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
3.1
are valid, then the sequence
(Y^{k},Z^{k},K^{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges in
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
to the unique solution
(Y,Z,K)
of the reflected BSDE (
3.3
), meaning that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Z^{k}-Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}=0,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|K^{k}-K\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0.

(3.17)

Proof.
The properties
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Z^{k}-Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}=0
are immediate from the Banach fixed point theorem
(see Lemma
3.3
). It thus suffices to show
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|K^{k}-K\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0
. If we denote
\widetilde{Y}^{k}:=Y^{k}-Y
,
\widetilde{Z}^{k}:=Z^{k}-Z
and
\widetilde{K}^{k}:=K^{k}-K
,
then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}d\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}=-\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}\,dQ_{t}+\widetilde{Z}^{k,*}_{t}\,dM_{t}-d\widetilde{K}^{k}_{t},\\
\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{T}=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}:=g(t,Y^{k-1}_{t},Z^{k-1}_{t})-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})
. We claim that that there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{K}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}

(3.18)

where

\displaystyle J_{k}

\displaystyle:=\|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}+\|\widetilde{Z}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}

with
\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}:=Y^{k-1}-Y
and
\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}:=Z^{k-1}-Z
. Assuming that (
3.18
) holds, from the convergence
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(Y^{k},Z^{k})-(Y,Z)\|_{\beta}^{2}=0
, we deduce that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}J_{k}=0
and thus we see that (
3.17
) is indeed a consequence of (
3.18
). It thus remains to show that (
3.18
) is satisfied.

Step 1.
We will first show that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}.

(3.19)

The Itô formula gives

\displaystyle d|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}=2\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t-}\,d\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}+d[\widetilde{Y}^{k}]_{t}

and thus

\displaystyle d|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}=-2\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}\,dQ_{t}+2\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t-}\widetilde{Z}^{k,*}_{t}\,dM_{t}-2\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t-}\,d\widetilde{K}^{k}_{t}+d[\widetilde{Y}^{k}]_{t}

where the process

\displaystyle[\widetilde{Y}^{k}]_{t}=\sum_{0\leq u\leq t}|\Delta\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u}|^{2}+\langle\widetilde{Y}^{k,c}\rangle_{t}

is nondecreasing. As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we infer from the Skorokhod conditions that
\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{K}^{k}_{u}\leq 0
for every
t\in[0,T]
and thus

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{u}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\widetilde{Z}^{k,*}_{u}\,dM_{u},

which gives

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\leq 2\,\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\,dQ_{t}+2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,dM^{k}_{u}\Big|

(3.20)

where the
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
M^{k}
is given by
M^{k}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k,\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}
so that
\langle M^{k}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\|m_{u}\widetilde{Z}^{k}_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}
.
Using also the inequality
|\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\leq\widehat{L}\big(|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}_{t}|+\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}_{t}\|\big)
, we deduce that there exists a positive constant
C
(as usual,
the value of
C
may vary from place to place in the remainder of the proof) such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\leq\widehat{L}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|\big(|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}_{t}|+\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}_{t}\|\big)\leq C\big(|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}_{t}|^{2}+|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}+\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}_{t}\|^{2}\big)

and thus it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\,dQ_{t}\bigg]\leq CJ_{k}.

(3.21)

Furthermore, an application of the Davis inequality gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,dM^{k}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq 2\sqrt{6}\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}|^{2}\,d[M^{k}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]\leq 6\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}|^{2}\,d[M^{k}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right].

Consequently, using also the elementary inequalities

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}|^{2}\,d[M^{k}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|\,[M^{k}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{16}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\Big]+4\,{\mathbb{E}}\big([M^{k}]_{T}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\Big]+4\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle M^{k}\rangle_{T}\big),

we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,dM^{k}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{3}{8}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\Big]+24\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle M^{k}\rangle_{T}\big).

(3.22)

It is now easy to deduce from (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) that there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\Big]\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\,dQ_{t}\bigg]+C\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle M^{k}\rangle_{T}\big)\leq CJ_{k}

and thus (
3.19
) is valid.

Step 2.
In the second step, we will show that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{K}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}.

(3.23)

Using the equality

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{k}_{t}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}-\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{k}-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{u}\,dQ_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k,\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}-\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{k}-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{u}\,dQ_{u}+M^{k}_{t},

we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{K}^{k}_{t}|\leq 2\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|+\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\,dQ_{t}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|M^{k}_{t}|

and thus

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{K}^{k}\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{K}^{k}_{t}|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|\,dQ_{t}\Big)^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|M^{k}_{t}|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[J_{k}+\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|^{2}\,dQ_{t}+\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k}_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]

where we used (
3.19
), the boundedness of
Q
and we observed that the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality with
p=2

(see, e.g., Theorem 10.36 in He et al.
[
33
]
or Theorem 11.5.5 in Cohen and Elliott
[
9
]
) yields (one may take here
C=4
)

\displaystyle\|M^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\,\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|M^{k}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}([M^{k}]_{T})=C\,{\mathbb{E}}(\langle M^{k}\rangle_{T})=C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k}_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]=\|\widetilde{Z}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

Using the inequality
|\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}|^{2}\leq C\big(|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}_{t}|^{2}+\|m_{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}_{t}\|^{2}\big)
, we obtain (
3.23
). To conclude that inequality (
3.18
)
is valid, it suffices to combine (
3.19
) with (
3.23
).

4
Doubly Reflected BSDEs with RCLL Martingales

The goal of this section is to study the
doubly reflected backward stochastic differential equation
(DRBSDE) on
[0,T]
with data
(g,\eta,D,\xi,\zeta)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})\,dQ_{t}+Z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}+dD_{t}-dL_{t}+dU_{t},\ Y_{T}=\eta,\\
\xi_{t}\leq Y_{t}\leq\zeta_{t},\ \forall\,t\in[0,T],\\
\int_{0}^{T}(Y_{t}-\xi_{t})\,dL^{c}_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}(\zeta_{t}-Y_{t})\,dU^{c}_{t}=0,\\
\Delta L^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta L^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{(Y-D)_{\tau-}=(\xi-D)_{\tau-}\}}\ \mbox{\rm and}\ \Delta U^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta U^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{(Y-D)_{\tau-}=(\zeta-D)_{\tau-}\}},\ \forall\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{p},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where the data
(g,\eta,D,\xi,\zeta)
satisfies the following assumption

Assumption 4.1
.

The quintuplet
(g,\eta,D,\xi,\zeta)
is such that: (i) the generator
g
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous and the process
g(\cdot,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, (ii) the process
D
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
and the random variable
\eta-D_{T}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, (iii) the processes
\xi-D
and
\zeta-D
belong to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and satisfy
\xi_{t}-D_{t}\leq\zeta_{t}-D_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
; in addition,

\xi_{T}\leq\eta\leq\zeta_{T}
.

In (
4.1
),
\mathcal{T}_{p}
denotes all
\mathbb{F}
-predictable stopping times taking values in
[0,T]
,
L
and
U
are non-decreasing, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes with
L_{0}=U_{0}=0
and such that the measures generated by
L
and
U
satisfy
dL_{t}\perp dU_{t}
where the notation
dL_{t}\perp dU_{t}
means that the measures generated by nondecreasing processes
L
and
U
are
singular
,
in the sense that there exists an
\mathbb{F}
-predictable set
F\subset\Omega\times[0,T]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{F(t)}\,dL_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{F^{c}(t)}\,dU_{t}=0

where
F(t):=\{\omega\in\Omega\,|\,(\omega,t)\in F\}
and
F^{c}(t)
is its complement.
Note that here the equality
L=L^{c}+L^{d}
(respectively,
U=U^{c}+U^{d}
) gives the pathwise decomposition of
L
(respectively,
U
) into its continuous and jump components. The last two rows in (
4.1
) are collectively referred to as the
Skorokhod
(or the
minimality
) conditions for DRBSDEs.

To examine solutions to the RDBSDE (
4.1
), we introduce the spaces of stochastic processes
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\mathbb{H}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
(recall that the spaces
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}},\mathbb{S}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}},{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
are introduced in Section
3
).

Definition 4.1
.

A
solution
to the DRBSDE (
4.1
) with data
(g,\eta,D,\xi,\zeta)
is a quadruplet of stochastic processes
(Y,Z,L,U)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
satisfying (
4.1
), in the sense that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Y_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}-(D_{T}-D_{t})+(L_{T}-L_{t})-(U_{T}-U_{t}),\ \forall\,t\in[0,T]\bigg)=1,

\mathbb{P}(\xi_{t}\leq Y_{t}\leq\zeta_{t},\,\forall\,t\in[0,T])=1
and the Skorokhod (minimality) conditions stated in (
4.1
) hold.
We say that the
uniqueness of a solution to
(
4.1
) holds if for any two solutions
(Y,Z,L,U)
and
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime},L^{\prime},U^{\prime})
to (
4.1
) we have
\|(Y,Z,L,U)-(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime},L^{\prime},U^{\prime})\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}}=0
.

It can be observed that, without loss of generality, it suffices to consider the modified obstacles

\xi^{\prime}_{t}:=\xi_{t}\mathds{1}_{\{t<T\}}+\eta\mathds{1}_{\{t=T\}},\quad\zeta^{\prime}_{t}:=\zeta_{t}\mathds{1}_{\{t<T\}}+\eta\mathds{1}_{\{t=T\}},

and thus the random variable
\eta
can be omitted, provided that we postulate that
\xi_{T}=\zeta_{T}
in (
4.1
).

As in preceding sections, we make use of the following transformations:
\widetilde{Y}:=Y-D
,
\widetilde{\xi}=\xi^{\prime}-D
,
\widetilde{\zeta}=\zeta^{\prime}-D
and
\widetilde{g}(t,y,z):=g(t,y+D_{t},z)
and we set
\widetilde{Z}=Z,\,\widetilde{L}=L,\,\widetilde{U}=U
. Therefore, we henceforth study a solution
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{L},\widetilde{U})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
to the transformed DRBSDE with data
(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\xi}_{T},0,\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\zeta})

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}d\widetilde{Y}_{t}=-\widetilde{g}(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t},\widetilde{Z}_{t})\,dQ_{t}+\widetilde{Z}^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}-d\widetilde{L}_{t}+d\widetilde{U}_{t},\ \widetilde{Y}_{T}=\widetilde{\eta}_{T},\\
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}\leq\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq\widetilde{\zeta}_{t},\ \forall\,t\in[0,T],\\
\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{Y}_{t}-\widetilde{\xi}_{t})\,dL^{c}_{t}=\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\zeta}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}_{t})\,d\widetilde{U}^{c}_{t}=0,\\
\Delta\widetilde{L}^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta\widetilde{L}^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{\tau-}=\widetilde{\xi}_{\tau-}\}}\ \mbox{\rm and}\ \Delta\widetilde{U}^{d}_{\tau}=\Delta\widetilde{U}^{d}_{\tau}\mathds{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{\tau-}=\widetilde{\zeta}_{\tau-}\}},\ \forall\,\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{p},\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

and we argue that DRBSDEs (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are equivalent under Assumption
4.1
. Notice that, in view of inequalities

\widetilde{\xi}\leq\widetilde{Y}\leq\widetilde{\zeta}
and Assumption
4.1
(iii), it is readily seen that the process
\widetilde{Y}
necessarily belongs to the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
.

4.1
A Priori Estimates for Doubly Reflected BSDEs

To address the case of a uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous generator
g
, we will prove some auxiliary result furnishing the

estimates for solutions to (
4.2
). As we argued before, we may and do assume, without loss of generality, that
D=0
and
\xi_{T}=\zeta_{T}=\eta
and thus we henceforth examine the DRBSDE with data
(g,\xi_{T},0,\xi,\zeta)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
i=1,2
, let
(Y^{i},Z^{i},L^{i},U^{i})
be a solution to the doubly reflected BSDE with data
(g^{i},\xi^{i}_{T},0,\xi^{i},\zeta^{i})
and a uniformly
m
-Lipschitz
continuous generator
g^{i}

y:=Y^{1}-Y^{2}

\displaystyle(\beta-2\widehat{L}_{1}-\gamma^{-1})\|y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)}+(1-2\widehat{L}_{1}^{2}\gamma)\|z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\leq 2\gamma\|\overline{g}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)}

where
\widehat{L}_{1}
is the Lipschitz constant associated with generator
g^{1}
.

Proof.
By taking the difference of the two DRBSDEs, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}y_{t}=-\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}-(dL^{1}_{t}-dL^{2}_{t})+(dU^{1}_{t}-dU^{2}_{t}),\\
y_{T}=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\delta_{t}:=g^{1}(t,Y^{1}_{t},Z^{1}_{t})-g^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z^{2}_{t})

3.2

\begin{split}&e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}+\beta{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\\
&=2\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u}\delta_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+2{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}\,dL^{1}_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}\,dL^{2}_{u}\bigg]\\
&-2\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}\,dU^{1}_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}\,dU^{2}_{u}\bigg].\end{split}

On the one hand, using the Skorokhod conditions, we observe that

\displaystyle y_{u-}\,dL^{1,c}_{u}=y_{u}\,dL^{1,c}_{u}=\big[(Y^{1}_{u}-\xi_{u})-(Y^{2}_{u}-\xi_{u})\big]\,dL^{1,c}_{u}=-(Y^{2}_{u}-\xi_{u})\,dL^{1,c}_{u}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle y_{u-}\,\Delta L^{1,d}_{u}=\big[(Y^{1}_{u-}-\xi_{u-})-(Y^{2}_{u-}-\xi_{u-})\big]\,\Delta L^{1,d}_{u}\leq 0

so that
y_{u-}\,dL^{1}_{u}\leq 0
. Analogous arguments show that
y_{u}\,dL^{2,c}_{u}\geq 0
and
y_{u-}\,\Delta L^{2,d}_{u}\geq 0
and thus the inequality

-y_{u-}\,dL^{2}_{u}\leq 0
is valid as well. On the other hand, we observe that

\displaystyle y_{u-}\,dU^{1,c}_{u}=y_{u}\,dU^{1,c}_{u}=\big[(Y^{1}_{u}-\zeta_{u})-(Y^{2}_{u}-\zeta_{u})\big]\,dU^{1,c}_{u}=-(Y^{2}_{u}-\zeta_{u})\,dU^{1,c}_{u}\geq 0

and

\displaystyle y_{u-}\,\Delta U^{1,d}_{u}=\big[(Y^{1}_{u-}-\zeta_{u-})-(Y^{2}_{u-}-\zeta_{u-})\big]\,\Delta U^{1,d}_{u}\geq 0

so that
y_{u-}\,dU^{1}_{u}\geq 0
. Similarly, one can show that
y_{u}\,dU^{2,c}_{u}\leq 0
and
y_{u-}\,\Delta U^{2,d}_{u}\leq 0
and thus we conclude that

-y_{u-}\,dU^{2}_{u}\geq 0
. Therefore, we obtain

e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}+\beta{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\leq 2\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u}\delta_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg].

Then, arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we obtain the desired inequality.

4.2
Existence and Uniqueness Theorem for Doubly Reflected BSDEs

Before stating the next result, we need to introduce some notations. For any process

\widetilde{\xi}^{g}_{t}:=\widetilde{\xi}_{t}-{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\widetilde{\xi}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg],\quad\widetilde{\zeta}^{g}_{t}:=\widetilde{\zeta}_{t}-{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\widetilde{\zeta}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg],

(4.6)

so that
\widetilde{\xi}^{g}_{T}=\widetilde{\zeta}^{g}_{T}=0

\widetilde{\zeta}

\widetilde{\xi}

{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

\widetilde{\xi}^{g}
and
\widetilde{\zeta}^{g}

{\mathcal{S}}^{2}

\{I^{g,k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

\{J^{g,k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle I^{g,k+1}_{\sigma}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[J^{g,k}_{\tau}+\widetilde{\xi}^{g}_{\tau}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\big],\quad J^{g,k+1}_{\sigma}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[\sigma,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[I^{g,k}_{\tau}-\widetilde{\zeta}^{g}_{\tau}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\big]

Lemma 4.1
.

The sequences of processes

(4.7)

If

The proof of Lemma 3.3 in

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{\{v_{t}>\varphi_{t}\}}\,dB^{c}_{t}=0\ \ \mbox{\rm and}\ \ \Delta B^{d}_{t}=\Delta B^{d}_{t}\mathds{1}_{\{v_{t-}=\varphi_{t-}\}}.

For a fixed

Lemma 4.3
.

If Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
and
4.1
–
4.2

Proof.
We consider two pairs

Theorem 4.1

Proof.

(4.10)

4.3
Picard’s Iterations for Solutions to Doubly Reflected BSDEs

To construct the Picard’s sequence converging to the solution of a
(Y,Z,L,U)
, we consider the sequence
\{(Y^{k},Z^{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
where
(Y^{k},Z^{k})=S^{k}(w,v)
for some
(w,v)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\beta}
. The associated sequence
\{(L^{k},U^{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is obtained by choosing for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the last two components of the unique solution
(Y^{k},Z^{k},L^{k},U^{k})
to the DRBSDE with generator
g^{k-1}_{t}:=g(t,Y^{k-1}_{t},Z^{k-1}_{t})
. Notice that the existence of a solution is guaranteed under the conditions of Proposition
4.3
. The next result can be demonstrated in a similar way to the proof of Proposition
3.4
and thus we merely sketch the main steps in the proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Proposition 4.4
.

If Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
and
4.1
–
4.2
are valid, then

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(L^{k}-L)-(U^{k}-U)\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|Z^{k}-Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}=0.

(4.11)

Proof.
We first observe that last convergence in (
4.11
) is clear from Lemma
4.3
. If we denote
\widetilde{Y}^{k}:=Y^{k}-Y
,
\widetilde{Z}^{k}:=Z^{k}-Z
,

\widetilde{L}^{k}:=L^{k}-L
and
\widetilde{U}^{k}:=U^{k}-U
, then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}d\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}=-\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}\,dQ_{t}+\widetilde{Z}^{k,*}_{t}\,dM_{t}-d\widetilde{L}^{k}_{t}+d\widetilde{U}^{k}_{t},\\
\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{T}=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{t}:=g(t,Y^{k-1}_{t},Z^{k-1}_{t})-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})
. We claim that that there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{L}^{k}-\widetilde{U}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}

(4.12)

where

\displaystyle J_{k}:=\|\widetilde{Y}^{k-1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|\widetilde{Z}^{k-1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}+\|\widetilde{Z}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

Assuming (
4.12
) and using Lemma
4.3
and the Banach theorem, we deduce that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}J_{k}=0
and thus (
4.11
) follows. It thus remains to show that (
4.12
) is valid.

Step 1.
To show that
\|\widetilde{Y}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}
for some constant
C\geq 0
, it suffices to follow Step 1 in Proposition
4.2
. By applying the Itô formula to
|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}
and using the Skorokhod conditions, we get
\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{L}^{k}_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{U}^{k}_{u}\leq 0
for every
t\in[0,T]
, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\|m_{u}\widetilde{Z}^{k}_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\sum_{t<s\leq T}|\Delta\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u}|^{2}-\int_{t}^{T}\|m_{u}^{d}(\widetilde{Z}^{k}_{u})^{d}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{u}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Y}^{k}_{u-}\widetilde{Z}^{k,*}_{u}\,dM_{u}.

The rest follows by repeating the arguments from Step 1 in Proposition
4.2
.

Step 2.
We claim that
\|\widetilde{L}^{k}-\widetilde{U}^{k}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\leq CJ_{k}
.
To check this, we observe that

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}^{k}_{t}-\widetilde{U}^{k}_{t}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}-\widetilde{Y}_{t}^{k}-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{g}^{k-1}_{u}\,dQ_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{k,\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}.

and by using the arguments from Step 2 in Proposition
3.4
, we can obtain the desired inequality.

Representation Stability for Disks in a Strip
Nicholas Wawrykow

Abstract
We consider the ordered configuration space of n n open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width w w . In the spirit of Arnol’d and Cohen, we provide a finite presentation for the rational homology groups of this ordered configuration space as a twisted algebra. We use this presentation to prove that the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width w w exhibits a notion of first-order representation stability similar to Church–Ellenberg–Farb and Miller–Wilson’s first-order representation stability for the ordered configuration space of points in a manifold. In addition, we prove that for large w w this disk configuration space exhibits notions of second- (and higher) order representation stability.

Introduction

Given a manifold
X
, the
ordered configuration space of
n
points in
X
, denoted
F_{n}(X)
, is the space of ways of embedding
n
labeled points in
X
, where the topology is the subspace topology of
X^{n}
:

F_{n}(X):=\big\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}|x_{i}\neq x_{j}\big\}.

The
unordered configuration space of
n
points in
X
, denoted
C_{n}(X)
, is the quotient of
F_{n}(X)
by the symmetric group action on the labels of the points:

C_{n}(X):=F_{n}(X)/S_{n}.

Stability patterns among the homology groups of configuration spaces of points have been studied for almost half a century.
If
X
is a connected non-compact finite type manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
, McDuff
[
McD75
]
and Segal
[
Seg79
]
proved that the homology groups of the unordered configuration space of points in
X
stabilize: if
n\geq 2k+2
, there is an isomorphism
H_{k}\big(C_{n}(X)\big)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{k}\big(C_{n+1}(X)\big)
.
This is
homological stability
for unordered configuration spaces of points.
Ordered configuration spaces of points do not exhibit homological stability as the rank of homology is polynomial in the number of points in the configuration space.
However, Church–Ellenberg–Farb proved that if
X
is orientable, the homology groups of the ordered configuration space of points in
X
stabilize in a representation-theoretic sense.
In particular, they proved that for fixed
k
, the sequence
H_{k}\big(F_{\bullet}(X)\big)
has an algebraic structure called an FI#-module structure, and this structure is finitely generated in degree at most
2k

[
CEF15
]
.
This
first-order representation stability
for ordered configuration spaces of points, which was extended to non-orientable
X
by Miller–Wilson
[
MW19
]
, implies that if
n>2k
, the decomposition of
H_{k}\big(F_{n}(X);\mathbb{Q}\big)
into irreducible symmetric group representations is entirely determined by the decompositions of the
H_{k}\big(F_{m}(X);\mathbb{Q}\big)
into irreducible symmetric group representations for all
m\leq 2k
.
The stabilization maps
H_{k}\big(C_{n}(X)\big)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{k}\big(C_{n+1}(X)\big)
and
H_{k}\big(F_{n}(X)\big)\to H_{k}\big(F_{n+1}(X)\big)
arise from maps between configuration spaces
C_{n}(X)\to C_{n+1}(X)
and
F_{n}(X)\to F_{n+1}(X)
that “add a point at infinity”; for a more precise definition, see, for example,
[
MW19
, Section 1.1]
. In the case of homological stability, the stabilization map takes a class in
H_{k}\big(C_{n}(X)\big)
and tensors it with the fundamental class of
H_{0}\big(C_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
to get a class in
H_{k}\big(C_{n+1}(X)\big)
; see Figure
1
.
The representation stability map is defined similarly, up to the relabeling of points.

Figure 1:
The action of the stabilization map on a class in
H_{2}\big(C_{3}(T^{\circ})\big)
, where
T^{\circ}

or the other points, which correspond to an embedded non-bounding torus in configuration space.

Miller–Wilson also proved that there is a stability pattern among the unstable classes of first-order representation stability.
Namely, they proved that for fixed
k
the sequence
\mathcal{W}^{X}_{i}(\bullet):=H^{\text{FI\#}}_{0}\Big(H_{\frac{|\bullet|+i}{2}}\big(F_{\bullet}(X);\mathbb{Q}\big)\Big)
of FI#-homology generators has an algebraic structure, namely that of a finitely generated FIM
+
-module
[
MW19
]
.
In this
second-order representation stability
, the stabilization map arises from the map on the homology of configuration space that tensors a class in
H_{k}\big(F_{n}(X)\big)
with the fundamental class of
H_{1}\big(F_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)
, which can be thought of as an orbiting pair of points at infinity, producing a class in
H_{k+1}\big(F_{n+2}(X)\big)
; see Figure
2
and
[
MW19
, Section 1.1]
.
Their proof, like many other (representation) stability arguments, relies on a Quillen-type argument on a carefully chosen spectral sequence.
See
[
MW19
,
Ho20
,
Waw22b
]
for more on higher-order representation stability for ordered configuration spaces of points.

to a class in
H_{3}\big(F_{5}(T^{\circ})\big)

4
and
5
arise from the fundamental class of

.

Recently, there has been increased interest in geometric generalizations of configuration spaces of points.
Perhaps the most studied of these generalizations are disk configuration spaces, where disks replace points, and the geometry of the underlying manifold
X
comes to the forefront.
The structure of these disk configuration spaces, which had been studied in the contexts of motion planning in
[
Far08
]
, physical chemistry in
[
Dia09
]
, and physics in
[
CGKM12
]
, can be approached via Morse theory as done by Barynshnikov–Bubenik–Kahle in
[
BBK14
]
.
The most studied of these disk configuration spaces is
\text{conf}(n,w)
,
the ordered configuration space of
n
open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
:

\text{conf}(n,w):=\Big\{(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}|(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}+(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\geq 1\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leq y_{i}\leq w-\frac{1}{2}\Big\},

here
(x_{i},y_{i})
parametrizes the center of the
i^{\text{th}}
disk.
Alpert–Kahle–MacPherson proved that the rank of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w)\big)
grows exponentially in
n

[
AKM21
]
; as a result, the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
is not first-order representation stable in the sense of Church–Ellenberg–Farb, Miller–Wilson.
However, in the case
w=2
, Alpert proved that the ordered configuration space of
n
open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
2
exhibits a reasonable notion of first-order representation stability, as for all
k
the homology groups
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,2)\big)
have the structure of a finitely generated FI
k+1
-module, generated in degree at most
3k
, where FI
k+1
is a generalization of FI#
[
Alp20
]
.
One can use Alpert’s results to decompose
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,2);\mathbb{Q}\big)
into a direct sum of irreducible
S_{n}
-representations as was done in
[
Waw22a
]
.
Alpert’s proof relies on determining a basis for
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,2)\big)
, and using this basis to show that there are well-defined ways to insert the fundamental class of
H_{0}\big(\text{conf}(1,w)\big)
into a class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,2)\big)
to get a class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n+1,2)\big)
.

Does Alpert’s proof technique extend to larger widths
w
?
No.
Alpert–Manin found bases for all
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w)\big)
analogous to Alpert’s bases for
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,2)\big)
, and they proved that these homology groups are free abelian
[
AM21
, Theorem B]
.
They also proved that for
w\geq 3
, these bases are not well behaved under the symmetric group action
[
AM21
, Proposition 8.6]
, and that this precludes the possibility of there being enough well-defined ways of inserting the fundamental class of
H_{0}\big(\text{conf}(1,w)\big)
into a homology class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w)\big)
to get all the classes in
H_{0}\big(\text{conf}(n+1,w)\big)
, that is, it shows there are not enough ways to give
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w)\big)
the structure of a finitely generated FI
d
module.
In fact, their argument suggests that such problems are unavoidable when one considers integral homology.
In addition to computing bases for homology, Alpert–Manin proved that
H_{*}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Z}\big)
has an algebraic structure, that of a finitely generated twisted (non-commutative) algebra
[
AM21
, Theorem A]
.

In this paper, we consider the rational homology of the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
, and we find a new set of generators for
H_{*}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
as a twisted algebra.
Through a series of inductive arguments we use these generators to construct a basis for
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
, distinct from Alpert–Manin’s.
We exhibit families of relations among products of our generators by studying certain cellular complexes, and use our basis to prove that these relations and our generators give a finite presentation of
H_{*}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
as a twisted algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

The homology groups
H_{*}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
have the structure of a finitely presented twisted algebra.

The relations in the presentation given in the proof of Theorem
1.1
show that our generators are well-behaved under the symmetric group action, and we use this to show that there is a family of well-defined disk insertion maps between rational homology classes.
Additionally, letting
w\to\infty
, our presentation recovers results of Arnol’d
[
Arn69
]
and Cohen
[
CLM76
, Chapter 3]
on the structure of
H_{*}\big(F_{\bullet}(\mathbb{R}^{2});\mathbb{Q}\big)
as a twisted algebra; for more, see, for example,
[
Sin06
,
Knu18
]
.
We use this presentation to prove that the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
satisfies a notion of first-order representation stability.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
k\geq 0
and
w\geq 2
, the sequence
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
has the structure of a finitely generated FI
b+1
-module over
\mathbb{Q}
, where
b=\big\lfloor\frac{k}{w-1}\big\rfloor
, finitely generated in degree at most
2k
for
w\geq 3
, and degree at most
3k
for
w=2
.

In the spirit of
[
CEFN14
,
CEF15
,
Ram17
]
, one can use this FI
b+1
-module structure and results of Ramos on finitely generated FI
b+1
-modules to get upper bounds for the multiplicities of irreducible symmetric group representations in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
for large
n
.
Namely, for
w\geq 3
, if for every
m\leq 2k
we have a direct sum decomposition of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(m,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
into irreducible
S_{m}
-representations, then for
n>2k
we have upper bounds for the multiplicities of the irreducible
S_{n}
-representations that occur in a direct sum decomposition of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
into irreducible
S_{n}
-representations.
This in turn gives upper bounds on the Betti numbers of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
for
n>2k
, if for every
m\leq 2k
we know the Betti numbers of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(m,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
.

The FI
b+1
-module structure of
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
arises from the twisted algebra structure of homology as follows:
Any class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
can be written as a sum of products of generators called
wheels
and

; see Section
3
for their definitions.
Given a decomposition of a class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
as a product of these generators, at most

of the generators occupy the entire width of the strip as the most “efficient” generators use
w

To send a class in

to a class in
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n+1,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)

1

H_{0}\big(\text{conf}(1,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)

The relations given in our proof of Theorem

Miller–Wilson’s proof of second-order representation stability for ordered configuration spaces of points relies on the Noetherianity of a twisted skew-commutative algebra.
Since the twisted algebras corresponding to third and higher order representation stability are not known to be Noetherian, it is not possible to extend Miller–Wilson’s methods to the higher order cases at the moment.
We run into a similar problem when trying to use such a method to prove higher order representation stability for the ordered configuration space of open-unit diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
.
As such, we must get our hands dirty studying the homology groups of
\text{conf}(n,w)
.
Fortunately, a large part of this work is already done in the proof of Theorem
1.1

An inductive argument determining the structure of the quotient of the twisted algebra
H_{*}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
by the twisted commutative algebras corresponding to higher order representation stability allows us to prove that the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
satisfies notions of
1^{\text{st}}
- through
\big\lfloor\frac{w+1}{3}\big\rfloor^{\text{th}}
-order representation stability.

3
The Rational Homology of
\text{conf}(n,w)

In this section we recall the definitions of Alpert–Manin’s two types of homology generators: wheels and filters.
We define a new type of rational homology class that we will call averaged-filters, and demonstrate a relation in rational homology between concatenation products of wheels and these averaged-filters.
We use this relation and several others to give a basis for rational homology distinct from Alpert–Manin’s basis.

3.1
Spin-maps

In order to define the homology classes called wheels, filters, and averaged-filters that we will use throughout the paper we need to define what we will call
spin
-maps between weighted cellular complexes.
These are not the
spin
-maps of Section 4 of Alpert–Manin
[
AM21
, Section 4]
, which are maps from weighted permutohedra to weighted configuration spaces, rather they are maps from weighted configuration spaces to weighted configuration spaces that Alpert–Manin reference in passing.
First, we state a generalized version of a technical lemma of Alpert–Manin that will allow us to construct our

-maps
[
AM21
, Lemma 4.6]
.
The proof of this lemma follows from their proof with the obvious generalizations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{W})
be a finite weighted set and
\big(\{a\},\{w_{a}\}\big)
,
\big(\{b\},\{w_{b}\}\big)
, and
\big(\{c\},\{w_{c}\}\big)

Then, there is a map

\iota:[0,1]\times P\big(A\cup\{a\},\mathcal{W}\cup\{w_{a}\}\big)\to P\big(A\cup\{b,c\},\mathcal{W}\cup\{w_{b},w_{c}\}\big)

with the following properties:

1.

It is a homeomorphism and a cellular map.
That is, it sends every
k
-face to a disk which is a union of
k

2.

It is equivariant with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-actions given by

and

on the codomain.

3.

For each cell

under

is the interior of the cell of

.

4.

It takes

to the union of those cells in which
b
and
c
are contained in separate blocks with
b
preceding

.
Similarly, it takes

to the union of those cells in which

precedes
b
.

3.2

Alpert–Manin noted that ordering the elements of

They used this filtration to decompose the homology of

We recall some of their results, which will be of use to us later, and define generalized notions of wheels and filters, which will allow us to better describe the relations in our presentation for

Definition 3.3
.

Write the elements of

Definition 3.5
.

Given an element

.

Given

\sigma

\big(n-\#\sigma,\mathcal{W}(\sigma)\big)

For a weighted set
(A,\mathcal{W})
and
\sigma\in S_{A}
, we write
\big(A-\#\sigma,\mathcal{W}(\sigma)\big)
for the ordered weighted set whose elements are labeled by the wheels of
\sigma
such that the weight of a wheel is the total weight of the elements in the wheel and such that the set of wheels is ordered by the largest element in each wheel.

Given

First, we state Alpert–Manin’s basis theorem as we will use it to prove that our basis for rational homology is, in fact, a basis.

Theorem 3.19
.

(Alpert–Manin
[
AKM21
, Theorem B]
) The homology group
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w)\big)
is free abelian and has a basis consisting of concatenations of proper wheels where the largest labeled disk comes first and non-trivial proper filters on at least 2 wheels such that in each wheel the largest labeled disk comes first.
We say one proper wheel ranks above another if it has more disks, or has the same number of disks and its largest label is greater. A cycle is in the basis if and only if:

1.

The proper wheels inside each filter on more than
3
wheels are in order of increasing largest label (regardless of the number of disks).

2.

The proper wheels inside a filter on 2 wheels are in order of increasing rank.

3.

Adjacent proper wheels not inside a proper filter are in order of decreasing rank.

4.

Every proper wheel immediately to the left of a proper filter ranks above the least wheel in the filter.

This basis is not preserved by the action of the symmetric group as the order of the wheels in a filter and the order of the disks in a wheel matters.
The latter problem is a minor one due to Proposition
3.20
.
The former is much more of a challenge.
Soon, we will replace filters with an average of filters.
These averages behave nicely when the wheels or the disks are permuted, and they will allow us to provide a finite presentation for
H_{*}\big(\text{cell}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
as a twisted algebra.

3.3
Wheel and Filter Relations

We prove several relations on wheels and filters that will be used throughout this paper to find a new basis for the homology of the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w

Proposition 3.20
.

For

, any wheel

Proof.

The only elements in the basis for

∎

Proposition

Proposition 3.21

Given a wheel

\text{spin}_{W}=\sum a_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\text{spin}_{W(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}.

One can check that Proposition

It follows that this is true for the

Next, we state a relation that will allow us to (anti)-commute certain pairs of wheels.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
W_{1}

W_{2}
be two wheels on
n_{1}

W_{1}|W_{2}=(-1)^{\big(n_{1}-1\big)\big(n_{2}-1\big)}W_{2}|W_{1}.

Proof.

Note that the homology classes
W_{1}|W_{2}
and
W_{2}|W_{1}
arise from the cycles
W_{1}|W_{2}
and
W_{2}|W_{1}
in
C_{0}\Big(P\big(\{W_{1},W_{2}\},\{n_{1},n_{2}\},w\big)\Big)

\text{spin}_{W_{1}}
and
\text{spin}_{W_{2}}
.
Since
n_{1}+n_{2}\leq w
, the cell
W_{1}W_{2}
is in
P\big(\{W_{1},W_{2}\},\{n_{1},n_{2}\},w\big)

Taking the boundary of

\partial(W_{1}W_{2})=(-1)^{n_{1}}W_{1}|W_{2}+(-1)^{n_{2}}(-1)^{n_{1}n_{2}}W_{2}|W_{1}.

In homology it follows that the class arising from
(-1)^{n_{1}}W_{1}|W_{2}+(-1)^{n_{2}}(-1)^{n_{1}n_{2}}W_{2}|W_{1}
is trivial.
Therefore

(-1)^{n_{1}}W_{1}|W_{2}+(-1)^{n_{2}}(-1)^{n_{1}n_{2}}W_{2}|W_{1}=0,

which can be rewritten as

W_{1}|W_{2}=(-1)^{\big(n_{1}-1\big)\big(n_{2}-1\big)}W_{2}|W_{1}.

∎

Proposition
3.22

The wheels

and

can freely move past each other in the strip as

.
It follows that the

The difference in sign arises from the way we parametrize the embedded torus in

yielding these classes.
This

(n_{1}-1)
-torus yielding

and an
(n_{2}-1)
-torus yielding

as we need
\big(n_{1}-1\big)\big(n_{2}-1\big)

-1
.

We state a relation that proves that any sufficiently nice filter can be written as a sum of proper filters in Alpert–Manin’s basis.

Then, the filter

F(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})

can be written as a sum of proper filters

F(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})=\sum a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n_{1}+\cdots+i_{n_{m}}}}F\big(W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})\big)

such that each filter is in the basis of Theorem
3.19
, and the labels of disks in the wheel
W_{j}
are the same as the labels of the disks in the wheel
W(i_{j,1},\dots,i_{j,n_{j}})
.

Proof.

By the definition of a filter, the filter

is the image of
\text{spin}_{W_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ\text{spin}_{W_{1}}\circ i_{id}(Z)
in

, where

is the boundary of the top dimensional cell of

, i.e.,

By Proposition

we can write

as a sum of maps of the form

, where the labels of disks are the same as the labels of the disks in

\text{spin}_{W_{j}}=\sum a_{(i_{j,1},\dots,i_{j,n_{j}})}\text{spin}_{W(i_{j,1},\dots,i_{j,n_{j}})}.

Since these are chain maps it follows that

\text{spin}_{W_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ\text{spin}_{W_{1}}\circ i_{id}(Z)\\
=\big(\sum a_{(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})}\text{spin}_{W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})}\big)\circ\cdots\circ\big(\sum a_{(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}})}\text{spin}_{W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}})}\big)\circ i_{id}(Z)\\
=\sum a_{(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})}\cdots a_{(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}})}\text{spin}_{W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})}\circ\cdots\circ\text{spin}_{W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}})}\circ i_{id}(Z).

Note that the cycle

represents the homology class

.
It follows that

can be written as a sum of proper filters on
m
wheels in the basis of Theorem
3.19
, such that the labels of disks in the wheel

are the same as the labels of the disks in the wheels

F(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})=\sum a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n_{1}+\cdots+i_{n_{m}}}}F\big(W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})\big).

∎

3.4
A New Basis for Rational Homology

In this subsection we consider the rational homology of
\text{conf}(n,w)
.
Not much is lost in using rational coefficients as
H_{k}\big(\text{cell}(n,w)\big)
is free abelian; moreover, there is much to be gained.
Representations of the symmetric group over
\mathbb{Q}
are easier to work with than representations over
\mathbb{Z}
, and we need to be able to take averages over families of chain maps in order to define a new type of class in rational homology that we will call averaged-filters.
We will prove a relation between concatenation products of wheels and averaged-filters, and we will use this relation to find a basis for rational homology distinct from that of Alpert–Manin.
Additionally this relation will play a critical role in determining the how much higher order representation stability
\text{conf}(\bullet,w)
exhibits.
All of the results in this subsection hold over a commutative ring
R
where
n!
is invertible.

Definition 3.24
.

Fix an ordering of the weighted set
(A,\mathcal{W})
, and label the cells of

so that the labels in each block are in increasing order.
For

See Figure

.

Since

is a sum of chain maps, it is a chain map, and we use

to define a new family of classes in rational homology of

that are much better behaved with respect to the symmetric group actions than filters.

Definition 3.25
.

For

, let
(A,\mathcal{W})=\big(\{W_{1},\dots,W_{m}\},\{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\}\big)

is a permutation of

Let

that is the boundary of
W_{1}\,\cdots\,W_{m}\in C_{m-1}\big(P(A,\mathcal{W});\mathbb{Q}\big)
.
An
averaged-filter
is the image of

in
H_{n-2}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
under
q
and any sequence of

.
If the

In a proper averaged-filter the wheels are proper.
It also follows from the definition that there are at most
\frac{3w}{2}
disks in an averaged-filter, and more than
w
disks in a non-trivial averaged-filter.
If
n\leq w
, then
P(A,\mathcal{W},w)
is contractible and the averaged-filter is trivial in homology.
We will still consider such filters as they will aid in the statement of Lemma
3.33
.

If
n\leq\frac{3w}{2}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
is such that any collection of all but one of the wheels in
\sigma
has total weight at most
w
and
\sigma
has at least
2
wheels, then
P\big(n-\#\sigma,\mathcal{W}(\sigma),w\big)
is homotopy equivalent to
S^{m-2}
.
As a result, a non-trivial averaged-filter has
m\geq 2
wheels on
n>w
total disks such that each wheel is made of at least
n-w
disks, and the wheels are ordered by the largest label.
The wheels in the averaged-filter can move as follows:
Every wheel can perform its rotations independently, for a total of
n-m
degrees of freedom, resulting in a
T^{n-m}
; additionally, each wheel can move left and right in the strip, crossing over or under each other in any order, and we take the average of all possible crossings in the same direction.
Any
m-1
wheels can have the same
x
-coordinate (since they contain at most
w
disks total), but all
m
cannot (this is not true for trivial averaged-filters).
This movement can be thought of as an average of embedded copies of
S^{m-2}
in configuration space.
As a result, the whole
(n-2)
-cycle arises from an average of embedded copies of
S^{m-2}\times T^{n-m}
.
See Figure
20
for an example of an averaged-filter on
3
wheels each consisting of
1

We write
AF(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})
for the averaged-filter on the ordered set of wheels
W_{1},\dots,W_{m}
.
Here the orientation of the corresponding class represented by the embedded
(\sum S^{m-2})\times T^{n-m}
is determined by the order of the wheels. We write
AF\big(W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})\big)
for the proper averaged-filter on the proper wheels
W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,W(i_{m,1},\dots,i_{m,n_{m}})
.
It follows from the definition of
q
that

AF(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})=\frac{1}{|A|!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{A}}\text{wsgn}(\sigma)F(W_{\sigma(1)},\dots,W_{\sigma(m)}),

hence an averaged-filter is an average of filters.
As a result, the averaged-filter on two wheels
AF(W_{1},W_{2})
is equal to the filter
F(W_{1},W_{2})
.
Alternatively, one could note that the complexes
P\big(\{W_{1},W_{2}\},\{n_{1},n_{2}\},w\big)
and
\text{cell}\big(\{W_{1},W_{2}\},\{n_{1},n_{2}\},w\big)
have identical cellular structures, so
i_{id}=q
and
AF(W_{1},W_{2})=F(W_{1},W_{2})
.

Unlike filters where the order of the wheels determines both the filter and its orientation, the order of the wheels in an averaged-filter only determine its orientation.
Namely, for the averaged-filter
AF(W_{1},\dots,W_{m})
, the symmetric group
S_{m}
determines the orientation of the average of the spheres by an action of weighted sign, as well as determining the order in which we consider the tori.
The following proposition shows we can rearrange the wheels in an averaged-filter so the wheels are in increasing order of largest labeled disk or in order of increasing rank at the cost of a sign change.

Proposition 3.26
.

Let

be an averaged-filter, such that

is a disk on

Proof.

The averaged-filter

, the top dimensional cell of the weighted permutohedron

.
The codimenison-1 cells of

are of the form

and every codimension-1 cell appears in the boundary of

The sign of the cell

in

is

(-1)^{n_{i_{1}}+\cdots+n_{i_{l}}}\cdot\text{wsgn}(\text{permutation }W_{1}\cdots W_{m}\mapsto W_{i_{1}}\cdots W_{i_{l}}|W_{i_{l+1}}\cdots W_{i_{m}}),

and the sign of

in
(A,\mathcal{W},w)
is

(-1)^{n_{i_{1}}+\cdots+n_{i_{l}}}\cdot\text{wsgn}(\text{permutation }\sigma^{-1}(W_{1}\cdots W_{m})\mapsto W_{i_{1}}\cdots W_{i_{l}}|W_{i_{l+1}}\cdots W_{i_{m}})

=(-1)^{n_{i_{1}}+\cdots+n_{i_{l}}}\cdot\text{wsgn}(\text{permutation }W_{1}\cdots W_{m}\mapsto W_{i_{1}}\cdots W_{i_{l}}|W_{i_{l+1}}\cdots W_{i_{m}})\cdot\text{wsgn}(\sigma).

Thus, they differ by

Therefore

∎

We will show that we can replace proper filters with proper averaged-filters in a basis for rational-homology.
First, we show that every averaged-filter can be written as a sum of proper averaged-filters.

Proposition 3.32

1.

The largest labeled disk in each proper wheel (regardless of whether or not it is in a (averaged-) filter) comes first.

In an averaged-filter on
2

Proof.

3

By Proposition
3.28

m
wheels in the basis of Theorem

Next, we prove the existence of a family of relations that relate concatenation products of wheels and averaged-filters.

Lemma 3.33
.

Given
m+1\geq 3

W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,W(i_{m+1,1},\dots,i_{m+1,n_{l+1}})

m-1
of the

=\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}\pm AF\big(W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,\widehat{W(i_{k,1},\dots,i_{k,n_{k}})},\dots,W(i_{m+1,1},\dots,i_{m+1,n_{m+1}})\big)|W(i_{k,1},\dots,i_{k,n_{k}}),

We prove this by considering cycles in

as the images of these cycles in

are the classes in question,

23
for visualization of an example of this relation at the level of weighted permutohedra.
The top dimensional cell

is the only
m
-cell in

.
Since
\partial
is a boundary map it follows that
\partial^{2}(W_{1}\,\cdots\,W_{m+1})=0
in
C_{m-2}\Big(P\big(\{W_{1},\dots,W_{m+1}\},\{n_{1},\dots,n_{m+1}\}\big);\mathbb{Q}\Big)

By our assumption on the sizes of the wheels with respect to
w
, every
(m-2)

By the nature of permutohedra, every codimension-1 cell in

\pm 1

\partial^{2}(W_{1}\,\cdots\,W_{m+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\\
i_{j+1}<\cdots<i_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\pm\partial(W_{i_{1}}\,\cdots\,W_{i_{j}}|W_{i_{j+1}}\,\cdots\,W_{i_{m+1}})=0

in
C_{m-2}\Big(P\big(\{W_{1},\dots,W_{m+1}\},\{n_{1},\dots,n_{m+1}\},w\big);\mathbb{Q}\Big)
.

Not all
(m-1)
-cells in

are in

;
namely, the cells of the form

and

Moreover, for all

+\sum_{j=2}^{m+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\\
i_{j+1}<\cdots<i_{m+1}\end{subarray}}\pm\partial(W_{i_{1}}\,\cdots\,W_{i_{j}}|W_{i_{j+1}}\,\cdots\,W_{i_{m+1}}).

=\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}\pm W(i_{k,1},\dots,i_{k,n_{k}})|AF\big(W(i_{1,1},\dots,i_{1,n_{1}}),\dots,\widehat{W(i_{k,1},\dots,i_{k,n_{k}})},\dots,W(i_{m+1,1},\dots,i_{m+1,n_{m+1}})\big)\\

Lemma 3.34
.

The homology group
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(n,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
has a basis consisting of concatenations of proper wheels and proper non-trivial averaged-filters such that in each wheel inside or outside an averaged-filter the highest labeled disk comes first.
We say one wheel ranks above another if it has more disks, or has the same number of disks and its largest label is greater.
A cycle is in the basis if and only if:

1.

The wheels in an averaged-filter are in order of increasing rank.

2.

Adjacent wheels not inside an averaged-filter are in order of decreasing rank.

3.

Every wheel immediately to the left of an averaged-filter ranks above the least wheel in the averaged-filter.

Proof.

There is a bijection between the basis in Theorem
3.19
and the set described in the lemma arising from that map that takes proper filters to proper averaged-filters on the same set of wheels (note an averaged-filter on 2 wheels is the same as a filter on 2 wheels).
Thus, if we can write the basis elements of Theorem
3.19
in terms of the elements described in the lemma, it follows that the set described in the lemma is a basis.

An element in the basis of Theorem
3.19
is a concatenation product of proper wheels and proper filters, i.e., it is of the form

Z_{1}|\cdots|Z_{r}

for some
r
, where
Z_{j}
is either a proper wheel
W_{j}
or a proper filter
F_{j}
, and consecutive
Z_{j}
satisfy the requirements of points
3
and
4
of Theorem
3.19
.
By Propositions
3.28
and
3.26
we can write the proper filters on
m
wheels in a basis element of Theorem
3.19
as a sum of proper averaged-filters on at most
m
wheels such these averaged-filters satisfy point
1
, after potentially applying the relation of Proposition
3.26
to rearrange the wheels in an averaged-filter.
Moreover, the largest labeled disk in each wheel comes first.
Therefore,

Z_{1}|\cdots|Z_{r}=\sum a_{Z^{\prime}_{1}|\cdots|Z^{\prime}_{r}}Z^{\prime}_{1}|\cdots|Z^{\prime}_{r}

where, if
Z_{j}
is the proper wheel
W_{j}
, then
Z^{\prime}_{j}
is the proper wheel
W_{j}
, and if
Z_{j}
is a proper filter on
m\geq 3
wheels, then
Z^{\prime}_{j}
is a proper averaged-filter on at most
l\leq m
wheels
AF_{j}(W_{j,1},W_{j,2},\dots,W_{j,l})
.
Note, if
Z_{j}=F(W_{j,1},W_{j,2})
, then
Z^{\prime}_{j}=AF(W_{j,1},W_{j,2})=F(W_{j,1},W_{j,2})
.
Unfortunately, it does not follow that consecutive
Z^{\prime}_{j}
satisfy the requirements of points
2
and
3
of the lemma.

It suffices to show that every concatenation product of the form

Z^{\prime}_{1}|\cdots|Z^{\prime}_{k}

where the
Z^{\prime}_{j}
are proper wheels where the largest label comes first or proper averaged-filters on at least
2
wheels satisfying point
1
such that the largest label in each wheel comes first, can be written as a sum of elements in the set described in the lemma.

The conditions in the lemma allow anything (a wheel or an averaged-filter) to come after an averaged-filter.
Therefore, it suffices to show that if we have a concatenation product of proper wheels such that in each proper wheel the largest label comes first and such that together the wheels satisfy property
3
, and we concatenate to the right of this any proper wheel such that the largest label comes first or any proper averaged-filter satisfying point
1
such that in each wheel the largest label is first the resulting concatenation product is equal to a sum of elements in the set described in the lemma such that the set of proper wheels (both those not in and in an averaged-filter) is the same.

We proceed by induction on the number of wheels in the string of wheels.
This is true if we start with the empty string of wheels, as anything can follow an averaged-filter.
Next, we assume this is true if there are
j-1
wheels in the concatenation product.
Since the
Z^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Z^{\prime}_{j}
are all wheels, we write them as
Z^{\prime}_{l}=W_{l}
where for all

W_{l}
is a proper wheel such that the largest label comes first, i.e.,
W_{l}=W(i_{l,1},\dots,i_{l,n_{l}})
such that

for all

In this section we recall the definition of a twisted (commutative) algebra, and use twisted algebras to state and prove our main results. Alpert–Manin proved that the homology of the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width

Proposition 4.22
.

For all

,

4.3
Higher Order Stability

In this section, we prove that the ordered configuration space of open unit-diameter disks in the infinite strip of width
w
exhibits reasonable notions of higher order representation stability.
In the previous section, we proved that
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b+1)

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b+1)
varies.
This is different from the case of first-order representation stability for configuration spaces of points in connected noncompact manifolds, where the twisted algebra of first-order stability is always isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{1}

.
In their statement of second-order representation stability for the ordered configuration space of points in a connected noncompact manifold, Miller–Wilson used the zeroth
\mathcal{L}_{1}
-homology of the homology of configuration space.
Unfortunately, we cannot do the same for finite
b
.
However, by taking a limit we are able to remedy this without losing any information.

Recall that Proposition
4.17
proves that any module
M
over
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b)
can be made a module over
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b+1)

for all

is the fundamental class of the

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b)
.
By setting

for all

and all

and where

, it follows that for all
k
and
w
we have that

is an
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(\infty)
-module, and

H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(\infty)}_{0}\Big(H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)\Big)=H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(b+1)}_{0}\Big(H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)\Big).

As a corollary to Theorem
4.23
, for all
k\geq 0
and
w\geq 2
, the homology groups
H_{k}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)
have the structure of an
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(\infty)
-module, finitely generated in degree

for

and degree

for

.

It follows that the twisted algebra

-module, though it is not finitely generated as we need generators in each degree

.
Still, we can make sense of the the zeroth

-homology of

.
Doing so will allow us prove higher order representation stability results.

Proposition 4.24

\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)

The maximal number of rational
d
-barriers in
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
is equal to the largest non-trivial term of the sequence
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)
.
By Lemma
4.26
, every element in
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)
can be written as a sum of products of wheels on at least
d+1
disks, and averaged-filters.

When
2d+1\leq w
, the least efficient of these generators in terms of homological degree to the number of disks are the wheels on
d+1
disks.
When
|\bullet|=(d+1)i
, the quotient
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d-1}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)\neq 0
, as the product of
i

.

\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)

\leq(d+1)i

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
-module.
Since each term of
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
is finite dimensional, it follows that
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
-module.

If
2d=w
, the least efficient of the generators in terms of homological degree to the number of disks are the proper averaged-filters on
w+1
disks.
When
|\bullet|=(d+1)i+d
, the quotient
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)\neq 0
, as the product of
i

w+1

\leq(d+1)i+d

We use Proposition
4.27
to prove the following theorem which shows that the ordered configuration space of open unit diameter disks in the infinite strip of width

1^{\text{st}}-
through

Theorem 4.28
.

Let

\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet):=H^{\mathcal{L}_{d-1}(\infty)}_{0}\Bigg(\cdots\bigg(H^{\mathcal{L}_{1}(\infty)}_{0}\Big(H_{\frac{(d-1)|\bullet|+i}{d}}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)\Big)\bigg)\cdots\Bigg).

For
1\leq d\leq\big\lfloor\frac{w+1}{3}\big\rfloor
,

\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)

is a finitely generated
\mathcal{L}_{d}(b+1)
-module, where
b=\big\lfloor\frac{di}{w-d}\big\rfloor
, generated in degree
(d+1)i
for
2d+1\leq w
, and degree
(d+1)i+d
for
2d=w

Proof.

By Proposition
4.27
, for
1\leq d\leq\big\lfloor\frac{w+1}{3}\big\rfloor
,

\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet):=H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d-1}(\infty)}_{0}\Bigg(\cdots\bigg(H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{1}(\infty)}_{0}\Big(H_{\frac{(d-1)|\bullet|+i}{d}}\big(\text{conf}(\bullet,w);\mathbb{Q}\big)\Big)\bigg)\cdots\Bigg)

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
-module generated in degree
(d+1)i
for
w\geq 2d+1
, and degree
(d+1)i+d
for
w=2d
.

We count the maximum number of barriers in an element in
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
as this will allow us to replace
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
with the finitely generated twisted algebra
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(b+1)
without loss of information.

For
2d+1\leq w
, the module
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
is generated in degree at most
(d+1)i
.
This occurs in homological degree

\frac{(d-1)(d+1)i+i}{d}=di.

By Lemma
4.26
, every element in
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)
can be written as a product of wheels on at least
d+1
disks, and averaged-filters.
The smallest of these generators that are also rational
d
-barriers are the wheels on
w+1-d
disks, which have homological degree
w-d
.
We can have at most
\big\lfloor\frac{di}{w-d}\big\rfloor
rational
d
-barriers in an element of
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
.
Since multiplication is well defined by Proposition
4.22
, we only need the first

of the copies of

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)

.

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
to
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(b+1)
, and not lose information on the structure of
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
as an
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)
-module. It follows from Proposition
4.27
, that for
w\geq 2d+1
,
\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(b+1)
-module generated in degree

.

\frac{(d-1)\big((d+1)i+d\big)+i}{d}=\frac{d^{2}i-i+d^{2}-d+i}{d}=di+d-1.

By Lemma
4.26
, every element in
H^{\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)}_{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{d}_{i}(\bullet)\big)

d+1
disks, and averaged-filters.

d
-barriers in terms of the homological degree are the wheels on
w+1-d

w-d
.
Therefore, since
2d=w

b+1:=\big\lfloor\frac{di}{w-d}\big\rfloor+1
of the copies of

\mathcal{L}^{w}_{d}(\infty)

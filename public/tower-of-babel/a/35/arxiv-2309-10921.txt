Octopuses in the Boolean cube: families with pairwise small intersections, part II
Andrey Kupavskii, Fedor Noskov

Abstract
The problem we consider originally arises from 2-level polytope theory. This class of polytopes generalizes a number of other polytope families. One of the important questions in this filed can be formulated as follows: is it true for a d d -dimensional 2-level polytope that the product of the number of its vertices and the number of its d − 1 d-1 dimensional facets is bounded by d ​ 2 d − 1 d2^{d-1} ? Recently, Kupavskii and Weltge [ 9 ] settled this question in positive. A key element in their proof is a more general result for families of vectors in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} such that the scalar product between any two vectors from different families is either 0 0 or 1 1 . Peter Frankl noted that, when restricted to the Boolean cube, the solution boils down to an elegant application of the Harris–Kleitman correlation inequality. Meanwhile, this problem becomes much more sophisticated when we consider several families. Let ℱ 1 , … , ℱ ℓ \mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{\ell} be families of subsets of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\} . We suppose that for distinct k , k ′ k,k^{\prime} and arbitrary F 1 ∈ ℱ k , F 2 ∈ ℱ k ′ F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{k},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}} we have | F 1 ∩ F 2 | ⩽ m . |F_{1}\cap F_{2}|\leqslant m. We are interested in the maximal value of | ℱ 1 | ​ … ​ | ℱ ℓ | |\mathcal{F}_{1}|\ldots|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}| and the structure of the extremal example. In the previous paper on the topic, the authors found the asymptotics of this product for constant ℓ \ell and m m as n n tends to infinity. However, the possible structure of the families from the extremal example turned out to be very complicated. In this paper, we obtain a strong structural result for the extremal families.

1.2
Problem statement and results

In this work, we study the following problem.

Problem 1

It is easy to see that

]

11

Ryser studied a similar question about the structure of sets with overlapping property. In particular, he showed that if for

Definition 2

Given an arbitrary family

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\mathcal{F}=\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k+1}:\exists F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t. }F^{\prime}\subset F\right\}.

4.3
Symmetrization lemma

The central lemma of this subsection uses a symmetrization argument inspired by the famous Zykov’s symmetrization
[
12
]
who used it for an alternative proof of Turan’s theorem. Before we will provide the statement, we introduce some new notation. Given a family
\mathcal{F}
and a set
S
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\overset{\circ}{S}\right)

\displaystyle=\{F\setminus S:F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }S\subset F\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\overset{\bullet}{S}\right)

\displaystyle=\{F:F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }S\subset F\}.

The family
\mathcal{F}\left(\overset{\circ}{S}\right)
coincides with previously defined
\mathcal{F}(S)
. However, we need some notation to describe precisely whether the set
S
remains or not. Symbols ‘‘
\circ
’’ and ‘‘
\bullet
’’ can be naturally combined in our context, for example,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\overset{\circ}{S}\sqcup\overset{\bullet}{T}\right)=\{F\setminus S:F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }S\cup T\subset F\}.

Besides, we remind that
\mathcal{F}(\overline{S})
is usually used for the sets that do not intersect
S
. For the sets that do not contain
S
we use
\mathcal{F}(\overset{\diamond}{S}):=\mathcal{F}\setminus\left[\mathcal{F}(\overset{\bullet}{S})\right]
.

As before, we suppress braces of singletons.

Under a
tentacle
of a family
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
we mean any its set
S_{0}\subset\bigcup_{k^{\prime}:(k,k^{\prime})\in E(T_{\ell})}C^{*}_{k^{\prime}}
. As it was discussed in Section
3
, the idea of the lemma is enlarging families by chopping off tentacles of small degree and replacing them with a copy of a large degree tentacle. The method can be described as follows: if
d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})
is small, choose
S
of a large degree, then for each set
F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S})
add
F\cup S_{0}
to the family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
.

Such a scheme typically breaks the
\mathbf{m}
-overlapping property. Instead, we use the modified procedure. It consists of three steps:

1.

Replace all families
\mathcal{F}_{k}
with
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}})
.

2.

Choose
S
such that
\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|/|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|
is large and for each porbabilistic center
C^{*}_{k}
it holds that
|S\cap C^{*}_{k}|=|S_{0}\cap C^{*}_{k}|
.

3.

For each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
, add
F\cup S_{0}
to
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}
. Denote this new family by
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
.

If we would not intersect
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S})
with
\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
, then the
\mathbf{m}
-overlapping property can be broken. Indeed, no one guarantees that there is no set
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S})
and a set
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
for some
k^{\prime}\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}
such that
|(F\cup S_{0})\cap F^{\prime}|>\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, families
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\bullet}{S^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}})
preserve the
\mathbf{m}
-overlapping property for any

, and the intersection from the step

makes the procedure correct. If

then
\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
is assumed to be
\mathcal{F}_{k}
.

Under some conditions

turns out to be larger than the initial product, and that leads to contradiction if the families
\mathcal{F}_{k}
form an extremal example. For instance, step
1
should not reduce families significantly, and thus all degrees

must be small. In what follows, we show that it is true if the tentacle
S_{0}

G
has cardinality
|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|^{-1}\cdot O(n^{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}}\log n)
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}d_{k_{1}}(T)\leqslant n^{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}n^{-2\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+|S_{0}|-3}=n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2}.

(22)

If
S_{0}
completely lies in
C^{*}_{k_{1}}
for some
k_{1}
, i.e. we are in case
(a)
, then modify families in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}}),&k\neq k_{1},\\
\mathcal{F}_{k\setminus k_{0}}\cap\bigcap_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}(\overset{\diamond}{T}),&k=k_{1}.\end{cases}

(23)

In case
(b)
, just consider
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}})
for all
k
and choose
S_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
arbitrary.

Next, we need to add sets to
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
. Define:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}=\begin{cases}\left[\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right]\setminus\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{T}),&\text{if \ref{symmetrization lemma: case 1} holds},\\
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}}),&\text{if\penalty\ \ref{symmetrization lemma: case 2} holds}.\end{cases}

Also define
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}=\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}\sqcup(\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}\vee\{S_{0}\})
. The families
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}
,
k\neq k_{0}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
are the desired ‘‘symmetrized’’ families.

We should check that the
\mathbf{m}
-overlapping property is preserved. First, we check that the families
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime},k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\},
are down-closed. The case of
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}
,
k\in[n]\setminus\{k_{0}\}
, is obvious. Proving that for
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
is a bit more tricky. Consider a set
F\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
.

•

If
S_{0}\subset F
then
F\not\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}
and, hence,
F\setminus S_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}
, we have
F\setminus S_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
. The latter family is down-closed; consequently, for any
G\subset F\setminus S_{0}
we also have
G\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
. Moreover, if
(a)
holds, for any
T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})
, we have
T\not\subset F
which implies
T\not\subset G
, and so
G\not\in\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{T})
. Thus,
G\cup S_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}\vee\{S_{0}\}
, and so
G\cup S_{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
.

Since
G\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})
, for any
S^{\prime}\not\subset S_{0}
we have
G\cup S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
. But
S_{0}\subsetneq G\cup S^{\prime}
, and, hence,
G\cup S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
. As a result, any subset of
F
belongs to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}
.

•

If
S_{0}\not\subset F
, then
F\in\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}
which is down-closed.

Since we verified down-closeness, it is enough to check that there are no two families indexed by
k^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime\prime}
that both contain a set of size
\mathbf{m}_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}+1
due to Claim
13
. Consider several cases.

•

If
k_{0}\not\in\{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime\prime}}
, and, consequently, they can not share a set of size
\mathbf{m}_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}+1
.

•

If
k_{0}\in\{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\}
, say
k^{\prime\prime}=k_{0}
, and
k^{\prime}\neq k_{1}
then
\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}\vee\{S_{0}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}})
have no common sets. At the same time,
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}
and, thus,
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
does not contain a set of size
\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k^{\prime}}+1
.

•

The final case
\{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\}=\{k_{0},k_{1}\}
appears only if the case
(a)
holds. Suppose that there exists
F\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}
of size
\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1
. If
S_{0}\subset F
, then
F\setminus S_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle F\setminus S_{0}\not\in\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{T})

by the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}
. Since
|F\setminus S_{0}|=\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|
, it implies
F\setminus S_{0}\not\in\mathcal{R}
. At the same time, the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}
requires

\displaystyle F\in\bigcap_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}(\overset{\diamond}{T}),

that necessarily implies either
F\setminus S_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
or
F\cap C^{*}_{k_{0}}\neq\varnothing
. We saw the former does not hold just above. By the definition (
23
), sets from
\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}
do not intersect
C^{*}_{k_{0}}
, so the latter also leads to contradiction.

The last point is to consider the product of the modified families. In case
(a)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\diamond}{S_{0}})|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}+\frac{|\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1-d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})\right)+\left(\frac{\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})}d_{k_{0}}(S_{1}\cup T)\right).

(24)

Since
S_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
, we have
\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subset S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|\geqslant\left(d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+\Delta\right)|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|
by the definition. Meanwhile, by the definition of
\mathcal{R}
, we have
T\cap C^{*}_{k_{0}}=\varnothing
for each
T\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})
. Due to Proposition
12
, it holds that

\displaystyle d_{k_{0}}(S_{1}\cup T)\prod_{k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}}d_{k}\left((S_{1}\cup T)\cap C^{*}_{k}\right)=O(n^{-|S_{1}\cup T|})=O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-1}),

so
d_{k_{0}}(S_{1}\cup T)=O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-1})
due to Claim
16
. Claim
19
guarantees that
|\mathcal{N}_{G}(S_{1})|=deg_{G}(S_{1})=\frac{O(n^{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}})\log n}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}
, and, combining the above with inequality (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}\geqslant 1-d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}=1+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}.

(25)

At the same time,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}|}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}\setminus k_{0}}\cap\bigcap_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}(\overset{\diamond}{T})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}|}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}\setminus\left[\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}\to k_{0}}\cup\bigcup_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}(\overset{\bullet}{T})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-d_{k_{1}\to k_{0}}-\sum_{T\in\binom{[n]\setminus C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}+1-|S_{0}|}\setminus\mathcal{R}}d_{k_{1}}(T).

The sum can be bounded by inequality (
22
), and we have
d_{k_{1}\to k_{0}}=e^{-\Omega(n)}
according to Lemma
17
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}|}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-e^{-\Omega(n)}-n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2}).

(26)

Finally, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}|/|\mathcal{F}_{k}|\geqslant 1-\frac{\Delta/2}{\ell-2}
for
k\not\in\{k_{0},k_{1}\}
due to the condition. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}|\prod_{k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}|}{\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}\right)\left(1-O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2})\right)\left(1-\frac{\Delta/2}{\ell-2}\right)^{\ell-2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}\right)\left(1-O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2})\right)\left(1-\Delta/2\right),

where we used bounds (
25
) and (
26
). Due to extremality of the families
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, the right-hand side can not be large than
1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left(1+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}\right)\left(1-\Delta/2\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2}),

\displaystyle 1+\Delta-\frac{O(n^{-1}\log n)}{|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2})}{1-\Delta/2},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1-\Delta/2}{\Delta(1-\Delta)/2-O(n^{-\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{1}}-2})}\cdot O\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right).

If
(b)
holds, assume that
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}\neq\varnothing
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{0}})|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}+\frac{|\mathcal{V}_{k_{0}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+\frac{\left|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S_{1}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subset S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{definition of }\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}}{\geqslant}1-d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+d_{k_{0}}(S_{0})+\Delta

\displaystyle=1+\Delta.

Due to the condition in case
(b)
, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}|\geqslant\left(1-\frac{\Delta/2}{\ell-1}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{k}|
for
k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k_{0}}|\cdot\prod_{k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}|}{\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+\Delta)\left(1-\frac{\Delta/2}{\ell-1}\right)^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\geqslant(1+\Delta)(1-\Delta/2)

\displaystyle=1+\Delta/2-\Delta^{2}/2

\displaystyle>1.

Thus, the assumption that
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}\neq\varnothing
was wrong.

∎

4.4
Localization of tentacles

In this section, we refine structure of the extremal examples. In Section
4.2
, we showed that each extremal example forms an oriented graph. Using our octopus metaphor, it means that for any two octopuses only one puts their tentacles into another’s body. Next, we prove that tentacles of different octopuses essentially do not overlap.

We assume that there exists at least one pair
\{k,k^{\prime}\}
such that
\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}>0
. Otherwise, results of this section become meaningless. The first result is about the source in the oriented graph
T_{\ell}
if it exists.

Proposition 21
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be families from an extremal example and
T_{\ell}
be an oriented graph defined above. If
k_{0}\in V(T_{\ell})
is a source and
n
is large enough, then
C^{*}_{k_{0}}=\varnothing
.

Proof.

Let
k_{0}
be a source and suppose the probabilistic center
C^{*}_{k_{0}}
of
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
is not empty. Take
x_{0}\in C^{*}_{k_{0}}
. Consider the families
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overline{x_{0}})
. Due to Lemma
17
, if
k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0}\}
, then
|\mathcal{G}_{k}|\geqslant\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{k}|
, and
|\mathcal{G}_{k_{0}}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|
because of down-closeness. Next, consider an arbitrary index
k_{1}\neq k_{0}
such that
\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}\neq 0
for some
k_{2}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}_{k},&\text{if }k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{1},k_{2}\},\\
\mathcal{G}_{k}\cup\{F\cup\{x_{0}\}:F\in\mathcal{F}_{k\setminus k_{0}}\},&\text{if }k=k_{1},\\
\mathcal{G}_{k}\cup\{F\cup\{x_{0}\}:F\subset C^{*}_{k}\},&\text{if }k=k_{2}.\end{cases}

In this way, we obtain that
|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}|\geqslant\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{k}|
if
k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0},k_{1},k_{2}\}
, and
|\mathcal{G}_{k_{1}}^{\prime}|=(2-e^{-\Omega(n)})|\mathcal{F}_{k_{1}}|
due to Lemma
17
. For
k=k_{2}
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{k_{2}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant n^{\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{2}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}}\cdot 2^{|C^{*}_{k_{2}}|},

and, consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{k_{2}}^{\prime}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{2}}|}\geqslant\begin{cases}1-e^{-\Omega(n)}+n^{-\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{2}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}},&\text{if }k_{0}\neq k_{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}+n^{-\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{2}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}},&\text{if }k_{0}=k_{2}.\end{cases}

Analogously,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{k_{0}}^{\prime}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}\geqslant\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\text{if }k_{0}\neq k_{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}+n^{-\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{2}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}},&\text{if }k_{0}=k_{2}.\end{cases}

Thus, if
k_{2}=k_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}|\geqslant 2\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+n^{-\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{2}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}}\right)\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)^{\ell-2}\prod_{k\in\ell}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|.

If
k_{2}\neq k_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}|\geqslant 2\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}+n^{-\sum_{k\in[\ell]\setminus k_{0}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k_{2}}}\right)\frac{1}{2}\left(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\right)^{\ell-2}\prod_{k\in\ell}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|.

Both cases contradict the extremality of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
since families
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}
’s are
\mathbf{m}
-overlapping. Thus, the source has an empty center.
∎

Together with Lemma
20
, Proposition
21
allows to give a refined description of the structure of the families. We use the following notation: for an extremal family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and an oriented graph
T_{\ell}
define

\displaystyle In_{k}

\displaystyle=\{k^{\prime}:(k^{\prime},k)\in E(T_{\ell})\},

\displaystyle Out_{k}

\displaystyle=\{k^{\prime}:(k,k^{\prime})\in E(T_{\ell})\}.

Then, we state the following.

Corollary 22
.

For two distinct families
\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}
from the extremal example, define

\displaystyle A_{k,k^{\prime}}=\left\{x\in C^{*}_{k^{\prime}}:d_{k}(x)\geqslant\frac{n^{-1}}{4\ell-2}\right\}.

Then, for any edge
(k,k^{\prime})
in the oriented graph
T_{\ell}
, the set
A_{k,k^{\prime}}
has size
\Omega(n)
, and

(A_{k,k^{\prime}})_{(k,k^{\prime})\in E(T_{\ell})}
is a partition of
[n]
. In addition, for each
k
the center
C^{*}_{k}
is partitioned by
A_{k,k^{\prime}}
,
k^{\prime}\in In_{k}
.

Proof.

Given
x
, there is
k_{0}
such that
x\in C^{*}_{k_{0}}
due to Lemma
15
. Proposition
21
guarantees that there is
k_{1}\in In_{k_{0}}
. Moreover, from Lemma
17

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in C^{*}_{k_{0}}}d_{k_{1}}(y)\geqslant d_{k_{1}\to k_{0}}=1-O(n^{-1/2}),

and, consequently, the set
J=\{y\in C^{*}_{k_{0}}:d_{k_{1}}(y)\geqslant n^{-1}/2\}
has size
\Omega(n)
.

Assume that
x\not\in\bigcup_{(k,k^{\prime})\in E(T_{\ell})}A_{k,k^{\prime}}
. Then,
d_{k^{\prime}}(x)<n^{-1}/(4\ell-2)
for any
k^{\prime}\neq k_{0}
. Apply Lemma
20
with
S_{0}=\{x\}
,
\mathcal{S}_{k}=\binom{C^{*}_{k_{0}}}{1}
and
\Delta=n^{-1}(1/2-(4\ell-2)^{-1})
. Then
\binom{J}{1}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
. On the one hand,
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
has logarithmic size, and on the other hand,
|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|\geqslant|J|=\Omega(n)
, a contradiction.

Obviously,
C^{*}_{k_{0}}
is partitioned by sets
A_{k,k_{0}}
for
k\in In_{k_{0}}
. Since
J\subset A_{k_{1},k_{0}}
by definition,
A_{k_{1},k_{0}}
has size
\Omega(n)
.
∎

Now, it is easy to obtain the following improvement of Lemma
11
:

Proposition 23
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,
k\in[\ell]
be families from the extremal example. Define

\displaystyle Supp_{k}=\bigsqcup_{k^{\prime}\in In_{k}}A_{k^{\prime},k}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}A_{k,k^{\prime}}=C^{*}_{k}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}A_{k,k^{\prime}}.

Then, for each
k

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}\right|\geqslant\left(1-\delta_{n}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{k}|,

where
\delta_{n}=O(n^{-1})
.

Proof.

Consider some particular
k_{0}\in[\ell]
. Let
x\not\in Supp_{k_{0}}
. Then there are
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
,
k_{0}\not\in\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
such that
d_{k_{1}}(x)=\Theta(1)
due to Lemma
15
and
d_{k_{2}}(x)=\Theta(n^{-1})
according to Corollary
22
. From Proposition
12
we have

\displaystyle d_{k_{0}}(x)d_{k_{1}}(x)d_{k_{2}}(x)=O(n^{-3}),

and, consequently,
d_{k_{0}}(x)=O(n^{-2})
. Finally, we get

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|_{Supp_{k_{0}}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}|}\geqslant 1-\sum_{x\not\in Supp_{k_{0}}}d_{k_{0}}(x)=1-O(n^{-1}).

One nice property of
\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}
is that
\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{k}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}=2^{\bigcup_{k^{\prime}\in In_{k}}A_{k^{\prime},k}}\vee\bigvee_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{A_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}.

(27)

For families
\mathcal{H}_{k},k\in[\ell]
, the following claim holds.

Claim 24
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be an extremal family. Then
\mathcal{H}_{k}
defined in (
27
) has cardinality

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}_{k}|=(1-O(n^{-1}))|\mathcal{F}_{k}|.

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\prod_{k\in[\ell]}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}\right|\leqslant\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{H}_{k}|\leqslant\prod_{k\in[\ell]}|\mathcal{F}_{k}|.

Since
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}|=(1-O(n^{-1}))|\mathcal{F}_{k}|
for any
k
, the statement holds.
∎

Note, that the degrees
|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|
for different
S
can be expressed preciesly as a function of
A_{k,k^{\prime}},k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}
. For example, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(x)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}=\frac{\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}\setminus\{x\}|}{\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}-1}}{\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}{\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}}=\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right).

for any
(k,k^{\prime})\in E(T_{\ell})
and
x\in A_{k,k^{\prime}}
. In what follows, we show that for any set
S
of cardinality at most
\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\approx\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|},

for an extremal
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. However, we start by the following simple claim.

Claim 25
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}_{k},k\in[\ell]
form an extremal example. Then, for any edge
(k_{1},k_{2})
in the corresponding oriented graph
T_{\ell}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\min_{x\in A_{k_{1},k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)\leqslant\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}{|A_{k_{1},k_{2}}|}+O\left(n^{-3/2}\right).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle d_{k_{1}\to k_{2}}^{(\geqslant\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\binom{C^{*}_{k_{2}}}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}}d_{k_{2}}(S)\geqslant\frac{1}{\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}\sum_{x\in C^{*}_{k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)-d_{k_{1}\to k_{2}}^{(<\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}})},

where the last inequality follows from the double counting. Due to Lemma
17
, we have
d_{k\to k^{\prime}}^{(\geqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}})}\leqslant 1-O(n^{-1/2})
, and, consequently, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in C^{*}_{k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}-O(n^{-1/2}).

(28)

If
x\in C^{*}_{k_{2}}\setminus A_{k_{1},k_{2}}
, then there exists
k_{3}\in In_{k_{2}}
such that
d_{k_{3}}(x)=\Omega(n^{-1})
due to Corollary
22
. Due to Proposition
12
, we have

\displaystyle d_{k_{3}}(x)d_{k_{2}}(x)d_{k_{1}}(x)=O(n^{-3}).

Since
d_{k_{1}}(x)=\Omega(1)
due to the definition of
C^{*}_{k},k\in[\ell]
, we get
d_{k_{2}}(x)=O(n^{-2})
. Thus, the sum (
28
) can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}-O(n^{-1/2})\leqslant\sum_{x\in C^{*}_{k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)=\sum_{x\in A_{k_{1},k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)+\sum_{x\in C^{*}_{k_{2}}\setminus A_{k_{1},k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)=\sum_{x\in A_{k_{1},k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)+O(n^{-1}).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{x\in A_{k_{1},k_{2}}}d_{k_{1}}(x)\leqslant\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k_{1},k_{2}}}{|A_{k_{1},k_{2}}|}+O(n^{-3/2}),

where we used the fact that
|A_{k_{1},k_{2}}|=\Omega(n)
due to Corollary
22
.
∎

Since we want to prove
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subset\mathcal{F}_{k}
, the natural question is arising: how families behave outside
Supp_{k}
? We will provide the answer after the following auxiliary proposition that continues Claim
16
in some sense.

Proposition 26
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k},k\in[\ell]
form an extremal example and suppose that
(k_{1},k_{2})
is an edge in the corresponding oriented graph
T_{\ell}
. Then a set

\displaystyle I_{T}^{k_{1},k_{2}}=\{x\in A_{k_{1},k_{2}}:d_{k_{1}}(T\cup\{x\})\leqslant 2^{-|T|-1}d_{k_{1}}(x)\}

has cardinality
O(\sqrt{n\log n})
uniformly over
T\subset\binom{C^{*}_{k_{1}}}{\leqslant\max_{S}\mathbf{m}_{S}}
.

The proof is given in the appendix, Section
A.1
.

The next corollary explains how
\mathcal{F}_{k}
behaves outside
Supp_{k}
.

Corollary 27
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
be a family from the extremal example. Then for each
S\in\bigvee_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k_{0}}}\binom{A_{k_{0},k^{\prime}}}{\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k^{\prime}}}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\not\in Supp_{k_{0}}}d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})=O\left(n^{-|S|-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\right).

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{F}_{k_{0}}
and
S
from the statement and define
S_{k}=S\cap C^{*}_{k}
,
k\in[\ell]
. Consider
x
outside
Supp_{k_{0}}
and suppose that
x\in A_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}\subset C^{*}_{k^{\prime\prime}}
for some
k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}
. From Proposition
12
we get

\displaystyle d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime}}\cup\{x\})d_{k^{\prime\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime\prime}}\cup\{x\})\prod_{k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0},k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\}}d_{k}(S_{k})=O(n^{-|S|-3}).

The element
x
contributes to the exponent
3
and the partition of
S
contributes
|S|
. Using Claim
16
, we infer
d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime}}\cup\{x\})=O(n^{-|S|-3})
. Consider
I^{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}_{S_{k^{\prime}}}
from Proposition
26
. If
x\not\in I^{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}_{S_{k^{\prime}}}
, then
d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime}}\cup\{x\})=\Omega(d_{k^{\prime}}(x))=\Omega(n^{-1})
by the definition of
I^{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}_{S_{k^{\prime}}}
, and so
d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})=O(n^{-|S|-2})
.

If
x\in I_{S_{k^{\prime}}}^{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime}})d_{k^{\prime\prime}}(S_{k^{\prime\prime}}\cup\{x\})\prod_{k\in[\ell]\setminus\{k_{0},k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\}}d_{k}(S_{k})=O(n^{-|S|-1}),

and, consequently,
d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})=O(n^{-|S|-1})
. Define
I=\bigsqcup_{k^{\prime}\in[\ell]\setminus\{k\}}\bigsqcup_{k^{\prime\prime}\in Out_{k}^{\prime}}I_{S_{k^{\prime}}}^{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}}
. Note that
|I|=O(\sqrt{n\log n})
by Proposition
26
. We get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in[n]\setminus Supp_{k}}d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})\leqslant\sum_{x\in[n]\setminus(Supp_{k}\cup I)}d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})+\sum_{x\in I}d_{k_{0}}(S\cup\{x\})=O\left(n^{-|S|-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\right).

∎

For a reminder, we expect that for families from an extremal example it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\approx\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}

for any tentacle
S
. Corollary
27
implies that for any
S
we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\leqslant\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}+O\left(n^{-|S|-1/2}\sqrt{\log n}\right).

Since
|\mathcal{H}_{k}|=|\mathcal{F}_{k}|(1-O(n^{-1}))
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\approx\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}(S)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}.

In the next section, we prove that
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}(S)|\approx|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|
for any tecntacle
S
. The key ingredients of the proof will be induction on
|S|
and the symmetrization.

4.5
Asymptotics of normalized degrees

In this section, we aim to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\approx\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}

indeed holds for any tentacle
S
. Before we move on to the key lemma of this section, we need one auxiliary proposition. As it was mentioned above, we will use the symmetrization argument. Thus, we will need to check conditions of Lemma
20
. Let
S_{0}
be from the statement of the lemma. Coniditions of the lemma require to bound normalized degrees of
S_{0}
. Unfortunately, bounds provided by Proposition
12
turn out to be too rough in some cases. To overcome this issue, we impose the following proposition.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
,
k\in[\ell]
be the families from the extremal examples. Suppose that there are three families
k_{0},k_{1},k_{2}\in[\ell]
such that
(k_{0},k_{1}),(k_{1},k_{2}),(k_{0},k_{2})\in E(T_{\ell})
. If

\left|d_{k_{0}}(x_{2})-\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k_{0},k_{2}}}{|A_{k_{0},k_{2}}|}\right|\leqslant C_{1}n^{-3/2}\log^{1/2}n,

for some constant
C_{1}
then
d_{k_{1}}(x_{2})\leqslant e^{-C_{2}n}
for some other constant
C_{2}
that depends on
C_{1}
.

Figure 5
:
Illustration for Proposition
28
.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
17
so we give it in the appendix, Section
A.2
.

The remaining part of this section is dedicated to proof of the following fact: given
k\in[\ell]
and
S\in\bigvee_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{A_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}
,
d_{k}(S)
has order
n^{-|S|}
. The proof is organized as multidimensional induction over vectors
\mathbf{s}_{k}=(|S\cap A_{k,k^{\prime}}|)_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}
. We define a family
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}=\bigvee_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{A_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}
. We say
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}\leqslant\mathbf{s}_{k}
if the coordinate-wise inequalities hold, and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}<\mathbf{s}_{k}
if and only if
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}\leqslant\mathbf{s}_{k}
and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}\neq\mathbf{s}_{k}
.

The induction first investigates all vectors
\mathbf{s}_{k},k\in[\ell]
with one non-zero entry. Then it deals with vectors
\mathbf{s}_{k}
independently for different
k
. We formalize this procedure as follows:

Induction hypothesis.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{k}
be a vector defined above.

1.

If
\mathbf{s}_{k}
has exactly one non-zero coordinate
\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}
, then for any
\hat{k}
and
\tilde{k}\in Out_{\hat{k}}
,
s<\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}
and
S\in\binom{A_{\hat{k},\tilde{k}}}{s}
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|d_{\hat{k}}(S)-\binom{\mathbf{m}_{\hat{k},\tilde{k}}}{s}/\binom{|A_{\hat{k},\tilde{k}}|}{s}\right|\leqslant O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)n^{-s}.

2.

Otherwise, if
\mathbf{s}_{k}
has more than one non-zero entry, then the previous statement holds for any
\hat{k}\in[\ell]
and any possible
\mathbf{s}_{\hat{k}}
with one non-zero position, and, in addition,

\displaystyle\left|d_{k}(S)-\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}/\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\right|\leqslant O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)n^{-|S|}

holds for any
\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\prime}<\mathbf{s}_{k}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}
.

To illustrate how the proof works, consider
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}
. The goal is to show
d_{k}(S)\approx\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(S)|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}
. Then, the steps of the proof are as follows:

•

using the induction hypothesis, show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})\cap\mathcal{G}\right|\geqslant\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right),

where
\mathcal{G}
is a family of some ‘‘good’’ sets;

•

using the symmetrization argument, i.e. Lemma
20
, show that for
S_{0}=\operatorname{argmin}_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}d_{k}(\tilde{S})
, we have

\displaystyle d_{k}(S_{0})\approx\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}||\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})\cap\mathcal{G}\right|\approx\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}d_{k}(\tilde{S});

•

using the definition of
S_{0}
, establish the lower bound on
d_{k}(S)
:

\displaystyle d_{k}(S)\geqslant\frac{1-o(1)}{|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}d_{k}(\tilde{S})\geqslant\frac{1-o(1)}{|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}|}\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}};

•

using Corollary
27
and the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{k}
, obtain the upper bound on
d_{k}(S)
.

As we clarify the proof strategy, we are ready to state the result.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be an arbitrary family from the extremal example and
S
be a subset of
\bigcup_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}A_{k,k^{\prime}}
such that
\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}:=|S\cap A_{k,k^{\prime}}|\leqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}
. Then there is a constant
C
, depending on
\mathbf{m}
only, such that

\displaystyle\left|d_{k}(S)-\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}/\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}\right|\leqslant C\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}n^{-|S|}.

Proof.

First, we obtain the lower bound on
d_{k}(S)
. Define
S_{0}=\arg\min_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}d_{k}(S)
. Choose
k^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}>0
. Define
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}<\mathbf{s}_{k}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k,k^{\prime\prime}}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime\prime}},&\text{if }k^{\prime\prime}\neq k^{\prime},\\
\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime\prime}}-1,&\text{if }k^{\prime\prime}=k^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Then, by double counting

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|=\frac{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{-1}}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\sum_{x\in A_{k,k^{\prime}}\setminus\tilde{S}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}}\cup\overset{\circ}{\{x\}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|.

(29)

Due to down-closeness, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}}\cup\overset{\circ}{\{x\}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|\geqslant\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{\{x\}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|.

Since
d_{k\to k^{\prime}}^{(\geqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}})}=1-O(n^{-1})
from Lemma
17
, we may consider only sets that intersect
C^{*}_{k^{\prime}}
by exactly
\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}
elements to bound sum (
29
) from below. A set
T\subset A_{k,k^{\prime}}
of size
\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}
such that
\tilde{S}\subset T
is counted
\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}-\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
times in the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in A_{k,k^{\prime}}\setminus\tilde{S}}

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{\{x\}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\sum_{x\in A_{k,k^{\prime}}\setminus\tilde{S}}

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}}\cup\overset{\bullet}{\{x\}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|\geqslant

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}-\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|-O(n^{-1}).

(30)

If
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{k}
is the zero vector, we already proved the following:
\sum_{x\in A_{k,k^{\prime}}}d_{k}(x)\geqslant\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}-O(n^{-1})
.

Otherwise, we use the induction hypothesis. We may apply Corollary
27
and show that for any
\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}
:

\displaystyle\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}/\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2|\tilde{S}|}}}\right)\overset{\text{I.H.}}{\leqslant}d_{k}(\tilde{S})\overset{\text{Cor.\penalty\ \ref{corollary: outside degree}}}{\leqslant}\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})|_{Supp_{k}}|}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2|\tilde{S}|}}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Claim\penalty\ \ref{claim: hh_k approximation quality}}}{\leqslant}(1+O(n^{-1}))\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})|_{Supp_{k}}|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2|\tilde{S}|}}}\right)\leqslant(1+O(n^{-1}))\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{k}(\tilde{S})|}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2|\tilde{S}|}}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{by the definition\penalty\ \eqref{eq: definition of hh_k}}\\
\text{of }\mathcal{H}_{k}\end{subarray}}{\leqslant}\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}/\binom{|A_{k,k^{\prime}}|}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2|\tilde{S}|}}}\right).

Given that
\mathcal{F}_{k}|_{Supp_{k}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{k}
the claim of the inequality above also implies
|\mathcal{H}_{k}(\tilde{S})\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})|/|\mathcal{H}_{k}|=O\left(n^{-|\tilde{S}|-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\right)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|\geqslant\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})|_{Supp_{k}}\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})|_{Supp_{k}}\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},k}}\left[\left|\mathcal{H}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{H}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|-O\left(n^{-|\tilde{S}|-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right).

Combining the above and inequalities (
29
), (
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}-\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right).

(31)

Now apply Lemma
20
for
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}
,
S_{0}=\arg\min_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}}d_{k}(S)
and
\Delta=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)n^{-|S_{0}|}
. Check the assumptions of the lemma.

•

If
\mathbf{s}_{k}
has one non-zero entry
\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}
, then case
(a)
holds. Let
\tilde{k}\in[\ell]\setminus\{k,k^{\prime}\}
be an index such that we want to show
d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})\ll\Delta
. If
|S_{0}|=1
then
d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})=O(n^{-2})
according to Propositions
12
and
22
. Next, assume
|S_{0}|\geqslant 2
and choose
x\in S_{0}
. Due to the induction hypothesis,
d_{k}(S_{0}\setminus\{x\})=\Omega(n^{-|S_{0}|+1})
. Meanwhile,

\displaystyle d_{k}(S_{0}\setminus\{x\})d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{0})

\displaystyle=O(n^{-3|S_{0}|+2}),

\displaystyle d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})=O(n^{-2|S_{0}|+1})

\displaystyle=O(n^{-|S_{0}|-1})\ll\Delta.

•

If
\mathbf{s}_{k}
has more than one non-zero entry, then we are in case
(b)
and
|S_{0}|\geqslant 2
. Let
\tilde{k}\in[\ell]\setminus\{k\}
be an arbitrary index. If
\tilde{k}\in Out_{k}
,
d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})=\exp(-\Omega(n))
because of either Proposition
28
or Lemma
17
. Otherwise, choose some
x\in S_{0}
and write down

\displaystyle d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})d_{k}(S_{0}\setminus\{x\})\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}d_{k^{\prime}}(S_{0}\cap C^{*}_{k^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=O(n^{-3|S_{0}|+2}),

\displaystyle d_{\tilde{k}}(S_{0})=O(n^{-2|S_{0}|+1})

\displaystyle=O(n^{-|S_{0}|-1})\ll\Delta.

Here we use the induction hypothesis and Proposition
12
.

Thus, the assumptions of Lemma
20
indeed hold. For a reminder, the conclusion is that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}=\left\{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}:\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{\tilde{S}})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(\overset{\circ}{S^{\prime}})\right|\geqslant d_{k}(S_{0})+\Delta\right\}

has cardinality
O\left(\frac{1}{\Delta}\frac{\log n}{n}\right)=O\left(n^{|S_{0}|}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)
. Decomposing the sum in (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(S^{\prime})\right|+(|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{s},k}|-|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|)(d_{k}(S_{0})+\Delta)\geqslant\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}\binom{\mathbf{m}_{k,k^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{s}_{k,k^{\prime}}}-O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right).

(32)

From Proposition
12
, we have

\displaystyle d_{k}(\tilde{S})\prod_{k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}}d_{k^{\prime}}(\tilde{S}\cap C^{*}_{k^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=O(n^{-|\tilde{S}|}).

Claim
16
guarantees that
d_{k^{\prime}}(\tilde{S}\cap C^{*}_{k^{\prime}})=\Omega(1)
for
k^{\prime}\in Out_{k}
. Thus
d_{k}(\tilde{S})=O(n^{-|\tilde{S}|})
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}\sum_{\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(\tilde{S})\cap\bigcap_{S^{\prime}\subsetneq S_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(S^{\prime})\right|\leqslant|\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}|O(n^{-|S_{0}|})=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right).

Consequently, the left-hand side of (

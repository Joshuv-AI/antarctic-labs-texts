Learning Recurrent Neural Net Models of Nonlinear Systems
Joshua Hanson, Maxim Raginsky, Eduardo Sontag

Abstract
We consider the following learning problem: Given sample pairs of input and output signals generated by an unknown nonlinear system (which is not assumed to be causal or time-invariant), we wish to find a continuous-time recurrent neural net with hyperbolic tangent activation function that approximately reproduces the underlying i/o behavior with high confidence. Leveraging earlier work concerned with matching output derivatives up to a given finite order ( Sontag 1998 ) , we reformulate the learning problem in familiar system-theoretic language and derive quantitative guarantees on the sup-norm risk of the learned model in terms of the number of neurons, the sample size, the number of derivatives being matched, and the regularity properties of the inputs, the outputs, and the unknown i/o map.



1
Introduction

We consider a learning-theoretic framework for system identification, where the goal is to approximate an unknown nonlinear input/output (i/o) map by a continuous-time recurrent neural net (RNN) on the basis of a finite collection of input/output pairs. The approximation criterion is the
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
norm of the difference between the ground-truth output and the one predicted by the learned model.

Earlier work
(
Sontag 1998
)
has addressed a related problem of reproducing output
k
-jets as a function of input
(k-1)
-jets via RNNs, where a
k
-
jet
is defined as the vector of derivatives up to order
k
of the output (respectively, input) signal evaluated at the initialization time. Equivalently,
k
-jets can be defined as optimal degree-
k
polynomial approximations, i.e., truncated Taylor series. In this work, we build on the aforementioned result about matching
k
-jets, but work in a familiar system-theoretic setting using the language of i/o maps.

The training procedure used both in that work and here differs to some extent from the standard “backpropagation through time” algorithm often used for training RNNs. Rather than forward-propagating training inputs through the net or setting up an adjoint equation for the net parameters, we “pull back” the input signals to the initialization time and compute the corresponding output
k
-jets, which can be expressed explicitly as a function of the weights of the neural net, its initial state, and the
(k-1)
-jets of the input. The loss function is then evaluated with the predicted and ground-truth output jets. At training time, there is no requirement to advance inputs through the network. In essence, the RNN can be trained using conventional nonlinear regression as if it were a single-layer feedforward net.

In the following section, we describe the learning problem, state our assumptions, and introduce the needed notation. Proceeding in Section
3
, we develop the setting of jets and use this language to formulate the proposed learning procedure and state our main results. Some directions for future research are also outlined. Section
4.1
contains some technical lemmas, followed by the proof of the main theorems in Sections
4.2
and
4.3
.

2
Problem formulation

In this work, a
system
(or an
i/o map
) is a nonlinear operator
\mathsf{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C([0,T])\to C([0,T])
, where
C([0,T])
is the Banach space of continuous functions
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the sup norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u(t)|.

The learning problem can be phrased as follows: Let
N
pairs
(u^{1},y^{1}),\ldots,(u^{N},y^{N})
be given, where
u^{i}\in C([0,T])
are the inputs and
y^{i}=\mathsf{F}u^{i}\in C([0,T])
are the corresponding outputs. We wish to construct a system
\hat{\mathsf{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C([0,T])\to C([0,T])
that approximately reproduces the unknown system
\mathsf{F}
on a given class of inputs
\mathcal{U}\subset C([0,T])
. (We focus on single-input, single-output systems mainly for simplicity; our approach easily extends to multiple inputs and outputs.) Next, we impose a set of minimal assumptions on the system
\mathsf{F}
and specify the accuracy criterion.

We first recall the definition of the
modulus of continuity
of a function
u\in C([0,T])
(see, e.g., Ch. 2 of
DeVore and Lorentz 1993
):

\displaystyle\omega_{u}(\delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]\atop|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq\delta}|u(t_{1})-u(t_{2})|.

The function
\delta\mapsto\omega_{u}(\delta)
is nondecreasing, and, since any
u\in C([0,T])
is uniformly continuous,
\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\omega_{u}(\delta)=\omega_{u}(0)=0
. (In fact, we will refer to any function with these properties that majorizes
\omega_{u}
as a modulus of continuity of
u
.) We impose the following assumption on the class of inputs
\mathcal{U}
:

Assumption 2.1
.

The inputs in
\mathcal{U}
are uniformly bounded, i.e.,
R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\|u\|_{\infty}<\infty
, and equicontinuous with common modulus of continuity
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\delta)
:

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\omega_{u}(\delta)\leq\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\delta),

where
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\delta)
is a nondecreasing function that satisfies
\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\delta)=\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(0)=0
.

We also use moduli of continuity to describe the regularity of
\mathsf{F}
:

Assumption 2.2
.

The output space
\mathcal{Y}=\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{U})
, i.e., the image of
\mathcal{U}
under
\mathsf{F}
, is equicontinuous with a common modulus of continuity
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}(\delta)
.

Finally, we assume that
\mathsf{F}
has the bounded-in, bounded-out property:

Assumption 2.3
.

\gamma_{\mathsf{F}}(R)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\sup_{u\in C[0,T]\atop\|u\|_{\infty}\leq R}\|\mathsf{F}u\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

Examples of classes of inputs that satisfy Assumption
2.1
include:

•

finite combinations of Fourier terms

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum^{m}_{i=1}c_{i}\sin(\omega_{i}t+\alpha_{i}),

(2.1)

for all
m\geq 1
, provided the coefficients
c_{i}
and the frequencies
\omega_{i}
satisfy the inequalities
\sum^{m}_{i=1}|c_{i}|\leq R
and
\sum^{m}_{i=1}|c_{i}\omega_{i}|\leq L
for some fixed finite constants
R
and
L
;

•

polynomial inputs

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum^{m}_{i=0}c_{i}t^{i}

(2.2)

for all
m\geq 0
, provided the coefficients
c_{i}
satisfy the inequalities
\sum^{m}_{i=0}|c_{i}|T^{i}\leq R
and
\sum^{m}_{i=1}i|c_{i}|T^{i-1}\leq L
for some
R,L<\infty
.

In both cases, Assumption
2.1
holds with
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\delta)=L\delta
. As an example of a system satisfying our hypotheses, consider a state-space model of the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=f(x(t))+g(x(t))u(t),

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=h(x(t))

where
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an internal state with a given initial condition
x(0)=\xi
. Let
\mathsf{F}
be the induced i/o map that sends the input
u\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
to the output
y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Then it is not hard to verify that Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
will hold if the functions
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are all Lipschitz continuous.

We assume the sample inputs
u^{i}
are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random elements of
C([0,T])
drawn according to a fixed Borel probability measure
\mu
supported on
\mathcal{U}
. Thus, the input-output pairs
(u^{1},y^{1}),\ldots,(u^{N},y^{N})
, with
y^{i}=\mathsf{F}u^{i}
, are themselves i.i.d. random elements of the product space
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{Y}
. For instance,
\mathsf{F}
could be a model of a two-terminal electronic device whose terminals are connected through a switch to an excitation circuit consisting of linear and nonlinear elements and current and/or voltage sources
(
Chua 1980
)
. Suppose this excitation circuit is specified by a vector of parameters
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; e.g., it could be used to generate sinusoidal or polynomial inputs, as in (
2.1
) or (
2.2
). We can then generate
N
i.i.d. samples
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{N}
according to a fixed Borel probability measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Each sample
\theta^{i}
corresponds to a random realization of the excitation circuit so that, when the switch is closed at time
t=0
and open at time
t=T
, we can take the input
u^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
to be the voltage waveform across
\mathsf{F}
and the output
y^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
to be the current waveform through
\mathsf{F}
. (This assumes that
\mathsf{F}
is voltage-controlled.) Thus, the probability measure on the input-output space
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{Y}
is well-defined but specified
indirectly
through
\nu
, the structure of the excitation circuit, and
\mathsf{F}
. At any rate, given
\mathsf{F}
and an approximating system
\hat{\mathsf{F}}
, we define the
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
risk

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathsf{F}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\big[\|\hat{\mathsf{F}}u-\mathsf{F}u\|_{\infty}\big],

where the expectation is taken with respect to the probability measure
\mu
on the input space
\mathcal{U}
. The goal is to generate, on the basis of the observed input-output pairs
(u^{i},y^{i})
, an approximate system
\hat{\mathsf{F}}
from a given model class
\mathcal{F}
, so that the risk
\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathsf{F}})
(which is a random variable due to its dependence on the training data) is small with high probability.

3
The proposed learning procedure and its performance

Recurrent neural nets.

We first specify the model class
\mathcal{F}
from which our learning procedure will select the approximation
\hat{\mathsf{F}}
. The systems in
\mathcal{F}
are described by differential equations of the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{(n)}(Ax(t)+bu(t))

(3.1a)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=c^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}x(t)

(3.1b)

for
t\in[0,T]
with initial condition
x(0)=\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Here,
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
,
b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
\sigma^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the diagonal map
\sigma^{(n)}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\sigma(x_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(x_{n}))^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}
with
\sigma(r)=\tanh(r)
. The system (
3.1
) is a continuous-time
recurrent neural net
(RNN) with
n
neurons. Each pair
(\Sigma,\xi)
, where
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(A,b,c)
, specifies an input-output map
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}
that sends an input
u\in C([0,T])
to the corresponding output
y
given by

\displaystyle y(t)=c^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}\xi+\int^{t}_{0}c^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}\sigma^{(n)}(Ax(\tau)+bu(\tau))\dif\tau,\qquad t\in[0,T].

For
0<M<\infty
, we define the class of systems

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}=\mathsf{F}_{(A,b,c),\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\|A\|,|b|,|c|,|\xi|\leq M\right\},

where
\|A\|
is the spectral norm of
A
, and
|b|,|c|,|\xi|
are the
\ell^{2}
norms of
b,c,\xi
. Our learning procedure will generate a pair
(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{\xi})
based on the data
\{(u^{i},y^{i})\}
, and output the model
\hat{\mathsf{F}}=\mathsf{F}_{\hat{\Sigma},\hat{\xi}}\in\mathcal{F}(M)
. With a slight abuse of notation, we will often write
(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)
instead of
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}\in\mathcal{F}(M)
.

Jets.

While it is well-known that RNNs of the form (
3.1
) are universal approximators for i/o maps
\mathsf{F}
that admit smooth nonlinear state-space realizations
\dot{x}=f(x,u)
,
y=g(x)

(
Sontag 1992
;
Funahashi and Nakamura 1993
;
Hanson and Raginsky 2020
)
, here we are
not
assuming that
\mathsf{F}
admits such a realization (in fact, we are not even requiring
\mathsf{F}
to be causal or time-invariant). Nevertheless, we will show that, provided Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
hold for our learning problem, we will be able to approximate
\mathsf{F}
by a recurrent net model which will have the properties of causality and time invariance by construction. Our approach proceeds by way of reducing the infinite-dimensional problem of learning the i/o map
\mathsf{F}_{\hat{\Sigma},\hat{\xi}}
to a certain finite-dimensional problem
(
Sontag 1998
)
. To that end, consider a system of the form (
3.1
) fed with an input
u
which has at least
k-1
derivatives at
t=0
. Then the output
y=\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
will have at least
k
derivatives at
t=0
, which can be computed explicitly as

\displaystyle y^{(0)}(0)

\displaystyle=c^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}\xi,\quad y^{(1)}(0)=c^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}\sigma^{(n)}(A\xi+bu(0)),\quad\ldots.

We can then define the map
Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}
according to

\displaystyle Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}((u(0),u^{\prime}(0),\ldots,u^{(k-1)}(0))^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(y(0),y^{\prime}(0),\ldots,y^{(k)}(0))^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}},

where
y^{(\ell)}(0)=\frac{\dif^{\,\ell}}{\dif t^{\ell}}\big|_{t=0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u(t)
for
0\leq\ell\leq k
. We can also phrase this in terms of
jets
, where the
k
-jet at
t=0
of a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
which is
C^{k}
in some neighborhood of
t=0
is the degree-
k
polynomial

\displaystyle J^{k}_{0}f(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum^{k}_{\ell=0}f^{(\ell)}(0)\frac{s^{\ell}}{\ell!}.

Then, for inputs that are
C^{k-1}
in some neighborhood of
t=0
, the map
Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}
can be lifted to a map from
{(k-1)}
-jets to
k
-jets via
J^{k-1}_{0}u\mapsto J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
, where the vector of coefficients of
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
is the image of the vector of coefficients of
J^{k-1}_{0}u
under
Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}
. Thus, at least for inputs
u
that are of class
C^{k-1}
, the i/o map
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}
will be a good approximation to
\mathsf{F}
provided (a) the outputs
\mathsf{F}u
and
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
can be accurately approximated by their
k
-jets
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}u
and
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
(e.g., if
k
is sufficiently large) and (b) the
k
-jets
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}u
and
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}u
are close to one another in sup norm on
[0,T]
.

The learning procedure.

Since the inputs in
\mathcal{U}
and the corresponding outputs in
\mathcal{Y}
are only assumed to be continuous, we will first approximate them by functions that have as many derivatives at
t=0
as needed. To that end, we start by defining for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
two linear maps
\mathsf{S}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C([0,T])\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to C([0,T])

according to

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{k}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Big(B_{k-1}(u,0),\frac{\dif}{\dif t}{\Big|}_{t=0}B_{k-1}(u,t),\dots,\frac{\dif^{\,k-1}}{\dif t^{k-1}}{\Big|}_{t=0}B_{k-1}(u,t)\Big)^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}},

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}((a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1})^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}})(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}a_{\ell}\frac{t^{\ell}}{\ell!},

where

\displaystyle B_{m}(u,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum^{m}_{i=0}u\left(\frac{iT}{m}\right){m\choose i}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)^{i}\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)^{m-i},\qquad t\in[0,T]

is the degree-
m

Bernstein polynomial
of
u\in C([0,T])

(
DeVore and Lorentz 1993
, Ch. 1)
. Whenever no confusion will arise, we will also write
B_{m}u
instead of
B_{m}(u,\cdot)
.
Observe that
(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u=B_{k-1}(u,\cdot)
. To motivate the introduction of these objects, we give the following bound on the expected risk of any recurrent net model
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, the following holds for any
\Sigma=(A,b,c)
,
\xi
, and
k\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi})&\leq 2\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+2|c||b|e^{\|A\|T}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}\left(\frac{2T}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+4|c|Te^{\|A\|T}\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}[\|J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u\|]\\
&\qquad\qquad+{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\left[\|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{F})u\|_{\infty}\right].\end{split}

(3.2)

Note that the last term on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) is the expectation, with respect to
u\sim\mu
, of the sup norm of the difference between the degree-
k
Bernstein polynomial
B_{k}\mathsf{F}u
and the
k
-jet
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u
.

We are now ready to present our learning procedure. For
1\leq j\leq k
, let
t_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=jT/k
, and consider the following Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) scheme:

\displaystyle(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{\xi})\in\argmin_{(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)}\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u(t_{j})-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1})y(t_{j})\right|.

(3.3)

The objective being minimized in (
3.3
) is simply the empirical expectation of the maximum absolute difference between the degree-
k
polynomials
B_{k}\mathsf{F}u
and
J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u
on the finite grid
\{T/k,2T/k,\ldots,(k-1)T/k,T\}\subset[0,T]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
are satisfied, and
N\geq k(6n^{6}+10n^{3}\log_{2}k)
. Then with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\hat{\mathsf{F}})&\leq 4\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+2M^{2}e^{MT}\omega_{\mathcal{U}}\left(\frac{2T}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+6MTe^{MT}\sqrt{\frac{n}{k}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+2\sup_{(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}[\|J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u\|_{\infty}]\\
&\qquad\qquad+\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{*}+c\left(M(M+\sqrt{n}T)+\gamma_{\mathsf{F}}(R)\right)\sqrt{\frac{k(n^{6}+n^{3}\log_{2}k)\log N+\log(\frac{1}{\delta})}{N}},\end{split}

(3.4)

where
c>0
is an absolute constant and

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\left[\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\left|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u(t_{j})-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1})y(t_{j})\right|\right].

(3.5)

The first three terms in (
3.4
) can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
k
sufficiently large. The exponential dependence on
M
and
T
is an unavoidable artifact of the Grönwall lemma, and can be removed under appropriate stability assumptions
(
Hanson and Raginsky 2020
)
. The next term is the worst-case, over
(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)
, expected error between
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u
(i.e., the response of the RNN
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}
to the polynomial input
B_{k-1}u
) and its
k
-jet. This term will only vanish with increasing
k
if all the functions
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u
are entire. The remaining two terms are, respectively, the approximation error and the estimation error of the ERM procedure (
3.3
). While the latter can be made arbitrarily small by increasing the size
N
of the training set, the former is an intrinsic measure of the ability of recurrent neural nets to approximate output
k
-jets for a randomly chosen input. This minimal error value can be decreased by considering larger, more expressive nets; a quantitative analysis of this term is a promising direction for further work. Note also that, for a fixed
k
, we only need to collect input and output samples on an equispaced grid
\{0,T/k,2T/k,\ldots,T\}
. On the other hand, in many applications (e.g., medical or electronic system modeling), the input/output data are often available at non-uniformly spaced times
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}\leq T
that are not under the learner’s control. Extending the approach of this paper to non-uniform (or even random) sampling of time instants is another interesting future direction.

4
Proofs

4.1
Technical lemmas

In this section, we collect a few results that will be used in the proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
u\in C([0,T])
with modulus of continuity
\omega
and any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the Bernstein polynomial
B_{k}(u,\cdot)
has modulus of continuity
2\omega
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|u-B_{k}(u,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\omega\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right).

(4.1)

Proof.

The statement about the modulus of continuity of
B_{k}(u,\cdot)
is a result of
Li 2000
. The error bound (
4.1
) is well-known, but we give a self-contained probabilistic proof. Let
\bar{\omega}
be the smallest concave majorant of
\omega
. Then
\bar{\omega}
is also a modulus of continuity of
u
, which further satisfies the inequality
\omega(\delta)\leq\bar{\omega}(\delta)\leq 2\omega(\delta)

(
DeVore and Lorentz 1993
, Lemma 6.1)
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, we can express the value
B_{k}(u,t)
as an expectation

\displaystyle B_{k}(u,t)={\mathbf{E}}\Big[u\Big(\frac{T}{k}X_{t}\Big)\Big]

where
X_{t}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(k,t/T)
. Then we have the following chain of (in)equalities:

\displaystyle|u(t)-B_{k}(u,t)|

\displaystyle=\left|u(t)-{\mathbf{E}}\Big[u\Big(\frac{T}{k}X_{t}\Big)\Big]\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(a)}}}{{\leq}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left|u(t)-u\Big(\frac{T}{k}X_{t}\Big)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(b)}}}{{\leq}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\bar{\omega}\left(\left|\frac{T}{k}X_{t}-t\right|\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(c)}}}{{\leq}}\bar{\omega}\left({\mathbf{E}}\left|\frac{T}{k}X_{t}-t\right|\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(d)}}}{{\leq}}2\omega\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right),

where (a) is by Jensen’s inequality, (b) follows from the fact that
\bar{\omega}
is a modulus of continuity of
u
, (c) is by Jensen’s inequality and by concavity of
\bar{\omega}
, and (d) uses the monotonicity of
\omega
and
\bar{\omega}
, the inequality
\bar{\omega}\leq 2\omega
, and the inequality

for the mean absolute deviation of a

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathsf{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C([0,T])\to C([0,T])
be a Lipschitz-continuous i/o map, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{u_{1},u_{2}\in C([0,T])\atop u_{1}\neq u_{2}}\frac{\|\mathsf{G}u_{1}-\mathsf{G}u_{2}\|_{\infty}}{\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty}}<\infty.

Then, for any integer
k\geq 2
and any
u\in C([0,T])
,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{G}u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{G}\circ\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\omega_{u}\left(\frac{2T}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+2\omega_{\mathsf{G}B_{k-1}u}\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right).

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\|\mathsf{G}u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{G}\circ\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u\|_{\infty}\\
&\qquad\leq\|\mathsf{G}u-(\mathsf{G}\circ\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u\|_{\infty}+\|(\mathsf{G}\circ\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{G}\circ\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u\|_{\infty}\\
&\qquad=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{1}+T_{2}.\end{split}

Since
(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u=B_{k-1}u
, Lemma
4.1
gives

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\|u-B_{k-1}u\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\omega_{u}\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k-1}}\right)\leq 2\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\omega_{u}\left(\frac{2T}{\sqrt{k}}\right),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\sqrt{k-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{k}
for
k\geq 2
. Similarly,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathsf{G}B_{k-1}u-B_{k}\mathsf{G}B_{k-1}u\|_{\infty}\leq 2\omega_{\mathsf{G}B_{k-1}u}\left(\frac{T}{\sqrt{k}}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be the i/o map of the differential dynamical system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=f(x(t),u(t)),\quad y(t)

\displaystyle=h^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}x(t);\qquad t\in[0,T],\,x(0)=\xi

(4.2)

with input
u(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
, state
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and output
y(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
f(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded, i.e.,
\sup_{(x,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x,u)|<\infty
, and Lipschitz-continuous, i.e.,

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},u_{1})-f(x_{2},u_{2})|\leq L_{X}|x_{1}-x_{2}|+L_{U}|u_{1}-u_{2}|.

Then, for any input
u\in C([0,T])
, the output
y=\mathsf{G}u
is bounded with

\displaystyle\|y\|_{\infty}\leq|h|\left(|\xi|+T\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\right),

(4.3)

and has modulus of continuity

\displaystyle\omega_{y}(\delta)\leq\frac{|h|}{L_{X}}\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\left(e^{L_{X}\delta}-1\right).

(4.4)

Moreover, the i/o map
\mathsf{G}
is Lipschitz-continuous, with

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{G}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq\frac{|h|L_{U}}{L_{X}}\left(e^{L_{X}T}-1\right).

(4.5)

Proof.

For a given input
u\in C([0,T])
and vector
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
\varphi^{u}_{s,t}(\xi)
,
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, denote the
flow
of (
4.2
), i.e., the solution of the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\varphi^{u}_{s,t}(\xi)=f(\varphi^{u}_{s,t}(\xi),u(t)),\qquad s\leq t\leq T,\,\varphi^{u}_{s,s}(\xi)=\xi.

The flow map evidently has the semigroup property

\displaystyle\varphi^{u}_{s,t}(\xi)=\varphi^{u}_{r,t}(\varphi^{u}_{s,r}(\xi)),\qquad 0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq T.

(4.6)

In terms of the flow, the i/o map
\mathsf{G}
is given by
y(t)=(\mathsf{G}u)(t)=h^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}\varphi^{u}_{0,t}(\xi)
. To show that
y
is bounded, we first estimate

\displaystyle|\varphi^{u}_{0,t}(\xi)|=\left|\xi+\int^{t}_{0}f(\varphi^{u}_{0,\tau}(\xi),u(\tau))\dif\tau\right|\leq|\xi|+T\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T

and then
|y(t)|\leq|h||\varphi^{u}_{0,t}(\xi)|
by Cauchy–Schwarz. This gives (
4.3
).

To obtain the modulus of continuity of
y
, consider two times
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
. Then, using (
4.6
) and the time invariance of (
4.2
), we can write

\displaystyle|\varphi_{0,t_{2}}^{u}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t_{1}}^{u}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=|\varphi_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{u}(\varphi_{0,t_{1}}^{u}(\xi))-\varphi_{0,t_{1}}^{u}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=|\varphi_{0,t_{2}-t_{1}}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime})-\xi^{\prime}|,

where
\xi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi_{0,t_{1}}^{u}(\xi)
and
\bar{u}(t)
is any continuous extension of the map
[0,t_{2}-t_{1}]\ni t\mapsto u(t+t_{1})
to
[0,T]
. Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus and the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}|\varphi_{0,t_{2}-t_{1}}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime})-\xi^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big|\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime}),\bar{u}(\tau))\dif\tau\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big(|f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime}),\bar{u}(\tau))-f(\xi^{\prime},\bar{u}(\tau))|+|f(\xi^{\prime},\bar{u}(\tau))|\big)\dif\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big(L_{X}|\varphi_{0,\tau}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime})-\xi^{\prime}|+\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\big)\dif\tau

\displaystyle\qquad=L_{X}\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big(|\varphi_{0,\tau}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime})-\xi^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{L_{X}}\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\big)\dif\tau.

Grönwall’s inequality
(
Hirsch and Smale 1974
)
applied to the function

\mu(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\varphi_{0,t}^{\bar{u}}(\xi^{\prime})-\xi^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{L_{X}}\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|

yields

\mu(t)\leq\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\frac{1}{L_{X}}e^{L_{X}t}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle|y(t_{1})-y(t_{2})|

\displaystyle=|h^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}(\varphi^{u}_{0,t_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi^{u}_{0,t_{2}}(\xi))|

\displaystyle\leq|h||\varphi^{u}_{0,t_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi^{u}_{0,t_{2}}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|h|\sup_{x,u}|f(x,u)|\frac{1}{L_{X}}\big(e^{L_{X}(t_{2}-t_{1})}-1\big).

Interchanging the roles of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, we obtain (
4.4
).

For any time
t\in[0,T]
, the difference in flows due to distinct inputs
u_{1},u_{2}\in C([0,T])
is bounded by

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}|\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big|\int_{0}^{t}f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{1}}(\xi),u_{1}(\tau))-f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{2}}(\xi),u_{2}(\tau))\dif\tau\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t}\big(|f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{1}}(\xi),u_{1}(\tau))-f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{2}}(\xi),u_{1}(\tau))|+|f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{2}}(\xi),u_{1}(\tau))-f(\varphi_{0,\tau}^{u_{2}}(\xi),u_{2}(\tau))|\big)\dif\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t}\big(L_{X}|\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi)|+L_{U}|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau))|\big)\dif\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\leq L_{X}\int_{0}^{t}\big(|\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi)|+\frac{L_{U}}{L_{X}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty}\big)\dif\tau.

Grönwall’s inequality applied to the function

\mu(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi)|+\frac{L_{U}}{L_{X}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty}

yields

\mu(t)\leq\frac{L_{U}}{L_{X}}e^{L_{X}t}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty},

hence we have

|\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi)|\leq\frac{L_{U}}{L_{X}}\big(e^{L_{X}t}-1\big)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{G}u_{1}-\mathsf{G}u_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|h^{\hbox{{\tiny T}}}(\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{1}}(\xi)-\varphi_{0,t}^{u_{2}}(\xi))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|h|L_{U}}{L_{X}}\big(e^{L_{X}T}-1\big)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty},

and (
4.5
) follows.
∎

We also need the following result on the Rademacher averages of VC-subgraph classes
(
Farrell et al. 2020
)
. Recall that a class
\mathcal{G}
of measurable functions
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
VC-subgraph class

(
Giné and Nickl 2016
, Sec. 3.6.2)
if the class of all sets of the form
\{(x,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}g(x)\geq r\}
with
g\in\mathcal{G}
is a Vapnik–Chervonenkis (or VC) class, i.e., there exists a finite
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that, for each
m\leq D
, there exist
m
points
(x^{1},r^{1}),\ldots,(x^{m},r^{m})
that are
shattered by

\mathcal{G}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\{({\mathbf{1}}_{\{g(x^{1})\geq r^{1}\}},\ldots,{\mathbf{1}}_{\{g(x^{m})\geq r^{m}\}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}g\in\mathcal{G}\}=\{0,1\}^{m},

and no such
m
-tuple of points exists for
m>D
. This
D
is called the
VC-subgraph dimension
(or
pseudo-dimension
) of
\mathcal{G}
, and is denoted by
\mathrm{vc}(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be VC subgraph class of real-valued measurable functions
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,B]
. Let
\boldsymbol{x}=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{N})
be an arbitrary
N
-tuple of points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and define the Rademacher average

\displaystyle R_{N}(\mathcal{G};\boldsymbol{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{E}}\left[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\left|\sum^{N}_{i=1}\varepsilon^{i}g(x^{i})\right|\right],

where
\varepsilon^{1},\ldots,\varepsilon^{N}
are i.i.d. random variables with
\mathbf{P}[\varepsilon^{i}=\pm 1]=\frac{1}{2}
. Then, for any
N\geq\mathrm{vc}(\mathcal{G})
,

\displaystyle R_{N}(\mathcal{G};\boldsymbol{x})\leq cB\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{vc}(\mathcal{G})\log N}{N}}

for some universal constant
c
.

,

\displaystyle\|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{F})u\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,\cdot)-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t)-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t)|

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t_{j})-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t_{j})|\\
&\qquad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min_{1\leq j\leq k}|J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t)-J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t_{j})|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min_{1\leq j\leq k}|B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t_{j})-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t)|\\
&\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|J^{k}_{0}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t_{j})-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t_{j})|\\
&\qquad+2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u(t)-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u(t)|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min_{1\leq j\leq k}|\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u(t)-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u(t_{j})|\\
&\qquad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min_{1\leq j\leq k}|B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t_{j})-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t)|,\end{split}

where the last two terms can be upper-bounded using the moduli of continuity of
B_{k}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,\cdot)
and
B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,\cdot)
, which, by Lemma
4.1
, are upper-bounded by twice the moduli of continuity of
\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u
and
\mathsf{F}u
, respectively. Thus, using Assumption
2.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{F})u\|_{\infty}\\
&\qquad\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|B_{k}(\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u,t_{j})-B_{k}(\mathsf{F}u,t_{j})|\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{k}MTe^{MT}+2\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\frac{T}{k}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+2\|J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

Taking expectation of both sides with respect to
\mu
yields, for any
(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\left[\|(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ Y_{k,\Sigma,\xi}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k})u-(\mathsf{S}^{*}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{S}_{k+1}\circ\mathsf{F})u\|_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\Sigma,\xi)+\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{k}MTe^{MT}+2\omega_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\frac{T}{k}\right)+2\sup_{(\Sigma,\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(M)}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}[\|J^{k}_{0}\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u-\mathsf{F}_{\Sigma,\xi}B_{k-1}u\|_{\infty}].

Using this and (
4.14
) in the bound of Theorem
3.1
, we get the statement of the theorem.

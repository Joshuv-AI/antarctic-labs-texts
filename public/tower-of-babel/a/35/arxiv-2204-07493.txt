Prescribed Mean Curvature Min-Max Theory in Some Non-compact Manifolds
Liam Mazurowski

Abstract
. This paper develops a technique for applying one-parameter prescribed mean curvature min-max theory in certain non-compact manifolds. We give two main applications. First, fix a dimension 3 ≤ n + 1 ≤ 7 3\leq n+1\leq 7 and consider a smooth function h : ℝ n + 1 → ℝ h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R} which is asymptotic to a positive constant near infinity. We show that, under certain additional assumptions on h h , there exists a closed hypersurface Σ \Sigma in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} with mean curvature prescribed by h h . Second, let ( M 3 , g ) (M^{3},g) be an asymptotically flat 3-manifold and fix a constant c > 0 c>0 . We show that, under an additional assumption on M M , it is possible to find a closed surface Σ \Sigma of constant mean curvature c c in M M .

PMC Surfaces in Euclidean Space

In his list of problems in differential geometry

For which functions

In this paper we consider a closely related question. Namely, given a dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7

h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}

-PMC

In dimension three, Yau’s question can be investigated using the mapping method. Indeed, branched immersed

Theorem 1
.

Fix a dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7
and let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function satisfying hypotheses
(\operatorname{H1})
-
(\operatorname{H4})
. Then there exists a smooth, almost embedded
h
-PMC in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Here a surface
\Sigma
is called almost embedded provided in a neighborhood of each point
p\in\Sigma
either

(i)

\Sigma
is embedded, or

(ii)

\Sigma
decomposes into an ordered union of embedded sheets.

Roughly speaking, the hypotheses (H1)-(H4) are as follows:

(H1)

The function
h
is asymptotic to a positive constant
c
at infinity.

(H2)

The radial derivative of
h
is smaller than
c/\|x\|
near infinity.

(H3)

The function
h
is admissible for PMC min-max theory.

(H4)

The mountain pass energy level for
h
is not attained by spheres at infinity.

See Section
3
for the precise formulation of these hypotheses.

It is worth comparing Theorem
1
with the result of
[
3
]
in the case that
n+1=3
. First, our assumption (H2) is weaker than the corresponding assumption in
[
3
]
which asks that the radial derivative be bounded by
c/\|x\|^{2}
everywhere. Also the
h
-PMCs we obtain are almost embedded, whereas the
h
-PMCs in
[
3
]
are branched immersed. On the other hand, the
h
-PMCs constructed in
[
3
]
have the topology of
S^{2}
whereas there is no control over the topology of the surfaces produced by Theorem
1
.

1.2.
CMC Surfaces in Asymptotically Flat Manifolds

The proof of Theorem 1 uses a cutoff trick to reduce min-max on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to min-max on a sequence of larger and larger balls. We are hopeful that this cutoff trick may be adapted to perform min-max in other non-compact manifolds. As an example, in Section
4
, we show that essentially the same argument can be used to produce constant mean curvature surfaces in some asymptotically flat manifolds. This partially answers a question posed by Zhou in
[
34
]
.

Recall that a manifold
(M^{3},g)
is called asymptotically flat if there is a compact set
K
such that
M^{3}\setminus K
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{1}(0)
, and moreover, the metric
g
is asymptotic to the Euclidean metric in the coordinates induced by this diffeomorphism. Asymptotically flat manifolds are important in general relativity, where they model the gravitational field of an isolated object.
Huisken and Yau
[
11
]
showed that manifolds asymptotic to a Schwarzchild solution of positive mass admit a foliation by constant mean curvature (CMC) surfaces near infinity. See Ye
[
32
]
for another proof. This existence result has since been generalized by Eichmair-Koerber
[
9
]
, Huang
[
10
]
, Metzger
[
21
]
, Nerz
[
22
]
, and others to allow for weaker decay of the metric to the flat metric near infinity. The uniqueness of such CMC foliations has also been an active topic of research. See Eichmair-Koerber
[
9
]
for a comprehensive list of references.

Note that the spheres in these foliations near infinity are very large and hence have small mean curvature. Ye
[
31
]
showed that manifolds admit foliations by small CMC spheres near non-degenerate critical points of the scalar curvature. Of course, these spheres have very large mean curvature. Thus, in general, an asymptotically flat manifold contains closed CMCs for very small values of the mean curvature as well as very large values of the mean curvature. It is natural to ask whether such a manifold also contains CMCs for intermediate values of the mean curvature. Our second main result shows that, under an additional assumption on
M
, this is indeed the case.

Theorem 2
.

Fix a constant
c>0
. Let
(M^{3},g)
be an asymptotically flat manifold which satisfies hypothesis
(H)
for the constant
c
. Then there exists a closed, almost-embedded
c
-CMC in
M
.

Here hypothesis (H) asks that a certain mountain pass energy level is not attained by spheres at infinity. See Section
4
for the precise formulation of hypothesis (H), as well as the exact definition of an asymptotically flat manifold.

1.3.
Outline of Proof

In the remainder of the introduction we give a heuristic outline of the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
B_{r}
denote the ball of radius
r
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function which satisfies (H1)-(H3). Without loss of generality, we can assume that
h
is asymptotic to
n
at infinity. We make this choice for convenience because the mean curvature of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is equal to
n
.

Given an open set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with smooth boundary, define

A^{h}(\Omega)=\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega)-\int_{\Omega}h.

A set
\Omega
with smooth boundary is a critical point for
A^{h}
precisely when
\partial\Omega
is an
h
-PMC. Call a smoothly varying family of open sets
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
a mountain pass path if
\Omega_{0}=\emptyset
and
A^{h}(\Omega_{1})<0
. Define the mountain pass energy level

\omega=\inf_{\{\Omega_{t}\}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h}(\Omega_{t})\right]

where the infimum is taken over all mountain pass paths
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
.
The strategy is to try and obtain an
h
-PMC
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
with
A^{h}(\Omega)=\omega
by using Zhou and Zhu’s prescribed mean curvature min-max theory.

The major difficulty with this approach is that there are no barriers at infinity for the
A^{h}
functional. Indeed, without further assumptions on
h
, it may happen that min-max detects a sphere at infinity. For example, suppose that
h
is radially increasing. Then
\Omega_{t}^{x}=B_{rt}(x)
is a mountain pass path provided
\|x\|
and
r
are large enough. Moreover

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h}(\Omega_{t}^{x})\to\omega=A^{n}(B_{1})

as
\|x\|\to\infty
. In this example, every min-max sequence converges to a sphere of radius 1 at infinity.

This particular behavior can be ruled out by requiring that
\omega<A^{n}(B_{1})
. This is hypothesis (H4). However, even with hypothesis (H4), the lack of barriers makes it difficult to directly perform min-max.
To get around this, we introduce a family of approximate problems which do admit barriers. Namely, for each
R>0
, let
h_{R}
be a function which agrees with
h
on
B_{R}
, falls off linearly on
B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R}
, and is identically 0 outside
B_{R+1}
. Then large spheres centered at the origin serve as barriers for
h_{R}
-PMCs. Consequently, it is possible to apply the above min-max scheme with
A^{h_{R}}
in place of
A^{h}
to produce an
{h_{R}}
-PMC
\Sigma_{R}=\partial\Omega_{R}
contained in
B_{R+1}
. Using hypothesis (H2) together with Strüwe’s monotonicity trick, we verify that the surfaces
\Sigma_{R}
have uniform area bounds. Moreover, the surfaces
\Sigma_{R}
have index at most one for
A^{h_{R}}
.

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that
\Sigma_{R}
does not intersect the transition region
B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R}
when
R
is large. Suppose to the contrary that every surface
\Sigma_{R}
intersects the transition region
B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R}
. Translate each
\Sigma_{R}
to get a new surface
\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}
that passes through the origin. By the area and index bounds, these surfaces
\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}
converge to a limiting surface
\Sigma_{\infty}=\partial\Omega_{\infty}
as
R\to\infty
. The limiting surface
\Sigma_{\infty}
is an
h_{\infty}
-PMC where the function
h_{\infty}
is
n
on one side of a slab, falls off linearly across the slab, and is 0 on the other side of the slab. The montonicity of
h_{\infty}
implies that
\Sigma_{\infty}
must be a sphere of radius 1 contained in the region where
h_{\infty}=n
. This is a contradiction to hypothesis (H4) because
A^{n}(B_{1})=A^{h_{\infty}}(\Omega_{\infty})=\lim_{R\to\infty}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{R})=\omega
.

2.
Background and Notation

We will need the following concepts from geometric measure theory. Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold. In this paper,
M
will always be either Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
or an asymptotically flat manifold
(M^{3},g)
. Let
\mathcal{C}(M)
denote the set of all Caccioppoli sets with compact support in
M
. The flat topology on
\mathcal{C}(M)
is induced by the metric

\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})=\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{1}\Delta\Omega_{2})

where
\Delta
denotes the symmetric difference. For
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(M)
the notation
\partial\Omega
stands for the boundary of
\Omega
in the sense of flat chains mod two. The
\mathbf{F}
-topology on
\mathcal{C}(M)
is given by

\mathbf{F}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})=\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})+\mathbf{F}(|\partial\Omega_{1}|,|\partial\Omega_{2}|).

Here
|\partial\Omega_{i}|
denotes the varifold induced by the flat chain
\partial\Omega_{i}
and
\mathbf{F}
is the
\mathbf{F}
-metric on varifolds (see
[
23
]
).

Let
h\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.

Definition 3
.

The functional
A^{h}\colon\mathcal{C}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

A^{h}(\Omega)=\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega)-\int_{\Omega}h.

Proposition 4
.

The first variation of
A^{h}
at a set
\Omega
with smooth boundary is given by

\delta A^{h}\bigg|_{\Omega}(X)=-\int_{\partial\Omega}H\cdot X+\int_{\partial\Omega}hX\cdot\nu.

Here
H
denotes the mean curvature vector for
\partial\Omega
and
\nu
is the inward normal to
\partial\Omega
.

It is clear from the previous formula that a smooth critical point of
A^{h}
gives rise to an
h
-PMC. In the special case where
h=c
is constant, the functional simplifies to

A^{c}(\Omega)=\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega)-c\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega).

Smooth critical points of the
A^{c}
functional give rise to
c
-CMCs.

Next we give the basic definitions regarding the min-max scheme for
A^{h}
.

Definition 5
.

Let
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a family in
\mathcal{C}(M)
varying continuously in the
\mathbf{F}
-topology. The family
\{\Omega_{t}\}
is called an
h
-mountain pass path provided
\Omega_{0}=\emptyset
and
A^{h}(\Omega_{1})<0
. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all
h
-mountain pass paths.

Definition 6
.

The one parameter min-max width for
A^{h}
is the number

\omega=\inf_{\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h}(\Omega_{t})\right].

3.
PMCs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

3.1.
Set Up

Fix a dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7
and let
M
be
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
equipped with the Euclidean metric. Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function which satisfies the following hypotheses:

(H1)

The function
h
converges smoothly to a constant
c>0
at infinity;

(H2)

There are numbers
\rho>0
and
0<\sigma<1
such that
|\nabla h(x)\cdot x|<\sigma h(x)
whenever
\|x\|\geq\rho
.

(H3)

The function
h
satisfies the admissibility condition
(\dagger)
from
[
36
]
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all
h
-mountain pass paths. It is clear that
\mathcal{P}
is non-empty. Indeed, the path
t\mapsto B_{tr}(x)
is a mountain pass path provided
\|x\|
and
r
are large enough. Let
\omega
be the min-max width for
A^{h}
as in the previous section. Then
\omega
satisfies the following basic estimate.

Proposition 7
.

The one parameter min-max width satisfies
0<\omega\leq A^{c}(B_{n/c})
.

Proof.

The fact that
\omega>0
is a consequence of the isoperimetric inequality. In more detail, the isoperimetric inequality says there is a constant
C
depending only on the dimension such that

\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega)\geq C\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)^{n/({n+1})}

for all
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Let
M=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}h<\infty
. Then

A^{h}(\Omega)\geq C\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}-\int_{\Omega}h\geq\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\left[C-M\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\right]

for all
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. In particular, there are positive constants
v,a>0
such that
A^{h}(\Omega)\geq 0
whenever
\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)\leq v
and
A^{h}(\Omega)\geq a
whenever
\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)=v
. It follows that
\omega\geq a>0
.

The upper bound on
\omega
is proved by analyzing explicit mountain pass paths near infinity. Fix a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\|x\|
large. Also fix a very large number
r
. Then
\Omega_{t}=B_{rt}(x)
is a mountain pass path. Moreover,

\displaystyle A^{h}(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega_{t})-\int_{\Omega_{t}}h

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Area}(\partial B_{rt})-c\operatorname{Vol}(B_{rt})-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(h-c).

Elementary calculus shows that
\operatorname{Area}(\partial B_{rt})-c\operatorname{Vol}(B_{rt})
is maximized for
t=\frac{n}{cr}
and the maximum value is
A^{c}(B_{n/c})
. Thus

\displaystyle A^{h}(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle\leq A^{c}(B_{n/c})+\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{t})\sup_{\Omega_{t}}|h-c|

\displaystyle\leq A^{c}(B_{n/c})+\operatorname{Vol}(B_{r})\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus B}|h-c|,

where
B
is a ball of radius
\|x\|-r
centered at the origin.
Since
t
was arbitrary and

\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus B}|h-c|\to 0

as
\|x\|\to\infty
by (H1), it follows that
\omega\leq A^{c}(B_{n/c})
.
∎

Throughout the rest of the paper we make the following additional assumption on
h
.

(H4)

The width
\omega
is strictly less than
A^{c}(B_{n/c})
.

This will be the case, for example, if
h(x)>c
for all
x
in some sufficiently large ball.

Remark 8
.

For the remainder of the paper we will assume that the constant
c
is equal to
n
. It is easy to see that this causes no loss of generality.
For the reader’s convenience we now summarize the hypotheses (H1)-(H4) in the case that
c=n
.

(H1)

The function
h
converges smoothly to the constant
n
at infinity.

(H2)

There are numbers
\rho>0
and
0<\sigma<1
such that
|\nabla h(x)\cdot x|<\sigma h(x)
whenever
\|x\|\geq\rho
.

(H3)

The function
h
satisfies the admissibility condition
(\dagger)
from
[
36
]
.

(H4)

The width
\omega
is strictly less than
A^{n}(B_{1})
.

3.2.
The Approximate Problems

Fix a function
h
satisfying (H1)-(H4). In this section, we define and solve a sequence of approximate problems for which it is easy to confine the corresponding min-max surface inside a compact region of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\zeta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth, decreasing function such that

\zeta(r)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }r\leq 0\\
0,&\text{if }r\geq 1\end{cases}

For each positive number
R
define
\zeta_{R}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\zeta_{R}(x)=\zeta(\|x\|-R)
. Finally let
h_{R}=\zeta_{R}h
.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{R}
denote the set of all
h_{R}
-mountain pass paths, and let
\omega(R)
be the one parameter min-max width for
h_{R}
.

Next we record several important properties of the width
\omega(R)
. The following lemma is a consequence of the the fact that large spheres centered at the origin serve as a barrier to
h_{R}
-PMCs.

Definition 11
.

For each
R>0
, select a number
\varepsilon_{R}>0
so that

\zeta(r)<\frac{1}{4(r+R)}\quad\text{for all }r\geq 1-2\varepsilon_{R}.

Without loss of generality,
\varepsilon_{R}
can be chosen to be a decreasing function of
R
. For
R
sufficiently large, this choice of
\varepsilon_{R}
guarantees that

h_{R}(x)<{n}/{\|x\|}

for
\|x\|\geq R+1-2\varepsilon_{R}
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Then
\Omega\cap B_{R+1-2\varepsilon_{R}}
also belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and satisfies
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega\cap B_{R+1-2\varepsilon_{R}})\leq A^{h_{R}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
B=B_{R+1-2\varepsilon_{R}}
. The intersection of two Caccioppoli sets is a Caccioppoli set. Therefore

\Omega\cap B

belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. The vector field
X(x)=x/\|x\|
satisfies

\operatorname{div}(X)={n}/{\|x\|}.

Thus
h_{R}(x)<\operatorname{div}(X)
on the complement of
B
. Hence the divergence theorem implies that
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega\cap B)\leq A^{h_{R}}(\Omega)
.
∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{R}
be the collection of paths
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}
such that
\Omega_{t}
is supported in
B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\omega(R)=\inf_{\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{Q}_{R}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\right].

In other words, the min-max value does not change if we restrict to only those mountain pass paths which are supported in
B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
.

Proof.

Fix a number
\varepsilon>0
. Choose a mountain pass path
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R)+\varepsilon.

Define
U_{t}=\Omega_{t}\cap B_{R+1-2\varepsilon_{R}}
. By Lemma
35
, each
U_{t}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and satisfies
A^{h_{R}}(U_{t})\leq A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})
. Moreover, as shown in
[
8
]
,
U_{t}
varies continuously in the flat topology and has no concentration of mass (as defined in
[
17
]
). Hence, by the interpolation theorems of Zhou
[
33
]
, there is an
\mathbf{F}
-continuous family
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
such that

(i)

W_{0}=\emptyset
and
W_{1}=U_{1}
,

(ii)

W_{t}
is supported in
B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
for all
t
, and

(iii)

A^{h_{R}}(W_{t})\leq A^{h_{R}}(U_{t})+\varepsilon
for all
t
.

This family
\{W_{t}\}
belongs to
\mathcal{Q}_{R}
and satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(W_{t})\leq\omega(R)+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, the proposition follows.
∎

The next propositions establish the monotonicity of
\omega(R)
and show that
\omega(R)\to\omega
as
R\to\infty
.

Proposition 14
.

The width
\omega(R)
is a decreasing function of
R
.

Proof.

Fix two numbers
R<R^{\prime}
. Also fix some small
\varepsilon>0
. By definition, there is a family
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R)+\varepsilon.

Since
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{{R^{\prime}}}
and
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\leq A^{h_{R^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{t})
for all
t\in[0,1]
, this implies that

\omega({R^{\prime}})\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}^{\prime}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R)+\varepsilon.

The result follows since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary.
∎

Proposition 15
.

The widths
\omega(R)
satisfy
\omega(R)\to\omega
as
R\to\infty
. In particular,
\omega(R)<A^{n}(B_{1})
when
R
is large by
(\operatorname{H4})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\omega(R)\geq\omega
for all
R
. Since
\omega(R)
is decreasing, it is enough to show that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is an
R
such that
\omega(R)\leq\omega+\varepsilon
. So fix a small number
\varepsilon>0
. Choose a mountain pass path
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}
for which

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega+\varepsilon.

We claim that

(1)

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{t}\setminus B_{R})<\varepsilon

for
R
large enough. Indeed, this follows from the fact that
\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{t}\setminus B_{R})
is a continuous function of
(t,R)\in[0,1]\times(0,\infty)
, the fact that
[0,1]
is compact, and the fact that
\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{t}\setminus B_{R})\to 0
as
R\to\infty
for each fixed
t\in[0,1]
.

Choose
R
large enough that (
1
) holds. By making
R
even larger if necessary, we can assume that
\Omega_{1}\subset B_{R}
. Then
\{\Omega_{t}\}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left[\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega_{t})-\int_{\Omega_{t}}h_{R}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\omega+\varepsilon+2n\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{t}\setminus B_{R})\leq\omega+(1+2n)\varepsilon.\phantom{\int}

Thus
\omega(R)\leq\omega+(1+2n)\varepsilon
and the proposition follows.
∎

In order to establish area bounds on the min-max
h_{R}
-PMCs, it is necessary to construct mountain pass paths which have somewhat controlled behavior in the transition region
B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R}
.

Definition 16
.

For each
R>0
,
C>0
,
\eta>0
, and
\theta>0
, define
\mathcal{N}_{R,C,\eta,\theta}
to be the set of paths
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}
such that the following properties hold:

(i)

every
\Omega_{t}
is supported in
B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
,

(ii)

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R)+\theta
,

(iii)

\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h\leq C

for all
t
such that
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\geq\omega(R)-\eta
.

The next propositions exploit the monotonicity of
\omega(R)
in order to construct mountain pass paths that belong to the nice class
\mathcal{N}_{R,C,\eta,\theta}
. This technique is inspired by Strüwe’s monotonicity trick
[
28
]
. The monotonicity trick was also used by Cheng and Zhou in their construction of constant mean curvature 2-spheres inside of Riemannian 3-spheres
[
5
]
as well as their construction of curves with constant geodesic curvature in Riemannian 2-spheres
[
6
]
.

Proposition 17
.

There is a sequence
R_{j}\to\infty
such that
0\geq\omega^{\prime}(R_{j})\geq-{2}/{R_{j}}

for all
j
.

Proof.

The function
\omega(R)
is a decreasing function of
R
and consequently it is differentiable almost everywhere. Moreover, for any real numbers
0<a<b
there is an estimate

\int_{a}^{b}\omega^{\prime}(R)\,dR\geq\omega(b)-\omega(a)\geq\omega-\omega(a).

Select
a
large enough that
\omega(a)-\omega\leq 1
. Then for any positive integer
n\geq a
we have

0\geq\int_{n}^{2n}\omega^{\prime}(R)\,dR\geq-1

and consequently there must be an
R\in[n,2n]
such that
|\omega^{\prime}(R)|\leq 2/R
. This implies the result.
∎

Proposition 18
.

Assume that
0\geq\omega^{\prime}(R)\geq-c
for some constant
c>0
. Then there is an
\eta>0
such that the set
\mathcal{N}_{R,5c,\eta,\theta}
is non-empty for all sufficiently small
\theta
.

Proof.

For each positive integer
k
, let
R_{k}=R-k^{-1}
. Then

0\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{\omega(R_{k})-\omega(R)}{R_{k}-R}\right)\geq-c

and so

(2)

0\leq\frac{\omega(R_{k})-\omega(R)}{k^{-1}}\leq{2c}

for all sufficiently large
k
. By Proposition
13
, for every
\theta>0
, it is possible to choose a path
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\in{\mathcal{Q}_{R_{k}}}
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R_{k}}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R_{k})+\theta.

Combined with (
2
), this yields

(3)

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R_{k}}}(\Omega_{t})\leq\omega(R)+2ck^{-1}+\theta.

Now notice that

A^{h_{R_{k}}}(\Omega_{t})=A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})+\int_{\Omega_{t}}h_{R}-h_{R_{k}}

and so

(4)

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left[A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})+\int_{\Omega_{t}}h_{R}-h_{R_{k}}\right]\leq\omega(R)+2ck^{-1}+\theta.

Also observe that
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{R}
.

We claim
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
actually belongs to the class
\mathcal{N}_{R,5c,ck^{-1},\theta}
provided
k
is large enough and
\theta
is small enough (depending on
k
). To see this, suppose that
\Omega_{t}
satisfies
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\geq\omega(R)-ck^{-1}
. Then (
4
) gives

[\omega(R)-ck^{-1}]+\int_{\Omega_{t}}h_{R}-h_{R_{k}}\leq\omega(R)+2ck^{-1}+\theta\leq\omega(R)+3ck^{-1},

where the last inequality follows provided we assume
\theta\leq ck^{-1}
.
Rearranging the previous line shows that

\int_{\Omega_{t}}h_{R}-h_{R_{k}}\leq 4ck^{-1}.

Hence for
k
large enough

\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{d}{dR}h_{R}\leq\frac{5}{4}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{h_{R}-h_{R_{k}}}{k^{-1}}\leq 5c.

Thus (iii) holds provided
k
is large enough and
\theta\leq ck^{-1}
.
Moreover, since
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t})\leq A^{h_{R_{k}}}(\Omega_{t})
for all
t
, the inequality (
3
) implies that (ii) holds as well. Finally, it is clear that (i) holds. Thus
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
belongs to the nice class
\mathcal{N}_{R,5c,ck^{-1},\theta}
provided
k
is large enough and
\theta\leq ck^{-1}
.
∎

Everything is now set up to carry out the min-max argument.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\{R_{j}\}
be the sequence from Proposition
15
and let
h_{j}=h_{R_{j}}
and
\zeta_{j}=\zeta_{R_{j}}
. Then for each
j
there is a smooth, almost embedded
h_{j}
-PMC

\Sigma_{j}=\partial\Omega_{j}

supported in
B_{R_{j}+1}
with
A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})=\omega(R_{j})
. The index of
\Omega_{j}
with respect to
A^{h_{j}}
is at most one and
\int_{\Omega_{j}}|\nabla\zeta_{j}|h\leq 10/R_{j}
.

Proof.

Fix an integer
j
. For notational convenience denote
R_{j}
by
R
and
h_{R_{j}}
by
h_{R}
. By Proposition
17
and Proposition
18
, it is possible to find a sequence of mountain pass paths
\{\Omega_{t}^{k}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
which belong to the class
\mathcal{N}_{R,10/R,\eta,\theta_{k}}
. Here
\eta>0
is a fixed positive number and
\theta_{k}\to 0
. Since
\theta_{k}\to 0
, it follows that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(\Omega^{k}_{t})\to\omega(R)

as
k\to\infty
. We would like to apply Zhou’s prescribed mean curvature min-max theorem (Theorem 1.7 in
[
33
]
) to the sequence
\{\Omega^{k}_{t}\}
in order to produce an
h_{R}
-PMC. Unfortunately, the theorem does not directly apply in our situation. Nevertheless the proof of the theorem still applies with minor modifications. We now explain the necessary changes.

Let
M=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}|h|
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all Caccioppoli sets
\Omega\subset B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
with

\operatorname{Area}(\partial\Omega)\leq 2\omega(R)+M\operatorname{Vol}(B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}).

Likewise let
\mathcal{V}
be the set of all varifolds with support in
\overline{B}_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}
and mass bounded by

2\omega(R)+M\operatorname{Vol}(B_{R+1-\varepsilon_{R}}).

Let
A
be the set of all
V\in\mathcal{V}
with
M
-bounded first variation. By standard arguments, there exists an
\mathbf{F}
-continuous tightening map
\psi\colon\mathcal{C}\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with the following properties:

(i)

\psi(\Omega,0)=\Omega
,

(ii)

\psi(\Omega,t)=\Omega
if
|\partial\Omega|\in A
,

(iii)

A^{h_{R}}(\psi(\Omega,t))\leq A^{h_{R}}(\Omega)
,

(iv)

if
|\partial\Omega|\notin A
then

A^{h_{R}}(\psi(\Omega,1))-A^{h_{R}}(\Omega)\leq L(\mathbf{F}(|\partial\Omega|,A))

where
L\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function with
L(0)=0
and
L(t)>0
for
t>0
,

(v)

\mathbf{F}(\psi(\Omega,t),\Omega)\leq g(\mathbf{F}(|\partial\Omega|,A))
where
g\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function with
g(0)=0
.

For each integer
k
define the sets

\displaystyle I_{k}=\{t\in[0,1]:\,A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t}^{k})\geq\omega(R)-\eta/2\},

\displaystyle J_{k}=\{t\in[0,1]:\,A^{h_{R}}(\Omega_{t}^{k})>\omega(R)-\eta\}.

Then
I_{k}
is a compact subset of
J_{k}
, and
J_{k}
is an open subset of
(0,1)
. Let
\tau_{k}\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a continuous function which is one on
I_{k}
and zero outside of
J_{k}
.

Let
U^{k}_{t}=\psi(\Omega^{k}_{t},\tau_{k}(t))
. Then
\{U^{k}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{R}
. By property (iii) of the tightening map,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}A^{h_{R}}(U^{k}_{t})\to\omega(R)

as
k\to\infty
. A critical sequence is a sequence
\{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

A^{h_{R}}(U^{k}_{t_{k}})\to\omega(R),\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

Define the critical set

\mathcal{K}=\big\{V\in\mathcal{V}:\,V=\lim_{k\to\infty}|\partial U^{k}_{t_{k}}|\text{ for some critical sequence }\{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\}\big\}.

We claim that
\mathcal{K}\subset A
. To see this, suppose that
\{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\}
is a critical sequence and let
V=\lim_{k\to\infty}|\partial U^{k}_{t_{k}}|
. Notice that

A^{h_{R}}(U^{k}_{t_{k}})-A^{h_{R}}(\Omega^{k}_{t_{k}})\to 0,\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

By property (iv) of the tightening map and the fact that
t_{k}\in I_{k}
for
k
large enough, this implies that

\mathbf{F}(|\partial U^{k}_{t_{k}}|,A)\to 0.

The claim follows.

Again consider a critical sequence
\{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\}
. Since
\mathbf{F}(|\partial U^{k}_{t_{k}}|,A)\to 0
, property (v) of the tightening map implies that

\operatorname{Vol}(U^{k}_{t_{k}}\Delta\Omega^{k}_{t_{k}})\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Now observe that

\displaystyle\int_{U^{k}_{t_{k}}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{k}_{t_{k}}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h+\int_{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\setminus\Omega^{k}_{t_{k}}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{R}+C\operatorname{Vol}(U^{k}_{t_{k}}\setminus\Omega^{k}_{t_{k}}),\phantom{\int}

where we’ve used the fact that
t_{k}\in I_{k}
and
\{\Omega^{k}_{t}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{R,10/R,\eta,\theta_{k}}
.
It follows that

(5)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{U^{k}_{t_{k}}}|\nabla\zeta_{R}|h\leq\frac{10}{R},

and so there is good control over
U^{k}_{t_{k}}
in the transition region.

By hypothesis (H3), the function
h_{R}
is an admissible prescription function on
B_{R+1}
. (See
[
36
]
for the definition of admissible prescription functions.) Moreover, there is no closed, embedded minimal surface contained in the level set
\{h_{R}=0\}
. The almost-minimizing, regularity, and index arguments now carry through unchanged to show that there is a critical sequence
\{U^{k}_{t_{k}}\}
and a Caccioppoli set
\Omega
such that

\mathbf{F}(U^{k}_{t_{k}},\Omega)\to 0.

Moreover,
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
is a smooth, almost embedded
h_{R}
-PMC, and
A^{h_{R}}(\Omega)=\omega(R)
, and the index of
\Omega
with respect to
A^{h_{R}}
is at most one. Finally
\Omega
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\zeta_{j}|h\leq\frac{10}{R}

by equation (
5
). This proves the result.
∎

3.3.
Finding the
h
-PMC

Again assume the function
h
satisfies (H1)-(H4). In this section, we show that, for large
j
, the surfaces
\Sigma_{j}
constructed in Proposition
19
have a component that does not intersect the transition region
B_{R_{j}+1}\setminus B_{R_{j}}
. The first step is to establish uniform area bounds on the surfaces
\Sigma_{j}
.

In the following proof, the idea of differentiating
A^{h_{j}}
along the homothetic regions
s\Omega_{j}
is taken from
[
4
]
. This is the only place in the proof where hypothesis (H2) is needed.

Proposition 20
.

There is a constant
C
which does not depend on
j
such that
\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})\leq C
for all
j
.

Proof.

Fix a positive integer
j
. For each
s>0
consider the homothetic regions
s\Omega_{j}
. Since
\Sigma_{j}
is an
h_{j}
-PMC, it follows that

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=1}A^{h_{j}}(s\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=1}\operatorname{Area}(s\Sigma_{j})-\int_{\Sigma_{j}}h_{j}X\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=n\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})-\int_{\Omega_{j}}\operatorname{div}(h_{j}X),

where
\nu
is the outward normal to
\Sigma_{j}
and
X
denotes the vector field
X(x)=x
. Now observe that

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(h_{j}X)

\displaystyle=h_{j}\operatorname{div}(X)+\nabla h_{j}\cdot X

\displaystyle=(n+1)h_{j}+\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X+h\nabla\zeta_{j}\cdot X.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(n+1)A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle=(n+1)A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})-\frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=1}A^{h_{j}}(s\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})-\int_{\Omega_{j}}(\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X+h\nabla\zeta_{j}\cdot X).

By construction,
\Omega_{j}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{j}}h\nabla\zeta_{j}\cdot X\leq 2R_{j}\int_{\Omega_{j}}|\nabla\zeta_{j}|h\leq 2R_{j}\left(\frac{10}{R_{j}}\right)\leq 20.

Combined with the previous equation, this yields

\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})\leq(n+1)A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})+20+\int_{\Omega_{j}}\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X.

It follows that

(6)

(1-\sigma)\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})\leq\left(n+1-\sigma\right)A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})+20+\int_{\Omega_{j}}(\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma{h_{j}}).

But there is an estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{j}}(\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma{h_{j}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{j}}\zeta_{j}(\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma{h})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\rho}}(|\nabla h\cdot X|+\sigma|h|)+\int_{\Omega_{j}\setminus B_{\rho}}\zeta_{j}(\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma h)

and assumption (H2) guarantees that
\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma h<0
outside
B_{\rho}
. Therefore

\int_{\Omega_{j}}(\zeta_{j}\nabla h\cdot X-\sigma{h_{j}})\leq C

for a constant
C
that does not depend on
j
. Using this estimate in (
6
) yields

(1-\sigma)\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})\leq(n+1-\sigma)A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})+C.

The desired upper bound for
\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})
follows.
∎

The following result of Topping
[
29
]
shows that bounds on area and mean curvature imply a bound on diameter.

Proposition 21

(Special Case of Theorem 1.1 in
[
29
]
)
.

Assume that
\Sigma^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a closed, connected, immersed hypersurface. Then

\operatorname{diam}(\Sigma)\leq C\int_{\Sigma}|H|^{n-1}

where
C
depends only on
n
, and
H
denotes the mean curvature of
\Sigma
.

This immediately implies the next corollary.

Corollary 22
.

There is a constant
C
which does not depend on
j
such that any connected component
\Gamma_{j}
of
\Sigma_{j}
satisfies
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{j})\leq C
.

It remains to show that some component of
\Sigma_{j}
does not intersect the transition region when
j
is large. This will complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 23
.

Fix a unit vector
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and a constant
\tau
and let

h_{\infty}(x)=n\zeta(x\cdot\nu+\tau).

Every closed
h_{\infty}
-PMC is a union of spheres of radius 1 contained in the region where
h_{\infty}=n
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
be a compact, almost embedded
h_{\infty}
-PMC. Differentiating
A^{h_{\infty}}
along the translated regions
\Omega+t\nu
gives

0=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}A^{h_{\infty}}(\Omega+t\nu)=-\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\int_{\Omega+t\nu}h_{\infty}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla h_{\infty}\cdot\nu.

Since
h_{\infty}
is monotone in the
\nu
direction, it follows that
\Omega
is contained in the region where
h_{\infty}\equiv n
. Hence, by the well-known result of Alexandrov
[
1
]
, the
h_{\infty}
-PMC
\Sigma
is a union of spheres of radius 1.
∎

Proposition 24
.

Some connected component of
\Omega_{j}
is contained in
B_{R_{j}}
when
j
is large.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary there is a subsequence (not relabeled) such that every component of
\Omega_{j}
intersects
B_{R_{j}+1}\setminus B_{R_{j}}
. Let
\Theta_{j}
be a component of
\Omega_{j}
and let
p_{j}
be a point of
\Gamma_{j}=\partial\Theta_{j}
which is farthest from the origin. Define the translations

\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}=\Gamma_{j}-p_{j},\quad\Theta_{j}^{\prime}=\Theta_{j}-p_{j},\quad h_{j}^{\prime}(x)=h_{j}(x+p_{j}).

Note that the surfaces
\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}
are all contained in a fixed ball
B
centered at the origin by Corollary
22
.

Passing to a subsequence (not relabeled) and using (H1), there is a unit vector
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and a constant
\tau
, and a function
h_{\infty}(x)=n\zeta(x\cdot\nu+\tau)
such that
h_{j}^{\prime}
converges smoothly to
h_{\infty}
on
B
. By the compactness theorem for prescribed mean curvature hypersurfaces with bounded area and index (
[
33
]
Theorem 2.6), passing to a subsequence (not relabelled) there is a smooth, almost-embedded
h_{\infty}
-PMC
\Gamma_{\infty}=\partial\Theta_{\infty}
contained in
B
such that
\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}
converges smoothly with multiplicity one to
\Gamma_{\infty}
away from at most one point. In particular,

A^{h_{j}}(\Theta_{j})=A^{h_{j}^{\prime}}(\Theta_{j}^{\prime})\to A^{h_{\infty}}(\Theta_{\infty}).

By Lemma
23
, the set
\Theta_{\infty}
is a union of balls of radius 1 contained in the region where
h_{\infty}=n
. It follows that
A^{h_{\infty}}(\Theta_{\infty})\geq A^{n}(B_{1})
. Repeating the above argument with any remaining components of
\Omega_{j}\setminus\Theta_{j}
extracts more spheres at infinity. (There can be at most finitely many such components by the uniform area bound.) Thus

\lim_{j\to\infty}A^{h_{j}}(\Omega_{j})\geq A^{n}(B_{1}),

and this is a contradiction.
∎

The following corollary shows that the energy identity holds provided there are no
h
-PMCs with negative energy.

Corollary 25
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying the assumptions of Theorem

. Assume in addition that there are no smooth, almost embedded

-PMCs
\Gamma=\partial\Theta
with

. Then there is a smooth, almost embedded

with

The proof of Proposition

shows that any component of

drifting to infinity carries energy at least

∎

4.
CMCs in Asymptotically Flat Manifolds

In this section, we show that essentially the same argument can be used to construct CMCs in an asymptotically flat manifold.

4.1.
Set Up

To begin, we recall the notion of an asymptotically flat 3-manifold.

Definition 26
.

A manifold
(M^{3},g)
is called asymptotically flat if there is a decomposition
M=K\cup E
where
K
is compact and
E
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{R}
for some
R>0
. In the corresponding coordinates on
E
, it is further required that

g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}

where
h_{ij}\to 0
smoothly as
r\to\infty
and moreover

h_{ij}=O(r^{-1}),\quad h_{ij,k}=O(r^{-2})

as
r\to\infty
.

For the rest of this section, let
(M^{3},g)
be a fixed asymptotically flat 3-manifold. Let
M=K\cup E
be the decomposition of
M
as per Definition
26
. Fix a constant
c>0
.

Definition 27
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all
c
-mountain pass paths in
M
. Also let
\omega
be the one-parameter min-max width for
A^{c}
.

Proposition 28
.

The one parameter min-max width satisfies
0<\omega\leq A^{c}(B_{2/c})
. Here
B_{2/c}
is a Euclidean ball of radius
2/c
and
A^{c}
is computed with respect to the Euclidean metric.

Proof.

The isoperimetric inequality holds in an asymptotically flat manifold. Therefore the proof is identical to that of Proposition
7
.
∎

The crucial assumption required for one-parameter min-max to detect a CMC is the following:

(H)

The one parameter width
\omega\text{ is strictly less than }A^{c}(B_{2/c})
.

We will discuss some geometric conditions which imply that (H) holds in a later section. For simplicity, in the rest of the paper we will assume that
c=2
. In this case, hypothesis (H) becomes that
\omega<A^{2}(B_{1})=\frac{4\pi}{3}
.

4.2.
The Approximate Problems

As above fix
c=2
. Let
(M^{3},g)
be an asymptotically flat manifold satisfying condition (H) for
c=2
, and let
M=K\cup E
be its decomposition into a compact piece and an asymptotically flat end. Let
S_{r}
be the sphere of Euclidean radius
r
in
E
, and let
B_{r}
be the open, bounded set enclosed by
S_{r}
in
M
. The following asymptotic formula for the mean curvature of
S_{r}
was proved by Huang in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 29
.

The mean curvature of
S_{r}
satisfies

H_{S_{r}}=\frac{2}{r}+O(r^{-2})

as
r\to\infty

4.3.

The goal of this section is to prove that the surfaces

The next proposition constructs a vector field on

Proposition 44

There is a vector field

\operatorname{div}_{P}X(x)\leq\frac{7}{3}

Since

is asymptotically flat, it follows that

(7)

|g(\nabla_{w}Y,w)-1|\leq\frac{1}{100}

Proposition 45

\displaystyle 0=\delta A^{2\zeta_{j}}\bigg|_{\Omega_{j}}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{j}}\operatorname{div}_{T_{p}\Sigma_{j}}(X)-2\int_{\Sigma_{j}}\zeta_{j}X\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{j}}\operatorname{div}_{T_{p}\Sigma_{j}}(X)-2\int_{\Omega_{j}}\operatorname{div}(\zeta_{j}X).

\operatorname{div}(\zeta_{j}X)=\langle\nabla\zeta_{j},X\rangle+\zeta_{j}\operatorname{div}(X).

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{8}{3}A^{2\zeta_{j}}(\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{j})-\frac{16}{3}\int_{\Omega_{j}}\zeta_{j}

4.4.
Examples

In this section, we construct a family of examples to which Theorem
2
applies. Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and fix a function
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

v={1}/{r}

near infinity. Then the conformal metrics

g_{t}=(1+tv)^{4}g_{\text{euc}}

are asymptotically flat. Let
B_{r}
denote a ball of radius
r
centered at the origin in the Euclidean metric. For
t>0
, the sign of the scalar curvature of
g_{t}
at
x
is equal to the sign of
-\Delta v(x)
.

Proposition 49
.

Assume that
\Delta v<0
on
B_{2}
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg[\operatorname{Area}_{g_{t}}(\partial B_{1})-2\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t}}(B_{1})\bigg]<0.

Proof.

Observe that

\operatorname{Area}_{g_{t}}(\partial B_{1})-2\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t}}(B_{1})=\int_{\partial B_{1}}(1+tv)^{4}-2\int_{B_{1}}(1+tv)^{6},

where the integrals are taken with respect to the Euclidean surface measure and the Euclidean volume respectively. Thus

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg[\operatorname{Area}_{g_{t}}(\partial B_{1})-2\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t}}(B_{1})\bigg]=4\int_{\partial B_{1}}v-12\int_{B_{1}}v.

Define the auxiliary function

\phi(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}v-\frac{3}{r^{3}}\int_{B_{r}}v.

To prove the proposition, we must check that
\phi(1)<0
. To this end, note that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\partial_{\nu}v-\frac{3}{r^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}v+\frac{9}{r^{4}}\int_{B_{r}}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\Delta v-\frac{3}{r}\phi(r).

Multiplying this by
r^{3}
and using the fact that
\Delta v<0
gives that

r^{3}\phi^{\prime}(r)+3r^{2}\phi(r)<0.

Finally, integrating and using the fact that
\phi(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
proves that
r^{3}\phi(r)<0
for all
r\in(0,2]
. In particular, this implies that
\phi(1)<0
, as needed.
∎

This has the following corollary.

Abstract
We analyze a set of contributions to the superconformal index of 4d 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) super Yang-Mills using the Bethe Ansatz approach. These contributions dominate at the large N N limit, where their leading order in N N reproduces various supersymmetric Euclidean black hole saddles in the dual theory, and they also dominate for finite N N in high temperature Cardy-like limits. u\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z})
, however, there is a degenerate relation

\widetilde{\Gamma}(u;\tau,\tau)=\frac{e^{-\pi i\mathcal{Q}(u;\tau,\tau)}}{\theta_{0}(\frac{u}{\tau};-\frac{1}{\tau})}\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\psi\left(\frac{k+1+u}{\tau}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{k-u}{\tau}\right)}\;,

(C.20)

the function
\psi
is the elliptic digamma function defined below and the polynomial
\mathcal{Q}
reduces to

\mathcal{Q}(u;\tau,\tau)=\frac{(2u-2\tau+1)(2u(u+1)-2\tau(2u+1)+\tau^{2})}{12\tau^{2}}\;.

(C.21)

Using

\mathcal{Q}(u+1;\tau,\tau)-\mathcal{Q}(u;\tau,\tau)=\frac{(u+1)(u+1-2\tau)}{\tau^{2}}+\frac{5}{6}\;,

(C.22)

one can check that
\widetilde{\Gamma}(u;\tau,\tau)
is invariant under
u\to u+1
.

Function
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}
.

We find it convenient to define a function,
\mathcal{G}
, which is closely related to the logarithmic derivative of
\theta_{0}
,

\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\log\left(\frac{\theta_{0}(\Delta-u;\tau)}{\theta_{0}(\Delta+u;\tau)}\right)\;.

(C.23)

It has the series expansion

\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(z^{\ell}+z^{-\ell})\bigl(y^{\ell}-(q/y)^{\ell}\bigr)}{1-q^{\ell}}

(C.24)

which converges for
|q|<|yz|<1
and
|q|<|y/z|<1
, with
z=e^{2\pi iu}
and
y=e^{2\pi i\Delta}
.

The function has the properties

\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)=\mathcal{G}(-u,\Delta;\tau)=-\mathcal{G}(u,\tau-\Delta;\tau)\;.

(C.25)

and also the modular properties, which follow from the ones of
\theta_{0}
:

\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau+1)=\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)\;,\qquad\mathcal{G}\left(\frac{u}{\tau},\frac{\Delta}{\tau};-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)=\tau-2\Delta-1+\tau\,\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)\;.

(C.26)

Its periodicities are

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}(u+1,\Delta;\tau)=\mathcal{G}(u+\tau,\Delta;\tau)=\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta+1;\tau)

(C.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta+\tau;\tau)

\displaystyle=2+\mathcal{G}(u,\Delta;\tau)\;.

In particular,
\mathcal{G}
is an elliptic function of
u
, and quasi-elliptic of
\Delta
.

Function
\boldsymbol{\psi}
.

Define, for
\im(t)<0
, the function

\psi(t)=\exp\left[t\log\bigl(1-e^{-2\pi it}\bigr)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-2\pi it})\right]=\exp\left[-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\ell}+\frac{1}{2\pi i\,\ell^{2}}\right)e^{-2\pi it\,\ell}\right]\;.

(C.28)

The branch of the logarithm is determined by its series expansion
\log(1-z)=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}z^{\ell}/\ell
, whereas
\text{Li}_{2}(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}z^{\ell}/\ell^{2}
is the dilogarithm. One can show that the branch cut discontinuities of the logarithm and the dilogarithm cancel in the definition of
\psi(t)
, therefore the latter extends to a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane. Some useful properties of
\psi(t)
are:

\psi(t)\,\psi(-t)=e^{-\pi i(t^{2}-1/6)}\;,\qquad\qquad\psi(t+n)=(1-e^{-2\pi it})^{n}\,\psi(t)\qquad\text{for}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\;.

(C.29)

In particular, from (
C.28
),
\psi(0)=e^{\pi i/12}
.

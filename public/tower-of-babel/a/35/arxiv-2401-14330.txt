On strong growth conditions for weighted spaces of entire functions Thanks: This research was funded in whole by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) project 10.55776/P33417
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We characterize the inclusion relations between weighted classes of entire functions with rapid decreasing growth and study strong growth comparison relations between given weights. In our considerations first we focus on weights defined in terms of the so-called associated weight function where the weight(system) is based on a given sequence. Then the abstract weight function case is reduced to the weight sequence setting by using the so-called associated weight sequence. Finally, we compare weighted entire function spaces defined in terms of so-called dilatation-type and exponential-type weight systems.

(*)

2.
Weights, weighted spaces and growth conditions

2.1.
Basic convention

We use the notation
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\dots,\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{>0}=\{1,2,\dots\}
.

We call a weight
v

normalized
when
v(t)=1
for all
t\in[0,1]
. A normalized
v
satisfies
v(t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
.

When both
v
,
w
are (normalized) weight functions, then the product
v\cdot w
is so, too.

For defining the class in (
1.1
) normalization can be assumed w.l.o.g.: Otherwise replace
v
by a normalized weight, say
v^{n}
, and such that
v(t)=v^{n}(t)
for all large
t>1
. This gives
H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})=H^{\infty}_{v^{n}}(\mathbb{C})
as l.c.v.s.

Since
w(t),v(t)\neq 0
for all
t
, (
1.2
) precisely means that

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;w(t)\leq Cv(t).

2.2.
Weight systems and corresponding weighted classes

We revisit the notions introduced in
[
weightedentirecharacterization
, Sect. 2.2]
. Let
\underline{\mathcal{V}}=(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
be a non-increasing sequence of weights, i.e.
v_{n}\geq v_{n+1}
for all
n
. Then we define the (LB)-space
H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathbb{C})
of the Banach spaces
H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
, so

(2.1)

H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathbb{C}):=\varinjlim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}(\mathbb{C}).

Analogously, if
\overline{\mathcal{V}}=(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
is a non-decreasing sequence of weights, i.e.
v_{n}\leq v_{n+1}
for all
n
, then we define the Fréchet-space
H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathbb{C})
to be the projective limit of the Banach spaces
H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
, so

(2.2)

H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathbb{C}):=\varprojlim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}(\mathbb{C}).

Let
v
be a weight function and
c>0
, then set

(2.3)

v_{c}:t\mapsto v(ct),\hskip 20.0ptv^{c}:t\mapsto v(t)^{c},

and write again
v
instead of
v_{1}
resp.
v^{1}
.
v
is a weight function if and only if some/each weight
v_{c}
resp. if and only if some/each
v^{c}
is so. Note that for all
d\geq c>0
and all large
t
we have
v^{d}(t)\leq v^{c}(t)
since eventually
v(t)\leq 1
and we have this estimate for all
t\geq 0
if
v
is normalized. Moreover,
v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w
if and only if
v_{c}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{c}
if and only if
v^{c}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{c}
for some/each
c>0
.

Consider the corresponding weight systems

(2.4)

\underline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v_{c})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}},\hskip 20.0pt\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v_{\frac{1}{c}})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}.

Thus
\underline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-increasing sequence of weights, whereas
\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-decreasing sequence. Similarly, if
v\leq 1
, then consider

(2.5)

\underline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v^{c})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}},\hskip 20.0pt\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v^{\frac{1}{c}})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}.

Since
v\leq 1
the set
\underline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-increasing sequence of weights, whereas
\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-decreasing sequence. The assumption
v\leq 1
holds if
v
is normalized.

Definition 2.1
.

The weight systems in (
2.4
) are called to be of dilatation-type and the systems in (
2.5
) of exponential-type.

Let us write
v\hypertarget{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if

(2.6)

\exists\;c\geq 1:\;\;\;v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{c},

and
v\hypertarget{simc}{\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, if

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptw\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}v.

Similarly, write
v\hypertarget{ompreceqpowc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if

(2.7)

\exists\;c\geq 1:\;\;\;v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{c},

and
v\hypertarget{simpowc}{\sim^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, if

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptw\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}v.

Note:

(*)

Both
v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
and
v\hyperlink{ompreceqpowc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
are weaker than

.

(*)

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
resp.
v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if and only if

\exists\;c\geq 1\;\forall\;a>0:\;\;\;v_{a}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{ac}\;\;\;\text{resp.}\;\;\;v^{a}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{ac}.

2.3.
Weight sequences and conditions

Let
M=(M_{j})_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The corresponding sequence of quotients is denoted by
\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
,
j\geq 1
, and set
\mu_{0}:=1
.
M
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
and
M
is called
log-convex
if

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1},

equivalently if the sequence
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is non-decreasing. If
M_{0}=1
and
M
is log-convex, then
(M_{j})^{1/j}\leq\mu_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
j\mapsto(M_{j})^{1/j}
is non-decreasing (see e.g.
[
diploma
, Lemma 2.0.4]
) and (see e.g.
[
diploma
, Lemma 2.0.6]
)

(2.8)

\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{j}M_{k}\leq M_{j+k}.

Now recall
[
weightedentirecharacterization
, Def. 2.4]
:

Definition 2.2
.

M
is called a weight sequence if

1=M_{0}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty.

Moreover consider the set

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;M\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty\},

i.e.
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
if and only if
M
is a log-convex weight sequence with
M_{1}\geq 1
. Note that each
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
is non-decreasing since
\mu_{j}\geq 1
for all
j
and
j\mapsto\mu_{j}
is non-decreasing.

M
(with
M_{0}=1
) has condition
moderate growth
, denoted by
(\text{mg})
, if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k}M_{j}M_{k}.

In
[
Komatsu73
]
this is denoted by
(M.2)
and also known under the name
stability under ultradifferential operators.

Let
M,N\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we write
M\leq N
if
M_{j}\leq N_{j}
for all
j
and
M\hypertarget{preceq}{\preceq}N
if
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}<+\infty
.

M
and
N
are called
equivalent
, written
M\hypertarget{approx}{\approx}N
, if

M\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}N\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptN\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}M.

Property
(\operatorname{mg})
is clearly preserved under
\approx
.

In
[
weightedentirecharacterization
, Rem. 2.5]
we have shown that for any log-convex weight sequence
M
there exists an equivalent
N\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
such that even
\mu_{j}=\nu_{j}
for all
j
sufficiently large.

2.4.
Associated weight function

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
(with
M_{0}=1
), then the
associated weight function

\omega_{M}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\omega_{M}(t):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)\;\;\;\text{for}\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\hskip 30.0pt\omega_{M}(0):=0.

For an abstract introduction of the associated function we refer to
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I]
, see also
[
Komatsu73
, Definition 3.1]
and the more recent work
[
regularnew
]
.

If
\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}>0
, then
\omega_{M}(t)=0
for sufficiently small
t
, since
\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)<0\Leftrightarrow t<(M_{j})^{1/j}
holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. (In particular, if
M_{j}\geq 1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\omega_{M}
is vanishing on
[0,1]
.) Moreover, under this assumption
t\mapsto\omega_{M}(t)
is a continuous non-decreasing function, which is convex in the variable
\log(t)
and tends faster to infinity than any
\log(t^{j})
,
j\geq 1
, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty
implies that
\omega_{M}(t)<+\infty
for each finite
t
which shall be considered as a basic assumption for defining
\omega_{M}
. In particular, this holds provided
M
is a weight sequence.

If
M
is a log-convex weight sequence, then let us introduce the counting function

(2.9)

\Sigma_{M}(t):=|\{j\geq 1:\;\;\;\mu_{j}\leq t\}|,\;\;\;t\geq 0.

In this case we can compute
M
by involving
\omega_{M}
as follows, see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I, 1.4, 1.8]
and also
[
Komatsu73
, Prop. 3.2]
:

(2.10)

M_{j}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))},\;\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

If
M
is a weight sequence which is not log-convex, then the right-hand side of (
2.10
) yields
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{j}
with
M^{\operatorname{lc}}
denoting the
log-convex minorant
of
M
and
M^{\operatorname{lc}}
is a log-convex weight sequence.

Moreover, one has

\forall\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}]:\;\;\;\omega_{M}(t)=0,

which follows by the known integral representation formula (see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, 1.8. III]
and also
[
Komatsu73
,
(3.11)
]
)

(2.11)

\omega_{M}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du=\int_{\mu_{1}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du.

In particular, if
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
, then
\omega_{M}
vanishes on
[0,1]
.

Finally, for any weight sequence
M
we have

(2.12)

\omega_{M^{\operatorname{lc}}}=\omega_{M}.

2.6.
On the class of (log-)convex weights

We recall definitions and statements from
[

, Sect. 2.6 & 2.7]
; see also the citations there. Let

be a normalized (radial) weight function, then set

(2.17)

\omega^{u}(t):=-\log(u(t)),\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty),

and we get:

(*)

\omega^{u}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega^{u}(t)=+\infty
and

(*)

(*)

The fact that

\hypertarget{om3}{(\omega_{3})}:\;\;\;\log(t)=o(\omega^{u}(t))\hskip 15.0ptt\rightarrow+\infty.

(*)

We have that

(2.18)

t\mapsto\omega^{u}(e^{t})(=-\log(u(e^{t})))\;\;\;\text{is convex on}\;\mathbb{R},

if and only if
\varphi_{\omega^{u}}:t\mapsto\omega^{u}(e^{t})

is named after

]

.

Definition 2.5

We call a weight

Let
u
be a normalized and convex weight and let

be given by (

M^{u}=(M^{u}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

Note that in order to avoid confusion instead of

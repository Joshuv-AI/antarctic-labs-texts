Radial and orbital decomposition of charge radii of Ca nuclei: Comparative study of Skyrme and Fayans functionals
T. Inakura, N. Hinohara, H. Nakada

Abstract
We investigate the charge and point-proton radii of the Ca nuclei in detail in the density functional theory framework. As the Fayans energy density functional provides characteristic N N -dependence, successfully describing the parabolic behavior of the differential charge radii in 20 ≤ N ≤ 28 20\leq N\leq 28 , we pose our particular focus on its physics origin, by decomposing them into the radial and orbital contributions. The results are compared with those from the Skyrme plus usual pairing functional, which is taken as a representative of the functionals having normal pairing channels. We point out that, because the enhancement of the differential charge radii in N < 20 N<20 with the Fayans functional, which is contradictory with the data, has the origin parallel to the parabolic behavior in 20 ≤ N ≤ 28 20\leq N\leq 28 , it is significant to describe both N N regions simultaneously.



I
Introduction

Radius is one of the fundamental properties representing nuclear structure.
It has been known that the radii of stable nuclei are proportional
to
A^{1/3}
in the first approximation, where
A
is the mass number,
linked to the saturation of the densities
[
1
]
.
This feature is a result of the balance between the attraction among nucleons
at low density
and the repulsive effects that become dominant at high density.
The self-consistent mean-field calculations
or the density functional theory (DFT)
have been developed and are now recognized as one of the standard approaches
to the nuclear structure.
Since the nuclei are self-bound systems,
self-consistent calculations in the DFT
provide a suitable framework for studying nuclear radii.
An accurate description of radii is fundamental to the DFT,
because the density
\rho_{t}(\mathbf{r})
(
t=\mathrm{n},\mathrm{p}
) is usually taken
as a principal variable
[
2
]

and the mean-square radius
is the lowest-order moment of
\rho_{t}(\mathbf{r})
,

\langle r^{2}\rangle_{t}=\frac{1}{N_{t}}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\,\rho_{t}(\mathbf{r}),

(1)

where
N_{t}
is the neutron or proton number (
i.e.
,
N
or
Z
).

The nuclear charge radii are measured by electric probes
[
3
]
,
and their data are much less ambiguous than those obtained by hadronic probes.
Owing to the recent development
of the laser spectroscopy experiments
[
4
]
,
abundant data on the differential charge radii
have been accumulated via the isotope-shift measurements,
by which many intriguing results beyond the
A^{1/3}
rule have been reported.
The kinks at magic neutron numbers are among them:
the kinks at
N=126
near Pb isotopes
[
5
]
,
at
N=82
in the Sn isotopes
[
6
]
,
and at
N=28
in the Ca and Ni isotopes
[
7
,
8
]
.
Arguments linking the kinks to properties of the nucleonic interaction
or the energy density functional (EDF) have been given.
The kinks are attributed to a property of the spin-orbit interaction
between nucleons in Refs.
[
9
,
10
]
,
which could be congenial to relativistic approaches
[
11
]
.
Additional effects of density dependence in the spin-orbit interaction
connected to the three-nucleon interaction
have been discussed
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
In contrast,
Fayans proposed an EDF with an extended form of the pairing functional
[
15
,
16
]
,
which has been claimed to play significant roles in the kinks
[
17
]
.

Another interesting result of the charge radii has been known in
{}^{40\textrm{--}48}
Ca.
The charge radii of the even-
N
nuclei in this region
vary parabolically as a function of
N
.
This behavior is hard to account for.
There has been a suggestion from the shell model
that the parabolic behavior may be a result of proton excitation
across
Z=20

[
18
]
.
A correlation of the charge radii and the quadrupole collectivity
has also been argued
[
19
]
.
On the other hand,
the Fayans EDF reproduces the parabolic behavior
in the self-consistent calculations that do not activate proton excitations across
Z=20

[
16
,
17
]
.
However, even-odd staggering of the charge radii tends to be too strong
when applying the Fayans EDF with parameters reproducing the parabola well,
and the behavior of the charge radii in the neutron-deficient region
[
20
]
,
which was argued in connection to the
\ell s
-closed nature of
N=20
and called
anti-kink
in Ref.
[
21
]
,
seems incorrect.

We investigate the charge radii of the Ca isotopes in the framework of nuclear DFT.
As mentioned above, the Fayans EDF has distinguished characters,
being able to describe the parabolic behavior of the charge radii in
20\leq N\leq 28
.
In Ref.
[
17
]
,
this property has been pointed out to originate
in its specific pairing channel.
Despite the remarkable result,
it is not obvious whether this picture is truly reasonable.
We inspect the radial and orbital contributions to the radii
in comparison with other EDFs,
particularly a Skyrme EDF.
The results may help to discriminate the EDF in future experiments.

II
Method

II.1
Skyrme and Fayans EDF

The Skyrme and Fayans EDFs are the functionals of several sets of local densities, commonly written in the form:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}r\,{\cal E}(\mathbf{r});

(2)

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=\mathrm{n},\mathrm{p}}\frac{\tau_{t}(\mathbf{r})}{2M}+{\cal E}_{\rm ph}(\mathbf{r})+{\cal E}_{\rm Coul}(\mathbf{r})+{\cal E}_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r}),

(3)

where
{\cal E}
is composed of the kinetic energy term with the averaged nucleon mass
M
, nuclear particle-hole part, Coulomb, and pairing part.
In this paper, we assume the time-reversal symmetry, restricting ourselves to even-even nuclei.
For a Skyrme EDF,
the time-even particle-hole part of
{\cal E}
is given by

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm ph}[\rho,\tau,\mathbf{J}]

\displaystyle={\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm v}[\rho,\tau]+{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm s}[\rho]+{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm ls}[\rho,\mathbf{J}],

(4)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm v}[\rho,\tau]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0,1}C_{k}^{\rho}[\rho_{0}][\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}+C_{k}^{\tau}\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})\tau_{k}(\mathbf{r}),

(5)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm s}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0,1}C_{k}^{\Delta\rho}\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})\Delta\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r}),

(6)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Sk}_{\rm ls}[\rho,\mathbf{J}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0,1}C_{k}^{\nabla J}\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{k}(\mathbf{r}),

(7)

where

\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})
,
\tau_{k}(\mathbf{r})
, and
\mathbf{J}_{k}(\mathbf{r})
are the local particle densities, kinetic densities, and spin-orbit densities
[
22
]
, with
k=0
and
1
corresponding to the isoscalar and isovector parts;
e.g.
,
\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})=\rho_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathbf{r})+\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})
and
\rho_{1}(\mathbf{r})=\rho_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathbf{r})-\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})
.
The
\rho_{0}
-dependent coupling constant is given in the form
C^{\rho}_{k}[\rho_{0}]=C_{k}^{\rho}[0]+C_{kD}^{\rho}[\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\alpha}
.

The particle-hole part of the Fayans EDF
[
15
]
, which does not contain
\tau_{k}(\mathbf{r})
,
is composed of the volume, surface, and spin-orbit parts,

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Fy}_{\rm ph}[\rho,\mathbf{J}]

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{\rm v}^{\rm Fy}[\rho]+{\cal E}_{\rm s}^{\rm Fy}[\rho]+{\cal E}_{\rm ls}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\mathbf{J}],

(8)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Fy}_{\rm v}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{F}\rho_{\rm sat}}{3}\left\{a_{+}^{\rm v}\frac{1-h_{1+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}}{1+h_{2+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+a_{-}^{\rm v}\frac{1-h_{1-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})}{1+h_{2-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})}[x_{1}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{\rm Fy}_{\rm s}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{F}\rho_{\rm sat}}{3}\frac{a_{+}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}}{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}+h^{\rm s}_{\nabla}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}},

(10)

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\rm ls}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\mathbf{J}]

\displaystyle=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}r_{s}^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}[\kappa\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{0}(\mathbf{r})+\kappa^{\prime}\rho_{1}(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{1}(\mathbf{r})].

(11)

The volume and surface terms of the Fayans EDF are given in a form analogous to the Padé approximant. The density
\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})
is used in terms of
x_{k}(\mathbf{r})=\rho_{k}(\mathbf{r})/\rho_{\rm sat}
, normalized with the saturation density
\rho_{\rm sat}=0.16
fm
-3
.
The parameters
\epsilon_{F}
and
r_{s}
are the Fermi energy and the Wigner-Seitz radius, whose values are given in Appendix
A
.
The volume term corresponds to the Skyrme coupling constants as

\displaystyle C_{0}^{\rho}[\rho_{0}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{F}a_{+}^{\rm v}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\frac{1-h_{1+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}}{1+h_{2+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}},

(12)

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\rho}[\rho_{0}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{F}a_{-}^{\rm v}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\frac{1-h_{1-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})}{1+h_{2-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})},

(13)

and the surface term of the Fayans EDF roughly corresponds to
the isoscalar surface term
C_{0}^{\Delta\rho}\rho_{0}\Delta\rho_{0}
of the Skyrme EDF with a density-dependent coupling constant.
The isovector coupling term
C_{1}^{\Delta\rho}
is not present in the
Fayans surface term, and the effective mass of the Fayans EDF is kept unity.
The spin-orbit term of the Fayans EDF has the same functional form as the extended Skyrme EDF
[
10
]
.
The two parameters
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
are used that are related to the Skyrme coupling constants as

\displaystyle C_{0}^{\nabla J}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}r_{s}^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\kappa,

(14)

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\nabla J}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}r_{s}^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\kappa^{\prime}.

(15)

The Coulomb EDF is written as a sum of the direct and exchange contributions,

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\rm Coul}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{2}[\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})]^{\frac{4}{3}}\left\{1-h_{\rm Coul}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}\right\},

(16)

where the point-proton density
\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})
is employed instead of the charge density.
The Coulomb exchange energy is evaluated with the Slater approximation, apart from the Coulomb-nuclear correlation term controlled by the parameter
h_{\rm Coul}
.
In the Skyrme EDF,
h_{\rm Coul}=0
.

The pairing functionals of the Skyrme and Fayans EDFs are

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\rm Sk}[\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t={\rm n,p}}\frac{V_{t}}{4}\left[1-\eta\frac{\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})}{\rho_{\rm pair}}\right]|\tilde{\rho}_{t}(\mathbf{r})|^{2},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pair}}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\left\{f^{\xi}_{\rm ex}+h_{+}^{\xi}[x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})]^{\gamma}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+h_{\nabla}^{\xi}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\}\sum_{t={\rm n,p}}|\tilde{\rho}_{t}(\mathbf{r})|^{2},

(18)

with
x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})=\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})/\rho_{\rm pair}
.
It is customary to set
\rho_{\rm pair}=\rho_{\rm sat}
.
In the Skyrme EDF, we use the mixed-type pairing with
\eta=1/2

unless explicitly specified.
When
\gamma=1
, the Fayans pairing parameters
f^{\xi}_{\rm ex}
and
h^{\xi}_{+}
are related to the Skyrme pairing parameters as

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}f^{\xi}_{\rm ex},

(19)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=-\frac{h^{\xi}_{+}}{f^{\xi}_{\rm ex}}.

(20)

On the other hand, the
h^{\xi}_{\nabla}
term that depends on the derivative of the isoscalar density is not present in the standard density-dependent pairing used with the Skyrme EDF.

The variational condition of the EDF leads to the
Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov equation,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}h_{t}-\lambda_{t}&\tilde{h}_{t}\\
-\tilde{h}_{t}^{\ast}&-h_{t}^{\ast}+\lambda_{t}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{U}_{\mu,t}\\
\mathcal{V}_{\mu,t}\end{pmatrix}=E_{\mu,t}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{U}_{\mu,t}\\
\mathcal{V}_{\mu,t}\end{pmatrix}.

(21)

The particle-hole and pairing parts of the local mean-field Hamiltonian are given by

\displaystyle h_{t}(\mathbf{r};s^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\nabla^{2}\delta_{s^{\prime}s}+U_{t}(\mathbf{r})\delta_{s^{\prime}s}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{i}(\mathbf{\nabla}\times\hat{\bm{\sigma}}_{s^{\prime}s})W_{t}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\mathbf{\nabla},

(22)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{t}(\mathbf{r};s^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle=\tilde{U}_{t}(\mathbf{r})\delta_{s^{\prime}s},

(23)

where
U_{t}(\mathbf{r}),W_{t}(\mathbf{r})
,
and
\tilde{U}_{t}(\mathbf{r})
are
the central, spin-orbit, and pairing potentials, respectively,

\lambda_{t}
is the chemical potential,
and
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}
stands for the Pauli matrix.
The potentials are calculated by the functional derivatives with respect to the corresponding densities

\displaystyle U_{t}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}[\rho,\mathbf{J},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta\rho_{t}},

(24)

\displaystyle W_{t}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}[\rho,\mathbf{J},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta(\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{t})},

(25)

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{t}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}[\rho,\mathbf{J},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}_{t}}.

(26)

The pair gap is defined as a density-averaged pair potential,

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}=\frac{1}{N_{t}}\int d^{3}r\tilde{U}_{t}(\mathbf{r})\rho_{t}(\mathbf{r}).

(27)

Because it plays an important role in the charge radius in the Ca isotope,
we explicitly write the rearrangement term,
the contribution to the central potential
U_{t}(\mathbf{r})
from the pairing EDF
[
17
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm pair}^{\rm Sk}}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta}{\rho_{\rm pair}}\sum_{t^{\prime}={\rm n,p}}\frac{V_{t^{\prime}}}{4}|\tilde{\rho}_{t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})|^{2},

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm pair}^{\rm Fy}}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\left\{\left[\frac{\gamma h_{+}^{\xi}}{\rho_{\rm pair}}[x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})]^{\gamma-1}-\frac{2h^{\xi}_{\nabla}r_{s}^{2}}{\rho_{\rm pair}^{2}}\Delta\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{t^{\prime}={\rm n,p}}|\tilde{\rho}_{t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\frac{2h^{\xi}_{\nabla}r_{s}^{2}}{\rho_{\rm pair}^{2}}\sum_{t^{\prime}={\rm n,p}}{\rm Re}\left[\tilde{\rho}_{t^{\prime}}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{\nabla}\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})\cdot\mathbf{\nabla}\tilde{\rho}_{t^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})\right]\right\}.

(29)

The other terms of the potentials in the Fayans functionals are given in Appendix
B
.

II.2
Differential radii and densities

The mean-square radius of point protons or neutrons is related to their density distributions via Eq. (
1
).
In the present work, the spherical symmetry is assumed for all nuclei under investigation, and the densities are functions of
r=|\mathbf{r}|
.
While the charge radius and the charge density are primarily subject to the distribution of the point protons,
the finite-size and the relativistic corrections
[
23
,
24
]
are taken into account as summarized in Appendix
C
.

The isotopic differences of the mean-square proton and charge radii are denoted by

\displaystyle\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A_{0},A}_{\mathrm{p}}

\displaystyle=\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{p}}(^{A}\mathrm{Ca})-\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{p}}(^{A_{0}}\mathrm{Ca}),

(30)

\displaystyle\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A_{0},A}_{\mathrm{ch}}

\displaystyle=\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{ch}}(^{A}\mathrm{Ca})-\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{ch}}(^{A_{0}}\mathrm{Ca}),

(31)

with a reference nucleus
{}^{A_{0}}
Ca.
We also define the differential proton and charge densities as

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}\rho_{\rm p}^{A_{0},A}(r)

\displaystyle:=(4\pi r^{2})r^{2}\left[\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(r,\,^{A}\mathrm{Ca})-\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(r,\,^{A_{0}}{\rm Ca})\right],

(32)

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}\rho_{\rm ch}^{A_{0},A}(r)

\displaystyle:=(4\pi r^{2})r^{2}\left[\rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(r,\,^{A}\mathrm{Ca})-\rho_{\mathrm{ch}}(r,\,^{A_{0}}{\rm Ca})\right],

(33)

so that the integration of
\bar{\Delta}\rho_{\rm p}^{A_{0},A}(r)
[
\bar{\Delta}\rho_{\rm ch}^{A_{0},A}(r)
] over
r
should be equal to
\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A_{0},A}_{\mathrm{p}}
[
\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle^{A_{0},A}_{\mathrm{ch}}
].

In Sec.
III.3
, we will further consider the contributions of individual proton orbits
j
to the proton radius and density.
For this purpose, we consider the following quantities:

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}\rho_{{\rm p},j}^{A_{0},A}(r)

\displaystyle:=(4\pi r^{2})r^{2}\sum_{m}\left[\big|\varphi_{\mathrm{p},jm}(\mathbf{r},\,^{A}\mathrm{Ca})\big|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.-\big|\varphi_{\mathrm{p},jm}(\mathbf{r},\,^{A_{0}}\mathrm{Ca})\big|^{2}\right],

(34)

where
\varphi_{\mathrm{p},jm}
is the wave functions of the proton single-particle (s.p.) orbit
j
with the magnetic quantum number
m
.
We here use the quantum number
j
also for representing the s.p. orbit, for simplicity.
The sum of
\bar{\Delta}\rho_{{\rm p},j}^{A_{0},A}(r)
over the occupied orbit
j
is equal to
\bar{\Delta}\rho_{\rm p}^{A_{0},A}(r)
in Eq. (
32
).
For the radii, we consider the difference of the root-mean-square radii of the orbit,

\displaystyle\delta\big[\sqrt{\langle r^{2}\rangle}\big]^{A_{0},A}_{\mathrm{p},j}

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,(4\pi r^{2})r^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{2j+1}\sum_{m}\big|\varphi_{\mathrm{p},jm}(\mathbf{r},\,^{A}\mathrm{Ca})\big|^{2}\bigg\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad-\bigg\{\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,(4\pi r^{2})r^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{2j+1}\sum_{m}\big|\varphi_{\mathrm{p},jm}(\mathbf{r},\,^{A_{0}}\mathrm{Ca})\big|^{2}\bigg\}^{1/2}.

(35)

IV
Summary and discussions

We have investigated the charge and point-proton radii of the Ca nuclei in the DFT framework.
It has been argued that the Fayans EDF provides parabolic
N
-dependence in the differential charge radii in
20\leq N\leq 28
, which looks compatible with the experimental data,
due to its unique pairing channel.
By decomposing the charge and proton radii into the radial and orbital contributions and paying special attention to the differential values from a reference nucleus, mainly
40
Ca, it is analyzed how the Fayans EDF provides specific
N
-dependence of the radii.
The results of the Fayans EDF (FaNDF
0
) have been compared with those of SkM
∗
, which is a representative of EDFs having a usual pairing channel.

The characteristic
N
-dependence in the
20\leq N\leq 28
region produced by the Fayans EDF has been traced back to the neutron pair potential, which has nodal behavior with a peak at smaller
r
than the pair potential by the SkM
∗
, a dip and rapid damping.
It influences the central part of the proton single-particle potential as found via
r^{2}\Delta U_{\mathrm{p}}^{40,A}
,
and then the proton and charge densities.
In terms of the orbitals, influences on the
sd
-shell orbits,
1d_{3/2}
and
2s_{1/2}
in particular, are significant.
However, although the
N
-dependence of the charge radii has been compatible with the experimental data in
20\leq N\leq 28
as a result,
the same mechanism gives rise to the erroneous enhancement of the charge radii in the
N<20
region.
For the charge radii of
{}^{40\textrm{--}48}
Ca, there have also been studies suggesting roles of proton excitation across the
Z=20
magic number.
For pinning down correct physics in the charge radii in the Ca isotopes, it is significant to discuss
36,38
Ca simultaneously.
It would be interesting if radial or orbital information of the radii is obtained in future experiments.

We have investigated
N
-dependence of the charge and proton radii in the neutron-rich region as well.
Irrespective of EDFs with no qualitatively apparent distinction, a steady increase of the charge radii for increasing
N
is predicted, although there is a quantitative difference.

Appendix B
Fayans potential

We here provide the explicit form for the central, spin-orbit, and pairing potentials in the Fayans EDF.
The neutron and proton central potentials are given by

\displaystyle U_{t}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}[\rho,\mathbf{J},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm v}^{\rm Fy}[\rho]}{\delta\rho_{t}}+\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm s}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,(\nabla\rho)^{2}]}{\delta\rho_{t}}+\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm ls}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\mathbf{J}]}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm Coul}[\rho]}{\delta\rho_{t}}+\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm pair}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta\rho_{t}},

(40)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm v}^{\rm Fy}}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{F}}{3}a_{+}^{\rm v}\left[-\sigma\frac{h_{1+}^{\rm v}+h_{2+}^{\rm v}}{\{1+h_{2+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}\}^{2}}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma+1}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+2\frac{1-h_{1+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}}{1+h_{2+}^{\rm v}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\epsilon_{F}}{3}a_{-}^{\rm v}\left[-\frac{h_{1-}^{\rm v}+h_{2-}^{\rm v}}{[1+h_{2-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}}[x_{1}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\pm 2\frac{1-h_{1-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})}{1+h_{2-}^{\rm v}x_{0}(\mathbf{r})}x_{1}(\mathbf{r})\right],

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm s}^{\rm Fy}}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{F}}{3}\frac{\sigma h_{+}^{\rm s}a_{+}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma-1}}{\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}+h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\frac{a_{+}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}\right\}\Delta\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})}{\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}+h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\epsilon_{F}a_{+}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}h_{+}^{\rm s}\sigma[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma-1}[\nabla\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}-3h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}}{\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}+h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\}^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}^{3}}\frac{a_{+}^{\rm s}h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{4}\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}\right\}}{\left\{1+h_{+}^{\rm s}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}+h_{\nabla}^{\rm s}r_{s}^{2}[\nabla x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right\}^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{ab}[\nabla_{a}\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})][\nabla_{b}\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})][\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})],

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm ls}^{\rm Fy}}{\delta\rho_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}r_{s}^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}[\kappa\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{0}(\mathbf{r})\pm\kappa^{\prime}\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{1}(\mathbf{r})],

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm Coul}}{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{2}[\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})]^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\{1-h_{\rm Coul}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma\frac{h_{\rm Coul}}{\rho_{\rm sat}}\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{2}[\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})]^{\frac{4}{3}}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma-1},

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm Coul}}{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{n}}}

\displaystyle=\sigma\frac{h_{\rm Coul}}{\rho_{\rm sat}}\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{2}[\rho_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})]^{\frac{4}{3}}[x_{0}(\mathbf{r})]^{\sigma-1}.

(45)

The last term in Eq. (
40
) is the pairing rearrangement term given in Eq. (
29
).
The spin-orbit potential is given by

\displaystyle W_{t}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm ls}^{\rm Fy}}{\delta(\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{t})}=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}r_{s}^{2}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}[\kappa\rho_{0}(\mathbf{r})\pm\kappa^{\prime}\rho_{1}(\mathbf{r})].

(46)

The plus (minus) sign is taken for the neutron (proton) potential
in Eqs. (
41
), (
43
), and (
46
).

The pairing potential is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{t}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta{\cal E}_{\rm pair}^{\rm Fy}[\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}]}{\delta\tilde{\rho}^{\ast}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\epsilon_{F}}{3\rho_{\rm sat}}\left[f_{\rm ex}^{\xi}+h_{+}^{\xi}[x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})]^{\gamma}+h_{\nabla}^{\xi}r_{s}^{2}[{\nabla}x_{\rm pair}(\mathbf{r})]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\tilde{\rho}_{t}(\mathbf{r}).

(47)

Appendix C
Charge density

The charge density is computed from the convoluted single-nucleon densities
[
24
]
,

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm ch}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{t={\mathrm{n},\mathrm{p}}}[\rho_{ct}(\mathbf{r})+\omega_{ct}(\mathbf{r})],

(48)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{ct}(\mathbf{r})=\int d^{3}x\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{r})}G_{Et}(q^{2})\rho_{t}(\mathbf{x}),

(49)

\displaystyle\omega_{ct}(\mathbf{r})=\int d^{3}x\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{r})}F_{2t}(q^{2})\omega_{t}(\mathbf{x}).

(50)

In Eq. (
49
),
\rho_{t}(\mathbf{r})
is the point proton or neutron density.
We use the non-relativistic form for the spin-orbit density,

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(\mathbf{r})=-\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{t}\hbar^{2}}{2(Mc)^{2}}\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{t}(\mathbf{r}),

(51)

where
Mc^{2}=939
MeV is the averaged nucleon mass,
and
\mu^{\prime}_{t}
is the anomalous magnetic moment,
related to the nucleon magnetic moments
\mu_{\mathrm{n}}=-1.913
and
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}=2.793
as
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mu_{\mathrm{n}}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{p}}=\mu_{\mathrm{p}}-1
.
The Sachs and Pauli form factors are chosen to be

\displaystyle G_{E\mathrm{n}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+r_{+}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1+r_{-}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}},

(52)

\displaystyle G_{E\mathrm{p}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+r_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}},

(53)

\displaystyle F_{2\mathrm{n}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{E\mathrm{p}}(q^{2})-G_{E\mathrm{n}}(q^{2})/\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{n}}}{1+q^{2}/4M^{2}},

(54)

\displaystyle F_{2\mathrm{p}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{E\mathrm{p}}(q^{2})}{1+q^{2}/4M^{2}},

(55)

and
r_{\mathrm{p}}=0.84
fm
[
33
]
,
r_{\pm}^{2}=(0.830)^{2}\mp 0.058
fm
2

[
34
]
are used.

The charge radius is computed from the charge density distribution,

\displaystyle\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\rm ch}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\rho_{\rm ch}(\mathbf{r})

(56)

\displaystyle=\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\rm p}+r_{\rm p}^{2}+\frac{N}{Z}(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2})+\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle_{c},

(57)

where

\displaystyle\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\rm p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\rho_{\rm p}(\mathbf{r}),

(58)

and the relativistic correction
\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle_{c}
is,

\displaystyle\delta\langle r^{2}\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\hbar^{2}}{4(Mc)^{2}}+\big(1+\frac{1}{2\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{p}}}\big)\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{n}}(\mathbf{r}).

(59)

Though not shown here, the integral of
r^{2}\omega_{t}(\mathbf{r})
can be expressed by the expectation value of
\bm{\ell}\cdot\hat{\bm{\sigma}}

(
\bm{\ell}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}
).

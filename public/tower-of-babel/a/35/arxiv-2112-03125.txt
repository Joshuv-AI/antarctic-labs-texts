An arbitrary-order fully discrete Stokes complex on general polyhedral meshes.
Marien-Lorenzo Hanot

Abstract
In this paper we present an arbitrary-order fully discrete Stokes complex on general polyhedral meshes. We enriche the fully discrete de Rham complex with the addition of a full gradient operator defined on vector fields and fitting into the complex. We show a complete set of results on the novelties of this complex: exactness properties, uniform Poincaré inequalities and primal and adjoint consistency. The Stokes complex is especially well suited for problem involving Jacobian, divergence and curl, like the Stokes problem or magnetohydrodynamic systems. The framework developed here eases the design and analysis of scheme for such problems. Schemes built that way are nonconforming and benefit from the exactness of the complex. We illustrate with the design and study of a scheme to solve the Stokes equations and validate the convergence rates with various numerical tests. Keywords: Discrete Stokes complex, Discrete de Rham complex, compatible discretization, polyhedral methods, arbitrary order MSC2010 classification: 65N30, 65N99, 76D07

The development of discrete counterparts of this smoother complex is much more complicated.

[

Although such constructions exist (for example
[
5
]
)

Recurrent problems can be a large minimal degree and thus numerous unknowns

The subject is very active with many recent advances:
[
11

12
]
.
Another issue of these constructions is that they are frequently constrained to conformal simplicial meshes,
which is limiting for some geometries as well as for the possibility of refinement or agglomeration.
A construction of the Stokes complex in virtual finite elements on general meshes has also been recently developed (see
[
13
]
).

Our construction works on general polyhedral meshes and for arbitrary polynomial degrees.
The discrete spaces consist of polynomial spaces on the elements of all geometric dimensions: cells, faces, edges and vertices.
Compared to the virtual finite element method, the basis functions are explicitly known but do not live in a subspace of continuous functions.
The discrete differential operators are therefore necessarily different from the continuous operators.
They are constructed according to integration by parts formulae and in a sense converge with the discrete spaces to the continuous operators
(see the consistency results of Section
5
).
A discretization of the de Rham complex (
1.1
) has been developed in detail by D. A. Di Pietro and J. Droniou
[
14
]
.
One can find in the introduction a very complete comparison of the different methods leading to discrete de Rham complex on polytopal meshes.
Our paper is a continuation of
[
14
]
:
Our construction is based upon it, and we add the necessary basis functions required for the increased smoothness of the Stokes complex.
We define and analyze in detail the Jacobian operator while checking its compatibility with the complex.

More precisely we show the exactness of the complex, the existence of uniform Poincaré inequalities and many consistency results
as well as a discrete version of the right inverse for the divergence for the discrete norm
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}

Finally, we apply this to the Stokes equations: we show well-posedness, give an error estimate and find an optimal convergence rate of order
\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1})
,

being the size of the mesh and
k\geq 0
the chosen polynomial degree.
We also explore other choices of boundary conditions and validate numerically every result.

We define the discrete spaces and operators (interpolators, differential operators and norms) in Section
3

In Section
4

In Section

The Stokes equations are defined in Section

3.3.4
Divergence.

Finally, we define the discrete divergence operator, for all

by:

D_{T}^{k}:=\Tr\TGRAD_{T}^{k+1}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T).

As in the continuous case the divergence is the trace of the gradient,
but we can also define it by a formula mimicking the integration by parts.
By Remark
17
,
\forall\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}\in\underline{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\TGRAD,T}^{k}
,
D_{T}^{k}
is such that
\forall q_{T}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)

\displaystyle\int_{T}D_{T}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}q_{T}=

\displaystyle\int_{T}\Tr(\TGRAD_{T}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T})q_{T}

(3.49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{T}\TGRAD_{T}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}\tdot(q_{T}\ID)

\displaystyle-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{w}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}},T}\cdot\GRAD q_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}(\gamma_{\TGRAD,F}^{k+2}\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F})q_{T}

\displaystyle-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{w}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}},T}\cdot\GRAD q_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\omega_{TF}\int_{F}w_{F}q_{T}.

We get the same definition as the one of the de Rham complex of

3.4

-product.

We build scalar products on the discrete spaces.

Since we will not need their definitions on

and

to study the Jacobian operator,
we will not write them down explicitly.
They are quite similar to the

but require the introduction of potential reconstruction operators

First we define them locally for all

For all

\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{F}}h_{E}^{2}\int_{E}(P_{\TGRAD,T}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{v}_{E})\cdot(P_{\TGRAD,T}^{k+1}\underline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{w}_{E}).

5
Consistency results.

The last things we need to show in order to efficiently use this complex are consistency results.
First we show primal consistency results, controlling the error made when we use the interpolators.
Then we show some Poincare type results useful to show stability, including a discrete counterpart to the right inverse for the divergence Lemma
34
.
Finally we show adjoint consistency results, which control the error made when we perform a discrete integration by parts.

\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\in T
is the point given by (

We identify the coordinate with three variables
x
,
y
and
z

\mathcal{P}^{k}[X,Y](T)=\{P\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T),\;\text{deg}_{Z}(P)=0\}

Lemma 42
.

Keeping the notations of (
A.1
),
the subset
\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}}^{c,k}(T)=\{W\in(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{R}}^{c,k}(T)^{\intercal})^{3}\,:\,\Tr{W}=0\}

is characterized by:

\begin{matrix}P_{1}=yzC^{1}+y\gamma-z\beta\\
P_{2}=xzC^{2}-x\gamma+z\lambda\\
P_{3}=xyC^{3}+x\beta-y\lambda\end{matrix},\quad\begin{matrix}\lambda\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[Y,Z],\beta\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[X,Z],\gamma\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[X,Y],\\
C^{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-3}[X,Y,Z],C^{1}+C^{2}+C^{3}=0.\end{matrix}

Proof.

The proof relies on repeated use of Euclidean division.
When we append
x
(resp.
y
,
z
) as subscript we mean that the polynomial does not depend on
x
(resp.
y
,
z
).
We write:

\displaystyle P_{2}=

\displaystyle xQ_{2}+R^{2}_{x},

\displaystyle P_{3}=

\displaystyle xQ_{3}+R^{3}_{x}.

Then the condition
\Tr{W}=0
becomes
xP_{1}+yP_{2}+zP_{3}=x(P1+yQ_{2}+zQ_{3})+yR^{2}_{x}+zR^{3}_{x}=0
.
Looking at the degree in
X
, we must have

P_{1}+yQ_{2}+zQ_{3}=0
and
yR^{2}_{x}+zR^{3}_{x}=0
.
Hence there exists
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[Y,Z]
such that

R^{2}_{x}=z\lambda,\;R^{3}_{x}=-y\lambda.

On the other hand, writing
P_{1}=yA^{1}+zA^{1}_{y}+A^{1}_{y,z}
, we have

yA^{1}+zA^{1}_{y}+A^{1}_{y,z}+yQ_{2}+zQ_{3}=0
.
Looking at the degree in
Y
and
Z
we must have
A^{1}_{y,z}=0
.
Let us write
Q_{3}=yB^{3}+B^{3}_{y}
, we have

y(A^{1}+Q_{2}+zB^{3})+z(A^{1}_{y}+B^{3}_{y})=0
.
The degree in
Y
shows that
A^{1}_{y}+B^{3}_{y}=0
.
Thus there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[X,Z]
such that

A^{1}_{y}=-\beta,\;B^{3}_{y}=\beta.

Let
A^{1}=zC^{1}+C^{1}_{z}
and
Q_{2}=zC^{2}+C^{2}_{z}
.
We are left with
A^{1}+Q_{2}+zB^{3}=z(C^{1}+C^{2}+B^{3})+C^{1}_{z}+C^{2}_{z}=0
.
Once again the degree in

shows that
C^{1}_{z}+C^{2}_{z}=0
and
C^{1}+C^{2}+B^{3}=0
,
so there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}^{k-2}[X,Y]
such that

C^{1}_{z}=\gamma,\;C^{2}_{z}=-\gamma.

We conclude by writing
B^{3}:=C^{3}
and substituting back each term in the development of
P_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 47
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{x}_{T}
defined as in (
2.5
), if
B=B(\boldsymbol{x}_{T},h_{B})\subset T
with
h_{B}\gtrsim h_{T}
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)
then
\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\approx\left\|Q\right\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
h_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
such that
T\subset B(\boldsymbol{x}_{T},h_{o})
and
h_{o}\lesssim h_{T}
(
h_{o}
exists by the regularity assumption on the mesh sequence
\mathcal{M}_{h}
).
Let
\boldsymbol{v}
by any vector such that
\left\|\boldsymbol{v}\right\|=1
, then

\forall\alpha>0
such that
\boldsymbol{x}_{T}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v}\in T
,

Q(\boldsymbol{x}_{T}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\partial_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{i}Q(\boldsymbol{x}_{T})}{i!}\alpha^{i},

so

\left|Q(\boldsymbol{x}_{T}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v})\right|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\left\|\partial_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{i}Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}}{i!}\alpha^{i}.

By the discrete Poincare inequality Lemma
2
we have
\forall i
,
\left\|\partial_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{i}Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim h_{B}^{-i}\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}
.
Moreover
\boldsymbol{x}_{T}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v}\in T
so
\alpha<h_{o}
and

\left|Q(\boldsymbol{x}_{T}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v})\right|\lesssim\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}h_{o}^{i}h_{B}^{-i}
.
Since
h_{o}\lesssim h_{B}
we can conclude that

\left\|Q\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left\|Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}
.
∎

Any

Lemma 48
.

Let
P=\prod_{i\leq\left|\mathcal{F}_{T}\right|}P_{i}
,
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in T
such that
B_{0}=B(\boldsymbol{x}_{0},h_{T}/2)\subset T
and

B=B(\boldsymbol{x}_{0},h_{T}/4)

then
\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in B}P(\boldsymbol{x})>\left(\frac{h_{T}}{4}\right)^{\left|\mathcal{F}_{T}\right|}

\left\|P\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)} \lesssim h_{T}^{\left|\mathcal{F}_{T}\right|}
.

Proof.

For any
i\leq\left|\mathcal{F}_{T}\right|

P_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})

\boldsymbol{x}

For any
\boldsymbol{x}\in B

\boldsymbol{x}

of any face since

We obtain the lower bound taking the product over all faces.
Conversely, using the mesh regularity we can find

.

Lemma 50
.

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and

there is

\pi_{\mathcal{P},T}^{k}P=q

\left\|P\right\|\approx\left\|q\right\|

Proof.

Let
P=\Pi Q
with
\Pi
given by Lemma
48
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)
.
The application

\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)\ni Q\rightarrow\left(\lambda\rightarrow\int_{T}P\lambda\right)\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{\prime}

(A.4)

is linear and between two spaces of same dimension, thus it is enough to check that it is injective.
Let
Q\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)
such that
\forall\lambda\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)

\int_{T}\Pi Q\lambda=0
;
in particular
\int_{T}\Pi Q^{2}=0
.
However since

\sqrt{\Pi}Q\equiv 0
, so

A.4
) is injective.

P\in\mathcal{P}^{k+\left|\mathcal{F}_{T}\right|}(\overline{T})

such that
P_{|\partial T}=0
and
\pi_{\mathcal{P},T}^{k}P=q
.
Let us show that
P
also satisfies the norm equivalence:
In particular we must have

\begin{gathered}\int_{T}(P-q)Q=0,\\

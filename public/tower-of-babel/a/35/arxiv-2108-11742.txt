Separability of Klein-Gordon Equation on Near Horizon Extremal Myers-Perry Black Hole Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hovhannes Demirchian, Saeedeh Sadeghian

Abstract
We investigate the separability of Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon of d-dimensional rotating Myers-Perry black hole in two limits : 1) generic extremal case and 2) extremal vanishing horizon case. In the first case , there is a relation between the mass and rotation parameters so that black hole temperature vanishes. In the latter case, one of the rotation parameters is restricted to zero on top of the extremality condition. We show that the Klein-Gordon equation is separable in both cases. Also, we solved the radial part of that equation and discuss its behaviour in small and large r regions.



I
Introduction

The four dimensional Kerr black hole has been widely studied from various aspects. From the geometric point of view, Carter showed that it has integrable geodesics
Carter:1968rr
.
The extension to higher dimensions is known as Myers-Perry black hole. In d-dimensions, it is described by one mass parameter and
N(=[\frac{d}{2}])
number of rotation parameters. It has integrable geodesic equations just like its 4-dimensional equivalent. Other extensions such as including the cosmological constant and NUT charge does not change this behavior
Frolov:2006pe
;
Krtous2007
;
PhysRevLett.98.061102
. The integrability of the Klein-Gordon and Maxwell field equations have been also studied on the d-dimensional Kerr-(A)dS-NUT space-time
Frolov:2006pe
;
Krtous2007
;
Cariglia2011
;
Kolar:2015hea
;
Lunin2017
;
Krtous2018
.

One can construct another solution to Einstein equations in the Near Horizon extremal limit
PhysRevD.60.104030
;
Kunduri2007
;
Kunduri2009
;
Kunduri2013
of MP (NHEMP) black hole
Figueras2008
(see
Hakobyan:2017qee
;
Demirchian2018
;
Galajinsky2013
;
Galajinsky2013a
;
Demirchian:2017uvo
;
Kolar:2017vjl
for recent studies). The extremal limit of the parameters describes a black hole with the biggest allowed angular momentum for a given BH mass. When one of the rotation parameters of the BH vanishes, we arrive to yet another solution of Einstein equations referred to as extremal vanishing horizon (EVH) geometry
DeBoer2012
;
Sheikh-Jabbari2011
. Although the generic extremal limit of Myers-Perry black hole exists in both even and odd dimensions, the special EVH limit exists only in odd-dimensions.

A set of the Killing vectors of NHEMP obey the structural relation of
SL
(2,R) algebra corresponding to
AdS_{2}
subspace. It has been demonstrated (e.g.
Hakobyan2009
;
Hakobyan2010
;
Galajinsky2011
;
Galajinsky2008
) that the Casimir element of this
SO
(2, 1) algebra gives rise to a reduced Hamiltonian system called spherical or angular mechanics, which contains all the necessary information about the near horizon geometry. In other words, a massive particle moving in the near horizon geometry of an extremal rotating black hole possesses a dynamical conformal symmetry, i.e. defines “conformal mechanics”
Galajinsky2013a
;
Galajinsky2013
;
Hakobyan:2017qee
;
Demirchian:2017uvo
;
Galajinsky2008
;
Galajinsky2010
;
Galajinsky2011
;
Galajinsky2012
;
Galajinsky2011a
;
Bellucci2011
;
Saghatelian2012
;
Galajinsky2016
, whose Casimir element can be viewed as a reduced Hamiltonian, which contains all the necessary information about the whole system.

An eye-catching difference between NHEMP and its vanishing horizon limit is that the latter has a larger isometry group
SADEGHIAN2015222
;
SADEGHIAN2016488
;
PhysRevD.96.044004
: it includes two copies of SL(2,R) corresponding to the
AdS_{3}
subspace instead of one copy of SL(2,R) for the
AdS_{2}
factor of the metric. This symmetry enhancement does not add to number of independent constants of motion though
Demirchian2018
.

The near horizon geometry of Myers-Perry black holes contains integrable and superintegrable systems like Rosochatius and Pöschl-Teller which are interestingly related to Klein-Gordon equation through a geometrization procedure
Evnin2017
. The angular part of near horizon limit of fully isotropic Myers-Perry black hole is a superintegrable mechanics called Rosochatius system. This is a direct generalization of the Higgs oscillator. Separation of variables in Rosochatius system results into a recursive family of one-dimensional Pöschl-Teller system. The quantum equivalents of Higgs oscillator, Rosochatius and Pöschl-Teller systems can be associated with a Klein-Gordon equation on a static spacetime.

In this work, we study the separability of Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon geometries of odd-dimensional Myers-Perry black hole. First, we take the near horizon geometry in the generic extremal case and use the elliptical coordinates in which the geodesic equation is separable. The SL(2,R)
\times
U(1)
N
isometry group of the background metric helps us to simply separate the part of Klein-Gorodon equation related to
AdS_{2}
subspace and azimuthal angles. We observe that the Klein-Gordon equation is separable in the elliptical coordinates. We also find the solution to the radial part of the Klein-Gordon equation and discuss some general properties of the solution. Then, we study the near horizon EVH space-time and show the separability of the Klein-Gordon equation on that metric.

II
Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon extremal geometry

The near horizon extremal metric of odd dimensional
(d=2N+1)
Myers-Perry black hole (NHEMP) in Gaussian null coordinates was given in
Figueras2008
and can be written in the Boyer-Lindquist coordinates as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{F_{H}}{b(r_{H})}\left(-r^{2}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(r_{H}^{2}+a_{i}^{2})d\mu_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\gamma_{ij}D\varphi^{i}D\varphi^{j}\,,

where,

D\varphi^{i}\equiv d\varphi^{i}+\frac{B^{i}}{b}rd\tau,\qquad B^{i}=\frac{2}{r_{H}^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}}{m_{i}^{2}}\,.

(2)

The metric functions are

\begin{gathered}F_{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mu_{i}^{2}}{m_{i}},\qquad b(r_{H})=\frac{4}{r_{H}^{2}}\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\,,\\
\gamma_{ij}=(r_{H}^{2}+a_{i}^{2})\mu_{i}^{2}\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{F_{H}}a_{i}\mu_{i}^{2}a_{j}\mu_{j}^{2}\,,\end{gathered}

(3)

Here,
m_{i}
’s are some constant parameters related to the horizon radius (
r_{H}
) and
N
number of rotation parameters (
a_{i}
’s) corresponding to azimuthal coordinates
\varphi^{i}
defined by,

m_{i}=\frac{r_{H}^{2}+a_{i}^{2}}{r_{H}^{2}}\geq 1\,,

(4)

and
\mu_{i}
are the latitudinal coordinates which satisfy the following relation

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}=1\,.

(5)

The location of the horizon
r_{H}
is determined by the largest positive solution of

\Pi(r_{H})=m\,r_{H}^{2},

(6)

where
\Pi(r)\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{N}(r^{2}+a_{i}^{2})
and
m
is a constant related to the mass of the Myers-Perry black hole.

The extremal limit is given by

\Pi^{\prime}(r_{H})=2m\,r_{H}\,.

(7)

By combining these two relations one finds that
m_{i}
’s are restricted by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}}=1\,.

(8)

By substituting
\mu_{i}=x_{i}/\sqrt{m_{i}}
for
i=1\ ...\ N
, the new form of NHEMP metric in an arbitrary odd dimensions becomes

\displaystyle\frac{ds^{2}}{r^{2}_{H}}=

\displaystyle A(x)\left(-r^{2}d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}dx_{i}dx_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}D\varphi^{i}D\varphi^{j}\,,

where

\displaystyle D\varphi^{i}\equiv d\varphi^{i}+k^{i}rd\tau,\qquad k^{i}=\frac{B^{i}}{b}\,,

\displaystyle A(x)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}x^{2}_{i}/m^{2}_{i}}{r_{H}^{2}\ b},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{x^{2}_{i}}{m_{i}}=1\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{\sum_{l}^{N}x_{l}^{2}/m^{2}_{l}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}x_{i}}{m_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}-1}x_{j}}{m_{j}}\,.

(10)

The behavior of a massive scalar field
\Phi
in the gravitational background is
governed by the Klein-Gordon equation:

\square\Phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}{\partial_{\alpha}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\Phi)}=M^{2}\Phi\,,

(11)

where
M
is the mass of the scalar field and
g
is the determinant of the metric. Separation of variables of Klein-Gordon equation in the background of odd dimensional NHEMP can be carried out in elliptic coordinates
\lambda_{a}
which is related to
x_{i}
with the following relation

x^{2}_{i}=(m_{i}-\lambda_{i})\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\frac{m_{i}-\lambda_{j}}{m_{i}-m_{j}}\,,

(12)

where
\lambda_{N}<m_{N}<\ldots<\lambda_{2}<m_{2}<\lambda_{1}<m_{1}
. To resolve the last relation in (
II
) one should choose
\lambda_{N}=0
. In these coordinates
\lambda_{a}

\sum_{i=1}^{N}dx_{i}^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}h_{a}(\lambda)d\lambda_{a}^{2}\,,

(13)

with

h_{a}=-\frac{\lambda_{a}\prod_{b\neq a}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})}{4\prod_{i=1}^{N}(m_{i}-\lambda_{a})}\,.

(14)

the NHEMP metric (
9
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{ds^{2}}{r^{2}_{H}}=

\displaystyle A(\lambda)\left(-r^{2}d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}h_{a}(\lambda)d\lambda_{a}^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}x_{i}(\lambda)x_{j}(\lambda)D\varphi^{i}D\varphi^{j},

here

A(\lambda)=\frac{1}{b}\frac{\prod\limits_{a=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{a}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}}.

(16)

To analysis the Klein-Gordon equation (
11
), we need to find the determinant and the inverse metric of (
15
).
The inverse of NHEMP metric is

\displaystyle r^{2}_{H}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{A(\lambda)r^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}r\ k^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{i}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}}{A(\lambda)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{2}+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}h^{a}(\lambda)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{a}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\tilde{\gamma}^{ij}\frac{1}{x_{i}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{x_{j}(\lambda)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{j}}\,,

where
h^{a}(\lambda)
and
\tilde{\gamma}^{ij}
are the inverses of
h_{a}(\lambda)
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
respectively

h^{a}(\lambda)=h_{a}^{-1}(\lambda),\qquad\tilde{\gamma}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}-x_{i}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}}{m_{i}}x_{j}\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}-1}}{m_{j}}.

(17)

The determinant of metric (
15
) has the following form

-\det{g}=A(\lambda)r^{2}\cdot\frac{A(\lambda)}{r^{2}}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}h_{a}\cdot det\left(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}\right).

(18)

Taking into account the definition of
h_{a}
(
13
) and the relation (
12
),
\prod_{a}h_{a}
simplifies to

\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}h_{a}=c\cdot\frac{A(\lambda)\left(\prod\limits_{a<b}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})\right)^{2}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}}

,

\displaystyle c=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}b\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\right)^{2}}{4^{N-1}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}(m_{i}-m_{j})}.

(19)

Using matrix determinant lemma (
69
), the determinant of
\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}
will take a simple form

\det(\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}x_{i}x_{j})=\frac{1}{b\,A(\lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}

(20)

Finally inserting (
II
) and (
20
) in (
18
), we find

-\det{g}=c^{\prime}\left(\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{a}\right)^{2}\left(\prod\limits_{a<b}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})\right)^{2}\,,

(21)

with

\qquad c^{\prime}=4^{1-N}\left(b\prod\limits_{i<j}^{N}(m_{i}-m_{j})\right)^{-2}

(22)

Equipped with (
II
) and (
21
), we can rewrite the Klein-Gordon equation (
11
), and noting two important relations

\partial_{\lambda_{c}}\sqrt{-\det{g}}=\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq c\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{c}-\lambda_{b}}\right)\sqrt{-\det{g}}\,,

(23)

\partial_{\lambda_{a}}h^{a}=\left(-\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}}-\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}-\lambda_{a}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{b}}\right)h^{a}\,,

(24)

we rewrite the Klein-Gordon equation on NHEMP metric

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A(\lambda)}\left(-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}rk^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{i}}\right]^{2}\varPhi+r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\varPhi+2r\partial_{r}\varPhi\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}h^{a}\partial^{2}_{\lambda_{a}}\varPhi-\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{h^{a}}{m_{i}-\lambda_{a}}\partial_{\lambda_{a}}\varPhi

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\partial^{2}_{\varphi_{i}}\varPhi-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}}{m_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}-1}}{m_{j}}\partial_{\varphi_{i}}\partial_{\varphi_{j}}\varPhi=M^{2}\varPhi\,.

To separate the variables in this equation, we apply the following ansatz

\varPhi=R_{r}(r)\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}R_{\lambda_{a}}(\lambda_{a})\cdot e^{i\omega\tau}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{N}e^{iL_{b}\varphi_{b}}\,,

(26)

where
\omega
and
L_{i}
are arbitrary constants. In this form
\lambda_{a}
and
r

\varPhi
will be

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\varPhi=\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}\varPhi,\qquad\partial^{2}_{r}\varPhi=\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}\varPhi\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda_{a}}\varPhi=\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}\varPhi\,,\qquad\partial^{2}_{\lambda_{a}}\varPhi=\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}\varPhi\,,

(27)

and the Klein-Gordon equation transforms into

\displaystyle\left[\vphantom{\sum_{i,j}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}}{m_{i}}}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A(\lambda)}\left(r^{2}\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}+2r\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,(\omega-r\sum_{i=1}^{N}k^{i}L_{i})^{2}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}h^{a}\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}-\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{h^{a}}{m_{i}-\lambda_{a}}\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{L^{2}_{i}}{x_{i}^{2}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{m_{i}-1}}{m_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{j}-1}}{m_{j}}L_{i}L_{j}\right]\varPhi=M^{2}\varPhi\,.

One can see that the first term in the above equation only depends on
r
. The separability requirement forces it to be a constant,

r^{2}\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}+2r\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{r}}{R_{r}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,\left(\omega-r\sum_{i=1}^{N}k^{i}L_{i}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{C}_{2}\,.

(30)

To write the radial equation (
30
) as a known differential equation, we change the radial variable

to

by

z=\frac{2\,i\,\omega}{r}\,,

(31)

which brings the equation (
30
) into the familiar form of Whittaker’s equation

\frac{d^{2}R_{r}}{dz^{2}}+\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{K}{z}+\frac{(1/4-\mu^{2})}{z^{2}}\right)\,R_{r}=0\,,

(32)

with

K=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}k^{j}L_{j}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{2}=\frac{1}{4}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}k^{i}L_{i}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{C}_{2}\,.

(33)

The general solutions to the equation (
32
) are Whittaker’s functions :
\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}(z)
and
\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}(z)
. These are related the confluent hypergeometric functions.It is interesting to study the behaviour of these functions in small and large
z
region. (we keep
K,\mu
parameter fixed and generic.)
For the small

region, which is related to
r\to\infty
region in the space-time, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}(z)\sim z^{\mu+1/2}\,,\quad\text{as $z\to 0$}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}(z)\sim\alpha_{1}\,z^{\mu+1/2}+\alpha_{2}\,z^{-\mu+1/2}\,,\text{\footnotesize{ if $Re(\mu)<\frac{1}{2}$}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}(z)\sim\,z^{-\mu+1/2}\text{\footnotesize{ if $Re(\mu)\geq\frac{1}{2}$}}

(34)

here,
\alpha_{1,2}
are some constants related to
\mu,K
. The asymptotic behaviour (
z\to\infty
) of
\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}(z)
and
\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}(z)
which is corresponding to
r\to 0
region of space-time is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}(z)\sim z^{-K}e^{z/2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}(z)\sim z^{K}e^{-z/2}\,,\quad\text{as $z\to\infty$}

(35)

Depending on the values of
\mu
and
K
in (

), the behaviour of the solution is different :
\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}
is blowing up in the

(

r\to\infty
),
\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}
is vanishing if
Re(\mu)<1/2
which is guaranteed by e.g.
\mathcal{C}_{2}<0
.

The rest of the variables can be separated after applying the relations (
66
), (
67
) and (
68
) to the Klein-Gordon equation which will be transformed to

\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{P_{\lambda_{a}}(\lambda_{a})-M^{2}(-\lambda_{a})^{N-2}}{\prod_{b=1;a\neq b}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})}=0\,,

(36)

where
P_{\lambda_{a}}(\lambda_{a})
are defined by

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptP_{\lambda_{a}}(\lambda_{a})\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\lambda_{a}}\left(\frac{R^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}-\frac{R^{{}^{\prime}}_{\lambda_{a}}}{R_{\lambda_{a}}}\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{1}{m_{i}-\lambda_{a}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(m_{j}-\lambda_{a})

(37)

\displaystyle+\frac{b}{\lambda_{a}}\mathcal{C}_{2}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}+(-1)^{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{g_{\varphi_{i}}}{m_{i}-\lambda_{a}}

\displaystyle+g_{0}(-\lambda_{a})^{N-2}\,.

here

and

g_{\varphi_{i}}\equiv\frac{k^{2}_{\varphi_{i}}}{m_{i}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

where

III
Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon EVH geometry

The metric of near horizon EVH Myers-Perry in the elliptical coordinates parameterized with
(\tau,\rho,\psi,\lambda_{a},\varphi_{i})
, in
d=2N+1
dimensions is of the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle F(\lambda)\left(-\rho^{2}d\tau^{2}+\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}d\psi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\hat{h}_{a}d\lambda_{a}^{2}+\sum_{a,b=1}^{N-1}\hat{\gamma}_{ab}\,\hat{x}_{a}(\lambda)\hat{x}_{b}(\lambda)d\varphi_{a}d\varphi_{b}\,,

where the metric functions are

\displaystyle F(\lambda)=\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{\lambda_{a}}{m_{a}},\qquad\hat{h}_{a}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{{\prod_{{b\neq a}}^{N-1}}\left(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}\right)}{\prod_{c=1}^{N-1}\left(m_{c}-\lambda_{a}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\hat{\gamma}_{ab}=\delta_{ab}+\frac{1}{F(\lambda)}\frac{\hat{x}_{a}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{m_{a}}}\frac{\hat{x}_{b}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{m_{b}}}\,,

(41)

with

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{a}^{2}(\lambda)=\frac{\prod\limits_{b=1}^{N-1}(m_{a}-\lambda_{b})}{\prod\limits_{l\neq a}^{N-1}(m_{a}-m_{l})}\,,\qquad\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{m_{a}}=1.

(42)

(We start this section in elliptical coordinates to avoid writing multiple metrics. For more details, we refer the reader to
DeBoer2012
;
Hakobyan:2017qee
;
Demirchian2018
.)

The inverse of metric (
III
) is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(\lambda)}\left(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau})^{2}+\rho^{2}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho})^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi})^{2}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}{\hat{h}}^{a}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{a}})^{2}+\sum_{a,b=1}^{N-1}\frac{\hat{\gamma}^{ab}}{\hat{x}_{a}\hat{x}_{b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{a}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi^{b}}\,,

where

\hat{\gamma}^{ab}=\delta_{ab}-\frac{\hat{x}_{a}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{m_{a}}}\frac{\hat{x}_{b}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{m_{b}}},\qquad\hat{h}^{a}=\left(\hat{h}_{a}\right)^{-1}\,.

(44)

To study the Klein-Gordon equation on this metric,

\square\Phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}{\partial_{\alpha}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\Phi)}=M^{2}\Phi\,,

(45)

we need to compute the determinant of the metric,

-\det{g}=\rho^{2}F_{0}(\lambda)^{3}\left(\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\hat{h}_{a}\right)\det{(\hat{\gamma}_{ab}\hat{x}_{a}\hat{x}_{b})}\,,

(46)

It is easy to show that

\prod_{l=1}^{N-1}\hat{h}_{l}=\hat{c}\cdot\frac{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\left(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}\right)}{\prod\limits_{a=1}^{N-1}\hat{x}_{a}^{2}},\quad\hat{c}=4^{1-N}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\left(m_{a}-m_{b}\right)^{-1}\,,

(47)

Using matrix determinant lemma (
69
), we have

\det{(\hat{\gamma}_{ab}\hat{x}_{a}\hat{x}_{b})}=\frac{1}{F(\lambda)}\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\hat{x}_{a}^{2}\,.

(48)

Finally, the determinant of metric (
III
) simplifies to

-\det{g}=c^{\prime\prime}\,\rho^{2}\,\left(\prod_{l=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{l}\right)^{2}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
b<a\end{subarray}}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})\right)^{2}\,,

(49)

with

c^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{4^{N-1}}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{N-1}{m_{l}}\right)^{-2}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
b<a\end{subarray}}^{N-1}(m_{b}-m_{a})\right)^{-2}\,.

(50)

The key relations for the separability of the Klein-Gordon equation are as follows

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda_{a}}\sqrt{-\det{g}}=\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}}\right)\sqrt{-\det{g}}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda_{a}}\hat{h}^{a}=\left(-\sum_{b=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{m_{b}-\lambda_{a}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}}\right)\hat{h}^{a}\,.

The Klein-Gordon equation becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(\lambda)}\left(-\frac{\partial_{\tau}^{2}\varPhi-\partial_{\psi}^{2}\varPhi}{\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}^{2}\varPhi+3\rho\partial_{\rho}\varPhi\right)

into the Klein-Gordon equation (

(55)

IV
Discussions

We studied the separability of Klein-Gordon equation on two near horizon geometries in d-dimensions : generic extremal and extremal vanishing horizon cases. Since the latter case exists only in odd dimensions, we take
d=2n+1
in both cases. We do not expect that the separability of Klein-Gordon equations changes for the first case if we take even-dimensions. This expectation roots from the fact that the separability of geodesic equation has been shown for both even and odd dimensions
Demirchian2018
and the Klein-gordon and geodesic equation are related in the semi-classical limit : if we write the solution to the Klein-Gordon equation
\Phi
as
\Phi=\mathcal{N}\,exp\left(\frac{iS}{\alpha}\right)
, we get the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for
S
in the so-called semi-classical limit,
\alpha\to 0
.
(see
Sergyeyev:2007gf
for an explicit example.)

It is worth mentioning that since the extremal/EVH limit does not commute with near horizon limit, we
could not
get the Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon EVH geometry by taking the EVH limit on NHEMP metric. Therefore, we studied the Klein-Gordon equation on near horizon EVH geometry, independently.

One of the interesting problems to which we will come back in future is to study the cases when some or all of the rotation parameters are equal.

Regarding the solution to the radial equations, we discussed the asymptotic behaviour for the generic values of the parameters. There are some special limits in the parameter space which can lead to different asymptotic behaviour (like
\mu=0
of
\mathcal{M}_{K,\mu}
or
\mathcal{W}_{K,\mu}
). These special limits are known for Whittaker and Bessel functions. We are interested in the interpretation of their consequences in near horizon geometries.

Our next step is to study the other probes on these near horizon geometries such as Dirac, Maxwell and p-form fields and show the separability of their field equations.

Appendix A
Useful Identities

The following identity holds between
N-1
independent variables
\lambda_{a}
and any parameter
\kappa

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
{b\neq a}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}(\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{b})}\frac{(\lambda_{a})^{\alpha}}{\kappa-\lambda_{a}}=\frac{(\kappa)^{\alpha}}{\prod\limits_{a=1}^{N-1}(\kappa-\lambda_{a})}-\delta_{\alpha,N-1}\,,

(65)

where
\alpha=\{0,...,N-1\}
. For
\alpha=0
, this equation reduces to

\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{a=1}^{N-1}(\lambda_{a}-\kappa)}=\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{b=1;a\neq b}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})}\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}-\kappa}

(66)

and for an additional condition of
\kappa=0
we get

\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{N-1}}=\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\prod_{b=1;b\neq a}^{N-1}(\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a})}\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}}

(67)

On the other hand, setting
\kappa=0
in (
65
) results into

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda^{\beta}_{i}}{\prod_{j=1;i\neq j}^{N}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})}=\delta_{\beta,N-1}\quad{\rm for}\quad 0\leq\beta\leq N-1.

(68)

Another relation that we use in this paper is the so called matrix determinant lemma which states

\det(\mathrm{I}+\bm{xy}^{T})=1+\bm{y}^{T}\bm{x}

(69)

where
\mathrm{I}
is a unit matrix and
\bm{xy}^{T}
is the outer product of two vectors
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
.

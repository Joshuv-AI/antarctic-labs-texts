1.
Introduction

Let
f(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be a cubic form with non-zero discriminant. For each integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

N_{f}(m)=:\#\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:f(x,y)=m\right\}.

It has been a topic of interest to study how large the size of
N_{f}(m)
can be.

In this direction, the first significant result is due to Chowla. In
1933
, Chowla
[
1
]
proved that if
f(x,y)=x^{3}-ky^{3}
, where
k
is a nonzero integer, then
there is a positive constant
c
such that the following inequality holds for infinitely many positive integers
m

N_{f}(m)>c\,\left(\log\log m\right).

In
1935
, Mahler
[
3
]
improved Chowla’s result by showing that for every cubic form
f(x,y)
with nonzero discriminant, there exists a constant
c_{f}>0
, which depends on
f
such that

(1)

N_{f}(m)>c_{f}\,\left(\log|m|\right)^{1/4}

holds for infinitely many integers
m
.

Invoking the theory of height functions, Silverman
[
6
]
extended the idea of Chowla and Mahler. This resulted in an improvement of the exponent to
1/3
in (
1
) and simplification of the calculations as well. Further in
2008
, Stewart
[
9
]
improved this exponent to
1/2
. Chowla, Mahler, Silverman, Stewart, all of their ideas connected this problem to the construction of elliptic curves of high rank, and it turns out that one can do much better if we restrict
f(x,y)
to be
x^{3}+y^{3}
.

More precisely, if
f(x,y)=x^{3}+y^{3},
using a suitable elliptic curve of rank
3
, Silverman proved that there is a positive constant
c_{0}
such that

N_{f}(m)>c_{0}\left(\log|m|\right)^{3/5}

holds for infinitely many integers
m
.

Moreover, in
[
9
]
Stewart mentions that the exponent can be improved to
11/13
by using an elliptic curve of rank
11
which was constructed by Elkies and Rogers
[
2
]
in
2004
.

It is to be noted that, in all the previous works of Chowla, Mahler, Silverman, and Stewart, the constant
c_{f}
was not explicit. In this short note, using methods employed by Silverman and exploiting the properties of canonical height function on elliptic curves, we explicitly capture the dependence of the implied constant on the cubic form
f(x,y)=x^{3}+y^{3}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f(x,y)=x^{3}+y^{3}
and
m_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be a non zero integer. Consider the curve
E
with homogeneous equation

E:f(x,y)=m_{0}\,z^{3}

with the point
O=[1,-1,0]
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Using
O
as origin, we give
E
the structure of an elliptic curve. Let

r=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})

be the rank of the Mordell-Weil group of
E/\mathbb{Q}
. Then for infinitely many integers
m
,

N_{f}(m)>\frac{1}{(9\times 2^{r+1}-20)^{r/r+2}(\hat{h}(\bar{P}))^{r/r+2}}(\log|m|)^{r/(r+2)},

where
\hat{h}(\bar{P})
denotes the canonical height of a specified point
\bar{P}
on
E^{\prime}:Y^{2}=X^{3}-432m_{0}^{2}
, defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, with the base point
[0,1,0]
at infinity.

As an immediate corollary, we have the following.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f(x,y)=x^{3}+y^{3}
. Then there exist infinitely many integers
m
, such that

N_{f}(m)>4.2\times 10^{-6}(\log|m|)^{11/13}

2.
Preliminaries

We state and develop the necessary tools to prove our main theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose there exist integers
x
,
y
,
z
and
m_{0}
, such that
x^{3}+y^{3}=m_{0}z^{3}
with
gcd(x,y,z)=1
,
gcd(12m_{0}z,x+y)=d
, then

|d|<3^{1/3}12m_{0}^{5/2}|z|^{1/2}
.

Proof.

Suppose

da=12m_{0}z
and
db=x+y
, where
gcd(a,b)=1
.

We want to show that

d^{2}|3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b.

Let
p
be a prime dividing
d
and
p^{k}||d
,
we show that,
p^{2k}
always divides
3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b
.

As
p^{k}|d\Rightarrow{p^{k}|12m_{0}z}
, there are two possible cases

p^{k}|12m_{0}
or
p|z
.

If
p^{k}|12m_{0}
, then we are done. So, we only need to consider the case when
p|z
.

Note that,

\displaystyle x^{3}+y^{3}

\displaystyle=m_{0}z^{3}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}(x+y)((x+y)^{2}-3xy)=12^{3}m_{0}^{3}z^{3}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b(d^{2}b^{2}-3xy)=d^{2}a^{3}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow d^{2}|3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}bxy.

Also,

p|d\Rightarrow p|x+y
.

On the other hand, we have
p|z
and
gcd(x,y,z)=1
, which implies that
p\nmid xy
.

As
d^{2}|3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}bxy
, the primes dividing
d
and not dividing
xy
, should be completely contained in the factorisation of
3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b
.

Hence,
p^{2k}|3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b
, for all prime
p
dividing
d

\Rightarrow d^{2}|3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}b
.

This implies

\displaystyle|d|^{2}\leq 3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}|b|

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|d|^{3}\leq 3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}|b||d|=3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2}|x+y|

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|d|^{6}\leq(3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2})^{2}(|x+y|)^{2}\leq(3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2})^{2}|x^{3}+y^{3}|=(3\times 12^{3}m_{0}^{2})^{2}m_{0}|z|^{3}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|d|<3^{1/3}12m_{0}^{5/2}|z|^{1/2}.

∎

Now, we discuss some essential properties of heights on elliptic curves.

Definition 2.1
.

The canonical height or Néron-Tate height denoted by
\hat{h}
on an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, is defined as the following

\hat{h}(P)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{{k\to\infty}}\left(4^{-k}h_{x}\left([2^{k}]P\right)\right)
,
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
,

where
h_{x}(P)=\log\left(H\left([x(P),1]\right)\right)
and
H
is the height on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

For our purpose, we will only need the height
H
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, which is given by the following

H(P)=\text{max}\{|p|,\,\,|q|\},

where
P=[p,q]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
is written in the reduced form such that
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\text{gcd}(p,q)=1
.

For a more general definition of height on projective space, see
[
7
]
, pp.
226
.

Next, we state a result due to Néron
[
5
]
and Tate
[
4
]
, which gives the required properties of canonical height.(See pp.
248-251

[
7
]
.)

Lemma 2.2

(
Néron, Tate
)
.

Let
\hat{h}
be the canonical height or Néron-Tate height on an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, then

(a)

(Parallelogram law) For all
P,Q\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
we have

\hat{h}(P+Q)+\hat{h}(P-Q)=2\hat{h}(P)+2\hat{h}(Q).

(b)

For all
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\hat{h}([m]P)=m^{2}\hat{h}(P).

(c)

The canonical height
\hat{h}
is a quadratic form on
E
, i.e.,
\hat{h}
is an even function, and the pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\times E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathbb{R},\quad\langle P,Q\rangle=\hat{h}(P+Q)-\hat{h}(P)-\hat{h}(Q),

is bilinear.

(d)

Let
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then
\hat{h}(P)\geq 0
, and
\hat{h}(P)=0
if and only if
P
is a torsion point.

(e)

\hat{h}=\frac{1}{2}h_{x}+O(1),

where the
O(1)
depends on
E
and
x
.

Note that, in
(e)
of Lemma
2.2
, the implied constant is not explicit. In 1990, Silverman proved a general result which explicitly determines this constant in terms of the
j
-invariant and the discriminant of the elliptic curve. We invoke a specific case of this result here.(See [
[
8
]
, pp.
726
.])

Lemma 2.3
.

Let,
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve given by the Weierstrass equation

y^{2}=x^{3}+B
, then for every
P\in E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})

-\frac{1}{6}h(B)-1.48\leq\hat{h}(P)-\frac{1}{2}h_{x}(P)\leq\frac{1}{6}h(B)+1.576

Once we obtain the estimate of
N_{f}(m)
in Theorem
1.1
, the explicit result in Corollary
1.1
follows from the existence of a specific elliptic curve of rank 11, which was constructed by Elkies and Rogers in
[
2
]
, as stated below.

Theorem 2.1

(
Elkies, Rogers
)
.

The elliptic curve given by the equation

x^{3}+y^{3}=m_{0}z^{3}
or the Weierstrass form
Y^{2}=X^{3}-432m_{0}^{2}
,

where
m_{0}=13293998056584952174157235
has the Mordell-Weil rank
11
.

Moreover,
\max\{h_{x}(P_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq 11\}=76.61
, where
P_{i}
varies over
11
independent points of the Mordell-Weil group.

For the construction of this elliptic curve and the list of 11 independent points; see pp. 192-193
[
2
]
. The rest follows by simple computation.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1

Consider the elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
given by

E:x^{3}+y^{3}=m_{0}z^{3}

with base point
[1,-1,0]
and Mordell-Weil rank
r
.

Observe that any non-torsion point
Q=[x(Q),y(Q),z(Q)]\in E(\mathbb{Q})
has
z(Q)\neq 0
because the only point in
E(\mathbb{Q})
with
z(Q)=0
is
Q=[1,-1,0]
, the identity element of the Mordell-Weil group.

As the rank of
E(\mathbb{Q})
is
r
, we can choose
r
independent points
P_{1},...,P_{r}
from the free part of the group
E(\mathbb{Q})
and for any point
Q\in E(\mathbb{Q})
, we write
Q=[x(Q),y(Q),z(Q)]
with
x(Q),y(Q),z(Q)\in\mathbb{Z}
and
gcd\left(x(Q),y(Q),z(Q)\right)=1
.

Now fix a large positive integer
N
. For each
n=(n_{1},...,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
with

1\leq n_{i}\leq N
, consider the sum

Q_{n}=n_{1}P_{1}+...+n_{r}P_{r}

which gives
N^{r}
distinct points in
E(\mathbb{Q})
. Now consider

m=m_{0}\prod_{n}z(Q_{n})^{3},

where the product runs over all
r-
tuples
(n_{1},...,n_{r})
. Note that,
m\neq 0
as
z(Q_{n})\neq 0
.

Hence for each
r
-tuple
n^{\prime}=(n_{1}^{\prime},...,n_{r}^{\prime})
, the equation
f(x,y)=x^{3}+y^{3}=m
has the following integral solution

(x,y)=\left(x(Q_{n^{\prime}})\prod_{n\neq n^{\prime}}z(Q_{n})^{3},y(Q_{n^{\prime}})\prod_{n\neq n^{\prime}}z(Q_{n})^{3}\right).

Therefore, we get

N_{f}(m)>N^{r}
.

We will now use the properties of height function to give an upper bound for
m
in terms of
N
and
r
. To do this explicitly, we first transform the elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
into its Weierstrass form and then proceed by using the explicit properties of the height function on that Weierstrass form.

Consider the following morphism which takes
E/\mathbb{Q}
to its Weierstrass form
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}

\Phi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}

defined as

\Phi([x,y,z])=\begin{cases}\left[12m_{0}\,\frac{z}{y+x},\,36m_{0}\,\frac{y-x}{y+x},1\right],&z\neq 0,\\
[0,1,0],&z=0,\end{cases}

where
E^{\prime}:Y^{2}=X^{3}-432\,m_{0}^{2}
denotes the Weierstrass form of
E:x^{3}+y^{3}=m_{0}z^{3}
.

Note that,
\Phi
induces a group homomorphism
\Phi:E(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
of the corresponding Mordell-Weil groups.

Let
Q_{n}=[x(Q_{n}),y(Q_{n}),z(Q_{n})]\in E(\mathbb{Q})
be as above. As
z(Q_{n})\neq 0
, we have

x\left(\Phi(Q_{n})\right)=12m_{0}\frac{z(Q_{n})}{y(Q_{n})+x(Q_{n})}.

For simplicity we write
x,y,z
for
x(Q_{n}),y(Q_{n})
and
z(Q_{n})
respectively.

Hence,

\displaystyle h_{x}\left(\Phi(Q_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(H\left([12m_{0}\frac{z}{y+x},1]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(H\left([12m_{0}z,x+y]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\text{max}\{|\frac{12m_{0}z}{d}|,|\frac{x+y}{d}|\}\right),

where
d=gcd(12m_{0}z,x+y)
and
\log\left(\text{max}\{|\frac{12m_{0}z}{d}|,|\frac{x+y}{d}|\}\right)\geq\log\left(\frac{|12m_{0}z|}{|d|}\right)
.

So,

h_{x}\left(\Phi(Q_{n})\right)+\log(|d|)\geq\log(12|m_{0}|)+\log(|z|).

Now, using Lemma
2.1

h_{x}\left(\Phi(Q_{n})\right)\geq b_{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\log(|z|),

where
b_{m_{0}}
is a constant depending on
m_{0}
. Further, using (e) of Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\log(|z|)\leq 4\hat{h}(\Phi(Q_{n}))+c_{m_{0}}
,

where
c_{m_{0}}
is another constant depending on
m_{0}
.

As
\Phi:E(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
is group homomorphism,

\hat{h}\left(\Phi(Q_{n})\right)=\hat{h}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\Phi(P_{i})\right)
.

Using (a), (b) and (d) of Lemma
2.2
, we deduce the following estimate for
\hat{h}
.

\displaystyle\hat{h}(\Phi(Q_{n}))\leq(3\times 2^{r-1}-2)\max\{\hat{h}(n_{i}\Phi(P_{i}))\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}

\displaystyle\leq(3\times 2^{r-1}-2)N^{2}\max\{\hat{h}(\Phi(P_{i}))\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}

For ease of notation, write
\hat{h}(\bar{P}):=\max\{\hat{h}(\Phi(P_{i}))\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}
, where
\bar{P}=\Phi(P_{i})
for some
i
.

Hence,

\displaystyle\log(|z(Q_{n})|)

\displaystyle\leq 4(3\times 2^{r-1}-2)N^{2}\hat{h}(\bar{P})+c_{m_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq(3\times 2^{r+1}-7)N^{2}\hat{h}(\bar{P}),

as we can choose a large
N
such that
c_{m_{0}}\leq N^{2}\hat{h}(\bar{P})
.

Now, we have a total of
N^{r}
points
Q_{n}
on
E(\mathbb{Q})
. Therefore for large
N
,

\log|m|=3\sum_{n}\log|z(Q_{n})|+\log(|m_{0}|)\leq(3^{2}\times 2^{r+1}-20)N^{r+2}\hat{h}(\bar{P}).

So, we conclude

N_{f}(m)>N^{r}\geq\frac{1}{(9\times 2^{r+1}-20)^{r/r+2}(\hat{h}(\bar{P}))^{r/r+2}}(\log|m|)^{r/(r+2)}

which proves Theorem
1.1
, because
N
can be chosen arbitrarily large, giving infinitely many choices for
m
.

The proof of the Corollary
1.1
follows by using the elliptic curve of Theorem
2.1
in Theorem
1.1
. Observe that, using Lemma
2.3
on the elliptic curve
Y^{2}=X^{3}-432m_{0}^{2}
with
m_{0}=13293998056584952174157235
, we have

\hat{h}(\bar{P})\leq 121.767/6+76.61/2+1.576=60.17.

Now, by putting
r=11
, the required estimate holds for infinitely many integers
m
and we conclude

N_{f}(m)>4.2\times 10^{-6}(\log|m|)^{11/13}.

4.
Concluding remarks

It will be interesting to know, if a similar explicit estimate can be obtained for other cubic forms as well. The key idea is to get a result similar to Lemma
2.1
for a general form, then the methods in this article are amenable to give an explicit estimate.

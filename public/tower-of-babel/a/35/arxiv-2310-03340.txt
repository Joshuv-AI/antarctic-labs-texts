Constant rank subspaces of alternating bilinear forms from Galois Theory Thanks: Thanks:
Ashish Gupta, Sugata Mandal

Abstract
. Let L / K L/K be a cyclic extension of degree n = 2 ​ m n=2m . It is known that the space Alt K ⁡ ( L ) \alt_{K}(L) of alternating K K -bilinear forms (skew-forms) on L L decomposes into a direct sum of K K -subspaces A σ i A^{\sigma^{i}} indexed by the elements of Gal ⁡ ( L / K ) = ⟨ σ ⟩ \gal(L/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle . It is also known that the components A σ i A^{\sigma^{i}} can have nice constant-rank properties. We enhance and enrich these constant-rank results and show that the component A σ A^{\sigma} often decomposes directly into a sum of constant rank subspaces, that is, subspaces all of whose non-zero skew-forms have a fixed rank r r . In particular, this is always true when − 1 ∉ L 2 -1\not\in L^{2} . As a result we deduce a decomposition of Alt K ⁡ ( L ) \alt_{K}(L) into subspaces of constant rank in several interesting situations. We also establish that a subspace of dimension n 2 \frac{n}{2} all of whose nonzero skew-forms are non-degenerate can always be found in A σ i A^{\sigma^{i}} where σ i \sigma^{i} has order divisible by 2 2 . 3.
Preliminary results

Our aim in this section is to establish certain facts which will be found useful in the subsequent sections and are also interesting in their own right.
Recall that if
F
is an intermediate subfield and
a\in L
then the
L/F
-norm

N_{L/F}(a)
of
a
is defined as
N_{L/F}(a)=\displaystyle\prod_{\theta\in\gal(L/F)}\theta(a)
.

Notation 1
.

Throughout this section
L/K
denotes a cyclic extension with Galois group
\gal(L/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. For the sake of convenience in what follows we shall denote the subfield
L^{\langle\sigma^{i}\rangle}
as
L_{i}
.

We begin by noting the following restatement of the degeneracy criterion Lemma
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
b\in L
. Then the skew-form
f_{b,\sigma}
is degenerate if and only if

(3.1)

N_{L/L_{2}}(\sigma(b)/b)=1,

that is,
f_{b,\sigma}
is degenerate if and only if

(3.2)

N_{L/L_{2}}(b)=b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{n-2}(b)\in K.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, the skew form
f_{b,\sigma}
is degenerate if and only if
\sigma(b)/b=\sigma^{2}(c)/c
for some
c\in L
. The first assertion is now clear in view of the Hilbert Theorem 90.
Moreover the condition
N_{L/L_{2}}(\sigma(b)/b)=1
is easily seen to be equivalent to the product
b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{n-2}(b)
being
\sigma
-invariant.

∎

Suppose that
\sigma^{i}
is not an involution. By Lemma
2.1
the skew-form
f_{b,\sigma^{i}}\in A^{i}\subseteq\alt_{K}(L)
is degenerate if and only if
\sigma^{i}(b)/b=\sigma^{2i}(c)/c
. As
\sigma^{2i}
is a generator for
\gal(L/L_{2i})
, in view of Hilbert Theorem 90,
f_{b,\sigma^{i}}
is degenerate if and only if
N_{L/L_{2i}}(\sigma^{i}(b)/b)=1
.
A glance at Proposition
3.1
above shows that this is precisely the condition for the skew-form
\displaystyle f^{\sim}_{b,\sigma^{i}}\in\alt_{L_{i}}(L)
defined by

f^{\sim}_{b,\sigma^{i}}=\tr^{L}_{L_{i}}(b(x\sigma(y)-\sigma(x)y)),\qquad\forall x,y\in L.

to be degenerate (we write
f^{\sim}_{b,\sigma^{i}}
instead of
f_{b,\sigma^{i}}
to emphasize the fact that we are now considering
L
as
L_{i}
-space).

Let us write

\displaystyle A^{\sim 1}:=\{\displaystyle f^{\sim}_{b,\sigma^{i}}\mid b\in L\}
.
In view of Remark
2.1
we then have a K-isomorphism
A^{i}\equiv L
via
f_{b,\sigma^{i}}\mapsto b
and an
L_{i}
-isomorphism
L\equiv A^{\sim 1}
via

b\mapsto f^{\sim}_{b,\sigma^{i}}
. The composition of these maps clearly yields a
K
-isomorphism

A^{i}\cong A^{\sim 1}
. The following is then clear.

Remark 3.1
.

With respect to the above isomorphism if an
L_{i}
-subspace
\mathcal{W}\leq A^{\sim 1}
has all its non-zero skew forms non-degenerate (or all its non-zero skew forms degenerate) then the same is true for the corresponding (K-) subspace in

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n=2^{\alpha}k
where
\alpha\geq 2
and
k
is odd. Suppose that
L
is a cyclic extension of a field
K
of degree
n
with Galois group
\gal(L/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then the following hold.

(i)

For
1\leq i\leq\alpha-1
the subspace
E_{i}:=\{b\in L:\sigma^{n/2^{i}}(b)=-b\}\leq L
has dimension
n/2^{i}
.

(ii)

Let
V_{1}:=\{b\in L:\sigma^{k}(b)=b\}
and
V_{2}:=\{b\in L:\sigma^{k}(b)=-b\}
. Then
\dim(V_{1})=\dim(V_{2})=k
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq i\leq\alpha-1
. As the order of the automorphism
\sigma^{n/2^{i}}
is
2^{i}
so the fixed field
L_{n/2^{i}}
of
\sigma^{n/2^{i}}
has dimension
n/2^{i}
over
K
. We can view
\sigma^{n/2^{i}}
as a
K
-linear map of
L
. By the Dedekind independence theorem the minimal polynomial of
\sigma^{n/2^{i}}
is
x^{2^{i}}-1
. Let
j_{i}\in L
be an eigenvector of
\sigma^{n/2^{i}}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
-1
. It is easily checked that the corresponding eigenspace is

. It follows that
\dim(E_{i})=n/2^{i}
. The proof of (ii) is similar.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n=2^{\alpha}k
where
\alpha\geq 2
and
k
is odd. Suppose that
L
is a cyclic extension of a field
K
of degree
n
with Galois group
\gal(L/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Then
\forall b_{i}\in E_{i}\setminus\{0\}

(3.3)

N_{L/L_{2}}(b_{i})=(-1)^{n/2^{2}}w_{i}^{2^{i}},

where
w_{i}:=b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i})
. Moreover,
f_{b_{i},\sigma}
is degenerate if and only if
\eta_{i}:=\sigma(w_{i})/w_{i}
is a

-th root of unity in

\sigma(\eta_{i})=-{\eta_{i}}^{-1}
. In particular,

is non-degenerate for all

Proof.

In view of the chain of inclusions

L\supset L_{n/2}\supset\cdots\supset L_{n/2^{i-1}}\supset E_{i},

we have for

\displaystyle N_{L/L_{2}}(b_{i})

\displaystyle=b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n-2}(b_{i})

\displaystyle=\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2-2}(b_{i})\right)\left(\sigma^{n/2}(b_{i})\sigma^{n/2+2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2+n/2-2}(b_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2-2}(b_{i})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/4-2}(b_{i})\right)^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \vdots

\displaystyle=\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i-1}-2}(b_{i})\right)^{2^{i-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i})\right)\left(\sigma^{n/2^{i}}(b_{i})\sigma^{n/2^{i}+2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}+n/{2^{i}}-2}(b_{i})\right)\right]^{2^{i-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i})\right)\left((-b_{i})(-\sigma^{2}(b_{i}))\cdots(-\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i}))\right)\right]^{2^{i-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[(-1)^{n/2^{i+1}}\left(b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i})\right)^{2}\right]^{2^{i-1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n/{2^{2}}}[b_{i}\sigma^{2}(b_{i})\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{i}-2}(b_{i})]^{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n/2^{2}}w_{i}^{2^{i}}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{N_{L/L_{2}}(\sigma(b_{i}))}{N_{L/L_{2}}(b_{i})}=\left(\frac{(\sigma((-1)^{n/{2^{2}}}w_{i}))}{(-1)^{n/{2^{2}}}w_{i}}\right)^{2^{i}}=\left(\frac{\sigma(w_{i})}{w_{i}}\right)^{2^{i}}=\eta_{i}^{2^{i}}.

Set
\eta_{i}:=\frac{\sigma(w_{i})}{w_{i}}
.
By Proposition
3.1
,
f_{b_{i},\sigma}
is degenerate if and only if
\eta_{i}
is a
2^{i}
-th root of unity
\eta_{i}
. Moreover,

-w_{i}=\sigma^{2}(w_{i})=\sigma(\eta_{i}w_{i})=\sigma(\eta_{i})\eta_{i}w_{i},

whence
\sigma(\eta_{i})\eta_{i}=-1
, that is,
\sigma(\eta_{i})=-\eta_{i}^{-1}
. The last assertion in the theorem is now clear.
∎

Case I:

. Then the field

has dimension

. Again by Dedekind’s independence theorem it follows that the minimal polynomial of

are

\displaystyle N_{L/L_{2}}(b)

\displaystyle=b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{n-2}(b)

\displaystyle=\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{n/2^{\alpha-1}-2}(b)\right)^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{2k-2}(b)\right)^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)\right)\left(\sigma^{k+1}(b)\cdots\sigma^{2k-2}(b)\right)\right]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)\right)\left(\sigma(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-2}(b)\right)\right]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=[b\sigma(b)\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=N_{L/L_{2}}(\sigma(b)).

\displaystyle=b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{n-2}(b)

\displaystyle=\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{2k-2}(b)\right)^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)\right)\left(\sigma^{k+1}(b)\cdots\sigma^{2k-2}(b)\right)\right]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(b\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)\right)\left((-\sigma(b))\cdots(-\sigma^{k-2}(b)\right)\right]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=[b\sigma(b)\sigma^{2}(b)\cdots\sigma^{k-1}(b)]^{2^{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=N_{L/L_{2}}(\sigma(b)).

Consequently

\forall b\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}

\alt_{K}(L)

]

[

]
respectively. In particular, it is unknown to the authors if there is a

of dimension four.

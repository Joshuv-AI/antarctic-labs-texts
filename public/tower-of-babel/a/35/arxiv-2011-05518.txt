Abstract
. The Busemann-Petty problem asks whether symmetric convex bodies in the Euclidean space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with smaller central hyperplane sections necessarily have smaller volume. The solution has been completed and the answer is affirmative if n ≤ 4 n\leq 4 and negative if n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . In this paper, we investigate the Busemann-Petty problem on entropy of log-concave functions: For even log-concave functions f f and g g with finite positive integrals in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , if the marginal ∫ ℝ n ∩ H f ⁡ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}f(x)dx of f f is smaller than the marginal ∫ ℝ n ∩ H g ⁡ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}g(x)dx of g g for every hyperplane H H passing through the origin, whether the entropy Ent ⁡ ( f ) {\rm Ent}(f) of f f is bigger than the entropy Ent ⁡ ( g ) {\rm Ent}(g) of g g ? The Busemann-Petty problem on entropy of log-concave functions includes the Busemann-Petty problem, hence, its answer is negative when n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . For 2 ≤ n ≤ 4 2\leq n\leq 4 we give a positive answer to the Busemann-Petty problem on entropy of log-concave functions.

A subset in the

Suppose that
K

L
are origin-symmetric convex bodies in the
n

\mathbb{R}^{n}

V_{n-1}(K\cap H)\leq V_{n-1}(L\cap H)

H

that

V(K)\leq V(L)?

The Busemann-Petty problem has a long and dramatic history. It is not so difficult
to see that the Busemann-Petty problem has a positive answer for

Intersection bodies introduced by Lutwak

In 1999 Zhang

A unified solution to

The main aim of this paper is to study the Busemann-Petty problem on
entropy
of log-concave functions, an important concept that is applied in physics, information theory,
computer science, convex geometry, differential geometry, probability theory, analysis and other applied mathematical fields.
A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is
log-concave
if for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<t<1
,

\displaystyle f((1-t)x+ty)\geq f^{1-t}(x)f^{t}(y).

(1.1)

A typical example of log-concave functions is the characteristic function
\mathcal{X}_{K}
of a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\mathcal{X}_{K}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\text{if}&x\in K,\\
0&\text{if }&x\not\in K.\end{array}\right.

The
total mass functional

J(f)
of an integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle J(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx.

(1.2)

If
f
is an integrable log-concave function with
J(f)>0
, the
entropy

{\rm Ent}(f)
of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm Ent}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx-J(f)\log J(f).

(1.3)

The entropy of log-concave functions provides multiple connections
between convex bodies and log-concave functions. In
[
5
]
, the authors provided a functional version of the affine isoperimetric inequality for log-concave functions which turned out to be an inverse form of logarithmic Sobolev inequality for entropy. Colesanti and Fragalà showed that the entropy is one part of the functional form of Minkowski first inequality
(see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 5.1]
). Inequalities on entropy were obtained in
[
13
]
by using a geometric inequality involving the
L_{p}
affine surface area.

In the present paper, we will study the Busemann-Petty problem for entropy of log-concave functions which asks:

Problem 1
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are even log-concave functions with finite positive integrals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}f(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}g(x)dx

for each hyperplane
H
passing through the origin.
Does it follow that

\displaystyle{\rm Ent}(f)\geq{\rm Ent}(g)?

At first glance, the solution to Problem
1
is more difficult than the traditional Busemann-Petty problem since
there is no any information in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Similar to the traditional Busemann-Petty problem (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
), the symmetric assumption on functions is necessary.

The work of Colesanti and Fragalà
[
17
, Theorem 5.1]
tells us that the entropy is an important part in the functional version of Minkowski’s first inequality and another part is the total mass functional. It is clear that the total mass functional is a more direct extension of volume. We consider
the following Busemann-Petty problem for the total mass functional of log-concave functions:

Problem 2
.

Suppose
f
,
g
are even log-concave functions with finite positive integrals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}f(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}g(x)dx

for every hyperplane
H
passing through the origin. Does it follow that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx?

When
f
and
g
are, respectively, the characteristic functions of origin-symmetric convex bodies
K
and
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
Problem
2
deduces the Busemann-Petty problem.
Hence Problem
2
has a negative answer for
n\geq 5
.
Note that scalar multiplication does not affect Problem
2
and the function
t\log t
is increasing when
t>e^{-1}
, the crucial step to solve Problem
1
is the following problem:

Problem 1
′
.

If
f,g
are even log-concave functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite positive integrals such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}f(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}g(x)dx

for every hyperplane
H
passing through the origin.
Does it follow that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\log fdx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\log gdx?

We realize that the dual Brunn-Minkowski theory is essential in solving the Busemann-Petty problem. This inspired us that the dual Brunn-Minkowski theory for functions will be needed in solving Problems
1
and
2
. Therefore, we extend the harmonic combination of star bodies to functions in Section
3
.

For any two functions
\varphi,\psi
(not necessary convex) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
harmonic combination
,
\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, of
\varphi
and
\psi
is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)=\varphi+t\psi,\quad t>0.

(1.4)

In particular, if
\varphi,\psi
are both lower semi-continuous convex functions, then

\displaystyle\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)=(\varphi^{*}\square(\psi^{*}t))^{*},

(1.5)

where
\square
is the infimal convolution and
\varphi^{*}
is the Legendre transform (see details in Section
2
). In the sense of (
1.5
),
\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)
is an extension of the harmonic combination of star bodies.

For any two functions
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, let
f=e^{-\varphi}
and

g=e^{-\psi}
. If
f,\ g
are integrable,
then the
functional dual mixed volume
of
f
and
g
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)=-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}t\psi(x)})-J(f)}{t}.

(1.6)

We prove that the dual mixed volume of star bodies is contained in
\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)
.
Moreover, we provide an integral formula of
\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)
and establish a functional version of the dual Minkowski inequality:

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)\geq\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)+J(f)\log\frac{J(f)}{J(g)},

(1.7)

with equality if and only if
f=ag
with
a>0
.

Note that
\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)
does not agree with
J(f)
, but it satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\log fdx.

(1.8)

Hence, Problem
1^{\prime}
can be rewritten as

Problem 3
.

If
f,\ g
are even log-concave functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite positive integrals such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}f(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap H}g(x)dx,

for every hyperplane
H
passing through the origin.
Does it follow that

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)\leq\widehat{\delta J}(g,g)?

It is not hard to see that Problem
2
and Problem
3
include the Busemann-Petty problem.
In Section
6
we will show that the Busemann-Petty problem can be
deduced from Problem
1
.
Therefore Problem
1
, Problem
2
and Problem
3
can be called the
functional Busemann-Petty problems
,
and they have negative answers for
n\geq 5
.
Hence we just need to consider the functional Busemann-Petty problems in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
2\leq n\leq 4
.
Obviously, if Problem
2
and Problem
3
have positive answers for
2\leq n\leq 4
then Problem
1
will
have a positive answer for
2\leq n\leq 4
.

For our purposes, the functional version of intersection bodies (or the intersection function) is needed. The
intersection function

\mathcal{I}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, of a non-negative integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)=\exp\left\{-\|x\|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\bar{x}^{\perp}}f(z)dz\right)^{-1}\right\},

(1.9)

when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathcal{I}f(0)=1
. Here
\|x\|
denotes the Euclidean normal of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
. If there exists a non-negative integrable function
f
such that
g(x)=\mathcal{I}f(x)
then
g
is an
intersection function
.
Let
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|
, and
\omega_{n}=\pi^{\tfrac{n}{2}}/\Gamma(1+\tfrac{n}{2})
denote the volume of the unit ball, where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. We obtain the following basic inequality for intersection functions:

The functional Busemann intersection inequality.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative continuous integrable function
with
f(0)>0
. Then

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{I}f)\leq\Gamma(n+1)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-2}}\|f\|_{\infty}J(f)^{n-1},

(1.10)

with equality for
n=2
if and only if
cf
is the characteristic function of an origin-symmetric star body, and for
n\geq 3
if and only if
cf
is the characteristic function of an origin-symmetric ellipsoid (where
c>0
).

The intersection function not only contains the intersection body (of star bodies) but also plays a crucial role in solving the functional Busemann-Petty problems.

Characteristic theorem.

Problem
2
and Problem
3
have affirmative answers in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if every even integrable log-concave function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an intersection function.

For a given continuous function (not necessary log-concave), Ball
[
6
]
introduced a body associated with it. Using Ball’s body, a further connection between the intersection function and the intersection body will be given in Lemma
5.4
. Together with the solution to the Busemann-Petty problem assure that Problem
2
and Problem
3
have affirmative answers when
2\leq n\leq 4
. Therefore, we conclude that:

Theorem 1
.

Functional Busemann-Petty problems (i.e., Problem
1
, Problem
2
and Problem
3
) have affirmative answers when
2\leq n\leq 4
and negative answers when
n\geq 5
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

Preliminaries

2.1.
Harmonic combination

Let
S^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|=1\}
denote the unit sphere in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
V(K)
denote the
n
-dimensional volume of convex body (i.e., a compact,
convex subset with nonempty interior)
K
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
denote the set of convex bodies containing the origin in their interiors. We write
\text{GL}(n)
for the group of general
linear transformations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
T\in\text{GL}(n)
write
T^{t}
for the transpose of
T
,
T^{-t}
for the inverse of the
transpose (contragradient) of
T
.

For a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its support function,
h(K,\cdot)=h_{K}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is defined by

\displaystyle h(K,x)=\max\{x\cdot y:y\in K\}

(2.1)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
x\cdot y
is the usual inner product of
x
and
y
. The
polar body
,
K^{\circ}
, of
K
is defined by

\displaystyle K^{\circ}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot y\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad y\in K\right\}.

(2.2)

The radial function,
\rho_{K}(\cdot)=\rho(K,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, of a compact, star shaped (about the origin)
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined, for
x\neq 0
, by

\displaystyle\rho(K,x)=\max\{t\geq 0:tx\in K\}.

(2.3)

If
\rho_{K}
is positive and continuous, call
K
a
star body
(about the origin). Let
\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
denote the set of star bodies (about the origin) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Two star bodies
K
and
L
are said to dilates (of one another) if
\frac{\rho_{K}(u)}{\rho_{L}(u)}
is independent of
u\in S^{n-1}
.
The radial function is positively homogeneous of degree
-1
, that is,

\displaystyle\rho_{K}(tx)=t^{-1}\rho_{K}(x),\quad t>0.

(2.4)

For star bodies
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
and real numbers
s,t>0
and
p\geq 1
, the harmonic
p
-combination
s\diamond K\widehat{+}_{p}t\diamond L
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho(s\diamond K\widehat{+}_{p}t\diamond L,\cdot)^{-p}=s\rho(K,\cdot)^{-p}+t\rho(L,\cdot)^{-p}.

(2.5)

In particular,

\displaystyle s\diamond K=s^{-\frac{1}{p}}K.

When
K,L\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
, the harmonic
p
-combination
K\widehat{+}_{p}L
can rewrite as

\displaystyle K\widehat{+}_{p}L=(K^{\circ}+_{p}L^{\circ})^{\circ},

(2.6)

where
K+_{p}L
denotes the
L_{p}
Minkowski sum of
K,L\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}
with support function

\displaystyle h_{K+_{p}L}^{p}=h_{K}^{p}+h_{L}^{p}.

The
L_{p}
dual mixed volume of
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L)=-\frac{p}{n}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{V(K\widehat{+}_{p}t\diamond L)-V(K)}{t}.

(2.7)

The definition above and the polar coordinate formula for volume give the following integral representation of the
L_{p}
dual mixed volume:

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_{K}^{n+p}(u)\rho_{L}^{-p}(u)du.

(2.8)

The basic inequality for the dual mixed volumes
\widetilde{V}_{-p}
is that for star bodies
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L)^{n}\geq V(K)^{n+p}V(L)^{-p},

(2.9)

with equality if and only if
K
and
L
are dilates.

For a star body
K\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
, we denote its
Minkowski functional
by

\displaystyle\|x\|_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\rho_{K}^{-1}(x)&\text{if}&x\neq 0,\\
0&\ \text{if }&x=0.\end{array}\right.

2.2.
Log-concave functions

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Then
\varphi
is convex if for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]

\varphi((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\leq(1-\lambda)\varphi(x)+\lambda\varphi(y).

Let

\text{dom}(\varphi)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{R}\}.

We say that
\varphi
is
proper
if
\text{dom}(\varphi)\neq\emptyset
.
The
Legendre transform
of
\varphi
is the convex function defined by

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{x\cdot y-\varphi(x)\right\}\quad\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.11)

For convex functions
\varphi,\psi
, the
infimal convolution
is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi\Box\psi(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\varphi(x-y)+\psi(y)\}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.12)

and the
right scalar multiplication
is defined by,

\displaystyle(\varphi t)(x)=t\varphi\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),\quad\text{for}\quad t>0.

(2.13)

From the definition of log-concave function (
1.1
), we known that every log-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
has the form

\displaystyle f=e^{-\varphi},

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is convex. A log-concave function is degenerate if it vanishes almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A non-degenerate, log-concave function
e^{-\varphi}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if
[
18
]

\lim_{\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty.

Note that all log-concave functions are differentiable almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\Big|\quad\varphi\text{ is proper, convex, }\lim_{\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\Big|\quad f=e^{-\varphi},\varphi\in\mathcal{L}\right\}.

The subset of
\mathcal{A}
that contains the even log-concave functions is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{e}
.

2.3.
The Rodan transform

Let
f
be a compactly supported continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Sigma_{n}
be the space of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The
Radon transform

\mathcal{R}f
of
f
is defined by
[
28
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}f(\xi)=\int_{\Sigma_{n}}f(x)dx,\quad\xi\in\Sigma_{n}.

Here the integral is taken with respect to the natural hypersurface measure
dx
. Observe that any element of
\Sigma_{n}
is characterized as the solution locus of an equation

\displaystyle y\cdot u=r,

where
u\in S^{n-1}
is a unit vector and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus the
n
-dimensional Radon transform
be rewritten as a function on
S^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
via

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}f(u,r)=\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u\cdot y=r\}}f(y)dy.

(2.14)

The Rodan transform of
f
in the direction of
x
at
r
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}f(x,r)=\|x\|^{-1}\mathcal{R}f\left(\frac{x}{\|x\|},\frac{r}{\|x\|}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

(2.15)

For a continuous function
f
on
S^{n-1}
, the
spherical Rodan transform

Rf
of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm R}f(u)=\int_{S^{n-1}\cap u^{\bot}}f(v)dv,\quad\quad u\in S^{n-1},

where
u^{\bot}
is the
(n-1)-
dimensional subspace orthogonal to the unit vector
u
.

3.
The harmonic combination for functions

Motivated by (
2.6
), connection between harmonic
p
-combination and
L_{p}
Minkowski sum, the harmonic combination of convex functions

\varphi,\psi
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)=(\varphi^{*}\square(\psi^{*}t))^{*},\quad t>0.

(3.1)

If
\varphi,\psi
are lower semi-continuous convex functions then (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle(\varphi^{*}\square(\psi^{*}t))^{*}=\varphi+t\psi,\quad t>0.

We extend the harmonic combination of convex functions to non-convex functions as following:

Definition 3.1
.

For any two functions
\varphi,\psi
(not necessary convex) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the harmonic combination
\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)
of
\varphi
and
\psi
is defined by

\displaystyle\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)(x)=\varphi(x)+t\psi(x),

(3.2)

for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We extend the dual mixed volume in the dual Brunn-Minkowski theory to its functional version.

Definition 3.2
.

For
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, let
f=e^{-\varphi},g=e^{-\psi}
. If
f,g
are integrable, then the functional dual mixed volume of
f
and
g
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)=-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}t\psi(x)})-J(f)}{t}.

(3.3)

Definition
3.2
includes the
L_{p}
dual mixed volume of star bodies.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
and
p\geq 1
. If
\varphi(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{p},\psi(x)=\|x\|_{L}^{p}
and
f=e^{-\varphi}
and
g=e^{-\psi}
, then

\displaystyle J(f)=\Gamma(1+\tfrac{n}{p})V(K),

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)=\frac{n}{p}\Gamma(1+\tfrac{n}{p})\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L).

Proof.

If
\varphi(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{p}
and
\psi(x)=\|x\|_{L}^{p}
, a direct calculation shows that

J(f)=\Gamma(1+\tfrac{n}{p})V(K),

and

\varphi(x)+t\psi(x)=\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)+t\rho_{L}^{-p}(x)=\rho_{K\hat{+}_{p}t\diamond L}^{-p}(x)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. The definition of
L_{p}
dual mixed volume (
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-\rho_{K\hat{+}_{p}t\diamond L}^{-p}(x)})-J(e^{-\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)})}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{p}\Gamma(1+\tfrac{n}{p})\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L).

∎

Next, we give an integral representation for
\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be any two functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f=e^{-\varphi},g=e^{-\psi}
are integrable. If
J(f)>0
, then
\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)\in[cJ(f),+\infty]
with
c=\inf\psi
, and

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)e^{-\varphi(x)}dx.

(3.4)

Proof.

Set

f_{t}=e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)}=e^{-(\varphi+t\psi)},\quad c=\inf\psi,\quad\tilde{f}_{t}=e^{-(\varphi+t(\psi-c))}.

Since
\psi-c\geq 0
, then
\tilde{f}_{t}
is pointwise decreasing with respect to
t
, and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have
f(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\tilde{f}_{t}(x)
.
By monotone convergence theorem, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}J(\tilde{f}_{t})=J(f)
. Since
f_{t}=e^{-ct}\tilde{f}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{J(f_{t})-J(f)}{t}=\frac{e^{-tc}[J(\tilde{f}_{t})-J(f)]}{t}+\frac{(e^{-tc}-1)J(f)}{t}.

(3.5)

Let us consider separately the following two cases:

\displaystyle\exists\quad t_{0}>0\quad\text{such that}\quad J(\tilde{f}_{t_{0}})=J(f),

and

\displaystyle J(\tilde{f}_{t})<J(f)\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

In the first case, since
J(\tilde{f}_{t})
is a decreasing function of
t
, necessarily it holds

J(\tilde{f}_{t})=J(f)
for every
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. Hence the first addendum in the right hand side
of (
3.5
) is zero, and we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(f_{t})-J(f)}{t}=-cJ(f).

For the second case, we write

\displaystyle\frac{J(\tilde{f}_{t})-J(f)}{t}=\frac{\log J(\tilde{f}_{t})-\log J(f)}{t}\cdot\frac{J(\tilde{f}_{t})-J(f)}{\log J(\tilde{f}_{t})-\log J(f)}.

By Hölder inequality
\log J(\tilde{f}_{t})
is a decreasing convex function of
t
. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\log J(\tilde{f}_{t})-\log J(f)}{t}\in[-\infty,0],

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(\tilde{f}_{t})-J(f)}{\log J(\tilde{f}_{t})-\log J(f)}=J(f)>0.

(3.7)

From (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(\tilde{f}_{t})-J(f)}{t}\in[-\infty,0].

(3.8)

Together with (
3.5
) and (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(f_{t})-J(f)}{t}\in[-\infty,-cJ(f)].

Therefore, the definition of
\widehat{\delta J}(\cdot,\cdot)
concludes that
\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)\in[cJ(f),+\infty]
.

To prove (
3.4
), firstly we assume

\psi\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{J(e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)(x)})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}\left(\frac{e^{-t\psi(x)}-1}{t}\right)dx.

Since
\psi\geq 0
, by the monotone convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\psi)(x)})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)e^{-\varphi(x)}dx.

In the general case when the assumption
\psi\geq 0
is removed, we consider the function
\tilde{f}_{t}
. Let
\tilde{\psi}=\psi-c
. From (
3.5
) and the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-tc}J(e^{-\varphi\widehat{\square}(t\tilde{\psi})})-e^{-tc}J(f)}{t}-\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{(e^{-tc}-1)J(f)}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{\psi}(x)e^{-\varphi(x)}dx+cJ(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)e^{-\varphi(x)}dx.

We complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

We are going to present a functional form of the dual Minkowski
inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be any two functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f=e^{-\varphi},g=e^{-\psi}
are integrable. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-[(1-t)\varphi]\widehat{\square}[t\psi]})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}=\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)-\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)

(3.9)

Proof.

For
t\in(0,1)
, let
a(t)=\frac{t}{1-t}
.
Set
\varphi_{a(t)}=\varphi\widehat{\square}[a(t)\psi]
, and

\displaystyle b_{t}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(1-s)\varphi_{a(t)}(x)}dx,\quad s\in(0,1).

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}b_{t}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(1-s)\varphi_{a(t)}(x)}\varphi_{a(t)}(x)dx.

First, we assume that
\psi\geq 0
. Then for every fixed
t\in(0,1)
, by Lagrange theorem there exists a
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-[(1-t)\varphi]\widehat{\square}[t\psi](x)})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-(1-t)[\varphi(x)+a(t)\psi(x)]}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-[\varphi(x)+a(t)\psi(x)]}dx\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-[\varphi(x)+a(t)\psi(x)]}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}dx\right]

\displaystyle\quad=b_{t}^{\prime}(s_{0})+\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-[\varphi(x)+a(t)\psi(x)]}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}dx\right].

Since
\psi\geq 0
, so that
\varphi(x)+a(t)\psi(x)
is increasing with respect to
t
.
Then, from the monotone convergence theorem, the fact
s_{0}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
a(t)\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-[(1-t)\varphi]\widehat{\square}[t\psi](x)})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}\varphi(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varphi(x)}\psi(x)dx.

The desired equality follows from the integral representation (
3.4
).

It remaind to remove the assumption
\psi\geq 0
. In the general case, we set

c=\inf\psi,\quad\tilde{\psi}=\psi-c.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(e^{-[(1-t)\varphi]\widehat{\square}[t\psi](x)})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}e^{-tc}\frac{J(e^{-[(1-t)\varphi(x)+t(\psi(x)-c)]})-J(e^{-\varphi})}{t}+\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}J(e^{-\varphi})\frac{e^{-tc}-1}{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))e^{-\varphi(x)}dx.

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following theorem gives a functional form of the dual Minkowski inequality.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be any two functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f=e^{-\varphi},g=e^{-\psi}
are integrable. If
J(f)>0
and
J(g)>0
, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)\geq\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)+J(f)\log\frac{J(f)}{J(g)},

(3.10)

with equality if and only if
f=ag
with
a>0
.

Proof.

By Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle J(e^{-(1-t)\varphi-t\psi})\leq J(e^{-\varphi})^{(1-t)}J(e^{-\psi})^{t}\quad\text{for}\ t\in(0,1),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\log J(e^{-(1-t)\varphi-t\psi})\leq(1-t)\log J(e^{-\varphi})+t\log J(e^{-\psi}).

This means that
\Upsilon(t)=\log J(e^{-(1-t)\varphi-t\psi})
is a convex function on
[0,1]
, hence

\displaystyle\Upsilon(t)\leq\Upsilon(0)+t[\Upsilon(1)-\Upsilon(0)],

(3.11)

for
t\in[0,1]
. As a consequence, the derivative of the function
\Upsilon
at
t=0
satisfies

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{\prime}(0)\leq\Upsilon(1)-\Upsilon(0).

(3.12)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)-\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)}{J(f)}\leq\log J(g)-\log J(f).

Finally, we assume that
f=ag
for some
a>0
. Then
(
3.10
) holds with equality
sign
by (
3.4
). Conversely, assume that (
3.10
) holds with equality sign.
By inspection of the above proof one sees immediately that inequality in (
3.11
) also holds,
and hence the equality must hold in inequality (
3.12
). This entails that the Hölder inequality holds as an equality, and therefore there exists a
a>0
such that
f=ag
.
∎

For
t>0
, note that

\log\frac{1}{t}\geq 1-t,

with equality if and only if
t=1
. Therefore, by (
3.10
) we have:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be any two functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f=e^{-\varphi},g=e^{-\psi}
are integrable. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,g)\geq\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)+J(f)-J(g),

(3.13)

with equality if and only if
f=g
.

The functional form of the dual Minkowski inequality (
3.10
) includes the following geometric inequality.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
and
p\geq 1
. If
\varphi(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{p},\psi(x)=\|x\|_{L}^{p}
, then (
3.10
) implies

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{-p}(K,L)\geq V(K)+V(K)\log\left(\frac{V(K)}{V(L)}\right)^{\frac{p}{n}},

(3.14)

with equality if and only if there exists a constant
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)=\rho_{L}^{-p}(x)+t
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)e^{-\varphi(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)e^{-\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}r^{n+p-1}\rho_{K}^{-p}(u)e^{-r^{p}\rho_{K}^{-p}(u)}drdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}r^{\frac{n}{p}}\rho_{K}^{n}(u)e^{-t}dtdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{n}{p}\Gamma(\tfrac{n}{p}+1)V(K).

The inequality (
3.14
) follows from Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
. The equality condition of Theorem
3.2
implies that
e^{-\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)}=ae^{-\rho_{L}^{-p}(x)}
with
a>0
, that is,
\rho_{K}^{-p}(x)=\rho_{L}^{-p}(x)-\log a
.
∎

4.
Intersection bodies for functions

4.1.
Marginals

In this subsection, we will introduce marginals of a function. More detailed information can be found in the book
[
10
]
.

Denote by
G_{n,k}
, the Grassmann manifold of
k
-dimensional linear subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an integrable function.
Let
1\leq k<n
be an integer and let
F\in G_{n,k}
.
The
marginal

\pi_{F}(f):F\rightarrow[0,\infty)
of
f
with respect to
F
is defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{F}(f)(x)=\int_{x+F^{\bot}}f(y)dy,

(4.1)

where
F^{\bot}
denotes the orthocomplement space of
F
.

We need the following properties of marginals.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
10
, Proposition 5.1.11]
).

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an integrable function and let
1\leq k<n
and
F\in G_{n,k}
.

(1)

If
f
is log-concave, then
\pi_{F}(f)
is log-concave.

(2)

We have
\int_{F}\pi_{F}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx
.

(3)

If
f
is even then the same holds true for
\pi_{F}(f)
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\bar{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
, we define the
parallel section function

t\mapsto A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
of integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)=\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=t\}}f(z)dz.

(4.2)

If
F=r\bar{x}
(which belongs to
G_{n,1}
),
r\in\mathbb{R}
, then
F^{\bot}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\cdot\bar{x}=0\}
and

\displaystyle r_{0}\bar{x}+F^{\bot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{r_{0}\bar{x}+y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\cdot\bar{x}=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=r_{0}\},

where
r_{0}\bar{x}\in F
. Hence,

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)=\pi_{F}(f)(t).

(4.3)

Let
K\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
and
u\in S^{n-1}
. The
parallel section function

t\mapsto A_{K,u}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
of
K
is defined by

\displaystyle A_{K,u}(t)=V_{n-1}(K\cap(u^{\perp}+tu)).

Indeed,
A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
can be called the
functional version of parallel section function
,
since it inherits almost all properties of the parallel section function of a star body.

A convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
k
-smooth for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, if its boundary is a
C^{k}
-smooth hypersurface of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the set of
k
-times
continuously differentiable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{e}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
k=0,1,2,\cdots
. Then for all

\bar{x}=\tfrac{x}{\|x\|}\in S^{n-1}
(
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
), functions
A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
are uniformly
k
-times continuous differentiable in some neighborhood of zero.

Proof.

By layer-cake representation and Fubini’s theorem yield

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=t\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}_{[f]_{s}}(z)dsdz

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\{z\in[f]_{s}\cap\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\cdot\bar{x}=t\}\}}dzds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A_{[f]_{s},\bar{x}}(t)ds,

where

[f]_{s}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geq s\}

and

A_{[f]_{s},\bar{x}}(t)=V_{n-1}([f]_{s}\cap\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\cdot\bar{x}=t\}).

Since
f\in\mathcal{A}_{e}\cap C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\partial[f]_{s}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)=s\}

is a
C^{k}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with non-zero normal
vector
\nabla f
and
f
is integrable. Therefore,
[f]_{s}
is a
k
-smooth
origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for each
s>0
. By
Koldobsky’s
[
33
, Lemma 2.4]
, we proved that
A_{[f]_{s},\bar{x}}(t)
(uniformly with respect to
\bar{x}
) are
k

continuous differentiable in some neighborhood of zero. By (
4.4
) we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
. Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, let

\bar{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
and
A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
be defined in
(
4.2
). Then we have:

(1)

The function
A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
is log-concave on its support.

(2)

We have
\int_{\mathbb{R}}A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)dt=J(f)
.

(3)

If
f
is even, then, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)\leq A_{f,\bar{x}}(0).

(4)

If
f\in\mathcal{A}_{e}\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
A_{f,\bar{x}}^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Note that (1) and (2) follow from Lemma
4.1
and
(
4.2
). (3) comes from that a log-concave even
function has maximum at zero. If, in addition,
f
is
2
-smooth,
then, as we showed in Lemma
4.2
,
the function
A_{f,\bar{x}}(t)
is twice differentiable in a
neighborhood of zero. We proved (4).
∎

4.2.
Intersection functions

Let
L\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
. We recall that the intersection body,
{\rm I}L
, of star body
L
is defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{{\rm I}L}(u)=V_{n-1}(L\cap u^{\perp}),\ u\in S^{n-1},

where
u^{\perp}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional subspace orthogonal to the unit vector
u
. The radial function of
{\rm I}L
equals the spherical Radon transform of
\frac{1}{n-1}\rho_{L}^{n-1}
, that is

\displaystyle\rho_{{\rm I}L}(u)={\rm R}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\rho_{L}^{n-1}\right)(u).

A slightly more general notion was defined in
[
27
]
, as follows. An origin-symmetric star body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be an intersection body if there exists a finite non-negative Borel measure
\mu
on
S^{n-1}
so that the radial function
\rho_{K}
of
K
equals the sphere Radon transform of
\mu
.

Inspired by the definition of intersection bodies, we define the intersection function by using marginals.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an integrable function. The intersection function,
\mathcal{I}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)=e^{-\|x\|A_{f,\bar{x}}(0)^{-1}},

(4.5)

when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
with
\bar{x}=\frac{x}{\|x\|}
, and
\mathcal{I}f(0)=1
. In addition, we say that
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
g(0)>0
is an intersection function if there exists a non-negative integrable function
f
such that
g(x)=\mathcal{I}f(x)
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a compactly supported continuous function, then the definition of Rodan transform (
2.15
) yields

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=0\}}f(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x\|\mathcal{R}f(x,0).

Hence, the intersection function can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)=e^{-\mathcal{R}f(x,0)^{-1}}.

(4.6)

The next lemma shows that the intersection function includes the intersection body.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
. If
f(x)=e^{-c\|x\|_{K}}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
c>0
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)=e^{-c^{(n-1)}\Gamma(n)^{-1}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K}}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Trivially, the equality in this lemma holds when
x=0
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, a direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=0\}}f(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap\bar{x}^{\perp}}t^{n-2}e^{-tc\|v\|_{K}}dvdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{-(n-1)}\Gamma(n)\rho_{{\rm I}K}(\bar{x}).

∎

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an integrable function with
f(0)>0
. For any
p>0
, the set
K_{p}(f)
was introduced by Ball
[
6
]
,

\displaystyle K_{p}(f)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\int_{0}^{\infty}f(rx)r^{p-1}dr\geq\frac{f(0)}{p}\right\}.

(4.8)

From the definition it follows that the radial function of
K_{p}(f)
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{p}(f)}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}f(rx)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for

.

The following properties were showed in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an integrable function with
f(0)>0
.
For every
p>0
,
K_{p}(f)
has the following properties:

(1)

0\in K_{p}(f)
.

(2)

K_{p}(f)
is a star-shape set.

(3)

K_{p}(f)
is symmetric if
f
is even.

(4)

K_{p}(f)
is a convex body if

is log-concave and has finite positive integral.

(5)

V(K_{n}(f))=\frac{1}{f(0)}J(f)
.

A connection of intersection functions and intersection bodies is presented in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous integrable function with
f(0)>0
. If
f
has finite positive integral, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{f(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f)}\right\},

(4.9)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is trivial for
x=0
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, by Fubini’s Theorem we have

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\bar{x}\cdot y=0\}}f(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}\int_{S^{n-1}\cap\bar{x}^{\bot}}f(ru)dudr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}\cap\bar{x}^{\bot}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}f(ru)dr\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0){\rm R}\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\rho_{K_{n-1}(f)}^{n-1}\right)(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(0)\rho_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f)}(\bar{x}).

The desired formula follows from the definition of intersection functions and (
2.4
).
∎

Lutwak
[

]
proved that for all
T\in{\rm GL}(n)
and
all
K\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
,

\displaystyle{\rm I}(TK)=|\det T|T^{-t}{\rm I}K.

(4.10)

In analytic case we have:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous integrable function with
f(0)>0
and let

.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(f\circ T)=(\mathcal{I}f)\circ\tfrac{T^{-t}}{|\det T^{-1}|}.

(4.11)

Proof.

Let
T\in{\rm GL}(n)
. The definition of
K_{p}(f)
(where
p>0
) tells us that

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{p}(f\circ T)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}f(rTx)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{p}(f)}(Tx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{T^{-1}K_{p}(f)}(x).

Combining with Lutwak’s result (
4.10
) and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(f\circ T)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{f(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}(T^{-1}K_{n-1}(f))}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{f(0)}\|x\|_{|\det T|^{-1}T^{t}{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{f(0)}\||\det T|T^{-t}x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f\left(\tfrac{T^{-t}}{|\det T^{-1}|}x\right).

∎

For an integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the dual difference function
\widetilde{D}f
, of
f
is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}f(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}f(x)+\tfrac{1}{2}f(-x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(4.12)

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that
f=e^{-\varphi}
is integrable. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{D}f)=\mathcal{I}f,

(4.13)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)\leq\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)

(4.14)

with equality if and only if
f
is even.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle A_{\widetilde{D}f,\bar{x}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z\cdot\bar{x}=0\}}(f(z)+f(-z))dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{f,\bar{x}}(0),

hence, from the definition of intersection functions we deduce that
\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{D}f)=\mathcal{I}f
.

Let
\bar{f}(x)=f(-x)
. Then
J(f)=J(\bar{f})
, and from Theorem
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{D}f(x)\log\widetilde{D}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(f,\widetilde{D}f)+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(\bar{f},\widetilde{D}f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(\bar{f},\bar{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f),

with equality if and only if

, namely,

∎

4.3.
The Busemann intersection inequality for functions

For
K\in\mathcal{S}_{o}^{n}
, the Busemann intersection inequality says that (see, e.g.,
[
49
]
)

\displaystyle V({\rm I}K)\leq\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-2}}V(K)^{n-1},

(4.15)

with equality for
n=2
if and only if
K
is an origin-symmetric star body, and for
n\geq 3
if and only if
K
is an origin-symmetric ellipsoid.

In this subsection, we will prove the Busemann intersection inequality for functions. A proof of the following lemma can be found in
[
10
]
when the function
f
is log-concave, and we note that the log-concavity can be removed. We remark that the equality condition is a new result.

Lemma 4.8
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.2.4]
).
Let
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a bounded integrable function. If
0<p<q<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\frac{q}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}f(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(4.16)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\frac{q}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}f(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=c,

(4.17)

if and only if
f(r)=\|f\|_{\infty}
when
r\in[0,c]
and
f(r)=0
when
r\in(c,\infty)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|f\|_{\infty}=1
. We set

\displaystyle F(p)=\left(p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then for any
0<p<q
and
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{F(q)^{q}}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}f(r)dr

(4.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}r^{q-1}f(r)dr+\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}r^{q-1}f(r)dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}r^{q-1}f(r)dr+\alpha^{q-p}\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(r)dr.

We observe that equality holds if and only if
f(r)=0
when
r\in(\alpha,\infty)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}r^{q-1}f(r)dr+\alpha^{q-p}\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}r^{p-1}f(r)dr

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\ \alpha^{q-p}\frac{F(p)^{p}}{p}-\alpha^{q}\int_{0}^{1}(r^{p-1}-r^{q-1})f(\alpha r)dr

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\ \alpha^{q-p}\frac{F(p)^{p}}{p}-\alpha^{q}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(4.19)

Equality holds if and only if
f(\alpha r)=1
when
r\in[0,1]
and
\alpha>0
. The desired inequality follows from the choice
\alpha=F(p)
. The equality conditions of (
4.18
) and (
4.3
) imply that equality holds in (
4.16
) if and only if
f(r)=1
when
r\in[0,\alpha]
and
f(r)=0
when
r\in(\alpha,\infty)
with
\alpha>0
.
∎

The following inclusion has been showed in
[
10
]
for log-concave functions.
We give a slightly more generalized version as follow.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a bounded, continuous integrable function with
f(0)>0
. If
0<p\leq q
, then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(f)\subseteq\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}K_{q}(f).

(4.20)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(f)=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}K_{q}(f)=K

(4.21)

if and only if
f
is a constant on
K
and vanishes outside of
K
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|f\|_{\infty}=1
. For any
u\in S^{n-1}
, by Lemma
4.8
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{q}(f)}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}qr^{q-1}f(ru)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}qr^{q-1}f(ru)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}f(ru)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\rho_{K_{p}(f)}(u).

The equality condition of Lemma
4.8
implies that there is an equality in (
4.20
) if and only if
f
is a positive constant on
K
and vanishes outside of
K
.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a bounded, continuous integrable function with
f(0)>0
. Then

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{I}f)\leq\Gamma(n+1)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-2}}\|f\|_{\infty}J(f)^{n-1},

(4.22)

with equality for
n=2
if and only if
cf
is the characteristic function of an origin-symmetric star body, and for
n\geq 3
if and only if
cf
is the characteristic function of an origin-symmetric ellipsoid, where
c>0
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.5
and the Busemann intersection inequality (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{I}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{f(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f)}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1)f^{n}(0)V({\rm I}K_{n-1}(f))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1)f^{n}(0)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}V(K_{n-1}(f))^{n-1},

with equality for
n=2
if and only if
K_{n-1}(f)
is an origin-symmetric convex body, and for
n\geq 3
if and only if
K_{n-1}(f)
is an origin-symmetric ellipsoid.

From (
4.20
) and Lemma
4.4
, it follows that

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{I}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1)f^{n}(0)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}V(K_{n-1}(f))^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1)f^{n}(0)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}V\left(\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{1}{n}}K_{n}(f)\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1)f^{n}(0)\frac{\omega_{n-1}^{n}}{\omega_{n}^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{f(0)}\right)\left(\frac{J(f)}{f(0)}\right)^{n-1}.

The desired equality conditions come from the equality conditions of Lemma
4.9
and the Busemann intersection inequality (
4.15
).
∎

5.
The solutions to Problems
2
and
3

In this section we will solve Problems
2
and
3
. It is not hard to see that the even condition in Problem
2
is necessary, since it recovers the geometric case when functions
f
and
g
are limited to the characteristic function of convex bodies. Next, we prove that the even condition in Problem
3
is also necessary.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f,g
be integrable log-concave functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
f
is not even, then there exists
an even continuous function
g
such that for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathcal{I}g(x)<\mathcal{I}f(x)

with

\widehat{\delta J}(g,g)>\widehat{\delta J}(f,f).

Proof.

Lemma
4.7
tells that the intersection function of
\widetilde{D}f
agree with that of
f
, that is,

\displaystyle A_{\widetilde{D}f,\bar{x}}(0)=A_{f,\bar{x}}(0),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Since
f
is not even, by
Lemma
4.7
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)<\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f).

(5.1)

Let
f=e^{-\varphi}
and
\bar{f}=e^{-(\varphi+c)}
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is clear that

\widetilde{D}\bar{f}=e^{-c}\widetilde{D}f
, and

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(\bar{f},\bar{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-c}\left[\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)+cJ(f)\right]

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle e^{-c}\left[\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)+cJ(\widetilde{D}f)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}\bar{f},\widetilde{D}\bar{f}).

This means that adding a constant to
\varphi
does not change inequality (
5.1
). We may therefore add a constant to
\varphi
and assume that

\inf\varphi>1.

Combining this assumption with Theorem
3.1
, we infer that
\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)>0
.

Let
g(x)=\varepsilon\widetilde{D}f
with

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)}{\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)}\right)<1.

Then, we have

\displaystyle A_{g,\bar{x}}(0)=\varepsilon A_{\widetilde{D}f,\bar{x}}(0)>A_{f,\bar{x}}(0),\quad{\text{f}or}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(g,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left[\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)-\log\varepsilon J(\widetilde{D}f)\right]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\varepsilon\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(\widetilde{D}f,\widetilde{D}f)+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f).

Therefore
g
is the even continuous function we are looking for.
∎

To prove the characteristic theorem we need the following two geometric results.

Lemma 5.2
.

(
[
41
, Theorem 10.1]
)
.
If
K
is an intersection body and
L
is an origin-symmetric star body, and for all
u\in S^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle V_{n-1}(K\cap u^{\perp})\leq V_{n-1}(L\cap u^{\perp}),

then

\displaystyle V(K)\leq V(L),

with equality if and only if
K=L
.

Lemma 5.3
.

(
[
41
, Theorem 12.2]
)
.
If
K
is an origin-symmetric star body whose radial function is in
C^{\infty}_{e}(S^{n-1})
, then if
K
is not an intersection body, there exists an origin-symmetric body
L
, such that, for all
u\in S^{n-1}

\displaystyle V_{n-1}(L\cap u^{\perp})<V_{n-1}(K\cap u^{\perp}),

but

\displaystyle V(L)>V(K).

We are ready to prove the characteristic theorem presented in Introduction.

Proposition 5.1
.

Problems
2
and
3
have affirmative answers in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if every even integrable log-concave function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an intersection function.

Proof.

The proofs of the statements for Problems
2
and
3
in this proposition are similar, hence, we only give a detailed proof for Problem
3
and the case of Problem
2
follows from the same line.

Assume that both
f
and
g
are even integrable log-concave functions and intersection functions.
Let
f_{0},\ g_{0}
be integrable continuous functions with
f_{0}(0)>0
and
g_{0}(0)>0
such that

\mathcal{I}f_{0}(x)=f(x)
and
\mathcal{I}g_{0}(x)=g(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
To prove the sufficiency, it suffices to show that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)\leq\mathcal{I}g(x)

implies that

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)\leq\widehat{\delta J}(g,g),

with equality if and only if
f=g
.

Lemma
4.5
implies that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle f(x)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{f_{0}(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f_{0})}\right\},

and

\displaystyle g(x)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{g_{0}(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(g_{0})}\right\}.

By Lemma
4.3
, the assumption

\mathcal{I}f(x)\leq\mathcal{I}g(x)

is equivalent to

\displaystyle f_{0}(0)^{n-1}{\rm I}({\rm I}K_{n-1}(f_{0}))\subseteq g_{0}(0)^{n-1}{\rm I}({\rm I}K_{n-1}(g_{0})).

By Lemma
5.2

we have

\displaystyle f_{0}(0)^{n}V({\rm I}K_{n-1}(f_{0}))\leq g_{0}(0)^{n}V({\rm I}K_{n-1}(g_{0})),

(5.2)

with equality if and only if
{\rm I}K_{n-1}(f_{0})={\rm I}K_{n-1}(g_{0})
, i.e.,
f=g
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
and (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\Gamma(n+1)f_{0}(0)^{n}V({\rm I}K_{n-1}(f_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\Gamma(n+1)g_{0}(0)^{n}V({\rm I}K_{n-1}(g_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(g,g),

with equality if and only if
f=g
. This finishes the proof of sufficiency.

To prove the necessity part, it suffices to show that the existence of nonintersection
functions implies a negative answer to Problem
3
.

Let
K
be an origin-symmetric star body whose radial function is in
C^{\infty}_{e}(S^{n-1})
, and let
K
be not an intersection body. We claim that
f(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{K}}\in C_{e}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and it is not an intersection function. In fact, if
e^{-\|x\|_{K}}
is an intersection function, then there exists a non-negative integrable function
h
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{K}}=e^{-(\mathcal{R}h)^{-1}(x,0)}
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle e^{-\|x\|_{K}}=\mathcal{I}h(x)=\exp\{-h(0)\rho_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(h)}^{-1}(x)\},

which implies that
K
is an intersection body. This is a contradiction.

By a direct calculation, we know that if
f(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{K}}
then

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)=n\Gamma(n+1)V(K).

From Lemma
5.3
, it follows that there exists an even function
g(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{L}}
(where
L
is an origin-symmetric star body), such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}f(x)<\mathcal{I}g(x)

but

\displaystyle\widehat{\delta J}(f,f)>\widehat{\delta J}(g,g).

We complete the proof.
∎

Next, we investigate the equivalence between intersection functions and intersection bodies.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative continuous integrable function with
f(0)>0
. Then
f
is an intersection function if and only if
K_{n-1}(f)
is an intersection body.

Proof.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative, continuous, integrable function with
g(0)>0
.
Assume that
f
is an intersection function of
g
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle f(x)=\mathcal{I}g(x)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{g(0)}\|x\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(g)}\right\}.

(5.3)

Moreover, from the definition of the body
K_{n-1}(f)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{n-1}(f)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n-1)r^{n-2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{g(0)}\|rx\|_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(g)}\right\}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}f(0)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}g(0)\rho_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(g)}(x).

This means that
K_{n-1}(f)
is an intersection body.

If
K_{n-1}(f)
is an intersection body, there exists a star body
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{n-1}(f)}(x)=\rho_{{\rm I}L}(x).

Let
g(x)=e^{-\|x\|_{L}}
. It is clear that

K_{n-1}(g)=\Gamma(n)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}L.

By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{n-1}(f)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n)^{-1}\rho_{{\rm I}K_{n-1}(g)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n)^{-1}\mathcal{R}g(x,0).

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{n-1}(\mathcal{I}g)}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}e^{-\mathcal{R}g(rx,0)^{-1}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}e^{-r\mathcal{R}g(x,0)^{-1}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n)\mathcal{R}g(x,0)^{n-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{n-1}(f)}(x)=\Gamma(n)^{-\frac{n}{n-1}}\rho_{K_{n-1}(\mathcal{I}g)}(x).

Hence,
f
is an intersection function.
∎

Now, we give a positive answer to Problem
2
and Problem
3
when
2\leq n\leq 4
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Problems
2
and
3
have affirmative answers when
2\leq n\leq 4
.

Proof.

In
[
25
]
, authors proved that every origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is an intersection body when
2\leq n\leq 4
.
Therefore, By Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.4
,
Problems
2
and
3
have affirmative answers when
2\leq n\leq 4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

In this section we will finish the proof of Theorem

.
The following result is the key reason why Problem

Proposition 6.1
.

Problem

, Problem

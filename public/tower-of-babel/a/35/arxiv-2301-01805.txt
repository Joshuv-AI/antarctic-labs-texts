Unsupervised Manifold Linearizing and Clustering
Tianjiao Ding Shengbang Tong Kwan Ho Ryan Chan Xili Dai Yi Ma Benjamin D. Haeffele

Abstract
We consider the problem of simultaneously clustering and learning a linear representation of data lying close to a union of low-dimensional manifolds, a fundamental task in machine learning and computer vision. When the manifolds are assumed to be linear subspaces, this reduces to the classical problem of subspace clustering, which has been studied extensively over the past two decades. Unfortunately, many real-world datasets such as natural images can not be well approximated by linear subspaces. On the other hand, numerous works have attempted to learn an appropriate transformation of the data, such that data is mapped from a union of general non-linear manifolds to a union of linear subspaces (with points from the same manifold being mapped to the same subspace). However, many existing works have limitations such as assuming knowledge of the membership of samples to clusters, requiring high sampling density, or being shown theoretically to learn trivial representations. In this paper, we propose to optimize the Maximal Coding Rate Reduction metric with respect to both the data representation and a novel doubly stochastic cluster membership, inspired by state-of-the-art subspace clustering results. We give a parameterization of such a representation and membership, allowing efficient mini-batching and one-shot initialization. Experiments on CIFAR-10, -20, -100, and TinyImageNet-200 datasets show that the proposed method is much more accurate and scalable than state-of-the-art deep clustering methods, and further learns a latent linear representation of the data. 4 4 4 Code is available at https://github.com/tianjiaoding/mlc .

1.2
Learning Diverse and Discriminative Features: from Supervised to Unsupervised Learning

Meanwhile, learning a

Unfortunately, the representation learned by CE fails to achieve this property and exhibits

Question 2
.

For data lying close to a union of manifolds, can we learn a union-of-orthogonal-subspaces representation, without access to the ground-truth labels?

Self-supervised Representation Learning.

An important line of research that learns a representation without using ground-truth labels is that of self-supervised learning. It has seen remarkable recent progress thanks to the so-called

Formulation

We begin by making clear the problem of interest.

Problem 1

(Unsupervised Manifold Linearizing and Clustering)
.

Suppose
{\bm{X}}=[{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{n}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D\times n}
is a dataset of
n
points lying on an union of
k
low-dimensional manifolds
\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
. Given
{\bm{X}}
, we aim to simultaneously

1.

Cluster the samples
: find
\hat{{\bm{y}}}
such that
{\bm{x}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\hat{y}(i)}
;

2.

Learn a linear representation
: find a transformation
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, such that features
f({\bm{x}}_{i})
’s from the same cluster spread uniformly in a low-dimensional linear subspace, and the subspaces arising from different clusters are orthogonal.

In §
2.1
, we review the principle of Maximal Coding Rate Reduction (
MCR
2
) which is designed to learn ideal representations in a
supervised
manner, i.e., when the ground-truth membership is given. Then in §
2.2
, we discuss the challenges of simultaneous clustering and learning a representation (i.e., addressing
1
in its entirety), for which we propose our
MLC
objective (
4
). Later in §
3
, we further give an algorithm to optimize
MLC
(
4
).

2.1
Supervised Manifold Linearizing via
MCR
2

When the cluster membership is given as supervision,
MCR
2

[
78
]
is designed to solve part 2) of
1
. To begin with, let
f_{\bm{\theta}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
be a transformation parameterized by a neural network; this in turn gives the features
{\bm{Z}}:=[{\bm{z}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{z}}_{n}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times n}
of data with
{\bm{z}}_{i}:=f_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{x}}_{i})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

MCR
2
aims to learn an ideal representation by utilizing the coding rate measures. Define the
coding rate

R({\bm{Z}};\,\epsilon):=\log\det\left({\bm{I}}+\frac{d}{n\epsilon^{2}}{\bm{Z}}{\bm{Z}}^{\top}\right).

Intuitively
7
7

7

For a rigorous treatment, see for example
[
45
, §2.1]
.
,
R({\bm{Z}};\,\epsilon)
measures some volume of features in
{\bm{Z}}
up to
\epsilon>0
precision, so maximizing it would
diversify
features of all the samples. Likewise, one can apply the measure to features of each cluster and take weighted mean over the clusters; namely, define
R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Pi};\,\epsilon)
as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\langle\bm{\Pi}_{j},{\bm{1}}\rangle}{n}\log\det\left({\bm{I}}+\frac{d}{\langle\bm{\Pi}_{j},{\bm{1}}\rangle\epsilon^{2}}{\bm{Z}}\Diag(\bm{\Pi}_{j}){\bm{Z}}^{\top}\right).

Here
\bm{\Pi}=[\bm{\Pi}_{1},\dots,\bm{\Pi}_{k}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}
is a given
point-cluster
membership such that
\Pi_{ij}=1
if
{\bm{x}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
and
\Pi_{ij}=0
otherwise,
{\bm{1}}
is a vector of all ones so
\langle\bm{\Pi}_{j},{\bm{1}}\rangle
is the number of points in cluster
j
, and for any
{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\Diag({\bm{v}})
denotes a diagonal matrix with the entries of
{\bm{v}}
along the diagonal. Minimizing
R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Pi};\,\epsilon)
thus pushes features in each cluster to stay close.
MCR
2
learns an ideal representation by
maximizing the coding rate reduction
(hence the acronym)

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\theta}}\quad R({\bm{Z}};\,\epsilon)-R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Pi};\,\epsilon)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad{\bm{Z}}=f_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})

(1)

Notably, it has been shown that given
\bm{\Pi}
, the features obtained by maximizing the rate reduction has the property that the features of each cluster spread uniformly within a subspace (within-cluster diverse), and subspaces from different clusters are orthogonal (between-cluster discriminative), under relatively mild assumptions
[
78
, Theorem 2.1]
.

2.2
Unsupervised Manifold Linearizing

and Clustering

While
MCR
2
is designed to learn ideal representations (§
1
) when the membership
\bm{\Pi}
is given, here we are interested in the
unsupervised
setting where one does
not
have access to membership annotations. To address both parts 1) and 2) of
1
, a natural idea is to optimize
MCR
2
over both the representation
{\bm{Z}}
and membership
\bm{\Pi}
via

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\theta},\bm{\Pi}\in\Omega_{\circ}}R({\bm{Z}};\,\epsilon)-R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Pi};\,\epsilon)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad{\bm{Z}}=f_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}}).

(2)

Here
\Omega_{\circ}:=\{\bm{\Pi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}:\forall i\in[n],\,\,\exists\hat{y}(i)\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\,\,\Pi_{i\hat{y}(i)}=1\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\Pi_{ij}=0\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,j\neq\hat{y}(i)\}
is the set of all ‘hard’ assignments, i.e., each row of
\bm{\Pi}
is a one-hot vector. However, this optimization is in general combinatorial: its complexity grows exponentially in
n
and
k
, and it does not allow smooth and gradual changes of
\bm{\Pi}
. Further, a second challenge is the chicken-and-egg nature of this problem: If one already has an ideal representation
{\bm{Z}}
, then existing subspace clustering methods can be applied on
{\bm{Z}}
to estimate the membership. Likewise, if one is given the ground-truth membership
\bm{\Pi}
of clusters, then solving (
1
) would lead to an ideal representation. However, the
{\bm{Z}}
and
\bm{\Pi}
at the beginning of optimization are typically far from ideal.

In the rest of this section, we propose a so-called
doubly stochastic membership
to deal with the combinatorial challenge. To tackle the chicken-and-egg nature, we parameterize and initialize the representation and membership in an efficient and effective way, as we will discuss in §
3
.

Doubly Stochastic Subspace Clustering.
To address the combinatorial nature of estimating the memberships, we draw inspiration from the closely related problem of
subspace clustering
, where the goal is to cluster
n
samples assumed to lie close to a union of
k
low-dimensional subspaces (§
1.1
). In this case, one typically does not directly learn an
n\times k
matrix denoting memberships of
n
points into
k
subspaces. Instead, one first learns an affinity matrix
\bm{\Gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
signaling the similarities between pairs of points, and then applies spectral clustering on the learned
\bm{\Gamma}
to obtain a final clustering
[
20
,
22
,
44
,
41
,
28
,
75
]
. In particular, requiring doubly-stochastic constraints on the affinity
\bm{\Gamma}
is shown theoretically to suppress false inter-cluster connections for clustering
[
18
]
along with state-of-the-art empirical performance for subspace clustering
[
39
]
.

Inspired by the above, we propose a constraint set
\Omega
for matrix
\bm{\Gamma}
to be the set of
n\times n
doubly stochastic matrices,

\Omega=\{\bm{\Gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}:\bm{\Gamma}\geq 0,\quad\bm{\Gamma}{\bm{1}}=\bm{\Gamma}^{\top}{\bm{1}}={\bm{1}}\}.

(3)

However, this constraint alone is insufficient for strong clustering performance: Consider optimizing
MCR
2
with respect to
\bm{\Gamma}\in\Omega
only, and note that the objective is convex with respect to
\bm{\Gamma}
. Since we maximize a convex function with respect to convex constraints
\Omega
, an optimal
\bm{\Gamma}
would lie at an extreme point of
\Omega
, which for doubly stochastic matrices is a permutation matrix. This is not ideal for clustering, as it implies that every point is assigned to its own distinct cluster, and there is no incentive to merge points into larger clusters.
To resolve this issue, we use a negative entropy regularization
\sum_{ij}\Gamma_{ij}\log(\Gamma_{ij})
to
\bm{\Gamma}
which biases
\bm{\Gamma}
toward the uniform matrix
\tfrac{1}{n}\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}
. By tuning the coefficient of such regularization, we can also tune the sparsity level of
\bm{\Gamma}
. This regularization can be conveniently integrated into the network architecture, as we will see in §
3
. Now we are ready to state our proposed formulation Manifold Linearizing and Clustering (
MLC
):

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\theta}}\quad R({\bm{Z}};\,\epsilon)-R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Gamma};\,\epsilon)

(4)

\displaystyle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad{\bm{Z}}=f_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}}),\,\,\bm{\Gamma}=h_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})\in\Omega,\text{ where }

\displaystyle\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle R_{c}({\bm{Z}},\bm{\Gamma};\,\epsilon)=\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\log\det\left({\bm{I}}+\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{d}{\epsilon^2}{\bm{Z}}\Diag((\bm{\Gamma})_{j}){\bm{Z}}^{\top}\right)$}}.

Note that both
{\bm{Z}}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
are parameterized by neural networks. While a doubly stochastic membership
\bm{\Gamma}
may seem large in size and hard to constrain, we explain in §
3
how we parameterize
\bm{\Gamma}
so that the constraints are satisfied by construction and efficient mini-batching is allowed.

.

studies the same

3.1
Efficient Parameterization

Parameterizing
{\bm{Z}}
.
As is common practice, we take an existing network architecture such as ResNet-18 as the backbone. We append a few affine layers with non-linearities as the
feature head
to further transform the output of the backbone to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, followed by a projection layer to respect the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
constraint.

Parameterizing
\bm{\Gamma}
.
Different from parameterizing
{\bm{Z}}
, this is much less trivial: If one were to directly take
\bm{\Gamma}
as decision variables in
\Omega
, it would lead to maintaining
O(n^{2})
variables, which is prohibitive for large datasets (e.g.,
n=10^{6}
for ImageNet). To allow efficient computation, we again draw inspiration from
subspace clustering
: There, the membership
\bm{\Gamma}
given data
{\bm{X}}
often takes the form of
g({\bm{X}})^{\top}g({\bm{X}})
for some transformation
g
, such as in the inner product kernel
[
28
,
18
]
where
g({\bm{X}})={\bm{X}}
or the least square regression
[
44
]
where
g({\bm{X}})=({\bm{I}}+\lambda{\bm{X}}^{\top}{\bm{X}})^{-1/2}{\bm{X}}
. It is then tempting to take a neural network
g_{\bm{\theta}}
and use
{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}
as the membership where
{\bm{C}}=g_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})
. Nevertheless, such a matrix is in general
not
doubly stochastic, i.e.,
{\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}}\notin\Omega
. To obtain a doubly stochastic membership, we further apply a Sinkhorn projection layer
P_{\Omega,\eta}(\cdot)

[
56
,
19
]
, which gives
\bm{\Gamma}=P_{\Omega,\eta}({\bm{C}}^{\top}{\bm{C}})\in\Omega
, where
\eta
is the coefficient of entropy regularization.
8
8

8

So in (
4
),
\bm{\Gamma}=h_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})
is simply
\bm{\Gamma}=P_{\Omega,\eta}(g_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})^{\top}g_{\bm{\theta}}({\bm{X}})).

As in parameterizing
{\bm{Z}}
, we implement
g_{\bm{\theta}}
by taking the same backbone and appending layers of the same type to be the
cluster head
. As we shall see soon in §
3.2
, such a parameterization further allows us to initialize both
{\bm{Z}}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
in
one shot
using self-supervised learning.

Thanks to the above parameterization, we can do forward and backward passes efficiently via mini-batches. For a mini-batch of
n_{b}
samples (
n_{b}\ll n
typically), the mini-batched versions of
{\bm{Z}}
,
{\bm{C}}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
have sizes

,

and

, respectively (

).

9

]

3.2
Efficient Initialization

Since the proposed

Initializing
{\bm{Z}}
: Self-supervised Representation Learning.
Randomly initialized features could be far from being ideal (in the sense defined in §
1
), and further may not respect the invariance to augmentation, i.e., the augmented samples should have their representation close to each other. Thus, we initialize the features using a self-supervised learning called
total coding rate
(
TCR
)
[
38

Doubly Stochastic Membership vs. Point-Cluster Membership.

Different from the doubly stochastic

•

Stable
: As
\bm{\Gamma}
is initialized deterministically, the performance of

•

Structured
: Initialization of
\bm{\Gamma}

{\bm{Z}}

automatically gains from developments of self-supervised representation learning.

•

Efficient
: Once
{\bm{Z}}
is initialized,
\bm{\Gamma}
can be initialized with no additional cost whatsoever, compared to using any extra training stage to initialize the cluster head. In contrast, e.g.,

[
64

trains the cluster head with
10

5
Conclusion

This paper studies the problem of simultaneously clustering and learning an union-of-orthogonal-subspaces representation for data, when data lies close to a union of low-dimensional manifolds. To address the problem we propose an objective based on
maximal coding rate reduction
and
doubly stochastic
membership inspired by the state-of-the-art subspace clustering results. We provide an efficient and effective parameterization of the membership variables as well as a meta-algorithm to optimize the representation and membership jointly. We further conduct experiments on datasets with larger number of clusters and imbalanced clusters and show that the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance. We believe that our work provides a general and unified framework for unsupervised learning of structured representations for multi-modal data.

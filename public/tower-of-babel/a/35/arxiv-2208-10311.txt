Two weight bump conditions for compactness of commutators Thanks: The second author is supported by Simons Collaboration Grant for Mathematicians, 160427. A version of this paper will serve as a chapter in the second author’s Ph.D. dissertation.
Adam Mair, Kabe Moen

Abstract
. We prove certain two weight bump conditions are sufficient for the compactness of the commutator [ b , T ] [b,T] where b ∈ C ​ M ​ O b\in CMO and T T is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. This is the first result for compactness in the two weight setting without additional assumptions on the individual weights.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B20, 42B25

1.
Introduction

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator (CZO) and
b
be a
BMO
function. Define the commutator of
b
and
T
to be

[b,T]f=bTf-T(bf).

In this note we are interested in conditions on
b
and
(u,v)
that ensure compactness of
[b,T]
between two different weighted spaces
L^{p}(v)
and
L^{p}(u)
.

The boundedness of the commutator is well established. Coifmann, Rochberg, and Weiss
[
6
]
showed that
[b,T]
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
b\in BMO
. Chung, Pereyra, and Pérez
[
4
]
showed that
[b,T]
is bouneded on
L^{p}(w)
when
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
class and gave the sharp quantitative control of the constants. The two weight boundedness of
[b,T]
from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
is significantly more difficult. Cruz-Uribe and the second author
[
8
]
showed that if one strengthens the two weight
A_{p}
condition with certain logarithmic bumps then
[b,T]
is a bounded linear operator from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
(see below for relevant definitions). We also refer readers to more recent results by Lerner, Ombrosi, and Riveria-Ríos
[
18
]
, who improved the results in
[
8
]
. Finally, we mention in passing the work of Isralowitz, Pott, and Treil
[
14
]
and Cruz-Uribe, the second author, and Tran
[
9
]
in which the two weight boundedness of
[b,T]
and higher order commutators is obtained assuming more general assumptions on
b
and the weights
(u,v)
.

Recall that a linear operator between two Banach spaces
T:X\rightarrow Y
is compact if
T(B_{X})
has compact closure in
Y
(here
B_{X}
is the unit ball in
X
). The compactness of commutators was first investigated by Uchiyama
[
21
]
, who was motivated by applications to complex analysis and Hankel operators. Uchiyama showed that if
b\in CMO
, the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the
BMO
norm, then
[b,T]
is a compact operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The original statement in
[
21
]
was that
b\in VMO
, the space
vanishing mean oscillation
, is sufficient for compactness of
[b,T]
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However the spaces
CMO
and
VMO
do not coincide over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the correct space for compactness of commutators is
CMO
. This misnomer has appeared several times in the literature (including
[
5
]
) as the article by Bourdaud
[
2
]
points out. Wu and Yang
[
22
]
show that
CMO
actually characterizes the compactness of
[b,T]
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for specific operators
T
like the Riesz transforms. Iwaniec
[
15
]
proved a Fredholm alternative for Beltrami equations as an application of the compactness of
[b,T]
. Cruz and Clop
[
5
]
were also interested in applications to quasiregular mappings and showed that commutator
[b,T]
is compact on
L^{p}(w)
when
b\in CMO
and
w\in A_{p}
. Recently Hytönen and Lappas
[
12
]
have shown an extrapolation theorem for compact operators on weighted spaces. Finally we mention some very recent two weight results which are related to the so-called Bloom
BMO
. Namely, under certain weighted
BMO
assumptions on
b
, it is shown that the operator
[b,T]
is a compact operator from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
provided one assumes the side condition
u,v\in A_{p}
. We refer the interested reader to Chen, Lacey, Li, and Vempati
[
3
]
and Hytönen, Oikari, and Sinko
[
13
]
for more on compactness in this setting. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
T
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator and
b\in CMO
. If
u
and
v
are weights that satisfy

(1.1)

\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}(\log L)^{2p-1+\delta},Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\log L)^{2p^{\prime}-1+\delta},Q}<\infty

for some
\delta>0
, then the operator
[b,T]
is a compact operator from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
.

Condition (
1.1
) is a strengthening of the classic two weight
A_{p}
condition. It does not require individual assumptions on
u
and
v
. For example,
u
can be
any
weight and
v=M^{k}u
where
k=\lfloor 2p\rfloor+1
and
M^{k}
is the
k
-fold composition of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
(see
[
20
]
). We also remark that conditions for compactness of the higher order iterated commutators are more in-depth and will be addressed in a forthcoming paper.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation. A measurable function
b
is said to belong to
BMO
if

\|b\|_{BMO}=\sup_{Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}\Big|b(x)-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}b\Big|\,dx<\infty.

The quantity
\|b\|_{BMO}
is a semi-norm which can be made into a norm by considering
BMO
modulo constants. Henceforth, we will just refer to
\|b\|_{BMO}
as the norm of
b
in
BMO
. The space
CMO
is the subspace obtained by taking the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{BMO}
.

The Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator given by

Mf(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy.

A Calderón-Zygmund operator is an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
bounded operator associated to a kernel in the sense

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,\quad f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),x\notin\mathsf{supp}\,f.

We will assume the kernel
K(x,y)
, defined on
\{(x,y):x\not=y\}
, satisfies the size estimate

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n}}

and smoothness estimate

|\nabla K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n+1}}.

We will also need the maximal truncation operator which is given by

T^{\sharp}f(x)=\sup_{\eta>0}\left|\int_{|x-y|>\eta}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|.

It is well-known that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, CZOs, and commutators of CZOs with
BMO
functions are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p<\infty
. Moreover, these operators are also bounded on
L^{p}(w)
when the weight
w
belongs to
A_{p}
:

\sup_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

Following the book
[
7
]
, we will work with pairs of weights
(u,v)
, that is, non-negative
L^{1}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions, such that
u>0
on a set of positive measure and
v>0
almost everywhere. We will work a strengthened version of the two weight
A_{p}
condition

\sup_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}u\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}v^{1-p^{\prime}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

In order to understand the bump conditions we need some background on Young functions and Orlicz spaces. A Young function,
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, is an increasing, convex function that satisfies
\Phi(0)=0
and
\Phi(t)/t\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Given a Young function
\Phi
one may define the Orlicz average of
f
over a cube
Q

\|f\|_{\Phi,Q}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}\Phi\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)\,dx\leq 1\right\}

and this quantity is a norm. For our purposes we will only need Young functions of the form
\Phi(t)=t^{p}\big[\log(e+t)\big]^{a}
, where
p>1
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. In this case we will use the notation

\|f\|_{\Phi,Q}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\log L)^{a},Q}

and refer to such averages as logarithmic bumps.

In Pérez
[
19
]
, it is shown that if the pair of weights satisfies the condition

(2.1)

\sup_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}u\right)\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\log L)^{p^{\prime}-1+\delta},Q}<\infty,

for some
\delta>0
then
M:L^{p}(v)\rightarrow L^{p}(u)
. For CZOs bump conditions on both weights are needed. Lerner
[
17
]
showed that the condition

(2.2)

\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}(\log L)^{p-1+\delta},Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\log L)^{p^{\prime}-1+\delta},Q}<\infty

for some
\delta>0
is sufficient for the boundedness of
T
and
T^{\sharp}
from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
. Finally, for commutators of
BMO
functions the condition

(2.3)

\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}(\log L)^{2p-1+\delta},Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\log L)^{2p^{\prime}-1+\delta},Q}<\infty

is sufficient for the boundedness of
[b,T]
from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
(see
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
). We note that the condition (
2.3
) is stronger than both (
2.2
) and (
2.1
). In particular, if
(u,v)
satisfy (
2.3
), then all of the operators
M,T,
and
T^{\sharp},
are bounded from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
as well as the commutator
[b,T]
when
b\in BMO
. We will use this fact in our proof. We also point out that this is the benefit of the bump conditions, which are universal conditions that are not operator specific.

To prove compactness of the operator
[b,T]
we will need a characterization of compact subsets of Lebesgue spaces, known as the Kolomogorov-Riesz theorem. The survey paper by Hanch-Olsen and Holden
[
11
]
contains the basic statement and history of the original theorem. A characterization of compact subsets of weighted Lebesgue spaces has a more complicated development and was just recently resolved. Early weighted versions of the Kolomogorv-Riesz theorem required extra assumptions on the weight
u
, such as
u\in A_{p}
,
u\in A_{\infty}
, or
u^{1-p_{0}^{\prime}}\in L^{1}_{\textsf{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
p_{0}
(see
[
5
]
and
[
23
]
). Guo and Zhao
[
10
]
proved a general Kolomogorov-Riesz theorem in Banach function spaces that generalize
L^{p}(u)
. This generalization is paramount for proving two weight compactness, because we are not assuming additional assumptions on the weight
u
.

Theorem 2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
u
be a weight, and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{p}(u)
. If the family
\mathcal{F}
satisfies

(
a
)

\mathcal{F}
is a bounded subset, i.e.,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C;

(
b
)

\mathcal{F}
uniformly vanishes at infinity, that is,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{|x|>N}|f(x)|^{p}u(x)\,dx=0;

(
c
)

\mathcal{F}
is equicontinuous, that is,

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+h)-f(x)|^{p}u(x)\,dx<\epsilon,

then the family
\mathcal{F}
has compact closure in
L^{p}(u)
.

Finally, it will be useful to regularize our operators, an idea that goes back to Krantz and Li
[
16
]
(see also
[
1
]
). Given
\eta>0
, we let
K_{\eta}(x,y)
be a smooth truncation of the
K(x,y)
such that

(
a
)

K_{\eta}(x,y)=0
if
|x-y|\leq\eta
;

(
b
)

K_{\eta}(x,y)=K(x,y)
if
|x-y|>2\eta
;

(
c
)

K_{\eta}
satisfies the same size and regularity estimates as
K
, namely

(2.4)

|K_{\eta}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla K_{\eta}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n+1}}.

We let
T^{\eta}
be the operator associated to the kernel
K_{\eta}
. We also note that
T^{\eta}
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
whenever
M
and
T^{\sharp}
are also bounded, for example if
(u,v)
satisfy (
2.2
) or (
2.3
). Indeed,

|T^{\eta}f(x)|\leq\left|\int_{\eta<|x-y|\leq 2\eta}K_{\eta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|+\left|\int_{2\eta<|x-y|}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|\\
\leq\int_{\eta<|x-y|\leq 2\eta}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}\,dy+T^{\sharp}f(x)\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{n}}\int_{|x-y|\leq 2\eta}|f(y)|\,dy+T^{\sharp}f(x)\\
\leq CMf(x)+T^{\sharp}f(x).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

We will make several reductions to prove the compactness of
[b,T]
from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
. First we use the well-known fact that the space of compact operators,
\mathcal{K}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))
, is a closed subset of the space of bounded operators,
\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))
, in the operator norm topology. The precise bound for
[b,T]
from
[
8
]
is that if
b\in BMO
and
(u,v)
satisfy
2.3
, then

\|[b,T]f\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C\|b\|_{BMO}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}

and in particular

\|[b,T]\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))}\leq C\|b\|_{BMO}.

The commutator is linear in
b
which implies

\|[b_{1},T]-[b_{2},T]\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))}=\|[b_{1}-b_{2},T]\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))}\leq C\|b_{1}-b_{2}\|_{BMO}.

Thus if
b\in CMO
and
\{b_{j}\}
is a sequence of smooth functions converging to
b
in
BMO
, then
[b_{j},T]\rightarrow[b,T]
in the operator topology. It is this estimate that breaks down for the higher order iterated commutators. Next, notice that if
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then by using the mean value theorem on
b
we have

\displaystyle|[b,T]f(x)-[b,T^{\eta}]f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|x-y|\leq 2\eta}|b(x)-b(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|\leq 2\eta}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-k}\eta<|x-y|\leq 2^{-k+1}\eta}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\eta C\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,2^{-k+1}\eta)}{|f(y)|}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\eta C\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}Mf(x).

This implies that

\|[b,T^{\eta}]-[b,T]\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))}\leq\eta C_{b}\|M\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(v),L^{p}(u))}

and hence
[b,T^{\eta}]
converges to
[b,T]
as
\eta\rightarrow 0
in the operator topology. It suffices to show that for a fixed
\eta>0
and a fixed
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that
[b,T^{\eta}]
is a compact operator from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
. Fix
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\eta>0
and note that our estimates may depend on
b
and
\eta
. Consider the unit ball in
L^{p}(v)
,

B_{L^{p}(v)}=\{f\in L^{p}(v):\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\leq 1\}.

To show that
[b,T^{\eta}]
is a compact operator we will show that
\mathcal{F}=[b,T^{\eta}](B_{L^{p}(v)})
satisfies (a), (b), and (c) of Theorem
2
, hence proving that
\mathcal{F}
has compact closure in
L^{p}(u)
.

Since the operator
[b,T^{\eta}]
is bounded from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
, we have that

\|[b,T^{\eta}]f\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\leq C

for
f\in B_{L^{p}(v)}
and thus
\mathcal{F}
is a bounded subset of
L^{p}(u)
, i.e., it satisfies (a). To see that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the uniform vanishing at infinity (b), let
f\in B_{L^{p}(v)}
and
Q
be a cube containing
\mathsf{supp}\,b
. Choose
N>1
such that

N>2\sup\{|y|:y\in Q\}.

For such
N
and
|x|>N
we have

\displaystyle||[b,T^{\eta}]f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-b(y))K_{\eta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathsf{supp}\,b}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|b\|_{\infty}}{|x|^{n}}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|b\|_{\infty}}{|x|^{n}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\left(\int_{Q}v^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{b,v}}{|x|^{n}}.

We note that

\left(\int_{Q}v^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C|Q|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\log L)^{2p^{\prime}-1+\delta},Q}<\infty

and hence we have

|[b,T^{\eta}]f(x)|\leq\frac{C_{b,v}}{|x|^{n}}

with a finite constant
C_{b,v}
that depends on
b
and
v
, but not
f
. Integrating, over
|x|>N
yields

\int_{|x|>N}|[b,T^{\eta}]f(x)|^{p}u(x)\,dx\leq C_{b,v}\int_{|x|>N}\frac{u(x)}{|x|^{np}}\,dx.

Since
M(\chi_{[-1,1]^{n}})(x)\geq C(1+|x|)^{-n}
and
M
is bounded from
L^{p}(v)
to
L^{p}(u)
we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)}{(1+|x|)^{np}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M(\chi_{[-1,1]^{n}})(x)^{p}u(x)\,dx\leq Cv([-1,1]^{n})<\infty.

This implies that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|>N}\frac{u(x)}{|x|^{np}}\,dx=0

and thus the family
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (b) of Theorem
2
.

We now prove the most difficult of the estimates, the equicontinuity estimate, (c), from Theorem
2
. Let
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be sufficiently small to be chosen later and
f\in B_{L^{p}(v)}
. Consider

\displaystyle T^{\eta}f(x+h)-T^{\eta}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x+h)-b(y))K_{\eta}(x+h,y)f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-b(y))K_{\eta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=(b(x+h)-b(x))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{\eta}(x,y)f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(y)-b(x+h))(K_{\eta}(x,y)-K_{\eta}(x+h,y))f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=Af(x)+Bf(x),

where we have added and subtracted the term
b(x+h)T^{\eta}f(x)
. The first term is easy to handle with the smoothness of
b
:

|Af(x)|\leq|h|\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}|T^{\eta}f(x)|\leq C|h|\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}(Mf(x)+T^{\sharp}f(x)).

Hence

\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\leq 1}\|Af\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C|h|

which will go to zero uniformly as
h\rightarrow 0
. For the other term, notice that if
|h|<\frac{\eta}{4}
and
|x-y|<\frac{\eta}{2}
then
|x+h-y|<|x-y|+|h|<\eta
. Hence
K_{\eta}(x+h,y)=K_{\eta}(x,y)=0
for such
|h|<\frac{\eta}{4}
, and we have

\displaystyle|Bf(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}}(b(y)-b(x+h))(K_{\eta}(x,y)-K_{\eta}(x+h,y))f(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|b\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}}|K_{\eta}(x+h,y)-K_{\eta}(x,y)||f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2C|h|\|b\|_{\infty}\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,dy

where we have used the well-known fact that the gradient estimate (
2.4
) implies the standard kernel estimate

|K_{\eta}(x+h,y)-K_{\eta}(x,y)|\leq C\frac{|h|}{|x-y|^{n+1}}

when
|x-y|\geq 2|h|
. Notice
|x-y|\geq 2|h|
is satisfied since
|x-y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\geq 2|h|
. We now have

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}\eta\leq|x-y|<2^{k}\eta}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,2^{k}\eta)}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\eta}Mf(x).

The boundedness of the maximal function implies

\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\leq 1}\|Bf\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C|h|.

These estimates do not depend on
f
and go to zero uniformly as
h\rightarrow 0
hence we have

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}\leq 1}\|[b,T^{\eta}]f(\cdot+h)-[b,T^{\eta}]f\|_{L^{p}(u)}=0.

This concludes our proof thus showing that
[b,T]
is a compact operator.

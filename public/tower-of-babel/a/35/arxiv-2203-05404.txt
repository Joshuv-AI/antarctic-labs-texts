About an extension of the Matsumoto-Yor property
Gérard Letac, Jacek Wesołowski

Abstract
If α , β > 0 \alpha,\beta>0 are distinct and if A A and B B are independent non-degenerate positive random variables such that S = 1 B ​ β ​ A + B α ​ A + B and T = 1 A ​ β ​ A + B α ​ A + B S=\tfrac{1}{B}\,\tfrac{\beta A+B}{\alpha A+B}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptT=\tfrac{1}{A}\,\tfrac{\beta A+B}{\alpha A+B} are independent, we prove that this happens if and only if the A A and B B have generalized inverse Gaussian distributions with suitable parameters. Essentially, this has already been proved in Bao and Noack (2021) with supplementary hypothesis on existence of smooth densities. The sources of these questions are an observation about independence properties of the exponential Brownian motion due to Matsumoto and Yor (2001) and a recent work of Croydon and Sasada (2000) on random recursion models rooted in the discrete Korteweg - de Vries equation, where the above result was conjectured. We also extend the direct result to random matrices proving that a matrix variate analogue of the above independence property is satisfied by independent matrix-variate GIG variables. The question of characterization of GIG random matrices through this independence property remains open.



Keywords
: Bessel differential equation, GIG distribution, discrete Korteweg - de Vries equation, Matsumoto-Yor property, Matrix GIG distribution

Detailed balance equation for discrete KdV model

Croydon and Sasada (2020) introduced two so called detailed balance equations. For type I model we say that a pair of probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on some spaces
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the detailed balance equation for a map
F=(F_{1},F_{2}):\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
iff

F(\mu\otimes\nu)=\mu\otimes\nu,

(1)

where
F(\mu\otimes\nu)
means
(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ F^{-1}
.

For type II model we say that two pairs of probability measures
\mu
,
\nu
on
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{V}
and
\widetilde{\mu}
,
\widetilde{\nu}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
satisfy the detailed balance equation for a map
F=(F_{1},F_{2}):\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
iff

F(\mu\otimes\nu)=\widetilde{\mu}\otimes\widetilde{\nu}.

(2)

In other terms for the type II model, one considers independent random variables
X
and
Y
with distributions
\mu
and
\nu
such that random variables
U=F_{1}(X,Y)
and
V=F_{2}(X,Y)
are independent and have distributions
\widetilde{\mu}
and
\widetilde{\nu}
, respectively. Type I model is more restrictive since it imposes the additional condition that
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}U
and
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}V
.

As an example of type II model, if
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=(0,\infty)
and

F(x,y)=(x+y,\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{x+y})
Matsumoto and Yor (2001) have observed that if
X
and
Y
are respectively Gamma and generalized inverse Gaussian (GIG) distributed with suitable parameters and independent, the same is true for the pair
U,V
with different parameters (a description of the GIG distribution and of its limiting cases appears in Section 2 and in Section 3 respectively). This phenomena has been called the Matsumoto-Yor (MY) property, a name coined by Stirzaker (2005), p. 43. In Letac and Wesołowski (2000) a characterisation by independence of
X
,
Y
and independence of
U
,
V
of these pairs Gamma, GIG is given in .

In the present paper we investigate a type II model, again with
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=(0,\infty)
which has been considered by Croydon and Sasada (2020), Sec. 3.2. They introduced the involutive map
F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}:(0,\infty)^{2}\to(0,\infty)^{2}
defined by

F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=\left(y\tfrac{\beta xy+1}{\alpha xy+1},\,x\tfrac{\alpha xy+1}{\beta xy+1}\right)

(3)

for
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and
\alpha\neq\beta

(n,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
one associates a vector
(x_{n}^{t},y_{n}^{t})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
defined by
(x_{n}^{t-1},y_{n-1}^{t})
and by the formula

(x_{n}^{t},y_{n}^{t})=F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x_{n}^{t-1},y_{n-1}^{t}).

For instance
(x_{n}^{t},y_{n}^{t})
is known for all
n,t>0
if it is known along
(0,n)

and
(t,0)
for all

If
X
and
Y
are independent as well as
U
and
V
defined by
(U,V)=F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X,Y)
the fact that

is involutive permits to create a stationary measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
for the above dynamics by taking
(x_{n}^{t},y_{n}^{t})=(X,Y)
or
(U,V)
according to the fact that

is even or odd. The straight discrete KdV model corresponds to the particular case
\beta=0.

For details, see Sect. 2.2 in Croydon, Sasada and Tsujimoto (2020) with slightly different notations.

Croydon and Sasada (2020) observed that the choice of
\mu
and

as generalized inverse Gaussian (GIG) distributions with suitable parameters fits with the detailed balance equation of type II model . Actually, their Proposition 3.9 considers the type I model, but the proof translates immediately to the type II model with
\widetilde{\mu}

being also GIG distributions. Furthermore, their Conjecture 8.6 predicts that these sets of GIG distributions are the only possible ones for the type II model. This has been proved by Bao and Noack (2021) under the technical assumptions that the involved distributions have strictly positive densities on
(0,\infty)
with second derivatives. Their approach was based on expressing the independence condition through the functional equation for logarithms
r_{X}
,
r_{Y}
,

and

of densities of

,
Y
,

and

r_{U}\left(\tfrac{y(1+\beta xy)}{1+\alpha xy}\right)+r_{V}\left(\tfrac{x(1+\alpha xy)}{1+\beta xy}\right)=r_{X}(x)+r_{Y}(y),\hskip 20.00003ptx,y>0.

The first main result of the present paper proves the Croydon and Sasada Conjecture 8.6 in full generality. The technique uses a extended Laplace transform
L_{X}
of a positive random variable

defined by

L_{X}(s,\sigma,\theta)={\mathbb{E}}\,X^{s}\,e^{\sigma X+\frac{\theta}{X}},\hskip 20.00003pt(s,\sigma,\theta)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,\infty)^{2}

(4)

and leads to a related function

z^{2}g_{s}^{\prime\prime}(z)+zg_{s}^{\prime}(z)-(z^{2}+\nu^{2})\,g_{s}(z)=0.

One can mention here that the main tool of the paper Letac and Wesołowski (2000) is the simpler function
(\sigma,\theta)\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}\,e^{\sigma X+\frac{\theta}{X}}.

To describe our second main result we note that the limiting cases for
\alpha=0

\beta=0

defined by

where

GIG laws and the characterization

Denote by
\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,a,b)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a,b>0
, the generalized inverse Gaussian distribution. It is defined by the density

f(x)=\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^{\lambda/2}\,\tfrac{1}{2K_{\lambda}(2\sqrt{ab})}\,x^{\lambda-1}\,\exp\left(-ax-\tfrac{b}{x}\right)\,I_{(0,\infty)}(x),

where
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the third kind, see (
51
).

The following reciprocity property of the GIG distribution is well known and easily seen: if a random variable
X
has the
\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,a,b)
distribution, then the distribution of
X^{-1}
is
\mathrm{GIG}(-\lambda,b,a)
. A less known property of GIG is the form of the extended Laplace transform defined in (
4
), which has the form

L_{X}(s,\sigma,\theta)=\left(\tfrac{b-\theta}{a-\sigma}\right)^{\tfrac{\lambda+s}{2}}\,\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^{\tfrac{\lambda}{2}}\,\tfrac{K_{\lambda+s}(2\sqrt{(a-\sigma)(b-\theta)})}{K_{\lambda}(2\sqrt{ab})}

(5)

for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\sigma<a
and
\theta<b
.
Note that
L_{X}(0,\sigma,\theta)
as above with
s=0
uniquely determines the law
X\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda,a,b)
.
Actually
L(s,\theta,\sigma)
of the above form for any fixed
s
determines the GIG distribution as shown by the next proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that there exists
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

L_{X}(s,\theta,\sigma)=c\left(\tfrac{b-\sigma}{a-\theta}\right)^{\tfrac{\nu}{2}}\,K_{\nu}(2\sqrt{(a-\theta)(b-\sigma)}),\hskip 10.00002pt\theta<a,\;\sigma<b

for some constants
a,b,c>0
. Then
X\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\nu-s,a,b)
.

Proof.

Take
\tilde{a}<a
and
\tilde{b}<b
. Consider a random variable
Y
with the distribution

{\mathbb{P}}_{Y}(dx)=\tfrac{x^{s}e^{\tilde{a}x+\frac{\tilde{b}}{x}}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{X}(dx)}{L_{X}(s,\tilde{a},\tilde{b})}.

Then for
\theta<a-\tilde{a}
and
\sigma<b-\tilde{b}
we have

L_{Y}(0,\theta,\sigma)=\tfrac{L_{X}(s,\tilde{a}+\theta,\tilde{b}+\sigma)}{L_{X}(s,\tilde{a},\tilde{b})}=\left(\tfrac{(b-\tilde{b}-\sigma)(a-\tilde{a})}{(a-\tilde{a}-\theta)(b-\tilde{b})}\right)^{\nu/2}\,\tfrac{K_{\nu}(2\sqrt{(a-\tilde{a}-\theta)(b-\tilde{b}-\sigma)})}{K_{\nu}(2\sqrt{(a-\tilde{a})(b-\tilde{b})})}.

Comparing this form with (
5
) we conclude that
Y\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\nu,a-\tilde{a},b-\tilde{b})
. Consequently,

{\mathbb{P}}_{X}(dx)\propto\tfrac{1}{x^{s}e^{\tilde{a}x+\frac{\tilde{b}}{x}}}\,\,{\mathbb{P}}_{Y}(dx)=\left(\tfrac{b-\tilde{b}}{a-\tilde{a}}\right)^{\nu/2}\,\tfrac{x^{\nu-s-1}\,\exp\left(-ax-\tfrac{b}{x}\right)}{K_{\nu}(2\sqrt{(a-\tilde{a})(b-\tilde{b})})}.

Thus,
{\mathbb{P}}_{X}(dx)\propto x^{\nu-s-1}\,\exp\left(-ax-\tfrac{b}{x}\right)
and the result follows.
∎

\widetilde{v}_{s}=\widetilde{y}_{s}
we obtain
A\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda^{\prime},\,\alpha\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\,\theta^{\prime}_{0})
and
S\sim\mathrm{GIG}(\lambda^{\prime},\alpha\theta^{\prime}_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{0})
for some
\sigma_{0}^{\prime},\theta_{0}^{\prime}>0
and
\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Consequently, there exist constants
C_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
, which may depend on
s
such that

\displaystyle x_{-s}=C_{1}\left(\tfrac{\theta_{0}^{\prime}-\theta}{\sigma_{0}^{\prime}-\sigma}\right)^{(\lambda^{\prime}-s)/2}\,K_{\lambda^{\prime}-s}\left(2\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma^{\prime}_{0}-\sigma)(\theta^{\prime}_{0}-\theta)}\right),

\displaystyle y_{s}=C_{2}\left(\tfrac{\theta_{0}-\theta}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\right)^{(\lambda+s)/2}\,K_{\lambda+s}\left(2\sqrt{\beta(\sigma_{0}-\sigma)(\theta_{0}-\theta)}\right),

\displaystyle u_{-s}=C_{3}\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{0}^{\prime}-\sigma}{\theta_{0}^{\prime}-\theta}\right)^{(\lambda^{\prime}-s)/2}\,K_{\lambda^{\prime}-s}\left(2\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma^{\prime}_{0}-\sigma)(\theta^{\prime}_{0}-\theta)}\right),

\displaystyle v_{s}=C_{4}\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}{\theta_{0}-\theta}\right)^{(\lambda+s)/2}\,K_{\lambda+s}\left(2\sqrt{\beta(\sigma_{0}-\sigma)(\theta_{0}-\theta)}\right).

Thus (
17
) implies

C_{1}C_{2}\,\left(\tfrac{\theta_{0}^{\prime}-\theta}{\sigma_{0}^{\prime}-\sigma}\right)^{(\lambda^{\prime}-s)/2}\,\left(\tfrac{\theta_{0}-\theta}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\right)^{(\lambda+s)/2}=C_{3}C_{4}\,\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{0}^{\prime}-\sigma}{\theta_{0}^{\prime}-\theta}\right)^{(\lambda^{\prime}-s)/2}\,\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}{\theta_{0}-\theta}\right)^{(\lambda+s)/2},

for all
\sigma<0
,
\theta<0
. Consequently,
\lambda+s=-(\lambda^{\prime}-s)
, i.e.
\lambda=-\lambda^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\theta_{0}=\theta^{\prime}_{0}:=c_{2}
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma^{\prime}_{0}:=c_{1}
. Thus the result follows.

5
GMY property for GIG matrices

Let
\Omega
be the Euclidean space of symmetric
r\times r
matrices with real entries and the inner product defined by
\langle x,y\rangle=\;\mathrm{tr}\,(xy)
,
x,y\in\Omega
. Denote by
\Omega_{+}
the cone of symmetric positive definite
r\times r
matrices. We say that an
\Omega_{+}
-valued random matrix
X
has a matrix GIG law,
\mathrm{MGIG}(p,a,b)
, for
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
a,b\in\Omega_{+}
if it has the density with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dx
in
\Omega
of the form

\mu_{p,a,b}(x)=\tfrac{1}{K_{p}(a,b)}\,(\det\,x)^{p-\tfrac{r+1}{2}}\,\exp\left(-\tfrac{\;\mathrm{tr}\,(ax)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,(bx^{-1})}{2}\right)\,I_{\Omega_{+}}(x),

see e.g. Sec. 2 of Letac and Wesołowski (2000).

For
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
we recall the map
F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
on
\Omega_{+}\times\Omega_{+}
defined in the introduction by the formula

F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,y)=\left(y(I+\alpha xy)^{-1}(I+\beta xy),\,x(I+\beta yx)^{-1}(I+\alpha yx)\right),

(56)

where
I
denotes the identity matrix. Note that for
\alpha=0
,
\beta>0
and
\alpha>0
,
\beta=0
the problem we discuss reduces to the matrix variate version of the original Matsumoto-Yor property given in Letac and Wesołowski (2000). Moreover,
F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\alpha)}
is just the identity. Therefore, we are not interested in these cases in the sequel.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every distinct
\alpha,\beta>0
the map
F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a differentiable involution on
\Omega_{+}\times\Omega_{+}
. Moreover, its Jacobian equals 1.

Proof.

For distinct
\alpha,\beta>0
we denote
F:=F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
. For
x,y\in\Omega_{+}
set
(u,v)=F(x,y)
. Note that

y(I+\alpha xy)^{-1}=(I+\alpha yx)^{-1}y\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pty(I+\beta xy)=(I+\beta yx)y.

Combining these two identities we get

u=y(I+\alpha xy)^{-1}(I+\beta xy)=(I+\alpha yx)^{-1}y(I+\beta xy)=(I+\alpha yx)^{-1}(I+\beta yx)y.

But the right hand side above equals
u^{T}
, i.e.
u
is symmetric. Similarly, we conclude that
v
is symmetric.
Since
u
and
v
are products of positive definite matrices, they are also positive definite.
Consequently,
F(\Omega_{+}\times\Omega_{+})\subset\Omega_{+}\times\Omega_{+}
.
Note that
v^{T}=(I+\alpha xy)(I+\beta xy)^{-1}x=(I+\beta xy)^{-1}(I+\alpha xy)x
. Since
v=v^{T}
we get

uv=yx.

(57)

Plugging (
57
) or its transpose to definitions of
u
and
v
we get

u=y(I+\alpha vu)^{-1}(I+\beta vu)\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003ptv=x(I+\beta uv)^{-1}(I+\alpha uv)

whence

x=v(I+\alpha uv)^{-1}(I+\beta uv)\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pty=u(I+\beta vu)^{-1}(I+\alpha vu).

Thus
F
is an involution, i.e.
F=F^{-1}
. Differentiability on
\Omega_{+}\times\Omega_{+}
is clear.

To compute the Jacobian
J_{F}
of
F
we first note that
F=\phi^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\phi
, where
\phi(x,y)=(x,y^{-1})
and

\psi(x,y)=\left(y^{-1}(y+\beta x)(y+\alpha x)^{-1},\;x^{-1}(y+\beta x)(y+\alpha x)^{-1}\right)=:(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}).

(58)

Thus

J_{F}(x,y)=J_{\phi^{-1}}(x,y)J_{\psi}(x,y^{-1})J_{\phi}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}).

Note that

u^{\prime}=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}\,y^{-1}+\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}(y+\alpha x)^{-1}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003ptv^{\prime}=x^{-1}-(\alpha-\beta)(y+\alpha x)^{-1}.

Recall that the derivative
D_{x}(x^{-1})
is
-{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}
, where
{\mathbb{P}}_{a}
is the endomorphism on
\Omega
defined by
{\mathbb{P}}_{a}(h)=aha
,
h\in\Omega
. Consequently, the derivatives
D_{x}
and
D_{y}
of
u^{\prime}
are

D_{x}(u^{\prime})=-(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003ptD_{y}(u^{\prime})=-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}-\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}.

Similarly, the derivatives of
v^{\prime}
are

D_{x}(v^{\prime})=-{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}+(\alpha-\beta)\alpha{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003ptD_{y}(v^{\prime})=(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}.

For clarity we write
\det x
for the determinant of
x\in\Omega
and
\mathrm{Det}\,M
for the determinant of the endomorphism
M
on
\Omega
or
\Omega^{2}
. For example, in the sequel we will need the formula

\mathrm{Det}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x}=(\det\,x)^{r+1},

(59)

see e.g. Faraut and Koranyi (1994), p. 52. Thus

\displaystyle J_{\psi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Det}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}&-\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}-\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\\
&\\
-{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}+(\alpha-\beta)\alpha{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}&(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Det}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}&\ \ -\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}-\tfrac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\\
&\\
-{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}&\ \ -\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}\end{array}\right].

Using the Cholesky decomposition we thus get

\displaystyle J_{\psi}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Det}\,\left\{(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Det}\left\{-\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+(\alpha-\beta)\,{\mathbb{P}}_{(y+\alpha x)^{-1}}\right)\right\}.

Using the fact that
{\mathbb{P}}_{a^{-1}}={\mathbb{P}}_{a}^{-1}
we see that the operator under the second determinant has the form

\displaystyle-\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}\left(\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+(\alpha-\beta)\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x^{-1}}\left[-\alpha\beta(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{x}+\beta\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}+(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{y}\right]{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}

For
x,y\in\Omega
denote by
\mathbb{L}_{x,y}
the endomorphism of
\Omega
defined by
\mathbb{L}_{x,y}(h)=xhy+yhx
,
h\in\Omega
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}={\mathbb{P}}_{y}+\alpha\mathbb{L}_{x,y}+\alpha^{2}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}

whence

-\alpha\beta(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{x}+\beta\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}+(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{y}=-\alpha\beta(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{x}+\beta(\alpha^{2}{\mathbb{P}}_{x}+\alpha\mathbb{L}_{x,y}+{\mathbb{P}}_{y})+(\alpha-\beta){\mathbb{P}}_{y}=\alpha{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\beta x}.

Summing up, in view of (
59
), we obtain

J_{\psi}(x,y)=\tfrac{\mathrm{Det}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\beta x}}{\mathrm{Det}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y+\alpha x}\;\mathrm{Det}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{x}\;\mathrm{Det}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{y}}=\left(\tfrac{\det(y+\beta x)}{\det(y+\alpha x)\det(x)\det(y)}\right)^{r+1}.

Thus

J_{\psi}(x,y^{-1})=\left(\tfrac{\det(I+\beta xy)\det(y)}{\det(I+\alpha xy)\det(x)}\right)^{r+1}

Note that
J_{\phi}(x,y)=\mathrm{Det}\left\{-{\mathbb{P}}_{y^{-1}}\right\}=\tfrac{(-1)^{r}}{(\det\,y)^{r+1}}
. Therefore,

J_{F}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{(\det\,y)^{r+1}}\,\left(\tfrac{\det(I+\beta xy)\det(y)}{\det(I+\alpha xy)\det(x)}\right)^{r+1}\tfrac{1}{(\det(v^{\prime}(x,y^{-1})))^{r+1}}.

From (
58
) we have
\det\,v^{\prime}(x,y^{-1})=\tfrac{\det(I+\beta xy)}{\det(x)\,\det(I+\alpha xy)}
. Thus we conclude that
\left|J_{F}(x,y)\right|=1
.
∎

Now we are ready to formulate the main result of this section which gives a detailed balance equation of the generalized Matsumoto-Yor type satisfied by GIG random matrices.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha a,b)\otimes\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta b,a)
.
Then

(U,V)=F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(X,Y)\sim\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\alpha b,a)\otimes\mathrm{MGIG}(\lambda,\beta a,b).

Proof.

Consider the joint density
f
of
(U,V)
. Since the Jacobian of
F_{dK}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is 1. It follows that

\displaystyle f(u,v)

\displaystyle=f_{X}(x(u,v))f_{Y}(y(u,v))

\displaystyle\propto(\det(xy))^{\lambda-\tfrac{r+1}{2}}\,\exp\left(-\tfrac{\alpha\;\mathrm{tr}\,(ax)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,(bx^{-1})+\beta\;\mathrm{tr}\,(by)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,(ay^{-1})}{2}\right)

We now consider the exponent. Denoting
\widetilde{\alpha}=I+\alpha uv\in\Omega_{+}
,
\widetilde{\beta}=I+\beta uv\in\Omega_{+}
and using the fact that
\;\mathrm{tr}\,\,a=\;\mathrm{tr}\,\,a^{T}
we get

\displaystyle\alpha\;\mathrm{tr}\,(ax)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(bx^{-1}\right)+\beta\;\mathrm{tr}\,(by)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(ay^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(av\widetilde{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(b\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}v^{-1}\right)+\beta\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(bu\left(\widetilde{\beta}^{T}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}^{T}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(a\left(\widetilde{\alpha}^{T}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}^{T}u^{-1}\right).

Since

u\left(\widetilde{\beta}^{T}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}^{T}=\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}u\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\left(\widetilde{\alpha}^{T}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}^{T}u^{-1}=u^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}

We get

\displaystyle\alpha\;\mathrm{tr}\,(ax)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(bx^{-1}\right)+\beta\;\mathrm{tr}\,(by)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(ay^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(a\left(u^{-1}+\alpha v\right)\widetilde{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(b\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}\left(v^{-1}+\beta u\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(au^{-1}(I+\alpha uv)\widetilde{\alpha}^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(b\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}\widetilde{\alpha}(I+\beta uv)v^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(au^{-1}(I+\beta uv)\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(b(I+\alpha uv)v^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(au^{-1}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,(\beta av)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(bv^{-1}\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,(\alpha bu).

Combining this with (
57
) we get

f(u,v)\propto(\det(u))^{\lambda-\tfrac{r+1}{2}}\exp\left(-\tfrac{\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\alpha bu\right)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(au^{-1}\right)}{2}\right)\,(\det(v))^{\lambda-\tfrac{r+1}{2}}\,\exp\left(-\tfrac{\;\mathrm{tr}\,(\beta av)+\;\mathrm{tr}\,\left(bv^{-1}\right)}{2}\right),

which ends the proof.
∎

Bibliographical comments

Let us mention that the MY property and related characterization triggered a lot of further research developing in several directions: (1) more general algebraic structures as, a multivariate tree-generated version in Massam and Wesołowski (2004), matrix variate versions in Letac and Wesołowski (2000), Wesołowski (2002), Massam and Wesołowski (2006), a combination of the matrix variate and multivariate tree-generated setting in Bobecka (2015), symmetric cone variate in Kołodziejek (2017), a version in free probability in Szpojankowski (2017); (2) characterizations based on a weaker assumption of constancy of regressions of moments of
T
given
S
instead of the assumption of independence of
S
and
T
- in univariate case in Wesołowski (2002), Chou and Wang (2004) and in free probability in Szpojankowski (2017) and Świeca (2021); (3) a search of more general maps of the form
\psi_{f}(a,b)=(f(a+b),\,f(a)-f(a+b))
and product measures
\mu\otimes\nu
, such that
\psi_{f}(\mu\otimes\nu)
remains a product measure and characterization of respective
\mu
and
\nu
by the independence property in Koudou and Vallois (2012) with a genuine special case of
\mu
and
\nu
being the Kummer and gamma distributions, see also Koudou and Vallois (2011), Koudou (2011), Wesołowski (2015), Piliszek and Wesołowski (2016), Kołodziejek (2018). For a survey on characterizations of the GIG distribution and other refrences (up to 2014) see Koudou and Ley (2014).

A Tighter Analysis of Spectral Clustering, and Beyond A preliminary version of this work appeared at ICML 2022. This work is supported by a Langmuir PhD Scholarship, and an EPSRC Early Career Fellowship (EP/T00729X/1).
Peter Macgregor, He Sun

Abstract
This work studies the classical spectral clustering algorithm which embeds the vertices of some graph G = ( V G , E G ) G=(V_{G},E_{G}) into ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} using k k eigenvectors of some matrix of G G , and applies k k -means to partition V G V_{G} into k k clusters. Our first result is a tighter analysis on the performance of spectral clustering, and explains why it works under some much weaker condition than the ones studied in the literature. For the second result, we show that, by applying fewer than k k eigenvectors to construct the embedding, spectral clustering is able to produce better output for many practical instances; this result is the first of its kind in spectral clustering. Besides its conceptual and theoretical significance, the practical impact of our work is demonstrated by the empirical analysis on both synthetic and real-world datasets, in which spectral clustering produces comparable or better results with fewer than k k eigenvectors.

Introduction

Graph clustering is a fundamental problem in unsupervised learning, and has comprehensive applications in computer science and related scientific fields. Among various techniques to solve graph clustering problems, spectral clustering is probably the easiest one to implement, and has been widely applied in practice. Spectral clustering can be easily described as follows: for any graph
{G}=({V}_{G},{E}_{G})
and some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
as input, spectral clustering embeds the vertices of
{V}_{G}
into
\mathbb{R}^{k}
based on the bottom
k
eigenvectors of the Laplacian matrix of
{G}
, and employs
k
-means on the embedded points to partition
{V}_{G}
into
k
clusters. Thanks to its simplicity and excellent performance in practice, spectral clustering has been widely applied over the past three decades
[
ST96
]
.

In this work we study spectral clustering, and present two results.
Our first result is a tighter analysis of spectral clustering for well-clustered graphs.
Informally, we analyse the performance guarantee of spectral clustering under a simple assumption
1

1

This assumption will be formally defined in Section
3
.
on the input graph. While all the previous work (e.g.,
[
LGT14
,
KM16
,
Miz21
,
PSZ17
]
) on the same problem suggests that the assumption on the input graph must depend on
k
, our result demonstrates that the performance of spectral clustering can be rigorously analysed under a general condition independent of
k
.
To the best of our knowledge, our work presents the first result of its kind, and hence we believe that this result and the novel analysis used in its proof are important, and might have further applications in graph clustering.

Secondly, we study the clustering problem in which the crossing edges between the optimal clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
present some noticeable pattern, which we call the
meta-graph
in this work. Notice that, when viewing every cluster
{S}_{i}
as a “giant vertex”, our meta-graph captures the intrinsic connection between the optimal clusters, and could be significantly different from a clique graph. We prove that, when this is the case, one can simply apply classical spectral clustering while employing fewer than
k
eigenvectors to construct the embedding and, surprisingly, this will produce a better clustering result.
The significance of this result is further demonstrated by our extensive experimental analysis on the well-known BSDS, MNIST, and USPS datasets

Preliminaries

Let
{G}=({V}_{G},{E}_{G},{w})
be an undirected graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges, and weight function
{w}:{V}_{G}\times{V}_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
For any edge
e=\{u,v\}\in{E}_{G}
, we write the weight of
\{u,v\}
by
{w}_{uv}
or
{w}_{e}
.
For a vertex
u\in{V}_{G}
, we denote its
degree
by
{d}_{G}(u)\triangleq\sum_{v\in{V}}{w}_{uv}
.
For any two sets
{S},{T}\subset{V}_{G}
, we define the
cut value

{w}({S},{T})\triangleq\sum_{e\in{E}_{G}({S},{T})}{w}_{e}
, where
{E}_{G}({S},{T})
is the set of edges between
{S}
and
{T}
.
For any set
{S}\subseteq{V}_{G}
, the
volume
of
{S}
is
\mathrm{vol}_{G}({S})\triangleq\sum_{u\in{S}}{d}_{G}(u)
,
and we write
\mathrm{vol}({G})
when referring to
\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})
. For any nonempty subset
{S}\subseteq{V}_{G}
,
we define the
conductance
of
{S}
by

\Phi_{G}({S})\triangleq\frac{{w}({S},{V}\setminus{S})}{\mathrm{vol}_{G}({S})}.

Furthermore, we define the conductance of the graph
{G}
by

\Phi_{G}\triangleq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}{S}\subset{V}\\
\mathrm{vol}({S})\leq\mathrm{vol}({V})/2\end{subarray}}\Phi_{G}({S}).

We call subsets of vertices
{A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{k}
a
k-way partition
of
{G}
if
{A}_{i}\cap{A}_{j}=\emptyset
for different
i
and
j
, and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}{A}_{i}={V}
. Generalising the definition of conductance, we define
k
-way expansion constant
by

\rho(k)\triangleq\min_{\mathrm{partition}\ {A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{k}}\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\Phi_{G}({A}_{i}).

Next we define the matrices of any
{G}=({V}_{G},{E}_{G},{w})
. Let
{D}_{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the diagonal matrix defined by
({D}_{G})_{uu}={d}_{G}(u)
for all
u\in{V}_{G}
, and we denote by
{A}_{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
the
adjacency matrix
of
{G}
, where
({A}_{G})_{uv}={w}_{uv}
for all
u,v\in{V}_{G}
.
The
normalised Laplacian matrix
of
{G}
is defined by
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\triangleq{I}-{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{G}{A}_{G}{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{G}
, where
{I}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. Since
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}
is symmetric and real-valued, it has
n
real eigenvalues denoted by
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}
; we use
{f}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to denote the eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n
. It is known that
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{n}\leq 2

[
Chu97
]
.

For any sets
{S}
and
{T}
, the symmetric difference between
{S}
and
{T}
is defined by

{S}\triangle{T}=({S}\setminus{T})\cup({T}\setminus{S}).

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define
[k]\triangleq\{1,\ldots,k\}
. We sometimes drop the subscript
{G}
when it is clear from the context.
The following higher-order Cheeger inequality will be used in our analysis.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
LGT14
]
)
.

It holds for any
k\in[n]
that

\lambda_{k}/2\leq\rho(k)\leq O\left(k^{3}\right)\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}.

3
Encoding the Cluster-Structure into the Eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}

Let
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be any optimal
k
-way partition that achieves
\rho(k)
. We define the indicator vector of cluster
{S}_{i}
by

{\chi}_{i}(u)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }u\in{S}_{i},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

and the corresponding
normalised indicator vector
by

\bar{{g}}_{i}\triangleq\frac{{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\chi}_{i}}{\|{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\chi}_{i}\|}.

One of the basic results in spectral graph theory states that
{G}
consists of at least
k
connected components if and only if
\lambda_{i}=0
for any
i\in[k]
, and

\mathrm{span}\left(\{{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\right)=\mathrm{span}\left(\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\right)

[
Chu97
]
.
Hence, one would expect that, when
{G}
consists of
k
densely connected components (clusters) connected by sparse cuts, the bottom eigenvectors
\{{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}
are close to
\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
. This intuition explains the practical success of spectral methods for graph clustering, and forms the basis of many theoretical studies on various
spectral clustering algorithms (e.g.,
[
KLL+13
,
LGT14
,
NJW01
,
vL07
]
).

Turning this intuition into a mathematical statement, Peng et al.
[
PSZ17
]
study the quantitative relationship between
\{{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
through the function
\Upsilon(k)
defined by

\Upsilon(k)\triangleq\frac{\lambda_{k+1}}{\rho(k)}.

(3.2)

To explain the meaning of
\Upsilon(k)
, we assume that
{G}
has
k
well-defined clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
. By definition, the values of
\Phi({S}_{i})
for every
{S}_{i}
, as well as
\rho(k)
, are low;
on the other hand, any
(k+1)
-way partition of
{V}_{G}
would separate the vertices of some
{S}_{i}
, and as such
\rho(k+1)
’s value will be high.
Combining this with the higher-order Cheeger inequality, some lower bound on
\Upsilon(k)
would be sufficient to ensure that
{G}
has exactly
k
clusters.
In their work, Peng et al.
[
PSZ17
]
assumes
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega(k^{2})
, and proves that the space spanned by
\{{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and the one spanned by
\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are close to each other.
Specifically, they show that

1.

every
\bar{{g}}_{i}
is close to some linear combination of
\{{f}_{i}\}^{k}_{i=1}
, denoted by
\widehat{{f}}_{i}
, i.e., it holds that

\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1/\Upsilon(k);

2.

every
{f}_{i}
is close to some linear combination of
\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, denoted by
\widehat{{g}}_{i}
, i.e., it holds that

\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1.1k/\Upsilon(k).

In essence, their so-called structure theorem gives a quantitative explanation on why spectral methods work for graph clustering
when there is a clear cluster-structure in
{G}
characterised by
\Upsilon(k)
.
As it holds for graphs with clusters of different sizes and edge densities, this structure theorem has been shown to be a powerful tool in analysing clustering algorithms, and inspired many subsequent works (e.g.,
[
CSWZ16
,
CPS15
,
KUK17
,
KM16
,
LV19
,
Miz21
,
Pen20
,
PY20
,
SZ19
]
).

In this section we show that a stronger statement of the original structure theorem holds under a much weaker assumption. Our result is summarised as follows:

Theorem 1

(The Stronger Structure Theorem)
.

The following statements hold:

1.

For any
i\in[k]
, there is
\widehat{{f}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is
a linear combination of
{f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{k}
, such that

\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1/\Upsilon(k).

2.

There are vectors
\widehat{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{{g}}_{k}
, each of which is a linear combination of
\bar{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{k}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq k/\Upsilon(k).

Proof.

Let
\widehat{{f}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}
, and we write
\bar{{g}}_{i}
as a linear combination of the vectors
{f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{n}
by

\bar{{g}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}
.
Since
\widehat{{f}}_{i}
is a projection of
\bar{{g}}_{i}
, we have that
\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}
is perpendicular to
\widehat{{f}}_{i}
and

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}.

Now, let us consider the quadratic form

\displaystyle\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathcal{N}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}^{\intercal}\right)\mathcal{L}_{G}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{k+1}\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2},

(3.3)

where the last inequality follows by the fact that
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
holds for any
1\leq i\leq n
. This gives us that

\displaystyle\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in{E}_{G}}{w}(u,v)\left(\frac{\bar{{g}}_{i}(u)}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}-\frac{\bar{{g}}_{i}(v)}{\sqrt{{d}(v)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in{E}_{G}}{w}(u,v)\left(\frac{{\chi}_{i}(u)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}-\frac{{\chi}_{i}(v)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{w}({S}_{i},{V}\setminus{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(k).

(3.4)

Combining (
3.3
) with (
3.4
), we have that

\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}}{\lambda_{k+1}}\leq\frac{\rho(k)}{\lambda_{k+1}}\leq\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)},

which proves the first statement of the theorem.

Now we prove the second statement.
We define for any
1\leq i\leq k
that

\widehat{{g}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{j}
, and have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\|{f}_{i}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=k-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\bar{{g}}_{j},{f}_{i}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle\bar{{g}}_{j},{f}_{i}\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\left\|\bar{{g}}_{j}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\bar{{g}}_{j}-\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the last inequality follows by the first statement of Theorem
1
.
∎

To examine the significance of Theorem
1
, we first highlight that these two statements hold for any
\Upsilon(k)
, while the original structure theorem relies on the assumption that
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega(k^{2})
. Since
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega(k^{2})
is a strong and even questionable assumption when
k
is large, e.g.,
k=\Omega\!\left(\mathrm{polylog}(n)\right)
, obtaining these statements for general
\Upsilon(k)
is important.
Secondly, our second statement of Theorem
1
significantly improves the original theorem. Specifically, instead of stating
\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1.1k/\Upsilon(k)
for any
i\in[k]
, our second statement shows that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq k/\Upsilon(k)
;
hence, it holds in expectation that
\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq 1/\Upsilon(k)
, the upper bound of which matches the first statement.
This implies that the vectors
{f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{k}
and
\bar{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{k}
can be linearly approximated by each other with
roughly the same
approximation guarantee.
Thirdly, rather than employing the machinery from matrix analysis used by Peng et al.
[
PSZ17
]
,
to prove the original theorem, our proof is simple and purely linear-algebraic.
Therefore, we believe that both of our stronger statements and much simplified proof are significant, and could have further applications in graph clustering and related problems.

4
Tighter Analysis of Spectral Clustering

In this section, we analyse the spectral clustering algorithm.
For any input graph
{G}=({V}_{G},{E}_{G})
and
k\in[n]
, spectral clustering consists of the three steps below:

1.

compute the eigenvectors
{f}_{1},\ldots{f}_{k}
of
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}
, and embed each
u\in{V}_{G}
to the point
F(u)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
according to

F(u)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}\left({f}_{1}(u),\ldots,{f}_{k}(u)\right)^{\intercal};

(4.1)

2.

apply
k
-means on the embedded points
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
;

3.

partition
{V}_{G}
into
k
clusters based on the output of
k
-means.

We will consider spectral clustering for graphs with clusters of
almost-balanced
size.

Definition 1
.

Let
{G}
be a graph with
k
clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
.
We say that the clusters are almost-balanced if

(1/2)\cdot\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})/k\leq\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})/k

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

Our main result is given in Theorem
2
, where we take
APT
to be the approximation ratio of the
k
-means algorithm used in spectral clustering.
Recall that we can take
APT
to be some small constant
[
KSS04
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{G}
be a graph with
k
clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of almost balanced size, and

\Upsilon(k)\geq 2176(1+\textsf{APT})
.
Let
\{{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be the output of spectral clustering and, without loss of generality, the optimal correspondent of
{A}_{i}
is
{S}_{i}
. Then, it holds that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{vol}\left({A}_{i}\triangle{S}_{i}\right)\leq 2176~(1+\textsf{APT})~\frac{\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Notice that some condition on
\Upsilon(k)
is needed
to ensure that an input graph
{G}
has
k
well-defined clusters, so that misclassified vertices can be formally defined.
Taking this into account, the most significant feature of Theorem
2
is its upper bound of misclassified vertices with respect to
\Upsilon(k)
: our result holds, and is non-trivial, as long as

\Upsilon(k)
is lower bounded by some constant
2
2

2

Note that we can take any constant approximation in Definition
1
with a different corresponding constant in Theorem
2
.
.
This significantly improves most of the previous results of graph clustering algorithms, which make stronger assumptions on the input graphs. For example, Peng et al.
[
PSZ17
]
assumes that
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega\!\left(k^{3}\right)
, Mizutani
[
Miz21
]
assumes that
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega\!\left(k\right)
, the algorithm presented in Gharan and Trevisan
[
GT14
]
assumes that
\lambda_{k+1}=\Omega\!\left(\mathrm{poly}(k)\lambda^{1/4}_{k}\right)
, and the one presented in Dey et al.
[
DPRS19
]
further assumes some condition with respect to
k
,
\lambda_{k}
, and the maximum degree of
{G}
.
While these assumptions require at least a linear dependency on
k
, making it difficult for the instances with a large value of
k
to satisfy, our result suggests that the performance of spectral clustering can be rigorously analysed for these graphs.
In particular, compared with previous work, our result better justifies the widely used eigen-gap heuristic for spectral clustering
[
NJW01
,
vL07
]
.
This heuristic suggests that spectral clustering works when the value of
|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}|
is much larger than
|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1}|
, and in practice, the ratio between the two gaps is usually a constant rather than some function of
k
.

4.1
Properties of Spectral Embedding

Now we study the properties of the spectral embedding defined in (
4.1
), and show in the next subsection how to use these properties to prove Theorem
2
.
For every cluster
{S}_{i}
, we define the vector
{p}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
by

{p}^{(i)}(j)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}\langle{f}_{j},\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle,

and view these
\{{p}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{k}
as the approximate centres of the embedded points from the optimal clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
.
We prove that the total
k
-means cost of the embedded points can be upper bounded as follows:

Lemma 4.1
.

It holds that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{{f}_{j}(u)}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}-\frac{\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left({f}_{j}(u)-\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{i}(u)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left({f}_{j}(u)-\widehat{{g}}_{j}(u)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|{f}_{j}-\widehat{{g}}_{j}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the final inequality follows by the second statement of Theorem
1
and it holds for
u\in{S}_{x}
that
\widehat{{g}}_{i}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{j}(u)=\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{x}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{x}(u)
.
∎

The importance of Lemma
4.1
is that, although the optimal centres for
k
-means are unknown, the existence of
\{{p}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is sufficient to show that the cost of an optimal
k
-means clustering on
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
is at most
k/\Upsilon(k)
.
Since one can always use an
O(1)
-approximate
k
-means algorithm for spectral clustering (e.g.,
[
KMN+04
,
KSS04
]
), the cost of the output of
k
-means on
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
is
O\left(k/\Upsilon(k)\right)
.
Next, we show that the
length of
{p}^{(i)}
is approximately equal to

1/\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})
,
which will be useful in our later analysis.

Lemma 4.2
.

It holds for any
i\in[k]
that

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)\leq\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{j},\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle^{2}=\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}=1-\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{i}-\bar{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\geq 1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the inequality follows by Theorem
1
. The other direction of the inequality follows similarly.
∎

In the remainder of this subsection, we will prove a sequence of lemmas showing that any pair of
{p}^{(i)}
and
{p}^{(j)}
are well separated.
Moreover, notice that their distance is essentially independent of
k
and
\Upsilon(k)
, as long as
\Upsilon(k)\geq 20
.

Lemma 4.3
.

It holds for any different
i,j\in[k]
that

\left\|\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\cdot{p}^{(i)}-\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\cdot{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}\geq 2-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\cdot{p}^{(i)}-\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\cdot{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\left(\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle-\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle^{2}\right)+\left(\sum_{x=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)-2\sum_{x=1}^{k}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\|^{2}-2\left\lvert\widehat{{f}}_{i}^{\intercal}\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-2\left\lvert(\bar{{g}}_{i}+\widehat{{f}}_{i}-\bar{{g}}_{i})^{\intercal}(\bar{{g}}_{j}+\widehat{{f}}_{j}-\bar{{g}}_{j})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=2\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-2\left(\left\lvert\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},\widehat{{f}}_{j}-\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle+\langle\bar{{g}}_{j},\widehat{{f}}_{i}-\bar{{g}}_{i}\rangle+\langle\widehat{{f}}_{i}-\bar{{g}}_{i},\widehat{{f}}_{j}-\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-6\cdot\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}

\displaystyle\geq 2-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}.\qed

Lemma 4.4
.

It holds for any different
i,j\in[k]
that

\left\|\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\|^{2}\geq 2-\frac{20}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that

\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|.

Let
{a}_{i}=\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}{p}^{(i)}
and
{a}_{j}=\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}{p}^{(j)}
and notice that
\left\|{a}_{i}\right\|\leq\left\|{a}_{j}\right\|\leq 1
.
Then, using Lemma
4.2
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|{a}_{i}-\frac{\left\|{a}_{i}\right\|}{\left\|{a}_{j}\right\|}{a}_{j}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|{a}_{i}-{a}_{j}\right\|-\left(\left\|{a}_{j}\right\|-\left\|{a}_{i}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}}-\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\cdot\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|-\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}\left(1-\frac{4}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)+\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}}-1

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{\Upsilon(k)}-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{2}-\frac{7}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the second inequality follows by the triangle inequality, and the third and fourth use Lemma
4.3
. We also use the fact that for
x\leq 1
, it is the case that
\sqrt{1-x}\geq 1-x
.
This gives

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2-\frac{14\sqrt{2}}{\Upsilon(k)}\geq 2-\frac{20}{\Upsilon(k)},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

It holds for any
i,j\in[k]
with
i\neq j
that

\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right).

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\geq\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|
. Then, let
\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|=\alpha\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
By Lemma
4.2
, it holds that

\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\|}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right).

Additionally, notice that by the proof of Lemma
4.4
,

\left\langle\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|},\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rangle\leq\sqrt{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\frac{7}{2\Upsilon(k)},

where we use the fact that if
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
, then
x+y\leq\sqrt{2}
.
One can understand the equation above by considering the right-angled triangle with one edge given by
{p}^{(i)}/\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|
and another edge given by
({p}^{(i)}/\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|).({p}^{(j)}/\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|)
.
Then,

\displaystyle\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}-2\left\langle\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|},\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rangle\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq(1+\alpha)\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}-\left(\sqrt{2}+\frac{7}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)\alpha\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-(\sqrt{2}-1)\alpha-\frac{7}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{7}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

We remark that, despite the similarity in their formulation, the technical lemmas presented in this subsection are stronger than the ones in Peng et al.
[
PSZ17
]
. These results are obtained through our stronger structure theorem (Theorem
1
), and are crucial for us to prove Theorem
2
.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2

In this subsection, we prove
Theorem
2
,
and explain why a mild condition like
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega\!\left(1\right)
suffices for spectral clustering to perform well in practice.
Let
\{{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be the output of spectral clustering, and we denote the centre of the embedded points
\{F(u)\}
for any
{A}_{i}
by
{c}_{i}
.
As the starting point of our analysis, we claim that every
{c}_{i}
will be close to its “optimal” correspondent
{p}^{(\sigma(i))}
for some
\sigma(i)\in[k]
. That is, the actual centre of embedded points from every
{A}_{i}
is close to the approximate centre of the embedded points from some optimal
{S}_{i}
.
To formalise this, we define the function
\sigma:[k]\rightarrow[k]
by

\sigma(i)=\argmin_{j\in[k]}\left\|{p}^{(j)}-{c}_{i}\right\|;

(4.2)

that is, cluster
{A}_{i}
should correspond to
{S}_{\sigma(i)}
in which the value of
\|{p}^{(\sigma(i))}-{c}_{i}\|
is the lowest among all the distances between
{c}_{i}
and all of the
{p}^{(j)}
for
j\in[k]
.
However, one needs to be cautious as (
4.2
) wouldn’t necessarily define a permutation, and there might exist different
i,i^{\prime}\in[k]
such that both of
{A}_{i}
and
{A}_{i^{\prime}}
map to the same
{S}_{\sigma(i)}
.
Taking this into account, for any fixed
\sigma:[k]\rightarrow[k]
and
i\in[k]
, we further define
{M}_{\sigma,i}
by

{M}_{\sigma,i}\triangleq\bigcup_{j:\sigma(j)=i}{A}_{j}.

(4.3)

The following lemma shows that, when mapping every output
{A}_{i}
to
{S}_{\sigma(i)}
, the total ratio of misclassified volume with respect to each cluster can be upper bounded:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\{{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be the output of spectral clustering, and
\sigma
and
{M}_{\sigma,i}
be defined as in (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
If
\Upsilon(k)\geq 32
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\leq 64(1+\textsf{APT})\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Proof.

Let us define
{B}_{ij}={A}_{i}\cap{S}_{j}
to be the vertices in
{A}_{i}
which belong to the true cluster
{S}_{j}
.
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}^{k}\mathrm{vol}({B}_{ij})\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{\sigma(i)})}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({B}_{ij})}{\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{\sigma(i)}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\}},

(4.4)

and that

\displaystyle\mathrm{COST}({A}_{1},\ldots{A}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{A}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{c}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in{B}_{ij}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{c}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in{B}_{ij}}{d}(u)\left(\frac{\left\|{p}^{(j)}-{c}_{i}\right\|^{2}}{2}-\left\|{p}^{(j)}-F(u)\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in{B}_{ij}}\frac{{d}(u)\left\|{p}^{(j)}-{p}^{(\sigma(i))}\right\|^{2}}{8}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in{B}_{ij}}{d}(u)\left\|{p}^{(j)}-F(u)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}\mathrm{vol}({B}_{ij})\frac{\left\|{p}^{(j)}-{p}^{(\sigma(i))}\right\|^{2}}{8}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|{p}^{(i)}-F(u)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
j\neq\sigma(i)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({B}_{ij})}{8\min\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{\sigma(i)}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|{p}^{(i)}-F(u)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{16}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the second inequality follows by the inequality of

\left\|{a}-{b}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\left\|{b}-{c}\right\|^{2}}{2}-\left\|{a}-{c}\right\|^{2},

the third inequality follows since
{c}_{i}
is closer to
{p}^{(\sigma(i))}
than
{p}^{(j)}
, the fifth one follows from Lemma
4.5
, and the last one follows by (
4.4
).

On the other side, since
\mathrm{COST}({A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{k})\leq\textsf{APT}\cdot k/\Upsilon(k)
, we have that

\frac{1}{16}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)-\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}\leq\textsf{APT}\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq 16\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{8}{\Upsilon(k)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq 64\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)},

where the last inequality holds by the assumption that
\Upsilon(k)\geq 32
.
∎

It remains to study the case in which
\sigma
isn’t a permutation. Notice that, if this occurs, there is some
i\in[k]
such that
{M}_{\sigma,i}=\emptyset
, and different values of
x,y\in[k]
such that
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)=j
for some
j\neq i
.
Based on this, we construct the function
\sigma^{\prime}:[k]\rightarrow[k]
from
\sigma
based on the following procedure:

•

Set
\sigma^{\prime}(z)=i
if
z=x
;

•

Set
\sigma^{\prime}(z)=\sigma(z)
for any other
z\in[k]\setminus\{x\}
.

Notice that one can construct
\sigma^{\prime}
in this way as long as
\sigma
isn’t a permutation, and this constructed
\sigma^{\prime}
reduces the number of
{M}_{\sigma,i}
being
\emptyset
by one.
We show one only needs to construct such
\sigma^{\prime}
at most
O\!\left(k/\Upsilon(k)\right)
times
to obtain the final permutation called
\sigma^{\star}
, and it holds for
\sigma^{\star}
that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\star},i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}=O\!\left(\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}\right).

Combining this with the fact that
\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})=\Theta(\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})/k)
for any
i\in[k]
proves Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\mathsf{COST}({S}_{1},\ldots,{S}_{k})\leq\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Combining this with the fact that one can apply an approximate
k
-means clustering algorithm with approximation ratio
APT
for spectral clustering, we have that

\mathsf{COST}({A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{k})\leq\textsf{APT}\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Then, let
\sigma:[1,k]\rightarrow[1,k]
be the function which assigns the clusters
{A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{k}
to the ground truth clusters such that

\sigma(i)=\argmin_{j\in[k]}\left\|{p}^{(j)}-{c}_{i}\right\|.

Then, it holds by Lemma
4.6
that

\epsilon(\sigma)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\leq 64\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

(4.5)

Now, assume that
\sigma
is not a permutation, and we’ll apply the following procedure inductively to construct a permutation from
\sigma
. Since
\sigma
isn’t a permutation, there is
i\in[k]
such that
M_{\sigma,i}=\emptyset
. Therefore, there are different values of
x,y\in[k]
such that
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)=j
for some
j\neq i
. Based on this, we construct the function
\sigma^{\prime}:[k]\rightarrow[k]
such that
\sigma^{\prime}(z)=i
if
z=x
, and
\sigma^{\prime}(z)=\sigma(z)
for any other
z\in[k]\setminus\{x\}
. Notice that we can construct
\sigma^{\prime}
in this way as long as
\sigma
isn’t a permutation.
By the definition of
\epsilon(\cdot)
and function
\sigma^{\prime}
, the difference between
\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})
and
\epsilon(\sigma)
can be written as

\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})-\epsilon(\sigma)=\underbrace{\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}-\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\right)}_{=:\alpha}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},j}\triangle{S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}-\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\right)}_{=:\beta}.

(4.6)

Let us consider
4
cases based on the signs of
\alpha,\beta
defined above.
In each case, we bound the cost introduced by the change from
\sigma
to
\sigma^{\prime}
, and then consider the total cost introduced throughout the entire procedure of constructing a permutation.

Case 1:
\alpha<0,\beta<0
.

In this case, it is clear that
\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})-\epsilon(\sigma)\leq 0
, and hence the total introduced cost
is at most
0
.

Case 2:
\alpha>0,\beta<0
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})-\epsilon(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},i}\triangle{S}_{i})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})+\left\lvert\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},j}\triangle{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},i}\triangle{S}_{i})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})+\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},j}\triangle{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{i})-\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\cap{S}_{i})+\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\cap{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\cdot\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\cdot\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})},

where the last inequality follows by the fact that the clusters are almost balanced.
Since each set
{A}_{x}
is moved at most once in order to construct a permutation, the total introduced cost due to this case is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{{A}_{x}\in{M}_{\sigma,j}}\frac{8\cdot\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\leq 8\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\leq 512\cdot\left(1+\textsf{APT}\right)\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Case 3:
\alpha>0,\beta>0
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})-\epsilon(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},i}\triangle{S}_{i})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})+\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},j}\triangle{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{i})-\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\cap{S}_{i})+\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\cap{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}\left(2\cdot\mathrm{vol}({S}_{x}\cap{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\cdot\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}{\min(\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j}))}

\displaystyle\leq 8,

where the last inequality follows by the fact that the clusters are almost balanced.
We will consider the total introduced cost due to this case and Case 4 together, and so let’s first examine Case 4.

Case 4:
\alpha<0,\beta>0
.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma^{\prime})-\epsilon(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\left(\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\prime},j}\triangle{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,j}\triangle{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}\left(\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\cap{S}_{j})-\mathrm{vol}({A}_{x}\setminus{S}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}

\displaystyle=1.

Now, let us bound the total number of times we need to construct
\sigma^{\prime}
in order to obtain a permutation.
For any
i
with
{M}_{\sigma,i}=\emptyset
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}=1,

so the total number of required iterations is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\left\lvert\{i:{M}_{\sigma,i}=\emptyset\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma,i}\triangle{S}_{i})}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq 64\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

As such, the total introduced cost due to Cases 3 and 4 is at most

8\cdot 64\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}=512\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

Putting everything together, we have that

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon(\sigma)+1024\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}

\displaystyle\leq 1088\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{k}{\Upsilon(k)}.

This implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{vol}({M}_{\sigma^{\star},i}\triangle{S}_{i})\leq 2176\cdot(1+\textsf{APT})\cdot\frac{\mathrm{vol}({V}_{G})}{\Upsilon(k)}

and completes the proof.
∎

We remark that this method of upper bounding the ratio of misclassified vertices is very different from the ones used in previous references, e.g.,
[
DPRS19
,
Miz21
,
PSZ17
]
. In particular, instead of examining all the possible mappings between
\{{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
,
we directly work with some specifically defined function
\sigma
, and construct our desired mapping
\sigma^{\star}
from
\sigma
. This is another key for us to obtain stronger results than the previous work.

5
Beyond the Classical Spectral Clustering

In this section we propose a variant of spectral clustering which employs fewer than
k
eigenvectors to find
k
clusters. We prove that,
when the structure among the optimal clusters in an input graph satisfies certain conditions,
spectral clustering with fewer eigenvectors is able to produce better results than classical spectral clustering.
Our result gives a theoretical justification of the surprising showcase in Section
1
,
and presents a significant speedup on the runtime of spectral clustering in practice, since fewer eigenvectors are used to construct the embedding.

5.1
Encoding the Cluster-Structure into Meta-Graphs

Suppose that
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is a
k
-way partition of
{V}_{G}
for an input graph
{G}
that minimises the
k
-way expansion
\rho(k)
. We define the matrix
{A}_{{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
by

{A}_{M}(i,j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{w}({S}_{i},{S}_{j})&\mbox{if }i\neq j,\\
2{w}({S}_{i},{S}_{j})&\mbox{if }i=j\end{array}\right.

and, taking
{A}_{M}
to be the adjacency matrix, this
defines a graph
{M}=({V}_{M},{E}_{M},{w}_{M})
which we refer to as the
meta-graph
of the clusters.
We define the normalised adjacency matrix of
{M}
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{M}\triangleq{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{M}{A}_{M}{D}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

and the normalised Laplacian matrix of
{M}
by

{\mathcal{N}}_{M}\triangleq{I}-{\mathcal{A}}_{M}.

Let the eigenvalues of
{\mathcal{N}}_{M}
be
\gamma_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\gamma_{k}
, and
{g}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
be the eigenvector corresponding to
\gamma_{i}
for any
i\in[k]
.

The starting point of our novel approach is to look at the structure of the meta-graph
{M}
defined by
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of
{G}
, and study how the spectral information of
{\mathcal{N}}_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
is encoded in the bottom eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}
.
To achieve this,
for any
\ell\in[k]
and vertex
i\in{V}_{M}
, let

\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\triangleq\left({g}_{1}(i),\ldots,{g}_{\ell}(i)\right)^{\intercal};

(5.1)

notice that
\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
defines the spectral embedding of
i\in{V}_{M}
through the bottom
\ell
eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{N}}_{M}
.

Definition 2

(
(\theta,\ell)
-distinguishable graph)
.

For any
{M}=({V}_{M},{E}_{M},{w}_{M})
with
k
vertices,
\ell\in[k]
, and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we say that
{M}
is
(\theta,\ell)
-distinguishable if

•

it holds for any
i\in[k]
that
\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\geq\theta
, and

•

it holds for any different
i,j\in[k]
that

\left\|\frac{\bar{{x}}^{(i)}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{\bar{{x}}^{(j)}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\|^{2}\geq\theta.

In other words, graph
{M}
is
(\theta,\ell)
-distinguishable if (i) every embedded point
\bar{{x}}^{(i)}
has squared length at least
\theta
, and (ii) any pair of embedded points with normalisation are separated by
a distance
of at least
\theta
.
By definition, it is easy to see that, if
{M}
is
(\theta,\ell)
-distinguishable for some large value of
\theta
, then the embedded points
\{\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\}_{i\in{V}_{M}}
can be easily separated even if
\ell<k
.
The two examples below demonstrate that it is indeed the case and, since the meta-graph
{M}
is constructed from
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, this well-separation property for
\{\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\}_{i\in{V}_{M}}
usually implies
that the clusters
\{{S}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are also well-separated when the vertices are mapped to the points
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
, in which

F(u)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}\cdot\left({f}_{1}(u),\ldots{f}_{\ell}(u)\right)^{\intercal}.

(5.2)

From these examples, it is clear to see that there is a close
connection between the embedding
\{\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\}
defined in (
5.1
) and the embedding
\{F(u)\}
defined in (
5.2
). To formally analyse this connection, we study the structure theorem with meta-graphs.

We define vectors
\bar{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
which represent the eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{N}}_{M}
“blown-up” to
n
dimensions.
Formally, we define
\bar{{g}}_{i}
such that

\bar{{g}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\chi}_{j}}{\left\|{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\chi}_{j}\right\|}\cdot{g}_{i}(j),

where
{\chi}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the indicator vector of cluster
{S}_{j}
defined in (
3.1
).
By definition, it holds for any
u\in{S}_{j}
that

\bar{{g}}_{i}(u)=\sqrt{\frac{{d}(u)}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}}\cdot{g}_{i}(j).

The following lemma shows that
\{\bar{{g}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
form an orthonormal basis.

Lemma 5.1
.

The following statements hold:

1.

it holds for any
i\in[k]
that
\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}\right\|=1
;

2.

it holds for any different
i,j\in[k]
that
\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle=0
.

Proof.

By definition, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}=\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}\bar{{g}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{j}}\bar{{g}}_{i}(u)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{j}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}}\cdot{g}_{i}(j)\right)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{g}_{i}(j)^{2}=\left\|{g}_{i}\right\|^{2}=1,

which proves the first statement.

To prove the second statement, we have for any
i\neq j
that

\displaystyle\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{x}}\bar{{g}}_{i}(u)\bar{{g}}_{j}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{x}}\frac{{d}(u)}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{x})}\cdot{g}_{i}(x){g}_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}{g}_{i}(x){g}_{j}(x)

\displaystyle={g}_{i}^{\intercal}{g}_{j}

\displaystyle=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

We will later use the following important relationship between the eigenvalues of
{\mathcal{N}}_{M}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

It holds for any
1\leq i\leq k
that
\lambda_{i}\leq\gamma_{i}
.

Proof.

Notice that we have for any
j\leq k
that

\displaystyle\bar{{g}}_{j}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in{E}_{G}}{w}_{G}(u,v)\left(\frac{\bar{{g}}_{j}(u)}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}-\frac{\bar{{g}}_{j}(u)}{\sqrt{{d}(v)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k-1}\sum_{y=x+1}^{k}\sum_{a\in{S}_{x}}\sum_{b\in{S}_{y}}{w}(a,b)\left(\frac{\bar{{g}}_{j}(a)}{\sqrt{{d}(a)}}-\frac{\bar{{g}}_{j}(b)}{\sqrt{{d}(b)}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k-1}\sum_{y=x+1}^{k}{w}({S}_{x},{S}_{y})\left(\frac{{g}_{j}(x)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{x})}}-\frac{{g}_{j}(y)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{y})}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={g}_{j}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{M}{g}_{j}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have an
i
-dimensional subspace
{S}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\max_{{x}\in{S}_{i}}\frac{{x}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}{x}}{{x}^{\intercal}{x}}=\gamma_{i},

from which the statement of the lemma follows by the Courant-Fischer theorem.
∎

Next, similar to the function
\Upsilon(k)

defined in (
3.2
), for any input graph
{G}=({V}_{G},{E}_{G},{w}_{G})
and
(\theta,\ell)
-distinguishable meta-graph
{M}
, we define the function
\Psi(\ell)

by

\Psi(\ell)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\lambda_{\ell+1}}.

Notice that we have by the higher-order Cheeger inequality that
\gamma_{i}/2\leq\rho_{M}(i)
holds for any
i\in[\ell]
, and
\rho_{M}(i)\leq\rho_{G}(k)
by the construction of matrix
{A}_{M}
. Hence, one can view
\Psi(\ell)
as a refined definition of
\Upsilon(k)
.

We now show that the vectors
{f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{\ell}
and
\bar{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{\ell}
are well approximated by each other.
In order to show this, we define for any
i\in[\ell]
the vectors

\widehat{{f}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}~\mbox{\ \ \ and\ \ \ }~\widehat{{g}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{j},

and present the structure theorem with meta-graphs.

Theorem 3

(The Structure Theorem with Meta-Graphs)
.

The following statements hold:

1.

it holds for any
i\in[\ell]
that

\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq\gamma_{i}/\lambda_{\ell+1};

2.

it holds for any
\ell\in[k]
that

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\lambda_{\ell+1}}.

Proof.

For the first statement, we write
\bar{{g}}_{i}
as a linear combination of the vectors
{f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{n}
, i.e.,

\bar{{g}}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}.

Since
\widehat{{f}}_{i}
is a projection of
\bar{{g}}_{i}
, we have that
\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}
is perpendicular to
\widehat{{f}}_{i}
, and that

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)=\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}.

Now, we study the quadratic form
\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}
and have that

\displaystyle\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}^{\intercal}\right){\mathcal{N}}_{G}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle{f}_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\bar{{g}}_{i},{f}_{j}\rangle^{2}\lambda_{j}\geq\lambda_{\ell+1}\left\|\bar{{g}}_{i}-\widehat{{f}}_{i}\right\|^{2}.

By the proof of Lemma
5.2
, we have that
\bar{{g}}_{i}^{\intercal}{\mathcal{N}}_{G}\bar{{g}}_{i}\leq\gamma_{i}
, from which the first statement follows.

Now we prove the second statement.
We define the vectors
\bar{{g}}_{k+1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{n}
to be an arbitrary orthonormal basis of the space orthogonal to the space spanned by

\bar{{g}}_{1},\ldots,\bar{{g}}_{k}
. Then, we can write any
{f}_{i}
as

{f}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{j}
,
and have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\|{f}_{i}-\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\left\|{f}_{i}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{g}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{i},\bar{{g}}_{j}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\langle\bar{{g}}_{j},{f}_{i}\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\left\|\bar{{g}}_{j}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|\bar{{g}}_{j}-\widehat{{f}}_{j}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{\gamma_{j}}{\lambda_{{\ell}+1}},

where the final inequality follows by
the first statement of the theorem.
∎

5.2
Spectral Clustering with Fewer Eigenvectors

In this section, we analyse spectral clustering with fewer eigenvectors. Our presented algorithm is essentially the same as the standard spectral clustering described in Section
4
, with the only difference that every
u\in{V}_{G}
is embedded into a point in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
by the mapping
defined in
(
5.2
). Our analysis follows from the one from Section
4
at a very high level.
However, since we require that
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
are well separated in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
for some
\ell<k
, the proof is more involved.

For any
i\in[k]
, we define the approximate centre
{p}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
of every cluster
{S}_{i}
by

{p}^{(i)}(j)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}\cdot\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{j},\bar{{g}}_{x}\rangle\cdot{g}_{x}(i),

and prove that the total
k
-means cost for the points
\{F(u)\}_{u\in{V}_{G}}
can be upper bounded.

Lemma 5.3
.

It holds that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\leq\Psi(\ell).

Proof.

By definition, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in S_{i}}{d}(u)\left\|F(u)-{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}{d}(u)\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{{f}_{j}(u)}{\sqrt{{d}(u)}}-\left(\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{j},\bar{{g}}_{x}\rangle\frac{{g}_{x}(i)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left({f}_{j}(u)-\left(\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{j},\bar{{g}}_{x}\rangle\bar{{g}}_{x}(u)\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in{S}_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left({f}_{j}(u)-\widehat{{g}}_{j}(u)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|{f}_{j}-\widehat{{g}}_{j}\right\|^{2}\leq\Psi(\ell),

where the final inequality follows from the second statement of Theorem
3
.
∎

We now prove a sequence of lemmas which will establish that the distance between different
{p}^{(i)}
and
{p}^{(j)}
can be lower bounded with respect to
\theta
and
\Psi(\ell)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

It holds for
i\in[k]
that

\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right)\frac{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\leq\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}\left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right).

Proof.

It holds by definition that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})\cdot\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle{g}_{y}(i)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{z=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle{g}_{y}(i){g}_{z}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle^{2}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}+\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z=1\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle{g}_{y}(i){g}_{z}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle^{2}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}+\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z=1\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}^{\ell}{g}_{y}(i){g}_{z}(i)\cdot\left(\widehat{{f}}_{y}^{\intercal}\widehat{{f}}_{z}\right).

(5.3)

We study the two terms of (
5.3
) separately. For the second term, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z=1\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}^{\ell}{g}_{y}(i){g}_{z}(i)\cdot\left(\widehat{{f}}_{y}^{\intercal}\widehat{{f}}_{z}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert{g}_{y}(i)\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert{g}_{z}(i)\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{{f}}_{y}^{\intercal}\widehat{{f}}_{z}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert{g}_{y}(i)\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert{g}_{z}(i)\right\rvert\left\lvert\left(\bar{{g}}_{y}+\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y}\right)^{\intercal}\left(\bar{{g}}_{z}+\widehat{{f}}_{z}-\bar{{g}}_{z}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert{g}_{y}(i)\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert{g}_{z}(i)\right\rvert\left\lvert\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle+\langle\widehat{{f}}_{z}-\bar{{g}}_{z},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle+\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\widehat{{f}}_{z}-\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert{g}_{y}(i)\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z=1\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\left\lvert{g}_{z}(i)\right\rvert\left\lvert\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}\right)\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert{g}_{z}(i)\right\rvert\left\lvert\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}{g}_{z}(i)^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y}\right\|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)},

where we used the fact that
\sum_{y=1}^{k}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}=1
for all
i\in[k]
. Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle^{2}\right){g}_{y}(i)^{2}+\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}\leq\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}+\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}\leq\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}+\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right).

On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{x=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{x},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle^{2}\right){g}_{y}(i)^{2}-2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}\geq\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{y}\right\|^{2}{g}_{y}(i)^{2}-2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\Psi(\ell)\right)\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}-2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}\geq\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right),

where the last inequality holds by the fact that
\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|\leq 1
and
\Psi(\ell)<1
. Hence, the statement holds.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

It holds for
i\neq j
that

\left\|\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(i)}-\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}\geq\theta-3\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}.

Proof.

By definition, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(i)}-\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle\left(\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle^{2}\left(\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\left(\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right)\left(\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right).

We upper bound the second term by

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{y}\rangle\langle{f}_{t},\bar{{g}}_{x}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{{f}}_{y}^{\intercal}\widehat{{f}}_{z}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\left\lvert\left(\bar{{g}}_{y}+\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y}\right)^{\intercal}\left(\bar{{g}}_{z}+\widehat{{f}}_{z}-\bar{{g}}_{z}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\left\lvert\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{{g}_{y}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{y}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right)^{2}\right)\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left\lvert\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rvert\left\lvert\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle\right\rvert\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{{g}_{z}(i)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}-\frac{{g}_{z}(j)}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\right)^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq z\leq\ell\\
z\neq y\end{subarray}}\langle\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y},\bar{{g}}_{z}\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\left\|\widehat{{f}}_{y}-\bar{{g}}_{y}\right\|^{2}}\leq 2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)},

from which we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(i)}-\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})}}{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|}\cdot{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\Psi(\ell)\right)\theta-2\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\theta-3\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}.

With this we proved the statement.
∎

We set the parameter

Lemma 5.7
.

It holds for different
i,j\in[k]
that

\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\theta^{2}-20\sqrt{\theta\cdot\Psi(\ell)}}{16\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\geq\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}
. Then, by Lemma
5.4
and the fact that
\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\|^{2}\geq\theta
holds for any
i\in[k]
,
we have

\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right)\cdot\frac{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i})},

\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\theta}\right)\cdot\frac{\left\|\bar{{x}}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}}{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})},

which implies that

\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\theta-4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}.

Now, we will proceed by case distinction.

Case 1:
\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\geq 4\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|
. In this case, we have

\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|\geq\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|-\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|\geq\frac{3}{4}\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|

and

\displaystyle\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{9}{16}\cdot\frac{\theta-4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta\left(\theta-20\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)/\theta}\right)}{16\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta^{2}-20\sqrt{\theta\cdot\Psi(\ell)}}{16\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}},

since
\theta<1
.

Case 2:
\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|=\alpha\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|
for some
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{4},1\right]
.
By Lemma
5.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|},\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{4}-8\sqrt{\frac{\Psi}{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{\theta}{8}+2\sqrt{\frac{\Psi}{\theta}}.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{p}^{(i)}-{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|^{2}-2\left\langle\frac{{p}^{(i)}}{\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|},\frac{{p}^{(j)}}{\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|}\right\rangle\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|\left\|{p}^{(j)}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\alpha^{2}\right)\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}-2\left(1-\frac{\theta}{8}+2\sqrt{\frac{\Psi(\ell)}{\theta}}\right)\alpha\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha+\frac{\theta}{4}\alpha-4\sqrt{\frac{\Psi(\ell)}{\theta}}\alpha\right)\left\|{p}^{(i)}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\theta}{4}-4\sqrt{\frac{\Psi(\ell)}{\theta}}\right)\cdot\alpha\cdot\frac{\theta-4\sqrt{\Psi(\ell)}}{\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\theta}{16}-\sqrt{\frac{\Psi(\ell)}{\theta}}\right)\left(\theta-4\sqrt{\Psi}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{16}-\frac{5}{4}\sqrt{\theta\Psi(\ell)}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta^{2}-20\sqrt{\theta\Psi(\ell)}}{16\min\left\{\mathrm{vol}({S}_{i}),\mathrm{vol}({S}_{j})\right\}}

which completes the proof.

In this section we empirically evaluate the performance of spectral clustering for finding
k

k
eigenvectors.
Our results on synthetic data demonstrate that for graphs with a clear pattern of clusters,

k
eigenvectors performs better.
This is further confirmed on real-world datasets including BSDS, MNIST, and USPS.
The code used to produce all experimental results is available at

We implement the spectral clustering algorithm in Python, using the

Our experiments on synthetic data are performed on a desktop computer with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-8500 CPU @ 3.00GHz processor and 16 GB RAM.

Future Work

Our work leaves a number of interesting questions for future research.
For spectral clustering, the only non-trivial assumption remaining in our analysis is that the optimal clusters have almost balanced size.
It is unclear whether, under the regime of
\Upsilon(k)=\Omega(1)
, this condition could be eventually removed, or if there’s some hard instance showing that our analysis is tight.
For spectral clustering with fewer eigenvectors, our presented work is merely the starting point, and leaves many open questions.
For example, although one can enumerate the number of used eigenvectors from
1
to
k
and take the clustering with the minimum
k
-way expansion,
we are interested to know whether the optimal number of eigenvectors
can be computed directly, and rigorously analysed for different graph instances.
We believe that the answers to these questions would not only significantly advance our understanding of spectral clustering, but also, as suggested in our experimental studies, have widespread applications in analysing real-world datasets.

On statistical arc length of the Riemann Z ⁡ ( t ) Z(t) -curve
Jan Moser

Abstract
. In this paper we study certain stochastic process that is generated by the Riemann-Siegel formula. Further, we construct corresponding statistical model by a way similar to those used in telecommunication. We define statistical arc length of the Riemann Z ⁡ ( t ) Z(t) -curve in this model and obtain an asymptotic formula for that length. This paper is English remake of our work of reference [ 4 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.

In the paper
[
5
]
we have studied the following integral

(1.1)

\int_{T}^{T+H}\sqrt{1+\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}^{2}}{\rm d}t=L(T,H)=L,

i.e. the arc length of the Riemann curve

y=Z(t),\ t\in[T,T+H],\ T\to\infty,

where (see
[
7
]
, pp. 79, 329)

\begin{split}&Z(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right),\\
&\vartheta(t)=-\frac{t}{2}\ln\pi+\mbox{Im}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{t}{2}\right).\end{split}

Next, we will denote the roots of the equations

Z(t)=0,\quad Z^{\prime}(t)=0

by the symbols

\{\gamma\},\quad\{t_{0}\};\quad t_{0}\not=\gamma

correspondingly.

Remark 1
.

On the Riemann hypothesis, the points of the sequences
\{\gamma\}
and
\{t_{0}\}
are separated each from other (see

[
2
]
, Corollary 3), i.e. in this case we have

\gamma^{\prime}<t_{0}<\gamma^{\prime\prime},

where
\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}
are neighboring points of the sequence
\{\gamma\}
. Of course,
Z(t_{0})
is an extremal value
of the function
Z(t)
in some neighborhood of the point
t_{0}
.

Namely, we have proved (see
[
5
]
) for integral (
1.1
) the following formula

(1.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{T}^{T+H}\sqrt{1+\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}^{2}}{\rm d}t=2\sum_{T\leq t_{0}\leq T+H}|Z(t_{0})|+\Theta H+\mathcal{O}\left(T^{\frac{A}{\ln\ln T}}\right),\\
&T\to\infty,\ \Theta=\Theta(T,H)\in(0,1),\ H=T^{\epsilon}\end{split}

for every fixed
\epsilon>0
.

The proof of the formula (
1.2
) was based on the following

(1.3)

\begin{split}&Z^{\prime}(t)=-2\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\ln\frac{P}{n}\sin\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n\}+\\
&+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4}\ln T),\ t\in[T,T+U],\ U\in(0,\sqrt{T}],\end{split}

that was obtained in our paper
[
3
]
as a variant of the Riemann-Siegel formula. One sight on formulae
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is sufficient for insight of the difficulty of the problem about the asymptotic
formula of the arc length of the Riemann curve.

1.2.

In connection with formulae (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) we give the following

Remark 2
.

The main difficulty lies probably in that we are absent of something like famous Dirac procedure

\sqrt{m^{2}c^{2}+p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}}=\alpha_{1}p_{1}+\alpha_{2}p_{2}+\alpha_{3}p_{3}+\beta,

i.e. extracting of the square root of the operator - see
[
1
]
, p. 255, equations (5), (7). This was the
way towards Dirac’s relativistic wave equation for the electron - the fundamental law of the quantum
mechanics.

1.3.

Next, let us remind, in connection with (
1.1
)–(
1.3
), that in our recent paper we have proved
the following result on metamorphoses: there is an infinite set of elements

(1.4)

\{\alpha_{0}(T),\alpha_{1}(T),\dots,\alpha_{k}(T)\},\ T\in(T_{0},+\infty),\ T_{0}>0

where
T_{0}
is sufficiently big, such that

(1.5)

\begin{split}&\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{n\leq\tau(\alpha_{r})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(\alpha_{r})-\alpha_{r}\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{r}^{-1/4})\right|\sim\\
&\sim\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{\left|\sum_{n\leq\tau(\alpha_{0})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(\alpha_{0})-\alpha_{0}\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{0}^{-1/4})\right|}},\\
&T\to\infty,\end{split}

where

\Lambda=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{H}{H_{k}}\ln^{k}T,\ \tau(t)=\sqrt{\frac{t}{2\pi}},\ k=1,\dots,k_{0},\ k_{0}\in\mathbb{N},

i.e. to the infinite subset

\{\alpha_{1}(T),\dots,\alpha_{k}(T)\}

an infinite set of metamorphoses of the multiform on the left-hand side of (
1.5
) into quite
distinct form on the right-hand side of (
1.5
) corresponds.

Now we rewrite the formula (
1.5
) in the following form

(1.6)

\begin{split}&\sqrt{\frac{1}{\Lambda}\left|\sum_{n\leq\tau(\alpha_{0})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(\alpha_{0})-\alpha_{0}\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{0}^{-1/4})\right|}\sim\\
&\sim\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{n\leq\tau(\alpha_{r})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(\alpha_{r})-\alpha_{r}\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{r}^{-1/4})\right|^{-1}.\end{split}

Remark 3
.

We see, in connection with (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and the Remark 2, that the square root of the
weighted nonlinear monoform on the left-hand side of (
1.6
) is asymptotically expressed as the rational
function of the multiform for infinite set of elements (
1.4
).

1.4.

In this paper, we introduce new method to study the main integral (
1.1
). Namely, we use certain stochastic
process generated by the formula (
1.3
). We use simple properties of this process together with the
Ljapunov’s central limit theorem for a construction of an integral that we call

statistical arc length of the Riemann
Z(t)
-curve
. Consequently, an asymptotic formula for this
integral is obtained.

2.
Tranformation of the formula (
1.1
)

In the formula (see (
1.1
))

L=L(T,U)=\int_{T}^{T+U}\sqrt{1+\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}^{2}}{\rm d}t

we put

Z^{\prime}(t)=Z_{1}(t)+R_{1}(t),\ t\in[T,T+U],

where (see (
1.3
))

\begin{split}&Z_{1}(t)=2\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\ln\frac{P}{n}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n+\pi/2\},\\
&R_{1}(t)=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/4}\ln T).\end{split}

Since

\ln\frac{P}{n}=\mathcal{O}(\ln P),\ \ln\frac{P}{n-1}>\ln\frac{P}{n},\ 2\leq n<P,

then, similarly to
[
7
]
, pp. 92, 93, we obtain the following estimate

Z_{1}(t)=\mathcal{O}(T^{1/6}\ln^{2}T),\ t\in[T,T+U].

Hence,

\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}^{2}=\{Z_{1}(t)\}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/12}\ln^{3}T),

and

\begin{split}&\sqrt{1+\{Z^{\prime}(t)\}^{2}}=\sqrt{1+\{Z_{1}(t)\}^{2}}\left\{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{T^{-1/12}\ln^{3}T}{1+\{Z_{1}(t)\}^{2}}\right)\right\}=\\
&=\sqrt{1+\{Z_{1}(t)\}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/12}\ln^{3}T).\end{split}

Consequently, we have the following

Lemma 1
.

(2.1)

L=L(T,U)=\int_{T}^{T+U}\sqrt{1+\{Z_{1}(t)\}^{2}}{\rm d}t+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/12}\ln^{3}T),

(2.2)

Z_{1}(t)=2\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\ln\frac{P}{n}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n+\pi/2\},\quad U\in(0,\sqrt{T}].

3.
Definition of the statistical arc length of the Riemann curve and Theorem

3.1.

Let

\varphi_{n}\in[-\pi,\pi],\ n<P

be the system of independent random variables each of them uniformly distributed on the segment
[-\pi,\pi]
.
Putting these
\varphi_{n}
into the arguments of cosine-functions in (
2.2
) one obtains the following
stochastic process

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{1}(t)=\Phi_{1}(t,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots)=\\
&=2\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\ln\frac{P}{n}\cos\{a(t,n)+\varphi_{n}\},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&a(t,n)=\vartheta(t)-t\ln n+\frac{\pi}{2},\\
&t\in[T,T+U],\ U\in(0,\sqrt{T}].\end{split}

Remark 4
.

The following is true: every realization

\Phi_{1}(t,\bar{\varphi}_{1},\bar{\varphi}_{2},\dots),\ t\in[T,T+U]

of the stochastic process (
3.1
) is a continuous function of the variable
t
for every admissible vector

\bar{\varphi}=(\bar{\varphi}_{1},\bar{\varphi}_{2},\dots)

and. consequently, there is the Riemann integral

\int_{T}^{T+U}\Phi_{1}(t,\bar{\varphi}){\rm d}t,\quad\forall\bar{\varphi}.

We also use the one-dimensional Gaussian density of probability

(3.2)

w_{\infty}(\Phi_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\ln^{3/2}P}e^{-\beta(\Phi_{1})^{2}},\quad\beta=\frac{3}{4\ln^{3}P}.

3.2.

Now, we define the following stochastic process

(3.3)

\Phi_{2}=\Phi_{2}(T,U,\varphi)=\int_{T}^{T+U}\sqrt{1+\{\Phi_{1}(t)\}^{2}}{\rm d}t,\ U\in(0,\sqrt{T}],

for every sufficiently big
T
. Expectation value of this process is given by the formula

(3.4)

E(\Phi_{2})=\int_{T}^{T+U}E(\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}}){\rm d}t.

Next, we define the following asymptotic expectations

(3.5)

E_{\infty}(\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}}w_{\infty}(\Phi_{1}){\rm d}\Phi_{1},

and, consequently, (see (
3.3
)–(
3.5
))

(3.6)

E_{\infty}(\Phi_{2})=\int_{T}^{T+U}E_{\infty}(\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}}){\rm d}t.

Thus, the comparison of the formulae (
2.2
), (
3.1
) and (
2.1
), (
3.6
) leads us
to the following

Definition
.

(3.7)

\langle L(t,U)\rangle|_{S}=E_{\infty}(\Phi_{2}),

where

\langle L(t,U)\rangle|_{S}

is the statistical arc length of the Riemann
Z(t)
-curve.

Consequently, we notice that the basis of constructed statistical model lies in the following (purely) mathematical

Theorem
.

(3.8)

E_{\infty}(\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}})\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{6\pi}}\ln^{3/2}T,\ T\to\infty.

Since (see (
3.6
), (
3.8
))

(3.9)

E_{\infty}(\Phi_{2})\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{6\pi}}U\ln^{3/2}T,\ T\to\infty,

then we obtain from (
3.9
) the following

Corollary
.

\langle L(t,U)\rangle|_{S}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{6\pi}}U\ln^{3/2}T,\ U\in(0,\sqrt{T}],\ T\to\infty.

4.
Statistical considerations about the Gaussian distribution

4.1.

The following lemma holds true

Lemma 2
.

(4.1)

E(\Phi_{1})=0,\ \Var(\Phi_{1})\sim\frac{2}{3}\ln^{3}P,\ T\to\infty.

Proof.

We have (see (
3.1
))

\Phi_{1}(t;\varphi)=\sum_{n<P}X_{n},\ X_{n}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\ln\frac{P}{n}\cos\{\varphi_{n}+a\}.

Since
\varphi_{n}
are independent and uniformly distributed then we obtain that

E(X_{n})=0,\ \Var(X_{n})=\frac{2}{n}\ln^{2}\frac{P}{n}

and

E(\Phi_{1})=0,\ \Var(\Phi_{1})=2\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{n}\ln^{2}\frac{P}{n}.

Next, for
\Var
we use the Euler-MacLaurin summation formula (comp.
[
7
]
, p. 13) for the function

f(x)=\frac{1}{x}\ln^{2}\frac{P}{x},\ x\in[1,P].

Since

f^{\prime}(x)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln^{2}P}{x^{2}}\right),\ f(1)=\mathcal{O}(\ln^{2}P),\ f(P)=0,

then

\begin{split}&\sum_{n<P}f(n)=\int_{1}^{P}\ln^{2}\frac{P}{x}\frac{{\rm d}x}{x}+\mathcal{O}\left(\ln^{2}P\int_{1}^{P}\frac{{\rm d}x}{x^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\ln^{2}P)=\\
&=\frac{1}{3}\ln^{3}P+\mathcal{O}(\ln^{2}P),\end{split}

and, consequently,

\Var(\Phi_{1})\sim\frac{2}{3}\ln^{3}P,\ T\to\infty.

∎

4.2.

Next, we give the following

Lemma 3
.

It is true that in our case the Ljapunov’s condition in the central limit theorem is fulfilled.

Proof.

Since

\begin{split}&E(|X_{n}-E(X_{n})|^{3})=E(|X_{n}|^{3})=\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}\ln^{3}P\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\cos^{3}(\varphi_{n}+a)|{\rm d}\varphi_{n}<\\
&<\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}\ln^{3}\frac{P}{n},\end{split}

then we have the following estimate for the third absolute central moment

\begin{split}&\sum_{n<P}E(|X_{n}|^{3})\leq 8\sum_{n<P}\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\ln^{3}\frac{P}{n}<8\ln^{3}P\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}=A\ln^{3}P.\end{split}

Now, (see (
4.1
))

B_{P}=\sqrt{\Var(\Phi_{1})}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\ln^{3/2}P

and, consequently,

(4.2)

\frac{1}{B_{P}^{3}}\sum_{n<P}E(|X_{n}|^{3})<2A\frac{\ln^{3}P}{\ln^{9/2}P}\xrightarrow{T\to\infty}0,

i.e. the Ljapunov’s condition is fulfilled.
∎

Remark 5
.

Our choice of the Gaussian asymptotics in (
3.2
) is based upon the results (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
respectively.

5.
Proof of Theorem

We put (see (
3.2
) – (
3.5
))

(5.1)

E_{\infty}(\sqrt{1+(\Phi_{1})^{2}})=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\ln^{3/2}P}F(\beta),

where

(5.2)

F(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{1+x^{2}}e^{-\beta x^{2}}{\rm d}x,\ \beta=\frac{3}{4\ln^{3}P}.

We need to obtain an asymptotic formula for
F(\beta)
for small values of
\beta
(i.e. for
large values of
T
).

First of all, we have that

\begin{split}&F(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cosh^{2}te^{-\beta\sinh^{2}t}{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\cosh 2t+1)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\cosh 2t}{\rm d}t=\\
&=\frac{1}{4}e^{\beta/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\cosh t+1)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\cosh t}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Now, we use the Schläfli’s integral (see
[
8
]
, p. 81)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\cosh t}\cosh\nu t{\rm d}t=K_{\nu}(t),\ \re\{z\}>0,

for the modified Bessel’s function of the second kind, and then we obtain

(5.3)

F(\beta)=\frac{1}{4}e^{\beta/2}\left\{K_{0}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)+K_{1}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\right\}.

Further, we use the following representations of the
K_{\nu}
-functions (see
[
8
]
, p. 80)

\begin{split}&K_{0}(z)=I_{0}(z)\ln\frac{z}{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2m}}{2^{m}(m!)^{2}}\psi(m+1),\\
&K_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{m}\frac{(n-m-1)!}{m!\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{n-2m}}+\\
&+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{n+2m}}{m!(n+m)!}\left\{\ln\frac{z}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\psi(m+1)-\frac{1}{2}\psi(n+m+1)\right\},\end{split}

where (see
[
8
]
, p. 77)

I_{0}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(m!)^{2}}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2m},

and (see
[
9
]
, p. 241)

\psi(x)=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\ln\Gamma(x)=-c-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k+x}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right),

where
c
is the Euler’s constant.

Since

\psi(m+1),\psi(n+m+1)=\mathcal{O}(1),

and (see (
5.2
))

\begin{split}&\frac{\beta}{2}=\frac{3}{8\ln^{3}P},\ \ln\frac{\beta}{4}=-3\ln\ln P+\mathcal{O}(1),\\
&I_{0}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\ln^{6}P}\right),\end{split}

then

\begin{split}&K_{0}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)=3\ln\ln P+\mathcal{O}(1),\\
&K_{1}\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)=\frac{2}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(\beta|\ln\beta|)=\frac{8}{3}\ln^{3}P+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln\ln P}{\ln^{3}P}\right).\end{split}

Consequently (see (
5.3
))

F(\beta)=\frac{2}{3}\ln^{3}P+\mathcal{O}(\ln\ln P),

and from this (see (
5.1
)) the formula (
3.8
) follows, of course,

\ln^{3/2}P\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\ln^{3/2}T,\ T\to\infty.

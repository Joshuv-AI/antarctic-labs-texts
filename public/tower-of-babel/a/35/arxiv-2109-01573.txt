Properties of the Semigroup in L 1 L_{1} Associated with Age-Structured Diffusive Populations
Christoph Walker

Abstract
. The linear semigroup associated with age-structured diffusive populations is investigated in the L 1 L_{1} -setting. A complete determination of its generator is given along with detailed spectral information that imply, in particular, an asynchronous exponential growth of the semigroup. Moreover, regularizing effects inherited from the diffusion part are exploited to derive additional properties of the semigroup.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

A prototype model for the evolution of a diffusive population structured by
age reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,x)\nabla_{x}u\big)-m(a,x)u\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u(t,0,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a,x)u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=0\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega\,,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle u(0,a,x)

\displaystyle=\phi(a,x)\ ,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,.

(1.1d)

Here,
u=u(t,a,x)\geq 0
is the population density at time
t\geq 0
, age
a\in[0,a_{m})
with maximal age
a_{m}\in(0,\infty]
, and spatial position
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The total population at a fixed time
t
is

\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\int_{\Omega}u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

The age specific processes include death and birth processes with rates
m=m(a,x)\geq 0
respectively
b=b(a,x)\geq 0
. Spatial dispersal is governed by the diffusion term in (
1.1a
) with speed
d(a,x)>0
. The initial distribution of the population is
\phi=\phi(a,x)\geq 0
, and
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal on
\partial\Omega
. The investigation of linear and non-linear age-structured populations without and with spatial diffusion has a long history and there are many variants of Problem (
1.1
) and different techniques to tackle them. We refer to
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
23
,
24
]
and, for more recent contributions, to
[
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
20
,
19
,
22
]
and the references therein, though these lists are far from being complete.

Problem (
1.1
) can be put in a more abstract framework by setting

A(a)w:=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,\cdot)\nabla_{x}w\big)-m(a,\cdot)w\,,\quad w\in E_{1}\,,

where e.g.
E_{1}:=W_{q,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
consists of all functions
w
in the Sobolev space
W_{q}^{2}(\Omega)
with
q\in(1,\infty)
satisfying the boundary condition
\partial_{\nu}w=0
on
\partial\Omega
. For a smooth and positive function
d
, the operator
A(a)
is then the generator of an analytic semigroup in
E_{0}:=L_{q}(\Omega)
with domain
E_{1}
.

Our attention is thus focused in the following on the abstract problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u\,

\displaystyle=A(a)u\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle u(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t>0\,,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle u(0,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.2c)

for a function
u=u(t,a):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[0,a_{m})\rightarrow E_{0}^{+}
, where
a_{m}\in(0,\infty]
and

A(a):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\rightarrow E_{0}

is for each
a\in[0,a_{m})
the generator of an analytic semigroup on some Banach lattice
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
. We shall be more specific about the assumptions when presenting the main results below. It is worth pointing out that the parabolic operator
A(a)
and the age derivative
\partial_{a}
– being supplemented with a nonlocal boundary condition (
1.2b
) – act on different “variables”.

It is known
[
24
]
that a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}:=L_{1}((0,a_{m}),E_{0})
can be associated with (
1.2
) if
A
is independent of age and generates itself a strongly continuous semigroup on
E_{0}
. Indeed, integrating (
1.2a
) formally along characteristics gives the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
almost explicitly (see (
1.8
) below). However, the corresponding infinitesimal generator
\mathbb{A}
has not been characterized completely except for the case that more restrictive conditions on the operator
A
are imposed. More precisely, in
[
21
]
the generator
\mathbb{A}
is identified assuming the operator
A
to possess
maximal
L_{p}
-regularity
(e.g. see
[
1
]
for more information on this property) restricting the phase space to
L_{p}((0,a_{m}),E_{0})
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and thus excluding the biologically “natural” space
L_{1}((0,a_{m}),E_{0})
which is sufficient to give a meaning to the (local) overall population
\int_{0}^{a_{m}}u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a
. The first aim of this research is to remedy this deficiency and improve the results of
[
21
]
: we characterize the domain of the infinitesimal generator
\mathbb{A}
of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
also in the framework of
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}((0,a_{m}),E_{0})
and without assuming the operator
A
to have maximal
L_{p}
-regularity. The characterization of the generator in turn yields detailed information on its spectrum which implies, in particular, asynchronous exponential growth of the semigroup.

The second aim of this research is then to provide further properties of the linear semigroup and its generator exploiting the regularizing properties inherited from the parabolic character of the diffusion operator. Such regularizing effects are derived from the explicit formula (
1.8
) for the semigroup associated with (
1.2
). They pave the way for the well-posedness
[
18
,
19
,
22
]
of nonlinear variants of (
1.2
) featuring a nonlinear operator
A=A(u)
or a nonlinear birth rate
b=b(u)
. Furthermore, the characterization of the generator in the framework of
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}((0,a_{m}),E_{0})
is particularly useful in the study of stability properties of equilibria in nonlinear problems, e.g. in order to derive a principle of linearized stability in a forthcoming work.

It is worth mentioning that other approaches than the one we choose herein (originating from
[
24
]
), e.g. relying on integrated semigroups
[
17
,
6
]

or on perturbation techniques of Miyadera type
[
14
,
15
]
have been pursued as well and also yield the above mentioned asynchronous exponential growth. We also refer to the recent works
[
11
,
12
]
on related age-structured equations with nonlocal diffusion.

Assumptions and Notations

Set
J:=[0,a_{m}]
if
a_{m}<\infty
and
J:=[0,\infty)
if
a_{m}=\infty
. We let
E_{0}
be a real Banach lattice ordered by closed convex cone
E_{0}^{+}
and assume throughout that

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{0}\,,

that is,
E_{1}
is a dense subspace of
E_{0}
with continuous and compact embedding. Given
\theta\in(0,1)
and an admissible interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
(see
[
1
]
), we set
E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
. Then

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{\theta_{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{\theta_{0}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{0}\,,\quad 0\leq\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}\leq 1\,.

E_{\theta}
is equipped with the order naturally induced by
E_{0}^{+}
. We assume that there is
\rho>0
such that

A\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,,

(1.3a)

where
\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
is the subset of
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
of all generators of analytic semigroups on
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
.
Then (
1.3
) and
[
1
, II.Corollary 4.4.2]
imply that
A
generates a parabolic evolution operator

\{\Pi(a,\sigma)\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})\,;\,a\in J\,,\,0\leq\sigma\leq a\}\,,

on
E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
in the sense of
[
1
, Section II.2.1]
. That is,

\Pi\in C\big(J_{\Delta},\mathcal{L}_{s}(E_{0})\big)\cap C\big(J_{\Delta}^{*},\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{1})\big)

with

J_{\Delta}:=\{(a,\sigma)\in J\times J\,;\,0\leq\sigma\leq a\}\,,\quad J_{\Delta}^{*}:=\{(a,\sigma)\in J\times J\,;\,0\leq\sigma<a\}

satisfies

\Pi(a,a)=1_{E_{0}}\,,\qquad\Pi(a,\sigma)=\Pi(a,s)\Pi(s,\sigma)\,,\quad(a,s),(s,\sigma)\in J_{\Delta}\,,

and, for
a\in J
,

\Pi(\cdot,a)\in C^{1}\big((a,a_{m}),\mathcal{L}(E_{0})\big)\,,\quad\Pi(a,\cdot)\in C^{1}\big([0,a),\mathcal{L}_{s}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)

with

\partial_{1}\Pi(a,\sigma)=A(a)\Pi(a,\sigma)\,,\quad\partial_{2}\Pi(a,\sigma)=-\Pi(a,\sigma)A(\sigma)\,,\quad(a,\sigma)\in J_{\Delta}^{*}\,.

We further assume that
there is
\varpi\in\mathbb{R}
such that, if
\alpha\in[0,1]
, then

\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})}+(a-\sigma)^{\alpha}\,\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\leq M_{\alpha}e^{\varpi(a-\sigma)}\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

(1.3b)

for some
M_{\alpha}\geq 1
(if
a_{m}<\infty
this is automatically satisfied, see
[
1
, II.Lemma 5.1.3]
) and

\text{if $a_{m}=\infty$, then $\varpi<0$ in \eqref{EO}}\,.

(1.3c)

As for the birth rate
b
we assume that there is
\vartheta\in(0,1)
with

b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\cap L_{1}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\vartheta\}\,,

(1.4)

and

b(a)\Pi(a,0)\in\mathcal{L}_{+}(E_{0})\ \text{is strongly positive}\text{ for $a$ in a subset of $J$ of positive measure}\,.

(1.5)

We set

\|b\|_{\theta}:=\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta}))}

in the following.

Remark 1.1
.

The assumptions that we impose are natural and easily checked in concrete applications. Indeed, (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) hold for uniformly elliptic operators satisfying the maximum principle while (
1.4
) is a regularity assumption, e.g. see
[
4
,
22
]
. For instance, the assumptions are satisfied for problem (
1.1
) with
a_{m}<\infty
provided that
E_{0}:=L_{q}(\Omega)
and
E_{1}:=W_{q,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
with
q\in(1,\infty)
,
d\in C^{1}(J\times\bar{\Omega})
with
d(a,x)>0
,
m\in C^{1}(J,C(\bar{\Omega}))
, and
b\in C(J,C^{2}(\bar{\Omega}))
is nonnegative and nontrivial (the regularities assumptions on
d,m
, and
b
can even be weaken), see e.g.
[
19
,
22
]
.

Note that if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then

\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma):=e^{-\lambda(a-\sigma)}\Pi(a,\sigma)\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

is the evolution operator associated with
-\lambda+A(a)
.
In particular, for
x\in E_{0}
and
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
, the function
v\in C(J,E_{0})
, given by

v(a)=\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)x+\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,

(1.6)

is the
mild solution
to the Cauchy problem

\partial_{a}v=(-\lambda+A(a))v+\phi(a)\,,\quad a\in\dot{J}:=(0,a_{m}]\,,\qquad v(0)=x\,.

Also recall from
[
1
, II. Theorems 1.2.1 & 1.2.2]
that

\begin{split}&\text{if $x\in E_{1}$ and $\phi\in C^{\theta}(J,E_{0})+C(J,E_{\theta})$ with $\theta\in(0,1]$,}\\
&\text{then $v\in C^{1}(J,E_{0})\cap C(J,E_{1})$ is a strong solution\,.}\end{split}

(1.7)

We will use these facts frequently later on.

The Semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}

Integrating (
1.2a
) formally along characteristics yields that the solution

[\mathbb{S}(t)\phi](a):=u(t,a)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,\quad a\in J\,,

to (
1.2
) with
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
is given by

\big[\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\big](a)\,:=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi(a,a-t)\,\phi(a-t)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ 0\leq t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi(a,0)\,B_{\phi}(t-a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ t>a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8a)

where
B_{\phi}:=u(\cdot,0)
satisfies the Volterra equation

B_{\phi}(t)\,=\,\int_{0}^{t}\chi(a)\,b(a)\,\Pi(a,0)\,B_{\phi}(t-a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}-t}\chi(a+t)\,b(a+t)\,\Pi(a+t,a)\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

(1.8b)

with
\chi
denoting the characteristic function of the interval
(0,a_{m})
. That is,
B_{\phi}
is such that

B_{\phi}(t)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\big[\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\big](a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

(1.9)

The first result entails that
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous positive semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
enjoying compactness properties and exhibiting regularizing effects induced by the parabolic evolution operator
\Pi
. We also provide a perturbation result in preparation for a future study of stability properties in nonlinear variants of (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

(a)

(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
defined in (
1.8
) is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
which is eventually compact if
a_{m}<\infty
and quasi-compact if
a_{m}=\infty
. If
b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}_{+}(E_{0})\big)
, then
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is positive.

(b)
If
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}:=L_{1}(J,E_{\alpha})
, then

\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq M_{\alpha}\,e^{\varpi t}\,\left(\frac{\gamma\big(\|b\|_{0}M_{0}t,1-\alpha\big)}{(\|b\|_{0}M_{0})^{\alpha}}\,e^{\|b\|_{0}M_{0}t}+t^{-\alpha}\right)\,,\quad t>0\,.

(1.10)

In fact,

\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\theta})}\leq M_{\theta}e^{(\varpi+\|b\|_{\theta}M_{\theta})t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\vartheta\}\,.

(1.11)

(c)
Let
\mathbb{A}
be the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Consider
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
. Then
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
with
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}):=\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
satisfying

\mathbb{T}(t)\phi=\mathbb{S}(t)\phi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\,\mathbb{B}\,\mathbb{T}(s)\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,.

(1.12)

Moreover, there are
N_{\alpha}\geq 1
and
\varsigma_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\mathbb{T}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq N_{\alpha}\,e^{\varsigma_{\alpha}t}t^{-\alpha}\,,\quad t>0\,.

(1.13)

If
b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}_{+}(E_{0})\big)
and
\mathbb{B}\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}^{+}
for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}^{+}
, then the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is positive.

That
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
defines a strongly continuous positive semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
with the stated compactness properties can be verified by direct computations as in
[

. The additional estimates (

) and (

) are due to (

), where
\gamma
denotes the lower incomplete gamma function

\gamma(x,\xi):=\int_{0}^{x}s^{\xi-1}\,e^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\Gamma(\xi)\,,\qquad x\geq 0\,,\quad\xi>0\,.

(c)
of Theorem

Suppose (

The Generator
\mathbb{A}

As pointed out in the introduction the infinitesimal generator
\mathbb{A}
associated with the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is identified
[
21
]
only in
L_{p}(J,E_{0})
with
p\in(1,\infty)
when assuming that
A
has the property of maximal
L_{p}
-regularity. That this additional assumption is not needed for a characterization of
\mathbb{A}
for the case
p=1
is shown in the next theorem. It relies on an explicit formula for the resolvent of
\mathbb{A}
(see (
3.1
) below). Setting

\mathbb{D}:=\left\{\psi\in C^{1}(J,E_{0})\cap C(J,E_{1})\,;\,\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\right\}

we also show that the subspace
\mathbb{D}
is a core for the domain
D(\mathbb{A})
if
a_{m}<\infty
; that is,
\mathbb{D}
is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
when the latter is equipped with its graph norm.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and (
1.5
). Let
\mathbb{A}
denote the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

(a)

\psi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
if and only if there exists
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
such that
\psi\in C(J,E_{0})\cap\mathbb{E}_{0}
is the mild solution to

\partial_{a}\psi=A(a)\psi+\phi(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,,

(1.14)

with

\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

(1.15)

In this case,
\mathbb{A}\psi=-\phi
.

(b)
The embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is continuous and dense for
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

(c)
If
a_{m}<\infty
and if (
1.4
) is valid also for
\theta=1
, then
\mathbb{D}

is a core for
D(\mathbb{A})
. If
\psi\in\mathbb{D}
, then
\mathbb{A}\psi=-\partial_{a}\psi+A\psi
.

That the domain of
\mathbb{A}
is characterized in Theorem
1.4

(a)
in terms of
mild
solutions to (
1.14
) reflects the hyperbolic part of the operator
-\partial_{a}+A(a)
while the regularizing effects stated in Theorem
1.4

(b)
are due to its parabolic part.

For Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
it suffices that
E_{0}
is an ordered Banach space; that is, no lattice property is needed.

Asynchronous Exponential Growth

Based on the compactness properties of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, the characterization of the generator
\mathbb{A}
from Theorem
1.4
entails information on its spectrum. We shall see that the spectrum is a pure point spectrum and in fact, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>\varpi
if
a_{m}=\infty
) is an eigenvalue of
\mathbb{A}
with eigenvector
\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})\setminus\{0\}
, that is, if
(\lambda-\mathbb{A})\phi=0
, then

\phi(a)=\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\phi(0)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\phi(0)=Q_{\lambda}\phi(0)\,,

(1.16)

where the operator
Q_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
is defined as

Q_{\lambda}:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

(1.17)

Clearly, (
1.16
) implies that
1
is an eigenvalue of
Q_{\lambda}
with eigenvector
\phi(0)
. The properties of the evolution operator, the compact embeddings of the interpolation spaces, and (
1.5
)
entail that
Q_{\lambda}
is a compact and (for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
) strongly positive operator on
E_{0}
. Hence, by the Krein-Rutman Theorem, the spectral radius
r(Q_{\lambda})
is positive and a simple eigenvalue of
Q_{\lambda}
. Moreover, there is a unique
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

r(Q_{\lambda_{0}})=1

(1.18)

and there are a quasi-interior point
\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}
in
E_{0}^{+}
and a positive functional
\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}\in E_{0}^{\prime}
with
Q_{\lambda_{0}}\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}=\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}
respectively
Q_{\lambda_{0}}^{\prime}\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}^{\prime}=\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}^{\prime}
. Actually, we shall see that
\lambda_{0}
is a dominant and simple eigenvalue of
\mathbb{A}
. This ensures asynchronous exponential growth of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
as stated in the next theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose (
1.3
), (
1.5
), and suppose (
1.4
) for
\theta\in\{0,\vartheta,1\}
. Moreover, if
a_{m}=\infty
suppose
r(Q_{0})\geq 1
. Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be as in (
1.18
). There are
\varepsilon>0
and
N\geq 1
such that

\left\|e^{-\lambda_{0}t}\,\mathbb{S}(t)-P_{\lambda_{0}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})}\leq Ne^{-\varepsilon t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

Here,
P_{\lambda_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})
is the spectral projection onto
\ker(\lambda_{0}-\mathbb{A})
given by

Proof of Estimates (

As for (

(2.2)

2.2

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

where we used implicitly that

for splitting the integral though the final estimate remains true for

Proof of Eventual Compactness when

In order to prove that

and

and that (

) implies the existence of

with

\|B_{\phi}(t-a-h)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\leq c_{2}\,(t-a-h)^{-\vartheta}\,,\quad a+h\in J\,.

Therefore, we infer from these observations along with (
1.3b
) and (
1.8
) that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|(\widetilde{\mathbb{S}(t)\phi})(a&+h)-(\mathbb{S}(t)\phi)(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}-h}\|\Pi(a+h,0)-\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta},E_{0})}\,\|B_{\phi}(t-a-h)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{a_{m}-h}\|\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|B_{\phi}(t-a-h)-B_{\phi}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\qquad+\int_{a_{m}-h}^{a_{m}}\|\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|B_{\phi}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq c_{1}c_{2}h^{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{a_{m}-h}(t-a-h)^{-{\vartheta}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\qquad+M_{0}\,e^{|\varpi|a_{m}}\,\int_{t+h-a_{m}}^{t}\|B_{\phi}(s-h)-B_{\phi}(s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+M_{0}\,e^{|\varpi|a_{m}}\,\int_{t-a_{m}}^{t+h-a_{m}}\|B_{\phi}(s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

with tilde indicating the trivial extension.
As for the last two terms on the right-hand side of this estimate, by (

) there is
c_{3}=c_{3}(t,\mathcal{B})>0
with

\|B_{\phi}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\leq c_{3}\,,\quad a\in J\,,

and we thus infer from (

) that, for

\begin{split}\|&B_{\phi}(s-h)-B_{\phi}(s)\|_{E_{0}}\\
&\leq\Big\|\int_{s-h-a_{m}}^{s-h}b(s-h-a)\,\Pi(s-h-a,0)B_{\phi}(a)\,\mathrm{d}a-\int_{s-a_{m}}^{s}b(s-a)\,\Pi(s-a,0)B_{\phi}(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\Big\|_{E_{0}}\\
&\leq\int_{s-h-a_{m}}^{s-h}\big\|b(s-h-a)\,\Pi(s-h-a,0)-b(s-a)\,\Pi(s-a,0)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|B_{\phi}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\qquad+\left(\int_{s-h}^{s}+\int_{s-h-a_{m}}^{s-a_{m}}\right)\big\|b(s-a)\,\Pi(s-a,0)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|B_{\phi}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq c_{3}\int_{s-h}^{s-h-a_{m}}\big\|b(s-h-a)\,\Pi(s-h-a,0)-b(s-a)\,\Pi(s-a,0)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\qquad+c_{3}\left(\int_{s-h}^{s}+\int_{s-h-a_{m}}^{s-a_{m}}\right)\big\|b(s-a)\,\Pi(s-a,0)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\mathrm{d}a\,.\end{split}

Noticing that (
1.4

we conclude that

\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|(\widetilde{\mathbb{S}(t)\phi})(a+h)-(\mathbb{S}(t)\phi)(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a=0\,,\ \text{ uniformly w.r.t. $\phi\in\mathcal{B}$}\,.

(2.3)

Next, (

) and (

) (with

\|(\mathbb{S}(t)\phi)(a)\|_{E_{\vartheta}}\leq\|\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\vartheta})}\,\|B_{\phi}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\leq c(t,\mathcal{B})\,a^{-\vartheta}\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,.

Given

let

be the

Therefore,

(c)

and consider

. We shall see later in Corollary

that

is continuously embedded into

Hence, if

and

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|\mathbb{B}\,\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}t&\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|\mathbb{B}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\,\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\

We are thus in a position to apply the Miyadera-Voigt perturbation theorem
[

, III. Corollary 3.16]
and conclude that
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}

satisfying

\mathbb{T}(t)\phi=\mathbb{S}(t)\phi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\,\mathbb{B}\,\mathbb{T}(s)\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\quad\phi\in D(\mathbb{A})\,.

(2.6)

If

, then

[

now implies that there are

and
\varsigma_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

The density of

in

yields (
1.13

1.13
), (

), and Lebesgue’s theorem, the density of

in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
also entails (
1.12

Suppose now that
\mathbb{B}\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}^{+}
for

and take

where the proof of

shows that the Neumann series converges since

is a Miyadera-Voigt perturbation of

Let

is the restriction of
\mathbb{S}(t)
to
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}=L_{1}(J,E_{\alpha})
. Thus, in order to prove that

is a strongly continuous positive semigroup on

it suffices to prove the strong continuity. To this end, let

We then obtain from (

) and (

that

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{S}(t)\phi-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|B_{\phi}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Pi(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})}\,\|\phi(a)\|_{E_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\

&\quad+\int_{t}^{a_{m}}\|\Pi(a,a-t)\,\phi(a)-\phi(a)\|_{E_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}a\\

. That this semigroup in

) using the fact that

.∎

Resolvent Representation Formula

In the following,

I:=\mathbb{R}\ \text{ if }\ a_{m}<\infty\,,\qquad I:=(\varpi,\infty)\ \text{ if }\ a_{m}=\infty\,.

Recall that the operators
Q_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\in I
are defined in (
1.17
). Their spectral radii determine the spectrum of
\mathbb{A}
as we shall see below. Let us first note from (
1.3b
) and (
1.4
) the regularizing property

Q_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\vartheta})

and hence
Q_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
is compact due to the compact embedding of
E_{\vartheta}
in
E_{0}
. Consequently,
\sigma(Q_{\lambda})\setminus\{0\}
consists only of eigenvalues. Moreover, (
1.5
) implies that
Q_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
is strongly positive for
\lambda\in I
. Based on the Krein-Rutman Theorem, the following result is shown in
[
21
, Lemma 2.4, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\lambda\in I
, the spectral radius
r(Q_{\lambda})
is positive and a simple eigenvalue of
Q_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
with an eigenvector
\zeta_{\lambda}\in E_{1}
that is quasi-interior in
E_{0}^{+}
. Moreover,
r(Q_{\lambda})
is an eigenvalue of the dual operator
Q_{\lambda}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0}^{\prime})
with a positive eigenfunctional
\zeta_{\lambda}^{\prime}\in E_{0}^{\prime}
. The mapping

I\rightarrow(0,\infty)\,,\quad\lambda\mapsto r(Q_{\lambda})

is continuous and strictly decreasing with

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}r(Q_{\lambda})=0\,.

If
a_{m}<\infty
, then

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\infty}r(Q_{\lambda})=\infty\,.

According to Lemma
3.1
, if
\lambda\in I
is large enough, then
r(Q_{\lambda})<1
so that
(1-Q_{\lambda})^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
.
Next, introducing for
\lambda\in I
the operator
H_{\lambda}
as

H_{\lambda}\phi:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi(\sigma)\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,,

we can state the following auxiliary result for further use.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\in I
. Then

H_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\theta},E_{\theta})\cap\mathcal{L}\big(L_{\infty}(J,E_{0}),E_{\theta}\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\vartheta\}\,.

Moreover, if
a_{m}<\infty
, if (
1.4
) is valid for
\theta=1
, and if
\phi\in C(J,E_{\xi})+C^{\xi}(J,E_{0})
for some
\xi\in(0,1]
, then
H_{\lambda}\phi\in E_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\{0,\vartheta\}
.
Noticing from (
1.3b
) that

\begin{split}\left\|H_{\lambda}\phi\right\|_{E_{\theta}}&\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|b(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}\int_{0}^{a}\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\theta})}\,\|\phi(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq M_{\theta}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|b(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}\int_{0}^{a}e^{(\varpi-\lambda)(a-\sigma)}\,(a-\sigma)^{-\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\,\|\phi\|_{L_{\infty}(J,E_{0})}\end{split}

for
\phi\in L_{\infty}(J,E_{0})
, it readily follows from (
1.4
) that
H_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{\infty}(J,E_{0}),E_{\theta}\big)
. Similarly,

\begin{split}\left\|H_{\lambda}\phi\right\|_{E_{\theta}}&\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|b(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta}}\int_{0}^{a}\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}\,\|\phi(\sigma)\|_{E_{\theta}}\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq M_{\theta}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|b(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}\int_{0}^{a}e^{(\varpi-\lambda)(a-\sigma)}\,\,\|\phi(\sigma)\|_{E_{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\end{split}

for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
,
so that again (
1.4
) implies
H_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\theta},E_{\theta})
.

Finally, let
a_{m}<\infty
and consider
\phi\in C(J,E_{\xi})+C^{\xi}(J,E_{0})
for some
\xi\in(0,1]
. Setting

v(a):=\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi(\sigma)\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,

we have
v\in C(J,E_{1})
by (
1.7
). Hence,
H_{\lambda}\phi\in E_{1}
provided (
1.4
) is valid for
\theta=1
.
∎

The following representation formula for the resolvent of
\mathbb{A}
is fundamental for determining the domain of
\mathbb{A}
. It has already been observed in
[
21
]
(but was then used only under more restrictive conditions). We include the proof here for the reader’s ease. Recall that the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
given by

\omega_{0}:=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,;\,\exists\,M\geq 1:\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})}\leq Me^{\omega t}\,,\,t\geq 0\}

while

s(\mathbb{A}):=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\,;\,\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})\}

is the spectral bound of the generator
\mathbb{A}
.
Setting

\omega_{*}:=\varpi+M_{0}\|b\|_{0}

we have
\omega_{*}\geq\omega_{0}\geq s(\mathbb{A})
due to (
1.11
).

Proposition 3.3
.

If
\lambda>\omega_{*}
with
(1-Q_{\lambda})^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})
, then

\big[(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\phi\big](a)=\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)(1-Q_{\lambda})^{-1}H_{\lambda}\phi

(3.1)

for
a\in J
and
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
.

Proof.

The choice of
\lambda
ensures that it belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathbb{A}
and that
H_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},E_{0})
. Using the Laplace transform formula

(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\phi=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\,\mathrm{d}t

for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
, we infer from
[
10
, p.69 f]
and (
1.8
) that, for a.a.
a\in J
,

\begin{split}\big[(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\phi\big](a)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\big[\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\big](a)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,t)\,\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}B_{\phi}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.\end{split}

Since
\lambda>\omega_{*}
, it follows from (
2.2
) that

\Psi:=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}B_{\phi}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\in E_{0}\,,

and using (
1.8
) and (
1.9
), we obtain

\begin{split}\Psi&=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\big[\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\big](a)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\Psi+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,t)\,\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}a=Q_{\lambda}\Psi+H_{\lambda}\phi\,,\end{split}

that is,

\Psi=(1-Q_{\lambda})^{-1}H_{\lambda}\phi

from which the claim follows.
∎

We obtain also the following information on the domain of
\mathbb{A}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\alpha\in[0,1)
, then the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is continuous and dense.

Proof.

Recall from (
1.10
) that there are
c_{2}>0
and
\omega_{2}>0
such that

\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq c_{2}\,e^{\omega_{2}t}\,\left(t^{-\alpha}+1\right)\,,\quad t>0\,.

(3.2)

Fix
\lambda>\max\{\omega_{0},\omega_{2}\}
. Given
\psi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
set
\phi:=(\lambda-\mathbb{A})\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Since

(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\phi=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mathbb{S}(t)\phi\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

we derive from (
3.2
) that

\|\psi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\,\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq c_{2}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(-\lambda+\omega_{2})t}\,\big(t^{-\alpha}+1\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\leq c_{3}\,\|\psi\|_{D(\mathbb{A})}\,

what yields the continuity of the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
. That this embedding is also dense follows from the fact that, for
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
,

\phi_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}(s)\phi\,\mathrm{d}s\in D(\mathbb{A})\,,\quad t>0\,,

and

\phi_{t}\to\phi\quad\text{ in }\ \mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\quad\text{ as }\ t\to 0

since
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
by Corollary
1.3
.
∎

(

Moreover, we assume for this section that

(4.1)

Note that

(4.2)

due to (

Also note that (

Spectrum of
\mathbb{A}

The compactness property of
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
stated in Theorem
1.2
provides information on the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbb{A})
of the generator
\mathbb{A}
, in particular, that it is a pure and discrete point spectrum. Moreover, the eigenvalues
\mu
of
\mathbb{A}
are related to the operator
Q_{\mu}
. Recall that we have introduced the interval
I
as

I=\mathbb{R}\ \text{ if }\ a_{m}<\infty\,,\qquad I=(\varpi,\infty)\ \text{ if }\ a_{m}=\infty\,.

Lemma 4.1
.

(a)
If
a_{m}<\infty
, then the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbb{A})
is countable and consists of poles of the resolvent
R(\cdot,\mathbb{A})
of finite algebraic multiplicities (in particular,
\sigma(\mathbb{A})
is a pure point spectrum). Moreover, the set
\{\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})\,;\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq r\}
is finite for each
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)
If
a_{m}=\infty
, then the set
\{\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})\,;\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq 0\}
is finite and consists of poles of the resolvent
R(\cdot,\mathbb{A})
of finite algebraic multiplicities.

(c)
Let
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\mu\in I
. Then
\psi\in\mathrm{ker}(\mu-\mathbb{A})
if and only if there is
\psi_{0}\in E_{1}
with

\psi(a)=\Pi_{\mu}(a,0)\psi_{0}\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\psi_{0}=Q_{\mu}\psi_{0}\,.

(4.3)

In particular,
\mathrm{ker}(\mu-\mathbb{A})\subset\mathbb{D}
.

(d)
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\mu\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})
has geometric multiplicity
m
if and only if
1\in\sigma(Q_{\mu})
has geometric multiplicity
m
.

Proof.

(a)
Since the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is eventually compact if
a_{m}<\infty
according to Theorem
1.2
, this is a consequence of
[
7
, V. Corollary 3.2]
.

(b)
Since the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is quasi-compact if
a_{m}=\infty
according to Theorem
1.2
, this is a consequence of
[
7
, V. Theorem 3.7]
.

(c)
Let
\mu\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})
and
\psi\in\mathrm{ker}(\mu-\mathbb{A})\subset\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
. Since
\mathbb{A}\psi=\mu\psi
, we infer from Theorem
1.4
(a) that
\psi
is the mild solution to

\partial_{a}\psi=A(a)\psi-\mu\psi(a)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

(4.4)

with
\phi:=-\mu\psi\in C(J,E_{0})
. Clearly, (
4.4
) implies (
4.3
) with
\psi_{0}=\psi(0)\in E_{1}
due to (
4.2
). Hence,
\psi\in\mathbb{D}
by (
1.7
).
Conversely, if
\psi
satisfies (
4.3
), then
\psi
obviously satisfies (
4.4
). Moreover, since
\mathrm{Re}\,\mu\in I
, we have
-\mu\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
due to (
4.3
) and (
1.3b
). Therefore,

\psi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
with
(\mu-\mathbb{A})\psi=0
owing to Theorem
1.4
(a). This proves
(c)
.

(d)
Let
\mu\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})
have geometric multiplicity
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there are linearly independent
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}\in\mathrm{ker}(\mu-\mathbb{A})
, and (
4.3
) yields

\psi_{j}(a)=\Pi_{\mu}(a,0)\psi_{j}(0)\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\psi_{j}(0)=Q_{\mu}\psi_{j}(0)\,.

This readily implies that
\psi_{1}(0),\ldots,\psi_{m}(0)\in E_{0}
are linearly independent eigenvectors of
Q_{\mu}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
.

Conversely, let
1\in\sigma(Q_{\mu})
have geometric multiplicity
m\in\mathbb{N}
so that there are linearly independent
\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{m}\in E_{0}
with
\Psi_{j}=Q_{\mu}\Psi_{j}
. Set

\psi_{j}(a):=\Pi_{\mu}(a,0)\Psi_{j}\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j=1,\ldots,m\,.

Then
\psi_{j}\in\mathrm{ker}(\mu-\mathbb{A})
due to
(c)
. If
\zeta:=\sum_{j}\xi_{j}\psi_{j}=0
for some
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, the unique solvability of

\partial_{a}\zeta=(-\mu+A(a))\zeta\,,\quad a\in J\,,\qquad\zeta(0)=\sum_{j}\xi\Psi_{j}

readily implies
\zeta(0)=0
, hence
\xi_{j}=0
so that that
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m}
are linearly independent.
∎

We next characterize the spectral bound

Proposition 4.2
.

s(\mathbb{A})=\lambda_{0}
is a simple and dominant eigenvalue of
\mathbb{A}
and

\ker(\lambda_{0}-\mathbb{A})=\mathrm{span}\{\Pi_{\lambda_{0}}(\cdot,0)\zeta_{\lambda_{0}}\}

and note from
[

is a positive semigroup on the Banach lattice

\psi\in\ker({\lambda_{0}}-\mathbb{A})^{2}

\phi_{0}:=({\lambda_{0}}-\mathbb{A})\psi\in\ker({\lambda_{0}}-\mathbb{A})

so that
\phi_{0}=\gamma\varphi
for some

is real and that
\gamma>0

\tau>0
such that

Proof.

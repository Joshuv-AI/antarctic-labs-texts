Optimal transport map estimation in general function spaces
Vincent Divol Jonathan Niles-Weed Aram-Alexandre Pooladian

Abstract
We study the problem of estimating a function T T given independent samples from a distribution P P and from the pushforward distribution T ♯ ​ P T_{\sharp}P . This setting is motivated by applications in the sciences, where T T represents the evolution of a physical system over time, and in machine learning, where, for example, T T may represent a transformation learned by a deep neural network trained for a generative modeling task. To ensure identifiability, we assume that T = ∇ φ 0 T=\nabla\varphi_{0} is the gradient of a convex function, in which case T T is known as an optimal transport map . Prior work has studied the estimation of T T under the assumption that it lies in a Hölder class, but general theory is lacking. We present a unified methodology for obtaining rates of estimation of optimal transport maps in general function spaces. Our assumptions are significantly weaker than those appearing in the literature: we require only that the source measure P P satisfy a Poincaré inequality and that the optimal map be the gradient of a smooth convex function that lies in a space whose metric entropy can be controlled. As a special case, we recover known estimation rates for Hölder transport maps, but also obtain nearly sharp results in many settings not covered by prior work. For example, we provide the first statistical rates of estimation when P P is the normal distribution and the transport map is given by an infinite-width shallow neural network.

Introduction

A central question in statistics is to estimate the relationship between two data sets.
For instance, in the regression problem, the statistician observes data
(X_{1},Y_{1}),\dots,(X_{n},Y_{n})
satisfying

Y_{i}=f^{\star}(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}\,,

where

There are a number of ad hoc strategies for estimating

under different structural assumptions, see, e.g.,

The statistician faced with a regression problem can choose a class
\mathcal{F}

\mathbb{E}\|\hat{f}-f^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-f^{\star}\|_{L^{2}}+\delta_{n,\mathcal{F}}\,.

(1)

The first term in (

The second term is the estimation error, and scales with the complexity of the function class

(

, Chapter 13)
.
The statistician has the freedom to choose the class

to balance these sources of error.

Background on optimal transport under the quadratic cost

For a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\Omega)
be the space of probability measures having a finite second moment on
\Omega

Our analysis primarily hinges on existence results and stability bounds for transport maps, which we will later relate to empirical processes. We refer the reader to standard texts on optimal transport for more details e.g.,

The
Monge problem
in optimal transport seeks to find a transport map that minimizes the average cost of displacement between two measures

\displaystyle\inf_{T\in\mathcal{T}(P,Q)}\int\|x-T(x)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\,,

(5)

where
\mathcal{T}(P,Q)\coloneqq\{T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ T_{\sharp}P=Q\}
is the set of transport maps from
P
to
Q
, two measures in
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
The value of this optimization problem is the squared Wasserstein distance
W_{2}^{2}(P,Q)
.

We henceforth suppose that the target measure is given by
Q\coloneqq(\nabla\varphi_{0})_{\sharp}P
, for some differentiable convex function
\varphi_{0}
.
We then obtain the dual characterization

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}W_{2}^{2}(P,Q)&=\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P+\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}Q-\inf_{\varphi\in L^{1}(P)}S(\varphi)\\
&:=\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P+\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}Q-\inf_{\varphi\in L^{1}(P)}\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}P+\int\varphi^{*}\,\mathrm{d}Q,\end{split}

(6)

where
\varphi^{*}
denotes convex conjugation.
The functional
S(\varphi)
is known as the
semi-dual
of the original transport problem.
This dual characterization can be used to show that if

\varphi_{0}
is smooth and convex, then

minimizes the semi-dual, and

The function

Recall the setup: we suppose that there exists a convex,

\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}\in\argmin_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}}\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}P_{n}+\int\varphi^{*}\,\mathrm{d}Q_{n}\,,

Remark 3

As

We make the following assumptions throughout this paper.

(A1)

There exists

such that every

is

-smooth, as well as

(A2)

The probability measure

\mathrm{Var}_{P}(f)\leq C_{\texttt{PI}}\int\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x),

(12)

where

Remark 4

The Poincaré inequality is ubiquitous in probability and analysis, being for instance connected to the phenomenon of concentration of measure

)

In general, the main obstruction to obtain a Poincaré inequality is when the measure has disconnected support.
Examples of conditions that give rise to a probability measure satisfying

•

any measure having a density bounded away from zero and infinity on a bounded Lipschitz domain (or more generally with a support satisfying the cone condition, see
(

•

any measure having a density bounded away from zero and infinity on a compact, connected submanifold (in particular,

3.1
The bounded case

Our first result deals with the case where

has a bounded support. We require a control on the size of the class
\mathcal{F}
of candidate potentials as well as a uniform bound on the associated transport maps on the support of

on the size of the support of

For a subset

is defined as the smallest number of balls of radius
h
in
(E,d)
that are needed to cover

.

(B1)

There exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that for every

\log\mathcal{N}(h,\mathcal{F},L^{\infty})\lesssim_{\log_{+}(1/h)}h^{-\gamma};

(14)

(B2)

P
is supported in

.

Let

be a probability measure and

,

3.2
The strongly convex case

For
\alpha>0

In the second setting, we do not assume that

(C1)

4.1
Transport between Location-Scale families

We first verify our theorems on general location-scale families. For concreteness, we begin with the case of multivariate Gaussian distributions. Let
P
be the Gaussian distribution
N(m_{P},\Sigma_{P})
, and let

be
N(m_{Q},\Sigma_{Q})

\varphi_{0}:x\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}(x-{m}_{P})^{\top}\left({\Sigma}_{P}^{-1/2}({\Sigma}_{P}^{1/2}{\Sigma}_{Q}{\Sigma}_{P}^{1/2})^{1/2}{\Sigma}_{P}^{-1/2}\right)(x-{m}_{P})+m_{Q}^{\top}x\,.

(23)

In the two-sample problem, where we have
n
samples from both
P
and

, the plug-in estimator is given by

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{n}(x)=\hat{m}_{Q}+\left(\hat{\Sigma}_{P}^{-1/2}(\hat{\Sigma}_{P}^{1/2}\hat{\Sigma}_{Q}\hat{\Sigma}_{P}^{1/2})^{1/2}\hat{\Sigma}_{P}^{-1/2}\right)(x-\hat{m}_{P})\,,

where
\hat{m}_{P}
(resp.
\hat{m}_{Q}
) and
\hat{\Sigma}_{P}
(resp.
\hat{\Sigma}_{Q}
) are empirical estimates of the mean and covariance of
P
(resp.
Q
) from samples. One can show by direct arguments that
\hat{T}_{n}
achieves parametric rates; see
Flamary et al. 2019
. More generally, let
P_{0}
be a centered, isotropic base distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If

and

, then the optimal Brenier potential is again given by (
23
), and

achieves the parametric rate.

As a sanity check, we show that our procedure recovers the rates of estimation in this simple case. The location-scale setup suggests to consider a family of candidate potentials

\mathcal{F}_{\text{quad}}=\{x\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}x^{\top}Bx+\langle b,x\rangle:\ B\in\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

(24)

Concretely, our estimator is
\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{n}
with

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{n}=\argmin_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{quad}}}\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}P_{n}+\int\varphi^{*}\,\mathrm{d}Q_{n}\,.

One can verify that
\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{n}
precisely matches the plug-in estimator. However,
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
(and its gradient) does not immediately fall into our framework since the set

is not precompact, and therefore its covering numbers are infinite. However, we show in
Appendix

D
that it suffices to control the covering number of the smaller set

\mathcal{F}_{\text{quad},0}=\{x\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}x^{\top}Bx:\ B\in\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+},\ \|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\}\,

which grows at most logarithmically.

2
then yields the following bound.

4.2
Finite set

We now consider the setup from
Vacher and Vialard 2021
, who consider the problem of estimating an optimal Brenier potential over a finite class of functions
\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{K}\}
. In addition, the authors focus on the case where (i) the measure

\varphi_{k}

\varphi_{0}

is constant for

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log n}\min_{k\in[K]}\|\nabla\varphi_{k}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}+n^{-1}\,.

(28)

To put it another way, assuming a Poincaré inequality is enough to improve the excess of risk from

to

(up to logarithmic factors).

4.3
Parametric space

More generally, one can consider a set of potentials parametrized by a bounded set
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Consider a set
\mathcal{F}=\{\varphi_{\theta}:\ \theta\in\Theta\}
and assume that there exist constants
L,p\geq 0
such that

\forall\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ |\varphi_{\theta}(x)-\varphi_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|\leq L\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\langle x\rangle^{p}.

(29)

Then, for
\eta>p
, the log-covering number at scale
h
of
\mathcal{F}
for the norm

is controlled by the log-covering number of the set
\Theta
, which scales as

. Therefore, condition
(C1)
is satisfied for
\gamma=0
. Let
P
be a probability measure satisfying a Poincaré inequality and assume that all potentials in
\mathcal{F}
are
(\beta,a)
-smooth.

implies that

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log n}\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}\|\nabla\varphi-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}+n^{-1},

(30)

that is, we obtain a parametric rate of convergence.

4.4
Large parametric spaces

A common technique in nonparametric inference is to approximate a (nonparametric) class of functions
\mathcal{F}
by a large finite dimensional space (using e.g., Fourier series, wavelets, or neural networks). If

is a bounded convex domain, the authors study the estimator

\hat{\varphi}_{(n,\text{W})}=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{J}(\text{W}^{\alpha,\beta})}\coloneqq\argmin_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{J}(\text{W}^{\alpha,\beta})}S_{n}(\varphi),

(38)

where
\mathcal{F}_{J}(\text{W}^{\alpha,\beta})
is the space of functions having a finite wavelet expansions up to some depth
J
,
while also being
\alpha
-strongly convex
(details on wavelet expansions are given in
Appendix

H
). Hütter and Rigollet show that, if the source measure
P
has a density bounded away from zero and infinity on
\Omega
, then this estimator enjoys the following rate of convergence (which is minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors), for
s\geq 2
,

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{(n,\text{W})}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log n}n^{-\frac{2(s-1)}{2s+d-4}}.

(39)

Using
3
, we are able to improve the results of Hütter and Rigollet in several directions. First, we relax the assumptions on
P
, which does not need to have a convex support but is only required to satisfy a Poincaré inequality
(A2)
as well as a local Poincaré inequality
(C3)
, while being a doubling measure
(C4)
. Second, we relax the hypothesis of
P
having a bounded support, which allows us to handle the case of smooth and strongly log-concave measures, see
1
.

The third improvement we make is related to the computation of the estimator. Indeed, in their numerical implementations, Hütter and Rigollet did not compute the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{(n,\text{W})}=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{J}(\text{W}^{\alpha,\beta})}
(where
\mathcal{F}_{J}(\text{W}^{\alpha,\beta})
is the set of
\alpha
-strongly convex
functions with a finite wavelet expansion), but removed the
\alpha
-strong convexity assumptions (for otherwise, the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{(n,\text{W})}
is not computable). They actually compute the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{J}(R_{c})}
, where, for an integer
J>0
and
R_{c}>0
, the set
\mathcal{F}_{J}(R_{c})
contains all potentials having a finite wavelet expansion of depth
J
on the cube of side-length
R_{c}
, with bounded coefficients, and extended by an arbitrary fixed convex function outside the cube. Their work does not supply a theoretical justification for this modification, but our
3
shows it achieves the minimax rate. See
Appendix

H
for details.

4.4.2
Application: Transport between log-concave measures

When the source measure potentially has unbounded support, we additionally assumed that the true Brenier potential
\varphi_{0}
has upper and lower bounded Hessian (or can be well-approximated by such a potential). We want to emphasize that sometimes these assumptions can be quantitatively verified based on structural properties of the source and target measures alone.
For example, consider the case when the source and target measures are appropriately log-smooth and log-strongly concave:
P=\exp(-V)
and
Q=\exp(-W)
with
0\preceq\alpha_{V}I\preceq\nabla^{2}V(x)\preceq\beta_{V}I
and similarly
0\preceq\alpha_{W}I\preceq\nabla^{2}W(y)\preceq\beta_{W}I
, with
P
and
Q
having full support on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A seminal result by
Caffarelli 2000
states that the optimal Brenier potential
\varphi_{0}
is then
\sqrt{\alpha_{V}/\beta_{W}}
-strongly convex and
\sqrt{\beta_{V}/\alpha_{W}}
-smooth, thus verifying the smoothness and strong convexity requirements of
3
. This principle is known as
Caffarelli’s contraction theorem
; see e.g.,
Chewi and Pooladian 2022
for a short proof based on entropic optimal transport.

Recall the function class
{\mathcal{F}_{J}(R_{c})}
from the previous section. The proof in
Appendix

H
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
P
and
Q
be log-smooth and log-strongly convex, with
Q=(\nabla\varphi_{0})_{\sharp}P
. Then, for
R_{c}\asymp J\asymp\frac{1}{d}\log(n)
, where
n
is the number of samples from each of
P
and
Q
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{J}(R_{c})}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}\lesssim_{\log n}n^{-\frac{2}{d}}\,.

(40)

To our knowledge, this is the first result establishing rates of estimation for transport maps between general log-concave measures.

As already mentioned in the introduction, several empirical works have proposed optimizing the semidual problem

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{C}^{s}_{L}(\Omega)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi-\varphi_{j}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\alpha/2\text{ and }\|\varphi-\varphi_{j}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim 2^{-j(s-1)},

4.5
Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces

We turn our attention to a class of transport maps given by smooth potential functions that lie in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). We briefly recall that a RKHS is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of functions over a domain
\mathcal{X}
where there exists a
kernel

\mathcal{K}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle f(x)=\langle f,\mathcal{K}(\cdot,x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

We refer to
Schölkopf et al. 2002
,
Wainwright 2019
for more details on the properties of such spaces.

For our application of interest, we let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the unit ball in some RKHS pertaining to some fixed kernel
\mathcal{K}
. We will assume that
\mathcal{K}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{4}
, which is enough to ensure that all potentials
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
are
\beta
-smooth for some
\beta>0
according to
Zhou 2008
. To complete our analysis, we use existing bounds on the
L^{\infty}
-covering numbers over such a set (see e.g.,
Yang et al. 2020b
), which are expressed in terms on the spectral properties of the kernel operator. For simplicity, we assume that either the spectrum is finite or has eigenvalues that decay exponentially fast,
see
Appendix

I
for details on these definitions and assumptions. For example, when the kernel is associated to a finite-dimensional feature mapping, the RKHS is finite-dimensional and thus exhibits a finite spectrum. The well-known Gaussian kernel

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{2}(x,y)=\exp(-\|x-y\|^{2}/\sigma^{2})\,,

is an example of a kernel with exponentially fast decaying spectrum over the sphere, with
\sigma^{2}>0
. For such smooth kernels, one can show that the log-covering numbers exhibit the following bound
(
Yang et al. 2020b
, Lemma D.2)

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(h,\mathcal{F},L^{\infty})\lesssim\log_{+}(1/h)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\,,

for some
\gamma^{\prime}\geq 1
.
Assume that
P
satisfies a Poincaré inequality
(A2)
and has a bounded support. Take
R
large enough so that the support of
P
is included in
B(0;R)
and that
\|\nabla\varphi\|\leq R
on the support of
P
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
(such a
R
exists as the potentials in
\varphi
are uniformly bounded in
C^{1}
-norm). We then modify the potentials
\varphi
to be equal to
+\infty
outside
B(0;2R)
, so that condition
(B2)
is satisfied for the set of modified potential
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. By Theorem
2
, we obtain parametric convergence i.e., for
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log(n)}\inf_{\varphi\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}}\|\nabla\varphi-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}+n^{-1}\,.

4.6
“Spiked” potential functions

The
spiked transport model
was recently proposed by Niles-Weed and Rigollet to model situations where two probability measures of interest living in a high-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
only differ on a low-dimensional subspace; see
Niles-Weed and Rigollet 2022
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{k\times d}
be the Stiefel manifold, consisting of all
k\times d
matrices with orthonormal rows.
We assume that there exists a matrix
U\in\mathcal{V}_{k\times d}
such that
Y\sim Q
can be obtained from
X\sim P
through a map
T
that can be decomposed into
T(x)=U^{\top}T^{\prime}(Ux)+(I-U^{\top}U)x
. At the level of the potentials, this is equivalent to assuming that the Brenier potential
\varphi_{0}
belongs to the function space

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}\coloneqq\left\{x\mapsto\varphi(Ux)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-U^{\top}Ux\|^{2}:\ \varphi\in\mathcal{F},\ U\in\mathcal{V}_{k\times d}\right\},

(42)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a class of potentials defined on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The covering numbers of the class
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}
are controlled by the covering numbers of the class
\mathcal{F}
up to logarithmic factors.

Proposition 4
.

For
\eta\geq 2
, it holds that

\log\mathcal{N}(h,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}},L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta}))\leq\log\mathcal{N}(h/2,\mathcal{F},L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta}))+C_{\eta}dk\log_{+}(1/h).

(43)

To obtain rates of estimation that depend solely on the intrinsic dimension
k
of the problem, it remains to prove that condition
(C2)
is satisfied for some value of
m
depending only on
k
. We show that this is for instance the case when
P
has a density bounded away from zero and infinity on a convex domain.

Proposition 5
.

Let
P
have a density bounded away from zero and infinity on a convex domain.
Then,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}
satisfies
(C2)
for
m=2k
.

Proofs of Propositions
4
and
5
can be found in
Appendix

J
.
5
is likely suboptimal, as one would expect the dimension parameter
m
to be equal to the dimension
k
of the spike.
Still, these two properties are enough to show that one can obtain rates of convergence with exponents depending only on the effective dimension
k
of the problem, the dependency on
d
only existing through prefactors. For instance, let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of
(\beta,a)
-smooth potentials containing some
\alpha
-strongly convex potential
\varphi_{0}^{\prime}
and satisfying
(C1)
for some
\gamma\in[0,2)
. If
\varphi_{0}
is given by
x\mapsto\varphi^{\prime}_{0}(U_{0}x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-U_{0}^{\top}U_{0}x\|^{2}
for some
U_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{k\times d}
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log n,d}n^{-\frac{(2k-\gamma)}{2k+\gamma(k-2)}}.

(44)

Finding the optimal function in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}
requires to solve an optimization problem defined over the Stiefel manifold. Such a problem is non-trivial.
In fact, following a strategy from
Niles-Weed and Rigollet 2022
, we show that this estimation problem possesses a
computational-statistical gap

(
Berthet and Rigollet 2013
,
Bandeira et al. 2018
,
Brennan et al. 2018
,
Brennan and Bresler 2020
)
. Specifically, in
Section

5
, we demonstrate that constructing a computationally efficient estimator for Brenier potentials in the class
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked}}
would yield a polynomial-time algorithm for the Gap Shortest Vector Problem (see
Section

5.1
), which is conjectured to be impossible even for quantum computers.

4.7
Barron spaces

For large-scale tasks, neural networks are often used to parametrize mappings or deformations from a set of data to another.
These deformations can be interpreted as transport maps between a source distribution (often a standard Gaussian measure) and a target distribution, which is to be learned from samples.
Several works have proposed to estimate such maps using optimal transport principles
(
Makkuva et al. 2020
,
Huang et al. 2021
,
Bunne et al. 2022a
)
, but with few accompanying guarantees.

Our general techniques allow us to obtain statistical results in this setting, namely when the Brenier potential lies in a class of shallow neural networks with unbounded width, referred to as a
Barron space

(
E et al. 2022
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
k
-dimensional smooth compact manifold (possibly with smooth boundary), and consider an activation function
\sigma:(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
. We define the
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{M})
-norm of a function
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
as in the Euclidean case (
37
), by using an arbitrary systems of charts on
\mathcal{M}
. We assume that

(D1)

for every
v\in\mathcal{M}
,
x\mapsto\sigma(x,v)
is convex with
\sigma(0,v)=0
, and is
(\beta,a)
-smooth,

(D2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function
v\mapsto\sigma(x,v)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{M})
for some
s>0
, with a uniformly bounded
\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{M})
-norm.

For a given activation function, we define the Barron space
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
as the space of functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
for which there exists a measure
\theta
on
\mathcal{M}
with finite total variation such that

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \varphi(x)=\int\sigma(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\theta(v),

(45)

while the Barron norm of
\varphi
is defined as the infimum of the total variations of measures
\theta
such that the representation (
45
) holds. We let
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{1}
be the unit ball in
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
.

Remark 8
.

Note that the optimal Brenier potential,
\varphi_{0}
, is therefore associated with an optimal measure
\theta_{0}
on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. In other words,

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(x)=\int\sigma(x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{0}(v)\,.

From a practitioner’s perspective, it suffices to find the optimal weights associated to the neural network by traditional means (e.g., stochastic gradient descent on the objective function of interest).

Theorem 4
.

Let
P
be a probability measure satisfying
(A2)
,
(C3)
and
(C4)
, and let
\sigma
be an activation function satisfying
(D1)
and
(D2)
. Let
\varphi_{0}
be

(\beta,a)
-smooth. Then,

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{1}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}]\lesssim_{\log n,d}\inf_{\varphi\in(\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{1})_{\alpha}}\|\nabla\varphi-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}+n^{-\frac{2s+k(1-2/d)}{2s+2k(1-2/d)}}.

(46)

In particular, the first term vanishes if
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{1}
.

The exponent in
4
always lies between
-1
and
-1/2
, in particular not depending on the ambient dimension
d
. Note however that the hidden prefactor does depend exponentially on
d
, so that finer bounds would be needed to completely break the curse of dimensionality.
A case of interest for applications is when
\sigma(x,v)=\sigma(\langle x,v\rangle)
for some convex function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
v
is in the unit sphere
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Activation functions of the form
\sigma(u)=\sigma_{s}(u)=u_{+}^{s}
are particularly studied in the literature
(
Bach 2017
)
.
In this case,
4
applies with
k=d-1
. One can actually use the spherical symmetry of the problem to obtain tighter rates of convergence, by replacing the dimension
d
appearing in the rate in
4
by
d-1
, that is the dimension of the sphere. We also give a near matching minimax lower bound for this problem.

Proposition 6
.

Let
s\geq 2
and let
P
be a probability measure satisfying
(A2)
. Write
X=RU
the polar decomposition of
X\sim P
. Assume that the law of
U
has a density bounded away from zero and infinity on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Further assume that there exists two constants
M_{\min},M_{\max}>0
such that for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
\mathbb{E}[R^{2s}|U=\theta]\leq M_{\max}
and
\mathbb{E}[R^{2s-2}|U=\theta]\geq M_{\min}
. Let
\sigma=\sigma_{s}
and let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma_{s}}
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma_{s}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}]\lesssim_{\log n,d}n^{-\frac{2s+d-3}{2s+2(d-3)}}.

(47)

Furthermore, if
d
is odd and
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{1}
, it holds that

\inf_{\hat{T}}\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma_{s},P}}\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{T}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}]\gtrsim_{d}n^{-\frac{2s+d-1}{2s+2d-3}},

(48)

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma_{s},P}
is the set of measures obtained as a pushforward of
P
by
\nabla\varphi
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma_{s}}
.

For large
d
, the exponent in both the upper and lower bounds is
-\frac{2s+d}{2s+2d}(1+o_{d}(1))
. The conditions on
P
in the proposition are mild, and are satisfied for example for a large class of radial distributions, including the Gaussian.
4
and
6
are proven in
Appendix

K
.

As a particular case of the above theorem, when
s=2
, we have
\sigma_{s}(u)=\frac{u_{+}^{2}}{2}
, so that
\sigma^{\prime}_{s}(u)=u_{+}
is the ReLu activation function. The transport maps arising from the set
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma_{s}}
are of the form
x\mapsto\int\langle x,v\rangle_{+}v\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\theta(v)
, which are shallow neural networks with a ReLu activation function.
6
gives the upper bound
n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d-1}}
, with the near-matching lower bound
n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{4d+2}}
. For instance, for
d=11
, the exponent in the upper bound is
0.6
, whereas the exponent in the lower bound is approximately equal to
0.612
. We conjecture that the correct minimax rate for this problem is the upper bound
n^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d-1}}
.

4.8
Input convex neural networks

As a last example,
Makkuva et al. 2020
propose to use input convex neural networks to estimate Brenier potentials. In our language, their estimator is exactly
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a class of input convex neural networks that can be parametrized using
D
weights (for some large
D
). Assuming that all the weights of the neural network are uniformly bounded, one can check that (
29
) is satisfied, so that the log-covering number at scale
h
is of order
D\log_{+}(1/h)
. One can then use
2
to obtain a rate of convergence of order

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\lesssim_{\log n,D}\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}\|\nabla\varphi-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}+n^{-1}.

(49)

To obtain a more satisfying rate of convergence, one needs to understand the approximation properties of input convex neural networks, in order to be able to control the bias term as a function of
D
in the above upper bound.
Moreover, the dependence on
D
in the estimation error is also likely not tight for overparameterized networks, which have good performance even when
n\ll D
.
Improving this bound is an important question in the theory of statistical properties of input convex neural networks, which is an attractive topic for future work.

5
Computational aspects

In this section, we investigate the computational properties of the optimization problem (
2
) defining our estimator.
We begin by noting that the objective function
S_{n}(\varphi)
is
convex
in
\varphi
: indeed, the conjugation operator
(\cdot)^{*}
satisfies

((1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}+\lambda\varphi_{1})^{*}\leq(1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}^{*}+\lambda\varphi_{1}^{*}

for any
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]

(see
24
for a short proof).
Since
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are positive measures, the functional
S_{n}(\varphi)=\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}P_{n}+\int\varphi^{*}\,\mathrm{d}Q_{n}
is therefore convex as well.

The convexity of
S_{n}
suggests that solving (
2
) can be computationally feasible whenever the set
\mathcal{F}
is itself convex and tractable to optimize over.
In particular, we can obtain a practical algorithm in the setting where
\mathcal{F}
is given by the convex hull of a (potentially large) parametric family, as in
Section

4.4
.
Fixing a set of functions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
, define the class

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{J}=\left\{\varphi_{\lambda}\ \bigg|\lambda\in\Delta_{J}\right\}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\ \bigg|\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J},\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}=1\right\}\,.

(50)

Given i.i.d. data
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\sim P
on a compact set
\Omega
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\sim(T_{0})_{\sharp}P
, for some optimal transport map
T_{0}=\nabla\varphi_{0}
, we wish to estimate the best approximation to
\varphi_{0}
in the class
\mathcal{F}_{J}
by
\widehat{\varphi}=\varphi_{\hat{\lambda}}
, where
\hat{\lambda}
solves
\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{J}}S_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq\int\varphi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}P_{n}+\int\varphi^{*}_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}Q_{n}
, which we can write explicitly as

\displaystyle\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{J}}S_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}(X_{i})\right)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\right)^{*}(Y_{k})\,.

(51)

It is natural to solve this problem via a first-order method, iteratively updating the parameters
\lambda
using gradient information of
S_{n}(\cdot)
. Note that by the envelope theorem
(
Milgrom and Segal 2002
, Theorem 1)
, the gradient of
S_{n}(\lambda)
can be written

\displaystyle[\nabla_{\lambda}S_{n}(\lambda)]_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(X_{i})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{k})\,,

where
x^{*}_{k}
is the solution to the following sub-problem

\displaystyle x^{*}_{k}\coloneqq\argmax_{x}\left\{\langle x,Y_{k}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}(x)\right\}\,.

(52)

We propose to minimize (
51
) via
projected gradient descent

(
Beck 2017
)
: initialize at
\lambda^{(0)}\in\Delta_{J}
, fix a step size
\eta>0
, and iterate the sequence

\displaystyle\lambda^{(k+1)}\leftarrow\text{Proj}_{\Delta_{J}}\bigl(\lambda^{(k)}-\eta\nabla_{\lambda}S_{n}(\lambda^{(k)})\bigr)\,,

(53)

where the maximum operation is computed component-wise.
Each iteration of this algorithm requires
O(J)
arithmetic operations and solving the
n
auxiliary problems given by (
52
).

The following theorem establishes the convergence of the iterates given by
Equation

53
.
A proof appears in
Appendix

L
.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that the elements of
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are strongly convex over
\Omega
.
Then
S_{n}(\cdot)
is
L_{J}
-smooth, for some
L_{J}>0
.
Moreover, if
\eta<1/L_{J}
, then the iterates of projected gradient descent (

\displaystyle S_{n}(\lambda^{(k)})-S_{n}(\hat{\lambda})\lesssim k^{-1}\bigl(L_{J}\|\lambda^{(0)}-\hat{\lambda}\|^{2}_{2}+S_{n}(\lambda^{(0)})-S_{n}(\hat{\lambda})\bigr)\,.

Implementing

Equation

52

Projected gradient descent for
S_{n}(\lambda)
is therefore tractable if we assume the existence of a
conjugate oracle
for functions of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}
.
Whether this assumption is reasonable depends on the structure of the set
\mathcal{F}_{J}
.

If we assume that the base functions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are both strongly convex and smooth, then implementing a conjugate oracle is straightforward: gradient ascent on the objective in
Equation

52
computes an
\varepsilon
-approximate optimal solution in
O(\log(1/\varepsilon))
time
(
Bubeck 2015
, Theorem 3.10)
.
Combined with
5

1

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{J}
be as in
Equation

50
.
If the elements of
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are smooth and strongly convex over a compact set
\Omega
, then
a solution of
Equation

2
satisfying the guarantees of
1
can be found in
O_{J}(n^{2}\log n)
time.

Though projected gradient descent can be applied to
S_{n}(\lambda)
even when the functions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}
are not convex, polynomial-time conjugate oracles are unlikely to exist for general function classes.
Since
\varphi^{*}(0)=-\min_{x}\varphi(x)
for any function
\varphi
, computing the convex conjugate of a function, even at a single point, is as hard as general (non-convex) optimization in the worst case.
Therefore, absent further assumptions, the time required for a conjugate oracle will typically scale exponentially in
d
.
Implementing such an oracle can therefore be practical when the dimension is very small, but typically not in the high-dimensional setting.

The estimation procedure developed in
Hütter and Rigollet 2021
is an example of this phenomenon.
The authors of that work consider a large parametric class
\mathcal{F}
consisting of functions with finite wavelet expansions.
Since these functions lack any convexity properties, it is not clear how to efficiently implement a conjugate oracle.
In
Hütter and Rigollet 2021
, the authors use the Fast Legendre Transform
(
Lucet 1997
)
to compute approximate conjugates, in running time exponential in the dimension.
Computing their estimator is feasible if
d\leq 3
, but generally infeasible otherwise.

5.1
Hardness results

The forgoing discussion suggests that it may not always be possible to minimize
S_{n}(\varphi)
over arbitrary families
\mathcal{F}
in polynomial time.
In this section, we give evidence that this deficiency is, in fact, fundamental and arises from a
computational-statistical gap
.
Concretely, we show that, under weak computational assumptions, no polynomial-time estimator can achieve the rates presented in
2
for a general family
\mathcal{F}
.
This result implies that the computational challenges discussed above are intrinsic to the problem, and cannot be avoided by adopting a different estimation strategy.

Our computational lower bound is based on a reduction from the
Continuous Learning With Errors (CLWE)
problem.
The pathbreaking work of
Bruna et al. 2021
showed that CLWE is as hard as the worst-case
Gap Shortest Vector Problem (GapSVP)
, a notoriously difficult problem in quantum computing.
Informally, this is a decision problem whose input is a
d
-dimensional lattice
4
4

4

A discrete additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

L
and a threshold
\tau>0
, and whose output is “YES” if there exists a vector in
L
whose length is at most
\tau
, and “NO” if every vector in
L
has length at least
\Omega(d\tau)
.
It is widely conjectured that no polynomial-time algorithm for worst-case GapSVP exists,
even for quantum computers

(
Regev 2005
,
Regev 2010
)
.
In fact, this conjecture is so universally believed that GapSVP has been adopted as the standard for post-quantum cryptography, that is, for cryptographic protocols that are robust even against adversaries with access to quantum computers.
By contrast, many problems that are hard for classical computers and form the backbone of classical cryptographic systems, such as integer factoring, are known to possess polynomial-time quantum algorithms
(
Bernstein et al. 2009
)
.

The following theorem shows that estimating optimal transport maps in general function spaces is as hard as CLWE, and therefore as hard as worst-case GapSVP.
This implies that, absent additional assumptions, no polynomial-time estimators achieving the rates in
2
are likely to exist, even if we permit the statistician to use quantum computers.
The class of potentials for which we show our hardness result is the simple “spiked” class defined in
Section

4.6
, with
k=1
and the base class
\mathcal{F}
consisting of smooth and strongly convex functions:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked},1}(c,C)=\left\{x\mapsto\varphi(\langle u,x\rangle)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-\langle u,x\rangle x\|^{2}:\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},c\leq\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq C,\|u\|=1\right\}\,,

for universal constants
c,C>0
.
As indicated in
4
, for any
\eta\geq 2
, this class satisfies

\log\mathcal{N}(h,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked},1},L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta}))\lesssim_{\log_{+}(1/h),\delta}dh^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,,

so

implies that for any constants

, if
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{spiked},1}(c,C)
, then
\nabla\varphi_{0}
can be estimated at the rate

, up to logarithmic factors.
In particular, if the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}
could be computed in polynomial in
n
and
d
, then it would be possible to estimate the optimal map to accuracy
O(1/\sqrt{d})
in polynomial time.
The following theorem shows that any such estimator would violate the conjectured hardness of the CLWE and GapSVP problems.

Theorem 7

There exist positive constants

Let

As mentioned above, this theorem indicates that estimating transport maps from the class

6.1
Empirical risk minimizers with convex criteria

As already stated, the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{F}}
is nothing but an empirical risk minimizer. As such, its risk can be bounded using standard theorems such as Massart’s analysis for the risk of an empirical risk minimizer
(
Massart 2007
, Theorem 8.3)
. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be a sample of i.i.d. observations from some law
\mathbb{P}
. In full generality, Massart’s theorem concerns the minimization of a criterion of the form

a\in\mathbb{A}\mapsto\int\gamma(a,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi),

where
\mathbb{A}
is a vector space endowed with some pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
a^{*}
be a minimizer of this functional and define the excess of risk
\ell(a,a^{*})=\int\gamma(a,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi)-\!\int\gamma(a^{*},\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi)
. Let

\overline{\gamma}_{n}(a)=\gamma_{n}(a)-\int\gamma(a,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma(a,\xi_{i})-\int\gamma(a,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi)

be the associated centered empirical process.
The minimizer
a^{*}
is approximated by the empirical risk minimizer
\hat{a}\in\argmin_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\overline{\gamma}_{n}(a)
, where
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
is a class of candidates. Let
\overline{a}\in\mathcal{A}
be a “good candidate” so that
\ell(\overline{a},a^{*})
is small (e.g., let
\overline{a}\in\argmin_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\ell(a,a^{*})
). Then, it is known that estimation is possible (i) under a control of the excess of risk around its minimum of the form

\forall a\in\mathbb{A},\ \|a-a^{*}\|\leq w(\ell(a,a^{*}))

(54)

for some appropriate function
w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, and (ii) under a control of local fluctuations given by a bound on

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A},\ \|a-\overline{a}\|\leq\tau}|\overline{\gamma}_{n}(a)-\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\overline{a})|\right].

(55)

Using this general theorem would be enough for obtaining the right bound in the bounded case (
1
), where
\mathbb{A}
is equal to the set of twice differentiable functions
\varphi
with
\nabla\varphi\in L^{2}(P)
, that we endow with the pseudo-norm
\varphi\mapsto\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(P)}
, and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{F}
is the class of candidate potentials.
This strategy, however, needs to be refined for our second scenario, where the measure
P
is not bounded. Indeed, in that case, as the proof will show, a tight control of the local fluctuations (
55
) is only possible when the potentials
a=\varphi
in the supremum are strongly convex. As such, to apply Massart’s theorem, we need to restrict ourselves to families of candidate potentials containing only strongly convex functions (as it is the case in
Hütter and Rigollet 2021
).
Such a limitation is not natural, as most families of approximation functions (neural networks, wavelets, Fourier functions) are not convex, hindering the applicability of our theorem.

To overcome this issue, we introduce an auxiliary norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
, and make the key observation that, under a convexity assumption of the criterion
\gamma
, it is sufficient to control the local fluctuations
|\overline{\gamma}_{n}(a)-\overline{\gamma}_{n}(\overline{a})|
for
\|a-\overline{a}\|\leq\tau
while
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|a-\overline{a}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is also arbitrarily small. In our setting,
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
will be the
C^{2}
-norm, while
\overline{a}=\overline{\varphi}
will be an
\alpha
-strongly convex potential attaining the infimum
\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}}(S(\varphi)-S(\varphi_{0}))
. In particular, any potential
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\varphi-\overline{\varphi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\alpha/2
is
(\alpha/2)

We state a general theorem for controlling the risk of an empirical risk minimizer with convex criterion. Besides introducing the auxiliary norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}

Appendix A
Orlicz spaces

We gather in this section results on random variables in Orlicz spaces relevant to us. Let
p\in(0,1]
. Recall that we let
\psi_{p}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a given convex increasing function with
\psi_{p}(0)=0
and
\psi_{p}(x)=e^{x^{p}}-1
for
x\geq c_{p}
, where the constant
c_{p}
is chosen so that such a convex increasing function exists. We say that a random variable
X
belongs to the Orlicz space of exponent
p
if there exists
c>0
with
\mathbb{E}[\psi_{p}(\|X\|/c)]\leq 1
. The smallest number
c
satisfying this condition is called the
p
-Orlicz norm of
X
, denoted by
\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}
. Having a finite Orlicz norm directly implies a control on the bound of
X
:

\forall t>c_{p}\|X\|_{\psi_{p}},\ \mathbb{P}(\|X\|>t)\leq\left(\psi_{p}\left(\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1}\leq\kappa_{p}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}}\right)^{p}\right),

(71)

for some constant
\kappa_{p}
.
By integrating this tail bound, subexponential variables are seen to have controlled moments.

Lemma 9
.

Let
p\in(0,1]
and let
X
be a random variable with
\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq m<+\infty
. Let
a\geq 1
. Then, there exists
C_{p,a}>0
such that for all
r\geq c_{p}m
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{a}]\leq C_{p,a}m^{a}\\
&\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{a}\mathbf{1}\{\|X\|\geq r\}]\leq C_{p,a}r^{a}\left(\frac{r}{m}\right)^{-p}e^{-\left(\frac{r}{m}\right)^{p}}.\end{split}

(72)

Proof.

Let us prove the first inequality. According to (
71
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{a}]

\displaystyle\leq(c_{p}m)^{a}+\int_{(c_{p}m)^{a}}^{+\infty}\mathbb{P}(\|X\|^{a}>t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq(c_{p}m)^{a}+\kappa_{p}\int_{(c_{p}m)^{a}}^{+\infty}\exp(-t^{p/a}/m^{p})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq(c_{p}m)^{a}+\kappa_{p}m^{a}\frac{a}{p}\int_{c_{p}^{p}}^{+\infty}u^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\exp(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u\leq C_{p,a}m^{a}.

The second inequality is obtained in a similar fashion.
∎

We conclude this section by a technical lemma, that shows that the integral of a function
f
against a subexponential distribution
P
is stable under polynomial perturbations of
f
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with
0\leq f(x)\leq L\langle x\rangle^{a}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
L,a\geq 0
. Let
P
be a subexponential distribution (that is such that
\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}<+\infty
if
X\sim P
) and let
I=\mathbb{E}_{P}[f(X)]
. Then, for every
b\geq 0
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{P}[f(X)\langle X\rangle^{-b}]\geq CI\log_{+}(1/I)^{-b}\\
&\mathbb{E}_{P}[f(X)\langle X\rangle^{b}]\leq CI\log_{+}(1/I)^{b},\end{split}

(73)

where
C
depends on
L,a,b
and
\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}
.

Proof.

We only prove the first inequality, the second one being proven similarly. Fix a threshold
r\geq 1
and write

\displaystyle\int f(x)\langle x\rangle^{-b}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\geq\frac{1}{(2r)^{b}}\int_{\|x\|\leq r}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}P(x).

According to
9
with
m=\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}
, if
r\geq c_{1}m
,

\int_{\|x\|\geq r}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\leq LC_{a}r^{a}e^{-r/m}.

Pick
r=m\lambda\log_{+}(1/I)
for some
\lambda>0
. For
\lambda
large enough with respect to the different constants involved, the above display is smaller than
I/2
. Therefore,

\int_{\|x\|\leq r}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}P(x)=I-\int_{\|x\|\geq r}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\geq I/2,

concluding the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Properties of strongly convex and smooth potentials

We gather in this section the different growth properties of
(\alpha,a)
-strongly convex and
(\beta,a)
-smooth potentials (as well as their convex conjugates). We start with a simple lemma that we give without proof, and that follows easily from writing Taylor expansions. Remark that the results given in this section also hold for
a=0
for strongly convex or smooth differentiable functions in the classical sense, without requiring second differentiability.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\varphi
be a
(\beta,a)
-smooth function for some
\beta,a\geq 0
. Let
x,y\in B(0;r)
for some
r\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)\|\leq\beta\langle r\rangle^{a}\|x-y\|\leq 2\beta\langle r\rangle^{a+1},

(74)

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq(2\beta\langle r\rangle^{a+1}+\|\nabla\varphi(0)\|)\|x-y\|\leq 2\beta^{\prime}\langle r\rangle^{a+2},

(75)

where
\beta^{\prime}=2\beta+\|\nabla\varphi(0)\|
.
Furthermore, if
\nabla\varphi
is invertible with
\|\nabla\varphi^{-1}(x)\|,\|\nabla\varphi^{-1}(y)\|\leq t
, then

\|\nabla\varphi^{-1}(x)-\nabla\varphi^{-1}(y)\|\geq\frac{\|x-y\|}{\beta}\langle t\rangle^{-a}.

(76)

Lemma 12
.

Let
\varphi
be a
(\alpha,a)
-strongly convex function for some
\alpha,a\geq 0
. Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
r=\max\{\|x\|,\|y\|\}
. Then,
\nabla\varphi
is bijection on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)\|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2^{a+2}}\langle r\rangle^{a}\|x-y\|,

(77)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi^{-1}(x)\|\leq 4\alpha^{-\frac{1}{a+1}}(\|x\|+\|\nabla\varphi(0)\|)^{\frac{1}{a+1}},

(78)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi^{-1}(x)-\nabla\varphi^{-1}(y)\|\leq\frac{2^{a+2}}{\alpha}\|x-y\|

(79)

Proof.

First, note that
\varphi
is in particular
\alpha
-strongly convex of exponent
0
(i.e., strongly convex in the classical sense), so that
\nabla\varphi
is indeed a bijection.
We prove the first inequality. Assume without loss of generality that
\|y\|=r
. It holds that

\|\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)\|\geq\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}\varphi(tx+(1-t)y))\|x-y\|\,\mathrm{d}t.

For
0\leq t\leq 1/4
, we have
\|tx+(1-t)y\|\geq r-2tr\geq r/2
. Therefore,

\|\nabla\varphi(x)-\nabla\varphi(y)\|\geq\frac{\alpha}{4}\langle\frac{r}{2}\rangle^{a}\|x-y\|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2^{a+2}}\langle r\rangle^{a}\|x-y\|.

The second inequality is obtained by inverting the first one and using that
\langle r\rangle\geq r
. The third one is also obtained by inverting the first one, and using that
\langle r\rangle\geq 1
.
∎

We now turn to bounds on the convex conjugates.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\varphi
be a
(\alpha,a)
-strongly convex function for some
\alpha,a\geq 0
, with
\varphi(0)=0
. Then,
\nabla\varphi
is invertible, and, for

,

\varphi^{*}(y)\leq\alpha^{-\frac{1}{1+a}}\|\nabla\varphi(0)-y\|^{\frac{2+a}{1+a}}.

(80)

Proof.

We write a Taylor expansion around

. For every

,

\displaystyle\varphi(z)

\displaystyle\geq\varphi(0)+\langle\nabla\varphi(0),z\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}\varphi(tz))\|z\|^{2}t\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla\varphi(0),z\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\alpha\langle t\|z\|\rangle^{a}\|z\|^{2}t\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\langle\nabla\varphi(0),z\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\alpha(t\|z\|)^{a}\|z\|^{2}t\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla\varphi(0),z\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{a+2}\|z\|^{a+2}.

Therefore,

\varphi^{*}(y)\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle z,y-\nabla\varphi(0)\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{a+2}\|z\|^{a+2}.

We conclude thanks to the formula for the convex conjugate of the function

.
∎

The following lemma states that, for points

that stays at a bounded distance from

the convex conjugate
\varphi^{*}(y)
is lower bounded by a quadratic approximation of
\varphi
at

.

Lemma 14

(Bounds of convex conjugates: quadratic behavior)
.

Let
\varphi
be a
(\beta,a)
-smooth function for some
\beta,a\geq 0
. Let
\ell\geq 0
,

and

be such that

\varphi^{*}(y)\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle y,x\rangle+\frac{1}{2D_{x}}\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|^{2},

where
D_{x}=\beta\langle\|x\|+\frac{\ell}{\beta}\rangle^{a}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

, with

. A Taylor expansion yields

\begin{split}\varphi(z)&\leq\varphi(x)+\langle\nabla\varphi(x),z-x\rangle+\frac{\beta}{2}\langle r\rangle^{a}\|z-x\|^{2}=q_{x}(z).\end{split}

Let

\begin{split}\varphi^{*}(y)&\geq\langle z,y\rangle-\varphi(z)\geq\langle z,y\rangle-q_{x}(z)\\

(83)

A little bit of algebra yields

,

\displaystyle S(\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\int\langle\nabla\varphi_{0}(x),x\rangle\,\mathrm{d}P(x)+\int\frac{1}{2D_{x}}\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)-\nabla\varphi_{1}(x)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle=S(\varphi_{0})+\int\frac{1}{2D_{x}}\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)-\nabla\varphi_{1}(x)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)

Note that
D_{x}
grows at most polynomially with
\|x\|
, with degree
a(a+1)
. The inequality then follows from
10
: we obtain that, for
I=\|\nabla\varphi_{0}-\nabla\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}
,

I\log_{+}(1/I)^{-b}\leq C\ell.

(85)

One can invert this inequality to obtain an inequality of the form
I\leq C^{\prime}\ell\log_{+}(1/\ell)^{b}
.

It remains to prove the second inequality. If
\varphi_{1}
is
(\alpha,0)
-strongly convex (with domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x)+\varphi_{1}^{*}(\nabla\varphi_{0}(x))\leq\langle x,\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)-\nabla\varphi_{1}(x)\|^{2}.

We obtain the second inequality by integrating the two sides of this equation with respect to
P
and using
\int\langle x,\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}P(x)=S_{0}(\varphi)
.
∎

When
y
is far away from
\nabla\varphi(x)
, as
\varphi
grows polynomially (of order
2+a
), we expect the convex conjugate to behave like the convex conjugate of the function
z\mapsto\|z-\nabla\varphi(x)\|^{a}
, which scales like
\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|^{\frac{2+a}{1+a}}
, where
\frac{2+a}{1+a}
is the conjugate exponent of
2+a
.

Lemma 15

(Lower bound of convex conjugates: polynomial behavior)
.

Under the same assumptions as
14
, if
\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|\geq\beta\max\{1,\|x\|^{a+1}\}
, then

\varphi^{*}(y)\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle y,x\rangle+2^{-2a-1}\beta^{-\frac{1}{a+1}}\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|^{\frac{2+a}{1+a}}.

(86)

Proof.

Let
v=\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|
.
We start again from (
82
). Let
e=\frac{y-\nabla\varphi(x)}{\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|}
and choose
z=x+e\cdot t\cdot(v/\beta)^{\frac{1}{1+a}}
for some parameter
t
to fix. Thanks to the condition on
v
, we may choose the upper bound
r
on the norm of
x
and
z
as
r=(t+1)(v/\beta)^{\frac{1}{1+a}}
. As
v\geq\beta
, it holds that
\langle r\rangle^{a}\leq(t+2)^{a}(v/\beta)^{\frac{a}{1+a}}
. We obtain

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\langle z,y\rangle-q_{x}(z)

\displaystyle\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle x,\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle+\langle z,y-\nabla\varphi(x)\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}\langle r\rangle^{a}t^{2}(v/\beta)^{\frac{2}{1+a}}

\displaystyle\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle y,x\rangle+tv^{1+\frac{1}{1+a}}\beta^{-\frac{1}{1+a}}-\frac{\beta}{2}t^{2}(2+t)^{a}(v/\beta)^{\frac{2+a}{1+a}}

\displaystyle\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle y,x\rangle+\beta^{-\frac{1}{1+a}}v^{\frac{2+a}{1+a}}(t-\frac{t^{2}(2+t)^{a}}{2}).

We let
t=2^{-2a}
to obtain that
t-\frac{t^{2}(2+t)^{a}}{2}\geq\frac{t}{2}
, concluding the proof.
∎

If one does not care about the tight exponent
\frac{2+a}{1+a}
, it is always possible to lower bound the last term of (
86
) by a term proportional to
\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|
(recall that we assume that
\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|\geq\beta
in this lemma).

We can also gather
14
and
15
together to obtain, with
\ell=\beta\max\{1,\|x\|^{a+1}\}
and
v=\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|
,

\varphi^{*}(y)\geq-\varphi(x)+\langle y,x\rangle+\min\{\frac{1}{2D_{x}}v^{2},C_{a,\beta}v\},

(87)

where
D_{x}
is bounded by a polynomial expression of degree
a(a+1)
in
\|x\|
.

Appendix C
Covering numbers and suprema of empirical processes

We give in this section proofs related to covering numbers. We start by a simple lemma that we will repeatedly use.

Lemma 16

(Covering of lines)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subset of a normed space
(E,\|\cdot\|)
, bounded by
R
. Let
g\in\mathcal{F}
and consider the set of lines
\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\{tf+(1-t)g:\ f\in\mathcal{F},\ 0\leq t\leq 1\}
. Then, there exists a constant
c
depending on
R
such that for
h>0
,

\log\mathcal{N}(h,\overline{\mathcal{F}},E)\leq\log\mathcal{N}(h/2,\mathcal{F},E)+c\log_{+}(1/h).

(88)

Proof.

Let
A
be a minimal
(h/2)
-covering of
\mathcal{F}
and let
T
be a
(h/(4R))
-covering of
[0,1]
. For
tf+(1-t)g\in\mathcal{F}
, we let
f_{0}
be the closest element to
f
in the covering
A
, and define
t_{0}
likewise. Then,

\displaystyle\|tf+(1-t)g-(t_{0}f_{0}+(1-t_{0})g)\|\leq\|f-f_{0}\|+2R|t-t_{0}|\leq h.

Therefore, the set
\{t_{0}f_{0}+(1-t_{0})g:\ f_{0}\in A,\ t_{0}\in T\}
is a
h
-covering of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
whose size satisfies the inequality in (
88
).
∎

Lemma 17

(Covering numbers under affine transformations and dilations)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
. Assume that linear functions are bounded for this norm and that
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|\leq\sigma
. For
R\geq 1
, let

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\{x\mapsto b\varphi(x)+\langle t,x\rangle:\varphi\in\mathcal{F},\ 0\leq b\leq R,\|t\|\leq R\},

(89)

Then, for
h>0
,

\log\mathcal{N}(h,\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\|\cdot\|)\leq\log\mathcal{N}(h/(2R),\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|)+c\log_{+}(1/h),

(90)

where
c
depends on
\sigma
,
R
,
d
and on
\|\cdot\|
.

Proof.

Assume that the norm
\|\cdot\|
of the function
x\mapsto\langle t,x\rangle
is smaller than
L
for every
\|t\|\leq R
.
Let
h>0
. Let
A
be a minimal
h/(2R)
-covering of
\mathcal{F}
, let
B
be a minimal
h/(4\sigma)
-covering of
[0,R]
, and let
T
be a minimal
h/(4L)
-covering of
B(0;R)
. Let
b\varphi+\langle t,\cdot\rangle\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, and consider the function
b_{0}\varphi_{0}+\langle t_{0},\cdot\rangle
obtained by considering the closest
\varphi_{0}
to
\varphi
,
b_{0}
to
b
, and
t_{0}
to
t
in their respective nets. Then,

\displaystyle\|b\varphi+\langle t,\cdot\rangle-(b_{0}\varphi_{0}+\langle t_{0},\cdot\rangle)\|\leq|b-b_{0}|\sigma+R\|\varphi-\varphi_{0}\|+L\|t-t_{0}\|\leq h.

Hence, the set
\{b_{0}\varphi_{0}+\langle t_{0},\cdot\rangle,\ \varphi_{0}\in A,b_{0}\in B,t_{0}\in T\}
is a
h
-net of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, whose size satisfies the inequality in (
90
).
∎

We now prove
1
, that gives sufficient conditions for Condition
(C2)
to be satisfied.

Proof of
1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be any class of
(\beta,a)
-smooth potentials satisfying
(A1)
. We assume without loss of generality that
\supp(P)\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
. We can actually even assume that
\mathrm{dom}(\varphi)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, up to minor modifications in the general case.
Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\{t\varphi+(1-t)\overline{\varphi}:\ \varphi\in\mathcal{F},\ 0\leq t\leq 1\}
as in
(C2)
and let
\tau>0
. We first bound the covering numbers of
B_{\tau}
.

As a preliminary remark, let us notice that if
g\in B_{\tau}
, then
\|\nabla g(0)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}(0)\|\lesssim 1+\tau
. Indeed, let
\|\nabla g(0)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}(0)\|=K
. As
g
and
\overline{\varphi}
are
(\beta,a)
-smooth, it holds according to
11
that for all
x
such that
4\beta\langle x\rangle^{a+1}\leq K/2
, we have
\|\nabla g(x)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}(x)\|\geq K/2
. Hence,

\tau^{2}\geq\int\|\nabla g-\nabla\overline{\varphi}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P\geq\int_{4\beta\langle x\rangle^{a+1}\leq K/2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\frac{K^{2}}{4},

an inequality that is only possible if
K\lesssim 1+\tau
.
Hence, it holds that

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla g(0)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}(0)\|\leq K

(91)

for some constant
K
satisfying
K\lesssim 1+\tau
.

Let
h>0
. As the potentials in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
are
(\beta,a)
-smooth, it holds thanks to
9
and
11
that for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
,
\int_{|x|>R}|g(x)-\overline{\varphi}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)<h^{2}/16

for some choice of
R
of the form
R=c_{0}\log_{+}(K/h)^{q}
for some
c_{0},q>0
. According to
(C3)
, the local Poincaré inequality holds for all balls centered at a point in
B(0,R)
of radius smaller than
r_{\max}=\kappa\langle R\rangle^{-\theta}
. Let
B_{1},\dots,B_{I}
be a packing of
\supp P\cap B(0;R)
with balls of radius
\lambda h/(2\tau)
, for some parameter
\lambda
to be specified. In particular, the balls
\tilde{B}_{1},\dots,\tilde{B}_{I}
obtained by doubling the radius of the balls
B_{i}
cover
\supp P\cap B(0;R)
. Assume first that
\lambda h/\tau\leq r_{\max}
, so that the local Poincaré inequality can be applied to these balls.
We let
(\chi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq I}
be a partition of unity adapted to this covering. Let
f=g-\overline{\varphi}
and let
\tilde{f}=\sum_{i}(P_{\tilde{B}_{i}}f)\chi_{i}
. As the balls
B_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, each ball
\tilde{B}_{i}
can intersect at most
C_{d}
balls
\tilde{B}_{j}
for
i\neq j
, where
C_{d}
depends only on
d
. Hence

\displaystyle\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\|\chi_{i}(f-P_{\tilde{B}_{i}}f)\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{16}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\sum_{i=1}^{I}P(\tilde{B}_{i})\int_{\tilde{B}_{i}}|f-P_{\tilde{B}_{i}}f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P_{\tilde{B}_{i}}+\frac{h^{2}}{16}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}C_{\texttt{LPI}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{\lambda^{2}h^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{i}}\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P+\frac{h^{2}}{16},

where we use the local Poincaré inequality at the last step. Using once again that each
\tilde{B}_{i}
intersects at most
C_{d}
balls
\tilde{B}_{j}
, we have

\|f-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}\leq C_{d}^{2}C_{\texttt{LPI}}\lambda^{2}\frac{h^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\int\|\nabla f\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P+\frac{h^{2}}{16}\leq\frac{h^{2}}{4}

(92)

for
\lambda=1/\sqrt{4C_{\texttt{LPI}}C_{d}^{2}}
.

Furthermore, as
\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla g(0)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}(0)\|<K
, and as the functions in
\mathcal{G}
are smooth of order
(a,\beta)
, it holds thanks to
11
that
L=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\sup_{x\in B(0;R)}|g(x)-\overline{\varphi}(x)|
is at most of order polynomial in
\tau
. The vector
(P_{\tilde{B}_{i}}f)_{1\leq i\leq I}
is a subset of
[-L,L]^{I}
.

Equation

92
shows that a
(h/2)
-covering of
[-L,L]^{I}

for the
\infty
-norm induces a
h
-covering of
B_{\tau}
for the norm
L^{2}(P)
. This covering has a log-size of order
I\log_{+}(L/h)
,
where
I\lesssim(h/(\tau R))^{-m}
. This gives the desired bound when
\lambda h\leq r_{\max}\tau
.

It remains to bound the covering numbers for large values of
h<\tau
, that is when the inequality
h\lambda\leq r_{\max}\tau
is not satisfied.
Let
h_{\max}
be the largest value of
h
such
that this inequality is satisfied, and remark that
h_{\max}>c_{3}\tau/\log(K)^{q^{\prime}}

for some
c_{3},q^{\prime}>0
.
We use the bound

\log\mathcal{N}(h,B_{\tau},L^{2}(P))\leq\log\mathcal{N}(h_{\max},B_{\tau},L^{2}(P))\lesssim_{\log_{+}(1/\tau)}1.

As
h<\tau
, this bound is once again (up to polylogarithmic factors), of order
(h/\tau)^{-m}
, concluding the proof.

We now succinctly explain how to bound the covering numbers of
B_{\tau}^{*}
. As
\nabla\varphi_{0}
is locally Lipschitz continuous and
P
is a locally doubling measure satisfying a local Poincaré inequality, the probability measure
Q=(\nabla\varphi_{0})_{\sharp}P
also satisfies a local Poincaré inequality, see
(
Heinonen et al. 2015
, Lemma 8.3.18)
. Furthermore,
(\alpha,a)
-strong convexity of the potentials
g\in\mathcal{G}
ensures that the potentials

have sublinear growth (
13
). As for the previous bound, we obtain that
\sup_{g^{*}\in\mathcal{G}^{*}}\|\nabla g^{*}(0)-\nabla\overline{\varphi}^{*}(0)\|\lesssim 1+\tau
and that
\int_{|T_{0}(x)|>R}|g^{*}(T_{0}(x))-\nabla\overline{\varphi}^{*}(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\leq h^{2}/32
for
R\lesssim_{\log(K/h)}1
. Furthermore, as
T_{0}
is

-Lipschitz continuous on

, the covering number of

is bounded (up to polylogarithmic constants) by the covering number of
\supp(P)\cap B(0;R)
. We may now proceed as in the proof of the bound of the covering numbers of
B_{\tau}
by consdering a packing number of
T_{0}(\supp(P)\cap B(0;R))
and using that
Q
satisfies a local Poincaré inequality.
∎

At last, we prove

, which gives bound on the suprema of empirical processes.

Proof of
7
.

Consider first the case
\eta=0
. For
\varepsilon=0
, the result is given by
(
Vaart and Wellner 2023
, Theorem 2.14.21)
. The case
\varepsilon>0
is obtained by a truncation scheme.
For
g\in\mathcal{G}
, let
X_{g}=\sqrt{n}(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g)
. As in the proof of
(
Vaart and Wellner 2023
, Theorem 2.14.21)
, we remark that by Bernstein’s inequality the process
(X_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
satisfies a property of the form

\forall x>0,\ f,g\in\mathcal{G},\ \mathbb{P}(|X_{f}-X_{g}|>c_{1}\sqrt{x}\|f-g\|_{L^{2}(P)}+c_{2}x\frac{\|f-g\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{n}})\leq 2e^{-x}

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are numerical constants.
Let
\varepsilon_{0,2}=\sigma
and
\varepsilon_{0,\infty}=M
.
Assume without loss of generality that
0\in\mathcal{G}
, and, for
i\in\{2,\infty\}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{0,i}=\{0\}
. For
j\geq 1
, let
\varepsilon_{j,i}
be the largest
h\leq\varepsilon_{0,i}
such that
H_{i}(h)\geq 2^{j}
. As the function
H_{i}
is increasing and converges to
+\infty
as
h\to 0
, the sequence
(\varepsilon_{j,i})_{j\geq 0}
is a nonincreasing sequence converging to
0
. More precisely, this sequence is constant equal to
\varepsilon_{0,i}
until the largest value
j=j_{0}
such that
H_{i}(\varepsilon_{0,i})\geq 2^{j_{0}}
, and is given for
j>j_{0}
by
\varepsilon_{j,i}=H_{i}^{-1}(2^{j})
. Let
J\geq 1
be such that
\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}>\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{J,\infty}
. The condition
\varepsilon<M=\varepsilon_{0,\infty}
and the fact that the sequence converges to
0
ensures the existence of such an index. Moreover, we have
\varepsilon_{J,\infty}\leq\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0,\infty}
, so necessarily
\varepsilon_{J,\infty}=H_{\infty}^{-1}(2^{J})
.

For
i\in\{2,\infty\}
and
j\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{G}_{j,i}
be a minimal
(2\varepsilon_{j,i})
-covering of the set
\mathcal{G}
for the metric
\|\cdot\|_{i}
. If
\varepsilon_{j,i}=\varepsilon_{0,i}
, we can simply pick
\mathcal{G}_{j,i}=\{0\}
. Otherwise, we have,
\log|\mathcal{G}_{j,i}|\leq H_{i}(2\varepsilon_{j,i})\leq 2^{j}
. By a standard argument, we may assume that the sets

\{\mathcal{G}_{j,i}\}_{j=1,\dots,J}
are nested, at the price of increasing their logarithmic size by a constant factor.
The net
\mathcal{G}_{j,i}
induces a partition
P_{j,i}
of
\mathcal{G}
of the same cardinality, where each cell in the partition is given by the set of points in
\mathcal{G}
whose nearest neighbor (in the appropriate distance) is a certain element of
\mathcal{G}_{j,i}
. For all pairs of cells
Q_{1}
,
Q_{2}
from
P_{j,2}
,
P_{j,\infty}
, respectively, consider the cells of the form
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
. Let
\pi_{j}
be a piecewise constant function on each of the cells, equal to an arbitrary point of the given cell. Let
\mathcal{G}_{j}=\{\pi_{j}g:\ g\in\mathcal{G}\}
. Note that by construction,
d_{i}(g,\pi_{j}g)\leq 2\varepsilon_{j,i}
for
i\in\{2,\infty\}
, while
\log|\mathcal{G}_{j}|\leq\log(|\mathcal{G}_{j,1}||\mathcal{G}_{j,2}|)\leq c_{3}2^{j}
for some numerical constant
c_{3}>0
. The nestedness of the partitions
\{\mathcal{G}_{j,i}\}_{j=1,\dots,J}
implies that for
1\leq j\leq J
,
\pi_{j-1}\pi_{j}g=\pi_{j-1}g
. As
\pi_{0}g=0
, we write

\displaystyle|X_{g}|=|X_{g}-X_{\pi_{0}g}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|X_{\pi_{j}g}-X_{\pi_{j-1}g}|+\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|X_{\pi_{J-1}g}-X_{g}|.

According to
(
Vaart and Wellner 2023
, Lemma 2.2.13)
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|X_{\pi_{j}g}-X_{\pi_{j-1}g}|]\leq c_{4}(\varepsilon_{j,2}2^{j/2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{j,\infty}2^{j}}{\sqrt{n}})

for some numerical constant
c_{4}>0
. Note also that
|X_{\pi_{J-1}g}-X_{g}|\leq 2\sqrt{n}\|g-\pi_{J-1}g\|_{\infty}\leq 4\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}
. By assumption, it holds that

H_{\infty}(K\varepsilon)\leq\frac{1}{2}H_{\infty}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{1}{2}H_{\infty}(\varepsilon_{J,\infty})=2^{J-1}.

Hence,
\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}\leq K\varepsilon
. We obtain

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g)|=\mathbb{E}[\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|X_{g}|]\leq 4K\sqrt{n}\varepsilon+c_{4}\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(\varepsilon_{j,2}2^{j/2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{j,\infty}2^{j}}{\sqrt{n}}).

(93)

Let us assume that
J\geq 2
, for otherwise the sum is empty and the conclusion holds. By construction, for all
j\geq 1
,
H_{\infty}(h)\geq 2^{j}
for
h\leq\varepsilon_{j,\infty}
.
Hence, it holds that

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(\varepsilon_{j-1,\infty}-\varepsilon_{j,\infty})2^{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\int_{\varepsilon_{j,\infty}}^{\varepsilon_{j-1,\infty}}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\leq\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}}^{\varepsilon_{0,\infty}}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

As
\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\varepsilon_{j-1,\infty}2^{j}=2\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\varepsilon_{j,\infty}2^{j}
,
it holds that

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\varepsilon_{j,\infty}2^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(\varepsilon_{j-1,\infty}-\varepsilon_{j,\infty})2^{j}+\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}2^{J}-2\varepsilon_{0,\infty}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}(\varepsilon_{j-1,\infty}-\varepsilon_{j,\infty})2^{j}+\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}2^{J}.

We have
2^{J-1}\leq H_{\infty}(h)
for
h\leq\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}
, hence
\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}2^{J}\leq 4\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}/2}^{\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h
. This yields

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\varepsilon_{j,\infty}2^{j}\leq 4\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}/2}^{\varepsilon_{0,\infty}}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

Likewise, we find

\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\varepsilon_{j,2}2^{j/2}\leq c_{5}\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,2}/2}^{\varepsilon_{0,2}}\sqrt{H_{2}(h))}\,\mathrm{d}h

for some constant
c_{5}
. In total, we have

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g)|\lesssim K\sqrt{n}\varepsilon++\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,2}/2}^{\varepsilon_{0,2}}\sqrt{H_{2}(h)}\,\mathrm{d}h+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}/2}^{\varepsilon_{0,\infty}}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

(94)

By construction,
\varepsilon_{J-1,\infty}\geq\varepsilon
. Let
0<\tilde{\varepsilon}<\sigma=\varepsilon_{0,2}
be any number such that
H_{2}(\tilde{\varepsilon})\geq H_{\infty}(\varepsilon)\geq 2^{J-1}
, so that
\varepsilon_{J-1,2}\geq\tilde{\varepsilon}
. We therefore obtain

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g)|\lesssim K\sqrt{n}\varepsilon+\int_{\tilde{\varepsilon}/2}^{\sigma}\sqrt{H_{2}(h)}\,\mathrm{d}h+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\varepsilon/2}^{M}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

(95)

This proves the result in the case
\eta=0
.

For the case
\eta>0
, we let
L=(C\log_{+}(1/\varepsilon))^{1/p}
and write
g^{L}
the function
g\in\mathcal{G}
restricted to
B(0,L)
. Then, one can check that according to
9
, for
C
large enough with respect to the

-Orlicz norm of
\xi\sim\mathbb{P}
,
\eta
and

,

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g-g^{L})|\leq 2M\sqrt{n}\int_{\|\xi\|>L}\langle\xi\rangle^{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\xi)\leq 2M\sqrt{n}\varepsilon\langle L\rangle^{\eta}.

(96)

Let
\mathcal{G}^{L}=\{g^{L}:\ g\in\mathcal{G}\}
.
The
L^{\infty}
-norm on
B(0,L)
and the
L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta})
-norm are related by

\forall f:B(0,L)\to\mathbb{R},\ \|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\langle L\rangle^{\eta}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta})}.

Hence, for

Furthermore, any function

satisfies
|g^{L}(\xi)|\leq M^{\prime}=M\langle L\rangle^{\eta}
. We apply the case
\eta=0
to
\mathcal{G}^{L}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon\langle L\rangle^{\eta}
, with
H_{2}
the upper bound on the

-covering numbers and
H^{\prime}_{\infty}
the upper bound on the
L^{\infty}
-covering number. It holds that

\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P})(g^{L})|

\displaystyle\lesssim K\sqrt{n}\varepsilon\langle L\rangle^{\eta}+\int_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{\sigma}\sqrt{H_{2}(h)}\,\mathrm{d}h+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\varepsilon\langle L\rangle^{\eta}}^{M\langle L\rangle^{\eta}}H_{\infty}(h\langle L\rangle^{-\eta})\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle\lesssim K\sqrt{n}\varepsilon\langle L\rangle^{\eta}+\int_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}\sqrt{H_{2}(h)}\,\mathrm{d}h+\frac{\langle L\rangle^{\eta}}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{M}H_{\infty}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

This concludes the proof together with (

Appendix D
Affine transformations of potentials

In
Section

4.1
, we claimed that as a particular case of our general theorems, we cover the case where

is the normal distribution and

(97)

is the set of quadratics. However, as the set

is not precompact, the covering numbers of

are infinite. Thus, condition
(C1)
is not satisfied and
2
cannot be readily applied. Despite this, all potentials in
\mathcal{F}_{\text{quad}}
can be obtained by scaling and translating potentials from the set

whose covering numbers can easily be controlled, thus satisfying condition
(C1)
. The next proposition asserts that if one performs such transformations on an arbitrary “base set”
\mathcal{F}_{0}
satisfying condition
(C1)
,
then
2
also holds on the larger class
\mathcal{F}
that is induced by scaling and translating.
To this end, given a class of potentials
\mathcal{F}
and
0\leq r_{1},r_{2}\leq+\infty
, we introduce the modified class

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{r_{1},r_{2}}\coloneqq\{x\mapsto b\varphi(x)+\langle t,x\rangle:\ \varphi\in\mathcal{F},\ 0\leq b\leq r_{1},\ \|t\|\leq r_{2}\}.

(98)

Proposition 8
.

Let
P
be a subexponential distribution and let
Q=(\nabla\varphi_{0})_{\sharp}P
for some
(\beta,a)
-smooth convex potential
\varphi_{0}
with
\beta,a\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of potentials satisfying
(A1)
with
\nabla\varphi(0)=0
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
, that contains at least one
\alpha
-strongly convex potential for some
\alpha>0
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{r_{1},+\infty}
for some
r_{1}\geq 1
. We consider two scenarios:

1.

either
r_{1}<+\infty
;

2.

or either
r_{1}=+\infty
and every potential
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
is convex, and
\alpha
-strongly convex in at least one direction in the sense that there exists a unit vector
e
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(y)+\langle x-y,\nabla\varphi(y)\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\langle x-y,e\rangle|^{2}
. Furthermore, the marginal of
P
in the direction
e
has a bounded density.

Then, there exists
r>0
and
\ell>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}\}]\leq c\exp\left(-Cn^{\ell}\right),

(99)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{r,r}
, with constants
r
,
\ell
,
c
and
C
depending on the different parameters involved and on
P
.

Proof.

Let us write the sample
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\sim Q
as
Y_{i}=\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})
, where
X^{\prime}_{i}\sim P
. We let
P^{\prime}_{n}
be the empirical measure associated with the points
X^{\prime}_{i}
. We write
m_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
the empirical mean of
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
.
We can write
\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}(x)=b\varphi(x)+\langle t,x\rangle
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
, and
b
and
t
are two parameters. Let
\overline{\varphi}
be an
\alpha
-strongly convex potential in
\mathcal{F}
.
Note that
S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})-S_{n}(\overline{\varphi})\leq 0
. We have for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}(y)=b\varphi^{*}\left(\frac{y-t}{b}\right),

so that

\displaystyle S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})=P_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})+Q_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}^{*})

\displaystyle=bP_{n}(\varphi)+\langle t,m_{n}\rangle+\frac{b}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{*}\left(\frac{\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t}{b}\right).

Our goal is to show that the condition
S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})-S_{n}(\overline{\varphi})\leq 0
implies that both
b
and
\|t\|
are upper bounded by some parameter
r
, which implies that
\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}
. In particular, we then have
\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}=\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}
.

Bound on
b
:

In the first scenario,
b\leq b_{\max}=r_{1}
. In the second scenario, by definition of the convex conjugate, it holds that

b\varphi^{*}\left(\frac{\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t}{b}\right)\geq-b\varphi(m_{n})+\langle m_{n},\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t\rangle.

(100)

Hence,
S_{n}(\overline{\varphi})\geq S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})\geq b(P_{n}(\varphi)-\varphi(m_{n}))+\langle m_{n},P^{\prime}_{n}(\nabla\varphi_{0})\rangle
. Assume without loss of generality that
\varphi
is
\alpha
-strongly convex in the direction
x_{1}
. We have by strong convexity that
P_{n}(\varphi)-\varphi(m_{n})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i,1}-m_{n,1}|^{2}=u_{n}
. Hence,

b\leq b_{\max}=\max\{1,\frac{S_{n}(\overline{\varphi})-\langle m_{n},P^{\prime}_{n}(\nabla\varphi_{0})\rangle}{u_{n}}\}.

(101)

Note that we have
b_{\max}\geq 1
, a property which will be used.

Bound on
\|t\|
:

Finding a bound on the norm of the parameter
t
proves to be more delicate, and relies on lower bounds on the convex conjugate given in
Appendix

B
. Apply (
87
) to
x=m_{n}
and
y=\frac{\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t}{b}
. The parameter
D_{x}
appearing in (
87
) can be upper bounded by a polynomial of order
a(a+1)
with respect to
m_{n}
. In total, we obtain a lower bound of the form

\displaystyle b\varphi^{*}\left(\frac{\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t}{b}\right)\geq-b\varphi(m_{n})+\langle m_{n},\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t\rangle+\frac{C}{\langle m_{n}\rangle^{a(a+1)}}\min\{v_{i}^{2},v_{i}\},

where
v_{i}=\|\frac{\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})-t}{b}-z\|
, and we define
z=\nabla\varphi(m_{n})
. Assuming without loss of generality that
\|t\|\geq 1
, we lower bound
v_{i}^{2}
,

\displaystyle v_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\|t\|^{2}-2\|t\|(\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})\|+b_{\max}\|z\|)}{b_{\max}^{2}}\geq\frac{\|t\|-2\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})\|-2b_{\max}\|z\|}{b_{\max}^{2}}\eqqcolon U_{i},

as well as
v_{i}
,

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\|t\|-\|\nabla\varphi_{0}(X^{\prime}_{i})\|-b_{\max}\|z\|}{b_{\max}}\geq U_{i},

where we use that
b_{\max}\geq 1
.
We obtain

\begin{split}S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})&\geq bP_{n}(\varphi)+\langle t,m_{n}\rangle-b\varphi(m_{n})+\langle m_{n},P^{\prime}_{n}(\nabla\varphi_{0})-t\rangle+\frac{C}{n\langle m_{n}\rangle^{a(a+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\\
\end{split}

(102)

As
\varphi
is
(\beta,a)
-smooth with
\nabla\varphi(0)=0
, we have
|\varphi(x)|\leq 4\beta\langle x\rangle^{a+2}
according to
11
.
Therefore, applying Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle S_{n}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})

\displaystyle\geq-4\beta b_{\max}(P_{n}(\langle x\rangle^{a+2})+P(\langle x\rangle^{a+2}))+\langle m_{n},P^{\prime}_{n}(\nabla\varphi_{0})\rangle+\frac{C}{n\langle m_{n}\rangle^{a(a+1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}.

As
b_{\max}\geq 1
, we obtain a bound of the form

\displaystyle\frac{C\|t\|}{\langle m_{n}\rangle^{a(a+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2CP^{\prime}_{n}(\|\nabla\varphi_{0}\|)+2Cb_{\max}\|z\|+b_{\max}^{2}\langle m_{n},P^{\prime}_{n}(\nabla\varphi_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+b_{\max}^{3}(S_{n}(\overline{\varphi})+4\beta(P_{n}(\|x\|^{a+2})+P(\|x\|^{a+2}))).

By
11
,
12
, and using that
\overline{\varphi}
is
\alpha
-strongly convex, we find that

is bounded by a polynomial expression
T_{\max}
with variables given by
b_{\max}
and the moments of
P_{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
.

Conclusion:

Let
r\geq 1
. One can make the key observation that if
\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}
, then either
b_{\max}>r
or
T_{\max}>r
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}\}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}\}]

\displaystyle\quad+2\|\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\mathbb{P}(\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}).

By
11
, it holds that

\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}(x)\|\leq b_{\max}\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}(x)\|+T_{\max}\|x\|\leq(b_{\max}\beta\langle x\rangle^{a}+T_{\max})\|x\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}-\nabla\varphi_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P)}\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\neq\hat{\varphi}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{r}}\}]\leq c\mathbb{E}[b_{\max}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{b_{\max}>r\}]+c\mathbb{E}[T_{\max}^{2}\mathbf{1}\{T_{\max}>r\}]

for some constant

.
In the first scenario,

is constant and it remains to bound quantities of the form

and

Lemma 18
.

Let
P
be a subexponential distribution with
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim P
a sample of i.i.d. random variables. Then, for all
q\geq 1
, there exists
r>0

\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|X_{i}\|^{q}\mathbf{1}\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|X_{i}\|^{q}>r\}]\leq c\exp\left(-Cn^{1/(2q)}\right).

(103)

Proof.

Let
Z=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|X_{i}\|^{q}
. According to
(
Vaart and Wellner 2023
, Theorem 2.14.23)
, as the random variable

for

We obtain the conclusion by integrating this bound.

Lemma 19
.

Under the assumptions of the second scenario in
8
, for all
q\geq 1
there exists
r>0
such that
\mathbb{E}[u_{n}^{-q}\mathbf{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}>r\}]\leq Ce^{-cn^{1/4}}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[u_{n}^{-q}\mathbf{1}\{u_{n}^{-1}>r\}]=\int_{r^{q}}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(u_{n}^{-q}>x)\,\mathrm{d}x=q\int_{0}^{r^{-1}}\mathbb{P}(u_{n}<t)t^{1-q}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We bound

or

When

, we use the crude bound

, where

is the amplitude of the sample. Hence,

. Consider intervals of the form
B_{k}=[\sqrt{2nt}k,\sqrt{2nt}(k+2)]
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
A_{n}<\sqrt{2nt}
, then all the points
X_{i}
are in the same interval

, so that

We use two different bounds on

. Second, as
P
is subexponential,

when
\sqrt{2nt}|k|>c_{0}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(u_{n}<t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{n\log(1/t)}{\sqrt{nt}}(2p_{\max}\sqrt{2nt})^{n}+\sum_{C\sqrt{2nt}|k|>n\log(1/t)/2}e^{-C\sqrt{2nt}|k|}

\displaystyle\lesssim n\log(1/t)(C\sqrt{nt})^{n-1}+t^{n/2}\lesssim n\log(1/t)(C\sqrt{nt})^{n-1},

where we compute the geometric sum that appears at the second line. Thus,

\int_{0}^{n^{-2}}\mathbb{P}(u_{n}<t)t^{q-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq n(C\sqrt{n})^{n-1}\int_{0}^{n^{-2}}\log(1/t)t^{n/2+q-3/2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim_{\log n}n^{-2q}\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{n-1}.

(104)

When
t>n^{-2}
, we use that

is concentrated around the variance

. Indeed,

belongs the Orlicz space of exponent

,
(
Vaart and Wellner 2023

implies that

for some

. In particular, as long as

Hence, using (
104

\displaystyle\varphi_{c}^{*}(y)-\varphi_{0,c}^{*}(y)

In total, as

(109)

. In total, we obtain that

(110)

Then,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{c}-\overline{\varphi}_{c}\|_{L^{2}(P)}+\|\varphi_{c}^{*}-\overline{\varphi}_{c}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{c}-\varphi_{0,c}\|_{L^{2}(P)}+\|\overline{\varphi}_{c}-\varphi_{0,c}\|_{L^{2}(P)}+\|\varphi_{c}^{*}-\varphi_{0,c}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\overline{\varphi}_{c}^{*}-\varphi_{0,c}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq(3R+2C_{\texttt{PI}}^{1/2})(\|\nabla\overline{\varphi}-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(P)}+2\|\nabla\varphi_{0}-\nabla\overline{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(P)})

\displaystyle\leq(3R+2C_{\texttt{PI}}^{1/2})(\tau+2\sqrt{C_{0}\ell^{\star}}),

where we apply
1
at the last line.

As

by assumption, we obtain the conclusion.

Appendix H
Besov spaces

We now prove the results in
Section

4.4.1
by recalling relevant facts about Besov spaces, which can be found in many textbooks (see e.g.,
Giné and Nickl 2021
). The Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
are a family of functional spaces, with
s
representing a regularity index, that can be defined using a wavelet basis. A wavelet basis is an orthonormal basis
(\Gamma_{j,k})_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfy some desirable properties. Let
s>0
. We will always assume that the regularity of the wavelet basis is large enough with respect to
s
and
d
. Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. If a function
\varphi
admits a wavelet representation

\varphi=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\gamma_{k,j}\Gamma_{k,j},

(118)

then, the Besov norm
B^{s}_{p,q}
of
\varphi
is defined as

\|\varphi\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\|\gamma\|_{b^{s}_{p,q}}=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jq(s+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\gamma_{j,k}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(119)

with generalizations when
p
or
q
equal to
\infty
.

Let
R>0
. Each wavelet
\Gamma_{k,j}
has compact support of diameter of order
2^{-j}
, and, for a fixed
j
, the number of wavelets
\Gamma_{k,j}
that intersect the ball
B(0;R)
is of order
R^{d}2^{jd}
. Let
I_{j}(R)
be the set of indexes
k
such that this intersection is nonempty. Let
J_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
B(0;R)
is included in the support of a single wavelet
\Gamma_{k,J_{0}}
. We can pick
J_{0}
such that
J_{0}\gtrsim-\log R
.
Let
\eta(x)=\alpha\|x\|^{2}/8
.
We define the set
\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
as the set of functions of the form

\eta+\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{J}\sum_{k\in I_{j}(R)}\gamma_{k,j}\Gamma_{k,j},

where,
\|\gamma\|_{b^{s}_{1,\infty}}\leq L
for some
L>0
.

To put it another way, functions
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
are functions with finite wavelet expansions up to depth
J
and bounded
B^{s}_{1,\infty}
norm on a set of size roughly
R
, whereas we add a quadratic term to ensure that they are strongly convex at infinity. Note however that functions in
\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
are not necessarily convex.

Let us show that we can apply
3
to obtain the rate displayed in (
39
). By hypothesis, we suppose that
P
satisfies
(A2)
,
(C3)
and
(C4)
. In particular,
(C2)
is satisfied. Furthermore, as the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-norm is controlled by the
B^{2}_{1,\infty}
-norm and that
s\geq 2
, all potentials in
\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
are
(\beta,0)
-smooth for some
\beta
of order
L
, so that
(A1)
is satisfied. It remains to check the conditions
(P1)
and
(P2)
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
, with associated coordinates
\gamma^{1}
and
\gamma^{2}
. We have for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle|\varphi_{1}(x)-\varphi_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{j=J_{0}}^{J}\sum_{k\in I_{j}(R)}(\gamma_{j,k}^{1}-\gamma_{j,k}^{2})\Gamma_{k,j}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{d/2}2^{Jd/2}\|\gamma^{1}-\gamma^{2}\|_{\infty},

where we use a standard bound on the
L^{\infty}
-norm in terms of wavelet coefficients (see e.g.,
(
Hütter and Rigollet 2021
, Lemma 24)
). Note that only the coefficients
\gamma^{1}_{k,j}-\gamma^{2}_{k,j}
with
k\in I_{j}(R)
are non zero. Therefore, the bracketing number of
\mathcal{F}_{J}(R)
at scale
h
with respect to
L^{\infty}(\langle\ \cdot\ \rangle^{-\eta})
(for
\eta>2
) is controlled by the covering number at scale of order
hR^{-d/2}2^{-Jd/2}
of the
\ell_{\infty}
-ball in dimension
\sum_{j=-J_{0}}^{J}|I_{j}(R)|\lesssim R^{d}2^{Jd}
. By
Schütt 1984
, the logarithm of such a covering number is of order

R^{d}2^{Jd}\log(R^{d/2}2^{Jd/2}/h)\lesssim R^{d}2^{Jd}(\log(R)+J)\log_{+}(1/h),

that is condition
(P1)
holds.

It remains to check
(P2)
. Let
\gamma^{0}
be the vector of coefficients of
\varphi_{0}
in the wavelet basis, and let
\varphi_{0,J}=\eta+\psi_{0,J}
, where

(120)

As
s\geq 2
, the norm of the Hessian of

at

Appendix I
On the spectrum of kernels for RKHS

Here we provide some details about the spectrum of kernels, following the discussion of
Section

4.5
. To this end, recall that a kernel
\mathcal{K}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
defines a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) if for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
(the Hilbert space over
\mathcal{X}
) and all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(x)=\langle f,\mathcal{K}(\cdot,x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

In
Section

4.5
, we provided log-covering bounds from
Yang et al. 2020b
that required that the kernel have either finite spectrum, or exponentially decaying eigenvalues; the following is borrowed from
(
Yang et al. 2020b
, Section 2.2 and Appendix D)
. To this end, we note that
\mathcal{K}
induces an integral operator
T_{\mathcal{K}}:L^{2}(\mathcal{X})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
defined as

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{K}}f(y)=\int\mathcal{K}(y,x)f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
. Mercer’s theorem (see e.g.,
Schölkopf et al. 2002
) states that this operator has countably many, positive eigenvalues with corresponding eigenfunctions
\{(\sigma_{i},\vartheta_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
which form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
, whence the kernel admits the following spectral decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\vartheta_{i}(x)\vartheta_{i}(y)\,.

(121)

The two conditions we presented are the following:

1.

\gamma
-finite spectrum
:
\sigma_{j}=0
for all
j\geq\gamma
,

2.

\gamma
-exponential decay
: there exist absolute constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that for all
j\geq 1
,
\sigma_{j}\leq C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}j^{\gamma})
,

where for the latter, we also require that there exist constants
\tau\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}),C_{\vartheta}>0
such that
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sigma_{j}^{\tau}|\vartheta_{j}(x)|\leq C_{\vartheta}
for all
j\geq 1
. In fact, over the sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that the Gaussian kernel exhibits
\gamma
-exponential decay for any
\tau>0

(
Yang et al. 2020b
, Appendix B.3)
, wherein several more examples of kernels are presented. The following is a proposition from
Yang et al. 2020b
, which is an upper bound on the covering numbers we employ in
Section

4.5
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}
be the unit ball in an RKHS corresponding to some kernel
\mathcal{K}
i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}\coloneqq\{f:\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1\}
, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a kernel with
\gamma
-finite spectrum, or
\gamma
-exponentially decaying spectrum, as described above, such that
\sup_{z}\mathcal{K}(z,z)\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(h,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq\begin{cases}C\gamma(\log(1/h)+c)&\gamma\text{-finite spectrum}\,,\\
C(\log(1/h)+c)^{1+1/\gamma}&\gamma\text{-exponentially decaying spectrum}\,,\end{cases}

where the absolute constants
c,C>0
depend on
C_{\vartheta},C_{1},C_{2},\gamma
and
\tau
.

\begin{split}\int&|\varphi_{1}(T_{1}^{-1}(T_{0}(x)))-\varphi_{2}(T_{1}^{-1}(T_{0}(x)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)\\
&\qquad\lesssim CM_{\max}\int|\varphi_{1}(T_{1}^{-1}\circ T_{0}(r(u)u))-\varphi_{2}(T_{1}^{-1}\circ T_{0}(r(u)u))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P_{U}(u).\end{split}

(132)

We use the change of variables
\Psi(u)=r(u)T_{1}^{-1}\circ T_{0}(u)
. The function
\Psi
is one-to-one. Indeed, if
u
and
u^{\prime}
are such that
T_{1}^{-1}\circ T_{0}(u)
is positively colinear to
T_{1}^{-1}\circ T_{0}(u^{\prime})
, one can see by composing by
T_{1}
and using the homogeneity properties of
T_{1}
and
T_{0}
that
u
and
u^{\prime}
are also positively colinear (and therefore equal as they both belong to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
). As
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{0}
are smooth and strongly convex on a ball, the function
\Psi
is therefore a diffeomorphism onto its image, whose Jacobian is bounded away from zero and infinity. As
P_{U}
is assumed to have a density bounded away from zero on infinity on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, this shows that

\int|\varphi_{1}(\Psi(u))-\varphi_{2}(\Psi(u))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P_{U}(u)\lesssim\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(P_{U})}.

Applying the same argument to
\int|\varphi_{1}(T_{2}^{-1}(T_{0}(x)))-\varphi_{2}(T_{2}^{-1}(T_{0}(x)))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)
, we obtain that

\|\varphi_{1}^{*}-\varphi_{2}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\lesssim\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(P_{U})}.

A similar argument shows that
\|\nabla\varphi_{1}^{*}-\nabla\varphi_{2}^{*}\|\gtrsim\|\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(P_{U})}
. Hence, we conclude as for
B_{\tau}
that the the covering numbers of
B_{\tau}^{*}
satisfy the condition in
(C2)
.

Finally, we prove the minimax lower bound. Consider
\mathcal{C}^{s+\frac{d+1}{2}}(B(0;1))
to be the space of functions of regularity
s+\frac{d+1}{2}
on the unit ball
B(0;1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. According to
(
Hütter and Rigollet 2021
, Theorem 2 and Remark 3)
, the minimax rate of estimation for potentials
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{s+\frac{d+1}{2}}(B(0;1))
is of order at least
n^{-\frac{2s+d-1}{2s+2d-3}}
. According to Proposition 5 and the homogeneous reformulation in
Bach 2017
, the space
\mathcal{C}^{s+\frac{d+1}{2}}(B(0;1))
is a subset of
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma}
. Therefore, the minimax lower bound on
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\sigma}
is larger than the one on
\mathcal{C}^{s+\frac{d+1}{2}}(B(0;1))
, giving the minimax lower bound.
∎

Lemma 24
.

For any
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, the inequality

((1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}+\lambda\varphi_{1})^{*}\leq(1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}^{*}+\lambda\varphi_{1}^{*}

holds.

Proof.

The proof follows from subadditivity of the supremum:

\displaystyle((1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}+\lambda\varphi_{1})^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x}\ \langle x,y\rangle-(1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}(x)-\lambda\varphi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x}\ (1-\lambda)\left\{\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi_{0}(x)\}+\lambda\{\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi_{1}(x)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\varphi_{0}^{*}(y)+\lambda\varphi_{1}^{*}(y)\,.\qed

Proof of

.

It suffices to prove convexity and smoothness of the functional
S_{n}(\cdot)
; as the constraint set
\Delta_{J}
is clearly convex. The runtime result is standard (see
(
Bubeck 2015
, Theorem 3.7)
for example).

The convexity of
S_{n}(\lambda)
in
\lambda
is inherited from the convexity of
S_{n}(\varphi)
in
\varphi
; see
24
.

To prove smoothness, we assume all the functions
\varphi_{j}
are strongly convex on
\Omega
.
Since
\Omega
is compact and convex functions are locally Lipschitz, we may also assume that each
\varphi_{j}
is Lipschitz on
\Omega
.
Recall that for a given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{J}_{+}
, the gradient of the
j^{\text{th}}
component reads

\displaystyle[\nabla_{\lambda}S_{n}(\lambda)]_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(X_{i})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{k})\,,

where
x^{*}_{k}
is the maximizer in
Equation

52
.
This expression depends on
\lambda
only through
x^{*}_{k}
.
Thus, for fixed
y=Y_{k}
, it suffices to show that there exists
L_{J}
such that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{\lambda})-\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{\eta})|\leq L_{J}\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|\,,

(133)

where
x^{*}_{\lambda}
and
x^{*}_{\lambda}
are the maximizers for two different parameters
\lambda
and
\eta
. Note that

\displaystyle y=\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{*}_{\lambda})=\nabla\varphi_{\eta}(x^{*}_{\eta})\,,

by virtue of
x^{*}_{\lambda}
(resp.
x^{*}_{\eta}
) being maximizers to the conjugate objective. Since
\varphi_{\lambda}
and
\varphi_{\eta}
are convex combinations of strongly convex functions, they are both strongly convex, and the following two inqualities hold for some
\alpha>0
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{*}_{\eta})\geq\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{*}_{\lambda})+\langle y,x^{*}_{\eta}-x^{*}_{\lambda}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\eta}(x^{*}_{\lambda})\geq\varphi_{\eta}(x^{*}_{\eta})+\langle y,x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|^{2}\,.

Adding these two inequalities and using the fact that each
\varphi_{j}
is
L
-Lipschitz for some

gives the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq[\varphi_{\lambda}-\varphi_{\eta}](x^{*}_{\eta})+[\varphi_{\eta}-\varphi_{\lambda}](x^{*}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(\lambda_{j}-\eta_{j})(\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{\eta})-\varphi_{j}(x^{*}_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\leq L\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\lambda_{j}-\eta_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{J}L\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|\|\lambda-\eta\|_{2}\,.

Altogether, this reads

\displaystyle\|x^{*}_{\lambda}-x^{*}_{\eta}\|\leq\sqrt{J}\alpha L\|\lambda-\eta\|_{2}\,.

Thus, the Lipschitz constant of each coordinate

\sqrt{J}\alpha L
, and
\nabla_{\lambda}S_{n}(\lambda)
is
L_{J}
Lipschitz for
L_{J}=J\alpha L
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
27
.

Let
F_{\rho}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
and
F_{\mu}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
.
The optimal transport map between
\rho
and
\mu
is given by
(
Santambrogio 2015
, Theorem 2.5)

T(x)=F_{\mu}^{-1}(F_{\rho}(x))\,,

which implies that

T^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\rho(x)}{\mu(F_{\mu}^{-1}(F_{\rho}(x)))}\,.

For two functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{R}
, we use the notation
f\asymp g
to indicate that there exist positive constants (depending on
c
and
C
) such that
cf(x)\leq g(x)\leq Cf(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
It therefore suffices to show that
\rho\asymp\mu(F_{\mu}^{-1}(F_{\rho}))
, or, equivalently, that

\rho\circ F_{\rho}^{-1}(t)\asymp\mu\circ F_{\mu}^{-1}(t)\quad\forall t\in(0,1)\,.

In the case of
\rho
, well known bounds on the Mills’ ratio
Duembgen 2010
imply

\rho(x)\asymp(1+|x|)\min\{F_{\rho}(x),1-F_{\rho}(x)\}

The assumption that
\rho\asymp\mu
implies that
F_{\rho}\asymp F_{\mu}
and
1-F_{\rho}\asymp 1-F_{\mu}
, which implies that

\mu(x)\asymp(1+|x|)\min\{F_{\mu}(x),1-F_{\mu}(x)\}

also holds.

We therefore obtain that
\rho
satisfies

\rho\circ F_{\rho}^{-1}(t)\asymp(1+|F_{\rho}^{-1}(t)|)\min\{t,1-t\}\quad\forall t\in(0,1)

and the analogous bound for
\mu
.
Hence, the desired claim is equivalent to

1+|F_{\rho}^{-1}(t)|\asymp 1+|F_{\mu}^{-1}(t)|\,.

It suffices to verify this claim for
t\to 0
and
t\to 1
, because when
t
is bounded away from
0
and
1
, both sides are of constant order.
By symmetry, we focus without loss of generality on the case
t\to 0
.

To conclude, we observe that since
F_{\rho}
and
F_{\mu}
are increasing, a bound of the form
c\rho\leq\mu\leq C\rho
implies

F_{\rho}^{-1}(t/C)\leq F_{\mu}^{-1}(t)\leq F_{\rho}^{-1}(t/c)\,.

Since
F_{\rho}(x)\asymp\frac{1}{(1+|x|)}e^{-x^{2}/2}
for
x\leq 0
, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{F_{\rho}^{-1}(t/c)}{F_{\rho}^{-1}(t/C)}=1\,.

Therefore, as
t\to 0
,
F_{\mu}^{-1}(t)\asymp F_{\rho}^{-1}(t)
, proving the claim.
∎

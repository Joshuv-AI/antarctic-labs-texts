Long-time asymptotics for coagulation equations with injection that do not have stationary solutions
Iulia Cristian, Marina A. Ferreira, Eugenia Franco, Juan J. L. Velázquez

Abstract
In this paper we study a class of coagulation equations including a source term that injects in the system clusters of size of order one. The coagulation kernel is homogeneous, of homogeneity γ < 1 \gamma<1 , such that K ⁡ ( x , y ) K(x,y) is approximately x γ + λ ​ y − λ x^{\gamma+\lambda}y^{-\lambda} , when x x is larger than y y . We restrict the analysis to the case γ + 2 ​ λ ≥ 1 \gamma+2\lambda\geq 1 . In this range of exponents, the transport of mass toward infinity is driven by collisions between particles of different sizes. This is in contrast with the case considered in [ 9 ] where γ + 2 ​ λ < 1 \gamma+2\lambda<1 . In that case, the transport of mass toward infinity is due to the collision between particles of comparable sizes. In the case γ + 2 ​ λ ≥ 1 \gamma+2\lambda\geq 1 , the interaction between particles of different sizes leads to an additional transport term in the coagulation equation that approximates the solution of the original coagulation equation with injection for large times. We prove the existence of a class of self-similar solutions for suitable choices of γ \gamma and λ \lambda for this class of coagulation equations with transport. We prove that for the complementary case such self-similar solutions do not exist.



Keywords:
coagulation equations, source term, long-time behavior.

Introduction

1.1
Aim of the paper

In this paper we study the long-time behavior of the coagulation equation with injection:

\partial_{t}f\left(t,x\right)=\mathbb{K}[f](t,x)+\eta\left(x\right),

(1.1)

where

\mathbb{K}[f](t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y-\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y

(1.2)

and where
\eta\geq 0
. We will assume in all the following that
\eta(x)\not\equiv 0
and that it is either compactly supported or it decays fast enough with the cluster size.

The study of problems with this form arises naturally in problems of aerosols and atmospheric science (
[
12
,
18
,
21
]
). In this context the function
f(t,x)
denotes
the density of clusters with size
x
at time
t
.

The collision operator
\mathbb{K}[f](t,x)
in (
1.2
) was introduced by Smoluchowski (see
[
19
]
). The kernel
K(x,y)
encodes the information about the mechanism driving the coagulation of clusters.
In this paper we are interested in kernels arising in atmospheric science applications. A common feature of these kernels is the homogeneity property. Indeed, in many cases the rate of aggregation scales like a power law with the cluster size. This means that the coagulation kernel
K(x,y)
satisfies

K(ax,ay)=a^{\gamma}K(x,y)\textup{ for }a>0\textup{ and }(x,y)\in\mathbb{(}0,\infty)^{2},

(1.3)

for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

On the other hand, since the coagulation process does not depend on the order in which the clusters of size
x
and
y
are chosen we have the symmetry property

K(x,y)=K(y,x)\textup{ for }(x,y)\in(0,\infty)^{2}.

(1.4)

We will assume that the coagulation kernel
K
satisfies

c_{2}\left[\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{y^{\lambda}}+\frac{y^{\gamma+\lambda}}{x^{\lambda}}\right]\leq K\left(x,y\right)\leq c_{1}\left[\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{y^{\lambda}}+\frac{y^{\gamma+\lambda}}{x^{\lambda}}\right]\textup{ and }\gamma+2\lambda\geq 0,

(1.5)

with
0<c_{2}\leq c_{1}<\infty
, where
\gamma
is the homogeneity parameter introduced in (
1.3
) and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
The polynomial bounds (
1.5
) are satisfied by many of the most relevant collision kernels arising in aerosol and atmospheric science, such as the
diffusive
coagulation kernel and the
free molecular
kernel (see for instance
[
12
]
).

Notice that the condition
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 0
in (
1.5
) does not imply any loss of generality. This can be seen from the fact that the function
x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}+x^{\beta}y^{\alpha}
can be written as
\left[\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{y^{\lambda}}+\frac{y^{\gamma+\lambda}}{x^{\lambda}}\right]
with
\gamma+\lambda=\max\{\alpha,\beta\}
and
-\lambda=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
.

Since we will consider solutions of (
1.1
), (
1.2
) in which
f(t,\cdot)
is a Radon measure, it is convenient to impose the following condition on the kernel
K
:

K\in\textup{C}((0,\infty)^{2}).

(1.6)

It is well known that part or all the mass of the solutions of (
1.1
), (
1.2
) can escape towards
x=\infty
in finite, or even zero time. This phenomenon is known as
gelation
. For a more detailed analysis on this matter, see for example
[
3
,
4
]
. In order to guarantee that gelation does not take place, we will assume in all the following that

\gamma<1\textup{ and }\gamma+\lambda<1.

(1.7)

See
[
5
]
for a more detailed discussion on the gelation regimes.

We expect that gelation does not take place under the weaker assumptions
\gamma\leq 1,\gamma+\lambda\leq 1
(see
[
3
]
). However, in the critical cases
\gamma=1
or
\gamma+\lambda=1
, the self-similar solutions are not defined using power laws to scale the particle sizes but most likely using exponential functions. Given that the analysis of these solutions would require arguments different from the ones in this paper, we will not consider this case here.

The existence of solutions for equation (
1.1
), (
1.2
) has been considered in
[
6
]
and in
[
7
]
. In this paper we study the long time behavior of the solutions of equation (
1.1
).
Due to the presence of the source we can expect the solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) to converge to a stationary non-equilibrium solution
\overline{f}=\overline{f}(x)
as
t\rightarrow\infty,
i.e. to a solution of

\mathbb{K}[f](x)+\eta(x)=0.

(1.8)

However, it turns out that if
\eta\not\equiv 0
and
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1,
a solution for (
1.8
) does not exist. In
[
13
]
, the discrete stationary coagulation model

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}K_{k,n-k}f_{k}f_{n-k}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}K_{k,n}f_{k}f_{n}+\overline{\delta}_{k,n}=0,\textup{ }k\geq 1,

(1.9)

has been studied for the explicit coagulation kernel

K_{k,n}=k^{\gamma+\lambda}n^{-\lambda}+k^{-\lambda}n^{\gamma+\lambda},\textup{ }k,n\in\mathbb{N}

(1.10)

when
|\gamma+\lambda|<1
,
|\lambda|<1
,
|\gamma|<1
.
Formal asymptotics for the large size behavior of the solutions
f_{k}
of (
1.9
) has been obtained in
[
13
]
. The results in that paper indicate that a solution of (
1.9
) exists if and only if
\gamma+2\lambda<1
.

In the case of general kernels satisfying the assumptions (
1.4
),
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), and source terms
\eta
decreasing
fast enough, it has been proved in
[
10
]
that the solutions of (
1.8
) (as well as its discrete counterpart) exist, if and only if
\gamma+2\lambda<1.

It is worth to remark that it has been proved in
[
10
]
that the solutions of (
1.8
) can be estimated, up to a multiplicative constant, from above and below by the power law
x^{-\frac{3+\gamma}{2}}
for large values of
x
.

Since in the case
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
a stationary solution of (
1.8
) does not exist, we cannot expect the solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) to behave as the stationary solution
\overline{f}
of
(
1.8
) as
t\rightarrow\infty
for
x
of order one.
It is then natural to ask what is the long time asymptotics of the solutions
to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) for large values of
t
and
x.
The scaling hypothesis that has been extensively used in the study of coagulation equations suggests that the mass of the particle distributions
f\left(t,x\right)
is concentrated in cluster sizes
x
of order
t^{p}
for a suitable exponent
p
that would be determined from dimensional considerations, which take into account the way in which the mass rescales in time. In the case of kernels
K\left(x,y\right)=x^{\gamma+\lambda}y^{-\lambda}+y^{-\lambda}x^{\gamma+\lambda}
,
-1<\lambda<0
with
0\leq\gamma+\lambda<1
and
\gamma<1
, it was suggested in
[
5
]
, using a combination of matched asymptotics and
numerical simulations, that the long time behavior of the solutions of (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is given by self-similar solutions with the form

f_{s}\left(t,x\right)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}\Phi\left(\xi\right)\ ,\ \ \xi=\frac{x}{t^{\frac{2}{1-\gamma}}}.

(1.11)

The approximation (
1.11
) can be expected to be valid for large
cluster sizes, i.e.
x\gg 1
. We will use from now the notation with
x\gg 1
to indicate large cluster sizes
x
.
In the case considered in
[
5
]
we have that
\gamma+2\lambda<1
and
therefore stationary solutions
f_{s}
solving (
1.8
) exist. In this case, (
1.8
) and (
1.11
) suggest that
\Phi\left(\xi\right)
behaves for small values of
\xi
as
K\xi^{-\frac{3+\gamma}{2}}
, for a suitable constant
K>0.
More precisely, plugging (
1.11
) in (
1.1
), (
1.2
), it follows that
\Phi
solves

-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\Phi_{\xi}-\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Phi=\mathbb{K}\left[\Phi\right],

(1.12)

where
\Phi
satisfies the following boundary condition at
\xi\rightarrow 0
that guarantees that there is a constant flux of particles from the origin:

\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{R}\textup{d}\xi\int_{R-\xi}\textup{d}\eta K\left(\xi,\eta\right)\xi\Phi\left(\xi\right)\Phi\left(\eta\right)=J,

(1.13)

with
J=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\eta\left(x\right)\textup{d}x.

The existence of solutions of equation
(
1.12
) satisfying the constant flux solution condition at
\xi=0
, (
1.13
),
has been rigorously proved in
[
9
]
for kernels
K

satisfying (
1.4
), (
1.5
), (
1.6
),
(
1.7
) with
\gamma+2\lambda<1
.

The picture described above, which combines the stationary behavior
\overline{f}
, (cf. (
1.8
)) for cluster sizes
x
of order one, and the self-similar behavior (
1.18
) for large cluster sizes, provides a rather complete description of the long time behavior of the solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) in the case
\gamma+2\lambda<1.

However, the same scenario cannot yield a description of the long time asymptotics of the solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) if
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1,
because, as explained above, in this case a solution of (
1.8
)
does not exist.

Notice that the existence/non-existence of solutions to (
1.8
) is related to the existence of stationary solutions of (
1.8
) yielding a constant flux of particles with the form of a power law, i.e.
\overline{f}(x)=cx^{-\frac{\gamma+3}{2}}
. These solutions exist for
\gamma+2\lambda<1
and they do not exist for
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
. In the framework of the wave turbulence it would be stated that the kernels
K
with
\gamma+2\lambda<1
satisfy the locality property, while the kernels
K
with
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
do not have the locality property, see
[
22
]
.

In this paper we study the long term behavior of the
solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) in the case
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
(and the non-gelling regime
\gamma<1
and
\gamma+\lambda<1
) when the kernel is homogeneous, hence it can be expressed as

K\left(x,y\right)=\left(x+y\right)^{\gamma}F\left(\frac{x}{x+y}\right)\ \ ,\ \ F\left(s\right)=F\left(1-s\right)\ \ \text{for }s\in\left(0,1\right).

(1.14)

Notice that (
1.5
) implies that

\overline{c}_{1}\leq F(s)\leq\overline{c}_{2}

for some
\overline{c}_{1},\overline{c}_{2}>0
.
We will assume in the following a condition that is more restrictive than (
1.5
), namely

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left[s^{\lambda}F\left(s\right)\right]=1.

(1.15)

In order to prove that in the case
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
there are no solutions of (
1.8
), the main idea used in
[
10
]
is based on the fact that, for this range of exponents, the transfer of clusters
of size
x
of order one towards very large cluster sizes is so fast
that the concentration of clusters with size of order one would become zero.

In the case of time dependent solutions having initially finite mass, this
increases linearly due to the fact that

\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}xf(t,x)\textup{d}x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\eta(x)\textup{d}x.

For large times, due to the
increase of the average cluster size, we might expect that, if
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
, there should be a fast transport of the newly injected
clusters of order one towards much larger cluster sizes. This almost instantaneous transport
results in small concentrations of clusters of order one for large times.

We now remark that the part of the coagulation operator
which describes the coagulation between particles of different sizes can be
approximated by means of a transport operator in the space of cluster sizes.
These arguments, that will be described in detail using formal asymptotics in
Section
2
, show that the solutions of
(
1.1
), (
1.2
) for large cluster sizes can be
approximated by means of the following equation if
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1

\partial_{t}f\left(t,x\right)+\frac{\partial_{x}\left(x^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\right)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,z\right)\textup{d}z}=\mathbb{K}\left[f\right](t,x),\ \ \text{for }x\gg 1.

(1.16)

We emphasize that the non-local transport term
\frac{\partial_{x}\left(x^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\right)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,z\right)\textup{d}z}
is a consequence of the presence
of the source
\eta\left(x\right)
in (
1.1
). The fact
that the contributions to the coagulation operator
\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]
which are due to the aggregation of particles with very different
sizes can be approximated by a differential operator has been extensively used in the literature of coagulation equations
(cf.
[
12
]
). The resulting first order terms are often
referred to represent
heterogeneous condensation
(cf.
[
12
]
).
On the other side, the term
\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]
in
(
1.16
) describes the aggregation of clusters of comparable sizes.

The solutions of (
1.16
) are expected to describe the asymptotic behavior of the solution of equation (
1.1
) both when
\gamma+2\lambda>1
and when
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, even if in these two cases we have two slightly different scenarios.
Namely, when
\gamma+2\lambda>1
, we will have that if
x
is of order
1
, then
f(t,x)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, while when
\gamma+2\lambda=1
we will have that if
x
is of order
1
, then
f(t,x)\rightarrow f_{s}(x)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, where
f_{s}
is a solution of

\mathbb{K}[f](x)+\eta(x)-x^{-\lambda}f(x)=0,

(1.17)

(compare with (
1.8
)). Due to the presence of the term
x^{-\lambda}f(x)
, equation (
1.17
) might have solutions when
\gamma+2\lambda=1
.

The scaling properties of (
1.16
) suggest that this equation is
compatible with the existence of self-similar solutions for equation (
1.16
) with the form

f_{s}\left(t,x\right)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}\Phi\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{2}{1-\gamma}}}\right).

(1.18)

Here the self-similar profile
\Phi
satisfies the following equation, obtained by substituting equality (
1.18
) in equation (
1.16
) and using the self-similar change of variables
\xi=\frac{x}{t^{\frac{2}{1-\gamma}}}
,

-\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Phi\left(\xi\right)-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\partial_{\xi}\Phi\left(\xi\right)+\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi\left(\eta\right)\textup{d}\eta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi\left(\xi\right)\right)=\mathbb{K}\left[\Phi\right]\left(\xi\right)\ \ ,\ \ \xi>0.

(1.19)

The main result of this paper is to determine the range
of exponents
\gamma
and
\lambda
satisfying
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
and the non-gelation conditions (
1.7
) for which self-similar solutions of
(
1.16
) with the form (
1.18
)
exist (see Figure
1
for a classification of these exponents). Specifically, we will prove the following. Suppose that
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
and that (
1.7
) holds. Then

•

If
\gamma>-1,
there exists at least one self-similar solution of
(
1.16
) with the form (
1.18
).

•

If
\gamma\leq-1
and
\gamma+2\lambda>1
, no solutions of (
1.16
) with the form
(
1.18
) exist (See Figure
1
).

•

If
\gamma\leq-1
and
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, we prove that there are no self-similar solutions
f_{s}
of the form (
1.18
) such that
\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\lambda}\Phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty
.

The meaning of the condition
\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\lambda}\Phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty
is that the number of clusters removed by the coagulation process in any bounded time interval is finite.
We therefore do not exclude the existence of a self-similar solution of equation (
1.16
) with
\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\lambda}\Phi(x)\textup{d}x=\infty
.

A remarkable property of the self-similar solutions of (
1.16

0<\xi\ <\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}):=\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}

\displaystyle M_{\gamma+\lambda}:=\int_{(0,\infty)}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

The fact solutions
\Phi
of (
1.19
) vanish in an interval
(0,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}))
means that, for large

the injected particles are transferred almost instantaneously to
clusters with sizes

Moreover, the fraction

for very long times. The existence of this ”minimal” cluster size for large

[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
,
[

]
,
[

In particular, it is worth to notice that this

\gamma+2\lambda<1
in
[

.

. In the case

1.2
Notation and plan of the paper

We use the notation
\mathbb{R}_{*}:=(0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty).

Given an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we denote with
C_{c}(I)
the Banach space of the functions on
I
that are continuous and compactly supported. We endow the space
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
with the supremum norm denoted with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}.

We denote with
\mathcal{M}_{+}(I)
the space of the non-negative Radon measures on
I
.
Given a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(I)
we denote with
\|\mu\|_{TV}
the total variation norm of
\mu.

For a compact interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\ast}
and two bounded measures
\mu,\nu,
we denote the Wasserstein metric by
W_{1},
namely

\displaystyle W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{||\varphi||_{\textup{Lip}}\leq 1}\int_{I}\varphi(x)(\mu-\nu)(\textup{d}x),

(1.20)

where the supremum is taken over the Lipschitz functions and where
\|f\|_{\textup{Lip}}=\|f\|_{\infty}+[f]_{\textup{Lip}}
, with
[f]_{\textup{Lip}}=\sup_{\underset{x\neq y}{x,y\in I}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}
.

To keep the notation lighter, we denote with
C
and
c
constants that might change from line to line in the computations. In addition, we use the notation
f\lesssim g,
for two functions
f,g
, to mean that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f\leq Cg
.

Moreover, given a measure
\mu
, we denote with
M_{\alpha}(\mu)
the
\alpha
moment of
\mu
, i.e.

M_{\alpha}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\alpha}\mu(\textup{d}x).

To simplify the notation, in some cases, we write
M_{\alpha}
instead
M_{\alpha}(\mu)
if the choice of the measure
\mu
is clear in the argument.

We use the notation
f\sim g
as
x\rightarrow x_{0}
to indicate that
\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1
, while we use the notation
f\approx g
to say that there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\frac{1}{M}\leq\frac{f}{g}\leq M.

As previously mentioned, we use the notation
x\gg 1
for large cluster sizes
x
. Additionally, for cluster sizes
x,y
, we denote
x\gg y
or
y\ll x
to mean that
x
is much larger than
y
. For two terms,
A
and
B
, we use the notation
A\simeq B
to mean informally that
A
can be approximated in terms of
B
in the region under consideration in the respective formula.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we present a heuristic motivation to study the existence of the self-similar solutions considered in this paper.
In Section
3
we present the main results of the paper regarding the existence and non-existence of a self-similar profile and its properties.
In Section
4
we explain the main ideas behind the proofs of existence and non-existence, skipping the technical difficulties of the proofs.
Section
5
deals with the proof of the existence of a self-similar solution for equation (
1.19
) when
\gamma>-1
. We also prove in this section that the solution decays exponentially for large values. Section
6
deals with the non-existence of self-similar solutions for equation (
1.19
) when
\gamma\leq-1
.

2
Asymptotic description of the long time behavior

2.1
The case
\gamma+2\lambda>1

In this section we describe the long time asymptotics of the solutions of
(
1.1
), (
1.2
) if
\gamma+2\lambda>1
using
formal asymptotic arguments.

As indicated in the Introduction, we expect
f\left(t,x\right)
to
converge to zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
for
x
of order one. Therefore, the
contribution due to the term
\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y
can be expected to be
negligible in this region since this term is quadratic in
f
and we can
expect the linear term
\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}yf\left(t,x\right)
to give a larger contribution. We will
check that these assumptions are self-consistent, in the sense that they will
predict an asymptotic behavior for
f
for which the assumptions made hold.

We examine the asymptotic behavior of the linear term
\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}yf\left(t,x\right)

when
x
is of order one.
Due to the effect of the coagulation, we expect the
distribution
f\left(t,y\right)
to be concentrated for long times in the
larger cluster sizes
y
as
t\rightarrow\infty.
Using the assumptions
(
1.14
) and (
1.15
) we obtain the following asymptotic
behavior of
K\left(x,y\right)
for
x\ll y

K\left(x,y\right)\simeq y^{\gamma+\lambda}x^{-\lambda}.

(2.1)

We then expect to have the following asymptotics as
t\rightarrow\infty,
due
to the concentration of
f
in the large cluster sizes

\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\sim x^{-\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\ \ \text{as\ \ }t\rightarrow\infty

for
x
of order one.
Then, considering the dominant terms in (
1.1
), (
1.2
) for
x
of order one, we obtain the following equation

\partial_{t}f\left(t,x\right)=-x^{-\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y+\eta\left(x\right).

(2.2)

As explained in the introduction, if
\gamma+2\lambda>1,

since steady
states describing the cluster concentrations with
x
of order one do not exist, we expect
to have
f\left(t,x\right)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty.
This
suggest that we should have
M_{\gamma+\lambda}=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty.
Suppose that
\partial_{t}M_{\gamma+\lambda}\ll M_{\gamma+\lambda}

as
t\rightarrow\infty
(something that would happen if
M_{\gamma+\lambda}

behaves like a power law, as we will see to be the case). Then (
2.2
)
implies the following asymptotic behavior for
f\left(t,x\right)

f\left(t,x\right)\sim\frac{x^{\lambda}\eta\left(x\right)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)dy}\ \ \text{as\ \ }t\rightarrow\infty

(2.3)

for
x
of order one. We notice that the higher order contributions to
(
2.3
) due to the contributions of the term
\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y
are
of order
\frac{1}{\left(M_{\gamma+\lambda}\right)^{2}}
or smaller.
Therefore, these contributions will be neglected in the following.

The equation (
2.3
) yields an approximate formula for the concentration of
clusters with
x
of order one. We now approximate the part of the coagulation
operator which is due to the aggregation of particles with size one with very
large particles. To this end we introduce a characteristic length
L=L\left(t\right)\gg 1
such that we can approximate
f\left(t,y\right)
by means
of (
2.3
) for
y\leq L.
We attempt to approximate the evolution of
the distribution
f\left(t,x\right)
for
x\gg L.

Then, the coagulation
operator in (
1.2
) can be approximated, using the symmetry
properties of the first term on the right of (
1.2
), as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y-\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{L}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y-\int_{0}^{L}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{L}^{x-L}K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y-\int_{L}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\right].

We can rewrite this formula as

\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)=\left[\int_{0}^{L}\left[K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)-K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)\right]f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\right]+\mathbb{K}\left[f\chi_{\left[L,\infty\right)}\right]\left(t,x\right),

(2.4)

where
\chi_{\left[L,\infty\right)}
denotes the characteristic function
of the interval
\left[L,\infty\right).
We now approximate
\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)
for large values of
x
and

t\rightarrow\infty
and more precisely for
x\gg L.
To this end we use
(
2.3
) and we assume also that
K
and
f
are sufficiently regular for
large values of
x.
Then, using the fact that
y\ll x
we obtain the
following approximation

K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)-K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)\simeq-y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)\right].

(2.5)

Moreover, (
2.1
) yields an approximation for
K\left(x,y\right)
if

x\ll y.
Exchanging the roles of
x
and
y
we obtain
K\left(x,y\right)\sim x^{\gamma+\lambda}y^{-\lambda}
as
\frac{x}{y}\rightarrow\infty
and
plugging this formula into (
2.5
) we obtain

K\left(x-y,y\right)f\left(t,x-y\right)-K\left(x,y\right)f\left(t,x\right)\simeq-y^{1-\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\right].

Using this approximation in (
2.4
) we then obtain

\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)=-\left[\int_{0}^{L}y^{1-\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\right]\right]+\mathbb{K}\left[f\chi_{\left[L,\infty\right)}\right]\left(t,x\right)

for
x\gg 1
and
t\rightarrow\infty.
We can now use (
2.3
) to derive a
formula for
\int_{0}^{L}y^{1-\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y.

We then obtain the approximation

\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)\simeq-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,x\right)\right]+\mathbb{K}\left[f\chi_{\left[L,\infty\right)}\right]\left(t,x\right).

(2.6)

Notice that we use that
\int_{0}^{L}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y
since by assumption
\eta\left(y\right)
decreases sufficiently fast for large values of
y.

We will denote as
f_{out}
the distribution of particles in the region where

x\gg 1.
More precisely we write
f_{out}=f\chi_{\left[L,\infty\right)}.

Combining (
1.1
), (
1.2
) with (
2.6
) we obtain
the following evolution equation for
f_{out}

\partial_{t}f_{out}\left(t,x\right)+\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,x\right)\right]=\mathbb{K}\left[f_{out}\right]\left(t,x\right).

(2.7)

Notice that we use the approximation
\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y
that might be expected because
f\left(t,y\right)\rightarrow 0
for
y\leq L
(cf. (
2.3
)).

In the rest of the paper we will study the properties of the self-similar
solutions associated to the equation (
2.7
). It is worth to remark that
the transport term on the left of (
2.7
) is the way in which the injection of
particles with size
x
of order one affects the outer distribution of
clusters
f_{out}
. Indeed, multiplying (
2.7
) by
x
and integrating we
obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}xf_{out}\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x\right)+\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,x\right)\right]\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\mathbb{K}\left[f_{out}\right]\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x.

(2.8)

The mass conservation property associated to the coagulation kernel yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}x\mathbb{K}\left[f_{out}\right]\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x=0.

On the other hand, integrating by parts in the second term on the left
of (
2.8
) we obtain
\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,x\right)\right]\textup{d}x=-\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{out}\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x.
Combining these results we
obtain

\partial_{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}xf_{out}\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\eta\left(x\right)\textup{d}x.

(2.9)

The identity (
2.9
) states that the total mass of the clusters in the
outer region is equal to the injection rate. This result, that holds for long
times, could be expected because in the regime described in this section, the
injected particles are transferred instantaneously to large cluster sizes. This is also consistent with
f(t,x)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
when
x\approx 1
.

2.2
The case
\gamma+2\lambda=1

In the case
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, the approximation of the concentrations of clusters
f(t,\cdot)
in
the region where
x
is of order one must be obtained in a different manner.
The
reason is that in this case we cannot expect the moment
M_{\gamma+\lambda}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}f(t,x)\textup{d}x

to converge to infinity as
t\rightarrow\infty.
Indeed, suppose that most
of the mass of the monomers is distributed in a characteristic length
L(t)

that increases as
t\rightarrow\infty.
Then, if we denote as
M_{0}
and

M_{1}
the moments of
f
of order zero and one, respectively, (i.e.
\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}xf\left(t,x\right)\textup{d}x
, respectively), we have that
M_{1}=M_{0}L(t)
and
M_{\gamma+\lambda}=M_{0}L(t)^{\gamma+\lambda}.

Assuming that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x\eta\left(x\right)\textup{d}x=1
and hence that
M_{1}\simeq t
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, we deduce that
M_{0}L(t)=t
.
The rescaling properties of
(
1.1
), (
1.2
) suggest that
\frac{M_{0}}{t}\simeq\left(M_{0}\right)^{2}L(t)^{\gamma}.
Therefore, plugging the
identity
M_{0}=\frac{t}{L(t)}
in this formula we obtain
L(t)=t^{\frac{2}{1-\gamma}}.
Hence

M_{\gamma+\lambda}=M_{0}L(t)^{\gamma+\lambda}=tL(t)^{\gamma+\lambda-1}=t^{1+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\left(\gamma+\lambda-1\right)}=1.

(2.10)

It then follows that the self-similar rescaling ansatz implies that

M_{\gamma+\lambda}
remains of order one for large times. A consequence of
this is that we cannot approximate (
1.1
), (
1.2
)
for clusters of order one by means of the equation (
2.2
). Instead of this
we will use a different approximation by splitting
f
in an outer part which
describes the cluster distribution for
x
of order
L
and an inner part
that describes the cluster distribution for
x
of order one. More precisely,
we write

f\left(t,x\right)=f_{inner}\left(t,x\right)+f_{outer}\left(t,x\right),

(2.11)

where
f_{out}=f\chi_{[L,\infty)}
, while
f_{inn}=f\chi_{(0,L]}
, for a constant
L>0.

Using (
1.1
), (
1.2
) we would then obtain the
following evolution equation for
f_{inner}

\partial_{t}f_{inner}\left(t,x\right)=\mathbb{K}\left[f_{inner}\right]\left(t,x\right)-f_{inner}\left(t,x\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}K\left(x,y\right)f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y+\eta\left(x\right),

(2.12)

where we have used the decomposition (
2.11
) in the lost term of the
coagulation operator
\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]
. We use also the fact that
in order to compute the gain term for
x
of order one we need to use only

f_{inner}.

By assumption, the main contribution of
f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)
is due
to clusters with size
L\gg 1.
On the other hand, for
x
of order one, we have the
approximation

K\left(x,y\right)f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)\simeq y^{\gamma+\lambda}x^{-\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)

and (
2.12
) becomes

\partial_{t}f_{inner}\left(t,x\right)=\mathbb{K}\left[f_{inner}\right]\left(t,x\right)-f_{inner}\left(t,x\right)x^{-\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y+\eta\left(x\right).

(2.13)

The scaling argument above, (
2.10
), suggests that
\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y
approaches to a positive constant as

t\rightarrow\infty
if
f_{outer}
behaves in a self-similar manner and

\gamma+2\lambda=1.
We will write

M_{\gamma+\lambda}=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y.

(2.14)

On the other hand, we will assume that the function
f_{inner}
, which is
described by means of (
2.13
), approaches to a stationary solution for large
times. We then obtain the following equation which would be expected to
describe the behavior of the solutions of (
2.13
) for long times

\mathbb{K}\left[f_{inner}\right]\left(x\right)-M_{\gamma+\lambda}f_{inner}\left(x\right)x^{-\lambda}+\eta\left(x\right)=0.

(2.15)

We can now derive an equation describing the
evolution of
f_{outer}.
To this end we argue as in the case of

\gamma+2\lambda>1
in order to approximate the effect in
f_{outer}
due to
the collisions of clusters with size
L
with clusters with size of order
one. Using approximations analogous to the ones used in the derivation of
(
2.7
) (cf. (
2.4
), (
2.5
)) we obtain the following approximation
for
\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)

\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)\simeq-\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1-\lambda}f_{inner}\left(t,y\right)\textup{d}y\right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,x\right)\right]\right]+\mathbb{K}\left[f_{outer}\right]\left(t,x\right)

(2.16)

for
x
of order
L.

On the other hand, multiplying (
2.15
) by
x
and integrating in
\left(0,\infty\right)
we obtain, since
f_{inner}
is zero for
x>L
, that

M_{\gamma+\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1-\lambda}f_{inner}\left(y\right)\textup{d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y.

Using this formula to eliminate
\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{1-\lambda}f_{inner}\left(y\right)\textup{d}y
in (
2.16
) we obtain

\mathbb{K}\left[f\right]\left(t,x\right)\simeq-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}y\eta\left(y\right)\textup{d}y}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}f_{outer}\left(t,x\right)\right]\right]+\mathbb{K}\left[f_{outer}\right]\left(t,x\right).

Therefore, we obtain that
f_{outer}
satisfies the equation (
2.7
).

The whole asymptotic behavior derived here relies on the
existence of solutions of the equation (
2.15
).
In this equation, the value
of
M_{\gamma+\lambda}
is chosen as one associated to a self-similar solution
of (
2.7
). The equation (
2.15
) can then be interpreted as a stationary
solution for a coagulation equation with source
\eta
and a removal term

-M_{\gamma+\lambda}f_{inner}\left(x\right)x^{-\lambda}.
The
results in
[
10
]
imply that no solutions of (
2.15
) exist if

M_{\gamma+\lambda}=0
and
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, but when
M_{\gamma+\lambda}>0
the existence/non-existence of stationary solutions for equation (
2.15
) is still an open problem.

3
Setting and main results

The main results of this paper concern the existence and the non-existence of solutions to the following equation

-\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Phi\left(\xi\right)-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\Phi_{\xi}\left(\xi\right)+\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi\left(\eta\right)\textup{d}\eta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi\left(\xi\right)\right)=\mathbb{K}\left[\Phi\right]\left(\xi\right),\ \ \xi>0.

(3.1)

We write now precisely what we mean by a solution of equation (
3.1
) and then we state the main theorems on the existence of a self-similar profile under certain assumptions on the parameters
\gamma
and
\lambda
(Theorem
5.1
), on its properties (Theorem
6.1
) and on the non-existence of the self-similar solutions under different assumptions on the parameters
\gamma
and
\lambda
(Theorem
3.5
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K
be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with homogeneity
\gamma<1
and with
\gamma+\lambda<1
and
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
. A self-similar profile of equation (
2.7
) with respect to the kernel
K
, is a measure
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
such that

0<\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty\text{ and }\int_{(0,1)}x^{1-\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty

(3.2)

and such that it satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\left[\frac{2}{1-\gamma}x-\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}z^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}z)}\right]\Phi(\textup{d}x)-\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(x)\Phi(\textup{d}x)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}K(x,y)\left[\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right]\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

for every test function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*}).

Remark 3.2
.

For every test function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
(hence such that
\varphi=0
near zero) all the integrals in equation (
3.3
) are finite. The integrals in the left-hand side of equation (
3.3
) are bounded due to the fact that
\varphi
is compactly supported and that
\Phi
is a Radon measure.
We analyse now the right hand side.
Since
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}K(x,y)\left|\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right|\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq 4c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{(0,y]}x^{-\lambda}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\left|\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right|\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{(0,y]}x^{-\lambda}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\left(\left|\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(y)\right|+|\varphi(x)|\right)\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq c||\varphi^{\prime}||_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{(0,1)}x^{1-\lambda}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle+c\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{[1,y]}x^{-\lambda}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\left|\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(y)\right|\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)+c\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}y)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{-\lambda}|\varphi(x)|\Phi(\textup{d}x).

Using (
3.2
), the fact that
-\lambda\leq\gamma+\lambda
, as well as the fact that
\varphi
is compactly supported the desired conclusion follows.

Theorem 3.3

(Existence of the self-similar profiles)
.

Let
K
be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that

-1<\gamma,\quad\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Then there exists a self-similar profile
\Phi
as in Definition
3.1
.
Moreover,
\Phi
is such that
\Phi((0,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})))=0
for

\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}):=\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}.

(3.4)

4
Main ideas of the proofs

In this section we explain the main ideas for the proofs of existence/non-existence of self-similar solutions.
Both in the case
\gamma+2\lambda>1
and
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, to prove that a self-similar solution exists, we find an invariant region for the evolution equation corresponding to (
3.1
), namely the following equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi(t,\xi)-\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Phi(t,\xi)-\frac{2\xi}{1-\gamma}\partial_{\xi}\Phi(t,\xi)+\frac{\partial_{\xi}\left(\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(t,\xi)\right)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(t,\textup{d}x)}=\mathbb{K}[\Phi](t,\xi).

(4.1)

By Tychonoff fixed point theorem the existence of an invariant region implies that there exists a solution of equation (
3.1
).

We prove that the set

P=\left\{H:\quad\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\infty}xH(x)\textup{d}x=1,\ \frac{1}{C_{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}H(x)\textup{d}x\leq C_{1},\\
&H((0,\rho(C_{1})))=0,\ \int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2-\gamma-\lambda}H(x)\textup{d}x\leq C_{2}\end{aligned}\right\}

is invariant when
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
and
\gamma>-1
for suitable constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
\rho(C_{1})
given by

\rho(C_{1}):=\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2C_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}.

(4.2)

To prove that the set
P
is invariant we proceed as follows.

1.

We prove that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}(x)\textup{d}x=1
implies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x=1
, for every
t>0
. This is done multiplying by
x
equation (
4.1
), integrating from zero to infinity and then studying the ODE for the zeroth moment obtained in this manner.

2.

As a second step we prove that there exists an upper bound for
M_{\gamma+\lambda}
.
To this end we multiply both sides of equation (
3.1
) by
x^{\gamma+\lambda}
and we integrate over
x
in
(0,\infty)
to obtain an ODE for the
\gamma+\lambda
moment.
Using Grönwall’s lemma, the fact that
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
and that
\gamma>-1
, the desired conclusion follows.

3.

As a third step we use the fact that
M_{\gamma+\lambda}\leq C_{1}
to prove that
\Phi(t,(0,\rho(C_{1}))=0.

Indeed, the evolution described by equation (
4.1
) is driven by two mechanisms: coagulation, which increases the average size of the particles in the system, and the growth term,

\frac{\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi,

which is positive for every
\xi<\rho(C_{1})
.
Hence, if we start from an initial data
\Phi_{0}
such that
\Phi_{0}((0,\rho(C_{1})))=0
, then we will have that
\Phi(t,(0,\rho(C_{1})))=0
, for every
t>0.

4.

Using the fact that
\gamma+\lambda<1
,
\gamma>-1
,
\Phi(t,(0,\rho(C_{1})))=0
, as well as the fact that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(t,\textup{d}x)\leq C_{1}
, we prove that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2-\gamma-\lambda}\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x\leq C_{2}
.

5.

Finally, from the upper bound for
M_{2-\gamma-\lambda}
, we derive a lower bound for
M_{\gamma+\lambda}
. Indeed, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

\displaystyle 1=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{2-\gamma-\lambda}\Phi(t,x)\textup{d}x.

(4.3)

Hence
\frac{1}{C_{2}}\leq M_{\gamma+\lambda}.

In order to prove non-existence we proceed by contradiction.
Due to the contribution of the coagulation operator in equation (
1.16
), we expect the zeroth moment of
f
to decay in time.
However, assuming the self-similar change of variable (
1.18
), we have that

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t,x)\textup{d}x=t^{-\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)\textup{d}x.

If
\gamma\leq-1
, then
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t,x)\textup{d}x
is constant or increasing in time and this gives a contradiction. Hence we cannot expect self-similar solutions to exist.
To make the argument rigorous we will have to prove that
0<\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty
.
When
\gamma+2\lambda>1
we do this by proving that for each self-similar profile there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
and that
\Phi
tends to zero sufficiently fast as
x\rightarrow\infty
.
Instead, when
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, the methods used in this paper do not allow to prove that
\Phi
is equal to zero near the origin, hence we use (

) to prove that

.

For

by the definition of

in (

), we have

is

and we can assume it has compact support as we are only interested in the region

. Thus, we have that the first derivative of

is bounded from above. Moreover, there exist constants
L_{1}(t),L_{2}(t)>0

L_{1}(t)|\xi-\tilde{\xi}|\leq|X(t,\xi)-X(t,\tilde{\xi})|\leq L_{2}(t)|\xi-\tilde{\xi}|
.

For
I_{2},
we use

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{1})M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}|\partial_{z}\varphi(t,z)|z^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{2}(t,\textup{d}z)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(C_{2})^{2}}\sup_{z\in[\rho(C_{1}),8R]}|\partial_{z}\varphi(t,z)|.

If we prove that there exists a constant
C>0,
which can depend on
\rho(C_{1}),R,
and the norms of
\Phi_{1},
and
\Phi_{2},
but does not vary depending on the choice of
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in[\rho(C_{1}),8R]}|\partial_{z}\varphi(t,z)|\leq C,

(5.29)

then there exists a constant
C>0,
which can depend on
\rho(C_{1}),

R
and the norms of
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,X(t,\xi))-\varphi(t,X(t,\tilde{\xi}))|

\displaystyle\leq C|X(t,\xi)-X(t,\tilde{\xi})|

\displaystyle+C\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(s,X(s,\xi))-\varphi(s,X(s,\tilde{\xi}))|.

(5.30)

Let us now prove (
5.29
). From equation (
5.28
) and the fact that
K_{R}
is in

with compact support, we obtain an upper bound for

.

We then use that there exist some constants

such that

) and obtain that

Thus, we can conclude that also

(Continuity of the semigroup in the weak topology)
.

Assume

), (

∗

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
. In order to prove continuity in the weak-
∗

\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}

then we have that for every

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(t,x)[{\Phi_{1}}(t,\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{2}}(t,\textup{d}x)]-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(0,x)[{\Phi_{\textup{in},1}}(\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{\textup{in},2}}(\textup{d}x)]

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\partial_{x}\varphi(s,x)x[{\Phi_{1}}(s,\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{2}}(s,\textup{d}x)]\textup{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}K_{R}(x,y)\chi_{\varphi}(s,x,y)[{\Phi_{1}}(s,\textup{d}y)+{\Phi_{2}}(s,\textup{d}y)][{\Phi_{1}}(s,\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{2}}(s,\textup{d}x)]\textup{d}s,

where

, for every

and we notice that

satisfies the following equation for every

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(t,x)\tilde{\Phi}(t,\textup{d}x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(0,x)[{\Phi_{\textup{in},1}}(\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{\textup{in},2}}(\textup{d}x)]

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\partial_{s}\varphi(s,x)\tilde{\Phi}(s,\textup{d}x)\textup{d}s-\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(s,x)\tilde{\Phi}(s,\textup{d}x)\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\partial_{x}\varphi(s,x)x\tilde{\Phi}(s,\textup{d}x)\textup{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}z^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{1}(s,\textup{d}z)}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\partial_{x}\varphi(s,x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\tilde{\Phi}(s,\textup{d}x)\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\mathbb{L}[\varphi](s,x)\tilde{\Phi}(s,\textup{d}x)\textup{d}s.

First we make the following notation:

\displaystyle C_{norm}:=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\ast}}[\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}](s,\textup{d}x)<\infty.

Notice that

can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle C_{norm}=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\ast}}[\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}](s,\textup{d}x)\leq C(R,\rho(C_{1}),t)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\ast}}[\Phi_{\textup{in},1}+\Phi_{\textup{in},2}](\textup{d}x).

Let now
\varphi
be the solution found in Lemma
5.9
with coagulation kernel
K_{R}
. By Proposition
5.11
, we know that there exists a constant
C(t,R,\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, that depends only on the norm of
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
, but is otherwise independent of the choice of
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
, such that
|\varphi(s,\xi)-\varphi(s,\tilde{\xi})|\leq C(t,R,\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})|\xi-\tilde{\xi}|,
for every

and

. We thus look at the set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\rho(C_{1}),R}:=

\displaystyle\{\chi\in\textup{C}([\rho(C_{1}),8R])||\chi(\xi)-\chi(\tilde{\xi})|\leq C(t,R,\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})|\xi-\tilde{\xi}|,

\displaystyle\textup{ for all }\xi,\tilde{\xi}\in[\rho(C_{1}),8R]\}\subset\textup{C}([\rho(C_{1}),8R]).

Then we obtain that:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(t,x)\tilde{\Phi}(t,\textup{d}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(0,x)[{\Phi_{\textup{in},1}}(\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{\textup{in},2}}(\textup{d}x)]=:T_{1}.

(5.32)

We can bound

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}|[\varphi(0,x)-\chi_{i}(x)][{\Phi_{\textup{in},1}}(\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{\textup{in},2}}(\textup{d}x)]|

\displaystyle+\max_{i=1}^{N}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\chi_{i}(x)[{\Phi_{\textup{in},1}}(\textup{d}x)-{\Phi_{\textup{in},2}}(\textup{d}x)]\big)

where in the last step we used (
5.31
).

5.3
Existence of the self-similar profile

To keep the notation lighter, in the previous sections we denoted with
\Phi
the solution of the truncated problem. In this section, since we pass to the limit as
R
tends to infinity, we add the label
R
to
\Phi
and we will denote with
\Phi
the self-similar profile.
For simplicity, we denote by
\rho:=\rho(C_{1}),
where
\rho(C_{1})
was defined in (
4.2
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

For every
R>1
, sufficiently large, there exist
T>0
and
\Phi_{R}\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{*}))
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(x)\dot{\Phi}_{R}(t,\textup{d}x)-\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(x)\Phi_{R}(t,\textup{d}x)+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)x\Phi_{R}(t,\textup{d}x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{R}(t))}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi^{\prime}(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(t,\textup{d}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}K_{R}(x,y)\left[\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right]\Phi_{R}(t,\textup{d}x)\Phi_{R}(t,\textup{d}y),

where
K_{R}
is the coagulation kernel defined in (
5.3
). Notice that the bounds in Proposition
5.7
are independent of
R>1
.

Thanks to Theorem
5.6
we know that a measure
\Phi_{R}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
satisfying equation (
5.16
) and satisfying the bounds
(
5.17
) exists.
We notice also that, since
\gamma+\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)=\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\leq\rho^{-\gamma-\lambda}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\leq\rho^{-\gamma-\lambda}C_{1}.

(5.33)

Hence, Banach-Alaoglu Theorem implies that there exists
\Phi
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{R}\rightharpoonup\Phi\text{ as }R\rightarrow\infty

(5.34)

in the weak-
∗
topology.

We now prove that the measure
\Phi
in (
5.34
) satisfies equation (
3.3
) by taking the limit as
R\rightarrow\infty
. Fix
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{\ast})
.
We start with passing to the limit in the coagulation term.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We first show that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\left(K(\xi,z)-K_{R}(\xi,z)\right)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\bigg|\leq\varepsilon,

(5.35)

for sufficiently large
R
. We then prove that

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]

(5.36)

converges to

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)\Phi(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]

(5.37)

as
R\rightarrow\infty.

For (
5.35
), we have that:

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\left(K(\xi,z)-K_{R}(\xi,z)\right)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[\rho,\frac{R}{4}]}\int_{[\rho,\frac{R}{4}]}\bigg|\left(K(\xi,z)-K_{R}(\xi,z)\right)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\bigg|

\displaystyle+2\int_{(\frac{R}{4},\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\bigg|\left(K(\xi,z)-K_{R}(\xi,z)\right)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\bigg|

\displaystyle=T_{1}+T_{2}.

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle T_{1}\leq e^{-R}3||\varphi||_{\infty}\rho^{-2},

where we used the definition of
K_{R}
in (
5.3
) and the fact that the total mass of the measures is equal to
1
.

For the region
(\frac{R}{4},\infty)\times[\rho,\infty)
, we use
K_{R}\leq K
to prove that

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 12||\varphi||_{\infty}\int_{(\frac{R}{4},\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(R^{-\gamma-2\lambda}M_{\gamma+\lambda}^{2}(\Phi_{R})+R^{\gamma+\lambda-1}\rho^{-\gamma-2\lambda}M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{R})\right),

which gives a small contribution due to the uniform estimates for
\Phi_{R}
if we make
R
sufficiently large.

We now analyse (
5.36
). We consider a continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
g(x)=1
, when
x\leq 1
, and
g(x)=0
, when
x\geq 2
.
We define the function
p_{M}
as

\displaystyle p_{M}(x,y)=g\left(\frac{x}{M}\right)g\left(\frac{y}{M}\right),

(5.38)

where
M
is a positive constant.
By the construction of
p_{M}
and given the fact that
\Phi_{R}
is supported in the region
[\rho,\infty)
, for every

, we have that, given any function
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
,

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)p_{M}(\xi,z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]=

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,2M]}\int_{[\rho,2M]}K(\xi,z)p_{M}(\xi,z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)].

Therefore, since
\Phi_{R}\rightharpoonup\Phi
in the weak-
∗
topology, we can conclude that
\Phi_{R}\Phi_{R}\rightharpoonup\Phi\Phi
in the weak-
∗
topology as

if we work on the set

and we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)p_{M}(\xi,z)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)p_{M}(\xi,z)\Phi(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi(\textup{d}z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]

as
R
tends to infinity.

To conclude that, for every test function
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*}),
we have that (
5.36
) converges to (
5.37
) as
R\rightarrow\infty
, we have to prove that the reminder terms, namely

\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[M,\infty)}K(\xi,z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z),

(5.39)

\int_{[M,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z),

(5.40)

\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[M,\infty)}K(\xi,z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\Phi(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi(\textup{d}z),

(5.41)

\int_{[M,\infty)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}K(\xi,z)[\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)]\Phi(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi(\textup{d}z),

(5.42)

tend to zero as
M\rightarrow\infty
.

Let us look at the term (
5.39

\{\xi\geq M\}
, hence
\xi^{\gamma+\lambda-1}\leq M^{\gamma+\lambda-1}
as
\gamma+\lambda<1
. Therefore, for every
\varphi\in\textup{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[M,\infty)}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}z^{-\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)|\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)|\lesssim M^{\gamma+\lambda-1}\rho^{-\gamma-2\lambda}M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{R}),

where we remind that

is bounded uniformly in

and that the mass of
\Phi_{R}
is equal to one.
Similarly, the fact that
\xi\geq M
implies that
\xi^{-\gamma-2\lambda}\leq M^{-\gamma-2\lambda}
since
\gamma+2\lambda>0
.
We then obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\int_{[M,\infty)}z^{\gamma+\lambda}\xi^{-\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}\xi)\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}z)|\varphi(z+\xi)-\varphi(z)-\varphi(\xi)|\leq CM^{-\gamma-2\lambda}M_{\gamma+\lambda}^{2}(\Phi_{R}).

From these two inequalities and the fact that

) tends to zero as

By a symmetric argument we prove that the term (
5.40
) tends to zero as
M\rightarrow\infty
.
The fact that the two terms (
5.41
) and (
5.42
) tend to zero as

follows similarly by the fact that the

The linear terms for the self-similar profiles of the truncated problem will converge as

to the desired terms thanks to (

).

To conclude, we need to prove the convergence of the remainder term. We thus have to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)

goes to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)

as

. We have that

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi_{R})}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi)}\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)}\bigg|\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle+\bigg|\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)}-\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)}\bigg|\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|.

We can analyse the two terms separately. For the first term, we know by (

) that the

moment is uniformly bounded from below and thus

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)}\bigg|\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq M_{2-\gamma-\lambda}(\Phi_{R})\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

and then we use that
\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|\rightarrow 0
by (
5.34
).

For the second term, we split the integral into a region with compact support and the remainder regions. More precisely,

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)}{\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)}\bigg|\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)-\int_{[\rho,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\bigg|M_{2-\gamma-\lambda}(\Phi_{R})M_{2-\gamma-\lambda}(\Phi)M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\Phi)

\displaystyle\lesssim C\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}g\left(\frac{x}{M}\right)\left[x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)-x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\right]\bigg|+2C\bigg|\int_{[M,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\bigg|

\displaystyle+2C\bigg|\int_{[M,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\bigg|.

We have that
\bigg|\int_{[\rho,\infty)}g(\frac{x}{M})[x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)-x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)]\bigg|\rightarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow\infty
by (
5.34
). For the regions close to infinity, we use

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{[M,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi_{R}(\textup{d}x)\bigg|\leq M^{\gamma+\lambda-1},

as the total mass is uniformly bounded. Since
\gamma+\lambda<1,
the remaining term goes to zero as
M\rightarrow\infty
.

As a last step we prove that, if
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
for some
\delta>0
, then
\Phi((0,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})))=0
for
\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
given by (
3.4
).
Since the proof is standard we only sketch it here.
Consider
x\in[\frac{\delta}{2},\frac{1}{2}\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}))
, then, since
\Phi
satisfies (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle c\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}x\right)\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|1+\gamma|}{1-\gamma}\int_{\delta}^{x}\Phi(\eta)\textup{d}\eta+\int_{\delta}^{x}\int_{\delta}^{y-\delta}K(y-\eta,\eta)\Phi(y-\eta)\Phi(\eta)\textup{d}\eta\textup{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\int_{\delta}^{x}\Phi(\eta)\textup{d}\eta,

for some positive constants
\tilde{c},c>0
.
Using Grönwall’s lemma, this implies that
\Phi(x)\leq e^{\frac{\tilde{c}}{c}x}\Phi(\frac{\delta}{2})=0
. Hence
\Phi([\delta,\frac{1}{2}\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})))=0
.
Iterating this argument, we deduce that
\Phi((0,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})))=0
.
The argument can be made rigorous by working with the weak formulation of equation (
3.1
).
∎

5.4
Properties of the constructed self-similar profile

In this section we aim at proving Theorem

, satisfies the properties stated in Theorem
3.3
.
In other words we have to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.13
.

Assume
K
to be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that

-1<\gamma,\quad\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Let
\Phi

.

\phi

To prove this theorem we start by proving that every self-similar profile

3.1
and such that

, for a positive

Proposition 5.14

(Regularity)
.

Assume
K
to be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
be a self-similar profile as in Definition
3.1
.
Assume additionally that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
for
\delta>0
.
Then
\Phi
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
Its density
\phi\in L^{1}([\delta,\infty))
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}x\phi(x)dx-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}z^{2}\phi(z)+\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\phi)}z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\phi(z)-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\phi)}\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x

(5.43)

\displaystyle=-J_{\phi}(z),\quad a.e.\ z>\delta

where

J_{\phi}(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z-x}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x.

(5.44)

Proof.

We just sketch the proof as similar arguments have been repeatedly used in the analysis of coagulation equations, see for instance the proof of Lemma 4.9 in
[
9
]
. An analogous proof will be presented in the proof of Proposition
6.2
.
Let
\Phi
be a solution of (
3.1
), then

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}\left[\left(\frac{\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\right)\Phi(\xi)\right]=\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\Phi(\xi)+\mathbb{K}[\Phi](\xi).

Using the fact that

\mathbb{K}[\Phi](\xi)\leq\int_{\delta}^{\xi-\delta}K(\xi-\eta,\eta)\Phi(\eta)\Phi(\xi-\eta)\textup{d}\eta,\quad\xi>\delta

and that
\int_{\delta}^{\xi-\delta}K(\xi-\eta,\eta)\Phi(\eta)\Phi(\xi-\eta)\textup{d}\eta
is a bounded measure in every compact set, we deduce that the measure

\left(\frac{\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\right)\Phi(\xi)

is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the set
(\delta,\infty)
if
\delta>\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
, while it
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure in the set
(\delta,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}))\cup(\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}),\infty)
if
\delta\leq\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
. In the latter case, we therefore have that
\Phi(\textup{d}x)=\phi(x)\textup{d}x+c_{0}\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}(x)

with density
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
and with
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
To exclude that the self-similar solution
\Phi
has a Dirac in
\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
,
we notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\xi-\delta}K(\xi-\eta,\eta)c_{0}\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}(\eta)c_{0}\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}(\xi-\eta)\textup{d}\eta

\displaystyle=c_{0}^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\xi-\delta}K(\xi-\eta,\eta)\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}(\eta)\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}(\xi-\eta)\textup{d}\eta

is non-zero only if
\xi=2\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
.
Hence the coagulation operator applied to measure
c_{0}\delta_{\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})}
produces a Dirac in
2\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})
, contradicting the fact that
\Phi_{|(\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}),\infty)}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. As a consequence, we deduce that
c_{0}=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.15

(Moment bounds)
.

Assume
K
to be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that

\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{*})
be a self-similar profile as in Definition
3.1
and assume additionally that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
, where
\delta>0
.
Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\mu}\Phi(\textup{d}x)=\int_{[\delta,\infty)}x^{\mu}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty,\quad\forall\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

For
\mu\leq\gamma+\lambda
the statement follows by the fact that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
and that by definition of self-similar profile we know that

\int_{[\delta,\infty)}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty.

We want to prove the statement for
\mu>\gamma+\lambda.

As a first step, we prove that there exists a
\overline{\delta}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda+\overline{\delta}}\phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty,

(5.45)

where
\phi
is the density of
\Phi
.
As a second step we will prove that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda+n\overline{\delta}}\phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty

(5.46)

for
n\geq 1
implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}x^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}}\phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty.

(5.47)

The desired conclusion then follows by induction.

Step 1

Consider an integrable function
\varphi
and integrate both sides of equation (
5.43
) against the function
\psi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\varphi(y)\textup{d}y
to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(z)z\phi(z)\textup{d}z-\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(z)\psi^{\prime}(z)\left(\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}z^{2}\right)\textup{d}z-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(z)z^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(z)\textup{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\left(\psi(x+y)-\psi(x)\right)\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x.

(5.48)

We remark that the above formulation requires that
\psi
has to decay fast enough so that all the integrals in (
5.4
) are finite.

Select
\overline{\delta}>0
such that

\max\{0,1-2(\gamma+\lambda)\}<\overline{\delta}<1-\gamma-\lambda
and consider
\psi_{R}(x)=x^{\gamma+\lambda+\overline{\delta}-1}
if
x\leq R
while
\psi_{R}(x)=R^{\overline{\delta}}x^{\gamma+\lambda-1}
if
x>R
.
Using (
5.4

\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1
and that
\psi_{R}(x+y)-\psi_{R}(x)\leq 0
we deduce that

\displaystyle 0<\frac{\gamma+2\lambda-1+2\overline{\delta}}{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{R}z^{\gamma+\lambda+\overline{\delta}}\phi(z)\textup{d}z\leq\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{2(\gamma+\lambda)+\overline{\delta}-1}\phi(z)\textup{d}z<\infty,

where the moment
M_{2(\gamma+\lambda)+\overline{\delta}-1}
is bounded because, due to the choice of
\overline{\delta}
, we have that
2(\gamma+\lambda)+\overline{\delta}-1<\gamma+\lambda
.
Passing to the limit as
R
tends to infinity we deduce that (
5.45
) holds.

Step 2

We assume that inequality (
5.46
) holds and we want to prove (
5.47
).
Taking
\psi_{R}(x)=x^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}
when
x\leq R
and
\psi_{R}(x)=R^{\overline{\delta}}x^{\gamma+\lambda+n\overline{\delta}-1}
when
x>R
in (
5.4
) we deduce that

\displaystyle 0<\frac{\gamma+2\lambda-1+2\overline{\delta}(n+1)}{1-\gamma}\int_{0}^{R}z^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}}\phi(z)\textup{d}z\leq\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{2(\gamma+\lambda)+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}\phi(z)\textup{d}z

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\left[\psi_{R}(x+y)-\psi_{R}(x)\right]\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(z)\psi_{R}^{\prime}(z)z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\phi(z)\textup{d}z.

Thanks to the choice of
\overline{\delta}
, we have that
2(\gamma+\lambda)+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1\leq\gamma+\lambda+n\overline{\delta}

and the desired conclusion follows for
n
such that
\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1<1
by the fact that in that case
\psi_{R}
is decreasing and hence the coagulation term is negative as well as the term
\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(z)\psi_{R}^{\prime}(z)z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\phi(z)
.
We examine now the case in which
\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1\geq 1
.
In this case we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\left[\psi_{R}(x+y)-\psi_{R}(x)\right]\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\left[(x+y)^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}-x^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}\right]\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\{(x,y)\in[\delta,\infty)^{2}:x\leq y\}}x^{1-\lambda}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\left[(x+y)^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}-x^{\gamma+\lambda+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}\right]\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\delta}^{\infty}x^{1-\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x\int_{\delta}^{\infty}y^{2(\gamma+\lambda)+(n+1)\overline{\delta}-1}\phi(y)\textup{d}y<\infty.

Since we also have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(z)\psi_{R}^{\prime}(z)z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\phi(z)\textup{d}z\leq c(n)\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(z)z^{2(\gamma+\lambda)+(n+1)\delta-1}\phi(z)\textup{d}z<\infty

the desired conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 5.16

(Exponential bound)
.

Assume
K
to be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that

\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be as in Proposition
5.14
and let
\phi
be its density.
Then there exist two positive constants
L
and
M
such that

\int_{M}^{\infty}\phi(z)e^{Lz}\textup{d}z<\infty.

(5.49)

Proof.

Adapting the approach used in
[
11
]
we define the function
\Psi_{a}
as

\Psi_{a}(L):=\int_{M}^{\infty}\frac{e^{L\min\{x,a\}}}{\min\{x,a\}}x^{2}\phi(x)\textup{d}x

where
M>\delta
.
Hence, by its definition

\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)=\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{L\min\{x,a\}}x^{2}\phi(x)\textup{d}x.

We consider a function
\psi
in (
5.4
) with

\psi^{\prime}(x):=e^{L\min\{x,a\}}

to deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{\gamma+\lambda-1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{L\min\{x,a\}}x^{2}\phi(x)\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{\delta}^{M}e^{L\min\{x,a\}}x^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\phi(x)\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}xK(x,y)\int_{y}^{x+y}e^{L\min\{w,a\}}\textup{d}w\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}x\textup{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta}^{\overline{z}}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}x^{\gamma+\lambda}-x\right)\psi(x)\phi(x)\textup{d}x,

for
\overline{z}:=\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}
, where we are assuming without loss of generality that
\overline{z}>\delta
and since for every
z\geq\overline{z}
we have

z-\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}>0.

(5.50)

Thus, there exists a

such that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)\leq\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)\frac{M^{\gamma+\lambda-1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}+c+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}xK(x,y)\int_{y}^{x+y}e^{L\min\{w,a\}}\textup{d}w\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}x\textup{d}y.

As in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
we can deduce, using Jensen’s inequality together with the fact that
\gamma+\lambda<1
and that
-\lambda<1
, that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}xK(x,y)\int_{y}^{x+y}e^{L\min\{w,a\}}\textup{d}w\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}x\textup{d}y\leq\Psi_{a}(L)^{1-\gamma-\lambda}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)^{\gamma+\lambda}.

This, together with the fact that we can take
M
arbitrary large, implies that

\displaystyle c(\gamma,\lambda)\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)\leq\Psi_{a}(L)^{1-\gamma-\lambda}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)^{\gamma+\lambda}+c,

(5.51)

for a positive constant
c
and for
c(\gamma,\lambda)=\frac{2}{1-\gamma}-\frac{M^{\gamma+\lambda-1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}>0.

By the definition of
\Psi_{a}
we have that

\Psi_{a}(0)\leq\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\left(1+\frac{c_{1}}{a}\right)\rightarrow\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\text{ as }a\rightarrow\infty.

If we prove that
\limsup_{a\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{a}(L)<\infty
for some
L
, then we can conclude.
Indeed we would have

M\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{Lx}\phi(x)\textup{d}x\leq\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{Lx}x\phi(x)\textup{d}x<\infty.

Let us prove that
\limsup_{a\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{a}(L)<\infty
for some
L
.
First of all notice that this is true if
\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)\leq 1
as in this case
\Psi_{a}(L)\leq L+\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{a}(0)
and the desired conclusion follows.
If instead
\Psi_{a}^{\prime}>1
, then

c(\gamma,\lambda)\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)\leq\Psi_{a}(L)^{1-\gamma-\lambda}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)^{\gamma+\lambda}+c\leq\alpha\Psi_{a}(L)^{1-\gamma-\lambda}\Psi_{a}^{\prime}(L)+c.

Without loss of generality we can assume
\alpha>0
such that
\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{a}(0)\neq\alpha
. This implies that a solution of the ODE

\Psi_{a}^{\prime}\left(1-\alpha\Psi_{a}^{1-\gamma-\lambda}\right)=c

exists for small intervals around
0
and is such that
\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{a}(L)<\infty
for
L
in that interval.
∎

Proposition 5.17

(Exponential decay)
.

Assume
K
to be a homogeneous symmetric coagulation kernel, of homogeneity
\gamma
, satisfying
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), with
\gamma,\lambda
such that (
1.7
) holds and such that
\gamma+2\lambda\geq 1.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be as in Proposition
5.14
and let
\phi
be its density.
Then there exists a positive constant
\tilde{M}
such that

\limsup_{z\rightarrow\infty}\phi(z)e^{\tilde{M}z}<\infty.

(5.52)

Proof.

From equation (
5.43
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{1-\gamma}z^{2}-\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\phi)}\right)\phi(z)\leq\int_{0}^{z}x\phi(x)\textup{d}x-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}(\phi)}\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x+J_{\phi}(z).

We now show that
J_{\phi}
decays exponentially and that the term

\int_{0}^{z}x\phi(x)\textup{d}x-\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x

decays exponentially too.

First of all, we prove that there exists a constant
M_{1}>0
such that
J_{\phi}(z)\leq e^{-M_{1}z}
for large
z
.
To this end we notice that the bound (
5.49
) implies that

\displaystyle J_{\phi}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z-x}^{\infty}e^{-L(x+y)}e^{L(x+y)}xK(x,y)\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq e^{-Lz}\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z-x}^{\infty}e^{L(x+y)}xK(x,y)\phi(x)\phi(y)\textup{d}y\textup{d}x\leq e^{-M_{1}z}.

On the other side, for large values of
z
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}x\phi(x)\textup{d}x-\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\left(\int_{0}^{z}x\phi(x)\textup{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y-\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y-\int_{0}^{z}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(x)\textup{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\int_{z}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y\leq C(\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda}))e^{-M_{2}z},

where
M_{2}>0
.

We deduce that for large values of
z

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2z^{2}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}\right)\phi(z)\leq c\max\{e^{-zM_{1}},e^{-zM_{2}}\}.

The conclusion follows since, for every
z>\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2z^{2}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(y)\textup{d}y}>0.

∎

6
Non-existence results and properties of the self-similar profiles

To study the non-existence of the self-similar solutions we proceed by contradiction and start by assuming that a self-similar solution exists.
To find a contradiction we need to analyse the properties of each self-similar profile.
In the case
\gamma+2\lambda>1
the fundamental properties that we prove to be true are that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
for some positive
\delta>0
and that
\Phi
decays sufficiently fast for large sizes.
When
\gamma+2\lambda=1
, it is possible to prove that
\Phi
decays fast for large sizes, but we have not been able to prove that
\Phi((0,\delta))=0
for some
\delta>0
.
This is the reason why we require the additional condition (
3.5
) in Theorem
3.5
.

6.1
Properties of the self-similar profiles

In this section we study the properties of each self-similar solution that do not rely on the existence of the self-similar solution and hence do not rely on the assumption
\gamma>-1
.

Theorem 6.1

(Properties of the self-similar profiles)

\gamma
, satisfying
(

), (

), with

) holds.

\gamma+2\lambda>1
,

\Phi
as in Definition

\Phi((0,\rho(M_{\gamma+\lambda})))=0

Additionally,
\Phi
is such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}e^{Lx}\Phi(\textup{d}x)<\infty

for

\limsup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}}\phi(x)e^{\overline{L}x}\leq c

Proposition 6.2

Proof.

We start explaining the proof in a heuristic manner without entering in the technical details that will be explained later.
Equation (
1.19
) can be rewritten in the following flux form

\partial_{x}\left(J_{\Phi}(x)+\left(\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2x^{2}}{1-\gamma}\right)\Phi(x)\right)=\left(\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-x\right)\Phi(x),

(6.3)

where

is given by (

).
For

smaller than
\overline{\xi}

we deduce that
L\geq 0.

We make a scaling argument to identify the value of

To this end we notice that the units of the flux are given by
[J_{\Phi}(x)]=[\Phi]^{2}[x]^{3+\gamma}
, where from now on we indicate with
[\cdot]

[\Phi]=\left[x\right]^{-\frac{(3+\gamma)}{2}}.

We deduce that

[x^{\gamma+\lambda+1}\Phi]=[x^{\gamma+\lambda+1}][\Phi]=[x^{\frac{\gamma+2\lambda-1}{2}}].

Since

this implies that as

, hence

J_{\Phi}(x)\sim L
as
x\rightarrow 0
. Finally, we prove that

, in agreement with the statement proven in
[
10
]
that, when

, there are no solutions to the constant flux equation.
Integrating (
6.3
), we deduce that

A detailed analysis of this ODE for

implies that

\left(0,\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\lambda}}\right)

We now explain the proof in detail.
Testing (

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\left(\xi-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\right)\varphi(\xi)\Phi(\textup{d}\xi)=\int_{(0,\infty)}\varphi^{\prime}(\xi)W[\Phi](\textup{d}\xi),

(6.5)

Step 1: Regularity of

and an integral representation formula

First of all we prove that, for every
\overline{\delta}>0

\phi

We now prove that

(0,\overline{\xi}]

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)\leq 0
for
\xi\in\left(\frac{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}{2},\infty\right)
.
Substituting
\varphi_{n}
in (
6.4
) we deduce that for every

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\xi\varphi_{n}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi-\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(\xi)\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)W_{\phi}(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

Notice that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi\varphi_{n}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\rightarrow\int_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}\xi\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\quad\text{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty

and that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\varphi_{n}(\xi)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\rightarrow\int_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\quad\text{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Additionally, since

is in

and satisfies (
6.4
) we deduce that
W_{\phi}\in W^{1}_{loc}(0,\overline{\xi})
and hence is continuous in
(0,\overline{\xi})
. Hence we have that

\int_{(0,\infty)}\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)W_{\phi}(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\rightarrow W_{\phi}(\xi_{1})-W_{\phi}(\xi_{2})\quad\text{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

As a consequence, we deduce that

\displaystyle W_{\phi}(\xi_{2})-W_{\phi}(\xi_{1})=\int_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

(6.6)

Since if
\xi\leq\overline{\xi}
, then

\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\geq 0
,
we deduce that there exists a
\overline{\xi}
such that
W_{\phi}
is increasing in
(0,\overline{\xi}]
.
Hence, since
W_{\phi}
is bounded, we have that the following limit exists

0\leq L:=\lim_{\xi\rightarrow 0^{+}}W_{\phi}(\xi)<\infty.

Due to (
3.2
) we can take the limit as
\xi_{1}\rightarrow 0
in (
6.6
) and deduce that

\displaystyle W_{\phi}(\xi_{2})-L=\int_{(0,\xi_{2}]}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

(6.7)

Step 2: we prove that
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}J_{\phi}(\varepsilon x)\varphi(x)\textup{d}x\rightarrow L\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(x)\textup{d}x

Since
W_{\phi}
is bounded in
(0,\overline{\xi}]
and
\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\xi\geq 0
for every

is also bounded in
(0,\overline{\xi}]
.
Since

[2/3z,z]\times[2/3z,z]\subset\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}:0<x\leq z,z-x\leq y<\infty\right\}=:\Omega_{z}

(6.8)

we deduce that for every

\frac{1}{z}\int_{2z/3}^{z}\phi(x)\textup{d}x\leq\frac{C}{z^{\frac{\gamma+3}{2}}}\quad z\in(0,\overline{\xi}].

(6.9)

Equation (

) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi\left(x\right)J_{\phi}(\varepsilon x)\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)J_{\phi}(x)\textup{d}x=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\left[\frac{x^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2}{1-\gamma}x^{2}\right]\phi(x)\textup{d}x

(6.10)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\int_{0}^{x}\left[\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-z\right]\phi(z)\textup{d}z\textup{d}x+L\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(x)\textup{d}x

where

for every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}J_{\phi}(\xi)\varphi\left(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}\right)\textup{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\varphi(\xi)J_{\phi}(\varepsilon\xi)\textup{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}xK(x,y)\left(\psi\left(\frac{x+y}{\varepsilon}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

and therefore

\Phi

\displaystyle W_{\phi}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\quad\text{ for }z\in(0,\overline{\xi}).

Or, equivalently, since

\displaystyle J_{\phi}(z)+\left(\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2z^{2}}{1-\gamma}\right)\phi(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi\quad\text{ for }a.e.z\in(0,\overline{\xi}).

We now rewrite (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z-x}^{z}xK(x,y)\Phi(\textup{d}y)\Phi(\textup{d}x)+\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\Phi(\textup{d}y)\Phi(\textup{d}x)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2z^{2}}{1-\gamma}\right)\phi(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

If

is different to zero near the origin, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\int_{z}^{\infty}xK(x,y)\Phi(\textup{d}x)\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\int_{0}^{z}x^{1-\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}x)\int_{z}^{\infty}y^{\gamma+\lambda}\Phi(\textup{d}y)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=J_{\phi}(z)+\left(\frac{z^{\gamma+\lambda+1}}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}-\frac{2z^{2}}{1-\gamma}\right)\phi(z)-\int_{0}^{z}\left(\frac{1}{M_{\gamma+\lambda}}\xi^{\gamma+\lambda}-\xi\right)\phi(\xi)\textup{d}\xi

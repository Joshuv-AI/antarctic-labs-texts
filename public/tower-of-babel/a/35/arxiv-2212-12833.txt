On the empty balls of a critical or subcritical branching random walk
Jie Xiong, Shuxiong Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:
Let
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a critical or subcritical
d
-dimensional branching random walk started from a Poisson random measure whose intensity measure is the Lebesugue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denote by
R_{n}:=\sup\{u>0:Z_{n}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<u\})=0\}
the radius of the largest empty ball centered at the origin of
Z_{n}
. In this work, we prove that after suitable renormalization,
R_{n}
converges in law to some non-degenerate distribution as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, our work shows that the renormalization scales depend on the offspring law and the dimension of the branching random walk, which completes the results of
[
12
]
for the critical binary branching Wiener process.

Key words and phrases
: empty ball; dimension; branching random walk; Super-Brownian motion

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020): 60J68; 60F05; 60G57

1
Introduction and Main results

1.1
Introduction

In this work, we consider a branching random walk (BRW) model
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
started from the Poisson random measure with Lebesgue intensity. This model is a measure valued process, which is governed by a probability distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
on natural numbers (called the offspring distribution) and a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vector
X
(called the step size or displacement). Let us define it in the following way.

At time
0
, there exist infinite many particles distributed according to the Poisson random measure, i.e. for any Borel measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}(A)=k)=\frac{|A|^{k}}{k!}e^{-|A|},~k\geq 0,

where
|\cdot|
stands for the Lebesgue measure, and by convention
0=|A|^{k}e^{-|A|}
if
|A|=\infty
. Then, these particles die and produce offsprings independently according to the offspring distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
in a instant. Afterwards, the offspring particles move independently according to the law of
X
in unit time. This forms a random measure at time
1
, denoted by
Z_{1}
. Write
u\in Z_{i}
if
u
is a particle at time
i
in the branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Denote by
S_{u}
the position of particle
u
. Let
N_{v},~v\in Z_{n},~n\geq 0
be independent random variables with common distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
X^{v}_{i},1\leq i\leq N_{v},~v\in Z_{n},~n\geq 0
be independent copies of
X
. In words,
N_{v}
stands for the children number of
v
and
X^{v}_{i}
stands for the displacement of the
i
-th child of
v
. Hence,

Z_{1}=\sum_{u\in Z_{0}}\sum^{N_{u}}_{i=1}\delta_{S_{u}+X^{u}_{i}}.

Generally, the measure valued process
Z_{n}
,
n\geq 2
is defined by the following iteration

Z_{n}:=\sum_{x\in Z_{n-1}}\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x},

where

\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x}:=\sum^{N_{x}}_{i=1}\delta_{S_{x}+X^{x}_{i}},~x\in Z_{n-1}

(conditioned on
Z_{n-1}
) are independent.

Let
m:=\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}
be the mean of the offspring law. We call
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

a supercritical (critical, subcritical) branching random walk if
m>1
(
=1
,
<1
). In the remainder of
this paper, we always consider the critical and subcritical case (i.e.
m\leq 1
). In order to avoid trivialities, we always assume that

p_{0}<1,~p_{1}<1.

Denote by
|Z_{n}|:=Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})
the total population at generation
n
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
be the probability measure under which a measure valued process (say, branching random walk or super-Brownian motion; see Section
3
below) starts from the measure
\mu
. We write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
if
\mu
is the Poisson random measure with Lebesgue intensity.

Since
|Z_{0}|=\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for any integer
k,n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(|Z_{n}|>0)\geq\mathbb{P}_{k\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)=1-(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|=0))^{k},

where the right hand side converges to
1
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, under
\mathbb{P}
,
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
survives almost surely. So, the following notation
R_{n}
is well-defined. Let
B(u):=\{u:|x|<u\}
be the
d-
dimensional open ball with radius
u
. Denote by

R_{n}:=\sup\{u>0:Z_{n}(B(u))=0\}

the radius of the largest empty ball centered at the origin of
Z_{n}
, where we take
0=\sup\emptyset
by convention. Or, in other words,
R_{n}
is the shortest distance of the particles at time
n
from the origin.

In this paper, we aim at finding suitable renormalization scales
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
such that for any
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{a_{n}}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{d}(r)},

where
F_{d}(r)\in(0,\infty)
.

The research on this question was first conducted by
[
12
]
for the binary branching Wiener process (i.e.
p_{0}=p_{1}=1/2
and
X
is a standard normal random vector). He proved that if
d=1
, then
R_{n}/n
converges in law as
n\to\infty
. For
d=2
and
d\geq 3
, he gave two
conjectures; see Remark
1.1
and Remark
1.3
below. Later,
[
5
]
partially confirmed Révész’s conjecture for
d\geq 3
; also see
[
15
]
for this question about the critical super-Brownian motion model.

Our method to the proof is different from Révész. We first use the Laplace functional formula of the Poisson random measure to show that

\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq a_{n}r)=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(a_{n}r))>0)dx\right\}.

Then, we prove the theorems by analyzing lower bounds and upper bounds of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(a_{n}r))>0)dx
. In case
d=1
, for the lower bound, we force every branching random walk started from
B(nr)
to have one particle locate in
B(nr)
at time
n
. For the upper bound, the key is to use the deviation probabilities of a random walk to argue that branching random walks emanating out from
B(n(r+\delta))
can hardly reach
B(nr)
at time
n
, where
\delta>0
is a small constant. In case
d=2
, using the fact that the scaling limits of the branching random walk is a super-Brownian motion and results of empty balls for the super-Brownian motion (see
[
15
]
), we obtain the desired lower bound. The upper bound is obtained in virtue of the extreme value theory for branching random walks (see
[
7
]
) and the Poisson
cluster representation of super-Brownian motions. In case
d\geq 3
, we use Paly-Zygmund and Markov inequalities to prove the lower bound and upper bound, respectively. We also consider the subcritical case. Unlike the critical case,
R_{n}
increases exponentially in the subcritical case.

1.2
Main results

Write
\sigma^{2}:=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k^{2}p_{k}-\left[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}kp_{k}\right]^{2}
. In the following of this paper, to simplify our statement, we always assume that the step size
X
is a mean zero random vector and each component of
X
are identically independent distributed. The first theorem consider the
1
-dimensional critical branching random walk.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
m=1
,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
and
d=1
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>2
. Then for
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{n}\geq r\right)=e^{-\frac{4r}{\sigma^{2}}}.

The next theorem considers the case of
d=2
. Denote by
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
a critical
d
-dimensional super-Brownian motion with branching mechanism
\psi(u)=\sigma^{2}u^{2}
(for its serious definition see Section
3
below). Define
B(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x|<r\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
m=1
,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
and
d=2
. Suppose
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{4}]<\infty
and the correlation coefficient matrix of
X
is the identity matrix. Then there exists a function
C(\cdot)>0
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}C(\varepsilon)=\infty
, and for any
r,\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{2\pi(1+\delta)r^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-C(\delta)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)\leq\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx\right\}.

Remark 1.1
.

From
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx}{2\pi r^{2}/\sigma^{2}}=1.

In
[
12
, Conjecture 1]
, Révész conjectured that for a two-dimensional critical binary branching Wiener process (thus
\sigma^{2}=1
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{2}(r)},

where
F_{2}(r)\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{F_{2}(r)}{2\pi r^{2}}=1.

(1.2)

Unfortunately, we can not prove
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)
exists. But if proved, (
1.2
) is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
. Thus, in a sense, our lower bound and upper bound are relatively sharp.

Remark 1.2
.

Observe that under
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\big(\cdot\big||Z_{n}|>0\big)
,

\left\{\frac{1}{n}Z_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\sqrt{n}\cdot)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
converges weakly to some measure-valued process
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
as
n\to\infty
, where
\{Y_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is related to
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
by the Poisson cluster representation; see (
3.49
) below. On the other hand, from (
3.23
) below, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{-n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0\Big||Z_{n}|>0\right)dx\right\}.

Thus, under the assumption of Theorem
1.2
, we conjecture that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right)dx\right\}

(namely, the upper bound is sharp). However, since
{\bf 1}_{\{z>0\}}
is not a continuous function on
[0,\infty)
, the conjecture is not a direct corollary of the weak convergence.

Denote by
v_{d}(r):=\frac{\pi^{d/2}r^{d}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}
(where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the so-called Gamma function) the volume of a
d
-dimensional ball with radius
r
. Let
X^{(1)}
be the first component of vector
X
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
m=1
,
\sigma^{2}<\infty
and
d\geq 3
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{3}]<\infty
. Then there exists a positive function
F_{d}(r)
,
r>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}\geq r\right)=e^{-F_{d}(r)}.

Moreover,

\frac{v_{d}(r)}{1+\sigma^{2}C_{d}(r)r^{2}}\leq F_{d}(r)\leq v_{d}(r),

where
C_{d}(r):=\frac{2\Big[1+6\mathbb{E}[|X^{(1)}|^{3}]/\left(r\mathbb{E}[|X^{(1)}|^{2}]^{3/2}\right)\Big]^{d}}{(d-2)}+1.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.3
, one can see that there exists a constant
C(d,\sigma)>0
such that for
r>1
,

C(d,\sigma)r^{d-2}\leq F_{d}(r)\leq v_{d}(1)r^{d}.

Révész
[
12
, Conjecture 1]
conjectured that for a
d
-dimensional (
d\geq 3
) critical binary branching Wiener process,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{F_{d}(r)}{C_{d}r^{d-2}}=1,

where
C_{d}>0
is a constant satisfying
\lim_{d\to\infty}C_{d}/[v_{d}(1)^{(d-2)/d}]=1
. According to our results for the super-Brownian motion
[
15
, Theorem 3]
, we think this conjecture is true. Unfortunately, our method to deal with high-dimensional (
d\geq 3
) branching random walks is not delicate enough, especially the lower bound of
\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}\geq r\right)
.

Remark 1.4
.

Hu
[
5
]
studied this question for a
d
-dimensional (
d\geq 3
) critical binary branching Wiener process (i.e.
p_{0}=p_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
,
X
is a normal random vector). Our result is similar to those of Hu, but the conditions are much weaker.

The above three theorems consider the case of
\sigma^{2}<\infty
. A natural question is to consider the case of
\sigma^{2}=\infty
. For this purpose, we consider the case that the offspring law is in the domain attraction of an
(1+\beta)
-stable law, where
\beta\in(0,1)
. Unlike the case of
\sigma^{2}<\infty
, in this case, the renormalization scale depends on the offspring law parameter
\beta
. Denote by
f(s):=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}p_{k}s^{k},~s\in[0,1]
the generating function of the offspring law, and let
L(s),s\in[0,1]
be a slowly varying function as
s\to 0+
(i.e.
\lim_{s\to 0+}L(sx)/L(s)=1
for
x\in(0,1]
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
m=1
and
f(s)=s+(1-s)^{1+\beta}L(1-s)
for some
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{d}\right]
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>(\beta+1)d
. Then for
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}\right\},

where

b_{n}:=\Big[nL\big(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\beta d}},~n\geq 1.

The above four theorems consider the critical case
m=1
. It turns out that
R_{n}
basically grows like a power function. However, the next theorem tells us that, in the subcritical case (i.e.
m<1
),
R_{n}
increases exponentially. The result holds in a very general setting.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
m<1
,
\sum_{k\geq 0}p_{k}k\log k<\infty
and
d\geq 1
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]<\infty
for some
\alpha>1
. Then for
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{(1/m)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\geq r\right)=e^{-Q(0)v_{d}(r)},

where
Q(s),~s\in[0,1]
is the unique solution of the functional equation (see
[
1
, p40]
):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&Q(f(s))=mf(s);\cr&Q(1)=0,\lim\limits_{s\to 1}Q^{\prime}(s)=1.\end{cases}

Moreover
Q(s)>0
for
s\in[0,1)
.

2
-
4
are devoted to study the case
m=1
and
\sigma^{2}<\infty
, where Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
are proved, respectively. We shall consider the case
m=1
and
\sigma^{2}=\infty
in Section

, and Theorem
1.4
is proved in this section. We study the case
m<1
in Section
6
, where Theorem
1.5
is proved.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
m=1
,
d=1

Let
\{W_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a random walk with increment
X
. For the remainder of the paper, we use
\mathbb{P}_{x}
(and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
) to mean that the random walk
\{W_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
(or Brownian motion) starts from
x
, and write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{0}
for short. We first give a lemma which concerns the deviation probabilities of a random walk.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
d=1
. If
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 1
, then there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for any
x>0
and
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}|\geq xn)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{n^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

For the case of
\alpha\geq 2
, the lemma is a direct consequence of
[
10
, Corollary 1.8]
. In the next, we only deal with the case
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Let
X_{i},~i\geq 1
be i.i.d. copies of
X
. From
[
10
, Corollary 1.6]
: if
A^{+}_{\alpha}:=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}^{\alpha}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{i}\geq 0\}}\right]<\infty
, then for any
z,~y>0
satisfying
y^{\alpha}\geq 4A^{+}_{\alpha}
and
z>y
,

\mathbb{P}(W_{n}\geq z)\leq n\mathbb{P}(X>y)+\left(\frac{eA^{+}_{\alpha}}{zy^{\alpha-1}}\right)^{\frac{z}{2y}}.

Let
z=nx
,
y=z/2
. Hence, if
(nx/2)^{\alpha}\geq 4n\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{n}\geq xn)

\displaystyle\leq n\mathbb{P}(X>nx/2)+\frac{eA^{+}_{\alpha}}{nx(nx/2)^{\alpha-1}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\alpha}n\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]}{(nx)^{\alpha}}+\frac{2^{\alpha-1}en\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]}{(nx)^{\alpha}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]+2^{\alpha-1}e\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{\alpha}\right]}{n^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha}}.

(2.3)

Thus, the lemma follows.

\Box

In literature, we call
\{|Z_{n}|\}_{n\geq 0}
, under
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}
, a branching process or Galton-Watson process; see
[
1
]
. It is well known that a branching process with a critical or subcritical offspring law will become extinct with probability
1
. Thus, the survival probability
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. The following lemma concerns the decay rate of it. Recall that
\sigma^{2}:=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k^{2}p_{k}-\left[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}kp_{k}\right]^{2}
,
f(s)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}p_{k}s^{k}
and
L(s)
is a slowly varying function at
0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(survival probabilities)

(i) If
m=1
and
\sigma^{2}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}.

(ii) If
m=1
and
f(s)=s+(1-s)^{1+\beta}L(1-s)
for some
\beta\in(0,1]
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)^{\beta}L(\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0))=\frac{1}{\beta}.

(iii) If
m<1
and
\sum_{k\geq 0}k\log kp_{k}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m^{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)=Q(0)\in(0,\infty),

where
Q(\cdot)
is given in Theorem
1.5
.

Proof.

(i) and (iii) come from
[
1
, p19]
and
[
1
, p40]
, respectively. (ii) follows from
[
14
, Lemma 2]
.

\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Upper bound.
Recall that for a particle
u
,
S_{u}
stands for its position. Denote by
\{Z^{\delta_{x}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
the branching random walk started from a single particle at position
x
. Recall that we write
u\in Z_{0}
if
u
is a particle at time
0
. Since
Z_{0}
is a Poisson random measure, by the branching property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq nr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}(B(nr))=0\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\forall u\in Z_{0},Z^{\delta_{S_{u}}}_{n}(B(nr))=0\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Pi_{u\in Z_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{S_{u}}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))=0)\right]

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\sum_{u\in Z_{0}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{S_{u}}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))=0)}\right]

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))=0)Z_{0}(dx)}\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx},

(2.9)

where the last equality follows from the Laplace functional formula of the Poisson random measure; see
[
2
, p19]
.
Let
L_{n}:=\min\{|S_{u}|:u\in Z_{n}\}
, where we define
+\infty=\min\emptyset
by convention. Recall that
\{W_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
stands for a random walk with increment
X
, and we use
\mathbb{P}_{x}
to mean that
\{W_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
starts from
x
and write
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{0}
for short. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0\big||Z_{n}|>0\right)\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(Z_{n}(B(nr))=0\big||Z_{n}|>0\right)\right]\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(L_{n}\geq nr\big||Z_{n}|>0\right)\right]\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}\left(|W_{n}|\geq nr\big||Z_{n}|>0\right)\right]\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|x+W_{n}|<nr\right)\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0),

where the inequality follows from the fact that
|W_{n}|\geq L_{n}
and the last equality follows from the independent of the branching and the motion.

Therefore, for
\delta\in(0,r)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}+x|<nr)dx

(2.14)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|\leq n(r-\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}+x|<nr)dx

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq r-\delta}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}+ny|<nr)dy

(2.16)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq r-\delta}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|W_{n}|}{n}<\delta\right)dy

(2.17)

\displaystyle=2(r-\delta)n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|W_{n}|}{n}<\delta\right).

(2.18)

Hence, by the law of large numbers and Lemma
2.2
(i), we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx\geq\frac{4(r-\delta)}{\sigma^{2}}.

Plugging above into (
2.4
) and letting
\delta\to 0
give that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq nr)\leq e^{-\frac{4r}{\sigma^{2}}}.

Lower bound.
Let
\delta\in(0,\infty)
. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|\leq n(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)dx+\int_{|x|>n(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx

(2.20)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)n(r+\delta)+\int_{|x|>n(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx.

(2.21)

By the Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z_{n}(B(nr))]

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[|Z_{n}|]\mathbb{P}_{x}(|W_{n}|\leq nr)

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(|x+W_{n}|\leq nr)

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(|x|-|W_{n}|\leq nr)

(2.25)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}|\geq|x|-nr),

(2.26)

where the first equality follows from the fact that the branching and motion are independent.

Since
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>2
, by (
2.22
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>n(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|>n(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}|\geq|x|-nr)dx

(2.27)

\displaystyle=n\int_{|y|>r+\delta}\mathbb{P}\left(|W_{n}|\geq n(|y|-r)\right)dy

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|y|>r+\delta}\frac{C_{1}}{n^{\alpha-2}(y-r)^{\alpha}}dy,

(2.29)

where the right hand side converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Plugging (
2.27
) into (
2.19
), and then using Lemma
2.2
(i), to get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(nr))>0)dx\leq\frac{4(r+\delta)}{\sigma^{2}}.

Plugging above into (
2.4
) and letting
\delta\to 0
give that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq nr)\geq e^{-\frac{4r}{\sigma^{2}}}.

We have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
m=1
,
d=2

The key to prove Theorem
1.2
is to use the fact that the scaling limits of the branching random walk is a super-Brownian motion. So, we first give a brief introduction to the super-Brownian motion.

Let
M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of finite measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the topology of weak convergence. Let
\psi(u)
be a function of the form

\psi(u)=au+bu^{2}+\int_{(0,\infty)}\left(e^{-ru}-1+ru\right)n(dr),~u\geq 0,

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\geq 0
and
n
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{(0,\infty)}(r\wedge r^{2})n(dr)<\infty
. The super-Brownian motion with initial value
\mu\in M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and branching mechanism
\psi
is an
M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued process, whose transition probabilities are characterized through their Laplace transforms. For any
\mu\in M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and nonnegative continuous function
\phi(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)X_{t}(dx)}\right]=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\mu(dx)},

(3.1)

where
u(t,x)
is the unique positive solution to the following nonlinear partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-\psi(u(t,x)),\cr u(0,x)=\phi(x).\end{cases}

In above,
\Delta u(t,x):=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\frac{\partial^{2}u(t,x)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
is the Laplace operator.
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called a supercritical (critical, subcritical) super-Brownian motion if
a<0~(=0,>0).
We refer the reader to
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
for a more detailed overview to super-Brownian motion.

In this paper, we always consider the critical branching mechanism
\psi(u)=\sigma^{2}u^{2}
(recall that
\sigma^{2}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k^{2}p_{k}-\left[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}kp_{k}\right]^{2}
). We first present a lemma concerning the probability of a super-Brownian motion charges the level set
[x,\infty)
. Denote by
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the historical super Brownian motion; see
[
11
, p187]
for a formal definition. Intuitively,
H_{t}
keeps track of the histories of all the masses of
X_{t}
. Let
S(H_{t})
be the closed support of the random measure
H_{t}
and
C(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the space of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}
. From
[
11
, p195]
, we have the following:

(i) there exist a constant
c>2
and a random variable
\Delta
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}
almost surely, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle S(H_{t})\subset\left\{y(\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):|y(r)-y(s)|\leq c|(r-s)\log(r-s)|^{1/2},\forall r,s>0,|r-s|\leq\Delta\right\};

(3.2)

(ii) there are constants
\rho,~\kappa>0
depending only on
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(\Delta\leq r)\leq\kappa r^{\rho}~\text{for}~r\in[0,1].

(3.3)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an one-dimensional critical super-Brownian motion. Then there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for any
x>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(X_{1}([x,\infty))>0)<C_{2}e^{-x}.

Proof.

Let

T:=\inf\{s\geq 0:X_{s}([x,\infty))>0\}.

Define

\displaystyle a(x):

\displaystyle=\lfloor e^{x/\rho}\rfloor;

(3.4)

\displaystyle A(t,x):

\displaystyle=\left\{y(\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\sup_{s\in[0,t]}y(s)\geq x\right\}.

Because
\lim_{x\to\infty}a(x)=+\infty
, there exists a constant
M
such that for all
x>M
and
|r-s|<\frac{2}{a(x)}
,

\displaystyle c|(r-s)\log(r-s)|^{1/2}<1.

(3.5)

Since under the event
\{\exists s\in[0,1],H_{s}(A(s,x))>0\}
, we have
T\in(0,1]
, there exists an integer
i\in\{0,1,...,a(x)-1\}
such that

T\in(i/a(x),(i+1)/a(x)]
.

For
i\geq 1
, if
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has charged the set
[x-1,\infty)
at time
(i-1)/a(x)
, then

H_{i/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))>0.

Otherwise it has not charged the set
[x-1,\infty)
, then the support process for
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has to travel a distance of at least
1
on time interval
[(i-1)/a(x),T]\subset[(i-1)/a(x),(i+1)/a(x)]
.
This, combined with (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), entails that for
x>M
,

\Delta<\frac{2}{a(x)}.

For
i=0
, since
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has charged the set
[x,\infty)
on time interval
(0,1/a(x)]
, we have

\Delta<\frac{1}{a(x)}.

Putting above together, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}([x,\infty))>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\exists s\in[0,1],X_{s}([x,\infty))>0\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\exists s\in[0,1],H_{s}(A(s,x))>0\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{a(x)}_{i=1}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(H_{i/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))>0\right)+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\Delta<\frac{2}{a(x)}\right).

(3.8)

Define
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(H_{s},s\in[0,t])
. Let
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an one-dimensional Brownian motion started from the origin. Note that
\{H_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a time-inhomogeneous Markov process. Write
\mathbb{P}_{{s,u}}(H_{s+t}\in\cdot):=\mathbb{P}(H_{s+t}\in\cdot|H_{s}=u)
for
s,t\geq 0
and a measure
u
on the space of c
\grave{\text{a}}
dl
\grave{\text{a}}
g paths from
[0,s]
to
\mathbb{R}
; see
[
11
, p188]
for a more detailed description. By the Markov property of

and
[
11
, p194, Lemma III.1.2]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(H_{i/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))>0\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left[\mathbb{P}_{(i-1)/a(x),H_{(i-1)/a(x)}}\left[H_{i/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))=0|\mathcal{F}_{(i-1)/a(x)}\right]\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\left[\exp\left\{-2a(x)H_{(i-1)/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))\right\}\right]

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq 2a(x)\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\left[H_{(i-1)/a(x)}(A((i-1)/a(x),x-1))\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle=2a(x)\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,(i-1)/a(x)]}|B_{s}|\geq x-1\right),

(3.13)

where the inequality follows from the fact that
1-e^{-x}\leq x
and the last equality is because that the mean measure of
H_{t}
is the Brownian motion stopped at time

(i.e. one moment formula of
H_{t}
; see
[
11

).

It is well-known that
\sup_{s\in(0,t)}B_{s}
and
|B_{t}|
have the same law under
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}
. For
i\geq 2
and
x>2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,(i-1)/a(x)]}|B_{s}|\geq x-1\right)

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,(i-1)/a(x)]}B_{s}\geq(x-1)~\text{or}~\inf_{s\in[0,(i-1)/a(x)]}B_{s}\leq-(x-1)\right)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,(i-1)/a(x)]}B_{s}\geq(x-1)\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{(i-1)/a(x)}|\geq(x-1)\right)

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{P}\left(B_{1}\geq x-1\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/4},

(3.19)

where the last inequality follows from the following classical estimate for the standard normal
random variable:

\mathbb{P}\left(B_{1}>x\right)\leq\frac{1}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}.

Recall that
a(x)=\lfloor e^{x/\rho}\rfloor
. Plugging (
3.14
), (
3.9
) into (
3.6
) yields that there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for
x

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}([x,\infty))>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16a^{2}(x)}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/4}+\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(\Delta<\frac{2}{a(x)}\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}(x)e^{-x^{2}/4}+\kappa 2^{\rho}a^{-\rho}(x)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}e^{-|x|},

where the second inequality follows from (

). Thus, the lemma follows.

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
. The idea of the proof is as follows. For the upper bound, from
[
7

we know that under

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\frac{Z_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}(\sqrt{n}\cdot)}{n}\right\}_{t\geq 0}\overset{\text{weakly}}{=}\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},

(3.22)

where
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a two-dimensional critical super-Brownian motion with branching mechanism

and initial value

Thus, the Laplace functional of

converges to the Laplace functional of

, which can be used to deal with the probability
\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)
. By this method, we transform the empty ball problem for branching random walks to the problem for super-Brownian motions which have been studied in
[
15
]
. For the lower bound, we mainly use the fact that the condition maximum of a critical branching random walk converges in distribution to the maximum of some measure valued process

3.1
to obtain the desired lower bound.

Proof.

Upper bound.

Similarly to (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(\sqrt{n}r))>0)dx\right\}

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}.

(3.24)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)=\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0\right)\right]^{n}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0\right)

(3.25)

\displaystyle=1-\exp\left\{\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\right\}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\left[1+\frac{1}{2n}\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\right]

(3.27)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\right]

(3.28)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(|Z_{n}|=0\right)\right],

(3.29)

where the first inequality follows from the fact that

for
x<0
. By (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lim_{\theta\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{E}_{n\delta_{0}}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\theta\sqrt{n}B(x,r))}\right]

(3.31)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{E}_{n\delta_{0}}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\theta\sqrt{n}B(x,r))}\right]

(3.32)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}\left[e^{-X_{1}(\theta B(x,r))}\right]

(3.33)

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}(B(x,r))=0\right),

(3.34)

where the inequality follows from the fact that
-\log\mathbb{E}_{n\delta_{0}}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\theta\sqrt{n}B(x,r))}\right]
is increasing w.r.t.
\theta
. Plugging (
3.30
) and (
3.25
) into (

) yields that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)

(3.35)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}

(3.36)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx\right\}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{P}_{n\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{n}Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))=0\right)\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(|Z_{n}|=0\right)\right]dx\right\}

(3.38)

(3.39)

where the first inequality follows from Fatou’s Lemma.

Lower bound.

. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq\sqrt{n}r)

.

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Y_{1}([|x|-r))>0\right)dx.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}([|x|-r,\infty))>0\right)

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Y_{1}([|x|-r,\infty))>0\right)}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(Y_{1}([|x|-r,\infty))>0\right)

\displaystyle=-\log\left[1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}\left(X_{1}([|x|-r,\infty))>0\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(1+\delta)e^{-(|x|-r)},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma

. Plugging above into (
3.50

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|>(1+\delta)r}n\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(Z_{n}(\sqrt{n}B(x,r))>0)dx

Thus, the desired lower bound follows by letting

The first result below tells us that for any

converges in law. The result is universal for any critical and subcritical branching random walk.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume

Proof.

Similarly to (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}(B(r))=0\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))=0)dx}.

(4.2)

For
\theta>0
, write

u_{\theta}(k,x):=-\log\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-\theta Z_{k}(B(r))}\right],~k\geq 1.

Since
u_{\theta}(k,x)
is increasing w.r.t.
\theta

u(k,x):=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}u_{\theta}(k,x)
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-\theta Z_{n}(B(r))}\right]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}e^{-u_{\theta}(n,x)}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=e^{-u(n,x)}.

(4.5)

This, combined with (
4.1
), gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1-e^{-u(n,x)}dx\right\}.

Note that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle u(n,x)

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))=0)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq-\log\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|=0)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=:c_{n}.

(4.8)

Since
y-\frac{y^{2}}{2}<1-e^{-y}<y

y>0

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n,x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle<\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\geq r)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-\left(1-\frac{c_{n}}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n,x)dx\right\}.

(4.11)

Recall that
f(s)=\sum_{k\geq 0}p_{k}s^{k}
. By the branching property,

\displaystyle e^{-u_{\theta}(n,x)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-\theta Z_{n}(B(r))}\right]

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-\theta\sum_{u\in Z_{1}}Z^{\delta_{S_{u}}}_{n-1}(B(r))}\right]

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}p_{k}\mathbb{E}^{k}_{\delta_{x}}\left[e^{-\theta Z^{\delta_{W_{1}}}_{n-1}(B(r))}\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}p_{k}\mathbb{E}^{k}_{x}\left[e^{-u_{\theta}(n-1,W_{1})}\right]

(4.15)

\displaystyle=f\left(\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-u_{\theta}(n-1,W_{1})}\right]\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-u_{\theta}(n-1,W_{1})}\right]

(4.17)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u_{\theta}(n-1,W_{1})\right]},

where the first inequality follows by the fact that

(see
[
1
, p4]
) and the second inequality follows from the Jensen inequality. Thus, this yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\theta}(n,x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[u_{\theta}(n-1,W_{1})\right]dx

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\theta}(n-1,x+X)dx\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\theta}(n-1,x)dx.

is increasing w.r.t.
\theta

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n,x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n-1,x)dx.

Therefore, by the monotone convergence theorem
\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n,x)dx}
exists.

On the other hand, using the assumption that the branching process is non-supercritical, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}=0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}\geq r\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(n,x)dx}~\text{exists}.

Upper bound.

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))>0)dx\right\}.

By the Paly-Zygmund inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))>0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}^{2}[Z_{n}(B(r))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z^{2}_{n}(B(r))]}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}(|x+W_{n}|<r)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z^{2}_{n}(B(r))]},

(4.23)

where the equality follows from the independent of the branching and the motion. It is simple to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}[Z^{2}_{n}(B(r))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{u\in Z_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{v\in Z_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}\in B(r)\}}\Big)\Big]

(4.24)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Bigg[\sum_{u\in Z_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}+\sum_{u,v\in Z_{n}\atop u\neq v}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}\in B(r)\}}\Bigg]

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(|x+W_{n}|<r)+\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Bigg[\sum_{u,v\in Z_{n}\atop u\neq v}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}\in B(r)\}}\Bigg].

(4.26)

In the next, we are going to calculate
\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Bigg[\sum\limits_{u,v\in Z_{n}\atop u\neq v}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}\in B(r)\}}\Bigg].

j

i\geq 0

u
and
v
is in generation
k
.

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma(|Z_{i}|,i\leq n)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{x}}\Bigg[\sum_{u,v\in Z_{n}\atop u\neq v}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\in B(r)\}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}\in B(r)\}}\Bigg]

(4.32)

(4.44)

(4.45)

C_{d}(r):=\frac{2(r+C_{4})^{d}}{(d-2)r^{d}}+1

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}(|x+W_{n}|<r)}{1+\sigma^{2}C_{d}(r)r^{2}}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(r))>0)dx

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}nb^{d-\alpha}_{n}=0.

Thus, the second term on the right hand side of (
5.14
) converges to
0
as
n\to\infty.
This, together with (
5.14
) and (
5.2
), implies that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{b_{n}}\geq r\right)\geq e^{-v_{d}(r)(\beta^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}}.

\Box

6
Proof of Theorem
1.5
:
m<1

Again, the proof of this theorem is similar to that for Theorem
1.1
in spirit.

Proof.

Upper bound
.
Similarly to (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{(1/m)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\geq r\right)=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx\right\}.

(6.1)

Fix
\delta\in(0,r)
. Similarly to (
5.10
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|\leq m^{-n/d}(r-\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx

(6.2)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|x|\leq m^{-n/d}(r-\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}+x|\leq m^{-n/d}r)dx

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)\int_{|y|\leq r-\delta}\mathbb{P}(|W_{n}+m^{-n/d}y|\leq m^{-n/d}r)m^{-n}dy

(6.4)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}\int_{|y|\leq r-\delta}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{W_{n}}{m^{-n/d}}+y\right|\leq r\right)dy

(6.5)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}v_{d}(r-\delta)\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{W_{n}}{m^{-n/d}}\right|\leq\delta\right).

(6.6)

It is simple to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{W_{n}}{m^{-n/d}}\right|\leq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{S^{(1)}_{n}}{m^{-n/d}}\right|\leq\delta\right)^{d}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(|X^{(1)}_{i}|\leq m^{-n/d}\delta/n~,\forall i=1,2,...,n\right)^{d}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\mathbb{P}\left(|X^{(1)}|\geq m^{-n/d}\delta/n\right)\right]^{nd}.\frac{}{}

(6.9)

If
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then by Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|X^{(1)}|\geq m^{-n/d}\delta/n\right)\leq\left(\frac{n}{m^{-n/d}\delta}\right)^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[|X^{(1)}|^{\alpha}\right].

Plugging above into (
6.7
) yields that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{W_{n}}{m^{-n/d}}\right|\leq\delta\right)=1.

Thus, from (
6.2
) and Lemma
2.2
, we obatin

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{(1/m)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx\right\}

(6.10)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r-\delta)\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}\right\}

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r-\delta)Q(0)\right\},

which implies the desired upper bound by letting
\delta\to 0
.

Lower bound
.
Fix
\delta>0
, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|\leq m^{-n/d}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(|Z_{n}|>0)dx+\int_{|x|>m^{-n/d}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}v_{d}(r+\delta)+\int_{|x|>m^{-n/d}(r+\delta)}\mathbb{E}_{\delta_{0}}[|Z_{n}|]\mathbb{P}\left(|W_{n}+x|\leq m^{-n/d}r\right)dx

(6.14)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}v_{d}(r+\delta)+\int_{|y|>r+\delta}\mathbb{P}\left(|W_{n}+m^{-n/d}y|\leq m^{-n/d}r\right)dy.

(6.15)

If
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]<\infty
for some
\alpha>1
, then by Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|W_{n}+m^{-n/d}y|\leq m^{-n/d}r\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|W_{n}|\geq m^{-n/d}(|y|-r)\right)

(6.16)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\exists 1\leq i\leq n~\text{s.t.}~|X_{i}|\geq m^{-n/d}(|y|-r)/n\right)

(6.17)

\displaystyle\leq n\mathbb{P}\left(|X|\geq m^{-n/d}(|y|-r)/n\right)

(6.18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{1+\alpha}}{m^{-n\alpha/d}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|X|^{\alpha}]}{(|y|-r)^{\alpha}},

(6.19)

which implies the second term on the r.h.s. of (
6.12
) tends to
0
. This, together with (
6.12
), yields that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{(1/m)^{\frac{n}{d}}}\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{x}}(Z_{n}(B(m^{-n/d}r))>0)dx\right\}

(6.20)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r+\delta)\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(|Z_{n}|>0)m^{-n}\right\}

(6.21)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-v_{d}(r+\delta)Q(0)\right\},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
2.2
. The desired lower bound follows by letting
\delta\to 0
.

\Box

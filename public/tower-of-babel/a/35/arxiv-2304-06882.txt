On the dimension of c c -nilpotent multiplier of n n -Lie algebras
Farshid Saeedi ∗ and Seyedeh Nafiseh Akbarossadat

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional
n
-Lie algebra with free presentation
F/R
. Then the concept of
c
-nilpotent multiplier of
L
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
, is defined as follows:

\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}.

In this paper, we obtain some inequalities and certain bounds for the dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
by using the basic commutators. Also, we discuss the relationship between the dimension of the
c
-nilpotent multiplier of
L
and the
c
-nilpotent multiplier of some factor of
L
.
We further obtain an inequality between dimensions of
c
-nilpotent multiplier of
n
-Lie algebra and non-abelian tensor (exterior) product of a central ideal by its abelianized factor
n
-Lie algebra. Finally, we also determine the dimension and structure of
c
-nilpotent multipliers Heisenberg
n
-Lie algebras, which can be a useful tool for determining the dimension of the multiplier of nilpotent
n
-Lie algebras of class
2
.

Key words:

n
-Lie algebra, Non-abelian tensor product,
c
-Nilpotent multiplier,
c
-Capable.

17B05, 17B30, 17B60.

In what follows, we recall the definition of crossed modules of
n
-Lie algebras.

Definition 1.2

(Crossed module)
.

A crossed module is a homomorphism of Lie
n
-algebras
\mu:L\longrightarrow P

together with an action
\{g_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
of
P
on
L
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\mu
is compatible with the action of
P
on
L
, that is,

\mu\left(g_{i}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{i},l_{1},\ldots,l_{n-i})\right)=[p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-i},\mu(l_{1}),\ldots,\mu(l_{i})],

for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

(2)

For all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

g_{i}(\mu(l_{1}),\ldots,\mu(l_{i}),l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{n})=[l_{1},\ldots,l_{i},l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{n}].

(3)

For all
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
i+1\leq j\leq n
,

g_{i}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{i},l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{n})=(-1)^{j-i-1}g_{i+1}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{i},\mu(l_{j}),l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{j-1},l_{j+1},\ldots,l_{n}).

It is easy to check that the kernel of
\mu
is in the center of
L
and that its image is an ideal in
P
.
Furthermore, the
n
-Lie algebra
\mathrm{Im}(\mu)
acts trivially on the center
Z(L)
, and so trivially on

\ker\mu
. Hence
\ker\mu
inherits an action of
P/\mathrm{Im}(\mu)
making
\ker\mu
a representation of the
n
-Lie algebra
P/\mathrm{Im}(\mu)
.

Preliminary results

In this section, we try to present some important and practical tools in order to prove the main results of this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0
be a free presentation of the
n
-Lie algebra
L
and let
M
be an ideal of
L
with free presentation
S/R
, for some ideal
S
of
F
. Then

\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M\cong\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)}.

Proof.

By the definition of the lower central series, we know that

\displaystyle\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M=\gamma_{c+1}(F/R)\cap(S/R)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)+R}{R}\cap\dfrac{S}{R}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)+R)\cap S}{R}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)+(R\cap S)}{R}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)+R}{R}

\displaystyle\cong\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)}{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)\cap R}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}

\displaystyle\cong\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)}.

∎

(2)

3.
On the
c
-nilpotent multiplier of
n
-Lie algebras

In this section, first we introduce some inequalities for the dimension of the
c
-nilpotent multiplier of the
n
-Lie algebras
and their factors. Then, we show that every
c
-perfect
n
-Lie algebra has at least one cover. Also, we give some upper bounds for the dimension of
n
-nilpotent multiplier of
n
-Lie algebras using the basic commutators.

The following lemma is one of the important tools for obtaining the required inequalities, which is similar to the work of Araskhan and Rismanchian
[
7
]
for the Lie algebras and the work of Jones
[
12
]
for the group case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L
be an
n
-Lie algebra with dimension finite, and let
M
be its ideal. Then there exists an
n
-Lie algebra
K
with its ideal
S
such that

(1)

\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M=K/S
;

(2)

S\cong\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
;

(3)

\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
is a homomorphic image of
K
.

(4)

If, in addition,
M
is a
c
-central ideal of
L
, then
\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M
is a homomorphic image
of
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R\unlhd\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S
, so by Lemma
2.1
, it is enough to put
K/M=\dfrac{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}
.

(2)

By the definition of the
c
-nilpotent multiplier of
L
and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain
S=\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
.

(3)

Consider the free presentation

L/M=\dfrac{F/R}{S/R}\cong F/S,

and hence

\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}.

Now, consider the map
\alpha:(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)\longrightarrow\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}
.
It is easy to check that
\alpha
is an epimorphism of
n
-Lie algebras with the kernel ideal
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
.

(4)

Since
M
is a
c
-central ideal, so
\gamma_{c+1}(M,L,\dots,L)=1
. Hence
\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)+R}{R}=R
. Thus
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq R
. Therefore,
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R
and
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S
.
Also, by the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we know that

\displaystyle\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M\cong\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}\cong\dfrac{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}}{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}}=\dfrac{\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)}{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}}.

Thus the proof is completed by considering the epimorphism
\alpha:\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\longrightarrow\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M=\dfrac{\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)}{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap R}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}}
.

∎

The following corollary follows immediately from the previous lemma.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
L
be an
n
-Lie algebra with dimension finite, and let
M
be its ideal. Then we have the following inequality:

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M).

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
, we have
\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M=K/S
and
S\cong\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
. Thus

\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M)=\dim K-\dim S,\qquad\dim S=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L).

Hence,

\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M)=\dim K-\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L).

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
is a homomorphic image of
K
, so
\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\leq\dim K
. Therefore,

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M).

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
L
be an
n
-Lie algebra with dimension finite and free presentation
F/R
, and let
M
be a central ideal of
L
with free presentation
S/R
, for some ideal
S
of
F
. Then

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(M)+\dim((L/M)^{ab}\ {}^{c}\otimes M).

Proof.

Since
M
is central, so
\gamma_{2}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq R
. By Corollary
3.2
and the proof of part (3) of Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
is an isomorphic image of
(\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
with the kernel ideal
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
. Also, we have

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M).

On the other hand, we know that

\dfrac{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}}{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}.

Hence

\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}-\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}=\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}.

Also, by this fact that
M
is a central ideal, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{c+1}(S)\subseteq\gamma_{2}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq R

(3.4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(R,F,\dots,F)\subseteq R.

Therefore, it follows from equations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) that

(3.5)

\gamma_{2}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)\subseteq R.

On the other hand, since
R\subseteq S
, so

(3.6)

\gamma_{2}(R,F,\dots,F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(S),

and hence equations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) imply that

(3.7)

\gamma_{2}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(S)\cap R.

Thus

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(\gamma_{c+1}(L)\cap M)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}

\displaystyle\quad+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle(\text{by Lemma \ref{lem2.3(2)}})\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\left((L/M)^{ab}\ {}^{c}\otimes M\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(S)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{equation(5)}})\qquad\qquad\qquad\

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(S)\cap R}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)+\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(M)

\displaystyle\quad+\dim\left((L/M)^{ab}\ {}^{c}\otimes M\right).

∎

The following proposition plays a fundamental and important role in obtaining some main results of this section.

Proposition 3.4

Let

n

Corollary 3.5

Let

(1).

The

\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\cong\dfrac{M\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L)}{\gamma_{c+1}(M,L,\dots,L)}

(1).

It is clear according to the short exact sequence (

(2).

According to part

of Proposition

(3).

The short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow L^{*}\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0
of
n
-Lie algebras is called

-stem cover, if

and

. Then

is called

-cover of

. Specially, if

, then it is the definition of cover.

Moreover, an
n
-Lie algebra

is called

-perfect, if
\gamma_{c+1}(L)=L
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
L
be a
c
-perfect finite-dimensional
n
-Lie algebra with
c
-cover
L^{*}
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
is trivial.

Proof.

By the definition of
c
-cover, there exists an ideal
M
of
L
such that

L^{*}/M\cong L,\qquad M\subseteq Z_{c}(L^{*})\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L^{*}),\qquad\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\cong M.

Since
M
is
c
-central and
L
is
c
-perfect, that is,
L=\gamma_{c+1}(L)
, so by part (5) of
3.5
, we have

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*})+\dim(M\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L^{*}))\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*}/M)+\underbrace{\dim(M\wedge^{c}\dfrac{L^{*}}{\gamma_{c+1}(L^{*})})}_{0}.

Thus

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*}/M)=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L).

Hence
\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*})\leq 0
, which implies that
\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L^{*})=0
.
∎

The authors in
[

, Proposition 1.6]
proved that if

is an

-Lie algebra with the free representation

and the map
\pi:F/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)\longrightarrow F/R
is an epimorphism, then

. Using this fact, we prove the following practical theorem.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional
n
-Lie algebra and let
M
be an ideal of
L
with
M\subseteq Z_{c}(L)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

M\subseteq Z^{*}_{c}(L)
;

(2)

The natural map
\alpha:\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
is a monomorphism;

(3)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(M\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L))
.

Proof.

Similar to
[
2
, Lemma 1.7]
, it can be proved that
M\subseteq Z^{*}_{c}(L)
if and only if
\alpha:\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)
is a monomorphism.

If
M\subseteq Z^{*}_{c}(L)
, then by part (4) of Lemma
3.1
and part (2), we have the following sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)\longrightarrow M\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L)\longrightarrow 0.

Hence

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim(M\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L)).

Conversely, if part (3) is hold, then by comparing part (3) of Corollary
3.5
and part (2), we get
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
. On the other hand, since
R\subseteq S
, so
\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)
. Therefore
\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)=\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
, and this fact is equivalent to
S/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)\subseteq Z_{c}(F/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F))
. From
[
2
, Proposition 1.6]
we know that
Z_{c}^{*}(L)=\pi\left(Z_{c}(F/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F))\right)
, and hence
\pi(S/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F))\subseteq Z_{c}^{*}(L)
. Therefore,
M\subseteq Z_{c}^{*}(L)
, which completes the proof.
∎

The following proposition states that every
c
-perfect
n
-Lie algebra has at least one cover.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
L
be a
c
-perfect
n
-Lie algebra. Then
L
has at least one cover.

Proof.

Let
\pi:F\longrightarrow L
be an epimorphism with
\ker\pi=R
, and hence
F/R
be a free presentation of

. Then

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{R}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\longrightarrow\dfrac{F}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\pi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}L\longrightarrow 0,

be the free
c
-central extension of
L
.
Since
\bar{\pi}(\gamma_{c+1}(F)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F))=L
, so by restricting
\pi
to
\gamma_{c+1}(F)/\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)
, we have

\displaystyle\ker\bar{\pi}=\ker\pi\bigcap\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{R}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\bigcap\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}=\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L).

Thus we get the following

-central extension:

(3.17)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\longrightarrow\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0.

On the other hand,
L
is
c
-perfect, so

L\cong\dfrac{F}{R}\longrightarrow F\cong L\oplus R\longrightarrow F=\gamma_{c+1}(F)+R.

Thus

\displaystyle\gamma_{c+1}\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(\gamma_{c+1}(F),F,\dots,F)+\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(\gamma_{c+1}(F)+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}.

Therefore,
\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}
is
c
-perfect and so (
3.17
) is a stem
c
-central extension of
L
.
∎

In the following theorem, we give upper and lower bounds for the dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional nilpotent
n
-Lie algebra of class
m
, let
d=d(L)
, and let
\dim\gamma_{i+1}(L)=a_{i}
, for all
i\geq 1
. Then
the following inequalities are hold:

l_{d}^{n}(c+1)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)\leq l_{d}^{n}(c+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}d^{c(n-1)-i+1}.

If
L
is abelian, then the lower and upper bounds are attained.

Proof.

Since
L
is nilpotent, so
\Phi(L)=L^{2}
, and hence
L/L^{2}
is an abelian
n
-Lie algebra of dimension
d
. If we put
M=L^{2}
, then by Proposition
1.5
we have

(3.18)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/M)=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/L^{2})=l_{d}^{n}(c+1).

Thus by putting
M=L^{2}
in part
(2)
of Corollary
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L/L^{2})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\dfrac{L^{2}\cap\gamma_{c+1}(L)}{\gamma_{c+1}(L^{2},L,\dots,L)}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(L)}{\gamma_{c+2}(L)}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L).

Therefore, by (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we have

(3.20)

l_{d}^{n}(c+1)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L).

Now let
0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0
be a free presentation of
L
, and put
M=L^{2}
, in part
(1)
of Proposition
2.6
. Then

L=\dfrac{F}{R}\longrightarrow M=L^{2}=\left(\dfrac{F}{R}\right)^{\prime}=\dfrac{F^{2}+R}{R},

and hence
T_{c+1}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}
, and it is an isomorphic image of
L^{2}\wedge^{c}L
, by part
(1)
of Proposition
2.6
. That is, there is an epimorphism
\psi:L^{2}\wedge^{c}L\longrightarrow T_{c+1}
such that

(3.21)

\dfrac{L^{2}\wedge^{c}L}{\ker\psi}\cong T_{c+1}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}.

Now, if
M=L=F/R
, then

T^{\prime}_{c+1}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}
.
Thus

(3.22)

\displaystyle\dfrac{T^{\prime}_{c+1}}{T_{c+1}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}}{\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}}

\displaystyle\cong\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}.

On the other hand, by (
3.22
) we know that there exists the following epimorphism:

\displaystyle\theta:T^{\prime}_{c+1}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle epi.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{T^{\prime}_{c+1}}{T_{c+1}}=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}.

Then
\ker\theta=\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}
, and so we obtain the following short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\ker\theta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle inc.}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{\prime}_{c+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{T^{\prime}_{c+1}}{T_{c+1}}\longrightarrow 0.

That is,

(3.23)

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle inc.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus by (
3.21
) and (
3.23
), we have

(3.24)

L^{2}\wedge^{c}L\longrightarrow\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2}+R,F,\dots,F)}\longrightarrow 0.

Now, by part (3) of Corollary
3.5
, and then using (
3.24
) and Theorem
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)

\displaystyle=\dim\left(\gamma_{c+1}\left(\dfrac{F}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dim(L^{2}\wedge^{c}L)+\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(\underbrace{F^{2}+R}_{\supseteq F^{2}},F,\dots,F)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dim(L^{2}\wedge^{c}L)+\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F^{2},F,\dots,F)}\right)

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\dim(L^{2}\wedge^{c}L)+\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+2}(F)}\right).

We know that
L^{2}\wedge^{c}L=(\dots((L^{2}\wedge L)\wedge L)\dots)\wedge L
and also that the generators of
L^{2}\wedge L
are as
a_{1}\wedge l_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge l_{n}
,
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\wedge l_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge l_{n},\ldots,
and
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge a_{n-1}\wedge l_{n}
, where
a_{i}\in L^{2}
, and
l_{j}\in L
, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
2\leq j\leq n
. Indeed by using equation (
1.1
), one can easily check that
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\wedge l_{3}\wedge\dots\wedge l_{n}\in L^{3}\wedge L
, and similarly,
a_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge a_{i}\wedge l_{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge l_{n}\in L^{i+1}\wedge L
. Thus

(3.26)

\dim(L^{2}\wedge^{c}L)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}d^{c(n-1)-i+1}.

Therefore, by (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we obtain

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)\leq l_{d}^{n}(c+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}d^{c(n-1)-i+1}.

∎

The next theorem states an upper bound for the dimension of
c
-nilpotent multiplier of finite-dimensional nilpotent
n
-Lie algebras of class
m
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
L
be a
d
-dimensional nilpotent
n
-Lie algebra of class
m
. Then

(3.27)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\leq\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{c}l_{d}^{n}(m+k),

when
m\leq c
, and we have

(3.28)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\leq\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}l_{d}^{n}(c+k),

for all
c
in which
m\geq c+1
, and if
L
is abelian, then the equality is attained.

Proof.

Let
L\cong\dfrac{F}{R}
. First assume that
m\leq c
. Since
\gamma_{m+1}(L)=0
, so

\displaystyle 0=\gamma_{m+1}(L)=\gamma_{m+1}\left(\dfrac{F}{R}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{m+1}(F)+R}{R}\cong\dfrac{R}{\gamma_{m+1}(F)\cap R},

which implies that

\gamma_{m+1}(F)+R=\gamma_{m+1}(F)\cap R=R,

that is ,

\gamma_{m+1}(F)\subseteq\gamma_{m+1}(F)\cap R\subseteq R.

Also, we know that
\gamma_{m+1}(F)\subseteq\gamma_{m}(F)
. Thus

\gamma_{m+1}(F)\subseteq\gamma_{m}(F)\cap R
.
Therefore,

\gamma_{c+m+1}(F)\subseteq\gamma_{c+m}(F)\subseteq\dots\subseteq\gamma_{m+2}(F)\subseteq\gamma_{m+1}(F)\subseteq R\cap\gamma_{m}(F).

Hence

(3.29)

\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{m}(F)}{\gamma_{c+m+1}(F)}\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{m}(F)}{\gamma_{c+m+1}(F)}=\sum_{k=0}^{c}l_{d}^{n}(m+k).

On the other hand, we know that

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)=\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{m}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{m}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{m}(F)}{\gamma_{c+m+1}(F)}.

Thus by (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), we obtain that

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{c}l_{d}^{n}(m+k).

Now, suppose that
c+1\leq m
. Then

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)=\dim\dfrac{R\cap\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}

\displaystyle=\dim\dfrac{\Big(R\cap\gamma_{c+1}(F)\Big)/\gamma_{m+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)/\gamma_{m+1}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)/\gamma_{m+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)/\gamma_{m+1}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{m+1}(F)}-\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(R,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{m+1}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{m+1}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+m+1}(F)}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}l_{d}^{n}(c+k).

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
K
be an
n
-Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic zero, and also suppose that the algebra
L=\dfrac{K}{Z_{c}(K)}
satisfies in the stated conditions of Theorem
3.9
. Then

\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K)\leq l_{d}^{n}(c+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}d^{c(n-1)-i+1}.

Proof.

Let
F/R
and
S/R
be a free presentations of
K
and
Z_{c}(K)
, respectively, where
S
is an ideal of
F
. Therefore, according to Theorem
3.9
, we have

(3.31)

\displaystyle\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)=\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K/Z_{c}(K))=\dim\gamma_{c+1}(F/S)=\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)+S}{S}=\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}.

Hence

\displaystyle\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)=\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K/Z_{c}(K))

\displaystyle=\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(K)+Z_{c}(K)}{Z_{c}(K)}

(3.32)

\displaystyle=\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K)+\dim Z_{c}(K)-\dim Z_{c}(K)=\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K).

Therefore, by equations (
3.31
) and (
3.32
), we have

\displaystyle\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K)=\dim\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S}

\displaystyle=\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)/\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(F)\cap S/\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dim\left(\dfrac{\gamma_{c+1}(F)}{\gamma_{c+1}(S,F,\dots,F)}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(K/Z_{c}(K))+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(K/Z_{c}(K))

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)+\dim\gamma_{c+1}(L)

\displaystyle\leq l_{d}^{n}(c+1)+\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}d^{c(n-1)-i+1}.

∎

The following corollary determines the dimension of
c
-nilpotent multiplier of nilpotent
n
-Lie algebras of maximal class. We recall that a

-dimensional

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
L
be an
n
-Lie algebra of maximal nilpotency class

\dim\mathcal{M}^{(c)}(L)\leq l_{d-1}^{n}(c+1)+n^{c(n-1)}.

L

Exact Instability Radius of Discrete-Time LTI Systems
Chung-Yao Kao, Sei Zhen Khong, Shinji Hara, Yu-Jen Lin

Abstract
The robust instability of an unstable plant subject to stable perturbations is of significant importance and arises in the study of sustained oscillatory phenomena in nonlinear systems. This paper analyzes the robust instability of linear discrete-time systems against stable perturbations via the notion of robust instability radius (RIR) as a measure of instability. We determine the exact RIR for certain unstable systems using small-gain type conditions by formulating the problem in terms of a phase change rate maximization subject to appropriate constraints at unique peak-gain frequencies, for which stable first-order all-pass functions are shown to be optimal. Two real-world applications — minimum-effort sampled-data control of magnetic levitation systems and neural spike generations in the FitzHugh–Nagumo model subject to perturbations — are provided to illustrate the utility of our results.



keywords
robust instability analysis; minimum-norm strong stabilization; discrete-time LTI systems; magnetic levitation; neural dynamics.

Introduction

Robust stability analysis, which assesses systems stability in the face of uncertainty and/or parametric variation,
is fundamentally important in the study of control systems
[
1
]
. The small-gain theorem is a
classical robust stability result that quantifies the largest stable perturbation a stable system can tolerate
before becoming unstable. This number is referred to as the “robust stability radius”, which plays an essential
role in certain approaches to designing feedback mechanisms, such as the
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
control
[
2
]
.

An important counterpart of the robust stability analysis is the so-called “robust instability analysis” for nominally unstable systems.
Robust instability analysis considers the problem of quantifying the maximal stable perturbation that an unstable system can tolerate
before becoming stable. Compared to the robust stability problem which has been extensively studied and is well understood, research on
robust instability is not much less emphasized. However, the analysis does find important applications in, for example, feedback control
to maintain nonlinear oscillation under the influence of structured perturbations. Robustness of such non-equilibrium states is difficult to analyze
in general, and robust instability analysis (of linear systems) provides key insights for maintaining certain classes of nonlinear oscillations
as demonstrated in
[
3
,
4
]
, where robust oscillations in the sense of Yakubovich are guaranteed by instability and ultimate boundedness
[
5
]
.

Robust instability analysis is a much more difficult problem than robust stability, of which the challenges can be understood as follows. Unlike “robust stability radius”,
to characterize “robust instability radius” (RIR) one needs to find the smallest (stable) perturbation that moves not one but
all

unstable poles to the stable region of the complex plane. As such, the small-gain condition in terms of the
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-norm
is only sufficient but not necessary for the robust instability. Furthermore, the search for the worst case stable perturbation in robust
instability analysis provides a solution to the strong stabilization; i.e., stabilization by a stable controller
[
7
,
6
]
. In fact,
robust instability is equivalent to strong stabilization by a minimum-norm controller, which is a long-standing problem where challenges arise from its
non-convexity nature and the fact that no upper bound on the order of stable stabilizing controllers has been found. It is noteworthy that the recent work
[
8
]
examines the problem of robust instability from a different perspective involving the
\mathscr{L}_{2}
-gap.

Recently, the authors of
[
9
]
considered the robust instability problem for classes of linear time-invariant (LTI)
continuous-time systems which
have one or two
unstable poles and a single peak-gain frequency, and provided an almost complete solution in terms of the small-gain-type condition.
It was found that for the aforementioned classes of systems, the worst-case perturbations for a given unstable nominal plant are
those which
compensate the phase of the plant at the peak-gain frequency and make the loop’s “phase change rate” positive at that frequency.
The discovery led to the so-called “phase change rate (PCR) maximization” problem for finding conditions to characterize robustness
instability radius. It is shown in
[
9
]
that the solutions to the PCR maximization problem are provided by first-order all-pass functions.

As a complement to the work of
[
9
]
, this paper addresses the robust instability analysis of LTI discrete-time systems. In contrast to the
preliminary work
[
10
]
, which presents a concise framework for determining the exact RIR of discrete-time LTI systems, our objectives in this paper
are to provide detailed proofs and offer several remarks on the main results to highlight noteworthy aspects. Additionally, we describe practical applications in
neural dynamics and magnetic levitation to demonstrate how our findings offer valuable insights into real-world problems. The results in this paper are derived
purely in the discrete-time setting and the mathematical expressions of the conditions, as well as the derivations of these results,
are significantly distinct from their continuous-time counterparts. It is noteworthy that it is not viable to reproduce these results using the findings
in
[
9
]
by applying the bilinear transformation to discrete-time transfer functions. This is because the use of the bilinear transformation
generally gives rise to biproper continuous-time transfer functions, whereas only strictly proper continuous-time transfer functions were investigated
in
[
9
]
.

The remainder of this paper is arranged as follows. Notation and terminology are defined in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we present a necessary and sufficient condition for marginal stabilization of certain classes of LTI discrete-time systems. This is applied to derive conditions for such systems
to have exact RIR in Section
4
. Similar to the continuous-time setting, the proof of the main results in Section
4

relies on solving a phase change rate maximization problem, which is examined in Section
5
. Two examples demonstrating our results
are given in Section
6
and some concluding remarks are drawn in Section
7
.

2
Notation and Terminology

All systems considered in this paper are single-input-single-output, and therefore their frequency-domain representations are scalar transfer functions.
The unit circle in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
is denoted by
\mathbb{T}
, while the open (closed) unit disk is denoted by
\mathbb{D}
(
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
).
We use
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
to denote the set of all real rational functions that are bounded on
\mathbb{T}
. The subset of
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
consisting of functions that are analytical and uniformly bounded outside
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{RH}_{\infty}
. The norms in
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
and

\mathcal{RH}_{\infty}
are denoted by

\|\cdot\|_{L_{\infty}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H_{\infty}}
, respectively. Given
g\in\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
and
f\in\mathcal{RH}_{\infty}
, we define
\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}:=\sup_{\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]}|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|
and
\|f\|_{H_{\infty}}:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f(s)|
. It can be shown that

\|f\|_{H_{\infty}}=\sup_{\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]}|f(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|
.

It is well-known that any discrete time, finite dimensional, linear time invariant and stable system can be identified with a frequency-domain transfer function in
\mathcal{RH}_{\infty}
. Consider the class of unstable systems that identify with transfer functions
from the following set:
\mathcal{G}:=\{g\in\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}\,|\,g\,\text{ is proper and has poles outside $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$}\}
.
The robust instability radius for
g\in\mathcal{G}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{*}(g):=\inf_{\delta\in{\mathbb{S}}(g)}||\delta||_{H_{\infty}},

where
\mathbb{S}(g)
is the set of real-rational, proper, stable transfer functions that internally stabilize
g
. It is
well-known that
\rho_{*}(g)
is finite if and only if
g
satisfies the so-called parity interlacing property (PIP)
condition
[
11
]
, i.e., the number of unstable real poles between any pair of unstable real zeros is even, wherein strictly proper systems are considered to have one real unstable zero at infinity.
As such, the class of systems of our interest
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{n}:=\{g\in{\mathcal{G}}\,|\,g\,

has
n
unstable poles and

\displaystyle\text{ satisfies the PIP condition}\}.

It is known that the
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
-norm of
g
serves as a lower bound of
\rho_{*}(g)
; i.e.,
\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq\rho_{*}(g)
.
A system
g

is said to have “exact RIR” if
\rho_{*}(g)
is exactly equal to
\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}
. Our primary interest here is to derive
conditions on
g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
under which
g
has exact RIR.
To this end, let us introduce the following notion of
marginal stability
to facilitate a clear presentation of our results.

A discrete-time system with transfer function
g
is called
marginally stable
if all its poles are in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, and those on
\mathbb{T}
are simple. A marginally stable system
g
is called “
single-mode
” marginally stable if all its poles
are in
\mathbb{D}
except for a complex conjugate pair of poles on
\mathbb{T}
, one pole at
1
, or one pole at
-1
.

In the continuous-time setting, it is shown in
[
4
]
that, given an unstable system
g
, if
a stable system
\delta
has no unstable pole/zero cancellation with
g
, and the positive feedback system with loop transfer
function
\delta g
is single-mode marginally stable, then the
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
-norm of
\delta
provides an upper bound
for
\rho_{*}(g)
. The conclusion can be reached in the discrete-time setting via the bilinear transformation, which is known to preserve stability.
As such, in this paper the same technical mechanism via single-mode marginal stabilization is adopted for obtaining
conditions for “exact RIR”.

3
Marginal Stabilization of Discrete-Time LTI Systems with a Single Peak-gain Frequency

An extended version of the Nyquist criterion for characterizing single-mode marginal stability of discrete-time LTI systems is
presented in this section. It will be used for deriving stabilizing control synthesis conditions and conditions for characterizing systems with
exact RIR. The results presented here are the discrete-time counterparts to those in
[
9
]
. To this end, let us first introduce the concept
of magnitude and phase change rates for an
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
function.

Let
L
be a complex function from the class
\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
. The logarithmic gain and the phase of
L
at a complex number
z

such that
L(z)\not=0
are denoted by
\ln|L(z)|
and
\angle L(z)
, respectively. Note that
\angle L(z)
should be chosen so that
\omega\mapsto\angle L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
is continuous on
(-\pi,\pi]
, except where
L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})=0
. Denote the logarithmic gain and the phase of the frequency
response
L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
by

\displaystyle A_{L}(\omega):=\ln|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|\,,\quad\theta_{L}(\omega):=\angle L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega}).

Define the gain and the phase change rate of
L
w.r.t.
\omega
as

\displaystyle A_{L}^{\prime}(\omega):=\frac{d}{d\omega}\ln|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|\,,\quad\theta_{L}^{\prime}(\omega):=\frac{d}{d\omega}\angle L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega}).

For
L\in\mathcal{RL}_{\infty}
with no zeros on
\mathbb{T}
,
\log L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
is well-defined as
A_{L}(\omega)+j\cdot\theta_{L}(\omega)
.
Therefore,
A_{L}^{\prime}(\omega)
and
\theta_{L}^{\prime}(\omega)
can be found by computing the real and imaginary parts of

\frac{d}{d\omega}\log L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
, respectively.

3.1
Criterion for marginal stability

We provide conditions for marginal stability of a given positive feedback
system with an unstable loop transfer function
L
.
To facilitate the development, let us define the following quantities. Consider
the Nyquist plot of
\displaystyle\left.L(z^{-1})\right|_{z=(1-\epsilon)\mathrm{e}^{j\omega}}
, where
\epsilon\geq 0

and
\omega
ranges from
-\pi
to
\pi
.
Define
\nu_{+}(\epsilon)
as the number of transverse crossing points on the real semi-interval
(1,\infty)

from the negative imaginary region to the positive one by the Nyquist plot, and similarly
\nu_{-}(\epsilon)

from positive to negative. Let
\nu_{o}(\epsilon):=\nu_{+}(\epsilon)-\nu_{-}(\epsilon)
.

The following lemma states the discrete-time counterpart of Lemma 4 of
[
9
]
, which provides a necessary and
sufficient condition for marginal stability. Note that the statement, which makes use of the modified Nyquist contour

\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}
is
\{z~|~z=(1-\epsilon)\mathrm{e}^{j\omega},\ \omega\in[-\pi,\pi],\epsilon\geq 0\}
, is essentially an extended version of the classical
Nyquist criterion equipped with the standard Nyquist contour
\mathcal{N}_{0^{-}}
.
Notice the orientation of
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}
is counter-clockwise.

Lemma 1
.

Consider a positive feedback discrete-time system with loop transfer function
L\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. The feedback system has all poles in

\overline{\mathbb{D}}
if and only if there exists an
\epsilon_{+}>0
such that the following two equivalent conditions hold for all

\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{+})
.

(1)

The number of clockwise encirclements of the Nyquist plot of

\displaystyle\left.L(z^{-1})\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}}
about
1+j0

is equal to
n
, the number of unstable poles of
L
.

(2)

\nu_{o}(\epsilon)=-n
for the Nyquist plot of
\displaystyle\left.L(z^{-1})\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}}

Moreover, the feedback system is marginally stable if and only if the following two conditions hold in addition
to condition (1) or (2): (3-a)
\exists\,\omega
such that
L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})=1
; (3-b) If
L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})=1
, then

\left.\frac{d}{dz}L(z)\right.|_{z=\mathrm{e}^{j\omega}}\not=0
.

PROOF.

First, we note that one can readily verify that
\nu_{o}(\epsilon)=-n
means exactly that the
number of clockwise encirclements of the Nyquist plot of
\displaystyle\left.L(z^{-1})\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}}
about

1+j0
is equal to
n
.

A proof for condition (1) can be derived from the classical Nyquist criterion. Let
\tilde{L}(z):=L(z^{-1})

and note that an unstable pole of
L
gives a pole of
\tilde{L}
in
\mathbb{D}
.
Moreover, let

G(z):=\frac{L(z)}{1-L(z)},\quad\tilde{G}(z):=\frac{\tilde{L}(z)}{1-\tilde{L}(z)}.

Clearly
\tilde{G}(z)=G(z^{-1})
, and therefore
G
has all poles in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
if and only if
\tilde{G}

has no pole in
\mathbb{D}
. To show the latter, note that the Nyquist contour
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}
is a counter-clockwise
oriented circle with radius smaller than and arbitrarily close to 1 as
\epsilon\to 0
. Condition (1) implies that
the number of counter-clockwise encirclements of the Nyquist plot of

\displaystyle\left.\tilde{L}(z)\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}}
about
1+j0
is
-n
, matching the number of the
poles of
\tilde{L}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, by the Nyquist criterion, the closed-loop system
\tilde{G}
has no poles in
\mathbb{D}
,
which in turn implies that all poles of
G
are in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Finally, the additional conditions (3-a) and (3-b)
is equivalent to that
G
has poles on
\mathbb{T}
and that those poles are simple. By definition this means
G
is marginally
stable.

Remark 2
.

Note that condition (1) is equivalent to that the number of
counter-clockwise
encirclements of the Nyquist plot of

\displaystyle\left.L(z)\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{+}}}
about
1+j0
is equal to
n
, where the Nyquist contour

\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{+}}
is defined exactly the same as
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon^{-}}
except that
(1-\epsilon)
is
replaced by
(1+\epsilon)
. Proposition
3
below is derived using the equivalent condition.

Built upon Lemma
1
, the following proposition gives a necessary and sufficient condition for single-mode marginal stability.

Proposition 3
.

Consider a positive feedback system with loop transfer function
L\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
, where
n
is a positive integer. Suppose that

\omega_{c}\in[0,\pi]
is given such that
A_{L}^{\prime}(\omega_{c})=0
. The feedback system is single mode marginally stable if and only if condition (i)
and one of conditions (ii-a) and (ii-b), indicated below, are satisfied.

(i)

L
satisfies the followings:

\displaystyle L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{c}})=1,\quad\left.\frac{d}{dz}L(z)\right|_{z=\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{c}}}\neq 0

\displaystyle L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{c}})\neq 1,\quad\forall\omega\neq\pm\omega_{c}.

(ii-a)

The Nyquist plot of
\left.L(z)\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{0}}
satisfies either
\nu_{o}(0)=n-1
if
\omega_{c}\in\{0,\pi\}
or

\nu_{o}(0)=n-2
if
\omega_{c}\notin\{0,\pi\}
, and
\theta_{L}^{\prime}(\omega_{c})>0
.

(ii-b)

The Nyquist plot of
\left.L(z)\right|_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{0}}
satisfies
\nu_{o}(0)=n
and
\theta_{L}^{\prime}(\omega_{c})<0
.

PROOF.

The result may be established using similar arguments to those of Proposition 2 in
[
9
]
.

4
Main Results

In this section, we exploit Proposition
3
to obtain conditions for (single mode) marginal
stabilizability of certain classes of unstable discrete-time LTI systems. They are in turn used to derive conditions
for those systems to have exact RIR. Specifically, we consider the following subsets of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Given
\omega_{*}\in[0,\pi]
, define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{\omega_{*}}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}~|~\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}=|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{*}})|>|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|,

\displaystyle\hskip 106.69783pt\forall\,\omega\in[0,\pi],\ \omega\not=\omega_{*}\},

{\mathcal{G}}^{0,\pi}_{n}:={\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{n}\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\pi}_{n}
and

{\mathcal{G}}^{\#}_{n}:=\bigcup_{\omega_{*}\in(0,\pi)}{\mathcal{G}}^{\omega_{*}}_{n}
.
These subsets contain functions from
\mathcal{G}_{n}
which have an unique peak gain frequency. The assumption on the
unique peak gain frequency is not unreasonable from the practical point of view; furthermore, extending our results to cover
systems with multiple peak gain frequencies is possible in a similar vein to the continuous-time counterpart in
[
9
]
.

4.1
Conditions for marginal stability

The following theorem provides necessary and sufficient conditions for marginal stabilization
of system
g
in
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}^{0,\pi}
or
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}^{\#}
, by some system
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H_{\infty}}=1/\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}
. Furthermore, the unique-peak-gain property of
g

implies that the marginal stability is of single mode.
As
1/\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}
is a lower bound on the RIR of
g
, single mode
marginal stabilization of
g
by such
f
implies that
g
has the exact RIR equal to
1/\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}
,
as explained at the end of Section
2
.

Theorem 4
.

The following hold on the classes
{\mathcal{G}}^{0,\pi}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{\#}_{n}
:

(I)

Let
g\in{\mathcal{G}}^{0,\pi}_{n}
whose peak gain frequency
\omega_{p}
is either
0
or
\pi
.
Then
g
can be marginally stabilized by a stable system
f
with
||f||_{H_{\infty}}=1/||g||_{L_{\infty}}=1/|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})|

if and only if
n=1
and

\displaystyle\theta_{g}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})>0\ .

(1)

(II)

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\#}
whose peak gain occurs at
\omega_{p}
. Then
g
can be marginally stabilized by
a stable system
f
with
||f||_{H_{\infty}}=1/||g||_{L_{\infty}}=1/|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})|
if and only
if
n=2
and

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{g}(\omega_{p})>|\sin(\theta_{g}(\omega_{p}))/\sin\omega_{p}|.

(2)

PROOF.

The proof follows arguments similar to those of Theorem 1 of
[
9
]
. Here we briefly explain the underlying ideas but handle the differences with care. The sufficiency is proven by explicitly identifying
f
which marginally stabilizes
g

and applying Proposition
3
. For
g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{0,\pi}
, or
g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\#}
such that

\sin(\theta_{g}(\omega_{p}))=0
, the marginally stabilizing
f
is a real constant whose magnitude equals to
1/\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}
. For

g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\#}
such that
\sin(\theta_{g}(\omega_{p}))\not=0
,
f
is a first-order all-pass function of the form

\frac{1}{\|g\|_{L_{\infty}}}\left(\frac{az+1}{z+a}\right)
, where
|a|<1
is chosen so that
L(z):=g(z)f(z)
is equal
to
1
at

z=\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}}
. By these choices of
f
, one can verify that the corresponding loop-gain
L
satisfies
conditions (i) and (ii-a)
of Proposition
3
. Therefore, the positive feedback system with loop-gain
L:=gf
is single mode
marginally stable.

For necessity, the arguments go as follows. Assume that there exists
f
with

||f||_{H_{\infty}}=1/||g||_{L_{\infty}}=1/|g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})|

such that the the positive feedback system with loop-gain
L:=gf
is marginally stable. Note that in this case,
|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|

is less than or equal to
1
for all
\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]
. This property in turn implies that the marginal stability
must be of single mode, and
\nu_{o}(0)
must be equal
to
0
. Thus, conditions (i) and (ii-a) of Proposition
3
must hold, and we have
n=1
if peak gain
of
g
occurs at
\omega_{p}=0

or
\pi
, and
n=2
otherwise. Moreover, the condition
\theta_{L}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})>0
implies

\theta_{g}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})>-\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})
, and
the phase change rate conditions stated in (
1
) and (
2
) emerge as
the infimum of
-\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})
, or equivalently, the supremum of
\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})
over the following
set of constraints:
f\in\mathcal{RH}_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})f(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})=1,\quad\text{and}\\
&|f(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|\leq|f(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})|=1/\|g\|_{L_{\infty}},\ \ \forall\omega\in[0,\pi].\end{split}

(3)

We refer to such problem as “phase change rate maximization” problem, of which the solution is presented in Section
5
.

The following remarks provide further insights about Theorem
4
.

Remark 5
.

We note important differences between the discrete-time result and its continuous-time counterpart. In
the discrete-time setting, we allow for the scenario where the peak-gain frequency
\omega_{p}=\pi
;
the corresponding continuous-time frequency would be
\omega_{p}=\infty
, which was not considered in
[
9
]
.
In particular, the continuous-time setting in
[
9
]
requires the system be strictly proper; as such,
\omega_{p}

is always finite. Here, no such restriction on properness of the transfer function under study is imposed. Moreover, for
continuous-time systems in
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\#}
, the corresponding
inequality (
2
) was found to be
\theta^{\prime}_{g}(\omega_{p})>|\sin(\theta_{g}(\omega_{p}))/\omega_{p}|
—
the denominator is
\omega_{p}
instead of
\sin\omega_{p}
. This important difference can only be identified following arduous mathematical derivations performed in Section
5
.

Remark 6
.

For
g\in{\mathcal{G}}^{0,\pi}_{n}
or
{\mathcal{G}}^{\#}_{n}
with
n\geq 3
, it is still possible to find a stable
f

with
||f||_{H_{\infty}}=1/||g||_{L_{\infty}}
such that all closed-loop poles are on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. However,
the norm constraint on
f
implies that the loop transfer function
L
has a single peak at
\omega_{p}
, which in turn
implies that the closed-loop poles on
\mathbb{T}
must be located at
\pm\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}}
,
1
(when
\omega_{p}=0
),
or
-1
(when
\omega_{p}=\pi
). As such, the multiplicity of these poles must be larger than one, i.e. marginal stability does not hold.

4.2
Conditions for exact RIR

We obtain here conditions
for a discrete-time system
g\in\mathcal{G}_{1}^{0,\pi}
or
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\#}
to have exact RIR based on Theorem
4
.
Moreover, we also show that systems from the class of
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\#}
do not have exact RIR.

Lemma 7
.

Given
\omega_{p}\in[0,\pi]
, integer
n\geq 1
, and
L\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
,
consider the positive feedback system with loop transfer function
L
satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle\hskip-21.33955pt1=\|L\|_{L_{\infty}}=|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega_{p}})|>|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|\,,\ \forall\omega\in[0,\pi]\backslash\{\omega_{p}\}.

(4)

If the feedback system has all its poles in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then
\theta^{\prime}_{L}(\omega_{p})\geq 0
.

PROOF.

The arguments for proving Lemma
7
are identical to those of its continuous-time
counterpart
[
9
, Lemma 5]
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and suppose
g(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
has a unique peak gain at
\omega_{p}
. Consider the exact RIR condition

Conclusions

In this paper, we established necessary and sufficient conditions for certain classes of unstable discrete-time
plants to have the exact robust instability radius (RIR). These conditions are acquired via solving
a phase change rate maximization problem, the solutions of which also lead to the minimum stable perturbation that destroys
instability of an unstable plant, or from another point of view, the minimum stable control that stabilizes the plant.
We demonstrated the utility of our results by two practical examples, including the strong stabilization of a magnetic levitation system
and robust analysis of oscillatory behaviors in neural dynamics.
The results in this paper provide the first step towards a complete theory of robust instability analysis.
Extending the results to more general classes of unstable networked systems is currently being pursued.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(A_{f}(\omega)-A_{f}(\omega_{p}))(1-\cos\omega\cos\omega_{p})}{(\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p})^{2}}d\omega

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|A_{f}(\omega)-A_{f}(\omega_{p})|(1-\cos\omega\cos\omega_{p})}{(\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p})^{2}}d\omega

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\frac{A_{f}(\omega)-A_{f}(\omega_{p})}{\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p}}\right|\left|\frac{1-\cos\omega\cos\omega_{p}}{\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p}}\right|d\omega,

where we used the fact that
A_{f}(\omega)\leq A_{f}(\omega_{p})
for all
\omega\in[0,\pi]
and the other terms are all positive to obtain the second equality. By the above expression of
\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega)
, it is clear that
\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega)\leq 0
regardless of the value of

\omega_{p}
. Now, given
\omega_{p}\notin\{0,\pm\pi\}
, since
|1-\cos\omega\cos\omega_{p}|/|\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p}|\geq 1
for all
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91684pt|\sin\omega_{p}|\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})\leq-

\displaystyle\frac{|\sin\omega_{p}|}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\frac{A_{f}(\omega)-A_{f}(\omega_{p})}{\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p}}\right|d\omega

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq-\frac{|\sin\omega_{p}|}{\pi}\left|\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{A_{f}(\omega)-A_{f}(\omega_{p})}{\cos\omega-\cos\omega_{p}}d\omega\right|=-|\theta_{f}(\omega_{p})|,

and thus

\displaystyle\theta_{f}^{\prime}(\omega_{p})\leq-\left|\frac{\theta_{f}(\omega_{p})}{\sin\omega_{p}}\right|.

This concludes the proof.

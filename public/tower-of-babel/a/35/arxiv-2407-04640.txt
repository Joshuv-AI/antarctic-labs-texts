Global Spectral Gap in Bosonic Molecular Hamiltonians
Sebastian Gherghe 1

Abstract
. We consider a neutral bosonic molecule in the Born-Oppenheimer approximation without spin and assume the physically obvious assertion that a neutral molecule prefers to break into smaller neutral clusters. We prove the existence of a global in space/uniform spectral gap between the ground state and first excited state energies. To do so, we improve upon previous results using a different tool, the time-independent Feshbach-Schur map.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider a neutral molecule in the Born-Oppenheimer approximation and investigate the presence of a global in space/uniform spectral gap between the ground state energy and the rest of the spectrum. Consider a molecule consisting of
N
spinless electrons of mass
m_{e}
and charges
-e
and
M
nuclei of masses
m_{j}
and charges
+eZ_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,M
. Its Schrödinger operator is given by

(1.1)

H_{N,M}(y)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{j}}+V_{e}(x)+V_{en}(x,y)+V_{n}(y),

acting on either
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
or a symmetry subspace, and

(1.2)

\displaystyle V_{e}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}\frac{e^{2}}{\lvert{x_{i}-x_{j}}\rvert},

(1.3)

\displaystyle V_{en}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{e^{2}Z_{j}}{\lvert{x_{i}-y_{j}}\rvert},

(1.4)

\displaystyle V_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant M}\frac{e^{2}Z_{i}Z_{j}}{\lvert{y_{i}-y_{j}}\rvert},

are the Coulomb interaction potentials of electron-electron, electron-nuclei, and nuclei-nuclei, respectively. The operator
H_{N,M}(y)
is parameterized by
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}
with
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
for
i\neq j
, the positions of the nuclei, and the positions of electrons are given by the

coordinates. We denote by
Z=\sum_{j=1}^{M}Z_{j}
the total nuclear charge, and for a neutral molecule, we have

(1.5)

N=Z.

Let
E_{0}(y)
denote the ground state energy and
E_{1}(y)
the infimum of the rest of the spectrum,

(1.6)

\displaystyle E_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lVert{\phi}\rVert=1\\
\phi\in{\mathcal{D}}(H_{N,M}(y))\end{subarray}}\langle\phi,H_{N,M}(y)\phi\rangle,

(1.7)

\displaystyle E_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\left(\sigma(H_{N,M}(y))\setminus\{E_{0}(y)\}\right).

We are interested for which
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3M}
the uniform
spectral gap condition
holds,

(1.8)

E_{1}(y)-E_{0}(y)\geqslant\delta>0,\quad\delta>0\text{ independent of }y\in\mathcal{A}.

By the HVZ Theorem, the existence of such a spectral gap implies that
E_{0}(y)
is an isolated eigenvalue, below the essential spectrum of
H_{N,M}(y)
. In particular, this indicates that the system is stable with respect to breaking up into smaller clusters, known as
stability with respect to break up
.

We briefly discuss the challenges posed by problem (
1.8
). For bosonic systems or systems without particle statistics, it is well-known that
E_{0}(y)
is non-degenerate (see for example
[
25
, Theorem XIII.46]
), ensuring the existence of a spectral gap (
1.8
) on bounded open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{3M}
. However, this does not exclude the possibility of the gap closing as
\left|y\right|\to\infty
. Consequently, we must consider the system’s break-up limits into configurations of smaller clusters, which may be neutral or ionic. This is the first point of difficulty and will involve precise notation and set-up. The physically intuitive assertion that neutral systems prefer neutral clusters upon separation remains an open conjecture in mathematical quantum mechanics (see
[
3
]
and references therein), which we adopt here as an assumption.

Assumption [LN]
.

(Local neutrality.) A neutral molecule that breaks up into groups of smaller molecules which recede from each other does so in a manner that keeps each smaller system neutral.

To analyze the gap in such limits, we must determine the asymptotics of both
E_{0}(y)
and
E_{1}(y)
as
\left|y\right|\to\infty
. While the asymptotics for the ground state energy
E_{0}(y)
are relatively straightforward, those for
E_{1}(y)
require consideration of both the first excited states of neutral clusters and the ground state energies of ions. This adds complexity, as the space of candidate states is large and must be examined carefully.

The
Local neutrality
conjecture is partly motivated by the van der Waals force, a quantum effect that arises between two neutral molecules at a certain distance
1
1

1

Quantum correlations between intermolecular forces and spins induce an attractive force that decays as the inverse sixth power of the distance between the molecules.
. The existence of this force was rigorously proven in
[
18
,
20
]
, with its exact form under additional non-degeneracy conditions derived in
[
3
]
. The van der Waals force implies that all neutral molecules can bind in the Born-Oppenheimer approximation, meaning the ground state energy has a global minimum with respect to nuclear positions. For any molecular split, the energy remains higher than the lowest possible energy due to this force. While the minimizer is non-unique (due to translational symmetry), these minima, corresponding to molecular shapes (see for example
[
20
]
), are fundamental in quantum chemistry .

Our main theorem can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a neutral molecule of interacting bosons with two or more nuclei (
M\geqslant 2
) and assuming Assumption
[LN]
holds, the gap condition (
1.8
) holds with
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}^{3M}
.

Remark
.

While electrons are fermions, nuclei can be either fermions or bosons

2

Nuclei of different charges are different species, in the sense that they do not obey any particle statistics upon exchange.

Remark
.

For fully fermionic systems, the ground state energy is generally degenerate, breaking the spectral gap. Our main result applies to molecules with fermionic electrons if non-degeneracy of the ground state energy is assumed for both the system and any subsystem. In such cases, the proof follows similarly, with antisymmetric wavefunctions in place of symmetric ones. Since this assumption is strong and rarely satisfied, even in simple cases like hydrogen gas
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Without a non-degeneracy condition, a global spectral gap for fermionic systems is unlikely, as these systems typically exhibit eigenvalue crossings, where eigenvalues of

may intersect or merge into the continuous spectrum. Avoided crossings, where eigenvalues come very close but remain distinct, also challenge the adiabatic theory. Figure

The presence of eigenvalue crossings complicates theories like time-dependent BO and limits their application to chemically relevant cases. A systematic framework characterizing when and where such crossings occur is non-existent. The first rigorous study of molecular Hamiltonian eigenvalue crossings was by Hagedorn

To conclude this introduction we briefly sketch our approach. The total symmetry of the eigenstates and non-degeneracy of the ground state energy are both essential ingredients for the proof. Assume there exists a sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3M}
such that

(1.9)

E_{1}(y^{n})-E_{0}(y^{n})\to 0.

If
\max_{i\neq j}\left|y_{i}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}\right|
is a bounded sequence, then, after translating so that
y_{1}^{n}=0
,
\{y^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
must be a bounded sequence. Hence there must exist a convergent subsequence
\{y^{n_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
y^{n_{k}}\to y^{0}
, implying
E(y^{0})=E_{1}(y^{0})
and contradicting the non-degeneracy of the ground state.
Observe that translations in the
y
-coordinates only affect
H_{N,M}(y)
via the nuclear-nuclear interaction
V_{N}(y)
, and shift the spectrum
\sigma(H_{N,M}(y))
by a constant and do not affect the size of the gap. By translating
y_{1}^{n}
to
0
and considering
\max_{i\neq j}\{\left|y_{i}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}\right|\}
we account for rigid translations of the system (we can also work in center-of-mass coordinates).

If
\max_{i\neq j}\{\left|y_{i}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}\right|\}
is unbounded, we consider a decomposition of the system into non-interacting clusters. The nuclei partition into at least two smaller subgroups which recede from each other, denote this partition of nuclear coordinates by
\mathcal{N}
. Then, the electrons must choose how to partition themselves amongst these subgroups, so we denote the set of all these possible electronic partitions by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

For a given electronic partition
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}
, we may decompose the operator
H_{N,M}(y^{n})
as

(1.10)

H_{N,M}(y^{n})=H_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})+I_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n}),

where
H_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})
represents the free Hamiltonian of the decomposed system (clusters with intracluster interactions) and
I_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})
the intercluster interactions. Intuitively, we expect
I_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})=O(1/R_{n})
, where
R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
is the minimum distance between separate clusters. With (
1.10
) in mind, we expect the eigenvalues of
H_{N,M}(y^{n})
to approach those of
H_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})
as
n\to\infty
.

To formalize this, define the new Hilbert space

(1.11)

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\bigotimes_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})

and let

(1.12)

H_{\infty}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}}H_{\mathcal{E}},

where the individual clusters are assumed to reach some sort of fixed configuration in the limit. Let
E_{k}(H_{\infty})
denote the
k^{th}
eigenvalue of
H_{\infty}
(counting multiplicity), and by
k=\infty
denote the bottom of the essential spectrum. To list
E_{k}(H_{\infty})
, one can list all of
E_{k}(H_{\mathcal{E}})
for each decomposition
\mathcal{E}
and then combine them into one list, counting multiplicity. Then, we prove that

(1.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{0}(H_{N,M}(y^{n}))=E_{0}(H_{\infty}),\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{1}(H_{N,M}(y^{n}))=E_{1}(H_{\infty}).

In particular, consider the system breaking into
k\geqslant 2
clusters. For the
j^{\text{th}}
cluster, denote by
E_{j,0}
the neutral cluster ground state energy,
E_{j,1}
the neutral cluster first excited state energy, and
E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}
the ground state energy of the
\delta_{j}
ion (i.e. the system having lost or gained
\left|\delta_{j}\right|
electrons depending if
\delta_{j}>0
or
\delta_{j}<0
, respectively). Then, we also show that

(1.14)

\displaystyle E_{0}(H_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{j,0}(y^{0}),

(1.15)

\displaystyle E_{1}(H_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\min_{l,\>\delta_{j}}\left\{E_{j,l}(y^{0})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{k}E_{j,0}(y^{0}),\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}(y^{0})\right\}.

Identity (
1.14
) follows from Assumption
[LN]
. (
1.15
) establishes that the first excited state energy may come from either a configuration of neutral clusters, with all clusters but one being in their ground states and the one cluster in the first excited state, or some configuration of ions (clusters with positive or negative charge) in their ground states. The ion charges
\delta_{j}
in the minimum in (
1.15
) are subject to natural constraints (such as conservation of the number of electrons).

In
[
18
]
, Morgan and Simon established (
1.13
) for all eigenvalues
E_{k}(H_{\infty})
,
k\geqslant 1
, including
k=\infty
, without assuming
[LN]
. Their analysis, originally for two atoms, generalizes easily to multiple atoms. Here, we explicitly characterize
E_{0}(H_{\infty})
and
E_{1}(H_{\infty})
in (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), using the time-independent FS map (see Appendix
A
) and Assumption
[LN]
to define candidate states for eigenstates of the decomposed system.

The FS map, introduced by Feshbach in
[
10
]
for perturbation problems in nuclear reactions, presents an effective Hamiltonian as a Schur complement
[
26
]
. The FS map was applied to eigenvalue perturbation problems in
[
5
]
, with further refinements in
[
9
]
. The FS map also featured in the the study of the van der Waals force in
[
3
]
.

The main advantage of the time-independent FS map is that it transforms the eigenvalue problem of the operator in question,
H_{N,M}(y)
(which is acting on an infinite dimensional Hilbert space) into an eigenvalue problem for
F_{P}(\lambda)
acting on
{\rm{Ran\,}}P
(which is a lower dimensional space), where
F_{P}(\cdot)
is the FS map,
P
is an orthogonal projection of finite rank, and
\lambda
is a spectral parameter. In particular, we choose
P
to be the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspaces corresponding to
E_{0}(H_{\infty})
and
E_{1}(H_{\infty})
(after some consideration and making use of Assumption
[LN]
). Let
P^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement. After demonstrating that
P^{\perp}\left(H_{N,M}(y^{n})-E_{1}(y^{n})\right)P^{\perp}
is invertible, the FS method states that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
H_{N,M}(y)
if and only if it is also an eigenvalue of

(1.16)

\displaystyle F_{P}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=PH_{N,M}(y)P-PH_{N,M}(y)P^{\perp}(H_{N,M}^{\perp}(y)-\lambda)^{-1}P^{\perp}H_{N,M}(y)P,

acting on the finite dimensional subspace
\textrm{Ran }P
. Hence, the eigenvalue problem

(1.17)

H_{N,M}(y)\psi=\lambda(y)\psi,

is mapped into the equivalent eigenvalue problem

(1.18)

F_{P}(\lambda(y))\phi=\lambda(y)\phi,

where the latter is lower dimensional and in particular, finite dimensional due to the rank of
P
. In turn, the price paid is that the problem is non-linear (in
\lambda
). Examining the problem (
1.18
) for
\lambda(y)=E_{0}(y)
and
\lambda(y)=E_{1}(y)
, we obtain expansions for these quantities around
E_{0}(H_{\infty})
and
E_{1}(H_{\infty})
respectively, and demonstrate that the errors are
o(1)
in the limit (
1.13
). Since
E_{0}(H_{\infty})
must be non-degenerate also, then
E_{1}(y^{n})-E_{0}(y^{n})>0
in the limit
n\to\infty
.

2.1.
The Schrödinger Hamiltonian for a molecule

We consider a neutral molecule as described by the Hamiltonian
H_{N,M}(y)

Recall we denote by

Theorem 2.1

(a) (Hunziker-van Winter-Zhislin) We have

\displaystyle E_{0}(y)=\langle\psi_{0}(y),H_{N,M}(y)\psi_{0}(y)\rangle\leqslant\langle\psi_{0}(y+h),H_{N,M}(y)\psi_{0}(y+h)\rangle.

Similarly for

\displaystyle E_{0}(y+h)

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{0}(y+h),H_{N,M}(y+h)\psi_{0}(y+h)\rangle\leqslant\langle\psi_{0}(y),H_{N,M}(y+h)\psi_{0}(y)\rangle.

b) We have that

2.2.
Cluster decompositions

In general, a breakup of a molecule into smaller molecules or atoms is denoted by a cluster decomposition

(2.13)

\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}:=\big\{\{\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1}\},\dots,\{\mathcal{N}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k}\}\big\},

where
\mathcal{N}=\{\mathcal{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{k}\}
is a partition of the nuclear coordinates
\{1,\dots,M\}
into disjoint subsets such that
\sqcup_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{N}_{j}=\{1,\dots,M\}
and
\mathcal{E}=\{\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{k}\}
is a partition of the electron coordinates
\{1,\dots,N\}
into disjoint subsets such that
\sqcup_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{j}=\{1,\dots,N\}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of all possible cluster decompositions
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}
. Intuitively, as the system breaks into clusters, electrons must choose a nuclei to stay with. Then, let

(2.14)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}=\big\{\mathcal{E}=\{\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{k}\}\>|\>\textrm{ such that }\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}\big\},

i.e.
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}

(1)

(a)

If the subsequence

\mathcal{N}_{2}=\{3\}
.

(2)

Else,
\lvert{y^{n}_{2}}\rvert\to\infty

1
and
2

(a)

If

3

2

(b)

2.3.
Local neutrality

As described in Section
1
, in
[
18
]
Morgan and Simon proved that in the decomposition limit the energy of the molecule becomes the sum of the energies of the sub-systems. For the ground state we may write

(2.22)

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{0}(H_{N,M}(y^{n}))=\min_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{0}(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}(Y^{0}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}))\right\},

where recall
Y^{0}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}
is the limit of the
y
-coordinates in cluster center of mass coordinates.

It is a famous conjecture that the minimum on the right-hand side of (
2.22
) is attained only for the neutral configurations, i.e. the
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}
for which
\lvert\mathcal{E}_{j}\lvert=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}Z_{i}
(in everyday experience, most objects encountered are neutral). We assume this to be true in the Assumption
[LNc]
.

Assumption [LNc]
.

(Local neutrality for clusters.) Given a cluster decomposition of the nuclei, the electrons divide themselves amongst the clusters so that each cluster is neutral,

(2.23)

\left|\mathcal{E}_{j}\right|=\tilde{Z}_{j}\quad\forall j=1,\dots,k,

i.e. given a partition of the nuclei
\mathcal{N}
,

(2.24)

\min_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{0}(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}})\right\}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{0}(H_{\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{j}}),

where the minimizing partition
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is only attained by neutral clusters. (The minimizer is not unique, as any minimizer remains a minimizer under any permutation of electrons that preserves the clusters.)

It was established by in
[
20
]
that all neutral molecules can bind in the BO approximation, meaning that there exists a minimum of the ground state energy
E_{0}(y)
with respect to the nuclear positions.

It was shown in
[
3
,
1
]
that Assumption
[LNc]
holds for a system of hydrogen atoms, see the discussions surrounding property (E) contained in
[
3
, Appendix A]
and
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
. For other systems of atoms (and molecules), it is an open problem if local neutrality holds.

2.4.
Particle statistics

None of the results mentioned in the two previous subsections depend on the statistics of particles, or on spin. However, as we remark at the end of this section, the particle statistics play a very important role in the spectral gap result.

Let
S_{N}
denote the set of permutations of the coordinates
\{1,\dots,N\}
and let
\pi
be an element of
S_{N}
. Let
T_{\pi}
be a unitary representation of
S_{N}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
, defined through

(2.25)

(T_{\pi}\psi)(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\psi(x_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\dots,x_{\pi^{-1}(N)}).

For
\pi\in S
we denote by
\lvert{\pi}\rvert
the number of transpositions making up the permutation
\pi
, so that
(-1)^{\lvert{\pi}\rvert}
is the parity of
\pi\in S_{n}
. Then we denote the subspaces of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
for bosons (totally symmetric wavefunctions) and fermions (totally anti-symmetric wavefunctions) respectively as

(2.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})\>:\>T_{\pi}\psi=\psi\>\>\>\forall\pi\in S_{N}\},

(2.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{a}

\displaystyle=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})\>:\>T_{\pi}\psi=(-1)^{\lvert{\pi}\rvert}\psi\>\>\>\forall\pi\in S_{N}\}.

Futhermore, let
S
denote the symmetrizer and
A
the anti-symmetrizer respectively,

(2.28)

S=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{N}}T_{\pi},\qquad A=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{N}}(-1)^{\lvert{\pi}\rvert}T_{\pi}.

Recall that
S
and
A
are orthogonal projections on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
, i.e.
S^{2}=S
,
A^{2}=A
, and
SA=0=AS
.

We will denote by
H^{S}_{N,M}
or
H^{A}_{N,M}
the operator
H_{N,M}
acting on the corresponding symmetry subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
or
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
respectively. More specifically,

(2.29)

H^{S}_{N,M}(y)=SH_{N,M}(y)S|_{\textrm{Ran }S},\qquad H^{A}_{N,M}(y)=AH_{N,M}(y)A|_{\textrm{Ran }A}.

In general
\sigma(H^{S}_{N,M}(y))\neq\sigma(H_{N,M}(y))
as operators acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
, but the two operators have the same ground state as the ground state energy of
H_{N,M}(y)
is non-degenerate, positive, and
[S,H_{N,M}]=0
. (Hence if
H_{N,M}\psi=\lambda\psi
then
H_{N,M}S\psi=\lambda S\psi
. As
\psi>0
, then
S\psi>0
. Therefore
S\psi
must also be an eigenfunction and uniqueness holds, see
[
25
]
.) Moreover, the symmetry ensures that if the system decomposes into neutral non-interacting clusters, the ground state of the decomposed system is still non-degenerate. This is a key point in the proof of the main result and we illustrate it with the simple case of the gas
H_{2}
.

Example 1
.

Consider two hydrogen atoms consisting of two hydrogen nuclei at positions
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
and let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
denote the position coordinates of the electrons. We consider the simplest possible limit by changing coordinates, so that
y_{1}=0
and fixing a unit vector
\hat{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, so that
y_{2}=R\hat{e}
. We consider the limit as
R\to\infty
.

The Hamiltonian of this system can be written as

(2.30)

\displaystyle H(R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{1}}-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{2}}+\frac{e^{2}}{\lvert{x_{1}-x_{2}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{1}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{2}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{1}-R\hat{e}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{2}-R\hat{e}}\rvert}.

Consider, without loss of generality, the nuclear partition
\mathcal{N}=\{\{1\},\{2\}\}
and the electron partitions
\mathcal{E}=\{\{1\},\{2\}\}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{\{2\},\{1\}\}
. Then, for the cluster decomposition
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}
, we have

(2.31)

H_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{1}}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{1}}\rvert},\qquad H_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}=-\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{2}}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{2}-R\hat{e}}\rvert},

and

(2.32)

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{e^{2}}{\lvert{x_{1}-x_{2}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{2}}\rvert}-\frac{e^{2}Z}{\lvert{x_{1}-R\hat{e}}\rvert},

and analogously for the decomposition
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. Notice that the clusters are just single hydrogen atoms with interaction
I_{\mathcal{E}}
between them. Let
E_{0}
denote the ground state energy of a neutral hydrogen atom and let
\psi_{i}(x)
for
i=1,2
denote the normalized ground state of each hydrogen atom, localized at
y_{i}
. Then,

(2.33)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2},H(R)\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2E_{0}+o(1),

(2.34)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{2}\otimes\psi_{1},H(R)\psi_{2}\otimes\psi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=2E_{0}+o(1),

where in (
2.33
) and (
2.34
) we used that
\langle\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2},I_{\mathcal{E}}\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2}\rangle=o(1)
in
R
by the exponential localization of eigenfunctions (
2.4
). It is then easy to see that, without symmetry, the ground state of the decomposed system
H_{\mathcal{E}}
is
\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2}
and similarly
\psi_{2}\otimes\psi_{1}
is for
H_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
. Thus the lowest eigenvalue of
H_{\infty}
must have a double degeneracy and so the gap condition cannot hold. However, symmetrizing the ground states yields
S(\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2})=S(\psi_{2}\otimes\psi_{1})
, eliminating the degeneracy of
H_{\infty}
.

2.5.
Re-statement of main result and proof

We now re-state our main result as Theorem
2.5
and afterwards give the proof. In the proof we defer the justification of two statements to Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

(Global gap condition for bosons) Let
M\geqslant 2
and Assumption
[LNc]
hold. Then, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}
,

(2.35)

E_{1}^{S}(y)-E_{0}^{S}(y)\geqslant\delta>0,

where
E_{0}^{S}(y)=\inf\sigma(H_{N,M}^{S}(y))
and
E_{1}^{S}(y)=\inf\left(\sigma(H_{N,M}^{S}(y))\setminus\{E_{0}^{S}(y)\}\right)
.

Remark
.

With very strong non-degeneracy assumptions on ground states of the system and any cluster decompositions, we can also show that there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3M}
,

(2.36)

E_{1}^{A}(y)-E_{0}^{A}(y)\geqslant\delta>0,\quad\delta\text{ independent of }y,

where
E_{1}^{A}(y)=\inf\left(\sigma(H_{N,M}^{A}(y))\setminus\{E_{0}^{A}(y)\}\right)
in the fermionic case. To prove this, one can repeat the proof of Theorem
2.5
by replacing the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{s}
with
\mathcal{H}_{a}
and the projection
S
with
A
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

In what follows, we drop the superscript
S
denoting the symmetric case. Following Lemma
2.3
b), it is sufficient to examine the spectral gap for the electronic Hamiltonian
H_{N,M}(y)
without
V_{n}(y)
, i.e. without the nucleus-nucleus interaction terms. Hence, we replace
H_{N,M}(y)
with
\tilde{H}_{N,M}(y)
, where

(2.37)

\tilde{H}_{N,M}(y)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2m_{e}}\Delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{e^{2}}{\lvert{x_{i}-x_{j}}\rvert}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{e^{2}Z_{j}}{\lvert{x_{i}-y_{j}}\rvert}.

Of course, Lemma
2.4
applies to
\tilde{H}_{N,M}(y)
as well, and we drop the tilde when we apply the cluster breakup of
\tilde{H}_{N,M}
, i.e. (cf. (
2.16
))

(2.38)

\tilde{H}_{N,M}(y^{n})=H_{\mathcal{E}}(Y^{n})+I_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n}),

where
H_{\mathcal{E}}(Y^{n})
and
I_{\mathcal{E}}(y^{n})
are given by (
2.17
).

Aiming to argue by contradiction, assume there exists a sequence
\{y^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that

(2.39)

E_{1}(y^{n})-E_{0}(y^{n})\to 0.

After translating so that
y_{1}^{n}=0
, if
\max_{i\neq j}\{\left|y_{i}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}\right|\}
is a bounded sequence, then it must have a convergent subsequence, so
y^{n_{k}}\to y^{0}
. By the continuity of
E(y)
(see Section
2.1
), this implies that
E(y^{0})=E_{1}(y^{0})
, contradicting the non-degeneracy of the ground state. Hence, we may assume
\max_{i\neq j}\{\left|y_{i}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}\right|\}\to\infty

, we obtain an admissible partition

, which we now consider fixed.

The partitions
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{N}}
may assign electrons in such a way that some clusters are neutral and some are ions. Let us examine which configurations could yield suitable limits for the system described by

, in its ground state or first excited state.

In the case of neutral clusters, we denote

(2.40)

E_{j,0}=\inf\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}),\qquad E_{j,1}=\inf\left(\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}})\setminus\{E_{j,0}\}\right).

Similarly in the case of ions, we denote

(2.41)

E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}=\inf\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}),\qquad E_{j,1}^{\delta_{j}}=\inf\left(\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}})\setminus\{E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}\}\right),

where
\delta_{j}
is the ionic charge

(2.42)

\delta_{j}\equiv\left|\mathcal{E}_{j}\right|-\tilde{Z}_{j},

and recall
\tilde{Z}_{j}
is the total nuclear charge,
\tilde{Z}_{j}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}Z_{i}
(note the cluster is neutral if
\delta_{j}=0
or
\left|\mathcal{E}_{j}\right|=\tilde{Z}_{j}
). The eigenvalues of the
H_{\mathcal{E}}
Hamiltonian will be sums over all clusters of the eigenvalues of the clusters.

For
\delta_{j}\geqslant 0
(positive or neutral ions),
E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}
is a simple eigenvalue below the continuum, and
\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j}})
has an infinite amount of eigenvalues below its essential spectrum (see Theorem
2.1
). Hence for positive/neutral ions,

(2.43)

E_{j,0}<E_{j,1}<E_{j,2}<\inf\sigma_{ess}(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j},1})=E_{j,0}^{1}<E_{j,0}^{2}<\dots<E_{j,0}^{\tilde{Z}_{j}-1}.

If
\delta_{j}<0
is sufficiently negative (i.e. if a cluster of nuclei gains too many electrons), it may be the case that
E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}
is not an eigenvalue below the continuum, i.e.
\sigma(H_{\mathcal{E}_{j},\delta_{j}})=[E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}},\infty)
. For such a non-binding result for molecules we refer back to (
2.2
) and the discussion surrounding.
Hence, if
0>-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)\geqslant\delta_{j}
, then the negative ion with charge
\delta_{j}
has no well-defined ground state. By the HVZ Theorem (Theorem
2.1
), this implies

(2.44)

E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)}=E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)-1}=\dots=E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)-l}=\dots

for all integers
l>0
.

Otherwise (if
0>\delta_{j}>-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)
), we can only say, (with the exception of the non-existence of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}

Combining (
2.44
), (
2.43
), and (

) we obtain the following chain of inequalities relating the ground state energies (and in the neutral case, also the first excited state energy) of the ions of a molecule of total charge
\tilde{Z}_{j}
and number of nuclei
\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|
. For any integer
l>0
,

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle=E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)-l}=\dots=E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)-1}=E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)}\leqslant E_{j,0}^{-(\tilde{Z}_{j}+\left|\mathcal{N}_{j}\right|)+1}

(2.46)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dots\leqslant E_{j,0}^{-1}\leqslant E_{j,0}<E_{j,1}<E_{j,2}<E_{j,0}^{1}<E_{j,0}^{2}<\dots<E_{j,1}^{\tilde{Z}_{j}-1}.

By Assumption
[LNc]
the configuration of lowest energy is the partition of the system into neutral clusters, each in their ground states. We denote this energy by

, or more precisely,

(2.47)

E_{\infty,0}(Y^{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{j,0}(Y^{n}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}),

so we expect the limit

(2.48)

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{0}(y^{n})=E_{\infty,0}(Y^{0}).

For the next energy value, our candidates must be neutral clusters with one cluster in the first excited state (and the rest in ground states), which has energy

(2.49)

E_{\infty,0}^{1}(Y^{n})=\min_{1\leqslant l\leqslant k}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{k}E_{j,0}(Y^{n}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}})+E_{l,1}(Y^{n}_{\mathcal{N}_{l}})\right\},

or some configuration of ions in their ground states, which has energy

E_{\infty,0}^{I}(Y^{n})=\min_{I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}(Y^{n}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}})\right\},

(2.51)

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{1}(y^{n})=\min\left\{E_{\infty,0}^{1}(Y^{0}),\>\>E^{I}_{\infty,0}(Y^{0})\right\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

The minimizer of the right-hand side of (

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

(See also Lemma
6
in
[

]
.) In the case of neutral clusters with one cluster in an excited state, (

2.1

2.50
), requires a proof. Suppose that

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant l\leqslant k\\
I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})\end{subarray}}\left\{E_{\infty,0}^{1}(Y^{0}),\>\>E^{I}_{\infty,0}(Y^{0})\right\}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}(Y^{n}_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}),

for some configuration of ions
I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})
given by (
2.21
), and in what follows we drop the

dependence for notational convenience. We construct an eigenstate corresponding to the right hand side of (

) by finding eigenstates for each energy

and taking the symmetrized tensor product over

In view of the HVZ theorem, it is enough to show that

for all

. Assume that for some

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}E_{l,0}^{\delta_{l}}

\displaystyle=E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}E_{l,0}^{\delta_{l}}>E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}+E_{i,0}^{\delta_{i}+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{k}E_{l,0}^{\delta_{l}},

We defer the proofs of (

,

where

given by (

). As

for all

, there exists a constant

such that

(note in the case of part b) we have assumed that all ground state energies are non-degenerate). Hence in all cases it is clear to see that

∎

E_{0}(y^{n})=\nu_{0}(E_{0}(y^{n})),\qquad E_{1}(y^{n})=\nu_{1}(E_{1}(y^{n})),

where recall that the ground state
E_{0}
is non-degenerate. Hence, we use the min-max theorem to write

(3.33)

\displaystyle E_{0}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\textrm{ Ran }P\\
\lVert{\phi}\rVert=1\end{subarray}}\langle\phi,F_{P}(E_{0}(y^{n}))\phi\rangle=\langle\tilde{S}(\psi_{0}^{\mathcal{E},n}),F_{P}(E_{0}(y^{n}))\tilde{S}(\psi_{0}^{\mathcal{E},n})\rangle,

and

(3.34)

\displaystyle E_{1}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset\textrm{ Ran }P\\
\textrm{dim }A=2\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in A\\
\lVert{A}\rVert=1\end{subarray}}\langle\psi,F_{P}(E_{1}(y^{n}))\psi\rangle.

We use the expansion (
3.31
) and estimates (
3.26
) and (
3.27
). Note that the infimum in (
3.34
) is taken over 2-dimensional subspaces of
\textrm{Ran }P
, as opposed to in (
3.28
) where it was taken over 2-dimensional subspaces of the much larger space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
. Hence by repeating the argument preceding the estimate (
3.29
) we improve it, obtaining

(3.35)

\displaystyle E_{0}(y^{n})=

\displaystyle E_{\infty,0}(Y^{n})+o(1),

(3.36)

\displaystyle E_{1}(y^{n})=

\displaystyle E_{\infty,1}(Y^{n})+o(1),

from which the limits (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) follow and the proof of Theorem
3.1
concludes.
∎

3.2.

We first begin by defining the constant

where we define the positive constants

in the following ways. Then

and we can see that (

First, we consider the smallest gap between the ground state energy and first excited state energy of neutral clusters,

Clearly
G_{1}>0

(cf. (
2.46
)). Next, we consider gaps between eigenvalues of the ionic minimizers, when they exist.

(3.39)

G_{2}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant j\leqslant k,\\
I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})\text{ i.m. }\end{subarray}}\left\{E_{j,1}^{\delta_{j}}-E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}\right\},

as long as the set is non-empty.
G_{2}
is the smallest gap between the ground state energy and first excited state energy of ionic minimizers.

2.6

(3.40)

G_{3}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant j\leqslant k,\\
I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})\text{ i.m. }\\
\delta_{j}\geqslant 10\end{subarray}}\left\{E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}}-E_{j,0}^{\delta_{j}-1}\right\},

the smallest gap between the ground state energies of strictly positive ionic minimizers and the ionic minimizer gaining one electron (note this also includes the neutral energy, i.e.
E_{j,0}^{1}>E_{j,0}
).
G_{3}>0
by Theorem
2.1
(cf. (
2.46
)), as we only consider strictly positive ions. Lastly, we define

(3.41)

G_{4}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})\\
\text{ not i.m.}\end{subarray}}\left\{E_{\infty,0}^{I}-E_{\infty,1}\right\},

the smallest gap between the total ground state energy of non-minimizing ionic clusters and the total first excited state energy

Now, we move to showing the estimate (

Obtaining estimate (

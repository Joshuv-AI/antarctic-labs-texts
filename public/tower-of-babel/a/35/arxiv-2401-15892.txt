A generalization of the Romanoff theorem
Yuchen Ding and Wenguang Zhai

Abstract
. Let 𝒫 \mathcal{P} be the set of primes and ℕ \mathbb{N} the set of positive integers. Let also r 1 , … , r t r_{1},...,r_{t} be positive real numbers and R 2 ​ ( r 1 , … , r t ) R_{2}(r_{1},...,r_{t}) the set of odd integers which can be represented as p + 2 ⌊ k 1 r 1 ⌋ + ⋯ + 2 ⌊ k t r t ⌋ , p+2^{\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+2^{\lfloor k_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}, where p ∈ 𝒫 p\in\mathcal{P} and k 1 , … , k t ∈ ℕ k_{1},...,k_{t}\in\mathbb{N} . Recently, Chen and Xu proved that the set R 2 ​ ( r 1 , … , r t ) R_{2}(r_{1},...,r_{t}) has positive lower asymptotic density, provided that r 1 − 1 + ⋯ + r t − 1 ≥ 1 r_{1}^{-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1 and at least one of r 1 , … , r t r_{1},...,r_{t} is an integer. Their result reduces to the famous theorem of Romanoff by taking t = r 1 = 1 . t=r_{1}=1. In this note, we remove the unnecessary condition: ‘ at least one of r 1 , … , r t r_{1},...,r_{t} is an integer ’.

1.
Introoduction

The aim of this note is the following generalization of the classical theorem named after Romanoff
[
21
]
, which states that the set of odd integers whose elements can be written as the sum of a prime and a power of two has positive lower asymptotic density.

Theorem 1
.

Let
r_{1},...,r_{t}
be positive real numbers. Then the set

R_{2}(r_{1},...,r_{t})=\left\{\text{odd}~n:n=p+2^{\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+2^{\lfloor k_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor},~p\in\mathcal{P},k_{1},...,k_{t}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

has positive lower asymptotic density if and only if
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1
, where
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathbb{N}
are the sets of primes and positive integers, respectively.

This generalization was introduced in a recent article of Chen and Xu
[
3
]
but only with an additional condition (which is crucial in their argument): ‘
at least one of
r_{1},...,r_{t}
is an integer
’.

Before the proof of Theorem
1
, We mention the history involving the Romanoff theorem below.
de Polignac
[
19
]
conjectured that every odd number greater than 1 is the sum of a prime and a power of 2. But soon, he
[
19
]
recognized that Euler had already mentioned a counterexample 959 in a letter to Goldbach. Towards the positive direction,
Romanoff
[
21
]
proved that there is a positive proportion of the odd numbers which can be written as the sum of a prime and a power of two. Later, Erdős
[
10
]
and van der Corput
[
4
]
showed that the odd numbers which can not be represented by the form
p+2^{m}
with
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
also possess positive lower asymptotic density. Crocker
[
5
]
proved that there are infinitely many odd numbers which cannot be represented as
p+2^{m}+2^{n}
with
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, which was later improved significantly by Chen–Feng–Templier
[
2
]
and Pan
[
17
]
.

Erdős paid much attention to Romanoff’s theorem and the proof of it was later simplified by Erdős and Turán
[
11
]
and Erdős
[
9
]
. In
[
10
]
, Erdős extended powers of
2
in the Romanoff theorem to a more general sequence
\{a_{k}\}
. Precisely, let
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots
be an infinite sequence of positive integers satisfying
a_{k}|a_{k+1}
. Then Erdős proved that the necessary and sufficient condition that the sequence
p+a_{k}
should have positive density is that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log a_{k}}{k}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{d|a_{i}}\frac{1}{d}<c

for some absolute constant
c
and any
a_{i}
.

Following a question of Chen
[
1
]
, Yang and Chen
[
23
]
considered variants of the Romanoff theorem with
c
-condition. A set
\mathcal{B}
is said to be of
c
-condition if there is some absolute constant
0<c<1
so that
\mathcal{B}(cx)\gg\mathcal{B}(x)
. Yang and Chen studied the following sumset

\mathcal{S}=\big\{p+b:p\in\mathcal{P},b\in\mathcal{B}\big\}.

They proved that

\frac{x}{\log x}\min\bigg\{\mathcal{B}(x),\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\bigg\}\ll\mathcal{S}(x)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\min\big\{\mathcal{B}(x),\log x\big\}.

As an application, Yang and Chen obtained the following estimate

\#\big\{n\leq x:n=p+2^{a^{2}}+2^{b^{2}},~p\in\mathcal{P},a,b\in\mathbb{N}\big\}\gg\frac{x}{\log\log x}.

Moreover, for any
m
they constructed a subset
\mathcal{B}
with
c
-condition so that

\mathcal{B}(x)=\frac{1+o(1)}{m+1}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right)^{m+1}

and
\mathcal{S}(x)\ll x/\log\log x
. These naturally led them to the following two problems.

Problem 1
.

Does there exist a real number
\alpha>0
and a subset
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{N}
with
c
-condition such that
\mathcal{B}(x)\gg x^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{S}(x)\ll x/\log\log x
?

Problem 2
.

Does there exist a positive integer
k
such that the set of positive integers which can be represented as
p+\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{m_{i}^{2}}
with
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
has a positive lower density? If such
k
exists, what is the minimal value of such
k
?

In a prior article, the first named author
[
6
]
completely solved Problem
2
by showing that
k=2
is admissible. Thus,

\#\big\{n\leq x:n=p+2^{a^{2}}+2^{b^{2}},~p\in\mathcal{P},a,b\in\mathbb{N}\big\}\gg x.

In a subsequent article, the first named author
[
7
]
answered Problem
1
negatively and proved that
for any subset
\mathcal{B}
of
c
-condition with
\mathcal{B}(x)\gg x^{\alpha}
we have

\mathcal{S}(x)\gg x/\log\log\log x.

The first named author also pointed out that the above lower bound is sharp via concrete examples. Following the line of Problem
2
, Chen and Xu
[
3
]
obtained Theorem
1
with additional condition: ‘
at least one of
r_{1},...,r_{t}
is an integer
’. And in
[
3
, Remark 1]
, they commented that ‘
We believe that it is unnecessary that at least one of
r_{1},...,r_{t}
is an integer in our theorem
’.
It is just our purpose to remove this additional condition which forms a clean generalization (Theorem
1
) of the Romanoff theorem.

For more variants on the Romanoff theorem, see e.g. Filaseta–Finch–Kozek
[
13
]
, Pan–Li
[
18
]
, Lee
[
15
]
, Shparlinski–Weingartner
[
22
]
,
Elsholtz–Luca–Planitzer
[
8
]
.

2.
Proofs

Throughout the proof,
\mathcal{S}(x)
denotes the number of elements of
\mathcal{S}
not exceeding
x
and
x
is supposed to be sufficiently large.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

One can also easily note that
R_{2}(r_{1},...,r_{t})
is of density zero if
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+r_{t}^{-1}<1
, making the necessary condition valid. So, we only need to prove the sufficient point.

Suppose first that there exists some
1\leq s\leq t
such that
r_{s}^{-1}\geq 1
, then

\left\{2^{\lfloor k_{s}^{r_{s}}\rfloor}:k_{s}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}=2^{\mathbb{N}}

and hence

\mathcal{P}+2^{\mathbb{N}}\subset R_{2}(r_{1},...,r_{t}).

In this case, there is nothing to prove for our theorem as it is just the Romanoff theorem.

From now on, we assume that
r_{i}^{-1}<1
for any
1\leq i\leq t
. So, we would clearly have
t\geq 2
. On observing Chen–Xu’s result, we can also assume without loss of generality that none of
r_{i}
belongs to integers. The proof begins with the following observation of Chen and Xu
[
3
, Eq. (4.1)]
: There exists an integer
s\geq 2
such that

\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{r_{s-1}}<1\leq\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{r_{s-1}}+\frac{1}{r_{s}},

from which it follows that there is real number
\lambda\geq 1
satisfying that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{r_{s-1}}+\frac{1}{\lambda r_{s}}=1.

(1)

Let

\mathcal{A}=\left\{2^{\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+2^{\lfloor k_{s-1}^{r_{s-1}}\rfloor}+2^{\left\lfloor\lfloor k_{s}^{\lambda}\rfloor^{r_{s}}\right\rfloor}\right\}.

It suffices to prove that the set
\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{A}
has positive lower asymptotic density since

\left\{2^{\left\lfloor\lfloor k_{s}^{\lambda}\rfloor^{r_{s}}\right\rfloor}:k_{s}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\left\{2^{\lfloor k_{s}^{r_{s}}\rfloor}:k_{s}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Denote by
r(n)
the number of representations of
n=p+a
with
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
. We note from Chen and Xu
[
3
, Pages 77 and 78]
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x)\asymp\log x.

(2)

It is plain that

\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)\geq\mathcal{A}(x/2)\mathcal{P}(x/2)\gg\log x(x/\log x)\gg x.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we have

\bigg(\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)\bigg)^{2}\leq\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
r(n)\geq 1\end{subarray}}1\Bigg)\bigg(\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)^{2}\bigg).

We are going to show that

\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)^{2}\ll x,

from which we would get

\left(\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{A}\right)(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,~r(n)\geq 1\end{subarray}}1\gg x.

Expanding and then exchanging the sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+a_{1}=p_{2}+a_{2}\leq x\\
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{P},~a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\leq p_{1}\leq x\\
p_{1}-p_{2}=a_{2}-a_{1}\end{subarray}}1.

For the innermost sum in the right–hand side above, we separate it into two cases according to
a_{1}=a_{2}
or not. The contribution of the sum for
a_{1}=a_{2}
can be controlled by

\mathcal{A}(x)\mathcal{P}(x)\ll x

via Eq. (
2
) and the prime number theorem.
Thus, to complete our proof, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle S(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}<p_{1}\leq x\\
p_{1}-p_{2}=a_{2}-a_{1}\end{subarray}}1\ll x.

(3)

For
h\neq 0
, let
\pi_{2}(x,h)
be the number of prime pairs
p
and
q
with
q-p=h
below
x
. It is well known (see e.g.
[
16
, Theorem 7.3]
) that

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(x,h)\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\prod_{p|h}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

(4)

Inserting Eq. (
4
) into Eq. (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle S(x)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\prod_{p|a_{2}-a_{1}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

We are leading to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|a_{2}-a_{1}\\
p>2\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2}.

Following an idea of Elsholtz
et al.

[
8
]
, we note that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|a_{2}-a_{1}\\
p\geq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)<\left(1+\frac{1}{\log x}\right)^{\log x/\log 2}\ll 1

since the number of prime factors of
|a_{1}-a_{2}|\leq x
is trivially not exceeding
\log x/\log 2
.
Thus, it reduces to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|a_{2}-a_{1}\\
2<p<\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|a_{2}-a_{1}\\
2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)<\log x\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}\ll(\log x)^{2},

(5)

where
P^{+}(d)
denotes the largest prime factor of
d
with convention
P^{+}(1)=1
and
\mu(d)
the usual Möbius function.
On noting the definition of
\mathcal{A}
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|a_{2}-a_{1}\\
2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)<\log x\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{i},\widetilde{k_{i}}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{i}}}\\
1\leq i\leq s-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{k_{s},\widetilde{k_{s}}\leq 2\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{kr_{s}}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|h_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+h_{s-1}+h_{s}-\left(\widetilde{h_{1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\widetilde{h_{s-1}}+\widetilde{h_{s}}\right)\\
\widetilde{h_{1}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\widetilde{h_{s-1}}+\widetilde{h_{s}}<h_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+h_{s-1}+h_{s}\leq x\\
2\nmid d,~P^{+}(d)<\log x\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{2-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}~\max_{|g|\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x,~2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)<\log x\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\\
2^{\left\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}\equiv g\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1,

(6)

where the last estimate comes from Eq. (
1
) and

h_{i}=2^{\left\lfloor k_{i}^{r_{i}}\right\rfloor},~~~\widetilde{h_{i}}=2^{\left\lfloor k_{i}^{r_{i}}\right\rfloor}\quad(1\leq i\leq s-1),\quad\quad h_{s}=2^{\left\lfloor\lfloor k_{s}^{\lambda}\rfloor^{r_{s}}\right\rfloor},~~~\widetilde{h_{s}}=2^{\left\lfloor\left\lfloor\widetilde{k_{s}}^{\lambda}\right\rfloor^{r_{s}}\right\rfloor}.

For some integers
d
, suppose first that the number of
k_{1}
satisfying the congruence

\displaystyle 2^{\left\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}\equiv g\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)

(7)

is not large than
(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}/\log\log x
,
then it is admissible for those
d
since the double sums in right–hand side of Eq. (
2
) are bounded now by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x,~2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)<\log x\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{\log\log x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{\log\log x}\prod_{2<p<\log x}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{\log\log x}\exp\left(\sum_{2<p<\log x}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{\log\log x}\exp\left(\sum_{2<p<\log x}\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{1/r_{1}},

(8)

where the last estimate follows from the famous theorem of Mertens. We are left over to consider the remaining
d
for which the number of
k_{1}
satisfying Eq. (
7
) is greater than
(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}/\log\log x
. In this case, let
k_{1,0}
be such a solution. Then we have

2^{\left\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}\equiv 2^{\left\lfloor k_{1,0}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d).

So, we would get

\displaystyle\left\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor\equiv\ell\ ({\rm{mod}}\ e_{2}(d))

(9)

for some
0\leq\ell\leq e_{2}(d)-1
, where
e_{2}(d)
is the least positive integer
m
so that
d|2^{m}-1
. Furthermore, in case that (
9
) has more than
(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}/\log\log x
solutions of
k_{1}
, we claim that

\displaystyle e_{2}(d)\ll_{r_{1}}(\log x)^{1-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\log\log x,

(10)

which would be one of the key points in the following arguments.
In fact, suppose that
L
is the minimal distance between two consecutive solutions
k_{1}^{\prime}
and
k_{1}^{\prime}+L
of the congruence equation (
9
) with
k_{1}^{\prime}>(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}
, then
the number of solutions
k_{1}
to (
9
) is not more than

\displaystyle(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}+\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{L}+1.

Hence, by our assumption on the number of the solutions, we have

\displaystyle(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}+\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{L}+1\geq\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{\log\log x},

which clearly means that

L\leq 2\log\log x.

By the definition of
L
, we also have

\displaystyle\lfloor(k_{1}^{\prime}+L)^{r_{1}}\rfloor-\lfloor(k_{1}^{\prime})^{r_{1}}\rfloor\geq e_{2}(d),

from which it follows that

\displaystyle e_{2}(d)\leq(k_{1}^{\prime}+L)^{r_{1}}-(k_{1}^{\prime})^{r_{1}}+1\ll_{r_{1}}(k_{1}^{\prime})^{r_{1}-1}L\ll(\log x)^{1-1/r_{1}}\log\log x.

We now conclude from (
2
), (
9
), (
10
) and the above proofs that we only need to show

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x,~2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)<\log x\\
e_{2}(d)\leq(\log x)^{1-1/r_{1}}\log\log x\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\\
\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor\equiv\ell\ ({\rm{mod}}\ e_{2}(d))\end{subarray}}1\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}.

(11)

For any
k_{1}\leq(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}
satisfies (
9
), we clearly have

e_{2}(d)q+\ell\leq k_{1}^{r_{1}}<e_{2}(d)q+\ell+1

for some nonnegative integer
q
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell-1}{e_{2}(d)}<q\leq\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell}{e_{2}(d)}.

(12)

It is plain that an integer
q
satisfies (
12
) if and only if

\left\lfloor\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell}{e_{2}(d)}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell-1}{e_{2}(d)}\right\rfloor=1,

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\\
\lfloor k_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor\equiv\ell\ ({\rm{mod}}\ e_{2}(d))\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\left\lfloor\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell}{e_{2}(d)}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell-1}{e_{2}(d)}\right\rfloor\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{1}{e_{2}(d)}-\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell}{e_{2}(d)}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}-\ell-1}{e_{2}(d)}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll_{r_{1}}\frac{(\log x)^{1/r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+O\bigg(\max_{|\theta|\leq 1}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right)\bigg|\bigg),

(13)

where
\psi(t)=\{t\}-1/2
is the sawtooth function.
Now, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right)=S_{1}+S_{2},

(14)

where

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{k\leq e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}}\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}=\sum_{e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}<k\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}}\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right).

Trivially, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}\ll e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}.

(15)

We are now in a position to use the following van der Corput type bounds
[
14
, Lemma 4.3]
for a suitable estimate of
S_{2}
.

Lemma 1.

For any real number
t,
let
\psi(t)=\{t\}-1/2
. Suppose
K\geq 3
is a real number,
Y>0.
Suppose
\alpha>0~(\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z})
is a fixed real number. Then for any exponent pair
(\kappa,\lambda)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{K\leq k<2K}\psi\left(Yk^{\alpha}+\theta\right)\ll\frac{K^{1-\alpha}}{Y}+Y^{\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}K^{\frac{\lambda+\kappa\alpha}{1+\kappa}}.

For
e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}\leq K\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}
, by
Lemma 1
with
Y=\frac{1}{e_{2}(d)}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{K\leq k_{1}<2K}\psi\left(\frac{k_{1}^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\leq k<2K}\psi\left(Yk_{1}^{r_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{YK^{r_{1}-1}}+Y^{\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}K^{\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{1+\kappa}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{e_{2}(d)}{K^{r_{1}-1}}+e_{2}(d)^{-\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}K^{\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{1+\kappa}}

since
Y=1/e_{2}(d)
, which combining a splitting argument yields

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum_{e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}<k_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\log 2x}{\log 2}\right)^{1/r_{1}}}\psi\left(\frac{k^{r_{1}}}{e_{2}(d)}+\theta\right)\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}\cdot\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{1+\kappa}}e_{2}(d)^{-\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}+e_{2}(d)^{1/r_{1}}.

(16)

Taking (
15
) and (
16
) into (

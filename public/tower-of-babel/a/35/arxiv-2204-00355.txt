Inverse problem for a subdiffusion equation with the Caputo derivative
O.T. Muhiddinova

Abstract




Uzbek Mathematical

Journal, 2021, No 1, pp.
id1

Abstract.
The article investigates an inverse problem of determining the right-hand side of a subdiffusion
equation with Caputo fractional derivative whose elliptic part has the most general
form and is defined on an
N
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. The Fourier method is used to prove theorems on the existence and uniqueness of the classical solution of the initial-boundary value problem and on the unique reconstruction of the
unknown right-hand side of the equation. Requirements for the initial function and for the additional condition are established under which the classical Fourier method can be applied to the inverse problem under
consideration.

Keywords:
Subdiffusion equation, the Caputo derivatives,
Fourier method, inverse and initial-boundary value problem.

MSC (2010):
35K35; 35C10.

1
Introduction

It is well known that the interest in the
study of inverse problems for equations of mathematical physics is due to the importance of their
applications in various branches of mechanics, seismology, medical tomography, and geophysics. It
should be noted that inverse problems are well studied for differential equations of integer order.
A lot of research is also devoted to inverse problems of determining the right-hand side of subdiffusion equations (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
-
[
14
]
and the bibliography therein). The paper
[
14
]
studied the inverse
problem of determining the right-hand side of the subdiffusion equation with Caputo fractional
derivatives whose elliptic part is an arbitrary elliptic operator with discrete spectrum and proved
the existence and uniqueness of a generalized solution by the Fourier method. In the paper
[
14
]
conditions for
\varphi(x)
and
\Psi(x)
are assumed to be defined in the operator’s domain. The other papers, including
[
9
]
-
[
12
]
, take the Caputo derivative as the fractional derivative.
In the paper
[
15
]
, authors studied the inverse problem of determining the right-hand side of the subdiffusion equation with Riemann–Liouville fractional derivatives whose elliptic part is an elliptic operator of an arbitrary order defined in bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary.

In this article we investigate an inverse problem of determining the right-hand side of a subdiffusion
equation with Caputo fractional derivative whose elliptic part has the most general
form and is defined on an
N
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. The Fourier method is used to prove theorems on the existence and uniqueness of the classical solution of the initial-boundary value problem and on the unique reconstruction of the
unknown right-hand side of the equation. Requirements for the initial function and for the additional condition are established under which the classical Fourier method can be applied to the inverse problem under
consideration.

Let is move on to precise descriptions of the objects of study.

The fractional derivative in the sense of Caputo of order
0<\rho<1
of the function
f(t)
defined on
[0,\infty)
has the
form (see, for example,
[
16
]
, p. 14)

D_{t}^{\rho}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\rho)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\frac{d}{d\tau}f(\tau)d\tau}{(t-\tau)^{\rho}},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,

provided the right-hand side exists. Here
\Gamma(\sigma)
is
Euler’s gamma function. If in this definition we interchange
differentiation and fractional integration, then we get the
definition of the Riemann-Liouville derivative:

\partial_{t}^{\rho}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\rho)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(\tau)d\tau}{(t-\tau)^{\rho}},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0.

Note that if
\rho=1
, then fractional derivatives coincides with
the ordinary classical derivative of the first order:

\partial_{t}f(t)=D_{t}f(t)=\frac{d}{dt}f(t)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{N}
be
N
-dimensional torus:
\mathbb{T}^{N}=(-\pi,\pi]^{N}
,
N\geq 1
and

A(D)=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}
be a homogeneous elliptic symmetric positive
differential operator with constant coefficients.

Problem:
Find the functions
\{u(x,t),f(x)\}
from the class: A function
f(x)\in C({T}^{N})
and a function
u(x,t)\in C({T}^{N}\times[0,T])
with the properties
D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t),A(x,D)u(x,t)\in C({T}^{N}\times(0,T])
that satisfies the following differential equation (
0<\rho\leq 1
)

D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)+A(D)u(x,t)=f(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\mathbb{T}^{N},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0;

(1.1)

and the initial

u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\mathbb{T}^{N};

(1.2)

and additional conditions

u(x,T)=\Psi(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\mathbb{T}^{N}.

(1.3)

Instead of the boundary conditions we will require that the desired functions
u(x,t)
and
f(x)

are
2\pi
-periodic in each variable
x_{j}
. We will also assume that the given functions
\varphi(x)
and
\Psi(x)

are
2\pi
-periodic in each variable
x_{j}
as well.

2
Main result

To formulate the main result of this paper we recall some
definitions and statements.
By
A
we denote the operator
A(D)
defined on
2\pi
-periodic functions in
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The closure
\hat{A}

of such an operator in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is self-adjoint. The operator
\hat{A}

has a complete in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-orthonormal
system of eigenfunctions
\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
A(n)
,
n\in(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Therefore, by virtue of the von
Neumann theorem, for each
\tau\geq 0
the operator

\hat{A}^{\tau}
acts by the rule
\hat{A}^{\tau}g(x)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}A^{\tau}(n)g_{n}e^{inx}
,

where the
g_{n}
are the Fourier coefficients of the function
g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
in the trigonometric system

\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}
. The domain of this operator is determined from the condition

\hat{A}^{\tau}g(x)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and has the form

D(\hat{A}^{\tau})=\{g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}A^{2\tau}(n)|g_{n}|^{2}<\infty\}.

(2.1)

To define the domain of the operator

\hat{A}^{\tau}
in terms of Sobolev spaces, recall the definition of these
spaces (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
): a function
g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is said to belong to the Sobolev space
L_{2}^{a}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
with
a real number
a>0
,
if the norm

||g||^{2}_{L_{2}^{a}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}=\sum\limits_{n\in Z^{N}}(1+|n|^{2})^{a}|g_{n}|^{2}.

(2.2)

is finite. In the case of noninteger
a
this space is also referred to as the Liouville space.

It can readily be verified that there exist constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that one has the estimates

c_{1}(1+|n|^{2})^{\tau m}\leq 1+A^{2\tau}(n)\leq c_{2}(1+|n|^{2})^{\tau m}.

Consequently, comparing the expressions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
we see that
D(\hat{A}^{\tau})=L_{2}^{\tau m}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

First, let us construct a formal solution of the inverse problem (
1.1
) - (
1.3
).
According to the Fourier method, we look for a solution of Problem (
1.1
) - (
1.3
) in the form of the following series:

u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}T_{n}(t)e^{inx},

(2.3)

here the functions
T_{n}(t)
are solutions of the Cauchy type problem

D_{t}^{\rho}T_{n}+A(n)T_{n}=f_{n}(t),T_{n}(0)=\varphi_{n}.

(2.4)

Here and everywhere below we define by
g_{n}
the Fourier coefficients of the function
g(x)
.
Note that the unknowns in problem (
2.4
) are also the coefficients
f_{n}
. To determine these numbers,
from (
1.3
) we obtain the additional condition

T_{n}(T)=\Psi_{n}.

(2.5)

The unique solution of problem (
2.4
) has the form (see, for
example,
[
16
]
, p. 16 or
[
18
]
, p. 173)

T_{n}(t)=\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)t^{\rho})+f_{n}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\xi^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-A(n)\xi^{\rho})d\xi,

(2.6)

where
E_{\rho,\mu}
- is the Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\rho,\mu}(t)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{\Gamma(\rho k+\mu)}

and
\Gamma(k)
- is the Euler’s gamma function.

For the integral one has the formula (see, for example.
[
18
]
, formula (4.4.4))

\int\limits_{0}^{t}\xi^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-\ A(n)\xi^{\rho})d\xi=t^{\rho}\,E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho}).

(2.7)

and we have

T_{n}(t)=\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)t^{\rho})+f_{n}t^{\rho}\,E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho}).

(2.8)

To determine the coefficient
f_{n}
, by virtue of the additional condition
(
2.5
) we obtain the equation

T_{n}(T)=\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})+f_{n}T^{\rho}\,E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})=\Psi_{n}.

Consequently,

f_{n}=\frac{\Psi_{n}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}-\frac{\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}.

(2.9)

Thus, we have constructed the formal series (see (
2.3
) and (
2.8
))

u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx}+\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f_{n}t^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx},

(2.10)

for the solution of the inverse problem, where the numbers
f_{n}
are determined using formula (
2.9
) and

f(x)=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}-\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}.

(2.11)

Obviously, the function (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) are formal solutions

Now let us state the following theorem on the existence of a solution of the inverse problem on
the torus.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\tau}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and

\Psi\in L^{\tau+1}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, where
\tau>\frac{N}{2}
.
Then there exists a unique
periodic solution
\{u(x,t),f(x)\}
of the inverse problem (
1.1
) - (
1.3
) which can be represented in the
form of the series (
2.10
) and (
2.11
).

3
Uniqueness

Theorem 3.1
.

(on uniqueness). Let the function
\varphi(x)
and
\Psi(x)

be continuous in
{\mathbb{T}^{N}}
. Then there can exist only one classical solution
\{u(x,t),f(x)\}

of the inverse problem (
1.1
)- (
1.3
).

Proof.

Assume that under the assumptions in the theorem there are two pair of solutions,
\{u_{1}(x,t),f_{1}(x)\}
and
\{u_{2}(x,t),f_{2}(x)\}
. Let us prove that
u(x,t)=u_{1}(x,t)-u_{2}(x,t)\equiv 0
and
f(x)=f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)\equiv 0
. Since the problem under consideration is linear, we have the following problem for determining the functions

u(x,t)
and
f(x)
:

D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)+A(D)u(x,t)=f(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in T^{N},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0;

(3.1)

u(x,0)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptx\in T^{N},

(3.2)

u(x,T)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptx\in T^{N}.

(3.3)

Here, instead of boundary conditions, we require the functions we
are looking for to be periodic with respect to each variable.

Let
u\left(x,t\right)
be a solution of problem
(
3.1
) - (
3.3
).
Consider the following function

\ w_{n}\left(t\right)=(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}u\left(x,t\right)\cdot e^{inx}dx.

(3.4)

If we differentiate this equality under the integral and take into account equation (
3.1
), we obtain the following equality

D_{t}^{\rho}w_{n}(t)=(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}D_{t}^{\rho}u\left(x,t\right)\cdot e^{inx}dx=

=-(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}A\left(D\right)u\left(x,t\right)\cdot e^{inx}dx+(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}f(x)\cdot e^{inx}dx,t>0.

Integrating here by parts and taking into account that the operator
\hat{A}
is self-adjoint, we obtain the following equality

D_{t}^{\rho}w_{n}(t)=-(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}u\left(x,t\right)A\left(D\right)\cdot e^{inx}dx+f_{n}

=-A(n)\cdot(2\pi)^{-{\raise 1.89871pt\hbox{$N$}\!\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{N2}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}\!\lower 1.89871pt\hbox{$2$}}}\int_{T^{N}}u\left(x,t\right)\cdot e^{inx}dx+f_{n};

D_{t}^{\rho}w_{n}(t)=-A(n)\ w_{n}\left(t\right)+f_{n},t>0.

Now, taking into account initial condition (
3.2
), we conclude that the function
\ w_{n}\left(t\right)
is the solution of the following Cauchy problem:

D_{t}^{\rho}w_{n}(t)+A(n)\ w_{n}\left(t\right)-f_{n}=0,t>0;\ w_{n}\left(0\right)=0.

The solution of this problem has the form (see, for
example,
[
16
]
p. 16 or
[
18
]
, p. 173 )

w_{n}(t)=f_{n}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\xi^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-\ A(n)\xi^{\rho})d\xi,

For the integral one has the formula (see, for example.
[
18
]
, formula (4.4.4))

\int\limits_{0}^{t}\xi^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-\ A(n)\xi^{\rho})d\xi=t^{\rho}\,E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho}).

(3.5)

Therefore, using the additional condition (
3.3
), we obtain
w_{n}(T)=f_{n}\,T^{\rho}\,E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})=0

and since
0<E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})\leq 1
, we conclude that

f_{n}=0
for all
n
. In turn, this implies that
w_{n}(t)\equiv 0
for all
n
. Consequently, by virtue of the completeness of the system of eigenfunctions
\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}
, we have
f(x)=0
and
u(x,t)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
and
t>0
. Thus, we have proved the uniqueness of
solution of the inverse problem (
1.1
)- (
1.3
) .

4
Existence

The proof of existence is based on the following lemma, which is, in fact, corollary of the classical Sobolev embedding theorem (see M.A. Krasnosel’skii et.al.,
[
20
]
, p. 453).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tau>\frac{|\alpha|}{m}+\frac{N}{2m}

. Then for any
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,
the operator

D^{\alpha}\left(\hat{A}+{\rm I}\right)^{-\tau}

maps (completely) continuously from
L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)
into
C\left(T^{N}\right)
, moreover the following
estimate holds true:

\left\|D^{\alpha}\left(\hat{A}+{\rm I}\right)^{-\tau}g\right\|_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)}

(4.1)

where we denote by
{\rm I}
the unit operator in

L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)
.

Proof.
Since the embedding theorem one has

\left\|D^{\alpha}\left(\hat{A}+{\rm I}\right)^{-\tau}g\right\|_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\left\|D^{\alpha}\left(\hat{A}+{\rm I}\right)^{-\tau}g\right\|_{L_{2}^{a}\left(T^{N}\right)},

for any
a>\frac{N}{2}
. Therefore it is sufficient to prove the inequality

\left\|D^{\alpha}\left(\hat{A}+{\rm I}\right)^{-\tau}g\right\|_{L_{2}^{a}\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)}.

But this is a consequence of the estimate

\sum_{n\in Z^{n}}\left|g_{n}\right|^{2}\left|n\right|^{2\left|\alpha\right|}(1+A\left(n\right))^{-2\tau}\left(1+\left|n\right|^{2}\right)^{a}\leq C\sum_{n\in Z^{n}}\left|g_{n}\right|^{2},

that is valid for
\frac{N}{2}<a\leq\tau m-\left|\alpha\right|
.

Now we show how the existence of the solution of problem (
1.1
)- (
1.3
) can be derived from this lemma.

Let the assumptions of Theorem 2.1 be satisfied. First, let us prove the absolute and uniform
convergence of the series (
2.11
) for the function
f
. Set

f^{1}_{j}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx},

(4.2)

f^{2}_{j}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}.

(4.3)

The condition of the theorem implies the convergence of the series

\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{2(\tau+1)}|\Psi_{n}|^{2}\leq C_{\Psi}<\infty,

for some
\tau>\frac{N}{2m}
and the convergence of the series

\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{2\tau}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\leq C_{\varphi}<\infty.

for the function
\varphi
.

Since
(\hat{A}+I)^{-\tau}e^{inx}=(A(n)+1)^{-\tau}e^{inx}
, for
f^{1}_{j}
we have

f^{1}_{j}(x)=(\hat{A}+I)^{-\tau}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}\,\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}.

Then, applying the lemma we obtain

||f^{1}_{j}||_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}=||(\hat{A}+I)^{-\tau}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}\,\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}||_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq

\leq C||\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}\,\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}||_{L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)}.

(4.4)

Based on this, by virtue of the orthonormality of the system
\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}
, we have

||f^{1}_{j}||^{2}_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\bigg|\frac{\Psi_{n}\,\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}\bigg|^{2}.

Let us give estimates for the Mittag-Leffler function required in the sequel. For large
t
one has
the asymptotic estimate (see e.g.,

[
16
]
, p. 13, recall that
0<\rho\leq 1
)

E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-t)=\frac{1}{t}\big(1+O(\frac{1}{t})\big),\hskip 9.24994ptt>1,

(4.5)

and for arbitrary complex numbers
\mu
we have the estimate

0<|E_{\rho,\mu}(-t)|\leq\frac{C}{1+t},\hskip 9.24994ptt>0.

(4.6)

Let us get back to estimating the norm of the function
f_{j}^{1}
. By virtue of the estimate
(
4.5
), we have

||f^{1}_{j}||^{2}_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{2(\tau+1)}|\Psi_{n}|^{2}\leq C_{\Psi}<\infty.

(4.7)

Applying the same argument as above to the function
f_{j}^{2}
, we arrive at the inequality

||f^{2}_{j}||_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C||\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{n}\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx}||_{L_{2}\left(T^{N}\right)}.

(4.8)

Hence, by virtue of the orthonormality of the system
\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}
, we have

||f^{2}_{j}||^{2}_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq C\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\bigg|\frac{\varphi_{n}\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-A(n)T^{\rho})}\bigg|^{2}.

Further, applying the estimates (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain

||f^{2}_{j}||^{2}_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq\frac{C}{T^{\rho}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{2\tau}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{T^{\rho}}C_{\varphi}<\infty.

(4.9)

The estimates (
4.7
) and (
4.9
) imply the uniform convergence of series (
2.11
),defining the function
f(x)
. On the other hand, the sums in (
4.4
) and (
4.8
) converge under any permutation of their
terms, because these terms are mutually orthogonal. This implies the absolute convergence of
series (
2.11
).

Let us proceed to considering the series in (
2.10
) for the function

u
. To this end, using the definition
of the coefficients
f_{n}
, we study the following three sums:

u^{1}_{j}(x,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx},

(4.10)

u^{2}_{j}(x,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\Psi_{n}t^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})}e^{inx},

(4.11)

u^{3}_{j}(x,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})}{T^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)T^{\rho})}\,t^{\rho}E_{\rho,\rho+1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx}.

(4.12)

Let
|\alpha|=m
and
\delta>0
. We need to show that each of these sums converges uniformly and
absolutely in the domain
\mathbb{\mathbb{T}}^{N}\times[\delta,T]
after the operators
D^{\alpha}
and
D^{\rho}_{t}

are term-by-term applied to the sum.

We have

u^{1}_{j}(x,t)=(\hat{A}+I)^{-\tau-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau+1}\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx}.

Therefore, by the lemma, one has the estimate

||D^{\alpha}u^{1}_{j}||_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}=||D^{\alpha}(\hat{A}+I)^{-\tau-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau+1}\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx}||_{C\left(T^{N}\right)}\leq

\leq C||\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\ (A(n)+1)^{\tau+1}\varphi_{n}E_{\rho,\ 1}(-\ A(n)t^{\rho})e^{inx}||_{L_{2}(\left(T^{N}\right)}.

(4.13)

Using the orthonormality of the system
\{(2\pi)^{-N/2}e^{inx}\}

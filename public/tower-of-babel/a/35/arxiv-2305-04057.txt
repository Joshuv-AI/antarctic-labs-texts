Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits and topological pressure
Fangzhou Cai

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the notion of Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits and use it to study topological pressure. We prove that the topological pressure of a dynamical system can be computed by measuring the Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits complexity of the shift map on the potential function, which extends Barge and Swanson’s result on entropy and pseudo-orbits [ 9 ] .



1.
Introduction

By a
topological dynamical system
(TDS for short) we mean a pair
(X,T)
where
X
is a compact metric space with metric
d
and
T:X\to X
is a continuous map.
Topological entropy was introduced originally by Adler, Konheim and McAndrew
[
1
]
in 1965. Later, Dinaburg
[
2
]
and Bowen
[
3
]
gave several equivalent
definitions by using separated and spanning sets. Bowen also gave a
characterization of dimension type for topological entropy, which generalized the definition on non-compact sets
[
4
]
. Topological entropy is an important dynamical invariant of topological conjugacy. Roughly speaking, Topological entropy measures the maximal exponential growth rate of different forward orbits for a topological dynamical system.

In the years since their development by Bowen
[
5
]
and Conley
[
7
]
, pseudo-orbits have
proved to be a powerful tool in dynamical systems.
A remarkable result by Misiurewicz

[
8
]
stated that the topological entropy can be computed by measuring the exponential growth rate of the
numbers of pseudo-orbits. Later Barge and Swanson
[
9
]
gave a similar result for periodic pseudo-orbits. In
[
10
]
, Hurley
considered pseudo-orbits for inverse images and showed that the point entropy of pseudo-orbits is in fact equal to the topological entropy.
In
[
11
]
, Yan and Zeng obtained similar results for continuous maps on compact uniform spaces. Recently, Cheng and Li used pseudo-orbits to characterize the notion of scaled pressure
[
23
]
.

As a non-trivial and natural generalization of topological entropy, the notion of topological pressure was introduced in dynamical systems by Ruelle
[
21
]
inspired by the theory of Gibbs states in statistical mechanics. Later Walters
[
22
]
extended it to general topological dynamical systems. From a viewpoint of dimension theory, Pesin and Pitskel
[
18
]
generalized Bowen’s definition of
topological entropy and defined the
topological pressure on non-compact sets.
Topological pressure is a fundamental notion in thermodynamic
formalism and constitute the main components
of the thermodynamic formalism (
[
6
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
).

In 2017, Kwietniak and Łacka
[
24
]
introduced the Feldman-Katok
metric as the topological counterpart of edit
distance
\bar{f}
which was introduced by Feldman
[
12
]
to study loosely Bernoulli system (see also
[
13
,
14
]
). In recent years, the Feldman-Katok
metric proved to be a useful tool in dynamical systems. In
[
25
]
, the authors used it to characterize zero
entropy loosely Bernoulli systems and presented a
purely topological characterization of their topological models. In
[
15
]
, Downarowicz, Kwietniak and Łacka introduced
the idea of
\bar{f}
-pseudometric to finite-valued
stationary stochastic processes and used it to study the entropy rate. In
[
16
]
,
Cai and Li gave
entropy formulas defined by Feldman-Katok metric.

The advantage to use Feldman-Katok metric is that it allows time delay by ignoring the synchronization of points in orbits with only order preserving required. Inspired by previous nice works, in this paper, we bring the idea from the definition of Feldman-Katok metric into pseudo-orbits and attempt to use it to study topological pressure.

The paper is organized as follows: We first introduce the notion of Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits. Then we give a formula for topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits. We also consider scaled pressure and show that in
[
23
, Theorem F]
, the Lipschitz continuity of
T
is redundant.
Finally we introduce and study topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok metric.

2.
Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits and topological pressure

In this section we introduce the notion of Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits and use it to study topological pressure. Our idea is from the definition of Feldman-Katok metric, which allows time delay in orbits with only order preserving required.

2.1.
Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS
and
\alpha>0
. Recall that we say
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in X^{\mathbb{N}}
is an
\alpha
-pseudo-orbit
if

d(Tx_{i},x_{i+1})\leq\alpha,i=0,1\ldots,n\ldots.

Denote by
PO_{\alpha}(X,T)
or simply
PO_{\alpha}
the set of
\alpha
-pseudo-orbits.

We bring time delay and “jump” in classical pseudo-orbits and give the definition of Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\delta>0.

We say
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in X^{n}
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
if there exist
(1-\delta)n<k\leq n
and

0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n-1,

0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq n-1

such that

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{t}}(x_{i_{t}}),x_{i_{t+1}})\leq\alpha,t=1,\ldots,k-1.

Remark 2.2
.

In the definition above, if we choose
k=n
and
i_{1}=j_{1}=0,\ldots,i_{n}=j_{n}=n-1
, we get the definition of
\alpha
-pseudo-chain (see
[
9
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS,

\delta>0
and
N_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
. We say
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in X^{\mathbb{N}}
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-orbit of density
1-\delta
controlled by
N_{\delta}
if there exist a sequence
0\leq s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{i}<\ldots
in
\mathbb{N}
with

s_{0}\leq N_{\delta},s_{i+1}-s_{i}\leq\ N_{\delta},i=0,1,\ldots,

such that for all
m>n
,
(x_{s_{n}},x_{s_{n}+1},\ldots,x_{s_{m}-1})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
.

Denote by
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
the set of
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-orbit of density
1-\delta
controlled by
N_{\delta}
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

N_{\delta}
is needed to control the length, for we must avoid the situation that the first finite components of a point
{\bf x}
can be chosen arbitrary, since in this situation we obtain a dense set in
X^{\mathbb{N}}
.

(2)

It is easy to see that
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
contains
PO_{\alpha}(X,T)
by definition.

(3)

We can see points with the following form

(x,y_{1},T^{2}x,y_{3},\ldots,T^{2n}x,y_{2n+1},\ldots)

are in
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
for
\alpha>0,\delta>\frac{1}{2}
and
N_{\delta}\geq 4.
Hence there is an essential difference between
FK
-pseudo-orbits and pseudo-orbits.

(4)

If
\delta N_{\delta}<1
, the choice of
k
in the definition is only
n
, hence to avoid triviality, we can make
\delta N_{\delta}
large enough by choosing appropriate
N_{\delta}
.

(5)

It is easy to see that
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
is monotonically increasing with respect to
\alpha
. But
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
may not monotonous with respect to
\delta
.

In the sequel we fix
\{N_{\delta}:\delta>0\}\subset\mathbb{N}
.

We simply write
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
instead of
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}(X,T,N_{\delta})
.

The following proposition shows that
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
has a good structure.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS and
\alpha,\delta>0
. Then
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
is closed in
X^{\mathbb{N}}
and invariant under the shift
\sigma
.

Proof.

Since there is no restriction on
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{s_{0}-1})
in the definition of
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
, it is easy to see that
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
is invariant under
\sigma
.
Now we prove
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
is closed.

Let
{\bf x}^{h}\in FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
with
{\bf x}^{h}\rightarrow{\bf x}
.
By definition there exist a sequence
s_{0}^{h}<s_{1}^{h}<\ldots<s_{i}^{h}<\ldots
with

s_{0}^{h}\leq\ N_{\delta},s_{i+1}^{h}-s_{i}^{h}\leq N_{\delta},\ i=0,1,\ldots,

such that for all
m>n
,
(x_{s_{n}^{h}}^{h},x_{s_{n}^{h}+1}^{h},\ldots,x_{s_{m}^{h}-1}^{h})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
. We can find
s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{i}<\ldots
with

s_{0}\leq N_{\delta},s_{i+1}-s_{i}\leq N_{\delta},i=0,1,\ldots,

such that for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist infinite
h
satisfy

(s_{0}^{h},s_{1}^{h},\ldots,s_{l}^{h})=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}).

Fix
m>n
. Now we prove
(x_{s_{n}},x_{s_{n}+1},\ldots,x_{s_{m}-1})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
. For
m
, there exist infinite
h
with

(s_{0}^{h},s_{1}^{h},\ldots,s_{m}^{h})=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}).

For such
h
, since

(x_{s_{n}^{h}}^{h},x_{s_{n}^{h}+1}^{h},\ldots,x_{s_{m}^{h}-1}^{h})=(x_{s_{n}}^{h},x_{s_{n}+1}^{h},\ldots,x_{s_{m}-1}^{h})

is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
, there exist
k_{h}
with

(1-\delta)(s_{m}-s_{n})<k_{h}\leq(s_{m}-s_{n})

and

0\leq i_{1}^{h}<i_{2}^{h}<\ldots<i_{k_{h}}^{h}\leq s_{m}-s_{n}-1,

0\leq j_{1}^{h}<j_{2}^{h}<\ldots<j_{k_{h}}^{h}\leq s_{m}-s_{n}-1,

such that

d(T^{j_{t+1}^{h}-j_{t}^{h}}x_{i_{t}^{h}+s_{n}}^{h},x_{i_{t+1}^{h}+s_{n}}^{h})\leq\alpha,t=1,\ldots,k_{h}-1.

We can choose some
k
with

(1-\delta)(s_{m}-s_{n})<k\leq(s_{m}-s_{n})

and

0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq s_{m}-s_{n}-1,

0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq s_{m}-s_{n}-1,

such that there exist infinite
h
satisfy
k_{h}=k
and

(i_{1}^{h},i_{2}^{h},\ldots,i_{k}^{h})=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}),

(j_{1}^{h},j_{2}^{h},\ldots,j_{k}^{h})=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{k}).

For such
h
we have

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{t}}x_{i_{t}+s_{n}}^{h},x_{i_{t+1}+s_{n}}^{h})\leq\alpha,t=1,\ldots,k-1.

Let
h\to\infty
we have

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{t}}x_{i_{t}+s_{n}},x_{i_{t+1}+s_{n}})\leq\alpha,t=1,\ldots,k-1.

Hence
(x_{s_{n}},x_{s_{n}+1},\ldots,x_{s_{m}-1})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
.
∎

2.2.
Topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits

In this subsection we use Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits to study topological pressure.

First let us recall some basic concepts.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS. For
x,y\in X,

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
, set

d_{n}(x,y)=\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}d(T^{i}x,T^{i}y)

be the Bowen metric.
We say
E\subset X
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set of
X
if for each
x\neq y\in E
,
d_{n}(x,y)>\epsilon
.
We say
F\subset X
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-spanning set of
X
if for every
x\in X
there is
y\in F
with
d_{n}(x,y)\leq\epsilon.

Let
f\in C(X)
, define

sp_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)=\inf\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:F\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-spanning\ set \ of }X\},

sr_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)=\sup\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:E\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated\ set \ of }X\}.

It is known that the topological pressure of
(X,T)
with potential
f
is
defined by

\begin{split}P(X,T,f)&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log sp_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log sr_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f).\end{split}

For
{\bf x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots),{\bf y}=(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots)
in
X^{\mathbb{N}},
the metric
\tilde{d}
on
X^{\mathbb{N}}
is defined by

\tilde{d}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{d(x_{i},y_{i})}{2^{i}}.

For
Y\subset X^{\mathbb{N}}
, we say
E\subset Y
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set of
Y
if for each
{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}
in
E
, there exists
0\leq i\leq n-1
such that
d(x_{i},y_{i})>\epsilon
.
We say
F\subset Y
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-spanning set of
Y
if for every
{\bf x}\in Y,
there is
{\bf y}\in F
with
d(x_{i},y_{i})<\epsilon,0\leq i\leq n-1.

For
\alpha,\delta>0
and
f\in C(X)
, define

\begin{split}&FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}(X,T,f)=\\
&\inf\{\sum_{{\bf x}\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:F\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-spanning\ set \ of }FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}\},\end{split}

\begin{split}&FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}(X,T,f)=\\
&\sup\{\sum_{{\bf x}\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:E\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated\ set \ of }FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}\}.\end{split}

We simply write
FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}
and
FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}
if there is no confusion.

Similar as the definition in
[
9
]
, we
define

\begin{split}PFKPO(X,T,f,\epsilon,\delta)&=\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}(X,T,f)\\
&=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}(X,T,f).\end{split}

The limit exists since
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
is monotonically increasing with respect to
\alpha
. Now we define

PFKPO(X,T,f,\epsilon)=\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}PFKPO(X,T,f,\epsilon,\delta).

Note that here
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
may not monotonous with respect to
\delta
, hence “
\varlimsup\limits_{\delta\to 0}
” can not be replaced by “
\inf\limits_{\delta>0}
” or “
\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}
”.

It is easy to see
PFKPO(X,T,f,\epsilon)
is non-increasing with respect to
\epsilon
. We define

PFKPO(X,T,f)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}PFKPO(X,T,f,\epsilon)

be the
topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits.

The next lemma shows that we can use separated set in the definition above.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS and
f\in C(X).
Then

PFKPO(X,T,f)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}(X,T,f).

Proof.

By definition it is easy to see

PFKPO(X,T,f)\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}.

Now we prove the opposite direction.
Let
\alpha,\delta>0.
For
\epsilon>0,
set

L(\epsilon)=\sup_{|x-y|\leq\epsilon}|f(x)-f(y)|.

Assume that
E
be an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set and
F
be an
(n,\frac{\epsilon}{2})
-spanning set of
FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}
. The map
\phi:E\to F
is defined by choosing for each
{\bf x}\in E,
some point
\phi({\bf x})\in F
with

d(x_{i},\phi({\bf x})_{i})\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Then
\phi
is injective and

\begin{split}\sum_{{\bf y}\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(y_{i})}&\geq\sum_{{\bf x}\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(\phi({\bf x})_{i})}\\
&=\sum_{{\bf x}\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(\phi({\bf x})_{i})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}\\
&\geq e^{-nL(\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\sum_{{\bf x}\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}.\end{split}

Thus

\begin{split}&\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}\\
&\leq\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsp_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{2};\alpha,\delta}+L(\frac{\epsilon}{2}).\end{split}

Let
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsr_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}\leq PFKPO(X,T,f).

∎

Now we show that the topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits is equal to the classical topological pressure. We have following theorem:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS and
f\in C(X)
. Then

PFKPO(X,T,f)=P(X,T,f).

Proof.

By definition we have
PFKPO(X,T,f)\geq P(X,T,f).
Now we prove the opposite direction.

Fix
\epsilon>0.
Let
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}X_{i}
with
diamX_{i}<\frac{\epsilon}{4},i=1,\ldots,l.
Choose some
z_{i}\in X_{i}
.

For
\delta<\frac{\epsilon}{2},n>\max\{\frac{N_{\delta}}{\delta},\frac{2diamX}{\delta}\},m>3n,
let
F
be an
(n+m,\frac{\delta}{4})
-spanning set of
X
. We can find

0<\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\ldots<\delta_{2(n+m)}=\frac{\delta}{4}

such that for every
\delta_{k},k=0,1,\ldots,2(n+m)-1
,

d(x,y)<\delta_{k}\Rightarrow d(T^{i}x,T^{i}y)<\delta_{k+1},i=0,\ldots,2(n+m)-1.

Let
\alpha<\min\{\delta_{0},\delta_{k+1}-\delta_{k}:k=0,1,\ldots,2(n+m)-1\}.

For
x\in F,
let
F_{x}
be the collection of points with following form:

(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{i_{1}-1},T^{j_{1}}x,y_{i_{1}+1},\ldots,y_{i_{k}-1},T^{j_{k}}x,y_{i_{k}+1},\ldots,y_{m+n-1},z_{1},z_{1},\ldots),

where

(1-3\delta)(n+m)<k\leq(n+m),

0\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n+m-1,

0\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq n+m-1

and
y_{i}\in\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}\}.

We have

|F_{x}|\leq\sum_{(1-3\delta)(n+m)<k\leq n+m}(C_{n+m}^{k})^{2}l^{n+m-k}.

Claim
:

FKPO_{\alpha,\delta}\subset\bigcup_{x\in F}\bigcup_{{\bf y}\in F_{x}}B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon),

where
B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)
denotes the open
\epsilon
-ball of
{\bf y}
with respect to
\tilde{d}_{m}
.

proof of claim:
For
{\bf x}\in FKPO_{\alpha,\delta},

by definition there exist
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{i}<\ldots
with

s_{0}\leq N_{\delta},s_{i+1}-s_{i}\leq N_{\delta},i=0,1,\ldots,

such that for all
b>a
,
(x_{s_{a}},x_{s_{a}+1},\ldots,x_{s_{b}-1})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo-chain of density
1-\delta
.
Let
b
satisfy
s_{b}\leq n+m
and
s_{b+1}>m+n
. It is easy to check

s_{b}-s_{0}>(1-2\delta)(n+m).

Since
(x_{s_{0}},x_{s_{0}+1},\ldots,x_{s_{b}-1})
is a
FK
-
\alpha
-pseudo chain of density
1-\delta
, we can find
k
with

(1-3\delta)(n+m)\leq(1-\delta)(s_{b}-s_{0})<k\leq s_{b}-s_{0}\leq n+m

and

s_{0}\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq s_{b}-1\leq n+m-1,

0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq s_{b}-s_{0}-1\leq n+m-1

such that

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{t}}(x_{i_{t}}),x_{i_{t+1}})\leq\alpha,t=1,\ldots,k-1.

Now we prove

(1)

d(T^{j_{t}-j_{1}}(x_{i_{1}}),x_{i_{t}})<\delta_{2(t-2)}<\frac{\delta}{4},t=2,\ldots,k.

We prove by induction. When
t=2
it is clear. If (
1
) holds for
t
, we have

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{1}}(x_{i_{1}}),T^{j_{t+1}-j_{t}}x_{i_{t}})<\delta_{2(t-2)+1},

hence

d(T^{j_{t+1}-j_{1}}(x_{i_{1}}),x_{i_{t+1}})<\delta_{2(t-2)+1}+\alpha\leq\delta_{2(t-1)}.

By induction (
1
) is proved.

Since
F
is an
(n+m,\frac{\delta}{4})
-spanning set of
X
, there exists
x\in F
such that

d(T^{h}x,T^{h}x_{i_{1}})<\frac{\delta}{4},h=0,\ldots,n+m-1.

Hence

d(T^{j_{t}-j_{1}}x,x_{i_{t}})<\frac{\delta}{2},t=1,\ldots,k.

We assume
x_{i}\in X_{h_{i}},i=0,1,\ldots,m+n-1.

Note that
\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2diamX}
. We have
{\bf x}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon),

where

{\bf y}=(z_{h_{0}},z_{h_{1}},\ldots,z_{h_{i_{1}-1}},x,z_{i_{1}+1},\ldots,z_{h{{}_{i_{k}-1}}},T^{j_{k}-j_{1}}x,z_{h_{i_{k}+1}},\ldots,z_{h_{m+n-1}},z_{1},z_{1},\ldots).

The claim is proved.

Denote

\begin{split}&Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta)=\\
&\inf\{\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:\mathcal{A}\text{ is\ a\ finite\ subcover\ of\ }\vee_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{-i}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}\text{ covers }FKPO_{\beta,\delta}\},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}
denote the cover of
(X^{\mathbb{N}},\tilde{d})
consists of all open
\epsilon
-balls.

By claim we have

Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha,\delta)\leq\sum_{x\in F}\sum_{{\bf y}\in F_{x}}\sup_{{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})}.

Note that
\alpha
dependents on
m
and
\delta
.

For
x\in F
, if
{\bf y}\in F_{x}
and
{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)
we have
d(y_{i},z_{i})<\epsilon,i=0,\ldots,m-1.

Note that
m>3n
, we have

(1-3\delta)(n+m)-n>(1-4\delta)m

and

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)\leq mL(\epsilon)+2||f||4\delta m,

where

L(\epsilon)=\sup_{|x-y|\leq\epsilon}|f(x)-f(y)|.

Hence

\begin{split}\sum_{{\bf y}\in F_{x}}\sup_{{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})}\leq|F_{x}|e^{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)}e^{(mL(\epsilon)+8||f||\delta m)}.\end{split}

Note that

\begin{split}|F_{x}|&\leq\sum_{(1-3\delta)(n+m)<k\leq n+m}(C_{n+m}^{k})^{2}l^{n+m-k}\\
&\leq 3\delta(n+m)(C_{n+m}^{[3\delta(n+m)]})^{2}l^{3\delta(n+m)}.\end{split}

We have

\begin{split}Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha,\delta)&\leq 3\delta(n+m)(C_{n+m}^{[3\delta(n+m)]})^{2}l^{3\delta(n+m)}\\
&\cdot e^{(mL(\epsilon)+8||f||\delta m)}\sum_{x\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)}\\
&\leq 3\delta(n+m)(C_{n+m}^{[3\delta(n+m)]})^{2}l^{3\delta(n+m)}\sum_{x\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-1}f(T^{i}x)}\\
&\cdot e^{(mL(\epsilon)+8||f||\delta m)+n||f||}.\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}&\inf_{m}\inf_{\alpha>0}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha,\delta)\\
&\leq(L(\epsilon)+8||f||\delta)+\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log(C_{n+m}^{[3\delta(n+m)]})^{2}+3\delta\log l+\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log sp_{m,\frac{\delta}{4}}.\end{split}

It is easy to see that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta>0
, we have

Cov_{m+n,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta)\leq Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta)Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta).

Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta)=\inf_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta).

It is easy to see that

FKPOsr_{m,2\epsilon;\beta,\delta}\leq Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\beta,\delta).

Hence

\begin{split}\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log FKPOsr_{m,2\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}&\leq\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha,\delta)\\
&\leq\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\inf_{m}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha,\delta)\\
&\leq\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log sp_{m,\frac{\delta}{4}}+L(\epsilon)\\
&=P(X,T,f)+L(\epsilon).\end{split}

Let
\epsilon\to 0,
we have
PFKPO(X,T,f)\leq P(X,T,f).

∎

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

It is easy to see that

\begin{split}P(X,T,f)&\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\inf_{\delta>0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta}\\
&\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup_{\delta\to 0}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKPOsp_{n,\epsilon;\alpha,\delta},\end{split}

we can easily establish a similar result if we replace “
\varlimsup\limits_{\delta\to 0}
” by “
\inf\limits_{\delta>0}
” in the definition of
PFKPO(X,T,f).

(2)

The set of periodic Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits contains the set of periodic pseudo-orbits and is contained in the set of Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits, hence the result with respect to periodic Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits can be directly obtained.

2.3.
Pseudo-orbits and scaled pressure

In
[
23
]
, the authors studied scaled pressure with respect to pseudo-orbits. In this subsection we show that in
[
23
, Theorem F]
, the condition that
T
is Lipschitz continuous is redundant.

First we recall some concepts.

A function
S:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
is called a
scale function
if for any
\lambda\in(0,\infty),

\lim\limits_{x\to 0}\frac{S(\lambda x)}{S(x)}=1.

Let
f\in C(X)
and
S
be a scale function, define

Spa_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f,S)=\inf\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:F\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-spanning\ set \ of }X\},

Sep_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f,S)=\sup\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:E\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated\ set \ of }X\}.

Scaled pressure of
(X,T)
with respect to
f
and
S
is defined by

\begin{split}Sdim(X,T,f,S)&=\varlimsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Spa_{n,\epsilon}}{S(\epsilon)}\\
&=\varlimsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Sep_{n,\epsilon}}{S(\epsilon)}.\end{split}

Denote by

PPO_{n,\alpha}(X,T)=\{{\bf x}\in PO_{\alpha}(X,T):x_{n+k}=x_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}

the
n
-period
\alpha
pseudo-orbits.

Define

\begin{split}&POSpa_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(X,T,f,S)(resp.\ PPOSpa_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(X,T,f,S))=\\
&\inf\{\sum_{{\bf x}\in F}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:F\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-spanning\ set \ of }PO_{\alpha}(resp.\ PPO_{n,\alpha})\},\end{split}

\begin{split}&POSep_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(X,T,f,S)(resp.\ PPOSep_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(X,T,f,S))=\\
&\sup\{\sum_{{\bf x}\in E}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:E\text{ is\ an }(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated\ set \ of }PO_{\alpha}(resp.\ PPO_{n,\alpha})\}.\end{split}

Define
scaled pressure with respect to pseudo-orbits (resp. periodic pseudo-orbits)
by

\begin{split}&PSdim(X,T,f,S)(resp.\ PPSdim(X,T,f,S))\\
&=\varlimsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log POSpa_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(resp.\ PPOSpa_{n,\epsilon,\alpha})}{S(\epsilon)}\\
&=\varlimsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log POSep_{n,\epsilon,\alpha}(resp.\ PPOSep_{n,\epsilon,\alpha})}{S(\epsilon)}.\end{split}

Now we show that in
[
23
, Theorem F]
, the condition that
T
is Lipschitz continuous is redundant.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS,
f\in C(X)
and
S
be a scale function. Then

Sdim(X,T,f,S)=PSdim(X,T,f,S).

If in addition
f\geq 0
and
S
is non-increasing, then

Sdim(X,T,f,S)=PPSdim(X,T,f,S).

Proof.

By definition we have
Sdim(X,T,f,S)\leq PSdim(X,T,f,S).
Now we prove the opposite direction.

Fix
\epsilon>0.
Let
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}X_{i}
with
diamX_{i}<\frac{\epsilon}{4},i=1,\ldots,l.
Choose some
z_{i}\in X_{i}
.

For
n>\frac{2diamX}{\epsilon}
and
m
, let
F
be an
(n+m,\frac{\epsilon}{4})
-spanning set of
X
. We can find

0<\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}<\ldots<\delta_{2(n+m)}=\frac{\epsilon}{4}

such that for every
\delta_{k},k=0,1,\ldots,2(n+m)-1
,

d(x,y)<\delta_{k}\Rightarrow d(T^{i}x,T^{i}y)<\delta_{k+1},i=0,\ldots,2(n+m)-1.

Let

\alpha<\min\{\delta_{0},\delta_{k+1}-\delta_{k}:k=0,1,\ldots,2(n+m)-1\}.

Let

\tilde{F}=\{(x,Tx,\ldots,T^{i}x,\ldots,T^{m+n-1}x,z_{1},z_{1},\ldots):x\in F\}.

We have
\bigcup_{{\bf y}\in\tilde{F}}B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)
covers
PO_{\alpha}
.
Denote

\begin{split}&Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta)=\\
&\inf\{\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})}:\mathcal{A}\text{ is\ a\ finite\ subcover\ of\ }\vee_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{-i}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}\text{ covers }PO_{\beta}\},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}
denote the cover of
(X^{\mathbb{N}},\tilde{d})
consists of all open
\epsilon
-balls.
We have

Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha)\leq\sum_{{\bf y}\in\tilde{F}}\sup_{{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})}.

Here
\alpha
dependents on
m
.

For
x\in F,{\bf y}=(x,Tx,\ldots,T^{i}x,\ldots,T^{m+n-1}x,z_{1},z_{1},\ldots)\in\tilde{F}
and
{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)
, we have
d(T^{i}x,z_{i})<\epsilon,i=0,\ldots,m-1.

It follows that

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)\leq mL(\epsilon),

where

L(\epsilon)=\sup_{|x-y|\leq\epsilon}|f(x)-f(y)|.

Hence

\begin{split}\sum_{{\bf y}\in\tilde{F}}\sup_{{\bf z}\in B_{\tilde{d}_{m}}({\bf y},\epsilon)}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(z_{i})}\leq\sum_{x\in F}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)}e^{S(\epsilon)mL(\epsilon)}.\end{split}

We have

\begin{split}Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha)&\leq e^{S(\epsilon)mL(\epsilon)}\sum_{x\in F}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(T^{i}x)}\\
&\leq e^{S(\epsilon)(mL(\epsilon)+n||f||)}\sum_{x\in F}e^{S(\epsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-1}f(T^{i}x)}.\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}\inf_{m}\inf_{\alpha>0}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha)\leq S(\epsilon)L(\epsilon)+\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log Spa_{m,\frac{\epsilon}{4}}.\end{split}

It is easy to see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta)=\inf_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log Cov_{n,\epsilon}(\beta)

and

POSep_{m,2\epsilon,\beta}\leq Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\beta).

Hence

\begin{split}\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log POSep_{m,2\epsilon,\alpha}&\leq\inf_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha)\\
&\leq\inf_{\alpha>0}\inf_{m}\frac{1}{m}\log Cov_{m,\epsilon}(\alpha)\\
&\leq\varlimsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log Spa_{m,\frac{\epsilon}{4}}+S(\epsilon)L(\epsilon).\end{split}

It follows that

\frac{\inf\limits_{\alpha>0}\varlimsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log POSep_{m,2\epsilon,\alpha}}{S(2\epsilon)}\leq\frac{S(\frac{\epsilon}{4})}{S(2\epsilon)}\frac{\varlimsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log Spa_{m,\frac{\epsilon}{4}}}{S(\frac{\epsilon}{4})}+\frac{S(\epsilon)}{S(2\epsilon)}L(\epsilon).

Let
\epsilon\to 0,
we have
Sdim(X,T,f,S)\geq PSdim(X,T,f,S).

If in addition
f\geq 0
and
S
is non-increasing, then

Sdim(X,T,f,S)=PPSdim(X,T,f,S)

just follows from the discussion in
[
23
, Theorem F]
.
∎

3.
Topological pressure for Feldman-Katok metric

In this section we study topological pressure for Feldman-Katok metric.

First we recall the definition of Feldman-Katok metric.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
. For
x,y\in X
, we define an
(n,\delta)
-match of
x
and
y
to be an order
preserving (i.e.
\pi(i)<\pi(j)
whenever
i<j
) bijection
\pi:D(\pi)\rightarrow R(\pi)
such that
D(\pi),R(\pi)\subset\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}

and for every
i\in D(\pi)
we have
d(T^{i}x,T^{\pi(i)}y)<\delta
. Let
|\pi|
be the cardinality of
D(\pi)
. We set

\bar{f}_{n,\delta}(x,y)=1-\frac{\max\{|\pi|:\pi\text{ is an }(n,\delta)\text{-match of }x\text{ and }y\}}{n}

Define the Feldman-Katok metric by

d_{FK_{n}}(x,y)=\inf\{\delta>0:\bar{f}_{n,\delta}(x,y)<\delta\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
,
We say a subset
E\subset X
is a
FK
-
(n,\epsilon)
-
spanning set of
X
if for any
x\in X
, there exists
y\in E
with
d_{FK_{n}}(x,y)\leq\epsilon.

We say a subset
F\subset X
is a
FK
-
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set of
X
if for
x\neq y\in F,

d_{FK_{n}}(x,y)>\epsilon.

For
f\in C(X)
, define

FKsp_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)=\inf\{\sum_{x\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:F\text{\ is\ a\ }FK\text{-}(n,\epsilon)\text{-spanning\ set\ of}\ X\},

FKsr_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)=\sup\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}:E\text{\ is\ a\ }FK\text{-}(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated\ set\ of}\ X\}.

Define the
topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok metric
by

PFK(X,T,f)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKsp_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f).

Similar as Lemma
2.6
, we can use separated set in the definition above.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS and
f\in C(X).
Then

\begin{split}PFK(X,T,f)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKsr_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f).\end{split}

Now we prove the topological pressure with respect to Feldman-Katok metric is equal to the classical topological pressure.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS and
f\in C(X)
.
Then

PFK(X,T,f)=P(X,T,f).

Proof.

It is easy to see that
PFK(X,T,f)\leq P(X,T,f)
, we prove the opposite direction.

Fix
\epsilon>0.
Let
X=\cup_{i=1}^{l}X_{i}
with
diamX_{i}<\epsilon,i=1,\ldots,l.
For
\delta\leq\epsilon
, let
F
be a
FK
-
(n,\frac{\delta}{2})
-spanning set of
X
. Let
x\in F
and consider sets with following form

A_{0}\cap T^{-1}A_{1}\cap\ldots\cap T^{-(j_{1}-1)}A_{j_{1}-1}\cap T^{-j_{1}}B_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(T^{i_{1}}x)\cap T^{-(j_{1}+1)}A_{j_{1}+1}\cap\ldots

\cap T^{-(j_{k}-1)}A_{j_{k}-1}\cap T^{-j_{k}}B_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(T^{i_{k}}x)\cap T^{-(j_{k}+1)}A_{j_{k}+1}\ldots\cap T^{-(n-1)}A_{n-1},

where

(1-\frac{\delta}{2})n<k\leq n,

0\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n-1,

0\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq n-1

and

A_{i}\in\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{l}\}.

For every non-empty set with the form above, choose some point
y
in it.
Let
F^{\prime}_{x}
be the set consists of such
y
and let
F^{\prime}=\cup_{x\in F}F^{\prime}_{x}.
We will prove

F^{\prime}
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-spanning set.
For every
z\in X
, since
F
is a
FK
-
(n,\frac{\delta}{2})
-spanning set, there is some
x\in F
with
d_{FK_{n}}(x,z)<\frac{\delta}{2}
. Hence there exist

(1-\frac{\delta}{2})n<k\leq n,

0\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n-1,

0\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{k}\leq n-1

such that

d(T^{i_{h}}x,T^{j_{h}}z)<\frac{\delta}{2},h=1,\ldots,k.

Assume
T^{i}z\in X_{t_{i}},i=0,\ldots,n-1.
Then

X_{t_{0}}\cap\ldots\cap T^{-(j_{1}-1)}X_{t_{j_{1}-1}}\cap T^{-j_{1}}B_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(T^{i_{1}}x)\cap T^{-(j_{1}+1)}X_{t_{j_{1}+1}}\ldots\cap T^{-(n-1)}X_{t_{n-1}}\neq\emptyset

since
z
is in it. By the construction of
F^{\prime}
, there is
y\in F^{\prime}
in the set above. It is easy to see that
d_{n}(y,z)<\epsilon.
Hence
F^{\prime}
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-spanning set.

We have

\begin{split}sp_{n,\epsilon}(X,T,f)&\leq\sum_{y\in F^{\prime}}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}y)}\leq\sum_{x\in F}\sum_{y\in F^{\prime}_{x}}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}y)}\\
&\leq\sum_{x\in F}\sum_{y\in F^{\prime}_{x}}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}y)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}\\
&\leq\sum_{x\in F}\sum_{y\in F^{\prime}_{x}}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}e^{nL(\frac{\delta}{2})+n\delta||f||},\end{split}

where
L(\epsilon)=\sup\limits_{|x-y|\leq\epsilon}|f(x)-f(y)|.

Since

\begin{split}|F^{\prime}_{x}|\leq\sum_{(1-\frac{\delta}{2})n<k\leq n}(C^{k}_{n})^{2}l^{n-k}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}n(C^{[\frac{\delta}{2}n]}_{n})^{2}l^{\frac{\delta}{2}n},\end{split}

we have

sp_{n,\epsilon}\leq e^{(nL(\frac{\delta}{2})+n\delta||f||)}\frac{\delta}{2}n(C^{[\frac{\delta}{2}n]}_{n})^{2}l^{\frac{\delta}{2}n}\sum_{x\in F}e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^{i}x)}.

Hence we have

sp_{n,\epsilon}\leq e^{(nL(\frac{\delta}{2})+n\delta||f||)}\frac{\delta}{2}n(C^{[\frac{\delta}{2}n]}_{n})^{2}l^{\frac{\delta}{2}n}FKsp_{n,\frac{\delta}{2}}.

It follows that

\begin{split}\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log sp_{n,\epsilon}\leq L(\frac{\delta}{2})+\delta||f||+\frac{\delta}{2}\log l+\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}2\log C_{n}^{[\frac{\delta}{2}n]}+\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log FKsp_{n,\frac{\delta}{2}}.\end{split}

Let
\delta\to 0
, we have

\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log sp_{n,\epsilon}\leq PFK(X,T,f).

Let
\epsilon\to 0
, we get
P(X,T,f)\leq PFK(X,T,f).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Both Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits and Feldman-Katok metric allow appropriate time delay and error when computing topological pressure. Feldman-Katok pseudo-orbits contain pseudo-orbits hence contain real orbits, it approximate topological pressure from upper bound. On the other hand, Feldman-Katok metric is less than Bowen metric, it approximate topological pressure from lower bound.

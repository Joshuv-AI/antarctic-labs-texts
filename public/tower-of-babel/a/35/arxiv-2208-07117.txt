Topological Complexity of S 3 / Q 8 S^{3}/Q_{8} as fibrewise L-S category
Norio Iwase, Yuya Miyata

Abstract
. In 2010, M. Sakai and the first author showed that the topological complexity of a space X X coincides with the fibrewise unpointed L-S category of a pointed fibrewise space pr 1 : X × X → X \operatorname{pr}_{1}:X\times X\to X with the diagonal map Δ : X → X × X \Delta:X\to X\times X as its section. In this paper, we describe our algorithm how to determine the fibrewise L-S category or the Topological Complexity of a topological spherical space form. Especially, for S 3 / Q 8 S^{3}/Q_{8} where Q 8 Q_{8} is the quaternion group, we write a python code to realise the algorithm to determine its Topological Complexity.

1.
Intoroduction

Topological complexity was introduced in
[
4
]
by Michael Farber as a numerical homotopy invariant.
It attracts many authors including people working on similar homotopy invariant of Lusternik-Schnirelmann category, L-S category, for short.
Recently, many authors started to use a ‘normalized’ or a ‘reduced’ version of it including Farber himself.
In this paper, we use the symbol ‘
\operatorname{tc}
’ for the reduced version of it to distinguish from the original denoted by
\operatorname{TC}
.

The reduced version of topological complexity is defined as follows:
let
X
be a path-connected space,
\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}(X)=\operatorname{Map}([0,1],X)
and
\varpi:\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}(X)\to M\times M
the projection given by
\varpi(u)=(u(0),u(1))
.
Using a projection
p_{t}:\operatorname{\mathcal{P}}(X)\to X
defined by
p_{t}(u)=u(t)
,
t\in\{0,1\}
, we may write
\varpi=p_{0}\times p_{1}
.
Topological complexity of
X
, denoted by
\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
, is the least integer
n
such that there is an open covering
U_{0},\dots,U_{n}
of
M\times M
on each of which
\varpi
admits a section.
We remark that, if a subset
A\subset X
is contractible in
X
, there is a section of
\varpi
on
A
.
The definition reminds us a well-known homotopy invariant: L-S category of a space
X
, denoted by
\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)
, is the least integer
n
such that there is an open covering
U_{0},\dots,U_{n}
of
X
each of which is contractible in
X
.

We can verify the following relation among these invariants (see Farber
[
4
]
).

\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X)\leq\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X\times X)\leq 2\operatorname{cat}\hskip 0.59998pt(X).

More practically for an abelian group
R
, Farber introduced the zero-divisors ideal
I_{\varpi}(X;R)=\ker\Delta^{*}:H^{*}(X\times X;R)\to H^{*}(X;R)
and the zero-divisors cup-length
\operatorname{cup}_{\varpi}(X;R)
for a space
X

[
4
]
, and then the TC-weight
\operatorname{wgt}_{\varpi}(z;R)
for
z\in I_{\varpi}(X;R)
with Mark Grant
[
3
]
:

\operatorname{cup}_{\varpi}(X;R)\leq\operatorname{Max}\{\operatorname{wgt}_{\varpi}(z;R)\mid z\in I_{\varpi}(X;R)\smallsetminus\{0\}\}\leq\operatorname{tc}\hskip 0.59998pt(X).

In this paper, we adopt fibrewise method and skip the precise definition of the above notions.

Since the theory of topological complexity is growing and spreading rapidly, there are many open problems.
Among such problems, we are especially interested in the topological complexity of spherical space forms, and in particular, real projective spaces, aiming to give a natural and computational way to determine topological complexity, since Farber showed that the topological complexity of a real projective space coincides with its immersion dimension.

In this paper, we focus on the work of Kenso Fujii on the
K
-theory of a spherical space forms obtained as the orbit space of the unit sphere of
\mathbb{H}^{t}
, where
t
is a non-negative integer and
\mathbb{H}
is the set of all quaternionic numbers, by the diagonal action of the subgroup
Q_{8}
of
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
, represented as
Q_{8}=\langle a,b\ \mathstrut\vrule\hskip 0.1pt\ a^{4}\!=b^{4}\!=aba\bar{b}=1,\,b^{2}\!=a^{2}\rangle
, where
\bar{g}
stands for
g^{-1}
for any element
g\!\in\!Q_{8}
.

Our results are stated in §
2
and the proofs are in §
3
-
4
, but the actual calculation is done with computer.
Because of the size of the computer resources required, we gave up to use the usual method for topological complexity but the fibrewise L-S method by Iwase-Sakai
[
7
]
and Iwase-Sakai-Tsutaya
[
6
]
.
It results that we don’t need to calculate the bar resolution of
G\times G
but
G
fibrewise.
This significantly reduces the computer resources required, while the explicit answer to our equation is still too long to print out.
So we include the algorithm and python code in Appendix, instead.
We hope our method can be applied to more general cases.

We work in the convenient category
\mathcal{NG}

[

\mathbb{F}_{2}

Let us recall the James fibrewise theory.

Definition 2.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Fibrewise spaces and maps are defined as follows.

(1)

A fibrewise space is a tuple
(E,p,X)
consisting of spaces
E
and

with a map

A fibrewise map from a fibrewise space

\phi:F\to E

(\phi,f):(F,q,Y)\to(E,p,X)
.

(E,p,X)
is often denoted simply by
E
, and
(\phi,f)
by
\phi
.

(2)

A fibrewise pointed space is a pair
(E,s)
of a fibrewise space

and a section

of

, i.e,
p\hskip 1.0pt\text{\footnotesize$\circ$}\hskip 1.0pts=\operatorname{id}_{X}
.
A fibrewise pointed map from a fibrewise pointed space
(F,r)
to a fibrewise pointed space
(E,s)
is a fibrewise map
(\phi,f):(F,r)\to(E,s)
satisfying
\phi\hskip 1.0pt\text{\footnotesize$\circ$}\hskip 1.0ptr=s\hskip 1.0pt\text{\footnotesize$\circ$}\hskip 1.0ptf

(E,s)
is often denoted simply by

, and
(\phi,f)
by
\phi
.

For instance, we have a Borel construction
EG\times_{G}X
over

as a fibrewise space for a topological group

and a

, where we denote by

-space.
Further, for an adjoint action of

as a fibrewise pointed space over

, which is, in fact, a fibrewise group.

James has also introduced a fibrewise version of an ordinary cohomology as a direct summand of the oridinary cohomology of the total space.
From now on, we use a subscript

to indicate that the notion is ‘fibrewise notion’ over some base space, even if the base space is not the same as

For a fibrewise pointed space

Definition 2.2

(

For any map

(
[

]

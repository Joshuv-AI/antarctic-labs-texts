Abstract
. Grauert showed that it is possible to construct complete Kähler metrics on the complement of complex analytic sets in a domain of holomorphy. In this note, we study the holomorphic sectional curvatures of such metrics on the complement of a principal divisor in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . In addition, we also study how this metric and its holomorphic sectional curvature behaves when the corresponding principal divisors vary continuously.

1.
Introduction

According to Grauert
[
1
]
, it is possible to construct a complete Kähler metric on the complement of a complex analytic set in a domain of holomorphy. The purpose of this note is twofold. First, we study the holomorphic sectional curvatures of the metric that is obtained by this process on the complement of a principal divisor in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. The case
n=1
have been studied in detail in the last section. Secondly, we show that if
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
is a domain of holomorphy (or more generally, admits a complete Kähler metric) with
H^{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})=0
, then Grauert’s construction possesses an intrinsic continuity property when the principal divisors vary continuously in an appropriate sense.

To set things in context, recall that the set of divisors on a complex manifold
M
is

\mathcal{D}(M)=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}V_{i}:n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

where
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a locally finite system of irreducible analytic hypersurfaces in
M
and the support of a divisor
D\in\mathcal{D}(M)
is the union of the hypersurfaces
V_{i}
– this will be denoted by
|D|
. The set of non-negative divisors
\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)\subset\mathcal{D}(M)
consists of those divisors wherein
n_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in I
. The natural map

{\sf Div}:\mathcal{O}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)

defined by

{\sf Div}(f)=\sum_{i}\left({\rm ord}_{V_{i}}f\right)V_{i}

assigns a holomorphic function to the divisor defined by its zero set; here,
\{V_{i}:i\in I\}
are the irreducible components of
\mathcal{Z}(f)
, the zero set of a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}(M)
, and
{\rm ord}_{V_{i}}f
is the order of
V_{i}\subset\mathcal{Z}(f)
. The set of
principal divisors
arises as the image
{\sf Div}(\mathcal{O}(M))\subset\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)
and this will be denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{P}(M)
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}_{P}^{+}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be a principal divisor. Choose
f\in\cal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
{\sf Div}(f)=D
. Recall that the Grauert metric on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
(see
[
1
]
) is a conformal metric defined by

(1.1)

g=\Big(1+\left|w\right|^{2}u^{2}\big(|w|^{2}\big)\Big)\left|dw\right|^{2}

where
u:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the function
u(t)=(t-1)/(t\log{t})
. It was shown in
[
3
]
that the curvature of
g
approaches
-4
or
0
according as
|z|\rightarrow 0
or
+\infty
respectively, and is non-positive everywhere on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
. Consider the complete Kähler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
defined by

(1.2)

\phi(z,V)=f^{*}(g)(z,V)+|V|^{2}=\Big(1+\left|f(z)\right|^{2}u^{2}\big(|f(z)|^{2}\big)\Big)\left|df(z)(V)\right|^{2}+|V|^{2}

where
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

For
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, denote the holomorphic sectional curvature of
\phi
at
(p,V)
by
K(p,V)
. Set
K^{+}(p)
to be the supremum of
K(p,V)
as
V
varies in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a non-singular vector field
X
near

p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
, consider the restriction of
\phi
to the leaf of the foliation defined by
X
passing through
p
. Thus, we get a conformal metric on the germ of a Riemann surface containing
p
. Let
\cal{K}^{X}(p)
denote the Gaussian curvature of this metric at
p
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(D,f,\phi)
be as above on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.

(i)

If
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and
V\in{\rm Ker}(df(p))
, then
K(p,V)\leq 0
. In particular, if
{\rm Rank}(df(p))=0
, then
K^{+}(p)\leq 0
.

(ii)

If
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and
f_{z_{i}}(p)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}(p)\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
, then there exists a non-singular holomorphic vector field
X
near
p
such that
X(p)\notin{\rm Ker}(df(p))
and
\cal{K}^{X}(p)\leq 0
.

(iii)

If
p\in|D|
and
f_{z_{i}}(p)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}(p)\neq 0
, then there exists a non-singular holomorphic vector field
X
near
p
such that

\lim_{z\to p}\cal{K}^{X}(z)=-4<0

To summarize, for points away from the support of
D
, (i) shows that the curvature
K(p,X)
is non-positive along certain directions. On the other hand, though the conclusions of (ii) and (iii) are less precise, they show that the curvature of the restriction of
\phi
to the leaves of a certain foliation is again non-positive.

It is natural to identify a non-negative divisor with the current of integration over it, namely:

D(\alpha)=\langle\alpha,D\rangle=\Sigma_{j}m_{j}\int_{V_{j}}\alpha

for each smooth compactly supported
(n-1,n-1)-
form
\alpha
on
X
where
D=\Sigma_{j}m_{j}V_{j}
. Thus
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
can be considered as a subset of the dual space of
D^{(n-1,n-1)}(M)
, the space of compactly supported
(n-1,n-1)-
smooth forms on
M
. Therefore
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
inherits two natural topologies namely, the relative weak* topology and the relative strong topology.

Weak* Topology
: A net
\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
in
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
converges to
D_{0}\in\cal{D}^{+}(M)
iff for every compactly supported smooth
(n-1,n-1)-
form
\xi
on
M
,

\lim_{\alpha\in A}\int_{D_{\alpha}}\xi=\int_{D_{0}}\xi

Strong Topology
: A net
\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
in
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
converges to
D_{0}\in\cal{D}^{+}(M)
iff it converges in the weak* sense and if, moreover, the convergence is uniform on bounded sets in the space
D^{(n-1,n-1)}(M)
.

There is another topology on
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
introduced by Stoll
[
6
]
which is defined in function-theoretic terms:

Stoll’s Topology:
A net
\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
in
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
converges to
D_{0}\in\cal{D}^{+}(M)
iff there is an open cover
\cal{V}=\{V_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M
such that for each
\alpha\in A
, there is an
f_{j\alpha}\in\cal{O}(V_{j})
such that
{\sf Div}(f_{j\alpha})=D_{\alpha}|_{V_{j}}
and
f_{j\alpha}
converges uniformly on compacts in
V_{j}
to
f_{j0}\in\cal{O}(V_{j})
, where
{\sf Div}(f_{j0})=D_{0}|_{V_{j}}
.

Lupacciolu-Stout
[
5
]
have shown that on
\cal{D}^{+}(M)
, the above three topologies are equivalent. Using this equivalence they showed:
For a complex-analytic manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 1
satisfying
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})=0
and
H^{1}(M,\cal{O})=0
, there exist a continuous map
\psi:\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(M)\rightarrow\cal{O}(M)
such that
{\sf Div}(\psi(D))=D
for all
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(M)
. In addition, if
M
is a domain in a Stein manifold
N
satisfying
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})=0
, then there exist a continuous map
\psi:\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(M)\rightarrow\cal{O}(M)
such that
{\sf Div}(\psi(D))=D
for all
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(M)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain of holomorphy or more generally a domain that has a complete Kähler metric
\Phi_{U}
. Let
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)
be a principal divisor and
f\in\cal{O}(U)
be a holomorphic function such that
D={\sf Div}(f)
. Define a pseudometric on
U\setminus|D|
by
\tilde{\phi}_{D}:=f^{*}(g)
, where
g
is the Grauert metric on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore,
\phi_{D}=\tilde{\phi}_{D}+\Phi_{U}
is a complete Kähler metric on
U\setminus|D|
.

The following statements clarify the dependence of the Grauert metric and its curvature as a function of the divisor
D
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
be a domain as above, which also satisfies
H^{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})=0
. If
\{D_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)
is a sequence of non-negative principal divisors such that
D_{j}
converges to
D_{0}\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)
with respect to any of the equivalent topologies above, then

(i)

there exists a sequence of complete Kähler metrics
\{\phi_{j}\}
and
\phi_{0}
on
U\setminus|D_{j}|
and
U\setminus|D_{0}|
repectively such that
\phi_{j}
converges to
\phi_{0}
uniformly on compacts of
U\setminus|D_{0}|
.

(ii)

for a fixed complete Kähler metric on
U\setminus|D_{0}|
of the form
\xi=f^{*}(g)+\Phi_{U}
where
D_{0}={\sf Div}(f)
, we can choose a sequence of complete Kähler metrics
\{\xi_{j}\}
on
U\setminus|D_{j}|
such that
\xi_{j}
converges to
\xi
uniformly on compacts of
U\setminus|D_{0}|
.

Fix
p\in U\setminus|D_{j}|
and a non-singular holomorphic vector field
X
near
p
. For a complete Kähler metric
\phi_{j}
on
U\setminus|D_{j}|
, denote the Gaussian curvature of the metric
\phi_{j}
restricted to the leaves of foliation induced by
X
by
\cal{K}^{X}_{\phi_{j}}(q)
for points
q\in U\setminus|D_{j}|
near
p
. For
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
K_{\phi_{j}}(p,V)
denote the holomorphic sectional curvature of the metric
\phi_{j}
at
(p,V)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(U,\phi_{j},\phi_{0})
be as in the above theorem and fix
p\in U\setminus|D_{0}|
. Then

(i)

for a non-singular holomorphic vector field
X
near
p
, there exists a neighourhood
U_{p}
of
p
in
U\setminus|D_{0}|
such that
\cal{K}^{X}_{\phi_{j}}(q)\rightarrow\cal{K}^{X}_{\phi_{0}}(q)
for all
q\in U_{p}

(ii)

K_{\phi_{0}}(p,V)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{K_{\phi_{j}}(p,V)}
for all
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

Fix a non-negative principal divisor
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
f\in\cal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
{\sf Div}(f)=D
. The corresponding complete Kähler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
is given by:

\phi(z,V)=\Big(1+\left|f(z)\right|^{2}u^{2}\big(|f(z)|^{2}\big)\Big)\left|df(z)(V)\right|^{2}+|V|^{2}

where
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

For
(i)
, let
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
and let
X
be a non-singular holomorphic vector field near
p
such that
X_{p}=X(p)\in{\rm Ker}\big(df(p)\big)
. Note that since
n\geq 2
, we have dim
\Big({\rm Ker}\big(df(p)\big)\Big)\geq 2-1=1
. For a disc
B(0,\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{C}
, let
Z:B(0,\epsilon)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
be a holomorphic parametrization of a germ of a leaf of
X
through
p
, i.e.,

Z^{\prime}(T)=X\big(Z(T)\big)

for all
T\in B(0,\epsilon)
and
Z(0)=p
. We will write
Z_{T}
to denote
Z(T)
, and just
u
instead of
u\big(|f(Z_{T})|^{2}\big)
. Therefore

Z^{*}(\phi)(T)=\left\{\big(1+\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}u^{2}\big)\left|df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right|^{2}+\left|X(Z_{T})\right|^{2}\right\}|dT|^{2}

and this can be written as
Z^{*}(\phi)(T)=h(T)\left|dT\right|^{2}
, where

h(T)=\Big(\big(1+\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}u^{2}\big)\left|df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right|^{2}\Big)+\left|X(Z_{T})\right|^{2}.

Note that
\partial h(T)=\frac{\partial h(T)}{\partial T}=A_{1}(T)+A_{2}(T)+A_{3}(T)
, where

\displaystyle A_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\big(u^{2}+2\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}uu^{\prime}\big)\left|df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right|^{2}\left\langle df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big),f(Z_{T})\right\rangle,

\displaystyle A_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=\big(1+\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}u^{2}\big)\left\langle\partial\Big(df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\Big),df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle A_{3}(T)

\displaystyle=\left\langle dX(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big),X(Z_{T})\right\rangle.

Using the fact that
\overline{h(T)}=h(T)
, it can be checked that
\bar{\partial}h(T)=\overline{\partial h(T)}
. Also
A_{1}(0)=A_{2}(0)=\bar{\partial}A_{1}(0)=0
, and

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}A_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=P(T)+\Big(\big(1+\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}u^{2}\big)\left|\partial\Big(df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\Big)\right|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}A_{3}(T)

\displaystyle=\left|dX(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right|^{2}.

where
P(T)=\bar{\partial}\Big(1+\left|f(Z_{T})\right|^{2}u^{2}\Big)\left\langle\partial\Big(df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\Big),df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\right\rangle
satisfies
P(0)=0
.

Thus, we get that
h(0)=\left|X_{p}\right|^{2}
,
\partial h(0)=\left\langle dX(p)\big(X_{p}\big),X_{p}\right\rangle
,
\bar{\partial}h(0)=\left\langle X_{p},dX(p)\big(X_{p}\big)\right\rangle
and

\bar{\partial}\partial h(0)=\left|dX(p)\big(X_{p}\big)\right|^{2}+\Big(1+|f(p)|^{2}u^{2}(|f(p)|^{2})\Big)\left|\partial\Big(df(Z_{T})\big(X(Z_{T})\big)\Big)\big|_{T=0}\right|^{2}\geq\left|dX(p)\big(X_{p}\big)\right|^{2}

and this implies that

h(0)\bar{\partial}\partial h(0)-\partial h(0)\bar{\partial}h(0)\geq\left|X_{p}\right|^{2}\left|dX(p)(X_{p})\right|^{2}-\left|\left\langle dX(p)(X_{p}),X_{p}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\geq 0

by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Therefore, the Gaussian curvature of the leaves of the foliation induced by
X
at
p
is

\cal{K}^{X}(p)={-2}(h(0))^{-3}\left\{h(0)\bar{\partial}\partial h(0)-\partial h(0)\bar{\partial}h(0)\right\}\leq 0.

By
[
7
]
, there exist a non-singular vector field
Y
near
p
such that
Y(p)=V
and
K(p,V)=\cal{K}^{Y}(p)
. Since the above argument is true for all non-singular holomorphic vector fields near
p
which equal
V
at point
p
, it follows that

K(p,V)\leq 0.

Now, if
{\rm Rank}\big(df(p)\big)=0
at some
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
, then
{\rm Ker}\big(df(p)\big)=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and therefore
K(p,V)\leq 0
for all
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus

K^{+}(p)=\sup_{V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}{K(p,V)}\leq 0.

Note:

M
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
equipped with a metric
\phi

f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a non-constant holomorphic map and assume that
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is equipped with a metric
\psi
. Then the metric
\Phi:=f^{*}(\psi)+\phi

for all

and
V\in{\rm Ker}\big(df(p)\big)
.

Now let
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

and suppose that
f_{z_{1}}(p)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}(p)\neq 0
. Then
W(z)=\left(f(z),z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\right)

W^{-1}(w)=\left(\tilde{g}(w),w_{2},\ldots,w_{n}\right)

\left((W^{-1})^{*}\phi\right)(w,V)=(1+\left|w_{1}\right|^{2}u^{2}(|w_{1}|^{2}))\left((W^{-1})^{*}\left|df(z)(dz)\right|^{2}(w,V)\right)+(W^{-1})^{*}(|dz|^{2})(w,V)

\tilde{\phi}(w,V):=\left((W^{-1})^{*}\phi\right)(w,V)=\left(1+\left|w_{1}\right|^{2}u^{2}(|w_{1}|^{2})+\left|\tilde{g}_{w_{1}}(w)\right|^{2}\right).

\tilde{X}(w)\equiv V
near the point

X:=W^{*}(\tilde{X})

and

be a local parametrization of leaf of

passing through

. We get

Z^{*}(\tilde{\phi})(T)=\left(\left(1+\left|w_{1}+T\right|^{2}u^{2}\right)+\left|\tilde{g}_{w_{1}}(Z_{T})\right|^{2}\right)\left|dT\right|^{2}=\big(A(T)+B(T)\big)\left|dT\right|^{2}

where

and

.

For
(ii)
, suppose that
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus|D|
. Fix
w=W(p)
and observe that
A(T)|dT|^{2}
is the Grauert metric restricted in a neighourhood of

. It is straightforward to check that
\partial B(T)=\tilde{g}_{w_{1}w_{1}}(Z_{T})\overline{\tilde{g}_{w_{1}}(Z_{T})}

For

\cal{K}^{X}(z)=\cal{K}^{\tilde{X}}(W(z))=-2\left(\frac{(A+B)(0)\bar{\partial}\partial(A+B)(0)-\bar{\partial}(A+B)(0)\partial(A+B)(0)}{\left(A(0)+B(0)\right)^{3}}\right)=-2\frac{N(0)}{D(0)}

\lim_{z\to p}\cal{K}^{X}(z)=\lim_{w\to W(p)}\left(-2\frac{N(0)}{D(0)}\right)=\lim_{w\to W(p)}\left(-2\frac{N(0)}{\big(A(0)\big)^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{\big(A(0)\big)^{3}}{D(0)}\right)

gives

This completes the proof.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Since
H^{1}(U,\mathbb{Z})=0
,
[
5
]
shows that there exists a continuous map
\psi:\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)\rightarrow\cal{O}(U)
such that
{\sf Div}(\psi(D))=D
for all
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)
. Let
\Phi
denote the complete Kähler metric on
U
. Now for any
D\in\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)
and
f\in\cal{O}(U)
such that
{\sf Div}(f)=D
, we have the following complete Kähler metric on
U\setminus|D|

For

\phi_{0}=f_{0}^{*}(g)+\Phi

U\setminus|D_{j}|

U\setminus|D_{0}|

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{i,k=1}^{n}\left(1+\left|f_{j}(z)\right|^{2}u^{2}(|f_{j}(z)|^{2})\right)\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\overline{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial z_{k}}(z)}dz_{i}d\overline{z_{k}}+\Phi(z),

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(z)

f_{0}

\phi_{j}\to\phi_{0}

For
(ii)

\tilde{\psi}:\cal{D}^{+}_{P}(U)\rightarrow\cal{O}(U)

\tilde{f_{j}}\to\tilde{f_{0}}

{\sf Div}(\tilde{f_{j}})=D_{j}

5.
The case
n=1

For a non-constant holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, consider the complete Kähler metric on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{f=0\}
given by
\phi(z)=f^{*}(g)+|dz|^{2}
. We have

\phi(z)=h(z)|dz|^{2}=\left(|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\left(1+\left|f(z)\right|^{2}u^{2}(|f(z)|^{2})\right)+1\right)|dz|^{2}

Denote the Gaussian curvature of the metric
\phi
by
K
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(f,\phi,K)
be as above and
p\in\mathbb{C}
.

(i)

If
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{f=0\}
, then
K(p)\leq 0
. In addition, if
f^{\prime}(p)=0
then
K(p)=0
if and only if
f^{\prime\prime}(p)=0
.

(ii)

If
p\in\{f=0\}
, then

\lim_{z\to p}{K(z)}=-4

The proof relies on the following observations.

Lemma 5.2
.

For an integer
k\geq 1
, consider the metric

\phi_{k}(z)=k^{2}\left|z\right|^{2(k-1)}\left(1+\left|z\right|^{2k}u^{2}(|z|^{2k})\right)|dz|^{2}=h_{k}(z)|dz|^{2}

on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
The Gaussian curvature of this metric
K_{k}
satisfies
K_{k}(z)=K_{g}(z^{k})
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and

\lim_{z\to 0}{K_{k}(z)}=-4

Also, the function
h_{k}
satisfies
\lim_{z\to 0}{h_{k}(z)}=+\infty
and

\frac{\partial h_{k}(z)}{\big(h_{k}(z)\big)^{3}},\frac{\bar{\partial}h_{k}(z)}{\big(h_{k}(z)\big)^{3}},\frac{\bar{\partial}\partial h_{k}(z)}{\big(h_{k}(z)\big)^{3}}\rightarrow 0

as
z\to 0
.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{k}(z)=h_{k}(z)|dz|^{2}
, the curvature is given by

K_{k}(z)=-2\left(\frac{h_{k}(z)\bar{\partial}\partial h_{k}(z)-\partial h_{k}(z)\bar{\partial}h_{k}(z)}{\big(h_{k}(z)\big)^{3}}\right)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. We will write just
u
instead of
u(|z|^{2k})
. Observe that:

\partial h_{k}(z)=\overline{\bar{\partial}h_{k}(z)}=k^{2}\bar{z}\left((k-1)\left|z\right|^{2k-4}+(2k-1)\left|z\right|^{4k-4}u^{2}+2k\left|z\right|^{6k-4}uu^{\prime}\right)

\bar{\partial}\partial h_{k}(z)=|z|^{2k-4}\big(k^{2}(k-1)^{2}+k^{2}(2k-1)^{2}|z|^{2k}u^{2}+2k^{3}(5k-2)|z|^{4k}uu^{\prime}+2k^{4}|z|^{6k}\big((u^{\prime})^{2}+uu^{\prime\prime}\big)\big)

Upon simplification, we get

h_{k}(z)\bar{\partial}\partial h_{k}(z)-\partial h_{k}(z)\bar{\partial}h_{k}(z)=k^{6}\left|z\right|^{6k-6}M(|z|^{2k})

where
M:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

M(t)=u^{2}+6tuu^{\prime}+2t^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{2}uu^{\prime\prime}+2t^{2}u^{3}u^{\prime}-2t^{3}u^{2}(u^{\prime})^{2}+2t^{3}u^{3}u^{\prime\prime}

Now
[
3
]
tells us that

K_{g}(z)=-2\frac{M(|z|^{2})}{\left(1+\left|z\right|^{2}u^{2}(|z|^{2})\right)^{3}}

for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Now note that

K_{k}(z)=-2\frac{k^{6}\left|z\right|^{6k-6}M(|z|^{2k})}{k^{6}\left|z\right|^{6k-6}\left(1+\left|z\right|^{2k}u^{2}(|z|^{2k})\right)^{3}}=-2\frac{M(|z^{k}|^{2})}{\left(1+\left|z^{k}\right|^{2}u^{2}(|z^{k}|^{2})\right)^{3}}=K_{g}(z^{k})

This shows that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
K_{k}(z)=K_{g}(z^{k})
and

\lim_{z\to 0}{K_{k}(z)}=\lim_{z\to 0}{K_{g}(z^{k})}=-4

To focus on the function
h_{k}(z)
, observe that

\lim_{z\to 0}h_{k}(z)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}{k^{2}t^{k-1}\left(1+t^{k}u^{2}(t^{k})\right)}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{k^{2}t^{2k-1}(t^{k}-1)^{2}}{t^{2k}(\log{t^{k}})^{2}}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{k^{2}(t^{k}-1)^{2}}{t(\log{t^{k}})^{2}}=+\infty

Now substituting for
u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}
, simplifying the resulting expression and using the fact that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t(\log{t})=0
, we get

\frac{\left|z\right|^{2k-4}}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}},\frac{\left|z\right|^{4k-4}u^{2}}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}},\frac{\left|z\right|^{6k-4}uu^{\prime}}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}},\frac{\left|z\right|^{8k-4}\left((u^{\prime})^{2}+uu^{\prime\prime}\right)}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}}\to 0

as
z\to 0
. It is now evident that

\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\partial h_{k}(z)}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}}=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\bar{\partial}h_{k}(z)}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}}=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\bar{\partial}\partial h_{k}(z)}{\left(h_{k}(z)\right)^{3}}=0

∎

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{f=0\}
, the curvature is given by

K(z)=-2\left(\frac{h(z)\partial\bar{\partial}h(z)-\partial h(z)\bar{\partial}h(z)}{\big(h(z)\big)^{3}}\right).

\hat{g}^{*}(\phi)(w)=\left(1+\left|\hat{g}^{\prime}(w)\right|^{-2}\left(1+\left|w\right|^{2}u^{2}(|w|^{2})\right)\right)|\hat{g}^{\prime}(w)|^{2}|dw|^{2}

K_{1}

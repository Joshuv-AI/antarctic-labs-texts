Substochastic operators in symmetric spaces
Maciej Ciesielski and Grzegorz Lewicki

Abstract
. First, we solve a crucial problem under which conditions increasing uniform K K -monotonicity is equivalent to lower locally uniform K K -monotonicity. Next, we investigate properties of substochastic operators on L 1 + L ∞ L^{1}+L^{\infty} with applications. Namely, we show that a countable infinite combination of substochastic operators is also substochastic. Using K K -monotonicity properties, we prove several theorems devoted to the convergence of the sequence of substochastic operators in the norm of a symmetric space E E under addition assumption on E E . In our final discussion we focus on compactness of admissible operators for arbitrary Banach couples.

Symmetric spaces, Banach couples, substochastic operators, admissible operators, uniform
K
-monotonicity.

1.
Introduction

Substochastic operators have been researched and applied intensively in many fields of mathematics since the early 20th century. Namely, the first interesting result concerning a substochastic operator appeared in 1929 (see
[
13
,
19
]
), where Hardy, Littlewood and Pólya presented a connection between existence of double stochastic matrix and a majorization one vector by another with respect to the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya relation
\prec
. Next, in 60-ties Calderón and Ryff showed that for any two non-negative measurable functions
f,g
such that
f\prec{g}
and
f\in{L^{1}+L^{\infty}}
there exists a substochastic operator
T
such that
Tg=f
a.e. (see
[
21
,
3
]
). It is worth mentioning that this result was a crucial key in the Calderón’s proof to establish an equivalence criterion for the exact interpolation space between a Banach couple
(L^{1},L^{\infty})

[
3
]
. It is necessary to recall some investigations devoted to relationships between substochastic operators and semigroups, among other used to explore properties of Markov processes or Markov chains, which have a substantial history dating back also to the 1960s (see
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
). The next motivation for this paper we find in
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
11
]
, where authors presented equivalent conditions for
K
-monotonicity properties with applications in symmetric spaces. In the spirit of the previous investigations we give a complete answer for the essential problem about a relation between uniform
K
-monotonicity properties. Namely, we provide complete criteria for equivalence of increasing uniform
K
-monotonicity and lower local uniform
K
-monotonicity in symmetric spaces.
The second goal of this paper is to study a relationship between convergence of substochastic operators and
K
-monotonicity properties in symmetric spaces. First, we prove that a countable infinite combination of substochastic operators is substochastic and discuss some basic examples of
substochastic operators in symmetric spaces. We start our investigation assuming the weakest property lower locally uniform
K
-monotonicity in a symmetric space
E
. Under additional assumption of the symmetric space
E
, we establish a norm convergence of a sequence of specific substochastic operators given by a countable infinite sum of the integral averaging on positive finite measurable sets. Next, letting the stronger property decreasing uniform
K
-monotonicity in a symmetric space
E
we research norm and point convergence of the sequence of substochastic operators given in the descending order with respect to the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya relation
\prec
. In view of the previous result we also research norm convergence of
A^{n}
a geometric sequence generated by a fixed substochastic operator
A
in a symmetric space
E
. We also focus our attention on the convergence of a sequence of substochastic operator given in the ascending order for the partial order
\leq
in a symmetric space
E
, equipped with uniformly
K
-monotone norm.
Finally, we show compactness of the admissible operator between Banach couples under additional assumptions of the interpolation spaces.

Preliminaries

Now we recall basic notation and definition. Let
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\mathbb{N}
be the sets of reals, nonnegative reals and positive integers, respectively. In a Banach space
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
we denote the unit sphere and the closed unit ball by
S(X)
and
B(X))
, respectively. A function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called

\phi(t)
is increasing and
\phi(t)/t
is decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and also
\phi(t)=0\Leftrightarrow{t=0}
. We denote as usual by
\mu
the Lebesgue measure on
I=[0,\alpha)
, where
\alpha=1
or
\alpha=\infty
, and by

the set of all (equivalence classes of) extended real valued Lebesgue measurable functions on
I
. Let us use the notation
A^{c}=I\backslash A
for any measurable set
A
. By a
Banach function space
(or a
Köthe space
) we mean a Banach lattice
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
that is a sublattice of
L^{0}
and possesses the following conditions

(1)

If
x\in L^{0}
,
y\in E
and
|x|\leq|y|
a.e., then
x\in E
and
\|x\|_{E}\leq\|y\|_{E}
.

(2)

There exists a strictly positive
x\in E
.

To simplify the notation in the whole paper we use the symbol
E^{+}={\{x\in E:x\geq 0\}}
. An element
x\in E
is called a
point of order continuity
if for any
(x_{n})\subset{}E^{+}
such that
x_{n}\leq\left|x\right|
and
x_{n}\rightarrow 0
a.e. we get
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow 0.
A Banach function space
E
is said to be
order continuous

(shortly
E\in\left(OC\right)
) if any element
x\in{}E
is a point of order continuity (see
[
18
]
). We say that a Banach function space
E
has the
Fatou property
if for any
\left(x_{n}\right)\subset{}E^{+}
,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|_{E}<\infty
and
x_{n}\uparrow x\in L^{0}
, then we have
x\in E
and
\|x_{n}\|_{E}\uparrow\|x\|_{E}
. Unless it is said otherwise, in the whole article it is considered that
E
has the Fatou property. For any function
x\in L^{0}
we denote the
distribution function
by

d_{x}(\lambda)=\mu\left\{s\in[0,\alpha):\left|x\left(s\right)\right|>\lambda\right\},\qquad\lambda\geq 0.

and also the
decreasing rearrangement
of
x
by

x^{\ast}\left(t\right)=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:d_{x}\left(\lambda\right)\leq t\right\},\text{ \ \ }t\geq 0.

The following notation is used
x^{*}(\infty)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}x^{*}(t)
if
\alpha=\infty
and
x^{*}(\infty)=0
if
\alpha=1
. For any function
x\in L^{0}
we define the
maximal function
of
x^{\ast}
by

x^{\ast\ast}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}x^{\ast}(s)ds.

For any element
x\in L^{0}
, it is well known that
x^{\ast}\leq x^{\ast\ast},

x^{\ast\ast}
is decreasing, continuous and subadditive. For more details of
d_{x}
,
x^{\ast}
and
x^{\ast\ast}
the reader is referred to see
[
1
,
17
]
. Let us mention that two functions
x,y\in{L^{0}}
are said to be
equimeasurable
(shortly
x\sim y
) if
d_{x}=d_{y}
. A Banach function space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
is called
symmetric
or
rearrangement invariant
(r.i. for short) if whenever
x\in L^{0}
and

such that

x\in E
and

. For a symmetric space

we define
\phi_{E}

given by
\phi_{E}(t)=\|\chi_{(0,t)}\|_{E}
for any
t\in[0,\alpha)
(see
[
1
]
). For any two functions

the

x\prec y\Leftrightarrow x^{\ast\ast}(t)\leq y^{\ast\ast}(t)\text{ for
all }t>0.\text{ }

A symmetric space
E
is called
K
-
monotone
(shortly
E\in(KM)
)
if for any
x\in L^{1}+L^{\infty}

x\in E
and
\|x\|_{E}\leq\|y\|_{E}.
It is commonly known that a symmetric space
E
is
K
-monotone if and only if
E
is exact interpolation space between
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}.
It is worth mentioning that a symmetric space
E
equipped with an order continuous norm or with the Fatou property is
K
-monotone (see
[
17
]
).
Given
x\in{E}
is called a

-monotonicity
of

LKM

point
(resp. a

K
-monotonicity
of
E

UKM

point
) if for any
y\in{E}
,
y^{*}\neq{x^{*}}
with
y\prec{x}
(resp.
x\prec{y}
), then
\left\|y\right\|_{E}<\left\|x\right\|_{E}
(resp.
\left\|x\right\|_{E}<\left\|y\right\|_{E}
). Let us mention that given a symmetric space
E
is called
strictly
K
-monotone
(shortly
E\in(SKM)
) if any element of
E
is an
LKM
point or equivalently any element of
E
is a
UKM
point.

Given
x\in{E}
is said to be a
point of lower local uniform
K
-monotonicity
of
E
, shortly an
LLUKM
point, if for any
(x_{n})\subset{E}
with
x_{n}\prec{x}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow\left\|x\right\|_{E}
, then
\left\|x^{*}-x_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}
.

A symmetric space
E
is called
Lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone
, shortly
E\in(LLUKM)
, whenever any element
x\in{}E
is a
LLUKM
point.

We refer the reader for more information to see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
10
]
.
In
[
11
]
, authors introduced some new notions which are a generalization of uniform monotonicity properties in symmetric spaces. The generalization was obtained by replacing a partial order
\leq
by a weaker relation
\prec
, in definition of monotonicity properties. It is worth mentioning that the new properties characterizes a completely different geometric structure of symmetric spaces than monotonicity properties. A symmetric space
E
is said to be
uniformly
K
-monotone
, shortly
E\in(UKM)
, if for any
(x_{n}),(y_{n})\subset{E}
such that
x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}<\infty
we have
\left\|x_{n}^{*}-y_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}
.
A symmetric space
E
is called
decreasing (resp. increasing) uniformly
K
-monotone
, shortly
E\in(DUKM)
(resp. shortly
E\in(IUKM)
), if for any
(x_{n}),(y_{n})\subset{E}
such that
x_{n+1}\prec{}x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}<\infty
(resp.
x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}\prec{y_{n+1}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}<\infty
), we have
\left\|x_{n}^{*}-y_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}
.

An operator
T
from a Banach function space
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
into a Banach function space
(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y})
is called a
positive contraction
if its norm is at most
1
and it satisfies the property that
T(x)\geq{0}
whenever
x\geq{0}
. An admissible operator for a Banach couple
(L^{1},L^{\infty})
is said to be a
substochastic operator
whenever it is positive contraction on
L^{1}
and on
L^{\infty}
. In addition, on the space
L^{1}+L^{\infty}
we may employ an equivalent characterization of substochastic operators, i.e.
T
is substochastic if and only if
T(x)\prec{x}
for all
x\in L^{1}+L^{\infty}
(see
[
1
]
).

Unless we say otherwise, we assume in the whole paper that two substochastic operators
T
and
S
satisfies
T\leq S
(resp.
T\prec S
) on
L^{1}+L^{\infty}
, whenever for any
x\in{L^{1}+L^{\infty}}
we have

(resp.
Tx\prec Sx
). For any quasiconcave function
\phi
on

(resp.
M_{\phi}
) is a subspace of
L^{0}
such that

\left\|x\right\|_{M_{\phi}^{(*)}}=\sup_{t>0}\{x^{*}(t)\phi(t)\}<\infty

{\left(\textnormal{resp. }\left\|x\right\|_{M_{\phi}}=\sup_{t>0}\{x^{**}(t)\phi(t)\}<\infty\right).}

It is well known that
M_{\phi}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}M_{\phi}^{(*)}
and also the Marcinkiewicz space
M_{\phi}^{(*)}
(resp.
M_{\phi}
) is a r.i. quasi-Banach function space (r.i. Banach function space) with the fundamental function
\phi
on

. Let us recall the common fact that
E\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}M_{\phi}
for any symmetric space

with the fundamental function

(for more details see

3.
Increasing uniform
K
-monotonicity

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a symmetric space and let
\phi_{E}
be the fundamental function of
E
. Then,
E
is increasing uniformly
K
-monotone if and only if
E
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone and

\lim_{t\rightarrow{0^{+}}}\frac{t}{\phi_{E}(t)}=0.

Proof.

Necessity
. Let
(x_{n}),(y_{n})\subset{E}
,
x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}\prec{y_{n+1}}
and

(1)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=d,

where
d\in(0,\infty)
. Clearly, by Proposition 5.9 in
[
1
]
we get
E
is embedded in the Marcinkiewicz space
M_{\phi_{E}}
with the norm one, and so

\phi_{E}(t)y_{n}^{*}(t)\leq\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}\leq{d}<\infty

for all
t>0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, by Helly’s Selection Theorem
[
22
]
, passing to subsequence if necessary we may assume that there is
y_{0}^{*}=y_{0}\in{L^{0}}
such that
y_{n}^{*}\rightarrow{y_{0}^{*}}
a.e. on
I
. Next, applying (
1
) and by assumption that
E
has the Fatou property, in view of Fatou’s Lemma
[
1
]
it follows that
y_{0}\in{E}
and

(2)

\left\|y_{0}\right\|_{E}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}.

Moreover, we have for any
t>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3)

x_{n}^{**}(t)\leq{y}_{n}^{**}(t)\leq{y_{n+1}^{**}(t)}\leq\frac{\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}}{\phi_{E}(t)}\leq\frac{d}{\phi_{E}(t)}.

Consequently, for any
t>0
we obtain

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}{y}_{n}^{**}(t)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{y}_{n}^{**}(t)\leq\frac{d}{\phi_{E}(t)}.

Hence, since
y_{n}^{*}\rightarrow{y_{0}^{*}}
, by Fatou’s Lemma we conclude that

(4)

{y_{0}^{**}(t)}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{y}_{n}^{**}(t)<\infty

for any
t\in{I}
,
t>0
. Now, in view of Corollary 5.3 in
[
1
]
, by assumption
\lim_{t\rightarrow{0^{+}}}t/\phi_{E}(t)=0
and by (
3
) it follows that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
t_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all
t\in(0,t_{\epsilon}]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5)

\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{n}^{*}\leq\frac{t}{\phi_{E}(t)}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

Furthermore, since
y_{n}^{*}\rightarrow{y_{0}^{*}}
a.e. on
I
, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem we observe that for any
0<s<t
, there exists
N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq{}N_{\epsilon}
,

\left|\int_{s}^{t}{y_{n}^{*}}-\int_{s}^{t}{y_{0}^{*}}\right|<\frac{d\epsilon}{4}.

Next, without loss of generality taking additionally
s<t_{\epsilon}
for the previous inequality and using (
5
) for any
n\geq{N_{\epsilon}}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{n}^{*}=\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{s}y_{n}^{*}+\frac{1}{d}\int_{s}^{t}y_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{t_{\epsilon}}y_{n}^{*}+\frac{1}{d}\left|\int_{s}^{t}y_{n}^{*}-\int_{s}^{t}y_{0}^{*}\right|+\frac{1}{d}\int_{s}^{t}y_{0}^{*}

\displaystyle<\frac{t_{\epsilon}}{\phi_{E}(t_{\epsilon})}+\frac{\epsilon}{4}+\frac{1}{d}\int_{s}^{t}y_{0}^{*}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{1}{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{0}^{*}.

In consequence, applying (
3
) and (
4
) we conclude that for all
t\in{I}
with
t>0
,

(6)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}y_{n}^{**}(t)={y_{0}}^{**}(t).

Now, since
y_{n}\prec{y_{n+1}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we claim that
y_{n}\prec{y_{0}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, if it is not true then there exist
t_{0}>0
,
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
(n_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

y_{0}^{**}(t_{0})<y_{n_{k}}^{**}(t_{0})-\epsilon_{0}.

On the other hand, in view of (
6
) there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
n\geq{N_{0}}
,

y_{n}^{**}(t_{0})<y_{0}^{**}(t_{0})+\epsilon_{0}/2,

which yields a contradiction and proves the claim. Hence, by assumption
x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}\prec{y_{n+1}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we get
x_{n}\prec{y_{0}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by symmetry of
E
we have
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|y_{0}\right\|_{E}
, and so in view of the facts (
1
) and (
2
) we observe that

(7)

d=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}=\left\|y_{0}\right\|_{E}.

In consequence, sine
x_{n}\prec{y_{0}}
and
y_{n}\prec{y_{0}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by assumption that
E
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}^{*}-y_{0}^{*}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}^{*}-y_{0}^{*}\right\|_{E}=0.

Finally, using the triangle inequality of the norm in
E
we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}^{*}-x_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}=0.

Sufficiency.
Immediately, by Remark 4.1 in
[
11
]
it is enough to prove that increasing uniform
K
-monotonicity implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow{0^{+}}}\frac{t}{\phi_{E}(t)}=0.

By Corollary 5.3 in
[
1
]
, we suppose for a contrary that
\lim_{t\rightarrow{0^{+}}}{\phi_{E}(t)/t}=c>0
. Define for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

x_{n}=n\chi_{[0,\frac{1}{n})}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad y_{n}=2n\chi_{[0,\frac{1}{2n})}.

Then, it is easy to see that
x_{n}\prec{y_{n}}\prec{y_{n+1}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi_{E}(1/n)}{1/n}=c\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi_{E}(1/2n)}{1/2n}=c.

In consequence, by assumption that
E
is increasing uniformly
K
-monotone it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}^{*}-x_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}=0.

On the other hand, by symmetry of
E
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}^{*}-x_{n}^{*}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|2n\chi_{[0,\frac{1}{2n})}-n\chi_{[0,\frac{1}{n})}\right\|_{E}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|n\chi_{[0,\frac{1}{2n})}-n\chi_{[\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{n})}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi_{E}(1/n)}{1/n}=c>0,

which contradicts and completes the proof.
∎

4.
Substochastic operators

This section is devoted to investigation of convergence of the substochastic operators in symmetric spaces. First, we prove that any countable sum of substochastic operators is substochastic.

Remark 4.1
.

Let us recall some basic facts about substochastic operators, which will use in our further investigation. Letting
\mathbb{S}
is a family of all substochastic operators on
L^{1}+L^{\infty}
it is easy to show that
\mathbb{S}
is convex and closed under the composition. Indeed, by subadditivity of the Hardy operator (see Theorem 3.4 in
[
1
]
) taking
A,B\in\mathbb{S}
, for any
\theta\in[0,1]
and
x\in L^{1}+L^{\infty}
we have

\displaystyle\theta A(x)+(1-\theta)B(x)\prec x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad A\circ B(x)\prec B(x)\prec x.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(T_{j})
be a sequence of substochastic operators on a Banach pair
(L^{1},L^{\infty})
and let
(E_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a countable collection of pairwise disjoint subsets of
I
, each with finite positive measure. Assume that
E=I\setminus{\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{J}}}E_{j}
and
f\in{L^{0}}
be locally integrable function on every
E_{j}
. Then the operator
\mathcal{A}:L^{0}\rightarrow{L^{0}}
given by

\mathcal{A}(f)=f\chi_{E}+\sum_{j\in J}T_{j}(f\chi_{E_{j}})\chi_{E_{j}}

holds
\mathcal{A}(f)\prec{f}
.

Proof.

Assume first
\mathcal{A}(f)=f\chi_{E}+T_{1}(f\chi_{E_{1}})\chi_{E_{1}}
for
0<\mu(E_{1})<\infty
and
E=I\setminus{E_{1}}
. Then, taking
F\subset{I}
,
t=\mu(F)\in{I}\setminus\{0\}
,
t_{0}=\mu(F\cap E_{1})
we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{F}|\mathcal{A}f|d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{F\cap{E}}|\mathcal{A}f|d\mu+\int_{F\cap E_{1}}|\mathcal{A}f|d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{F\cap{E}}|f|d\mu+\int_{F\cap E_{1}}|T_{1}(f\chi_{E_{1}})|d\mu.

Next, by assumption that
T_{j}
is a substochastic operator on
L^{1}+L^{\infty}
and by Lemma 2.5 in
[
1
]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{F\cap E_{1}}|T_{1}(f\chi_{E_{1}})|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\mu(F\cap E_{1})(T_{1}(f\chi_{E_{1}}))^{**}(\mu(F\cap E_{1}))d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\mu(F\cap E_{1})(f\chi_{E_{1}})^{**}(\mu(F\cap E_{1}))d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mu(F\cap E_{1})}(f\chi_{E_{1}})^{*}(s)ds.

Since
t_{0}=\mu(F\cap E_{1})\leq\mu(E_{1})
, by Proposition 3.3(b) in
[
1
]
there exists a subset
B\subset{E_{1}}
such that
\mu(B)=t_{0}
and

\int_{0}^{\mu(F\cap E_{1})}(f\chi_{E_{1}})^{*}(s)ds=\int_{B}|f\chi_{E_{1}}|d\mu=\int_{B}|f|d\mu.

In consequence, we get

\displaystyle\int_{F}|\mathcal{A}f|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{F\cap{E}}|f|d\mu+\int_{0}^{\mu(F\cap E_{1})}(f\chi_{E_{1}})^{*}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{F\cap{E}}|f|d\mu+\int_{B}|f|d\mu=\int_{B\cup F\cap{E}}|f|d\mu

Next, since
B\cap(F\cap E)=\emptyset
and
\mu(F\cap E\cup B)=\mu(E\cup F)+\mu(B)=t
, by the Hardy-Littlewood inequality (Theorem 2.2 in
[
1
]
) it follows that

\int_{F}|\mathcal{A}f|d\mu\leq\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds.

In consequence, by Proposition 3.3
[
1
]
taking supremum over all sets
F
of a measure
t
we conclude that for all
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}(\mathcal{A}f)^{*}(s)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)ds.

Now, we consider the case when

is a finite index set. Define

I_{n}=I\setminus\bigcup_{m=1}^{n}E_{m}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{n}f=f\chi_{E}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}T_{m}(f\chi_{E_{m}})\chi_{E_{m}}

for all
n\in{J}
. We easily observe that

\mathcal{A}_{n}f\prec\mathcal{A}_{n-1}f\prec\mathcal{A}_{n-2}f\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{A}_{1}f\prec f.

Now, assume that
J=\{1,2,\dots\}
is countable infinite. We claim that
|\mathcal{A}f|\leq\mathcal{A}|f|
. Indeed, by assumption that
T_{j}
is a positive contraction on a pair
(L^{1},L^{\infty})
and since
-|f|\leq{f}\leq|f|
it follows that
-\mathcal{A}|f|\leq{\mathcal{A}f}\leq\mathcal{A}|f|
. Now, it is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{A}f\prec{f}
for all nonnegative
f
. Consider for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{n}=f\chi_{E}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}f\chi_{E_{m}}.

It is easy to see that
0\leq{f_{n}}\uparrow{f}
a.e. and since
T_{j}
for each
j\in{J}
is a positive contraction we provide

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(f\chi_{E})+\mathcal{A}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}f\chi_{E_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=f\chi_{E}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}T_{m}\left(f\chi_{E_{m}}\right)\chi_{E_{m}}\rightarrow f\chi_{E}+\sum_{m\in J}T_{m}\left(f\chi_{E_{m}}\right)\chi_{E_{m}}

a.e. on
I
. Moreover, we have

\mathcal{A}f_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}f_{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}f\prec{f}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, whence by Proposition 3.2
[
1
]
this yields
\mathcal{A}f\prec{f}
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

(\mathcal{H}_{n})

is lower locally uniformly

[

E

n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

(B_{0}x)^{**}(t)=y^{**}(t)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(A^{n}x)^{**}(t)\leq(A^{n}x)^{**}(t)

for any
t\in{I}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying again Calderón and Ryff Theorem we employ a sequence of the substochastic operators
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that

B_{n}\circ A^{n}x=B_{0}x=|y|

a.e. on
I
and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, applying (
16
) we prove the first part of our theorem. Now, assume that
\left\|A^{n}x\right\|_{E}\rightarrow\left\|x\right\|_{E}
. Then, since
y\prec{x}
, by assumption that
E
is decreasing uniformly
K
-monotone and by Remark 4.1 in
[
11
]
it follows that
x=|y|
a.e. on
I
and completes the proof.
∎

The immediate consequence of the previous theorem is the following result.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
E
be a symmetric space with the fundamental function
\phi
such that
\phi(\infty)=\infty
and
A
be a substochastic operator on
L^{1}+L^{\infty}
. If
E
is decreasing uniformly
K
-monotone and if for any
x\in{E^{+}}
we have
Ax\leq{x}
and
\left\|A^{n}x\right\|_{E}\rightarrow\left\|x\right\|_{E}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
then
\left\|A^{n}x-x\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Directly, by Theorem
4.8
we obtain

(17)

\left\|x^{*}-(A^{n}x)^{*}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Next, since
Ax\leq{x}
for any
x\in{E^{+}}
it follows that

A^{n}x\leq\frac{A^{n}x+x}{2}\leq{x}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, by symmetry of
E
and by assumption that
\left\|A^{n}x\right\|_{E}\rightarrow\left\|x\right\|_{E}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
we have

\frac{1}{2}\left\|{A^{n}}x+x\right\|_{E}\rightarrow\left\|x\right\|_{E}\quad\textnormal{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

In consequence, since
\frac{1}{2}(A^{n}x+x)\prec{x}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, in view of the assumption that
E
is decreasing uniformly
K
-monotone we conclude that

\left\|\frac{1}{2}({A^{n}}x+x)^{*}-x^{*}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{ as }\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, by (
17
) and by Lemma 2.2 in
[
12
]
this yields that
A^{n}x\rightarrow{x}
globally in measure. Furthermore, since
\phi(\infty)=\infty
, by Proposition 4.11 in
[
11
]
we provide
E
is order continuous. Finally, by (
17
) and by Proposition 2.4 in
[
12
]
we get end of the proof.
∎

Now we focus our attention on conditions that guarantee the convergence of substochastic operators in symmetric spaces.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
X,Y
be symmetric spaces and let
T_{n},T\in B(X,Y)
be substochastic operators such that
T_{n}\leq{T_{n+1}}\leq{T}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone, then

\left\|T_{n}(x)-T(x)\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

for some
x\in{X^{+}}
such that
\left\|x\right\|_{X}=\left\|T(x)\right\|_{Y}=1
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then, there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
\left\|x_{1}\right\|_{X}=1
,
x_{1}\geq{0}
and

\left|\left\|T_{1}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}-\left\|T_{1}\right\|\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Moreover, without loss of generality passing to subsequence and relabeling we may assume that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left|\left\|T_{n}\right\|-1\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2},

whence we get

(18)

\left|\left\|T_{1}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}-1\right|<{\epsilon}.

Next, since
T_{n}\leq{T_{n+1}}\leq{T}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

T_{1}(x_{1})\leq{T_{n}(x_{1})}\leq{T_{n+1}(x_{1})}\leq{T(x_{1})},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By symmetry of
Y
, we conclude

(19)

\left\|T_{1}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T_{n+1}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, in view of (
18
) we easily get

1-\epsilon<\left\|T_{1}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq{1},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. So, since
\epsilon
is arbitrary chosen we obtain

(20)

\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow\left\|T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}=1\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Now, applying analogous techniques as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
and passing to subsequence and also relabelling if necessary we may show that there exists
S(x_{1})\in{Y}
such that

(21)

{T_{n}(x_{1})}^{*}\rightarrow{S(x_{1})}^{*}\quad\textnormal{a.e. on $I$,}\qquad{T_{n}(x_{1})}^{**}(t)\rightarrow{S(x_{1})}^{**}(t)\quad\textnormal{for all }t\in{I}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
and

(22)

\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow\left\|S(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad T_{n}(x_{1})\prec{S(x_{1})}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, since
T_{n}\leq{T}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
on the positive cone of
X
we have
T_{n}(x_{1})^{*}\leq{T(x_{1})}^{*}
. Hence, by (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
) this yields
S(x_{1})^{*}\leq{T(x_{1})^{*}}
a.e. on
I
and
\left\|S(x_{1})\right\|_{y}=1
. Consequently, since
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone by Remark 4.1 in
[
11
]
it follows that
Y
is strictly
K
-monotone and so
T(x_{1})^{*}=S(x_{1})^{*}
a.e. on
I
. Moreover, it is clearly obvious that
T_{n}(x_{1})\prec T_{n+1}(x_{1})\prec T(x_{1})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and in view of (
20
) and by assumption that
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone we get

(23)

\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})^{*}-T(x_{1})^{*}\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Now, we shall prove that

(24)

\left\|\frac{(T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1}))}{2}^{*}-T(x_{1})^{*}\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

First, we observe that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

T_{n}(x_{1})\leq\frac{T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1})}{2}\leq T(x_{1}).

Hence, by symmetry of
Y
this implies

\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}}{2}\leq\left\|T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}=1,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which provides in view of (
22
) that

\frac{\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}}{2}\rightarrow{1}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1})\leq 2T(x_{1})
. In consequence, proceeding analogously as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we may see that

\frac{T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1})}{2}\prec T(x_{1})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\frac{(T_{n}(x_{1})+T(x_{1}))}{2}^{*}\rightarrow T(x_{1})^{*}\quad\textnormal{a.e. on }I.

Thus, by assumption that
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone we prove (
24
). Next, according to Lemma 2.2
[
12
]
it follows that
T_{n}(x_{1})
converges to
T(x_{1})
globally in measure on
I
. Consequently, in view of Remark 4.1 in
[
11
]
and by assumption that
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone we get
Y
is order continuous. Finally, by (
23
) and by Proposition 2.4
[
12
]
we obtain that

\left\|T_{n}(x_{1})-T(x_{1})\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
X,Y
be symmetric spaces and let
T_{n},T\in{B(X,Y)}
be substochastic operators such that for any
x\in{X^{+}}
we have
\left\|T_{n}(x)\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow\left\|T(x)\right\|_{Y}
and
T_{n}\leq{T_{n+1}}\leq{T}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
Y
is uniformly
K
-monotone, then for any
x\in{X}
we have

\left\|T_{n}(x)-T(x)\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Since the convergence is satisfied for
x=0
we may assume without loss of generality that
x\neq{0}
.
Proceeding analogously as in the proof of Theorem
4.10
and assuming that
\left\|T_{n}(x)\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow\left\|T(x)\right\|_{Y}=1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
we obtain that for any
x\in X^{+}\setminus\{0\}
,

\left\|T_{n}(x)-T(x)\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}.

Since for any substochastic operator
S:X\rightarrow Y
we have
S(x)=S(x^{+}-x^{-})=S(x^{+})-S(x^{-})
where
x\in{X}
, we easily get for any
x\in{X}
,

0\leq\left\|T_{n}(x)-T(x)\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|T_{n}(x^{+})-T(x^{+})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|T_{n}(x^{-})-T(x^{-})\right\|_{Y}\rightarrow{0}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\overline{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
,
\overline{Y}=(Y_{0},Y_{1})
be two Banach couples and let
X,Y
be interpolation spaces with respect to the couples
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
, where
X,Y
are of exponent
\theta\in[0,1]
. Assume that
Y_{1}
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone and
T:\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{Y}
is an admissible operator such that
T:{X_{1}}\rightarrow{Y_{1}}
compactly. Then
T:{X}\rightarrow{Y}
is compact and there exists a sequence of admissible operators
T_{n}:\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{Y}
such that

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X,Y)}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad{n}\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Since every finite rank operator is compact and the norm-limit of compact operators is also a compact operator, it is enough to prove that
T:\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{Y}
an arbitrary admissible operator is a limit of the finite rank admissible operators. Define analogously as in Theorem
4.5
the operator
F_{n}
by

F_{n}(x)=x\chi_{\Omega_{n}}+\sum_{j\in J_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu\left(E_{j}^{(n)}\right)}\int_{E_{j}^{(n)}}xd\mu\right)\chi_{E_{j}^{(n)}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in{Y_{0}+Y_{1}}
. Let
T:{X_{0}+X_{1}}\rightarrow{Y_{0}+Y_{1}}
be admissible operator and let
(X,Y)
be an intermediate couple for
(X_{0},X_{1})
and
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
. Denote
T_{n}=F_{n}\circ{T}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly,
T_{n}
is a finite rank operator and

(25)

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{i},Y_{i})}=\sup_{\left\|x\right\|_{X_{i}}\leq{1}}\left\|T(x)-F_{n}\circ{T}(x)\right\|_{Y_{i}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
V=\{T(z):\left\|z\right\|_{X_{1}}\leq{1}\}
has a compact closure in
Y_{1}
, for each
v\in V
there exists
(z_{n})\in X
,
\left\|z_{n}\right\|_{X_{1}}\leq{1}
such that
T(z_{n})\rightarrow{v}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in
Y_{1}
. Therefore, we have

\sup_{\left\|x\right\|_{X_{1}}\leq{1}}\left\|T(x)-F_{n}\circ{T}(x)\right\|_{Y_{1}}=\sup_{v\in{V}}\left\|v-F_{n}(v)\right\|_{Y_{1}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Next, by Theorem
4.5
, since
Y_{1}
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone, it follows that
\left\|v-F_{n}(v)\right\|_{Y_{1}}\rightarrow{0}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence, since
V
has a compact closure in
Y_{1}
, by (
25
) we get

(26)

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}=\sup_{v\in{V}}\left\|v-F_{n}(v)\right\|_{Y_{1}}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

By assumption that
(X,Y)
are the interpolation spaces of exponent
\theta\in[0,1]
, we conclude

\displaystyle\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{0},Y_{0})}^{1-\theta}\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|T\right\|+\left\|T_{n}\right\|\right)^{1-\theta}\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|T\right\|+\left\|F_{n}\right\|\left\|T\right\|\right)^{1-\theta}\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}.

Since
F_{n}(x_{i})\prec{x_{i}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{i}\in{X_{i}}
, this yields that
\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{i},Y_{i})}\leq{1}
, whence

\displaystyle\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{0},Y_{0})}\right)^{1-\theta}\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{0},Y_{0})}\right)^{1-\theta}\left(2\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle=2\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{0},Y_{0})}^{1-\theta}\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}.

Moreover, by (
26
) we obtain

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X,Y)}\leq\left(2\left\|T\right\|_{B(X_{0},Y_{0})}\right)^{1-\theta}\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X_{1},Y_{1})}^{\theta}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

To show the second part of the theorem we define
F_{n}
as follows

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\sigma_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu\left(E_{j}^{(n)}\right)}\int_{E_{j}^{(n)}}xd\mu\right)\chi_{E_{j}^{(n)}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in{X_{0}+X_{1}}
. Then, we proceed as previously to get

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{B(X,Y)}\rightarrow{0}\quad\textnormal{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Furthermore,
T_{n}=F_{n}\circ{T}
is admissible with respect to the couples
(X_{0},X_{1})
and
(Y_{0},Y_{1})
, i.e. for any
x\in{X_{i}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}(x)\right\|_{Y_{i}}=\left\|F_{n}\circ T(x)\right\|_{Y_{i}}\leq\left\|F_{n}\circ T\right\|_{B(X,Y)}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{i}}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{B(X,Y)}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{i}},

and consequently

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\max_{i=0,1}\left\|F_{n}\circ T\right\|_{B(X_{i},Y_{i})}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

∎

Remark 4.13
.

Now, taking an arbitrary Banach couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
we recall a construction

K_{\theta,q}(\overline{A})=\{a\in A_{0}+A_{1}:\Phi_{\theta,q}(K(t,a))<\infty\}

for
\theta\in[0,1]
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, where

\Phi_{\theta,q}(f(t))=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-\theta}f(t))^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad K(t,a)=\inf_{a=a_{0}+a_{1}}\{\left\|a_{0}\right\|_{A_{0}}+t\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{A_{1}}\}.

It is well known that
K_{\theta,q}
is an exact interpolation functor of exponent
\theta
, see
[
2
]
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
\overline{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
,
\overline{Y}=(Y_{0},Y_{1})
be two Banach couples such that
X_{i},Y_{i}\hookrightarrow{L^{1}+L^{\infty}}
for each
i=0,1
. If
Y_{1}
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone and
T:\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{Y}
is an admissible operator such that
T:{X_{1}}\rightarrow{Y_{1}}
compactly, then
T:K_{\theta,q}(\overline{X})\rightarrow{K_{\theta,q}(\overline{Y})}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
g\in[1,\infty)
. Then, by Theorem 3.1.2
[
2
]
it follows that
K_{\theta,q}(\overline{X})
and
K_{\theta,q}(\overline{Y})
are exact interpolation functors of exponent
\theta
on the category
\mathcal{N}
. Hence, by Theorem
4.12
, since
Y_{1}
is lower locally uniformly
K
-monotone this yields that any admissible operator
T:K_{\theta,q}(\overline{X})\rightarrow{K}_{\theta,q}(\overline{Y})
is compact.
∎

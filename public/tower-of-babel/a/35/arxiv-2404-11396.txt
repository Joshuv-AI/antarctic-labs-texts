Convergece rate and uniform Lipschitz estimate in periodic homogenization of high-contrast elliptic systems
Xin Fu, Wenjia Jing

Abstract
. We consider the Dirichlet problem for elliptic systems with periodically distributed inclusions whose conduction parameter exhibits a significant contrast compared to the background media. We develop a unified method to quantify the convergence rates both as the periodicity of inclusions tends to zero and as the parameter approaches either zero or infinity. Based on the obtained convergence rates and a Campanato-type scheme, we also derive the regularity estimates that are uniform both in the periodicity and the contrast. Key words : Periodic homogenization, layer potential, high-contrast media, regularity estimates, perforated domains. Mathematics subject classification (MSC 2020) : 35B27, 35J70, 35P20

Introduction

In this paper we study quantitative homogenization of elliptic systems with rapidly oscillating and high-contrast periodic coefficients.

To state the problem in precisely, we need to impose two assumptions: the coefficient condition (
\mathbf{H1}
) and the geometric condition (
\mathbf{{H2}}
). Throughout this paper, we will assume that
d\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
are integers.

(
H1
)
Assumptions on the coefficients.
We assume the coefficient

A(y)=\big(a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(y)\big):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{(dm)^{2}},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d,\ 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m

satisfies

(a)

(Symmetry) For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(y)=a_{ji}^{\beta\alpha}(y),\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d,\ 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m.

(1.1)

(b)

(Strong ellipticity) There exists
\mu>0
such that

\mu|\xi|^{2}\leq a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(y)\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\xi_{j}^{\beta}\leq\mu^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{dm}.

(1.2)

(c)

(Periodicity)

A(y+\mathbf{n})=A(y),\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \forall\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(1.3)

(d)

(Hölder continuity) There exists
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\tau>0
such that

|A(y)-A(w)|\leq\tau|y-w|^{\lambda},\qquad\forall y,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.4)

(
H2
)
Assumptions on the Geometric set-up.
Let
Y=(-1/2,1/2)^{d}
denote the unit cell,
and let
\omega\subset Y
be a simply connected domain with Lipschitz boundary such that
\mathrm{dist}(\omega,\partial Y)>0
. Consider
\Omega
as a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(sometimes we may impose more regularity on the boundary
\partial\Omega
such that
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain). For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}:=\varepsilon(\mathbf{n}+\omega)
be a rescaled and translated version of
\omega
, for a fixed
\kappa>0
, we then define the indices set

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\,\text{ and }\,\mathrm{dist}(\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon},\partial\Omega)>\kappa\varepsilon\},

and let

D_{\varepsilon}:=\bigcup\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}.

(1.5)

The set
D_{\varepsilon}
consists of periodic inclusions of size
\varepsilon
within
\Omega
which are well-separated from
\partial\Omega
. The complementary domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{D_{\varepsilon}}
then represents the background matrix surrounding these inclusions.

The pair
(\Omega,D_{\varepsilon})
satisfying the geometric condition (
\mathbf{GC}
) is referred to as the type II perforated domain, as defined in
[
20
]
. In comparison, the type I perforated domain refers to the following construction:

D_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\cap\bigcup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon},\qquad\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{D_{\varepsilon}},

(1.6)

see Figure
1
.

We are now in the position of describing our problem. For any
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, let

\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x):=\delta\mathbbm{1}_{D_{\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)

be the contrast function, where
\delta
denotes the contrast of physical parameters between inclusions and background (in practice,
\delta
could be compressibility, permeability, conductivity and etc.). We study the quantitative homogenization of the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta})=f&&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,\\
&u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=g&&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u):=-\mathrm{div}\left[\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)A\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\nabla u\right]=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\left(x/\varepsilon\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}u^{\beta}\right),

which acts on functions defined on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Note that since we allow
\delta
approaches extremal value (
0
or
\infty
), the coefficient
\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)A(x/\varepsilon)
exhibits high contrast between inclusions and background, and thus
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
models the media with high-contrast small inclusions.

For each fixed
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, it is well known that the periodic homogenization is valid: as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the solution
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
converges (e.g., in
L^{2}(\Omega)
) to
\widehat{u}_{\delta}
, the solution of the
homogenized
problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}(\widehat{u}_{\delta})=-\mathrm{div}\,(\widehat{A}_{\delta}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\delta})=f&&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,\\
&\widehat{u}_{\delta}=g&&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

where
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}=-\mathrm{div}\,(\widehat{A}_{\delta}\nabla)
is the homogenized operator with coefficient

\widehat{A}_{\delta}=\big(\widehat{a}_{\delta,ij}^{\alpha\beta}\big)
defined by

\widehat{a}_{\delta,ij}^{\alpha\beta}=\int_{Y}\Lambda_{\delta}(y)\Big[a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(y)+a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\chi_{\delta,j}^{\gamma\beta}\Big]\,dy.

(1.9)

Here
\Lambda_{\delta}(y)=\delta\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}(y)+\mathbbm{1}_{Y\setminus\overline{\omega}}(y)
is the contrast function in the unit cell, and for each
i
and
\alpha
,
\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}=\big(\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha 1},\cdots,\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha m}\big)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the solution of the
cell problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{1,\delta}\big(\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}+y_{i}e^{\alpha}\big)=0\qquad\mathrm{in}\ Y,\\
&\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}\ \mathrm{is\ }Y\mathrm{-periodic\ and\ mean\ zero},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

where
e^{\alpha}=(0,\cdots,1,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
1
in the
\alpha^{\mathrm{th}}
position. We denote the tensor
\big(\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha\beta}\big)
by
\chi_{\delta}
. Note that, for
\delta=1
,
\chi_{1}
is the solution to the classical cell problem in standard periodic homogenization. The optimal
H^{1}
convergence rate
O(\varepsilon)
with some proper correctors and the regularity estimates for
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
are also obtained, see the monograph
[
25
]
and references therein.

It is natural to ask whether the above quantitative results are uniform for
\delta\in(0,\infty)
. Such problems are basic for understanding physical and engineering models with rapidly oscillating and high-contrast coefficients. For instance,

(a)

In biomedical imaging, there is a need to probe anomalous tissues, such as tumors or thrombus. The elastic modulus of these anomalous tissues is often significantly greater than that of the surrounding normal tissues, as observed in
[
18
]
.

(b)

Designing meta-materials exhibiting novel electromagnetic or elastic properties. Meta-materials are composite materials typically composed of arrays of small resonators structured on the microscale, the resonance occurs when the electromagnetic or elastic modulus of resonators are high-contrast compared to that of the background, see
[
23
,
3
]
.

(c)

The double porosity problem, studied by Arbogast, Douglas, and Hornung
[
4
]
, which models single phase flow in fractured porous media. In their context,
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is the fluid pressure, and
\delta=\varepsilon^{2}
is the permeability that is much smaller in the porous rocks
D_{\varepsilon}
than in the network of fractures
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Main results.

The first main result of this article concerns the uniform convergence rate of
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in
0<\delta<\infty
. As far as we know, this is the first result in the full inhomogeneous boundary value problem (that is, with nonzero source term and boundary data) in the high contrast setting.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) and
\mathbf{(H1)}
, let
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be the solution of (
1.7
) and
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}^{-1}(f)
be the solution of (
1.8
). Let
v=\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,1}(v)=f&\mathrm{in}\ D_{\varepsilon},\\
&v=0&\mathrm{on}\ \Omega\setminus D_{\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

Then we have

\displaystyle\Big\|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\delta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\delta}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)S_{\varepsilon}\Big(\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle-\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(1.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big).

Here
C
depends only on
d,m,\kappa,\mu,\Omega
and
\omega
,
S_{\varepsilon}
is a smoothing operator defined in (
2.6
), and
\eta_{\varepsilon}
is a cut-off function supported away from
\partial\Omega
defined in (
4.5
).

Remark 1
.

By a simple rescaling argument, it is clear that

\varepsilon^{-2}\|\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{D_{\varepsilon}}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{D_{\varepsilon}}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

so if
\delta\gg\varepsilon
, the term
\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)
is negligible as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Similarly, if we modify
f
in (
1.7
) by

f_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),\qquad&\text{for }\,\delta\geq 1,\\
\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)f(x),\qquad&\text{for }\,\delta\in(0,1),\end{cases}

(1.13)

and replace
\widehat{u}_{\delta}
by
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta}^{-1}(f_{\varepsilon,\delta})
in (
1.12
), we can drop the term
\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)
there as well.

The second main objective of this paper is to establish uniform regularity estimates for
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
both in
0<\varepsilon<1
and
0<\delta<\infty
. From the aforementioned convergence rate result, it is reasonable that we replace
f
by
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
for
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
(\mathbf{H1})
and
(\mathbf{H2})
, further assume
\Omega,\omega
are
C^{1,\alpha}
domains for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p\geq d
and
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
. For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, let
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be defined by (
1.13
), in other words, for relatively soft inclusions, we assume that the source term inside
D_{\varepsilon}
is of order
O(\delta)
. Then there is a constant
C>0
that depends only on
\kappa,\mu,\lambda,\tau,d,m,p,\alpha,\Omega
and
\omega
such that if
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
solves (
1.7
) with
f
replaced by
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, then we have

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}\right).

(1.14)

This is a global Lipschitz regularity estimate for
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
that is uniform both in
\varepsilon
and
\delta
. The interior version was essentially established in Shen’s work. We base our work on Shen’s and establish the boundary regularity by carrying out the approximation scheme.

Remark 2
.

Several remarks are in order.

(1) It is possible to include the two extreme settings, namely
\delta=0
and
\delta=\infty
in our analysis. For
\delta=0
, we consider the heterogeneous elliptic system posed on the perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with zero Neumann boundary condition on the interior boundary
\Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{i}\left(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon})\partial_{j}u^{\beta}_{\varepsilon}\right)=f^{\alpha}&&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\nu^{i}a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon})\partial_{j}u^{\beta}_{\varepsilon}(x)=0&&\mathrm{on}\ \Omega\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=g&&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.15)

For
\delta=\infty
, we consider the so-called stiff inclusion problem: find
u_{\varepsilon}=(u_{\varepsilon}^{\beta})
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
so that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a constant vector in each of the inclusion
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}
of
D_{\varepsilon}
, and
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{i}\left(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon})\partial_{j}u^{\beta}_{\varepsilon}\right)=f^{\alpha}&&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
&\int_{\partial\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}}e^{\alpha}\cdot\left.\left(\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right\rvert_{+}\,d\sigma=\int_{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}}e^{\alpha}\cdot f&&\text{for each }\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}\subset D_{\varepsilon},\\
&u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=g&&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.16)

The quantitative homogenization of (
1.15
) is similar to the analysis in
[
15
,
22
]
; that for the stiff inclusion problem is similar to
[
10
]
. In particular, the homogenized tensors are
\widehat{A}_{0}
and
\widehat{A}_{\infty}
defined by (
3.12
) and (

(2) The ellipticity condition (
1.2
) is often referred to as
strong
ellipticity, to be distinguished from the

setting.
It is possible to combine the method of this paper and those of Shen to analyze the same problem for the elasticity system. The main difference is, in the latter setting

This is a problem in monograph by Jikov, Kozlov and Oleinik
[
13
]
.
In the scalar case with

,
A=I

]
, the

[

]
. Also see earlier work by Schweizer
[

]
and Masmoudi

for related uniform estimates in the case

. Russell
[

]
established the large-scale interior Lipschitz estimate for the system of elasticity with bounded measurable coefficients in the case

[

]
. The case

was treated by Russell
[

]

.
In the stochastic setting with
\delta=0
, Armstrong and Dario
[
5
]
obtained quantitative homogenization and large-scale regularity results for the random conductance model on a supercritical percolation.

For the case
1<\delta<\infty
, Shen
[
27
]
established the interior Lipschitz estimates by adapting a recent method proposed by Armstrong et al.
[
6
]
, which is based on a Caccioppoli type inequality and the idea that transfer the higher-order regularity of

We briefly sketch our approach to the proof. In this paper, we adapt the approximation method originated by Armstrong and Smart
[
8
]
, which was used by Russell
[
21
,
22
]
for treating the case
0\leq\delta<1
. The main difficulty of this method in the case
1<\delta<\infty
is the establishment of uniform

convergence rate. To this end, we use the periodic layer potentials to obtain the uniform controll for quantities associated to the cell problem, i.e., the homogenized tensor
\widehat{A}
, the flux corrector and etc. This combine with a standard elliptic estimate yield the
H^{1}
convergence rate in
D_{\varepsilon}
. Then a key observation is that the discrepancy function could be decomposed to a regular one and a singular one, the regular part is easy to be bounded, and

norm of the singular one is bounded by its

norm. These complete the proof of

convergence rate. Via the results of convergence rates, a Caccippoli-type inequality by Shen
[
27
]
and its variants, and an approximation method (also known as a Campanato-type scheme) by Armstrong and Smart
[
8
]
, we establish the regularity estimates.

2.
Layer potentials, Extension operators and basic elliptic estimates

2.1.
Periodic layer potential

Let the coefficient
A
satisfies (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), in view of the interior Lipschitz regularity for
\mathcal{L}
, proved by Avellaneda and Lin
[
9
]
, one has the periodic fundamental solution
\Gamma(x,y)=(\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}(x,y)):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(x)\big(\Gamma^{\alpha}(x,y)\big)=(\delta_{y}(x)-1)e^{\alpha},&(x,y)\in Y\times Y,\\
&\int_{Y}\Gamma^{\alpha}(x,y)\,dx=0,&y\in Y,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
\delta_{y}(x)
is the Dirac measure massed at
y
, and the estimates

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|\Gamma(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{2-d},\\
&|\nabla_{x}\Gamma(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}\Gamma(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{1-d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

The function
\Gamma(x,y)
is called the periodic fundamental solution for the operator
\mathcal{L}
with pole at
y
.
For construction of the periodic fundamental solution and estimates (
2.2
), we refer to Hofmann and Kim
[
12
]
, where they treat the case of zero Dirichlet boundary condition, but their methods apply also in the periodic setting.

For

, based on the estimates (
2.2
), one can define the single-layer potential

by

\big(\mathcal{S}f\big)^{\alpha}(x)=\int_{\partial\omega}\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}(x,y)f^{\beta}(y)\,d\sigma(y)

(2.3)

while the Neumann-Poincaré (NP) operator

is defined by

\big(\mathcal{K}^{*}f\big)^{\alpha}(x)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\partial\omega}\left(\frac{\partial\Gamma^{\beta}}{\partial\nu_{x}}\right)^{\alpha}(x,y)f^{\beta}(y)\,d\sigma(y),

(2.4)

where P.V. stands for the Cauchy principal value integral. For more regular
\omega
, say
\partial\omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the above can be replaced by the regular integral.

Denote by
L^{2}_{0}(\partial\omega)
the space
\{\phi\in L^{2}(\partial\omega)\,:\,\int_{\partial\omega}\phi=0\}
. The following properties are standard:

(a)

For
\psi\in L^{2}(\partial\omega)
,
\mathcal{S}\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

(b)

For

, jump relation holds, i.e.,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\big(\mathcal{S}\psi\big)\Big|_{\omega\pm}=\Big(\pm\frac{1}{2}I+\mathcal{K}^{*}\Big)\psi.

(2.5)

(c)

For
\psi\in L^{2}_{0}(\partial\omega)
,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}\psi)=0
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\partial\omega
.

(d)

The spectrum of
\mathcal{K}^{*}
on

is contained in

.

We refer to
[

,

]

\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}w=\left((\partial_{\nu}w)^{\alpha}\right),\quad\text{with}\ \ (\partial_{\nu}w)^{\alpha}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nu_{i}A^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{j}w^{\beta}.

2.2.
Extension operators

The following extension operator is useful in the treatment of perforated domains. The problem setting for high contrast inclusions is in a similar situation.
Recall that

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(\mathbf{H2})
, there exists a linear extension operator
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}:H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
such that for any

,

\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}v=v\quad\mathrm{in}\ \Omega_{\varepsilon}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\|\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

We refer to
[
10
]
or
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
; see also
[
1
, Appendix B]
. The basic idea is, at the unit scale around a typical obsctale
\omega
and for a function
v\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})
with mean value
c=\fint_{\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega}}v
,
we can apply the usual extension operator to
v-c
and obtain a
v_{1}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega})
so that

\|v_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq\|v-c\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{(}\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})},

where the last step results from Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality. Then
\tilde{v}:=c+v_{1}
is an extension of
v
from
\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega}
to
\widetilde{\omega}
and satisfies

\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})}.

Under
(\mathbf{H2})
, the desired extension
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
in the whole domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is by gluing the rescaled local extensions together.
∎

As we will see, the extension above is useful for small
\delta
in the high contrast setting of this paper. For large
\delta
, we need the following operator that extends functions defined in the inclusions with zero mean in each inclusion to the outside.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
(\mathbf{H2})
, there exists a linear map
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}:H^{1}(D_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

such that
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(v)
is supported in
\cup_{\mathbf{n}}\widetilde{\omega}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}
, and

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(v)=v-q^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon},\qquad q^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}}v,\qquad\mathrm{in\ each\ }\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{\varepsilon}.

Moreover,

\|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(v)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}.

Proof.

As usual we first consider an extension operator in the unit scale. Suppose
\omega
is the model inclusion of unit length, and
\widetilde{\omega}
is the enlarged inclusion. Then there is a standard linear extension operator
\mathcal{Q}
from
H^{1}(\omega)
to
H^{1}_{0}(\widetilde{\omega})
so that

\|\mathcal{Q}(v)\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}.

Since the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality holds for
v-q
, where
q=\int_{\omega}v
, we get

\|\mathcal{Q}(v-q)\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq C\|v-q\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

In each small inclusion
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}
that is contained in
\Omega
, we let

\mathcal{Q}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}(v)(x):=\mathcal{Q}\Big(v(\varepsilon(\cdot+\mathbf{n}))-q^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon\mathbf{n}}{\varepsilon}\Big),\qquad x\in\widetilde{\omega}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}.

Then we see that
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}(v)
belongs to
H^{1}_{0}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}})
and satisfies
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}(v)=v-q^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}
, and

\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}(v)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}})},

where the constant
C
does not change due to the scaling-invariant feature of the estimate. For
v\in H^{1}(D_{\varepsilon})
, we use the map piecewise for each component
\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}
of
D_{\varepsilon}
and glue them together,
we then get a linear map
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
with the desired properties.
∎

2.3.
Other useful tools and estimates

We will use the now standard smoothing operator: fix a function
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,\frac{1}{2}))
such that
0\leq\xi(x)\leq 1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(x)\,dx=1
, for any
v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
S_{\varepsilon}(v)
is the smooth function defined by

S_{\varepsilon}(v)(x):=\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(x-y)\xi(y/\varepsilon)\,dy.

(2.6)

The operator is very useful in quantitative homogenization theory; it allows one to use two-scale expansions because the regularization error can be well controlled. In particular, we have the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then for any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\|f(x/\varepsilon)S_{\varepsilon}(g)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We refer to Shen
[
25
, Proposition 3.1.5]
for the proof, together with more useful properties of
S_{\varepsilon}
. We end the section by the following useful but standard estimate for elliptic systems.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A=\big(a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}\big)
be a constant tensor satisfying (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain with connected boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
u
be the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\mathrm{div}\,(A\nabla u)=f&\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,\\
&u=g&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{t})}+t\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{O}_{t})}\leq Ct^{1/2}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big),

where
C
depends only on
\mu
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

This follows from the interior estimates and the nontangential-maximal-function estimate
for second-order elliptic equations with constant coefficients. See
[
17
]
for reference.
∎

Remark 3
.

Note that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain, it is impossible to control
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, since the solution may have a singular behavior near the irregular points of
\partial\Omega
, even in the simplest case of the Laplace equation, if
\Omega
is a non-convex polygon in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it is well known
[
11
]
that
u\notin H^{2}(\Omega)
in general, even if
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
g=0
.

3.
Limiting behavior associated to cell problem

Our overall strategy in this work is to treat
\varepsilon
and
\delta
as two independent parameters. This approach was mentioned already in Chap 3 of
[
13
]
. In general, we can consider the diagram

\begin{CD}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}@>{\delta\to 0\text{ or }\infty}>{}>u_{\varepsilon}\\
@V{\varepsilon\to 0}V{}V@V{}V{\varepsilon\to 0}V\\
\widehat{u}_{\delta}@>{\delta\to 0\text{ or }\infty}>{}>\widehat{u}\end{CD}

(3.1)

We show in this section that
\widehat{u}_{\delta}
, or the equation for it, behaves quite well uniformly in
\delta
. In the scalar setting, such uniform in
\delta
results were established in Jikov using variational techniques. Here, we obtain the results for the elliptic system using layer-potential techniques.

Note that to use layer potentials, we require the coefficient
A
satisfies (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) (see Section
2.1
), nevertheless, by using a ’weak’ form of layer potentials, one can obtain all of results in this section, see Appendix
A
for the construction of ’weak’ form of layer potentials. For simplicity, we assume (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) for
A
in this section, but as we have mentioned, the readers should keep in mind that the results in this section also holds for
A
satisfying (
1.2
)-(
1.3
).

3.1.
Uniform bounds for the cell problems

Recall that

\chi_{\delta}=(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\alpha})_{1\leq j\leq d,1\leq\alpha\leq m},

denotes the solution to the cell problem that arises in the standard homogenization of (
1.7
) for each fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
. What is most important is to obtain estimates that are either uniform in
\delta
or have explicit dependence in
\delta
. We achieve this goal through the layer potential representation of the cell problem.

Lemma 3.1
.

For fixed
0<\delta<\infty
, let
\chi_{\delta}=(\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha})
,
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, be the solution of the cell problem (
1.10
). Then for
\delta\neq 1
,
\chi_{\delta}
has the folowing layer potential representation:

\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}=\chi^{\alpha}_{1,i}+\mathcal{S}\left[\big(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\right]-q_{\delta,i}^{\alpha},\quad\lambda(\delta)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\delta-1},

(3.2)

where

\phi_{i}^{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}\big(y_{i}e^{\alpha}+\chi_{1,i}^{\alpha}\big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}\big(y_{i}e^{\alpha}+\chi_{1,i}^{\alpha}\big)\in H_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial\omega),

(3.3)

and

q_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}=\int_{Y}\mathcal{S}\left[\big(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\right](y)\,dy.

(3.4)

Here, the periodic single-layer potential operator
\mathcal{S}
and the associated Neumann-Poincaré operator
\mathcal{K}^{*}
are defined by (
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
u=\chi^{\alpha}_{\delta,i}-\chi^{\alpha}_{1,i}
, then

u
is the solution of the transmission problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}u=0&\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\partial\omega,\\
&\delta\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}=(1-\delta)\phi_{i}^{\alpha}&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\omega,\\
&\int_{Y}u(y)\,dy=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We aim to represent the solution by
u=\mathcal{S}\psi-q
, where
q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In view of the properties of layer potentials (See Section
2.1
),
\psi
and
q
must satisfy

\big(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)\psi=\phi_{i}^{\alpha},\qquad q=\int_{Y}\mathcal{S}\psi(y)\,dy.

The integral system above is solvble for
\psi\in L_{0}^{2}(\partial\omega)
, since
|\lambda(\delta)|>1/2
when
0<\delta<\infty
and the spectrum of
\mathcal{K}^{*}
on
L^{2}_{0}(\partial\omega)
is contained in
(-1/2,1/2)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that
\lambda(\delta)
converges to
-1/2
as
\delta\to 0+
, and converges to
1/2
as
\delta\to\infty
. We define the limit tensors
\chi_{0}=(\chi^{\alpha}_{0,i})
and
\chi_{\infty}=(\chi^{\alpha}_{\infty,i})
,
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\chi^{\alpha}_{0,i}:=\chi^{\alpha}_{1,i}+\mathcal{S}\big(-I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}_{0,i},\\
&\chi_{\infty,i}^{\alpha}:=\chi^{\alpha}_{1,i}+\mathcal{S}\big(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}-q^{\alpha}_{\infty,i},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

where
\phi_{i}^{\alpha}
is defined in (
3.3
) and

q_{0,i}^{\alpha}=\int_{Y}\mathcal{S}\left[\big(-I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\right](y)\,dy,\qquad q_{\infty,i}^{\alpha}=\int_{Y}\mathcal{S}\left[\big(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\right](y)\,dy.

(3.6)

Indeed, as we show next,
\chi_{0}
and
\chi_{\infty}
are the limits of
\chi_{\delta}
as
\delta
tends to
0
and to
\infty
, respectively. In fact, they correspond to the cell problem of the soft (
\delta=0
) and hard (
\delta=\infty
) inclusion problems, respectively.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
1\leq i\leq d
and
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, the function
\chi_{0,i}^{\alpha}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the solution of the soft problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(\chi_{0,i}^{\alpha})=-\mathcal{L}(y_{i}e^{\alpha})&\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\partial\omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\chi_{0,i}^{\alpha}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}=-\frac{\partial(y_{i}e^{\alpha})}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\omega,\\
&\int_{Y}\chi_{0,i}^{\alpha}(y)\,dy=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

The function
\chi^{\alpha}_{\infty,i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the solution of the stiff problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(\chi_{\infty,i}^{\alpha})=-\mathcal{L}(y_{i}e^{\alpha})&\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\partial\omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\chi_{\infty,i}^{\alpha}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}=-\frac{\partial(y_{i}e^{\alpha})}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\omega,\\
&\int_{Y}\chi_{\infty,i}^{\alpha}(y)\,dy=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.8)

Moreover, there is a universal constant
C<\infty
so that

\|\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(Y)}\leq C\delta\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\|\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{\infty}\|_{H^{1}(Y)}\leq C\delta^{-1},

(3.9)

and hence also
\|\chi_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C
.

Proof.

In view of Section
2.1
, especially the jump relation, it is clear that
\chi^{\alpha}_{0,i}
and
\chi^{\alpha}_{\infty,i}
is the solution of problem (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), respectively. We now prove (
3.9
).

Since
\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*}\rightarrow\mp\frac{1}{2}I-\mathcal{K}^{*}
as
\delta
tends to
0
or
\infty
and
\mp\frac{1}{2}I-\mathcal{K}^{*}
are bijections on
L^{2}_{0}(\partial\omega)
, by the basic theory of functional analysis, we obtain that
\|(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\|
is uniformly bounded in
0<\delta<\infty
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\big\|\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}-\chi_{0,i}^{\alpha}\big\|_{H^{1}(Y)}\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{S}\big[(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}-(-I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\big]\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\big\|_{H^{1}(Y)}+\big|q_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}-q_{0,i}^{\alpha}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\big\|\mathcal{S}\big[(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}-(-I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\big]\phi_{i}^{\alpha}\big\|_{H^{1}(Y)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\lambda(\delta)+1/2\big)\big\|\big(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\big\|\cdot\big\|(I/2+\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta.

This proves the convergence rate when
\delta\rightarrow 0
. The proof for rate of
\delta\rightarrow\infty
follows the same lines and is hence omitted. The uniform estimates for
\|\chi_{\delta}\|_{H^{1}}
follows also because
\|\chi_{1}\|_{H^{1}}
is easily bounded, and by (
3.5
) the bounds for
\chi_{0},\chi_{\infty}
also hold.
∎

Remark 4
.

In Appendix
A
,
we define a
’weak’ form of single layer potentials via Lax-Milgram theorem, which maps from
H^{-1/2}
to
H^{1/2}
, and the corresponding NP operator without smoothness condition (
1.4
). Using the ’weak’ form of layer potentials, all above results can be recovered by the same analysis.

3.2.
Uniform bound for the homogenized tensor

Recall that, for each fixed
\delta>0
, the homogenized tensor for (
1.7
) is
\hat{A}_{\delta}
defined by (
1.9
). We show that
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
enjoys uniform (in dependent of
\delta
) ellipticity.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
0<\delta<\infty
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
,
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m
, we have

\Big\|\Lambda_{\delta}\Big(a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}+a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\chi_{\delta,j}^{\gamma\beta}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C.

(3.10)

Proof.

The aim is to bound the
L^{2}(Y)
norm of

\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{m}\Lambda_{\delta}(y)a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\left(\chi^{\gamma\beta}_{\delta,j}(y)+y_{j}e^{\beta}\right),

for each fixed
i,j,\alpha
and
\beta
. By Theorem
3.2
, we have

\Big\|\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0})\Big\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\delta\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\Big\|\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}(\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{\infty})\Big\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C\delta^{-1}.

(3.11)

For
\delta\in(0,1]
, the desired estimates follow from the boundedness of
a
,
\|\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}
and
\delta\leq 1
. For
\delta\in(1,\infty)
, after replacing
\chi_{\delta}
by
\chi_{\infty}
and applying the above inequality, it remains to control

\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{m}\Lambda_{\delta}(y)a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\left(\chi^{\gamma\beta}_{\infty,j}(y)+y_{j}e^{\beta}\right)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

because
\Lambda_{\delta}=1
outside
\omega
and
\|\nabla\chi_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded. Now in view of the stiff cell problem (
3.8
) (for the part in
\omega
), we must have
\chi_{\infty,j}^{\beta}+y_{j}e^{\beta}=c
for some constant vector
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, the above quantity vanishes. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The homogenized tensor
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
is continuous as a function of
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, we denote
\widehat{A}_{0}
by
\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\widehat{A}_{\delta}
and
\widehat{A}_{\infty}
by
\lim_{\delta\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{A}_{\delta}
, then

\widehat{a}_{0,ij}^{\alpha\beta}=\int_{Y\setminus\overline{\omega}}\Big[a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}+a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\chi_{0,j}^{\gamma\beta}\Big]\,dy,

(3.12)

and

\widehat{a}_{\infty,ij}^{\alpha\beta}=\int_{\omega}a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial w_{j}^{\gamma\beta}}{\partial y_{k}}\,dy+\int_{Y\setminus\overline{\omega}}\Big[a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}+a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\chi_{\infty,j}^{\gamma\beta}\Big]\,dy,

(3.13)

where for each fixed
j=1
and
\alpha
, the function
w^{\alpha}_{j}\in H^{1}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the unique (up to an additive constant) solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}(w_{j}^{\alpha})=0&\mathrm{in}\ \omega,\\
&\frac{\partial w_{j}^{\alpha}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{-}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}(\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}+y_{j}e^{\alpha})\Big|_{+}&\mathrm{on}\ \partial\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.14)

It is worth to mention that, the tensor
\widehat{A}_{0}
(respectively,
\widehat{A}_{\infty}
) is the homogenized tensor for the soft inclusion problem (respectively, stiff inclusion problem); see Remark
2
.

Proof.

By the definition (
1.9
) of
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
and the single-layer potential representations of the solutions to cell problem, it is clear that
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
is continuous as a function of
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, and (
3.12
) follows directly from the convergence
\|\chi_{\delta}-\chi_{0}\|_{H^{1}(Y)}\leq C\delta
. It remains to verify
(
3.13
).

First we note that the problem for
w_{j}^{\alpha}
is well posed. We need to check that the Neumann data integrates to zero over
\partial\omega
. By the definition (
3.5
) and the jump relation of layer potentials,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}(\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}+y_{j}e^{\alpha})\Big|_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}(\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}+y_{j}e^{\alpha})\Big|_{-}+\big(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\big(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*}\big)^{-1}\phi_{j}^{\alpha}.

The solvability of
w_{j}^{\delta}
is verified. Moreover, we check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\alpha}-\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\delta-1}w_{j}^{\alpha})=0\;\text{ in }\omega,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\alpha}-\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\delta-1}w_{j}^{\alpha})\Big\rvert_{-}=\left[I+(-I/2+\mathcal{K}^{*})(\lambda(\delta)I-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}-\frac{1}{\delta}(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\right]\phi_{j}^{\alpha}.

The operators
(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})
and its inverse act on
L^{2}_{0}(\partial\omega)
. For
\delta>1
, the inverse operator can be written as Neumann series, and the second line becomes

\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\alpha}-\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\delta-1}w_{j}^{\alpha})\Big\rvert_{-}=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(\delta-1)^{\ell}}(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-\ell}.

By basic estimates of Neumann problem of elliptic system we get

\|\nabla(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\alpha}-\chi^{\alpha}_{\infty,j}-\frac{1}{\delta-1}w^{\alpha}_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\delta^{-2},

(3.15)

for all
\delta>1
sufficiently large, and
C<\infty
depends only on the norm of
\|(I/2-\mathcal{K}^{*})^{-1}\|
. Using the fact that
\chi_{\infty,j}^{\alpha}+y_{j}e^{\alpha}
is a constant vector in
\omega
, we rewrite

\displaystyle\widehat{a}_{\delta,ij}^{\alpha\beta}-\widehat{a}_{\infty,ij}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{\omega}a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\big(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\gamma\beta}-\chi^{\gamma\beta}_{\infty,j}-\frac{1}{\delta-1}w_{j}^{\beta}\big)\,dy+\int_{Y\setminus\overline{\omega}}a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\big(\chi_{\delta,j}^{\gamma\beta}-\chi_{\infty,j}^{\gamma\beta}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta-1}\int_{\omega}a_{ik}^{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial u_{j}^{\gamma\beta}}{\partial y_{k}}\,dy.

Send
\delta\to\infty
, due to the bounds (
3.15
) and (
3.10
), we obtain the desired result. In fact, we get
|\widehat{A}_{\delta}-\widehat{A}_{\infty}|\leq C\delta^{-1}
for sufficiently large
\delta
.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
3.4
, we show that
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
is uniform elliptic in
0<\delta<\infty
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
A
satisfies (
1.2
)(
1.3
), then
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
is uniformly elliptic in
\delta
, i.e. there exists a universal positive number
\mu_{1}
, so that for any
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, we have
|\widehat{A}_{\delta}|\leq\mu_{1}^{-1}
and

\widehat{a}_{\delta,ij}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\xi_{j}^{\beta}\geq\mu_{1}|\xi|^{2}\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{any}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{dm}.

(3.16)

Proof.

We have established a uniform in
\delta
bound of
|\widehat{A}_{\delta}|
, so we concentrate here on proving (
3.16
). Given
\xi=(\xi_{i}^{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}^{dm}
, let
w_{\delta}=\xi^{\alpha}_{i}(y_{i}e^{\alpha}+\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha})
be the combination of the correctors
\chi_{\delta}
’s. We have

\displaystyle\widehat{a}_{\delta,ij}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\xi_{j}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=(J^{Y\setminus\overline{\omega}}+\delta J^{\omega})(w_{\delta})

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\delta,1\}\mu\int_{Y}|\nabla w_{\delta}|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\delta,1\}\mu\left|\int_{Y}\nabla w_{\delta}\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\min\{\delta,1\}\mu|\xi|^{2}.

The last identity holds because the integral of
\nabla\chi_{\delta,i}^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
vanishes. Therefore, for
\delta\geq 1
, the lower ellipticity constant for
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
is uniformly bounded by
\mu
from below. For
\delta\in(0,1)
, in view of the continuity of
\widehat{A}_{\delta}
with respect to
\delta
, it sufficies to prove that
\widehat{A}_{0}
is elliptic, but this follows by the same reasoning as above.
∎

3.3.
Uniform bound of the flux corrector

Given a tensor
F=(F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij})
on
Y
, for
1\leq i,j\leq d
,
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq m
, such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\int_{Y}F^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(y)\,dy=0.

(3.17)

It is proved, for example see
[
25
]
, there exists a
tensor
\Psi=(\Psi^{\alpha\beta}_{kij})
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

and there exists a constant

\Psi

associated to

In this paper, we need to use the flux corrector

It is easy to check condition (

) for

, so

exists and

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

be defined by (

be the skew-symmetric flux-tensor related to

by (

\delta

4.1.
Uniform energy estimates

We first derive uniform

Theorem 4.1

Assume (

(4.1)

For

, if we replace

on the right hand side of (

Proof.

Note that for fixed
\varepsilon,\delta
, the uniquely solvability of
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is routine. Our goal is to establish the above uniform energy estimates.

Step 1: Control of the energy inside the inclusions
. Let
\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
be the extension
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}\rvert_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})
given by Lemma
2.1
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

The ellipticity of

and Poincaré inequality
\|u_{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}
yields

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\delta^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})},

\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(4.2)

with some bounding constant

that is independent of
\varepsilon,\delta
and

For

\delta\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}).

(4.3)

Step 2: Control of the total energy
. Let

be a function such that

on

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\big(\delta\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\big)\|\nabla G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

First consider the case of
\delta\leq 1

\delta
is not large, we get

The above combined with (
4.2

4.1

For the case of

) in the energy estimate and the argument above, the desired inequality for

∎

by
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{1}
. In other words, define

w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}+w_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{D_{\varepsilon}}.

(4.26)

Then we check that
w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and below it is referred to as the “regular” part of
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. In view of the estimates (
4.20
) and (
4.24
), we have

\big\|\nabla\big(w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}-\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(F)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|P_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|R_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

(4.27)

Similarly, denote by the differece between
w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}
by

w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=w_{\varepsilon,\delta}-w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}.

(4.28)

Then
w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
as well and below it is referred to as the “singular part”. Moreover, by definition we have

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,1}(w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta})=0\qquad\mathrm{in}\ \Omega\setminus\partial D_{\varepsilon}.

(4.29)

The next lemma plays an important role in our proof of Theorem
1.1
. It says that the distributions of energy in
w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
(in terms of the
L^{2}
norm of its gradient) is balanced inside and outside of the obstacles
D_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) and
(\mathbf{H2})
, then

C\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx\leq C^{-1}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\,dx.

(4.30)

Proof.

For simplicity, we simply denote
w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
by
w
in this proof. We divide the proof to several steps.

Step 1: We verify the first inequality by a standard extension argument
. Consider the extension operator
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}:H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\to H^{1}(\Omega)
defined in Lemma
2.1
. Let
\widetilde{w}=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}(w\rvert_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})
. Then we have
\widetilde{w}-w
vanishes at
\partial D_{\varepsilon}
. Multiply on both sides of (
4.29
) and integrate in
D_{\varepsilon}
, we find

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

where
C
only depends on the ellipticity bounds and the model domain
\omega
.

Step 2. We verify that for each inclusion
\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
,

\int_{\partial\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{\partial w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{+}\,d\sigma=0.

(4.31)

The above is understood as the pairing of
\partial_{\nu}w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}
with constant functions. By definition, along the boundary of each inclusion, we have

\partial_{\nu}w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}=\partial_{\nu}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}-\partial_{\nu}w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+},

where we used the fact that
w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, i.e., outside the inclusions. By an inspection of the equations satisfied by
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}-N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{+}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{+}=\delta\partial_{\nu}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-\delta N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{-}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{-},

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}-N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{+}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{+}=\partial_{\nu}w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{-}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{-},

which leads to

\partial_{\nu}w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{+}=\delta\partial_{\nu}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-\partial_{\nu}w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-(\delta-1)N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\rvert_{-}.

Integrate the equation of
w_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and the equation of
w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in
\omega^{\mathrm{n}}_{\varepsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\omega^{\mathrm{n}}_{\varepsilon}}\delta\partial_{\nu}w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-\delta N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\,d\sigma=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\omega^{\mathrm{n}}_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\nu}w^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\rvert_{-}-N^{i}P_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}-N^{i}Q_{\varepsilon,\delta,i}\,d\sigma=0.

Combining the results above we get (
4.31
).

Step 3. Proof of the second inequality
. Consider the map
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
defined in Lemma
2.2
, and apply it to
w\rvert_{D_{\varepsilon}}
to define

\widetilde{w}=\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(w\rvert_{D_{\varepsilon}}).

Then
\widetilde{w}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(in fact,
\tilde{w}
is supported in the union of bubbles surrounding compoenents of
D_{\varepsilon}
). Moreover,

\widetilde{w}-w=q_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}},\qquad\text{in each }\,\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Multiply by
\widetilde{w}-w
on both sides of (
4.29
) and integrate by parts. We obtain

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla w\cdot\nabla(w-\widetilde{w})\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\cdot(w-\widetilde{w})=-\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\int_{\partial\omega_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}\Big\rvert_{+}\cdot(w-\widetilde{w})=0.

From this we get by ellipticity that

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}.

The last inequality follows from Lemma
2.2
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are in a position to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the decomposition above (see (
4.26
) ad (
4.28
)), we have

w_{\varepsilon,\delta}=w_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{\mathrm{R}}+w_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{\mathrm{S}}.

In view of (
4.27
) we only need to estimate
\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Case 1:
0<\delta<1
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

(4.32)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|P_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|R_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big),

where the last equality follows from
(
4.9
) and (
4.27
) (noting that
\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)
is supported outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
).

Case 2:
1\leq\delta<\infty
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla w^{\mathrm{S}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\big(w^{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}-\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)\big)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}+C\delta^{-1}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}+C\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(D_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|P_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|R_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big),

where the last equality follows from (
4.27
), (
4.9
), a standard energy estimate for (
4.18
), and the fact that
\delta\geq 1
.

Combine the above estimates with (
4.27
). We conclude that, for all
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, we have found a universal constant
C<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\big(w_{\varepsilon,\delta}

\displaystyle-\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(F)\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|P_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|Q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|R_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

The desired estimate (
1.12
) then follows from (
4.25
).
∎

Remark 5
.

Note that using a boundary cut-off, for Type I domain, the same method of analysis still yields a similar
H^{1}
convergence rate result away the boundary:

\displaystyle\Big\|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\delta}-\varepsilon\chi_{\delta}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)S_{\varepsilon}\Big(\eta_{\varepsilon}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle-\delta^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{D_{\varepsilon}}^{-1}(f)\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{O}_{c})}

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{tan}}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\big)

where
c>0
is a fixed number independent of
\varepsilon
.

5.
Caccioppoli-type inequalities

We will prove the uniform Lipschitz estimate for

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})=f_{\varepsilon,\delta},\qquad&&x\in\Omega,\\
&u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=g,\qquad&&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

Here,
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}=f
for
\delta\in(1,\infty)
, and
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}f
if
\delta\in(0,1]
. We follow the now standard approach by Shen
[
25
]
. A key step in the scheme is to establish (interior and boundary) Caccioppoli inequalities. In the high contrast setting, this turns out to be a non-trivial task especially for large
\delta
. The following rescaling property of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
will be used throughout the rest of the paper: if we define
\tilde{u}(\cdot)=u(r\cdot)
and
\tilde{f}(\cdot)=r^{2}f(r\cdot)
,

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon/r,\delta}(\tilde{u})=\tilde{f}.

In particular when
r=\varepsilon
, we relate the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
to
\mathcal{L}_{1,\delta}
. The latter operator was studied by Shen in
[
27
]
, where Caccioppoli inequalities and large scale interior Lipschitz regularity were proved. We modify Shen’s proof to obtain Caccioppoli inequalities for large
\delta
allowing the presence of source term
f
and boundary data
g
. Those combined with the convergence rate result of the previous section allows us to obtain boundary Lipschitz regularity estimate in the high contrast setting.

From here, we always assume assumptions
(\mathbf{H1})
and
(\mathbf{H2})
hold. We start from two basic energy estimates with cut-off functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a solution to
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f
in
\Omega
. Then for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)|u|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|f|\varphi^{2}|u|.

(5.2)

Proof.

Let
v=u\varphi^{2}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then we have

\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)A(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon})\nabla u\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v,

the conclusion follows by integration by parts and the ellipticity of
A
, we refer
[
25
, Theorem 2.1.3]
to the readers for details.
∎

For a boundary version of Lemma
5.1
, we use the following standard notation: let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r>0
, let
\mathbf{D}_{r}=\mathbf{D}_{r}(x_{0},\psi)
be the boundary cylinder

\mathbf{D}_{r}=\Phi\{(x^{\prime},z)\,:\,|x^{\prime}-x_{0}|<r,0<z-\psi(x^{\prime})<M_{0}r\},

where
(x^{\prime},z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
denotes local coordinate close to
x_{0}
,
\Phi=\Phi_{x_{0}}
is an affine transform that maps
(0,0)
in the new coordinate system to
x_{0}
, and allign the subspace
\{(x^{\prime},0)\,:\,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\}
to the tangent space of
T_{x_{0}}\partial\Omega
, and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
(or Lipschitz) function in the new coordinate whose graph (after mapped by
\Phi
) corresponds to
\partial\Omega
, and
M_{0}
is a regularity character of
\psi
. Let

\Delta_{r}=\partial\mathbf{D}_{r}\cap\partial\Omega.

Note that
\Delta_{r}
is part of
\partial\Omega
and is a proper subset of
\partial\mathbf{D}_{r}
. It is clear that the boundary cylinders
\{\mathbf{D}_{r}(x_{0})\}
are comparable with
\{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega\}
, in the sense that one class can cover the other.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f
in
\Omega
with
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, then for any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbf{D}_{R})
with support of
\varphi
away from
\partial\mathbf{D}_{R}\cap\Omega
, we have

\int_{\mathbf{D}_{R}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbf{D}_{R}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)|u|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+C\int_{\mathbf{D}_{R}}|f|\varphi^{2}|u|.

(5.3)

The proof is exactly as in the interior case, because, thanks to the zero Dirichlet data
u=0
on
\Delta_{R}
, the function
\varphi^{2}u
belongs to
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbf{D}_{R})
. We hence omit the details.

5.1.
Caccioppoli inequalities for small
\delta

For relatively soft inclusions, the Caccioppoli inequality can be obtained by the standard method provided that the source term
f
is of order
\delta
in the inclusions. Recall that for
\delta\in(0,1]
,
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
is defined by (
1.13
).

Recall that
\widetilde{\omega}
denotes an enlarging dilation of
\omega
so that
\overline{\omega}\subset\widetilde{\omega}\subset Q_{7/8}(0)
, and
\widetilde{\omega}^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(\mathbf{n}+\widetilde{\omega})
(simplified by
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
) is the dilation of
\omega^{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}
(simplified by
\omega_{\varepsilon}
). The next lemma provides a way to control the energy
inside

\omega_{\varepsilon}
of a solution to the elliptic system by its energy
outside
, namely the energy in
\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{m})
solves
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,1}(u)=f
in
\omega_{\varepsilon}
, then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left(\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon})}\right).

Proof.

This result is essentially Lemma 2.3 of
[
27
]
and it is proved by a simple extension argument. By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
. Let
v
be the extension of
u-\fint_{\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega}}u
from
\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega}
to
\widetilde{\omega}
, then
\|v\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}\setminus\overline{\omega})}
. The desired result then follows by testing
u-v\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega)
against the equation
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}(u)=f
.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Caccioppoli inequalities for small
\delta
)
.

Suppose
\delta\in(0,1]
and let
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
be defined by (
1.13
).

(i)

(Interior estimate)
Let
R\geq 4\varepsilon
and
Q_{2R}
be a cube in
\Omega
. If
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in
Q_{2R}
,
then

\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\fint_{Q_{2R}}|u|^{2}+R^{2}\fint_{Q_{2R}}|f|^{2}\right).

(5.4)

(ii)

(Boundary estimate)
Let
R\geq 4\varepsilon
and
\mathbf{D}_{R}
be a boundary cylinder. If
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in
\mathbf{D}_{2R}
and
u=g
in
\Delta_{2R}
, then

\fint_{\mathbf{D}_{3R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left\{\frac{1}{R^{2}}\fint_{\mathbf{D}_{2R}}|u|^{2}+R^{2}\fint_{\mathbf{D}_{2R}}|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2R})}+\|\nabla_{\rm tan}g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2R})}\right\}.

(5.5)

Proof of (i) – the interior case.

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(Q_{2R};[0,1])
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
in
Q_{3R/2}
and
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C/R
, plug it in (
5.2
) and replacing
f
there by
f_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. Using Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{3R/2}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)|\nabla u|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{2R}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)|u|^{2}+CR^{2}\int_{Q_{2R}}\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)|f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{2R}}|u|^{2}+CR^{2}\int_{Q_{2R}}|f|^{2}.

Now for each
\omega_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{\varepsilon}
that has non-empty intersection with
Q_{R}
, its enlarged domain
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
is contained in
Q_{R+2\varepsilon}\subset Q_{3R/2}
. By Lemma
5.3
, we have

\int_{Q_{R}\cap D_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{Q_{3R/2}\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{Q_{R}\cap D_{\varepsilon}}\delta^{-2}|f_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right).

We combine the two estimates above and get

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{2R}}|u|^{2}+R^{2}\int_{Q_{2R}}|f|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{Q_{R}\cap D_{\varepsilon}}\delta^{-2}|f_{\varepsilon,\delta}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{2R}}|u|^{2}+R^{2}\int_{Q_{2R}}|f|^{2}\right),

here we used the fact that
\varepsilon<R
to deduce the second line. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of (ii) – the boundary case.

By rescaling we assume
R=1
. We first solve the problem

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(v)=h\ \ \text{in }\,\mathbf{D}_{2+4\varepsilon},\qquad v=\widetilde{g}\ \ \text{on }\,\partial\mathbf{D}_{2}.

Here, we let the inclusions in
\mathbf{D}_{2+4\varepsilon}
be exactly those of
D_{\varepsilon}
that had non-empty intersections with
\mathbf{D}_{2}
. Let
F
denote the union of these inclusions, then the pair
(\mathbf{D}_{2+4\varepsilon},F)
satisfying Assumption
\mathbf{G}
is a type-II perforated domain. The source term
h
is still
\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)f(x)
except that the weight
\Lambda_{\varepsilon,\delta}
are determined by the locations of the inclusions just described. The boundary data
\widetilde{g}
is an extension of
g\rvert_{\Delta_{1}}
that is compactly supported in
\Delta_{3/2}
and is zero on
\partial\mathbf{D}_{2}\cap\Omega
. We can apply Theorem
4.1
to get

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Delta_{2})}\right).

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u-v)=0
in
\mathbf{D}_{3/2}
and
u-v=0
in
\Delta_{3/2}
, so the general setting is reduced to the special setting of
g=0
(in fact also
f=0
). Thanks to Lemma
5.2
, the special setting is exactly the same as in the interior setting.
∎

5.2.
Caccioppoli inequalities for large
\delta

For relatively hard obstacles, the Caccioppoli inequalities are harder to get. Shen
[
27
]
nevertheless established the interior version with zero source term
f
. We use his method to prove the boundary version and in a general setting.

Theorem 5.5

(Caccioppoli inequality for large
\delta
without source term)
.

Suppose
\delta\in(1,\infty)
. Let
R\geq 16\varepsilon
, if
u\in H^{1}(Q_{2R};\mathbb{R}^{m})
that solves
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=0
in
Q_{2R}
, and for any positive integer
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C_{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{2R}}|u|^{2}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right)^{2\ell}\int_{Q_{2R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

(5.6)

This result is precisely Theorem 3.3 of Shen
[
27
]
after the rescaling
u\to\tilde{u}(\cdot)=u(\varepsilon\cdot)
. Note that the source term is zero in the equation. Using the method in the proof of item (ii) of Theorem
5.4
, we can get the general setting with a nonzero source
f
.

On the other hand, we may repeat the argument of Shen while allowing the presence of the source term. The next three lemmas provide the detailed process.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\delta\in(1,\infty)
and let
\omega_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{\varepsilon}
be a typical obstacle. If
u\in H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{m})
solves
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f
in
\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\delta\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon})}\right\},

for
p=\frac{2d}{d+2}
if
d\geq 3
and
p>1
for
d=2
.

Proof.

The choice of
p
guarantees
\varepsilon^{2-d/p}\leq\varepsilon^{1-d/2}
. By rescaling, we only need to consider the case
\varepsilon=1
and then the result is Lemma 3.1 of Shen
[
27
]
. We refer to the proof there. The key idea is to extend the function
u
from the inside of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
to a function
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{m})
and use it as the test function of the equation.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\delta\in(1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon,h\in(0,1)
,
R\geq 16\varepsilon
and
Q_{R}
be a cube inside
\Omega
. If
u\in H^{1}(Q_{R};\mathbb{R}^{m})
solves
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f
in
Q_{R}
, then for any
r\in[R/2,R-8\varepsilon]
, we have

\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{h(R-r)^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx+\left(h+\frac{C}{\delta}\right)\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+CR^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}.

Proof.

First note that the result only concerns interior of
\Omega
. By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
. The result is then essentially Lemma 3.2 of Shen
[
27
]
except that a nonzero source term
f
is allowed. We repeat the proof for the sake of completeness.

Fix a cut-off function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{0}(Q_{R-2};[0,1])
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
in
Q_{r+3}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
so that
\widetilde{\omega}^{k}=\widetilde{\omega}+k
is still in
Q_{R}
, let
w_{k}
be an extension of
(u\varphi^{2}-c_{k})\rvert_{\omega^{k}}
to
H^{1}_{0}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})
, where
c_{k}
is the mean of
u\varphi^{2}
in
\omega^{k}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|w_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(u\varphi^{2})\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{k})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{k})}+C\|u\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{k})}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\setminus\overline{\omega}^{k})}+C\delta^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})}+C\|u\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\omega^{k})}.

Here the first inequality results from the extension estimate and the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality. The second line is due to Lemma
5.6
(by noting
p<2
for
d\geq 3
and by choosing
p=2
for
d=2
).

Now let
\phi=u\varphi^{2}-\sum_{k}w_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(Q_{R})
, where
k
is summed over the range described above. Let
F
denote the closure of the union of such
\omega^{k}
’s. Then because
\phi=c_{k}
are constants in
\omega^{k}
, we get

\int_{Q_{R}\setminus F}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{Q_{R}}f\cdot\phi.

In view of the definition of
\phi
, we obtain

\left|\int_{Q_{R}\setminus F}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u\varphi^{2})\,dx\right|\leq\int_{Q_{R}}|f||\phi|+C\sum_{k}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\setminus\overline{\omega}^{k})}\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\setminus\overline{\omega}^{k})}.

(5.8)

For the term involving
w_{k}
, we use (
5.7
) and Young’s inequality and bound the sum by

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left([h+C\delta^{-1}]\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\setminus\overline{\omega}^{k})}^{2}+C\delta^{-1}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega_{k})}+Ch^{-1}\int_{\omega^{k}}|u|^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{C}{\delta}+h\right)\int_{Q_{R}\setminus F}|\nabla u|^{2}+CR^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}+\frac{C}{h(R-r)^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}\setminus\overline{Q}_{r}}|u|^{2},

where we use the relation
R^{2}>16^{2}>\delta^{-1}
, and the fact that
|\nabla\varphi|
is supported in
Q_{R}\setminus Q_{r+3}
and bounded by
C|R-r|^{-1}
.

For the integral
\int_{Q_{R}}|f||\phi|
, we have

\int_{Q_{R}}|f||\phi|\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}+\sum_{k}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{k})},

using Poincaré inequality for
w_{k}
and (
5.7
), we conclude that

\int_{Q_{R}}|f||\phi|\leq CR^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}.

By plugging
\nabla(u\varphi^{2})=\varphi^{2}\nabla u+2\varphi u\otimes\nabla\varphi
in the integral on the left of (
5.8
), using the fact
\varphi=1
in
Q_{r+3}
and the estimate

\left|\int_{Q_{R}\setminus F}2\varphi A\nabla u:(u\otimes\nabla\varphi)\right|\leq h\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{C}{h(R-r)^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}\setminus Q_{r}}|u|^{2},

which is easily checked as before, we obtain

\int_{Q_{r+3}\setminus F}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{h(R-r)^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx+\left(h+\frac{C}{\delta}\right)\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+CR^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}.

Finally we conclude the proof by using the estimate

\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq CR^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}+C\int_{Q_{r+3}\setminus F}|\nabla u|^{2},

which is easily implied by Lemma
5.6
.
∎

Theorem 5.8

(Caccioppoli inequality for large
\delta
: interior estimate)
.

Let
\delta\in(1,\infty)
. Let
u\in H^{1}(Q_{R};\mathbb{R}^{m})
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(u)=f
in
Q_{R}
for some
R\geq 32\varepsilon
. Then for any

,

\int_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C_{\ell}\left\{\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right)^{2\ell}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}+R^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}\right\}.

Proof.

We modify the proof of Shen
[
27
, Theorem 3.3]
and add
f
to the right hand side. By rescaling we assume
\varepsilon=1
. Given
R\geq 32
, and an integer
\ell\geq 1
, let
k
be the largest integer so that
R2^{-k-1}\geq 8
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
r_{i}=R(1-2^{-i})
. Then we verify that, for all
1\leq i\leq k

r_{i+1}\leq 16,\quad\tfrac{1}{2}r_{i+1}\leq r_{i}\leq r_{i+1}-8.

So, for any
h\in(0,1)
, we can apply the estimate in the previous Lemma
5.7
and obtain

\int_{Q_{r_{i}}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{h(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{2}}\int_{Q_{r_{i+1}}}|u|^{2}+(h+C\delta^{-1})\int_{Q_{r_{i+1}}}|\nabla u|^{2}+Cr_{i+1}^{2}\int_{Q_{r_{i+1}}}|f|^{2}.

By iteration, we finally get

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(h+C\delta^{-1})^{i-1}}{(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}+C\sum_{i=1}^{k}(h+C\delta^{-1})^{i-1}r_{i+1}^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}

\displaystyle+(h+C\delta^{-1})^{k}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Plug in the value
r_{i+1}-r_{i}=2^{-i-1}R
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4C}{hR^{2}(h+C\delta^{-1})}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(4h+4C\delta^{-1})^{i}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle+CR^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(h+C\delta^{-1})^{i-1}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}+(h+C\delta^{-1})^{k}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

We now fix
\ell\geq 1
, note that if
\delta\in[1,2^{2\ell+2}C)
, the conclusion directly follows from the classical setting. Hereafter we assume
\delta\geq 2^{2\ell+2}C
.
We choose
h=2^{-2\ell-2}
so that
4h+4C\delta^{-1}\leq 2^{-2\ell+1}
. Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(4h+4C\delta^{-1})^{i}\leq 1
, and the estimate above yields

\int_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{R^{2}}\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}+C_{\ell}R^{2}\int_{Q_{R}}|f|^{2}+(2^{-2\ell+1})^{k}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Note that we fixed
R\geq 32
first, and then choose
k
so that
2^{k+4}\leq R<2^{k+5}
, so
(2^{-2\ell+1})^{k}\approx R^{-2\ell}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Uniform Lipschitz regularity

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
and hence establish the uniform (in
\varepsilon

\delta

provided that for small

\varepsilon

The main difference compared with Shen’s work is that, Shen did not use the fact that for each fixed
\delta

\delta
, for
\delta\in(1,\infty)
. For

, although the homogenized problem is no longer a good approximation, a simple additional corrector is sufficient.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Bigg\{\left(\fint_{\mathbf{D}_{t}}|w-E_{t}x-q|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{2}\left(\fint_{\mathbf{D}_{t}}|f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|g-E_{t}x-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{t})}

\displaystyle+t\|\nabla_{\rm tan}(g-E_{t}x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{t})}+t^{1+\rho}|\nabla_{\rm tan}(g-E_{t}x)\rvert_{C^{\rho}(\Delta_{t})}\Bigg\}.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose that

in

(6.12)

Proof.

We have shown in Theorem

has constant elliptic tensor with uniform in

regularity estimate of constant coefficients. We refer to

for the proof with

∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\theta
be determined in Lemma

and
c>0
be a number so that

. Suppose

is a weak solution of

\displaystyle H[u_{\varepsilon,\delta}](\theta r)

\displaystyle+C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)^{1/2}\Big(H[u_{\varepsilon,\delta}](2r)+h[u_{\varepsilon,\delta}](2r)\Big)+C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)^{3/2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}.

Let

(6.14)

\displaystyle H[u_{\varepsilon,\delta}](\theta r)\leq

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

For notational simplicity we will write
u
for
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,

, and
h(r)=h[u](r)
. Let

c\theta\geq 4
. We consider below
r\geq c\varepsilon
. We divide the proof to several steps.

Step 1

\ell=1
in Theorem
5.9
, we have

\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}\leq C\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+\frac{1}{r}\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+r\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+\frac{1}{r}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2r})}+\|\nabla_{\rm tan}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2r})}\right\}.

Because we can insert any constant
q
simultaneously to
u
and
g
above, and note that
\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}\leq\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}
for
p>2
, we actually get

\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{r}\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+C\Phi(2r),

where

\Phi(2r)=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{1}{r}\left\{\|u-q\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+r^{2}\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}+\|g-q\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2r})}+r\|\nabla_{\rm tan}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{2r})}\right\}.

From the definitions of
H
and
h
we verify
\Phi(2r)\leq C(H(2r)+h(2r))
, and hence we conclude that

\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}\leq C\left(H(2r)+h(2r)\right)+\frac{C\varepsilon}{r}\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2r})}.

Iterating the inequality above, for the largest integer
M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
2^{M}r\leq 2
, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{M}\frac{C^{n}\varepsilon^{n-1}}{2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}r^{n-1}}\Big(H(2^{n}r)+h(2^{n}r)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{M}\varepsilon^{M}}{2^{M(M-1)/2}r^{M}}\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2^{M}r})}.

Since for any
\varepsilon>0
and

,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}2^{n}\cdot\frac{C^{n}\varepsilon^{n-1}}{2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}r^{n-1}}+\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{C^{M}\varepsilon^{M}}{2^{M(M-1)/2}r^{M}}<\infty,

we obtain

\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\frac{H(2^{n}r)+h(2^{n}r)}{2^{n}}+C\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}.

(6.15)

Step 2
. As is standard, from the definitions (
6.10
) and (
6.11
) it is not hard to verify that

\displaystyle H(t)\leq CH(2r)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall t\in[r,2r],

(6.16)

\displaystyle|h(t)-h(s)|\leq CH(2r)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall t,s\in[r,2r].

Moreover, those inequalities lead to

h(a)+H(a)\leq C\left\{H(2)+h(2)+\int_{a}^{2}\frac{H(s)}{s}\,ds\right\},\quad\forall a\in[\varepsilon,2].

(6.17)

We refer to
[
25
, page 157]
for the verification of the last estimate. We then get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{M}\frac{H(2^{n}r)+h(2^{n}r)}{2^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left[H(2)+h(2)+\int_{2^{n}r}^{2}\frac{H(t)}{t}\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(H(2)+h(2)+\int_{r}^{2}\frac{H(t)}{t}\,dt\right).

By taking
E=0
and
q=0
in (

H(2)\leq\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\rho}(\Delta_{2})},

(6.18)

and since

, we also have

h(2)\leq C\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|E_{1}x+q\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}\leq C\{H(2)+\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}\}.

We hence get

H(2)+h(2)\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|\nabla_{\rm tan}g\|_{C^{0,\rho}(\Delta_{2})}\right).

In the last line we used the freedom to insert any constant
q
to

and
g

Step 3
: Combine the estimates above. We get, for

,

\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{r})}\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|f\|_{\underline{L}^{p}(\mathbf{D}_{2})}+\|\nabla_{\rm tan}g\|_{C^{0,\rho}(\Delta_{2})}\right)+C\int_{r}^{2}\frac{H(t)}{t}\,dt.

Step 4
. We estimate the integral

H(\theta r)\leq\frac{1}{2}H(r)+C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{1/2}\Phi_{2}+C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)^{1/2}\int_{r}^{2}\frac{H(t)}{t}\,dt,

\displaystyle+C\int_{c^{\prime}\varepsilon}^{1/2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\right)^{1/2}\int_{r}^{2}\frac{H(t)}{t}\,dt\,\frac{dr}{r}.

Appendix A
A weak form of layer potentials

In this appendix, we construct the ‘weak’ form of single layer potential and the NP operator, and explain how does the results in Section
3
are still valid provided the Hölder continuity condition (
1.4
) is removed.

Let
\psi\in H_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial\omega)
, we consider the following variational problem:

\int_{Y}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dy+\langle\psi,v\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}=0,\qquad\mathrm{for\ any}\ v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

(A.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}
denotes
H^{-1/2}-H^{1/2}
pair. By Lax-Milgram theorem and Poincaré inequality, there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that
\int_{Y}u(y)\,dy=0
, we denote such
u
by
\mathcal{S}\psi
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{S}\psi=0
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\partial\omega
and

I=\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|_{+}-\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|_{-}.

(A.2)

We then define the boundary operator
K
by

K:H\rightarrow H,\quad\psi\mapsto\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}\psi}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\psi,

(A.3)

where
H
is the Hilbert space
H_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial\omega)
with a new inner product: for any
\phi,\psi\in H_{0}^{-1/2}(\partial\omega)
,

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{H}:=\int_{Y}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}\psi\,dy.

(A.4)

By definition of
K
and (
A.2
), we have

\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}\psi}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|_{+}=\left(\frac{1}{2}I+K\right)\psi,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}\psi}{\partial\nu_{A}}\right|_{-}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}I+K\right)\psi.

(A.5)

Proposition A.1
.

K
is self-adjoint, and the spectrum of
K
is contained in
(-1/2,1/2)
.

Proof.

Step 1
. We compute that

\langle K\phi,\psi\rangle_{H}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\omega}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}\psi\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{Y\setminus\omega}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}\psi\,dy,

this shows that
K
is self-adjoint, in particular, the spectrum is real and for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle(\lambda I-K)\phi,\phi\rangle_{H}=\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\,dy+\left(\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{Y\setminus\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\,dy.

(A.6)

If
\lambda\notin(-1/2,1/2)
, then

\langle(\lambda I-K)\phi,\phi\rangle_{H}\geq\min\left\{\int_{\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\,dy,\ \int_{Y\setminus\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\,dy\right\}.

(A.7)

Step 2
. Let
Q:H^{1}(\omega)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be the usual bounded extension operator, we define
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

v=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{S}\phi-\int_{\omega}\mathcal{S}\phi&&\mathrm{in}\ \omega,\\
&Q(v|_{\omega})&&\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
u=\mathcal{S}\phi
, and test
v
and
\mathcal{S}\phi
in (
A.1
), respectively, note that
\langle\phi,v\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}=\langle\phi,\mathcal{S}\phi\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}
, we get

\int_{Y}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\nabla v=\int_{Y}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2},

from the definition of
v
, we have

\int_{Y\setminus\omega}|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\leq\int_{Y\setminus\omega}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\nabla v,

thus
\|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(Y\setminus\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Y\setminus\omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}
. Therefore,

\int_{\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\geq C\int_{Y}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}=C\|\phi\|_{H}^{2}.

(A.8)

Step 3
. Let
P:H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\omega)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be the usual bounded extension operator, we define
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

w=\left\{\begin{aligned} &P(w|_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\omega})&&\mathrm{in}\ \omega,\\
&\mathcal{S}\phi-\int_{Y\setminus\omega}\mathcal{S}\phi&&\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\setminus\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
u=\mathcal{S}\phi
, and test
w
and
\mathcal{S}\phi
in (
A.1
), respectively, note that
\langle\phi,w\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}=\langle\phi,\mathcal{S}\phi\rangle_{-1/2,1/2}
, we get

\int_{Y}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\nabla w=\int_{Y}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2},

from the definition of
w
, we have

\int_{\omega}|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\leq\int_{\omega}A\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\nabla w,

thus
\|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{1}(Y\setminus\omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi\|_{L^{2}(Y\setminus\omega)}
. Therefore,

\int_{Y\setminus\omega}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}\geq C\int_{Y}A|\nabla\mathcal{S}\phi|^{2}=C\|\phi\|_{H}^{2}.

(A.9)

Step 4
. Combine
(
A.7
), (
A.8
), (
A.9
), we obtain the coercive inequality: for
\lambda\notin(-1/2,1/2)
,
\langle(\lambda I-K)\phi,\phi\rangle_{H}\geq C\|\phi\|_{H}^{2}
, this implies that
\lambda I-K
is bijective, and hence the spectrum of
K
is in
(-1/2,1/2)
.
∎

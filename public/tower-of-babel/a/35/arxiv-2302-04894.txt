Two-loop hard thermal loops for any model
Andreas Ekstedt , a, b c

Abstract
Hard thermal loops describe how soft gauge fields are screened and damped in hot plasmas. As such they are used to calculate transport coefficients, Sphaleron rates, equations of state, and particle production. However, most calculations are done using one-loop self-energies. And two-loop contributions can be large. To that end this paper provides vector two-loop self-energies for generic models: Any scalar, fermion, or vector representation; and all possible renormalizable terms. Several examples are given to showcase the results. Two-loop results for higher-point functions are also given.

[

can be treated as quasiparticles. For example, we can describe electrons with the Vlasov equation:

\displaystyle\dot{N}_{F}^{\pm}+\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\nabla}N_{F}^{\pm}\pm e\left(\vec{E}+\vec{v}\times\vec{B}\right)\cdot\vec{\nabla}^{p}N_{F}^{\pm}=0.

(2.6)

If we now assume that the electrons are driven slightly away from equilibrium by the electric field, we can expand the electron distribution as

\displaystyle N_{F}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}-n_{F}-\delta n_{F}^{\pm},\quad v\cdot\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt\delta n_{F}^{\pm}(\vec{p},x)=\mp e\vec{v}\cdot\vec{E}\frac{d}{dp}n_{F}(p),

(2.7)

\displaystyle v^{\mu}=(1,\vec{v}),\quad\vec{v}\equiv\frac{\vec{p}}{p^{0}},\quad n_{F}(p)=\left(e^{p/T}+1\right)^{-1}.

(2.8)

The photon self-energy then follows from the electron current

]

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.9}{$\partial$}\kern-0.75pt_{\mu}F^{\nu\mu}=e\left\langle\bar{\Psi}{}^{\nu}\Psi\right\rangle\sim e\int_{p}v^{\mu}(N^{+}_{F}-N^{-}_{F}),

(2.9)

where

denotes the average over hard modes with characteristic momenta

That is

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.9}{$\partial$}\kern-0.75pt_{\mu}F^{\nu\mu}=-e\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\text{Tr}\left[\not{p}{}^{\nu}\right]\Delta_{F}(p)=2e\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}v^{\nu}\left[N^{+}(p,x)-N^{-}(p,x)\right]

\displaystyle=4e^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{v^{\nu}\vec{v}\cdot\vec{E}(x)}{v\cdot K}n^{\prime}_{F}(p)=-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{3}\int\frac{d{}_{v}}{4\pi}\frac{v^{\nu}\vec{v}\cdot\vec{E}(x)}{v\cdot K}\equiv\Pi^{\nu\mu}A_{\mu},

(2.11)

where
\vec{E}=-\vec{\nabla}A^{0}-\dot{\vec{A}}

2.2 Using kinetic theory beyond leading order

There are two ways that we can go about applying the kinetic approach at two loops. First, we can include resummed self-energies directly in the transport equations and use this to derive effective particle distributions
[
37
]
. While possible, this approach involves evaluating self-energies at finite external momentum. Instead we elect to only use leading-order transport equations—two-loop results are then obtained by calculating corrections to the fermion current
\left\langle\bar{\Psi}{}^{\nu}\Psi\right\rangle
. At first glance it seems like we are back to brute-force evaluating diagrams. Be that as it may, working with currents is considerably easier than calculating self energies. And as we shall see, the results for different kinds of particles involve the same compact expressions.

2.3 Two-loop hard thermal loops

Let us demonstrate our approach for quantum electrodynamics. The two-loop contribution to the electron current is shown in figure
1(a)
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{\Psi}{}^{\mu}\Psi\right\rangle_{\text{2-loop}}=e^{2}\int_{PQ}F^{\mu}

\displaystyle\left\{\Delta_{F}(P)\Delta_{V}(Q)\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{R}(P+Q)+\Delta^{A}(P)\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\Delta_{F}(P)\Delta_{F}(P+Q)\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{A}(Q)+\Delta^{A}(P)\Delta^{R}(Q)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\Delta_{F}(P+Q)\Delta_{V}(Q)\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{A}(P)\right]\right\},

(2.12)

where

To evaluate the integrals we have to define

\displaystyle\delta(p^{2})\Delta^{R/A}(P).

(2.13)

This expression contains terms with two delta functions—these must be regulated. To do so we use the original approach

\displaystyle\pi\delta(P^{2})\Delta^{R/A}(P)=\frac{-i}{P^{2}\mp i\eta p^{0}}\frac{\eta}{P^{4}+\eta^{2}}=\pm p^{0}\left[\frac{\eta}{P^{4}+\eta^{2}}\right]^{2}-i\frac{P^{2}\eta}{(P^{4}+\eta^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\pm p^{0}\left[\pi\delta(P^{2})\right]^{2}-\frac{i}{2}\frac{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75ptp_{0}^{2}}\left[\pi\delta(P^{2})\right].

As an example, consider

\displaystyle\int_{PQ}F^{\mu}\Delta_{F}(P)\Delta_{V}(Q)\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{R}(P+Q)+\Delta^{A}(P)\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right].

(2.16)

After using equation (

\displaystyle\pi\Delta_{F}(P^{2})\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{R}(P+Q)+\Delta^{A}(P)\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right]

\displaystyle=p^{0}\left[\pi\delta(P^{2})\right]^{2}\left[\Delta^{R}(P+Q)-\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right]-\frac{i}{2}\frac{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75ptp_{0}^{2}}\left[\pi\delta(P^{2})\right]\left[\Delta^{R}(P+Q)+\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right].

2.4 Procedure for general diagrams

Irrespective of the diagram or particle, the only terms that contribute are of the form

\displaystyle\int_{PQ}(ap^{\mu}+bq^{\mu})(P+Q)^{2}\Delta_{X}(P)\Delta_{Y}(Q)\left[\Delta^{R}(P)\Delta^{R}(P+Q)+\Delta^{A}(P)\Delta^{A}(P+Q)\right].

(2.22)

The piece going with
p^{\mu}
give terms proportional to

\displaystyle\int_{PQ}\frac{N_{Y}(q)}{qp}\left\{\left[\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt^{p}_{0}N_{X}(p)-\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt^{p}_{0}\overline{N}_{X}(p)\right]v_{p}^{\mu}-\frac{v_{p}^{\mu}-n^{\mu}}{p}(N_{X}(p)-\overline{N}_{X}(p))\right\},

(2.23)

and the term multiplying
q^{\mu}
give terms of the form

\displaystyle\int_{PQ}v^{\mu}_{q}\left(N_{Y}(q)-\overline{N}_{Y}(q)\right)\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left\{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\partial$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial$}}\kern-0.75pt^{p}_{0}\left[N_{X}(p)+\overline{N}_{X}(p)\right]-\frac{1}{p}\left(N_{X}(p)+\overline{N}_{X}(p)\right)\right\}.

(2.24)

\displaystyle v^{\mu}_{p}=\frac{p^{\mu}}{p^{0}}\rightarrow v_{p}^{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}}{2p^{2}}\left[v_{p}^{\mu}-n^{\mu}\right]+\ldots,

(2.25)

where

represents hard charges obtaining a thermal mass.

The second structure, on the other hand, represents a renormalization of the hard distributions themselves. Connected with this the momentum integral in equation (
2.24

3.3 Two-loop hard thermal loops

At two loops various diagrams introduce factors of

We separate the result as

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{NLO}}=-\left[\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{V}}+\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{SV}}+\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{FV}}+\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{SF}}\right],

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{\text{V}}=11T^{2}\frac{\log(\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T})-\frac{1}{22}}{36\pi^{2}}g_{V}^{adc}g_{V}^{cef}g_{V}^{dfn}g_{V}^{enb}\Pi_{1}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{T^{2}}{12\pi^{2}}g_{V}^{adc}g_{V}^{cef}g_{V}^{dfn}g_{V}^{enb}\Pi_{2}^{\mu\nu}.

\displaystyle-T^{2}\frac{\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}}{24\pi^{2}}g_{V}^{aec}g_{V}^{bdc}\text{Tr}\left[g_{S}^{d}g_{S}^{e}\right]\Pi_{1}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{T^{2}}{48\pi^{2}}g_{V}^{aec}g_{V}^{bdc}\text{Tr}\left[g_{S}^{d}g_{S}^{e}\right]\Pi_{2}^{\mu\nu}

So far we have focused on the self-energy, but higher-point correlators can be extracted from the results in section

\displaystyle\left[v\cdot D,\delta N^{\pm}_{X}\right]^{a}=\mp\vec{v}\cdot\vec{E}^{a}n^{\prime}_{X}(p),

5  Conclusions

In this paper we have provided hard thermal loops, for vector boson self-energies, at two-loops for any renormalizable model. This was made possible by using transport equations to simplify the calculations—thus extending known one-loop methods
[
12
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
. In particular, this approach provides compact expression for each particle type; the result is independent of the matching scale; and known results for Debye masses are reproduced in the appropriate limit. We also demonstrated how higher-point functions can be extracted from the results.

The results of this paper can be used to study particle production in the early universe; transport coefficients; and wall speeds in first-order phase transitions
[
51
]
. The effect of including two-loop contributions is likely significant for the strong interaction, since the coupling constant is rather large
N\alpha_{S}\sim 0.3
when
T\sim 100\penalty\ \text{GeV}
.

The next step is to provide two-loop hard thermal loops for fermion propagators. Performing these calculations for quarks, by using Feynman diagrams, is likely arduous beyond leading order. However, we expect that similar methods as used in this paper will prove useful in this endeavour.

E.4 Power corrections before field redefinitions

The scalar loop gives

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{S}(K)=\text{Tr}\left[g_{S}^{a}g_{S}^{b}\right]\frac{K^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}+k^{0}L(K)\right\},

(E.13)

\displaystyle\Pi^{00,ab}_{S}(K)=-\text{Tr}\left[g_{S}^{a}g_{S}^{b}\right]\frac{1}{3}\frac{k^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}+1+\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{k^{2}}(k^{0}L(K)-1)\right\}

(E.14)

The fermion loop gives

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{F}(K)=\text{Tr}\left[g_{F}^{a}g_{F}^{b}\right]\frac{K^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{2}{\epsilon}+4\left(\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}+\log 4\right)-2+4k^{0}L(K)\right\},

(E.15)

\displaystyle\Pi^{00,ab}_{F}(K)=-\text{Tr}\left[g_{F}^{a}g_{F}^{b}\right]\frac{1}{3}\frac{k^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{2}{\epsilon}+4\left(\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}+\log 4\right)-2+2k^{0}\left(3-\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)L(K)+2\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{k^{2}}\right\}.

For non-abelian diagrams we group ghosts and vectors together, the result is

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}_{V}(K)=\text{Tr}\left[g_{V}^{a}g_{V}^{b}\right]\frac{K^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{5}{\epsilon}+10\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}-3+10k^{0}L(K)\right\},

(E.16)

\displaystyle\Pi^{00,ab}_{V}(K)=-\text{Tr}\left[g_{V}^{a}g_{V}^{b}\right]\frac{1}{3}\frac{k^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{5}{\epsilon}+10\log\frac{\mu e}{4\pi T}-1+2k^{0}\left(6-\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)L(K)+2\frac{(k^{0})^{2}}{k^{2}}\right\}.

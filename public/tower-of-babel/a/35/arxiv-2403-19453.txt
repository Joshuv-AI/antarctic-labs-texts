The submodularity of the covolume function in global function fields
Gukyeong Bang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the submodularity of the covolume function in global function fields. The submodular property is often needed in the study of homogeneous dynamics, especially to define a Margulis function. We prove that the covolume function is submodular when the class group of the global function field is trivial.

†

Keywords:
function fields, lattices, submodularity.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be a collection of subspaces of a vector space
V
. A function
\phi:\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called
submodular
if

{\phi(W_{1})+\phi(W_{2})\geq\phi(W_{1}+W_{2})+\phi(W_{1}\cap W_{2})}

for any
W_{1},W_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(V)
.
The submodular property of the covolume function often appears in the study of homogeneous dynamics.
For example, the submodularity is necessary to construct a
Margulis function
, which guarantees that most of the points in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
tend not to be close to the cusp on average (see
[
EMM
]
).
Another example is the
Harder-Narasimhan filtration
, which is a critical tool in the study of parametric geometry (see
[
HN
]
and
[
Sol
]
).
Hence, proving the submodularity in global function fields will be the first step for several problems of homogeneous dynamics, Teichmüller dynamics, and Diophantine approximations.

Let us first introduce the function field setting we will consider.
Let
K
be a global function field over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements for a prime power
q
, that is, the function field of a geometrically connected smooth projective curve
\mathbf{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We fix a discrete valuation
\nu
on
K
and denote by
K_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
the completion of
K
with respect to
\nu
and its valuation ring, respectively.
Fix an element
\pi_{\nu}\in K
such that
\nu(\pi_{\nu})=1
(called a
uniformizer
) and denote the residual field by
k_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}_{\nu}/\pi_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
and
q_{\nu}=\Card{(k_{\nu})}
.
The absolute value
|\cdot|
associated with
\nu
is defined by
|x|=q_{\nu}^{-\nu(x)}
for any
x\in K_{\nu}
.
Note that
K_{\nu}
is isomorphic to
k_{\nu}((\pi_{\nu}))
, the formal Laurent series with the variable
\pi_{\nu}
, and the absolute value of
\sum_{i=-\ell}^{\infty}a_{i}\pi_{\nu}^{i}
is
q_{\nu}^{\ell}
(see
[
BPP
]
for instance).

Let
R_{\nu}
be the affine algebra of affine curve
\mathbf{C}-\{\nu\}
. Note that
R_{\nu}
is discrete in
K_{\nu}
and plays a similar role to the set of integers
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
We remark that
R_{\nu}
is a Dedekind domain in general, but
we assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain
, that is, the ideal class group of
R_{\nu}
is trivial (see
[
Ser
, II.2]
and
[
Nar
, Chapter 1]
). The standard example is
\mathbf{C}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the standard point at infinity
\nu=[1:0]
. In this case, we have
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
,
K_{\nu}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((T^{-1}))
,
R_{\nu}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, and
k_{\nu}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
with the uniformizer
T^{-1}
.

We define the max norm on the exterior algebra of
K_{\nu}^{n}
. Let
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n}
be the standard basis of
K_{\nu}^{n}
.
Denote
\llbracket d_{1},d_{2}\rrbracket=\{d_{1},d_{1}+1,d_{1}+2,\dots,d_{2}\}
for each
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
d_{1}\leq d_{2}
.
Given
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, we define

{\wp^{n}_{i}=\{I\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:\Card{(I)}=i\}.}

Given
I=\{\iota_{1},\dots,\iota_{i}\}\in\wp^{n}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{i}}\in K_{\nu}^{n}
, denote
\mathbf{v}_{I}=\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{i}}
.
As
\{\mathbf{e}_{I}\}_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}
is the standard basis of
\bigwedge^{i}K_{\nu}^{n}
, define the norm on
\bigwedge^{i}K_{\nu}^{n}
by

{\|\mathbf{v}\|=\max_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}|c_{I}|}

given
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I}\in\bigwedge^{i}K_{\nu}^{n}
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
V
be a subspace of
K_{\nu}^{n}
and
\Delta
be a discrete
R_{\nu}
-module contained in
V
.
We say that
\beta
is a
R_{\nu}
-basis
of
\Delta
if
\beta
is a basis of
\Delta
over
R_{\nu}
and is a linearly independent set over
K_{\nu}
.
The
rank
rk(
\Delta
) is defined by the dimension of
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}(\Delta)
as a vector space over
K_{\nu}
.

\Delta
is called a
lattice
in
V
if
\Delta
has a
R_{\nu}
-basis and
\textrm{rk}(\Delta)=\dim_{K_{\nu}}(V)
. We will skip writing the containing vector space if it is not essential.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V
be a subspace of
K_{\nu}^{n}
and
\Delta
be a lattice in
V
.
A subspace
W
of
V
is called
\Delta
-
rational
if
W\cap\Delta
is a lattice in
W
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
V
be a subspace of
K_{\nu}^{n}
and
\Delta
be a lattice in
V
.
Given a
\Delta
-rational subspace
W
of
V
, define

{d_{\Delta}(W)=\|\mathbf{w}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{w}_{i}\|,}

where
\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{i}\}
is any
R_{\nu}
-basis of
W\cap\Delta
and
d_{\Delta}(\{0\}):=1
.

Denote by
M_{m,n}(D)
the set of
m\times n
matrices over a ring
D
.

Remark 1.3
.

d_{\Delta}
is well-defined: Let
\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{i}\}
and
\{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{i}\}
be
R_{\nu}
-bases of
W\cap\Delta
.
Denote by
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
matrices with columns
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{i}
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{i}
, respectively.
Then there exist
A,B\in M_{i,i}(R_{\nu})
such that
M_{1}=M_{2}A
and
M_{2}=M_{1}B
, which implies
AB=BA=1
. Thus, we have
\det{A}\in R_{\nu}^{\times}
, which implies
|\det{A}|=1
(
[
BPP
, Section 14]
).
Hence,
\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{w}_{j}\|=|\det{A}|\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{j}\|=\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{j}\|
.

In the real case,
d_{\Delta}(W)
is the covolume of
W\cap\Delta
. However, there is a normalization issue in the case of function fields. If we define the covolume of a lattice
\Lambda
in
K_{\nu}^{n}
by
\mathrm{Covol}_{n}\,(\Lambda)=\mathrm{Vol}_{n}\,(K_{\nu}^{n}/\Lambda)
, where
\mathrm{Vol}_{n}
is the Haar measure on
K_{\nu}^{n}
, then it is known that

{\mathrm{Covol}_{n}\,(R_{\nu}^{n})=q^{(g-1)n},}

where
g
is the genus of curve
\mathbf{C}
(see
[
BPP
, Lemma 14.4]
). Thus, it can be easily deduced that for any lattice
\Delta
in
K_{\nu}^{n}
, we have

{\mathrm{Covol}_{n}\,(\Delta)=\mathrm{Covol}_{n}\,(R_{\nu}^{n})d_{\Delta}(K_{\nu}^{n}).}

From the above equality, it is natural to define the
covolume
of a lattice
\Lambda
in
V(\subseteq K_{\nu}^{n})
by

{\mathrm{Covol}\,(\Lambda)=\mathrm{Covol}_{\dim(V)}\,(R_{\nu}^{\dim(V)})d_{\Lambda}(V)=q^{(g-1)\dim(V)}d_{\Lambda}(V).}

The main theorem is the submodularity of
\log{\mathrm{Covol}}
, which is an analogue of
[
EMM
, Lemma 5.6]
for global function fields.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain. Let
\Delta
be a lattice in
K_{\nu}^{n}
. Then for any
\Delta
-rational subspaces
L
and
M
of
K_{\nu}^{n}
,

{d_{\Delta}(L)d_{\Delta}(M)\geq d_{\Delta}(L\cap M)d_{\Delta}(L+M),}

or equivalently,

{\quad\mathrm{Covol}\,(L\cap\Delta)\mathrm{Covol}\,(M\cap\Delta)\geq\mathrm{Covol}\,(L\cap M\cap\Delta)\mathrm{Covol}\,((L+M)\cap\Delta).}

We remark that the above inequalities are equivalent since

{\begin{split}\mathrm{Covol}\,(R_{\nu}^{\dim(L)})\mathrm{Covol}\,(R_{\nu}^{\dim(M)})&=q^{(g-1)(\dim(L)+\dim(M))}\\
&=\mathrm{Covol}\,(R_{\nu}^{\dim(L+M)})\mathrm{Covol}\,(R_{\nu}^{\dim(L\cap M)}).\end{split}}

The submodular property is necessary for several theorems.
For instance, it is used for the key inequality to define a Margulis function by Kadyrov, Kleinbock, Lindenstrauss and Margulis in the real case:
given
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})
, denote

{g_{t}=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{\frac{t}{m}}I_{m}&\\
&e^{\frac{-t}{n}}I_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad u_{s}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{m}&s\\
&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).}

Then there exists
c_{0}>0
such that given any
t\geq 1
, one can choose
\omega>0
such that
for any lattice
\Delta\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
i\in\llbracket 1,m+n-1\rrbracket
,

{\begin{split}\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})}\alpha_{i}(g_{t}u_{s}\Delta)d\rho(s)&\leq c_{0}te^{-mnt}\alpha_{i}(\Delta)^{\beta_{i}}\\
&+\omega^{2\beta_{i}}\max_{0<j\leq\min\{m+n-i,i\}}(\sqrt{\alpha_{i+j}(\Delta)\alpha_{i-j}(\Delta)})^{\beta_{i}},\end{split}}

where
\alpha_{i}(\Delta)=\max\{d_{\Delta}(H)^{-1}:\dim(H)=i\}
,
\beta_{i}=\frac{m}{i}
for
i\leq m
and
\beta_{i}=\frac{n}{m+n-i}
otherwise, and
\rho
is the Gaussian distribution.
This inequality explains that when we have an
i
-dimensional subspace
H
with small
d_{\Delta}(H)
,
then the
u_{s}
-average of
\alpha_{i}(g_{t}u_{s}\Delta)
is bounded by the exponential speed.
There may be no subspace for dimension
i
,
but we can still bound it by the second term obtained by the submodularity.
It is known that the above inequality implies that there exists a height function
\tilde{\alpha}=\sum_{i=0}^{m+n}\omega_{i}\alpha_{i}
such that

{\int_{M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R})}\tilde{\alpha}(g_{t}u_{s}\Delta)d\rho(s)\leq 3c_{0}te^{-mnt}\tilde{\alpha}(\Delta)}

for any
\Delta
with sufficiently large
\tilde{\alpha}(\Delta)
depending on
t
. As the height function measures how far a lattice is from the cusp,
we can bound the trajectory of
g_{t}u_{s}\Delta
using the exponential term (see
[
KKLM
]
). In a subsequent paper
[
BKL
]
, we obtain an analogous inequality for global function fields and calculate an upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of singular matrices in function fields.

The submodularity is also used to define the Harder-Narasimhan filtration.
Given a lattice
\Delta\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, let

{S_{\Delta}=\{p_{\Gamma}:=(\text{rk}(\Gamma),\log{\text{Covol}\ (\Gamma)})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\Gamma\text{ is a sublattice of }\Delta\}.}

If we denote the extreme points of
the convex hull of
S_{\Delta}
by
p_{0},\dots,p_{k}
ordering by the first coordinate,
the submodularity implies that for each
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
,
there exists unique
\Delta
-subspace
H_{i}
such that
p_{i}=p_{H_{i}\cap\Delta}
.
The filtration
\{0\}=H_{0}\cap\Delta<\dots<H_{k}\cap\Delta=\Delta
is called the Harder-Narasimhan filtration.
As we have
\alpha_{\dim(H_{i})}(\Delta)=d_{\Delta}(H_{i})^{-1}
by the extremity, this filtration gives the information of how far
\Delta
is from the cusp.
Hence, by observing the filtration of
g_{t}\Delta
along time
t
, we can track how often
g_{t}\Delta
is close to the cusp in
t\in[0,\infty)
.
It is proven that there always exists a piecewise linear map approximating the filtration, called
template (or system)
,
and thus, we can study Diophantine approximations by drawing suitable piecewise linear maps. This approximation theory is called the parametric geometry of numbers (see
[
Sol
]
,
[
DFSU
]
or
[
Sax
]
).

We expect that by Theorem
1.4
, we can prove the analogous theorems above in global function fields.

2.
The Orthogonality in global function fields

We use the orthogonality for global function fields to prove Theorem
1.4
.
The orthogonality and corresponding properties in ultrametric space can be found in several references (see
[
Wei
, Chapter II]
,
[
KlST
]
, or
[
PR
]
for example).
We organized the properties necessary for our setting with original proofs.
We start by defining the orthogonality in non-archimedean vector spaces using Parseval’s identity.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space over
K_{\nu}
. A subset
S=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
of
V
is called
orthogonal
if we have

{\|{a_{1}}{\mathbf{v}_{1}}+\dots+{a_{i}}{\mathbf{v}_{i}}\|=\text{max}(|a_{1}|{\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\|},\dots,|a_{i}|{\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|})}

for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{i}\in K_{\nu}
. An orthogonal set
S
is called
orthonormal
if
\|\mathbf{v}\|=1
for any
\mathbf{v}\in S
.

Note that for each element in the orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, its column vectors form an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A similar group exists in non-archimedean vector spaces.
Recall that
M_{m,n}(D)
the set of
m\times n
matrices whose entries are in a ring
D
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
(non-archimedean) orthogonal group
is defined by

{\ \mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})=\{A\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}):|\det{A}|=1\},}

and the
special orthogonal group
is defined by

{\ \operatorname{SO}_{n}(K_{\nu})=\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})\cap\operatorname{SL}_{n}(K_{\nu})=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}).}

Remark 2.3
.

\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
is a group. Given
A\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
, we have
A^{-1}=(\det{A})^{-1}\mathrm{adj}\,(A)
, where
\mathrm{adj}\,(A)
is the adjugate matrix of
A
.
Since
A\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
, we have
\mathrm{adj}\,(A)\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
by the ultrametric property.
Since
|\det{A}|=1
, we have
A^{-1}\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
g\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
,
\{g\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,g\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
K_{\nu}^{n}
.
Conversely, given an orthonormal basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}
of
K_{\nu}^{n}
, the matrix
V
with columns
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
is in
\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in K_{\nu}^{n}
. Then we have

(2.1)

{\|g\mathbf{x}\|=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}g\mathbf{e}_{i}\|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(\|x_{i}g\mathbf{e}_{i}\|)\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(|x_{i}|)=\|\mathbf{x}\|,}

where the last inequality holds since
g\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
.
Since
\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
is a group, by applying
g^{-1}\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
and
g\mathbf{x}\in K_{\nu}^{n}
to (
2.1
) again, we have
\|\mathbf{x}\|=\|g^{-1}(g\mathbf{x})\|\leq\|g\mathbf{x}\|
, which implies

(2.2)

{\|g\mathbf{x}\|=\|\mathbf{x}\|}

for any
\mathbf{x}\in K_{\nu}^{n}
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in K_{\nu}^{n}
. Then

{\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}g\mathbf{e}_{i}\|=\|g\mathbf{a}\|=\|\mathbf{a}\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(\|a_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}\|)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}(\|a_{i}g\mathbf{e}_{i}\|)}

where the second and fourth equalities hold by (
2.2
). Hence,
\{g\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,g\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
K_{\nu}^{n}
.

Conversely, let
\beta=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
K_{\nu}^{n}
and let
V=(v_{ij})
be the matrix with columns
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
Then
V\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
since
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=1,

\forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
.
Observe that for any
M=(m_{ij})\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
, we have from the Leibniz formula for the determinant that

(2.3)

{|\det{M}|=|\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i\sigma(i)}|\leq\max_{\sigma\in S_{n}}|\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i\sigma(i)}|\leq 1.}

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in K_{\nu}^{n}
. By the orthogonality of
\beta
, we have

{\|V\mathbf{x}\|=\|x_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|=\max(\|x_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}\|,\dots,\|x_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}\|).}

Since
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=1,\forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, we have

(2.4)

{\|V\mathbf{x}\|=\max(|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|)=\|\mathbf{x}\|.}

Applying
\mathbf{x}=V^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{i}
to (
2.4
), we obtain
\|V^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{i}\|=1,\forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, which implies
V^{-1}\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
. Applying
V,V^{-1}\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
to (
2.3
), we have
|\det{V}|=1
. Therefore, we have
V\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
.
∎

The following lemma says that the non-archimedean norm is invariant under
\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
g\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
and
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge K_{\nu}^{n}
, we have

\|g.\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|
.

Proof.

Let
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
.
For any pair
I,J\in\wp^{n}_{i}
, we denote by
\det_{I,J}g
the (
I,J
)-minor of
g
, that is, the determinant of the
(i,i)
-submatrix of
g
containing those rows and columns whose indices are in
I,J
, respectively.

Let
\mathbf{v}=\sum\limits_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I}\in\bigwedge^{i}K_{\nu}^{n}
and
g\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
. By the ultrametric property,

{\|g.\mathbf{v}\|=\|\sum_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}(g.\mathbf{e}_{I})\|=\|\sum_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}(\sum_{J\in\wp^{n}_{i}}\mathrm{det}_{I,J}\,g\ \mathbf{e}_{J})\|\leq\max_{I,J\in\wp^{n}_{i}}|c_{I}\mathrm{det}_{I,J}\,g|.}

Since
|\mathrm{det}_{I,J}\,g|\leq 1
by
g\in M_{n,n}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
and the ultrametric property, we have

(2.5)

{\|g.\mathbf{v}\|\leq\max_{I,J\in\wp^{n}_{i}}|c_{I}\mathrm{det}_{I,J}\,g|\leq\max_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}|c_{I}|=\|\mathbf{v}\|.}

By applying (
2.5
) to
g^{-1}\in\mathrm{O}_{n}(K_{\nu})
and
g.\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{i}K_{\nu}^{n}
again,
we obtain
\|\mathbf{v}\|=\|g^{-1}.(g.\mathbf{v})\|\leq\|g.\mathbf{v}\|
as well.
∎

By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\beta=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis on
K_{\nu}^{n}
. Let
g
be the matrix with columns
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
. Then
\|g.\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge K_{\nu}^{n}
.

There exists an alternative definition of the orthonormal basis, which can be defined for any subspaces of
K_{\nu}^{n}
. We prepare two lemmas to define it.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
i\in\llbracket 1,n-1\rrbracket
. Given unit vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\in K_{\nu}^{n}
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=1
,
we can find
\mathbf{v}_{i+1},\mathbf{v}_{i+2},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\in K_{\nu}^{n}
such that the matrix with columns
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
is in
\operatorname{SO}_{n}(K_{\nu})
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I}
.
Since
\max_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}{|c_{I}|}=\|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{v}_{j}\|=1
, there exists
I_{0}\in\wp^{n}_{i}
satisfying
|c_{I_{0}}|=1
.
Let
I_{0}^{c}=\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket-I_{0}=\{\iota_{1},\dots,\iota_{n-i}\}
.
Define
\mathbf{v}_{i+\alpha}=\mathbf{e}_{\iota_{\alpha}}
for
1\leq\alpha<n-i
and
\mathbf{v}_{n}=\sigma_{I_{0}}c_{I_{0}}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{\iota_{n-i}}
, where
\sigma_{I_{0}}\in\{1,-1\}
is the constant satisfying
\mathbf{e}_{I_{0}}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{I_{0}^{c}}=\sigma_{I_{0}}\mathbf{e}_{\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}
. Then

(2.6)

{\begin{split}\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{j}&=(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{v}_{j})\wedge(\bigwedge_{j=i+1}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{j})=(\sum_{I\in\wp^{n}_{i}}c_{I}\mathbf{e}_{I})\wedge(\sigma_{I_{0}}c_{I_{0}}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{I_{0}^{c}})\\

Let

∎

Claim 2.

{\|\alpha_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\alpha_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{k-1}\mathbf{v}_{k-1}+\mathbf{v}_{k}\|=q_{\nu}^{-l}.}

For each

\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{i}\}

.

Let

and

be the subspaces of
K_{\nu}^{n}
generated by
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}
, and by
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{i}

and

3.
The submodularity of covolumes in global function fields

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

. Given a lattice
\Delta
in
K_{\nu}^{n}
,
the sublattices induced by
\Delta
-rational subspaces have the following property.

Definition 3.1
.

be a lattice in a subspace

A

-submodule
\Lambda
of

in

of

in

Note that given a lattice

To calculate covolumes in Theorem

\Delta
-rational subspaces.

is a sublattice of

-basis of the primitive lattice can be extended to a

-basis of larger lattice when

is a principal ideal domain.

Let us begin with the equivalent conditions for the discreteness analogous to the real vector spaces.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a non-trivial

-submodule of

. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Lambda
is discrete.

(2)

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda):=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda-\{0\}}\|\mathbf{x}\|>0
.

(3)

\Card{(\Lambda\cap S)}

<\infty
for any bounded set

.

(4)

\Lambda
contains a shortest non-zero vector.

Proof.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
).
For each

, denote by

the open ball of radius
q_{\nu}^{-l}
centered at 0 in
K_{\nu}^{n}
.
Since
\Lambda
is discrete, there exists

such that

. Hence, we have
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda-\{0\}}\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq q_{\nu}^{-l_{0}}>0
.

(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
).
Since the image of
\|\cdot\|
is
\{q_{\nu}^{-l}:l\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{0\}
, there exists

such that
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)=q_{\nu}^{-r}
. To prove (

), it suffices to check the case

, where

is an integer less than
r
.
Suppose
\Lambda\cap S
is an infinite set by contradiction.
Then, there exists a sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
of distinct elements in
\Lambda\cap S
.
Since
S=B(q_{\nu}^{-l})=(\pi_{\nu}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{\nu})^{n}
is compact, there exists a converging subsequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
.
Since
\{\mathbf{x}_{n_{k}}\}
is a Cauchy sequence of distinct elements in
\Lambda\cap S
, for any small
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\mathbf{x}_{n_{s}},\mathbf{x}_{n_{t}}\in\Lambda\cap S
such that
0<\|\mathbf{x}_{n_{s}}-\mathbf{x}_{n_{t}}\|<\epsilon
.
Since
\Lambda
is an additive group, we have
\mathbf{x}_{n_{s}}-\mathbf{x}_{n_{t}}\in\Lambda-\{0\}
. Hence, we obtain

{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda-\{0\}}\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{n_{s}}-\mathbf{x}_{n_{t}}\|<\epsilon,}

which implies
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)=0
contradicting (
2
).

(
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
4
).
There exists a non-zero element
\mathbf{y}\in\Lambda-\{0\}
since
\Lambda
is non-trivial.
Let

.
By (
3
) with a bounded set
S=B(q_{\nu}^{-l+1})
,
\Lambda\cap S
is finite.
Pick a shortest non-zero vector in
\Lambda\cap S
. By the definition of

, this vector is also the shortest in
\Lambda-\{0\}
.

(
4
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
).
By (
4
), there exists a shortest vector
\mathbf{v}_{0}
in
\Lambda-\{0\}
, which implies
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)=\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|>0
.
To prove the discreteness of
\Lambda
, it suffices to show
(\mathbf{x}+B(\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)))\cap\Lambda=\{\mathbf{x}\}
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda

\mathbf{x}+B(\lambda_{1}(\Lambda))
is an open neighborhood of
\mathbf{x}
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda
and suppose there exists
\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbf{x}+B(\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)))\cap\Lambda-\{\mathbf{x}\}
by contradiction.
Since

and
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
, we have

.
Since
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{x}+B(\lambda_{1}(\Lambda))
, we have

,
which contradicts that

is a shortest non-zero vector in

.
∎

We will prove that the image of a lattice under any projection onto a

-subspace deleting some lattice vectors is a lattice again, which is used for the inductive proof of Theorem
1.4
.
We start by checking its discreteness in Lemma
3.4
.
Given a linearly independent set
\alpha
over

and a basis
\beta
of

extending
\alpha
, denote by

the projection of

along

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
i,k\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
with
i\leq k
.
Let
\Lambda
be a lattice in a
k
-dimensional subspace of
K_{\nu}^{n}
and
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}
be independent vectors over
K_{\nu}
in
\Lambda
.
Let
\alpha
be a basis of
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
and
\beta
be a basis of
K_{\nu}^{n}
extending
\alpha
. Then
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)
is a discrete
R_{\nu}
-submodule of
K_{\nu}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)
. Choose
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\Lambda
such that
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=\mathbf{x}
.
We have
\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\in\text{ker}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta})=\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
since

(3.1)

{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x})=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})-\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})-{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}}^{2}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=0.}

Write
\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}=a_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
with some
a_{1},\dots,a_{i}\in K_{\nu}
.
By
[
BPP
, Lemma 14.3]
,
K_{\nu}/(R_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Hence, there exists a finite subset
H
of
K_{\nu}
such that every
y\in K_{\nu}
can be written as
y=y_{h}+y_{r}+y_{o}
with
y_{h}\in H
,
y_{r}\in R_{\nu}
, and
y_{o}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
.
Applying it to
a_{1},\dots,a_{i}
, we have
a_{j}=a_{j,h}+a_{j,r}+a_{j,o}
for some
a_{j,h}\in H
,
a_{j,r}\in R_{\nu}
and
a_{j,o}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu},\forall j\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket
.
Define a lift function
f_{\alpha,\beta}:\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\Lambda
by

{f_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(a_{j}-a_{j,r})\mathbf{v}_{j}.}

Note that
f_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})
is not canonical since it depends on the choice of
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
and
a_{j,r}
.
To check that
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is well-defined, observe that

{f_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}\mathbf{v}_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j,r}\mathbf{v}_{j}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j,r}\mathbf{v}_{j}\in\Lambda}

since
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\Lambda
and
\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j,r}\mathbf{v}_{j}\in\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}\subset\Lambda
.
For any
\mathbf{x}\in\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)
, we have by the ultrametric property and
|a_{j,o}|\leq 1
that

(3.2)

{\begin{split}\|f_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}\|&=\|\sum_{j=1}^{i}(a_{j,h}+a_{j,o})\mathbf{v}_{j}\|\leq\max_{j=1}^{i}(\max(|a_{j,h}|,|a_{j,o}|)\cdot\|\mathbf{v}_{j}\|)\\
&\leq\max(\max_{h\in H}|h|,1)\cdot\max_{j=1}^{i}\|\mathbf{v}_{j}\|.\end{split}}

Denote
C=q_{\nu}\cdot\max(\max_{h\in H}|h|,1)\cdot\max_{j=1}^{i}\|\mathbf{v}_{j}\|
.
For any
\mathbf{x}\in\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)
,

{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x}))=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(a_{j}-a_{j,r})\mathbf{v}_{j})=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}}

since
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\text{ker}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta})
and (
3.1
). Hence,
f_{\alpha,\beta}
is injective.

Let
S
be a bounded set in
K_{\nu}^{n}
. It follows from (
3.2
) that

{f_{\alpha,\beta}(S\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda))\subseteq(S+B(C))\cap f_{\alpha,\beta}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)),}

where
B(C)
is the ball of radius
C
centered at 0. By the injectivity of
f_{\alpha,\beta}
,

{\begin{split}\Card{(S\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda))}&=\Card{(f_{\alpha,\beta}(S\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)))}\\
&\leq\Card{((S+B(C))\cap f_{\alpha,\beta}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)))}\\
&\leq\Card{((S+B(C))\cap\Lambda)}.\end{split}}

Since
S+B(C)
is bounded and
\Lambda
is discrete,
\Card{((S+B(C))\cap\Lambda)}
is finite by Lemma
3.2
(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
). By Lemma
3.2
(
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
),
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Lambda)
is discrete.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Delta
be a lattice in
K_{\nu}^{n}
.
If a finite subset of
\Delta
is a linearly independent set over
R_{\nu}
, then it is a linearly independent set over
K_{\nu}
.

Proof.

Let
A=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
be a linearly independent subset of
\Delta
over
R_{\nu}
.
Since
\Delta
has a
R_{\nu}
-basis, which is a linearly independent set over
K_{\nu}
, there exists
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K_{\nu})
such that
\Delta=gR_{\nu}^{n}
. Since
A
is a linearly independent set if and only if
g^{-1}A(\subset R_{\nu}^{n})
is, it suffices to show when
\Delta=R_{\nu}^{n}
.

We use induction on
i=\Card{(A)}
. It is clear when
i=1
.
Suppose that Lemma
3.5
holds for any subset whose cardinality is less than
i

(i\geq 2)
.
Recall that
\mathbf{v}_{I}=\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{\iota_{j}}
for each
I=\{\iota_{1},\dots,\iota_{j}\}\in\wp_{j}^{n}
and
\mathbf{v}_{I}
= 1 when
I
is empty.
Suppose
\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}=0
for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{i}\in K_{\nu}
.
Given each distinct pair
\alpha,\beta\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket
, multiplicating
\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\alpha,\beta\}}
to the above equation,

(3.3)

{a_{\alpha}\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\beta\}}+(-1)^{\alpha+\beta+1}a_{\beta}\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\alpha\}}=0.}

Since both
A-\{\mathbf{v}_{\alpha}\}
and
A-\{\mathbf{v}_{\beta}\}
are linearly independent sets over
R_{\nu}
of the cardinality less than
i
, they are linearly independent over
K_{\nu}
by the induction hypothesis, and thus,
\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\alpha\}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\beta\}}
are non-zero.
Since
A\subset R_{\nu}^{n}
, all coefficients of
\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\alpha\}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{\beta\}}
are in
R_{\nu}
. Thus, (
3.3
) implies that
\frac{a_{\alpha}}{a_{\beta}}
or
\frac{a_{\beta}}{a_{\alpha}}
is in
K=\text{frac}(R_{\nu})
if
a_{\alpha}\neq 0
or
a_{\beta}\neq 0
. Hence, if there exists a non-zero
a_{j}\in K_{\nu}
for some
j\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket
, then

{\frac{a_{1}}{a_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+\frac{a_{i}}{a_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{i}}

is a linear combination over
K
.
Since the linear independence over
R_{\nu}
and
K
are equivalent,
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\cdots+\frac{a_{i}}{a_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{i}=0
implies
a_{t}=0,\forall t\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket-\{j\}
, and thus,
a_{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}=0
.
But since
\mathbf{v}_{j}\neq 0
, we have
a_{j}=0
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
A
is a linearly independent set over
K_{\nu}
. ∎

By Lemma
3.5
, given a finite subset
\beta
of the lattice
\Delta
in
K_{\nu}^{n}
,
it suffices to check the linear independence over
R_{\nu}
, rather than over
K_{\nu}
, for
\beta
to be a
R_{\nu}
-basis of
\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}{\beta}
. Thus, we will use the abbreviation
R_{\nu}
-basis as a basis.

While the lemmas so far hold for any global function field,
the following Proposition needs to assume that the class group of
K
is trivial.
Recall that
R_{\nu}
is a Dedekind domain, and
K
is its field of fractions (see
[
BPP
, Section 14]
).
Note that little is known about number fields of class number one except for imaginary quadratic fields or cyclotomic fields, while the function fields of class number one are completely classified with the explicit list of equations (see
[
MS
]
or
[

]
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain. Let
\Delta
be a lattice in
K_{\nu}^{n}
and
\Lambda
be a
R_{\nu}
-submodule of
\Delta
. Then

(1)

\Lambda
is a lattice in
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\Lambda
.

(2)

Any primitive set
P
in
\Lambda
can be extended to a basis of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

(
1
). Since
R_{\nu}
is Noetherian and
\Delta
is finitely generated, its
R_{\nu}
-submodule
\Lambda
is finitely generated.
Since
\Lambda
is torsion-free as a submodule of free module
\Delta
,
\Lambda
is a free module by the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain. Therefore,
\Lambda
has a basis over
R_{\nu}
. By Lemma
3.5
,
\Lambda
is a lattice in
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\Lambda
.

(
2
). Let
\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda/\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}P
.
We checked that
\Lambda
is finitely generated in the proof of (
1
), and thus,
\overline{\Lambda}
is finitely generated as a quotient module of
\Lambda
.

Suppose
r\mathbf{v}\in\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}P
for some
r\in R_{\nu}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda
.
If
r\neq 0
, then
r^{-1}\in\text{frac}(R_{\nu})=K\subseteq K_{\nu}
, and thus,
\mathbf{v}=r^{-1}(r\mathbf{v})\in\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}P
.
Since
P
is a primitive set in
\Lambda
, we have
\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\cap\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}P=\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}P
. Thus,
\overline{\Lambda}
is torsion-free.
Since
\overline{\Lambda}
is torsion-free and finitely generated over a principal ideal domain
R_{\nu}
,
\overline{\Lambda}
is free by the structure theorem. From the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}P\rightarrow\Lambda\rightarrow\overline{\Lambda}\rightarrow 0
, we have

\Lambda\cong\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}P\oplus\overline{\Lambda}
, and we can find an extended basis of
\Lambda
using the freeness of
\overline{\Lambda}
.
∎

We remark that any finitely generated torsion-free module of rank
n
over a Dedekind domain
R
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
R^{n-1}
and a projective module
I
of rank 1.
We have
I=R
, and thus, given module has a basis when
R
is a principal ideal domain, but it fails for general Dedekind domains (See
[
Bou
, Chapter VII.4, Proposition 24]
).

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
R_{\nu}
is a principal ideal domain.
Let
\Delta
be a lattice in

, and let

and

be
\Delta
-rational subspaces of

and

are
\Delta
-rational subspaces of

Proof.

Since
L
and

are
\Delta
-rational, there exist bases
\beta_{L}
of

and
\beta_{M}

over

is
\Delta
-rational, it suffices to prove the following claim:

Claim 1.
If a subspace

of
K_{\nu}^{n}
contains a subset

satisfying
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}S_{N}=N
, then
N
is
\Delta
-rational.

Proof of Claim 1.

Let
j=\dim(N)
and
s=\text{rk}(N\cap\Delta)
.
Let
\beta_{1}=\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{j}\}
be a basis of
N
over
K_{\nu}
contained in

and

\beta_{2}=\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{s}\}
be a basis of

by Proposition
3.6
(
1
).
Since
\beta_{2}
is a linearly independent subset of
N
over
K_{\nu}
, we have

.
Denote by
W
the
(n,j)
-matrix with columns
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{j}
and

the
(n,s)
-matrix with columns
\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{s}
. Since
\beta_{1}\subset N\cap\Delta
and
\text{span}_{R_{\nu}}\beta_{2}=N\cap\Delta
,
there exists
B\in M_{s,j}(R_{\nu})
such that

.

Denote by
\text{rank}_{M}(X)
the matrix rank of the matrix
X
. Since
\beta_{1}
is a linearly independent set over

,
\text{rank}_{M}(W)=j
. Hence, we have

{j=\text{rank}_{M}(W)=\text{rank}_{M}(UB)\leq\text{rank}_{M}(B)\leq\min(s,j).}

Hence, we have

, and thus,
N
is
\Delta
-rational.
∎

Now, let us prove that
L\cap M
is
\Delta
-rational. Since
L
,
M
, and
L+M
are
\Delta
-rational and
\dim(L\cap M)=\text{dim}(L)+\text{dim}(M)-\text{dim}(L+M)

(3.4)

{\begin{split}\text{rk}(L\cap M\cap\Delta)&\leq\text{dim}(L)+\text{dim}(M)-\text{dim}(L+M)\\
&=\text{rk}(L\cap\Delta)+\text{rk}(M\cap\Delta)-\text{rk}((L+M)\cap\Delta).\end{split}}

On the other hand, define a
R_{\nu}
-module homomorphism
\phi
from
L\cap\Delta
to
\frac{(L+M)\cap\Delta}{M\cap\Delta}
by
\phi(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v}+(M\cap\Delta)
.
Observe that since
\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}(M\cap\Delta)=M
,

{M\cap\Delta=\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}(M\cap\Delta)\cap\{(L+M)\cap\Delta\},}

which means that
M\cap\Delta
is primitive in
(L+M)\cap\Delta
.

Let
\beta=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}\}
be a basis of

.

\beta

By Proposition

(

),

{\frac{(L+M)\cap\Delta}{M\cap\Delta}\cong\bigoplus_{j=m+1}^{s}R_{\nu}\mathbf{v}_{j},}

which means that

-module, and

{\begin{split}\text{rk}(L\cap\Delta)&=\text{rk}(\text{ker}(\phi))+\text{rk}(\text{Image}(\phi))\\

{\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}=a\mathbf{c}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket},\ \ \bigwedge_{s=i+1}^{j}\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{v}_{s})=\sum_{J\in\wp}b_{J}\mathbf{c}_{J}}

{d_{\Delta}(L\cap M)=\|\mathbf{v}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}\|=\|a\mathbf{c}_{\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket}\|=|a|.}

\sum_{s=i+1}^{j}a_{s}\mathbf{v}_{s}

\text{ker}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta})=\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
.
Since
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{j}
are linearly independent over

, we have
a_{i+1}=\cdots=a_{j}=0
, and thus,
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\alpha_{H}-\alpha_{0})
is a linearly independent set over
K_{\nu}
.

On the other hand, let
\mathbf{x}\in\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H\cap\Delta)
.
Choose

such that

. Since
\alpha_{H}
is a basis of

, there exist

such that

.
Since
\text{ker}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta})=\text{span}_{K_{\nu}}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{i}\}
, we have

{\mathbf{x}=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\sum_{s=1}^{j}b_{s}\mathbf{v}_{s})=\sum_{s=i+1}^{j}b_{s}\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{v}_{s}),}

which implies that
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\alpha_{H}-\alpha_{0})
generates
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H\cap\Delta)
over
R_{\nu}
. Hence,
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\alpha_{H}-\alpha_{0})
is a
R_{\nu}
-basis of
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H\cap\Delta)
.

In particular, applying

, we get a

-basis of
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)
, which implies that
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)
is a lattice in
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(K_{\nu}^{n})
.
By (
3.6
),
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H)
is
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)
-rational, and thus, we can define
d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H))
by a basis
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\alpha_{H}-\alpha_{0})
.
It follows from (
3.8
) and Corollary
2.6
that

(3.11)

{d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(H))=\|\bigwedge_{s=i+1}^{j}\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{v}_{s})\|=\|\sum_{J\in\wp}b_{J}c_{J}\|=\max_{J\in\wp}|b_{J}|.}

Thus,
Claim 1
follows from (
3.9
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
).
∎

Applying
L
,

, and
L+M
to
H
of

, it suffices to prove that

{d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M))\geq d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L+M)).}

Since
L\cap M\subseteq L
and

, we have

{\begin{split}\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L)\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)&=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L\cap M)=\{0\};\\
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L)+\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)&=\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L+M),\end{split}}

which implies that

{\text{dim}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L+M))=\text{dim}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))+\text{dim}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)).}

Let
s=\text{dim}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))
and

.
Since
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L),\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)
, and
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L+M)
are

-rational by (

), their intersection with

has a basis whose cardinality is the same as the dimension, respectively.
Let

, and

be a basis of
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L)\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)
,
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)\cap\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)
and

Then we have

(3.12)

{\begin{split}d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))&=\|\mathbf{x}_{\llbracket 1,s\rrbracket}\|;\\

By Corollary

&=d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))\cdot d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)).\end{split}}

Since

{\mathbf{x}_{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{s+t}c_{il}\mathbf{z}_{l}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{y}_{j}=\sum_{l=1}^{s+t}c_{(s+j)l}\mathbf{z}_{l}}

&\leq d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(L))d_{\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta)}(\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)).\end{split}}

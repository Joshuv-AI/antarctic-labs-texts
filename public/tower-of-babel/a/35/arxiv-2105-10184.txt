Balancing the Spread of Two Opinions in Sparse Social Networks
Dušan Knop, Šimon Schierreich, Ondřej Suchý

Abstract
Inspired by the famous Target Set Selection problem, we propose a new discrete model for simultaneously spreading several opinions within a social network and perform an initial study of its complexity. Here, we are given a social network, a seed-set of agents for each opinion, and two thresholds for each agent. The first threshold represents willingness of an agent to adopt an opinion if the agent has no opinion at all, while the second threshold states for willingness to acquire second opinion. The goal is to add as few agents as possible to the initial seed-sets such that, once the process started with these seed-sets stabilizes, each agent has either both opinions or none. We show that the problem is NP-hard . Further, we investigate the complexity from the parameterized point-of-view. The problem is W[1]-hard with respect to the solution size. The problem remains W[1]-hard even for the combination of parameters the solution size and treewidth of the network even if all thresholds are at most 3, or the activation process stabilizes within 4 rounds. On the other hand, the problem is FPT when parameterized by the number of rounds, maximum threshold, and treewidth. This algorithm applies also for combined parameter treedepth and maximum threshold. Finally, we show that the problem is FPT when parameterized by vertex cover number of the input network alone. Our results also implies that the original Target Set Selection problem is FPT when parameterized by 3-PVC.

Introduction

Our work is motivated by the recently proposed stochastic models in opinion diffusion
[
18
,
1
]
, where an extension of the
Target Set Selection
(
TSS
for short) problem is proposed.
TSS
was introduced by Domingos and Richardson
[
14
]
in order to study influence of direct marketing on a social network (see, e.g., the monographs
[
4
,
6
,
23
]
and references therein) and is defined as follows.
We are given a (social) network (i.e., an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
) and a
threshold value

f(v)\in\mathbb{N}
for every agent
v\in V
.
The task is to select a set of agents
T\subseteq V
so that when the following
activation process

P^{0}=T\qquad\text{and}\qquad P^{i+1}=P^{i}\cup\{v\in V\setminus P^{i}\,\mid\,\left|P^{i}\cap N(v)\right|\geq f(v)\}

stabilizes
with the set
P^{\mathcal{T}}
(i.e.,
P^{\mathcal{T}}=P^{\mathcal{T}+1}
) the size of
P^{\mathcal{T}}
is maximized; an important setting being we want
P^{\mathcal{T}}=V
.
It is worth mentioning that this problem is closely related to the model of cascades
[
16
, Chapter 16]
.
Later, Garimella et al.
[
18
]
extended this model to capture diffusion of two opinions and recently Becker et al.
[
1
]
studied three and more opinions.
These works are mainly motivated by diffusion in social network where it is desirable for the considered opinions to spread roughly equally and thus preventing unwanted behavior of the social network such as, e.g.,
echo bubbles
or
strengthening of extreme agents
(see, e.g.,
[
10
, Figure 1]
).
That is, the most desired outcome is that every agent, in the case for two opinions, either has no opinion or both using a similar activation process as above.
Before we formally define the model we propose in this work (the
2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
problem;
2
OTSS
for short), let us briefly mention that in the two and more opinion setting the motivation is to
balance the spread
and not to maximize one opinion spread. Then we discuss the differences to the model of Garimella et al.
[
18
]
in more detail.

Balancing Two Opinions

One might ask why to balance the spread of the two opinions already present in a social network.
Suppose there are two (rather antagonistic) opinions in a social network—if one knows which of these is the truthful one, they might, e.g., block the other.
What can we do if we can’t tell which one is truthful?
If many agents receive only the opinion which later turns out to be deceptive, then these agents might feel deceived by the network.
Instead, we decide to help both the opinions to spread evenly.
The agents receiving two different opinions can take adequate meassures to react to the situation.
Real world examples of this kind of information spread include, e.g., two experts having different opinions on covid-19 vaccination and both claiming having data supporting their opinion—how can an AI or even a human agent tell which of the two is trustworthy?

Two-Opinion Target Set Selection

We are given a graph
G=(V,E)
, seed sets
{S_{a},S_{b}\subseteq V}
, threshold functions
f_{1},f_{2}\colon V\to\mathbb{N}
, positive integer
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{N}
, and a positive integer
B\in\mathbb{N}
.
The task is to select two additional seed sets
T_{a},T_{b}\subseteq V
with
|T_{a}|+|T_{b}|\leq B
such that the following
selection process
terminates with
P_{a}^{\mathcal{T}}=P_{a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=P_{b}^{\mathcal{T}}=P_{b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
.
The initialization is by setting

P_{a}^{0}=S_{a}\cup T_{a}\qquad\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\qquad P_{b}^{0}=S_{b}\cup T_{b}

and the process continues with

\displaystyle P_{c}^{i+1}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V\setminus\left(P_{a}^{i}\cup P_{b}^{i}\right)\mid\left|P_{c}^{i}\cap N(v)\right|\geq f_{1}(v)\}\cup\{v\in P_{\neg c}^{i}\mid\left|P_{c}^{i}\cap N(v)\right|\geq f_{2}(v)\}\cup P_{c}^{i}

for
i\geq 0
and
c\in\{a,b\}
, where
\neg c
is the element in the set
\{a,b\}\setminus\{c\}
.
I.e., an agent
v
gains opinion
c
if she has no opinion and the number of her neighbors already having opinion
c
is at least
f_{1}(v)
or if she already has the other opinion and the number of her neighbors already having opinion
c
is at least
f_{2}(v)
.
It is not hard to see that the above process terminates in at most
2n
steps, where
n=|V|
, since in each nonterminal step we add at least one vertex to
P_{a}^{i}\cup P_{b}^{i}
(i.e.,
\left|P_{a}^{i}\setminus P_{a}^{i-1}\right|+\left|P_{b}^{i}\setminus P_{b}^{i-1}\right|\geq 1
).
Thus, letting
\mathcal{T}=2n
corresponds to putting no restriction on the length of the process.

Note that we model the network as an undirected graph.
Furthermore, unlike previous works, we do not have opinion-specific threshold values but we rather distinguish the order in which the agent is exposed to the opinions in question.
We believe that this is an interesting setting as it can capture different agent’s mindsets.
First, an agent
v
might be tougher towards the second opinion (by setting, e.g.,
f_{2}(v)=3f_{1}(v)
) for which we can see an application in modeling of, e.g., following political leaders.
Second, an agent
v
might infer the second opinion easily (by setting, e.g.,
f_{2}(v)=\frac{1}{4}f_{1}(v)
) for which we can see an application in modeling of, e.g., spread of different viruses in a population, since the first virus in a human body decreases its ability to resist other viruses.
It is worth pointing out that (even in the first mindset) if both opinions are strong enough at the same time, then the agent receives both the opinions (and evaluates the number of neighboring agents having a certain opinion only against the threshold value
f_{1}(v)
). For a running example of the selection process we refer the reader to
\Cref
fig:running_example (in the appendix).

It is easy to note that we can always assume
S_{a}\cap S_{b}=\emptyset
(unless
f_{i}
is defined as some function of
\deg(v)
); see
\cref
rr:vertexWithBothOpinions.
Furthermore, without loss of generality, we can assume that
B<|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|
as otherwise
T_{a}=S_{b}
and
T_{b}=S_{a}
is a solution.

Finally,
TSS
can be modelled by
2
OTSS
by taking the same network, an empty
S_{a}
and adding each vertex to
S_{b}
and treating its
f
as
f_{2}
.
The task is then to select minimum
T_{a}
such that the process covers the whole vertex set with opinion
a
.
Therefore many known lower bounds for
TSS
apply also to
2
OTSS
.

1.1
Related Work

As already mentioned, our work builds on foundations from the study of
Target Set Selection
. This problem itself was introduced in the context of direct marketing in social networks
[
14
]
; followed by more results and applications (see, e.g., the works Kempe et al.
[
21
]
or Richardson and Domingos
[
33
]
for the relation to viral marketing).
The initial research direction focused on studying the stochastic setting where the threshold values are selected at random and we try to maximize the expected spread of information
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
29
]
.
It is not surprising that
TSS
is
NP-hard
(the “static” variant where threshold values are part of the input), moreover, both its natural optimization variants (either maximize the size of
P^{\mathcal{T}}
or minimize the size of
T
so that
P^{\mathcal{T}}
is the whole vertex set) are hard to approximate.
In fact, for the minimization variant Chen
[
5
]
gave a polylogarithmic approximation lower bound which holds even if the input graph is bipartite and has bounded degree and all threshold values are either
1
or
2
.

It is not hard to see that
TSS
itself captures, e.g.,
Vertex Cover
.
It follows from Mathieson
[
28
]
that from the parameterized perspective
TSS
is
W[P]-complete
(which implies it is
W[1]-hard
) for the natural parameter (size of the solution set
T
); see Luo et al.
[
25
]
for the relation of the
r
-
Degenerate Editing
problem studied by Mathieson to
TSS
.
Given all of this it is not surprising that some special variants were introduced—such as when all threshold values are bounded by a constant or the
majority
variant in which all threshold values are set to half of the neighborhood size.
Cicalese et al.
[
9
,
8
]
considered a variant in which the number of rounds of the activation process is bounded.
Later Ben-Zwi et al.
[
2
]
initiated a systematic study of
TSS
for structural parameters.
Together with sequel works
[
3
,
7
,
15
,
19
,
31
]
this yields fairly complete understanding of tractability for structural parameters:

•

TSS
is
FPT
when parameterized by the vertex cover number, bandwidth, or feedback edge number,

•

the majority variant is
FPT
for parameters neighborhood diversity or twin-cover number,

•

the majority variant is
W[1]-hard
for parameter treedepth or modularwidth, and

•

TSS
is
FPT
when parameterized by cliquewidth if all threshold values are bounded by a parameter (or constant).

As we have already mentioned, the study of related problems concerning more than one opinion has been initiated rather recently
[
18
]
.
Both Garimella et al.
[
18
]
and Becker et al.
[
1
]
study related problem in stochastic setting, i.e., they assume the input is a directed graph and there is certain probability that an opinion is passed along an arc.

1.2
Our Contribution

In this work we propose a new discrete model for spreading information with at least two opinions—via the related computation problem
2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
—and perform initial study of its (parameterized) complexity. It should be pointed out that our model is (probably) the simplest and most natural combination of the deterministic model of
[
21
]
and the probabilistic model of
[
18
]
for spreading two (more) opinions. It is worth pointing out that the model we propose is the first allowing a certain level of interaction between the two opinions spreading in the network. In the previously proposed probabilistic model each agent has opinion-specific thresholds. Thus, our model enables the study of spreading opinions where if an agent receives one of them, this directly affects their ability/willingness to acquire the other.

The two most natural parameters for this problem are the sizes of the seed-sets (i.e.,
|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|
) and the solution size (i.e., the budget
B
).
The
2
OTSS
problem is
W[1]-hard
with respect to both these parameters (
\cref
thm:twoTSSIsHardForTWandConstFMax:simplified) and thus, following the approach for single opinion, we focus on studying its complexity for structural parameters of the input graph.
Limiting the structure of the input is further motivated by the recent study of Maniu et al.
[
26
]
who showed that many real-world networks exhibit bounded treewidth.
However, we show (
\cref
thm:twoTSSIsHardForTWandConstFMax:simplified) that the
2
OTSS
problem is
W[1]-hard
for the combination of parameters
|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|
and the treewidth of the input graph even if we assume that
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)\leq 3
for all
v\in V(G)
.
It is worth pointing out that the activation process is quite long in this case; thus one may ask what happens if we limit its length.
The
2
OTSS
problem is
W[1]-hard
for the combination of parameters
|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|
and the treewidth of the input graph even if we assume that any activation process stabilizes within 4 rounds (
\cref
thm:twoTSSIsHardForTDandConstRounds:simplified) and
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for all
v\in V(G)
.
In order to more precisely understand the complexity of the problem, we use more fine grained structural parameters such as pathwidth, feedback vertex number, or
4-path vertex cover number; which we defer to the appendix.

An algorithmic counterpart to the two hardness results is then the following.
We show that
2
OTSS
is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by the number of rounds of the activation process, maximum threshold, and the treewidth of the input graph (
\cref
thm:twoTSSIsFPTWrtRoundsFmaxTw).
The same algorithm applies for the combined parameter treedepth of the input graph and the maximum threshold. Note that we do not assume anything about the relation of
f_{1}(v)
and
f_{2}(v)
. We summarize our complexity results in
\Cref
tab:resultsSurvey.

Furthermore,
2
OTSS
is in
FPT
for the parameter vertex cover number (
\cref
thm:twoTSSIsFPTWrtVC) and
3
-path vertex cover (
\cref
thm:3-pvc). We note that the second algorithm applies also to the original
TSS
and was previously not known.

Preliminaries

For a set of vertices

For a set of vertices

For further notions from graph theory we refer the reader to the monograph of Diestel

A

Parameterized Complexity

An instance of a parameterized problem is

, where
x
is the problem input and

is the value of the parameter.
A parameterized problem

The class

contains both all problems in

and also problems believed not to be in

Hence, if a problem is proven

For more comprehensive overview of parameterized complexity theory we refer the reader to the monograph of Cygan et al.
[

]

Since

]

While designing the selection gadget (as well as in the proof of
\cref
lem:atLeastOneBVertexInSelectionGadget) we used an important trick.
One of the leaves of a guard path must receive both opinions,
a
and
b
, in the same round of the activation process.
We are going to utilize the same trick once again when designing gadget for checking incidence.

The Construction (Sketch)

For each edge
\{ww^{\prime}\}inE(H)
we add a selection gadget for vertices in
V_{w}
and for edges in

E_{ww^{\prime}}=\{\{u,v\}\mid\{u,v\}\in E(G),u\in V_{w},v\in V_{w^{\prime}}\}\,;

it should be pointed out that the preselected vertices in the selection gadget for edges are in
S_{b}
, while those of vertex selection gadget are in
S_{a}
.
Now, the idea is as follows.
We connect the selection parts of these gadgets using long paths with a special
sentry
vertex.
The length of a path encodes the selected vertex (the vertex incident to the selected edge, respectively).
The thresholds are set for the sentry vertex in such a way that it receives both opinions if and only if the two “selected paths” are of the same length (i.e., the vertex is in fact incident to the edge).
Otherwise, the sentry vertex receives only one of the two opinions.

Theorem 10
.

2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
is
W[1]-hard
when parameterized by
|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|
and the treewidth of the input graph combined, even if the maximum threshold
f_{\max}
is
3
and
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for every vertex
v
.

3.2
Constant Duration of Activation Process

In this section we show how our “colored” selection gadgets and the ideas presented in the
W[1]-hardness
reduction (with respect to treewidth) of Ben-Zwi et al.
[
3
]
yield a hardness result even if we assume that the length of an activation process is bounded by a constant.
The reduction is again from
PSI
, and again uses the edge representation strategy. Let
(G,H,\psi)
be an instance of
PSI
.

The key idea behind the reduction of Ben-Zwi et al. is to assign two enumerations to every vertex in
V_{w}
for
w\in V(H)
with
\operatorname{low}\colon V_{w}\to[|V_{w}|]
and
\operatorname{high}\colon V_{w}\to[|V_{w}|]
such that for every vertex
v\in V_{w}
we have
\operatorname{low}(v)+\operatorname{high}(v)=|V_{w}|
; if the reader is familiar with the original proof, we must admit that our use of their gadgets works in a somewhat simpler regime.
This time all our selection gadgets (i.e., vertex and edge selection gadgets) are the same as in
\Cref
fig:coloredVertexSelectionGadget (i.e., we only use
T_{b}
for the selection) and we set
f_{1}(v)=f_{2}(v)=\deg(v)
for every selection vertex
v
in these gadgets.
Crucially for us their reduction is highly “compact” in the sense that the result of their reduction has very low diameter which then yields a strong bound on the number of rounds of a (successful) selection process.
We set the budget
B
to the number of selection gadgets, i.e.,
B=|V(H)|+|E(H)|
.

The Construction (Sketch)

We introduce an incidence gadget for every edge
\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
and a node
w
.
As usual, the purpose is to check that the selected vertex in the set
V_{w}
is incident to the selected edge in the set
E_{ww^{\prime}}
.
To that end we define the mappings
\operatorname{low}
and
\operatorname{high}
for edges as well, however, now there are two such pairs of mappings for each edge.
That is, for an edge
e\in E_{ww^{\prime}}
we have mappings
\operatorname{low}_{w},\operatorname{high}_{w},\operatorname{low}_{w^{\prime}}
, and
\operatorname{high}_{w^{\prime}}
, where
\operatorname{low}_{w}(e)=\operatorname{low}(V_{w}\cap e)
and similarly for the other mappings.

We connect each vertex of a selection gadget to
|V_{w}|
new vertices we divide into groups representing the numbers
\operatorname{low}
and
\operatorname{high}
.
Then, we introduce two
sentry
vertices; one of which checks if
\operatorname{low}(v)+\operatorname{high}_{w}(e)\geq|V_{w}|
, while the other checks
\operatorname{high}(v)+\operatorname{low}_{w}(e)\geq|V_{w}|
.
This happens for both the sentry vertices if and only if the selected vertex is incident to the selected edge and we check this by some further auxiliary vertices.

Theorem 11
.

2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
is
W[1]-hard
when parameterized by the treedepth of the input graph and the budget
B
combined, even if
\mathcal{T}=4
rounds and
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for every vertex
v
.

4
Conclusions

We have initiated the study of the
2
OTSS
problem from the computational complexity perspective.
Similarly to
TSS
most combinations of natural parameters do not lead to efficient algorithms, with the parameterized complexity with respect to the number of rounds
\mathcal{T}
and the maximum threshold of
f_{\max}
(and possibly the budget
B
) being an interesting open problem. We also showed that there are promising algorithms for networks that are sparse (which is often the case in practice).
We believe that identifying and studying important special cases of
2
OTSS
(such as the majority version of the
TSS
problem) is an interesting research direction.

For our hardness results we heavily use that if both opinions pass the first threshold of an agent at the same time, she receives both these opinions in the next round.
What if, in the activation process, the agent prefers one of the opinions; i.e., if both the opinions pass the first threshold she only receives the preferred opinion.
We believe that our hardness reductions can be adapted to the case where the preferred opinion is agent-specific.
What is the complexity of the problem with one opinion preferred instance-wide?

Observation 15
.

form a solution if and only if they comply with the solution pattern

at the root node

.

\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a}|_{\beta(y)},r_{b}|_{\beta(y)},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable for
\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}
and the solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}
.

To sum up, if
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
is the minimum size solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
and it is of size
s
, then the algorithm did not set
\operatorname{DP}_{x}[\mathbf{p}]
to
s^{\prime}
with
s^{\prime}<s
. This could only happen if
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}]=s^{\prime}-\operatorname{cost}(a,\mathbf{p},v)-\operatorname{cost}(b,\mathbf{p},v)
, which would imply existence of a solution
\overline{T}^{x}_{a},\overline{T}^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
of size
s^{\prime}
, as we have shown and since
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}]
was computed correctly, contradicting the minimality of
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
. Moreover, there is a solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}
of size
s-\operatorname{cost}(a,\mathbf{p},v)-\operatorname{cost}(b,\mathbf{p},v)
that complies with
\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}
. Thus, as
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}]
was computed correctly,
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(y)}]\leq s-\operatorname{cost}(a,\mathbf{p},v)-\operatorname{cost}(b,\mathbf{p},v)
and therefore
\operatorname{DP}_{x}[\mathbf{p}]
is set to at most
s
by the algorithm. Hence
\operatorname{DP}_{x}[\mathbf{p}]=s
and it is computed correctly.
∎

Lemma 17
.

Let
x
be a node introducing an edge
\{u,v\}
with the child node
y
.
If the
\operatorname{DP}_{y}
was computed correctly, and
\operatorname{DP}_{x}
is computed using the algorithm for Introduce Edge Node from page
2.2
, then
\operatorname{DP}_{x}
is also computed correctly.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(r_{a},r_{b},g_{a},g_{b},h_{a},h_{b})
be a valid solution pattern for
x
.
Suppose first, that
h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=-1
for some
c\in\{a,b\}
. This implies that either
r_{c}(v)+1<r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
or
r_{c}(v)\leq\mathcal{T}
and
r_{c}(u)=\infty
and
h_{c}(u)=0
. Assume for contradiction that there is a solution
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
that complies with
\mathbf{p}
. Consider the sets
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
. As
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G_{x}
, we have
v\in N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(v)}_{c}
. If
r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
, then
r_{c}(u)-2\geq r_{c}(v)
which implies that
\left|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}\right|\geq 1>h_{c}(v)
.
If
r_{c}(u)=\infty
, then this implies that
\left|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}\right|\geq 1>h_{c}(v)
.
In both cases this contradicts the viability of the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
.
Hence, if
h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=-1
, then no solution complies with
\mathbf{p}
. By a similar argument this also holds if
h^{\prime}_{c}(v)=-1
and the answer of the algorithm is correct in these cases.

Note that otherwise
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
is a valid pattern as
\mathbf{p}
is valid and the only way
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
could be invalid is if
g_{c}(x)=0
or
h_{c}(x)=0
and
g^{\prime}_{c}(x)\neq 0
or
h^{\prime}_{c}(x)\neq 0
for some
x\in\{u,v\}
, which is handled by the maximum in the definition and the previous case, respectively.

Next we show that if there is a solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}=V_{x}
that complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, then it also complies with
\mathbf{p}
. The condition that
r^{-1}_{c}(0)=(S_{c}\cup T_{c}^{x})\cap\beta(x)
for
c\in\{a,b\}
is the same, as
\beta(x)=\beta(y)
. As the neighborhood of each vertex in
V_{x}\setminus\beta(x)
is the same in
G_{x}
and
G_{y}
the sets
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
obtained in the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
are the same as in the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
. Note that since
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable. The condition
\widehat{P}_{a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
remains valid.
Furthermore, for all
v^{\prime}\in\beta(y)\setminus\{u,v\}
, all
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
, and each
c\in\{a,b\}
we have
N_{G_{x}}(v^{\prime})\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}=N_{G_{y}}(v^{\prime})\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
. As
g_{c}(v^{\prime})=g^{\prime}_{c}(v^{\prime})
and
h_{c}(v^{\prime})=h^{\prime}_{c}(v^{\prime})
for these vertices, the viability conditions remain valid for these vertices.

For each
c\in\{a,b\}
if
r_{c}(v)\leq r_{c}(u)
and
r_{c}(v)\neq\infty
, then we have
N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}=N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
for
i\in\{r_{c}(v)-2,r_{c}(v)-1\}\cap\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
,
g^{\prime}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)
and
h^{\prime}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
. Hence, the viability condition remains valid for
v
in this case. Similarly, if
r_{c}(v)=r_{c}(u)=\infty
, then
N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}=N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}
.
If
r_{c}(v)=r_{c}(u)
, then the argument also holds for
u
by symmetry as
g^{\prime}_{c}(u)=g_{c}(u)
and
h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=h_{c}(u)
in this case.
If
r_{c}(v)+1=r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
, then we have
|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|=1+|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|\geq 1+g^{\prime}_{c}(u)=1+\max(0,g_{c}(u)-1)\geq g_{c}(u)
and
|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|=|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|\leq h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=h_{c}(u)
.
If
r_{c}(v)+1<r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
, then we have
|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|=1+|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|\geq 1+g^{\prime}_{c}(u)=1+\max(0,g_{c}(u)-1)\geq g_{c}(u)
and
|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|=1+|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|\leq 1+h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=1+h_{c}(u)-1=h_{c}(u)
.
If
r_{c}(v)\leq\mathcal{T}
and
r_{c}(u)=\infty
, then we have
|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}|=1+|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}|\leq 1+h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=1+h_{c}(u)-1=h_{c}(u)
. Therefore, the viability condition also remains valid for
u
in this case. The other cases follow by a symmetrical argument. Hence the process is viable and
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}
also complies with
\mathbf{p}
.

Now we show that if there is a solution
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}=V_{y}
that complies with
\mathbf{p}
, then it also complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
. As observed above, the only difference between the conditions imposed are the viability conditions for
u
and
v
.
Let again
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
be the sets obtained in the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
(or the other one, they are the same). For each
c\in\{a,b\}
if
r_{c}(v)\leq r_{c}(u)
and
r_{c}(v)\neq\infty
, then we have
N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}=N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
for
i\in\{r_{c}(v)-2,r_{c}(v)-1\}\cap\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
,
g^{\prime}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)
and
h^{\prime}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
. Hence, the viability condition remains valid for
v
in this case. Similarly, if
r_{c}(v)=r_{c}(u)=\infty
, then
N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}=N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}
.
If
r_{c}(v)=r_{c}(u)
, then the argument also holds for
u
by symmetry as
g^{\prime}_{c}(u)=g_{c}(u)
and
h^{\prime}_{c}(u)=h_{c}(u)
in this case.
If
r_{c}(v)+1=r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
, then we have
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|=|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|-1\geq g_{c}(u)-1
, hence
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|\geq\max(0,g_{c}(u)-1)=g^{\prime}_{c}(u)
and
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|=|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|\leq h_{c}(u)=h^{\prime}_{c}(u)
.
If
r_{c}(v)+1<r_{c}(u)\leq\mathcal{T}
, then we have
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|=|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|-1\geq g_{c}(u)-1
, hence
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-1}_{c}|\geq\max(0,g_{c}(u)-1)=g^{\prime}_{c}(u)
and
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|=|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{r_{c}(u)-2}_{c}|-1\leq h_{c}(u)-1=h^{\prime}_{c}(u)
.
If
r_{c}(v)\leq\mathcal{T}
and
r_{c}(u)=\infty
, then we have
|N_{G_{y}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}|=|N_{G_{x}}(u)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}|-1\leq h_{c}(u)-1=h^{\prime}_{c}(u)
. Therefore, the viability condition also remains valid for
u
in this case. The other cases follow a symmetrical argument. Hence, the process is viable and
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
also complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
.

To sum up, let
T_{a}^{x},T_{b}^{x}\subseteq V_{x}
be the minimum size solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
and it is of size
s
.
If we had
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]<s
, then, since
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]
was computed correctly, there would be a corresponding solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}
which complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
and is of size strictly less than

. But, as we have shown, this solution would comply with
\mathbf{p}
, contradicting the minimality of
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
. Hence, we have
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]\geq s
. Moreover, as
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, we have
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]\leq s
, that is,
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]=s
. Hence,
\operatorname{DP}_{x}[\mathbf{p}]=s
and it is computed correctly.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
x
be a node forgetting a vertex
v
with the child node
y
.
If the
\operatorname{DP}_{y}
was computed correctly, and
\operatorname{DP}_{x}

2.2
, then
\operatorname{DP}_{x}
is also computed correctly.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{p}
be a valid solution pattern for

.
Let us first note, that for each
\mathbf{q}=(\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b},\overline{g}_{a},\overline{g}_{b},\overline{h}_{a},\overline{h}_{b})
in
\mathcal{Q}
the pattern

\mathbf{p}

and for

we have the following.
First, if
r_{a}(v)=\overline{r}_{a}=\infty
, then
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}_{\infty,\infty}
, thus

, and vice versa. Second, if

Third, for each

in
\mathcal{Q}

, then
g_{c}(v)=\overline{g}_{c}=0
, whereas if
r_{c}(v)=\overline{r}_{c}\in\{0,1\}
, then

.

Now we show that if there is a solution
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}=V_{y}
that complies with
\mathbf{p}
, then it also complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q})
for some
\mathbf{q}=(\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b},\overline{g}_{a},\overline{g}_{b},\overline{h}_{a},\overline{h}_{b})
in
\mathcal{Q}
. Consider the sets
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
and let
\widehat{P}^{-1}_{c}=\emptyset
. Let
\overline{r}_{c}
be such that
v\in\left(\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{c}}_{c}\setminus\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{c}-1}_{c}\right)
or
\infty
if
v\notin\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{c}
. Note that we have
v\in S_{c}\cup T^{x}_{c}=\widehat{P}^{0}_{c}
if and only if
\overline{r}_{c}=0
. Thus
\mathbf{q}_{\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b}}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.

We want to show that also
\mathbf{q}_{\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b}}\in\mathcal{Q}
and that
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q}_{\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b}})
. Assume that
\overline{r}_{a}\leq\overline{r}_{b}
.
If
\overline{r}_{a}\in\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
, then, since
v\in\left(\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}}_{a}\setminus\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}-1}_{a}\right)
and

, we have
f_{1}(v)=\overline{g}_{a}\leq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}-1}_{a}|
. If
\overline{r}_{a}\in\{2,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
, then, as
v\notin\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}-1}_{a}
, we have
f_{1}(v)-1=\overline{h}_{a}\geq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}-2}_{a}|
. Similarly, if
\overline{r}_{a}=\infty
, then, as
v\notin\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}+1}_{a}
, we have
f_{1}(v)-1=\overline{h}_{a}\geq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{a}|
. If
\overline{r}_{b}=\overline{r}_{a}
, then we have the same bounds for
\overline{g}_{b}
and
\overline{h}_{b}
.
If
\overline{r}_{b}=\overline{r}_{a}+1
, then, as
v\in\left(\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}}_{b}\setminus\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{b}\right)
and
v\in\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{a}
we have
f_{2}(v)=\overline{g}_{b}\leq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{b}|
and, as
v\notin\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-2}_{a}
, that
f_{1}(v)-1=\overline{h}_{b}\geq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-2}_{b}|
.
If
\overline{r}_{b}\geq\overline{r}_{a}+2
, then, as
v\in\left(\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}}_{b}\setminus\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{b}\right)
and
v\in\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{a}
we have
f_{2}(v)=\overline{g}_{b}\leq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-1}_{b}|
and, as
v\in\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-2}_{a}
, that
f_{2}(v)-1=\overline{h}_{b}\geq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-2}_{b}|
.
In particular these inequalities imply that all coordinates of
\mathbf{q}_{\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b}}
are at least
0
and, hence,
\mathbf{q}_{\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b}}\in\mathcal{Q}
.

To show that
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=(r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},g^{\prime}_{a},g^{\prime}_{b},h^{\prime}_{a},h^{\prime}_{b})
note that
r^{\prime-1}_{c}(0)=(S_{c}\cup T_{c}^{x})\cap\beta(y)
due to the way we defined
\overline{r}_{c}
and consider the sets
\overline{P}^{i}_{c}
obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
. Again, due to the way we defined
\overline{r}_{c}
we have
\overline{P}^{i}_{c}=\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
and each
c\in\{a,b\}
. Hence the condition
\widehat{P}_{a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
remains valid. Similarly, the viability conditions are satisfied for each vertex
v^{\prime}\in\beta(x)
as
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|_{\beta(x)}=\mathbf{p}|_{\beta(x)}
. The viability conditions for the vertex
v
are given by the inequalities of the previous paragraph. Hence
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
is viable and
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
.

Next we show that if there is a solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}=V_{y}
that complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q})
for some
\mathbf{q}=(\overline{r}_{a},\overline{r}_{b},\overline{g}_{a},\overline{g}_{b},\overline{h}_{a},\overline{h}_{b})
in
\mathcal{Q}
, then it also complies with
\mathbf{p}
. Let
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=(r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},g^{\prime}_{a},g^{\prime}_{b},h^{\prime}_{a},h^{\prime}_{b})
. Since
r_{a}=r^{\prime}_{a}|_{\beta(x)}
,
r^{\prime-1}_{c}(0)=(S_{c}\cup T_{c}^{y})\cap\beta(y)
implies
r^{-1}_{c}(0)=(S_{c}\cup T_{c}^{y})\cap\beta(x)
. Our goal is to show the viability of the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
based on the viability of the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
. To this end let
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
and
\overline{P}^{i}_{c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
.
We claim that
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}=\overline{P}^{i}_{c}
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
and each
c\in\{a,b\}
. Once this is proven, the viability of
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
follows, since
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|_{\beta(x)}
.

We prove the claim by induction on

. For

we have
\widehat{P}^{0}_{c}=\overline{P}^{0}_{c}=T^{y}_{c}\cup(S_{c}\cap V_{x})
, constituting the base case of the induction. For

note that for each
v^{\prime}\in V_{x}\setminus\{v\}
the conditions for
v^{\prime}
to be included in
\widehat{P}^{i}_{c}
and in
\overline{P}^{i}_{c}
are the same, as
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{c}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{c}
.
Assume that
\overline{r}_{a}\leq\overline{r}_{b}
.

•

If
i<\overline{r}_{a}
, then
v\notin\left(\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}\cup\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}\right)
and, as the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable, we have
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}|\leq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{a}-2}_{a}|\leq h^{\prime}_{a}(v)=\overline{h}_{a}=f_{1}(v)-1
. As
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{a}
by induction hypothesis, this implies that
v\notin\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
. Similar argument works for
\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
, even for
i=\overline{r}_{a}<\overline{r}_{b}
.

•

If
i=\overline{r}_{a}
, then
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}
, and, as the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable, we have
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}|\geq g^{\prime}_{a}(v)=\overline{g}_{a}=f_{1}(v)
. As
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{a}
by induction hypothesis, this implies that
v\in\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
. Similar argument works for
\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
if
\overline{r}_{b}=\overline{r}_{a}
.

•

If
\overline{r}_{a}<i<\overline{r}_{b}
, then
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{a}
,
v\notin\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}
and, as the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable, we have
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{b}|\leq|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{\overline{r}_{b}-2}_{b}|\leq h^{\prime}_{b}(v)=\overline{h}_{b}=f_{2}(v)-1
. As
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{b}
by induction hypothesis, this implies that
v\notin\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
.

•

If
i=\overline{r}_{b}>\overline{r}_{a}
, then
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{a}
,
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}
and, as the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r^{\prime}_{a},r^{\prime}_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
is viable, we have
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{b}|\geq g^{\prime}_{b}(v)=\overline{g}_{b}=f_{2}(v)
. As
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{b}
by induction hypothesis, this implies that
v\in\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
.

•

If
i>\overline{r}_{b}
, then
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{a}
and
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i-1}_{b}
, thus
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}
,
v\in\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
,
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}
, and
v\in\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
. Therefore
\widehat{P}^{i}_{a}=\overline{P}^{i}_{a}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{b}=\overline{P}^{i}_{b}
.

The proof for the case
\overline{r}_{a}>\overline{r}_{b}
follows by a symmetric argument.
This finishes the proof of the induction step and the proof of the claim.

To sum up, let
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
be a minimum size solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
and let it be of size
s
.
If we had
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]<s
for
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q})
for some
\mathbf{q}\in Q
, then, since
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]
was computed correctly, there would be a corresponding solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}
which complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
and is of size strictly less than
s
. But, as we have shown, this solution would comply with
\mathbf{p}
, contradicting the minimality of
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
. Hence we have
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]\geq s
for every
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q}),\mathbf{q}\in Q
. Moreover, as
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
for
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\cup(v\mapsto\mathbf{q})
for suitable
\mathbf{q}\in Q
, we have
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]\leq s
for this
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
, that is,
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]=s
. Hence
\operatorname{DP}_{x}[\mathbf{p}]=s
and it is computed correctly.
∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
x
be a join node with children
y
and
z
.
If the
\operatorname{DP}_{y}
and
\operatorname{DP}_{z}
were computed correctly, and
\operatorname{DP}_{x}
is computed using the algorithm for Join Node from page
2.2
, then
\operatorname{DP}_{x}
is also computed correctly.

Proof.

Note first that, since each edge is introduced only once, for each vertex
v\in\beta(x)=\beta(y)=\beta(z)
we have
N_{G_{x}}(v)=N_{G_{y}}(v)\cup N_{G_{z}}(v)
and
N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap N_{G_{z}}(v)=\emptyset
. Note also that
V_{y}\cap V_{z}=\beta(x)
and for each vertex
v\in\alpha(y)
we have
N_{G_{x}}(v)=N_{G_{y}}(v)\subseteq V_{y}
and similarly for
v\in\alpha(z)
.

Let first
\mathbf{p}=(r_{a},r_{b},g_{a},g_{b},h_{a},h_{b})
be a valid pattern for
x
,
\mathbf{p}_{y}=(r^{y}_{a},r^{y}_{b},g^{y}_{a},g^{y}_{b},h^{y}_{a},h^{y}_{b})
be a valid pattern for
y
, and
\mathbf{p}_{z}=(r^{z}_{a},r^{z}_{b},g^{z}_{a},g^{z}_{b},h^{z}_{a},h^{z}_{b})
be a valid pattern for
z
such that for every
v\in\beta(x)
and every
c\in\{a,b\}
we have
r^{y}_{c}(v)=r^{z}_{c}(v)=r_{c}(v)
,
g^{y}_{c}(v)+g^{z}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)
, and
h^{y}_{c}(v)+h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
.
Let
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}
be a solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and
T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b}\subseteq V_{z}
be a solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}_{z}
.

We want to show that
T^{x}_{a}=T^{y}_{a}\cup T^{z}_{a}
and
T^{x}_{b}=T^{y}_{b}\cup T^{z}_{b}
form a solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
and has size
|T^{y}_{a}|+|T^{y}_{b}|+|T^{z}_{a}|+|T^{z}_{b}|-\sum_{c\in\{a,b\}}\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
.
Since
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}_{y}
we have that
v\in\beta(y)
is in
T^{y}_{c}
for
c\in\{a,b\}
if and only if
r^{y}_{c}(v)=0
and
v\notin S_{c}
. However, this is exactly when
\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p}_{y},v)
is
1
. Similarly for
T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b}
. Since
r_{c}^{y}=r_{c}^{z}=r_{c}
, it follows that
T^{y}_{c}\cap\beta(x)=T^{z}_{c}\cap\beta(x)=T^{x}_{c}\cap\beta(x)
,
|T^{y}_{c}\cap T^{z}_{c}|=\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
, and
|T^{y}_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c}|=|T^{y}_{c}|+|T^{z}_{c}|-\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
, giving the size bound.

We want to show the viability of the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
.
To this end let
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
,

\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
, and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(z,r_{a},r_{b},T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b})
.
We claim that
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}\cup\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}\cap\beta(x)
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
and each
c\in\{a,b\}
.

We prove the claim by induction on
i
. For
i=0
we have
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(y),c}=(S_{c}\cup T^{y}_{c})\cap V_{y}
,
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(z),c}=(S_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c})\cap V_{z}
, and
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(x),c}=(S_{c}\cup T^{x}_{c})\cap V_{x}=(S_{c}\cup T^{y}_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c})\cap(V_{y}\cup V_{z})=(S_{c}\cap V_{y})\cup\left((T^{y}_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c})\cap V_{y}\right)\cup(S_{c}\cap V_{z})\cup\left((T^{y}_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c})\cap V_{z}\right)=(S_{c}\cap V_{y})\cup(T^{y}_{c}\cap V_{y})\cup(S_{c}\cap V_{z})\cup(T^{z}_{c}\cap V_{z})=\left((S_{c}\cup T^{y}_{c})\cap V_{y}\right)\cup\left((S_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c})\cap V_{z}\right)=\widehat{P}^{0}_{(y),c}\cup\widehat{P}^{0}_{(z),c}
.
Moreover, as we have shown,
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(x),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{0}_{(y),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{0}_{(z),c}\cap\beta(x)=r^{-1}_{c}(0)\setminus S_{c}
.
This constitutes the base case of the induction.

Assume
i\geq 1
and the claim holds for all lesser
i
.
For
v\in\beta(x)
we have
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
if and only if
r_{c}(v)\geq i
, and, as
r_{c}=r^{y}_{c}=r^{z}_{c}
, the condition for inclusion in
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}
and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
is exactly the same. Hence
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}\cap\beta(x)=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}\cap\beta(x)
.
For
v\in\alpha(y)
we have
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
if either
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(y),c}
, or
v\notin(\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{i-1}\cup\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{i-1})
and
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{1}(v)
, or

v\in\widehat{P}_{(y),\neg c}^{i-1}
and
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{2}(v)
.
However, since
N_{G_{x}}(v)=N_{G_{y}}(v)\subseteq V_{y}
,
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(x),c}=\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(y),c}\cup\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(z),c}
and, thus,
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(y),c}=V_{y}\cap\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(x),c}
, we have
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|=|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|
. Therefore,
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
if either
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(x),c}
, or
v\notin(\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{i-1}\cup\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{i-1})
and
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{1}(v)
, or

v\in\widehat{P}_{(x),\neg c}^{i-1}
and
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{2}(v)
. However, this is exactly if
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
.
A completely analogous argument shows, that for
v\in\alpha(z)
we have
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}
if and only if
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
.
This finishes the proof of the induction step and the proof of the claim.

Since the processes
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
and
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(z,r_{a},r_{b},T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b})
are viable, we have
\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
and

\widehat{P}_{(z),a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(z),a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(z),b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(z),b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
.
As
\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\cup\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{\mathcal{T}}
and
\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{\mathcal{T}+1}\cup\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
, it follows that
\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
.

For each
c\in\{a,b\}
and all
v\in\beta(x)
with
1\leq r_{c}(v)<\infty
we have
\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|+\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|\geq g^{y}_{c}(v)+g^{z}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)
.
If
r_{c}(v)\geq 2
, we also have
\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|+\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|\leq h^{y}_{c}(v)+h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
.
Finally, for each
c\in\{a,b\}
and all
v\in\beta(x)
with
r_{c}(v)=\infty
we have
\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|+\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|\leq h^{y}_{c}(v)+h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
. Hence the process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
is viable for
\mathbf{p}
and
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
is a solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
.

Next we show that if
\mathbf{p}=(r_{a},r_{b},g_{a},g_{b},h_{a},h_{b})
is a valid pattern for
x
and
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
is a solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
, then there is a valid pattern
\mathbf{p}_{y}=(r_{a},r_{b},g^{y}_{a},g^{y}_{b},h^{y}_{a},h^{y}_{b})
for
y
, a valid pattern
\mathbf{p}_{z}=(r_{a},r_{b},g^{z}_{a},g^{z}_{b},h^{z}_{a},h^{z}_{b})
for
z
, a solution
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}\subseteq V_{y}
which complies with
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and a solution
T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b}\subseteq V_{z}
which complies with
\mathbf{p}_{z}
such that for every
v\in\beta(x)
and every
c\in\{a,b\}
we have
g^{y}_{c}(v)+g^{z}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)
,
h^{y}_{c}(v)+h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)
, and
|T^{y}_{a}|+|T^{y}_{b}|+|T^{z}_{a}|+|T^{z}_{b}|=|T^{x}_{a}|+|T^{x}_{b}|+\sum_{c\in\{a,b\}}\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
.

Let
T^{y}_{c}=T^{x}_{c}\cap V_{y}
and
T^{z}_{c}=T^{x}_{c}\cap V_{z}
. Since
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}
we have that
v\in\beta(x)
is in
T^{x}_{c}
if and only if
r_{c}(v)=0
and
v\notin S_{c}
for
c\in\{a,b\}
. However, this is exactly when
\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
is
1
. Furthermore, the same holds for
T^{y}_{c}
and
T^{z}_{c}
, as
\beta(x)=V_{y}\cap V_{z}
.
It follows that
T^{y}_{c}\cap\beta(x)=T^{z}_{c}\cap\beta(x)=T^{x}_{c}\cap\beta(x)
,
|T^{y}_{c}\cap T^{z}_{c}|=\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
, and
|T^{y}_{c}\cup T^{z}_{c}|=|T^{y}_{c}|+|T^{z}_{c}|-\sum_{v\in\beta(x)}\operatorname{cost}(c,\mathbf{p},v)
, giving the size bound.

Before we define
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and
\mathbf{p}_{z}
we consider the modified processes corresponding to the solutions.
Let
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
,

\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
, and
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}
be the sets obtained in process
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(z,r_{a},r_{b},T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b})
.
We claim that
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}\cap V_{y}
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
and each
c\in\{a,b\}
.

We prove the claim by induction on
i
. For
i=0
we have
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(x),c}=(S_{c}\cup T^{x}_{c})\cap V_{x}
while
\widehat{P}^{0}_{(y),c}=(S_{c}\cup T^{y}_{c})\cap V_{y}=\widehat{P}^{0}_{(x),c}\cap V_{y}

This constitutes the base case of the induction.

Assume
i\geq 1
and the claim holds for all lesser
i
.
For
v\in\beta(x)
we have
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
if and only if
r_{c}(v)\geq i
, and the condition for inclusion in
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
is exactly the same.
For
v\in\alpha(y)
we have
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
if either
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(x),c}
, or
v\notin(\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{i-1}\cup\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{i-1})
and
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{1}(v)
, or

v\in\widehat{P}_{(x),\neg c}^{i-1}
and
|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{2}(v)
.
However, since
N_{G_{x}}(v)=N_{G_{y}}(v)\subseteq V_{y}
,
\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(y),c}=\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(x),c}\cap V_{y}
, we have
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|=|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{i-1}|
. Therefore,
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}
if either
v\in\widehat{P}^{i-1}_{(y),c}
, or
v\notin(\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{i-1}\cup\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{i-1})
and
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{1}(v)
, or

v\in\widehat{P}_{(y),\neg c}^{i-1}
and
|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{i-1}|\geq f_{2}(v)
. However, this is exactly if
v\in\widehat{P}^{i}_{(y),c}
.
This finishes the proof of the induction step and the proof of the claim.

A completely analogous argument shows, that
\widehat{P}^{i}_{(z),c}=\widehat{P}^{i}_{(x),c}\cap V_{z}
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,\mathcal{T}\}
and each
c\in\{a,b\}
. Since
\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(x),a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(x),b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
, it follows that
\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(y),a}^{\mathcal{T}+1}=\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{P}_{(y),b}^{\mathcal{T}+1}
and similarly for
z
.

Now we define the rest of
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and
\mathbf{p}_{z}
. Let
c\in\{a,b\}
and
v\in\beta(x)
.
If
r_{c}(v)\in\{0,\infty\}
then let
g^{y}_{c}(v)=g^{z}_{c}(v)=0
(note that
g_{c}(v)=0
in this case by validity of
\mathbf{p}
).
Otherwise we let
g^{y}_{c}(v)=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|
and
g^{z}_{c}(v)=g_{c}(v)-g^{y}_{c}(v)
.
Note that in this case, by viability of
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(x,r_{a},r_{b},T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b})
we have
g_{c}(v)\leq\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|
and as
N_{G_{x}}(v)=N_{G_{y}}(v)\cup N_{G_{z}}(v)
and
N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap N_{G_{z}}(v)=\emptyset
we have
\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|=\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|-\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-1}\right|\geq g_{c}(v)-g^{y}_{c}(v)=g^{z}_{c}(v)
.

If
r_{c}(v)\in\{0,1\}
then let
h^{y}_{c}(v)=h^{z}_{c}(v)=0
(note that
h_{c}(v)=0
in this case).
If
r_{c}(v)=\infty
, then we let
h^{y}_{c}(v)=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|
and
h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)-h^{y}_{c}(v)
.
In this case
\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|\leq h_{c}(v)
and, thus,
\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|=\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|-\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{\mathcal{T}}\right|\leq h_{c}(v)-h^{y}_{c}(v)=h^{z}_{c}(v)
.
Otherwise let
h^{y}_{c}(v)=\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|
and
h^{z}_{c}(v)=h_{c}(v)-h^{y}_{c}(v)
.
In this case
\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|\leq h_{c}(v)
and
\left|N_{G_{z}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(z),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|=\left|N_{G_{x}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(x),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|-\left|N_{G_{y}}(v)\cap\widehat{P}_{(y),c}^{r_{c}(v)-2}\right|\leq h_{c}(v)-h^{y}_{c}(v)=h^{z}_{c}(v)
.

It follows directly from the definition and the notes therein that
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and
\mathbf{p}_{z}
are valid and that the processes
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(y,r_{a},r_{b},T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b})
and
\mathcal{\widehat{P}}(z,r_{a},r_{b},T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b})
are viable for them.
Thus
T^{y}_{a},T^{y}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}_{y}
and
T^{z}_{a},T^{z}_{b}
complies with
\mathbf{p}_{z}
.

To sum up, let
T^{x}_{a},T^{x}_{b}\subseteq V_{x}
be a minimum size solution which complies with
\mathbf{p}
and let it be of size
s
.
On one hand, if we had
\operatorname{DP}_{y}[\mathbf{p}_{y}]=s_{y}
and
\operatorname{DP}_{z}[\mathbf{p}_{z}]=s_{z}
for some pair of valid patterns
\mathbf{p}_{y}=(r^{y}_{a},r^{y}_{b},g^{y}_{a},g^{y}_{b},h^{y}_{a},h^{y}_{b})
and

Proof of
\cref

We prove by induction on the length

of the walk

that
\operatorname{td}(G)\geq\lceil\log_{r+1}(h+2)\rceil
.

If

contains a nonempty walk, then it must have at least one vertex and, thus,

.
Therefore the lemma is true whenever the length of the walk is

.

Assume now that
h\geq r
and the lemma holds for all shorter walks.
Note that the walk contains at least two different vertices and hence
|V(G)|>1
.
If
G
is disconnected, then we can limit ourselves to the component which contains the walk, since

, where

are connected components of

.
Hence, we assume that

Let

be the vertex such that

.
If
u
is not part of the walk, then the walk
w
must be contained in a single connected component
C
of

\operatorname{td}(G)=1+\operatorname{td}(G\setminus\{u\})\geq\operatorname{td}(C)

Hence, we assume that

As

is a walk not containing

Theorem 25
.

2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
is
W[1]-hard
when parameterized by the treedepth, the feedback vertex number, the 4-path vertex cover number of the input graph and the budget
B

\mathcal{T}=4
rounds and
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for every vertex
v
.
Moreover, unless ETH fails, there is no algorithm for
2
-Opinion Target Set Selection
running in
g(k)n^{o(k/\log k)}
, where
k
is the sum of the budget
B
, the treedepth, the feedback vertex number, and the 4-path vertex cover number of the input graph and
g
is an arbitrary function, even if any successful activation process is guaranteed to stabilize in
\mathcal{T}=4
rounds and
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for every vertex
v
.

Proof.

Let us again first verify the parameters of our reduction.
If we remove from
\widehat{G}
all the checking vertices as well as the vertices of both guard paths in each selection gadget (let us denote this set
X
), we obtain a forest, composed of stars of two kinds.
Namely, the first kind of stars is formed by a special vertex
s_{ww^{\prime}}
and leaves
A_{ww^{\prime}}
for some
\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
.
The second kind is a selection vertex with several groups of adjacent vertices, one for each incidence gadget it is a part of.
As both of them are stars, their treedepth is
2
and they contain no paths on
4

|X|=2\cdot 2\cdot|E(H)|+6(|V(H)|+|E(H)|)=O(|E(H)|)
, the treedepth, the feedback vertex number, and the 4-path vertex cover number of

are
O(|E(H)|)
. Furthermore, as
S_{a}\cup S_{b}\subseteq X
, we also have
B<|S_{a}|+|S_{b}|=O(|E(H)|)
. Hence, once we verify the correctness of the reduction, the results will follow from
\cref
thm:PSI_ETH and the discussion thereafter.

Suppose that
(T_{a},T_{b})
is not good.
Then, by
\cref
lem:atLeastOneBVertexInSelectionGadget there exists a vertex in a guard path with
v\in P^{\mathcal{T}}_{a}\setminus P^{\mathcal{T}}_{b}
and thus such a pair cannot be a valid solution.

By
\cref
lem:goodGivesAToAllInFourRounds we have
P^{4}_{a}=V(\widehat{G})
, that is, the activation process for
a
stabilizes in four rounds.
By
\cref
lem:checkingVerticesInRoundTwoIFFIncident we have that the checking vertices are in
P^{2}_{b}
if and only if the selected vertices are incident to the selected edges, i.e., if and only if the original instance of PSI is a yes-instance.
Note that if there exists a checking vertex
c
with
c\notin P^{2}_{b}
, then (by
\cref
lem:checkingVerticesInRoundTwoIFFIncident) we also have
c\in P^{2}_{a}
.
It follows from the proof of
\cref
lem:checkingVerticesInRoundTwoIFFIncident that for the vertices
A_{ww^{\prime}}
adjacent to
c
it holds that
A_{ww^{\prime}}\notin P^{3}_{b}
.
Since we have
f_{2}(c)=\deg(c)
and since no vertex in
N(A_{ww^{\prime}})
can be in
P^{k}_{b}\setminus P^{2}_{b}
, we get that if this is the case, then
(T_{a},T_{b})
is not a target set for
\widehat{G}
.
Consequently, if there is no good
(T_{a},T_{b})
which is a target set for
\widehat{G}
, then the original instance of PSI is a No-instance.

On the other hand, if all checking vertices are in

, then by
\cref
lem:checkingVerticesInRoundTwoIFFIncident we have that

as well as all special vertices are in

.
Furthermore, we get that all vertices incident to the checking vertices are in

Note that now we have the set

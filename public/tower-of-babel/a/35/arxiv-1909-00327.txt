Alcove paths and Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns
Hideya Watanabe and Keita Yamamura

Abstract
. In their study of the equivariant K-theory of the generalized flag varieties G / P G/P , where G G is a complex semisimple Lie group, and P P is a parabolic subgroup of G G , Lenart and Postnikov introduced a combinatorial tool, called the alcove path model. It provides a model for the highest weight crystals with dominant integral highest weights, generalizing the model by semistandard Young tableaux. In this paper, we prove a simple and explicit formula describing the crystal isomorphism between the alcove path model and the Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern model for type A A .

1.
Introduction

Lenart and Postnikov
[
LP07
]
provided a Chevalley-type formula for the equivariant
K
-theory of generalized flag varieties
G/P
, where
G
is a complex semisimple Lie group, and
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Their formula is based on a combinatorial model for the highest weight crystals, called the alcove path model, which was also introduced by them. To be more specific, let
\lambda
be a dominant integral weight, and
A_{\circ}
the fundamental alcove. Then, Lenart-Postnikov’s Chevalley-type formula tells us that the product of a Schubert class and the class of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
corresponding to
-\lambda
in the equivariant
K
-theory of
G/P
is determined by counting the number of certain subsequences (called the admissible subsets) of a fixed sequence of adjacent alcoves (called a reduced alcove path) from
A_{\circ}
to
A_{\circ}-\lambda
.

Their formula has applications in the representation theory of
G
, and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For each dominant integral weight
\lambda
, there exists a unique irreducible highest weight module
V(\lambda)
of highest weight
\lambda
. For each integral weight
\mu
, let
V(\lambda)_{\mu}
denote the weight space of weight
\mu
. The characters
s_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\mu}(\dim V(\lambda)_{\mu})e^{\mu}
, where the sum runs over the integral weights
\mu
, and
e^{\mu}
is the standard basis of the group algebra of the integral weight lattice, play important roles in the representation theory of
\mathfrak{g}
. To each admissible subset
J
, an integral weight
\operatorname{wt}(J)
, called its weight, is assigned. Then, Lenart and Postnikov proved the following character formula:

s_{\lambda}=\sum_{J}e^{\operatorname{wt}(J)},

where the sum runs over the admissible subsets. Since this formula is cancellation-free, the set of admissible subsets (resp., admissible subsets of weight
\mu
) is in one-to-one correspondence with any basis of
V(\lambda)
(resp.,
V(\lambda)_{\mu}
).

The irreducible highest weight module
V(\lambda)
has a distinguished basis
B(\lambda)
; Lusztig’s canonical basis
[
Lu90
]
, or Kashiwara’s global crystal basis
[
K91
]
. In fact, the canonical basis, or global crystal basis is a basis of the irreducible highest weight module
V_{q}(\lambda)
of highest weight
\lambda
over the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. Taking the limit
q\rightarrow 0
, we obtain the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Although
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is no longer a basis of
V_{q}(\lambda)
nor
V(\lambda)
, it parametrizes the basis elements of
V(\lambda)
. Moreover, to each element
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, an integral weight
\operatorname{wt}(b)
is assigned, and we have
s_{\lambda}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}e^{\operatorname{wt}(b)}
. Hence, there should exist a natural bijection between the set of admissible subsets and
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
which preserves the weights.

As we have mentioned above, the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is the limit at
q\rightarrow 0
of
B(\lambda)
, which is a genuine basis of
V_{q}(\lambda)
. Hence, for each
x\in B(\lambda)
, the products
E_{i}x,F_{i}x\in V_{q}(\lambda)
make sense, where
E_{i},F_{i}
,
i\in I
are Chevalley generators of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. Kashiwara
[
K90
]
defined operators
\widetilde{E}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{i}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\sqcup\{0\}
which are, roughly speaking, the limits of the actions of
E_{i},F_{i}
at
q\rightarrow 0
. These maps equip the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
with a combinatorial structure, called a crystal structure.

In
[
LP08
]
, Lenart and Postnikov defined operators
\widetilde{E}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{i}
on the set of admissible subsets, and proved that these operators together with the map
\operatorname{wt}
give rise to a structure of crystal isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. One feature of the crystal structure of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is the existence of the highest weight vector. Namely, there exists a special element
b_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
such that

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)=\{\widetilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{F}_{i_{l}}(b_{\lambda})\mid l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}\in I\}\setminus\{0\}.

Hence, there exists a special admissible subset
J_{\lambda}
such that the map
\widetilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{F}_{i_{l}}(J_{\lambda})\mapsto\widetilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{F}_{i_{l}}(b_{\lambda})
gives a weight preserving bijection between the set of admissible subsets and the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. In fact, it is an isomorphism of crystals.

Now, let us consider the case when
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
. It is well-known that the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is modeled by the set
\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
filled with letters in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Namely,
\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
is equipped with a crystal structure in a way such that it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Combining the discussion above, we obtain an isomorphism of crystals between the set of admissible subsets and
\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
. Then, it is natural to ask for an explicit description of this isomorphism.

The goal of this paper is to provide a simple and explicit formula describing such an isomorphism. In fact, our formula gives an isomorphism between the set of admissible subsets and the set of Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns of shape
\lambda
, the latter of which is in a natural one-to-one correspondence with
\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
.

The crucial point in our strategy is to extend the fixed reduced alcove path. Recall that in the alcove path model, one has to fix a reduced alcove path from
A_{\circ}
to
A_{\circ}-\lambda
, and consider its admissible subsets. In this paper, we fix a reduced alcove path
\Pi=(A_{\circ}=A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{u}=w_{\circ}A_{\circ}-\lambda)
from
A_{\circ}
to
w_{\circ}A_{\circ}-\lambda
, where
w_{\circ}
denotes the longest element of the Weyl group of
G
. After modifying the definition of admissible subsets, we prove that the set of admissible subsets of
\Pi
is equipped with a crystal structure isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

When
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
, to each admissible subset
J
of
\Pi
, we can associate a tuple
N(J)=(N_{i,j}(J))_{1\leq i<j\leq n}
of nonnegative integers. Here, we omit the precise definition of
N(J)
; see Subsection
4.2
instead. Then, our main result in this paper is the following:

Theorem
.

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
,
\lambda
be a dominant integral weight,
\Pi
a reduced alcove path from
A_{\circ}
to
w_{\circ}A_{\circ}-\lambda
. Let
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be the partition corresponding to
\lambda
. Then, the assignment
J\mapsto(\lambda_{i}-N_{i,j}(J))_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}
, where
N_{i,i}(J):=0
, gives rise to an isomorphism of crystals from the set of admissible subsets and the set of Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns of shape
\lambda
.

In fact, the crystal isomorphism between the alcove path model and the Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern model, or the semistandard tableau model was constructed in
[
LL15
]
in more generality. However, we do not use any results in
[
LL15
]
in this paper. Also, our formula is easier and more explicit; the isomorphism can be calculated just by counting the root multiplicities in a given admissible subset.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prepare basic notions concerning crystals. Especially, we recall the string data of Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns. Section 3 is devoted to reviewing Lenart-Postnikov’s alcove path model. In Section 4, we introduce the extended alcove path model, and prove the main theorem by comparing the string data of the admissible subsets with those of the Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns.

2.
Crystals

In this section, we recall basic notions of (abstract) crystals, especially the highest weight crystals, string data, and the Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns. For details, see e.g.,
[
BS17
]
. We also refer the readers to
[
BB05
]
or
[
H90
]
for basic knowledge about Coxeter groups.

2.1.
Finite root systems

Let
\Phi
be a finite root system in a Euclidean space
(E,(\cdot,\cdot))
,
\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
a set of simple roots,
\Phi^{+}
the corresponding set of positive roots. For
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote its coroot by
\alpha^{\vee}:=\frac{2}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha
. For
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
s_{\alpha}
the reflection with respect to
\alpha
, i.e.,

s_{\alpha}(v):=v-(v,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha\quad\text{ for }v\in E.

Let

\Lambda:=\{v\in E\mid(v,\alpha^{\vee})\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\}

be the weight lattice, and

\Lambda^{+}:=\{v\in\Lambda\mid(v,\alpha^{\vee})\geq 0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\}

the set of dominant integral weights.

For
\alpha\in\Phi
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the hyperplane

H_{\alpha,k}:=\{v\in E\mid(v,\alpha^{\vee})=k\},

and the affine reflection

s_{\alpha,k}:E\rightarrow E;\ v\mapsto v-((v,\alpha^{\vee})-k)\alpha=s_{\alpha}(v)+k\alpha.

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
t_{\lambda}:E\rightarrow E;\ v\mapsto v+\lambda
denote the translation by
\lambda
. Then, from the definition of
s_{\alpha,k}
, we see that
s_{\alpha,k}=t_{k\alpha}s_{\alpha}
.

Let
W
(resp.,
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
) be the subgroup of the affine transformation group of
E
generated by
\{s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
(resp.,
\{s_{\alpha,k}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
). It is the Weyl group (resp., affine Weyl group) associated to the coroot system
\Phi^{\vee}:=\{\alpha^{\vee}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}
. It is well-known that
W
(resp.,
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
) is generated by
S:=\{s_{i}:=s_{\alpha_{i}}\mid i\in I\}
(resp.,
S_{\mathrm{aff}}:=S\sqcup\{s_{0}:=s_{\theta,1}\}
, where
\theta
is such that
\theta^{\vee}
is the highest coroot). Moreover,
(W,S)
(resp.,
(W_{\mathrm{aff}},S_{\mathrm{aff}})
) forms a Coxeter system.

Example 2.1.1
.

Let
E=\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0\}
, where
\{\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,
\Phi:=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}
forms the root system of type
A_{n-1}
with simple roots
\Delta:=\{\alpha_{i}:=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
and positive roots
\Phi^{+}:=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
. The Weyl group
W
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
; the reflection
s_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}
corresponds to the transposition
(i,j)
. Each
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
is identified with a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
by

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}=(\lambda,\alpha_{i}),\quad\lambda_{n}=0.

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
,
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,

s_{\beta,l}(H_{\alpha,k})=H_{s_{\beta}(\alpha),k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})}.

Proof.

Let
v\in H_{\alpha,k}
. Then, we have

(s_{\beta,l}(v),s_{\beta}(\alpha)^{\vee})=(s_{\beta}(v)+l\beta,s_{\beta}(\alpha^{\vee}))=(v-l\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee}).

This implies that
s_{\beta,l}(H_{\alpha,k})\subset H_{s_{\beta}(\alpha),k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})}
. Replacing
(\alpha,k)
by
(s_{\beta}(\alpha),k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee}))
, we obtain

s_{\beta,l}(H_{s_{\beta}(\alpha),k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})})\subset H_{\alpha,k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})-l(\beta,s_{\beta}(\alpha^{\vee}))}=H_{\alpha,k}.

Hence, we conclude that
s_{\beta,l}(H_{\alpha,k})=H_{s_{\beta}(\alpha),k-l(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})}
.
∎

Let
w_{\circ}\in W
denote the longest element. Set
N:=\ell(w_{\circ})=|\Phi^{+}|
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

A reduced word (for
w_{\circ}
) is an
N
-tuple
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})\in I^{N}
such that
w_{\circ}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}
.

Definition 2.1.4
.

A reflection order (also known as a convex order) is a total order
\leq
on
\Phi^{+}
satisfying the following: If
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
is such that
\alpha<\beta
and
\alpha+\beta=\gamma
, then we have
\alpha<\gamma<\beta
.

To a reduced word
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
, we associate a sequence
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N})
of positive roots by

\beta_{1}:=\alpha_{i_{1}},\quad\beta_{j}:=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}(\alpha_{i_{j}})\text{ for }2\leq j\leq N.

It is well-known that
\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}\}=\Phi^{+}
, and the total order
<_{\mathbf{i}}
on
\Phi^{+}
given by
\beta_{1}<_{\mathbf{i}}\cdots<_{\mathbf{i}}\beta_{N}
is a reflection order. Moreover, this assignment gives a bijection between the set of reduced words and the set of reflection orders.

Example 2.1.5
.

Consider the case when our root system is of type
A_{2}
. There are only two reduced words

\mathbf{i}_{1}:=(1,2,1),\and\mathbf{i}_{2}:=(2,1,2),

and only two reflection orders

\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}<_{\mathbf{i}_{1}}\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3}<_{\mathbf{i}_{1}}\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3},\and\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}<_{\mathbf{i}_{2}}\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3}<_{\mathbf{i}_{2}}\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}.

Remark 2.1.6
.

Let
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
be a reduced word. Since the Weyl group for the root system
\Phi
is the same as that for
\Phi^{\vee}
, the word
\mathbf{i}
is also a reduced word for
\Phi^{\vee}
. Hence, the total order
\leq_{\mathbf{i}}^{\vee}
on
(\Phi^{\vee})^{+}:=\{\alpha^{\vee}\mid\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
, defined by the same way as the reflection order
\leq_{\mathbf{i}}
on
\Phi^{+}
, is a reflection order. Note that we have
\alpha^{\vee}\leq_{\mathbf{i}}^{\vee}\beta^{\vee}
if and only if
\alpha\leq_{\mathbf{i}}\beta
. Then, for each
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha<_{\mathbf{i}}\beta
and
\gamma^{\vee}=\alpha^{\vee}+\beta^{\vee}
, we have
\alpha<_{\mathbf{i}}\gamma<_{\mathbf{i}}\beta
.

2.2.
Crystals

Definition 2.2.1
.

A crystal is a set
B
equipped with maps
\operatorname{wt}:B\rightarrow\Lambda
,
\widetilde{E}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{i}:B\rightarrow B\sqcup\{0\}
(
0
is a formal symbol),
i\in I
satisfying the following:

(1)

For each
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
i\in I
, we have
\widetilde{F}_{i}(b)=b^{\prime}
if and only if
b=\widetilde{E}_{i}(b^{\prime})
.

(2)

For each
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
i\in I
, if
\widetilde{F}_{i}(b)=b^{\prime}
, then
\operatorname{wt}(b^{\prime})=\operatorname{wt}(b)-\alpha_{i}
.

(3)

For each
b\in B
and
i\in I
, we have
\varphi_{i}(b)=\varepsilon_{i}(b)+(\operatorname{wt}(b),\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
, where

\varphi_{i}(b):=\max\{k\geq 0\mid\widetilde{F}_{i}^{k}(b)\neq 0\},\quad\varepsilon_{i}(b):=\max\{k\geq 0\mid\widetilde{E}_{i}^{k}(b)\neq 0\}.

Definition 2.2.2
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be crystals. A morphism
\psi:B_{1}\rightarrow B_{2}
of crystals is a map
\psi:B_{1}\sqcup\{0\}\rightarrow B_{2}\sqcup\{0\}
satisfying the following:

(1)

For each
b\in B_{1}
and
i\in I
, if
\psi(b)\in B_{2}
, then we have
\operatorname{wt}(\psi(b))=\operatorname{wt}(b)
,
\varphi_{i}(\psi(b))=\varphi_{i}(b)
, and
\varepsilon_{i}(\psi(b))=\varepsilon_{i}(b)
.

(2)

\psi(0)=0
.

(3)

For each
b\in B_{1}
and
i\in I
, we have
\widetilde{F}_{i}(\psi(b))=\psi(\widetilde{F}_{i}(b))
and
\widetilde{E}_{i}(\psi(b))=\psi(\widetilde{E}_{i}(b))
.

A morphism
\psi
is said to be an isomorphism if it is a bijection and if
\psi^{-1}
is a morphism of crystals.

In some literature, what we just defined are called seminormal crystals or semiregular crystals, and strict morphisms of crystals.

To a crystal
B
, we associate a colored directed graph as follows. The vertex set is
B
. For
b,b^{\prime}\in B
, we put an arrow colored by
i\in I
from
b
to
b^{\prime}
if
b^{\prime}=\widetilde{F}_{i}(b)
. This graph is called the crystal graph of
B
.

The notion of crystals originates in the representation theory of complex semisimple Lie algebras (or, associated quantum groups). Given a finite-dimensional representation of the complex semisimple Lie algebra whose root system is isomorphic to our root system
\Phi
, one can obtain a crystal by extracting some information about its module structure. In particular, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, there exists a unique crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
coming from the irreducible highest weight module
V(\lambda)
of highest weight
\lambda
. One feature of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is the existence of the highest weight vector
b_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
; it satisfies
\operatorname{wt}(b_{\lambda})=\lambda
, and

\mathcal{B}(\lambda)=\{\widetilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{F}_{i_{l}}(b_{\lambda})\mid l\geq 0,\ i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}\in I\}\setminus\{0\}.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Let
B
be a crystal,
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})\in I^{N}
a reduced word. The
\mathbf{i}
-string datum
\operatorname{string}_{\mathbf{i}}(b)
of
b\in B
is an
N
-tuple of nonnegative integers given by

\operatorname{string}_{\mathbf{i}}(b):=(\varepsilon_{i_{1}}(b),\varepsilon_{i_{2}}(\widetilde{E}_{i_{1}}^{\max}(b)),\ldots,\varepsilon_{i_{N}}(\widetilde{E}_{i_{N-1}}^{\max}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{i_{1}}^{\max}(b))).

When

, it is known that the map

\widetilde{E}_{i_{N}}^{\max}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{i_{1}}^{\max}(b)=b_{\lambda}

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be crystals isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
,
\mathbf{i}
a reduced word. Suppose that there exists a bijection
\psi:B_{1}\rightarrow B_{2}
such that
\operatorname{string}_{\mathbf{i}}(\psi(b))=\operatorname{string}_{\mathbf{i}}(b)
for all
b\in B_{1}
. Then,
\psi
is an isomorphism of crystals.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
B_{1}=B_{2}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Then, the injectivity of
\operatorname{string}_{\mathbf{i}}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
implies that
\psi(b)=b
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Hence,
\psi
is the identity map on
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, which is an isomorphism of crystals. This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns

In this subsection, assume that our root system is of type
A_{n-1}
. In particular, we identify the dominant integral weight
\lambda
with the partition
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
as in Example
2.1.1
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern of shape
\lambda
is a tuple
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}
of nonnegative integers satisfying the following:

•

a_{i,i}=\lambda_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

•

a_{i+1,j}\leq a_{i,j}\leq a_{i,j-1}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

Let
\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
denote the set of Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns of shape
\lambda
.

\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
is in a natural bijection with the set
\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
of semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
filled with letters in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The bijection is given as follows. Let
T\in\operatorname{SST}(\lambda)
. We denote by
T(i,j)
the entry of the box in the
i
-th row and the
j
-th column. Then, the corresponding Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}
is given as follows; for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, the tuple
(a_{1,j},a_{2,j+1},\ldots,a_{n-j+1,n})
is the partition representing the shape of the tableau obtained from
T
by deleting the boxes whose entries are greater than
n-j+1
. In other words,

a_{i,j}=\sharp\{(k,l)\mid k=i\and T(k,l)\leq n-j+i\}.

Via this bijection,
\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
is equipped with a crystal structure isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

3.1.
Alcove paths and admissible subsets

Definition 3.1.1
.

An alcove is a connected component of
E\setminus\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}H_{\alpha,k}
. The fundamental alcove
A_{\circ}
is the alcove defined by

A_{\circ}=\{v\in E\mid 0<(v,\alpha^{\vee})<1\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}.

Two alcoves
A,B
are said to be adjacent if
A\neq B
and if their closures have a common facet (face of codimension
1
). Such a common facet
F
is unique. In general, if
F
is a facet of an alcove, then there exist unique
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
F\subset H_{\beta,l}
. In this case, we set
s_{F}:=s_{\beta,l}
.

Definition 3.1.2
.

An alcove path is a sequence
\Pi:=(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s})
of alcoves such that
A_{i-1}
and
A_{i}
are adjacent for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. An alcove path is said to be reduced if it has minimal length among all alcove paths from
A_{0}
to
A_{s}
. The sequence of positive roots associated to
\Pi
is
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s})
, where
\beta_{i}
is the positive root such that the common facet
F_{i}
of
A_{i-1}
and
A_{i}
lies in
H_{\beta_{i},l_{i}}
for some
l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.1.3

(
[
LP07
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Let
v\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
. Then, there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the set of reduced expressions of
v
and the set of reduced alcove paths from
A_{\circ}
to
vA_{\circ}
.

From now on, we fix
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
. Let
\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
denote the set of reduced alcove paths from
A_{\circ}
to
A_{\circ}-\lambda
. Let
\Pi=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{s})\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
with
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s})
.

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Then, we have

l_{i}=-\sharp\{j<i\mid\beta_{j}=\beta_{i}\}.

Proof.

Since
\Pi
is reduced, a hyperplane of the form
H_{\beta_{i},k}
lies between
A_{0}=A_{\circ}
and
A_{i}
if and only if
l_{i}\leq k\leq 0
. Then, for each such
k
, there is a unique
j\leq i
such that
\beta_{j}=\beta_{i}
and
l_{j}=k
. Thus, the assertion follows.
∎

The sequence of positive roots
\Gamma(\Pi)
is characterized by the following conditions:

Proposition 3.1.5

(
[
LP08
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s}\in\Phi^{+}
. Then, there exists
\Pi\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
such that
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s})
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

•

For each
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, we have
\sharp\{i\mid\beta_{i}=\beta\}=(\lambda,\beta^{\vee})
,

•

For each
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\gamma^{\vee}=\alpha^{\vee}+\beta^{\vee}
, consider the subsequence
(\beta_{i_{1}},\ldots,\beta_{i_{k}})
of
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s})
consisting of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Then,
\beta_{i_{m}}\in\{\alpha,\beta\}
if
m
is odd, while
\beta_{i_{m}}=\gamma
if
m
is even.

Remark 3.1.6
.

In the second condition, we have
k=(\lambda,\alpha^{\vee})+(\lambda,\beta^{\vee})+(\lambda,\gamma^{\vee})=2(\lambda,\gamma^{\vee})
. In particular,
\beta_{i_{k}}=\gamma
.

Definition 3.1.7
.

Let
\Pi\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
with
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s})
. An admissible subset associated to
\Pi
is a subset
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}
and that there exists a saturated chain

e\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}s_{\beta_{j_{2}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}s_{\beta_{j_{2}}}\cdots s_{\beta_{j_{t}}}

in the Bruhat graph of
W
, i.e., for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, we have
\ell(s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}\cdots s_{\beta_{j_{k}}})=k
. We understand that the empty set is an admissible subset. Let
\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
denote the set of admissible subsets associated to
\Pi
.

Remark 3.1.8
.

When we consider a subset
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,s\}
, we always assume that
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}
.

Example 3.1.9
.

Suppose that our root system is of type
A_{2}
, and
\lambda=(2,1,0)
. Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Pi_{1}=(A_{\circ},s_{1}A_{\circ},s_{1}s_{2}A_{\circ},s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}A_{\circ},s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}s_{0}A_{\circ}),\\

Then, these are elements of

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma(\Pi_{1})=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}),\\

The admissible subsets are the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{1})=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{3\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{3,4\},\{1,2,3\}\},\\
&\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{2})=\{\emptyset,\{3\},\{1\},\{1,3\},\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\{1,4\},\{1,2,3\}\}.\end{split}

Let

,

3.2.
Galleries

For our purposes, it is convenient to rewrite the admissible subsets in terms of galleries, which we recall now.

Definition 3.2.1
.

A gallery is a sequence
\gamma=(A_{0},F_{1},A_{1},F_{2},A_{2},\ldots,F_{s},A_{s},\mu)
satisfying the following:

•

A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
are alcoves.

•

F_{i}
is a common facet of
A_{i-1}
and
A_{i}
.

•

\mu\in\Lambda
is a vertex of (the closure of)
A_{s}
.

Given a gallery
\gamma
, set

J(\gamma):=\{i\mid A_{i-1}=A_{i}\}.

Example 3.2.2
.

Let
\Pi=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{s})
be an alcove path. Let
F_{i}
denote the unique common facet of
A_{i-1}
and
A_{i}
. Then, for each vertex
\mu\in\Lambda
of
A_{s}
, the sequence
\gamma(\Pi;\mu):=(A_{0},F_{1},A_{1},\ldots,F_{s},A_{s},\mu)
is a gallery. In this case, we have
J(\gamma(\Pi;\mu))=\emptyset
.

Definition 3.2.3
.

Let
\gamma=(A_{0},F_{1},A_{1},\ldots,F_{s},A_{s},\mu)
be a gallery, and
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.

(1)

Let
\phi_{j}(\gamma)=(A^{\prime}_{0},F^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{s},A^{\prime}_{s},\mu^{\prime})
be the gallery defined by

(a)

A^{\prime}_{i}:=\begin{cases}A_{i}\quad&\text{ if }0\leq i<j,\\
s_{F_{j}}(A_{i})\quad&\text{ if }j\leq i\leq s.\end{cases}

(b)

F^{\prime}_{i}:=\begin{cases}F_{i}\quad&\text{ if }1\leq i<j,\\
s_{F_{j}}(F_{i})\quad&\text{ if }j\leq i\leq s.\end{cases}

(c)

\mu^{\prime}:=s_{F_{j}}(\mu)
.

(2)

For a subset
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,s\}
with
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{t}
, set

\phi_{J}(\gamma):=\phi_{j_{1}}\cdots\phi_{j_{t}}(\gamma).

Here, we understand that
\phi_{J}(\gamma)=\gamma
if
J=\emptyset
.

For each alcove path
\Pi=(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s})
and a vertex
\mu
of
A_{s}
, let
G(\Pi;\mu)
denote the set of galleries of the form
\phi_{J}(\gamma(\Pi;\mu))
,
J\subset\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Since
J(\phi_{J}(\gamma(\Pi;\mu)))=J
, we may identify
\phi_{J}(\gamma(\Pi;\mu))
with
J
.

3.3.
Crystal structure

Throughout this subsection, we fix an alcove path
\Pi=(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s})
and a vertex
\mu
of
A_{s}
. We identify
\phi_{J}(\gamma(\Pi;\mu))\in G(\Pi;\mu)
with
J\subset\{1,\ldots,s\}

\operatorname{wt}:G(\Pi;\mu)\rightarrow\Lambda

\widetilde{E}_{p},\widetilde{F}_{p}:G(\Pi;\mu)\rightarrow G(\Pi;\mu)\sqcup\{0\}

and

Let

•

•

•

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{F}&:=\min\{i\in I(J,p)\mid l^{J}_{i}=M(J,p)\},\\

Also, we define

\widetilde{E}_{p}(J):=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{ if }M(J,p)=(\mu^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee}),\\
(J\setminus\{k_{E}\})\cup\{m_{E}\}\quad&\text{ if }M(J,p)<(\mu^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee})\and k_{E}\neq k^{\prime},\\

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{E}&:=\max\{i\in I(J,p)\mid l^{J}_{i}=M(J,p)\},\\
m_{E}&:=\min(I(J,p)\cap\{k_{E}+1,\ldots,s\}).\end{split}

Note that by definition, it always holds that

.

3.4.
Yang-Baxter moves

Let
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
. As we have seen above, both
\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{1})
and
\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{2})
are equipped with crystal structures isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. In particular, there exists a unique isomorphism
\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{2})
of crystals. Such an isomorphism can be realized as a sequence of Yang-Baxter moves, which we briefly explain now.

Recall from Lemma
3.1.3
that each
\Pi\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
corresponds to a reduced expression of
v_{\lambda}
, where
v_{\lambda}\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
is such that
v_{\lambda}A_{\circ}=A_{\circ}-\lambda
. By Matsumoto’s theorem, any two reduced expressions of
v_{\lambda}
can be transformed from one into the other by a sequence of braid moves. The Yang-Baxter moves are the translations of the braid moves in the language of alcove paths.

A sequence of Yang-Baxter moves which transforms
\Pi_{1}
into
\Pi_{2}
induces an isomorphism
Y:\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{2})
of crystals. For the precise definition of this isomorphism, see
[
L07
, Section 4]
.

4.
Extended alcove path model

4.1.
Extended alcove path model

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of extended alcove path model, which also gives a combinatorial realization of the highest weight crystals.

Let
\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
denote the set of reduced alcove paths from
A_{\circ}
to
w_{\circ}A_{\circ}-\lambda
. Let
\Pi=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{u})\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
with
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{u})
. Let
l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that the common facet of
A_{i-1}
and
A_{i}
is contained in the hyperplane
H_{\beta_{i},l_{i}}
. As in the ordinary alcove path model, we have

(1)

\displaystyle l_{i}=-\sharp\{j<i\mid\beta_{j}=\beta_{i}\}

for all
i=1,\ldots,u
.

By the arguments in
[
LP08
, Propositions 10.2–10.3]
, the sequences
\Gamma(\Pi)
of positive roots associated to reduced alcove paths
\Pi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
are characterized as follows (compare with Proposition
3.1.5
).

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{u}\in\Phi^{+}
. Then, there exists
\Pi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
such that
\Gamma(\Pi)=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{u})
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

•

For each
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, we have
\sharp\{i\mid\beta_{i}=\beta\}=(\lambda,\beta^{\vee})+1
.

•

For each
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\gamma^{\vee}=\alpha^{\vee}+\beta^{\vee}
, consider the subsequence
(\beta_{i_{1}},\ldots,\beta_{i_{k}})
of
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{u})
consisting of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Then,
\beta_{i_{m}}\in\{\alpha,\beta\}
if
m
is odd, while
\beta_{i_{m}}=\gamma
if
m
is even.

Remark 4.1.2
.

In the second condition, we have
k=((\lambda,\alpha^{\vee})+1)+((\lambda,\beta^{\vee})+1)+((\lambda,\gamma^{\vee})+1)=2(\lambda,\gamma^{\vee})+3
. In particular,
\beta_{i_{k}}\in\{\alpha,\beta\}
.

Definition 4.1.3
.

An admissible subset associated to
\Pi=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{u})\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
is a subset
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,u\}
such that
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{N}
and that there exists a saturated chain

e\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}s_{\beta_{j_{2}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j_{1}}}s_{\beta_{j_{2}}}\cdots s_{\beta_{j_{N}}}=w_{\circ}

in the Bruhat graph of
W
. Let
\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
denote the set of admissible subsets associated to
\Pi
. For
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\}\in\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
and
1\leq k<l\leq N
, we define
\overline{w}_{k,l}(J)
,
w_{k,l}(J)
,
\overline{w}(J)
, and
w(J)
by the same way as in the ordinary alcove paths model.

Remark 4.1.4
.

Let
\Pi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
,
J\in\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
. Opposed to the ordinary alcove path model, the size of
J
and the Weyl group element
\overline{w}(J)
is independent of
J
; we have
|J|=N
and
\overline{w}(J)=w_{\circ}
. However,
w(J)
depends on
J
; there exists
\nu=\nu(J)\in\Lambda
such that
w(J)=t_{\nu}w_{\circ}
.

Lemma 4.1.6

Lemma 4.1.7

Let
\Pi=(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s})\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{\Pi}=(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{s},A_{s+1},\ldots,A_{s+N})\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)
be as before. Let

. By

, there exists a unique saturated chain

such that

\Phi:\mathcal{A}(\Pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi});\ \{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}\mapsto\{j_{1},\ldots j_{t},s+i_{1},s+i_{2}\ldots,s+i_{N-t}\}.

The inverse map is given by
\tilde{J}\mapsto\tilde{J}\cap\{1,\ldots,s\}

Proposition 4.1.8
.

Let
\Pi,\widetilde{\Pi}
be as above. Then, the bijection
\Phi:\mathcal{A}(\Pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi})
commutes with
\operatorname{wt},\widetilde{E}_{p},\widetilde{F}_{p}
,
p\in I
; here we understand
\Phi(0)=0
. Consequently,
\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi})
equipped with the maps
\operatorname{wt},\widetilde{E}_{p},\widetilde{F}_{p}
,
p\in I
is a crystal isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, and
\Phi:\mathcal{A}(\Pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi})
is an isomorphism of crystals.

Proof.

Let
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t}\}\in\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
,
p\in I
. We use the notation above. In particular, we write
\Phi(J)=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{t},s+i_{1},s+i_{2},\ldots,s+i_{N-t}\}
. In terms of galleries, let us write

J=(A^{J}_{0},F^{J}_{1},A^{J}_{1},\ldots,F^{J}_{s},A^{J}_{s},\lambda^{J}).

Then,
\Phi(J)
is of the form

(A^{J}_{0},F^{J}_{1},A^{J}_{1},\ldots,F^{J}_{s},A^{J}_{s},F^{J}_{s+1},A^{J}_{s+1},\ldots,F^{J}_{s+N},A^{J}_{s+N},\mu^{J}).

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s+N\}
, let
\beta^{J}_{i}\in\Phi^{+}
and
l^{J}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
F_{i}^{J}\subset H_{\beta^{J}_{i},l^{J}_{i}}
.

First, we compute
\operatorname{wt}(\Phi(J))
. By definition, we have

\operatorname{wt}(\Phi(J))=-w(\Phi(J))(-\lambda)=-w(J)s_{F_{s+i_{1}}}\cdots s_{F_{s+i_{N-t}}}(-\lambda).

Recall from Lemma
4.1.7
that the facets
F_{s+i_{1}},\ldots,F_{s+i_{N-t}}
contain
-\lambda
. Therefore, the corresponding affine reflections stabilize
-\lambda
. Hence, we obtain

\operatorname{wt}(\Phi(J))=-w(J)(-\lambda)=\operatorname{wt}(J),

as desired.

Next, we compute
\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))
. Obviously,
I(\Phi(J),p)=I(J,p)\sqcup I^{\prime}
for some subset
I^{\prime}
of
\{s+1,\ldots,s+N\}
. Since the facets
F_{s+k}
,

contain
-\lambda
, the facets
F^{J}_{s+k}
contain
\mu^{J}
. Hence, for each

, we have

l^{J}_{i^{\prime}}=(\mu^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee})=(\lambda^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee}).

Here, we used
\mu^{J}=-\operatorname{wt}(\Phi(J))=-\operatorname{wt}(J)=\lambda^{J}
. Therefore, we obtain

L(\Phi(J),p)=L(J,p),\and M(\Phi(J),p)=M(J,p).

Now, we have three possibilities:

(1)

M(\Phi(J),p)=M(J,p)=0
. In this case, we have

\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))=0,\quad\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)=0,

and hence
\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))=\Phi(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J))
.

(2)

M(\Phi(J),p)=M(J,p)<0
and
\{i\in I(J,p)\mid l^{J}_{i}=M(J,p)\}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, we have
\{i\in I(\Phi(J),p)\mid l^{J}_{i}=M(\Phi(J),p)\}\neq\emptyset
, and therefore,
m_{F}:=m_{F}(\Phi(J),p)=m_{F}(J,p)

. Hence,

\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))=(\Phi(J)\setminus\{m_{F}\})\sqcup\{k_{F}\}=\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)\sqcup\{s+i_{1},\ldots,s+i_{N-t}\}.

Since
\overline{w}(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J))=\overline{w}(J)
(by Proposition
3.3.3
(
6
)), it follows that
\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)\sqcup\{s+i_{1},\ldots,s+i_{N-t}\}=\Phi(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J))
. This shows
\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))=\Phi(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J))
.

(3)

M(\Phi(J),p)=M(J,p)<0
and
\{i\in I(J,p)\mid l^{J}_{i}=M(J,p)\}=\emptyset
. In this case, we have
M(J,p)=(\lambda^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee})
, and
\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)=J\sqcup\{k^{\prime}\}
, where
k^{\prime}:=\max I(J,p)\in I(J,p)\cap\{j_{t}+1,j_{t}+2,\ldots,s\}
.

Let us show that
I^{\prime}
is not empty. Recall that we have a saturated chain

\overline{w}(J)\rightarrow\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{N-t}}}=w_{\circ}.

By Proposition
3.3.3
(
7
), we have
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)>\overline{w}(J)
. Since
s_{p}w_{\circ}<w_{\circ}
, we can take the minimal
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-t\}
such that
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k}}}<\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k}}}
. Then, applying (the contraposition of)
[
BB05
, Corollary 2.2.8 (i)]
to
s=s_{p}
,
t=s_{\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k-1}}}(\gamma_{i_{k}})}
, and
w=\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k-1}}}
, we obtain
\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k-1}}}(\gamma_{i_{k}})=\alpha_{p}
. This implies that
i_{k}\in I^{\prime}
.

Set
m^{\prime}:=\min I^{\prime}
. Since
l^{J}_{i^{\prime}}=(\lambda^{J},\alpha_{p}^{\vee})=M(\Phi(J),p)
for all
i^{\prime}\in I^{\prime}
, we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{F}&:=\max(I(\Phi(J),p)\cap\{1,\ldots,m^{\prime}-1\})\\
&=\max I(J,p)=k^{\prime},\end{split}

and hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))&=(\Phi(J)\setminus\{m^{\prime}\})\sqcup\{k^{\prime}\}\\
&=\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)\sqcup(\{s+i_{1},\ldots,s+i_{N-t}\}\setminus\{m^{\prime}\}).\end{split}

Let us write
\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)=\{j^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<j^{\prime}_{t}<j^{\prime}_{t+1}\}
and
m^{\prime}=s+i_{m}
for some
1\leq m\leq N-t
. Then,

e\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j^{\prime}_{1}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{\beta_{j^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdots s_{\beta_{j^{\prime}_{t+1}}}=\overline{w}(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J))=s_{p}\overline{w}(J)

e
to
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)
. Now, we show that

s_{p}\overline{w}(J)\rightarrow s_{p}\overline{w}_{J}s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{p}\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{m-1}}}=\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{m}}}

is a saturated chain from
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)
to
\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{m}}}
. Let

and set
w_{k}:=\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k-1}}}
. Assume that
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{p}\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{k-1}}}=s_{p}w_{k}

w_{k}\rightarrow w_{k}s_{\gamma_{i_{k}}}\and w_{k}\rightarrow s_{p}w_{k}

[
BB05
, Corollary 2.2.8 (i)]
again, both

is saturated. Then, by induction on

, one can prove the claim.

This far, we have obtained saturated chains from

to
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)
, from
s_{p}\overline{w}(J)
to
\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{m}}}
, and from
\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{m}}}
to
\overline{w}(J)s_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{i_{N-t}}}=w_{\circ}
. Concatenating these chains, we obtain a saturated chain from

to
w_{\circ}
, which implies

\widetilde{F}_{p}(\Phi(J))=\Phi(\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)).

The assertion concerning
\widetilde{E}_{p}
is proved similarly.

Corollary 4.1.9
.

Let
\Pi\in\operatorname{AP}(\lambda)
,
J\in\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi})
,
p\in I
. If
\widetilde{F}_{p}(J)\neq 0
(resp.,

Let
\Pi=(A_{0},\ldots,A_{u})\in\widetilde{\operatorname{AP}}(\lambda)

\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
admits a crystal structure isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)

\mathcal{A}(\Pi)
with
\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime\prime})
for a certain alcove path

Lemma 4.1.10

with

v(\alpha,l):=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}}(-l,c_{1},\ldots,c_{|I|}),

is such that
\alpha^{\vee}=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}

LP08

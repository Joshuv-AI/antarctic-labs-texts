Seminar on Quillen’s proof of the Quillen-Suslin Theorem
Warren Dicks

Abstract




Let
K
be a field, and
n
be a positive integer.
[
Serre 1955
, p. 243]

remarked “On ignore s’il existe des
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
-modules projectifs de type fini qui ne soient
pas libres.”.
Motivated by this remark,
[
Quillen 1976
]
and
[
Suslin 1976
]
independently
showed that every finitely generated,
projective
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
-module is free, a
result that is now known as
the Quillen-Suslin theorem
.

[
Lam 2006
]
gave an extensive collection of proofs and related results that
were available 30 years later.

We construct an idempotent-matrix-based version of Quillen’s proof
by translating most of Paul Roberts’
geometry-free proof of Horrocks’ theorem and translating part of
Moshe Roitman’s proof of Quillen’s extension theorem.
We described all of this in the Bedford College Ring Theory Study Group in the autumn term of 1977,
and gave the
participants copies of the three-page handout
[
Dicks 1977
]
later
cited by
[
Lam 2006
, pp.
\mkern 1.0mu160,\mkern 0.0mu201
]
.
The present write-up includes minor changes and
three pages of introductory material.

1 Definitions and Notation
.

(i).
As Bourbaki intended, we let

\mathbb{N}
denote the set of finite cardinals; that is,
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,,\ldots\}
.

(ii).
By a
ring

R
, we mean an associative ring with
1
.
A left
R
-module
M
is said to be
R
-free
(resp.
R
-projective
,
resp.
finitely
R
-generated
) if
M
is a free (resp. projective, resp. finitely generated)
left
R
-module. For any subring
R_{0}
of
R
, if

M\underset{R}{\simeq}R\otimes_{R_{0}}M_{0}

for some left
R_{0}
-module
M_{0}
,
then, for any ideal
J
of
R
, we have

(R/J)\otimes_{R}M\underset{R/J}{\simeq}(R/J)\otimes_{R_{0}}M_{0}.

If, moreover, the natural map
R_{0}\to R/J
is bijective, then

R_{0}\otimes_{R}M\underset{R_{0}}{\simeq}M_{0}
; here,

M_{0}
is uniquely determined by
M
up to
R_{0}
-isomorphism, and,
if
M
is a finitely generated, projective left
R
-module,
then
M_{0}
is a finitely generated, projective left
R_{0}
-module.

(iii).
Whenever
R
is a commutative ring, the following will apply:
R[x]
denotes
the polynomial ring over
R
in one central variable denoted
x
;
R(x)
denotes the
localization of
R[x]
at the set of all monic polynomials in
R[x]
;

\operatorname{Max}(R)
denotes the set of all
maximal proper ideals of
R
; and, for each
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(R)
,

R_{\mathfrak{m}}
denotes
the localization of
R
at
R{-}\mathfrak{m}
, the complement of
\mathfrak{m}
in
R
.
Also, we shall say the following:
R
is a
domain
if
R
is nonzero and
R

has no nonzero zerodivisors;
R
is a
Dedekind domain
if
R
is a domain and,
for each
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(R)
, the localization
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a PID;
and
R
is
local
if
R
has a unique maximal proper ideal.
∎

2 Review
.

Let
R
be a ring.

(i).
Let
m,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We write
\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
to
denote the set of all
m\times n
matrices over
R
.
We give
\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
the standard ring structure.
We set
R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}:=\hbox{}^{1}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
, a free left
R
-module.
For each
A\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
, right multiplication by
A
gives an
R
-linear map

R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}\overset{A\,\,}{\to}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
, whose image will be denoted
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}A
.
Conversely, every
R
-linear map
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}\overset{\alpha}{\to}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
is given by
right multiplication by
a unique
A\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
; we shall view
A
and
\alpha
as
interchangeable. ∎

(ii).
Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
E\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}

such that
E\mkern 1.0mu^{2}=E
; we say that
E
is
idempotent
. We now show that

R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
is a finitely generated, projective left
R
-module.

It suffices to show that
R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}=R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E\oplus R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}(I_{n}{-}E)
as left
R
-modules.
It is clear that
R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}=R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E+R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}(I_{n}{-}E)
, since each
A\in R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
equals

A{\cdot}E+A{\cdot}(I_{n}{-}E)
.
To see that
R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E\cap R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}(I_{n}{-}E)=\{0\}
,
notice that this intersection is annihilated by right multiplication by
I_{n}{-}E
and by
right multiplication by
E
; hence it is annihilated by right multiplication by
E+(I_{n}{-}E)=I_{n}
.
The assertion is now proved. ∎

(iii).
Let
P
be a finitely generated, projective left
R
-module; thus, there
exist an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a left
R
-module
Q
such that

R^{n}\underset{R}{\simeq}P{\oplus}Q
.
We now show that there exists an idempotent
E\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}

such that
P\underset{R}{\simeq}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
.

We have a surjective
R
-linear map

\alpha{:\,}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}\simeq P{\oplus}Q\hskip-2.0pt\to\mkern-13.0mu\to P
and
an injective
R
-linear map
\beta{:\,}P\hookrightarrow P{\oplus}Q\simeq R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
such that

\beta{\cdot}\alpha=1_{P}
, where composition is to be read from left to right, and maps
are to be applied on the right of their arguments. We view the
R
-linear map

\alpha{\cdot}\beta{:\,}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}\to R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}

as an element
E
of
\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
.
Then
E^{2}=\alpha{\cdot}\beta{\cdot}\alpha{\cdot}\beta=\alpha{\cdot}1_{P}{\cdot}\beta=\alpha{\cdot}\beta=E
; thus,

E
is idempotent. Also,
P=(R\mkern 2.0mu^{n})\alpha\underset{R}{\simeq}((R\mkern 2.0mu^{n})\alpha)\beta=R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
.∎

(iv).
For idempotent matrices
E
,
F
over
R
,

we write
E\underset{R}{\sim}F
to mean that
there exist matrices
A
,
B
over
R
such that
A{\cdot}B=E
and
B{\cdot}A=F
.

Let
m
and
n
be elements of
{\mathbb{N}}
,
E
an idempotent element of

\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{m}
,
and
F
an idempotent element of
\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
.
We now show that
E\underset{R}{\sim}F
if and only if

R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\underset{R}{\simeq}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
.

Consider first the situation where
E\underset{R}{\sim}F
. Here, there exist

A\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}

and
B\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{m}
such that
A{\cdot}B=E
and
B{\cdot}A=F
.
One fact that we shall use repeatedly throughout these notes is that

E{\cdot}A=A{\cdot}F
here, since both sides equal
A{\cdot}B{\cdot}A
.
Now
A
may be viewed as the
R
-linear map
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}\to R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
given by right
multiplication by
A
. The
R
-submodule
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E
of
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}

is carried by
A
to
(R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E){\cdot}A=(R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}A){\cdot}F\subseteq R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
, giving an
R
-linear map
\alpha{:\,}R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\to R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
.
Similarly,
B
gives an
R
-linear map
\beta{:\,}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F\to R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E
. The total
effect of applying
\alpha
and then
\beta
to
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E
is to right multiply by
A{\cdot}B=E
,
which fixes all elements of
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E
. Similarly
\beta{\cdot}\alpha
acts as the identity
endomorphism of
R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
. Hence,

R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\underset{R}{\simeq}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
.

Finally, suppose that we have an
R
-linear isomorphism

R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\overset{\alpha\,}{\to}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F
.
The composite
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}\xrightarrow{E}R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\xrightarrow{\alpha}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F\subseteq R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
acts as right multiplication by a unique

A\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
. The composite

R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}\xrightarrow{F}R\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}F\xrightarrow{\alpha^{-1}}R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\subseteq R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}
acts as right multiplication by a unique

B\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR\mkern 2.0mu^{m}
. The composite
A{\cdot}B

equals the composite
R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}\xrightarrow{E}R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}{\cdot}E\subseteq R\mkern 2.0mu^{m}
, which
is right multiplication by
E
; hence,
A{\cdot}B=E
. Similarly
B{\cdot}A=F
.
Thus,
E\underset{R}{\sim}F
. ∎

(v).
It is now clear that
\underset{R}{\sim}
is an equivalence relation on the set of all
idempotent (square) matrices over
R
. Also, the set of equivalence classes is in bijective correspondence
with the collection of all isomorphism classes of all finitely generated, projective left
R
-modules.
∎
\Box

3 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a local commutative ring. Let

\mathfrak{m}
denote the maximal proper ideal of
R
. Set
\overline{R}:=R/\mathfrak{m}
, the
residue field, and let
R\to\overline{R}
,
r\mapsto\overline{r}
, denote the quotient map.
We extend the overline notation to polynomials and matrices.
We identify
R(x)/(\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x))
with
\overline{R}(x)
,
which is the field of fractions of
\overline{R}[x]
.

(i)
. Each element of
R(x)
equals
\frac{f}{g}

for some
f,\,g\in R[x]
with
g
monic. The

polynomial division algorithm says that,
for any
f,\,g\in R[x]
with
g
monic, there exist
unique
q,\,r\in R[x]
such that

f=g{\cdot}\,q+r
and
\operatorname{deg}r<\operatorname{deg}g
,
where
\operatorname{deg}0:=-\infty
;
here,
q

is uniquely determined by
\frac{f}{g}
, and we call

q
the
polynomial part of

\frac{f}{g}
, denoted
[\frac{f}{g}]
. The map

R(x)\to R[x]
,

\frac{f}{g}\mapsto[\frac{f}{g}]
, is an
R
-linear retraction. We denote the kernel by

R(x)_{\circ}:=\{\frac{f}{g}\in R(x):\operatorname{deg}f<\operatorname{deg}g\}
,
and obtain an
R
-module decomposition
R(x)=R[x]\oplus R(x)_{\circ}
. ∎

(ii)
. For
r\in R
, if
\overline{r}\neq\overline{0}
, then
r\notin\mathfrak{m}
, hence

R\mkern 2.0mur
is not a proper ideal of
R
, and hence
r
is invertible in
R
. ∎

(iii)
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We now show that, for each invertible

A\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0mu\overline{R}(x)\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
, there exists an invertible

B\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR(x)\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
such that
\overline{B}=A
; we say that
the invertible matrix over
\overline{R}(x)

lifts back
to some invertible matrix over
R(x)
.

By
(ii)
above, each invertible element of the field
\overline{R}
lifts back to some invertible
element of
R
. It then follows that each invertible element of the field
\overline{R}(x)

lifts back to some invertible element of
R(x)
.
It then follows that each invertible elementary matrix over
\overline{R}(x)

lifts back to some invertible elementary matrix over
R(x)
.
Over the field
\overline{R}(x)
, each invertible matrix is a product of
invertible elementary matrices, and, hence, lifts back to some invertible matrix over
R(x)
. ∎

(iv)
. Let
W
be an
R
-module, and
W^{\prime}
be an
R
-submodule of
W
such that, firstly,

W/W^{\prime}
is finitely
R
-generated, and, second,

\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}W{+}W^{\prime}=W
.
We shall now show that
W^{\prime}=W
.
(This is the local commutative case of one form of Nakayama’s Lemma.)

Since
W/W^{\prime}
is finitely
R
-generated,
there exists a smallest
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that there exist
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W

such that
R{\cdot}w_{1}{+}\cdots{+}R{\cdot}w_{n}+W^{\prime}=W
. We want to show that
n=0
.
Suppose that
n\geqslant 1
; it suffices to obtain a contradiction.
Set
W^{\prime\prime}:=R{\cdot}w_{2}{+}\cdots{+}R{\cdot}w_{n}{+}W^{\prime}
.
Then

W=R{\cdot}w_{1}{+}W^{\prime\prime}
. By the
minimality of
n
,
W^{\prime\prime}\neq W
; hence,
w_{1}\not\in W^{\prime\prime}
.
Now
w_{1}\in W=\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}W{+}W^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}(R{\cdot}w_{1}{+}W^{\prime\prime}){+}W^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}w_{1}{+}W^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence, there exists some
r\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
w_{1}\in r{\cdot}w_{1}{+}W^{\prime\prime}
, and than

(1{-}r){\cdot}w_{1}\in W^{\prime\prime}
.
By
(ii)
above,
1{-}r
is invertible in
R
, since

\overline{1{-}r}=\overline{1}{-}\overline{0}\neq\overline{0}
.
Hence,
w_{1}\in(1{-}r)^{-1}{\cdot}W^{\prime\prime}\subseteq W^{\prime\prime}
,
which contradicts
w_{1}\not\in W^{\prime\prime}
. ∎

(v)
. We now give an elementary proof that if the local commutative ring
R
is a PID,
then
R(x)
is a PID. (This is the local case of
[
Quillen 1976
, Lemma 2]
.)

If
R
is a field, then
R(x)
is the field of fractions of
R[x]
, which is clearly a PID;
thus, we may assume that the local PID
R
is not a field. Here
\mathfrak{m}=R{\cdot}p
for some nonzero
p\in R
.
Then
p
is irreducible in
R
, and each nonzero element of
R
has a unique expression
as
u{\cdot}p^{n}
where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
u\in R{-}R{\cdot}p
.
Each nonzero element of
R(x)
has a unique expression as
f{\cdot}p^{n}

where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in R(x){-}R(x){\cdot}p
.
We identified
R(x)/(R(x){\cdot}p)=\overline{R}(x)
, the field of fractions
of
\overline{R}[x]
; thus,
R(x){\cdot}p\in\operatorname{Max}(R(x))
.

Let
J
be an arbitrary ideal of
R(x)
. We wish to show that
J
is a principal ideal of
R(x)
.
We may assume that
J\neq\{0\}
.
Let
K
denote the field of fractions of
R
. Then
R(x)
lies in the field of fractions
K(x)
of

K[x]
. The subring
R(x){\cdot}K
of
K(x)
is a PID, since it
is the localization of the PID
K[x]
at the set of monic polynomials in
R[x]
.
As
J{\cdot}K
is a nonzero ideal of the PID
R(x){\cdot}K
, there exists some nonzero

f\in J{\cdot}K
such that
J{\cdot}K=R(x){\cdot}K{\cdot}f
. We may arrange that

f\in J{-}J{\cdot}p
.
It suffices to show that
J=R(x){\cdot}f
. It is clear that
R(x){\cdot}K{\cdot}f\supseteq J\supseteq R(x){\cdot}f
.
Consider any nonzero element of
J
and express it as
g{\cdot}f
for some
g\in R(x){\cdot}K
;
it suffices to show that
g\in R(x)
. We may write
g=p^{-n}{\cdot}g^{\prime}
, where
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}

and
g^{\prime}\in R(x){-}R(x){\cdot}p
; it suffices to show that
-n\geqslant 0
. Suppose that

n\geqslant 1
; it suffices to obtain a contradiction.
Since
R(x){\cdot}p\in\operatorname{Max}(R(x))
,
we see that
R(x){\cdot}p+R(x){\cdot}g^{\prime}=R(x)
. Thus,
p
and (hence)
p^{n}
become invertible in

R(x)/(R(x){\cdot}g^{\prime})
.
As
g^{\prime}{\cdot}f=p^{n}{\cdot}g{\cdot}f\in p^{n}{\cdot}J
, we see that
g^{\prime}
,
p^{n}
,

and (hence)
1
all lie in the ideal

\{h\in R(x):h{\cdot}f\in p^{n}{\cdot}J\}
of
R(x)
. Now

1{\cdot}f\in p^{n}{\cdot}J
, and this contradicts

f\not\in J{\cdot}p
. ∎
\Box

By
[
Quillen 1976
, Theorem 3]
, if
R
is a commutative ring and
P
is a finitely
generated, projective
R[x]
-module such that
R(x){\otimes}_{R[x]}P
is
R(x)
-free,
then
P
is
R[x]
-free.
Using
[
Horrocks 1964
, Theorem 1]
,
M. Pavaman Murthy

[
Bass 1973
,
Lemma on p. 24
]
had proved the case where
R
is noetherian and local,
which is already sufficient for our main purpose. Here, we shall prove the case where
R
is local.

4 Theorem
.

If
R
is a local commutative ring and
P
is a finitely
generated, projective
R[x]
-module such that
R(x){\otimes}_{R[x]}P
is
R(x)
-free, then
P
is
R[x]
-free.

Proof
(
Paul Roberts
)
.

We use the notation of
Lemma
3
above.

By Review
2
(iii)
above, there exist an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an idempotent

E\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
such
that
P\underset{R[x]}{\simeq}R[x]\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
; hence,

R(x)\otimes_{R[x]}\mkern-3.0muP\underset{R(x)}{\simeq}R(x)\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
.
By Review
2
(iv)
above, since
R(x)\otimes_{R[x]}P
is
R(x)
-free,
there exist an
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, an
A\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR(x)\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
,
and a
B\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR(x)\mkern 0.0mu^{n}

such that
A{\cdot}B=E
and
B{\cdot}A=I_{m}
.
Also by Review
2
(iv)
above, to show that
P
is
R[x]
-free it suffices to show that
there exist an

A^{\prime\prime}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
and a
B^{\prime\prime}\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 2.0mu^{n}
such that

A^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime\prime}=E
and
B^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime\prime}=I_{m}
.

Here,
\overline{E}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-2.0mu\overline{R}[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
.
Now
\overline{R}[x]
is a PID; hence, finitely generated, projective

\overline{R}[x]
-modules are
\overline{R}[x]
-free.
By Review
2
(iv)
above, there exist an
m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a

\hat{C}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0mu\overline{R}[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m^{\prime}}
, and a

\hat{D}\hskip-1.0pt\in\hskip-1.0pt\hbox{}^{m^{\prime}}\mkern 0.0mu\overline{R}[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}

such that
\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}=\overline{E}
and

\hat{D}{\cdot}\hat{C}=\overline{I_{m^{\prime}}}
, where the circumflexes are reminders
that the matrices are over
\overline{R}[x]
.
Over the field
\overline{R}(x)
, we then have
\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{B}=\overline{E}=\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}
,
while
\overline{B}{\cdot}\overline{A}=\overline{I_{m}}
and

\hat{D}{\cdot}\hat{C}=\overline{I_{m^{\prime}}}
.
Now
\hat{D}{\cdot}(\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{B}){\cdot}\hat{C}=\hat{D}{\cdot}(\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}){\cdot}\hat{C}=\overline{I_{m^{\prime}}}{\cdot}\overline{I_{m^{\prime}}}

and
\overline{B}{\cdot}(\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}){\cdot}\overline{A}=\overline{B}{\cdot}(\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{B}){\cdot}\overline{A}=\overline{I_{m}}{\cdot}\overline{I_{m}}.
Hence, over the field
\overline{R}(x)
,
the matrices
\hat{D}{\cdot}\overline{A}

and
\overline{B}{\cdot}\hat{C}
are mutually inverse,
so square; thus,
m^{\prime}=m
.
By Lemma
3
(iii)
above,
there exists some invertible
U\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR(x)\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
such that

\overline{U}=\hat{D}{\cdot}\overline{A}
(and
\overline{U^{-1}}=\overline{B}{\cdot}\hat{C}
).
Over
R(x)
, set
A^{\prime}:=A{\cdot\,}U^{-1}
and
B^{\prime}:=U{\cdot}B
;
then
A^{\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime}=E
and
B^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}=I_{m}
.
Over
\overline{R}(x)

\overline{A^{\prime}}=\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{U^{-1}}=\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{B}{\cdot}\hat{C}=\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}{\cdot}\hat{C}=\hat{C}{\cdot}\overline{I_{m}}=\hat{C}
and
\overline{B^{\prime}}=\overline{U}{\cdot}\overline{B}=\hat{D}{\cdot}\overline{A}{\cdot}\overline{B}=\hat{D}{\cdot}\hat{C}{\cdot}\hat{D}=\overline{I_{m}}{\cdot}\hat{D}=\hat{D}.

Thus,
\overline{A^{\prime}}
,
\overline{B^{\prime}}
are matrices over
\overline{R}[x]
,
which means that
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
are matrices over the subring
R[x]+\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)

of
R(x)
. By Lemma
3
(i)
above,
R(x)=R[x]\oplus R(x)_{\circ}
as
R
-modules. Hence,

R[x]+\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)=R[x]\oplus\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ}
, and,
over this subring of
R(x)
, we have
A^{\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime}=E
and

B^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}=I_{m}
.

Set

[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]:=\{[F{\cdot}A^{\prime}]:F\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}\}\subseteq\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
, where the polynomial part of
F{\cdot}A^{\prime}
is taken coordinate-wise.
We claim that
[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]=\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
.
Consider any
G\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
;
it suffices to show that
G\in[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]
.
There exists a monic
h\in R[x]
such that
h{\cdot}B^{\prime}\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
.
Now
[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]\ni[(G{\cdot}(h{\cdot}B^{\prime})){\cdot}A^{\prime}]=[G{\cdot}h{\cdot}I_{m}]=G{\cdot}h
;
hence,
[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]\supseteq\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}{\cdot}h
.
Here,

\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}/(\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}{\cdot}h)
is finitely

R
-generated, as it is a finitely generated
(R[x]/(R[x]{\cdot}h))
-module and
h
is monic.
Hence,
\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}/[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]
too is
finitely
R
-generated. By Lemma
3
(iv)
above, to prove

[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]=\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
, it suffices to show

G\in\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}{+}[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]
.
In
\overline{R}[x]
,

\displaystyle\overline{G}=\overline{G{\cdot}I_{m}}=\overline{G{\cdot}B^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}}=\overline{G{\cdot}B^{\prime}}{\cdot}\overline{A^{\prime}}=\overline{[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]}{\cdot}\overline{A^{\prime}}=\overline{[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]{\cdot}A^{\prime}}=\overline{[[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]{\cdot}A^{\prime}]}.

Hence, each coordinate of
G{-}[[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]{\cdot}A^{\prime}]
lies in

R[x]\cap\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)=\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R[x]
. This shows that

G=(G{-}[[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]{\cdot}A^{\prime}]){+}[[G{\cdot}B^{\prime}]{\cdot}A^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}{+}[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]
,
as desired; thus, we have
[\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]=\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
. The point of this paragraph is that
we may now fix an
F^{\prime}\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
such that

[F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}]=I_{m}
. By symmetry,
we may fix a
G^{\prime}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
such that

[B^{\prime}{\cdot}G^{\prime}]=I_{m}
.

Notice that each coordinate
of
F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}{-}I_{m}

lies in
(R[x]\,{\oplus}\,\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ})\cap R(x)_{\circ}=\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ}
;
thus,
\operatorname{Det}(F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime})\in 1{+}\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ}
.
Now
1{+}\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ}=\{\frac{f}{g}\in R(x):f,g\,\,{\rm monic},\overline{f}=\overline{g}\}
, which
is a multiplicative group. Over
R[x]\oplus\mathfrak{m}{\cdot}R(x)_{\circ}
,

F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}
is therefore invertible, and
we may form
A^{\prime\prime}:=A^{\prime}{\cdot}\,(F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime})^{-1}
and

B^{\prime\prime}:=(F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}){\cdot}B^{\prime}
; then
A^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime\prime}=E
and
B^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime\prime}=I_{m}
.
As before, we may fix a

G^{\prime\prime}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}
such that

[B^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}G^{\prime\prime}]=I_{m}
.
Then

B^{\prime\prime}=F^{\prime}{\cdot}A^{\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime}=F^{\prime}{\cdot}E\in\hbox{}^{m}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}

and
A^{\prime\prime}=A^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}I_{m}=A^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}[B^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}G^{\prime\prime}]=A^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}B^{\prime\prime}{\cdot}G^{\prime\prime}=E{\cdot}G^{\prime\prime}\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{m}

∎

5 Theorem
.

If
R
is a domain
and
P
is a finitely
generated, projective
R[x]
-module such that

R_{\mathfrak{m}}(x){\otimes}_{R[x]}P\,
is
R_{\mathfrak{m}}(x)
-free
for

each
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(R)
, then

P\underset{R[x]}{\simeq}R[x]\otimes_{R}\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 1.0mu^{\prime}
for some finitely generated,
projective
R
-module
P\mkern 1.0mu^{\prime}
.

Proof
(
Quillen, Vaserstein, Roitman
\mkern 3.0mu)
.

Set
R[x,y]:=R[x][y]=R[y][x]
.
For each
f\in R[x,y]
, we write

(y\mapsto f){:\,}R[x,y]\to R[x,y]
,
g\mapsto g^{(y\mapsto f)}
, to
denote the unique ring endomorphism of
R[x,y]
which sends
y
to
f
and fixes
each element of
R[x]
. We define
(x\mapsto f)
analogously.
We extend these maps to matrices coordinate-wise.

By
Review
2
(iii)
above,
there exist an
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

and an idempotent

E\in\hbox{}^{n}\mkern-3.0muR[x]\mkern 0.0mu^{n}
such that
P\underset{R[x]}{\simeq}R[x]\mkern 2.0mu^{n}{\cdot}E
.
Set
J:=\{j\in R\mid E^{(x\mapsto x+j{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}\\
E\}
.

We now show that
J
is an ideal of
R
. Clearly
0\in J
.
Consider any
j,j^{\prime}\in J
. Here,
E^{(x\mapsto x+j{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E
,
and applying
(x\mapsto x+j^{\prime}{\cdot}y)
yields

E^{(x\mapsto x+j^{\prime}{\cdot}y+j{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E^{(x\mapsto x+j^{\prime}{\cdot}y)}
. Since
E^{(x\mapsto x+j^{\prime}{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E
,
we see that
E^{(x\mapsto x+j^{\prime}{\cdot}y+j{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E
, whence
j+j^{\prime}\in J
.
Now consider any
j\in J
and
r\in R
. Since
E
is a matrix over
R[x]
,
applying
(y\mapsto r{\cdot}\,y)
to
E^{(x\mapsto x+j{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E

yields

E^{(x\mapsto x+j{\cdot}r{\cdot}y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E
, whence
j{\cdot}r\in J
.
Hence,
J
is an ideal of
R
.

Consider an arbitrary
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(R)
.
By hypothesis,

R_{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]}(R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]{\otimes}_{R[x]}P)

is
R_{\mathfrak{m}}(x)
-free
. Then, by Theorem
4
above,

R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]{\otimes}_{R[x]}P
is
R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]
-free
.
We may view
R
as a subring of
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
, since
R
is a domain.
By Review
2
(iv)
above,
there exist matrices
I
,
A
, and
B
over
R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]
such that
I
is an identity matrix,

A{\cdot}B=E
, and
B{\cdot}A=I
.
We extend our ring-endomorphism notation from
R[x,y]
to
R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x,y]
,
and set
C:=A{\cdot}B^{(x\mapsto x{+}y)}
and

D:=A^{(x\mapsto x{+}y)}{\cdot}B
.
There exists some
r\in R{-}\mathfrak{m}
such that
r{\cdot}C
and
r{\cdot}D

are matrices over
R[x,y]
.
On viewing
C
as a polynomial in
y
with coefficients that are matrices over
R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]
,
we see that the constant term is
C^{(y\mapsto 0)}=A{\cdot}B=E
, which is a matrix over
R[x]
.
Hence,
C^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}
is a matrix over
R[x,y]
. Similarly,

D^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}
is a matrix over
R[x,y]
. Over
R_{\mathfrak{m}}[x,y]
,

\displaystyle C^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}D^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}

\displaystyle=A{\cdot}B^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}A^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}B=A{\cdot}(B{\cdot}A)^{(x\mapsto x+r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}B=A{\cdot}I{\cdot}B=E,

\displaystyle D^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}C^{(y\mapsto r{\cdot}y)}

\displaystyle=A^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}B{\cdot}A{\cdot}B^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}=A^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}{\cdot}I{\cdot}B^{(x\mapsto x{+}r{\cdot}y)}=E^{(x\mapsto x+r{\cdot}y)}.

Hence,

E\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E^{(x\mapsto x+r{\cdot}y)}
; that is,

r\in J
. Since
r\not\in\mathfrak{m}
, we have
J\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}
.
Since
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(R)
is arbitrary,
1\in J
; that is,

E^{(x\mapsto x+y)}\underset{R[x,y]}{\sim}E
.
Thus,

there exist matrices
A_{0}
,
B_{0}
over
R[x,y]

such that
A_{0}{\cdot}B_{0}=E^{(x\mapsto x+y)}
and

B_{0}{\cdot}A_{0}=E
.
Then
A_{0}^{(y\mapsto-x)}
and

are matrices over

,

A_{0}^{(y\mapsto-x)}{\cdot}B_{0}^{(y\mapsto-x)}=(E^{(x\mapsto x+y)})^{(y\mapsto-x)}=E^{(x\mapsto 0)}
, and

B_{0}^{(y\mapsto-x)}{\cdot}A_{0}^{(y\mapsto-x)}=E^{(y\mapsto-x)}=E
.
Thus,
E^{(x\mapsto 0)}\underset{R[x]}{\sim}E
,
which is the desired conclusion, by Review
2
(iv)
above.
∎

6 The Quillen-Suslin Theorem
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
K
be a Dedekind
domain
,
let
R
denote
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and let
P
be a finitely generated,
projective
R
-module. Then
P\underset{R}{\simeq}R\otimes_{K}\mkern-3.0muP_{0}

for some finitely generated, projective
K
-module
P_{0}
. If
K
is a PID, then

P_{0}
is
K
-free and
P
is
R
-free.

Proof
(
Quillen
)
.
We argue by induction on
n
. As the result is clear for
n=0
,
we may assume that
n\geqslant 1
and that the result holds with
n{-}1
in place of
n
.

Set
X:=\{x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
Consider an arbitrary
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(K[X])
. There then exists some

\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Max}(K)
such that
\mathfrak{m}\cap K\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.
By hypothesis,
P
is a finitely generated, projective
K[x_{1}][X]
-module.
We view
K[x_{1}][X]\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]
.
Then

K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]\otimes_{K[x_{1}][X]}P
is a finitely generated, projective

K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]
-module;
it is
K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]
-free by
the induction hypothesis, since
K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})
is a PID by Lemma
3
(v)
above,
since
K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
is a local PID.
We now view
K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]\subseteq K[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1})
.
Then
K[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1})\otimes_{K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]}\hskip-1.8pt(K_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}(x_{1})[X]\otimes_{K[x_{1}][X]}P)
is
K[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1})
-free;
that is,
K[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1})\otimes_{K[X][x_{1}]}\hskip-1.8ptP
is

K[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1})
-free. Since
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}(K[X])
is arbitrary,
Theorem
5
above now shows that

P\mkern-5.0mu\underset{K[X][x_{1}]}{\simeq}K[X][x_{1}]\otimes_{K[X]}\mkern-3.0muP\mkern 1.0mu^{\prime}

for some finitely generated, projective
K[X]
-module
P\mkern 1.0mu^{\prime}
.
By the induction hypothesis again,

P\mkern 1.0mu^{\prime}\mkern-5.0mu\underset{K[X]}{\simeq}K[X]\otimes_{K}P_{0}
for some finitely generated, projective
K
-module

P_{0}
, and then

P\mkern-5.0mu\underset{K[X][x_{1}]}{\simeq}K[X][x_{1}]\otimes_{K}\mkern-3.0muP_{0}
. ∎

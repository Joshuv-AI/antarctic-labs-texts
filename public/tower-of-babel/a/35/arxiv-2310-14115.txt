Moduli Spaces of Positive Curvature Metrics in Dimension Four and Beyond
Thorsten Hertl

Abstract
We construct non-trivial elements in the homotopy groups of the observer moduli space of positive sectional curvature metrics on ℂ ​ P n \mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n} and non-trivial elements in the homotopy groups of the observer moduli space of positive scalar curvature metrics on ℂ ​ P 2 ​ ♯ ​ M 4 \mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}\sharp M^{4} .



1
Introduction

During the last decades, the space
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
of Riemannian metrics satisfying a curvature condition
C
, like positive sectional curvature or positive scalar curvature, has attracted a lot of attention, see for example
[
14
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
20
]
for a more comprehensive survey.
That these spaces may be non contractible were firstly observed by Hitchin,
[
14
]
who used information about homotopy groups of diffeomorphism groups and the pullback action of the diffeomorphism on the space of positive scalar curvature metrics to construct ’exotic positive scalar curvature metrics’ on
8k
-dimensional spheres and ’exotic loops’ of those.

One could argue that it is more natural to study the moduli space
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
instead because it is this space that actually carries geometric significant information without the shadow of the topology of the underlying manifold tripling through.
Whether or not one agrees with this opinion, it is unarguably true that the pullback action
\mathrm{Diff}(M)\curvearrowright\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
is not free in general, so the quotient space is often not well behaved.

Therefore, the focus has shifted towards the
observer moduli space

\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)}
obtained by dividing out the smaller group
\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)
of
observer diffeomorphism
consisting of all diffeomorphisms that fixes an initially given point
x_{0}
and whose differential at
x_{0}
is the identity.
The pullback action
\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)\curvearrowright\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
is indeed free and the quotient
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)}
is closely related to the classifying space
B\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)
.
This observation was used in
[
4
]
to construct the first examples of non-trivial elements in higher homotopy groups of observer moduli spaces of positive scalar curvature metrics.
These arguments were subsequently refined by
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
to observer moduli spaces of positive Ricci curvature metrics and certain positive intermediate curvature metrics, respectively.

The main result of this article provides to the authors knowledge the first examples of non-trivial elements in higher homotopy groups of observer moduli spaces of positive sectional curvature metrics.

Theorem A
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
3\leq j\leq n-1
be odd.
Let further
g_{FS}
be the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}
.
Then we have the following results:

(1)

\pi_{2}(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})},[g_{FS}])
contains an element of order greater than
n+1
if
n
is even and an element of order greater than
(n+1)/2
if
n
is odd.

(2)

\pi_{2j}(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})},[g_{FS}])\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\neq 0
.

We wish to emphasise that all previously obtained examples require the degree of the homotopy groups to be small compared to the dimension to the manifold, while our examples do not.

These examples are established using homotopy theory.
However, the employed homotopy-theoretical construction also has a geometric interpretation.
For positive scalar curvature metrics, which experience more flexible gluing constructions, this geometric interpretation allows us to prove that observer moduli spaces of positive scalar curvature metrics have non-trivial second homotopy groups for a large class of oriented, connected four manifolds.

Theorem B
.

If
M^{4}
is a closed, connected, oriented, smooth manifold of dimension four that carries at least one positive scalar curvature metric
g_{0}
, then

\pi_{2}\left(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{scal}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}\sharp M)\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}\sharp M)},[g_{FS}\sharp g_{0}]\right)\neq 0.

The paper is structured as follows: We first provide in Section
2
foundational material of (homotopy) moduli spaces of positively curved metrics that differs slightly from the presentations in the existing literature.
Next, we construct in Section
3
a non-trivial smooth fibre bundle
\mathcal{DO}(1)
over
S^{2}
with fibre
D\mathcal{O}(1)
, the disc bundle of the dual tautological line bundle over
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
, that is trivial near the boundary.
We use this bundle in Section
4
to establish the non-trivial elements in the observer moduli spaces mentioned in Theorem
B
.
In the final Section
5
, we present a more geometrically flavoured proof of the first part of Theorem
A
and a more homotopy-theoretical one of the second part.

2
Foundations on Moduli Spaces

We shortly recall the theory and different versions of moduli spaces and construct the fibre bundles we will need in subsequent sections.
Unless stated otherwise,
M
denotes a smooth, closed, connected, and oriented manifold of dimension
d
.

Let
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
be the topological group of all orientation preserving diffeomorphisms and let
\mathrm{Riem}(M)
be the space of Riemannian metrics both equipped with their usual
C^{\infty}
-topology.
The diffeomorphism group
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
acts from the right on
\mathrm{Riem}(M)
and all subspaces
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
via pullback, where
C
denotes a diffeomorphism invariant curvature condition.
However, we will use the induced left action by pulling back their inverses instead.
The only relevant curvature conditions for us in this article are positive sectional curvature
C=\mathrm{sec}>0
and positive scalar curvature
C=\mathrm{scal}>0
, which yield open, diffeomorphism-invariant subspaces of
\mathrm{Riem}(M)
.
The quotient
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
is called the
moduli space
of positive sectional/scalar curvature metrics.

Usually, the action of
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
on
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
is not free.
One possibility to solve this issue is to use the subgroup of
observer diffeomorphisms

\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M):=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)\,:\,\varphi(x_{0})=x_{0}\text{ and }D_{x_{0}}\varphi=\mathord{\mathrm{id}}\},

because this subgroup acts freely.
Indeed, since geodesics are uniquely determined by their initial values, isometric observer diffeomorphisms are the identity on a geodesic neighbourhood of
x_{0}
.
An open-and-closed argument now implies that isometric observer diffeomorphisms are the identity on the entire connected component of
x_{0}
.
The quotient
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)}
is called the
observer moduli space
.

Another approach is to enlarge
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
so that the induced action becomes free:
Assume that a topological group
G
acts on a topological space
X
(not necessarily freely).
Let
G\rightarrow EG\rightarrow BG
be the universal
G
-principal bundle.
The space
EG
is contractible, so
EG\times X
has the same homotopy type as
X
but the diagonal action is now free.
The Borel construction
X\!/\!/G:=EG\times_{G}X
is a fibre bundle over
BG
with fibre
X
.
Furthermore, the projection onto the second component yields a comparison map
X\!/\!/G\rightarrow X/G
, which is a weak homotopy equivalence if the action on
X
is free and proper.
A bit less known is the following statement, which is not hard to prove.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the identification
\pi_{n}(BG)\cong\pi_{n}(\Omega BG)\cong\pi_{n-1}(G)
via fibre-transportation, the boundary operators in the induced long exact sequence of homotopy groups agree with the homomorphism induced by the orbit map.
In other words, we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\pi_{n-1}(G,e)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[g]\mapsto[g_{\bullet}x_{0}]}
\textstyle{\pi_{n}(BG,\ast)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{n}}
\textstyle{\pi_{n-1}(X,x_{0}).}

If
X=\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)
and
G=\mathrm{Diff}(M)
or
\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)
for some fixed
x_{0}\in M
, we call
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
the
homotopy moduli space
and
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)}
the
homotopy observer moduli space
, respectively.

An intriguing model for
B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
and hence for
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
that allows to interpret it as a manifold version of the infinite Grassmannian manifold
B\mathrm{SO}(d)=\mathrm{Gr}^{+}_{d}(\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty})
is presented in
[
3
]
:
Whitney’s embedding theorem implies that the space of embeddings
\mathrm{Emb}(M,\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty})
is a (weakly) contractible topological space on which
\mathrm{Diff}(M)
acts freely and properly from the right, hence

B\mathrm{Diff}(M)=\mathrm{Emb}(M,\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty})/\mathrm{Diff}(M)=\{N\subseteq\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}\,:\,N\cong M\}

can be modelled by the space of all submanifolds of
\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
that are diffeomorphic to
M
.
The associated universal
M
-fibre bundle in this picture is

M_{\mathrm{univ}}:=\mathrm{Emb}(M;\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty})\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}M\cong\{(N,p)\,:\,p\in N\subseteq\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty},N\cong M\}.

Using this model of
B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
, the homotopy moduli space can now be described by

\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)=\{(N,g)\,:\,M\cong N\subseteq\mathord{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty},\,g\in\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(N)\}.

Note that the Riemannian metric
g
is not required to arise from the embedding of
N
.
The pullback of
M_{\mathrm{univ}}
to
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
has a canonical Riemannian metric
h
satisfying curvature condition
C
on its vertical tangent bundle
T^{\mathrm{vert}}M_{\mathrm{univ}}=E\mathrm{Diff}(M)\times_{\mathrm{Diff}(M)}TM
that is given by the formula

h^{\mathrm{univ}}_{[\iota,g,m]}([\iota,v],[\iota,w])=g_{m}(v,w).

The fibre metric
h
is well defined because the two actions of the diffeomorphism group cancel against each other.

Of course,
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)}
has a similar description.
In particular, over this space exists the universal
M
-fibre bundle with structure group
\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(M)
equipped with a fibrewise Riemannian metric satisfying condition
C
.

Like
B\mathrm{Diff}(M)
the homotopy moduli space
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
is a classifying space for a homotopy functor.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space.
The map that assigns to a homotopy class
[f]\in[X;\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)]
the homotopy class
[h^{\mathrm{univ}}\circ f]
yields a one to one correspondence

\textstyle{[X;\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{1:1}
\textstyle{\underset{[E]}{\coprod}\pi_{0}\left(\mathrm{Riem}^{C}_{\mathrm{vert}}(E)\right),}

where
[E]
runs over the set of isomorphism classes of numerable
M
-fibre bundles over
X
and where
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}_{\mathrm{vert}}(E)
denotes the space of Riemannian metrics on the vertical tangent bundle
T^{\mathrm{vert}}E
.

Proof.

It is fairly easy to see, cf.
[
19
]
*Section 5.6, that two maps
f,g\colon X\rightarrow E
into the total space of a fibration
p\colon E\rightarrow B
are homotopic if and only if
pf
and
pg
are homotopic and the fibre transport
h^{\sharp}(f)
is fibre-homotopic to
g
, where
h\colon X\times[0,1]\rightarrow B
is a homotopy between
f
and
g
.

Continuous maps
\varphi\colon X\rightarrow E
are in one to one correspondence with sections into the pullback fibration
(p\varphi)^{\ast}E\rightarrow X
, so
f,g
are homotopic if and only if
h^{\sharp}(f)
and
g
are homotopic sections in
(pg)^{\ast}E
.

We now deduce the stated claim by applying the previous observations to the homotopy observer moduli space
\mathrm{Riem}^{C}(M)\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(M)
and by using the fact that numerable fibre bundles are fibrations
[
19
]
*Theorem 13.4.1 and that the pullback of the universal
M
-fibre bundle with a homotopy class induces a bijection between
[X,B\mathrm{Diff}(M)]
and isomorphism classes of numerable
M
-fibre bundles over
X
.
∎

\mathcal{DO}(1)

H_{0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi})\xleftarrow[\cong]{H_{0}(\mathrm{incl})}H_{0}(\mathrm{pt})\xrightarrow[\cong]{H_{0}(\mathrm{incl})}H_{0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{1})
.
, we derive

\lambda_{2}=p^{\ast}c\cap\left(\xi(T^{\mathrm{vert}}\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi})\cap[\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi}]\right)
.
The Poincare dual of
p^{\ast}c
is the homology class
H_{4}(\iota)([\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}])
, so

\displaystyle\kappa_{\xi}=\xi(T^{\mathrm{vert}}\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi})\cap p^{\ast}c\cap[\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi}]=\iota_{\ast}\left(\iota^{\ast}\xi(T^{\mathrm{vert}}\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi})\cap[\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}]\right)=\iota_{\ast}\left(\xi(T\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2})\cap[\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}]\right)

only depends on the fibre.

5
Proof of Theorem
A

To equip
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{2}_{\Phi}
from the previous section with a fibrewise positive sectional curvature metric it will be more convenient to use a different description so that we do not need to rely on gluing methods.

Definition 5.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, we define

\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}:=S^{3}\times_{S^{1},(\cdot)^{-1}}\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}=\bigl(S^{3}\times\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}\bigr)/\sim

where
\bigl(z,[w_{0}:w_{1}:w_{2}:\dots:w_{n}]\bigr)\sim(\mathrm{e}^{\mathord{\mathrm{i}}\theta}\cdot z,[\mathrm{e}^{\mathord{\mathrm{i}}\theta}w_{0}:\mathrm{e}^{\mathord{\mathrm{i}}\theta}w_{1}:w_{2}:\dots:w_{n}]).

Completely analogous to Lemma
3.5
one proves the next result.

Lemma 5.2
.

The map
p\colon\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}\rightarrow\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
induced by the projection to
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}
induces a fibre bundle structure on
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}
.
Over
U_{j}=\{[a_{0}:a_{1}]\,:\,a_{j}\neq 0\}
the following maps

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}\colon p^{-1}(U_{j})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U_{j}\times\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n},

\displaystyle\left[z,[w_{0}:\dots:w_{n}]\right]

\displaystyle\mapsto\left([z],[\sigma_{j}([z])z^{-1}w_{0}:\sigma_{j}([z])z^{-1}w_{1}:w_{2}:\dots:w_{n}]\right)

are local trivialisations.
The corresponding transition functions are given by

g_{01}([a_{0}:a_{1}])([w_{0}:\dots:w_{n}])=\bigl(\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}^{-1}([a_{0}:a_{1}])w_{0}:\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}^{-1}([a_{0}:a_{1}])w_{1}:w_{2}:\dots:w_{n}\bigr).

Its structure group can be reduced to
\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n},D^{2n})
, the group of all diffeomorphisms that are the identity on
D^{2n}=\{[w_{0}:\dots:w_{n-1}:1]\,:\,|w_{0}|^{2}+\dots+|w_{n-1}|^{2}\leq 1\}
.

Since the chosen action in Definition
5.1
is isometric with respect to the Fubini-Study metric
g_{FS}
, the bundle
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}
carries a fibre metric with positive sectional curvature.
Together with Lemma
5.2
we get a classifying map

f_{\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi},g_{FS}}\colon S^{2}=\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})}.

The first part of Theorem
A
now specifies to the following result.

Theorem 5.3
.

The element
[f_{\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi},g_{FS}}]\in\pi_{2}\left(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})},[g_{FS}]\right)
has at least order
n+1
if
n
is even and at least order
(n+1)/2
if
n
is odd.

Proof.

Under the forgetful map
\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})}\rightarrow B\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
this element gets mapped to
[f_{\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}}]
, the class that is represented by the classifying map of the bundle
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}
.
Since the transition functions of
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}
take values in
\mathrm{PU}(n+1)
, this class lifts to an element
\pi_{2}(B\mathrm{PU}(n+1))
and since
\mathrm{det}(g_{01})\colon U_{0}\cap U_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(1)
is a map of degree
\pm 2
it follows that
[f_{\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}}]
corresponds to the element
\pm 2
under the identification
\pi_{2}(B\mathrm{PU}(n+1))\cong\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{n+1}
.

It was shown in
[
17
]
that the canonical map
B\mathrm{PU}(n+1)\rightarrow B\mathrm{hAut}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
, which of course factors through
B\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
, induces an isomorphism between their second homotopy groups.
Thus
[f_{\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}_{\Phi}}]
generates
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{n+1}
if
n
is even and
2\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{n+1}
if
n
is odd.
∎

Exploiting information about the rational homotopy groups of
B\mathrm{hAut}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
we can deduce the existence of non-trivial elements in higher homotopy groups of the observer moduli space in a stable range.
This will prove the second part of Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem
A
(2).

Since the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(n+1)
acts isometrically on
(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n},g_{FS})
,
the bundle
f^{\ast}E\mathrm{U}(n+1)\times_{\mathrm{U}(n+1)}\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}
carries a fibrewise positive sectional curvature metric for every element
[f]\in\pi_{2j}(B\mathrm{U}(n+1))
.
The choice of such a fibre metric yields an element

[f_{g_{FS}}]\in\pi_{2j}\left(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/\!/\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}),[g_{FS}]\right)

that lifts the image of
[f]
under the homomorphism induced by the canonical map
B\mathrm{U}(n+1)\rightarrow B\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
.

We first prove that
[f_{g_{FS}}]
has infinite order:
The classifying map of the canonical inclusion
B\mathrm{PU}(n+1)\rightarrow B\mathrm{hAut}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
, which factors of course through
B\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
, induces an isomorphism on all rational homotopy groups, see
[
17
]
.
The rational homotopy groups of
B\mathrm{hAut}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})
are given by

\pi_{2j}\left(B\mathrm{hAut}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\right)\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for all }2\leq j\leq n+1

and all other are zero, see
[
18
]
*p.314.
Thus, if
[f]\in\pi_{2j}(B\mathrm{U}(n+1))\cong\pi_{2j}(B\mathrm{PU}(n+1))
has infinite order, then the lift
[f_{g_{FS}}]
has infinite order, too.

It remains to show that a finite multiple of
[f_{g_{FS}}]
lifts to the (homotopy) observer moduli space.
From the long exact sequence on (rational) homotopy groups induced by the fibre bundle
\mathrm{SO}(2n)\rightarrow\mathrm{Fr}^{+}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\rightarrow\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}
we deduce for all
4\leq k\leq 2n-1
that

\pi_{k-1}(\mathrm{Fr}^{+}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}))\otimes{\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}}\cong\pi_{k-1}(\mathrm{SO}(2n))\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\begin{cases}\mathord{\mathbb{Q}},&\text{if }k\equiv 0\mod 4,\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

The second part of Proposition
2.3
now implies that a finite multiple of
[f_{g_{FS}}]
lifts to
\pi_{2j}(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathrm{sec}>0}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})\!/\!/{\mathrm{Diff}_{x_{0}}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n})})
if
2\leq j\leq n
is odd. This lift, of course, automatically has infinite order.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

A careful analysis of the involved homomorphism even shows that the composition

\pi_{2j}(B\mathrm{PU}(n+1))\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\pi_{2j}(B\mathrm{Diff}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}))\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\pi_{2j-1}(\mathrm{Fr}^{+}(\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}))\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}

is injective if
2\leq j\leq n+1
is even.
Hence, this approach cannot produce more non-trivial elements in the rational homotopy groups of the observer moduli space of positive sectional curvature metrics on
\mathord{\mathbb{C}P}^{n}
.

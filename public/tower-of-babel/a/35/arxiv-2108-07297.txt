K 4 K_{4} -free graphs have sparse halves
Christian Reiher

Abstract
. Every K 4 K_{4} -free graph on n n vertices has a set of ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ \lfloor n/2\rfloor vertices spanning at most n 2 / 18 n^{2}/18 edges.



1.
Introduction

By a well-known theorem of Mantel
[
15
]
every graph on
n
vertices with more
than
\frac{1}{4}n^{2}
edges contains a triangle. As balanced complete bipartite graphs
show, the number
\frac{1}{4}n^{2}
appearing in this result is optimal. However, the
only extremal graph for this problem contains a large independent
set (of size
\lceil\frac{1}{2}n\rceil
) and thus it is natural to wonder whether graphs
with fewer yet more uniformly distributed edges need to contain triangles as well.
An especially prominent and still open version of this question, due to Erdős
[
8
]
,
asks whether every
n
-vertex graph
G
contains a triangle provided that all
sets
X\subseteq V(G)
of
|X|=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\rfloor
vertices span more
than
\frac{1}{50}n^{2}
edges.
Balanced blow-ups of the pentagon show that the denominator
50
would be optimal.
Moreover, Simonovits observed that balanced blow-ups of the Petersen graph achieve
equality as well (e.g. see
[
9
]
).
This so-called
sparse halves conjecture
attracted
the attention of many researchers
[
2
,
12
,
11
,
16
]
; despite
the recent investigations of Razborov
[
17
]
the problem remains elusive.

There is a similar situation for graphs not containing a
K_{4}
, i.e., four mutually adjacent
vertices. Generalising Mantel’s result Turán
[
19
]
proved that every graph on
n

vertices possessing more than
\frac{1}{3}n^{2}
edges contains a
K_{4}
. The extremal graphs for
this problem are balanced tripartite graphs and thus they contain independent sets of size

\lceil\frac{1}{3}n\rceil
as well as sets of size
\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\rfloor
spanning only
about
\frac{1}{18}n^{2}
edges (the precise value depending on the residue class of
n

modulo
6
). This state of affairs prompted Chung and Graham
[
5
]
and, independently,
Erdős et al.
[
6
]
to formulate a sparse halves conjecture for
K_{4}
-free graphs,
which is resolved in this article and restated in the above abstract.
A more precise version of our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If a graph
G
on
n
vertices has the property that every set

X\subseteq V(G)
of size
|X|=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\rfloor
spans at
least
\frac{1}{18}n^{2}
edges, then either
G
contains a
K_{4}

or
n
is divisible by
6
and
G
is a tripartite Turán graph.

The most recent contribution to this problem is due to X. Liu and J. Ma
[
13
]
, who
proved Theorem
1.1
under the additional assumption that
G
is regular.
Several parts of their argument do not depend on degree regularity and
we shall utilise some of their results. Another important idea of our approach
is to analyse the neighbourhoods of vertices by means of Ramsey-Turán theory,
appealing to a lemma due to Łuczak, Polcyn, and Reiher.

Throughout the rest of this article we shall call an
n
-vertex graph
G

extremal

if it is
K_{4}
-free and every set
X\subseteq V(G)
of size
|X|=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\rfloor

spans at least
\frac{1}{18}n^{2}
edges. Thus we are to prove that every extremal graph is a
tripartite Turán graph whose number of vertices is divisible by
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Parity

A well-known blow-up argument, which we recapitulate below, shows that it suffices to
establish Theorem
1.1
for even values of
n
. In other words, we only need
to prove the following apparently weaker claim.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be an extremal graph on
n
vertives. If
n
is even, then it is divisible by
3

and
G
is a tripartite Turán graph.

The reason why this statement implies Theorem
1.1
is as follows: If
n
is odd
we construct a graph
H
by replacing every vertex
x
of
G
by two new vertices
x^{\prime}
,
x^{\prime\prime}

and every edge
xy\in E(G)
by all four possible edges from
\{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\}
to
\{y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\}
.
Evidently,
H
is still
K_{4}
-free and it is not difficult to verify that any
n
vertices
of
H
span at least
\frac{2}{9}n^{2}
edges. So Proposition
2.1
tells us that
2n

is divisible by
3
and that
H
is a tripartite Turán graph. Consequently,
G
itself
is the complete tripartite graph all of whose vertex classes have size
\frac{1}{3}n
. Recalling
that
n
is odd we can form a set
X\subseteq V(G)
of size
\lfloor\frac{1}{2}n\rfloor

by taking one of these vertex classes together with
\lfloor\frac{1}{6}n\rfloor
vertices from
another vertex class. But now
e(X)=\frac{1}{3}n\cdot\lfloor\frac{1}{6}n\rfloor<\frac{1}{18}n^{2}

contradicts the hypothesis.

2.2.
Ramsey-Turán theory

Given natural numbers
n\geq s\geq 1
and
r\geq 3
Vera T. Sós asked to determine
the largest number
f_{r}(n,s)
of edges that a
K_{r}
-free graph on
n
vertices
can have provided that none of its independent sets consists of more than
s
vertices.
For instance, in the case
r=3
one can exploit that in triangle-free graphs the
neighbourhoods of all vertices are independent sets. This yields the
trivial
bound

f_{3}(n,s)\leq\frac{1}{2}ns
, which can be shown to be sharp in surprisingly many
cases
[
4
]
.
The study of
K_{4}
-free graphs turned out to be much more difficult and following the
works
[
3
,
7
,
10
,
18
]

it has been proved in
[
14
]

that
f_{4}(n,\delta n)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{4}+\delta-\delta^{2}+o(1)\bigr)n^{2}
holds for every sufficiently
small
\delta>0
and that there are analogous results for larger forbidden cliques.

When
\frac{s}{n}
is large, however, even the case
r=3
of triangle-free graphs is not
completely understood.
An old result due to Andrásfai
[
1
]
asserts
f_{3}(n,s)=n^{2}-4ns+5s^{2}

whenever
s\in\bigl[\frac{2}{5}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr]
. This was recently reproved
by Łuczak, Polcyn, and Reiher
[
20
]

and their alternative proof suggests the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices. If for some natural number

m\leq n
it is the case that every set
X\subseteq V
of size
m
induces at
least
\frac{2}{9}m^{2}
edges, then there are disjoint independent sets
A,B\subseteq V

such that
t=|A|+|B|

satisfies

(2.1)

n\leq\frac{2m}{3}+\frac{5t}{8}-\frac{2m^{2}}{9t}\leq\frac{m}{3}+\frac{3t}{4}\,.

In particular, the assumption is only satisfiable for
m\geq\frac{3}{4}n
.

Before beginning the proof we quote
[
20
]
*Lemma 2.2. Here
\alpha(G)
denotes
the maximum size of an independent set in a graph
G
.

Lemma 2.3

(Łuczak, Polcyn, and Reiher)
.

Given a graph
G
, suppose

\bullet

that
A\subseteq V(G)
is an independent set of size
\alpha(G)
,

\bullet

and that
M
is a matching in
G
from
V(G)\smallsetminus A
to
A

the size of which is as large as possible.

If
G^{\prime}
denotes the graph obtained from
G
by isolating the vertices in

A\smallsetminus V(M)
, i.e., by deleting all edges incident with them, then

\alpha(G^{\prime})=\alpha(G)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Pick an independent set
A\subseteq V
of size
|A|=\alpha(G)
as well as an

of size
|B|=\alpha(G-A)
. We shall prove
that

has the desired property.

Before reaching this goal we need to establish several weaker estimates involving
the cardinalities of
A
and
B
. First, the local density assumption yields

|E|\geq\frac{2}{9}n^{2}
and thus there exists a vertex
x
whose degree is at
least
\frac{4}{9}n
. As the neighbourhood of
x
is independent,
we have
|A|\geq\tfrac{4}{9}n>\frac{2}{5}n
, whence
|A|>\tfrac{2}{3}(n-|A|)
.
Since
e(A)=0<\frac{2}{9}m^{2}
yields
|A|<m
, we can deduce

(2.2)

(m-|A|)+\frac{2|A|(n-|A|)}{3(m-|A|)}>(m-|A|)+\frac{4(n-|A|)^{2}}{9(m-|A|)}\geq\frac{4}{3}(n-|A|)\,,

where the last step utilises the AM-GM inequality.

Next, for every
W\subseteq V\smallsetminus A
of
size

we have
e(A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}W)\geq\frac{2}{9}m^{2}

\tfrac{2}{9}m^{2}\leq\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}e(A,V\smallsetminus A)+\left(\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}\right)^{2}e(V\smallsetminus A)\,.

Together with the estimates
e(A,V\smallsetminus A)\leq|A||B|
and

,
which follow from the fact that

holds for every vertex

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{9}m^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}|A||B|+\left(\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}\right)^{2}|B|(n-|A|-|B|)

\displaystyle=\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}|B|m-\left(\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}\right)^{2}|B|^{2}\,,

which rewrites as

0\leq\left(\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}|B|-\frac{m}{3}\right)\left(\frac{2m}{3}-\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}|B|\right)\,.

This implies

(2.3)

\frac{m-|A|}{n-|A|}|B|\geq\frac{m}{3}\,,

whence

|A|+2|B|+m\geq|A|+\frac{2m}{3}\cdot\frac{n-|A|}{m-|A|}+m=\frac{2n}{3}+\frac{4|A|}{3}+(m-|A|)+\frac{2|A|(n-|A|)}{3(m-|A|)}\,.

Due to (
2.2
) this proves

(2.4)

|A|+2|B|+m>2n\,.

Finally, we remark that (
2.3
) implies
|B|\geq\tfrac{1}{3}n
, for which reason

(2.5)

21t=21(|A|+|B|)\geq 21(\tfrac{4}{9}+\tfrac{1}{3})n>16n\geq 12n+4m\,.

M
be a maximum

and, consequently, there exist disjoint sets

such that

and

. Now

entails

\frac{2}{9}m^{2}\leq e(V\smallsetminus X)=\sum_{y\in Y}d(y)+\sum_{a\in A}|N(a)\smallsetminus(X\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Y)|+e(V-(A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}X\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Y))\,.

.

. Third,

and Mantel’s theorem yield

Owing to

Now we multiply by

this completes the proof of (

). Moreover

.
∎

2.3.
Edges in neighbourhoods

In this subsection we study lower bounds on the number of edges spanned by the
neighbourhood of a vertex in an extremal graph. We commence with the following
variant of a result by X. Liu and J. Ma, see
[
13
]
*Theorem 1.5(1).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m
,
n
,
q
be positive integers such that
q\geq\frac{2}{9}m^{2}
and
n\geq m
.
If a triangle-free graph
G
has the property that every set
X\subseteq V(G)

of size
|X|=m
spans at least
q
edges, then
e(G)\geq\frac{nq}{2m-n}
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.2
informs us that
m\geq\frac{3}{4}n
and for this reason
the division by
2m-n
is permissible. Now, arguing indirectly, we consider
a counterexample
(n,m,q,G)
such that
n

is minimal.

First Case: There is a vertex
x
whose degree is less than
n-m
.

Set
d=d(x)
and
G^{\prime}=G-x
. Because of
9d\leq 9(n-m-1)\leq 12m-9m-9<4m-2

we have

q-d>\frac{2m^{2}}{9}-\frac{4m-2}{9}=\frac{2(m-1)^{2}}{9}\,.

Moreover, every set
X\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
of size
|X|=m-1
satisfies

e(X)\geq e(X\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{x\})-d\geq q-d
. For these reasons the quadruple

(n-1,m-1,q-d,G^{\prime})
satisfies the assumptions and the minimality of
n

discloses

e(G^{\prime})\geq\frac{(n-1)(q-d)}{2m-n-1}\,.

Together with

q\geq\frac{2m^{2}-(4m-3n)(5m-3n)}{9}=(2m-n)(n-m)>(2m-n)d

this shows

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=e(G^{\prime})+d\geq\frac{(n-1)(q-d)}{2m-n-1}+d=\frac{(n-1)q}{2m-n-1}-\frac{2(n-m)d}{2m-n-1}

\displaystyle>\frac{(n-1)q}{2m-n-1}-\frac{2(n-m)q}{(2m-n-1)(2m-n)}=\frac{nq}{2m-n}\,,

contrary to
(n,m,q,G)
being a counterexample.

Second Case: The minimum degree of
G
is at least
n-m
.

Consider a vertex
x\in V(G)
. For every set
R\subseteq N(x)
of size
m+d(x)-n

the set
R\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}(V\smallsetminus N(x))
has size
m
and thus it spans at least
q
edges.
By averaging over
R
we infer

q\leq e(V\smallsetminus N(x))+\frac{m+d(x)-n}{d(x)}e(N(x),V\smallsetminus N(x))\,,

whence

\frac{n-m}{d(x)}e(N(x),V\smallsetminus N(x))\leq e(G)-q\,.

Summing over
x
we obtain

(n-m)\sum_{x\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(x)}\sum_{y\in N(x)}d(y)\leq n(e(G)-q)\,.

As the left side rewrites as

(n-m)\sum_{xy\in E(G)}\left(\frac{d(y)}{d(x)}+\frac{d(x)}{d(y)}\right)

we are led to
2(n-m)e(G)\leq n(e(G)-q)
, which implies the desired lower bound
on
e(G)
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices.
If
x\in V
has degree
d(x)\geq n/2
, then

e(N(x))\geq\frac{n^{2}}{18}\cdot\frac{d(x)}{n-d(x)}

and there exist two disjoint independent sets
A,B\subseteq N(x)
such
that
|A|+|B|\geq\frac{4}{3}d(x)-\frac{2}{9}n
.

Moreover, if
Y\subseteq V(G)
of size
|Y|\geq\frac{1}{2}n
induces no
triangles, then

e(Y)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{18}\cdot\frac{|Y|}{n-|Y|}\,.

Proof.

The subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
induced by
N(x)
is triangle-free.
Now the statements addressing
N(x)
follow from
Lemma
2.4
applied to
n/2
,
d(x)
,
n^{2}/18
, and
G^{\prime}
here in
place of
m
,
n
,
q
, and
G
there and from Lemma
2.2
.
Similarly, Lemma
2.4
implies the last claim.
∎

We proceed with a lower bound on the number of edges spanned by “small” sets.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices.
If the size of
X\subseteq V(G)
belongs to
\bigl[\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr]
,
then
e(X)\geq\frac{1}{18}(3|X|-n)(6|X|-n)
.

Proof.

Suppose first that there is a set
A\subseteq V(G)\smallsetminus X
of size
|A|=\frac{1}{2}n-|X|

such that
|N(a)\cap X|\leq\frac{2}{3}|X|
holds for every
a\in A
.
Because of
|A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}X|=\frac{1}{2}n
we have

\tfrac{1}{18}n^{2}\leq e(A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}X)\leq e(A)+\tfrac{2}{3}|A||X|+e(X)\,.

Since Turán’s theorem and
|A|\leq|X|
imply
e(A)\leq\frac{1}{3}|A|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}|A||X|
,
this shows indeed that

e(X)\geq\tfrac{1}{18}n^{2}-|A||X|=\tfrac{1}{18}(3|X|-n)(6|X|-n)\,.

If no such set
A
exists, then there is a set
B\subseteq V(G)\smallsetminus X

of size
|B|=\frac{1}{2}n
such that every
b\in B
satisfies
|N(b)\cap X|>\frac{2}{3}|X|
.
If some three vertices
b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime\prime}\in B
form a triangle, then

|N(b^{\prime})\cap X|+|N(b^{\prime\prime})\cap X|+|N(b^{\prime\prime\prime})\cap X|>2|X|

implies that for some
x\in X
we have a clique
b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime\prime}x
of order four in
G
,
which is absurd. So
B
induces a triangle-free subgraph of
G
. Owing to
e(B)\geq n^{2}/18

there exists some vertex
b_{\star}\in B

such that
|N(b_{\star})\cap B|\geq\frac{2}{9}n>\frac{1}{2}n-|X|
.
Pick an arbitrary set
W\subseteq N(b_{\star})\cap B
of size
|W|=\frac{1}{2}n-|X|
.
Together with
b_{\star}
any edge connecting two vertices of
W
would yield
a triangle whose three vertices are in
B
. So
W
is independent and

\frac{1}{18}n^{2}\leq e(X\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}W)=e(X)+e(W,X)\leq e(X)+|W||X|\,,

as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

If
G
denotes an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices,
then
\alpha(G)\leq\frac{1}{3}n
.

Proof.

If
X\subseteq V(G)
satisfies
|X|\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr]
,
then Lemma
2.6
yields
e(X)>0
, so
X
cannot be independent.
∎

2.4.
Inequalities

In this subsection we prepare a stability analysis
of extremal graphs
G
satisfying
e(G)\geq\frac{7}{24}v(G)^{2}
. This will involve
the following inequality.

Fact 2.8
.

Define
h\colon\bigl[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]\longrightarrow\mathds{R}
by
h(\xi)=(3\xi-1)(6\xi-1)
.
If
\xi\in\bigl[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]
and
\gamma\in\bigl[\frac{7}{24},\frac{1}{3}\bigr]
,
then

(2.7)

\frac{h(\xi)^{2}}{\xi}\geq\frac{\xi(1+2\gamma)-8\gamma^{2}}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}\,.

Proof.

Writing
F(\gamma,\xi)
for the right side of (
2.7
) we have

\frac{\partial F(\gamma,\xi)}{\partial\gamma}=-\frac{8(1+\gamma)(2\gamma-\xi)}{(1-2\gamma)^{4}}<0

and, in particular,
F(\gamma,\xi)
is decreasing in
\gamma
.
As the left side of (
2.7
) is nonnegative, this shows that
it suffices to prove

(2.8)

\frac{h(\xi)^{2}}{\xi}\geq\frac{25}{24}F\bigl(\tfrac{7}{24},\xi\bigr)=\frac{114\xi-49}{5}\,.

Due to

100(h(\xi)-\xi)=2(30\xi-13)^{2}+(7-10\xi)+5(114\xi-49)

we have indeed

\frac{h(\xi)^{2}}{\xi}\geq 4(h(\xi)-\xi)>\frac{114\xi-49}{5}\,.\qed

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices which
has
\gamma n^{2}
edges. If
\gamma\geq\frac{7}{24}
, then

(2.9)

e(N(x))^{2}\geq\frac{d(x)n^{2}}{324}\cdot\frac{(1+2\gamma)d(x)-8\gamma^{2}n}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}

holds for every vertex
x\in V(G)
.

Proof.

If
d(x)<\frac{1}{3}n
, then because of
1+2\gamma-24\gamma^{2}=(1-4\gamma)(1+6\gamma)<0

the right side of (
2.9
) cannot be positive and the claim is clear.

Suppose next that
d(x)=\xi n
holds for some
\xi\in\bigl[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]
.
Recall that Turán’s theorem implies
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{3}
. Lemma
2.6
yields

e(N(x))\geq\frac{1}{18}h(\xi)n^{2}\geq 0
, where
h
indicates the function studied in
Fact
2.8
, and thus we have indeed

\frac{e(N(x))^{2}}{d(x)}\geq\frac{n^{3}}{324}\cdot\frac{h(\xi)^{2}}{\xi}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{324}\cdot\frac{(1+2\gamma)d(x)-8\gamma^{2}n}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}\,.

Finally, if
d(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}n
, then Corollary
2.5
yields

e(N(x))^{2}\geq\frac{d(x)n^{2}}{324}\cdot\frac{d(x)n^{2}}{\bigl(n-d(x)\bigr)^{2}}

and it remains to observe that

\frac{d(x)n^{2}}{\bigl(n-d(x)\bigr)^{2}}-\frac{(1+2\gamma)d(x)-8\gamma^{2}n}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}=\frac{\bigl(d(x)-2\gamma n\bigr)^{2}\bigl(2n-(1+2\gamma)d(x)\bigr)}{\bigl(n-d(x)\bigr)^{2}(1-2\gamma)^{3}}

is nonnegative because of
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
d(x)\leq n
.
∎

3.
Two independent sets

In this section we explore some consequences of the following observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
G=(V,E)
denotes an extremal graph on an even number
n

of vertices. If
A,B\subseteq V
are two disjoint independent sets, then

e(A,B)\leq|E|-\frac{2}{9}n^{2}
.

Proof.

Set
C=V\smallsetminus(A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B)
and notice that
|B|<\frac{1}{2}n
implies

|C|>\frac{1}{2}n-|A|
. Now every set
Z\subseteq C
of size
|Z|=\frac{1}{2}n-|A|

satisfies
e(A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Z)\geq\frac{1}{18}n^{2}
. Averaging over
Z
we learn

\frac{n^{2}}{18}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{2}n-|A|}{|C|}e(A,C)+\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}n-|A|}{|C|}\right)^{2}e(C)\,,

whence

\frac{n^{2}|C|}{9(n-2|A|)}\leq e(A,C)+\frac{\frac{1}{2}n-|A|}{|C|}e(C)\,.

This remains valid if we replace
A
by
B
and by adding both
estimates we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}|C|}{9}\left(\frac{1}{n-2|A|}+\frac{1}{n-2|B|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e(A,C)+e(B,C)+\frac{n-|A|-|B|}{|C|}e(C)

\displaystyle=|E|-e(A,B)\,.

Together with

\frac{1}{n-2|A|}+\frac{1}{n-2|B|}\geq\frac{4}{(n-2|A|)+(n-2|B|)}=\frac{2}{|C|}

this proves the desired inequality.
∎

X. Liu and J. Ma proved in
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.1 that
e(G)>\frac{1}{4}v(G)^{2}

holds for every extremal graph
G
. In fact, they even obtained such a result
with
\frac{1}{4}
replaced by
\frac{32}{123}
. Utilising Lemma
3.1

one can go slightly further.

Lemma 3.2
.

Every extremal graph
G
on an even number
n
of vertices
has at least
\frac{7}{24}n^{2}
edges.

Proof.

Define
\gamma>0
by
|E(G)|=\gamma n^{2}
and recall that we already know
\gamma>\frac{1}{4}
.
By averaging there exists a vertex
x\in V(G)
satisfying
d(x)\geq 2\gamma n>\frac{1}{2}n
.
Corollary
2.5
leads to two disjoint independent sets

A,B\subseteq N(x)
such that the quantity
t=|A|+|B|
satisfies

(3.1)

t\geq\tfrac{4}{3}d(x)-\tfrac{2}{9}n\geq(\tfrac{8}{3}\gamma-\tfrac{2}{9})n>\tfrac{4}{9}n\,.

First Case:
t<\frac{1}{2}n

For
X=A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B
Lemma
2.6
yields

36e(A,B)\geq 2(n^{2}-9nt+18t^{2})=(3n-6t)^{2}+(18t-7n)n\overset{\eqref{eq:33}}{\geq}(48\gamma-11)n^{2}\,.

Together with Lemma
3.1
this tells us
48\gamma-11\leq 36\gamma-8
, i.e.,

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{4}
, which is absurd.

Second Case:
t\geq\frac{1}{2}n

By Corollary
2.5
applied to
Y=A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B
and by Lemma
3.1

we have

\frac{t}{n-t}\leq\frac{18e(A,B)}{n^{2}}\leq 18\gamma-4\,,

whence

9n\leq 9(n-t)(18\gamma-3)\overset{\eqref{eq:33}}{\leq}(11-24\gamma)(18\gamma-3)n\,,

i.e.,

0\leq(11-24\gamma)(6\gamma-1)-3=2(1-3\gamma)(24\gamma-7)\,,

which proves that
\gamma\in\bigl[\frac{7}{24},\frac{1}{3}\bigr]
.
∎

We conclude this section with a closely related result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices with
\gamma n^{2}
edges.
If
A,B\subseteq V(G)
are two disjoint independent sets, then
|A|+|B|\leq 2\gamma n
. Moreover, the maximum degree of
G
is at most
(\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\frac{1}{6})n
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that
t=|A|+|B|
satisfies
t>2\gamma n\geq\frac{1}{2}n
.
As in the second case of the previous proof one obtains

n\leq(n-t)(18\gamma-3)<(1-2\gamma)(18\gamma-3)n
, which yields the
contradiction
(6\gamma-2)^{2}=1-(1-2\gamma)(18\gamma-3)<0
.

Now for every vertex
x\in V(G)
with
d(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}n
Corollary
2.5

yields
2\gamma n\geq\frac{4}{3}d(x)-\frac{2}{9}n
, whence
d(x)\leq(\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\frac{1}{6})n
.
Due the
\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\frac{1}{6}>\frac{1}{2}
the claim on the maximum degree of
G
follows.
∎

4.
Three independent sets

We proceed with a stability result addressing extremal graphs.
With some additional assumptions on the degree distribution this
was obtained earlier by X. Liu and J. Ma in
[
13
]
*Lemma 4.10.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
G=(V,E)
is an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices.
If
|E|=\gamma n^{2}
, then there
are three mutually disjoint independent sets
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\subseteq V(G)

such that

|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|\geq\frac{n}{3(1-2\gamma)}\,.

Proof.

Recall that Lemma
3.2
yields
\gamma\geq\frac{7}{24}
.
For every edge
xy\in E
we denote the number of triangles containing it by
t_{xy}
.
Similarly, for every vertex
x
we write
t(x)
for the number of triangles containing
x

or, in other words, for the number of edges induced by
N(x)
.

For every non-isolated vertex
x\in V
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\sum_{y\in N(x)}t_{xy}^{2}\geq\frac{4t(x)^{2}}{d(x)}

and in view of Lemma
2.9
we obtain

(4.1)

\sum_{y\in N(x)}t_{xy}^{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{81}\cdot\frac{(1+2\gamma)d(x)-8\gamma^{2}n}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}\,.

This estimate clearly holds for isolated vertices
x\in V
as well.
Summing (
4.1
) over all vertices
x
we learn

\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}^{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{162}\cdot\frac{2(1+2\gamma)\gamma n^{2}-8\gamma^{2}n^{2}}{(1-2\gamma)^{3}}=\frac{\gamma n^{4}}{81(1-2\gamma)^{2}}\,.

Multiplying by

\gamma n^{2}\cdot\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}^{2}\geq\left(\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}\right)^{2}

and simplifying we deduce

\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}^{2}\geq\frac{n}{9(1-2\gamma)}\cdot\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}\,.

Let
T
denote the set of triangles in
G
. Due to
3|T|=\sum_{xy\in E}t_{xy}

the preceding estimate discloses

\sum_{xyz\in T}\bigl(t_{xy}+t_{yz}+t_{zx}\bigr)\geq\frac{n|T|}{3(1-2\gamma)}

and thus there exists some triangle
xyz\in T
such that

t_{xy}+t_{yz}+t_{zx}\geq\frac{n}{3(1-2\gamma)}\,.

Now
V_{1}=N(y)\cap N(z)
,

V_{2}=N(z)\cap N(x)
, and
V_{3}=N(x)\cap N(y)
are the desired independent sets.
∎

The next result occurs implicitly in the work of X. Liu and J. Ma,
or more precisely in their discussion of Case 1 in the proof of
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.9.
For the sake of completeness we include the short argument.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices.
If a partition
V=V_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{3}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Z
has the properties that

V_{i}
is independent and
|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}n

holds for every
i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
, then

|E|\geq\frac{n(n^{2}+n|Z|+|Z|^{2})}{3(n+2|Z|)}\,.

Proof.

Define the real numbers
\gamma
and
z
such that
|E|=\gamma n^{2}
and
|Z|=zn
.
Owing to
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|\geq\frac{3}{4}n
we have
z\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
For every
i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
we can apply Corollary
2.5
to
the set
Y=V_{i}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{i+1}
, thus inferring

e(V_{i},V_{i+1})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{18}\cdot\frac{|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|}{n-|V_{i}|-|V_{i+1}|}\,.

Due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this implies

\displaystyle\phantom{\geq}(n+2|Z|)\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|)\,e(V_{i},V_{i+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{18}\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(n-|V_{i}|-|V_{i+1}|)\times\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\frac{(|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|)^{2}}{n-|V_{i}|-|V_{i+1}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{18}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|)\Bigr)^{2}=\frac{2n^{2}}{9}(n-|Z|)^{2}\,,

whence

(4.2)

\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(|V_{i}|+|V_{i+1}|)\,e(V_{i},V_{i+1})\geq\frac{2n^{3}(1-z)^{2}}{9(1+2z)}\,.

Let us now consider any two distinct indices
i,j\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
.
In view of
{|V_{i-1}|+|V_{i+1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}n}
we
have
|V_{i}|+|Z|\leq\frac{1}{2}n
. Moreover,
every set
Q\subseteq V_{j}
of size
|Q|=\tfrac{1}{2}n-|V_{i}|-|Z|

satisfies

\frac{n^{2}}{18}\leq e(V_{i}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Q\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Z)=e(V_{i},Z)+e(Z)+e(V_{i},Q)+e(Q,Z)\,.

Multiplying by
|V_{j}|
and averaging over
Q
we learn

\frac{n^{2}|V_{j}|}{18}\leq|V_{j}|\bigl(e(V_{i},Z)+e(Z)\bigr)+(\tfrac{1}{2}n-|V_{i}|-|Z|)\bigl(e(V_{i},V_{j})+e(V_{j},Z)\bigr)\,.

As
i
and
j
vary there arise six estimates of this form whose sum simplifies to

\frac{n^{3}(1-z)}{9}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(|V_{i-1}|-|Z|)e(V_{i},V_{i+1})+(n-2|Z|)\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}e(V_{i},Z)+2(n-|Z|)e(Z)\,.

Adding (
4.2
) and taking into account that Turán’s theorem yields

e(Z)\leq\frac{1}{3}|Z|^{2}
we obtain

\frac{n^{3}(1-z)}{9}+\frac{2n^{3}(1-z)^{2}}{9(1+2z)}\leq\frac{n^{3}z^{2}}{3}+(1-2z)\gamma n^{3}\,.

Since
z\leq\frac{1}{4}<\frac{1}{2}
, this is equivalent to

\gamma\geq\frac{1+z+z^{2}}{3(1+2z)}\,.\qed

Our last preparatory result analyses partitions of extremal graphs into
three “almost independent” sets.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an extremal graph on an even number
n
of vertices.
If a partition
V=A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{3}
satisfies

e(A_{1})+e(A_{2})+e(A_{3})=\omega n^{2}
for some
\omega\leq\frac{1}{60}
,
then
|E|\geq(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{29}{18}\omega)n^{2}
.

Proof.

As usual we define
\gamma
such that
|E|=\gamma n^{2}
.
Notice that
e(A_{i})\leq\frac{1}{60}n^{2}
yields
|A_{i}|<\tfrac{1}{2}n

for every
i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
.
If
i,j\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
are distinct and
T\subseteq A_{j}
has size

|T|=\frac{1}{2}n-|A_{i}|
, then

\frac{n^{2}}{18}\leq e(A_{i}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}T)=e(A_{i})+e(A_{i},T)+e(T)\,.

By averaging over
T
and multiplying with
|A_{j}|/(\frac{1}{2}n-|A_{i}|)
we deduce

\frac{|A_{j}|(n^{2}-18e(A_{i}))}{9(n-2|A_{i}|)}\leq e(A_{i},A_{j})+\frac{\frac{1}{2}n-|A_{i}|}{|A_{j}|}e(A_{j})\,.

Again there are six estimates of this form and this time their addition yields

\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\frac{(n-|A_{i}|)(n^{2}-18e(A_{i}))}{9(n-2|A_{i}|)}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\bigl(2e(A_{i},A_{i+1})+e(A_{i})\bigr)\,.

Because of the identical equation

\frac{(n-|A_{i}|)(n^{2}-18e(A_{i}))}{9(n-2|A_{i}|)}+e(A_{i})=\frac{n^{2}}{18}+\frac{n(n^{2}-18e(A_{i}))}{18(n-2|A_{i}|)}

this implies

\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\frac{n^{2}-18e(A_{i})}{n-2|A_{i}|}\leq(36\gamma-3)n\,.

Owing to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\sqrt{n^{2}-18e(A_{i})}\Bigr)^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\frac{n^{2}-18e(A_{i})}{n-2|A_{i}|}\times\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}(n-2|A_{i}|)

and the concavity of the square root entails (by Karamata’s inequality)

(2+\sqrt{1-18\omega})n\leq\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\sqrt{n^{2}-18e(A_{i})}\,.

Altogether we have thereby proved

(2+\sqrt{1-18\omega})^{2}\leq 36\gamma-3\,,

i.e.,
8-18\omega+4\sqrt{1-18\omega}\leq 36\gamma
. Since
\sqrt{1-x}\geq 1-\frac{5}{9}x

holds for all
x\in\bigl[0,\frac{3}{10}\bigr]
, this implies

36\gamma\geq 8-18\omega+4(1-10\omega)=12-58\omega\,,

as desired.
∎

Recall that in Subsection
2.1
we explained why Proposition
2.1

implies Theorem
1.1
. Thus the argument that follows will complete the proof
of our main result.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Fix a partition
V=V_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{3}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Z

such that the sets
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
are independent and
subject to this
|Z|
is as small as possible. Define the real numbers

\gamma
and
z
by
|E|=\gamma n^{2}
and
|Z|=zn
. Owing to the Lemmata
3.2

and
4.1
we already know

(4.3)

\gamma\geq\frac{7}{24}\quad\text{ as well as }\quad z\leq 1-\frac{1}{3(1-2\gamma)}\leq\frac{1}{5}\,.

We proceed by verifying the main hypothesis of Lemma
4.2
.

Claim 4.4
.

If
i,j\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
are distinct, then
|V_{i}|+|V_{j}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}n
.

Proof.

If there are two exceptions, say
|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|<\frac{1}{2}n
and
|V_{1}|+|V_{3}|<\frac{1}{2}n
,
then

\displaystyle|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|

\displaystyle=2n-2|Z|-(|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|)-(|V_{1}|+|V_{3}|)

\displaystyle>n-2|Z|\overset{\eqref{eq:0027}}{\geq}\frac{2n}{3(1-2\gamma)}-n\overset{\eqref{eq:0027}}{\geq}2\gamma n

contradicts Lemma
3.3
.

So by symmetry it suffices to refute that
|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|<\frac{1}{2}n
, while

|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|,|V_{1}|+|V_{3}|\geq\frac{1}{2}n
.
Now every set
P\subseteq V_{2}
of size
|P|=\frac{1}{2}n-|V_{1}|
satisfies

e(P,V_{1})\geq\frac{1}{18}n^{2}
and by averaging we infer

e(V_{1},V_{2})\geq\frac{|V_{2}|n^{2}}{9(n-2|V_{1}|)}\,.

A similar estimate holds with
V_{3}
instead of
V_{2}
. Adding them both
and combining the result with Lemma
3.1
we get

\frac{|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|}{n-2|V_{1}|}\leq 18\gamma-4\,.

Intending to solve this for
t=|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|
we rewrite the denominator as

2t-(1-2z)n
and obtain
(1-2z)(18\gamma-4)n\leq 9(4\gamma-1)t
.
In view of
t<\frac{1}{2}n
and (
4.3
) this implies

\frac{(6\gamma-1)(18\gamma-4)}{3(1-2\gamma)}<\frac{9(4\gamma-1)}{2}\,,

which rewrites as
(24\gamma-7)(18\gamma-5)<0
.
This contradiction to
\gamma\geq\frac{7}{24}
concludes the proof of Claim
4.4
.
∎

Now Lemma
4.2
tells us that

(4.4)

\gamma\geq\frac{1+z+z^{2}}{3(1+2z)}\,.

Our next goal is to derive an upper bound on
\gamma
in terms of
z
.
To this end we shall estimate the degrees of the vertices in
V_{i}
as follows.

Claim 4.5
.

If
i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}
and
x_{i}\in V_{i}
, then

d(x_{i})\leq\frac{n}{3}+\frac{5(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)}{8}-\frac{n^{2}}{18(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)}\,.

Proof.

In terms of the function
h(t)=\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{5}{8}t-\frac{n^{2}}{18t}
, which
is defined for all
t>0
, we are to prove
d(x_{i})\leq h(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)
.
Since
t
is increasing, Claim
4.4
tells us

h(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)\geq h(\tfrac{1}{2}n)>\tfrac{1}{2}n

and thus we may assume that
d(x_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}n
. Due to Lemma
2.2

there are two disjoint independent sets
V^{\prime}_{i-1},V^{\prime}_{i+1}\subseteq N(x_{i})
such that

t^{\prime}=|V^{\prime}_{i-1}|+|V^{\prime}_{i+1}|
satisfies
d(x_{i})\leq h(t^{\prime})
. As
V_{i}
is independent, the
sets
V_{i}
,
V^{\prime}_{i-1}
, and
V^{\prime}_{i+1}
are mutually disjoint and thus the minimality
of
|Z|
implies
t^{\prime}\leq|V_{i-1}|+|V_{i+1}|
. So the monotonicity of
h
yields
indeed
d(x_{i})\leq h(|V_{i-1}|+|V_{i+1}|)
.
∎

Taking into account that due to Lemma
3.3
the vertices in
Z
have degree
at most
(\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\frac{1}{6})n
we now obtain

\displaystyle 2\gamma n^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V}d(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}|V_{i}|\left(\frac{n}{3}+\frac{5(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)}{8}-\frac{n^{2}}{18(|V_{i+1}|+|V_{i-1}|)}\right)+|Z|(\tfrac{3}{2}\gamma+\tfrac{1}{6})n\,.

In view of
|V_{1}||V_{2}|+|V_{2}||V_{3}|+|V_{3}||V_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{3}(n-|Z|)^{2}
and Nesbitt’s
inequality

\frac{|V_{1}|}{|V_{2}|+|V_{3}|}+\frac{|V_{2}|}{|V_{3}|+|V_{1}|}+\frac{|V_{3}|}{|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|}\geq\frac{3}{2}

this leads us to

(2-\tfrac{3}{2}z)\gamma\leq\tfrac{1}{3}(1-z)+\tfrac{5}{12}(1-z)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{12}+\tfrac{1}{6}z=(2-\tfrac{3}{2}z)(\tfrac{1}{3}-\tfrac{z}{4})+\tfrac{1}{24}z^{2}\,,

whence

(4.5)

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{z}{4}+\frac{z^{2}}{48-36z}\overset{\eqref{eq:0027}}{\leq}\frac{1}{3}-\frac{z}{4}+\frac{z^{2}}{36}\,.

Together with (
4.4
) this demonstrates

\frac{1+z+z^{2}}{3(1+2z)}\leq\frac{1}{3}-\frac{z}{4}+\frac{z^{2}}{48-36z}

and thus

0\leq z(2-21z+16z^{2})\,,

which is easily verified to imply

(4.6)

z\leq\tfrac{3}{29}

(or
z>1
, but this would contradict (
4.3
)). Next we plan to split
Z

into three parts and to adjoin these to
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
, thus creating the
situation considered in Lemma
4.3
. Of course it is recommendable to move every
vertex
z\in Z
into a vertex class
V_{i}
it has only few neighbours in and the
subsequent claim will help us with the analysis of this process.

Claim 4.6
.

If
x\in Z
, then
\min\bigl\{|N(x)\cap V_{i}|\colon i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}\bigr\}\leq\frac{2}{17}n

Proof.

Assume contrariwise that each of the three sets
K_{i}=N(x)\cap V_{i}
satisfies

|K_{i}|>\frac{2}{17}n
. By Corollary
2.7
we have
|V_{i+1}|\leq\frac{1}{3}n
for
every

and, hence, there exists a set
L_{i}\subseteq K_{i}

such that
\frac{2}{17}n<|L_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}n-|V_{i+1}|

4.4
shows that there is a set

e(M_{i}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}V_{i+1})\geq\frac{1}{18}n^{2}

Summing all three such estimates and

\sum_{i\in\mathds{Z}/3\mathds{Z}}\frac{|L_{i}||L_{i+1}|-e(L_{i},L_{i+1})}{|L_{i}||L_{i+1}|}<\frac{289}{4}\left(\frac{1-z}{2}-\frac{(1-z)^{2}}{3}-\frac{1}{6}\right)=\frac{289z(1-2z)}{24}\overset{\eqref{eq:45}}{<}1

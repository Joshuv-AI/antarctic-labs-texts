Abstract
We re-analyse the bulk reconstruction for a scalar field in Lorentzian AdS spacetime, both for the case of even and odd dimensions, for an extended range of conformal dimensions where the original HKLL reconstruction has to be modified. We also discuss the use of space-like Green’s functions in the bulk reconstruction. We demonstrate that in the extended range also the singular part of the Green’s function, omitted in the original papers, has be included. The results are particularly simple and physically interesting for integer conformal dimensions below the range considered in the original HKLL papers.



1
Introduction

The AdS/CFT correspondence
[
2
,
3
]

relates a theory of gravity in AdS space to a conformal field
theory on the boundary.
One consequence of the correspondence is that bulk quantum fields
can be expressed as CFT operators.
In the large N limit the bulk fields are free and can be written
as smeared CFT operators.
The explicit construction, called the HKLL (Hamilton, Kabat,
Lifschytz, and Lowe)
bulk reconstruction, was accomplished in a series of papers

[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
In the simplest case a massive free scalar field operator
\Phi(Y)

is considered in
{\rm AdS}_{d+1}
.
The HKLL bulk reconstruction represents it in terms of the
boundary CFT primary of weight
\Delta
,
O(x)
, as

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{Y}}{\rm d}x\,K(Y,x)O(x),

(1)

where
K(Y,x)
is a smearing function, and the integration at the
boundary should be performed in a region
\Sigma_{Y}
space-like separated
from the bulk point
Y
. We refer to
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
for recent reviews.
See also
[
10
]
for an alternative derivation based
on Gel’fand-Graev-Radon transforms.
Later the reconstruction has been extended to higher spins
as well
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. After having constructed the
free case the next step is to study bulk interactions
[
16
]
.
An elegant way to introduce interactions as well as to reproduce the bulk
reconstruction for free fields is the method based on space-like Green’s functions

[
5
,
17
]
.

In the original papers it was not explicitly stated that
(
1
) holds only for
\Delta>d-1
, due to the convergence for the integral.
This restriction is not essential for applications of the AdS/CFT correspondence in the case of
supersymmetric gauge theories, in particular in the prime example of the
{\cal N}=4
SUSY U
(N)

gauge theory in
d=4
dimensions,
since the conformal dimensions of
physically relevant operators are typically (much) larger
than this lower bound
\Delta>d-1
. See however
[
18
]
for some explicit examples for
small
\Delta
primaries in the AdS/CFT context.
More importantly there exists an other family of models often
used in the AdS/CFT context, namely
O
(N)
vector models and their holographic
duals, higher spin theories in the bulk
[
19
,
20
]
.
In the most interesting
d=3
case, the simplest singlet operator
has
\Delta=1
(
d-2
).
Furthermore, its square, an operator which can be used as a relevant deformation, has

\Delta=2
(
d-1
).
The HKLL formula (
1
) can not be used to relate
these singlet scalar operators in the free
O(N)
vector model to bulk operators.

It turned out
[
6
]
that for the special case
\Delta=d-1

the smearing function in Poincare coordinates is supported on the intersection of the light-cone of the bulk point and the
boundary. In
[
21
]

the range of allowed
\Delta
values was extended down to
\Delta>d/2
by analytic
continuation.
While the bulk-boundary relation remains linear,
the smearing kernel in (
1
) is replaced by a suitable distribution.

In
[
1
]
we found, in some special cases mainly concentrating
on the (simpler) case of even AdS spaces, a
generalized HKLL formula for
\Delta
values below the original lower
bound
d-1
by a direct derivation, without using analytic continuation.
When we explicitly evaluated the results of

[
21
]
, we found that they precisely agree with the results
of the direct calculation in the range where they overlap. We also discussed the interesting special cases

\Delta=d-s
, where
s
is a positive integer only
limited by the requirement that the conformal weight satisfies the unitarity
bound
\Delta\geq(d-2)/2
(equality holds for the free scalar theory).
In these integer
\Delta
cases the bulk operator
\Phi(Y)
is expressed
in terms of CFT operators living on

\Sigma_{Y}^{(0)}
(boundary points light-like separated from
Y
).

In this paper we carefully re-analyse the HKLL bulk reconstruction, both for
the case of even and odd AdS spaces (odd and even boundary manifolds),
paying special attention to the range of conformal dimensions where the
construction is valid (not emphasized in the original HKLL papers).

After a setup for the HKLL bulk reconstruction in section
2
, we consider the case of even and odd
{\rm AdS}_{d+1}

(odd and even
d
) in sections
3
and
4
, respectively and first recall the very well-known HKLL bulk
reconstruction
[
4
,
5
]
for a massive
free scalar boson field with conformal weight
\Delta>d-1
in each section.
The purpose of this
review is to introduce our notations and conventions,
which will be needed later in the paper when we extend the validity
of the construction to smaller values of
\Delta
.
We then recall some pertinent results from
[
1
]
in both sections
3
and
4
before discussing
explicit reconstruction formulas for the regions
d-1>\Delta>d-2
and

d-2>\Delta>d-3
.
Some detailed calculations and necessary properties are summarized in several appendices.
In addition, in appendices G and H, we discuss the use of space-like Green’s functions in the bulk reconstruction.
We demonstrate that this alternative method correctly reproduces the same results but in the extended range also
the singular part of the Green’s function, omitted in the original paper
[
5
]
, has to be included.

2
Setup for the HKLL bulk reconstruction in AdS
d+1

The HKLL bulk reconstruction
[
4
,
5
]
starts with a free scalar operator
\Phi(t,\rho,n)
on the
d+1
dimensional global AdS spacetime, whose metric is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{2}d\rho^{2}-R^{2}\cosh^{2}\rho\,dt^{2}+R^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho\,dn^{i}dn^{i},

(2)

where
R
is the AdS radius and
Y=(t,\rho,n^{i})
with
n\cdot n=1
(or
Y=(t,\rho,\Omega)
) are the standard global coordinates of AdS
d+1
.

The value of
\Phi
at the middle of the AdS,
Y_{o}=(0,0,n)
, is expressed as (see appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{o})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(1)+D_{1}(1),\quad D(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}z^{n},\ D_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}^{\dagger}z^{n},

(3)

where
d_{n}
and
d_{n}^{\dagger}
are (rescaled) annihilation and creation operators, and
\Delta
is related to the mass of the free scalar
m
as

m^{2}R^{2}=\Delta(\Delta-d)
.
On the other hand, using the BDHM relation

\displaystyle O(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\Delta}\Phi(\tilde{t},\rho,\tilde{n}),

(4)

where
O(x)
is a CFT operator with conformal dimension
\Delta
at the AdS boundary
x=(\tilde{t},\tilde{n})
with
\tilde{n}\cdot\tilde{n}=1
,
we have

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\tilde{t})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}\tilde{\Omega}\,O(\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega})=e^{-i\Delta\tilde{t}}B(-e^{-2i\tilde{t}})+e^{i\Delta\tilde{t}}B_{1}(-e^{2i\tilde{t}}),

(6)

\displaystyle B(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n},\quad B_{1}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}^{\dagger}z^{n},\

and
b_{n},b_{n}^{\dagger}
are related to
d_{n},d_{n}^{\dagger}
as

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Omega_{d}{P_{n}(1+\alpha)\over P_{n}(d/2)}d_{n},\quad b_{n}^{\dagger}:=\Omega_{d}{P_{n}(1+\alpha)\over P_{n}(d/2)}d_{n}^{\dagger},

(7)

where

\alpha:=\Delta-d/2
,
P_{n}(z):=\Gamma(z+n)/\Gamma(z)
is the Pochhammer symbol, and
\Omega_{d}=\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}

is the volume of the
d
dimensional unit sphere.

The HKLL bulk reconstruction goes as follows. First a relation between
\Phi(Y_{o})
and
O(x)
is derived, then
\Phi(Y)
is obtained using the AdS
isometry
g
and associated unitary operator
U(g)
as
\Phi(Y)=U^{\dagger}(g)\Phi(Y_{o})U(g)
, where
Y=g^{-1}Y_{o}
is a generic point in the AdS space.

We first consider the bulk reconstruction for the odd

3.1
Results of the original HKLL bulk reconstruction

In papers by HKLL
[
4
,
5
]
, a relation between
\Phi(Y_{o})

has been derived for odd

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{o})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\int{\cal D}x\,k_{0}(\tilde{t})\Theta(\pi/2-\tilde{t})\Theta(\tilde{t}+\pi/2)O(x),

(8)

where

\displaystyle\xi:={1\over\pi\Omega_{d}}{\Gamma(1-d/2)\Gamma(1+\alpha)\over\Gamma(\nu+1)},\quad k_{0}(u):=(2\cos u)^{\nu},\quad\nu:=\Delta-d,

(9)

The convergence of the
\tilde{t}

\tilde{t}=\pm\pi/2

\nu>-1
. Thus the condition
\Delta>d-1
is (implicitly) assumed for the original HKLL bulk reconstruction.

A relation at a generic point

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\int{\cal D}x\,I^{\nu}(Y,x)T(Y,x)O(x),

(10)

where

\displaystyle I(Y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2[\cosh(\rho)\cos(t-\tilde{t})-(\sinh\rho)n^{i}\tilde{n}^{i}]\quad T(Y,x)=\Theta(T_{2}-\tilde{t})\Theta(\tilde{t}-T_{1}),

(11)

where
T_{1}=t-\omega,T_{2}=t+\omega
with
\omega=\arccos[(\tanh\rho)n\cdot\tilde{n}]
and
0<\omega<\pi
. Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho,t\to 0}I^{\nu}(Y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{0}(\tilde{t}),\quad\lim_{\rho,t\to 0}T(Y,x)=\Theta(\pi/2-\tilde{t})\Theta(\tilde{t}+\pi/2).

(12)

3.2
Results of Ref.

In our previous paper

for

(17)

for

In the previous paper we have not derived a formula for

Results at the special points

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

(18)

for
\Delta=d-1

{\cal R}(Y,x)=\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\rho-(n\cdot\tilde{n})^{2}\sinh^{2}\rho}

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

Bulk reconstruction for the extended range

In this subsection we derive a bulk reconstruction formula for a generic bulk point for

3.3.1
Formula at a generic point by partial integration

Since it is not easy to transform (
13
) to a generic bulk point by the AdS isometry, we take a different strategy and we start from (
10
), which is first rewritten by partial integration as

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)=\eta_{o}(2\cosh\rho)^{\nu}\int{\rm d}\tilde{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left\{-{1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{-\omega}^{\omega}{\rm d}\tilde{t}\,\phi_{1}(\tilde{t})\dot{O}(t+\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega})\right.

(20)

\displaystyle+\left.K_{1}(\nu,\omega)\left[O(T_{1},\tilde{\Omega})+O(T_{2},\tilde{\Omega})\right]\right\},

where
\eta_{o}:=\Gamma(\nu+1)\xi={\Gamma(1-d/2)\over\pi\Omega_{d}}\Gamma(1+\alpha)
,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}{\rm d}v\,\phi_{0}(v),\ \phi_{0}(u):=(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu},\ K_{1}(\nu,\omega)={\phi_{1}(\omega)\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}.~~~~

(21)

Since
\phi_{1}(u)\sim(\omega-|u|)^{\nu+1}
at

, the
\tilde{t}
integral is convergent for
\nu>-2
.

Performing a second integration by parts, (
20
) becomes

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{o}(2\cosh\rho)^{\nu}\int{\rm d}\tilde{\Omega}\left\{{1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{-\omega}^{\omega}{\rm d}\tilde{t}\,\phi_{2}(\tilde{t})\ddot{O}(t+\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega})\right.

(22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.K_{2}(\nu,\omega)\left[\dot{O}(T_{1},\tilde{\Omega})-\dot{O}(T_{2},\tilde{\Omega})\right]+K_{1}(\nu,\omega)\left[O(T_{1},\tilde{\Omega})+O(T_{2},\tilde{\Omega})\right]\right\},

where

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\,{\rm d}v(u-v)\phi_{0}(v),\ \phi_{2}^{\prime}(u)=\phi_{1}(u),\ K_{2}(\nu,\omega)={\phi_{2}(\omega)\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}.

(23)

The
\tilde{t}
integral in this expression is convergent for
\nu>-3
.

Although we do not need to go further for later analysis,
we can repeat the procedure to obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{o}(2\cosh\rho)^{\nu}\int{\rm d}\tilde{\Omega}\left\{{(-1)^{k}\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{-\omega}^{\omega}{\rm d}\tilde{t}\,\phi_{k}(\tilde{t})O^{(k)}(t+\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega})\right.

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{\phi_{\ell}(\omega)\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\left[O^{(\ell-1)}(T_{1},\tilde{\Omega})+(-1)^{\ell-1}O^{(\ell-1)}(T_{2},\tilde{\Omega})\right]\right\}

for an arbitrary positive integer

, where

\displaystyle\phi_{\ell}(u):={1\over(\ell-1)!}\int_{0}^{u}{\rm d}v\,(u-v)^{\ell-1}\phi_{0}(v),\quad O^{(\ell)}(u,\tilde{\Omega}):={\partial^{\ell}\over\partial u^{\ell}}O(u,\tilde{\Omega}).

(25)

The
\tilde{t}
integral is convergent for
\nu>-(k+1)
.

3.3.2
Analytic continuation of
K_{1}(\nu,\omega)

While the
\tilde{t}
integral in (

) is convergent for
\nu>-2
,
we must show that
K_{1}(\nu,\omega)
is convergent for
\nu>-2
.
Using a limiting case of (3.663-1) in the table of integrals by Gradshteyn and Ryzhik,
K_{1}(\nu,\omega)
can be evaluated for
\nu>-1
as

\displaystyle K_{1}(\nu,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}2^{\nu}\left(\sin{\omega\over 2}\right)^{2\nu+1}{1\over\Gamma(\nu+3/2)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left({1\over 2},{1\over 2};\nu+{3\over 2};\sin^{2}{\omega\over 2}\right).

(26)

Since the Gamma function in the denominator of (

) regularizes the hypergeometric function, (
26
)
can be analytically continued to all
\nu
.
The integral part is convergent for
\nu>-2
and therefore (
20
) provides the analytic extension of the bulk reconstruction to the range
\nu>-2
.

3.3.3
Analytic continuation of
K_{2}(\nu,\omega)

Since the
\tilde{t}
integral in (
22
) is convergent for
\nu>-3
and we have already seen that
K_{1}(\nu,\omega)
is analytic for all
\nu
,
we now concentrate on the integral

\displaystyle K_{2}(\nu,\omega)=\omega K_{1}(\nu,\omega)-J_{1}(\nu,\omega),

(27)

where

\displaystyle J_{1}(\nu,\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u\,u(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu},

which, unfortunately, can not be found in integral tables.

In the absence of an explicit formula for (

The integrals related to the right hand side of (

\nu>-2
and so this relation can be used to extend the left hand side

\nu>-2

to
\nu>-3

way we can extend, step by step,

for all
\nu
.
Therefore, (

K_{2}(\nu,\omega)
is analytic for all
\nu
.
Since the
\tilde{t}
integral is convergent for
\nu>-3
, and
K_{1,2}(\nu,\omega)
are analytic for all
\nu
,

\Phi(Y)
in (

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{o}\int{d\tilde{\Omega}\over{\cal R}(Y,x)}\left[O(T_{1},\tilde{\Omega})+O(T_{2},\tilde{\Omega})\right],

(35)

\displaystyle\Phi(Y)

\displaystyle=

4.1
Results of the original HKLL bulk reconstruction

Appendix A
Fock space representation of the bulk and boundary fields

The construction of a massive bulk scalar field and the corresponding
boundary field with conformal weight
\Delta>d/2-1
was reviewed in
[
1
]
.
Here we recall some formulas which will be used in this paper.

A free bulk scalar field

canonical creation and annihilation operators

and
{\cal A}_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}

\Phi(t,\rho,\Omega)=\sum_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}\sqrt{\frac{{\cal N}R}{2\nu_{n\ell}}}\Big\{u_{n\ell}(\rho)Y_{\ell{\underline{m}}}(\Omega){\cal A}_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}\,{\rm e}^{-i\nu_{n\ell}t}+u_{n\ell}(\rho)Y_{\ell{\underline{m}}}(\Omega){\cal A}_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}^{\dagger}\,{\rm e}^{i\nu_{n\ell}t}\Big\},

(72)

where
{\cal N}
is a normalization constant related to the free Lagrangian,

R
is the AdS radius,
\nu_{n\ell}=\Delta+\ell+2n
is the eigenfrequency,

u_{n\ell}(\rho)
is the radial wave function, and
Y_{\ell{\underline{m}}}(\Omega)

are hyper-spherical harmonics for the
d-1
dimensional sphere parametrized
alternatively by
the angular variables
\Omega
or by the
d
dimensional unit vector
n^{i}
.
Note that for simplicity we are using real hyper-spherical harmonics.

The field at the middle point, (
3
), can be expressed with the
rescaled Fock space operator,

d_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{{\cal N}R}{2\nu_{n0}}}\,(-1)^{n}\frac{P_{n}(d/2)}{n!}{\cal N}_{n0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega_{d}}}{\cal A}_{n0{\underline{0}}}.

(73)

The explicit value of the normalization constant
{\cal N}_{n0}
is not needed
in our calculation.

The BDHM relation
[
22
]
gives
the boundary field
O(\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega})
of conformal weight
\Delta
as

\displaystyle O(\tilde{t},\tilde{\Omega}):=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\left(\sinh\rho\right)^{\Delta}\Phi(\tilde{t},\rho,\tilde{\Omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}\sqrt{\frac{{\cal N}R}{2\nu_{n\ell}}}\Big\{{\rm e}^{-i\nu_{n\ell}\tilde{t}}\,\frac{P_{n}(1+\alpha)}{n!}{\cal N}_{n\ell}Y_{\ell{\underline{m}}}(\tilde{\Omega}){\cal A}_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}

(74)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{i\nu_{n\ell}\tilde{t}}\,\frac{P_{n}(1+\alpha)}{n!}{\cal N}_{n\ell}Y_{\ell{\underline{m}}}(\tilde{\Omega}){\cal A}_{n\ell{\underline{m}}}^{\dagger}\Big\}.

The integrated boundary field (

b_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{{\cal N}R}{2\nu_{n0}}}\,(-1)^{n}\frac{P_{n}(1+\alpha)}{n!}{\cal N}_{n0}\sqrt{\Omega_{d}}{\cal A}_{n0{\underline{0}}}=\Omega_{d}\,\frac{P_{n}(1+\alpha)}{P_{n}(d/2)}d_{n}.

(75)

Appendix B

B.1

For odd

Appendix F
Recursion relations

F.1
Recursion relation for
J_{1}(\nu,\omega)

Using the simple identity

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}(\cos u+u\sin u)=u\,\cos u

(182)

we can perform a partial integration in the definition of
J_{1}(\nu,\omega)

as follows. Here we require that
\nu>1
so that all subsequent manipulations
are well-defined.

\displaystyle J_{1}(\nu,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u(u\cos u-u\cos\omega)(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-1}

(183)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cos\omega\over\nu}J_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)-{(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-1}\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\nu-1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u(\cos u+u\sin u)(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-2}\sin u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cos\omega\over\nu}J_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)-{(1-\cos\omega)^{\nu-1}\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}+{\nu-1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u\big\{u\sin^{2}u(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-2}\big\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\nu-1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u\,\cos u(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-2}\sin u.

In the next to last line the integral, using
\sin^{2}u=1-\cos^{2}u
, can be
represented as

\Gamma(\nu-1)\sin^{2}\omega J_{1}(\nu-2,\omega)-\Gamma(\nu+1)J_{1}(\nu,\omega)-2\cos\omega\Gamma(\nu)J_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)

(184)

The integral in the last line can be done explicitly and gives

\frac{1}{\nu}(1-\cos\omega)^{\nu}+\frac{\cos\omega}{\nu-1}(1-\cos\omega)^{\nu-1}.

(185)

Putting everything together, after some simplifications we get

\sin^{2}\omega J_{1}(\nu-2,\omega)=\nu^{2}J_{1}(\nu,\omega)+(2\nu-1)\cos\omega J_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}(1-\cos\omega)^{\nu}.

(186)

Making the shift
\nu\rightarrow\nu+2
(so that the result is now valid
for
\nu>-1
), we finally arrive at the recursion (
29
).

F.2
Recursion relation for
P_{1}(\nu,\omega)

Using integration by parts we obtain

\displaystyle P_{1}(\nu,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u\,(\cos u-\cos\omega)(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-1}\ln(\cos u-\cos\omega)

(187)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cos\omega\over\nu}P_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)+\int_{0}^{\omega}{\rm d}u\,{\sin^{2}u\over\Gamma(\nu+1)}\Big\{(\nu-1)(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-2}\ln(\cos u-\cos\omega)

\displaystyle+(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{\nu-2}\Big\},

and use the identity

\sin^{2}u=\sin^{2}\omega-2\cos\omega(\cos u-\cos\omega)-(\cos u-\cos\omega)^{2}

(188)

to get

\displaystyle P_{1}(\nu,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cos\omega\over\nu}P_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)+\sin^{2}\omega\left[{P_{1}(\nu-2,\omega)\over\nu}+{K_{1}(\nu-2,\omega)\over\nu(\nu-1)}\right]

(189)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{2\cos\omega\over\nu}\left[(\nu-1)P_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)+K_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)\right]-(\nu-1)P_{1}(\nu,\omega)-K_{1}(\nu,\omega).~~~~~~~

&+2\cos\omega K_{1}(\nu-1,\omega)+\nu K_{1}(\nu,\omega)-\frac{\sin^{2}\omega}{\nu-1}K_{1}(\nu-2,\omega).\end{split}

(190)

Finally we make the shift

57

Appendix G
AdS Green’s functions

In this appendix we construct the space-like Green’s function in AdS space,
which will be useful (see appendix
H
) in an alternative method

[
5
,
17
]
of the bulk reconstruction. Here, for
simplicity, we will restrict our considerations to the range

A Green’s function of the massive scalar wave equation satisfies

({\cal D}-m^{2}){\cal G}(Y,Y^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\delta(Y,Y^{\prime}),\quad{\cal D}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_{\alpha}(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}),

(191)

where the Laplacian
{\cal D}
acts on
Y

and the mass is parametrized as
m^{2}=\Delta(\Delta-d)
(from now on we are

The metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
(and its determinant

and inverse

g^{\alpha\beta}

is encoded by the line element

{\rm d}s^{2}=({\rm d}\rho)^{2}-\cosh^{2}\rho\,({\rm d}t)^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho\,{\rm d}n^{i}{\rm d}n^{i}

(192)

{\rm d}s^{2}=-(1+y^{2})({\rm d}t)^{2}+\big(\delta_{ij}-\frac{y^{i}y^{j}}{1+y^{2}}\big){\rm d}y^{i}{\rm d}y^{j}

(193)

In the Green’s function method the AdS invariant (

(196)

\begin{split}y>&0:\ \ \Big[\frac{\omega^{2}}{1+y^{2}}+\Delta(d-\Delta)\Big]H(\omega,y)+\partial_{i}\left[{y^{i}\over y}\big(1+y^{2}\big)\partial_{y}H(\omega,y)\right]=0,\\

Appendix H

The starting point here is the identity involving the Green’s function

\sigma
in this case. Therefore,

\partial_{\rho}\overline{G}(\sigma)=\cos t\sinh R\,\overline{G}^{\prime}(\sigma)\approx\sigma\overline{G}^{\prime}(\sigma)

(226)

for large
R
, and we can write

\Phi(Y_{o})=\lim_{R\to\infty}(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-t_{1}}^{t_{1}}{\rm d}tC(t)[\sigma\overline{G}^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta\overline{G}(\sigma)].

(227)

Let us now separate the delta function and theta function parts of the
representation in (
227
) as

\Phi(Y_{o})=\lim_{R\to\infty}(\Phi_{0}+\Phi_{1}),

(228)

where

\Phi_{0}=(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-t_{1}}^{t_{1}}{\rm d}tC(t)[\sigma G_{\rm s}^{\prime}(x)+\Delta G_{\rm s}(x)+\pi c\delta(x)]

(229)

with
h(0)=J(1)=1
, and

\Phi_{1}=\pi c(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-t_{o}}^{t_{o}}{\rm d}tC(t)[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)].

(230)

The range of the
t
integral becomes
[-t_{o},t_{o}]
due to
\Theta(x)
, and we introduce
t_{o}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon_{o}
, where

\sin\varepsilon_{o}=1/\cosh R\to 0
for large
R
.

For technical reasons, we will now divide
\Phi_{1}
into two parts,
\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{1a}+\Phi_{1b}
,
where

\Phi_{1a}=\pi c(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-\pi/2+\varepsilon}^{\pi/2-\varepsilon}{\rm d}tC(t)[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)],

(231)

and

\begin{split}\Phi_{1b}=\pi c(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\Big\{\int_{\pi/2-\varepsilon}^{\pi/2-\varepsilon_{o}}{\rm d}t&C(t)[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)]\\
&+\int_{-\pi/2+\varepsilon_{o}}^{-\pi/2+\varepsilon}{\rm d}tC(t)[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)]\Big\}.\end{split}

(232)

Here
\varepsilon
is a small but fixed parameter, while
\varepsilon_{o}

tends to zero as
R\to\infty
. We will let
\varepsilon\to 0
at the end of
the calculation.

H.1
Calculation of
\Phi_{1a}

As discussed in appendix
I
,
J(\sigma)
has a power-law behaviour
for large
\sigma
if we restrict our considerations to
\Delta>d/2
, which is relevant only for
d=3
with an odd
d
since
\Delta>d-2
.
Using the asymptotics (see appendix

I
)

\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)\approx(\nu+\Delta)G_{o}\sigma^{\nu}

(233)

for
\sigma>\sin\varepsilon\cosh R\to\infty
,
we have

\Phi_{1a}\approx\pi cG_{o}(2\alpha)(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-\pi/2+\varepsilon}^{\pi/2-\varepsilon}{\rm d}t\,C(t)(\cos t\cosh R)^{\nu}=2^{\nu}\xi\int_{-\pi/2+\varepsilon}^{\pi/2-\varepsilon}{\rm d}t\,C(t)(\cos t)^{\nu}.

(234)

In the following we will continue the calculation for the cases:
\cal A

\nu>-1
,
\cal B

\nu=-1
,
\cal C

-1>\nu>-2
,
separately. For the simplest case,

\framebox{$\cal A$}\quad\Phi_{1a}\approx\xi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}t\,C(t)(2\cos t)^{\nu},

(235)

since the integral is convergent for
\varepsilon\to 0
in this range, while

\cal B

\Phi_{1a}=0
,
since
\xi
vanishes in this spacial case. In the most complicated case
we use a partial integration and obtain

\begin{split}\framebox{$\cal C$}\quad\Phi_{1a}&=-2^{\nu}\xi\int_{-\pi/2+\varepsilon}^{\pi/2-\varepsilon}{\rm d}t\,\dot{C}(t)g_{1}(t)+2^{\nu}\xi\big[C(\pi/2-\varepsilon)+C(-\pi/2+\varepsilon)\big]g_{1}(\pi/2-\varepsilon)\\
&\approx-2^{\nu}\xi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}t\,\dot{C}(t)g_{1}(t)+2^{\nu}\xi C_{+}(\varepsilon)\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}u(\sin u)^{\nu},\end{split}

(236)

where
g_{1}(t)
is the primitive function

g_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}u(\cos u)^{\nu}.

(237)

The last integral can be evaluated as (see appendix
I
)

\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}u(\sin u)^{\nu}=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}u[(\sin u)^{\nu}-u^{\nu}]+\frac{1}{\nu+1}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{\nu+1}-\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu+1}}{\nu+1}\approx\tilde{g}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu+1}}{\nu+1}.

(238)

Putting elements of this calculation together, we finally obtain

\Phi_{1a}=\xi\int_{\rm(sub)}{\rm d}t\,(2\cos t)^{\nu}\,C(t)+\frac{\eta}{2\Omega_{d}}C_{+}(\varepsilon)-2^{\nu}\xi C_{+}(\varepsilon)\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu+1}}{\nu+1},

(239)

where the first term is written in terms of the subtracted integral, defined in (
15
), by
reversing the partial integration.

H.2
Calculation of
\Phi_{1b}

As a first step, we simplify
\Phi_{1b}
as follows:

\Phi_{1b}\approx\pi cC_{+}(0)(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{1}^{M}\frac{{\rm d}\sigma}{\sqrt{(\cosh R)^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}\big[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)\big],

(240)

where the upper limit
M=\sin\varepsilon\cosh R
is large.
Next using

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(\cosh R)^{2}}}}-1\leq\frac{1}{\cos\varepsilon}-1={\rm O}(\varepsilon^{2})

(241)

we can further approximate
\Phi_{1b}
as

\Phi_{1b}\approx\pi cC_{+}(\varepsilon)(\cosh R)^{-(\nu+1)}{\cal F}(\sin\varepsilon\cosh R),\quad{\cal F}(M):=\int_{1}^{M}{\rm d}\sigma\big[\sigma J^{\prime}(\sigma)+\Delta J(\sigma)\big].

(242)

Using the asymptotic formula
J(\sigma)\approx G_{o}\sigma^{\nu}
again, we obtain

{\cal F}^{\prime}(M)\approx(\nu+\Delta)G_{o}M^{\nu}\longrightarrow{\cal F}(M)\approx\frac{2\alpha G_{o}}{\nu+1}M^{\nu+1}+{\rm const.}\ ,

(243)

with which
\Phi_{1b}
is evaluated case by case as before.
In the first case the constant term
is subleading and we obtain

\framebox{$\cal A$}\quad\Phi_{1b}\approx\frac{2^{\nu}\xi}{\nu+1}(\sin\varepsilon)^{\nu+1}C_{+}(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0,

(244)

while
\cal B

\Phi_{1b}\approx 0\ \ (c=0)

for the next case.
For the last case, the constant term dominates and the
M^{\nu+1}

contribution is subleading, so that
we obtain

\framebox{$\cal C$}\quad\Phi_{1b}\approx\frac{2^{\nu}\xi}{\nu+1}(\sin\varepsilon)^{\nu+1}C_{+}(0)+\pi cC_{+}(0)(\cosh R)^{-(\nu+1)}{\cal F}(\infty).

(245)

where the results in appendix
I
gives

{\cal F}(\infty)=-1+(\Delta-1)\int_{1}^{\infty}{\rm d}\sigma J(\sigma)=-1-\frac{d-1}{\nu+1}.

(246)

We can now add up the contributions
\Phi_{1a}
and
\Phi_{1b}
and find

\framebox{$\cal A$}\quad\Phi_{1}=\xi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}tC(t)(2\cos t)^{\nu},

(247)

\framebox{$\cal B$}\quad\Phi_{1}=0,

(248)

\framebox{$\cal C$}\quad\Phi_{1}=\xi\int_{\rm(sub)}{\rm d}tC(t)(2\cos t)^{\nu}+\frac{\eta}{2\Omega_{d}}C_{+}(0)+\pi cC_{+}(0){\cal F}(\infty)(\cosh R)^{-(\nu+1)}.

(249)

H.3
Calculation of
\Phi_{0}

Using the delta function identity
x\delta^{(k)}(x)=-k\delta^{(k-1)}(x)
,

\Phi_{0}
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\Phi_{0}=(\cosh R)^{-\nu}\int_{-t_{1}}^{t_{1}}{\rm d}t\,C(t)&\big\{f_{a-1}\delta^{(a)}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{a-1}[f_{k-1}+(\Delta-k-1)f_{k}]\delta^{(k)}(x)\\
&+[(\Delta-1)f_{0}+\pi c]\delta(x)\big\}.\end{split}

(250)

Using the relations

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}{\rm d}tC(t)\delta^{(k)}(x)=(-1)^{k}\left[\left(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\sigma}\right)^{k}\,\frac{C(t)}{\sqrt{(\cosh R)^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}\right]\Bigg|_{t=t_{o},\sigma=1},\quad\frac{{\rm d}t}{{\rm d}\sigma}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\cosh R)^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}

(251)

and

\int_{-t_{1}}^{0}{\rm d}tC(t)\delta^{(k)}(x)=(-1)^{k}\left[\left(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\sigma}\right)^{k}\,\frac{C(t)}{\sqrt{(\cosh R)^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}\right]\Bigg|_{t=-t_{o},\sigma=1},\quad\frac{{\rm d}t}{{\rm d}\sigma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\cosh R)^{2}-\sigma^{2}}}

(252)

we can evaluate (
250
) term by term. Starting with
k=0
, the sum of the
delta function integrals (
251
) and (
252
) give

(k=0)\quad\frac{C(t_{o})+C(-t_{o})}{\sinh R},

(253)

(k=1)\quad\frac{C^{\prime}(t_{o})-C^{\prime}(-t_{o})}{\sinh^{2}R}-\frac{C(t_{o})+C(-t_{o})}{\sinh^{3}R},

(254)

(k=2)\quad\frac{C^{\prime\prime}(t_{o})+C^{\prime\prime}(-t_{o})}{\sinh^{3}R}-\frac{3[C^{\prime}(t_{o})-C^{\prime}(-t_{o})]}{\sinh^{4}R}+\frac{C(t_{o})+C(-t_{o})}{\sinh^{3}R}+\frac{3[C(t_{o})+C(-t_{o})]}{\sinh^{5}R},

(255)

and so on. We see that the leading contribution is given by (
253
)
and all higher contributions are subleading (of order
(\sinh R)^{-(k+1)}
).
After this simplification, we find

\Phi_{0}\approx(\cosh R)^{-(\nu+1)}C_{+}(0)[(\Delta-1)f_{0}+\pi c],

(256)

which, using the results in appendix
I
, gives

\framebox{$\cal A$}\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\Phi_{0}=0,

(257)

\framebox{$\cal B$}\quad c=0\quad(\Delta-1)f_{0}=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{2\Omega_{d}}\quad\Phi_{0}=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{2\Omega_{d}}C_{+}(0),

(258)

\framebox{$\cal C$}\quad\Phi_{0}\approx(\cosh R)^{-(\nu+1)}C_{+}(0)c\pi\left\{1+\frac{d-1}{\nu+1}\right\}.

(259)

The final result of the bulk reconstruction by the Green’s function method is
given as

\begin{split}\framebox{$\cal A$}\quad\Phi(Y_{o})&=\xi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}t\,C(t)(2\cos t)^{\nu}=\eqref{eq:odd_HKLL},\\
\framebox{$\cal B$}\quad\Phi(Y_{o})&=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{2\Omega_{d}}C_{+}(0)=\eqref{eq:ours_special}\ [\mbox{for $\ell=0$}],\\
\framebox{$\cal C$}\quad\Phi(Y_{o})&=\xi\int_{\rm(sub)}{\rm d}t\,C(t)(2\cos t)^{\nu}+\frac{\eta}{2\Omega_{d}}C_{+}(0)=\eqref{eq:odd_ours}.\end{split}

(260)

These Green’s function results are in complete agreement with those in the
main text obtained by different methods, as shown in the last equalities.

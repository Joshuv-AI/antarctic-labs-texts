On the structure of finite groups determined by the arithmetic and geometric means of element orders
Valentina Grazian, Carmine Monetta, Marialaura Noce

Abstract
. In this paper we consider two functions related to the arithmetic and geometric means of element orders of a finite group, showing that certain lower bounds on such functions strongly affect the group structure. In particular, for every prime p p , we prove a sufficient condition for a finite group to be p p -nilpotent, that is, a group whose elements of p ′ p^{\prime} -order form a normal subgroup. Moreover, we characterize finite cyclic groups with prescribed number of prime divisors.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number. A finite group
G
is said to be
p
-nilpotent if it admits a normal
p
-complement, that is, a normal subgroup
H
of
G
of
p^{\prime}
-order and
p
-power index in
G
. The search for
p
-nilpotency criteria has been the subject of various works. In the last century the problem was approached looking at normalizers of
p
-subgroups of
G
, as in the famous Frobenius normal
p
-complement theorem,
[
13
, 10.3.2]
. However, in recent years, the study of
p
-nilpotency took a different direction, focusing on properties of elements’ orders of the group
G
. For instance in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
, some
p
-nilpotency criteria are obtained investigating the order of the product of element of coprime orders.

The aim of this work is to characterize
p
-nilpotency in terms of functions related to the arithmetic and geometric means of the orders of all elements of
G
.

For a finite group
G
, let
\psi(G)
and
\rho(G)
denote the sum and the product of element orders of
G
, respectively. For any positive integer
n
and any group
G
of order
n
, if we denote by
C_{n}
the cyclic group of order
n
, then
\psi(G)\leq\psi(C_{n})
and
\rho(G)\leq\rho(C_{n})
, and the equalities hold if and only if
G
is cyclic (see
[
1
,
8
]
). When
G
is a non-cyclic group of order
n
, these upper bounds can be sharpened using other functions depending both on
n
and on the least prime number dividing
n
(for example see
[
9
,
7
]
). As a result one can obtain criteria or necessary conditions for a finite group to be nilpotent, soluble or supersoluble as in
[
10
,
11
]
: we refer to
[
12
]
for a survey on this topic. Recently, some functions related to
\psi(G)
and
\rho(G)
have been considered, namely the functions

\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\displaystyle\frac{\psi(G)}{|G|^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad l(G)=\displaystyle\frac{\rho(G)^{1/|G|}}{|G|}.

We point out that
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)\cdot|G|
and
l(G)\cdot|G|
are the arithmetic and geometric means of the element orders of
G
, respectively. In particular
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)\geq l(G)
. For the convenience of the reader, we report some approximated values of
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)
and
l(G)
for certain finite groups, computed with the software GAP:

Theorem 1.1
.

[

[

Let

(a)

If

(d)

(e)

If

Theorem A

Let

If
G
is a finite group and
p

O_{p}(G)
and

the largest normal

p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, respectively. Our main result is the following:

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be an odd prime dividing the order of
G
. Suppose

l(G)\geq l(D_{2p}).

Then either

G\cong D_{2p}
or

l(G)>l(D_{2p})
and
G=O_{p}(G)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
O_{p}(G)
cyclic. In particular, if
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
, then

is

We point out that if
l(G)=l(\mathrm{D}_{2n})
, where
n
is an odd integer not necessarily prime, then in general it is not true that
G\cong D_{2n}
. For instance,
l(S_{3}\times C_{3})=l(\mathrm{D}_{18})
.
Moreover, the lower bound of Theorem
B
is the best possible for every odd prime
p
, as the group
D_{2p}
is not
p
-nilpotent. We remark that in
[
3
, Main Theorem]
, the authors proved that a similar result holds with respect to the function
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)
, that is, if
p
is an odd prime and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)>\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathrm{D}_{2p})=\frac{p^{2}+p+1}{4p^{2}}
then
G=O_{p}(G)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
O_{p}(G)
cyclic.
Furthermore, in the recent work
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, it is proved that if
p>5
,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\psi^{\prime\prime}(D_{2p})
and
G
is not
p
-nilpotent then
G\cong D_{2p}
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors also show that if
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)>\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathrm{D}_{2p})
then
G
is supersoluble with second derived subgroup contined in the center of
G
, namely,
[G^{\prime},G^{\prime}]\leq Z(G)
. However, this does not hold in general when
l(G)>l(\mathrm{D}_{2p})
, representing an important difference among the two functions
\psi^{\prime\prime}
and
l
. Indeed, there are infinite families of non-supersoluble finite groups satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
B
:

•

if
p>13
then
l(C_{p}\times A_{4})>l(D_{2p})
and
C_{p}\times A_{4}
is not supersoluble;

•

if
p>173
then
l(C_{p}\times A_{5})>l(D_{2p})
and
C_{p}\times A_{5}
is not soluble.

However, for small primes
p
, we can actually prove that if
f\in\{\psi^{\prime\prime},l\}
and
G
is a group whose order is divisible by
p
satisfying
f(G)>f(D_{2p})
, then
G
is supersoluble and, in some cases, even cyclic or nilpotent:

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a finite group whose order is divisible by the odd prime
p
. Suppose
f\in\{\psi^{\prime\prime},l\}
and
f(G)>f(D_{2p})
.

(1)

If
p=3
then
G
is cyclic.

(2)

If
p\leq 5
then
G
is nilpotent.

(3)

If
p\leq 13
then
G
is supersoluble.

Note that
if
p>3
then
f(C_{p}\times Q_{8})>f(D_{2p})
and
C_{p}\times Q_{8}
is not cyclic and
if
p>5
then
f(C_{p}\times S_{3})>f(D_{2p})
and
C_{p}\times S_{3}
is not nilpotent,
showing that the choice of primes in Theorem
C
is sharp.
Moreover, thanks to Theorem
C
, we are able to establish that more groups than the ones characterized in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
are cyclic or nilpotent. For instance,

l(D_{6})<l(C_{12})<l(C_{2}\times C_{2})
,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(D_{6})<\psi^{\prime\prime}(C_{180})<\psi^{\prime\prime}(C_{2}\times C_{2})

and
f(D_{10})<f(C_{5}\times Q_{8})<f(S_{3})
.

Theorem
B
also allows us to characterize nilpotency in some special cases, described by the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group such that
|G|=p^{a}q^{b}
for distinct primes
p
and
q
, with
p\neq 2
. If
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
then
G
is nilpotent.

Proof.

By Theorem
B
we get
G=O_{p}(G)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. From
|G|=p^{a}q^{b}
, we deduce that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=O_{q}(G)\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{q}(G)
. Hence all Sylow subgroups of
G
are normal in
G
and
G
is nilpotent.
∎

Finally, Theorem
B
allows us to characterize cyclic groups whose order has few prime divisors.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group of odd order and let
p
be the smallest prime divisor of
|G|
. If
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
then
G
is cyclic. Moreover, if the number of distinct primes dividing
|G|
is at most
\frac{p+1}{2}
, then
G
is cyclic if and only if
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
.

In general, if
p\geq 3
is the smallest prime divisor of the order of a group
G
, but there are more than
\frac{p+1}{2}
distinct primes dividing
|G|
, then the property
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
is not a necessary condition for
G
to be cyclic. For instance, the cyclic group of order
315=3^{2}*5*7
satisfies
l(C_{315})\approx 0.337<l(D_{6})
.

2.
Properties of the functions
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)
and
l(G)

In this section we analyse some properties of the functions
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)
and
l(G)
.
When
G
is a nontrivial group, then the following is clear.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any nontrivial finite group
G
we have
0<l(G)\leq\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)<1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
N
be a non-trivial proper normal subgroup of
G
and let
f\in\{\psi^{\prime\prime},l\}
.
Then

f(G)<f(G/N).

Proof.

If
f=\psi^{\prime\prime}
, this is
[
14
, property (1)]
for the inequality and
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
for the strict one. Suppose
f=l
.
Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in G
be such that

G=a_{1}N\cup a_{2}N\cup\dots\cup a_{m}N
and

a_{i}N\neq a_{j}N
whenever
i\neq j
.
So

\rho(G)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{n\in N}o(a_{i}n),

where
o(a_{i}n)
denotes the order of the element
a_{i}n
.
For every
1\leq i\leq m
, let
t_{i}
be the smallest positive integer such that
a_{i}^{t_{i}}\in N
. Then for every
n\in N
we have
(a_{i}n)^{t_{i}}\in N
and so
o(a_{i}n)
divides
t_{i}|N|
. Also, we can assume that
a_{1}=1
and so
t_{1}=1
and for
n=1
we get
o(a_{1}n)=1<1\cdot|N|
, as
N
is non-trivial. Thus there is at least one couple
(a_{i},n)
with
o(a_{i}n)<t_{i}|N|
. Therefore

\rho(G)<(t_{1}|N|)^{|N|}\cdot(t_{2}|N|)^{|N|}\cdots(t_{m}|N|)^{|N|}=\rho(G/N)^{|N|}|N|^{m|N|}=\rho(G/N)^{|N|}|N|^{|G|}.

Using the definition of
l(G)
, we get

l(G)=\frac{\rho(G)^{1/|G|}}{|G|}<\frac{\rho(G/N)^{|N|/|G|}|N|}{|G|}=l(G/N).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
f\in\{\psi^{\prime\prime},l\}
. Then

(1)

if
G
is a cyclic group and
K
is a proper subgroup of
G
, then
f(G)<f(K)
;

(2)

if
H
is a finite group and
(|G|,|H|)=1
then
f(G\times H)=f(G)\cdot f(H)
;

(3)

if
f(G)>\alpha
for some positive real number
\alpha
, then there exists and element
x\in G
such that
[G\colon\langle x\rangle]<\alpha^{-1}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), let
t
be the index of
K
in
G
. As
G
is cyclic, there exists a subgroup
H
of
G
of order
t>1
. Then
K
is isomorphic to
G/H
and
f(G)<f(G/H)=f(K)
by Lemma
2.2
.
Statement (2) is
[
14
, Lemma 1.2(2)]
and
[
4
, Lemma 2.7]
. Finally, part (3) for
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
. Now, if
l(G)>\alpha
then
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)\geq l(G)>\alpha
and the result follows.
∎

If
p
is a prime and
P
is a finite cyclic
p
-group, then we can compute the exact values of
\psi^{\prime\prime}(P)
and
l(P)
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
P
be a non-trivial cyclic
p
-group of order
p^{n}
. Then

\psi^{\prime\prime}(P)=\frac{p^{2n+1}+1}{p^{2n+1}+p^{2n}}\quad\text{ and }\quad l(P)=p^{-\frac{p^{n}-1}{p^{n}(p-1)}}.

In particular
p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq l(P)\leq p^{-\frac{1}{p}}
and, if
p
is odd, then
l(P)>p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}>l(D_{2p})
.

Proof.

The formulas for
\psi^{\prime\prime}(P)
and
l(P)
follow from
[
9
, Lemma 2.9(1)]
and
[
4
, Lemma 2.10]
. As for the last statement, note that, if we fix the prime
p
, then the function
p^{-\frac{p^{n}-1}{p^{n}(p-1)}}
is decreasing in
n
. Moreover, if
n=1
then
l(P)=p^{-\frac{1}{p}}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}p^{-\frac{p^{n}-1}{p^{n}(p-1)}}=p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
. Hence for every cyclic
p
-group
P
we have
p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\leq l(P)\leq p^{-\frac{1}{p}}
. Finally, if
p
is odd, then
2p<p^{2}-1
and we deduce that

l(P)\geq p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}>p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}>2^{-\frac{1}{2}}p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}=l(D_{2p}).

∎

Going further, we obtain a similar lower bound for
l(G)
when
G
is a cyclic group whose order is odd and divisible by a bounded number of primes.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite cyclic group of odd order and suppose
p\geq 3
is the smallest prime dividing
|G|
. If the number of prime divisors of
|G|
is at most
\frac{p+1}{2}
then
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
.

Proof.

let
p=p_{1}<p_{2}<\dots<p_{r}
be the distinct prime divisors of
|G|
and let
P_{i}
be a Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
G
for every
1\leq i\leq r
. Then by Lemma
2.3
(2) we have

l(G)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}l(P_{i}).

Now, for every
i\geq 2
we have
l(P_{i})>p_{i}^{-\frac{1}{p_{i}-1}}
by Lemma
2.4
.
Since the function
x^{-\frac{1}{x-1}}
is increasing for
x>1
, for every
i\geq 2
we deduce
l(P_{i})>p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
. Hence

l(G)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}l(P_{i})>p^{-\frac{r}{p-1}}

and
p^{-\frac{r}{p-1}}>l(D_{2p})=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}
if and only if
p^{\frac{p^{2}-2rp-1}{2p(p-1)}}>2^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Suppose
r\leq\frac{p+1}{2}
. Then

p^{\displaystyle\frac{p^{2}-2rp-1}{2p(p-1)}}>p^{\displaystyle\frac{-p-1}{2p(p-1)}}.

Now,
p^{\frac{-p-1}{2p(p-1)}}
is an increasing function of
p
and if
p=5
then
p^{\frac{-p-1}{2p(p-1)}}=5^{\frac{-6}{40}}>2^{-\frac{1}{2}}.
This proves that for
p\geq 5
we have
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
. Finally, suppose
p=3
and
|G|
is divided by two primes, say
3
and
q
. Then

l(G)\geq 3^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot q^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\geq 3^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 5^{-\frac{1}{4}}\approx 0.386>0.340\approx l(D_{6}).

Hence the statement is also true for
p=3
.
∎

We conclude the section with some structural results involving the functions
\psi^{\prime\prime}
and
l
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
p
be a prime and let
f\in\{\psi^{\prime\prime},l\}
. If
f(G)>p^{-1}
then
G
has a unique (normal) Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
and
P
is cyclic.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
(3), there exists an element
x\in G
such that

[G\colon\langle x\rangle]<p

and so
p
does not divide
[G\colon\langle x\rangle]
. Let
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(\langle x\rangle)
. Then
P
is cyclic and
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Since
\langle x\rangle\leq N_{G}(P)
, it follows that
[G\colon N_{G}(P)]<p
, which implies
[G\colon N_{G}(P)]=1
.
∎

The next lemma gives examples of finite groups
G
satisfying
l(G)>p^{-1}
that are not
p
-nilpotent. First we need some notation:

Notation 2.7
.

Let
p
be a prime. For
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
we set

\theta_{p}(x):=p^{\frac{-1+p-px}{px}}.

We point out that the real function
\theta_{p}(x)
is decreasing for every prime
p
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
G
be a finite group such that
G=PH
with
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G),P\cong C_{p},P\mathrel{\unlhd}G,C_{G}(P)=P
,
|H|>1
and
P\cap H=1
. Then

(1)

l(G)=\theta_{p}(|H|)\cdot l(H).

(2)

if
|H|=q
for some prime
q
, then
either
q=2
and
G\cong D_{2p}
or
q\geq 3
and
p^{-1}<l(G)<l(D_{2p})
.

Proof.

Note that
P
is the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and so
(|P|,|H|)=1
.
By
[
4
, Lemma 2.6]
we get

\rho(G)=\rho(P)^{|C_{H}(P)|}\cdot\rho(H)^{|P|}=p^{p-1}\cdot\rho(H)^{p}

Hence

l(G)=\frac{\rho(G)^{\frac{1}{p|H|}}}{p|H|}=\frac{(p^{p-1}\cdot\rho(H)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p|H|}}}{p|H|}=\frac{p^{\frac{p-1}{p|H|}}}{p}\cdot\frac{\rho(H)^{\frac{1}{|H|}}}{|H|}=p^{-\frac{p|H|-p+1}{p|H|}}\cdot l(H)=\theta_{p}(|H|)\cdot l(H).

Now suppose
|H|=q
for some prime
q
. Hence
H\cong C_{q}
and
l(H)=q^{-\frac{1}{q}}
by Lemma
2.4
.
If
q=2
then
G\cong D_{2p}.

Now let
q\geq 3
and, aiming for a contradiction, suppose
l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})
. By part (1) we get

p^{\frac{-pq+p-1}{pq}}\cdot q^{-\frac{1}{q}}=l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}

that corresponds to

2^{pq}\geq q^{2p}p^{(p-1)(q-2)}.

Note that
H\cong G/P=N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(C_{p})\cong C_{p-1}
. Hence
q
divides
p-1
and from
q\geq 3
we deduce that
p>2q
. Thus

2^{pq}\geq q^{2p}p^{(p-1)(q-2)}>q^{2p}\cdot(2q)^{(p-1)(q-2)}=q^{2p+(p-1)(q-2)}\cdot 2^{(p-1)(q-2)}

and so

2^{pq-(p-1)(q-2)}>q^{2p+(p-1)(q-2)}>2^{2p+(p-1)(q-2)}.

Now this is true if and only if

\displaystyle pq-(p-1)(q-2)

\displaystyle>2p+(p-1)(q-2)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow pq-pq+2p+q-2

\displaystyle>2p+pq-2p-q+2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow pq

\displaystyle<2q+2p-4<3p-4<3p

where in last line we used again that
2q<p
, obtaining a contradiction. Therefore
l(G)<l(D_{2p})
.

It remains to show that
l(G)>p^{-1}
, that is

p^{\frac{-pq+p-1}{pq}}\cdot q^{-\frac{1}{q}}>p^{-1}\Leftrightarrow p^{p}>p\cdot q^{p}.

Note that
2^{p}>p
and
q\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
, so

p^{p}>\frac{p}{2^{p}}p^{p}=p\cdot\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{p}>p\cdot q^{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Proof of the main results

In this final section we prove our main results: Theorem
B
, Theorem
C
and Corollary
1.3
. We start with some useful lemmas. We recall that for a finite group
G
, let
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
denote the largest normal
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
and let
\Phi(G)
denote the Frattini subgroup of
G
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a finite group. If
G/\Phi(G)
or
G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is
p
-nilpotent, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent.

Proof.

If
G/\Phi(G)
is
p
-nilpotent, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent by
[
13
, 9.3.4]
. Suppose
G/O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is
p
-nilpotent. Then there exists a normal subgroup
H
of
G
containing
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
such that
H
has
p
-power index in
G
and
([H\colon O_{p^{\prime}}(G)],p)=1
. Since
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
has
p^{\prime}
-order, we deduce that
H
has
p^{\prime}
-order and so
H
is a normal
p
-complement for
G
, that is,
G
is
p
-nilpotent.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

[
13
, 9.1.1 (i), 5.2.13 (ii)]

If
G
is a finite group and
N\mathrel{\unlhd}G
then
O_{p^{\prime}}(N)\leq O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
\Phi(N)\leq\Phi(G)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
p\geq 3
is a prime, then
l(D_{2p})>p^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have

l(D_{2p})={2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot p^{-\frac{p+1}{2p}}>p^{-1}\Longleftrightarrow 2^{-p}\cdot p^{-p-1}>p^{-2p}\Longleftrightarrow p^{p-1}>2^{p}\Longleftrightarrow p\geq 3.

∎

Now, we are in a position to prove Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Suppose
p
is an odd prime dividing the order of
G
.
We start proving that if
l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})
then either
G\cong D_{2p}
or
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
and
G
is
p
-nilpotent. Applying Lemma
3.3
and then Lemma
2.6
we immediately get that
P:=O_{p}(G)
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and it is cyclic.
Aiming for a contradiction, suppose there exists a finite group
G
such that
l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})
but either
l(G)=l(D_{2p})
and
G
is not isomorphic to
D_{2p}
or
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
and
G
is not
p
-nilpotent.
We can take such a
G
minimal with respect to the order. In particular, every finite group
H
with
|H|<|G|
and
l(H)>l(D_{2p})
is
p
-nilpotent.

Claim 1:
\bf O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\Phi(G)=1
:

Let
N\in\{O_{p^{\prime}}(G),\Phi(G)\}
and, aiming for a contradiction, suppose
N\neq 1
.
By Lemma
2.2
we have
l(G/N)>l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})
. By minimality of
G
we deduce that
G/N
is
p
-nilpotent and so
G
is
p
-nilpotent by Lemma
3.1
, a contradiction. Thus
N=1
, as wanted.

Claim 2:
\bf P\cong C_{p}
:

Since
P
is normal in
G
, we deduce that
\Phi(P)\leq\Phi(G)
by Lemma
3.2
. Now,
\Phi(G)=1
by Claim 1, so
\Phi(P)=1
and
P\cong C_{p}
.

Claim 3:
\bf C_{G}(P)=P
:

Note that
P\leq C_{G}(P)
, as
P
is abelian, so
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(C_{G}(P))
. Since
P\mathrel{\unlhd}C_{G}(P)
, the Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem
[
13
, 9.1.2]
gives
C_{G}(P)=PH
for some subgroup
H
of
G
of
p^{\prime}
-order. Note that
H\mathrel{\unlhd}C_{G}(P)
, as
P\leq Z(C_{G}(P))
. Since
P
is characteristic in
G
, so is
C_{G}(P)
and we deduce that
H\leq O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
by Claim 1. Therefore
P=C_{G}(P)
.

Claim 4:
\bf G=PQ
where
|Q|=q
for some prime
\bf q\neq p
:

Since
P\mathrel{\unlhd}G
, the Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem
[
13
, 9.1.2]
implies that there exists a subgroup
H
of
G
such that
G=PH
and
P\cap H=1
. Note that Claim 3 gives
H\cong G/P=N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)
, and so
H
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(C_{p})\cong C_{p-1}
. Thus
H
is cyclic. If
H=1
then
G=P
is a cyclic
p
-group, so
p
-nilpotent, and by Lemma
2.4
we get
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
, a contradiction. Therefore
H
is a non-trivial cyclic group of
p^{\prime}
-order.
Let
q
be a prime divisor of
|H|
and let
Q
be the unique subgroup of
H
of order
q
. Set
G_{1}=PQ\leq G
. Note that
C_{G_{1}}(P)=P
, so
N_{G_{1}}(P)/C_{G_{1}}(P)=G_{1}/P\cong Q
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. By Frobenius normal
p
-complement theorem (
[
13
, 10.3.2]
) we deduce that
G_{1}
is not
p
-nilpotent.
By Lemma
2.8
(1) applied first to
G=PH
and then to
G_{1}=PQ
we get

l(G)=\theta_{p}(|H|)l(H)
and
l(G_{1})=\theta_{p}(|Q|)l(Q)
. We want to prove that
Q=H
. Suppose for a contradiction that
Q<H
. Since
H
is cyclic, by Lemma
2.3
(1) we get
l(Q)>l(H)
. Using the fact that the function
\theta_{p}(x)
is decreasing we obtain

l(G_{1})=\theta_{p}(|Q|)l(Q)>\theta_{p}(|Q|)l(H)\geq\theta_{p}(|H|)l(H)=l(G)\geq l(D_{2p}).

So
l(G_{1})>l(D_{2p})
and the minimal choice of
G
implies that
G_{1}
is
p
-nilpotent, a contradiction. Thus
Q=H
and
G=PQ
.

Claims 1 to 4 show that
G
satisfies the assumptions of part (2) of Lemma
2.8
and so we deduce that either
G\cong D_{2p}
or
l(G)<l(D_{2p})
, a contradiction.

We showed that if
G
is a finite group satisfying
l(G)\geq l(D_{2p})
then either
G\cong D_{2p}
or
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
and
G
is
p
-nilpotent.

Finally, if
l(G)>l(D_{2p})
, then the group
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is a normal
p
-complement and we have already shown that
O_{p}(G)\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
, so
G=O_{p}(G)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(G).

∎

Next, we show how Theorem
B
together with
[
3
, Main Theorem]
and direct computations imply Theorem
C
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

Suppose
f(G)>f(D_{2p})
.
By Theorem
B
and
[
3
, Main Theorem]
we have
G=O_{p}(G)\times O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
O_{p}(G)
cyclic. Since
p
divides the order of
G
, we also have
O_{p}(G)\neq 1
and so by Lemma
2.3
(1) we get
f(O_{p}(G))\leq f(C_{p})
. Using Lemma
2.3
(2) we deduce that

f(C_{p})f(O_{p^{\prime}}(G))\geq f(O_{p}(G))f(O_{p^{\prime}}(G))=f(G)>f(D_{2p})

and so by Lemma
2.4
we have

\psi^{\prime\prime}(O_{p^{\prime}}(G))>\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(D_{2p})}{\psi^{\prime\prime}(C_{p})}=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(D_{2p})}{\frac{p^{3}+1}{p^{3}+p^{2}}}=\frac{\frac{p^{2}+p+1}{4p^{2}}}{\frac{p^{3}+1}{p^{3}+p^{2}}}=\frac{p^{2}+p+1}{4(p^{2}-p+1)}

and

l(O_{p^{\prime}}(G))>\frac{l(D_{2p})}{l(C_{p})}=\frac{l(D_{2p})}{p^{-\frac{1}{p}}}=2^{-{1/2}}p^{\frac{1-p}{2p}}.

The functions
\frac{p^{2}+p+1}{4(p^{2}-p+1)}
and
2^{-{1/2}}p^{\frac{1-p}{2p}}
are decreasing for every prime
p\geq 3
.

Suppose
p=3
. Then we get

\psi^{\prime\prime}(O_{3}^{\prime}(G))>\frac{13}{28}>\psi^{\prime\prime}(C_{2}\times C_{2})\quad\text{ and }\quad l(O_{3^{\prime}}(G))>2^{-{1/2}}3^{-{1/3}}>l(C_{2}\times C_{2})

and in both cases by Theorem
1.1
we deduce that the group
O_{3^{\prime}}(G)
is cyclic. Thus
G
is the direct product of two cyclic groups of coprime order and is therefore cyclic.

Suppose
p=5
. Then

\psi^{\prime\prime}(O_{5}^{\prime}(G))>\frac{31}{84}>\psi^{\prime\prime}(S_{3})\quad\text{ and }\quad l(O_{5^{\prime}}(G))>2^{-{1/2}}5^{-{2/5}}>l(S_{3})

and again by Theorem
1.1
we conclude that
O_{5^{\prime}}(G)
is nilpotent and so
G
is nilpotent.

Now, by Theorem
[
3
, Main theorem]
, if
\psi^{\prime\prime}(G)>\psi^{\prime\prime}(D_{2p})
then
G
is supersoluble for any prime
p
. As for the function
l
, suppose
p\leq 13
. Then

l(O_{p^{\prime}}(G))>2^{-{1/2}}p^{\frac{1-p}{2p}}>2^{-{1/2}}(13)^{-{12/26}}>l(A_{4})

and by
[
4
, Theorem 1.1(b)]
we conclude that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is supersoluble and so
G
is supersoluble.
∎

We conclude this section proving Corollary
1.3
.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Note that the function
x^{-\frac{x+1}{2x}}
is decreasing for every
x>0
. Hence if
2<p<q
then
l(D_{2p})>l(D_{2q})
. Thus by Theorem
B
we obtain that
G
is
q
-nilpotent with
O_{q}(G)
cyclic for every prime
q
. Therefore
G
is cyclic. The second statement follows from the first statement and Lemma
2.5
.
∎

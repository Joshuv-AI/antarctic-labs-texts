Introduction

Given a measurable function
p(.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right)
such that
0<\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\leq\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)<\infty,

\int\left|\frac{f(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{p(x)}dx<\infty.

We set

We see that

is a quasi normed space. As usual we will
denote
p_{+}=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)
and
p_{-}=\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x).

These spaces are referred to as the variable
L^{p}
spaces. In the last

[

space

0<p<\infty,
with the norn

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}}=\left\|\sup\limits_{t>0}\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\left|t^{-n}\varphi(t^{-1}.)\ast f\right|\right\|_{p},

In the paper
[

Preliminaries

Given a measurable function
p(.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right)

Definition 1
.

Define
\mathcal{F}_{N}=\left\{\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\sum\limits_{\left|\mathbf{\beta}\right|\leq N}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+\left|x\right|\right)^{N}\left|\partial^{\mathbf{\beta}}\varphi(x)\right|\leq 1\right\}
. Let
f\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the grand maximal operator given
by

\mathcal{M}f(x)=\sup\limits_{t>0}\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{N}}\left|\left(t^{-n}\varphi(t^{-1}.)\ast f\right)\left(x\right)\right|,

where
N
is a large and fixed integer. The variable Hardy space
H^{p(.)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is the set of all
f\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for which
\left\|\mathcal{M}f\right\|_{p(.)}<\infty
. In this case we define
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p(.)}}=\left\|\mathcal{M}f\right\|_{p(.)}
.

Definition 2
.

((p(.),p_{0},d)-atom)
. Let
p(.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right)
,
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<p_{0}\leq\infty
and
p_{0}\geq 1.
Fix an
integer
d\geq d_{p(.)}=\min\left\{l\in\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\}:p_{-}(n+l+1)>n\right\}.
A function
a
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
(p(.),p_{0},d)-
atom if there exists a cube
Q
such that

a_{1})

supp\left(a\right)\subset Q,

a_{2})

\left\|a\right\|_{p_{0}}\leq\frac{\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p(.)}},

a_{3})

\int a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leq d.

Definition 3
.

For sequences of nonnegative numbers
\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

and cubes
\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and for a function
p(.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(0,\infty\right)
, we define

\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(.)\right)=\left\|\left\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}\chi_{Q_{j}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q_{j}}\right\|_{p(.)}}\right)^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\underline{p}}}\right\|_{p(.)}.

The space

written as

(5)

f=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{atom}^{p(.),p_{0},d}}=\inf\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(.)\right)

Lemma 4
.

(
Lemma 2.2. in
[

)
Suppose that
p(.)

4

For all cubes

Lemma 5
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
invertible matrix. Let
p(.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a function satisfying
(
2
), (
4
),

0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty

, where

. Then

\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}^{\ast}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(.)\right)\lesssim\mathcal{A}\left(\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\left\{Q_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty},p(.)\right),

Since

4.

For
0<\alpha<n,
let

be the fractional integral operator (or
Riesz potential) defined by

(8)

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{n-\alpha}}f(y)dy,

f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

1\leq s<\frac{n}{\alpha}
. A well known result of Sobolev gives the
boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\frac{n}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}.
In
[
1
]
C. Capone, D. Cruz Uribe and A. Fiorenza
extend this result to the case of Lebesgue spaces with variable exponents
L^{p(.)}.
In
[
12
]
E. Stein and G. Weiss used the theory of harmonic
functions of several variables to prove that these operators are bounded
from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In
[
13
]
, M. Taibleson and G. Weiss obtained the boundedness of the
Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
from the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

into
H^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
for
0<p<1
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}.
We extend these results to the context of Hardy spaces with
variable exponents. The main tools that we use are Lemma 7 and the molecular
decomposition developed in
[
9
]
.

Definition 11
.

(Molecules) Let
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<p_{0}\leq\infty,

p_{0}\geq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z\cap}\left[d_{p(.)},\infty\right)
be fixed. One says that

\mathfrak{M}
is a
(p(.),p_{0},d)
molecule centered at a cube
Q\,
centered at
z
if it satisfies the following conditions.

1)
On
2\sqrt{n}Q,

\mathfrak{M}
satisfies the estimate
\left\|\mathfrak{M}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(2\sqrt{n}Q)}\leq\frac{\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p(.)}}.

2)
Outside
2\sqrt{n}Q,
we have
\left|\mathfrak{M}\left(x\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p(.)}}\left(1+\frac{\left|x-z\right|}{l(Q)}\right)^{-2n-2d-3}.
This condition
is called the decay condition.

3)
If
\mathbf{\beta}
is a multiindex with
\left|\mathbf{\beta}\right|\leq d,
then we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\mathbf{\beta}}\mathfrak{M}\left(x\right)dx=0.

This condition is called the moment condition.

Theorem 12
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and let
I_{\alpha}
be defined by (
8
). If
p(.)

is a measurable function that satisfies (
2
), (
4
) and

\frac{1}{q(.)}=\frac{1}{p(.)}-\frac{\alpha}{n}

Proof.

Since
2d_{q(.)}+2+\alpha+n\geq d_{p(.)}
, as Theorem 8, given
p_{0}
such that

, where
a_{j}
is an
(p(.),p_{0},2d_{q(.)}+2+\alpha+n)
-atom supported on the cube
Q_{j}
, where the convergence is in
H^{p(.)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
so it is enough to show that if
a
is an
(p(.),p_{0},2d_{q(.)}+2+\alpha+n)
-atom supported on the cube
Q
centered at
z
, then

is a
(q(.),q_{0},d_{q(.)})
-molecule centered at a cube
Q
for some fixed constant

independent of the atom
a
. Indeed, since
f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
in

then

in

in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Now from Theorem 5.2 in
[
9
]

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{H^{q(.)}}\lesssim\mathcal{A}(\{\lambda_{j}\},\{Q_{j}\},q(.))

by Lemma 7 and Theorem 4.6 in

\lesssim\mathcal{A}(\{\lambda_{j}\},\{Q_{j}\},p(.))\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p(.)}},

for all

, so the theorem follows from the density of
H^{p(.)}\cap L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
H^{p(.)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Now we show that there exists
c>0
such that

is a
\left(q(.),q_{0},d_{q_{(.)}}\right)-
molecule

1)
The Sobolev theorem and Lemma 4 give

\left\|I_{\alpha}\left(a\right)\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}(2\sqrt{n}Q)}\lesssim\left\|a\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\leq\frac{\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{p(.)}}\lesssim\frac{\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{q(.)}}.

Denote

As in the proof of Theorem 8 we

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{l(Q)^{d+1}}{\left|x-z\right|^{n-\alpha+d+1}}\left\|a\right\|_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{q(.)}}\left(\frac{l(Q)}{\left|x-z\right|}\right)^{{}^{n-\alpha+d+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{q(.)}}\left(1+\frac{\left|x-z\right|}{l(Q)}\right)^{{}^{-2n-2d_{q(.)}-3}},

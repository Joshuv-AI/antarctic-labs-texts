1.
Introduction

In this paper we examine coverings and partitions of large completely metrizable spaces with small ones.
By a “small” completely metrizable space we mean one with a countable basis, or (equivalently) a countable dense subset. This is the familiar class of Polish spaces.
A “large” completely metrizable space means anything else.

For each completely metrizable space
X
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)

\displaystyle=\,\min\left\{\left\lvert\mathcal{C}\right\rvert\colon\mathcal{C}\text{ is a covering of }X\text{ with Polish spaces}\right\}\!,\text{ }

\displaystyle\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)

\displaystyle=\,\min\left\{\left\lvert\mathcal{P}\right\rvert\colon\mathcal{P}\text{ is a partition of }X\text{ into Polish spaces}\right\}\!.

Observe that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
are well defined and both
\leq\!|X|
, because we may cover/partition
X
with singletons. Also,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for every
X
, because every partition of
X
is also a covering of
X
.
We are interested in two basic questions: (1) Can we determine
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
or
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
in terms of more familiar invariants? (2) Is it possible to have
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
?

Another preliminary observation is that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=|X|
whenever
|X|>\mathfrak{c}
.
This is because every Polish space has cardinality
\leq\!\mathfrak{c}
, and therefore a collection
\mathcal{F}
of Polish spaces covers at most
|\mathcal{F}|\cdot\mathfrak{c}
points.

This gives an easy answer to both of our questions above when
|X|>\mathfrak{c}
. Consequently, we are mostly interested in covering and partitioning non-Polish spaces with cardinality
\leq\!\mathfrak{c}
.
If
X
is such a space, then

\aleph_{1}\leq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
(otherwise
X
would be separable) and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}
(because we may partition
X
into singletons).
In this way,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
bear some resemblance to cardinal characteristics of the continuum.

Recall that the
weight
of a topological space
X
, denoted
wt(X)
, is the least cardinality of a basis for
X
.
The main results of this paper are:

\circ

If
X
is any completely metrizable space with
\aleph_{1}\leq wt(X)<\aleph_{\omega}
, then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=wt(X)
.

\circ

It is consistent relative to a huge cardinal that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for a broad class of completely metrizable spaces
X
with
\aleph_{\omega}\leq wt(X)<\mathfrak{c}
. This can be achieved, for example, by adding
>\!\aleph_{\omega+1}
Cohen reals to a model of
\mathsf{GCH}+{}\!\!
the Chang Conjecture
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
.

\circ

Suppose that for every singular cardinal
\mu<\mathfrak{c}
with
\mathrm{cf}(\mu)=\omega
, a weak form of
\square_{\mu}
holds (namely
\square_{\omega_{1},\mu}^{***}
), and
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\mu]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\mu^{+}
.
Then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for every completely metrizable space
X
,
and the value of
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
is determined by basic topological properties of
X
(see Theorem
4.8
).
Furthermore, the negation of these hypotheses implies
0^{\dagger}
exists; consequently, if
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for any completely metrizable space
X
, then
0^{\dagger}
exists.

These results are proved in Sections 2, 3, and 4, respectively.

and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt
below

For a set
A
and a cardinal
\mu<|A|
, recall that
[A]^{\mu}
denotes the set of all subsets of
A
with cardinality
\mu
. A subset
\mathcal{D}
of
[X]^{\mu}
is
cofinal
in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([A]^{\mu},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
if every member of
[A]^{\mu}
is included in some member of
\mathcal{D}
, and we define

\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([A]^{\mu},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\min\left\{|\mathcal{D}|\colon\mathcal{D}\text{ is cofinal in }\hskip-0.28453pt\big([A]^{\mu},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\right\}.

For example,
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{1}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{1}
, because (identifying an ordinal with the set of its predecessors, as usual)
\mathcal{D}=\left\{\alpha\colon\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
is cofinal in
{\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{1}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)}
.

Given a topological space
X
, recall that a
cellular family
in
X
is a collection
\mathcal{S}
of pairwise disjoint, nonempty open subsets of
X
. The
cellularity
of
X
, denoted
c(X)
, is defined as

c(X)=\sup\left\{\left\lvert\mathcal{S}\right\rvert\colon\mathcal{S}\text{ is a cellular family in }X\right\}.

We will need the following two facts about cellularity and weight:

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X
is a metric space of weight
\kappa
, then

(1)

c(X)=\kappa
.

(2)

X
has a basis
\mathcal{B}
of size
\kappa
such that every
x\in X
is contained in only countably many members of
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, see
[
6
, Theorem 4.1.15]
.
For the second assertion, first recall that by the Bing Metrization Theorem,
X
has a basis
\mathcal{B}_{0}
that is a countable union of cellular families. Every
x\in X
is contained in only countably many members of
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, and this remains true for any
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
By
[
6
, Theorem 1.1.15]
,
X
has a basis
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{0}
with
|\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
.
∎

Another fact used frequently throughout the paper is:
A subspace of a completely metrizable space is completely metrizable if and only if it is a
G_{\delta}
(see
[
6
, Theorems 4.3.23 and 4.3.24]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal, and let
X
be any completely metrizable space of weight
\kappa
. Then

\kappa\,\leq\,\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\,\leq\,\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big).

Furthermore, these bounds are attained: if
\kappa
is given the discrete topology, then both
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\omega}
are completely metrizable spaces of weight
\kappa
,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa)=\kappa
, and
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})={\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa>\aleph_{0}
, and fix a completely metrizable space
X
of weight
\kappa
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a collection of Polish subspaces of
X
with
|\mathcal{C}|<\kappa
. Because
c(X)=\kappa
(by Lemma
2.1
(1)
), there is a cellular family
\mathcal{S}
in
X
with
|\mathcal{C}|<|\mathcal{S}|
. Each member of
\mathcal{C}
has countable cellularity, and therefore each member of
\mathcal{C}
meets only countably many members of
\mathcal{S}
. Hence
\bigcup\mathcal{C}
meets at most
\aleph_{0}\cdot|\mathcal{C}|
members of
\mathcal{S}
. But
\mathcal{C}
is uncountable (otherwise
X
would be a countable union of separable subspaces, hence separable), so
\aleph_{0}\cdot|\mathcal{C}|=|\mathcal{C}|<|\mathcal{S}|
. Thus there are
U\in\mathcal{S}
with
U\cap\bigcup\mathcal{C}=\emptyset
, which means that
\mathcal{C}
does not cover
X
. Therefore
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\geq\kappa
.

Applying Lemma
2.1
(2)
, let
\mathcal{B}
be a basis for
X
with
|\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
, such that every point of
X
is contained in only countably many members of
\mathcal{B}
. Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[\mathcal{B}]^{\aleph_{0}}
be cofinal in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\mathcal{B}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
, and for each
A\in\mathcal{D}
define

Y_{A}=\left\{x\in X\colon A\supseteq\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\right\}.

It is straightforward to check that each
Y_{A}
is a closed subspace of
X
, hence completely metrizable. Also, each of the
Y_{A}
is second countable, with
\left\{Y_{A}\cap U\colon U\in A\right\}
as a basis.
Therefore
\left\{Y_{A}\colon A\in\mathcal{D}\right\}
is a collection of Polish subspaces of
X
.
Also,
\left\{Y_{A}\colon A\in\mathcal{D}\right\}
covers
X
: if
x\in X
then
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}
is countable by our choice of
\mathcal{B}
, and so
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq A
for some
A\in\mathcal{D}
, by our choice of
\mathcal{D}
.
Hence
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\mathcal{B}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.

This completes the proof of the first part of the theorem, and it remains to prove the “furthermore” part.
It is clear that both
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\omega}
(where
\kappa
is given the discrete topology) are completely metrizable spaces of weight
\kappa
, and that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa)=\kappa
. Also, it follows from what we already proved that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
. It remains to prove
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})\geq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a covering of
\kappa^{\omega}
with Polish spaces, and for each
Y\in\mathcal{C}
define

A_{Y}=\left\{x(n)\colon x\in Y\text{ and }n\in\omega\right\}.

Here, as usual, we are identifying the points of
\kappa^{\omega}
with functions
\omega\to\kappa
.
As each
Y\in\mathcal{C}
has countable cellularity,
\left\{x(n)\colon x\in Y\right\}
is countable for each
n
; hence
\bigcup_{n<\omega}\left\{x(n)\colon x\in Y\right\}=A_{Y}\in[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}
for every
Y\in\mathcal{C}
.
If
B\in[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}
, then let
x:\omega\to B
be an enumeration of
B
. Because
\mathcal{C}
covers
\kappa^{\omega}
, there is some
Y\in\mathcal{C}
with
x\in Y
, and this implies
B\subseteq A_{Y}
.
Therefore
\left\{A_{Y}\colon Y\in\mathcal{C}\right\}
is cofinal in
[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Because
\mathcal{C}
was an arbitrary covering of
\kappa^{\omega}
with Polish spaces, this implies
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\leq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})
.
∎

We observed above that
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{1}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{1}
because
\left\{\alpha\colon\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
is cofinal in
\big([\omega_{1}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.
Similarly,
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{n}]^{\aleph_{n-1}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{n}
for all
n>0
, because
\left\{\alpha\colon\omega_{n-1}\leq\alpha<\omega_{n}\right\}
is cofinal in
\big([\omega_{n}]^{\aleph_{n-1}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.
By induction, this can be used to show

\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{n}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{n}

for all
n>0
.
Combining this equality with Theorem
2.2
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space and suppose that
wt(X)=\aleph_{n}
for some
n\in\omega\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\aleph_{n}
.

More generally, for any cardinal
\kappa
with
\kappa=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\kappa
for every completely metrizable space
X
of weight
\kappa
.
This is fairly informative: if
0^{\dagger}
does not exist, then
\kappa=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
for all
\kappa
with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega
. This is discussed further in Section
4
.
On the other hand, if
\kappa
is uncountable and
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, then
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)>\kappa
.
(This is proved by a simple diagonal argument.
If
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\leq\kappa
, we would have a cofinal subset
\bigcup_{n<\omega}\mathcal{D}_{n}
of
[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}
with
|\mathcal{D}_{n}|<\kappa
for all
n
, but then choosing
\alpha_{n}\in\kappa\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{D}_{n}
for all
n
,
\left\{\alpha_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\}
would not be contained in any member of any
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.)

The situation for
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt
is different.
We shall see in the next section that
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)>\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
is consistent, relative to a huge cardinal, for certain completely metrizable spaces
X
of weight
\aleph_{\omega}
.
In particular, the upper bound for
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)
in Theorem
2.2
does not necessarily apply to
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
.
Nonetheless, we now show, via an inductive argument somewhat similar to that preceding Corollary
2.3
, that
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=wt(X)
whenever
wt(X)<\aleph_{\omega}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\kappa
be a cardinal with uncountable cofinality, and let
X
be a completely metrizable space of weight
\kappa
.
There is a size-
\kappa
partition of
X
into completely metrizable spaces of weight
<\!\kappa
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
(2)
, let
\mathcal{B}=\left\{U_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\}
be a basis for
X
such that each
x\in X
is contained in only countably many members of
\mathcal{B}
.
For each
\alpha<\kappa
, define

X_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in X\colon\sup\left\{\xi<\kappa\colon x\in U_{\xi}\right\}\leq\alpha\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}\setminus\textstyle\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}X_{\xi}.

By our choice of
\mathcal{B}
,
\left\{\xi<\kappa\colon x\in U_{\xi}\right\}
is countable for each
x\in X
. Because
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega
, this implies
\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}X_{\alpha}=X
. It follows that
\left\{Y_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\}
is a partition of
X
.
Also,
\left\{X_{\alpha}\cap U_{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\alpha\right\}
is a basis for
X_{\alpha}
, and therefore
wt(Y_{\alpha})\leq wt(X_{\alpha})\leq|\alpha|<\kappa
for each
\alpha<\kappa
.

To finish the proof, we must show that each of the
Y_{\alpha}
is completely metrizable.
It is not difficult to see that each of the
X_{\alpha}
is closed in
X
, and that the
X_{\alpha}
are increasing, i.e.,
\beta<\alpha
implies
X_{\beta}\subseteq X_{\alpha}
.
If
\alpha=\beta+1
is a successor ordinal, then
Y_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}\setminus X_{\beta}
(because the
X_{\alpha}
are increasing). It follows that
Y_{\alpha}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
, hence completely metrizable.
If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)=\omega
, fix an increasing sequence
\left\langle\beta_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\rangle
with limit
\alpha
. Then
Y_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}X_{\beta_{n}}
, so that once again
Y_{\alpha}
is a
G_{\delta}
in
X
, hence completely metrizable.
Finally, if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)>\omega
, then
Y_{\alpha}=\emptyset
, because by our choice of
\mathcal{B}
,
\sup\left\{\xi<\kappa\colon x\in U_{\xi}\right\}
cannot have uncountable cofinality, and so
x\in X_{\alpha}
implies
x\in X_{\xi}
for some
\xi<\alpha
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space and suppose that
wt(X)=\aleph_{n}
for some
n\in\omega\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\aleph_{n}
.

Proof.

That
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\aleph_{n}
is given by Corollary
2.3
. That
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\aleph_{n}
follows from the previous theorem and a straightforward induction, and the reverse inequality is given in Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose that there is a completely metrizable space
X
with
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
. Then
\mathfrak{c}\geq\aleph_{\omega+1}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{c}<\aleph_{\omega+1}
. Then
\mathfrak{c}<\aleph_{\omega}
, because
\mathfrak{c}
cannot have countable cofinality. If
wt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}
, then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
by the previous theorem. If
wt(X)>\mathfrak{c}
, then
|X|>\mathfrak{c}
and (as pointed out in the introduction) this implies
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=|X|
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
completely metrizable, with
\kappa=wt(X)<\aleph_{\omega}
.
If there is a continuous bijection
X\to[0,1]
, then there is a partition of
[0,1]
into
\kappa
Borel sets.

Proof.

Suppose
f:\kappa^{\omega}\to[0,1]
is a continuous bijection.
By the previous theorem, there is a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
\kappa^{\omega}
into
\kappa
Polish spaces.
By a theorem of Lusin and Suslin
[

, if
B\subseteq[0,1]
is a continuous bijective image of a Polish space, then
B
is Borel.
Hence
\left\{f[X]\colon X\in\mathcal{P}\right\}
is a partition of
[0,1]
into Borel sets.
∎

\left\{Z\times\omega^{\omega}\colon Z\in\mathcal{P}\right\}
is a partition of
\omega_{n}^{\omega}\times\omega^{\omega}\approx\omega_{n}^{\omega}
into
\aleph_{n}
copies of
\omega^{\omega}
.

3.
A model for
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt

In this section, every ordinal, when referred to as a topological space, carries the discrete topology.
We write
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
rather than
(\omega_{\omega})^{\omega}
for the countable power of the size-
\aleph_{\omega}
discrete space
\omega_{\omega}
.

The main result of this section is that it is consistent relative to a huge cardinal that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
. The space
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
has weight
\aleph_{\omega}
, so this result is optimal in some sense: by Theorem
2.5
, completely metrizable spaces
X
of smaller weight must have
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
.

Recall that
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the smallest cardinality of a collection
\mathcal{F}
of meager subsets of the Baire space
\omega^{\omega}
with
\bigcup\mathcal{F}=\omega^{\omega}
. The use of
\omega^{\omega}
in this definition is inessential: if
Y
is any Polish space without isolated points, then
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
is the smallest cardinality of a collection
\mathcal{F}
of meager subsets of
Y
with
\bigcup\mathcal{F}=Y
. (For a proof that these two ways of defining
\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
really are equivalent, see
[
9
, Proposition 2]
.)

Suppose
X
is a completely metrizable space, and
\mathcal{P}
is a partition of
X
into completely metrizable subspaces.
We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
skinny
if for every Polish
Y\subseteq X
,
\left\{Z\in\mathcal{P}\colon Z\cap Y\neq\emptyset\right\}
is countable.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
X
is a completely metrizable space, and
\mathcal{P}
is a partition of
X
into completely metrizable subspaces. If
\mathcal{P}
is not skinny, then
|\mathcal{P}|\geq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

This follows almost immediately from a result of Fremlin and Shelah (which answered a question going back to Hausdorff)
[
9
, Theorem 3]
:
Any partition of a Polish space into uncountably many
G_{\delta}
sets is of size
\geq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.

Suppose
X
is a completely metrizable space, and
\mathcal{P}
is a partition of
X
into completely metrizable subspaces.
If
\mathcal{P}
is not skinny, there is some Polish
Y\subseteq X
such that
\left\{Z\in\mathcal{P}\colon Y\cap Z\neq\emptyset\right\}
is uncountable.
But then
\left\{Y\cap Z\colon Z\in\mathcal{P}\right\}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
is an uncountable partition of
Y
into
G_{\delta}
sets, so it follows from Fremlin and Shelah’s theorem that
|\mathcal{P}|\geq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})
.
∎

Consider the following statement, abbreviated
(\kappa^{+},\kappa)\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\mu^{+},\mu)
:

For every model
M
for a countable language
\mathcal{L}
that contains a unary predicate
A
, if
|M|=\kappa^{+}
and
|A|=\kappa
then there is an elementary submodel
M^{\prime}\prec M
such that
|M^{\prime}|=\mu^{+}
and
|M^{\prime}\cap A|=\mu
.

The statement
(\kappa^{+},\kappa)\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\mu^{+},\mu)
is an instance of
Chang’s conjecture
. To prove the main theorem of this section, we use the statement obtained by taking
\kappa=\aleph_{\omega}
and
\mu=\aleph_{0}
above. This instance of Chang’s conjecture, abbreviated
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
, is known as
Chang’s conjecture for
\aleph_{\omega}
.

The usual Chang conjecture, which is the assertion
(\aleph_{2},\aleph_{1})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
, is equiconsistent with the existence of an
\omega_{1}
-Erdős cardinal. Chang’s conjecture for
\aleph_{\omega}
requires even larger cardinals.
\mathsf{GCH}
+
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
was first proved consistent relative to a hypothesis a little weaker than the existence of a
2
-huge cardinal in
[
18
]
. Recently this was improved to a huge cardinal in
[
7
]
. The precise consistency strength of
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
is an open problem, but significant large cardinal strength is known to be needed. This is because
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
implies the failure of
\square_{\aleph_{\omega}}
(see
[
20
]
, in particular Fact 4.2 and the remarks after it), and the failure of
\square_{\aleph_{\omega}}
carries significant large cardinal strength (see
[
3
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
implies that no partition of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
into Polish spaces is skinny.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a pairwise disjoint collection of Polish subspaces of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
. By Theorem
2.2
,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
, and a straightforward diagonal argument shows
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)>\aleph_{\omega}
. Hence
|\mathcal{P}|\geq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\geq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\geq\aleph_{\omega+1}
. For each
Y\in\mathcal{P}
, let

A_{Y}=\left\{x(n)\colon x\in Y\text{ and }n\in\omega\right\}.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.2
,
A_{Y}
is countable for each
Y\in\mathcal{P}
.

Let
(M,\in)
be a model of (a sufficiently large fragment of)
\mathsf{ZFC}
such that
\omega_{\omega}\subseteq M
,
\mathcal{P}\in M
, and
|M|=|M\cap\mathcal{P}|=\aleph_{\omega+1}
. (Such a model can be obtained in the usual way, via the downward Löwenheim-Skolem Theorem.) Let
\phi:M\to M\cap\mathcal{P}
be a bijection, and consider the model
(M,\in,\phi,\omega_{\omega})
for the
3
-symbol language consisting of a binary relation, a unary function, and a unary predicate. Applying the Chang conjecture
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
, there exists some
M^{\prime}\subseteq M
such that
|M^{\prime}|=\aleph_{1}
,
M^{\prime}\cap\omega_{\omega}
is countable, and
(M^{\prime},\in,\phi,\omega_{\omega})\prec(M,\in,\phi,\omega_{\omega})
.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}\cap M^{\prime}
. By elementarity, the restriction of
\phi
to
M^{\prime}
is a bijection
M^{\prime}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, and so
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}|=\aleph_{1}
.

Let
A=\omega_{\omega}\cap M^{\prime}
. If
Y\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, then
A_{Y}\in M^{\prime}
, and therefore
A_{Y}\subseteq M^{\prime}
(because
A_{Y}
is countable, and
M^{\prime}
models (enough of)
\mathsf{ZFC}
). Hence
Y\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
implies
A_{Y}\subseteq A
. Let

X=A^{\omega}
.
Then
X\approx\omega^{\omega}
(in particular,
X
is Polish), and
X\supseteq A_{Y}^{\omega}\supseteq Y
for all
Y\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.
In particular,
\left\{Y\in\mathcal{P}\colon Y\cap X\neq\emptyset\right\}\supseteq\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is uncountable, so
\mathcal{P}
is not skinny.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

It is consistent relative to a huge cardinal that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
.
More precisely, given a model of
\mathsf{GCH}+{}
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
and any
\lambda>\aleph_{\omega+1}
with
\mathrm{cf}(\lambda)>\omega
, there is a ccc forcing extension in which

\aleph_{\omega+1}\,=\,\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\,<\,\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\,=\,\lambda\,=\,\mathfrak{c}.

Proof.

Let
V
be a model of
\mathsf{GCH}
plus
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
. Recall that the existence of such a model is consistent relative to a huge cardinal.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be any ccc forcing poset such that
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{c}=\lambda
. For example, we could take
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Fn}(\lambda,2)
, the standard poset for adding
\lambda
Cohen reals, or
\mathbb{P}
could be the standard length-
\lambda
iteration forcing
\mathsf{MA}+\mathfrak{c}=\lambda
.

Because
\mathbb{P}
has the ccc,
\mathbb{P}
preserves cardinals; hence
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\aleph_{\omega+1}<\lambda=\mathfrak{c}
.
Also,
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{\omega+1}
because
\mathbb{P}
is ccc and the ground model satisfies
\mathsf{GCH}
.
(This fact is fairly well known, but we include a sketch of the argument here for completeness. By the comments following Corollary
2.3
,
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)>\aleph_{\omega}
.
Because
\mathbb{P}
has the ccc, every member of
[\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}
in the extension is contained in some member of
[\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap V
. That is,
[\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap V
is cofinal in
[\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Because
\mathsf{GCH}
holds in
V
, this means

\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\leq\left\lvert[\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap V\right\rvert=\aleph_{\omega+1}
.)
By Theorem
2.2
,

\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})=\aleph_{\omega+1}
.

Furthermore,
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
is preserved by ccc forcing. (This fact is considered folklore, but a proof can be found in
[
7
, Lemma 13]
.)
Hence
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\geq\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{c}
by the previous two lemmas.
Finally,
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})\leq|\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}|=|\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}|^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_{0}}=\mathfrak{c}
in the extension (because we may partition
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
into singletons).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

It is consistent relative to a huge cardinal that
\mathfrak{c}
is arbitrarily large, and
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)

for every completely metrizable space
X
with
\aleph_{\omega}\leq wt(X)<\mathfrak{c}
such that
X
contains a subspace homeomorphic to
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
.

Proof.

In statement of Theorem
3.3
, let us add the requirement that
\lambda
is not the successor of a singular cardinal with cofinality
\omega
.

In the generic extension, let
X
be a completely metrizable space with
wt(X)=\kappa
, where
\aleph_{\omega}\leq\kappa<\lambda=\mathfrak{c}
.
By Theorem
2.2
,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.3
above,
[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap V
is cofinal in
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
, and therefore
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\leq\left\lvert[\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap V\right\rvert=(\kappa^{\aleph_{0}})^{V}<\lambda
. (The last inequality uses the fact that
\mathsf{GCH}
holds in
V
, plus the assumption that
\lambda
is not the successor of a singular cardinal with cofinality
\omega
.) Hence
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{c}
. But if
Y
is any subspace of
X
homeomorphic to
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
and
\mathcal{P}
is any partition of
X
into Polish spaces, then
\left\{Z\cap Y\colon Z\in\mathcal{P}\right\}
is a partition of
Y
into Polish spaces. Therefore
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\geq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(Y)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})=\mathfrak{c}
.
∎

This raises the question of what completely metrizable spaces with weight
\geq\!\aleph_{\omega}
contain a copy of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
. A characterization is given at the beginning of the next section: roughly,
X
contains a (closed) copy of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
unless
X
and all its closed subsets are everywhere “locally” of weight
<\!\aleph_{\omega}
.

A collection
\mathcal{F}
of countable sets is called
sparse
if
\bigcup\mathcal{G}
is uncountable for every uncountable
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is sparse if no single countable set contains uncountably many members of
\mathcal{F}
.

Sparse cofinal subsets of
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
have appeared in diverse problems from infinite combinatorics:
a good deal about them can be found in Kojman, Milovich, and Spadaro’s
[
17
]
, and they appear also in Spadaro
[
21
]
, Blass
[
1
]
, and Nyikos
[
19
]
. (Nyikos refers to them as
Kuprepa families
.) It is known that
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
implies the non-existence of sparse cofinal families in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
, and
\square_{\aleph_{\omega}}
implies they do exist
[
17
, Section 3]
.

Observation
.

If there is a skinny partition of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
into Polish spaces, then there is a sparse cofinal subset of
{\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{P}
is a partition of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
into Polish spaces. In the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we showed that this implies

\mathcal{D}=\left\{\left\{x(n)\colon x\in X\text{ and }n\in\omega\right\}\colon X\in\mathcal{P}\right\}

is a cofinal subset of
{\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{D}
is not sparse. Then there is some
A\in[\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that
\left\{B\in\mathcal{D}\colon B\subseteq A\right\}
is uncountable. By the definition of
\mathcal{D}
, this means that
\left\{X\in\mathcal{P}\colon X\subseteq A^{\omega}\right\}
is uncountable, and in particular
\left\{X\in\mathcal{P}\colon X\cap A^{\omega}\neq\emptyset\right\}
is uncountable.
∎

In other words, a skinny partition of
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
is just a special kind of sparse cofinal family, in disguise. In light of this, our use of
(\aleph_{\omega+1},\aleph_{\omega})\hskip-1.42262pt\twoheadrightarrow\hskip-1.42262pt(\aleph_{1},\aleph_{0})
in this section, and our use of weak
\square
-like principles in the next, are unsurprising.
We note (without proof) that the partitions whose existence is implied in the proof of Theorem
2.5
or in the next section, which witness “small” values of
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
, are skinny, and therefore naturally give rise to sparse cofinal families.

4.
L
-like principles imply
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt

In this section we show that
if
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for any completely metrizable space
X
, then
0^{\dagger}
exists. We do this by extending the inductive arguments of Section
2
to spaces of weight
\geq\!\aleph_{\omega}
using “
L
-like” assumptions that hold if
0^{\dagger}
does not exist. Roughly, the assumptions we use are that the cardinals
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
are as small as possible, and that a weak
\square
principle holds at singular cardinals
<\!\mathfrak{c}
of cofinality
\omega
.

First we need a purely topological theorem that makes no assumptions beyond
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

Let us say that a space
X
is
locally
<\!\kappa
-like
if for every nonempty open
U\subseteq X
, there is a nonempty open
V\subseteq U
with
wt(V)<\kappa
.
Equivalently,
X
is locally
<\!\kappa
-like if
X
has a
\pi
-base of open sets with weight
<\!\kappa
.

On the other hand, let us say that a space
X
is
locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like
if every nonempty open
U\subseteq X
has
wt(U)\geq\kappa
. Note that
X
fails to be locally
<\!\kappa
-like if and only if some nonempty open subset of
X
is locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like.

Compare the following theorem with Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, and let
X
be a completely metrizable space with
wt(X)\geq\kappa
.
Then either:

(1)

some closed subset of
X
is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
, or

(2)

there is a partition of
X
into at most
wt(X)
completely metrizable spaces, each of weight
<\!\kappa
.

Furthermore, these two possibilities are mutually exclusive, and
(2)
holds if and only if every closed subset of
X
is locally
<\!\kappa
-like.

Proof.

To prove the theorem, we show:

(a)

If some closed
Y\subseteq X
is not locally
<\!\kappa
-like, then some closed
Z\subseteq Y
is homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
.

(b)

If every closed subspace of
X
is locally
<\!\kappa
-like, then
X
can be partitioned into at most
wt(X)
completely metrizable spaces, each of weight
<\!\kappa
.

We begin with
(a)
.
Suppose
X_{0}\subseteq X
is closed and is not locally
<\!\kappa
-like. This means there is a nonempty open
X_{1}\subseteq X_{0}
(open in
X_{0}
, not necessarily in
X
) that is locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like.
Let
Y=\overline{X_{1}}
. Note that
Y
is locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like because
X_{1}
is; in fact, if
U
is any nonempty open subset of
Y
, then
\overline{U}
is locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like.

Recall that a collection
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of some topological space is
discrete
if every point has a neighborhood meeting
\leq\!1
member of
\mathcal{C}
.

Claim
.

Suppose
Y
is a complete metric space and is locally
\geq\!\kappa
-like, and let
\varepsilon>0
. There is a discrete collection of
\kappa
open subsets of
Y
, each with diameter
\leq\!\varepsilon
.

Proof of claim:

By the Bing metrization theorem,
Y
has a
\sigma
-discrete base: that is, there is a sequence
\left\langle\mathcal{B}_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\rangle
of collections of open subsets of
Y
such that
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}
is a base for
Y
and each
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is discrete. By deleting any member of any
\mathcal{B}_{n}
with diameter
>\!\varepsilon
, we may (and do) assume without loss of generality that each member of each
\mathcal{B}_{n}
has diameter
\leq\!\varepsilon
.

If some
\mathcal{B}_{n}
has size
\geq\!\kappa
, we are done; so suppose this is not the case. Fix a discrete collection
\left\{U_{k}\colon k\in\omega\right\}
of open subsets of
Y
(e.g., any countable subset of some
\mathcal{B}_{n}
).
For each
k
and
n
, let
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{k}=\left\{U_{k}\cap V\colon V\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\right\}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
, and note that
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{B}_{n}^{k}
is a basis for
U_{k}
.
Fix a sequence
\left\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\right\rangle
of cardinal increasing up to
\kappa
.
Because
wt(U_{k})\geq\kappa
, there is some
n_{k}\in\omega
such that
|\mathcal{B}^{k}_{n_{k}}|\geq\mu_{k}
.
For each
k
, let
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\left\{\overline{V}\colon V\in\mathcal{B}^{k}_{n_{k}}\right\}
, and let
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}\mathcal{C}_{k}
.

{\footnotesize\qed}\

By recursion, we now construct a tree
\left\{V_{s}\colon s\in\kappa^{<\omega}\right\}
of closed subsets of
Y
as follows.
Let
V_{\emptyset}=Y
.
Given
s\in\kappa^{<\omega}
, suppose some open set
V_{s}
has already been chosen.
Applying the claim above, let
\left\{U_{s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\}
be a discrete collection of open subsets of
V_{s}
, each of diameter
\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2^{|s|}}}
.
Finally, let
V_{s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha}=\overline{U}_{s\!\,^{\frown}\alpha}
for each
\alpha<\kappa
.
This completes the recursion.

For each
x\in\kappa^{\omega}
, the completeness of our metric, together with the size restriction on the
V_{s}
, implies that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}V_{x\restriction n}
contains exactly one point.
Define
h:\kappa^{\omega}\to Y
by taking
h(x)
to be the unique point in
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}V_{x\restriction n}
for each
x\in\kappa^{\omega}
.

We claim
h
is a topological embedding.
For injectivity, if
x\neq y
then
x\!\restriction\!n\neq y\!\restriction\!n
for some
n
, and
x
and
y
are members of the disjoint sets
V_{x\restriction n}
and
V_{y\restriction n}
. Continuity follows from our size restriction on the
V_{s}
: if
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
, there is some
n
such that
V_{x\restriction n}\subseteq B_{\varepsilon}(h(x))
. Finally,
h
is a closed mapping: if
[\![s]\!]=\left\{x\in\kappa^{\omega}\colon s\subseteq x\right\}
is a basic closed set in
\kappa^{\omega}
, then
h([\![s]\!])=V_{s}\cap h(\kappa^{\omega})
.
This completes the proof of
(a)
.

To prove
(b)
, suppose every closed
K\subseteq X
is locally
<\!\kappa
-like.
Let
W
be a function choosing witnesses to this property: i.e., if
K
is a nonempty closed subset of
X
, then
W(K)
is a nonempty open subset of
K
with weight
<\!\kappa
.

Let
\lambda=wt(X)
.
Define a decreasing sequence
\left\langle X_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\lambda^{+}\right\rangle
of closed subspaces of
X
as follows:
let
X_{0}=X
,
let
\textstyle X_{\alpha}=\bigcap_{\xi<\alpha}X_{\xi}
for limit
\alpha<\lambda^{+}
, and
at successor stages let
X_{\alpha+1}=X_{\alpha}\setminus W(X_{\alpha})
.

Because
wt(X)=\lambda
, there is some
\alpha<\lambda^{+}
such that
X_{\beta}=X_{\alpha}
for all
\beta\geq\alpha
.
But this implies
X_{\beta}=\emptyset
for all
\beta\geq\alpha
, since our construction would otherwise give
W(X_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset
and therefore
X_{\alpha+1}\neq X_{\alpha}
.

Each
W(X_{\xi})
is an open subspace of a closed subspace of
X
, and therefore is completely metrizable. Furthermore
wt(W(X_{\xi}))<\kappa
for all
\xi
, and
\left\{W(X_{\xi})\colon\xi<\alpha\right\}
is a partition of
X\setminus X_{\alpha}=X
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, and let
X
be a completely metrizable space with
wt(X)=\kappa
. If some closed subset of
X
is not locally
<\!\kappa
-like, then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.

Proof.

Let
Z
be a closed subspace of
X
homeomorphic to
\kappa^{\omega}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
is a covering of
X
with Polish spaces, then
\left\{Y\cap Z\colon Y\in\mathcal{C}\right\}
is a covering of
Z
with Polish spaces. Hence
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\geq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(Z)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
. (The last equality is from Theorem
2.2
.) But we also have
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
by Theorem
2.2
, so
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space with
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
.
Then either:

(1)

\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\geq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
.

(2)

\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
.

Proof.

We show that statements
(1)
and
(2)
from Theorem
4.1
imply, respectively, the statements
(1)
and
(2)
in the statement of the corollary.

Suppose statement
(1)
from Theorem
4.1
holds, and let
Z
be a closed subspace of
X
homeomorphic to
\omega_{\omega}^{\omega}
.
By the previous corollary,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})=\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.
If
\mathcal{P}
is a partition of
X
into Polish spaces, then
\left\{P\cap Z\colon P\in\mathcal{P}\right\}
is a partition of
Z
into Polish spaces, and this shows that
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\geq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(Z)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
.

Suppose statement
(2)
from Theorem
4.1
holds, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of
X
into
\leq\!\aleph_{\omega}
completely metrizable spaces of weight
<\!\aleph_{\omega}
.
For each
Y\in\mathcal{P}
, there is by Theorem
2.5
a partition
\mathcal{P}_{Y}
of
P
into
<\!\aleph_{\omega}
Polish spaces.
Then
\bigcup\left\{\mathcal{P}_{Y}\colon Y\in\mathcal{P}\right\}
is a partition of
X
into
\leq\!\aleph_{\omega}
Polish spaces.
Therefore
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\aleph_{\omega}
. The reverse inequalities are provided by Theorem
2.2
.
∎

The next theorem improves Corollary
2.6
from Section
2
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
for some completely metrizable space
X
.
Then
\mathfrak{c}\geq\aleph_{\omega+2}
.

Proof.

If
wt(X)<\aleph_{\omega}
then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=wt(X)
by Theorem
2.5
. If
wt(X)>\mathfrak{c}
then
|X|>\mathfrak{c}
(by
[
6
, Theorem 4.1.15]
), so
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=|X|
.

Now suppose
wt(X)=\mathfrak{c}
.
Because
\mathfrak{c}\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\mathfrak{c}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)\leq\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_{0}}=\mathfrak{c}
, Theorem
2.2
gives
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{c}
.
Also,
\mathfrak{c}\leq|X|\leq\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_{0}}=\mathfrak{c}
(generally speaking,
wt(Y)\leq|Y|\leq wt(Y)^{\aleph_{0}}
for any metric space
Y
). Therefore
|X|=\mathfrak{c}
, and this implies
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}
(because we may partition
X
into singletons). Hence
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}
, and in particular
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
.

Therefore, if there is a completely metrizable space
X
with
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
, then
\aleph_{\omega}\leq wt(X)<\mathfrak{c}
. To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that if
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
and
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\omega+1}
, then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
.

Assume
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
and
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\omega+1}
. By Corollary
4.3
, we may also assume
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega^{\omega}_{\omega})
and
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\geq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})
.
But
|X|\leq\aleph_{\omega}^{\aleph_{0}}\leq\mathfrak{c}^{\aleph_{0}}=\mathfrak{c}
, hence
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}
(by partitioning
X
into singletons). Therefore

\aleph_{\omega+1}\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\omega_{\omega}^{\omega})=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{\omega+1}
.
∎

The
\aleph_{\omega+2}
bound given in this theorem is optimal, which can be seen by setting
\lambda=\aleph_{\omega+2}
in Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary
4.3
gives us our first inkling of how to extend the results of Section
2
to spaces of weight
\aleph_{\omega}
and beyond.
If we assume
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\omega_{\omega}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\aleph_{\omega+1}
and we can somehow prove that
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\aleph_{\omega+1}
whenever
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
, then using Corollary
4.3
we can conclude that
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
whenever
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
. This is precisely our strategy.
Proving
\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\aleph_{\omega+1}
whenever
wt(X)=\aleph_{\omega}
, and similarly for other cardinals of cofinality
\omega
, is where
\square
-like principles enter the picture.

Let
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
abbreviate the following statement:

\mathsf{SCH}^{+}\!:

If
\kappa
is a cardinal with uncountable cofinality, then
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\kappa
. If
\kappa
is a singular cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, then
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\kappa^{+}
.

The reason for calling this principle
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
is that the assertion “
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
holds for all
\kappa>\mathfrak{c}
” is equivalent to the Singular Cardinals Hypothesis, abbreviated
\mathsf{SCH}
.
This is because if
\kappa>\mathfrak{c}
, then
\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)=\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(\kappa^{\omega})=|\kappa^{\omega}|
; so
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
holds for all
\kappa>\mathfrak{c}
if and only if
\kappa^{\aleph_{0}}=\kappa^{+}
for all
\kappa>\mathfrak{c}
with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
.
(And this is equivalent to
\mathsf{SCH}
by a theorem of Silver.)

In
[
10
, Section 2]
, Fuchino and L. Soukup introduce
a weak version of Jensen’s principle
\square_{\kappa}
denoted
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
.
As one might guess from the notation, Fuchino and Soukup discuss a more generalized
2
-parameter version of this principle,
\square^{***}_{\lambda,\kappa}
. We will only need the special case
\lambda=\omega_{1}
, or rather a consequence of it described below.

Recall that
\square^{*}_{\kappa}
denotes the
weak square
principle at
\kappa
. This is a weakening of
\square_{\kappa}
, and is equivalent to the existence of a special
\kappa^{+}
-Aronszajn tree
[
14
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

(Fuchino and Soukup,
[
10
, Lemma 4]
)

Assume
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
, and let
\kappa
be a singular cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
. Then
\square^{*}_{\kappa}
implies
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
.

It is also worth mentioning that Foreman and Magidor in
[
8
]
considered an axiom called the
very weak square
principle for
\kappa
. The very weak square principle for
\kappa
implies
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
unconditionally.

Instead of applying
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
directly, we use a consequence of
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}+\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
proved by Fuchino and Soukup in
[
10
]
, namely the existence of something called a
(\omega_{1},\kappa)
-Jensen matrix.

Let
p
be a set and
\kappa
a cardinal, and let
\theta
be a regular cardinal with
\omega_{1},2^{\kappa},|\mathrm{tc}(p)|<\theta
, where
\mathrm{tc}(p)
denotes the transitive closure of
p
. Let
H_{\theta}
denote the set of all sets hereditarily smaller than
\theta
, and recall
H_{\theta}\models\mathsf{ZFC}^{-}
.
A
(\omega_{1},\kappa)
-Jensen matrix
over
p
is a matrix
\left\langle M_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\rangle
of elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
such that

(1)

For each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and
n<\omega
,
p\in M_{\alpha,n}
,
\kappa+1\subseteq M_{\alpha,n}
, and
\left\lvert M_{\alpha,n}\right\rvert<\kappa
.

(2)

For each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
,
\left\langle M_{\alpha,n}\colon n<\omega\right\rangle
is an increasing sequence.

(3)

If
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and
\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)>\omega
, then there is some
n^{*}<\omega
such that, for every
n\geq n^{*}
,
[M_{\alpha,n}]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap M_{\alpha,n}
is cofinal in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([M_{\alpha,n}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.

(4)

For each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
, let
M_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}M_{\alpha,n}
. Then
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\rangle
is continuously increasing, and
\kappa^{+}\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa^{+}}M_{\alpha}
.

The following is proved as Theorems 7 and 8 in
[
10
]
:

Lemma 4.6
.

(Fuchino and Soukup,
[
10
, Theorems 7 and 8]
)

Assume
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
,
and let
\kappa
be a singular cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
.
Then
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
holds if and only if
there is a
(\omega_{1},\kappa)
-Jensen matrix over
p
for some (or any)
p
.

We are now ready to prove the main technical result of this section. Compare the following with Theorems
2.4
and
4.1
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
and
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\kappa}
,
and let
\kappa
be a singular cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
.
If
X
is any completely metrizable space of weight
\kappa
, there is a partition of
X
into
\leq\!\kappa^{+}
completely metrizable spaces of weight
<\!\kappa
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a completely metrizable space of weight
\kappa
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, fix a basis
\mathcal{B}
for
X
such that
|\mathcal{B}|=\kappa
and every point of
X
is contained in only countably many members of
\mathcal{B}
. Applying
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
, let
\mathcal{D}
be a cofinal family in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\mathcal{B}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
with
|\mathcal{D}|=\kappa^{+}
, and fix a bijection
p:\kappa^{+}\to\mathcal{D}
.
Let
\left\langle M_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\rangle
be a
(\omega_{1},\kappa)
-Jensen matrix over
p
.
As in item
(4)
above, let
M_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}M_{\alpha,n}
for every
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
.

For each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and
n<\omega
, let

X_{\alpha,n}=\left\{x\in X\colon\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq M_{\alpha,n}\right\}

and let

\textstyle Y_{\alpha,n}=X_{\alpha,n}\setminus\bigcup\left\{X_{\beta,m}\colon\text{either }\beta<\alpha,\text{ or else }\beta=\alpha\text{ and }m<n\right\}.

We claim that
\left\{Y_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\}
is a partition of
X
into
\leq\!\kappa^{+}
completely metrizable spaces, each of weight
<\!\kappa
.

It is clear from the definitions that
\left\{Y_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\}
is a partition of
\bigcup\left\{X_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\}
, and that each
Y_{\alpha,n}
has
\left\{U\cap Y_{\alpha,n}\colon U\in\mathcal{B}\cap M_{\alpha,n}\right\}
as a basis, and therefore
wt(Y_{\alpha,n})\leq|M_{\alpha,n}|<\kappa
.
To finish the proof of the theorem, it remains to show that
\bigcup\left\{X_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\}=X
and that each
Y_{\alpha,n}
is completely metrizable.

Let
x\in X
. Then
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}
is countable (by our choice of
\mathcal{B}
), and there is some
A\in\mathcal{D}
with
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq A
(by our choice of
\mathcal{D}
). There is some
\gamma<\kappa
with
p(\gamma)=A
, and by
(4)
there is some
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and
n<\omega
with
\gamma\in M_{\alpha,n}
. As
p\in M_{\alpha,n}
by
(1)
, we have
p(\gamma)=A\in M_{\alpha,n}
and therefore
A\subseteq M_{\alpha,n}
. (The “therefore” part uses the elementarity of
M_{\alpha,n}
in
H_{\theta}
, which implies countable members of
M_{\alpha,n}
are subsets of
M_{\alpha,n}
.) Hence
x\in X_{\alpha,n}
. As
x
was arbitrary, it follows that
\bigcup\left\{X_{\alpha,n}\colon\alpha<\kappa^{+},n<\omega\right\}=X
.

It remains to show each
Y_{\alpha,n}
is completely metrizable.
For each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
, define
X_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{n<\omega}X_{\alpha,n}
.
Observe that each
X_{\alpha,n}
is closed in
X
, and each
X_{\alpha}
is therefore an
F_{\sigma}
subset of
X
.

Fix
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and
n<\omega
. We consider three cases.

First, suppose
\alpha=\gamma+1
is a successor ordinal. By
(4)
,

\textstyle\bigcup\left\{M_{\beta,m}\colon\beta<\alpha,m<\omega\right\}\,=\,\bigcup_{\beta\leq\gamma}M_{\beta}\,=\,M_{\gamma}\,=\,\bigcup\left\{M_{\gamma,m}\colon m<\omega\right\}.

From this and our definitions, it follows that

\textstyle\bigcup\left\{X_{\beta,m}\colon\beta<\alpha,m<\omega\right\}\,=\,\bigcup\left\{X_{\gamma,m}\colon m<\omega\right\}=X_{\gamma}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha,n}

\displaystyle\,=\,\textstyle X_{\alpha,n}\setminus\bigcup\left\{X_{\beta,m}\colon\text{either }\beta<\alpha,\text{ or else }\beta=\alpha\text{ and }m<n\right\}

\displaystyle\,=\,\textstyle X_{\alpha,n}\setminus\big(X_{\gamma}\cup\bigcup\left\{X_{\alpha,m}\colon m<n\right\}\big).

Because
X_{\gamma}
is an
F_{\sigma}
and each of the
X_{\alpha,m}
is closed, this shows that
Y_{\alpha,n}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

Next, suppose
\alpha
is a limit ordinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)=\omega
. Fix an increasing sequence
\left\langle\gamma_{k}\colon k<\omega\right\rangle
of ordinals with limit
\alpha
. Arguing in a way similar to the previous paragraph,
(4)
implies

\textstyle\bigcup\left\{M_{\beta,m}\colon\beta<\alpha,m<\omega\right\}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup_{k\in\omega}M_{\gamma_{k}}\,=\,\bigcup\left\{M_{\gamma_{k},m}\colon k,m<\omega\right\}.

From this and the definition of the
X_{\beta,m}
, it follows that

\textstyle\bigcup\left\{X_{\beta,m}\colon\beta<\alpha,m<\omega\right\}\,=\,\bigcup\left\{X_{\gamma_{k},m}\colon k,m<\omega\right\}=\bigcup_{k<\omega}X_{\gamma_{k}}.

Consequently, just as in the previous case,

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha,n}

\displaystyle\,=\,\textstyle X_{\alpha,n}\setminus\bigcup\left\{X_{\beta,m}\colon\text{either }\beta<\alpha,\text{ or else }\beta=\alpha\text{ and }m<n\right\}

\displaystyle\,=\,\textstyle X_{\alpha,n}\setminus\big(\bigcup_{k<\omega}X_{\gamma_{k}}\cup\bigcup\left\{X_{\alpha,m}\colon m<n\right\}\big),

and this shows that
X_{\alpha,n}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.

Finally, suppose
\alpha
is a limit ordinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\alpha)>\omega
.
By
(3)
, there is some
n^{*}<\omega
such that, for every
m\geq n^{*}
,
[M_{\alpha,m}]^{\aleph_{0}}\cap M_{\alpha,m}
is cofinal in
\hskip-0.28453pt\big([M_{\alpha,m}]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
.
Let
k=\max\{n,n^{*}\}
.
Fix
x\in X_{\alpha,n}
.
By
(2)
,
M_{\alpha,n}\subseteq M_{\alpha,k}
and therefore
x\in X_{\alpha,k}
; by definition, this means
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq M_{\alpha,n}
.
By our choice of
\mathcal{B}
,
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}
is countable.
Because
k\geq n^{*}
, there is a countable
A\in M_{a,n}
such that
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq A
.
Now
A\in M_{\alpha,n}
implies
A\in M_{\alpha}
, and by
(4)
(specifically, the “continuously increasing” part), there is some
\beta<\alpha
such that
A\in M_{\beta}
. In particular, this means
A\in M_{\beta,m}
for some
m<\omega
.
By the elementarity of
M_{\beta,m}
in
H_{\theta}
and the countability of
A
,
A\in M_{\beta,m}
implies
\left\{U\in\mathcal{B}\colon x\in U\right\}\subseteq A\subseteq M_{\beta,m}
, which implies
x\in X_{\beta,m}
.
Hence
x\notin Y_{\alpha,n}
.
Because
x
was an arbitrary member of
X_{\alpha,n}
, this shows
Y_{\alpha,n}=\emptyset
.
In particular,
Y_{\alpha,n}
is Polish.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
holds, and
\square^{***}_{\omega_{1},\mu}
holds for every singular
\mu
with
\mathrm{cf}(\mu)=\omega
. Then for any uncountable cardinal
\kappa
,

\circ

If
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega
, then
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\kappa
.

\circ

If
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, then either

\circ

every closed subspace of
X
is locally
<\!\kappa
-like, in which case
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\kappa
, or

\circ

some closed subspace of
X
is not locally
<\!\kappa
-like, in which case
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\kappa^{+}
.

In particular,
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)

.

Recall that Theorem
2.2
states
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)\leq\mathrm{cf}\hskip-0.28453pt\big([\kappa]^{\aleph_{0}},\subseteq\hskip-2.84526pt\big)
whenever
X
is a completely metrizable space of weight
\kappa
. Therefore
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
implies
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)=\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)=\kappa
whenever
wt(X)=\kappa
and
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega
.
It remains to prove the
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
case of the theorem.

For this case, note that Corollary
4.2
and
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
combine to give us the lower bounds we need. Specifically: if
\kappa
is an uncountable cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\omega
, then for every completely metrizable space
X
of weight
\kappa
,

\circ

\kappa\leq\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)\leq\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
.

\circ

If some closed subspace of
X
is not locally
<\!\kappa
-like, then

Theorem 4.9
.

Suppose there is some completely metrizable space
X
with
\mathfrak{cov}\hskip-0.42677pt(X)<\mathfrak{par}\hskip-0.28453pt(X)
. Then
0^{\dagger}
exists.

Proof.

If
\square^{***}_{\kappa}
fails for a singular cardinal
\kappa
, then
\square_{\kappa}
fails also by Lemma
4.5
.
The failure of
\square_{\kappa}
for a singular cardinal
\kappa
implies the existence of
0^{\dagger}
, and in fact much more, by results of Cummings and Friedman
[
3
]
.

Therefore, by the previous theorem, it suffices to show that the failure of
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
fails implies
0^{\dagger}
exists.

By an
inner model
we mean a (definable, with parameters) class
M
such that
M\models\mathsf{ZFC}
. An
inner model of
\mathsf{GCH}

means additionally
M\models\mathsf{GCH}
.
We say the Covering Lemma holds over an inner model
M
if for every uncountable
X\subseteq M
, there is some
Y\in M
with
Y\supseteq X
such that
|X|=|Y|
.

It is proved in
[
16
]
(cf. Lemmas 4.9 and 4.10) that if the Covering Lemma holds over an inner model of
\mathsf{GCH}
, then
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
holds.

By work of Dodd and Jensen
[
4
]
, if there is no inner model containing a measurable cardinal, then the Covering Lemma holds over an inner model of
\mathsf{GCH}
(specifically, the Dodd-Jensen core model
K^{\mathrm{DJ}}
). By further work of Dodd and Jensen
[
5
]
, if there is an inner model containing a measurable cardinal but
0^{\dagger}
does not exist, then the Covering Lemma holds over an inner model of
\mathsf{GCH}
(specifically, either
L[U]
for some measure
U
with
crit(U)
as small as possible, or
L[U,C]
for some
C
Prikry-generic over
L[U]
).

Either way, if
0^{\dagger}
does not exist then Jensen’s Covering Lemma holds over an inner model
M
of
\mathsf{GCH}
, and
\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
follows by
[
16
]
.
∎

This argument leaves open the precise consistency strength of
\neg\mathsf{SCH}^{+}
.

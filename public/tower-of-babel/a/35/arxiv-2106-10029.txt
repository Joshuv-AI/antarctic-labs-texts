Contravariant pairings between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules
Adam Brown and Anna Romanov

Abstract
. We classify contravariant pairings between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules over a complex semisimple Lie algebra. These contravariant pairings are useful in extending several classical techniques for category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} to the Miličić–Soergel category 𝒩 \mathcal{N} . We introduce a class of costandard modules which generalize dual Verma modules, and describe canonical maps from standard to costandard modules in terms of contravariant pairings. We show that costandard modules have unique irreducible submodules and share the same composition factors as the corresponding standard Whittaker modules. We show that costandard modules give an algebraic characterization of the global sections of costandard twisted Harish-Chandra sheaves on the associated flag variety, which are defined using holonomic duality of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules. We prove that with these costandard modules, blocks of category 𝒩 \mathcal{N} have the structure of highest weight categories and we establish a BGG reciprocity theorem for 𝒩 \mathcal{N} .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we classify contravariant bilinear pairings between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules. We use these pairings to adapt several classical proofs for Verma modules and category
\mathcal{O}
to the setting of Whittaker modules. We begin with some context.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
.

1

1

The results in this paper hold more generally for reductive Lie algebras. However, since many of our references assume semisimplicity, we chose to continue working under this assumption.

A contravariant form on a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is a bilinear form on
V
such that for
v,w\in V
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\langle X\cdot v,w\rangle=\langle v,\tau(X)\cdot w\rangle
, where
\tau
is the transpose antiautomorphism.
If the linear dual
V^{*}=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
is given the structure of a
\mathfrak{g}
-module via the action

X\cdot f(v)=f(\tau(X)\cdot v)

for
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
f\in V^{*}
,
v\in V
, then the space of contravariant forms on
V
is canonically isomorphic to

\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(V,V^{*}).

A classical result of Shapovalov shows that if
V
is a Verma module, this space is 1-dimensional
[
Sha72
]
.
In
[
BR20
]
, the authors generalize Shapovalov’s results by classifying contravariant forms on standard Whittaker modules. However, unlike for Verma modules, the space of contravariant forms on a standard Whittaker module is no longer guaranteed to be 1-dimensional. This feature of standard Whittaker modules presents an obstacle in generalizing several constructions for category
\mathcal{O}
to Whittaker modules. We provide a brief example of this obstacle below.

In
[
MS97
]
, Miličić–Soergel introduced a category
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules which interpolates between category
\mathcal{O}
and the category of non-degenerate Whittaker modules (Definition
2.2
). Category
\mathcal{N}
contains category
\mathcal{O}
as a full subcategory, and can be viewed as a natural generalization of it. Standard Whittaker modules, which are cyclically generated by a vector on which the nilpotent radical
\mathfrak{n}
of a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
acts by a character
\eta\in\mathfrak{n}^{\ast}
, play the role of Verma modules in category
\mathcal{N}
. One of the celebrated features of category
\mathcal{O}
is that its blocks have the structure of highest weight categories
[
CPS88
]
, with costandard modules defined as the
\mathfrak{n}
-finite vectors in the linear dual of a Verma module. It is natural to ask if this structure extends to
\mathcal{N}
. While it is known that blocks of category
\mathcal{N}
with regular integral infinitesimal character are highest weight categories (this follows by reducing the problem to a singular block of category
\mathcal{O}

[
MS97
]
),
the results of
[
BR20
]
show that the straight-forward generalization of the above definition of costandard modules does not yield the same result for
\mathcal{N}
: defining costandard modules to be spaces of
\eta
-twisted
\mathfrak{n}
-finite vectors in the linear dual of a standard Whittaker module does not give blocks of
\mathcal{N}
the structure of highest weight categories. The problem is the surplus of contravariant forms.

In this paper, we address these disparities by giving an alternate generalization of Shapovalov’s results. Our main contribution is the classification of
contravariant pairings
(Definition
5.1
) between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is regular. Let
M(\lambda,\eta)
be a standard Whittaker module (Definition
2.4
) and
M(\mu)
be a Verma module with highest weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Let
W_{\eta}
be the subgroup of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
determined by
\eta\in\mathfrak{n}^{*}
(Section
2.2
). Then

\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(\lambda,\eta),M(\mu)^{\ast})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\text{ if $\mu\in W_{\eta}\cdot\lambda$}\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Unlike contravariant forms on standard Whittaker modules, these contravariant pairings are unique up to scaling, a feature which more closely resembles the case of Verma modules. With this generalization, we extend well-known arguments for Verma modules directly to standard Whittaker modules. We give 5 examples.

(1)

We define a costandard module in
\mathcal{N}
to be the space of
\eta
-twisted
\mathfrak{n}
-finite vectors in the linear dual of a Verma module (Definition
6.2
). With this definition, contravariant pairings between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules induce canonical maps between standard and costandard modules in
\mathcal{N}
(Lemma
6.4
).

(2)

We show that costandard modules have unique irreducible submodules and share the same composition factors as the corresponding standard Whittaker modules, and that these properties uniquely define the costandard modules up to isomorphism (Theorem
6.9
).

(3)

We prove that costandard modules align under Beilinson–Bernstein localization with costandard
\eta
-twisted Harish-Chandra sheaves on the associated flag variety (Lemma
7.3
).

(4)

We prove that our definitions give blocks of category
\mathcal{N}
the structure of highest weight categories (Corollary
7.4
).

(5)

We prove a Beilinson-Gelfand-Gelfand reciprocity theorem for category
\mathcal{N}
(Theorem
8.2
).

Using contravariant pairings, we are able to generalize the proofs of these classical results for category
\mathcal{O}
in a way that clearly follows the structure of the original arguments. In each of the above cases, when we set
\eta=0
, we recover a traditional proof for category
\mathcal{O}
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
MS97
]
, certain equivalences between blocks of category
\mathcal{N}
with regular integral infinitesimal characters and singular blocks of category
\mathcal{O}
are established. One could alternatively define costandard modules as those corresponding to dual Verma modules under these equivalences. Using this approach, results analogous to (2), (3), (4), and (5) above could be deduced directly from the corresponding results in category
\mathcal{O}
. Our main results about contravariant pairings described above do not follow from these equivalences.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

). In Section

(Definition

), and show that contravariant pairings induce morphisms from standard modules to costandard modules in

Algebraic Preliminaries

Our algebraic setting is a category
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules introduced by Miličić–Soergel in
[
MS97
]
. Each simple module in
\mathcal{N}
is a Whittaker module, and
\mathcal{N}
contains all highest weight modules. In particular,
\mathcal{N}
contains Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand’s category
\mathcal{O}

[
BGG76
]
as a full subcategory. In this section, we define the Miličić–Soergel category
\mathcal{N}
and list some of its basic properties.

2.1.
A category of Whittaker modules

Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
contained in a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\bar{\mathfrak{n}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
be the corresponding triangular decomposition, with
\mathfrak{n}=[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]
. We denote by
\ch{\mathfrak{n}}
the set of Lie algebra homomorphisms
\eta:\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For any Lie algebra

U(\mathfrak{a})
its universal enveloping algebra and

the center of
U(\mathfrak{a})
. Denote by
\Max{Z(\mathfrak{a})}
the set of maximal ideals in
Z(\mathfrak{a})
. Any character
\eta\in\ch{\mathfrak{n}}
can be extended to an algebra homomorphism
\eta:U(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which we will call by the same name. We denote by
\ker\eta\subset U(\mathfrak{n})
the kernel of the algebra homomorphism.

Definition 2.1
.

A

in a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module

such that

for all

is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module which is cyclically generated by a Whittaker vector of type
\eta
.

In
[
MS97
, §1]
, Miličić–Soergel introduced a category

whose simple objects are irreducible Whittaker modules.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the category of
U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules which are

(1)

finitely generated,

(2)

locally
U(\mathfrak{n})
-finite, and

(3)

locally
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
MS14
, Lem. 2.1, Lem. 2.2]

The category

decomposes into

2.2.
Standard and simple Whittaker modules

From the data

Lemma 4.1

U(\mathfrak{g})
is a free
(U(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}),Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}))

Proof.

Let

\text{gr}(Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}))\cong\text{gr}Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}),

Algebraic proof.

First, using the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt basis of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, we have:

U(\mathfrak{g})=\bar{\mathfrak{n}}^{\eta}U(\mathfrak{g})\oplus U(\mathfrak{p}_{\eta}).

Therefore, as
U(\mathfrak{h})
-modules, we have

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},M(\lambda,\eta))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{p}_{\eta}}Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}^{\eta}U(\mathfrak{g})\oplus U(\mathfrak{p}_{\eta}))\otimes_{\mathfrak{p}_{\eta}}Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}}Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)

\displaystyle=H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta},Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)).

The character
\eta|_{\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}}\in\ch\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}
is nondegenerate, and
Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)
is a nondegenerate Whittaker module. Hence it suffices to prove the result for
\eta
nondegenerate. In this setting,
M(\lambda,\eta)=Y(\lambda,\eta)
, and we have a surjective
U(\mathfrak{h})
-module morphism

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{h})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},Y(\lambda,\eta))=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n})}\mathbb{C}_{\chi^{\lambda},\eta}

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\mapsto 1\otimes H\otimes 1.

By the Casselman–Osborne Lemma
[
CO75
, Lem. 2.5]
, if
z\in\xi(\lambda)
, then
p(z)
annihilates
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},Y(\lambda,\eta))
. Therefore, the above surjective homomorphism of
U(\mathfrak{h})
-modules factors through
V^{\lambda}=U(\mathfrak{h})/p(\xi(\lambda))U(\mathfrak{h})
:
V^{\lambda}
U(\mathfrak{h})
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},Y(\lambda,\eta))

Moreover, by Lemma
4.2

V^{\lambda}\cong\bigoplus_{W}\mathbb{C}_{w\cdot\lambda}.

Because
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a free
(U(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}),Z(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}))
-bimodule of rank
|W|
(Lemma
4.1
), we have that

\dim H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},Y(\lambda,\eta))=|W|.

Therefore, the surjective map
V_{\lambda}\rightarrow H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},Y(\lambda,\eta))
is an isomorphism of
U(\mathfrak{h})
-modules, and the theorem follows. ∎

Geometric proof.

We include a geometric proof for the case when
\eta
is nondegenerate, which, by the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem and the initial argument of the algebraic proof, implies the general result.

Assume
\eta\in\ch{\mathfrak{n}}
is nondegenerate. By
[
Milb
, Cor. 2.4]
, the only possible
\mathfrak{h}
-weights of
H_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}},M(\lambda,\eta))
are
\mu\in W\cdot\lambda
. For
\mu\in W\cdot\lambda
, we will can compute
H_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}},M(\lambda,\eta))
using Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.5
. Indeed, for
x\in C(w_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle H_{0}(\overline{\mathfrak{n}},M(\lambda,\eta))_{\mu}

\displaystyle=T_{x}(\Delta_{w_{0}\cdot\mu+\rho}(M(\lambda,\eta)))

\displaystyle=T_{x}(\mathcal{M}(w_{0},w_{0}\cdot\mu+\rho,\eta)).

Because
\mathcal{M}(w_{0},w_{0}\cdot\mu+\rho,\eta):=i_{w_{0}!}(\mathcal{O}_{C(w_{0}),\eta})
,

\dim T_{x}(\mathcal{M}(w_{0},w_{0}\cdot\mu+\rho,\eta))=1.

This implies the result. ∎

5.
Contravariant pairings of standard Whittaker modules

In this section, we define and classify contravariant pairings between standard Whittaker modules and Verma modules. Contravariant pairings play an analogous role for
\mathcal{N}
as contravariant forms for category
\mathcal{O}
. We prove that contravariant pairings are unique up to scalar multiple, and that
M(\lambda,\eta)
admits a nonzero contravariant pairing with a Verma module of highest weight
\mu
if and only if
\mu\in W_{\eta}\cdot\lambda
. We give an explicit construction of a contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
for each
w\in W_{\eta}
. This construction degenerates to the Shapovalov form on a Verma module when
\eta=0
. We finish by describing properties of the (left) radical of a contravariant pairing.

Let
\{y_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\cup\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Pi}
be a Chevalley basis of
\mathfrak{g}
with
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,
y_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
and
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}
such that
[x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}
. Let
\tau:U(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
be the transpose antiautomorphism defined by
\tau(x_{\alpha})=y_{\alpha}
and
\tau(h_{\alpha})=h_{\alpha}
. Let
M(\mu)
be the Verma module of highest weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
M(\lambda,\eta)
the standard Whittaker module (Definition
2.4
) corresponding to
(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\ch\mathfrak{n}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A
contravariant pairing
between
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
V
and
W
is a bilinear pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

such that

\langle uv,w\rangle=\langle v,\tau(u)w\rangle

for all
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
,
v\in V
, and
w\in W
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is regular. There exists a nonzero contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(\mu)
if and only if
\mu\in W_{\eta}\cdot\lambda
. Moreover, a contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(\mu)
is unique up to scalar multiple.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be the space of contravariant pairings between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(\mu)
. We have the following canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(\mu),M(\lambda,\eta)^{\ast})

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi(y)(\cdot):=\langle\cdot,y\rangle.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(\mu),M(\lambda,\eta)^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathfrak{b}}(\mathbb{C}_{\mu},M(\lambda,\eta)^{\ast})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(\mathfrak{n},M(\lambda,\eta)^{\ast})_{\mu}

\displaystyle=H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},M(\lambda,\eta))^{\ast}_{\mu}.

The last equality above follows from tensor-hom adjunction and the
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
X^{\ast}
(which accounts for the duality between
\mathfrak{n}
and
\bar{\mathfrak{n}}
), where we identify
H^{0}(\mathfrak{n},X^{\ast})
as
\Hom_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathbb{C},\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(X,\mathbb{C}))
and
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},X)
as
X\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}\mathbb{C}
, see Definition
2.10
and
2.11
. The result then follows from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

We will now give an explicit construction of a nonzero contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
for
w\in W_{\eta}
which generalizes the Shapovalov form on Verma modules. We start with some preparatory results.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\ker\eta
be the kernel of
\eta:U(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. There is a direct sum decomposition

U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{h})\oplus(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta).

Denote by
\pi_{\eta}:U(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{h})
projection onto the first coordinate in this decomposition.

Proof.

Choose an order on the set of roots so that

\{y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}x^{K}:=y_{\alpha_{n}}^{i_{n}}\cdots y_{\alpha_{1}}^{i_{1}}h_{\alpha_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots h_{\alpha_{r}}^{j_{r}}x_{\alpha_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{n}}^{k_{n}}\mid i_{\ell},j_{\ell},k_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

is a Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Here
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
,
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r})
, and
K=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
are multi-indices,
\bar{I}=(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
, and
y=(y_{\alpha_{n}},\ldots,y_{\alpha_{1}})
,
h=(h_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,h_{\alpha_{r}})
,
x=(x_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x_{\alpha_{n}})
. We can write
x^{\bar{I}}h^{J}x^{K}
as

y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}x^{K}=y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}(x^{K}-\eta(x^{K}))+\eta(x^{K})y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}.

The terms
y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}(x^{K}-\eta(x^{K}))
and
\eta(x^{K})y^{\bar{I}}h^{J}
are elements in
U(\mathfrak{h})+\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta
. By extending linearly, we can write any element of
U(\mathfrak{g})
as a sum of a vector in
U(\mathfrak{h})
, a vector in
\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})
and a vector in
U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta
. The intersection

U(\mathfrak{h})\cap(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta)=0,

so the sum is direct.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{l}
the corresponding reductive Levi subalgebra. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}
decomposes as
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
for a nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
N
be a
U(\mathfrak{l})
-module and
M
the
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module parabolically induced from
N
,

M=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p})}N

where
N
is considered as a
U(\mathfrak{p})
-module via the natural projection map
\mathfrak{p}\rightarrow\mathfrak{l}
. For
x\in N
and
v=1\otimes x\in M
,

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}+U(\mathfrak{g})\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{l})}x.

Proof.

By definition of
M
, we have

U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}+U(\mathfrak{g})\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{l})}x\subseteq\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v.

We will prove the reverse inclusion. Let
u\in\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v
. Then
u\otimes x=0
, so
u=u^{\prime}a
for
u^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
and
a\in\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{p})}x
; i.e.

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g})\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{p})}x.

Since
\mathfrak{n}
acts trivially on
N
and we have a PBW decomposition
U(\mathfrak{p})=U(\mathfrak{l})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n})
,

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{p})}x=U(\mathfrak{p})\mathfrak{n}+U(\mathfrak{p})\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{l})}x.

Hence the reverse inclusion holds.

∎

Proposition 5.5

(
[
Kos78
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
\xi(\lambda)=\ker\chi^{\lambda}\in\Max Z(\mathfrak{g})
,
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
be nondegenerate, and
\omega\in Y(\lambda,\eta)
be a nonzero Whittaker vector. Then

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega=U(\mathfrak{g})\xi(\lambda)+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta,

where
\ker\eta
is the kernel of the map
\eta:U(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
be arbitrary and recall the map
\xi_{\eta}:\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}\rightarrow\Max Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
. Let
v=1\otimes 1\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
and
\omega=1\otimes 1\otimes 1\in M(\lambda,\eta)
. We have

\displaystyle\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v

\displaystyle=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker(w\cdot\lambda),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega

\displaystyle=U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta+U(\mathfrak{g})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda).

Proof.

For the first equality, we apply Lemma
5.4
to the vector
v=1\otimes 1\in M(w\cdot\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathfrak{b}}\mathbb{C}_{w\cdot\lambda}
. By definition of
\mathbb{C}_{w\cdot\lambda}
, we have
\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{h})}x=\ker w\cdot\lambda
. Therefore,
\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker(w\cdot\lambda)
.

For the second equality, we apply Lemma
5.4
to
\omega=1\otimes 1\otimes 1\in M(\lambda,\eta)
. By Lemma
5.4
and Proposition
5.5
, we have

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega=U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}+U(\mathfrak{g})(U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\ker\eta|_{\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}}+U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)).

Clearly,
U(\mathfrak{g})U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\ker\eta|_{\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}}=U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta|_{\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}}
and
U(\mathfrak{g})U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{g})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)
. Moreover,
U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta|_{\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}}=U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta
. Therefore,

\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega=U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta+U(\mathfrak{g})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda).

∎

Let
\omega=1\otimes 1\otimes 1\in M(\lambda,\eta)
and
v=1\otimes 1\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
. For
w\in W_{\eta}
, define a bilinear pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
by

(5.1)

\langle x,y\rangle_{w}:=((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(\tau(u^{\prime})u),

where
u,u^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
are such that
x=u\omega
and
y=u^{\prime}v
, and
\pi_{\eta}
is the projection map defined in Lemma
5.3
. (Note that the choices of
u,u^{\prime}
are not necessarily unique.)

Theorem 5.7
.

The bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\times M(w\cdot\lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of equation
5.1
is well-defined, nonzero, and contravariant. When
\eta=0
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
is the Shapovalov form.

Proof.

The bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
is contravariant by construction. Moreover,
\langle\omega,v\rangle_{w}=1
for
\omega=1\otimes 1\in M(\lambda,\eta)
and
v=1\otimes 1\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
. Therefore, the pairings are nonzero. When
\eta=0
,
W_{\eta}=1
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}:M(\lambda)\times M(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the contravariant form defined by
\langle uv,u^{\prime}v\rangle_{1}=\lambda\circ p(\tau(u^{\prime})u)
, where
p
is the Harish-Chandra homomorphism. This is exactly the Shapovalov form
[
Sha72
, Eq. (5)]
.

We now prove the result for
\eta\neq 0
. Recall the map
\pi_{\eta}:U(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{h})
is given by projection onto the first coordinate in the decomposition
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{h})\oplus(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta)
. To show that the pairing is well-defined, we must prove that
\langle x,y\rangle_{w}
is independent of the choice of
u,u^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
such that
x=u\omega
and
y=u^{\prime}v
. Choose
\tilde{u},\tilde{u}^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
so that
y=u^{\prime}v=\tilde{u}^{\prime}v
and
x=u\omega=\tilde{u}\omega
. To establish that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
is well-defined, we need to check that

(w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta}(\tau(u^{\prime}-\tilde{u}^{\prime})u)=0\text{ and }(w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta}(\tau(u^{\prime})(u-\tilde{u}))=0.

As
\tau(u^{\prime}-\tilde{u}^{\prime})u=\tau(\tau(u)(u^{\prime}-\tilde{u}^{\prime}))\in\tau(\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v)
and
\tau(u^{\prime})(u-\tilde{u})\in\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega
, it suffices to show that

((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(\tau(\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}v))=0,\text{ and }((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(\Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}\omega)=0.

Using Corollary
5.6
, this reduces to showing

(5.2)

\displaystyle((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g}))

\displaystyle=0;

(5.3)

\displaystyle((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(\ker(w\cdot\lambda)U(\mathfrak{g}))

\displaystyle=0;

(5.4)

\displaystyle((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta)

\displaystyle=0;

(5.5)

\displaystyle((w\cdot\lambda)\circ\pi_{\eta})(U(\mathfrak{g})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=0.

Equalities (
5.2
) and (
5.4
) are obvious. Recall that
p_{\eta}:U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{h})
is the Harish-Chandra homomorphism of
U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
; i.e. projection onto the first coordinate in the decomposition
U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})=U(\mathfrak{h})\oplus(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})+U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})
. To establish (
5.5
), we first note that

\pi_{\eta}(\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))=p_{\eta}(\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))\subset\ker w\cdot\lambda

for
w\in W_{\eta}
.
The first equality follows from the fact that
\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)\subset Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
and
\pi_{\eta}
and
p_{\eta}
agree on
Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
. (Indeed, let
z\in Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
. By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem,
z\in U(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
, so we can express
z
as
z=h+xu
for
h\in U(\mathfrak{h}),x\in\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}
, and
u\in U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
, and the element
h\in U(\mathfrak{h})
in this decomposition is uniquely determined by
z
. As
xu\in\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\ker{\eta}
, we have
\pi_{\eta}(z)=h=p_{\eta}(z)
.) The inclusion follows from well-known properties of the Harish-Chandra homomorphism for
Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
.

Using the decomposition
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\oplus(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}^{\eta}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta})
,
we have that

\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{g})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))=\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))+\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)).

By
[
McD85
, Lem. 1.7]
,
[\mathfrak{l}_{\eta},\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}]\subset\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}
. This implies that
[U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}),\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}]\subset U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}
, hence
[\xi_{\eta}(\lambda),\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}]\subset U(\mathfrak{g})\ker\eta
. From this we conclude that
\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^{\eta}\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))=0
.

An application of Lemma
5.3
to
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
yields the decomposition

U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})=U(\mathfrak{h})\oplus(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})+U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\ker\eta|_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})}).

Because
\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)
commutes with
\ker\eta|_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})}
, we can use this decomposition to conclude that

\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))=\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{h})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda)).

Finally, because
Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})\subset U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})_{0}=\{u\in U(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}):[h,u]=0\text{ for all }h\in\mathfrak{h}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{h})\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=U(\mathfrak{h})\pi_{\eta}(\xi_{\eta}(\lambda))\subset\ker w\cdot\lambda.

This proves (
5.5
).

It remains to show (
5.3
). Because
\pi_{\eta}
is the identity on
\ker w\cdot\lambda
, we have

\pi_{\eta}(\ker w\cdot\lambda)\subset\ker w\cdot\lambda.

Moreover, because
[\mathfrak{h},\bar{\mathfrak{n}}]\subset\bar{\mathfrak{n}}
, we have
\pi_{\eta}(\ker(w\cdot\lambda)\bar{\mathfrak{n}}U(\mathfrak{g}))=0
. Using the decomposition in Lemma
5.3
, we conclude that

\pi_{\eta}(\ker(w\cdot\lambda)U(\mathfrak{g}))=\pi_{\eta}(\ker(w\cdot\lambda)U(\mathfrak{h}))=\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{h})\ker(w\cdot\lambda)).

Again, because
U(\mathfrak{h})\subset U(\mathfrak{g})_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\eta}(U(\mathfrak{h})\ker w\cdot\lambda)

\displaystyle=U(\mathfrak{h})\pi_{\eta}(\ker w\cdot\lambda)\subset\ker w\cdot\lambda.

This proves (
5.3
), and the proposition.
∎

Combining Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.7
, we see that if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a nonzero contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(\mu)
, then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a scalar multiple the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
for some
w\in W_{\eta}
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is regular. Any contravariant pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:M(\lambda,\eta)\times M(\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is uniquely determined by
\langle\omega,v\rangle
, where
\omega
and
v
are the generating Whittaker vectors in
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(\mu)
, respectively.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
be the contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
defined by equation
5.1
, for
w\in W_{\eta}
.

(1)

If
\nu,\gamma\in\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}
,
\nu\neq\gamma
,
x\in M(\lambda,\eta)_{\nu}
, and
y\in M(w\cdot\lambda)_{\gamma}
, then
\langle x,y\rangle_{w}=0
.

(2)

The left radical
\text{Rad}^{L}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}:=\{v\in M(\lambda,\eta):\langle v,M(w\cdot\lambda)\rangle_{w}=0\}
is the maximal proper submodule of
M(\lambda,\eta)
.

Proof.

The proof of (1) is identical to the standard category
\mathcal{O}
proof, which is as follows. Suppose
x\in M(\lambda,\eta)_{\nu}
and
y\in M(w\cdot\lambda)_{\gamma}
with
\nu\neq\gamma
as characters of
\mathfrak{z}
. By the contravariance of the bilinear pairing, for each
z\in\mathfrak{z}
(recall that
\tau(z)=z
), we have

\displaystyle\nu(z)\langle x,y\rangle_{w}

\displaystyle=\langle zx,y\rangle_{w}=\langle x,zy\rangle_{w}=\gamma(z)\langle x,y\rangle_{w}.

Therefore,
\langle x,y\rangle_{w}=0
.

We will now prove (2). If
\eta
is nondegenerate, or, more generally, if
M(\lambda,\eta)
is irreducible, then
\text{Rad}^{L}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}=0
. Assume that
\eta
is degenerate (i.e.
\eta
vanishes on at least one simple root space) and
M(\lambda,\eta)
is reducible. Let
N
be the maximal proper submodule of
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
x
a nonzero
\mathfrak{z}
-weight vector in
N
with weight
\mu
. By
[
McD85
, Thm. 2.5]
,
\mu\neq\lambda
as characters of
\mathfrak{z}
. The generating Whittaker vector (i.e. the highest weight vector)
v
of
M(w\cdot\lambda)
has
\mathfrak{z}
-weight
w\cdot\lambda
. Moreover, because
w\in W_{\eta}
, we have that
w\cdot\lambda=\lambda
as characters of
\mathfrak{z}
(recall that
M(w\cdot\lambda,\eta)\cong M(\lambda,\eta)
for each
w\in W_{\eta}
). Therefore, (1) implies

\langle x,v\rangle_{w}=0.

Because
\mathfrak{z}
acts semisimply on
N
and the pairing is bilinear, we have
\langle N,v\rangle_{w}=0
.

If
y\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
, then there exists
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
such that
y=uv
. Therefore, for any
x\in N
,

\langle x,y\rangle_{w}=\langle x,uv\rangle_{w}=\langle\tau(u)x,v\rangle_{w}=0,

because
\tau(u)x\in N
. Therefore,
N\subseteq\text{Rad}^{L}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
. The result then follows from the fact that the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
is nonzero and the left radical of the pairing is a submodule of
M(\lambda,\eta)
.

∎

Remark 5.10
.

In
[
Mat88
, §3.2]
, Matumoto uses the Shapovalov form to define a contravariant pairing between an irreducible module in category
\mathcal{O}
and its completion. A similar construction applies to Verma modules: because weight spaces of
M(w\cdot\lambda)
are orthogonal with respect to the Shapovalov form, the Shapovalov form extends to a contravariant pairing between the completed Verma module
\overline{M(w\cdot\lambda)}
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
. The Whittaker functors of Section
2.3
identify standard Whittaker modules with a subspace of the completion of a Verma module. Therefore, we can restrict the above pairing to define a contravariant pairing between
(\overline{M(w\cdot\lambda)})_{\eta}=\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}(M(w\cdot\lambda))
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
. By Proposition
2.9
,
\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}(M(w\cdot\lambda))=M(\lambda,\eta)
when
w\in W_{\eta}
. Therefore, for each
w\in W_{\eta}
, this construction yields a contravariant pairing between
M(\lambda,\eta)
and
M(w\cdot\lambda)
. However (in the setting where
\lambda+\rho
is regular and integral), unless
w
is the longest element of
W_{\eta}
, the pairing constructed in this way is trivial (i.e. the zero pairing). Therefore, this construction yields exactly one of the contravariant pairings
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
of Theorem
5.2
. It is interesting that the other contravariant pairings are not obtained in this way.

6.
Costandard modules

In this section we define costandard modules in the category
\mathcal{N}
(Definition
6
) and show that each contravariant pairing induces a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism from standard to costandard modules (Lemma
6.4
). These
\mathfrak{g}
-morphisms are the algebraic analogues to the canonical maps between standard and costandard twisted Harish-Chandra sheaves introduced in Section
3.2
. Our costandard modules share many of the fundamental properties of dual Verma modules: each has a unique irreducible submodule and the same set of composition factors as the corresponding standard module (Theorem
6.5
). In fact, these two conditions provide a set of universal properties for costandard modules (Theorem
6.9
).

Given a
U(\mathfrak{n})
-module
V
, recall from equation (
2.2
) that
(V)_{\eta}
denotes the space of
\eta
-twisted
U(\mathfrak{n})
-finite vectors in
V
:

(V)_{\eta}:=\{v\in V:\text{$\forall u\in U(\mathfrak{n})$, $\exists$ $k$ s.t. }(u-\eta(u))^{k}v=0\}.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
Kos78
, Lem. 4.2.1]
)
.

For any
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V
, the subspace
(V)_{\eta}
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-submodule.

Proof.

Because the action map
\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V\rightarrow V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module morphism, for any
X\in\mathfrak{n}
,
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
v\in(V)_{\eta}
, and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we have

(X-\eta(X))^{n}\cdot Y\cdot v=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}n\\
k\end{pmatrix}(\ad X)^{n-k}Y\cdot(X-\eta(X))^{k}\cdot v.

The adjoint action of
\mathfrak{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent, so there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(\ad X)^{\ell}Y=0
. Because
v\in(V)_{\eta}
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(X-\eta(X))^{m}\cdot v=0
. Hence for any
X\in\mathfrak{n}
,
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
v\in(V)_{\eta}
, we can choose
n
large enough so that every term in the sum is zero. For such an
n
,
(X-\eta(X))^{n}\cdot Y\cdot v=0
, and thus
Y\cdot v\in(V)_{\eta}
.
∎

For a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V
, denote by
V^{*}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
the full linear dual of
V
. The space
V^{*}
becomes a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module via the action
u\cdot f(-)=f(\tau(u)\cdot-)
for
u\in U(\mathfrak{g})
,
f\in V^{*}
.

Definition 6.2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
, and
w\in W_{\eta}
, define the
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module

M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta):=\left(M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast}\right)_{\eta}.

We call
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)
the
w
-costandard module
corresponding to the standard module
M(\lambda,\eta)
.

We will later show, in Corollary
6.10
, that
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)\cong M^{\vee}_{y}(\lambda,\eta)
for each

. Hence, up to isomorphism, the definition of
w
-costandard modules does not depend on the choice of
w\in W_{\eta}
. However, we will retain notation which reflects the choice of

until Corollary

.

Remark 6.3
.

We can describe the construction in Definition
6.2
in terms of coinduction, similarly to a dual Verma module. Indeed,
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
\eta
-twisted
U(\mathfrak{n})
-finite vectors in

\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda},\mathbb{C})=\Hom_{U(\mathfrak{b})}(U(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbb{C}_{\lambda})=\mathrm{coind}_{U(\mathfrak{b})}^{U(\mathfrak{g})}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}.

Lemma 6.4
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
, and
w\in W_{\eta}
, the
w
-costandard module
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
is an object in the category
\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
. Moreover, the contravariant pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\times M(w\cdot\lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined in (
5.1
) induces a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\varphi_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\rightarrow M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta).

Proof.

Recalling the definitions of Section
2.3
, the formal completion of the dual Verma module
M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda)
is canonically isomorphic to the linear dual of a Verma module (see
[
Bac97
, §3]
):

\displaystyle\overline{M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda)}

\displaystyle\cong((M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast})_{\eta=0})^{\ast}

\displaystyle\cong M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast}.

Therefore, the Whittaker functor
\overline{\Gamma}
applied to a dual Verma module is isomorphic to a costandard module:

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}(M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda))

\displaystyle=(M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast})_{\eta}

\displaystyle=M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta).

Because
\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}
is a functor from
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
to
\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
, the
w
-costandard module
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
is an object in
\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
.

Each contravariant pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
induces a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\varphi_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\rightarrow M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast},\hskip 5.69054ptv\mapsto\langle v,\cdot\rangle_{w}.

Because
\varphi
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism, the image of
\varphi
is contained in
(M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast})_{\eta}
. Therefore each contravariant pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
defines a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\varphi_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\rightarrow M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta),\hskip 5.69054ptv\mapsto\langle v,\cdot\rangle_{w}.

∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
, and
w\in W_{\eta}
. Then

(1)

[M(\lambda,\eta)]=[M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)]
in
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
, and

(2)

M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
contains a unique irreducible submodule, which is isomorphic to
L(\lambda,\eta)
.

Proof.

In the proof of Lemma
6.4
it was shown that

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}(M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda))

\displaystyle=M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta).

Because
\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}
is exact, we have a homomorphism of Grothendieck groups:
\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}:\mathcal{K}\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}\mathcal{N}
. Because
M(\lambda,\eta)=\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}(M(w\cdot\lambda))

[
Bac97
, Prop. 6.9]
and
[M(w\cdot\lambda)]=[M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda)]
in
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{O}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle[M(\lambda,\eta)]

\displaystyle=\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}([M(w\cdot\lambda)])

\displaystyle=\overline{\Gamma}_{\eta}([M^{\vee}(w\cdot\lambda)])

\displaystyle=[M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)].

This proves (1).

Again, by Lemma
6.4
, each contravariant pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
defines a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\varphi_{w}:M(\lambda,\eta)\rightarrow M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta),\hskip 5.69054ptv\mapsto\langle v,\cdot\rangle_{w}.

The kernel of this morphism is the left radical of the form,
\text{Rad}^{L}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
. By Proposition
5.9
,
\text{Rad}^{L}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
is the unique maximal proper submodule of
M(\lambda,\eta)
. Hence the image of
\varphi_{w}
is isomorphic to
L(\lambda,\eta)
.

We will now show that
\im\varphi_{w}
is the unique irreducible submodule of
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)
. Because
M(w\cdot\lambda)
is a free rank 1 left
U(\bar{\mathfrak{n}})
-module, we have that

\displaystyle H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta))

\displaystyle=H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast})

\displaystyle\cong H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},M(w\cdot\lambda))^{\ast}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}_{\lambda},

where the isomorphism are as
\mathfrak{z}
-modules.

Suppose that
X
is an irreducible submodule of
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)
. Then

H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},X)\subset H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta))\cong\mathbb{C}.

Any irreducible object in
\mathcal{N}
must contain a Whittaker vector (Proposition
2.5
(v)), so
H_{\eta}^{0}(\mathfrak{n},X)\neq 0
. Therefore,
H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},X)=H^{0}_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n},M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta))
and
X\cap\im\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}\neq 0
. By irreducibility,
X=\im\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w}
.
∎

The remaining results of this section, concluding with Theorem
6.9
, show that costandard Whittaker modules satisfy a universal property.

Lemma 6.6
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is regular. Let
V\in\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
be a module such that

H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0,

for some
w\in W_{\eta}
. Then there exists a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism from
V
to
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
.

Proof.

We begin the proof by showing that
\dim H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)<\infty
. By Theorem
4.3
,
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},M(\mu,\eta))
is finite-dimensional for each
\mu\in W_{\eta}\cdot\lambda
. Because each simple module
L(\mu,\eta)\in\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
is the quotient of a standard module and
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}-=H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},-)
is right exact,
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},L(\mu,\eta))
is finite dimensional for each simple module
L(\mu,\eta)\in\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
. Because
V
is finite length,
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)
is finite dimensional.

Therefore, if
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
, then
(H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)^{\ast})_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
.
By tensor-hom adjuction,

H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)^{\ast}\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\bar{\mathfrak{n}}}V,\mathbb{C})\cong\Hom_{\mathfrak{n}}(\mathbb{C},\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C}))\cong H^{0}(\mathfrak{n},V^{\ast}).

We have shown that if
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
, then
H^{0}(\mathfrak{n},V^{\ast})_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
. Therefore, there exists a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\varphi:M(w\cdot\lambda)\rightarrow V^{\ast}.

The morphism
\varphi
determines a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}:V

\displaystyle\rightarrow M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast}

given by
\hat{\varphi}(v)(x):=\varphi(x)(v)
for
v\in V
and
x\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
. We confirm that
\hat{\varphi}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism with the following simple calculation. For
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
v\in V
, and
x\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(uv)(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)(uv)

\displaystyle=(\tau(u)\varphi(x))(v)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\tau(u)x)(v)

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}(v)(\tau(u)x)

\displaystyle=(u\hat{\varphi}(v))(x).

Here
\tau:U(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
is the transpose antiautomorphism (Section
5
). Because
\varphi
is nonzero, there exists
x\in M(w\cdot\lambda)
and
v\in V
such that
\varphi(x)(v)\neq 0
. Therefore,
\hat{\varphi}(v)\neq 0
and
\hat{\varphi}
is nonzero. Because
V\in\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
, the image of
\hat{\varphi}
must be contained in
M(w\cdot\lambda)^{\ast}_{\eta}=M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
.
∎

The following proposition involves categories of
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
-modules as well as categories of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, and will require some additional notation. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\eta\in\ch\mathfrak{n}
, let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\widehat{\xi(\lambda)},\eta\right)
be the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules which are finitely generated, locally

-finite, locally
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite, annihilated by a power of
\xi(\lambda)
, and locally annihilated by a power of
\ker\eta
(see Proposition
2.3
). Recall that, for
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,
\xi_{\eta}(\mu)
is a maximal ideal in
Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
(see Section
2.2
). Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}\left(\widehat{\xi_{\eta}(\mu)},\eta\right)
be the category of
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
-modules which are finitely generated, locally
U(\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})
-finite, locally
Z(\mathfrak{l}_{\eta})
-finite, annihilated by a power of
\xi_{\eta}(\mu)
, and locally annihilated by a power of
\ker\eta|_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})}

(X)_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}
denote the generalized
\mathfrak{z}

Proposition 6.7

(
[
Bro19

)
.

The projection from a module
X\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\widehat{\xi(\lambda)},\eta\right)
onto a generalized
\mathfrak{z}
-weight space
(X)_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}
defines an exact functor

\displaystyle(\cdot)_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}:\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\widehat{\xi(\lambda)},\eta\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}\left(\widehat{\xi_{\eta}(\mu)},\eta\right).

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is regular. Let
V\in\mathcal{N}(\xi(\lambda),\eta)
. If
[V]=[M(\lambda,\eta)]
in the Grothendieck group
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{N}
, then
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
for each
w\in W_{\eta}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.5
,
M(\lambda,\eta)_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}=0
for each
\mu>\lambda
, where
>
denotes the partial order on
\mathfrak{z}^{\ast}
induced by
\Sigma^{+}-\Sigma^{+}_{\eta}
(see
[
McD85
, §1]
). Hence by Proposition
6.7
,
V_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}=0
for each
\mu>\lambda
.
By
[
McD85
, Prop. 5]
,

\bar{\mathfrak{n}}^{\eta}V_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}\subset\bigoplus_{\nu<\mu}V_{\nu_{\mathfrak{z}}}.

Therefore,
(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}^{\eta}V)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}=0
. (If not, then there must exist
\mu>\lambda
such that
V_{\mu_{\mathfrak{z}}}\neq 0
, a contradiction). This implies that
(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}V)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}\subset(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}V)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
because projection onto a generalized weight space is linear and

. Moreover, because
\mathfrak{z}
commutes with
\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}
, the action of
\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta}
preserves
\mathfrak{z}
-weights, and

. In other words, the map

induced by inclusion is injective.

By Proposition
2.5
,
M(\lambda,\eta)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}\cong Y_{\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}}(\lambda,\eta)
as
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
-modules. Because
[V]=[M(\lambda,\eta)]
, Proposition
6.7
then implies that

as well. An application of Theorem
4.3
to the

-module

lets us conclude that
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta},V_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}})_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0

. Therefore,
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
for each

by the injectivity of

.

∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Assume
\lambda+\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is regular. Suppose
V\in\mathcal{N}
is a module such that

(1)

[V]=[M(\lambda,\eta)]
in
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{N}
, and

(2)

V
contains a unique irreducible submodule which is isomorphic to

.

Then
V\cong M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
for each

.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.8
,
[V]=[M(\lambda,\eta)]
implies that
H_{0}(\bar{\mathfrak{n}},V)_{w\cdot\lambda}\neq 0
for each

. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
6.6
to get a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism
\phi_{w}:V\rightarrow M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
for each
w\in W_{\eta}
.

Recall that, by Theorem
6.5
,
M^{\vee}_{w}(\lambda,\eta)
contains a unique irreducible submodule which is isomorphic to

. Because
\phi_{w}
is nonzero,
\im\phi_{w}
must contain the unique irreducible submodule of
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
.

Let
K=\ker\phi_{w}
. The assumption that
[V]=[M(\lambda,\eta)]
implies (by Proposition
2.5
and Proposition
6.7
) that
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
and
V_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
are irreducible
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
-modules.
Let
(\phi_{w})_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}:V_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}\rightarrow M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
be the restriction of
\phi_{w}
to the generalized
\mathfrak{z}
-weight space of weight
\lambda
. By irreducibility of the
\mathfrak{l}_{\eta}
-modules and Dixmier’s lemma, either
(\phi_{w})_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}=0
or
(\phi_{w})_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
is an isomorphism. Because the unique irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
M_{w}^{\vee}(\lambda,\eta)
contains the weight space

and

-submodule, we conclude that

. Therefore,
(\phi_{w})_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{z}}}
is an isomorphism and

.

Because
\phi_{w}
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-morphism,
K
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
V
. Moreover,
K
is finite length and must have an irreducible submodule

. Because

,

. Therefore

\mathfrak{g}

.

∎

immediately implies that all

.

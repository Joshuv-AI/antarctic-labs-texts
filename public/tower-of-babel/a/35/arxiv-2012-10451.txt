RG flow of integrable ℰ \mathscr{E} -models Preprint: MI-TH-2033
Falk Hassler

Abstract
We compute the one- and two-loop RG flow of integrable σ \sigma -models with Poisson-Lie symmetry. They are characterised by a twist function with 2 ​ N 2N simple poles/zeros and a double pole at infinity. Hence, they capture many of the known integrable deformations in a unified framework, which has a geometric interpretation in terms of surface defects in a 4D Chern-Simons theory. We find that these models are one-loop renormalisable and present a very simple expression for the flow of the twist function. At two loops only models with N N =1 are renormalisable. Applied to the λ \lambda -deformation on a semisimple group manifold, our results reproduce the β \beta -functions in the literature.

There are two important concepts, integrability and duality, which have led to tremendous insights into the strong coupling region of quantum field theories
Beisert et al. 2012
. Both are closely tied to the paramount importance of symmetries in nature. On the one hand, symmetries constrain the dynamics of field theories by imposing that certain observables are conserved. In generic theories the number of independent conserved charges is very small compared to the total count of degrees of freedom. Integrable systems are distinguished by the fact that every degree of freedom comes with an independent conserved charge. Hence, their dynamics is completely governed by symmetry. But this does not render them trivial. A considerable challenge is that the relevant symmetry acts in a highly non-trivial way and identifying it remains a formidable task. Once completed, exact results for all thinkable observables are accessible. Hence, integrable models provide an ideal testing ground for new ideas and concepts in physics. Dualities on the other hand are symmetries on the moduli space of theories. They are versatile and for example relate the strong and the weak coupling regime of particular quantum field theories
Montonen and Olive 1977
, different space-time geometries in string theory
Buscher 1987
, or even gauge theory with quantum gravity
Maldacena 1999
.

In this letter we are interested in a setup where both, integrability and a duality, called Poisson-Lie (PL) T-duality
Klimčík and Ševera 1995
, go hand in hand. More specifically, we consider a class of 2D, integrable field theories whose couplings describe the geometry of a
D
-dimensional target space. The action describing the corresponding
\sigma
-model reads

S=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma}\left(g_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\star\mathrm{d}x^{j}+B_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right)\,.

(1)

An arbitrary choice of the target space metric
g_{ij}
and
B

does not result in integrability. On the contrary, integrable
\sigma

Integrable
\mathscr{E}
-models

may be understood as a rewriting of the action (
1

\mathscr{H}^{MN}=\begin{pmatrix}g_{mn}-B_{mk}g^{kl}B_{ln}\quad&B_{mk}g^{kn}\\

(3)

\sigma

\{J_{M}(\sigma),J_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\}=2\pi\alpha^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\eta_{MN}

\eta_{MN}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\delta_{n}^{m}\\

;

\displaystyle=F_{\widehat{A}\widehat{B}}{}^{\widehat{C}}J_{\widehat{C}}(\sigma)\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})+\eta_{\widehat{A}\widehat{B}}\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int J_{\widehat{A}}\mathscr{H}^{\widehat{A}\widehat{B}}J_{\widehat{B}}\,.

A first hint towards integrable
\mathscr{E}
-models is that the field equations for the currents
\partial_{\tau}J^{\widehat{A}}=\{J^{\widehat{A}},H\}
can be written in terms of the flatness condition

\mathrm{d}\mathscr{J}+\frac{1}{2}[\mathscr{J},\mathscr{J}]=0

As a first step to relate

We are interested in
\mathscr{E}
-models whose generalised fluxed are related to the twist function

Our discussion so far was only concerned with the classical regime. But clearly the ultimate objective is to quantize the structures we encountered. Unfortunately, it turns out that this task is more complicated than one might initially think. Quantisation of integrable systems usually employs the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method (QISM)

;
*Faddeev:1979gh

;
*Appadu:2018ioy

\lambda
-deformation. This observation emphasis how important it is to understand another quantum effect for these models, their RG flow. Fortunately, it is possible to exploit PL symmetry to extract it up to two loops with minimal effort.

Intriguingly, it is possible to find a vector
\beta^{(1)}_{A}
that solves (
20
) for arbitrary
N
. It can be written in terms of a function
f(z)
with
N
simple poles and a double pole at infinity,

f(z)=\sum\limits_{a=1}^{N}\frac{\res_{\zeta_{a}}\varphi^{-1}(z)}{z-\zeta_{a}}-\ell_{\infty}\,,

(24)

where
\ell_{\infty}
is fixed by the constraint
\sum_{A=1}^{2N}f(z_{A})=0
. With
f(z)
at hand, the one-loop
\beta
-functions of the coordinates
z_{A}
have the simple form

\beta^{(1)}_{A}=c_{\mathfrak{g}}f(z_{A})\,,

(25)

which was already conjectured in
Delduc et al. 2020
. Taking furthermore into account that
K_{A}
and
l_{\infty}
are invariant, the complete one-loop RG flow of the twist function is summarised by the compact equation

\dot{\varphi}(z)=c_{\mathfrak{g}}\sum\limits_{A=1}^{2N}\frac{f(z_{A})K_{A}}{(z-z_{A})^{2}}+\mathscr{O}(\alpha^{\prime 2})\,.

(26)

It is instructive to understand this result in terms of a hierarchy of consistent truncations. In a general
\sigma
-model an infinite number of couplings, the values of the metric and
B
-field at all points in the target space, flow. PL symmetry completely decouples
D^{2}
of them and it is save (or more formally consistent) to remove (truncate) the reset of them. This is not the end though. Remember that
D=N\dim\mathfrak{g}
, while we found
2N-1

There are various ways to interpret this result, among them are:

•

Integrability is broken by two-loop effects.

•

One can construct a larger class of integrable
\mathscr{E}
-models that are incorporated by the RG flow beyond one loop.

•

The
\mathscr{E}
-operator, and with it the Lax connection, receive quantum corrections. Saying that they are modified at the order
\alpha^{\prime}
.

It is beyond the scope of this short letter to explore these ideas further. At the moment, the last option seems most plausible. But there is definitely a lot more to learn from the intriguing connection of PL symmetry and integrability. Approaching this question from the 4D Chern-Simons theory description likely provides additional clues.

The
\lambda
-deformation

on a semisimple Lie group
G
provides a good crosscheck of our results as its one- and two-loop RG flow has been intensively studied in the literature recently. Its twist function is characterised by

We use the deformation parameter
\kappa=(1-\lambda)(1+\lambda)^{-1}

Hoare et al. 2019
;
Hassler and Rochais 2020b

\lambda

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\kappa

Itsios et al. 2014

f(z)=\frac{z(1-\kappa^{2})-2k\kappa^{3}}{2z(\kappa^{2}-1)+4k\kappa}\,,

\lambda

K_{A}=k(-1\,,\,1)

\kappa
=1. Form (

\beta

\beta_{A}=(\beta\,,\,-\beta)

\beta^{(1)}=c_{\mathfrak{g}}f(z)=-\frac{c_{\mathfrak{g}}\kappa}{2}\,.

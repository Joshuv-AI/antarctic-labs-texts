1.
Introduction

Time-dependent systems of coupled nonlinear Schrödinger equations of the form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{1}=\Delta\Phi_{1}+g_{1}(|\Phi_{1}|^{2})\Phi_{1}+\partial_{1}\varphi(|\Phi_{1}|^{2},|\Phi_{2}|^{2})\Phi_{1},\\
-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{2}=\Delta\Phi_{2}+g_{2}(|\Phi_{2}|^{2})\Phi_{2}+\partial_{2}\varphi(|\Phi_{1}|^{2},|\Phi_{2}|^{2})\Phi_{2},\\
\Phi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(x,t)\in\mathbb{C},\,j=1,2,N\geq 1,\end{cases}\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},

are used as model for various physical phenomena, for instance binary mixtures of Bose-Einstein condensates, or the propagation of mutually incoherent wave packets in nonlinear optics; see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
18
]
.
The ansatz
\Phi_{j}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{j}t}u_{j}(x),j=1,2
for solitary wave solutions leads to the elliptic system

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+\lambda_{1}u_{1}=f_{1}(u_{1})+\partial_{1}H(u_{1},u_{2}),\\
-\Delta u_{2}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}=f_{2}(u_{2})+\partial_{2}H(u_{1},u_{2}),\end{cases}\quad\hbox{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N},

with
f_{j}(u_{j})=g_{j}(|u_{j}|^{2})u_{j},j=1,2
and
H(u_{1},u_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(|u_{1}|^{2},|u_{2}|^{2})
.

Since the masses

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Phi_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,j=1,2

are preserved along trajectories of (
1.1
), it is natural to consider them as prescribed. A natural approach to obtain solutions to (
1.2
) satisfying the normalization constraints

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2}=a_{j},j=1,2

consists in finding critical points
(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}^{2})
of the energy functional

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(F_{1}(u_{1})+F_{2}(u_{2})+H(u_{1},u_{2})\right)\mathrm{d}x

under the constraint (
1.3
), where
F_{j}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f_{j}(\tau)d\tau
is the primitive function of
f_{j},j=1,2
. Then the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
appear as Lagrange multipliers.

In the present paper we focus on the existence of solutions
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the specific problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\mu_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\beta r_{1}|u|^{r_{1}-2}u|v|^{r_{2}}\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}|v|^{q-2}v+\beta r_{2}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}-2}v\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=b.\end{cases}

Here
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta>0
are prescribed as are the masses
a,b>0
. The corresponding energy functional
J:\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is
defined by

J[u,v]=\frac{1}{2}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]-\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

where

\mathcal{H}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is equipped with the norm

\|[u,v]\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left(\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The normalization constraint being
S_{a}\times S_{b}
where

S_{a}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{b}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}=b\right\}.

It is standard to show that
J:\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{1}
and that
J^{\prime}
takes bounded sets to bounded sets.

For the case
N=3,r_{1}=r_{2}=2,\mu_{1}=\mu_{2},p=q=4
, assuming that
\beta<0
, the existence of infinitely many positive solutions (namely
u>0
and
v>0
) to
Problem (
1.4
) was obtained by Bartsch and Soave in
[
5
]
. When
\beta>0
,
Gou and Jeanjean in
[
11
]
obtain a multiplicity result. More precisely, under one of the following conditions

(H_{0})

N\geq 1,1<p,q<2+\frac{4}{N},r_{1},r_{2}>1,2+\frac{4}{N}<r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
;

(H_{1})

N\geq 1,2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q<2^{*},r_{1},r_{2}>1,r_{1}+r_{2}<2+\frac{4}{N}
,

two couples of solutions to Problem (
1.4
) are obtained. One is a local minimizer for
J
constrained on
S_{a}\times S_{b}
, the other is obtained through a constrained mountain pass or a constrained linking. In both cases it is assumed that
0<\beta<\beta_{0}
is small enough.

For the pure mass super-critical case, i.e.,
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
, Bartsch, Jeanjean and Soave in
[
4
]
consider the case of
N=3,p=q=4,r_{1}=r_{2}=2
. They obtain the existence of positive solutions to Problem (
1.4
) provided
0<\beta<\beta_{1}(a,b)
or
\beta>\beta_{2}(a,b)
. A more general case is considered by Bartsch and Jeanjean in
[
3
]
but it is also required there that
0<\beta<\beta_{1}(a,b)
or
\beta>\beta_{2}(a,b)
. The values
\beta_{1}(a,b)
and
\beta_{2}(a,b)
depend on
a,b
heavily. Typically,
\beta_{1}(a,b)\rightarrow 0
as
a/b\rightarrow 0
or
a/b\rightarrow+\infty
, while
\beta_{2}(a,b)\rightarrow+\infty
as
a/b\rightarrow 0
or
a/b\rightarrow+\infty
. In particular, there is no value of
\beta>0
so that the results of
[
4
,
3
]
yield a solution to Problem (
1.4
) for all masses.

In view of the above considerations, it is natural to investigate whether there exists some
\beta>0
for which the existence of solutions is valid for all masses. Actually, this issue was already raised as an
open problem
by Bartsch, Jeanjean and Soave, see
[
4
, Remark 1.3-(a)and (d)]
. In
[
6
]
, Bartsch, Zhong and Zou gave a first answer.
Precisely, assuming that
N=3,p=q=4,r_{1}=r_{2}=2
, they develop a new approach based on fixed point index in cones, bifurcation theory, and a continuation method to deal with Problem (
1.4
). For the range of
\beta\in(0,\tau_{0}\min\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}]\cup(\tau_{0}\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\},+\infty)
, where
\tau_{0}\in(0,1)
is explicitly known, the existence result holds for all masses. For the range of
\beta\in(\tau_{0}\min\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\},\tau_{0}\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}]
whether the existence result holds depends on the masses
a
and
b
, more precisely, on the ratio
a/b
. For example, when
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
, for
a/b
small, the existence result is satisfied. On the contrary when
\mu_{2}\leq\beta\leq\tau_{0}\mu_{1}
, there is no positive normalized solution provided
a/b
is large.

Our aim in the present paper is to make further advances on Bartsch-Jeanjean-Soave’s open problem. Namely to explicit new ranges of
\beta>0
for which there exist a solution to Problem (
1.4
) for any
a>0
and
b>0
. To appreciate our results observe that in
[
6
]
all the nonlinear terms share the same homogeneity. In our results
p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}
are not necessary the same.

Remark 1.1
.

For the fixed frequencies problem (that is when
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
are given) when
N=3,r_{1}=r_{2}=2,\mu_{1}=\mu_{2},p=q=4
, usually one also need some structural conditions on the parameters to guarantee the existence of positive solutions. In that case there is also an open problem, which is called Sirakov’s open problem in the literature, concerning the determination of the best range for the existence of positive solutions. In that direction we refer to
[
16
, Remark 4]
and
[
19
]
.

Hypotheses and statement of results

In order to state our main results we first recall some classical facts.

For

[

For

It is also classical that these solutions are unique and can be obtained by scaling the solution to (

Precisely, for

Let us now describe the main steps of the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

The ground states will be obtain as minimum of the functional
J
on the natural constraint

(2.6)

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{[0,0]\}:P[u,v]=0\right\},

where

\displaystyle P[u,v]:=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

Here also one can show, in a standard way, that
P
is of class
C^{1}
and that
P^{\prime}
takes bounded sets into bounded sets.
This constraint is a natural one in the sense that, as a consequence of a Pohozaev identity, all critical points of
J
constrained to
S_{a}\times S_{b}
belong to
\mathcal{P}
. Thus, if we manage to show that

\inf_{\mathcal{P}\cap(S_{a}\times S_{b})}J[u,v]

is achieved by a critical point this will prove the existence of a ground state.

Instead of attacking directly this problem we consider a relaxed one.
For
a\geq 0
, we introduce the set

D_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq a\}

and for any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we define

\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}:=\mathcal{P}\cap(D_{a}\times D_{b}).

The relaxed problem is :

to find a critical point

\displaystyle[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{[0,0]\}\mbox{ of }J\mbox{ constrained to }D_{a}\times D_{b}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mbox{ at the level }C_{(a,b)}:=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}J[u,v].

Once this problem is settle, we show, see Lemma
8.2
, that
[u,v]\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
solving thus the initial problem.

This idea of first considering a relaxed problem is reminiscent of the works
[
7
,
15
]
. However the strategy of the proofs in
[
7
,
15
]
and in the present work are essentially distinct.

To solve Problem (
2
), we shall make use of a classical result due to Ghoussoub. This result
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
provides a sequence
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\mathcal{H}
for
J
restricted to
D_{a}\times D_{b}
at the level
C_{(a,b)}
which have useful additional properties. In particular it is
localized
around
\mathcal{P}
and this insure its boundedness.

To obtain the convergence of
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and thus the existence of a critical point at this level, a key point is to show that the level
C_{(a,b)}
is
sufficiently low
. More precisely, observe that

[z_{p,\mu_{1},a},0],[0,z_{q,\mu_{2},b}]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, and thus that it holds

C_{(a,b)}\leq\min\{m_{p,\mu_{1},a},m_{q,\mu_{2},b}\},

where we have set

m_{p,\mu_{1},a}:=J(z_{p,\mu_{1},a},0)\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{q,\mu_{2},b}:=J(0,z_{q,\mu_{2},b}).

The condition that we use to conclude to the convergence of
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\mathcal{H}
is

(2.8)

C_{(a,b)}<\min\{m_{p,\mu_{1},a},m_{q,\mu_{2},b}\}.

To prove (
2.8
) we study the behavior of
J
restricted to
\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
locally around
[z_{p,\mu_{1},a},0]
and
[0,z_{q,\mu_{2},b}]
in a way which seems new to us. In particular the inequality (
2.8
) is proved testing
J
on a point in
\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}\backslash\{S_{a}\times S_{b}\}
.
It is at this step, and only there, that appears the need to restrict our results to small values of
r_{1}>0
and
r_{2}>0
. Having proved (
2.8
) the convergence of
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\mathcal{H}
follows, leading to the existence of a ground state.

To obtain the additional properties of the ground states stated in Theorem
2.2
, and its corollaries, we use the fact that any minimum for

\inf_{\mathcal{P}\cap(S_{a}\times S_{b})}J[u,v]

is a critical point for
J
constrained to
S_{a}\times S_{b}
and that it is achieved by Schwartz symmetric functions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

we study the scalar problems which appear in the discussion of the convergence of our Palais-Smale sequence

.

3.
Some Preliminaries

We present in this section results which are slightly more general than needed.

4.
A Pohozaev type constraint and its properties

In this section we establish various properties of the constraint
\mathcal{P}
and we prove, in particular, it does not generate a Lagrange parameter. First observe that,

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the so-called Pohozaev manifold defined by (
2.6
).
If
[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{[0,0]\}
is a solution to

(4.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\mu_{1}|u|^{p-2}u+\beta r_{1}|u|^{r_{1}-2}u|v|^{r_{2}}\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\\
-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}|v|^{q-2}v+\beta r_{2}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}-2}v\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}\end{cases}

then
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

This result can be shown as in
[
4
, Lemma 4.6]
.
∎

Now, for any fixed
[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{[0,0]\}
, let us define
\Psi_{[u,v]}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\Psi_{[u,v]}(t):=J[t\star u,t\star v]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right]t^{2}-\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N}{2}}-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}}.

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J[t\star u,t\star v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right]t-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-2}{2}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-2}{2}}-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-2}{2}}.

We remark that
P[u,v]=\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)
and
P[t\star u,t\star v]=t\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{[0,0]\}
. Then:
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a critical point of
\Psi_{[u,v]}(t)
if and only if
[t\star u,t\star v]\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

By the fact
P[t\star u,t\star v]=t\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)
and
t\neq 0
, we see that

[t\star u,t\star v]\in\mathcal{P}\Leftrightarrow P[t\star u,t\star v]=0\Leftrightarrow\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)=0.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
.
Then for any
[0,0]\neq[u,v]\in D_{a}\times D_{b}
, there exists a unique
t=t_{[u,v]}>0
such that
[t\star u,t\star v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
. Furthermore,
t_{[u,v]}<(resp.\penalty\ =,>)1
if and only if
P[u,v]<(resp.\penalty\ =,>)0
.

Proof.

Noting that the fiber map (
3.1
) preserves the
L^{2}
-norm, we have that
[t\star u,t\star v]\in D_{a}\times D_{b}
for all
t>0
. Then by Lemma
4.2
,
[t\star u,t\star v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
if and only if
\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)=0
. Now by (
4
) and
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
, we obtain the uniqueness of
t=t_{[u,v]}
. Furthermore,
t_{[u,v]}
attains the maximum of
\Psi_{[u,v]}(t)
in
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)>0\;\hbox{for}\penalty\ 0<t<t_{[u,v]}\;\hbox{while}\penalty\ \Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)<0\;\hbox{for}\penalty\ t>t_{[u,v]}.

So combining with
P[u,v]=\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)
, we obtain that

P[u,v]<(resp.\penalty\ =,>)0\Leftrightarrow\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)<(resp.\penalty\ =,>)0\Leftrightarrow t_{[u,v]}<(resp.\penalty\ =,>)1.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
.
There exists some
C_{0}>0
depending only on
p,q,r_{1},r_{2},N
, such that

J[u,v]\geq C_{0}\Big[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\Big],\quad\mbox{for all }[u,v]\penalty\ \hbox{satisfying}\penalty\ P[u,v]=0.

Proof.

For
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
,
set

\tau:=\max\left\{\frac{2}{(p-2)N},\frac{2}{(q-2)N},\frac{2}{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}\right\},

then one can see that
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2}
.
By the definition of
\tau
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\left\{\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right\}

So for any
[u,v]
with
P[u,v]=0
, we have that

\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\leq\tau[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}].

Hence, we can take
C_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}-\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle J[u,v]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]-\left[\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]-\tau[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{0}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}].

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
. For any
a>0,b>0
,

J\big|_{\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}
is coercive, i.e.,

\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}}{{\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow+\infty}}}J[u,v]=+\infty.

Proof.

It follows by Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
.
For any given
a\geq 0,b\geq 0
with
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, there exists some
\delta_{(a,b)}>0
such that

\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}\Big[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\geq\delta_{(a,b)}.

Proof.

Recalling the well known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,

\|u\|_{p}\leq C_{N,p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}},\quad\mbox{for all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\penalty\ \hbox{and all }\penalty\ \penalty\ 2<p<2^{*},

for any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}
,
P[u,v]=0
implies that at least one of the following holds.

(i)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x
.

If
(i)
holds, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}C_{N,p}^{p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2N-(N-2)p}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}C_{N,p}^{p}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{2N-(N-2)p}{4}}.

By
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
, we have that
\frac{N(p-2)}{4}>1
and
\frac{2N-(N-2)p}{4}>0
. Hence,

(4.3)

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\geq\left(\frac{3(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}C_{N,p}^{p}\right)^{-\frac{4}{N(p-2)-4}}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{2N-(N-2)p}{N(p-2)-4}}.

Similarly, if
(ii)
holds, we have

(4.4)

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\geq\left(\frac{3(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}C_{N,q}^{q}\right)^{-\frac{4}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{2N-(N-2)q}{N(q-2)-4}}.

If
(iii)
holds, by Hölder inequality, a similar argument shows that

(4.5)

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\geq\left(\frac{3(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta C_{N,r_{1}+r_{2}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\right)^{-\frac{4}{N(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)-4}}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{2N-(N-2)(r_{1}+r_{2})}{N(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)-4}}.

For
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
, by (
4.3
)-(
4.5
), we can find some
\delta_{(a,b)}>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\geq\delta_{(a,b)},\quad\mbox{for all }[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}.

∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
. For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

C_{(a,b)}=\inf_{[0,0]\neq[u,v]\in D_{a}\times D_{b}}\max_{t>0}J[t\star u,t\star v]>0.

Proof.

For any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, by Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
, we have that

J[u,v]\geq C_{0}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]\geq C_{0}\delta_{(a,b)}>0.

Hence,
C_{(a,b)}
is well defined and
C_{(a,b)}\geq C_{0}\delta_{(a,b)}>0
. Furthermore, by Corollary
4.3
, one can see that

\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}J[u,v]=\inf_{[0,0]\neq[u,v]\in D_{a}\times D_{b}}\max_{t>0}J[t\star u,t\star v].

∎

We end this section by showing that any critical point of
J
restricted to
\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
is a critical point of
J
restricted to
D_{a}\times D_{b}
. This implies, in particular, that any minimum of
C_{(a,b)}
is a ground state to Problem (
1.4
).

Lemma 4.8
.

For any critical point of
J\big|_{\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}
, if
\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)\neq 0
, then there exists some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

J^{\prime}[u,v]+\lambda_{1}[u,0]+\lambda_{2}[0,v]=0.

Proof.

Firstly, it is easy to see that there exists some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.6)

J^{\prime}[u,v]+\lambda_{1}[u,0]+\lambda_{2}[0,v]+\mu P^{\prime}[u,v]=0\penalty\ \hbox{in $\mathcal{H}^{-1}$, the dual space of}\penalty\ \mathcal{H}.

Hence, we only need to prove that
\mu=0
. The functional associated to this
equation
is given by

\Phi[u,v]:=J[u,v]+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\mu P[u,v].

The fact that
[u,v]
solves (
4.6
) implies that
t=1
is a critical point of

\tilde{\Psi}_{[u,v]}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}_{[u,v]}(t):=\Phi[t\star u,t\star v]=

\displaystyle J[t\star u,t\star v]+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\mu P[t\star u,t\star v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{[u,v]}(t)+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\mu t\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t).

We have

\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\Phi[t\star u,t\star v]=(1+\mu)\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)+\mu\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(t).

Substituting

and
\Psi^{\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)=P[u,v]=0
, we obtain that

\mu\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)=0
. Then it follows
\mu=0
by
\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)\neq 0

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
. Then for any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}
, we have
\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)<0

Proof.

By a direct computation, we have that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(t)=

\displaystyle[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\frac{(p-2)N-2}{2}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\frac{(q-2)N-2}{2}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-2}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)=

\displaystyle[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\frac{(p-2)N-2}{2}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle-\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\frac{(q-2)N-2}{2}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-2}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, for any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{[u,v]}(1)=

\displaystyle-\left[(p-2)N-4\right]\frac{(p-2)N}{4p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\left[(q-2)N-4\right]\frac{(q-2)N}{4q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle-\left[(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4\right]\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{4}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

So by

5.
Rearrangement

For any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, let
u^{*},v^{*}
be the Schwartz symmetrization of
u
and
v
. We refer to
[
14
]
for a definition and the main properties of Schwartz symmetrization. We have the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
.
For any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, there exists a unique
t=t_{[u^{*},v^{*}]}\in(0,1]
such that
[t\star u^{*},t\star v^{*}]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
and we have
J[t\star u^{*},t\star v^{*}]\leq J[u,v].

Proof.

For any
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, we have
[u,v]\neq[0,0]
. So by
\|u^{*}\|_{2}^{2}=\|u\|_{2}^{2}
and
\|v^{*}\|_{2}^{2}=\|v\|_{2}^{2}
, we see that
[u^{*},v^{*}]\in(D_{a}\times D_{b})\backslash\{[0,0]\}
. Then by Corollary
4.3
, there exists a unique

t=t_{[u^{*},v^{*}]}>0
such that
[t\star u^{*},t\star v^{*}]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
.
In particular, by the properties of rearrangement, we have

\begin{cases}\|\nabla u^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\|\nabla v^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2},\\
\|u^{*}\|_{p}^{p}=\|u\|_{p}^{p},\|v^{*}\|_{q}^{q}=\|v\|_{q}^{q},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u^{*}|^{r_{1}}|v^{*}|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.\end{cases}

This implies that
P[u^{*},v^{*}]\leq P[u,v]=0
. Hence, by Corollary
4.3
, we obtain
t_{[u^{*},v^{*}]}\leq 1
.
We remark that for any
[u,v]\neq[0,0]
, we have that
J[u^{*},v^{*}]\leq J[u,v]
. Combined with the fact that

s\star u^{*}=(s\star u)^{*},\quad\mbox{for all }s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\max_{s>0}J[s\star u^{*},s\star v^{*}]=J[t\star u^{*},t\star v^{*}]=

\displaystyle J[(t\star u)^{*},(t\star v)^{*}]\leq J[t\star u,t\star v]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{s>0}J[s\star u,s\star v]=J[u,v].

∎

Remark 5.2
.

Lemma
5.1
implies, in particular, that if
C_{(a,b)}
is achieved then it is achieved by a couple
[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}
of Schwartz symmetric functions.

6.
Existence of a special Palais-Smale sequence at the level
C_{(a,b)}

In this section we prove the existence of a particular Palais-Smale sequence for
J
restricted to
D_{a}\times D_{b}
at the level
C_{(a,b)}
.

Let us define the class of paths

\Gamma_{(a,b)}=\{g\in C([0,1],D_{a}\times D_{b}):g(0)\in L_{(a,b)}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,P(g(1))<0\}

where

L_{(a,b)}:=\{[u,v]\in\mathcal{H}:||\nabla u||_{2}^{2}+||\nabla u||_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta_{(a,b)}\}.

Note that
\Gamma_{(a,b)}\neq\emptyset.
Indeed
L_{(a,b)}\neq\emptyset
and from Corollary
4.3
we known that the set where
P<0
is non void.

Next we define

\gamma_{(a,b)}=\inf_{g\in\Gamma_{(a,b)}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(g(t)).

We claim that

\gamma_{(a,b)}=C_{(a,b)}.

Indeed, combining Corollary
4.3
and Lemma
4.6
we see that
P>0
on
L_{(a,b)}
and thus, by continuity, for any
g\in\Gamma_{(a,b)}
there exists a
t\in[0,1]
such that
P(g(t))=0
. It implies that
\gamma_{(a,b)}\geq C_{(a,b)}
. The reverse inequality
\gamma_{(a,b)}\leq C_{(a,b)}
follows from Corollary
4.3
.

As a consequence of the equality
\gamma_{(a,b)}=C_{(a,b)}
we deduce that

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}}J(u)\geq\gamma_{(a,b)}.

At this point we observe that we are in the setting of
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
. This theorem says to us that, for any sequence of paths
\{g_{n}\}\subset\Gamma_{(a,b)}
such that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

(5.1)

\max_{[0,1]}J(g_{n}(t))\rightarrow C_{(a,b)},

there exists a sequence
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

(i)

J[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow C_{(a,b)}
,

(ii)

J^{\prime}[u_{n},v_{n}]\Big|_{D_{a}\times D_{b}}\rightarrow 0
,

(iii)

dist([u_{n},v_{n}],\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)})\rightarrow 0
,

(iv)

dist([u_{n},v_{n}],g_{n}[0,1])\rightarrow 0.

Let
[u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*}]\subset\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
be a minimizing sequence for
C_{(a,b)}
. We know by Lemma
5.1
that such a sequence exists. Now let the sequence of paths
\{g_{n}\}\subset\Gamma_{(a,b)}
be given by :

g_{n}(t)=[(s_{1}^{n}+ts_{2}^{n})*u_{n}^{*},(s_{1}^{n}+ts_{2}^{n})*v_{n}^{*}]

where
s_{1}^{n}>0
is sufficiently small and
s_{2}^{n}>0
sufficiently large in order to insure that
g_{n}\in\Gamma_{(a,b)}
. We know from Corollary
4.7
that the sequence
\{g_{n}\}
satisfy (
5.1
).

From (i)-(ii) we deduce that
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
J
restricted to
D_{a}\times D_{b}
at the level
C_{(a,b)}
. The property (iii), combined with the fact that
J
is coercive on
\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, see
Corollary
4.5
, and that
J^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
take bounded sets into bounded set implies that
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}
is bounded and that
P[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow 0
. Now, by compactness, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
s_{n}\in[s_{1}^{n},s_{1}^{n}+s_{2}^{n}]
such that

(5.2)

dist\Big([u_{n},v_{n}],g_{n}([0,1])\Big)=\Big|\Big|[u_{n},v_{n}]-[s_{n}*u_{n}^{*},s_{n}*v_{n}^{*}]\Big|\Big|.

Up to a subsequence we can assume that
[s_{n}*u_{n}^{*},s_{n}*v_{n}^{*}]\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
\mathcal{H}
and

[s_{n}*u_{n}^{*},s_{n}*v_{n}^{*}]\rightarrow u

strongly in
L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
2<\eta<2^{*}
. For
N\geq 2
this strong convergence is a consequence of the fact that the functions are radial. When
N=1
it follows from the fact that the functions are nonincreasing with respect to the origin, see
[
8
, Proposition 1.7.1]
.

Now, in view of (
5.2
), these convergence properties hold for the Palais-Smale sequence
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}.

Summarizing we have obtained a bounded sequence
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset D_{a}\times D_{b}
such that

(i)

J[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow C_{(a,b)}
,

(ii)

J^{\prime}[u_{n},v_{n}]+\lambda_{1,n}[u_{n},0]+\lambda_{2,n}[0,v_{n}]\to 0,\quad\mbox{in }\mathcal{H}^{-1}
for some real sequences
\{\lambda_{1,n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,n}\}
,

(iii)

P[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow 0
.

(iv)

[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightharpoonup[u,v]
weakly in
\mathcal{H}
and
[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow[u,v]
strongly in
L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
2<\eta<2^{*}
. In addition
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0
.

7.
Estimation of
C_{(a,b)}

In view of the definitions of
z_{p,\mu,a}
by (
2.4
) and
\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}
by (
2.5
), a direct calculation shows that

\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{p,\mu,a}^{\frac{p-2-r}{p-2}}\mu^{\frac{r}{p-2}}\inf_{h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p}^{r}h^{2}\mathrm{d}x}.

Substituting the value of
\lambda_{p,\mu,a}
(see (
2.3
)), we obtain that

\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{rN-4}{N(p-2)-4}}\,\|U_{p}\|_{2}^{\frac{4(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}a^{-\frac{2(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}\inf_{h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p}^{r}h^{2}\mathrm{d}x}.

Lemma 7.1
.

For
N=1,2
,
p\in(2,+\infty),\mu>0,a>0,r>0
, it holds that

\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=0
.

Proof.

For the case of
N=1
, let
h(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
h(x)\equiv 1
for
|x|\leq 1
,
h(x)\equiv 0
for
|x|\geq 2
and
0\leq h(x)\leq 1
. Define

\displaystyle h_{n}(x):=h\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)
.
Then
\|\nabla h_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
While

\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{p}^{r}(x)h_{n}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{|x|\leq n}U_{p}^{r}(x)\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{p}^{r}(x)\mathrm{d}x>0.

So

\inf_{0\neq h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\frac{\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{p}^{r}(x)h(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}x}=0

and thus
\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=0
for
N=1
.

For
N=2
, we remark that the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
fails, so there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}=1
, but
\|\phi_{n}\|_{\infty}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In particular, by the property of rearrangement, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}^{*}
. So we have that

\phi_{n}(x)=\phi_{n}(|x|)\penalty\ \hbox{and}\penalty\ \phi_{n}(0)=\|\phi_{n}\|_{\infty}.

Now, we set

h_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi_{n}(0)\quad&\hbox{if}\penalty\ |x|\leq n,\\
\phi_{n}(|x|-n)\quad&\hbox{if}\penalty\ |x|\geq n.\end{cases}

Then
\|\nabla h_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=1
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}U_{p}^{r}(x)h_{n}^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{|x|\leq n}\phi_{n}^{2}(0)U_{p}^{r}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\|\phi_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{|x|\leq n}U_{p}^{r}(x)\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\infty\penalty\ \hbox{as}\penalty\ n\rightarrow\infty.

So we also have

\inf_{0\neq h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\frac{\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}U_{p}^{r}(x)h^{2}(x)}\mathrm{d}x=0,

and thus
\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=0
also holds for
N=2
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

For
N\geq 3
, it holds that
\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}>0
. In particular, for
r>\frac{4}{N}
and fixed
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*},a>0
, we have

(6.1)

\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=+\infty.

Furthermore, for
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*},\mu>0,r>1
fixed, we have that

(i)

if
p<r+2<2^{*}
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow 0}\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=+\infty
.

(ii)

if
r+2=p
, then there exists
\tilde{C}_{p,\mu}<\tilde{D}_{p,\mu}
independent of
a
, such that

\tilde{C}_{p,\mu}\leq\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}\leq\tilde{D}_{p,\mu},\forall a>0.

(iii)

if
2+r<p
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow 0}\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=+\infty
and
\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}=0
.

Proof.

Noting that
U_{p}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and decays exponentially to

as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
,
by Hölder inequality, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U_{p}^{r}|h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\|U_{p}\|_{\frac{2^{*}r}{2^{*}-2}}^{r}\|h\|_{2^{*}}^{2}.

So by the critical Sobolev inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{rN-4}{N(p-2)-4}}\,\|U_{p}\|_{2}^{\frac{4(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}a^{-\frac{2(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}\frac{\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}}{\|U_{p}\|_{\frac{2^{*}r}{2^{*}-2}}^{r}\|h\|_{2^{*}}^{2}}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{rN-4}{N(p-2)-4}}\,\|U_{p}\|_{2}^{\frac{4(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}a^{-\frac{2(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}\|U_{p}\|_{\frac{2^{*}r}{2^{*}-2}}^{-r}S_{N},

where

is the sharp constant in the critical Sobolev inequality.
So it is easy to see that (
6.1
) holds.

On the other hand, by taking

, we obtain that

(6.3)

\beta_{p,\mu,a,N,r}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{rN-4}{N(p-2)-4}}\|U_{p}\|_{2}^{\frac{4(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}a^{-\frac{2(p-2-r)}{N(p-2)-4}}\frac{\|\nabla U_{p}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|U_{p}\|_{r+2}^{r+2}}.

Then by (
7
) and (
6.3
), it is easy to see that the conclusions (i)-(iii) above hold.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

(i)

if

, then

. If

, then

.

(ii)

if

, then
C_{(a,b)}<m_{p,\mu_{1},a}
. If
r_{2}=2
, then
C_{(a,b)}<m_{p,\mu_{1},a}
provided
\beta>\beta_{p,\mu_{1},a,N,r_{1}}
.

Proof.

We only prove (ii), the proof of (i) being directly similar. We write
z:=z_{p,\mu_{1},a}
for simplicity. For any
h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|h\|_{2}^{2}=1
, we have that
[z,sh]\in D_{a}\times D_{b}
provided
|s|<\sqrt{b}
.
For any
s

t=t(s)>0
such that

[t\star z,t\star sh]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
, where
t=t(s)
is determined by

\displaystyle\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}+s^{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|z\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|h\|_{q}^{q}\,s^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)s^{r_{2}}\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{2}}.

From the Implicit Function Theorem we know that
t(s)\in C^{1}
locally around
s=0
.
Taking the derivative with respect to
s
at both sides and we obtain that

\displaystyle t^{\prime}(s)=\frac{P_{h}(s)}{Q_{h}(s)},

where

\displaystyle P_{h}(s):=

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}s-\frac{(q-2)N}{2}\mu_{2}\,\|h\|_{q}^{q}\,|s|^{q-2}s\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\,r_{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)|s|^{r_{2}-2}s\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{2}}

and

\displaystyle Q_{h}(s):=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\,\|z\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-6}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(q-2)N-4}{2}\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\,\|h\|_{q}^{q}\,s^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-6}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{2}\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)s^{r_{2}}\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-6}{2}}.

Case of

:
In such a case, for
|s|
small,

and

Q_{h}(s)=\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}\,\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|z\|_{p}^{p}(1+o(1)).

So

t^{\prime}(s)=-M_{h}r_{2}|s|^{r_{2}-2}s(1+o(1)),

where

Then

t(s)=1-M_{h}|s|^{r_{2}}(1+o(1))

and for any
\tau>0
,

t(s)^{\tau}=1-\tau M_{h}|s|^{r_{2}}(1+o(1)).

Noting that for any
[\phi,\psi]\in\mathcal{P}
, it holds that

\displaystyle J[\phi,\psi]=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N-4}{4}\,\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|\phi\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N-4}{4}\,\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|\psi\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{4}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\phi|^{r_{1}}|\psi|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

Then we have

\displaystyle J[t(s)\star z,t(s)\star(sh)]-J[z,0]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}\,(t^{2}(s)-1)-\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\,\|z\|_{p}^{p}\,(t(s)^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}-1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}\,s^{2}\,t^{2}(s)-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\,\,\|h\|_{q}^{q}\,s^{q}\,t(s)^{\frac{(q-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)|s|^{r_{2}}\,t(s)^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}\,M_{h}\,|s|^{r_{2}}o(1)-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)|s|^{r_{2}}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)|s|^{r_{2}}(1+o(1)).

Hence, if
1<r_{2}<2
, by taking
s
close to
0
, we obtain that

C_{(a,b)}\leq J[t(s)\star z,t(s)\star(sh)]<J[z,0]=m_{p,\mu_{1},a}.

Case of

: In such a case, we have, for

small,

P_{h}(s)=\left[2\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}-N\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]s(1+o(1))

\bar{M}_{h}:=\frac{2\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}-N\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x}{\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}\|\nabla z\|_{2}^{2}}.

\displaystyle J[t(s)\star z,t(s)\star(sh)]-J[z,0]=

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}\,s^{2}\,t(s)^{2}-\frac{1}{q}\mu_{2}\,\|h\|_{q}^{q}\,s^{q}\,t(s)^{\frac{(q-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)|s|^{2}t(s)^{\frac{r_{1}N}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla h\|_{2}^{2}-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{r_{1}}|h|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]s^{2}(1+o(1)).

Then for any

. Hence,

∎

8.
Convergence of our Palais-Smale sequence and Proof of Theorem
2.2

Theorem
2.2
will be a direct consequence of the following result.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
1\leq N\leq 4
,
2+\frac{4}{N}<p,q,r_{1}+r_{2}<2^{*}
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta,a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let

\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset D_{a}\times D_{b}
be a bounded sequence such that

(i)

J[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow C_{(a,b)}
,

(ii)

J^{\prime}[u_{n},v_{n}]+\lambda_{1,n}[u_{n},0]+\lambda_{2,n}[0,v_{n}]\to 0,\quad\mbox{in }\mathcal{H}^{-1}
for some real sequences
\{\lambda_{1,n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,n}\}
,

(iii)

P[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow 0
,

(iv)

[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightharpoonup[u,v]
weakly in
\mathcal{H}
and
[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow[u,v]
strongly in
L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
2<\eta<2^{*}
. In addition
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0
.

Assume that

(7.1)

C_{(a,b)}<\min\{m_{p,\mu_{1},a},m_{q,\mu_{2},b}\}.

Then, up to a subsequence,
[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow[u,v]
in
\mathcal{H}
and
[u,v]\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
[u_{n},v_{n}]\neq[0,0]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
since
J[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow C_{a,b}>0=J[0,0]
, see Corollary
4.7
.
First note that (ii) can be rewrite as : there exists sequences
\{\lambda_{1,n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,n}\}
such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}+\lambda_{1,n}u_{n}-\mu_{1}u_{n}^{p-1}-\beta r_{1}u_{n}^{r_{1}-1}v_{n}^{r_{2}}=o(1),\\
-\Delta v_{n}+\lambda_{2,n}v_{n}-\mu_{2}v_{n}^{q-1}-\beta r_{2}u_{n}^{r_{1}}v_{n}^{r_{2}-1}=o(1).\end{cases}

From the boundedness of
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
\{\lambda_{1,n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,n}\}
are also bounded and we can assume that
\lambda_{1,n}\rightarrow\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2,n}\rightarrow\lambda_{2}
for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
.

We claim that

(7.2)

u\neq 0,v\neq 0.

If not, without loss of generality, we may assume that
u=0
. Then we have that
v\neq 0
. If not, from
P[u_{n},v_{n}]\to 0
we get that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{r_{1}}|v_{n}|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x+o(1),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1),

where we have used that

\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}=o(1),\|v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}=o(1)\mbox{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}^{r_{1}}v_{n}^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x=o(1),

by the Hölder inequality and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. This contradicts Lemma
4.6
.
We deduce that

0<\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus one can see that
v
is a non-trivial (non-negative) solution to

(7.3)

-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}v^{q-1}\;\hbox{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Recalling the Pohozaev identity

\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q},

we deduce, from (
7.3
), that

\lambda_{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{2q-(q-2)q}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}>0.

Hence,
\lambda_{2}>0
. So by

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{2,n}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\|v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}+\beta r_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}^{r_{1}}v_{n}^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+o(1),

we have that
v_{n}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Now using that
P[u_{n},v_{n}]\to 0,
and
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
2<\eta<2^{*}
, it is easy to prove that

u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
So

J[0,v]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J[u_{n},v_{n}]=C_{(a,b)}.

Put
\delta:=\|v\|_{2}^{2}
, then
\delta\in(0,b]
.
By Lemma
3.1
, one has that

v=z_{q,\mu_{2},\delta}
and
C_{(a,b)}=J[0,v]=m_{q,\mu_{2},\delta}
.
However, by Lemma
3.2
-(i), and (
7.1
) we have

m_{q,\mu_{2},\delta}\geq m_{q,\mu_{2},b}>C_{(a,b)},

a contradiction. This ends the proof of (
7.2
) and thus

0<\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Now, from the convergence properties (iv), we deduce that

[u,v]
is a non-trivial, solution to (
4.1
). In particular it holds that
P[u,v]=0
and by

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\leq o(1)+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1)+\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{r_{1}}|v_{n}|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1)+\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1)+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2},

we obtain that
u_{n}\rightarrow u,v_{n}\rightarrow v
in
D_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence,
\displaystyle J[u,v]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J[u_{n},v_{n}]=C_{(a,b)}.

Recalling (
4.1
) and
1\leq N\leq 4
, we conclude, using
[
12
, Lemma A.2]
, that
\lambda_{1}>0,\lambda_{2}>0
.
Then by

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1,n}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}^{r_{1}}v_{n}^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{r_{1}}v^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+o(1),

we obtain that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Similarly, we can prove that
v_{n}\rightarrow v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
To obtain that
[u,v]\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
and completed the proof we shall use Lemma
8.2
below.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
[u,v],\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be given by Theorem
8.1
. Since
\lambda_{1}>0
we have that
u\in S_{a}
. Similarly,
\lambda_{2}>0
imply that
v\in S_{b}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda_{1}>0
, we shall prove that
u\in S_{a}
. If not,
\delta:=\|u\|_{2}^{2}\in(0,a)
. Then for
s>0
small enough, we still have that
[(1+s)u,v]\in(D_{a}\times D_{b})\backslash\{[0,0]\}
. For any given
s
, by Corollary
4.3
, there exists a unique
t=t(s)>0
such that

[t\star(1+s)u,t\star v]\in\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
.
Precisely,
t=t(s)
is determined by

\displaystyle(1+s)^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-4}{2}}(1+s)^{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-4}{2}}(1+s)^{r_{1}}.

From the Implicit Function Theorem we have that
t(s)\in C^{1}
. Then, since

\displaystyle J[t\star(1+s)u,t\star v]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}(1+s)^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}]t^{2}-\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}(1+s)^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}\,t^{\frac{(q-2)N}{2}}-\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}}(1+s)^{r_{1}},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}J[t\star(1+s)u,t\star v]=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}(1+s)t^{2}-\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}(1+s)^{p-1}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle-\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x(1+s)^{r_{1}-1}\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}(1+s)^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}\right)t\,t^{\prime}-\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}(1+s)^{p}\,t^{\frac{(p-2)N-2}{2}}\,t^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{(q-2)N}{2q}\mu_{2}\|v\|_{q}^{q}t^{\frac{(q-2)N-2}{2}}\,t^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N}{2}\beta\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)(1+s)^{r_{1}}\,t^{\frac{(r_{1}+r_{2}-2)N-2}{2}}\,t^{\prime},

here
t=t(s),t^{\prime}=t^{\prime}(s)
.
Putting
s=0
and noting that
t(0)=1
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}J[t(s)\star(1+s)u,t(s)\star v]\Big|_{s=0}=

\displaystyle\big[\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu_{1}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\beta r_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r_{1}}|v|^{r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\big]+P[u,v]t^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

Then

C_{(a,b)}\leq J[t(s)\star(1+s)u,t(s)\star v]<J[u,v]=C_{(a,b)},\hbox{ for $s>0$ small enough},

a contradiction.
Similarly, using that
\lambda_{2}>0
, we can prove that
v\in S_{b}
.
∎

At this point we can give the

Proof of Theorem
2.2
:

Under the assumptions, by Lemma
7.3
, we have that

C_{(a,b)}<\min\{m_{p,\mu_{1},a},m_{q,\mu_{2},b}\}.

Let
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a Palais-Smale sequence as obtained in Section
6
. By Theorem
8.1
,
[u_{n},v_{n}]\rightarrow[u,v]\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
and, in particular,
[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}_{a,b}
and
J[u,v]=C_{(a,b)}
. Invoking Remark
5.2
we deduce that
J
restricted to
\mathcal{P}_{(a,b)}
admits a minimum, say
[\overline{u},\overline{v}]
which consists in
Schwarz symmetric functions. The fact that this minimum is a critical point of
J
constrained to
D_{a}\times D_{b}
follows from Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
. As in the proof of Theorem
8.1
one deduces that the associated Lagrange multipliers
\overline{\lambda}_{1}
and
\overline{\lambda}_{2}
are both strictly positive. At this point we have proved that

(\overline{\lambda}_{1},\overline{\lambda}_{2},\overline{u},\overline{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}
is a ground state to Problem (
1.4
) having the required symmetry properties.

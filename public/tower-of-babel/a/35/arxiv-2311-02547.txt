Weighted and Restricted Sum Formulas of Euler Sums
Jianqiang Zhao

Abstract




Abstract.

One of the most interesting formulas for multiple zeta values is the sum formula proved by Granville and Zagier independently in 1990s.
Many variations and generalizations of it have been found since then. In this paper, we will provide a uniform approach to proving these results using the regularized double shuffle relations of Euler sums. We summarize these formulas including many new ones with three tables in the Appendix at the end of the paper.

Keywords
: Multiple zeta values, Euler sums, multiple
T
-values, multiple
t
-values, multiple
S
-values, weighted sum formulas.

Introduction

1.1
Multiple zeta values

The multiple zeta values have attracted immense interest during the past 30 years due to their
important roles in the studies of the motives, knot theory and many other branches of mathematics as well
as in the computation of Feynman integrals in physics (see, for example,
[
4
,
2
,
3
,
23
]
or the website on MZVs maintained by M. Hoffman).

For any
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define the multiple zeta values (MZVs) by

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

It is easy to see that these multiple series converge if and only if
s_{1}\geq 2
, in which case
we say
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}
is
admissible
. We call
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|:=s_{1}+\dotsm+s_{d}
the
weight
and
{\rm{dp}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=d
the
depth
.

In a few correspondences with Goldbach starting from 1742, Euler studied the depth two MZVs,
also called double zeta values. He later published some
of his results in
[
7
]
. Among these, the celebrated decomposition formula states that
for all positive integers
2\leq s\leq w-2
,

\sum_{k=2}^{w-1}\left\{{k-1\choose s-1}+{k-1\choose w-s-1}\right\}\zeta(k,w-k)=\zeta(s)\zeta(w-s).

This identity can be regarded as a weighted sum formula for double zeta values.
For more general MZVs, the well-known sum formula has the form

\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,{\rm{dp}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d,{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\text{ is admissible}}\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\zeta(w)\qquad\forall w>d>0.

(1)

This was proved independently by A. Granville
[
9
]
and D. Zagier
using generating functions. We refer the reader to section 5.11 of the author’s book
[
23
]

for a detailed account of the history of the sum formula and its various generalizations.

The most important properties of MZVs is that they possess two different product structures, which can be derived from
their series representation and their iterated integral representations, respectively. These lead to the
the double shuffle relations, which can be generalized to Euler sums (see next subsection).

In this paper, we will establish many weighted and restricted sum formulas for the Euler sums.
Here, “restricted” means that in the formulas some of the components will have prescribed properties/values.
For example, Eie et al.
[
6
]
showed that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, weight
w=u+\ell
and
depth
d

\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=u,\ {\rm{dp}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=d}\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}},1_{\ell})=\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{\sl{t}}}|=u+\ell,\ {\rm{dp}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{t}}})=\ell+1,\ t_{1}>u-d}\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{t}}}),

(2)

where
t_{1}
is the first component of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{t}}}
and
1_{\ell}
means that
1
repeats
\ell
times. The left-hand side is a
restricted sum and the right-hand side has much less terms if
u
is large and
\ell
is small.

1.2
Euler sums

Euler sums are the alternating version of MZVs. For any
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{d})\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
, we define

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}}):=\zeta\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\bfs}{\bfmu}:=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{\mu_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\mu_{d}^{k_{d}}}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

This series converges if and only if
(s_{1},\mu_{1})\neq(1,1)
, in which case
we say
({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
is
admissible
. The weight
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|
and the depth are defined similarly as with MZVs.
Conventionally, as a shorthand notation we put a bar on top of
s_{j}
if
\mu_{j}=-1
. For example,

\zeta(2,1;-1,1):=\zeta(\bar{2},1)=\sum_{k>l>0}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k^{2}l}.

For convenience, we now consider a sort of double cover of the set
\mathbb{N}
of positive integers.
Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be the set of
signed numbers

{\mathbb{D}}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\overline{\mathbb{N}},\quad\text{where}\quad\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\{\bar{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(3)

Define the absolute value function
|\cdot|
on
{\mathbb{D}}
by

|k|=|\bar{k}|=k
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and the sign function by

\sgn(k)=1
and
\sgn(\bar{k})=-1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
On
{\mathbb{D}}
we define a commutative and associative
binary operation
\oplus
(called
O-plus
)
as follows: for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{D}}

a\oplus b=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{|a|+|b|},&\hbox{if $\sgn(a)\neq\sgn(b)$;}\\
|a|+|b|,&\hbox{if $\sgn(a)=\sgn(b)$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

(4)

Then the stuffle relation for Euler sums can be expressed more compactly. For example, for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{1\}

\zeta(a)\zeta(b)=\zeta(a,b)+\zeta(b,a)+\zeta(a\oplus b).

Let
M
be a large positive integer and
{\varepsilon}>0
be a very small number. For any composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

and
\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{d}=\pm 1
, we consider two variations of the Euler sums. First, set

\zeta^{(M)}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}}):=\zeta^{(M)}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\bfs}{\bfmu}:=\sum_{M\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\frac{\mu_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots\mu_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

(5)

and

I^{({\varepsilon})}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}}):=I^{({\varepsilon})}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\bfs}{\bfmu}:=\int_{0}^{1-{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{a_{1}-t}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{d}-1}\frac{dt}{a_{d}-t}

(6)

where
a_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\mu_{j}^{-1}
. Then
\zeta^{(M)}
satisfies the stuffle product

\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1};\mu_{1})\zeta^{(M)}(s_{2};\mu_{2})=\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{2};\mu_{1},\mu_{2})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{2},s_{1};\mu_{2},\mu_{1})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1}+s_{2};\mu_{1}\mu_{2}).

(7)

On the other hand, by shuffle product of the iterated integrals we see that

I^{({\varepsilon})}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{s_1}{\mu_1}I^{({\varepsilon})}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{s_2}{\mu_2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\geq 1\\
t_{1}+t_{2}=s_{1}+s_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1}I^{({\varepsilon})}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{t_1,\ \ t_2\ \ }{\mu_1, \mu_1\mu_2}+\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{2}-1}I^{({\varepsilon})}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{t_1,\ \ t_2\ \ }{\mu_2, \mu_1\mu_2}\Bigg].

(8)

It is well-known that for admissible
({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
we have

\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})=\lim_{M\to\infty}\zeta^{(M)}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})=\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}}I^{({\varepsilon})}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}}).

Therefore, one can derive the so-called double shuffle relation using the two different product structures, namely, the stuffle and shuffle prodcts. Then by the usual regularization process (cf.
[
13
]
) one can discover the extremely useful regularized double shuffle relation. Briefly speaking, for every
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{d}
(admissible or non-admissible) there are two polynomials of
T
, denoted by
\zeta_{*}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
(
*
-regularzed) and
\zeta_{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
(
\shuffle
-regularzed), such that

(
DBSF1)

\zeta_{*}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\zeta_{*}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}^{\prime})
can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of
*
-regularized Euler sums of weight
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|+|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}^{\prime}|
using the stuffle product.

(
DBSF2)

\zeta_{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\zeta_{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}^{\prime})
can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of
\shuffle
-regularized Euler sums of weight
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|+|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}^{\prime}|
using the shuffle product.

(
DBSF3)

There is an explicitly defined
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\rho

\rho(e^{Tu})=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\rho(T^{j})}{j!}u^{j}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n}\zeta(n)}{n}u^{n}\right)e^{Tu},

(9)

such that
\rho\circ\zeta_{*}=\zeta_{\shuffle}
. In particular, for all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{d}
,

\zeta_{*}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=\zeta_{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})\qquad\text{ if }(s_{1},s_{2})\neq(1,1).

(10)

We will not go into the details of this theory, instead, we would like to refer the interested reader to
[
23
, §13.3.1]
.

1.3
Multiple mixed values

Recently, a few variations of MZVs are suggested and their properties have been investigated. Hoffman

[
12
]
defined an odd variant of MZVs as follows. For admissible
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, the multiple

t
-values (MtVs) are defined by

t({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{(1-(-1)^{k_{1}})\cdots(1-(-1)^{k_{d}})}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{2^{d}}{(2k_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}\cdots(2k_{d}+1)^{s_{d}}}.

This is slightly different from Hoffman’s version because of the 2-powers. It is obvious that MtVs satisfy the so-called stuffle relations. On the other hand, the
multiple
T
-values
(MTVs) are
defined by Kaneko and Tsumura
[
15
]
as follows:

T({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0\\
k_{j}\equiv d-j+1\text{\ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{d}}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{(1+(-1)^{d+k_{1}})(1-(-1)^{d+k_{2}})\cdots(1-(-1)^{k_{d}})}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}k_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

They proved that the MTVs can be expressed using iterated integrals and therefore satisfy the shuffle relations
and the duality relations similar to the MTVs.

In a series of papers
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
Xu and the current author studied the
multiple
S
-values
(MSVs)
defined by

S({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0\\
k_{j}\equiv d-j\text{\ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{d}}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{(1-(-1)^{d+k_{1}})(1+(-1)^{d+k_{2}})\cdots(1+(-1)^{k_{d}})}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}k_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}

and more generally, the
multiple mixed values
(MMVs)

M\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\bfs}{\bfeps}:=\sum_{k_{1}>\cdots>k_{d}>0}\frac{(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}(-1)^{k_{1}})\cdots(1+{\varepsilon}_{d}(-1)^{k_{d}})}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

To save space, we put a check on top of
s_{j}
(resp. nothing) if
{\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
(resp.
{\varepsilon}_{j}=1
)
and say that the signature of
s_{j}
is odd (resp. even). For examples,

T(3,2,1)=M(\breve{3},2,\breve{1}),\qquad t(3,2,1)=M(\breve{3},\breve{2},\breve{1}),\qquad S(3,2,1)=M(3,\breve{2},1).

It is clear that MMVs can be expressed as
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations of Euler sums. Observe that

M\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\bfs}{\bfeps}:=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\sgn(1+\mu_{j}+{\varepsilon}_{j})\Big)\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})

where
\sgn(a)=1
if
a\geq 0
and
\sgn(a)=-1
if
a<0
. Hence the alternating sign
\mu_{j}
of
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
contributes to the sign in front of
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}})
if and only if the signature of
s_{j}
is odd. For example,

\displaystyle M(3,{\breve{2}},{\breve{1}})

\displaystyle\,=\zeta(3,2,1)+\zeta(\bar{3},2,1)-\zeta(3,\bar{2},1)-\zeta(\bar{3},\bar{2},1)

\displaystyle\,-\zeta(3,2,\bar{1})-\zeta(\bar{3},2,\bar{1})+\zeta(3,\bar{2},\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{3},\bar{2},\bar{1})

In general, there are
2^{d}
terms when expressing a MMV of depth
d
in terms of Euler sums.
In order to express the weighted and restricted sum formulas of Euler sums succinctly,
we need to define some incomplete MMVs by selecting some of the terms from the full expansions.
We need some additional notation.
For any index subset
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}\subsetneq[d]:=\{1,2,\dotsc,d\}

we denote
\bar{I}=[d]\setminus I
the complement of
I
in
[d]
. For
j\in\bar{I}
we only allow
{\varepsilon}_{j}=\pm 1
. But for
j\in I
,

{\varepsilon}_{j}
can be either one of the following three forms:

{\varepsilon}_{j}=\pm 1,\quad\text{or}\quad{\varepsilon}_{j}=\prod_{i\in J_{j}}e_{i},\quad\text{or}\quad{\varepsilon}_{j}=\overline{\prod_{i\in J_{j}}e_{i}}

for some
J_{j}\subseteq\bar{I}
, where
e_{j}
’s are formal symbols.
We then define the
incomplete MMVs
by

M_{I}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}})=M_{I}\binom{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}{{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}}:=\sum_{\mu_{j}=\pm 1\ \forall j\in\bar{I}}\Big(\prod_{j\in\bar{I}}\sgn(1+\mu_{j}+{\varepsilon}_{j})\Big)\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{\mu}}),

where for all
j\in I
,

\mu_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ \ \ {\varepsilon}_{j},&\hbox{if ${\varepsilon}_{j}=1$ or ${\varepsilon}_{j}=-1$ ;}\\
\ \prod_{i\in J_{j}}\mu_{i},&\hbox{if ${\varepsilon}_{j}=\prod_{i\in J_{j}}e_{i}$ for some $J_{j}\subseteq\bar{I}$;}\\
-\prod_{i\in J_{j}}\mu_{i},&\hbox{if ${\varepsilon}_{j}=\overline{\prod_{i\in J_{j}}e_{i}}$ for some $J_{j}\subseteq\bar{I}$.}\end{array}\right.

(11)

It is clear there are
2^{{\rm{dp}}(\bar{I})}
terms of Euler sums in the expansion of
M_{I}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}})
, for each of which the sign pattern of
every component corresponding to
j\in I
is prescribed by
{\varepsilon}_{j}
. Further, we may use the shorthand notation as follows:
for
j\in I
, we put a bar on top of
s_{j}
if
{\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
, or write
s_{j}{\varepsilon}_{j}
otherwise;
for
j\in\bar{I}
, we put a check on top of
s_{j}
if
{\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
, or just write
s_{j}
if
{\varepsilon}_{j}=1
.
For example, when
d=4
,
I=\{1,3\}
and
\bar{I}=\{2,4\}
, we have

M_{13}(\bar{a},b,c\overline{e_{2}},\breve{d})=M_{13}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{0}{\ a\ ,\, b\, ,\, c\, ,\ d\ }{-1,\,1\,,\ol{e_2},-1}=\zeta({\bar{a}},b,{\bar{c}},d)+\zeta({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}},c,d)-\zeta({\bar{a}},b,{\bar{c}},{\bar{d}})-\zeta({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}},c,{\bar{d}}).

(12)

Here, all the four possible combinations of alternating signs for components
b
and
d
can appear.
The sign
-1
for
a
means only
{\bar{a}}
can appear in the first component while
\overline{e_{2}}
means that the
alternating sign for
c
in each of the Euler sums on the right-hand side of (
12
)
is always opposite to that of the second component
b
. Further, the signs
+1
for
b
and
-1
for
d
imply that the sign in front of each Euler sum is determined only by whether there is a bar on top of the fourth component
d
or not, since
\sgn(1+\mu_{j}+{\varepsilon}_{j})=-1
only if
\mu_{j}={\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the following special case when

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
and

Proof.

Set
{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}})=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,\mu_{d}^{\prime})
and
{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}})=(\nu_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,\nu_{e}^{\prime})
, namely,
\mu_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mu_{i}
and

\nu_{k}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\nu_{i}
for all

and

. First we observe that

{\bf q}({\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}}),{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}))={\bf q}(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,\mu_{d}^{\prime},\nu_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,\nu_{e}^{\prime})=(\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{d},\mu_{d}^{\prime}\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dotsc,\nu_{e}).

If

then we see that

{\boldsymbol{\xi}}={\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{0}:=({\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}}),{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}))

-sign does not change if we swap any two adjacent
\mu_{j}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{k}^{\prime}
since every
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}})\shuffle{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}})

Claim.

\ell=d+e

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\,({\lambda}_{1},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{i},\mu_{j}^{\prime},\nu_{k}^{\prime},{\lambda}_{i+3},\dots,{\lambda}_{\ell})\in{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}})\shuffle{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{2}=

\displaystyle\,({\lambda}_{1},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{i},\nu_{k}^{\prime},\mu_{j}^{\prime},{\lambda}_{i+3},\dots,{\lambda}_{\ell})\in{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\mu}})\shuffle{\bf p}({\boldsymbol{\nu}}).

(14)

(i)

. Then we have

\displaystyle{\bf q}({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{1})=

\displaystyle\,{\bf q}({\lambda}_{1},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{\ell-2},\mu_{d}^{\prime},\nu_{e}^{\prime})=({\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{1}{\lambda}_{2},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{\ell-3}{\lambda}_{\ell-2},{\lambda}_{\ell-2}\mu_{d}^{\prime},\mu_{d}^{\prime}\nu_{e}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\,({\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{1}{\lambda}_{2},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{i-1}{\lambda}_{i},{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{j}^{\prime},\mu_{j}^{\prime}\nu_{k}^{\prime},\nu_{k}^{\prime}{\lambda}_{i+3},{\lambda}_{i+3}{\lambda}_{i+4},\dotsc,{\lambda}_{\ell-1}{\lambda}_{\ell}),

We end this section by remarking that sometimes we may obtain weighted sum formulas for Euler sums
but not MTVs or other variants because we cannot remove the non-admissible terms.
For example, in
[
1
, Thm. 4.10]
we find the sum formula for

\sum{}2^{b}\zeta_{*}(a,b,c),\quad\sum{}2^{b}\zeta({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}},c),\quad\sum{}2^{b}\zeta({\bar{a}},b,c),\quad\sum{}2^{b}\zeta_{*}(a,{\bar{b}},c),

but not the other four alternating sign types since they are pairwise intertwined, namely, the sum
\zeta({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}},{\bar{c}})+\zeta({\bar{a}},b,{\bar{c}})

cannot be split, neither can
\zeta(a,{\bar{b}},{\bar{c}})+\zeta(a,b,{\bar{c}})
. The non-admissible terms are provided by
restricted weighted sums such as
\sum_{b+c=w}2^{b}\zeta_{*}(1,b,c)
for which we cannot find a closed formula.

6
Weighted and restricted sum formulas: general depth

In
[
10
]
Guo and Xie obtained the general weighted sum formula for multiple zeta values:
for positive integers
d,s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}2^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{j}-j}+2^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}-d}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-1}2^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{j}-j}+2^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}-d+1}.

Then

\sum\limits_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}=w\atop s_{i}\geq 1,s_{1}\geq 2}[\mathcal{C}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1})-\mathcal{C}(s_{2},\ldots,s_{d-1})]\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})=w\zeta(w).

(32)

7
Concluding remarks

We remark that it is possible to generalize the various restricted/weighted sum formulas to colored MZVs, i.e., special values of multiple polylogarithms at
N
-th roots of unity. We plan to work on this project in the near future.

In
[
14
]
Kaneko and Tasaka considered the double zeta values and double Eisenstein series at level 2. Yuan and and the author
[
22
]
generalized these to level
N
by considering the subseries of a MZV series where the summation indices run through any fixed congruence class modulo
N
. Similarly, the MMVs are clearly the multiple variable version at level 2 and therefore can be extended to level
N
. In
[
22
]
we defined their two ways of regularization and the corresponding double shuffle relations so that it is conceivable that many results in this paper may be extended to arbitrary levels using the results on colored MZVs.

We now propose a few questions with some partial answers.

Question 1.

For all
d\geq 5
and
k\leq d-2
, are there restricted sum formulas of Euler sums
of depth
d
when
k
components are restricted to 1’s?

Partial Answers.
Yes if
k=1,2
or if all
k
components are consecutive; but unknown in other cases for general
d\geq 5
.
For examples, we know the formulas for the following restricted sums exist:

\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,{\bar{b}},1,c,d),\qquad\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,b,1,{\bar{c}},1),\qquad\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,{\bar{b}},1_{\ell},{\bar{c}}),

where
\sum^{\prime}=\sum_{a+b+c+d=w-1,a\geq 2}
; but not for

\sum_{a+b=w,a\geq 2}\zeta(a,1,1,b,1).

Question 2.

For
r\neq 0,1,2
, are there formulas for the weighted sums of the following form

\sum\limits_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\ |{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|=w,\ s_{1}\geq 2}r^{s_{1}}\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})?

Partial Answers.
Yes in two cases: (i)
d=r=2
or (ii)
d=2
,
r=-1
, and
w
is even; but unknown for all other cases.

Question 3.

Is there a restricted sum formula for

f(w):=\sum\limits_{a+b=w,\ a,b\text{ even}}\zeta(a,{\bar{b}})?

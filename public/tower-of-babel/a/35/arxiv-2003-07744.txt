Homotopy properties of the space O ​ S f ​ ( X ) OS_{f}(X)
Kh. Kh. Kurbanov, A. Ya. Ishmetov

Abstract
For a given compact Hausdorff space X X , we construct the space O ​ S f ​ ( X ) OS_{f}(X) of normed, order-preserving, weakly additive, positively homogeneous and semi-additive functionals (for brevity, semi-additive functionals) and it is proved that the hyperspace exp ⁡ X \exp\,X of the space X X is a deformation retract of the constructed space. Further we show that the shapes of the spaces O ​ S f ​ ( X ) OS_{f}(X) and exp ⁡ X \exp\,X coincide. We establish that if the space exp ⁡ X \exp\,X is contractible, then the space O ​ S f ​ ( X ) OS_{f}(X) is also contractible. 2010 MSC: 54C65, 52A30. Key words and phrases: semi-additive functional; contractible space; shape.

\operatorname{supp}\,\mu\subset A\cap B
. From here
\mu\in OS_{f}(A\cap B)
, i. e.
OS_{f}(A\cap B)\supset OS_{f}(A)\cap OS_{f}(B)
.

\scriptstyle\blacksquare

Thus, the following main result of the section is proved.

Theorem 2.1

OS_{f}\colon\,\mathfrak{Comp}\to\mathfrak{Comp}
is a normal functor.

Theorem 3.1

For an arbitrary Hausdorff compact space

\exp\,X
is a strongly deformation retract of the Hausdorff compact space

Proof.

Consider a map

defined by the formula

\displaystyle h(\mu,\,t)=h_{t}(\mu)=(1-t)\cdot\mu+t\cdot r_{e}^{O}(\mu),\qquad(\mu,\,t)\in OS_{f}(X)\times[0,\,1].

It is easy to verify that the map

, i. e.,

Further, we have

for all

Theorem 3.2

If for a Hausdorff compact space

Proof.

We show more: the functor
OS_{f}
preserves the homotopy of maps. Let
h_{0}
,
h_{1}\colon X\to Y
be homotopical maps,
h\colon X\times[0,\,1]\to Y
be the homotopy connecting the maps
h_{0},\ \ h_{1}
, i. e.
h(x,\,0)=h_{0}(x)
,
h(x,\,1)=h_{1}(x)
. The embedding
i_{t_{0}}\colon X\times\{t_{0}\}\to X\times I
defined by the equality
i_{t_{0}}(x,\,t_{0})=(x,\,t_{0})
,
x\in X

OS_{f}(i_{t_{0}})\colon OS_{f}(X\times\{t_{0}\})\to OS_{f}(X\times I)

OS_{f}(X\times\{t_{0}\})
is naturally homeomorphic to
OS_{f}(X)\times\{t_{0}\}
. This homeomorphism can be realized, as it is easy to see, using equality (
1
{}^{\,\prime}
) and the correspondence
\mu_{t_{0}}\leftrightarrow(\mu,\,t_{0})
, where for each
\{\xi_{s}:\,s\in S\}\subset P_{f}(X)
:

\displaystyle\mu(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}(\varphi):\,s\in S\right\},\qquad\varphi\in C(X),

\displaystyle\mu_{t_{0}}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{(x_{s\,i},\,t_{0})}(\phi):\,s\in S\right\},\qquad\phi\in C(X\times\{t_{0}\}).

We now define a map
OS_{f}(h)\colon OS_{f}(X)\times[0,\,1]\to OS_{f}(Y)
by the equality

OS_{f}(h)\left(\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}:\,s\in S\right\},\,t\right)=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{h(x_{s\,i},\,t)}:\,s\in S\right\}.

We have

\displaystyle OS_{f}(h)\left(\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}:\,s\in S\right\},\,0\right)=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{h(x_{s\,i},\,0)}:\,s\in S\right\}=

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{h_{0}(x_{s\,i})}:\,s\in S\right\}=OS_{f}(h_{0})\left(\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}:\,s\in S\right\}\right),

\displaystyle OS_{f}(h)\left(\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}:\,s\in S\right\},\,1\right)=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{h(x_{s\,i},\,1)}:\,s\in S\right\}=

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{h_{1}(x_{s\,i})}:\,s\in S\right\}=OS_{f}(h_{1})\left(\sup\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n_{s}}{\Sigma}}\alpha_{s\,i}\delta_{x_{s\,i}}:\,s\in S\right\}\right),

i. e.
OS_{f}(h)(\mu,\,0)=OS_{f}(h_{0})(\mu)
and

for each

is the homotopy connecting

and

maps. Thus, the functor

\scriptstyle\blacksquare

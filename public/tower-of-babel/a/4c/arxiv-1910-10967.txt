Joint User Selection and Precoding in Multiuser MIMO Systems via Group LASSO
Saba Asaad 1 , Ali Bereyhi 1 , Ralf R. Müller 1 , and Rafael F. Schaefer 2

Abstract
Joint user selection and precoding in multiuser MIMO settings can be interpreted as group sparse recovery in linear models. In this problem, a signal with group sparsity is to be reconstructed from an underdetermined system of equations. This paper utilizes this equivalent interpretation and develops a computationally tractable algorithm based on the method of group LASSO. Compared to the state of the art, the proposed scheme shows performance enhancements in two different respects: higher achievable sum-rate and lower interference at the non-selected user terminals.



I
Introduction

Performance gains are often achieved in multiuser massive
mimo
(
mimo
) systems with a large number of transmit antennas per user
[
1
]
. As a result, in dense settings in which the number of available users is comparable to the number of transmit antennas, user selection is required along with downlink beamforming
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

The conventional approach for user selection and precoding is to divide them into two separate problems: First, a subset of users is selected; then, the information signals of the selected users are precoded via a classic precoding scheme
[
5
]
. Generally, the optimal approach for user selection deals with integer programming. Hence, this problem is often addressed via sub-optimal greedy algorithms
[
2
,
3
]
. In this work, we deviate from the conventional approach and propose a scheme for joint user selection and downlink beamforming.

I-A

User Selection and Precoding as Group Sparsity

Joint user selection and beamforming is interpreted as the problem of constructing a signal with
group sparsity
. To clarify this point, assume a multiuser downlink scenario with
M
transmit antennas and
K
users in which we wish to select a subset of
L
users. A linear precoder in this problem can be seen as a signal with
MK
entries, such that each block of size
M
represents an individual beamforming vector. By such a formulation, joint user selection and downlink beamforming with respect to some performance metric, e.g., the achievable sum-rate or
mse
(
mse
), reduces to the problem of finding a signal with group sparsity: A signal of size
MK
in which only
L
blocks of size
M
have non-zero entries.

Following this equivalent interpretation, we employ the
glse
(
glse
) framework for precoding, recently developed in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, to formulate joint user selection and precoding as the problem of group sparse recovery in a linear model. A computationally tractable algorithm is then developed based on group
lasso
(
lasso
) to address this problem. Our investigations show significant performance enhancements compared to the state of the art.

II
Problem Formulation

Consider a multiuser
mimo
system with multiple
bs
which are equipped with transmit antenna arrays of size
M
. The system is intended to serve
K
single-antenna
ut
. For mathematical tractability, we focus on a single
bs
which aims to transmit information to a group of
L\leq K

ut
.

II-A

System Model

The system operates in the
tdd
(
tdd
) mode. Hence, the uplink and downlink channels are reciprocal. In each coherence time interval, the
ut
transmit known training sequences. The
bs
then utilizes these sequences to estimate the
csi
(
csi
).

Let
{\mathbf{h}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
denote the vector of uplink channel coefficients between
ut

k
and the
bs
. The signal received by
ut

k
is hence given by

\displaystyle y_{k}={\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}+z_{k}

(1)

where
z_{k}
is additive complex Gaussian noise with zero mean and variance
\sigma_{k}^{2}
, i.e.,
z_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}\left(0,\sigma_{k}^{2}\right)
, and
{\boldsymbol{x}}
is the downlink transmit signal constructed from the information symbols of the selected
ut
and the
csi
via linear precoding. As a result, the transmit signal is written as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbbmss{S}}\sqrt{p_{\ell}}s_{\ell}\mathbf{w}_{\ell}.

(2)

where
\mathbbmss{S}
,
s_{\ell}
,
p_{\ell}
and
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}
are defined as follows:

1.

\mathbbmss{S}\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,K\right\}
represents the subset of
L

ut
selected by the
bs
for downlink transmission.

2.

s_{\ell}
is the information symbol of user
\ell
which is assumed to be zero-mean and unit-variance.

3.

p_{\ell}
denotes the power allocated to
ut

\ell\in\mathbbmss{S}
.

4.

\mathbf{w}_{\ell}
is the beamforming vector of
ut

\ell
.

The transmit power at the
bs
is restricted. It is hence assumed that
{\boldsymbol{x}}
satisfies the power constraint
\mathbbmss{E}\left\{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\}\leq P
for some non-negative real
P
.

II-B

Performance Measure

There are various metrics characterizing the performance of the downlink transmission in this system. One well-known metric is the
weighted average throughput
which is defined as

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbbmss{S}}w_{\ell}R_{\ell}

(3)

for some non-negative weights
\left\{w_{\ell}\right\}
and transmission rates

\displaystyle R_{\ell}=\log\left(1+{\mathrm{SINR}}_{\ell}\right).

(4)

In (
4
),
{\mathrm{SINR}}_{\ell}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathrm{SINR}}_{\ell}=\dfrac{\displaystyle p_{\ell}\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{\ell}\rvert^{2}}{\displaystyle\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+\sum_{j=1,j\neq\ell}^{K}p_{j}\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j}\rvert^{2}}.

(5)

From signal processing points of view, precoding can be interpreted as
channel inversion
. In this problem, the ultimate aim is to construct the transmit signal such that at a selected
ut

\ell
,
{\mathbf{h}}_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\beta s_{\ell}
, for some scaling factor
\beta
, and at
ut

k
which has not been selected, we have
{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}=0
. The former guarantees channel inversion at the selected
ut
which results in minimal post-processing load, and the latter restricts the precoder to have zero leakage at the non-selected
ut
.

By this alternative viewpoint, a suitable performance measure is the

rss
(
rss
)
at the
ut
defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{RSS}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\beta a_{k}s_{k}\rvert^{2}\right\},

(6)

where
a_{k}=1
if
ut

k
is selected and is zero otherwise.

III
Optimal User Selection and Precoding

Let
{\boldsymbol{s}}=\left[s_{1},\ldots,s_{K}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
collect the information symbols of all
ut
. By defining
p_{k}=0
for those
ut
which are not selected, the transmit signal is compactly represented as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}=\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}{\boldsymbol{s}}.

(7)

where
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{P}
are defined as follows:

1.

\mathbf{W}=\left[\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{K}\right]
is the beamforming matrix.

2.

\mathbf{P}=\mathrm{diag}\left({\mathbf{p}}\right)
with
{\mathbf{p}}=\left[p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
.

The notation
\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}
moreover denotes a matrix whose entries are the square root of the entries of
\mathbf{P}
. Similarly, the vector of receive signals
{\boldsymbol{y}}=\left[y_{1},\ldots,y_{K}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
reads

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{y}}=\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{z}}

(8)

where
\mathbf{H}=\left[{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{K}\right]
and
{\boldsymbol{z}}=\left[z_{1},\ldots,z_{K}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
.

III-A

User Selection and Precoding with Minimum RSS

We design the transmit signal by considering the
rss
as the performance measure. In this respect, the optimal approach for joint user selection and precoding is to find
\mathbf{W}
and
{\mathbf{p}}
such that the
rss
is minimized and the signal constraints are satisfied. In the sequel, we formulate this approach in a standard form.

Objective Function

Following the given representation, the
rss
is written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{RSS}=\frac{1}{K}\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lVert\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}{\boldsymbol{s}}-\beta\mathbf{A}{\boldsymbol{s}}\rVert^{2}\right\},

(9)

where
\mathbf{A}=\mathrm{diag}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{K}\right)
. In this formulation,
\mathbf{A}
is ineffective and can be dropped. To show this, note that for any non-selected
ut

k
,
{\boldsymbol{x}}
is independent of
s_{k}
and hence

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}-\beta s_{k}\rvert^{2}\right\}\hskip-1.99168pt

\displaystyle=\hskip-1.99168pt\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\rvert^{2}\right\}\hskip-1.99168pt+\hskip-1.99168pt\beta^{2}\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lvert s_{k}\rvert^{2}\right\}

(10a)

\displaystyle=\hskip-1.99168pt\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lvert{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\rvert^{2}\right\}\hskip-1.99168pt+\hskip-1.99168pt\beta^{2}.

(10b)

Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{RSS}=\frac{1}{K}D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)-\left(1-\frac{L}{K}\right)\beta^{2},

(11)

where
D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)
is defined as

\displaystyle D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\lVert\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}{\boldsymbol{s}}-\beta{\boldsymbol{s}}\rVert^{2}\right\}

(12a)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{Q}\right\}{}

(12b)

with
\mathbf{Q}={\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}-\beta\mathbf{I}_{K}}
. We hence set the objective function to
D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)
.

Constraints

There are two main constraints:

1.

The number of selected
ut
should be less than
L
.

2.

The average transmit power is constrained.

Noting that the number of selected
ut
in the system is given by the
sparsity
of
{\mathbf{p}}
, i.e.,
\lVert{\mathbf{p}}\rVert_{0}
, the first constraint is written as

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{p}}\rVert_{0}\leq L.

(13)

For the second constraint, we note that

\displaystyle\mathbbmss{E}\left\{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbbmss{E}\left\{{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\mathsf{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}{\boldsymbol{s}}\right\}

(14a)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\dagger}}{{=}}\mathbbmss{E}\left\{\mathrm{tr}\left\{\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}{\boldsymbol{s}}{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\}\right\}

(14b)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{W}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\}

(14c)

where
\dagger
follows the fact that
\mathbbmss{E}\left\{{{\boldsymbol{s}}{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\mathsf{H}}}\right\}=\mathbf{I}_{K}
. As a result, the transmit power constraint reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{W}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\}\leq P.

(15)

Optimization Problem

Considering the objective function and constraints, the jointly optimal approach for user selection and precoding is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K},{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}}

\displaystyle D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{1}:}\ \lVert{\mathbf{p}}\rVert_{0}\leq L,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{2}:}\ \mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{W}\mathrm{diag}\left({\mathbf{p}}\right)\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{H}}\right\}\leq P.

The optimization problem in its initial form is not tractable, since both the objective function and constraints are not convex. We address this issue by converting (
16
) into a
group selection
problem. We then develop an algorithm based on
group
lasso
to estimate the solution.

IV
Precoding via Group LASSO

The optimization problem in (
16
) can be converted into a group selection problem. To show this, let
\mathbf{V}\coloneqq\mathbf{W}\sqrt{\mathbf{P}}
be the
overall precoding matrix
. The objective function is rewritten in terms of
\mathbf{V}
as

\displaystyle D\left(\mathbf{W},{\mathbf{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\left(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}-\beta\mathbf{I}_{K}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}-\beta\mathbf{I}_{K}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}-\left.\beta\right.\mathbf{I}_{K}\rVert_{F}^{2}.

(17)

The power constraint is further given in terms of
\mathbf{V}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{V}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{V}\right\}=\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq P.

(18)

To represent constraint
\rm C_{1}
in terms of
\mathbf{V}
, we note that only the column vectors in
\mathbf{V}
whose corresponding
ut
is selected have non-zero entries. This equivalently means that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{k}\rVert\neq 0&\text{if \acs{ut} $k$ is selected}\\
\lVert\mathbf{v}_{k}\rVert=0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

(19)

where
\mathbf{v}_{k}=\sqrt{p_{k}}\mathbf{w}_{k}
denotes the
k
-th column vector of
\mathbf{V}
. As the result, one can write

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{2,0}=\lVert{\mathbf{p}}\rVert_{0},

(20)

where
\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{p,q}
denotes the
\ell_{p,q}
norm of
\mathbf{V}
defined as

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{p,q}\coloneqq\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\lVert\mathbf{v}_{k}\rVert_{p}\right)^{q}\right]^{1/q}.

(21)

From the above derivations, we conclude that the optimal approach for joint user selection and precoding reduces to the following programming:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}}

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}-\left.\beta\right.\mathbf{I}_{K}\rVert_{F}^{2}

(22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{1}:}\ \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{2,0}\leq L,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{2}:}\ \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq P.

The optimization in (
22
) describes a group selection problem in which a matrix with
group sparsity
is to be recovered, i.e., a matrix with a certain fraction of column or row vectors being zero. Such a problem raises in several applications, e.g., distributed compressive sensing and machine leaning
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Group selection in its primitive form is a
np
(
np
)-hard problem, since it reduces to an integer programming. To address this problem tractably, several suboptimal approaches have been developed in the literature which approximate the solution. Group
lasso
is one of the most efficient approaches which relaxes the problem of group selection into a convex programming
[
12
,
13
]
. In the sequel, we use group
lasso
to develop a computationally tractable algorithm for joint user selection and precoding.

IV-A

A Tractable Algorithm via Group LASSO

Group selection is an extension of the basic
sparse recovery
problem in which a sparse vector is to be recovered from an underdetermined system of equations
[
14
,
15
]
. Group
lasso
extends Tibshirani’s regularization approach
[
16
]
and convexifies the non-convex
\ell_{0}
-norm
with the
\ell_{1}
-norm
. This means that constraint
\rm C_{1}
is relaxed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{1}:}\ \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{2,1}\leq\eta L

(23)

for some
\eta
which regularizes the relaxation. By doing so, the joint user selection and precoding reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}}

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{V}-\left.\beta\right.\mathbf{I}_{K}\rVert_{F}^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{subject\ to}

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{1}:}\ \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{2,1}\leq\eta L,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C_{2}:}\ \lVert\mathbf{V}\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq P.

This relaxed program represents a group
lasso
algorithm which is convex and is posed as a generic linear programming.

VI
Conclusions

A joint user selection and precoding scheme has been proposed for multiuser
mimo
systems based on group
lasso
. The scheme depicts performance enhancement in two different aspects:

1.

The throughput of the system, defined as the sum-rate divided by the number of active users, shows some gains.

2.

The interference imposed by downlink transmission at the non-selected
ut
is significantly reduced. For instance, when
L=8

ut
are selected out of
K=16
users, there is almost zero interference, when the
bs
is equipped with
M=64
antennas.

These observations indicate that the proposed scheme is a good candidate for massive
mimo
settings.

The current work can be pursued in various directions. For example, considering the
rls
-based derivation in (
25
), an iterative algorithm can be developed via
amp
implementing the proposed scheme with low computational complexity. Another direction is to extend the current framework to wiretap settings following the approach in
[
21
]
. The work in these directions is currently ongoing.

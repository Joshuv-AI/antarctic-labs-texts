Pinched-up periodic KPZ fixed point
Jinho Baik, Zhipeng Liu

Abstract
The periodic KPZ fixed point is the conjectural universal limit of the KPZ universality class models on a ring when both the period and time critically tend to infinity. For the case of the periodic narrow wedge initial condition, we consider the conditional distribution when the periodic KPZ fixed point is unusually large at a particular position and time. We prove a conditional limit theorem up to the “pinch-up” time. When the period is large enough, the result is the same as that for the KPZ fixed point on the line obtained by Liu and Wang in 2022. We identify the regimes in which the result changes and find probabilistic descriptions of the limits.

1.5
Structure of the paper

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
–
1.4

[
2
]

The method is similar to that of
[

]
for the KPZ fixed point, but since the formulas for the periodic KPZ fixed point are more complicated, the analysis is more involved.
The other difficulty is to find probabilistic descriptions of the limits of the formulas, especially for Theorem

The explicit formula of the multi-time, multi-position distributions of the periodic KPZ fixed point involves an integral of a Fredholm determinant.
In Section

We then state four propositions, Propositions
2.8
–

Lemma 3.2

\begin{split}\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathsf{G}_{1}(x)\right)\leq-5a\ell^{3/2}\delta_{x}+|c-6ad|d.\end{split}

from (

4.2

The function

For every

|\mathrm{Li}_{a}(z)|=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{a}}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|z|^{n}\leq 2|z|\qquad\text{for $|z|\leq 1/2$.}

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\mathbf{z}
given in (

such that

|C^{\bullet}(\mathbf{z})-1|\leq c\ell p^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\ell p}{2}}e^{c\ell p^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\ell p}{2}}}

uniformly in
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]^{m}
eventually for all three Cases.

Proof.

From (
74
),
|z_{i}|\leq e^{-\frac{\ell p}{2}}
.
If
\ell p\geq 2
, then
|z_{i}|\leq e^{-1}\leq 1/2
.
From the formula (
40
) of
C^{\bullet}(\mathbf{z})
, the bounds (
76
) and (
77
), and the choice of the parameters (
43
), we find, using the inequality
|e^{w}-1|\leq|w|e^{|w|}
for all complex number
w

c>0
so that

|C^{\bullet}(\mathbf{z})-1|\leq c(\ell p^{-1/2}+p^{-3/2})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}|\big)e^{c(\ell p^{-1/2}+p^{-3/2})\sum_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}|}.

Since
\ell p\geq 2
, we see
\ell p^{-1/2}+p^{-3/2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\ell p^{-1/2}

Using

Note that

In all Cases,

. Hence, the term

.
For Case 1 and 2, The term

\ell p^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\ell p}{2}}\to 0

For Case 3, we have

and
\ell p\geq 4\log\ell

Thus,
\ell p^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{\ell p}{2}}\leq\frac{\ell^{5/4}}{(\log\ell)^{4}}e^{-2\log\ell}\to 0

Hence,

∎

e^{\frac{4}{3}\ell^{3/2}}\left|E_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathsf{U}(\mathbf{k}),\mathsf{U}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}))\right|\leq e^{-\frac{4\delta}{3}\ell^{3/2}-c_{2}|\mathbf{n}|\ell^{3/2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}e^{-c_{*}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|k^{(i)}_{j}|}-c_{*}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|\hat{k}^{(i)}_{j}|}}

(100)

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
,
\mathbf{k},\hat{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{n}}
,
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]^{m}
, and
\ell\geq c_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta t_{i}
are positive constants (recall that
t_{1},\cdots,t_{m-1}
are distinct) that add up to
1
, we find that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\Delta t_{i}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(n_{i}-1)\Delta t_{i}\geq 1+\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\Delta t_{i}\}\qquad\text{for every $\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}$.}

Let
c
and
\delta
be any positive constants satisfying
1+\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\Delta t_{i}\}=\frac{1+\delta}{1-c}
. Then,

(1-c)\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\Delta t_{i}\geq 1+\delta\qquad\text{for all $\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}$.}

(101)

This inequality implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\Delta t_{i}\geq 1+\delta+c\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\Delta t_{i}\geq 1+\delta+c|\mathbf{n}|\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\Delta t_{i}\}

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
.
Thus,

\begin{split}&\log\left(c_{1}^{|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{i=1}^{m}e^{-\frac{4n_{i}\Delta t_{i}}{3}\ell^{3/2}-2n_{i}\Delta h_{i}\ell^{3/4}}\right)\\
&\leq-\frac{4(1+\delta)}{3}\ell^{3/2}-|\mathbf{n}|\ell^{3/2}\left(\frac{4c}{3}\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\Delta t_{i}\}-2\ell^{-3/4}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|\Delta h_{i}|-\ell^{-3/2}\log|c_{1}|\right).\end{split}

The last parenthesis term is larger than or equal to a positive constant
c_{2}
if
\ell
is large enough.
Thus, we obtain the result from Lemma
4.4
after adjusting the constant
c_{0}
.
∎

4.6

From (

and

Recall from (

\mathsf{K}(X;Y)=\det\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}+y_{j}}\right)_{i,j=1}^{a}=\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq a}(x_{j}-x_{i})(y_{j}-y_{i})}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{a}(x_{i}+y_{j})}

From the definition (

To estimate

Lemma 4.7
.

For every
i,i^{\prime}=1,\cdots,m
and
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)+\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})|\geq\sqrt{\ell p}.

Proof.

From the definition (
78
),
\mathrm{Re}(\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)^{2})>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(\mathsf{u}_{i}(k))<0
. Thus,
\arg(-\mathsf{u}_{i}(k))\in(-\pi/4,\pi/4)

Using polar forms
-\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)=ce^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}

and
-\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})=c^{\prime}e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi^{\prime}}
for some
c,c^{\prime}>0
and
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in(-\pi/4,\pi/4)
, we find that

|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)+\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})|=|c+c^{\prime}e^{\mathrm{i}(\varphi^{\prime}-\varphi)}|\geq|c+c^{\prime}\cos(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})|\geq c=|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|\geq\sqrt{\ell p}

for all

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

We have

|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|\leq 5\sqrt{\ell p}+5\sqrt{|k|}

(106)

for all
i=1,\dots,m
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\ell,p>0
satisfying
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}
and
\ell p\geq 1
.

Proof.

If
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}

2\ratio\rho_{i}\leq 2\ratio\rho_{1}\leq\ell p
(recall (

|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|^{4}=(\ell p+2\ratio\rho_{i})^{2}+(4\pi k-2\theta_{i})^{2}\leq 4(\ell p)^{2}+(4\pi|k|+2\pi)^{2}\leq 4(\ell p)^{2}+32\pi^{2}|k|^{2}+8\pi^{2}.

for all
\theta_{i}\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Hence, for
\ell p\geq 1

|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|\leq\left((4+8\pi^{2})(\ell p)^{2}+32\pi^{2}|k|^{2}\right)^{1/4}\leq(4+8\pi^{2})^{1/4}\sqrt{\ell p}+(32\pi^{2})^{1/4}\sqrt{|k|}.

Since
(4+8\pi^{2})^{1/4}\approx 3.01
and
(32\pi^{2})^{1/4}\approx 4.21
, we obtain the result.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

(a)

For every
r>0
,
a\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r^{a}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|k|^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{k}{r}}}\leq r\int_{\frac{1}{r}}^{\infty}y^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}}}\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(107)

(b)

Recall that
\ratio=\ell^{1/4}p
.
For every
\epsilon>0
and
a\geq 0
,
there is a positive constant
C=C(a,\epsilon)
such that

\begin{split}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{|k|}{\ratio}}}\leq C(\ell p)^{\frac{a}{2}+1}\end{split}

for all
i=1,\dots,m
and
\ell,p>0
satisfying
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}
and
\ell p\geq 1
.

Proof.

(a) For
k\geq 1
, we have
k\geq\frac{k+1}{2}\geq\frac{x}{2}
for all
x\in[k,k+1]
.
Thus,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r^{a}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{k}{r}}}\leq\frac{1}{r^{a}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{k}^{k+1}x^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{x}{2r}}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{r^{a}}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{x}{2r}}}\mathrm{d}x=r\int_{\frac{1}{r}}^{\infty}y^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}}}\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(b)
The result (a) implies that

\frac{1}{\ratio{}^{a}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|k|^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{|k|}{\ratio}}}\leq\delta_{a=0}+2\ratio B_{a}\quad\text{where $B_{a}=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{y}{2}}}\mathrm{d}y$.}

Hence, if
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}
and
\ell p\geq 1
, then (
106
) implies that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathsf{u}_{i}(k)|^{a}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{|k|}{\ratio}}}\leq 5^{a}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(2^{a}(\ell p)^{a/2}+2^{a}|k|^{a/2})e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{|k|}{\ratio}}}\leq 10^{a}\left((\ell p)^{a/2}(1+2\ratio B_{0})+2\ratio^{a/2+1}B_{a/2}\right).

Since
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}
implies that
\ratio\leq\frac{\ell p}{2\rho_{1}}
,
the above is bounded by a constant times

if
\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}
and
\ell p\geq 1
.
We thus obtain the result.
∎

Lemma 4.10

For every
\epsilon>0

\frac{\left|\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{u}_{i}(\mathbf{k}),-\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime});\mathsf{u}_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}),-\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}))\right|}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}|u_{i}(\hat{k}_{j})|\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}|u_{i^{\prime}}(k^{\prime}_{j})|}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|k_{j}|}-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|\hat{k}_{j}|}}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|k_{j}^{\prime}|}-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|\hat{k}_{j}^{\prime}|}}\leq\left(\frac{C_{0}(\ell p)^{2}}{\ratio{}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+n^{\prime}}{2}}

for all two distinct integers

from

,

For vectors

\left|\mathsf{K}(X;Y)\prod_{p=1}^{a}f_{p}\right|=\left|\det\left(\frac{f_{p}}{x_{q}+y_{p}}\right)_{q,p=1}^{a}\right|\leq\prod_{q=1}^{a}\sqrt{\sum_{p=1}^{a}\frac{f_{p}^{2}}{(x_{q}+y_{p})^{2}}}.

\begin{split}&\frac{\left|\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{u}_{i}(\mathbf{k}),-\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime});\mathsf{u}_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}),-\mathsf{u}_{i^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}))\right|}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}|u_{i}(\hat{k}_{j})|\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}|u_{i^{\prime}}(k_{j}^{\prime})|}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|\hat{k}_{j}|}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}e^{-\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\ratio}|k_{j}^{\prime}|}}\\

Consider the first sum.
From (

|u_{i}(k)|\geq\sqrt{\ell p}

Since we assume that the components of

\ell^{3}\geq 16\rho_{1}^{4}

Lemma 5.2
.

Recall that
\{x\}
denotes the equivalence class of the real number
x
in the quotient space
I=\mathbb{R}/\ratio\mathbb{Z}
.
Recall the distance
\mathrm{dist}(\{x\},\{y\})=\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-y-\ratio k|
between equivalence classes is defined in (

For every two real-valued random variables

and

,

\bigcup_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{X-Y\geq-\ratio k,\,\,X+Y\in[\ratio k,\ratio(k+1))\right\}=\left\{X\geq\mathrm{dist}(\{Y\},\{0\})\right\}.

(174)

Proof.

Since both sides of the equation (
174
) are unchanged if we replace

with
Y\pm\ratio
, we may assume, without loss of generality,

-\ratio/2\leq Y<\ratio/2
. Under this assumption,

.

Suppose that there exists a

such that

and

. If

, then
X\geq Y
and
X\geq-Y
. Thus

. If

Therefore, we find that the left hand side of (

Now we suppose that

There is an integer

.
Note that

Hence, the proof is complete.

Thus,

\begin{split}|\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j)})-\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j-1)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j-1)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j-1)})|&=\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta^{\prime}_{j};\gamma^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{j})+\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta_{j};\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})-2\mathbf{F}_{2}(\beta^{\prime}_{j},\beta_{j};\gamma^{\prime}_{j},\gamma_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{j},\tau_{j})\\
&\leq\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta^{\prime}_{j};\gamma^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{j})+\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta_{j};\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})-2\mathbf{F}_{2}(\beta^{\prime}_{j},\beta_{j}-\epsilon;\gamma^{\prime}_{j},\gamma_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{j},\tau_{j})\end{split}

for every
\epsilon>0
.
If
(\gamma^{\prime}_{j},\tau^{\prime}_{j},\beta^{\prime}_{j})
is close enough to
(\gamma_{j},\tau_{j},\beta_{j})
, then
(\gamma_{j}^{\prime},\gamma_{j},\tau_{j}^{\prime},\tau_{j},\beta_{j}^{\prime},\beta_{j}-\epsilon)\in U^{2}_{+}
.
Thus, the continuity of
\mathbf{F}_{2}
on
U^{2}_{+}
implies that

\begin{split}&\limsup_{(\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\to(\gamma,\tau,\beta)}|\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j)})-\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j-1)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j-1)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j-1)})|\\
&\quad\leq 2\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta_{j};\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})-2\mathbf{F}_{2}(\beta_{j},\beta_{j}-\epsilon;\gamma_{j},\gamma_{j},\tau_{j},\tau_{j})=2\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta_{j};\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})-2\mathbf{F}_{1}(\beta_{j}-\epsilon;\gamma_{j},\tau_{j})\end{split}

where we used the fact that
\mathbf{F}_{2}(a,b;\gamma,\gamma,\tau,\tau)=\mathbf{F}_{1}(a;\gamma,\tau)
if
a<b
, which follows from the definition of (
179
).
Since the inequality holds for every
\epsilon>0
and the one-point distribution function
\mathbf{F}_{1}
is continuous, we find that

\begin{split}&\limsup_{(\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\to(\gamma,\tau,\beta)}|\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j)})-\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime}_{(j-1)};\gamma^{\prime}_{(j-1)},\tau^{\prime}_{(j-1)})|=0.\end{split}

Summing over
j
, we conclude that

\limsup_{(\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\to(\gamma,\tau,\beta)}\left|\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})-\mathbf{F}_{m}(\beta;\gamma,\tau)\right|=0,

proving the desired continuity.
∎

Appendix B
Formula of
D_{\mathbf{n}}(z)

We state the formula of
D(\mathbf{z})
given in
[
2
, Lemma 2.10]
and show that it can be written as the form (
25
) in Subsection
2.2
.
It is enough to check it when
p=1
since the general
p
case follows from the property (
9
).

For complex vectors
W=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
and
W^{\prime}=(w_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,w_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime})
, we denote

\Delta(W)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(w_{j}-w_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(W;W^{\prime})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}(w_{i}-w_{j}^{\prime}).

We also use the notation that for a function
g
of a single variable and a vector
W=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
,

g(W):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}g(w_{i}).

For
0<|z|<1
, the sets
\mathrm{L}_{z}
and
\mathrm{R}_{z}
are the discrete sets in the complex plane defined as

\mathrm{L}_{z}=\{w:e^{-w^{2}/2}=z,\,\mathrm{Re}(w)<0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{R}_{z}=\{w:e^{-w^{2}/2}=z,\,\mathrm{Re}(w)>0\}.

The series formula of
D(\mathbf{z})
given in
[
2
, Lemma 2.10]
is

D(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{0,1,\cdots\}^{m}}\frac{1}{(\mathbf{n}!)^{2}}D_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{z})

where for
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})
and
0<|z_{1}|<\cdots<|z_{m}|<1
,

\begin{split}D_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{z})=\left(1-\frac{z_{i-1}}{z_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}&\left(1-\frac{z_{i}}{z_{i-1}}\right)^{n_{i-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U^{(i)}\in\mathrm{L}_{z_{i}}^{n_{i}}\\
V^{(i)}\in\mathrm{R}_{z_{i}}^{n_{i}}\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)})^{2}\Delta(V^{(i)})^{2}}{\Delta(U^{(i)};V^{(i)})^{2}}\hat{f}_{i}(U^{(i)})\hat{f}_{i}(V^{(i)})\\
&\qquad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)};V^{(i-1)})\Delta(V^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})}{\Delta(U^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(V^{(i)};V^{(i-1)})}\frac{e^{-h(V^{(i)},z_{i-1})-h(V^{(i-1)},z_{i})}}{e^{h(U^{(i)},z_{i-1})+h(U^{(i-1)},z_{i})}}\end{split}

(181)

with

\begin{split}&h(w,z)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{w}\mathrm{Li}_{1/2}(ze^{(w^{2}-y^{2})/2})\mathrm{d}y,\qquad&\mathrm{Re}(w)<0,\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{-w}\mathrm{Li}_{1/2}(ze^{(w^{2}-y^{2})/2})\mathrm{d}y,\qquad&\mathrm{Re}(w)>0,\end{cases}\end{split}

\begin{split}&f_{i}(w)=\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{1}{3}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})w^{3}+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1})w^{2}+(\beta_{i}-\beta_{i-1})w},\qquad&\mathrm{Re}(w)<0,\\
e^{\frac{1}{3}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})w^{3}-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1})w^{2}-(\beta_{i}-\beta_{i-1})w},\qquad&\mathrm{Re}(w)>0,\end{cases}\end{split}

and

\hat{f}_{i}(w)=\frac{1}{w}f_{i}(w)e^{2h(w,z_{i})}.

Note that
w\in\mathrm{R}_{z}
if and only if
-w\in\mathrm{L}_{z}
. Thus, setting
\hat{U}^{(i)}=-V^{(i)}
, the sum in (
181
) can be written as

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U^{(i)},\hat{U}^{(i)}\in\mathrm{L}_{z_{i}}^{n_{i}}\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)})^{2}\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}\hat{f}_{i}(U^{(i)})\hat{f}_{i}(-\hat{U}^{(i)})\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})}{\Delta(U^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})}\frac{e^{-h(-\hat{U}^{(i)},z_{i-1})-h(-\hat{U}^{(i-1)},z_{i})}}{e^{h(U^{(i)},z_{i-1})+h(U^{(i-1)},z_{i})}}.\end{split}

Since
h(-w,z)=h(w,z)
, after inserting the formula
\hat{f}_{i}(w)=\frac{1}{w}f_{i}(w)e^{2h(w,z_{i})}
and using the notation
E^{i,\pm}
of (
32
) instead of
f_{i}
, we can express the above sum as

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U^{(i)},\hat{U}^{(i)}\in\mathrm{L}_{z_{i}}^{n_{i}}\\
i=1,\cdots,m\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{e^{2h(\hat{U}^{(i)},z_{i})+2h(\hat{U}^{(i)},z_{i})}}{e^{h(U^{(i-1)},z_{i})+h(U^{(i+1)},z_{i})+h(\hat{U}^{(i-1)},z_{i})+h(\hat{U}^{(i+1)},z_{i})}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}E^{i,+}(u^{(i)}_{j})E^{i,-}(\hat{u}^{(i)}_{j})\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\frac{-1}{u^{(i)}_{j}\hat{u}^{(i)}_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)})^{2}\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})}{\Delta(U^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})}\end{split}

where we set
U^{(0)}=\hat{U}^{(0)}=U^{(m+1)}=\hat{U}^{(m+1)}=\emptyset
so that
e^{h(U^{(0)};z_{1})}=1
, and so on.
The product involving the function
h
is
H_{\mathbf{n}}(U,\hat{U})
of (
29
)
and the next product involving
E^{i,\pm}
is
E_{\mathbf{n}}(U,\hat{U})
of (
32
).
Finally, again with the convention
U^{(0)}=\hat{U}^{(0)}=U^{(m+1)}=\hat{U}^{(m+1)}=\emptyset
, we have

\begin{split}&\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)})^{2}\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i)})^{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})}{\Delta(U^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{\Delta(U^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(U^{(i)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)})}{\Delta(U^{(i-1)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(U^{(i)};U^{(i-1)})\Delta(-\hat{U}^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i-1)})\Delta(U^{(i)};-\hat{U}^{(i)})}\\
&=(-1)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\mathsf{K}(U^{(i-1)},-\hat{U}^{(i)};\hat{U}^{(i-1)},-U^{(i)})\end{split}

in terms of the Cauchy determinant (
30
).
This is a factor of
R_{\mathbf{n}}(U,\hat{U})
of (
31
), and we thus find that
D_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{z})
is equal to the form (
25
)
in Subsection
2.2
.

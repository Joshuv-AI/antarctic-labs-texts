Holographic Complexity in dS d + 1 \text{dS}_{d+1}
Eivind Jørstad, Robert C. Myers, and Shan-Ming Ruan

Abstract
We study the CV, CA, and CV2.0 approaches to holographic complexity in ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional de Sitter spacetime. We find that holographic complexity and corresponding growth rate presents universal behaviour for all three approaches. In particular, the holographic complexity exhibits ‘hyperfast’ growth Susskind:2021esx and appears to diverge with a universal power law at a (finite) critical time. We introduce a cutoff surface to regulate this divergence, and the subsequent growth of the holographic complexity is linear in time.

The goal of this paper is to study the generalization of all of these approaches to holographic complexity in
\text{dS}_{d+1}

Let us consider a candidate surface
\mathcal{B}

\text{dS}_{d+1}

\left(u(\lambda),r(\lambda)\right)

(74)

where
\dot{x}=\frac{dx(\lambda)}{d\lambda}

\mathcal{L}=\sqrt{-f(r)\dot{u}^{2}-2\dot{u}\dot{r}}\left(\frac{r(\lambda)}{L}\right)^{d-1}

\sqrt{-f(r)\dot{u}^{2}-2\dot{u}\dot{r}}=\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{d-1}\,.

(75)

The holographic complexity (
74

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}=\frac{4N}{L}\int\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)^{2(d-1)}\,d\lambda\,,

(76)

where the integral would be performed on the extremal surface.

Since
\mathcal{L}
does not have any explicit dependence on

13

\int^{r\to r_{\rm{turn}}}d{r}\,\frac{-P_{u}}{f({r})\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}-U({r})}}\sim\lim_{r\to r_{\rm{turn}}}\,\frac{2P_{u}}{f(r_{\rm{turn}})\sqrt{U^{\prime}(r_{\rm{turn}})}}\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}-r}\sim 0\,.\\

(88)

So we can conclude that

\tau(P_{u})\,\,\text{is finite}\,,\qquad\forall\quad|P_{u}|\,.

(89)

Of course, this conclusion is true even for
|P_{u}|\to\infty
, as we have explicitly shown above in eq. (

). The key point in producing this finiteness is that the derivative of the effective potential was non-vanishing at the turning point. Applying the analogous analysis for the finiteness for asymptotically AdS black holes (
e.g.,

Belin:2021bga
;
longpaper
), one finds that
\tau\to\infty
precisely when the trajectory approaches a local maximum in the effective potential,
i.e.,

U^{\prime}(r=r_{\rm{turn}})=0
.

Divergent behaviour for
\tau\to\tau_{\infty}

We have eq. (
83
) relating the growth rate of the holographic complexity to the conserved momentum. Hence we know that the growth rate diverges as
\tau\to\tau_{\infty}
, since this corresponds to
P_{u}\to\infty
. However, we would now like to extend the analysis above to show that the CV proposal exhibits the same hyperfast growth as
\tau\to\tau_{\infty}
that we found for the CV2.0 and CA proposals.

To make a controlled approach the critical time
\tau_{\infty}
, we consider
P_{u}\gg 1
and consider a large-
P_{u}
expansion of eq. (
84
),

\begin{split}\tau&=\int^{r_{\rm{turn}}}_{r_{\rm{min}}}\frac{dr}{L}\,\frac{-P_{u}}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}-U(r)}}\,,\\
&\approx-\int^{r_{\rm{turn}}}_{r_{\rm{min}}}\frac{dr}{f(r)L}\left(1+\frac{U(r)}{2P_{u}^{2}}+\frac{3U(r)^{2}}{8P_{u}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{P_{u}^{6}}\right)\right)\,,\end{split}

(90)

where the first term matches eq. (
85
), which yields the critical time
\tau_{\infty}
. However, note from eq. (
81
) that
P_{u}\simeq\left(\frac{r_{\rm{turn}}}{L}\right)^{d}
in this regime. Hence, after integration around the turning point, all of the subleading terms yield corrections of the same order
\frac{1}{r_{\rm{turn}}}\sim\frac{1}{P_{u}^{1/d}}
. Therefore in order to derive the leading corrections to
\tau_{\infty}-\tau
in the limit
P_{u}\to\infty
, we need to account for all of the
\mathcal{O}(1/r_{\rm{turn}})
contributions together,
i.e.,

\begin{split}\tau(P_{u})-\tau_{\infty}&=\frac{1}{L}\left(\int^{\infty}_{r_{\rm{turn}}}\frac{dr}{f(r)}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{r_{\rm{turn}}}^{r_{\rm{min}}}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{2^{n}n!}\left(\frac{U(r)}{P_{u}^{2}}\right)^{n}\frac{dr}{f(r)}\right)\\
&\approx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2dn-1)2^{n}n!}\,\frac{L}{r_{\rm{turn}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L^{2}}{r_{\rm{turn}}^{2}}\right)\,,\\
\end{split}

(91)

where we have used the Taylor expansion for
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!x^{n}}{2^{n}n!}
. Summing this infinite series, we find that to leading order, the time becomes

Alternatively, we can notice that the leading correction to the boundary time around
\tau_{\infty}
is dominated by the integral around

\frac{-P_{u}}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}-U(r)}}\approx\frac{r_{\rm{turn}}^{d}}{r^{2}\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}^{2d}-r^{2d}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{r_{\rm{turn}}^{d}}{r^{4}\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}^{2d}-r^{2d}}}\right)\,,

and the first subleading term at the order

Extremal surfaces joining the cutoff surface

The above analysis of the time evolution of the extremal surface has revealed divergent behaviour of
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(\tau)

as
\tau\to\tau_{\infty}
, which reminds us of that found for CV2.0 and CA. To regulate the divergences and examine the evolution beyond
\tau_{\infty}
, we introduced a cutoff surface near future timelike infinite
i^{+}
for those approaches to holographic complexity. Motivated by the results derived in previous sections, we now consider using the same geometric cutoff for the CV approach.
Our results above (
e.g.,
see figure
11
) imply that the extremal surfaces connecting two boundaries at equal times on the stretched horizon simply disappear when the anchor time moves beyond a critical value
\tau_{\infty}
. In other words, as formulated initially, the CV proposal (
3
) does not work for late times (
i.e.,

\tau\gtrsim\tau_{\infty}
) due to the absence of an extremal surface. However, when we introduce a cutoff surface at
r=r_{\rm{max}}
, we will demand that the surfaces yielding the holographic complexity do not extend beyond this maximal radius. Hence, we will need to modify the prescription for the CV complexity in any event.

A natural proposal for the modified CV prescription is illustrated in figure
12
. In this regime, the ‘extremal’ surface is defined piecewise with three components
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
and eq. (
3
) is replaced by

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}=\mathrel{\mathop{\rm max}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\Sigma=\partial\mathcal{B}}}{}\!\!}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}})+\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon})+\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}})}{G_{\textrm{\tiny N}}\,L}\right]\,.

(97)

Here, the segments
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
extend from the stretched horizon, across the cosmological horizon, and out to some large
r
. For early times
\tau\lesssim\tau_{\infty}
, these spacelike surfaces could not reach the cutoff surface at
r=r_{\rm{max}}
, and hence there will not be a segment
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
,
i.e.,
this component is the empty set. With our symmetric configuration,
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
will meet at
t=0
at some
r=r_{0}
. We would extremize the local profiles of these segments and also the position
r_{0}
. This will result in the smooth extremal surfaces found with our previous analysis for early times
\tau\lesssim\tau_{\infty}
,
e.g.,
the extremal value of the meeting point would be
r_{0}=r_{\rm{turn}}
.

Now for
\tau\gtrsim\tau_{\infty}
,
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
can reach the cutoff surfaces. Hence as well as the two-component candidate surfaces considered above, we also include surfaces with a nontrivial
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
component. That is, a candidate surface
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
will extend from the stretch horizon to the cutoff surface at some
t=t_{\textrm{\tiny R},\varepsilon}>0
, while
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
intersects the cutoff surface at
t=t_{\textrm{\tiny L},\varepsilon}<0
. They are connected by
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
, which simply stretches along the cutoff surface from
t_{\textrm{\tiny L},\varepsilon}
to
t_{\textrm{\tiny R},\varepsilon}
. In this case, the maximization in eq. (
97
) involves locally extremizing the profiles of
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
, and also the positions of the intersection points,
t_{\textrm{\tiny L},\varepsilon}
and
t_{\textrm{\tiny R},\varepsilon}
, on the cutoff surface.

With the anchor surfaces placed symmetrically on the stretched horizons, we can expect that even with our modified prescription (
97
), the extremal surface will be left-right symmetric in the Penrose diagram. Hence, we focus our attention on
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
extending from the stretched horizon to the cutoff surface. To reduce the clutter in our equations, we denote the position of the intersection
t=t_{\varepsilon}=L\,\tau_{\varepsilon}
and
r=r_{\rm{max}}=L/\varepsilon
. Because the intersection between the extremal surface and the cutoff surface is free, there are infinite extremal surfaces labeled by conserved momenta
P_{u}\in(0,+\infty)
at a fixed boundary time
t_{\textrm{\tiny R}}=\tau L
. However, the extremization equations are still the same as before, but one of the boundary conditions is modified. Since the turning point should now be at
r_{\rm{turn}}\geq r_{\rm{max}}
, the conserved momenta of interest are bounded from below,
viz.,

P_{u}\geq\frac{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\equiv P_{\rm crt}\,.

(98)

The lower bound corresponds to the critical case where the extremal surface just touches the cutoff surface with
r_{\rm{turn}}(P_{\rm crt})=L/\varepsilon
. As before, the infinite limit with
P_{u}\to\infty
pushes the extremal surface to become a null surface located at

u=\text{constant}=t_{\textrm{\tiny R}}-r^{\ast}(\rho L)=L\left(\tau-\text{arctanh}\rho\right)=L\left(\tau-\tau_{\infty}\right)\,.

(99)

Following the analysis in the previous subsection, the contribution of the left and right segments is given by

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}=\frac{\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}})+\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}})}{G_{\textrm{\tiny N}}\,L}=8N\int^{L/\varepsilon}_{L\,\rho}\frac{(r/L)^{2(d-1)}}{\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}+f(r)(r/L)^{2(d-1)}}}\,\frac{dr}{L}\,.

(100)

Since the cutoff surface is simply given by
r=r_{\rm{max}}
, it is straightforward to derive the volume for
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
,

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon})}{G_{\textrm{\tiny N}}\,L}={8N}\,\frac{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,\tau_{\varepsilon}\,.

(101)

Of course, the intersection time
\tau_{\varepsilon}
on the cutoff surface is not totally free in that the profile of
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
connects it to the time
\tau
on the stretched horizon for a given momentum
P_{u}\geq P_{\rm crt}
. Similar to the eq. (
84
), the extremization equation implies the relation between
\tau
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}
,
i.e.,

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon}-\tau&=\int^{L/\varepsilon}_{L\rho}\frac{dr}{L}\,\frac{P_{u}}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}+f(r)\,\,(r/L)^{2(d-1)}}}\,.\\
\end{split}

(102)

Implicitly, we are considering the case where
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
do not intersect at
r=r_{0}<r_{\rm{max}}
. Hence, the intersection time (for the right part) is constrained by
\tau_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
. This implies the transition time from smooth extremal surfaces to the piecewise extremal surfaces is located at

\tau_{\rm crt}=\int^{L/\varepsilon=r_{\rm{turn}}}_{L\rho}\frac{dr}{L}\,\frac{P_{\rm crt}}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{\rm crt}^{2}+f(r)\,\,(r/L)^{2(d-1)}}}\simeq\tau_{\infty}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{2d-1}{2d}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2d}\right)}\,\varepsilon\,,

(103)

where the final approximation was derived by considering
\varepsilon\ll 1
. We note that this result is similar to eq. (
35
) where it was found that the effect of the cutoff surface was first felt for CV2.0 (and CA) at a time
\tau_{*}=\tau_{\infty}-\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
.

For any boundary time beyond this critical time
\tau\geq\tau_{\rm crt}
, we can find a (continuous) family of piecewise surfaces where
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
are locally extremized away from the cutoff surface. As described above, it remains to find the surface that maximizes the holographic complexity in eq. (
97
) by extremizing over the intersection time
\tau_{\varepsilon}
. That is, we determine the surface with the maximal volume by performing the maximization:

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}\left(\tau\geq\tau_{\rm crt}\right)=\max_{P_{u}\geq P_{\rm crt}}\left({\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}\left(P_{u}\right)+{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\right)\,,

(104)

where the time
\tau
is fixed and so eq. (
102
) determines
\tau_{\varepsilon}
in terms of the conserved momentum
P_{u}
.

First, it is easy to find that the leading contributions in
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}\left(\tau\right)
, and
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\varepsilon})
in the regime
\varepsilon\ll 1
are

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}\approx\frac{8N}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{2d-1}{2d}\right)}{(d-1)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2d}\right)}\,,\qquad{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}\approx\frac{8N\tau_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,,

(105)

respectively. One may naively expect that the maximization should identify the maximal-complexity surfaces in eq. (
104
) are those which maximize the extent of
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
along the cutoff surface because its contribution
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}
dominates above. However, we note that the naive expectation is incorrect because the variations in

\tau_{\varepsilon}
are only
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
and so
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}
and
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}
compete on an equal footing in the maximization (
104
). More precisely, we obtain the maximal and minimal values for
\tau_{\varepsilon}
as follows

\begin{split}\tau_{\varepsilon}|_{\rm max}&=\tau-\tau_{\infty}+\text{arctanh}\left(\varepsilon\right)\,,\quad\text{with}\qquad P_{u}\to\infty\,,\\
\tau_{\varepsilon}|_{\rm min}&=\tau-\tau_{\rm crt}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\text{with}\qquad P_{u}=P_{\rm crt}\,,\\
\end{split}

(106)

and as noted above, we will find
\tau_{\varepsilon}|_{\rm max}-\tau_{\varepsilon}|_{\rm min}\sim\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
.

6
Discussion

In this paper, we investigated three different proposals for holographic complexity applied to (
d+1
)-dimensional de Sitter space. We found that the hyperfast growth regime first discussed in
Susskind:2021esx
for the CV proposal appears quite generally. That is, the complexity grows much faster than the linear growth observed for black holes,
e.g.,

Susskind:2014moa
;
Brown:2015lvg
;
Carmi:2017jqz
. In fact, all three proposals exhibit a pole of the form

\frac{d{\cal C}}{d\tau}\approx\frac{N}{(\tau_{\infty}-\tau)^{d}}\,,

(111)

as we approach the critical time
\tau_{\infty}
– see eqs. (
32
), (
61
) and (
93
). An exception to this behaviour was the CV complexity for
d=1
, which yields
d{\cal C}/d\tau\approx N/\sqrt{\tau_{\infty}-\tau}
, as shown in eq. (
132
). This critical time has a clear geometric explanation in the gravity calculations
Susskind:2021esx
. When the null sheets are emitted to the future from time slices on the left and right stretched horizons, they will intersect in the region behind the cosmological horizon (at early times,
0\leq\tau\leq\tau_{\infty}
). At
\tau=\tau_{\infty}
, the position of this intersection just reaches timelike infinity
i^{+}
in the Penrose diagram, and of course, for
\tau>\tau_{\infty}
, these null sheets no longer intersect (
i.e.,
they reach
i^{+}
before intersecting).

As indicated in eq. (
111
), all three approaches to holographic complexity yield a factor of
N
, the de Sitter entropy (
2
). As this entropy is thought to measure the number of holographic degrees of freedom,
e.g.,

WFN
;
Banks:2000fe
;
Bousso:2000nf
, it is natural that such a factor should appear in the holographic complexity. Eq. (
111
) is also written in terms of a dimensionless time coordinate on the stretched horizons
Susskind:2021esx
,
i.e.,
we chose
t_{\textrm{\tiny R}}=\tau L=-t_{\textrm{\tiny L}}
. Expressing the result in terms of the coordinate time
t
would introduce factors of
L
, the dS curvature scale, or alternatively of the Hawking temperature (
1
) measured by an observer at the center of the static patch. Perhaps a more natural approach is to use the proper time measured along the stretched horizon,
t_{p}=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}L\,\tau
. Expressing eq. (
111
) in terms of
t_{p}
would then introduce factors of the blue-shifted temperature measured by observers traveling along the stretched horizon,
i.e.,

T_{\rm{stretch}}=\frac{1}{2\pi\,\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}L}\,.

(112)

Of course, this is the natural temperature to associate with the holographic theory on the stretched horizons, and then eq. (
111
) becomes

\frac{d{\cal C}}{dt_{p}}\approx\frac{N\,T_{\rm{stretch}}}{[T_{\rm{stretch}}(t_{p,\infty}-t_{p})]^{d}}\,.

(113)

It is interesting to compare the results for holographic complexity here with the analogous results in AdS spacetime. One immediate difference is that the present complexities are finite at early times
\tau\lesssim\tau_{\infty}
. In contrast, in AdS spacetime, one finds universal UV divergences due to the contributions near the asymptotic boundary,
e.g.,
see
Carmi:2016wjl
;
Chapman:2016hwi
. These divergences are associated with introducing entanglement down to small UV distance scales in the boundary theory. Hence the lack of divergences at early times in de Sitter space is not surprising because it is expected that the holographic dual only has a finite number of degrees of freedom. As noted above, this number of degrees of freedom appears as the overall factor of
N
in the dS complexity. This is analogous to the leading AdS contribution with CV or CV2.0, which is also proportional to the number of degrees of freedom in the boundary CFT
Carmi:2016wjl
. That is, one finds the leading contribution to the complexity is proportional to
c_{T}\,{\rm vol.}/\delta^{d-1}
where
c_{T}
is a central charge characterizing the boundary CFT and
{\rm vol.}/\delta^{d-1}
counts the number of cutoff-sized cells in the corresponding boundary time slice. This general feature also extends to CA, although additional logarithmic factors exist.
15
15

15

Let us also note that if we had not included the null boundary counterterm (
54
) in our complexity=action calculations in section
4
, the leading divergence would have been
d{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny A}}/d\tau\approx N\log\left(\tau_{\infty}-\tau\right)/\left(\tau_{\infty}-\tau\right)
. This raises a number of interesting questions, but it also means that the results would depend on the parametrization of the null boundaries.
For example, the factor
\log(L/(d-1)\ell_{\rm ct})
appearing in eq. (

P_{u}=\frac{e^{\tau}-e^{-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\left(\frac{1+\rho^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}}-\cosh\left(2\tau\right)\right)}}\,.

(128)

Substituting eq. (
128
) to eq. (
126
), we finally obtain the time dependence of holographic complexity
\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
:

\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(\rho;P_{u})=4N\left(\pi-2\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\left(\tau\right)}\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho^{2}-(1-\rho^{2})\cosh(2\tau)}{2(1-\rho^{2})}}\right)\right)\,.

(129)

It is straightforward to check that its time derivative is given by

\begin{split}\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}}{d\tau}=8N\sinh({\tau})\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\rho^{2})}{\rho^{2}+1-\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\cosh(2{\tau})}}=8NP_{u}\,,\end{split}

(130)

as we claimed before in eq. (
83
).
Taking the limit
\tau\to\tau_{\infty}
, one can find

\lim_{\tau\to\tau_{\infty}}\,\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}\simeq 4\pi\,N-8N\sqrt{2\rho(\tau_{\infty}-\tau)}+\mathcal{O}((\tau_{\infty}-\tau)^{3/2})\,,

(131)

which is approaching a constant
4\pi N
rather than being divergent. However, we can still find the hyperfast growth of complexity,
i.e.,

\lim_{\tau\to\tau_{\infty}}\,\frac{d\mathcal{C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}}{d\tau}\approx 8N\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{2\left(\tau_{\infty}-\tau\right)}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\tau_{\infty}-\tau}\right)\,,

(132)

which is different from the power law in eq. (
93
) for
\text{dS}_{d+1}
. See figure.
16
for the time dependence of complexity and its growth rate in
\text{dS}_{2}
.

Introducing a cutoff surface

In contrast to the CV complexity in higher dimensional
\text{dS}_{d+1}
,
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
in
\text{dS}_{2}
remains finite at the critical time
\tau=\tau_{\infty}
, as shown in eq. (
131
). However, we still need a new description for
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
to understand the behaviour after the critical time due to the absence of the extremal surfaces connecting the two stretched horizons for
\tau>\tau_{\infty}
. Following the approach in section
5
, we introduce a cut-off surface at
r=L/\delta
and use the modified proposal in eq. (
97
) where the extremal surfaces are defined in a piecewise manner beyond the critical time
\tau_{\infty}
. The conclusions for
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
at later times in
\text{dS}_{2}
are then essentially the same as in higher dimensional dS. This subsection will show more analytical results to support the general analysis in section
5
.

From the extremality equation derived in eq. (
121
), the conserved momentum of the extremal surfaces that can touch the cut-off surface should satisfy the following constrain, namely

|P_{u}|\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}\,.

(133)

Correspondingly, the contribution of the extremal surfaces
\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny L}},\mathcal{B}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}
to complexity is defined in eq. (
100
) and derived as

\begin{split}{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}&=8N\left(\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{1+P_{u}^{2}}}\right)-\arcsin\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1+P_{u}^{2}}}\right)\right)\,.\end{split}

(134)

The complexity from the cut-off surface part
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
then reads

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}=8N\tau_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}\,,

(135)

where the intersection time
\tau_{\varepsilon}
on the cut-off surface is related to the boundary time
\tau
by

\tau-\tau_{\varepsilon}=\text{arctanh}\left(\frac{P_{u}\rho}{\sqrt{1+P_{u}^{2}-\rho^{2}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\log\left|\frac{\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}+1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}}-P_{u}/\varepsilon}{\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}+1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}}+P_{u}/\varepsilon}\right|\,.

(136)

The transition time is derived as

\tau_{\rm crt}=\text{arctanh}\left(\frac{P_{\rm crt}\rho}{\sqrt{1+P_{\rm crt}^{2}-\rho^{2}}}\right)=\text{arctanh}\left(\rho\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\rho^{2}}}\right)<\tau_{\infty}\,.

(137)

After the transition time
\tau_{\rm crt}
, we pick up the one with the maximal volume among those infinite discontinuous surfaces by performing the maximization:

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}\left(\tau\geq\tau_{\rm crt}\right)=\max_{|P_{u}|\geq P_{\rm crt}}\left({\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}\left(P_{u}\right)+{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\right)\,.

(138)

Explicitly, one can find the monotonicity with respect to the momentum
P_{u}
,
i.e.,

\frac{\partial\left({\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\rm ext}+{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}^{\varepsilon}\right)}{\partial P_{u}}=\frac{8N\varepsilon}{1+P_{u}^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}-P_{u}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1+P_{u}^{2}-\rho^{2}}}-\frac{1/\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1+P_{u}^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}}}\right)\leq 0\,,

(139)

where the non-positivity is guaranteed by the constrain in eq. (
133
) as well as
\rho<1<1/\delta
. As a result, we conclude that the maximal volume for those surfaces anchoring at boundary time
\tau
on the stretched horizon is derived as

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}\left(\tau\geq\tau_{\rm crt}\right)=8N\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arcsin\left(\rho\varepsilon\right)+\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}\left(\tau-\text{arctanh}\left(\frac{\rho\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\rho^{2}}}\right)\right)\right)\,,

(140)

with a linear growth,
i.e.,

\frac{d{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}}{d\tau}\bigg|_{\tau\geq\tau_{\rm crt}}=8N\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}\,.

(141)

Appendix B
Multiple Extremal Surfaces and Maximization

:

\text{dS}_{2+1}

r=\rho L

\rho=0

Except for the similarities with
\text{dS}_{2}
, a new feature associated with the higher dimensional dS spacetime is that the relation between
\tau
and momentum
P_{u}
is not monotonic in general. In other words, the sign of the derivative
\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}
is not fixed for
\text{dS}_{d+1}
for an arbitrary stretched horizon,
i.e.,
for small values of
\rho
. For example, we show the relation in figure
17
for
\text{dS}_{3}
. This feature implies that there is more than one extremal surface associated with a specific boundary time
\tau
.

Taking the boundary time
\tau
related to the extremal surfaces with a momentum
P_{u}
,
i.e.,
eq. (
84
), we first examine the derivative of
\tau
with respective to
P_{u}
,
viz.,

\begin{split}\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}=-\frac{dr_{\rm{turn}}}{dP_{u}}\frac{P_{u}/L}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}-U(r)}}\Bigg|_{r\to r_{\rm{turn}}}+\int^{r_{\rm{turn}}}_{r_{\rm{min}}}\frac{dr}{L}\frac{U(r)}{f(r)\left(P_{u}^{2}-U(r)\right)^{3/2}}\,,\end{split}

(142)

where
dr_{\rm{turn}}/dP_{u}
is given by

\frac{dP_{u}}{dr_{\rm{turn}}}=\frac{U^{\prime}(r_{\rm{turn}})}{2P_{u}}\,,

(143)

with using the definition of the turning point shown in eq. (
86
). It is obvious that both terms in eq. (
142
) are divergent due to the same singular point at
r=r_{\rm{turn}}
. However, we can explicitly find that these two divergences are exactly canceled and
\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}
is always finite. Focusing only on the divergent terms, we can get

\frac{dr_{\rm{turn}}}{dP_{u}}\frac{P_{u}}{f(r)\sqrt{P_{u}^{2}-U(r)}}\Bigg|_{r\to r_{\rm{turn}}}\sim\frac{2U(r_{\rm{turn}})}{f(r_{\rm{turn}})\left(U^{\prime}(r_{\rm{turn}})\right)^{3/2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}-r}}+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}-r})\,,

(144)

and also

\begin{split}\int^{r_{\rm{max}}}_{\rho}\frac{U(r)\,dr}{f(r)\left(P_{u}^{2}-U(r)\right)^{3/2}}&\sim\int^{r\to r_{\rm{max}}}\frac{U(r)\,dr}{f(r)\left(U^{\prime}(r_{\rm{turn}})(r_{\rm{turn}}-r)\right)^{3/2}}\,d\tilde{r}\\
&\sim\frac{2U(r_{\rm{turn}})}{f(r_{\rm{turn}})\left(U^{\prime}(r_{\rm{turn}})\right)^{3/2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}-r}}+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{r_{\rm{turn}}-r})\,.\end{split}

(145)

with substituting eq. (
144
) and performing the integral around the maximal radius.
As a result, the potential divergences appearing
\frac{dt_{\textrm{\tiny R}}}{dP_{u}}
are canceled. Correspondingly, we arrive at the second conclusion about the finiteness,
i.e.,

\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}\,\,\text{is finite}\,,\qquad\forall\quad|P_{u}|\,,

(146)

for the extremal surfaces in
\text{dS}_{d+1}
. This also indicates the difference with the linear growth for the extremal surfaces in asymptotically AdS whose potential contains a maximum at the turning point.

Although we have shown the finiteness of
\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}
, its sign is still undetermined because it depends on the choice of the stretched horizon. Due to
f(r_{\rm{turn}})<0
, the two terms shown in eq. (
142
) are positive and negative, respectively. However, it is still straightforward to show
\frac{d}{dr_{\rm{min}}}\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}>0
.
When the minimal radius,
i.e.,

r_{\rm{min}}=\rho L
is too small,
\frac{d\tau}{dP_{u}}
could be negative since the second term in eq. (
142
). A characteristic behavior of
P_{u}(\tau)
is illustrated in figure.
18
. Taking any time slice at
\tau\in(-\tau_{\infty},+\tau_{\infty})
, there are three corresponding momentums
P_{u}
,
i.e.,
three extremal surfaces anchoring at this boundary time. Among these candidates, we should pick up the one with the maximal volume for holographic complexity
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
. We will prove in the following that the maximal one is always given by the extremal surface with larger momentum
|P_{u}|
, which is also the one smoothly related to the critical null surface
u=0
.

:
Multiple extremal surfaces at a fixed boundary time
\tau
.

Let us first take the boundary time
\tau=0
as an example. The corresponding momentums are denoted by
P_{+},P_{0}=0,P_{-}
, as shown in figure.
18
. Assuming the complexity
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}
at
P_{0}=0
is given by
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0})
and considering the evolution of the extremal surface from
P_{0}
to
P_{+}
, we rewrite the holographic complexity
{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+})
as

\begin{split}{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+})={\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-})&={\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0})+\int_{0}^{-\tau_{\rm max}}d\tau\frac{d{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}}{d\tau}+\int^{0}_{-\tau_{\rm max}}d\tau\frac{d{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}}{d\tau}\\
&={\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0})+8N\int_{-\tau_{\rm max}}^{0}d\tau\left(-P_{u}(-)+P_{u}(+)\right)\,,\\
\end{split}

(147)

where contributions from
P_{u}(-)
,
P_{u}(+)
are negative and positive, respectively. As illustrated in figure.
18
, the negative and positive contributions are represented by the area of the gray region and yellow region, respectively. Obviously, we have
P_{u}(+)>P_{u}(-)
as well as

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+})={\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-})>{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0})\,,\quad\text{with}\qquad\tau=0\,.

(148)

We can then move to an arbitrary boundary time
\tau\in(-\tau_{\infty},\tau_{\infty})
with three extremal surfaces labeled by conserved momentums
P_{0}^{\prime},P_{+}^{\prime},P_{-}^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, we consider an example with
\tau>0
as indicated by the purple line in figure.
18
. Following the method introduced above, one can find

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+}^{\prime})>{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+})\,,\quad{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0}^{\prime})<{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0})\,,\quad{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-}^{\prime})<{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-})\,,\quad{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-}^{\prime})>{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0}^{\prime})\,,

(149)

Combing the these inequalities with eq. (
148
), we finally conclude that

{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{+}^{\prime})>{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{-}^{\prime})>{\cal C}_{\textrm{\tiny V}}(P_{0}^{\prime})\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\tau\in[0,+\tau_{\infty}]\,.

(150)

This inequality indicates that the maximization over all extremal surfaces at a fixed boundary time always arrives at the extremal surface with a larger
|P_{u}|
, which belongs to the branch with the critical null surface at
u=0
.

Robust VAEs via Generating Process of Noise Augmented Data
Hiroo Irobe, Wataru Aoki ∗, Kimihiro Yamazaki, Yuhui Zhang, Takumi Nakagawa, Hiroki Waida, Yuichiro Wada, Takafumi Kanamori

Abstract
Advancing defensive mechanisms against adversarial attacks in generative models is a critical research topic in machine learning. Our study focuses on a specific type of generative models - Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs). Contrary to common beliefs and existing literature which suggest that noise injection towards training data can make models more robust, our preliminary experiments revealed that naive usage of noise augmentation technique did not substantially improve VAE robustness. In fact, it even degraded the quality of learned representations, making VAEs more susceptible to adversarial perturbations. This paper introduces a novel framework that enhances robustness by regularizing the latent space divergence between original and noise-augmented data. Through incorporating a paired probabilistic prior into the standard variational lower bound, our method significantly boosts defense against adversarial attacks. Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that this approach, termed Robust Augmented Variational Auto-ENcoder (RAVEN), yields superior performance in resisting adversarial inputs on widely-recognized benchmark datasets.

2.1
Variational Auto-Encoder

The VAE stands out as a widely recognized unsupervised generative model, based on an auto-encoder architecture. The auto-encoder is bifurcated into two components: an encoder
\mathrm{Enc}_{\bm{\phi}}
and the decoder
\mathrm{Dec}_{\bm{\theta}}
, where
\bm{\phi}
and
\bm{\theta}
are a set of the trainable parameters.
Given a data
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, the encoder returns
(\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}},\bm{\sigma}_{\bm{x}})=\mathrm{Enc}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{\sigma}_{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to define the latent variable
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\bm{z}=\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}}+\bm{\sigma}_{\bm{x}}\odot\bm{\varepsilon}
, where
\odot
is the Hadamard product and
\bm{\varepsilon}
is a
d
-dimensional random vector, whose distribution is a unit Gaussian
\bm{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{0},\bm{I}\right)
. The two symbols
\bm{0}\;\text{and}\;\bm{I}
respectively express the zero mean vector and the variance-covariance matrix defined by an identity matrix, respectively. By the definition,
\bm{z}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}},\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{x}}\right)
,
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{x}}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{\bm{x}}\odot\bm{\sigma}_{\bm{x}}\right)
.

The auto-encoder is trained under an objective that maximizes the log-likelihood
\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x})
. Since the direct maximization is intractable, the authors of
[
1
]
proposed the variational lower-bound to indirectly maximize
\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x})
, where
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x})=\int p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})p(\bm{z})\mathrm{d}\bm{z}
,
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})
is a likelihood defined by the decoder
\mathrm{Dec}_{\bm{\theta}}
, and
p(\bm{z})
is the prior distribution. The lower-bound
\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{x};\bm{\phi},\bm{\theta}\right)
has a form of

\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{x};\bm{\phi},\bm{\theta}\right):=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{z}|\bm{x})}\left[\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})\right]}_{\text{Reconstruction error}}-\underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{z}|\bm{x})\|p(\bm{z})\right]}_{\text{Regularization term}},

(2)

where
q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{z}|\bm{x})
is the approximated posterior distribution defined by
\mathrm{Enc}_{\bm{\phi}}
. The symbol
D_{\textrm{KL}}
means the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. In the original VAE, the normal Gaussian is set to the prior, and the KL term can be expressed as a closed form
[
1
]
. Usually, the reconstruction term is approximately computed by a sample from the approximated posterior.

2.2
Existing Methods for Building Robust VAE

The researchers in
[
12
]
introduced the regularization method called
total correlation
to the original VAE, which serves to smoothen the embeddings in the latent space. Additionally, by introducing a hierarchical VAE, it was able to achieve both adversarial robustness and high reconstruction quality.
The authors of
[
13
]
suggest that VAEs do not consistently encode typical samples generated from their own decoder. They propose an alternative construction of the VAE with a Markov chain alternating between encoder and decoder, to enforce their consistency. Their method is reported to perform well in downstream classification tasks with adversarial inputs.

2.3
Smooth Encoder

As briefly mentioned in Section
1
, the authors of
[
9
]
experimentally found that the original VAE was not robust against an input out of the the empirical distributional support. To solve the problem,
they proposed SE. This method assumes that the types of future attacks are known beforehand, thereby generating adversarial data
\bm{x}
from the original data
\tilde{\bm{x}}
tailored to these expected attacks. The method then employs a regularization to ensure the proximity between the representations of the original and the adversarial data.
This subsequently leads to a notable minimization of the entropy-regularized Wasserstein distance across the data representations. In SE’s framework, the derivation of a lower bound on the joint distribution of
(\tilde{\bm{x}},\bm{x})
plays a crucial role, leading to a corresponding lower bound on the log-likelihood by integrating out
x^{\prime}
.

In contrast to SE, our method is not reliant on foreknowledge of attack types, thus simplifying its computational process and eliminating the iterative gradient calculations required for creating adversarial data in SE. Additionally, our method is based on a variational lower bound directly on the joint distribution over the augmented data pair, as we will soon discuss.

3
Proposed Method

Let
\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}^{\prime}
be two augmented data instances generated from the original data
\tilde{\bm{x}}
. The joint distribution of this pair, denoted as
p\left(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\right)
, is assumed to have the following distribution
[
17
]
:

\begin{split}p(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})&=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\bm{x}}\sim p(\tilde{\bm{x}})}\left[A(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}})A(\bm{x}^{\prime}|\tilde{\bm{x}})\right]\\
&=\int A(\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}})A(\bm{x}^{\prime}|\tilde{\bm{x}})p(\tilde{\bm{x}})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{x}},\end{split}

(3)

where

p(\tilde{\bm{x}})
is the original data distribution, and

A(\cdot|\tilde{\bm{x}})
is the augmented data distribution conditioning on
\tilde{\bm{x}}
.

Similarly to the original VAE objective in Eq.(
2
), we consider a variational lower-bound of log-likelihood of the joint distribution over
\bar{\bm{x}}=\left(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}\right)
, as follows:

\begin{split}\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bar{\bm{x}})&\geq\mathcal{L}\left(\bar{\bm{x}};\bm{\phi},\bm{\theta}\right)\\
&=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bar{\bm{z}}\sim q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bar{\bm{z}}|\bar{\bm{x}})}\left[\log p_{\bm{\theta}}(\bar{\bm{x}}|\bar{\bm{z}})\right]}_{\text{\rm\textcircled{\scriptsize 1}}}\underbrace{-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[q_{\bm{\phi}}(\bar{\bm{z}}|\bar{\bm{x}})\|p(\bar{\bm{z}})\right]}_{\text{\rm\textcircled{\scriptsize 2}}},\end{split}

(4)

where
\bar{\bm{z}}=\left(\bm{z},\bm{z}^{\prime}\right)
are the corresponding latent variables to
\bar{\bm{x}}=(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
.

Inspired by the formulation presented in Eq.(
3
), we reconsider the generative process of the augmented data from the latent space perspective. We assume that, the corresponding latent variables,
\bm{z}
and
\bm{z}^{\prime}
, follow some conditional distribution given the original one
a(\cdot|\tilde{\bm{z}})
. By adopting Gaussian distributions for
a(\cdot|\tilde{\bm{z}})
, we achieve a closed-form derivation for Eq.(
4
). Our construction of a robust VAE will be based on the maximization of the novel lower bound.

Definition 1
.

(Variant of Example 3.8 in
[
17
]

The prior

in Eq.(

p(\bar{\bm{z}})=\int a(\bm{z}|\tilde{\bm{z}})a(\bm{z}^{\prime}|\tilde{\bm{z}})p(\tilde{\bm{z}})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{z}},

Note that we can recover Definition

[

4.1
Experiment Setup

4.1.1
Adversarial attacks

An adversarial attack is some carefully crafted alternations of the input data,
designed to mislead models into yielding erroneous predictions or classifications
[
22
]
.
In our study, we focus on small perturbations on the input data that can provoke substantial deviations in representations.
Given attackers an accessible encoder model
q_{\bm{\phi}}(\cdot|\bm{x})
, and a specified attack budget
\|\bm{\epsilon}\|_{p}\leq\delta
,
their goal is to induce the maximum change in model representations,
measured by some dissimilarity function
\Delta
:

\bm{\epsilon}=\arg\max_{\|\bm{\epsilon}\|_{p}\leq\delta}\Delta\left[q_{\bm{\phi}}(\cdot|\bm{x}),q_{\bm{\phi}}(\cdot|\bm{x}+\bm{\epsilon})\right].

(13)

In Eq.(
13
), we adopted
p=\infty
hence the maximum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
\Delta
takes Wasserstein distance or KL divergence.

Therefore, throughout our experiments, there are 2 types of adversarial attacks being considered in total.

To optimize Eq.(
13
) w.r.t.
\bm{\epsilon}
, we apply the Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm with 50 iterations and stepsize
\delta/25
, maximizing either Wasserstein distance or KL divergence between the encoded representation posteriors.

4.1.2
Experiment protocols

Referring the experiment protocols in
[
9
]
, our experiments with adversarial attack proceeds as follows:

As the first step, we train the VAE independently of downstream tasks until model converges.
The encoder and the decoder are symmetric 3-layer MLPs with PReLU activations
[
23
]
, without normalization layers.
The model is trained with RAdam optimizer
[
24
]
and learning rate 0.001.

We then freeze the encoder parameters, and extract representation
\bm{z}_{i}
for each training data
\bm{x}_{i}
.
The mean given by the encoder will be the representation
\bm{z}_{i}
:

\bm{z}_{i}=\arg\max_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{z};\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}_{i}},\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{x}_{i}}\right)=\bm{\mu}_{\bm{x}_{i}}.

(14)

Secondly, following the common linear evaluation method, we train a simple linear classifier on the pairs of fixed representations
\{\bm{z}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and corresponding labels
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
via the cross-entropy loss.

Finally, we assess model robustness by generating adversarial examples for each test instance using Eq.(
13
).
We report their test-set classification accuracy under such attacks in our results.

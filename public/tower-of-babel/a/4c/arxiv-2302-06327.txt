A damped elastodynamics system under the global injectivity condition: Local wellposedness in L p L^{p} -spaces
Sébastien Court

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to model mathematically mechanical aspects of cardiac tissues. The latter constitute an elastic domain whose total volume remains constant. The time deformation of the heart tissue is modeled with the elastodynamics equations dealing with the displacement field as main unknown. These equations are coupled with a pressure whose variations characterize the heart beat. This pressure variable corresponds to a Lagrange multiplier associated with the so-called global injectivity condition. We derive the corresponding coupled system with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions where the pressure variable appears. For mathematical convenience a damping term is added, and for a given class of strain energies we prove the existence of local-in-time solutions in the context of the L p L^{p} -parabolic maximal regularity.



Keywords:
Nonlinear elastodynamics,
L^{p}
-maximal parabolic regularity, Cardiac electrophysiology.

AMS subject classifications (2020): 74B20, 35K20, 35K55, 35K61, 74F99, 74H20, 74H30, 74-10.

1
Introduction

Heart beats enable the oxygenation of cardiac tissues and so guarantees an healthy electric activity. Mechanically, the oxygenation is related to the variations of the
hydraulic pressure

[
CTB88
]
, which is mathematically a Lagrange multiplier corresponding to the constraint of constant global volume of the heart tissues, as the latter are crossed by blood, considered to be an incompressible fluid. The time variation of this pressure quantifies shape variations of the heart via its deformation, and so determines the intensity of this contraction. In particular, this quantity is central when studying defibrillation, which is the underlying motivation of our contribution.

The focus of the present article lies in the mathematical modeling of the time deformations of the heart tissues, and related wellposedness questions for the derived system of partial differential equations dealing with the displacement field as main unknown. The heart is modeled as an hyperelastic tissue, crossed by blood, assumed to be an incompressible fluid, and so the total volume inside the heart has to remain constant through the time. Considering that the exterior part of the boundary of the domain is only subject to rigid displacements (see Figure
1
), this means that the total volume of the heart itself has to remain constant through the time. Therefore, beside the displacement field, we also introduce a pressure variable, that is a Lagrange multiplier associated with this constraint.

Figure 1:
Slice of the elastic domain
\Omega
. The exterior boundary
\Gamma_{D}
is immobile (or only subject to rigid displacements), while the boundary
\Gamma_{N}
interacting with the blood is free, subject to the constraint of constant total volume.

We are conscious that a complete modeling of such a problem would involve multiphysics considerations. More precisely, we would need, for example, to couple the bidomain model with hyperelasticity for describing the state equations
[
Dal+13
,
Ben+19
]
. The analysis and computations related to such a coupled problem are both challenging topics. Due to the obvious complexity, we consider in this paper only hyperelasticity, and rather focus on the modeling and wellposedness questions that arise. Hyperelasticity is already a difficult topic from a mathematical point of view, therefore analysis is achieved with the help of an artificial damping in the hyperelastic model.

A damped hyperelastic model.

Given a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2
or
3
), the state equation we consider in this paper is an elastodynamics system with damping and global volume preserving constraint. Its unknowns are a displacement field denoted by
u
, and a pressure
\mathfrak{p}
depending only on the time variable, playing the role of a multiplier for taking into account this constraint. Data are represented by right-hand-sides
f
and
g
, that represent volume forces in
\Omega
and boundary forces on
\Gamma_{N}
, respectively. The initial state is given by the couple
(u_{0},\dot{u}_{0})
. The system that
(u,\mathfrak{p})
is assumed to satisfy is given as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\ddot{u}-\kappa\Delta\dot{u}-\divg\left((\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\Sigma(u)\right)=f&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\left(\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}}{\partial n}+(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\Sigma(u)\right)n+\mathfrak{p}\,\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)n=g&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\Omega&&\text{in }(0,T),\\
\displaystyle u=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T),\\
\displaystyle u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\quad\dot{u}(\cdot,0)=\dot{u}_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{array}

(1)

The density of the material is assumed to be constant equal to
1
for simplicity. The strain energy model chosen for describing the elastic behavior determines the tensor field
\Sigma
(see section
2.3
for details about the elasticity model). The damping term
\kappa\Delta\dot{u}
(with
\kappa>0
constant) is added for the sake of mathematical convenience. Indeed, it enables us to rely on a well-established framework for parabolic equations, while the original system is hyperbolic and nonlinear. The constraint in the third equation of (
1
), namely the so-called Ciarlet-Nečas global injectivity condition studied in
[
CN87
]
, reflects the fact that the global volume of the domain deformed by
\mathrm{Id}+u
remains constant over time. Note that the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on
\Gamma_{D}
could be replaced by
u=0

up to rigid displacements
, leading us to consider solutions in quotient spaces. We explain how to derive system (
1
) in section
A.2
. From section
2.3
we will use the notation
\sigma(\nabla u)=(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\Sigma(u)
and
\Phi(u)=\mathrm{I}+\nabla u
.

Wellposedness questions for nonlinear elasticity models.

Without damping, and without volume constraints, global-in-time existence of elastic waves was obtained in
[
Sid96
,
Sid00a
,
Age00
]
assuming the so-called
null condition
, and in
[
Sid00
]
assuming that the stored energy satisfies a
nonresonance
condition. When considering the more classical incompressibility condition, namely the local constraint
\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\equiv 1
, the same question was investigated in
[
Ebi93
,
Ebi96
,
Tho03
,
ST05
,
ST07
,
Lei16
]
, assuming that the data are small enough. While the system (
1
) satisfied by the displacement is written using the Lagrangian formalism, existence results were obtained in
[
LSZ15
]
(in the context of Hookean elasticity) and in
[
Yin16
]
by adopting the Eulerian formalism. For convenience, we prefer to consider a parabolic regularization, represented by the dissipation term
-\kappa\Delta\dot{u}
, orienting the study of system (
1
) towards methods related to parabolic equations. In this fashion, let us mention the contributions of
[
ZY09
,
Dia17
,
LT17
,
Rah17
,
GL20
]
. The parabolic framework is in particular suitable for realizing numerical simulations with finite element methods. As far as we know, the mathematical study of system (
1
) with the non-local constraint of constant global volume has never been addressed in the unsteady case.

Strategy.

It is reasonable to assume that the state variables are continuous in time, with values in smooth spaces. Therefore a strong functional framework is adopted, corresponding to the so-called
L^{p}
-maximal parabolic regularity
[
DHP03
]
, leading us to assume that a solution
u
of (
1
) satisfies

\dot{u}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{2,p}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega)),

with
p>3
. General assumptions are considered for the strain energy, and thus for the tensor operator
\Sigma
. With the help of results obtained in
[
Pru02
,
DHP07
]
, we first study system (
1
) linearized around
0
, leading to a wellposedness result for the nonhomogeneous linear system. Next, Lipschitz estimates are obtained for the nonlinearities, and we use a fixed-point method for proving existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
1
), by assuming that
T>0
is small enough. The difficulty lies in the careful and delicate derivation of these Lipschitz estimates in function of positive powers of
T
. Finally, a continuation argument enables us to characterize the lifespan of the solutions. The main result is stated in Theorem
4.1
.

Remark 1.1
.

For the sake of convenience we will assume
u_{0}=0
, meaning that the initial deformation
\mathrm{Id}+u_{0}
is the identity, and therefore that the reference configuration (described by
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
as given in Figure
1
) corresponds to the initial configuration at
t=0
. Considering the more general case
u_{0}\neq 0
would enable us to choose a reference configuration as convenient as desired (in particular smooth), but would introduce other difficulties: Either system (
1
) would need to be linearized around
u_{0}
, leading to a linear system with non-constant coefficients, or additional terms in the technical Lipschitz estimates of section
4.2
would need to be taken into account. Therefore we choose
u_{0}=0
, preferring making assumptions on the initial geometric configuration (see section
2.2
). However, for numerical realizations, considering
u_{0}\neq 0
would be of interest, as it would enable us to choose a simple computational domain.

Plan.

The paper is organized as follows: We introduce notation, assumptions and functional setting in section
2
. In particular, in section
2.4
we give examples of class of strain energies that fulfill the assumptions that we introduce in section
2.3
. In section
3
we study the linearized system in the context of the
L^{p}
-maximal parabolic regularity, and deduce in Corollary
3.1
a compact estimate for the nonhomogenenous system. Section
4
is devoted to the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of solutions for system (
1
). We prove Lipschitz estimates in section
4.2
, and derive the main result in section
4.3
, namely Theorem
4.1
. In Appendix
A
, we present modeling aspects of the problem, in particular how to derive system (
1
) from the least-action principle. Appendix
B
contains the proof of a technical lemma.

2.1
Linear Algebra notation

The inner product between two vectors
u
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted as
u\cdot v
, the corresponding Euclidean norm as
|u|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, and the tensor product is denoted by
u\otimes v\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, such that
(u\otimes v)_{ij}:=u_{i}v_{j}
. The inner product between two matrices
A
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is denoted as
A:B=\mathrm{trace}(A^{T}B)
, and the associated Euclidean norm satisfies
|AB|_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\leq|A|_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}|B|_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}
. The tensor product between matrices is denoted as
A\otimes B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d\times d}
, such that for all matrix
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
(A\otimes B)C:=(B:C)A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. We denote by
\mathrm{cof}(A)
the cofactor matrix of any matrix field
A
. Recall that this is a polynomial function of the coefficients of
A
. When
A
is invertible, the following formula holds

\mathrm{cof}(A)=(\mathrm{det}(A))A^{-T}.

Recall that
H\mapsto(\mathrm{cof}A):H
is the differential of
A\mapsto\mathrm{det}(A)
at point
A
. In order to distinguish scalar fields, vector fields and matrix fields, we will use the following type of notation

\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)=\left\{\varphi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mid\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|_{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\mathrm{d}\Omega<\infty\right\},\quad\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega)=[\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)]^{d},\quad\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)=[\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)]^{d\times d},

that we transpose by analogy to other kinds of Lebesgue, Sobolev and Besov spaces.

The domain

(2)

2.3
Assumptions on the strain energy

We introduce the deformation gradient tensor associated with a displacement field
u
as follows:

\Phi(u)=\mathrm{I}+\nabla u.

The strain energy of the elastic material is denoted by
\mathcal{W}
, and is assumed to be a function of the Green–Saint-venant strain tensor

E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Phi(u)^{T}\Phi(u)-\mathrm{I}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left((\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{T}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)-\mathrm{I}\right).

We denote classically
[
Cia88
]
by
\check{\Sigma}
the differential of
\mathcal{W}
:

\check{\Sigma}(E)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial E}(E).

In system (
1
), the tensor
\Sigma
is related to the strain energy
\mathcal{W}
as

\Sigma(u):=\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial E}(E(u)).

The derivation of system (
1
) from
\mathcal{W}
is given in section
A.2
. We make the following assumptions on
\mathcal{W}
:

\mathbf{A1}

The Nemytskii operator
\mathcal{W}:\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\ni E\mapsto\mathcal{W}(E)\in\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously Fréchet-differentiable.

\mathbf{A2}

For all symmetric matrix
E\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the matrix
\check{\Sigma}(E)
is symmetric too.

In section
2.4
we show that well-known examples of strain energies from the literature fulfill this assumption. From section
3
we will use the notation

\sigma(\nabla u):=(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\Sigma(u)=(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial E}(E(u)).

Note that the tensor
\sigma
is a function of
\nabla u
only, since the strain energy is chosen to be a function of he Green – St-Venant strain tensor
E(u)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}+\nabla u^{T}\nabla u)
. Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
implies that
\sigma
is locally Lipschitz from
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. This is useful for deriving in Lemma
4.3
Lipschitz estimates on the term
\sigma(\nabla u)
. For our purpose, namely proving Theorem
4.1
, it would be sufficient to assume that
\mathcal{W}
is only of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,\delta}
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, implying that
\sigma
is
\delta
-Hölder, but in view of the examples given in section
2.4
, it is reasonable to simply consider Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
, avoiding to deal with pointless technicalities.

Therefore Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
allows us to calculate the derivative of
\sigma
, denoted by
\sigma^{\prime}(\nabla u)\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega);\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))
at point
u
, as follows

\sigma^{\prime}(\nabla u).(\nabla v)=\displaystyle(\nabla v)\Sigma(u)+\Phi(u)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{W}}{\partial E^{2}}(E(u)).(E^{\prime}(u).v)\right),

(6)

where
E^{\prime}(u).v=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Phi(u)^{T}\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}\Phi(u)\right)
. Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
implies that the mapping
\mathbf{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\sigma^{\prime}(\nabla u)\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega);\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))
is continuous. Assumption
\mathbf{A2}
is used in section
A.2
only.

2.4
Examples of strain energies

Let us show that the assumptions
\mathbf{A1}
-
\mathbf{A2}
are satisfied by classical elasticity models.

The Saint Venant-Kirchhoff’s model.

It corresponds to the following strain energy

\mathcal{W}_{1}(E)=\mu_{L}\mathrm{tr}\left(E^{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda_{L}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(E)^{2},

where
\mu_{L}>0
and
\lambda_{L}\geq 0
are the so-called Lamé coefficients. The energy is clearly twice differentiable, its first- and second-derivatives of
\mathcal{W}_{1}
are given respectively by

\check{\Sigma}_{1}(E)=2\mu_{L}E+\lambda_{L}\mathrm{tr}(E)\mathrm{I},\qquad\frac{\partial\check{\Sigma}_{1}}{\partial E}(E)=2\mu_{L}\mathbb{I}+\lambda_{L}\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{I},

where
\mathbb{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d\times d}
denotes the identity tensor of order
4
. In particular, we see that if the matrix
E
is symmetric, then
\check{\Sigma}_{1}
defines a symmetric matrix.

The Fung’s model.

It corresponds to the following strain energy

\mathcal{W}_{2}(E)=\mathcal{W}_{2}(0)+\beta\left(\exp\left(\gamma\ \mathrm{tr}(E^{2})\right)-1\right),

where
\mathcal{W}_{2}(0)\geq 0
,
\beta>0
and
\gamma>0
are given coefficients. The space
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is invariant under composition of the exponential function when
p>d
(see
[
BB74
]
, Lemma A.2. page 359). The first- and second-derivatives of
\mathcal{W}_{2}
are given respectively by

\check{\Sigma}_{2}(E)=2\gamma\beta\exp\left(\gamma\ \mathrm{tr}(E^{2})\right)E,\qquad\frac{\partial\check{\Sigma}_{2}}{\partial E}(E)=\beta\exp\left(\gamma\ \mathrm{tr}(E^{2})\right)\left(2\gamma\mathbb{I}+(2\gamma)^{2}E\otimes E\right).

Again, if
E
is symmetric, then
\check{\Sigma}_{2}
is symmetric.

The Ogden’s model.

The family of strain energies corresponding to this model are linear combinations of energies of the following form

\mathcal{W}_{3}(E)=\mathrm{tr}\left((2E+\mathrm{I})^{\gamma}-\mathrm{I}\right),

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the tensor
2E+\mathrm{I}
is real and symmetric, the expression
(2E+\mathrm{I})^{\beta}
makes sense for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
by diagonalizing
2E+\mathrm{I}
, and the energy
\mathcal{W}_{3}(E)
can be expressed in terms of the eigenvalues of
2E+\mathrm{I}
. Since
2E(u)+\mathrm{I}=(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{T}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)
, if
(\lambda_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
denote the singular values of
\mathrm{I}+\nabla u
, and
(\mu_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
denote those of
E(u)
, we have

\mathcal{W}_{3}(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\lambda_{i}^{2\gamma}-1\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left((1+2\mu_{i})^{\gamma}-1\right),\qquad\check{\Sigma}_{3}(E)=2\gamma(2E+\mathrm{I})^{\gamma-1}.

Denoting by
(v_{l})_{1\leq l\leq d}
the normalized orthogonal eigenvectors of
E
, we further write

\check{\Sigma}_{3}(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\gamma(2\mu_{i}+1)^{\gamma-1}v_{i}\otimes v_{i}.

Note that the operator
v_{i}\otimes v_{i}
is the projection on
\mathrm{Span}(v_{i})
, and Assumption
\mathbf{A2}
is satisfied by
\check{\Sigma}_{3}
. The sensitivity of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors with respect to the matrix can be derived for example from
[
SS90
]
(see Theorem IV.2.3 page 183, and Remark 2.9 page 239). Thus after calculations we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\check{\Sigma}_{3}}{\partial E}(E)&=&\displaystyle 2\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{d}2(\gamma-1)(2\mu_{i}+1)^{\gamma-2}(v_{i}\otimes v_{i})\otimes(v_{i}\otimes v_{i})\\
&&+\displaystyle 2\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{d}(2\mu_{i}+1)^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}(v_{j}\otimes v_{i})\otimes(v_{j}\otimes v_{i}).\end{array}

This expression shows that the strain energy
\mathcal{W}_{3}
fulfills also Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
.

3
On the linearized system

Let be
T>0
. The goal of this section is to study system (
1
) linearized around
(\dot{u},\mathfrak{p})=(0,0)
. Note that the global injectivity constraint and the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on
\Gamma_{D}
, namely the third and fourth equations of system (
1
) respectively, can be derived in time, leading to the following system (see section
A.1
for more details):

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\ddot{u}-\kappa\Delta\dot{u}-\divg\sigma(\nabla u)=f&&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}}{\partial n}+\sigma(\nabla u)n+\mathfrak{p}\,\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right)n=g&&\text{on $\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T)$},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\dot{u}\cdot\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=0&&\text{in }(0,T)\\
\dot{u}=0&&\text{on $\Gamma_{0}\times(0,T)$},\\
u(\cdot,0)=0,\displaystyle\quad\dot{u}(\cdot,0)=\dot{u}_{0}&&\text{in $\Omega$}.\end{array}

(7)

In what follows, the variable
v
will play the role of
\dot{u}
, and therefore we will consider

v\in\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)=\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{W}^{1,p}_{0,D}(\Omega))\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega)).

We rewrite (
7
) in terms of
v
, by splitting the linear part and the remainder as follows

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{v}-\kappa\Delta v=f+\divg\sigma(\nabla u)&&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}+\mathfrak{p}\,n=g-\sigma(\nabla u)n+\mathfrak{p}\,(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right))n&&\text{on $\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T)$},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot\left(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right)\right)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}&&\text{in }(0,T)\\
v=0&&\text{on $\Gamma_{0}\times(0,T)$},\\
\displaystyle u(\cdot,t)=\int_{0}^{t}v(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,\displaystyle\quad v(\cdot,0)=\dot{u}_{0}&&\text{in $\Omega$}.\end{array}

(8)

We will assume the compatibility conditions
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{0}}{\partial n}+\sigma(0)n=g(\cdot,0)
on
\Gamma_{N}
and
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\dot{u}_{0}\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=0
. We want to prove existence of solutions for system (
8
) in the context of the
L^{p}
-maximal regularity
[
DHP07
]
, namely solutions
v
that lie in the space
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)
. Recall roughly the rigidity of this functional framework: The fact that if an operator owns the
L^{p}
-maximal regularity property for a given time exponent
p\in(1,\infty)
(namely the exponent
p
that appears in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T)
and
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T)
spaces), then it also owns this property for any
p\in(1,\infty)
. Further, this property is also independent of
T\in(0,\infty)
.

We rely on the main result of
[
Pru02
]
, stating maximal parabolic regularity in
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)
for a linear parabolic problem with mixed boundary conditions. Keep in mind that
p>3
. We recall and adapt this result to our context in Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that

F\in\mathcal{F}_{p,T}(\Omega),\quad G\in\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N}),\quad v_{0}\in\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{W}^{1/{p^{\prime}},p}_{0,D}(\Omega),

with the compatibility condition
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial v_{0}}{\partial n}=G(\cdot,0)
on
\Gamma_{N}
. Then there exists a unique solution

to the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\dot{v}-\kappa\Delta v=F&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=G&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T),\\
v=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T),\\
v(\cdot,0)=v_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(9)

Moreover, there exists a constant

such that

\|v\|_{\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)}\leq C(T)\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{p,T}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N})}\right).

(10)

In particular, the constant
C(T)
is non-decreasing with respect to
T
.

In the rest of the paper the notation
C(T)
will refer generically to a positive constant depending only on
\Omega
, the exponent
p
, the regularizing coefficient
\kappa
and non-decreasingly on
T
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
Pru02
, Theorem 4.3]
.
∎

In order to introduce the pressure variable, we need the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
n\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
, and that

\langle g;\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N});\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}=0

such that
\displaystyle\langle\varphi;n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}=0
. Then there exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
g=\mathfrak{p}\,n
in
\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
.

Proof.

We set
\mathfrak{p}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}\langle g;n\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}
, and for all
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
we calculate

\displaystyle\langle g-\mathfrak{p}\,n;\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g;\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}-\mathfrak{p}\langle\varphi;n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g;\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}-\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}\langle g;n\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}\langle\varphi;n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle g;\varphi-\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}\langle\varphi;n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}n\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}.

Since
\tilde{\varphi}:=\displaystyle\varphi-\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}\langle\varphi;n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}n
satisfies
\langle\tilde{\varphi};n\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}=0
, by assumption we deduce
\langle g-\mathfrak{p}\,n;\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})}=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
, namely
g-\mathfrak{p}\,n=0
in
\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
, and thus
g=\mathfrak{p}\,n\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{N})
, which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}H(0)n
such that

{\overline{H}_{0}}_{|\Gamma_{N}}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}H(0)n,\qquad\|\overline{H}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|H(0)\|_{\mathbb{R}}.

This is possible when
n\in\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}}-1/p,p}(\Gamma_{N})=\mathbf{W}^{2-3/p,p}(\Gamma_{N})
, which is the case because we assumed
n\in\mathbf{W}^{2-1/p,p}(\Gamma_{N})
in section
2.2
. From
H
we define an extension
\bar{H}
by solving the following heat equation:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{\bar{H}}-\kappa\Delta\bar{H}=0&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\bar{H}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}Hn&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T),\\
\bar{H}=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T),\\
\bar{H}(0)=\overline{H}_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{array}

Since
H
does not depend on the space variable and
n\in\mathrm{W}^{2-1/p,p}(\Gamma_{N})
, we see easily that the Dirichlet condition on
\Gamma_{N}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}Hn\in\mathrm{W}^{1-1/(2p),p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{2-1/p,p}(\Gamma_{N}))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1-1/(2p),p}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Gamma_{N}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{2-1/p,p}(\Gamma_{N})),

and since the compatibility condition
{\overline{H}_{0}}_{|\Gamma_{N}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}|}H(0)n
is satisfied, we derive from
[
Pru02
]
the following estimate

\|\bar{H}(0)\|_{\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)}+\kappa\left\|\frac{\partial\bar{H}}{\partial n}\right\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N})}\leq C\left(\|H\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1-1/{2p},p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}+\|H(0)\|_{\mathbb{R}}\right)\leq C\|H\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,T}}.

(19)

Now define
\bar{v}=v-\bar{H}
, which satisfies

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\dot{\bar{v}}-\kappa\Delta\bar{v}=F&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial\bar{v}}{\partial n}+\mathfrak{p}\,n=G-\kappa\frac{\partial\bar{H}}{\partial n}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\bar{v}\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=0&&\text{in }(0,T),\\
\bar{v}=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T),\\
\bar{v}(\cdot,0)=v_{0}-\bar{H}(0)&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{array}

From Proposition
3.2
, there exists a unique couple
(\bar{v},\mathfrak{p})\in\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{P}_{p,T}
solution of the system above, and satisfying

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\bar{v}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)}+\|\mathfrak{p}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}&\leq&C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}_{p,T}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N})}\right.\\
&&\left.+\displaystyle\|\bar{H}(0)\|_{\mathbf{W}^{2/{p^{\prime}},p}(\Omega)}+\kappa\left\|\frac{\partial\bar{H}}{\partial n}\right\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N})}\right).\end{array}

Combining this estimate with (
19
) enables us to conclude the proof.
∎

Estimate (
18
) of Corollary
3.1
is used for the fixed-point strategy in section
4
.

4
Local-in-time wellposedness for the main system

System (
8
) can be rewritten in the following form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\dot{v}-\kappa\Delta v=f+F(v)&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}+\mathfrak{p}\,n=g+G(v)&&\text{on }\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=H(v)&&\text{in }(0,T),\\
v=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\times(0,T),\\
v(\cdot,0)=\dot{u}_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega,\end{array}

where we introduce

\displaystyle u(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}v(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,

(20a)

\displaystyle F(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\divg\left(\sigma(\nabla u)\right),

(20b)

\displaystyle G(v,\mathfrak{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sigma(\nabla u)n+\mathfrak{p}\left(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u))\right)n,

(20c)

\displaystyle H(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot\left(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u))\right)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}.

(20d)

Solutions of (
8
) are fixed points of the following mapping

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathcal{K}:&\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{P}_{p,T}&\rightarrow&\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{P}_{p,T}\\
&(v_{a},\mathfrak{p}_{a})&\mapsto&(v_{b},\mathfrak{p}_{b})\end{array}

(21)

where
(v_{b},\mathfrak{p}_{b})
is the solution of system (
17
) with
(F,G,H,v_{0})
replaced by
(f+F(v_{a}),g+G(v_{a},\mathfrak{p}_{a}),H(v_{a}),\dot{u}_{0})
as data. Following Corollary
3.1
, these data must have the required regularity, which is proven in Proposition
4.1
. Further, to confirm that
\mathcal{K}
is well-defined, the data must satisfied compatibility conditions, namely

\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{0}}{\partial n}=g(\cdot,0)+G(v_{a},\mathfrak{p}_{a})(\cdot,0),\qquad\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\dot{u}_{0}\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=H(v_{a})(0).

Since we assume that
u_{0}=0
,
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}_{0}}{\partial n}+\sigma(0)n=g(\cdot,0)
on
\Gamma_{N}
and
\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\dot{u}_{0}\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=0
, these conditions are automatically satisfied.
Now for
R>0
define the set

\mathcal{B}_{R}(T)=\left\{(v,\mathfrak{p})\in\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R})\mid\|v\|_{\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)}+\|v_{|\Gamma_{N}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Gamma_{N}))}+\|\mathfrak{p}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,T}}\leq 2C_{0}(T)R\right\},

(22)

where the constant
C_{0}(T)
is the one which appears in estimate (
18
).
The ball
\mathcal{B}_{R}(T)
is clearly closed in
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{p,T}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{P}_{p,T}
. Let us prove that
\mathcal{K}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{B}_{R}(T)
, for
R
large enough and
T
small enough. For that we need Lipschitz estimates on the nonlinear terms (
20b
)–(
20d
). We first prove a set of technical lemmas.

4.1
Technical lemmas

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
B
be a Banach space and
\varphi\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)
. We have

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;B)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+T^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)},

(23)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T^{1/p}\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+T\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)},

(24)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\alpha,p}(0,T;B)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T^{(1-\alpha)/p}\left(\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}\right),

(25)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T^{1/p}\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+C\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)},

(26)

for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, assuming
T\leq 1
.

Since the local-in-time existence result, namely Theorem
4.1
, is obtained by assuming
T
small enough, in the rest of this section we will assume
T\leq 1
, for the sake of concision in the different estimates, but without loss of generality.

Proof.

We write
\varphi(t)=\varphi(0)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\varphi}(s)\mathrm{d}s
, and from the Hölder’s inequality we get

\|\varphi(t)\|_{B}\leq\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+t^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)},

which leads to the first estimate. Since

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}\leq T^{1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;B)},

we deduce the second estimate from the first one. Estimate (
25
) is deduced by interpolation:

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\alpha,p}(0,T;B)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}^{1-\alpha}\|\varphi\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}.

Using (
24
), the subadditivity of the function
x\mapsto x^{1-\alpha}
and the Young’s inequality, this yields

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\alpha,p}(0,T;B)}&\leq&\left(T^{1/p}\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+T\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}\right)^{1-\alpha}\|\varphi\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&\left(T^{(1-\alpha)/p}\|\varphi(0)\|^{1-\alpha}_{B}+T^{1-\alpha}\|\dot{\varphi}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}\right)\|\varphi\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&T^{(1-\alpha)/p}\|\varphi(0)\|^{1-\alpha}_{B}\|\varphi\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}+T^{1-\alpha}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&T^{(1-\alpha)/p}\left((1-\alpha)\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+\alpha\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}\right)+T^{1-\alpha}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)},\end{array}

leading to the announced estimate, as
T^{1-\alpha}\leq T^{(1-\alpha)/p}
when we assume
T\leq 1
. Finally, estimate (
26
) is obtained as follows

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;B)}&\leq&\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}+\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&T^{1/p}\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+(T+1)\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&T^{1/p}\|\varphi(0)\|_{B}+C\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;B)},\end{array}

where we have used (
24
), concluding the proof.
∎

We will also need a result concerning the stability by product of fractional Sobolev spaces. We deduce from
[
BM01
, Lemma 4.1]
, the so-called Runst-Sickel lemma, a consequence of
[
RS96
, p. 345]
, the following result:

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\varphi_{1}\in\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
for some
1/p<\beta<1
, and
\varphi_{2}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi_{2}(0)=0
. Then we have

\|\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\leq CT^{(1-\beta)/p}\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\right)\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
T\leq 1
.

Proof.

When
\beta p>1
we have
\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, and
\varphi_{1}(0)
,
\varphi_{2}(0)
make sense. Further,
[
BM01
, Lemma 4.1]
yields

\|\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\right).

By following the proof of
[
BM01
, Lemma 4.1]
, one can show that the constant
C>0
depends only on
\beta
and
p
, but not on the size of the domain
T
. Combined with (
23
), we deduce

\|\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}+T^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\dot{\varphi}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\right),

because we have assumed
\varphi_{2}(0)=0
. Next we use (
25
) for controlling
\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}
as follows

\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\leq T^{(1-\beta)/p}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})},

leading to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}&\leq&C\left(T^{(1-\beta)/p}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\right.\\
&&\left.+T^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\beta,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})}\right).\end{array}

Since
p\geq 1
and
\beta>1/p
, we have
(1-\beta)/p<1/p^{\prime}
, and therefore for
T\leq 1
we have
T^{(1-\beta)/p}\geq T^{1/{p^{\prime}}}
. Thus the announced estimate follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under Assumption
\mathbf{A1}
, if

(27)

In particular, for all

6

\mathcal{B}_{R}(T)
is convex, we get

\|\sigma(\nabla u_{1})-\sigma(\nabla u_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\leq C_{R}(T)\|\nabla u_{1}-\nabla u_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}

C_{R}(T):=\sup_{\|\nabla\hat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\leq 2C_{0}(T)R}\left(\left\|\sigma^{\prime}(\nabla\hat{u})\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathscr{L}\left(\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega);\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\right))}\right).

where we denote
\hat{v}
such that
\hat{u}(\cdot,t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\hat{v}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s
. Since from (
24
) we have

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\leq T\|\nabla\hat{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\leq 2C_{0}(T)TR,

\mathbf{A1}

is non-decreasing with respect to

28

and

, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{2,p}(\Omega))

and recall the notation
\Phi(u)=\mathrm{I}+\nabla u

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{cof}\Phi(u)\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}&\leq&C\left(\|\nabla\dot{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\right)\left(1+\|\nabla\dot{u}\|^{d-2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\right).\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\mathrm{cof}\Phi(u_{1})-\mathrm{cof}\Phi(u_{2})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}&\leq&C\|\nabla\dot{u}_{1}-\nabla\dot{u}_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\\
&&\times\left(1+\|\nabla\dot{u}_{1}\|^{d-2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\dot{u}_{2}\|^{d-2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))}\right).\end{array}

,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\mathrm{cof}(A)-\mathrm{cof}(B)\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}&\leq&C\|A-B\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\left(1+\|A\|^{d-2}_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|B\|^{d-2}_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\right),\\

23
) for controlling in the right-hand-side the matrix fields
A
and
B

Appendix A
Modeling aspects

In this section we address the modeling aspects of the problem, in particular the global injectivity constraint and the way system (
1
) is derived, as well as the form of the control operator.

A.1
On the global injectivity condition and the invertibility condition

The so-called global injectivity condition, studied by Ciarlet-Nečas
[
CN87
]
, writes

\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{det}\left(I+\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right)-\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u_{0})\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega=0,\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,T).

(37)

After derivation in time, and by using the Piola identity and the divergence formula, it is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u):\nabla\dot{u}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\divg\left(\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{T}\dot{u}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega=\int_{\partial\Omega}\dot{u}\cdot\mathrm{cof}\left(I+\nabla u\right)n\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=0.

Since we assume that
\dot{u}=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
, we then consider the following equivalent constraint:

\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\dot{u}\cdot\mathrm{cof}\left(I+\nabla u\right)n\,\mathrm{d}\Omega=0.

(38)

Furthermore, for the sake of consistency, modeling elastic deformations requires to guarantee that the mapping
\mathrm{Id}+u(\cdot,t)
is invertible for
t\geq 0
. Actually, assuming that
\mathrm{Id}+u(\cdot,0)
is invertible, and under regularity assumptions, this invertibility condition can be relaxed, provided that
t>0
is small enough. More precisely, we have the following result:

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega))
the following estimate

\|\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u(\cdot,t))-\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u(\cdot,0))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq t^{1-1/p}C\left(1+\|\nabla u\|^{d-1}_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega))}\right)\|\nabla\dot{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega))}

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

The result is provided by
[
CK18
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

Therefore, if
\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u(\cdot,0))>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, by choosing
T>0
small enough
we deduce that
\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u(\cdot,t))>0
also, and
\mathrm{Id}+u(\cdot,t)
remains invertible.

A.2
Derivation of the PDE system

Let us explain how the system (
1
) of partial differential equations can be derived from the least action principle. The kinetic energy of the system and the potential stored energy are respectively given by

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\dot{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega,\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}(E(u))\mathrm{d}\Omega,

where
\rho>0
denotes the density of the material, and
E
denotes the so-called Green – St-Venant strain tensor. We consider for hyperelastic materials some general strain energy function
\mathcal{W}(E)
satisfying assumptions
\mathbf{A1}-\mathbf{A2}
. Recall the notation
\Phi(u)=\mathrm{I}+\nabla u
., and note that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}(E(u))}{\partial u}.v=\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial E}(E(u)):(E^{\prime}(u).v)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\check{\Sigma}(E(u)):\left(\Phi(u)^{T}\nabla v+\nabla v^{T}\Phi(u)\right)=\Sigma(u):\left(\Phi(u)^{T}\nabla v\right),\end{array}

as the tensor
\check{\Sigma}(E)
is assumed to be symmetric.
Denoting by
\mathfrak{p}
a Lagrange multiplier for the constraint (
37
), we consider a saddle-point of the following Lagrangian functional:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L(u,\dot{u},\mathfrak{p})&=&\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho|\dot{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega-\mathcal{W}(E(u))\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega}f\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Gamma_{N}}g\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}\right)\\[10.0pt]
&&\displaystyle-\mathfrak{p}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{det}\left(\Phi(u)\right)-\mathrm{det}\left(\Phi(u_{0})\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega.\end{array}

Using the Green formula, the first-order derivatives of
L
are obtained as follows

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta u}&=&\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{u}}\right),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta u}(u,\mathfrak{p}).v&=&\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\rho\ddot{u}\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\Omega-\int_{\Omega}\nabla v:(\Phi(u)\Sigma(u))\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega}g\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}\\
&&\displaystyle-\mathfrak{p}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right):\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\Omega\\[10.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho\ddot{u}-\divg(\Phi(u)\Sigma(u))-f\right)\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\Omega\\[10.0pt]
&&\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\left(\Phi(u)\Sigma(u)n+\mathfrak{p}\,\mathrm{cof}\left(\Phi(u)\right)n-g\right)\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mathfrak{p}}(u,\mathfrak{p})&=&\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{det}\left(\Phi(u)\right)-\mathrm{det}\left(\Phi(u_{0})\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega.\end{array}

Thus, from the Euler-Lagrange equation, a critical point
(u,\mathfrak{p})
of the functional
L
satisfies the Ciarlet-Nečas condition (
37
), and

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\rho\ddot{u}-\divg\sigma(\nabla u)=f&&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
\sigma(\nabla u)n+p\ \mathrm{cof}\left(F(u)\right)n=g&&\text{on $\Gamma_{N}\times(0,T)$},\end{array}

recalling the notation
\sigma(\nabla u)=\Phi(u)\Sigma(u)
. For the sake of simplicity, and without loss of generality, we choose
\rho\equiv 1
. The first equation above is hyperbolic. For mathematical purpose we introduce a parabolic regularization, by adding the diffusion term
-\kappa\Delta\dot{u}
in the first equation, and its corresponding Neumann term
\kappa\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{u}}{\partial n}
in the second equation, for some constant
\kappa>0
. Replacing equivalently the Ciarlet-Nečas condition by its time-derivative (
38
), the resulting system is system (
7
), equivalent to system (
1
).

A.3
The pressure as a function of the displacement field

Keep in mind that the pressure does not depend on the space variable. In the case where
g=0
, One can multiply the second equation of (
1
) by
\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{-1}=\mathrm{det}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{-1}(\mathrm{I}+\nabla u)^{T}=\mathrm{det}(\Phi(u))^{-1}\Phi(u)^{T}
, and then one derives an expression for the pressure, written in terms of
(u,\dot{u})
, as follows:

\mathfrak{p}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{N}}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\mathrm{det}(\Phi(u))^{-1}\Phi(u)^{T}\left(\kappa\frac{\partial\dot{u}}{\partial n}+\sigma(\nabla u)n-g\right)\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}.

(39)

Remark A.1
.

Note that in the formal case where
\kappa=0
and
g=0
, and for strain energy density functions that are function of the so-called symmetric right Cauchy–Green deformation tensor
C:=\Phi(u)^{T}\Phi(u)
, so that we have
\sigma(\nabla u)=\Phi(u)\hat{\Sigma}(C)
, and the pressure
\mathfrak{p}
can be expressed only in terms of
C
as follows

\mathfrak{p}\,n=-\mathrm{det}(C)^{-1/2}C\hat{\Sigma}(C)n,\quad\mathfrak{p}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma_{N}}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\mathrm{det}(C)^{-1/2}\hat{\Sigma}(C)n\cdot Cn\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}.

Thus, physically,
\mathfrak{p}
is a function of local change in distances due to the deformation
x\mapsto\mathrm{Id}+u
.

Using (
39
) and the same previous techniques of estimation, we can deduce the following regularity for the pressure:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u\in\mathcal{U}_{p,T}(\Omega)\\
g\in\mathcal{G}_{p,T}(\Gamma_{N})\end{array}\right.\Rightarrow\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{W}^{1/2p^{\prime},p}(0,T;\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{P}_{p,T}.

In particular, the pressure
\mathfrak{p}
is continuous in time.

Remark A.2
.

Assume
\kappa=0
and
g=0
. Taking the scalar product of the second equation of (
1
) by any test function
v
, and integrating over
\Gamma_{N}
, we get

\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot\sigma(\nabla u)n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}+\mathfrak{p}\int_{\Gamma_{N}}v\cdot\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u))n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}=0

The displacement
u
defines the deformation
\mathrm{Id}+u
. Recall that
\sigma(\nabla u)=\Phi(u)\Sigma(u)
, where
\Sigma
is also called the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor. It is related with the so-called Cauchy stress tensor
\mathcal{T}
– defined in the deformed configuration
(\mathrm{Id}+u)(\Gamma_{N})
– via the following relation

\sigma(\nabla u)=\Phi(u)\Sigma(u)=(\mathcal{T}\circ(\mathrm{Id}+u))\,\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u)).

The integrals on
\Gamma_{N}
are transformed into integrals on the deformed boundary
\Gamma_{N}(t):=(\mathrm{Id}+u)(\Gamma_{N})
as follows

\int_{\Gamma(t)}(v\circ(\mathrm{Id}+u)^{-1})\cdot\mathcal{T}n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}(t)+\mathfrak{p}\int_{\Gamma_{N}(t)}(v\circ(\mathrm{Id}+u)^{-1})\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}(t).

See
[
Gur81
, formula
(14)
, page 51]
. Now choosing
v
such that
(v\circ(\mathrm{Id}+u)^{-1})=n
on
\Gamma_{N}(t)
, namely
v=(\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u))n)/|\mathrm{cof}(\Phi(u))n|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
on the reference configuration
\Gamma_{N}
, we obtain

\mathfrak{p}=-\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{N}(t)|}\int_{\Gamma_{N}(t)}\mathcal{T}n\cdot n\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{N}(t),

showing that the pressure writes simply in terms of the Cauchy stress tensor, more specifically via a traction term on the deformed boundary
\Gamma_{N}(t)=(\mathrm{Id}+u)(\Gamma_{N})
.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
2.1

Let us show that functions of
\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)
are
(\gamma-1/p)
-Hölder. For any
r>0
and
t\in(0,T)
, introduce

\omega_{t,r}:=\{s\in(0,T)\mid|s-t|\leq r\}\cap(0,T),\quad\quad\quad\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,r}:=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\omega_{t,r}|}\int_{\omega_{t,r}}\varphi(s)\mathrm{d}s.

We write

|\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)|\leq|\varphi(t)-\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,t}|+|\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,t}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{0,t}|+|\langle\varphi\rangle_{0,t}-\varphi(0)|.

(40)

Note that
|\omega_{0,t}|=t
and
t\leq|\omega_{t,t}|\leq 2t
. We first estimate the second term of the right-hand-side, by using the Hölder’s inequality, as follows

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,t}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{0,t}|&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{t|\omega_{t,t}|}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\omega_{t,t}}|\varphi(s)-\varphi(s^{\prime})|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{(t|\omega_{t,t}|)^{1/p}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\omega_{t,t}}|\varphi(s)-\varphi(s^{\prime})|^{p}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{(t|\omega_{t,t}|)^{1/p}}\left(\sup_{\text{\small$s\in\omega_{t,t},\ s^{\prime}\in\omega_{0,t}$}}|s-s^{\prime}|^{\gamma+1/p}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2/p}}(2t)^{\gamma+1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)},\end{array}

referring to (
2
) for the definition of
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}
. Thus

|\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,t}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{0,t}|\leq Ct^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}.

(41)

The first and third terms of (
40
) are treated similarly. The Lebesgue differentiation theorem states that
\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r}=\tau
for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
, that we use for
\tau\in\{0,t\}
. For any
r>0
, we estimate as previously

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,2r}|&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\omega_{\tau,r}||\omega_{\tau,2r}|}\int_{\omega_{\tau,r}}\int_{\omega_{\tau,2r}}|\varphi(s)-\varphi(s^{\prime})|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{(|\omega_{\tau,r}||\omega_{\tau,2r}|)^{1/p}}\left(\sup_{\text{\small$s\in\omega_{\tau,2r},\ s^{\prime}\in\omega_{\tau,r}$}}|s-s^{\prime}|^{\gamma+1/p}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&C|\omega_{\tau,r}|^{-1/p}|\omega_{\tau,2r}|^{\gamma}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}\\
&\leq&Cr^{-1/p}(4r)^{\gamma}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}\leq Cr^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)},\end{array}

where we used
|\omega_{\tau,r}|\geq r
and
|\omega_{\tau,2r}|\leq 4r
. Now we choose
r=r_{i}:=2^{-i}t
, and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we deduce

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r_{k}}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,t}|&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r_{i}}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r_{i-1}}|\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,r_{i}}-\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,2r_{i}}|\\
&\leq&Ct^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{-i(\gamma-1/p)}\right)\leq Ct^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}.\end{array}

Passing to the limit when
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

|\varphi(\tau)-\langle\varphi\rangle_{\tau,t}|\leq Ct^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}.

By choosing
\tau=0
and
\tau=t
we then get

|\varphi(0)-\langle\varphi\rangle_{0,t}|+|\varphi(t)-\langle\varphi\rangle_{t,t}|\leq Ct^{\gamma-1/p}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\gamma,p}(0,T;B)}.

(42)

Combining (
40
), (
41
) and (
42
) yields the first announced estimate. The second estimate follows by the triangular inequality, and completes the proof.

Data Freshness in Mixed-Memory Intermittently-Powered Systems
James Scott Broadhead and Przemysław Pawełczak

Abstract
aoi ( aoi ) is a key metric to understand data freshness in iot ( iot ) devices. In this paper we analyse an intermittently-powered iot sensor—with mixed-memory (volatile and non-volatile) architecture—that uses a tdc ( tdc ) scheme. We derive the average paoi ( paoi ) and average aoi of the system, and use these metrics to understand which device parameters most significantly influence performance. We go on to consider how the average paoi of a mixed-memory system compares with entirely volatile or entirely non-volatile architecture, and also introduce an alternative tdc strategy to improve system resilience in unpredictable environmental conditions.

IX
Improving System Resilience in Variable Environmental Conditions

Thus far we have considered a checkpointing system that can be optimised based on a known expected number of failures, yet in reality
ipd
are often placed in environments with variable and unpredictable failure rates—making it difficult to pre-determine an optimum rate of checkpointing. We now propose an alternative method of
tdc
for mixed-memory devices to improve system resilience—

(
sfc
)—in which the inter-checkpointing time varies between predefined intervals
\alpha
and
\beta

II
and
III

, varies between two fixed amounts,
K_{i,\alpha}
and
K_{i,\beta}
. Then, the processing time
P_{i}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{h_{\alpha}}K_{i,\alpha}+\sum_{\beta=1}^{h_{\beta}}K_{i,\beta}
. Additionally, the expected wasted processing per fail would be
\EX[L]=p_{\alpha}\EX[L_{\alpha}]+p_{\beta}\EX[L_{\beta}]
where
p_{\alpha}
and
p_{\beta}
are the probabilities of failure during an
\alpha
and
\beta
checkpoint, respectively, such that
p_{\alpha}=\frac{\EX[K_{\alpha}]}{\EX[K_{\alpha}]+\EX[K_{\beta}]}
and
p_{\beta}=\frac{\EX[K_{\beta}]}{\EX[K_{\alpha}]+\EX[K_{\beta}]}
.
\EX[L_{\alpha}]
and
\EX[L_{\beta}]
are the expected wasted processing due to a failure in an
\alpha
and
\beta

\EX[L_{\alpha}]=\frac{\EX[K_{\alpha}]+D+1}{2}
and
\EX[L_{\beta}]=\frac{\EX[K_{\beta}]+D+1}{2}
. This can also be expressed as

\EX[L]=\frac{\EX[K_{i,\alpha}]^{2}+\EX[K_{i,\beta}]^{2}}{2(\EX[K_{i,\alpha}]+\EX[K_{i,\beta}])}+\frac{D+1}{2}.

Following the same derivation of average

VI

paoi

+\EX[P]+2\EX[f]\left(C_{1}+\frac{\EX[K_{i,\alpha}]^{2}+\EX[K_{i,\beta}]^{2}}{2([\EX[K_{i,\alpha}]+\EX[K_{i,\beta}])}\right).

From this expression we observe that the average

On the structure of the affine asymptotic Hecke algebras
Roman Bezrukavnikov, Stefan Dawydiak, Galyna Dobrovolska

Abstract
. According to a conjecture of Lusztig, the asymptotic affine Hecke algebra should admit a description in terms of the Grothedieck group of sheaves on the square of a finite set equivariant under the action of the centralizer of a nilpotent element in the reductive group. A weaker form of this statement, allowing for possible central extensions of stabilizers of that action, has been proved by the first named author with Ostrik. In the present paper we describe an example showing that nontrivial central extensions do arise, thus the above weaker statement is optimal. We also show that Lusztig’s homomorphism from the affine Hecke algebra to the asymptotic affine Hecke algebra induces an isomorphism on cocenters and discuss the relation of the above central extensions to the structure of the cocenter.

2.2.
The affine Hecke algebra

Let
W
be as above and let

be the set of simple reflections. Let

be the length function
on

be the affine Hecke algebra over
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
attached to

-module,

is free with standard basis

T_{w}T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{ww^{\prime}}

\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
and the quadratic relation
(T_{s}-v^{2})(T_{s}+1)=0
for

. We write

for the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis element defined by

C_{w}=\sum_{y\leq w}(-1)^{\ell(w)-\ell(y)}v^{\ell(w)}v^{-2\ell(y)}P_{y,w}(v^{-2})T_{y},

where the

are the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials and

is the strong Bruhat order. We write

for the structure constants of

with respect to the

-basis.

2.2.1.
Cells, nilpotents, and the

-function

Given an element

of

, and likewise the

Using the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials, Lusztig defined partitions of

into left, right, and two-sided cells.
For

, write

or

There are finitely-many cells of each type. Each two-sided cell is a union of one-sided cells.
Lusztig proved in

that the two-sided cells of

are in bijection with nilpotent
conjugacy classes in
{\mathfrak{g}\check{\ }}
.

Lusztig defined a function

by setting

to be the smallest natural number such that

is a polynomial in

for any

. The

is crystallographic,

is always finite. In fact, if

is the nilpotent corresponding to the cell

under Lusztig’s bijection, then

, where

is the flag variety of
{\mathfrak{g}\check{\ }}
and

is the Springer fiber corresponding to

.

Define

to the constant term of

.

Let

be the degree of

.
The elements of
\mathcal{D}
all obey

and are called the

of

There are finitely-many distinguished involutions in

4.
The Springer fiber

In this section we show existence of nontrivial central extensions by an argument based on geometry of the Springer fiber
{\mathcal{B}}_{e}

using the criterion of Lemma
2

Let

be a 6 dimensional symplectic space, we have
{G\check{\ }}=\mathrm{Sp}(V)
.

We write

where

is a two-dimensional symplectic and

is a 3 dimensional orthogonal

Let

be a nonzero nilpotent acting on

Then the reductive part of the centralizer

{G\check{\ }}
.

Consider the Springer fiber

.

Proposition 1
.

The natural map

(5)

K^{Z}({\mathcal{B}}_{e})\to K({\mathcal{B}}_{e})

is not surjective.

Proof.

The variety

has 3 components

,

,

.

Here

consists of isotropic flags
(F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{6})
such that

, thus

.

The component

consists of flags such that

the following condition. Notice that the projectivization

is identified with

so it contains a fixed quadric

The component

consists of flags such that

Let

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting

Let
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of integral operators

\theta_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}
for which
there exists
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that for all
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0

the kernels
k_{t}
satisfy the following size and regularity conditions:

(
C
1)

\displaystyle\lvert k_{t}(x,y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(t+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\alpha+d}}

(
C
2)

\displaystyle\lvert k_{t}(x,y)-k_{t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\lvert x-x^{\prime}\rvert^{\alpha}+\lvert y-y^{\prime}\rvert^{\alpha}}{(t+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\alpha+d}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }\lvert x-x^{\prime}\rvert+\lvert y-y^{\prime}\rvert<t.

Let
S
be the square function

Sf(x)\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert\theta_{t}f(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

By the
T(1)
theorem of Christ and Journé
[
CJ87
]

it is known that
S
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if

\theta_{t}
applied to the constant function
1
gives rise to a Carleson measure

\nu\coloneqq\lvert\theta_{t}1(x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{t}/t\,\mathrm{d}{x}
on the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
.
A Carleson measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
is a measure which acts like a
d
-dimensional measure in the following sense.
Let
Q
be a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.
Denote by
\ell Q
and
\lvert Q\rvert
the side length and the Lebesgue measure of
Q
, so that
(\ell Q)^{d}=\lvert Q\rvert
.
Consider the Carleson box
B_{Q}\coloneqq Q\times(0,\ell Q)
.
Then
\nu
is a Carleson measure if

\nu(B_{Q})/\lvert Q\rvert
is finite for any cube
Q
.

Let
\mathbbm{1}_{Q}
be the indicator function on
Q
.
It has been shown
[
Hof08
,
Hof10
,
LM17
]

that
S
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if there exists a constant
C_{\mathsf{T}}>0
such that
for any cube
Q

the following
local
testing condition holds

(
T
)

\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{\ell Q}\lvert\theta_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C_{\mathsf{T}}\lvert Q\rvert.

A standard example for which these conditions hold is
\theta_{t}f=f*\psi_{t}
, where
\psi_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\psi(t^{-1}x)

and
\psi
is the mean zero Schwartz function which gives rise to the Littlewood–Paley square function
[
Gra14
, §6.1]
.
In particular, conditions (
C1
) and (
C2
) are off-diagonal conditions compatible with the scaling
while (
T
) is the cancellation condition
\int\psi=0
.

The aim of this paper is to show that this Carleson condition (
T
) is enough to obtain something better: a sparse domination.

1.2.
Main result

A collection of dyadic cubes
\mathscr{S}
is
\tau
-sparse if
for any
Q\in\mathscr{S}
there exists a subset
E_{Q}\subset Q
with the property that

\{E_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}
are pairwise disjoint and the ratio
\lvert E_{Q}\rvert/\lvert Q\rvert\geq\tau
for a fixed
\tau\in(0,1)
.

Our main result is an optimal sparse domination of
S
under the minimal condition (
T
).

Theorem 1.1
.

If
S
satisfies the testing condition (
T
)
then for any pair of compactly supported functions
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

there exists a sparse collection
\mathscr{S}
such that

\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(Sf)^{2}g\,\mathrm{d}{x}\Big\rvert\leq C\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\left(\frac{1}{\lvert Q\rvert}\int_{Q}\lvert f\rvert\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lvert Q\rvert}\int_{Q}\lvert g\rvert\right)\lvert Q\rvert

where
C=C(\alpha,d)
is a positive constant independent of
f
and
g
.

1.2.1.
Sharp weighted inequalities

Under condition (
T
)
the square function
S
is bounded on the weighted space
L^{p}(w)
for
p\in(1,\infty)
,
provided that
w
belongs to the
A_{p}
class of weights for which

[w]_{A_{p}}\coloneqq\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{\lvert Q\rvert}\int_{Q}w\right)\left(\frac{1}{\lvert Q\rvert}\int_{Q}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w
in
A_{p}
, let
\alpha(p)
be the best exponent in the inequality

(1.1)

\sup_{f\neq 0}\frac{\lVert Sf\rVert_{L^{p}(w)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(w)}}\leq C(S,p)[w]_{A_{p}}^{\alpha(p)}.

When
p=2
,
Buckley
[
Buc93
]
showed the upper bound
\alpha(2)\leq 3/2
.
Later Wittwer improved it to
\alpha(2)=1
and showed that it’s sharp
for the dyadic and the continuous square functions
[
Wit02
, Theorem 3.1–3.2]
.
The same result was obtained independently by Hukovic, Treil and Volberg using Bellman functions
[
HTV00
, Theorem 0.1–0.4]
.

Andrei Lerner was the first to prove
that
\alpha(p)=\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1}\}
cannot be improved
[
Ler06
, Theorem 1.2]

and to conjecture estimate (
1.1
) for Littlewood–Paley square functions.
After improving the best known exponent for
p>2

[
Ler08
, Corollary 1.3]
,
Lerner proved the estimate

(1.2)

\lVert Sf\rVert_{L^{3}(w)}\leq C[w]_{A_{3}}^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{3}(w)}

for Littlewood–Paley square functions
pointwise controlled by the intrinsic square function
[
Ler11
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Lerner achieved this by applying the local mean oscillation formula
to a dyadic variant of the Wilson intrinsic square function
[
Wil07
]
.
Then the sharp estimate (
1.1
) for all
1<p<\infty
follows
from (
1.2
) by the sharp extrapolation theorem
[
Dra+05
]
, see also
[
Gra14
, Theorem 7.5.3]
.
A proof of the sharp bound (
1.1
) for the dyadic square function
using local mean oscillation can be found in
[
CMP12
, Theorem 1.8]
.

While Lerner’s result relies on a pointwise control of the square function
S
,
our
1.1
implies the weighted estimate (
1.2
) by duality, and so
the estimate (
1.1
) in the full range with optimal
dependence on the
A_{p}
characteristic.

Weak type estimates
[
LS12
]
and mixed
A_{p}-A_{\infty}
estimates
[
LL16
,
DLR16
]

for square functions have also been studied
using sparse domination.

After the solution of the
A_{2}
conjecture by Hytönen
[
Hyt12
]
, sparse domination has been used to obtain
a simpler proof of the
A_{2}
theorem
[
Ler13
,
Lac17
]
and to deduce weighted estimates for a plethora of different operators including:
Calderón–Zygmund operators
[
Con+17
,
CR16
,
Ler16
,
CDO18a
]
,
bilinear Hilbert transform
[
CDO18
]
,
variational Carleson operators
[
DDU18
]
, oscillatory and random singular integrals
[
LS17
]
,
pseudodifferential operators
[
BC17
]
,
Stein’s square function
[
CD17
]
, and singular Radon transforms
[
Obe19
]
.

The sparse paradigm has already been extended beyond the classical Calderón–Zygmund theory
to control more general bilinear forms
[
BFP16
]
and
to obtain weighted estimates for Bochner–Riesz multipliers
[
LMR19
,
BBL17
]
.

Another take on sparse domination, which inspired this work,
is the sparse
T1
theorem for Calderón–Zygmund operators
[
LM17a
]
,
where Lacey and Mena obtained a sparse domination under a minimal testing assumption.

For two positive quantities
X
and
Y
the notation
X\lesssim Y
means that there exists a constant
C>0

such that
X\leq CY
. The dependence of
C
on other parameters
will be indicated by subscripts

when appropriate.

Given a cube
Q

, the quantities

,

and

.
We also denote by

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dyadic cubes

The standard dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a collection of nested cubes organised in generations

\mathcal{D}_{j}\coloneqq\{2^{-j}([0,1)^{d}+m),m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}.

Each generation
\mathcal{D}_{j}
is a partition of the whole space
and
\mathcal{D}=\cup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}_{j}
.
Any cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
has
2^{d}
children in
\mathcal{D}_{j+1}
and one parent in
\mathcal{D}_{j-1}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
Q^{(k)}
the
k
-ancestor of
Q
, that is the unique cube
R
in the same grid
\mathcal{D}

such that
R\supset Q
and
\ell R=2^{k}\ell Q
. We also denote by
\mathsf{ch}_{k}(Q)
the set of the
k
-grandchildren of
Q
, so that
if
P\in\mathsf{ch}_{k}(Q)
then
P^{(k)}=Q
.

2.2.
Haar functions

Given a dyadic system
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
Haar functions are an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by
linear combinations of indicator functions supported on cubes in
\mathcal{D}
.

On
\mathbb{R}
, for a given interval
I\in\mathcal{D}

let
I^{-}
and
I^{+}
be the left and the right dyadic child of
I
.
Consider the functions
h_{I}^{0}\coloneqq\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}\mathbbm{1}_{I}

and
h_{I}^{1}\coloneqq(\mathbbm{1}_{I^{-}}-\mathbbm{1}_{I^{+}})\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}
.
Then
\{h_{I}^{1}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
is an orthonormal complete system of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
In higher dimensions, as a cube
I
is the product of intervals
I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{d}
,
a non-constant Haar function
h_{I}^{\epsilon}
is the product
h_{I_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\times\dots\times h_{I_{d}}^{\epsilon_{d}}
, where
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{i})_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{d}\setminus\{0\}^{d}
.

A function
f
in
L^{2}
can be written in the Haar basis:

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}\setminus\{0\}^{d}}\langle f,h_{I}^{\epsilon}\rangle h_{I}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{J\in\mathsf{ch}_{1}(I)}\left(\langle f\rangle_{J}-\langle f\rangle_{I}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{J}\eqqcolon\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Delta_{I}f.

In this paper the sum over
\epsilon
is not important, so both the superscript and the sum will be omitted and

h_{I}
will denote a non-constant Haar function.
Two bounds that will be used are

(2.1)

\lVert\Delta_{I}f\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\lvert\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\rvert\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}\leq\int_{I}\lvert f\rvert,\qquad\lVert\Delta_{I}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lvert\langle f,h_{I}\rangle\rvert\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}\leq\fint_{I}\lvert f\rvert.

2.3.
Good and bad cubes

A cube is called
good
if it is distant from the boundary of any much larger cube.
More precisely, we have the following

Definition 2.1

(Good cubes)
.

Given two parameters
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
a cube
R\in\mathcal{D}
is
r
-good if
\operatorname{d}(R,\partial P)>(\ell R)^{\gamma}(\ell P)^{1-\gamma}

for any
P\in\mathcal{D}
with
\ell P\geq 2^{r}\ell R
.

A cube which is not good is a bad cube.

It is useful to fix
\gamma=\alpha/(4\alpha+4d)
. This is just a convenient choice and
any other value of
\gamma
strictly between
0
and
\alpha/(2\alpha+2d)
would work as well.

2.4.
Shifted dyadic cubes

Given a sequence
\omega=\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\{0,1\}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
and a cube
R\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
of length
2^{-j}
,
the translation of
R
by
\omega
is defined by

R\dot{+}\omega\coloneqq R+x_{j}\qquad\text{ where }\quad x_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{i>j}\omega_{i}2^{-i}.

For a fixed
\omega
, let
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
be the collection of dyadic cubes in
\mathcal{D}
translated by
\omega
.
The standard dyadic grid corresponds to
\mathcal{D}^{0}
where
\omega_{i}=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Shifted dyadic grids enjoy the same nested properties of the standard grid
\mathcal{D}^{0}
,
together with other properties that will be useful later, see
Remark

4.5
.
For more on dyadic grids, we refer the reader to the beautiful survey
[
Per19
, §3]
.

2.5.
Random shifts

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the unique probability measure on
\Omega\coloneqq(\{0,1\}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
the coordinate projections are independent and uniformly distributed.
Fix
R\in\mathcal{D}^{0}
with
\ell R=2^{-j}
and consider
J\in\mathcal{D}^{0}
with
\ell J>\ell R
.
The translated cube
J\dot{+}\omega
is

\displaystyle J\dot{+}\omega

\displaystyle=J+\sum_{2^{-i}<\ell R}\omega_{i}2^{-i}+\sum_{\ell R\leq 2^{-i}<\ell J}\omega_{i}2^{-i},

\displaystyle R\dot{+}\omega

\displaystyle=R+\sum_{2^{-i}<\ell R}\omega_{i}2^{-i}.

The position of
R\dot{+}\omega
depends on the
i
such that
2^{-i}<\ell R

while the goodness of
R\dot{+}\omega
, since
R
and
J
are translated by the same
\omega
,
depends on the
i
such that
2^{-i}\geq\ell R
.
Then position and goodness of a cube are independent random variables, see
[
Hyt12
]
.

Let
\mathbbm{1}_{\text{good}}
be the function on
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
which takes value
0
on bad cubes and
1
on good cubes.
The probability of a cube
R
to be good is
\pi_{\text{good}}=\mathbb{P}(R\dot{+}\omega\,\text{is good})=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{good}}(R\dot{+}\omega)]
,
where
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
is the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. The probability
\pi_{\text{good}}>0
provided to choose
r
large enough, see
[
Hyt17
, Lemma 2.3]
.
The indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{R\dot{+}\omega}(\,\cdot\,)
depends only on the position of
R\dot{+}\omega
, so by the independence of
goodness and position, for any cube
R\in\mathcal{D}^{0}
we have

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{good}}(R\dot{+}\omega)]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\mathbbm{1}_{R\dot{+}\omega}(\,\cdot\,)]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{R\dot{+}\omega\text{ good}\}}(\,\cdot\,)].

2.6.
Calderón–Zygmund decomposition on dyadic grandchildren

Let
R
be a dyadic cube. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
R_{r}
a
r
-dyadic child of
R
in
\mathsf{ch}_{r}(R)
, so that
R_{r}^{(r)}=R
.

Proposition 2.2

(Calderón–Zygmund decomposition on
r
-grandchildren)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
f
be a function in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For any
\lambda>0
there exists a collection of maximal dyadic cubes
\mathcal{L}
and
two functions
a
and
b

such that
f=a+b
, with
\lVert a\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{d(r+1)}\lambda
and

b\coloneqq\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{L_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(L)}b_{L_{r}},\quad\text{ where }\quad b_{L_{r}}\coloneqq\Big(f-\langle f\rangle_{L_{r}}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{L_{r}}.

Remark 2.3
.

When
r=0
, this is the usual Calderón–Zygmund decomposition of
f
, see
[
Gra14
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.

Proof.

Given
\lambda>0
, let
\mathcal{L}
be the collection of maximal dyadic cubes
L
covering the set

E\coloneqq\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{Q}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)>\lambda\Big\}=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}L

so that

\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{L}\in(\lambda,2^{d}\lambda]
for each
L\in\mathcal{L}
.
Let

a\coloneqq f\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\complement}}+\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{L_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(L)}\langle f\rangle_{L_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{L_{r}},\qquad b\coloneqq f-a.

The cubes in
\mathsf{ch}_{r}(L)
are a partition of
L
. Since the cubes
L
in
\mathcal{L}
are disjoint, we have

\lVert a\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lambda+\sup_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\sup_{L_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(L)}\lvert\langle f\rangle_{L_{r}}\rvert.

Let
L^{(1)}
be the dyadic parent of
L
.
Then the average of
f
is controlled by

\Big\lvert\frac{1}{\lvert L_{r}\rvert}\int_{L_{r}}f\Big\rvert\leq\frac{\lvert L^{(1)}\rvert}{\lvert L_{r}\rvert}\fint_{L^{(1)}}\lvert f\rvert\leq 2^{d(r+1)}\lambda.

∎

3.
Decomposition and good reduction

For any fixed
\omega\in\Omega
the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
can be decomposed
in the Whitney regions

W_{R}\coloneqq R\times\left[\frac{\ell R}{2},\ell R\right),\quad R\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}.

Thus we can write

\langle(Sf)^{2},g\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\lvert\theta_{t}f(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}}\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert\theta_{t}f(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}.

Then we decompose
f=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}}\Delta_{P}f
. Given
R\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
, we distinguish
two collections of
P
:

\mathcal{P}^{\omega}_{R}\coloneqq\{P\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\,:\,P\supset R^{(r)}\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{D}^{\omega}\setminus\mathcal{P}^{\omega}_{R}.

We shall sometimes omit the superscript
\omega
in the following.
Bound the operator:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert\theta_{t}f(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}g\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\Big(\big\lvert\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{R}}\theta_{t}\Delta_{P}f\big\rvert^{2}+\big\lvert\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}\theta_{t}\Delta_{P}f\big\rvert^{2}\Big)\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}.

Consider the second term in (
3.1
).
Let
P_{R}
be the dyadic child of
P
containing
R
.
Then
\Delta_{P}f\mathbbm{1}_{P}=\Delta_{P}f\mathbbm{1}_{P\setminus P_{R}}+\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}

and we split the operator accordingly as before to obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert\theta_{t}f(x)\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}g\,\mathrm{d}{x}\lesssim

(I)

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\Big\lvert\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{R}}\theta_{t}\Delta_{P}f\Big\rvert^{2}\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

(II)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\eqref{term3}=

3.1.
Good reduction

Averaging over all dyadic grids
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\lvert\theta_{t}f\rvert^{2}\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}}\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert\theta_{t}f\rvert^{2}\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\textup{I}+\textup{II}+\textup{III}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\textup{I}+\textup{II}+\textup{III}_{a}\big]+\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\textup{III}_{b}\big]

is linear.

By using the identity (

) and writing

one can turn a sum over all cubes in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\textup{I}+\textup{II}+\textup{III}_{a}\big]=\pi_{\text{good}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{good}}(R\dot{+}\omega)\big(\textup{I}+\textup{II}+\textup{III}_{a}\big)\big],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\textup{III}_{b}\big]=\pi_{\text{good}}^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{good}}(Q\dot{+}\omega)\big(\textup{III}_{b}\big)\big].

We refer the reader to

, §2.2]
for an expanded version of (

)
with

.

From now on, the cubes

in (

III

The superscript in
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
, as well as the expectation

.

We apply

for

with

\}

and estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\Big\lvert\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\theta_{t}(\Delta_{P}f)\Big\rvert^{2}\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\lesssim\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\lvert\langle f,h_{P}\rangle\rvert\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{D(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert P\rvert^{1/2}\right)^{2}\lvert g\rvert\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

Let

.

Lemma 4.4

(Common ancestor)

Let

be disjoint cubes with

If

[

, Lemma 3.7]
.
When

.

For any

.

.

4.3.
P
far from

In this case
\operatorname{d}(P,R)>\ell P
and
\ell P=\max(\ell P,\ell R)
, so the hypotheses of
Lemma

4.4
are satisfied.
Let
K
be the common ancestor of
P
and

given by
Lemma

4.4
.
Since
\ell P\geq 2^{r+1}\ell R
, let
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K
and
\ell R=2^{-i-j}\ell K

for some

, with

. We have

\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{R}g\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{far}}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{\operatorname{d}(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\right)=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\,:\,R\subset K\\
\ell R=2^{-i-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\int_{R}g\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\\
\operatorname{d}(P,R)>\ell P\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{\operatorname{d}(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}.

By using the lower bound
\operatorname{d}(P,R)\gtrsim_{r}(\ell K)^{1-\gamma}(\ell R)^{\gamma}
with

, we estimate

(4.3)

\frac{\sqrt{\ell P\ell R}}{\operatorname{d}(P,R)}\lesssim_{r}\frac{2^{-j-i/2}\ell K}{\ell K2^{-(i+j)\gamma}}\quad\text{ so that }\quad\frac{(\sqrt{\ell P\ell R})^{\alpha}}{\operatorname{d}(P,R)^{\alpha+d}}\lesssim_{r,\alpha,d}\frac{2^{-(j+i/2)\alpha}}{2^{-(i+j)\gamma(\alpha+d)}\lvert K\rvert}=\frac{2^{-(3j+i)\alpha/4}}{\lvert K\rvert}.

For any fixed integer
m
, the set
\{R\subset K\,:\,\ell R=2^{-m}\ell K\}
is a partition of

, so we bound

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{i,j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\,:\,R\subset K\\
\ell R=2^{-i-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\int_{R}g\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\\
\operatorname{d}(P,R)>\ell P\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{\operatorname{d}(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-3j\alpha/4}\sum_{i\geq r+1}2^{-i\alpha/4}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\fint_{K}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}.

We can sum in
i
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-3j\alpha/4}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{d}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-3j\alpha/4}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{3K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset 3K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-3j\alpha/4}B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}(g,f).

A sparse domination of

is proved in
Section

.

4.4.

Recall that

4.5.

In this case

and

. Using the trivial bound

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{R}\lvert g\rvert\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{close}}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{D(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r,\alpha}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{R}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,3P\supset R\\

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{R}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{k=0}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,3P\supset R\\

In this section we prove the following bound

(5.1)

\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert g\rvert\Big\lvert\sum_{P\,:\,P\supset R^{(r)}}\theta_{t}(\Delta_{P}f\mathbbm{1}_{P\setminus P_{R}})\Big\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\lesssim B_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(g,f).

The dyadic form

defined in (

)
is controlled by a sparse form in

.

Remark 5.1
.

The goodness of
R
gives the lower bound on the distance

\operatorname{d}(R,\partial P)>(\ell P)^{1-\gamma}(\ell R)^{\gamma}
.

As will be clear from the proof,
inequality (
5.1
) holds if one replaces
the indicator
\mathbbm{1}_{P\setminus P_{R}}
with
\mathbbm{1}_{K\setminus P_{R}}
where
K
is
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or any other larger cube containing
P
.

To prove (
5.1
), we use
a classical estimate for the Poisson kernel.

Lemma 5.2

(Poisson off-diagonal estimates)
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1],r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma
as in the introduction
and let
Q,P\in\mathcal{D}
such that
Q^{(r)}\subset P
and
Q
is
r
-good.
Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus P}\frac{(\ell Q)^{\beta}}{\operatorname{d}(y,Q)^{\beta+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell Q}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta}

where
\eta=\beta-\gamma(\beta+d)
.

Proof.

Decompose
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus P
in annuli
A_{k}=3^{k+1}P\setminus 3^{k}P
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then on each annulus
\operatorname{d}(y,Q)>\operatorname{d}(\partial(3^{k}P),Q)
.
Since
\ell P>2^{r}\ell Q
, use the goodness of
Q
to obtain the bound.
∎

Proof of (
5.1
).

When
(x,t)\in W_{R}
the size condition (
C1
) and
Lemma

5.2
give

\theta_{t}(\Delta_{P}f\mathbbm{1}_{P\setminus P_{R}})(x)\lesssim\lVert\Delta_{P}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{P\setminus P_{R}}\frac{(\ell R)^{\alpha}}{(\ell R+\operatorname{d}(y,R))^{\alpha+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim\frac{\lvert\langle f,h_{P}\rangle\rvert}{\lvert P\rvert^{1/2}}\left(\frac{\ell R}{\ell P_{R}}\right)^{\eta}

where
\eta=\alpha-\gamma(\alpha+d)>0
.
The sum
\sum_{P\supset R^{(r)}}(\ell R/\ell P_{R})^{\eta}
is a geometric series. An application of Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\iint_{W_{R}}\lvert g\rvert\left\lvert\sum_{P\supset R^{(r)}}\frac{\lvert\langle f,h_{P}\rangle\rvert}{\lvert P\rvert^{1/2}}\left(\frac{\ell R}{\ell P_{R}}\right)^{\eta}\right\rvert^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{P\supset R^{(r)}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\left(\frac{\ell R}{\ell P_{R}}\right)^{\eta}\int_{R}\lvert g(x)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\geq r+1}2^{-i\eta}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\subset P\\
\ell R=2^{-i}\ell P\end{subarray}}\int_{R}\lvert g\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq r+1}2^{-i\eta}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\int_{P}\lvert g\rvert.

We sum in
i
and then we bound by the dyadic form
B_{0}^{\mathcal{D}}(g,f)
.
∎

6.2.
Reduction to a telescoping sum

We follow the decomposition in
[

,
MM14
]
where the sum
\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}
is decomposed in a telescopic sum plus off-diagonal terms.
The off-diagonal terms are then bounded by a sum of the dyadic forms
B^{\mathcal{D}}_{j}(g,f)

or directly by a sparse form.

Given
S\in\mathscr{S}
such that
S\supset P_{R}
,
the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}
can be written as
\mathbbm{1}_{S}-\mathbbm{1}_{S\setminus P_{R}}
.
Recall that
\widehat{P_{R}}
is the minimal stopping cube containing
P_{R}
. Then

(6.2)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}=

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}-\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}\setminus P_{R}}

if
P_{R}\not\in\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}=

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}=\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}=

\displaystyle\phantom{\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}}=(\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P})+\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}\setminus\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P}

if
P_{R}\in\mathscr{S}
.

The term
\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}\setminus P_{R}}
is supported away from
R
,
so one can use off-diagonal estimates as in (
5.1
).
Also notice that in the bound (
5.1
) and in its proof
one can replace
\lvert g\rvert
by
\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{R}
.
In the same way, off-diagonal estimates are used for
\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}\setminus\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P}

as shown in
Lemma

6.4
below.

The terms
\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P}

left from (
6.2
) and (
6.3
)
are rearranged to obtain a telescopic series. We have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}=\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P}\qquad\text{ when }P_{R}\not\in\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P}\qquad\text{ when }P_{R}\in\mathscr{S}.

If
P_{R}\not\in\mathscr{S}
then

P
and
P_{R}
are contained in the same minimal stopping cube
\widehat{P}
.
So
\widehat{P_{R}}=\widehat{P}

and the two cases add up to
2(\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P})

which leads to the telescopic sum

(6.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{D}\\
R^{(r)}\subset P\subseteq Q_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P}=\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{R^{(r)}}}\langle f\rangle_{R^{(r)}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Q_{0}}}\langle f\rangle_{Q_{0}}.

Since
f
is supported on a fixed
Q_{0}
, the average on larger cubes
Q^{(n)}_{0}
containing
Q_{0}
decreases:

\langle f\rangle_{Q^{(n)}_{0}}=\frac{1}{\lvert Q^{(n)}_{0}\rvert}\int_{Q_{0}}f\leq\frac{1}{\lvert Q^{(n)}_{0}\rvert}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}}\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Thus when the sum in (
6.4
) extends to all

,
the term
\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{R^{(r)}}}\langle f\rangle_{R^{(r)}}
is the only one remaining.

We have then identified three terms

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{D}\\
P\supset R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{P_{R}}=\sum_{\text{telescopic}}-\sum_{\text{far}}+\sum_{\text{sparse}}

where

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{far}}\coloneqq\sum_{P\,:\,P\supset R^{(r)}}\langle\Delta_{P}f\rangle_{P_{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}\setminus P_{R}}\,,\qquad\sum_{\text{sparse}}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\supset R^{(r)}\\
P_{R}\in\mathscr{S}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}\setminus P_{R}}\langle f\rangle_{P}

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\text{telescopic}}\coloneqq\sum_{P\supset R^{(r)}}2(\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P_{R}}}\langle f\rangle_{P_{R}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{P}}\langle f\rangle_{P})=\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{R^{(r)}}}\langle f\rangle_{R^{(r)}}.

Since the case with

is done in (
5.1
),
we show how to deal with the remaining two cases.

\lvert x-x_{S_{r}}\rvert\leq\ell S_{r}/2<\ell R/2<t
, so
by conditions (
C2
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{S_{r}}^{-}f(x)\cdot\mathcal{K}_{S_{r}}^{+}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\frac{\lvert x-x_{S_{r}}\rvert^{\alpha}}{(t+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{y}\cdot\int\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(t+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=\left(\int\frac{(t\lvert x-x_{S_{r}}\rvert)^{\alpha/2}}{(t+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{y}\right)^{2}\eqqcolon\big(K_{t}^{S_{r}}f(x)\big)^{2}.

∎

The operator
K_{t}^{S_{r}}
satisfies Poisson-like off-diagonal estimates.

Lemma 7.4

(Estimates for
K_{t}^{S_{r}}
)
.

Let
x\in S_{r}\subset R
and
t\in(\ell R/2,\ell R)
. Let
Q\in\mathcal{D}
such that
Q\supset S_{r}
.
Then there exists
\eta>0
such that the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}(x)\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\max(\ell R^{(r)},\ell Q)}\right)^{\eta},

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)\lesssim\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{\lvert R\rvert}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2}.

Remark 7.5
.

Notice that the first estimate is better than the one in
Lemma

5.2

on smaller scale (when
\ell Q<\ell R^{(r)}
).
For the second one,
since
\ell S_{r}<\ell R
, we can also estimate

K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)\lesssim\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{\lvert R\rvert}

provided that
x\in S_{r}
and
t\in(\ell R/2,\ell R)
.

Proof of
Lemma

7.4
.

For the second estimate, by forgetting the distance in the denominator, we simply have

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{Q})(x)\lesssim\int_{Q}\frac{(\ell S_{r}\ell R)^{\alpha/2}}{(\ell R+\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r}))^{\alpha+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lvert Q\rvert}{\lvert R\rvert}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2}.

For the first estimate, when
Q\supset R^{(r)}

use
(a+b)^{\alpha}=(a+b)^{2\alpha/2}\geq(2ab)^{\alpha/2}
in order to apply off-diagonal estimates.
For
x\in S_{r}
and
t\in(\ell R/2,\ell R)
we bound

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}\frac{(\ell S_{r}\ell R)^{\alpha/2}}{(\ell R+\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r}))^{\alpha+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}\frac{(\ell S_{r}\ell R)^{\alpha/2}}{(\ell R\cdot\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r}))^{\alpha/2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{y}}{\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r})^{d}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}\frac{(\ell S_{r})^{\alpha/2}}{\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r})^{\alpha/2+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}.

Then apply
Lemma

5.2
with
\beta=\alpha/2

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}\frac{(\ell S_{r})^{\alpha/2}}{\operatorname{d}(y,S_{r})^{\alpha/2+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell Q}\right)^{\eta}.

When
S_{r}\subset Q\subset R^{(r)}
, split
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}
as
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus R^{(r)}}+\mathbbm{1}_{R^{(r)}\setminus Q}
.
Estimate
K_{t}^{S_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus R^{(r)}})
as in (
7.4
). Then applying
Lemma

5.2
with
\beta=\alpha/2
gives

K_{t}^{S_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus R^{(r)}})(x)\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}

where
\eta
is positive and equals
\frac{\alpha}{2}-\gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2}+d)<\frac{\alpha}{2}
.
For
K_{t}^{S_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{R^{(r)}\setminus Q})
we bound

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{R^{(r)}\setminus Q})(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{R^{(r)}}\frac{(\ell S_{r}\ell R)^{\alpha/2}}{(\ell R)^{\alpha/2}(\ell R)^{\alpha/2+d}}\,\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lvert R^{(r)}\rvert}{\lvert R\rvert}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2}\lesssim_{r,d}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2}=2^{r\alpha/2}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\alpha/2}.

Adding the two bounds gives

\displaystyle K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Q}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}+\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\leq 2\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}

since
\ell S_{r}<\ell R^{(r)}
and
\min(\eta,\alpha/2)=\eta
.
∎

7.2.

Then we bound

.

Lemma 7.6
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}

\displaystyle\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\iint_{W_{R}}\langle f\rangle_{S}^{2}\left(K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{S}\right)^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\lvert\EuScript{P}_{S_{r}}g\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{x}\lesssim\Lambda_{\mathscr{S}}(f,g)

\displaystyle\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\langle f\rangle_{S}^{2}\sum_{R:R\supset S}\iint_{W_{R}}\left(K_{t}^{S_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{S}(x)\right)^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\lvert\EuScript{P}_{S_{r}}g(x)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{x}

∎

7.4.
Subscale term

Lemma 7.7
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\iint_{W_{R}}\Big(K_{t}^{S_{r}}\sum_{\mathrm{subscale}}\Big)^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\lvert\EuScript{P}_{S_{r}}g\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{x}\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-j\alpha/4}B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}(g,f).

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\sum_{R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:P\supset S\\

4.3
where

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\sum_{R\supset S}\iint_{W_{R}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}F_{P}\left(\frac{\ell S_{r}}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\Bigg)^{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{t}}{t}\lvert\EuScript{P}_{S_{r}}g\rvert\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\sum_{S_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S)}\lVert\EuScript{P}_{S_{r}}g\rVert_{L^{1}}\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}F_{P}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta/2}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta/2}\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}F_{P}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta/2}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta/2}\Bigg)^{2}.

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}

\displaystyle\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}F_{P}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta/2}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta/2}\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}F_{P}^{2}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:P\supset S\\
P\subseteq R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}

The last sum is finite:
since
P\supset S
there is only one ancestor for each generation.
Since all terms are non-negative, we bound by removing the restriction
P\subset R^{(r)}
in the sum in
P
.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{R\,:\,R\supset S}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell R^{(r)}}\right)^{\eta}\sum_{P:P\supset S}F_{P}^{2}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\mathscr{S}}\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{P:P\supset S}F_{P}^{2}\left(\frac{\ell S}{\ell P}\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}F_{P}^{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-i\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathscr{S}\\
S\subset P\\
\ell S=2^{-i}\ell P\end{subarray}}\int_{S}\lvert g\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}F_{P}^{2}\int_{P}\lvert g\rvert\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-i\eta}.

The two cases for
F_{P}
are as at the end of the proof of

.
∎

8.
Sparse domination of the dyadic form

In this section we prove a sparse domination of the dyadic form

defined in (

).

Writing

as
\langle\mathbbm{1}_{P}\rangle_{P}
we have

B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}(g,f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{3K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{D}\\

\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}.

Let

be a dyadic cube containing the support of

On the complement of

Lemma 8.1
.

Let

be the restriction of

to the complement

B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{Q_{0}^{\complement}}(g,f)\lesssim_{d}2^{-jd}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{Q_{0}}\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{Q_{0}}^{2}\lvert Q_{0}\rvert.

Proof.

Decompose
(Q_{0})^{\complement}
in the union of
Q_{0}^{(k+1)}\setminus Q_{0}^{(k)}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
The non-zero terms in
B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{Q_{0}^{\complement}}(g,f)
are the ones where
P
intersects
Q_{0}
and
(Q_{0}^{(k)})^{\complement}
.
Then
P\supset Q_{0}^{(k)}
and in particular
P=Q_{0}^{(m)}
for

.
There is only one ancestor for each

\displaystyle B_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{Q_{0}^{(k+1)}\setminus Q_{0}^{(k)}}(g,f)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{0}^{(k+1)}\setminus Q_{0}^{(k)}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{3K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset 3K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\\

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m=k+1}^{\infty}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{3Q_{0}^{(m+j)}}\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{Q_{0}^{(m)}}^{2}\lvert Q_{0}^{(m)}\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k+1}^{\infty}3^{-d}2^{-(m+j)d}\langle\lvert g\rvert\rangle_{Q_{0}}2^{-2md}\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{Q_{0}}^{2}2^{md}\lvert Q_{0}\rvert

The last sum is bounded by

and summing over

It’s enough to construct a sparse family inside

.
Taking the supremum of

over all

we have

(8.1)

\ell P=2^{-j}\ell R\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x).

As we see below,

is pointwise
controlled by the square function
S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}f(x)
given by

\big(S^{\mathcal{D}}_{j}f(x)\big)^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{D}\\
P\subset Q\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell Q\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert P\rvert}\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x).

Proposition 8.2

(Pointwise control)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For all

it holds that

S^{\mathcal{D}}_{j}f(x)\leq S_{j}^{3\mathcal{D}}f(x)\leq 3^{d/2}S^{\mathcal{D}}_{j}f(x)

Proof.

The enlarged cube
3R
is the union of
3^{d}
cubes
\{R_{a}\}_{a}
in the same dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}
. So

\displaystyle\left(S_{j}^{3\mathcal{D}}f(x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\subset 3R\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell R\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x)}{\lvert P\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{a=1}^{3^{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\subset R_{a}\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell R_{a}\end{subarray}}\langle f,h_{P}\rangle^{2}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x)}{\lvert P\rvert}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{3^{d}}\left(S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}f(x)\right)^{2}\leq 3^{d}\left(S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}f(x)\right)^{2}.

∎

We show that the square function
S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies a weak
(1,1)
bound.
The proof follows the one for dyadic shifts without separation of scales
[

, Theorem 5.2]

and
[

, Lemma 4.4]
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. There exists
C>0
such that for any

it holds that

\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\lvert\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}f(x)>\lambda\}\rvert\leq C(1+j)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}}.

In particular
\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}\rVert_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
grows at most polynomially in
j
.

Proof.

First,
S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}
is bounded in
L^{2}
with norm independent of
j
.

We want to show that for any
\lambda>0
we have

\lvert\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,S^{\mathcal{D}}_{j}f(x)>\lambda\}\rvert\leq C\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{1}}{\lambda}.

Let
f=g+b
be the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition of
f
at height
\lambda>0
.
Then
\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2^{d}\lambda
and in particular
\lVert g\rVert_{2}^{2}\lesssim\lambda\lVert f\rVert_{1}
, while

b=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{L}}b_{Q}
, where
b_{Q}
is supported on
Q
and
\int b_{Q}=0
.
The cubes
Q
in
\mathcal{L}
are maximal dyadic cubes
such that
\lambda<\langle\lvert f\rvert\rangle_{Q}\leq 2^{d}\lambda
.

Let
E
be the union of the cubes in
\mathcal{L}
.
Then
\lvert E\rvert=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{L}}\lvert Q\rvert\leq\lambda^{-1}\lVert f\rVert_{1}
so
it is enough to estimate the superlevel sets on the complement of

. Using the decomposition of

we have

\displaystyle\lvert\{x\in E^{\complement}\colon S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}f(x)>\lambda\}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\left\{x\colon S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}g(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\{x\in E^{\complement}\colon S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{1}}{\lambda}+\frac{2}{\lambda}\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}.

The last bound follows by using Chebyshev’s inequality for the good part:

\left\lvert\left\{S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}g>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}\right\rvert\leq\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}g\rVert_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\lVert g\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{1}}{\lambda}

and Markov’s inequality for the bad part. The sublinearity of
S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}
and the triangle inequality imply that

\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{L}}\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}.

For each
Q\in\mathcal{L}
, only dyadic cubes

contribute to the norm
\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}
,
since if
K\subseteq Q
, then
K
would be inside
E
.
Thus
K
is an ancestor of
Q
, so
K=Q^{(k)}
for some integer

.
For
k>j
each
j

contains
Q
, and so
\langle b_{Q},h_{P}\rangle
vanishes,
by the zero average of
b_{Q}
. Thus we estimate

\displaystyle\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E^{\complement}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\lvert\langle b_{Q},h_{P}\rangle\rvert\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{P}(x)}{\lvert P\rvert^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\supset Q\\
\ell K=\ell Q^{(k)}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\lvert\langle b_{Q},h_{P}\rangle\rvert\lvert P\rvert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\supset Q\\
\ell K=\ell Q^{(k)}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset K\\
\ell P=2^{-j}\ell K\end{subarray}}\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(P)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\supset Q\\
\ell K=\ell Q^{(k)}\end{subarray}}\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(K)}=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}}.

Since
\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(K)}=\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\lambda\lvert Q\rvert<\int_{Q}\lvert f\rvert
, and
there is only one ancestor of
Q
for each

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j}\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{Q}\lvert f\rvert\leq j\int_{Q}\lvert f\rvert.

Summing over all

gives the bound

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{L}}\lVert S_{j}^{\mathcal{D}}b_{Q}\rVert_{L^{1}(E^{\complement})}\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{L}}j\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(Q)}\leq j\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

The proof adapts the one in

Lemma 8.4

)

Let

Proof of

8.4

containing the union of the supports of

and

By

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,3P\supset R\\
\ell R\leq\ell P\leq\ell R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{D(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert P\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,3P\supset R\\
\ell R\leq\ell P\leq\ell R^{(r)}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert P\rvert}{\lvert R\rvert}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,3P\supset R\\
\ell P=2^{k}\ell R\end{subarray}}2^{kd}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{rd}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\lvert\{P\,:\,3P\supset R,\ell P=2^{k}\ell R\}\rvert\leq 2^{rd}3^{d}(r+1).

Where to estimate the number of
P
we used
Lemma

9.1
.

subscale
,
P\subset 3R
:

The leading term in the long-distance
D(R,P)
is again
\ell R
.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there are
3^{d}2^{kd}
cubes
P
such that
P\subset 3R
and
2^{k}\ell P=\ell R
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{P\,:\,P\subset 3R}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{D(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert P\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\subset 3R}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R}\right)^{\alpha/2+d}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\subset 3R\\
\ell P=2^{-k}\ell R\end{subarray}}2^{-k\frac{\alpha}{2}}2^{-kd}\lesssim_{d}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k\frac{\alpha}{2}}<\infty.

subscale
,
P\not\subset 3R
:

In this case
\operatorname{d}(P,R)>\ell R>\ell P
.
Regroup the
P
according to length and distance:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,P\not\subset 3R\\
\ell P<\ell R\end{subarray}}\frac{(\sqrt{\ell R\ell P})^{\alpha}}{D(R,P)^{\alpha+d}}\lvert P\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\,:\,2^{k}\ell P=\ell R\\

2^{m+1}\geq\operatorname{d}(P,R)/\ell R>2^{m}\end{subarray}}2^{-k(d+\alpha/2)}2^{-md}2^{-m\alpha}\leq\sum_{k,m}2^{-k\alpha/2}2^{-m\alpha}.

This because there are at most

cubes
R
in the range given by the distance,
which means at most
2^{md}\cdot 2^{kd}

P
with

∎

Proof of
Lemma

4.4
(for
\ell P<\ell R
).

Recall that
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Let
K
be the minimal cube
K\supset R
such that
\ell K\geq 2^{r}\ell R
and
\operatorname{d}(P,R)\leq\ell K\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}
.
(The set of such cubes is not empty since
\ell K\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}
equals
\ell P\left(\frac{\ell K}{\ell P}\right)^{1-\gamma}
which goes to infinity as
\ell K\to\infty
.)
First, observe that
P\subset K
. Suppose not, then

\ell K\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}<\ell K\left(\frac{\ell R}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}<\operatorname{d}(R,\partial K)\overset{P\subset K^{\complement}}{\leq}\operatorname{d}(R,P)

which is absurd because of the second condition on
K
.
It remains to show the upper bound for
\ell K
. By minimality of
K
, one of the following conditions holds: either

\frac{\ell K}{2}<2^{r}\ell R\quad\text{ or }\quad\frac{\ell K}{2}\left(\frac{\ell P}{\frac{1}{2}\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}<\operatorname{d}(P,R).

Since by hypothesis
\operatorname{d}(P,R)>(\ell R)^{1-\gamma}(\ell P)^{\gamma}
,
the first implies

\ell K\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}\leq 2^{r}\ell R\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell K}\right)^{\gamma}\leq 2^{r}\ell R\left(\frac{\ell P}{\ell R}\right)^{\gamma}<2^{r}\operatorname{d}(P,R).

The latter gives:
\ell K(\ell P/\ell K)^{\gamma}<2\operatorname{d}(P,R)\leq 2^{r}\operatorname{d}(P,R)
.
∎

Appendix A
Conditional expectation and Haar projections

In this appendix we recall some known bounds for the Haar projection.
These involve conditional expectation and martingales related to the Haar system, see also
[
Gra14
, §6.4]
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be the stopping family defined in
Section

6.1
.
Given
S\in\mathscr{S}
, let
\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
be the maximal stopping cubes inside
S
.
Let
\mathscr{G}_{S}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
.
A function is measurable with respect the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{G}_{S}
if and only if it is constant on any cube in
\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
.

A.1.
Conditional expectation

Denote by
\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|\mathscr{G}_{S}]
the projection on the space of measurable functions with respect to
the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{G}_{S}
.

\mathbb{E}[f|\mathscr{G}_{S}](x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&if $x\in S\setminus\mathcal{A}(S)$\\
\langle f\rangle_{S^{\prime}}&if $x\in S^{\prime}$ for some $S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)$.\end{cases}

For more details about this operator, we refer the reader to
[
Hyt+16
, §2.6]
.
Let
\mathscr{S}
be a stopping family for
f
.
The supremum of
\mathbb{E}[f|\mathscr{G}_{S}]
in
S
is either
f(x)
(if
\mathcal{A}(S)
is empty), or
\langle f\rangle_{S^{\prime}}
for some
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
.
In both cases
\lVert\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}]\rVert_{L^{\infty}(S)}\lesssim_{d}\langle f\rangle_{S}
, since
\langle f\rangle_{S^{\prime}}\leq 2^{d}A\langle f\rangle_{S}

by the stopping conditions.

A.2.
Haar projection

Given
S\in\mathscr{S}
, let
\mathsf{Tree}(S)=\{Q\in\mathcal{D}\,:\,\widehat{Q}=S\}
be the collection of cubes
Q

such that
S
is the minimal stopping cube containing
Q
.

The Haar projection on
S
is given by

\EuScript{P}_{S}f\coloneqq\sum_{I\in\mathsf{Tree}(S)}\Delta_{I}f=\sum_{I\in\mathsf{Tree}(S)}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}\setminus\{0\}^{d}}\langle f,h_{I}^{\epsilon}\rangle h_{I}^{\epsilon}

where
\{h_{I}^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
are the Haar functions on
I
.
Being a sum of Haar functions on cubes in
\mathsf{Tree}(S)
, the Haar projection
\EuScript{P}_{S}f

is constant on any
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
, so it’s measurable on
\mathscr{G}_{S}
.
It also holds that
\EuScript{P}_{S}f=\EuScript{P}_{S}\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}]
.

The Haar projection
\EuScript{P}_{S}f
can be seen as
a martingale transform, and so it satisfies the following

Lemma A.1

(
L^{p}
bound for martingale transform
[
Bur84
]
)
.

For
1<p<\infty
we have

(A.1)

\lVert\EuScript{P}_{S}\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}]\rVert_{p}\leq C_{p}\lVert\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}]\rVert_{p}.

Combining (
A.1
) with the estimate for the supremum of
\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}]
one obtains that

\lVert\EuScript{P}_{S}f\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{p}\langle f\rangle_{S}.

A.3.
Richer
\sigma
–algebras and
r
-Haar projections

The same idea works with slight modifications when
S
is the minimal stopping cube containing the
r
-ancestor of
Q
.
Let
\mathsf{Tree}_{r}(S)
be the collection of cubes
Q
such that
\widehat{Q^{(r)}}=S
.
Define the
r
-Haar projection on
S
as

\EuScript{P}_{S}^{r}f=\sum_{Q\in\mathsf{Tree}_{r}(S)}\Delta_{Q}f.

Remark A.2
.

The projection
\EuScript{P}_{S}^{r}f
is
not
measurable on
\mathscr{G}_{S}
in general,
but it is measurable with respect to the richer
\sigma
-algebra generated by the
r
-grandchildren of
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
, which is

\mathscr{G}_{S}^{r}\coloneqq\sigma\Big(\{(S^{\prime})_{r}\in\mathsf{ch}_{r}(S^{\prime}),S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)\}\Big).

Then
\EuScript{P}_{S}^{r}f=\EuScript{P}_{S}^{r}\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}^{r}]

and we have the following

Lemma A.3
.

Given a function
f
, let
S
be a stopping cube in
\mathscr{S}_{f}
as defined in
Section

6.1
. Then

\lVert\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}^{r}]\rVert_{L^{\infty}(S)}\lesssim_{d,r}\langle f\rangle_{S}.

Proof.

Either
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq A\langle f\rangle_{S}
for all
x\in S
, or there exists
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}(S)
with
x_{0}\in(S^{\prime})_{r}

and
\mathbb{E}[f\mathbbm{1}_{S}|\mathscr{G}_{S}^{r}](x_{0})=\langle f\rangle_{(S^{\prime})_{r}}
.
Let
P
be the dyadic parent of
(S^{\prime})_{r}
. Then
P\in\mathsf{Tree}_{r}(S)
and we have

\langle f\rangle_{(S^{\prime})_{r}}\leq 2^{d}\langle f\rangle_{P}\leq 2^{d}2^{dr}\langle f\rangle_{P^{r}}\leq 2^{d(r+1)}A\langle f\rangle_{S}

where we used the stopping condition in the last inequality.
∎

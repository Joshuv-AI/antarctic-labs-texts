Braided tensor products and polynomial invariants for the quantum queer superalgebra
Zhihua Chang, Yongjie Wang

Abstract
The classical invariant theory for the queer Lie superalgebra 𝔮 n \mathfrak{q}_{n} investigates its invariants in the supersymmetric algebra 𝒰 s , l r , k := Sym ⁡ ( V ⊕ r ⊕ Π ​ ( V ) ⊕ k ⊕ V ∗ ⊕ s ⊕ Π ​ ( V ∗ ) ⊕ l ) , \mathcal{U}_{s,l}^{r,k}:=\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\oplus\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}\oplus\Pi(V^{*})^{\oplus l}\right), where V = ℂ n | n V=\mathbb{C}^{n|n} is the natural supermodule, V ∗ V^{*} is its dual and Π \Pi is the parity reversing functor. This paper aims to construct a quantum analogue ℬ s , l r , k \mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l} of 𝒰 s , l r , k \mathcal{U}_{s,l}^{r,k} and to explore the quantum queer superalgebra U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) -invariants in ℬ s , l r , k \mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l} . The strategy involves braided tensor products of the quantum analogues 𝖠 r , n \mathsf{A}_{r,n} , 𝖠 k , n Π \mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi} of the supersymmetric algebras Sym ⁡ ( V ⊕ r ) \mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\right) , Sym ⁡ ( Π ​ ( V ) ⊕ k ) \mathrm{Sym}\left(\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\right) , and their dual partners 𝖠 ¯ s , n \bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n} , and 𝖠 ¯ l , n Π \bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}^{\Pi} . These braided tensor products are defined using explicit braiding operator due to the absence of a universal R-matrix for U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) . Furthermore, we obtain an isomorphism between the braided tensor product 𝖠 r , n ⊗ 𝖠 k , n \mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n} and 𝖠 r + k , n \mathsf{A}_{r+k,n} , an isomorphism between 𝖠 k , n Π \mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi} and 𝖠 k , n \mathsf{A}_{k,n} , as well as the corresponding isomorphisms for their dual parts. Consequently, the U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) -supermodule superalgebra ℬ s , l r , k \mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l} is identified with ℬ s + l , 0 r + k , 0 \mathcal{B}^{r+k,0}_{s+l,0} . This allows us to obtain a set of generators of U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) -invariants in ℬ s , l r , k \mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l} . MSC(2020): 17B37, 16T20, 20G42. Keywords: Quantum queer superalgebra; Braided tensor product; Invariant theory.

Inspired by Howe’s unified approach
[
13
]
to investigate classical invariant theory, Sergeev obtained polynomial invariants of matrix Lie superalgebras in
[
23
]
. For the universal enveloping superalgebra
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
of type
Q
, its invariants in the supersymmetric algebra

\mathcal{U}^{r,k}_{s,l}:=\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\oplus\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}\oplus\Pi(V^{*})^{\oplus l}\right),

were explicitly described, where
V
is the natural
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule. The
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule
V^{\oplus r}\oplus\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}\oplus\Pi(V^{*})^{\oplus l}
is identified with
\left(\mathbb{C}^{r|k}\otimes V\right)\oplus\left(V^{*}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{s|l}\right)
. It is pointed out in
[
23
]
that
\mathbb{C}^{r|k}\otimes V
can be further identified with
\mathbb{C}^{r+k|r+k}\circledast V
that is one of the two isomorphic irreducible sub-
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{r+k})\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules of
\mathbb{C}^{r+k|r+k}\otimes V
. This leads to a
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{r+k})
-supermodule structure on
\mathbb{C}^{r|k}\otimes V
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Sym}\left(\mathbb{C}^{r+k|r+k}\circledast V\right)
admits a multiplicity-free decomposition as a
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{r+k})\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule by the Howe duality
[
7
]
.
This procedure is also valid for the dual part. Thus the superalgebra
\mathcal{U}^{r,k}_{s,l}
is identified with
\mathcal{U}^{r+k,0}_{s+l,0}
.

The purpose of this paper is to illustrate such an isomorphism in quantum case, and obtain a set of generators of the quantum queer superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariants in a quantum analogue
\mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l}
of the supersymmetric algebra
\mathcal{U}^{r,k}_{s,l}
.

A quantum deformation of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
was constructed in G. Olshanski’s letter
[
21
]
by using Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT) formalism. Since the queer Lie superalgebra does not have an even, non-degenerate, invariant bilinear form, the
r
-matrix
r\in\mathfrak{q}_{n}^{\otimes 2}
does not satisfy the classical Yang-Baxter equation, thus the quantum queer superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
is not a quasi-triangular Hopf superalgebra.
The highest weight representation theory and their crystal basis theory for quantum queer superalgebra were investigated in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The authors established a Howe duality for quantum queer superalgebras by introducing a quantum coordinate superalgebra
[
5
]
, which is isomorphic to a braided supersymmetric algebra. Based on the Howe duality, we established in
[
6
]
a first fundamental theorem (FFT) of invariant theory for
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
acting on a quantum analogue
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
of
\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}\right)
.

Since the quantum queer superalgebra does not possess a universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix, the technique developed in establishing the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for quantum groups
[
14
]
could not be used in the quantum queer superpalgebra setting. Our approach to obtain the invariants for quantum queer superalgebra takes advantage of an explicit braiding operator
\Upsilon
between a quantum coordinate superalgebra and a dual quantum coordinate superalgebra. The braiding operator enables us to define a braided tensor product, or
R
-twisted tensor products over
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
(that is,
\mathcal{B}^{r,0}_{s,0}
). More important, it is compatible with the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action. The definition of the braiding operator is based on the
S
-matrix on the natural representation instead of the universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix. One of the nice aspects of our approach is that all relations could be expressed via matrics, this allows us to avoid a lot of complicated calculations, see
[
6
]
for more details.

In this paper, we continue to utilize this advantage to investigate the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariants in
\mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l}
. We extend the work of
[
23
, Theorem 1.5.1]
to quantum queer superalgebra. On one hand, we explicitly construct a braiding operator
\Theta:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
(resp.
\bar{\Theta}:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\rightarrow\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}
),
and prove that the resulting braided tensor product of
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}
) is isomorphic to
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s+l,n}
). Thus, we obtain that
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
) is isomorphic to the braided tensor product of
r
-copies of the quantum supersymmetric algebra
S_{q}(V)
(resp.
S_{q}(V^{*})
). On the other hand, we define a quantum analogue
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}^{\Pi}
) of the supersymmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(\Pi(V)^{\oplus k})
(resp.
\mathrm{Sym}(\Pi(V^{*})^{\oplus l})
), which is isomorphic to the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}
). This allows us to identify
\mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}^{\Pi}
with
\mathcal{B}^{r+k,0}_{s+l,0}=\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s+l,n}
. Combining with our pervious result
[
6
, Theorem 5.10]

\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariants in
\mathcal{B}^{r,k}_{s,l}
.

Quantum queer superalgebra and their coordinate superalgebra

Let

(i_{A},i_{B})(a\otimes b)=i_{A}(a)i_{B}(b)

Twisted tensor products can be characterized by twisting maps. According to
[
4

\mu_{\sigma}:=(\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{B})(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\sigma\otimes\text{id}_{B}),

(resp.

[

For the quantum group

-matrix as pointed out in

Quantum Coordinate Superalgebras

A quantum coordinate superalgebra

\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
satisfy the relation:

\displaystyle q^{\delta_{p+i,p+j}}t_{p+i,a}t_{p+j,b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(a,b)}t_{p+j,b}t_{p+i,a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi(\delta_{a<b}-\delta_{p+j<p+i})t_{p+j,a}t_{p+i,b}+(-1)^{|b|}\xi\delta_{-a<b}t_{p+j,-a}t_{p+i,-b}.

Hence, there is a well-defined homomorphism of associative superalgebras
\iota_{p}:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}

Note that the

-actions on

\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
are given by (

\iota_{p}

-supermodules.

The injectivity of

\prod\limits_{i=1,\ldots,r,a\in I_{n|n}}t_{ia}^{d_{ia}},\quad d_{ia}\geq 0\text{ if }|a|=\bar{0}\text{ and }d_{ia}\in{0,1}\text{ if }|a|=\bar{1}

form a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-basis of
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
, where the product is taken with respect to the lexicographic order.
∎

is a

-supermodule superalgebra. In terms of generators, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
. Suppose that

\Theta(y\otimes x)=\sum\limits_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i},

where
x_{i}\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
,
y_{i}\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
and the summation is taken over a finite set. Then the following equality holds in
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}

\iota_{0}(y)\iota_{k}(x)=\sum\limits_{i}\iota_{k}(x_{i})\iota_{0}(y_{i}),

where
\iota_{0}:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
and
\iota_{k}:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
are the maps given in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

The superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading by setting
\deg(t_{ia})=1
,
i=1,\ldots,r
and
a\in I_{n|n}
since the defining relation (
3.3
) is homogeneous with respect to this grading. Similarly, there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
with
\deg(t_{jb})=1
,
j=1,\ldots,k
and
b\in I_{n|n}
. The lemma will be proved by induction on
(\deg x,\deg y)
.

It follows from the definition of
\Theta
in Proposition
3.3
that
\deg(x_{i})\leq\deg(x)
and
\deg(y_{i})\leq\deg(y)
.

The statement is true when
\deg(x)=0
or
\deg(y)=0
since

\Theta(y\otimes 1)=1\otimes y,\text{ and }\Theta(1\otimes x)=x\otimes 1.

If
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1
, we set
x=t_{ia}
and
y=t_{jb}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, and
a,b\in I_{n|n}
. We deduce from (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\Theta(t_{jb}\otimes t_{ia})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(b,a)}t_{ia}\otimes t_{jb}+\delta_{b<a}\xi t_{ib}\otimes t_{ja}+\delta_{-b<a}(-1)^{|a|}\xi t_{i,-b}\otimes t_{j,-a}.

In
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
, the defining relation (
3.3
) yields that

\displaystyle\iota_{0}(t_{j,b})\iota_{k}(t_{i,a})=

\displaystyle t_{j,b}t_{k+i,a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(b,a)}t_{k+i,a}t_{j,b}+\xi(\delta_{b<a}-\delta_{k+i<j})t_{k+i,b}t_{j,a}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|a|}\xi(\delta_{-b<a}-\delta_{k+i<-j})t_{k+i,-b}t_{j,-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(b,a)}\iota_{k}(t_{i,a})\iota_{0}(t_{j,b})+\xi\delta_{b<a}\iota_{k}(t_{i,b})\iota_{0}(t_{j,a})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{-b<a}\iota_{k}(t_{i,-b})\iota_{0}(t_{j,-a}).

This shows that the statement is also true when
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1
.

Now, we assume that the lemma is true for all
x,y
with
\deg(x)\leq m
and
\deg(y)\leq m^{\prime}
. Note that an element in
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
of degree
m+1
can be written as a linear combination of
xx^{\prime}
with
\deg(x)\leq m,\deg(x^{\prime})\leq m
, we consider such an element
xx^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
with
\deg(y)\leq m^{\prime}
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\Theta(y\otimes x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i},\text{ and }\Theta(y_{i}\otimes x^{\prime})=\sum_{j}x_{ij}^{\prime}\otimes y_{ij},\text{ for each }j,

where
x_{i},x_{ij}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y_{i},y_{ij}\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
. Since
\deg(x)\leq m
and
\deg y\leq m^{\prime}
, the hypothesis implies that

\displaystyle\iota_{0}(y)\iota_{k}(x)=\sum_{i}\iota_{k}(x_{i})\iota_{0}(y_{i}),\text{ and }\iota_{0}(y_{i})\iota_{k}(x^{\prime})=\sum_{j}\iota_{k}(x_{ij}^{\prime})\iota_{0}(y_{ij})\text{ for each }j.

(3.9)

By the commutative diagram (
2.4
) for
\Theta
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta(y\otimes xx^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\Theta\circ(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})(y\otimes x\otimes x^{\prime})=(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\circ(1\otimes\Theta)\circ(\Theta\otimes 1)(y\otimes x\otimes x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\circ(1\otimes\Theta)(x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes x^{\prime})=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}x_{ij}^{\prime}\otimes y_{ij}.

Note that both
\iota_{0}
and
\iota_{k}
are homomorphisms of associative superalgebras, we verify by using (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\iota_{k}(x_{i}x_{ij}^{\prime})\iota_{0}(y_{ij})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\iota_{k}(x_{i})\iota_{k}(x_{ij}^{\prime})\iota_{0}(y_{ij})=\sum_{i}\iota_{k}(x_{i})\iota_{0}(y_{i})\iota_{k}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{0}(y)\iota_{k}(x)\iota_{k}(x^{\prime})=\iota_{0}(y)\iota_{k}(xx^{\prime}).

This shows that the lemma is also true for
xx^{\prime}
and
y
.

Similarly, we show that the lemma is true for
x
and
yy^{\prime}
for
x\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
provided that
\deg(x)\leq m
and
\deg(y),\deg(y^{\prime})\leq m^{\prime}
. Then the lemma is true for all
x\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
by induction.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map

\sigma:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n},\quad x\otimes y\mapsto\iota_{k}(x)\iota_{0}(y),\quad x\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n},\quad y\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}

is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebras, where
\iota_{k}:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
and
\iota_{0}:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
are the maps given in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\sigma
is a homomorphism of associative superalgebras. By Lemma
3.2
,
\iota_{k}:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
and
\iota_{0}:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
are homomorphisms of associative superalgebras. For
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma\left((x^{\prime}\otimes 1)(x\otimes y)\right)=

\displaystyle\sigma(x^{\prime}x\otimes y)=\iota_{0}(x^{\prime}x)\iota_{k}(y)=\iota_{0}(x^{\prime})\iota_{0}(x)\iota_{r}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(x^{\prime}\otimes 1)\sigma(x\otimes y).

\displaystyle\sigma\left((x\otimes y)(1\otimes y^{\prime})\right)=

\displaystyle\sigma(x\otimes yy^{\prime})=\iota_{0}(x)\iota_{r}(yy^{\prime})=\iota_{0}(x)\iota_{r}(y)\iota_{r}(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(x\otimes y)\sigma(1\otimes y^{\prime}).

Note that
(1\otimes y)(x\otimes 1)=\Theta(y\otimes x)
in the braided tensor product
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
. We assume that

\Theta(y\otimes x)=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i},

where
x_{i}\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
y_{i}\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
. It follows from Lemma
3.4
that

\sigma((1\otimes y)(x\otimes 1))=\sum_{i}\iota_{k}(x_{i})\iota_{0}(y_{i})=\iota_{0}(y)\iota_{k}(x)=\sigma(1\otimes y)\sigma(x\otimes 1).

Hence,
\sigma
is a homomorphism of associative superalgebras.

Since
\iota_{k}:\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
and
\iota_{0}:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
are both homomorphisms of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules and
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra, we have

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\Phi_{u}(x\otimes y)\right)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}(-1)^{|u_{(2)}||x|}\sigma\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}(x)\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}(-1)^{|u_{(2)}||x|}\iota_{k}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}(x)\right)\iota_{0}\left(\Phi_{u_{(2)}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}(-1)^{|u_{(2)}||x|}\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\left(\iota_{k}(x)\right)\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\left(\iota_{0}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}\left(\iota_{k}(x)\iota_{0}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(\sigma(x\otimes y)).

This shows that
\sigma
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules.

The homomorphism
\sigma
is surjective since
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
is generated by
t_{ia}
for
i=1,\ldots,r+k,a\in I_{n|n}
. While the injectivity of
\sigma
follows from the fact that a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-basis of
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
(resp.
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
) is given by monomials

\prod\limits_{i=1,\ldots,r,a\in I_{n|n}}t_{ia}^{d_{ia}},\quad\left(\text{resp}.\prod\limits_{i=1,\ldots,k,a\in I_{n|n}}t_{ia}^{d_{ia}}\right)

where
d_{ia}\geq 0
if
|a|=\bar{0}
and
d_{ia}\in{0,1}
if
|a|=\bar{1}
, the product is taken with respect to the lexicographic order (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.6.4]
).
∎

Given two quantum coordinate superalgebras
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
, the braided tensor product
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
is also a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra, which is isomorphic to
\mathsf{A}_{r+k,n}
by Theorem
3.5
. Hence, a further braided tensor product
\left(\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\right)\otimes\mathsf{A}_{p,n}
is permitted. It is also a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra isomorphic to
\mathsf{A}_{r+k+p,n}
. Consequently, the braided tensor product of quantum coordinate superalgebras of type
Q
is associative.

Corollary 3.6
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebras

\left(\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\right)\otimes\mathsf{A}_{p,n}\cong\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\left(\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{p,n}\right).

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

The braided tensor product
\mathsf{A}_{1,n}^{\otimes r}
of
r
-copies of
\mathsf{A}_{1,n}
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
as
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebras.∎

Remark 3.8
.

The quantum coordinate superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{1,n}
is isomorphic to quantum supersymmetric algebra
\mathrm{S}_{q}(V)
, that is a unital associative superalgebra presented by generators
v_{a}
for
a\in I_{n|n}
, and relation:

\displaystyle qv_{a}v_{b}=q^{\varphi(a,b)}(-1)^{|a||b|}v_{b}v_{a}+\delta_{a<b}\xi v_{a}v_{b}+\delta_{-a<b}\xi(-1)^{|b|}v_{-a}v_{-b},\quad a,b\in I_{n|n}.

By
[
3
, Proposition 3.6.4]
, there is a parity preserving vector superspace isomorphism between
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\mathrm{S}_{q}(V)^{\otimes r}
. According to Corollary
3.7
,
\mathrm{S}_{q}(V)^{\otimes r}
is understood as an associative superalgebra obtained by the braided tensor product (see
[
1
]
for the braided symmetric algebra in the case of a quantum group). It is isomorphic to
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
as
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebras. Consequently, the quantum coordinate superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
(or
\mathrm{S}_{q}(V)^{\otimes r}
) is a flat deformation of supersymmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\right)
.

Next, we construct a quantum analogue of
\mathrm{Sym}\left(\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\right)
, where
\Pi
is the parity reversing functor. For any
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule
M=M_{\bar{0}}\oplus M_{\bar{1}}
, define

\Pi(M)=\Pi(M)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\Pi(M)_{\bar{1}},\quad\Pi(M)_{i}=M_{i+\bar{1}},~\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Then
\Pi(M)
is also a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule with the same action.

Definition 3.9
.

We define
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
to be the unital associative superalgebra presented by the generators
t_{ia}^{\pi}
of parity
|a|+\bar{1}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and
a\in I_{n|n}
and the defining relation

R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}=\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12},

(3.10)

where
\check{T}_{+}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\sum\limits_{a\in I_{n|n}}E_{ia}\otimes t_{ia}^{\pi}
, the tensor matrix
R_{+}
is given in (
3.4
),

\displaystyle S_{J}=

\displaystyle(1\otimes J)S(1\otimes J)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{a,b\in I_{n|n}}q^{-\varphi(a,b)}E_{aa}\otimes E_{bb}+\xi\sum\limits_{b<a}(-1)^{|a|}(E_{ba}+E_{-b,-a})\otimes E_{ab},

and
J=\sum\limits_{a\in I_{n|n}}(-1)^{|a|}E_{-a,a}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra with the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action
\Phi^{\pi}
determined by

L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}=\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}.

(3.12)

Proof.

Let
W\subset\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
be the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-subspace spanned by
t_{ia}^{\pi}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
and
a\in I_{n|n}
. We first show that (
3.12
) defines a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule structure on
W
. Note that
L
is the generator matrix of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
, we verify that

\displaystyle\left(L^{[2]3}L^{[2]4}S^{34}\right)\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}=

\displaystyle L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(L^{[2]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}\right)S^{34}=L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{14}S^{34}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}S^{14}S^{34},

\displaystyle\left(S^{34}L^{[2]4}L^{[2]3}\right)\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}=

\displaystyle S^{34}L^{2[4]}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}\right)=S^{34}L^{2[4]}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{34}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{14}S^{13}=\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{34}S^{14}S^{13}.

Since
S
satisfies the quantum Yang-Baxter equation, (
3.12
) determines a well-defined
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action on
W
.

The
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action is extended to the free associative superalgebra generated by
t_{ia}^{\pi}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
and
a\in I_{n|n}
according to the definition of a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra:

\Phi^{\pi}_{u}(xy)=\sum\limits_{(u)}(-1)^{|u_{(2)}||x|}\Phi^{\pi}_{u_{(1)}}(x)\Phi^{\pi}_{u_{(2)}}(y),\quad u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}),x,y\in\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\pi}.

(3.13)

It suffices to show that the action
\Phi^{\pi}_{L_{ab}}
preserves the defining relation (
3.10
) of
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
for
a,b\in I_{n|n}
.

By (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=\left(L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\right)\left(L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S^{14}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}S^{24},

\displaystyle L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\right)=\left(L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)\left(L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\right)=\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}S^{24}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S^{14}.

Then the defining relation (
3.10
) implies that

\displaystyle L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=

\displaystyle R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S^{14}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}S^{24}=\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12}S^{14}S^{24},

\displaystyle L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12}\right)=

\displaystyle\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S^{24}S^{14}S_{J}^{12}.

and

\displaystyle S_{J}^{12}S^{14}S^{24}=

\displaystyle J^{\underline{2}}S^{12}J^{\underline{2}}S^{14}S^{24}=J^{\underline{2}}S^{12}S^{14}S^{24}J^{\underline{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{\underline{2}}S^{24}S^{14}S^{12}J^{\underline{2}}=S^{24}S^{14}J^{\underline{2}}S^{12}J^{\underline{2}}=S^{24}S^{14}S_{J}^{12},

L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=L^{[3]4}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\left(\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12}\right).

3.12
) determines a well-defined
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action on

, under which

is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra.
∎

The defining relation (
3.10
) for
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
can be written in terms of generators as:

\displaystyle q^{\delta_{ij}}t^{\pi}_{ia}t^{\pi}_{jb}-(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{-\varphi(a,b)}t^{\pi}_{jb}t^{\pi}_{ia}=\xi\left(\delta_{b<a}-\delta_{j<i}\right)t^{\pi}_{ja}t^{\pi}_{ib}+(-1)^{|b|}\xi\delta_{b<-a}t^{\pi}_{j,-a}t^{\pi}_{i,-b}.

One observes that the classical limit of
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
as
q\mapsto 1
is isomorphic to the associative superalgebra
\mathrm{Sym}\left(\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\right)
. Moreover, it follows from (
3.12
) that, for each fixed
i=1,\ldots,k
, the
\mathbb{C}(q)
sub-superspace of
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
spanned by

with

is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-sub-supermodule that is isomorphic to

. Consequently,
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
is a quantum analogue of
\mathrm{Sym}\left(\Pi(V)^{\oplus k}\right)
.

It has been shown in
[

, Remark 3.2]
that the
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus k}\right)
. Such an isomorphism is also valid in the quantum case.

Proposition 3.11
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebras

\tau:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{k,n},

such that

\tau\left(\check{T}_{+}\right)=T_{+}(J\otimes 1).

(3.14)

Proof.

We first show that (
3.14
) determines a well-defined homomorphism of associative superalgebras
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
. Since
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
is generated by entries of the generator matrix
\check{T}_{+}
, it suffices to verify that (
3.14
) preserves the defining relation (
3.10
) for
\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
.

\displaystyle\tau\left(R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=

\displaystyle R_{+}^{12}T_{+}^{1[3]}J^{\underline{1}}T_{+}^{2[3]}J^{\underline{2}}=T_{+}^{2[3]}T_{+}^{1[3]}S^{12}J^{\underline{1}}J^{\underline{2}},

\displaystyle\tau\left(\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12}\right)=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}J^{\underline{2}}T_{+}^{1[3]}J^{\underline{1}}S_{J}^{12}=T_{+}^{2[3]}T_{+}^{1[3]}J^{\underline{2}}J^{\underline{1}}S_{J}^{12},

where
J^{\underline{1}}=J\otimes 1\otimes 1
and
J^{\underline{2}}=1\otimes J\otimes 1
.

Since

\displaystyle J^{\underline{2}}J^{\underline{1}}S_{J}^{12}=J^{\underline{2}}J^{\underline{1}}J^{\underline{2}}S^{12}J^{\underline{2}}=-J^{\underline{2}}J^{\underline{2}}S^{12}J^{\underline{1}}J^{\underline{2}}=S^{12}J^{\underline{1}}J^{\underline{2}},

we have

\tau\left(R_{+}^{12}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\right)=\tau\left(\check{T}_{+}^{2[3]}\check{T}_{+}^{1[3]}S_{J}^{12}\right),

and (
3.14
) determines a superalgebra homomorphism
\tau:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{k,n}
.

The superalgebra homomorphism
\tau
is an isomorphism since it has the inverse
\tau^{-1}:\mathsf{A}_{k,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{k,n}^{\Pi}
given by

\tau^{-1}\left(T_{+}\right)=-\check{T}_{+}(J\otimes 1).

Moreover, we compute that

\displaystyle\tau\left(L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}\right)=

\displaystyle\tau\left(\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}\right)=T_{+}^{1[2]}J^{\underline{1}}S^{13},

\displaystyle L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}\tau\left(\check{T}_{+}^{1[2]}\right)=

\displaystyle L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi^{\pi}}{\cdot}T_{+}^{1[2]}J^{\underline{1}}=T_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}J^{\underline{1}},

which shows that
\tau
is also a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules since
J^{\underline{1}}S^{13}=S^{13}J^{\underline{1}}
.
∎

4
Dual Quantum Coordinate Superalgebras

As a quantum analogue of the supersymmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{*\oplus s}\right)
, the
dual quantum coordinate superalgebra
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
was introduced in
[
6
, Section 2]
. This section is devoted to discussing a braided tensor product between two dual quantum coordinate superalgebras
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
and
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{l,n}
.

The dual quantum coordinate superalgebra

\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is a unital associative superalgebra generated by elements
\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
with
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
b\in I_{n|n}
, which satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle\bar{t}_{\alpha,b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\alpha|+|b|}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-b},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{1[3]}\bar{T}^{2[3]}S^{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{12}\bar{T}^{2[3]}\bar{T}^{1[3]},

(4.2)

where
\bar{T}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in I_{s|s},b\in I_{n|n}}(-1)^{|b|(|\alpha|+|b|)}E_{b\alpha}\otimes\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

An alternative presentation of the superalgebra
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is given by generators
\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
with
\alpha=-1,\ldots,-s,b\in I_{n|n}
and the relation

\bar{T}_{-}^{1[3]}\bar{T}_{-}^{2[3]}R_{-}^{12}=S^{12}\bar{T}_{-}^{2[3]}\bar{T}_{-}^{1[3]},

(4.3)

where
\bar{T}_{-}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=-s}^{-1}\sum\limits_{b\in I_{n|n}}E_{b\alpha}\otimes\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
,
and

R_{-}=\sum\limits_{i,j=-s}^{-1}q^{-\delta_{ij}}E_{ii}\otimes E_{jj}-\xi\sum\limits_{-s\leqslant j<i\leqslant-1}E_{ji}\otimes E_{ij}

(4.4)

is the submatix of
S
involving the terms
E_{ik}\otimes E_{jl}
with
-s\leqslant i,j,k,l\leqslant-1
.

Proof.

The relation (
4.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle q^{\varphi(\alpha,\beta)}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{(|\alpha|+|a|)(|\beta|+|b|)}q^{\varphi(a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(\alpha,\beta,b)\xi\left(\delta_{b<a}-\delta_{\alpha<\beta}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+\theta(\alpha,\beta,b)(-1)^{|a|+|\beta|}\xi\left(\delta_{-\alpha<\beta}-\delta_{b<-a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b},

for
\alpha,\beta\in I_{s|s}
and
a,b\in I_{n|n}
. While the relation (
4.3
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{\alpha\beta}}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{(|a|+1)(|b|+1)}q^{\varphi(a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{\alpha<\beta}-\delta_{b<a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}-(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{b<-a}\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b},

for
\alpha,\beta=-1,\ldots,-s
and
a,b\in I_{n|n}
. We verify that (
4.5
) is equivalent to (
4.6
) provided that
\bar{t}_{\alpha,b}=(-1)^{|\alpha|+|b|}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-b}
for
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
b\in I_{n|n}
.

The relation (
4.6
) is the special case of (
4.5
) where
\alpha,\beta=-1,\ldots,-s
.
Conversely, it suffices to show the relation (
4.6
) also implies (
4.5
). We consider the following four cases separately:

Case1:

\alpha<0,\beta<0
. In this situation, the relations (
4.6
) and (
4.5
) are same.

Case2:

\alpha>0,\beta<0
. We deduce from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{-\alpha,\beta}}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|a|}q^{\varphi(-a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{-\alpha<\beta}-\delta_{b<-a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{b<a}\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-b}.

Since
\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-a}=(-1)^{|a|}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{-\alpha,\beta}}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|a|}q^{\varphi(-a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{-\alpha<\beta}-\delta_{b<-a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}+(-1)^{|b|}\xi\delta_{b<a}\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b},

which coincides with (
4.5
) in the situation of
\alpha>0
and
\beta<0
.

Case 3:

\alpha<0
,
\beta>0
. We deduce from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{-\beta,-b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|b|}q^{\varphi(a,-b)}\bar{t}_{-\beta,-b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{\alpha<-\beta}-\delta_{-b<a}\right)\bar{t}_{-\beta,a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}-(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{-b<-a}\bar{t}_{-\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}.

Then
\bar{t}_{-\beta,-b}=(-1)^{|b|}\bar{t}_{\beta b}
implies that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|b|}q^{\varphi(a,-b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{\alpha<-\beta}-\delta_{-b<a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}-(-1)^{|b|}\xi\delta_{a<b}\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}.

Hence,

\displaystyle q^{\varphi(\alpha,\beta)}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|b|}q^{\varphi(a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q^{-\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}}+\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}\xi\right)\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|b|}\left(q^{-\varphi(a,b)}+(-1)^{|b|}\left(\delta_{ab}+\delta_{a,-b}\right)\xi\right)\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{\alpha<-\beta}-\delta_{-b<a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}-(-1)^{|b|}\xi\delta_{a<b}\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}

\displaystyle+\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}\xi\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}\left(\delta_{ab}+\delta_{a,-b}\right)\xi\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{a<b}+\delta_{ab}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{\alpha<-\beta}-\delta_{-b<a}+\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}-\delta_{a,-b}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|b|}\xi\left(\delta_{b<a}-1\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+(-1)^{|a|+|b|}\xi(\delta_{-\alpha<\beta}-\delta_{b<-a})\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b},

which yields with (
4.5
) in the situation of
\alpha<0
and
\beta>0
.

Case 4:

\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
. We deduce from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{\alpha,\beta}}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-a}\bar{t}_{-\beta,-b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(-a,-b)}\bar{t}_{-\beta,-b}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{\beta<\alpha}-\delta_{a<b}\right)\bar{t}_{-\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{-b<a}\bar{t}_{-\beta,a}\bar{t}_{-\alpha,b}.

It follows from
\bar{t}_{-\beta,-a}=(-1)^{|a|}\bar{t}_{\beta a}
that

\displaystyle q^{-\delta_{\alpha\beta}}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(-a,-b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}=\xi\left(\delta_{\beta<\alpha}-\delta_{a<b}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{-b<a}\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}.

Hence,

\displaystyle q^{\varphi(\alpha,\beta)}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}q^{\varphi(a,b)}\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(q^{-\delta_{\alpha\beta}}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\xi\right)\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|}\left(q^{\varphi(-a,-b)}+(-1)^{|b|}\left(\delta_{ab}+\delta_{a,-b}\right)\xi\right)\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{\beta<\alpha}-\delta_{a<b}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\delta_{-b<a}\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}

\displaystyle+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\xi\bar{t}_{\alpha a}\bar{t}_{\beta b}-(-1)^{|a||b|+|b|}\left(\delta_{ab}+\delta_{a,-b}\right)\xi\bar{t}_{\beta b}\bar{t}_{\alpha a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\delta_{\beta<\alpha}-\delta_{a<b}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{ab}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\left(\delta_{-b<a}+\delta_{-b,a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi(\delta_{b<a}-\delta_{\alpha<\beta})\bar{t}_{\beta a}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}+(-1)^{|a|}\xi\left(1-\delta_{b<-a}\right)\bar{t}_{\beta,-a}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-b},

which yields with (
4.5
) in the situation of
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

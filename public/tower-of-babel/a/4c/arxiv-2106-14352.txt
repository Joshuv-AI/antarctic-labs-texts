Instance-optimality in optimal value estimation: Adaptivity via variance-reduced Q Q -learning


Abstract
Various algorithms in reinforcement learning exhibit dramatic variability in their convergence rates and ultimate accuracy as a function of the problem structure. Such instance-specific behavior is not captured by existing global minimax bounds, which are worst-case in nature. We analyze the problem of estimating optimal Q Q -value functions for a discounted Markov decision process with discrete states and actions and identify an instance-dependent functional that controls the difficulty of estimation in the ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} -norm. Using a local minimax framework, we show that this functional arises in lower bounds on the accuracy on any estimation procedure. In the other direction, we establish the sharpness of our lower bounds, up to factors logarithmic in the state and action spaces, by analyzing a variance-reduced version of Q Q -learning. Our theory provides a precise way of distinguishing “easy” problems from “hard” ones in the context of Q Q -learning, as illustrated by an ensemble with a continuum of difficulty.

Introduction

The need for data-driven decision-making has fueled tremendous
interest in Markov decision processes and reinforcement learning (RL).
Indeed, such techniques have found use cases across a wide range of
application domains
[
22
,
10
,
17
]
. An intriguing fact is that in many
applications, RL algorithms behave far better than the theoretical
bounds provided by worst-case analyses would suggest. This gap
provides impetus for a more refined
instance-specific
analysis,
one which highlights the properties of a given instance that render it
“easy” or “difficult.”

Instance-dependent analysis of RL algorithms has become of substantial
interest in recent years
[
18
,
28
,
29
,
12
,
15
,
9
, see, e.g.,]
. By
now, we have a fairly refined understanding of instance-dependence for
policy evaluation problems, including work on temporal difference (TD)
algorithms under the
\ell_{2}
-norm
[
4
,
11
,
6
]
, as well as bounds for
the LSTD estimator in the

\ell_{\infty}
-norm
[
15
]
. A subset
of the current authors
[
9
]
provided a sharper
instance-dependent
\ell_{\infty}
-bounds for a variance-reduced version
of the TD
(0)
algorithm, and showed that this algorithm is optimal in
a local non-asymptotic minimax sense.

For TD and LSTD methods, the underlying structure is linear in
nature—in particular, it corresponds to solving a linear system—a
property which greatly facilitates the analysis. In the current
paper we undertake a similar instance-dependent analysis in the more
challenging setting of
Q
-learning, for which the underlying updates
are non-linear. Our main contributions are to identify a natural
functional of the problem instance and show that it controls the
fundamental difficulty of estimating optimal
Q
-value functions. We
do so by establishing non-asymptotic lower bounds within a local
minimax framework and matching those bounds, up to logarithmic factors, by
analyzing a version of variance-reduced

Q
-learning
[
19
,
20
,
26
]
.

This work is done in the context of Markov decision processes (MDPs) with a
finite set of states
\mathcal{X}
and a finite set of possible actions

\mathcal{U}
. We proceed to provide some background and notation to be
able to introduce the functional that plays a central role in our
analysis, and describe our contributions in more detail.

1.1
Some background

In a Markov decision process, the state
x
evolves dynamically
in time under the influence of the actions. More
precisely, there is a collection of probability transition kernels,

\{\mathbf{P}_{u}(\cdot\mid x)\mid(x,u)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}\}
, where
\mathbf{P}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x)
denotes the transition to the state
x^{\prime}

when the action
u
is taken at the current state
x
. In
addition, an MDP is equipped with a reward function
r
that maps every
state-action pair,
(x,u)
, to a real number

r(x,u)
. The reward
r(x,u)
is
the reward received upon performing an action
u
in the state

x
. Overall, a given MDP is characterized by the problem pair

(\mathbf{P},r)
, along with a discount factor
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

A deterministic policy
\pi
is a mapping
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
: the quantity
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{U}
indicates
the action to be taken in the state
x
. The value of a policy
is defined by the expected sum of discounted rewards in an infinite
sample path. For a given policy
\pi
and discount factor

\gamma\in(0,1)
, the
Q
-function is given by

\displaystyle\theta^{\pi}(x,u)

\displaystyle:\,=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r(x_{k},u_{k})\mid x_{0}=x,\,u_{0}=u\right],\qquad\text{where $u_{k}=\pi(x_{k})$
for all $k\geq 1$.}

(1)

When both the state space
\mathcal{X}
and action space
\mathcal{U}

are finite, the
Q
-function
\theta
can be conveniently represented as
an element of
\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{U}|}
.

There are various observation models in reinforcement learning, and in
this paper we study the
generative setting
in which we have
the ability to draw next-state samples from the MDP when initialized
with an arbitrary state-action pair
(x,u)
. More
precisely, we are given a collection of
N
i.i.d. samples of
the form
\{(\textbf{Z}_{k},R_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
, where both

\textbf{Z}_{k}
and
R_{k}
are random matrices in

\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{U}|}
. For each
state-action pair
(x,u)
, the entry
\textbf{Z}_{k}(x,u)
is drawn according to the transition kernel

\mathbf{P}_{u}(\cdot\mid x)
, whereas the entry

R_{k}(x,u)
is a zero-mean random variable with
mean
r(x,u)
and
\sigma_{r}
-sub-Gaussian tails,
corresponding to a noisy observation of the reward function. Here the
rewards
\{R_{k}(x,u)\}_{(x,u)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}}
are independent across the all
state-action pairs, and the random rewards
\{R_{k}\}
are
independent of the randomness in
\{\textbf{Z}_{k}\}
.

Based on the observations, our goal is to estimate the optimal

Q
-value function
\theta^{\star}
, along with an optimal policy
\pi^{\star}
.
From the classical theory of MDPs
[
14
,
16
,
2
]
, the optimal
Q
-function is a fixed
point of the Bellman (optimality) operator
\mathbf{T}
, a map from

\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{U}|}
to itself given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(\theta)(x,u)

\displaystyle:\,=r(x,u)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x)\max_{u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}}\theta(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),

(2)

and an optimal policy
\pi^{\star}
can be obtained from the optimal

Q
-function
\theta^{\star}
via the maximization
\pi^{\star}(x)\in\arg\max\limits_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)
. In this paper, we measure the quality of a given estimate

\widehat{\theta}
in terms of the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm error:

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}=\max_{(x,u)}|\widehat{\theta}(x,u)-\theta^{\star}(x,u)|.

(3)

1.2
Contributions of this paper

The main contribution of this paper is to show that for a given MDP, the
difficulty of estimating the optimal
Q
-value function in

\ell_{\infty}
-norm is characterized by a particular functional of the
problem instance
(\mathbf{P},r)
, defined here.

An instance-dependent functional:

Given a
sample
(\textbf{Z},R)
from our observation model,
we can define the single-sample empirical Bellman operator

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{T}}(\theta)

\displaystyle:\,=R(x,u)+\gamma\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\textbf{Z}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x)\max_{u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}}\theta(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),

(4)

where we have introduced
\textbf{Z}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x):\,=\mathbbm{1}_{\textbf{Z}(x,u)=x^{\prime}}
.

Note that for any fixed
Q
-function
\theta
, the difference

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}(\theta)-\mathbf{T}(\theta)
is a zero-mean random matrix, and
a key object in this paper is the matrix
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{U}|}
with entries

\displaystyle\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)(x,u)

\displaystyle:\,=\sqrt{\var\Big(\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\big)^{-1}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{T}}(\theta^{\star})-\mathbf{T}(\theta^{\star})\big)\Big)}.

(5)

More explicitly, the quantity
\mathbf{P}^{\pi}
is a
right-linear mapping of
\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{U}|}
to itself, given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\theta(x,u)

\displaystyle:\,=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbf{P}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x)\cdot\theta(x^{\prime},\pi(x^{\prime}))\qquad\mbox{for each $(x,u)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}$,}

(6)

and the square-root and variance operators in equation (
5
)
are applied elementwise.

Let us provide some intuition as to why
\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)
plays a fundamental role. The appearance of
the zero mean term
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}(\theta^{\star})-\mathbf{T}(\theta^{\star})
is natural: it
reflects the noise present in the empirical Bellman
operator (
4
) as an estimate of the population
Bellman operator (
2
). As for the pre-factor

(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{-1}
, by a von Neumann
expansion we can write

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{k}.

The sum of the powers of
\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi}
account for the
compounded effect of an initial perturbation when following the Markov
chain specified by the policy
\pi
.

Upper and lower bounds:

With these definitions in place, the core of our work involves
proving, via a combination of a lower and an upper bound (matching up
to logarithmic factors), that the instance-specific difficulty of
estimating the
Q
-function is captured by the quantity
\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}
. Here
\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}
denotes the set of all
optimal policies for the MDP instance
(\mathbf{P},r)
. This functional
exhibits a wide range of behaviors: in Example
1
to
follow in Section
2.1.2
, we exhibit a family of MDPs

(\mathbf{P}_{\lambda},r_{\lambda})
, parameterized by a scalar

\lambda\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P}_{\lambda},r_{\lambda},\gamma)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\asymp\Big(\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2}-\lambda}.

The setting
\lambda=0
recovers a “hard” instance, one for which
the global minimax bound for estimation of
Q
-functions, known from
past work
[
1
]
on batched
Q
-learning, is sharp.
On the other hand, as
\lambda
grows, the problems in this family
become progressively easier, so that the global minimax bound is no
longer sharp.

In more detail, we prove a non-asymptotic lower bound, stated as
Theorem
1
to follow, by adapting a particular definition
of local minimax risk studied in past work on shape-constrained
estimation
[
5
]
. The central challenge in this
proof is that perturbations to the transition matrices of a given MDP
change not only the transitions themselves, but also the structure of
the optimal policies. In order to prove matching upper bounds, given
the role of the empirical operators
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}
in our lower bound,
which are used in the classical
Q
-learning
algorithm
[
27
,
23
,
21
,
7
]
, a natural thought
would be to analyze this operator directly. However, it is known from past
work
[
25
]
that the classical
Q
-learning
algorithm is
non-optimal
, even when assessed when using the
coarser metric of global minimax. Thus, in order to obtain a sharp
upper bound, we turn to variance-reduced forms of
Q
-learning, as
introduced in past
work
[
19
,
20
,
26
]

and shown to be optimal in a globally minimax sense. Our main
contribution is to show that under certain structural conditions and lower
bounds on the sample size, there is a form of variance-reduced

Q
-learning that achieves our local minimax lower bound up to a
logarithmic factor. These upper bounds, stated precisely in
Theorem
2
, confirm that our lower bound technique has
extracted a useful form of instance dependence for estimating optimal

Q
-functions.

Main results

We proceed to provide precise statements of the main results of
this paper, along with a discussion of some of their consequences. In
Section

, we define a notion of a local non-asymptotic
minimax risk, and then state Theorem

2.1
Instance-dependent lower bounds

In this section, we state a non-asymptotic lower bound for estimating
optimal
Q
-function in the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm. This lower bound, to
be stated in Theorem
1
, is instance-dependent, meaning
that it depends on the particular instance of the MDP
(\mathbf{P},r)
at hand. This dependence should be contrasted with
classical global minimax bounds, which are oblivious to such
local properties.

The starting point of our lower bound development is the two-point
framework introduced by Cai and Low
[
5
]
for
local minimax bounds for nonparametric shape-constrained inference;
here we adapt it to our current setting. Focusing on the

\ell_{\infty}
-norm error metric, the
local non-asymptotic
minimax risk
for
\theta(\mathcal{P})
at an instance

\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},r)
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P}):\,=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{N}}\max_{\mathcal{Q}\in\{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\}}\sqrt{N}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Q}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta(\mathcal{Q})\|_{\infty}\right].

(7)

Here the infimum is taken over all estimators
\hat{\theta}_{N}
that are
measurable functions of the
N
i.i.d. observations drawn
according to our observation model (see Section
1.1
).

The intuition underlying the definition (
7
) is
that given an instance
\mathcal{P}
, the adversary that defines the
instance-dependent non-asymptotic risk

\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P})
behaves as follows: it extracts
the hardest alternative
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
relative to
\mathcal{P}
,
and then measures the worst-case risk over
\mathcal{P}
and this
alternative
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.

2.1.1
Lower bounds for
Q
-function estimation

We now turn to the statement of some lower bounds for estimating the
optimal
Q
-function. Recall the definition (
6
) of
the operator
\mathbf{P}^{\pi}
, along with the functional

\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)
from
equation (
5
). We let
\nu^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)
denote the matrix obtained by taking squares
entrywise. Our first step is to provide a decomposition of this
matrix into two separate components, corresponding to the noisiness in
the reward function observation and transition matrix observations,
respectively.

In order to deal with the latter source of noise, with a slight abuse
of notation, we use the observed matrix
Z
to define a
stochastic analog of
\mathbf{P}^{\pi}
—namely, the (random)
right-linear operator

\displaystyle(\textbf{Z}^{\pi}\theta)(x,u)

\displaystyle:\,=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\textbf{Z}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x)\cdot\theta(x^{\prime},\pi(x^{\prime})),\quad\text{where}\quad\textbf{Z}_{u}(x^{\prime}\mid x):\,=\mathbbm{1}_{\textbf{Z}(x,u)=x^{\prime}}.

(8)

By assumption, the randomness in our observations of the reward and
transitions are independent, so that for any
optimal
1
1

1

Optimality of
\pi
is required so that

\mathbf{T}(\theta^{\star})=r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\theta^{\star}
,
with a similar relation for the empirical Bellman operator.
policy

\pi
, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle\nu^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)(x,u)=\gamma^{2}\rho^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)(x,u)+\sigma^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)(x,u).

(9a)

Here we define

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)

\displaystyle:\,=\var\left((\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{-1}(\textbf{Z}^{\pi}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi})\theta^{\star}\right),\qquad\text{and}

(9b)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)

\displaystyle:\,=\var\left((\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi})^{-1}(R-r)\right),

(9c)

where we compute the variances in an elementwise sense.

With this notation, we have the following guarantee:

Theorem 1
.

There exists a universal constant

such that for any instance

\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},r)
, the local non-asymptotic minimax
risk is lower bounded as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P})\geq c\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}.

(10a)

This bound is valid for all sample sizes
N
that satisfy the
lower bound

\displaystyle N\geq N_{0}:\,=\max\left\{\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}},\,\frac{2\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}\|\rho^{2}(\pi^{\star};\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}}\right\},

(10b)

where
\pi^{\star}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}

We prove this theorem in Section

. The
main take-away is that the functional
\max\limits_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}
controls the local minimax risk. In order to gain

behaviors exhibited by this functional.

2.1.2
Exploring the range of possible behaviors

One point of comparison is between the instance-dependent lower bound
from Theorem
2
with the existing minimax lower bounds for

Q
-learning. Azar et al.
[
1
]
provided a global
minimax lower bound on the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm error for estimating the
optimal
Q
-function. For a
\gamma
-discounted MDP, they
showed that the
\ell_{\infty}
-error of any procedure is lower bounded
by the quantity
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
, up to logarithmic factors in dimension.

This lower bound is optimal in a globally minimax sense, and it is
worthwhile understanding the properties of instances that exhibit this
worst-case behavior—that is, instances for which
\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}\asymp\tfrac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}
. It is
also worthwhile understanding the properties of problems that are much
“easier” than this worst-case theory would suggest. The following
construction, which takes inspiration from
[
15
,
9
]
, allows us
to explore this continuum.

Example 1

(A continuum of local minimax risks)
.

Consider an MDP with two states
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
, two actions

\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, and with transition functions and reward
functions given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{u_{1}}=\begin{bmatrix}p&1-p\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\quad\mathbf{P}_{u_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad\mbox{and}\quad r=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\tau&0\end{bmatrix}.

(11)

We assume that there is no randomness in the rewards. Here, the pair

(p,\tau)
along with the discount factor
\gamma
are
parameters of the construction, and we consider a sub-family of these
parameters indexed by a scalar
\lambda\geq 0
. For any such

\lambda
and discount factor
\gamma\in(\tfrac{1}{4},1)
,
consider the settings

\displaystyle p=\frac{4\gamma-1}{3\gamma},\quad\text{and}\quad\tau=1-(1-\gamma)^{\lambda}.

With these choices, the optimal
Q
-function
\theta^{\star}
takes the form

\displaystyle\theta^{\star}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{3+\tau}{1-\gamma}&\frac{\gamma}{4}\cdot\frac{3+\tau}{1-\gamma}\\
\frac{\tau}{1-\gamma}&\frac{\gamma\tau}{1-\gamma}\end{bmatrix},

with an unique optimal policy
\pi^{\star}(x_{1})=\pi^{\star}(x_{2})=u_{1}
. We can then compute that

\displaystyle\max_{\pi^{\star}\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi^{\star};\mathbf{P}_{\lambda},r_{\lambda})\|_{\infty}=c\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right)^{1.5-\lambda}.

(12)

See Appendix
A
for the details of this
calculation.

Substituting into equation (
10a
) yields
that the local minimax risk is lower bounded as

\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P})\geq c\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5-\lambda}}
.
Consequently, for
\lambda>0
, our lower bounds suggest it should be possible to estimate the
optimal
Q
-function more accurately by a factor
(1-\gamma)^{\lambda}
; note that this difference is particularly
significant for values of the discount factor
\gamma
that are
close to one.

2.2
Instance-dependent upper bounds

Thus far, we have stated some instance-dependent lower bounds on the
sample complexity of estimating
Q
-value functions. As we saw in the
preceding Example
1
, these lower bounds exhibit a wide
range of behavior depending on the structure of the transition
functions, discount parameter and reward functions. However, these
differences in the lower bounds are only interesting if we can show
that they are optimal, meaning that there is a (hopefully practical)
algorithm that matches the behavior predicted by the lower bounds.

In this section we close this gap, in particular via a careful
analysis of variance-reduced
Q
-learning (or
VR-QL
for short).
Variance-reduced forms of
Q
-learning have been proposed and shown to
be globally minimax in previous
work
[
19
,
20
,
26
]
;
the version analyzed here is motivated from
[
26
]
. In
Theorem
2
, we show that the
VR-QL
algorithm is
instance-optimal up to logarithmic factors under two different sets of
assumption.

2.2.1
From standard to variance-reduced
Q
-learning

The classical
Q
-learning algorithm is a stochastic approximation
algorithm for estimating the unique fixed point
\theta^{\star}
of the
Bellman operator
\mathbf{T}
. Recall the
definition (
4
) of the empirical Bellman
operator
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}
. At each iteration
k=1,2,\ldots
, standard

Q
-learning performs an update of the form

\displaystyle\theta_{k+1}=(1-\alpha_{k})\theta_{k}+\alpha_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}(\theta_{k}),

(13)

where
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
is a stepsize parameter. Appropriately
decaying choices of the stepsize ensure that the estimate
\theta_{k}

converges to
\theta^{\star}
. Unfortunately, the convergence rate is known
to be non-optimal, failing to achieve the global minimax
rate
[
25
]
, let alone the finer-grained
instance-dependent requirements in this paper. This non-optimality
has to do with the rate at which variance accumulates as the procedure
is run.

Variance reduction is a general principle that can be applied to
stochastic approximation schemes so as to accelerate their
convergence. Here we describe the variance-reduced version of

Q
-learning that we analyze here. Similar to standard
variance-reduced schemes for stochastic optimization
[
8
, see, e.g.,]
, the algorithm consists of a
sequence of epochs. Within each epoch, we run a re-centered version
of the
QL
update. The re-centering is done in such a way, using a
Monte Carlo approximation of the population Bellman operator
\mathbf{T}
, so
that the re-centered updates have lower variance. We leave the details
of the epochs and Monte Carlo to Section
2.2.4
; here let us
describe the basic form of the updates within a given epoch.

Suppose that we run the algorithm using a total of
M
epochs.
At epoch
m
, the algorithm uses a re-centering point
\overline{\theta}_{m}
in
order to re-center the update, where
\overline{\theta}_{m}
acts as the current
best estimate of
\theta^{\star}
. Ideally, we should re-center the operator

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}
using the quantity
\mathbf{T}(\overline{\theta}_{m})
, but we lack the
access to it; instead, we use the Monte Carlo approximation

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}(\overline{\theta}_{m}):\,=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{m}}\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{i}(\overline{\theta}_{m}).

(14)

Given the pair
(\overline{\theta}_{m},\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}(\overline{\theta}))
and a stepsize
parameter
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we define the
variance-reduced

Q
-learning update
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta&\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{k}\left(\theta;\alpha,\overline{\theta}_{m},\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}\right):\,=(1-\alpha)\theta+\alpha\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}(\theta)-\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}(\overline{\theta}_{m})+\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}(\overline{\theta}_{m})\right\},\end{split}

(15)

where the operator
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}
is independent of the set of operators

\{\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{m}}
, used to compute the Monte
Carlo approximation
\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}
. As a result, the stochastic
operator
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{k}
is independent of the re-centering quantity

\overline{\mathbf{T}}_{N_{m}}(\overline{\theta}_{m})
. See Section
2.2.4
for the
details on how the epoch lengths and re-centering sample sizes

\mathcal{D}_{m}
are chosen.

2.2.2
Non-asymptotic guarantees for variance-reduced
Q
-learning

In this section, we state some non-asymptotic guarantees for
the
VR-QL
algorithm. We provide guarantees under two conditions,
both of which involve the structure of the set of optimal policies.
We begin by introducing some definitions that underlie these two
conditions.

Given an MDP instance
(\mathbf{P},r)
, we define the

optimality gap

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle:\,=\min_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}\setminus\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\theta^{\star}-\{r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\theta^{\star}\}\|_{\infty},

(16)

where
\theta^{\star},\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}
, and
\mathbf{\Pi}
, respectively, denote
the optimal
Q
-function, the set of optimal policies, and the set of
all policies for MDP
(\mathbf{P},r)
. Observe that the scalar
\Delta

captures the difficulty in detecting the set of optimal policies. In
other words, when
\Delta
is small, it is hard to distinguish an
optimal policy from a suboptimal policy.

Our second set of conditions involves the family of right-linear
operators
\{\mathbf{P}^{\pi}\;:\;\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}\}
defined in
equation (
6
). For any
Q
-value function
\theta
, we
say that a policy
\pi
is greedy with respect to
\theta
if

\pi(x)\in\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\theta(x,u)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Note that any policy

\pi^{\star}
that is greedy with respect to the optimal
Q
-value
function
\theta^{\star}
is an optimal policy. We say that the operators
satisfy a
Lipschitz condition
if there is a constant
L

such that for any
Q
-value function
\theta
and associated
greedy-optimal policy
\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{P}^{\pi}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})\;(\theta-\theta^{\star})\|_{\infty}\leq L\|\theta-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(17)

Intuitively, this condition means that the operator difference

\mathbf{P}^{\pi}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}
is small whenever the
underlying
Q
-value functions that induce the policies are close.
Conditions of this type were introduced by Puterman and
Brumelle
[
13
]
in their classical analysis of the convergence rates
of policy iteration algorithms for exact dynamic programming.

With these definitions in place, we can specify the two settings under
which we provide upper bounds on the
VR-QL
algorithm:

Setting UNQ:

There is a unique optimal policy
\pi^{\star}
, and
the sample size
N
is lower bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{N}{(\log N)^{2}}\geq c_{2}\log(D/\delta)\cdot\frac{(1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}\cdot\max\{1,\,\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(1-\gamma)^{\beta}}\}\quad\text{for
some}\;\;\beta>0.

(18)

Setting LIP:

The Lipschitz condition (
17
)
condition holds, and the sample size is lower bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{N}{(\log N)^{2}}\geq c_{2}\log(D/\delta)\frac{(1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{3+\beta}}\cdot\min\{\frac{L^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}},\,\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\}\qquad\mbox{for
some $\beta>0$.}

(19)

In all cases, we assume that we are given an initial point
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{1}

such that

\displaystyle\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{1}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|r\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}.

(20)

Such an initial condition has already been used in the literature
[
26
]
, and it can be ensured by first running Algorithm
VR-QL
for a total of
\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}
samples (up to logarithmic factor corrections).

Theorem 2
.

Under either settings (UNQ) or (LIP), there are choices of epoch
parameters such that given any discount parameter
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and an initial point
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{1}
satisfying the
initialization condition (
20
),
Algorithm
VR-QL
run for
M:\,=\log_{4}\left(\frac{N(1-\gamma)^{2}}{8\log((16D/\delta)\cdot\log N)}\right)
epochs yields an estimate

\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{M+1}
such that

\displaystyle\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{M+1}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)}{N}}\max_{\pi^{\star}\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi^{\star};\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}+c_{1}\cdot\frac{\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)}{N}\cdot\frac{\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{1-\gamma},

(21)

with probability exceeding
1-\delta
.

See Section
4
for the proof of this claim.

Comparing the upper and lower bounds:

Assuming the sample size lower bound from Theorem
1
are valid, we see that the second term in the bound (
21
) is of smaller order. In this case, the upper bound from Theorem
2
and the lower bound from Theorem
1
matches, and we conclude that the
VR-QL
algorithm is
instance optimal
.

Although the guarantee (
21
) involves the
same
1/\sqrt{N}
rate and complexity term
\max_{\pi^{\star}\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi^{\star};\mathbf{P},r,\gamma)\|_{\infty}
as the
lower bound in Theorem
1
, it should be noted that the
sample size lower bounds required for Theorem
2
are
more stringent than that in Theorem
1
. Moreover, our
lower bound does not require the side conditions—either the unique optima
or Lipschitz conditions—that are imposed in Theorem
2
.
Closing these remaining differences between the two results is a worthwhile goal for future work.

In this section, we provide the complete details of the algorithm
used in our version of variance-reduced
Q
-learning.

A single epoch:

A single epoch of the overall variance-reduced

algorithm involves

\mathcal{V}_{k}

from equation (
15
). The epochs are indexed with
integers

corresponds to the total number of epochs to be run. Each epoch

\overline{\theta}

•

a positive integer

denoting the number of steps
within the given epoch;

•

a positive integer

to calculate the Monte Carlo update (
14
);

\mathcal{C}_{m}

N_{m}
and
K
,

N_{m}
, which we
call
\mathcal{D}_{m}
, is used to construct the Monte Carlo
approximation (
14
). The second subset, of size

K
is used to run the
K

Proof of Theorem
1

Given an MDP instance
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},r)
, we start by
introducing the following two classes of alternative MDPs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{P}^{\prime},r^{\prime})\mid r^{\prime}=r\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{P}^{\prime},r^{\prime})\mid\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=\mathbf{P}\}.

(23)

We consider the restricted version of the local minimax risk at the instance
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to the classes
\mathcal{S}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{i}):\,=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{N}}\max_{\mathcal{Q}\in\{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}[\sqrt{N}\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta(\mathcal{Q})\|_{\infty}],\quad i=1,2.

(24)

The main part of the proof involves showing that there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{1})

\displaystyle\geq c\cdot\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\gamma\rho(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty},\quad\text{and}

(25a)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{2})

\displaystyle\geq c\cdot\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\sigma(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty},

(25b)

where
\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}
denotes the optimal policy set for
(\mathbf{P},r)
. We can then conclude

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P})&\geq\max\{\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{1}),\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{2})\}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{1})+\mathfrak{M}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}_{2})\right)\\
&\geq\frac{c}{2}\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\gamma\rho(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}+\frac{c}{2}\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\sigma(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}\\
&\geq\frac{c}{2}\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

The last inequality above follows from the
decomposition (
9a
). It remains to prove

25a
) and (

). More

where we

\displaystyle\underline{\mathfrak{M}}_{N}(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{S}):\,=\sup_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\sqrt{N}\cdot\|\theta(\mathcal{P})-\theta(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\|_{\infty}\mid d_{\text{Hel}}\left(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{N}}\right\}.

\bar{\mathbf{P}}
has the following properties:

(a)

The
\bar{\mathbf{P}}
is a probability transition kernel.

(b)

The MDP instances
\mathcal{P}=(\mathbf{P},r)
and
\mathcal{P}_{1}=(\bar{\mathbf{P}},r)

{|\!|\!|\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}|\!|\!|_{{\infty\rightarrow\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}}
.

(c)

Each entry of
(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}[\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}]\theta
is non-negative.

See Appendix
B.2
for a
proof of this lemma.

Equipped with these tools, we are now ready to lower bound the norm

\|\theta-\bar{\theta}\|_{\infty}
. The optimal
Q
-functions

\theta
and
\bar{\theta}
satisfy the following Bellman equations:

\displaystyle\theta=r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}\theta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{\theta}=r+\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\bar{\pi}}\bar{\theta},

(29)

where
{\pi_{1}}\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}
is the optimal policy that achieves
\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\rho(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}
, and
\bar{\pi}
is an optimal policy for
(\bar{\mathbf{P}},r)
. By the optimality of policy
\bar{\pi}
and the
Q
-function
\bar{\theta}
, we have the entrywise inequality
\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\bar{\pi}}\bar{\theta}\succeq\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}\bar{\theta}
, which implies
(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})\bar{\theta}\succeq(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\bar{\pi}})\bar{\theta}=r
. Thus, using the identity
A_{1}^{-1}-A_{0}^{-1}=A_{1}^{-1}(A_{0}-A_{1})A_{0}^{-1}
for invertible operators
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\theta}-\theta&\succeq\left[(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\right]r\\
&=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\left[(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})\right](\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}r\\
&=\gamma(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\left[\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right](\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}r\\
&\quad+\gamma\left((\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\right)(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}r\\
&=\gamma(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\left[\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}\right]\theta\\
&\quad+\gamma\left((\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}\right)(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta,\end{split}

where the final equation follows from the Bellman optimality condition (
29
). Lemma
3
(c) guarantees that the entries of
(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}[\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}]\theta
are non-negative,
and therefore we conclude

\displaystyle\|\bar{\theta}-\theta\|_{\infty}\geq\gamma\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}[\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}]\theta\|_{\infty}-\gamma\|((\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1})(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}.

(30)

Consider the second term

T_{2}:\,=\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1})(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}
.
We have

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\|[(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})-\mathbf{I}](\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|\!|\!|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})-\mathbf{I}|\!|\!|_{{\infty\rightarrow\infty}}\cdot\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\gamma|\!|\!|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})|\!|\!|_{{\infty\rightarrow\infty}}\cdot\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}|\!|\!|\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}}|\!|\!|_{{\infty\rightarrow\infty}}\cdot\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty},

where the last inequality uses
Lemma
3
(b) and the first part of the
minimum sample size
assumption (
10b
). Combining this result with the bound (
30
) we conclude

\displaystyle\|\bar{\theta}-\theta\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta\|_{\infty}.

With this result in hand, substituting the value of the transition kernel

\bar{\mathbf{P}}
from equation (
28
) and recalling the
definition of state-action pair
z
from equation (
26
) we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\cdot\|\bar{\theta}-\theta\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{\gamma\sqrt{N}}{2}\cdot(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma\sqrt{N}}{2\sqrt{2}}\cdot\sum_{z}\mathbf{U}_{\bar{z},z}\cdot(\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{\pi_{1}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{1}})\theta(z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\geq}}\frac{\gamma}{4\tilde{\rho}(\bar{z})}\sum_{z}(\mathbf{U}_{\bar{z},z})^{2}\varphi^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{=}}\frac{\gamma\tilde{\rho}(\bar{z})}{4}=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\gamma\rho(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty},

where step (i) follows by substituting the value of the transition kernel
\bar{\mathbf{P}}
(cf. Proof of Lemma
3
part (c)), and step (ii) follows by using the expression (

).
This completes the proof of part (a) of Lemma

Borrowing the notation from part (a) of the proof, let

{\pi_{2}}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\sigma(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(\bar{z}):\,=\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\sigma(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}^{2}=\|\sigma({\pi_{2}};\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}^{2},\qquad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{U}:\,=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})^{-1}.

We define our perturbed reward function to be

For
\mathcal{P}_{2}:\,=(\mathbf{P},\bar{r})

\displaystyle d_{\text{Hel}}\left(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{2}\right)^{2}\leq D_{KL}(\mathcal{N}(r,\sigma_{r}^{2}\mathbf{I})\mid\mathcal{N}(\bar{r},\sigma_{r}^{2}\mathbf{I}))=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}\|r-\bar{r}\|_{2}^{2}.

Substituting the value of the reward
\bar{r}
from

\displaystyle d_{\text{Hel}}\left(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{2}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}\|\bar{r}-r\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}(\bar{z})\cdot 4N}\sum_{z}(\mathbf{U}_{\bar{z},z})^{2}\sigma_{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{4N},

where the last equality uses the definition of the term

\sigma^{2}(\bar{z})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(\bar{z})=\sum_{z^{\prime}}(\mathbf{U}_{\bar{z},z^{\prime}})^{2}\sigma_{r}^{2}.

(33)

It remains to prove a lower bound on the

-norm between the

-functions for instances
\mathcal{P}
and

Let

\bar{\theta}:\,=\theta(\mathbf{P},\bar{r})

\mathcal{P}:\,=(\mathbf{P},r)
and

\mathcal{P}_{2}:\,=(\mathbf{P},\bar{r})
, respectively. Note that

\theta
and
\bar{\theta}
satisfy the Bellman equations

\displaystyle\theta=r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}}\theta,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\theta}=\bar{r}+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\bar{\pi}}\bar{\theta},

(34)

where

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})\bar{\theta}\succeq\bar{r}\implies\bar{\theta}\succeq(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})^{-1}\bar{r},

Combining the last inequality with the Bellman equation (

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}-\theta\succeq(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})^{-1}(\bar{r}-r)

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})^{-1}(\bar{r}-r)\|_{\infty}\geq(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi_{2}})^{-1}(\bar{r}-r)(\bar{z})

5
Discussion

In this paper, we presented an analysis of
Q
-learning through the
instance-dependent framework in the synchronous setting. For

\gamma
-discounted MDPs with finite state space
\mathcal{X}
and
action space
\mathcal{U}
, we have proved a local non-asymptotic
lower bound for estimating the
Q
-function dependent on a functional

of the MDP instance

(\mathbf{P},r)
that measures the variance of solving for the

-function. In addition, we have provided an analysis of a form of

-learning and obtained instance-dependent
guarantees on the

-error for sample sizes

\frac{N}{(\log N)^{2}}\geq c\cdot\frac{\log(D/\delta)}{(1-\gamma)^{3}\Delta^{@}}

conjecture that optimality of Algorithm

, and is left for further
endeavours.

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{N}}\max_{\mathcal{Q}\in\{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}\left[\|\theta-\theta(\mathcal{Q})\|_{\infty}\right]\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\right]\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\right]\geq\delta\left[\mathcal{P}^{N}\left(\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}\geq\delta\right)+(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})^{N}\left(\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\geq\delta\right)\right].

\displaystyle\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}<\delta_{01}\implies\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}>\delta_{01},

yielding

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})^{N}}\left[\|\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta\|_{\infty}\right]

where the maximum operator
\max\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is applied
entry-wise. Combining the last two bounds with
Lemma
10
, and using the lower bound assumption
on the epoch sample size
N_{m}
we obtain

\displaystyle|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}|\preceq\max\{|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}|_{+},|\widehat{\theta}-\theta^{\star}|_{+}\}

\displaystyle\preceq|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(r-\widetilde{r}),(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}})^{-1}(r-\widetilde{r})|

\displaystyle\preceq|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(r-\widetilde{r})|,

where the last inequality uses
\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}=\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}
(cf. Lemma
10
). It remains
to prove the bounds (
52a
)
and (
52b
).

Proof of bounds (
52a
) and (
52b
):

By optimality of policies
\widehat{\pi}
and
\pi^{\star}
for
Q
-functions

\widehat{\theta}
and
\theta^{\star}
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\theta^{\star}=r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star}\succeq r+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\widehat{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\theta}=\widetilde{r}+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\widehat{\theta}\succeq\widetilde{r}+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\theta^{\star}.

(53)

This yields:

\displaystyle\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}

\displaystyle=r-\widetilde{r}+\gamma(\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star}-\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\widehat{\theta})\preceq r-\widetilde{r}+\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}(\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}).

(54)

Rearranging the last inequality, and using the non-negativity of the
entries of
(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}
we conclude

\displaystyle(\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta})\preceq(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(r-\widetilde{r}).

This completes the proof of the bound (
52a
). The
proof of bound (
52b
) is similar.

C.2
Proof of Lemma
9

Recall the definition
\widetilde{r}:\,=\widehat{R}+\gamma(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\overline{\pi}}-\mathbf{P}^{\overline{\pi}})\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}
,

where
\overline{\pi}
a policy greedy with respect to
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}
; that is,

\overline{\pi}(x)=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}(x,u)
, where we break ties in the
\arg\max
by choosing the
action
u
with smallest index. We have

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(\widetilde{r}-r)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\left\{(\widehat{R}-r)+\gamma(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\pi^{\star}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})\theta^{\star}\right\}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad+\gamma\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\left\{(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star})-(\mathbf{P}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star})\right\}\|_{\infty}.

Observe that the random variable
\widehat{R}
and
\widehat{\textbf{Z}}
are
averages of
N_{m}
i.i.d. random variables
\{R_{i}\}
and

\{\widehat{\textbf{Z}}_{i}\}
, respectively. Additionally, entrywise, the random
reward is a Gaussian random variable with variance
\sigma_{r}^{2}
,
and by the generative model assumption, the randomness in the random
rewards
\{R_{i}\}
is independent of the randomness in
\{\widehat{\textbf{Z}}_{i}\}
. Consequently, applying Hoeffding’s bound on the term
involving
\{R_{i}\}
, a Bernstein bound on the term
involving
\{\widehat{\textbf{Z}}_{i}\}
and a union bound yields the following
result which holds with probability at least
1-\frac{\delta}{4M}
:

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}\left\{(\widehat{R}-r)+\gamma(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\pi^{\star}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})\theta^{\star}\right\}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{N_{m}}}\cdot\|\nu(\pi^{\star};\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}\cdot\sqrt{\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)}+\frac{4\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{(1-\gamma)N_{m}}\cdot\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{N_{m}}}\cdot\max_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi^{\star}}}\|\nu(\pi;\mathbf{P},r)\|_{\infty}\cdot\sqrt{\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)}+\frac{4\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{(1-\gamma)N_{m}}\cdot\log_{4}(8DM/\delta).

Finally, for each state-action pair
(x,u)
the random
variable
(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star})(x,u)
has expectation
(\mathbf{P}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})(x,u)
with entries uniformly bounded
by
2\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}
. Consequently, by a standard
application of Hoeffding’s inequality combined with the lower bound

N_{m}\geq c_{3}\frac{4^{m}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\cdot\|(\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\widehat{\textbf{Z}}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star})-(\mathbf{P}^{\overline{\pi}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}}{33},

with probability exceeding
1-\frac{\delta}{4M}
. The
statement then follows from combining these two high-probability
statements with a union bound.

C.3
Proof of Lemma
8

By Lemma
6
and Lemma
9
, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(\widetilde{r}-r)\|_{\infty}

Recalling the
bounds (
52a
)–(
52b
), we
have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(\widetilde{r}-r)\|_{\infty}+\gamma\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})(\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}})^{-1}(\widetilde{r}-r)\|_{\infty}+\frac{L\gamma}{1-\gamma}\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}^{2}

where the last inequality uses the Lipschitz
condition (
17
). If we can show
{\frac{L\gamma}{1-\gamma}\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}}
, we are done. In order to do so, we require the
following auxiliary result:

Lemma 11
.

Given a batch size
N_{m}
lower bounded as
N_{m}\geq c_{3}\frac{\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}\cdot\frac{1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{\sqrt{N_{m}}(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\cdot\log_{4}(8DM^{2}/\delta)

with probability at least
1-\frac{\delta}{4M}
.

With the above lemma at hand and using
N_{m}\geq c\log_{4}(8DM/\delta)\frac{L^{2}(1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{5}}
we conclude

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1-\gamma}{2L},

as desired. It remains to prove Lemma
11
.

Proof of Lemma
11
:

This proof exploits the result of Lemma
4
, that with probability at least
1-\frac{\delta}{M^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}}{33}+\frac{1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}}+\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}\cdot\frac{\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{1-\gamma}.

(55)

Following an argument similar to the proof of Theorem
2
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{m+1}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{m}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}}{16}+c\left\{\frac{1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}}+\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}\cdot\frac{\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{1-\gamma}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.23611pt]{2.48053pt}{0.43057pt}}{\theta}_{1}-\theta^{\star}\|_{\infty}}{16^{m}}+2c\left\{\frac{1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}}+\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}\cdot\frac{\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{1-\gamma}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\|r\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\sqrt{N_{m}}}\cdot\frac{1}{4^{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2c\left\{\frac{1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma}}{(1-\gamma)^{1.5}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}}+\frac{\log(8DM^{2}/\delta)}{N_{m}}\cdot\frac{\|\theta^{\star}\|_{\mathrm{span}}}{1-\gamma}\right\},

.

C.4
Proof of Lemma
10

The proof of this lemma exploits the optimality of the policies

\pi^{\star}
and
\widehat{\pi}
with respect to the
Q
-functions
\theta^{\star}
and

\widehat{\theta}
, respectively. Accordingly, we have for all state action pair

(x,u)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\widehat{\theta}(x,u)+\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)-\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}\widehat{\theta}(x,u)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\widehat{\theta}(x,u)-\|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)+\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\widehat{\theta}(x,u)-\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)-\|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}\theta^{\star}(x,u)-2\|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}\|_{\infty}.

(57)

The first inequality follows from the optimality of the policy

\widehat{\pi}
with respect to the
Q
-function
\widehat{\theta}
. This completes the
proof of the first part of the lemma.

Turning to the second part, invoking
Lemma
11
with a batch size
N_{m}\geq\frac{(1+\|r\|_{\infty}+\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}\cdot\frac{\log(DM^{2}/\delta)}{\Delta^{2}}
guarantees
that

\displaystyle 2\|\theta^{\star}-\widehat{\theta}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle<\Delta.

This inequality, combined with the bound (
57
) and the
definition of the optimality gap
\Delta
, implies that
\widehat{\pi}
is an
optimal policy, and hence
\mathbf{P}^{\widehat{\pi}}=\mathbf{P}^{\pi^{\star}}
under the unique policy or Lipschitz assumptions.

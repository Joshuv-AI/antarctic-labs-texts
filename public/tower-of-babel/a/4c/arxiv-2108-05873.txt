Reverse Order Law for Generalized Inverses with Indefinite Hermitian Weights
K. Kamaraj 1 , P. Sam Johnson 2 and Athira Satheesh K 3

Abstract
. In this paper, necessary and sufficient conditions are given for the existence of Moore-Penrose inverse of a product of two matrices in an indefinite inner product space (IIPS) in which reverse order law holds good. Rank equivalence formulas with respect to IIPS are provided and an open problem is given at the end.



1.
Introduction

The reverse order law for generalized inverse plays an important role in the
theoretic research and numerical computations in many areas, including the
singular matrix problems, ill-posed problems, optimization problems, and statistics problems (see, for instance,
[
1
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
9
,
13
,
6
]
). A classical result of Greville
[
12
]
gives necessary and sufficient conditions for the two term reverse order law for the Moore-Penrose inverse in the Euclidean space. It is known that the reverse order law does not hold for various classes of generalized inverses
[
2
,
5
]
. Hence, a significant number of papers treat the sufficient or equivalent conditions such that the reverse order law holds in some sense. Sun and Wei established some sufficient and necessary conditions for inverse order rule for weighted generalized inverses with positive definite weights
[
14
,
15
]
. The concept of the Moore-Penrose inverse between indefinite inner product spaces has been introduced and mentioned in
[
8
]
that if the weights are positive definite, then the weighted generalized inverse and the Moore-Penrose inverse between indefinite inner product spaces are the same. In this paper, we give some necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of Moore-Penrose inverse of a product of two matrices and to hold reverse order law in an IIPS. Also, we claim that our results are more general than the existing ones for weighted Moore-Penrose inverse.

2.
Preliminaries

We consider matrices on the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers and denote the space of complex matrices of order
m\times n
by
\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
. The
range
and the
rank
of
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
are denoted by
R(A)
and
rank(A)
respectively. The
index
of
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is the least positive integer
p
such that
rank
(A^{p})=\emph{rank}(A^{p+1})
and it is denoted by
ind(A)
.

For a complex square matrix
A
, we call it
Hermitian
if
A=A^{*}
, where
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint of
A
with respect to the Hermitian inner product
\langle\centerdot,\centerdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(i.e., complex conjugate transpose). Let
N
be an invertible Hermitian matrix of order
n
. An
indefinite inner product
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is defined by an equation

[x,y]=\langle x,Ny\rangle

where
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Such a matrix
N
is called a
weight
. A space with an indefinite inner product is called an
indefinite inner product space (IIPS)
. Let
M
and
N
be weights of order
m
and
n
, respectively. The
MN
-adjoint
of an
m\times n
matrix
A
denoted
A^{[*]}
is defined by

A^{[*]}=N^{-1}A^{*}M.

Sun and Wei
[
14
]
used the terminology
weighted conjugate transpose
for
MN
-adjoint
. In an IIPS, by considering the same weights
M=N
, a complex square matrix
A
is called
N
-Hermitian
if
A^{[*]}=A
; it is called
N
-range Hermitian
if
R(A)=R(A^{[*]})
. If the IIPS is understood from the context, then instead of
saying that
A
is
N
-Hermitian (
N
-range Hermitian), we may simply say that
A
is Hermitian (range Hermitian).

The
MN
-
Moore-Penrose inverse
A^{[{\dagger}]}
of
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
between IIPSs is defined to be the
unique
solution
X\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
, if it exists, to the equations

AXA=A

(2.1)

XAX=X

(2.2)

(AX)^{[*]}=AX

(2.3)

(XA)^{[*]}=XA.

(2.4)

The reference to
MN
will be dropped when there is no ambiguity and
A^{[\dagger]}
will be simply called the Moore-Penrose inverse of
A
. If
A
is invertible, then
A^{[{\dagger}]}=A^{-1}
. It is easy to observe that if
M
and
N
are the identity matrices, then
A^{[{\dagger}]}=A^{\dagger}
, where
A^{\dagger}
denotes the usual Moore-Penrose inverse in an Euclidean space. Sun and Wei used the notation
A^{{\dagger}}_{MN}
for
A^{[{\dagger}]}
to emphasize on the weights of positive definite Hermite matrices
M
and
N
. In this case,
A^{{\dagger}}_{MN}
exists for all matrices
A
and
A^{{\dagger}}_{MN}=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(M^{\frac{1}{2}}AN^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{\dagger}M^{\frac{1}{2}}

[
14
]
. Unlike the Euclidean case and weighted Moore-Penrose inverse, a matrix need not have a Moore-Penrose inverse between IIPSs
[
8
]
. The following result gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of Moore-Penrose inverse of a matrix between IIPSs.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
. Then
A^{[{\dagger}]}
exists
iff
rank(A)=rank(AA^{[*]})=rank(A^{[*]}A)
.

For the sake of clarity as well as for easier reference we mention the following properties of Moore-Penrose inverse between IIPSs.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
8
]
, Section 4)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
be such that
A^{[{\dagger}]}
exists. Then the following statements hold :

(i)

A^{[*]}=A^{[*]}AA^{[{\dagger}]}=A^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}
.

(ii)

(A^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}=(A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}
.

(iii)

(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}
and
(A^{[*]}A)^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. In this
case,
(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}=(A^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
and

(A^{[*]}A)^{[{\dagger}]}=A^{[{\dagger}]}(A^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}
.

(iv)

A^{[{\dagger}]}=A^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}=(A^{[*]}A)^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}
.

(v)

(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}(AA^{[*]})A=A=(AA^{[*]})(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}A
.

(vi)

(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}(AA^{[*]})=(AA^{[*]})(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}
.

This section is ended with some known results which will be used in the sequel.

3.
Reverse Order Law

We start the section with examples which illustrate that between IIPSs,

(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}
may not exist although
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, and even though Moore-Penrose inverses of
A,B
and
AB
exist, the reverse order law
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
does not hold.

Example 3.1
.

Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
,
B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
and
M=N=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
. Clearly,
B^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
and
(AB)^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
-1&1\end{array}\right)
. Then
A
is non-singular and
rank(B)=rank(BB^{[*]})=rank(B^{[*]}B)
, so both
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. Also,
AB=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
and
rank(AB)\neq rank((AB)^{[*]}AB)
, hence
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}
does not exist.

Example 3.2
.

Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&2\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
,
B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
and
M=N=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
. Clearly,
A^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-2&0\end{array}\right)
and
B^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
. Then

AA^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-3&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right),A^{[*]}A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&2\\
-2&-4\end{array}\right),\\
BB^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
and
B^{[*]}B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}4&2\\
-2&-1\end{array}\right).
Hence
A^{[{\dagger}]}=-\frac{1}{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-2&0\end{array}\right)
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
. Moreover,
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}=-\frac{1}{9}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
. Thus
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}\neq B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
.

Motivated by the above examples, we show when the reverse order law holds good in an indefinite inner product space. Before presenting the main results, we collect some basic results.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
be matrices with suitable orders. If

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)=rank(A)=rank(B)=rank(C),

then
rank(A)=rank(D)
.

Proof.

Let
M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)
. It is given that
rank(M)=rank(A)
. Then by Lemma
2.6
, we have
D-CA^{\dagger}B=0
,
R(C^{*})\subseteq R(A^{*})
and
R(B)\subseteq R(A).

This implies
D=CA^{\dagger}B\implies rank(D)\leq rank(C)=rank(A)
.

Now to prove the reverse inequality,
rank(A)=rank(B)=rank(C)\implies R(A)=R(B)
and
R(A^{*})=R(C^{*})
.
Since
D=CA^{\dagger}B
we get
C^{\dagger}D=C^{\dagger}CA^{\dagger}B=C^{\dagger}{(C^{\dagger})}^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}B
.
Now,

R(A^{*})=R(C^{*})\implies R(A^{\dagger})=R(C^{\dagger})
. Using the fact that if
R(E)\subseteq R(F)
then
FF^{\dagger}E=E
, we get
C^{\dagger}{(C^{\dagger})}^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}.
This implies
C^{\dagger}D=A^{\dagger}B\implies C^{\dagger}DB^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}BB^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}{(B^{\dagger})}^{\dagger}B^{\dagger}
.
Now,
R(A)=R(B)\implies R((A^{\dagger})^{*})=R((B^{\dagger})^{*})
and using the fact that if
R(E^{*})\subseteq R(F^{*})
then
EF^{\dagger}F=E
we get,
C^{\dagger}DB^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}\implies rank(A^{\dagger})\leq rank(D)\implies rank(A)\leq rank(D).
This completes the proof.
∎

Next we prove the indefinite version of Lemma
2.7
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
be matrices with suitable orders. If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
exists, then

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}B\\
CA^{[*]}&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&CA^{[*]}\\
A^{[*]}B&A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(A)+rank(D-CA^{[{\dagger}]}B).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
we can easily verify the following relations.

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}B\\
CA^{[*]}&D\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AA^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CA^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AA^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CA^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}B\\
CA^{[*]}&D\end{array}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}B\\
CA^{[*]}&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AA^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CA^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(A)+rank(D-CA^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[{\dagger}]}B)\text{ (by Lemma \ref{prop})}

\displaystyle=rank(A)+rank(D-CA^{[{\dagger}]}B).

Similarly we can prove the other equality.

∎

It is known in the Euclidean case that the single expression
R(A^{*}ABB^{*})=R(BB^{*}A^{*}A)
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the reverse order law to hold (
[
1
]
, p.161). This condition was later shown (
[
11
]
, p.231) to hold in a more general setting. The main result and its proof closely follow those of Greville
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
.
If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, then the following are
equivalent:

(i)

A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}
is range Hermitian.

(ii)

R(A^{[*]}AB)\subseteq R(B)
and
R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})\subseteq R(A^{[*]})
.

(iii)

BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A
and
A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}
are range
Hermitian.

(iv)

BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB=A^{[*]}AB
and
A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}A^{[*]}=BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
:

As
B=BB^{[{\dagger}]}B=BB^{[*]}(B^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}
, we have

R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]})=R(A^{[*]}AB)
. Suppose that
A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}
is range Hermitian. Then

R(A^{[*]}AB)=R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]})=R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}A)\subseteq R(B).

The second part follows similarly.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
:

Let
C=A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}
. Then
C(B^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}=A^{[*]}AB.
Hence
R(C)=R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]})\subseteq R(A^{[*]}AB)=R(C(B^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]})\subseteq R(C)
. Thus
R(C)=R(A^{[*]}AB)
. Similarly
R(C^{[*]})=R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}).
Thus
A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}
is range Hermitian iff
R(A^{[*]}AB)=R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})
. Suppose
R(A^{[*]}AB)\subseteq R(B)
. It is a well-known fact that
ind(A^{[*]}A)=1.
Thus by Lemma
2.3
,
R(A^{[*]}AB)=R(A^{[*]}A)\cap R(B)=R(A^{[*]})\cap R(B)
. On the other hand, again by Lemma
2.3
,
R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})\subseteq R(A^{[*]})

and
ind(BB^{[*]})=1
give
R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})=R(BB^{[*]})\cap R(A^{[*]})=R(B)\cap R(A^{[*]}).
Thus
R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})=R(A^{[*]}AB).

Therefore,
A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}
is range Hermitian.

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
:
Straight forward.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
:

Suppose that
R(A^{[*]}AB)\subseteq R(B)
.
As
R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]})\subseteq R(A^{[*]}AB)\subseteq R(B)
, we get

A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}=BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}
and hence it can be shown that

R((BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A)^{[*]})=R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]})=R(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A).

Thus
BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A
is range Hermitian. In a similar way, using the inclusion relation

R(BB^{[*]}A^{[*]})\subseteq R(A^{[*]}),

we can prove that
A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}
is also range Hermitian.

(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
:

Suppose
BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A
is range Hermitian. Then
R(BB^{{}^{[{\dagger}]}}A^{[*]}A)=R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}
). It clear that
R(A^{[*]}AB)=R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}B)\subseteq R(A^{[*]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]})=R(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}A)\subseteq R(B)
. Thus
BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB=A^{[*]}AB
. Similarly, we can prove
A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}A^{[*]}=BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}
.

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
and
D=AB
. If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, then the following are
equivalent:

(i)

rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)=rank(D)
, where
D=AB
.

(ii)

(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}
exists and

(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
:

First we prove the existence of Moore-Penrose inverse of
AB
. For that, let
E=AA^{[*]}D
. It is easy to observe that
D=AB=(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}(AA^{[*]})AB=(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}E
. Thus
rank(D)=rank((AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}E)\leq rank(E)=rank(AA^{[*]}D)\leq rank(D)
.
It shows that
rank(D)=rank(AA^{[*]}D)
. Similarly, we can prove that
rank(D)=rank(DB^{[*]}B)
.

Suppose
rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)=rank(D)
. Then
rank(D)=rank(DD^{[*]}D)
by Lemma
3.3
. It concludes that
rank(D)=rank(DD^{[*]})=rank(D^{[*]}D)
. Thus
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}
exists. By Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(D^{[*]})+rank(D-AA^{[*]}(D^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}B)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(D^{[*]}-B^{[*]}BD^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}).

Hence, by the assumption
rank(D^{[*]}-B^{[*]}BD^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]})=0
. Thus
D^{[*]}=B^{[*]}BD^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}
. Pre-multiplying by
(B^{[*]}B)^{[{\dagger}]}
and post-multiplying by
(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}
we get

(B^{[*]}B)^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}=(B^{[*]}B)^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}BD^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}.

By Theorem
(ii)
(v) and (vi),

\displaystyle B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}

\displaystyle=(B^{[*]}B)^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}(DD^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}

\displaystyle=B^{[*]}A^{[*]}(DD^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}

\displaystyle=D^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}=(D^{[*]}D)^{[{\dagger}]}D^{[*]}AA^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}

\displaystyle=D^{[{\dagger}]}=(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
:

By Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(D^{[*]})+rank(D-AA^{[*]}(D^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}B)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(D-AA^{[*]}(B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}B^{[*]}B)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(D-AA^{[*]}(A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}(B^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}B^{[*]}B)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(D-AB)

\displaystyle=rank(D).

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
such that
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. If any one of the conditions listed in Theorem
3.5
holds, then

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&A^{[*]}AD\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)=rank(D).

Proof.

Suppose that

BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB=A^{[*]}AB
and
A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}A^{[*]}=BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}
hold. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}AB&ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB\\
ABB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB&AB(AB)^{[*]}AB\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&DB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}D&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right).

By Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&AA^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}B&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D&DB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}D\\
DB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}D&DD^{[*]}D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(DD^{[*]}D-DB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}DB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}D)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(DD^{[*]}D-ABB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(DD^{[*]}D-ABB^{[*]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]}AB)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(DD^{[*]}D-ABB^{[*]}A^{[*]}AB)

\displaystyle=rank(D)+rank(DD^{[*]}D-DD^{[*]}D)

\displaystyle=rank(D).

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
such that
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. If any one of the conditions listed in Theorem
3.5
holds, then
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}
exists and

(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n},B\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times\ell}
and
C\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times n}
. Then

(i)

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}\\
B^{[*]}\end{array}\right)

(ii)

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A\\
C\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&C^{[*]}\end{array}\right).

Proof.

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}\\
B^{[*]}\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}A^{*}M\\
L^{-1}B^{*}M\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}A^{*}\\
L^{-1}B^{*}\end{array}\right)&M\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}A^{*}\\
L^{-1}B^{*}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}AN^{-1}&BL^{-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\end{array}\right)&\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}&0\\
0&L^{-1}\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\end{array}\right).

Similarly we can prove (ii).

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{g\times h}
. If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, then

(i)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right)^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&0\\
0&B^{[*]}\end{array}\right)

(ii)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right)^{[{\dagger}]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[{\dagger}]}&0\\
0&B^{[{\dagger}]}\end{array}\right)

(iii)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A\\
B&0\end{array}\right)^{[{\dagger}]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&B^{[{\dagger}]}\\
A^{[{\dagger}]}&0\end{array}\right).

Proof.

(i)

Let
K=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&0\\
0&G\end{array}\right)
and
L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N&0\\
0&H\end{array}\right).

Without loss of generality we may assume that

T^{[*]}=L^{-1}T^{*}K,\text{where }T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right).

Then

\displaystyle T^{[*]}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}&0\\
0&H^{-1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{*}&0\\
0&B^{*}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&0\\
0&G\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}N^{-1}A^{*}M&0\\
0&H^{-1}B^{*}G\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&0\\
0&B^{[*]}\end{array}\right).

(ii)

Suppose
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist.

T^{[*]}T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}A&0\\
0&B^{[*]}B\end{array}\right)\text{ and }TT^{[*]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}AA^{[*]}&0\\
0&BB^{[*]}\end{array}\right).

Thus
rank(T^{[*]}T)=rank(A)+rank(B)=rank(TT^{[*]})=rank(T),

which implies
T^{[{\dagger}]}
exists.

Also it is easy to verify that
T^{[{\dagger}]}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[{\dagger}]}&0\\
0&B^{[{\dagger}]}\end{array}\right)
satisfies the Moore-Penrose equations.

(iii) is similiar to (ii).

∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
A,B,C,D,P
and
Q
be matrices with suitable orders such that
P^{[{\dagger}]}
and
Q^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. Then

\displaystyle rank(D-CP^{[{\dagger}]}AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}P^{[*]}AQ^{[*]}&P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0\\
Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B\\
0&CP^{[*]}&-D\end{array}\right)-rank(P)-rank(Q).

Proof.

It is observed that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CP^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&D\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&C\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Q^{[{\dagger}]}\\
P^{[{\dagger}]}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&D\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&C\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&P\\
Q&0\end{array}\right)^{[{\dagger}]}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right)

(by Lemma
3.10
(iii)).

Thus by Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CP^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{[*]}MM^{[*]}&M^{[*]}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&B\end{array}\right)\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
0&C\end{array}\right)M^{[*]}&\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-A&0\\
0&-D\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right)-rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&P\\
Q&0\end{array}\right),

where
M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&P\\
Q&0\end{array}\right).

By Lemma
3.10
(ii),

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CP^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B\\
P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0&P^{[*]}A&0\\
0&AQ^{[*]}&-A&0\\
CP^{[*]}&0&0&-D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle-rank(P)-rank(Q).

Also

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&I&P^{[*]}&0\\
I&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&I\\
0&0&I&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B\\
P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0&P^{[*]}A&0\\
0&AQ^{[*]}&-A&0\\
CP^{[*]}&0&0&-D\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&I&0&0\\
I&0&0&0\\
Q^{[*]}&0&0&I\\
0&0&I&0\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}P^{[*]}AQ^{[*]}&P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0&0\\
Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B&0\\
0&CP^{[*]}&-D&0\\
0&0&0&-A\end{array}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CP^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}P^{[*]}AQ^{[*]}&P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0&0\\
Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B&0\\
0&CP^{[*]}&-D&0\\
0&0&0&-A\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle-rank(P)-rank(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}P^{[*]}AQ^{[*]}&P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0\\
Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B\\
0&CP^{[*]}&-D\end{array}\right)+rank(A)

\displaystyle-rank(P)-rank(Q).

But by Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B\\
CP^{[{\dagger}]}A&D\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=rank(A)+rank(D-CP^{[{\dagger}]}AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B).

Thus

\displaystyle rank(D-CP^{[{\dagger}]}AQ^{[{\dagger}]}B)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}P^{[*]}AQ^{[*]}&P^{[*]}PP^{[*]}&0\\
Q^{[*]}QQ^{[*]}&0&Q^{[*]}B\\
0&CP^{[*]}&-D\end{array}\right)-rank(P)-rank(Q).

∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
such that
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. Then

\displaystyle rank(AB-ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)+rank(AB)-rank(A)-rank(B).

Proof.

Replace
D
by
AB
,
C
by
AB
,
P
by
B,

A
by
I
,
Q
by
A
and
B
by
AB
in Theorem
3.11
, we get

\displaystyle rank(AB-ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}&0\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&0&A^{[*]}AB\\
0&ABB^{[*]}&-AB\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle-rank(A)-rank(B).

Also,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0&0\\
0&I&A^{[*]}\\
0&0&I\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}&0\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&0&A^{[*]}AB\\
0&ABB^{[*]}&-AB\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&0&0\\
0&I&0\\
0&B^{[*]}&I\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}&0\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}&0\\
0&0&-AB\end{array}\right)

Therefore

\displaystyle rank(AB-ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}\end{array}\right)+rank(AB)

\displaystyle-rank(A)-rank(B).

Moreover, by observing the following facts

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&{A^{[{\dagger}]}}^{[*]}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&{B^{[{\dagger}]}}^{[*]}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&B^{[*]}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}\end{array}\right),

we have

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}BB^{[*]}\\
A^{[*]}AA^{[*]}&A^{[*]}ABB^{[*]}\end{array}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle rank(AB-ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)+rank(AB)-rank(A)-rank(B).

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two N-Hermitian idempotent matrices of suitable orders. Then

\displaystyle rank(PQ-QP)=2\;rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P&Q\end{array}\right)+2\;rank(PQ)-2\;rank(P)-2\;rank(Q).

Proof.

Since
P
and
Q
are two idempotent matrices, by Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle rank(PQ-QP)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P\\
Q\end{array}\right)+rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P&Q\end{array}\right)+rank(PQ)

\displaystyle+rank(QP)-2\;rank(P)-2\;rank(Q).

By Lemma
3.9
,

\displaystyle rank(PQ-QP)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P^{[*]}&Q^{[*]}\end{array}\right)+rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P&Q\end{array}\right)+rank(PQ)

\displaystyle+rank(P^{[*]}Q^{[*]})-2\;rank(P)-2\;rank(Q).

Since
P
and
Q
are Hermitian we get,

\displaystyle rank(PQ-QP)=2\;rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P&Q\end{array}\right)+2\;rank(PQ)-2\;rank(P)-2\;rank(Q).

∎

Lemma 3.14
.

If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, then
rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}BB^{[{\dagger}]}&A^{[{\dagger}]}A\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right).

Proof.

The conclusion may be arrived easily by using the following two equations

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}BB^{[{\dagger}]}&A^{[{\dagger}]}A\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&0\\
0&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right)\text{and}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[{\dagger}]}&0\\
0&(A^{[{\dagger}]})^{[*]}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}BB^{[{\dagger}]}&A^{[{\dagger}]}A\end{array}\right).

∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
. If
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist, then

rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A-A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]})=2\;rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&B\end{array}\right)+2\;rank(AB)-2\;rank(A)-2\;rank(B).

Proof.

Clearly
A^{[{\dagger}]}A
and
BB^{[{\dagger}]}
are Hermitian and idempotent, then by Lemma
3.13
,

\displaystyle rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A-A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]})

\displaystyle=2\;rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}BB^{[{\dagger}]}&A^{[{\dagger}]}A\end{array}\right)+2\;rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A)

\displaystyle-2\;rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]})-2\;rank(A^{[{\dagger}]}A)

\displaystyle=2\;rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&A^{[*]}\end{array}\right)+2\;rank(AB)

\displaystyle-2\;rank(B)-2\;rank(A)\ \ (\text{by Lemma \ref{rankblock}}).

Also,

\displaystyle rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A)

\displaystyle=rank(B(B^{[*]}B)^{[{\dagger}]}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}(AA^{[*]})^{[{\dagger}]}A)

\displaystyle\leq rank(B^{[*]}A^{[*]})=rank(AB)

\displaystyle\text{ and }rank(AB)=rank(B^{[*]}A^{[*]})

\displaystyle=rank(B^{[*]}BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AA^{[*]})

\displaystyle\leq rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A).

Thus
rank(AB)=rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A).

∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\ell}
such that
A^{[{\dagger}]}
and
B^{[{\dagger}]}
exist. If

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&B\end{array}\right),

then
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
is equivalent to any one of the conditions given in Theorem
3.5
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&B\end{array}\right),

by Theorem
3.15
and Corollary
3.12
,

2\;rank(AB-ABB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}AB)=rank(BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A-A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}).

Thus if
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
, then
BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}A=A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}
. Therefore

BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}=BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}=A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[{\dagger}]}BB^{[*]}A^{[*]}=A^{[{\dagger}]}ABB^{[*]}A^{[*]}.

Similarly, we prove
BB^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[*]}AB=A^{[*]}AB
.
Thus we obtain condition (iv) of Theorem
3.5
.

∎

4.
An Open problem

We can observe from Theorem
3.16
that
(AB)^{[{\dagger}]}=B^{[{\dagger}]}A^{[{\dagger}]}
is equivalent to any one of the conditions given in Theorem
3.5
by assuming the rank equality

rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{[*]}A^{[*]}&B^{[*]}B\\
AA^{[*]}&AB\end{array}\right)=rank\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{[*]}&B\end{array}\right).

But in the Euclidean case such a rank equality assumption is not required. Thus it is an open question for giving proof for Theorem
3.16
without assuming the rank equality, or finding a counter example.

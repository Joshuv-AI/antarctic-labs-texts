Compactness for Ω \Omega -Yang-Mills connections Thanks: R.W.’s research is supported in part by NSF grant DMS-1906403.
Xuemiao Chen, Richard A. Wentworth

Abstract
. On a Riemannian manifold of dimension n n we extend the known analytic results on Yang-Mills connections to the class of connections called Ω \Omega -Yang-Mills connections, where Ω \Omega is a smooth, not necessarily closed, ( n − 4 ) (n-4) -form on M M . Special cases include Ω \Omega -anti-self-dual connections and Hermitian-Yang-Mills connections over general complex manifolds. By a key observation, a weak compactness result is obtained for moduli space of smooth Ω \Omega -Yang-Mills connections with uniformly L 2 L^{2} bounded curvature, and it can be improved in the case of Hermitian-Yang-Mills connections over general complex manifolds. A removable singularity theorem for singular Ω \Omega -Yang-Mills connections on a trivial bundle with small energy concentration is also proven. As an application, it is shown how to compactify the moduli space of smooth Hermitian-Yang-Mills connections on unitary bundles over a class of balanced manifolds of Hodge-Riemann type. This class includes the metrics coming from multipolarizations, and in particular, the Kähler metrics. In the case of multipolarizations on a projective algebraic manifold, the compactification of smooth irreducible Hermitian-Yang-Mills connections with fixed determinant modulo gauge transformations inherits a complex structure from algebro-geometric considerations.

\displaystyle\sum_{j}4\int_{M}\langle F_{A}(\nabla_{\partial_{r}}X,e_{j}),F_{A}(\partial_{r},e_{j})\rangle+\sum_{i\geq 2}\sum_{j}4\int_{M}\langle F_{A}(\nabla_{e_{i}}X,e_{j}),F_{A}(e_{i},e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\xi^{\prime}r|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+\sum_{j}4\int_{M}\xi|F_{A}(\partial_{r},e_{j})|^{2}+\sum_{i\geq 2}\sum_{j}4\int_{M}\xi|F_{A}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}+\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\xi^{\prime}r|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+4\int_{M}\xi|F_{A}|^{2}+\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi|F_{A}|^{2}.

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\text{div}(X)-2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}|X||F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}(\xi^{\prime}r+(n-4)\xi+O(r^{2}))&-2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}\xi r|F_{A}|^{2}\\

Now by replacing

\displaystyle\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}(-\tau\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}+(n-4)\xi_{\tau})-2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}r|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{M}4\tau\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}(\tau\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}+(4-n)\xi_{\tau})+2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}r|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\tau\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}.

Multiply the above by
e^{a\tau}\tau^{3-n}
where

determined later,
and use the fact that
\xi_{\tau}r|F_{A}|^{2}\leq\xi_{\tau}\tau|F_{A}|^{2}
,

\xi_{\tau}

\{|x|\leq\tau\}
. We conclude

\displaystyle e^{a\tau}\frac{d}{d\tau}(\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2})+e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+e^{a\tau}\tau^{3-n}\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}.

which implies

\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{d\tau}(e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2})\\
\geq&4e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}+e^{a\tau}\tau^{3-n}\int_{M}O(r^{2})\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}+ae^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}\\
&-e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}2\sup|d\Omega|\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2}\end{aligned}.

Now choose

\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}=-\frac{r}{\tau}\xi_{\tau}^{\prime}
is nonnegative,

\frac{d}{d\tau}(e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\xi_{\tau}|F_{A}|^{2})\geq 4e^{a\tau}\tau^{4-n}\int_{M}\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}\geq 4\int_{M}e^{ar}r^{4-n}\frac{d\xi_{\tau}}{d\tau}|\iota_{\partial_{r}}F_{A}|^{2}

if

. By integrating the inequality above from

to

∎

2.2.
\epsilon
-Regularity

The goal of this section is to prove the following
\varepsilon
-regularity
result.

Theorem 2.3
.

There exist positive constants
\epsilon_{0}
,
r_{0}
, and
C
, depending
only on the geometry of
(M,g)
and
\Omega
,
with the following property.
If

is a smooth solution to the
\Omega
-Yang-Mills equations
(

There are two approaches to the regularity of Yang-Mills equations in
higher dimensions, and both make use of the monotonicity formula.
Nakajima
[
17
]
uses a Bochner-Weitzenböck formula for the
curvature to directly get the bound in Theorem

This is similar to Schoen’s approach for the harmonic map problem.

[

derives

estimates from

, and then uses a continuity method
to reduce to the case of connections with
L^{p}
bounds.
This has the advantage of applying to a larger class of connections
satisfying curvature bounds rather than equations.
Interestingly, both methods apply directly to the case of
\Omega
-YM
connections, and we find it useful to present each one here.

2.2.1.
Method I

Suppose
A
is a smooth solution to (
1.1
).
Then (
2.2
) implies

\Delta_{A}F_{A}=\mp d_{A}*(F_{A}\wedge d\Omega).

In particular, by the Weitzenböck formula, we have

(2.8)

\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}F_{A}=\mp d_{A}*(F_{A}\wedge d\Omega)+\{F_{A},R_{g}\}+\{F_{A},F_{A}\}\ .

Proposition 2.4
.

A solution to (
1.1
) satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F_{A}|^{2}\geq-|F_{A}|^{2}-c|R_{g}||F_{A}|^{2}-\frac{c^{2}}{4}|d\Omega|^{2}|F_{A}|^{2}-c|\nabla d\Omega||F_{A}|^{2}

for some constant

depending only on
(M,g)
.

Proof.

Indeed, from (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-<\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}F_{A},F_{A}>+<\nabla_{A}F_{A},\nabla_{A}F_{A}>

\displaystyle\geq-|F_{A}|^{3}-|R_{g}||F_{A}|^{2}-|d_{A}*(F_{A}\wedge d\Omega)||F_{A}|+|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-|F_{A}|^{3}-|R_{g}||F_{A}|^{2}-c(|d\Omega||\nabla_{A}F_{A}||F_{A}|+|\nabla d\Omega||F_{A}|^{2})+|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-|F_{A}|^{3}-|R_{g}||F_{A}|^{2}-\frac{c^{2}}{4}|d\Omega|^{2}|F_{A}|^{2}-c|\nabla d\Omega||F_{A}|^{2}

The last inequality follows from completion of square.
∎

Given this, we can repeat the argument in
[

to prove Theorem

3.

The results in this section are all local. We will fix a sequence of

\Omega

over

is endowed with any fixed

We assume the standard coordinates are geodesic normal with respect to the

Define

The monotonicity formula obtained in Theorem

3.1.

By passing to a subsequence, we can assume

(1)

(2)

.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following properties hold:

(1)

For a.e.

\lim_{i}\mu_{i}(B_{r}(x))=\mu(B_{r}(x))

(2)

a.e.

(3)

(4)

for

a.e.

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{4-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|F_{A_{\infty}}|^{2}dV=0.

(1) follows from the elementary fact that
\mu(\partial B_{r}(x))=0
for a.e.

. The first part of (2) now follows from (1) and the fact that

increases as

[

.

[

∎

.

From this, we know

3.2.

\displaystyle\sigma^{4-n}\int_{B_{\sigma}(x)}\phi|F_{A_{i,\lambda_{i}}}|^{2}\,

\displaystyle dV_{\lambda_{i}}-\rho^{4-n}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\phi|F_{A_{i,\lambda_{i}}}|^{2}\,dV_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma}^{\rho}ds\,\frac{d}{ds}\left\{s^{4-n}\int_{B_{s}(x)}\phi|F_{A_{i,\lambda_{i}}}|^{2}\,dV_{\lambda_{i}}\right\}

Denote

. It suffices to show that

satisfies

(6.1)

-\Delta f+\alpha\frac{|df|^{2}}{f}-c|F_{A}|^{2}f\leq Cf

. Now the existence of the gauge

[

, App. C, Thm. 19]
.
It remains to show that

satisfies the inequality (
6.1
). By (

) we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+(\nabla_{A}^{*}\nabla_{A}F_{A},F_{A})

\displaystyle=-|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+(\{F_{A},F_{A}\},F_{A})+(\{R_{g},F_{A}\},F_{A})+(\{d\Omega,\nabla_{A}F_{A}\},F_{A})

which implies

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F_{A}|^{2}+|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+|d_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+|d_{A}^{*}F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\{F_{A},F_{A}\},F_{A})+(\{R_{g},F_{A}\},F_{A})+(\{d\Omega,\nabla_{A}F_{A}\},F_{A})+|d\Omega\wedge F_{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|F_{A}|^{3}+C_{\epsilon}|F_{A}|^{2}+\epsilon|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}

where the last line follows from Hölder’s inequality, and

is to be determined later. This then implies

-\frac{1}{2}\Delta|F_{A}|^{2}+(1-\epsilon)(|\nabla_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+|d_{A}F_{A}|^{2}+|d_{A}^{*}F_{A}|^{2})-C|F_{A}|^{3}\leq C_{\epsilon}|F_{A}|^{2}.

Now the improved Kato inequality (see
[

, Thm. 5]
)

Combined with (

Substituting

Choose

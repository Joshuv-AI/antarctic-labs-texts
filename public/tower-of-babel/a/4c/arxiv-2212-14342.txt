On the absence of solutions of differential inequalities with ∞ \infty -Laplacian
A. A. Kon’kov

Abstract
. For differential inequalities with the ∞ \infty -Laplacian in the principal part, we obtain conditions for the absence of solutions in unbounded domains. Examples are given to demonstrate the accuracy of these conditions.



Introduction.

We study solutions of the problem

(1)

\Delta_{\infty}u\geq f(x,u)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\qquad\left.u\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

where
\Omega
is unbounded domain
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,

\Delta_{\infty}u=\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}

is the
\infty
-Laplacian, and
f
is some function such that

(2)

f(x,t)>0

for all
t>0
and
x\in\Omega
.
Let us agree to denote by
B_{r}
and
S_{r}
, respectively, the open ball and sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius
r>0
centered at zero. We shall assume that
S_{r}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
for all
r>r_{*}
, where
r_{*}>0
is some real number.
Also let there be real numbers
\sigma>1
and
\theta>1
such that

(3)

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{r,\sigma},\,\zeta\in(t/\theta,t\theta)}f(x,|x|\zeta)\geq q(r)g(t)

for all
t>0
and
r>r_{*}
, where

\Omega_{r,\sigma}=\{x\in\Omega:r/\sigma<|x|<r\sigma\}

and
q
and
g
are non-negative measurable functions such that

\inf_{K}g>0

for any compact set
K\subset(0,\infty)
.

Solutions of the problem (
1
) is understood in the viscosity sense [1, 2].
If
\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, i.e.
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then the boundary condition in (
1
) is assumed to be satisfied automatically.

The phenomenon of the absence of non-trivial solutions for differential equations and inequalities (blow-up) has been studied by many authors [3–19].
In the case when the main part of the differential operator has a divergent form, the method of nonlinear capacity has proven itself well. The main ideas of this method are presented in [6, 18].
For inequalities (
1
) this method obviously does not work.
Under the assumption that
\Omega
is a bounded domain, the blow-up phenomenon for inequalities of the form (
1
) was studied in [20–22].
These papers dealt with the so-called “boundary blow-up” or, in other words, “large solutions”, tending to infinity when approaching
\partial\Omega
.
The dependence of the right-hand side on the spatial variable was essentially not taken into account in the works [20–22].
In the case of unbounded domains, the conditions for the absence of nontrivial solutions to the problem (
1
) have not been known until now. Theorem 1 proved in our paper eliminates this gap.

Definition 1.

A function

u:\overline{\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is called upper semicontinuous at a point

x\in\overline{\Omega}

if

\limsup_{y\to x}u(y)\leq u(x).

We say that
u
is upper semicontinuous on the set
{\mathcal{G}}\subset\overline{\Omega}
if it is upper semicontinuous at every point of
x\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

Definition 2.

The second-order superjet
J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)
of a function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
at a point
x\in\Omega
is the set of ordered pairs
(v,A)
, where
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is some vector and
A
is a symmetric
n\times n
matrix such that

u(y)-u(x)\leq\langle v,y-x\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle A(y-x),y-x\rangle+\bar{\bar{o}}(|y-x|^{2})

for all
y
from some neighborhood of the point
x
. Here and below, angle brackets denote the standard scalar product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Note that in the case of
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
the set
J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)
is not empty for any point
x\in\Omega
, because

(Du(x),D^{2}u(x))\in J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x),

where
Du(x)
is the gradient vector and
D^{2}u(x)
is the matrix of second derivatives of the function
u
at the point
x
.

Definition 3.

A function
u\geq 0
is called a non-negative solution of problem (
1
) if
u
is upper semicontinuous on
\overline{\Omega}
, vanishes on
\partial\Omega

and for any
x\in\Omega
such that
J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)\neq\emptyset
the relation

(4)

\langle Av,v\rangle\geq f(x,u(x))

is valid for all
(v,A)\in J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)
.

In the general case [1], an upper semicontinuous function
u
is called a viscosity solution of the inequality

F(x,u,Du,D^{2}u)\leq 0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

where
F
is some function if for any point
x\in\Omega
such
that
J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)\neq\emptyset
the relation

(5)

F(x,u(x),v,A)\leq 0

is valid fro all
(v,A)\in J_{\Omega}^{2,+}u(x)
.
In particular, taking

(6)

F(x,u,Du,D^{2}u)=f(x,u)-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}

in formula (
5
), we obtain (
4
).

In order for the classical solutions to be simultaneously viscous, the function
F
is subjected to the condition of degenerate ellipticity

F(x,\zeta,v,A)\leq F(x,\zeta,v,B)

for all
x\in\Omega
, real numbers
\zeta
, vectors
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and symmetric
{n\times n}
-matrices
A\geq B
. The last inequality is understood in the sense of quadratic forms.
Namely, we say that
A\geq B
if

\langle Av,v\rangle\geq\langle Bv,v\rangle

for all
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
It is obvious that the function
F
defined by (
6
) satisfies the condition of degenerate ellipticity.

1. Main results.
The following statements are valid.

Theorem 1.

Suppose that

(7)

\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}(g(t)t)^{-1/4}dt<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{\infty}q(r)dr=\infty.

Then any non-negative solution of (
1
) is identically equal to zero.

A special case of (
1
) is the problem

(8)

\Delta_{\infty}u\geq c(|x|)u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\qquad\left.u\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

where
c
is a positive non-increasing function.

Corollary 1.

Suppose that
\lambda>3
and

\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{\infty}r^{\lambda}c(r)dr=\infty.

Then any non-negative solution of (
8
) is identically equal to zero.

Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 will be proved in Paragraph 2.
Now we give examples demonstrating the accuracy of these results.

Example 1.

Consider the inequality

(9)

\Delta_{\infty}u\geq c_{0}(1+|x|)^{s}u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
c_{0}=const>0
.
According to Corollary 1, the conditions

\lambda>3\quad\mbox{and}\quad s\geq-\lambda-1

guarantee that any non-negative solution of (
9
) is identically equal to zero.
These conditions are exact. Indeed, it is not difficult to verify that, for

\lambda>3
and
s<-\lambda-1
, the expression

u(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|x|^{-(4+s)/(\lambda-3)}-r_{0}^{-(4+s)/(\lambda-3)},&&|x|>r_{0},\\
&0,&&|x|\leq r_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
r_{0}>0
is some real number,
defines a non-negative solution of (
9
).
At the same time, in the case of
\lambda\leq 3
, by somewhat more complicated reasoning, one can show that the inequality (
9
) has a non-trivial non-negative solution for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Example 2.

We examine the critical exponent
s=-\lambda-1
in the right-hand side of (
9
).
Let
u\geq 0
satisfy the inequality

(10)

\Delta_{\infty}u\geq c_{0}(1+|x|)^{-\lambda-1}\ln^{\mu}(2+|x|)u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
c_{0}=const>0
. Applying Corollary 1, we obtain that, for

(11)

\lambda>3\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu\geq-1,

the function
u
is identically equal to zero.
At the same time, by direct calculations, one can verify that, for
\lambda>3
and
\mu<-1
, the relation

u(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &|x|\ln^{-(\mu+1)/(\lambda-3)}|x|-r_{0}\ln^{-(\mu+1)/(\lambda-3)}r_{0},&&|x|>r_{0},\\
&0,&&|x|\leq r_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
r_{0}>0
is large enough, defines a non-negative solution (
10
).
Thus, the second condition in (
11
) is exact. The first condition in (
11
) is also exact. It can be shown that, in the case of
\lambda\leq 3
, inequality (
10
) has a non-trivial non-negative solution for all
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Example 3.

Now we examine investigate the critical exponent
\lambda=3
in (
9
).
Namely, consider the inequality

(12)

\Delta_{\infty}u\geq c_{0}(1+|x|)^{s}u^{3}\ln^{\nu}(2+u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
c_{0}=const>0
.
According to Theorem 1, if

(13)

\nu>4\quad\mbox{and}\quad s\geq-4,

then any non-negative solution of (
12
) is identically equal to zero.
In so doing, if
\nu>4
and
s<-4
, then

u(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{|x|^{(4+s)/(4-\nu)}}-e^{r_{0}^{(4+s)/(4-\nu)}},&&|x|>r_{0},\\
&0,&&|x|\leq r_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
r_{0}>0
is large enough, is a non-negative solution of (
12
).
If the first condition in (
13
) is violated, i.e.
\nu\leq 4
, then (
12
) has a non-trivial non-negative solution for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Thus, both conditions in (
13
) are exact.

2. Proof of Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.

Below we assume that conditions (
7
) are satisfied.
Take a function
\omega\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
positive on the interval
(-1,1)
, equal to zero on the set
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-1,1)
, and such that

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega(t)dt=1.

We denote

\omega_{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\delta}\omega\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right),\quad\delta>0.

Lemma 1.

Let
\mu>1
,
\nu>1
, and
0<\alpha\leq 1
be some real numbers and, moreover,

\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

and

H:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be measuable functions satisfying the inequality

\eta(t)\leq\inf_{(t/\mu,t\mu)}H

for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Then

\left(\int\limits_{M_{1}}^{M_{2}}\eta^{-\alpha}(t)t^{\alpha-1}\,dt\right)^{1/\alpha}\geq C\int\limits_{M_{1}}^{M_{2}}\frac{dt}{H(t)}

for all real numbers
M_{1}>0
and
M_{2}>0
such that
M_{2}\geq\nu M_{1}
,
where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
,
\nu
and
\mu
.

Lemma 2.

Let
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
be some real numbers and

\eta:(r_{1},r_{2})\to[0,\infty)
be measurable function. Then

\int\limits_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\left(\int\limits_{r_{1}}^{\rho}\eta(\xi)d\xi\right)^{\alpha}d\rho\geq C\int\limits_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\left(1-\frac{\xi}{r_{2}}\right)^{\alpha}(\xi\eta(\xi))^{\alpha}d\xi,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
.

Lemma 3.

Let

0<r_{1}<r_{2}

and

0<\alpha<1

be some real numbers and

\eta:(r_{1},r_{2})\to[0,\infty)

be a measurable function. Then

(14)

\left(\int\limits_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\eta(\xi)d\xi\right)^{\alpha}\geq C\int\limits_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\eta(\xi)\varkappa^{\alpha-1}(\xi)d\xi,

where

\varkappa(\xi)=\int\limits_{\xi}^{r_{2}}\eta(\zeta)d\zeta,

and the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
.

Remark 1.

If
\varkappa(\xi)=0
for some
\xi\in(r_{1},r_{2})
, then

\eta(\zeta)=0
for almost all
\zeta\in(\xi,r_{2})
.
In this case, we assume that

\eta(\xi)\varkappa^{\alpha-1}(\xi)

is also equal to zero on the right in (
14
).

Lemma 4.

Let
\gamma>0
,
\lambda>1
, and
r_{0}>r_{*}
be some real numbers and, moreover,

p:[r_{*},\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a locally integrable function such that

\frac{rp(r)}{\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{r}p(\xi)d\xi}\leq\gamma

for all
r\geq r_{0}
.
Then

\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{\lambda r}p(\xi)d\xi\leq C\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{r}p(\xi)d\xi

for all
r\geq r_{0}
, where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\gamma
and
\lambda
.

The proof of Lemma 1 can be found in [13, Lemma 2.3].
Lemmas 2 and 3 were proved in [12, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]. Lemma 4 is proved in [14, Lemma 2.7].

Lemma 5.

Let
\mu>1
be some real number and
H:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a measurable function such that

\eta(t)=\inf_{(t/\mu,t\mu)}H>0

for all
t\in(0,\infty)
and, moreover,

(15)

\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt<\infty.

Then there exists an infinitely smooth function
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfying the following conditions:

1)

h(t)\leq H(t)
for all
t\in(0,\infty)
;

2)

h(t_{2})\geq h(t_{1})
for all
t_{2}\geq t_{1}>0
;

3)

for any real number
\alpha>0
there is a constant
\beta>0
,
depending only on
\alpha
and
\mu
such that

h(\alpha t)\leq\beta h(t)
for all
t\geq 2
;

4)

(16)

\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}(h(t)t)^{-1/4}dt<\infty.

Proof.

Take
t_{k}=\mu^{k/4}
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.
Arguing by induction, we construct a sequence of monotonically nondecreasing functions

h_{k}:[1,t_{k}]\to(0,\infty)
,

k=1,2,\ldots
,
with the following properties:

i)

h_{k}(t)\leq h_{s}(t)\leq H(t)

for all
t\in[1,t_{s}]
,
1\leq s\leq k
;

ii)

h_{k}(\mu^{1/4}t)\leq\mu^{2}h_{k}(t)

for all
t\in[1,t_{k-1}]
,
k=1,2,\ldots
;

iii)

(17)

\int\limits_{1}^{t_{k}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{\mu^{1/2}}{1-\mu^{-1/16}}\int\limits_{1}^{t_{k}}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt

for all
k=1,2,\ldots
.

Denote

\gamma_{k}=\sup_{(t_{k-1},t_{k+1})}\eta,\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

Let us put
h_{1}=\gamma_{1}
.
Assume now that for some
k\geq 2
the function
h_{k-1}
is already known. We take

h_{k}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\{\gamma_{k},\,h_{k-1}(t)\},&&t\in[1,t_{k-1}],\\
&\min\{\gamma_{k},\,h_{k-1}(t_{k-1})\},&&t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}]\end{aligned}\right.

if

(18)

\mu h_{k-1}(t_{k-1})\geq\gamma_{k}

and

h_{k}(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &h_{k-1}(t),&&t\in[1,t_{k-1}],\\
&\frac{t_{k}-t+\mu(t-t_{k-1})}{t_{k}-t_{k-1}}h_{k-1}(t_{k-1}),&&t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\end{aligned}\right.

if condition (
18
) is not fulfilled.
It is easy to see that
h_{k}
satisfies conditions i) and ii).
Let us show that
h_{k}
satisfies inequality (
17
).
In the case
k=1
this inequality follows from the definitions of
h_{1}
and
\gamma_{1}
.
Let
k\geq 2
and

(19)

\int\limits_{1}^{t_{s}}(h_{s}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{\mu^{1/2}}{1-\mu^{-1/16}}\int\limits_{1}^{t_{s}}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt

for all
1\leq s\leq k-1
.
At first, we assume that (
18
) holds. Let
s\geq 0
be the minimum integer such that
\mu h_{k}(t_{s})\geq h_{k}(t_{k})
.
It follows from the definition of the function
h_{k}
that
h_{s}=h_{k}
on the interval
[1,t_{s}]
.
Since

h_{k}(t)\geq\frac{h_{k}(t_{k})}{\mu}\geq\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\mu^{2}}

for all
t\in(t_{s},t_{k})
, we obtain

\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{k}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{4}{3}\mu^{1/2}\gamma_{k}^{-1/4}t_{k}^{3/4}.

Combining the last inequality with the obvious relation

\int\limits_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\geq\frac{4}{3}\gamma_{k}^{-1/4}(t_{k}^{3/4}-t_{k-1}^{3/4})=\frac{4}{3}\gamma_{k}^{-1/4}(1-\mu^{-3/16})t_{k}^{3/4},

we have

(20)

\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{k}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{\mu^{1/2}}{1-\mu^{-3/16}}\int\limits_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt.

If
s=0
, then (
20
) immediately implies (
17
). Assume that
s\geq 1
.
In view of the fact that
s\leq k-1
, estimate (
19
) is valid, summing which with (
20
), we derive (
17
).

Now assume that (
18
) does not hold.
In this case,
h_{k}(t_{k})=\mu h_{k}(t_{k-1})
.
Let
s\geq 1
be the minimum integer such that

h_{k}(t_{k})=\mu^{k-s}h_{k}(t_{s})
.
We have

\int\limits_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt=\mu^{-(i-s)/16}\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{s+1}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt

for all
s\leq i\leq k-1
; therefore,

(21)

\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{k}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{1}{1-\mu^{-1/16}}\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{s+1}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt.

At the same time, taking into account the monotonicity of the function
h_{k}
and the fact that

h_{k}(t_{s})=h_{k}(t_{s-1})\geq\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\mu},

we have
h_{k}(t)\geq\eta(t)/\mu
for all
t\in(t_{s},t_{s+1})
.
Thus, in view of (
21
), the estimate

\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{k}}(h_{k}(t)t)^{-1/4}dt\leq\frac{\mu^{1/4}}{1-\mu^{-1/16}}\int\limits_{t_{s}}^{t_{s+1}}(\eta(t)t)^{-1/4}dt

is valid.
Since
h_{s}=h_{k}
on the interval
[1,t_{s}]
, adding this with (
19
), we again arrive at (
17
).

By (
15
),

\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{k}=\infty;

therefore, the sequence
h_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, stabilizes at
k\to\infty
on any compact set belonging to the set
[1,\infty)
.
In particular, for all
t\in[1,\infty)
there exists a limit

\tilde{h}(t)=\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{k}(t).

Let us extend the function
\tilde{h}
to the entire real axis, putting

\tilde{h}(t)=\min\left\{\tilde{h}(1),\inf_{(t,1]}H\right\}

for
t\in(0,1)
and
\tilde{h}(t)=0
for
t\in(-\infty,0]
.
To complete the proof of Lemma 5, it remasins to take

h(t)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega_{1/2}(t-\tau)\tilde{h}\left(\tau-\frac{1}{2}\right)d\tau,\quad t>0.

We prove Theorem 1 in several steps.
Let us agree to denote by
C
positive, possibly different, constants depending only on
\sigma
and
\theta
.

Step 1.

Applying Lemma 5 with
\mu=\theta^{1/2}
and

H(t)=\sup_{(t/\mu,t\mu)}g,

we have a non-decreasing infinitely smooth function
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

(22)

h(4t)\leq Ch(t)

for all
t\geq 2
, condition (
16
) is valid and, moreover,

(23)

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{r,\sigma},\,\zeta\in(\theta^{-1/2}t,\theta^{1/2}t)}f(x,|x|\zeta)\geq q(r)h(t)

for all
t>0
and
r>r_{*}
.

Step 2.

We take a continuous function

p:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and a real number
R_{*}>r_{*}
,
satisfying the following conditions:

p(r)=0\quad\mbox{for all }0\leq r\leq R_{*};

(24)

p(r)=e^{-1/(r-R_{*})+1}p(R_{*}+1)>0\quad\mbox{for all }R_{*}<r\leq R_{*}+1;

(25)

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{r,\sigma^{1/4}},\,\zeta\in(\theta^{-1/2}t,\theta^{1/2}t)}f(x,|x|\zeta)\geq p(r)h(t)\quad\mbox{for all }t>0,r>R_{*};

(26)

rp(r)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for all }r\geq 0;

(27)

\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{\infty}p(r)dr=\infty.

Let us show that such a function
p
and a real number
R_{*}
exist, and
R_{*}
can be made arbitrarily large. We put

Q_{\delta}(r)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega_{\delta}(r-\rho)Q(\rho)d\rho,\quad\delta>0,

where

Q(\rho)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&\rho\in(-\infty,r_{*}],\\
&\sup_{(\sigma^{-1/4}\rho,\sigma^{1/4}\rho)\cap(r_{*},\infty)}q,&&\rho\in(r_{*},\infty).\end{aligned}\right.

Taking into account (
23
), we have

(28)

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{r,\sigma^{1/2}},\,\zeta\in(\theta^{-1/2}t,\theta^{1/2}t)}f(x,|x|\zeta)\geq Q_{\delta}(r)h(t)

for all
t>0
and
r>r_{*}
, where
\delta>0
is a sufficiently small real number.
At the same time, for any
\delta>0
there is a real number
r_{\delta}>r_{*}
such that

\inf_{(\sigma^{-1/8}r,\sigma^{1/8}r)}Q_{\delta}\geq q(r)

for all
r\in[r_{\delta},\infty)
.
Denote

M_{\delta}(r)=\min\left\{Q_{\delta}(r),\frac{1}{r}\right\}.

We show that

(29)

\int\limits_{r_{*}}^{\infty}M_{\delta}(r)dr=\infty

for any
\delta>0
.
Indeed, let there be a real number
r>0
in every neighborhood of infinity such that

q(r)\geq 1/r
.
Then

M_{\delta}(\rho)\geq\sigma^{-1/8}/\rho

for all

\rho\in(\sigma^{-1/8}r,\sigma^{1/8}r)
;
therefore,

\int\limits_{\sigma^{-1/8}r}^{\sigma^{1/8}r}M_{\delta}(\rho)d\rho=\frac{1}{4}\sigma^{-1/8}\ln\sigma.

According to the necessary criterion for the convergence of an improper integral, this proves (
29
).
Suppose now that
q(r)<1/r
for all
r>0
in some neighborhood of infinity.
In this case, we obtain

M_{\delta}(r)\geq\min\left\{q(r),\frac{1}{r}\right\}=q(r)

for all sufficiently large
r>0
. Hence, (
29
) follows from the second condition of (
7
).

Thus, it remains to take

p(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&r\in[0,R_{*}],\\

Lemma 6.

Lemma 8.

Assume the contrary. Let

In view of (

u(y)-u(x)\leq U(y)-U(x)=\langle DU(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}U(x)(y-x),y-x\rangle+\bar{\bar{o}}(|y-x|^{2})

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}U(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial U(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial U(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\geq f(x,u(x))>{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x)).

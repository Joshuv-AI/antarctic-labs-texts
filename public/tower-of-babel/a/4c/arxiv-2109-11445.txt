Critical temperatures of a model cuprate
Yu. D. Panov

Abstract
The problem of competing orderings in the high-temperature cuprate materials is widely discussed for the last years. We present the mean-field approximation results for the spin-pseudospin model accounting for the on-site and inter-site correlations, the antiferromagnetic exchange coupling, the one- and two-particle transport. The explicit form of the equations for the critical temperatures of the most significant order parameters of the model are given.



I
Introduction

Over the past fifteen years, numerous experimental results have shown the presence of various ordered states in the so-called pseudogap region of the cuprate phase diagram.
The relationship between superconductivity and other competing orders remains a hotly debated topic in the physics of high-
T_{c}
cuprate materials
Fradkin2012
.
A minimal model to describe the charge degree of freedom in cuprates was introduced recently
and makes use of the
S=1
pseudospin formalism
Moskvin2011
;
Moskvin2013
.
It implies that for the CuO
4
centers in CuO
2
plane
the on-site Hilbert space reduced to a charge triplet
formed by the three many-electron valence states CuO
{}_{4}^{7-,6-,5-}
(nominally Cu
1+,2+,3+
).
These states are described as the components of the
S=1
pseudospin triplet with
M_{S}={-}1,\,0,\,{+}1
.
Effective pseudospin Hamiltonian of the model cuprate with the addition of the Heisenberg spin-spin exchange coupling
of the
s=1/2
CuO
{}_{4}^{6-}
(Cu
2+
) centers can be written as follows:

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{ch}+\mathcal{H}_{exc}+\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(1)}+\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(2)}-\mu\sum_{i}S_{zi}.

(1)

Here, the first term

\mathcal{H}_{ch}=\Delta\sum_{i}S_{zi}^{2}+V\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle}S_{zi}S_{zj}

(2)

describes the on-site and inter-site nearest-neighbour density-density correlations, respectively,
so that
\Delta=U/2
,
U
being the correlation parameter, and
V>0
.
The sums run over the sites of a 2D square lattice,
\left\langle ij\right\rangle
means the nearest neighbors.
The second term

\mathcal{H}_{exc}=Js^{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}

(3)

is the antiferromagnetic (
J>0
) Heisenberg exchange coupling for the CuO
{}_{4}^{6-}
centers,
where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=P_{0}\mathbf{s}/s
operators take into account the on-site spin density
P_{0}=1-S_{z}^{2}
,
and
\mathbf{s}
is the spin
s=1/2
operator
Panov2016
.
The third term

\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(1)}\;=\;-t_{p}\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle\sigma}\big(P_{i}^{\sigma{+}}P_{j}^{\sigma}+P_{j}^{\sigma{+}}P_{i}^{\sigma}\big)\;-\\
-\;t_{n}\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle\sigma}\big(N_{i}^{\sigma{+}}N_{j}^{\sigma}+N_{j}^{\sigma{+}}N_{i}^{\sigma}\big)\;-\\
{}-\frac{1}{2}\,t_{pn}\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle\sigma}\big(P_{i}^{\sigma{+}}N_{j}^{\sigma}+P_{j}^{\sigma{+}}N_{i}^{\sigma}+N_{i}^{\sigma{+}}P_{j}^{\sigma}+N_{j}^{\sigma{+}}P_{i}^{\sigma}\big)

(4)

with the transfer integrals
t_{p}
,
t_{n}
,
t_{pn}

describes the three types of the correlated ”one-particle” transport

Moskvin2011
;
Moskvin2013
.

\sigma
is the spin index,
\sigma=\,\uparrow,\,\downarrow
,
and the orbital part of the
P
and
N
operators expressed in terms of the pseudospin
S{=}1
operators:

P^{+}\propto\left(S_{+}+T_{+}\right)
,
N^{+}\propto\left(S_{+}-T_{+}\right)
,
T_{+}=S_{z}S_{+}+S_{+}S_{z}
.
The next term

\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(2)}=-t_{b}\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle}\big(S_{{+}i}^{2}S_{{-}j}^{2}+S_{{+}j}^{2}S_{{-}i}^{2}\big)

(5)

with the transfer integral
t_{b}
describes the two-particle (”composite boson”) transport

Moskvin2011
;
Moskvin2013
.
The last term with chemical potential
\mu
is needed to account for the charge density constraint,

nN=\left\langle\sum_{i}S_{zi}\right\rangle=const
.

In this paper we present the mean-field results for the spin-pseudospin model (
1
).
We use the simplest Hartree approximation
and get the explicit form of the equations for the critical temperatures of the most significant order parameters of the model.

II
Mean-field approximation

Using the combinations
B_{x}=S_{-}^{2}+S_{+}^{2}
,
B_{y}=i\left(S_{-}^{2}-S_{+}^{2}\right)
,
we write
\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(2)}
in the form

\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(2)}=-\frac{t_{b}}{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{B}_{j}.

(6)

To simplify further calculations, we will use the Hartree approximation.
This allows us to rewrite the expression for
\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(1)}
as

\mathcal{H}_{tr}^{(1)}=-\frac{t_{p}}{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\mathbf{P}_{i}^{\sigma}\mathbf{P}_{j}^{\sigma}-\frac{t_{n}}{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\mathbf{N}_{i}^{\sigma}\mathbf{N}_{j}^{\sigma}-{}\\
{}-\frac{t_{pn}}{4}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle\sigma}\left(\mathbf{P}_{i}^{\sigma}\mathbf{N}_{j}^{\sigma}+\mathbf{N}_{i}^{\sigma}\mathbf{P}_{j}^{\sigma}\right),

(7)

where
P_{x}^{\sigma}=P^{\sigma}+P^{\sigma{+}}
,
P_{y}^{\sigma}=i\left(P^{\sigma}-P^{\sigma{+}}\right)
,

N_{x}^{\sigma}=N^{\sigma}+N^{\sigma{+}}
,
N_{y}^{\sigma}=i\left(N^{\sigma}-N^{\sigma{+}}\right)
.

We use the mean-field approximation and the Bogolubov inequality for the grand potential
\Omega(\mathcal{H})
:

\Omega(\mathcal{H})=\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{0})+\left\langle\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{H}_{0}\right\rangle
,
to estimate the free energy of a system per one site,
f=\Omega/N+\mu n
.
Within a two-sublattice (
A
and
B
) approximation, we introduce the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{0}

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\sum_{c=1}^{N/2}\mathcal{H}_{c},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{c}=\mathcal{H}_{A}+\mathcal{H}_{B},

(8)

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}=\Delta S_{z\alpha}^{2}-\left(h_{z}\pm h_{z}^{a}\right)S_{z\alpha}-\left(\mathbf{g}\pm\mathbf{g}^{a}\right)\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha}-\left(\mathbf{h}_{2}\pm\mathbf{h}_{2}^{a}\right)\mathbf{B}_{\alpha}-{}\\
{}-\sum_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{h}_{p}^{\sigma}\pm\mathbf{h}_{p}^{a,\sigma}\right)\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}-\sum_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{h}_{n}^{\sigma}\pm\mathbf{h}_{n}^{a,\sigma}\right)\mathbf{N}_{\alpha}^{\sigma},

(9)

where
\alpha=A,B
, the upper (lower) sign corresponds to
A
(
B
) sublattice, and

h_{z}
,
h_{z}^{a}
,
\mathbf{g}
,
\mathbf{g}^{a}
,
\mathbf{h}_{2}
,
\mathbf{h}_{2}^{a}
,

\mathbf{h}_{p}^{\sigma}
,
\mathbf{h}_{p}^{a,\sigma}
,
\mathbf{h}_{n}^{\sigma}
,
\mathbf{h}_{n}^{a,\sigma}

(
\sigma=\,\uparrow,\,\downarrow
)
are the molecular fields.
Using the partition function
Z_{c}=\mathrm{Tr\,}\left[\exp\left(-\beta\mathcal{H}_{c}\right)\right]
,
\beta=1/T
,
we obtain the expressions for the charge density
n
and the order parameters:

n=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial h_{z}},\qquad a=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial h_{z}^{a}},

(10)

\mathbf{m}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{g}},\qquad\mathbf{l}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{g}^{a}},

(11)

\mathbf{B}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{2}},\qquad\mathbf{B}_{a}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{2}^{a}},

(12)

\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{p}^{\sigma}},\qquad\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{p}^{a,\sigma}},

(13)

\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{n}^{\sigma}},\qquad\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z_{c}}{\partial\mathbf{h}_{n}^{a,\sigma}}.

(14)

The free energy per one site is given by

f=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\ln Z_{c}+2V\left(n^{2}-a^{2}\right)+{}\\
{}+2Js^{2}\left(\mathbf{m}^{2}-\mathbf{l}^{2}\right)-t_{b}\left(\mathbf{B}^{2}-\mathbf{B}_{a}^{2}\right)-{}\\[5.0pt]
{}-t_{p}\sum_{\sigma}\left({\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}}^{2}-{\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}}^{2}\right)-t_{n}\sum_{\sigma}\left({\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}}^{2}-{\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}}^{2}\right)-{}\\
{}-t_{pn}\sum_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}-\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}\right)+{}\\
{}+h_{z}n+h_{z}^{a}a+\mathbf{g}\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{g}^{a}\mathbf{l}+\mathbf{h}_{2}\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{h}_{2}^{a}\mathbf{B}_{a}+{}\\[5.0pt]
{}+\sum_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{h}_{p}^{\sigma}\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}+\mathbf{h}_{p}^{a,\sigma}\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}+\mathbf{h}_{n}^{\sigma}\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}+\mathbf{h}_{n}^{a,\sigma}\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}\right).

(15)

By minimizing the free energy, we get a system of equations to determine the values of the order parameters:

4Va=h_{z}^{a},\quad-4Js^{2}\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{g},\quad 4Js^{2}\mathbf{l}=\mathbf{g}^{a},

(16)

2t_{b}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{h}_{2},\quad-2t_{b}\mathbf{B}_{a}=\mathbf{h}_{2}^{a},

(17)

2t_{p}\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}+t_{pn}\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{h}_{p}^{\sigma},\quad t_{pn}\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}+2t_{n}\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{h}_{n}^{\sigma},

(18)

-2t_{p}\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}-t_{pn}\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{h}_{p}^{a,\sigma},\quad-t_{pn}\mathbf{P}_{a}^{\sigma}-2t_{n}\mathbf{N}_{a}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{h}_{n}^{a,\sigma}.

(19)

From the stability conditions for a minimum of
f
that corresponds to the high-temperature disordered phase, we obtain the equations for the critical temperatures.

4V=\left.\frac{\partial h_{z}^{a}}{\partial a}\right|_{0},

(20)

-4Js^{2}=\left.\frac{\partial g_{\alpha}}{\partial m_{\alpha}}\right|_{0},\quad 4Js^{2}=\left.\frac{\partial g_{\alpha}^{a}}{\partial l_{\alpha}}\right|_{0},\quad\alpha=x,y,z;

(21)

2t_{b}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{2,\alpha}}{\partial B_{\alpha}}\right|_{0},\quad-2t_{b}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{2,\alpha}^{a}}{\partial B_{a,\alpha}}\right|_{0},\quad\alpha=x,y;

(22)

\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial P_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}+\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial N_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}-2\left(t_{p}+t_{n}\right)\pm{}\\
{}\pm\sqrt{\left(\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial P_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}-\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial N_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}-2\left(t_{p}-t_{n}\right)\right)^{2}+4t_{pn}^{2}}=0,\\
\alpha=x,y;

(23)

\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial P_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}+\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial N_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}+2\left(t_{p}+t_{n}\right)\pm{}\\
{}\pm\sqrt{\left(\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial P_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}-\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial N_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}+2\left(t_{p}-t_{n}\right)\right)^{2}+4t_{pn}^{2}}=0,\\
\alpha=x,y.

(24)

Here, the index 0 denotes the minimum of the high-temperature disordered phase,
where all molecular fields are zero, except for
h_{z}
.
In equations (
23
,
24
) we used that

\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial N_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial P_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=0,\quad\alpha=x,y;

(25)

\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial N_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial P_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=0,\quad\alpha=x,y.

(26)

The proof of this statement is given in the Appendix.
In the case
t_{pn}=0
, the equations (
23
,
24
) have the following form

2t_{p}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial P_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0},\quad 2t_{n}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{\sigma}}{\partial N_{\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0},\quad\alpha=x,y;

(27)

-2t_{p}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{p,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial P_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0},\quad-2t_{n}=\left.\frac{\partial h_{n,\alpha}^{a,\sigma}}{\partial N_{a,\alpha}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0},\quad\alpha=x,y.

(28)

III
Critical temperatures

In the high-temperature disordered phase, all molecular fields are zero, except for
h_{z}
.
The partition function is given by

Z_{c}=4\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}\right)^{2},

(29)

where
\delta=\beta\Delta
,
\eta_{z}=\beta h_{z}
. We can find
n
using (
10
),
and get an explicit expression for the molecular field
h_{z}
:

\eta_{z}=\beta h_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\left(1+n\right)\left(\phi+n\right)}{\left(1-n\right)\left(\phi-n\right)},

(30)

where
\phi=\sqrt{\left(1-n^{2}\right)e^{-2\delta}+n^{2}}
.

Given the signs of the
V
,
J
and the transfer integrals
t_{p}
,
t_{n}
,
t_{pn}
and
t_{b}
, non-trivial solutions for the critical temperatures exist for the following order parameters:

a
,
\mathbf{l}
,
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{P}^{\sigma}
,
\mathbf{N}^{\sigma}
.
Taking into account the isotropy of the exchange interaction and transfer,
we write the explicit analytical form of the equations for the critical temperatures of

a
,
l_{z}
,
B_{x}
,
P_{x}^{\sigma}
and
N_{x}^{\sigma}
.

In order to find the critical temperatures of the charge ordering,
T_{CO}
, we take a small variation of
h_{z}^{a}

at the minimum of the high-temperature disordered phase
and obtain the partition function to be

Z_{c}=4\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\left(\eta_{z}+\eta_{a}\right)\right)\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\left(\eta_{z}-\eta_{a}\right)\right),

(31)

where
\eta_{a}=\beta h_{z}^{a}
. This allows us to write the equation (
20
) as

4\nu=\frac{\left(e^{\delta}+\cosh\eta_{z}\right)^{2}}{e^{\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}+1},

(32)

where
\nu=\beta V
. Using (
30
), we obtain the equation for
T_{CO}
in the form:

4\nu\left(1-n^{2}\right)=1+\phi^{-1}.

(33)

This equation reproduce the result of our recent work
Panov2019
, and generalize the well-known result for the critical temperatures of the charge ordering for the hard-core bosons
Micnas1990
.

The variation of
g_{z}^{a}

at the minimum of the high-temperature disordered phase
to find the equation for the critical temperature of antiferromagnetic ordering,
T_{AFM}
, gives the partition function

Z_{c}=4\left(\cosh\gamma_{a}+e^{-\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}\right)^{2},

(34)

where
\gamma_{a}=\beta g_{z}^{a}
. The equation (
21
) for
l_{z}
takes the form

4j=1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}

(35)

where
j=\beta Js^{2}
. Using (
30
), we obtain the equation for
T_{AFM}
in the form:

4j\left(1-n^{2}\right)=1+\phi.

(36)

This equation was also found in
Panov2019
.

The variation of
h_{2}

at the minimum of the high-temperature disordered phase
yields the partition function

Z_{c}=4\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\sqrt{\eta_{z}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2},

(37)

where
\eta_{2}=\beta h_{2}
. The equation (
21
) for
B_{x}
takes the form

2\tau n=\eta_{z},

(38)

where
\tau=\beta t_{b}
, and from (
30
)
we obtain the equation for the superconducting temperature
T_{B}

(or the critical temperature of superfluidity of the composite bosons)

4\tau n=\ln\frac{\left(1+n\right)\left(\phi+n\right)}{\left(1-n\right)\left(\phi-n\right)}.

(39)

This equation generalizes the well-known result for the superconducting temperature of the preformed local pairs (or the critical temperature of superfluidity of the charged hard-core bosons)
Micnas1990
.

Using the same considerations for the

where

,

.
This yields

\left.\frac{\partial\eta_{p}^{\sigma}}{\partial P_{x}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=\frac{\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}\right)\left(\delta-\eta_{z}\right)}{1-e^{-\delta+\eta_{z}}}.

Taking into account (
30
) and the first equation (
27
),
we obtain the critical temperature
T_{p}
for the case
t_{pn}=0
in the form

2\tau_{p}=\frac{1+\phi}{\left(1+n\right)\left(1-2n-\phi\right)}\ln\frac{1-n}{\phi+n}.

(42)

where
\tau_{p}=\beta t_{p}
.
For the order parameters
N_{x}^{\sigma}
,
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
, we get

\left.\frac{\partial\eta_{n}^{\sigma}}{\partial N_{x}^{\sigma}}\right|_{0}=\frac{\left(1+e^{-\delta}\cosh\eta_{z}\right)\left(\delta+\eta_{z}\right)}{1-e^{-\delta-\eta_{z}}},

(43)

where
\eta_{n}^{\sigma}=\beta h_{n,x}^{\sigma}
,
and the second equation (

) yields the critical temperature
T_{n}
for the case

where

(

Appendix

If the parameter
t_{pn}\neq 0
, then it is necessary to analyze the case of simultaneous variation of
\eta_{p}^{\sigma}
and
\eta_{n}^{\sigma}
.
The molecular fields
\eta_{z}
,
\eta_{p}^{\sigma}
,
\eta_{n}^{\sigma}
and the order parameters
n
,
P^{\sigma}
and
N^{\sigma}
in this case are related by the equations

n=\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\eta_{z}},\quad P^{\sigma}=\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\eta_{p}^{\sigma}},\quad N^{\sigma}=\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\eta_{n}^{\sigma}},

(45)

where the partition function

Z=\sum_{l=1}^{4}e^{-\lambda_{l}}

(46)

expressed through the roots
\lambda_{l}
,
l=1,\ldots 4
, of the secular equation

\Phi\equiv\lambda^{4}-2\delta\lambda^{3}+\lambda^{2}\left(\delta^{2}-\eta_{z}^{2}-\eta_{p}^{2}-\eta_{n}^{2}\right)+{}\\
+\lambda\left(\delta\left(\eta_{p}^{2}+\eta_{n}^{2}\right)+\eta_{z}\left(\eta_{p}^{2}-\eta_{n}^{2}\right)\right)+\left(\eta_{p}^{\uparrow}\eta_{n}^{\uparrow}-\eta_{p}^{\downarrow}\eta_{n}^{\downarrow}\right)^{2}=0.

(47)

where
\eta_{\alpha}^{2}={\eta_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}}^{2}+{\eta_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}}^{2}
,
\alpha=p,n
.
Differentiating the equations (
45
) by
P^{\sigma}
, we get the system

\sum_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}\ln Z}{\partial\eta_{i}\partial\eta_{j}}\frac{\partial\eta_{j}}{\partial P^{\sigma}}=\delta_{x_{i},P^{\sigma}},\;\eta_{i},\eta_{j}=\eta_{z},\eta_{p}^{\uparrow},\eta_{p}^{\downarrow},\eta_{n}^{\uparrow},\eta_{n}^{\downarrow},

(48)

where
x_{i}=n,P^{\uparrow},P^{\downarrow},N^{\uparrow},N^{\downarrow}
.
The matrix elements in (
48
) are

\frac{\partial^{2}\ln Z}{\partial\eta_{i}\partial\eta_{j}}=-\frac{1}{Z^{2}}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\eta_{i}}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\eta_{j}}+\frac{1}{Z}\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial\eta_{i}\partial\eta_{j}},

(49)

and we will use that

\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\eta_{i}}=-\sum_{l=1}^{4}e^{-\lambda_{l}}\frac{\partial\lambda_{l}}{\partial\eta_{i}},

(50)

\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial\eta_{i}\partial\eta_{j}}=\sum_{l=1}^{4}e^{-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{\partial\lambda_{l}}{\partial\eta_{i}}\frac{\partial\lambda_{l}}{\partial\eta_{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\lambda_{l}}{\partial\eta_{i}\partial\eta_{j}}\right).

(51)

From (
47
) we obtain

\frac{d\Phi}{d\eta_{i}}=0=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\eta_{i}}+\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\lambda}\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial\eta_{i}},

(52)

and see that

\left.\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\eta_{i}}\right|_{0}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\left.\frac{\partial\lambda_{l}}{\partial\eta_{i}}\right|_{0}=0\;\Rightarrow\;\left.\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\eta_{i}}\right|_{0}=0,\;\eta_{i}=\eta_{p}^{\sigma},\eta_{n}^{\sigma}.

(53)

Here point 0 is the minimum of the high-temperature disordered phase,
where all molecular fields are zero, except for
\eta_{z}
.

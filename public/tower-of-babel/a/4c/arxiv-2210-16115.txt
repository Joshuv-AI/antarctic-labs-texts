A new blow-up criterion for the 2D full compressible Navier-Stokes equations without heat conduction in a bounded domain
Jie Fan 1 , Quansen Jiu 2

Abstract




2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B65, 35D30, 76D05

abstract

This paper is to derive a new blow-up criterion for the 2D full compressible Navier-Stokes equations without heat conduction in terms of the
density
\rho
and the pressure
P
. More precisely, it indicates that in a bounded domain the strong solution exists globally if the norm

\|\rho||_{{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})}}+||P||_{L^{p_{0}}(0,t;L^{\infty})}<\infty
for some constant
p_{0}
satisfying
1<p_{0}\leq 2
. The boundary condition is imposed as a Navier-slip boundary one and the initial vacuum is permitted. Our result extends previous one which is stated as
\|\rho||_{{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})}}+||P||_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})}<\infty
.

1.
Introduction

The full compressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n=2,3)
bounded or unbounded read as:

\begin{cases}&\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
&\rho u_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\text{div\,}u=0,\\
&c_{v}[\rho\theta_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla\theta]+P\text{div\,}u-\kappa\Delta\theta=\frac{\mu}{2}\left|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\text{tr}}\right|^{2}+\lambda(\text{div\,}u)^{2},\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

with the following initial data

(\rho,u,\theta)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})\quad\text{in}~~\Omega,

and the Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle

\displaystyle u=0,~~\theta=0\quad\text{on}~~\partial\Omega,

(1.2)

or the Neumann boundary condition on
\theta
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle u=0,~~\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial n}=0,~\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega.

(1.3)

Here,
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\Omega
is the spatial coordinate and
t>0
is the time,
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=(u_{1}(x,t),...,u_{n}(x,t))
stand for the flow density and velocity respectively, and the pressure
P
is determined by

\displaystyle P=R\rho\theta,R>0.

(1.4)

The positive constant
c_{v}
and
\kappa
are respectively the heat capacity, the thermal conductivity coefficient.
\mu
and
\lambda
are the coefficients of viscosity, which are assumed to be constants, satisfying the following
physical restrictions:

\mu>0,\ 2\mu+n\lambda\geq 0.

(1.5)

There are a large number of literatures about the well-posedness to the compressible Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
). Matsumura-Nishida
[
26
]
first obtained the global classical solution when initial data is close to a non-vacuum equilibrium and the solution has
small perturbations from a uniform nonvacuum state. Later, in the absence of vacuum the global existence of weak solutions for discontinuous initial data
was studied by Hoff
[
13
]
. For isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations, Lions
[
25
]
obtained the existence of global weak solutions with
\gamma\geq\frac{3N}{N+2}
for
N=2,3.
Feireisl
[
11
]
extended Lion’s result with
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
and Jiang-Zhang
[
20
]
relaxed the restriction for
\gamma>1
with spherical symmetric initial data. For the initial data far away from vacuum, Danchin
[
5
]
established the local well-posedness in critical
spaces. If vacuum is taken into account, the local existence of a strong solution with initial data containing the compatibility condition was established by Cho and Kim

[
3
]
. For the Cauchy problem or for the initial-boundary value problem of 3D and 2D barotropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations,
Huang-Li-Xin
[
18
]
, Li-Xin
[
24
]
and Cai-Li
[
4
]
established the global existence of classical solutions for initial data with small
energy but possibly large oscillations and containing vacuum states, respectively. Xin
[
31
]
(see
[
23
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
32
]
for further states) first proved
that full compressible Navier-Stokes equations with
\kappa=0
will blow up in finite time if the initial density has compact support. For this reason, many studies are devoted to
the mechanism of blow up and structure of possible singularities of strong solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
More precisely, suppose that
0<T^{*}<\infty
is the maximal time of existence of a strong solution of system (
1.1
). Then when
\kappa>0,
kinds of blow-up criteria have been established, of which some are listed as follows:

Fan-Jiang-Ou
[
7
]
:

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\right)=\infty,~~~(\lambda<7\mu);

Sun-Wang-Zhang
[
30
]

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left(\|(\rho,\frac{1}{\rho},\theta)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\right)=\infty,~~~(\lambda<7\mu);

Jiu-Wang-Ye
[

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\theta\|_{L^{p}(0,t;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)=\infty,~\text{with}~~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2,\ \ q>\frac{3}{2}~~(\lambda<3\mu);

(1.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\text{div\,}u\|_{L^{2}(0,t;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\theta\|_{L^{p}(0,t;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big)=\infty,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\text{with}~~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2,\ \ q>\frac{3}{2};

Fang-Zi-Zhang
[

:

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\Big)=\infty

(1.8)

for smooth bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and boundary condition (
1.2

Fan-Jiu-Wang
[
8

:

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\theta||_{L^{p}(0,t;L^{q}(\Omega))}\Big)=\infty,~\text{with}~~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,\ \ 2\leq q\leq\infty

for smooth bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and boundary condition (
1.3
).

Feireisl-Wen-Zhu
[
12

removed the technical conditions in
[
22

relating the values of the shear and bulk viscosity coefficients, and proved

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\theta\|_{L^{p}(0,t;L^{q}(\Omega))}\Big)=\infty,~\text{with}~~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2,\ \ q\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty]

(1.9)

for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or boundary condition (
1.3
).

This paper is denoted to establishing some blow-up criteria for the 2D full compressible Navier-Stokes equations without heat conduction (
\kappa=0
) in a bounded domain. Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. When
\kappa=0
and take
c_{v}=R=1
for simplicity, the system (
1.1
) can
be rewritten as

\begin{cases}&\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
&\rho u_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\text{div\,}u=0,\\
&P_{t}+\text{div}(Pu)+P\text{div}u=\frac{\mu}{2}\left|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\text{tr}}\right|^{2}+\lambda(\text{div}u)^{2}.\end{cases}

(1.10)

Here
\mu
and
\lambda
are the coefficients of viscosity, which are assumed to be constants, satisfying the following physical restrictions:

\displaystyle\mu>0,\,\,\mu+\lambda\geq 0.

(1.11)

System (
1.10
)-(
1.11
) is supplemented with the initial data

\displaystyle\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad{\rho u(x,0)=m_{0}(x),}\quad{P(x,0)=P_{0},}~~x\in\Omega,

(1.12)

and Navier-slip boundary conditions:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle u\cdot{n}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad{\text{curl}u=0}\quad\text{on}\quad{\partial\Omega,}

(1.13)

where
n=(n_{1},n_{2})
is the unit outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega.

Suppose that
0<T^{*}<\infty
is the maximal time for the existence of a strong solution to (
1.10
).
Huang
[
19
]
established a blowup criterion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or in a bounded domain with the Dirichlet boundary condition for the velocity:

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\theta||_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\Big)=\infty,~~~(\mu>4\lambda).

Zhong
[
35
,
36
]
obtained a blowup criterion in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or in a bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the Dirichlet boundary condition for the velocity:

\displaystyle\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+||P||_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\Big)=\infty.

(1.14)

Wang
[
34
]
proved that

\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\Big(\|\rho||_{{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}+\|\theta||_{L^{2}(0,t;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\Big)=\infty.

After some modifications, the local existence and uniqueness of the strong solution to (
1.10
)-(
1.13
) with initial data containing vacuum can be established as in
[
3
]
, which can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty).
Assume that
the initial data satisfies

\rho_{0}\in W^{1,q},\,\,u_{0}\in D^{1}\cap D^{2},\,\,P_{0}\in W^{1,q},

and the following compatibility condition:

\displaystyle-\mu\triangle u_{0}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\text{div}u_{0}+\nabla P=\sqrt{\rho}_{0}g,

(1.15)

for some
g\in L^{2}
. Then there exist a positive
T_{0}
and a unique strong solution
(\rho,u,P)
to (
1.10
)-(
1.13
) such that,

\begin{cases}&\rho\geq 0,\rho\in C([0,T_{0}];W^{1,q}),\,\,\rho_{t}\in C([0,T_{0}];L^{q}),\\
&u\in C([0,T_{0}];D^{1}\cap D^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};D^{2,q}),\\
&\sqrt{\rho}u,\,\,\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}),\\
&P\geq 0,\,\,P\in C([0,T_{0}];W^{1,q}),\,\,P_{t}\in C([0,T_{0}];L^{q}).\end{cases}

(1.16)

Then our main results can be stated as:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(\rho,u,P)
is the unique strong solution in Theorem
1.1
to (
1.10
)-(
1.13
). If the maximal
existence time
T^{\ast}
is finite, then there holds

\displaystyle\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left(\|\rho||_{{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})}}+||P||_{L^{p_{0}}(0,t;L^{\infty})}\right)=\infty,

(1.17)

where
p_{0}
is some constant satisfying
1<p_{0}\leq 2.

Remark 1.1
.

In comparision with
[
19
]
in which a blow-up criterion in terms of the upper bound of the temperature was obtained under the condition
\mu>4\lambda,
there is no need to impose the technical conditions relating the values of the viscosity coefficients
\mu,\lambda
as well as we establish a blow-up criterion in terms of the integrability of the pressure. This result gives an answer of Nash’s conjecture proposed in
[
28
]
without thermal conductivity, which says:

”Probably one should first try to prove a conditional existence and uniqueness theorem for flow equations. This should give existence, smoothness, and continuation (in time) of flows, conditional on the non-appearance of
certain gross type of singularity, such as infinities of temperature or density.”

Remark 1.2
.

Due to the fact that
||P||_{L^{p_{0}}(0,t;L^{\infty})}\leq||P||_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})},
we extend Zhong’s result in
[
35
,
36
]
under Navier-slip conditions.

To prove our main result, we apply for the effective viscous flux
F
, and the vorticity
w
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle F\triangleq(2\mu+\lambda)\text{div}u-P,\quad{w\triangleq\text{curl}u=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot{u}=\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1}-\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}}.

(1.18)

The main result is proved by a contradiction argument. Suppose that (
1.17
) was false. Then we first obtain the uniform estimate of
\|\nabla u||_{{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}}.
The second step is to estimate
\|\nabla u||_{{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}}.
In
order to get the
L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}
norm of
\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}
(in Lemma
2.5
), we need to deal with the term
\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}},
where
\dot{u}=u_{t}+u\cdot{\nabla u}
represents the material derivative. And we also need the estimate of
||P||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}.
The effective viscous flux
F
and the vorticity
w
solve the Neumann problem and
the related Dirichlet problem respectively. We use the standard
L^{p}
theory to estimate
\|\nabla F||_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}
and
\|\nabla w||_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}
which plays a
crucial role in estimating
\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}(p=3,p=4).
The main difficulty in carrying out this construction is that integrals on the boundary

\partial\Omega,

which are
\int_{\partial\Omega}F_{t}(\dot{u}\cdot{n})ds
and
\int_{\partial\Omega}u\cdot{\nabla F}(\dot{u}\cdot{n})ds,
appear and are required to be dealt with. To overcome this difficulty, as in Cai-Li
[
4
]
, together with the slip boundary condition
u\cdot{n}|_{\partial\Omega}
, we have

\displaystyle u=(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})n^{\perp},(u\cdot{\nabla})u\cdot{n}=-(u\cdot{\nabla})n\cdot{u},

(1.19)

where
n^{\perp}
is the unit tangential vector on the boundary
\partial\Omega
denoted by

\displaystyle n^{\perp}\triangleq(n_{2},-n_{1}).

Furthermore, using Green formula yields that for
f\in H^{1},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}u\cdot{\nabla f}ds=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})n^{\perp}\cdot{\nabla f}ds

(1.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla f\cdot{\nabla^{\perp}}(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})dx\leq C||f||_{H^{1}}||u||_{H^{1}},

where
\nabla^{\perp}f=(\partial_{2},-\partial_{1})f.

Making full use of (
1.19
) and (
1.20
), we can rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}F((u\cdot{\nabla u})\cdot{\nabla n}\cdot{u})ds

(1.21)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}F(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})n^{\perp}\cdot{\nabla u_{i}\partial_{i}n_{j}u_{j}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{\perp}\cdot{\left(\nabla u_{i}\partial_{i}n_{j}u_{j}F(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})\right)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i}\cdot{\nabla^{\perp}\left(\partial_{i}n_{j}u_{j}F(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})\right)}dx,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(u\cdot{\nabla F})(\dot{u}\cdot{n})ds=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(u\cdot{n^{\perp}})n^{\perp}\cdot{\nabla F(\dot{u}\cdot{n})}ds

(1.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{\perp}\cdot(({u\cdot{n^{\perp}})\nabla F(\dot{u}\cdot{n})})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla F\cdot{\nabla^{\perp}((u\cdot{n^{\perp}})(\dot{u}\cdot{n}))}dx.

According to local well-posedness in Theorem
1.1
, we need to estimate
\|\nabla\rho||_{{L^{\infty}}(0,T;L^{p})}
and
\|\nabla P||_{{L^{\infty}}(0,T;L^{p})}
by solving a logarithmic Gronwall inequality, where
p>2.

\|\nabla\rho||_{{L^{\infty}}(0,T;L^{p})}
can be determined by
\|\nabla u||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty})}
due to the scalar hyperbolic structure of

\eqref{FNS}_{1}.

For
\|\nabla P||_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}
, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}\leq C(||P||_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}})(\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}}).

(1.23)

Thus, in order to control the norm of
\|\nabla P||_{{L^{\infty}}(0,T;L^{p})}
, it suffices to get the upper bound of
\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}}.

Indeed, we will obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p^{2}-2}}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}),

(1.24)

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p^{2}(p-2)}{2(p-1)(p^{2}-2)}}_{L^{2}}+||P||_{L^{\infty}}\right)\log(e+\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}})+C\|\nabla u||_{L^{2}}+C.

(1.25)

where
\frac{p(p-2)}{p^{2}-2},\frac{p^{2}(p-2)}{2(p-1)(p^{2}-2)}\in(0,1).

Then it yields

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|\nabla u||_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}}

(1.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p^{2}-2}}_{L^{2}}\left(1+\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p^{2}(p-2)}{2(p-1)(p^{2}-2)}}_{L^{2}}+||P||_{L^{\infty}}\right)\log(e+\|\nabla^{2}u||_{L^{p}})+\quad\text{other}\quad\text{terms}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+\quad\text{other}\quad\text{terms}.

It should be remarked that in the process of our proof, we just used
||P||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty})}
during estimating the low regularity of the velocity. When we get the improved regularity of the density and pressure, we need
||P||_{L^{p_{0}}(0,T;L^{\infty})}
,where
p_{0}
is some constant satisfying
1<p_{0}\leq 2.

Putting (
1.26
) into (
1.23
) leads to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}

(1.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p^{2}(p-2)}{2(p-1)(p^{2}-2)}}_{L^{2}}+||P||_{L^{\infty}}\right)\log\left(e+\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p^{2}-2}}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}\right)\left(\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p^{2}-2}}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}+1\right).

Together with Young inequality we have,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla P||_{L^{p}}\leq C||P||^{p_{0}}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\dot{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+\quad\text{other}\quad\text{terms},

(1.28)

where
p_{0}
is some constant satisfying
1<p_{0}\leq 2
, which leads to the estimates of
\|\nabla P||_{L^{\infty}}(0,T;L^{p}).

Some notations are introduced. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L^{p}(\Omega)
represents the usual
Lebesgue spaces. For a nonnegative integer

\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=0}^{k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

A function

and

For simplicity, we write

.

2.
Regularity of the velocity and pressure

We will prove our main result by contradiction arguments.
Suppose that the assertion of the Theorem
1.2
was false, namely

\displaystyle\lim\sup\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left(\|\rho||_{{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{\infty})}}+||P||_{L^{p_{0}}(0,t;L^{\infty})}\right)\leq M,

(2.1)

where
p_{0}
is some constant satisfying
1<p_{0}\leq 2,
and
M
is a finite number.

The div-curl control in
[
1
,
27
,
33
]
will be used to get the estimate of
\|\nabla u||_{L^{p}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega.
For
u\in W^{1,q},
if
\Omega
is simply connected and
u\cdot{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega,
then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u||_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\|\text{div}~u||_{L^{p}}+\|\text{curl}~u||_{L^{p}}\right).

(2.2)

The following is the standard energy estimate.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that (
2.1
) is valid. Then there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho|u|^{2}+P\right)dx+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\leq C,

(2.3)

where C denotes generic positive constant depending only on
\Omega,

M,

\lambda,

\mu
,
T^{*}

and the initial data for any
T\in[0,T^{\ast}).

Proof.

The pressure
P
satisfies

\displaystyle P_{t}+u\cdot{\nabla P}+2P\text{div}u=G\triangleq\frac{\mu}{2}\left|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\text{tr}}\right|^{2}+\lambda(\text{div}u)^{2}\geq 0.

(2.4)

Define the particle path before blow-up time

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}X(x,t)=u(X(x,t),t),\\
X(x,0)=x.\end{cases}

(2.5)

Along with the particle path, we obtain from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P(X(x,t),t)=-2P\text{div}u+G,

which implies

\displaystyle P(X(x,t),t)=\exp\left(-2\int_{0}^{t}\text{div}uds\right)\left[P_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(2\int_{0}^{s}\text{div}ud\tau\right)Gds\right]\geq 0.

Integrating (
2.4
) over
\Omega
and using slip boundary condition (
1.13
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}Pdx=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}P\text{div}udx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\left|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\text{tr}}\right|^{2}+\lambda(\text{div}u)^{2}\right)dx

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||P||^{2}_{L^{2}}+C_{0}\|\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||P||_{L^{\infty}}||P||_{L^{1}}+C_{0}\|\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
C_{0}
is contant depending on
\lambda,\mu.

In view of
\Delta u=\nabla\text{div}u+\nabla^{\perp}w,
we rewrite the equation of conservation of momentum
\eqref{FNSZ}_{2}
as

\displaystyle\rho\dot{u}+\nabla P=(2\mu+\lambda)\nabla\text{div}u+\mu\nabla^{\perp}w.

(2.7)

Multiplying (
2.7
) by
u
and integrating over
\Omega
, together with the boundary conition (
1.13
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|u|^{2}dx+(2\mu+\lambda)\int_{\Omega}(\text{div}u)^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}w^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}P\text{div}udx

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||P||_{L^{\infty}}||P||_{L^{1}}+\frac{\epsilon}{4}\|\nabla u||^{2}_{L^{2}},

together with (
2.2
), we obtain after choosing
\epsilon
suitably small that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle C||P||_{L^{\infty}}||P||_{L^{1}}.

(2.9)

Multiplying (
2.9
) by
(C_{0}+1)
and then adding to (
2.6
), one gets that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left(P+(C_{0}+1)\rho|u|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C||P||_{L^{\infty}}||P||_{L^{1}}.

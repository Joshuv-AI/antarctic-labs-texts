A Fuglede type theorem for Fourier multiplier operators
Ben de Pagter, Werner J. Ricker, Dedicated in admiration to Jaap Korevaar, on the occasion of his 100-th birthday

Abstract
Let E E be a translation invariant Banach function space over an infinite compact abelian group G G and M φ M_{\varphi} be a Fourier multiplier operator (with symbol φ \varphi ) acting on E. It is assumed that E E has order continuous norm and that E E is reflection invariant (which ensures that φ ¯ \bar{\varphi} is also a multiplier symbol for E E ). The following Fuglede type theorem is established. Whenever T T is a bounded linear operator on E E satisfying M φ ​ T = T ​ M φ M_{\varphi}T=TM_{\varphi} , then also M φ ¯ ​ T = T ​ M φ ¯ M_{\bar{\varphi}}T=TM_{\bar{\varphi}} .

1
Introduction

A classical result of B. Fuglede states if
A
is a bounded normal operator
on a Hilbert space
H
and
T
is a bounded linear operator on
H

satisfying
TA=AT
, then
TA^{\ast}=A^{\ast}T
,
[
7
]
. More generally,
C.R. Putnam showed if
A
and
B
are bounded normal operators on
H
such
that
AT=TB
, then
A^{\ast}T=TB^{\ast}
,
[
16
]
. This more general
version actually follows from the original Fuglede theorem,
[
3
]
.
Various different proofs of Fuglede’s theorem are known; see, for example,

[
6
]
, p. 934,
[
8
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.

An abstraction of the Fuglede theorem could be as follows. Let
X
be a
Banach space and
A
belong to a subalgebra of the bounded linear operators
on
X
which has an involution
\sharp
. Does it follows that
TA^{\sharp}=A^{\sharp}T
whenever
T
is a bounded linear operator on
X
satisfying
TA=AT
? For
X=L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
, with
1\leq p<\infty
and
\mathbb{T}
the circle group, let
\mathcal{M}_{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)

denote the algebra of all bounded functions
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
having the property that, for every
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
there exists
g\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
, necessarily
unique, such that its Fourier transform satisfies
\hat{g}=\varphi\hat{f}

on
\mathbb{Z}
. Denoting
g
by
M_{\varphi}^{\left(p\right)}f
, the
bounded linear operator
M_{\varphi}^{\left(p\right)}
so generated on
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
is called the
p
-multiplier operator
corresponding to
\varphi
. It is well known that the complex conjugate
\bar{\varphi}
of
\varphi
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
whenever
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
.
Clearly
M_{\varphi}^{\left(p\right)}\longmapsto M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{\left(p\right)}
is an involution on the algebra of all
p
-multiplier
operators on
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
. In this setting the ”Fuglede
theorem” is indeed valid,
[
13
]
.

The aim of this note is to give a far reaching generalization of the above
result beyond the setting of
L^{p}
-spaces. The group
\mathbb{T}
can be
replaced with any infinite compact abelian group
G
and the
L^{p}
-spaces
can be replaced by the significantly larger class of
translation
invariant Banach function spaces

E
over
G
. However, for this class of
spaces
E
three phenomena arise which do not occur for
L^{p}
-spaces over
G
. First, the space
E
need
not
contain
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
. Second, the reflection of a function in
E
need not belong to
E
(which has the effect that
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)

need not imply that
\bar{\varphi}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
) and third, the space of all continuous functions
C\left(G\right)
on
G
, even if
C\left(G\right)\subseteq E
, need not be dense
in
E
. However, for the very large and important class of spaces
E
which

do
have all three of these properties it is established in Theorem
5.2
that the ”Fuglede theorem” does hold.

Since the methods used rely heavily of the fact that the spaces
E
are all

Banach function spaces
over
G
(in particular, Banach lattices)
and that certain non-trivial properties of
E
arising from translation
invariance have to be developed and established, we need to include some
sections to address these features. An attempt has been made to keep the
note as self-contained as possible.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we collect together some definitions and facts concerning
Banach function spaces. Let
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
be a
measure space. Since we are interested in the case where
\Omega=G
is an
infinite compact abelian group with normalized Haar measure
\mu
, we will
assume that
\mu\left(\Omega\right)=1
and that
\mu
is atomless. We
denote by
L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
the space of all
\mathbb{C}
-valued,
\Sigma
-measurable functions (with the usual identification of functions
which are equal
\mu
-a.e. on
\Omega
). Then
L^{0}\left(\mu\right)

is a Dedekind complete complex Riesz space (or, vector lattice). Recall that
a subset
A\subseteq L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
is called an (order)
ideal
whenever
A
is a linear subspace of
L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
with
the property that
\left|f\right|\leq\left|g\right|
,
with
f\in L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
and
g\in A
, implies that
f\in A
.

Definition 2.1

A
Banach function space
(briefly B.f.s.) over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
is a Banach space
\left(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}\right)
, where
E\subseteq L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
is an ideal and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}
is an absolutely monotone norm on
E

(that is,
\left\|f\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{E}

whenever
\left|f\right|\leq\left|g\right|
in
E
).

Evidently, B.f.s.’ are complex (Dedekind complete) Banach lattices.
Therefore, we can freely apply the general theory of Banach lattices to
B.f.s.’ For the theory of Banach lattices we refer the reader to
[
1
]
,

[
12
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
23
]
, and for the theory of B.f.s.’ to
[
22
]

(Chapter 15),
[
2
]
. The class of B.f.s.’ includes the
L^{p}
-spaces (
1\leq p\leq\infty
), Orlicz spaces, Lorentz spaces, Marcinkiewicz spaces
and many more. If
E
and
F
are two B.f.s.’ over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
satisfying
E\subseteq F
, then the natural embedding of
E

into
F
is continuous. Indeed, the embedding operator is linear and
positive, and a positive linear operator on a Banach lattice is
automatically continuous (see e.g. Theorem 83.12 in
[
23
]
).

Remark 2.2

In Chapter 15 of
[
22
]
and in the series of papers
[
11
]
, B.f.s.’
are introduced via function norms. We briefly recall this approach. Denote
by
M\left(\mu\right)
the set of all (equivalence classes of) extended
\mathbb{C}
-valued measurable functions on
\Omega
(that is, every
f\in M\left(\mu\right)
is of the form
f=g+ih
with
g,h:\Omega\rightarrow\left[-\infty,\infty\right]
measurable). The set of all
\left[0,\infty\right]
-valued functions in
M\left(\mu\right)
is denoted by
M\left(\mu\right)^{+}
. A
function norm

\rho
is defined to be a
map
\rho:M\left(\mu\right)^{+}\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right]

satisfying:

(i)

If
u\in M\left(\mu\right)^{+}
and
\rho\left(u\right)=0
,
then
u=0
;

(ii)

\rho\left(\lambda u\right)=\lambda\rho\left(u\right)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
u\in M\left(\mu\right)^{+}
;

(iii)

\rho\left(u+v\right)\leq\rho\left(u\right)+\rho\left(v\right)
for all
u,v\in M\left(\mu\right)^{+}
;

(iv)

\rho\left(u\right)\leq\rho\left(v\right)
whenever
u\leq v
in
M\left(\mu\right)^{+}
.

If
u\in M\left(u\right)^{+}
and
\rho\left(u\right)<\infty
, then
u\left(x\right)<\infty
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
(i.e.,
u\in L^{0}\left(\mu\right)^{+}
); see
[
22
]
, Section 63, Theorem 1.
Define

E_{\rho}=\left\{f\in M\left(\mu\right):\rho\left(\left|f\right|\right)<\infty\right\}.

(1)

Then
E_{\rho}
is an ideal in
L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
. Setting
\left\|f\right\|_{E_{\rho}}=\rho\left(\left|f\right|\right)
for all
f\in E_{\rho}
, the space
\left(E_{\rho},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E_{\rho}}\right)
is a normed space. If
E_{\rho}
is
complete, then it is a B.f.s.

Conversely, if
\left(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}\right)
is a
B.f.s. and we set

\rho\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left\|u\right\|_{E}&\text{if}&u\in E^{+}\\
\infty&\text{if}&u\notin E^{+}\end{array}\right.,\ \ \ u\in M\left(\mu\right)^{+},

then
\rho
is a function norm and the space
E
is recovered via (
1
). This shows that the approach in
[
22
]
is equivalent to ours
and so, all results from
[
22
]
may be used in our setting (see also

[
23
]
, Section 112).

Given a B.f.s.
\left(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}\right)
over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
, there exists a set
A_{0}\in\Sigma

with the property that
f=0

\mu
-a.e. on
\Omega\backslash A_{0}
for
each
f\in E
and, for every
A\in\Sigma
satisfying
A\subseteq A_{0}

and
\mu\left(A\right)>0
, there exists
B\in\Sigma
such that
B\subseteq A
,
\mu\left(B\right)>0
and
\chi_{B}\in E
(see see
[
22
]
, Section 72 or
[
23
]
, Section 86). The set
A_{0}
is uniquely
determined (modulo
\mu
-null sets) and is called the
carrier
of
E
. Moreover, there exists a sequence
\left(A_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

in
\Sigma
such that
A_{n}\subseteq A_{0}
and
\chi_{A_{n}}\in E
for
all
n
, and
A_{n}\uparrow_{n}A_{0}
. Any B.f.s. that we encounter in this
note has its carrier equal to
\Omega
. In general, if
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
, then one can replace, without loss of generality,
\Omega
by
A_{0}
.

Recall that a B.f.s.
\left(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}\right)

over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
has
order continuous norm

(briefly o.c.-norm) if
\left\|f_{\tau}\right\|_{E}\downarrow_{\tau}0
whenever
f_{\tau}\downarrow_{\tau}0
in
E
(that is,
\left(f_{\tau}\right)
is a downwards directed net in the positive cone
E^{+}

with infimum
0
). Since
E
is Dedekind complete, it follows from Theorem
103.6 in
[
23
]
that
E
has o.c.-norm if and only if
E
has
\sigma
-o.c.-norm (that is,
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{E}\downarrow_{n}0
for
any sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
E^{+}
satisfying
f_{n}\downarrow_{n}0
). For a sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
E^{+}
the condition
f_{n}\downarrow_{n}0
is equivalent with
f_{n}\left(x\right)\downarrow 0
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
. By way of
example, for
1\leq p<\infty
the space
E=L^{p}\left(\mu\right)
has
o.c.-norm but,
L^{\infty}\left(\mu\right)
does not have o.c.-norm.

Let
\left(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E}\right)
be a B.f.s. over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
with carrier
\Omega
. The
Köthe dual
(or
associate space
)
E^{\times}
of
E
is defined
by

E^{\times}=\left\{g\in L^{0}\left(\mu\right):\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}<\infty\right\},

where

\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left|fg\right|d\mu:f\in E,\left\|f\right\|_{E}\leq 1\right\},\ \ \ g\in L^{0}\left(\mu\right).

(2)

Then
\left(E^{\times},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E^{\times}}\right)

is a B.f.s. over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
with carrier equal to

\Omega
; see
[
22
]
, Sections 68, 69 and also
[
23
]
, p. 418. From
the definition it is also clear that
Hölder’s inequality
holds,
that is,

\left|\int_{\Omega}fg\;d\mu\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|fg\right|d\mu\leq\left\|f\right\|_{E}\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}},\ \ \ f\in E,\ \ g\in E^{\times}.

(3)

According to
[
22
]
, Section 69, Theorem 1, we also have

\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{\Omega}fg\;d\mu\right|:f\in E,\left\|f\right\|_{E}\leq 1\right\},\ \ \ g\in E^{\times}.

(4)

For each
g\in E^{\times}
, define the linear functional
\varphi_{g}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by setting

\varphi_{g}\left(f\right)=\int_{\Omega}fg\;d\mu,\ \ \ f\in E.

It is easy to see that
\varphi_{g}\in E^{\ast}
(the dual Banach space of

E
) and that
\left\|\varphi_{g}\right\|_{E^{\ast}}=\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}
for all
g\in E^{\times}
(see (
4
)).
Consequently, the map
g\longmapsto\varphi_{g}
, for
g\in E^{\times}
,
is an isometric isomorphism from
E^{\times}
into
E^{\ast}
. Via this
map,
E^{\times}
may be identified with a closed subspace of
E^{\ast}

and we frequently denote the
dual pairing
of
E
and
E^{\times}

by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, that is,

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fg\;d\mu,\ \ \ f\in E,\ \ g\in E^{\times}.

(5)

Since the carrier of
E^{\times}
is equal to
\Omega
(whenever the
carrier of
E
is
\Omega
), it follows that
E^{\times}
separates the
points of
E
(but, in general,
E^{\times}
is not norming).

The functionals in
E^{\ast}
which are of the form
\varphi_{g}
for some

g\in E^{\times}
may be identified with the band
E_{n}^{\ast}
in
E^{\ast}
of all order continuous functionals on
E
; see
[
22
]
,
Section 69, Theorem 3, and also
[
23
]
, Section 112. In particular,
E^{\ast}=E^{\times}
if and only if
E
has o.c.-norm (cf. Theorem 2.4.2
in
[
12
]
).

A B.f.s.
E
over
\left(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu\right)
has the
Fatou property
if, for any net
0\leq u_{\alpha}\uparrow_{\alpha}
in
E

satisfying
\sup_{\alpha}\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{E}<\infty
,
there exists
u\in E
such that
0\leq u_{\alpha}\uparrow_{\alpha}u
and
\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{E}\uparrow_{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{E}
. In this case, sequences also suffice; cf.
[
23
]
,
Theorem 113.2. By a theorem of Halperin and Luxemburg (see
[
22
]
,
Section 71, Theorem 1), a B.f.s.
E
has the Fatou property if and only if
E=E^{\times\times}
isometrically. We point out that in
[
2
]
the
Fatou property is included in the definition of a B.f.s.

3
Translation invariant Banach function spaces

Let
G
be an infinite compact abelian group and denote by
\mu
normalized
Haar measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
.
The space of all regular, complex Borel measures in
G
is denoted by
M\left(G\right)
. The group operation is denoted by
+
and the identity
element of
G
is denoted by
0
. Moreover,
\mu\left(-A\right)=\mu\left(A\right)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
. For the
general theory of locally compact abelian groups we refer the reader, for
instance, to
[
10
]
,
[
19
]
. We denote
L^{0}\left(\mu\right)
by
the more traditional notation
L^{0}\left(G\right)
and the corresponding
L^{p}
-spaces by
L^{p}\left(G\right)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. A B.f.s.
over
\left(G,\mathcal{B}\left(G\right),\mu\right)
is simply called a
B.f.s. over
G
. For each
y\in G
, define the
translation operator

\tau_{y}:L^{0}\left(G\right)\rightarrow L^{0}\left(G\right)
by setting

\left(\tau_{y}f\right)\left(x\right)=f\left(x-y\right),\ \ \ x\in G,

for all
f\in L^{0}\left(G\right)
.

Definition 3.1

A
translation invariant B.f.s
over
G
is a B.f.s.
E\subseteq L^{0}\left(G\right)
such that
\tau_{y}f\in E
with
\left\|\tau_{y}f\right\|_{E}=\left\|f\right\|_{E}
for all
y\in G
, whenever
f\in E
.

Such B.f.s.’ include all spaces
L^{p}\left(G\right)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, all Orlicz spaces, Lorentz spaces and Marcinkiewicz spaces over
G
.
Actually, any rearrangement invariant B.f.s. over
G
is translation
invariant. However, there exist plenty of translation invariant B.f.s.’
which are
not
rearrangement invariant. We present an example (see
also Example
3.11
below).

Example 3.2

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two infinite compact abelian groups
(with normalized Haar measure
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, respectively) and
consider the product group
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
(with normalized Haar
measure
\mu=\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}
). Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Define the
function norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p\times q}
on
L^{0}\left(G\right)
by

\left\|f\right\|_{p\times q}=\left(\int_{G_{1}}\left(\int_{G_{2}}\left|f\left(x,y\right)\right|^{q}d\mu_{2}\left(y\right)\right)^{p/q}d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)\right)^{1/p},\ \ \ f\in L^{0}\left(G\right),

and let

E_{p\times q}=\left\{f\in L^{0}\left(G\right):\left\|f\right\|_{p\times q}<\infty\right\}.

Equipped with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p\times q}
the space

E_{p\times q}
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
with the Fatou
property and o.c.-norm. It is readily verified that
E_{p\times q}
is
not
rearrangement invariant whenever
p\neq q
.

The following observation may be intuitively clear.

Lemma 3.3

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
. Then the carrier of
E
is
G
.

Proof.
Let
B\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
with
\mu\left(B\right)>0
be
given. We have to show that there exists
C\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)

satisfying
\mu\left(C\right)>0
,
C\subseteq B
and
\chi_{C}\in E
. By
assumption there is
0<f\in E
. Hence, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
satisfying
\mu\left(A\right)>0
and
0<\varepsilon\chi_{A}\leq f
. In particular,
\chi_{A}\in E
. It follows
from Theorem F in Section 59 of
[
9
]
that there exists
y_{0}\in G

such that
\left(\tau_{y_{0}}\chi_{A}\right)\chi_{B}>0
. Setting
C=\left(A+y_{0}\right)\cap B
, it follows that
\mu\left(C\right)>0

and
\chi_{C}\leq\tau_{y_{0}}\chi_{A}\in E
, which implies that
\chi_{C}\in E
. Hence, the set
C
satisfies the requirements and the proof is
complete.

Using the definition (see (
2
)), it is routine to verify that the Köthe dual
E^{\times}
is translation invariant whenever
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
. If
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
is any rearrangement invariant B.f.s. over
G
, then
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
(as
\mu
is
atomless with
\mu\left(G\right)<\infty
). The following result shows,
for any translation invariant B.f.s.
E
over
G
, that the inclusion
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
always holds. Even if
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
, the other inclusion
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E

may fail, in general (see Example
3.6
below).

Proposition 3.4

Let
E
be any translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
. Then
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
with a continuous embedding.

Proof.
We can assume that
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
. Since then also
E^{\times}\neq\left\{0\right\}
, there exists
0\leq g\in E^{\times}
with
\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}=1
. Given any
0\leq f\in E
, the
non-negative function
\left(x,y\right)\longmapsto f\left(x+y\right)g\left(y\right)
, for
\left(x,y\right)\in G\times G
, is known to be
Borel measurable on
G\times G
(
[
19
]
, p. 5). Since
\int_{G}fd\mu=\int_{G}\tau_{-y}fd\mu
for all
y\in G
(the value
+\infty
is
possible at this stage), we deduce from the Fubini-Tonelli theorem (for
positive functions) that

\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}f\left(x+y\right)g\left(y\right)d\mu\left(y\right)\right)d\mu\left(x\right)\\
=\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}f\left(x+y\right)d\mu\left(x\right)\right)g\left(y\right)d\mu\left(y\right)\\
=\left(\int_{G}fd\mu\right)\left(\int_{G}g\;d\mu\right).

But, for each
x\in G
, it follows from (
3
) that

\int_{G}f\left(x+y\right)g\left(y\right)d\mu\left(y\right)=\int_{G}\left(\tau_{-y}f\right)g\;d\mu\leq\left\|f\right\|_{E}\left\|g\right\|_{E^{\times}}=\left\|f\right\|_{E}<\infty

and so, by the previous identity, we have that

\left(\int_{G}fd\mu\right)\left(\int_{G}g\;d\mu\right)\leq\mu\left(G\right)\left\|f\right\|_{E}=\left\|f\right\|_{E}<\infty.

(6)

Since
g>0
, its integral
\alpha=\int_{G}g\;d\mu>0
and so
\left\|f\right\|_{1}=\int_{G}fd\mu<\infty
, that is,
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
. It follows that
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
. As noted
earlier, the natural embedding
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
is
necessarily continuous.

Corollary 3.5

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
.

(i)

With continuous inclusions we have that
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E^{\times}\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
.

(ii)

If, in addition,
E
has the Fatou property, then
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
, with
continuous inclusions.

Proof.
(i). Since
E^{\times}\neq\left\{0\right\}
is also a translation
invariant B.f.s., we can apply Proposition
3.4
to
E^{\times}

to conclude that
E^{\times}\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
continuously.
Again by Proposition
3.4
, now applied to
E
, we have
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
continuously and hence,
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)=L^{1}\left(G\right)^{\times}\subseteq E^{\times}
, continuously.

(ii). Part (i), applied to
E^{\times}
, yields
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E^{\times\times}\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
continuously.
If
E
has the Fatou property, then
E=E^{\times\times}
, which completes
the proof.

There exist non-trivial translation invariant B.f.s.’ over
G
for which
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\nsubseteq E
.

Example 3.6

This example is inspired by the results in
[
20
]
. Let
G

be an infinite compact abelian group, equipped with normalized Haar measure
\mu
. Let
\mathcal{O}
denote the collection of all open and dense subsets
of
G
. Note that
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\in\mathcal{O}
whenever
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{O}
and that
x+U\in\mathcal{O}
whenever
U\in\mathcal{O}
and
x\in G
. Define

J_{\mathcal{O}}=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}\left(G\right):\exists\ U\in\mathcal{O}\ \text{such that }f\chi_{U}=0\right\}.

It is readily verified that
J_{\mathcal{O}}
is an ideal in
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
, which is also translation invariant (that is,
\tau_{x}f\in J_{\mathcal{O}}
whenever
f\in J_{\mathcal{O}}
and
x\in G
).
Furthermore,
J_{\mathcal{O}}\neq\left\{0\right\}
. Indeed, there exists
U\in\mathcal{O}
with
\mu\left(U\right)<1
(actually, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
V\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\mu\left(V\right)<\varepsilon
; see Theorem 2.4 in
[
20
]
), in which case
0\neq\chi_{G\backslash U}\in J_{\mathcal{O}}
. Let
E_{\mathcal{O}}

denote the norm closure of
J_{\mathcal{O}}
in
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
. Since the norm closure of any ideal is again an ideal (cf. Theorem 100.11
in
[
23
]
), it follows that
E_{\mathcal{O}}
is an ideal in
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
and it is easily verified that
E_{\mathcal{O}}
is
translation invariant. Hence,
\left(E_{\mathcal{O}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}\right)
is a non-zero translation invariant B.f.s.
over
G
.

The claim is that
\chi_{G}\notin E_{\mathcal{O}}
(and hence, that
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\nsubseteq E_{\mathcal{O}}
). Actually,
E_{\mathcal{O}}\cap C\left(G\right)=\left\{0\right\}
. Indeed, suppose that

0\neq f\in E_{\mathcal{O}}\cap C\left(G\right)
. Then there exist an
\varepsilon>0
and a non-empty open set
W\subseteq G
such that
\left|f\left(x\right)\right|>\varepsilon
, for
x\in W
. Choose

g\in J_{\mathcal{O}}
such that
\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon/2
, in which case
\left|g\left(x\right)\right|>\varepsilon/2
for all
x\in W
. But, then
g
cannot vanish
\mu
-a.e.
on any set from
\mathcal{O}
, contradicting that
g\in J_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Our next aim is to show that
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E

whenever
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
and
E
has o.c.-norm (see Proposition
3.8
). First we require some preliminary results.

Lemma 3.7

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
with o.c.-norm. For each set
A\in\Sigma
with
\chi_{A}\in E
the following assertions hold.

(i)

For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\|\chi_{B}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon
whenever
B\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
satisfies
B\subseteq A
and
\mu\left(B\right)\leq\delta
.

(ii)

If a sequence
\left(A_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
satisfies
A_{n}\downarrow\emptyset
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{y\in G}\left\|\chi_{A_{n}}\left(\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}=0.

(iii)

For each
B\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
we have that
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\left\|\left(\chi_{B}-\tau_{y}\chi_{B}\right)\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}=0
.

(iv)

\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\left\|\chi_{A}-\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}=0
.

Proof.
(i). If the statement does not hold, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a
sequence
\left(B_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
with
B_{n}\subseteq A
,
\mu\left(B_{n}\right)\leq 2^{-n}

and
\left\|\chi_{B_{n}}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The sets
C_{n}=\bigcup\nolimits_{k=n}^{\infty}B_{k}

satisfy
\mu\left(C_{n}\right)\leq 2^{-n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

and
C_{n}\downarrow_{n}
. Hence,
\chi_{A}\geq\chi_{C_{n}}\downarrow 0

in
E
and so, by order continuity of the norm, we have
\left\|\chi_{C_{n}}\right\|_{E}\downarrow 0
. But,
\chi_{C_{n}}\geq\chi_{B_{n}}

implies that
\left\|\chi_{C_{n}}\right\|_{E}\geq\left\|\chi_{B_{n}}\right\|_{E}\geq\varepsilon
for all
n
, which is a
contradiction.

(ii). Given
\varepsilon>0
, by part (i) there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left\|\chi_{B}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon
whenever
B\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
satisfies
B\subseteq A
and
\mu\left(B\right)\leq\delta
. Since
A_{n}\downarrow\emptyset
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu\left(A_{n}\right)\leq\delta
for all
n\geq N
. Observe that

\left\|\chi_{A_{n}}\left(\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}=\left\|\tau_{-y}\left(\chi_{A_{n}}\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}=\left\|\left(\tau_{-y}\chi_{A_{n}}\right)\chi_{A}\right\|_{E},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ \ y\in G.

Since
\left(\tau_{-y}\chi_{A_{n}}\right)\chi_{A}=\chi_{\left(A_{n}-y\right)\cap A}
and

\mu\left(\left(A_{n}-y\right)\cap A\right)\leq\mu\left(A_{n}-y\right)=\mu\left(A_{n}\right)\leq\delta,\ \ \ n\geq N,

it follows that
\left\|\left(\tau_{-y}\chi_{A_{n}}\right)\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon
for all
y\in G
and
n\geq N
.
Hence,

\sup_{y\in G}\left\|\left(\tau_{-y}\chi_{A_{n}}\right)\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \ n\geq N,

which completes the proof of part (ii).

(iii). Fix
B\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
. Note that
\left|\chi_{B}-\tau_{y}\chi_{B}\right|=\chi_{\left(B+y\right)\Delta B}
, for

y\in G
, where
\Delta
denotes the symmetric difference. It follows from
the continuity of translations in
L^{1}\left(G\right)
(see
[
19
]
,
p.3) that
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\left\|\chi_{B}-\tau_{y}\chi_{B}\right\|_{1}=0
and hence, that
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\mu\left(\left(B+y\right)\Delta B\right)=0
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, it follows
from part (i) that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\|\chi_{C}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon
whenever
C\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
satisfies
C\subseteq A
and
\mu\left(C\right)\leq\delta
.
If
U
is a neighbourhood of
0\in G
such that
\mu\left(\left(B+y\right)\Delta B\right)\leq\delta
for all
y\in U
, then
\mu\left(A\cap\left(\left(B+y\right)\Delta B\right)\right)\leq\delta
and so,

\left\|\left(\chi_{B}-\tau_{y}\chi_{B}\right)\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}=\left\|\chi_{A\cap\left(\left(B+y\right)\Delta B\right)}\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \ y\in U.

This suffices for the proof of part (iii).

(iv). Since the carrier of
E
is
G
(cf. Lemma
3.3
), there
exists a sequence
\left(H_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
with
\chi_{H_{n}}\in E
for all
n
and
H_{n}\uparrow G
, i.e.,
G\backslash H_{n}\downarrow\emptyset
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, it
follows from part (ii) that there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sup_{y\in G}\left\|\chi_{G\backslash H_{N}}\left(\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon/3
. By part (iii), there is a
neighbourhood
U
of
0\in G
such that
\left\|\chi_{H_{N}}\left(\chi_{A}-\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon/3
for all
y\in U
. Consequently, for each
y\in U
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{A}-\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{H_{N}}\left(\chi_{A}-\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}+\left\|\chi_{G\backslash H_{N}}\left(\chi_{A}-\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon/3+\left\|\chi_{G\backslash H_{N}}\left(\tau_{0}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}+\left\|\chi_{G\backslash H_{N}}\left(\tau_{y}\chi_{A}\right)\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon.

This completes the proof of part (iv).

We can now establish the result alluded to above.

Proposition 3.8

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
with o.c.-norm. The following statements hold.

(i)

For each
f\in E
it is the case that
\lim_{y\rightarrow 0}\left\|f-\tau_{y}f\right\|_{E}=0
.

(ii)

L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
with continuous inclusions.

(iii)

C\left(G\right)
is a dense subspace of
E
.

Proof.
(i). Denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)
the
space of all simple functions based on
\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
. Claim:

\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)\cap E
is dense in
E
. Indeed, given
0\leq f\in E
, there exists a sequence
\left(s_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)^{+}
such that
0\leq s_{n}\uparrow_{n}f

\mu
-a.e. on
G
. Since
E
is an ideal in
L^{0}\left(G\right)
, it is clear that
s_{n}\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)\cap E
, for

n\in\mathbb{N}
. The order continuity of the norm in
E
implies that
\left\|f-s_{n}\right\|_{E}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
This suffices for the proof of the claim.

Let
s\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)\cap E
.
Then
s=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}
, where
A_{j}\in\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)
with
\chi_{A_{j}}\in E
and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
,
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore, Lemma
3.7
(iv) implies that
\left\|s-\tau_{y}s\right\|_{E}\rightarrow 0
as
y\rightarrow 0
in

G
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{s}im}\left(\mathcal{B}\left(G\right)\right)\cap E

is dense in
E
, the result of (i) is now clear.

(ii). That
E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
has been shown in Proposition
3.4
. For the inclusion of
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
into
E
,
we begin with a general observation, which is of independent interest (only
special cases will be used in the proofs of (ii) and (iii)).

Let
f\in E
and
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
. Since
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
(cf. Proposition
3.4
), the convolution
f\ast\lambda\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
exists (see e.g.
[
19
]
, Section 1.3.2). The
claim is that
f\ast\lambda\in E
. Indeed, it follows from part (i) that
the function
F:y\longmapsto\tau_{y}f
, for
y\in G
, is continuous from
G
into
E
. So the range of
F
is a compact metric space and hence, is
separable. Via the Pettis measurability theorem (
[
5
]
, p. 42) it
follows that
F
is strongly
\left|\lambda\right|
-measurable
and bounded. Consequently, the Bochner integral
\int_{G}^{\left(B\right)}Fd\lambda=\int_{G}^{\left(B\right)}\tau_{y}fd\lambda\left(y\right)\in E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
exists. Using
[
5
]
, Theorem
II.2.6 (for continuous functionals on
E
) and Fubini’s theorem, we find for
all
g\in L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E^{\times}\subseteq E^{\ast}
that

\displaystyle\left\langle\int_{G}^{\left(B\right)}\tau_{y}fd\lambda\left(y\right),g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left\langle\tau_{y}f,g\right\rangle d\lambda\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}\left(\tau_{y}f\right)\left(x\right)g\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)\right)d\lambda\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}f\left(x-y\right)d\lambda\left(y\right)\right)g\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\left\langle f\ast\lambda,g\right\rangle.

We can conclude that

f\ast\lambda=\int_{G}^{\left(B\right)}\tau_{y}fd\lambda\left(y\right)\in E,\ \ \ f\in E,\ \ \lambda\in M\left(G\right).

(7)

This proves the claim.

Fix any
0<f_{0}\in E
. By what has just been proved, we have that
f_{0}\ast\mu\in E
. But,
f_{0}\ast\mu=\left(\int_{G}f_{0}d\mu\right)\chi_{G}
and so we can conclude that
\chi_{G}\in E
and hence, that
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
. Furthermore, if
f\in L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
, then
\left|f\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\chi_{G}
implies that
\left\|f\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|\chi_{G}\right\|_{E}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
. Consequently, the embedding
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
is continuous.

(iii). Let
f\in E
be fixed and
\varepsilon>0
be given. It follows from
(i) that there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
G
such that
\left\|f-\tau_{y}f\right\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon
for all
y\in U
.
Defining
h=\mu\left(U\right)^{-1}\chi_{U}
, we have
f\ast h\in C\left(G\right)
(as
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
and
h\in L^{\infty}\left(G\right)
; cf.
[
19
]
, Section 1.1.6). Furthermore, (
7
)
implies that

f-\left(f\ast h\right)=\mu\left(U\right)^{-1}\int_{U}^{\left(B\right)}\left(f-\tau_{y}f\right)d\mu\left(y\right),

which implies that

\left\|f-\left(f\ast h\right)\right\|_{E}\leq\mu\left(U\right)^{-1}\int_{U}\left\|f-\tau_{y}f\right\|_{E}d\mu\left(y\right)\leq\varepsilon;

cf.
[
5
]
, Theorem II.2.4 (ii). The proof is thereby complete.

Remark 3.9

(a)

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant B.f.s.
over
G
with o.c.-norm. Then
f\ast\lambda\in E
for all
f\in E
and
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
; see the proof of part (ii) in the above
proposition. Moreover, it follows from (
7
) that
\left\|f\ast\lambda\right\|_{E}\leq\left\|\lambda\right\|_{M\left(G\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{E}
. Consequently, for each
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
, the operator
T_{\lambda}^{E}:E\rightarrow E
of
convolution with
\lambda
exists and satisfies
\left\|T_{\lambda}^{E}\right\|\leq\left\|\lambda\right\|_{M\left(G\right)}
.

It can be shown that a similar result holds for translation invariant
B.f.s.’ over
G
which have the Fatou property. However, for general
translation invariant B.f.s.’ over
G
this result fails. In fact, for the
translation invariant B.f.s.
E_{\mathcal{O}}
of Example
3.6
it
can be shown that the only measures
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
for
which the convolution operator
T_{\lambda}^{E_{\mathcal{O}}}:E_{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow E_{\mathcal{O}}
exists, are the discrete measures. See
[
15
]
.

(b)

A translation invariant B.f.s.
E
over
G
with the property
that
\left\|f-\tau_{y}f\right\|_{E}\rightarrow 0
as
y\rightarrow 0
in
G
, for every
f\in E
, necessarily has o.c.-norm. The
proof of this fact is somewhat involved and is not needed in this note. See

[
15
]
. This fact also implies that any translation invariant B.f.s. over

G
in which
C\left(G\right)
is dense, necessarily has o.c.-norm.

Next we discuss the phenomenon that a general translation invariant B.f.s.
need not be reflection invariant (see the definition below). For any
f\in L^{0}\left(G\right)
, its reflection
\tilde{f}\in L^{0}\left(G\right)

is defined by

\tilde{f}\left(x\right)=f\left(-x\right),\ \ \ x\in G.

Definition 3.10

A B.f.s.
E
over
G
is called
reflection invariant

if it has the property that
\tilde{f}\in E
with
\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{E}=\left\|f\right\|_{E}
whenever
f\in E
.

Evidently, every rearrangement invariant B.f.s. over
G
is reflection
invariant. Note that the B.f.s.
E_{p\times q}
of Example
3.2
, with
p\neq q
, is translation and reflection invariant but, it is not
rearrangement invariant. Furthermore, it is routine to verify, for any
reflection invariant B.f.s.
E
over
G
with carrier equal to
G
, that the
Köthe dual
E^{\times}
is also reflection invariant. However, a
translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
need not be reflection invariant.

Example 3.11

Let
0<g\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
be fixed. Define

\left\|f\right\|_{E_{g}}=\sup_{y\in G}\int_{G}\left|f\right|\left(\tau_{y}g\right)d\mu,\ \ \ f\in L^{0}\left(G\right),

(8)

and let

E_{g}=\left\{f\in L^{0}\left(G\right):\left\|f\right\|_{E_{g}}<\infty\right\},

(9)

equipped with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E_{g}}
. It is
readily verified that
E_{g}
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G

with the Fatou property. Actually,
E_{g}
is the largest translation
invariant B.f.s.
F
over
G
with the property that
g\in F^{\times}
.

Consider the group
G=\mathbb{T}
with normalized Haar measure (that is,
d\mu=\frac{1}{2\pi}dx
on
\left[-\pi,\pi\right]
, where
dx
denotes
Lebesgue measure). Functions on
\mathbb{T}
will be identified with
2\pi
-periodic functions on
\mathbb{R}
. The
2\pi
-periodic function
g:\mathbb{R\rightarrow R}
is defined by setting

g\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if }-\pi<x\leq 0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}&\text{if }0<x\leq\pi\end{array}\right..

Define the function norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{E_{g}}
on
L^{0}\left(G\right)
by (
8
) and let
E_{g}
be the translation
invariant B.f.s. defined by (
9
). It is clear that the function
g\notin E_{g}
. However, a direct computation shows that
\tilde{g}\in E_{g}
. Therefore,
E_{g}
is not reflection invariant. It can be shown that the
space
E_{g}
does not have o.c.-norm. There also exist translation
invariant B.f.s.’ over
\mathbb{T}
which are not reflection invariant but,
have both the Fatou property and o.c.-norm.

4
Fourier multiplier functions

As before,
G
is an infinite compact abelian group equipped with normalized
Haar measure
\mu
. Let
\Gamma
be the dual group of
G
; see, for
example, Section 1.2 of
[
19
]
. For
x\in G
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
we
write
\gamma\left(x\right)=\left(x,\gamma\right)
. Since we
frequently consider
\Gamma
, as well as its linear span
\tau\left(G\right)
, as subsets of
C\left(G\right)
, we shall write the group
operation in
\Gamma
as
multiplication
, that is, if
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
, then
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
is
given by

\left(x,\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\right)=\left(x,\gamma_{1}\right)\left(x,\gamma_{2}\right),\ \ \ x\in G.

The identity element of
\Gamma
is, of course,
\chi_{G}
. In particular,

\left(x,\gamma^{-1}\right)=\overline{\left(x,\gamma\right)}=\left(-x,\gamma\right),\ \ \ x\in G,\ \ \gamma\in\Gamma,

(10)

where the bar denotes complex conjugation. Denote by
\tau\left(G\right)

the linear subspace of
C\left(G\right)
consisting of all
trigonometric polynomials
on
G
, that is,
\tau\left(G\right)
is the
linear span of
\Gamma\subseteq C\left(G\right)
. The following fact,
which is a consequence of Proposition
3.8
(iii), will be used.

Proposition 4.1

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
with o.c.-norm. Then
\tau\left(G\right)
is a dense
subspace of
E
.

Proof.
The dual group
\Gamma
separates the points of
G
(see
[
19
]
,
Section 1.5.2) and so it follows from the Stone-Weierstrass theorem that
\tau\left(G\right)
is dense in
C\left(G\right)
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
. By Proposition
3.8

(iii),
C\left(G\right)
is dense in
E
. Since the embedding of
C\left(G\right)
into
E
is continuous (as
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
continuously), we can conclude that
\tau\left(G\right)
is dense in
E
.

For each
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
its Fourier transform, denoted by
\hat{f}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, is given by

\hat{f}\left(\gamma\right)=\int_{G}f\left(x\right)\left(-x,\gamma\right)d\mu\left(x\right),\ \ \ \gamma\in\Gamma,

and satisfies
\hat{f}\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
with
\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{1}
. Furthermore, if
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
and
\hat{f}=0
, then
f=0
. For these facts,
see for instance Section 1.2 of
[
19
]
.

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be translation invariant B.f.s.’ over
G
.
Recall that
E
is contained in
L^{1}\left(G\right)
and so
\hat{f}

exists for
f\in E
. A function
\varphi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
called an
E
-multiplier function
for
G
if, for every
f\in E
,
there exists a function
g\in F
such that
\hat{g}=\varphi\hat{f}

(pointwise on
\Gamma
). If such a function
g
exists, then it is
necessarily unique by the injectivity of the Fourier transform. Denote
g

by
M_{\varphi}^{E}f
. The so defined map
M_{\varphi}^{E}:E\rightarrow E

is linear and satisfies

\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}f\right)^{\wedge}=\varphi\hat{f},\ \ \ f\in E.

(11)

The collection of all
E
-multiplier functions for
G
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
, which clearly is a (complex) vector space
of functions on
\Gamma
. For
E=L^{p}\left(G\right)
, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we use the simpler notation
\mathcal{M}_{p}\left(G\right)
in
place of
\mathcal{M}_{L^{p}\left(G\right)}\left(G\right)
. A routine
application of the closed graph theorem shows that
M_{\varphi}^{E}
is
continuous, i.e.,
M_{\varphi}^{E}\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
, for
every
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
. Here,
\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
denotes the Banach space of all continuous linear
operators of
E
into itself, equipped with the operator norm. It should be
observed that if
E
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
with
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
(in which case
\gamma\in E
for
all
\gamma\in\Gamma
) then, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
, we have

M_{\varphi}^{E}\gamma=\varphi\left(\gamma\right)\gamma,\ \ \ \gamma\in\Gamma.

(12)

Indeed, given
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have that
\hat{\gamma}=\chi_{\left\{\gamma\right\}}
, from
which it follows that
\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}\gamma\right)^{\wedge}=\varphi\hat{\gamma}=\left(\varphi\left(\gamma\right)\gamma\right)^{\wedge}
. This implies (
12
). It follows from (
11
) that
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
is an algebra for pointwise multiplication
of functions on
\Gamma
.

It is shown in the following lemma that always
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\subseteq\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Recall that if
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
, then
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E^{\times}
; see Corollary
3.5
(i). In particular,
\gamma\in E^{\times}
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Moreover,
\left\|\gamma\right\|_{E^{\times}}=\left\|\chi_{G}\right\|_{E^{\times}}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, because
\left|\gamma\right|=\chi_{G}
. Recall also that
\left\|f\right\|_{E^{\times}}=\left\|f\right\|_{E^{\ast}}
for each
f\in E^{\times}
; see the discussion following formula (
4
).

Lemma 4.2

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s.’ over
G
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\subseteq\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
and

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|M_{\varphi}^{E}\right\|,\ \ \ \varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right).

(13)

Proof.
Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and set
T=M_{\varphi}^{E}
. As observed prior to the lemma,
T
is continuous and so we can
consider the Banach space adjoint operator
T^{\ast}:E^{\ast}\rightarrow E^{\ast}
. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
and consider
\gamma\in E^{\times}\subseteq E^{\ast}
. The claim is that
T^{\ast}\gamma=\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\gamma\in E^{\times}\subseteq E^{\ast}
. Indeed,
given
f\in E
we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle f,T^{\ast}\gamma\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle Tf,\gamma\right\rangle=\int_{G}\left(Tf\right)\left(x\right)\left(x,\gamma\right)d\mu\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left(Tf\right)\left(x\right)\left(-x,\gamma^{-1}\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\left(Tf\right)^{\wedge}\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\hat{f}\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)=\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\int_{G}f\left(x\right)\left(-x,\gamma^{-1}\right)d\mu\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\int_{G}f\left(x\right)\left(x,\gamma\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\left\langle f,\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\gamma\right\rangle.

Since
E
separates the elements of
E^{\ast}
, the claim follows. Defining
the
reflected function

\tilde{\varphi}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
\tilde{\varphi}\left(\gamma\right)=\varphi\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)
, for
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we find that

\left\|T\right\|=\left\|T^{\ast}\right\|\geq\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\left\|T^{\ast}\gamma\right\|_{E^{\times}}}{\left\|\gamma\right\|_{E^{\times}}}=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\left|\tilde{\varphi}\left(\gamma\right)\right|\left\|\gamma\right\|_{E^{\times}}}{\left\|\gamma\right\|_{E^{\times}}}=\left\|\tilde{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}.

Since
\left\|\tilde{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}
, this suffices for the proof.

For the case
E=L^{p}\left(G\right)
with
1\leq p<\infty
, the
inequality (
13
) is well known; see Corollary 4.1.2 of
[
10
]
,
for example.

Define the family of operators

\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)=\left\{M_{\varphi}^{E}:\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\left(E\right).

(14)

For each
x\in G
, a direct calculation shows that the Dirac point measure
\delta_{x}
satisfies
\tau_{x}f=f\ast\delta_{x}
, for
f\in E
. Since
\tau_{x}\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
and
\hat{\delta}_{x}\left(\gamma\right)=\left(-x,\gamma\right)
, for
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it follows
that
\left(x,\cdot\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
for every
x\in G
and
\left\{\tau_{x}:x\in G\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
. In particular, the identity operator
I=\tau_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
and the constant
function
\hat{\delta}_{0}=\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
.

The following result is a consequence of Lemma
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant
B.f.s. over
G
. The set
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
is a unital
subalgebra of
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
and the map
\Phi:\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
,
defined by

\Phi\left(\varphi\right)=M_{\varphi}^{E},\ \ \ \varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right),

is a unital algebra isomorphism onto the subalgebra
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)\subseteq\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
is a unital
commutative Banach subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
.

Proof.
It is routine to verify that
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
is a unital
subalgebra of
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
and that the map
\Phi:\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
is
a unital algebra homomorphism. Consequently,
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)=\Phi\left(\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)\right)
is a
unital commutative subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
. The
injectivity of
\Phi
follows from Lemma
4.2
.

To show that
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
is norm closed
in
\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
, let
\left(M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
satisfying
\left\|S-M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}\right\|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
S\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
. Lemma
4.2
implies that
\left(\varphi_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
. So, there exists
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
such that
\left\|\varphi-\varphi_{n}\right\|_{\infty}\rightarrow 0
for
n\rightarrow\infty
. It follows, for a fixed
f\in E\subseteq L^{1}\left(G\right)
, that
\hat{f}\in c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
and so

\left(M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}f\right)^{\wedge}=\varphi_{n}\hat{f}\rightarrow\varphi\hat{f},\ \ \ n\rightarrow\infty,

(15)

in
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
. On the other hand,
M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}f\rightarrow Sf
in
E
for
n\rightarrow\infty
. Since
E
is
continuously included in
L^{1}\left(G\right)
, it follows that
M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}f\rightarrow Sf
in
L^{1}\left(G\right)
. By the
continuity of the Fourier transform from
L^{1}\left(G\right)
into
c_{0}\left(\Gamma\right)
we can conclude that
\left(M_{\varphi_{n}}^{E}f\right)^{\wedge}\rightarrow\left(Sf\right)^{\wedge}
in
\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Then (
15
) implies that
\left(Sf\right)^{\wedge}=\varphi\hat{f}
. But,
f\in E
is arbitrary and
so
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
with
S=M_{\varphi}^{E}
.
The proof is complete.

Remark 4.4

(a)

For
E=L^{2}\left(G\right)
it is a classical result that
\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(G\right)=\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
,
which is an immediate consequence of the fact that the Fourier transform is
a surjective isometry from
L^{2}\left(G\right)
onto
\ell^{2}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Conversely, if
E
is a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
with
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
and
\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)=\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
, then it follows from
Proposition 1.6 of
[
14
]
that
E=L^{2}\left(G\right)
with equivalent
norms.

(b)

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation invariant B.f.s.
over
G
with
o.c.-norm
. As observed in Remark
3.9
, for each
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
the operator
T_{\lambda}^{E}:E\rightarrow E

of convolution with
\lambda
exists. Recalling that
\left(f\ast\lambda\right)^{\wedge}=\hat{\lambda}\hat{f}
for
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)

and
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
, it follows that
\hat{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
with
M_{\hat{\lambda}}^{E}=T_{\lambda}^{E}
for all
\lambda\in M\left(G\right)
. Hence,

\left\{\hat{\lambda}:\lambda\in M\left(G\right)\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right).

A similar result holds if
E
has the Fatou property.

For any function
f\in L^{0}\left(G\right)
, the function
f^{\natural}\in L^{0}\left(G\right)
is defined by

f^{\natural}\left(x\right)=\overline{f\left(-x\right)},\ \ \ \mu\text{-a.e}\ \ x\in G.

Let
E
be a reflection and translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
. Then

f^{\natural}\in E\ \ \text{and\ \ }\left\|f^{\natural}\right\|_{E}=\left\|f\right\|_{E},\ \ \ f\in E,

and the map
f\longmapsto f^{\natural}
, for
f\in E
, is a conjugate
linear isometric involution in
E
. For each operator
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
the operator
T^{\natural}\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)

is defined by

T^{\natural}f=\left(Tf^{\natural}\right)^{\natural},\ \ \ f\in E,

and satisfies
\left\|T^{\natural}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|
. Then
T\longmapsto T^{\sharp}
, for
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
,
is a conjugate linear isometric involution in
\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
.

Proposition 4.5

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation and reflection
invariant B.f.s. over
G
.

(i)

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
, then also
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}=\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}\right)^{\natural}
.

(ii)

The map
M_{\varphi}^{E}\longmapsto M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}
, for

\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
, is a conjugate linear
isometric involution in
\mathcal{M}_{E}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)
.

Proof.
(i). Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
. A direct (but
careful) computation shows that

\left(\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}\right)^{\natural}f\right)^{\wedge}=\bar{\varphi}\hat{f},\ \ \ f\in E.

Consequently,
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}=\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}\right)^{\natural}
.

(ii). This follows immediately from part (i) in combination with the
observations made prior to the present proposition.

For
E=L^{2}\left(G\right)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(G\right)=\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
, it follows (via
Plancherel’s formula) that
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{\left(2\right)}=\left(M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}\right)^{\ast}
is the Hilbert space
adjoint of
M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}
.

5
A Fuglede type theorem

Again let
G
be an infinite compact abelian group with normalized Haar
measure
\mu
. As observed in Proposition
4.5
(i), if
E
is a
translation and reflection invariant B.f.s. over
G
, then
\bar{\varphi}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
whenever
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
. For
E=L^{2}\left(G\right)
, we noted that
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{\left(2\right)}=\left(M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}\right)^{\ast}

is the Hilbert space adjoint of
M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}
. In
particular,
M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}
is a normal operator on
L^{2}\left(G\right)
(as
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}p}}\left(G\right)

is commutative; cf. Corollary
4.3
). Consequently, it follows from
Fuglede’s theorem (see, for instance, Theorem IX.6.7 in
[
4
]
) that if
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}_{2}\left(G\right)=\ell^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\right)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(L^{2}\left(G\right)\right)
satisfy

M_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}T=TM_{\varphi}^{\left(2\right)}
, then
also
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{\left(2\right)}T=TM_{\bar{\varphi}}^{\left(2\right)}
. As noted in the Introduction, this latter result was extended
to the case
E=L^{p}\left(\mathbb{T}\right)
with
1\leq p<\infty
in
Theorem 3.1 of
[
13
]
. The purpose of this section to extend this result
to all translation and reflection invariant B.f.s.’ with o.c.-norm over
arbitrary compact abelian groups (see Theorem
5.2
below).

For any
f\in L^{1}\left(G\right)
we denote

\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\hat{f}\right)=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\hat{f}\left(\gamma\right)\neq 0\right\}.

We begin with the following result.

Lemma 5.1

Let
E
be a translation invariant B.f.s. over
G
with
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
. Let
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
.

(i)

Suppose that
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
. Then, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it is the case that
\varphi\left(\xi\right)=\psi\left(\gamma\right)
for all
\xi\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)
.

(ii)

Assume, in addition, that
E
has o.c.-norm. Let
\varphi
and
\psi
have the property that, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have
\varphi\left(\xi\right)=\psi\left(\gamma\right)
for all
\xi\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)
. Then
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
.

Proof.
(i). Fix
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
, we have that
\gamma\in E
and so

M_{\varphi}^{E}T\gamma=TM_{\psi}^{E}\gamma.

(16)

Recalling from (
12
) that
M_{\psi}^{E}\gamma=\psi\left(\gamma\right)\gamma
, it follows that
TM_{\psi}^{E}\gamma=\psi\left(\gamma\right)T\gamma
. Hence,

\left(TM_{\psi}^{E}\gamma\right)^{\wedge}=\psi\left(\gamma\right)\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}.

(17)

On the other hand,

\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}=\varphi\cdot\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}.

(18)

A combination of (
16
), (
17
) and (
18
) yields
that

\varphi\left(\xi\right)\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\left(\xi\right)=\psi\left(\gamma\right)\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\left(\xi\right),

(19)

for all
\xi\in\Gamma
. If
\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)=\emptyset
, then there is nothing to be proved. So,
suppose there exists
\xi\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)
. Then
\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\left(\xi\right)\neq 0
and hence, (
19
) implies that
\varphi\left(\xi\right)=\psi\left(\gamma\right)
. This proves part (i).

(ii). Fix
\gamma\in\Gamma
. If
\xi\in\Gamma\backslash\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)
, i.e.,
\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\left(\xi\right)=0
, then it is clear that (
19
) holds. On the other hand, if
\xi\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right)
, then it follows from the hypothesis
that (
19
) holds. Hence,

\varphi\cdot\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}=\psi\left(\gamma\right)\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}.

Via (
17
) and (
18
) this yields that

\left(M_{\varphi}^{E}T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}=\left(TM_{\psi}^{E}\gamma\right)^{\wedge}.

The uniqueness of Fourier transforms in
L^{1}\left(G\right)
then implies
(
16
).

Since
\gamma\in\Gamma
is arbitrary, the linearity of
T
,
M_{\varphi}^{E}
and
M_{\psi}^{E}
imply that

M_{\varphi}^{E}Tg=TM_{\psi}^{E}g,\ \ \ g\in\tau\left(G\right),

where, as before,
\tau\left(G\right)\subseteq L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
denotes the space of all trigonometric polynomials on
G
. But,

E
has o.c.-norm and so
\tau\left(G\right)
is dense in
E
; see
Proposition
4.1
. The operators
T
,
M_{\varphi}^{E}
and
M_{\psi}^{E}
are continuous and hence, we can conclude that
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
. The proof is thereby complete.

The following Fuglede type theorem is a consequence of Lemma
5.1
.

Theorem 5.2

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be a translation and reflection
invariant B.f.s. over
G
with o.c.-norm. Suppose that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)

satisfy
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
. Then
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}T=TM_{\bar{\psi}}^{E}
.

Proof.
The reflection invariance of
E
ensures that
\bar{\varphi},\bar{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and the order continuity of the norm in
E

implies that
L^{\infty}\left(G\right)\subseteq E
. By Lemma
5.1
(i), the condition
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
implies,
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, that

\varphi\left(\xi\right)=\psi\left(\gamma\right),\ \ \ \xi\in\mathop{\mathrm{s}upp}\left(\left(T\gamma\right)^{\wedge}\right).

(20)

It is clear that the functions
\bar{\varphi}
and
\bar{\psi}
(in place of

\varphi
and
\psi
, respectively) also satisfy (
20
).
Therefore, via Lemma
5.1
(ii) applied to
\bar{\varphi},\bar{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
, we can conclude that
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}T=TM_{\bar{\psi}}^{E}
. This completes the proof.

The following result is a special case of Theorem
5.2
.

Corollary 5.3

Let
E\neq\left\{0\right\}
be any rearrangement invariant B.f.s. over
G

with o.c.-norm. Suppose that
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\left(G\right)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E\right)
satisfy
M_{\varphi}^{E}T=TM_{\psi}^{E}
. Then
M_{\bar{\varphi}}^{E}T=TM_{\bar{\psi}}^{E}
.

Finally the results are illustrated for the Painlevé-Gullstrand extension of Schwarzschild spacetime.

Introduction

Unimodular Gravity (UG) is a restriction of General Relativity to unimodular metrics

g\equiv\left|\det\,g_{\mu\nu}\right|=1

(1)

A preliminary question is why Unimodular Gravity? The answer is that in this theory a constant vacuum density does not weigh, so that it does not induce a cosmological constant (cf. the first reference in
[
4

). Although that fact is not a full solution of the cosmological constant problem, it does solve half of it, namely why the vacuum energy usually believed to be generated in several phase transitions in the history of the universe do not induce a huge cosmological constant. It remains to explain the observed (small and positive) value of the cosmological constant. UG has nothing new to say about this.

In this paper, we want to study the conserved currents for the Unimodular Gravity in two equivalent formulation of General Relativity (GR), the Hilbert Lagrangian

2
Conserved currents in Unimodular Gravity.

Let us begin with a short review of the consequences of the full Diff symmetry. After that, we study the special case of UG with only TDiff symmetry and finally we derive our main results in the case of Weyl
\rtimes
TDiff symmetry for Hilbert’s and Einstein’s Lagrangians.

Assume a Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}
, scalar under the full Diff group. The invariance of the action under an arbitrary diffeomorphism generated by
\xi^{\mu}
implies that the variation of the action reads

\delta S=\int d^{n}x\,\partial_{\mu}(\mathcal{L}\xi^{\mu})

(15)

On the other hand it is well-known that under a general variation of the metric (without changing the domain of integration) the variation of the action reads

\delta S=\int d^{n}x\sqrt{|g|}\bigg\{\frac{\delta S}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}\bigg\}

(16)

on shell, that is, on the submanifold that obeys the equation of motion, this variation reduces to

\delta S=\int d^{n}x\sqrt{|g|}\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}=\int d^{n}x\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{|g|}j^{\mu})

(17)

The fact that this two variations have to be consistent independently of the integration domain means that the Noether current

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{N}=\partial_{\mu}(\mathcal{L}\xi^{\mu}-\sqrt{g}j^{\mu})=0

(18)

has got to be conserved.

Let us derive Noether’s current for a general Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(g_{\alpha\beta},\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta},\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta})
. The variation of Lagrangian is

\delta\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}

(19)

Euler-Lagrange’s equation reads

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}=0

(20)

and Noether’s current is

j_{N}^{\mu}=\Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}-\partial_{\nu}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\Big)\delta g_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\partial_{\nu}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}

(21)

which for a Diff generated by the vector field
\xi^{\mu}
can be written as

j_{N}^{\mu}=\sqrt{|g|}\left(T_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu}\xi^{\rho}+U_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}+V_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu(\lambda\tau)}\partial_{\lambda}\partial_{\tau}\xi^{\rho}\right)

(22)

with

\displaystyle T_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}\mathcal{L}-\Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}}-\partial_{\nu}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\Big)\partial_{\rho}g_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}g_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle U_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu\nu}

\displaystyle=2\Big(\partial_{\tau}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tau}g_{\alpha\beta}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\Big)g_{\rho\sigma}I^{\nu\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}}\partial_{\rho}g_{\alpha\beta}-2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tau}g_{\alpha\beta}}I^{\nu\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\tau}g_{\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle V_{\rho}^{\penalty\ \mu(\lambda\tau)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tau}g_{\alpha\beta}}g_{\rho\sigma}I^{\lambda\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta}}g_{\rho\sigma}I^{\tau\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}

(23)

with

I^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}\delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\Big)

(24)

What changes in UG, where we only have TDiff invariance?
The fact that the gauge parameters are not independent is the source of some small technical complications. One way out is to write

where the tensor

assuming that

\left.\Omega_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{\partial M}=0

therefore

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{n}x\partial_{\mu}\Big[\sqrt{g}\left(T^{\mu}_{\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+U_{\rho}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+V_{\rho}^{\mu(\lambda\tau)}\nabla_{\lambda}\nabla_{\tau}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{n}x\sqrt{g}\Big[-(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\rho})\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+(T^{\mu}_{\rho}+\nabla_{\lambda}U_{\rho}^{\lambda\mu})\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+

2.1
Hilbert’s UG Lagrangian

The Unimodular Hilbert action is

S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}[\gamma]\equiv-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n}x\hat{R}

(35)

where the metric is unimodular
\gamma=|\text{det}\gamma_{\mu\nu}|=1
. As has been already remarked, it is possible to formulate the theory in such a way that it has an added Weyl invariance by writing

\gamma_{\mu\nu}=|g|^{-\frac{1}{n}}g_{\mu\nu}

(36)

if we apply this transformation, the action for this new unrestricted metric reads

S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}[g]=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n}xg^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big[R-\frac{(n-1)(5n-2)}{4n^{2}}g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\nabla_{\mu}g\nabla_{\nu}g}{g^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{\Box g}{g}\Big]

(37)

Under a general variation
\delta g_{\mu\nu}\equiv h_{\mu\nu}
(forgetting the prefactor
-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}
but keeping total derivatives)

\delta S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}=\int d^{n}x\sqrt{|g|}\bigg\{\frac{\delta S}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}\bigg\}

(38)

explicitly

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}=g^{1/n}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{n}Rg_{\mu\nu}-R_{\mu\nu}+\frac{(n-2)(2n-1)}{4n^{2}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mu}g\nabla_{\nu}g}{g^{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}g\nabla^{\alpha}g}{g^{2}}g_{\mu\nu}\right)-\frac{n-2}{2n}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}g}{g}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\Box g}{g}g_{\mu\nu}\right)\Bigg\}\,h^{\mu\nu}+

\displaystyle+\sqrt{g}\Bigg\{\nabla_{\mu}\Big[g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\nabla_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}\Big]-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\nu}\Big[h^{\mu\nu}g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\frac{\nabla_{\mu}g}{g}\Big]-\frac{1}{n}\left(\nabla_{\lambda}\Big[g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\nabla^{\lambda}h\Big]-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\lambda}\Big[hg^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\frac{\nabla^{\lambda}g}{g}\Big]\right)\Bigg\}

(39)

The corresponding equation of motion is Weyl invariant and, up to total derivatives, traceless.
This means that

\displaystyle j^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\Big[\nabla_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}\frac{\nabla_{\nu}g}{g}-\frac{1}{n}\left(\nabla^{\mu}h-\frac{1}{2}h\frac{\nabla^{\mu}g}{g}\right)\Big]

(40)

and using the previous results, the total TDiff current reads

j^{\mu}_{N}=\sqrt{g}\left(T^{\mu}_{\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+U_{\rho}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}+V_{\rho}^{\mu(\lambda\tau)}\nabla_{\lambda}\nabla_{\tau}\nabla_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha\rho}\right)

(41)

where

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\Big[R-\frac{(n-1)(5n-2)}{4n^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\nabla_{\alpha}g\nabla_{\beta}g}{g^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{\Box g}{g}\Big]

\displaystyle U_{\rho}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{\frac{2-3n}{2n}}\Big[g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}g+\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}\nabla^{\nu}g-\frac{1}{n}\delta^{\nu}_{\rho}\nabla^{\mu}g\Big]

\displaystyle V_{\rho}^{\mu(\lambda\tau)}

\displaystyle=g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\Big[-g^{\lambda\tau}\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}-\frac{n-2}{2n}\left(\delta^{\lambda}_{\rho}g^{\mu\tau}+\delta^{\tau}_{\rho}g^{\mu\lambda}\right)\Big]

(42)

In the unimodular Weyl gauge

g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\gamma_{\mu\nu}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{with}\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma=|\text{det}\gamma_{\mu\nu}|=1

(43)

these expressions reduce to

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\hat{R}\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\rho}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\rho}^{\mu(\lambda\tau)}

\displaystyle=-\gamma^{\lambda\tau}\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}-\frac{n-2}{2n}\left(\delta^{\lambda}_{\rho}\gamma^{\mu\tau}+\delta^{\tau}_{\rho}\gamma^{\mu\lambda}\right)

(44)

we continue to denote the unimodular objects with hatted symbols.

As a trivial check, under a Weyl transformation

\delta g_{\alpha\beta}=h_{\alpha\beta}=2\omega g_{\alpha\beta}

(45)

the Weyl current (
40

\displaystyle j_{W}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g^{\frac{2-n}{2n}}\Big[2\nabla_{\nu}(g^{\mu\nu}\omega)-\omega g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\nabla_{\mu}g}{g}-2\nabla^{\mu}\omega+\omega\frac{\nabla^{\mu}g}{g}\Big]=0

(46)

which vanishes identically. This is of course due to the tautological character of Weyl symmetry in our problem.

3
Schwarzschild’s UG spacetime.

As a simple application of the above, let us work out an explicit example, namely Schwarzschild spacetime from the Unimodular Gravity viewpoint. Schwarschild spacetime is still a solution of the equation of motion for Unimodular Gravity, although more general spherically symmetric vacuum solutions are possible. The reason is that for the usual Unimodular Hilbert Lagrangian

S_{\text{\tiny{H}}}[\gamma]\equiv-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n}x\hat{R}

2.1

\gamma=1

(61)

allowing Schwarzschild-(anti)-de Sitter spacetimes, in such a way that the naive version of Birkhoff’s theorem is not valid.

We started with a particular set of coordinates for the Schwarzschild metric, the Painlevé-Gullstrand (PG) coordinates

4
Conclusions

In the Weyl invariant formulation of Unimodular Gravity the unimodular metric is represented as

\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\equiv g^{-\frac{1}{4}}\,g_{\alpha\beta}

(66)

where the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
is unrestricted. This introduces a Weyl abelian symmetry under

g_{\alpha\beta}\rightarrow\Omega^{2}(x)\,g_{\alpha\beta}

(67)

The manifold of solutions is then a set of Weyl gauge orbits, with one representative in each orbit with a unimodular metric
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
and the other metrics in the orbit related to that one by Weyl rescalings

g_{\alpha\beta}=g(x)^{1\over n}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}

(68)

(with
g(x)
an arbitrary function).

This Weyl invariance is a bit peculiar; we call it
tautological
, in the sense that it disappears under a field redefinition. This translates into the fact that the associated Weyl current vanishes identically (in Hilbert’s version)

j_{W}^{\mu}=0

(69)

Let us make a comment on Weyl invariance. The horizon
r=r_{S}
in Schwarzschild’s spacetime has an associated Bekenstein-Hawking entropy
[
7
]
given by

S={A\over 4G}=\pi{r_{s}^{2}\over G}

(70)

which according to Wald
[
20
]
can be interpreted as the value of the Noether charge associated to diffeomorphism invariance. In the Weyl orbit there are static spacetimes without a Killing horizon (of course there are also non-static spacetimes without any Killing).
Given the fact that in the vicinity of the horizon

g_{00}\sim\frac{x-x_{s}}{r_{s}^{3}}+\ldots

(71)

(with
x_{s}\equiv\frac{r_{s}^{3}-b}{3}
) the rescaling

g(x)\sim\frac{1}{(x-x_{s})^{4}}

(72)

is singular at
x=x_{S}
, and there are corresponding singularities in the derivatives of
g(x)
. This is the the price to pay to get rid of the horizon. If only nonsingular rescalings are accepted, then
g_{00}=0
all along Weyl’s orbit.

The issue treated in this paper is that physical characteristics of different spacetimes (like total mass in the asymptotically flat case) are not Weyl invariant. This means that in some sense naive TDiff conserved currents are not constant on Weyl orbits. It seems desirable to have a definition of TDiff conserved currents that is Weyl invariant.

What we provide in this paper is precisely such a Weyl gauge invariant formulation of the TDiff currents, although we claim that the physical value is the one corresponding to the unimodular gauge fixing. This is more or less obvious owing to the way that Weyl invariance has been introduced in the theory.

Asymptotic convergence for a class of anisotropic curvature flows
Haizhong Li, BOTONG XU, RUIJIA ZHANG

Abstract
. In this paper, by using new auxiliary functions, we study a class of contracting flows of closed, star-shaped hypersurfaces in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} with speed r α β ​ σ k 1 β r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}} , where σ k \sigma_{k} is the k k -th elementary symmetric polynomial of the principal curvatures, α \alpha , β \beta are positive constants and r r is the distance from points on the hypersurface to the origin. We obtain convergence results under some assumptions of k k , α \alpha , β \beta . When k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , 0 < β ≤ 1 0<\beta\leq 1 , α ≥ β + k \alpha\geq\beta+k , we prove that the k k -convex solution to the flow exists for all time and converges smoothly to a sphere after normalization, in particular, we generalize Li-Sheng-Wang’s result from uniformly convex to k k -convex. When k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , β = k \beta=k , α ≥ 2 ​ k \alpha\geq 2k , we prove that the k k -convex solution to the flow exists for all time and converges smoothly to a sphere after normalization, in particular, we generalize Ling Xiao’s result from k = 2 k=2 to k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 .

1.
introduction

The classical Brunn-Minkowski theory plays an important role in the study of convex geometry and develops rapidly in recent years. Area measures and curvature measures are basic concepts in this theory. The classical Minkowski problem is a problem of prescribing a given
n
th area measure, which was finally settled by Cheng and Yau
[
8
]
. While, the Christoffel-Minkowski problem is a problem of prescribing a given
k
-th area measure. In
[
15
]
, Guan and Ma gave a sufficient condition for the existence of convex solution to this problem.

In 1962, Firey
[
11
]
generalized the Minkowski combination to p-sums from
p=1
to
p\geq 1
, which was used for developing a
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowki theory (see
[
27
]
). Later on, Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
16
]
(also see
[
26
]
) introduced the dual
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem.
This sort of problem aims to find a convex body
\Omega
satisfying the following equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle r^{-(n+1-q)}u^{1-p}\left(x\right)\det\left(D^{2}u+uI\right)=f(x)\quad{\rm on}\ \mathbb{S}^{n},

where
f
is a given positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
u
is the support function of
\Omega
,
D
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
r=\sqrt{u^{2}+|Du|^{2}}
. When
q=n+1
, it is the
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
25
,
24
,
34
]
). When
p=0
, it corresponds to the dual Minkowski problem (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
33
]
).
Meanwhile, Guan, Lin and Ma
[
14
]
considered the following prescribed
(n-k)
-th curvature measure problem

(1.2)

\displaystyle r^{n+1}u^{-1}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=f,

where
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
is defined as the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
of
\partial\Omega
, i.e.

\sigma_{m}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{m}}.

Combining (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), it’s interesting to consider the following prescribed curvature problem

(1.3)

\displaystyle r^{\alpha}u^{-\beta}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=f,

where
\alpha
,
\beta
are constants.
When
f
is a constant and
\beta=1
, the convex solution to equation (
1.3
) has been well studied (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
12
,
20
,
19
]
) . Therefore, it is natural for us to consider the following equation in this paper, i.e.
f\equiv c
in (
1.3
)

(1.4)

\displaystyle r^{\alpha}u^{-\beta}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=c.

Curvature flows are effective tools to study the above fully nonlinear partial differential equations (see, e.g.,

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first derive the evolution equations of the normalized flow (
1.8
).

2.1.
Evolution equation of the flow

For convenience, we rewrite (
1.8
) as

(2.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=-\Phi\nu(x,t)+\gamma X,

where
\Phi=r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}F
,
F=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
and
\gamma=(C_{n}^{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
.

Let
f(\kappa)
be a symmetric function of the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. There exists a function
\mathcal{F}(A)
, where
A=\{h_{i}{}^{j}\}
, such that
F(A)=f(\kappa)
.
Since
h_{i}{}^{j}=\sum_{l}h_{il}g^{lj}
,
\mathcal{F}
can be viewed as a function
\hat{\mathcal{F}}(h_{ij},g_{ij})
defined on the second fundamental form
\{h_{ij}\}
and the metric
\{g_{ij}\}
. We denote

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{pq}(A):=\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial h_{pq}}(h_{ij},g_{ij})\ ,\quad\ddot{\mathcal{F}}^{pq,rs}(A):=\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{\mathcal{F}}}{\partial h_{pq}\partial h_{rs}}(h_{ij},g_{ij}).

If we diagonalize
A
and
\{g_{ij}\}
at one point, we have

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{p}(\kappa):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\kappa_{p}}(\kappa)=\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{pp}(A)\ ,\quad\ddot{f}^{pq}(\kappa):=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\kappa_{p}\partial\kappa_{q}}=\ddot{\mathcal{F}}^{pp,qq}(A).

We refer to
[
4
]
for detailed properties of

\mathcal{F}(A)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Along the flow (
1.8
), we have the following evolution equations (also see
[
4
,
19
,
32
]
)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}=

\displaystyle-2\Phi h_{ij}+2\gamma g_{ij},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu=\nabla\Phi,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=

\displaystyle-\Phi+\gamma u+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\langle X,\nabla F\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Phi\langle X,\nabla\log r\rangle,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}{}^{j}=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{i}{}^{j}-\left(\frac{k}{\beta}-1\right)\Phi h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}h_{lq}h_{i}{}^{j}

\displaystyle+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{rs}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla^{j}F\nabla_{i}r

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}F\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1\right)r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2}F\nabla_{i}r\nabla^{j}r-\gamma h_{i}{}^{j},

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric on
M_{t}
.

Proof.

By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}=

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}\langle\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{i}\left(-\Phi\nu+\gamma X\right),\partial_{j}X\rangle+\langle\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}\left(-\Phi\nu+\gamma X\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Phi\left(\langle\partial_{i}\nu,\partial_{j}X\rangle+\langle\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}\nu\rangle\right)+2\gamma g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\Phi h_{ij}+2\gamma g_{ij},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\nu,\partial_{j}X\rangle g^{il}\partial_{l}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\nu,\partial_{j}\left(-\Phi\nu+\gamma X\right)\rangle g^{jl}\partial_{l}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\Phi g^{jl}\partial_{l}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi.

Using (
2.5
), we have the evolution of the support function
u

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle X,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-\Phi\nu+\gamma X,\nu\rangle+\langle X,\nabla\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Phi+\gamma u+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\langle X,\nabla F\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Phi\langle X,\nabla\log r\rangle.

Now we calculate the evolution of
h_{ij}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\langle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}X,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left(-\Phi\nu+\gamma X\right),\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi-\Phi h_{i}{}^{l}h_{lj}+\gamma h_{ij}.

From (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}=-g^{il}\left(\partial_{t}g_{lm}\right)g^{mj}=2\Phi h^{ij}-2\gamma g^{ij}.

Thus,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}{}^{j}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{il}g^{lj}+h_{il}\partial_{t}g^{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi+\Phi h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}-\gamma h_{i}{}^{j}.

For the first term of (
2.6
), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}F+\nabla^{j}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\nabla_{i}F+\nabla^{j}F\nabla_{i}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}+F\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}\nabla^{j}h_{rs}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla^{j}r\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla^{j}F\nabla_{i}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}F\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1\right)r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2}F\nabla^{j}r\nabla_{i}r.

Using the Codazzi equation, the Ricci identities and
\dot{F}^{pq}h_{q}{}^{l}=\dot{F}^{lq}h_{q}{}^{p}
, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}=

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla^{j}\nabla_{q}h_{pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{q}\nabla^{j}h_{pi}+\dot{F}^{pq}h_{i}{}^{l}R_{lpq}{}^{j}+\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}R_{liq}{}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{i}{}^{j}+\dot{F}^{pq}h_{i}{}^{l}h_{lq}h_{p}{}^{j}-\dot{F}^{pq}h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}h_{pq}+\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}h_{lq}h_{i}{}^{j}-\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}h_{qi}h_{l}{}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{i}{}^{j}-\frac{k}{\beta}Fh_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}+\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}h_{lq}h_{i}{}^{j},

where we used the fact that
\dot{F}^{pq}h_{pq}=\frac{k}{\beta}F
because
F
is homogeneous of degree
\frac{k}{\beta}
.
Inserting (
2.8
) and (
2.7
) into (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}{}^{j}=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{i}{}^{j}-\left(\frac{k}{\beta}-1\right)\Phi h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{l}h_{lq}h_{i}{}^{j}

\displaystyle+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}\nabla^{j}h_{rs}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla_{i}F\nabla^{j}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\nabla^{j}F\nabla_{i}r

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}F\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}r+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1\right)r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2}F\nabla_{i}r\nabla^{j}r-\gamma h_{i}{}^{j}.

∎

2.2.
Evolution of the radial graph

For a star-shaped hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we can parameterize it as a graph of the radial function
r(\theta):\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

\displaystyle M=\{r(\theta)\theta:\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\},

where
\theta=(\theta^{1},\cdots,\theta^{n})
is a local normal coordinate system of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
f_{i}=D_{i}f
,
f_{ij}=D_{ij}f
,
f_{ijk}=D_{ijk}f
, where
D
denotes the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Similar to
[
19
, p. 839]
, the flow (
1.8
) can be written as the scalar equation

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}r(\theta,t)=&-r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\sqrt{r^{2}+|Dr|^{2}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\gamma r,\\
r(\cdot,0)=&r_{0}(\cdot).\end{aligned}\right.

We define
\varphi=\log r
, then

(2.10)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log r=-e^{{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1})\varphi}\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\gamma.

Now we define
\rho=\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}
. The support function
u=\langle X,\nu\rangle
, induced metric
g_{ij}
and the second fundamental form
h_{ij}
of hypersurface
M
can be expressed as

(2.11)

\displaystyle u=\frac{r}{\rho},\quad g_{ij}=r^{2}(\delta_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}),\quad g^{ij}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\delta_{ij}-\frac{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}}{\rho^{2}}),

(2.12)

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\frac{r}{\rho}(\delta_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{ij}),\quad h_{i}{}^{j}=\frac{1}{r\rho}(\delta_{ij}-\varphi_{ij}+\frac{\varphi_{i}{}^{l}\varphi_{l}\varphi_{j}}{\rho^{2}}).

2.3.
Elementary symmetric functions

Here we state some algebraic properties of the elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)
,
m=1,\cdots,n
, where
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. We recall
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)
defined by

\sigma_{m}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{m}},\quad m=1,2,\cdots,n.

The Garding cone
\Gamma_{m}^{+}
is an open symmetric convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with vertex at the origin, given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}^{+}=\{\left(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sigma_{j}(\kappa)>0,\forall j=1,\cdots,m\}.

Clearly
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)=0
for
\kappa\in\partial\bar{\Gamma}_{m}^{+}
and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{+}\subset\cdots\subset{\Gamma}_{m}^{+}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma_{1}^{+}.

In particular,
\Gamma^{+}=\Gamma_{n}^{+}
is called the positive cone,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}=\{\left(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\kappa_{1}>0,\cdots,\kappa_{n}>0\}.

We collect some inequalities related to the polynomial
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
, which are needed in the following text. For
\kappa\in\Gamma_{m}^{+}
, from the symmetry of
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)
, we can assume that
\kappa_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n}
. Define
\sigma_{m}\left(\kappa|i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{j}\right)
as the
m
-th elementary symmetric function of
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n}
with
\kappa_{i_{1}}=0,\kappa_{i_{2}}=0,\cdots,\kappa_{i_{j}}=0
. Then
\frac{\partial\sigma_{m}}{\partial\kappa_{i}}(\kappa)=\sigma_{m-1}(\kappa|i)
. At the same time, if we set
\sigma_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{l}=0
for
l>n
, one can verify the following properties (see
[
12
,
21
]
,
[
31
, pp. 183-184]
)

(1)

\sigma_{m}(\kappa)=\sigma_{m}(\kappa|i)+\kappa_{i}\sigma_{m-1}(\kappa|i),

(2)

\sigma_{m}(\kappa)\sigma_{m-2}(\kappa)\leq\frac{(m-1)(n-m+1)}{m(n-m+2)}\sigma_{m-1}^{2}(\kappa),

(3)

\left(\frac{\sigma_{m}(\kappa)}{C_{n}^{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{l}(\kappa)}{C_{n}^{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{l}},1\leq l\leq m,

(4)

\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{m}}{\partial\kappa_{i}}\kappa_{i}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{m}-(m+1)\sigma_{m+1}
,

(5)

\kappa_{1}\sigma_{m-1}(\kappa|1)\geq\frac{m}{n}\sigma_{m}\left(\kappa\right).

We can also find the following properties (see
[
22
]
). If
\kappa\in\Gamma_{m}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}\geq

\displaystyle 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq m,

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}\left(\kappa|i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{j}\right)>

\displaystyle 0,\quad\forall\{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{j}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\cdots,n\},\ l+j\leq m.

Besides,
\left(\frac{\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}(\kappa)
and
\sigma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}(\kappa)
are concave on
\Gamma_{m}^{+}
.
\sigma_{m}^{\frac{1}{m}}(\kappa)
is inverse-concave on
\Gamma^{+}
. Here, we call
f(\kappa)
inverse-concave, if

\displaystyle f_{-1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n}):=f(\kappa_{1}^{-1},\kappa_{2}^{-1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{-1})^{-1}

is concave.
We refer readers to
[
7
,
30
]
for details.

3.
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates

In this section, we first give
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates of the normalized flow (
1.8
). As a corollary, we show that the hypersurface preserves star-shaped along the flow (
1.8
).

3.1.
C^{0}
estimate

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r(\cdot,t)
be a positive,
k
-convex smooth solution to (
1.8
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T)
. If
\alpha\geq k+\beta
,
\beta>0
, then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}r(\cdot,0)
and
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}r(\cdot,0)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq r(\cdot,t)\leq C,\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

Proof.

Now we focus on the scalar equation (
2.10
) of
\varphi
. Fix some time
t\in[0,T)
, at the maximum point
(\theta_{0},t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
of
\varphi(\cdot,t)
, we know
|D\varphi|=0
and
\{\varphi_{ij}\}
is negative semi-definite, which imply

\displaystyle\rho=1,\quad h_{i}^{j}=e^{-\varphi}(\delta_{ij}-\varphi_{ij})\geq e^{-\varphi}\delta_{ij}.

If we let
\varphi_{\max}(t)=\max\varphi(\cdot,t)
, from (
2.10
) and
\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(h_{i}{}^{j})\geq\gamma e^{-\frac{k}{\beta}\varphi}
at
(\theta_{0},t)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\varphi_{\max}}{dt}(t)=

\displaystyle-e^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1\right)\varphi_{\max}(t)}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(h_{i}{}^{j})+\gamma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\gamma e^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1-\frac{k}{\beta}\right)\varphi_{\max}(t)}+\gamma.

For the same reason, let
\varphi_{\min}(t)=\min\varphi(\cdot,t)
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d\varphi_{\min}}{dt}(t)\geq-\gamma e^{\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1-\frac{k}{\beta}\right)\varphi_{\min}(t)}+\gamma.

If
\alpha=\beta+k
, it means
\varphi_{\min}(0)\leq\varphi(t)\leq\varphi_{\max}(0)
along the normalized flow. If
\alpha>\beta+k
, it implies
\min\{\varphi_{\min}(0),0\}\leq\varphi(t)\leq\max\{\varphi_{\max}(0),0\}
. So we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq r\leq C

for some constant
C>0
, which only depends on the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
.
∎

3.2.
C^{1}
estimate

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r(\cdot,t)
be a positive, smooth solution to (
1.8
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T)
. If
\alpha\geq k+\beta
,
\beta>0
, we have

|Dr|\leq C,

where
C
only depends on
M_{0}
.

Proof.

For a fixed time
t
, we assume that
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the spatial maximum point of
\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2}
. At
(\theta_{0},t)
, we have by use of (
2.10
)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})=

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\partial_{t}\varphi)_{i}=\varphi_{i}(-e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}-e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}\varphi_{i}(\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i},

where we used
\rho_{i}=(\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}),_{i}=0
at
(\theta_{0},t)
. For the last term of (
3.2
), we know

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}{}^{q}}h_{p}{}^{q}{},_{i}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\sigma_{k}^{pq}g_{qs}h_{p}{}^{s}{},_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\sigma_{k}^{pq}\left((g_{qs}h_{p}{}^{s}),_{i}-h_{p}{}^{s}g_{qs,i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\sigma_{k}^{pq}(h_{pq,i}-h_{p}{}^{s}g_{qs,i}),

where we regarded
h_{pq}
,
g_{qs}
as tensors on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Now we calculate the term
h_{pq,i}
. Differentiating
\varphi_{i}=\frac{r_{i}}{r}
, we have
\varphi_{ij}=\frac{r_{ij}}{r}-\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}
, which gives
\frac{r_{ij}}{r}=\varphi_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}
.
By (
2.12
), we have at
(\theta_{0},t)

(3.4)

\displaystyle h_{pq,i}=\frac{r}{\rho}\varphi_{i}(\delta_{pq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\varphi_{pq})+\frac{r}{\rho}(\varphi_{pi}\varphi_{q}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{qi}-\varphi_{pqi}).

From (
2.11
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have at
(\theta_{0},t)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}h_{pq,i}\varphi_{i}-\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}(\sigma_{k})^{pq}h_{p}{}^{s}g_{qs,i}\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\frac{r}{\rho}\left(|D\varphi|^{2}(\delta_{pq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\varphi_{pq})+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{q}\varphi_{pi}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{p}\varphi_{qi}-\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pqi}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{s}\varphi_{i}\left(2e^{2\varphi}(\delta_{qs}+\varphi_{q}\varphi_{s})\varphi_{i}+e^{2\varphi}\varphi_{qi}\varphi_{s}+e^{2\varphi}\varphi_{q}\varphi_{si}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{r}{\rho}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\delta_{pq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\varphi_{pq})|D\varphi|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{r}{\rho}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pqi}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\beta}e^{2\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}^{pq}h_{p}{}^{s}(\delta_{qs}+\varphi_{q}\varphi_{s})|D\varphi|^{2},

where we used
\frac{1}{2}D_{p}|D\varphi|^{2}=\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pi}=0
. Inserting (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) into (
3.5
), we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}k\sigma_{k}|D\varphi|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{e^{\varphi}}{\rho}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pqi}-\frac{2}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}k\sigma_{k}|D\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{e^{\varphi}}{\rho}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pqi}.

By the Ricci identity

\displaystyle\varphi_{pqi}=

\displaystyle\varphi_{piq}+\varphi_{l}R^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{lpqi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{ipq}+\varphi_{l}(\delta_{lq}\delta_{pi}-\delta_{li}\delta_{pq})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{ipq}+\varphi_{q}\delta_{pi}-\varphi_{i}\delta_{pq}.

Thus

(3.7)

\varphi_{i}\varphi_{pqi}=\varphi_{i}\varphi_{ipq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}=(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})_{pq}-\varphi_{ip}\varphi_{iq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}.

Putting (
3.7
) into (
3.6
), we have

(3.8)

\varphi_{i}(\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})_{i}=-\frac{k}{\beta}\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{e^{\varphi}}{\rho}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\left((\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})_{pq}-\varphi_{ip}\varphi_{iq}+\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}\right).

Plugging (
3.8
) into (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})=

\displaystyle-(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\rho\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}+e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\rho\frac{k}{\beta}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\beta}e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\rho\sigma_{k}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\frac{e^{\varphi}}{\rho}\left(\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})_{pq}-\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{ip}\varphi_{iq}+\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha-\beta-k)}{\beta}e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\rho\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\beta}e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\left(\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})_{pq}-\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{ip}\varphi_{iq}+\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}-\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha-\beta-k)}{\beta}e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\rho\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}|D\varphi|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\varphi_{ip}\varphi_{iq}

(3.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}),

where the last inequality was from
(|D\varphi|^{2})_{pq}\leq 0
at
(\theta_{0},t)
. When
\alpha\geq\beta+k
, from
\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}\geq 0
and
\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}\geq 0
, we get
\partial_{t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})\leq 0
at
(\theta_{0},t)
. So
|D\varphi|
is bounded from above by a constant. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
|Dr|\leq C
for some constant
C
.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the same assumptions in Lemma
3.1
, along the normalized flow (
1.8
), the hypersurface
M_{t}
preserves star-shaped and the support function
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\leq u\leq C

for some constant
C>0
, where
C
only depends on the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
.

Proof.

As
|D\varphi|
and
r
are bounded by two positive constants, it follows that

\displaystyle u=\frac{r}{\rho}=\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}}

is also bounded, i.e.,
\frac{1}{C}\leq u\leq C
for some
C
. Since
u=\langle X,\nu\rangle=r\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle=\frac{u}{r}=\frac{1}{\rho}=(1+|D\varphi|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}

for some
C^{\prime}>0
. Thus, the hypersurface preserves star-shaped along the flow (
1.8
).
∎

4.
C^{2}
estimates

In this section, we will derive the
C^{2}
estimates of flow (
1.8
). To simplify the statements of following lemmas, we always assume
\alpha\geq\beta+k
and the initial hypersurface is smooth, closed, star-shaped and at least
k
-convex. Besides, we define a parabolic operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\partial_{t}-r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}.

4.1.
The bounds of
F=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Along the normalized flow (
1.8
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

F\geq C.

Proof.

First, we calculate the evolution equation of
\Phi
. From (
2.6
), we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\partial_{t}F+F\partial_{t}\left(r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{i}{}^{j}}\partial_{t}h_{i}{}^{j}+\frac{\alpha}{2\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2}F\partial_{t}\langle X,X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{i}{}^{j}}(\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi+\Phi h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}-\gamma h_{i}{}^{j})+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2}F(-\Phi u+\gamma r^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Phi\dot{F}^{ij}h_{i}{}^{l}h_{lj}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\Phi^{2}}{r^{2}}u+\frac{(\alpha-k)\gamma}{\beta}\Phi.

At the spatial minimum point of
\Phi
on
M_{t}
, we have
r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi\geq 0
and
r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Phi\dot{F}^{ij}h_{i}{}^{l}h_{l}{}^{j}=r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Phi\dot{f}^{i}\kappa_{i}{}^{2}\geq 0
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{\min}(t)\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\Phi_{\min}^{2}(t)}{r^{2}}u+\frac{(\alpha-k)\gamma}{\beta}\Phi_{\min}(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-c_{1}\Phi_{\min}^{2}(t)+\gamma\Phi_{\min}(t),

for some constant
c_{1}>0
, where
c_{1}
can be given by Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.1
. If
0\leq\Phi_{\min}(t)\leq\frac{\gamma}{c_{1}}
, we have
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{\min}(t)\geq 0
. Therefore
\Phi_{\min}(t)\geq\min\{\Phi_{\min}(0),\frac{\gamma}{c_{1}}\}
. Since
F=r^{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\Phi
and
r
is bounded,
F
is bounded from below by a constant
C
that only depends on
M_{0}
,
\alpha
,
\beta
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Along the normalized flow (
1.8
), there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

F\leq C.

Proof.

Let
Q=\log\Phi-\log(u-a)
, where
a=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{M\times[0,T)}u
. Now we calculate the evolution of
u
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}u=

\displaystyle\langle X,\partial_{i}\nu\rangle=h_{i}{}^{l}\langle X,\partial_{l}X\rangle,

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u=

\displaystyle\nabla^{l}h_{ij}\langle X,\partial_{l}X\rangle+h_{ij}-h_{i}{}^{l}h_{lj}u,

where we used the Codazzi equation in the second equality. From (
2.3
), we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1+\frac{k}{\beta}\right)\Phi+\gamma u+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Phi\langle X,\nabla\log r\rangle+r^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{ij}h_{i}{}^{l}h_{lj}u.

For a fixed time
t
, we calculate at the maximum point of
Q

As a consequence, we can prove a similar result to Theorem

. Because the argument is similar, we omit the explicit proof of the conclusion.

Now we have obtained the priori estimates of flow (
1.8
), with the initial hypersurfaces mentioned in Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
. From Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
4.1
, these flows have short time existence. Using the
C^{2}
estimates given in Lemma
4.3
, Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
, due to Krylov
[
18
]
, we can get the
C^{2,\lambda}
estimate of the scalar equation (
2.9
). Hence, we get the long time existence of these flows.

Lemma 4.8
.

The smooth solution of (
1.8
) with the initial hypersuface mentioned in Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
or Theorem
1.4
exists for all time

.

5.
Asymptotic convergence and proofs of Theorem
1.1
–
1.4

In this section, we improve the
C^{1}
estimates in Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
r(\cdot,t)
be the solution to (
1.8
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T)
. We admit the priori estimates given in Section
3
,
4
for various situations and assume that the corresponding hypersurfaces
M_{t}
are
k
-convex along the normalized flow. If
\alpha\geq\beta+k
,
\beta>0
and
n\geq 2
, there exist positive constants
C
and
a
only depend on
M_{0}
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
k
, such that

\frac{|Dr(\cdot,t)|}{r(\cdot,t)}\leq Ce^{-at},\quad\forall\ t>0.

Proof.

Same as the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we only need to calculate at the maximum point p of
|D\varphi|^{2}
for each time.

Case 1:
\alpha>\beta+k
. By (
3.9
), we obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})\leq-\left(\frac{(\alpha-\beta-k)}{\beta}e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1)\varphi}\sqrt{1+|D\varphi|^{2}}\sigma_{k}{}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)|D\varphi|^{2}.

Then from the priori estimates, we know
\partial_{t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})\leq-a|D\varphi|^{2}
for some
a>0
and get the exponential convergence of the flow (
1.8
).

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta+k
. By (
3.9
), we have

(5.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})\leq-\frac{1}{\beta}e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q}).

At p, we have from (
2.12
)

(5.2)

\displaystyle h_{i}{}^{j}=\frac{1}{r\rho}(\delta_{ij}-\varphi_{ij}+\frac{\varphi_{i}{}^{l}\varphi_{l}\varphi_{j}}{\rho^{2}})=\frac{1}{r\rho}(\delta_{ij}-\varphi_{ij}),

where we used
\varphi_{i}{}^{l}\varphi_{l}=0
at p.
Multiplying the both sides of (
5.2
) by
\varphi_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}\varphi_{j}=\frac{1}{r\rho}(\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{ij}\varphi_{j})=\frac{1}{r\rho}\varphi_{i},

which means
e_{1}=\frac{D\varphi}{|D\varphi|}
is an eigenvector of
\{h_{i}^{j}\}
with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{r\rho}
. For convenience, we choose a local orthonormal frame filed
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
such that
\{h_{i}^{j}\}
is diagonal at p. Then from (
2.11
), we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\sigma_{k}{}^{pq}(\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}{}^{s}}g^{qs}(\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}{}^{s}}e^{-2\varphi}(\delta_{qs}-\frac{\varphi_{q}\varphi_{s}}{\rho^{2}})(\delta_{pq}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2)\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}{}^{s}}(\delta_{ps}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{s}).

Now we use the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
to estimate the right hand side of (
5.3
),

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}{}^{s}}(\delta_{ps}|D\varphi|^{2}-\varphi_{p}\varphi_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D\varphi|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)-\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|p)\varphi_{p}{}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa)-\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa)+\kappa_{1}\sigma_{k-2}(\kappa|1)\right)|D\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((n-k)\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa)+\kappa_{1}\sigma_{k-2}(\kappa|1)\right)|D\varphi|^{2}.

From (
5.1
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{2}|D\varphi|^{2})\leq-e^{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-2)\varphi}\sigma_{k}{}^{(\frac{1}{\beta}-1)}\left((n-k)\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa)+\kappa_{1}\sigma_{k-2}(\kappa|1)\right)|D\varphi|^{2}.

Now we divide the discussion into two subcases.

Subcase 2.1:
k<n
. By Newton-MacLaurin inequality and the fact
\sigma_{k}\geq c
, we have
(\sigma_{k-1})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\geq c_{1}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{k}}\geq c_{2}>0
for some constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
. Since
(\kappa|1)\in\Gamma^{+}_{k-1}
, we know
\sigma_{k-2}(\kappa|1)>0
. Then
\partial_{t}|D\varphi|^{2}\leq-c|D\varphi|^{2}
and the exponentially decay of
\frac{|Dr|^{2}}{r^{2}}=|D\varphi|^{2}
follows from standard argument.

Subcase 2.2:
k=n\geq 2
. In this situation, the principal curvatures have uniform upper and lower bounds by Lemma
4.7
. Therefore
\partial_{t}|D\varphi|^{2}\leq-c|D\varphi|^{2}
for some
c
at point
p
, where
c
only depends on the priori estimates. Hence the lemma is true.
∎

6.
A counter example

In this section, we prove that if
\alpha<\beta+k
, the flow (
1.8
) may have unbounded ratio of radii, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\left(X\left(\cdot,t\right)\right):=\frac{\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}r(\cdot,t)}{\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}r(\cdot,t)}\rightarrow\infty\quad{\rm as}\quad t\rightarrow T

for some
T>0
. Since the construction of the counter example is almost the same as
[
19
, Section 5]
. In this section, we only give a sketch proof of Proposition
1.2
. For more details, we refer to
[
20
, Section 6]
and
[
32
, Section 7]
.

First, we consider a function defined on
B_{1}^{n}(0)\times[-1,0)
,

(6.1)

\phi(x,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &-|t|^{\theta}+|t|^{-\theta+\sigma\theta}|x|^{2},\quad&{\rm if}\ |x|<|t|^{\theta},\\
&-|t|^{\theta}-\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}|t|^{\theta(1+\sigma)}+\frac{2}{1+\sigma}|x|^{1+\sigma},\quad&{\rm if}\ |t|^{\theta}\leq|x|\leq 1,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\sigma=\frac{q\theta-\beta}{k\theta}
,
q=-\alpha+\beta+k>0
and
\theta>\frac{1}{q}
is a positive constant.

Next, for every
t\in(-1,0)
, we extend the graph of
\phi(\cdot,t)
to a closed, convex, rotationally symmetric hypersurface
\hat{M}_{t}
enclosing
B_{1}(z)
, where
z=(0,\cdots,0,10)
. By choosing
a
large enough, we can see that
\hat{M}_{t}
is a sub-solution to

(6.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}r(x,t)=&-ar^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\\
r(\cdot,0)=&r_{0}(\cdot).\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists a large constant
C
such that
\hat{M}_{t}\subset B_{C}(0)
for all
t\in(-1,0)
. Define
b=(2C)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}
, we construct a new flow,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{X}(x,t)=-ba\tilde{r}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\nu(x,t),

where
\tilde{r}=|X-z|
. We can choose
\tau\in(-1,0)
close to 0 and the initial hypersurface of the flow as
\tilde{X}(\cdot,\tau)=\partial B_{1}^{n}(z)
, such that the flow enclosed
B_{\frac{1}{2}}(z)
on time interval
(\tau,0)
. We select a hypersurface
M_{0}
inside
\hat{M}_{\tau}
and enclosing the ball
B_{1}(z)
. By the comparison principle,
M_{t_{0}}
touch the origin at some
t_{0}\in(\tau,0)
and meantime enclose
B_{\frac{1}{2}}(z)
.

Finally, rescaling
M_{t}
to
\tilde{M}_{t}=a^{-\frac{1}{q}}M_{t}
, one easily verifies that
\tilde{M}_{t}
is a solution to flow (
1.5
) and satisfies the property (
1.9
). We complete the proof of Proposition
1.2
.

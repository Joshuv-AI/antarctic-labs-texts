A Conforming virtual element approximation for the Oseen eigenvalue problem Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Danilo Amigo, Felipe Lepe, Nitesh Verma

Abstract
In this paper we analyze a conforming virtual element method to approximate the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of the two dimensional Oseen eigenvalue problem. We consider the classic velocity-pressure formulation which allows us to consider the divergence-conforming virtual element spaces employed for the Stokes equations. Under standard assumptions on the meshes we derive a priori error estimates for the proposed method with the aid of the compact operators theory. We report some numerical tests to confirm the theoretical results.



keywords

Oseen equations, eigenvalue problems, virtual element method, a priori estimates and error analysis.

Introduction

This paper is focused on the numerical approximation of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Oseen spectral problem. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open and bounded polygonal domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
The equations of the Oseen eigenvalue problem are given as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p&=&\lambda\boldsymbol{u},&\text{in}\,\Omega,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}&=&0,&\text{in}\,\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=&\boldsymbol{0},&\text{on}\,\partial\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p&=&0,&\text{in}\,\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{u}
is the displacement,
p
is the pressure and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is a given vector field, representing a
steady flow velocity
and
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity.

The Oseen problem is a linearised version of the Navier-Stokes equations. The difference is precisely on the convective term that in (
1
) is linear, something that on the Navier-Stokes system is not, leading to a number of well known difficulties. The literature on fluid mechanics and more precisely on the Navier-Stokes and related problems as the Oseen system is well established and we refer to
[
11
,
13
]
as main references on the mathematical and numerical analysis. Let us mention that these references, among others, are focused on the load problem associated to fluid mechanics. Our contribution goes beyond that.

Our aim is to analyze numerically the eigenvalue problem (
1
). This problem has the particularity of being non-selfadjoint, where the desirable symmetry is no longer valid due the presence of the convective term. Recently in
[
17
]
, problem (
1
) has been studied under the approach of a finite element method. Moreover, important properties of the problem at the continuous level are now available, such as the relation between the spectrum of the Oseen and Stokes eigensystems when the convective term of Oseen vanishes, stability of the Oseen eigenvalue problem and its dual counterpart and, from the numerical point of view, a priori and a posteriori error estimates and of course, numerical results that help to valid new methods.

The present paper is focused in the development and analysis of a virtual element method (VEM) to approximate the solutions of (
1
) on the polygonal meshes. To do this task, we need to resort to suitable virtual spaces to approximate the solutions of Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations, or variations of these systems. On this subject, the literature available is ongoing progress for different formulations such as
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
10
,
21
]
among others. However, our intention is to apply these spaces for an eigenvalue problem, which is a different topic that has its intrinsic difficulties in the analysis, which are documented on other works using the VEM to approximate eigenvalues and eigenfunction. On this subject we refer to
[
1
,
16
]
as recent contributions. Following this, our approach for the analysis of (
1
) is the classic velocity-pressure formulation in order to extend the results provided by
[
1
]
for the a priori error analysis of the Stokes eigenvalue problem to our Oseen system, where the conforming virtual spaces under consideration are those proposed in
[
21
]
. The main difference is the lack of symmetry of the Oseen spectral problem, which demands to consider here the discrete dual problem in order to derive error estimates. Hence, the VEM spaces employed by
[
21
]
need to be capable to discretize properly such a dual problem.
According to
[
17
]
, the solution operator for the Oseen eigenvalue problem results to be compact
and hence, the classic theory for compact operators of
[
5
]
is applied in order to derive error estimates for the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, together with the spurious free feature of the method. On this subject, it is important to perform an extra computational analysis, since the VEM on its nature, depends on some stabilization terms in order to scale correctly the discrete bilinear forms with respect to the continuous ones. These stabilizations depend on physical parameters and the geometrical properties of the mesh and the domain, so if it is not correctly scaled, spurious eigenvalues may appear as in, for instance,
[
1
,
2
,
16
,
19
]
. Hence, a complete computational study of the method is performed in order to confirm the order of convergence and the manner in which the spurious eigenvalues are precisely avoided according to the theory.

The structure of our paper is as follows: Section
2
is dedicated to present the variational framework in which our work is supported, where sesquilinear forms, primal and adjoint eigenvalue problems, continuous solution operators and regularity results are presented. In section
3
we introduce the virtual element method for the numerical approximation of the problem (
1
).
This implies the presentation of the virtual element spaces, degrees of freedom, discrete seminorms, discrete sesquilinear forms and of course the VEM discretization for the primal and adjoint problems. Finally, in section
4
, we present numerical tests to show the computed order of convergence in convex and non-convex domains, and also, observed the influence of stabilization parameters on the computation of the spectrum.

2
The variational formulation

We begin by assuming that the viscosity
\nu
is bounded, more precisely, we assume the existence of two constants

\nu^{+}
and
\nu^{-}
such that the
\nu^{-}<\nu<\nu^{+}
. On the steady flow velocity, we assume that
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
and the following standard assumptions (see
[
13
]
):

•

\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\sim 1
if
\nu\leq\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}
,

•

\nu\sim 1
if
\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}<\nu
,

where the first point is the case more close to the real applications. Moreover, we assume the existence of a number
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathrm{L}^{2+\varepsilon_{1}}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
. This leads to the skew-symmetry of the convective term (see
[
13
, Remark 5.6]
) which claims that for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, there holds

\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}.

(2)

Now we introduce the functional spaces and norms for our analysis. To simplify the presentation of the material, let us define the spaces

\mathcal{X}:=\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{Y}:=\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\times\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
. For the space
\mathcal{X}
we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}:=\|\cdot\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\|\cdot\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}
whereas for
\mathcal{Y}
the norm will be
\|(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}
, for all
(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Let us introduce the following sesquilinear forms
a:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

and
b:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

a(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}):=a^{\nabla}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})+a^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})\quad\text{and}\quad b(\boldsymbol{v},q):=-\int_{\Omega}q\,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v},

where
a^{\nabla},a^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are two sesquilinear forms defined by

a^{\nabla}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{w}:\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\quad\quad a^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}-\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\right),

whereas
c(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}):=(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})_{0,\Omega}
is the standard inner product in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
. Observe that the resulting eigenvalue problem is non-symmetric due the presence of the sesquilinear form
a^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

With these sesquilinear forms at hand, we write the following weak formulation for (
1
): Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})+b(\boldsymbol{v},p)&=&\lambda c(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\\
b(\boldsymbol{u},q)&=&0&\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\end{array}\right.

(3)

where

\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}):=\left\{q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\,:\,\int_{\Omega}q=0\right\}.

Let us define the kernel
\mathcal{K}
of
b(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows

\mathcal{K}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\,:\,b(\boldsymbol{v},q)=0\,\,\,\,\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\}.

With this space at hand, it is easy to check with the aid of (
2
) that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{K}
-coercive. Moreover, bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following inf-sup condition

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}}\frac{b(\boldsymbol{\tau},q)}{\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\geq\beta\|q\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}).

(4)

Let
\boldsymbol{T}
be the so-called solution operator which is defined as follows

\boldsymbol{T}:\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\qquad\boldsymbol{f}\mapsto\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{f}:=\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},

(5)

where the pair
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathcal{X}
is the solution of the following well-posed source problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v})+b(\boldsymbol{v},\widehat{p})&=&c(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\\
b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},q)&=&0&\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\end{array}\right.

(6)

implying that
\boldsymbol{T}
is well defined due to the Babuška-Brezzi theory. Moreover, from
[
13
, Lemma 5.8]
we have the following estimate for the velocity

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\frac{C_{pf}}{\nu}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\Omega)},

(7)

and for the pressure we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{p}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\Omega)}+\nu^{1/2}\|\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,\Omega}\left(\nu^{1/2}+C_{pf}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{0,\infty}}{\nu^{1/2}}\right)\right),

(8)

where
C_{pf}>0
represents the constant of the Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality and
\beta>0
is the inf-sup constant given in (
4
).

We observe that
(\lambda,(\boldsymbol{u},p))\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{X}
solves (
3
) if and only if
(\kappa,\boldsymbol{u})
is an eigenpair of
\boldsymbol{T},i.e.,\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}=\kappa\boldsymbol{u}
with
\kappa:=1/\lambda
.

Under the assumptions on
\boldsymbol{\beta}
, we have that
a^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is well defined and hence, it is enough to consider the classic Stokes regularity results (see
[
20
]
for instance) in order to derive the following additional regularity result for the solutions of the Oseen system.

Theorem 1
.

There exists
s>0
such that for all
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, the solution
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathcal{X}
of problem (
6
), satisfies for the velocity
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, for the pressure
\widehat{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
, and

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}\|_{s,\Omega}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},.

where
C:=\dfrac{C_{pf}}{\beta}\max\left\{1,\dfrac{C_{pf}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}}{\nu}\right\}
and
\beta>0
is the constant associated to the inf-sup condition (
4
).

The above regularity result, together with the compact inclusion
\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, allows us to conclude the compactness of
\boldsymbol{T}
. Now we have the following spectral characterization for
\boldsymbol{T}
.

Lemma 2
.

(Spectral Characterization of
\boldsymbol{T}
).
The spectrum of
\boldsymbol{T}
is such that
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{T})=\{0\}\cup\{\kappa_{k}\}_{k\in{N}}
where
\{\kappa_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is a sequence of complex eigenvalues that converge to zero, according to their respective multiplicities.

We conclude this section by redefining the spectral problem (
3
) in order to simplify the notations for the forthcoming analysis. With this in mind, let us introduce the sesquilinear form
A:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

A((\boldsymbol{u},p);(\boldsymbol{v},q)):=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})+b(\boldsymbol{v},p)-b(\boldsymbol{u},q),\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X},

which allows us to rewrite problem (
3
) as follows: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\mathcal{X}
such that

A((\boldsymbol{u},p),(\boldsymbol{v},q))=\lambda c(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}.

(9)

From
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
, we have that the following inf-sup conditions for
A(\cdot,\cdot)
holds

\begin{split}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w},r)\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}}{\sup}\dfrac{A((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{w},r))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|\|(\boldsymbol{w},r)\|}&=\gamma,\\
\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w},r)\in\mathcal{X}}{\sup}\dfrac{A((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{w},r))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|\|(\boldsymbol{w},r)\|}&=\gamma,\end{split}

(10)

where
\gamma
is a positive constant uniform with respect to
\nu
.

Since problem (
3
) is non-selfadjoint, it is necessary to introduce the adjoint eigenvalue problem, which reads as follows: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and a pair
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u}^{*},p^{*})\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}^{*})-b(p^{*},\boldsymbol{v})&=&\overline{\lambda}c(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}^{*})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\\
-b(q,\boldsymbol{u}^{*})&=&0&\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\end{array}\right.

(11)

Now we introduce the adjoint solution operator defined by

\boldsymbol{T}^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\qquad\boldsymbol{f}\mapsto\boldsymbol{T}^{*}\boldsymbol{f}:=\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*},

(12)

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
is the adjoint velocity of
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}
and solves the following adjoint source problem: Find

(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*},\widehat{p}^{*})\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a(\boldsymbol{v},\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*})-b(\widehat{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{v})&=&c(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{f})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\\
-b(q,\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*})&=&0&\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}).\end{array}\right.

(13)

Similar to Theorem
1
, let us assume that the dual source and eigenvalue problems are such that the following estimate holds.

Theorem 3
.

There exists
s^{*}>0
such that for all
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, the solution
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*},\widehat{p}^{*})
of problem (
13
), satisfies
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s^{*}}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
and
\widehat{p}^{*}\in\mathrm{H}^{s^{*}}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
, and

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}\|_{1+s^{*},\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}^{*}\|_{s^{*},\Omega}\leq C^{*}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},

where
C^{*}>0
.

Now we hace the following spectral characterization for
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
.

Lemma 4
.

(Spectral Characterization of
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
).
The spectrum of
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
is such that
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{T}^{*})=\{0\}\cup\{\kappa_{k}^{*}\}_{k\in{N}}
where
\{\kappa_{k}^{*}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is a sequence of complex eigenvalues that converge to zero, according to their respective multiplicities.

It is easy to prove that if
\kappa
is an eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{T}
with multiplicity
m
,
\overline{\kappa}
is an eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
with the same multiplicity
m
.

Let us define the sesquilinear form
\widehat{A}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\widehat{A}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{u}^{*},p^{*})):=a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}^{*})-b(p^{*},\boldsymbol{v})+b(q,\boldsymbol{u}^{*}),

which allows us to rewrite the dual eigenvalue problem (
11
) as follows: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and the pair
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u}^{*},p^{*})\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\widehat{A}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{u}^{*},p^{*}))=\lambda(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}^{*})\quad\forall(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}.

(14)

Similar to the continuous case, from
[
17
, Lemma 2.7]
we have that
\widehat{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following inf-sup conditions

\begin{split}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w},r)\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}}{\sup}\dfrac{\widehat{A}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{w},r))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|\|(\boldsymbol{w},r)\|}&=\gamma^{*},\\
\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\mathcal{X}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w},r)\in\mathcal{X}}{\sup}\dfrac{\widehat{A}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\boldsymbol{w},r))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|\|(\boldsymbol{w},r)\|}&=\gamma^{*},\end{split}

where
\gamma^{*}
is a positive constant uniform with respect to
\nu
.

Remark 5
.

Let us note the following: since we are considering primal and dual problems, and consequently we have primal and dual solutions, according to Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
, the corresponding constants on the estimates of these theorems depend on the nature of the problem and, strictly speaking, are not the same. We need to take this into account in the following analysis because, in order to avoid an excess of notation, some constants will be generic, regardless of whether the problem is primal or dual.

3
The virtual element method

The following section is dedicated to the analysis of the virtual element approximation for the eigenproblem
presented in Problem
3
. To do this task, first we need to introduce the following definitions.
Let
\left\{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}_{h>0}
be a sequence of decompositions of
\Omega

into polygons
K
. Besides, we will denote by
\ell
a generic edge of
\partial K
and by
h_{\ell}
its length. The set of all the edges in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Let us denote by
h_{K}
the diameter of the element
K

and
h
the maximum of the diameters of all the elements of the mesh,
i.e.,
h:=\max_{K\in\Omega}h_{K}
.

For the mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
we will consider
the following assumptions:

•

A1.
There exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that, for all meshes

{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, each polygon
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is star-shaped with respect to a ball
of radius greater than or equal to
\gamma h_{K}
.

•

A2.
The distance between any two vertexes of
K
is
\geq Ch_{K}
, where
C
is a positive constant.

Sesquilinear forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)
can be decomposed into local contributions as follows

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})+a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})\qquad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},

\displaystyle b(\boldsymbol{v},q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v},q)\qquad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\text{and }q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle c(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}c^{K}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})\qquad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}.

In the same way, we split elementwise the norms of
\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
and
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},

\displaystyle\|q\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|q\|_{0,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}).

Further, we introduce the following function space of piecewise
\mathrm{H}^{1}
functions

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,{\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}~:\boldsymbol{v}|_{K}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})^{n}\},

associated with the semi-norm

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1,h}:=\left(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

Inspired by
[
21
,
22
]
, we will construct the virtual spaces to analyze the discrete problem.
Let us begin with the velocity virtual space:
for a simple polygon
K
we define

\boldsymbol{\mathbb{B}}_{\partial K}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in[C^{0}(\partial K)]^{2}:\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\ell}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(\ell)\text{ and }\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}|_{\ell}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\ell)\ \ \forall\ell\subset\partial K\}.

With this space at hand, we consider the
following local finite dimensional
space:

\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in[\mathrm{H}^{1}(K)]^{2}:\Delta\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\nabla s\in\nabla\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)^{\perp}\text{ for some }s\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(E),\right.\\
\left.\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{\partial K}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{B}}_{\partial K}\right\}.

The following linear operators are well defined for all
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}
:

•

\mathcal{V}_{K}^{h}
: The (vector) values of
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
at the vertices.

•

\mathcal{L}_{K}^{h}
: the value of

\frac{1}{|\ell|}\int_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\quad\forall\text{ edges }\ell\in\partial K.

•

\mathcal{K}_{K}^{h}
: the value of

\int_{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot g^{\perp}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{g}^{\perp}\in\nabla\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)^{\perp}.

Let us remark that the set of linear operators
\mathcal{V}_{K}^{h}
,
\mathcal{L}_{K}^{h}
, and
\mathcal{K}_{K}^{h}
constitutes a set of degrees of
freedom for the local virtual space
\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}
(see
[
21
]
). Moreover, it is easy to check that

[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}\subset\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}
. This will guarantee the good approximation properties for the space. Now we define the projector

\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}:\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}\longrightarrow[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}\subset\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}

for each
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}
as the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a^{\nabla,K}(\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})&=a^{\nabla,K}(\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\quad\forall\mathbf{p}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2},\\
\\
\displaystyle|\partial K|^{-1}\int_{\partial K}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}&=\displaystyle|\partial K|^{-1}\int_{\partial K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}.\end{array}\right.

We observe that
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{K}
is well defined on
\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}
and computable. Now we define the local virtual element space
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
as follows,

\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\boldsymbol{V}}_{h}^{K}:\displaystyle\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}^{\perp}=\int_{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}^{\perp}\text{ for }\boldsymbol{g}^{\perp}\in\nabla\mathbb{P}_{2}(K)^{\perp}\right\}.

In addition, the standard
[{\mathrm{L}}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C})]^{2}
-projector operator

\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}:\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}\to[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}
can be computed. In fact, for all
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
, the function
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}
is defined by:

\displaystyle\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\left(\nabla p_{2}+\nabla p_{2}^{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{K}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})p_{2}+\int_{\partial K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n})p_{2}+\displaystyle\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{2}^{\perp},

for every
\boldsymbol{p}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}
.

Remark 6
.

In the case when we need to denote the aforementioned projectors as global, we will drop the superindex
K
when is necessary.

Now we introduce the global virtual space:
for every decomposition
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
of
\Omega
into
simple polygons
K
, we define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{V}_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}:\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{K}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K},\quad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}.

In agreement with the local choice of the degrees of freedom, in

\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
we choose the following degrees of freedom:

•

\mathcal{V}^{h}
: the (vector) values of
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
at the vertices of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

•

\mathcal{L}^{h}
: the value of

\frac{1}{|\ell|}\int_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\quad\forall\ell\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}.

On the other hand, the pressure space is given by

\mathrm{Q}_{h}:=\{q_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\,:\,q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K),\,\quad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

where the degrees of freedom are one per element, given by the value of the function on the element. Moreover and for simplicity, we define
\mathcal{X}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.

3.1
The discrete sesquilinear forms

Now we will introduce the discrete versions of the sesquilinear forms
a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let us begin by introducing the discrete counterpart of
a^{\nabla}(\cdot,\cdot)
, which is defined by

a^{\nabla}_{h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),

where
a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the sesquilinear form defined on

\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}\times\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
by

a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=a^{\nabla,K}\big(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\big)+S^{K}\big(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\big),

for every
\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
, where
S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes any symmetric positive definite bilinear form that satisfies

c_{0}a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\leq S^{K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\leq c_{1}a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}\cap\text{ker}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}),

with
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
being positive constants depending on the mesh assumptions.
Moreover, it is easy to check that
a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\cdot,\cdot)
must admit the following properties:

•

Consistency: For all
\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{K}

a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{q}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{q}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),

•

Stability: There exists two positive constants
\alpha_{*}
and
\alpha^{*}
, independent of
h
and
K
such that

\alpha_{*}a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq\alpha^{*}a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{K}.

Also, we introduce the discrete counterpart of
a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\cdot,\cdot)
, which is defined by

\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\right)\quad\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K},

(15)

where
a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=((\Pi_{0}^{0,K}\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{0,K}
and
\Pi_{0}^{0,K}:\nabla\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)^{2\times 2}
is the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)^{2\times 2}
. Then, we define

\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),

Now, we define
c_{h}^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
as the sesquilinear form defined on

\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}\times\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
by

c_{h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=c^{K}\big(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\big)\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}.

According to the definition of
c_{h}^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the approximation properties of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}
, it is not difficult to prove that:

c_{h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{q}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=c^{K}(\boldsymbol{q}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\qquad\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2},

and
\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,K}=\inf_{\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)]^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{q}\|_{0,K}
.
We remark that for all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, the local sesquilinear form
b^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
is computable from the degrees of freedom. In fact, for any function
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{K}
and
q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}
we have

b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})=\int_{K}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}q_{h}=\sum_{\ell\subset\partial K}\int_{\ell}(q_{h}\boldsymbol{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h}.

Finally, the global discrete counterparts of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined by

a_{h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+a_{h}^{\nabla}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\quad c_{h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}c_{h}^{K}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

With all these ingredients at hand, the discrete version of (
3
) reads as follows: Find
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{C}
and
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})&=&\lambda_{h}c_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},\\
\displaystyle b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h})&=&0&\forall\ q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.\end{array}\right.

(16)

From (
16
) we have for the sesquilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
, the following inf-sup condition given by
[
21
, Lemma 4.3]
. More precisely,
there exists a positive constant
\bar{\beta}
, independent of
h
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}\frac{b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\geq\bar{\beta}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

(17)

On the other hand, observe that (
15
) vanishes when
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
, obtaining easily the coercivity of
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, which depends only of
a_{h}^{\nabla}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then, following
[
21
, Section 3.2]
, there exists
\tilde{\alpha}>0
, independent of
h
such that

a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\geq\tilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}.

(18)

Let us introduce the discrete solution operator
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
defined by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{T}_{h}:\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\quad\boldsymbol{f}\mapsto\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}:=\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
is the solution of the following discrete source problem: Given a source
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, find

\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a_{h}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})&=&c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\\
\displaystyle b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},q_{h})&=&0&\forall\ q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.\end{array}\right.

(19)

From (
17
) and (
18
) and the Babuška-Brezzi theory, we have that
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
is well defined.
Moreover, as in the continuous case, it is easy to check that
(\kappa_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
is an eigenpair of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
if only if there exists
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
such that
(\lambda_{h},(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}))\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathcal{X}_{h}
solves (
16
), i.e.
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=\mu_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
with
\mu_{h}:=1/\lambda_{h}
and
\lambda_{h}\neq 0
.

Analogously to the continuous case, we rewrite problem (
19
) as follows: Given
\boldsymbol{f}\in[\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, find
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
such that

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}))=c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\quad(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h},

where
A_{h}:\mathcal{X}_{h}\times\mathcal{X}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the sesquilinear form defined by

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})):=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})-b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h}),

for all
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
.

3.2
Technical approximation results

The following approximation result for
polynomials in star-shaped domains (see for instance
[
9
]
),
is derived from results of interpolation between Sobolev spaces
(see for instance
[
11
, Theorem I.1.4]
) leading to an analogous
result for integer values of
s
.

Lemma 7
(Existence of a virtual approximation operator).

If assumption
A1
is satisfied, then for every
s
with

0\leq s\leq 1
and for every
\boldsymbol{v}\in[\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(K,\mathbb{C})]^{2}
, there exists

\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)
such that

\left\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}\right\|_{0,K}+h_{K}\left|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{\pi}\right|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{1+s}\left\|\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{1+s,K},

where the hidden constant depends only on mesh regularity constant
\gamma
.

We also have the following result that provides the existence of an interpolated function for the velocity, together with an approximation error (see
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Lemma 8
(Existence of an interpolation operator).

Under the assumptions
A1
and
A2
, let
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\cap[\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})]^{2}
, with
0\leq s\leq 1
. Then, there exists
\boldsymbol{v}_{I}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

\|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{I}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}_{I}|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{1+s}|\boldsymbol{v}|_{1+s,K},

where the hidden constant is positive and independent of
h_{K}
.

Finally, let
\mathcal{P}_{h}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\to\left\{q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}):q|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)\right\}
be the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
-orthogonal projector which satisfies the following approximation property.

Lemma 9
.

If
0\leq s\leq 1
, it holds

\|q-\mathcal{P}_{h}(q)\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\|q\|_{s,\Omega}\qquad\forall q\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})\cap\mathrm{Q}.

As in the continuous case, we also have an inf-sup condition for
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
. This is stated in the following result.

Lemma 10
.

The following inf-sup conditions for
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
holds

\begin{split}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\sup}\dfrac{A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|\|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\|}&=\widetilde{\gamma},\\
\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\sup}\dfrac{A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|\|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\|}&=\widetilde{\gamma}.\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is divided in three steps.

Step 1.
Observe that

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}))=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\geq C\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}^{2},

and invoking the Archimedean property, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}))=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\geq C\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}^{2}\geq C\varepsilon\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|^{2}.

Step 2.
For
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
we have

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},0))=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})+b(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},q_{h}).

Invoking the inf-sup condition for
b(\cdot,\cdot)
, for
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
there exists
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

b(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},q_{h})\geq\widetilde{\beta}\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega},

and defining
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}:=\dfrac{\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}}{\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}
we have

\|\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}=\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad b(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},q_{h})\geq\widetilde{\beta}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

Therefore, we obtain
-b(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},q_{h})\leq-\widetilde{\beta}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}
.

On the other hand it is direct the following estimate

a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\leq C\max\{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\nu\}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq C_{\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|^{2}.

Hence we obtain

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},0))\leq-\widetilde{\beta}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+C_{\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|^{2}.

Step 3.
Substituting
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}
by
-\boldsymbol{w}_{h}
in the last estimate on
Step 2
, we obtain

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(-\boldsymbol{w}_{h},0))=-A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},0))\geq\widetilde{\beta}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}-C_{\boldsymbol{\beta},\nu}\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|^{2}.

(20)

With the above estimate at hand and invoking
[
8
, Lemma 5]
, we conclude directly that

A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h}))\geq\widetilde{\gamma}\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|\|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\|.

This concludes the proof.

As a consequence of Lemma
10
, we have that
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is stable in the sense that given
(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
, there exists a pair
(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},s_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
such that
\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq A_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},s_{h}))
and
\|\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|s_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C
.

3.3
The dual discrete eigenvalue problem

With our discrete spaces at hand, we are in position to introduce the virtual element discretization of (
11
) which reads as follows: Find
\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{C}
and
(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*},p_{h}^{*})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*})-b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h}^{*})&=&\overline{\lambda_{h}}c_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\\
-b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*},q_{h})&=&0&\forall q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.\end{array}\right.

Then, we introduce the adjoint of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
, which is defined as follows

\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\qquad\boldsymbol{f}^{*}\mapsto\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}\boldsymbol{f}^{*}:=\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*},

where the pair
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*},\widehat{p}_{h}^{*})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
is the solution of the following well posed dual discrete source problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{*},\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*})-b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}^{*})&=&c_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{f}^{*})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\\
-b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*},q_{h})&=&0&\forall q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.\end{array}\right.

(21)

implying that
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}
is well defined due to the Babuŝka-Brezzi theory.

The discrete counterpart of (
9
) is defined by

\widehat{A}_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*},p_{h}^{*})):=a_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*})-b(p_{h},\boldsymbol{v})+b(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*},q_{h}).

As in the primal problem, we also have an inf-sup condition for
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
. The proof is similar to those in Lemma
10
, so we skip the details.

Lemma 11
.

The following inf-sup conditions for
\widehat{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
holds

\begin{split}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\sup}\dfrac{\widehat{A}_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|\|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\|}&=\widetilde{\gamma}^{*},\\
\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\inf}\underset{(\boldsymbol{0},0)\neq(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}}{\sup}\dfrac{\widehat{A}_{h}((\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\|\|(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},r_{h})\|}&=\widetilde{\gamma}^{*}.\end{split}

Finally, we observe that the discrete eigenvalues associated to
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}
are the conjugates of the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
.

Now, due to the compactness of
\boldsymbol{T}
, we are able to prove that
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
converge to
\boldsymbol{T}
as
h\downarrow 0
in norm. This is contained in the following result.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
be such that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}:=\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{f}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
\boldsymbol{C}
, independent of
h
, such that

\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},\quad\sigma:=\min\{1,s\}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be such that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}:=\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{f}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}
, where the pairs
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\widehat{p})\in\mathcal{X}
and
(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
are the solutions of (
6
) and (
19
), respectively. Then, we have

\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}=\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}-\widehat{p}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\\
\leq\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})\|_{0,\Omega}.

(22)

Observe that invoking Lemmas
8
and
9
we obtain

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}\|_{1,\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{1+s,\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\widehat{p}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\|\widehat{p}\|_{s,\Omega}.

Now, we need to control the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
22
). First we note that, since
A_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is stable we have

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\widehat{p}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})\|_{0,\Omega}\leq A_{h}((\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})),

(23)

with
(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
being chosen in such a way that
\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq 1
. On the other hand, manipulating the right-hand side of (
23
) we obtain

A_{h}((\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}))=A_{h}((\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}))-c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\\
=\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p}))+b^{K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},q_{h})\right]-c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\\
=\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+a^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+a^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\right.\\
\displaystyle\left.+b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p}))+b^{K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},q_{h})\right]-c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\\
=\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+a^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\right.\\
\displaystyle\left.+b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})-\widehat{p})+b^{K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},q_{h})\right]-c(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

Next, using triangle inequality and invoking Lemmas

and

a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq\nu|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}|_{1,K}|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{1,K}\\
\lesssim\nu\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}\left(|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}|_{1,K}+|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}|_{1,K}\right)\lesssim\nu h_{K}^{s}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}.

(24)

Invoking again Lemma
7
we obtain

a^{\nabla,K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq\nu|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\pi}|_{1,K}|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{1,K}\lesssim\nu h_{K}^{s}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}.

(25)

Now, invoking Lemma
9
and
8
we obtain

\begin{split}b^{K}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})-\widehat{p})&\leq|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{1,K}\|\widehat{p}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\widehat{p})\|_{0,K}\leq h_{K}^{s}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}\|\widehat{p}\|_{s,K},\\
b^{K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},q_{h})&\leq|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}|_{1,K}\|q_{h}\|_{0,K}\leq h_{K}^{s}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|q_{h}\|_{0,K}.\end{split}

(26)

On the other hand, using approximation properties of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}
, we have

c(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq h\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}.

(27)

Finally, we need to control the convective terms. To do this task, we have

\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=\underbrace{\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}_{\textrm{(I)}}+\underbrace{\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}_{\textrm{(II)}}.

Observe that
(II)
is easily bounded by using Lemma
8

\textrm{(II)}\leq\dfrac{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}}{2}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}|_{1,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\dfrac{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}}{2}|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{1,K}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}\|_{0,K}\\
\lesssim\dfrac{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}}{2}h_{K}^{s}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}+\dfrac{\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}}{2}h_{K}^{1+s}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}\\
\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}h_{K}^{\sigma}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1+s,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}

To estimate
(I)
, we observe that the following identity can be obtained

\left((\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{0,\Omega}=\left((\nabla\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{0,\Omega}=\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{u},(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v})^{t}\right)_{0,\Omega},

where
t
denotes the traspose operator. On the other hand, observe that by the definition of the projectors
\Pi_{0}^{0,K}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}
, for every

we have

(\Pi_{0}^{0,K}\nabla\boldsymbol{v},c)_{0,K}=(\nabla\boldsymbol{v},c)_{0,K}=(\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v},c)_{0,K},

obtaining that
\Pi_{0}^{0,K}\nabla\boldsymbol{v}=\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{v}
. With the previous identities at hand, we have

a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=\left((\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)_{0,K}-\left((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\right)_{0,K}\\
=\left((\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)_{0,K}+\left((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)_{0,K}\\
=\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{t}\right)_{0,K}+\left((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)_{0,K}\\
=\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}))^{t}\right)_{0,K}\\
+\left((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta}),\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right)_{0,K}\\
+\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{t}-\Pi_{0}^{0,K}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{t}\right)_{0,K}.

Hence, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain

a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\leq\|(\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}((\nabla\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I})\boldsymbol{\beta})\|_{0,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}\\
+|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}|_{1,K}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\|_{0,K}+\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{t}\|_{0,K}\right)\\
\lesssim h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}|_{1,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K},

where in the last inequality we have used the stability of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and error estimates for
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}^{K}
, together with the assumption that
(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{t}
has suffient regularity. Changing the roles of
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
we conclude that

\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{I},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\lesssim h_{K}^{\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,K}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{1,K}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{1,K},

(28)

where

. Therefore, summing over all polygons, using the estimates (

), invoking Theorem

and gathering (

\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},

where if

(29)

This concludes the proof.

3.4
Error estimates

To do the task of deriving error estimates, we begin by recalling some definitions. Let
\kappa
be a nonzero isolated eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{T}
with algebraic multiplicity
m
and let
\Gamma

be a disk of the complex plane centered in
\kappa
, such that
\kappa
is the only eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{T}
lying in
\Gamma
and
\partial\Gamma\cap\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{T})=\emptyset
. We define the spectral projections of
\boldsymbol{E}
and
\boldsymbol{E}^{*}
, associated to
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
, respectively, as follows:

1.

The spectral projector of
\boldsymbol{T}
associated to
\kappa
is
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Gamma}(z\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{T})^{-1}\,dz;

2.

The spectral projector of
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
associated to
\overline{\kappa}
is
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}^{*}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Gamma}(z\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{T}^{*})^{-1}\,dz,

where
\boldsymbol{I}
represents the identity operator. Let us remark that
\boldsymbol{E}
and
\boldsymbol{E}^{*}
are the projections onto the generalized eigenvector
R(\boldsymbol{E})
and
R(\boldsymbol{E}^{*})
, respectively.

A consequence of Lemma
12
is that there exist
m
eigenvalues that lie in
\Gamma
, namely
\kappa_{h}^{(1)},\ldots,\kappa_{h}^{(m)}
, repeated according their respective multiplicities, that converge to
\kappa
as
h
goes to zero. With this result at hand, we introduce the following spectral projection

\boldsymbol{E}_{h}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Gamma}(z\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})^{-1}\,dz,

which is a projection onto the discrete invariant subspace
R(\boldsymbol{E}_{h})
of
\boldsymbol{T}
, spanned by the generalized eigenvector of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
corresponding to

\kappa_{h}^{(1)},\ldots,\kappa_{h}^{(m)}
.
Now we recall the definition of the
gap

\widehat{\delta}
between two closed
subspaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
:

\widehat{\delta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\max\big\{\delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}),\delta(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X})\big\},\text{ where }\delta(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\sup_{\underset{\left\|x\right\|_{0,\Omega}=1}{x\in\mathcal{X}}}\left(\inf_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\left\|x-y\right\|_{0,\Omega}\right).

We end this section proving error estimates for the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues.

Theorem 15
.

The following estimates hold

\widehat{\delta}(R(\boldsymbol{E}),R(\boldsymbol{E}_{h}))\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma},\quad\widehat{\delta}(R(\boldsymbol{E}^{*}),R(\boldsymbol{E}_{h}^{*}))\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\kappa-\widehat{\kappa}_{h}|\leq\widetilde{\boldsymbol{C}}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}},

where
\displaystyle\widehat{\kappa}_{h}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{h}^{(i)}
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{C}}>0
is independent of
h
and
\boldsymbol{C}>0
is defined in Lemma
12
.

Proof.

The proof of the gap between the eigenspaces is a direct consequence of the convergence in norm between
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
as
h
goes to zero.
We focus on the double order of convergence for the eigenvalues. Let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be such that
\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}=\kappa\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
, for
k=1,\ldots,m
, and let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be a dual basis for
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
. This basis satisfies
A((\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p),(\boldsymbol{u}_{l}^{*},p^{*}))=\delta_{k.l},

where
\delta_{k.l}
represents the Kronecker delta.
From
[
4
, Theorem 7.2]
, the following identity holds true

|\kappa-\widehat{\kappa}_{h}|\lesssim\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lvert\left\langle(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}\right\rangle\right|+\|\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}))}\|\boldsymbol{T}^{*}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}))},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the corresponding duality pairing. Observe that the last term is estimate invoking Lemma
12
, obtaining

\|\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}))}\|\boldsymbol{T}^{*}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}))}\leq\boldsymbol{C}^{2}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

Hence, we only need to control the first term in the above estimate. In order to obtain such a estimate, observe that the following identity holds

\left\langle(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}\right\rangle=A(((\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*},p^{*}))\\
=A(((\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},p^{*}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))\\
+A(((\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p-p_{h}),((\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))\\
=A(((\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},p^{*}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))\\
+A((\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p),((\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))-A((\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p_{h}),((\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))\\
=\underbrace{A(((\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},p^{*}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))}_{\textrm{(I)}}\\
+\underbrace{A_{h}((\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p_{h}),((\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))-A((\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},p_{h}),((\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I},\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})))}_{\textrm{(II)}}\\
+\underbrace{\left[c(\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-c_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]}_{\textrm{(III)}}.

Now, our task is to estimate the contributions
(I)
,
(II)
and
(III)
. For
(I)
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and invoking Lemmas
12
,
8
and
9
we obtain

\textrm{(I)}\leq\nu\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\|_{1,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}|_{1,\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}\|_{0,\Omega}\\
+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}|_{1,\Omega}+\|p^{*}-\mathcal{P}_{h}(p^{*})\|_{0,\Omega}\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\|_{1,\Omega}\\
\leq\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\boldsymbol{C}\}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

(30)

For
(III)
, using the definition of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}
and it error estimates, together with the stability of the virtual interpolant, we obtain

\textrm{(III)}=c(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\\
\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\|_{0,\Omega}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\|_{0,\Omega}\leq h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

(31)

Finally, we need to estimate
(II)
. For this, first we note that

\textrm{(II)}=\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\\
+\left[\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right].

Hence, we need to control the terms associated to the gradients and the convective terms. So, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\\
\leq C\nu\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|_{1,K}|(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}|_{1,K}\\
\leq C\nu|\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|_{1,h}|(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}|_{1,h},

and adding and subtracting
\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
in
|\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|_{1,h}
, and
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*}
in
|(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}|_{1,h}
, together with triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-a^{\nabla,K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\leq\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\boldsymbol{C}\}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}},

where in the last inequality we have invoked Lemmas
12
,
8
and
7
. For the convective terms, we proceed as in the proof of Lemma
10
, obtaining

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\\
\leq 2\|(\nabla\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}((\nabla\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta})\|_{0,\Omega}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}\|_{0,\Omega}\\
+2|\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|_{1,h}\left(\|(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}))^{t}\|_{0,\Omega}\right.\\
\left.+\|(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})^{t}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}(\boldsymbol{\beta}\otimes(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})^{t}\|_{0,\Omega}\right).

Let us focus on the term
\|(\nabla\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}((\nabla\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta})\|_{0,\Omega}
of the previous estimate. On this term we add and subtract the terms
(\nabla\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}((\nabla\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\boldsymbol{\beta})
. Now, for the term
|\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}|_{1,\Omega}
we add and subtract
\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\nabla}\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
. Then, applying triangle inequality in each term, together with Lemmas
12
and the error estimates for
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}
, yields to

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[a_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-a^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\leq 2\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

Then, changing the roles of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
and
(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I}
and proceeding in a similar way, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[\widehat{a}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})-\widehat{a}^{\boldsymbol{\beta},K}(\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{k},(\boldsymbol{u}_{k}^{*})_{I})\right]\leq 2\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\textrm{(II)}\leq\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\}h^{\sigma+\sigma^{*}}.

(32)

Finally, gathering (

), (
31
) and (
32
), and defining

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{C}}:=\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C},1\},

where
\boldsymbol{C}

Our next goal is to obtain an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm estimate for the velocity. To do this task we use a standard duality argument.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in R(\boldsymbol{E})
be such that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}:=\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{f}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}
. Then, the following estimate holds

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{\mathtt{C}}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},

where
\boldsymbol{\mathtt{C}}>0
is independent of
h
.

Proof.

Let us consider the following auxiliary problem: Find
(\boldsymbol{z},\phi)\in\mathcal{X}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{z})+b(\boldsymbol{v},\phi)&=&c(\boldsymbol{v},\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})&\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2},\\
b(\boldsymbol{z},q)&=&0&\forall q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}).\end{array}\right.

(33)

Observe that this problem is well-posed and its solution satisfies the following estimate

\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1+r,\Omega}+\|\phi\|_{r,\Omega}\leq C\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega},

(34)

where
C>0
is the constant given by Theorem
1
. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{z}
and
\phi
satisfies the estimates (
7
) and (
8
) respectively. Now we need to estimate the term on the right-hand side of (
34
). First, we define
\widetilde{r}:=\min\{1,r\}
. Now, testing (
33
) with
\boldsymbol{v}:=\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
we obtain

\begin{split}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}&=c(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})\\
&=a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z})+b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\phi)\\
&=\underbrace{a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{I})}_{\textrm{(I)}}+\underbrace{b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\phi-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\phi))}_{\textrm{(II)}}+\underbrace{a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})}_{\textrm{(III)}},\end{split}

where in the last equality we have used that
b(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\mathcal{P}_{h}(\phi))=0
from the second equations of (
6
) and (
19
).

The task now is to estimate each of the contributions
(I)
,
(II)
and
(III)
on the above identity. For
(I)
, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and invoking Lemmas
12
and
8
we have

\textrm{(I)}\leq\nu\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{I}|_{1,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}\nu h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1+\widetilde{r},\Omega}\\
\leq\boldsymbol{C}C\nu h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(35)

For
(II)
, we invoke Lemmas
12
and
9
, obtaining

\textrm{(II)}\leq\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\phi-\mathcal{P}_{h}(\phi)\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{r},\Omega}\\
\leq\boldsymbol{C}Ch^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(36)

Finally, observe that for
(III)
, using the first equations in (
6
) and (
19
), the following identity can be obtained

\begin{split}a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})&=a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})-a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})\\
&=c(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})-b(\boldsymbol{z}_{I},p)-a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})\\
&=\underbrace{c(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})-c_{h}(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})}_{\textrm{(IV)}}+\underbrace{b(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{I},p-p_{h})}_{\textrm{(V)}}+\underbrace{a_{h}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})-a(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{z}_{I})}_{\textrm{(VI)}}.\end{split}

Note that the contributions
(IV)
and
(V)
can be easily estimated, obtaining

\begin{split}\textrm{(IV)}&\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}_{I}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}\boldsymbol{z}_{I}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1,\Omega},\\
\textrm{(V)}&\leq\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}|\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}_{I}|_{1,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1+\widetilde{r},\Omega},\end{split}

whereas for
(VI)
we have

\textrm{(VI)}\leq 2\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},1\}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1,\Omega}.

Hence, we obtain for
(III)
that

\textrm{(III)}\leq\widetilde{C}^{*}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{1+\widetilde{r},\Omega},

(37)

where

\widehat{C}^{*}:=\max\left\{\dfrac{C_{pf}}{\nu},\boldsymbol{C}C,2\max\{\boldsymbol{C}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},\boldsymbol{C}\nu,\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega},1\}\dfrac{C_{pf}}{\nu}\right\},

and gathering (
35
), (
36
) and (
37
), together with (
34
), we conclude that

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\textbf{C}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},\quad\mathtt{C}:=\max\{\boldsymbol{C}C\nu,\boldsymbol{C}C,\widetilde{C}^{*}\}.

This completes the proof.

Observe that similar arguments can be done for the dual problem. Since the proof is identically, we skip the proof.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in R(\boldsymbol{E}^{*})
be such that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}:=\boldsymbol{T}^{*}\boldsymbol{f}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*}:=\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}\boldsymbol{f}
. Then, the following estimate holds

\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}^{*}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\mathtt{C}h^{\widetilde{r}^{*}+\sigma^{*}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{1,\Omega},

where
\mathtt{C}>0
is defined in Lemma
16
.

3.5
Error in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm

Our aim is to derive an error estimate for the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm of the velocity. To do this task, we define the solution operator on the space
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
as
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, which is defined by
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}\boldsymbol{f}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
, where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
is solution of (
6
). Observe that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
is well-defined and compact, but not self-adjoint. Hence, we define the dual operator of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
as
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}^{*}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
as
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}^{*}\boldsymbol{f}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}
, where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^{*}
is solution of (
13
). Finally, we remark that the spectra of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{T}
coincide.

Now we present the following result, in which the convergence of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
to
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
as
h\downarrow 0
is obtained.

Lemma 18
.

For every
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, the following estimate holds

\|(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega},

where
\boldsymbol{C}>0
is defined in Lemma
12
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same steps of those in the proof of Lemma
12
, but considering the source
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
.

Similar arguments can be used in order to obtain the convergence of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}
to
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}^{*}
as
h\downarrow 0
, which we present in the following result.

Lemma 19
.

For every
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
, the following estimate holds

\|(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}^{*}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\sigma^{*}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega},

where
\boldsymbol{C}>0
is defined in Lemma
12
.

Finally, we conclude this section with error estimates for primal and dual eigenfunctions.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
be an eigenfunction of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
associated with the eigenvalue
\kappa_{h}^{(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, with
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}=1
. Then, there exists an eigenfunction
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
of
\boldsymbol{T}
associated to the eigenvalue
\kappa
such that

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma},

where
\boldsymbol{C}>0
is independent of
h
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
18
and
[
4
, Theorem 7.1]
, we have spectral convergence of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
to
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
. Now, due to the relation between the eigenfunctions of
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
with those of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}
, we have
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in R(\boldsymbol{E}_{h})
and there exists
\boldsymbol{u}\in R(\boldsymbol{E})
such that

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\underset{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\in R(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h}):\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\|_{0,\Omega}=1}{\sup}\|(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\|_{0,\Omega},

where
R(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{E}}_{h})
is an eigenspace of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}
. On the other hand, using Lemma
16
, for all
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\in R(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{E}})
, if
\boldsymbol{f}\in R(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{E}})
is such that
\boldsymbol{f}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}
, then

\|(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{T}}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\widetilde{\boldsymbol{f}}\|_{0,\Omega}=\|(\boldsymbol{T}-\boldsymbol{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\widetilde{r}+\sigma}.

This concludes the proof.

Observe that the same arguments can be used for the dual problem, obtaining the following result.

Lemma 21
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*}
be an eigenfunction of
\boldsymbol{T}_{h}^{*}
associated with the eigenvalue
\kappa_{h}^{*(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, with
\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*}\|_{0,\Omega}=1
. Then, there exists an eigenfunction
\boldsymbol{u}^{*}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})^{2}
of
\boldsymbol{T}^{*}
associated to the eigenvalue
\kappa^{*}
such that

\|\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{*}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\boldsymbol{C}h^{\widetilde{r}^{*}+\sigma^{*}},

where
\boldsymbol{C}>0
is independent of
h
.

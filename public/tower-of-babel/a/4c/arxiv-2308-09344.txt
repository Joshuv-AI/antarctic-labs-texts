Preliminaries

Let
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. A
permutation
of length
n
is a bijective map
x:[n]\to[n]
. Let
S_{n}
be the set of permutations of length
n
. We denote a permutation
x\in S_{n}
by the string
x=x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}
, where
x_{i}=x(i)
. Let
\mathrm{ind}_{x}(i)=x^{-1}(i)
be the index such that
x_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(i)}=i
. For example,
\mathrm{ind}_{12534}(5)=3
. If
i<j
(respectively,
i>j
), we say that
x_{i}

appears to the left of (resp. to the right of)

x_{j}
in
x
. An entry
x_{i}
is a
left-to-right minimum
(briefly, ltr minimum) of
x
if
x_{i}
is smaller than each entry that appears to its left. Furthermore, we let
x_{[i,j]}
denote the subsequence
x_{i}x_{i+1}\dots x_{j}
, and we let
\{x_{[i,j]}\}
be the set
\{x_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{j}\}
. For
k\geq 1
, we also let
x+k=(x_{1}+k)(x_{2}+k)\dots(x_{n}+k)
denote the string obtained by adding
k
to each entry of
x
. As an example to illustrate the above definitions, let
x=524613
. The ltr-minima of
x
are
5
,
2
, and
1
. For
i=2
and
j=5
, we have
x_{[i,j]}=2461
and
\{x_{[i,j]}\}=\{2,4,6,1\}
. Finally, we have
x+3=857946
.

A short introduction to permutation patterns can be found in Bevan’s note
[
3
]
. Let us recall some notions that will be used in this paper.
Given two permutations
x\in S_{n}
and
y\in S_{m}
, with
m\leq n
, we say that
x

contains
the pattern
y
if there exists some
c(1)<c(2)<\dots<c(m)
such that the subsequence
z=x_{c(1)}x_{c(2)}\dots x_{c(m)}
is order-isomorphic to
y
. If so, we say that
z
is an
occurrence
of
y
in
x
. If
x
does not contain the pattern
y
, then
x

avoids

y
. Let
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(y)
denote the set of permutations of length
n
that avoid
y
. Similarly, for a set of patterns
T
we let
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(T)
be the set of permutations in
S_{n}
avoiding all the patterns in
T
. Bivincular patterns
[
4
]
are obtained by allowing constraints of adjacency on positions and values. Mesh patterns generalize both classical and bivincular patterns in the following sense. A
mesh pattern

[
5
]
is a pair
(y,R)
, where
y\in S_{m}
is a permutation and
R\subseteq\left[0,m\right]\times\left[0,m\right]
is a set of pairs of integers. Each pair in
R
determines the lower left corners of unit squares which specify forbidden regions in the plot of permutations. An occurrence of the mesh pattern
(y,R)
in the permutation
x
is an occurrence of the classical pattern
y
such that no other points of
x
occur in the forbidden regions specified by
R
. For the rest of this paper, we shall denote by
123^{\star}
and
132^{\star}
the bivincular patterns depicted–as mesh patterns–in
Figure

3
. For example, an occurrence of
123^{\star}
in a permutation
x
is a classical occurrence
x_{c(1)}x_{c(2)}x_{c(3)}
of
123
such that
c(3)=c(2)+1
and
x_{c(3)}=x_{c(2)}+1
.

2.1.
West’s Bijection

In 1995, West
[
15
]
presented a new bijection between
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)
and
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
. Let
x=x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n-1}\in S_{n-1}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, denote by
x^{i}
the permutation
x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{i-1}nx_{i}\dots x_{n-1}
obtained by inserting
n
in the
i
th
site
of
x
(i.e. the position immediately before
x_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-1
, or at the end, if
i=n
). Given a pattern
y
, define the
i
th site of
x
to be
active
with respect to
y
if
x^{i}
avoids
y
. For example, if
x=45231
, then
i\in\{1,3,5,6\}
are active with respect to
y=132
, but
i\in\{2,4\}
are not since both
x^{2}=465231
and
x^{4}=452631
contain
132
. Now, let
\mathrm{small}_{k}(x)
denote the subsequence of the smallest
k
numbers in
x
. Given a pattern
y
, let
\mathrm{Ac}_{j}(x;y)
denote the set of active sites in
\mathrm{small}_{|x|+1-j}(x)
with respect to the pattern
y
. Note that
\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;y)
is equal to the number of active sites of
x
with respect to
y
. For example,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(45231;132)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(45231;132)|=4
, because the set of active sites with respect to
132
pattern for
x=45231
is
\{1,3,5,6\}
and for
\mathrm{small}_{4}(x)=4231
is
\{1,2,4,5\}
. The
signature
of
x
with respect to
y
is the word

\mathfrak{S}(x;y)=|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;y)|\cdot|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;y)|\dots|\mathrm{Ac}_{n-1}(x;y)|,

where
\cdot
denotes concatenation. For example,
\mathfrak{S}(45231;132)=44332
. In addition, for a set of permutations
T
, let
\mathfrak{S}(T;y):=\{\mathfrak{S}(x;y)\mid x\in T\}
.
West
[
15
]
showed that permutations in
\mathrm{Av}(123)
, as well as permutations in
\mathrm{Av}(132)
, are uniquely determined by their signature. Furthermore, he showed that
\mathfrak{S}(\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132);132)=\mathfrak{S}(\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123);123)
. This naturally induces a bijection between
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)
and
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
. For example,
x=45231
is the only
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{5}(132)
such that
\mathfrak{S}(x;132)=44332,
and
y=42153
is the only
y\in\mathrm{Av}_{5}(123)
such that
\mathfrak{S}(y;123)=44332
.

3.
The
(132,321)
-machine

This section is devoted to the
(132,321)
-machine. First we prove that
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)=\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
. Then we show that West’s bijection maps
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
to
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132,123^{\star})
. Finally, we enumerate
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
by providing a generating function for
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132,123^{\star})
. We end the section by sketching a bijection between
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
and the set of Dyck paths of semilength
n
that avoid
dudu
as a factor.

Let us begin by establishing a property that all ltr minima of the input permutation must satisfy.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum of
x
and
\mathrm{ind}_{s_{132,321}(x)}(x_{i})<\mathrm{ind}_{s_{132,321}(x)}(x_{j})
, then
j<i
.

Proof.

Because
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum and the stack must avoid the
132
pattern, the stack must be increasing from top to bottom right after
x_{i}
enters the stack. Now, because
z_{2}>z_{3}
in any
132
or
321
pattern
z
and the numbers below
x_{i}
in the stack are increasing,
x_{i}
can only leave the stack after every number has entered or left the stack. Therefore, if
x_{j}
appears to the right of
x_{i}
in
s_{132,321}(x)
, then
x_{j}
must have been in the stack when
x_{i}
entered the stack. Thus,
j<i
.
∎

Next, we show that any permutation in
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
must avoid the
123
pattern.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
, then
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. Let
x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}
be the lexicographically smallest
123
pattern of
x
so that
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum. By
Lemma

3.1
,
x_{i}
must be in the stack when
x_{k}
enters the stack. Now,
x_{j}
must leave the stack before
x_{k}
enters the stack, because
x_{k}x_{j}x_{i}
is a
321
pattern. As a result,
x_{j}
must appear to left of
x_{k}
in
s_{132,321}(x)
. Furthermore, by
Lemma

3.1
,
x_{i}
must appear to the right of
x_{j}
and
x_{k}
in
s_{132,321}(x)
. Thus,
x_{j}x_{k}x_{i}
is a
231
pattern in
s_{132,321}(x)
—a contradiction to
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
by
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

Due to
Proposition

3.2
, the behavior of the
(132,321)
-stack on any
(132,321)
-sortable permutation is equivalent to the behavior of a
132
-stack. Therefore,

\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)=\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132)\cap\mathrm{Av}(123).

The next proposition shows that any permutation in
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
must avoid the
132^{\star}
pattern.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
, then
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. Let
x_{i}x_{j}x_{j+1}
be the lexicographically smallest
132^{\star}
pattern of
x
so that
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum. Now, suppose that
x_{j}
has just entered the stack. Because
x_{j}=x_{j+1}+1
, if
x_{j+1}
entering the stack introduces a
132
or
321
pattern to the stack, then
x_{j+1}
and
x_{j}
must both be a part of the pattern. However, in a
132
pattern
z
, it must be that
z_{2}-z_{1}\geq 2
and in a
321
pattern
z
, it must be that
z_{2}<z_{1}
. Yet,
x_{j}-x_{j+1}=1
, and so the entrance of
x_{j+1}
into the stack cannot introduce a
132
or
321
pattern. Therefore,
x_{j+1}
must be to the left of
x_{j}
in
s_{132,321}(x)
. In addition, by
Lemma

3.1
,
x_{i}
must be to the right of
x_{j}
and
x_{j+1}
in
s_{132,321}(x)
. Thus,
x_{j+1}x_{j}x_{i}
is a
231
pattern in
s_{132,321}(x)
—a contradiction to
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
by
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

We shall prove that the conditions of Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
are sufficient for a permutation to be in
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
. First, a simple lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
x_{i}
is not a ltr minimum of

and
i<j
, then
x_{i}>x_{j}
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. Because
x_{i}
is not a ltr minimum, there exists
x_{k}
such that
x_{k}<x_{i}
and
k<i
. Thus,
x_{k}x_{i}x_{j}
is a
123
pattern in
x
—a contradiction.
∎

Next, we prove a property that all
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
must satisfy.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
x_{i}
is not a ltr minimum of
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
, then

\mathrm{ind}_{s_{132,321}(x)}(x_{i})<\mathrm{ind}_{s_{132,321}(x)}(x_{i+1}).

Proof.

First, suppose that
x_{i+1}
is a ltr minimum. Then because
x_{i}
is not a ltr minimum, there must be some ltr minimum
x_{j}
such that
x_{j}<x_{i}
and
j<i
. Now, by
Lemma

3.1
,
x_{j}
must be in the stack when
x_{i+1}
enters the stack. Then because
x_{i+1}
is a ltr minimum, the subsequence
x_{i+1}x_{i}x_{j}
must be a
132
pattern. Therefore,
x_{i}
must leave the stack before
x_{i+1}
enters the stack.

Next, suppose that
x_{i+1}
is not a ltr minimum. First, because
x_{i}
is not a ltr minimum,
x_{i}>x_{i+1}
by
Lemma

3.4
. In addition, there is some ltr minimum
x_{k}
such that
x_{k}<x_{i+1}
and
k<i
.
Furthermore,
x_{i}>x_{i+1}+1
, because otherwise,
x_{k}x_{i}x_{i+1}
is a
132^{\star}
pattern. Now, if
x_{i+1}+1
appears to the right of
x_{i+1}
, then
x_{k}x_{i+1}\cdot(x_{i+1}+1)
is a
123

3.4
,
x_{i+1}+1
must be a ltr minimum, and by

3.1
,
x_{i+1}+1
must be in the stack when
x_{i+1}
enters the stack. Now,
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}+1
cannot both be in the stack when
x_{i+1}
enters the stack, because
x_{i+1}x_{i}\cdot(x_{i+1}+1)
is a
132
pattern. Thus,
x_{i}
must leave the stack before
x_{i+1}
enters the stack.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
, then
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321).

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.1
, any ltr minimum
x_{i}
must remain in the stack until every other number has entered the stack. By
Lemma

3.5
, any
x_{i}
that is not a ltr minimum of
x
must leave the stack before
x_{i+1}
enters the stack. Therefore, any ltr minimum of
x
must appear to the right of any number that is not a ltr minimum of
x
in
s_{132,321}(x)
. Now, because the ltr minima of
x
must decreasing from the left to right, by
Lemma

3.1
, the ltr minima of
x
must appear in
s_{132,321}(x)
in increasing order. Furthermore, by Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
, the numbers that are not ltr minima of
x
must appear in
s_{132,321}(x)
in decreasing order. Thus,
s_{132,321}(x)
must avoid the
231
pattern and by
Lemma

2.1
, must be in
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)
.
∎

Now, we collate propositions
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.6
.

Theorem 3.7
.

For all
n
, it holds that
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)=\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
.

Our next goal is to show that West’s bijection maps
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
to
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132,123^{\star})
. Recall from
Section

2.1
that permutations in
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
, as well as in
\mathrm{Av}(132)
, are uniquely determined by their signature. West’s bijection then follows from the equality between the corresponding sets of signatures

\mathfrak{S}(\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123),123)=\mathfrak{S}(\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132),132).

We wish to prove that any permutation
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
contains
132^{\star}
if and only if there exists some
1\leq i\leq n-2
such that

\mathfrak{S}(x;123)_{i}=\mathfrak{S}(x;123)_{i+1}\leq\mathfrak{S}(x;123)_{i+2}.

The desired result follows from the fact that the same property holds when we replace the pattern
123
with
132
and the pattern
132^{\star}
with
123^{\star}
.

Let us take care of
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
first. To start, we show that removing the maximum of a permutation preserves the property of avoiding both patterns.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
, then
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(123,132^{\star})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(123)
. Suppose that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\not\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(132^{\star})
. Then
x_{j}x_{\mathrm{ind_{x}(n)}-1}x_{\mathrm{ind_{x}(n)}+1}
must be a
132^{\star}
pattern in
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)
, because
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
. However, then
x_{j}x_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1}\cdot n
is a
123
pattern in
x
—a contradiction to
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
.
∎

Now, let
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
. It is easy to see that the set of active sites
\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)
of
x
with respect to
123
forms an interval. The entry
x_{|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|}
preceding the leftmost site that is not active can be characterized as follows.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
and suppose that
x
is not the decreasing permutation
n(n-1)\dots 1
. Then
x_{|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|}=\max\{x_{i}\mid x_{i}+i\neq n+1\}.

Proof.

We induct on
n.
The statement is vacuously true for
n=1.
First, suppose that
x_{1}\neq n
. Clearly,
x^{j}\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
for
1\leq j\leq\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n),
since if
(x^{j})_{a}\cdot(x^{j})_{b}\cdot(n+1)
is a
123
pattern in
x^{j},
then
x_{a}x_{b}n
is a
123
pattern in
x
. Conversely, if
j>\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)
, then
x^{j}_{1}\cdot n\cdot(n+1)
is a
123
pattern in
x^{j},
and so

. Hence,
\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)=\{1,2,\cdots,\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)\}
and
x_{|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|}=\max\{x_{i}\mid x_{i}+i\neq n+1\}=n
.

Now, suppose that
x_{1}=n
. Then
\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)=\{j\mid j-1\in\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)\}\cup\{1\}
, because no
123
pattern in
x^{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n+1
begins with
n
or
n+1
. Thus,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|+1
, and the statement of the lemma follows by the inductive hypothesis.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
and
|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;123)|=\mathfrak{|}\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;123)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;123)|
for some
1\leq i\leq n-2
, then
x\not\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star}).

Proof.

We induct on
n
; it is easy to see that the statement holds for
n\leq 3
. Now, because
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;123)=|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x;123))
, if
n\geq 4
and
2\leq i\leq n-2
, then by
Lemma

3.8
and the inductive hypothesis,
x\not\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
. Thus, assume
i=1
.

Next, if
x_{1}=n
, then
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|+1
by
Lemma

3.9
. Similarly, if
x_{1}=n-1
, then
|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{3}(x;123)|+1
by
Lemma

3.9
on
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)
. Thus,
x_{1}\leq n-2
.

Now,
n
appears to the left of
n-1
in
x
, because
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
and
x_{1}\leq n-2
. But if
n\cdot(n-1)
is not a substring of
x
, then by
Lemma

3.9
and
x_{1}\leq n-2
,

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)<\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)}(n-1)=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|.

Thus,
x\not\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
, because
x_{1}\cdot n\cdot(n-1)
form a
132^{\star}
pattern in
x
.
∎

The converse of
Proposition

3.10
is also true, as we show below.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)\setminus\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
, then for some
1\leq i\leq n-2
, it holds that
|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;123)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;123)|
.

Proof.

We induct on
n
; the statement holds for
n\leq 3,
because
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(132;123)=222.
Now, suppose for the sake of contradiction that
n\geq 4
and that there is no
1\leq i\leq n-3
such that
|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;123)|=\mathfrak{|}\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;123)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;123)|.
Then, since
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;123)=|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x);123),
we have that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(132^{\star})
from the inductive hypothesis. Now,
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)\setminus\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
, but
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132^{\star})
, and so
\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)=\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n-1)-1>1
.

Now,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)
from
Lemma

3.9
. Similarly,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|=\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)}(n-1)=\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)
from
Lemma

3.9
on
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)
. Therefore,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|
. Now, because
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
, the substring
x_{[1,\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1]}
must be decreasing. Thus, for all
1\leq j\leq\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1
, it must be that
x_{j}+j\leq n-1
. Therefore, for each
1\leq j\leq\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1
, if
x_{j}+j\neq n-1
, then
x_{j}\leq n-2-j
. Thus,

and by

,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{3}(x;123)|\neq j
. Thus,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{3}(x;123)|\geq|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;123)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;123)|
.
∎

Now, propositions

.

Theorem 3.12
.

For
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
, there exists some

such that
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;123)_{i}=\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;123)_{i+1}\leq\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;123)_{i+2}

.

Let us now turn our attention to
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132,123^{\star})
. We shall replicate the approach used for
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123,132^{\star})
to obtain an analogous characterization of permutations in

that contain

Lemma 3.13
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132,123^{\star})
, then
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(132,123^{\star})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(132)
. It thus suffices to show that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(123^{\star})
. Suppose otherwise. Because
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123^{\star})
, the

pattern in
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)
must be
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)_{j}\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1}\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)}
for some

. However, then
x_{j}\cdot n\cdot\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)}
is a

pattern in

—a contradiction to

.
∎

Given
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)
, we express
\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;132)
in terms of
\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;132)

Lemma 3.14
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)
, then

\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;132)=\{i\mid i-1\in\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;132)\cap[\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n),n]\}\cup\{1\}.

It is clear that

if and only if
\{x_{[1,i-1]}\}=[n-i+2,n]
. Thus, if

, then
i\in\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;132)
if and only if

. But, if
\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)+1\leq i
, then
\{x_{[1,i-1]}\}=[n-i+2,n]
if and only if
\{\mathrm{small}(x)_{[1,i-2]}\}=[n-i+2,n-1]
if and only if

.
∎

The following results are the analogs of
Proposition

3.10
and

for the

Proposition 3.15
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)

|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;132)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;132)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;132)|

; it is easy to see that the statement holds for

. Now, because

, if

and

, then by
Lemma

3.13
and the inductive hypothesis,

. Thus, assume

.

Now, if
x_{1}=n,
then by
Lemma

3.14
,
|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;132)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;132)|+1
. Similarly, if
x_{1}=n-1,
then
|\mathrm{Ac}_{2}(x;132)|=|\mathrm{Ac}_{3}(x;132)|+1
by
Lemma

3.14
on
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)
. Thus,
x_{1}\leq n-2.
Now,
n
appears to the right of
n-1

x
, because

. Furthermore,

\{x_{[1,\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n-1)]}\}=[n-\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n-1),n-1]

Proposition 3.16
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)\setminus\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123^{\star})
, then for some

, it holds that
|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;132)|=\mathfrak{|}\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;132)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;132)|
.

We induct on
n
; the statement holds for
n\leq 3,
because
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(123;132)=222.
Now, suppose that
n\geq 4
and suppose for the sake of contradiction that there is no
i
such that
|\mathrm{Ac}_{i}(x;132)|=\mathfrak{|}\mathrm{Ac}_{i+1}(x;132)|\leq|\mathrm{Ac}_{i+2}(x;132)|.
Then, since
\mathfrak{S}_{n}(x;132)=|\mathrm{Ac}_{1}(x;132)|\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{n}(\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x);132),
we have that
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(123^{\star})
from the inductive hypothesis. Now,
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)\setminus\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123^{\star})
but
\mathrm{small}_{n-1}(x)\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123^{\star})

4.
The
(123,321)
-machine

In this section, we enumerate the set
\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)
of permutations sorted by the
(123,321)
-machine. A direct computation shows that the sequence
|\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)|
,
n\geq 1
, starts with
1,2,4,7
. Our goal is to prove that, for
n\geq 5
, this sequence obeys the simple recursion

|\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)|=2|\mathrm{Sort}_{n-1}(123,321)|.

We start by proving that
(123,321)
-sortable permutations avoid
123
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321),
then
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. Let
x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}
be the lexicographically smallest occurrence of
123
in
x
. Note that
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum. Now, if
x_{i}
is in the stack when
x_{k}
enters the stack,
x_{j}x_{k}x_{i}
is a
231
pattern in
\mathrm{s}_{123,321}(x)
, because
x_{k}x_{j}x_{i}
is a
321
pattern. Thus, by
Lemma

2.1
,
x_{i}
must leave the stack before
x_{k}
enters the stack.

Now, because
x_{i}
is a ltr minimum, the stack below
x_{i}
must be decreasing, since the stack is
123
-avoiding. But the stack must also be
321
-avoiding, so there can be at most two numbers below
x_{i}
in the stack. Therefore, for
x_{i}
to leave the stack before
x_{k}
enters the stack, either
\min\{x_{[i+1,k]}\}<x_{i}
, or the stack below
x_{i}
reads
x_{b}x_{c}
and
\max\{x_{[i+1,k]}\}>x_{b}
. In the first case,
x_{i}x_{k}\cdot\min\{x_{[i+1,k]}\}
is a
231
pattern in
s_{123,321}(x)
, and in the second case,
x_{b}\cdot\max\{x_{[i+1,k]}\}\cdot x_{c}
is a
231
pattern in
s_{123,321}(x)
. Hence, by
Lemma

2.1
,
x\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123).

∎

In analogy with what observed after
Proposition

3.2
, we have

\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132,321)=\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(132)\cap\mathrm{Av}(123).

The following two results show that if
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)
, then
x_{1}\geq n-1
and
x_{n}\leq 2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321),
then
x_{1}\in\{n-1,n\}
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. By
Proposition

4.1
, either
x_{2}=n
or
x_{2}<x_{1}
. If
x_{2}=n
, then
s_{123,321}(x)_{[n-1,n]}=n\cdot x_{1}
. Therefore,
(n-1)\cdot n\cdot x_{1}
is a
231
pattern in
s_{123,321}(x)
, and
x\not\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)
by
Lemma

2.1
. Thus,
x_{2}<x_{1}
, and as a result, the stack must only contain
x_{1}
when
1
enters the stack. Then because
x_{1}
and
1
do not exit the stack until the input permutation is empty,
s_{123,321}(x)_{[n-1,n]}=1\cdot x_{1}
. Furthermore,
s_{123,321}(x)_{1}=n
, because otherwise,
s_{123,321}(x)_{1}\cdot n\cdot 1
is a
231
pattern in
s_{123,321}(x)
, and
x\not\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)
by
Lemma

2.1
. Now, immediately after
n
leaves the stack,
x_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1}
must leave the stack, because
n
cannot be part of a
321
or
123
pattern after the next number in the input permutation enters the stack. By
Proposition

4.1
,
x_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1}\neq n-1
. Thus,
x_{\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)-1}\cdot(n-1)\cdot 1
is a
231
pattern in
s_{123,321}(x)
—a contradiction by
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
x\in\mathrm{Sort}_{n}(123,321)
, then
x_{n}\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. By
Lemma

4.2
,
x_{1}\in\{n-1,n\}
, and so either
x_{2}<x_{1}
, or
x_{[1,2]}=(n-1)\cdot n
. If
x_{2}<x_{1}
, then the stack must only contain
x_{1}
when
1
enters the stack. Otherwise, the stack reads
n\cdot(n-1)
when
1
enters the stack. In either case,

cannot leave the stack until the input permutation is empty, and so

appears to the right of both

and

in

. Now,
2
must leave the stack before
x_{n}
enters the stack, because

is a

is a

—a contradiction by
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

-sortable permutations. From now on, assume

. First we show that

and

Lemma 4.4
.

If

, then
\mathrm{ind}_{x}(n)<\min(\mathrm{ind}_{x}(1),\mathrm{ind}_{x}(2))
.

By

4.2
,
x_{1}\geq n-1
. If

, then the claim is immediate. Thus, suppose that

4.3
,

enters the stack and must read

when

enters the stack. Then

is a

.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If

\mathrm{ind}_{s_{123,321}(x)}(2)=\mathrm{ind}_{s_{123,321}(x)}(1)-1

By Lemmas

,

in the stack. Now, if

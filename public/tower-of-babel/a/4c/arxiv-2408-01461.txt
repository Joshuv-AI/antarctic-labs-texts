Characterizations of ℕ \mathbb{N} -compactness and realcompactness via ultrafilters in the absence of the axiom of choice
AliReza Olfati, Eliza Wajch

Abstract
This article concerns the Herrlich-Chew theorem stating that a Hausdorff zero-dimensional space is ℕ \mathbb{N} -compact if and only if every clopen ultrafilter with the countable intersection property in this space is fixed. It also concerns Hewitt’s theorem stating that a Tychonoff space is realcompact if and only if every z z -ultrafilter with the countable intersection property in this space is fixed. The axiom of choice was involved in the original proofs of these theorems. The aim of this article is to show that the Herrlich-Chew theorem is valid in 𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{ZF} , but it is an open problem if Hewitt’s theorem can be false in a model of 𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{ZF} . It is proved that Hewitt’s theorem is true in every model of 𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{ZF} in which the countable axiom of multiple choice is satisfied. A modification of Hewitt’s theorem is given and proved true in 𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{ZF} . Several applications of the results obtained are shown. Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary 54D60, 54D35; Secondary 54A35, 03E25 Keywords and phrases : ℕ \mathbb{N} -compactness, realcompactness, clopen sets, zero-sets, ultrafilters

Introduction

The standard Zermelo-Fraenkel system of axioms
\mathbf{ZF}

\mathbf{AC}

\mathbf{ZFC}

In what follows, our notation and terminology, if not introduced here, is standard. The set
\omega

For a set

\mathcal{A}_{\delta}:=\{\bigcap\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{U}\in[\mathcal{A}]^{\leq\omega}\setminus\{\emptyset\}\}.

Given a family

We usually denote topological and metric spaces with boldface letters, and their underlying sets with lightface letters.

We recall the following well-known result.

Proposition 2.7
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let
\mathbf{Y}
be a Hausdorff space and let

be a dense subset of a topological space
\mathbf{X}
. If
f,g\in C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
are such that

, then

.

The proof of the following theorem is a modification of
[
4
, proof of Theorem 3.11.3]

Theorem 2.8
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let
\mathbf{E}
be a non-empty Hausdorff space and let
\mathbf{X}
be an
\mathbf{E}
-completely regular space.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbf{E}
-compact;

(ii)

for every Hausdorff space
\mathbf{Y}
containing
\mathbf{X}
as a dense subspace such that, for every
f\in C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{E})
, there exists
\tilde{f}\in C(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{E})
with
\tilde{f}|_{X}=f
, the equality
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{Y}
holds;

(iii)

the set
e_{C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{E})}[X]
is closed in
\mathbf{E}^{C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{E})}
.

Proof.

(i)\rightarrow(ii)
Suppose that there exist a non-empty set
J
and a homeomorphic embedding
h
of
\mathbf{X}
into
\mathbf{E}^{J}
such that
h[X]
is a closed subset of
\mathbf{E}^{J}
. Let
\mathbf{Y}
be a Hausdorff space such that
\mathbf{X}
is a dense subspace of
\mathbf{Y}
and, for every
f\in C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{E})
, there exists
\tilde{f}\in C(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{E})
with
\tilde{f}|_{X}=f
. For every
j\in J
, there exists a unique
h_{j}\in C(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{E})
such that, for every
x\in X
,
h_{j}(x)=\pi_{j}\circ h(x)
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{j}:j\in J\}
. Then
e_{\mathcal{H}}\in C(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{E}^{J})
and
e_{\mathcal{H}}[Y]\subseteq h[X]
. Therefore, we can consider the mapping
h^{-1}\circ e_{\mathcal{H}}:Y\to X
. Since, for every
x\in X
,
h^{-1}(e_{\mathcal{H}}(x))=x
, it follows from Proposition
2.7
that, for every
y\in Y
,
h^{-1}(e_{\mathcal{H}}(y))=y
. This implies that
Y=X
. Hence (i) implies (ii).

(ii)\rightarrow(iii)
Now, let
J=C(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{E})
and let
\mathbf{Y}
be the subspace of
\mathbf{E}^{J}
with the underlying set
Y=\cl_{\mathbf{E}^{J}}e_{J}[X]
. It follows from (ii) that
Y=e_{J}[X]

3
The
\mathbf{ZF}
-proof of the Herrlich-Chew theorem

To prove that if
\mathbf{X}
is an

-compact space, then every clopen ultrafilter with the countable intersection property in

is fixed, Chew applied in
[

the Tychonoff Product Theorem to have that

is compact. However, the following proposition shows that, in

Proposition 3.1
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
The following statements (i) and (ii) are both equivalent to
\mathbf{BPI}
:

(i)

For every infinite set
J
, the space
\mathbb{N}(\infty)^{J}
is compact.

(ii)

For every zero-dimensional
T_{1}
-space,
v_{\mathbb{N}(\infty)}\mathbf{X}
is compact.

Proof.

We use the equivalents of
\mathbf{BPI}
cited in Remark
1.6
(c). Since for every infinite set
J
, the Cantor cube
\mathbf{2}^{J}
is a closed subspace of the space
\mathbb{N}(\infty)^{J}
, and
\mathbf{BPI}
implies that
\mathbb{N}(\infty)^{J}
is compact, we deduce that (i) and
\mathbf{BPI}
are equivalent. Clearly, (i) implies (ii).

To show that (ii) implies (i), we fix an infinite set
J
and put
\mathbf{X}=\mathbb{N}(\infty)
. Since
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact, it follows from Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
that
v_{\mathbb{N}(\infty)}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}
. Hence (ii) implies (i).
∎

With Proposition
3.1
in hand, one can easily check that the
\mathbf{ZFC}
-proof of the Herrlich-Chew theorem (see Theorem
1.3
), given in
[
2
]
, although incorrect in
\mathbf{ZF}
, remains correct in
\mathbf{ZF+BPI}
. The main goal of this section is to modify it to a proof of Theorem
1.7
(i) reformulated as follows:

Theorem 3.2
.

(Cf. Theorem
1.7
(i).) It is true in
\mathbf{ZF}
that, for every zero-dimensional
T_{1}
-space
\mathbf{X}
, the space
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact if and only if every clopen ultrafilter with the countable intersection property in
\mathbf{X}
is fixed.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a non-empty zero-dimensional
T_{1}
-space. We put
J=C(\mathbf{X},\mathbb{N}(\infty))
,
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbb{N}(\infty)^{J}
and
P=\cl_{\mathbf{Y}}e_{J}[X]
. In view of Theorem
2.9
, for the subspace
\mathbf{P}
of
\mathbf{Y}
, we have
\mathbf{P}=v_{\mathbb{N}(\infty)}\mathbf{X}
. One can easily check that the following condition
(a)
is satisfied:

(\forall A,B\in\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X}))\cl_{\mathbf{P}}(e_{J}[A\cap B])=\cl_{\mathbf{P}}(e_{J}[A])\cap\cl_{\mathbf{P}}(e_{J}[B]).

Suppose that
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact, and
\mathcal{U}
is a free clopen ultrafilter in
\mathbf{X}
. We are going to prove in
\mathbf{ZF}
that
\mathcal{U}
does not have the countable intersection property. To this aim, we modify the standard proof of the Tychonoff Product Theorem. Namely, we define
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}:=\{\cl_{\mathbf{P}}(e_{J}[U]):U\in\mathcal{U}\}
and, for every
j\in J
, we put

\mathcal{U}_{j}:=\{\cl_{\mathbb{N}(\infty)}\pi_{j}[U]:U\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}\}.

We notice that it follows from
(a)
that
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
is a clopen ultrafilter in
\mathbf{P}
. Since
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
is a filter, it follows that, for every
j\in J
, the family
\mathcal{U}_{j}
has the finite intersection property. By the compactness of
\mathbb{N}(\infty)
, for every
j\in J
,
\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{J}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, since
\mathbb{N}(\infty)
is well-orderable, we can fix
p\in Y
such that, for every
j\in J
,
p(j)\in\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{j}
. It is easy to check that
p\in P
. Suppose that there exists
U_{0}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
such that
p\notin U_{0}
. There exists a family
\{V_{j}:j\in J\}
of clopen sets of
\mathbb{N}(\infty)
such that the set
K=\{j\in J:V_{j}\neq\mathbb{N}(\infty)\}
is finite and, for
V=\prod_{j\in J}V_{j}
, we have
p\in V\subseteq Y\setminus U_{0}
. For every
j\in K
and
U\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
, we have
U\cap\pi_{j}^{-1}[V_{j}]\neq\emptyset
, so
\pi_{j}^{-1}[V_{j}]\cap P\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
because
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
is a clopen ultrafilter in
\mathbf{P}
. This implies that
V\cap P\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
which is impossible. The contradiction obtained proves that
p\in\bigcap\mathcal{U}^{\ast}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is free, we deduce that
p\notin e_{J}[X]
. This, together with the
\mathbb{N}
-compactness of
\mathbf{X}
and Theorem
2.16
, implies that there exists
f\in C(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{N}(\infty))
such that
f(p)=\infty
and
f[e_{J}[X]]\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. In much the same way, as in the proof of Theorem A in
[
2
]
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the set
V_{n}=\mathbb{N}(\infty)\setminus\{i\in\mathbb{N}:i\leq n\}
and
W_{n}=f^{-1}[V_{n}]
. We observe that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
e_{J}^{-1}[W_{n}]\in\mathcal{U}
but
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}e_{J}^{-1}[W_{n}]=\emptyset
, so
\mathcal{U}
does not have the countable intersection property.

Now, suppose that
\mathbf{X}
is not
\mathbb{N}
-compact. It follows from Theorem
2.16
that there exists
p_{0}\in P\setminus e_{J}[X]
such that, for every
f\in C(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{N}(\infty))
with
f[e_{J}[X]]\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we have
f(p_{0})\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{A\in\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X}):p_{0}\in\cl_{\mathbf{P}}(e_{J}[A])\}
. It follows from
(a)
that
\mathcal{F}
is a clopen ultrafilter in
\mathbf{X}
such that
\bigcap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
. To show that
\mathcal{F}
has the countable intersection property, we fix a subfamily
\{F_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathcal{F}
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
F_{n+1}\subseteq F_{n}
. Suppose that
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}=\emptyset
. In much the same way, as in
[
2
, proof of Theorem A]
, we define a function

f_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in X\setminus F_{1},\\
n+1&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in F_{n}\setminus F_{n+1}.\end{cases}

There exists a unique

Theorem 4.2
.

(Cf. Theorem

(iii).)
[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let

be a zero-dimensional

\mathbb{N}

(i) Suppose that the space

.

(ii) Now, suppose that
\mathbf{X}
is not
\mathbb{N}

. This shows that

is a

is free, so is

.

[\mathbf{ZF}]

(i)

-compact, so also realcompact.

(ii)

It has been known for a long time that every strongly zero-dimensional realcompact space is

Lemma 6.2
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let
\mathbf{X}
be a strongly zero-dimensional realcompact space and let
\{Z_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{X})
. Then
\mathbf{CMC}
implies that there exists a family
\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of clopen sets in
\mathbf{X}
such that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z_{n}=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
and, for every
m\in\omega
,
\bigcap_{i\in m+1}Z_{i}\subseteq C_{m}
.

Proof.

Assuming
\mathbf{CMC}
, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that there exists a family
\{f_{n}:n\in\omega\}
such that, for every
n\in\omega
,
f_{n}\in C(\mathbf{X},[0,1])
and
Z_{n}=Z(f_{n})
. For every
n\in\omega
, let
g_{n}=\sum_{i\in n+1}f_{i}
. Then, for every
n\in\omega
,
\bigcap_{i\in n+1}Z_{i}=Z(g_{n})
and
g_{n}\leq g_{n+1}
. For every
n\in\omega
, let

\mathcal{D}_{n}=\Bigl\{D\in\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X}):Z(g_{n})\subseteq D\subseteq\Bigl\{x\in X:\frac{1}{2}\leq g_{n}(x)\Bigr\}\Bigr\}.

Since
\mathbf{X}
is strongly zero-dimensional, the families
\mathcal{D}_{n}
are all non-empty. It follows from
\mathbf{CMC}
that there exists a family
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of non-empty finite sets such that, for every
n\in\omega
,
\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{n}
. For every
n\in\omega
, let
C_{n}=\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Then the sets
C_{n}
are all clopen in
\mathbf{X}
and, moreover,
Z(g_{n})\subseteq C_{n}\subseteq\bigl\{x\in X:\frac{1}{2}\leq g_{n}(x)\bigr\}
. Then
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z_{n}\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
. Suppose that
x\in X\setminus\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z_{n}
. Then there exists
m_{0}\in\omega
such that
x\notin Z(g_{m_{0}})
. There exists
m_{1}\in\omega
such that
m_{0}<m_{1}
and
\frac{1}{2^{m_{1}}}<g_{m_{0}}(x)
. Then
\frac{1}{2^{m_{1}}}<g_{m_{1}}(x)
, so

. This shows that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}C_{n}\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z_{n}
. Hence
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}C_{n}=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z_{n}
.

\mathbf{2}

\mathbf{S}

\mathbf{X}

Theorem 6.10
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let
\mathbf{X}
be a
T_{1}
-space. Suppose that

where the set
K
is compact in
\mathbf{X}
. Then the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(
[
6
, Theorem 8.16]
) if
\mathbf{X}
is completely regular and the subspace
\mathbf{S}
of

is realcompact, then

(ii)

is zero-dimensional and the subspace
\mathbf{S}
of
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact, then
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a Tychonoff space (respectively, a zero-dimensional space). Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a free ultrafilter in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{X})
(respectively, in
\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X})

\mathcal{F}_{S}=\{S\cap F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Since
\bigcap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
and
K

\mathbf{X}
, we can fix

such that

. Now, one can easily check that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is a filter in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{S})
(respectively, in
\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{S})
). Let us assume that
W\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{S})
.

(i) Assuming that
\mathbf{X}
is a Tychonoff space, by the compactness of

in
\mathbf{X}
, we can choose

such that
K\subseteq\inter_{\mathbf{X}}(Z_{1})
and
Z_{0}\cap Z_{1}=\emptyset
. In much the same way, as in
[
23
, proof of Fact 21]
, one can show that
W\cap Z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{X})
. Suppose that, for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{S}
,
W\cap T\neq\emptyset
. Then, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
,

. Hence

. This proves that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is an ultrafilter in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{S})
.

Now, assume that
\mathcal{F}
has the weak countable intersection property. Let us show that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
has the weak countable intersection property. To this aim, we assume that
\{f_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is a family of functions from

such that, for every
n\in\omega
,
Z(f_{n})\in\mathcal{F}_{S}
. We can fix

such that

. Using the idea from
[

, proof of Fact 21]
, for every

, we define a function

h_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{f_{n}(x)}{f_{n}(x)+g(x)}&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in X\setminus\inter_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{1};\\
1&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in Z_{1}.\end{cases}

We notice that, for every
n\in\omega
,
Z(f_{n})\cap Z_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
and

. Since
\mathcal{F}
has the weak countable intersection property,
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}Z(h_{n})\neq\emptyset
. In consequence,
\mathcal{F}_{S}
has the weak countable intersection property. Since
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is free,
\mathbf{S}
is not realcompact by Theorem

. This proves (i).

(ii) Now, we assume that
\mathbf{X}
is zero-dimensional,
\mathcal{F}
is a free ultrafilter in
\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X})
, and

is such that

. We notice that if
W\in\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{S})
, then,
W\cap Z_{0}\in\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{X})
, so, arguing in much the same way, as in the proof of (i), we can show that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is an ultrafilter in
\mathcal{CO}(\mathbf{S})
. Assuming that
\mathcal{F}
has the countable intersection property, to check that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
has the countable intersection property, it suffices to notice that if

is a collection of members of
\mathcal{F}_{S}
, then
\{E_{n}\cap Z_{0}:n\in\omega\}
is a collection of members of
\mathcal{F}
. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, if
\mathbf{S}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact, so is
\mathbf{X}
.
∎

Remark 6.14
.

In
[

, Remark (iii), p. 234]
, there is the following statement: “a topology stronger than a realcompact topology is realcompact”. However, it follows from our Corollary

that, in

-compact topology need not be

To show a reasonable sufficient condition for a stronger topology than a given realcompact (respectively,
\mathbb{N}
-compact) topology to be realcompact (respectively,
\mathbb{N}
-compact), let us give the following definitions and prove two lemmas.

Definition 6.15
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
be topological spaces. A continuous surjection

is called
z
-
perfect
(respectively,
c_{\delta}
-

, the set
f^{-1}[\{p\}]
is compact in
\mathbf{X}
and, for every closed set
C
in
\mathbf{X}
and every

, there exists
Z\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{Y})
(respectively,
Z\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}(\mathbf{Y})
) such that
y\in Z\subseteq Y\setminus f[C]
.

Definition 6.16
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\langle X,\tau\rangle
be a topological space.

(i)

(Cf., e.g.,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
.) The
Baire topology
of
\mathbf{X}
is the topology
\tau_{z}
on

such that

is a base of

.

(ii)

The

of

such that
\mathcal{CO}_{\delta}(\mathbf{X})

Lemma 6.17
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Let
C
be a subset of a topological space
\mathbf{X}
. Then
C\in\mathcal{CO}_{\delta}(\mathbf{X})
if and only if there exists
f\in C(\mathbf{X},\mathbb{N}(\infty))
such that
C=f^{-1}[\{\infty\}]
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
f\in C(\mathbf{X},\mathbb{N}(\infty))
and
C=f^{-1}[\{\infty\}]
, then
C
is a
c_{\delta}
-set of
\mathbf{X}
. On the other hand, if
C
is clopen in
\mathbf{X}
, we define a function

g(x)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in X\setminus C;\\
\infty&\quad\text{if}\quad x\in C.\end{cases}

Then
C=g^{-1}[\{\infty\}]
.

Let us assume that
C
is not clopen in
\mathbf{X}
but

\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}

and, for every

.

Lemma 6.18
.

[\mathbf{ZF}]
Suppose that
\mathbf{X}
and

(respectively,
\tilde{f}[v_{0}X\setminus X]\subseteq v_{0}Y\setminus Y

Proof.

Suppose that

(respectively,

\tilde{f}(p)\in Y
. There exists

and

. Since

m\in\omega
,
X\setminus B_{m}\in\mathcal{B}
. We may also assume that
\mathcal{B}
is closed under finite intersections. Suppose that, for every
n\in\omega
, the family
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\{C\in\mathcal{U}:C\cap K_{n}=\emptyset\}
is non-empty. We fix
n\in\omega
and
C\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Then
C
is a countable intersection of some members of
\mathcal{B}
. Since
C\cap K_{n}=\emptyset
, it follows from the compactness of
K_{n}
that there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
C\subseteq B
and
B\cap K_{n}=\emptyset
. Hence, the set
A_{n}=\{m\in\omega:B_{m}\in\mathcal{U}\wedge B_{m}\cap K_{n}=\emptyset\}
is non-empty. We can define in
\mathbf{ZF}
the number
m(n)=\min A_{n}
. Then we obtain the family
\{B_{m(n)}:n\in\omega\}
of members of
\mathcal{U}
such that
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}B_{m(n)}=\emptyset
. This contradicts the countable intersection property of
\mathcal{U}
. The contradiction obtained shows that there exists
n_{0}\in\omega
such that
\mathcal{C}_{n_{0}}=\emptyset
. By the compactness of
K_{n_{0}}
,
K_{n_{0}}\cap\bigcap\mathcal{U}\neq\emptyset
. This, together with Theorem
3.2
, shows that
\mathbf{X}
is
\mathbb{N}
-compact.
∎

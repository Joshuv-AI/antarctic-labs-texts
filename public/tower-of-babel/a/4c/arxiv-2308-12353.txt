Abstract
. We prove that the closure of the numerical range of a ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -periodic and ( 2 ​ m + 1 ) (2m+1) -banded Toeplitz operator can be expressed as the closure of the convex hull of the uncountable union of numerical ranges of certain symbol matrices. In contrast to the periodic 3 3 -banded (or tridiagonal) case, we show an example of a 2 2 -periodic and 5 5 -banded Toeplitz operator such that the closure of its numerical range is not equal to the numerical range of a single finite matrix.

[
2
]
already include the case for banded Toeplitz operators of arbitrary band, they are constrained to biperiodic Toeplitz operators. Hence, our results may also be regarded as a generalization of those in
[
2
]
. Later, in
[
14

, the authors were able to show that the closure of the numerical range of a
(n+1)
-periodic tridiagonal operator is equal to the numerical range of a single
2(n+1)\times 2(n+1)

5
-banded Toeplitz operator for which the closure of its numerical range cannot be the numerical range of a single complex matrix.

Also, as a consequence of the above representation of the numerical range of a periodic banded Toeplitz operator as the closure of the convex hull of the union of numerical ranges of finite matrices, we obtain the result that there exists a real binary polynomial
g(x,y)
such that the boundary of the numerical range of a periodic banded Toeplitz operator is a subset of the set of zeroes of
g(x,y)
.

We divide this work in three sections. In Section 2 we establish the definitions needed and state and prove the main result of this paper. In Section 3 we prove a consequence of the main result; namely the existence of a real binary form such that the boundary of the numerical range of a periodic banded Toeplitz operator is contained in the zero set of such form. Finally, in Section 4 we give an example of a 2-periodic and 5-banded Toeplitz operator for which the closure of the numerical range is not the numerical range of a single matrix.

f_{0,0}&f_{1,0}&\dots&f_{n,0}&f_{0,1}&f_{1,1}&\dots&f_{n,1}&\dots&\dots&f_{0,s-1}&f_{1,s-1}&\dots&f_{n,s-1}\\
\vrule&\vrule&&\vrule&\vrule&\vrule&&\vrule&&&\vrule&\vrule&&\vrule\end{pmatrix}.

The matrix
U
is useful in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
n
,
s
and
\mu
be as above. Then
C_{\mu}
is unitarily equivalent to a block diagonal matrix. More precisely,

U^{*}C_{\mu}U=\Phi(0)\oplus\Phi(2\pi/s)\oplus\Phi(4\pi/s)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Phi(2(s-1)\pi/s).

Proof.

Let
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}
be integers such that
0\leq q_{1},q_{2}\leq s-1
and
0\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq n
. Observe that

\displaystyle\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{u=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\langle C_{\mu}\rho^{uq_{1}}e_{p_{1}+u(n+1)},\rho^{vq_{2}}e_{p_{2}+v(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{u=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{uq_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\langle C_{\mu}e_{p_{1}+u(n+1)},e_{p_{2}+v(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{u=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{uq_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+u(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}+v(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{u=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{uq_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+(u-v)(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle,

where the last equality follows from the
(n+1)
-periodicity of
T
.

Making the change of variables
u=v+r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-v}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle.

For each
v=0,1,2,\dots,s-1
, we consider the inner sum of the above expression and rewrite it as

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-v}^{-v+s-1}

\displaystyle\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-v}^{-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle+\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle.

Making another change of variables in the first summand above, we obtain

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-v}^{-1}

\displaystyle\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle+\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=s-v}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r-s)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+(r-s)(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle+\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=s-v}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w-1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle+\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=s-v}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle+\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{-v+s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+w\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w+1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle.

Hence

\begin{split}\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle&=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w+1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{(v+r)q_{1}}\rho^{-vq_{2}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w+1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{rq_{1}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w+1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{v(q_{1}-q_{2})}.\end{split}

(4)

Now, assume
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
. Since this implies that
\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{v(q_{1}-q_{2})}=0
, equation (
4
) gives

\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle=0.

If
q_{1}=q_{2}
, then
\sumop\displaylimits_{v=0}^{s-1}\rho^{v(q_{1}-q_{2})}=s
and hence equation (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{rq_{1}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+r(n+1)+(w+1)\mu},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{rq_{1}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+(r+(w+1)s)(n+1)},e_{p_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{w\in{\mathbb Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\rho^{(r+(w+1)s)q_{1}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+(r+(w+1)s)(n+1)},e_{p_{2}}\rangle,

since
\rho^{s}=1
.

Observe that when
r
ranges from
0
to
s-1
and
w
ranges in
{\mathbb{Z}}
, the number
r+(w+1)s
ranges over all the integers, and hence the previous expression can be written as

\langle C_{\mu}f_{p_{1},q_{1}},f_{p_{2},q_{2}}\rangle=\sumop\displaylimits_{u\in{\mathbb Z}}\rho^{uq_{1}}\langle Te_{p_{1}+u(n+1)},e_{p_{2}}\rangle,

which equals the
(p_{2},p_{1})
entry of the matrix
\Phi\left(\frac{2\pi q_{1}}{s}\right)
.

The above computations show that the matrix of
C_{\mu}
with respect to the orthogonal basis
\{f_{p,q}\,:\,0\leq p\leq n,\ 0\leq q\leq s-1\}
is block-diagonal and each of the
s
blocks on the diagonal is of the form
\Phi\left(\frac{2\pi q}{s}\right)
, for
q=0,1,\dots,s-1
. This proves the desired result.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
n,m\in{\mathbb N}
and let
T_{+}
be an
(n+1)
-periodic and
(2m+1)
-banded selfadjoint Toeplitz operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})
and let
\Phi
be its symbol (which is a Hermitian matrix). Let
\lambda^{-}(\theta)
and
\lambda^{+}(\theta)
denote the
smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\Phi(\theta)
and set

a=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{-}(\theta)\quad\text{ and }\quad b=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{+}(\theta).

Then the closure of
W(T_{+})
is the closed interval

\overline{W(T_{+})}=[a,b].

Proof.

The proof follows the same argument as that of Theorem 2.6 in
[
13
]
. We include the first part since some details are different and for the sake of completeness.
We first show that
[a,b]\subseteq\overline{W(T_{+})}
. Let
s\in{\mathbb N}
such that
\mu:=s(n+1)\geq 2m+1
. Let
\lambda_{s}\in\sigma(C_{s(n+1)})
.
Lemma
2.5
then implies that there exists
r=0,1,2,\dots,s-1
such that
\lambda_{s}\in\sigma\left(\Phi(\frac{2r\pi}{s})\right)
. We can choose an eigenvector

\vec{v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
of
\Phi(\frac{2r\pi}{s})
of norm
1/\sqrt{s}
. In the manner of Lemma
2.5
, the vector
\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}
is an eigenvector of norm
1
for the matrix
C_{s(n+1)}
.

Recall that
T_{s(n+1)}
denotes the compression of the operator
T_{+}
to the subspace of
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})
spanned by the first
\mu=s(n+1)
vectors in the canonical basis:
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{\mu-1}\}
. We then have

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{s(n+1)}\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle C_{s(n+1)}\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle+\left\langle\left(T_{s(n+1)}-C_{s(n+1)}\right)\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{s}+\left\langle\left(T_{s(n+1)}-C_{s(n+1)}\right)\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle.

Since the real number
\left\langle T_{s(n+1)}\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle
is contained in
W(T_{s(n+1)})\subseteq W(T_{+})
, we see that

\lambda_{s}+\left\langle\left(T_{s(n+1)}-C_{s(n+1)}\right)\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle\in W(T_{+}).

(5)

Observe that the entries of
C_{s(n+1)}
and
T_{s(n+1)}
coincide everywhere except at the “triangular corners” on the top-right and down-left positions. Hence
b_{j,k}
, the
(j,k)
entry of
C_{s(n+1)}-T_{s(n+1)}
, is zero if
|j-k|<s(n+1)-m
, and therefore at most
m(m+1)
entries of the matrix
C_{s(n+1)}-T_{s(n+1)}
are nonzero. If we set
\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}=(w_{0},w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{\mu-1})
, and we recall that the modulus of each coordinate of
\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}
is equal to the modulus of some coordinate of
\vec{v}
, we have
|w_{j}|\leq\|\vec{v}\,\|
, and hence

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\left(C_{s(n+1)}-T_{s(n+1)}\right)\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\mu-1}|b_{j,k}|\ |w_{j}|\ |w_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\mu-1}|b_{j,k}|\ \|\vec{v}\,\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{|b_{j,k}|\,:\,j,k=0,1,2,\dots,\mu-1\right\}\ m(m+1)\ \|\vec{v}\,\|^{2}.

Since each nonzero
b_{j,k}
is multiple, of modulus one, of an element of the finite set
\left\{a_{j}^{(k)}\right\}
, and since
\|\vec{v}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{s}
it follows that

\left|\left\langle\left(C_{s(n+1)}-T_{s(n+1)}\right)\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>},\,\overrightarrow{V_{r}\>}\right\rangle\right|\leq\max\left\{\left|a_{j}^{(k)}\right|\,:\,j=0,1,\dots,n,\ k=-m,\dots,0,\dots,m\right\}\ \frac{m(m+1)}{s}.

Hence, the previous estimate ensures that if we can find a sequence
(\lambda_{s})
which converges as
s\to\infty
, then, by expression (
5
), we would have

\lim_{s\to\infty}\lambda_{s}\in\overline{W(T_{+})}.

(6)

We now propose two suitable sequences of eigenvalues
(\lambda_{s})
. For each
s
, let
\lambda_{s}^{-}
and

\lambda_{s}^{+}
denote the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
C_{s(n+1)}
, respectively.
We will prove that

\lim_{s\to\infty}\lambda_{s}^{-}=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{-}(\theta)

(7)

where
\lambda^{-}(\theta)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
\Phi(\theta)
. It will follow then, by taking the limit as

s\to\infty
, that
a=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{-}(\theta)\in\overline{W(T_{+})}
.

By Proposition
2.3
and since
\lambda_{s}^{-}
is the smallest eigenvalue of
C_{s(n+1)}
, we have

\sigma(C_{s(n+1)})=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{r=0}^{s-1}\sigma\left(\Phi\left(\frac{2r\pi}{s}\right)\right).

Bu then
\lambda_{s}^{-}
is not only an eigenvalue of
\Phi(\frac{2\pi r}{s})
for some
r
but it is, in
fact, the smallest among the eigenvalues of all symbols
\Phi(\frac{2r\pi}{s})
for

; i.e.,

\lambda^{-}_{s}=\lambda^{-}\left(\frac{2r\pi}{s}\right)
for some

.

Let

\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{-}(\theta)

where
\theta^{*}
is the point where the minimum is reached.
Therefore
\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})\leq\lambda^{-}\left(\frac{2r\pi}{s}\right)=\lambda_{s}^{-}
. Using the continuity of
\lambda^{-}(\cdot)
, for all

\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that
|\theta-\theta^{*}|<\delta
implies

\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})\leq\lambda^{-}(\theta)<\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})+\epsilon
. Also, there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for

we may choose
0\leq j\leq s-1
such that

. Thus for all

\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})\leq\lambda_{s}^{-}=\lambda^{-}\left(\frac{2r\pi}{s}\right)\leq\lambda^{-}\left(\frac{2j\pi}{s}\right)<\lambda^{-}(\theta^{*})+\epsilon.

This proves Equation (
7
). Analogously, one can show that

\lim_{s\to\infty}\lambda_{s}^{+}=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{+}(\theta),

where
\lambda_{s}^{+}
is the largest eigenvalue of
C_{s(n+1)}
and
\lambda^{+}(\theta)
is the largest eigenvalue of
\Phi(\theta)
.

Hence, Expression (
6
) proves that

a=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\lambda^{-}(\theta)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lambda_{s}^{-}\in\overline{W(T_{+})}

and

Convexity then shows that
[a,b]\subseteq\overline{W(T_{+})}
.

The proof of the inclusion
\overline{W(T_{+})}\subseteq[a,b]
is now exactly the same as that of Theorem 2.6 in
[
13
]
and we omit the proof.

3.
The boundary of the numerical range of a periodic banded Toeplitz operator

In this section we shall consider the boundary of the numerical range
W(T_{+})
of the
(n+1)
-periodic and
(2m+1)
-banded Toeplitz operator
T_{+}
. By Theorem
2.7
, every boundary point of
W(T_{+})
lies on the boundary of the set

{\mathcal{O}}:=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}W(\Phi(\theta))

or on one of the support lines of
\mathcal{O}
. Each symbol matrix
\Phi(\theta)
is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
complex matrix whose entries are trigonometric polynomials in
\theta
. So we can express
\Phi(\theta)
as a finite sum of the form

\Phi(\theta)=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-L}^{L}A(r)e^{ir\theta},

for some
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
where each
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix
A(r)
does not depend on
\theta
. Hence, for each
\xi=(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, we have

\langle\Phi(\theta)\xi,\xi\rangle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-L}^{L}\langle A(r)e^{ir\theta}\xi,\xi\rangle=\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-L}^{L}e^{ir\theta}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{n}a_{j,k}(r)\xi_{j}\overline{\xi_{k}},

where
a_{j,k}(r)
is the
(j,k)
entry of the matrix
A(r)
. Hence

{\mathcal{O}}=\left\{\sumop\displaylimits_{r=-L}^{L}e^{ir\theta}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}a_{j,k}(k)\xi_{j}\overline{\xi_{k}}:\xi=(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb C}^{n+1},\|\xi\|=1,0\leq\theta<2\pi\right\}.

Under the identification of
{\mathbb C}
with
{\mathbb R}^{2}
, the set

\{\xi\in{\mathbb C}^{n+1}\ :\ \|\xi\|=1\}\times S^{1}

can be written as

\{(x_{0},y_{0},x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n}y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+2}\ :\ x_{0}^{2}+y_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+\dots x_{n}^{2}+y_{n}^{2}=1\}\times\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb R}^{2}:x^{2}+y^{2}=1\},

which is an algebraic set (see, for example,
[
4
]
). But then,
{\mathcal{O}}
is the image of a real algebraic set under a polynomial map from
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+4}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Then, by the Tarski-Seidenberg Theorem (cf.
[
4
]
, page 60, Theorem 2.3.4),
{\mathcal{O}}
is a semi-algebraic set. Hence, the boundary of
\mathcal{O}
lies on some real algebraic curve; that is, there is a non-zero real polynomial
f(x,y)
for which every boundary point
(x,y)
of
{\mathcal{O}}
satisfies

f(x,y)=0.

Since the set
\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}\,:\,\|\xi\|=1\}\times S^{1}
is compact and connected, its image
{\mathcal{O}}
under
a continuous map is also compact and connected. Since the number of connected components of a semialgebraic set is finite
[
5
, Theorem 2.4.5]
, this implies that the boundary of
\mathcal{O}
has a finite number of connected components.

Consider the set of polynomials
f(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
such that

\partial{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,:\,f(x,y)=0\}.

By the comment above, this set is nonempty. We choose
f_{0}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
, of smallest degree, satisfying

\partial{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb R}^{2}:f_{0}(x,y)=0\}.

Clearly, since
{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
is a unique factorization domain, we can write
f_{0}(x,y)
as

f_{0}=f_{1}^{m_{1}}f_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots f_{k}^{m_{k}}

where
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{k}
are the mutually inequivalent irreducible factors of
f_{0}
and the numbers
m_{j}
are positive integers. Since
f_{0}(x,y)
is of minimum degree, clearly we must have that
m_{j}=1
for all
j
.

The curve
\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:f_{0}(x,y)=0\}
may have multitangents. Such a multitangent corresponds to some singular point of the dual curve of the multiplicity free curve
f_{0}(x,y)=0
; therefore, the number of such lines is at most finite. We denote the union of such multitangents as
g_{0}(x,y)=0
using some
polynomial
g_{0}(x,y)\in{\mathbb R}[x,y]
. By letting
g(x,y)=f_{0}(x,y)g_{0}(x,y)
, we have the following inclusion

\partial\mathrm{Conv}\left({\mathcal{O}}\right)\subseteq\{(x,y):g(x,y)=0\}.

We have obtained the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T_{+}
be an
(n+1)
-periodic and
(2m+1)
-banded Toeplitz operator acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb N_{0}})
. Then there exists a nonzero real binary polynomial
g(x,y)
such that if
(x,y)\in\partial W(T_{+})
, then
g(x,y)=0
.

4.
A Toeplitz operator for which the closure of the numerical range is not the numerical range of a matrix

We examine the following example of a
2
-periodic and
5
-banded Toeplitz operator
T_{+}
. We set the sequences
a^{(0)}
,
a^{(-1)}
and
a^{(-2)}
to be the constant sequences of zeroes, we set the sequence
a^{(1)}=\left(a_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with
a_{j}^{(1)}=-1
if
j
is an even integer and
a_{j}^{(1)}=2
if
j
is an odd integer; and we set the sequence
a^{(2)}
to be the constant sequence of ones. That is, the matrix of
T_{+}
has the upper triangular form

\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&1&0&\cdots\\
0&0&2&1&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&-1&1&0&\cdots\\
0&0&0&0&2&1&0&\cdots\\
\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&0&0&0&0&-1&1&0&\cdots\\
\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

By Definition
2.1
, the symbol of
T_{+}
is the
2\times 2
matrix function

\Phi(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta}&-1\\
2e^{i\theta}&e^{i\theta}\end{pmatrix}.

The numerical range of
\Phi(\theta)
is the convex set bounded by the ellipse with focii the points

e^{i\theta}+i\sqrt{2}e^{i\theta/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad e^{i\theta}-i\sqrt{2}e^{i\theta/2},

major axis of length
3
, and minor axis of length
1
.

By Theorem
2.7
, the closure of
W(T)
is given as the closure of the convex hull of the union of the above ellipses.

Each ellipse can be parametrized as

\displaystyle X(t)

\displaystyle=\cos(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta/2)\cos(t)-\frac{3}{2}\sin(\theta/2)\sin(t),

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle=\sin(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}\sin(\theta/2)\cos(t)+\frac{3}{2}\cos(\theta/2)\sin(t),

where
t\in[0,2\pi)
. The Cartesian equation of each ellipse is

(20+16\cos\theta)(X-\cos\theta)^{2}+(20-16\cos\theta)(Y-\sin\theta)^{2}+32\sin\theta(X-\cos\theta)(Y-\sin\theta)=9.

or equivalently,

(16X^{2}-16Y^{2}-40X+16)\cos\theta+(32XY-40Y)\sin\theta+(20X^{2}+20Y^{2}-32X+11)=0.

We now consider the function

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle(X,Y;\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(16X^{2}-16Y^{2}-40X+16)\cos\theta+(32XY-40Y)\sin\theta+(20X^{2}+20Y^{2}-32X+11).

Observe that, for each
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, the boundary of the numerical range of
\Phi(\theta)
is given by the ellipse
H(X,Y;\theta)=0
. To find the envelope of this family of ellipses, we write

H(X,Y;\theta)=\alpha\cos\theta+\beta\sin\theta+\gamma

where

\alpha=16X^{2}-16Y^{2}-40X+16,\quad\beta=32XY-40Y,\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma=20X^{2}+20Y^{2}-32X+11.

As is well-known
[
6
, p. 76]
, the envelope consists of those points
(X,Y)
such that there exists
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
such that

H(X,Y;\theta)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}H(X,Y;\theta)=0.

In other words, to find the points
(X,Y)
in the envelope, we need to check if there exists
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
such that the system

\alpha\cos\theta+\beta\sin\theta+\gamma=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta\cos\theta-\alpha\sin\theta=0,

has a solution. A sufficient and necessary condition for this system to have a solution is that
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=\gamma^{2}
; that is

(16X^{2}-16Y^{2}-40X+16)^{2}+(32XY-40Y)^{2}=(20X^{2}+20Y^{2}-32X+11)^{2}.

Equivalently,

16X^{4}+32X^{2}Y^{2}+16Y^{4}-72X^{2}-72Y^{2}+64X-15=0.

(9)

Solving for
Y
and using the second derivative test for convexity, it is straightforward to check that the points satisfying the above equation are the boundary of a convex set, except for the isolated point
(\frac{1}{2},0)
which lies in the interior of said set. Hence, the union of the ellipses
H(X,Y;\theta)=0
and their interiors is a convex set. Also it is clear that the union of the family of the ellipses
H(X,Y;\theta)=0
and their interiors is a closed set. Therefore, by Theorem
2.7
, we conclude that a point
X+iY
is in the boundary of the numerical range of the operator
T_{+}
if and only if it satisfies the equation

16X^{4}+32X^{2}Y^{2}+16Y^{4}-72X^{2}-72Y^{2}+64X-15=0,

and
(X,Y)\neq(\frac{1}{2},0)
.

Hence, the boundary of
W(T_{+})
consists of the points described by the set

\left\{(X,Y)\in{\mathbb R}^{2}:L(1,X,Y)=0\right\}\setminus\left\{(\tfrac{1}{2},0)\right\}

where

L(U,X,Y)=16X^{4}+32X^{2}Y^{2}+16Y^{4}-72U^{2}X^{2}-72U^{2}Y^{2}+64U^{3}X-15U^{4},

(10)

see Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
The red solid curve represents the points such that
L(1,X,Y)=0
and the black dotted curves represent the ellipses
H(X,Y;\theta)=0
for six different values of
\theta
.

The complex projective curve

\left\{[U;X;Y]\in{\mathbb CP}^{2}:L(U,X,Y)=0\right\}

has an ordinary double point at
(U,X,Y)=(2,1,0)
and a pair of
(2,3)
-cusps at
(U,X,Y)=(0,1,\pm i)
. This curve has no other singular points.
These properties imply that the quartic polynomial
L(U,X,Y)
is irreducible in the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[U,X,Y]
(see
[
9
]
).

Also, we consider the dual curve
\ell(t,x,y)=0
of the curve
L(U,X,Y)=0
under the pairing

tU+xX+yY=0.

The curve
\ell(t,x,y)=0
is also an irreducible quartic algebraic curve and a computation shows that
\ell
is given by

\ell(t,x,y)=16t^{4}+32t^{3}x-72t^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})-216t(x^{3}+xy^{2})-135x^{4}-162x^{2}y^{2}-27y^{4}.

(11)

The irreducibility of
L
and
\ell
will be useful when answering the main question of this section: Is there some
k\times k
complex matrix
B
for which

\overline{W(T_{+})}=W(B)?

The following theorem shows that the answer is negative.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
a^{(0)}
,
a^{(-1)}
and
a^{(-2)}
be the constant sequences of zeroes, let
a^{(1)}=\left(a_{j}^{(1)}\right)_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the sequence defined by
a_{j}^{(1)}=-1
if
j
is an even integer and
a_{j}^{(1)}=2
if
j
is an odd integer; and let
a^{(2)}
be the constant sequence of ones. Suppose that
T_{+}
is the biperiodic and
5
-banded Toeplitz operator acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})
defined by these sequences, which has a matrix representation given in (
8
). For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is no
k\times k
matrix
B
such that

\overline{W(T_{+})}=W(B).

Before proving this theorem, we introduce a notion which was originally formulated for the principal symbol of a partial differential operator.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose that
F(t,x,y)
is a real homogeneous polynomial of degree
k\geq 1
. The form
F
is said to be
hyperbolic
with respect to
(1,0,0)
if for every nonzero
(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the equation

F(t,x_{0},y_{0})=0

as a polynomial in the variable
t
has
k
real solutions counting multiplicities.

Observe that if
F_{B}
is the Kippenhahn polynomial associated with an
k\times k
matrix
B
; i.e.,

F_{B}(t,x,y)={\rm det}(tI_{m}+x\mathrm{Re}(B)+y\mathrm{Im}(B)),

then the equation
F_{B}(t,x,y)=0
is the characteristic equation of the Hermitian matrix
-x\mathrm{Re}(B)-y\mathrm{Im}(B)
and hence, for each
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, it has
k
real roots counting multiplicites. So the form
F_{B}
is hyperbolic with respect to
(1,0,0)
(cf.
[
16
]
).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By contradiction, we assume that for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a
k\times k
matrix
B
satisfying
W(B)=\overline{W(T_{+})}
.

By Kippenhahn’s theorem, the numerical range of a matrix
B
equals the convex hull of the real affine part of the dual curve of
F_{B}(t,x,y)=0
. Let us denote by
f_{B}(U,X,Y)
the dual curve of
F_{B}(t,x,y)
. As we have seen, a point
(X_{0},Y_{0})
on the boundary of the numerical range of
T_{+}
satisfies the equation
L(1,X_{0},Y_{0})=0
, where
L(U,X,Y)
is given in (
10
). Then the assumption implies that
f_{B}(1,X_{0},Y_{0})=0
. By Bézout’s Theorem,
L(U,X,Y)
and
f_{B}(U,X,Y)
must have a common component. Since
L(U,X,Y)
is irreducible, we conclude that
L(U,X,Y)
is a factor of
f_{B}(U,X,Y)
. It follows that its dual curve,
\ell(t,x,y)
, described in (
11
), must be a factor of
F_{B}(t,x,y)
.

By the relation (3.12) of
[
1
, p. 130]
, the ternary form
\ell(t,x,y)
as a factor of
F_{B}(t,x,y)
has to be hyperbolic with respect to
(1,0,0)
, that is, the quartic equation
\ell(t,-\cos\theta,-\sin\theta)=0
in
t
has
4
real solutions counting its multiplicities for any angle
0\leq\theta<2\pi
. But the equation for
\theta=\pi/2
is expressed as

16t^{4}-72t^{2}-27=\left(4t^{2}-(6\sqrt{3}+9)\right)\,\left(4t^{2}+(6\sqrt{3}-9)\right)=0.

The equation
4t^{2}-(6\sqrt{3}+9)=0
has two real solutions, but since
6\sqrt{3}-9>0
, the equation
4t^{2}+(6\sqrt{3}-9)
has no real solutions. Hence, the quartic equation
16t^{4}-72t^{2}-27=0
in
t
does not have only real solutions. So the ternary form
\ell(t,x,y)
is not hyperbolic with respect to
(1,0,0)
. This contradiction implies that our assumption on the existence of a
k\times k
matrix
B
with
W(B)=\overline{W(T_{+})}
is false.
∎

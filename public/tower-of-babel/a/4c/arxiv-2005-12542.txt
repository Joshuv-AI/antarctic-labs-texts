Abstract
. We formulate a number of new results in Algebraic Geometry and outline their derivation from Theorem 2.12 which belongs to Algebraic Combinatorics.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we present a derivation of a number of results in Algebraic Geometry from a theorem (Theorem
2.12
) in Algebraic Combinatorics and it extension (Theorem
8.3
) to the case of
p
-adic rings. In the first half of the article we outline the derivations of results in Algebraic Geometry relying on results of
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
. Afterwards we present a proof of
an extension of Theorem
2.12
to the
p
-adic realm and an application of this result to
Algebraic Geometry.

1.1.
Basic definitions

We will denote finite fields by
k
and general fields by
K
.
It will be important to distinguish between an algebraic variety
{\mathbf{X}}
defined over a field
K
and the set
X:={\mathbf{X}}(K)
of
K
-points of
{\mathbf{X}}
. For a polynomial

P:V\to K
we write
{\mathbf{X}}_{P}:=P^{-1}(0)\subset{\mathbf{V}}
and

X_{P}:=P^{-1}(0)\subset V
. So
X_{P}={\mathbf{X}}_{P}(K)
.

In this paper we heavily use the notion of rank of polynomials introduced in a paper by Schmidt
[
20
]
, which characterizes complexity of polynomials.

1
1

1

This notion of complexity was also introduced later in the work of Ananyan and Hochster
[
1
]
where it is called
strength
.
.

Definition 1.1

(Schmidt rank)
.

Let
P
be a polynomial of degree
d
on a
K
-vector space
V
. We define the
rank

r_{K}(P)
as the minimal number
r
such that
P
can be written in the form
P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}R_{i}
, where
Q_{i},R_{i}
are
K
-polynomials on
V
of degrees
<d
.

When there is no confusion we will omit the subscript
K
from
r_{K}(P)
.

Example 1.2
.

If
P:V\to K
is a non-degenerate quadratic form then

\mathrm{dim}(V)/2\leq r(P)\leq 3\mathrm{dim}(V)/2
.

We show that morphisms
{\mathbf{P}}:{\mathbf{V}}\to{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
defined by polynomials
of sufficiently high rank and degree less then characteristic of
K

possess a number of nice properties. In particular we show that these morphisms are flat and that their fibers are complete intersections with rational singularities. In the case of fields of small characteristic we have to replace
the rank
r(P)
by the non-classical rank
r_{nc}(P)
defined in
2.3
.

For polynomials over finite fields there is also a notion of an analytic rank.

Definition 1.3

(Analytic rank)
.

Let
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and

P
be a polynomial of degree
d
on a
k
-vector space
V
. Fix a non trivial additive character
\psi:k\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and define the
analytic rank

a_{k}(P):=-\log_{q}|q^{-\dim V}\sum_{v\in V}\psi(P(v))|
.

To simplify notations we assume throughout most of the introduction that
V=V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}
and that polynomials

P:V\to K
are multilinear. In this case the analytic rank does not depend on a choice a non-trivial additive character.

Most of the results in this paper are based on
the following Theorem relating rank and analytic rank for multilinear polynomials over finite fields (see
[
3
,
19
]
):

Fact 1.4
.

There exist a function
\gamma_{d}(s)
,
\lim_{s\to\infty}\gamma_{d}(s)=\infty
, such that for any finite field
k
, and degree
d
multilinear polynomial
P
we have
a_{k}(P)\geq\gamma_{d}(r_{k}(P))
.

1.2.
Basic conjectures and questions

Before describing the our results we formulate some basic questions and conjectures regarding the notions of rank defined above.

Conjecture 1.5

(d)
.

For any
d\geq 2
there exists
\kappa_{d}>0
such that
for any multilinear polynomial
P
we have

r_{K}(P)\leq\kappa_{d}r_{\bar{K}}(P)
where
\bar{K}
is the algebraic closure of
K
.

Remark 1.6
.

(1)

It is easy to see that Conjecture
1.5
holds for
d=2
with
\kappa_{2}=1/2
.

(2)

Since in the case when
d=3
the Schmidt rank is equal to the
slice
rank the validity of Conjecture
1.5
(3) follows from Theorem 2.5 and Proposition 4.9 in
[
6
]
.

Conjecture 1.7

(d)
.

For any
d\geq 2
there exists
\epsilon_{d}>0
such that
a_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(P)\geq\epsilon_{d}r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(P)

for any multilinear polynomial of degree
d
.

Remark 1.8
.

(1)

The inequality

is known. See
[
13
,
16
]
.

(2)

It is easy to see that Conjecture

holds for
d=2
with
\epsilon_{2}=1/2
.

Let
K
be an algebraically close field, and
(P_{s})_{s\in S}
be a family of polynomials. We define
U\subset S
as the subset of points

t\in S
such that
r(P_{t})=\max_{s\in S}r(P_{s})
. It is easy to see that this subset is constructible.

Question 1.9
.

Is the subset
U\subset S
locally closed?

1.3.
Main results

We formulate in the introduction special cases of the main results. To simplify notations we assume that
K
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
>d=\deg P
.
For the validity of these results we do not have to assume that polynomials
P
are multilinear.

Theorem 1.10
.

For any
d\geq 2
there exists
r(d)
such that varieties
{\mathbf{X}}_{P}\subset{\mathbf{V}}
are geometrically irreducible normal varieties with rational singularities.

For a formulation of two other results we need a couple of definitions.

Definition 1.11
.

A polynomial
P
of degree
d
on a
K
-vector space
K^{m}

is
m-universal
if for any

Q\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
of degrees
\leq d
there exists an affine map

\phi:K^{m}\to V
such that
Q=\phi^{\star}(P)
.

Theorem 1.12

(Universal)
.

There exists a function
R(d,m)
such that any
polynomial
P

of degree
d
and rank
\geq R(d,m)
is
m
-universal.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
V
be a
K
-vector space and
X
a subset of
V
.
A function
f:X\to K
is weakly polynomial of degree
\leq a
, if for any affine subspace
L\subset X

the restriction of
f
on
L
is a polynomial of degree
\leq a
.

Theorem 1.14
.

For any
a,d\geq 1
there exists
r(a,d)
such that the following holds.

For any polynomial
P:V\to K
of degree
d
and rank
\geq r(a,d)
, any weakly polynomial function
of degree
\leq a
on
X_{P}\subset V
is a restriction of a polynomial of degree
\leq a
on
V
.

The last result we state is for polynomials over finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem 1.15
.

There exists a function
\rho(d)>0
such that for any finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
>d
and polynomials
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
such that
\deg(P)=d
,
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(P)\geq\rho(d)
,
\deg(Q)\leq q/d
and such that
Q(\bar{x})=0
for all
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
such that
P(\bar{x})=0
, we have
Q=PR
for some
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
.

In the paper all the above mentioned results are extended to the case when we replace a polynomial
P
by a
collection
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{c})
of polynomials on
V
.

2.
The main tool

We start with defining the Schimdt rank for a family of polynomials.

Definition 2.1

(Schmidt rank)
.

(1)

Let
P
be a polynomial of degree
d
on a
K
-vector space
V
. We define the
rank

r_{K}(P)
as the minimal number
r
such that
P
can be written in the form
P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}R_{i}
, where
Q_{i},R_{i}
are polynomials on
V
of degrees
<d
.

(2)

For a collection of polynomials
\bar{P}=(P_{i})_{1\leq i\leq c}
on
V
we define the rank
r_{k}(\bar{P})
as the minimal rank of polynomials
P_{\bar{a}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}P_{i}
,
\bar{a}\in K^{c}\setminus\{0\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

We will often consider
\bar{P}
as a morphism from
{\mathbf{V}}
to
{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
.

(2)

When there is no confusion we write
r(\bar{P})
instead of
r_{k}(\bar{P})
.

When the characteristic of the field is smaller then the maximal degree in
\bar{P}
we need to replace the notion of rank with non-classical rank.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P
be a polynomial of degree
d
on a
K
-vector space
V
.

(1)

We denote by
\tilde{P}:V^{d}\to k
the symmetric multilinear form given by

\tilde{P}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}):=\Delta_{h_{1}}\ldots\Delta_{h_{d}}P:V^{d}\to k
,
where
\Delta_{h}P(x):=P(x+h)-P(x)
. Note that
\tilde{P}
only depends on the top-degree part of
P
.

(2)

We define the
non-classical rank (nc-rank)

r_{nc}(P)
to be the rank of
\tilde{P}
.

(3)

For a collection
\bar{P}=(P_{i})_{1\leq i\leq c}
of polynomials on
V
we define the nc-rank
r_{nc}(\bar{P})
as the minimal nc-rank of
polynomials
P_{\bar{a}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}P_{i}
,
\bar{a}\in k^{c}\setminus\{0\}
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

If char
(K)>d
then
r(P)\sim r_{nc}(P)
(see
[
10
]
).

(2)

In low characteristic it can happen that
P
is of high rank while
\tilde{P}
is of low rank.

Example 2.5
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic

2
,
{\mathbf{V}}={\mathbf{A}}^{n}
and
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{1<i<j<k<l\leq n}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}x_{l}
is of rank
\sim n
, but of nc-rank
3
, (see
[
21
]
).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
k:=\mathbb{F}_{q},V
be a
k
-vector space and
\bar{P}:V\to k^{c}
be a map. We define

(1)

F_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}):=\{v\in V|\bar{P}(v)=\bar{t}\}
,
\bar{t}\in k^{c}
.

(2)

\nu_{\bar{P}}:k^{c}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is the function given by
\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t}):=|F_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})|/q^{\dim(V)-c}
.

(3)

A map
\bar{P}
is
s
-uniform
if
|\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t})-1|\leq q^{-s}

for all
\bar{t}\in k^{c}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
,
P_{i}:V\to k
be a collection of maps such that all the maps
P_{\bar{a}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{c}a_{i}P_{i}
,
\bar{a}\in k^{c}\setminus\{0\}
are
s
-uniform. Then the map
\bar{P}
is
(s-c)
-uniform.

Proof.

Let
A
be a finite commutative group written additively and
\Xi
the group of characters

\chi:A\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
of
A
. For a function
f:A\to{\mathbb{C}}
we define the Fourier transform
\hat{f}(\chi):\Xi\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\hat{f}(\chi)=|A|^{-1}\sum_{a\in A}\chi(a)f(a)
. The next statement is the formula for the inverse Fourier transform.

Fact 2.8
.

\sum_{\chi\in\Xi}\hat{f}(\chi)\chi(-a_{0})=f(a_{0})
for any
a_{0}\in A
.

Let
A=k^{c}
.
We fix a non-trivial additive character
\psi:k\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
and associate
with any
\bar{a}\in k^{c}
a character
\chi_{\bar{a}}:k^{c}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
by

\chi_{\bar{a}}(\bar{t})=\psi(\langle\bar{a},\bar{t}\rangle)
where
\langle,\rangle:k^{c}\times k^{c}\to k
is the natural pairing. The map
\bar{a}\to\chi_{\bar{a}}
is a group isomorphism between
A
and the group
\Xi
of characters of
A
.

The Fourier transform of the function
\nu
is given by

\hat{\nu}_{\bar{P}}(\chi_{\bar{a}})=q^{-c}\sum_{\bar{t}\in k^{c}}\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t})\chi(\bar{t})=q^{-\dim(V)}\sum_{v\in V}\psi(P_{\bar{a}}(v))

Since, by the assumptions of the Lemma, for any
\bar{a}\neq 0
the map
P_{\bar{a}}
is
s
-uniform
we see that
|\hat{\nu}_{\bar{P}}(\chi_{\bar{a}})|\leq q^{-s}
for
\bar{a}\neq 0
.

As follows from Claim
2.8
we have

\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t})=\sum_{\bar{a}\in k^{c}}\psi_{\bar{a}}(-\bar{t})\hat{\nu}_{\bar{P}}(\bar{a})=1+\sum_{\bar{a}\in k^{c}\setminus\{0\}}\psi_{\bar{a}}(-\bar{t})\hat{\nu}_{\bar{P}}(\bar{a})

for all
\bar{t}\in k^{c}
. So
|\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t})-1|\leq q^{-(s-c)}
.
∎

The following result is from Algebraic Combinatorics (see
[
3
]
,
[
19
]
).

Fact 2.9
.

There exist a function
\alpha_{d}(s)
,
\lim_{s\to\infty}\alpha_{d}(s)=\infty
, such that all polynomials
P:V\to k
of degree
d
and
r_{nc}(P)\geq\alpha_{d}(s)
are

s
-uniform.

Remark 2.10
.

(1)

Fact
2.9
is the corner stone of our work.

(2)

Note that
\alpha_{d}(s)
is independent of the finite field
k
and of the (dimension of the)
k
-vector space
V
.

(3)

A stronger form of this result showing the existence of explicitly definable functions
e(d),\gamma(d)
such that one can take
\alpha_{d}(s)=e(d)s^{\gamma(d)}
is proven in
[
19
]
and
[
8
]
. This improvement has a number of important applications in Algebraic Combinatorics. One can ask whether it is possible to replace
\gamma(d)
by
1
.

Definition 2.11
.

For
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{c})
we write

r_{\bar{d}}:=\alpha_{d}(c+2)
.

The following result, to which we often refer, follows immediately from Lemma
2.7
and Claim
2.9
.

Theorem 2.12

(Uniform)
.

Let
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
be a collection of polynomials of degrees
\leq d
and of
nc
-rank
r\geq r_{\bar{d}}
. Then

|\nu_{\bar{P}}(\bar{t})-q^{-c}|\leq q^{-(c+2)}
for all
\bar{t}\in k^{c}
.

3.
Irreducibility of fibers

In this section we show a derivation of the following result from Theorem
2.12
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(1)

For any field
K
and a collection
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
, of polynomials
P_{i}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of degrees
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{c})
and nc-rank over the algebraic closure of
K
is greater then
r_{\bar{d}}
, all the fibers
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}):=\bar{\mathbf{P}}^{-1}(\bar{t})\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{n}
,
\bar{t}\in K^{c}
are non-empty reduced, geometrically irreducible complete intersections.

(2)

The schemes
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
are reduced.

(3)

The schemes
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
are normal.

(4)

If
K={\mathbb{R}}
and all degrees
d_{i},1\leq i\leq c
are odd, then
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})({\mathbb{R}})\neq\emptyset,\bar{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{c}
.

Remark 3.2
.

For fields of characteristic zero Theorem
3.1
follows from
[
20
]
. We include proofs to present our technique in details in the simplest case.

Proof.

We first prove part
(1)
.
As is well known (see Krull’s principal theorem,
[
17
]
) any irreducible component
{\mathbf{Y}}
of

{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
is of dimension
\geq(n-c)
. So it is sufficient to show that the varieties
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
are irreducible and of dimension
\leq(n-c)
.

We first consider the case when
K
is a finite field when we can use the following result (see
[
15
]
).

Let
k:=\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
k_{l}:=\mathbb{F}_{q^{l}}
. Let
{\mathbf{X}}
be an
m
-dimensional algebraic variety defined over
k
and
c({\mathbf{X}})
be the number of irreducible components of
{\mathbf{X}}
of dimension
m
(considered as a variety over the algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
). We define
\tau_{l}({\mathbf{X}}):=\frac{|{\mathbf{X}}(k_{l})|}{q^{ml}}
, for
l\geq 1
.

Claim 3.3
.

There exists
u\geq 1
such that
\lim_{l\to\infty}\tau_{lu}({\mathbf{X}})=c({\mathbf{X}})
.

For a proof of Theorem
3.1
in the case when
K={\mathbf{F}}_{q}
it is sufficient to observe that Claim
3.3
and Theorem
2.12
imply that
\dim({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}))=n-c
and
c({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}))=1
.

Now we consider the case when
K
is an algebraic closure of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.
Since
K=\bigcup{\mathbf{F}}_{q^{n}}
we may assume that
\bar{t}\in{\mathbf{F}}_{q^{n}}^{c}
. So
\dim({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}))=n-c
and
c({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}))=1
. Therefore
Theorem
3.1
is proven in the case when
K
is an algebraic closure of a finite field.

We start the reduction of the general case of Theorem
3.1
to the case when
K
is an algebraic closure of a finite field with a reformulation.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{c})
and
n\geq 1
.
A field
K
has property
\star(n,\bar{d})
if for any collection
\bar{P}=(P_{i})_{i=1}^{c}
of polynomials
P_{i}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

of degrees
d_{i}
and
nc
-rank
\geq r_{\bar{d}}
, all the varieties
\mathbb{F}_{\bar{t}},\ \bar{t}\in K^{c}
are irreducible of dimension
\dim(V)-c
, where
r_{\bar{d}}
is as in Definition
2.11
.

Claim 3.5

(n)
.

All fields have the property
\star(n,\bar{d})
for any
\geq 1,\bar{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{c})
.

Proof.

It is clear that it is sufficient to prove this claim for algebraic closed fields.
Our proof of Claim
3.5
(n)
uses the following result from
Model theory (see
[
18
]
).

Fact 3.6
.

Let
T
be the theory of algebraically closed fields. Then any first order property in
T
which is true for all algebraic closures of finite fields
is true for all algebraically closed fields.

Since
\star(n,d)
is a first order property in
T
, Claim
3.5
(n)
,
n\geq 1
are proven.
∎

Since the constant
r(\bar{d})
does not depend on
n
, the validity of Claim
3.5
(n)
for all
n\geq 1
, implies the validity of the part
(1)
of
Theorem
3.1
.

Our proof of parts
(2)
and
(3)
is based on the following statement (see
[
14
]
).

Fact 3.7
.

There exists a function
\tilde{r}(r,\bar{d})
such that for any collection
\bar{P}
of degree
\bar{d}
and
nc
-rank
\geq\tilde{r}(r,\bar{d})
there exist
v_{i},w_{i}\in V,1\leq i\leq c
such that the collection

\bar{Q}=(\bar{P},P_{i}/\partial_{v_{i}},\partial P_{i}/\partial_{w_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq c}
is of
nc
-rank
\geq r
.

(2) Since (as follows from
(1)
) varieties
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
are irreducible complete intersections, to show that schemes
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
are reduced
it is sufficient
to show that they are reduced at the generic point, and to show the normality of
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
it is sufficient
to show that they are non singular in codimension
1
.

To show that a variety
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
is reduced at the generic point it is sufficient to show the existence
of vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{c}\in V
such that
\partial P_{i}/\partial_{v_{i}}(x)\not\equiv 0,x\in{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})
.

For
\bar{v}\in V^{c}
we define the collection
\bar{R}(\bar{v})
by
\bar{R}(\bar{v})=(\bar{P},\partial P_{i}/\partial_{v_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq c}
. Let
\bar{d}^{\prime}
be the degree of the collection
\bar{R}
.
As follows from Claim
3.7
in the case when
nc
-rank of
\bar{P}
is greater than
\tilde{r}(r_{\bar{d}^{\prime}},\bar{d})

we can find
\bar{v}\in V^{c}
such the
nc
-rank of
\bar{R}(\bar{v})
is greater than
r_{\bar{d}^{\prime}}
. We fix such
\bar{v}
and write
\bar{R}:=\bar{R}(\bar{v})
. We consider

\bar{R}
as a morphism
\bar{R}:{\mathbf{V}}\to{\mathbf{A}}^{c}\times{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
.

As follows from the part (1) we have

\dim(\mathbf{R}_{\bar{t},\bar{s}})=\dim(V)-2c
for all
\bar{t},\bar{s}\in{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
.

Let
{\mathbf{Z}}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
be the subvariety of
\bar{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{c})
such that
s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{c}=0
and

{\mathbf{Y}}=\bar{R}^{-1}(\bar{t}\times{\mathbf{Z}})\subset{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
. Since
\dim(\mathbf{R}_{\bar{t},\bar{s}})=\dim(V)-2c
for all
\bar{t},\bar{s}\in{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
we see that
\dim({\mathbf{Y}})=\dim(V)-c-1<\dim({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}})
. Since the scheme
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
is reduced at any
x\in{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}\setminus{\mathbf{Y}}
we see that the scheme
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
is reduced.

The proof of
(3)
is completely analogous. We choose
v_{i},w_{i}\in V
,
1\leq i\leq c
such that the rank of the collection

\bar{Q}=(\bar{P},\partial P_{i}/\partial_{v_{i}},\partial P_{i}/\partial_{w_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq c}
is greater than
\tilde{r}(r_{\bar{d}"},\bar{d})
where
\bar{d}"
be the degree of the collection
\bar{Q}
. We consider
\bar{Q}
as a morphism
\bar{Q}:{\mathbf{V}}\to{\mathbf{A}}^{c}\times{\mathbf{A}}^{c}\times{\mathbf{A}}^{c}
. As follows from
(1)
the fibers of the morphism

\bar{Q}
are of dimension
\dim(V)-3c
.

Let
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}:=\bar{Q}^{-1}(\bar{t}\times{\mathbf{Z}}\times{\mathbf{Z}})\subset{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
. Since
\dim(\mathbf{R}_{\bar{t},\bar{s}})=\dim(V)-2c

\dim({\mathbf{Y}})=\dim(V)-c-2=\dim({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}})-2
. Since the scheme
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
is smooth at any

we see that the scheme
{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}
is normal.

For the proof of part
(4)
we fix a generic subspace
{\mathbf{W}}\subset{\mathbf{V}}
of dimension

. Since

\dim({\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P}))=n-c
it follows from the Bézout theorem that

|{\mathbf{F}}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{P})\cap{\mathbf{W}}({\mathbb{C}})|=\prod_{i}d_{i}
is odd.
Therefore there exists a point

which is invariant under complex conjugation. But then

.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For any infinite field
K
,
K
-vector spaces

and a collection
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
of polylinear functions

Proof.

We fix
\bar{t}\in K^{c}
and for

{\mathbf{Y}}\subset{\mathbf{W}}
be the subvariety of

. As follows from

3.1
the closure
\bar{\mathbf{Y}}\subset{\mathbf{W}}
of

∎

4.
Universality of high rank polynomial mappings

Definition 4.1
.

A collection
\bar{P}=(P_{i})_{1\leq i\leq c}
of degree
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{c})
of polynomials on a

if for any collection
\bar{Q}=(Q_{i})_{1\leq i\leq c}

of polynomials

\phi:K^{m}\to V

for all

.

Theorem 4.2

(Universal)
.

There exists a function
R(\bar{d},m)
such that any collection of polynomials
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
of degrees
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{c})
and

nc
-rank
\geq R(\bar{d},m)
is
m
-universal, where
K
is a field
which is either finite or algebraically closed.

Proof.

To simplify notations we only outline the proof in the case when
c=1
. Let
\Phi
be the vector space of affine maps
\phi:K^{m}\to V
and
L
be the
vector space of polynomials
Q\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
of degree
\leq d
. Choose
a basis
\lambda_{j},j\in J
of the dual space to
L
. For any polynomial
P

of degree
d
on
V
we define the map
\bar{R}_{P}:\Phi\to K^{J}
by

\bar{R}_{P}(\phi)=(\lambda_{j}(\phi^{\star}(P)))_{j\in J}
. We have to show the surjectivity of the map
\bar{R}_{P}
.
This surjectivity follows immediately from the following result (see Claim 3.11 in
[
10
]
), and Fact
2.9
.

Fact 4.3
.

For any
r
there exists
h(r,d,m)
such that the nc-rank of
\bar{R}_{P}
is
\geq r
for any polynomial
P
on
V
of nc-rank
\geq h(r,d,m)
.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If
K={\mathbb{R}}
and all the degrees

,
1\leq i\leq c
in a collection
\bar{P}
are odd then under the conditions of Theorem
4.2
the collection
\bar{P}
is m-universal.

Proof.

In this case the polynomials in
\bar{R}_{\bar{P}}
are of the same degrees and Corollary
4.4
follows from the part
(2)
of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

5.
The universality for number fields

Let
K
be a number field.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
m,\bar{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{c})
there exists
r(\bar{d},m)
such that any collection
\bar{P}:V\to K^{c}
of degree
\bar{d}
and
rank
\geq r(\bar{d},m)
is
m
-universal if either
K
is totally imaginary or all degrees
d_{i}
are odd or the collection
\bar{P}
consists of polylinear functions.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be the space of affine maps from
{\mathbf{A}}^{m}
to
V
.
The condition
\phi^{\star}(\bar{P})=\bar{Q}
is equivalent to a system
\bar{G}=\bar{G}(\bar{P},\bar{Q})
of
M=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\binom{m+d_{i}}{d_{i}}
equations of degrees
\leq d=\max_{1\leq i\leq c}d_{i}
on
\phi\in\Phi
. Let
{\mathbf{X}}\subset\Phi
be the subvariety defined by the system
\bar{G}
and
{\mathbf{X}}_{\operatorname{sing}}\subset{\mathbf{X}}
be the singular locus.

Let
D=d^{M}
,
E=(D-1)D^{(2^{D})}+1
. As follows from Claim 3.10 in
[
10
]
there exist
\tilde{r}
such
the inequality
r_{{\mathbb{C}}}(P)\geq r
implies
r_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\bar{G})\geq D!(E+M)
. By Proposition III
C
of
[
20
]
we know that
\operatorname{codim}_{{\mathbf{X}}}({\mathbf{X}}_{\operatorname{sing}})>E+M
. Since the field
K
is infinite
there exists a
K
- subspace
{\mathbf{W}}\subset\Phi
of codimension
E+M+1
such that the intersection
{\mathbf{Z}}={\mathbf{X}}\cap{\mathbf{W}}
is non-singular and geometrically irreducible. Since
\dim({\mathbf{W}})\geq M+E
and
\operatorname{codim}_{\Phi}({\mathbf{X}})\leq M
we have
\dim({\mathbf{Z}})\geq E
. Since
\deg({\mathbf{Z}})=\deg({\mathbf{X}})\leq d^{M}

we see that
{\mathbf{Z}}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem 4.2 in
[
22
]
. Now Theorem
5.1
follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
5
]
, part (2) of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.8
.
∎

6.
Weakly polynomial functions

We start the next topic with a couple of definitions.

Definition 6.1
.

(1)

Let
K
be a field,
V
be a
K
-vector space and
X
a subset of
V
.
A function
f:X\to K
is
weakly polynomial
of degree
\leq a
, if for any affine subspace
L\subset X

the restriction of
f
on
L
is a polynomial of degree
\leq a
.

(2)

A subset
X\subset V

satisfies the condition
\star_{a}

if any weakly polynomial function of degree
\leq a
on
X
is a restriction of a polynomial function of degree
\leq a
on
V
.

The following example demonstrates the existence of cubic surfaces
X\subset K^{2}
which do not have the property

\star_{1}
for any field
K\neq\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
V=K^{2}
,
Q=xy(x-y)
, and let
X
be the variety defined by
Q
. Then
X=X_{0}\cup X_{1}\cup X_{2}
where

X_{0}=\{v\in V|x=0\},X_{1}=\{v\in V|y=0\},X_{2}=\{v\in V|x=y\}
.
The function
f:X\to K
such that
f(x,0)=f(0,y)=0,f(x,x)=x
is weakly linear but one can not extend it to a linear function on
V
.

Definition 6.3
.

A field
K
is
e-admissible
for
e\geq 1
if
K^{\star}
contains the subgroup
\mu_{e}
of roots of order
e
.

Remark 6.4
.

To simplify notations we define
C:=\mu_{e}
.

Theorem 6.5

(Extension)
.

There exists an
S=S(a,d)
such that any hypersurface
Y\subset V
of degree
d
and
nc
-rank
\geq S
satisfies the condition
\star_{a}
if
K
is an
ad
-admissible field which is either finite field or is algebraically closed or is
a number field
.

Remark 6.6
.

(1)

The main difficulty in a proof of Theorem
6.5
is the non-uniqueness of an extension of
f
to a polynomial on
V
in the case when
a>d
.

(2)

An analogous statement is true for weakly polynomial functions on subsets
X_{\bar{P}}
where
\bar{P}
is a collection of a sufficiently high
nc
-rank.

Proof.

We fix the degree
d
of
P
.
The proof consists of two steps. We first construct a collection
{\mathbf{X}}_{m}\subset{\mathbf{V}}_{m}
of hypersurfaces of degree
d
and nc-rank
\geq m
defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that for all
ad
-admissible fields the subsets

\star_{a}
.
In the second step we derive the general case of Theorem
6.5

from this special case.

6.1.
The first step

We denote by
x^{i},1\leq i\leq d
the coordinate functions on
\mathbb{A}^{d}
.

Definition 6.7
.

(1)

{\mathbf{W}}:={\mathbf{A}}^{d}
and
\mu:{\mathbf{W}}\to{\mathbf{A}}
is the product
\mu(x^{1},\dots,x^{d}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}x^{j}
.

(2)

{\mathbf{V}}_{m}:={\mathbf{W}}^{m}
and
Q_{m}(w_{1},\dots,w_{m}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(w_{i})
,

(3)

{\mathbf{X}}_{m}={\mathbf{X}}_{Q_{m}}
.

Proposition 6.8
.

(1)

r_{nc}(Q_{m})\geq m
.

(2)

For any
ad
-admissible field
K
the subvariety
{\mathbf{X}}_{m}(K)\subset{\mathbf{V}}_{m}(K)
has the property
\star_{a}
.

Proof.

The proof of the inequality

is easy. In the case when

the result follows immediately from Lemma 16.1 of
[
20
]
.

To outline a proof of the second statement we introduce a number of definitions. We fix

and write
X
instead of

. Since our field

is
ad
-admissible the group
K^{\star}
contains a finite subgroup

Definition 6.9
.

(1)

W_{1}:=\{(x^{1},\dots,x^{d})\in W|x^{i}=1,i\geq 2\}

(2)

L:=W_{1}^{m}\cap X

(3)

(4)

(5)

We write elements of

(6)

(7)

\mathcal{P}_{a}(X)\subset\mathcal{P}_{a}^{w}(X)

\leq a
.

(8)

For

6.2.
The second step

The proof of the general case of Theorem

is based on the following result.

.

There exists a function

such that
the following holds. Let

be a field which is either finite or

a

-vector space,

-rank of the restriction of

on

is
\geq r(d,a)

\leq a

.

After subtracting a polynomial from

The function

We start with a construction of an extension of

to a function

on

. Given a point

is weakly polynomial, the restriction

is a polynomial

vanishing at

. We define

as the value of

at
0
.
It is clear that the following two results (see Proposition 4.33 of
[

]
) imply the validity of

.

Claim 6.11
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
6.10
,
there exist

such that

(1)

|\mathcal{L}^{-}|/|\mathcal{L}|<1/q
if

and

(2)

We extend

.

Claim 6.12
.

The function

∎

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem

.

be as Theorem

or in Theorem

. Indeed, let

be a weakly polynomial function on

∎

7.
Nullstellensatz

Let
K
be a field and
V
be a finite dimensional
K
-vector space. We denote by
{\mathbf{V}}
the
corresponding
K
-scheme, and by
\mathcal{P}(V)
the algebra of polynomial functions on
{\mathbf{V}}
defined over
K
.

For a finite collection
\bar{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{c})
of polynomials on
{\mathbf{V}}
we denote by
J(\bar{P})
the
ideal in
\mathcal{P}(V)
generated by these polynomials, and by
{\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}
the subscheme of
{\mathbf{V}}
defined by
this ideal.

Given a polynomial
R\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbf{V}})
, we would like to find out whether it belongs to the ideal

J(\bar{P})
. It is clear that the following condition is necessary for the inclusion
R\in J(\bar{P})
.

(N)
R(x)=0
for all
K
-points
x\in{\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}(K)
.

Claim 7.1

(Nullstellensatz)
.

Suppose that the field
K
is algebraically closed and that the ideal
J(\bar{P})
is radical.
Then any polynomial
R
satisfying the condition
(N)
lies
in
J(\bar{P})
.

We will show that the analogous result hold for
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
if
{\mathbf{X}}_{{\mathbf{P}}}
is of high
nc
-rank.

From now on we fix a degree vector
\bar{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{c})
and write

D:=\prod_{i=1}^{c}d_{i}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\bar{d}}({\mathbf{V}})
the space of
\bar{d}
-families of polynomials
{\mathbf{P}}=(P_{i})_{i=1}^{c}
on
V
such that
\operatorname{deg}(P_{i})\leq d_{i}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

There exists and an bound
r(\bar{d})>0
such that for any finite field
k=\mathbb{F}_{q},a<q/D
and any collection
\bar{P}
of degrees
\bar{d}
and nc-rank
\geq r(\bar{d})
the following holds. Any polynomial

Q
on
V
of degree
a
vanishing on
{\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}(k)
belongs to the ideal

J({\mathbf{P}})
.

Proof.

Our proof is based on the following
rough bound
(see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\bar{P}=(P_{i})_{i=1}^{c}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a collection of polynomials of degrees
d_{i},1\leq i\leq c
such that the variety

{\mathbf{Y}}:={\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{n}
is of dimension
n-c
. Then

|{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\leq q^{n-c}D
where
D:=\prod_{i=1}^{c}d_{i}
.

For the convenience we reproduce the proof of this result.

Proof.

Let
F
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the intersection of
{\mathbf{Y}}(F)
with hypersurfaces
{\mathbf{Y}}_{j},1\leq j\leq n
defined by the equations
h_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
where

h_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{j}^{q}-x_{j}
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n-c}
be generic linear combinations of the
h_{j}
with algebraically independent
coefficients from an transcendental extension
F^{\prime}
of
F
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-c}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{n}
be the corresponding hypersurfaces.

Intersect successively
{\mathbf{Y}}
with
{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{2},\dots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-c}
. Inductively we see that for each
j\leq n-c
, each component
C
of the intersection

{\mathbf{Y}}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{j}
has dimension
n-c-j
. Indeed, passing from
j
to
j+1
for
j<n-c
we have
\dim(C)=n-c-j>0
. So not all the functions
h_{i}
vanish on
C
. Hence by the genericity of the choice of linear combinations
\{H_{j}\}
we see that

H_{j+1}
does not vanish on
C
and therefore
{\mathbf{Z}}_{j+1}\cap C
is
of pure dimension
n-c-j-1
. Thus the intersection

{\mathbf{Y}}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-c}
has dimension
0
. This concludes the induction step.

By Bézout’s theorem we see that
|{\mathbf{Y}}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-c}|\leq q^{n-c}D
. Since
{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})={\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cap{\mathbf{Y}}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Y}}_{n-c}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we see that

|{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\leq q^{n-c}D
.
∎

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem
7.2
. Let
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial of degree
a
vanishing on the set
{\mathbf{X}}_{R}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Suppose that
R
does not lie in the ideal generated by the
P_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq c
. Let
{\mathbf{Y}}:={\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}
. Since
R
vanishes on

{\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we have
{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})={\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Since
{\mathbf{X}}_{\bar{P}}
is irreducible we have
\dim({\mathbf{Y}})=n-c-1
. As follows from Lemma
7.3
we have the upper bound

|{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\leq aDq^{n-c-1}
. On the other hand as follows from Theorem
2.12
there exists an bound
r(\bar{d})>0
such that the condition
r_{nc}(\bar{P})\geq r(\bar{d})
implies the inequality

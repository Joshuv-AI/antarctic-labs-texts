Quadratic rates of asymptotic regularity for the Tikhonov-Mann iteration
Horaţiu Cheval, Laurenţiu Leuştean, [2mm] Research Center for Logic, Optimization, Security (LOS), Department of Computer Science Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, University of Bucharest.Academiei 14, 010014 Bucharest, Romania, [1mm] Simion Stoilow Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian AcademyCalea Griviţei 21, 010702 Bucharest, Romania[2mm] E-mails: andrei.cheval@s.unibuc.ro, laurentiu.leustean@unibuc.ro

Abstract
In this paper, we compute quadratic rates of asymptotic regularity for the Tikhonov-Mann iteration in W W -hyperbolic spaces. This iteration is an extension to a nonlinear setting of the modified Mann iteration defined recently by Boţ, Csetnek and Meier in Hilbert spaces. Furthermore, we show that the Douglas-Rachford and forward–backward algorithms with Tikhonov regularization terms are special cases, in Hilbert spaces, of our Tikhonov-Mann iteration. Keywords: Mann iteration; Tikhonov regularization; Rates of asymptotic regularity; Douglas-Rachford algorithm; forward–backward algorithm; Proof mining. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 47J25, 47H09, 03F10.

Introduction

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
T:H\to H
be a nonexpansive mapping
(that is, a mapping satisfying
\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in H
). We denote by
Fix(T)
the set of
fixed points of
T
.

One of the well-known nonlinear iterations is the
Mann iteration

[
26
,
14
]
, defined as follows:

x_{n+1}=(1-\lambda_{n})x_{n}+\lambda_{n}Tx_{n},

(1)

where
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in
[0,1]
and
x_{0}\in H
is the starting point of the iteration.
A classical result on the asymptotic behaviour of this iteration, proved by Reich
[
28
]
in a more general
setting than Hilbert spaces,
is the following: if
Fix(T)\neq\emptyset
and
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(1-\lambda_{n})=\infty
, then
(x_{n})

converges weakly to a fixed point of
T
.

By using the so-called
Tikhonov regularization terms
, studied in relation with the
proximal point algorithm
[
3
,
21
]
,
Boţ, Csetnek and Meier
[
7
]
introduced recently the following modified Mann iteration:

x_{n+1}=(1-\lambda_{n})\beta_{n}x_{n}+\lambda_{n}T(\beta_{n}x_{n}),

(2)

where
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},(\beta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are sequences in
[0,1]
and
x_{0}\in H
.

The main result of
[
7
]
is the strong convergence of the iteration
(x_{n})
under
some hypotheses
on the sequences
(\lambda_{n}),(\beta_{n})
. The first
main step in the strong convergence proof is to obtain asymptotic regularity,
a very important concept in nonlinear analysis and convex optimization, defined for the first time by Browder
and Petryshyn
[
9
]
for the
Picard iteration and extended by Borwein, Reich and Shafrir
[
6
]
to the Mann iteration. Thus, a sequence
(a_{n})
in a metric space
(X,d)
is said to be

asymptotically regular
if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},a_{n+1})=0

and
T
-asymptotically regular
if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(a_{n},Ta_{n})=0
.

Let us consider in the sequel the following conditions on the sequences
(\lambda_{n})
and

(\beta_{n})
:

\displaystyle(C1)\quad

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\beta_{n})=\infty,

\displaystyle(C2)\quad

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}=0,

\displaystyle(C3)\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|<\infty,

\displaystyle(C4)\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|<\infty,

\displaystyle(C5)\quad

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=1,

\displaystyle(C6)\quad

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}>0.

The following result is implicit in
[
7
]
, its proof being contained in the proof of

[
7
, Theorem 3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
7
]

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
T:H\to H
be a nonexpansive mapping such that

Fix(T)\neq\emptyset
and
(x_{n})
be given by (
2
).
Assume that the following hold:

(i)

(C1) (or, equivalently, (C2) if
\beta_{n}>0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
);

(ii)

(C3), (C4), (C5) and (C6).

Then

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-Tx_{n}\|=0.

A quantitative analysis of the proof of Theorem
1.1
was obtained recently by Dinis and
Pinto (see
[
13
, Lemma 5]
).

In this paper we generalize this quantitative analysis in a two-fold way:

(i)

we consider
W
-hyperbolic spaces
[
15
]
instead of Hilbert spaces;

(ii)

we extend the modified Mann iteration to the so-called
Tikhonov-Mann iteration

(x_{n})
(see (
3
)).

Our main results (Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
)
provide uniform rates of asymptotic regularity for the Tikhonov-Mann iteration
(x_{n})
, that is rates of convergence towards
0
of the
sequences
(d(x_{n},x_{n+1}))
,
(d(x_{n},Tx_{n}))
. As an immediate consequence, we obtain quadratic
rates of asymptotic regularity for the Tikhonov-Mann iteration in
W
-hyperbolic spaces.

We obtain these quantitative results by applying methods from
proof mining
, an
applied area of mathematical logic, developed by Kohlenbach beginning with the 1990s (see
[
16
]
for
a standard reference or
[
18
]
for a recent survey).

2
W
-hyperbolic spaces

Let us define a
W-space
to be a structure of the form
(X,d,W)
, where

(X,d)
is a metric space and
W:X\times X\times[0,1]\to X
is a function. The
mapping
W
was already considered by Takahashi
[
29
]
in the 1970s. We also say that

W
is a
convexity mapping
, as
W(x,y,\lambda)
could be regarded as an abstract convex
combination of the points
x
and
y
with parameter
\lambda
. That is why we use in the sequel

the notation

(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y
for
W(x,y,\lambda)
.

A very important class of
W
-spaces are the
W
-hyperbolic spaces, introduced by
Kohlenbach
[
15
]
under the name of “hyperbolic spaces”. A
W
-hyperbolic space
is a
W
-space
(X,d,W)
satisfying the following
for all
x,y,w,z\in X
and all
\lambda,~\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{(W1)}&d(z,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\leq(1-\lambda)d(z,x)+\lambda d(z,y),\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W2)}&d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y,(1-\tilde{\lambda})x+\tilde{\lambda}y)=|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(x,y),\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W3)}&(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x,\\[2.84526pt]
\text{(W4)}&d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\lambda)y+\lambda w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w).\end{array}

A normed space is a
W
-hyperbolic space: one defines simply

W(x,y,\lambda)=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y
. Furthermore, important classes of geodesic spaces
such as Busemann spaces
[
27
]
or
CAT(0) spaces
[
1
,
8
]
are
W
-hyperbolic.
In fact, Busemann spaces are exactly the uniquely geodesic

W
-hyperbolic spaces (see
[
2
, Proposition 2.6]
) and, as pointed out in

[
16
, p. 386-388]
, CAT(0) spaces are the
W
-hyperbolic spaces
(X,d,W)
satisfying
the following reformulation of
the Bruhat-Tits inequality: for all
x,y,z\in X
,

d^{2}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2}y\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(z,x)+\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(z,y)-\frac{1}{4}d^{2}(x,y).

Let
(X,d,W)
be a
W
-hyperbolic space.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following hold for all
x,y,z,w\in X
and
all
\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}\in[0,1]
:

(i)

d(x,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=\lambda d(x,y)
and
d(y,(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)=(1-\lambda)d(x,y)
.

(ii)

1x+0y=x
and
0x+1y=y
.

(iii)

(1-\lambda)x+\lambda x=x
.

(iv)

d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\tilde{\lambda})y+\tilde{\lambda}w)\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)+|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(y,w)
.

(v)

d((1-\lambda)x+\lambda z,(1-\tilde{\lambda})x+\tilde{\lambda}w)\leq\lambda d(z,w)+|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(x,w)
.

Proof.

(
i
) holds already in the setting of convex metric spaces, defined by Takahashi

[
29
]
as
W
-spaces satisfying
(W1)
.

(
ii
), (
iii
) follow immediately from (
i
).

(
iv
) Let
u=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda z
and
v=(1-\tilde{\lambda})y+\tilde{\lambda}w
. We have that

\displaystyle d(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq d(u,(1-\lambda)y+\lambda w)+d((1-\lambda)y+\lambda w,v)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)+d((1-\lambda)y+\lambda w,v)\,\,\text{by (W4)}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)d(x,y)+\lambda d(z,w)+|\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}|d(y,w)\quad\text{by (W2)}.

(
v
) is obtained by letting
y=x
in (
iv
).
∎

For all
x,y\in X
, let us denote

[x,y]=\{(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\mid\lambda\in[0,1]\}.

By Lemma
2.1
.(
iii
),
[x,x]=\{x\}
.
It is easy to see that

(X,d,W)
is a geodesic space and, for all
x,y\in X
,
[x,y]
is a geodesic segment joining

x
and
y
.

A nonempty subset
C\subseteq X
is said to be
convex
if

[x,y]\subseteq C
for all
x,y\in C
. Any convex subset
C
of
X
is, in an obvious way, a
W
-hyperbolic space.

3
The Tikhonov-Mann iteration

In the sequel,
(X,d,W)
is a
W
-hyperbolic space,
C
is a convex subset of
X

and
T:C\to C
is a nonexpansive mapping. Let us denote by
Fix(T)
the set of fixed points of
T
. We assume that
T
has fixed points, i.e.

Fix(T)\neq\emptyset
.

We define the
Tikhonov-Mann iteration
starting with
x_{0}\in C
as follows:

x_{n+1}=(1-\lambda_{n})u_{n}+\lambda_{n}Tu_{n},

(3)

where

u_{n}=(1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}x_{n},

(4)

with
u\in C
and
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},(\beta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
sequences in
[0,1]
.

Remark that if
X
is a normed space,
C=X
and
u=0
, then
u_{n}=\beta_{n}x_{n}
, hence
(
3
) becomes
(
2
). Therefore, our iteration
(x_{n})
is a generalization to
the nonlinear setting of
W
-hyperbolic spaces of the modified Mann iteration introduced
in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be a fixed point of
T
. Define

M=\max\{d(x_{0},p),d(u,p)\}.

(5)

The following hold for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

(i)

d(x_{n+1},p)\leq(1-\beta_{n})d(u,p)+\beta_{n}d(x_{n},p)
.

(ii)

d(x_{n},p)\leq M
and
d(x_{n},u)\leq 2M
.

(iii)

d(u_{n},p)\leq M
and
d(u_{n},Tu_{n})\leq 2M
.

Proof.

(i)

Applying (W1) twice and the fact that
d(Tu_{n},p)=d(Tu_{n},Tp)\leq d(u_{n},p)
, we get that

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},p)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{n})d(u_{n},p)+\lambda_{n}d(Tu_{n},p)\leq d(u_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta_{n})d(u,p)+\beta_{n}d(x_{n},p).

(ii)

Use (
i
) and induction on
n
to get that
d(x_{n},p)\leq M
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Obviously,

d(x_{n},u)\leq d(x_{n},p)+d(u,p)\leq 2M
.

(iii)

By (W1) and (
ii
), we have that

d(u_{n},p)\leq(1-\beta_{n})d(u,p)+\beta_{n}d(x_{n},p)\leq M.

Furthermore,

d(u_{n},Tu_{n})\leq d(u_{n},p)+d(p,Tu_{n})\leq 2d(u_{n},p)\leq 2M
.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
p
be a fixed point of
T
and
M
be defined by (
5
). For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle d(u_{n+1},u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+2M|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|,

(6)

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+2M\left(|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|+|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|\right),

(7)

\displaystyle d(x_{n},u_{n})

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n}),

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})&\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})\\
&+(1-\lambda_{n})\beta_{n}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(u,Tx_{n}),\end{split}

(9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+2M(1-\beta_{n}).

(10)

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle d(u_{n+1},u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|d(u,x_{n})\quad\text{by Lemma~\ref{W-hyperbolic-spaces-basic}.\eqref{W42-xzw-ineq1}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+2M|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|\quad\text{by Lemma~\ref{xn-yn-bounded}.\eqref{xn-bounded}}.

Thus, (
6
) holds.

We prove (
7
) as follows:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda_{n+1})d(u_{n+1},u_{n})+\lambda_{n+1}d(Tu_{n+1},Tu_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|d(u_{n},Tu_{n})\qquad\text{by Lemma~\ref{W-hyperbolic-spaces-basic}.\eqref{W42-xyzw-ineq1}}

\displaystyle\leq d(u_{n+1},u_{n})+|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|d(u_{n},Tu_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n+1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})+2M\left(|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|+|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|\right)

\displaystyle\quad\text{by \eqref{dyn-consecutive} and Lemma~\ref{xn-yn-bounded}.\eqref{yn-bounded}}.

(
8
) follows immediately from (
4
) and
Proposition
2.1
.(
i
).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+1},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+(1-\lambda_{n})d(u_{n},Tx_{n})+\lambda_{n}d(u_{n},x_{n})

\displaystyle\quad\text{by \eqref{def-TKM-W}, (W1) and the nonexpansiveness of~}T

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}d(u_{n},x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})d(u_{n},(1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda_{n})d((1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}Tx_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle=d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}d(u_{n},x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})d(u_{n},(1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(u,Tx_{n})\quad\text{by Proposition~\ref{W-hyperbolic-spaces-basic}.\eqref{W10-W-hyp}}

\displaystyle=d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(u,Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda_{n})d((1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}x_{n},(1-\beta_{n})u+\beta_{n}Tx_{n})

by (
8
) and (
4
)

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(u,Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda_{n})\beta_{n}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})\quad\text{by (W4)}.

Therefore, (
9
) is satisfied.

Finally, let us prove (
10
). Applying (
9
) and the fact that

d(u,Tx_{n})\leq d(u,x_{n})+d(x_{n},Tx_{n})
we get that

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+\lambda_{n}(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})\beta_{n}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})(1-\beta_{n})d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle=d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})+(1-\lambda_{n})d(x_{n},Tx_{n}).

Move
(1-\lambda_{n})d(x_{n},Tx_{n})
to the left-hand side and apply Proposition
3.1
.(
ii
)
to obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+(1-\beta_{n})d(u,x_{n})\leq d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+2M(1-\beta_{n}).

∎

4
Main theorems

The main results of the paper are effective versions of a generalization of Theorem
1.1
,
providing uniform rates of asymptotic regularity, in the setting of
W
-hyperbolic spaces, for
the Tikhonov-Mann iteration
(x_{n})
defined by (
3
).

Before giving the main theorems, we recall some quantitative notions. Let
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a
sequence in a metric space
(X,d)
,
a\in X
and
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
.
If
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=a
, then
\varphi
is a
rate of convergence
for
(a_{n})
(towards
a
) if

\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall n\geq\varphi(k)\left(d(a_{n},a)\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

If
(a_{n})
is Cauchy, then
\varphi
is a
Cauchy modulus
for
(a_{n})
if

\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall n\geq\varphi(k)\,\forall p\in{\mathbb{N}}\left(d(a_{n+p},a_{n})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\right).

Let
(b_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers. If the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}

diverges, then a
rate of divergence
of the series is a function
\theta:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}b_{i}\geq n
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. A Cauchy modulus of a convergent series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}

is a Cauchy modulus of the sequence
\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and a rate of convergence
of a convergent product
\prod\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
is a rate of convergence of the sequence

\left(\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

We consider in the following quantitative versions of (C1)-(C6):

(C1_{q})

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\beta_{n})
diverges with rate of divergence
\sigma_{1}
;

(C2_{q})

\prod\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n+1}=0
with rate of convergence
\sigma_{2}
;

(C3_{q})

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}|
converges with Cauchy modulus
\sigma_{3}
;

(C4_{q})

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|
converges with Cauchy modulus
\sigma_{4}
;

(C5_{q})

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=1
with rate of convergence
\sigma_{5}
;

(C6_{q})

\Lambda\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and
N_{\Lambda}\in{\mathbb{N}}
are such that
\lambda_{n}\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda}

for all
n\geq N_{\Lambda}
.

(X,d,W)
is a

6
Tikhonov versions of the Douglas-Rachford and forward-backward algorithms

Boţ, Csetnek and Meier
[
7
]
derived from their modified Mann iteration a forward–backward and a Douglas–Rachford algorithm, both endowed with Tikhonov regularization terms. In this section, we define more general versions of these two algorithms and we show that both of them are instances, in the setting of Hilbert spaces, of the Tikhonov-Mann iteration (
3
). Furthermore, we apply our main results from Section
4
to obtain uniform effective rates of asymptotic regularity.

In the sequel,
H
is a Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and associated norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. Let us recall some fundamental notions and results from convex optimization and monotone operator theory that will be used in this section. We refer to
[
5
]
for details.

Let
A:H\rightrightarrows H
be an arbitrary set-valued operator, characterized by its graph,
\text{gra}A=\{(x,u)\in H\times H\mid u\in Ax\}
.
The inverse
A^{-1}:H\rightrightarrows H
of
A
is given by
\text{gra}A^{-1}=\{(u,x)\in H\times H\mid(x,u)\in\text{gra}A\}
. The set of zeros of
A
is
\text{zer}A=\{x\in H\mid 0\in Ax\}
.
If
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
B:H\rightrightarrows H
is another operator, then
\gamma A:H\rightrightarrows H
and
A+B:H\rightrightarrows H
are defined as follows:
(\gamma A)(x)=\{\gamma u\mid u\in Ax\}
and
(A+B)(x)=\{u+v\mid u\in Ax,v\in Bx\}
.

The resolvent
J_{A}:H\rightrightarrows H
and the reflected resolvent
R_{A}:H\rightrightarrows H
of
A
are given by

J_{A}=(\text{Id}+A)^{-1},\quad R_{A}=2J_{A}-\text{Id},

where
\text{Id}:H\to H,\,\text{Id}(x)=x
is the identity mapping.

We say that
A
is monotone if
\left\langle x-y,u-v\right\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in H
,
u\in Ax
and
v\in Ay
, and that
A
is maximally monotone if it is monotone
and its graph is not properly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator. It is well-known that if
A
is maximally monotone, then
J_{A},R_{A}:H\to H
are single-valued mappings.

Let
T:H\to H
be a mapping. We say that
T
is
\beta
-cocoercive
(for some
\beta>0
) if
\left\langle x-y,Tx-Ty\right\rangle\geq\beta\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|^{2}

for all
x,y\in H
.
Furthermore,
T
is said to be
\alpha
-averaged
(for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
) if
there exists a nonexpansive mapping
U:H\to H
such that
T=(1-\alpha)\text{Id}+\alpha U
.
Obviously,
1
-averaged mappings are exactly the nonexpansive ones.

Abstract
. We study a family of self-affine tiles in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} ( d ≥ 2 d\geq\text{2} ) with noncollinear digit sets, which naturally generalizes a class studied originally by Deng and Lau in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{\text{2}} and its extension to ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{\text{3}} by the authors. By using Brouwer’s invariance of domain theorem, along with a tool which we call horizontal distance, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for the tiles to be d d -dimensional tame balls. This answers positively the conjecture in an earlier paper by the authors stating that a member in a certain class of self-affine tiles is homeomorphic to a d d -dimensional ball if and only if its interior is connected.

1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
A
be a
d\times d
expanding matrix (i.e., all of its eigenvalues are greater than 1 in modulus). It is well known that (see, e.g.,
[
Hutchinson
,
Lagarias-Wang_1996
]
) for any finite set
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a unique nonempty compact set
T=T(A,{\mathcal{D}})
such that

T=\bigcup\nolimits_{d_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}}}A^{-1}(T+d_{i}).

The above set equation can be rewritten as
AT=T+{\mathcal{D}}
and
T
can be expressed as

T=\Bigl\{\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}A^{-k}d_{i_{k}}:\,d_{i_{k}}\in{\mathcal{D}}\Bigr\}.

(1.1)

We call
{\mathcal{D}}
a
digit set
,
(A,{\mathcal{D}})
a
self-affine pair
, and
T
a
self-affine set
. If
\#{\mathcal{D}}=|\det(A)|
is an integer and the interior of
T
(denoted by
T^{\circ}
) is nonempty, the set
T
actually tiles
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the sense that there exists a discrete set
{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies (i)
T+{\mathcal{L}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and (ii)
(T^{\circ}+\iota_{1})\cap(T^{\circ}+\iota_{2})=\emptyset
for all distinct
\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Such a
T
is called a
self-affine tile
.

The theory of self-affine tiles was established by Kenyon
[
Kenyon
]
, Lagarias and Wang
[
Lagarias-Wang_1996
,
Lagarias19962
,
Lagarias1997
,
wang
]
in the 1990s. In recent years, self-affine tiles play an important role in fractal geometry, wavelet theory and other fields. A basic central topic about self-affine sets is to study their topological properties and structure, including connectedness (see, e.g.,
[
Kirat2000
,
Aki2004
,
Kirat2004
,
Leung-Lau_2007
,
he
,
Leung-Luo_2012
]
and the references therein), and ball-likeness (see, e.g.
[
Bandt-Wang_2001
,
Luo-Rao-Tan_2002
,
Leung-Lau_2007
,
Kamae
,
Conner
,
R3
,
Rn
]
and the references therein). Here a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
ball-like
if it is homeomorphic to a
d
-dimensional unit ball. If
d=2
, a ball-like set is also called a
disk-like set
. All the balls mentioned in this paper are closed.

When the integral expanding matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is given, the structure of the digit set strongly affects the topological properties of the self-affine set
T
. Hata
[
Hata
]
obtained an important tool to study the connectedness of more general self-similar sets. By using a graph structure on
{\mathcal{D}}
, Kirat and Lau
[
Kirat2000
]
developed the following criterion for the connectedness of self-affine tiles generated by collinear digit sets: If
{\mathcal{D}}=\{0,v,\ldots,|(\det A|-1)v\}
and the characteristic polynomial of
A
is height reducing, then
T=T(A,{\mathcal{D}})
is connected (see also
[
Kirat2004
]
). As it is easy to describe all quadratic polynomials by this method, Kirat and Lau proved the connectedness of a class of tiles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Akiyama and Gjini
[
Aki2004
]
extended Kirat-Lau’s conclusion to the case of
d=3,4
by using the Schur-Cohn criterion. They confirmed that all characteristic polynomials are height reducing when
d\leq 4
. For
d>4
, He
et al.

[
he
]
described an algorithm to determine whether a polynomial is height reducing. This method is not applicable if the digit set is nonconsecutive or noncollinear. A lot of research has focused on collinear digit sets or more special consecutive digit sets (see
[
he
,
Kirat2000
,
LiuJC2014
]
). One of the reasons is that this case involves canonical number systems. It is a widely studied basic case (for canonical number systems and tiles see
[
Gilbert
,
Katai
,
Kovacs
]
; for some progress on the topology of the tiles, see
[
Aki2000
,
AkiThus2005
]
). Another reason is that in most cases, the connectedness can be revealed only by consecutive digits. If, on the other hand, a “scattered” digit set is used, it is easy to find a disconnected sub-block, making it more difficult to study the topological structure of the tile.

In 2011, Deng and Lau
[
DengLau2011
]
initiated the study of self-affine tiles with noncollinear digit sets, and obtained the following result concerning connectedness.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Deng-Lau
[
DengLau2011
]
)

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|p|,|q|\geq 2
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{0,1,\dots,|p|-1\}

A\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\[-3.0pt]
-a&q\end{pmatrix},{\mathcal{D}}\!=\!\big\{(i,j+b_{i}):\,0\leq i\leq|p|-1,\ 0\leq j\leq|q|-1\big\}.

(1.2)

Then the self-affine set

is a tile, and it is connected if and only if

\Big|\frac{b_{i+1}-b_{i}}{q}+\frac{\textup{sign}\,(p)(b_{0}-b_{|p|-1})-a}{q(q-\textup{sign}\,(p))}\Big|\leq 1,\text{ for all }i,

(1.3)

where
\textup{sign}\,(x)=-1,0
or 1 according to
x<0
,
x=0
or
x>0
.

Leung and Luo
[
Leung2015
]
showed the connectedness of the self-affine set
T
in the case

are linearly independent), under the condition that

[

has been extended by Liu

to the digit set

]

In the two-dimensional case, the topological structure of tiles has been studied extensively, and disk-like tiles have been identified in many cases. Due to the existence of Alexander’s horned balls in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it is very difficult to verify whether a specific three-dimensional self-affine tile is ball-like. So, when
d\geq 3
, the topology of self-affine tiles is very complex and little is known.
In studying higher-dimensional tiles, Gelbrich
[
Gelbrich
]
first proposed a part of the following question, which appeared recently in
[
Bandtpre
,
DengJiang2012
]
.

Question 1.2
.

Can a self-affine tile
(
or its boundary
)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
become a manifold in a nontrivial way, and is there a method to identify whether a self-affine tile (or its boundary) can be a manifold?

We recall the definitions of tame ball and wild ball. Suppose a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is homeomorphic to a
d
-dimensional unit ball. If the homeomorphism can be extended to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
E
is called a
tame ball
; otherwise
E
is called a
wild ball
.
In 2015, Kamae, Luo and Tan
[
Kamae
]
used Barnsley’s theory of attractors with condensation
[

]
to construct a class of
d
-dimensional self-affine tiles
T=T(A,{\mathcal{D}})
that are tame balls; here
A:=3\textup{diag}(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

{\mathcal{D}}:=\bigg\{\bigg(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}+t\Big(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}i_{j}^{2}\Big)\bigg):\,i_{j}\in\{-1,0,1\},1\leq j\leq n\bigg\},

and
t\in(-3,3)
. In 2016, Conner and Thuswaldner
[
Conner
]
proposed an algebraic method to determine whether a given three dimensional self-affine tile is a tame ball, by using geometric topology tools developed by Cannon and Edwards. They confirmed that several known three-dimensional self-affine tiles
[
BandtMessing
,
Gelbrich
]
are ball-like. They also constructed a self-affine tile in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
that is not a topological manifold (its boundary is a two-dimensional wild sphere). Their method makes use of the representation of a class of algebraic geometric complexes with recursive structure (which allowed arbitrary precision calculations). These complexes were used to approximate self-affine tiles, and then determine the homeomorphism type of the boundary with the help of computer. In 2020, Thuswaldner and Zhang
[
Thu
]
considered the boundary problem of self-affine tiles. Under the assumption that the digit set is consecutive, they constructed a class of self-affine tiles each of whose boundary is a two-dimensional sphere.

In
[
R3
]
, authors of the present paper studied Question

and extended some results in
[

]

.

(Deng-Liu-Ngai

Let

-t&-s&r\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathcal{D}}&=&\big\{(i,j,k):\,0\leq i<|p|,\\
&&\,\,0\leq j<|q|,0\leq k<|r|\big\},\end{array}

By assuming that

, i.e.,

E|_{\pi(y)}:=\{P\in E:\,\mathcal{P}_{v}(P)=y\}.

We point out that
d_{h}
is not a metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, since the triangle inequality may fail. Nevertheless, the following limit property holds. We omit the proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{P_{n}\},\{Q_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two convergent sequences in the sense of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. If for each
n\geq 1
,
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
have the same height, then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}d_{h}(P_{n},Q_{n})=d_{h}\Big(\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{n},\lim_{n\to+\infty}Q_{n}\Big).

For
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
2{\bm{x}}^{\oplus}\geq\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}
, we divide the index set
\{j:\,|2x_{j}|\geq\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})\}
into three parts as follows.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{j:\,x_{j}u_{j}>0,\ 2|x_{j}|\geq\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{j:\,x_{j}u_{j}<0,\ 2|x_{j}|\geq\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})\},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{j:\,2|x_{j}|\geq\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})=1,\ u_{j}=0\}.

So, we can rewrite the pseudo-distances
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})
and
d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})&:=&\max\Big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}):\ j\in\Lambda_{1}\Big\};\\[8.0pt]
d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})&:=&\max\Big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}):\,j\in\Lambda_{2}\cup\Lambda_{3}\Big\}.\end{array}

(3.9)

These new expressions in fact give the geometric meanings of
d_{t}^{+}
and
d_{t}^{-}
. Roughly speaking,
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})
is the horizontal distance between
{\bm{x}}
and
\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U^{+}
if we ignore all components of
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{u}}
that have different signs, while
d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
is the horizontal distance between
{\bm{x}}
and
\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}(\partial U^{+}\cup\partial U^{\updownarrow})
if we only consider the components of
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{u}}
that have different signs. This fact will be applied to the proofs of Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
, which will be used to estimate the horizontal distances of prisms and points in the next section.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
t\in[0,1)
. Suppose
P=\Big({\bm{x}}+\dfrac{t}{2}{\bm{u}},t\varepsilon\Big)
with
2{\bm{x}}^{\oplus}\geq\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}
.

(1)
If
P
lies below
S_{\bm{u}}
, then

d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U-\dfrac{1+t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)\geq d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U).

(2)
If
P
lies above
S_{\bm{u}}
or
P\in S_{\bm{u}}
, then

d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)\geq(1-\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty})^{1/\eta}d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U).

To prove Lemma
3.4
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
0<\delta<1
and
x\geq\eta
. Then for
t\in[0,1]
, we have

x\sqrt{1-t}\geq(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}\big(x\sqrt{1-t}+(1-t)\delta\big).

Proof.

Note that
\eta\in(0,1)
. By the mean-value theorem, there exists some
\xi\in(0,\delta)
such that

\dfrac{1-(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}}{\delta}\times\dfrac{1}{(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}}=\dfrac{1}{\eta}{(1-\xi)^{1/\eta-1}}\dfrac{1}{(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}}>\dfrac{1}{\eta}.

Hence,

x\sqrt{1-t}\geq\eta\sqrt{1-t}\geq\dfrac{(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}\delta\sqrt{1-t}}{1-(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}}\geq\dfrac{(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}\delta(1-t)}{1-(1-\delta)^{1/\eta}}.

This implies the conclusion.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

When
{\bm{u}}=(0,\ldots,0)
, we know that
\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}=\textbf{1}_{d-1}
and hence

d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U-\dfrac{1+t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)=d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)=d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U),

provided
2{\bm{x}}^{\oplus}\geq\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}U
.
Thus, conclusions (1) and (2) are true.

Next, we assume
{\bm{u}}\neq(0,\ldots,0)
.

(1) Assume
P
lies below
S_{\bm{u}}
. Then
{\bm{x}}\in\textup{int}\,G_{t}
, which implies that
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})>d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
, namely,

\max_{j\in\Lambda_{1}}\Big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})\Big\}>\max_{j\in\Lambda_{2}\cup\Lambda_{3}}\Big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})\Big\}.

So, by setting
x_{j}^{\prime}=\omega_{j}({\bm{x}}^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}:\,{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\in\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\!\Big\{\!\max\big\{|x_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}|:\,j\in\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}\cup\Lambda_{3}\big\}:\,{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\in\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}U\!\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\!\big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}):\,j\in\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}\cup\Lambda_{3}\big\}\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\!\big\{|x_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}):\,j\in\Lambda_{1}\big\}=d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}}),

where
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}
are given as in (
3.8
). Recall that
\omega_{j}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})=t|u_{j}|+1-|u_{j}|
.
Without loss of generality, we assume

u_{1}>0\quad\text{and}\quad d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})=x_{1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{1}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})=x_{1}-\dfrac{tu_{1}+1-u_{1}}{2}.

(3.10)

On the other hand,

d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U-\dfrac{1+t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)\geq x_{1}-\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1+t}{2}u_{1}\Big)=x_{1}-\dfrac{-tu_{1}+1-u_{1}}{2}\geq d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}}).

So the conclusion is true in this case.

(2) Assume
P
lies on or above
S_{\bm{u}}
. When
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})>d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
, we also get
d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}U)=d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})
. Again we suppose that assumption (
3.10
) holds.
Since
P
lies on or above
S_{\bm{u}}
, we know
{\bm{x}}\notin(2\eta\sqrt{1-t}+1+(1-t){\bm{u}}^{\oplus})U^{\circ}
by the definition of
S_{\bm{u}}
, which implies that (see Figure
6
for the location of
P
)

d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})\geq\eta\sqrt{1-t}+(1-t)u_{1}.

Notice that

\displaystyle d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle x_{1}-\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1-t}{2}u_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})-(1-t)u_{1}\geq\eta\sqrt{1-t}.

Applying Lemma
3.5
for
x=(\sqrt{1-t})^{-1}d_{h}\big({\bm{x}},U+2^{-1}(1-t){\bm{u}}\big)
and
\delta=u_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle x\sqrt{1-t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-u_{1})^{1/\eta}\bigg(d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)+(1-t)u_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty})^{1/\eta}d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty})^{1/\eta}d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U).

When
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})\leq d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
, we know
d_{h}({\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U)=d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
. Without loss of generality, we assume
u_{1}\leq 0
and

d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})=x_{1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\omega_{1}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{t})=x_{1}-\dfrac{-tu_{1}+1+u_{1}}{2}.

Then

d_{h}\Big({\bm{x}},U+\dfrac{1-t}{2}{\bm{u}}\Big)\geq x_{1}-\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{(1-t)u_{1}}{2}\Big)=d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}}).

So the conclusion also holds in this case since
(1-\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty})^{1/\eta}\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

The following assertions concerning
S_{\bm{u}}
hold.

(1)

For each
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, the set
S_{\bm{u}}\cap\ell_{\bm{x}}

\mathcal{P}_{h}\big(S^{+}\cup S^{-}\cup S^{0}\big)=(U+2^{-1}{\bm{u}})\bigcup(U-2^{-1}{\bm{u}}).

(2)

The height of

is zero if

or
\mathcal{P}_{h}(P)\in(1-\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty})U

(3)

Let
P=({\bm{x}},y)\in S_{\bm{u}}
, where
{\bm{x}}\in\partial U+2^{-1}{\bm{u}}

\text{either }\omega_{j}(2{\bm{x}}-{\bm{u}})=\pm 1\quad\text{ or }\quad\big|\omega_{j}(2{\bm{x}}-{\bm{u}})\big|<1-2\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}.

(3.11)

Then

y=\begin{cases}\varepsilon,&{\bm{x}}\in\partial U^{+}+2^{-1}{\bm{u}},\\[-3.0pt]
0,&{\bm{x}}\in\big(\partial U^{-}\cup\partial U^{\updownarrow}+2^{-1}{\bm{u}}\big)\backslash\big(\partial U^{+}+2^{-1}{\bm{u}}\big).\end{cases}

(4)

Let
P=({\bm{x}},y)\in S_{\bm{u}}
, where
{\bm{x}}\in\partial U-2^{-1}{\bm{u}}
such that

\text{either }\omega_{j}(2{\bm{x}}+{\bm{u}})=\pm 1\quad\text{ or }\quad\big|\omega_{j}(2{\bm{x}}+{\bm{u}})\big|<1-2\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}.

Then

y=\begin{cases}-\varepsilon,&{\bm{x}}\in\partial U^{-}-2^{-1}{\bm{u}},\\[-3.0pt]
0,&{\bm{x}}\in\big(\partial U^{+}\cup\partial U^{\updownarrow}-2^{-1}{\bm{u}}\big)\backslash\big(\partial U^{-}-2^{-1}{\bm{u}}\big).\end{cases}

Proof.

We first consider the case
{\bm{u}}=(0,\ldots,0)
. We have
S^{+}=S^{-}=\emptyset
and
S^{0}=U\times\{0\}
. So,
S_{\bm{u}}\cap\ell_{\bm{x}}=\{({\bm{x}},0)\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{h}(S^{0})=U
, which implies conclusion (1). Conclusion (2) is obvious since each point in
S_{\bm{u}}
has height zero. Note that
\partial U^{+}+2^{-1}{\bm{u}}=\partial U^{-}-2^{-1}{\bm{u}}=\emptyset
and
\partial U^{\updownarrow}+2^{-1}{\bm{u}}=\partial U
. Now conclusions (3) and (4) become: If
P=({\bm{x}},y)\in S_{\bm{u}}
with
{\bm{x}}\in\partial U
, then
y=0
when
{\bm{x}}\in\partial U^{\updownarrow}
. This is obvious since
P=({\bm{x}},y)\in S_{\bm{u}}
implies that
y=0
. Now, we assume
{\bm{u}}\neq(0,\ldots,0)
.

(1) For a given vector
{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we denote
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}={\bm{x}}+2^{-1}(t-t^{\prime}){\bm{u}}
, where
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1]
satisfy
t>t^{\prime}
. We begin the proof with the following claim.

Claim.

(i)
If
{\bm{x}}\in\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}(U^{c}\cup\partial U^{+})
, then
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\notin\hat{\bm{u}}_{t^{\prime}}U
.
(ii)
If
{\bm{x}}\in(2\eta\sqrt{1-t}+1+(1-t){\bm{u}}^{\oplus})U

{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\in(2\eta\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}+1+(1-t^{\prime}){\bm{u}}^{\oplus})U^{\circ}
.
(iii)
If
d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}})\geq d_{t}^{-}({\bm{x}})
, then
d_{t^{\prime}}^{+}({\bm{x}}^{\prime})>d_{t^{\prime}}^{-}({\bm{x}}^{\prime})

Proof of the claim.
When
{\bm{x}}\in\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}\partial U^{+}
, there exists an index
j
such that
x_{j}u_{j}>0
and
|x_{j}|=2^{-1}(t|u_{j}|+1-|u_{j}|)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
j=1
and
u_{1}>0
. Then

x_{1}^{\prime}=x_{1}+\dfrac{t-t^{\prime}}{2}u_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}(tu_{1}+1-u_{1})+\dfrac{t-t^{\prime}}{2}u_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}(t^{\prime}u_{1}+1-u_{1})+(t-t^{\prime})u_{1},

which implies
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\notin\hat{\bm{u}}_{t^{\prime}}U

{\bm{x}}\notin\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}U

j
such that

\displaystyle|2x_{j}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|2x_{j}+(t-t^{\prime})u_{j}|\geq|2x_{j}|-(t-t^{\prime})|u_{j}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle t|u_{j}|+1-|u_{j}|-(t-t^{\prime})|u_{j}|=t^{\prime}|u_{j}|+1-|u_{j}|,

which again implies
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\notin\hat{\bm{u}}_{t^{\prime}}U
, and thus (i) holds.

From the fact that
\eta>\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle 2\eta\sqrt{1-t}+(1-t){\bm{u}}^{\oplus}+(t-t^{\prime})\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle 2\eta\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}+2\eta\dfrac{t^{\prime}-t}{\sqrt{1-t}+\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}}+(1-t){\bm{u}}^{\oplus}+(t-t^{\prime})\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\eta\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}+(1-t^{\prime}){\bm{u}}^{\oplus}+(\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty}-\eta)(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2\eta\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}+(1-t^{\prime}){\bm{u}}^{\oplus},

which implies

(2\eta\sqrt{1-t}+1+(1-t){\bm{u}}^{\oplus})U+\dfrac{t-t^{\prime}}{2}{\bm{u}}\subset(2\eta\sqrt{1-t^{\prime}}+1+(1-t^{\prime}){\bm{u}}^{\oplus})U^{\circ}.

(3.12)

Thus, (ii) holds.

As for (iii), we let
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}

). Since
{\bm{x}}\notin\hat{\bm{u}}_{t}U
, not all sets

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(x_{1}+\dfrac{t-t^{\prime}}{2}u_{1}\Big)-\dfrac{1}{2}(t^{\prime}u_{1}+1-u_{1})

\displaystyle x_{1}-\dfrac{1}{2}(tu_{1}+1-u_{1})+(t-t^{\prime})u_{1}>d_{t}^{+}({\bm{x}}).

(in this case,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x_{j}|-\dfrac{t-t^{\prime}}{2}|u_{j}|-\dfrac{1}{2}(t^{\prime}|u_{j}|+1-|u_{j}|)

From the expression of

. This implies the following relationship among the

4.
A key homeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

4.1.
Definitions of transformations

In this subsection we present four transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which will be used to construct the desired homeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Before doing this, we introduce some notation, in addition to those in Subsection 2.3. Let
b>0
be a fixed positive constant as in Subsection 2.3. We choose
\varepsilon
stated as in Subsection 2.3 small enough such that the union
\cup_{k=0}^{r}S_{k}

S_{0}=\pi(0),S_{r}=\pi(b)

S_{k}=S_{{\bm{u}}_{k}}+\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}{\bm{u}}_{k}+{\bm{v}}_{k-1},y_{k}\Big),\quad 1\leq k<r.

(4.1)

Hence

{\bm{u}}

{\bm{u}}_{k}

(4.2)

For
1\leq k<r

, and
S^{\prime-}_{k}
are defined similarly. For convenience, we set

S_{0}^{0}=U\times\{0\},\quad S_{r}^{0}=(U+{\bm{v}}_{r-1})\times\{b\},\quad\text{and}\quad S_{0}^{+}=S_{0}^{-}=S_{r}^{+}=S_{r}^{-}=\emptyset.

We use
P
to denote
({\bm{x}},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times[0,b]
, unless stated otherwise.
Let
{\bm{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
{\bm{x}}(y)

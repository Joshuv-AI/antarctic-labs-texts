Radial Projections in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} Revisited
Paige Bright, Yuqiu Fu, Kevin Ren

Abstract
. We generalize the recent results on radial projections by Orponen, Shmerkin, Wang [ 10 ] using two different methods. In particular, we show that given X , Y ⊂ ℝ n X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} Borel sets and X ≠ ∅ X\neq\emptyset . If dim Y ∈ ( k , k + 1 ] \dim Y\in(k,k+1] for some k ∈ { 1 , … , n − 1 } k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\} , then sup x ∈ X dim π x ​ ( Y ∖ { x } ) ≥ min ⁡ { dim X + dim Y − k , k } . \sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}. Our results give a new approach to solving a conjecture of Lund-Pham-Thu in all dimensions and for all ranges of dim Y \dim Y . The first of our two methods for proving the above theorem is shorter, utilizing a result of the first author and Gan [ 1 ] . Our second method, though longer, follows the original methodology of Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang, and requires a higher dimensional incidence estimate and a dual Furstenberg-set estimate for lines. These new estimates may be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the relationship between Hausdorff dimension and
radial projections

\pi_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus x\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, defined via the equation

\pi_{x}(y):=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|},\hskip 28.45274pty\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}.

In particular, we generalize recent work of Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang to higher dimensions. Here is our main result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel sets with
X\neq\emptyset
and
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

Note that the case when
k=1
and
n=2
was solved by Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang in
[
10
]
. Similarly, using an analogous method to their Corollary 1.6, we obtain the following result, which was first proved as
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}.
Then,

\dim\underbrace{(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})<s\})}_{X}\leq\max\{k+s-\dim Y,0\}

for all
0\leq s\leq k
.

Proof.

First, suppose
s<k
, and assume for the sake of contradiction that the exceptional set
X
satisfies the following inequality:

\dim X>\max\{k+s-\dim Y,0\}.

Then, note that
X\neq\emptyset
as
\dim X>0
. Hence, applying Theorem
1
, we have

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}>s.

This is a contradiction with the definition of
X
. In the case
s=k
simplifies down to noticing
X
may be written as the following countable union

X=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})<k-1/j\}.

∎

We prove Theorem
1
in two ways. The first method for proving our main theorem is a direct proof utilizing a result of the first author and Gan
[
1
, Theorem 1]
, providing a shorter (generalized) proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
. Our second method, though longer, follows the original methodology of Orponen–Shmerkin–Wang’s Theorem 1.2. Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang first reduce the problem to a set of points and tubes with a “Furstenberg” type restriction (see
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
). Then, they apply Fu and Ren’s planar incidence estimate (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
) and a dual Furtenberg set estimate (see
[
10
, Lemma 3.13]
) to complete the proof. The (second) proof of our main result in this paper follows in a similar manner, and thus the largest barriers to proving Theorem
1
using Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang’s framework are

•

Generalizing the Fu-Ren incidence estimate between points and tubes to points and tubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

•

Proving the (generalized) dual Furstenberg estimate in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We find these two estimates, which may be of independent interest, and obtain the following:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,n]
and
\beta\in[0,2(n-1)]
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a

.

Theorem 4

Let
\alpha\in[0,n-1]
and
\beta\in(0,n]

1.1.
Recent Developments in Radial Projections

In recent years, many have been studying the effects of radial projections on Hausdorff dimension. The most recent thread of theorems started with the study of radial projections in finite fields studied by Lund, Pham, and Thu
[
8
]
. In particular, they showed that given
Y\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
M
a positive integer with
|Y|\gtrsim q^{n-1}
and
M\lesssim q^{n-1}
, then

|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:|\pi_{x}(Y)|\leq M\}|\lesssim q^{n-1}M|Y|^{-1}.

This led to the following conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(when
k=n-1
) put forward by Lund–Pham–Thu, which was later resolved by the first author and Gan:

Theorem 6

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then, for
0<s<k

\dim(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Y:\dim\pi_{x}(Y)<s\})\leq\max\{k+s-\dim Y,0\}

One can prove the following result (conjectured by Liu
[
7
]
) as a consequence of Theorem
6
.

Theorem 7

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 2)
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set with
\dim Y\in(k-1,k]
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then we have

\dim(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Y:\dim\pi_{x}(Y)<\dim Y\})\leq k.

Theorem
7
followed via a dimensional reduction by the first author and Gan, as well as a “swapping trick” of Liu. Note that finite field analogues of the above two theorems has recently been proven in
[
2
]
by the first author, Lund, and Pham. Furthermore, shortly after the first author’s and Gan’s proofs, Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang proved a plethora of new results regarding radial projections (including Theorems
6
and
7
). Among these new results were the following two theorems:

Theorem 8

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a (non-empty) Borel set which is not contained in any line. Then, for every Borel set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X,\dim Y,1\}.

Theorem 9

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be Borel sets with
X\neq\emptyset
and
\dim Y>1
. Then,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-1,1\}.

Theorem
8
generalized to higher dimensions for Borel subsets
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim Y>k-1/k-\eta
for
\eta>0
sufficiently small. This has been strengthened to all
Y
with
\dim Y>k-1
(by the third author in
[
11
]
), as was conjectured by Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang. On the other hand, Theorem
9
was not generalized to higher dimensions. This generalization to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the focus of this paper.

The notation

(X,d)
. If

Given

If

is defined via the equation

Frostman’s Lemma states that if

is a Borel set and

, then there exists a Borel probability measure

with

and

. See
[
9
, Chapter 8]
for more details.

Lastly, we define

-sets and Katz-Tao

-sets.

(

-sets)

,

. Then, we say that

A Direct Proof of Theorem
1
when

In this direct proof, we use Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
,
X\cap Y=\emptyset
.

Suppose that
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are Borel sets with
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
,
X\neq\emptyset
, and
X\cap Y=\emptyset
. If
\dim X=0
, then we wish to prove that

\sup_{x\in X}\dim(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\}))\geq\dim Y-k.

In fact, we will show that
\dim(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\}))\geq\dim Y-1
for any
x\in X
. By Frostman’s lemma, for any
s<\dim Y
, there exists a
s
-dimensional Frostman probability measure
\mu
supported on
Y
. Now for any ball
I
of diameter
r
on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
, we have that
T:=\pi_{x}^{-1}(I)\cap B(0,1)
is contained in
r^{-1}
many
r
-balls, so
\mu(T)\lesssim r^{s-1}
by the Frostman condition. Hence, the pushforward measure
\pi_{x}\mu
satisfies a
(s-1)
-dimensional Frostman condition, so by Frostman’s lemma again, we have that
\dim(\pi_{x}(Y))\geq s-1
. Since
s<\dim Y
was arbitrary, we have
\dim(\pi_{x}(Y))\geq\dim Y-1
, as desired.

Thus, assume
\dim X>0
. Let

s=\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

Let
\epsilon>0
be small enough such that
s-\epsilon>0
and
\dim X-\epsilon>0
, which we may assume as
\dim Y>k
and
\dim X>0
. Under these assumptions on
\epsilon
, we may apply Theorem
6
to
s-\epsilon
, we obtain

\dim(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Y:\dim\pi_{x}(Y)<s-\epsilon\})\leq\max\{k+s-\epsilon-\dim Y,0\}<\dim X.

Given
\dim(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Y:\dim\pi_{x}(Y)<s-\epsilon\})
is strictly less than
\dim X
, it follows that
X\not\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus Y:\dim\pi_{x}(Y)<s-\epsilon\}
. Given
X\cap Y=\emptyset
, this implies that for every
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists an
x\in X
such that

\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq s-\epsilon\ =\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}-\epsilon.

Given
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we obtain that

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

∎

\lesssim 1

Let

Remark 19

Note, that in either case, we have that

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

throughout this argument.

4.
A Dual Furstenberg Set Estimate

The purpose of this section is to prove a dual Furstenberg set estimate integral to the proof of Theorem
1
, analogous to Lemma 3.13 in
[
10
]
. This result will discuss
(\delta,s,C)
-sets of
\delta
-tubes
\mathbb{T}_{x}
,
x\in B^{n}
, such that
x\in T
for all
T\in\mathbb{T}_{x}
. Note that one can check that the
(\delta,s,C)
-set property of
\mathbb{T}_{x}
is equivalent to the directions of the tubes forming a
(\delta,s,C^{\prime})
-set as a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

We begin by proving a cardinality result for this dual Furstenberg set. This can be derived from
[
11
, Corollary 2.10]
, but we present the simple proof for completeness.

Lemma 23
.

For every
\xi>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\xi)>0
and
\epsilon=\epsilon(\xi)>0
such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
. Let
\alpha\in[0,n-1]
and
\beta\in(0,n].
Assume that
P\subset B^{n}
is a non-empty
(\delta,\beta,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set. Further, assume that for every
p\in P
there exists a
(\delta,\alpha,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of tubes
\mathbb{T}_{p}
with the property that
p\in T
for all
T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
. Then, we have that

\left|\bigcup_{p\in P}\mathbb{T}_{p}\right|\geq\delta^{-\gamma(\alpha,\beta)+\xi}

where
\gamma(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha+\min\{\alpha,\beta\},
and
\left|\bigcup_{p\in P}\mathbb{T}_{p}\right|
is the cardinality of the set of tubes
\bigcup_{p\in P}\mathbb{T}_{p}.

Proof.

First, since
|\mathbb{T}_{p}|\in(\delta^{-\alpha+\varepsilon},\delta^{-1})
for every
p\in P
, we may find a subset
P^{\prime}\subset P
with
|P^{\prime}|\gtrsim(\log\delta^{-1})^{-1}|P|
such that for each
p\in P^{\prime}
, we have
|T_{p}|\in[M,2M)
, for some
M\in(\delta^{-\alpha+\varepsilon},\delta^{-1})
. Next, we may prune each
|\mathbb{T}_{p}|
for
p\in P^{\prime}
to have exactly
M
elements, at the cost of making
\mathbb{T}_{p}
a
(\delta,\alpha,2\delta^{-\varepsilon})
-set of tubes. Notice that
P^{\prime}
is still a
(\delta,\beta,\delta^{-2\varepsilon})
-set. Now if we prove the lemma for
\varepsilon
and
\{T_{p}\}_{p\in P^{\prime}}
, then we also prove the lemma for
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
\{T_{p}\}_{p\in P}
. Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that
|\mathbb{T}_{p}|=M
for all
p\in P
.

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\bigcup_{p\in P}\mathbb{T}_{p}
. Then, we find a lower bound for
|\mathbb{T}|
by finding an upper and lower bound to the cardinality of the set

J(P,\mathbb{T}):=\{(p,p^{\prime},T)\in P^{2}\times\mathbb{T}\mid T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}_{p^{\prime}}\}.

(This is similar to, but different from the set
\{(p,p^{\prime},T)\mid p,p^{\prime}\in T,T\in\mathbb{T}\}
, because there can be tubes
T\in\mathbb{T}
passing through points
p
in
P
, yet
T\not\in\mathbb{T}(p)
.)
Then we have that

\displaystyle|J(P,\mathbb{T})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\#\{(p,p^{\prime})\in P^{2}:T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}_{p^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T}|\{p\in P:T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}\}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|\mathbb{T}|^{-1}\left(\sum_{T}|\{p\in P:T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}\}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{T}|^{-1}\left(\sum_{p}|\mathbb{T}(p)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|\mathbb{T}|^{-1}(|P|M)^{2}.

We now find an upperbound to
J(P,\mathbb{T}^{\prime})
using a geometric argument.

\displaystyle|J(P,\mathbb{T}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\sum_{p^{\prime},T}\mathbf{1}(p\in T)\mathbf{1}(p^{\prime}\in T)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p}\sum_{p^{\prime}}\#(\mathbb{T}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}_{p^{\prime}}).

Notice that
\#(\mathbb{T}_{p}\cap\mathbb{T}_{p^{\prime}})\lesssim M\delta^{-\varepsilon}\cdot(\frac{\delta}{d(p,p^{\prime})+\delta})^{\alpha}
since
\mathbb{T}_{p}
is a
(\delta,\alpha,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set and the set of tubes through both
p,p^{\prime}
lie in a common
\frac{\delta}{d(p,p^{\prime})+\delta}
-tube. Since
P
is a
(\delta,\beta,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set, using a dyadic decomposition, there are
|P|\delta^{-\epsilon}r^{\beta}
many
\delta
-balls with
d(p,p^{\prime})\sim r
. Hence, by summing over dyadic
r\in(\delta,1)
, we get

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p}\sum_{\delta\leq r\leq 1}|P|\delta^{-\varepsilon}r^{\beta}\cdot M\delta^{-\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\frac{\delta}{r}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq M|P|\delta^{\alpha-2\varepsilon}\sum_{p}\sum_{r}r^{\beta-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim M|P|^{2}\delta^{\alpha-2\varepsilon}\max\{\delta^{\beta-\alpha},1\}.

Hence, combining the upper and lower bounds, we obtain

|\mathbb{T}|^{-1}|P|^{2}M^{2}\leq|P|^{2}M\delta^{\alpha-2\varepsilon}\max\{\delta^{\beta-\alpha},1\}.

Therefore, using
M\geq\delta^{-\alpha+\varepsilon}
, we get

|\mathbb{T}|=\left|\bigcup_{x\in P}\mathbb{T}_{x}\right|\geq M\delta^{-\alpha+2\varepsilon}\min\{\delta^{\alpha-\beta},1\}\gtrsim\delta^{-2\alpha+\max\{\alpha-\beta,0\}+3\varepsilon}.

Notice that
2\alpha-\max\{\alpha-\beta,0\}=\alpha+\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
. This gives the desired result if we let
\varepsilon:=\frac{\xi}{4}
.
∎

Using this, and a standard Hausdorff content argument, we show that the union of tubes contains a
(\delta,\gamma(\alpha,\beta),\delta^{-\xi})
-set of tubes. First, we need a slight generalization of Proposition A.1 of
[
4
]
but with the exact same proof. The statement is in fact an equality, the
\leq
direction being easier.

Lemma 24
.

In
[0,1]^{n}
, let
P
be a set of dyadic
\delta
-cubes, and let
f:\mathcal{D}_{[\delta,1]}\to[1,\infty)
be a function, where
\mathcal{D}_{[\delta,1]}
is the set of dyadic cubes with side length in
[\delta,1]
. Then

(7)

\max\left\{|P^{\prime}|:P^{\prime}\subset P,|P^{\prime}\cap Q|\leq f(Q)\,\forall Q\in\mathcal{D}_{[\delta,1]}\right\}\\
\geq\min\left\{\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}f(Q)\mid\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{D}_{[\delta,1]},\mathcal{C}\text{ covers }P\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\delta=2^{-k}
. For each
0\leq i\leq k
, we construct a collection
\mathcal{C}_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\leq 2^{-i}}
and a subset
P_{i}\subset\mathcal{C}_{i}
such that:

(1)

P_{0}\subset P_{1}\subset\cdots\subset P_{k}=P
;

(2)

for each
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{i}
, we have
|P_{i}\cap Q|\leq f(Q)
;

(3)

for each
Q\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
, we have
|P_{i}\cap Q|=f(Q)
.

For
i=k
, we simply take
P_{k}=\mathcal{C}_{k}=P
. Now given
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
and
P_{i+1}
, we say that a square
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{r}
is rich for
P_{i+1}
if
|P_{i+1}\cap Q|>2^{(k-i)s}
; otherwise,
Q
is poor.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the union of (elements of
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
inside poor squares) and rich squares. Let
P_{i}
be the union of (elements of
P_{i+1}
inside poor squares) and, for each rich square
Q
, an arbitrary
2^{(k-i)s}
-element subset of
P_{i+1}\cap Q
. Then (1) is just true; (2) is true by definition of rich/poor square; and (3) is true by using (3) for
\mathcal{C}_{i+1}
and definition of rich/poor square.

Now for any
r=2^{-i}
and
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{r}
, we have by (1) and (2)

|P_{0}\cap Q|\leq|P_{i}\cap Q|\leq f(Q).

Furthermore, by (3) we have
|P_{0}|=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}|P_{0}\cap Q|=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}f(Q)
. Now
(P_{0},\mathcal{C}_{0})
is a witness to (
7
).
∎

Corollary 25
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a set of
\delta
-tubes, and let
s\geq 0
. Let
r(T)
be the thickness of tube
T
, and let

contains a

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}(n,1)
is locally homeomorphic to

, we can apply Lemma
24
with

. The maximizing set

satisfies

\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
is a
(\delta,s,O_{n}(1/K))

many dyadic tubes of radius
\sim r(T)

is dyadic. Then by the condition on

Lemma 26
.

For every
\xi>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\xi)>0
and

such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]

Assume that

there exists a
(\delta,\alpha,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of tubes
\mathbb{T}_{p}
with the property that
x\in T
for all
T\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
. Then, the union

contains a
(\delta,\gamma(\alpha,\beta),O_{N}(\delta^{-\xi}))
-set of tubes
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
, where
\gamma(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha+\min\{\alpha,\beta\}

Proof.

The proof of this statement essentially follows that of
[
5

, though we work through the details here.

By Corollary
25
, it suffices to check that the RHS of Corollary
25
is large. Fix a cover
\mathcal{C}
of

by dyadic tubes with thickness between

and

(i.e. a cover satisfying the conditions of Corollary

). By assumption, for every
p\in P
, there exists a set
\mathbb{T}_{p}
of tubes that is a

For each

\mathbb{T}

T\in\mathbb{T}_{x}
. Then,
\sigma\geq s+t-n+1-\zeta
.

Before proving this discretized result, we first recall some important preliminaries from the paper of Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang.

5.1.
Preliminaries

We write
\mathcal{P}(X)
for the family of Borel probability measures on a metric space
(X,d)
. We use the following notation: if
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
G\subset X\times Y
, we write

G|_{x}:=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in G\}\hskip 7.11317pt\text{and}\hskip 7.11317ptG|^{y}:=\{x\in X:(x,y)\in G\}

for
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. If
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Borel set, then so too are
G|_{x}
and
G|^{y}
as
\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Definition 30

(Thin tubes)
.

Let
K,t\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1]
. Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{spt}(\mu)=:X
and
\mathrm{spt}(\nu)=:Y
. We say that
(\mu,\nu)
has
(t,K,c)
-
thin tubes
if there exists a Borel set
G\subset X\times Y
with
(\mu\times\nu)(G)\geq c
with the following property: If
x\in X
, then

\nu(T\cap G|_{x})\leq K\cdot r^{t}\text{~~for all $r>0$ and all $r$-tubes $T$ containing $x$.}

We also say that
(\mu,\nu)
has
t
-thin tubes if
(\mu,\nu)
has
(t,K,c)
-thin tubes for some
K,c>0
.

We also recall Remark 2.3 from Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang due to its importance in our proofs.

Remark 31

(
[
10
]
, Remark 2.3)
.

Assume that
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has
t
-thin tubes for some
t>0
. Then, there exists
x\in\mathrm{spt}(\mu)
such that
\mathcal{H}^{t}(\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\}))>0
. In other words, this implies

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq t.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
29

We prove Theorem
29
analogous to Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang with a slight change in numerology.

Proof of Theorem
29
.

Fix
s\in[0,n]
,
t\in(n-1,n]
,
\sigma\in[0,n-1)
, and
\zeta>0
. Let
P_{K}
and
P_{E}
be as in the statement of the theorem– namely, let
P_{K}\subset B^{n}
be a
\delta
-separated
(\delta,t,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set and let
P_{E}\subset B^{n}
be a
\delta
-separated
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set. Furthermore, suppose that for every
x\in P_{E}
, there exists a
(\delta,\sigma,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of tubes
\mathbb{T}_{x}
with the properties that
x\in T
for all
T\in\mathbb{T}_{x}
, and

(8)

|T\cap P_{K}|\geq\delta^{\sigma+\epsilon}|P_{K}|

for all
T\in\mathbb{T}_{x}
. Given
\mathbb{T}_{x}
is a
(\delta,\sigma,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of tubes and
P_{K}
is a
(\delta,s,\delta^{-\epsilon})
-set of points, by Lemma
26
,
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{x\in P}\mathbb{T}_{x}
contains a
(\delta,\gamma(\sigma,s),\delta^{-\xi})
-set of tubes, which we denote by
\mathbb{T},
where
\gamma(\sigma,s)=\sigma+\min\{s,\sigma\}
.

We now state our conditions on
\epsilon
:

(9)

10\xi+2\epsilon\leq\zeta,\text{~~~and~~~}\sigma<n-1-5\xi-\epsilon,\text{~~~and~~~}\frac{t-n+1}{2}-5\xi-\epsilon>0.

Such an
\epsilon
exists as
\sigma<n-1
and
t>n-1
.

By (
8
), we have

|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\sigma+\epsilon}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}|T\cap P_{K}|=I(P_{K},\mathbb{T}).

We now compare this lowerbound against the upperbounds from Theorem
18
. Since
P_{K}
is a
(\delta,t)
-set and
\mathbb{T}
is a
(\delta,\gamma(\sigma,s))
-set, we consider the quantity:

\overline{\kappa}=\kappa(t,\gamma(\sigma,s))=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{n-1}{t-n+1+\gamma(\sigma,s)}\right\}.

We break up into three cases.

i)

Suppose that
\overline{\kappa}=\frac{n-1}{t-n+1+\gamma(\sigma,s)}
. Then, regardless of the value of
\gamma(\sigma,s)
, we have

|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\sigma+\epsilon}\leq I(P_{K},\mathbb{T})\leq|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\overline{\kappa}(t-n+1+\gamma(\sigma,s))-5\xi}=|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{n-1-5\xi}

Hence,
\sigma\geq n-1-5\xi
.
By the assumption (
9
), this is a contradiction.

ii)

Suppose that
\overline{\kappa}=1/2
. Then, we break into cases.

(a)

Assume
s\leq\sigma
so
\gamma(\sigma,s)=\sigma+s
. Then, we have

|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\sigma+\epsilon}\leq I(P_{K},\mathbb{T})\leq|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\overline{\kappa}(t-n+1+\gamma(\sigma,s))-5\xi}=|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{(t-n+1+\sigma+s)/2-5\xi}.

Hence,

\sigma\geq s+t-n+1-10\xi-2\epsilon\geq s+t-n+1-\zeta

where here we use the assumption that
\zeta\geq 10\xi-2\epsilon
. This gives the desired result.

(b)

Assume now that
s\geq\sigma
, so
\gamma(\sigma,s)=2\sigma
. Then, we have

|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\sigma+\epsilon}\leq I(P_{K},\mathbb{T})\leq|P_{K}||\mathbb{T}|\delta^{\overline{\kappa}(t-n+1+2\sigma)-5\xi}.

Hence,
\sigma\geq(t-n+1)/2-5\xi-\epsilon
, which contradicts (
9
).

This completes the proof of Theorem
29
.
∎

5.3.
A Discretized Version of Theorem
1

We now prove Theorem
32
.

Theorem 32
.

Let
s\in[0,n]
,
t\in(n-1,n]
,
0\leq\sigma<\min\{s+t-n+1,n-1\}
,
C>0
, and
\epsilon\in(0,1]

K=K(C,\epsilon,s,\sigma,t)>0
such that the following holds. Assume
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(B^{n})
satisfy

\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s}\text{~~and~~}\nu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{t}.

Then,
(\mu,\nu)
has
(\sigma,K,1-\epsilon)
-thin tubes.

In particular, whenever
s\in[0,n]
,
t\in(n-1,n]

I_{s}(\mu)<\infty
and
I_{t}(\nu)<\infty
, then
(\mu,\nu)
has
\sigma
-thin tubes for all
0\leq\sigma<\min\{s+t-n+1,n-1\}
.

We establish the necessary
\epsilon
-improvement of the result, Lemma
33
below, which iterated multiple times implies Theorem
32
.

Lemma 33
.

Let
s\in[0,n]
,
t\in(n-1,n]
, and
0\leq\sigma<\min\{s+t-n+1,n-1\}
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{10})
and

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(B^{n})
such that
\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{s}

for all

and

. If
(\mu,\nu)
has

-thin tubes, then there exists
\eta=\eta(s,\sigma,t)>0

(\mu,\nu)
has

-thin tubes. Furthermore,

is bounded away from zero on any compact subset of

\Omega:=\{(s,\sigma,t)\in[0,n]\times[0,n-1]\times(n-1,n]:\sigma<\min\{s+t-n+1,n-1\}\}.

Proof of Theorem
32
assuming Lemma
33
.

The proof is nearly identical to that of Orponen, Shmerkin, and Wang’s Theorem 3.20, with the main difference that our set
\Omega
is properly adjusted for this higher dimensional setting. In particular, we fix
0\leq\sigma<\min\{s+t-n+1,n-1\}

We automatically have that
(\mu,\nu)
has
(t-1,K_{0},1)
-thin tubes for some

\nu
, as we can take a
r

many delta balls, obtaining

(t-1,K_{0},1-\overline{\epsilon})
-thin tubes for every

\sigma

6.
Proof of Theorem
1

We now prove Theorem
1
for all values of
k
using a Marstrand projection theorem and one of the radial projection results of Orponen-Shmerkin-Wang
[
10
, Theorem 1.9.i]
. We restate the theorem here.

Theorem 34
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be Borel sets with
X\neq\emptyset
,
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
for some
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then,

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})\geq\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

Proof.

Note that if
k=n-1
, this result is precisely Theorem
1
, so we suppose
k<n-1
.

We break this in proof into two cases. Firstly, suppose that
\dim X>k+1
. In this case, then

\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}=k

as
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
. In this case, we apply Theorem 1.9.i from
[
10
]
(which we may do as
\dim X>k+1
), obtaining that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})

\displaystyle=\dim Y

\displaystyle\geq k

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

This concludes the result.

Otherwise, suppose
\dim X\leq k+1
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exists subsets
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
X\neq\emptyset
,
\dim X\leq k+1
, and
\dim Y\in(k,k+1]
such that

\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})<\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}.

Then by
[
9
, Corollary 9.4]
, for
\gamma_{n,k}
almost every
V\in G(n,k+1)
,

\dim P_{V}(X)=\dim X\hskip 7.11317pt\text{and}\hskip 7.11317pt\dim P_{V}(Y)=\dim Y,

where
P_{V}
is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
V.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{P_{V}(x)}(P_{V}(Y))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in X}\dim\pi_{x}(Y\setminus\{x\})

\displaystyle<\min\{\dim X+\dim Y-k,k\}

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim P_{V}(X)+\dim P_{V}(Y)-k,k\}.

This contradicts the
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
version of Theorem
1
(proven in Section
5
) in
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
applied to
P_{V}(X)
and
P_{V}(Y)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

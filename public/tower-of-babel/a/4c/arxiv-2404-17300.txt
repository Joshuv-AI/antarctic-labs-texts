Canonical vs. Grand Canonical Ensemble for Bosonic Gases under Harmonic Confinement
Andrea Crisanti 1 , Luca Salasnich 2,3,4, , Alessandro Sarracino 5 and Marco Zannetti 6

Abstract
We analyze the general relation between canonical and grand canonical ensembles in the thermodynamic limit. We begin our discussion by deriving, with an alternative approach, some standard results first obtained by Kac and coworkers in the late 1970s. Then, motivated by the Bose–Einstein condensation (BEC) of trapped gases with a fixed number of atoms, which is well described by the canonical ensemble and by the recent groundbreaking experimental realization of BEC with photons in a dye-filled optical microcavity under genuine grand canonical conditions, we apply our formalism to a system of non-interacting Bose particles confined in a two-dimensional harmonic trap. We discuss in detail the mathematical origin of the inequivalence of ensembles observed in the condensed phase, giving place to the so-called grand canonical catastrophe of density fluctuations. We also provide explicit analytical expressions for the internal energy and specific heat and compare them with available experimental data. For these quantities, we show the equivalence of ensembles in the thermodynamic limit.



I
Introduction

Bose–Einstein condensation (BEC) was first experimentally observed with ultracold and dilute atomic gases in 1995 by several groups
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Since then, the phenomenon has been reproduced in a huge variety of systems
[
4
]
, such as trapped alkali gases
[
5
,
6
]
, rotating systems
[
7
]
, quantum magnets
[
8
]
, and so on. For all these systems,
the number of atoms is conserved and the statistical properties are well described within the canonical ensemble (CE).
More recently, however, and quite surprisingly, BEC has also been realized with effectively massive photons in a dye-filled optical cavity
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, where it is possible to work in a regime with a number of photons that are conserved only on average. In this case, the proper ensemble to describe the system features is the grand canonical ensemble (GCE).

The relation between CE and GCE is a general problem of statistical mechanics and the two ensembles are usually equivalent in the thermodynamic limit. This is the case when
correlations are short-ranged and, due to the central limit theorem,
fluctuations in extensive quantities become
negligible for a large system size.
However, there exist noteworthy exceptions, where computations performed in the two ensembles lead to different results. This occurs, for instance, in long-range systems
[
16
]
, where the issue of ensemble inequivalence is very well studied. In the framework of systems of free bosons, this topic has been considered theoretically in several studies
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. The remarkable result is that, while, in the CE, density fluctuations vanish in the thermodynamic limit as generally expected, in the GCE, they are macroscopic and remain finite upon decreasing the temperature, leading to the so-called grand canonical catastrophe, with an explicit negative connotation. However, the experimental realization of BEC in genuine grand canonical conditions has confirmed the theoretical predictions obtained in the GCE, making the issue related to the inequivalence of ensembles in this context a heated topic of debate. In a previous paper
[
22
]
, building on an analogy with the celebrated spherical model of a ferromagnet introduced by Berlin and Kac
[
23
]
, some of us have interpreted the physical meaning of the grand canonical catastrophe predicted for a homogeneous ideal Bose gas as a phenomenon of the condensation of fluctuations
[
24
]
.

In this paper, before considering the experimentally relevant case of quantum gases of non-interacting bosons confined
in a two-dimensional harmonic trap, we first present an accurate discussion of
the relation between canonical and grand canonical ensembles in the general framework of statistical mechanics. In particular, we bring to the fore the mathematical aspects related to the (in-)equivalence between ensembles, focusing on the behaviors arising in the thermodynamic limit. We re-derive some important results obtained by Ziff et al.
[
17
]
for ideal gases, following an alternative approach, based on the theory of singular perturbations and boundary layer problems
[
25
]
. We then apply our formalism to the case of a free gas of bosons confined by a parabolic potential and investigate several physical features.
We find that, in the thermodynamic limit, in the Bose-condensed phase,
the density fluctuations and the spatial density–density correlation function
have a quite different behavior in the two ensembles. In contrast, other quantities,
such as the critical temperature, the condensate fraction, internal energy, and the specific heat behave similarly. For the last two quantities, we provide explicit analytical expressions that very well compare to the experimental data reported in
[
12
]
.

This paper is structured as follows: in Section
II
, we develop the general formalism to connect CE to GCE
with the kernel introduced in ref.
[
26
]
. In Section

III
, we discuss the problem of Bose–Einstein condensation for an ideal gas of massive bosons
confined in a two-dimensional harmonic potential. In Section
III
, we compare the theoretical predictions of the CE and GCE for several quantities. We discuss in particular the issue related to the grand canonical catastrophe and its meaning.
We also show that our theory reproduces the experimental results of internal energy and specific heat
of the photons in an optical cavity quite well. Conclusions are drawn in Section
IV
. It is important to stress that, in our two-dimensional problem, there is true BEC due to the presence of the harmonic confinement, while there is no Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless phase transition because the bosons are not interacting.

II
Relation between Canonical and Grand Canonical Ensembles

We start by discussing some standard results of statistical mechanics. This allows us to introduce the general problem and to fix the notation that will be used throughout the paper.
A general relation between the CE and GCE can
be established at different levels. We start by considering the
connection between the partition functions. We
denote by
{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{N})
the state of a generic system, and by
{\cal H}({\sigma})
its Hamiltonian.
The natural variables of the CE are the temperature
k_{B}T=\beta^{-1}
, the volume
V
, and
the number of elements
N
. It is important to underline that, in the subsequent sections, which investigate the bosonic system under harmonic confinement, the volume
V
will be an “effective volume” related to the frequency of the harmonic potential. The probability density of a configuration
{\sigma}
is

P_{\rm C}({\sigma}|\beta,V,N)=\frac{1}{Z_{\rm C}(\beta,V,N)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta{\cal H}({\sigma})},

(1)

where

Z_{\rm C}(\beta,V,N)=\sum_{\{{\sigma}\}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta{\cal H}({\sigma})}

(2)

is the canonical partition function, where
the sum extends over all possible system configurations.

In the GCE, the natural variables are the temperature

k_{B}T=\beta^{-1}
, the volume
V
, and the fugacity
z=e^{\beta\mu}
,
where
\mu
is the chemical potential.
The probability that the system is in the configuration
{\sigma}
with
N
elements
is, in the GCE,

P_{\rm GC}({\sigma},N|\beta,V,z)=\frac{z^{N}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta{\cal H}_{N}({\sigma})}}{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,V,z)},

(3)

where

\displaystyle Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,V,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 0}\,\sum_{\{{\sigma}\}}\,z^{N}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta{\cal H}_{N}({\sigma})}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 0}\,z^{N}Z_{\rm C}(\beta,V,N)

is the grand canonical
partition function defined for
\Re z<z_{0}
, where
z_{0}
is a model-dependent parameter ensuring the convergence of the sum in Equation (
4
).
We have added the subscript
N
to the Hamiltonian to indicate that

{\cal H}_{N}({\sigma})
refers to a system of
N
elements.
From the above relation, one immediately has
that the GCE partition function can be obtained as a
z-
transform of
the CE partition function. The Relation (
4
) can be
inverted as

Z_{\rm C}(N)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dz\,z^{-1-N}\,Z_{\rm GC}(z),

(5)

where
\Gamma
is a generic curve in the complex plane encircling
the origin
z=0
(to simplify the notation, we have only indicated the
relevant variables).

II.1
The Kernel
K(N|z)

The connection between the GCE and CE can also be established at the level
of probability density. Indeed, writing

P_{\rm GC}({\sigma},N|z)=\frac{z^{N}\,Z_{\rm C}(N)}{Z_{\rm GC}(z)}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta{\cal H}({\sigma})}}{Z_{\rm C}(N)},

(6)

and using (
1
), one obtains

P_{\rm GC}({\sigma},N|z)=P_{\rm C}({\sigma}|N)\,K(N|z),

(7)

where

K(N|z)=\frac{z^{N}\,Z_{\rm C}(N)}{Z_{\rm GC}(z)}

(8)

is the kernel
relating the two distributions, and represents the probability that the system consists of
N
elements for fixed
z
. Its explicit form depends on the
specific model. Celebrated examples are discussed in
[
26
]
for the spherical model and in
[
17
]
for the homogeneous ideal Bose gas. We will consider the trapped ideal Bose gas in
the following sections.

Finally, from the Relation
(
7
), a connection between averages readily follows. Indeed, we have

\langle f({\sigma})\rangle_{\rm GC}(z)=\sum_{N}\langle f({\sigma})\rangle_{\rm C}(N)\,K(N|z),

(9)

where we have denoted by
\langle\ldots\rangle_{\rm GC}
and
\langle\ldots\rangle_{\rm C}
averages
in the GCE and CE, respectively, and
f({\sigma})
is a generic
function of the system configuration.
The above equation can also be interpreted as the generating function of
\langle f({\sigma})\rangle_{\rm C}
, as first introduced in
[
17
]
; see their Equation (2.34)
.
As detailed in Appendix A,
this relation can be formally inverted, yielding

\langle f({\sigma})\rangle_{\rm C}(N)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{z}\frac{\langle f({\sigma})\rangle_{\rm GC}(z)}{K(N|z)}.

(10)

II.2
Thermodynamic Limit

The discussion presented in the previous section can be simplified in the large
N
limit,
leading to some more explicit general results.
The thermodynamic limit is obtained by taking

N\gg 1,\ V\gg 1\ \text{but}\ N/V=\rho=\text{constant}.

(11)

Rewriting the GCE partition function (
4
) as

Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)=\sum_{N}\mathrm{e}^{N\ln z+\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)},

(12)

since
\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)=O(N)
as
N\gg 1
,
the sum is dominated in the thermodynamic limit (
11
) by the term with the largest value of the
exponent:

Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)\sim z^{N^{*}(z)}\,Z_{\rm C}(N^{*}(z),V),

(13)

where

N^{*}(z)=max_{N}[N\ln z+\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)\bigr].

(14)

To evaluate
N^{*}(z)
, we rewrite Equation (
12
) as

Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)=V\,\sum_{N}\,\Delta\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{V\bigl[\rho\ln z+\frac{1}{V}\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho,V)\bigr]},

(15)

where
\Delta\rho=1/V\ll 1
as
V\gg 1
, and, in the thermodynamic limit, we have

Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)\sim V\int d\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{V\phi(\rho)},\,\,V\gg 1

(16)

with

\phi(\rho)=\rho\ln z+\frac{1}{V}\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho,V).

(17)

Note that, since
\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho,V)=O(V)
in the large volume limit, the second term on the right-hand side becomes independent of
V
. We will drop
O(1/V)
corrections in the following.
The integral can then be evaluated using the Laplace method, expanding
\phi(\rho)
as

\phi(\rho)=\phi(\rho^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{*})(\rho-\rho^{*})^{2}+O(\rho-\rho^{*})^{3},

(18)

where
\rho^{*}
is the solution of the stationary point equation

\phi^{\prime}(\rho^{*})=\ln z+\frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho^{*}}\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho^{*},V)=0.

(19)

Then, introducing
\sigma_{\phi}^{-2}=-(\partial/\partial\rho^{*})^{2}\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho^{*},V)>0
, we have

Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)\sim V\,\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\phi}^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{V\bigl[\rho^{*}\ln z+\frac{1}{V}\ln Z_{\rm C}(\rho^{*},V)\bigr]},V\gg 1.

(20)

Writing
N^{*}(z)=V\rho^{*}
, and taking only the leading term, we recover Equation (
13
),
with
N^{*}(z)
given by the solution of

N^{*}(z):\ \frac{\partial}{\partial N}\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)=-\ln z.

(21)

Equation (
13
) implies
that

\ln Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)=\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)+N\ln z,

(22)

where
N=N^{*}(z)
is obtained from Equation (
21
).
Equations (
21
) and (
22
) show that,
in the thermodynamic limit (
11
), the
\ln Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)
is the Legendre
transform of
\ln Z_{\rm C}(N,V)
with respect to
N
.

We now consider the kernel
K(N|z)
. From the previous discussion, it follows that, in the thermodynamic limit, for fixed
z
,

\displaystyle K(N|z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{N}\,Z_{\rm C}(N,V)}{Z_{\rm GC}(z,V)}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{V\phi(\rho)}}{V\int d\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{V\phi(\rho)}}

(23)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\phi}^{2}}(\rho-\rho^{*})^{2}}}{V\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{\phi}^{2}}},

where, in the second equality, we have used Equation (
17
),
and hence, since
\sigma^{2}_{\phi}=O(1/N)
,

K(N|z)\sim\Delta\rho\,\delta(\rho-\rho^{*})\sim\delta^{\rm Kr}\bigl(N-N^{*}(z)\bigr),

(24)

with
N^{*}(z)
the solution of (
21
)
and
\delta^{Kr}
the Kronecker delta. This result is also discussed,
for instance, in
[
17
]
—see their Equation (2.63)—and shows the equivalence of the ensembles in the thermodynamic limit.
The same result can be obtained starting from the expression of
Z_{\rm C}(N)
as a transform
of
Z_{\rm GC}(z)
, as reported in Appendix B of this paper. The above discussion shows that, for a fixed
z
, with
\Re z<z_{0}
,
there exists a corresponding
N^{*}(z)
and therefore there exists
an equivalent canonical ensemble with
N=N^{*}(z)
.

The general findings obtained so far break down in specific cases,
where the application of the Laplace method requires particular
care. This can lead to different behaviors in different ensembles, as
described in the next section.

III
Ideal Bose Gas in a Harmonic Trap

As a specific system, we consider a two-dimensional (2D) harmonically
trapped system of non-interacting massive bosons. This model has been
shown to provide a very good description of a photon gas confined in a
high-finesse dye-filled microcavity, studied in the
experiments
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, where Bose–Einstein
condensation has been observed. In particular, the microcavity was
realized by two curved mirrors, where photons were continuously
absorbed and re-emitted by the dye medium, which is crucial for the equilibration. Due to a cavity length of
the same order as the photon wavelength, a frequency gap between the
longitudinal resonator modes was realized. Thus, the system consists
of photons with a fixed longitudinal mode number that are allowed to
populate several transversally excited cavity modes, making the photon
gas effectively two-dimensional. The energy–momentum relation is
described by a quadratic term, with an effective mass, and a trapping
parabolic potential induced by the mirror curvature
[
10
]
.

It has been shown that this experimental setup realizes bona fide grand canonical
conditions where theoretical predictions can be tested
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
We are interested in the different behaviors that can arise in
different ensembles, in particular for what concerns the fluctuations
in the occupation number of the condensed phase. In the following, we
will apply the formalism introduced in the previous section to the
system of trapped bosons in 2D. Our theoretical treatment differs from
that presented in
[
9
]
, where the photon number
statistics are derived from a rate equation model in the stationary
regime. Our analysis follows rather the analytical theory of Ziff et
al.
[
17
]
, developed for the case of a free ideal
gas. The main difference relies on the derivation of the Kac kernel in
the region below the critical temperature, which we present here for the
case of a trapped Bose gas,
following an alternative approach based on the theory of singular perturbations
.

III.1
The Model

In second quantization, the Hamiltonian of the system can be
written as
[
27
]

{\hat{H}}=\int d^{2}{\bf r}\ {\hat{\psi}}^{\dagger}({\bf r})\left[-{\hbar^{2}\over 2m_{\textrm{eff}}}\nabla^{2}+U({\bf r})\right]{\hat{\psi}}({\bf r})\;,

(25)

where
{\hat{\psi}}({\bf r})
(
{\hat{\psi}}^{\dagger}({\bf r})
) is the bosonic field operator,
which destroys (creates) a boson at the position
{\bf r}=(x,y)
,

m_{\textrm{eff}}
is the effective mass of each photon, which depends on the mirror separation and on the linear index of the refraction of the medium (see
[
10
]
for details),
and

U({\bf r})={1\over 2}m_{\textrm{eff}}\omega^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})

(26)

is the 2D harmonic potential with trapping frequency
\omega
.
The local number density operator is given by

{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})={\hat{\psi}}^{\dagger}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}),

(27)

while

{\hat{N}}=\int d^{2}{\bf r}\,{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})

(28)

is the total number operator.

The single-particle quantum mechanics in the 2D harmonic potential
(
26
) are described by the stationary Schrödinger equation

\left[-{\hbar^{2}\over 2m_{\textrm{eff}}}\nabla^{2}+U({\bf r})\right]\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r})=\epsilon_{\bf m}\,\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r})\;,

(29)

where
{\bf m}=(m_{x},m_{y})
and the eigenvalues are given by

\epsilon_{\bf m}=\hbar\omega(m_{x}+m_{y}+1)

with
m_{x},m_{y}=0,1,2,...
being the two natural quantum numbers, while

\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r})
are the corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions.
In particular, for the single-particle ground state, we have

\epsilon_{\bf 0}=\hbar\omega

and

\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})={1\over\sqrt{\pi}l_{H}}e^{-{x^{2}+y^{2}\over 2l_{H}^{2}}},

(30)

with

l_{H}=\sqrt{\hbar\over m_{\textrm{eff}}\omega}

the characteristic length of the harmonic confinement.

The field operator
{\hat{\psi}}({\bf r})
can be expanded in any orthonormal
basis. Here, we choose the basis of the eigenfunctions

\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r})
of the single-particle problem of Equation (
29
),
i.e.,

\displaystyle{\hat{\psi}}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r}),

(31)

\displaystyle{\hat{\psi}}^{\dagger}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}^{\dagger}\phi_{\bf m}^{\ast}({\bf r}),

(32)

where
{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}
and
{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}^{\dagger}
are the ladder operators
which, respectively, destroy and create a boson in the single-particle
quantum state
\phi_{\bf m}({\bf r})
. Inserting the Formulas (
31
)
and (
32
) into Equation (
25
), and using Equation (
29
) and the orthonormal property, we obtain

{\hat{H}}=\sum_{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\epsilon_{\bf m}\ {\hat{N}}_{\bf m}\;,

(33)

where

{\hat{N}}_{\bf m}={\hat{a}}_{\bf m}^{\dagger}{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}

(34)

is the number operator of the single-particle
quantum state. Similarly, we find

{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})=\sum_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\hat{a}}_{\bf m}^{\dagger}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}\phi_{\bf m}^{\ast}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}({\bf r}),

(35)

and clearly

{\hat{N}}=\sum_{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}{\hat{N}}_{\bf m}\;

(36)

is the total number operator of the bosons under investigation.

The Fock state
|n_{\bf m}\rangle
represents the occupation number
quantum state describing the number
n

of bosons that are in the single-particle quantum state
|{\bf m}\rangle
.
It satisfies the eigenvalue equation

{\hat{N}}_{\bf m}|n_{\bf m}\rangle=n_{\bf m}|n_{\bf m}\rangle\;.

(37)

We can also introduce a generic multi-mode Fock state
[
27
,
22
]

of our problem associated with
the set of occupation numbers

{\bf n}=\{n_{\bf m}\}=(n_{00},n_{01},n_{10},n_{11},\ ...),

(38)

as

|{\bf n}\rangle=\prod_{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|n_{\bf m}\rangle=|n_{00}\rangle\ |n_{01}\rangle\ |n_{10}\rangle\ |n_{11}\rangle\ ...

(39)

This state is characterized by
n_{00}
photons in the single-particle state
|00\rangle
,

n_{01}
photons in the single-particle state
|01\rangle
,

n_{11}
photons in the single-particle state
|11\rangle
, et cetera.
These multi-mode Fock states can be used to obtain
the following spectral resolution of the identity

{\hat{1}}=\sum_{\bf n}|{\bf n}\rangle\langle{\bf n}|\;.

(40)

III.2
Grand Canonical Formulation

In order to describe the phenomenon of the Bose–Einstein condensation in this
system, we start from the GCE, where the density operator is

\hat{D}=e^{-\beta({\hat{H}}-\mu{\hat{N}})},

(41)

and the probability of the set of occupation numbers
{\bf n}

is given by

P_{\textrm{GC}}({\bf n})=\frac{1}{{Z}_{\textrm{GC}}}\langle{\bf n}|\hat{D}|{\bf n}\rangle=\frac{1}{{Z}_{\textrm{GC}}}e^{-\beta\sum_{\bf m}(\epsilon_{\bf m}-\mu)n_{\bf m}},

(42)

where

{Z}_{\textrm{GC}}=Tr[e^{-\beta({\hat{H}}-\mu{\hat{N}})}]=\prod_{\bf m}\left[1-e^{-(\beta\lambda_{\bf m}+\kappa)}\right]^{-1}

(43)

and

(44)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\bf m}=\epsilon_{\bf m}-\epsilon_{\bf 0},

(45)

\displaystyle\kappa=\beta(\epsilon_{\bf 0}-\mu).

(46)

III.2.1
Equation of State

The grand canonical
thermal average of the total number operator reads

\langle{\hat{N}}\rangle_{GC}=\frac{Tr[{\hat{N}}e^{-\beta({\hat{H}}-\mu{\hat{N}})}]}{{Z}_{GC}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\ln{Z}_{\textrm{GC}},

(47)

where

-\ln{Z}_{\textrm{GC}}=\sum_{\bf m}\,\ln\left[1-e^{-(\beta\lambda_{\bf m}+\kappa)}\right],

(48)

and

\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf m}\rangle=\frac{1}{e^{(\beta\lambda_{\bf m}+\kappa)}-1}.

(49)

In order to proceed further, we now introduce the definition of the thermodynamic limit for our system of bosons confined by a harmonic potential. Following
[
6
]
, we consider the conditions

N\gg 1,\quad\omega\ll 1,\qquad N\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}=\rho=\text{finite},

(50)

where we have included
\hbar
into the definition of the pseudo-density
\rho
for simplicity. To keep the notation formally similar to the more familiar case of a system of particles in a box of volume
V
, as considered in the previous sections, we now introduce a pseudo-volume
V=1/(\hbar\omega)^{2}
so that the condition of the thermodynamic limit can be also written as
N\gg 1
and
V\gg 1
, with
\rho=N/V
fixed.

Next, separating from the sum in Equation (
48
) the
{\bf m}=0
term, we have

\ln Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,s)=-\ln\bigl(1-s\bigr)-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}J(s),

(51)

where we have defined
s=e^{-\kappa}=z\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\epsilon_{\bf 0}}\leq 1
, which plays the role of a fugacity in a rescaled reference frame with zero lowest energy. In the following, we will find it more convenient to consider the thermodynamic quantities as a function of
s
rather than
z
.
The function

J(s)\equiv-g_{3}(s)
, where
g_{n}(s)
denotes the Bose functions
[
17
]
, represents the sum over the excited states and is given by

J(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}dy\,y\,\ln\bigl(1-s\,\mathrm{e}^{-y}\bigr).

(52)

Using (
51
), we have

\langle\hat{N}\rangle_{\rm GC}=s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\ln Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,s)=\frac{s}{1-s}+N_{1}(\beta,s),

(53)

where

N_{1}(\beta,s)=\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}I(s),

(54)

with

I(s){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\equiv g_{2}(s)}=-s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}J(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}dy\,\frac{y}{s^{-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{y}-1}.

(55)

III.2.2
Bose-Einstein Phase Transition

The standard argument leading to the phenomenon of condensation is as follows: since
I^{\prime}(s)>0
,
the function
I(s)
is a monotonous increasing function of
s
,
so that, for any
\beta
, one has

N_{1}(\beta,s)\leq N_{\rm c}(\beta)=\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}I(1).

(56)

If
I(1)
is finite, as in our case
I(1)=\pi^{2}/6
,
N_{\rm c}(\beta)
is a finite
decreasing function of
\beta
so that, for any fixed integer number
N
, there exists a finite
\beta_{\rm c}(N)
, the inverse critical temperature,
such that

\beta_{\rm c}(N):\quad N_{\rm c}(\beta_{\rm c})=N.

(57)

To address the issue of the ensemble (in)equivalence in the condensed phase, we consider
the condition

\langle\hat{N}\rangle_{\rm GC}=N,

(58)

where
N
now plays the role of a control parameter in the corresponding CE (see also the derivation reported in Appendix B leading to Equation (
149
)).
Then, for
N>N_{\rm c}(\beta)
, or
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}(N)
,
the contribution from the lowest energy level in
Equation (
53
) cannot be neglected.
In terms of the pseudo-density
\rho
, the conditions (
56
) and (
57
) become

\rho_{1}(\beta,s)\leq\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}I(1),

(59)

and

\beta_{\rm c}(\rho):\quad\rho_{\rm c}(\beta_{\rm c})=\rho.

(60)

Then, from the condition (
58
), one has

\rho=\frac{1}{V}\frac{s}{1-s}+\rho_{1}(\beta,s),

(61)

where

\rho_{1}(\beta,s)=N_{1}(\beta,V,s)/V
.
We stress that, in Equation (
60
), the density
\rho
is an arbitrary parameter, as
N
in Equation (
57
). In Equation (
61
), the density
\rho
on the left-hand side refers to
the canonical ensemble
(\beta,V,N)
while the quantities on the right-hand side refer to the
grand canonical ensemble
(\beta,V,s)
.

From this structure, it is clear that, in order to study the thermodynamic limit correctly,
we must distinguish two regions: the first, where

1-s=O(1)
as
V\gg 1
, which is appropriate for
\beta<\beta_{\rm c}
, and the second, where
1-s=O(1/V)
as

V\gg 1
, describing the case
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
. In singular perturbation theory, this case represents an instance of a
boundary layer problem
with a
boundary layer
at
s=1
. The regions are called, respectively, the
outer
and
inner
region in boundary layer theory.
We note that the results discussed in Section
II.2
hold for
\beta<\beta_{\rm c}
above the critical temperature.

III.3
The Kac Kernel

We now study the behavior of the Kac kernel, which represents the mathematical transformation connecting corresponding quantities in the two ensembles. This allows us to address the issue related to the ensemble equivalence, in the case
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
.
The explicit form of the kernel was first derived in
[
17
]
in the case of a uniform system. Here, we obtain the same results for the case of the trapped gas following an alternative approach. In particular,
we have to consider what happens
in the region where
1-s=O(1/V)
as

V\gg 1
, and this can be achieved by performing a convenient change in variable prior to the thermodynamic limit.
To be specific, let us rewrite
K(N|z)
in the following form (see Equation (
137
) in the Appendix A for details):

K(N^{\prime}|z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}d\xi\,\xi^{-1-N^{\prime}}\,\frac{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,\xi z)}{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,z)}.

(62)

We use
N^{\prime}
to emphasize that it is a running argument unrelated to
z
. The fugacity
z
is related to
\langle{\hat{N}}\rangle_{GC}

imposing the condition (
61
)
but substituting
\rho
with
\bar{\rho}=\langle{\hat{N}}\rangle_{GC}/V
, namely

\bar{\rho}=\frac{1}{V}\frac{s}{1-s}+\rho_{1}(\beta,s).

(63)

We introduce the variable
\eta
through the relation

s=z\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\epsilon_{\bf 0}}=1-\eta/V
, where
\eta=O(1)
as
V\gg 1
. A similar change in variable must be carried out for
\xi
.
We have seen in Section
II.2
that, in the limit of large
N
, the integral is dominated
by a path parallel to the imaginary axis. This remains true also for
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
because

(\partial/\partial\xi)^{2}\ln Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,\xi z)>0
. Thus, we write

\xi=1+iv/V
, where
v=O(1)
as
V\gg 1
.
Using (
51
) and

\xi s=(1+iv/V)(1-\eta/V)\sim 1-(\eta-iv)/V+O(1/V^{2}),

(64)

we have

\begin{split}\ln\frac{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,\xi z)}{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,z)}&\sim-\ln\bigl((\eta-iv)/V\bigr)+\ln\bigl(\eta/V\bigr)\\
&-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}\Bigl[J(1-(\eta-iv)/V)-J(1-\eta/V)\Bigr]\\
&\sim-\ln\bigl(1-iv/\eta)-J^{\prime}(1)\frac{iv}{\beta^{2}}+O(1/V)\\
&\sim-\ln\bigl(1-iv/\eta)+i\rho_{\rm c}v+O(1/V),\end{split}

(65)

because, from Equations (
55
) and (
59
), it follows that
J^{\prime}(1)/\beta^{2}=-\rho_{\rm c}
.
Using now

\xi^{-N^{\prime}}=\mathrm{e}^{-N^{\prime}\ln(1+iv/V)}\sim\mathrm{e}^{-i\rho^{\prime}v},

(66)

where
\rho^{\prime}=N^{\prime}/V
, we have

\begin{split}K(N^{\prime}|z)&\sim\frac{1}{2\pi V}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dv\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})v}}{1-iv/\eta}\\
&\sim-\frac{\eta}{2\pi iV}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dv\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})v}}{v+i\eta}\\
&\sim\Delta\rho^{\prime}\,\eta\,\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})\,\eta}\,\theta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{c}),\end{split}

(67)

with
\Delta\rho^{\prime}=1/V
.
This expression can be written in terms of
\rho=N/V
, expressing

\eta=V(1-z\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\epsilon_{0}})
and using Equation (
61
). Indeed,
substituting
s=1-\eta/V
into Equation (
61
), we obtain

\rho=\frac{1-\eta/V}{\eta}+{{\rho}_{1}}(1-\eta/V,\beta)\sim\eta^{-1}+\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)+O(1/V),

(68)

and replacing
\eta^{-1}=\rho-\rho_{\rm c}
in Equation (
67
),
we finally have the explicit form of the kernel:

K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)\equiv K(N^{\prime}|z)/\Delta\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})/(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}}{\rho-\rho_{\rm c}}\,\theta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c}),

(69)

valid for
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
.
This result was obtained by Ziff et al. for the free ideal Bose gas in
[
17
]
, and the kernel
K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)
is known as Kac density.
In Appendix C, we give an alternative derivation of Equations (
67
) and (
69
) that makes use of the Laplace method.

The explicit expression of the kernel can be used to connect averages in the CE and GCE expressed in terms of
\rho

\langle f\rangle_{GC}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\,K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)\,\langle f\rangle_{C}(\rho^{\prime}).

(70)

In particular, it is easy to show that

\int d\rho^{\prime}\,K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)=1
, and that
the following relation holds:

(\rho-\rho_{c})^{k}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\,K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)\frac{(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{c})^{k}}{k!}.

(71)

The above result will play an important role in the study of the occupation number fluctuations
in the canonical and grand canonical ensembles.

Finally, we observe that, when expressed in the variable
z
, the kernel takes the form

K(N^{\prime}|z)/\Delta\rho^{\prime}=V(1-s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-V(1-s)(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})},

(72)

and therefore, in the limit
V\gg 1
with
1-s=O(1)
, i.e., for
\eta\gg 1
, it reduces to

K(N^{\prime}|z)=\Delta\rho^{\prime}\delta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})={\delta^{Kr}(N^{\prime}-N_{\rm c})},

(73)

where
\delta^{Kr}
is the Kronecker delta.
Thus, as expected, in the limit
\beta\to\beta_{\rm c}^{-}
, we recover the expression
(
24
) valid for
\beta<\beta_{\rm c}
, i.e., in the non-condensed phase.

III.4
Density Fluctuations and Grand Canonical Catastrophe

As mentioned before, the explicit form of the Kac kernel allows us to connect averages in the GCE with those in the CE.
Since the analytical expression of the Kac kernel is the same as in the case of the uniform system, the relations between density fluctuations in the CE and in the GCE coincide with those derived in
[
17
]
. We report here the formulae to keep the paper self-contained.

In particular, the average number of bosons in the condensed phase in the GCE is given by Equation (
49
) and therefore
one has, for large
V
,

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}=\frac{1}{s^{-1}-1}\sim V(\rho-\rho_{c}),

(74)

where we have used Equation (
68
), and the dependence on
T
is through
\rho_{c}
.
From the relations (
70
) and (
71
), we then obtain that the averages of
\hat{N}_{\bf 0}
are equal in the two ensembles

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{C}=\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}.

(75)

On the contrary, considering the mean square occupation number, in the GCE, one has

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{GC}=\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}+2\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}^{2}=V(\rho-\rho_{c})+2V^{2}(\rho-\rho_{c})^{2}

(76)

and therefore

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{GC}-\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}^{2}=V(\rho-\rho_{c})+V^{2}(\rho-\rho_{c})^{2}.

(77)

Exploiting again the Relation (
71
),
in the CE, one obtains

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{C}=\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{C}+\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{C}^{2}=V(\rho-\rho_{c})+V^{2}(\rho-\rho_{c})^{2},

(78)

and

\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{C}-\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{C}^{2}=V(\rho-\rho_{c}).

(79)

In particular, for the second-order correlation function (at zero time delay; see also Section
III.4.1
),
we have

{g^{(2)}(0)\equiv}\frac{\langle{\hat{N}}_{0}^{2}\rangle-\langle{\hat{N}}_{0}\rangle}{\langle{\hat{N}}_{0}\rangle^{2}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}1&\mbox{in the CE}\\
2&\mbox{in the GCE,}\\
\end{matrix}\right.

(80)

in agreement with what was observed in experiments
[
28
]
. Indeed, in Ref.
[
28
]
(see Figure 2b), experimental results are reported showing that
g^{(2)}(0)=2
, even in the low-temperature phase, if a larger reservoir size is considered, which corresponds to the realization of a genuine grand canonical conditions. On the other hand, when canonical conditions are realized, the same figure shows that fluctuations are damped, and
g^{(2)}(0)=1
is observed.

Moreover, for the intensive density fluctuations, one has

\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{C}-\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{C}^{2}}{V^{2}}=0\;\;\textrm{in the CE},

(81)

while

\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle_{GC}-\langle\hat{N}_{\bf 0}\rangle_{GC}^{2}}{V^{2}}=(\rho-\rho_{c})^{2}\;\;\textrm{in the GCE}.

(82)

The latter result is known as a grand canonical catastrophe
[
17
]
because of the counter-intuitive phenomenon of macroscopic fluctuations,
non-vanishing in the low temperature limit
T\to 0
.
This finding shows the inequivalence of ensembles at the level of fluctuations and
has been interpreted as an example where the GCE is not appropriate for computing averages
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
However, in the experimental setup with photons in the microcavity, where GCE conditions are realized,
macroscopic fluctuations have been actually observed
[
14
]
,
making the prediction of the GCE physically consistent
[
24
]
.

The mathematical origin of such huge fluctuations can be traced back to the specific form of the Kac kernel
connecting the CE and GCE,
as detailed in the previous subsection
.

III.4.1
Spatial Density–Density Correlation Function of the Condensate

We now discuss another quantity that is affected by the grand canonical catastrophe:
the spatial density–density correlation function of the Bose–Einstein condensate.

We start by noting that, for both the CE and GCE,
the one-body correlation function

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle

can be written in terms of ladder operators in this way:

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime}}\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf m}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}\rangle\phi^{*}_{\bf m}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}({\bf r}^{\prime})

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf 0}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf 0}}\rangle\phi_{\bf 0}^{*}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})+...

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle\phi_{\bf 0}^{*}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})+...\;,

where the dots represent the contributions of the excited states.

Working at zero temperature, where the effect due to the excited states
goes to zero, we find that

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle=\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle\phi_{\bf 0}^{*}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\;.

(84)

Previously, we have seen that, at zero temperature,

\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle=N

(85)

for both the CE and GCE.
Thus,

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle=N\,\phi_{\bf 0}^{*}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\;.

(86)

Recalling Equation (
30
), namely

\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})=e^{-|{\bf r}|^{2}/(2l_{H}^{2})}/(\pi^{1/2}l_{H})

with
l_{H}=\sqrt{\hbar/(m_{eff}\omega)}
, we eventually obtain

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle={N\over\pi l_{H}^{2}}\,e^{-(|{\bf r}|^{2}+|{\bf r}^{\prime}|^{2})/(2l_{H}^{2})}\;.

(87)

Setting
{\bf r}^{\prime}={\bf 0}
, the previous expression becomes

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf 0})\rangle={N\over\pi l_{H}^{2}}\,e^{-|{\bf r}|^{2}/(2l_{H}^{2})},

(88)

while, setting
{\bf r}^{\prime}={\bf r}
, we obtain the result

\langle{\hat{\psi}}^{+}({\bf r}){\hat{\psi}}({\bf r})\rangle=\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle={N\over\pi l_{H}^{2}}\,e^{-|{\bf r}|^{2}/l_{H}^{2}}\;.

(89)

We then study the density–density
correlation function
\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle
, which, for both the CE and GCE, can be written in terms of
ladder operators as follows:

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf m}^{\prime\prime},{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf m}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}{\hat{a}}^{+}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime}}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\rangle

(90)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{\bf m}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}({\bf r})\phi^{*}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}({\bf r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf 0}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf 0}}{\hat{a}}^{+}_{{\bf 0}}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf 0}}\rangle|\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})|^{2}|\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})|^{2}+...

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle|\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})|^{2}|\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})|^{2}+...\;,

where the dots
stand for the contributions of the excited states. Similarly,
we have

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf m},{\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf m}^{\prime\prime},{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf m}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime}}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\rangle

(91)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{\bf m}({\bf r})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime}}({\bf r})\phi^{*}_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\phi_{{\bf m}^{\prime\prime\prime}}({\bf r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{\bf 0}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf 0}}\rangle\langle{\hat{a}}^{+}_{{\bf 0}}{\hat{a}}_{{\bf 0}}\rangle|\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})|^{2}|\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})|^{2}+...

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle^{2}|\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})|^{2}|\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})|^{2}+...\;.

Thus,
considering the zero temperature limit for simplicity and neglecting
the effect due to the excited states, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle-\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle-\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle^{2}\right)

(92)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle|\phi_{\bf 0}({\bf r})|^{2}|\phi_{{\bf 0}}({\bf r}^{\prime})|^{2}\;.

By using our previous results of Equations (
81
) and (
82
),
which crucially depend on the statistical ensemble,
at zero temperature, we then have

{\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}^{2}\rangle-\langle{\hat{N}}_{\bf 0}\rangle^{2}\over V^{2}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\mbox{ in the CE}\\
\rho^{2}&\mbox{ in the GCE.}\\
\end{matrix}\right.

(93)

As a consequence, for the density–density correlation function
per particle
,
we finally obtain

\frac{\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle-\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle}{N}=0

(94)

in the CE,
and

\frac{\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle-\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}^{\prime})\rangle}{N}={N\over\pi^{2}l_{H}^{4}}e^{-(|{\bf r}|^{2}+|{\bf r}^{\prime}|^{2})/l_{H}^{2}},

(95)

in the GCE. Equation (
95
) is not translationally invariant,
as expected, due to the presence of the confining harmonic potential.
Moreover, setting
{\bf r}^{\prime}={\bf 0}
, we obtain

\frac{\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r}){\hat{\rho}}({\bf 0})\rangle-\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf r})\rangle\langle{\hat{\rho}}({\bf 0})\rangle}{N}=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\mbox{ in the CE}\\
\\
{N\over\pi^{2}l_{H}^{4}}e^{-|{\bf r}|^{2}/l_{H}^{2}}&\mbox{ in the GCE},\\
\end{matrix}\right.

(96)

showing a decay to zero for a large
|{\bf r}|
with a correlation
length
l_{H}
, representing the size of the bosonic cloud, that diverges in the thermodynamic limit as
\omega\to 0
.
Note that the prefactor in the above expression remains finite in the thermodynamic limit.

III.5
Internal Energy and Specific Heat

To complete the description of the thermodynamic properties of the
system, we compute the internal energy and the specific heat in the normal and
condensed phases. These quantities have been measured in experiments
in
[
12
]
, where a comparison with a numerical
evaluation of the Bose–Einstein distribution function is
reported. Here, we provide close analytical expressions for average
energy and specific heat, valid both above and below the critical
temperature (see also ref.
[
29
]
for a similar
computation in a 3D system).

III.5.1
Grand Canonical Ensemble

The (average) internal energy of the system in the GCE is given by

\begin{split}E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,z)&=-{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{z}}\ln Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,z)\\
&={\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigr|_{\beta}}\left[\ln\bigl(1-s\bigr)+\frac{1}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}J(s)\right]\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{z}}s\\
&+{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{s}}\frac{1}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}J(s)\\
&=\overline{N}(\beta,z)\,\epsilon_{0}-2\frac{\beta^{-3}}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,J(s),\end{split}

(97)

where we have used

{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{z}}s=-\epsilon_{0}\,s,

(98)

and

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle s\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Bigr|_{\beta}}\left[\ln\bigl(1-s\bigr)+\frac{1}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}J(s)\right]=

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Bigr|_{\beta}}\ln Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,z)=\overline{N}(\beta,z)=\langle\hat{N}\rangle_{{\rm GC}}.

Note that zero-point energy goes to zero in the thermodynamic limit. Here, we keep this term because we take into account first-order corrections.

Evaluating the derivative, we have the explicit form

\overline{N}(\beta,z)=\frac{s}{1-s}+\frac{1}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}I(s).

(100)

In the above expressions,
J(s)
and
I(s)
are defined in Equations (
52
) and (
55
), respectively
.
Now using the condition
\langle\hat{N}\rangle_{{\rm GC}}=N
, we have

E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,z)=N\,\epsilon_{0}-2\frac{\beta^{-3}}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,J(s).

(101)

When
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
, i.e.,
T<T_{\rm c}
, the variable
s
saturates to
s=1
, so we have

E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,N)=\begin{cases}N\hbar\omega+2\dfrac{\zeta(3)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-3},&\beta>\beta_{\rm c}\\
\phantom{xx}\\
N\hbar\omega-2\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-3},&\beta<\beta_{\rm c}\\
\end{cases}

(102)

where we have used
J(1)=-\zeta(3)
. In the regime
\beta<\beta_{\rm c}
, the variable

s\equiv s(\beta,N)
is obtained from

\frac{1}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}I(s)=N,\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad I(s)=N(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}.

(103)

Using

\frac{1}{\hbar\omega}=\sqrt{\frac{N}{I(1)}}\,\beta_{\rm c}=\sqrt{\frac{6N}{\pi^{2}}}\,\beta_{\rm c},

(104)

we can express (
102
) in terms of
N
and
T_{\rm c}
. A simple calculation leads to

\frac{E_{\rm GC}}{NT_{\rm c}}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}\Bigr[\dfrac{1}{N^{1/2}}+\dfrac{12\,\sqrt{6}}{\pi^{3}}\,\zeta(3)\,\bigl(T/T_{\rm c}\bigr)^{3}\Bigr],&T<T_{\rm c}\\
\phantom{xx}\\
\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}}\Bigr[\dfrac{1}{N^{1/2}}-\dfrac{12\,\sqrt{6}}{\pi^{3}}\,J(s)\,\bigl(T/T_{\rm c}\bigr)^{3}\Bigr],&T>T_{\rm c}\\
\end{cases}

(105)

where
s=s(T,T_{\rm c})
for
T>T_{\rm c}
is the solution of
I(s)=(\pi^{2}/6)(T_{c}/T)^{2}
.

Having the expression of the (average) internal energy, we can compute the specific heat.
From (
102
), we have, for
\beta>\beta_{\rm c}
,

{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,N)=-6\dfrac{\zeta(3)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-4}

(106)

which, using
(\partial/\partial T)=-\beta^{2}(\partial/\partial\beta)
, leads to

{\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(T,N)=6\dfrac{\zeta(3)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,T^{2}=6N\dfrac{\zeta(3)}{I(1)}\,\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm{}_{c}}}\right)^{2},

(107)

Then,

c(T)=\frac{1}{N}{\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(T,N)=6\dfrac{\zeta(3)}{I(1)}\,\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm{}_{c}}}\right)^{2}

(108)

or

c(T)=\dfrac{36\,\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}\,\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm{}_{c}}}\right)^{2},\qquad T<T_{\rm c}.

(109)

This analytical result is in agreement with the very recent finding of ref.
[
30
]
.

For temperature
T{>}T_{\rm c}
, the calculation is a bit more involved. Again from
(
102
), we have, for
\beta<\beta_{\rm c}
,

\begin{split}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,N)&=6\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-4}-2\dfrac{J^{\prime}(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-3}\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}s\\
&=6\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-4}+2\dfrac{I(s)}{s(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-3}\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}s\end{split}

(110)

or, using (
103
),

{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,N)=6\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-4}+2\dfrac{N}{s}\,\beta^{-1}\,{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}s.

(111)

Now from (
103
), for
N=const
, we have

-2\dfrac{I(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\beta^{-3}\,d\beta+\dfrac{I^{\prime}(s)}{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,ds=0,

(112)

and then

{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}s=2\dfrac{I(s)}{I^{\prime}(s)}\beta^{-1},

(113)

with

\begin{split}I^{\prime}(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0^{+}}^{+\infty}dy\,\frac{y}{s^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{y}-1}=\int_{0^{+}}^{+\infty}dy\,\frac{y\,\mathrm{e}^{-y}}{(1-s\,\mathrm{e}^{-y})^{2}}\\
=-\frac{1}{s}\ln(1-s).\end{split}

(114)

Then,

{\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(\beta,N)=6\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-4}-4N\dfrac{I(s)}{\ln(1-s)}\,\beta^{-2},

(115)

which, with (
103
), gives

{\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(T,N)=-6\dfrac{J(s)}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}\,\beta^{-2}+4N^{2}\dfrac{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}{\ln(1-s)},\qquad T>T_{\rm c}.

(116)

so that, for
T>T_{\rm c}
,

c(T)=\frac{1}{N}{\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\Bigr|_{N}}E_{{\rm GC}}(T,N)=-6\dfrac{J(s)}{N(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}+4N\dfrac{(\beta\hbar\omega)^{2}}{\ln(1-s)}.

(117)

If we finally express
\hbar\omega
in terms of
N
and
T_{\rm c}
,
i.e., using Equation (
104
), we obtain

III.5.2
Canonical Ensemble

The same expression of the average internal energy, and therefore of the specific heat, also hold in the CE. Let us prove it.

From Equation (
9
)
, we know that

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle_{{\rm GC}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N^{\prime}\geq 0}\langle f\rangle_{{\rm C}}(N^{\prime})\,K(N^{\prime}|s)

(119)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N^{\prime}\geq 0}\Delta\rho^{\prime}\,\langle f\rangle_{{\rm C}}(N^{\prime})\,\dfrac{K(N^{\prime}|s)}{\Delta\rho^{\prime}},

where
\Delta\rho^{\prime}=1/V
; thus, in the thermodynamic limit, we have

\langle f\rangle_{{\rm GC}}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\,\langle f\rangle_{{\rm C}}(\rho^{\prime})\,K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho),

(120)

where
s=s(\rho)=1-\eta/V
with
\eta^{-1}=\rho-\rho_{\rm c}
, and

K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)\equiv K(N^{\prime}|s)/\Delta\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})/(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}}{\rho-\rho_{\rm c}}\,\theta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c}).

(121)

From Equation (
105
), neglecting the first term irrelevant for
N\to\infty
, we have

\dfrac{E}{NT_{\rm c}}=-\dfrac{2}{I(1)}\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}\,J\bigl(s(\rho)\bigr).

(122)

Then, substituting

\begin{split}J(1-\eta/V)&\sim J(1)-J^{\prime}(1)\,\eta/V+O(1/V^{2})\\
&\sim J(1)+I(1)\,\eta/V.+O(1/V^{2}),\quad V\to\infty,\end{split}

(123)

it follows that, in the grand canonical ensemble,

\dfrac{E_{\rm GC}}{NT_{\rm c}}\sim\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\zeta(3)\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}-2\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}\,\frac{1}{V(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}+O(1/V^{2}),

(124)

where we have used
J(1)=-\zeta(3)
and
I(1)=\pi^{2}/6
.

Using this expression in Equation (
120
) with the explicit form of the kernel, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\rho_{\rm c}}^{+\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})/(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}}{\rho-\rho_{\rm c}}\dfrac{E_{\rm C}(\rho^{\prime})}{NT_{\rm c}}\\
\sim\dfrac{E_{0}}{NT_{\rm c}}-2\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}\,\frac{1}{V(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}+O(1/V^{2})\end{split}

(125)

where

\dfrac{E_{0}}{NT_{\rm c}}=\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\zeta(3)\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}.

(126)

Now, using the normalization of
K(\rho^{\prime}|\rho)
,

\int_{\rho_{\rm c}}^{+\infty}d\rho^{\prime}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})/(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})}}{\rho-\rho_{\rm c}}=1,

(127)

it follows that

\dfrac{E_{\rm C}(\rho^{\prime})}{NT_{\rm c}}\sim\dfrac{E_{0}}{NT_{\rm c}}-2\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}\,\frac{1}{V}\,\delta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{c})+O(1/V^{2}),

(128)

as one can check by substituting back the above expression into Equation (
125
).

Recalling that
1/V=\Delta\rho^{\prime}
and using the identity

\Delta\rho^{\prime}\delta(\rho^{\prime}-\rho_{\rm c})=\delta^{\rm Kr}(N^{\prime}-N_{\rm c}),

(129)

we have

\dfrac{E_{\rm C}(N^{\prime})}{NT_{\rm c}}\sim\dfrac{E_{0}}{NT_{\rm c}}-2\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3}\,V\,\delta^{\rm Kr}(N^{\prime}-N_{\rm c})+O(1/V^{2}).

(130)

The number
N
in the grand canonical ensemble and
N^{\prime}
in the canonical ensemble
are related by the requirement
N\equiv\langle\hat{N}\rangle_{{\rm GC}}=N^{\prime}
;
hence, the Kronecker delta vanishes and we conclude that

\dfrac{E_{\rm C}(N,T)}{NT_{\rm c}}=\dfrac{E_{\rm GC}(N,T)}{NT_{\rm c}}=\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\zeta(3)\left(\dfrac{T}{T_{\rm c}}\right)^{3},\qquad T<T_{\rm c}.

(131)

in the thermodynamic limit.

IV
Conclusions

We have discussed a general formalism to derive physical quantities in the canonical ensemble
from the corresponding ones in the grand canonical ensemble, where the calculations are usually much simpler.
Then,
motivated by recent experiments with photons
, we have applied this formalism to the study of an ideal Bose gas of particles under harmonic confinement in two
spatial dimensions. Quite remarkably, also working in the thermodynamic limit, density fluctuations
and spatial density–density correlations of the Bose–Einstein condensate display a strongly different
behavior in the two ensembles. Similar to previous predictions for the uniform Bose gas
[
17
]
,
for the non-uniform condensate, we find that the density–density correlation is zero in the canonical
ensemble and non-zero in the grand canonical ensemble. This result, which is known as the grand canonical
catastrophe because of the counter-intuitive phenomenon of non-vanishing macroscopic fluctuations
in the low temperature limit
T\to 0
, turns out to be a real phenomenon as it has in fact been observed in experiments with photons in the microcavity
[
14
]
. Our study sheds new light on the underlying mathematical and physical mechanisms that induce this intriguing behavior.
In the last part of this paper, we have also obtained analytical formulas for the internal energy and the specific heat
both in the condensed phase and in the normal phase.
Similar results, obtained with a fully numerical procedure, can be found in ref.
[
12
]
.
For these quantities, we have provided explicit analytical expressions,
including also finite-size effects. The comparison with the experimental data of ref.
[
12
]

shows a good agreement between our analytical theory and the empirical results.

J^{\prime}(1)/\beta^{2}=-\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)
; thus,

\begin{split}\phi(s^{*})&\sim[\rho-\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)]\eta/V-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}J(1)+\ldots\\
&\sim 1/V-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}J(1)+\ldots.\end{split}

(186)

In conclusion, the leading term
\ln Z_{\rm C}(\beta,V,N)/V
as
V\to\infty
is

Z_{\rm C}(\beta,V,N)\sim\alpha_{0}^{N}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}\,J(1)}\qquad V\to\infty.

(187)

We can now go back to (
155
), which, using (
187
), becomes

K(N|z)\sim\frac{(z\alpha_{0})^{N}}{Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,V,z)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}\,J(1)},\qquad V\to\infty.

(188)

Now, recalling that the fugacity
z
is related to
\langle N\rangle_{\rm GC}
via (
63
),

(z\alpha_{0})^{N}=\mathrm{e}^{N\ln(z\alpha_{0})}\sim\mathrm{e}^{N\ln(1-\overline{\eta}/V)}\sim\mathrm{e}^{-\rho\,\overline{\eta}},

(189)

because
\alpha_{0}z=1-\overline{\eta}/V
with
\eta^{-1}=\overline{\rho}-\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)
.
Similarly,

\begin{split}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}\,J(1)}Z_{\rm GC}(\beta,V,s)^{-1}&=(1-\alpha_{0}z)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}\bigl[J(1)-J(z\alpha_{0})\bigr]}\\
&\sim\frac{\overline{\eta}}{V}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{\beta^{2}}J^{\prime}(1)\,\overline{\eta}/V}\sim\frac{\overline{\eta}}{V}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\rho_{\rm c}(\beta)\,\overline{\eta}},\qquad V\to\infty.\end{split}

(190)

Thus, collecting all the terms we have, cfr. (
67
) and (
69
),

\begin{split}K(N|z)=K(\rho|\overline{\rho})\,\Delta\rho&\sim\frac{\overline{\eta}}{V}\,\mathrm{e}^{-[\rho-\rho_{\rm c}]\,\overline{\eta}}\\
&\sim\Delta\rho\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\rho-\rho_{\rm c})/(\overline{\rho}-\rho_{\rm c})}}{\overline{\rho}-\rho_{\rm c}},\qquad\rho>\rho_{\rm c}(\beta),\ V\to\infty.\end{split}

(191)

where
\Delta\rho=1/V
.

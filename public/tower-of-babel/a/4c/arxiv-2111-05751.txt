On a girth–free variant of the Bourgain–Gamburd machine This work is supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant 19–11–00001.
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

A variant of the Bourgain–Gamburd machine without using any girth bounds is obtained.
Also, we find series of applications of the Bourgain–Gamburd machine
to problems of Additive Combinatorics, Number Theory and Probability.

1
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number and
A\subseteq{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a set of matrices.
Suppose for simplicity that
A=A^{-1}
, that is,
A
is a symmetric set. One can consider the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)
(here
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)=({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),E)
and the set of edges
E
is defined as
(x,y)\in E
iff
y=xa
,
a\in A
) of the set
A
and study the properties of
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)
.
It is a
fundamental
problem to show that
A
is an expander
[
13
]
,
[
10
]
under some conditions on
A
.
Equivalently,
we want to estimate nontrivially the operator norm of all representations of the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of
A
, i.e.,
\widehat{A}(\rho)
,
\rho\neq 1
.
In
[
1
]
(also, see
[
2
]
) Bourgain and Gamburd obtained

Theorem 1

Let
A\subseteq{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a generating set,
A=A^{-1}
,
\tau\in(0,1)
be a real number and

\mathrm{girth}({\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)\geqslant\tau\log_{|A|}p\,.

Then
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)
is an expander.

Recall that the girth of a finite graph is the length of its minimal cycle.
The proof of the result above involves some calculations with free groups
[
1
]
.
Moreover, usually in applications,
Theorem
1

is used for a set
of generators
A\subseteq{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that

A
generates a free subgroup of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, e.g.,

A=\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&s\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\,,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
t&1\end{array}\right)\right\rangle\,,\quad\quad|st|\geqslant 4

(1)

or for a randomly chosen
A
(it is known that for such
A
the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),A)
has large girth), see
[
1
]
.
To demonstrate transparently the strength of the result above we formulate a consequence of Theorem
1
, which was obtained in
[
15
]
(actually, in the only case
g(x)=-1/x
, see the complete proof in Theorem
8
below) and which was found further applications to the Zaremba conjecture in
[
16
]
.
Recall that
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
acts on the projective line via Möbius transformations:
x\to gx=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}
, where
g=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)
.

Theorem 2

Let
N\geqslant 1
be a sufficiently large integer,
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be sets, and
g\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a non–linear map.
Then there is
an absolute
constant
\kappa>0
such that

|\{g(c+a)=c+b~:~c\in 2\cdot[N],\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}|-\frac{|A||B|N}{p}\ll_{g}\sqrt{|A||B|}N^{1-\kappa}\,.

(2)

In particular, the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),S)
, where

S=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-2j\\
0&1\end{array}\right)g\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&2j\\
0&1\end{array}\right)~:~1\leq j\leq N\right\}\subset{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\,,

is an expander with
\|\widehat{S}(\rho)\|\ll|S|^{1-\kappa}
,

\kappa>0
for all non–trivial unitary irreducible representations
\rho\neq 1
.

As we said before the proof of Theorem

2

uses some calculations
in
free groups (one should take

s=t=2
in (
1
)),
as well as some
good lower bounds for the girth of the correspondent Cayley graph.
We avoid
to use
this technique
and obtain a more general (in the sense that the set
S
below can be much more general than
(c,c)
,
c\in 2\cdot[N]
) and more applicable result.

Theorem 3

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
,

N\geqslant 1
be a sufficiently large integer,
N\leqslant p^{c\delta}
for an absolute constant
c>0
,

A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be sets, and
g\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a non–linear map.
Suppose that
S
is a set,

S\subseteq[N]\times[N]
,
|S|\geqslant N^{1+\delta}
.
Then there is
a
constant
\kappa=\kappa(\delta)>0
such that

|\{g(\alpha+a)=\beta+b~:~(\alpha,\beta)\in S,\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}|-\frac{|S||A||B|}{p}\ll_{g}\sqrt{|A||B|}|S|^{1-\kappa}\,.

(3)

In Sections
4
,
5
we find several applications of Theorems
2
,
3
to problems of Incidence Geometry in
\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}
(Theorem
3
itself is a result about incidences between hyperbolae from
S
and Cartesian product
A\times B
)
and Probability.
We obtain a series of new upper bounds for some equations over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

(e.g., see the third part of Theorem
39
below).

Corollary 4

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a set,
|X|\leqslant 3p/4
, and
g
be a non–linear map.
Then there is an absolute constant
c>0
such that
for all
s\neq 0
either one has
|X\cap(X+s)|\leqslant(1-c)|X|
or

|g(X)\cap(g(X)+s^{-1})|\leqslant(1-c)|X|
.

The main point is the uniformity on
s
in Corollary
4
.
Also, we give a new optimal bound for the mixing time of a Markov chain, see Theorem
12
.
Finally, let us remark that Theorem
3
implies the main result of
[
16
]
concerning Zaremba’s conjecture from the theory of continued fractions (in this case one should choose
S=[N]\times[N]
).
Our argument of the proof of Theorem
3
is similar to the approach of paper
[
21
]
, where a modular form of Zaremba’s conjecture was proved, so such connection between the Bourgain–Gamburd machine and continued fractions is not very surprising but it nevertheless was not widely known.

The last Section of our paper
concerns some further applications of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
–actions to the question about
the intersections of additive shifts of multiplicative subgroups in
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
.
Consider the simplest multiplicative subgroup in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
, namely, the set of quadratic residues

R=\{x^{2}~:~x\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\}\,.

The set
R
is a good constructive model for the randomly chosen subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
(each element of the set is taken with probability
1/2
).
For example,
Vinogradov
[
28
]
considered the minimal distance
d(p)
between quadratic residues
and
it was conjectured that

d(p)\ll p^{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon>0
is any number.
The first non–trivial results in this direction were obtained in
[
17
]
,
[
27
]
.
In
[
5
]
it was proved that
d(p)\ll p^{1/4}(\log p)^{3/2}
and the best result at the moment is (see
[
6
]
)

d(p)\ll p^{1/4}\log p\,.

(4)

First of all, we show in the Appendix that bound (
4
) can be obtained using a combinatorial method,
which
differs from the classical approach and its variations, see
[
5
]

and
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.
We need the only consequence of the Weil bound on multiplicative character, namely, that for any different non–zero shifts
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
one has

|R_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}|:=|R\cap(R-s_{1})\cap\dots\cap(R-s_{k})|\leqslant\frac{p}{2^{k+1}}+k\sqrt{p}\,.

(5)

(a similar result for general multiplicative subgroups is contained in
[
23
]
).
In particular, if
k\sim\log p
, then the intersection of the additive shifts from (
5
) is
O(\sqrt{p}\log p)
.
Further, applying the Bougain–Gamburd machine and using
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
–actions, we break this square–root barrier for larger number of shifts, see Section
5
.
More precisely, having an arbitrary set of shifts
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}
, we want to expand this set constructively (that is, any random choice is forbidden) in the spirit of paper
[
3
]
and find
\overline{S}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k},t_{1},\dots,t_{K}\}
,
t_{j}=t_{j}(S)

such that
|R_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k},t_{1},\dots,t_{K}}|=o(\sqrt{p})
.
Our
simple but crucial
observation is that the group

{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
acts transparently on the sets
R_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}
(more generally, on sets of the form

\Gamma_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}
defined as in (
5
) or even on sets
(\alpha_{1}\Gamma+\beta_{1})\cap\dots\cap(\alpha_{k}\Gamma+\beta_{k})
,
\Gamma<\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
,
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
,
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
), e.g.,

R^{-1}_{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}}=R_{s^{-1}_{1},\dots,s^{-1}_{k}}\,,

(6)

provided
s_{j}\in R
.
Hence our additive/multiplicative problem on size of intersection of additive shifts of
R
can be treated via the methods of growth in
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
as in
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
22
]
etc.
The growth in
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is known to be very fast (as, e.g., Theorems
2
,
3
show this) and we apply the Bourgain–Gamburd machine to obtain some results in the direction in Section
5
.
Let us formulate a result
of this Section.

Theorem 5

Let
p
be a prime number,
n
be a positive integer,
\Gamma<\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
be a multiplicative subgroup,
\Gamma=-\Gamma
, and
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be an arbitrary set.
Then there is a constructive set
T=T(S)

(namely, defined in (
47
), (
48
) below),
|T|\leqslant 2^{n}|S|+n
such that
S\subseteq T
and

|\Gamma_{T}|\leqslant(1-c)^{n}|\Gamma_{S}|\,,

where
c>0
is an absolute constant, which does not depend on
S
and
T
.

The bound
|T|\leqslant 2^{n}|S|+n
is perhaps
non–optimal (the author believes in
|T|=O(|S|+n)
) and, probably, should take place for an arbitrary set
T
.
Nevertheless, it seems like that it is the first result of such type.

remarks.

Definitions

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite group with the identity
1
.
Given two sets
A,B\subset{\mathbf{G}}
, define the
product set
of
A
and
B
as

AB:=\{ab~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the higher product sets, e.g.,
A^{3}
is
AAA
.
Let
A^{-1}:=\{a^{-1}~:~a\in A\}
.
As usual, having two subsets
A,B
of a group
{\mathbf{G}}
, denote by

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|\{(a,a_{1},b,b_{1})\in A^{2}\times B^{2}~:~a^{-1}b=a^{-1}_{1}b_{1}\}|

the
common energy
of
A
and
B
.
If
A=B
, then we write
\mathsf{E}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}(A,A)
.
Clearly,
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\mathsf{E}(B,A)
and by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\mathsf{E}(A,B)|A^{-1}B|\geqslant|A|^{2}|B|^{2}\,.

(7)

To underline the group operation
*
we write
\mathsf{E}^{*}(A)
, e.g.,
\mathsf{E}^{+}(A)
or
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)
for
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{R}
or
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{C}
, say, considered with the addition
+
or with the multiplication
\times
.
We use representation function notations like
r_{AB}(x)
or
r_{AB^{-1}}(x)
, which counts the number of ways
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
can be expressed as a product
ab
or
ab^{-1}
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, respectively.
In a similar way,
r_{ABC}(x)
counts the number of ways
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
can be expressed as a product
abc
, where
a\in A
,
b\in B
,
c\in C
etc.
For example,
|A|=r_{AA^{-1}}(1)
and
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=r_{AA^{-1}BB^{-1}}(1)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbf{G}}}r^{2}_{A^{-1}B}(x)
.
In this paper we use the same letter to denote a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and its characteristic function
A:{\mathbf{G}}\to\{0,1\}
.

Let
g\in{\mathbf{G}}
and let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be any set.
Then put
A^{g}=g^{-1}Ag
and, similarly, let
x^{g}:=g^{-1}xg
, where
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
If
H\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
is a subgroup, then we
use the notation

H\leqslant{\mathbf{G}}
and
H<{\mathbf{G}}
if, in addition,
H\neq{\mathbf{G}}
.
Having a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
we use the symbol
\langle A\rangle

to denote
the subgroup, generated by
A
.

We write
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
for
\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
, where
q=p^{s}
,
p
is a prime number.
In the paper we consider the group
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of matrices

g=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)=(ab|cd)=(a,b|c,d)\,,\quad\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\,,\quad\quad\det(g)=ad-bc=1\,.

We need two specific subgroups of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, namely,

\mathbf{B}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&s\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\,,\quad\quad\mathbf{U}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&s\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\,,\quad\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\,s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\,.

By
g^{*}
denote the transpose of a matrix
g
.
Also, let us fix the notation for a unipotent and the Weyl element of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, namely,

u_{s}:=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&s\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\mathbf{U}\,,~~s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\,,\quad\quad\quad w:=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}}\right)\,.

Having a matrix
g=(ab|cd)\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, we write
\|g\|
for
\max\{|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\}
.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
All logarithms are to base
2
.
If we have a set
A
, then we will write
a\lesssim b
or
b\gtrsim a
if
a=O(b\cdot\log^{c}|A|)
,

.

On the Bourgain–Gamburd machine

In this Section we obtain Theorems
2
,
3
from the Introduction. We start with a consequence of the ping–pong lemma (e.g., see
[
14
]
) applied to some
{\rm SL}_{2}
–actions on
\mathbb{C}
.
The second part of Lemma
6
corresponds to the uniqueness of the continued fraction expansion.

Lemma 6

Let
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}[i]

be some numbers.
Suppose that
|s|,|t|\geqslant 2
.
Then there is no non–trivial words of the form
\lambda I
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with the letters
u^{*}_{s}
and
u_{t}
.
In particular, the subgroup of
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[i])
generated by
u^{*}_{s}
and
u_{t}
is free.

If we have any matrix
z=(ab|cd)
from
\langle u^{*}_{s},u_{t}\rangle
, then we can reconstruct
z
via
(b,d)
.

P r o o f.

Put
g=u^{*}_{s}
and
h=u_{t}
.
We will use the ping–pong lemma, e.g., see
[
14
]
.
Let
A=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~:~|y|>|x|\}
and
B=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}~:~|x|>|y|\}
,
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Notice that both regions are invariant under multiplication by any non–zero number
\lambda
:
(x,y)\to\lambda(x,y)
.
Then for
(x,y)\in B
and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
one has
g^{n}(x,y)=(x,snx+y)
and since

|snx+y|\geqslant|s||n||x|-|y|>(|s||n|-1)|x|\geqslant|x|\,,

it follows that
g^{n}(x,y)\in A
.
Similarly, taking
(x,y)\in A
and an arbitrary
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, we derive
h^{m}(x,y)=(x+tmy,y)
and thus
h^{m}(x,y)\in B
.
Having a non–trivial word
g^{n_{1}}h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}=\lambda I
in our alphabet
\{g,h\}
, we obtain for any
b\in B

B\ni\lambda\cdot Ib=g^{n_{1}}h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}b\in A

and this is a contradiction.
If
g^{n_{1}}h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}h^{m_{k}}=\lambda I
(and, similarly,
h^{n_{1}}g^{m_{1}}\dots h^{n_{k}}g^{n_{k}}=\lambda I
), then conjugating by an
g^{n}
,
n\neq n_{1},0
, we obtain

g^{n_{1}-n}h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}h^{n_{k}}g^{n}=\lambda I
and it contradicts the previous calculations.

Let
z\in\langle g,h\rangle
be a non–trivial word in our alphabet
\{g,h\}
,
z=(ab|cd)
.
Suppose that
z=h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}h^{m_{k}}

and hence
(b,d)^{*}=h^{m_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}(m_{k},1)^{*}
.
If we have
(b,d)^{*}=h^{m^{\prime}_{1}}\dots g^{n^{\prime}_{l}}(m^{\prime}_{l},1)^{*}
for other numbers
m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,n^{\prime}_{l},m^{\prime}_{l}
, then

g^{-n^{\prime}_{l}}\dots h^{m_{1}-m^{\prime}_{1}}\dots g^{n_{k}}(m_{k},1)^{*}=(m^{\prime}_{l},1)^{*}

and this is a contradiction because the right–hand side belongs to
B
but the left–hand side (if it is non–trivial) belongs to
A
.
If
z=h^{m^{\prime}_{1}}\dots g^{m^{\prime}_{l-1}}h^{m^{\prime}_{l-1}}g^{n^{\prime}_{l}}
, then

(b,d)^{*}=h^{m^{\prime}_{1}}\dots g^{m^{\prime}_{l-1}}(m^{\prime}_{l-1},1)^{*}
and we use the same argument.
Other words
z
can be considered similarly.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we obtain a generalization of Theorem
2
from the Introduction.

Theorem 7

Let
N\geqslant 1
be a sufficiently large integer,
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
be sets, and
g\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\setminus\mathbf{B}
.
Then there is
an absolute

\kappa>0
such that

|\{g(c+a)=c+b~:~c\in[N],\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}|-\frac{|A||B|N}{p}\ll_{g}\sqrt{|A||B|}N^{1-\kappa}\,.

P r o o f.

We use slightly more general arguments to use it in the proofs of the results below.
Let us remind that for
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
we write
u_{t}=(1t|01)
.
By the Bruhat decomposition and the condition
g\notin\mathbf{B}
we can write
g=u_{t_{1}}wdu_{t_{2}}
,

d=(\lambda 0|0\lambda^{-1})
and
w=(0(-1)|10)
.
Let
S=\{(c,c)~:~c\in J\}\subseteq[N]^{2}
.
In this terms our equation from (
8
) can be written as (let

h_{\alpha,\beta}\,a:=u_{t_{1}-\beta}wdu_{\alpha+t_{2}}\,a=u_{-\beta}gu_{\alpha}\,a=b\,.

(9)

We split the set of all pairs

onto congruence classes modulo two.
Thus
S
is a disjoint union of at most
4
sets
S_{ij}
,

and it is sufficient to obtain (
8
) for each set
S_{ij}
.
With some abuse of the notation we use the same letter

for

Let
H=\{h_{\alpha,\beta}~:~(\alpha,\beta)\in S\}\subseteq{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
It is easy to check that

h_{\alpha,\beta}h^{-1}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}=u_{t_{1}-\beta}wdu_{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}d^{-1}w^{-1}u_{\beta^{\prime}-t_{1}}=u_{t_{1}-\beta}u^{*}_{\lambda^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)}u_{\beta^{\prime}-t_{1}}\in HH^{-1}\,,

(10)

where

is the transpose of a matrix
M
.
For an arbitrary positive integer

has the form

u_{t_{1}-\beta_{1}}u^{*}_{\lambda^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}_{1}-\alpha_{1})}u_{\beta^{\prime}_{1}-\beta_{2}}u^{*}_{\lambda^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha_{2})}\dots u_{\beta^{\prime}_{k-1}-\beta_{k}}u^{*}_{\lambda^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}_{k}-\alpha_{k})}u_{\beta^{\prime}_{k}-t_{1}}\,.

(11)

Notice that one can easily remove left and right terms
u_{t_{1}}
,

and
t_{1}
,

.

[

]
or

.

Theorem 9

Let

N\leqslant p^{c\delta/k}

g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\setminus\mathbf{B}

Suppose that
S

any hyperplane of the form

P r o o f.

Let

5
On intersection of additive shifts of multiplicative subgroups

We begin with
deriving some further consequences of Theorem
2
.
Three parts of Theorem

below have the same spirit but there are some variations in parameters: we can take one or several shifts, we can control our shifts or not and, finally, there are several
upper bounds for
considered
intersections of different quality.
Also, let us remark
that in particular, inequality (
39
) (with
n=1
) shows that for any
s\neq 0
the set
\{1,u_{\pm s},u^{g}_{\pm s}\}
forms an expander in
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
Of course, Theorem 1 of
[
1
]
says that for any subset
S\subset{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}({\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),S)
is an expander iff the subgroup
\langle S\rangle
is non–elementary (that is,
\langle S\rangle
does not contain a solvable subgroup of finite index) and thus we can expect some properties of expansions of the set
\{1,u_{\pm s},u^{g}_{\pm s}\}
. Nevertheless, our Theorem
39
is a more delicate result, e.g., in the first part of this theorem the constant
c(\kappa)>0
does not depend on

(as
[
1
, Theorem 1]
guarantees).

Recall that we write
a^{g}
for
g^{-1}ag
and let by definition
a^{0}:=a
.
Below in this Section let
\kappa>0
be the absolute constant
from Theorem
2
or from Theorem

3
.

Theorem 17

Let
g\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be a non–linear map.
Also, let
\kappa>0
be the absolute constant from
Theorem
2
.
Then for any

X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and an integer parameter
N\geqslant 1
the following holds:

1)~
Suppose that

|X|\leqslant pN^{-\kappa}
.
Then there is
s\in 2\cdot[N]
such that

|(X+s)\cap g(X+s)|\leqslant|X|\cdot N^{-\kappa}\,.

(37)

In particular, for any positive integer
n
there are
s_{j}\in 2\cdot[N]
,
j\in[n]
with

\left|\bigcap_{\varepsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}}(g^{u_{s_{n}}})^{\varepsilon_{n}}\dots(g^{u_{s_{1}}})^{\varepsilon_{1}}X\right|\leqslant|X|\cdot N^{-\kappa n}\,.

(38)

2)~
If
Y:=g(X)
,

\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
be any real number and
|X|\leqslant pN^{-\kappa}
,
where

N\gg\varepsilon^{1/\kappa}

then either

|X\cap(X+2)\cap\dots\cap(X+2N)|\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)|X|\quad\mbox{ or }\quad|Y\cap(Y+2)\cap\dots\cap(Y+2N)|\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)|Y|\,.

3)~
As above put
Y:=g(X)
. If
|X|\leqslant 3p/4
, then for any
s\neq 0
either
|X\cap(X+s)|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))|X|
or
|Y\cap(Y+s^{-1})|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))|Y|
.

In particular, for any positive integer
n
the following holds

\left|\bigcap_{\varepsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}}u^{\varepsilon_{n}g}_{s^{1-2\varepsilon_{n}}_{n}}\dots u^{\varepsilon_{1}g}_{s^{1-2\varepsilon_{1}}_{1}}X\right|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))^{n}|X|\,.

P r o o f.

Let us begin with
1)
because it is just a direct application of Theorem
2

(thanks to this result we can even assume that
g(x)=1/x
, say).
Indeed, by formula (
2
), our assumption
|X|\leqslant pN^{-\kappa}
and the Dirichlet principle we can find

such that

|(X+s)\cap g(X+s)|\ll\frac{|X|^{2}}{p}+|X|N^{-\kappa}\ll|X|N^{-\kappa}

as required. Since
|(X+s)\cap g(X+s)|=|X\cap g^{u_{s}}X|
, we
get
(
38
) via iteration.

To obtain
2)
let us consider the sets
X_{*}:=X\cap(X+2)\cap\dots\cap(X+2N)\subseteq X
,

Y_{*}:=Y\cap(Y+2)\cap\dots\cap(Y+2N)\subseteq Y
and assume that
|X_{*}|\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)|X|
,
|Y_{*}|\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)|Y|
.
Since
Y=g(X)
and
X_{*}-2j\subseteq X
,
Y_{*}-2j\subseteq Y
,
\forall j\in[N]
, it follows that
|(Y_{*}-2j)\cap g(X_{*}-2j)|\geqslant 2\varepsilon|X|
for any

.
Hence the equation
y_{*}-2j=g(x_{*}-2j)
, where
j\in[N]
and
x_{*}\in X_{*}
,
y_{*}\in Y_{*}
has at least
2\varepsilon N|X|
solutions.
Again this contradicts formula (
2
) of Theorem
2
.

Finally, to get
3)
we use a variation of the argument from
[
9
]
and
[
22
]
.
Let
|X\cap(X+s_{1})|>(1-c)|X|
and
|Y\cap(Y+s_{2})|>(1-c)|Y|
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\neq 0
.
Dividing and redefining the sets
\tilde{X}=X/s_{1}
,
\tilde{Y}=Y/s_{2}
we can assume that
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
and let
\tilde{X}=\bigsqcup_{j\in J}I_{j}
, where
I_{j}
are some intervals with step one (and similar to the set
\tilde{Y}
).
Write

for a sufficiently small constant, which we will choose later.
From

|\tilde{X}\cap(\tilde{X}+1)|>(1-c)|X|
,

|\tilde{Y}\cap(\tilde{Y}+1)|>(1-c)|\tilde{Y}|
, we see that
|J|\leqslant c|X|
.
Put
L=|X|/|J|
and
let
\omega\in(0,1)
be a small parameter, which we will choose later.
One has
\sum_{j\in J}|I_{j}|=|X|
and hence
\sum_{j~:~|I_{j}|\geqslant\omega L}|I_{j}|\geqslant(1-\omega)|X|
.
Splitting
I_{j}
into intervals of length exactly

, we see that the rest is at most

.
Hence we have obtained some intervals

,
i\in I
, having lengths

and step one and such that
\sum_{i\in I}|I^{\prime}_{i}|\geqslant(1-2\omega)|X|
.
Put
X^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}I^{\prime}_{j}
.
Similarly, we construct
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
,
|Y^{\prime}|\geqslant(1-2\omega)|Y|
.
Then

|X|=|Y|=|Y\cap g(X)|=|s_{2}\tilde{Y}\cap g(s_{1}\tilde{X})|\leqslant|s_{2}Y^{\prime}\cap g(s_{1}X^{\prime})|+4\omega|X|\,.

(40)

It follows that
|s_{2}Y^{\prime}\cap g(s_{1}X^{\prime})|\geqslant(1-4\omega)|X|

Now our task is to obtain a good upper bound for the intersection.
Let
\mathcal{M}=[\zeta L_{\omega}]
, where
\zeta=2^{-6}
.
Thus
M:=|\mathcal{M}|\geqslant\zeta L_{\omega}/4
.
We have
|(I^{\prime}_{i}+m)\cap I^{\prime}_{i}|\geqslant(1-2\zeta)|I^{\prime}_{i}|

and hence
|(X^{\prime}+m)\cap X^{\prime}|\geqslant(1-2\zeta)|X^{\prime}|
.
Again, similarly, we obtain
|(Y^{\prime}+m)\cap Y^{\prime}|\geqslant(1-2\zeta)|Y^{\prime}|

Recalling

where we have chosen
\omega=\zeta=2^{-6}
.
From above, we get

\frac{7|X^{\prime}|M^{2}}{8}\leqslant|\{s_{1}(y^{\prime}+m_{1})=g(s_{2}(x^{\prime}+m_{2}))~:~x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime},\,y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime},\,m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{M}\}|\,.

g=(ab|cd)
,
c\neq 0
and
h_{s_{1},s_{2}}=(as_{2},b|s_{1}s_{2}c,ds_{1})
.
Then the last equation can be rewritten as

Now using
s_{1}s_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, we see that the lower left corner of

is
O(1)
. Thus we can apply Theorem
8
with
S=[M]\times[M]
(also, see Remark
8
) and obtain

\frac{7|X^{\prime}|M^{2}}{8}\leqslant\frac{M^{2}|X^{\prime}|^{2}}{p}+C_{*}|X^{\prime}|M^{2-\kappa}\,,

where
C_{*}>0
is an absolute constant,

M^{2}|X^{\prime}|\ll|X^{\prime}|M^{2-\kappa}\,.

(41)

Here we have used the assumption
|X^{\prime}|\leqslant|X|\leqslant 3p/4
.

41
) give us a contradiction for sufficiently large

, where
C>1
is an absolute constant.
Recall that
M\geqslant 2^{-8}L_{\omega}=2^{-15}L
.
Since
|J|\leqslant c|X|
and

, it follows that

M\geqslant 2^{-15}c^{-1}
. Taking

to be a sufficiently small number, we satisfy our condition

To obtain (
39
) we just apply 1) to derive
|X\cap u_{s}X|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))|X|
or

|g(X)\cap u_{s^{-1}}g(X)|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))|X|
for any
s\neq 0
. The second possibility is equivalent to
|X\cap u^{g}_{s^{-1}}X|\leqslant(1-c(\kappa))|X|
. In any case we have found a word
w\in\{u_{s},u^{g}_{s^{-1}}\}

such that

Remark 19

It is possible to consider general functions differ from Möbius transformation similarly
to paper

[
22
]
and obtain some analogues of Theorem
39
.
Nevertheless, it gives much weaker bounds for the considered intersections.
E.g., if a sufficiently small set
X\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
satisfies
X=1+1/(X-1)
, then for any
\lambda\neq 0
one has
|X\cap\lambda X|\leqslant(1-O(\exp(-\log p/\log\log p)))|X|
, see
[
22
, Theorem 1]
.

Now we apply the bounds above to multiplicative subgroups.

Let
\Gamma
be a multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
.
Also, let
k
be a positive integer.
Given non–zero numbers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
and any
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

consider a generalization of sets (
5
)

\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}=\Gamma_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k};\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}=(\alpha_{1}\Gamma+\beta_{1})\cap\dots\cap(\alpha_{k}\Gamma+\beta_{k})\,,

(42)

where
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
and
\bar{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})
.
In this Section we
want to obtain new upper bounds for size of the sets
\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}
.
The best current results
on additive shifts of multiplicative subgroups are contained in
[
23
]
. They say, basically, that for any sufficiently large
\Gamma\leqslant\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
,
|\Gamma|\leqslant p^{1-c}
one has
|\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}|\ll k|\Gamma|^{1/2+\varepsilon_{k}}
, where
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
We want somehow to break the ”square–root barrier”  (i.e.
\sqrt{|\Gamma|}
), which appears above.

First of all, let us show how a matrix
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
acts on sets of the form (
42
).

Lemma 20

Let
g=(ab|cd)\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
c\neq 0
,
k\geqslant 2
,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

Suppose that
\beta_{1}=g^{-1}(\infty)=-d/c

Then we have

g\Gamma_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k};\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}=\Gamma_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k};g(\infty),g(\beta_{2}),\dots,g(\beta_{k})}\,,

(43)

where

\gamma_{1}=-\frac{\det(g)}{c^{2}\alpha_{1}}
and

\gamma_{j}=\frac{\alpha_{j}\det(g)}{\alpha_{1}c(c\beta_{j}+d)}
,
j\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
.

P r o o f.

Let
|\Gamma|=t
and
g^{-1}=(AB|CD)=(d(-b)|(-c)a)\cdot\det^{-1}(g)
.
Take any
x\in g\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}
.
Then we have
g^{-1}x\in\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}
and this is equivalent to

\left(\frac{Ax+B}{Cx+D}-\beta_{j}\right)^{t}=\alpha^{t}_{j},\,\quad\quad j\in[k]

or, in other words,

(dx-b-\beta_{j}(-cx+a))^{t}=\alpha^{t}_{j}(-cx+a)^{t},\,\quad\quad j\in[k]

and hence

\left(x-\frac{a\beta_{j}+b}{c\beta_{j}+d}\right)^{t}=(x-g(\beta_{j}))^{t}=\alpha^{t}_{j}(-cx+a)^{t}(c\beta_{j}+d)^{-t}\,.

(44)

Let us show that the right–hand side of the last formula does not depend on
x
.
We know that
x\in g\Gamma_{\bar{\alpha};\bar{\beta}}
and in particular,
x\in g\Gamma_{\alpha_{1};\beta_{1}}
.
In view of our condition
\beta_{1}=-d/c
, we get

-cx+a=-c\cdot\frac{a(\alpha_{1}\omega+\beta_{1})+b}{c(\alpha_{1}\omega+\beta_{1})+d}+a=-c\cdot\frac{a(\alpha_{1}\omega+\beta_{1})+b}{c\alpha_{1}\omega}+a=-\frac{a\beta_{1}+b}{\alpha_{1}}\omega^{-1}\,,

where
\omega\in\Gamma
.
Hence
(-cx+a)^{t}=(-\frac{a\beta_{1}+b}{\alpha_{1}})^{t}
and thus (
44
) can be rewritten as

(x-g(\beta_{j}))^{t}=\left(\frac{-\alpha_{j}(a\beta_{1}+b)}{\alpha_{1}(c\beta_{j}+d)}\right)^{t}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{j}\det(g)}{\alpha_{1}c(c\beta_{j}+d)}\right)^{t}

for all
j\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
.
In other words,
x\in\Gamma_{\gamma_{j},g(\beta_{j})}
,
j>1

and, similarly,

g\Gamma_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}=\Gamma_{(a\beta_{1}+b)/c\alpha_{1};a/c}=\Gamma_{(a\beta_{1}+b)/c\alpha_{1};g(\infty)}=\Gamma_{-\frac{\det(g)}{c^{2}\alpha_{1}};g(\infty)}\,.

Notice that all
\gamma_{j}\neq 0
because
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and hence
\beta_{1}=-d/c\neq-b/a
.
Thus we have
obtained
the inclusion

g\Gamma_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k};\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}\subseteq\Gamma_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k};g(\infty),g(\beta_{2}),\dots,g(\beta_{k})}\,.

To get (
43
) apply the

inclusion and derive

|\Gamma_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k};g(\infty),g(\beta_{2}),\dots,g(\beta_{k})}|=|g^{-1}\Gamma_{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k};g(\infty),g(\beta_{2}),\dots,g(\beta_{k})}|\leqslant|\Gamma_{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\gamma^{\prime}_{k};g^{-1}(\infty),\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}}|\,,

(45)

where
\gamma^{\prime}_{j}
are some numbers, which we will calculate later.
Here we have used the fact that
g(\infty)=a/c=-D/C
and
a/c\neq-B/A
.
It is easy to check that

g^{-1}(\infty)=\frac{A}{C}=-\frac{d}{c}=\beta_{1}\,,

and thanks to

, we get

\gamma^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{Ag(\infty)+B}{C\gamma_{1}}=\frac{da/c-b}{-(a\beta_{1}+b)/\alpha_{1}}=\alpha_{1}\,.

Thus it remains to show that
\gamma^{\prime}_{j}=\alpha_{j}
for all

and then inequality (

) implies (

\Gamma_{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\gamma^{\prime}_{k};g^{-1}(\infty),\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}}=\Gamma_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k};\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}}
.
Taking

Lemma 24

Let
H,H_{*},a
be positive integers,
H_{*}\leqslant H
,
a+H<p
and

16H^{2}_{*}H<p
.
Then for all sufficiently large
H_{*}
the following holds

\left|\left\{\frac{y}{a+x}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}~:~(x,y)\in[H]\times[H_{*}]\right\}\right|\gg H_{*}H\,.

P r o o f.

We need to solve the equation

y(a+x^{\prime})\equiv y^{\prime}(a+x)\pmod{p}\,,\quad\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[H],\,y,y^{\prime}\in[H_{*}]\,.

Using the Dirichlet principle, we multiply the last expression by an appropriate

\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
such that
|\lambda|\leqslant\Delta
and for
A:=a\lambda\pmod{p}
one has
|A|\leqslant p/\Delta
, where our parameter
\Delta=(p/H)^{1/2}
.
Then we obtain the following equation in
\mathbb{Z}

y(A+\lambda x^{\prime})=y^{\prime}(A+\lambda x)

(50)

because by our assumption

2H_{*}(p/\Delta+\Delta H)=4H_{*}\sqrt{pH}<p\,.

In (
50
) we can assume that
(A,\lambda)=1
.
Let
S\subseteq[H]\times[H_{*}]
be the set of all pairs
(x,y)
such that
(A+\lambda x,y)=1
.
Hence identity (
50
) implies
x=x^{\prime}
,
y=y^{\prime}
, provided
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S
and our task is to show
|S|\gg H_{*}H
because the last fact implies the lemma in view of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
7
).
We have

|S|=\sum_{x\in[H]}\,\sum_{y\in[H_{*}]}\,\sum_{d|(y,A+\lambda x)}\mu(d)=\sum_{d\leqslant H_{*}}\mu(d)\sum_{y\in[H_{*}],\,d|y}\,\,\sum_{x\in[H],\,d|(A+\lambda x)}1=

=\sum_{d\leqslant H_{*},\,(d,\lambda)=1}\mu(d)\left(\frac{H_{*}}{d}+\theta^{\prime}_{d}\right)\left(\frac{H}{d}+\theta^{\prime\prime}_{d}\right)\,,

where for all
d
one has
|\theta^{\prime}_{d}|\leqslant 1
,
|\theta^{\prime\prime}_{d}|\leqslant 1
.
Here we have used the assumption that
(A,\lambda)=1
.
It follows that

|S|=H_{*}H\sum_{1\leqslant d,\,(d,\lambda)=1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}+O(H\log H_{*})\geqslant H_{*}H\left(2-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)+O(H\log H_{*})\gg H_{*}H

as required.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

P r o o f.

Let
P_{a}=a+\{0,1,\dots,d(p)-1\}=a+P_{0}\subseteq R

We have

jP^{1/2k}_{0}\subseteq P^{1/2}_{0}

\frac{P^{1/2k}_{0}}{P^{1/2}_{a}}\subseteq(R-1)\cap(2^{-1}\cdot R-2^{-1})\cap\dots(k^{-1}\cdot R-k^{-1})\,.

24

H_{*}=|P_{0}^{1/2k}|

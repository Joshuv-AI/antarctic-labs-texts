Abstract
. We study focal surfaces of (wave) fronts associated to unbounded principal curvatures near non-degenerate singular points of initial fronts. We give characterizations of singularities of those focal surfaces in terms of types of singularities and geometrical properties of initial fronts. Moreover, we investigate behavior of the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
53A05, 53A55, 57R45

1.
Introduction

Focal surfaces
(or
caustics
) of regular surfaces in the Euclidean
3
-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}

can be characterized by several ways: loci of centers of principal curvature spheres
of initial surfaces, singular value sets of the normal congruence,
and the bifurcation set of the family of distance squared functions for instance (cf.
[
12
,
15
,
28
,
4
]
).
Although initial surfaces have no singular points,
their focal surfaces have singularities in general (
[
2
,
1
,
37
,
12
,
28
,
29
]
).
It is known that types of (co)rank one singularities on focal surfaces correspond to
geometric properties arising from principal curvatures of initial surfaces (
[
12
,
28
,
29
]
).
Investigating singularities of focal surfaces, one might have new geometrical properties of surfaces
(
[
5
,
23
,
3
,
4
,
12
]
).

On the other hand, there are classes of surfaces with singular points
called
frontals
and (
wave
)
fronts
.
These surfaces admit smooth unit normal vector field even at singular points.
Owing to this property, frontals and fronts can be considered as
a kind of generalization of regular surfaces (i.e., immersions) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Recently, there are several studies on frontals and fronts from differential geometric viewpoint,
and various geometric invariants at singular points are introduced
(
[
8
,
6
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
34
,
26
,
11
,
25
,
27
,
10
,
9
,
7
]
).
Moreover, relation among behavior of the curvatures of frontals and fronts and geometric invariants are considered
(
[
21
,
11
,
34
,
36
,
32
]
).
In particular, for a front, it is known that
one principal curvature can be extended as a bounded
C^{\infty}
function
near a non-degenerate singular point, and another is unbounded near such a singular point (
[
24
,
36
,
22
]
).
Although one principal curvature of a front is unbounded near a non-degenerate singular point,
the radius function (i.e., the reciprocal) of it
can be extended as a
C^{\infty}
function near the singular point.
This fact plays a crucial role to study focal surfaces of fronts with bounded Gaussian curvature
because the bounded principal curvature vanishes on the set of singular points of such fronts,
and hence we cannot define corresponding focal surfaces near such points (cf.
[
24
,
17
,
19
,
30
]
).

In this paper, we investigate singularities and geometrical properties of focal surfaces of fronts
associated to unbounded principal curvatures.
We note that characterizations of singularities on focal surfaces
relative to bounded principal curvatures are given in
[
35
]
.
Moreover, if the initial front has a cuspidal edge,
then the focal surface associated to the unbounded principal curvature
is regular at the corresponding point (
[
35
]
).
Thus we consider focal surfaces of fronts with
singular points of the second kind,
which are classes of non-degenerate singular points of fronts.
A swallowtail singularity is a typical example. To give characterizations of singularities of the focal surface,
we recall behavior of principal curvatures and
principal vectors around a non-degenerate singular point in Section
3
.
By observing the unbounded principal curvature and corresponding principal vector,
we extend a concept of
sub-parabolic points
to singular points of the second kind on fronts in Section
4
.
In particular, we show relation between behavior of the Gaussian curvature and sub-parabolic points
of a front (Propositions
4.3
and
4.4
).

In Section
5
, we study a focal surface of a front associated to the unbounded principal curvature.
We give characterizations of singularities on focal surfaces
by geometrical properties of initial fronts (Theorem
5.6
and Proposition
5.9
).
Furthermore, we study contact between singular sets of the initial front
and the focal surface associated to the unbounded principal curvature.
We characterize types of singularities of the focal surface in terms of the contact order of these curves (Proposition
5.14
).
Finally, we observe that the behavior of the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface.
Especially, for the case of a singular point of the second kind,
we give characterization for the rational boundedness of the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface
by a certain geometrical property of the initial front (Theorem
5.15
).

2.1.
Fronts

Let
(\Sigma;u,v)
be a domain in the
(u,v)
-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a
C^{\infty}
map.
Then
f
is said to be a
frontal
if there exists a
C^{\infty}
map

\nu\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
\left\langle{df_{q}(\bm{X})},{\nu(q)}\right\rangle=0
for any
q\in\Sigma
and
\bm{X}\in T_{q}\Sigma
,
where
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is the standard unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

and
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
is the canonical inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Moreover, a frontal
f
is a
front
if
the pair
(f,\nu)\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
gives an immersion.
We call
\nu
a
unit normal vector field
or a
Gauss map
of
f
.

We fix a frontal
f
.
A point
p\in\Sigma
is called a
singular point
of
f
if
f
is not an immersion at
p
.
We denote by
S(f)
the set of singular points of
f
.
Set a function
\lambda\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.1)

\lambda(u,v)=\det(f_{u},f_{v},\nu)(u,v)\quad(f_{u}=\partial f/\partial u,\ f_{v}=\partial f/\partial v),

where
\det
is the determinant of
3\times 3
matrices.
Then one can check that
\lambda^{-1}(0)=S(f)
holds by the definition.
We call the function
\lambda
the
signed area density function
of
f
.

Take a singular point
p\in S(f)
of a frontal
f
.
Then
p
is said to be
non-degenerate
(resp.
degenerate
)
if
(\lambda_{u}(p),\lambda_{v}(p))\neq(0,0)
(resp.
(\lambda_{u}(p),\lambda_{v}(p))=(0,0)
) holds.
For a non-degenerate singular point
p
of
f
,
there exist an open neighborhood
U(\subset\Sigma)
of
p

and a regular curve
\gamma\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)\in U

(\varepsilon>0)
such that

\gamma(0)=p
and
\lambda(\gamma(t))=0
on
U

by the implicit function theorem.
Moreover, since a non-degenerate singular point
p
is a corank one singular point (i.e.,
\operatorname{rank}df_{p}=1
),
there exists a never-vanishing vector field
\eta
on
U
such that

df_{q}(\eta_{q})=0
for any
q\in S(f)\cap U
(
\eta_{q}\in T_{q}U
).
We call
\gamma
and
\eta
the
singular curve
for
f
and a
null vector field
, respectively.
We remark that one can take a null vector field
\eta
of a front near a corank one singular point
p
.

A non-degenerate singular point
p
is said to be of the
first kind

if
\gamma^{\prime}=d\gamma/dt
and
\eta
are linearly independent at
p=\gamma(0)
.
Otherwise, it is said to be of the
second kind
.
Let
p
be a singular point of the second kind of a frontal
f
.
Then
p
is said to be
admissible
if
for each open neighborhood
U
of
p
,
the intersection
S(f)\cap U
contains a singular point of the first kind.
Otherwise, we call
p

non-admissible
.
We say that a non-degenerate singular point
p
is
admissible

if
p
is either of the first kind or the admissible second kind.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma,p)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a
C^{\infty}
map germ and
p
a singular point of
f
.
Then

(1)

f
is a
cuspidal edge
at
p

if
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ
(u,v)\mapsto(u,v^{2},v^{3})
at the origin.

(2)

f
is a
swallowtail
at
p

if
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ
(u,v)\mapsto(u,4v^{3}+2uv,3v^{4}+uv^{2})
at the origin.

(3)

f
is a
cuspidal butterfly
at
p

if
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ
(u,v)\mapsto(u,5v^{4}+2uv,4v^{5}+uv^{2})
at the origin.

(4)

f
is a
cuspidal lips
at
p
if
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ

(u,v)\mapsto(u,3v^{4}+2u^{2}v^{2},v^{3}+u^{2}v)
at the origin.

(5)

f
is a
cuspidal beaks
at
p
if
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ

(u,v)\mapsto(u,3v^{4}-2u^{2}v^{2},v^{3}-u^{2}v)
at the origin.

Here, two map germs
f_{1},f_{2}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{3},0)
are
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent

if there exist diffeomorphism germs
\varphi\colon(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
on the source
and
\Phi\colon(\mathbb{R}^{3},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{3},0)
on the target
such that
f_{2}=\Phi\circ f_{1}\circ\varphi^{-1}
.

A cuspidal edge, a swallowtail and a cuspidal butterfly are non-degenerate front singular points.
Moreover, a cuspidal edge and a swallowtail are generic singularities of fronts in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
and a cuspidal lips/beaks and a cuspidal butterfly are generic corank one singularities of
one-parameter bifurcation of fronts (cf.
[
2
,
37
]
).
Further, a cuspidal edge is a singular point of the first kind, and
a swallowtail and a cuspidal butterfly are of the admissible second kind (cf.
[
21
]
).
For these singular points, the following characterizations are known.

Fact 2.2

(
[
18
,
33
,
13
,
14
]
)
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a corank one singular point of
f
.
Then we have the following.

(1)

f
is a cuspidal edge at
p
if and only if
\eta\lambda(p)\neq 0
.

(2)

f
is a swallowtail at
p
if and only if
d\lambda(p)\neq 0
,
\eta\lambda(p)=0
and
\eta\eta\lambda(p)\neq 0
.

(3)

f
is a cuspidal butterfly at
p
if and only if
d\lambda(p)\neq 0
,

\eta\lambda(p)=\eta\eta\lambda(p)=0
and
\eta\eta\eta\lambda(p)\neq 0
.

(4)

f
is a cuspidal lips at
p
if and only if
d\lambda(p)=0
and
\det(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})(p)>0
,
that is,
\lambda
has a Morse type singularity of index zero or two at
p
.

(5)

f
is a cuspidal beaks at
p
if and only if
d\lambda(p)=0
,
\eta\eta\lambda(p)\neq 0
and
\det(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})(p)<0
,
that is,
\lambda
has a Morse type singularity of index one and
\eta\eta\lambda\neq 0
at
p
.

Here,
\lambda
is the signed area density function of
f
as in (
2.1
),

\eta
is a null vector field,
\eta\lambda
means the directional derivative of
\lambda
in the direction
\eta

and
\det(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda})
is the Hessian of
\lambda
.

2.2.
Geometric invariants

We recall geometric invariants of fronts.

2.2.1.
Geometric invariants of cuspidal edges

First we consider the case of cuspidal edges.
Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front,
\nu
its Gauss map and
p
a cuspidal edge of
f
.
Let
\gamma(t)
be a singular curve passing through
p
and
\eta
a null vector field of
f
.
Then one can define the following geometric invariants: the
singular curvature

\kappa_{s}
(
[
34
]
),
the
limiting normal curvature

\kappa_{\nu}
(
[
34
,
21
]
),
the
cuspidal curvature

\kappa_{c}
(
[
21
]
)
and the
cuspidal torsion

\kappa_{t}
(
[
20
]
).
We note that
\kappa_{s}
is an intrinsic invariant and its sing has a geometrical meaning (see
[
34
,
9
]
).
It is known that
\kappa_{c}
does not vanish
when
\gamma
consists of cuspidal edges (
[
21
,
20
]
).
We remark that these invariants can be defined at singular points of the first kind for frontals but not fronts.
In such cases,
\kappa_{c}
vanishes at non-front singular points (cf.
[
21
, Proposition 3.11]
).

The limiting normal curvature
\kappa_{\nu}
relates to the boundedness of the Gaussian curvature of a front with a cuspidal edge.
In fact, the Gaussian curvature
K
of a front
f
is bounded on a sufficiently small neighborhood
U

of a cuspidal edge
p
if and only if
\kappa_{\nu}
vanishes along the singular curve
\gamma
through
p
(
[
21
, Theorem 3.9]
).
In this case,
K
can be extended as a
C^{\infty}
function on
U
.
Moreover, the following property holds.

Fact 2.3

(
[
21
, Remark 3.19]
,
[
7
, Theorem 1.9]
)
.

Let
f
be a front in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p
a cuspidal edge.
Let
K
be the Gaussian curvature of
f
defined on the set of regular points of
f
.
Suppose that
K
is bounded on a sufficiently small neighborhood
U
of
p
.
Then
K
satisfies

(2.2)

4K=-4\kappa_{t}^{2}-\kappa_{s}\kappa_{c}^{2}

at
p
.

On the other hand, we can take a coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

the
u
-axis is the singular curve,

(2)

\partial_{v}
gives a null vector field, and

(3)

there are no singular points other than the
u
-axis.

We call this local coordinate system
(U;u,v)

adapted
(
[
21
,
34
,
18
]
).
Moreover, an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
is called
special adapted

if the frame
\{f_{u},f_{vv},\nu\}
gives an orthonormal frame along the
u
-axis in addition to the above conditions
(
[
34
, Lemma 3.2]
,
[
21
]
).

On an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
, since
\eta=\partial_{v}
,
f_{v}(u,0)=0
holds.
Thus there exists a
C^{\infty}
map
g\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
f_{v}=vg
.
We note that
g
does not vanish along the
u
-axis since
f_{vv}=g
holds along the
u
-axis.
Therefore the pair
\{f_{u},g,\nu\}
gives a frame along
f
.
Moreover, when we take a special adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around a cuspidal edge
p
,
then
\{f_{u},g,\nu\}
gives an orthonormal frame along the
u
-axis.
In particular,
\nu
can be taken as
\nu=(f_{u}\times g)/|f_{u}\times g|
.
Using these mappings, we define the following functions:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{E}&=\left\langle{f_{u}},{f_{u}}\right\rangle,&\widetilde{F}&=\left\langle{f_{u}},{g}\right\rangle,&\widetilde{G}&=\left\langle{g},{g}\right\rangle,\\
\widetilde{L}&=-\left\langle{f_{u}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle,&\widetilde{M}&=-\left\langle{g},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle,&\widetilde{N}&=-\left\langle{g},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle.\end{aligned}

We remark that
\widetilde{E}\widetilde{G}-\widetilde{F}^{2}>0
on
U
.
Relation between geometric invariants stated above and the functions
in (
2.3
) are known (
[
9
,
36
]
).

2.2.2.
Geometric invariants at singular points of the second kind

We next consider geometric invariants at a singular point of the admissible second kind of a front.
Let
p
be a singular point of the second kind of a front
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then one can take a local coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
satisfying

(1)

f_{u}(p)=0
,

(2)

the
u
-axis is the singular curve on
U
, and

(3)

|f_{v}(p)|=1
.

We also call this local coordinate system
adapted
.
Further, if an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around a singular point of the second kind
p

satisfies
\left\langle{f_{uv}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle(p)=0
, then
(U;u,v)
is called a
strongly adapted coordinate system

(cf.
[
21
, Definitions 4.1 and 4.6]
).

Using an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around a singular point of the second kind
p
,
we define geometric invariants at
p
as follows:

(2.4)

\kappa_{\nu}(p)=\lim_{u\to 0}\dfrac{\left\langle{f_{uu}},{\nu}\right\rangle}{|f_{u}|^{2}}(u,0),\quad\mu_{c}=\dfrac{-\left\langle{f_{uv}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle}{|f_{uv}\times f_{v}|^{2}}(p).

\kappa_{\nu}(p)
is the
limiting normal curvature
and
\mu_{c}
is the
normalized cuspidal curvature
at
p

(see
[
21
, Proposition 2.9 and (4.7)]
).
These invariants are related to the boundedness of the Gaussian and the mean curvature near singular points
of the second kind (see
[
21
, Propositions 4.2 and 4.3, Theorem 4.4]
)

Let us take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)

around a singular point of the (admissible) second kind
p
.
Then one can take a null vector field
\eta
as

\eta=\partial_{u}+e(u)\partial_{v},

where
e(u)
is a
C^{\infty}
function with
e(0)=0
(
[
21
]

We note that there exists a positive integer
l
such that

e(0)=e^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=e^{(l-1)}(0)=0
and
e^{(l)}(0)\neq 0
if
p
is of the admissible (cf.
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Since
df(\eta)=\eta{f}=f_{u}+e(u)f_{v}=0
along the
u
-axis,
there exists a
C^{\infty}
map
h\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\eta{f}=vh
,
and hence
f_{u}=vh-e(u)f_{v}
.
Using this map

, we have
\lambda=\det(f_{u},f_{v},\nu)=v\det(h,f_{v},\nu)
.
By the non-degeneracy,
\lambda_{v}(p)\neq 0
holds.
Thus we have
\det(h,f_{v},\nu)(p)\neq 0
.
This implies that
\{h,f_{v},\nu\}
gives a frame along

p
.
We take
\nu
satisfying
\lambda_{v}(p)>0
,
so one may take
\nu
as

\nu=\dfrac{h\times f_{v}}{|h\times f_{v}|}

in what follows.

We define the following functions:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{E}&=\left\langle{h},{h}\right\rangle,&\widehat{F}&=\left\langle{h},{f_{v}}\right\rangle,&\widehat{G}&=\left\langle{f_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle,\\
\widehat{L}&=-\left\langle{h},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle,&\widehat{M}&=-\left\langle{h},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle,&\widehat{N}&=-\left\langle{f_{v}},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle.\end{aligned}

We note that
\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2}>0
on
U
.
Using functions as in (
2.5
),
differentials
\nu_{u}
and
\nu_{v}
of
\nu
can be written as follows.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
36
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

On an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
,

\nu_{u}
and
\nu_{v}
can be written as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \nu_{u}&=\frac{\widehat{F}(v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N})-\widehat{G}\widehat{L}}{\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2}}h+\frac{\widehat{F}\widehat{L}-\widehat{E}(v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N})}{\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2}}f_{v},\\

If
f
is a front at a singular point of the second kind

then
\eta\nu\neq 0

We rephrase this condition using functions as in (

By

2.2.3.
Rational boundedness of the Gaussian curvature

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a non-degenerate singular point of
f
.
Then the Gaussian curvature
K
of
f
is unbounded near
p
in general.
In
[
21
]
, a notion of the
rational boundedness
for unbounded functions was introduced.
(For precise definition and descriptions of the rational boundedness, see
[
21
, Definition 3.4 and Page 260]
.)
For the Gaussian curvature of a front, the following assertion holds.

Fact 2.6

(
[
21
, Corollary C]
)
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a non-degenerate singular point of
f
,
where
\Sigma
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The Gaussian curvature of
f
is rationally bounded at
p
.

(2)

The limiting normal curvature of
f
vanishes at
p
.

(3)

The Gauss map of
f
has a singularity at
p
.

We will characterize rational boundedness of the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface
in terms of certain geometrical property of the initial front.

2.3.
Second order derivatives of fronts

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front,
\nu
its unit normal vector and
p
a singular point of the second kind of
f
.
Take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
.
We then prepare expressions of
h_{u}
,
h_{v}
and
f_{vv}
by the frame
\{h,f_{v},\nu\}
along
f
,
where
h
is a
C^{\infty}
map defined on
U
satisfying
df(\eta)=vh
.
These shall play important role to analyze some geometric properties of focal surfaces of fronts.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front,
\nu
its unit normal vector and
p\in\Sigma
a singular point of the second kind.
Take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Then we have the following:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} h_{u}&=\frac{\widehat{E}_{u}\widehat{G}-2\widehat{F}A}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}h+\frac{2\widehat{E}A-\widehat{E}_{u}\widehat{F}}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}f_{v}+\widehat{L}\nu,\\
h_{v}&=\frac{\widehat{E}_{v}\widehat{G}-2\widehat{F}B}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}h+\frac{2\widehat{E}B-\widehat{E}_{v}\widehat{F}}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}f_{v}+\widehat{M}\nu,\\
f_{vv}&=\frac{2\widehat{G}(\widehat{F}_{v}-B)-\widehat{F}\widehat{G}_{v}}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}h+\frac{\widehat{E}\widehat{G}_{v}-2\widehat{F}\widehat{F}_{v}+2\widehat{F}B}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}f_{v}+\widehat{N}\nu,\end{aligned}

where
A=\left\langle{h_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
and
B=\left\langle{h_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
\{h,f_{v},\nu\}
is a moving frame along the front
f
,
there exist
C^{\infty}
functions
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}

(i=1,2,3)
such that

h_{u}=A_{1}h+A_{2}f_{v}+A_{3}\nu,\quad h_{v}=B_{1}h+B_{2}f_{v}+B_{3}\nu,\quad f_{vv}=C_{1}h+C_{2}f_{v}+C_{3}\nu.

We first consider for
h_{u}
.
Taking inner products
\left\langle{h_{u}},{h}\right\rangle(=\widehat{E}_{u}/2)
and
\left\langle{h_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle(=A)
, we have

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\widehat{E}_{u}}{2}\\
A\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{E}&\widehat{F}\\
\widehat{F}&\widehat{G}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Solving this equation for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
,
we have

A_{1}=\frac{\widehat{E}_{u}\widehat{G}-2\widehat{F}A}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})},\quad A_{2}=\frac{2\widehat{E}A-\widehat{E}_{u}\widehat{F}}{2(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}.

Moreover, since
\left\langle{h},{\nu}\right\rangle=0
, we have
\left\langle{h_{u}},{\nu}\right\rangle+\left\langle{h},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{h_{u}},{\nu}\right\rangle-\widehat{L}=0
,
and hence
A_{3}=\left\langle{h_{u}},{\nu}\right\rangle=\widehat{L}
.
Thus we have the assertion for
h_{u}
.
For
h_{v}
and
f_{vv}
, we can show similarly
by using
\widehat{F}_{v}=\left\langle{h},{f_{v}}\right\rangle_{v}=\left\langle{h_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{h},{f_{vv}}\right\rangle=B+\left\langle{h},{f_{vv}}\right\rangle
.
∎

On an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
, since
f_{u}=vh-e(u)f_{v}
,
f_{uu}
and
f_{uv}
can be written as

f_{uu}=vh_{u}-e^{\prime}(u)f_{v}-e(u)f_{uv}
and
f_{uv}=h+vh_{v}-e(u)f_{vv}
, respectively.
Thus we can write
f_{uu}
and
f_{uv}
as

(2.9)

\displaystyle f_{uu}

\displaystyle=(vA_{1}-e(u)(1+vB_{1}-e(u)C_{1}))h

\displaystyle+(vA_{2}-e^{\prime}(u)-e(u)(vB_{2}-e(u)C_{2}))f_{v}+(v(\widehat{L}-e(u)\widehat{M})+e(u)^{2}\widehat{N})\nu,

\displaystyle f_{uv}

\displaystyle=(1+vB_{1}-e(u)C_{1})h+(vB_{2}-e(u)C_{2})f_{v}+(v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N})\nu,

where
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}

(i=1,2)
correspond to the coefficient functions in (
2.8
).
By (
2.9
), it follows that
f_{uu}(p)=0
and
f_{uv}(p)=h(p)
hold.

For functions
A
and
B
, the following relation holds.

Lemma 2.8
.

Under the above setting, we have

A+e(u)B=-\widehat{E}+\widehat{F}_{u}+e(u)\widehat{F}_{v}-\frac{v\widehat{E}_{v}}{2},

where
A=\left\langle{h_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
and
B=\left\langle{h_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
\widehat{F}=\left\langle{h},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
, we have

\widehat{F}_{u}=\left\langle{h_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{h},{f_{uv}}\right\rangle=A+\left\langle{h},{f_{uv}}\right\rangle
.
By (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), it holds that

\left\langle{h},{f_{uv}}\right\rangle=\widehat{E}+e(u)(B-\widehat{F}_{v})+\frac{v\widehat{E}_{v}}{2}.

Thus we have the assertion.
∎

3.
Principal curvatures and principal vectors

We recall principal curvatures and principal vectors.
We focus on a singular point of the second kind in this section.
For the case of cuspidal edge, we can consider similar properties (see
[
35
,
36
]
).

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a singular point of the second kind.
Take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
.
Then we set functions
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
on the set of regular points of
f
by

(3.1)

\kappa_{1}=\dfrac{\hat{k}_{1}+\hat{k}_{2}}{2v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})},\quad\kappa_{2}=\dfrac{\hat{k}_{1}-\hat{k}_{2}}{2v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})},

where

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{G}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-2v\widehat{F}\widehat{M}+v\widehat{E}\widehat{N},

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\hat{k}_{1}^{2}-4v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})(\widehat{N}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-v\widehat{M}^{2})}.

Since
\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M}\neq 0
on the
u
-axis,

\hat{k}_{1}
and
\hat{k}_{2}
are
C^{\infty}
functions near
p
.
Moreover, by direct calculations, we have

\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}=K
and
\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}=2H
on the set of regular points of
f
,
where
K
is the Gaussian curvature and
H
is the mean curvature of
f
written as

(3.2)

K=\dfrac{\widehat{N}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-v\widehat{M}^{2}}{v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})},\quad H=\dfrac{\widehat{G}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-2v\widehat{F}\widehat{M}+v\widehat{E}\widehat{N}}{2v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})}

(see
[
21
, Pages 268 and 270]
).
Thus one can consider
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
as
principal curvatures
of
f
.
We note that
H
is unbounded near
p
(
[
34
, Corollary 3.5]
),
and hence at least one principal curvature may diverge near
p
.
Indeed, the following holds.

Proposition 3.1

(cf.
[
24
,
36
]
)
.

Let
f
be a front in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p
a non-degenerate singular point of
f
.
Then one principal curvature can be extended as a bounded
C^{\infty}
function at
p
,
and another is unbounded near
p
.

We note that Medina-Tejeda
[
22
]
shows boundedness of principal curvatures of fronts
with other singularities.

Let us denote by
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
the bounded principal curvature
and the unbounded principal curvature, respectively.
Then
\kappa(p)=\kappa_{\nu}(p)
holds at an admissible singular point
p
(
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
).
Moreover, setting
\hat{\kappa}=\lambda\tilde{\kappa}
,
where
\lambda
is the signed area density function of
f
in (
2.1
),

\hat{\kappa}
is bounded
C^{\infty}
function on
U
,
in particular,
\hat{\kappa}(p)\neq 0
(
[
36
, Remark 3.2]
).
When
p
is of the second kind, the relation
\hat{\kappa}(p)=\lambda_{v}(p)\mu_{c}
holds.
Furthermore, we note that the curvature radius function relative to
\tilde{\kappa}
can be extended as a
C^{\infty}
function on
U

because it can be written as
1/\tilde{\kappa}=\lambda/\hat{\kappa}
.
We denote by
\hat{\rho}
the curvature radius function
\lambda/\hat{\kappa}
of
the unbounded principal curvature.

We next consider principal vectors
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
associated to
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
, respectively.
Let
\hat{I}
and
\widehat{II}
be
2\times 2
matrices given by

\displaystyle\hat{I}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\left\langle{f_{u}},{f_{u}}\right\rangle&\left\langle{f_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle\\
\left\langle{f_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle&\left\langle{f_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v^{2}\widehat{E}-2ve(u)\widehat{F}+e(u)^{2}\widehat{G}&v\widehat{F}-e(u)\widehat{G}\\
v\widehat{F}-e(u)\widehat{G}&\widehat{G}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\widehat{II}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\left\langle{f_{u}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle&-\left\langle{f_{u}},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle\\
-\left\langle{f_{v}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle&-\left\langle{f_{v}},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v\widehat{L}-e(u)(v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N})&v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N}\\
v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N}&\widehat{N}\end{pmatrix}.

When
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
are principal vectors relative to
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
on
U\setminus\{v=0\}
,
they satisfy
(\widehat{II}-\kappa\hat{I}~)\bm{V}=\bm{0}
and
(\widehat{II}-\tilde{\kappa}\hat{I}~)\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\bm{0}
, respectively.
Solving the equation
(\widehat{II}-\kappa\hat{I}~)\bm{V}=\bm{0}
, we have

(3.3)

\bm{V}=(-\widehat{M}+\kappa\widehat{F},\widehat{L}-\kappa(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F})).

Since
\kappa
is bounded on
U
and
\widehat{L}(p)\neq 0
,
\bm{V}
can be defined on
U
(
[
36
, (3.2)]
).

On the other hand, we consider the equation
(\widehat{II}-\tilde{\kappa}\hat{I}~)\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\bm{0}
.
This equation can be modified as

(3.4)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\widehat{L}-\hat{\kappa}(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F})&\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}\\
v(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})-e(u)(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})&\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{1}\\
\widetilde{V}_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}

by multiplying the equation by
\lambda
and factoring out
v
from the first row.
We note that
\hat{\kappa}
does not vanish at
p
,
and hence we may take
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
on
U
as

(3.5)

\widetilde{\bm{V}}=(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G},-v(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})+e(u)(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})).

Remark that
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
as in (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) are linearly independent on
U
.
Indeed, when we identify
\widetilde{\bm{V}}=(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})=(\widetilde{V}_{1},-v\widehat{V}_{2}+e(u)\widetilde{V}_{1})

with
\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\partial_{u}+(-v\widehat{V}_{2}+e(u)\widetilde{V}_{1})\partial_{v}
,
where we set
\widetilde{V}_{1}=\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G}
and
\widehat{V}_{2}=\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}
,
it holds that

(3.6)

\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta-v\widehat{V}_{2}\partial_{v}.

Since
\bm{V}
is not parallel to
\eta
along the
u
-axis,
we have that
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
are linearly independent.
Moreover, by
df(\eta)(=\eta{f})=vh
, we have

df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\{(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})h-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})f_{v}\}
.

We now define
C^{\infty}
maps
\bm{x},\bm{y}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

(3.7)

\bm{x}=-v(\widehat{M}-\kappa\widehat{F})h+((\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-v\kappa\widehat{E})f_{v},\ \ \bm{y}=(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})h-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})f_{v}.

We note that
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
satisfy
\bm{x}=df(\bm{V})
and
df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\bm{y}
, respectively.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the above setting,
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
given by (
3.7
) are perpendicular to each other.

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation with relations
2\lambda H=\lambda\kappa+\hat{\kappa}
and
\lambda K=\kappa\hat{\kappa}
,
where
K
and
H
are the Gaussian and the mean curvature,
we have
\left\langle{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\right\rangle=0
.
∎

By this lemma,
\{\bm{x},\bm{y},\nu\}
gives an orthogonal frame along
f
.
Moreover, when we set
\bm{e}_{1}=\bm{x}/|\bm{x}|
and
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
,
then
\{\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2},\nu\}
is an orthonormal frame along
f
.
Particularly, the existence of curvature line coordinate systems for fronts
around non-degenerate singular points is known
[
24
, Lemma 1.3]
.
We note that a different approach to study singular surfaces using moving frame is known (
[
8
]
).

Remark 3.3
.

If
p
is a cuspidal edge of a front
f
,
then principal vectors are given by

\bm{V}=(\widetilde{N}-v\kappa\widetilde{G},-\widetilde{M}+\kappa\widetilde{F}),\quad\widetilde{\bm{V}}=(v(\lambda\widetilde{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{F}),-\lambda\widetilde{L}+\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{E})

by taking a special adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)

and using functions as in (
2.3
) (
[
35
,
36
]
),
where
\kappa
is the bounded principal curvature and
\hat{\kappa}=\lambda\tilde{\kappa}
.
In this case, we see that

df(\bm{V})=(\widetilde{N}-v\kappa\widetilde{G})f_{u}+v(-\widetilde{M}+\kappa\widetilde{F})g,\quad df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\left((\lambda\widetilde{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{F})f_{u}+(-\lambda\widetilde{L}+\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{E})g\right).

Then setting
\bm{x},\bm{y}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
by

\bm{x}=df(\bm{V})
and
\bm{y}=(\lambda\widetilde{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{F})f_{u}+(-\lambda\widetilde{L}+\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{E})g
,
one can see
df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\bm{y}
, and
it holds that
\left\langle{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\right\rangle=0
by a similar calculation as the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Thus we can take an orthonormal frame
\{\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2},\nu\}
along
f

by setting
\bm{e}_{1}=\bm{x}/|\bm{x}|
and
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
.

Remark 3.4
.

The pair of principal vectors
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
is a
curvature line frame generator
of a front (cf.
[
32
]
).
Moreover, the pair
\{\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2}\}
is a
curvature line frame corresponding to

\{\bm{V},\widetilde{\bm{V}}\}
.

For the unit normal vector
\nu
of a front
f
,
the following properties hold.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front,
\nu
its unit normal vector and
p
a singular point of the second kind.
Let
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
be the bounded and unbounded principal curvature on a neighborhood
U
of
p
, respectively.
Let
\bm{V}
and
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
the corresponding principal vectors.
Then we have

d\nu(\bm{V})=-\kappa df(\bm{V}),\quad df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=-\hat{\rho}d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}}),

where
\hat{\rho}=\lambda/\hat{\kappa}
.

Proof.

Let us take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
We first show the relation between
df(\bm{V})
and
d\nu(\bm{V})
.
Denote by
\bm{V}=V_{1}\partial_{u}+V_{2}\partial_{v}
the principal vector
relative to the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
(see (
3.3
)).
Then
d\nu(\bm{V})
can be written as

d\nu(\bm{V})=V_{1}\nu_{u}+V_{2}\nu_{v}=(V_{1}X_{1}+V_{2}Y_{1})h+(V_{1}X_{2}+V_{2}Y_{2})f_{v}

by Lemma
2.4
,
where
X_{i},Y_{i}
(
i=1,2
) are functions in (
2.6
).
By direct computations, we have

V_{1}X_{1}+V_{2}Y_{1}=v\kappa(\widehat{M}-\kappa\widehat{F})

and
V_{1}X_{2}+V_{2}Y_{2}=-\kappa((\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-v\kappa\widehat{E})
.
Thus it holds that

d\nu(\bm{V})=\kappa\left(v(\widehat{M}-\kappa\widehat{F})h-((\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})-v\kappa\widehat{E})f_{v}\right)=-\kappa df(\bm{V})(=-\kappa\bm{x})

by (
3.7
).

Next we consider the case of
df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})
.
By (
3.5
), we have

d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})(\nu_{u}+e(u)\nu_{v})-v(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\nu_{v}.

By (
2.6
), it follows that

\nu_{u}+e(u)\nu_{v}=\frac{v\widehat{F}\widehat{M}-\widehat{G}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})}{\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2}}h-\frac{v\widehat{E}\widehat{M}-\widehat{F}(\widehat{L}+e(u)\widehat{M})}{\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2}}f_{v}

holds.
Thus on the set of regular points
U\setminus\{v=0\}
, we have

d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\hat{\kappa}\left(-(\widehat{N}-\tilde{\kappa}\widehat{G})h+(\widehat{M}-\tilde{\kappa}\widehat{F})f_{v}\right).

By multiplying
\lambda
, we obtain

\lambda d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=v\hat{\kappa}\left(-(\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})h+(\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})f_{v}\right)=-v\hat{\kappa}\bm{y}=-\hat{\kappa}df(\widetilde{\bm{V}}).

Since
\hat{\kappa}\neq 0
on
U
, we have

df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=-\hat{\rho}d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})

on
U
.
∎

This lemma seems to correspond to the Rodrigues’ Theorem (cf.
[
29
, Theorem 10.2]
).
We note that if
f
is a front at a singular point of the second kind
p
,

d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=\widetilde{V}_{1}d\nu(\eta)\neq 0
holds at
p
.

Remark 3.6
.

If we take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around a singular point of the second kind
p
of a front
f
,
then the signed area density
\lambda
of
f
satisfies
\lambda(u,0)=0
.
Thus there exists a
C^{\infty}
function
\hat{\lambda}
on
U
such that
\lambda=v\hat{\lambda}
.
By the non-degeneracy,
\lambda_{v}(p)\neq 0
, and hence
\hat{\lambda}(p)\neq 0
.
Thus we have

d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=-\frac{\hat{\kappa}}{\hat{\lambda}}\bm{y}

on
U
.
Moreover, it holds that

d\nu(\bm{V})=-\kappa\bm{x}=-\kappa|\bm{x}|\bm{e}_{1},\quad d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=-\frac{\hat{\kappa}}{\hat{\lambda}}\bm{y}=-\frac{\hat{\kappa}}{\hat{\lambda}}|\bm{y}|\bm{e}_{2},

where
\bm{e}_{1}=\bm{x}/|\bm{x}|
and
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
.

4.
Sub-parabolic points

For a regular surface, one can define a ridge point and a sub-parabolic point of the surface
by using principal curvatures and corresponding principal vectors.
They are related to singularities and geometrical properties of focal surfaces (cf.
[
28
,
5
,
23
]
).
In this section, we investigate a sub-parabolic point of a front.
First, we give definitions of a ridge point and a sub-parabolic point for fronts
using the principal curvatures and corresponding principal vectors.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
(resp.
\bm{V}
) be a principal vector
with respect to the unbounded principal curvature
\tilde{\kappa}

(resp. the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
)
of a front
f
with a non-degenerate singular point
p
.
Then
p
is a
sub-parabolic point
(resp. a
ridge point
) of
f
with respect to
\kappa

if the directional derivative
\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa
(resp.
{\bm{V}}\kappa
) of
\kappa
in the direction
\widetilde{\bm{V}}

(resp.
{\bm{V}}
) vanishes at
p
.

By the property of the principal vector relative to the unbounded principal curvature,
the following characterization for a sub-parabolic point holds.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a non-degenerate singular point.
Then
p
is also a sub-parabolic point of
f
if and only if
\eta\kappa=0
at
p
,
where
\eta\kappa
means the directional derivative of the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
of
f

in the direction a null vector field
\eta
.

Proof.

Let us take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
.
Then the principal vector
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
associated to the unbounded principal curvature is parallel to
\eta

along the
u
-axis by (
3.6
) and Remark
3.3
.
Thus we have the assertion by the definition of a sub-parabolic point.
∎

For the case of a cuspidal edge
p
of a front
f
, the condition for
p
being a sub-parabolic point
is characterized by the geometric invariants (see
[
35
, Proposition 2.8]
).
On the other hand, for the case of a singular point of the second kind, we have the following
characterization relating the behavior of the Gaussian curvature of a front.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a singular point of the second kind.
If the Gaussian curvature
K
of
f
is rationally continuous,
then
p
is a sub-parabolic point.

For a notion of the
rational continuity
, see
[
21
, Definition 3.4]
.

Proof.

Let us take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Then we define a co-vector
\omega_{\nu}(p)
by

\omega_{\nu}(p)=\hat{\kappa}_{\nu}^{\prime}(p)du\in T_{p}^{\ast}\Sigma,

where
\hat{\kappa}_{\nu}^{\prime}(p)=d\kappa_{\nu}(u)/du|_{u=0}
.
We note that
\hat{\kappa}_{\nu}^{\prime}(p)
depends on the parameter
u
of the singular curve,
but
\omega_{\nu}
does not depend on
u
(see
[
21
, Page 270]
).
Since
\kappa_{u}(p)=\hat{\kappa}_{\nu}^{\prime}(p)
holds,
the co-vector
\omega_{\nu}(p)
may be written as
\omega_{\nu}(p)=\kappa_{u}(p)du
.
Moreover, by Proposition
4.2
and
\eta=\partial_{u}
at
p
,

\omega_{\nu}(p)=0
if and only if
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.
On the other hand, it is known that the Gaussian curvature
K
of
f
is rationally continuous at
p

if and only if
\kappa_{\nu}(p)=\omega_{\nu}(p)=0
(
[
21
, Theorem 4.4]
).
Thus we have the conclusion.
∎

We next consider the case that the Gaussian curvature
K
of a front
f

is bounded near a non-degenerate singular point
p
.
In such a case, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p\in\Sigma
a non-degenerate singular point.
Suppose that the Gaussian curvature
K
of
f
is bounded
on a sufficiently small neighborhood
U
of
p
.

(1)

When
p
is a cuspidal edge,
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
if and only if
K(p)=0
.

(2)

When
p
is of the second kind, then
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.

Proof.

First we show the case of a cuspidal edge.
In this case, it is known that
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f

if and only if
4\kappa_{t}^{2}+\kappa_{s}\kappa_{c}^{2}=0
holds at
p
(
[
35
, Proposition 2.8]
).
On the other hand, the Gaussian curvature satisfies
4K=-(4\kappa_{t}^{2}+\kappa_{s}\kappa_{c}^{2})
at
p

by Fact
2.3
.
This shows the first assertion.

We next consider the second assertion.
Let us take an adapted coordinate system
(u,v)
on
U
.
Since
K
is bounded on
U
, the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
satisfies
\kappa(u,0)=\kappa_{\nu}(u)=0
.
Thus there exists a
C^{\infty}
function
k\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\kappa=vk
.
Since the principal vector
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
relative to the unbounded principal curvature can be written
as
\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta-v\widehat{V}_{2}\partial_{v}
(see (
3.6
)),
it holds that

\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa=\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta(vk)-v\widehat{V}_{2}(k+vk_{v})=\widetilde{V}_{1}(vk_{u}+e(u)(k+vk_{v}))-v\widehat{V}_{2}(k+vk_{v})
.
Therefore we have
\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa=0
at
p
since
e(0)=0
.
Thus we get the conclusion.
∎

For the case of a front with a cuspidal edge
p
,
it is known that the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface
associated to the unbounded principal curvature vanishes at
p

if and only if
p
is a sub-parabolic point (
[
35
, Corollary 3.9]
).
For the case of a singular point of the second kind,
we will show relation between behavior of the Gaussian curvature of the focal surface
associated to the unbounded principal curvature and a sub-parabolic point
of the initial front in the next section.

5.
Focal surfaces of fronts

We consider singularities and geometric properties of focal surfaces of fronts
associated to unbounded principal curvatures.
In particular, we focus on the case that the initial front has
a singular points of the second kind.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front,
\nu
its Gauss map
and
p\in\Sigma
a non-degenerate singular point of
f
.
Take an adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Then we consider a map
\mathcal{F}\colon U\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by

\mathcal{F}(u,v,w)=f(u,v)+w\nu(u,v).

The map
\mathcal{F}
is called a
normal congruence
of
f
(cf.
[
15
]
).
We consider the singular set and the singular value set of
\mathcal{F}
.
By direct calculations using the Weingarten formula,
we have

\det(\mathcal{F}_{u},\mathcal{F}_{v},\mathcal{F}_{w})=(1-w\kappa)(\lambda-\hat{\kappa}w).

Thus the set of singular points
S(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the union of

S_{1}(\mathcal{F})=\{(u,v,w)\ |\ 1-\kappa(u,v)w=0\}

and
S_{2}(\mathcal{F})=\{(u,v,w)\ |\ \lambda(u,v)-\hat{\kappa}(u,v)w=0\}
.
Hence the image
\mathcal{F}(S(\mathcal{F}))
of
S(\mathcal{F})
by
\mathcal{F}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(S(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle=\left\{f(u,v)+\rho(u,v)\nu(u,v)\ |\ (u,v)\in U,\ w=\rho(u,v)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\cup\left\{f(u,v)+\hat{\rho}(u,v)\nu(u,v)\ |\ (u,v)\in U,\ w=\hat{\rho}(u,v)\right\},

where
\rho=1/\kappa
and
\hat{\rho}=\lambda/\hat{\kappa}
.
We define
C^{\infty}
maps
C,\widehat{C}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

(5.1)

C=f+\rho\nu,\quad\widehat{C}=f+\hat{\rho}\nu.

Then one can notice immediately that the singular value set of
\mathcal{F}
coincides with the union of the
images of
C
and
\widehat{C}
:
\mathcal{F}(S(F))=\operatorname{Im}{C}\cup\operatorname{Im}{\widehat{C}}
.
We call
C
and
\widehat{C}

focal surfaces
(or
caustics
) of
f
.
In particular, we call
\widehat{C}
the
focal surface associated to the unbounded principal curvature
of
f
.
We remark that
C
cannot be defined near a singular point
when the Gaussian curvature of an original front is bounded near the singular point.
However, we can define and consider
\widehat{C}
even if the Gaussian curvature is bounded.
We note that singularities of
C
for a front with non-degenerate singular points are studied in
[
35
]
.
Thus we focus on
\widehat{C}
of a front
f
in the following of this paper.

5.1.
Singularities of
\widehat{C}

We first investigate singularities of
\widehat{C}
.
When a front
f
has a cuspidal edge
p
,
then
\widehat{C}
is regular at
p
(
[
35
, Proposition 3.7]
).
Thus we treat the case that a front
f
has a
singular point of the second kind
p
in the following of this subsection.

Lemma 5.1
.

The map
\bm{y}
as in (
3.7
) is a normal vector to the focal surface
\widehat{C}
.

Proof.

Let us take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
.
By direct calculations, we have

\widehat{C}_{u}=f_{u}+\hat{\rho}\nu_{u}+\hat{\rho}_{u}\nu,\quad\widehat{C}_{v}=f_{v}+\hat{\rho}\nu_{v}+\hat{\rho}_{v}\nu.

On the other hand, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{y}},{f_{u}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F})-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})(v\widehat{F}-e(u)\widehat{G}),

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{y}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})\widehat{F}-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\widehat{G},

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{y}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})\widehat{L}+(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})(v\widehat{M}-e(u)\widehat{N}),

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{y}},{\nu_{v}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})\widehat{M}+(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\widehat{N}.

Therefore by
\hat{\rho}\hat{\kappa}=\lambda
and
\left\langle{\bm{y}},{\nu}\right\rangle=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle{\widehat{C}_{u}},{\bm{y}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})(\hat{\kappa}-2\lambda H+\lambda\hat{\rho}K)=-v(\widehat{E}\widehat{G}-\widehat{F}^{2})(\hat{\kappa}-(\lambda\kappa+\hat{\kappa})+\lambda\kappa)=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\widehat{C}_{v}},{\bm{y}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda(\widehat{N}\widehat{F}-\widehat{M}\widehat{G})+\hat{\rho}\hat{\kappa}(\widehat{M}\widehat{G}-\widehat{N}\widehat{F})=0

hold.
Thus we have the assertion.
∎

Since
\bm{y}
does not vanish near
p
,

\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
is a unit normal vector field of
\widehat{C}
by Lemma
5.1
.
Thus
\widehat{C}
is a frontal near
p
.
We remark that for the case of cuspidal edge, we have similar properties
using the map
\bm{y}
defined in Remark
3.3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f
be a front in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p
a singular point of the second kind of
f
.
Then the set of singular points
S(\widehat{C})
of
\widehat{C}
in (
5.1
)
coincides with the zero set of
\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho}
.
In particular,
p
is also a singular point of
\widehat{C}
.

Proof.

Let us take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
around
p
.
We denote by
\lambda^{\widehat{C}}
the signed area density function of
\widehat{C}

given by
\lambda^{\widehat{C}}=\det(\widehat{C}_{u},\widehat{C}_{v},\bm{e}_{2})=\left\langle{\widehat{C}_{u}\times\widehat{C}_{v}},{\bm{e}_{2}}\right\rangle
.
We calculate it explicitly.
Since
\nu
can be taken as

\nu=(h\times f_{v})/|h\times f_{v}|

and
\bm{e}_{2}
is perpendicular to
\nu
, we need

\widehat{C}_{u}\times\widehat{C}_{v}\equiv\hat{\rho}_{v}f_{u}\times\nu+\hat{\rho}\hat{\rho}_{v}\nu_{u}\times\nu+\hat{\rho}_{u}\nu\times f_{v}+\hat{\rho}\hat{\rho}_{u}\nu\times\nu_{v}\mod\nu.

Here for two
C^{\infty}
maps
\alpha,\beta\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\alpha\equiv\beta\mod\nu
implies that there exists a
C^{\infty}
function
\delta\colon U\to\mathbb{R}

such that
\alpha-\beta=\delta\nu
holds.
By the vector triple product and Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle f_{u}\times\nu

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(v\widehat{F}-e(u)\widehat{G})h+(e(u)\widehat{F}-v\widehat{E})f_{v}}{|h\times f_{v}|},\quad f_{v}\times\nu=\dfrac{\widehat{G}h-\widehat{F}f_{v}}{|h\times f_{v}|},

\displaystyle\nu_{u}\times\nu

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(e(u)\widehat{N}-v\widehat{M})h+\widehat{L}f_{v}}{|h\times f_{v}|},\quad\nu_{v}\times\nu=\dfrac{-\widehat{N}h+\widehat{M}f_{v}}{|h\times f_{v}|}.

Therefore, one can see that

\widehat{C}_{u}\times\widehat{C}_{v}\equiv\dfrac{\hat{\rho}_{u}}{\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}((\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G})h-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})f_{v})\\
+\dfrac{\hat{\rho}_{v}}{\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}((-v(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})+e(u)(\lambda\widehat{N}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{G}))h\\
\quad+(\lambda\widehat{L}-\hat{\kappa}(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F}))f_{v})\mod\nu.

On the other hand, for
\widetilde{\bm{V}}=(\widetilde{V}_{1},\widetilde{V}_{2})
as in (
3.5
), we note that

(\lambda\widehat{L}-\hat{\kappa}(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F}))\widetilde{V}_{1}+(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\widetilde{V}_{2}=0

holds by (
3.4
).
Since
\widetilde{V}_{1}\neq 0
near
p
, we have

(\lambda\widehat{L}-\hat{\kappa}(v\widehat{E}-e(u)\widehat{F}))=-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\dfrac{\widetilde{V}_{2}}{\widetilde{V}_{1}}.

Hence it follows that

\displaystyle\widehat{C}_{u}\times\widehat{C}_{v}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{\hat{\rho}_{u}\bm{y}}{\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}+\dfrac{\hat{\rho}_{v}}{\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}\left(\widetilde{V}_{2}h-(\lambda\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F})\dfrac{\widetilde{V}_{2}}{\widetilde{V}_{1}}f_{v}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{\hat{\rho}_{u}\bm{y}}{\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}+\dfrac{\widetilde{V}_{2}\hat{\rho}_{v}}{\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}(\widetilde{V}_{1}h-(\lambda\widetilde{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widetilde{F})f_{v})

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\bm{y}}{\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}\mod\nu.

Summing up, we obtain

(5.2)

\lambda^{\widehat{C}}=\dfrac{(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})|\bm{y}|}{\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\kappa}|h\times f_{v}|}.

Thus
S(\widehat{C})=(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})^{-1}(0)
holds.
Further, since
\hat{\rho}_{u}(p)=\widetilde{V}_{2}(p)=0
,

\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\rho}_{u}+\widetilde{V}_{2}\hat{\rho}_{v}=0

holds at
p
.
Hence
p
is also a singular point of
\widehat{C}

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p
be a singular point of the second kind of a front
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then the focal surface
\widehat{C}
satisfies
\operatorname{rank}d\widehat{C}_{p}=1
.
Moreover,
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
can be taken as a null vector field
\eta^{\widehat{C}}
of
\widehat{C}
near
p
.

Proof.

Take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
By a direct calculation, we see that

\widehat{C}_{u}=0,\quad\widehat{C}_{v}=f_{v}+\hat{\rho}_{v}\nu=f_{v}+\dfrac{\lambda_{v}}{\hat{\kappa}}\nu\left(=f_{v}+\dfrac{1}{\mu_{c}}\nu\right)\neq 0

hold at
p
.
Thus we have the first assertion.

We next show the second assertion.
By Lemma
3.5
, we have

d\widehat{C}(\widetilde{\bm{V}})=df(\widetilde{\bm{V}})+\hat{\rho}d\nu(\widetilde{\bm{V}})+(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\nu=(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\nu.

Since
S(\widehat{C})=(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})^{-1}(0)
,
d\widehat{C}(\widetilde{\bm{V}})
vanishes on
S(\widehat{C})
.
This implies that
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
is a null vector field of
\widehat{C}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
p
be a singular point of the second kind of a front
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then we have the following:

(1)

When
p
is a swallowtail of
f
,
then
p
is a non-degenerate singular point of
\widehat{C}
.

(2)

When
p
is not a swallowtail of
f
, then

p
is a non-degenerate singular point of
\widehat{C}

if and only if
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\neq 0
at
p
,
where
\bm{V}
is a principal vector associated to the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
.

Proof.

Taking a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
with
\lambda_{v}(p)>0
,
we have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})_{u}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{V}_{1})_{u}\hat{\rho}_{u}+\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\rho}_{uu}+(\widetilde{V}_{2})_{u}\hat{\rho}_{v}+\widetilde{V}_{2}\hat{\rho}_{uv}=(\widetilde{V}_{2})_{u}\hat{\rho}_{v}=-e^{\prime}(0)\hat{\kappa}\dfrac{\lambda_{v}}{\hat{\kappa}}=-e^{\prime}(0)\lambda_{v},

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})_{v}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{V}_{1})_{v}\hat{\rho}_{u}+\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\rho}_{uv}+(\widetilde{V}_{2})_{v}\hat{\rho}_{v}+\widetilde{V}_{2}\hat{\rho}_{vv}=\widetilde{V}_{1}\hat{\rho}_{uv}=-\hat{\kappa}\hat{\rho}_{uv}

at
p
by (
3.5
).
Thus by the definition,
p
is a non-degenerate singular point of
\widehat{C}
if and only if

(e^{\prime}(0),\hat{\rho}_{uv})\neq(0,0)
at
p
because
\lambda_{v}(p)\neq 0
and
\hat{\kappa}(p)\neq 0
.
In particular, the condition
e^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
holds when
p
is a swallowtail of the initial front
f
by Fact
2.2
.
Thus the first assertion holds.

On the other hand,
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})
is calculated as

\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})=V_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})_{u}+V_{2}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})_{v}=e^{\prime}(0)\lambda_{v}\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\hat{\rho}_{uv}\widehat{L}

at
p
by (
3.3
).
Thus
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\neq 0
if and only if
\hat{\rho}_{uv}\neq 0
at
p

when
p
is not a swallowtail,
that is,
e^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Hence we obtain the second assertion.
∎

When the initial front
f
has a cuspidal butterfly at
p
,
the focal surface
\widehat{C}
may have a degenerate singularity at
p
by this lemma.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front with a singular point of the second kind
p
.
Then the focal surface
\widehat{C}
as in (
5.1
) is a front at
p
.

Proof.

We take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Since
\widehat{C}_{u}=0
at
p
and
p
is a corank one singular point of
\widehat{C}
,

\widehat{C}
is a front at
p
if and only if
(\bm{e}_{2})_{u}(p)\neq 0
,
where
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
and
\bm{y}
is a map given by (
3.7
).
We note that this condition is equivalent to
d\bm{e}_{2}(\widetilde{\bm{V}})\neq 0
at
p

because
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
is parallel to
\partial_{u}
at
p
(see (
3.5
)).
By a direct computation, it holds that

(\bm{e}_{2})_{u}=\dfrac{\bm{y}_{u}|\bm{y}|^{2}-\bm{y}\left\langle{\bm{y}_{u}},{\bm{y}}\right\rangle}{|\bm{y}|^{3}}.

We consider the expression of
\bm{y}_{u}
at
p
.
Since
\lambda(p)=\widehat{F}(p)=\widehat{G}_{u}(p)=0
and
\widehat{G}(p)=1
,
we get

\bm{y}_{u}=-\hat{\kappa}_{u}h-\hat{\kappa}h_{u}+\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}_{u}f_{v}

at
p
,
where

can be written as in (
2.8
).
By Lemma
2.8
,
A=\left\langle{h_{u}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle=\widehat{F}_{u}-\widehat{E}
holds at

.
Thus
\bm{y}_{u}
satisfies

\bm{y}_{u}=-\left(\hat{\kappa}_{u}+\dfrac{\hat{\kappa}\widehat{E}_{u}}{2\widehat{E}}\right)h+\hat{\kappa}\widehat{E}(f_{v}-\mu_{c}\nu)

at
p
since
\mu_{c}=(\widehat{L}/\widehat{E})(p)
holds by Lemma
2.5
.
Since
\bm{y}=-\hat{\kappa}h
at
p
, we have

\bm{y}_{u}|\bm{y}|^{2}-\bm{y}\left\langle{\bm{y}},{\bm{y}_{u}}\right\rangle=\hat{\kappa}^{3}\widehat{E}(\widehat{E}f_{v}-\widehat{L}\nu)=\hat{\kappa}^{3}\widehat{E}^{2}\left(f_{v}-\mu_{c}\nu\right)\neq 0

at
p
.
Therefore
(\bm{e}_{2})_{u}\neq 0
at
p
, and hence
\widehat{C}
is a front at
p
.
∎

If a front
f
is a cuspidal edge at
p
,
then
\widehat{C}
is regular at
p
, and hence we can also consider
\widehat{C}
as a front near
p
.

Summarizing above results and using the criteria for singularities given by Fact
2.2
,
we have the following characterizations.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

be a front and

a singular point of the second kind.
Then we have the following.

(1)

The focal surface

given by (

.

(2)

\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\neq 0

and

\widetilde{\bm{V}}\widetilde{\bm{V}}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})\neq 0

.

Let us take a strongly adapted coordinate system

.
By Lemma

\widetilde{\bm{V}}

.

\widetilde{\bm{V}}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})(p)=e^{\prime}(0)\hat{\kappa}(p)\lambda_{v}(p)=e^{\prime}(0)\mu_{c}

\widetilde{\bm{V}}\widetilde{\bm{V}}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})(p)=-3e^{\prime}(0)\hat{\kappa}(p)\widetilde{V}_{1}(p)((\widetilde{V}_{1})_{u}(p)\hat{\rho}_{v}(p)+\widetilde{V}_{1}(p)\hat{\rho}_{uv}(p))-e^{\prime\prime}(0)\hat{\kappa}(p)\widetilde{V}_{1}(p)^{2}\hat{\rho}_{v}(p)

Thus we have the assertions by Fact

, Lemma

and Proposition

.
∎

The image of focal surface

5.2.
Contact between singular curves

We now consider contact properties of singular curves of the initial front and its focal surface.
First we start with the definition of contact between regular curves on the plane (cf.
[
12
, Page 74]
).

Definition 5.13
.

Let
\alpha:I\ni t\mapsto\alpha(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a regular plane curve.
Let
\beta
be an another plane curve defined by the zero set of a smooth function
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
\alpha
has
(k+1)
-point contact at
t_{0}\in I
with
\beta

if the composite function
c(t)=F(\alpha(t))
satisfies

c(t_{0})=c^{\prime}(t_{0})=\cdots=c^{(k)}(t_{0})=0,\quad c^{(k+1)}(t_{0})\neq 0,

where
c^{(i)}=d^{i}c/dt^{i}

(1\leq i\leq k+1)
.
Moreover,
\alpha
has
at least
(k+1)
-point contact at
t_{0}
with
\beta
if
the function
c(t)=F(\alpha(t))
satisfies

c(t_{0})=c^{\prime}(t_{0})=\cdots=c^{(k)}(t_{0})=0.

In this case, we call the integer
k
the
order
of contact.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p
a singular point of the second kind.
Assume that the curve given by
(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})^{-1}(0)
is regular at
p
.
Then the singular curve
\gamma
for
f
through
\gamma(0)=p
has

1
-point contact
(
resp.
2
-point contact
)
at
p
with
(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho})^{-1}(0)
if and only if

p
is a swallowtail
(
resp. cuspidal butterfly
)
of
f
.
Moreover,
\widehat{C}
has a cuspidal edge
(
resp. swallowtail
)
at
p
.

Proof.

Let us take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Define the function
c=\widetilde{\bm{V}}\hat{\rho}\circ\gamma\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\widetilde{\bm{V}}=\widetilde{V}_{1}(\partial_{u}+e(u)\partial_{v})
and
\hat{\rho}=0
on the
u
-axis,
the function
c
can be written as

c(u)=e(u)\widetilde{V}_{1}(u,0)\hat{\rho}_{v}(u,0)=e(u)\psi(u)\quad(\psi(u)=\widetilde{V}_{1}(u,0)\hat{\rho}_{v}(u,0)).

By the Leibniz rule, one can see that

(5.3)

c^{(n)}(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}~e^{(k)}(u)\psi^{(n-k)}(u),

where
\binom{n}{k}
are binomial coefficients.
Thus we have the assertions from Theorem
5.6
.

5.3.
Behavior of the Gaussian curvature of
\widehat{C}

Let
p
be a singular point of the second kind of a front
f
.
Then the focal surface
\widehat{C}
given by (
5.1
) has a front-type singularity at
p
by Theorem
5.6
.
Thus the Gaussian curvature
K^{\widehat{C}}
of
\widehat{C}
may diverge at
p
(
[
34
,
21
]
).
However,
K^{\widehat{C}}
might be rationally bounded at
p
.
Hence we consider the condition for
K^{\widehat{C}}
to be rationally bounded.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p
a singular point of the second kind,
where
\Sigma
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
\widehat{C}
be the focal surface of
f
associated to the unbounded principal curvature
given by (
5.1
).
Suppose that
\widehat{C}
has a non-degenerate singularity at
p
.
Then the Gaussian curvature
K^{\widehat{C}}
of
\widehat{C}
is rationally bounded at
p

if and only if
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.
Moreover, the limiting normal curvature of
\widehat{C}
vanishes at
p
.

Proof.

Let us take a strongly adapted coordinate system
(U;u,v)
centered at
p
.
Then we consider the condition that the Gauss map of
\widehat{C}
has a singularity at
p
.
The Gauss map of
\widehat{C}
is given by
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{y}/|\bm{y}|
,
where
\bm{y}
is defined by (
3.7
).
Define a function
\Lambda^{\widehat{C}}\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Lambda^{\widehat{C}}(u,v)=\det((\bm{e}_{2})_{u},(\bm{e}_{2})_{v},\bm{e}_{2})(u,v).

Then zeros of
\Lambda^{\widehat{C}}
correspond to singular points of
\bm{e}_{2}
.
By a direct calculation,
\Lambda^{\widehat{C}}(p)=0
is equivalent to

\det(\bm{y}_{u},\bm{y}_{v},\bm{y})(p)=0
.
Thus we consider this condition.
By a direct calculation, we have

\bm{y}=-\hat{\kappa}h,\quad\bm{y}_{u}=\ast_{1}h-\hat{\kappa}h_{u}+\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}_{u}f_{v},\quad\bm{y}_{v}=\ast_{2}h-\hat{\kappa}h_{v}-(\lambda_{v}\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}_{v})f_{v}

at
p
, where
\ast_{i}

(i=1,2)
are some constants.
Therefore one can see that

\det(\bm{y}_{u},\bm{y}_{v},\bm{y})=-\hat{\kappa}\left(\hat{\kappa}^{2}\det(h_{u},h_{v},h)+\hat{\kappa}(\lambda_{v}\widehat{M}-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{F}_{v})\det(h_{u},f_{v},h)-\hat{\kappa}^{2}\widehat{F}_{u}\det(f_{v},h_{v},h)\right)

holds at
p
.
By Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
,
we see that

h_{u}=\dfrac{\widehat{E}_{u}}{2\widehat{E}}h+(\widehat{F}_{u}-\widehat{E})f_{v}+\widehat{L}\nu,\quad h_{v}=\dfrac{\widehat{E}_{v}}{2\widehat{E}}h+Bf_{v}+\widehat{M}\nu

holds at
p
, where
B=\left\langle{h_{v}},{f_{v}}\right\rangle
.
Since
\widehat{F}=\left\langle{f_{v}},{h}\right\rangle
, we have

\widehat{F}_{v}=\left\langle{f_{vv}},{h}\right\rangle+B
.
On the other hand, since
\widehat{N}=\left\langle{f_{vv}},{\nu}\right\rangle
,
it follows that
\widehat{N}_{u}=\left\langle{f_{uvv}},{\nu}\right\rangle+\left\langle{f_{vv}},{\nu_{u}}\right\rangle
.
By
f_{u}=vh-e(u)f_{v}
, one can see that
f_{uvv}=2h_{v}
holds at
p
.
Moreover,
\nu_{u}=-\mu_{c}h
holds at
p
by Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
.
Thus we obtain
\widehat{N}_{u}=2\widehat{M}-\mu_{c}\left\langle{f_{vv}},{h}\right\rangle
at
p
,
where we used the relation
\left\langle{h_{v}},{\nu}\right\rangle=\widehat{M}
.
Hence we get

B=\left\langle{f_{v}},{h_{v}}\right\rangle=\widehat{F}_{v}-\dfrac{2\widehat{M}-\widehat{N}_{u}}{\mu_{c}}

at
p
.
Therefore, by
\mu_{c}=(\widehat{L}/\widehat{E})(p)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\det(h_{u},h_{v},h)=\{(\widehat{F}_{u}\widehat{M}-\widehat{F}_{v}\widehat{L})+\widehat{E}(\widehat{M}-\widehat{N})\}\lambda_{v},\quad\det(h_{u},f_{v},h)=-\widehat{L}\lambda_{v},\quad\det(f_{v},h_{v},h)=\widehat{M}\lambda_{v}

hold at
p
.
By the above calculations and
\hat{\kappa}=\mu_{c}\lambda_{v}
at
p
, we have

\det(\bm{y}_{u},\bm{y}_{v},\bm{y})=-\hat{\kappa}^{3}\widehat{E}\lambda_{v}\widehat{N}_{u}

at
p
.

On the other hand, the directional derivative
\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa

of the bounded principal curvature
\kappa
in the direction
\widetilde{\bm{V}}
is calculated as

\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa=-\hat{\kappa}\widehat{N}_{u}=-\lambda_{v}\mu_{c}\widehat{N}_{u}

at
p
.
Thus
\lambda_{v}\widehat{N}_{u}=-\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa/\mu_{c}
holds at
p
, and hence we have

\det(\bm{y}_{u},\bm{y}_{v},\bm{y})=\lambda_{v}^{5}\mu_{c}^{2}(\widetilde{\bm{V}}\kappa)

at
p
by
\lambda_{v}(p)^{2}=\widehat{E}(p)
.
Therefore the Gauss map
\bm{e}_{2}
of
\widehat{C}
has a singularity at
p

if and only if
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.
Thus by Fact
2.6
, we have the conclusion.
∎

Theorem
5.15

gives geometrical meanings of a sub-parabolic point of a front with a singular point of the second kind.
As a corollary of this theorem, we have the following relation
between behavior of the Gaussian curvature of the initial front and of the focal surface.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a front and
p
a singular point of the second kind of
f
.
Suppose that the Gaussian curvature
K
is either bounded on a sufficiently small neighborhood of
p

or rationally continuous at
p
.
Then the Gaussian curvature
K^{\widehat{C}}
of
\widehat{C}
is rationally bounded at
p
.

Proof.

First we assume that
K
is bounded near
p
.
By Proposition
4.4
,
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.
Thus by Theorem
5.15
,
K^{\widehat{C}}
is rationally bounded at
p
.

Next we suppose that
K
is rationally continuous at
p
.
By Proposition
4.3
,
p
is a sub-parabolic point of
f
.
Thus it holds that the Gaussian curvature
K^{\widehat{C}}
of
\widehat{C}

is rationally bounded at
p
by Theorem
5.15
again.
Therefore we have the assertion.
∎

Hypocoercivity in algebraically constrained partial differential equations with application to Oseen equations
Franz Achleitner, Anton Arnold, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
The long-time behavior of solutions to different versions of Oseen equations of fluid flow on the 2D torus is analyzed using the concept of hypocoercivity. The considered models are isotropic Oseen equations where the viscosity acts uniformly in all directions and anisotropic Oseen-type equations with different viscosity directions. The hypocoercivity index is determined (if it exists) and it is shown that similar to the finite dimensional case of ordinary differential equations and differential-algebraic equations it characterizes its decay behavior.

1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the long-time behavior and hypocoercivity structure of (an)isotropic Oseen equations from fluid dynamics. The Oseen equations describe the flow of a viscous and incompressible fluid at low Reynolds numbers and they have the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}&=-(b\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u\,,\quad t>0\,,\\
0&=-\diver u\,.\end{cases}

(1)

The Oseen equations arise when one linearizes the incompressible or nearly incompressible
Navier-Stokes equations
describing the flow of a Newtonian fluid,

\displaystyle{u}_{t}

\displaystyle=-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u\,,\quad t>0\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\diver u\,,

around a prescribed vector field
b
, that is independent of space and time, see e.g.
Shinbrot 1973
.

As it also includes a (linear) convective term, it can be seen as an improvement of the flow description by the Stokes equations, see
(
Batchelor 2000
, §4.10)
,
(
Lagerstrom 1996
, Chap. 2, §11)
.
The Oseen equations are a typical example of an operator differential-algebraic equation (DAEs) of the form

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
p\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{C}&-\mathbf{D}^{T}\\
\mathbf{D}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
p\end{bmatrix}\ ,\quad t>0\,,

(2)

with the unbounded operators
\mathbf{C}=b\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta
and
\mathbf{D}=\diver
, see
(
Emmrich and Mehrmann 2013
, p. 466)
.

DAEs of the form (
2
) also arise when the Oseen system is semi-discretized in space e.g. via a finite element discretization. This constitutes what is often called a vertical method of lines approach, see e.g.,
John 2016
;
Layton 2008
;
Rannacher 2000
.

In all described cases the equations have to be supplemented by suitable initial and boundary conditions. While in applications,
see e.g.
Lagerstrom 1996
;
Bae and Jin 2008
;
Galdi 2011
, the Oseen equation is typically considered on subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or on unbounded exterior domains, to keep the presentations and the technicalities of our analysis simple, we analyze its long-time behavior here on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}:=(0,2\pi)^{2}
,
similar to
Foias et al 2001
;
Temam 1995
;
Temam 1997
.

We perform the analysis using the concept of
hypocoercivity
which was introduced in
Villani 2009
in the study of unconstrained evolution equations (mostly partial differential equations) of the form
\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\operatorname{d}\!t}{x}=-\mathbf{C}x
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, where the (possibly unbounded) operator
-\mathbf{C}
generates a uniformly exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-\mathbf{C}t})_{t\geq 0}
.
More precisely, for hypocoercive operators
\mathbf{C}
there exist constants
\mu>0
and
c\geq 1
, such that

\|e^{-\mathbf{C}t}x_{0}\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\leq c\,e^{-\mu t}\|x_{0}\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\qquad\mbox{\rm for all}\,x_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,

(3)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
is another Hilbert space, densely embedded in
(\ker\mathbf{C})^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{H}
.
Often, the evolution equation
\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\operatorname{d}\!t}{x}=-\mathbf{C}x
is also called hypocoercive.
For infinitesimal generators
-\mathbf{C}
, an estimate (
3
) with
c=1
holds if (and only if)
\mathbf{C}
is coercive.

The long-time behavior of many systems exhibiting hypocoercivity has been studied frequently in recent years, including Fokker–Planck equations
Achleitner et al 2015
;
Arnold and Erb 2014
;
Villani 2009
, kinetic equations
Dolbeault et al 2009
;
Dolbeault et al 2015
, and reaction-transport equations of BGK-type
Achleitner et al 2016
;
Achleitner et al 2018
. In these works, in particular in
Achleitner et al 2016
;
Achleitner et al 2018
;
Arnold and Erb 2014
, the issue was to determine the sharp (i.e. maximal) exponential decay rate
\mu
, while to determine at the same time the smallest multiplicative constant
c\geq 1
is a rather recent topic, e.g. see
Achleitner et al 2019
.
Also the short-time behavior of linear evolution equations and its link to the
hypocoercivity index
was recently discussed for systems of ordinary differential equations in
Achleitner et al 2023a
;
Achleitner et al 2023c
and for Fokker–Planck equations
(
Arnold et al 2022
, Th. 3.6)
.

In this paper we consider (unbounded) operators
\mathbf{C}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that the operator
\mathbf{C}
is
accretive
, i.e.
\mathbf{C}
has a nonnegative self-adjoint part, and that
-\mathbf{C}
generates a
contraction semigroup
, i.e., it satisfies
\|e^{-\mathbf{C}t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
.

We characterize those accretive operators
\mathbf{C}
which are hypocoercive, i.e., those for which
-\mathbf{C}
generates a uniformly exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup.
Furthermore, based on our characterization and following the Lyapunov theory on Hilbert spaces, see e.g.
Datko 1970
;
Grabowski 1990
;
Hansen and Weiss 1997
, we will construct appropriate strict Lyapunov functionals.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows:
In §
2
we review key notions from hypocoercivity for finite dimensional ODEs and DAEs, and then extend it to the Hilbert space case.
In §
3
we apply these techniques to analyze Oseen equations on the 2D torus where the viscosity acts uniformly in all directions.
In Section §
4
we then study two anisotropic Oseen-type equations on the 2D torus, where the viscosity is different in the different space directions and the drift is either constant or space dependent.

2
Hypocoercivity for finite and infinite dimensional evolution equations

In this section we recall the concepts of
hypocoercivity and the hypocoercivity index
for (constrained) evolution equations. Our discussion will be in three steps, starting with finite dimensional cases, then infinite dimensional operator evolution equations with bounded generators, and finally some cases of unbounded generators.
We begin with the class of ordinary differential equation (ODE) systems

\dot{x}(t)=\mathbf{A}x(t)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,

(4)

with some function
x:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a constant matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. The second class are differential-algebraic equation (DAE) systems

\mathbf{E}\dot{x}(t)=\mathbf{A}x(t)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,

(5)

for a pair
(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{A})
of constant matrices
\mathbf{E},\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}^{H}
positive semi-definite.

Note that if
\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
is positive definite, then it has a positive definite matrix square root
\mathbf{E}^{1/2}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
, and by a change of variables
y:=\mathbf{E}^{1/2}x
and by scaling the equation by
(\mathbf{E}^{1/2})^{-1}
, the DAE (
5
) takes the form

\dot{y}(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}y\qquad\text{where }\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}:=(\mathbf{E}^{1/2})^{-1}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{E}^{1/2})^{-1}\ .

(6)

However, if
\mathbf{E}
is singular then the behavior of the two systems (
4
)–(
5
) is fundamentally different.

Writing a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
as the sum of its Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{H}=(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^{H})/2
and skew-Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{S}=(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}^{H})/2
, we have the following definition.

Definition 1

(Definition 4.1.1 of
Bernstein 2018
)
.

A matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
dissipative
(resp.
semi-dissipative
) if the Hermitian part
\mathbf{A}_{H}
is negative definite (resp. negative semi-definite).
For a (semi-)dissipative matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the associated ODE (
4
) is called
(semi-)dissipative Hamiltonian ODE
.
A DAE (
5
) with (semi-)dissipative matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
(semi-)dissipative Hamiltonian DAE
.

The notion (semi-)dissipative Hamiltonian is motivated by the fact that if
\mathbf{A}_{H}=0
and
\mathbf{E}
is the identity, then (
4
) is a
Hamiltonian system
with
Hamiltonian

\mathsf{H}=(x^{H}\mathbf{E}x)/2
, see
Mehl et al 2018
and also

(
Achleitner et al 2023b
, Remark 1)
and
(
Achleitner et al 2023b
, Theorem 3(E1))
.

In the following (to avoid too many indices), we often write semi-dissipative matrices
\mathbf{A}
in the form
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{J}-\mathbf{R}
with a skew-Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{A}_{S}
and a positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{R}=-\mathbf{A}_{H}
.

For ODE systems, hypocoercivity and the hypocoercivity index are defined as follows:

Definition 2

(
Achleitner et al 2023a
)
.

A matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
coercive
if its Hermitian part
\mathbf{C}_{H}
is positive definite, and it is called
hypocoercive
if the spectrum of
\mathbf{C}
lies in the
open
right half plane.

Let
\mathbf{J},\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
satisfy
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}^{H}\geq 0
and
\mathbf{J}=-\mathbf{J}^{H}
.
The
hypocoercivity index (HC-index)
m_{HC}
of the matrix
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}
is defined as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if it exists) such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{J}^{j}\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{J}^{H})^{j}>0

(7)

holds.

Remark 1
.

(1)

Clearly, (
7
) is equivalent to the condition

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{J}^{j}\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{J}^{H})^{j}\geq\kappa\mathbf{I}

(8)

for some
\kappa>0
, where
\mathbf{I}
denotes the identity matrix.
This variant will be needed in the infinite dimensional case below.
Condition (
8
) was also used in
Achleitner et al 2018
.

(2)

By a non-trivial result, the HC-index characterizes the short-time decay of semi-dissipative Hamiltonian ODE systems
\dot{x}(t)=-\mathbf{C}x(t)
: Its system matrix
\mathbf{C}
has a HC-index
m_{HC}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
if and only if

\|e^{-\mathbf{C}t}\|_{2}=1-ct^{2m_{HC}+1}+\mathcal{O}(t^{2m_{HC}+2})\quad\mbox{for }t\to 0^{+},

(9)

for some
c>0
. Here we used that
\|e^{-\mathbf{C}t}\|_{2}
is a real analytic function on some (small) time interval
[0,t_{0})
, see Theorem 2.7(a) in
Achleitner et al 2023a
.

This classification can be extended to the DAE case by considering the HC-index of the dynamical part, see Proposition
6.29
in the Appendix

6.1
.

Condition (
7
) is equivalent to the well known
Kalman rank condition
:

Lemma 1

(
(
Achleitner et al 2018
, Proposition 1, Remark 4)
)
.

Let
\mathbf{J},\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
satisfy
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{R}^{H}\geq 0
and
\mathbf{J}=-\mathbf{J}^{H}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\rank[{\mathbf{R}},\mathbf{J}{\mathbf{R}},\ldots,\mathbf{J}^{m}{\mathbf{R}}]=n\,.

(10)

2.

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\bigcap_{j=0}^{m}\ker\big(\mathbf{R}^{1/2}\ \mathbf{J}^{j}\big)=\{0\}\,.

(11)

3.

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that (
7
) holds.

Moreover, the smallest possible
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in
1
,
2
, and
3
coincide.

A similar equivalence result (on a subspace describing the dynamics of the system) has also been shown for DAE systems in
Achleitner et al 2023b
, see Appendix
6.1
here.

The characterization of hypocoercive matrices is related to results in control theory (as discussed in
(
Achleitner et al 2023b
, Remark 2)
):

Remark 2

(Connection to control theory: finite dimensional setting)
.

Consider a state-space system

\dot{x}(t)=\mathbf{A}x+\mathbf{B}u

(12)

for constant matrices
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. A pair
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
of square matrices
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is called
controllable
if the controllability matrix
[\mathbf{B},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B},\mathbf{A}^{2}\mathbf{B},\ldots,\mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{B}]
has full rank.
For a controllable pair
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, the smallest possible integer
k
such that the controllability (sub)matrix
[\mathbf{B},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B},\mathbf{A}^{2}\mathbf{B},\ldots,\mathbf{A}^{k-1}\mathbf{B}]
has full rank, is called the
controllability index
, see e.g.
(
Chen 1999
, §6.2.1)
,
(
Kailath 1980
, §6.2.1)
, or
(
Wonham 1985
, §5.7)
.

•

For semi-dissipative matrices
\mathbf{J}-\mathbf{R}
, the HC-index of
\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}
is one less than the controllability index of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=(\mathbf{J},\mathbf{R})
.

•

Moreover, for general input-output systems there is also the dual concept of
observability
.
Conditions like (
11
) are most often formulated in the context of observability, but not frequently in the context of controllability.

•

Whereas the controllability/observability indices have a clear interpretation in the discrete-time setting, see e.g.
(
Chen 1999
, p.171)
; their interpretation in the continuous-time setting is not so clear.
In particular, we are not aware of a characterization which is comparable to (
9
) in Remark
1
.

While the two conditions (
10
) and (
11
)
are clearly equivalent in the finite dimensional ODE case, only the latter one may also be used for linear operators.
In fact, the above results are easily extended to
evolution equations of the form

\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!}{\,\operatorname{d}\!t}{x}=-\mathbf{C}x\,,\quad t>0,

(13)

with
\mathbf{C}
a bounded or unbounded operator
on some infinite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
:

Definition 3

(
(
Kato 1995
, §V.3.10)
)
.

A linear operator
\mathbf{C}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})
, is said to be
accretive
if the numerical range of
\mathbf{C}
is a subset of the right half plane, that is, if
\Re\langle{\mathbf{C}x},{x}\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})
.
In this case
-\mathbf{C}
is said to be
dissipative
.
And
\mathbf{C}
is called
coercive
if there exists
\gamma>0
such that
\langle{\mathbf{C}x},{x}\rangle\geq\gamma\|x\|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})
.

Note that in this definition we follow the convention in semigroup theory, see e.g.
(
Engel and Nagel 2000
, Proposition 3.23)
; whereas to be consistent with Definition
1
we would have to call such an operator
semi-dissipative
.

The classical Lyapunov characterization of (uniformly) exponentially stable semigroups on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces easily extends to infinite dimensional settings:

Theorem 1

(
(
Curtain and Zwart 2020
, Theorem 4.1.3)
,
Datko 1970
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{A}
is the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\mathbf{T}(t)
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Then
\mathbf{T}(t)
is uniformly exponentially stable if and only if there exists a bounded positive operator
\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\langle{\mathbf{A}x},{\mathbf{P}x}\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{P}x},{\mathbf{A}x}\rangle=-\langle{x},{x}\rangle\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A})\ .

(14)

Equation (
14
) is called
Lyapunov equation
.

If
\mathbf{T}(t)
is uniformly exponentially stable, then the unique self-adjoint solution of (
14
) is given by

\mathbf{P}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{T}(s)^{*}\mathbf{T}(s)x\,\operatorname{d}\!s\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathcal{H}\ .

(15)

Next we recall the definition of hypocoercive operators in
Villani 2009
, which generalizes Definition
2
:

Definition 4

(
(
Villani 2009
, §I.3.2)
)
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a (possibly unbounded) operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with kernel
\ker\mathbf{C}
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space, which is continuously and densely embedded in
(\ker\mathbf{C})^{\perp}
, endowed with a scalar product
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
.
The operator
\mathbf{C}
is called
hypocoercive
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
if
-\mathbf{C}
generates a uniformly exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-\mathbf{C}t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow(\ker\mathbf{C})^{\perp}
, i.e. (
3
) holds.

Next we shall generalize the notion
hypocoercivity index
to the infinite dimensional Hilbert space case. As in the finite dimensional case, we consider operators of the form
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}
with
\mathbf{R}
self-adjoint and nonnegative, and
\mathbf{J}
skew-adjoint. For technical reasons (related to the operator domain) we shall first discuss bounded operators
\mathbf{C}
and then some special situations of unbounded operators
\mathbf{C}
.

Definition 5
.

Consider bounded operators
\mathbf{R},\,\mathbf{J}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathbf{R}
is self-adjoint and nonnegative, and
\mathbf{J}
is skew-adjoint, i.e.,
\mathbf{J}^{*}=-\mathbf{J}
.
The
hypocoercivity index (HC-index)
m_{HC}
of the (accretive) operator
\mathbf{C}:=\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})

is defined as the smallest integer
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(if it exists) such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mathbf{J}^{j}\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{J}^{*})^{j}\geq\kappa\mathbf{I}

(16)

for some
\kappa>0
.

Here we generalized the uniform condition (
8
), since the example
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{R}=\diag(1/j;\,j\in\mathbb{N})
on
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
would satisfy the non-uniform condition (
7
). However, the corresponding semigroup satisfies
\|e^{-\mathbf{C}t}\|=1,\,t\geq 0
, hence
\mathbf{C}
is not hypocoercive.

When defining next the hypocoercivity index for unbounded operators, we do not aim at the largest generality of equations (
13
), but rather we present a framework that covers the Oseen equations in §
4
below.
In our extension, the unbounded operator
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}
is accretive
with the following assumptions:

Assumptions
.

(A1)

The unbounded operator
\mathbf{R}
with dense domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R})\subset\mathcal{H}
is self-adjoint (and hence closed) and nonnegative in
\mathcal{H}
.
The operator
\mathbf{J}
is bounded and skew-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}
.

(A2)

\mathbf{J}
satisfies
\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R}))\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R})
.

(A3)

For the self-adjoint (and hence closed) operator
\mathbf{R}^{1/2}
defined on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R}^{1/2})\supset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R})
assume that
\mathbf{J}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R}^{1/2}))\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R}^{1/2})
.

Under these assumptions, standard arguments from semigroup theory show that
-\mathbf{R}
and hence also
-\mathbf{C}=-\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{J}
are dissipative with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R})
and, hence, infinitesimal generators of
C_{0}
-semigroups of contractions on
\mathcal{H}
, see e.g. §1.4 of
Pazy 1983
.
Moreover these semigroups are analytic, see
(
Engel and Nagel 2000
, Theorem III.2.10)
:
Due to
(
Engel and Nagel 2000
, Corollary II.4.7)
, the operator
-\mathbf{R}
generates a bounded analytic semigroup on
\mathcal{H}
, and
\mathbf{J}
is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded with
\mathbf{R}
-bound
a_{0}=0
, see
(
Engel and Nagel 2000
, Definition III.2.1)
.

For unbounded operators, the relation between the domains of the operator, its self-adjoint part and its skew-adjoint part can be subtle.
Therefore, different extensions of Definition
5
are reasonable.
For example, under the assumptions
(A1)
–
(A2)
, each term of the sum (
16
) is well-defined on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R})
.
However, the following extension to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{R}^{1/2})
is more convenient for the subsequent lemma:

Definition 6
.

Let the (accretive) operator
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{J}
satisfy the Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A3)
.
Then the
hypocoercivity index
(HC-index)
m_{HC}
of
\mathbf{C}
is defined as the smallest integer

Proof 2.2

To prove Lemma

To this end, we will introduce the following Hilbert spaces and operators.

After recalling

Remark 3.5

(Possible extensions)
.

1.

Our hypocoercivity analysis can be extended to isotropic Oseen equations on the 3D torus:
A modal decomposition yields again a family of decoupled DAE-systems (but) with more complicated system matrices.
See also the hypocoercivity analysis of BGK-type equations on 1D, 2D, and 3D tori in
Achleitner et al 2018
.

2.

In case of the isotropic Oseen equation (
19
) in the whole space and in exterior domains, solutions exhibit only algebraic-in-time decay, see
Bae and Jin 2008
.
For kinetic equations in whole space and without confinement, the hypocoercivity analysis has been adapted to prove the expected algebraic [instead of exponential] temporal decay, see
Bouin et al 2020
;
Arnold et al 2021
.
Similarly, the hypocoercivity analysis can be extended to Oseen equations in whole space.

When modifying (
19
) into an anisotropic Oseen equation with viscosity only in the

-direction, the dynamics becomes more interesting:
Depending on the prescribed convection field

, the dynamics may be hypocoercive or not.
This is the topic of the next section.

4
Anisotropic Oseen-type equations on the 2D torus

Motivated by rotating fluids in geophysics

Chemin et al 2000

Paicu 2005a
;
Paicu 2005b

In analogy, we shall study now Oseen-type models with anisotropic viscosity on the torus (

We consider these equations as a useful simplified mathematical model to illustrate (hypo)coercivity, rather than considering them for their physical interest (similar to

For simplicity of the presentation we again confine ourselves here to the 2D case, i.e. to

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=-(b(x)\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p+\nu\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u\,,

\displaystyle t>0,\ \text{on }\mathbb{T}^{2},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\diver u\ ,

Remark 4.6
.

Navier-Stokes equation with anisotropic viscosity in 3D, are used to model rotating fluids in geophysics

;
Paicu 2005a
;
Paicu 2005b
;
Chemin et al 2006

We analyze this model in two variants: For a constant convection field

.

Proof 4.10
.

First we introduce the maximal domain of
\mathbf{C}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
:
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})=\{u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\ |\ \mathbf{C}u\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))^{2}\}
. Since
\mathbf{C}
has constant coefficients,
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})
implies
\mathbf{C}u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. For future reference we also give their characterization in Fourier space (cf. (
21
) and
(
Foias et al 2001
, §II.5)
):

\displaystyle u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\{\phi_{k}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})\;\mbox{ with }\phi_{0}=0,\;k\cdot\phi_{k}=0\;\mbox{for all}\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\{\phi_{k}\},\;\big\{\big(i(b\cdot k)+\nu k_{2}^{2}\big)\phi_{k}\big\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})\;

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt\mbox{ with }\phi_{0}=0,\;k\cdot\phi_{k}=0\;\mbox{for all}\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(34)

To investigate the coercivity of
\mathbf{C}
, we compute for
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})
:

\langle{u},{\mathbf{C}u}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}=\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\partial_{x_{2}}u|^{2}\,\operatorname{d}\!x=\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\big[(\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_{1}}u_{1})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x_{2}}u_{2})^{2}\big]\,\operatorname{d}\!x,

(35)

where we have used that
\diver u=0
.
The last integral does not involve
\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}
.
Hence,
u:=[0,\sin(x_{1})]^{\top}
is a counterexample to coercivity on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
since
\langle{u},{\mathbf{C}u}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}=0
.

If
\mathbf{C}
was hypocoercive, the trivial solution
u=0
would be asymptotically stable on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
due to the decay estimate (
3
).
But for any
v\in H^{1}_{per}(\mathbb{T})
with
\int_{\mathbb{T}}v(x_{1})\,\operatorname{d}\!x_{1}=0
, the (undamped) traveling wave

u(x,t)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
v(x_{1}-b_{1}t)\end{bmatrix}

(36)

solves (
32
) in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
, in violation of (
3
).

This lack of hypocoercivity in the model (
32
) with constant
b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
b_{1}\neq 0
can be understood quite easily:
It includes drift and diffusion in the
x_{2}
-direction, but the uniform transport in the
x_{1}
-direction does not entail mixing between the different vertical layers.
If the flow field has only a vertical component
u_{2}
, possibly different in each vertical layer (as shown in (
36
)), then the flow field gets transported in the
x_{1}
-direction and remains incompressible.

The lack of hypocoercivity can also be verified by considering (
32
) as a constrained partial differential equation (PDAE), and bringing its modal representation into staircase form, see Example
1
in Appendix
6.1
.

Remark 4.11
.

In contrast to the above case
b_{1}\neq 0
, for the anisotropic Oseen-type model (
32
) with
b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
b_{1}=0
, we have to define the Hilbert space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
as in (
33
) endowed with the
L^{2}
-inner product.
Using that
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
u_{2}=u_{2}(x_{2})
and using (
35
), this shows that
\langle{u},{\mathbf{C}u}\rangle_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\geq\nu\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}/2
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})=\{u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))^{2}\}
.
Due to the Poincaré inequality
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\geq\|u\|_{L^{2}}
for all
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
(trivially obtained from the Fourier representation (
21
); see also
Evans 2010
;
Foias et al 2001
),
\mathbf{C}
is coercive on (the smaller) Hilbert space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
here.

4.2
Oseen-type equation with non-constant drift velocity

As second example we consider the anisotropic equation (
32
) with a non-constant drift field
b(x)=[b_{1}(x_{2}),0]^{\top}
such that

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=-b_{1}(x_{2})\ \partial_{x_{1}}u-\nabla p+\nu\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u\,,

\displaystyle t>0,\ \text{on }\mathbb{T}^{2},

(37a)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\diver u\ ,

\displaystyle t\geq 0\ .

(37b)

In order to simplify the detailed hypocoercivity analysis below,
we choose
b_{1}(x_{2})=\sin(x_{2})
; in this case only neighboring
k_{2}
-modes are coupled.
We conjecture that choosing another non-constant
x_{2}
-periodic drift field
b_{1}
would lead to an analogous result, but it would need a more cumbersome analysis.

In this direction, the existence of stationary solutions for the isotropic Oseen equation (
19
) with space-dependent drift

and

has been studied, see
Amrouche and Consiglieri 2011
.

In contrast to §
4.1
, this model does not preserve vertical layers of the flow field but rather mixes them, hence giving rise to hypocoercivity.
While the pressure could have been eliminated from the beginning in the model with constant drift term, this is not possible here.
Hence, (
37
) with
b_{1}(x_{2})
has to be considered as a (true) constrained PDE.

Analytic framework and well-posedness:

For the analysis of this case we apply the divergence to the evolution equation (
37a
), leading to the following condition on the pressure:

\begin{cases}\Delta p=-\cos(x_{2})\ \partial_{x_{1}}u_{2},\quad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{2},\\
\text{periodic boundary conditions for $p$},\\
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}p\,\operatorname{d}\!x=0,\end{cases}

(38)

where the last condition was added to ensure uniqueness of
p
.
For a given inhomogeneity
u\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))^{2}
, Equation (
38
) has a unique weak solution in the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{1}_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, which we denote by

. A standard elliptic estimate shows

\|p\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})/\mathbb{R}}\leq\|u\|_{(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})/\mathbb{R})^{2}}.

(39)

The generator of the flow (
37
) with
b_{1}(x_{2})=\sin(x_{2})
takes the form

-\mathbf{C}u=-\sin(x_{2})\ \partial_{x_{1}}u-\nabla p[u]+\nu\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u.

(40)

Note that
\diver u=0
and
p[u]
from (

\diver(\mathbf{C}u)=0
. The assertion
\int\mathbf{C}u\,\operatorname{d}\!x=0
follows trivially from
\int u\,\operatorname{d}\!x=0
.
As before, we choose the Hilbert spaces

\mathcal{H}:=H_{per}(\diver 0,\mathbb{T}^{2}),\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:=\{u\in\mathcal{H}\ |\ \int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u\,\operatorname{d}\!x=0\},

and the maximal domain of
\mathbf{C}
is
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{C})=\{u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\ |\ \sin(x_{2})\partial_{x_{1}}u-\nu\partial^{2}_{x_{2}}u\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))^{2}\}
, since the linear map
u\mapsto\nabla p
from (
38
) is bounded on
(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))^{2}
.

With this framework we have the following result:

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
b_{1}(x_{2})=\sin(x_{2})
in (
37
).
Then:

1.

\ker\mathbf{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e. the constant-in-

-orthogonal complement of

is
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
.

2.

The operator
\mathbf{C}
is not coercive on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}

Proof 4.13
.

1

For

to obtain

, and hence

.
So, using

in (

\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}(x_{1})=-\frac{\partial_{x_{2}}p(x_{2})}{\sin(x_{2})}=\lambda\qquad\text{with some }\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

2

As in §

(2)

Proof 4.15
.

5
Conclusions

After extending the notion of hypocoercivity index to evolution equations in (infinite dimensional) Hilbert spaces, we have performed the analysis of the long-time decay behavior of three variants of isotropic and anisotropic Oseen-type equations from fluid dynamics (for simplicity on a 2D torus). Due to the torus setting we used DAE theory in Fourier space to classify the hypocoercivity index. These equations are either coercive, hypocoercive with index 1, or even not hypocoercive (the latter showing exponential convergence only to a traveling wave solution).

Dimers for Type D Relativistic Toda Model
Kimyeong Lee, and Norton Lee

Abstract
We construct dimer graphs for type D relativistic Toda models by introducing impurities into the Y 2 ​ N , 0 Y^{2N,0} square dimer graphs. By properly placing the impurities and changing the canonical variables assigned to the 1-loops on the dimer graph, we perform a "folding" of the graphs, which yields the type D relativistic Toda lattice Hamiltonian and monodromy matrix.

Introduction

The relativistic Toda lattice (RTL) was first introduced in
[
1
]
with a given Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. There are two Lax formalisms for RTL: the Lax triad, which consists of three
N\times N
Lax matrices
[
2
,
3
]
, and Sklyanin’s
2\times 2

R
-matrix formalism
[
4

5
]
.

Type-A RTL, which is defined based on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=A_{N-1}
, belongs to a class of cluster integrable systems proposed by Goncharov and Kenyon
[
6
]
: an integrable system corresponding to a periodic planar dimer placed on a torus
[
7

. The dual graph of the dimer graph is a planar, periodic quiver. The quiver gauge theory, called
Y^{p,q}

Y^{p,q}
quiver
[
9
]
. These dimer graphs share the same toric diagram but have different conserving Hamiltonians.

A natural question to ask is whether the correspondence between the dimer and RTL can be extended to RTLs defined on Lie algebras other than type A. In this note, we will focus on the type D RTL defined on the affine Lie algebra
\hat{D}_{N}
. Our goal is to find the correct dimer graph whose perfect matching reproduces the commuting Hamiltonians of the type D RTL.

It turns out that the connection between relativistic integrable systems and five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric gauge theory proves to be quite useful in the search for the correct dimer graph. The connection between supersymmetric gauge theories and algebraic integrable models, known as the
Bethe/Gauge correspondence
, was first observed between four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories and non-relativistic integrable systems. The geometry of the low-energy states of the four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theory is identified with the phase space of an algebraic integrable system
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. The Seiberg-Witten curve of the supersymmetric gauge theory coincides with the spectral curve of the algebraic integrable model, thus sharing the same toric diagram.

The Bethe/Gauge correspondence can be extended to a supersymmetric gauge theory with gauge group
G
and eight supercharges, compactified on a circle of radius
r
. Its Seiberg-Witten curve corresponds to the spectral curve of the relativistic integrable system with
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(G)
algebra. The radius
r=\frac{1}{c}
acts as the inverse of the speed of light. Indeed, the Bethe/Gauge correspondence is how the Seiberg-Witten curve for five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
theory was constructed
[
14
]
.

In this note, we will focus on finding the correct dimer graph for the
\hat{D}_{N}
RTL. The Bethe/Gauge correspondence provides an advantage, as the corresponding gauge theory can be embedded into a larger
SU
gauge theory and then reduced in degrees of freedom by folding. In the case of type D,
SO(2N)

In this note, we construct the dimer graph based on the type A square dimer with impurities or flavors introduced. By properly placing the impurities and identifying parameters through folding, we successfully construct the dimer graph for the
\hat{D}_{N}
RTL. The monodromy matrix of the type D RTL is constructed from the Kasteleyn matrix of the dimer, thus recovering all the conserving Hamiltonians.

Classical Relativistic Toda of type D

The relativistic Toda lattice describes
N
particles on a line or a ring. The
n
-th particle’s position and momentum are denoted by
\mathtt{q}_{n}
and
\mathtt{p}_{n}
, which satisfy the Poisson commutation relation:

\displaystyle\{\mathtt{q}_{n},\mathtt{p}_{m}\}=\delta_{nm},\ n,m=1,\dots,N.

(1)

For a given Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the relativistic Toda lattice (RTL for short) is defined on its simple root system
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{g}}

[
1
,
16
]
. In this paper, we focus on the Toda lattice defined on the affine Lie algebra of type D. The Hamiltonian of the relativistic Toda lattice associated with the root system of the
\hat{D}_{N}
Lie algebra
[
17
,
18
]
is given by:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{\hat{D}_{N}}={\rm H}_{0}+{\rm J}_{1}+{\rm J}_{N}

(2)

where
{\rm H}_{0}
is the open relativistic Toda lattice Hamiltonian of type
A
:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{0}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}2\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{n})+2{g}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}e^{\mathtt{q}_{n}-\mathtt{q}_{n+1}}\cosh\frac{\mathtt{p}_{n}+\mathtt{p}_{n+1}}{2},

(3)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm J}_{1}&=2{g}^{2}e^{-\mathtt{q}_{1}-\mathtt{q}_{2}}\cosh\frac{\mathtt{p}_{1}-\mathtt{p}_{2}}{2}+{g}^{4}e^{-2\mathtt{q}_{2}},\\
{\rm J}_{N}&=2{g}^{2}e^{\mathtt{q}_{N}+\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}\cosh\frac{\mathtt{p}_{N}-\mathtt{p}_{N-1}}{2}+{g}^{4}e^{2\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}.\end{split}

(4)

Here,
{g}
is the coupling constant.
The total number of terms (written individually in exponential form) in the
\hat{D}N
RTL Hamiltonian (
2
) is
2N+2(N-1)+3+3=4N+4
. Note that there are two additional terms,
{g}^{4}e^{-2\mathtt{q}_{2}}
and
{g}^{4}e^{2\mathtt{q}{N-1}}
, which do not originate from the simple roots of
\hat{D}_{N}
.

In the non-relativistic limit, we scale the coupling constant
g\to rg
and the momentum
\mathtt{p}\to r\mathtt{p}
, where
r
is the inverse of the speed of light. The relativistic Hamiltonian can be expanded in powers of
r
as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{\hat{D}_{N}}

\displaystyle=2N+2r^{2}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\mathtt{p}_{n}^{2}}{2}+g^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}e^{\mathtt{q}_{n}-\mathtt{q}_{n+1}}+g^{2}e^{-\mathtt{q}_{1}-\mathtt{q}_{2}}+g^{2}e^{\mathtt{q}_{N}+\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(r^{4}).

(5)

The coefficient of
r^{2}
in this expansion corresponds precisely to the Hamiltonian of the type D non-relativistic Toda lattice.

2.1
Integrability

The integrability of relativistic Toda lattice (RTL) is characterized by the
R
-matrix
R_{a_{i},a_{j}}:V_{a_{i}}\otimes V_{a_{j}}\to V_{a_{i}}\otimes V_{a_{j}}
, which satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation:

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})R_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x)R_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x^{\prime})=R_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x^{\prime})R_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime}).

(6)

The
2\times 2
Lax matrix is a special case of this
R
-matrix with the choice of:

V_{a_{1}}=V_{a_{2}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}:=V_{\rm aux},\ V_{a_{3}}=\mathcal{H}_{n}

defined for the
n
-th particle.

\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the Hilbert space of a particle, and
V_{\rm aux}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
is called the auxiliary space.
On each of the lattice site we define a
2\times 2
Lax operator as a
GL_{2}
-valued function
[
18
,
19
,
20
]

\displaystyle L_{n}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}2\sinh\frac{x-\mathtt{p}_{n}}{2}&-{g}e^{-\mathtt{q}_{n}}\\
{g}e^{\mathtt{q}_{n}}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\text{End}(\mathcal{H}_{n}\otimes V_{\text{aux}})

(7)

where
\mathtt{p}_{n}=-\hbar\partial_{\mathtt{q}_{n}}
and
\mathtt{q}_{n}
are the canonically conjugated momentum and coordinate of the
n
-th particle.
The
R
-matrix is given by

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}\sinh\frac{x-x^{\prime}+\hbar}{2}&0&0&0\\
0&\sinh\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}&\sinh\frac{\hbar}{2}&0\\
0&\sinh\frac{\hbar}{2}&\sinh\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{2}&0\\
0&0&0&\sinh\frac{x-x^{\prime}+\hbar}{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\text{End}(V_{\text{aux}}\otimes V_{\text{aux}}).

(8)

The commutation relations between two matrix components of the Lax operator is governed by the Yang-Baxter RLL-relation (train track relation)

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})L_{a_{1}}(x)L_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})=L_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})L_{a_{1}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})

(9)

which can be verified true by direct computation.

The monodromy matrix
\mathbf{T}(x)
of the type A relativistic Toda lattice (with periodic boundary conditions) is an ordered product of the Lax matrices across
N
particles:

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(x)=L_{N}(x)L_{N-1}(x)\cdots L_{2}(x)L_{1}(x)\in\text{End}\left(\bigotimes_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}\otimes V_{\text{aux}}\right).

(10)

It is evident that the monodromy matrix
\mathbf{T}(x)
satisfies the same Yang-Baxter equation as the Lax operator:

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{a_{1}}(x)\mathbf{T}_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})=\mathbf{T}_{a_{2}}(x^{\prime})\mathbf{T}_{a_{1}}(x)R_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x-x^{\prime})

(11)

The spectral curve of the integrable system is defined by introducing the spectral parameter
Y
:

\displaystyle\text{q-det}(\mathbf{T}(x)-Y)=Y^{2}-\text{Tr}(\mathbf{T}(x))Y+\text{q-det}(\mathbf{T}(x))=0.

(12)

2.2
RTL with boundary

E. Sklyanin points out that the monodromy matrix for type BCD relativistic Toda lattices (RTLs) can be obtained by introducing a reflection matrix as a boundary condition
[
4
,
17
]
. The transfer matrix of an RTL with boundary conditions is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(x)={\rm Tr}K_{+}(x)\mathbf{t}(x)K_{-}(x)\mathbf{t}^{-1}(-x)

(13)

where
\mathbf{t}(x)\in\text{End}\left(\bigotimes_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}\otimes V_{\text{aux}}\right)
is the monodromy matrix of the type A RTL, and
K_{\pm}(x)\in\text{End}(V_{\text{aux}})
are the reflection matrices obeying the reflection equations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&R_{12}(x-x^{\prime})K_{-,1}(x)R_{21}(x+x^{\prime}-\hbar)K_{-,2}(x^{\prime})\\
&=K_{-,2}(x^{\prime})R_{12}(x+x^{\prime}-\hbar)K_{-,1}(x)R_{21}(x-x^{\prime})\end{split}

(14)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&R_{12}(-x+x^{\prime})K^{T}_{-,1}(x)R_{21}(-x-x^{\prime}-\hbar)K^{T}_{-,2}(x^{\prime})\\
&=K^{T}_{+,2}(x^{\prime})R_{12}(-x-x^{\prime}-\hbar)K^{T}_{+,1}(x)R_{21}(-x+x^{\prime})\end{split}

(15)

Given a simple solution
K_{\pm}(x)
to the reflection equation, one can verify that:

\displaystyle U^{T}_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{t}_{+}^{T}(x)K_{+}^{T}(x)(\mathbf{t}^{-1}_{+}(-x))^{T}

(16a)

\displaystyle U_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{t}_{-}(x)K_{-}(x)\mathbf{t}_{-}^{-1}(-x)

(16b)

satisfy the same reflection equation if
\mathbf{T}_{\pm}(x)
satisfy the Yang-Baxter equation (
11
).
The transfer matrix (trace of monodromy matrix) is the generating function of the integral of motion:

\displaystyle\begin{split}T(x)&={\rm Tr}\ U_{+}U_{-}={\rm Tr}K_{+}(x)\mathbf{t}(x)K_{-}(x)\mathbf{t}(-x)^{-1}\\
&=e^{\frac{N}{2}x}\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{n}e^{-nx}\right]\end{split}

(17)

The reflection matrices
K_{\pm}
that satisfy the reflection equation are given by:

\displaystyle K_{+}(\mathtt{q}_{1},\mathtt{p}_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{+}e^{\frac{x}{2}}-\alpha_{2}^{+}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}&\delta^{+}(e^{x}+e^{-x})-\beta^{+}\\
\gamma^{+}-\delta^{+}(e^{x}+e^{-x})&\alpha_{2}^{+}e^{\frac{x}{2}}-\alpha_{1}^{+}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(18a)

\displaystyle K_{-}(\mathtt{q}_{N},\mathtt{p}_{N})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{1}^{-}e^{\frac{x}{2}}+\alpha_{2}^{-}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}&\gamma^{-}-\delta^{-}(e^{x}+e^{-x})\\
\delta^{-}(e^{x}+e^{-x})-\beta^{-}&-\alpha_{2}^{-}e^{\frac{x}{2}}+\alpha_{1}^{-}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(18b)

These reflection matrices lead to the following (first) Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{1}=&\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}2\cosh\mathtt{p}_{j}+\sum_{j=2}^{N-2}{g}^{2}e^{\mathtt{q}_{j}-\mathtt{q}_{j+1}}2\cosh\frac{\mathtt{p}_{j}+\mathtt{p}_{j+1}}{2}\\
&+\beta^{+}+\beta^{-}+{g}\alpha_{1}^{+}e^{-\frac{\mathtt{p}_{2}}{2}-\mathtt{q}_{2}}+{g}\alpha_{2}^{+}e^{\frac{\mathtt{p}_{2}}{2}-\mathtt{q}_{2}}+{g}\alpha_{1}^{-}e^{-\frac{\mathtt{p}_{N-1}}{2}+\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}+{g}\alpha_{2}^{-}e^{\frac{\mathtt{p}_{N-1}}{2}+\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}\\
&+\delta^{+}{g}^{2}e^{-2\mathtt{q}_{2}}+\delta^{-}{g}^{2}e^{2\mathtt{q}_{N-1}}.\\
\end{split}

(19)

The boundary reflection matrices
K_{\pm}
for the RTL of type D, as discussed in
[
18
]
, are given by:

\displaystyle K_{+}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{g}[e^{\frac{x}{2}}2\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{1}/2-\mathtt{q}_{1})-e^{-\frac{x}{2}}2\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{1}/2+\mathtt{q}_{1})]&2\cosh x-2\cosh{\mathtt{p}_{1}}\\
{g}^{2}[2\cosh 2\mathtt{q}_{1}-2\cosh{x}]&{g}[e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{1}/2-\mathtt{q}_{1})-e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{1}/2+\mathtt{q}_{1})]\end{pmatrix},

(20a)

\displaystyle K_{-}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{g}[e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{N}/2-\mathtt{q}_{N})-e^{\frac{x}{2}}\cosh(\mathtt{p}_{N}/2+\mathtt{q}_{N})]&{g}^{2}[2\cosh 2\mathtt{q}_{N}-\cosh x]\\

(20b)

The monodromy matrix
\mathbf{T}(x)
of
\hat{D}_{N}
RTL is defined by incorporating both the reflection matrices and the Lax operators. It is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(x)=K_{+}(x)\tilde{L}_{2}(x)\cdots\tilde{L}_{N-1}(x)K_{-}(x)L_{N-1}(x)\cdots L_{2}(x).

(21)

where the

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-{g}e^{-\mathtt{q}_{n}}\\
{g}e^{\mathtt{q}_{n}}&2\sinh\frac{x+\mathtt{p}_{n}}{2}\end{pmatrix}=g^{4}L_{n}^{-1}(-x).

(22)

These
\tilde{L}_{n}(x)
operators are related to the original Lax operators
L_{n}(x)
by

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}(x)=g^{4}L_{n}^{-1}(-x).

(23)

The construction of

involves alternating beteween the reflection matrices

and

3
Dimer integrable system

The relativistic Toda lattice system is part of the broader class of cluster integrable models, which were explored in depth by Kenyon and others
[
6
]
. These models can be constructed using the
dimer model
–also known as
brane tiling
– approach. In this framework, a dimer model on a torus is shown to define a relativistic integrable system.

The dimer model provides a graphical way to encode the integrable structure of these systems, where the interactions between particles or variables are represented through a tiling of the plane (or torus) with dimers, which are edges covering vertices in a bipartite graph.

Kenyon’s work establishes that any dimer model placed on a torus leads to an integrable system, thereby connecting the combinatorial and geometric aspects of dimer models to the theory of integrable systems.

Let us give a quick review on the dimer model.

A
bipartite graph

\Gamma=(B,W,E)
embedded on a oritented 2-manifold
\mathcal{S}
is defined by the following components
[
22
]
:

•

Black nodes

B
: a finite set of black nodes on
\mathcal{S}
, each of which is assigned to the set
B
.

•

White nodes

W
: a finite set of white nodes on
\mathcal{S}
, each of which is assigned to the set
W
.

•

Edges

E
: A finite set of edges that are embedded as closed intervals on
\mathcal{S}
. Each edge connects a black node to a white node. Importantly, edges can only intersect each other at their endpoints.

For a bipartite graph
\Gamma=(B,W,E)
to qualify as a dimer model it must satisfy the following conditions:

•

Every equivalent node is on the boundary of
\mathcal{S}
.

•

Every faces of
\Gamma
is simply-connected.

In this context, we focus on the case where
\mathcal{S}=T^{2}
(the torus). When embedded on the torus, a dimer model is characterized by a
unit cell
. This unit cell has periodicity in both the horizontal and vertical directions, aligning with the periodic boundary conditions of the torus. The boundary of the unit cell represents the two fundamental cycles of the torus:

•

Horizontal Cycle: Periodic boundary condition in the horizontal direction.

•

Vertical Cycle: Periodic boundary condition in the vertical direction.

Definition 3.1
(Perfect matching)

A perfect matching
\mathbb{M}\subset E
is a subset of edges such that each node (both black and white) is connected by exactly one edge in
\mathbb{M}
. The edges in
\mathbb{M}
are oriented from black nodes to white nodes by default.

Definition 3.2
(Opposite orientation)

For any perfect matching
\mathbb{M}
, the notation
-\mathbb{M}
refers to the same set of edges but with reversed orientation: edges are now oriented from white nodes to black nodes.

Definition 3.3
(Edge weights assignment)

Each oriented edges
e\in E
is assigned a weight
w_{e}
based on its orientation and its interaction with the boundaries of the unit cell on the torus. The assignment of weights is defined as follows:

•

Vertical Boundary Crossings:

–

Positive Orientation (edge crosses from the left boundary to the right boundary of the unit cell): The weight is
w_{e}=X
.

–

Negative Orientation (edge crosses from the right boundary to the left boundary): The weight is
w_{e}=X^{-1}
.

•

Horizontal Boundary Crossings:

–

Positive Orientation (edge crosses from the bottom boundary to the top boundary of the unit cell): The weight is
w_{e}=Y
.

–

Negative Orientation (edge crosses from the top boundary to the bottom boundary): The weight is
w_{e}=Y^{-1}
.

•

Within the Unit Cell:

–

Edges that do not cross any boundary: The weight is
w_{e}=1
.

The weight assignment can be organized as

\displaystyle w_{e}=\begin{cases}X,&\text{if edge crosses vertical boundary with positive orientation. }\\
X^{-1},&\text{if edge crosses vertical boundary with negative orientation. }\\
Y,&\text{if edge crosses horizontal boundary with positive orientation. }\\
Y^{-1},&\text{if edgees cross horizontal boundary with negative orientation }\\
1,&\text{The edge lies within the unit cell. }\end{cases}

(27)

The weight of a perfect matching
\mathbb{M}
is a product over the weights of all its edges:

\displaystyle W[\mathbb{M}]=\prod_{e\in\mathbb{M}}w_{e}

(28)

The spectral curve
\mathcal{C}
of the dimer graph
\Gamma
is the ensemble of all possible perfect matchings’ weight:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\left|W(X,Y)=\sum_{\{\mathbb{M}\}}W[\mathbb{M}]=0\right.\right\}

(29)

The dual graph of the dimer graph is a planar, periodic quiver
[
23
]
. Quiver gauge theories described by dimer graphs arise from the worldvolume of a stack of D3-branes probing a single Calabi-Yau (CY) 3-fold. The connection between dimer models and quivers has trivialized the determination of the Calabi-Yau geometry
[
23
,

,
25
]
.
This curve happens to be the mirror curve of the Calabi-Yau 3-fold.
It can also be obtained by considering the
Kasteleyn matrix
of the dimer
[
26
]
. The Kasteleyn matrix is a weighted adjacency matrix of the graph.
A toric diagram
\mathcal{T}
can be associated to the dimer graph
\Gamma
based on its curve
W(X,Y)

[
8
]
.

We need some additional definition to establich relation between dimer graph and integrable models

]
.

Definition 3.4
(Reference perfect matching)

In this note we always choose the perfect matching that corresponds to the furthest point in the positive
X
axis on the corresponding toric diagram
\mathcal{T}
as the prefect matching
\mathbb{M}_{\rm ref}
.

Definition 3.5
(1-loop)

A 1-loop is a path connecting from a node in the graph to the same node which passes through the boundary of the unit cell.

To systematically construct 1-loop, one can consider the difference between a general perfect matching and the reference perfect matching
\mathbb{M}-\mathbb{M}_{\rm ref}
.

Definition 3.6
(
n
-loop)

The
n
-loop is product of
n
non-overlapping 1-loops.

Definition 3.7
(Poisson commutation)

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be two oriented loops on the dimer graph
\Gamma
. The Poisson commutation relation between the loops are defined as

\displaystyle\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\}=\epsilon_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\tilde{W}(X,Y)=\det(\mathbf{T}(X)-Y)=0.

5
Summery and future directions

We construct the dimer graph for the type D relativistic Toda lattice by introducing a double impurity into the type A square dimer. The folding process from type A with impurity to the type D dimer involves identifying the faces and vertices in the dimer unit cell, followed by a change of variables as described in Eq. (
38
) across the impurity insertion square. Additionally, certain square loops are modified by an overall factor as given in Eq. (
39
).
The resulting Hamiltonian of the double impurity dimer indeed reproduces the
\hat{D}_{N}
relativistic Toda lattice.

Furthermore, we construct the Lax matrix and reflection matrix from the Kasteleyn matrix of the new dimer graph using the Baker-Akhiezer function associated with the latter. By managing the coefficients of the Baker-Akhiezer function related to the double impurity and properly assigning the canonical coordinates to the edges of the dimer graph, we can reconstruct both reflection matrices at the boundaries.

Let us end this note with some potential future direction:

We are interested in the quantum type D relativistic Toda lattice (RTL). Recent studies show that the Bethe/Gauge correspondence provides an excellent tool, particularly for solving the wavefunction of quantum integrable systems. Find potential dimer graphs for type B, C, and E relativistic Toda lattices (RTLs). We anticipate that this can be achieved by appropriately introducing impurities into the embedded type A dimer graph, followed by a folding process.

A Poisson graph (or quiver graph) can be constructed based on the blowup of the Lie algebra Dynkin diagram. Studying the quiver graph provides insights into the spectral properties of the relativistic Toda lattice (RTL)

For types B, C, D, and E, the situation is more complex, as the dual of the quiver graph derived from the Dynkin diagram is often non-planar. It is an interesting topic to explore the potential relationship between the dimer graph we constructed for type D and the Poisson graph.

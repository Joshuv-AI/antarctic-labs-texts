Holomorphic functions on complex Banach lattices
Christopher Boyd, Raymond A. Ryan, Nina Snigireva

Abstract
0 0 footnotetext: Keywords: Holomorphic function; Homogeneous polynomial; Banach lattice; Regular polynomial; Bohr radius. 0 0 footnotetext: MSC(2020): Primary 46G20; 46B42; 32A70; Secondary 46E10; 32A05. We introduce and study the algebraic, analytic and lattice properties of regular homogeneous polynomials and holomorphic functions on complex Banach lattices. We show that the theory of power series with regular terms is closer to the theory of functions of several complex variables than the theory of holomorphic functions on Banach spaces. We extend the concept of the Bohr radius to Banach lattices and show that it provides us with a lower bound for the ratio between the radius of regular convergence and the radius of convergence of a regular holomorphic function. This allows us to show that the radius of regular convergence coincides with the radius of convergence for holomorphic functions on finite dimensional spaces and orthogonally additive holomorphic functions but that these radii can be radically different on ℓ p \ell_{p} for p > 1 p>1 .



1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to build a framework
for the study of holomorphic functions on
complex Banach lattices that takes account
of the lattice structure of the domain,
a feature that is
rarely acknowledged in the literature in this area
at present. The crucial concept is regularity —
we require that the homogeneous polynomials that
make up the Taylor expansion of a holomorphic function
are regular, meaning that they have a modulus which is
also a homogeneous polynomial, and that the convergence of the series is regular, in a sense that we will define. This leads to a theory
which is closer in many respects to the classical
theory for several complex variables. For example,
it is possible to define the concept of logarithmic
convexity for sets in a complex Banach lattice and
we can show that the domain of convergence of a power
series with regular terms has this property.

To date, regular polynomials on Riesz spaces and Banach lattices have largely
been studied for real spaces. The study of regular holomorphic functions means
that we now have to consider homogeneous polynomial on complex Banach lattices.
A complex Banach lattice is,
by definition,
the complexification of a real Banach lattice. Therefore, in Section 2 we will concentrate on the complexification process
and see how regular polynomials on a complex Banach lattice have real and imaginary parts which are the complexifications of regular polynomials on the associated ‘real part’.
Moreover, the space of regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials on a complex Banach
lattice can be given a norm, known as the regular norm, with respect to which
it becomes a complex Banach lattice.

In Section 3 we will initiate our study of power series of regular polynomials
and introduce the concept of regular convergence.
We will see that the natural domain of regular convergence of
such a power series is a logarithmically
convex solid domain. In Section 4 we introduce
regular holomorphic functions as those holomorphic functions
whose derivatives at every point are regular
and whose Taylor series at every point is
regularly convergent in some neighbourhood of the point.
We shall see that the space of regular holomorphic functions forms a
holomorphy type in the sense of Nachbin,
[
36
]
, and that the theory
of regular holomorphic functions is more closely related to the theory of
several complex variables than it is to the theory of holomorphic functions on
Banach spaces.

With each regular holomorphic function
f
and each point
a
in the domain of

f
we associate two radii of convergence, the radius of convergence of
f

itself about
a
and the radius of regular convergence. In general, the radius of
regular convergence is smaller than the radius of convergence.
In Section 5
we begin by observing how the idea of the homogeneous Bohr radii introduced by
Defant, García and Maestre,
[
20
]
, for homogeneous polynomials on

\mathbb{C}^{k}
can be extended to homogeneous polynomials on general Banach
lattices. From this viewpoint, the Bohr
radius can be seen as a measure of the
equivalence of the regular and supremum norms.
We will then see how these Bohr radii provide us with a lower bound
for
the ratio between the radius of regular convergence and the radius of
convergence. This will allow us to show that for finite dimensional spaces the
radii of convergence and regular convergence coincide.

In Section 6 we will show that on
\ell_{p}
, for
1<p<\infty
, it is possible to
construct regular holomorphic functions with radius of convergence equal to
1

yet having arbitrary small radius of regular convergence. Finally, in
Section 7 we consider complex
orthogonally additive polynomials and
holomorphic functions.
We prove that for complex orthogonally
additive polynomials the regular and
supremum norms coincide and we use
this to show that the radius of convergence is equal to the radius
of regular convergence on the space of orthogonally additive holomorphic
functions.

For further reading on the theory of holomorphic functions on Banach spaces we
refer the reader to
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
while for the theory of Banach lattices we refer the reader to
[
2
]
,
[
33
]

and
[
39
]
.

2
Regular polynomials on complex Banach lattices

First we recall the definition of a complex Banach lattice. We denote by
E_{\mathbb{C}}
the complexification
of a real vector space
E
. Thus,

E_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector space in
which every element
z
can be
expressed uniquely in the form

z=x+iy
, where
x,y\in E
.
The real vectors
x
,
y
are referred
to as the real and imaginary parts,
respectively, of
z
and we write

x=\re z
and
y=\im z
. If
E
is a Banach lattice,
the modulus of
z=x+iy\in E_{\mathbb{C}}
is the element
of
E
given by

|z|=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}=\sup\{x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta:0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\},

(1)

where
these expressions are
defined using the Krivine
functional calculus
[
30
, Section 1.d]
. A norm
is defined on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
by

\|z\|=\|\,|z|\,\|

and the triple consisting of
E_{\mathbb{C}}
with the modulus
and norm is, by definition, a

complex Banach lattice
. Alternatively, one may
take an axiomatic approach. This was done by Mittelmeyer
and Wolff
[
34
]
and they showed that every complex
Banach lattice defined in their way is the
complexification of a (real) Banach lattice as outlined
above. We recommend the paper by Buskes and
Schwanke
[
13
]
for an informative discussion
of the Mittelmeyer-Wolff axioms and a general treatment
of complexification of vector lattices.

Let
E,F
be (real) Banach lattices.
An
m
-homogeneous polynomial

P\colon E\to F
is generated by
a unique symmetric
m
-linear mapping

A\colon E^{m}\to F
,
in the sense that
P(x)=A(x,\dots,x)

for every
x\in E
.
We write
P=\widehat{A}
. The
polynomial
P
is said to be

positive
if

A(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\geq 0

for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\geq 0

and
regular
if it is the
difference of two positive
polynomials. If
F
is Dedekind
complete, then the space

\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
of
regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials
is a Banach lattice with the

regular norm
, defined by

\|P\|_{r}=\||P|\|
, where
\|\cdot\|
is
the supremum norm
[
12
]
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m},F
be real vector
spaces
and let

A\colon E_{1}\times\dots\times E_{m}\to F

be an
m
-linear mapping.
Then

A

has a unique extension to a
complex
m
-linear mapping

A_{\mathbb{C}}\colon(E_{1})_{\mathbb{C}}\times\dots\times(E_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}\to F_{\mathbb{C}}

[
8
, Theorem 3]
.
For
z_{j}=x^{0}_{j}+ix^{1}_{j}\in(E_{j})_{\mathbb{C}}
,

1\leq j\leq m
, we have

A_{\mathbb{C}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\sum_{\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m}=0,1}i^{\sum\delta_{j}}A(x^{\delta_{1}}_{1},\dots,x^{\delta_{m}}_{m})\,.

We shall say that a complex
m
-linear
mapping from
(E_{1})_{\mathbb{C}}\times\dots(E_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}
into

F_{\mathbb{C}}
is
real

if it is the complexification of
a real
m
linear mapping from

E_{1}\times\dots\times E_{m}

into
F
. Every complex
m
-linear
mapping
A
can be decomposed
uniquely in the form

A=A_{0}+iA_{1}
, where

A_{0},A_{1}
are real
m
-linear mappings.
We let

A_{0}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\re A(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
and
A_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\im A(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

for real arguments and then extend
A_{0},A_{1}

to the complexification as described above.
It follows that the vector space of
complex
m
-linear mappings is the
complexification of the space of
real
m
-linear mappings:

L(^{m}(E_{1})_{\mathbb{C}},\dots,(E_{m})_{\mathbb{C}};F_{\mathbb{C}})\cong L(^{m}E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)_{\mathbb{C}}\,.

If the spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
are the same
then clearly an
m
-linear mapping
A

on
E^{m}
is symmetric if and
only if its complexification,
A_{\mathbb{C}}
,
is.
Thus, if
E
,
F
are real vector spaces,
then every
m
-homogeneous polynomial

P=\widehat{A}\colon E\to F
has a unique
extension to a complex
m
-homogeneous
polynomial
P_{\mathbb{C}}\colon E_{\mathbb{C}}\to F_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Moreover, we have

P_{\mathbb{C}}(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom{m}{2\ell}(-1)^{\ell}A(x^{m-2\ell},y^{2\ell})+i\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom{m}{2\ell+1}(-1)^{\ell}A(x^{m-2\ell-1},y^{2\ell+1})\Bigr)

for every
z=x+iy\in E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
As these complexifications are canonical,
we usually omit the subscript
\mathbb{C}
,
so
P(z)
will be understood to mean

P_{\mathbb{C}}(z)
.
A complex
m
-homogeneous polynomial
that arises in this way, as the complexification
of a polynomial from
E
into
F
, will be
referred to as a

real polynomial
.
It follows from our remarks
above that the space of complex

m
-homogeneous polynomials
is the complexification of the space
of real
m
-homogeneous polynomials:

P(^{m}E_{\mathbb{C}};F_{\mathbb{C}})\cong P(^{m}E;F)_{\mathbb{C}}\,.

Every complex
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P

can be written uniquely in the form

P=P_{0}+iP_{1}
, where
P_{0},P_{1}
are real

m
-homogeneous polynomials, defined,
for real arguments by

P_{0}(x)=\re P(x)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{1}(x)=\im P(x)

(2)

for
x\in E
. The polynomials
P_{0},P_{1}
are then extended
by complexification to all of

E_{\mathbb{C}}
. We note that it is not
correct so say that

P_{0}(z)=\re P(z)
for
complex vectors
z
;
the identity
(\re P)(z)=\re(P(z))
is only valid
for real arguments.
To give a simple example,
consider the
2
-homogeneous
polynomial
P(z)=z^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}
.
We have
P(z)=(x^{2}-y^{2})+i(2xy)

for
z=x+iy
. However,

x^{2}-y^{2}
and
2xy
are not
the real and imaginary parts of
P
.
Indeed,
P
is a real polynomial, being
the complexification of the real
polynomial
P(t)=t^{2}
and so its
imaginary part is zero.

We now consider polynomials on Banach
lattices. Let
E
be a complex Banach
lattice. The real part of
E
,
denoted by
E_{\mathbb{R}}
, is defined to be
the real linear span of the set

\{|z|:z\in E\}

and is a (real) Banach lattice,
with the norm induced from
E
.
Then
E
, as a vector space,
is the complexification of
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.
And we recall that the norms
on
E
and
E_{\mathbb{R}}
are connected by the
relation
\|z\|_{E}=\||z|\|_{E_{\mathbb{R}}}
for
z\in E
.
Our definition of regularity, positivity, respectively, for multilinear
and polynomial mappings is the same
as the linear case
[
33
, Section 2.2]
.
So we say that an
m
-linear mapping
on
E^{m}
,
or an
m
-homogeneous polynomial on
E
,
is regular, positive, respectively, if both its real
and imaginary parts are regular, positive on
(E_{\mathbb{R}})^{m}

or
E_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.

If

E,F
are (real) Banach lattices with
F
Dedekind
complete, then the space
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

of regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials, with
the regular norm
\|P\|_{r}=\||P|\|
,
is a Banach lattice
[
12
]
.
Now let
E,F
be complex
Banach lattices, with
F
Dedekind complete.
Then it follows from the above that
the space
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{n}{E};{F})

of regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials
is the complexification
of the space
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{n}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{F_{\mathbb{R}}})

of real, regular
m
-homogeneous
polynomials:

\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{n}{E};{F})=\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{n}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{F_{\mathbb{R}}})\bigr)_{\mathbb{C}}\,.

Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{n}{E};{F})

can be endowed with a complex Banach lattice structure,
with the norm given by
\|P\|_{r}=\|\,|P|\,\|
.
We summarize these observations:

Proposition 1

Let
E
,
F
be complex Banach lattices, with
F

Dedekind complete.
The space of regular
m
-homogeneous
polynomials from
E
into
F

with the regular norm
\|P\|_{r}=\|\,|P|\,\|
is
a Dedekind complete complex Banach lattice.

Note that this result in particular tells us that the absolute
value of a complex regular

m
-homogeneous polynomial

P=P_{0}+iP_{1}
satisfies

|P|=\sqrt{|P_{0}|^{2}+|P_{1}|^{2}}\,,

where this formula is understood in the sense of the Krivine functional calculus
[
30
]
.

To illustrate the difference between
the regular and supremum norms,
we have the following useful
result by Choi-Kim-Ki
[
16
]
:

Proposition 2
(
[
16
]
, Theorem 2.4)

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
with
|a|<1
,
|b|<1

and
2<|c|\leq 4
.
Suppose
P(x,y)=ax^{2}+by^{2}+cxy\in\mathcal{P}(^{2}{\ell_{1}^{2}})
.
Then, over both the real and complex numbers,

\|P\|=1\quad\text{ if and only if }\quad 4|c|-c^{2}=4(|a+b|-ab)\,.

We can use this result to construct examples of polynomials
on
\ell_{1}^{2}
for which the regular
norm is greater than the supremum norm.

Example 1

On
\ell_{1}^{2}
, let

P(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+(2+\sqrt{3})xy\,.

Then

\|P\|=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\|P\|_{r}=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{4}>1\,.

over both
the real and complex numbers.

The fact that
\|P\|=1
follows from the Choi-Kim-Ki result,
but can also be seen by means of an elementary calculation. We have

|P|(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+(2+\sqrt{3})xy

and thus

\|P\|_{r}\geq|P|\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{4}>1\,.

A calculation shows
that in fact,
\|P\|_{r}=\dfrac{3+\sqrt{3}}{4}
in the real case.
As the coefficients of
|P|
are positive, the regular norm
over the complex numbers is the same.

The absolute value of a regular linear
mapping
T\colon E\to F
between complex
Banach lattices satisfies

|T(z)|\leq|T|(|z|)

for every
z\in E

[
33
]
.
We will show that there is a corresponding result
for complex regular polynomials.
For this, we need a little
preparation.

Let
E,F
be real Banach lattices,
with
F
Dedekind complete.
The Banach lattice of
regular
m
-linear mappings
from
E
into
F
is denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

and the Banach sublattice of
symmetric, regular
m
-linear mappings
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{s}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
.
The definition of the
order relation shows that

\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
and

\mathcal{L}^{s}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

are isomorphic as vector lattices.
Bu and Buskes
[
12
]
showed that
for
A\in\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
,
the absolute value
|A|
is
given by

|A|(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\sup\Bigl\{\,\sum_{i_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{m}}|A(u^{1}_{i_{1}},\dots,u^{m}_{i_{m}})|:u^{1}\in\Pi(x_{1}),\dots,u^{m}\in\Pi(x_{m})\Bigr\}

for
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\geq 0
.
Here,
\Pi(x)
denotes the set
of partitions of a positive element

x
, namely, finite sets of positive
vectors whose sum is
x
.
Now
\Pi(x)
is directed by set
inclusion and so the iterated suprema
that appear in the formula above
can be interpreted as limits
of increasing nets, with

\Pi(x_{1}),\dots,\Pi(x_{m})

as the indexing sets. Thus, we
can interchange the operations
of supremum with the finite sums.

If we define

A^{(k)}\colon E^{m-k}\to\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})

by

A^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m-k})(x_{m-k+1},\dots,x_{m})=A(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\,,

then the correspondence

A\leftrightarrow A^{(k)}

is an
isometric isomorphism between

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

and
\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})})
.
Furthermore, it follows from the remarks above
that we have
|A|^{(k)}=|A^{(k)}|
for
every
A\in\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
and
every
k
. Therefore, this correspondence
is also a lattice homomorphism.
Thus, the Banach lattices

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

and
\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})})

are isometrically lattice isomorphic.

Proposition 3

Let
E
,
F
be real or complex Banach lattices,
with
F
Dedekind complete. Then the mapping

A\in\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})\mapsto A^{(k)}\in\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})})

is a Banach lattice isometric isomorphism
for every
k=1,\dots,m-1
.

Proof:

We have already established this result
for real Banach lattices.
Now let
E,F
be complex Banach
lattices, with
F
Dedekind complete.
Then, complexifying the above isomorphism,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{F_{\mathbb{R}}})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{F_{\mathbb{R}}})})_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E_{\mathbb{R}}};{F_{\mathbb{R}}})_{\mathbb{C}}})\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})})\,.

Therefore

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
and

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})})

are isometrically isomorphic as
complex Banach lattices.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

Proposition 4

Let
E
,
F
be complex Banach lattices, with
F

Dedekind complete and let

P\colon E\to F
be a regular

m
-homogeneous polynomial. Then

|P(z)|\leq|P|(|z|)

for all
z\in E
.

Proof:

Let
A
be the associated
regular
m
-linear mapping.
We will show that

|A(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})|\leq|A|(|z_{1}|,\dots,|z_{m}|)

for all
z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\in E
.
The proof is by induction.
The case
m=1
is the linear
result
[
33
]
. Assume the result
holds for
(m-1)
-linear
mappings. Then,
using Proposition
3
,

\displaystyle|A(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})|

\displaystyle=|A^{(m-1)}(z_{1})(z_{2},\dots,z_{m})|\leq|A^{(m-1)}(z_{1})|(|z_{2}|,\dots,|z_{m}|)

\displaystyle\leq|A^{(m-1)}|(|z_{1}|)(|z_{2}|,\dots,|z_{m}|))=|A|(|z_{1}|,\dots,|z_{m}|)\,.

Thus the result follows for every
m
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

Our next result shows that the complexification process preserves the absolute value.

Proposition 5

Let
E
,
F
be real Banach lattices, with
F
Dedekind
complete and let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
. Then

|P_{\mathbb{C}}|=|P|_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof:

We begin with the case
m=1
. Let
T\colon E\to F

be a regular linear operator. For
u\in E_{+}
we have

\displaystyle|T_{\mathbb{C}}|(u)

\displaystyle=\sup\{|T_{\mathbb{C}}(x+iy)|:x,y\in E,|x+iy|\leq u\}=\sup\{|Tx+iTy|,|x+iy|\leq u\}

\displaystyle=\sup\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\{|(\cos\theta)Tx+(\sin\theta)Ty|,|x+iy|\leq u\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\{|T||(\cos\theta)x+(\sin\theta)y|,|x+iy|\leq u\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{|T||x+iy|,|x+iy|\leq u\}=|T|(u)=|T|_{\mathbb{C}}(u)\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|T|_{\mathbb{C}}(u)

\displaystyle=|T|(u)=\sup\{|T(v)|:v\in E,|v|\leq u\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{|T(x+iy)|:x,y\in E,|x+iy|\leq u\}=|T_{\mathbb{C}}|(u)

for every
u\in E_{+}
. Therefore
|T_{\mathbb{C}}|=|T|_{\mathbb{C}}
on
E_{+}
and
it follows that this holds on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.

For the case
m>1
we have, using
Proposition
3
, Banach lattice
isomorphisms

\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m}{E_{\mathbb{C}}};{F_{\mathbb{C}}})\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}({E_{\mathbb{C}}};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-1}{E_{\mathbb{C}}};{F_{\mathbb{C}}})})\cong\mathcal{L}_{r}({E};{\mathcal{L}_{r}(^{m-1}{E};{F})})_{\mathbb{C}}\,.

Thus the result follows by induction on
m
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

The supremum norm of a polynomial on real Banach spaces is not,
in general, preserved by complexification. Gustavo, Muñoz, Tonge
have shown that if
E
,
F
are
real Banach spaces and
P\colon E\to F
is a bounded
m
-homogeneous
polynomial, then the norm of its complexification satisfies

\|P_{\mathbb{C}}\|_{\nu}\leq 2^{m-1}\|P\|

where
\nu
is any natural complexification process

[
35
, Prop. 18]
, and this inequality is sharp.
In the Banach lattice case, the regular norm
is much better behaved.

Theorem 1

Let
E
,
F
be real Banach lattices, with
F
Dedekind
complete and let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
. Then

\|P_{\mathbb{C}}\|_{r}=\|P\|_{r}
.

Proof:

Clearly, we have
\|P\|_{r}\leq\|P_{\mathbb{C}}\|_{r}
. Conversely,
using Proposition
5
,

\displaystyle\|P_{C}\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\|\,|P_{\mathbb{C}}|\,\|=\|\,|P|_{\mathbb{C}}\|=\sup\{\bigl||P|_{\mathbb{C}}(z)\bigr|:z\in E_{\mathbb{C}},\|z\|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{|P|_{\mathbb{C}}(|z|):z\in E_{\mathbb{C}},\|z\|\leq 1\}=\sup\{|P|(|z|):z\in E_{\mathbb{C}},\|z\|\leq 1\}=\|P\|_{r}\,.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

Now let
E
,
F
be complex
Banach lattices. A complex linear
operator
T\colon E\to F
is a homomorphism
if
|T(z)|=T(|z|)
for every
z\in E
.
This is equivalent to
T
being the
complexification of a lattice homomorphism
between the real vector lattices
E_{\mathbb{R}}
and
F_{\mathbb{R}}

[
39
, p. 136]
.

Recall that the
k
th Fréchet
derivative of an
m
-homogeneous
polynomial
P=\widehat{A}\colon E\to F

between real or complex vector spaces
is given by

\widehat{d}^{k}P(x)(y)=m(m-1)\dots(m-k+1)A(x^{m-k}y^{k})\,.

Thus
\widehat{d}^{k}P

is, up to a constant multiple, the
k
-homogeneous polynomial
associated with the

(m-k)
-linear

.
The following proposition follows immediately from this fact.

Proposition 6

Let

The Krivine functional calculus

Proof:
Let

3
Power series with regular terms

Absolute convergence of a power
series
\sum_{m}c_{m}z^{m}
in one complex
variable at a point requires that
the numbers
|c_{m}z^{m}|
are summable.
Of course, this is the same as
summability of the numbers
|c_{m}||z|^{m}
.
However, on a Banach lattice, these
lead to two different conditions.

We shall say that a power series
\sum_{m}P_{m}

with regular terms on
a complex Banach lattice
E

is
regularly convergent
at a point

z\in E
if the series
\sum_{m}|P_{m}|(|z|)
converges.
Note that this is stronger than absolute convergence,
since

|P_{m}(z)|\leq|P_{m}|(|z|)

for all
z\in E
.
We shall see that regular convergence
is in fact a strictly stronger
condition than absolute convergence.

If
E
has a
1
-unconditional Schauder basis,
then, by
[
25
]
, every regular
m
-homogeneous polynomial

P_{m}
on
E
has a pointwise unconditionally convergent
monomial expansion of the form

P_{m}(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\,.

The converse is also true: every unconditionally
pointwise convergent monomial expansion of degree
m
defines a regular
m
-homogeneous polynomial.
Furthermore, by
[
25
]
, we have

|P_{m}|(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}|z^{\alpha}\,.

Thus a power series
\sum_{m}P_{m}(z)

with regular terms
can be written as a formal monomial expansion

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}

and regular convergence of the power series at a point
z

is equivalent to the condition

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\mathbb{N})}}|c_{\alpha}||z|^{\alpha}<\infty\,.

So we see that regular convergence of a power
series is a natural
abstraction to the Banach lattice setting
of the familiar concept of pointwise absolute
convergence of a monomial expansion.

For a power series
f=\sum_{m}P_{m}

on a complex Banach space,
the radius of convergence is
given by the formula

r(f,0)=\Bigl(\limsup\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}\Bigr)^{-1}\,.

This number is the supremum of the set
of nonnegative real numbers
r
for
which the series is uniformly convergent
on the closed ball of radius
r
. Bearing in
mind that the regular norm of
a regular
m
-homogeneous polynomial

P_{m}
is given by
\|P_{m}\|_{r}=\||P_{m}|\|
, we may define
the
radius of regular convergence
of a power series

f=\sum_{m}P_{m}
to be

|r|(f,0)=\Bigl(\limsup\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m}\Bigr)^{-1}\,.

This is the supremum of the set
of nonnegative real numbers
\rho
for
which the series is uniformly
regularly convergent in the closed
ball of radius
\rho
.
Since the regular norm is at least
as big as the uniform norm,
we see that the radius of regular
convergence is no bigger than
the radius of convergence, i.e.,

|r|(f,0)\leq r(f,0)\,.

We shall see that these
radii can be different.

The radius of regular convergence
gives some useful information about
the behaviour of a power series.
However, this information is somewhat
limited as the natural domain
of convergence is not generally a ball.
Let us recall some facts about power
series in several complex variables.
A subset
D
of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
is
a complete Reinhardt domain
if
z\in D
implies that

w\in D
whenever
|w_{j}|\leq|z_{j}|

for
1\leq j\leq k
.
A complete
Reinhardt domain
D
is said to be
logarithmically convex
if, for every
z,w\in D

and every
\theta\in(0,1)

the point

(|z_{1}|^{\theta}|w_{1}|^{1-\theta},\dots,|z_{k}|^{\theta}|w_{k}|^{1-\theta})

belongs to
D
.
The domain of convergence of a
power series on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
is defined
to be the interior of the set
of points at which the series
is absolutely convergent.
In general, the domain of
convergence is either a non-empty
open set, or is empty.
It is a fundamental result
for several complex variables that
the domain of convergence of a power
series is a logarithmically convex
Reinhardt domain
[
27
, Section 2.4]
.

Example 2

This result of Matos
[
31
]

illustrates the difference in behaviour
of the domain of convergence in finite
and infinite dimensions:
if
z=(z_{j})
is a sequence of
complex numbers, then the series

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\mathbb{N})}}z^{\alpha}

is absolutely convergent if and only
if
z
belongs to
\ell_{1}

and
|z_{j}|<1
for every
j
.
Therefore, on
\ell_{1}
this
monomial expansion has a non-empty
domain of convergence. However, on
c_{0}
,
the set of points of absolute convergence
is a dense subset of the closed unit ball
with no interior points.

We now see how some results
from several complex variables
can be formulated for power series
on complex Banach lattices.
We recall the following elementary fact:
If
\sum c_{\alpha}(z-w)^{\alpha}
is a monomial
expansion on
C^{k}
whose terms are
bounded at some point
z=w+a

where
a>0
,
then the expansion is absolutely
convergent on the polydisc

D(w,a)=\{z\in C^{k}:|z_{j}-w_{j}|\leq a_{j},1\leq j\leq n\}
and the convergence
is uniform on smaller polydiscs

D(w,\lambda a)
,
0<\lambda<1
.
We extend the definition of a polydisc
to complex Banach lattices in the obvious
way. If
a
is a positive element
in a complex Banach lattice
E
,
the polydisc with centre
w

and polyradius
a

is
D(w,a)=\{z\in E:|z-w|\leq a\}
.
We note that
D(0,a)
is the closed
unit ball of the principal ideal
E_{a}

with the order unit norm defined by
a
.
Note that if a power series
centred at the origin is regularly
convergent at a point
z
, then it
is regularly convergent at every
point in the polydisc
D(0,|z|)
.

Lemma 1

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice
and let
(P_{m})
be a sequence
of regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials
on
E
.
If
\{P_{m}(a)\}
is a bounded subset of

\mathbb{C}
for some positive element
a\in E
,
then the power series
\sum_{m}P_{m}

is regularly convergent on the polydisc

D(0,a)
and the convergence is
uniform on every smaller polydisc

D(0,\lambda a)
,

0<\lambda<1
.

Proof:

This follows from the fact that
if
|z|\leq\lambda a
, then

||P_{m}|(z)|\leq\lambda^{m}|P_{m}|(a)
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

This is stronger than the corresponding result
for Banach spaces. In general, all we can deduce
from the boundedness of the values
P_{m}(z)

at some point in a Banach space is that the
power series
\sum_{m}P_{m}
is absolutely
convergent in the one dimensional complex disc determined
by the point
z
.

We recall that a set
D
in a real or complex
Banach lattice is said to be solid if
z\in D

and
|w|\leq|z|
imply that
w
belongs to

D
. For subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the solid
sets are precisely the complete Reinhardt domains.

A solid subset
D
of
E
is said to be

logarithmically convex
if, for
every
x,y\in D
,
\theta\in(0,1)
,
the point
|x|^{\theta}|y|^{1-\theta}
also
belongs to
D
.
The domain of convergence
of a power series in finite
dimensions is logarithmically convex. The same is true for regularly convergent
power series on a Banach lattice:

Theorem 2

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice and let
\sum_{m}P_{m}

be a power series on
E
with regular terms.
Then the set of points at which the series
converges regularly is solid and logarithmically convex.

Proof:

Let
z\in D
and suppose
w\in

satisfies
|w|\leq|z|
. It follows
from the fact that
|P_{m}|(|w|)\leq|P_{m}|(|z|)

that
w\in D
. Thus
D
is a solid set.

Now let
z,w\in D
.
Using Proposition
7
and the inequality between the weighted geometric and arithmetic
means, we get

|P_{m}|(|z|^{\theta}|w|^{1-\theta})\leq|P_{m}|(|z|)^{\theta}|P_{m}|(|w|)^{1-\theta}\leq\theta|P_{m}|(|z|)+(1-\theta)|P_{m}|(|w|)

and it follows immediately that
|z|^{\theta}|w|^{1-\theta}\in D
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

4
Regular holomorphic functions

Let
E,F
be complex Banach lattices, with
F
Dedekind complete. Let
U
be an open subset
of
E
. A function
f\colon U\to F
is

regularly holomorphic

if

(a)

f
is holomorphic on
U
.

(b)

For every
z\in U
, the derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)
are regular
m
-homogeneous polynomials.

(c)

For every
z\in U
, the Taylor series of
f
at
z

is regularly convergent in some neighbourhood of
z
.

Suppose a power series
\sum P_{m}

is regularly convergent at some point
z
.
This means that the series
\sum|P_{m}|(|z|)

converges. Then, since
\bigl||P_{m}|(z)\bigr|\leq|P_{m}|(|z|)
, it follows that the
series
\sum|P_{m}|(z)
is absolutely convergent.
So, applying the Cauchy-Hadamard condition
to the power series
\sum|P_{m}|
,
we see that condition (c) above is equivalent
to

\limsup_{m}\,\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty

(4)

for every
z\in U
. We denote the space
of regularly holomorphic functions
by
\mathcal{H}_{r}({U};{F})
,
or by
\mathcal{H}_{r}({U})
when
F=\mathbb{C}
.

Let
U
be an open set and let
z
be a point in
U
.
We shall say that a function
f\colon U\to F
is
regularly holomorphic at
z
if the derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)

of
f

at
z
are regular and (
4
) holds.

Proposition 8

If a holomorphic mapping is regularly holomorphic
at a point in its domain, then it
is regularly holomorphic in some
neighbourhood of that point.

Proof:

Suppose that
f
is holomorphic on
an open set
U
and that
f
is regular
at a point
z_{0}
in
U
.
Let
\displaystyle P_{m}=\widehat{A_{m}}=\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z_{0})
. Then there
exist
C,\rho>0
such that

\|P_{m}\|_{r}\leq C\,\rho^{m}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
\|z-z_{0}\|<\rho
, then the derivatives
of
f
at
z
are given by

\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{f}(z)=\sum_{m\geq k}\binom{m}{k}A_{m}(z-z_{0})^{m-k}

where this series converges in

\mathcal{P}(^{k}{E};{F})
with the supremum
norm.
We will show that this series is
absolutely convergent in
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{k}{E};{F})

with the regular norm.
Taking
\|z-z_{0}\|\leq\sigma<(2e\rho)^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq k}

\displaystyle\Bigl\|{\binom{m}{k}A_{m}(z-z_{0})^{m-k}}\Bigr\|_{r}=\sum_{m\geq k}\Bigl\|{\binom{m}{k}\bigl|A_{m}(z-z_{0})^{m-k}\bigr|}\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq k}\Bigl\|{\binom{m}{k}\bigl|A_{m}\bigr|\,\bigl|z-z_{0}|^{m-k}\bigr|}\Bigr\|\leq\sum_{m\geq k}\binom{m}{k}\bigl\|A_{m}\bigr\|_{r}\bigl\|(z-z_{0})\bigr\|^{m-k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq k}\binom{m}{k}e^{m}\bigl\|P_{m}\bigr\|_{r}\bigl\|(z-z_{0})\bigr\|^{m-k}\leq C\,\sum_{m\geq k}2^{m}e^{m}\rho^{m}\sigma^{m-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{1-2e\rho\sigma}\bigl(2e\rho\bigr)^{k}\,.

Therefore
\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{f}(z)

is a regular
k
-homogeneous polynomial
for every
k
, provided

\|z-z_{0}\|<(2e\rho)^{-1}
.
Furthermore, the above calculation
shows that

\limsup_{k}\,\Bigl\|{\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{f}(z)}\Bigr\|^{\frac{1}{k}}_{r}<\infty\,.

Therefore
f
is regularly holomorphic
in the open ball with centre
z_{0}

and radius
(2e\rho)^{-1}
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

We now look at the special case of Banach lattices
in which the lattice structure is defined by
an unconditional Schauder basis.
Let
E
be complex Banach space with
an unconditional Schauder basis
(e_{n})_{n}
.
We may assume, without loss of generality,
that every point
z
in
E

with coordinate expansion
z=\sum_{j}z_{j}e_{j}\in E
satisfies

\|z\|=\sup\Bigl\{\Bigl\|\sum_{j}w_{j}e_{j}\Bigr\|:|w_{j}|\leq|z_{j}|\quad\text{ for every }j\Bigr\}\,.

(The norm on
E
can always be replaced
by an equivalent norm with this property.)
Then
E
is a complex Banach lattice
with the modulus defined coordinatewise:

|z|=\sum_{j}|z_{j}|e_{j}.

Now let
P_{m}=\widehat{A}_{m}
be a bounded

m
-homogeneous polynomial on
E
.
The value of
P_{m}
at a point
z
may
be expanded, using the multilinearity
of
\widehat{A}_{m}
to give an expression
such as

P_{m}(z)=\sum_{j_{m}}\dots\sum_{j_{1}}A_{m}(e_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{j_{m}})\,z_{j_{1}}\dots z_{j_{m}}\,.

In general, there is no guarantee that this
multiple series will converge absolutely.
Matos and Nachbin
[
32
]
isolated the space
of
m
-homogeneous polynomials for
which this expansion is absolutely
convergent at every point in
E
.
They defined a norm for this space
and proved that it is a Banach space
in this norm. Grecu and Ryan
[
25
]

showed that the Matos-Nachbin
polynomials coincide with the
polynomials that are regular
with respect to the Banach lattice
structure of
E
, and furthermore,
the Matos–Nachbin norm is precisely
the regular norm. In other words,
the space of regular
m
-homogeneous
polynomials on
E
is exactly the
space of
m
-homogeneous polynomials

P_{m}
that have pointwise absolutely
convergent monomial expansion of the
form

P_{m}(z)=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\mathbb{N})}}}{{|\alpha|=m}}}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}

and the regular norm is

\|P_{m}\|_{r}=\sup\Bigl\{\Bigl\|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}||z|^{\alpha}\Bigr\|:\|z\|\leq 1\Bigr\}\,.

The following result, and its proof, is
based on Theorem 3.10 in
[
31
]
.

Theorem 3
(cf.
[
31
]
, Prop. 3.10)

Let
E
be a complex Banach
space with an unconditional Schauder
basis
and let
U
be an open subset of
E
.
A function
f\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
is
regularly holomorphic if and only if,
for every
z\in U
, the monomial
expansion of
f
around
z
is absolutely
convergent to
f
in some neighbourhood
of
z
.

Proof:

First, suppose that
f
is regularly
holomorphic on
U
. Let
z\in U
.
Then

\limsup_{m}\,\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}^{\frac{1}{m}}=\rho<\infty

and so there is a positive constant
C

satisfying

\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}\leq C\,\rho^{m}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now
\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)
,
being regular, has an absolutely
pointwise convergent monomial
expansion of the form
[
31
]

\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)(w)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}w^{\alpha}

and so we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}|\,|w-z|^{\alpha}\leq\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}\|w-z\|^{m}\leq C\,\rho^{m}\,\|w-z\|^{m}\ \,.

It follows that the monomial
expansion

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\mathbb{N})}}c_{\alpha}(w-z)^{\alpha}

is absolutely convergent to
f(w)
for

\|w-z\|<\rho^{-1}
.

Now suppose that
f
is locally representable
by pointwise absolutely convergent
monomial expansions. So, for each
z\in U
,
there exists
\rho>0

and a monomial expansion of the form

f(w)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{(\mathbb{N})}}c_{\alpha}(w-z)^{\alpha}

that converges absolutely at every
point in the ball
\|w-z\|<\rho
.
It follows that
f
is Gateaux-holomorphic
in this ball. By Baire’s theorem,
f
has
at least one point of continuity in this ball
and hence
f
is holomorphic there. The derivatives of
f
at
z
are given by

\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)(w-z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}(w-z)^{\alpha}\,.

Choose
0<\sigma<\rho

so that the holomorphic function

\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|(w-z)^{\alpha}

is bounded by
C>0
in the ball

\|w-x\|\leq\sigma
.
For every non-zero
\xi\in E
,

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}||\xi|^{\alpha}=\Bigl(\frac{\|\xi\|}{\sigma}\Bigr)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}|\bigl(\sigma\,\xi/\|\xi\|\bigr)^{\alpha}\leq C\Bigl(\frac{\|\xi\|}{\sigma}\Bigr)^{m}\,.

Thus
\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)

is a regular

m
-homogeneous polynomial for
every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and the inequality
above shows that

\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}=\sup\bigl\{\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}||\xi|^{\alpha}:\|\xi\|\leq 1\bigr\}\leq C\,\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigr)^{m}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
f
is regularly
holomorphic on
U
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

Let
E,F
be complex Banach lattices, with
F
Dedekind complete. Let
U
be an open subset
of
E
. A function
f\colon U\to F
is

regularly holomorphic of bounded

on

|r|(f,z)=\left(\limsup_{m}\,\Bigl\|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d^{m}}{f}(z)\Bigl\|_{r}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right)^{-1}\geq d(z,\partial U)\,.

(5)

Thus, the Taylor series of
f
at
every point

is required to be
uniformly regularly convergent on
every ball with centre

in

. We denote the space

\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U};{F})
,
or by
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
when

.

Proposition 9

Let

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

We recall the definition of a holomorphy type,
introduced by Nachbin

.

Definition 1

,

P\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(^{m}{E};{F})
, then

\widehat{d}^{k}P\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(^{m-k}{E};{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(^{k}{E};{F})})
and

\Bigl\|\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d}^{k}P(a)\Bigr\|_{\theta}\leq\sigma^{m}\|P\|_{\theta}\|a\|^{m-k}\,.

Proposition 10
(cf.
[
31
]
, Prop. 3.8 )

The sequence
\displaystyle\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E,F}),\|\cdot\|_{r}\bigr)_{m}

is a holomorphy type on the class of pairs
(E,F)
of
complex Banach lattices with
F
Dedekind complete.

Proof:

Properties (a) and (b) are obvious. To show that (c) is satisfied,
let
P=\widehat{A}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E};{F})
. Then, using
Proposition
6
and
the polarization inequality,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{P}(a)\Bigl\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\;\Bigl|\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{P}(a)\Bigr|\;\Bigl\|\leq\Bigl\|\frac{1}{k!}\widehat{d^{k}}{|P|}(a)\Bigl\|

\displaystyle=\binom{m}{k}\Bigl\|\;|A|a^{m-k}\Bigr|\;\Bigr\|\leq\binom{m}{k}\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\|\;|P|\;\|\|a\|^{m-k}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{m}\,\|P_{m}\|_{r}\,\|a\|^{m-k}

where

.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

In some circumstances, it is possible to define a complex
lattice structure on the space of regularly holomorphic
functions. We shall use the axiomatization of complex
vector lattices given by Mittelmeyer–Wolff
[
34
]
.
They define an
Archimedean modulus
on a complex
vector space
G
to be a
function
m\colon G\to G
satisfying

(0)

m(m(x))=m(x)
for every
x\in G
.

(i)

m(\alpha x)=|\alpha|m(x)

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
x\in G
.

(ii)

m\bigl(m(m(x)+m(y))-m(x+y)\bigr)=m(x)+m(y)-m(x+y)
.

(iii)

G
is the linear span of
m(G)
.

(iv)

m(m(y)-km(x))=m(y)-km(x)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
implies
x=0
.

A
complex vector lattice
is a complex vector space
G

equipped with a modulus.
It is shown in
[
34
]
that the real linear
span of the subset
m(G)
of
G
is a real vector lattice
with
m(G)
as the positive cone.

Proposition 11

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice
and let
U
be an open ball in
E

with centre
z_{0}
. Then the space
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
of
regularly holomorphic functions of
bounded type on
E
is a complex Archimedean
Riesz space with the modulus
given by

m(f)=|f|:=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d}^{m}f(z_{0})\Bigr|\,.

Proof:

The proof consists of straightforward calculations. For example, to show
that property (ii) in the definition is satisfied,
let
f=\sum_{m}P_{m}
and
g=\sum_{m}Q_{m}
be
the Taylor expansions at
z_{0}
. Then,
since
m
coincides with the modulus
on
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E})
,

m\bigl(m(m(f)+m(g))-m(f+g)\bigr)\\
=\biggl|\Bigl|\sum_{m}|P_{m}|+|Q_{m}|\Bigr|-\sum_{m}|P_{m}+Q_{m}|\biggr|=\biggl|\sum_{m}|P_{m}|+|Q_{m}|-|P_{m}+Q_{m}|\biggr|\\
=\sum_{m}|P_{m}|+|Q_{m}|-|P_{m}+Q_{m}|=m(f)+m(g)-m(f+g)\,.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

Is it possible to define a complex lattice
structure on
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
for domains
other than open balls?
It would be reasonable to expect
that, if
B(a,r)
is an open
ball contained in
U
, then the restriction
mapping from
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
to
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({B(a,r)})

would be a lattice homomorphism. If

\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
carried a complex
lattice structure satisfying this condition,
then it would follow that

|f|=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Bigl|\frac{1}{m!}\widehat{d}^{m}f(a)\Bigr|

in the ball
B(a,r)
.

However, this attempt to define
|f|
locally
fails, due to a lack of coherence.

To see this, take

E=\mathbb{C}
,
and consider a holomorphic function
f
on
B(a,r)
. Then for each point
z_{0}
in
B(a,r)
we can expand
f
as a
Taylor series

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(z-z_{0})^{m}

for
|z|<r-|z_{0}|
. Note that the coefficients
(a_{m})_{m}
depend on the point

z_{0}
and are given by
\displaystyle a_{m}=\frac{f^{(m)}(z_{0})}{m!}
.

For each point
z_{0}
we define the holomorphic function
|f|_{z_{0}}
by

|f|_{z_{0}}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|(z-z_{0})^{n}.

Note that this series converges on
B(z_{0},r-|z_{0}|)
.

However,
these expansions lack
coherence. To see this take the
function
f\colon B(0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

f(z)=\frac{1}{1-z}.

We first expand
f
about the origin to get the Taylor series expansion

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}.

The Taylor series expansion of
f
about
\frac{i}{2}
is

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\frac{i}{2})^{m+1}}\right)\left(z-\frac{i}{2}\right)^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{5}(2+i)\right)^{m+1}\left(z-\frac{i}{2}\right)^{m}.

Then

|f|_{0}(z)=f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}

and

|f|_{\frac{i}{2}}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^{m+1}\left(z-\frac{i}{2}\right)^{m}.

We now observe that
|f|_{0}(\frac{i}{2})=\frac{2}{5}(2+i)
while
|f|_{\frac{i}{2}}(\frac{i}{2})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}
.

So it appears that, in general, it is not
possible to define a complex lattice
structure on
\mathcal{H}_{rb}({U})
.

5
The Bohr radius on Banach lattices

In this section we show how the Bohr radius
for complex sequence spaces can be generalized
to the wider setting of complex Banach lattices. In Theorem
4
we prove
that the growth of the Bohr radii controls
the radius of regular convergence and we show
that on finite dimensional sequence spaces, the radii of convergence and
regular convergence are equal.

In 1914 H. Bohr
[
9
]
showed that if
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{k}
is a
power series on the unit disc centred at
0
,
D(0,1)
, with
\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{k}\right|\leq 1
for all
z
with
|z|<1
then
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|c_{k}z^{k}|\leq 1
for all
z
with
|z|\leq\frac{1}{3}
. Moreover,
\frac{1}{3}

is the optimal radius for which this inequality holds.

More generally, given a Reinhardt domain
R
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the Bohr radius
of
R
,
K(R)
, is defined as the supremum over all
r\geq 0
such that if

\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
is a power series on
R
with
\left|\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right|\leq 1
for all
z
in
R
then

\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}|\leq 1
for all
z
in
rR
. It follows from
results of Aizenberg
[
1
]
, Boas
[
6
]
, Boas and
Kavinson
[
7
]
and Dineen and Timoney
[
23
]
, that there is a constant

c
, independent of
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, such that

\frac{1}{c}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min(p,2)}}\leq K(B_{\ell_{p}^{n}})\leq c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min(p,2)}}.

In 2003, Defant, García and Maestre
[
20
]
refined the concept of
Bohr radius and introduced the concept of homogeneous Bohr radius. Given a
finite dimensional Banach space
X=(\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
with canonical
basis
(e_{k})_{k=1}^{n}
they define
K_{m}(B_{X})
as the supremum over all
r
in

[0,1]
such that if
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
is an
m
-homogeneous
polynomial on
X
with
\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right|\leq 1

for all
z
in
B_{X}
then
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}|\leq 1
for all

z
in
rB_{X}
.

Let
E
be a Banach space with an unconditional basis,
(x_{n})_{n}
. Then the unconditional basis constant of
(x_{n})_{n}
,
\chi((x_{n})_{n})
,
is defined by

\chi((x_{n})_{n})=\inf\left\{C:\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\mu_{k}x_{k}\right\|\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}x_{k}\right\|:\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{C},|\epsilon_{j}|=1,j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

In
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
it is shown that if
X=(\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
then

K_{m}(B_{X})=\frac{1}{\root m \of{\chi_{mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X}))}}

where
\chi_{mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X}))
denotes the unconditional basis constant
of the monomials in
\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X})
.

Given any
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
,
when we regard
\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X})
as a Banach lattice, the absolute value of

P
is given by
|P|=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_{\alpha}|z^{\alpha}
. Motivated by this
observation we now introduce the
m
-th Bohr radius of a general Banach
lattice.

Definition 2

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice and
m
be a positive integer. We define the

m
-th Bohr radius of
E
,
K_{m}(B_{E})
by

\displaystyle K_{m}(B_{E})

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\rho:\sup_{z\in\rho B_{E}}||P|(z)|\leq\|P\|\hbox{ for all }P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E})\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\rho:\rho^{m}\|P\|_{r}\leq\|P\|\text{ for all $P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E})$}\}.

Note that
0\leq K_{m}(B_{E})\leq 1
and that
K_{m}(B_{E})
may be
0
.

Indeed, we observe that
K_{m}(B_{E})>0
if and only if the regular and supremum norms are
equivalent when restricted to
\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E})
. In this case,

{K_{m}(B_{E})}^{-m}

is the norm of the identity mapping from
(\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E}),\|\cdot\|)
onto
(\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E}),\|\cdot\|_{r})
.

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice,
U
be an open subset of
E
and
f\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
be a homomorphic function such that
f(a+z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)
is the Taylor series of
f
about
a
with each
P_{m}
a regular

m
-homogeneous polynomial. Our next theorem provides a lower
bound for
the radius of regular convergence,

|r|(f,a)
, in terms of the
homogeneous Bohr radii.

Theorem 4

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice
and
U
an open subset of
E
. Let
f\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
be a regular
holomorphic function and
a\in U
. Then,

\liminf_{m\to\infty}(K_{m}(B_{E}))r(f,a)\leq|r|(f,a)\leq r(f,a).

Moreover, for each
a\in U
, both the upper and lower bounds are sharp.

Proof:
If
\liminf_{m\to\infty}K_{m}(B_{E})=0
then there is nothing to
prove. Let us now suppose that there is
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so
that
K_{m}(B_{E})>0
for each
m\geq m_{0}
. Write the Taylor series of
f

about
a
as

f(a+z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z).

Then, by the definition of the Bohr radius, we have that

(K_{m}(B_{E}))^{m}\|P_{m}\|_{r}\leq\|P_{m}\|

for each
m
which we will rewrite as

\|P_{m}\|_{r}\leq\frac{1}{(K_{m}(B_{E}))^{m}}\|P_{m}\|

for all
m\geq m_{0}
. Taking
m
-th roots we get that

\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m}\leq\frac{1}{(K_{m}(B_{E}))}\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}.

We now let
m
tend to infinity to get

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m}\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{(K_{m}(B_{E}))}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\limsup_{m\to\infty}a_{m}}=\liminf_{m\to\infty}a_{m}

we get that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m}\leq\frac{1}{\liminf_{m\to\infty}(K_{m}(B_{E}))}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}

and inverting we obtain

\liminf_{m\to\infty}(K_{m}(B_{E}))r(f,a)\leq|r|(f,a).

Therefore, we have

\liminf_{m\to\infty}(K_{m}(B_{E}))r(f,a)\leq|r|(f,a)\leq r(f,a).

Let us now see that both sides of this inequality are sharp. If we
consider a
holomorphic function
f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}
with
P_{m}\geq 0
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
then we have that
|r|(f,0)=r(f,0)
.

To show that the lower bound is optimal we consider two cases.
We first assume that
K_{m}(B_{E})>0
all
but finitely many
m
. Then, discarding finitely many terms, we can assume
that

K_{m}(B_{E})>0
for all
m
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
we choose
P_{m}\not=0

so that

\frac{1}{2}\|P_{m}\|\leq K_{m}(B_{E})^{m}\|P_{m}\|_{r}\leq\|P_{m}\|.

(6)

Dividing
P_{m}
by
\|P_{m}\|
we may assume that
\|P_{m}\|=1
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If we let
f=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}P_{m}
then
r(f,0)=1
.

From (
6
) we obtain

\left(\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}\right)^{-1}\leq K_{m}(B_{E})^{-1}(\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m})^{-1}\leq 2^{1/m}(\|P_{m}\|^{1/m})^{-1}\,,

or

K_{m}(B_{E})\left(\|P_{m}\|^{1/m}\right)^{-1}\leq(\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m})^{-1}\leq 2^{1/m}K_{m}(B_{E})(\|P_{m}\|^{1/m})^{-1}.

As
\|P_{m}\|=1
for all
m
we get

K_{m}(B_{E})\leq(\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m})^{-1}\leq 2^{1/m}K_{m}(B_{E}).

Letting
m
tend to infinity we get that

\left(\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|P_{m}\|_{r}^{1/m}\right)^{-1}=\liminf_{m\to\infty}K_{m}(B_{E})

and therefore we see that the lower bound is attained.

Let us now suppose that we have infinitely many
m
with
K_{m}(B_{E})=0
.
Then we can choose a
subsequence
(m_{k})_{k}
so that
K_{m_{k}}(B_{E})=0
for all
k
. Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for each
k
in
\mathbb{N}
choose
P_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m_{k}}{E})
so that

\sup_{z\in\frac{1}{j}B_{E}}||P_{k}|(z)|>\|P_{k}\|

or that

\|P_{k}\|_{r}>j^{m_{k}}\|P_{k}\|.

Then repeating the above argument with
K_{m}(B_{E})
replaced with
\frac{1}{j}

we get

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|P_{k}\|_{r}^{1/m_{k}}\geq\liminf_{m_{k}\to\infty}{j}

and therefore

|r|(f,0)\leq\frac{1}{j}.

As this holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we see that we can find regular holomorphic
functions with radius of convergence
1
but radius of absolute convergence
arbitrarily small.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

As we have mentioned above, it follows from
[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
that if

X
is
(\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
then

K_{m}(B_{X})=\frac{1}{\root m \of{\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X}))}}
.
In this case Theorem
4
gives us that

\frac{1}{\limsup_{m\to\infty}\root m \of{\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X}))}}r(f,a)\leq|r|(f,a)\leq r(f,a).

If
X
is the finite dimensional Banach lattice
(\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
,
with any norm for which
the unit vector basis
is a
1
-unconditional
Schauder basis, then it
follows from
[
19
, Lemma 11..2.2]
that
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sqrt[m]{\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{X}))}=1
. Hence, we have the following result.

Theorem 5

Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
,
with any norm for which
the unit vector basis
is a
1
-unconditional
Schauder basis. Then
r(f,a)=|r|(f,a)
for every

.

Theorem

is not true for real analytic functions on subsets
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, Hayman,
[
26
]
, shows that if

-homogeneous polynomial, converges on the

then

.
Moreover, an
example is provided in
[

]
to show that the factor of

is

Regular Holomorphic Functions on
\ell_{p}

In this section we will look at regular holomorphic functions on

In our constructions we need to consider
the Bohr radius,

\mathcal{P}(^{m}{\ell_{p}})

We begin with the observation that for any positive integer

[

and

Let
\pi_{k}
denote the canonical

\ell_{p}
onto

\ell_{p}^{k}
.
Our plan is to define a holomorphic

\ell_{p}
of the form

where for each
m
,
P_{m}
is an
m
-homogeneous polynomial on

\ell_{p}^{n_{m}}
with

as

.

We need some notation. Let
(A_{mn})_{m,n}

(B_{mn})_{m,n}

It follows from

\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{\ell_{p}^{n}}))\sim\left(1+\frac{n}{m}\right)^{(m-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{\min(p,2)}\right)}.

\alpha_{m}>0
.

\displaystyle\root m \of{\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}{\ell_{p}^{n_{m}}}))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\left(\left(1+\frac{n_{m}}{m}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min(p,2)}}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}

\displaystyle>1+\alpha_{m}.

.

for each

|r|(f,0)\leq\left(\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{m}}\right)^{-1}
.

Let us show that if
p>1
then for each
m
in
\mathbb{N}
and each

n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
and an

\|P_{\eta}\|=1

.

\alpha_{m}

.
From the above construction we know that we can find
n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
and

. We now choose a

.

Let us now consider the function

then we have an

-homogeneous polynomial

on

with

and

. We now define

f
on
B_{\ell_{p}}
by

to obtain
the following result.

Proposition 12

Let
p>1
. Then for each

there is a holomorphic function

on

with

and

|r|(f,0)=\tau
.

For
\ell_{1}
, Matos
[
31
, Prop. 3.7]

showed that every bounded
m
-homogeneous
polynomial is regular and that
the regular and supremum norms
on
\mathcal{P}(^{m}{\ell_{1}})
are equivalent.
More precisely,

\|P\|\leq\|P\|_{r}\leq e^{m}\|P\|

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}{\ell_{1}})
.
Thus, on
\ell_{1}
, we have

e^{-1}r(f,0)\leq|r|(f,0)\leq r(f,0)
for every
holomorphic function
f
on
\ell_{1}
or its unit ball.
We do not know if this inequality is
sharp.

7
Orthogonally Additive Holomorphic Functions

In this
section
we
study
orthogonally additive holomorphic functions on
Banach lattices and show that for this class of functions the radii of
convergence and regular convergence coincide.

We begin by looking at what is known for finite dimensional Banach lattices.
Let us suppose that
X
is

\mathbb{C}^{n}
endowed with a norm under
which
(e_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a Schauder basis with unconditional basis constant
1
.
Given
a subset
J

of
\{1,\ldots,n\}^{m}
for some natural number
m
, Bayart,
Defant and Schlüters,
[
5
]
, use
\mathcal{P}(^{J}{X})
to denote the closed
subspace of all holomorphic function
f
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(B_{X})
for which

c_{\alpha}(f)=0
if
\alpha\not\in J
.
In other words,
{\cal P}(^{J}X)

is the span of
\{z^{j};j\in J\}
.

In the case where we take

J=\{(k,\ldots,k):k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\}

the space

\mathcal{P}(^{J}{X})
is the space of
m
-homogeneous orthogonally additive
polynomials on
X
. Using
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
Bayart, Defant and Schlüters,
deduce on
[
5
, Page 113]
that
\chi_{\rm mon}(\mathcal{P}(^{J}{X}))=1
. In
particular, this means that
\|P\|=\|P\|_{r}
for every
m
-homogeneous
orthogonally
additive polynomial on
X
. We now extend this result to orthogonally additive
polynomials on any complex Banach lattice.

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice. Then
z,w\in E
are said to be
disjoint
, denoted by
z\perp x
, if
|z|\wedge|w|=0
in
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2

Let
E
be a complex Banach
lattice and let
z=x+iy,w=u+iv

be elements of
E
. Then
z\perp w
if and only if
x,y\perp u,v
.

Proof:

We have

|z|\wedge|w|=\\
\sup\{|x|\cos\theta+|y|\sin\theta:\theta\in[0,2\pi]\}\wedge\sup\{|u|\cos\phi+|v|\sin\phi:\phi\in[0,2\pi]\}\\
=\sup_{\theta}\sup_{\phi}\;\bigl(|x|\cos\theta+|y|\sin\theta\bigr)\wedge\bigl(|u|\cos\phi+|v|\sin\phi\bigr)\,,

(7)

using the infinite distributive property
of the lattice operations

[
2
, Theorem 1.8]
.

Suppose that
z\perp w
. Then, by (
7
),

\bigl(|x|\cos\theta+|y|\sin\theta\bigr)\wedge\bigl(|u|\cos\phi+|v|\sin\phi\bigr)\leq 0\,

for all
\theta,\phi
.
Taking
\theta,\phi=0,\pi/2
, we get

|x|\wedge|u|=|x|\wedge|v|=|y|\wedge|u|=|y|\wedge|v|=0\,.

The converse follows easily from (
7
).

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

We will now make use of the following property of
real orthogonally additive polynomials. Let
Q

be an
m
-homogeneous polynomial on
E
, generated by the symmetric

m
-linear form
B
. Then
B
is
said to be
orthosymmetric
if

B(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=0
whenever any two of
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}

are disjoint.
This property of
B
is equivalent
to
Q
being orthogonally additive

[
12
]
.

Proposition 13

Let
E
be a complex Banach lattice and
P
be a regular
m
-homogeneous polynomial on
E
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

P
is orthogonally additive on
E
.

(b)

P
is orthogonally additive on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(c)

The real and imaginary parts of
P
are orthogonally additive on
E
.

Proof:

The implications (a) implies (b) and (c) implies (a) are trivial.

To show that (b) implies (c), suppose that
P=P_{0}+iP_{1}
is orthogonally additive on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then for
x,y\in E_{\mathbb{R}}
with
x\perp y
, we have
P(x+y)=P(x)+P(y)
. Taking real and imaginary parts of
P
and using (
2
), we get

P_{0}(x+y)=P_{0}(x)+P_{0}(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{1}(x+y)=P_{1}(x)+P_{1}(y).

That is,
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
are orthogonally additive on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.

To complete the proof, we must show that complexifications of
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
are orthogonally additive
on
E
.
Let
z=x+iy
,
w=u+iv
be disjoint
elements of
E
. We have

P_{0}(z+w)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}i^{k}\binom{m}{k}A_{0}(x+u)^{m-k}(y+v)^{k}\,,

where
A_{0}
is the symmetric
m
-linear form
that generates
P_{0}
. Expanding, we get

P_{0}(z+w)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}i^{k}\binom{m}{k}\sum_{r=0}^{m-k}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\binom{m-k}{r}\binom{k}{s}A_{0}(x^{m-k-r}u^{r}y^{k-s}v^{s})\,.

Since
P_{0}
is orthogonally additive on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
, the
m
-linear
form
A_{0}
is orthosymmetric on
E_{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Thus,
we have that

A_{0}(x^{m-k-r}u^{r}y^{k-s}v^{s})
vanishes if either
of
x,y
appears along with either of
u,v
in the
argument. Therefore, for each
k
, there are only
two non-zero terms,
A_{0}(x^{m-k}y^{k})

and
A_{0}(u^{m-k}v^{k})
and hence

P_{0}(z+w)=P_{0}(z)+P_{0}(w)\,.

A similar argument shows that

P_{1}
is orthogonally additive on
E
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

It follows from the above proposition
that the Banach lattice of orthogonally additive
m
-homogeneous polynomials on a complex Banach lattice
E
is the complexification of the lattice of orthogonally additive
m
-homogeneous polynomials on
E_{\mathbb{R}}
.

A regular holomorphic function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
orthogonally additive if
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)
whenever
x
and
y
are disjoint.
Orthogonally additive holomorphic functions have previously been defined on

C(K)
spaces by Carando, Lassalle and Zalduendo,
[
15
]
and on

C^{*}
-algebras by Jaramillo, Prieto and Zalduendo,
[
28
]
and Peralta and
Puglisi,
[
37
]
. The proof of
[
15
, Lemma 1.1]
for
C(K)
spaces
trivially extends to arbitrary complex Banach lattices to show that if

f\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
is a regular holomorphic function which has a Taylor
series expansion

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}P_{k}(z)
about
0
then
f
is orthogonally additive if and
only if
P_{k}
orthogonally additive for each
k
in
\mathbb{N}
.

In
[
11
]
the authors proved that if
P
is an
m
-homogeneous
orthogonally additive polynomial on a real Banach lattice then
\|P\|=\|P\|_{r}

when
m
is odd and
\|P\|\leq\|P\|_{r}\leq 2\|P\|
when
m
is even. Moreover,
the polynomial
P(x)=x_{1}^{m}-x_{2}^{m}
, with
m
even, on
\ell_{\infty}^{2}
shows that this bound is
sharp.

Let us now consider what happens for orthogonally additive polynomials on
complex Banach lattices.

Proposition 14

Let
P
be an orthogonally additive

m
-homogeneous polynomial
on the complex Banach lattice
C(K)
.
Then
\|P\|_{r}=\|P\|
.

Proof:

Using
[
3
]
we know that every orthogonally
additive
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P
on
C(K)
has an extension,

\tilde{P}
, to
C(K)^{\prime\prime}
, the bidual of
C(K)
. Moreover, it follows from

[
17
]
that
\|\tilde{P}\|=\|P\|
and from
[
14
, Corollary 2.1]
that

\tilde{P}
is orthogonally additive. We observe that
\mathcal{B}(K)
, the space
of all bounded Borel measurable functions on
K
is a closed subspace of

C(K)^{\prime\prime}
.
This means that each
m
-homogeneous orthogonally additive polynomial
P
on

C(K)
has an extension
P_{B}
to
\mathcal{B}(K)
as an orthogonally additive
polynomial with
\|P_{B}\|=\|P\|
.

Since
P
is orthogonally additive on
C(K)
, there is a complex measure

\mu
on
K
such that

P(x)=\int_{K}x^{m}(t)\,d\mu(t)

for all
x
in
C(K)
. This implies that
|P|
is represented
by the measure
|\mu|
. It follows from

[
14
, Corollary 2.1]

that

P_{B}(y)={\tilde{P}}(y)=\int_{K}y^{m}(t)\,d\mu(t)

for all bounded Borel measurable functions
y
on
K
.

By
[
38
, Theorem 6.12]
, there exists a Borel measurable function

\rho\colon K\to\mathbb{C}
with
|\rho(t)|=1
for all
t
in
K
such that

d|\mu|=\rho d\mu
.

Let us choose a branch
\rho^{1/m}
of the
m
-th root of
\rho
. For each
x

in
C(K)
, we note that
x\rho^{1/m}
is a bounded Borel function on
K
.
Therefore, given an orthogonally additive
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P
on

C(K)
we have

\displaystyle|P|(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}x(t)^{m}\,d|\mu|(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}x(t)^{m}\rho(t)\,d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}(x\rho^{1/m}(t))^{m}\,d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=P_{B}(x\rho^{1/m}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|P\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1,x\in C(K)}||P|(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|x\|\leq 1,x\in C(K)}|{P_{B}}(x\rho^{1/m})|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|y\|\leq 1,y\in B(K)}|{P_{B}}(y)|

\displaystyle=\|P\|.

As
\|P\|\leq\|P\|_{r}

in general, we have
\|P\|=\|P\|_{r}
.

\blacksquare\ \ \ \

We can extend this result to all Banach lattices by
localising to principal ideals.
If
E
is a real or complex Banach lattice, then for
a\in E_{+}
,
the principal ideal
E_{a}
is the ideal in
E
consisting of all
x

satisfying
|x|\leq c\,a
for some
c>0
. With the
norm

\|x\|_{a}=\inf\{c:|x|\leq c\,a\}\,,

it becomes a Banach lattice.
As it
is an AM-space with unit, the Kakutani Representation
Theorem says that
E_{a}
is Banach lattice
isometrically isomorphic to
C(K_{a})
for some
compact Hausdorff space
K_{a}
. It is easy to see
that, in the complex case,
E_{a}
is the Banach
lattice complexification of the real
Banach lattice
(E_{\mathbb{R}})_{a}
.

The norm of a real or complex Banach lattice
E
is determined by the norms
of its principal ideals. For every
z\in E
, we have

\|z\|=\inf\{\|a\|\,\|z\|_{a}:a\in E,a>0\}

and this infimum is attained when

a=|z|
.
It follows that the closed
unit ball of
E
is

B_{E}=\bigcup_{a>0}\|a\|^{-1}\,B_{E_{a}}

(8)

and so the
norm of a regular
m
-homogeneous polynomial

P
is given by

\|P\|=\sup\{\|a\|^{-m}\|P_{a}\|:a\in E,a>0\}\,,

where
P_{a}
denotes the restriction of
P

to the Banach lattice
E_{a}
, equipped with the principal ideal norm.
Applying this to the regular norm, we have

\|P\|_{r}=\sup\{\|a\|^{-m}\||P|_{a}\|:a\in E,a>0\}\,.

In the real case, the restriction mapping

P\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E})\mapsto P_{a}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(^{m}{E_{a}})

is a lattice homomorphism
[
10
]
.
It is easy to see that, in the complex case,
the restriction mapping is the complexification
of the real restriction mapping. Therefore
the restriction mapping is a complex
lattice homomorphism
[
39
, p. 136]
.
So we have

|P|_{a}=|P_{a}|
and it follows that

\displaystyle\|P\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|a\|^{-m}\||P|_{a}\|:a\in E,a>0\}=\sup\{\|a\|^{-m}\||P_{a}|\|:a\in E,a>0\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|a\|^{-m}\|P_{a}\|_{r}:a\in E,a>0\}\,.

Thus, from Proposition
14
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6

Let
P
be an orthogonally additive
m
-homogeneous polynomial on a complex
Banach lattice
E
. Then
\|P\|=\|P\|_{r}
.

In a similar way we can show that if
m
is an odd integer and
P
is an

m
-homogeneous polynomial on a (real) Banach lattice
E
then
\|P\|=\|P\|_{r}
,
recovering the first part of
[
11
, Corollary 1]
.

From Theorem
6

we immediately obtain the
following result.

Theorem 7

Let
f
be an orthogonally additive holomorphic function on a complex Banach
lattice
E
. Then for each
a\in E
we have

|r|(f,a)=r(f,a).

This contrasts with the results of Section 6,
where we have seen that the radii of convergence can be different in general.

Abstract
. Well-known work of Renault shows that if ℰ \mathcal{E} is a twist over a second countable, effective, étale groupoid G G , then there is a naturally associated Cartan subalgebra of the reduced twisted groupoid C ∗ C^{*} -algebra C r ∗ ​ ( G , ℰ ) C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{E}) , and that every Cartan subalgebra of a separable C ∗ C^{*} -algebra arises in this way. However twisted C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of non-effective groupoids G G can also possess Cartan subalgebras: In [ 12 ] , sufficient conditions on a subgroupoid S S of G G were found that ensure that S S gives rise to a Cartan subalgebra in the cocycle-twisted C ∗ C^{*} -algebra of G G . In this paper, we extend these results to general twists ℰ \mathcal{E} , and we refine the conditions on the subgroupoid for C r ∗ ​ ( S , ℰ S ) C^{*}_{r}(S;\mathcal{E}_{S}) to be a Cartan subalgebra of C r ∗ ​ ( G , ℰ ) C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{E}) .



1.
Introduction

Cartan subalgebras have been an active area of research in operator
algebras since the work of Feldman and Moore
[
16
]
who
realized von Neumann algebras with Cartan subalgebras as the von
Neumann algebras of Borel equivalence relations twisted by Borel

2
-cocycles. Generalizing the Feldman–Moore Theorem to the setting
of
C^{*}
-algebras was one of the impetuses for studying the
C^{*}
-algebras
of locally compact groupoids. Unfortunately, the basic theory only
accommodates continuous
2
-cocycles since the groupoid extension
determined by a Borel
2
-cocycle need not have a compatible locally
compact topology as is the case for locally compact groups. A very
useful solution to this dilemma was provided by Kumjian in

[
20
]
where he suggested dispensing with explicit mention
of the
2
-cocycle on a groupoid
G
and to instead work with a
groupoid extension of
G
which is a natural groupoid analogue of
central group extensions of the circle
\mathbf{T}
. He called these
extensions

over
G
. The term
\mathbf{T}
-groupoid is also
used. Then given a twist
\mathcal{E}
over
G
, Kumjian showed how to
construct a
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G;\mathcal{E})
, and its reduced counterpart

, which are analogues of the classical group

C^{*}
-algebras twisted by a

2
-cocycle.

There has been a great deal of work invested in the study of these
twisted groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras. In particular, in

[
29
]
Renault established a correspondence between
separable
C^{*}
-algebras with Cartan subalgebras and twists over
locally compact, Hausdorff,
second countable, effective, étale groupoids.

Notation and Conventions

All our groupoids are assumed to be locally compact and Hausdorff.
We say that
a groupoid

G
is
abelian
if it is a bundle of abelian
groups. For a unit
u\in G^{(0)}
, we write

G^{u}\coloneq r^{-1}(\{u\})
and

G_{u}\coloneq s^{-1}(\{u\})
. The
isotropy group
at
u
is
denoted
G(u)\coloneq G^{u}\cap G_{u}
, and the
isotropy
bundle
by
{G^{\prime}}=\bigcup_{u\in G^{(0)}}G(u)
. A groupoid is

étale
if the range and source maps are local homeomorphisms.
This implies
G^{(0)}
is open in
G
. If in addition, the interior of

{G^{\prime}}
is reduced to
G^{(0)}
, then
G
is called
effective
.
A
subgroupoid
S
in
{G^{\prime}}
is called
normal
if
Sg=gS

for all
g\in G
so that
G
acts on
S
by conjugation.

Given a topological space
X
, we will denote the interior of a
subspace
Y
by
\operatorname{int}_{X}\left(Y\right)
; if (or as soon as) the ambient space is
understood, we will instead write
Y^{\circ}
. We will denote the

open support
of a function

by
\supp^{\circ}(f)
, while
\supp(f)

will as usual denote its closure.

Example 2.1
.

Throughout

Remark 2.2

(Pedantry)
.

The notation in (

(2.5)

and to observe that the left-hand side of (

chosen to write (

formulas less daunting. It also helps us connect to more general

[

simply the integral of the function

with respect to counting measure on

Example 2.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is a twist over an étale groupoid
G
and that
\mathcal{E}
is a bundle of abelian groups. That is,
we assume that
\mathcal{E}
is an abelian twist. Of course, this implies
that
G
is abelian as well. Then

is commutative.
Hence

is commutative.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{E}
is not a bundle of abelian groups, then
C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{E})
is non-commutative. To see this, assume first that there exists
e\in\mathcal{E}
with
r(e)\neq s(e)
. Let
h\in C_{0}(G^{(0)})
with
h(r(e))=1
and
h(s(e))=0
and
f\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{E})
with
f(e)\neq 0
and
\pi(\supp^{\circ}(f))
a bisection (Lemma
2.3
). Then

(h\ast f)(e)=h(r(e))f(e)=f(e)\neq 0=f(e)h(s(e))=(f\ast h)(e),

so
C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{E})
is not commutative.

Next, assume
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{E}(u)
for some
u\in G^{(0)}
are such that
e_{1}e_{2}\neq e_{2}e_{1}
. Take elements
f_{i}\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{E})
with
\pi(\supp^{\circ}(f_{i}))
a bisection and
f_{i}(e_{i})\neq 0
, so that

(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(e_{1}e_{2})=f_{1}(e_{1})f_{2}(e_{2})\neq 0.

On the other hand,

(f_{2}\ast f_{1})(e_{1}e_{2})=\sum_{\{\,\pi(e)\in G:r(e)=u\,\}}f_{2}(e)f_{1}(e^{-1}e_{1}e_{2}).

By assumption,

is the unique element of

, so that

(f_{2}\ast f_{1})(e_{1}e_{2})=f_{2}(e_{2})f_{1}(e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}e_{2}).

If

, then

and thus

(f_{2}\ast f_{1})(e_{1}e_{2})=0\neq(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(e_{1}e_{2}).

If
\pi(e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}e_{2})=\pi(e_{1})
, then by the assumption that

, there exists

In both cases, we conclude

Remark 3.6

, and give rise to a regular abelian
subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(G,\mathbf{c}_{\theta})
. However, only

\mathbf{Z}\cdot(1,0)
,
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(0,1)
, and
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
with

\gcd(m,n)=1
are
maximal
with symmetric
2
-cocycle.
(The
latter subgroups were forgotten in
[
14
]
*p. 2099, Example 1.)

This leaves
the
case that

\theta=p/q\in\mathbf{Q}
. If
p=0
,
then
G
itself is
maximal. If
p\neq 0
, we may assume
\gcd(p,q)=1
.

Claim
.

\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
is maximal among
subgroups on which the
2
-cocycle

c_{p/q}

is symmetric if and only if

nk=q
.

Proof of Claim.

It is easy to check that the
2
-cocycle restricted to

\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
is symmetric if and only if
either
k=0
or
q\mid nk
. In particular, no subgroup of the
form
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)

with
n>0

can give rise to a Cartan
subalgebra, as it as properly contained in, for example,

\mathbf{Z}\cdot(q,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
and hence not
maximal.
Similarly, if
k>0
, then
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\subset\mathbf{Z}\cdot(1,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(0,q)
.
So let
us focus on the subgroups
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
with

kn>0
and
q\mid nk
.

Note first that
H=\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
is a subgroup of

K=\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k^{\prime},0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
if and only if

\displaystyle k^{\prime}\mid k,\quad n^{\prime}\mid n,\quad\text{and}\quad k^{\prime}n^{\prime}\mid(mn^{\prime}-nm^{\prime}).

(4.5)

In particular, if
nk=q
and
m
is arbitrary, then
H
is
maximal: if
H\leq K

and

\mathbf{c}_{p/q}
is symmetric
on
K
, then the first two conditions of (
4.5
)
imply that
n^{\prime}=n
and
k^{\prime}=k
, and the last condition boils down to

k\mid(m-m^{\prime})
, which is symmetric in
m
and
m^{\prime}
, forcing

H=K
.

So assume now that
q\mid nk
but
q<nk
; we must show that
H
is not
maximal.

If
k\mathbin{\nmid}q
, then there exists
k^{\prime}\mid k
,
k^{\prime}\neq k
,
such that
q\mid nk^{\prime}
, so that
\mathbf{c}_{p/q}
is symmetric on

\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k^{\prime},0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
which, by (
4.5
) and
since
k\neq k^{\prime}
, properly contains
H
. We can thus assume that

q=kn^{\prime}
for some
n^{\prime}\in\mathbf{N}
; note that
q\mid nk
then means
n^{\prime}\mid n
,
and
q<nk
means
n^{\prime}<n
.

If
\gcd(n/n^{\prime},k)=l>1
, then
\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k/l,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
properly
contains
H
. Since
n^{\prime}l
divides
n
by choice of
l
, we see
that
q=n^{\prime}k=(n^{\prime}l)(k/l)
divides
n(k/l)
, so that the
2
-cocycle
is symmetric on this supergroup of
H
, proving that
H
is not
maximal. We can thus assume that
\gcd(n/n^{\prime},k)=1
.

By Bezout’s identity, there exist
b,c\in\mathbf{Z}
with
(n/n^{\prime})b+kc=1
. Multiplying both sides by
n^{\prime}m
, we conclude

n^{\prime}k\mid(mn^{\prime}-n(bm)).

Together with
n^{\prime}\mid n
, this implies by (
4.5
) that

H\leq\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(bm,n^{\prime})
and that,
as
n^{\prime}\neq n
, this
containment is proper. As
kn^{\prime}=q
, the
2
-cocycle is symmetric on
this supergroup, proving that
H
is not maximal.
∎

To sum up:
if
\gcd(p,q)=1
, a subgroup
H=\mathbf{Z}\cdot(k,0)\oplus\mathbf{Z}\cdot(m,n)
of
\mathbf{Z}^{2}

gives rise to a Cartan subalgebra of
C^{*}_{r}(G,\mathbf{c}_{p/q})
if and only if
q=nk
.

Example 4.9
.

The example here is a slight simplification of the one given in

[
12
]
*Section 7.
Consider the set

G=\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}
with the discrete topology. We make
it a non-abelian group with
2
-cocycle
{c}
by defining

(x,y,z)\cdot(m,n,k)=(x+m+2zn,y+n,z+k)\quad\text{and}\quad{c}((m,n,k),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime},k^{\prime}))=(-1)^{nm^{\prime}}.

We will show that
C^{*}_{r}(G,{c})
does not have a Cartan subalgebra that comes from a subgroupoid.

The maximal abelian subgroups on which
{c}
is symmetric (i.e.,
for which the twist is abelian) are

S_{1}\coloneq\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}\times 2\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}\coloneq 2\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}\times\mathbf{Z}\times 2\mathbf{Z}.

For trivial reasons, these are clopen and have full unit
space, and since

\displaystyle(m,n,k)\cdot(x,y,z)\cdot(m,n,k)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(x+2(ky-zn),y,z),

we see that both subgroups are normal and that

\{\,s\cdot(0,1,1)\cdot s^{-1}\mid s\in S_{i}\,\}=\{\,(0,1,1),(2,1,1)\,\}.

Thus,

\Omega_{S_{i}}\bigl((0,1,1)\bigr)=|\operatorname{Ad}_{S_{i}}\bigl((0,1,1)\bigr)|=2,

so

(0,1,1)\in\Omega_{S_{i}}^{-1}(\mathbf{Z}_{>1}).

In other words, the two maximal subgroups of
G
with abelian twist do
not satisfy Condition (
ricc
). Thus,
Corollary
3.23

yields that
C^{*}_{r}(S_{1})
and
C^{*}_{r}(S_{2})
are not Cartan subalgebras. And indeed,

\delta_{(0,1,1)}+\delta_{(2,1,1)}
commutes with the

C^{*}
-algebras

C^{*}_{r}(S_{i})
while not being contained in them,
proving that
C^{*}_{r}(S_{1})
and
C^{*}_{r}(S_{2})
are not maximal abelian.

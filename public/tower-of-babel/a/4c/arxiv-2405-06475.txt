Introduction

The importance of triangulated categories in modern mathematics is difficult to overestimate. However, triangulated categories have some well-known drawbacks. One of them is that they are not (co)complete and Freyd’s adjoint functor theorem does not apply. Brown representability comes as a substitute since in the presence of this property, triangulated functors commuting with (co)products have adjoints.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated
R
-linear category, where
R
is a base commutative ring. The theory of Brown representability looks for conditions (necessary and sufficient) for a contravariant functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
to be representable. Initially, this was done by Brown itself for the homotopy category of spectra,
[
4
]
; this involved contravariant functors sending coproducts into products and was generalized for compactly, and even for well generated triangulated categories. The common feature of these categories is that they that can be constructed in some way from a small subcategory (see
[
14
, Chapter 8]
). Initially, the objects in the small category were supposed to be nice enough, but later it became clear that more important was the fact that they are only a set; see
[
15
]
. A different, but somehow related problem is the so-called Brown-Adams representability. Now we consider a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
and the restricted Yoneda functor
\mathbf{y}X=\mathcal{D}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{C}}
, mapping an object
X\in\mathcal{D}
to a contravariant functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
. The problem is to identify functors
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
and natural transformations between them that are of the form
\mathbf{y}X
, for some
X\in\mathcal{D}
, respectively
\mathbf{y}(f)
, for some
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{D}
. If
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{D}
, then we recover the Brown representability above, where representability is taken in the categorical sense. But the prototypical example is when
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{D}^{c}
is the category of compact objects. For this case, we know that every cohomological functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
is of the form
\mathbf{y}X
, for some
X\in\mathcal{D}
and every natural transformation between cohomological functors comes from a map in
\mathcal{D}
(that is,
\mathbf{y}
is full and its essential image consists of all cohomological functors) if and only if pure projective dimension of
\mathcal{D}
is
\leq 1
; see
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we consider an
R
-linear category
\mathcal{D}
. All subcategories will be full and all functors will be
R
-linear.
The first Section gives a very general criterion of representability, see Corollary
1.2
. Here we do not need the entire triangulated structure on
\mathcal{D}
, the existence of weak (co)kernels being enough. In this context, we also find a criterion for a subcategory to be coreflective; see Corollary
1.4
.

Brown representability for categories without (co)products seems to be a more intricate problem than the classical one. To the best knowledge of the author, there are only three instances of results of this kind in the literature, namely
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
19
, Theorem 4.20]
and
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
. In Section 2 we combine ideas from the last two to give a new version which is intended to be as general as possible. Theorem
2.2
contains this result. Essentially, the idea is the one already mentioned: all objects of the category have to be constructed in a finite number of steps from a small set by taking direct summands, shifts, and triangles. The method also works for categories with coproducts: Allowing not only summands, shifts, and triangles but also coproducts, we get Brown representability for "big" categories as in
[
15
]
; see Corollary
2.4
.

Section 3 contains a relativization of the results concerning the relationship between the fullness of the restricted Yoneda functor
\mathbf{y}:\mathcal{D}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
and the pure projective dimension of objects in
\mathcal{D}
.

In Section 4 we assume that
\mathcal{D}
has a single compact generator
G
, and we show that the restriction of
\mathbf{y}
to an appropriate subcategory
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
of
\mathcal{D}
is full. Moreover, we also identify the essential image of this restriction; see Theorem
4.3
. The idea of the proof comes from a combination between
[
17
, Section 7]
and the so-called "two-sided approximation" of
[
3
]
. Note that under suitable hypotheses, namely
R
is noetherian and
\mathcal{D}(G,G[n])
finitely generated for all
n
, the category
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
contains exactly those objects
X\in\mathcal{D}
for which
\mathcal{D}(C,X)
is finitely generated for all
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
, and the essential image of the functor
\mathbf{y}:\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
consists exactly from those functors (modules)
M:\mathcal{D}^{c}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
satisfying the property that
M(C)
is finitely generated, for all
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
.

Section 5 contains an application of our results, where the category
\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}({A})
is the derived category of a dg-algebra
A
. It is clear that our results are more general than the corresponding results in
[
17
]
and
[
3
]
. Indeed, these papers assume (weakly) approximability to show Brown-Adams representability (i. e., that the restriction of the functor
\mathbf{y}
is full). This assumption is not necessary in Proposition
5.2
. Here
R
is supposed to be noetherian and
A
is a dg-algebra with coherent cohomology. Then
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
coincides with
\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
, the subcategory of
\mathbf{D}({A})
consisting of all
X\in\mathbf{D}({A})
having coherent cohomology.

1.
A general representability criterion

Throughout this section
R
is a commutative ring with one and
\mathcal{D}
is an additive
R
-linear category. We denote by
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
the category of all
R
-modules and by
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
the category of finitely presented
R
-modules. Let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
be a contravariant functor. It is customary to regard the category
\mathcal{D}
as being a ring with several objects, and further the functor
M
as being a right
\mathcal{D}
-module. Remark that the hypothesis on
\mathcal{D}
implies that it has finite biproducts. We call
\mathcal{D}

karoubian
, if every idempotent endomorphism in
\mathcal{D}
splits. In what follows, we look for some necessary and sufficient conditions for
M
to be representable, that is, to be of the form
\mathcal{D}(-,X)
for some
X\in\mathcal{D}
. The functor
M
is called
finitely generated
if there is a natural epimorphism
\mathcal{D}(-,D)\to M
, for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
.
Dually we say that
M
is a subfunctor of a representable functor if there is a natural monomorphism
M\to\mathcal{D}(-,D)
.
Furthermore, the functor
M
is called
finitely presented
if there is an exact sequence

\mathcal{D}(-,C)\to\mathcal{D}(-,D)\to M\to 0.

It is routine to show that
M
is finitely presented if and only if it is finitely generated and for every
D\in\mathcal{D}
, the kernel of any natural epimorphism
\mathcal{D}(-,D)\to M
is also finitely generated.

For two contravariant functors
M,N:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
, denote by
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,N)
the class of all natural transformations between them.
We denote by
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
the class of all contravariant functors
\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
; as we noticed, we view them as
\mathcal{D}
-modules. In order to organize
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
as a category with the morphisms
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,N)
we usually have to enlarge the universe. However, this set-theoretic issue does not appear in the following two situations:
If
\mathcal{D}
is essentially small, then
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,N)
is actually a set; therefore
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
belongs to the same universe as
\mathcal{D}
.
If
M
is finitely presented, it follows by the Yoneda lemma that
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,N)
is a set. Therefore we can construct, without enlarging the universe, the category whose objects are all finitely presented contaravariant functors
\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
and whose morphisms are natural transformations. We denote by
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
this category and we call it the category of finitely presented (right)
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

For the rest of this section, fix a
\mathcal{D}
-module
M
.
Consider now a (full) subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. We say that
M
is
\mathcal{A}
-finitely generated
if there is
A\in\mathcal{A}
and a natural transformation
\alpha:\mathcal{D}(-,A)\to M
such that

\alpha^{X}:\mathcal{D}(X,A)\to M(X)\to 0

is exact for all
X\in\mathcal{A}
. Remark that
M
is finitely generated exactly if it is
\mathcal{D}
-finitely generated and the two definitions are consistent, in the sense that
M
is
\mathcal{A}
-finitely generated if and only if its restriction
M|_{\mathcal{A}}
is finitely generated. Indeed by Yoneda lemma both natural transformations
\mathcal{D}(-,A)\to M
, respectively
\mathcal{A}(-,A)\to M|_{\mathcal{A}}
are in correspondence with an element in
M(A)
, therefore given one we can construct the other one.

Assume now that
\mathcal{D}
has
weak kernels
, that is, given
Y\to Z
in
\mathcal{D}
, there is
X\to Y
such that the sequence of functors

\mathcal{D}(-,X)\to\mathcal{D}(-,Y)\to\mathcal{D}(-,Z)

is exact. In this case, it is clear that the composite morophism
X\to Y\to Z
vanishes, and we call it a
weak kernel sequence
. The notions
weak cokernel
and
weak cokernel sequence
are defined dually. By
[
10
, Theorem 1.4]
we know that the existence of weak kernels is equivalent to the fact that
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
is abelian (see also
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
). Moreover, projective objects in
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
are direct summands of representable functors, see
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
. Consider further an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, and a covariant functor
F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
. By
[
8
, Universal Property 2.1]
there is a unique, up to a natural isomorphism, right exact (covariant) functor
\widehat{F}:\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{A}
such that
\widehat{F}\mathcal{D}(-,X)=F(X)
for all
X\in\mathcal{D}
. By a combination of
[
8
, Lemma 2.5 and Proposition 2.3]
, we infer that the functor
\widehat{F}
is exact if and only if
F
is
weak left exact
, that is, it sends every weak kernel sequence
X\to Y\to Z
to an exact sequence
F(X)\to F(Y)\to F(Z)
in
\mathcal{A}
. Remark that it is enough to check the weak left exactness for any particular choice of a weak kernel for
Y\to Z
. In fact, if
X\to Y\to Z
and
X^{\prime}\to Y\to Z
are two weak kernel sequences, then the maps
X\to Y
and
X^{\prime}\to Y
factor through each other, therefore the images of the maps
F(X)\to F(Y)
and
F(X^{\prime})\to F(Y)
coincide.
If
F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
is now a contravariant functor, we can apply the above statements for it, seen as a covariant functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
and we get a unique contravariant functor
\widehat{F}:\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{A}
such that
\widehat{F}\mathcal{D}(-,X)=F(X)
for all
X\in\mathcal{D}
and
\widehat{F}
sends cokernels to kernels. Specializing in the discussion above in the case where the codomain category is
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
, we obtain a generalization (and a completion) of
[
12
, Lemma 1]
:

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
has weak kernels and let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
be a contravariant functor.

a)

There is a natural isomorphism
\widehat{M}\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(-,M)
.
In particular,
M
is finitely presented if and only if
\widehat{M}
is representable.

b)

If, in addition,
M
is a subfunctor of a representable functor, then every kernel of a natural transformation
\mathcal{D}(-,C)\to M
is finitely generated. Consequently
M
is finitely presented if and only if it is finitely generated.

Proof.

a) The isomorphism follows by the facts that
\Hom_{D}(-,M)
sends cokernels to kernels, and
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D}(-,X),M)\cong M(X)
, according to the Yoneda lemma. The last statement is now obvious,
\widehat{M}
being represented by
M
; note that the notation
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(-,M)
makes sense even for
M\notin\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
.

b) From the hypothesis there is a natural monomorphism
M\to\mathcal{D}(-,D)
, for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
. If
\alpha:\mathcal{D}(-,C)\to M
is a natural transformation with the kernel
K
, then the composed map
\mathcal{D}(-,C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\to}}M\to\mathcal{D}(-,D)
is of the form
g^{*}:\mathcal{D}(-,C)\to\mathcal{D}(-,D)
, for some
g:C\to D
, by Yoneda. It follows
K=\Ker\alpha=\Ker(g^{*})
. Complete
g
to a weak kernel sequence
B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}D
. The diagram with exact rows

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(-,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(-,C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(-,D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(-,C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(-,D)}

can be completed commutatively with a map
\mathcal{D}(-,B)\to K
, which must be a natural epimorphism.
∎

Consider again a contravariant functor
F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
. In this case, the exactness of
\widehat{F}
is clearly equivalent to the exactness of the sequence
F(Z)\to F(Y)\to F(X)
for every weak kernel sequence
X\to Y\to Z
in
\mathcal{D}
. We call
weak right exact
a contravariant functor
F
satisfying this last property. Note that when we use this terminology we see the contravariant functor
F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
rather as a covariant functor
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
; this is the reason we refer to contravariant functors and we do not identify them neither with covariant functors
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
nor with functors
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
because we want to see them in one way or in another depending on the context.

An abelian category is said to be
Frobenius
, provided that projective and injective objects coincide.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
has weak kernels and that
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
is Frobenius. Let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
be a contravariant functor. If
M
is weak right exact, then
M
is isomorphic to a direct summand of a representable functor if and only if
M
is finitely presented. If, in addition,
\mathcal{D}
is karoubian, then
M
is representable if and only if
M
is finitely presented.

Proof.

A representable functor is obviously finitely presented, therefore so is any direct summand of it. Conversely, if
M
is finitely presented, that is,
M\in\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
, it follows by Proposition
1.1
a) that
\widehat{M}\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(-,M)
is representable. But
M
is weak right exact, and therefore
\widehat{M}
has to be exact. This means
M
is an injective object in
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
. By hypothesis, injective objects in
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
coincide with projective objects and are direct summands of representable functors.
∎

Let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
be a contravariant functor. If
\mathcal{D}
is essentially small, then we know that
M
is the colimit of all representable functors mapping to it. More precisely, we consider the category
\mathcal{D}/M
(which is also essentially small) whose objects are pairs of the form
(D,x)
with
D\in\mathcal{D}
and
x\in M(D)
, and the morphisms between
(C,y)
and
(D,x)
are those maps
f:C\to D
in
\mathcal{D}
for which
M(f)(x)=y
. Then
M\cong\colim_{(D,x)\in\mathcal{D}/M}\mathcal{D}(-,D)
, that is,
M
is the colimit of the functor
\mathcal{D}/M\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}},(D,x)\mapsto\mathcal{D}(-,D)
, where the canonical maps of the colimit are the natural transformations
\xi:\mathcal{D}(-,D)\to M
associated to
x\in M(D)
via the Yoneda isomorphism. If we want to express the same result for categories which are not essentially small, one option is to enlarge the universe. But the existence of some colimits and adjoints is sensitive with respect to this enlargement, therefore, in the following we will make another choice, namely we remain in the same universe, but we consider big colimits only locally. More precisely, let
\mathcal{I}
be a category. An
\mathcal{I}
-shaped diagram of contravariant functors is an assignment
i\mapsto M_{i}
, where
M_{i}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R

for any object
i
of
\mathcal{I}
and
\alpha\mapsto M_{\alpha}
, where
M_{\alpha}
is a natural transformation
M_{i}\to M_{j}
, for every map
\alpha:i\to j
in
\mathcal{I}
, satisfying the obvious functorial properties. We say that the contravariant functor
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
is the large colimit of
(M_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
, and we denote
M=\Colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}M_{i}
if there are natural transformations
\xi_{i}:M_{i}\to M
such that the cocone
\left(\xi_{i}^{X}:M_{i}(X)\to M(X)\right)_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
is a colimit cocone in
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
for all
X
in
\mathcal{D}
, that is

M(X)\cong\colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}M_{i}(X),\hbox{ for all }X\in\mathcal{D}.

Note that the last colimit is genuine in
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
even if it is indexed over a possibly large class. It is clear that, if they exist, colimits indexed over the large class
\mathcal{I}
are exact in
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
, provided that
\mathcal{I}
is directed; consequently, large directed colimits are component-wise exact. Furthermore, the Yoneda lemma implies that if
M\cong\Colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}M_{i}
as before and
X\in\mathcal{D}
then
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D}(-,X),M)\cong\colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}M_{i}(X)
. Therefore if we suppose further that
\mathcal{I}
is directed, and
N
is finitely presented, the exactness of
\mathcal{I}
-indexed colimits leads to existence of an isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(N,M)\cong\colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(N,M_{i}).

An example of a big colimit as before is obtained by observing that for any category
\mathcal{D}
we have

M\cong\Colim_{(D,x)\in\mathcal{D}/M}\mathcal{D}(-,D).

We call
flat
a contravariant functor
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
with the property that there is a directed category
\mathcal{I}
and an
\mathcal{I}
-shaped diagram
\left(\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i})\right)_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
consisting of representable functors such that

M\cong\Colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i}).

Proposition 1.3
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
is an additive
R
-category with weak kernels and weak cokernels. Let
M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
be a contravariant functor. Then

a)

M
is flat if and only if
M
is weak left exact.

b)

If
M
is weak left exact and finitely presented, then
M
is a direct summand of a representable functor.

Proof.

a). If M is flat, then
M\cong\Colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i})
, with
D_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is a directed category. If
X\to Y\to Z
is a cokernel sequence, then

\mathcal{D}(Z,D_{i})\to\mathcal{D}(Y,D_{i})\to\mathcal{D}(X,D_{i})

is exact by the definition of the weak cokernel, and since directed colimits are exact in
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
we get the weak left exactness of
M
. Conversely, we noted before that

M\cong\Colim_{(D,x)\in\mathcal{D}/M}\mathcal{D}(-,D).

We only have to show that if
M
is weak left exact, then
\mathcal{D}/M
is directed. It is enough to show that it has coproducts and coequalizers. The existence of coproducts is obvious. Furthermore, if
f,g:(C,y)\to(D,x)
are two parallel maps in
\mathcal{D}/M
, that is,
f,g:C\to D
are genuine maps in
\mathcal{D}
such that
M(f)(x)=y=M(g)(x)
, then choosing a weak cokernel for
f-g
we get a weak cokernel sequence
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f-g}}{{\longrightarrow}}D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\longrightarrow}}E
. The exactness of the sequence
M(E)\to M(D)\to M(C)
gives us an element
z\in M(E)
such that
M(h)(z)=x
, which shows that
\mathcal{D}/M
has coequalizers, proving our claim.

b) Since
M
is weak left exact, it is flat as we have seen above, therefore
M\cong\Colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i})
, with
D_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is a directed category. If, in addition,
M
is finitely presented, then

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,M)\cong\colim_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i})).

This implies that
1_{M}
is the image of some map in
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(M,\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i}))
, that is, it factors as
M\to\mathcal{D}(-,D_{i})\to M
, for some
i\in\mathcal{I}
. This means
M
is a direct summand of a representable functor.

∎

In order to formulate the next result, we need to introduce some terminology. If
\mathcal{S}
is a (full) subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
and recall that an
\mathcal{S}
-precover
of
D\in\mathcal{D}
is a morphism
S\to D
such that the induced map
\mathcal{D}(X,S)\to\mathcal{D}(X,D)
is surjective for all
X\in\mathcal{S}
. The subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is called
precovering
, provided that every
D\in\mathcal{D}
has an
\mathcal{S}
-precover.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}
is a karoubian additive
R
-category with weak kernels and weak cokernels. Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
be a full subcategory which is closed under weak cokernels and direct summands. If
\mathcal{S}
is precovering, then
\mathcal{S}
is coreflective.

Proof.

Let
D\in\mathcal{D}
. Consider an
\mathcal{S}
-precover
S\to D
and complete it to a weak kernel sequence
C\to S\to D
. Consider further an
\mathcal{S}
-precover
T\to C
. Together, these imply that the sequence

\mathcal{S}(X,T)\to\mathcal{S}(X,S)\to\mathcal{D}(X,D)\to 0

is exact for all

X\in\mathcal{S}
. This means that the cohomological functor
\mathcal{D}(-,D)|_{\mathcal{S}}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
is finitely presented. By hypothesis
\mathcal{S}
is karoubian and closed under weak cokernels. That means
\mathcal{S}
itself has weak cokernels, and
\mathcal{D}(-,D)|_{\mathcal{S}}
sends them to exact sequences. Moreover, if

is a map in
\mathcal{S}
, then taking first a weak kernel in
\mathcal{D}
, namely

and then a
\mathcal{S}
-precover

we get a weak kernel sequence

in
\mathcal{S}
. All the hypotheses of Proposition
1.3

\mathcal{D}(-,D)|_{\mathcal{S}}
are satisfied, so it is representable. Finding an object

that represents it is equivalent to defining a right adjoint

to the inclusion functor

.

Remark 1.5
.

Let specialize us to the when case
\mathcal{D}
is a triangulated category. With this assumption the completion of a map
Y\to Z
(respectively
X\to Y
) to a triangle
X\to Y\to Z\rightsquigarrow
provides a weak (co)kernel, therefore a functor
F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
is weak left exact if and only if it weak right exact and if and only if is homological. Moreover we know by
[

, Corollary 5.1.23]
, that
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}
is Frobenius. A subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is called

if it is closed under extensions and positive shifts. Therefore if
\mathcal{S}
is a suspended subcategory, then it is closed under weak cokernels in
\mathcal{D}
and all hypotheses of Corollary
1.4
are satisfied, leading to the following generalization for
[
16
, Proposition 1.4]
: Every suspended karoubian subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{D}
is coreflective. Note also that despite the fact that in
[
16
, Proposition 1.4]
, the subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is supposed to be triangulated, therefore we may apply Corollary
1.2
in order to get the same conclusion, the original proof is much closer to the first one which uses Corollary
1.4
. The fact that the proof in
[
16
]
can be applied for subcategories which are only suspended (and not necessary triangulated) was also observed in
[

, Theorem 2.9]
. We can compare Corollary

with the results appeared in
[

: The same conclusion that

is coreflective it is proved under slightly different hypotheses.

In this section, the category
\mathcal{D}
is triangulated. As we noted before,
\hbox{\rm mod-}{\mathcal{D}}

\mathcal{D}(-,G)
is

-finite. Then a contravariant functor

is isomorphic to a direct summand of a representable functor if and only if it is cohomological and

-finite. If further

is karoubian, then

is representable if and only if it is cohomological and

-finite.

Proof.

It is clear that

is stable under shifts and, by hypothesis,
\mathcal{A}^{*n}=\mathcal{D}
for some

. The argument used in
[

to show that
M
is
\mathcal{A}
-finitely generated (the base case of the induction there) goes as follows: Let

be such that
M(G[n])=0
, for
|n|>m
. Let
\{a_{n,i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k_{n}\}
be a (finite) set of generators for
M(G[n])
for
|n|\leq m
. By Yoneda we get a set of natural transformations
\alpha_{n,i}:\mathcal{D}(-,G[n])\to M
, with

. For

, we get a natural transformation

which is an epimorphism when it is restricted to
\mathcal{A}
. Since the functor
\mathcal{D}(-,G)
is
G
-finite, it follows immediately that
\mathcal{D}(-,D)
is
G
-finite cohomological functor for all

. The argument used above for

, applied this time for
\mathcal{D}(-,D)
, with

arbitrary chosen, shows that
\mathcal{A}
is precovering. If

is the kernel of a natural transformation
\mathcal{D}(-,D)\to M

is
\mathcal{A}
-finitely generated. Therefore, Theorem

∎

\mathcal{A}

3.
Pure dimension less than or equal to 1

From now on
\mathcal{D}
will be a triangulated category with arbitrary coproducts. Recall that an object
C\in\mathcal{D}
is called compact if the covariant functor
\mathcal{D}(C,-):\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
commutes with coproducts. Denote by
\mathcal{D}^{c}
the subcategory of all compact objects in
\mathcal{D}
. Suppose
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated, that is,
\mathcal{D}^{c}
is essentially small and
\mathcal{D}(C,X)=0
for all
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
implies
X=0
.
A main tool in our consideration is the restricted Yoneda functor
\mathbf{y}:\mathcal{D}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
given by
\mathbf{y}X=\mathcal{D}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. Thus, if we know that
\mathcal{D}^{c}
is essentially small, then
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated exactly if the functor
\mathbf{y}
reflects zero objects. Since a map
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{D}
is an isomorphism if and only if the third term of its completion to a triangle vanishes, it follows further that this is equivalent to
\mathbf{y}
reflecting isomorphisms, see
[
14
, Lemma 6.2.8]
. Recall that the kernel ideal of
\mathbf{y}
is the ideal of
phantom maps
, that is, a map
\phi
in
\mathcal{D}
is phantom exactly if
\mathbf{y}(\phi)=0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
9
, Lemma 1.3]
For a triangle

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}X[1]

in
\mathcal{D}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{y}(\alpha)
is a monomorphism.

(ii)

\mathbf{y}(\beta)
is an epimorphism.

(iii)

\mathbf{y}(\phi)=0
, that is
\phi
is a phantom map.

(iv)

The sequence
0\to\mathbf{y}X\to\mathbf{y}Y\to\mathbf{y}Z\to 0
is short exact in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
.

We call
pure
triangles satisfying the equivalent conditions in Lemma
3.1
above. An object
X\in\mathcal{D}
is called pure projective (injective) if
\mathcal{D}(X,-)
(respectively,
\mathcal{D}(-,X)
sends pure triangles into short exact sequences in
\mathrm{Mod}\textrm{-}R
. By
[
6
, Corollary 4.5]
or
[
1
, Lemma 8.1]
, the class of pure projective objects in
\mathcal{D}
coincides with
\mathrm{Add}({\mathcal{D}^{c}})
. It is clear that this class is precovering, in the sense that for every
X\in\mathcal{D}
there is an object
P_{X}\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathcal{D}^{c})
and a map
P_{X}\to X
such that every map
P\to X
, with
P\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathcal{D}^{c})
factors through the previous one. We say that
X\in\mathcal{D}
is of pure projective dimension less than or equal to 1, and we write
\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
if there is a pure triangle

P_{1}\to P_{0}\to X\rightsquigarrow
with
P_{0},P_{1}
being pure projective. We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{d}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of the objects
X\in\mathcal{D}
with
\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The following are equivalent for an objects
X\in\mathcal{D}
:

(i)

\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
.

(ii)

\mathbf{y}_{X,Y}:\mathcal{D}(X,Y)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{y}X,\mathbf{y}Y)
is surjective, for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
.

In particular, the restriction of
\mathbf{y}
to
\mathcal{D}_{d}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is full and its essential image consists of those
{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
-modules of projective dimension less than or equal to 1.

Proof.

First note that if
P
is pure projective, then
\mathbf{y}_{P,Y}
is bijective for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
. Indeed this property are inherited by direct summands, and for a coproducts of compact objects
P\cong\coprod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}C_{i}
, this follows from Yoneda lemma since

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}\left(\mathbf{y}(\coprod_{i\in I}C_{i}),\mathbf{y}Y\right)

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}\left(\coprod_{i\in I}\mathbf{y}(C_{i}),\mathbf{y}Y\right)\cong\prod_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{y}C_{i},\mathbf{y}Y)

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}(C_{i},Y)\cong\mathcal{D}\left(\coprod_{i\in I}C_{i},Y\right).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Let
X\in\mathcal{D}
be an object with
\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
. Then there is a pure triangle
P_{1}\to P_{0}\to X\rightsquigarrow
, with
P_{1},P_{0}\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathcal{D}^{c})
. For

, the commutative diagram with exact rows

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(X,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{y}_{X,Y}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(P_{0},Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{y}_{P_{0},Y}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(P_{1},Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{y}_{P_{1},Y}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{y}X,\mathbf{y}Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{y}P_{0},\mathbf{y}Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{y}P_{1},\mathbf{y}Y)}

shows that
\mathbf{y}_{X,Y}
is surjective.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Consider a projective presentation
\mathbf{y}P_{1}\to\mathbf{y}P_{0}\to\mathbf{y}X\to 0
of

. Since
\mathbf{y}_{P,-}
is bijective for all

, there are unique maps

that induce the above presentation. The composition

vanishes, therefore completing
\alpha

\mathcal{D}

\textstyle{P_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathbf{y}(\beta)
is surjective, therefore the lower triangle is pure. On the other hand, from the commutative diagram with exact rows in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}

\textstyle{\mathbf{y}P_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

that completes it commutatively. By hypothesis, it must be of the form
\mathbf{y}(\delta)
for some

in

. It follows that
\mathbf{y}(\gamma\delta)
is an isomorphism, therefore, so is

too. This implies that the above morphism of triangles splits, so

is isomorphic to a direct summand of

. This shows that

is pure projective, which precisely means that

.

Finally, it is clear that the functor
\mathcal{D}_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{y}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}

. In contrast, if

has

, then there is a short exact sequence

in

, with

. The map
\mathbf{y}P_{1}\to\mathbf{y}P_{0}
is of the form
\mathbf{y}(\alpha)
for a unique

in
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathbf{y}(\alpha)
is a monomorphism, it follows that the triangle that completes it
P_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{0}\to X\rightsquigarrow

\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
and

.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{D}
such that
X\cong\underrightarrow{\mathrm{hocolim}}_{n\geq 0}P_{n}
, with
P_{n}\in\mathrm{Add}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. Then
X\in\mathcal{D}_{d}
and, consequently,
\mathbf{y}_{X,Y}
is surjective for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Note that the triangle defining the homotopy colimit (up to a non-canonical isomorphism):

\coprod_{n\geq 0}P_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-shift}}{{\longrightarrow}}\coprod_{n\geq 0}P_{n}\longrightarrow X\rightsquigarrow

is pure according to
[

, Lemma 2.8]
. Therefore,
\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
and consequently, Proposition
3.2
applies.
∎

4.
Locally coherent cohomological functors

Fix a module
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
, and an object
G\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. We will say that
M
is
G
-
locally coherent
, provided that the following two conditions hold:

(LC1)

There are
D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
and a natural transformation
\alpha:\mathbf{y}D\to M
such that
\alpha^{G}:\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D)\to M(G)
is surjective.

(LC2)

If
\beta:\mathbf{y}D\to M
is a natural transformation, then there is a map
f:C\to D
in
\mathcal{D}^{c}
such that
\beta\mathbf{y}f=0
and

\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,C)\to\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D)\to M(G)

is exact.

If
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}
is a set of compact objects, we say that
M
is
\mathcal{G}
-
locally coherent
, provided that it is
G
-locally coherent for every
G\in\mathcal{G}
.
We will say that an object
X\in\mathcal{D}
has
G
-
locally coherent cohomology
if
\mathbf{y}X\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is

G[\mathbb{Z}]
-locally coherent.

Note that conditions (LC1) and (LC2) above are inspired by conditions (a) and (b) characterizing locally finitely presented functors in
[
19
, pag. 205]
. Sure, we regard the category
\mathcal{D}^{c}
as a subcategory in the larger
\mathcal{D}
but this difference is not essential. More important is that the requirement in (LC1) and (LC2) is only about
\alpha^{G}
, respectively
\beta^{G}
, while
[
19
]
the conclusion is about
\alpha^{G[n]}
and
\beta^{G[n]}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Even if we consider a
G[\mathbb{Z}]
-locally coherent module (i. e. functor)
M
, it seems that our notion is weaker than that of a locally finitely presented functor in
[
19
]
, since the natural transformations
\alpha
and
\beta
depend here on
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, while in
[
19
]
they are unique.
However, some arguments can be used, as observed in
[
19
, Remark 4.7]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
and
G\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. Then:

(a)

\mathcal{D}^{c}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{G}
.

(b)

M
is
G
-locally coherent if and only if
M
satisfies (LC1) and the kernel of any natural transformation
\mathbf{y}C\to M
satisfies (LC1).

(c)

If
\beta_{i}:\mathbf{y}D_{i}\to M
, with
i=1,2
are so that (LC2) holds for
\beta=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}:\mathbf{y}(D_{1}\oplus D_{2})\to M
, then (LC2) holds for
\beta_{i}
(
i=1,2
).

(d)

Assume (LC1) holds. If (LC2) holds for those
\beta:\mathbf{y}D\to M
, with
D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
, for which
\beta^{G}:\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D)\to M(G)
is surjective, then (LC2) holds for all
\beta
.

(e)

If
M
is
G
-locally coherent, then
M
is
X
-locally coherent for any direct summand
X
of
G
.

Suppose in addition that
M
is cohomological; then

(f)

If
X_{1}\to X\to X_{2}\rightsquigarrow
is a triangle and
M
is
X_{i}
-locally coherent, for
i=1,2
, then
M
is
X
-locally coherent.

(g)

If
M
is
G[\mathbb{Z}]
-locally coherent then
M
is
\mathrm{thick}\,(G)
-locally coherent.

Proof.

For (a) use the proof of
[
19
, lemma 4.2]
. Statement (b) is obvious, and statement (e) follows immediately. For (c) and (d) use the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
. Statement (f) uses the same argument as
[
19
, Lemma 4.6]
and statement (g) follows from (e) and (f).
∎

Note that if
G\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
is a single generator for
\mathcal{D}
in the sense that

such that
\mathcal{D}(G[i],X)=0

, then

. In this case, we denote

and Lemma

does not really depend on

\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid X\hbox{ has }D\hbox{-locally coherent cohomology, for any }D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}\}.

Lemma 4.2

. We denote by

, for all

.

For an integer

, the object

Finally, if

. Since every object in

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category that has a compact generator
G
. Then every

is a homotopy colimit of compact objects. Furthermore, the functor

\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{y}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}

is full, and its essential image consists of
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-locally coherent cohomological modules. Moreover, every locally coherent cohomological
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-module
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is of projective dimension
\leq 1
.

Proof.

We use the same symbol
\mathbf{y}
for both the initial functor
\mathcal{D}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
and its restriction
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. As we already noted
\mathcal{D}^{c}=\mathrm{thick}\,(G)
, since
G
is a compact generator. By the very definition of
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
, for every
X\in\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
, the cohomological
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-module
\mathbf{y}X
is
G[\mathbb{Z}]
-locally coherent, consequently, it satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
4.2
. Then there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbf{y}D\cong\mathbf{y}X
, for some
D=\underrightarrow{\mathrm{hocolim}}_{n\geq 0}D_{n}
, with
D_{n}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. Furthermore, Corollary
3.3
implies that this natural transformation comes from a map

in
\mathcal{D}
, which has to be an isomorphism, since
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated. Therefore, the same Corollary
3.3
implies that
\mathbf{y}:\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is full. Finally, if
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is a locally coherent cohomological
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-module, then applying Lemma
4.2
again, we get an isomorphism
\mathbf{y}D\to M
, for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
. The assumptions in
M
imply
D\in\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}
. The last statement is now obvious.
∎

Keep the hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
, namely
\mathcal{D}
is a triangulated category with a compact generator
G
. Define:

\mathcal{D}^{-}_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\mid\mathcal{D}(G[-n],X)=0\hbox{ for }n\gg 0\},

\mathcal{D}^{+}_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\mid\mathcal{D}(G[-n],X)=0\hbox{ for }n\ll 0\},

and
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{lcoh}=\mathcal{D}^{-}_{lcoh}\cap\mathcal{D}^{+}_{lcoh}
.
For brevity, we will introduce the following shorthand. If
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is a module, we will say that
M^{*}(G)
is
s
-bounded, with

, if
\Sigma^{n}M(G)=0
, for
n\gg 0
,
n\ll 0
, respectively
|n|\gg 0
(i. e., the graded
R
-module

is bounded above, bellow, respectively, two-sided). Therefore, we get
\mathcal{D}^{s}_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\mid\mathbf{y}X\hbox{ is }s\hbox{-bounded}\}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category that has a compact generator

. Then the functor

\mathcal{D}^{s}_{lcoh}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}},\hbox{ with }s\in\{-,+,b\},

is full and its essential image consists of locally coherent cohomological modules

, which are

In all three cases, the functors are full, as they are restrictions of the (full) functor
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. Furthermore, the description of the essential image follows from the definition of the categories

,
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{lcoh}
and

.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
such that
M(G)
is a finitely generated
R
-module, for some object
G\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. Then there are
D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
and a natural transformation
\mathbf{y}D\to M
inducing a surjective map
\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D)\to M(G).

Proof.

Since all modules are epimorphic images of free ones, we know that there is a natural epimorphism
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{y}D_{i}\to M
and evaluating it at
G
we obtain an exact sequence in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{R}
:

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D_{i})\to M(G)\to 0.

Since
M(G)
is finitely generated, there is a finite subset
J\subseteq I
, such that

\bigoplus_{i\in J}\mathcal{D}^{c}(G,D_{i})\to M(G)\to 0

remains exact. Therefore, it is enough to take
D=\coprod_{i\in J}D_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
and the restricted natural transformation
\mathbf{y}D\to M
does the job we need.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
is a compact generator for
\mathcal{D}
, with the property that
\mathcal{D}(G,G[n])
is a finitely generated
R
-module for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the
R
-module
\mathcal{D}(C,D)
is finitely generated for all
C,D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}

Proof.

The proof is an immediate consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{D}^{c}=\mathrm{thick}\,(G)
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume the ring
R
is noetherian. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
has a compact generator
G\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
such that
\mathcal{D}(G,G[n])\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
is a cohomological module, then
M
is
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-locally coherent if and only if
M(C)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, for all
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. Consequently

\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{D}(C,X)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R\hbox{ for all }C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}\}.

Proof.

Clearly,
M(G[n])\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
M(C)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
for all
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
. If
M(C)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
and
K
is the kernel of a natural transformation
\mathbf{y}D\to M
, with
D\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
, then the noetherian assumption on

implies that
K(C)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
. Therefore,
M
is
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-locally coherent, by Lemma
4.5
. Conversely, if
M
is
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-locally coherent, then
M(C)
is a quotient of
\mathcal{D}(C,D)
, hence it belongs to
\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
.
∎

5.
Application: the case of derived categories over dg-algebras

In this Section we will particularize our general results for the case of derived categories of dg-algebras. Recall that a dg-algebra over
R
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{n}
, together with a differential
d:A\to A
, which is a
R
-linear map, homogeneous of degree 1, satisfying
d^{2}=0
and the graded Leibnitz rule, namely
d(ab)=d(a)b+(-1)^{n}ad(b)
, for all
a\in A^{n}
and all
b\in A
. We follow
[
7
]
in the construction of the following categories: The category of all dg-
A
-modules, the homotopy category of
A
and the derived category of
A
; this last category is denoted
\mathbf{D}({A})
, is compactly generated with
A
a single compact generator. The subcategory of compact objects in
\mathbf{D}({A})
consists of the so-called perfect complexes and is denoted by
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})
. Note that perfect complexes can be described as direct summands of an object in
\mathbf{D}({A})
, which is represented by a differential graded module, having a finite filtration by differential graded submodules, whose quotients are finite direct sums of shifts of
A
(see
[
20
, Tag 09R3]
). Recall also that the dg-algebra
A
is called
proper
, respectively
regular
, if
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{n}A\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, respectively
A
is a strong generator of
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})
, see
[
18
, Definition 1.5]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring, and let
A
be a proper and regular dg-algebra over
R
, satisfying also the property that
H^{n}(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then a module
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}
is representable if and only if
M
is cohomological and

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Sigma^{n}M(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R

(that is,
M
is
A
-finite).

Proof.

Since
A
is regular,
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})
is strongly generated by
A
. Moreover, the properness can be reformulated by saying that the module
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})(-,A)
is
A
-finite. Then the conclusion here follows from Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Furthermore, we consider the subcategory:

\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({A})\mid H^{i}(X)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R\hbox{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

A priori, we did not impose any finiteness conditions on
A
. Even in this generality, we can define

\mathbf{D}^{-}_{coh}({A})=\{H\in\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})\mid H^{n}(X)=0\hbox{ for }n\gg 0\},

\mathbf{D}^{+}_{coh}(A)=\{H\in\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})\mid H^{n}(X)=0\hbox{ for }n\ll 0\},

and

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({A})=\{H\in\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})\mid H^{n}(X)=0\hbox{ for }|n|\gg 0\}.

We observe that, by Corollary
4.7
,
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}
is
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})
-locally coherent if and only if
\Sigma^{n}M(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, this is equivalent to
M:\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})\to\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
; we will simply call
locally coherent
a functor that satisfies this property.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring, and let
A
be a dg-algebra over
R
, with the property that
H^{n}(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the following statements hold:

(a)

The functor

\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{D}({A})\longrightarrow\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}

is full, and its essential image consists of locally coherent cohomological modules.

(b)

\mathrm{pp.dim}X\leq 1
holds for every
X\in\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
.

(c)

For
s\in\{-,+,b\}
, the functor

\mathbf{D}^{s}_{coh}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{D}({A})\longrightarrow\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}

is full and its essential image consists of locally coherent cohomological modules
M\in\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}
, which are
s
-bounded.

Proof.

Statements (a) and (b) follow from Theorem
4.3
and statement (c) follows from Corollary
4.4
. To be more precise, put
\mathcal{D}=\mathbf{D}({A})
. Then, it is enough to observe that for a complex
X\in\mathbf{D}({R})
, we have
H^{n}(X)\cong\mathbf{D}({A})(A[-n],X)
, therefore
\mathcal{D}_{lcoh}=\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
, according to Corollary
4.7
. Analogous identifications can be made for
\mathbf{D}^{-}_{coh}({A})
,
\mathbf{D}^{+}_{coh}(A)
, and
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({A})
.
∎

It is sometimes useful to assume that
A
is non-positive, that is,
A^{n}=0
, for
n>0
. If this is the case, then we know by
[
17
, Remark 3.3]
, that the category
\mathbf{D}({A})
is approximable in the sense of
[
17
, Definition 0.21]
. Moreover, the pair
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})

is a t-structure, where

\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({A})\mid H^{n}(X)=0\hbox{ for }n>0\},

\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({A})\mid H^{n}(X)=0\hbox{ for }n<0\}.

This t-structure is generated by the compact generator
A
of
\mathbf{D}({A})
in the sense that
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}({A})\mid\mathbf{D}({A})(A,X[n])=0\hbox{ for }n>0\}
; in particular, this t-structure is in the preferred equivalence class, defined in
[
17
, Definition 0.14]
. As usual, denote
\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-n]
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider further
\mathcal{D}^{-}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}
and
\mathcal{D}^{+}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}
. Therefore, we get
\mathbf{D}^{-}_{coh}({A})=\mathcal{D}^{-}\cap\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
and
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({A})=\mathcal{D}^{-}\cap\mathcal{D}^{+}\cap\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring, and let
A
be a non-positive dg-algebra over
R
, with the property that
H^{n}(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the functor

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\subseteq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{D}({A})\longrightarrow\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbf{D}_{perf}({A})}

is not only full, but also faithful.

Proof.

The faithfulness of this functor follows from
[
17
, Theorem 0.3]
, since in this case the category
\mathbf{D}({A})
is approximable.
∎

We end this paper with some considerations about the way our results apply for schemes.
We consider a scheme
\mathbb{X}
that is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Then it is known that the category
\hbox{\rm Qcoh-}\mathbb{X}
of quasi-coherent
\mathbb{X}
-modules is Grothendieck, and the category
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
of
\mathbb{X}
-modules with quasi-coherent cohomology is compactly generated and has a single compact generator
G
. Moreover, compact objects in
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
are precisely perfect complexes; recall that a complex is perfect means that it is locally quasi-isomorphic a bounded complex of vector bundles; see
[
2
, Theorem 3.1.1]
. We denote by
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({\mathbb{X}})=\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})^{c}
.
According to
[
2
, Corollary 3.1.8]
,

\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
is equivalent to the derived category of dg-algebra
A=A(\mathbb{X})
with bounded cohomology. Using this observation, we can apply Proposition
5.2
for the category
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
. To be more precise, the equivalence

\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})\to\mathbf{D}({A})

sends the compact generator
G
to
A\in\mathbf{D}({A})
. Using this observation we can see that

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})_{lcoh}

\displaystyle=\{X\in\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})\mid\mathcal{D}(C,X)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R\hbox{ for all }C\in\mathbf{D}_{perf}({\mathbb{X}})\}

\displaystyle=\{X\in\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})\mid\mathcal{D}(G[n],X)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R\hbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is exactly the image of
\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
under converse
\mathbf{D}({A})\to\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
of the above equivalence. We don’t know, but it seems to be very interesting, if
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})_{lcoh}
can be characterized cohomologically in the same vein as
\mathbf{D}_{coh}({A})
can. We also note that
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})^{-}_{lcoh}
,
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})^{+}_{lcoh}
, and
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})^{b}_{lcoh}
are images of
\mathbf{D}^{-}_{coh}({A})
,
\mathbf{D}^{+}_{coh}(A)
, respectively
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({A})
. We also record the property stated in
[
19
, Proposition 6.9]
: For
X\in\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
, the
\mathbf{D}_{perf}({\mathbb{X}})
-module
(\mathbf{y}X)^{*}(G)
is
s
-bounded, with
s\in\{-,+,b\}
, if and only if
H^{n}(X)=0
for
n\gg 0
, respectively,
n\ll 0
, or
|n|\gg 0
, consequently the boundedness property can be characterized cohomologically. If
\mathbb{X}
is quasi-compact and separated, then the category
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})
is equivalent to
\mathbf{D}({\hbox{\rm Qcoh-}\mathbb{X}})
and it is approximable, see
[
17
, Example 3.6]
. Therefore,
[
17
, Theorem 0.3]
applies in this case in order to construct a fully faithful functor similar to the one in Proposition
5.3
, even if we are not sure that the corresponding dg-algebra
A(\mathbb{X})
is non-positive. However, to avoid confusion, we have to point out that the domain of this functor in
[
17
, Theorem 0.3]
is not generally known to coincide with
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{coh}({\mathbb{X}})
. In order to have this equality, we have to assume more restrictive conditions on
\mathbb{X}
, e. g., to be noetherian. If
X
is proper over the noetherian ring
R
, then the condition
H^{n}(A)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R
is automatically satisfied for
A=A(\mathbb{X})
.

Assume now that
\mathbb{X}
is projective over the noetherian ring
R
, that is,
\mathbb{X}
is a closed subscheme of the projectivization of a vector bundle over
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
(see
[
3
, Definition 1.1]
). Then by
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
we know

\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})_{lcoh}=\{X\in\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})\mid H^{n}(X)\in\mathrm{mod}\textrm{-}R\hbox{, for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

that is,
\mathbf{D}_{Qcoh}({\mathbb{X}})_{lcoh}=\mathbf{D}_{coh}({\mathbb{X}})
can be characterized cohomologically in this case. Therefore, Proposition
5.2
can be stated in this case simply by replacing
A
everywhere with
\mathbb{X}
. Furthermore, an analogous statement with Proposition
5.3
holds true, but with the same warning as before, the involved dg-algebra is not necessary known to be non-positive.

Multiple Packing: Lower Bounds via Error Exponents
Yihan Zhang 1 Shashank Vatedka 2

Abstract
We derive lower bounds on the maximal rates for multiple packings in high-dimensional Euclidean spaces. Multiple packing is a natural generalization of the sphere packing problem. For any N > 0 N>0 and L ∈ ℤ ≥ 2 L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2} , a multiple packing is a set 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of points in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} such that any point in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} lies in the intersection of at most L − 1 L-1 balls of radius n ​ N \sqrt{nN} around points in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We study this problem for both bounded point sets whose points have norm at most n ​ P \sqrt{nP} for some constant P > 0 P>0 and unbounded point sets whose points are allowed to be anywhere in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Given a well-known connection with coding theory, multiple packings can be viewed as the Euclidean analog of list-decodable codes, which are well-studied for finite fields. We derive the best known lower bounds on the optimal multiple packing density. This is accomplished by establishing a curious inequality which relates the list-decoding error exponent for additive white Gaussian noise channels, a quantity of average-case nature, to the list-decoding radius, a quantity of worst-case nature. We also derive various bounds on the list-decoding error exponent in both bounded and unbounded settings which are of independent interest beyond multiple packing.

I-C

Error exponents

Our lower bounds on
C_{L-1}(P,N)
and
C_{L-1}(N)
are derived by making an interesting connection between list-decodable codes for adversarial (omnsicient jamming) channels and list-decodable codes for the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel.

Loosely speaking, we show that any code that is
(L-1)
-list-decodable over the AWGN
{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma^{2})
channel with exponentially decaying probability of error
e^{-nE+o(n)}
for some
E>0
can be expurgated without loss of rate to give a code with Chebyshev radius

. We then derive bounds on the list-decoding random coding and expurgated error exponents for the AWGN channel, and use these to obtain lower bounds on the (adversarial) list-decoding capacity. A similar approach was used to derive lower bounds on the zero-rate threshold of
binary
channels under (adversarial) list-decoding in
[
DG21
]
.
However, no lower bounds on the list-decoding capacity were derived below the zero-rate threshold.

List-decoding error exponents for discrete memoryless channels (DMCs) were originally studied by Gallager
[
Gal68
]
and Viterbi and Omura
[
VO13
]
. A more systematic study of list-decoding error exponents for DMCs was made by Merhav
[
Mer14
]
. Merhav
[
Mer14
]
gave bounds on the list-decoding random coding and expurgated error exponents for both constant and exponential (in
n

We also derive these bounds in the case where input constraints are imposed on the channel through an extension of the same ideas. The techniques used are standard, following
[
Gal68
]
and in fact, our expressions for the DMC without input constraints numerically match those in Gallager
[
Gal68
]
and Merhav
[
Mer14

Mer14

We also solve the optimization problem in an alternate form that allows us to get a simple closed form expression for the achievable (adversarial) list-decoding rate.

\displaystyle E_{L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}E_{\mathrm{r},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr}),&R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\leq R\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\mathsf{snr})\\
E_{\mathrm{sl},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr}),&R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\leq R\leq R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\\
E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr}),&0\leq R\leq R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\end{cases},

where
E_{\mathrm{r},L-1},E_{\mathrm{sl},L-1},E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}
denote the random coding exponent, the straight line bound and the expurgated exponent, respectively.
These bounds read as follows:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{r},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[e^{2R}-\frac{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{4e^{2R}}{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathsf{snr}}{4e^{2R}}\left(e^{2R}+1-(e^{2R}-1)\sqrt{1+\frac{4e^{2R}}{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sl},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-R(L-1)+\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(L+\mathsf{snr}+\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(L-\mathsf{snr}+\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\left(L+\mathsf{snr}-\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)-\frac{L}{2}\ln(2L),

(10)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\mathsf{snr}(Lt-1)}{2Lt},

(11)

where

is the unique solution to the equation
(Lt-1)e^{2R}=(L-1)t^{\frac{L}{L-1}}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\mathsf{snr}}{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{2(L-2)}{L^{2}}\mathsf{snr}+\frac{\mathsf{snr}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln\frac{\sqrt{L^{2}+\mathsf{snr}^{2}-2\mathsf{snr}(L-2)}+L+\mathsf{snr}}{2L}+\frac{1}{L-1}\ln\frac{\sqrt{L^{2}+\mathsf{snr}^{2}-2\mathsf{snr}(L-2)}+L-\mathsf{snr}}{2L}\right).

(13)

L-1=1
, the above bounds recover the Gallager’s exponents

,

All bounds in this paper hold for any

In this section, we discuss the impact of our finite-

Some of these results were known previously and others follow from the bounds in the current paper.

Characterizing

However, if the list-size

It is well-known that

]

Theorem 1

(Folklore,
[

]
)
.

Let

.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

1.

There exist
(P,N,L-1)
-multiple packings of rate
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{P}{N}-\varepsilon
for some

2.

Any
(P,N,L-1)
-multiple packing of rate
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{P}{N}+\varepsilon
must satisfy

.

Therefore,

.

A simple calculation reveals that

equals

for large

It is an interesting open question to resolve whether this is indeed the right scaling.

(

]

.

There exist

IV
Our techniques

To derive lower bounds on list-decoding capacity, the most popular strategy is random coding with expurgation
[
ZV22c
]
, a standard tool from information theory.
To show the existence of a list-decodable code of rate
R
, we can simply randomly sample
e^{nR}
points independently each according to a certain distribution.
We then throw away (a.k.a. expurgate) one point from each of the bad lists.
By carefully analyzing the error event and choosing a proper rate, we can guarantee that the remaining code has essentially the same rate after the removal process.
We then get a list-decodable code of rate
R
by noting that the remaining code contains no bad lists.

The challenge is, however, that analyzing the error event involving the Chebyshev radius is a tricky task.
In this paper, we take a different approach via a proxy known as the
error exponent
for an AWGN channel.
The latter quantity is the optimal exponent of the probability of list-decoding error of a code used over a Gaussian channel which inflicts an additive white Gaussian noise.
We establish a curious inequality which relates the Chebyshev radius of lists in a code to the error exponent of the code.
This inequality and connection originally appeared in
[
Bli99
]
, but some of the details were missing (see
Section

VIII-F
).
We use different ideas to and provide a complete alternate proof in
Section

VIII
, which is a major contribution of this work.
Towards this end, we provide geometric understanding of the higher-order Voronoi partition induced by
L
-lists which naturally arises as the error regions under maximum likelihood list-decoding.
We obtain sharp estimates on the Gaussian measure of the higher-order Voronoi region associated with a list which relates the error probability to the Chebyshev radius of the list.
This inequality bridges two quantities of fundamentally different natures.
The Chebyshev radius is a combinatorial characteristic of a code against worst-case errors, whereas the error exponent is a probabilistic characteristic of a code against average-case errors.
The multiple packing problem then reduces to bounding the error exponent.

Our results on list-decoding error exponents of Gaussian channels are of independent interest beyond the study of multiple packing.
We borrow standard techniques from information theory to prove bounds on list-decoding error exponents.
Specifically, in the bounded case, we follow Gallager’s approach
[
Gal65
,
Gal68
]
and analyze random spherical codes; in the unbounded case, we mix the ideas in
[
IZF12
,
AB10
]
and analyze PPPs and their expurgated versions (known as Matérn processes) using tools from stochastic geometry, e.g., the Slivnyak’s theorem and the Campbell’s theorem.
It has been long known that list-decoding with any subexponential (in
n
) list-sizes does not increase the capacity of any discrete memoryless channel (DMC) or Gaussian channel.
Our results further show that list-decoding with constant list-sizes does not even improve the error exponent of capacity-achieving codes.
In fact, for any
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
and any rate
R
above a certain critical rate
R_{\mathrm{crit},1}
below the capacity, the
(L-1)
-list-decoding error exponent coincides with the unique-decoding error exponent (i.e., when
L=2
).
However, the error exponent
does
strictly increase under list-decoding when
R
is below
R_{\mathrm{crit},1}
.
By carefully analyzing the aforementioned ensembles of random codes and solving delicate optimization problems coming out of the analysis, we obtain explicit bounds on the list-decoding error exponent of Gaussian channels with or without input constraints.
These expressions, to the best of our knowledge, are not known before.
Moreover, they recover prior results by Gallager
[
Gal65
]
,
[
Gal68
, Theorem 7.4.4]
(in the bounded case) and Poltyrev
[
Pol94
]
(in the unbounded case) for
L=2
.

Basic definitions and facts

Given the intimate connection between packing and error-correcting codes, we will interchangeably use the terms “multiple packing” and “list-decodable code”.

Some choices of
{\mathcal{B}}
may be more convenient than the others for analyzing certain ensembles of packings.
Therefore, we do not fix the choice of
{\mathcal{B}}
in
Definition

2
.

Remark 2
.

It is a slight abuse of notation to write
R({\mathcal{C}})
to refer to the rate of either a bounded packing or an unbounded packing.
However, the meaning of
R({\mathcal{C}})
will be clear from the context.
The rate of an unbounded packing (as per
Equation

18
) is also called the
normalized logarithmic density
in the literature.
It measures the rate (w.r.t.
Equation

16
) per unit volume.

Note that the condition given by
Equations

15
and
17
is equivalent to that for any
({\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L})\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}
,

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{L}{\mathcal{B}}^{n}({\underline{x}}_{i},\sqrt{nN})=\emptyset.

(19)

Definition 3

(Chebyshev radius of a list)
.

Let
{\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L}
be
L
points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then the
squared Chebyshev radius

\rad^{2}({\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L})
of
{\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L}
is defined as the (squared) radius of the smallest ball containing
{\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rad^{2}({\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\max_{i\in[L]}\left\|{\underline{x}}_{i}-{\underline{y}}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(20)

Remark 3
.

One should note that for an
L
-list
{\mathcal{L}}
of points, the smallest ball containing
{\mathcal{L}}
is not necessarily the same as the
circumscribed ball
, i.e., the ball such that all points in
{\mathcal{L}}
live on the boundary of the ball.
The circumscribed ball of the polytope
\conv\left\{{\mathcal{L}}\right\}
spanned by the points in
{\mathcal{L}}
may not exist.
If it does exist, it is not necessarily the smallest one containing
{\mathcal{L}}
.
However, whenever it exists, the smallest ball containing
{\mathcal{L}}
must be the circumscribed ball of a certain
subset
of
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Definition 4

(Chebyshev radius of a code)
.

Given a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of rate
R
, the
squared
(L-1)
-list-decoding radius
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}}).

(21)

Note that
(L-1)
-list-decodability defined by
Equation

15
or
Equation

19
is equivalent to
\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>nN
.
We also define the
(P,N,L-1)
-list-decoding capacity
(a.k.a.
(P,N,L-1)
-multiple packing density
)

C_{L-1}(P,N)\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\limsup_{{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{n}(\sqrt{nP})\colon\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>nN}R({\mathcal{C}}),

and the
squared
(L-1)
-list-decoding radius
at rate
R
with input constraint
P

\displaystyle\rad^{2}_{L}(P,R)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\limsup_{{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{n}(\sqrt{nP})\colon R({\mathcal{C}})\geq R}\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}),

and their unbounded analogues
(N,L-1)
-list-decoding capacity
(a.k.a.
(N,L-1)
-multiple packing density
)
C_{L-1}(N)
and the
squared
(L-1)
-list-decoding radius

\rad^{2}_{L}(R)
at rate
R
:

\displaystyle C_{L-1}(N)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\limsup_{{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\colon\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>nN}R({\mathcal{C}}),

\displaystyle\rad^{2}_{L}(R)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\limsup_{{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\colon R({\mathcal{C}})\geq R}\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}).

VIII
Lower bounds on list-decoding capacity via error exponents

In this section, we will show the following lower bound on
C_{L-1}(P,N)
.

Theorem 3
.

For any
P,N>0
such that
N\leq\frac{L-1}{L}P
and any
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
, the
(P,N,L-1)
-list-decoding capacity
C_{L-1}(P,N)
is at least

\displaystyle C_{L-1}(P,N)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\frac{(L-1)P}{LN}+\frac{1}{L-1}\ln\frac{P}{L(P-N)}\right].

(22)

Remark 4
.

When
L\to\infty
, the above bound (
Equation

22
) converges to the list-decoding capacity
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{P}{N}
for
L\to\infty
(see
Section

III
).
For
L=2
, it recovers the best known bound
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{P^{2}}{4N(P-N)}
(see, e.g.,
[
ZV22c
]
).
Furthermore, it is tight at
N/P=0
where the optimal density is
\infty
and
N/P=\frac{L-1}{L}
where the optimal density is
0
(see
[
ZV22c
]
for the
Plotkin point
).

To handle the Chebyshev radius, we follow an indirect approach which relates the Chebyshev radius to a quantity called
error exponent
.
To this end, we take a detour by first introducing the notion of error exponent and then presenting bounds on it.
We find it curious that the
(P,N,L-1)
-list-decodability against
worst-case
errors can be related to the error exponent of a
Gaussian
channel that only inflicts
average-case
errors.

VIII-A

Basic definitions regarding list-decoding error exponents

We first introduce maximum likelihood list-decoding and error exponents in the context of transmission over AWGN channels.
Relevant definitions for more general channels can be found in
Section

IX
.

Consider a Gaussian channel
{\underline{\mathbf{y}}}={\underline{\mathbf{x}}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}
where the input
{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}
satisfies
\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{nP}
and
{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(\underline{0},\sigma^{2}I_{n})
is an additive white Gaussian noise with mean zero and variance
\sigma^{2}
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}
be a codebook for the above Gaussian channel, that is,
\left\|{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{nP}
for all
1\leq i\leq M
.

We are interested in the
probability of
(L-1)
-list-decoding error
of
{\mathcal{C}}
under the
maximum likelihood (ML)
(L-1)
-list-decoder
.
Formally, let
\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L-1}
denote the ML
(L-1)
-list-decoder.
Given
{\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
, the ML list-decoder outputs the list of the nearest
L-1
codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\underline{\mathbf{y}}}
.
We say that an
(L-1)
-list-decoding error
occurs if the transmitted codeword
{\underline{x}}_{i}
does not lie within the list
\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}({\underline{x}}_{i}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})
.
Let us define
P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(i,{\mathcal{C}})
to be the conditional probability of a decoding error when the
i
-th codeword is transmitted, i.e., the probability that the decoder outputs a list of codewords that does not contain
{\underline{x}}_{i}
, conditioned on the event that
{\underline{x}}_{i}
was sent:

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(i,{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Pr\left[\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}({\underline{x}}_{i}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\not\ni{\underline{x}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\exists\left\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{i\right\}}{L-1},\;\forall j\in[L-1],\;\left\|{\underline{x}}_{i_{j}}-({\underline{x}}_{i}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\right\|_{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}\right].

Occasionally, we also write
P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{i},{\mathcal{C}})
to denote the same quantity above.
Then, the
average (over codewords) probability of
(L-1)
-list-decoding error
of
{\mathcal{C}}
under
\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}
is defined as

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(i,{\mathcal{C}}).

VIII-B

Connection between list-decoding error exponents and Chebyshev radius

In this subsection, we present a connection between list-decoding error exponents of a code used over an AWGN channel to the Chebyshev radius of the same code. We show that the Chebyshev radius of a code can be bounded by a quantity that depends on the probability of error of the code for transmission over a suitable AWGN channel.

Lemma 4
.

For any code
{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}
, there exists a subcode
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
of size
M^{\prime}\coloneqq|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}|\geq M/2
such that for all
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}{L}
,

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}}),

where

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{L}\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}},{\mathcal{L}}),

and

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Pr\left[\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{L}}}({\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\not\ni{\underline{x}}\right]=\Pr\left[\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\},\;\left\|{\underline{x}}^{\prime}-({\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\right\|_{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}\right].

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that the codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
are listed according to ascending order of
P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(i,{\mathcal{C}})
, that is,

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(1,{\mathcal{C}})\leq P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(2,{\mathcal{C}})\leq\cdots\leq P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(M,{\mathcal{C}}).

By Markov’s inequality (
Lemma

23
), each of the first (at least)
M/2
codewords has probability of error at most
2P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\{{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M/2}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
.
Take any
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}{L}
and any
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}
.

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\},\;\left\|{\underline{x}}^{\prime}-({\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\right\|_{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left[\bigcup_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}}{L-1}}\left\{\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime},\;\left\|{\underline{x}}^{\prime}-({\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\right\|_{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}}).

Therefore

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}}),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L}\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an arbitrary set of
L
(where
L\geq 2
) points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying
(i)
there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
n
such that
\left\|{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{nC}
for all
1\leq i\leq L
;
(ii)
there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
n
such that
\left\|{\underline{x}}_{i}-{\underline{x}}_{j}\right\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{nc}
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq L
.
Then

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right).

(23)

Note that the case where
L-1=1
is trivial which corresponds to unique-decoding.
Indeed, suppose
{\mathcal{L}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2}\right\}
.
Without loss of generality, assume
{\underline{x}}_{1}=\underline{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\underline{x}}_{2}=[a,0,\cdots,0]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for some
a\geq\sqrt{nc}
.
It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{1},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\left\|{\underline{x}}_{2}-({\underline{x}}_{1}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}})\right\|_{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\left\|{\underline{x}}_{2}-{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}<\left\|{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[(a-{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}(1))^{2}<{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}(1)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}(1)>a/2\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{(a/2)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right).

The last equality is by
Lemma

24
.
By symmetry,
P_{{\mathrm{e}},1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{1},{\mathcal{L}})=P_{{\mathrm{e}},1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{2},{\mathcal{L}})
both of which are equal to
P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})
.
Since
\sqrt{\rad^{2}(\left\{{\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2}\right\})}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\underline{x}}_{1}-{\underline{x}}_{2}\right\|_{2}=a/2
, we see that
Theorem

5
holds for
L-1=1
.

We prove the above theorem in two subsequent subsections. The special case of
L-1=2
is easier to handle as it exhibits a simpler geometric structure and admits more explicit calculations. We give a proof of
Theorem

5
for this special case in
Section

VIII-C
.
In fact we will prove a stronger statement:

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},2}^{\mathrm{ML}}(\{{\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2},{\underline{x}}_{3}\})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2},{\underline{x}}_{3})}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right).

We then prove
Theorem

5
in
Section

VIII-D
for general
L-1\geq 2
using the Laplace’s method (
Theorem

27
).

VIII-C

Proof of
Theorem

5
when
L-1=2

VIII-C
1
Voronoi partition and higher-order Voronoi partition

We first introduce the notion of a
Voronoi partition
induced by a point set and its
higher-order
generalization.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a discrete set of points.
The
Voronoi region

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
associated with
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as the region in which any point is closer to
{\underline{x}}
than to any other points in
{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\},\;\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\}.

When the underlying point set
{\mathcal{C}}
is clear from the context, we write
{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}})
for
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
.
Clearly,
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}}^{\prime})=\emptyset
for
{\underline{x}}\neq{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
\bigcup\limits_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
is different from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by a set of zero Lebesgue measure.
The collection of Voronoi regions induced by
{\mathcal{C}}
is called the
Voronoi partition
induced by
{\mathcal{C}}
.
It is not hard to see that for any
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Voronoi region
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
contains exactly one point from
{\mathcal{C}}
, which is
{\underline{x}}
itself.

Every Voronoi region can be written as an intersection of halfspaces.
To compute
{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}})
for any
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, one can draw a hyperplane bisecting and perpendicular to the segment connecting
{\underline{x}}
and
{\underline{x}}^{\prime}
for each
{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}}({\underline{x}})
be the halfspace induced by the hyperplane that contains
{\underline{x}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}}({\underline{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left\langle{\underline{y}},{\underline{x}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\left\|{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}\right\}.

Then
{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}})
is nothing but the intersection of all such halfspaces, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}}({\underline{x}}).

More generally, one can define Voronoi regions associated with
subsets
of points in
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Let
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
.
The
order-
L
Voronoi region

{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})
associated with
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}
is defined as the region such that the set of the nearest
L
points from
{\mathcal{C}}
to any point in the region is
{\mathcal{L}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}},\;\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\max_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\}.

(24)

Again, we will ignore the subscripts if they are clear.
If
{\mathcal{L}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}
is a singleton set,
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},1}(\{{\underline{x}}\})={\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
.
Clearly,
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})=\emptyset
for
{\mathcal{L}}\neq{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}
and
\bigcup\limits_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}}{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{L}})={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(up to a set of measure zero).
The collection of order-
L
Voronoi regions induced by all
L
-subsets of
{\mathcal{C}}
is called the
order-
L
Voronoi partition
induced by
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Computing the order-
L
Voronoi partition of a point set
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is in general not easy for
L>1
.
Even when
n=2
, i.e., all points in
{\mathcal{C}}
are on a plane, the problem is not trivial and the resulting order-
L
Voronoi partition may exhibit significantly different behaviours from the
L=1
case
[
Lee82
, Fig. 2-5]
.

However, if one is given the order-
(L-1)
Voronoi partition of
{\mathcal{C}}
and the (first order) Voronoi partition for all sets
{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
(where
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L-1}
), then the order-
L
Voronoi partition of
{\mathcal{C}}
can be computed in the following way.
For
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}
, to compute
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})
, for each
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}
, compute the following set
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\left({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}\right)}({\underline{x}})
.
Then
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})
is nothing but their unions, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\left({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\left\{{\underline{x}}\right\}\right)}({\underline{x}}).

Let us return to the task of estimating the probability of

-list-decoding error of an

. Given the order-

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}},{\mathcal{L}})=\Pr\left[{\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{L}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\{{\underline{x}}\})\right],

i.e., the probability that

is the furthest point to

among
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Let
{\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2},{\underline{x}}_{2}
be three distinct points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
In the proceeding two subsections, we divide the analysis of

into two cases according to the largest angle of the triangle spanned by

VIII-C
3
Case 1: The largest angle of the triangle spanned by

As shown in

As explained in

The second order Voronoi partition can be computed given the (first order) Voronoi partition.
For example,

:

Again, draw the ball
{\mathcal{B}}^{L-1}({\underline{y}},\left\|{\underline{x}}_{0}-{\underline{y}}\right\|_{2})
(see the third subfigure of
Figure

10
).
It is not hard to see that there is no point from
{\mathcal{L}}
other than
{\underline{x}}_{0}
that is in
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{L}}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}^{L-1}({\underline{y}},\left\|{\underline{x}}_{0}-{\underline{y}}\right\|_{2})
, since by the
\sqrt{nc}
-minimum distance guarantee,
{\mathcal{B}}^{L-1}({\underline{x}}_{0},\sqrt{nc})\cap{\mathcal{L}}=\{{\underline{x}}_{0}\}
.
Therefore,
{\underline{x}}_{0}
is the furthest point in
{\mathcal{L}}
from
{\underline{y}}
, and given
{\underline{y}}
, the ML
(L-1)
-list-decoder will not output
{\underline{x}}_{0}
.
This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Provided
Lemma

6
, we are finally ready to estimate the probability of ML
(L-1)
-list-decoding error (
Equation

25
).
As before, let
r\coloneqq\sqrt{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}
.
We work with polar coordinates.
Let the apex of the cone
{\mathcal{D}}
be the origin
O
.
Parameterize
{\underline{x}}_{0}
as
[-r,{\underline{u}}_{0}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{L-1}
for some
{\underline{u}}_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}
.

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{L}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}}\setminus\{{\underline{x}}_{0}\})\right]

\displaystyle\geq\Pr\left[{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\in{\mathcal{D}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}}}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left\|{\underline{g}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}{\underline{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left\|\rho{\underline{u}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\rho^{L-2}\cdot|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|\cdot\mathds{1}_{-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}}}(\rho{\underline{u}})\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}\mu({\underline{u}})

(30)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\rho^{L-2}\cdot|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|\cdot\mathds{1}_{\rho^{-1}(-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}})}({\underline{u}})\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}\mu({\underline{u}})

(31)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\rho^{L-2}\cdot|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|\cdot\left(\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}}\mathds{1}_{\rho^{-1}(-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}})}({\underline{u}})\mathrm{d}\mu({\underline{u}})\right)\mathrm{d}\rho

(32)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\rho^{L-2}\cdot|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|\cdot\frac{|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}\cap\rho^{-1}(-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}})|}{|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|}\mathrm{d}\rho

(33)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\cdot|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}(\rho)\cap(-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}})|\mathrm{d}\rho.

(34)

In
Equation

30
, we switch to polar coordinates using
Lemma

25
where
\mu(\cdot)
denotes the uniform probability measure on
{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}
.

Equation

31
follows since
\left\|{\underline{u}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=1
for
{\underline{u}}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}
and the inner integral vanishes for any
\rho
such that
\rho=\left\|\rho{\underline{u}}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|-{\underline{x}}_{0}\right\|_{2}=r
.
In
Equation

32
, we interchange the inner and outer integrations.

Equation

33
follows by noting that the inner integral is nothing but the normalized surface area of the cap obtained by taking the intersection of
{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}
and the (shifted and rescaled) cone
\rho^{-1}(-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}})
.

Equation

34
follows from the fact that the
(L-2)
-dimensional volume scales like
|\rho{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|=\rho^{L-2}|{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}|
.

To proceed, we bound the volume of the cap by first computing its radius
s=s(\rho,\alpha,r)
as a function of
\rho
(and
\alpha,r
as well).
The geometry is depicted in
Figure

11
.

Fig. 11
:
In the above figure,
-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}}
is a cone of angular radius
\alpha
, the apex of which is
r
away from the origin
O
. To integrate using polar coordinates, for each radius
\rho\geq r
, we need to compute the surface measure of the cap obtained by taking the intersection of
{\mathcal{S}}^{L-2}(\rho)
and
-{\underline{x}}_{0}+{\mathcal{D}}
. It suffices to compute the radius
s
of the cap. This can be done by examining the elementary geometry depicted above.

By Pythagorean theorem, it is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{s}{\tan\alpha}+r\right)^{2}+s^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}.

Solving
s
, we get

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=s(\rho,\alpha,r)=\frac{(\tan\alpha)\left(\sqrt{(1+\tan^{2}\alpha)\rho^{2}-(\tan^{2}\alpha)r^{2}}-r\right)}{\tan^{2}\alpha+1}=(\sin\alpha)\left(\sqrt{\rho^{2}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha}-r\cos\alpha\right)

(35)

Since the volume of an
(L-2)
-dimensional cap is lower bounded by that of an
(L-2)
-dimensional ball of the same radius, continuing with
Equation

34
, we have

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\cdot|{\mathcal{B}}^{L-2}(s(\rho,\alpha,r))|\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-(L-1)/2}V_{L-2}(\sin^{L-2}\alpha)\int_{r}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\rho^{2}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha}-r\cos\alpha\right)^{L-2}\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-(L-1)/2}V_{L-2}(\sin^{L-2}\alpha)\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\left(\sqrt{r^{2}t^{2}-r^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha}-r\cos\alpha\right)^{L-2}r\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-(L-1)/2}V_{L-2}(\sin^{L-2}\alpha)r^{L-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\left(\sqrt{t^{2}-\sin^{2}\alpha}-\cos\alpha\right)^{L-2}\mathrm{d}t.

(36)

Define the following two functions

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}},\quad g(t)\coloneqq\left(\sqrt{t^{2}-\sin^{2}\alpha}-\cos\alpha\right)^{L-2}.

We note that
f^{\prime}(t)=t/\sigma^{2}
and in the domain
[1,\infty)
,
f(t)
attains its unique minimum
\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}
at
t^{*}=1
.
Furthermore,
g(t^{*})=g^{(1)}(t^{*})=g^{(2)}(t^{*})=\cdots=g^{(L-3)}(t^{*})=0
where
g^{(k)}(t)
denotes the
k
-th derivative of
g(t)
.
However, the
(L-2)
-st derivative of
g(t)
does not vanish at
t^{*}
and in fact one can check that it equals

\displaystyle g^{(L-2)}(t^{*})

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{(L-2)!t^{L-2}}{(t^{2}-\sin^{2}\alpha)^{(L-2)/2}}\right|_{t=t^{*}}=\frac{(L-2)!}{\cos^{L-2}\alpha}.

Now, we apply Laplace’s method (
Theorem

27
) to compute the integral above (
Equation

36
).
As
n\to\infty
, we have
r=\Theta(\sqrt{n})\to\infty
and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\left(\sqrt{t^{2}-\sin^{2}\alpha}-\cos\alpha\right)^{L-2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-r^{2}f(t)\right)g(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\exp\left(-r^{2}f(t^{*})\right)\cdot\frac{g^{(L-2)}(t^{*})}{(r^{2}f^{(1)}(t^{*}))^{L-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma^{2(L-1)}(L-2)!}{(\cos^{L-2}\alpha)r^{2(L-1)}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

Putting this back to
Equation

36
, we have, as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\pi}\right)^{(L-1)/2}V_{L-2}(\tan^{L-2}\alpha)\frac{(L-2)!}{r^{L-1}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

Since
\sigma,L,\alpha
are all constants independent of
n
, we have shown

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{L}P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\underline{x}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}})=\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right),

as desired.

VIII-E

Putting things together

Lemmas

4
and
5
imply the following corollary which gives a lower bound on the error probability of a code
{\mathcal{C}}
in terms of the Chebyshev radius.

Corollary 7
.

Let
P,\sigma>0
and
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
For any code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}^{n}(\sqrt{nP})
of size
M
and minimum pairwise distance at least
\sqrt{nc}
for some constant
c>0
, there exists a subcode
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
of size at least
M^{\prime}\geq M/2
such that for all
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}{L}
,

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right).

On the other hand, one can construct codes whose error probability is small.
By carefully analyzing a random code (with expurgation) in
Section

IX-G
, we have the following upper bound on the
(L-1)
-list-decoding error probability under ML list-decoder.
(Many other related results on list-decoding error exponents will also be proved in
Section

IX-G
.)

Theorem 8
.

Let
P,\sigma>0
and
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
There exist codes
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}(\sqrt{nP})
of rate
R
such that when used over an AWGN channel with input constraint
P
and noise variance
\sigma^{2}
, it attains the following expurgated error exponent under ML
(L-1)
-list-decoding.

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-nE_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)+o(n)\right),

where

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\min_{s\geq 0,\rho\geq 1}R(L-1)\rho-\rho\left[sLP+\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-2sP)+\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(1-2sP+\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}L\rho}\right)\right].

(37)

Remark 5
.

The above theorem follows from the intermediate result given by
Equation

101
in
Section

IX-G
.
We did not take the eventual explicit expression (without the minimization) in
Theorem

17
since for the purpose of this section, the minimization in
Equation

37
can be solved in a simpler manner when combined with
Corollary

7
.

Corollary

7
requires the minimum distance of the code to be at least
\sqrt{nc}
for an arbitrarily small constant
c>0
.
This turns out to be a mild condition and can be met without sacrificing the rate by taking a sufficiently small
c>0
.
Indeed, it was shown by Shannon
[
Sha59
]
(see also Eqn. (45) in
[
SEW13
]
) that even under unique-decoding, no rate loss is incurred if the code is expurgated so that the minimum distance is at least
\sqrt{nc}
for any
0\leq c\leq c(R)
where
c(R)\coloneqq\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{1-e^{-2R}}}
.
Therefore,
Theorem

8
continues to hold even under the
\sqrt{nc}
-minimum distance condition for any
0\leq c\leq c(R)
.

Now, combining
Corollary

7
and
Theorem

8
, we get a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}(\sqrt{nP})
of size
M=e^{nR}
which contains a subcode
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
of size at least
M/2
satisfying: for every
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}{L}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right)

\displaystyle\leq P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\exp\left(-nE_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)+o(n)\right).

(38)

For the subcode
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
to be
(P,N,L-1)
-list-decodable, we have
\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\min\limits_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})>nN
.
Therefore, by
Equation

38
,

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\sigma^{2}}\geq E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)-o(1).

(39)

We then ignore the
o(1)
factor and optimize out the ancillary parameters
s
and
\rho
to get an explicit bound on
R
in terms of
P,N,L
.
To this end, let

\displaystyle F(s,\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq R(L-1)\rho-\rho\left[sLP+\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-2sP)+\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(1-2sP+\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}L\rho}\right)\right].

(40)

The critical point
s
and
\rho
in the minimization of
E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)
satisfies:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F(s,\rho)

\displaystyle=-P\rho\left(L-\frac{1}{1-2Ps}-\frac{L-1}{1-2Ps+P/(\sigma^{2}L\rho)}\right)=0,

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}F(s,\rho)

\displaystyle=R(L-1)-sLP-\frac{1}{2}\ln(1-2Ps)-\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(1-2Ps+\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}L\rho}\right)-\frac{(L-1)P}{2(P+L(1-2Ps)\rho\sigma^{2})}=0.

(42)

From
Equation

41
, we have

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{(L-1)-2LPs}{2L^{2}s(1-2Ps)\sigma^{2}}.

(43)

Substitute
\rho
into
Equation

42
, we have

\displaystyle-\ln(1-2Ps)+(L-1)\left[2R-\ln\frac{(L-1)(1-2Ps)}{L(1-2Ps)-1}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(44)

Solving
R
from
Equation

44
, we get

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\frac{(L-1)(1-2Ps)}{L(1-2Ps)-1}+\frac{1}{L-1}\ln(1-2Ps)\right].

(45)

Note that for
Equation

45
to be valid, we need one additional constraint on
s
, i.e.,
s<\frac{1-1/L}{2P}
which implies
L(1-2Ps)-1>0
.
Now, putting the expressions of the critical
\rho
(
Equation

43
) and the critical
R
(
Equation

45
) into
F(s,\rho)
(
Equation

40
), we have

\displaystyle F(s,\rho)

\displaystyle=-\frac{P(L(1-2Ps)-1)}{2L\sigma^{2}(1-2Ps)}.

(46)

Substitute
Equation

46
back to the relation between
N
and
E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)
(
Equation

39
), we have

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\geq\frac{P(L(1-2Ps)-1)}{L(1-2Ps)}.

(47)

Note that there is no
\sigma^{2}
in the above relation as it is cancelled out.
Since the RHS increases as
s
decreases, to maximize the list-decoding radius
N
, we need to take the minimum
s
.
Therefore, we take
s
that saturates
Equation

47
:

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{(L-1)P-LN}{2L(P-N)P}.

(48)

Finally, putting
s
(
Equation

48
) to the expression of
R
(
Equation

45
), we get the desired bound

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\frac{(L-1)P}{LN}+\frac{1}{L-1}\ln\frac{P}{L(P-N)}\right].

(49)

As a sanity check, the critical value
s
given by
Equation

48
is indeed nonnegative since
N
is less than the Plotkin point
\frac{L-1}{L}P
.
Also, it is not hard to check that
s<\frac{1-1/L}{2P}
.
Putting the critical value of
s
(
Equation

48
) into the expression of
\rho
(
Equation

43
), we get

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{N(P-N)}{(L(P-N)-P)\sigma^{2}}.

We note that
\rho
is nonnegative for the same reason.
Moreover, since
\sigma^{2}
does not show up in the final bound on
R
, one can take a sufficiently small
\sigma^{2}
to make
\rho\geq 1
.
In particular, it suffices to take
0<\sigma\leq\sqrt{\frac{N(P-N)}{L(P-N)-P}}
.
Finally, to double check, we note that the expurgated exponent given by
Equation

37
is achievable if
R\leq R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})
where
R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})
is defined by
Equation

81
.
Since
R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})
is increasing as
\mathsf{snr}=P/\sigma^{2}
increases, that is, as
\sigma^{2}
decreases, the condition
R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\geq R
can be satisfied if we take
\sigma^{2}
to be sufficiently small so that
R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})
becomes larger than
Equation

49
.
The exact threshold is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{snr}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{P(L(P-N)-P)}{N(P-N)},

or

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{N(P-N)}{L(P-N)-P}},

(50)

which is the same as what we obtained before.

At a first glance, it may appear that the rate in
Equation

49
is achieved by any
\sigma
satisfying
Equation

50
above. It turns out that this is not true. The reason why
\sigma^{2}
does not appear in the final expression is because we chose
\rho
to maximize
R
. In this process,
\sigma^{2}

was conveniently canceled out. However, a numerical evaluation of
2\sigma^{2}E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}
reveals that this is in fact decreasing in
\sigma^{2}
, and the maximum is in fact achieved by taking
\sigma^{2}\to 0
.

VIII-F

Connections to
[
Bli99
]

The paper
[
Bli99
]
originally tried to build the connection between list-decoding radius and error exponent (
Equation

23
) and used it to obtain the same bound (
Equation

22
) as ours.
However, there were some gaps in the proof. The proof presented in the current paper uses the same high-level idea as that presented in
[
Bli99
]
, but we deviate in our approach towards characterizing the order-
(L-1)
Voronoi regions.

To the best of our understanding, the main idea in
[
Bli99
]
is to lower bound a higher-order Voronoi region (which arises as the list-decoding error region) by a (first order) Voronoi region whose Gaussian measure is then estimated.
Therefore,
[
Bli99
]
takes a different perspective than ours on a higher-order Voronoi region.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L-1}
.
In
[
Bli99
]
, it was claimed that the order-
(L-1)
Voronoi region
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})
associated with
{\mathcal{L}}
can be written as
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})=\bigcap\limits_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}{\mathcal{U}}({\underline{x}})
for a collection of
{\mathcal{U}}({\underline{x}})
each associated with a point
{\underline{x}}
.
It was then claimed that
{\mathcal{U}}({\underline{x}})={\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
, the RHS of which is the (first order) Voronoi region associated with
{\underline{x}}
.
However, it is not clear why this should be the case since different
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
’s are disjoint and their intersection is always empty.
On the other hand,
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})
is never empty.
In fact,
{\mathcal{U}}({\underline{x}})
also depends on
{\mathcal{L}}
and had better be denoted by
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}({\underline{x}})
.
To see this, note that the original definition of
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})
(
Equation

24
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\forall{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}},\forall{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}},\;\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}}}\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}}}\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\}.

Therefore, one can take

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}({\underline{x}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcap_{{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{L}}}\left\{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}>\left\|{\underline{y}}-{\underline{x}}\right\|_{2}\right\},

and it holds that
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})=\bigcap\limits_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}}{\mathcal{U}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}({\underline{x}})
.

Secondly, it was also claimed that
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{C}},L-1}({\mathcal{L}})\subset{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{C}}({\underline{x}})
for any
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{L}}
.
This seems to be inconsistent with the geometry even in the case of
L=3
.
Indeed, in
Figures

6
and
8
, neither
{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2})
nor
{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}}_{1})
is a subset of each other.

It is then claimed that that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[{\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\notin{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{L}}\cup\{{\underline{x}}\},L-1}({\underline{x}})\right]\geq\Pr\left[{\underline{x}}+{\underline{\mathbf{g}}}\notin{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathcal{L}}\cup\{{\underline{x}}\}}({\underline{x}})\right]\doteq\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

(51)

However, if we consider the example in
Figure

6
, there seems to be an issue with the above.
From the geometry therein, if we take
{\underline{x}}={\underline{x}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{L}}=\{{\underline{x}}_{2},{\underline{x}}_{3}\}
, the second probability in
Equation

51
should be larger than the RHS since the distance from
{\underline{x}}_{1}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus{\mathcal{V}}({\underline{x}}_{1})
is strictly less than
\sqrt{\rad^{2}({\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2},{\underline{x}}_{3})}
.
Moreover, the reason for
[
Bli99
]
to look at this probability is solely a result of the preceding arguments.
We instead study
Equation

25
in which is really the higher-order Voronoi region.

Finally,
[
Bli99
]
takes
\sigma\to 0
in order to obtain
Equation

23
.
In our alternate approach, this step seems to be avoided since the
\sigma^{2}
term conveniently cancels out. However, as pointed out earlier, it so happens that
2\sigma^{2}nE_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}
is decreasing in
\sigma^{2}
in the parameter regime of interest although explicit maximization is bypassed because we chose the
\rho
to maximize
R
.

The fundamental difference between
[
Bli99
]
and the results presented above is in handling the higher-order Voronoi region. In
[
Bli99
]
, an attempt is made to write the higher-order Voronoi region as the intersection of several conventional Voronoi regions.
To the best of our understanding, there is no simple relation (even inclusions) between the conventional Voronoi partition and the higher-order Voronoi partition.

VIII-G

Unbounded packings

We now adapt the techniques developed above for
unbounded
packings.
The two key ingredients are:
(i)
a lower bound on the list-decoding error probability in terms of the Chebyshev radius;
(ii)
an upper bound on the list-decoding error probability.
For
(ii)
, we have bounds in
Theorem

21
on the list-decoding error exponent of AWGN channels
without
input constraints. Unfortunately,
(i)
which was proved for finite codebooks cannot directly be generalized to the the setting of infinite codebooks. While
Theorem

5
is valid for arbitrary countable codebooks,
Lemma

4
is true only for finite codebooks. One approach is to derive list decoding error exponents for infinite constellations under maximum probability of error.

An easier approach is to consider a
finite
codebook
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
of sufficiently large size but restricted to lie within
[-a/2,a/2]^{n}
for a sufficiently large
a
. We construct an infinite constellation by tiling the codebook

{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{a}+a(1+o(1)){\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

We then lower bound the Chebyshev radius of this infinite constellation
{\mathcal{C}}
with the list decoding error exponent of
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
under maximum probability of error.

From infinite constellations to finite codebooks and back

Consider any infinite constellation
{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}
of rate
R
. Recall that

R=\limsup_{a\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}\cap[-a/2,a/2]^{n}|}{a^{n}}.

Fix
a=n^{2}
. Then, there exists
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
|({\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}+{\underline{x}})\cap[-a/2,a/2]^{n}|\geq a^{n}e^{nR}
. Let us define the finite codebook

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\coloneq({\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}+{\underline{x}})\cap[-a/2,a/2]^{n},

(52)

and the infinite constellation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\coloneq{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+a(1+n^{-1.4}){\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(53)

The above infinite constellation has rate
R(1-\delta_{n})
where
\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}=0
. Any two distinct shifts
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+{\underline{z}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+{\underline{z}}_{2},
where
{\underline{z}}_{1}\neq{\underline{z}}_{2}\in a(1+n^{-1.4}){\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, are separated by a distance of at least
n^{0.6}
. This immediately implies the following.

Lemma 9
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
be as defined in
Equations

52
and
53
, respectively. If
\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{a})=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
(as per
Definition

4
), then

\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{a})=\inf_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}}).

Proof.

Clearly,

\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{a})=\min_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}_{a}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})\geq\inf_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}}).

Consider any
{\mathcal{L}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
. If
{\mathcal{L}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+{\underline{z}}
for some
{\underline{z}}\in a(1+n^{-1.4}){\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, then
\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})\geq\min\limits_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}_{a}}{L}}\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})=\rad^{2}_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{a})
. If not, then there are at least two points
{\underline{x}}_{1},{\underline{x}}_{2}
in
{\mathcal{L}}
such that
{\underline{x}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+{\underline{z}}_{1}
and
{\underline{x}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}+{\underline{z}}_{2}
where
{\underline{z}}_{2}\neq{\underline{z}}_{1}\in a(1+n^{-1.4}){\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. But this implies that
\|{\underline{x}}_{1}-{\underline{x}}_{2}\|\geq n^{0.6}
and
\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})\geq n^{0.6}/2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and
R=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}\alpha^{2}}
.
Or equivalently,
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2R}}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}}}
.
In
Theorem

21
, we prove lower bounds on the achievable expurgated list decoding error exponents of infinite constellations. This is obtained by choosing the codebook to be a Matérn point process derived from a Poisson point process. This means that the average probability of error is upper bounded by
\exp\left(-nE_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(\alpha)+o(n)\right)
.

Let us take
{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}
to be the Matérn point process above,
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}={\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}\cap[-a/2,a/2]^{n}
for
a=n^{2}
. Using standard tail bounds for PPPs,

\Pr[|{\mathcal{C}}_{a}|<a^{n}(e^{nR}-n^{3})]\leq\exp(-\Theta(n^{2})),

or

\Pr[|{\mathcal{C}}_{a}|<a^{n}e^{nR(1-o(1))}]\leq\exp(-\Theta(n^{2})).

Therefore, with probability
1-e^{-\Theta(n^{2})}
, the rate of
{\mathcal{C}}
is

\displaystyle R({\mathcal{C}})\geq\frac{a^{n}e^{nR(1-o(1))}}{a^{n}(1+n^{-1.4})^{n}}=e^{nR(1-o(1))}.

(54)

Combining
Equation

54
above with
Lemma

9
, we get that for every
{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L}
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{nN}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\rad^{2}({\mathcal{L}})}{2\sigma^{2}}-o(n)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-nE_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(\alpha)+o(n)\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\geq 2\sigma^{2}E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(\alpha)=2\sigma^{2}E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2R}}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}}}\right).

(55)

It can be verified that the RHS as a function of
\sigma
is maximized at

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\exp\left(-\frac{L}{L-1}\ln L-\ln(2\pi e)-2R\right)},

(56)

which corresponds to

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-2R}}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}}}=\sqrt{L^{\frac{L}{L-1}}}\in\left[\sqrt{L},\sqrt{2L}\right].

Substituting the critical
\sigma
(
Equation

56
) into
Equation

55
, we get the following inequality relating
N
to
R
:

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\geq 2\exp\left(-\frac{L}{L-1}\ln L-\ln(2\pi e)-2R\right)\cdot E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}\left(\sqrt{L^{\frac{L}{L-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{L}{L-1}\ln L-\ln(2\pi e)-2R\right)\cdot\frac{L-1}{2}

\displaystyle=(L-1)\exp\left(-\left[\frac{\ln L}{L-1}+\ln(2\pi eL)+2R\right]\right).

Solving
R
, we get the following lower bound on the
(N,L-1)
-list-decoding capacity:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{L-1}{2\pi eNL}-\frac{\ln L}{2(L-1)},

We summarize our finding in the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

Let
N>0
and
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
The
(N,L-1)
-list-decoding capacity
C_{L-1}(N)
is at least

\displaystyle C_{L-1}(N)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{L-1}{2\pi eNL}-\frac{\ln L}{2(L-1)}.

VIII-H

Remark on the
\sigma^{2}
that maximizes the Chebyshev radius

To prove
Theorems

3
and
10
for the bounded and unbounded cases, respectively, we combine
Theorem

5
with bounds on error exponents.
This combination then gives rise to an inequality relating
N
to
R
.
See
Equations

39
and
55
for the bounded and unbounded cases.
In
Equation

39
, the variance
\sigma^{2}
of the Gaussian noise happens to cancel on both sides.
To maximize
R
, one then needs to take the largest possible error exponent which occurs in the expurgated regime
R\leq R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}
(the latter quantity is defined in
Equation

81
).
However, in
Equation

55
, the Gaussian variance
\sigma^{2}
does not cancel and one should optimize it out.
It turns out that the optimal
\sigma^{2}
does
not
lie in the expurgated regime.
Instead, one should use the error exponent in the “straight line” regime

(under the parameterization of

).
Unfortunately we do not have intuition of this phenomenon.

IX
List-decoding error exponents

IX-A

DMCs with input constraints

Consider a
discrete memoryless channel (DMC)

W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{Y}}|{\mathcal{X}})
with discrete input alphabet
{\mathcal{X}}
and discrete output alphabet
{\mathcal{Y}}
.
The probability of the reciever seeing
{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}
at the output of the channel when
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
is sent by the transmitter is equal to

\displaystyle\Pr\left[{\underline{\mathbf{y}}}={\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}={\underline{x}}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}({\underline{y}}(i)|{\underline{x}}(i)),

for every
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
and
{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}
. We also impose input constraints at the transmitter.
This is specified by a set
{\mathcal{P}}\subset\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
.
The constraints require that the empirical distribution of any codeword
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
sent by the transmitter to lie within
{\mathcal{P}}
.
Specifically, for
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
, let
\tau_{\underline{x}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
denote its
empirical distribution
(a.k.a.
histogram
or
type
) defined as

\displaystyle\tau_{\underline{x}}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{1}{\left\{{\underline{x}}(i)=x\right\}},

for any
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
.
Clearly,
(\tau_{\underline{x}}(x):x\in{\mathcal{X}})
is a valid probability mass function on
{\mathcal{X}}
.
An input sequence
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
is said to satisfy the constraints
{\mathcal{P}}
if
\tau_{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

Recall that the capacity of a DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
with input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
under unique decoding is
[
Sha48
]

\displaystyle C(W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}},{\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle=\max_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}}I({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{y}}),

where the mutual information

\displaystyle I({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{y}})\coloneqq\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}}(x,y)\log\frac{P_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}}(x,y)}{P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)P_{\mathbf{y}}(y)}

is evaluated w.r.t. the joint distribution
P_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}}\coloneqq P_{\mathbf{x}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
whose marginals are denoted by
P_{\mathbf{x}}
and
P_{\mathbf{y}}
.

List-decoding for DMCs

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{{\underline{x}}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
be a code satisfying the input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}
and equipped with an
(L-1)
-list-decoder
\dec_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}\colon{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}\to\binom{{\mathcal{C}}}{L-1}
.
The rate
R({\mathcal{C}})
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as
R({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\log|{\mathcal{C}}|
.

We are interested in deriving upper bounds on the average probability of error when the code
{\mathcal{C}}
and decoder
\dec_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}
are used for the DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
. The average probability of error is defined as

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(i,{\mathcal{C}}),

where

\displaystyle P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(i,{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathop{\Pr}_{{\underline{\mathbf{y}}}\sim\prod_{j=1}^{n}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}={\underline{x}}_{i}(j)}}\left[\dec_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}({\underline{\mathbf{y}}})\not\ni{\underline{x}}_{i}\right].

Besides being of independent interest, results for this problem are used in
Section

VIII
to obtain bounds on the list-decoding capacity against
worst-case
errors.

We use fairly standard techniques by following Gallager’s approach
[
Gal65
]
,
[
Gal68
, Theorem 7.4.4]
.
For a DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
with input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}\subset\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
,
we construct a random code
{\mathcal{C}}
of rate
R<C(W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}},{\mathcal{P}})
and analyze its average probability of error
P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}({\mathcal{C}})
under the ML
(L-1)
-list-decoder.
Results obtained using this approach can be generalized to memoryless channels with
continuous
alphabets, e.g., additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels with power constraints.

IX-B

Random coding exponent

Theorem 11
.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
For any DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{Y}}|{\mathcal{X}})
, there exists a sequence of codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
of increasing blocklengths, each of rate at least
R
and satisfying

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq E_{\mathrm{r},L-1}(R),

where

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{r},L-1}(R)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\max_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}})}\max_{0\leq\rho\leq 1}\left\{-(L-1)\rho R+E_{0}((L-1)\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right\},

and

\displaystyle E_{0}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\log\left[\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x)W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}\right)^{1+\rho}\right].

Proof.

We use the well-known approach of
[
Gal68
]
. Although these results are fairly standard, we give the proof for completeness. This will also help in generalizing the results to the input-constrained case, as well as for channels with continuous alphabet.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{2^{nR}\times n}
where every component of each codeword are drawn i.i.d. according to some
P_{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
.
Let
M\coloneqq 2^{nR}
.
We use the ML
(L-1)
-list-decoder
\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}\colon{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}\to\binom{[M]}{L-1}
.
That is, receiving
{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}
, the decoder outputs a list
\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]}{L}
such that for any other
m^{\prime}\in[M]\setminus\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}
,

\displaystyle W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m^{\prime}})\leq\min\left\{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m_{1}}),\cdots,W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m_{L-1}})\right\}.

An error occurs if
{\underline{x}}_{m}
was transmitted but
m\notin\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L_{1}}\right\}
.
For every
\rho>0
and
s>0
, the error indicator function can be bounded as follows

\displaystyle\mathds{1}{\left\{\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}({\underline{\mathbf{y}}})\not\ni m\right\}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathds{1}{\left\{\exists{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1},\forall i\in{\mathcal{L}},W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})>W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})\right\}}\right)^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\left(\frac{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})}{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})}\right)^{s}>1^{s}\right\}}\right)^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}\left(\frac{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})}{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})}\right)^{s}\right)^{\rho}.

(57)

Equation

57
follows from
\mathds{1}{\left\{a>1\right\}}<a,

for any
a>0
.

For any message
m
, the probability that
m
is incorrectly list-decoded is

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m,{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})\mathds{1}{\left\{\dec^{\mathrm{ML}}_{L-1,{\mathcal{C}}}({\underline{y}})\notowner m\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}\left(\frac{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})}{W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})}\right)^{s}\right)^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m})^{1-s(L-1)\rho}\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})^{s}\right)^{\rho}.

(58)

Averaged over the random generation of
{\mathcal{C}}
, the error probability is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m,{\mathcal{C}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m})^{1-s(L-1)\rho}\right]\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{i})^{s}\right]\right)^{\rho}

(59)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m})^{1-s(L-1)\rho}\right]\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{i})^{s}\right]\right)^{\rho},

(60)

where we have used the fact that each codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}
is independently generated.

Equation

59
is valid when
0<\rho\leq 1
since
(\cdot)^{\rho}
is concave.
We also use linearity of expectation here.
For any
i\in[M]
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{i})^{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{t},

which is independent of
i
.
Therefore,
Equation

60
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{1-s(L-1)\rho}\right)\left(\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\left(\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{s}\right)^{L-1}\right)^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\binom{M-1}{L-1}^{\rho}\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{1-s(L-1)\rho}\right)\left(\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{s}\right)^{(L-1)\rho}.

Letting
s=\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}
and using
\binom{n}{k}\leq n^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\binom{M-1}{L-1}^{\rho}\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}})^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}

\displaystyle\leq M^{(L-1)\rho}\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x_{i})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}({\underline{y}}(i)|x_{i})^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}

\displaystyle=M^{(L-1)\rho}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{y_{i}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x_{i})W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{i}|x_{i})^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}

\displaystyle=M^{(L-1)\rho}\left[\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x)W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\right]^{n}

(61)

\displaystyle=\exp_{2}\left(-n\left[-(L-1)\rho R+E_{0}((L-1)\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right]\right),

where

\displaystyle E_{0}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\log\left[\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x)W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}\right)^{1+\rho}\right].

Optimizing over
0\leq\rho\leq 1
, we get the random coding exponent

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{r},L-1}(R,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq\rho\leq 1}\left\{-(L-1)\rho R+E_{0}((L-1)\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right\}.\qed

IX-C

Expurgated exponent

Theorem 12
.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
For any DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{Y}}|{\mathcal{X}})
, there exists a sequence of codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
of increasing blocklengths, each of rate at least
R
and satisfying

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})\right]

\displaystyle\geq E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R),

where

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\max_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}})}\max_{\rho\geq 1}\left\{-(L-1)\rho R+E_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right\},

and

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\rho\log\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{1/\rho}\right].

Proof.

We begin by considering a random codebook and ML decoder as in the proof of
Theorem

11
. However, we will choose a different
\rho,s>0
and later expurgate the codebook.

Taking
\rho=1
and
s=1/L
in
Equation

58
, we have

\displaystyle P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m_{0},{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\underline{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{m_{0}})^{1/L}\sum_{{\mathcal{L}}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m\right\}}{L-1}}\prod_{i\in{\mathcal{L}}}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{y}}|{\underline{x}}_{i})^{1/L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}\sum_{(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|{\underline{x}}_{m_{i}}(j))^{1/L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}q({\underline{x}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{x}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{m_{L-1}}),

(63)

where in
Equation

63
we define

\displaystyle q({\underline{x}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{x}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{m_{L-1}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{y_{j}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|{\underline{x}}_{m_{i}}(j))^{1/L}.

Now for any
B>0
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m_{0},{\mathcal{C}})>B\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\mathds{1}{\left\{P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m_{0},{\mathcal{C}})>B\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\mathds{1}{\left\{\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}\left|\frac{q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})}{B}\right|>1\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\mathds{1}{\left\{\left(\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}\left|\frac{q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})}{B}\right|^{s}\right)^{1/s}>1\right\}}\right]

(64)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\mathds{1}{\left\{{\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}\frac{q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})^{s}}{B^{s}}}>1\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\textbf{}\right\}}{L-1}}\frac{q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})^{s}}{B^{s}}\right]

(65)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\{m_{1},\cdots,m_{L-1}\right\}\in\binom{[M]\setminus\left\{m_{0}\right\}}{L-1}}B^{-s}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})^{s}\right].

(66)

Equation

64
is valid for any

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{s}
is decreasing in

.

Equation

65
follows from
Section

IX-B
.
We then bound the above expectation.

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[q({\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{0}},{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{1}},\cdots,{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}_{m_{L-1}})^{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{({\underline{x}}_{0},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L-1})\in({\mathcal{X}}^{n})^{L}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}({\underline{x}}_{k}(j))\right]\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{y_{j}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|{\underline{x}}_{i}(j))^{1/L}\right)^{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{({\underline{x}}_{0},\cdots,{\underline{x}}_{L-1})\in({\mathcal{X}}^{n})^{L}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}({\underline{x}}_{k}(j))\right)\left(\sum_{y_{j}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|{\underline{x}}_{i}(j))^{1/L}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x_{0,1},\cdots,x_{L-1,1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}\\
\dots\\
(x_{0,n},\cdots,x_{L-1,n})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k,j})\right)\left(\sum_{y_{j}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|x_{i,j})^{1/L}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{(x_{0,j},\cdots,x_{L-1,j})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k,j})\right)\left(\sum_{y_{j}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y_{j}|x_{i,j})^{1/L}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{s}\right]^{n},

which is independent of
m_{0},\cdots,m_{L-1}
.
Then
Equation

66
becomes at most

\displaystyle M^{L-1}B^{-s}\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{s}\right]^{n}.

Choose
B
such that the above quantity equals
1/2
, i.e.,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}M^{-(L-1)}\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{s}\right]^{-n}\right)^{-1/s}

\displaystyle=(2M^{L-1})^{1/s}\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{s}\right]^{n/s}.

Under the above choice of
B
,
we get that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[\left|\left\{m\in[M]:P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(m,{\mathcal{C}})>B\right\}\right|\right]=\sum_{m\in[M]}\mathop{\Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m,{\mathcal{C}})>B\right]=M/2.

Therefore, if we expurgate all codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
with probability of error exceeding
B
, we get a code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
of expected size
M/2
whose codewords all have probability of error at most

P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(m,{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})\leq P_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}^{\mathrm{ML}}(m,{\mathcal{C}})\leq B
. The first inequality follows since the probability of error of each codeword does not increase if there are less competing codewords.
Letting
\rho\coloneqq 1/s\geq 1
and
R=\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{M}{2}
, we get the following upper bound on the error probability

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=(2^{L}(M/2)^{L-1})^{\rho}\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{1/\rho}\right]^{\rho n}

(67)

\displaystyle=2^{L\rho}\exp_{2}\left(-n\left[-(L-1)\rho R+E_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right]\right),

where

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\rho\log\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{1/\rho}\right].

The above bound can be translated to the following lower bound on the error exponent

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(R,P_{\mathbf{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\geq 1}\left\{-(L-1)\rho R+E_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\rho,P_{\mathbf{x}})\right\}.\qed

(68)

IX-D

Input constraints

We now derive bounds on the the achievable error exponents for discrete memoryless channels with input constraints.

Over the input alphabet
{\mathcal{X}}
, we associate a cost function
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
We impose the following constraint that every input sequence/codeword should satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\leq 0
.
We can alternatively write this constraint in terms of
\tau_{\underline{x}}
by observing that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}n\tau_{\underline{x}}(x)f(x).

Therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\leq 0
is equivalent to the input type constraint
\tau_{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}}):\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)f(x)\leq 0\right\}.

For example, the standard
\ell^{2}
norm constraint on any
{\mathcal{X}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
can be obtained by choosing
f(x)=x^{2}-P
, which implies that for every codeword
{\underline{x}}
, we must have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{2}(i)\leq nP
.

The following is our main result, which gives an upper bound on the probability of error.

Theorem 13
.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
Consider any DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{Y}}|{\mathcal{X}})
with input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}\subset\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
for some cost function
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then there exists a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
of rate
R
, satisfying the input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
and

P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\min_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}}\inf_{\delta>0}\min_{s\geq 0,0\leq\rho\leq 1}2^{nR(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\left[\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x)e^{sf(x)}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\right]^{n},

(69)

where

\displaystyle Z\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\otimes n}({\underline{x}})\mathds{1}{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\in[-\delta,0]\right\}}.

(70)

For the same channel, there also exists a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
of rate
R
, satisfying the input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
and

P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\min_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}}\inf_{\delta>0}\min_{s\geq 0,\rho\geq 1}2^{L\rho}2^{nR(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{L\rho}\\
\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(e^{s\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}f(x_{k})}\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{1/\rho}\right]^{\rho n},

(71)

where
Z
is defined in the same way as in
Equation

70
.

Proof.

For the DMC
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}
with input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
,
we sample codewords from
Q_{{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}}\in\Delta({\mathcal{X}}^{n})
which is obtained by truncating the input distribution
P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\otimes n}
so that it satisfies the power constraint.
Specifically, for some
\delta>0
, for any
{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
,

\displaystyle Q_{{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}}({\underline{x}})\coloneqq

\displaystyle Z^{-1}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\otimes n}({\underline{x}})\mathds{1}{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\in[-\delta,0]\right\}},

where

\displaystyle Z\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\underline{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\otimes n}({\underline{x}})\mathds{1}{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\in[-\delta,0]\right\}}

is a normalizing constant.
Though
Q_{{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}}
is not a product distribution, we will upper bound it pointwise by a product distribution.
Note that the indicator function of the power constraint can be bounded as follows,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}{\left\{-\delta\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\leq 0\right\}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}{\left\{0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))+\delta\leq\delta\right\}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}{\left\{e^{s\cdot 0}\leq e^{s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))+\delta\right)}\leq e^{s\delta}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{1}{\left\{e^{s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))+\delta\right)}\geq 1\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))+\delta\right)},

(72)

for any
s\geq 0
.

Equation

72
is by
Section

IX-B
.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle Q_{{\underline{\mathbf{x}}}}({\underline{x}})

\displaystyle\leq Z^{-1}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\otimes n}({\underline{x}})e^{s\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))+\delta\right)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(P_{\mathbf{x}}({\underline{x}}(i))e^{sf({\underline{x}}(i))}e^{s\delta/n}Z^{-1/n}\right).

Replacing
P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)
with
{P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)e^{sf(x)}e^{s\delta/n}}Z^{-1/n}
in
Equation

61
, we have a random coding bound with input constraints:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left[P^{\mathrm{ML}}_{{\mathrm{e}},L-1}(m,{\mathcal{C}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq M^{(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\left[\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{{\mathbf{x}}}(x)e^{sf(x)}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\right]^{n},

(73)

for
s\geq 0
and
0\leq\rho\leq 1
.
A similar substitution for
Equation

67
yields an expurgated bound with input constraints:

\displaystyle 2^{L\rho}M^{(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{L\rho}\left[\sum_{(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\in{\mathcal{X}}^{L}}\left(e^{s\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}f(x_{k})}\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\sum_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\right)^{1/\rho}\right]^{\rho n},

(74)

for
s\geq 0
and
\rho\geq 1
.
∎

IX-E

It is easy to extend the same ideas to continuous alphabets such as

Theorem 14
.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
Consider any memoryless channel
W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Delta({\mathbb{R}}|{\mathbb{R}})
over the reals with input constraints

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in\Delta({\mathbb{R}}):\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)f(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq 0\right\}\subset\Delta({\mathbb{R}})

.
Then there exists a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of rate
R
, satisfying the input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
and

P_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{avg},L-1}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\min_{P_{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{f}}\inf_{\delta>0}\min_{s\geq 0,0\leq\rho\leq 1}e^{nR(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)e^{sf(x)}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\mathrm{d}y\right]^{n},

where

\displaystyle Z\coloneqq

{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of rate
R
, satisfying the input constraints
{\mathcal{P}}_{f}
and

(77)

where
Z
is defined in the same way as in
Equation

76
.

Equations

73
and
74
can be generalized to channels over the reals in a straightforward manner:

\displaystyle M^{(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)e^{sf(x)}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)^{\frac{1}{1+(L-1)\rho}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1+(L-1)\rho}\mathrm{d}y\right]^{n},

where

\displaystyle 2^{L\rho}M^{(L-1)\rho}\left(\frac{e^{s\delta}}{Z}\right)^{L\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{L}}\left(e^{s\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}f(x_{k})}\prod_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{\mathbf{x}}(x_{k})\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x_{i})^{1/L}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\rho}\mathrm{d}(x_{0},\cdots,x_{L-1})\right]^{\rho n},

(79)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}P_{\mathbf{x}}^{{\otimes n}}({\underline{x}})\mathds{1}{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\underline{x}}(i))\in[-\delta,0]\right\}}\mathrm{d}{\underline{x}}.

IX-F

Random coding exponent for AWGN channels with input constraints

Theorem

14
gives non-explicit upper bounds on the probability of error.
In this section, we derive explicit lower bounds on
Equation

76
in the case of AWGN channels with input constraint
P
and noise variance
\sigma^{2}
under
(L-1)
-list-decoding.
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 15
.

Let
P,\sigma>0
and
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
.
There exist codes of rate
R
for the AWGN channel with input constraint
P
and noise variance
\sigma^{2}
such that the rate satisfies
0\leq R\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+P/\sigma^{2})
and the exponent
E_{L-1}(R,P/\sigma^{2})
of the probability of error (normalized by
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\ln(\cdot)
) under
(L-1)
-list-decoding is bounded as follows.

Let
\mathsf{snr}\coloneqq P/\sigma^{2}
and

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{x},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln\frac{\sqrt{L^{2}+\mathsf{snr}^{2}-2\mathsf{snr}(L-2)}+L+\mathsf{snr}}{2L}+\frac{1}{L-1}\ln\frac{\sqrt{L^{2}+\mathsf{snr}^{2}-2\mathsf{snr}(L-2)}+L-\mathsf{snr}}{2L}\right),

(81)

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\mathsf{snr}}{2L}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{2(L-2)}{L^{2}}\mathsf{snr}+\frac{\mathsf{snr}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\right).

(82)

1.

If

R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})\leq R\leq\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\mathsf{snr})
, then

\displaystyle E_{L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[e^{2R}-\frac{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{4e^{2R}}{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathsf{snr}}{4e^{2R}}\left(e^{2R}+1-(e^{2R}-1)\sqrt{1+\frac{4e^{2R}}{\mathsf{snr}(e^{2R}-1)}}\right).

(83)

2.

If

0\leq R\leq R_{\mathrm{crit},L-1}(\mathsf{snr})
,
then

\displaystyle E_{L-1}(R,\mathsf{snr})

\displaystyle\geq-R(L-1)+\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(L+\mathsf{snr}+\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(L-\mathsf{snr}+\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\left(L+\mathsf{snr}-\sqrt{(L-\mathsf{snr})^{2}+4\mathsf{snr}}\right)-\frac{L}{2}\ln(2L).

(84)

For an AWGN channel with input constraint
P
and noise variance
\sigma^{2}
,
the channel transition kernel is given by

\displaystyle W_{{\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}}(y|x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},

(85)

and the cost function is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=x^{2}-P.

(86)

Let
P_{\mathbf{x}}
be the Gaussian density with variance
P
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{x}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi P}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2P}}.

(87)

For a constant
\delta>0
, we claim that the factor
(e^{s\delta}/Z)^{1+(L-1)\rho}
that appears in
Equation

78
scales like
\poly(n)
for asymptotically large
n
and therefore does not effectively contribute to the exponent.
Indeed, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 16
.

Let
P,\sigma,\delta>0
be constants.
Let
P_{\mathbf{x}}
be the Gaussian density with variance
P
as defined in
Equation

Proof.

The proof follows from the central limit theorem.

Expurgated exponent

The bound on error exponent proved in the last section (

) can be improved using the expurgation technique when the rate is sufficiently low.
In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 21
.

For any
\sigma>0,\alpha\geq 1
and
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 2}
, there exists an unbounded code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of rate
R=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}\alpha^{2}}

\sigma^{2}

(L-1)
-list-decoding error of
{\mathcal{C}}
(normalized by
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\ln(\cdot)

E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(\alpha)
defined as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{ex},L-1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\ln\alpha-\frac{1}{2},&1\leq\alpha\leq\sqrt{L}\\
\frac{L-1}{2}-\frac{L}{2}\ln L+(L-1)\ln\alpha,&\sqrt{L}<\alpha\leq\sqrt{2L}\\
F(\alpha,L),&\alpha>\sqrt{2L}\end{cases},

(125)

\displaystyle F(\alpha,L)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{16}\sqrt{\alpha^{4}+8\alpha^{2}(2L-3)+16}-\frac{L-1}{2}\ln\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{4}+8\alpha^{2}(2L-3)+16}-\alpha^{2}+4\right)

\alpha\geq 1

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

(126)

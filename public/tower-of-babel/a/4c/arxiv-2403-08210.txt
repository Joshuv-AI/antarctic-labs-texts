Introduction

Let
G
be a graph. A
path system
of
G
is a set of paths in
G
. Given edges
e,f\in E(G)
and a path system
\mathcal{P}
of
G
, we say that
e
is
separated from

f
by
\mathcal{P}
if there exists a path
P\in\mathcal{P}
such that
e\in P
and
f\notin P
. A path system
\mathcal{P}

weakly separates

G
if for every pair of edges of
G
it separates one of them from the other, and
strongly separates

G
if it separates all the edges of
G
from each other. Let
ssp(G)
(resp.
wsp(G)
) be the minimum size of a path system that separates
G
strongly (resp. weakly), called the
strong
(resp.
weak
)
separation number
of
G
. Clearly,
wsp(G)\leq ssp(G)
for every graph
G
. We are interested in the problem of determining these two quantities when
G
is a complete graph on
n
vertices.

The study of general separating set systems was initiated by Rényi in the 1960s
[
9
]
. Various versions concerning the separation of edges by subgraphs were introduced around the 2000s by computer scientists who were seeking efficient ways to detect faulty links in
networks
[
5
,
6
,
10
,
12
]
.
The problem of determining tight upper bounds for the size of separating path systems of graphs on
n
vertices has become very popular in the combinatorics community during the last decade.

Among the first to consider this problem were Balogh, Csaba, Martin and Pluhár in the strong setting
[
1
]
, and Falgas-Ravry, Kittipassorn, Korándi, Letzter and Narayanan
[
3
]
in the weak setting. Both groups proved a common upper bound of
O(n\log n)
and conjectured one of
O(n)
. Letzter
[
7
]
came very close to the conjectured bound by proving that
ssp(G)=O(n\log^{*}n)
. The conjecture was eventually settled by Bonamy, Botler, Dross, Naia and Skokan
[
2
]
, who proved that
ssp(G)\leq 19n
. They further conjectured that
(1+o(1))n
is optimal.

Restricting the question to the class of complete graphs is an interesting and appropriate direction to follow, as noted e.g. by Letzter in the conclusion of
[
7
]
. This not only because of the reduced complexity but also because of the fact that complete graphs have the highest known lower bound for their weak separation number among all graph classes. It is therefore natural to suspect that they are extremal for this problem. Specifically, the lower bound
wsp(K_{n})\geq n-1
is tight
[
3
]
. As for the upper bound, owing to work by Fernandes, Oliveira Mota and Sanhueza-Matamala
[
4
]
, it is known that

ssp(K_{n})=(1+o(1))n.

Wickes showed that every complete graph on a prime number of vertices has a generating path, and that the set of all the rotations of a generating path is a weakly separating path system. Hence,
wsp(K_{n})\leq n
when
n
is prime. She also proved that
wsp(K_{n})\leq n
when
n-1
is prime or when
n\leq 20
.

In Section
3
, we give a similar upper bound for
wsp(K_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1
.

For every natural number
n
,
wsp(K_{n})\leq n+1
.

In combination with the lower bound of
n-1
, our result nearly resolves the problem of the size of minimum weakly separating path systems for complete graphs.
Moreover, our proof is both short and entirely constructive, providing concrete and relatively easy to describe examples of weakly separating path systems for all complete graphs. In brief, we decompose
n
into appropriate prime numbers
p_{k}
and a small rest, and then apply a construction akin to that of Wickes to each of the cliques
K_{p_{k}}
. Then begins the main work of our proof, which is to carefully modify the resulting path system so that it becomes weakly separating and has the required number of paths.

As we explain in Section
4
, the path system constructed in the proof of Theorem
1
leaves few edges that are not separated from every other edge, and we will show that these may be assumed to be spaced in a relatively convenient way. Consequently, we show that we can expand the path system of Theorem
1
by 8 more paths to make it strongly separating.

Theorem 2
.

For every natural number
n
,
ssp(K_{n})\leq n+9
.

2
Definitions and Preliminaries

A
labelled clique
is a complete graph
K_{n}
together with a labelling of
V(K_{n})
with numbers from an interval of the naturals of length
n
. The labelling naturally induces on
V(K_{n})
a cyclic ordering and a rotation action. Given a labelled clique
K_{n}
, we partition
E(K_{n})
in
types
as follows:
the type of an edge
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
is defined to be
\min\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
, where
z_{i}:=(x_{i}-x_{3-i})(\text{mod $n$})
. Note that the type of any edge is in
[\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\rceil]
for
i=1,2
.

Edges of the same type will be called
homotypical
. It is also useful to define the
clockwise distance
of two homotypical edges contained in a labelled clique as the minimum order of a rotation that maps one to the other.

We define a
generating path

P
of
K_{n}
as one that exhibits the following properties:

(GP1)

there is a type
c
such that
P
contains exactly one edge of type
c
;

(GP2)

P
contains exactly two edges of each other type;

(GP3)

any two pairs of homotypical edges in
P
have distinct clockwise distances.

Figure 1:
A generating path for
K_{11}
with
c=1
.

From these properties it is also clear that in the path system generated by the rotations of
P
each edge of type
c
is contained in exactly one path, whereas every other edge is contained in exactly two paths.

It is proved in
[
11
]
that every complete graph of prime order contains a
generating cycle
, i.e. a cycle containing exactly two edges of each type and satisfying
(GP3)
. In order to obtain a generating path from a generating cycle, we simply delete a single edge. It is also proved that every complete graph of order at most
20
contains a generating path.

We work under the convention that every path is
oriented
, i.e. has a first and last vertex. We denote
e(P,P^{\prime})
the edge connecting the last vertex of
P
to the first vertex of
P^{\prime}
.

3
Proof that
wsp(K_{n})\leq n+1

Let
3\leq n=\sum_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}+b
with
p_{k}
primes. For brevity, let
s_{k}:=p_{1}+...+p_{k}
. It is a corollary of
[
8
]
that we can choose each
p_{k}
so that
n-s_{k}\leq\frac{p_{k}-3}{2}
and
b\in\{3,...,19\}
. In particular,
p_{i}\geq p_{i+1}
for
1\leq i<k
.

We define pairwise disjoint induced cliques of
K_{n}
indexed by, and with sizes corresponding to, the terms of the above sum. We label the vertices of each clique
K_{p_{k}}
using the interval
[s_{k-1}+1,s_{k}]
. We call these labelled cliques
levels
, and we say that an edge
touches
a level if its smaller end is contained in that level. Note that exactly the edges that are contained in a level have a type in it. Therefore, whenever we refer to the type of an edge, it will always be an edge contained in a level and we will always mean its type in that level.

Let

\mathscr{B}:=\{\mathbf{B_{m+1}^{s_{m}+1}},...,\mathbf{B_{m+1}^{n}}\}

be a weakly separating path system for
K_{b}
given by a generating path with, say, only one edge of type
1
, and its rotations. Let
C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}
be a generating cycle for the clique
K_{p_{k}}
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{k}:=\{C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1},...,C_{k}^{s_{k}}\}

be the set of its rotations.
We construct a weakly separating path system
\mathscr{Q}
for
K_{n}
as follows.

To begin, we note that each generating cycle
C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}
defines a permutation
\sigma_{k}\in S_{\frac{p_{k}-1}{2}}
that maps each number in
\{1,...,\frac{p_{k}-1}{2}\}
to the clockwise distance in
K_{p_{k}}
of the homotypical edges of
C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}
that have it as their type. Based on this observation, for each
p_{k}
we define an injection
\phi_{k}:\{s_{k}+1,...,n\}\rightarrow\{1,2,...,\frac{p_{k}-1}{2}\}
in the following way:

\phi_{k}(s_{k}+1)
is arbitrary, and for each
j\geq 2
,
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)
is the clockwise distance of the two edges of type
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j-1)
in
C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}
if this allows
\phi_{k}
to retain its status as an injection, otherwise it is chosen arbitrarily to do so. Note that
\phi_{k}
is not surjective, since
n-s_{k}\leq\frac{p_{k}-3}{2}
.

If
im(\phi_{k})
is a union of cycles of
\sigma_{k}
, then we let
c_{k}\notin im(\phi_{k})
be arbitrary. Otherwise, we let
c_{k}
be the clockwise distance of the edges of type
\phi_{k}(n)
. From each cycle
C_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}
, we remove exactly one edge of type
c_{k}
, different for each
i
. This yields a generating path
P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}
and the corresponding weakly separating path system of
K_{p_{k}}
,

\mathcal{P}_{k}:=\{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1},...,P_{k}^{s_{k}}\}.

We then relabel the path of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
so that
P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+j}
starts from
s_{k-1}+j
.

For each path
P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
and each type
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\in im(\phi_{k})
, we remove exactly one edge of type
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)
in
P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}
, different for each
i
, and join the ends of that edge to
s_{k}+j
.
We name the resulting path
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}}
, and define

\mathscr{P}_{k}:=\{\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+1}},...,\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k}}}\}.

Figure 2:
For the sake of providing a manageable example, suppose that we decide to needlessly split
K_{14}
into two levels. Then the present figure depicts a choice for
\mathbf{P_{1}^{1}}
with
c=1
based on the generating path from Figure
1
.

It is worth noting that every path
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}}\in\mathscr{P}_{k}
:

(P1)

has exactly one edge in level
k
of each type in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
, which appears in no other path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
- in particular, this implies that each such edge is contained in exactly one path in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
;

(P2)

has exactly two edges in level
k
of each other type, which it inherits from
P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}
- in particular, this implies that every such edge is contained in exactly two paths of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
;

(P3)

has exactly two edges incident to each vertex
s_{k}+j
, one of which it shares with the path
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i+\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
and the other with the path
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i-\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
.

We also observe that, for each vertex
s_{k}+j
, there do not exist homotypical edges in any path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that have clockwise distance
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)
in
K_{p_{k}}
, since in every such path one edge of each such type has been substituted by a path of length two passing through another vertex in
\{s_{k}+1,...,n\}
as described above (or, in the case of
n
, they have been removed from the cycles of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
to form
\mathcal{P}_{k}
) - we let
(P4)
denote this property.

We continue by constructing sets of paths

\mathcal{R}_{k}:=\{R_{k}^{s_{k}+1},...,R_{k}^{n},\mathbf{R_{k}^{n+1}}\},

where
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
,
j\leq n-s_{k}
contains all the edges of
K_{p_{k}}
of type
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)
but one, and
\mathbf{R_{k}^{n+1}}
contains all the edges of
K_{p_{k}}
of type
c_{k}
but one. Specifically, we pick the edges excluded by the paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
to be from distinct paths in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
; this is property
(P5)
. We may impose it because each path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
has exactly one edge of each type in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
and that edge is different from those of all other paths in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, due to
(P1)
.

We then extend each path
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}\neq\mathbf{R_{m}^{n+1}}
by its
connecting edge
, namely
e(R_{k}^{s_{k}+j},P_{k+1}^{s_{k}+j})
if
k\leq m-1
and
j\leq p_{k+1}
, or
e(R_{k}^{s_{k}+j},R_{k+1}^{s_{k}+j})
if
k\leq m-1
and
j\geq p_{k+1}+1
, or
e(R_{m}^{s_{m}+j},\mathbf{B_{m+1}^{s_{m}+j})}
if
k=m
. We thus obtain a path
\mathbf{R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}}
. We define

\mathscr{R}_{k}:=\{\mathbf{R_{k}^{s_{k}+1}},...,\mathbf{R_{k}^{n}},\mathbf{R_{k}^{n+1}}\}.

Finally, we define

\mathscr{Q}:=\{\mathbf{Q^{1}},...,\mathbf{Q^{n}},\mathbf{Q^{n+1}}\}

where
\mathbf{Q^{\ell}}
is the
concatenation of all the boldface paths of exponent
\ell
, i.e.
\mathbf{Q^{\ell}}
is the concatenation of all paths
\mathbf{R^{\ell}_{i}}
that were defined, and of
\mathbf{P^{\ell}_{k}}
or
\mathbf{B^{\ell}_{m+1}}
if these were defined, where
k
is the appropriate index.

The reader can check that
\mathscr{Q}
is indeed a path system, as its elements are concatenations of pairwise internally disjoint paths. It also has size
n+1
. It remains to show that
\mathscr{Q}
is weakly separating.

We begin by noting that
\mathbf{Q^{1}}\cup...\cup\mathbf{Q^{\ell}}
, where
\ell=s_{k}
, contains exactly the edges that touch one of the first
k
levels.
Therefore, edges that touch lower levels are separated from edges that touch higher levels. Also, edges in level
m+1
are weakly separated by
\mathscr{B}
. On the other hand, all the edges that touch a level
k\leq m
are contained in the paths of
\mathscr{Q}_{k}:=\mathscr{P}_{k}\cup\mathscr{R}_{k}
. Moreover, any path
P\in\mathscr{Q}_{k}
extends to a different path
P^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Q}
. It is therefore sufficient to show that
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
weakly separates the edges that touch level
k
.

Let
e,e^{\prime}
be two distinct edges that touch level
k
but are not contained in it. Then the only paths that may contain them both are those of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, since every other path of
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
contains at most one such edge. So, suppose that
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}}
is a path containing both
e
and
e^{\prime}
.

If
e
and
e^{\prime}
have a common greater end, say
s_{k}+j
, then by
(P3)
the only other path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that contains, say,
e
is
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i-\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
, and the only other path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that contains
e^{\prime}
is
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i+\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
. But these are distinct paths, since
2\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\neq 0\text{(mod $p_{k}$)}
. So
e
and
e^{\prime}
are separated from each other.

If
e
and
e^{\prime}
have distinct greater ends, say
s_{k}+j_{1}
and
s_{k}+j_{2}
, then again by
(P3)
the only other path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that contains
e
is
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i\pm\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j_{1})\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
, and the only other path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that contains
e^{\prime}
is
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i\pm\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j_{2})\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
. Again, since
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j_{1})
and
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j_{2})
are distinct elements of
\{1,...,\frac{p_{k}-1}{2}\}
, these two paths are distinct, separating
e
and
e^{\prime}
from each other.

Hence, the edges that touch level
k
but are not contained in it are strongly separated.

Subsequently, let
e
and
e^{\prime}
both be contained in level
k
. If one of them is contained in one of the paths in
\mathscr{R}_{k}
and the other is not contained in the same path, then of course the former is separated from the latter (and the latter, as long as it is contained in at least two paths of
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
, is separated from the former). For this to not happen, one of the following must occur.

It may be that
e
and
e^{\prime}
are both contained in the same path of
\mathscr{R}_{k}
. But then they are homotypical, and their type is in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
. By
(P1)
, that means that each of
e
and
e^{\prime}
belongs to a different path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, so they are separated from each other.

It may be that
e
and
e^{\prime}
are not contained in any of the paths in
\mathscr{R}_{k}
because their types are not in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
. But then by
(P2)
each of them appears in exactly two paths of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, corresponding to the two paths in which it appears in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
, therefore they are separated from each other.

It may be that
e
is the unique edge of some type in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
that is not contained in any paths in
\mathscr{R}_{k}
. If the same holds for
e^{\prime}
, then, by
(P5)
,
e
and
e^{\prime}
are still separated from each other by
\mathscr{P}_{k}
. If instead
e^{\prime}
is of a type not in
im(\phi_{k})\cup\{c_{k}\}
, then by
(P2)

e^{\prime}
appears in two paths in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
and by
(P1)

e
appears only in one, so again
e^{\prime}
is separated from
e
.

Therefore, the edges contained in level
k
are weakly separated.

Finally, let
e
be contained in level
k
and let
e^{\prime}
touch level
k
but not be contained in it. Let
s_{k}+j
be the greater end of
e^{\prime}
. Let
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+i}}
be a path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
containing
e^{\prime}
, and suppose that it also contains
e
. Then by
(P3)
and without loss of generality
e^{\prime}
is also contained in
\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i+\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
. By
(P4)
, there do not exist homotypical edges in any path of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that have clockwise distance
\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)
in
K_{p_{k}}
, so
e\notin\mathbf{P_{k}^{s_{k-1}+(i+\phi_{k}(s_{k}+j)\text{(mod $p_{k}$)})}}
, implying that
e^{\prime}
is separated from
e
; and, if
e
is contained in at least two paths in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, or in a path in
\mathscr{R}_{k}
the connecting edge of which is not adjacent to
s_{k}+j
, then it too is separated from
e^{\prime}
.

This concludes the case analysis, and the proof.

4
Proof that
ssp(K_{n})\leq n+9

In the previous section, we explained that edges that touch lower levels are separated from edges that touch higher levels by
\mathscr{Q}
. We begin this section by noting that those of the latter that are contained in at least two paths are also separated from most of the former. Indeed, given an edge
e
touching level
k
and contained in two paths
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathscr{Q}
, the intersections of these two paths with each level
k^{\prime}<k
are distinct (hence edge-disjoint) paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
, so together
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
separate
e
from all of the edges that are contained in any of the lower levels. Therefore the only possibly offending edges are the connecting edges in
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, some of which may be present in both paths, so that
e
is not separated from them.

Moreover, in Section
3
, when examining the separation properties of
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
, we took care to note when both edges in a pair under examination were separated from each other. We invite the reader to revisit our case analysis and verify that there are only three kinds of
bad
edges, i.e. edges that may not be separated by
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
(resp.
\mathscr{B}
) from all the other edges that touch the same level
k\leq m
(resp.
m+1
):

•

Perhaps some edges in level
m+1
, since
\mathscr{B}
is only a weakly separating path system.

•

Those contained in a single path, which are not separated from all of the other edges in the same path. There are exactly
n-s_{k}+1
of these touching (in fact contained in) level
k
: they are the edges
e_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
of type
\phi(s_{k}+j)
that are excluded by the paths
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
, respectively, as well as the edge
e_{k}^{n+1}
of type
c_{k}
that is excluded by
R_{k}^{n+1}
. We shall call these the
e
-
edges
of the corresponding paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
. Note that no path contains its
e
-edge.

•

At most two edges in each path in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
that are not separated from its connecting edge, namely those that appear in the two paths of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
that contain the connecting edge. Let us call these
f_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
and
g_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
, under the arbitrary convention that the former shares with the connecting edge of
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
a path of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
with smaller exponent than the latter. These are respectively the
f
- and
g
-
edges
of the paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
that contain them.

We introduce the notations
E_{k}^{s_{k}+j}:=\{e_{k}^{s_{k}+1},...,e_{k}^{s_{k}+j}\}
and
E_{k}:=E_{k}^{n+1}
. The sets
F_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
,
F_{k}
,
G_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
and
G_{k}
are defined similarly. We also note that the bad edges of each kind in the entire graph are separated from each other by
\mathscr{Q}
, and need only to be collectively separated from all of the other edges.

In order to handle both the bad edges and the instances of coinciding connecting edges, it will help to have some extra wiggle space. It follows easily from
[
8
]
that for
n\geq 3
there exists a decomposition
n=\sum_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}+b
with
p_{k}
primes so that for the partial sums
s_{k}
we have
n-s_{k}\leq\frac{p_{k}}{4}
and
b\in\{3,...,19,29,31\}
, except that
(p_{m},b)
may equal one of
(19,5)
,
(19,6)
,
(23,7)
, or
(31,8)
. We hence decompose
K_{n}
according to this stronger decomposition and repeat the construction of Section
3
, but with the following modifications.

We say that level
k
is
arranged
if each of
E_{k}
,
F_{k}
and
G_{k}
can be decomposed into at most
2
linear forests. Naturally, level
m+1
is arranged. We inductively arrange all the levels of
K_{n}
, down to the first, as follows.

When tackling the weak version of the problem, the only restriction that we imposed on the choice of
\mathcal{R}_{k}
was that the edges in
E_{k}
be drawn from different paths of
\mathscr{P}_{k}
, which is property
(P5)
. Now we demand in addition that level
k
be arranged - this is property
(P6)
. We also prohibit that any two of the paths of
\mathcal{R}_{k}
have the same connecting edge (property
(P7)
).

To achieve these requirements, we sequentially choose each oriented path
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
and orient each maximal path in
E_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
,
F_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
(in short, we
establish

R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
), according to the following process. The path
R_{k}^{s_{k}+1}
is established arbitrarily. Once the paths
\{R_{k}^{s_{k}+1},...,R_{k}^{s_{k}+j}\}
have been established, we choose the path
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
so that:

(i)

the path in
\mathscr{P}_{k}
that contains
e_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
does not contain any edge in
E_{k}^{j}
(this is just
(P5)
);

(ii)

the

f
-edge,
g
-edge) of
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
is not incident to any end of an edge in

(resp.
F_{k}^{j}
,
G_{k}^{j}
), unless said end is the last vertex of a maximal path of
E_{k}^{j}
(resp.
F_{k}^{j}
,
G_{k}^{j}
);

(iii)

if the connecting edge of

has the same greater end as the connecting edge of a previously established path
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j^{\prime}+1}
,
j^{\prime}<j
, then
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
may not have the same last vertex as
R_{k}^{s_{k}+j^{\prime}+1}
(this is just
(P7)
).

We then reorient each maximal path in
E_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
,
F_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
and repeat. This process continues for as long as we can obey the above three conditions and then halts.

Item (ii) ensures that, when the above process cannot continue anymore, the bad edges of each kind that will have been already selected will have formed a linear forest. Indeed, each of
E_{k}^{s_{k}+1}
,
F_{k}^{s_{k}+1}
and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+1}
has at most one edge, hence is a linear forest. If
E_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
,
F_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+j}
are linear forests, then adding to each of them a single edge that is not incident to an internal vertex of any path and does not connect the ends of any single path also produces a linear forest.

We will iterate the entire process up to two times to arrange level
k
. At the end of two iterations, we must have selected a total of
n-s_{k}+1
paths, which is the required size of
\mathcal{R}_{k}
.

We begin with the first iteration. Jointly, items (i) and (ii) forbid at most
7j

R_{k}^{s_{k}+j+1}
. On the other hand, item (iii) forbids up to
4

k+1

5

and
4

\mathscr{B}
have pairwise distinct first vertices, so the connecting edges that are incident to them are necessarily distinct anyway. Therefore, letting
A
denote the index at which the first iteration stops, we can establish
A\geq\min\{\lceil\frac{p_{k}-4}{7}\rceil+1,n-s_{k}+1\}
(or
A\geq\min\{\lceil\frac{p_{k}}{7}\rceil,n-s_{k}+1\}
if

) paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
so that the properties
(P5)
and
(P7)
are satisfied and each of
E_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
,
F_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
is a linear forest.

If
A\leq n-s_{k}
, we select
R_{k}^{s_{k}+A+1}
arbitrarily (but obeying
\mathbf{(P5)}
) and begin the second iteration of the process. This time, in each step we apply item (ii) and the orientation of maximal paths only to the sets
E_{k}^{j}\setminus E_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
,
F_{k}^{j}\setminus F_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
and
G_{k}^{j}\setminus G_{k}^{s_{k}+A}

with
j\geq A
there are at most
j

6(j-A)
options that are forbidden due to item (ii). Moreover, there are up to
4
options that are forbidden by item (iii), unless of course
k=m

B\geq\min\{\lceil\frac{p_{k}-A-4}{7}\rceil,n-s_{k}-A+1\}
(or

) more paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
so that the properties
(P5)
and
(P7)
are satisfied and each of

,

and
G_{k}^{s_{k}+A+B}\setminus G_{k}^{s_{k}+A}
is a linear forest.

For

, one can show by routine calculation and the fact

that
A+B\geq n-s_{k}+1
, so this second iteration is indeed the final one, establishing the required

paths in
\mathcal{R}_{k}
in such a way that
(P5)
,
(P6)
and
(P7)
are obeyed. Almost all of the smaller cases, including the special cases with

For the cases
(19,6)

paths, respectively. However, in both cases it can be slightly tweaked to yield the desired additional path. For
(19,6)
, after establishing

In this way we arrive again to a weakly separating system

of

as in Section

(P6)
and
(P7)
. It remains to separate the bad edges from all of the other edges. In doing so, we will not modify any of the paths of
\mathscr{Q}
, but we will only construct additional paths, so that all of the other cases of edge separation, which are already achieved by
\mathscr{Q}
, will be preserved.

For each maximal linear forest made up of
f
-edges and contained in a single level, we can connect its separate paths by edges between their ends to form a single path. We may do so arbitrarily, since each
f
-edge in level
k
is already separated from every other edge in its level by
\mathscr{Q}_{k}
. In this manner we form in each level

two paths
F_{k}^{\alpha}
and
F_{k}^{\beta}
, the union of which contains all of the
f
-edges of level
k
. Note that for
k\leq m-1
there are at least
p_{k}-2(n-s_{k}+1)+2=p_{k}-2(n-s_{k})

edges between the first vertex of
F_{k+1}^{\alpha}
and the vertices of level
k
that are not internal in
F_{k}^{\alpha}
. The
f
-edges of level
k
are not separated from at most
n-s_{k}+1
of them. Since
p_{k}-3(n-s_{k})-1\geq\frac{p_{k}}{4}-1>0
, we can use one of the rest, if needed together with an edge connecting its other end with the last vertex of

, to connect
F_{k}^{\alpha}
and

by a path of length at most

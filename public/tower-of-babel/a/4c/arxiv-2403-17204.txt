Kinetic theory of vacuum pair production in uniform electric fields revisited
I. A. Aleksandrov, A. Kudlis, A. I. Klochai

Abstract
We investigate the phenomenon of electron-positron pair production from vacuum in the presence of a uniform time-dependent electric field of arbitrary polarization. Taking into account the interaction with the external classical background in a nonperturbative manner, we quantize the electron-positron field and derive a system of ten quantum kinetic equations (QKEs) showing that the previously-used QKEs are incorrect once the external field rotates in space. We employ then the Wigner-function formalism of the field quantization and establish a direct connection between the Dirac-Heisenberg-Wigner (DHW) approach to investigating the vacuum pair-production process and the QKEs. We provide a self-contained description of the two theoretical frameworks rigorously proving their equivalence and present an exact one-to-one correspondence between the kinetic functions involved within the two techniques. Special focus is placed on the analysis of the spin effects in the final particle distributions.

Introduction

One of the fundamental differences between ordinary quantum mechanics and quantum field theory is that the latter enables elementary particles to participate in reactions, in which the number of any species may not be conserved. In the context of quantum electrodynamics (QED), even in vacuum, this leads to spontaneous acts of creation and annihilation of virtual electron-positron pairs that affect the physical properties of real particles and the corresponding compound systems. Furthermore, these virtual quanta can be directly transformed into real
e^{+}e^{-}
pairs by making the physical vacuum interact with an external electromagnetic field. In the case of a static uniform electric background, this phenomenon is analogous to the process of quantum mechanical tunneling and is known as the Sauter-Schwinger mechanism

;

;

Within the realm of QED, a particularly exciting challenge revolves around the practical observation of the Sauter-Schwinger pair-production process, which is yet to be experimentally investigated (for recent developments regarding strong-field QED see reviews

;

;

The mechanism of
e^{+}e^{-}
pair production in realistic setups should be described by taking into account the corresponding spatiotemporal structure of the external fields. For instance, an inhomogeneous electromagnetic background efficiently generating pairs and preserving the nonperturbative nature of the process can be formed in a combination of high-intensity laser pulses. From a theoretical point of view, it is crucial to incorporate the interaction with the external classical field without relying on perturbative techniques. It turns out that an exact analytical treatment of the problem is only possible for a very limited number of simple field configurations, which is basically due to the lack of the analytical solutions to the Dirac equation in the presence of the external background (for exactly solvable setups see Refs.

The primary aim of the present study is to demonstrate the equivalence of the three exact approaches mentioned above in the case of a time-dependent external electric field of arbitrary polarization. Most important, we will establish an explicit connection between the QKE components and the DHW functions, which was previously done only in the case of a linearly polarized external field

Finally, we will consider the spin anisotropy of the particles produced. To properly address experimentally relevant setups, we will suggest that the electron and positron states are classified according to the particle’s helicity. The corresponding number densities will be deduced within the QKE approach by means of the Bogoliubov transformation and also expressed in terms of the DHW functions. Besides, we will explicitly show how to construct the corresponding projection of the Wigner function and to obtain the necessary distributions directly via the DHW formalism.

Here we reduce the DHW system to ten equations and explicitly show how the corresponding functions are expressed in terms of the ten QKE components. Finally, we discuss the physical interpretation of the spin states and provide the particle densities in terms of the helicity quantum number. We conclude in Sec.

II
Furry-picture quantization and kinetic theory

We will first discuss a general approach to canonical quantization of the electron-positron field in the presence of an external background. Although we neglect the quantized part of the electromagnetic field, the classical part is treated nonperturbatively. We will briefly recap a transition from the Schrödinger representation to the interaction picture and describe how to extract the electron-positron momentum distributions from the one-particle solutions of the Dirac equation. Next, we will introduce the adiabatic set of the Hamiltonian eigenfunctions in the case of a uniform background and derive the QKEs. This technique will be then related to the DHW formalism in Sec.
III
.

II.1
General quantization procedure. Heisenberg representation

We assume that the external electromagnetic field is described by the
c
-numbered function
A^{\mu}(x)
, where
x=(t,\,\mathbf{x})
, and vanishes outside the time interval
t_{\text{in}}<t<t_{\text{out}}
, where one implies
t_{\text{in/out}}\to\mp\infty
. We start from the Schrödinger picture and, following Ref.
fradkin_gitman_shvartsman
, introduce two sets of the one-particle Hamiltonian eigenfunctions at
t=t_{\text{in}}
and
t=t_{\text{out}}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e}(t_{\text{in}})\,{}_{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle={}_{\pm}\varepsilon_{n}(t_{\text{in}})\,{}_{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e}(t_{\text{out}})\,{}^{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle={}^{\pm}\varepsilon_{n}(t_{\text{out}})\,{}^{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\,,

where
\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}[-i\boldsymbol{\nabla}-e\mathbf{A}(x)]+eA_{0}(x)+\beta m
depends on time due to the presence of the external field and the eigenvalues denoted by plus (minus) are positive (negative). These sets are orthonormal and complete with respect to the usual inner product. The index
n
incorporates the necessary quantum numbers, e.g., momentum and spin. Let us then expand the field operator according to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi(\mathbf{x})&=\sum_{n}\big[\mathrm{a}_{n}(t_{\text{in}})\,{}_{+}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})+\mathrm{b}^{\dagger}_{n}(t_{\text{in}})\,{}_{-}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\big]\,,\\
\psi(\mathbf{x})&=\sum_{n}\big[\mathrm{a}_{n}(t_{\text{out}})\,{}^{+}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})+\mathrm{b}^{\dagger}_{n}(t_{\text{out}})\,{}^{-}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\big]\,,\end{aligned}

(2)

where we have introduced the electron (positron) creation and annihilation operators
\mathrm{a}^{\dagger}_{n}
(
\mathrm{b}^{\dagger}_{n}
) and
\mathrm{a}_{n}
(
\mathrm{b}_{n}
), respectively. These operators obey the usual anticommutation relations. Accordingly, the Hamiltonian

H_{e}(t)=\int\!\psi^{\dagger}(\mathbf{x})\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)\psi(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}

(3)

is diagonalized at time instants
t_{\text{in}}
and
t_{\text{out}}
. The corresponding vacuum states will be denoted by
|0,t_{\text{in}}\rangle
and
|0,t_{\text{out}}\rangle
, respectively.

To construct the field operators in the Heisenberg representation, one has to introduce the following unitary evolution operator:

U_{e}(t,t^{\prime})=T\,\mathrm{exp}\Bigg[-i\int\limits_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{e}(\tau)d\tau\Bigg]\,,

(4)

where
T
stands for the conventional time-ordering operator. The operator (
4
) is a solution of the equation

i\partial_{t}U_{e}(t,t^{\prime})=H_{e}(t)U_{e}(t,t^{\prime})

(5)

with the initial condition
U_{e}(t,t)=\mathbbm{1}
. We perform a transformation by means of the operator
U_{e}(0,t)
, so the field gains a temporal dependence:

\psi(t,\mathbf{x})\equiv\psi(x)=U_{e}(0,t)\psi(\mathbf{x})U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t)\,.

(6)

In the Heisenberg representation, the creation and annihilation operators are then given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{n}(\text{in})&=U_{e}(0,t_{\text{in}})\mathrm{a}_{n}(t_{\text{in}})U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t_{\text{in}})\,,\\
a_{n}(\text{out})&=U_{e}(0,t_{\text{out}})\mathrm{a}_{n}(t_{\text{out}})U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t_{\text{out}})\,.\end{aligned}

(7)

The other creation and annihilation operators are defined similarly. The unitary transformation preserves the anticommutation relations that take place in the Schrödinger picture. The
in
and
out
vacuum states are defined via

\displaystyle|0,\text{in}\rangle

\displaystyle=U_{e}(0,t_{\text{in}})|0,t_{\text{in}}\rangle\,,

(8)

\displaystyle|0,\text{out}\rangle

\displaystyle=U_{e}(0,t_{\text{out}})|0,t_{\text{out}}\rangle\,.

By computing the time derivative of (
6
) and using

\big[\psi(\mathbf{x}),\,H_{e}(t)]=\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)\psi(\mathbf{x})\,,

(9)

one finds that the Heisenberg field operator satisfies the equation

i\partial_{t}\psi(x)=\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)\psi(x)\,.

(10)

This means that the temporal evolution of
\psi(x)
is determined by the same equation as that for the time-dependent one-particle solutions. Let us introduce the corresponding evolution operator

G_{e}(t,t^{\prime})=T\,\mathrm{exp}\Bigg[-i\int\limits_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{e}(\tau)d\tau\Bigg]\,.

(11)

Suppose that the Schrödinger field operator
\psi(\mathbf{x})
is known, the Heisenberg operator
\psi(t_{\text{out}},\mathbf{x})
can be constructed in two different ways. First, one can carry out the transformation (
6
) at
t=t_{\text{out}}
. Alternatively, one can propagate the Heisenberg operator
\psi(t_{\text{in}},\mathbf{x})
by utilizing the operator
G_{e}(t_{\text{out}},t_{\text{in}})
. This can be expressed by the following relations:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi(t_{\text{out}},\mathbf{x})&=U_{e}(0,t_{\text{out}})\psi(\mathbf{x})U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t_{\text{out}})\,,\\
\psi(t_{\text{out}},\mathbf{x})&=G_{e}(t_{\text{out}},t_{\text{in}})U_{e}(0,t_{\text{in}})\psi(\mathbf{x})U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t_{\text{in}})\,.\end{aligned}

(12)

These allow one to express the
out
operators in terms of the
in
operators with the aid of the
G
-matrix elements. Using Eqs. (
2
) and (
7
), and the fact that the stationary states
{}_{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})
and
{}^{\pm}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})
form orthonormal and complete sets, one obtains

\displaystyle a_{n}(\text{out})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\big[a_{k}(\text{in})G({}^{+}|{}_{+})_{nk}+b^{\dagger}_{k}(\text{in})G({}^{+}|{}_{-})_{nk}\big]\,,

(13)

\displaystyle b_{n}(\text{out})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\big[a^{\dagger}_{k}(\text{in})G({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{kn}+b_{k}(\text{in})G({}_{-}|{}^{-})_{kn}\big]\,,

(14)

where

\displaystyle G({}^{\zeta}|{}_{\kappa})_{nk}=\int\!d\mathbf{x}\,{}^{\zeta}\varphi^{\dagger}_{n}(\mathbf{x})G_{e}(t_{\text{out}},t_{\text{in}})\,{}_{\kappa}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\,,

(15)

\displaystyle G({}_{\zeta}|{}^{\kappa})_{nk}=\big[G({}^{\kappa}|{}_{\zeta})_{kn}\big]^{*}\,,\quad\zeta,\kappa=\pm\,.

Similarly one finds

\displaystyle a_{n}(\text{in})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\big[a_{k}(\text{out})G({}_{+}|{}^{+})_{nk}+b^{\dagger}_{k}(\text{out})G({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{nk}\big]\,,

\displaystyle b_{n}(\text{in})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\big[a^{\dagger}_{k}(\text{out})G({}^{+}|{}_{-})_{kn}+b_{k}(\text{out})G({}^{-}|{}_{-})_{kn}\big]\,.

The
G
matrices are also orthonormal and complete in the following sense:

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta}G({}^{\pm}|{}_{\zeta})G({}_{\zeta}|{}^{\mp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta}G({}_{\pm}|{}^{\zeta})G({}^{\zeta}|{}_{\mp})=0\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta}G({}^{\pm}|{}_{\zeta})G({}_{\zeta}|{}^{\pm})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\zeta}G({}_{\pm}|{}^{\zeta})G({}^{\zeta}|{}_{\pm})=\mathrm{I}\,.

(17)

The Heisenberg field operator
\psi(x)
satisfies Eq. (
10
) and thus evolves in time according to

\psi(x)=G_{e}(x^{0},t_{\text{in}})\psi(t_{\text{in}},\mathbf{x})\,,

(18)

where the initial field operator

is given by

\psi(t_{\text{in}},\mathbf{x})=\sum_{n}\big[a_{n}(\text{in})\,{}_{+}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})+b^{\dagger}_{n}(\text{in})\,{}_{-}\varphi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\big]\,.

Here we have employed Eqs. (
2
), (
6
), and (
7
). Since the operator

evolves not only the field operator, but also the one-particle solutions, it follows from Eqs. (

) and (

\psi(x)=\sum_{n}\big[a_{n}(\text{in})\,{}_{+}\varphi_{n}(x)+b^{\dagger}_{n}(\text{in})\,{}_{-}\varphi_{n}(x)\big]\,,

where
{}_{+}\varphi_{n}(x)
and

are the so-called

The same way, one finds

\psi(x)=\sum_{n}\big[a_{n}(\text{out})\,{}^{+}\varphi_{n}(x)+b^{\dagger}_{n}(\text{out})\,{}^{-}\varphi_{n}(x)\big]\,,

(21)

where
{}^{+}\varphi_{n}(x)
and
{}^{-}\varphi_{n}(x)
are the

\displaystyle H_{e,\text{H}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{e}(0,t)H_{e}(t)U^{\dagger}_{e}(0,t)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\psi^{\dagger}(x)\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)\psi(x)d\mathbf{x}\,.

It has a diagonal form in terms of the

(

) particle-number operators only at

(

) because the time-dependent

where

Let us now compute the number density of the electrons produced in some given state

(we use the term “density” keeping in mind that

n^{(e^{-})}_{m}=\langle 0,t_{\text{in}}|U^{\dagger}_{e}(t_{\text{out}},t_{\text{in}})\mathrm{a}^{\dagger}_{m}(t_{\text{out}})\mathrm{a}_{m}(t_{\text{out}})U_{e}(t_{\text{out}},t_{\text{in}})|0,t_{\text{in}}\rangle.

Using then the relation

and inserting the identity operator

\displaystyle n^{(e^{-})}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}G({}^{+}|{}_{-})_{mn}G({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{nm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{G({}^{+}|{}_{-})G({}_{-}|{}^{+})\}_{mm}\,.

The analogous expressions for the number density of positrons are given by

\displaystyle n^{(e^{+})}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle 0,\text{in}|b^{\dagger}_{m}(\text{out})b_{m}(\text{out})|0,\text{in}\rangle\,,

(26)

\displaystyle n^{(e^{+})}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}G({}^{-}|{}_{+})_{mn}G({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{nm}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{G({}^{-}|{}_{+})G({}_{+}|{}^{-})\}_{mm}\,.

and

II.2
Uniform external field. Adiabatic basis

Consider an external time-dependent electric field
\mathbf{E}(t)
of an arbitrary direction (polarization). We choose the temporal gauge
A_{0}=0
, where
\mathbf{E}(t)=-\mathbf{A}^{\prime}(t)
. The vector potential at
t=t_{\text{in}}\to-\infty
can differ from its limit for
t=t_{\text{out}}\to+\infty
, so we introduce
\mathbf{A}_{\text{in}}\equiv\mathbf{A}(t_{\text{in}})
and
\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}}\equiv\mathbf{A}(t_{\text{out}})
. In practical calculations, it is convenient to choose
\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}}=\mathbf{0}
since in the case of a zero vector potential one does not need to distinguish between the canonical momentum and kinetic one and we are interested in computing the momentum distributions of the particles produced at
t=t_{\text{out}}\to+\infty
.

As the external field is spatially homogeneous, the coordinate dependence of the wave functions is trivial. We specify each solution by the corresponding generalized momentum
\mathbf{p}
and spin quantum number
s
and represent the
in
and
out
functions in the following way:

\displaystyle{}_{+}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,{}_{+}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,

(28)

\displaystyle{}^{+}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,{}^{+}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,

(29)

\displaystyle{}_{-}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,{}_{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,

(30)

\displaystyle{}^{-}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,{}^{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}^{+}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t\geqslant t_{\text{out}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-ip^{0}(\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}})(t-t_{\text{out}})}\,u_{\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}},s}\,,

\displaystyle{}_{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t\leqslant t_{\text{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{ip^{0}(\mathbf{p}+e\mathbf{A}_{\text{in}})(t-t_{\text{in}})}\,v_{-\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}_{\text{in}},s}\,,

\displaystyle{}^{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t\geqslant t_{\text{out}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{ip^{0}(\mathbf{p}+e\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}})(t-t_{\text{out}})}\,v_{-\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}_{\text{out}},s}\,,

where

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{0}(\mathbf{p})u_{\mathbf{p},s}\,,

\displaystyle\big(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\mathbf{p}+\beta m\big)v_{\mathbf{p},s}

\displaystyle u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}u_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}=v^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}v_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\,,\quad u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}v_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}=0\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1}\big(u_{\mathbf{p},s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}+v_{\mathbf{p},s}v^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}\big)=\mathrm{I}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1}u_{\mathbf{p},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p},s}=\frac{\gamma^{0}p^{0}(\mathbf{p})-\boldsymbol{\gamma}\mathbf{p}+m\mathrm{I}}{2p^{0}(\mathbf{p})}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1}v_{\mathbf{p},s}\overline{v}_{\mathbf{p},s}=\frac{\gamma^{0}p^{0}(\mathbf{p})+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\mathbf{p}-m\mathrm{I}}{2p^{0}(\mathbf{p})}\,.

Once
t\geqslant t_{\text{in}}
, the functions
{}_{\zeta}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)
gain a nontrivial temporal dependence, and their evolution is governed by

i\,{}_{\zeta}\dot{\chi}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)=\big\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,[\zeta\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t)]+\beta m\big\}{}_{\zeta}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,.

(39)

The
out
functions
{}^{\zeta}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)
satisfy the same equations. The
G
matrices (
23
) are diagonal with respect to momentum:

\displaystyle G({}_{+}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s;\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,g({}_{+}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle G({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s;\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,g({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle G({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s;\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,g({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle G({}_{-}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s;\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\,g({}_{-}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,

where the
g
matrices are given by

\displaystyle g({}_{+}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}_{+}\chi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,{}^{+}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(t)\,,

(41)

\displaystyle g({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}_{+}\chi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,{}^{-}\chi_{-\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(t)\,,

\displaystyle g({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}_{-}\chi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,{}^{+}\chi_{-\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(t)\,,

\displaystyle g({}_{-}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}_{-}\chi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,{}^{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(t)\,.

According to Eqs. (
25
) and (
27
), the number density of particles with given momentum
\mathbf{p}
in a spin state
s
has the following form (
n_{\mathbf{p},s}\equiv dN_{\mathbf{p},s}/d\mathbf{p}
):

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{-})}_{\mathbf{p},s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\big|g({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{-\mathbf{p},s^{\prime},s}\big|^{2}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{+})}_{\mathbf{p},s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\big|g({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{-\mathbf{p},s^{\prime},s}\big|^{2}\,.

(43)

Here
V
is the volume of the system (
V\to\infty
), and we have employed a standard regularization prescription
\delta(\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0})=V/(2\pi)^{3}
, so in the case of a uniform external field, it is the number of pairs
per unit volume
that yields a finite value. Due to the completeness property (
17
), the particle densities obey

\sum_{s}n^{(e^{-})}_{\mathbf{p},s}=\sum_{s}n^{(e^{+})}_{-\mathbf{p},s}\,.

(44)

In what follows, a vast part of our derivations will revolve around the concept of the
adiabatic
Hamiltonian eigenfunctions. Note that in the case of a uniform electric field
\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)
for given
t
is equivalent to the zero-field Hamiltonian up to a simple gauge transformation. Although the corresponding eigenfunctions can be directly found by solving the differential equation, the latter observation makes it extremely simple. We introduce the so-called
adiabatic basis
:

\mathcal{H}_{e}(t)\varphi^{(\zeta)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)=\zeta\omega(\zeta\mathbf{p},t)\varphi^{(\zeta)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)\,,\quad\zeta=\pm\,,

(45)

where
\omega(\mathbf{p},t)=p^{0}(\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t))=\sqrt{m^{2}+[\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t)]^{2}}
. In Eq. (
45
) we use a semicolon to evidently indicate that the
t
dependence is parametric. In the explicit form,

\displaystyle\varphi^{(+)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,u_{\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s}\,,

(46)

\displaystyle\varphi^{(-)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{x}}\,v_{-\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s}\,.

(47)

The Heisenberg field operator can be decomposed according to

\psi(x)=\sum_{s}\int\!d\mathbf{p}\big[a_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\varphi^{(+)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)+b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\varphi^{(-)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t)\big]\,,

(48)

where the adiabatic creation and annihilation operators vary in time [do not confuse the adiabatic operators with those introduced within the Schrödinger representation in Eqs. (
2
)]. At asymptotic times
t_{\text{in/out}}\to\mp\infty
, one recovers

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\zeta)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t_{\text{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{\zeta}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x})\,,\quad\varphi^{(\zeta)}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x};t_{\text{out}})={}^{\zeta}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(\mathbf{x})\,,

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{p},s}(t_{\text{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\mathbf{p},s}(\text{in})\,,\quad a_{\mathbf{p},s}(t_{\text{out}})=a_{\mathbf{p},s}(\text{out})\,,

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t_{\text{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\text{in})\,,\quad b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(t_{\text{out}})=b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p},s}(\text{out})\,.

In the next section, we will introduce adiabatic densities of particles and deduce the system of 12 ordinary differential equations describing the pair-production process.

II.3
Quantum kinetic equations (QKEs)

The main idea is to deduce a system of equations governing the adiabatic number density and then evolve it to
t=t_{\text{out}}

We start with the following decomposition of the
in

\displaystyle{}_{-}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle{}_{+}\varphi_{\mathbf{p},s}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}}\Big[A^{(3)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t)\varphi^{(+)}_{\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x};t)+A^{(4)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t)\varphi^{(-)}_{-\mathbf{p},s^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x};t)\Big]\,.

By using the explicit form of the adiabatic eigenfunctions, one obtains

\displaystyle u^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s^{\prime}}{}_{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,\qquad A^{(2)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t)=v^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s^{\prime}}{}_{-}\chi_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,

The asymptotic values read

\displaystyle 0\,,\qquad A^{(2)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{in}})=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\,,

(53)

\displaystyle A^{(3)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{in}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\,,\qquad A^{(4)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{in}})=0

(54)

and

\displaystyle A^{(1)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})

\displaystyle g^{*}({}_{-}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,\qquad A^{(2)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})=g^{*}({}_{-}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,

(55)

\displaystyle A^{(3)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{*}({}_{+}|{}^{+})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,,\qquad A^{(4)}_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})=g^{*}({}_{+}|{}^{-})_{\mathbf{p},s,s^{\prime}}\,.

III.4
Physical interpretation of the spin effects. Helicity states

While we already showed how one should combine the DHW functions to obtain the momentum spectra of the particles summed over spin [see Eq. (
151
)], it may be not yet clear how to obtain the spin-resolved distributions, i.e. the DHW analog of Eq. (
106
) should be derived. Moreover, the physical interpretation of the anisotropy in Eq. (
106
) should also be clarified.

Although one can, in principle, experimentally measure the densities (
106
) if the electrons are projected onto the corresponding states (
77
), from the physical viewpoint, these states may not be relevant ones. While it is shown in Sec.
II.5
that a
constant
rotation of the bispinor basis can hardly simplify the computations, one can easily perform a necessary transformation using the
final
values of the QKE functions. Since we always fix the momentum of the particles produced, it is the particle’s helicity that represents a relevant additional observable as the helicity operator commutes with the Hamiltonian. To construct the corresponding eigenfunctions, one has to perform the following unitary transformation:

u_{\mathbf{p},s}=\alpha^{(\text{L})}_{\mathbf{p},s}\,u_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{L})}+\alpha^{(\text{R})}_{\mathbf{p},s}\,u_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{R})}\,,

(162)

where

\alpha^{(\text{L})}_{\mathbf{p},-}=\alpha^{(\text{R})}_{\mathbf{p},+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathbf{p}|-p_{z}}{|\mathbf{p}|}}\,,\qquad\alpha^{(\text{L})}_{\mathbf{p},+}=-\big[\alpha^{(\text{R})}_{\mathbf{p},-}\big]^{*}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{p_{x}-ip_{y}}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{p}|(|\mathbf{p}|-p_{z})}}\,.

(163)

One can directly verify that
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{L})}
and
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{R)}}
are the eigenvectors of the helicity operator
(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{p})/|\mathbf{p}|
. The transformation of the bispinors
v_{\mathbf{p},s}
is exactly the same. To obtain the helicity-resolved densities, one has to perform the corresponding Bogoliubov transformation of the creation/annihilation operators:

a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{L})}(t)=\sum_{s=\pm}\alpha^{(\text{L})}_{\mathbf{q},s}\,a_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,\qquad a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{R})}(t)=\sum_{s=\pm}\alpha^{(\text{R})}_{\mathbf{q},s}\,a_{\mathbf{p},s}(t)\,,

(164)

where
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}(t)
, so the coefficients depend on time. The electron density functions corresponding to a negative helicity (L) and to a positive helicity (R) are then given by

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{L})}(\mathbf{p},t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\big\langle 0,\text{in}\big|\big[a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{L})}(t)\big]^{\dagger}a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{L})}(t)\big|0,\text{in}\big\rangle=f(\mathbf{p},t)-\frac{\mathbf{q}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{p},t)}{|\mathbf{q}|}\,,

(165)

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{R})}(\mathbf{p},t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}\big\langle 0,\text{in}\big|\big[a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{R})}(t)\big]^{\dagger}a_{\mathbf{p}}^{(\text{R})}(t)\big|0,\text{in}\big\rangle=f(\mathbf{p},t)+\frac{\mathbf{q}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{p},t)}{|\mathbf{q}|}\,.

(166)

The final densities of the electrons produced read

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{-}\text{L})}_{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{L})}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})\,,

(167)

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{-}\text{R})}_{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{R})}(\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})\,.

(168)

The sum over
s
in Eq. (
106
) obviously coincides with a sum of these two quantities. For the positrons produced, the analogous expressions have the form

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{+}\text{L})}_{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{R})}(-\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})\,,

(169)

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{V}n^{(e^{+}\text{R})}_{\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{L})}(-\mathbf{p},t_{\text{out}})\,.

(170)

By using the relation (
160

f^{(e^{-}\text{L/R})}(\mathbf{p},t)=f(\mathbf{p},t)\pm\frac{\mathbf{q}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}(\mathbf{p},t)}{4|\mathbf{q}|}\,.

(171)

We also note that the
G
matrices within the Furry-picture formalism can also be easily transformed in order to describe the helicity states.

Although the helicity-resolved observables can be acquired by means of the Bogoliubov transformation as was done above, it is also possible to obtain them directly within the Wigner-function approach. To this end, we will first introduce the adiabatic densities corresponding to given values of
s

(172)

Let us note then that one can straightforwardly prove the following identity:

\tilde{\mathfrak{e}}(\mathbf{p},t)=\mathrm{Tr}\ \bigg\{\frac{m\mathrm{I}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\mathbf{q}}{2q^{0}}\big[\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{p},t)\big]\bigg\}\,,

(174)

The explicit form of

f^{(e^{-})}_{s}(\mathbf{p},t)=\mathrm{Tr}\ \Big\{\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{q},s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q},s}\big[\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{p},t)\big]\Big\}\,.

(175)

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-}\text{L/R})}(\mathbf{p},t)

IV
Conclusion

In this study, we revisited the kinetic approach to describing vacuum pair production in strong electric backgrounds. We examined the techniques based on the QKEs and the DHW formalism and demonstrated their equivalence. To this end, we first considered the framework of Furry-picture quantization, where the observable number densities of the particles produced are expressed in terms of the one-particle transition amplitudes. Along the same lines, one can quantize the electron-positron field using the basis of the adiabatic Hamiltonian eigenfunctions. Utilizing this approach, we derived first the QKEs and showed that the correct form of the equations is different to what was known in the literature. Second, we employed the adiabatic basis to directly compute the Wigner function, which allowed us to explicitly connect the kinetic functions involved in the DHW and QKE techniques.

A practical significance of the one-to-one correspondences derived in this study was already demonstrated: it is very convenient to switch from one formalism to another on the level of the final expressions without performing all of the intermediate steps. Furthermore, a unified picture involving the available theoretical frameworks also provides a coherent set of tools from the viewpoint of numerical computations.

Finally, we addressed the effect of spin anisotropy, which appears in the particle distributions in the case of rotating external fields. We constructed the number densities corresponding to the states with well-defined helicity by means of both direct Bogoliubov transformation and projection method within the DHW formalism. A numerical implementation of the QKE system for the analysis of the helicity effects was recently performed in Ref.
aleksandrov_kudlis_prd
.

Our study is expected to illuminate the fundamental aspects of the kinetic description of vacuum pair creation and to provide a solid theoretical basis for computing the observable spectra of the particles produced.

Appendix A
Reduction of the QKEs in the case of linear polarization

Here we present a proof of the statement (
108
). First, let us define

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}\times\mathbf{e})-\dot{\mathbf{e}}\,.

(178)

The explicit form of this vector reads

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{0}}{\sqrt{q_{0}^{2}-(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{n})^{2}}}\Bigg\{\dot{\mathbf{n}}+\frac{(\mathbf{q}\dot{\mathbf{n}})}{q_{0}^{2}-(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{n})^{2}}

(179)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg[(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{n})\mathbf{n}-\frac{q^{0}}{q^{0}+m}\,\mathbf{q}\bigg]\Bigg\}\,.

Since the particle momentum
\mathbf{p}
governing
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{p}-e\mathbf{A}
is independent of the external field, from Eq. (
179
) it follows that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{0}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{0}\,.

(180)

Let us first assume that
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{u}_{\perp}
, and
\mathbf{v}_{\perp}
vanish and show that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{0}
. The nonzero longitudinal projections of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
together with
f
obey Eqs. (
109
). Since
\mathbf{u}_{\perp}=0
, we have
\dot{\mathbf{u}}=\dot{u}_{\parallel}\mathbf{e}+u_{\parallel}\dot{\mathbf{e}}
, and the similar relation holds also for
\dot{\mathbf{v}}
. From Eqs. (
102
) and (
103
) we obtain

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}u_{\parallel}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}v_{\parallel}=\mathbf{0}\,.

(181)

Thus,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{0}
, which was to be demonstrated.

Let us now assume
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{0}
and prove that
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{u}_{\perp}
, and
\mathbf{v}_{\perp}
vanish. For
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{0}
, the system (
100
)–(
103
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{f}&=-2\mu_{2}u_{\parallel}\,,\\
\dot{f}_{\parallel}&=0\,,\\
\dot{u}_{\parallel}&=\mu_{2}(2f-1)+2\omega v_{\parallel}\,,\\
\dot{v}_{\parallel}&=-2\omega u_{\parallel}\,,\\
\dot{\mathbf{f}}_{\perp}&=2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}\times\mathbf{f}_{\perp})-2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}\times\mathbf{v}_{\perp})\,,\\
\dot{\mathbf{u}}_{\perp}&=2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}\times\mathbf{u}_{\perp})+2\omega\mathbf{v}_{\perp}\,,\\
\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{\perp}&=2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{\perp})-2(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}\times\mathbf{f}_{\perp})-2\omega\mathbf{u}_{\perp}\,.\end{aligned}

(182)

Given the initial conditions, the last three equations yield
\mathbf{f}_{\perp}=\mathbf{u}_{\perp}=\mathbf{v}_{\perp}=\mathbf{0}
and the second equation leads to
f_{\parallel}=0
, which completes the proof. The resulting system is exactly that displayed in Eqs. (
109
).

Appendix C
Spin projections of the Wigner function

Here we will derive the operator
\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{(e^{-})}_{s}
, which allows one to extract the number density of the electrons produced with a given value of
s
according to Eq. (
174
). The main idea is to replace
\psi
and
\overline{\psi}
in Eq. (
117
) with their projections onto the corresponding subspace of
u
-bispinors with index
s
. The projection operator has the form

\hat{P}^{(e^{-})}_{s}\psi(t,\mathbf{x})=\int\!d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\int\!\frac{d\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}u_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-e\mathbf{A}(t),s}\psi(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\,.

(193)

One can easily demonstrate that
\hat{P}^{(e^{-})}_{s}
is Hermitian and idempotent. In Eq. (
117
) we now substitute
\psi
for (
193
). Accordingly, the necessary projection of the Wigner function is given by

\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{(e^{-})}_{s}W(\mathbf{p},t)=-\frac{1}{2}\int\!d\mathbf{s}\,\mathrm{e}^{-i[\mathbf{p}+e\mathbf{A}(t)]\mathbf{s}}\big\langle 0,\text{in}\big|\big[\hat{P}^{(e^{-})}_{s}\psi(t,\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{s}/2),\,\overline{\hat{P}^{(e^{-})}_{s}\psi(t,\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}/2)}\big]\big|0,\text{in}\big\rangle\,.

To reexpress the result in terms of
W(\mathbf{p},t)
, one should change the variable
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
193
) via
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{s}^{\prime}/2
together with
\mathbf{x}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{s}^{\prime}/2
in the analogous integral involving the field-operator conjugate. Given the spatial homogeneity of the Wigner function, the integrals over
\mathbf{x}_{0}
,
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}
, and
\mathbf{s}
are easily computed, so we obtain

\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{(e^{-})}_{s}W(\mathbf{p},t)=u_{\mathbf{p},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p},s}\gamma^{0}W(\mathbf{p},t)\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{p},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p},s}\,.

(194)

The final expression turns out to be
\mathbf{x}
-independent as it should be. It is equivalent to

\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{(e^{-})}_{s}\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)=u_{\mathbf{q},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{q},s}\gamma^{0}\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{q},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{q},s}\,.

(195)

According to Eq. (
174
), the electron number density reads

\displaystyle f^{(e^{-})}_{s}(\mathbf{p},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\ \bigg\{\frac{m\mathrm{I}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}\mathbf{q}}{q^{0}}\,u_{\mathbf{q},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{q},s}\gamma^{0}\big[\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{p},t)\big]\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{q},s}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{q},s}\bigg\}

(196)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{0}}\,\mathrm{Tr}\ \Big\{\big(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\mathbf{q}+\beta m\big)u_{\mathbf{q},s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q},s}\big[\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{p},t)\big]\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{q},s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q},s}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\ \Big\{\gamma^{0}u_{\mathbf{q},s}u^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q},s}\big[\tilde{W}(\mathbf{p},t)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{p},t)\big]\Big\}\,,

where we have used Eq. (
36
) and the orthonormality of the bispinors. This result coincides with Eq. (
175
).

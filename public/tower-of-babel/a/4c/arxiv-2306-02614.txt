Two interacting conformal Carroll particles
Roberto Casalbuoni a), Daniele Dominici a), Joaquim Gomis b)

Abstract
In this note we analyse two different models of two interacting conformal Carroll particles that can be obtained as the Carrollian limit of two relativistic conformal particles. The first model describes particles with zero velocity and exhibits infinite dimensional symmetries which are reminiscent of the BMS symmetries. A second model of interaction of Carrollian particles is proposed, where the particles have non zero velocity and therefore, as a consequence of the limit c → 0 c\to 0 , are tachyons. Infinite dimensional symmetries are present also in this model.

I
Introduction

Carroll symmetry, which was introduced by
LevyLeblond:1965
;
gupta1966analogue
as the limit of the
Poincaré symmetry where
the velocity of light is going to zero,
c\to 0
, has recently received a lot of attention, mainly in connection with its relation with the BMS group
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
.
Some applications of
Carrollian physics
allow,
for example, to understand the symmetries of null hypersurfaces, such as black-hole horizons
Donnay:2019jiz
, boundaries of asymptotically flat spacetimes
Duval:2014uva
, Carroll fluids
Ciambelli:2018wre
. For an introductory review of certain aspects of Non-Lorentzian
theories, see
Bergshoeff:2022eog
.

In particular, in this paper we are interested to have a dynamical understanding of the relation between the
conformal Carroll structures
Duval:2014uva
and the symmetries of Carrollian particles.

A (massive) non-conformal Carroll particle
was introduced as Carroll limit of relativistic massive particle

Bergshoeff:2014jla
; it can also be obtained from a coadjoint orbit of the Carroll group
Duval:2014uoa
. The (massless) Carroll particle was also considered in
Bergshoeff:2014jla
.
A model of two non-conformal interacting particles was also considered where the individual particles move but the center of mass does not move.

The symmetry of massive and massless free Carroll particles is infinite dimensional. In the massless case it contains the finite conformal Carroll symmetry introduced in
Bagchi:2016bcd
, see also
Chen:2021xkw
.
In the case of the two non-conformal particles the symmetry algebra is finite dimensional
Bergshoeff:2014jla
.

The causal structure of the Carroll space allows space-like intervals and therefore the existence of Carroll tachyons. In
deBoer:2021jej
a model is constructed where a tachyonic Carroll particle with zero energy moves.

In this paper we propose two models for two conformal Carroll particles as Carrollian limits
of two conformal relativistic particles introduced in
Casalbuoni:2014ofa
.

The first one describes particles which do not move and exhibits an infinite dimensional symmetry algebra.
Any free conformal Carroll particle has vanishing energy, therefore two free Carrollian particles have total energy equal to zero. The nice feature of this model is that the system can have a total energy different from zero and still be Carroll conformal invariant. Clearly the energy, which turns out to be constant, depends on the interaction.
We also study some possible connections with the infinite dimensional conformal symmetry of

Duval:2014uva
and the BMS symmetry.

In the second model the
particles move, their individual energy is zero and consequently they are Carrollian tachyons. Also in this case the symmetry turns out to be infinite dimensional.

II
Conformal Carroll particle

The canonical form of the action of a massless relativistic particle is given by
(we assume the signature of the metric
(+,-,-,-)
)

S=\int d\tau[-p\cdot\dot{x}-\frac{e}{2}p^{2}]\,,

(1)

where the dot denotes the differentiation with respect to an invariant parameter
\tau
.
The action is also invariant under the conformal group that contains dilatations and special conformal transformations (SCT). The infinitesimal transformations are given by the ordinary Poincaré
transformations, plus dilatations

\delta x^{\mu}=\epsilon_{D}x^{\mu},\,\,\,\,\delta p^{\mu}=-\epsilon_{D}p^{\mu},\,\,\,\delta e=2e\epsilon_{D}.

(2)

and SCT:

\delta x^{\mu}=b^{\mu}x^{2}-2(b\cdot x)x^{\mu},\,\,\,\,\delta p^{\mu}=-2b\cdot px^{\mu}+2b\cdot xp^{\mu}+2p\cdot xb^{\mu},

(3)

\delta e=-4eb\cdot x.

(4)

We next consider the Carroll limit which is given by

x^{0}=\frac{t}{\omega},\quad p_{0}=\omega E,

(5)

with
\omega\to\infty
. It is understood that, before taking the limit, we rescale the einbein variable like

\displaystyle e\to\frac{e}{\omega^{2}}.

(6)

Note that we use a dimensionless parameter
\omega
instead of the velocity of light. Indeed, if one considers the Carrollian counterpart of the non-relativistic limit of a string, one needs to use a dimensionless parameter
Gomis:2000bd
;
Gomis:2005pg
;
Barducci:2018wuj
.
The contractions we consider in this work correspond to the limit
c\to 0
of a world probed by particles. There are more general possible contractions that correspond to the non-Lorentzian limits of extended objects such as strings and branes.

In the Carroll limit the action (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle S_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\tau\,\big[-E\dot{t}+{\bf p}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}-\frac{e}{2}E^{2}\big]\,.

(7)

From the symplectic form of the canonical action we deduce the following Poisson brackets

\{E,\ t\}=1,\quad\{e,\ \pi\}=1,\quad\{x^{i},p^{j}\}=\delta^{ij}\,\quad i=1,2,3.

(8)

This action can also be obtained by the method of non-linear realisations
Bergshoeff:2014jla

and coadjoint orbit method

Duval:2014uoa

Since the action (
1

(
7

We have

II.1
Infinite-dimensional Symmetries

Now we want to analyse which is
the most general point transformation of the Carroll particle action (
1
) following the steps of

Bergshoeff:2014jla
.

In order to do that we want to write the Carollian Killing equations. Let us first consider the equations of motion deduced from (
7

(21)

where
\lambda(\tau)
is an arbitrary function and
\pi

variable which is constrained by
\pi=0

Consider the following generator of the canonical transformations

(22)

with
\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)\,,\xi^{i}({\bf x},t)
and

The transformations generated by this generator are given by

\displaystyle\delta t

\displaystyle=\{G,t\}=

\displaystyle\xi^{0}({\bf x},t),\quad\delta x^{i}=\{G,x^{i}\}=\xi^{i}({\bf x},t),\quad\quad\delta e=\{e,G\}=\gamma({\bf x},t)\,,

\displaystyle\delta E

\displaystyle=\{G,E\}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)E+\partial_{t}\xi^{i}({\bf x},t)p_{i}+\partial_{t}\gamma({\bf x},t)\pi\,,

\displaystyle\delta p^{i}

\displaystyle=\{G,p^{i}\}=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)E+\partial_{i}\xi^{j}({\bf x},t)p_{j}+\partial_{i}\gamma({\bf x},t)\pi\,.

(23)

These transformations are symmetries of the free Carroll particle, provided that

is a constant of
motion, i.e.,

.This leads to the following restriction, after use of Eq. (
21
) and
\pi
that is a primary constraint:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=E\left[\dot{t}\partial_{t}\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)+\dot{x_{j}}\partial^{j}\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)\right]+p_{i}\left[\dot{t}\partial_{t}\xi^{i}({\bf x},t)+\dot{x_{j}}\partial^{j}\xi^{i}({\bf x},t)\right]+\dot{\pi}\gamma({\bf x},t)

\displaystyle=-eE^{2}\partial_{t}\xi^{0}({\bf x},t)-eEp_{i}\partial_{t}\xi^{i}({\bf x},t)-\frac{1}{2}\gamma({\bf x},t)E^{2}\,.

(25)

for arbitrary parameter

(26)

particles

In this section we briefly recall the most relevant features of a model of two interacting conformal particles proposed in

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\tau\left(-p_{1}{{\dot{x}}}_{1}-p_{2}\cdot{\dot{x}}_{2}+\pi_{1}{\dot{e}_{1}}+\pi_{2}{\dot{e}_{2}}-H\right)\,,

(27)

where the Hamiltonian

is given by

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e_{1}\phi_{1}-e_{2}\phi_{2}-\pi_{1}\mu_{1}-\pi_{2}\mu_{2}\,,

(28)

\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are arbitrary functions of
\tau
and the mass-shells constraints are

\displaystyle\phi_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{1}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\phi_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{2}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,

(29)

The equations of motion are given by

(30)

(32)

III.1
Killing equations and symmetries

Like in the free particle case we would like to find the most general point transformations of the model. We want to write the corresponding Killing equations for the two particle model. In order to do that we write the most general generator

G=\sum_{a=1}^{2}\left[\xi_{a\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})p_{a}^{\mu}+\gamma_{a}\pi_{a}\right].

(34)

As shown in

\xi_{a}(x_{1},x_{2})=\xi_{a}(x_{a}),\,\,\,a=1,2,

(36)

(37)

(38)

From Eq. (

\tilde{\gamma}_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a\nu}\xi_{a}^{\nu},\,\,\,a=1,2.

(39)

We have verified that the model is invariant under the diagonal subgroup of the two conformal groups

acting on the two variables

(40)

We can now compute the transformation properties of the variables under dilatations and SCT

\delta e_{a}=\{e_{a},G\},\,\,a=1,2.

(42)

IV.1
Eliminating the einbeins

The einbeins are non-dynamical variables and can be eliminated through their equations of motion

In fact from the equations of motion of

\sqrt{\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4E_{1}^{2}{\bf r}^{2}}

{\bf r}
. Introducing the total and the relative energies of the two particles:

E_{T}=E_{1}+E_{2},~~~E_{r}=E_{1}-E_{2}\,,

(65)

we get

E_{T}^{2}=E_{r}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{{\bf r}^{2}}\,.

(66)

This equation shows that the minimum of the total energy is for
E_{1}=E_{2}=0
and for
{\bf r}\to\infty
.

The wave equation associated to the Eq. (
64
) is

[\partial_{t_{1}}\partial_{t_{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4{\bf r}^{2}}]\Phi(t_{1},t_{2},\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=0

(67)

IV.2
Invariance under dilatations and special conformal transformations

The Lagrangian (
51
) is invariant also under the dilatations and special conformal transformations.
Under dilatations we have:

\delta t_{a}=\epsilon_{D}t_{a},\,\,\,\,\delta{\bf x}_{a}=\epsilon_{D}{\bf x}_{a},\,\,\,\delta E_{a}=-\epsilon_{D}E_{a},\delta{\bf p}_{a}=-\epsilon_{D}{\bf p}_{a},\,\,\,\delta e_{a}=2\epsilon_{D}e_{a},\,\,\,a=1,2.

(68)

Under SCT:

\delta t_{a}=-b^{0}{\bf x}_{a}^{2}+2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}\,t_{a},\,\,\,\,\delta{\bf x}_{a}=-{\bf b}\,{\bf x}_{a}^{2}+2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}\,{\bf x}_{a},\,\,\,a=1,2.

(69)

\delta E_{a}=-2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}E_{a},\,\,\,\delta{\bf p}_{a}=-2(b^{0}E_{a}-{\bf b}\cdot{\bf p}_{a}){\bf x}_{a}-2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}\,{\bf p}_{a}+2(E_{a}t_{a}-{\bf p}_{a}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}){\bf b},\,\,

(70)

\delta e_{a}=4{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}e_{a},\,\,,a=1,2.

(71)

These SCT are obtained from the covariant ones given in Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
), considering

x_{a}^{0}=\frac{1}{\omega}t_{a},\,\,\,p^{0}_{a}=\omega E_{a},\,\,\,b^{0}=\frac{b^{0}}{\omega},\,\,\,a=1,2.

(72)

IV.3
Infinite dimensional symmetries

Like in the previous sections, here we would like to find the most general point symmetries of the model.

Let consider the generic Killing vector

G=\sum_{a=1,2}\left[\xi^{0}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})E_{a}-{\bm{\xi}}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\cdot{\bf p}_{a}+\gamma_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\pi_{a}\right]\,.

(73)

By considering the
\tau
derivative we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dG}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,c=1,2}\Big[\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}\dot{t}_{c}E_{a}-\frac{\partial\xi_{a}^{j}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}\dot{t}_{c}p_{a}^{j}-{\bm{\xi}}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\cdot\dot{\bf p}_{a}

(74)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\dot{\pi}_{a}\Big]\,,

where we have used the equations of motion

\dot{E}_{a}=0,\,\,\,\dot{\bf x}_{a}=0\quad

(75)

and the primary constraints
\pi_{a}=0
.
Using
Eqs. (
54
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{dG}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,c=1,2}\Big[\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}e_{c}E_{c}E_{a}-\frac{\partial\xi_{a}^{j}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}e_{c}E_{c}p_{a}^{j}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})E^{2}_{a}\Big]

(76)

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2{\bf r}^{4}}\sqrt{e_{1}e_{2}}({\bm{\xi}}_{1}-{\bm{\xi}}_{2})\cdot{\bf r}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8{\bf r}^{2}}\left[\gamma_{1}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\sqrt{\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}}}+\gamma_{2}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\sqrt{\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}}\right]\,.

Therefore the Killing condition
{dG}/{d\tau}=0
implies

\frac{\partial\xi_{a}^{j}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}=0,\,\,\,a,c=1,2,

(77)

or
\xi_{a}^{j}=\xi_{a}^{j}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})
.
We see that
\delta{\bf x}_{a}\equiv{\bm{\xi}}_{a}
do no depend
on the times
t_{1},t_{2}
.

By defining

\gamma_{a}=e_{a}\tilde{\gamma}_{a}

(78)

from
{dG}/{d\tau}=0
we must have also

\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{a}(t_{1},t_{2},{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})}{\partial t_{c}}=-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ac}\tilde{\gamma}_{a},,\,\,\,a,b,c=1,2,

(79)

and

\frac{\alpha^{2}}{{\bf r}^{2}}({\bm{\xi}}_{1}-{\bm{\xi}}_{2})\cdot{\bf r}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\frac{\delta{{\bf r}^{2}}}{{\bf r}^{2}}=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(\tilde{\gamma}_{1}+\tilde{\gamma}_{2}),\,\,\,a=1,2.

(80)

In the case
\alpha=0
, that is when we turn off the interaction, we see that we have two sets of independent Killing equations, one set for each particle. Therefore the infinite group of symmetry transformations is
{\cal G}_{1}\times{\cal G}_{1}
. When the interaction is on and
\alpha\not=0
, we get:

\frac{1}{{\bf r}^{2}}({\bm{\xi}}_{1}-{\bm{\xi}}_{2})\cdot{\bf r}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta{{\bf r}^{2}}}{{\bf r}^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4}(\tilde{\gamma}_{1}+\tilde{\gamma}_{2}),\,\,\,a=1,2.

(81)

From Eq. (
79
) we have

\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{1}}{\partial t_{2}}=0=\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{2}}{\partial t_{1}}\,.

(82)

Furthermore from Eq. (
81
) we note that since
{\bm{\xi}}_{a}
are only functions of
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
,

\tilde{\gamma}_{a}
depend only on
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
.
In conclusion we obtain

\xi^{0}_{a}\equiv\delta t_{a}=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})t_{a}+h_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}),\quad\delta e_{a}=\gamma_{a}={e_{a}}\tilde{\gamma}_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\,,

(83)

with the extra condition given by Eq. (
81
).

Summarising the Killing generator is given by

G=\sum_{a=1,2}\left[\big(-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})t_{a}+h_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\big)E_{a}-{\bm{\xi}}_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\cdot{\bf p}_{a}+{e_{a}}\tilde{\gamma}_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\pi_{a}\right]\,,

(84)

where
h_{a},{\bm{\xi}}_{a},\tilde{\gamma}_{a}
are arbitrary functions of
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
that must satisfy the condition (
79
).
Therefore the original ten arbitrary functions reduce to nine independent arbitrary functions of
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
.

Therefore the model of two conformal Carroll particles analysed in this note has an infinite dimensional group of symmetries that we will call
{\cal G}_{2}
. Notice that
{\cal G}_{2}\subset{\cal G}_{1}\times{\cal G}_{1}
. The main difference between these two groups is the transformation laws of the times
t_{a}
that in the free case are arbitrary functions of the times and the coordinates, while in the interacting case the Eq. (
79
) restricts the time dependence of the transformations of
\xi^{0}_{a}
to be linear in
t_{a}
.

It is convenient to make a comparison with
Duval:2014uva
.
Let us generalise to two particles the expression for the generators of the conformal Carrollian algebra from
Duval:2014uva
. We consider the diagonal subgroup of the two conformal Carroll groups.

For Carroll dilatations and SCT we have

\xi^{i}_{a}=(\epsilon_{D}+2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a})x^{i}_{a}-b^{i}{\bf x}_{a}^{2}\,,

(85)

\xi^{0}_{a}=(\epsilon_{D}+2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a})t_{a}+T_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})\,.

(86)

We have

\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{a}}{\partial t_{a}}=\epsilon_{D}+2{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{a}

(87)

and

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{1}}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\partial\xi^{0}_{2}}{\partial t_{2}}\right)=\epsilon_{D}+{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{1}+{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{2}

(88)

\displaystyle({\bm{\xi}}_{1}-{\bm{\xi}}_{2})\cdot{\bf r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D}{\bf r}^{2}+2({\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{1}){\bf x}_{1}\cdot{\bf r}-{\bf b}\cdot{\bf r}({\bf x}_{1})^{2}-2({\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{2}){\bf x}_{2}\cdot{\bf r}+{\bf b}\cdot{\bf r}({\bf x}_{2})^{2}

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D}{\bf r}^{2}+{\bf r}^{2}({\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{1}+{\bf b}\cdot{\bf x}_{2})\,.

In conclusion Eq. (
81
) is satisfied by Eq. (
85
) and Eq. (
86
) and the so-called super-translations
T_{a}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})
in our case are arbitrary functions of both particle positions
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
.

V
Two conformal Carroll particles. A tachyonic model.

In this Section, following the approach of
deBoer:2021jej

Gomis:2022spp
, we develop a model describing two interacting Carrollian tachyons. Let us consider the Lagrangian

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1,2}\left[-E_{a}\dot{t}_{a}+{\bf p}{\dot{\bf x}}_{a}-{e_{a}}\phi_{a}-\chi_{a}E_{a}\right]

\displaystyle-E_{1}\dot{t}_{1}+{\bf p}_{1}\cdot{\dot{\bf x}}_{1}-E_{2}\dot{t}_{2}+{\bf p}_{2}\cdot{\dot{\bf x}}_{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{{e}_{1}}{2}\left({\bf p}_{1}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}}}\frac{1}{{\bf r}^{2}}\right)-\frac{{e}_{2}}{2}\left({\bf p}_{2}^{2}-\frac{{\alpha}^{2}}{4}\sqrt{\frac{{e}_{1}}{{e}_{2}}}\frac{1}{{\bf r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\chi_{1}E_{1}-\chi_{2}E_{2}\,.

Notice the presence of the Lagrangian multipliers
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
, that implement
that the energies are zero; these terms are necessary in order to have Carroll invariance. An analogous situation appears for single particle in
Gomis:2022spp
where the action is named the magnetic Carroll particle.
In fact
this Lagrangian is invariant under the Carroll, Eqs. (
52
) and (
53

VI
Conclusions

Carroll symmetries have recently received a lot of attention because of several applications in different domains of theoretical physics, from theory of gravity and strings to studies of fractons in condensed matter.

In this paper, after reexamining the case of a single conformal Carroll particle, we derive the conformal Carroll generators, their algebra and the infinite extension of this algebra
Bergshoeff:2014jla
. Then we propose two different models of Carroll interacting particles that provide dynamical realisations of the Carroll conformal algebra. The models are obtained from the relativistic model of interacting conformal particles proposed in
Casalbuoni:2014ofa
through suitable limits for
c\to 0
.

Concerning the dynamics, the first model describes particles with zero velocity but with non vanishing energy as a consequence of the interaction. Free conformal Carroll particles have zero energy; here, when the interaction is on, the energy of one particle depends on the energy of the second one and on the particle relative distance which is constant.

The second model is a tachyonic one: the particles have non zero velocity and therefore, due to the limit
c\to 0
, they are necessarily tachyons. Nevertheless their energy is vanishing.

Both models exhibit, after a complete analysis of the most general point symmetry transformations, infinite dimensional symmetries which include super-translations as in the case of the BMS group
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
, the symmetry that arises in asymptotically flat space-time at null infinity. These infinite dimensional algebras contain the Carroll conformal one
Duval:2014uva
, which are equivalent to BMS ones.

In the future several directions could be investigated: on the one hand it would be interesting to extend the analysis of the two conformal Carroll models to include
corrections to the next order in
c\to 0
expansion, see for example
Gomis:2022spp
,
on the other hand a deeper analysis of the infinite symmetries could be performed to check similarities with the BMS group.

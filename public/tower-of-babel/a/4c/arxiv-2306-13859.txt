On the existence of two affine-equivalent frameworks with prescribed edge lengths in Euclidean d d -space
Victor Alexandrov

Abstract
. We study the problem of existence of two affine-equivalent bar-and-joint frameworks in Euclidean d d -space which have prescribed combinatorial structure and edge lengths. We prove that theoretically this problem is always solvable, but we cannot propose any practical algorithm for its solution. Keywords : Euclidean d d -space, graph, bar-and-joint framework, affine-equivalent frameworks, Cayley–Menger determinant, Cauchy rigidity theorem. Mathematics subject classification (2010) : 52C25, 05C62, 68U05.

Introduction

Given a graph
G
, we denote by
E(G)
and
V(G)
the sets of its edges
and vertices respectively.

A
bar-and-joint framework
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geqslant 1
,
is a graph
G
and a mapping
{\bf p}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which assigns a
point
{\bf p}_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to each vertex
i
of
G
and is such that

{\bf p}_{i}\neq{\bf p}_{j}
for all
(i,j)\in E(G)
.
The mapping
\bf{p}
is called a
configuration
;
the point
{\bf p}_{i}
is called its
node
; and
the straight-line segment with end-points
{\bf p}_{i}
,
{\bf p}_{j}
is called
a
bar
provided that
(i,j)
is an edge of
G
.
A bar-and-joint framework is denoted by
G(\bf{p})
.
This terminology and notation is standard in the rigidity theory of
bar-and-joint frameworks, see, e. g.,
[
5
]
and references given there.

In this article, we treat the configuration
\bf{p}
as a geometric realization of
G
and often abbreviate a “bar-and-joint framework” to a “framework”.

We address ourselves to the following problem:

Problem 1
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
be a natural number, let
G
be a graph, and
let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}:E(G)\to(0,+\infty)
be two functions
which assign real numbers
\lambda_{ij}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{ij}
to each
edge
(i,j)
of
G
.
Do there exist two frameworks
G(\bf{p})
and
G(\bf{p^{\prime}})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that

(a)
|{\bf p}_{i}-{\bf p}_{j}|=\lambda_{ij}
for every
(i,j)\in E(G)
,

(b)
|{\bf p^{\prime}}_{\!\!i}-{\bf p^{\prime}}_{\!\!j}|=\lambda^{\prime}_{ij}

for every
(i,j)\in E(G)
,

(c)
G(\bf{p})
and
G(\bf{p^{\prime}})
are affine-equivalent,
i.e., that there is an affine transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

A({\bf p}_{i})={\bf p^{\prime}}_{\!\!i}
for every
i\in V(G)
,

(d)
\operatorname{aff}({\bf p}(G))=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

\operatorname{aff}({\bf p}(G))
is the affine hull of the set

{\bf p}(G)=\{{\bf p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:i\in V(G)\}
?

In other words, Problem
1
asks if we can find two affine-equivalent
geometric realizations of
G
with prescribed lengths of bars.

Problem
1
generalizes the problem of recognition of affine-equivalent
polyhedra in Euclidean 3-space via their natural developments.
The latter is studied in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and is motivated by the
Cauchy Rigidity Theorem
[
1
]
.
The arguments used in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
rely significantly on the structure
of polyhedral surfaces in Euclidean 3-space and, thus, cannot be directly
applied to the study of Problem
1
.
In this article, we propose an algorithm that solves Problem
1
for any input
data, i. e., for any dimension
d
, any graph
G
, and any sets of prescribed
lengths
\{\lambda_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in E(G)}
,
\{\lambda^{\prime}_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in E(G)}

of the bars.
The algorithm proposed give the assurance that Problem
1
is always solvable,
rather than provides us with an effective tool for its practical solution.

Prior results

If
X
is a set, we denote by

the cardinality of

.
If

A

\operatorname{cmd}(Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&1&1&\dots&1\\
1&0&{\rho}^{2}_{01}&\dots&{\rho}^{2}_{0k}\\
1&{\rho}^{2}_{10}&0&\dots&{\rho}^{2}_{1k}\\
.&.&.&.&.\\
1&{\rho}^{2}_{k0}&{\rho}^{2}_{k1}&\dots&0\end{array}\right|.

(1)

The

) is called the

.

Suppose

Auxiliary result

Suppose

Define the metric

In this Section, our goal is to learn to distinguish between the following three cases

(A)

{\bf p}_{0}
and
{\bf p}_{d+1}
lie in the same open half-space

\operatorname{aff}(\Delta)

(B)

{\bf p}_{0}
and
{\bf p}_{d+1}
lie in
\operatorname{aff}(\Delta)

(C)

{\bf p}_{0}
and
{\bf p}_{d+1}
lie in different open half-spaces bounded by

\operatorname{aff}(\Delta)

Observe that
\operatorname{cmd}(K_{d+1})
is a quadratic polynomial with respect
to the variable
t=\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}
, whose coefficients are polynomials
with respect to
\rho_{ij}
,
\{i,j\}\neq\{0,d+1\}
.
Denote this polynomial as

.
It is straightforward to see that

.
Then, from (
2
) it follows that

U=(-1)^{d-1}2^{d-1}(d-1)![\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(\Delta)]^{2}
.
Hence,

U>0
if

is odd.

Lemma 1
.

Using the notation introduced above, we can assert that

\bullet
the case (A) holds true if and only if

(-1)^{d}(2U\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}+V)>0,

(3)

\bullet
the case (B) holds true if and only if

2U\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}+V=0,

(4)

\bullet
the case (C) holds true if and only if

(-1)^{d}(2U\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}+V)<0.

(5)

Proof.

Since
(K_{d+2},\rho)
embeds isometrically in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the condition (iv) of Theorem
1
holds true.
Hence,

is a root of
Ut^{2}+Vt+W
.

Suppose the case (A) holds true. Then,

has one more
positive root

, which corresponds to the configuration obtained from

{\bf p}
by replacement of
{\bf p}_{d+1}
by its image under the reflection
in the hyperplane
\operatorname{aff}(\Delta)
. This implies

.
Thus,

Arguing backwards, we see that (

Suppose the case (B) holds true. Then,

is a root of multiplicity 2 of

2\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}=2t_{1}=t_{1}+t_{2}=-V/U
, i. e., (

) holds true.
Arguing backwards, we see that (
4
) implies (B).

At last, suppose the case (C) holds true. Then, as before,

has one more

{\bf p}
by replacement of
{\bf p}_{d+1}

\operatorname{aff}(\Delta)

Thus,
2\rho^{2}_{0,d+1}=2t_{1}>t_{1}+t_{2}=-V/U

5
) holds true.
Arguing backwards, we see that (
5
) implies (C).
∎

.
If

5.
Final remarks

Theorem
2
allows us to solve not only Problem
1
,
but also a number of related problems.
For example, in order to solve Problem
1
without condition (d)
we should apply Theorem
2
to every
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
1\leqslant m<d
.
Another example is when we are given a framework
G(\bf{p})
and want
to know if there exists another geometric realization of the same graph
G
which has
prescribed edge lengths
\lambda^{\prime}_{ij}
and is affine-equivalent to
G(\bf{p})
.
In this case, according to Theorem
2
we should solve the
system (
6
)–(
12
) treating
z_{ij}
as prescribed real numbers
produced by
G(\bf{p})
.

According to Theorem
2
, the positive solution of Problem
1

is equivalent to the existence of a solution to the system of polynomial equalities
and inequalities (
6
)–(
12
) in the set of real numbers.
It follows from the Tarski–Seidenberg Theorem
[
7
, Corollary 3.3.18]

that unkown variables
z_{ij}
and
z^{\prime}_{ij}
,
(i,j)\notin E(G)
,
can be eliminated from (
6
)–(
12
)
so that the solvability of this system is equivalent to the validity of some
quantifier-free Boolean formula whose atomic formulas are polynomial equations and inequalities containing only the lengths of bars

\lambda_{ij}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{ij}
,
(i,j)\in E(G)
.
For this reason, we say that theoretically Problem
1
is always solvable.
However, the computational complexity of currently known algorithms for elimination
of variables is so high that they cannot be used in practice.
For this reason, we say that we cannot propose any practical algorithm for solution
of Problem
1
.

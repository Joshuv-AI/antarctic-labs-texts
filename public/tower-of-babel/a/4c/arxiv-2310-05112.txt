. The
complete elliptic integrals
of the first and second kinds
[
6
, Sect. 1.3]
are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}(k):=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathbf{E}(k):=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\theta.

The argument
k
indicated above is referred to as the
modulus
, and it is often convenient to write
k^{\prime}:=\sqrt{1-k^{2}}
to denote the
complementary modulus
, so that we may write, accordingly,
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}(k):=\mathbf{K}(k^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{E}^{\prime}(k):=\mathbf{E}(k^{\prime})
. By virtue of
Jacobi’s imaginary quadratic transformations
, the two complete elliptic integrals satisfy the following modular equations
[
6
, p. 72, Exs. 7–8]
:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}\left(i\frac{k}{k^{\prime}}\right)=k^{\prime}\mathbf{K}(k)\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathbf{E}\left(i\frac{k}{k^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}\mathbf{E}(k).

Furthermore, the differential equation such that

(2.6)

\frac{d\mathbf{K}}{dk}=\frac{\mathbf{E}-\left(k^{\prime}\right)^{2}\mathbf{K}}{k\left(k^{\prime}\right)^{2}}

is to be used in a key way in our work.

Next, for a positive argument
r
, the
elliptic lambda function

\lambda^{\ast}(r)
is defined so that
\lambda^{\ast}(r):=k_{r}
, where
0<k_{r}<1
is such that
\frac{\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{K}}(k_{r})=\sqrt{r}

[
6
, p. 67, eq. (3.2.2)]
. The function
\lambda^{\ast}
may equivalently be defined through the use of Ramanujan’s
G
-function
[
22
]
such that

(2.7)

\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1+e^{-(2k+1)\pi\sqrt{n}}\right)=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi\sqrt{n}/24}G_{n},

and the
G
-function in (
2.7
)
is to be heavily involved in our proofs of Aldawoud’s conjectures.

The
elliptic alpha function
is such that

\alpha(r):=\frac{\mathbf{E}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{K}}-\frac{\pi}{4\mathbf{K}^{2}}

[
6
, p. 152, eq. (5.1.1)]
,
under the modulus
k=\lambda^{\ast}(r)
, and we may rewrite this as
[
6
, p. 152, eq. (5.1.2)]
:

(2.8)

\alpha(r)=\frac{\pi}{4\mathbf{K}^{2}}-\sqrt{r}\left(\frac{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{K}}-1\right).

We adopt a notational convention from
[
2
]
by setting

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{n}^{3}}{\left(1\right)_{n}^{3}},\qquad B_{n}:=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)_{n}}{\left(1\right)_{n}^{3}},\qquad C_{n}:=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)_{n}}{\left(1\right)_{n}^{3}}.

We also set
D_{n}:=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}}{\left(1\right)_{n}^{3}}
.
A hypergeometric identity of fundamental importance in the study of Ramanujan-type series is such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}=\frac{4\mathbf{K}^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-x}}{2}}\right)}{\pi^{2}}.

This relation is highlighted in a different form in
[
6
, p. 180, Theorem 5.7(a), eq. (i)]
. The generating function of
B_{n}
is of
great importance in the quartic theory of elliptic functions,
and we recall the following equivalent formulation of
[
6
, p. 181, Theorem 5.7(b), eq. (iv)]
:

(2.9)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}x^{n}=\frac{4\sqrt{2}\,\mathbf{K}^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-x}}{x}}}{\sqrt{2}}}\right)}{\pi^{2}\sqrt[4]{2\sqrt{1-x}+2-x}}.

According to
[
6
, p. 181, Theorem 5.7(c), eq. (vi)]
,

can be stated as

(2.10)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{4-x}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\left(\frac{27x^{2}}{(4-x)^{3}}\right)^{n}.

Another specialization of Bailey’s transformation
[
6
, p. 181, eq. (5.5.9)]
tells us that

(2.11)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\left(-\frac{27x}{(1-4x)^{3}}\right)^{n}.

Furthermore, a limiting case of the

, as given by squaring
[

, provides a connection between the generating functions of

and

:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}\big(4y(1-y)\big)^{n}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{9-8y}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\left(\frac{64y^{3}(1-y)}{(9-8y)^{3}}\right)^{n}.

Theorem 3.2
.

[

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\pi}=&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}}{\left(1\right)_{n}^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{5719+13\sqrt{19}}{2250}n+\frac{1654+133\sqrt{19}}{6750}\Bigg)\times\\

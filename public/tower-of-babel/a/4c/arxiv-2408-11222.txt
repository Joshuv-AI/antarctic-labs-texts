Semiclassical estimates for the magnetic Schrödinger operator on the line
Andrés Larraín-Hubach, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
. We prove a weighted Carleman estimate for a class of one-dimensional, self-adjoint Schrödinger operators P ⁡ ( h ) P(h) with low regularity electric and magnetic potentials, where h > 0 h>0 is a semiclassical parameter. The long range part of either potential has bounded variation. The short range part of the magnetic potential belongs to L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) ∩ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , while the short range part of the electric potential is a finite signed measure. The proof is a one dimensional instance of the energy method, which is used to prove Carleman estimates in higher dimensions and in more complicated geometries. The novelty of our result lies in the weak regularity assumptions on the coefficients. As a consequence of the Carleman estimate, we establish optimal limiting absorption resolvent estimates for P ⁡ ( h ) P(h) . We also present standard applications to the distribution of resonances for P ⁡ ( 1 ) P(1) and to associated evolution equations.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

The goal of this paper is to prove sharp limiting absorption resolvent estimates for one dimensional magnetic Schrödinger operators with low regularity coefficients. More precisely, we consider operators of the form

(1.1)

\begin{split}P(h)&=\beta(x,h)(-h^{2}\partial_{x}(\alpha(x,h)\partial_{x})+hb(x,h)D_{x}+hD_{x}b(x,h))\\
&+V(x,h):L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx),\qquad h>0,\end{split}

with real valued coefficients, where
D_{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-i\partial_{x}
and
h
is a semiclassical parameter.

First, we fix the assumptions on the coefficients in (
1.1
). The electric potential
V
and magnetic potential
b
may depend on
h
and they decompose

(1.2)

\displaystyle V(x,h)=V_{0}(x,h)+V_{1}(x,h),

(1.3)

\displaystyle b(x,h)=b_{0}(x,h)+b_{1}(x,h),

where

(1.4)

V_{0}
is a finite signed Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle b_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

and

(1.6)

V_{1},\,b_{1}
have bounded variation (BV),

in the sense that each is a difference of bounded nondecreasing functions. As for the coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
, which may also depend on
h
, we suppose

(1.7)

\alpha,\,\beta:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
have BV,

and for all
h>0
,

(1.8)

\inf\alpha(\cdot,h),\,\inf\beta(\cdot,h)>0.

Recall that the distributional derivative of a BV function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a finite signed Borel measure, which we denote by
df
. We review this and other standard facts about BV functions in Section
2
.

Prior to studying resolvent estimates, it is important to describe a domain on which (
1.1
) is self-adjoint. Self-adjointness for large classes of Sturm-Liouville operators with singular coefficients was addressed systematically in
[
HrMy01
,
HrMy12
,
EcTe13
,
LSW24
]
. In
[
AlEy12
]
, self-adjointness of (
1.1
) was shown in the case
\alpha=\beta=1
,
V_{1}=b_{0}=0
, and
V_{0},b_{1},db_{1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We approach this task from an elementary viewpoint, using the calculus of BV functions and
L^{2}
-based estimates to characterize the realization of (
1.1
) stemming from the sesquilinear form

q(u,v)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\overline{u}^{\prime}v^{\prime}dx+ih\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(v\overline{u}^{\prime}-\overline{u}v^{\prime})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{c}v_{c}\beta^{-1}V_{0},

defined on the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. In Appendix
A
, we show (
1.1
) is self-adjoint with respect to

(1.9)

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{D}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}):h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu\text{ belongs to $L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$, has locally bounded variation, and}\\
\beta d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)+h\beta bD_{x}u+u_{c}V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\},\end{gathered}

where
u_{c}
denotes the unique continuous representative of
u
. For each
u\in\mathcal{D}
,
P(h)u\in L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)
is then defined distributionally by

(1.10)

P(h)u\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\beta d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)+h\beta bD_{x}u+u_{c}V.

Our principal result is a Carleman estimate for (
1.1

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
s>1/2
and
E_{\max},\,h_{0},\,\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Let the semiclassical parameter

vary in
(0,h_{0}]
. Suppose
\{E(h)\}_{h\in(0,h_{0}]}\subseteq[-E_{\max},E_{\max}]

\{\varepsilon(h)\}_{h\in(0,h_{0}]}\subseteq[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]
are families that may depend on

. Let
P(h)
be given by (
1.1
), with coefficients satisfying (
1.2
) through (
1.8
), and equipped with domain
\mathcal{D}
as in (
1.9
).

Suppose further that for each
h\in(0,h_{0}]
, there exists
R_{1}(h)\geq 0
so that

(1.11)

\inf_{|x|\geq R_{1}(h)}(\beta^{-1}(x,h)\alpha(x,h)(E(h)-V_{1}(x,h))+b^{2}_{1}(x,h))>0.

Fix a phase
\varphi=\varphi(\cdot,h)\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,\infty))

\varphi(0)=0

\varphi\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty)

(1.12)

\partial_{x}\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}k(h),&0<x\leq R_{1}(h),\\
0&x>2R_{1}(h),\end{cases}

where

(1.13)

\tau=\tau(h)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}((\beta^{-1}(x,h)\alpha(x,h)(E(h)-V_{1}(x,h))+\alpha^{2}(x,h)(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2}(x,h)+b^{2}_{1}(x,h))>0.

For each

depending on

, and the coefficients of (

with
\langle x\rangle^{2s}(P(h)-E(h)-i\varepsilon(h))v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle x\rangle^{-2s}(|e^{\varphi(x)/h}v|^{2}&+|(h\alpha\partial_{x}+ib)e^{\varphi(x)/h}v|^{2})dx\\

where
\langle x\rangle\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}

Remark 1.2
.

In one dimension, a change of variable can transform a first order perturbation into a zeroth order perturbation, reducing the proof of estimates like (
1.14
) to the case
P(h)=-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{x}+V
. But the most general transformation the authors know of
[
D’AnFa06
, Proposition 1.1]
requires the higher order coefficients to be absolutely continuous and have short range derivatives.

Remark 1.3
.

If
V_{1}(\cdot,h)
exhibits “long range” decay, i.e.,
\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}V_{1}(x,h)=0
, then (
1.11
) is satisfied for any
E(h)>0
. Hence (
1.14
) with
\varepsilon(h)=0
implies

has no positive eigenvalues. We can also rule out eigenvalues

for which (
1.11
) holds. Absence of positive eigenvalues for Schrödinger operators with locally
H^{-1}
potentials that have
L^{1}
decay was proved in
[
LSW24
]
.

If the coefficients depend on
h
in a uniform way, one can better track constants in the estimates leading to (
1.14
). Thus
C(h)
may be specified more precisely, yielding sharp exterior and exponential limiting absorption resolvent estimates.

In the sequel,
|\mu|
stands for the total variation of a finite signed Borel measure
\mu=\mu(h)
which may depend on
h
. We put
\|\mu\|\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\mu|(\mathbb{R})
. Recall that
|\mu|
is defined as the sum of the positive and negative variations of
\mu
, as supplied by the Jordan decomposition theorem
[
Fo99

.

.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem

\|V_{1}(\cdot,h)\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|dV_{1}(h)\|
,

Note that (

(5)

\|V_{0}(h)\|

Remark 1.7
.

A feature of Corollaries
1.4
and
1.6
, which is a little bit stronger than typical semiclassical estimates, is that given
any

h_{0}>0
, rather than just
some

small enough, the bounds (

) and (

Estimates like (
1.17
) and (
1.18
) were proved previously for
\alpha,\,\beta=1
,
V_{1},\,b_{0},\,b_{1}=0

[

]
. Thus, the novelty of our present work is that (

The program of establishing optimal semiclassical estimates was initiated by Burq
[
Bu98
,
Bu02
]
, where he proved an exponential bound like (

[
Bu02
]
, and then refined by Cardoso and Vodev
[
CaVo02
]
. Subsequent works have reduced the regularity and decay needed to have optimal bounds, see e.g.
[
Vo13
,
Da14
,
DadH16
,
Sh19
,
Vo20c
,
GaSh22b
,
Ob24
]
. Of particular salience to the present work is Vodev’s recent paper
[
Vo25
]
, which establishes optimal semiclassical estimates in dimension three and higher for Schrödinger operators with long range electric and magnetic potentials.

To obtain (
1.17
) and (
1.18
) in dimension greater than one, the proofs usually require the long range coefficients to be Lipschitz continuous in the radial variable. For short range,
L^{\infty}
electric or magnetic potentials, only versions of (
1.18
) with additional losses are known
[
KlVo19
,
Vo19
,
Vo20a
,
Vo20b
,
Sh20
,
GaSh22a
,
Sh24
,
Vo25
]

If each of
V_{0}
,

and

are supported in

in (

gave examples of smooth, compactly supported potentials

The proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section

is structured as a positive commutator-type argument in the context of the so-called energy method. This strategy has long been used to prove Carleman and related estimates
[

,
KlVo19
,

,

,

(1.19)

\begin{gathered}F(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|(h\alpha\partial_{x}+ib)u|^{2}+(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2}+b_{1}^{2})|u|^{2},\\
u=e^{\varphi/h}v,\qquad v=(P(h)-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).\end{gathered}

Remark 1.8
.

In the special case
\alpha,\beta=1
,
V_{1},b_{0},b_{1}=0
, and
\varphi=0
, the functional (
1.19
) simplifies to the one used in
[
LaSh24
]
.

The goal is to specify a weight
w(x)
having locally bounded variation, so that
d(wF)
is bounded from below by

(1.20)

-h^{-2}w|e^{\varphi/h}f|^{2}+2\varepsilon h^{-1}\beta^{-1}w\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha\partial_{x}u+ibu})u)+\langle x\rangle^{-2s}(|u|^{2}+|(h\alpha\partial_{x}+ib)u|^{2}),

plus a remainder, see (
3.14
). The condition (
1.11
) allows us to obtain the third term in (
1.20
). Our placement of
b
and
b_{1}
in (
1.19
) takes advantage of the characterization (
1.9
) of the domain, and respects the symmetry of the first order part of (
1.1

, several convenient cancellations occur, namely (
3.4
), and terms involving

V_{0}
appear (see (
3.5

V_{0}
term is part of the remainder because it is left out of (

The total remainder we incur upon computing
d(wF)
can be thought of as
-\mu
for the positive, finite measure
\mu
given by (

). The most concerning feature of

is its discrete part

, stemming from the point masses of

,

,

,

, and

\mu_{d}
has at most countably many point masses which are absolutely summable. Thus

can be compensated for by choosing a weight

that has an extra “regularization”. As in
[
LaSh24
]
,

depends on an additional parameter

In higher dimensions, there are strict requirements on the type of weight that can be used in the positive-commutator argument, see
[

1.1.
Applications

In Section
5
we present several standard applications of our results when
V
,
b
,
1-\alpha
, and
1-\beta
are independent of
h
and have compact support. In this setting, Theorem

is well known to have consequences for the imaginary parts of the scattering resonances for

(1.21)

H\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\beta(x)(-\partial_{x}(\alpha(x)\partial_{x})+b(x)D_{x}+D_{x}b(x))+V(x).

As in
[
SjZw91
]
, we define the resonances of

as the poles of the cutoff resolvent

(1.22)

\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D},\qquad\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,1]),

which continues meromorphically from

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
V(x)
,
b(x)
,
\alpha(x)
and
\beta(x)
(independent of
h
) are as in (
1.2
) through (
1.8
). Suppose also that
V
,
b
,
1-\alpha
, and
1-\beta
are supported in
[-R_{0},R_{0}]

. Fix

, as well as

so that for all

and

(1.23)

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{k_{1}}\to H^{k_{2}}}\leq C|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{k_{2}-k_{1}-1},\qquad k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0,\,1\}\,(H^{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\|Hu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}})^{1/2}

.

Since the hypotheses of Theorem

having a nonzero real resonance.

In higher dimensions, high frequency resolvent bounds similar to (

) and (

) were proved for Schrödinger operators with

electric and magnetic potentials
[
Vo14b
,
MPS20
]
. See also
[

,

]
. An exponential high frequency resolvent bound for smooth potentials on non-compact Riemannian manifolds was recently proved in
[

]
, extending results in
[

]
.

The existence of resonance free regions is a long-studied problem:
[

,
Zw87
,
Hi99
]
treat the case of an electric potential only, with

,

, and

Our proof of Theorem

[

, Section 5]

Remark 1.12

In Appendix

The proof of Corollary

) and (

Suppose

x\in\mathbb{R}

f^{L}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f(x-\delta),\qquad f^{R}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f(x+\delta),\qquad f^{A}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(f^{L}(x)+f^{R}(x))/2,

be the left- and right-hand limits, and average value of
f
, respectively. Recall that
f
is differentiable Lebesgue almost everywhere, so
f(x)=f^{L}(x)=f^{R}(x)=f^{A}(x)
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We may decompose
f
as

f=f_{r,+}-f_{r,-}+i(f_{i,+}-f_{i,-}),

where the
f_{\sigma,\pm}
,
\sigma\in\{r,i\}
, are increasing functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Each
f^{R}_{\sigma,\pm}
uniquely determines a regular Borel measure
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{1},x_{2}]=f^{R}_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{2})-f^{R}_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{1})
, see
[
Fo99
, Theorem 1.16]
. We put

(2.1)

df\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mu_{r,+}-\mu_{r,-}+i(\mu_{i,+}-\mu_{i,-}),

which is a complex measure when restricted to any bounded Borel subset. For any
a<b
,

(2.2)

\begin{gathered}\int_{(a,b]}df=f^{R}(b)-f^{R}(a),\\
\int_{(a,b)}df=f^{L}(b)-f^{R}(a).\end{gathered}

Proposition 2.1

(product rule)
.

Let
f,\,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be functions of locally bounded variation. Then

(2.3)

d(fg)=f^{A}dg+g^{A}df

as measures on a bounded Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2.2

(chain rule)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and have locally bounded variation. Then, as measures on a bounded Borel set of
\mathbb{R}
,

(2.4)

d(e^{f})=e^{f}df.

Proposition 2.3

(fundamental theorem of calculus)
.

Let
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}
,
\sigma\in\{r,\,i\}
be positive Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
which are finite on all bounded Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
has distributional derivative equal to
\mu=\mu_{r,+}-\mu_{r,-}+i(\mu_{i,+}-\mu_{i,-})
. Then
u
is of locally BV. For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
u
differs by a constant from the right continuous, locally BV function

(2.5)

f_{\mu}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}\int_{[a,x]}d\mu&x\geq a,\\
-\int_{(x,a)}d\mu&x<a.\end{cases}

Our calculations are facilitated by our characterization (

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the following calculations, given

as in (

), we work with fixed representatives of

and
h\alpha v^{\prime}+ibv
such that

and

are continuous and of locally bounded variation, respectively. This is permitted by (

and

on a set of Lebesgue measure zero, we may suppose without loss of generality that

too.

We compute

, as a measure on a bounded Borel subset, using (
2.3
):

(3.1)

\begin{split}dF&-|u|^{2}d(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})\\
&=2\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})d(h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu))+2(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})\mathop{\rm Re}(\overline{u}^{\prime}u)\\
&=-2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu))\\

Now expand

by using (
2.3
),
u=e^{\varphi/h}v
,
u^{\prime}=h^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}u+e^{\varphi/h}v^{\prime}
:

(3.2)

\begin{split}d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)&=d(e^{\varphi/h}(-h^{2}\alpha v^{\prime}-ihbv)-h\alpha\varphi^{\prime}u)\\

Into the right side of (

\begin{split}d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)&=e^{\varphi/h}(\beta^{-1}P(h)v-hbD_{x}v-\beta^{-1}vV)-2h\alpha\varphi^{\prime}u^{\prime}-ib\varphi^{\prime}u\\
&+\alpha(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}u-h\alpha^{A}ud(\varphi^{\prime})-h(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}ud\alpha\\
&=e^{\varphi/h}\beta^{-1}P(h)v-\beta^{-1}uV+ihbu^{\prime}-2h\alpha\varphi^{\prime}u^{\prime}\\
&+\alpha(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}u-h\alpha^{A}ud(\varphi^{\prime})-2ib\varphi^{\prime}u-h(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}ud\alpha.\end{split}

Inserting (
3.3
) into the right side of (

) yields

\begin{split}dF&-|u|^{2}d(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})\\
&=-2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(e^{\varphi/h}\beta^{-1}P(h)v-\beta^{-1}uV))\\
&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(-ihbu^{\prime}+2h\alpha\varphi^{\prime}u^{\prime}-\alpha(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}u+h\alpha^{A}ud(\varphi^{\prime})+2ib\varphi^{\prime}u+h(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}ud\alpha))\\
&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(\beta^{-1}(E-V_{1})+\alpha(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})u))+2h^{-1}\alpha^{-1}b_{1}^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u).\end{split}

Now we simplify terms where convenient. In particular,

(3.4)

\begin{split}-2&h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(ihbu^{\prime}))+2h^{-1}\alpha^{-1}b_{1}^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\
&=-2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(ib\alpha^{-1}(h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu)+\alpha^{-1}b^{2}u)+2h^{-1}\alpha^{-1}b_{1}^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u\\

We also add and subtract the term

. The output is

\begin{split}dF&-|u|^{2}d(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})\\
&=-2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})e^{\varphi/h}\beta^{-1}(P(h)-E-i\varepsilon)v)+2\varepsilon h^{-1}\beta^{-1}\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\
&+4h\alpha^{2}\varphi^{\prime}|u^{\prime}|^{2}-4\alpha b\varphi^{\prime}\mathop{\rm Im}(\overline{u}^{\prime}u)\\
&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})(h\alpha^{A}ud(\varphi^{\prime})+2ib\varphi^{\prime}u+h(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}ud\alpha)\\
&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)(\alpha^{-1}(b_{1}^{2}-b^{2})+\beta^{-1}V_{0}).\end{split}

Using

and the identity

\begin{split}-4\alpha b\varphi^{\prime}\mathop{\rm Im}(\overline{u}^{\prime}u)&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})2ib\varphi^{\prime}u)\\
&=-4h^{-1}b^{2}\varphi^{\prime}|u|^{2}-8h^{-1}b\varphi^{\prime}\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u),\end{split}

we arrive at

\begin{split}dF&-|u|^{2}d(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})\\
&=-2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})e^{\varphi/h}\beta^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f)+2\varepsilon h^{-1}\beta^{-1}\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\
&+4h\alpha^{2}\varphi^{\prime}|u^{\prime}|^{2}-4h^{-1}b^{2}\varphi^{\prime}|u|^{2}-8h^{-1}b\varphi^{\prime}\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\
&+2h^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)(h\alpha^{A}d(\varphi^{\prime})+h(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}d\alpha+\alpha^{-1}(b_{1}^{2}-b^{2})+\beta^{-1}V_{0}).\end{split}

Momentarily, we shall define a continuous weight

which is bounded, has locally BV,

&=(|h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu|^{2})^{A}dw+(\beta^{-1}\alpha(E-V_{1})+\alpha^{2}(\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+b_{1}^{2})^{A}|u|^{2}dw\\
&-2h^{-1}w\mathop{\rm Re}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})e^{\varphi/h}\langle x\rangle^{-s}f)+2\varepsilon h^{-1}\beta^{-1}w\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\
&+4h\alpha^{2}\varphi^{\prime}w|u^{\prime}|^{2}-4h^{-1}b^{2}\varphi^{\prime}w|u|^{2}-8h^{-1}b\varphi^{\prime}w\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u)\\

To find a lower bound for

, we discard the term

be the nonnegative, finite measure

\begin{split}\mu=\mu(h)&\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h^{-1}|\alpha^{-1}(b_{1}^{2}-b^{2})+\beta^{-1}V_{0}|+|\alpha^{A}d(\varphi^{\prime})|+|(\varphi^{\prime})^{A}d\alpha|+4h^{-1}|\varphi^{\prime}|(b^{2}+|b|)\\

where

(3.8)

w(x)=w_{\eta}(x)=-e^{q_{1,\eta}(x)}(e^{q_{2}(x)}-1)\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)+e^{q_{1,\eta}(x)}(e^{q_{2}(x)}-1)\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x),

Here,

-1&x<0,\end{cases}

\kappa\geq 1
and summable

will be chosen in due course, independent of
\eta
.

is bounded from above independent of

since for all

For later use, we put

By (

) and (

\end{split}

Substituting (
3.12
) into the right side (

), we conclude, for

to be chosen,

\begin{split}d(wF)&\geq|u|^{2}(\kappa\tau\langle x\rangle^{-2s}+(\kappa\tau-1)|w|\mu_{c})+|h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu|^{2}((\kappa-1)\langle x\rangle^{-2s}+(\kappa-1)|w|\mu_{c})\\
&+|w||u|^{2}\sum_{x_{j}\neq 0}(\tau\pi^{-1/2}\eta^{-1}W_{j}e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}-(1+\gamma_{j}^{-1})\mu_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}\big)\\

&-h^{-2}|w||e^{\varphi/h}f|^{2}+2\varepsilon h^{-1}\beta^{-1}w\mathop{\rm Im}((\overline{h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu})u).\end{split}

Note the

arise from using Young’s inequality at the point masses of

The summations appearing in lines two and three do not include the point mass that

may have at zero, since

vanishes there.

Now, fix

so that (

\begin{split}d(wF)&\geq\langle x\rangle^{-2s}|u|^{2}+\langle x\rangle^{-2s}|h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu|^{2}\\

To proceed, we integrate (
3.14
). because

\begin{split}\int&\langle x\rangle^{-2s}(|u|^{2}+|(h\alpha\partial_{x}+ib)u|^{2})dx\\

Estimate

\begin{split}\int\alpha|hv^{\prime}|^{2}&=\mathop{\rm Re}\langle(P(h)-E-i\varepsilon)v,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)}+2h\mathop{\rm Im}\int b\overline{v}^{\prime}v+\int\beta^{-1}|v|^{2}(E-V)\\

&+\big(\tfrac{\|V_{0}\|}{\inf\beta}+\tfrac{4\|b_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{\inf\alpha}\big)\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

Using (

) once more to bound the factor

in the last line of (

), we get a bound for

in terms of integrals of
|f|^{2}
and

. Combining this with (

), (

), and

implies

which concludes the proof of (

).

&\leq\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+\|[-\partial^{2}_{x},\chi]\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|\chi((H-\lambda^{2})+\lambda^{2})(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|[-\partial^{2}_{x},\chi]\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{2}\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)\|f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This implies (
1.24
) for
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0},\,0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1
, and that the continued resolvent
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D}
has no poles in
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
(since
\lambda_{0}>0
is arbitrary).

The last operator norm bound we prove is

(5.15)

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{1}\to L^{2}}\leq C|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-1},\qquad|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0},\,0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1.

For this, we employ the same notation as in (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), except now we suppose
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

From the proof of Lemma
A.1
, the form domain of
(H,\mathcal{D})
is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, so there exists a sequence
f_{k}\in\mathcal{D}
converging to
f
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and corresponding functions
v_{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f_{k}
converging to
v
in
(\mathcal{D},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}})
. Since
Hv_{k}=(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}H\chi f_{k}
,

(5.16)

\begin{split}\|\chi Hv\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\chi Hv_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}H\chi f_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Furthermore, by (
A.13
), for any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\begin{split}|\langle\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}H\chi f_{k},g\rangle_{L^{2}}|&=|\langle\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}H\chi f_{k},\beta g\rangle_{L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)}|\\
&=|\langle H\chi f_{k},\chi_{1}(H-(-\overline{\lambda})^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}\beta g\rangle_{L^{2}}|\\
&\leq C\|\chi f_{k}\|_{H^{1}}\|\chi_{1}(H-(-\overline{\lambda})^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}\beta g\|_{H^{1}}.\end{split}

Because
\|\chi_{1}(H-(-\overline{\lambda})^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}\beta v\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}
by (
5.11
),

\|\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi_{1}H\chi f_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\chi f_{k}\|_{H^{1}}.

Returning to (
5.16
), we now find

\|\chi Hu\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\chi f_{k}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}},\qquad|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0},\,0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1.

Thus we have established (
1.23
) and (
1.24
) in the upper half plane. To show these estimates continue to hold in a strip in the lower half plane, we appeal to a standard resolvent identity argument due to Vodev
[
Vo14a
, Theorem 1.5]
. In fact, the corresponding steps from
[
LaSh24
, Section 6]
can be followed with no changes.

5.2.
Consequences for the Schrödinger and wave propagators

With Theorem
1.9
in hand, we prove Corollary
1.11
. The strategies we employ to conclude (
1.25
) and (
1.26
), and (
1.27
) are well-known, see
[
DyZw19
, Sections 2.3 and 7.1]
, and are based on Stone’s formula.

For the meromorphic continuation of the operator
H
, we utilize the notation

R(\lambda)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi.

Proof of (
1.25
).

Fix
T>0
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,\infty)
. Define the operator
A:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}((-T,T)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x})
by
v\mapsto\chi\varphi(H)e^{-itH}v
. We show there exists
C_{1}
independent of
T
and
\varphi
so that
\|AA^{*}f\|^{2}\leq C^{2}_{1}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x})}
for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-T,T)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x})
. Then by
\|A\|^{2}=\|AA^{*}\|
and the density of
C^{\infty}_{0}((-T,T)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x})
in
L^{2}((-T,T)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{[-T,T]}(t)\|\chi e^{-iHt}\varphi(H)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt=\int_{-T}^{T}\|\chi e^{-iHt}\varphi(H)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C_{1}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\qquad v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Because
C_{1}
is independent of
T
and
\varphi
, we conclude (
1.25
) by applying the monotone convergence theorem twice: first to a sequence of
\varphi
’s increasing up to the indicator function of
[0,\infty)
, and then to sequence of
T
’s tending to infinity.

A straightforward calculation demonstrates

A^{*}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{isH}\varphi(H)\chi f(s,\cdot)ds,\qquad f\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-T,T)_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}),

and thus

AA^{*}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi e^{-i(t-s)H}\varphi^{2}(H)\chi f(s,\cdot)ds.

Now use Stone’s formula
[
Te14
, Section 4.1]
to expand
\chi e^{-i(t-s)H}\varphi^{2}(H)\chi f(s,\cdot)
in the sense of strong convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

(5.17)

\begin{split}\chi e^{-i(t-s)H}\varphi^{2}(H)\chi&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\tau}\varphi^{2}(\tau)\chi\big[(H-(\tau+i\varepsilon))^{-1}-(H-(\tau-i\varepsilon)^{-1})\big]\chi d\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\tau}\varphi^{2}(\tau)[R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau})\big]d\tau.\end{split}

We are able to set
\varepsilon=0
due to
R(\lambda)
having the meromorphic continuation supplied by Theorem
1.9
. We now have, by Fubini’s theorem,

\begin{split}AA^{*}f&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(t-s)\tau}\varphi^{2}(\tau)[R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau})\big]f(s,\cdot)d\tau ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-it\tau}\big[R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau})\big]\varphi^{2}(\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{is\tau}f(s,\cdot)dsd\tau\\
&=-i\mathcal{F}_{\tau\mapsto t}\big[(R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau}))\varphi^{2}(\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{s\mapsto\tau}(f(s,\cdot))\big],\end{split}

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform respectively. Applying Plancherel’s theorem twice:

\begin{split}\|AA^{*}f\|^{2}&=4\pi^{2}\|(R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau}))\varphi^{2}(\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{s\mapsto\tau}(f(s,\cdot))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq 4\pi^{2}\sup_{\tau\geq 0}(\|(R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau}))\varphi^{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}.\end{split}

Clearly
\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\|(R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau}))\varphi^{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
is independent of the support of
f
. In addition, (
1.23
) and the hypothesis that
H
has no zero resonance imply
\|(R(\sqrt{\tau})-R(-\sqrt{\tau}))\varphi^{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
is bounded independent of
\varphi
too. This completes the proof of (
1.25
).

∎

Proof of (
1.26
).

Let

(5.18)

X(t)=e^{C_{3}t}

for
C_{3}>0
to be chosen. First, decompose the wave propagator according to
X(t)
,

(5.19)

\chi\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)\chi v=\chi\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})\mathbf{1}_{[0,X(t)]}(H)\chi v+\chi\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})\mathbf{1}_{\geq X(t)}(H)\chi v.

Estimate the second term on the right side of (
5.19
) using the spectral theorem,

(5.20)

\begin{split}\|\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})&\mathbf{1}_{\geq X(t)}(H)\chi v\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\big\|\frac{\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})}{\sqrt{|H|}}\mathbf{1}_{\geq X(t)}(H)\big\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|\sqrt{|H|}\chi v\|_{L^{2}}\leq e^{-C_{3}t/2}\|\sqrt{|H|}\chi v\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

For the first term on the right side of (
5.19
), we use Stone’s formula and the change of variable
\tau\mapsto\lambda^{2}
,

(5.21)

\begin{split}\chi\cos(t\sqrt{|H|})&\mathbf{1}_{[0,X(t)]}(H)\chi v\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{X(t)}\cos(t\sqrt{\tau})\chi\big[(H-(\tau+i\varepsilon))^{-1}-(H-(\tau-i\varepsilon))^{-1}\big]\chi vd\tau\\
&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{X(t)}\lambda(e^{it\lambda}+e^{-it\lambda})\chi\big[(H-(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon))^{-1}-(H-(\lambda^{2}-i\varepsilon))^{-1}\big]\chi vd\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-X(t)}^{X(t)}\lambda e^{-it\lambda}[R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda)\big]vd\lambda.\end{split}

We may set
\varepsilon=0
in line three due to Theorem
1.9
and our hypothesis that
R(\lambda)
has no resonance at zero. In particular, by (
1.23
), there exists
\theta_{1}>0
sufficiently small so that
R(\lambda)
is analytic near the strip
-\theta_{1}\leq\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 0
, and

(5.22)

\|\lambda(R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda))\|_{H^{k}\to L^{2}}\leq C(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{-k},\qquad-\theta_{1}<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda<0,\,k\in\{0,1\},

for some
C>0
independent of
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|
.

Deform the contour in the last line of (
5.21
) into the lower half plane,

(5.23)

\begin{split}\int_{-X(t)}^{X(t)}\lambda e^{-it\lambda}&[R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda)\big]vd\lambda\\
&=e^{-\theta_{1}t}\int_{\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda=-X(t)}^{\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda=X(t)}(\lambda e^{-it\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda}[R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda)\big])\rvert_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=-\theta_{1}}vd(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)\\
&+\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=-\theta_{1}}^{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=0}(\lambda e^{-it\lambda}[R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda)\big])\rvert_{\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda=X(t)}vd(\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)\\
&-\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=-\theta_{1}}^{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=0}(\lambda e^{-it\lambda}[R(\lambda)-R(-\lambda)\big])\rvert_{\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda=-X(t)}vd(\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)\end{split}

Denote the terms in lines two, three, and four of (
5.23
) by
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
, and
I_{4}
, respectively. For some
C>0
independent of
t
, and
v
,

(5.24)

\begin{gathered}\|I_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CX(t)e^{-\theta_{1}t}\|v\|_{H^{1}}=Ce^{(C_{3}-\theta_{1})t}\|v\|_{H^{1}},\\
\|I_{3}\|_{L^{2}},\|I_{4}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CX^{-1}(t)\|v\|_{H^{1}}=Ce^{-C_{3}t}\|v\|_{H^{1}}.\end{gathered}

Setting now

, (
5.20
) and (
5.24
) conclude the proof of (

).

Appendix A
Self-adjointness of the magnetic Schrödinger operator

In this appendix, we show

P(h)=\beta(-h^{2}\partial_{x}(\alpha\partial_{x})+hbD_{x}+hD_{x}b)+V:L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx),

is self-adjoint with respect the domain
\mathcal{D}
defined in (
1.9
). The assumptions on the coefficients are more general than those prescribed in Section
1
. Suppose

(A.1)

\displaystyle\alpha,\,\beta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};(0,\infty))\text{ and $\inf\alpha,\,\inf\beta>0$, }

(A.2)

\displaystyle V=V_{0}+V_{1},\text{ where $V_{0}$ is a finite signed Borel measure on $\mathbb{R}$ and $V_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})$},

(A.3)

\displaystyle b=b_{0}+b_{1},\text{ where $b_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})$ and $b_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})$.}

Since
\beta
is bounded from above and below by positive constants,
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx)=L^{2}(\mathbb{R};dx)
and their norms are equivalent.

Lemma A.1
.

Under (
A.1
), (
A.2
), and (
A.3
), the subspace
\mathcal{D}
given by (
1.9
) is dense in
L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)
. The operator
P(h)
equipped with domain
\mathcal{D}
and defined by (
1.10
) and is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx)
.

Proof.

Since multiplication by
V_{1}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\beta^{-1}dx)
, by the Kato-Rellich theorem
[
Te14
, Theorem 6.4]
, we suppose
V_{1}=0
without loss of generality.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}\supseteq\mathcal{D}
be the set of
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
-h\alpha u^{\prime}-ibu
has locally bounded variation, and
P(h)u
, defined in the distributional sense by (
1.10
), belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We use
u_{c}
to denote the unique absolutely continuous representative of
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}
.

First we prove
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Since the reverse containment is trivial, we will conclude
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}=\mathcal{D}
. Our strategy is as follows. For
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}
, fix a representative
f
of
-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu
with locally bounded variation. If necessary, modify
f
on a set of Lebesgue measure zero so
f^{A}(x)=f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
(this simplifies steps that involve (
2.2
) or (
2.3
)). For
a>0
, define

\mathsf{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(-a,a)},\qquad\mathsf{y}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup_{[-a,a]}|u_{c}|,\qquad\mathsf{z}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup_{[-a,a]}|f|.

We show that a system of inequalities holds:

(A.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{x}^{2}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\mathsf{x}+C_{3}\mathsf{z}+C_{4}\mathsf{y}^{2}+C_{5}\mathsf{y}\mathsf{z},

(A.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{y}^{2}\leq C_{6}+C_{7}\mathsf{x},

(A.6)

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}^{2}\leq C_{8}+C_{9}\mathsf{x}+C_{10}\mathsf{y}\mathsf{z}.

for constants
C_{j}>0
,
1\leq j\leq 10
, which may depend on
h
, but are independent of
a
. After using (
A.5
) to eliminate
\mathsf{y}
from (
A.4
) and (
A.6
) , we obtain a system in
\mathsf{x}
and
\mathsf{z}
with quadratic left sides and subquadratic right sides. Hence
\mathsf{x}
,
\mathsf{y}
, and
\mathsf{z}
are bounded in terms of the
C_{j}
. In particular
u,f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and since
u^{\prime}=-h^{-2}\alpha^{-1}f-ih^{-1}\alpha^{-1}bu
Lebesgue almost everywhere,
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
too. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{\max}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
.

We now turn to establishing (
A.4
), (
A.5
), and (
A.6
). For
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}
,
f=-h^{2}\alpha u_{c}^{\prime}-ihbu_{c}
Lebesgue almost everywhere, so

(A.7)

\tfrac{1}{\sup\alpha}\int_{(-a,a)}|f|^{2}dx\leq\int_{(-a,a)}\tfrac{1}{\alpha}|f|^{2}dx=\int_{(-a,a)}(-h^{2}\overline{u}_{c}^{\prime}+ih\tfrac{b}{\alpha}\overline{u}_{c})fdx.

By (
2.3
) and
f^{A}=f
,
\overline{u}^{\prime}_{c}f=d(\overline{u}_{c}f)-\overline{u}_{c}df
. Inserting this into the right side of (
A.7
) and applying (
2.2
) gives

(A.8)

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{\sup\alpha}\int_{(-a,a)}|f|^{2}dx&\leq\tfrac{h}{\inf\alpha}(\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\mathsf{z}+\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\mathsf{x})\\
&-h^{2}((\overline{u}_{c}f)^{L}(a)-(\overline{u}_{c}f)^{R}(-a))+h^{2}\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}_{c}df\\
&\leq\tfrac{h}{\inf\alpha}(\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\mathsf{z}+\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\mathsf{x})+2h^{2}\mathsf{y}\mathsf{z}+h^{2}\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}_{c}df.\end{split}

Now use that, as measures on bounded Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}df&=\beta^{-1}P(h)u-hbD_{x}u-\beta^{-1}u_{c}V_{0}\\
&=\beta^{-1}P(h)u-i\tfrac{b}{h\alpha}f+\tfrac{b^{2}}{\alpha}u_{c}-\beta^{-1}u_{c}V_{0}.\end{split}

Thus, since

,

(A.9)

\begin{split}\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}_{c}df&=\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}_{c}(\beta^{-1}P(h)u-i\tfrac{b}{h\alpha}f+\tfrac{b^{2}}{\alpha}u_{c})dx-\int_{(-a,a)}\beta^{-1}|u_{c}|^{2}V_{0}\\
&\leq\tfrac{1}{\inf\beta}\|P(h)u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}}{h\inf\alpha}\mathsf{z}+\tfrac{\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}}{h\inf\alpha}\mathsf{x}\\
&+\tfrac{2\|b_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{\inf\alpha}\mathsf{y}^{2}+\tfrac{2}{\inf\alpha}\|b_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{\|V_{0}\|}{\inf\beta}\mathsf{y}^{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
A.7
), (
A.8
) and (
A.9
) yields a bound of the form (
A.4
).

Next,

\begin{split}\sup_{[-a,a]}|u_{c}|^{2}&=\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\big(|u_{c}(0)|^{2}+2\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{x}\overline{u}^{\prime}_{c}u_{c}dx\big)\\
&=\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\big(|u_{c}(0)|^{2}+2\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{x}(-\tfrac{1}{h^{2}\alpha}\overline{f}+\tfrac{ib}{h\alpha}\overline{u}_{c})u_{c}dx\big)\\
&\leq|u_{c}(0)|^{2}+\tfrac{2\|u\|_{L^{2}}}{h^{2}\inf\alpha}\mathsf{x},\end{split}

which is (
A.5
).

If
x\in(0,a)
and
f
is continuous at
x
then by (
2.2
), (

) and
df=\beta^{-1}P(h)u-hbD_{x}u-\beta^{-1}u_{c}V_{0}

(A.10)

\begin{split}|f&|^{2}(x)\\
&=(|f|^{2})^{R}(0)+\int_{(0,x]}d(\overline{f}f)\\
&=(|f|^{2})^{R}(0)+2\mathop{\rm Re}\int\overline{f}df\\
&=(|f|^{2})^{R}(0)+2\mathop{\rm Re}\Big(\int_{(0,x]}\overline{f}(\beta^{-1}P(h)u-hbD_{x}u)dx-\int_{(0,x]}\beta^{-1}\overline{f}u_{c}V_{0}\Big)\\

Computing further, using

Lebesgue almost everywhere, and again

Similar estimates hold for
x\in(-a,0)
at which

is continuous. Thus
|f(x)|^{2}\leq C_{9}+C_{9}\mathsf{x}+C_{10}\mathsf{y}\mathsf{z}
at every point of continuity of

. But this implies (

) because

(A.12)

\begin{split}|f(x)|^{2}=|f^{A}(x)|^{2}&=4^{-1}|f^{L}(x)+f^{R}(x)|^{2}\\
&\leq 2^{-1}(|f^{L}(x)|^{2}+|f^{R}(x)|^{2})=2^{-1}((|f(x)|^{2})^{L}+(|f(x)|^{2})^{R})=(|f(x)|^{2})^{A}\end{split}

and for all
x\in[-a,a]
,
(|f(x)|^{2})^{A}
is a limit of values of
|f|^{2}
at which
f
is continuous.

Next, equip
P(h)
with the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}=\mathcal{D}
; we show
P(h)
is symmetric. Let
u,\,v\in\mathcal{D}
. Since
P(h)u=\beta d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)+h\beta bD_{x}u+u_{c}V_{0}
as distributions and hence as Borel measures,

(A.13)

\begin{split}\langle P(h)u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{P(h)u})v\beta^{-1}dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}vd(-h^{2}\alpha\overline{u}^{\prime}+ihb\overline{u})+\int_{\mathbb{R}}[ihb\overline{u}^{\prime}vdx+\overline{u}_{c}v_{c}\beta^{-1}V_{0}]\\
&=h^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha\overline{u}^{\prime}v^{\prime}dx+ih\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(\overline{u}^{\prime}v-\overline{u}v^{\prime})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{u}_{c}v_{c}\beta^{-1}V_{0}\\
&=\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}q(u,v).\end{split}

Similarly,
\langle u,P(h)v\rangle_{L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)}
coincides with the third line of (
A.13
). Thus
P(h)
is symmetric.

The last step is to establish that
(P,\mathcal{D})
is densely defined and
P^{*}\subseteq P
. For this, define on

the sesquilinear form
q
as in (
A.13
). For any

,
\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}
. Therefore, by Young’s inequality:

ab\leq p^{-1}\gamma^{1-p}a^{p}+q^{-1}\gamma b^{q}\quad\text{for all $\gamma>0$ and all $p,\,q\geq 1$ such that $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1$},

we have

(A.14)

\begin{gathered}\big|\int|u_{c}|^{2}V_{0}\big|\leq\|V_{0}\|\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\tfrac{\|V_{0}\|^{2}}{h^{2}\inf\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{h^{2}\inf\alpha}{4}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\\
\begin{split}2h\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}bu\overline{u}^{\prime}dx\big|&\leq 2h\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}+2h\|b_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\tfrac{864\|b_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}}{h^{2}\inf^{3}\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{h^{2}\inf\alpha}{8}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{8\|b_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}{\inf\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{h^{2}\inf\alpha}{8}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}\end{gathered}

Note that to estimate the term
2h\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2h\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}
, we used Young’s inequality with
a=\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}}
,
b=\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}
,
p=4
,
p=4/3
, and
\gamma=h\inf\alpha/12
. We thus find,

(A.15)

q(u,u)\geq-\big(\tfrac{\|V_{0}\|^{2}}{h^{2}\inf\alpha}+\tfrac{864\|b_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}}{h^{2}\inf^{3}\alpha}+\tfrac{4\|b_{1}\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\inf\alpha}\big)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{h^{2}\inf\alpha}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

so
q
is semibounded and closed.

By Friedrichs’ result
[
Te14
, Theorem 2.14]
, there is a unique, densely defined, self-adjoint operator
(A,\mathcal{D}_{1})
with

(A.16)

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}):\text{there exists $\tilde{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$ with $q(u,v)=\langle\tilde{u},v\rangle_{L^{2}(\beta^{-1}dx)}$ for all $v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})$}\},\\
Au=\tilde{u}.\end{gathered}

By (
A.13
), this means that, for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{(h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}+ihbu)}v^{\prime}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{Au}\beta^{-1}-ib\overline{u}^{\prime})vdx-\int\overline{u}_{c}v_{c}\beta^{-1}V_{0},\qquad v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

Thus the distributional derivative of
\overline{(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)}
is the measure
(\overline{(Au)}\beta^{-1}-ihb\overline{u}^{\prime})-\overline{u}_{c}\beta^{-1}V_{0}
. Proposition
2.3
then implies
h\alpha u^{\prime}+ibu
has locally bounded variation, and
\beta d(-h^{2}\alpha u^{\prime}-ihbu)-ih\beta bu^{\prime}+u_{c}V_{0}=Au\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
Therefore,
(A,\mathcal{D}_{1})\subseteq(P,\mathcal{D}_{\max})
, so
P^{*}\subseteq A^{*}=A\subseteq P
. Since we already showed
P\subseteq P^{*}
(symmetricity),
P^{*}=P
as desired.

∎

Appendix C
Simple operators with no resonance at zero

In this appendix we give simple examples of operators
H
as in (
5.1
) that do not have a resonance at zero.
Take
\alpha=\beta=1
, and let
V=M\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}
,
b=\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}
be indicator functions, where
M>0
is to be chosen. Let
u
in the domain of
H
have the form
u\in(H-i\varepsilon)^{-1}(|x|+1)^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. Then by (
A.13
), for all
\gamma>0
,

(C.1)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|(|x|+1)^{\frac{3+\delta}{2}}(H-i\varepsilon)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(|x|+1)^{-\frac{3+\delta}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\geq\mathop{\rm Re}\langle(H-i\varepsilon)u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx-2\mathop{\rm Im}\int_{-1}^{1}\overline{u}^{\prime}udx+M\int_{-1}^{1}|u|^{2}dx\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(M-2)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}[-1,1]}.\end{split}

On the other hand

\begin{split}\int&(|x|+1)^{-3-\delta}|u|^{2}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2+\delta}\big(\int^{0}_{-\infty}\partial_{x}((-x+1)^{-2-\delta})|u|^{2}dx-\int^{\infty}_{0}\partial_{x}((|x|+1)^{-2-\delta})|u|^{2}dx\big)\\
&=\frac{2}{2+\delta}|u(0)|^{2}+\frac{2}{2+\delta}\mathop{\rm Re}\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(x+1)^{-2-\delta}\overline{u}^{\prime}udx-\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-x+1)^{-2-\delta}\overline{u}^{\prime}udx\big)\\
&\leq\frac{2}{2+\delta}|u(0)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int(|x|+1)^{-3-\delta}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2+\delta}\int(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Now use that, for any
v\in H^{1}[-1,1]
,
\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}[-1,1]}\leq 2^{-1}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}[-1,1]}+2\|v\|_{L^{2}[-1,1]}\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}[-1,1]}

Te14

\begin{split}\int&(|x|+1)^{-3-\delta}|u|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\frac{2}{2+\delta}\big(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}[-1,1]}+4\|u\|_{L^{2}[-1,1]}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}[-1,1]}\big)+\frac{2}{2+\delta}\int(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\\

Thus, if we

, i.e.,

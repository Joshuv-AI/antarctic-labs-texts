Abstract




Abstract

Let
G
be a group and
S\subseteq G
its subset such that
S=S^{-1}
, where
S^{-1}=\{s^{-1}\mid s\in S\}
. Then
the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is an undirected graph
\Gamma

with the vertex set
V(\Gamma)=G
and the edge set
E(\Gamma)=\{(g,gs)\mid g\in G,s\in S\}
. A graph
\Gamma
is said to be
integral
if every eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of

\Gamma
is integer.
In the paper, we prove the following theorem:
if a subset
S=S^{-1}
of
G
is normal and
s\in S\Rightarrow s^{k}\in S
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(k,|s|)=1
,
then

{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is integral.
In particular,
if
S\subseteq G
is a normal set of involutions, then
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is integral.

We also use the theorem to prove that
if
G=A_{n}
and
S=\{(12i)^{\pm 1}\mid i=3,\dots,n\}
, then

{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is integral.
Thus, we give positive solutions for both problems 19.50(a) and 19.50(b) in ‘‘Kourovka Notebook’’.

Key words:
Cayley graph, adjacency matrix of a graph, spectrum of a graph, integral graph, complex group algebra, irreducible module, character of a group.

Equivariant Chevalley, Giambelli, and Monk Formulae for the Peterson Variety
Rebecca Goldin, Rahul Singh

Abstract
. We present a formula for the Poincaré dual in the flag manifold of the equivariant fundamental class of any regular nilpotent or regular semisimple Hessenberg variety as a polynomial in terms of certain Chern classes. We then develop a type-independent proof of the Giambelli formula for the Peterson variety, and use this formula to compute the intersection multiplicity of a Peterson variety with an opposite Schubert variety corresponding to a Coxeter word. Finally, we develop an equivariant Chevalley formula for the cap product of a divisor class with a fundamental class, and a dual Monk rule, for the Peterson variety.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a complex semisimple Lie group
corresponding to a Dynkin diagram
\Delta
.
Let
B
and
B^{-}
a pair of opposite Borel subgroups in
G
,
and let
T:=B\cap B^{-}
be the corresponding maximal torus in
G
.
We identify
\Delta
with the set of simple roots of
(G,B,T)
.
The quotient
G/B
is the associated flag manifold, with a left
T
action.

The
T
-equivariant homology
H_{*}^{T}(G/B)
of
G/B
has a basis consisting of
Schubert varieties,
which are
B
orbit closures in
G/B
.
Since
G/B
is smooth, there exists a dual basis of the
T
-equivariant cohomology
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)

given by Schubert classes
\{\sigma_{w}:w\in W\}
, where
W
is the Weyl group.
This basis enjoys
Graham positivity
, i.e., the structure constants
c_{uv}^{w}\in H_{T}^{*}(pt)
defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}=\sum_{w\in W}c_{uv}^{w}\sigma_{w}

are polynomials with non-negative coefficients in the set
\alpha\in\Delta
for all
u,v,w\in W
.

This paper is concerned with a class of subvarieties of
G/B
called
Hessenberg varieties
, with a particular focus on the
Peterson variety
, a regular nilpotent Hessenberg variety.
Hessenberg varieties were first introduced by De Mari
[
MR2636295
]
as part of the study of certain matrix decomposition algorithms.
They were generalized outside type A by De Mari, Procesi, and Shayman
[
MR1043857
]
,
who further identified the permutahedral variety, a toric variety studied by Klyachko
[
klyachko85
,
klyachko95
]
, as a particular Hessenberg variety.
The Peterson variety is a flat degeneration of the permutahedral variety.
Peterson
[
peterson:notes
]
and Kostant
[
kostant:flag
]
showed that the coordinate ring of a particular open affine subvariety of the Peterson variety is isomorphic to the quantum cohomology ring of the flag variety; see also
[
rietsch:totally
]
.

The Peterson variety admits a natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action.
In
[
gms:peterson
]
, Goldin, Mihalcea, and Singh show that
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Peterson Schubert calculus also satisfies Graham positivity; see
Eq.

1
below.
The equivariant cohomology of Peterson varieties in all Lie types is described in
[
harada.horiguchi.masuda:Peterson
]
, using generators and relations.
Drellich
[
drellich:monk
]
found a Giambelli formula for certain Coxeter elements (see
1.2
) using a type-by-type analysis.
Horiguchi
[
horiguchi2021mixed
]
obtained a Monk rule for ordinary cohomology using similar methods.
In type A, an equivariant Monk rule was developed by Harada and Tymoczko
[
harada.tymoczko:Monk
]
. Goldin and Gorbutt
[
goldin.gorbutt:PetSchubCalc
]
subsequently found positive combinatorial formulae for all equivariant structure constants for the Peterson variety in type A.

In this paper, we prove an equivariant Giambelli formula for all Coxeter elements, in any Lie type, using type-independent methods.
We also prove an equivariant Monk rule (
1.5
) and a dual equivariant Chevalley formula (
1.4
), both of which use a pairing of equivariant cohomology and homology for the Peterson variety (see
1.3
).

We denote by
\phi
,
\phi^{+}
, and
W
the set of roots, set of positive roots, and Weyl group, respectively.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=Lie(G)
,
\mathfrak{b}=Lie(B)
,
\mathfrak{h}=Lie(T)
,
and let
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denote the root space corresponding to
\alpha\in\phi
.
Consider the subspace

\displaystyle H_{0}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}.

A
B
-stable subspace
H
of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
H_{0}
is called an
indecomposable Hessenberg space
.
For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have a corresponding
Hessenberg variety
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(x,H)=\left\{gB\in G/B\,\middle|\,Ad(g^{-1})x\in H\right\}.

The Hessenberg variety
\mathbf{H}(x,H)
admits an action by the projective centralizer of
x
,
given by
\widetilde{C}_{G}(x)=\left\{g\in G\,\middle|\,Ad(g)x=\lambda x\text{ for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

Fix non-zero elements
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta
,
and let

\displaystyle h=\sum_{\alpha\in\phi^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle e=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}e_{\alpha}.

The Hessenberg variety
\mathbf{P}:=\mathbf{H}(e,H_{0})
is called the
Peterson variety
,
and the Hessenberg variety
\mathbf{Perm}:=\mathbf{H}(h,H_{0})

is called the
permutahedral variety
.

Recall that
h
is a regular semisimple element, with
\widetilde{C}_{G}(h)=T
,
and hence
T
acts on
\mathbf{H}(h,H)
.
Let
\mathfrak{s}=\mathbb{C}h
be the one-dimensional Lie algebra spanned by
h
,
and let
S\subset T
be the one-dimensional torus corresponding to
\mathfrak{s}
.
Since
[h,e]=2e
, we have
S\subset\widetilde{C}_{G}(e)
,
and hence an
S
-action on
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{H}(e,H)
.
Let
t
be the image of a simple root
\alpha
under the natural restriction
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathfrak{s}^{*}
.
The element
t
is independent of the choice of
\alpha
,
and further,
H^{*}_{S}(pt;\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}[t]
.

Under the mild assumption
H_{0}\subset H
,
Abe, Fujita, and Zeng
[
abe.fujita.zeng
, Cor. 3.9]
have identified the Poincaré dual of the fundamental class
[\mathbf{H}(x,H)]
in ordinary cohomology
as the Euler class of the vector bundle
G\times^{B}(\mathfrak{g}/H)
.
Our first result (
4.1
) is an extension of their result to the equivariant setup.
For
\lambda
a character of
T
,
we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\to G/B
the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=G\times^{B}\mathbb{C}_{-\lambda}
.
Let
c_{1}^{T}
(resp.
c_{1}^{S}
) denote the
T
(resp.
S
)-equivariant first Chern class.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
H_{0}\subset H
.
We have the following equalities in the equivariant homology of the flag manifold
G/B
:

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}(e,H)]_{S}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits\left(c_{1}^{S}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})-t\right)\cap[G/B]_{S},

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}(h,H)]_{T}

\displaystyle=\prod\left(c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})\right)\cap[G/B]_{T},

where the product is over the set
\left\{\alpha\in\phi\,\middle|\,\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\not\subset H\right\}
.

Recall the Schubert classes
\sigma^{S}_{w}\in H^{\ell(w)}_{S}(G/B)
,
Poincaré dual to the Schubert varieties
X^{w}=\overline{B^{-}wB/B}
.
Let
i^{*}:H_{S}^{*}(G/B)\to H_{S}^{*}(\mathbf{P})
be the pullback induced by the inclusion
i:\mathbf{P}\to G/B
,
and let
p_{v}=i^{*}\sigma^{S}_{v}
.
For convenience, we write
\sigma_{\alpha}=\sigma_{s_{\alpha}}
and
p_{\alpha}=i^{*}\sigma^{S}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta
.
An element
v_{I}\in W
is called a
Coxeter element
for some
I\subset\Delta

if each simple reflection
s_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in I
appears exactly once in a reduced expression of
v_{I}
.

Fix a Coxeter element
v_{I}
for each
I\subset\Delta
.
In
[
gms:peterson
]
we prove that
\left\{p_{v_{I}}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
is a basis for
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
in all Lie types, and that the structure constants
c_{IJ}^{K}\in H_{S}^{*}(pt)
defined by the equation

(1)

p_{v_{I}}p_{v_{J}}=\sum_{K\subset\Delta}c_{IJ}^{K}p_{v_{K}}

are polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients.

Our next result (
5.5
)
is an equivariant Giambelli formula, expressing the pullback of a
Coxeter Schubert class
as a polynomial in the divisor classes
p_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Giambelli formula)
.

Let
v_{I}
be a Coxeter element for
I
,
let
R(v_{I})
be the number of reduced words for
v_{I}
,
and let
\Omega_{I}=\prod_{\alpha\in I}p_{\alpha}\in H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
.
We have

(2)

\displaystyle p_{v_{I}}=\frac{R(v_{I})}{|I|!}\Omega_{I}.

Equation

2
was first obtained by Drellich
[
drellich:monk
]

for a particular choice of Coxeter element
v_{I}
for each
I
,
using a type by type analysis,
and the localization formula of Andersen, Jantzen, and Soergel
[
AJS
]
,
and Billey
[
billey:kostant
]
.
Our proof has the benefit of being type independent, and working all Coxeter elements.

The Peterson variety admits a cell-stratification,

\mathbf{P}=\bigsqcup_{I\subset\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{I}^{\circ},

see
[
tymoczko:paving
,
precup
,
balibanu:peterson
]
.
Consequently, we have a natural basis for the equivariant homology
H_{*}^{S}(\mathbf{P})
,
given by the fundamental classes
\left\{[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
.
Let

\displaystyle\left\langle\ ,\,\right\rangle:H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\times H_{*}^{S}(\mathbf{P})\to H^{*}_{S}(pt)

be the pairing given by equivariant integration,
i.e.,
\left\langle\omega,[Z]\right\rangle=\int_{[Z]}\omega
.
Following
[
gms:peterson
, Thm 1.1]
,
if
v_{I}
is a Coxeter element for some
I\subset\Delta
,
then

(3)

\displaystyle\left\langle p_{v_{I}},[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle=m(v_{I})\delta_{IJ}

for some positive integer
m(v_{I})
.
In particular,
\left\{p_{v_{I}}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
is a basis of
H_{S}^{*}(\mathbf{P})
,
dual (up to scaling) to the fundamental class basis
\left\{[\mathbf{P}_{I}]\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
.
The multiplicities
m(v_{I})
, which depend only on
I
and not on
\Delta
,
were calculated for certain Coxeter elements
v_{I}
in
[
gms:peterson
, Thm 1.3]
.
We prove in
5.7
a general formula for
m(v_{I})
, for any Coxeter element
v_{I}
,
conjectured in
[
gms:peterson
]
.

Let
C_{I}
be the Cartan matrix of
I
.
Recall that
f_{I}=\det(C_{I})
is called the
connection index
of
I
, see
[
bourbaki:Lie46
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Multiplicity Formula)
.

Let
v_{I}
be a Coxeter element of
I
,
and let
R(v_{I})
denote the number of reduced expressions for
v_{I}
.
We have

m(v_{I})=\left\langle p_{v_{I}},[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\right\rangle=\frac{R(v_{I})|W_{I}|}{|I|!\,f_{I}}.

Let us say a few words about the proofs of
1.2
and
1.3
.
The Hessenberg varieties
\mathbf{H}(x,H)
,
as
x
varies over the set of regular elements in
\mathfrak{g}
,
form a flat family of subvarieties in
G/B
,
see
[
abe.fujita.zeng
, Prop. 6.1]
.
Further, following the work of Brosnan and Chow
[
brosnan-chow
]
,
and Bălibanu and Crooks
[
balibanu2020perverse
]
,
we have a commutative diagram (see
Section

),

(4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}G/B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(\mathbf{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(\mathbf{Perm})^{W}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}(\mathbf{Perm}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}

Here
j^{*}:H^{*}(G/B)\to H^{*}(\mathbf{Perm})
is the pullback induced by the inclusion

.
This allows us to relate computations in the ordinary cohomology of the Peterson variety
to corresponding computations on the ordinary cohomology of the permutahedral variety.
In
[

,
klyachko85

(see also

nadeau.tewari

Klyachko presented a Giambelli formula expressing the pullback class

\displaystyle j^{*}\sigma_{v}=\frac{1}{\ell(v)!}\sum_{\underline{v}\in\mathcal{R}(v)}\prod_{s_{\alpha}\in\underline{v}}j^{*}\sigma_{s_{\alpha}}.

Here

is the set of reduced expressions for

and the product is over all occurrences of

in the reduced expression

.
Following

, the same relation holds amongst the

and

(in ordinary cohomology).

is an equivariant version of
Eq.

3
.
We then use

and the duality (

3
)
to reduce the calculation of

in

to the non-equivariant integral

In our final results, we develop a
Chevalley formula
(
6.5
),
and a dual
Monk rule
(
6.7
).
A Monk rule for the ordinary cohomology of
\mathbf{P}
was recently obtained by Horiguchi
[
horiguchi2021mixed
]
.
The equivalence of the Chevalley formula and the Monk rule is a consequence of
Eqs.

3
and
1.3
.

Recall that for any Dynkin subdiagram
J\subset\Delta
,
we have unique elements
\varpi_{\alpha}^{J}
in the weight lattice of
J
,
called the fundamental weights,
satisfying
\left\langle\beta^{\vee},\varpi_{\alpha}^{J}\right\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
for all
\beta\in J
.
Similarly, we have fundamental coweights
\varpi_{\alpha}^{J\vee}
in the coweight lattice of
J
,
dual to the roots
\alpha\in J
.
We write
\varpi_{\alpha}
(resp.
\varpi_{\alpha}^{\vee}
) for the fundamental weights (resp. coweights) for
\Delta
.
In general, we have

\varpi_{\alpha}^{I}\neq\varpi_{\alpha}
and

\varpi^{I\vee}_{\alpha}\neq\varpi^{\vee}_{\alpha}
, see
Section

3.3
.

Theorem 1.4

(Equivariant Chevalley formula)
.

For
\alpha\in\Delta
,
J\subset\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\alpha\not\in J,\\
\left\langle 2\rho_{J}^{\vee},\varpi_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\,[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in J\\
K=J\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}&\text{if }\alpha\in J.\end{cases}

Here
\rho_{J}^{\vee}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\phi_{J}^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}
is one-half the sum of the positive coroots supported on
J
,
and
W_{J}
and
W_{K}
are the Weyl subgroups of the Dynkin diagrams
J
and
K
respectively.

It is common in the literature
(see, for example,
[
drellich:monk
,
insko.tymoczko:intersection.theory
,
goldin.gorbutt:PetSchubCalc
]
)
to fix a Coxeter element
v_{I}
for each

,
and to work with the basis
\left\{p_{v_{I}}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
of
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
. Following
1.2
,
\left\{\Omega_{I}:=\prod_{\alpha\in I}p_{\alpha}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
is also a basis of
H_{S}^{*}(\mathbf{P})
.
We develop a Monk rule for the basis
\{\Omega_{I}\}
, resulting in a formula that does not depend a choice of Coxeter element for each

. For the reader’s convenience, we present the Monk rule for the basis
\left\{p_{v_{I}}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
in

.

Theorem 1.5

(Equivariant Monk Rule)
.

For
\alpha\in\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{\alpha}\Omega_{I}=\begin{cases}\Omega_{I\cup\{\alpha\}}&\text{if }\alpha\not\in I,\\
2\left\langle\rho^{\vee}_{I},\varpi_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\Omega_{I}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Delta\backslash I\\
J=I\cup\{\gamma\}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{f_{J}}{f_{I}}\left\langle\varpi_{\gamma}^{J\vee},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\Omega_{J}&\text{if }\alpha\in I.\end{cases}

Consider
\alpha\in\Delta
, and let
I=\Delta\backslash\{\alpha\}
.
A key step in the proof of

1.4
is the following formula (see
6.3
):

(6)

\displaystyle(c_{1}^{S}(i^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})-t)\cap[\mathbf{P}]_{S}=\frac{|W|}{|W_{I}|}[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}.

As a further consequence of

, we
obtain a new proof of the description of

2.
Equivariant (Co)Homology

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety equipped with a left action of a torus
T
.
We recall aspects of the
T
-equivariant homology and cohomology of
X
.
We will use the Borel model of equivariant cohomology,
and equivariant Borel-Moore homology,
following the setup in Graham’s paper
[
graham:positivity
]
.
We refer to
[
fulton:IT
, Ch 19]
,
[
fulton:young
, Appendix B]
, and
[
chriss.ginzburg
, §2.6]

for more details about cohomology and Borel-Moore homology.

We will study (co)homology with rational coefficients
.

Recall that the cohomology ring
H^{*}_{T}(pt)
of a point is naturally identified with

\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
, the symmetric algebra of the dual of the Lie algebra of
T
.
The morphism
X\to\{pt\}
from
X
to a point
gives the equivariant cohomology
H^{*}_{T}(X)
the structure
of a graded algebra over
H^{*}_{T}(pt)
via the pullback map
H_{T}^{*}(pt)\to H_{T}^{*}(X)
.
In addition, the cap product

\displaystyle\cap:H^{k}_{T}(X)\times H_{\ell}^{T}(X)\to H_{\ell-k}^{T}(X)

endows the
equivariant homology
H_{*}^{T}(X)
with a graded module structure over
H^{*}_{T}(X)
.
Equivalently, there is a compatibility of cap and cup products given by

\displaystyle(a\cup b)\cap c=a\cap(b\cap c)

\displaystyle a,b\in H^{*}_{T}(X),\ c\in H_{*}^{T}(X).

For any map
S\to T
of tori,
we have a natural map of algebras
H^{*}_{T}(X)\to H^{*}_{S}(X)
,
compatible with the algebra map
H^{*}_{T}(pt)\to H^{*}_{S}(pt)
induced by
Lie(T)^{*}\to Lie(S)^{*}
.
In particular, taking
S
to be the trivial subgroup in
T
,
we obtain the restriction to ordinary cohomology,

H^{*}_{T}(X)\to H^{*}(X)
.

2.1.
The integration pairing

Each irreducible,
T
-stable, closed subvariety
Z\subset X
of complex dimension

k
has a fundamental class
[Z]_{T}\in H_{2k}^{T}(X)
. If
X
is smooth and irreducible,
then there exists a unique class
\eta_{Z}\in H^{2(\dim X-k)}_{T}(X)
,
called the Poincaré dual of
Z
,
such that

\eta_{Z}\cap[X]_{T}=[Z]_{T}.

Given a
T
-equivariant proper map
f:X\to Y
,
there is a push-forward
f_{*}:H_{i}^{T}(X)\to H_{i}^{T}(Y)
,
determined by the fact that if
Z\subset X
is irreducible and
T
-stable,
then

\displaystyle f_{*}([Z])=\begin{cases}d_{Z}[f(Z)]&\text{if }\dim f(Z)=\dim Z,\\
0&\text{if }\dim f(Z)<\dim Z,\end{cases}

where
d_{Z}
is the generic degree of the restriction
f:Z\to f(Z)
.
The push-forward and pull-back are related by the projection formula

(7)

f_{*}(f^{*}(\eta)\cap c)=\eta\cap f_{*}(c),

for
\eta\in H^{*}_{T}(Y)
and
c\in H^{T}_{*}(X)
.
Recall that we have an isomorphism

(8)

\displaystyle H^{j}_{T}(pt)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{-j}^{T}(pt),

\displaystyle a\mapsto a\cap[pt]_{T}.

In particular,
H^{*}_{T}(pt)
lives in non-negative degrees,
and
H_{*}^{T}(pt)
lives in non-positive degrees.

Suppose now that
X
is complete, so that
f:X\to pt
is proper.
For a homology class
c\in H_{-j}^{T}(X)
, we denote by

\int_{X}c
the class
f_{*}(c)\in H_{-j}^{T}(pt)
,
viewed as an element of
H_{T}^{j}(pt)
via
Eq.

8
.
Then we may define a pairing,

(9)

(cf.

]
)
.

2.3.
Chern classes and Euler classes

We will denote by
c_{i}^{T}(\ )
the
i^{th}

T
-equivariant Chern class,
and by
e^{T}(\ )
the
T
-equivariant Euler class of a vector bundle.
We say that a section
s
of a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}\to X
is regular if the codimension of the zero set of
s
equals the rank of
\mathcal{V}
.
Recall that the torus
T
acts on the space
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{V})
of sections via the formula
(z\cdot s)(x)=zs(z^{-1}x)
for all
x\in X
and
z\in T
.
The sections which are invariant under this action are precisely those that intertwine the action,
i.e., satisfy
s(zx)=zs(x)
for all
x\in X
and
z\in T
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(cf.
[
graham2020cominuscule
, Lemma 2.2]
)

If
\mathcal{L}
is a
T
-equivariant line bundle on a
T
-scheme
X
,
and
s
is a
T
-invariant regular section of
\mathcal{L}
with zero-scheme
Y
,
then

[Y]_{T}=c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L})\cap[X]_{T}.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a
T
-equivariant vector bundle on a
T
-scheme
X
,
and
s
is a
T
-invariant regular section of
\mathcal{V}
with zero-scheme
Y
,
then
[Y]_{T}=e^{T}(\mathcal{V})\cap[X]_{T}
.

For
\lambda
a character of
T
, let

\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}=X\times\mathbb{C}\to X

denote the (geometrically trivial) equivariant line bundle,
with
T
-action given by
z(x,v)=(zx,\lambda(z)v)
for all
z\in T
.
By a standard abuse of notation, we write
\lambda
for the
T
-equivariant first Chern class of
\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\to X
be a
T
-equivariant vector bundle.
For a character
\lambda
of
T
,
let
s
be a regular section of
\mathcal{V}
that lies in the
\lambda
-weight space, i.e.,

(z\cdot s)=\lambda(z)s
for all
z\in T
.
The zero scheme
Z(s)
of
s
is
T
-invariant, and we have

[Z(s)]_{T}=e^{T}\left(\mathcal{V}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}\right)\cap[X]_{T}.

If
\mathcal{V}
admits a filtration with
T
-equivariant line bundle quotients
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}
,
we have

[Z(s)]_{T}=\prod\left(c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L}_{i})-\lambda\right)\cap[X]_{T}.

Proof.

If
\lambda
is non-trivial, the section
s
is not invariant.
Observe however that the section
r=s\otimes 1

of the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}
is
T
-invariant, since

\displaystyle r(zx)

\displaystyle=s(zx)\otimes 1=\left(zz^{-1}s(zx)\right)\otimes 1

\displaystyle=\left(z\left(\left(z^{-1}\cdot s\right)(x)\right)\right)\otimes 1

\displaystyle=\left(z\left(\lambda(z^{-1})s(x)\right)\right)\otimes 1

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda(z^{-1})zs(x)\right)\otimes 1

\displaystyle=zs(x)\otimes\lambda(z^{-1})=z(s(x)\otimes 1)=zr(x).

Further,
r
has the same zero scheme as
s
, i.e.,
Z(r)=Z(s)
.
Hence the first equality follows from
2.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
admits a filtration by line bundles
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{V}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}

admits a filtration by line bundles
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}\}
.
Applying the splitting principle, we have

\displaystyle e^{T}\left(\mathcal{V}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}\right)=\prod\left(c_{1}^{T}\left(\mathcal{L}_{i}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{-\lambda}\right)\right)=\prod\left(c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L}_{i})-\lambda\right),

from which the second equality follows.
∎

3.
Flag Manifolds

Fix a complex semisimple Lie group
G
,
opposite Borel subgroups
B,B^{-}\subset G
,
and let
T=B\cap B^{-}
be the common maximal torus.
We will further assume that
G
is simply connected;
this ensures that all line bundles on the flag manifold
G/B
are
T
-equivariant.
Denote by
\Delta
the system of simple positive roots associated to
(G,B,T)
,
by
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
the set of positive roots included in the set of all roots,
by
s_{\alpha}
the simple reflections for
\alpha\in\Delta
,
and by
W
the Weyl group of
G
.
Recall also the connection index
f
of
\Delta
,
which equals the determinant of the Cartan matrix of
\Delta
.

For
I\subset\Delta
, we denote by
\phi_{I}
,
\phi^{+}_{I}
,
W_{I}
, and
f_{I}

the set of roots, positive roots, Weyl group, and the connection index of
I
respectively.

3.1.
Flag manifolds and Schubert varieties

The flag manifold
G/B
is a projective algebraic manifold
with a transitive action of
G
given by left multiplication.
It has a stratification into finitely many
B
-orbits
(resp.
B^{-}
-orbits) called the
Schubert cells

X_{w}^{\circ}:=BwB/B
(resp.
X^{w,\circ}:=B^{-}wB/B
), i.e.,

(10)

G/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}X_{w}^{\circ}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}X^{w,\circ}\/.

The closures
X_{w}:=\overline{X_{w}^{\circ}}
and
X^{w}:=\overline{X^{w,\circ}}
are called
Schubert varieties
.
The
Bruhat order
is a partial order on
W
characterized by inclusions of Schubert varieties,
i.e.,
X_{v}\subset X_{w}
if and only if
v\leq w
,
and
X^{w}\subset X^{v}
if and only if
v\leq w
.
Following
Lemma

2.1
, the fundamental classes

\left\{[X_{v}]_{T}\,\middle|\,v\leq w\right\}
(resp.
\left\{[X^{v}]_{T}\,\middle|\,w\leq v\right\}
) form a basis of
H_{*}^{T}(X_{w})
(resp.
H_{*}^{T}(X^{w})
).

The cohomology classes
\sigma_{v}^{T}\in H_{T}^{*}(X)
Poincaré dual to the
[X^{v}]_{T}
,
i.e. characterized by the equation
\sigma_{v}^{T}\cap[G/B]_{T}=[X^{v}]_{T}
,
are called
Schubert classes
.
Following
Lemma

2.1
,
the Schubert classes
\left\{\sigma_{v}^{T}\,\middle|\,v\in W\right\}
form a basis of
H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
as a module over
H_{T}^{*}(pt)
.

3.2.
Line bundles on the flag manifold

Recall that since
G
is simply connected,
the character group
\mathfrak{X}(T)
of
T
equals the weight lattice of
\Delta
.
For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(T)
,
let
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
be the one-dimensional
B
-representation on which
T
acts via the character
\lambda
.
We will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
the
T
-equivariant line bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}:=G\times^{B}\mathbb{C}_{-\lambda}\to G/B,

\displaystyle(g,v)\mapsto gB,

with
T
-action given by
t\cdot(g,v)=(tg,v)
.

3.3.
Stability of Dynkin diagrams

Let
a_{\alpha\beta}=\left\langle\beta^{\vee},\alpha\right\rangle
denote the
\alpha\beta^{th}
entry of the Cartan matrix.
For
I\subset\Delta
, the Cartan matrix of
I
is the submatrix of
\Delta
spanned by the rows and columns indexed by the roots in
I
.
In particular, the pairing
\langle\ ,\,\rangle
on
\phi_{I}^{\vee}\times\phi_{I}
is the restriction of the pairing
\phi^{\vee}\times\phi

to
\phi_{I}\subset\phi
and
\phi^{\vee}_{I}\subset\phi^{\vee}
.
We describe this by saying that the roots and coroots are stable for the inclusion of Dynkin diagrams.

Consider the elements
\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\in\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in I}\mathbb{Q}\alpha

and
\varpi^{I\vee}_{\alpha}\in\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in I}\mathbb{Q}\alpha^{\vee}

given by the equations

\displaystyle\langle\varpi^{I\vee}_{\alpha},\beta\rangle=\langle\beta^{\vee},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\forall\beta\in I.

Then
\varpi_{\alpha}:=\varpi_{\alpha}^{\Delta}
is the fundamental weight dual to
\alpha^{\vee}
,
and
\varpi_{\alpha}^{\vee}:=\varpi_{\alpha}^{\Delta\vee}
is the fundamental coweight dual to the root
\alpha
.
In general,

\displaystyle\varpi_{\alpha}^{I}\neq\varpi_{\alpha}

and

\displaystyle\varpi^{I\vee}_{\alpha}\neq\varpi^{\vee}_{\alpha}.

We express this fact by saying that the fundamental weights and coweights are
not stable
for the inclusion of Dynkin diagrams.

3.4.
The height function

Let

\displaystyle\rho_{I}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\phi_{I}^{+}}\alpha=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\varpi_{\alpha}^{I},

\displaystyle\rho_{I}^{\vee}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\phi_{I}^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\varpi_{\alpha}^{I\vee}.

We set
\rho=\rho_{\Delta}
, and
\rho^{\vee}=\rho^{\vee}_{\Delta}
.
Following
[
bourbaki:Lie46
, Ch 6, Prop 29]
,
we have
\langle\rho^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=1
for
\alpha\in\Delta
.
For
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}a_{\alpha}\alpha
, we define the height of
\lambda
to be

\displaystyle ht(\lambda)=\sum a_{\alpha}=\langle\rho^{\vee},\lambda\rangle.

Let
h=2\rho^{\vee}
, and let
\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subalgebra spanned by
h
.
Observe that
h
is in the coroot lattice,
and hence there exists a one-dimensional sub-torus
S\subset T
with
Lie(S)=\mathfrak{s}
.
For any
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
, we have
\left\langle h,\alpha\right\rangle=\left\langle h,\beta\right\rangle
,
and hence
\alpha|\mathfrak{s}=\beta|\mathfrak{s}
.
Let
t=\alpha|\mathfrak{s}
for some
\alpha\in\Delta
.
The restriction map
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathfrak{s}^{*}
(dual to the inclusion
\mathfrak{s}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
)
satisfies
\alpha\mapsto t
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
,
and hence is given by

\lambda\mapsto ht(\lambda)t=\left\langle\rho^{\vee},\lambda\right\rangle t
.

4.

In this section we define Hessenberg varieties
and compute the equivariant Poincaré duals (in

We also define the Peterson variety

and recall from
[

4.1.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=Lie(G)
,
\mathfrak{b}=Lie(B)
, and
\mathfrak{h}:=Lie(T)

A subspace

is called a

if it is

Let

We say that a Hessenberg space

Recall that the vector bundle

is trivialized by the map

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}:G\times^{B}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle(g,x)\mapsto Ad(g)x,

i.e., we have an isomorphism

given by

(g,x)\mapsto(gB,Ad(g)x)
.
Let
H
be a Hessenberg space,
and let
\mu_{H}
denote the restriction of
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
to the sub-bundle
G\times^{B}H\subset G\times^{B}\mathfrak{g}
.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times^{B}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/B\times\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}}

For

, the fibre

(viewed as a subscheme of

\mathbf{H}(x,H)
.
If

is indecomposable,
\mathbf{H}(x,H)
is reduced and irreducible for all

see
[
abe.fujita.zeng
, Thm 1.2]
.
In this case, we call
\mathbf{H}(x,H)
a
Hessenberg variety
.

For the rest of this article,
we assume without mention that the Hessenberg space

is indecomposable.

For each positive root
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,
choose a root vector
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
.
Set
e=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}e_{\alpha}
.
The element
e
is a regular nilpotent element in
\mathfrak{b}
,
see e.g.
[
mcgovern.collingwood:nilpotent.orbits
,
kostant:principal
]
.
Recall from
Section

3.4
the sub-torus
S\subset T
lifting the element
h=2\rho^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Following
[
bourbaki:Lie46
, Ch 6, Prop 29]
, we have
\langle\rho^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=1
,
and hence

\displaystyle[h,e]=[h,\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}e_{\alpha}]=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\langle h,\alpha\rangle e_{\alpha}=2e.

We see that the vector space
\mathbb{C}e
is
h
-stable, and hence also
S
-stable.
Consequently, the Hessenberg variety
\mathbf{H}(e,H)
is
S
-stable.
Since
h\in\mathfrak{h}
, the adjoint action of
T
on
\mathfrak{s}=\mathbb{C}h
is trivial.
In particular,
\mathfrak{s}
is
T
-stable, and hence so is
\mathbf{H}(h,H)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any indecomposable Hessenberg space
H
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}(h,H)]_{T}

\displaystyle=\prod\left(c_{1}^{T}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})\right)\cap[G/B]_{T},

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}(e,H)]_{S}

\displaystyle=\prod\left(c_{1}^{S}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})-t\right)\cap[G/B]_{S},

where the product is over the set
\left\{\alpha\in\phi^{+}\,\middle|\,\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\not\subset H\right\}
.

Proof.

Consider the vector bundle

\mathcal{V}=G\times^{B}(\mathfrak{g}/H)\to G/B,

which admits a filtration with quotient bundles

\left\{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\,\middle|\,\alpha\in\phi^{+},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\not\subset H\right\}.

For

a regular element of
\mathfrak{g}
,
let
s_{x}:G/B\to\mathcal{V}
be the section of
\mathcal{V}
given by

.
Following
[
abe.fujita.zeng
, Prop 3.6]
,
we have
\mathbf{H}(x,H)=Z(s_{x})
, the zero scheme of

.

Observe that
\mathcal{V}
is a
T
-equivariant vector bundle,
and
s_{h}
is a

-invariant section.
Therefore by

,
the fundamental class of
\mathbf{H}(h,H)=Z(s_{h})
is given by the first equality.
On the other hand, the section

lies in the

.
∎

4.2.

The Peterson variety is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}:=\mathbf{H}(e,H_{0})\subset G/B.

Following

∎

5.
The Giambelli formula and intersection multiplicities

In this section,

Lemma 5.4

(Ordinary Giambelli Formula)
.

Let
v_{I}
be a Coxeter element for
I\subset\Delta
,
and let
R(v_{I})
be the number of reduced words for
v_{I}
.
We have

(18)

i^{*}\sigma_{v_{I}}=\frac{R(v_{I})}{|I|!}\prod_{\alpha\in I}i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}(v_{I})
denote the set of reduced words for
v_{I}
.
Following
[
klyachko85
,
klyachko95
]
(see also
[
nadeau.tewari
, Thm 8.1]
),
we have

\displaystyle j^{*}\sigma_{v_{I}}=\frac{1}{\ell(v_{I})!}\sum_{\underline{v}\in\mathcal{R}(v_{I})}\prod_{s_{\alpha}\in\underline{v}}j^{*}\sigma_{\alpha},

where
\ell(\_)
denotes the length function on
W
.
Since
v_{I}
is a Coxeter word for
I
,
we have
\ell(v_{I})=|I|
.
Further,
every reduced word
\underline{v}\in\mathcal{R}(v_{I})
contains each simple reflection
\left\{s_{\alpha}\,\middle|\,\alpha\in I\right\}
exactly once.
Hence we have,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(v_{I})!}\sum_{\underline{v}\in\mathcal{R}(v_{I})}\prod_{s_{\alpha}\in\underline{v}}j^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{R(v_{I})}{|I|!}\prod_{\alpha\in I}j^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}.

The claim now follows from
Eq.

15
.
∎

Recall that we denote by
p_{w}
the pullback class
i^{*}\sigma_{w}^{S}
,
and by
p_{\alpha}
the pullback class
i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}^{S}
.
For convenience, we write the equivariant class

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}:=\prod_{\alpha\in I}p_{\alpha}=\prod_{\alpha\in I}i^{*}(\sigma^{S}_{\alpha})

for any

, where
i^{*}:H_{S}^{*}(G/B)\rightarrow H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
is the pullback map in equivariant cohomology induced by the inclusion
i:\mathbf{P}\to G/B
.

Theorem 5.5

(Equivariant Giambelli formula)
.

Let
v_{I}
be a Coxeter element for
I\subset\Delta
,
and let
R(v_{I})
be the number of reduced words for
v_{I}
.
We have

\displaystyle p_{v_{I}}=\frac{R(v_{I})}{|I|!}\Omega_{I}.

Proof.

Following
4.5
, we may assume
I=\Delta
.
We write
v=v_{\Delta}
.
Observe that the restriction to ordinary cohomology
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\to H^{*}(\mathbf{P})

is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{J}\mapsto\prod_{\alpha\in J}i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}.

Following
Lemmas

5.4
and
4.3
,
the set
\left\{\prod_{\alpha\in J}i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha}\,\middle|\,J\subset\Delta\right\}
is linearly independent,
and hence so is the set
\left\{\Omega_{J}\,\middle|\,J\subset\Delta\right\}
.
Further,
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
is a free module over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
,
hence we may view
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
as a subset inside the
\mathbb{Q}(t)
vector space
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\otimes\mathbb{Q}(t)
.
We have

\displaystyle\dim H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}(t)}=\dim H_{*}^{S}(\mathbf{P})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}(t)}=\#\left\{\Omega_{J}\,\middle|\,J\subset\Delta\right\},

and hence
\left\{\Omega_{J}\,\middle|\,\ J\subset\Delta\right\}
forms a basis of
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}(t)}
over
\mathbb{Q}(t)
.
Consequently, there exist
c_{v}^{J}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

(19)

\displaystyle p_{v}=i^{*}\sigma_{v}^{S}=\sum_{J\subset\Delta}c_{v}^{J}\Omega_{J},

where the
c_{v}^{J}
are
t
-monomials of degree
|\Delta|-|J|
.

Applying the specialization
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\to H^{*}(\mathbf{P})
,
we deduce from
Lemma

5.4
that

\displaystyle c_{v}^{\Delta}=\frac{R(v)}{|\Delta|!}.

It remains to show that
c_{v}^{J}=0
for all
J\subsetneq\Delta
.
Fix
J\subsetneq\Delta
, and
consider the inclusion
\iota_{J}:\mathbf{P}_{J}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{P}

and the corresponding pull-back
\iota_{J}^{*}:H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\to H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P}_{J})
.
Following
[
gms:peterson
, Thm. 6.6(b)]
,
v=v_{\Delta}
implies
\iota_{J}^{*}p_{v}=0
for
J\neq\Delta
,
and

\displaystyle\iota_{J}^{*}p_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p_{\alpha}&\text{for }\alpha\in J,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Applying
\iota_{J}^{*}
to
Eq.

19
,
we obtain a system of equations indexed by
J\subsetneq\Delta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset J}c_{v}^{K}\Omega_{K}=0

\displaystyle\text{in }H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P}_{J}).

Since the
\Omega_{K}
are linearly independent in
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P}_{J})
,
we deduce that
c_{v}^{J}=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

We have

\left\langle\Omega_{\Delta},[\mathbf{P}]_{S}\right\rangle=\frac{|W|}{f_{\Delta}},

where
f_{\Delta}
is the connection index of
\Delta
.

Proof.

Observe that since
\deg\left([\mathbf{P}]_{S}\right)=\deg\left(\Omega_{\Delta}\right)=|\Delta|
,
we have

\left\langle\Omega_{\Delta},[\mathbf{P}]_{S}\right\rangle=\left\langle\prod i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha},[\mathbf{P}]\right\rangle,

where the latter expression is the pairing in ordinary (co)homology.
Applying the forgetful maps
H^{*}_{T}(G/B)\to H^{*}(G/B)
and
H^{*}_{S}(G/B)\to H^{*}(G/B)
to the equations in
4.1
,
we deduce that

in

.
It follows that

\left\langle\Omega_{\Delta},[\mathbf{P}]_{S}\right\rangle=\left\langle\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha},[\mathbf{P}]\right\rangle=\left\langle\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}i^{*}\sigma_{\alpha},[\mathbf{Perm}]\right\rangle=\frac{|W|}{f_{\Delta}},

where the latter equality is from
[
klyachko85
, Thm 3]
.
∎

Theorem 5.7

(Multiplicity Formula)
.

v_{I}
a Coxeter element of
I
,
we have

\left\langle p_{v_{I}},[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\right\rangle=\frac{R(v_{I})|W_{I}|}{|I|!\,f_{I}}.

does not depend on the diagram

∎

Recall that
H^{*}_{T}(G/B)
is generated (as a ring) by the divisor classes.
Consequently, the
\left\{p_{\alpha}\,\middle|\,\alpha\in\Delta\right\}
are ring generators for
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
.

Let
I=\Delta\backslash\{\alpha\}
. Following
[
gms:peterson
, Thm 6.5]
and
6.5
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}-2t)\cap[\mathbf{P}]_{S}=p_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}=0.

By the universal coefficients theorem
[
MR1702278
, Ch 17, §3]
, the map

\omega\mapsto\omega\cap[\mathbf{P}]
is an isomorphism
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{*}^{S}(\mathbf{P})
.
It follows that
p_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}-2t)=0
,
and consequently, we obtain a surjective map

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{Q}[p_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in\Delta}}{\left\langle p_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}-2t)\right\rangle}_{\alpha\in\Delta}\longrightarrow H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P}).

It remains to show that this map is an isomorphism, i.e., there are no other relations.
Following
[
klyachko85
, Thm 3]
and
Eq.

15
,
we have

\displaystyle H^{*}(\mathbf{P})=\dfrac{\mathbb{Q}[p_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in\Delta}}{\left\langle p_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}\right\rangle_{\alpha\in\Delta}}.

We deduce that the kernel of the map in
Eq.

22
is
t
-divisible.
However,
t
acts freely on
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
,
and hence
Eq.

22
is an isomorphism.
∎

6.2.
The Chevalley and Monk formulae

Theorem 6.5

(Equivariant Chevalley formula)
.

For
\alpha\in\Delta
,
J\subset\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\alpha\not\in J,\\
\left\langle 2\rho_{J}^{\vee},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\,[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in J\\
K=J\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}&\text{if }\alpha\in J.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the inclusion map
\iota_{J}:\mathbf{P}_{J}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{P}
.
Following
[
gms:peterson
, Thm. 6.6(b)]
, we have

\iota_{J}^{*}p_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}p_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\in J,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, we have
p_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}=0
for
\alpha\not\in J
.

Next, for
\alpha\in J
, we have

\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in J}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\beta,

and hence

\displaystyle\iota_{J}^{*}p_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in J}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\iota_{J}^{*}q_{\beta}

\displaystyle\text{in }H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P}_{J}).

Following
6.3
, we have

\displaystyle q_{\beta}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}=\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}+2t[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S},

\displaystyle K=J\backslash\{\beta\}.

Further, for
\beta\in J
, we have
\iota_{J}^{*}q_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\in J}a_{\beta\alpha}\iota_{J}^{*}p_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in J}a_{\beta\alpha}p_{\alpha}=q_{\beta}
.
Hence, by the projection formula (
Eq.

7
),
we have

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in J}\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\rangle\,q_{\beta}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in J\\
K=J\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\left(2t[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}+\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle 2\rho_{J}^{\vee},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in J\\
K=J\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}.

∎

Example 6.6
.

Let
\Delta=B_{2}
.
\scriptscriptstyle 1
\scriptscriptstyle 2

We compute

\displaystyle p_{1}\cap[\mathbf{P}]_{S}=\left\langle 2\rho^{\vee},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle t\,[\mathbf{P}]_{S}+\left\langle\varpi^{\vee}_{1},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle\frac{|W|}{|W_{\{2\}}|}[\mathbf{P}_{\{2\}}]_{S}+\left\langle\varpi^{\vee}_{2},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle\frac{|W|}{|W_{\{1\}}|}[\mathbf{P}_{\{1\}}]_{S}.

Recall the realization of the root system of
B_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
given by
\alpha_{1}=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}
,
\alpha_{2}=\epsilon_{2}
.
We have
\varpi_{1}=\epsilon_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
.
Observe that
\left\langle\varpi_{j}^{\vee},\varpi_{i}\right\rangle
equals the coefficient of
\alpha_{j}
in the expansion of
\varpi_{i}
as a sum of simple roots.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\varpi_{1}^{\vee},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle=1,

\displaystyle\left\langle\varpi_{2}^{\vee},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle=1.

Furthermore,
2\rho^{\vee}=2\varpi_{1}^{\vee}+2\varpi_{2}^{\vee}
,
and hence
\left\langle 2\rho^{\vee},\varpi_{1}\right\rangle=4
.
Finally, we have
|W|=8
and
|W_{\{1\}}|=|W_{\{2\}}|=2
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle p_{1}\cap[\mathbf{P}]_{S}=4t[\mathbf{P}]_{S}+4[\mathbf{P}_{\{1\}}]_{S}+4[\mathbf{P}_{\{2\}}]_{S}.

Recall that
\Omega_{I}=\prod_{\alpha\in I}p_{\alpha}
,
so that, in particular,
\Omega_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}
.
Following
4.3
and
5.5
, the set
\left\{\Omega_{I}\,\middle|\,I\subset\Delta\right\}
is a basis of
H^{*}_{S}(\mathbf{P})
over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
.
Using
4.3
and
5.7
,
we can deduce a Monk rule for this basis from the Chevalley formula.

Theorem 6.7

(Equivariant Monk Rule)
.

Let
f_{J}
denote the
connection index
of the Dynkin diagram
J
,
i.e., the determinant of the Cartan matrix of
J
.
For
\alpha\in\Delta
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{\alpha}\Omega_{I}=\begin{cases}\Omega_{I\cup\{\alpha\}}&\text{if }\alpha\not\in I,\\
2\left\langle\rho^{\vee}_{I},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\Omega_{I}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Delta\backslash I\\
J=I\cup\{\gamma\}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{f_{J}}{f_{I}}\left\langle\varpi_{\gamma}^{J\vee},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\Omega_{J}&\text{if }\alpha\in I.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the coefficients
c_{\alpha I}^{J}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
in the product
\Omega_{\alpha}\Omega_{I}=\sum c_{\alpha I}^{J}\Omega_{J}
.
Following
4.3
we have

c_{\alpha I}^{J}=\frac{\left\langle\Omega_{\alpha}\Omega_{I},[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\Omega_{J},[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle}=\frac{\left\langle\Omega_{I},\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\Omega_{J},[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle}=\frac{f_{J}}{|W_{J}|}\left\langle\Omega_{I},\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}\right\rangle,

where the final equality is from
Lemma

5.6
.

Consider first
\alpha\not\in J
.
Following
6.5
, we have
\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}=0
,
and hence
c_{\alpha I}^{J}=0
.

Consider now
\alpha\in J
.
Recall from
4.3
that
\left\langle\Omega_{I},[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}\right\rangle=0
unless
I=K
.
Further, by
6.5
, the only
[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}
appearing in the expansion of
\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}

correspond to
I=J
or
I=J\backslash\{\gamma\}
for some
\gamma\in J
.
Thus
c_{\alpha I}^{J}=0
unless
I=J
or
I=J\backslash\{\gamma\}
for some
\gamma\in J
.
For
J=I
, we have,

\displaystyle c_{\alpha I}^{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{I}}{|W_{I}|}\left\langle\Omega_{I},\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{I}}{|W_{I}|}\left\langle\Omega_{I},\left\langle 2\rho_{I}^{\vee},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\,[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in I\\
K=I\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{I\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{|W_{I}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{I}}{|W_{I}|}\left\langle 2\rho_{I}^{\vee},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\,\left\langle\Omega_{I},[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle 2\rho_{I}^{\vee},\varpi^{I}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t.

In the case where
J=I\sqcup\{\gamma\}
for some
\gamma\in\Delta
,
we have

\displaystyle c_{\alpha I}^{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{J}}{|W_{J}|}\left\langle\Omega_{I},\Omega_{\alpha}\cap[\mathbf{P}_{J}]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{J}}{|W_{J}|}\left\langle\Omega_{I},\left\langle 2\rho_{J}^{\vee},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle t\,[\mathbf{P}_{J}]_{S}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in J\\
K=J\backslash\{\beta\}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\beta},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{K}|}[\mathbf{P}_{K}]_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{J}}{|W_{J}|}\frac{|W_{J}|}{|W_{I}|}\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\gamma},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\left\langle\Omega_{I},[\mathbf{P}_{I}]_{S}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\varpi^{J\vee}_{\gamma},\varpi^{J}_{\alpha}\right\rangle\frac{f_{J}}{f_{I}}.

∎

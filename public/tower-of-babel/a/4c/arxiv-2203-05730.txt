Abstract
. In earlier work, the authors introduced a conjecture which, for an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism φ : S → S \varphi\colon S\to S of a surface, connects a certain quantum invariant of φ \varphi with the hyperbolic volume of its mapping torus M φ M_{\varphi} . This article provides a proof of this conjecture in the simplest case where it applies, namely when the surface S S is the one-puncture torus and the mapping torus M φ M_{\varphi} is the complement of the figure-eight knot.

57K31, 57K32

This article is the second installment in a series of three, starting with
[
BWY21
]
and ending with
[
BWY22
]
. In
[
BWY21
]
, we introduced a conjecture that connects two apparently unrelated quantities associated to an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
of a surface
S
. One is a quantum invariant based on the representation theory of the Kauffman bracket skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
, and consists of a linear isomorphism
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon V\to V
of a large vector space
V
. This isomorphism
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
is defined up to conjugation and up to multiplication by a scalar of modulus 1, and depends on the diffeomorphism
\varphi
, on a character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
represented by a group homomorphism
r\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and invariant under the action of
\varphi
, on a root of unity
q
and on certain complex puncture weights strongly constrained by the earlier data. The other invariant comes from hyperbolic geometry, and is the volume
\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})
of the complete hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
, obtained from the product
S\times[0,1]
by gluing
S\times\{0\}
to
S\times\{1\}
through
\varphi
.

The conjecture of
[
BWY21
]
is essentially that, if we choose the root of unity
q_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
, the modulus of the trace of
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
exponentially grows as
\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})}
as
n
tends to
\infty
. See §
1
for a precise statement of this conjecture.

The current article proves the conjecture for what is essentially the simplest nontrivial example, where the surface
S
is the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
and where, using the classical identification of the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})
with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\varphi
corresponds to the matrix
LR=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. This example is also famous, because the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
is then diffeomorphic to the complement of the figure-eight knot in
S^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\cup\{\infty\}
. It has the advantage that both its hyperbolic geometry and its quantum topology are relatively simple. On the hyperbolic side, the hyperbolic metric of
M_{\varphi}
is isometric to the union of two regular hyperbolic ideal tetrahedra, and its volume is

\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{LR})=6\Lambda\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\approx 2.02988....

where
\Lambda(\theta)
denotes the Lobachevsky function. On the quantum topology side, the trace of
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
can be factored as a product of two simpler terms and, more critically, a cancellation of phases (see Remark
10
) makes the asymptotic analysis accessible by elementary methods.

The techniques used in this article are completely elementary, although sometimes challenging. These are intended to serve as a “sanity check” for the more sophisticated methods developed in
[
BWY22
]
, which combine harmonic analysis and complex geometry to address more general diffeomorphisms of the one-puncture torus. In particular, we will already encounter here, in a very concrete way, some of the cancellations that occur in the more general framework.

A large portion of this article, namely §
4
, is also used in a critical way in
[
BWY22
]
. Indeed, the limit of a certain normalization factor
\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}
is determined in that section. The mere existence of such a finite limit is already quite surprising.

1.
The Volume Conjecture for surface diffeomorphisms

We briefly describe the conjecture that motivates this article, referring to
[
BWY21
]
for details.

Let
\varphi\colon S\to S
be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of an oriented surface
S
of finite topological type. We assume that
\varphi
is pseudo-Anosov so that its
mapping torus

M_{\varphi}
, obtained from the product
S\times[0,1]
by gluing
S\times\{0\}
to
S\times\{1\}
through
\varphi
, admits a complete hyperbolic metric.

The diffeomorphism
\varphi
acts on any object that is naturally associated to the surface
S
. In particular, it acts on the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
–character variety

\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)=\{\text{group homomorphisms }r\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\}/\negthinspace\negthinspace/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

where
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acts on group homomorphisms
r\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by conjugation and where the quotient is taken in the sense of geometric invariant theory. Let
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
denote the corresponding algebraic isomorphism induced by
\varphi
.

The action of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
has many fixed points. Classical ones come from the monodromy
\bar{r}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\colon\pi_{1}(M_{\varphi})\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
. However, there are many more fixed points when
S
has at least one puncture. In fact, the dimension of the fixed point set of
\varphi^{*}\colon\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
has complex dimension
c
near these
\varphi
–invariant hyperbolic characters, where
c
is the cardinality of the orbit space of the action of
\varphi
on the set of punctures of
S
. (See for instance
[
BWY21
, §3.2]
.)

Now, suppose that we are given:

1.

the diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
;

2.

a character
[r]
that is in the smooth part of the character variety
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
, and is fixed by the action of
\varphi
;

3.

for each puncture
v
of the surface
S
, a number
\theta_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(a)

if
\alpha_{v}\in\pi_{1}(S)
is represented by a loop going once around the puncture
v
, its image
r(\alpha_{v})\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
has eigenvalues
-\mathrm{e}^{\pm\theta_{v}}
;

(b)

the puncture weights
\theta_{v}
are invariant under the action of
\varphi
, in the sense that
\theta_{\varphi(v)}=\theta_{v}
for every puncture
v
;

4.

a primitive
n
–root of unity
q
with
n
odd.

Deep results of
[
BW16
,
FKBL19
,
GJS19
]
associate to this data (where the puncture weights
\theta_{v}
are replaced by
p_{v}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}
) an irreducible representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
of the Kauffman bracket skein algebra
\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)
which, up to isomorphism, is fixed under the action of
\varphi
. This means that the representations
\rho
and
\rho\circ\varphi^{*}\colon\mathcal{K}^{q}(S)\to\mathrm{End}(V)
are isomorphic, by a linear isomorphism
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\colon V\to V
. We normalize
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
so that its determinant has modulus 1. Then, the modulus
\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}\right|
of its trace is uniquely determined; see
[
BWY21
, Prop. 4]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let the pseudo-Anosov surface diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to S
, the
\varphi
–invariant smooth character
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S)
and the
\varphi
–invariants puncture weights
\theta_{v}
as above be given. For every odd
n
, consider the primitive root of unity
q_{n}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
. Then

\lim_{n\,\text{odd}\,\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi}),

where
\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})
is the volume of the complete hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus
M_{\varphi}
.

The article is devoted to a proof of this conjecture for the simplest example where it applies, namely for a specific diffeomorphism of the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
; see Corollary
20
. For that case, we will actually prove in Theorem
19
a result that is stronger than the conjecture, by identifying constants
K_{1}
and
K_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n=1\,\mathrm{mod}\,4\end{subarray}}\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})}

\displaystyle=K_{1}

\displaystyle\text{and }\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n=3\,\mathrm{mod}\,4\end{subarray}}\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})}

\displaystyle=K_{3}.

This result is consistent with the dual convergence mode that was observed in the numerical experiments of
[
BWY21
, §2]
.

2.
The trace of the intertwiner for
\varphi=LR

For the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
, the mapping class group
\pi_{0}\,\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})
is determined by its action on the homology group
H_{1}(S_{1,1})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, which provides an identification
\pi_{0}\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(S_{1,1})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. A well-known property of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is that every conjugacy class is represented by an element
\varphi=\pm\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\dots\varphi_{k}
where each
\varphi_{i}
is equal to
L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)

or

R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. It turns out that the intertwiner
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
and the volume
\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi})
are both independent of the sign
\pm
. Also, there needs to be at least one
\varphi_{i}
equal to
L
and one
\varphi_{j}
equal to
R
if we want
\varphi
to be pseudo-Anosov. The simplest example where Conjecture
1
applies is therefore that of
\varphi=LR
.

We apply the algorithm of
[
BWY21
, §4]
to determine the trace of the intertwiner
\Lambda_{LR,r}^{q}
occurring in Conjecture
1
.

We start with the ideal triangulation sweep associated to
\varphi=LR
and with a periodic edge weight system for this sweep, as in
[
BWY21
, §3.2]
. In practice, this edge weight system is a finite sequence
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})
,
(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})
,
(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})\in\big(\mathbb{C}^{*}\big)^{3}
such that

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{0}^{-1}

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=(1+b_{0})^{2}a_{0}

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=(1+b_{0})^{-2}b_{0}^{2}c_{0}

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=c_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=(1+c_{1})^{2}b_{1}

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=(1+c_{1})^{-2}c_{1}^{2}a_{1}

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=a_{2}

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=b_{2}

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{2}.

This case is simple enough that these equations can be explicitly solved through the quadratic formula. Their solutions are of the form

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-\frac{3}{2}b_{0}-b_{2}^{2}\pm\sqrt{-b_{0}^{3}-\frac{7}{4}b_{0}^{2}-b_{0}}}{(1+b_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-\frac{3}{2}b_{0}-b_{2}^{2}\mp\sqrt{-b_{0}^{3}-\frac{7}{4}b_{0}^{2}-b_{0}}}{b_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{0}^{-1}

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle-1-\frac{3}{2}b_{0}-b_{2}^{2}\mp\sqrt{-b_{0}^{3}-\frac{7}{4}b_{0}^{2}-b_{0}}

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-\frac{3}{2}b_{0}-b_{2}^{2}\mp\sqrt{-b_{0}^{3}-\frac{7}{4}b_{0}^{2}-b_{0}}}{(1+b_{0})^{2}}

as
b_{0}
ranges over
\mathbb{C}-\{0,-1\}
and
\pm\in\{+,-\}
(with
\mp=-\pm
and an arbitrary choice of complex square roots).

As in

BWY21
, §3.2]
, such a periodic edge weight system defines a

–invariant character

In particular, the periodic edge weight system where

coming from the monodromy of the complete hyperbolic metric of the mapping torus

In the framework of Conjecture

, we are also given a puncture weight

.

\displaystyle=b_{k}

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{C_{k}}

\displaystyle=c_{k}

\displaystyle=1+a_{k}^{-1}

,

3.1.
The discrete and continuous quantum dilogarithms

The goal of this section is to obtain estimates on the size, as well as the phase, of the terms
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,2i)q^{2i^{2}-\widehat{k}i}
occurring in the sum
\Sigma_{n}
of (
2
).

For a primitive
n
–root of unity
q
with
n
odd, and for
u
,
v\in\mathbb{C}
with
v^{n}=1+u^{n}
, the term

{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,i)=v^{-i}\prod_{j=1}^{i}(1+uq^{-2j}).

is the Faddeev-Kashaev
discrete quantum dilogarithm function
. This is an
n
–periodic function of the integer
i
, which enables one to define it for negative
i\in\mathbb{Z}
as well.

A well-known interpolation of this discrete function is provided by the continuous quantum dilogarithm function of Faddeev
[
Fad95
,
FK94
]
. Because many different normalizations are used in the literature, we first summarize our notation and conventions, as well as the basic properties that we will need. We refer to
[
FC99
, §4.1]
or
[
WY20
, §2.3]
, for instance, for proofs of these properties.

We will actually distinguish two continuous quantum dilogarithm functions, a “small” one and a “big” one.

For
\hbar>0
, the
small continuous quantum dilogarithm
is the function

(3)

\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}^{\hbar}}(z)={2\pi\mathrm{i}\hbar}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{(2z-\pi)t}}{4t\sinh(\pi t)\sinh(\pi\hbar t)}\,dt,

defined for
-\frac{\pi\hbar}{2}<\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}z<\pi+\frac{\pi\hbar}{2}
,
where the integration domain
\Omega
is obtained from
\mathbb{R}
(oriented from
-\infty
to
+\infty
) by replacing a small interval around 0 with a semi-circle lying above the real line, in order to bypass the pole of the integrand at 0.

The following property shows that this function can be seen as a deformation of the
classical dilogarithm

\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}(u)=-\int_{0}^{u}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}\,dt

defined over the open simply-connected subset
\mathbb{C}-\left[1,+\infty\right[
.

Proposition 3
.

For every
z

0<\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}z<\pi
(so that
\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}z}\in\mathbb{C}-\left[1,+\infty\right[

Setting

Corollary 5
.

Let
U
and

such that
\mathrm{e}^{V}=1+\mathrm{e}^{U}
be fixed. For every odd

, set

,

and

. Then, for every

{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{j}{n}V}\ \frac{\operatorname{\mathrm{Li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-{\frac{2\pi j}{n}}\right)}{\operatorname{\mathrm{Li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}\right)}.\qed

This connection with the continuous quantum dilogarithm will enable us to obtain estimates for the discrete quantum logarithmic function
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)
. These involve the
Lobachevsky function
, which is the function

\Lambda(\theta)=-\int_{0}^{\theta}\log\left|2\sin t\right|\,dt

and is classically related to the dilogarithm function
\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}(z)
by the property that

(6)

\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\theta}\right)={\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}-\theta(\pi-\theta)+2\mathrm{i}\Lambda(\theta)

for every
\theta\in[0,\pi]
.

This analysis of
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)
splits into two cases, according to whether the numbers
\frac{2\pi j}{n}
corresponding to the indices
j
are close to
\frac{\pi}{2}
modulo
\pi
or not.
We first consider the latter case, where
\frac{2\pi j}{n}
stays away from the points
\frac{\pi}{2}
modulo
\pi
. By
n
–periodicity of the function
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)
, we can restrict attention to the case where
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{3\pi}{2}
.

Lemma 6
.

,

with

, and

and

sufficiently large and for every integer

1.

If

where
\widetilde{f}
,
\widetilde{g}_{n}\colon\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\right]\to\mathbb{C}
are continuous functions such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-t\right)-\frac{1}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}.

where
\widehat{f}
,
\widehat{g}_{n}\colon\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\right]\to\mathbb{C}
are continuous functions such that

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-t\right)-\frac{1}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\ \left(1-\mathrm{i}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}U}\right)\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}.

In both cases, the constants hidden in the condition “for
n
sufficiently large” and in the Landau symbol
O(\ )
can be chosen to depend only on
U
,
V
and
\delta
.

Proof.

We will rely on Corollary
5
and Proposition
3
.

First consider the case where
-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta
. If
t\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\right]
, then

\textstyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\delta\leqslant\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-t\right)\leqslant\pi-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\delta

for
n
large enough.
Because this quantity is in a compact subset of
\left]0,\pi\right[
, we can combine Proposition
3
with linear approximation to obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-t\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\big(-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}-2t\mathrm{i}}\big)+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(-\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)+\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}\frac{d}{ds}\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(-\mathrm{e}^{s-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)_{|s=0}+O(\frac{1}{n^{2}})

\displaystyle=\textstyle\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(-\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)-\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}\log\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{Li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-t\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\big({\textstyle\frac{n}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\big(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-t\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\exp\left({\textstyle\frac{n}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(-\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)+{\textstyle\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\log\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\exp\left({\textstyle\frac{n}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(-\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

We now use the relation (
6
) connecting the dilogarithm and Lobachevsky functions. This property enables us to conclude that

(7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{Li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}-t\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\exp\left(\textstyle\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-t\right)+\frac{n\pi}{48}\mathrm{i}-\frac{n}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

In particular, the case
t=0
gives

(8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{Li}_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\left(\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{n}+\frac{U}{2n\mathrm{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\exp\left(\textstyle\frac{n\pi}{48}\mathrm{i}+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

since

.

Combining Corollary
5
with (
7
) and (

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{{\frac{n}{2\pi}}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-t\right)-\frac{n}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}}\ 2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\ \mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}

for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with

. This is what we wanted. More precisely,

with

\widetilde{g}_{n}(t)=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\ \mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\exp\left(\textstyle\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\textstyle\frac{3\pi}{2}-t\right)-\frac{11n\pi}{48}\mathrm{i}-\frac{n}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}+\frac{n}{2}t\mathrm{i}+O\left(\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

Lemma 7
.

Given
U
,
V\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{e}^{V}=1+\mathrm{e}^{U}
, choose
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
,

and
v=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}V}
for every
n
. If
\delta>0
is sufficiently small and if
n
is sufficiently large, then

\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(2\delta\right)}\right)

whenever
-\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
or
\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
.

In addition, the constants hidden in the condition “
n
sufficiently large” and in the Landau symbol
O(\ )
can be chosen to depend only on
U
,
V
and
\delta
.

The term
\Lambda(2\delta)
in the estimate could be replaced by
\Lambda(\delta^{\prime})
for any
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
.

Proof.

By definition of
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j+1)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lvert 1+uq^{-2j-2}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=\frac{\big\lvert 1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}U-\frac{4\pi j}{n}\mathrm{i}-\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}\big\rvert}{\big\lvert\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}V}\big\rvert}.

The denominator
\big\lvert\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}V}\big\rvert
is bounded away from 0 by a positive constant depending only on
V
.
Because
-\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
or
\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
, the numerator
\big\lvert 1+\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}U-\frac{4\pi j}{n}\mathrm{i}-\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}\big\rvert
is close to
0
for
\delta
small and
n

1
, and therefore that
\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert
is a decreasing function of
j
if
\delta
is small enough and if
n
is sufficiently large.

As a consequence if
\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
or, equivalently,
\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\leqslant j\leqslant\frac{n}{4}+\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}\left(u,v\,|\,j\left\lfloor\textstyle\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right)\right\rvert

where the floor function
\lfloor a\rfloor
denotes the largest integer less than or equal to
a
. Since
\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta-\frac{2\pi}{n}\leqslant\frac{2\pi}{n}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta
, we can apply Lemma
6
to estimate
\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}\left(u,v\,|\,\left\lfloor\textstyle\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right)\right\rvert
and conclude that

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2\pi}{n}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\delta+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}\right)=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(2\delta\right)}\right)

for
\delta
sufficiently small and
n
sufficiently large, using for the second and third equalities the property that the Lobachevsky function

is increasing on a neighborhood of 0. This proves the required estimate when
\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
.

Similarly, when
-\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}\left(u,v\,|\,\left\lfloor\textstyle-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right)\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}\left(u,v\,|\,\left\lfloor\textstyle\frac{3n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\right)\right\rvert,

using the periodicity of
{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)
modulo
n
. Then,
\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta-\frac{2\pi}{n}\leqslant\frac{2\pi}{n}\left\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}-\frac{n\delta}{2\pi}\right\rfloor\leqslant\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta
and applying Lemma
6
again gives

\left\lvert{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\right\rvert=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(-\pi+\delta+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}\right)=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(2\delta\right)}\right).

This concludes the proof in this case as well.
∎

3.2.
A general lemma

After the estimates of §
3.1
, our asymptotic analysis of the sum
\Sigma_{n}
will be based on the following elementary (and relatively classical) lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let the continuous function
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
attain its maximum at a unique point
x_{0}\in\left]a,b\right[
, and let
g_{n}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
be a sequence of continuous functions that uniformly converges to
g_{\infty}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
with
g_{\infty}(x_{0})\neq 0
. Suppose that
f
is twice differentiable on a neighborhood of
x_{0}
and that
f^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})\neq 0
. Then, as
n
tends to
\infty
,

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\ a\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\asymp{\textstyle\frac{g_{\infty}(x_{0})}{\sqrt{-2\pi f^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})}}}\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{nf(x_{0})}

(12)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\ a\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b}(-1)^{j}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\prec\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{nf(x_{0})}.

Here the symbols
A\asymp B
and
A^{\prime}\prec B^{\prime}
respectively mean that, as
n
tends to
\infty
, the ratio
A/B
converges to 1 while
A^{\prime}/B^{\prime}
converges to 0.

In the above estimates, it is crucial that the function
f
be real valued.

Proof.

By a change of variable we can assume that
x_{0}=0
, which will alleviate the notation.
We can then write

f(x)=f(0)+x^{2}h(x)

for a continuous function
h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
with
h(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)<0
.

Also, since
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)<0
, there exists
a^{\prime}<0
and
b^{\prime}>0
such that
f
is increasing on
[a^{\prime},0]
and decreasing on
[0,b^{\prime}]
. We first consider the terms in
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
. Then,

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{n}}}\mathrm{e}^{-nf\left(0\right)}\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{n}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi^{2}j^{2}}{n}h\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{n}(x)\,dx

where
F_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is the step function defined by

F_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi^{2}j^{2}}{n}h\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}&\text{if }\frac{2\pi j}{\sqrt{n}}\leqslant x<\frac{2\pi(j+1)}{\sqrt{n}}\text{ for some }j\in\mathbb{Z}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ with }{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For a fixed
x
, if
\frac{2\pi j}{\sqrt{n}}\leqslant x<\frac{2\pi(j+1)}{\sqrt{n}}
, then
\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{2\pi}{n}<\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}
and therefore
\frac{2\pi j}{n}
tends to
0
as
n
tends to
\infty
. It follows that the function
F_{n}
simply converges to the function

F_{\infty}(x)=g_{\infty}\left(0\right)\mathrm{e}^{x^{2}h(0)}

as
n
tends to
\infty
.

Because the functions
g_{n}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
uniformly converge to the continuous function
g_{\infty}
, their moduli
\left\lvert g_{n}\right\rvert
are uniformly bounded by a constant
M
. Also,
h(x)<0
for every
x\neq 0
since
f(x)<f(0)
, while
h(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)<0
is also negative by hypothesis. It follows that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
h(x)\leqslant-\varepsilon
for every
x\in[a,b]
.

If
x\geqslant\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}
, it follows from the definition of
F_{n}(x)
and from the fact that the function
y\mapsto y^{2}h(y)=f(y)-f(0)
is decreasing on
[0,b^{\prime}]
that

\left\lvert F_{n}(x)\right\rvert\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{n\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)^{2}h\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{-\left(x-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\!\varepsilon}\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}x^{2}},

using the fact that
\frac{2\pi j}{n}>\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{2\pi}{n}
when
\frac{2\pi j}{\sqrt{n}}\leqslant x<\frac{2\pi(j+1)}{\sqrt{n}}
, and the naïve property that
x-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{n}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}x
when
x\geqslant\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}
for the last inequality.

If
x\leqslant 0
, the fact that the function
y\mapsto y^{2}h(y)
is increasing on
[a^{\prime},0]
similarly gives

\left\lvert F_{n}(x)\right\rvert\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{n\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}h\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)}\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon x^{2}}.

Finally, if
0\leqslant x<\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}
, by definition
F_{n}(x)
is equal to
g_{n}(0)
or
g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi^{2}}{n}h\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}
. Since
h\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)<0
, it follows that its absolute value
\left\lvert F_{n}(x)\right\rvert
is bounded by
M
.

Therefore, in all cases,
\left\lvert F_{n}(x)\right\rvert
is bounded by the integrable function

F(x)=\begin{cases}M\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon x^{2}}&\text{if }x<0\\
M&\text{if }0\leqslant x<\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}\\
M\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}x^{2}}&\text{if }x\geqslant\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}.\end{cases}

By Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\textstyle\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{n}}}\mathrm{e}^{-nf\left(0\right)}\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{\infty}(x)\,dx=g_{\infty}(0)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{x^{2}h(0)}dx

\displaystyle=\textstyle g_{\infty}(0)\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{-h(0)}}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}g_{\infty}(0)}{\sqrt{-f^{\prime\prime}(0)}}.

This proves that

\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\asymp{\textstyle\frac{g_{\infty}(0)}{\sqrt{-2\pi f^{\prime\prime}(0)}}}\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{nf(0)}.

We still have to take care of the terms that are in the intervals
\left[a,a^{\prime}\right[
and
\left]b^{\prime},b\right]
. Since
f
achieves its maximum only at
0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
f(x)\leqslant f(0)-\varepsilon
for every
x
in
\left[a,a^{\prime}\right[
or
\left]b^{\prime},b\right]
. Also, remember that the moduli
|g_{n}(x)|
are uniformly bounded by a constant
M
. Then

\left|\sum_{a\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<a^{\prime}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\right|\leqslant\left(\textstyle\frac{a^{\prime}-a}{2\pi}n+1\right)M\mathrm{e}^{n(f(0)-\varepsilon)}\prec\mathrm{e}^{nf(0)}.

A similar property holds for the interval
\left]b^{\prime},b\right]
.

Combining these estimates for the contributions of the intervals
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
,
\left[a,a^{\prime}\right[
and
\left]b^{\prime},b\right]
, we obtain that

\sum_{a\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\asymp{\textstyle\frac{g_{\infty}(0)}{\sqrt{-2\pi f^{\prime\prime}(0)}}}\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{nf(0)}.

which is our required estimate (
11
) (when
x_{0}=0
).

To prove (
12
), we again restrict attention to the contribution of the interval
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
. We then group consecutive terms in the alternating sum, and consider

S_{0}=\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{4\pi k}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}-\frac{2\pi}{n}}\left(g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{4\pi k}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{4\pi k}{n}\right)}-g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{4\pi k}{n}+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{4\pi k}{n}+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}\right).

We begin with an algebraic manipulation, and split this sum as
S_{0}=S_{1}+S_{2}
with

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{4\pi k}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}-\frac{2\pi}{n}}g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{4\pi k}{n}\right)\check{f}_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{4\pi k}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{4\pi k}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{4\pi k}{n}\leqslant b^{\prime}-\frac{2\pi}{n}}\check{g}_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{4\pi k}{n}+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{4\pi k}{n}+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)}

for the functions

\displaystyle\check{f}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{e}^{n\left(f\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)-f\left(x\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\check{g}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{n}\left(\textstyle x-\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)-g_{n}(x).

As
n
tends to infinity, the function
\check{f}_{n}(x)
uniformly converges to
1-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi f^{\prime}(x)}
. The argument that we used to prove (
11
) then shows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\textstyle\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-nf(0)}S_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{-f^{\prime\prime}(0)}}\,g_{\infty}(0)\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi f^{\prime}(0)}\right)=0

since
f^{\prime}(0)=0
.

Similarly,
\check{g}_{n}(x)
uniformly converges to 0 as
n
tends to
\infty
. Again, this implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\textstyle\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-nf(0)}S_{2}=0.

The combination of these two properties shows that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-nf(0)}S_{0}
tends to 0 as
n
tends to
\infty
. In other words,
S_{0}\prec\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{nf(0)}
.

For the original sum of (
12
), the difference

\sum_{a\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}\leqslant b}(-1)^{j}g\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}-S_{0}

consists of approximately
\frac{a^{\prime}-a+b-b^{\prime}}{2\pi}n
terms whose modulus is bounded by
M\mathrm{e}^{n(f(0)-\varepsilon)}
. As in the proof of (
11
), the contribution of these remaining terms is
\prec\mathrm{e}^{nf(0)}
. With our estimate for
S_{0}
, this proves (
12
).

This completes the proof of (
11
) and (
12
) when
x_{0}=0
. As indicated at the beginning, the general case follows by a change of variable.
∎

3.3.
Asymptotics of the sum
\Sigma_{n}

We now have all the tools we need to find the asymptotics, as
n
tends to infinity, of the sum

\Sigma_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,2i)q^{2i^{2}-\widehat{k}i}

of (
2
).

Proposition 9
.

Let
U
,
V\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{e}^{V}=1+\mathrm{e}^{U}
be given, as well as an integer
\widehat{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For every odd
n
, set
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
,
u=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}U}
and
v=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}V}
. Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,2i)q^{2i^{2}-\widehat{k}i}\asymp c_{n}(U,V,\widehat{k})\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}

as
n\to\infty
, where

c_{n}\big(U,V,\widehat{k}\big)=\begin{cases}3^{-\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\widehat{k}\pi\mathrm{i}}{6}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{V}{6}}\left(\textstyle\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}&\text{ if }\widehat{k}\text{ is odd,}\\
3^{-\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{-2\widehat{k}\pi\mathrm{i}}{3}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2V}{3}}\ \big(1-\mathrm{i}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}U}\big)\left(\textstyle\frac{1-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}&\text{ if }\widehat{k}\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

Note that
c_{n}\big(U,V,\widehat{k}\big)
is independent of
n
when
\widehat{k}
is odd, and depends only on the congruence class of
n
modulo 8 when
\widehat{k}
is even. In addition, the modulus
\left|c_{n}\big(U,V,\widehat{k}\big)\right|
depends only on
n
modulo 4 in this second case.

Proof.

Let us focus attention on the case where
\widehat{k}
is even. The argument will be essentially identical when
\widehat{k}
is odd.

We first rewrite the sum
\Sigma_{n}
in a slightly simpler form, by an algebraic manipulation. For this, let
\omega
be the (unique) square root of
q
such that
\omega^{n}=1
, namely
\omega=-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
. Then the function
j\mapsto{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}
is
n
–periodic. As
i
goes from 1 to
n
,
j=2i
also ranges over all numbers from
1
to
n
modulo
n
, since
n
is odd. This enable us to rewrite the sum
\Sigma_{n}
as

(13)

\Sigma_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,2i)\omega^{4i^{2}-2\widehat{k}i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}.

Choose a small
\delta>0
as in Lemma
7
. Then, using the periodicity of its terms, we can write the sum

\Sigma_{n}=\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

as
\Sigma_{n}=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}
with

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\kern-10.0pt\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta}\kern-10.0pt{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\kern-10.0pt\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\kern-10.0pt{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\kern-10.0pt\sum_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta}\kern-10.0pt{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle=\kern-10.0pt\sum_{\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta}\kern-10.0pt{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}.

By Lemma
7
, each term of the sum
S_{1}
is an
O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(2\delta\right)}\right)
. Since
S_{1}
has
\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{\pi}n\right\rfloor
terms and
\Lambda(2\delta)<\Lambda(3\delta)
, if follows that

(14)

S_{1}=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(3\delta\right)}\right).

Similarly

(15)

S_{3}=O\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(3\delta\right)}\right).

We now consider the sum
S_{2}
. By the first statement of Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\mathrm{e}^{n\widetilde{f}\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\ \widetilde{g}_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)(-1)^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}(j^{2}-\widehat{k}j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}(-1)^{j}\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\ g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)

for the functions
\widetilde{f}
and
\widetilde{g}_{n}
of Lemma
6
, and if we set
f(t)=\widetilde{f}(t)+\frac{1}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}
and
g_{n}(t)=\widetilde{g}_{n}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\widehat{k}}{2}t\mathrm{i}}
. We are here using the fact that
\widehat{k}
is even, so that
(-1)^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}=(-1)^{j}
.

In particular,
f(t)=\widetilde{f}(t)+\frac{1}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-u\right)
.

Remark 10
.

We pause here to emphasize a critical outcome of this computation, which is that
f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-u\right)
is real. This property will enable us to apply Lemma
8
, but is very specific to the special case
\varphi=LR
considered here. In the case of a general diffeomorphism
\varphi
, the existence of a phase leads to cancellations requiring more sophisticated methods, such as the saddle point method used in
[
BWY22
]
.

We now return to the proof, and apply Lemma
8
.
Over the interval
\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\right]
, the function
f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-t\right)
admits a unique maximum at
t=\frac{\pi}{3}
, where
f\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)
and
f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}<0
. The function

g_{n}(t)=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\widehat{k}}{2}t\mathrm{i}}\ \left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}

uniformly converges to

g_{\infty}(t)=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\widehat{k}}{2}t\mathrm{i}}\ \left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}.

We can therefore apply Lemma
8
, which shows that

(16)

S_{2}\prec\sqrt{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}.

Finally, we can tackle
S_{4}
. By the second statement of Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta}{\mathrm{QDL}}^{q}(u,v\,|\,j)\omega^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta}(-1)^{j}\mathrm{e}^{n\widehat{f}\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\ \widehat{g}_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)(-1)^{j^{2}-\widehat{k}j}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}(j^{2}-\widehat{k}j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta\leqslant\frac{2\pi j}{n}<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta}\mathrm{e}^{nf\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)}\ g_{n}\left(\textstyle\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)

for the functions
\widehat{f}
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
of Lemma
6
, and where this time
f(t)=\widehat{f}(t)+\frac{1}{4\pi}t^{2}\mathrm{i}
and
g_{n}(t)=\widehat{g}_{n}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\widehat{k}}{2}t\mathrm{i}}
.

Again, the function
f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-t\right)
admits a unique maximum over the interval
\left[\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\frac{3\pi}{2}-\delta\right]
, at
t=\frac{4\pi}{3}
where
f\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)
and
f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}
. We cannot quite apply Lemma
8
as stated, as the function

g_{n}(t)=2^{\frac{U-2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{n\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\widehat{k}}{2}t\mathrm{i}}\ \big(1-\mathrm{i}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}U}\big)\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{-2t\mathrm{i}}\right)^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}-U}{4\pi\mathrm{i}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{tV}{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}

does not have one, but four limits as
n
tends to infinity. More precisely, as the odd integer

We now consider the factor

We will see that the modulus

has two limits as

modulo

4.1.
An algebraic manipulation

Some of the quantum dilogarithms
{\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,i)
grow exponentially with

while others decrease exponentially. We first rearrange factors so that

is expressed as a product of terms reasonably close to 1, while also splitting this product as a preparation for the next steps.

Lemma 11
.

For every odd

, let
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
and
u=q\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{n}A}
for a fixed number

with
\mathrm{e}^{A}\neq-1
.

If
n=1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
, then for
m=\frac{n-1}{4}

\displaystyle\left|D^{q}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{2j-1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{2j-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}.

If

, then for

\displaystyle\left|D^{q}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+1}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{2j}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{2j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}.

In particular, in both cases
\left|D^{q}(u)\right|=1
when
A
is real.

Proof.

We begin by grouping together the quantum dilogarithms
{\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,i)
and
{\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,n-i)
. Using the property that

v^{n}=1+u^{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+uq^{-2k})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-i}(1+uq^{-2k})\prod_{j=1}^{i}(1+uq^{2j-2})

(setting
j=n-k+1
for the last term), we obtain that

\displaystyle\big|{\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,i){\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,n-i)\big|

\displaystyle=\left|v\right|^{-i}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\left|1+uq^{-2j}\right|\left|v\right|^{-n+i}\prod_{k=1}^{n-i}\left|1+uq^{-2k}\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{\left|1+uq^{-2j}\right|}{\left|1+uq^{2j-2}\right|}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{\left|1+uq^{-2j}\right|}{\left|1+\bar{u}q^{-2j+2}\right|}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{A}{n}}q^{-2j+1}\right|}{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\bar{A}}{n}}q^{-2j+1}\right|}.

Since
{\mathrm{QDL}}(u,v\,|\,n)=1
, can therefore rewrite

\displaystyle\left|D^{q}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\frac{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{A}{n}}q^{-2j+1}\right|}{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\bar{A}}{n}}q^{-2j+1}\right|}=\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{A}{n}}q^{-2i+1}\right|^{\frac{n-1}{2}-i+1}}{\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\bar{A}}{n}}q^{-2i+1}\right|^{\frac{n-1}{2}-i+1}}.

We now group these terms into pairs of indices that are symmetric with respect to the midpoint of
\{1,2,\dots,\frac{n-1}{2}\}
.

Namely, when
\frac{n-1}{2}
is even and equal to
2m
, we pair

with
i^{\prime}=m+j
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Note that

\displaystyle\left|1+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{A}{n}}q^{-2i+1}\right|^{\frac{n-1}{2}-i+1}

\displaystyle=\left|1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}\right|^{m+j}=\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}

\displaystyle\left|D^{q}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+1}}\ \prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m+j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}-1\right|^{m-j+1}}

4.2.

Lemma 13

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2j-1}{4m+1}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2j}{4m+3}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\log 2}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A.

Proof.

We will focus attention on the first case, coming from (
18
). The other case will essentially be identical.

We can write the sum as

\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)

with

f_{m}(t)=\left(4m+1\right)\left(2t-\frac{1}{4m+1}\right)\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}+4\pi\mathrm{i}t}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}+4\pi\mathrm{i}t}-1\right|}.

Sublemma 14
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=\left\lfloor\varepsilon(4m+1)\right\rfloor}^{m}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=-2\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A\int_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt.

Proof.

For every

,

\log\left|\mathrm{e}^{u+4\pi\mathrm{i}t}-1\right|=\log\left|\mathrm{e}^{4\pi\mathrm{i}t}-1\right|+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}u+\frac{1}{2}\cot 2\pi t\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}u+O\left(\left|u\right|^{2}\right)

by linear approximation. Therefore,

f_{m}(t)=-2t\cot 2\pi t\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)

where the constant hidden in the symbol

depends only on a compact subset of
\left]0,\frac{1}{4}\right]
containing

. In particular, the function

converges to

as

\left]0,\frac{1}{4}\right]
.

The result then immediately follows by a Riemann sum approximation.
∎

Based on Sublemma

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=-2\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt.

Sublemma 15
.

For a given
A

\mathrm{e}^{A}\neq-1
, the absolute values of the terms

f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=(2j-1)\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

with

and

.

Note that, when

, the function

itself is unbounded on the interval
\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right]

Proof.

It is easier to use a proof by contradiction. Suppose that the property fails, namely that there exists a sequence of integers
m_{k}
and
j_{k}
, with

, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)\right|=+\infty.

Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can arrange that the sequence
\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}
has a limit
L\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right]
as
k\to\infty
. We will then distinguish three cases, and reach a contradiction in each of them.

Case 1.
The limit
L=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}
is different from
0
.

In this case, the points
\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}
stay in a compact subset of
\left]0,\frac{1}{4},\right]
. In the proof of Sublemma

converges to

uniformly on this compact subset. Therefore,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)\right|=\left|2L\cot 2\pi L\,\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A\right|\neq+\infty,

which provides the contradiction in this case.

Case 2.
The integers
j_{k}
are uniformly bounded.

After passing to a subsequence, we can assume that the
j_{k}
are all equal to some integer
j_{0}
. Then,

\displaystyle f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(2j_{0}-1)\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1\right|}=(2j_{0}-1)\log\frac{\left|\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)\right|}{\left|\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)\right|}

\displaystyle=(2j_{0}-1)\log\frac{\left|{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}}\right)\right|}{\left|{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}}\right)\right|}

\displaystyle\to(2j_{0}-1)\log\frac{\left|{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}\right|}{\left|{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{0}-\pi\mathrm{i}}\right|}\text{ as }k\to\infty.

This limit is finite by our hypothesis that
\mathrm{e}^{A}\neq-1
, and this again contradicts the unboundedness of the sequence
\left|f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)\right|
.

When neither Case 1 nor Case 2 hold, we can again pass to a subsequence to be in the following remaining case.

Case 3.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}=0
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}j_{k}=+\infty
.

Let us write

f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)=(2j_{k}-1)\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}.

Note that, as
k\to\infty
,
\frac{1}{4m_{k}+1}
converges to 0 faster than
\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\left(1-\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)\right)-1}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\left(1-\frac{\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)\right)-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1\right)-\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)}{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1\right)-\frac{\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}m_{k}}\right)}{1-\frac{\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}m_{k}}\right)}

using the fact that
\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1\asymp\frac{\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{m_{k}}
. Another application of the same estimate gives that

\frac{\bar{A}+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\right)^{-1}=O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}}\right),

from which we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m_{k}+1}}-1}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\bar{A}-A}{4m_{k}+1}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-4\pi\mathrm{i}j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}}\right)^{-1}+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{2\pi j_{k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}^{2}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(2j_{k}-1)\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{2\pi j_{k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{m_{k}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{\pi}+O\left(\frac{j_{k}}{m_{k}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{j_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\to-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{\pi}\text{ as }k\to\infty.

This provides our final contradiction with the unboundedness of the sequence
\left|f_{m_{k}}\left(\frac{j_{k}}{4m_{k}+1}\right)\right|
, and therefore completes the proof of Sublemma
15
.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Lemma
13
.

Indeed, for every
\varepsilon>0
, Sublemma
14
shows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=\left\lfloor\varepsilon(4m+1)\right\rfloor}^{m}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=-2\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A\int_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt.

while Sublemma
15
proves that

\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\varepsilon(4m+1)\right\rfloor-1}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=O(\varepsilon).

It follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{4m+1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}f_{m}\left(\frac{j}{4m+1}\right)=-2\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt

after observing that this improper integral converges.

It turns out that the value of the above integral can be explicitly determined.

Sublemma 16
.

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt=\frac{\log 2}{8\pi}.

Proof.

By an algebraic manipulation followed by an integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}t\cot 2\pi t\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathrm{i}t+2\mathrm{i}t\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathrm{i}t\,dt+\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[t\log\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}\log\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\left[t^{2}\right]_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[t\log\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}}\left[\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi\mathrm{i}t}\right)\right]_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{32}+\frac{\log 2}{8\pi}-0+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}}\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}=\frac{\log 2}{8\pi},

using the special values
\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}(1)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
and
\operatorname{\mathrm{li}_{2}}(-1)=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}
of the dilogarithm function.
∎

This computation concludes the proof of Lemma
13
for the case when
n=4m+1
.

The case where
n=4m+3
is almost identical.
∎

–

) for

Lemma 17
.

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+1}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{\cosh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\cosh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+3}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{(A+\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{(A-\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
13
, we focus attention on the case where
n=4m+1
. The other case is almost identical.

We first rewrite

\displaystyle\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}.

Let us identify the leading terms of the numerator, namely

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-2A+2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}+1

\displaystyle=\textstyle\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)^{2}+2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right).

We see that, as
m
tends to
\infty
, the second term

\textstyle 2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)\asymp\left(\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)^{2}

dominates the first term

\textstyle\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)^{2}\asymp\left(\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}\right)^{2}.

A similar property holds for the denominator.

It is therefore natural to split

\displaystyle\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}

(20)

\displaystyle\qquad-\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}.

The last term is trivial since

\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}=\log\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\right|=0.

For the first term, our earlier computation shows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\left(1+\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)^{2}}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\bigg(\textstyle 1+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\right)\left(\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{m^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2(4m+1)^{2}}{(4\pi j-2\pi)^{2}}+O\left(1\right)\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{j^{2}}\right)\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{j^{2}}\right),

using the property that

\frac{1}{1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}}=\frac{2(4m+1)^{2}}{(4\pi j-2\pi)^{2}}+O\left(1\right)

when
1\leqslant j\leqslant m
. (This estimate would clearly fail if
j
was allowed to get close to
2m
.)

As a consequence, if we define

a_{m}(j)=\begin{cases}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+1}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}&\text{if }1\leqslant j\leqslant m\\
0&\text{if }j>m,\end{cases}

then the absolute value of each term
a_{m}(j)
is bounded (independently of
m
) by a term
a(j)
such that the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a(j)
is convergent. In addition, the above estimates show that, for each
j
,
a_{m}(j)
converges to

a_{\infty}(j)=\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\left(1+\frac{\left(-A+\pi\mathrm{i}\right)^{2}}{16\pi^{2}\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)

as
m
tends to
\infty
. By Lebesgue dominated convergence (or an elementary argument), it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{m}(j)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{\infty}(j)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4}\log\left|1+\frac{\left(-A+\pi\mathrm{i}\right)^{2}}{16\pi^{2}\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\right|.

Using Euler’s formula that

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\pi^{2}\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)=\cos z,

this infinite sum is equal to

\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\cosh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}\right|.

Therefore,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+1}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\cosh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}\right|.

A similar argument shows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+1}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j-2\pi}{4m+1}\right)}=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\cosh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}\right|.

Combining these two limits with (
20
), this proves the first statement of Lemma
17
, namely that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+1}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-3\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{\cosh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\cosh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|.

The second statement of Lemma
17
is proved by a very similar argument. We will just point out the main steps. As in (
20
), we first split

\displaystyle\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right|}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j}{4m+1}\right)}

(21)

\displaystyle\qquad-\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j}{4m+1}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}

and observe that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}}=0
.

The same estimates as before then show that the limit

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+3}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j}{4m+1}\right)}

is equal to

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4}\log\left|1+\frac{\left(-A+\pi\mathrm{i}\right)^{2}}{16\pi^{2}j^{2}}\right|=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|,

using the other Euler formula that

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\pi^{2}j^{2}}\right)=\frac{\sin z}{z}.

Similarly,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+3}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}\log\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}-1\right)}{2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+1}}\left(1-\cos\frac{4\pi j}{4m+1}\right)}=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|.

Combining these two limits with (
21
) then gives the expected limit

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m-j+1}{4m+3}\log\frac{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}{\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\bar{A}+4\pi\mathrm{i}j+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|\left|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-A+4\pi\mathrm{i}j-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4m+3}}-1\right|}=\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{(A+\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{(A-\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|.\qed

4.4.
The limit of
\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}

We can now determine the limit of the terms
\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}
as
n
tends to
\infty
in a given congruence class modulo 4.

Proposition 18
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}
be given, with
\mathrm{e}^{A}\neq-1
. For every odd
n
, set
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
and
u=q\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}A}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n=1\,\mathrm{mod}\,4\end{subarray}}\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{4\pi}}\left|\frac{\cosh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\cosh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
n=3\,\mathrm{mod}\,4\end{subarray}}\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{4\pi}}\left|\frac{\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Proof.

If we let
n
tend to
\infty
with
n=3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
, the combination of (
19
) with Lemmas
12
,
13
and
17
shows that
\log\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}
converges to

\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{\left|A-\pi\mathrm{i}\right|}{\left|\bar{A}-\pi\mathrm{i}\right|}-\frac{\log 2}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A+\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{(A+\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{(A-\pi\mathrm{i})\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|=-\frac{\log 2}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A+\frac{1}{4}\log\left|\frac{\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|.

Therefore,
\left|D^{q}(u)\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}
converges to
2^{-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}A}{4\pi}}\left|\frac{\sinh\frac{A-\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}{\sinh\frac{A+\pi\mathrm{i}}{4}}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}
.

The argument is very similar for the case where
n
tend to
\infty
with
n=1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
.
∎

The asymptotics of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}

We now have all the ingredients needed to complete our asymptotic analysis of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
.

Remember from the setup of §
2
that we are considering the diffeomorphism
\varphi=LR=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
of the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
.
Let
[r]\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}(S_{1,1})
be a
\varphi
–invariant character associated to the periodic edge weight system
(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})
,
(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})
,
(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})=(a_{0},b_{0},c_{0})
, and let
\theta_{v}\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{v}}=a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}
. A choice of “logarithms”

,

,

determines correction factors

,
\widehat{m}_{0}
,
\widehat{n}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. In addition, we set
U_{1}=2\pi\mathrm{i}-A_{1}
and
U_{2}=2\pi\mathrm{i}-A_{2}
.

For every odd integer
n
, let
\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q}
be the intertwiner to the data of
\varphi
,
[r]
,
q=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{n}}
and
p_{v}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{n}\theta_{v}}

.
The following estimate immediately follows by application of Propositions
9
and
18
to the expression of

given in (

).

Theorem 19
.

For the above data,

\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|\asymp\frac{\Big\lvert c_{n}(U_{1},V_{1},\widehat{l}_{0})\Big\rvert\Big\lvert c_{n}(U_{2},V_{2},\widehat{m}_{0})\Big\rvert}{d_{n}(A_{1})d_{n}(A_{2})}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{n}{\pi}\Lambda\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)}

as

odd tends to

\left|c_{n}(U,V,\widehat{k})\right|=\begin{cases}3^{-\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Im}}U-2\pi}{4\pi}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}V}{6}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\operatorname{\mathfrak{Re}}U}{12}}&\text{ if }\widehat{k}\text{ is odd,}\\

Corollary 20
.

For the above data,

\lim_{n\,\text{odd}\,\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{4\pi}\operatorname{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\varphi}),

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem

Afterword

For the diffeomorphism

1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
of the one-puncture torus
S_{1,1}
, we greatly benefitted from the phase cancellation mentioned in Remark
10
, which enabled us to analyze the contributions of the leading terms in the double sum expression of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
given in (
1
).

The left-hand side of Figure

is a good illustration of the cancellations in this double sum, for a specific example where

,

,

. This picture, analogous to the one we encountered for a single sum in Figure

, provides a plot of all terms in the double sum. We can here observe a nice pattern consisting of 7 “petals”, 6 of which occur in three pairs of two petals sitting opposite to each other. It follows from our analysis in §
3
that, in such a pair of opposite petals, the contributions of the two petals essentially cancel out; at least, their sum is negligible compared to the contribution of the remaining petal that has no opposite. Note that, in this example, the leading contributions to
\operatorname{\mathrm{Trace}}\Lambda_{\varphi,r}^{q_{n}}
come from the smallest petal.

to introduce some order in the chaos. Interestingly enough, this analysis will lead us to an outcome that is similar to the one we observed for

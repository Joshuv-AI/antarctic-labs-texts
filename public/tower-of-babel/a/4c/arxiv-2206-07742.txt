Estimates for the shifted convolution sum involving Fourier coefficients of cusp forms of half-integral weight
Abhash Kumar Jha and Lalit Vaishya

Abstract
. In this article, we obtain certain estimate for the shifted convolution sum involving the Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight cusp forms.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11F30, 11F37

1.
Introduction

The estimates for the shifted convolution sums involving the Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms have been investigated by several authors. Selberg
[
15
]
started the study of shifted convolution sums and obtained the analytic properties of the
L
-function associated with cusp forms. Goldfeld
[
4
]
, by using the analytic and arithmetic property of Poincaré series obtained an estimate of the following shifted convolution sum:

\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)\overline{a_{g}(n+m)}e^{-n/X}},

where
f
and
g
are cusp forms of weight
k
and
l,
respectively for the full modular group, and
a_{f}(n)
(respectively
a_{g}(n)
) denotes the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
(respectively
g
).

Hafner
[
5
]
extended the result of Goldfeld
[
4
]
for congruence subgroups by using spectral decomposition method. Similar sums have also been considered for other kinds of automorphic forms, see the list
[
2
,
7
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
18
]
. Recently, Luo
[
11
]
obtained an estimate for the following shifted convolution sum:

(1)

S(f,g,b):=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n+b)a_{g}(n)G(n),

where
f
is a cusp form of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
for the group
\Gamma_{0}(4N),~g
is a cusp form of weight
l
or a Maass cusp form for the group
\Gamma_{0}(1),
and
G
is a smooth function with the support in
[\frac{X}{2},\frac{5X}{2}]
satisfying
G^{(p)}(x)\ll(\frac{X}{P})^{-p}
for all integer
p\geq 0
, where
P
is a real number with
1\leq P\leq X.
The aim of this paper is to obtain an estimate for
S(f,g,b)
when
f
and
g
are both half-integral weight cusp forms by using a similar method as in
[
11
]
. Now, we state the main result of the paper.

Let
f
be a cusp form of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
and level
4N
with Fourier series expansion

(2)

\begin{split}\quad f(\tau)&=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)n^{k/2-1/4}e(n\tau)},\end{split}

and
g
be a newform of weight
l+\frac{1}{2}
and level
4N
with Fourier series expansion

(3)

\begin{split}\quad g(\tau)&=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{g}(n)n^{l/2-1/4}e(n\tau)}.\end{split}

For a fixed positive integer
b
and a smooth function
G(x)
as in (
1
), we consider the following sum:

(4)

\begin{split}S(f,g,b)\quad&=\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n+b)a_{g}(n)G(n).\end{split}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f,g
and
G
be as above, and
N
be an odd and squarefree positive integer. Then, we have

(5)

\begin{split}S(f,g,b)\quad&=\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n+b)a_{g}(n)G(n)\quad\ll_{\epsilon,f,g,b,G}\quad X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
denotes the complex upper-half plane. For a complex number
\tau,
we use the notation
e(\tau):=e^{2\pi i\tau}.

The full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
of level
N\in\mathbb{N}
is defined as follows;

SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},ad-bc=1\right\},

\Gamma_{0}(N):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\mod N\right\}.

The group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the complex upper half-plane

\mathcal{H}
via fractional linear transformation as follows;

\gamma\tau:=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},~{\rm where}~\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})~{\rm and}~\tau\in\mathcal{H}.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z_{+}},~\gamma=\Bigl(\begin{matrix}a\!&\!b\\[-4.0pt]
c\!&\!d\end{matrix}\Bigr)\in\Gamma_{0}(4N),
and a holomorphic function
f:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
define the weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
slash operator as follows;

(6)

f{\mid}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\gamma}(\tau):=\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{2k+1}(c\tau+d)^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}f(\gamma\tau),

where
\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol and
\epsilon_{d}=\begin{cases}1\quad{\rm if}\quad d\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
i\quad{\rm if}\quad d\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1
.

A modular form of weight weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
for
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
is a complex-valued holomorphic function
f:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

f{\mid}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\gamma}(\tau)=f(\tau)\;\;\;\;{\rm for~all~}\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(4N).

(2)

f
is holomorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
with the Fourier series expansion given by

f(\tau)=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{f}(n)e(n\tau)}.

Further, a modular form
f
of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
for
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
is said to be a cusp form if it vanishes at every cusp of
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
.

We denote by
M_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
and
S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
the space of modular forms and the space of cusp forms of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
for
\Gamma_{0}(4N),
respectively. The Kohnen plus space
S^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
is the subspace of cusp forms in
S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
whose
n
-th Fourier coefficient vanishes whenever
({-1})^{k}n\equiv 2,3{\pmod{4}}.
Kohnen using certain operators developed the theory of newforms of half-integral weight parallel to the Atkin-Lehner theory of newforms in integral weight case. For more details on the theory of modular forms and newforms of half-integral weight, we refer to
[
8
,
9
,
12
,
16
]
. We assume the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for the Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight newfroms, i.e., for any
\epsilon>0
, we have
a_{f}(n)\ll_{\epsilon}n^{k/2-1/4+\epsilon}.

3.
Preparatory Lemmas

In this section, we state some lemmas and recall some of the properties of Poincaré series which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First we state the Poisson-Voronoi summation formula for half-integral weight cusp forms.

For
f(\tau)=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)n^{k/2-1/4}e(n\tau)}\in S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(4N)
and a smooth function
G(x)
with compact support in
(0,\infty),
the Poisson-Voronoi summation formula is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any positive integers
c
and
a
with
\gcd(a,c)=1,
we have

(7)

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)e\left(\frac{an}{c}\right)G(n)&=\frac{2\pi i^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{c}\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{2k+1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)e\left(-\frac{{d}n}{c}\right)H(n),\end{split}

where
H(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx,~d
is the multiplicative inverse of
a
modulo
c,
and
J_{\nu}(z)
denotes a Bessel function of order
\nu.

Proof.

For a proof, we refer to
[
3
, Section 5]
.
∎

For every positive integer
m
, we define the
m
-th Poincaré series
{P_{m}(\tau)}
of weight
k+\frac{1}{2}
on the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(4N)
by

(8)

\begin{split}P_{m}(\tau)&:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty,4N}\setminus\Gamma_{0}(4N)}e^{2\pi im\tau}\mid_{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\gamma}(\tau),\\
&=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty,4N}\setminus\Gamma_{0}(4N)}\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{2k+1}(c\tau+d)^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\pi im\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}},\end{split}

where
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
and
\Gamma_{\infty,4N}=\left\{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\cap\Gamma_{0}(4N)
.

It is well-known that
{P_{m}(\tau)}\in S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
for
m\geq 1.
The
m
-th Poincaré series
{P_{m}(\tau)}
has a Fourier series expansion given by

P_{m}(\tau)=\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{P_{m}}(n)n^{k/2-1/4}e(n\tau)},

(9)

\begin{split}m^{k/2-1/4}a_{P_{m}}(n)&=\delta_{m,n}+2\pi i^{k+1/2}\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-1}S_{k}(m,n;c)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right),\end{split}

where
J_{\nu}(z)
denotes a Bessel function of order
\nu,
and
S_{k}(m,n;c)
denotes the Kloosterman sum defined by

\begin{split}S_{k}(m,n;c)=\sum_{a\!\!\!\!\pmod{c}\atop\gcd(a,c)=1}\epsilon_{a}^{-(2k+1)}\left(\dfrac{c}{a}\right)e\left(\frac{m{d}+na}{c}\right),\end{split}

here
d
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
a
modulo
c.

For more details on Poincaré series we refer to
[
3
, Section 6]
.

The Weil-Salié bound for the Kloosterman sum is given by
[
11
, pp. 2413]
:

(10)

\begin{split}S_{k}(m,n;c)\ll{\gcd(m,n,c)}^{1/2}d(c)c^{1/2},\end{split}

where for a positive integer
n,~d(n)
denotes the number of positive divisors of
n
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any integer
p\geq 0
and fixed
m,b\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\begin{split}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-1/2}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-1/2}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx\quad\ll\quad X([Pc(Xn)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{p}+n^{-\frac{p}{2}})\\
\qquad\quad\times~~{\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{k-1/2}\right){\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{nX}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{nX}}{c}\right)^{l-1/2}\right).\\
\end{split}

Proof.

For a proof, we refer to
[
11
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For sufficiently large
X
and any arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
, we have

\begin{split}\displaystyle{\sum_{c\leq X}\frac{d(c)}{c}}=\frac{1}{2}(\log X)^{2}+2\gamma\log X+O(1)\ll({\log X})^{2}\ll X^{\epsilon},\end{split}

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.

Proof.

Proof is an application of Abel’s partial summation formula and we refer to
[
1
, pp.70]
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

It is sufficient to obtain the estimate of
S(f,g,b)
when
f
is a Poincaré series, because the space of cusp forms
S_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4N))
is generated by the Poincaré series
\{{P_{m}(\tau)}\mid{m\geq 1}\}
. Thus, we obtain the estimate for
S(P_{m},g,b)=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{P_{m}}(n+b)a_{g}(n)G(n)},
where
a_{P_{m}}(n)
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
m
-th Poincaré series.
We assume that
X
is sufficiently large depending on
m
.

Substitute the expression of
a_{P_{m}}(n)
from (
9
) to obtain

\begin{split}&S({P_{m},g,b})=\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{P_{m}}(n+b)a_{g}(n)G(n)},\\
&=\!\!\frac{2\pi i^{k+1/2}}{m^{k/2-1/4}}\!\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}\!\!\!a_{g}(n)G(n)}\!\!\left(\!\!\frac{1}{2\pi i^{k+1/2}}\delta_{m,n+b}\!+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}\!\!\!\!\!\!c^{-1}S_{k}(m,n+b;c)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(n+b)}}{c}\right)\right),\end{split}

which yields

\begin{split}|S({P_{m},g,b})|&\ll|\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-1}\sum_{a\pmod{c}\atop\gcd(a,c)=1}\epsilon_{a}^{-(2k+1)}\left(\dfrac{c}{a}\right)e\left(\frac{m{d}+ba}{c}\right)}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{g}(n)G(n)e\left(\frac{na}{c}\right)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(n+b)}}{c}\right)|.\end{split}

Now, we apply Poisson-Voronoi summation formula to obtain

\begin{split}|S({P_{m},g,b})|&\ll|\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-1}\sum_{a\pmod{c}\atop\gcd(a,c)=1}\epsilon_{a}^{-(2k+1)}\left(\dfrac{c}{a}\right)e\left(\frac{m{d}+ba}{c}\right)}\times\frac{2\pi i^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{c}\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{2k+1}\\
&\times\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{g}(n)e\left(\frac{-n{d}}{c}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx|.\end{split}

(11)

\begin{split}|S({P_{m},g,b})|&\ll|\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx|.\end{split}

Without loss of generality, we may assume
n\ll X^{A}
, for a fixed large constant
A>0
. We now break the sum in (
11
) into three parts as follows;

Part I:

n\ll X^{4\epsilon}
.

Part II:

n\gg X^{4\epsilon}
and
Pc<(nX)^{1/2}X^{-\epsilon}
.

Part III:

n\gg X^{4\epsilon}
and
Pc\geq(nX)^{1/2}X^{-\epsilon}
.

Estimate for Part I:
In this case, the contribution for the sum (
11
) denoted by
S_{1},
is at most
X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}
which is obtained as follows:

\begin{split}S_{1}&=\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{n\ll X^{4\epsilon}}a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)\\
&\quad\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx.\end{split}

We apply the Weil-Salié bound for the Kloosterman sum
S_{k}(m-n,b;c)
and the Ramanujan-Petersson bound for the Fourier coefficients
a_{g}(n),
and then use Lemma
3.2
(with
p=0
);

\begin{split}|S_{1}|&\ll\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-2+1/2}d(c)}\sum_{n\ll X^{4\epsilon}}n^{\epsilon}X([Pc(Xn)^{-1/2}]^{p}+n^{-p/2})\\
&\qquad\times{\rm min}((\sqrt{X}/c)^{-1/2},(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2})\times{\rm min}((\sqrt{nX}/c)^{-1/2},(\sqrt{nX}/c)^{l-1/2}),\end{split}

\begin{split}|S_{1}|&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-3/2}d(c)}\quad{\rm min}((\sqrt{X}/c)^{-1/2},(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}),\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{X}}c^{-3/2}d(c)(\sqrt{X}/c)^{-1/2}}+\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq\sqrt{X}}c^{-3/2}d(c)(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}}\right),\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\left(\displaystyle{X^{-\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{X}}c^{-1}d(c)}+\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq\sqrt{X}}c^{-3/2}d(c)}\right),\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\left(\displaystyle{X^{-\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{c\leq\sqrt{X}}c^{-1}d(c)}+\displaystyle{\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}c^{-3/2}d(c)}\right)\ll X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon},\end{split}

here we have used Lemma
3.3
and partial summation formula to obtain the estimate for first sum, and the last sum is an absolutely convergent series.

Estimate for Part II:
In this case, the integral in Lemma
3.2
is of order
O(X^{-p\epsilon})
. Therefore, by choosing sufficiently large
p
, we see that the integral is of order
O(X^{-A}).
Hence the contribution for the sum in (
11
) is negligible.

Estimate for Part III:
In this case, we again decompose the sum

(12)

\begin{split}&\displaystyle{\sum_{c\geq 1,4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{n\gg X^{4\epsilon}\atop Pc\geq(nX)^{1/2}X^{-\epsilon}}a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)\\
&\quad\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx\end{split}

into sub-sums of type
M\leq n\leq 2M
using the dyadic division method and break the sum over
c
into the following two parts;

Part (a):

c>\sqrt{2MX}
.

Part (b):

\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}
.

Estimate for Part (a):
In this case, the contribution for the sum, denoted by
S_{a},
is at most
X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}
which is obtained as follows:

\begin{split}S_{a}&=\log X\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{c>\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}|a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)|\\
&\quad\times\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx\right|.\end{split}

Now, apply the Weil-Salié bound for the Kloosterman sum, Ramanujan-Petersson bound for the Fourier coefficients
a_{g}(n),
and then use Lemma
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\log X\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{c>\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2+1/2}d(c)}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}n^{\epsilon}\quad X([Pc(Xn)^{-1/2}]^{p}+n^{-p/2})

\displaystyle\qquad\times

\displaystyle{\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{k-1/2}\right){\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{MX}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{MX}}{c}\right)^{l-1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{c>\sqrt{2MX}}c^{-3/2}d(c)}(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}\times(\sqrt{MX}/c)^{l-1/2}\right).

Finally (by taking
p=0
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{a}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{c>\sqrt{2MX}}c^{-3/2}d(c)}(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}X^{\frac{k-1/2}{2}}\displaystyle{\sum_{c>\sqrt{2MX}}c^{-k-1}d(c)}\right),

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}X^{\frac{k-1/2}{2}}\int_{x=\sqrt{2MX}}^{\infty}x^{-k-1+\epsilon}dx\right),

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}X^{\frac{k-1/2}{2}}(MX)^{-k/2+\epsilon}\right),

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1-k/2+\epsilon}X^{-\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\right)\ll X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}.

Estimate for Part (b):

\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}
.

If
\sqrt{X}\geq\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P},
then we have
M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}
. In this case, the contribution, denoted by
S_{b,1},
is at most
X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}
which is obtained as follows:

\begin{split}S_{b,1}&=\log X\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}|a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)|\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx\right|.\end{split}

Now, apply the Weil-Salié bound for the Kloosterman sum, Ramanujan-Petersson bound for the Fourier coefficients
a_{g}(n),
and then use Lemma
3.2
to obtain

\begin{split}S_{b,1}&\ll\log X\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2+1/2}d(c)}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}n^{\epsilon}\quad X([Pc(Xn)^{-1/2}]^{p}+n^{-p/2})\\
&\qquad\times{\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{k-1/2}\right){\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{MX}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{MX}}{c}\right)^{l-1/2}\right),\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}}c^{-3/2}d(c)\quad{\rm min}\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\right)^{k-1/2}\right)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left((\sqrt{MX}/c)^{-1/2}\right),\\
\end{cases}\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\times\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{X}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-3/2}d(c)}(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}\times(\sqrt{MX}/c)^{-1/2}\quad+\\
\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{X}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-3/2}d(c)}(\sqrt{X}/c)^{-1/2}\times(\sqrt{MX}/c)^{-1/2},\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\times\begin{cases}(MX)^{-1/4}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{X}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-1}d(c)}\quad+\\
X^{-1/2+1/4}M^{-1/4}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{X}\atop 4N\mid c}\frac{d(c)}{c}},\end{cases}\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}\left(M^{1+\epsilon}\times(MX)^{-1/4}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}\frac{d(c)}{c}}\right),\\
&\ll\underset{X^{4\epsilon}\leq M\leq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}}{\rm max}\left(M^{\frac{3}{4}}X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}\right)\ll X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

If
\sqrt{X}\leq\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}
, then we have
M\geq P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}
. In this case, the contribution, denoted by
S_{b,2},
is at most
X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}
which is obtained as follows (by taking
p=0
):

\begin{split}S_{b,2}&=\log X\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}|a_{g}(n)S_{k}(m-n,b;c)|\\
&\quad\times\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x)J_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{m(x+b)}}{c}\right)J_{l-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{nx}}{c}\right)dx\right|.\end{split}

Now, apply the Weil-Salié bound for the Kloosterman sum, Ramanujan-Petersson bound for the Fourier coefficients
a_{g}(n),
and then use Lemma
3.2
to obtain

\begin{split}S_{b,2}&=\log X\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}c^{-2}}\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}|n^{\epsilon}c^{1/2}d(c)|\times X([Pc(Xn)^{-1/2}]+n^{-p/2})\\
&\qquad\times{\rm min}((\sqrt{X}/c)^{-1/2},(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2})\times{\rm min}((\sqrt{nX}/c)^{-1/2},(\sqrt{nX}/c)^{l-1/2}),\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}}c^{-3/2}d(c)(\sqrt{X}/c)^{k-1/2}\times(\sqrt{MX}/c)^{-1/2}.\\
\end{split}

Since
k\geq 2
and
\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c

\left(i.e.,\frac{\sqrt{X}}{c}\leq\frac{PX^{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{M}}\leq 1\right),
therefore

\begin{split}S_{b,2}&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}}c^{-3/2}d(c)\left(\frac{PX^{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{M}}\right)^{3/2}\times(\sqrt{MX}/c)^{-1/2},\\
&\ll X^{1+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}X^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{3}{2}\epsilon}\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}M^{1+\epsilon}\displaystyle{\sum_{\sqrt{MX}\frac{X^{-\epsilon}}{P}\leq c\leq\sqrt{2MX}\atop 4N\mid c}}M^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}}c^{-1}d(c),\\
&\ll X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}\underset{P^{2}X^{2\epsilon}\leq M\leq X^{A}}{\rm max}\quad(M)^{\epsilon}\log(MX)\qquad\ll X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}X^{A\epsilon}\ll\qquad X^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}P^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

Finally, all these estimates give us the required result.

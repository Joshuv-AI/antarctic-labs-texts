Parameterized Algorithms for the Steiner Arborescence Problem on a Hypercube
Sugyani Mahapatra, Manikandan Narayanan, N. S. Narayanaswamy

Abstract
Motivated by a phylogeny reconstruction problem in evolutionary biology, we study the minimum Steiner arborescence problem on directed hypercubes ( MSA-DH ). Given m m , representing the directed hypercube Q → m \vec{Q}_{m} , and a set of terminals R R , the problem asks to find a Steiner arborescence that spans R R with minimum cost. As m m implicitly represents Q → m \vec{Q}_{m} comprising 2 m 2^{m} vertices, the running time analyses of traditional Steiner tree algorithms on general graphs does not give a clear understanding of the actual complexity of this problem. We present algorithms that exploit the structure of the hypercube and run in time polynomial in | R | |R| and m m . We explore the MSA-DH problem on three natural parameters – | R | |R| , and two above-guarantee parameters, number of Steiner nodes p p and penalty q q . For above-guarantee parameters, the parameterized MSA-DH problem take p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 or q ≥ 0 q\geq 0 as input, and outputs a Steiner arborescence with at most | R | + p − 1 |R|+p-1 or m + q m+q edges respectively. We present the following results ( 𝒪 ~ \tilde{\mathcal{O}} hides the polynomial factors): 1. An exact algorithm that runs in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 3 | R | ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(3^{|R|}) time. 2. A randomized algorithm that runs in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 9 q ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(9^{q}) time with success probability ≥ 4 − q \geq 4^{-q} . 3. An exact algorithm that runs in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 36 q ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(36^{q}) time. 4. A ( 1 + q ) (1+q) -approximation algorithm that runs in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 1.25284 q ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1.25284^{q}) time. 5. An 𝒪 ⁡ ( p ​ ℓ max ) \mathcal{O}\left(p\ell_{\mathrm{max}}\right) -additive approximation algorithm that runs in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ℓ max p + 2 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell_{\mathrm{max}}^{p+2}) time, where ℓ max \ell_{\mathrm{max}} is the maximum distance of any terminal from the root.

Introduction

1.1
Problem and Motivation

A long-standing problem inspired by Darwin’s theory of evolution is to reconstruct the “tree of life” or phylogeny relating all current-day species from their DNA/protein sequences. The problem is to specifically infer a phylogenetic tree
Felsenstein 2004
that relates the observed sequences of current-day species via the unobserved sequences of their common ancestral species. We can model this problem as the hypercube variant of the Steiner tree problem, by considering the binary representation of the observed sequences as input terminals and unobserved ancestral sequences as Steiner nodes. Finding a Steiner tree in an undirected hypercube solves the phylogeny problem when mutations are reversible (i.e., both
0\rightarrow 1
and
1\rightarrow 0
bit flips/changes are allowed), and a directed hypercube solves the problem when mutations are irreversible (i.e., only
0\rightarrow 1
bit flips are possible). The former is better studied, and the focus of this work is on open questions related to the latter problem of finding a Steiner Arborescence in a directed hypercube. Given that the Steiner tree problem is one of Karp’s 21
NP
-complete problems
Book 1980
, there is rich work on the parameterized complexity of the Steiner tree problem on general as well as special-case graphs. For instance in general graphs, the Steiner tree problem is
FPT
on the number of input terminals
|R|
as parameter
Dreyfus and Wagner 1971
;
Nederlof 2013
, but
W[2]
-hard on the number of Steiner nodes
p
as parameter
Dvorák et al 2021
. Despite this rich literature, the directed hypercube variant of the Steiner tree focused in this study has not been addressed before.

We now formally state our problem.
For an

m

Every bit position is also known as a ‘

;
Blelloch et al 2006

denotes the directed hypercube rooted at

For an arborescence

1.2
Our Results

We consider three parameters - number of terminals
|R|
, number of Steiner nodes
p
and penalty
q
. To consider the parameterizations with respect to
p
and
q
as above guarantee parameters, we observe the following lower bounds on the cost of any solution. Since
|R|
is the number of terminals, any solution has at least
|R|-1
edges. Thus the cost of a Steiner arborescence
T
is given by
\mathtt{COST}(T)=|R|-1+p_{T}
where
p_{T}
is the number of Steiner nodes.
For the parameter
q
, note that a character with the same bit value across all the terminals does not play a significant role in the construction of the Steiner arborescence (cf.
1
); thus we assume that for each of the
m
characters, the terminal set can be partitioned into two non-empty sets – those in which the character is 0 and those in which it is 1. Thus in any solution, the value of each character must change at least once along some edge in
T
.
Thus,
\mathtt{COST}(T)\geq m
, and the penalty
q_{T}
of the arborescence
T
is given as
q_{T}=\mathtt{COST}(T)-m

Sridhar et al 2007
;
Blelloch et al 2006
.
The optimum number of Steiner nodes is denoted by
p_{\mathrm{opt}}=\min\limits_{T}p_{T}
; and the optimal penalty is denoted by
q_{\mathrm{opt}}=\min\limits_{T}q_{T}
.

The parameterized (decision) version of the problem is now defined as:

Parameterized MSA-DH problem

Instance
:

An integer
m
, and a set of terminals
R\subseteq V(\vec{Q}_{m})
.

Parameters
:

|R|
, penalty
q\geq 0
, number of Steiner nodes
p\geq 0
.

Question
:

Is there a Steiner arborescence
T
in
\vec{Q}_{m}
, rooted at
0^{m}
, that spans
R
and has cost at most
m+q
or at most
|R|+p-1
?

In the decision algorithms we attempt to compute the optimal Steiner arborescence, thus giving the value of
p_{\mathrm{opt}}
and
q_{\mathrm{opt}}
. Thus, if
q\geq q_{\mathrm{opt}}
(
p\geq p_{\mathrm{opt}}
), we output the Steiner arborescence
T
; otherwise when
q<q_{\mathrm{opt}}
(
p<p_{\mathrm{opt}}
), the algorithms output “No”.
We use the notation
FPT
[k]
to denote an
FPT
algorithm parameterized by
k
.

On the contrary, with parameterized approximation algorithms, we attempt to compute an approximate solution with better running times.
We adopt the definition used by Dvorák et al.
Dvorák et al 2021
, i.e., if
q\geq q_{\mathrm{opt}}
(
p\geq p_{\mathrm{opt}}
), we output an approximate solution with performance ratio
\alpha
on the cost; otherwise the output is arbitrary.

We first present a dynamic programming formulation similar to the
FPT
[|R|]
-algorithm for the Steiner tree problem in undirected graphs by Dreyfus and Wagner
Dreyfus and Wagner 1971
.

Theorem 1
.

The
MSA-DH
problem can be solved in
\mathcal{O}\left(3^{|R|}|R|m+|R|^{2}m^{2}\right)
time and
\mathcal{O}\left(2^{|R|}\log{|R|m}\right)
space.

For the parameter
q
, the undirected version of the problem admits a randomized
FPT
[q]
-algorithm (see
).
Inspired by this algorithm, we present a randomized
FPT
[q]
-algorithm for the parameterized
MSA-DH
problem as well.

Theorem 2
.

The parameterized
MSA-DH
problem can be solved in
\mathcal{O}\left(9^{q}q^{3}+q|R|m^{2}\right)
time with success probability
\geq 4^{-q}
.

The algorithm can be derandomized using a depth-bounded search tree in
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(36^{q})
time (cf.
4
).

We next present a novel parameterized approximation algorithm with improved running time but at the expense of exactness by using the exact algorithm for the minimum vertex cover problem
Cygan et al 2015
.

Theorem 3
.

There is a
(1+q)
-approximation algorithm for the parameterized
MSA-DH
problem that runs in
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1.25284^{q})
time, where
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
hides the polynomial factors in the input size.

The Steiner tree problem is infamously
W[2]
-hard on the parameter
p
, the number of Steiner nodes, for general graphs and has not been explored for DAGs and undirected hypercubes.
To the best of our knowledge, the parameterized complexity of the problem on directed hypercubes is yet to be known.
We present a novel parameterized approximation algorithm on the parameters
p
and
\ell_{\mathrm{max}}
for the same, where
\ell_{\mathrm{max}}
is the maximum distance of any terminal from the root.

Theorem 4
.

There is a
\mathcal{O}\left(p\ell_{\mathrm{max}}\right)
-additive approximation algorithm for the parameterized
MSA-DH
problem that runs in
\mathcal{O}\left(\ell_{\mathrm{max}}^{p+2}+\ell_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}|R|^{2}m\right)
time, where
\ell_{\mathrm{max}}
is the maximum distance of any terminal from the root.

In our algorithms, the key point is to note the polynomial dependence on the
m
and
|R|
(for the parameters
p
and
q
), and the exponential dependence on the parameter.
The algorithms have all exploited the nature of the directed hypercube, and we believe that the dependence on the parameters can be further improved.
In particular, we have not been able to improve the running time of the algorithm parameterized by the number of terminals from
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(3^{|R|})
to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(2^{|R|})
.
We also note that while these algorithms can be used to solve the Steiner problem in an undirected hypercube, the resulting tree may not be optimal (e.g.,
1
) or adhere to the approximation ratios.
Conversely, results on the Steiner problem in an undirected hypercube cannot of course be assumed to hold “as is” for its directed version (where
1\rightarrow 0
character flips/changes are disallowed). These observations motivate our results above on the complexity of the Steiner problem in a directed hypercube.

Clearly,
\mathtt{S}_{\ell-1}
is a minimum hitting set of
\mathcal{F}
, and is represented as
\mathtt{S}_{\ell-1}=\mathtt{MHS}(\mathcal{F})
.
We abuse the notation slightly and use
\mathtt{MHS}(\mathtt{S}_{\ell})
to represent
\mathtt{MHS}(\mathcal{F})
.
Notice that, since they are at the same level in
\vec{Q}_{m}

\mathtt{S}_{\ell}
has exactly
\ell
parents; and hence, to compute
\mathtt{S}_{\ell-1}

FPT
[\ell,k]
algorithm for the
\ell
-
MHS
problem
Niedermeier 2006
(every set in the input set system is of size
\ell
and
k
is the bound on the solution size).
So, if
R_{\ell}
is a subset of terminals at level
\ell
, we can recursively apply the
FPT
[\ell,p]
algorithm for the
\ell
-
MHS
problem and construct a Steiner arborescence on
R_{\ell}
.

Our proposed algorithm exploits this idea.
The set of all non-root terminals (
R\setminus\{0^{m}\}
) are partitioned based on their levels and a Steiner arborescence is constructed for each partition independently.
Finally, the union of all the constructed arborescences is returned.
For any partition
R_{\ell}
of the terminal set, the arborescence is constructed using the following recurrence relation:

\mathtt{S}_{i}^{\ell}=\begin{cases}\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime}),&if~i=\ell-1\\
\mathtt{MHS}(\mathtt{S}_{i+1}^{\ell}),&otherwise\end{cases}

(2)

Here,
R_{\ell}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{\ell}
is the subset of terminals at level
\ell
without any parents in the terminal set
R
and
\mathtt{S}_{i}^{\ell}
represents the set of Steiner nodes at level

R_{\ell}^{\prime}
. All the terminals in
X_{\ell}=R_{\ell}\setminus R_{\ell}^{\prime}
contain a parent in
R_{\ell-1}
, and hence are directly added as children to their respective parents.

Proof.

We first prove that if
p\geq p_{\mathrm{opt}}
, then
\forall~\ell\in[2,\ell_{\mathrm{max}}]

|\mathtt{S}_{\ell-1}^{\ell}|=|\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime})|\leq p
.
Let
p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{i}
be the number of Steiner nodes at level
i
in
T_{\mathrm{opt}}
.
So
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_{\mathrm{max}}-1}p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{i}=p_{\mathrm{opt}}
.
For any level
\ell\in[2,\ell_{\mathrm{max}}]
, since every terminal
t\in R_{\ell}^{\prime}
does not have a parent in
R_{\ell-1}
, the parent of
t
in
T_{\mathrm{opt}}
is a Steiner node.
Also the set of Steiner nodes spanning
R_{\ell}^{\prime}
in
T_{\mathrm{opt}}
is a hitting set.
Hence
|\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime})|\leq p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\ell-1}\leq p_{\mathrm{opt}}\leq p
.
Thus, if
\exists~\ell\in[2,\ell_{\mathrm{max}}]

|\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime})|>p
, then it is a No-Instance.
It may be that
\forall~\ell\in[2,\ell_{\mathrm{max}}]

|\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime})|\leq p
even when
p<p_{\mathrm{opt}}
in which case the output is arbitrary.
Henceforth we assume
p\geq p_{\mathrm{opt}}
to prove the approximation guarantee of the algorithm.

We first note that when the algorithm is constructing the arborescence on any level
\ell\in[2,\ell_{\mathrm{max}}]
,
R_{\ell-1}
is already in
T
.
So every terminal
t\in X_{\ell}
already has a parent in
T
and
T_{\ell}
is constructed only on
R_{\ell}^{\prime}
.
Also,
\forall~i\in[1,\ell-1]~|\mathtt{S}_{i}^{\ell}|\leq|\mathtt{S}_{i+1}^{\ell}|
.
If we are using an

\ell
-
MHS

p_{T_{\ell}}=\sum_{i=\ell-1}^{1}|\mathtt{S}_{i}^{\ell}|\leq\sum_{i=\ell-1}^{1}\alpha|\mathtt{MHS}(\mathtt{S}_{i+1}^{\ell})|\leq\sum_{i=\ell-1}^{1}\alpha|\mathtt{MHS}(R_{\ell}^{\prime})|\leq\sum_{i=\ell-1}^{1}\alpha p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\ell-1}=(\ell-1)\alpha p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\ell-1}

\begin{split}SOL&=|R|-1+p_{T}\leq OPT+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\ell_{\mathrm{max}}}p_{T_{\ell}}\leq OPT+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\ell_{\mathrm{max}}}(\ell-1)\alpha p_{\mathrm{opt}}^{\ell-1}\\

6
Conclusion and Future Work

We explored the
MSA-DH
problem from different viewpoints of parameterization and presented
FPT
algorithms for the same.
Specifically, we showed that the problem is in
FPT
on the number of terminals
|R|
and penalty
q
.
We also present a parameterized approximation algorithm for the parameter
q
that can be improved to run in polynomial time at the expense of the approximation factor, and another parameterized approximation algorithm involving parameters
p
and
\ell_{\mathrm{max}}
.
The major key point of all our algorithms is the polynomial dependence on
m
, and hence the logarithmic dependence on the size of the underlying graph.
All the algorithms exploit the nature of the directed hypercube to accomplish this motive.

Future work could focus on the open questions of whether parameterized algorithms with better running times or approximation ratios are possible for these problems (e.g., can the exponential factor in the time complexity of the
FPT
[|R|]
algorithm be improved from
3^{|R|}
to
2^{|R|}
?).
The
W
-hardness of the problem on the parameter
p
, remains an open question (i.e., is there an
FPT
[p]
algorithm that is independent of
\ell_{\mathrm{max}}
?).

The Steiner arborescence problem and algorithms presented here also opens up interesting directions/applications in other fields.
Steiner arborescence may provide a data structure to represent an ensemble of related graphs (using bit vectors that indicate the presence/absence of edges in each graph), and thereby help efficiently compute certain properties of these graphs.
An alternative representation of the terminal set as a set system may be used to explore connections to VC (Vapnik–Chervonenkis) dimension. For instance, it will be interesting to explore if the cost of the Steiner arborescence can be exploited to compute the VC dimension of a set family.

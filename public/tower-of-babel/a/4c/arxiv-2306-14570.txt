Norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity for the generalized improved Boussinesq equation
Pierre de Roubin

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the ill-posedness issue for the generalized improved Boussinesq equation. In particular we prove there is norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity at general initial data in ⟨ ∇ ⟩ − s ​ ( L 2 ∩ L ∞ ) ​ ( ℝ ) \langle\nabla\rangle^{-s}\big(L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}\big)(\mathbb{R}) for any s < 0 s<0 . This result is sharp in the L 2 L^{2} -based Sobolev scale in view of the well-posedness in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) ∩ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) . We also show that the same result applies to the multi-dimensional generalized improved Boussinesq equation. Finally, we extend our norm inflation result to Fourier-Lebesgue, modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces.



1.
Introduction

We consider the generalized improved Boussinesq equation (gIBq):

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u-\partial_{t}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}^{2}\left(f(u)\right)\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{cases}\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M},

where
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}
with
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and
f(u)=u^{k}
for
k\geq 2
an integer. The equation (
1.1
) appears in a wide variety of physical problems. Makhankov
[
21
]
derived the improved Boussinesq equation, namely with
f(u)=u^{2}
, in order to study ion-sound wave propagation and mentioned the case of the improved modified Boussinesq equation, with
f(u)=u^{3}
, as modeling nonlinear Alfvén waves. He also mentioned the possibility to use the linear term of (
1.1
) to describe waves propagating at right angles to the magnetic field. Besides, Clarkson-LeVeque-Saxton
[
14
]
considered the cases
f(u)=u^{p}
, with
p=3
or
5
, as a model for the propagation of longitudinal deformation waves in an elastic rod. See also
[
27
]
for further discussion on the physical aspect of (
1.1
).

This equation has also attracted attention from a mathematical point of view. In particular, the well-posedness of this problem has been studied extensively and Constantin-Molinet
[
15
]
proved local well-posedness for (
1.1
) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
(and global well-posedness under additional conditions). See also
[
11
,
17
,
33
]
. On the other hand, it is known that (
1.1
) possesses some undesirable behaviour in negative Sobolev spaces. Following the work of Bourgain
[
6
]
, it was showed in
[
17
]
that the solution map
\Phi\colon H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\to C([-T,T],H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
of (
1.1
), for nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
, fails to be
C^{k}
for
s<0
. This result in particular implies that we can not study the well-posedness of (
1.1
) via a contraction argument. Note however that their result does not imply failure of continuity for the data-to-soluton map in negative Sobolev spaces. Our main goal in this article is to complete the well-posedness issue of this problem by proving the discontinuity of the data-to-solution map of (
1.1
) in
\langle\nabla\rangle^{-s}\big(L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}\big)(\mathcal{M})
for any
s<0
. For more clarity, let us denote
\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq W^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})\times W^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
with

W^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})=\langle\nabla\rangle^{-s}\big(L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}\big)(\mathcal{M})=\{f,\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\}.

We exhibit the following norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity behaviour:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
s<0
. Fix
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
. Then, given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.1
) with nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
on
\mathcal{M}
and
t_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

(1.2)

\|(u_{\varepsilon}(0),\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(0))-(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon})\|_{W^{\sigma,2,\infty}}>\varepsilon^{-1}.

Note that, when
\sigma=s
and
(u_{0},u_{1})=(0,0)
, (
1.2
) is already called
norm inflation
. Since we have (
1.2
) for any arbitrary initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, we say that we have norm inflation at
general initial data
. Besides, Theorem
1.3
also yields (
1.2
) for any arbitrary
\sigma<s
, leading to the so-called
infinite loss of regularity
. Observe as well that, as a corollary, Theorem
1.1
gives the following discontinuity of the solution map.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
s<0
. Then, for any
T>0
, the solution map
\Phi\colon(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})\to(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C([-T,T],W^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M}))\times C([-T,T],W^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M}))
to the generalized improved Boussinesq equation (
1.1
) is discontinuous everywhere in
\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Throughout the rest of this paper, we prove in fact the following result of norm inflation at general initial data with infinite loss of regularity in negative Sobolev spaces:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
s<0
. Fix
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq H^{s}(\mathcal{M})\times H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
. Then, given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.1
) with nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
on
\mathcal{M}
and
t_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

(1.3)

\|(u_{\varepsilon}(0),\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(0))-(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{\sigma}}>\varepsilon^{-1}.

Once Theorem
1.3
is proved, Theorem
1.1
follows as a corollary. See Remark
3.10
. In fact, we first prove the usual norm inflation at general initial data, namely we prove (
1.3
) with
\sigma=s
. Then, the loss of regularity follows with a slight modification. See Remark
3.7
. Note also that, in a similar manner, Theorem
1.3
implies the discontinuity of the solution map in negative Sobolev spaces.

There are several ways to prove norm inflation results. Christ-Colliander-Tao
[
13
]
first introduced norm inflation with a method based on a dispersionless ODE approach. See also
[
7
,
32
]
. On the other hand, Carles with his collaborators in
[
1
,
3
,
8
]
proved norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity results for nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS) by using geometric optics.

Our strategy is to follow a Fourier analytic argument that originated from the abstract work of Bejenaru-Tao
[
2
]
on quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Their idea was to expand the solution into a power series and to exploit the
high-to-low energy transfer
in one of the terms to prove discontinuity of the solution map. This approach was refined later on by Iwabuchi-Ogawa
[
19
]
who used it to extend the ill-posedness result in
[
2
]
into a norm inflation result. Kishimoto
[
20
]
then further developed these methods to prove norm inflation for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Meanwhile, Oh
[
23
]
refined the argument of
[
19
]
in the context of cubic NLS by introducing a way to index the power series by trees, and to estimate each term separately. Forlano-Okamoto
[
18
]
proved afterwards norm inflation with an approach inspired by
[
23
]
for nonlinear wave equations (NLW) in Sobolev spaces of negative regularities, and we use the same reasoning in our proof. For other papers with similar argument, the interested reader might turn to
[
5
,
10
,
12
,
24
,
26
,
31
]
. See also
[
9
]
for an implementation of this method in probabilistic settings.

One of the key ingredients to our proof is the use of the Wiener algebra
\mathcal{F}L^{1}(\mathcal{M})
, which we define now. Given
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}
, let
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
denote the Pontryagin dual of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.

(1.4)

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}&\text{if}\quad\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R},\\
\mathbb{Z}&\text{if}\quad\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

Note that, when
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}
, we endow it with the counting measure. We can then define the following Fourier-Lebesgue spaces:

Definition 1.4

(Fourier-Lebesgue spaces)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\geq 1
, we define the Fourier-Lebesgue space
\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}(\mathcal{M})
as the completion of the Schwartz class of functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}(\mathcal{M})}=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{p}_{\xi}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})},

where
\langle\xi\rangle\coloneqq(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

In particular,
\mathcal{F}L^{0,1}(\mathcal{M})
corresponds to the Wiener algebra, and its algebra property allows us to prove easily that (
1.1
) is analytically locally well-posed in
\mathcal{F}L^{0,1}(\mathcal{M})
.

Another major point of our proof is the following power series expansion of a solution
u
to (
1.1
) with
(u,\partial_{t}u)|_{t=0}=\vec{u}_{0}
:

u=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0}),

where
\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0})
denotes a multilinear term in
\vec{u}_{0}
of degree
(k-1)j+1
(for nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
). More precisely, these multilinear terms are exactly the successive terms of a Picard iteration expansion. See Section
2
. Then, by explicit computation we show that
\Xi_{1}(\vec{u}_{0})
grows rapidly in a short time, achieving the desired growth, while we control the other terms. See Section
3
.

2.
Power series extension

In this section, we prove (
1.1
) with nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
is well-posed in the Wiener algebra, and it can be expanded into a power series. First, let us fix some notations. We define

\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{s,p}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{F}L^{s,p}(\mathcal{M})

and, for more clarity, we denote
\mathcal{F}L^{p}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}L^{0,p}(\mathcal{M})
and
\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{p}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{0,p}(\mathcal{M})
.

We denote
S(t)
the linear propagator:

(2.1)

S(t)(u,v)=\cos\left(tP(D)\right)u+\frac{\sin\left(tP(D)\right)}{P(D)}v,

with
P(D)\coloneqq\frac{\lvert\nabla\rvert}{\langle\nabla\rangle}
. Namely, for any
\xi\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathcal{F}\left[P(D)f\right](\xi)=\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert}{(1+\xi^{2})^{1/2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\eqqcolon\lambda(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

We also denote
\mathcal{I}_{k}
the multilinear Duhamel operator.

(2.2)

\mathcal{I}_{k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\sin\left((t-t^{\prime})P(D)\right)P(D)\prod^{k}_{j=1}u_{j}(t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

This gives the following Duhamel formulation for (
1.1
)

(2.3)

u(t)=S(t)(u_{0},u_{1})+\mathcal{I}_{k}(u).

Note that, in the aforementioned formula, we used the short-hand notation
\mathcal{I}_{k}(u)\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{k}(u,\dots,u)
.

To index the power series we intend to create, we need the following tree structure:

Definition 2.1

(
k
-ary trees)
.

(1)

Given a set
\mathcal{T}
with partial order
\leq
, we say that
b\in\mathcal{T}
with
b\leq a
and
b\neq a
is a child of
a\in\mathcal{T}
, if
b\leq c\leq a
implies either
c=a
or
c=b
. If the latter condition holds, we also say that
a
is the parent of
b
.

(2)

A tree
\mathcal{T}
is a finite partially ordered set, satisfying the following properties
2
2

2

We do not identify two trees even if there is an order-preserving bijection between them.
:

•

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\mathcal{T}
. If
a_{4}\leq a_{2}\leq a_{1}
and
a_{4}\leq a_{3}\leq a_{1}
, then we have
a_{2}\leq a_{3}
or
a_{3}\leq a_{2}
,

•

A node
a\in\mathcal{T}
is called terminal, if it has no child. A non-terminal node
a\in\mathcal{T}
, for
\mathcal{T}
a
k
-ary tree, is a node with exactly
k
children,

•

There exists a maximal element
r\in\mathcal{T}
, called the root node, such that
a\leq r
for all
a\in\mathcal{T}
,

•

\mathcal{T}
consists of the disjoint union of
\mathcal{T}^{0}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\infty}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{0}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\infty}
denote the collections of non-terminal nodes and terminal nodes, respectively.

(3)

Let
{\bf T}(j)
denote the set of all trees of
j
-th generation, namely trees with
j

Note that a tree of
j
-th generation
\mathcal{T}\in{\bf T}(j)
has
kj+1
nodes. Indeed, it has
j
non-terminal nodes by definition and an induction argument shows it has
(k-1)j+1
terminal nodes. Besides, we have the following bound on the number of trees of
j
-th generation:

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, depending only on
k
, such that, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.4)

\lvert{\bf T}(j)\rvert\leq\frac{C^{j}_{0}}{(1+j)^{2}}\leq C^{j}_{0}

The following proof is an adaptation of the one in
[
23
]
for ternary trees, we include it for completeness.

Proof.

We prove (
2.4
) by induction. Note that the right inequality is immediate, so we only have to prove

\lvert{\bf T}(j)\rvert\leq\frac{C^{j}_{0}}{(1+j)^{2}}

for any
j\geq 0
. Observe first that

\lvert{\bf T}(0)\rvert=\lvert{\bf T}(1)\rvert=1.

Then, fix
j\geq 2
. Assume equation (
2.4
) holds for any
0\leq m\leq j-1
and take
\mathcal{T}\in{\bf T}(j)
. By Definition
2.1
, there exist
k
trees
\mathcal{T}_{1}\in{\bf T}(j_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{T}_{k}\in{\bf T}(j_{k})
, with
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=j-1
, such that
\mathcal{T}
is the tree consisting of a root node whose children are
\mathcal{T}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Thus, applying the induction hypothesis we get

\displaystyle\lvert{\bf T}(j)\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=j-1,\\
j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\lvert{\bf T}(j_{1})\rvert\times\cdots\times\lvert{\bf T}(j_{k})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=j-1,\\
j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{C^{j_{1}}_{0}}{(1+j_{1})^{2}}\times\cdots\times\frac{C^{j_{k}}_{0}}{(1+j_{k})^{2}}\times\frac{(1+j)^{2}}{(1+j)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}\left(\sum_{j_{2},\cdots j_{k}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1+j_{2})^{2}\times\cdots\times(1+j_{k})^{2}}\right)\frac{C^{j-1}_{0}}{(1+j)^{2}}

where we used
k\max\left(1+j_{1},\dots,1+j_{k}\right)\geq 1+j
and rearranged the sum so the maximum is reached for
j_{1}
. Then, choosing
C_{0}=k^{2}\sum_{j_{2},\cdots j_{k}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1+j_{2})^{2}\cdots(1+j_{k})^{2}}<\infty
ends the induction.

∎

From now on, for any
\vec{\phi}\in\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}

,
j\geq 0

•

For instance, we have the two following terms:

\Xi_{0}(\vec{\phi})=S(t)\vec{\phi},\quad\text{ and }\quad\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi})=\mathcal{I}_{k}(S(t)\vec{\phi},\cdots,S(t)\vec{\phi}).

Let us now state some basic multilinear estimates that will be useful both for norm inflation and for local well-posedness in the Wiener algebra

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for any
\vec{\phi}\in\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}
,

and
0<T\leq 1

(2.6)

\big\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi})(T)\big\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\leq C^{j}T^{2j}\|\vec{\phi}\|^{(k-1)j+1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}

Moreover, if
j\geq 1
and
\vec{\psi}\in\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,

(2.7)

\left\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{\psi})(T)\right\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\infty}}\leq C^{j}T^{2j}\|\vec{\psi}\|^{(k-1)j-1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\|\vec{\psi}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}\in{\bf T}(j)
. Using the same tree structure argument as for Lemma

, there exist
k

\mathcal{T}_{1}\in{\bf T}(j_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{T}_{k}\in{\bf T}(j_{k})
, with
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=j-1
, such that the root nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{T}_{1}
are the children of the root node of
\mathcal{T}
. Thus, we can write

\Psi(\mathcal{T})(\vec{\phi})=\mathcal{I}_{k}(\Psi(\mathcal{T}_{1})(\vec{\phi}),\cdots,\Psi(\mathcal{T}_{k})(\vec{\phi})),

and
\Psi(\mathcal{T})(\vec{\phi})

with

Meanwhile, we deduce from (

) that

is unitary in
\mathcal{F}L^{1}
for any

, and, since

\|\mathcal{I}_{k}[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\leq\int^{T}_{0}\lvert T-t^{\prime}\rvert\lvert\lambda(\xi)\rvert^{2}\|u_{1}\cdots u_{k}\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\prod^{k}_{j=1}\|u_{j}\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}

Hence, (

.

Let

.

(2)

Moreover, u can be expressed as

(2.9)

u=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in{\bf T}(j)}\Psi(\mathcal{T})(\vec{u}_{0})

Proof.

Let us first prove our problem is locally well-posed in

Using the multilinearity of
\mathcal{I}_{k}
, we ensure, for

Then, the contraction mapping theorem and an a posteriori continuity argument proves the local well-posedness. Let us move onto the power series expansion.

Fix

. We choose

. Then, by continuity of

,

(2.11)

\|\Gamma[U]-\Gamma[U_{J}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

All that is left now is to estimate
U_{J}-\Gamma[U_{J}]
. Fix an integer
J\geq 1
. Then, from the tree structure argument we already used in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle U_{J}-\Gamma[U_{J}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0})-\sum_{0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq J}\mathcal{I}_{k}\left(\Xi_{j_{1}}(\vec{u}_{0}),\cdots,\Xi_{j_{k}}(\vec{u}_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum^{kJ}_{l=J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq J,\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{I}_{k}\left(\Xi_{j_{1}}(\vec{u}_{0}),\cdots,\Xi_{j_{k}}(\vec{u}_{0})\right).

Now a crude estimation of the sums, along with (
2.8
) and (
2.6
), give

\displaystyle\|U_{J}-\Gamma[U_{J}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\sum^{kJ}_{l=J}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq J,\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=l\end{subarray}}\ \prod^{k}_{m=1}\|\Xi_{j_{m}}(\vec{u}_{0})\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\sum^{kJ}_{l=J}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq J,\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=l\end{subarray}}\ \prod^{k}_{m=1}C^{j_{m}}T^{2j_{m}}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|^{(k-1)j_{m}+1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}T^{2}J^{k}M^{k}\sum^{\infty}_{l=J}(CT^{2}M^{k-1})^{l}

Since we assumed
0<T\ll\min(M^{-\frac{k-1}{2}},1)
, the sum converges and the right-hand side is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}T^{2}J^{k}M^{k}(CT^{2}M^{k-1})^{J}
, which tends to
0
as
J
tends to infinity. Thus, there exists
J_{3}\geq 1
such that for every
J\geq J_{3}
,

(2.12)

\|U_{J}-\Gamma[U_{J}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Now, for any
J\geq\max(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
, (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) imply

\|U-\Gamma[U]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\leq\|U-U_{J}\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}+\|U_{J}-\Gamma[U_{J}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}+\|\Gamma[U]-\Gamma[U_{J}]\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{F}L^{1}}<\varepsilon.

Therefore,
U
is a fixed point of
\Gamma
and this ends the proof by uniqueness.

∎

(3.2)

where

(3.3)

and

(3.4)

Note that the condition

ensures the union (
3.3

3.2

3.3
) and (
3.4

(3.5)

\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\sim RA\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\sim RN^{s}A^{1/2}.

We define finally
\vec{u}_{0,n}\coloneqq\vec{u}_{0}+\vec{\phi}_{n}

,

and

\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\ll RA.

Therefore, for each

(3.7)

0<T\ll\min((RA)^{-\frac{k-1}{2}},1),

Lemma
2.4
implies there exists a unique solution

) with

and admitting the power series expansion:

(3.8)

u_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0}+\vec{\phi}_{n}).

The purpose of this subsection is then to estimate the terms of the power series on the right-hand side of (
3.8
). But first, let us recall the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
A>0
, then we have

CA\chi_{a+b+Q_{A}}(\xi)\leq\chi_{a+Q_{A}}\ast\chi_{b+Q_{A}}(\xi)\leq\widetilde{C}A\chi_{a+b+Q_{2A}}(\xi)

where

The proof of the following lemma is essentially the same as in
[
18

and is included for completeness.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
s<0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the following estimates hold:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\vec{u}_{0,n}-\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\sim RN^{s}A^{1/2},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{0}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+RA^{1/2}N^{s},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C^{j}t^{2j}R^{(k-1)j}A^{(k-1)j}\left(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}+Rg_{s}(A)\right),

where

is defined by

(3.13)

g_{s}(A)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\textup{if }s<-\frac{1}{2},\\
\left(\log A\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\textup{if }s=-\frac{1}{2},\\
A^{\frac{1}{2}+s}&\textup{if }s>-\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

Proof.

The proofs of (
3.9

, (
3.5
), the unitarity of

for

and the fact that

is fixed, implying
\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\lesssim 1
. Besides, the definition (

) of
\Xi_{j}
and the multilinearity of

imply

\Xi_{1}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)=\sum\limits_{(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in E}\mathcal{I}_{k}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})(t)

where

such that at least one of the choice is

. Thus, since

, Young’s inequality, (
2.8
) and the unitarity of

imply

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\left(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|^{k-1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}+\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|^{k-1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\left(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|^{k-1}_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}+R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\right)

which, combined with assumption (
3.6

3.11

For the last inequality, we split the left-hand side into two terms:

(3.14)

\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)=\left(\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)-\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)\right)+\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)

which we will estimate separately.

Part 1:

\mathcal{F}\big(\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)\big)
essentially consists of

convolutions of terms of the form

. According to (

) and (

), the support of each of these terms is contained within at most

2.2
imply there exist

Since

is decreasing in

and Young’s inequality, (

yield

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\|_{L^{2}(\supp\mathcal{F}(\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)))}\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(t)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{\infty}}

\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim C^{j}t^{2j}R^{(k-1)j}A^{(k-1)j}Rg_{s}(A).

Part 2:
On the other hand, since

With the help of Lemma

Proposition 3.6
.

,

defined as in equation (

. Then, there exists a constant

\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim C^{k-1}R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}.

To simplify notation, we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})](T,\xi)=R^{k}\sum\limits_{(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})\in\Sigma^{k}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Lambda}\prod^{k}_{j=1}\cos(t^{\prime}\lambda(\xi_{j}))\chi_{\eta_{j}+Q_{A}}(\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

We split the set
\Sigma^{k}
, and hence the sum, into two parts
\Sigma_{1}
and

where

\Sigma_{1}=\left\{(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})\in\Sigma^{k}\colon\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{k}=0\right\}

and
\Sigma_{2}=\Sigma^{k}\backslash\Sigma_{1}
. Note that, for any integer
k\geq 2
,
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are both non-empty. We denote then

\mathcal{F}[\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})](T,\xi)=R^{k}\left(I_{1}(T,\xi)+I_{2}(T,\xi)\right)

with, for

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum\limits_{(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})\in\Sigma_{1}}\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\lambda(\xi)^{2}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\prod^{k}_{j=1}\chi_{\eta_{j}+Q_{A}}(\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{\Lambda}

\|I_{1}(T)\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim C^{k-1}_{1}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}.

Meanwhile, since
\sin(x)\sim x
for

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim R^{k}C^{k-1}_{1}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\big(A^{s}-N^{s}\big)

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.2

In this subsection we give the proof of Proposition

. We claim it suffices to show that, given

, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\quad RA^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{s}<\frac{1}{n},

(ii)

\displaystyle\quad T^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\ll 1,

(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\ll RA^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\text{ and }\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\ll RA,

(iv)

\displaystyle\quad T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}Rg_{s}(A)\ll T^{2}R^{k}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s},

(v)

\displaystyle\quad T^{2}R^{k}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}\gg n,

(vi)

\displaystyle\quad A\ll N

for some particular

,

and

, all depending on

Let us show conditions (i) through (vi) prove Proposition

. First, condition (i) along with (

in

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{j=2}\|\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(T)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}(CT^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1})^{j}\right)\left(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}+Rg_{s}(A)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}Rg_{s}(A).

Then, using (
3.8
), Lemma
3.5
and Proposition
3.6
- which is applicable by condition (vi) - conditions (i) through (v) give:

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(T)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigg\|\Xi_{0}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(T)+\left(\Xi_{1}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(T)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\right)+\sum^{\infty}_{j=2}\Xi_{j}(\vec{u}_{0,n})(T)\bigg\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}-1-RA^{1/2}N^{s}-T^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}-T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}Rg_{s}(A)

\displaystyle\sim R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}\gg n.

Thus, this verifies the second estimate in (
3.1
) at time

. Finally, a suitable choice of

in terms of

ensures

, for

sufficiently large. See details below. So ends the proof of Proposition

.

Consequently, it only remains to verify the conditions (i) through (vi). To do so, we express

,
R
and
T
in terms of

. More precisely, let us choose

where

. Since

is a constant, condition (vi) is trivially satisfied and, since

is fixed, condition (iii) reduces to

which is true from

, for

sufficiently large. Besides, for

\displaystyle RA^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{s}\sim N^{-\delta}\ll\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle T^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\sim N^{-\frac{k-1}{2}\delta}\ll 1

\displaystyle T^{2}R^{k}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+s}\sim N^{-s-\frac{k+1}{2}\delta}\gg 1

which prove conditions (i), (ii) and (v). Lastly, condition (iv) is equivalent to

which, in our setting, is equivalent to condition (ii) since

is constant. Finally, since

, observe that
T
goes to
0
as
N\to\infty
, so
T\in(0,\frac{1}{n})
for
N
sufficiently large. Taking

N=n^{\frac{2}{\delta}}

completes the proof since
\delta<1
.

Remark 3.7
.

In this remark, we use our previous arguments to prove infinite loss of regularity, namely Proposition
3.3
. The idea is to use the same construction as before. Indeed, this construction allowed us to choose our parameter
A
to be constant. In the following, we observe that the change of regularity between Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
is only expressed in powers of
A
, so that this change has no major implications whatsoever.

Let
s<0
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\vec{u}_{0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\in\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}
. Define
\vec{\phi}_{n}
and
\vec{u}_{0,n}
as before. There exists, for any given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a unique solution
u_{n}
to (
1.1
) in
C([0,T],\mathcal{F}L^{1})
, with
T
satisfying (
3.7
), such that
(u_{n},\partial_{t}u_{n})|_{t=0}=\vec{u}_{0,n}
. Moreover,
u_{n}
can be expressed as the power series in (
3.8
). First, we claim that it suffices to consider the case
\sigma<s
. Indeed, in the case
\sigma\geq s
the estimate on the initial data remains the same as for Proposition
3.2
and, by Sobolev embedding, we have

\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma}}\geq\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{H^{s}}>n.

Let us then assume
\sigma<s
. Lemma
3.5
, (
3.8
), (
3.7
) and (
3.6
) yield

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(T)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{n}(T)-\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+RA^{1/2}N^{s}+T^{2}(RA)^{k-1}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}+T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}Rg_{s}(A)

while Proposition
3.6
gives

\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{H^{\sigma}}\gtrsim R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}.

Therefore, the same arguments as before allow us to say that, to prove Proposition
3.3
, it suffices to verify the following hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\quad RA^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{s}<\frac{1}{n},

(ii)

\displaystyle\quad T^{2}R^{k-1}A^{k-1}\ll 1,

(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\ll RA^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}\text{ and }\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{\overrightarrow{\mathcal{F}L}^{1}}\ll RA,

(iv)

\displaystyle\quad T^{4}(RA)^{2(k-1)}Rg_{s}(A)\ll T^{2}R^{k}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma},

(v)

\displaystyle\quad T^{2}R^{k}A^{k-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}\gg n,

(vi)

\displaystyle\quad A\ll N.

Note that, compared to the conditions in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, the only changes are in conditions (iii), (iv) and (v), where the power of
A
changes. Yet, if we choose
A
to be constant again, the rest of the reasoning stays the same. Hence, the choices

A=10,\quad R=N^{-s-\delta},\quad T=N^{\frac{k-1}{2}(s+\frac{\delta}{2})},\quad\text{ and }\quad N=n^{\frac{2}{\delta}}

prove also Proposition
3.3
.

Remark 3.8
.

The proof we gave applies directly to the study of the multi-dimensional generalized improved Boussinesq equation (
1.5
). The only major change to make is in the definition of the functions
\phi_{n}
. Indeed, let us denote
e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we then define
\phi_{n}
by

\widehat{\phi}_{n}(\xi)=R\chi_{\Omega}=R\sum_{\eta\in\Sigma}\chi_{\{\eta e_{1}+Q_{A}\}}

with
\Sigma
as in (
3.4
) and
Q_{A}=[-\frac{A}{2},\frac{A}{2}]^{d}
. Then, the same arguments are still applicable. In particular, observe that Lemma
3.4
can be rephrased as:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A>0
, then we have

C_{d}A^{d}\chi_{a+b+Q_{A}}(\xi)\leq\chi_{a+Q_{A}}\ast\chi_{b+Q_{A}}(\xi)\leq\widetilde{C}_{d}A^{d}\chi_{a+b+Q_{2A}}(\xi)

where
C_{d},\widetilde{C}_{d}>0
are constants depending only on the dimension
d
.

Subsequently, the only changes caused from the change of dimension are expressed in powers of
A
. However, since
A
is chosen to be constant, it does not change the argument, as for when we proved infinite loss of regularity from “standard” norm inflation. Therefore, norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity for initial data
\vec{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
s<0
follows naturally from the same choice of parameters
A
,
R
,
T
and
N
.

Similarly, our result would still apply on the same multidimensional problem on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, although such a problem has not been seen in the litterature yet, at least not to our knowledge.

Remark 3.10
.

In this remark, we show that our proof also implies Theorem
1.1
. The idea is to use exactly the same construction and to estimate the norm. Using the same notations, observe that we have

\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{W^{\sigma,2,\infty}}\geq\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma}}>n.

Therefore, we only need to show that

\|\vec{u}_{0}-\vec{u}_{0,n}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}}<\frac{1}{n}

which means that we want
\vec{u}_{0}
and
\vec{u}_{0,n}
to satisfy

\|\vec{u}_{0}-\vec{u}_{0,n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}<\frac{1}{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\vec{u}_{0}-\vec{u}_{0,n}\|_{W^{s,\infty}\times W^{s,\infty}}<\frac{1}{n}.

We already proved that the first estimate is satisfied, so we are only interested in the second one. Observe that
\vec{u}_{0}-\vec{u}_{0,n}=\vec{\phi}_{n}=(\phi_{n},0)
and
\phi_{n}
is frequently supported on
\Omega
, defined by (
3.3
). Then, we get by Cauchy-Schwarz and inverse Fourier transform

|\langle\nabla\rangle^{s}\phi_{n}|\leq\int_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{\phi}_{n}(\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{\Omega}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|\widehat{\phi}_{n}(\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi\leq 2A^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}

Since
A=10
in our proof, and
\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2,\infty}}=\|\phi_{n}\|_{W^{s,2,\infty}}
, this shows the desired result and Theorem
1.1
. Furthermore, combining this argument with Remark
3.8
, this result still holds in higher dimension.

Appendix A
Norm inflation for other spaces

In this appendix, we come back to Remark
1.6
and show how to prove norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity at general initial data for equation (

) in Fourier-Lebesgue, modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces. However, we do not show the entire proof since it is mostly similar to the one we gave for Sobolev spaces. Indeed, we use the same construction and decomposition as before, so the first differences are in the estimates we find in Lemma
3.5
and Proposition
3.6
. Fortunately we find that the new estimates are, under the condition
A=10
, equivalent to the ones we had for Sobolev spaces, so the last part of our argument remains unchanged. Therefore, we just show quickly how to get these estimates. Again, a whole proof would be quite redundant with what was shown before, the arguments being essentially the same. Thus, we just point out the key differences. Before that, we introduce our spaces. Let us recall, at this point, that modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces were introduced by Feichtinger
[
16
]
.

A.1.
New spaces and some preliminary results.

First, let us recall that Fourier-Lebesgue spaces are defined in Definition
1.4
. Now, we introduce the modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define
Q_{n}=n+\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{d}
so that
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}Q_{n}
. Then, let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
and

(A.1)

\rho(\xi)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{ if }\quad\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
0\quad\text{ if }\quad\lvert\xi\rvert\geq 1\end{cases}

We also denote
\rho_{n}(\xi)=\rho(\xi-n)
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let us define

\sigma_{n}=\frac{\rho_{n}}{\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{l}}

and
P_{n}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\sigma_{n}\mathcal{F}(f))
for any suitable
f
. Then, we define the modulation spaces in the following way:

Definition A.1

(Modulation spaces)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\geq 1
. The Modulation space
M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the completion of the Schwartz class of functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\big\|(1+\lvert n\rvert^{s})\|P_{n}f\|_{L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}.

We also define Wiener amalgam spaces in the following way:

Definition A.2

(Wiener amalgam spaces)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\geq 1
. The Wiener amalgam space
W^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the completion of the Schwartz class of functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{W^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\big\|\|(1+\lvert n\rvert^{s})P_{n}f\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\big\|_{L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We have the following relations between modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces:

Lemma A.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}\leq\infty
and
d\geq 1
be any finite dimension. Then:

(1)

For
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
and
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
, we have

(A.2)

M^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p_{2},q_{2}}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and

(A.3)

W^{p,q_{1}}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p,q_{2}}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2)

for
q\leq p
, we have

(A.4)

M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and for
p\leq q
we have

(A.5)

W^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(3)

and we finally have

(A.6)

M^{p,\min(p,q)}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p,\max(p,q)}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The proof of this Lemma can be seen in
[
4
]
, but split into different proofs. For completeness and ease of reading, we include it here. However, we first need the following lemma, which proof can be seen in
[
28
, Lemma 6.1]
:

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, such that
\mathrm{diam}\ \Omega<2R
, with
R>0
, and
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
p
,
q
and
R
such that:

\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for any function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
is compactly supported in
\Omega
.

Proof of Lemma
A.3
.

The embeddings (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) follow from Lemma
A.4
and the fact that, for any
p\leq q
,
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\hookrightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Embeddings (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) follow from Minkowski’s inequality.

Finally, (
A.6
) is a combination of (
A.2
), (
A.3
), (
A.4
) and (
A.5
).
∎

Besides, we also have the following algebra property:

Lemma A.5
.

For any
d\geq 1
, the space
M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach algebra.

Proof.

We want to show that, for any
u,v\in M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\|uv\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|P_{n}(uv)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Note that
u=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{m}u
and
v=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{k}v
. Therefore

P_{n}(uv)=\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v).

The idea then is to study the Fourier support of each of the terms
P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v)
. Since, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{F}[P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v)](\xi)=\sigma_{n}(\xi)\int_{\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}\sigma_{m}(\xi_{1})\widehat{u}(\xi_{1})\sigma_{k}(\xi_{2})\widehat{v}(\xi_{2})\mathrm{d}\xi_{1}

and, for any
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\supp\sigma_{N}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\lvert\xi-N\rvert\leq 1\}
, we have then

\supp\big[(\sigma_{m}\widehat{u})\ast(\sigma_{k}\widehat{v})\big]\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\lvert\xi-m-k\rvert\leq 2\}

and

\mathcal{F}[P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v)]\equiv 0\quad\text{ if }\quad\lvert n-m-k\rvert>3.

Plancherel’s identity, Hölder’s inequality and (
A.2
) yield then:

\displaystyle\|P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{m}uP_{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\chi_{(\lvert n-k-m\rvert\leq 3)},

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{m}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|P_{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\chi_{(\lvert n-k-m\rvert\leq 3)},

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{m}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|P_{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\chi_{(\lvert n-k-m\rvert\leq 3)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|P_{n}(P_{m}uP_{k}v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|P_{m}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|P_{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\chi_{(\lvert n-k-m\rvert\leq 3)},

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|P_{m}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|P_{k}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

which ends the proof.

∎

Proofs of norm inflation in other spaces

In this subsection, we claim that the following result is true:

Theorem A.6
.

Assume that
s<0
and
1\leq q\leq\infty

(A.7)

) in

.

Namely, let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

and fix
\vec{u}_{0}\in Z_{s}^{q}\times Z_{s}^{q}

\varepsilon>0

1.1
) with

As explained before, the idea to prove our result is to use the same construction as in Section

A.2.1.
The case of Fourier-Lebesgue spaces

In the case of Fourier-Lebesgue spaces
\mathcal{F}L^{s,q}(\mathcal{M})

H^{s}(\mathcal{M})
:

Proposition A.7
.

Let
s<0
and

(A.9)

A.2.2.
The case of modulation spaces

The computations in this section follow the same ideas as in the previous section, but the modulation spaces make them a bit trickier to apply. We first state our results:

Proposition A.8
.

Let
s<0
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Keeping the same notations as before, and asssuming

\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,q}_{s}\times M^{2,q}_{s}}\lesssim 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}\ll R\|(1+\lvert n\rvert)^{s}\|_{\ell^{q}(1\leq\lvert n\rvert\leq A)},

(A.13)

\|\Xi_{0}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}\lesssim 1+RN^{s}A^{\frac{1}{2}},

(A.14)

Also, if

(A.16)

\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}\gtrsim R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-1}A^{\frac{1}{q}+s}.

Again, we see that if we choose

Proof of Proposition
A.8
.

First, we have from
\lvert\cos x\rvert\leq 1
and
\lvert\sin x\rvert\lesssim\lvert x\rvert
,

(A.17)

\|S(t)(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}+\lvert t\rvert\|u_{1}\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{M^{2,q}_{s}\times M^{2,q}_{s}}

for any
0\leq t\leq 1
and
u_{0},u_{1}\in M^{2,q}_{s}
. Besides, we also have

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}

\displaystyle=R\big\|(1+\lvert n\rvert)^{s}\|P_{n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\sum_{\eta\in\Sigma}\chi_{\eta+Q_{A}})\|_{L^{2}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}}

\displaystyle=R\big\|(1+\lvert n\rvert)^{s}\|\sigma_{n}\sum_{\eta\in\Sigma}\chi_{\eta+Q_{A}}\|_{L^{2}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}}

but since the sets
\eta+Q_{A}
are disjoint for any two
\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}
in
\Sigma
, we get

\left\|\sigma_{n}\sum_{\eta\in\Sigma}\chi_{\eta+Q_{A}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\sim\chi_{N+Q_{A}}(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma^{2}_{n}(\xi)\chi^{2}_{n+Q_{A}}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\sim\chi_{N+Q_{A}}(n)

and

\|\phi_{n}\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}\sim R\|(1+\lvert n\rvert)^{s}\chi_{N+Q_{A}}(n)\|_{\ell^{q}_{n}}\sim RN^{s}A^{\frac{1}{q}}

which, combined with (
A.17
), proves our first estimate. Besides, (
A.2
) and the algebra property of
M^{2,1}_{0}
yield, in a similar argument as for (
3.11

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{1}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)-\Xi_{1}[\vec{\phi}_{n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Xi_{1}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)-\Xi_{1}[\vec{\phi}_{n}](T)\|_{M_{0}^{2,1}},

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}\big(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}+\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}\big)^{k-1},

A.14
).

. Using the same argument on the support as in the proof for (

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{n}\mathcal{F}[\Xi_{j}(\vec{\phi}_{n})](T)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{j}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)-\Xi_{j}[\vec{\phi}_{n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2j}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}\big(\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}+\|\vec{\phi}_{n}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}\big)^{(k-1)j},

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2j}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}\times M^{2,1}_{0}}(RA^{\frac{1}{2}})^{(k-1)j},

which gives our third estimate.

Finally, our fourth estimate follows from a straightforward adaptaton of the proof of Proposition
3.6
, the only difference being the norm used.

∎

A.2.3.
The case of Wiener amalgam spaces

This section relies a lot on the interactions between modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces. Indeed, using (
A.6
) and Proposition
A.8
, we get respectively

(A.18)

\|\Xi_{0}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)\|_{W_{s}^{2,q}}\lesssim\|\Xi_{0}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,\min(2,q)}}\lesssim 1+RN^{s}A^{\frac{1}{2}},

as well as

(A.19)

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{1}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)-\Xi_{1}[\vec{\phi}_{n}](T)\|_{W_{s}^{2,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Xi_{1}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)-\Xi_{1}[\vec{\phi}_{n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,\min(2,q)}}

(A.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2}(RA)^{(k-1)}\|\vec{u}_{0}\|_{M^{2,1}_{0}(\mathcal{M})},

and, for any
j\geq 2
,

(A.21)

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{j}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)\|_{W_{s}^{2,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Xi_{j}[\vec{u}_{0,n}](T)\|_{M_{s}^{2,\min(2,q)}}

(A.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{2j}(RA)^{(k-1)j}R\|(1+\lvert n\rvert)^{s}\|_{\ell^{\min(2,q)}(1\leq\lvert n\rvert\leq A)}.

Besides, we also get the following lower bound:

(A.23)

\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{W_{s}^{2,q}}\gtrsim\|\Xi_{1}(\vec{\phi}_{n})(T)\|_{W_{s}^{2,\max(2,q)}}\gtrsim R^{k}T^{2}A^{k-1}A^{\frac{1}{q}+s}.

Again, these estimates are equivalent to the ones we had for Sobolev spaces under the condition
A=10
, so norm inflation with infinite loss of regularity follows.

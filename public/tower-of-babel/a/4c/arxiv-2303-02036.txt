Gromov ellipticity of cones over projective manifolds Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 14R10, 14D99; Secondary 32Q56.
S. Kaliman and M. Zaidenberg

Abstract
. We find classes of projective manifolds that are elliptic in the sense of Gromov and such that the affine cones over these manifolds also are elliptic off their vertices. For example, the latter holds for any generalized flag manifold of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 successively blown up in a finite set of points and infinitesimally near points. This also holds for any smooth projective rational surface. For the affine cones, the Gromov ellipticity is a much weaker property than the flexibility. Nonetheless, it still implies the infinite transitivity of the action of the endomorphism monoid on these cones.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be an algebraically closed field
of characteristic zero and

be the affine resp. projective

n

. All varieties and vector bundles

By

Let

Recall that a

of rank

at the origin

.
The constructive subset

is called the

-orbit of

The variety

The ellipticity is equivalent to the weaker properties

One says that

The variety

The ellipticity implies many other useful properties;

[

]
,

[
For17a
]
and
[
For23
]
. In particular,

of an elliptic variety

acts highly
transitivity of

, see Appendix A in
Section
5
.

In this paper we establish the ellipticity of

3.4

and
4.1
. Summarizing, we prove the
following theorem. Recall that

]

]
. The following question arises.

Preliminaries

In this section
X
is a smooth algebraic variety of positive dimension.

The following proposition is a version of the
Gromov Localization Lemma adopted to our setting;
see
[
Gro89
, 3.5.B]

[

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
X

and
(E,p,s)
be a spray
on
U=X\setminus{\rm supp}(D)
with values in
X

such that

is a trivial bundle of rank

.
Then there exists a spray
(\tilde{E},\tilde{p},\tilde{s})
on
X

whose restriction to
U
is isomorphic to
(E,p,s)
and
such that

\tilde{s}|_{{\tilde{p}}^{-1}(X\setminus U)}=\tilde{p}|_{{\tilde{p}}^{-1}(X\setminus U)}

and for each
x\in U
the
{\tilde{s}}
-orbit of

x
coincides with the
s
-orbit of
x
.

For the reader’s convenience
we provide a proof in Appendix B, see Section
6
.

Recall the notions of a locally nilpotent vector field and of its replica,
see
[

]
and
[

]

Definition 1.2
.

Consider a regular vector field
\nu

on a smooth affine variety

and the associated derivation
\partial

of the algebra

.
One says that

and

. Any

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action

.
One writes in this case

.
For any

The

-action

.
The points

.

In the sequel we deal with sprays generated

-actions.

.

Let

be an affine

.

′′

]

Proposition 1.5
.

Let

be an extended

-spray on

be a smooth subvariety of codimension at least

Then

where

{\tilde{E}}=\sigma^{*}E
. Letting

satisfies
\sigma\circ{\tilde{s}}=s\circ\tilde{\sigma}
.
In particular,
\sigma

-orbits to

is dominating when restricted
to a one-dimensional

Let

be the affine dense open subset carrying

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action
\lambda
such that
(E,p,s)
is extended
from the associate

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-spray on

, see Definition

.
Since

is affine,

The

By our assumption, each

2.
Ellipticity of cones

We introduce below the so called “curve-orbit” property
(*)
,
see Definition
2.7
.
Using this property we give a criterion
of ellipticity of the affine cones
over projective varieties with the vertex removed.

Recall that blowing up an affine cone
at its vertex yields a line bundle
on the underlined projective variety.
Removing the vertex of the cone results
in removing the zero section of the latter line bundle.
This results in a locally trivial fiber bundle
whose general fiber is
the punctured affine line
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{*}={\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
.
In subsection
2.2
we deal more generally with
locally trivial
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{*}
-fibrations.
In subsection
2.3
we extend our results
to twisted
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{*}
-fibrations
which are not locally trivial. In subsection
2.4

we give several examples of elliptic cones,
including cones over flag varieties.

The following fact is stated in

[
Gro89
, 3.5B
′′
]
. For the reader’s convenience
we give an argument.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varrho\colon Y\to X
be a locally trivial fiber bundle.
If
Y
is elliptic then
X
is elliptic too.

Proof.

Let us show that
X
is locally elliptic.
Let
({\hat{E}},{\hat{p}},{\hat{s}})
be a dominating spray on
Y
. For a point
x\in X

choose a neighborhood
U
of
x
in
X
such that the restriction

Y|_{U}\to U
is a trivial fiber bundle. Let
\xi\colon U\to Y
be a section
of
Y|_{U}\to U
, let
p\colon E_{U}:=\xi^{*}{\hat{E}}\to U
be the induced
vector bundle over
U
and

\varphi\colon E_{U}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\hat{E}}|_{\xi(U)}

be the induced isomorphism.
Letting
s=\varrho\circ{\hat{s}}\circ\varphi\colon E_{U}\to X
we obtain
a local spray
(E_{U},p,s)
on
U
with values in
X
.
Indeed, let
{\hat{Z}}
be the zero section of
{\hat{p}}\colon{\hat{E}}\to Y
and
Z_{U}

be the zero section of
p\colon E_{U}\to U
. Since
{\hat{s}}|_{\hat{Z}}={\hat{p}}|_{\hat{Z}}

we have
s|_{Z_{U}}=p|_{Z_{U}}
.

Let
y=\xi(x)\in{\hat{U}}=\varrho^{-1}(U)
. Let
0_{y}

be the origin of the vector space
{\hat{E}}_{y}={\hat{p}}^{-1}(y)
.
Since
({\hat{E}},{\hat{p}},{\hat{s}})
is dominating,

d{\hat{s}}|_{T_{0_{y}}{\hat{E}}_{y}}\colon T_{0_{y}}{\hat{E}}_{y}\to T_{y}Y
is surjective.
This yields a surjection

ds=d\varrho\circ d{\hat{s}}\circ d\varphi|_{T_{0_{x}}E_{x}}\colon T_{0_{x}}E_{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{0_{y}}{\hat{E}}_{y}\to T_{y}Y\to T_{x}X.

Thus, the spray
(E_{U},p,s)
is dominating at
x
.
This shows that
X
is locally elliptic, hence elliptic,
see
[
KZ23
, Theorem 0.1]
.
∎

The natural question arises whether the converse
implication holds.
Clearly, the product
X\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{*}
is not elliptic even if
X

is elliptic.
However, we indicate below some particular settings
where the answer is affirmative.

2.1.
Technical lemmas

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Then

extends to an isomorphism

.

Furthermore, suppose that

and

are equipped with
\mu_{2}
-actions
such that

and

for

-equivariant.
Then there are
\mu_{2}
-actions on

such that

and
\tau_{i}

-equivariant.

The pullback yields an isomorphism

,

Hence,

.

Fix a a Cartan subalgebra

of

-orbit

is isomorphic to

.

Recall that a variety

.

{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
such that
x_{0}=(0,0)
is the origin and
L=\{u=0\}
.
Then
{\tilde{U}}_{0}={\tilde{U}}_{1}\cup{\tilde{U}}_{2}
where
{\tilde{U}}_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
is
equipped with coordinates
(u_{i},v_{i})
so that (
\alpha
)
and (
\beta
) above hold.
Morphism

\sigma_{0}
sends a coordinate axis
L_{1}
in
{\tilde{U}}_{1}

isomorphically onto
L
and
\nu_{0}
sends

O_{{\tilde{x}}}
isomorphically onto
L_{1}
.

Replace now
U_{{\tilde{x}}}
by
U_{{\tilde{x}},1}:=\nu_{0}^{-1}({\tilde{U}}_{1})

and
\nu
by

\nu_{1}=\nu_{0}|_{U_{{\tilde{x}},1}}\colon U_{{\tilde{x}},1}\to{\tilde{U}}_{1}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.
We have
O_{{\tilde{x}}}=U_{{\tilde{x}},1}\cap{\tilde{C}}
and

\nu_{1}|_{O_{{\tilde{x}}}}\colon O_{{\tilde{x}}}\to L_{1}

is an isomorphism which sends
x_{0}\in O_{{\tilde{x}}}

to the origin
\bar{0}\in{\tilde{U}}_{1}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
.
Furthermore,
\nu_{1}
is étale on
O_{{\tilde{x}}}
and so,

U_{{\tilde{x}},1}
verifies (i) and (ii).

Consider now the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action

\lambda
on
{\tilde{U}}_{1}={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
by translations
in direction of
L_{1}
so that
L_{1}
is a
\lambda
-orbit.
Replacing

by an appropriate replica

acts freely on

and leaves invariant the center

\tilde{I}|_{U_{{\tilde{x}},1}}
of the blowup
\nu_{0}|_{U_{{\tilde{x}},1}}

Then

to

such that

Therefore,

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Clearly,
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and the Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{n}

with

verify the curve-orbit property.
It is well known that any rational
smooth projective surface
X
non-isomorphic to

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
results from a sequence of blowups starting
with
\mathbb{F}_{n}

for some

.
Moreover, properties (i)–(iv) of Proposition
3.1
hold for
the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration
\pi\colon\mathbb{F}_{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
By this proposition and the induction on the number of blowups
these properties still hold for
X
. Let
x\in X

lies on a fiber component

of the induced
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Choose two distinct points

Let

and

. Then

5.
Appendix A: High transitivity of the

\operatorname{{\rm End}}(X)
-action

It is known that the automorphism group
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)

of a flexible variety
X

acts highly transitively on the smooth locus

X_{\rm reg}
, that is, it acts
m
-transitively
for any
m\geq 1
,
see
[
AFKKZ13
, Theorem 0.1]
.
A similar transitivity property holds
for the monoid of endomorphisms
\operatorname{{\rm End}}(X)
provided
X

is a smooth elliptic variety in the Gromov’ sense.
Abusing the language we say that
\operatorname{{\rm End}}(X)

acts highly transitively on
X
if for
any two isomorphic zero-dimensional subschemes

Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
of
X
every isomorphism

Z_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z_{2}

extends to an endomorphism of
X
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be
a smooth quasiaffine variety.
If
X
is elliptic
then
\operatorname{{\rm End}}(X)
acts highly transitively on
X
.

The above proposition is an easy consequence of
the following epimorphism theorem due to
Kusakabe
[
Kus22
, Theorem 1.2]
,
which generalizes an earlier result of Forstnerič

[
For17b
, Theorem 1.6]

valid for complete elliptic varieties.
This phenomenon
was expected in
[
Gro89
, Sec. 3.4(C)]
; see also
[
Arz22
]

for analogs
in the case of flexible varieties.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any smooth (sub)elliptic variety
X
of dimension
n

there exists a morphism

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\to X
such that

\varphi({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\setminus\operatorname{{\rm Sing}}(\varphi))=X

where

\operatorname{{\rm Sing}}(\varphi)=\{x\in{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}|\varphi\,\,\,\text{is not smooth at}\,\,\,x\}

is the singular locus of
\varphi
.
If
X
is complete then the same is true with
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
instead of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}
.

Using the fact that any morphism of a zero-dimensional scheme
Z\to X

can be lifted through a smooth morphism

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\setminus\operatorname{{\rm Sing}}(\varphi)\to X
,
Kusakabe derives the following interpolation result,
see
[
Kus22
, Corollary 1.5]
; cf.
[
Gro89
, Sec. 3.4(E)]
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth (sub)elliptic variety,

Y
be an affine variety and
Z

be a zero-dimensional subscheme of
Y
.
Then for any morphism
f\colon Z\to X
there exists a
morphism
\tilde{f}\colon Y\to X
such that
\tilde{f}|_{Z}=f
.

In fact, Corollary
5.3
remains valid with the same proof
if one assumes
Y
to be quasiaffine.
Letting in Corollary
5.3

Y=X
and
Z_{1}=Z

we can extend the given morphism
f\colon Z_{1}\to Z_{2}

onto a zero-dimensional subscheme
Z_{2}
of
X

to an endomorphism
\tilde{f}\colon X\to X
.
This yields Proposition
5.1
.
Furthermore, taking in Corollary
5.3

Y={\mathbb{A}}^{1}

we obtain the following corollary, cf.
[
Gro89
, Sec. 3.4(B)]

and
[
KZ00
, p. 1661]
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Any smooth elliptic variety
X
is

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-rich
, that is, through any

m
distinct points of
X
passes an

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-curve
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to X
. Moreover, given a finite collection
of curve jets
j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}
on
X

with not necessarily distinct centers, there exists an

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-curve
f\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to X
interpolating these jets.

Notice that for a flexible smooth affine variety
X

one can find an orbit of a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
-action on
X

interpolating given curve jets, see
[
AFKKZ13
, Theorem 4.14]
.

6.
Appendix B: Gromov’s Extension Lemma

For the reader’s convenience we recall the formulation
of Proposition
1.1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
X

and
(E,p,s)
be a spray
on
U=X\setminus{\rm supp}(D)
with values in
X

such that
p\colon E\to X
is a trivial
bundle of rank
r\geq 1
.
Then there exists a spray
(\tilde{E},\tilde{p},\tilde{s})
on
X

whose restriction to
U
is isomorphic to
(E,p,s)
and
such that

\tilde{s}|_{{\tilde{p}}^{-1}(X\setminus U)}=\tilde{p}|_{{\tilde{p}}^{-1}(X\setminus U)}

and for each
x\in U
the
{\tilde{s}}
-orbit of

x
coincides with the
s
-orbit of
x
.

Proof.

Extend
E
to a trivial vector bundle on
X
;
abusing notation, the latter is again denoted by
p\colon E\to X
.
Let
\xi
be a canonical section of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\to X

such that
{\rm div}(\xi)=D
.

Consider the twisted vector bundle

p_{n}\colon E_{n}=E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-nD)\to X
where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
\xi
does not vanish on
U

the homomorphism
\varphi_{n}\colon E_{n}\to E

induced by the tensor multiplication by
\xi^{n}

restricts to an isomorphism on
U
which sends

s_{n}:=s\circ\varphi_{n}
to
s
.
We claim that
s_{n}
extends to a morphism

E_{n}\to X
for a sufficiently
large
n
. It is easily seen that in the latter case

({\tilde{E}},{\tilde{p}},{\tilde{s}})=(E_{n},p_{n},s_{n})
is a desired spray on
X
.

The argument is local and, thus,
we restrict consideration to
an affine neighborhood
\omega\subset X

of a given point
x_{0}\in\operatorname{{\rm supp}}(D)

such that

•

D|_{\omega}=h^{*}(0)
for some

h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{\omega}(\omega)
and

•

E_{n}|_{\omega}\cong_{\omega}\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}

is a trivial vector bundle.

Notice that over

\omega^{*}=\omega\setminus\operatorname{{\rm supp}}(D)
the spray

(E_{n},p_{n},s_{n})
is the
h^{n}
-homothety of
(E,p,s)
.
The latter means that
s_{n}
is given, after trivialization,
by

s_{n}\colon(x,v)\mapsto s(x,h^{n}(x)v)\qquad\forall(x,v)\in\omega^{*}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}.

Let
\omega\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{m}

be a closed embedding.
Then
s
yields a rational map

s\colon\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\quad\text{of the form}\quad(x,v)\mapsto x+\psi(x,v)

where
\psi
is a rational vector function
which is regular
in a neighborhood of
\omega^{*}\times\{0\}
and vanishes on

\omega^{*}\times\{0\}
.
Every coordinate function of
\psi

can be written in the form

\frac{q(v)}{h^{k}r(v)}\quad\text{where}\quad v=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\quad\text{and}\quad q,r\in{\mathcal{O}}_{\omega}(\omega)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]

are such that
q(0)=0
and the zero loci of
r
and
h

have no common component in
\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.

Furthermore,
we may suppose
that the divisors
r^{*}(0)
and
q^{*}(0)
have no common
irreducible component
T
in
\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
passing through

(x_{0},0)\in\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
.
Indeed, let
T
be such a component. It is well known that

\mathop{\rm Pic}(\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r})={\rm pr}_{1}^{*}(\mathop{\rm Pic}(\omega))
. Hence,
the divisor
T-{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}(T_{0})
is principal
for some divisor
T_{0}
on
\omega
.
Shrinking
\omega
one may suppose that
T_{0}
is principal.
Hence,
T
is principal,
i.e.,
T=f^{*}(0)
for some regular function

f
on
\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
. Thus, dividing
r
and
q

by
f
we can get rid of
T
.

This implies that
r
does not vanish on
\omega^{*}\times\{0\}

since otherwise
\psi
is not regular on
\omega^{*}\times\{0\}
.
In particular,
r(0)=ah^{l}
where
a
is a
non-vanishing function on
\omega
.

The
h^{n}
-homothety
s_{n}
of
s
yields a rational map

s_{n}\colon\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\dashrightarrow\omega\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{m},\quad(x,v)\mapsto x+\psi_{n}(x,v),

where
\psi
is a rational vector function
which is regular
in a neighborhood of
\omega^{*}\times\{0\}
and vanishes on

\omega^{*}\times\{0\}
.
Every coordinate function of
\psi_{n}\in
is of the form

\frac{q(h^{n}v)}{h^{k}r(h^{n}v)}.

Since
q(0)=0
and
[r(h^{n}v)-r(v)]|_{v=0}=0
we see that

q(h^{n}v)=h^{n}\tilde{q}(v)\quad\text{and}\quad r(h^{n}v)=r(0)+h^{n}\tilde{r}(v)=h^{l}a+h^{n}\tilde{r}(v).

Hence, for
n>k+l

the function

\frac{q(h^{n}v)}{h^{k}r(h^{n}v)}=\frac{h^{n-k-l}\tilde{q}(v)}{a+h^{n-l}\tilde{r}(v)}

is regular in a neighborhood
\Omega
of
\omega\times\{0\}

and vanishes to order
n-k-l

on
({\rm supp}(D)\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r})\cap\Omega
.

Consider now
s_{n+1}=s\circ\varphi_{n+1}=s_{n}\circ\varphi_{1}
where,
after trivialization,

\varphi_{1}|_{\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}}\colon\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\to\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r},\quad(x,v)\mapsto(x,h(x)v).

Letting
\widetilde{\Omega}=\varphi_{1}^{-1}(\Omega)\subset\omega\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
we see that

\varphi_{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})\subset\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad(\operatorname{{\rm supp}}(D)\cap\omega)\times{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}.

Hence,
s_{n+1}
is regular on
\widetilde{\Omega}
and so,
on
p_{n+1}^{-1}(\operatorname{{\rm supp}}(D)\cap\omega)
.
This yields a spray
(E_{n+1},p_{n+1},s_{n+1})
on

\omega\cup U
with values in
X
.
Choosing a finite cover of
\operatorname{{\rm supp}}(D)
by affine open subsets
\omega_{i}

and a sufficiently large
n
we get the desired extension of
(E,p,s)
to
a spray
({\tilde{E}},{\tilde{p}},{\tilde{s}})
on
X
.
∎

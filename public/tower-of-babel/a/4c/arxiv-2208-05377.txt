Light-front approach to axial-vector quarkonium γ ∗ ​ γ ∗ \gamma^{*}\gamma^{*} form factors
Izabela Babiarz, Roman Pasechnik, Wolfgang Schäfer, Antoni Szczurek

Abstract
In this work, we perform a detailed study of transition form factors for axial-vector meson production via the two-photon fusion process γ ∗ ​ γ ∗ → 1 + ⁣ + \gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++} , with space-like virtual photons in the initial state and a P P -wave axial-vector quarkonium in the final state. In this analysis, we employ the formalism of light-front quarkonium wave functions obtained from a solution of the Schrödinger equation for a selection of interquark potentials for Q ​ Q ¯ Q\bar{Q} interaction. We found the helicity structure and covariant decomposition of the matrix elements that can be generically applied for any q ​ q ¯ q\bar{q} axial-vector meson γ ∗ ​ γ ∗ → 1 + ⁣ + \gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++} transition, while our numerical results are given for the phenomenologically relevant charmonium χ c ​ 1 \chi_{c1} state. We present the helicity form factors as functions of both photon virtualities. We also obtain, that Q ​ F LT ​ ( Q 2 , 0 ) / F TT ​ ( Q 2 , 0 ) = const . QF_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)/F_{\rm TT}(Q^{2},0)={\rm const.}

I
Introduction

Exclusive production in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions in
\gamma\gamma
fusion has been considered as a probe for internal structure of QCD bounds states quantified by meson-photon transition form factors
Chernyak and Eidelman 2015
;
Köpp et al. 1974
;
Poppe 1986
;
Schuler et al. 1998
;
Li et al. 2022
. Besides the QED production mechanism, such form factor can be used also in color-singlet QCD production mechanism via gluon-gluon fusion. The latter has significant implications in physics of vector and axial-vector meson production in hadronic and nuclear collisions, which recently attracts a lot of attention
Brambilla et al. 2011
.

In Refs.
Babiarz et al. 2019
;
Babiarz et al. 2020a
;
Babiarz et al. 2020b
, we have analysed production of scalar and pseudo-scalar mesons in the light-cone formulation which are
P
- and
S
-wave
Q\bar{Q}
states, respectively. We present the results including the intrinsic (anti)quark motion in the meson wave function, and also taking the non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD) limit which in our case of the
P
-wave meson is proportional to the derivative of the wave function at the origin.

In this work, we continue our studies of the light-cone approach to the transition form factors focusing on
\gamma\gamma
fusion into axial-vector
J^{PC}=1^{++}
mesons. We utilise several well-known models for the interquark potentials in determination of the axial-vector meson wave function on the light cone. Our consideration is general for quarkonium states while our numerical analysis would be concentrated on
\chi_{c,J=1}
state. The corresponding form factors are derived for various meson polarisation states. Provided that, due to the Landau-Yang theorem
Landau 1948
;
Yang 1950
, the axial-vector meson does not decay into a pair of real photons, the corresponding transition form factors and hence the production helicity amplitudes become non-trivial only in the case of virtual initial-state photons.

Our formulas could easily be extended to light
f_{1}
or
a_{1}
mesons, adopting an appropriate light-front quark model. Here we restrict ourselves to heavy quarkonia in the numerical predictions as for light mesons the production is not fully under control
Szczurek 2020
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Sect.
II
we recall the basics of the LF approach to the axial-vector quarkonium and derive the

\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to A
amplitude in terms of the LFWF of the bound state.
In Sect.
III
we present a general covariant description of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
amplitude in terms of invariant helicity form factors which we relate to the transition form factors of our light cone amplitude. In Sect.
IV
we show our numerical results for the relevant form factors for the case of the
\chi_{c1}
meson. We will also dicuss the reduced width for the
\gamma^{*}\gamma
decay and the
Q^{2}
dependence of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma
cross section relevant to single tagged
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions. Finally, concluding remarks and a summary are given in Sect.
V
.

II
Light-front formulation of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
process

II.1
Axial-vector meson wave function

In order to construct the light-front wave function (LFWF) of the
Q\bar{Q}
bound state of good angular momentum quantum numbers, we follow the procedure based on the Melosh transformation
Melosh 1974
;
Jaus 1990
and Terent’ev substitution
Terentev 1976
starting from the quark-model rest-frame wave function (WF). In this work, we are particularly focused on the axial-vector
J^{PC}=1^{++}
quarkonium state (known as
{}^{2S+1}L_{J}={}^{3}P_{1}
), whose WF depends on the meson spin projections
\lambda_{A}=\pm 1,0
, as well as on polarizations
\tau,\bar{\tau}
of the heavy quark and antiquark (with mass
m_{Q}
):

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})}_{\tau\bar{\tau}}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L_{z}+S_{z}=\lambda_{A}}Y_{1L_{z}}(\hat{k})\Big\langle{\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}\tau\bar{\tau}}|{1S_{z}}\Big\rangle\langle{11L_{z}S_{z}}|{1\lambda_{A}}\rangle{u(k)\over k}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\sqrt{3\over 4\pi}\,\xi^{\tau\dagger}_{Q}\,\,\Big(\vec{\sigma}\cdot{\vec{k}\times\vec{E}(\lambda_{A})\over k}\Big)i\sigma_{2}\,\xi^{\bar{\tau}*}_{\bar{Q}}\,\,{u(k)\over k}\,.

Here,
\vec{k}
is the three-momentum of the heavy quark in the rest frame of the
Q\bar{Q}
pair,
k=|\vec{k}|
,
\vec{E}(\lambda_{A})
is the polarization vector of the axial-vector meson defined in Appendix
A
, and
u(k)
is the radial WF which we obtain from solutions of the Schrödinger equation for several different models of the quark-antiquark potential.

The Melosh transform of the spin-orbital part of the WF relies on the transition to LF spinors
\chi_{Q}^{\lambda},\chi_{\bar{Q}}^{\bar{\lambda}*}
, by means of a rotation:

\displaystyle\xi_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\chi_{Q}\,,\qquad\xi^{*}_{\bar{Q}}=R^{*}(1-z,-\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\chi^{*}_{\bar{Q}}\,,

(2)

with the unitary matrix
R(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})
defined as

\displaystyle R(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})={m_{Q}+zM_{Q\bar{Q}}-i\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{n}\times\vec{k})\over\sqrt{(m_{Q}+zM_{Q\bar{Q}})^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}}}={m_{Q}+zM_{Q\bar{Q}}-i\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{n}\times\vec{k})\over\sqrt{zM_{Q\bar{Q}}(M_{Q\bar{Q}}+2m_{Q})}}\,.

(3)

Here,
z
and
1-z
are the fractions of the meson’s LF plus-momentum carried by the quark and antiquark, respectively, while their transverse momenta are denoted by
\pm\mbox{\boldmath$k$}
. We also introduced the unit vector
\vec{n}=(0,0,1)
such that the vector product reads,
\vec{n}\times\vec{k}=(-k_{y},k_{x},0)
. Furthermore,
M_{Q\bar{Q}}
is the invariant mass of the
Q\bar{Q}
system obtained from

\displaystyle M_{Q\bar{Q}}^{2}={\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}\over z(1-z)}\,.

(4)

The LFWF then is obtained as follows

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})=\chi_{Q}^{\lambda\dagger}\,{\cal O}^{\prime}_{\lambda_{A}}\,i\sigma_{2}\,\chi_{\bar{Q}}^{\bar{\lambda}*}\,\,\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,\sqrt{2(M_{Q\bar{Q}}^{2}-4m_{Q}^{2})}\,,

(5)

where we pull out a square-root factor to simplify formulas further on.
The spin-orbital part is encoded in the
2\times 2
-matrix,

\displaystyle{\cal O}^{\prime}_{\lambda_{A}}=\sqrt{3\over 2}\,R^{\dagger}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big(\vec{\sigma}\cdot{\vec{k}\times\vec{E}(\lambda_{A})\over\sqrt{2}k}\Big)R(1-z,-\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,.

(6)

The momentum three-vector in the rest frame of the pair is obtained in terms of LF variables as

\displaystyle\vec{k}=(\mbox{\boldmath$k$},k_{z})=\Big(\mbox{\boldmath$k$},{1\over 2}(2z-1)M_{Q\bar{Q}}\Big)\,.

(7)

Finally, the “radial” LFWF is related to its rest-frame counterpart as

\displaystyle\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})={\pi\sqrt{M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\over 2\sqrt{2}}\,\,{u(k)\over k^{2}}\,.

(8)

Explicit expressions for the LFWF of Eq. (
5
) are found in Appendix
A
.

II.2
Master formula for the light-front helicity amplitude

We now turn to a calculation of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
-fusion amplitude. Analogously to our previous works
Babiarz et al. 2020a
;
Babiarz et al. 2019
;
Babiarz et al. 2020b
, we write down the amplitude in the frame where the photon momenta are
q_{1\mu}=q_{1+}n^{+}_{\mu}+q^{\perp}_{1\mu}
and
q_{2\mu}=q_{2-}n^{-}_{\mu}+q^{\perp}_{2\mu}
, with the light-like vectors
n^{+}_{\mu},n^{-}_{\mu}
, which fulfill
n^{+}\cdot n^{-}=1
. It proves convenient to choose the off-shell polarization-vectors
n^{+}_{\mu}
and
n^{-}_{\mu}
for the first and second photon, respectively. Our amplitude can be obtained from the convolution of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to Q\bar{Q}
amplitude with the LFWF of the axial-vector meson with polarization
\lambda_{A}
:

\displaystyle n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}(\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to A(\lambda_{A}))={\cal N}\int\frac{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}{z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\sum_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}{\cal A}_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\,,

(9)

where we pulled out the fine structure constant
\alpha_{\rm em}
and a normalization factor from the sum over colors, such that

\displaystyle{\cal N}=4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}e_{f}^{2}\,{\mathrm{Tr}\,\openone_{\mathrm{color}}\over\sqrt{N_{c}}}=4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\,.

(10)

Here,
N_{c}=3
, and the electric charge
e_{f}=2/3
for the charm quark of interest in this work.

Our master formula
Babiarz 2022
for the light-front helicity amplitude of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
process reads

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\lambda_{A})\equiv\int\frac{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}{z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\sum_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}{\cal A}_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}=(-2)\int{dz\,d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over\sqrt{z(1-z)}16\pi^{3}}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{-m_{Q}\Big[{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\Big(\sqrt{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$e$}(-)\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{++}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\sqrt{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$e$}(+)\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{--}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(2z(1-z)Q_{1}^{2}+(1-2z)(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})\Big)\Big[{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

\displaystyle-(1-2z)(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})\Big[{1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

\displaystyle+i[\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}]\Big[{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})-\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

\displaystyle+i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\Big[{1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}\,^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})-\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)\Big\}\,.

(11)

Here, we have introduced the shorthand notation,

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}=-\mbox{\boldmath$k$}+(1-z)\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\,,\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}=\mbox{\boldmath$k$}+z\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\,,\qquad Q_{1}^{2}\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}^{2}\,,

(12)

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}=m_{Q}^{2}+z(1-z)Q_{1}^{2}\,.

(13)

We now wish to derive a representation of the helicity amplitudes in terms of the radial LFWFs, which can be readily used in a numerical analysis. To this end, we introduce an abbreviated notation for integrals over the radial LFWF:

\displaystyle\langle{f(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})}\rangle\equiv(-2){\sqrt{3\over 2}}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,f(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,,

(14)

as well as

\displaystyle{\cal A}={1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\,,\qquad{\cal B}={1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\,.

(15)

II.3
Longitudinally polarised meson:
\lambda_{A}=0

Starting from the master formula (
11
), one straightforwardly obtains the helicity amplitude for
\lambda_{A}=0
state production:

\displaystyle{\cal T}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-2i)\sqrt{{3\over 2}}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big\{{4m_{Q}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}]{\cal A}+{4\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$k$}]{\cal A}

(16)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]{-4\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}{\cal B}\Big\}=i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]I_{0}+i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{1}]\,,

where

\displaystyle I_{0}=-4\,\left\langle{{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\,{\cal B}}\right\rangle\,,\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{1}=4\left\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\,{m_{Q}^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\,{\cal A}}\right\rangle\,.

(17)

Now, by rotational invariance, we can write

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\,I_{1}\,,\qquad I_{1}={\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{1}\over Q_{2}^{2}}={4\over Q_{2}^{2}}\left\langle{(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,{m_{Q}^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\,{\cal A}}\right\rangle\,,\qquad Q_{2}^{2}\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}^{2}\,.

(18)

We remind the reader that
M_{Q\bar{Q}}
depends both on
z
and
\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}
.
Finally, our amplitude reads

\displaystyle{\cal T}(0)=i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\Big(I_{0}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+I_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)\equiv i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\,\Phi_{0}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,.

(19)

We can write out the explicit expressions for integrals
I_{0},I_{1}
:

\displaystyle I_{0}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\sqrt{3\over 2}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\,\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\Big({1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle I_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8\sqrt{3\over 2}{1\over Q_{2}^{2}}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}){\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}\over M_{Q\bar{Q}}}\,(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big({1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\,.

II.4
Transversely polarised meson:
\lambda_{A}=\pm 1

In the case of transversely polarised axial-vector meson, the master formula (
11
) leads to

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-4i)\sqrt{3\over 2}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big\{[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]m_{Q}^{2}(1-2z){\cal A}+2Q_{1}^{2}z(1-z)[\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]{\cal A}

(21)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(1-2z)(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})[\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]{\cal B}-(1-2z)[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}){\cal B}+(1-2z)(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})[\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]{\cal A}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(1-2z)[\mbox{\boldmath$k$},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}](\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}){\cal A}\Big\}\,.

Here, we have integrals that involve the vector
k
as well as the tensor
k_{i}k_{j}
.

Let us introduce the following integrals,

\displaystyle J_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4m_{Q}^{2}\langle{(1-2z)\,{\cal A}}\rangle\,,\qquad{\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{1}}=8\langle{z(1-z)\,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\,{\cal A}}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\langle{(1-2z)\,\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\,{\cal B}}\rangle\,,\qquad J_{ij}=4\langle{(1-2z)k_{i}k_{j}\,{\cal A}}\rangle\,.

(22)

Then, our amplitude takes the form,

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]\,J_{0}+\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}^{2}[\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]-(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})[\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]-[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*})

(23)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle J_{ij}\varepsilon_{jl}\Big(q_{1i}E^{*}_{l}-E^{*}_{i}q_{1l}\Big)\,.

Again, we can simplify this structure by introducing scalar integrals:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{1}\over Q_{2}^{2}}={8\over Q_{2}^{2}}\,\langle{z(1-z)\,(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,{\cal A}}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$J$}_{2}\over Q_{2}^{2}}={4\over Q_{2}^{2}}\,\langle{(1-2z)\,(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\,{\cal B}}\rangle\,.

(24)

The tensor integral can be decomposed into two scalar functions,

\displaystyle J_{ij}=\Big(\delta_{ij}-{q_{2i}q_{2j}\over Q_{2}^{2}}\Big)\,J_{3}+{q_{2i}q_{2j}\over Q_{2}^{2}}\,J_{4}\,,

(25)

where

\displaystyle J_{3}={4\over Q_{2}^{2}}\,\left\langle{(1-2z)\Big(Q_{2}^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}-(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})^{2}\Big)\,{\cal A}}\right\rangle\,,\quad J_{4}={4\over Q_{2}^{2}}\,\left\langle{(1-2z)(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})^{2}\,{\cal A}}\right\rangle\,.

(26)

Then our amplitude becomes

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}](J_{0}+2J_{3})+[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]Q_{1}^{2}J_{1}

(27)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big((\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]+[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*})\Big){1\over Q_{2}^{2}}\Big((J_{4}-J_{3})-Q_{2}^{2}J_{2}\Big)\,.

It can be further simplified by noticing that

\displaystyle q_{1i}q_{2j}q_{2k}E^{*}_{l}\Big(\delta_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}+\delta_{jl}\varepsilon_{ik}\Big)=q_{1i}q_{2j}q_{2k}E^{*}_{l}\Big(\delta_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}-\delta_{lj}\varepsilon_{ki}\Big)\,.

(28)

The tensor in brackets is antisymmetric in
il
and only the symmetric part in
kj
enters. But in two dimensions, any antisymmetric in
il
tensor must be proportional to
\varepsilon_{il}
. One can easily check that in the above expression one can replace

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}-\delta_{lj}\varepsilon_{ki}\rightarrow\,\varepsilon_{il}\delta_{jk}\,,

(29)

thus, yielding a useful identity

\displaystyle(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]+[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*})=[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]Q_{2}^{2}\,.

(30)

The latter enables us to represent our amplitude in the following compact form,

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\pm)=[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]\,\Phi_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]\,\Phi_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

(31)

with two form factors

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=J_{0}+J_{3}+J_{4}-Q_{2}^{2}J_{2}\,,\qquad\Phi_{2}=Q_{1}^{2}\,J_{1}\,,

(32)

which, using the expressions in Appendix
B
can be represented in the following integral form

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\sqrt{3\over 2}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})(1-2z)\Big\{(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2})\Big({1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)

(33)

\displaystyle-(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big({1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-8\sqrt{3\over 2}{Q_{1}^{2}\over Q_{2}^{2}}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over z(1-z)16\pi^{3}}\psi(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})z(1-z)(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big({1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{A}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\,.

We thus have completed our first task and expressed our amplitude for the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to A(\lambda_{A})
process in terms of form factors that are calculated as integrals involving the LFWF.

II.5
NRQCD limit

In the nonrelativistic QCD (NRQCD) limit, we expand the integrand of our amplitude to linear order in
\xi=z-{1\over 2}
and
k
. Then,

\displaystyle z={1\over 2}+\xi\,,\quad 1-z={1\over 2}-\xi\,,\quad z(1-z)={1\over 4}-\xi^{2}\sim{1\over 4}\,.

(34)

We introduce
\mu^{2}\equiv(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2})/4
, and expand the relevant combinations of denominators:

\displaystyle{\cal A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon^{2}}-{1\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}^{2}_{B}+\varepsilon^{2}}={2\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}+\xi\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})\over\mu^{4}}\,,\qquad{\cal B}={1-z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon^{2}}+{z\over\mbox{\boldmath$l$}_{B}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}={1\over\mu^{2}}\,.

Inserting these expressions into our master formula, we obtain the amplitude in the following useful form,

\displaystyle{\cal T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-2\over\mu^{4}}\int{dzd^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\over\sqrt{z(1-z)}16\pi^{3}}\Big\{i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\mu^{2}\,\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})-\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

(36)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(Q_{1}^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\xi Q_{1}^{2}Q_{2}^{2}-2\xi\mu^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})\Big)\Big(\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{+-}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{-+}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2m_{Q}(\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$k$}+\xi\mbox{\boldmath$k$}^{2})\Big(\sqrt{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*}(-)\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{++}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})+\sqrt{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*}(+)\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1})\Psi^{(\lambda_{A})*}_{--}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$k$})\Big)\Big\}\,.

After somewhat lengthy calculations, we can reduce our result to a simple form:

\displaystyle{\cal T}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\,t_{0}\,{Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}\over[Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2}]^{2}},

\displaystyle{\cal T}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Big(Q_{1}^{2}[\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]-Q_{2}^{2}[\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}]\Big)\,t_{0}\,{2m_{Q}\over[Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2}]^{2}}\,,

(37)

with

\displaystyle t_{0}\equiv 16\sqrt{3\over 2}\,{R^{\prime}(0)\over\sqrt{\pi M^{3}}}\,=4\,R^{\prime}(0)\sqrt{3\over\pi m_{Q}^{3}}\,,

(38)

where the mass of the resonance
M=2m_{Q}
in the NRQCD approximation.
We can now easily read off the explicit expressions for the form factors
\Phi_{0,1,2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})
in the NRQCD limit:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{0}{Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}\over[Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2}]^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{0}\,{Q_{2}^{2}\,2m_{Q}\over[Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2}]^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{0}\,{-Q_{1}^{2}\,2m_{Q}\over[Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}+4m_{Q}^{2}]^{2}}\,.

(39)

III
Tensor decomposition of the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
amplitude

III.1
Helicity form factors

Let us start by parametrizing the covariant
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
amplitude by three separately gauge invariant tensor structures with the corresponding invariant form factors
F_{\rm TT}
,
F_{\rm LT}
, and
F_{\rm TL}
:

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Big(q_{1}-q_{2}+{Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\over(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}}(q_{1}+q_{2})\Big)_{\rho}\,\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}\,{M\over 2X}F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

(40)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle ie_{\mu}^{L}(q_{1})\tilde{G}_{\nu\rho}\,{1\over\sqrt{X}}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+ie_{\nu}^{L}(q_{2})\tilde{G}_{\mu\rho}\,{1\over\sqrt{X}}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,.

Above we introduced

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q_{1}^{\alpha}q_{2}^{\beta}\,,

(41)

and the polarization vectors of longitudinal photons

\displaystyle e_{\mu}^{L}(q_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{{-q_{1}^{2}\over X}}\Big(q_{2\mu}-{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}\over q_{1}^{2}}q_{1\mu}\Big)\,,\qquad e_{\nu}^{L}(q_{2})=\sqrt{{-q_{2}^{2}\over X}}\Big(q_{1\nu}-{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}\over q_{2}^{2}}q_{2\nu}\Big)\,.

(42)

We are interested in the spacelike virtualities of photons only, i.e.
Q_{i}^{2}=-q_{i}^{2}=\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{i}^{2}>0,\,i=1,2
, and we denote the standard kinematic variable

\displaystyle X=(q_{1}\cdot q_{2})^{2}-q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}={1\over 4}\Big(M^{4}+2M^{2}(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})+(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})^{2}\Big)>0\,.

(43)

In Eq. (
40
) we have introduced kinematic factors in such a way, that the form factors
F_{\rm TT},F_{\rm LT},F_{\rm TL}
all have the same mass-dimension as the invariant amplitude and are related to helicity amplitudes in the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
-c.m. frame in a straightforward manner without additional kinematic factors, for a more detailed discussion, see Appendix
C
. Different conventions and tensor structures can be useful depending on the application, see e.g. Refs.
Poppe 1986
;
Pascalutsa et al. 2012
;
Köpp et al. 1974
;
Milstein and Rudenko 2020
;
Hoferichter and Stoffer 2020
.
Then, the amplitude for the final-state meson with polarization
\lambda_{A}
is obtained as

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}(\lambda_{A})={\cal M}_{\mu\nu\rho}E^{\rho*}(\lambda_{A})\,,

(44)

in terms of the meson polarization four-vector
E^{\rho}(\lambda_{A})
. All three tensor structures in (
40
) are separately orthogonal to the four-momentum
P_{\rho}=(q_{1}+q_{2})_{\rho}
of the meson.
Here we are interested only in the case of the on-shell meson,
P^{2}=M^{2}
. For the case of
P^{2}\neq M^{2}
, the form factors must be regarded as a function of all virtualities,
Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2},P^{2}
and in addition an independent tensor structure
\propto P_{\rho}\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}
appears.
The associated form factor is the “anomaly form factor” of the vector-vector-axial correlator. As we have
P\cdot E^{*}(\lambda_{A})=0
for the physical state, it does not contribute to our problem.

In what follows, we choose a basis of polarization states for the axial-vector which is convenient for our light-front calculation of the relevant amplitudes. Such polarization states read explicitly:

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over M}P_{\rho}-{M\over P_{+}}n^{-}_{\rho}\,,\qquad E_{\rho}(\pm)=E^{\perp}_{\rho}(\pm)-{E^{\perp}(\pm)\cdot P\over P_{+}}n^{-}_{\rho}\,.

(45)

Here, the vector
E^{\perp}_{\mu}
has only transverse components
1
1

1

We use the light-front parametrization of four-momenta
a=(a_{+},a_{-},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}),a^{2}=2a_{+}a_{-}-\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{2}
.
:

\displaystyle E^{\perp}(\pm)=(0,0,\mbox{\boldmath$E$}(\pm))\quad{\rm with}\quad\mbox{\boldmath$E$}(\lambda)=-{1\over\sqrt{2}}(\lambda\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{x}+i\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{y})\,.

(46)

Symmetry under exchanging
q_{1}\leftrightarrow q_{2}\,,\,\mu\leftrightarrow\nu
(crossing symmetry) entails that our form factors must satisfy

\displaystyle F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})=-F_{\rm TT}(Q_{2}^{2},Q_{1}^{2})\,,\qquad F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})=-F_{\rm TL}(Q_{2}^{2},Q_{1}^{2})\,.

(47)

Due to its antisymmetry, the form factor
F_{\rm TT}
vanishes on the diagonal
F_{\rm TT}(Q^{2},Q^{2})=0
, and in particular for two on-shell photons
F_{\rm TT}(0,0)=0
, thereby fulfilling the Landau–Yang theorem.
The latter has no bearing on the behaviour of
F_{\rm LT},F_{\rm TL}
. However, when the virtuality of the longitudinal photon goes to zero, we have

\displaystyle F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\propto Q_{1}\,,\qquad{\rm for}\,\,Q_{1}^{2}\to 0\,,

(48)

and accordingly for
F_{\rm TL}
, in order to cancel a spurious singular behaviour of the tensor decomposition of
{\cal M}_{\mu\nu\rho}
. Note, that the limit

\displaystyle f_{\rm LT}=\lim_{Q^{2}\to 0}{F_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)\over Q}\,,

(49)

is therefore finite and generally nonzero. In absence of a finite decay width to
\gamma\gamma
it serves as a measure of the two-photon coupling strength of the axial-vector meson.

III.2
Projection on the light-front directions

We now want to calculate the projections of the amplitude on the light-front directions
n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}
. We are interested in the frame, where photon momenta have the form

\displaystyle q_{1\mu}=q_{1}^{+}n^{+}_{\mu}+q^{\perp}_{1\mu}\,,\qquad q_{2\mu}=q_{2}^{-}n^{-}_{\mu}+q^{\perp}_{2\mu}\,,

(50)

such that
Q_{i}^{2}=-q_{i}^{\perp 2}=\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{i}^{2}
. Let us collect a few useful contractions:

\displaystyle n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}\tilde{G}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\perp}_{\alpha\beta}q_{1}^{\alpha}q_{2}^{\beta}=[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\,,\quad n^{+}\cdot e^{L}(q_{1})={Q_{1}\over\sqrt{X}}q_{2}^{-}\,,\quad n^{-}\cdot e^{L}(q_{2})={Q_{2}\over\sqrt{X}}q_{1}^{+}\,.

(51)

Our amplitude now takes the form

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\Big(q_{1}-q_{2}+{Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\over M^{2}}(q_{1}+q_{2})\Big)_{\rho}\,{M\over 2X}F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

(52)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i{q_{1}^{+}q_{2}^{-}\over X}\Big(\varepsilon^{\perp}_{\rho\alpha}q^{\perp\alpha}_{2}Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+\varepsilon^{\perp}_{\rho\alpha}q^{\perp\alpha}_{1}Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i{[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\over X}\Big(Q_{2}q_{1}^{+}n^{+}_{\rho}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})-Q_{1}q_{2}^{-}n_{\rho}^{-}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)\,.

Furthermore, we obtain for the helicity amplitudes

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}{\cal M}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{+\mu}n^{-\nu}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu\rho}E^{\rho*}(0)

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\Big\{\Big(M^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\Big){F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\over 2X}+{Q_{2}M\over X}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big\}\,.

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}{\cal M}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}(\pm)){MF_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\over X}+i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}](\mbox{\boldmath$P$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}(\pm)){Q_{2}\over X}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{M^{2}+\mbox{\boldmath$P$}^{2}\over 2X}\Big(i[\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}(\pm),\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+i[\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}(\pm),\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}]Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)\,.

For the extraction of form factors from our amplitude, it is convenient to rewrite the transverse amplitude using the identities of Eq. (
30
).
We can then represent our helicity amplitudes as follows:

\displaystyle{X\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}{\cal M}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}]\Big\{{1\over 2}\Big(M^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\Big)F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+MQ_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big\}\,,

(54)

\displaystyle{X\over 4\pi\alpha_{\rm em}}{\cal M}(\pm)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]\Big(Q_{1}^{2}MF_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})-{1\over 2}(M^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-i[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}]\Big(Q_{1}^{2}Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+{1\over 2}(M^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle-[\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$E$}^{*}](\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2})\Big(MF_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big)\,.

It is useful to write down symmetric (
F_{S}
) and antisymmetric (
F_{A}
) combinations of form factors,

\displaystyle F_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})-Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle F_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,.

(55)

Now we wish to relate the form factors
\Phi_{i}
of our LF amplitude to the invariant form factors of the covariant amplitude in Eq. (
40
). To this end, it is instructive to turn to the helicity amplitudes corresponding to
linear
polarizations of the meson. These can then be expanded in a Fourier series in
\cos n\phi,\sin n\phi
, where
\phi
is the angle between the photon transverse momenta
\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}
. Here, we use the following frame: the longitudinal polarization defines the
z
-direction, and the
x
-direction is taken along the meson transverse momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$P$}=\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}+\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}
. For a covariant definition of this particular helicity frame, see Appendix
D
. We can now project the amplitude onto the linear polarizations and obtain the following results:

\displaystyle(-i){\cal T}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{1}Q_{2}\sin\phi\,\Phi_{0}\ ,

\displaystyle(-i)|\mbox{\boldmath$P$}|\,{\cal T}_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{1}Q_{2}\sin\phi\,\Big(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle(-i)|\mbox{\boldmath$P$}|\,{\cal T}_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{2}\Phi_{1}+Q_{2}^{2}\Phi_{2}+Q_{1}Q_{2}\cos\phi\Big(\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}\Big)\,.

(56)

We have collected rather lengthy expressions for our covariant amplitude in Appendix
D
. Let us now make some observations on these results. Firstly, we see that in the amplitudes of Eq. (
56
), there are no terms proportional to
\cos 2\phi
or
\sin 2\phi
. These do appear, however, in the decomposition of the general invariant amplitude
{\cal M}
– they obviously are related to the last term in Eq. (
III.2
). These terms are all multiplied by the combination,

\displaystyle f_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\equiv MF_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+F_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

(57)

which, for consistency with Eq. (
56
), has to vanish identically, i.e.

\displaystyle f_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\equiv 0\,.

(58)

This appears to be a peculiar identity related to the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to Q\bar{Q}
helicity amplitudes.
Consequently, the number of independent form factors is reduced to two, say,
F_{A},F_{S}
. In effect, the form factor for two transverse photons is entirely determined through a mixture of longitudinal/transverse (
F_{\rm LT},F_{\rm TL}
) form factors.

Interestingly, after exploiting the cancellation of Eq. (
58
), all our amplitudes have simple symmetry properties, namely:

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-iQ_{1}Q_{2}\over 2X}\cdot\sin\phi\cdot f_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$P$}|{\cal M}_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-iQ_{1}Q_{2}\over 2X}\cdot\sin\phi\cdot Mf_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle|\mbox{\boldmath$P$}|{\cal M}_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{i\over 2X}\Big\{\Big(2Q_{1}^{2}Q_{2}^{2}+\nu(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})\Big)F_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+\nu(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})F_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

(59)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Q_{1}Q_{2}\cos\phi\Big[(2\nu+Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})F_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})F_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\Big]\Big\}\,.

Here, we defined

\displaystyle\nu\equiv q_{1}\cdot q_{2}={1\over 2}(M^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})\,,

(60)

as well as

\displaystyle f_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+MF_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\over M}F_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+MF_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,.

Let us now proceed and try to extract form factors
F_{A},F_{S}
. To this end, we chose the
y
-component of the amplitude, i.e. the linear polarization orthogonal to the “production plane”. As a short-hand notation, we define the form factors

\displaystyle G_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{2}\Phi_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+Q_{2}^{2}\Phi_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+{1\over 2}(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{S}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle G_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})+\Phi_{2}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})=\Phi_{A}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\,,

(62)

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{S}\equiv\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\,,\qquad\Phi_{A}\equiv\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}\,,

(63)

are symmetric and antisymmetric in
Q_{1}^{2}\leftrightarrow Q_{2}^{2}
combinations. We then derive a system of linear equations:

\displaystyle e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\,\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}\\
G_{2}\end{pmatrix}={1\over 2X}\begin{pmatrix}2Q_{1}^{2}Q_{2}^{2}+\nu(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})&\nu(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})\\
2\nu+Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}&Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}F_{A}\\
F_{S}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(64)

Here, the determinant of the
2\times 2
matrix is
\det(\dots)=-2X(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})
so that we can invert the matrix to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}F_{A}\\
F_{S}\end{pmatrix}={-e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\over Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}&-\nu(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})\\
-(2\nu+Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})&2Q_{1}^{2}Q_{2}^{2}+\nu(Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}\\
G_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(65)

This finally gives us

\displaystyle F_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\Big\{(\nu-Q_{1}^{2})\Phi_{1}+(\nu-Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{2}\Big\}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\over 2}\Big\{M^{2}\Phi_{A}-(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{S}\Big\}\,,

and

\displaystyle F_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\Big\{(\nu+Q_{1}^{2})\Phi_{1}-(\nu+Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{2}\Big\}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\over 2}\Big\{(M^{2}+2Q_{1}^{2}+2Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{S}+(Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{A}\Big\}\,,

or, finally, the original form factors
F_{\rm TL},F_{\rm LT}
:

\displaystyle Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\over 2}\Big\{(\nu-Q_{1}^{2})\Phi_{A}-(\nu+Q_{1}^{2})\Phi_{S}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{N_{c}}\over 2}\Big\{(\nu-Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{A}+(\nu+Q_{2}^{2})\Phi_{S}\Big\}\,,

(68)

and

\displaystyle F_{\rm TT}=-{1\over M}\Big\{Q_{1}F_{\rm LT}+Q_{2}F_{\rm TL}\Big\}\,.

(69)

A final comment on the determination of
F_{\rm TT},F_{\rm TL},F_{\rm LT}
is in order. Evidently, we have at our disposal three helicity amplitudes, and we start out with three form factors. However, as it turns out, a cancellation that relates
F_{\rm TT}
with
F_{\rm TL}
and
F_{\rm LT}
makes our system over determined. In fact, we could have obtained our form factors
F_{A},F_{S}
just as well from the comparison of
{\cal M}_{x}
and
{\cal M}_{y}
or from
{\cal M}_{z}
and one of the transverse polarizations. If we stay within the transverse polarizations, there is no ambiguity, and the expressions in term of
\Phi_{1,2}
will be the same. However, involving the longitudinal polarization involves the form factor
\Phi_{0}
, which is not related in any obvious way to
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
. This issue is evidently related to the problems with rotational symmetry in LF quantization. We believe that it is an artefact of the treatment of the bound state as a pure
Q\bar{Q}
state, however, a detailed discussion of the latter goes beyond the scope of this work.

Before we come to our full numerical results including the integrals over the quarkonium wave function, we present the NRQCD limits of the invariant form factors. They are all related to the derivative of the rest frame WF at the origin,
R^{\prime}(0)
, as follows:

\displaystyle F_{\rm TT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{6N_{c}\over\pi M^{3}}R^{\prime}(0)\,{Q_{1}^{2}-Q_{2}^{2}\over\nu}\,,

\displaystyle F_{\rm LT}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{6N_{c}\over\pi M}R^{\prime}(0){(\nu+Q_{2}^{2})Q_{1}\over\nu^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{\rm TL}(Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e_{f}^{2}\sqrt{6N_{c}\over\pi M}R^{\prime}(0){(\nu+Q_{1}^{2})Q_{2}\over\nu^{2}}\,,

(70)

where, within the accuracy of the NRQCD approximation, one should use
M=2m_{Q}
.

IV
Form factor visualization and observables

We now come to the presentation of our numerical results.
First we will discuss our results for the form factors
\Phi_{A,S}
of the light front amplitude from which all invariant form factors are calculated.

Then, we will recall the main observables which can be extracted in
e^{+}e^{-}
reactions and how they are related to the invariant form factors.

All our results are obtained for the
\chi_{c1}
meson. Below we use the mass
M=3.410\,\rm{GeV}
. The light front wave functions were obtained from the radial wave functions in the
1P
partial wave. More details on the WF and the
c\bar{c}
potentials used can be found in Ref.
Babiarz et al. 2020b
.

IV.1
Form factors of the LF amplitude

Let us first discuss the form factors
\Phi_{0,1,2}

IV.4
Virtual photon cross sections

The transition form factors obtained in this work can be used to make predictions for the production of axial-vector mesons in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}A
reaction which proceeds via the
\gamma\gamma
mode. Here one distinguishes between double-tagged processes, where both final state leptons are registered and single-tagged production, where only one of the leptons is measured. The former reactions allow access to the full dependence on
Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2}
of form factors, while for the latter ones
one of photons will be quasi-real, and only the one related to the tagged lepton will have finite virtuality
Q^{2}
.

It is a standard procedure to write the cross section for the
e^{+}e^{-}
reaction in terms of a density matrix of photon fluxes and virtual photon cross sections as well as response functions related to interferences, see e.g. Ref.
Budnev et al. 1975
;
Poppe 1986
.

The non-vanishing virtual photon cross sections for the production of an axial-vector resonance read

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm TT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4\sqrt{X}}\,\Big(W(++,++)+W(+-,+-)\Big)={1\over 4\sqrt{X}}\,W(++,++)\,,\,\,

\displaystyle\ \sigma_{\rm LT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2\sqrt{X}}W(0+,0+)\,,\qquad\sigma_{\rm TL}={1\over 2\sqrt{X}}W(+0,+0)\,,

(72)

where for the resonance of mass
M
and width
\Gamma
we can write

\displaystyle W(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4})={M\Gamma\over(\hat{s}-M^{2})^{2}+M^{2}\Gamma^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{A}}{\cal M}^{*}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{\cal M}_{\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}}\,.

(73)

Here
\sqrt{\hat{s}}
is the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
cm energy, and
{\cal M}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
are the helicity amplitudes in the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
cm frame. We have collected the latter in Appendix
C
. For the narrow resonance as the
\chi_{c1}
of this work, the Breit-Wigner prefactor above can be replaced by
\pi\delta(\hat{s}-M^{2})
.

Current experiments are not able to perform the
\rm TT
vs
\rm LT,TL
separation, and when tagging, say, the first lepton, one is sensitive to the total
\gamma^{*}\gamma
cross section

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm tot}(Q^{2},0)\equiv\sigma_{\rm TT}(Q^{2},0)+\epsilon\sigma_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)=\epsilon\sigma_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)\Big(1+{1\over\epsilon}R_{\rm TL}(Q^{2})\Big)\,.

(74)

with the photon polarization parameter
\epsilon
, which in practice is close to one,
\epsilon\approx 1
. Now notice, that we have shown that for the
Q\bar{Q}
bound state
F_{\rm TT}
is calculable from
F_{\rm LT}
and
F_{\rm TL}
. In particular, for the case of one real photon, we have

\displaystyle F_{\rm TT}(Q^{2},0)=-{Q\over M}F_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)\,,

(75)

so that we obtain the universal result

\displaystyle R_{\rm TL}={F^{2}_{\rm TT}(Q^{2},0)\over 2F^{2}_{\rm LT}(Q^{2},0)}={Q^{2}\over 2M^{2}}\,,

(76)

which does not depend on the wave function parameters/potential model used.

While the
1^{++}
meson does not decay into two real photons, it is common practice to introduce (convention-dependent) effective virtual photon widths, see e.g. Refs.
Aihara et al. 1988
;
Olsson 1988
. These are defined through their relation to virtual photon cross sections
defined
through Eq. (
72
). We follow the convention

(77)

where

V
Conclusions

We have studied the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to 1^{++}
quarkonium transition form factors in a light front approach. The quarkonium LFWFs have been obtained from solutions of the Schrödinger equation for
c\bar{c}
interaction transformed to the LF via the Terent’ev prescription for the radial part and a Melosh transform for the spin-orbit part. Different interaction potentials from the literature have been used to generate the charmonium wave function.

In our derivation we started from the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
-fusion amplitude for the light-like photon polarizations
n^{+}_{\mu},n^{-}_{\mu}
. Subsequently we have demonstrated how to extract invariant form factors
F_{\rm TT}
,
F_{\rm LT}
,
F_{\rm TL}
, which refer to photon polarizations in the
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}
c.m.-frame. We have obtained these form factors as a function of both photon virtualities. We have found that for the
Q\bar{Q}
–bound state the form factor
F_{\rm TT}
is determined through
F_{\rm LT}
and
F_{\rm TL}
.

We have also analyzed the situation where only one photon is virtual and the second photon is assumed to be real, which is relevant for single-tagged
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions. Here, the ratio
F_{\rm TT}/F_{\rm LT}
turned out to be universal and independent of the potential used.

Our predictions for
\gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to\chi_{c1}
are ready for experimental verification. This could be done for example by the Belle-2 collaboration in
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}\chi_{c1}
reaction, by measuring e.g.
\chi_{c1}\to J/\psi\gamma
with one and two electron tagging (
{\rm Br}(\chi_{c1}\to J/\psi\gamma)
=
(34.3\,\pm\,1.0)
%
Workman 2022
). A similar study has already been initiated in Ref.
Teramoto et al. 2021
for the axial-vector meson
\chi_{c1}(3872)
(or X(3872)), using the
X(3872)\to J/\psi\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay. This meson is supposed to have a large non-
c\bar{c}
component, and is still rather enigmatic (see e.g. Ref.
Cisek et al. 2022
for a consideration of its production in the gluon-gluon fusion process including a
c\bar{c}
admixture).

Bicocycle Double Cross Constructions
Oğul Esen, Partha Guha, Serkan Sütlü

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a bicocycle double cross product (resp. sum) Lie group (resp. Lie algebra), and a bicocycle double cross product bialgebra, generalizing the unified products. On the level of Lie groups the construction yields a Lie group on the product space of two pointed manifolds, none of which being necessarily a subgroup. On the level of Lie algebras, similarly, a Lie algebra is obtained on the direct sum of two vector spaces, none of which is required to be a subalgebra. Finally, on the quantum level the theory presents a bialgebra, on the tensor product of two (co)algebras that are not necessarily sub-bialgebras, the semidual of which being a cocycle bicrossproduct bialgebra.

Notation and Conventions

Unadorned morphisms are assumed to be the morphisms of the ambient category, the category of vector spaces, groups, bialgebras etc. and the additional properties (such as algebra morphism, coalgebra morphism etc.) will be noted on, or under the arrows. On the level of Lie algebras we shall consider a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, with a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{m}\,\oplus\,\mathfrak{h}
into vector spaces, the generic elements of which to be denoted by
\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime},...\in\mathfrak{m}
,
Z,Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime},...\in\mathfrak{h}
, and
(\xi,Z),(\xi^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly, we shall consider a Lie group
G

G\cong M\times H
into manifolds. We shall, in this case, refer the generic elements as
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},...\in M
and
h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime},...\in H

(x,h),(x^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in G
. On the level of bialgebras, we shall make use of decompositions
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathfrak{M}\otimes\mathcal{H}

x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},\ldots\in\mathfrak{M}

h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime},\ldots\in\mathcal{H}

2.1.
Bicocycle Double Cross Sum Lie Algebras

In this subsection we shall introduce the construction of a Lie algebra out of two subspaces. More precisely, given two linear spaces
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{h}
we shall record the necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
to be a Lie algebra.

Accordingly, let
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be equipped with

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi:\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime}\mapsto\phi(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})=:\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta:\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime}\mapsto\theta(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})=:\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad Z\otimes Z^{\prime}\mapsto\mu(Z\otimes Z^{\prime})=:\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\gamma:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad Z\otimes Z^{\prime}\mapsto\gamma(Z\otimes Z^{\prime})=:\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),

as well as the cross-relations given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad Z\otimes\xi\mapsto\varphi(Z\otimes\xi)=:\varphi(Z,\xi),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\psi:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad Z\otimes\xi\mapsto\psi(Z\otimes\xi)=:\psi(Z,\xi).

Proposition 2.1
.

Given a pair
(\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{h})
of vector spaces, equipped with the linear maps (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
); the direct sum

\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{m}\,\oplus\,\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra via

(2.7)

[\xi+Z,\xi^{\prime}+Z^{\prime}]=\Big(\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})+\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime})-\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi)+\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime})+\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime})-\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi)+\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\Big).

if and only if

(2.8)

\displaystyle\phi(\xi,\xi)=0,\quad\theta(\xi,\xi)=0,\quad\gamma(Z,Z)=0,\quad\mu(Z,Z)=0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\varphi(Z,\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}))=\phi(\varphi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\phi(\xi,\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime}))+\varphi(\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})-\varphi(\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi)+\gamma(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),Z),

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mu(Z,\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}))=\theta(\varphi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\theta(\xi,\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime}))+\psi(\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})-\psi(\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime})),\xi)-\psi(Z,\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\varphi(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)=\varphi(Z,\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi))-\varphi(Z,\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi))+\gamma(\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime})+\gamma(Z,\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi))-\phi(\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\psi(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)=\mu(Z,\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi))+\mu(\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime})+\psi(Z,\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi))-\psi(Z^{\prime},\varphi(Z,\xi))-\theta(\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})}\,\phi(\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})}\,\varphi(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})=0,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})}\,\psi(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})}\,\theta(\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})=0,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{(Z,Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime})}\,\gamma(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{(Z,Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime})}\,\varphi(Z,\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}))=0,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{(Z,Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime})}\,\mu(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{(Z,Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime})}\,\psi(Z,\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}))=0,

where the summations are over the cyclic permutations of the indicated elements.

Proof.

Let us begin with the anti-symmetry of the bracket (
2.7
). We have

[\xi,\xi]=0

if and only if

\phi(\xi,\xi)=0,\qquad\theta(\xi,\xi)=0.

Similarly we obtain

[Z,Z]=0

if and only if

\gamma(Z,Z)=0,\qquad\mu(Z,Z)=0.

As such, the bracket (
2.7
) is anti-symmetric if and only if (
2.8
) holds.

As for the mixed Jacobi identities, let us first consider

(2.17)

[[\xi,\xi^{\prime}],Z]+[[\xi^{\prime},Z],\xi]+[[Z,\xi],\xi^{\prime}]=0,

where

\displaystyle[[\xi,\xi^{\prime}],Z]=[\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})+\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\,Z]=[\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),Z]+[\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),Z]=

\displaystyle\Big(-\varphi(Z,\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}))+\gamma(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),Z)\Big)+\Big(\mu(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),Z)-\psi(Z,\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}))\Big).

Similarly,

\displaystyle[[\xi^{\prime},Z],\xi]=[-\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime})-\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\,\xi]=-[\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi]-[\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi]=

\displaystyle=\Big(-\phi(\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi)-\varphi(\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi)\Big)+\Big(-\psi(\psi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi)-\theta(\varphi(Z,\xi^{\prime}),\xi)\Big),

and finally

\displaystyle[[Z,\xi],\xi^{\prime}]=[\varphi(Z,\xi)+\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime}]=[\varphi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime}]+[\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime}]=

\displaystyle=\Big(\phi(\varphi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\varphi(\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(\psi(\psi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\theta(\varphi(Z,\xi),\xi^{\prime})\Big).

Hence, (
2.17
) is satisfied if and only if (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) hold.

Let us next consider

(2.18)

[[Z,Z^{\prime}],\xi]+[[Z^{\prime},\xi],Z]+[[\xi,Z],Z^{\prime}]=0.

This time we have

\displaystyle[[Z,Z^{\prime}],\xi]=[\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime})+\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi]=[\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi]+[\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi]=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)+\varphi(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)\Big)+\Big(\psi(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)+\theta(\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),\xi)\Big)

while,

\displaystyle[[Z^{\prime},\xi],Z]=[\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi)+\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi),Z]=[\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi),Z]+[\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi),Z]=

\displaystyle\Big(-\varphi(Z,\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi))+\gamma(\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi),Z)\Big)+\Big(\mu(\psi(Z^{\prime},\xi),Z)-\psi(Z,\varphi(Z^{\prime},\xi))\Big),

and

\displaystyle[[\xi,Z],Z^{\prime}]=[-\varphi(Z,\xi)-\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime}]=-[\varphi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime}]-[\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime}]=

\displaystyle\Big(\varphi(Z^{\prime},\varphi(Z,\xi))-\gamma(\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(-\mu(\psi(Z,\xi),Z^{\prime})+\psi(Z^{\prime},\varphi(Z,\xi))\Big).

As such, (
2.18
) is satisfied if and only if (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) hold.

We next proceed to the Jacobi identity

(2.19)

[[\xi,\xi^{\prime}],\xi^{\prime\prime}]+[[\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}],\xi]+[[\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi],\xi^{\prime}]=0.

For the first summand we have,

\displaystyle[[\xi,\xi^{\prime}],\xi^{\prime\prime}]=[\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime})+\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime}]=[\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime}]+[\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime}]=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})+\varphi(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})\Big)+\Big(\psi(\theta(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})+\theta(\phi(\xi,\xi^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime\prime})\Big).

Similarly, we observe that

\displaystyle[[\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}],\xi]=[\phi(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})+\theta(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi]=[\phi(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi]+[\theta(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi]=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(\phi(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi)+\varphi(\theta(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi)\Big)+\Big(\psi(\theta(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi)+\theta(\phi(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime}),\xi)\Big).

Finally,

\displaystyle[[\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi],\xi^{\prime}]=[\phi(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi)+\theta(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime}]=[\phi(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime}]+[\theta(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime}]=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(\phi(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\varphi(\theta(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(\psi(\theta(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime})+\theta(\phi(\xi^{\prime\prime},\xi),\xi^{\prime})\Big).

Accordingly, (
2.19
) is satisfied if and only if (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) hold.

We are finally left with the Jacobi identity

(2.20)

[[Z,Z^{\prime}],Z^{\prime\prime}]+[[Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}],Z]+[[Z^{\prime\prime},Z],Z^{\prime}]=0.

Just as above, we have

\displaystyle[[Z,Z^{\prime}],Z^{\prime\prime}]=[\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime})+\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime}]=[\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime}]+[\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime}]=

\displaystyle\Big(-\varphi(Z^{\prime\prime},\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}))+\gamma(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime})\Big)+\Big(-\psi(Z^{\prime\prime},\gamma(Z,Z^{\prime}))+\mu(\mu(Z,Z^{\prime}),Z^{\prime\prime})\Big),

where similarly

\displaystyle[[Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}],Z]=[\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime})+\mu(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}),Z]=[\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}),Z]+[\mu(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}),Z]=

\displaystyle\Big(-\varphi(Z,\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}))+\gamma(\mu(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}),Z)\Big)+\Big(-\psi(Z,\gamma(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}))+\mu(\mu(Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}),Z)\Big),

and

\displaystyle[[Z^{\prime\prime},Z],Z^{\prime}]=[\gamma(Z^{\prime\prime},Z)+\mu(Z^{\prime\prime},Z),Z^{\prime}]=[\gamma(Z^{\prime\prime},Z),Z^{\prime}]+[\mu(Z^{\prime\prime},Z),Z^{\prime}]=

\displaystyle\Big(-\varphi(Z^{\prime},\gamma(Z^{\prime\prime},Z))+\gamma(\mu(Z^{\prime\prime},Z),Z^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(-\psi(Z^{\prime},\gamma(Z^{\prime\prime},Z))+\mu(\mu(Z^{\prime\prime},Z),Z^{\prime})\Big).

Accordingly, (
2.20

[z_{i},z_{j}]=(j-i)z_{i+j},\qquad i,j\geqslant-1.

Let also

\mathfrak{m}:=\langle z_{\ell}\mid\ell=4k,\text{ or }\ell=4k+1,\,k\geqslant 0\rangle,\qquad\mathfrak{h}:=\langle z_{\ell}\mid\ell=4k-1,\text{ or }\ell=4k+2,\,k\geqslant 0\rangle.

It is then evident that neither

, nor

. Nevertheless, they are both subspaces, and

. As such,

where the structure maps are given by

\displaystyle\phi:\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad\phi(z_{4k},z_{4t})=4(t-k)z_{4(k+t)},\quad\phi(z_{4k},z_{4t+1})=(4(t-k)+1)z_{4(k+t)+1},

\displaystyle\mu:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad\mu(z_{4k-1},z_{4t-1})=\begin{cases}4(t-k)z_{4(k+t-1)+2}&\text{ if }t+k\geqslant 1,\\
0&\text{ if }t=k=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\theta:\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad\theta(z_{4k+1},z_{4t+1})=4(t-k)z_{4(k+t)+2},

\displaystyle\gamma:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad\gamma(z_{4k-1},z_{4t+2})=(4(t-k)+3)z_{4(k+t)+1},\quad\gamma(z_{4k+2},z_{4t+2})=4(t-k)z_{4(k+t+1)},

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m},\qquad z_{4k-1}\triangleright z_{4t+1}=(4(t-k)+2)z_{4(t+k)},

\displaystyle\psi:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad\psi(z_{4k-1},z_{4t})=(4(t-k)+1)z_{4(t+k)-1},\quad\psi(z_{4k+2},z_{4t})=(4(t-k)-2)z_{4(t+k)+2},

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\psi(z_{4k+2},z_{4t+1})=(4(t-k)-1)z_{4(t+k+1)-1}.

2.2.
Bicocycle Double Cross Product Lie Groups

In the present subsection we shall develop a theory for the Lie groups of bicocycle double cross sum Lie algebras. In other words, we shall now introduce a construction that allows to construct a Lie group over two manifolds that are not necessarily subgroups. From the decomposition point of view, we shall present a theory that allows to decompose a Lie group into two submanifolds, none of which being necessarily a subgroup. The construction we present here generalizes a double cross product by two (twisted) 2-cocycles, and a unified product by one (twisted) 2-cocycle.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(M,H)
be a pair of two (pointed) manifolds; namely
(M,e)
and
(H,1)
, equipped with the (smooth) maps

(2.22)

\displaystyle\varphi:H\times M\to M,\qquad(h,x)\mapsto\varphi(h,x),

(2.23)

\displaystyle\psi:H\times M\to H,\qquad(h,x)\mapsto\psi(h,x),

(2.24)

\displaystyle\phi:M\times M\to M,\qquad(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto\phi(x,x^{\prime})=:x\cdot x^{\prime},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\theta:M\times M\to H,\qquad(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto\theta(x,x^{\prime}),,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\mu:H\times H\to H,\qquad(h,h^{\prime})\mapsto\mu(h,h^{\prime})=:h\ast h^{\prime}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\gamma:H\times H\to M,\qquad(h,h^{\prime})\mapsto\gamma(h,h^{\prime})

that satisfy

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varphi(1,x)=x,\qquad\varphi(h,e)=e,\\
&\psi(h,e)=h,\qquad\psi(1,x)=1,\\
&e\cdot e=e,\qquad 1\ast 1=1,\\
&\theta(e,e)=1,\qquad\gamma(1,1)=e.\end{split}

Then,
M\times H
is a Lie group with the multiplication

(2.29)

(x,h)(x^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\Big(x\cdot[\varphi(h,x^{\prime})\cdot\gamma(\psi(h,x^{\prime}),h^{\prime})],\,[\theta(x,\varphi(h,x^{\prime}))\ast\psi(h,x^{\prime})]\ast h^{\prime}\Big),

and the unit
(e,1)\in M\times H
if and only if

(2.30)

\displaystyle e\cdot x=x=x\cdot e,\qquad 1\ast h=h=h\ast 1,

(2.31)

\displaystyle\theta(x,e)=1=\theta(e,x),\qquad\gamma(h,1)=e=\gamma(1,h),

(2.32)

\displaystyle\varphi(h,x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime\prime})\cdot\gamma(\psi(h,x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime\prime}),\theta(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\ast h^{\prime\prime})=

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\varphi(h,x^{\prime})\cdot[\varphi(\psi(h,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime})\cdot\gamma(\psi(\psi(h,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime}),h^{\prime\prime})],

(2.33)

\displaystyle\psi(h,x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime\prime})\ast[\theta(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\ast h^{\prime\prime}]=[\theta(\varphi(h,x^{\prime}),\varphi(\psi(h,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime}))\ast\psi(\psi(h,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime})]\ast h^{\prime\prime},

(2.34)

\displaystyle\theta(x\cdot\gamma(h,h^{\prime}),\varphi(h\ast h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))\ast\psi(h\ast h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})=

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt[\theta(x,\varphi(h,\varphi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})))\ast\psi(h,\varphi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))]\ast\psi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}),

(2.35)

\displaystyle[x\cdot\gamma(h,h^{\prime})]\cdot\varphi(h\ast h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})=x\cdot[\varphi(h,\varphi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))\cdot\gamma(\psi(h,\varphi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})),\psi(h^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))],

(2.36)

\displaystyle x\cdot(x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime\prime})=(x\cdot x^{\prime})\cdot[\varphi(\theta(x,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime})\cdot\gamma(\psi(\theta(x,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime}),h^{\prime\prime})],

(2.37)

\displaystyle\theta(x,x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime\prime})\ast[\theta(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\ast h^{\prime\prime}]=[\theta(x\cdot x^{\prime},\varphi(\theta(x,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime}))\ast\psi(\theta(x,x^{\prime}),x^{\prime\prime})]\ast h^{\prime\prime},

(2.38)

\displaystyle[x\cdot\gamma(h,h^{\prime})]\cdot\gamma(h\ast h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})=x\cdot[\varphi(h,\gamma(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}))\cdot\gamma(\psi(h,\gamma(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})),h^{\prime}\ast h^{\prime\prime})],

(2.39)

\displaystyle(h\ast h^{\prime})\ast h^{\prime\prime}=[\theta(x,\varphi(h,\gamma(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})))\ast\psi(h,\gamma(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}))]\ast(h^{\prime}\ast h^{\prime\prime}),

for any
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in M
, and any
h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
, and furthermore

(2.40)

\displaystyle\text{for any }(x,h)\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{H}\text{ there is }(x^{r},h^{r})\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{H}\text{ such that }x\cdot x^{r}=e,\text{ and }h\ast h^{r}=1,

(2.41)

\displaystyle\text{for any }(x,h)\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{H}\text{ there is }(x^{\ell},h^{\ell})\in\mathfrak{M}\times\mathcal{H}\text{ such that }x^{\ell}\cdot x=e,\text{ and }h^{\ell}\ast h=1.

Proof.

Let us begin with the identity element. On one hand we have

(e,1)(x,1)=(x,1)=(x,1)(e,1)

for any

and

[\theta(e,x)\ast 1]\ast 1=1=[\theta(x,e)\ast 1]\ast 1.

On the other hand,

(e,h)(e,1)=\Big(e\cdot(e\cdot\gamma(h,1)),\,(1\ast h)\ast 1\Big)

Thus,

As a result, in view of the last line of (

) and (

Local Lie groups

Let us first recall from
[

[

if there are smooth maps

\mu:U\to M,\qquad i:V\to M,

where
U\subseteq M\times M
is an open subset such that
(\{e\}\times M)\cup(M\times\{e\})\subseteq U
, and
V\subseteq M
is also an open subset with
e\in V
, so that
V\times i(V)\subseteq U
, and that

, satisfying

(i)

\mu(e,x)=x=\mu(x,e)

x\in M
,

(ii)

\mu(i(y),y)=e=\mu(y,i(y))
, for any
y\in V
, and

(iii)

\mu(x,\mu(y,z))=\mu(\mu(x,y),z)
for any
(x,y),(y,z),(x,\mu(y,z)),(\mu(x,y),z)\in U

As was pointed out in
[

to be any neighbourhood of the identity. Then,

defines a local Lie group by restricting the group multiplication
m:G\times G\to G
to any open subset
U\subseteq M\times M
satisfying
(\{e\}\times M)\cup(M\times\{e\})\subseteq U\subseteq(M\times M)\cap\mu^{-1}(M)
, and the inversion
i:G\to G
to any open subset
V\subseteq M
subject to

Introduction

Given a graph
H
, we say that a graph
G
is
H
-saturated if it is maximally
H
-free, meaning
G
contains no copy of
H
but adding any new edge to
G
creates a copy of
H
. The saturation problem is to determine, or at least estimate, the saturation number
sat(n,H)
, defined (for graphs
H
with at least one edge) to be the minimum number of edges in an
H
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices. We are usually interested in fixed
H
and large
n
. The saturation problem is dual to the Turán forbidden subgraph problem of determining the extremal number
ex(n,H)
, defined to be the maximum number of edges in an
H
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices. However, much less is known for the saturation problem than for the Turán problem. See
[
1
]
for a survey of the saturation problem.

For certain
H
the saturation number is known exactly, however. In particular, Erdős, Hajnal and Moon showed (Theorem 1 in
[
2
]
) that for
r\geq 2
and
n\geq r-2
,
sat(n,K_{r})=(r-2)n-\binom{r-1}{2}
and that the unique extremal graph consists of a
K_{r-2}
fully connected to an independent set of size
n-(r-2)
(for
n<r
,
K_{n}
is the unique
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices). Note that for large
n
this graph contains many vertices of degree
r-2
. Moreover, this is the smallest possible degree of a vertex in a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n\geq r-1
vertices. One might therefore ask what happens if we forbid vertices of degree
r-2
, and more generally, vertices of small degree. This leads us to define
sat(n,K_{r},t)
for
r\geq 3
,
t\geq r-2
and large enough

to be the minimum number of edges in a
K_{r}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
(more precisely, such graphs exist if and only if

). This quantity (or rather the one obtained by replacing the condition
\delta(G)\geq t
with
\delta(G)=t
) was first considered by Duffus and Hanson (second paragraph of the Introduction in
[
3
]
).

Proving a conjecture of Bollobás, Day showed that for fixed

and

for large enough

is some constant depending on

and

. He raised the problem of estimating

and proved the bounds

2^{t}t^{3/2}\ll_{r}c(r,t)\leq t^{t^{2t^{2}}}

for fixed

and large

(Theorem 1.2 and the previous two paragraphs in
[
4
]
)(see section

for precise definitions of asymptotic notation). In this paper we show that for fixed

and large

is given by

. Moreover, we investigate the dependence on

.

Theorem 1
.

For all integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r-2
,

\frac{4^{t-r}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ll c(r,t)\ll\frac{4^{t-r}\min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ .

Note that the lower and upper bounds are the same up to the
\min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}
factor. We conjecture that the lower bound is tight. The

terms cannot be absorbed into the other terms, because when

is not much larger than
r-2
, the

terms dominate. Indeed, for such

1
gives

.

We further characterise the extremal graphs for large

, and more generally,
K_{r}
-saturated graphs

n
vertices with

not much larger than

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\geq 3
,

and

(Blowing up a vertex refers to replacing it by several copies - see section

Using ideas similar to those in the proof of Theorem

-saturated graphs. Pikhurko showed that for fixed

-saturated graph has only a bounded number of vertices of degree at most

The problems of estimating

and

Theorem 6

Let

be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set

1.

|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all

2.

|A_{i}\cap B_{i}|\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

|A_{i}\cap B_{j}|>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

Then
|I|\ll\binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}
.

As we shall see later, Theorem

13

is that it only requires the bound

Notation

In this section we establish some graph theory and asymptotic notation and state some order theory definitions.

2.1

Graph theory notation

Given a graph

,

,

the subgraph of

and

. Given a subset

for the number of edges between

and

in

. Given an integer

for the graph obtained by adding
s
conical vertices to

(see section
5

2.2

Asymptotic notation

Given two real valued functions
f
and
g
of several variables, we write
f\ll g
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f\leq Cg
for all possible values of the variables. We write

if

, and

if
f\ll g
and
f\gg g
.

denotes a function

such that

, and

denotes a function

such that

. Given a variable, say

for any fixed

. Similarly, given other variables, say

, etc.

2.3

Order theory definitions

Given an order

on a set
S
, the dual order

on

is given by

if and only if

. Given an order

on a set

and a subset

, the induced order

on

is given by

if and only if

. Given orders

, the sum of the orders is the order

on

given by

and

or

and

for some

.

Blow-ups

In this section we first define blow-ups and observe that the operation of blowing up preserves both the properties of being
K_{r}
-saturated (with some trivial exceptions) and of having large minimum degree. We then explain how Day proved that for fixed
r
and
t
,
sat(n,K_{r},t)=tn-c(r,t)
for large enough
n
, which leads to an alternative definition of
c(r,t)
. We define a similar quantity
c^{\prime}(r,t)
and pose a question about how these two quantitites are related. Finally, we define collections of graphs
C(r,t,k)
and
C^{\prime}(r,t,k)
, show that
C^{\prime}(r,t,k)
is always finite and observe that Theorem
2
is equivalent to the statement that
C(r,t,k)
is always finite.

A blow-up of a graph
H
is a graph
G
obtained by replacing each vertex of
H
with some positive number of copies. Two copies in
G
are adjacent if and only if the vertices in
H
they are copies of are adjacent (in particular copies of the same vertex are non-adjacent). It is easy to check that
G
is
K_{r}
-saturated if and only if
H
is
K_{r}
-saturated, unless
H
is a clique on at most
r-2
vertices and
G
is a proper blow-up of
H
. Note that
\delta(G)\geq\delta(H)
.

Hence, given integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r-2
and a
K_{r}
-saturated graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq t
, we can generate other
K_{r}
-saturated graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq t
by taking blow-ups of
H
(the conditions
\delta(H)\geq t\geq r-2
rule out the trivial exceptions). To minimise the number of edges in
G
given the number of vertices, one should blow up non-adjacent vertices in
H
of minimum degree. We then have
e(G)-\delta(H)|G|=e(H)-\delta(H)|H|
. In particular, if
\delta(H)=t
,
e(G)-t|G|=e(H)-t|H|
.

Day proved that for fixed
r
and
t
,
sat(n,K_{r},t)=tn-c(r,t)
for large enough
n
as follows. He first proved (and this is where the difficulty lies) that for fixed
r
and
t
the quantity
t|G|-e(G)
is bounded above when
G
ranges over all
K_{r}
-saturated graphs with
\delta(G)\geq t
(Theorem 1.1 in
[
4
]
). We can thus define

c(r,t)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\ K_{r}\text{-sat.}\\
\delta(G)=t\end{subarray}}t|G|-e(G)\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ c^{\prime}(r,t)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\ K_{r}\text{-sat.}\\
\delta(G)\geq t\end{subarray}}t|G|-e(G)\ .

(It is easy to check that for all integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r-2
,
K_{r}
-saturated graphs
G
with
\delta(G)=t
do exist.)

Take a
K_{r}
-saturated graph
H
with
\delta(H)=t
and
t|H|-e(H)=c(r,t)
. Then by blowing up non-adjacent vertices in
H
of degree
t
, we can construct
K_{r}
-saturated graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq t
(in fact with equality),

and
e(G)=tn-c(r,t)
for all

, so
sat(n,K_{r},t)\leq tn-c(r,t)
for large enough

. Now suppose
G
is a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
. If
\delta(G)=t
,
e(G)\geq tn-c(r,t)
, whereas if
\delta(G)>t
,
e(G)\geq(t+1)n-c^{\prime}(r,t+1)
. But
(t+1)n-c^{\prime}(r,t+1)>tn-c(r,t)
for large enough
n
, so

if

is large enough. Hence
sat(n,K_{r},t)\geq tn-c(r,t)
for large enough

, so
sat(n,K_{r},t)=tn-c(r,t)
for large enough

.

Clearly
c^{\prime}(r,t)\geq c(r,t)
. It is unclear whether we have equality.

Question 1

Do we have

We now define collections of graphs

Conical vertices

In this section we first define conical vertices and note that the operation of adding a conical vertex interacts well with the property of being
K_{r}
-saturated. This fact allows us to obtain some inequalities for
c(r,t)
and

A conical vertex is a vertex adjacent to all other vertices. It is easy to check that for every graph

is

-saturated if and only if
G
is
K_{r}
-saturated. We thus obtain the following two lemmas, which we will use later.

Lemma 1
.

For all integers
r\geq 3
,
t\geq r-2
and
s\geq 0
,

c(r+s,t+s)\geq c(r,t)+ts+\binom{s+1}{2}\ .

Lemma 2
.

For all integers
r\geq 3
,
t\geq r-2
and
s\geq 0
,

f(r+s,t+s)\geq f(r,t)\ .

Day speculated that perhaps for all fixed
r\geq 4
and
t\geq r-2
and large enough
n
, all extremal graphs for
sat(n,K_{r},t)
have a conical vertex (last paragraph in
[
4
]
). This is equivalent to the statement that every graph in
C(r,t)
has a conical vertex for
r\geq 4
. The author believes this to be true.

Conjecture 1
.

For all integers
r\geq 4
and
t\geq r-2
, every graph in
C(r,t)
has a conical vertex.

There is some evidence for Conjecture
1
. Hajnal showed that every
K_{r}
-saturated graph
G
with
\delta(G)<2(r-2)
has a conical vertex (Theorem 1 in
[
6
]
). Hence Conjecture
1
is true for
t<2(r-2)
. Moreover, Alon, Erdös, Holzman and Krivelevich showed that Conjecture
1
is true when
r=4=t
, the first case not covered by Hajnal’s result (Theorem 8 and Corollary 3 in
[
7
]
).

An equivalent way of stating Conjecture
1
is that for all integers
r\geq 4
and
t\geq r-2
,
C(r,t)
is the collection of graphs that can be obtained by adding a conical vertex to a graph in
C(r-1,t-1)
. This would imply that we have equality in Lemma
1
, or equivalently that

c(r,t)=c(3,t-(r-3))+(r-3)t-\binom{r-3}{2}

for all integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r-2
.

By Theorem
1
,
c(3,t)\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
, so Conjecture
1
would imply

c(r,t)\asymp\frac{4^{t-r}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ ,

or in other words, that the lower bound in Theorem
1
is tight. (Indeed, we will prove the lower bound in Theorem
1
by first using the graph
G_{t}
from Section
3
to show
c(3,t)\gg 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
and then using Lemma
1
.) So the fact that the upper bound in Theorem
1
comes close to showing the lower bound is tight is further evidence for Conjecture
1
.

Main results

In this section we prove Theorems
1
through
5
. We first state some results that we will need in the proofs. To state the first result, we need the following definition.

Definition 1
.

For integers
a,b\geq 0
, an
(a,b)

set system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for all
i<j\in I
.

The first result is the following known version of the Two Families Theorem (see
[
8
]
).

Theorem 7
(Skew Two Families Theorem, Frankl, 1982).

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b)
set system. Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
.

To state the next result, we need the following definition.

Definition 2
.

For integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, an
(a,b,c)

modified set system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

|A_{i}\cap B_{i}|\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

|A_{i}\cap B_{j}|>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

The result consists of Theorem
6
and two further results covering the remaining, degenerate cases
b>c\geq a
and
b\leq c
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
a,b,c\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b,c)
modified set system. Then in each of the following cases we have the following bounds for
|I|
.

1.

If
a,b>c
,

|I|\ll\binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}\ .

2.

If
b>c\geq a
,

|I|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{c-a+1}\right\rfloor+1\ .

3.

If
b\leq c
,

|I|\leq 1\ .

We will prove Theorem
8
in section
7
.

A key step in the proofs of Theorems
1
and
3
is defining a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite sets with properties similar to those in Definition
2
, but with cardinality replaced by clique number in properties 2 and 3. The following graph theory lemma will allow us to replace these conditions by those in Definition
2
, after which we can apply Theorem
8
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then there is a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
|S|-w(S)=|G|-w(G)
and
|S|\geq 2w(S)
.

Proof.

Consider the subsets
S\subseteq V(G)
satisfying
|S|-w(S)=|G|-w(G)
. Such sets do exist since we can take
S=V(G)
. Take a minimal such
S
. Then the intersection of the maximum cliques of
G[S]
must be empty, for if there was a vertex
v
in every maximum clique of
G[S]
, we would have
|S\setminus\{v\}|-w(S\setminus\{v\})=|G|-w(G)
, contradicting the minimality of
S
. Hajnal showed (the lemma in
[
6
]
) that for any graph
H
and non-empty collection
C
of maximum cliques in
H
, we have
|\cup_{T\in C}T|+|\cap_{T\in C}T|\geq 2w(H)
. In particular, for any graph
H
we have
u(H)+i(H)\geq 2w(H)
, where
u(H)
and
i(H)
are the size of the union and intersection of all the maximum cliques of
H
, respectively. Hence, if
i(H)=0
, we have
|H|\geq u(H)\geq 2w(H)
. So
|S|\geq 2w(S)
.

∎

We will deduce Theorems

Lemma 4

For all

Since for every integer

If

, so suppose

. Consider all sequences
(v_{i})_{i\in I}
of vertices in

(a)

For all

\ |\Gamma(v_{i})\cap\cup_{k<i}\ \Gamma(v_{k})|+\frac{1}{2}\ |\Gamma(v_{i})\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ \Gamma(v_{k})|\leq t\ .

(b)

For all
i\in I

.

For each
i\in I

|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|+\frac{1}{2}\ |A_{i}\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\leq t

(1)

for all
i\in I

|B_{i}|\leq t

(2)

for all

.

We now show that

2

w(A_{i}\cap B_{i})\leq r-3

for all

are non-adjacent and

Let us now show property 1 holds. Since this only requires a qualitative bound, we can afford to be less careful when bounding quantities. We have
S=\cup_{i\in I}\ A_{i}
. By (

Let us now show property 2 holds. Since this requires a quantitative bound, we will need to be more careful when bounding quantities. We have

|S|=|\cup_{i\in I}A_{i}|=\sum_{i\in I}|A_{i}\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|

e(S)=e(\cup_{i\in I}\ A_{i})\geq\sum_{i\in I}e\left(\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k},\ A_{i}\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}\right)\geq\sum_{i\in I}|A_{i}\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\ |B_{i}|

t|S|-e(S)\leq\sum_{i\in I}|A_{i}\setminus\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\ (t-|B_{i}|)\leq 2\sum_{i\in I}(t-|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|)\ (t-|B_{i}|)

(5)

Our aim now is to show

\sum_{i\in I}(t-|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|)\ (t-|B_{i}|)\ll\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ ,

(7)

for all

for all

(13)

Note that for

Theorem
1
.

For all integers

and

\frac{4^{t-r}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ll c(r,t)\leq c^{\prime}(r,t)\ll\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ .

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound. By Lemma
1
, it is sufficient to prove the special case
r=3
, which states that
c(3,t)\gg 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
for all integers
t\geq 1
. By properties 1 and 4 in section

, for all integers

,
G_{t}
is
K_{3}
-saturated and
\delta(G_{t})=t

c(3,t)\geq t|G_{t}|-e(G_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(t-1)}{t-1}+2t(t-1)\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}

for all integers

c(3,1),c(3,2)>0
(in fact, as we shall see in section

and
c(3,2)=5
), so
c(3,t)\gg 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
for all integers
t\geq 1
.

We now prove the upper bound. Let
G
be a
K_{r}
-saturated graph with
\delta(G)\geq t
. We need to show that

e(G)\geq t|G|-O\left(\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\ \right)\ .

By Lemma
4
, there exists a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
with the properties stated in the lemma. Let
T=S\cup\{v\in V(G):\ |\Gamma(v)\cap S|>t\}
. Then

e(T)\geq e(S)+e(S,T\setminus S)

\geq t|S|-O\left(\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\right)+t|T\setminus S|=t|T|-O\left(\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\right)

by property 2 in the lemma and the definition of

.

We now claim that for all

we have

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\geq t\ .

Indeed, by property 3 in the lemma, either
|\Gamma(w)\cap S|>t
for all
w\in\Gamma(v)\setminus S
or

|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ .

In the first case we have
\Gamma(v)\subseteq T
, so

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|=d(v)\geq t\ ,

since
\delta(G)\geq t
. In the second case we have

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\geq|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ ,

since
S\subseteq T
.

Hence

e(G)=e(T)+\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus T}\left(|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\right)

\geq t|T|-O\left(\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\right)+t|V(G)\setminus T|=t|G|-O\left(\frac{4^{t-r}\ \min{(r,\sqrt{t-r+3})}}{\sqrt{t-r+3}}+r^{2}\right)\ .

∎

We now deduce Theorem

, in its equivalent form, from Lemma

Theorem
2
.

For all integers
r\geq 3
,
t\geq r-2
and
k
,
C(r,t,k)
is finite.

Proof.

Let
G\in C(r,t,k)
. We need to show that
|G|=O_{r,t,k}(1)
. By Lemma
4
, there is a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
with the properties stated in the lemma. Let
T=S\cup\{v\in V(G):\ |\Gamma(v)\cap S|>t\}
as before. Then

e(T)\geq e(S)+e(S,T\setminus S)

\geq t|S|-O_{r,t}(1)+(t+1)|T\setminus S|=t|T|-O_{r,t}(1)+|T\setminus S|

by property 2 in the lemma and the definition of
T
.

We now claim that for all
v\in V(G)
we have

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\geq t\ ,

with equality if and only if
\Gamma(v)\subseteq T
and
d(v)=t
. Indeed, by property 3 in the lemma, either
|\Gamma(w)\cap S|>t
for all
w\in\Gamma(v)\setminus S
or

|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ .

In the first case we have
\Gamma(v)\subseteq T
and

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|=d(v)\geq t\ ,

as before. In the second case we have

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\geq|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ ,

as before.

Let
R=\{v\in V(G):\ \Gamma(v)\subseteq T,d(v)=t\}
. We then have

t|G|+k\ =\ e(G)\ =\ e(T)+\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus T}\left(|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\right)

\geq\ t|T|-O_{r,t}(1)+|T\setminus S|+t|V(G)\setminus T|+\frac{1}{2}|V(G)\setminus(T\cup R)|\ =\ t|G|-O_{r,t}(1)+|T\setminus S|+\frac{1}{2}|V(G)\setminus(T\cup R)|\ .

Hence
|T\setminus S|,\ |V(G)\setminus(T\cup R)|=O_{r,t,k}(1)
. We also have
|S|=O_{r,t}(1)
by property 1 in the lemma. Combining these, we obtain
|V(G)\setminus(R\setminus T)|=O_{r,t,k}(1)
.

Note that
R\setminus T
is an independent set of vertices of degree
t
. We now claim that for every subset
N\subseteq V(G)\setminus(R\setminus T)
there are at most
t
vertices
v\in R\setminus T
with
\Gamma(v)=N
. We then have
|R\setminus T|=O_{r,t,k}(1)
and hence
|G|=O_{r,t,k}(1)
, completing the proof. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there is a subset
N\subseteq V(G)\setminus(R\setminus T)
such that
|U|>t
, where
U=\{v\in R\setminus T:\Gamma(v)=N\}
.

Pick a subset
W\subset U
with
|W|=t
and let
H=G[V(G)\setminus(U\setminus W)]
. Note that
H
is smaller than
G
and that
G
can be obtained from
H
by blowing up vertices in
W
, which are non-adjacent and of degree
t
. Hence
H
is
K_{r}
-saturated. The only vertices with smaller degree in
H
than in
G
are those in
N
. But these vertices are adjacent in
H
to all vertices in
W
, so
\delta(H)=t
. Since
U\setminus W
is an independent set of vertices of degree
t
,
e(H)=t|H|+k
. This contradicts the minimality of
G
.

∎

Theorem

For all integers

We first prove the lower bound. We need to show that

K_{r-1}

We now prove the upper bound. Let

-saturated graph. We need to bound the number of vertices in

Theorem
4
.

Every large
K_{r}
-saturated graph (

) with

.

Let

-saturated graph with

.

has size
\Omega(\min{(e/\log e,n)})
. The broad idea of the proof is as follows. Note that by properties 1, 2, 3 and 5 in section
3
,
G_{t}
is
K_{3}
-saturated,
|G_{t}|\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
,
e(G_{t})\asymp 4^{t}t^{1/2}
and every vertex cover in
G_{t}
has size at least
\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(t-1)}{t-1}\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
, respectively. Hence
G_{t}
can be used to prove the result when
e/\log e\asymp n
. To extend this to general
n
and
e
, we will modify
G_{t}
depending on whether
e/\log e\ll n
or
e/\log e\gg n
.

We first consider the case
e/\log e\ll n
. We are given large quantities
n
and
e
with
e/\log e\ll n\ll e
and need to construct a
K_{3}
-saturated graph
G
with
|G|\asymp n
and
e(G)\asymp e
such that every vertex cover in
G
has size
\Omega(e/\log e)
. The key to doing this is the fact that
G_{t}
has bounded chromatic number.

Pick an integer
t\geq 3
such that
4^{t}t^{1/2}\asymp e
. Note that
e/\log e\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
. By property 6 in section
3
, there exist three maximally independent subsets of
V(G_{t})
that cover it. Let
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G_{t}
as follows. Add three new non-adjacent vertices, one for each maximally independent set, and join each new vertex to all vertices in its maximally independent set. Then add one final vertex and join it to the three new vertices.

It is easy to check that
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
has the properties of
G_{t}
stated previously -
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
is
K_{3}
-saturated,
|G_{t}^{\prime\prime}|\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
,
e(G_{t}^{\prime\prime})\asymp 4^{t}t^{1/2}
and every vertex cover in
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
has size at least
\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(t-1)}{t-1}\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}
- and the additional property of having a vertex of degree three. Pick an integer
N\geq 0
such that
N\asymp n
. Let
G
be the graph obtained from
G_{t}^{\prime\prime}
by blowing up the vertex of degree three by
N+1
. Then
G
is
K_{3}
-saturated and every vertex cover in
G
has size
\Omega(e/\log e)
. Note that

|G_{t}^{\prime\prime}|\ \asymp\ 4^{t}t^{-1/2}\ \asymp\ e/\log e\ \ll\ n\ \asymp\ N\ \ll\ e\ \asymp\ 4^{t}t^{1/2}\ \asymp\ e(G_{t}^{\prime\prime})\ .

Hence
|G|=|G_{t}^{\prime\prime}|+N\asymp n
and
e(G)=e(G_{t}^{\prime\prime})+3N\asymp e
.

We now consider the case
e/\log e\gg n
. We are given large quantities
n
and
e
with
n\log n\ll e\ll n^{2}
and need to construct a
K_{3}
-saturated graph
G
with
|G|\asymp n
and
e(G)\asymp e
such that every vertex cover in
G
has size
\Omega(n)
. Pick an integer
t\geq 3
such that
t\asymp e/n
and
4^{t}t^{-1/2}\gg n
. This is possible since
e/n\gg\log n
. Note that
t\ll n
. Let
G_{t}^{\prime}
be as in property 7 in section
3
.

Pick an integer
N
with
0\leq N\leq\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(t-1)}{t-1}
and
N\asymp n
. This is possible since
\frac{1}{2}\binom{2(t-1)}{t-1}\asymp 4^{t}t^{-1/2}\gg n
. Let
G
be a graph obtained from
G_{t}^{\prime}
by keeping
N
of the pairs of matching vertices in
R
and removing the rest. Then
G
is
K_{3}
-saturated by property 7 in section
3
and contains a matching of size
N
, so every vertex cover in
G
has size
\Omega(n)
. We have
|G|=2N+(2t-1)\asymp n
and
e(G)=(2t-1)N+2(t-1)\asymp e
.

∎

7

The Two Families Theorem

In this section we prove Theorem
8
, Lemma
5
and further related results. We first establish some linear algebra notation in section
7.1
. Next, we put our results into context in section
7.2
. In section
7.3
we introduce some definitions necessary for stating and proving these results, which we then prove in section
7.4
.

7.1

Linear algebra notation

Let
F
be an
infinite
field. All our vector spaces will be over
F
and finite dimensional. Given such a vector space
V
, we write
\text{dim}(V)
for the dimension of
V
. We denote by
0
the zero subspace of
V
. Given subspaces
V_{i}
of
V
, indexed by some finite set
I
, we write
\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}:v_{i}\in V_{i}\text{ for all }i\right\}
for the smallest subspace of
V
contatining all the
V_{i}
. We say that the sum
\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}
is a direct sum if each of its elements can be written
uniquely
as a sum of
v_{i}\in V_{i}
. Given a subset
S
of
V
, we denote by
\text{span}(S)
the linear span of
S
, the smallest subspace of
V
containing
S
, consisting of all linear combinations of elements of
S
.

7.2

Context

The Two Families Theorem is a celebrated result in extremal set theory. It was first stated and proved by Bollobás in 1965 (see the lemma in
[
9
]
for the original version of the theorem, which is a weighted generalisation of Theorem
9
below; see also
[
10
]
for an alternative, elegant proof of Theorem
9
by Katona). Since then it has been generalised in several different ways and found numerous applications (see
[
11
]
through
[
22
]
). The simplest version of the Two Families Theorem is as follows.

Theorem 9
(Two Families Theorem, Bollobás, 1965).

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a collection of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\neq j\in I
.

Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
.

One can see that Theorem
9
is tight by taking
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
to be the collection of all partitions of a set of size
a+b
into subsets
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
of size
a
and
b
, respectively. Moreover, this is the unique way of achieving equality. An interesting feature of this theorem is that the bound does not depend on the size of the ground set. This is ultimately the reason
c(r,t)
and
f(r,t)
are constants independent of
n
.

We now recall Theorem
7
, which is a generalisation of Theorem
9
allowing one to relax condition 3.

Theorem
7
(Skew Two Families Theorem, Frankl, 1982).

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for all
i<j\in I
.

Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
.

In this version of the theorem there are many ways of achieving equality. There is no known combinatorial proof of the Skew Two Families Theorem - all known proofs use linear algebra in some form. One can deduce Theorem
7
from the following vector space analogue (see Theorem 4.9 in
[
23
]
).

Theorem 10
(Vector Space Two Families Theorem, Lovász, 1977).

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of pairs of finite dimensional vector spaces over
F
, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

dim(A_{i})\leq a
and
dim(B_{i})\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq 0
for all
i<j\in I
.

Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
.

Theorem
10
is proved using exterior algebra. We now explain how to deduce Theorem
7
from Theorem
10
, since the same construction will be useful for us later. Suppose
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
7
and has ground set
S
. Let
V
be a vector space over
F
with a basis consisting of vectors
e_{s}
indexed by
s\in S
. Let
A^{\prime}_{i}=\text{span}(\{e_{a}:a\in A_{i}\})
and
B^{\prime}_{i}=\text{span}(\{e_{b}:b\in B_{i}\})
for each
i\in I
. Then
(A^{\prime}_{i},B^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
10
, so
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
. We will refer to this construction as
the vector space construction
.

Theorem
6
is similar to the following generalisation of Theorem
7
(see
[
24
]
), but with the bound
|A_{i}|\leq a
replaced by the weaker bound
|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\leq a
.

Theorem 11
(Threshold Two Families Theorem, Füredi, 1984).

Let
a,b\geq c\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

|A_{i}\cap B_{i}|\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

|A_{i}\cap B_{j}|>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b-2c}{a-c}
.

Taking
c=0
gives Theorem
7
. One can see that Theorem
11
is tight as follows. Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be the collection of all partitions of a set
S
of size
a+b-2c
into subsets
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
of size
a-c
and
b-c
, respectively. Order
I
arbitrarily and let
T
be a set of size
c
disjoint from
S
. Now consider the the sequence
(A^{\prime}_{i},B^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
A^{\prime}_{i}=A_{i}\cup T
and
B^{\prime}_{i}=B_{i}\cup T
for each
i\in I
.

Füredi deduced Theorem
11
from the following vector space analogue using the vector space construction (see
[
24
]
).

Theorem 12
(Vector Space Threshold Two Families Theorem, Füredi, 1984).

Let
a,b\geq c\geq 0
be integers and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of pairs of finite dimensional vector spaces over
F
, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

dim(A_{i})\leq a
and
dim(B_{i})\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

dim(A_{i}\cap B_{i})\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

dim(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

Then
|I|\leq\binom{a+b-2c}{a-c}
.

Note that taking
c=0
gives Theorem
10
. Füredi proved Theorem
12
by reducing it to Theorem
10
. Since the same argument will be useful later, we explain how this reduction works here. We first need a linear algebra fact. For any two subspaces
U
and
W
of a finite dimensional vector space
V
we have
\text{dim}(U\cap W)\geq\max{(\text{dim}(U)+\text{dim}(W)-\text{dim}(V),0)}
. We say that
U
and
W
are in
general position
if we have equality. The fact we will use is the following. Suppose we have a
finite
collection of subspaces of a finite dimensional vector space
V
over an
infinite
field. Then for every integer
0\leq d\leq\text{dim}(V)
, there exists a subspace
W
of
V
with
\text{dim}(W)=d
that is in general position with all subspaces in the collection.

Now suppose
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
12
. Let
V
be an ambient vector space containing all the
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
. We may assume that
V
is finite dimensional and that
\text{dim}(V)\geq c
. Pick a subspace
W
of
V
with
\text{dim}(W)=\text{dim}(V)-c
that is in general position with
A_{i}
,
B_{i}
and
A_{i}\cap B_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Let
A^{\prime}_{i}=A_{i}\cap W
and
B^{\prime}_{i}=B_{i}\cap W
for each
i\in I
.
Then
(A^{\prime}_{i},B^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
10
, with
a
and
b
replaced by
a-c
and
b-c
, respectively, so
|I|\leq\binom{a+b-2c}{a-c}
. We will refer to this argument as
Füredi’s reduction argument
.

7.3

Definitions

In this section we introduce some definitions necessary for stating and proving our results. We first recall Definitions
1
and
2
.

Definition
1
.

For integers
a,b\geq 0
, an
(a,b)

set system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for all
i<j\in I
.

Definition
2
.

For integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, an
(a,b,c)

modified set system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite sets, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

|A_{i}\cap\cup_{k<i}\ A_{k}|\leq a
and
|B_{i}|\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

|A_{i}\cap B_{i}|\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

|A_{i}\cap B_{j}|>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

We will also need the vector space analogues of these definitions.

Definition 4
.

For integers
a,b\geq 0
, an
(a,b)

vector space system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite dimensional vector spaces over
F
, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

\text{dim}(A_{i})\leq a
and
\text{dim}(B_{i})\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
.

3.

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq 0
for all
i<j\in I
.

Note that these are the conditions in Theorem
10
.

Definition 5
.

For integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, an
(a,b,c)

modified vector space system
is a sequence
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairs of finite dimensional vector spaces over
F
, indexed by a finite, ordered set
I
, with the following properties.

1.

\text{dim}(A_{i}\cap\sum_{k<i}A_{k})\leq a
and
\text{dim}(B_{i})\leq b
for all
i\in I
.

2.

\text{dim}(A_{i}\cap B_{i})\leq c
for all
i\in I
.

3.

\text{dim}(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>c
for all
i<j\in I
.

We now name the maximum possible size of the index set in a modified set system.

Definition 6
.

For all integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, let
i_{s}(a,b,c)
be the maximum of
|I|
over all
(a,b,c)
modified set systems.

We will also need the vector space analogue of this definition.

Definition 7
.

For all integers
a,b,c\geq 0
, let
i_{v}(a,b,c)
be the maximum of
|I|
over all
(a,b,c)
modified vector space systems.

We will see in section
7.4
that these maxima do exist, i.e. that
|I|
is bounded for both
(a,b,c)
modified set systems and
(a,b,c)
modified vector space systems.

To prove our results, we will need to also consider the following maximum.

Definition 8
.

For all integers
a,b\geq 0
, let
u_{s}(a,b)
be the maximum of
|\cup_{i\in I}B_{i}|
over all
(a,b)
set systems.

We will also need the vector space analogue of this definition.

Definition 9
.

For all integers
a,b\geq 0
, let
u_{v}(a,b)
be the maximum of
\text{dim}\left(\sum_{i\in I}B_{i}\right)
over all
(a,b)
vector space systems.

It is clear that these maxima do exist, i.e. that
|\cup_{i\in I}B_{i}|
and
\text{dim}(\sum_{i\in I}B_{i})
are bounded, since Theorems
7
and
10
give
|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}
and condition 1 in Definitions
1
and
4
gives
|B_{i}|\leq b
and
\text{dim}(B_{i})\leq b
for
(a,b)
set and vector space systems, respectively, so
u_{s}(a,b),u_{v}(a,b)\leq b\ \binom{a+b}{a}
. We will obtain better bounds, however.

We can now state our results.

Theorem 13
.

We have the following estimates for
i_{s}
,
i_{v}
,
u_{s}
and
u_{v}
.

1.

For all integers
a,b\geq c\geq 0
,

i_{s}(a,b,c),\ i_{v}(a,b,c)\ \asymp\ \binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}\ .

2.

For all integers
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 1
,

u_{s}(a,b),\ u_{v}(a,b)\ \asymp\ \binom{a+b+1}{a+1}\ .

3.

For all integers
b,c\geq a\geq 0
,

i_{s}(a,b,c)\ =\ i_{v}(a,b,c)\ =\ \left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{c-a+1}\right\rfloor+1\ .

4.

For all integers
a\geq 0
and
c\geq b\geq 0
,
i_{s}(a,b,c)=i_{v}(a,b,c)=1
.

5.

For all integers
a\geq 0
,
u_{s}(a,0)=u_{v}(a,0)=0
.

Note that Theorem
13
implies Theorem
8
and moreover that Theorem
8
is tight.

7.4

Proofs

In this section we prove Theorem
13
and Lemma
5
. We first prove some preliminary lemmas. Using the vector space construction, one obtains the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

For all integers
a,b,c\geq 0
,
i_{s}(a,b,c)\leq i_{v}(a,b,c)
. For all integers
a,b\geq 0
,
u_{s}(a,b)\leq u_{v}(a,b)
.

By Lemma
6
, to prove parts 1, 2 and 3 of Theorem
13
, it will be sufficient to prove lower bounds for
i_{s}(a,b,c)
and
u_{s}(a,b)
and upper bounds for
i_{v}(a,b,c)
and
u_{v}(a,b)
.

Recall that we obtained an extremal construction for Theorem
11
from the extremal construction for Theorem
9
by adjoining
c
new elements to all the sets. Similarly, adjoining
d
new elements to all the sets gives
i_{s}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\geq i_{s}(a,b,c)
and the vector space analogue of this argument gives
i_{v}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\geq i_{v}(a,b,c)
. Moreover, Füredi’s reduction argument shows that the second inequality is in fact an equality.

Lemma 7
.

For all integers
a,b,c,d\geq 0
,
i_{s}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\geq i_{s}(a,b,c)
and
i_{v}(a+d,b+d,c+d)=i_{v}(a,b,c)
.

Proof.

Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b,c)
modified set or vector space system with ground set
S
or ambient vector space
V
, respectively. Let
T
or
W
be a set or vector space over
F
of size or dimension
d
disjoint from
S
or intersecting
V
in the zero subspace, respectively. For each
i\in I
, let
A^{\prime}_{i}
be
A_{i}\cup T
or
A_{i}+W
and
B^{\prime}_{i}
be
B_{i}\cup T
or
B_{i}+W
, respectively. Then
(A^{\prime}_{i},B^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}
is an
(a+d,b+d,c+d)
modified set or vector space system, respectively, so
i_{s}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\geq i_{s}(a,b,c)
and
i_{v}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\geq i_{v}(a,b,c)
.

It remains to show that
i_{v}(a+d,b+d,c+d)\leq i_{v}(a,b,c)
. Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a+d,b+d,c+d)
modified vector space system with ambient vector space
V
. We may assume that
V
is finite dimensional and that
\text{dim}(V)\geq d
. Pick a subspace
W
of
V
with
\text{dim}(W)=\text{dim}(V)-d
that is in general position with
A_{i}\cap\sum_{k<i}A_{k}
,
B_{i}
and
A_{i}\cap B_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Let
A^{\prime}_{i}=A_{i}\cap W
and
B^{\prime}_{i}=B_{i}\cap W
for each
i\in I
. Then
(A^{\prime}_{i},B^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}
is an
(a,b,c)
modified vector space system, so the inequality follows.

∎

Lemma
7
will allow us to only consider
i_{s}(a,b,0)
and
i_{v}(a,b,0)
when proving part 1 of Theorem
13
and
i_{s}(0,b,c)
and
i_{v}(0,b,c)
when proving part 3 of Theorem
13
.

We now prove two lemmas. The first, Lemma
8
, gives an upper bound for
i_{v}(a,b,0)
in terms of
u_{v}(a,b)
. The second, Lemma
9
, gives an upper bound for
u_{v}(a,b)
in terms of the values of
i_{v}(c,a,0)
for integers

. Combining these two will allow us to prove upper bounds for both

and
u_{v}(a,b)
by induction. This is the reason for introducing
u_{v}(a,b)
in the first place.

The main idea in the proof of Lemma
8
is that, given an
(a,b,0)
modified vector space system, we would like to replace each
A_{i}
by
A_{i}\cap\sum_{k<i}A_{k}
, since condition 1 in Definition
5
gives an upper bound for
\text{dim}\left(A_{i}\cap\sum_{k<i}A_{k}\right)
rather than
\text{dim}(A_{i})
, and then apply Theorem
10
. However, this makes the
A_{i}
smaller, so while condition 2 in Definition
5
will still hold, condition 3 might not. The solution is to find a subset
J\subseteq I
in which condition 3 does still hold that is maximal with respect to this property in a certain way. We can then bound
|J|
using Theorem
10
and
|I\setminus J|
using the maximality of

.

Lemma 8
.

For all integers
a,b\geq 0
,
i_{v}(a,b,0)\leq\binom{a+b}{a}+u_{v}(a,b)
.

Proof.

Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b,0)
modified vector space system. Define a subset
J\subseteq I
recursively (in the dual order) as follows. For every

,
i\in J
if and only if
A_{i}\cap\left(\sum_{k<i}A_{k}\right)\cap B_{j}\neq 0
for all

with

has the following two properties:

1.

For all

A_{i}\cap\left(\sum_{k<i}A_{k}\right)\cap B_{j}\neq 0\
.

2.

For all

, there is some
j\in J
with
i<j

A_{i}\cap\left(\sum_{k<i}A_{k}\right)\cap B_{j}=0\
.

(Indeed,

A^{\prime}_{i}=A_{i}\cap\sum_{k<i}A_{k}
for each
i\in J
. Order

using the order induced by

. Then by property 1,
(A^{\prime}_{i},B_{i})_{i\in J}
is an
(a,b)
vector space system, so

by Theorem

\text{dim}\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\leq u_{v}(a,b)
by definition.

For all
i\in I\setminus J

\text{dim}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\setminus J\\
k\leq i\end{subarray}}A_{k}\right)\cap\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\right]\geq\text{dim}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\setminus J\\
k<i\end{subarray}}A_{k}\right)\cap\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\right]\ .

We now show that this inequality is strict. By property 2, there is some
j\in J

i<j

A_{i}\cap\left(\sum_{k<i}A_{k}\right)\cap B_{j}=0

u_{v}(a,b)\geq\text{dim}\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\geq\text{dim}\left[\left(\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}A_{i}\right)\cap\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\right]

=\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}\left(\text{dim}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\setminus J\\
k\leq i\end{subarray}}A_{k}\right)\cap\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\right]-\text{dim}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\setminus J\\
k<i\end{subarray}}A_{k}\right)\cap\left(\sum_{k\in J}B_{k}\right)\right]\right)\geq|I\setminus J|\ .

Recall that

Lemma 9
.

For all integers

u_{v}(a,b)\leq\sum_{c=0}^{b-1}\ \min{\left\{i_{v}(c,a,0),\binom{a+b}{a}\right\}}\ .

Proof.

Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b)

|I|\leq\binom{a+b}{a}

\text{dim}\left(\sum_{i\in I}B_{i}\right)=\ \sum_{i\in I}\left[\text{dim}\left(\sum_{k\geq i}B_{k}\right)-\text{dim}\left(\sum_{k>i}B_{k}\right)\right]=\ \sum_{i\in I}\left[\text{dim}(B_{i})-\text{dim}\left(B_{i}\cap\sum_{k>i}B_{k}\right)\right]=\ \sum_{c=1}^{b}|I_{c}|\ .

For each integer

Lemma 12

(32)

Theorem
13
.

We have the following estimates for
i_{s}
,
i_{v}
,
u_{s}
and
u_{v}
.

1.

For all integers
a,b\geq c\geq 0
,

i_{s}(a,b,c),\ i_{v}(a,b,c)\ \asymp\ \binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}\ .

2.

For all integers
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 1
,

u_{s}(a,b),\ u_{v}(a,b)\ \asymp\ \binom{a+b+1}{a+1}\ .

3.

For all integers
b,c\geq a\geq 0
,

i_{s}(a,b,c)\ =\ i_{v}(a,b,c)\ =\ \left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{c-a+1}\right\rfloor+1\ .

4.

For all integers
a\geq 0
and
c\geq b\geq 0
,
i_{s}(a,b,c)=i_{v}(a,b,c)=1
.

5.

For all integers
a\geq 0
,
u_{s}(a,0)=u_{v}(a,0)=0
.

Proof.

To prove part 1 it suffices to show
i_{s}(a,b,c)\gg\binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}
and
i_{v}(a,b,c)\ll\binom{a+b-2c+1}{a-c+1}
by Lemma
6
. By Lemma
7
, it suffices to prove the special case
c=0
, which states that
i_{s}(a,b,0)\gg\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
and
i_{v}(a,b,0)\ll\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
for all integers
a,b\geq 0
. Let us first prove the lower bound. By Lemma
10
with
c=0
and part 3 with
a=0=c
, we have
i_{s}(a,b,0)\geq\max_{0\leq c\leq b}\ (c+1)\binom{a+b-c}{a}\asymp\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
. The maximum is attained at
c=\left\lceil\frac{b-a}{a+1}\right\rceil
.

Let us now prove the upper bound. We will show that
i_{v}(a,b,0)\leq 2\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
and
u_{v}(a,b)\leq 2\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}-\binom{a+b}{a}
for all integers
a,b\geq 0
by induction on
a+b
. By induction, we may assume
i_{v}(c,a,0)\leq 2\binom{a+c+1}{a}
for all integers
0\leq c\leq b-1
. Then

u_{v}(a,b)\leq\sum_{c=0}^{b-1}\min{\left\{2\binom{a+c+1}{a},\binom{a+b}{a}\right\}}\leq 2\sum_{c=0}^{b}\binom{a+c}{a}-\binom{a+b}{a}=2\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}-\binom{a+b}{a}\

by Lemma
9
and
i_{v}(a,b,0)\leq 2\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
by Lemma
8
.

To prove part 2 it suffices to show
u_{s}(a,b)\gg\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
and
u_{v}(a,b)\ll\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
by Lemma
6
. We have already proved the upper bound, so let us prove the lower bound. By Lemma
11
,
u_{s}(a,b)\geq|I|u_{s}(c,d)+|\cup_{i\in I}B_{i}|\geq|I|u_{s}(c,d)
for all integers
0\leq c\leq a
and
0\leq d\leq b
and all
(a-c,b-d)
set systems
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
, so
u_{s}(a,b)\geq\binom{a+b-c-d}{a-c}u_{s}(c,d)
for all integers
0\leq c\leq a
and
0\leq d\leq b
.

Taking
c=0
and using the fact that
u_{s}(0,d)=d
for all integers
d\geq 0
, we obtain
u_{s}(a,b)\geq\max_{0\leq d\leq b}\ d\binom{a+b-d}{a}\asymp\binom{a+b}{a+1}
. The maximum is attained at
d=\left\lceil\frac{b}{a+1}\right\rceil
. Taking

and using the fact that
u_{s}(c,1)=c+1
for all integers

, we obtain
u_{s}(a,b)\geq\max_{0\leq c\leq a}\ (c+1)\binom{a+b-1-c}{b-1}\asymp\binom{a+b}{a}
. The maximum is attained at

. Combining the two bounds, we obtain
u_{s}(a,b)\gg\binom{a+b}{a+1}+\binom{a+b}{a}=\binom{a+b+1}{a+1}
.

To prove part 3 it suffices to show
i_{s}(a,b,c)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{c-a+1}\right\rfloor+1
and
i_{v}(a,b,c)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{c-a+1}\right\rfloor+1
by Lemma
6
. Let us first prove the lower bound. By Lemma
7
, it suffices to prove the special case
a=0
, which states that
i_{s}(0,b,c)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{b}{c+1}\right\rfloor+1
for all integers
b,c\geq 0

(A_{i})_{i\in I}
be a sequence of disjoint sets of size

, indexed by an ordered set

with

. For each

(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
is a
(0,b,c)
modified set system.

We now prove the upper bound. Let
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
(a,b,c)
modified vector space system. If

is empty, the bound holds, so suppose otherwise. Let

Remark 3
.

Using the set analogues of the proofs of Lemmas

, one can prove the set analogues of these lemmas, namely

from the upper bound for

as we did for

to deduce their set analogues.

One could instead generalise Definition

to that of an

and

by a finite computation. In practice, however, even for small values of

For

is known. To the best of the author’s knowledge, no value of

is known with

. We first consider the case

. Recall (see section
1
) that for all integers

and that the unique extremal graph consists of a
K_{r-2}
fully connected to an independent set of size
n-(r-2)
. Note that (for

) this graph has minimum degree
r-2
and can be obtained from
K_{r-1}
by blowing up a vertex by

. Hence

,

and
C^{\prime}(r,r-2)=\emptyset
for all integers
r\geq 3
. So the answer to Question
1
(whether

) is “Yes.” when

.

Next, we consider the case
t=r-1
. It is easy to show that all
K_{3}
-saturated graphs
G
with
\delta(G)=2
are blow-ups of either the complete bipartite graph

or the cycle
C_{5}
. Recall (see section
5
) that every

-saturated graph

\delta(G)<2(r-2)

-saturated graph

. Hence, all

-saturated graphs

are blow-ups of either

or

. So

and

for all integers

.

Finally, we consider the case

. Duffus and Hanson showed that

(Theorem 4 in

) and came close to determining

and

Let

Recall our lower bound

A Menon-type Identity derived using Cohen-Ramanujan sum
Arya Chandran, K Vishnu Namboothiri

Abstract
. Menon’s identity is a classical identity involving gcd sums and the Euler totient function ϕ \phi . We derived the Menon-type identity ∑ m = 1 ( m . n s ) s = 1 n s ( m − 1 , n s ) s = Φ s ​ ( n s ) ​ τ s ​ ( n s ) \sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\ (m.n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(m-1,n^{s})_{s}=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})\tau_{s}(n^{s}) in [ Czechoslovak Math. J., 72(1):165–176 (2022) ] where Φ s \Phi_{s} denotes the Klee’s function and ( a , b ) s (a,b)_{s} denotes a a generalization of the gcd function. Here we give an alternate method to derive this identity using the concept of Cohen-Ramanujan sum.

The classical Menon’s identity which originally appeared in
[
7

is a gcd sum turning out to be equal to a product of the Euler totient function
\phi

\tau

(m,n)
denotes the gcd of

(1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m.n)=1\end{subarray}}^{n}(m-1,n)=\phi(n)\tau(n).

It has been generalized and extended by many authors. For example, see the papers
[
6
]

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
14

For positive integer
s
, integers
a,b
, not both zero, the largest
l^{s}
(where
l\in\mathbb{N}
) dividing both
a
and
b
denoted by
(a,b)_{s}
is called the generalized gcd of
a
and
b
(following E. Cohen
[
4

). When
s=1
, this will be equal to the usual gcd of
a
and
b
.

For positive integers
s
and
n
, the Klee’s function
\Phi_{s}(n)
is defined to give the cardinality of the set
\{m\in\mathbb{N}:1\leq m\leq n,(m,n)_{s}=1\}
.

Li and Kim gave a generalization of the identity (
1

[
6
]

Theorem 1.1
.

[
1
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{k},b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{r},n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
(a_{i},n^{s})_{s}=1
,
i=1,2,\cdots,k
. Then

(2)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}\leq n^{s}\\
(m_{1},n^{s})_{s}=1\\
(m_{2},n^{s})_{s}=1\\
\cdots\\
(m_{k},n^{s})_{s}=1\\
1\leq b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}\leq n^{s}\end{subarray}}(m_{1}-a_{1},m_{2}-a_{2},

\displaystyle\ldots,m_{k}-a_{k},b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{r},n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})^{k}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}|n^{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{(d^{s})^{r}}{\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}})^{k-1}}.

As a corollary to this, we derived the Menon-type identity

1

(3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
(m.n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(m-1,n^{s})_{s}=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})\tau_{s}(n^{s}).

The Ramanujan sum denoted by
c_{r}(n)

[
8
]
is defined to be the sum of certain powers of a primitive
r
th root of unity. That is,

\displaystyle c_{r}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{r}}

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Ramanujan sum has been generalized in many ways. E. Cohen
[
2
]
defined

(4)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{(h,r^{s})_{s}=1}\\
h=1\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi inh}{r^{s}}}.

When
s=1
, this reduces to the Ramanujan sum. This sum will be henceforth called as the Cohen-Ramanujan sum. Some other important properties of this sum is given in the next section.

Our proof of the above theorem
[
1
]
used elementary number theory techniques. The original proof of the Menon’s identity was also given using elementary number theory technques. In
[
12
]
, L. Tóth gave a proof of the classical Menon’s identity (
1

3
) using the properties of the generalized gcd function and the Cohen-Ramanujan sum.

2.
Notations and basic results

A postive integer
a
is said to be
s-
power free or
s-
free if no
l^{s}
where
l\in\mathbb{N}
divides
a
.

The Euler totient function
\phi
has several generalizations of which three are relevant to us. They are the Jordan totient function, Cohen totient function and the Klee’s function. The Jordan totient function
J_{s}(n)

[
5
, pp 95-97]
defined for positive integers
s
and
n
gives the number of ordered sets of
s
elements from a complete residue system (mod
n
) such that the greatest common divisor of each set is prime to
n
. The Cohen totient function
\phi_{s}

[
4
]
is defined as follows. If
(a,b)_{s}=1
, then
a
and
b
are said to be relatively
s-
prime. The subset
N
of a complete residue system
M
(mod
n^{s}
) consisting of all elements of
M
that are relatively
s-
prime to
n^{s}
is called an
s-
reduced residue system (mod
n
). Cohen totient function
\phi_{s}(n)
gives the number of elements of an
s-
reduced residue system (mod
n
).

Some of the important properties of the Klee’s function
\Phi_{s}
are listed in
[
1
, Section 2]
. Most notably, it has a product formula

(5)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(n)=n\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{s}\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right).

In
[
4
, Theorem 3]
, Cohen showed that
\phi_{s}(n)=n^{s}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{s}\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}})
. So
\phi_{s}(n)=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})
. Further
J_{s}
and
\phi_{s}
are related by the identity
J_{s}(n)=\phi_{s}(n)

[
4
, Theorem 5]
. Note also that
\Phi_{1}=J_{1}=\phi_{1}=\phi
.

The Cohen-Ramanujan sum
c_{r}^{(s)}(n)
definition of which is given in the introduction above has the following properties.

CRS1)

c_{r}^{(s)}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|r\\
d^{s}|n\end{subarray}}\mu(r/d)d^{s}
where
\mu
is the Möbius function
[
2
]
.

CRS2)

c_{r}^{(s)}(n)=\frac{\phi_{s}(r)\mu(d)}{\phi_{s}(d)}=\frac{\Phi_{s}(r^{s})\mu(d)}{\Phi_{s}(d^{s})}
where
d^{s}=\frac{r^{s}}{(n,r^{s})_{s}}

[
3
]
.

CRS3)

c_{r}^{s}(n)=c_{r}^{s}(-n)
.

CRS4)

c_{r}^{s}(1)=\mu(r)
.

3.
Main Results

We will derive the Menon-type identity (
3
) in this section. To begin with, we prove three lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

For positive integers
n,s
, we have

\frac{n^{s}}{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu(d))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(d^{s})}
.

Proof.

Let
n=p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots p_{k}^{a_{k}}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{n^{s}}{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{s}\mid n^{s}\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{s}\mid n^{s}\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1+\frac{1}{p^{s}-1})

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{p_{1}^{s}-1}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p_{2}^{s}-1}\right)\cdots\left(1+\frac{1}{p_{k}^{s}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{k}\left(1+\frac{(\mu(p_{i}))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(p_{i}^{s})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu(d))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(d^{s})}.

∎

An element in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
will be denoted by
(a;b)
to avoid any confusion with the notation of gcd of
a
and
b
. The next result generalizes
[
13
, Proposition 1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{N}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an arbitrary function and
k,n,s
positive integers. Then we have

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\psi(k;n^{s})(k,n^{s})_{s}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(n^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}\psi(d^{s}j;n^{s}).

Proof.

We have
n^{s}=\sum\limits_{d|n}J_{s}(d)

[
9
, Section V.3]
and
J_{s}(n)=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})
. Hence
n^{s}=\sum\limits_{d|n}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})=\sum\limits_{d^{s}|n^{s}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})
. So

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\psi(k;n^{s})(k,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\psi(k;n^{s})\sum\limits_{d^{s}|(k,n^{s})_{s}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\psi(k;n^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid k\\
d^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s}).

Since
d^{s}\mid k
and
1\leq k\leq n^{s}
, we may write
k=d^{s}j
where
1\leq j\leq\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}
and hence we get the first part of the lemma
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\psi(k;n^{s})(k,n^{s})_{s}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}(d^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}\psi(d^{s}j;n^{s})
.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
n,s,j
positive integers, we have that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n^{s}}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid(j,n^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}).

Proof.

Define
\psi(b;n)=e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n}}
so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}\psi(b;n^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}({d^{s}})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}\psi({k},{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}({d^{s}})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}}.

Note that
\frac{1}{d^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{d^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ikn}{d^{s}}}=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }d^{s}\mid n\\
0\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

So

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\\
\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}\mid j\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}({d^{s}}){\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid n^{s}\\
{d^{s}}\mid j\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}({\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}){{d^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{d^{s}}\mid(j,n^{s})_{s}\end{subarray}}\Phi_{s}({\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}}}){{d^{s}}}.

∎

Now we are ready to derive identity (
3
) which we restate below.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
n,s
positive integers, we have that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(a-1,n^{s})_{s}=\Phi_{s}(n^{s})\tau_{s}(n^{s}).

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(a-1,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\\
a-1\equiv b(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a-1\equiv b(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}(b,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(b,n^{s})_{s}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ij(1-a+b)}{n^{s}}}.

To arrive at the last line above, we used the fact that
\frac{1}{d^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\end{subarray}}^{d^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{d^{s}}}=1
if
d^{s}|n
.
Continuing with the computation, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a.n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(a-1,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{n^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}(b,n^{s})_{s}e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi ia(-j)}{n^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{n^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b(\text{mod }n^{s})\end{subarray}}(b,n^{s})_{s}e^{\frac{2\pi ibj}{n^{s}}}c_{n}^{s}(j).

Now let
(j,n^{s})_{s}=\delta^{s}\Rightarrow\delta^{s}\mid n^{s},\delta^{s}\mid j\Rightarrow\delta^{s}\mid n^{s},j=t\delta^{s}
for some integer
t
.
Also
(\frac{j}{\delta^{s}},\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})_{s}=1
. That is
(t,\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})_{s}=1
.

Since
\frac{n^{s}}{(j,n^{s})_{s}}=\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}}=(\frac{n}{\delta})^{s}
, by property CRS2 of the Cohen-Ramanujan sum given in section 2 and Lemma (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a.n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}

\displaystyle(a-1,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(t,\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})_{s}=1\\
\delta^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\pi it\delta^{s}}{n^{s}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid\delta^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}})\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})\mu(\frac{n}{\delta})}{\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\frac{n}{\delta})}{\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid\delta^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(t,\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\pi it\delta^{s}}{n^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\frac{n}{\delta})}{\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid\delta^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}})c_{\frac{n}{\delta}}^{s}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta^{s}\mid n^{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu(\frac{n}{\delta}))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{\delta^{s}})}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}\mid\delta^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(\frac{n^{s}}{d^{s}})\text{ since }\mu(\frac{n}{\delta})=c_{\frac{n}{\delta}}^{s}(1)\text{ (by CRS4)}.

Now
d^{s}\mid\delta^{s},\delta^{s}\mid n^{s}\Rightarrow n^{s}=\delta^{s}h^{s}=d^{s}k^{s}h^{s}
so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,n^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{n^{s}}(a-1,n^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{s}=d^{s}k^{s}h^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(k^{s}h^{s})\frac{(\mu(h))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(h^{s})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{s}=d^{s}m^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(m^{s})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=kh\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu(h))^{2}}{\Phi_{s}(h^{s})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{s}=d^{s}m^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}\Phi_{s}(m^{s})\frac{m^{s}}{\Phi_{s}(m^{s})}\hskip 10.0pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{s}=d^{s}m^{s}\end{subarray}}d^{s}{m^{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{s}|n^{s}\end{subarray}}n^{s}

\displaystyle={\Phi_{s}(n^{s})}\tau_{s}(n^{s})

which is what we claimed.

∎

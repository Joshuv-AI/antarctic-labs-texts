On minimal extended representations of generalized power cones
Víctor Blanco †,‡ and Miguel Martínez-Antón †,‡ † Institute of Mathematics (IMAG) ‡ Dpt. Quant. Methods for Economics & Business Universidad de Granada vblanco@ugr.es , mmanton@ugr.es

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze minimal representations of ( p , 𝜶 ) (p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}) -power cones as simpler cones. We derive some new results on the complexity of the representations, and we provide a procedure to construct a minimal representation by means of second order cones in case 𝜶 {\boldsymbol{\alpha}} and p p are rational. The construction is based on the identification of the cones with a graph, the mediated graph. Then, we develop a mixed integer linear optimization formulation to obtain the optimal mediated graph, and then, the minimal representation. We present the results of a series of computational experiments in order to analyze the computational performance of the approach, both to obtain the representation and its incorporation into a practical conic optimization model that arises in facility location.

In this paper, we analyze a general type of power cone, the

An extended representation of a cone (in general, of any set) is an equivalent reformulation of the set through its projection onto a higher dimensional space and its identification with a product of simpler sets. In general, different representations are valid for the same set. Thus, a natural measure of the goodness of a representation is the complexity of the representation, that is, the dimension of the space where the set is embedded and the number of simpler factor sets in the representation. In this paper, we derive different extended representations of the
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-power cone by means of simpler power cones and study its complexity. In case

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are rational, we provide extended representations of the cone as
3

We deal with the problem of constructing optimal representations by solving a mathematical optimization problem.

Apart from the particular simplifications, this type of generalized power cone appears, for instance, when modeling attraction between different bodies. According to the Universal Law of Gravitation by Newton, the attraction of two objects can be obtained as:

F=\frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{\|\mathbb{x}_{1}-\mathbb{x}_{2}\|^{2}_{2}}

where
G
is the gravity constant,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are the masses of the two bodies, and
\mathbb{x}_{1}
and
\mathbb{x}_{2}
their positions in the space. Thus, given a minimum threshold for the attraction between the two objects,
T
, the inequality modeling all the masses and positions with such a minimum attraction can be written as
\frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{\|\mathbb{x}_{1}-\mathbb{x}_{2}\|^{2}_{2}}\geq T
, or equivalently:

\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{G}{T}m_{1}m_{2},\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
\mathbb{x}=\mathbb{x}_{1}-\mathbb{x}_{2}
. Thus, the region defined by the inequality above is exactly one of the cones in our family of generalized power cones, specifically
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{3+2}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Clearly, the use of different distances between the two bodies in higher dimensional spaces (
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
and generalizing the product of two attributes (as the masses) to more than two,
d_{2}
, and aggregating them by using the weighted geometric mean of them, is a reasonable measure of attraction, whose representation results in the shape of the generalized power cones that we analyze here.

In this paper, we analyze this general cone, and apart from providing constructive approaches to simply represent the cones, we derive a novel approach to construct minimal representations of the cone as simpler second order cones. The main structures that we use for this construction are the so-called
mediated graphs
. These graphs are related to the concept of mediated sets that were introduced in
[
33
]
. The connections between these sets and the power cone were suggested in
[
39
]
. In this paper, these structures are also applied to analyze the Sum of Non-negative Circuits (SONC) problem
[
38
]
or the Sum of Squares (SOS) problem
[
21
]
. Here, we propose, for the first time, an exact mathematical optimization-based methodology to derive minimal representations of the power cone through the problem that we call the Minimum Cardinality Mediated Graph Problem (MCMGP).

Finally, in Section

Preliminaries

The main goal of this paper is to analyze the representations of
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-power cones as a product of simpler cones in higher dimensional spaces. The formal definition of this representation is stated as follows.

Definition 1

(Extended representation)
.

Given a set
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that a finite family
\mathcal{E}=\{Q_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}
with
Q_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{l}}
, for
l=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
is an
extended representation
of
Q
if there exist
m_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and projections
\pi_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{l}}
, for
l=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
, such that:

\mathbb{x}\in Q\text{ if, and only if, }\exists\mathbb{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}}\mbox{such that }\pi_{l}(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{w})\in Q_{l}\text{ }\forall l=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}.

We denote by
{{\rm Ext}}(Q)
the set of families which are extended representations of
Q
.

For any extended representation
\mathcal{E}
of the set
Q
, the integer
m_{\mathcal{E}}
in the above definition will be denoted as the
extended dimension
of
\mathcal{E}
to represent
Q
and the integer
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
will be called the
cardinality of the representation
.

Note that this definition differs slightly from the notion of
extended formulation
of a polytope that has been widely studied in combinatorial optimization (see e.g.
[
22
]
). The key idea of our notion of extended representation is to lift the original set
Q
onto an extended higher dimensional space, keeping the components of the original set
Q
, but allowing a simpler and more suitable representation at the price of adding some new variables and constraints. A clear illustration of this type of extension is the one commonly used to reformulate disjunctive linear programming problems. Let
Q={\rm conv}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i})
the convex hull of the union of
n
polytopes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, each of them defined by means of linear inequalities,
P_{i}=\{\mathbb{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A_{i}\mathbb{z}\leq\mathbb{b}_{i}\}
with
A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}\times d}
,
\mathbb{b}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. While
Q
is difficult to be represented as a set of inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it can be represented by the set:

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}=\{

\displaystyle(\mathbb{z},\mathbb{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{z}_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{dn}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:

\displaystyle\mathbb{z}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{z}_{i}=0,

\displaystyle A_{i}\mathbb{z}_{i}-\mathbb{b}_{i}\lambda_{i}\leq 0,\forall i=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\forall i=1,\ldots,n\}.

Defining the projection
\pi
as the identity in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{dn}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is known (see e.g.,
[
3
]
) that
\mathbb{z}\in Q
if and only if there exists
\mathbb{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\pi(\mathbb{z},\mathbb{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{z}_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in Q^{\prime}
. Thus
\mathcal{E}=\{Q^{\prime}\}
is an extended representation of
Q
with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}=1
and
m_{\mathcal{E}}=dn+n
.

An extended representation can be seen as a generalized intersection of the sets that are part of the extension, as stated in the following result whose proof is straightforward from the definition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{E}=\{Q_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}
with
Q_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{l}}
, for
l=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Then,
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm Ext}(Q)
if and only if there exist
m_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and projections
\pi_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{l}}
, for
l=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}
, such that:

Q={\rm proj}_{d}\left(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}Q_{l}\right)\right)={\rm proj}_{d}\left(\bigcap_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}\left(\pi_{l}^{-1}(Q_{l})\right)\right)\cong{\rm proj}_{d}\left(\bigcap_{l=1}^{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}\left(Q_{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}-d_{l}}\right)\!\!\right),

where
{\rm proj}_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the canonical projection of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}=(\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}).
Here,
\cong
stands for the bijection equivalence relation, i.e.,
A\cong B
if
A
and
B
are bijective sets.

The constructions derived in this paper will be based on nesting sequential representations. The correctness of the global extended representation of this construction is based on the following result.

Proposition 3

Let
\mathcal{E}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}}\}\in{{\rm Ext}}(Q)

\pi_{ls}^{\prime}=\pi_{ls}\circ\left(\pi_{l}\times id_{m_{\mathcal{E}_{l}}}\right)\circ\left(id_{d\times m_{\mathcal{E}}}\times{\rm proj}_{m_{\mathcal{E}_{l}}}\right)

\textstyle{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}}

\textstyle{\mathbb{R}^{d_{l}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}_{l}}}}

\mathbb{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{\mathcal{E}}}

3.
Extended representations of the generalized power cone

In this section, we provide valid extended representations of the
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-power cone by means of simpler power cones. We analyze the complexities of the different representations.

The first result is based on splitting the constraint defining the
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-cone in two simple constraints (with a single linking variable), being the obtained constraints of simpler cones, in this case, a
p
-order cone and an
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-power cone. As a consequence, being able to represent the original power cone stems from adequately representing (separately) each of these two cones.

.

with

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}=2

Proof.

The proof is straightforward.

Let

Let

(

Proof.

The proof is based on that, for

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{x_{j}^{p}}{w^{p-1}}\leq w

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{x_{j}^{p}}{w^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq w

Which can be split into the following inequalities by adding auxiliary variables

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}

(2)

\displaystyle x_{j}^{p}

Thus,

In case

4.
Optimal SOC representations of the rational power cone

The extended representations derived in the previous section allow one to reduce the representation of the complex cone
\mathcal{K}_{p}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
by means of simpler power cones
\mathcal{K}^{1+d_{2}}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
for
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\Lambda_{d_{2}}
. For the sake of simplification, in the rest of the paper, we denote
d=d_{2}
. The main implications of these representations come from the fact that one can restrict the variables of an optimization problem to belong to a
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-power cone, but formulate the constraints as constraints in the form
x\leq\mathbb{z}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
for some
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\Lambda_{d}
. In case the components of
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are rational (
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, or equivalently,
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=(\frac{s_{1}}{\hat{s}},\ldots,\frac{s_{d}}{\hat{s}})\in\Lambda_{d}
with
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\gcd(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})=1
, and
\hat{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}
), one can go further and derive representations by means of second order cones. In this section, we will present the main contribution of this paper, a mathematical optimization-based approach to construct a minimal extended representation of rational power cones (in terms of the cardinality of the representation and extended dimension).

The mathematical optimization model that we propose to construct a minimal extended representation of the cone is based on the notion of
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph that we define as follows.

The following result states the correspondence between the extended representations and the above defined mediated graphs.

Theorem 14
.

There exists a
one-to-one
correspondence between
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and the set of extended representations by second order cones of
\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
.

Proof.

Let us first proof any
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph
G=(\mathcal{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\cup X,A)
with
X=\{\mathbb{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{x}_{\mathcal{L}}\}
induces an extended representation of
\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})=\{x\leq\mathbb{z}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\}
using second order cones
\mathcal{K}^{1+2}(\frac{1}{2})
.

Let us define the set of indices
\mathbb{L}=\{1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}+d\}
such that
\mathbb{x}_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathbb{b}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
,
\mathbb{x}_{\mathcal{L}+i}=\hat{s}\mathbb{e}_{i}
for
i=1,...,d-1
, and
\mathbb{x}_{\mathcal{L}+d}=\mathbb{0}
. Additionally, we denote by
\sigma(j)\subset\mathbb{L}
(with
|\sigma(j)|=2
) the set of indices for the
j
-th mediated element such that
\mathbb{x}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\in\sigma(j)}\mathbb{x}_{\ell}
, for
j=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}
.

With the above notation, we have that:

\pi_{j}(\bar{\mathbb{w}})=(\bar{w}_{j},\bar{\mathbb{w}}_{\sigma(j)}),\forall j=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}

where
\bar{\mathbb{w}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{\mathcal{L}-1},x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})
, define an extended second order cone representation of the cone
\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
.

Let us consider consider
(x,\mathbb{z})\in\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
, that is,
x\leq\mathbb{z}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
. As already mentioned, there always exists
\mathbb{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{L}-1}
verifying
w_{j}\leq\mathbb{z}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}-1
, where
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{j}
are the coordinates of
\mathbb{x}_{j}
as point in the convex hull of
\mathcal{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
and

\bar{w}_{j}\leq\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\in\sigma(j)}\bar{w}_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, if
\pi_{j}(\bar{\mathbb{w}})=(\bar{w}_{j},\bar{\mathbb{w}}_{\sigma(j)})\in\mathcal{K}^{1+2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),\forall j=1,\ldots,\mathcal{L}.
In particular,
\pi_{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{\mathbb{w}})=(x,\bar{\mathbb{w}}_{\sigma(\mathcal{L})})\in\mathcal{K}^{1+2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
and then:

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\in\sigma(\mathcal{L})}\bar{w}_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\in\sigma(\mathcal{L})}\mathbb{z}^{\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\mu^{\ell}}}=\mathbb{z}^{\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\sigma(\mathcal{L})}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\ell}}=\mathbb{z}^{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}.

Thus,
(x,\mathbb{z})\in\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}).

Note that the above proof is fully reversible, so any second order cone extended representation for
\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
can be identified with an element in
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
.
∎

The above result does not just relate mediated graphs with SOC representations of
\mathcal{K}^{1+d}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
, this makes the problem of finding an
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph of the minimum cardinality equivalent to the problem of finding the minimum SOC representation. Moreover, the cardinality of the representation is the cardinality of the mediated set in the mediated graph.

Remark 15

(Lower and Upper bounds for the cardinality of a
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph)
.

In
[
39
]
, the author proves two lower bounds for the number of nodes in a minimal
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph, one based on the dimension of the nodes (
d
) and other in the sum of the elements

,
\hat{s}
, being then a suitable lower bound for the
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated set the maximum of both values,
\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}=\max\{d-1,\lceil\log_{2}(\hat{s})\rceil\}
.

On the other hand, as already mentioned in
[

]
, one can overestimate the size of the representation for the cone by using a binary representation of the elements
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}
. Specifically, for
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we denote by
\Omega(a)
the set of exponents with strictly positive coefficients in the binary decomposition of
a
, i.e., if
a=\sum_{k=0}^{K}b_{k}2^{k}
, with
b_{k}\in\{0,1\}
for
k=1,\ldots,K
, then
\Omega(a)=\{k:b_{k}=1\}_{k=0}^{K}
. Then, an upper bound for the minimum size of an
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated set is
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\Omega(s_{i})|+|\Omega(2^{\lceil\log_{2}(\hat{s})\rceil}-\hat{s})|-1
. The representations reaching this upper bound are the most common when representing
p
-order cones in several applications in the literature (see e.g.
[
8
,
10
,
12
]
).

4.1.
A mathematical optimization model to construct minimal mediated graphs

Now, we are in conditions to present the mathematical optimization model that we propose to construct minimal
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graphs, and then also to obtain minimal representations of power cones. We call the problem the
Minimum Cardinality
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-Mediated Graph Problem
(
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-MCMGP, for short).

Given
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\Lambda_{d}
, the goal of the
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-MCMGP is to construct an
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph with the minimum number of nodes. The
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-MCMGP has the flavor of a Continuous Location problem, where a set of services (the mediated elements) is to be located in a given space, taking into account a given set of
users
(the elements in
\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
) located at certain coordinates in the space. The interested reader is referred to
[
12
,
11
,
31
]
for further details on this family of problems.

We would like first to highlight that the
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-MCMGP may not be unique, that is, it may exist different
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graphs with minimum cardinality. A counterexample is described in the following example.

G=(\mathcal{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\cup X,A)
, we consider the following binary variables that account for the active arcs in the graph:

y_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $(\mathbb{x}_{i},\mathbb{x}_{j})\in A$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

for all
i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},j\in I
,
i\neq j
.

•

In order to account for the number of effective mediated elements in the overestimated set, we use the standard variables in facility location to activate nodes:

z_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if node $\mathbb{x}_{j}$ is an active node in the mediated graph,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

for all
j\in I
.

Note that the amount
\sum_{j\in I_{M}}z_{j}
provides the number of different nodes in the
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated set, and then, its cardinality. Since all the elements in
\mathcal{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\cup\{\mathbb{b}_{\alpha}\}
must be nodes of the graph, we fix
z_{0}=\cdots=z_{d}=1
in our model.

•

We consider continuous variables in
[0,\hat{s}]
to model the coordinates of the nodes in the mediated graph:

x_{jr}\in[0,\hat{s}]:\text{$k$th coordinate of the $j$-th node in the mediated graph.}

for
j\in I
,
k=1,\ldots,d-1
. The first
d
nodes have fixed coordinates to the elements in
\mathcal{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\cup\{\mathbb{b}_{\alpha}\}
that must be part of the mediated graph. Thus, we fix their values to
\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{b}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
\mathbb{x}_{j}=\hat{s}\mathbb{e}_{j}
(for
j=1,\ldots,d-1
), and
\mathbb{x}_{d}=\mathbf{0}
.

With the above variables, the mathematical optimization model that we propose to find a minimal
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph, and then, the minimal extended representation of the cone, is the following:

(5)

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I_{M}\cup\{0\}}z_{j}

(6)

s.t.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}y_{ij}=2z_{i},\forall i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},

(7)

\displaystyle y_{ij}\leq z_{j},\forall i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},j\in I,

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{x}_{j}+\mathbb{x}_{k})-\hat{s}(2-y_{ij}-y_{ik}),\forall i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},j\neq k\in I,

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{x}_{j}+\mathbb{x}_{k})+\hat{s}(2-y_{ij}-y_{ik}),\forall i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},j\neq k\in I,

(10)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{x}_{i}-\mathbb{x}_{j}\|_{1}\geq\varepsilon(z_{i}+z_{j}-1),\forall i,j\in I(i\neq j),

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{0}=\mathbb{b}_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{i}=\hat{s}\mathbb{e}_{i},\forall i\in I_{A}\setminus\{0,d\},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{d}=\mathbb{0},

(14)

\displaystyle z_{0}=\cdots=z_{d}=1,

(15)

\displaystyle y_{ij}\in\{0,1\},\forall i\in I_{M}\cup\{0\},j\in I,i\neq j,

(16)

\displaystyle z_{j}\in\{0,1\},\forall j\in I,

(17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\forall i\in I_{M}.

The objective function in the above problem accounts for the number of (active) nodes in the mediated set of the
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
-mediated graph. Constraints (
6
) ensures that there are exactly two outgoing arcs from each active node in the mediated set, with endnodes in
\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
or in the new set of mediated nodes. Constraints (
7
) ensure that only active nodes can be chosen to construct arcs in the mediated graph. Constraints (
8
) and (
9
) are the requirements for the mediated graph, i.e., the arcs are constructed as averages of the endnode coordinates. With Constraints (
10
) we assure that in case two mediated are active, then their coordinates must be different (since their
\ell_{1}
distance between them must be greater than
\varepsilon
, and zero otherwise). The rest of the constraints are the variable fixing equations and the domains for each of the variables.

The above formulation is a mixed integer linear programming model, which can be hard to solve for general instances of the problem since the number of binary variables to decide is
\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}+(\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}+d-1)\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
. We incorporate different valid inequalities that strengthen the linear relaxation and break symmetries of the problem.

Specifically, in case
\mathbb{x}_{j}
is not an active node, we fix its coordinates to the arbitrary vector
(\hat{s},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d-1)}}{{\ldots}},\hat{s})
, by adding the following constraints:

(18)

\hat{s}(1-z_{j})\leq x_{jk}\leq\hat{s}(1+z_{j}),\forall j\in I,k=1,\ldots,d-1.

Note that in case
z_{j}=0
, these constraints enforce that
\mathbb{x}_{j}=(\hat{s},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d-1)}}{{\ldots}},\hat{s})
, otherwise, the constraint is redundant (
0\leq x_{jk}\leq 2\hat{s}
, which is always satisfied). Observe that in case the node is not activated, the assignment of coordinates to the node is not relevant, and any value is possible. Thus, fixing it to a given value allows to reduce the exploration of the branch-and-bound tree.

The second set of valid inequalities that we consider are the following:

(19)

z_{j}\leq z_{j-1},\forall j\in I\backslash\{0\}

These constraints force the non-activated nodes to be assigned to the last indices in
I
. Thus, given
\kappa=\sum_{j\in I}z_{j}
, the nodes in the mediated graph have coordinates
\mathbb{x}_{0},\ldots,\mathbb{x}_{d+\kappa}
, being the remainder,
\mathbb{x}_{d+\kappa+1}=\cdots=\mathbb{x}_{d+\Delta_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}=(\hat{s},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d-1)}}{{\ldots}},\hat{s})
by constraints (
18
).

Observe that there is still a lot of symmetry in the problem since any permutation of the coordinates of the active mediated nodes is an alternative solution with the same objective value. We break symmetries in our model by enforcing the active nodes to be sorted in non-decreasing order with the following valid inequalities:

(20)

x_{jk}\leq x_{(j-1)k},\forall j\in I_{M}\backslash\{d\}.

where
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
is a chosen coordinate to sort the elements in the mediated set.

.

Remark 18
.

The representations that have been already applied in the literature, specially in the representation of
p
-order cones, and that allow us to derive an upper bound for the cardinality of the optimal mediated graphs (Remark
15
) can be easily constructed using binary decomposition (see
[
12
]

\sum_{i\in I_{M}}y_{ij}\leq z_{j},\forall j\in I_{M}.

5.2.
Computational experiments for the Gravitational Covering Location Problem

The second set of experiments that we designed to test our approach is the use of a minimal extended representation of the
(p,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
-power cone in a practical application. Specifically, we apply the representation to a continuous covering location problem whose coverage follows the Universal Law of Gravitation.

The goal of the problem that we propose is to optimally locate a given number of facilities in a
d
-dimensional space,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and decide their coverage radii based on different features to maximize the overall coverage of a given set of users.

This problem belongs to the family of Covering Location problems, that arise whenever a decision-maker aims to cover a given demand in case the facilities have a limited coverage area. In several practical situations, the services to be located are unable to satisfy the demand outside their coverage area but the demand covered by the services wants to be maximized. Church
[
16
]
introduces the Maximal Covering Location Problem (MCLP) as the problem of finding the positions of
p
services on a given space, each of them endowed with a given coverage area, maximizing the covered demand of the users. The MCLP has attracted significant attention from both researchers and practitioners
[
40
]
. Specifically, this type of problem has been applied in different fields, such as in the location of emergency services
[
36
]
, leak detection
[
10
]
, mail advertising
[
17
]
, archaeology
[
4
]
, among many others. The interested reader is referred to
[
18
]
for further details and recent advances in covering location.

To formulate the problem we first describe the parameters and decision variables that we use in our mathematical optimization model.

We are given a set of demand points
\{\mathbb{a}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
indexed by the set
I
. We assume that
|J|
facilities are to be located in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, indexed by the set
J
.

We assume that each demand point is allowed to be serviced by a facility if it belongs to a coverage area induced by a
\ell_{p}
-norm ball centered at the facility’s coordinates. The radius of each facility has to be determined based on different features, such as the capacity of the facility, the satisfaction level, or the distance to certain points of interest. In particular, a demand point located at
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be covered by a facility located at
\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
only if
\|\mathbb{x}-\mathbf{a}\|_{p}\leq Gm_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots m_{l}^{\alpha_{l}}
, where
m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}
are the different features affecting the coverage radius, and
G\in\mathbb{R}_{+},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l})\in\Lambda_{l}
are the aggregation parameters for these values. Note that the shape of this coverage area extends the notion of attraction between two bodies (in this case the demand points and the facilities) stated in the Universal Law of Gravitation by Newton
[
30
]
, where the attraction of two objects can be obtained as:

\frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{\|\mathbb{x}_{1}-\mathbb{x}_{2}\|^{2}_{2}}

where
G
is the gravity constant,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are the masses of the two bodies, and
\mathbb{x}_{1}
and
\mathbb{x}_{2}
their positions in the space.

Observe that the assumption of the dependence of the coverage radii with different features has a practical interest in real-world situations where the decision-makers may decide to give a different service to the users based on their characteristics (certain profile of users of interest, preferred communities, serve minorities instead of just those closer to the facility, etc). Thus, the consideration of attraction-function measures, as the one generalizing Newton’s law, would provide insights into the convenience of locating the service in different places, as well as which users are more favorable to the coverage. Specifically, one facility may sacrifice to cover certain users which are closer at the price of covering others that are further but provide better coverage.

The values of the features for the coverage radius could depend only on the demand point (i.e., certain characteristics of the specific demand point) or only on the facility (as the capacity level), or both (as the interest of a demand point for a given facility or viceversa). The values of these features are unknown, and determining their values informs the decision-maker about the importance that should be given to each of them in the coverage of demand points. A budget
B
is available to invest in the features.

In addition, each demand point
i\in I
is endowed with a weight
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which models the population of the region identified with the demand point or the profit for covering it.

The goal of the Gravitational Maximal Covering Location problem is to determine the optimal positions of the facilities indexed in
J
that maximize the overall profit for covering the demand
I
such that the cost for the values of the different features does not exceed the given budget.

To derive our mathematical programming formulation for the problem, we use the following sets of decision variables:

y_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if demand point $i$ is accounted as covered by facility $j$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad\forall i\in I,j\in J.

\mathbb{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{ Coordinates of the placement of the facilities, }\quad\forall j\in J.

m_{ijk}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\text{ Value of feature $k$ affecting the coverage of demand point $i$ by facility $j$.}

for
i\in I,j\in J,k=1,\ldots,l
.

The Gravitational Covering Location Problem can be formulated as the following Mathematical Optimization Model:

(24)

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}\omega_{i}y_{ij}

(25)

s.t.

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{x}_{j}-\mathbb{a}_{i}\|_{p}\leq G_{ij}m_{ij1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots m_{ijl}^{\alpha_{l}}+M(1-y_{ij}),\forall i\in I,j\in J,

(26)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}y_{ij}\leq 1,\forall i\in I,

(27)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\displaystyle\sum_{j\in P}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}m_{ijk}\leq B,

\displaystyle\mathbb{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\forall j\in J,

\displaystyle y_{ij}\in\{0,1\},\forall i\in I,j\in J,

\displaystyle m_{ijk}\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\forall i\in I,j\in J,k=1,\ldots,l.

The objective function model is the overall weighted coverage of the solution. Constraints (
25
) enforce that in case the demand point
i
is covered by facility
j
,
\mathbb{a}_{i}
must belong to the coverage area determined by the different features. Otherwise, the value of the parameter
M>\max_{i,i^{\prime}\in I}\|a_{i}-a_{i^{\prime}}\|_{p}
makes this constraint redundant. Constraints (
26
) state that each demand point can be accounted as covered by at most one facility. Constraint (
27
) is the budget constraint. Finally, the domains of the variables are detailed.

Observe that constraints (
25
) can be written as conic constraints in the shape of those in Remark
12
, i.e., Constraints (
25
) are equivalent to:

(\mathbb{x},\mathbb{y},\textbf{m})\in\mathcal{K}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})[f_{ij},g_{ij},h_{ij}],\forall i\in I,j\in J,

where
f_{ij}(\mathbb{x})=\mathbb{x}_{j}-\mathbb{a}_{i}
,
h_{ij}(\textbf{m})=G_{ij}\cdot\left(m_{ij1},\ldots,m_{ijl}\right)
, and
g_{ij}(\mathbb{y})=M(1-y_{ij})

Note that one can incorporate more sophisticated expressions for the
m
-variables in the form of linear or conic constraints keeping a similar structured problem.

where

\gamma(2|I|+|J|)
, where

; and the constants
G_{ij}
are all equal to
1
. We solved the problem for

.

Conclusions

In this paper, we analyze extended representations of general cones, the so-called
(p,)
-power cones. These cones are highly practical when modeling constraints in optimization problems. We study different representations for general values for and
p
as simpler cones and study further simplifications to second order cones in case and
p
have rational entries. Specifically, we provide a novel mathematical optimization-based procedure to construct minimal representations, i.e. using the minimum number of extra variables and constraints in the representation. The idea behind our approach is the identification of each extended representation with a graph, the mediated graph, whose size coincides with the complexity of the representation. Thus, we introduce, for the first time, the Minimum Cardinality Mediated Graph Problem (MCMGP) and derive a suitable mixed integer linear programming formulation for it. We tested the approach both based on the computational difficulty of solving the -MCMGP and the ability to use the minimal representations in a facility location problem compared to the representation provided by the upper bound (Remark
15
). The main conclusion of our computational experience is that the use of minimal representations of generalized is advisable in practice and that its study is worth it, not only for the mathematical challenges that it poses but also for its practical application.

Further research on the topic includes a deeper study of the minimal mediated graphs that induce a minimal representation of a cone. The geometric properties of these intriguing graphs would result in a direct impact, in the form of valid inequalities for the -MCMGP, in the solution times required to solve the problem. The analysis of particular values of
p
and whose representation can be
easily
derived would be also useful. We conjecture that, in general, the -MCMGP is NP-hard. This fact would imply that finding a minimal representation of these cones is a challenging combinatorial problem. The proof of this conjecture will be the topic of our forthcoming research.

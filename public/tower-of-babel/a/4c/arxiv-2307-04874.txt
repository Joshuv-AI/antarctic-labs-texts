Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities
Diego N. Guajardo

Abstract
Given a Euclidean submanifold g : M n → ℝ n + p g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p} , Chern and Kuiper provided inequalities between μ \mu and ν g \nu_{g} , the ranks of the nullity of M n M^{n} and the relative nullity of g g respectively. Namely, they prove that ν g ≤ μ ≤ ν g + p . \nu_{g}\leq\mu\leq\nu_{g}+p. (1) In this work, we study the submanifolds with ν g ≠ μ \nu_{g}\neq\mu . More precisely, we characterize locally the ones with 0 ≠ ( μ − ν g ) ∈ { p , p − 1 , p − 2 } 0\neq(\mu-\nu_{g})\in\{p,p-1,p-2\} under the hypothesis of ν g ≤ n − p − 1 \nu_{g}\leq n-p-1 .

Introduction

There are two associated distributions to a submanifold
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
, the
nullity

\Gamma\subseteq TM
of the curvature tensor and the
relative nullity

\Delta_{g}\subseteq TM
, i.e., the nullity of the second fundamental form
\alpha
of
g
.
The relative nullity plays a fundamental role in many works of submanifold theory; for example
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
11
]
.
In many of them, this distribution coincides with the nullity, turning the problem into an intrinsic one; besides the ones already cited, see
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
.

We want to understand the submanifolds whose relative nullity does not coincide with the nullity.
There are two natural families of submanifolds with
\nu_{g}\neq\mu
.
Firstly, if
M^{n}
is flat and
g
is not (an open subset of) an affine subspace then
\Delta_{g}\neq TM=\Gamma
.
Secondly, we have the compositions, that is, if
\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}
has nontrivial nullity and
h:U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a flat submanifold with
\hat{g}(M^{n})\subseteq U
then generically
g=h\circ\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
has less relative nullity, in particular
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
.
Theorem 1 of
[
13
]
is an example of this phenomenon.

As a starting point, Gauss equation shows that
\Delta_{g}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Furthermore, Chern and Kuiper provided a complementary relation in
[
1
]
.
Namely, they showed that the ranks
\mu:=\dim(\Gamma)
and
\nu_{g}:=\dim(\Delta_{g})
are related by (
1
).
Straightforward computations show that if
\nu_{g}=\mu-p
then
M^{n}
is flat and
\nu_{g}=\mu-p=n-p
.
Proposition 7 of
[
4
]
analyzes the next case of the Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities in a restricted situation.
It shows that if
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
has
\nu_{g}=\mu-1=n-3
then
g
is locally a composition.
However, the authors’ approach seems difficult to generalize.
The first result of this work extends that proposition, and the generalization is in two directions.
We allow higher codimensions and do not impose a particular rank for the nullity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be a submanifold with
p\geq 2
and

\nu_{g}=\mu-p+1\leq n-p-1.

Then
g=G\circ\hat{g}
is a composition, where
G:N^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a flat submanifold and
\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n+1}
is an isometric embedding.
Moreover,
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=\Gamma
and
\nu_{G}=(n+1)-(p-1)
.

In particular, with Theorem
1.1
we characterize locally the submanifolds
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
with
\mu\neq\nu_{g}
.
Observe that the inequality condition in the last result is equivalent to
M^{n}
being nowhere flat.

Using our technique, we analyze the next case of Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities.
We show that if
p\geq 3
and
\nu_{g}=\mu-p+2\leq n-p-1
then, on connected components of a dense subset of
M^{n}
,
g
is also a composition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be an isometric immersion with
p\geq 3
and

\nu_{g}=\mu-p+2\leq n-p-1.

Let
U
be a connected component of an open dense subset of
M^{n}
where
(p-\ell):=\dim(\mathcal{S}(\alpha|_{TM\times\Gamma}))
is constant.
Then
\ell\in\{1,2\}
and
g|_{U}=G\circ\hat{g}
is a composition, where
G:N^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a flat submanifold and
\hat{g}:U\subseteq M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n+\ell}
is an isometric embedding.
Moreover,
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=\Gamma
and
\nu_{G}\in\{(n+1)-(p-j)\}_{j=\ell}^{2}
.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall flat bilinear forms, properties of the nullities, and ruled extensions, among others.
Section
3
is devoted to analyzing the submanifolds with
\nu_{g}\neq\mu
.
More precisely, is divided into subsections to analyze each possible value of
\mu-\nu_{g}
.
Lastly, in Section
4
, we give some final comments about this work.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the main techniques used in this article. Firstly, we discuss the basic properties of bilinear forms.
Then, we analyze the two principal distributions of this work, which are the nullity and the relative nullity.
The final subsection summarizes the properties of ruled extensions.

2.1
Flat bilinear forms

Given a bilinear map
\beta:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
between real vector spaces, set

\mathcal{S}(\beta)=\text{span}\{\beta(X,Y):X\in\mathbb{V},Y\in\mathbb{U}\}\subseteq\mathbb{W}.

The (left)
nullity
of
\beta
is the vector subspace

\Delta_{\beta}=\{X\in\mathbb{V}:\beta(X,Y)=0\,,\,\forall Y\in\mathbb{U}\}\subseteq\mathbb{V}.

For each
Y\in\mathbb{U}
we denote by
\beta^{Y}:\mathbb{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
the linear map defined by
\beta^{Y}\hskip-2.84544pt(X)=\beta(X,Y)
.
Let

\text{Re}(\beta)=\{Y\in\mathbb{U}:\dim(\text{Im}(\beta^{Y}))\text{ is maximal}\}

be the set of (right)
regular elements of

\beta
, which is open and dense in
\mathbb{U}
.
There are similar definitions for left regular elements and right nullity.

Assume now that
\mathbb{W}
has a positive definite inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
\beta
is
\mathit{flat}
if

\langle\beta(X,Y),\beta(Z,W)\rangle=\langle\beta(X,W),\beta(Z,Y)\rangle\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X,Z\in\mathbb{V}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Y,W\in\mathbb{U}.

The next result is due to Moore in
[
16
]
.
It lets us determine the nullity of a flat bilinear form.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
be a flat bilinear form.
If
Z_{0}\in\mathbb{U}
is a right regular element, then
\Delta_{\beta}=\ker(\beta^{Z_{0}})
.
In particular,

\dim(\Delta_{\beta})=\dim(\mathbb{V})-\dim(\mathrm{Im}(\beta^{Z_{0}}))\geq\dim(\mathbb{V})-\dim(\mathbb{W})
.

2.2
Intrinsic and relative nullities

We describe now the two main distributions of this work, the nullity of a Riemannian manifold and the relative nullity of a submanifold.

Given a Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
and
x\in M^{n}
, the
nullity of

M^{n}

at

x
is the nullity of its curvature tensor
R
at
x
, that is, the subspace of
T_{x}M
given by

\Gamma(x)=\{X\in T_{x}M:R(X,Y)=0,\forall Y\in T_{x}M\}.

The
rank of

M^{n}

at

x
is defined by
n-\mu
, where
\mu=\dim(\Gamma(x))
.
As the results that we are looking for are of local nature and our subspaces are all either kernels or images of smooth tensor fields, without further notice we will always work on each connected component of an open dense subset of
M^{n}
where all these dimensions are constant and thus all the subbundles are smooth.
In particular, we assume that
\mu
is constant and hence the second Bianchi identity implies that
\Gamma
is a totally geodesic distribution, namely,
\nabla_{\Gamma}\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma
.

For an isometric immersion
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
we denote by
\alpha^{g}:TM\times TM\rightarrow T^{\perp}_{g}M
its second fundamental form.
We define the
relative nullity of

g

at

x
as the nullity of
\alpha^{g}(x)
, that is,
\Delta_{g}(x):=\Delta_{\alpha^{g}}
.
The
rank of

g
is the number
n-\nu_{g}
, where
\nu_{g}=\dim(\Delta_{g})
.
Gauss equation implies that
\Delta_{f}\subseteq\Gamma
, while Codazzi equation implies that it is a totally geodesic distribution of
M^{n}
.

The isometric immersion
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is said to be
R^{d}
-ruled
, if
R^{d}\subseteq TM
is a
d
-dimensional totally geodesic distribution whose leaves are mapped by
g
onto (open subsets of) affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
.

2.3
Revisiting ruled extensions

Given a submanifold
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with
\mu\neq\nu_{g}\leq n-p-1
, we want to describe
g
as a composition
G\circ\hat{g}
, where
G:N^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is flat as in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
For this, we will use ruled extensions.
The present subsection describes the basic properties of these extensions, many of which are already present in the literature; see
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
for example.

In order to describe
g
as such a composition, the first step is to find a rank
\ell
subbundle
L=L^{\ell}\subseteq T^{\perp}_{g}M
to be a candidate of normal bundle of
\hat{g}
.
Then, we consider the tensor
\phi:=\phi_{L}:TM\times(TM\oplus L)\rightarrow L^{\perp}
given by

\phi(X,v)=(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}v)_{L^{\perp}},

(2)

where
\tilde{\nabla}
is the connection of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
and the subindex denotes the orthogonal projection on the respective subspace, that is,
L^{\perp}
.
Proposition 17 of
[
11
]
shows the importance of this tensor for our work.
Namely, the flatness of
\phi
is equivalent to the local existence of an isometric immersion
\hat{g}:U\subseteq M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}
whose normal bundle is
L
(up to a parallel identification), and its second fundamental form is the orthogonal projection of
\alpha^{g}
onto
L
.
However, meaningful cases also occur when
\phi
is non-necessarily flat, as shown by Theorem
3.10
.

Consider the covariant derivative of
\phi
as

(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(Y,v):=\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}(\phi(Y,v))\big)_{L^{\perp}}-\phi(\nabla_{X}Y,v)-\phi(Y,(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}v)_{TM\oplus L}).

Notice that

\displaystyle(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(Y,v)-(\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\phi)(X,v)

\displaystyle=\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}(\phi(Y,v)\big)_{L^{\perp}}-\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}(\phi(X,v))\big)_{L^{\perp}}-\phi([X,Y],v)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\phi(X,(\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}v)_{TM\oplus L})-\phi(Y,(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}v)_{TM\oplus L})

\displaystyle=\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}v-\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\tilde{\nabla}_{X}v-\tilde{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}v\big)_{L^{\perp}},

but the curvature of the ambient space is zero, so
\phi
satisfies the following Codazzi equation

(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(Y,v)=(\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\phi)(X,v),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X,Y\in TM,\,\forall v\in TM\oplus L.

(3)

We denote by
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}
and
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
the left and right nullities of
\phi
respectively.
Certainly,
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
.
Moreover, Codazzi equation (
3
) implies that
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq TM
is integrable and
\tilde{\nabla}_{\Delta_{\phi}^{l}}\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\subseteq\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
.
In particular, if
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
then
g
is
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}
-ruled.

Given such
\phi
, we define the
curvature of
\phi
as the tensor
R_{\phi}
given by

R_{\phi}(X,Y,v,w)=\langle\phi(X,w),\phi(Y,v)\rangle-\langle\phi(X,v),\phi(Y,w)\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X,Y\in TM,\,\forall v,w\in TM\oplus L.

In particular,
\phi
is flat when its curvature is zero.
Intuitively,
\phi
and
R_{\phi}
are the second fundamental form and curvature of the extension respectively.

The curvature of
\phi
satisfies the following Bianchi identities.

Lemma 2.2

(Bianchi’s identities)
.

The curvature of
\phi
satisfies the following first and second Bianchi identities

\sum R_{\phi}(S,T,U,v)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall S,T,U\in TM,\,\forall v\in TM\oplus L,

(4)

\sum(\nabla_{S}R_{\phi})(T,U,v,w)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall S,T,U\in TM,\,\forall v,w\in TM\oplus L.

(5)

where the sum denotes the cyclic sum over
S,T,
and
U
.

Proof.

The first Bianchi identity comes from opening the curvatures and simplifying terms.

To prove the second identity, we make the computations at a fixed point
q\in M^{n}
, and we take smooth sections such that the derivatives between
S,T,U,v
, and
w
are zero at
q
, that is,
(\nabla_{S}T)(q)=0
,
(\tilde{\nabla}_{S}v)_{TM\oplus L}(q)=0
, and so on.
Denote by
B
the left-hand side of (
5
), and notice that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sum S\big(R_{\phi}(T,U,v,w)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum\Big[\langle(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(T,w),\phi(U,v)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\phi(T,w),(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(U,v)\rangle\Big]\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\sum\Big[\langle(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(T,v),\phi(U,w)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\phi(T,v),(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(U,w)\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum\Big[\langle(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(T,w),\phi(U,v)\rangle\hskip-1.70717pt-\hskip-1.70717pt\langle\phi(T,v),(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(U,w)\rangle\Big]\hskip-1.42262pt+\hskip-1.42262pt\sum\Big[\langle\phi(T,w),(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(U,v)\rangle\hskip-1.70717pt-\hskip-1.70717pt\langle(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(T,v),\phi(U,w)\rangle\Big]\hskip-0.56905pt,

rearranging the terms of both sums we get

B=\sum\big[\langle(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(T,w)-(\overline{\nabla}_{T}\phi)(S,w),\phi(U,v)\rangle\big]+\sum\big[\langle\phi(T,w),(\overline{\nabla}_{S}\phi)(U,v)-(\overline{\nabla}_{U}\phi)(S,v)\rangle\big],

which is zero since
\phi
satisfies Codazzi equation (
3
).
∎

We denote by
\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
and
\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
the left and right nullities of
R_{\phi}
, that is

\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}:=\{X\in TM|\,R_{\phi}(X,TM,TM\oplus L,TM\oplus L)=0\}\subseteq TM,

and

\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}:=\{v\in TM\oplus L|\,R_{\phi}(TM,TM,TM\oplus L,v)=0\}\subseteq TM\oplus L.

Certainly,
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
and
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
.
The first Bianchi identity (
4
) shows that
\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
.
Moreover, the second one implies that
\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq TM
is integrable and
\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}}\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}\big)_{TM\oplus L}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
.
In particular,
\tilde{\nabla}_{\Delta_{\phi}^{l}}\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
.

Consider the vector bundle
\Lambda:=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap(\Delta_{\phi}^{l})^{\perp}\subseteq TM\oplus L
, and suppose that
\text{rank}(\Lambda)=\ell=\text{rank}(L)
.
The
ruled extension

G:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
of
g
is given by

G(\xi_{q})=g(p)+\xi_{q},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall q\in M^{n},\,\forall\xi_{q}\in\Lambda_{q}

We restrict
G
to a neighborhood
N^{n+\ell}
of the zero section
\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n+\ell}\subseteq\Lambda
in order to
G
being an immersion.
Moreover, we endow
N^{n+\ell}
with the induced metric by
G
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
and
\rank(\Lambda)=\ell=\rank(L)
.
Then
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
is the nullity of
G
, that is,
\Delta_{G}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
up to a parallel identification along
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}
.
Similarly, the nullity of
N^{n+\ell}
is given by
\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
.

Proof.

First,
G
is
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
-ruled since
\tilde{\nabla}_{\Delta_{\phi}^{l}}\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\subseteq\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
.
Take a section
\xi
of
N^{n+\ell}\subseteq\Lambda
and
Y\in TM
, then

G_{*}(\xi_{*}Y)=g_{*}Y+\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi\in TM\oplus L=G_{*}(TN).

Notice that
TM\oplus L
is parallel along
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
since
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
, so
\Lambda\subseteq\Delta_{G}
.
As
TN\cong TM\oplus\Lambda
, to compute the second fundamental form of
G
is enough to understand
\alpha^{G}|_{TM\times(TM\oplus L)}
.
If
X\in TM
then

\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\big(G_{*}(\xi_{*}Y)\big)=g_{*}\nabla_{X}Y+\alpha(X,Y)+\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi,

so

\alpha^{G}(X,\xi_{*}Y)=\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\big(G_{*}(\xi_{*}Y)\big)\big)_{L^{\perp}}=\big(\alpha(X,Y)\big)_{L^{\perp}}+\big(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi\big)_{L^{\perp}}=\phi(X,Y)+\phi(X,\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi)=\phi(X,\xi_{*}Y),

up to parallel identifications.
This proves that
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}=\Delta_{G}
.

Finally, Gauss equation shows that the curvature tensor
R_{N}
of
N^{n+\ell}
is given by

R_{N}(X,Y,v,w)=\langle\alpha^{G}(X,w),\alpha^{G}(Y,v)\rangle-\langle\alpha^{G}(X,v),\alpha^{G}(Y,w)\rangle=R_{\phi}(X,Y,v,w),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X,Y\in TM,\,\forall v,w\in TM\oplus L,

which shows that
\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
is the nullity of
N^{n+\ell}
since the remaining values of
R_{N}
involve terms of relative nullity.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We can give a weaker version of the last proposition for
0\leq\rank(\Lambda)<\text{rank}(L)
.
In that case, there is an orthogonal decomposition
T_{G}^{\perp}N=\mathcal{L}\oplus E
such that
\text{rank}(\mathcal{L})=\text{rank}(L)-\rank(\Lambda)
,
G
is
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
-ruled, and this distribution coincides with the nullity of the
E
-component of
\alpha^{G}
.

3
Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities

In this section, we describe the basic properties of the submanifolds
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
whose relative nullity
\Delta_{g}
does not coincide with the intrinsic nullity
\Gamma
.
In the following subsections, we analyze the cases
\nu_{g}=\mu-p
,
\nu_{g}=\mu-p+1
, and
\nu_{g}=\mu-p+2
respectively.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be a submanifold with non-trivial intrinsic nullity
\Gamma\neq 0
.
Call
\alpha
its second fundamental form and
\Delta_{g}
its relative nullity.
Gauss equation implies that
\Delta_{g}\subseteq\Gamma
and the flatness of the bilinear tensor
\beta:=\alpha|_{TM\times\Gamma}
.
Let
\Delta_{\beta}
be the (left) nullity of
\beta
.
The flatness of
\beta
implies that

\alpha(Y,X)\in\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Y\in\Delta_{\beta},\,\forall X\in TM.

(6)

So in particular

\alpha(Y,X)\in\mathcal{S}(\beta)\cap\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp}=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Y\in\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Gamma,\,\forall X\in TM,

which shows that
\Delta_{g}=\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Gamma
.
Then, we have the following relation

\nu_{g}+\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\mu.

(7)

Notice that
\Delta_{\beta}\subseteq TM
is an integrable distribution.
Indeed, Codazzi equation for
T_{1},T_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta}
gives

\alpha([T_{1},T_{2}],Z)=\alpha(T_{1},\nabla_{T_{2}}Z)-\alpha(T_{2},\nabla_{T_{1}}Z),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z\in\Gamma,

but the left-hand side belongs to
\mathcal{S}(\beta)
and the right-hand side to
\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp}
by (
6
), so
[T_{1},T_{2}]\in\Delta_{\beta}
.

Let us recall Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities, and provide a quick proof.

Proposition 3.1

(Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities
[
1
]
)
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be a submanifold, then (
1
) holds.

Proof.

As
\Delta_{g}\subseteq\Gamma
then
\nu_{g}\leq\mu
.
Take
Z_{0}\in\text{Re}(\beta)\subseteq\Gamma
a (right) regular element of
\beta
, then by Lemma
2.1
and (
7
) we get that

\nu_{g}+n\geq\nu_{g}+\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\mu=n-\dim(\text{Im}(\beta^{Z_{0}}))+\mu\geq n-p+\mu.

(8)

which proves the second inequality of (
1
).
∎

Before analyzing the inequality cases of the Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities, we present a result that gives us bounds for the rank of
\mathcal{S}(\beta)
under the hypothesis of
\nu_{g}\leq n-p-1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be a submanifold with
\nu_{g}\leq n-p-1
.
Then

\mu-\nu_{g}\leq\dim\big(\mathcal{S}(\beta)\big)\leq p-1.

(9)

Proof.

The first inequality comes from (
7
) and Lemma
2.1
since

n+\nu_{g}\geq\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)+\nu_{g}=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\mu\geq n-\dim\big(\mathcal{S}(\beta)\big)+\mu.

On the other hand, suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{S}(\beta)=T^{\perp}_{g}M
. Then, by
(
6
), we have that
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{g}
.
However, in this case, Lemma
2.1
implies that

\nu_{g}=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})\geq n-\dim\big(\mathcal{S}(\beta)\big)=n-p,

which is absurd.
∎

3.1
The case
\nu_{g}=\mu-p

In this subsection, we analyze the maximal case of Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities.
We also describe the technique that will be used for the following cases.

The next result shows that only flat submanifolds attain the second inequality of (
1
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be a submanifold with

\mu=\nu_{g}+p.

(10)

Then
M^{n}
is flat, in particular
\mu=\nu_{g}+p=n
.

Proof.

In this case we must have equalities in (
8
), hence
\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma=TM
and
\text{Im}(\beta^{Z_{0}})=\mathcal{S}(\beta)=T^{\perp}_{g}M
.
Then (
6
) implies that
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{g}\subseteq\Gamma
, and thus
\Gamma=\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma=TM
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

There are natural parametrizations for flat submanifolds attaining (
10
); see
[
7
]
for
p=1
and
[
10
]
for
p=2
.
This is generalized in
[
15
]
for any
p\leq n
.

Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities and Proposition
3.3
characterize the hypersurfaces with
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
by means of the Gauss parametrization.
Hence, we assume from now on that
p\geq 2
.

There is a natural way to produce submanifolds
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
using compositions.
Consider a submanifold
\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}
with
\Gamma=\Delta_{\hat{g}}\neq 0
,
\ell<p
, and let
G:U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be an isometric immersion of an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell}
with
\hat{g}(M^{n})\subseteq U
.
Then
g:=G\circ\hat{g}
generically has less nullity than
\hat{g}
, so
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
.
Conversely, we will use the following strategy to prove that such a
g
must be a composition.
Naively,
\mathcal{S}(\beta)
should be
T^{\perp}_{j}U
(or, at least, contained), and so
L:=\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp}\subseteq T^{\perp}_{g}M
is a candidate to be
T^{\perp}_{\hat{g}}M
.
Hence, we can use the techniques of Section
2.3
.
Namely, we will study the properties of the tensor
\phi=\phi_{L}
given by (
2
) associated with
L
, then we will use Proposition
2.3
to obtain the desired composition.

3.2
The case
\nu_{g}=\mu-p+1

This subsection is dedicated to analyzing the following case of Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities.
We will prove a more general statement.
We characterize the submanifolds such that the first inequality of Lemma
3.2
is attained; they are all flat compositions.

Suppose that
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a submanifold with
\mu=\nu_{g}+p-1
and
p\geq 2
.
Lemma
3.2
implies that

\dim\big(\mathcal{S}(\beta)\big)=\mu-\nu_{g}=p-1.

In particular, we are in a situation where the lower bound of (
9
) is attained.
The following result analyzes this equality in complete generality.
Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence of it.

Theorem 3.5
.

Consider a submanifold
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
.
Let
\beta=\alpha|_{TM\times\Gamma}
and suppose that

p-\ell:=\dim\big(\mathcal{S}(\beta)\big)=\mu-\nu_{g}<p.

Then
g=G\circ\hat{g}
is a composition, where
G:N^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a flat submanifold and
\hat{g}:M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n+\ell}
is an isometric embedding.
Moreover,
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=\Gamma
and
\nu_{G}=(n+1)-(p-\ell)
.

Proof.

Let
Z_{0}\in\Gamma
be a (right) regular value of
\beta
.
Lemma
2.1
and (
7
) imply that

\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\dim(\Gamma)-\dim(\Delta_{g})=n-\dim(\text{Im}(\beta^{Z_{0}}))+\mu-\nu_{g}\geq n,

which shows that
\alpha(Z_{0},TM)=L^{\perp}
and
\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma=TM
.
In particular,
\mathcal{S}(\beta)=\mathcal{S}(\alpha|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma})
.

Let
L:=\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp}\subseteq T^{\perp}_{g}M
and consider the tensor
\phi=\phi_{L}
given by (
2
).
We will use Proposition
2.3
to prove that
g
is such a composition.
Hence, we need to show that
\phi
is flat,

\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{r})=\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{l})+\ell=n+\ell-(p-\ell),

and

\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM.

(11)

Notice that
\phi(\Delta_{\beta},TM)=0
by (
6
), and so
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
.
Moreover, if
Y\in\Delta_{\beta}
then Codazzi equation for
\xi\in L
and
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\Gamma
gives us that

\langle\phi(Y,\xi),\alpha(Z_{1},Z_{2})\rangle=\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{Y}\xi,\alpha(Z_{1},Z_{2})\rangle=-\langle\xi,(\nabla_{Y}^{\perp}\alpha)(Z_{1},Z_{2})\rangle=\langle\xi,\alpha(Y,\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{2})\rangle=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\Gamma,

since
\Gamma\subseteq TM
is totally geodesic.
Hence,
\phi(Y,\xi)=0
since
\mathcal{S}(\alpha|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma})=\mathcal{S}(\beta)=L^{\perp}
, and so
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{l}
.
This proves (
11
).

As
TM=\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma
and (
11
) holds, the flatness of
\phi
is equivalent to the flatness of
\phi|_{\Gamma\times(\Gamma\oplus L)}
.
Notice that
\phi|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}=\alpha|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
is flat by Gauss equation.
On the other hand, if
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}\in\Gamma
and
\xi\in L
then

\langle\phi(Z_{1},\xi),\phi(Z_{2},Z_{3})\rangle=\langle\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{1}}\xi,\alpha(Z_{2},Z_{3})\rangle=-\langle\xi,(\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{1}}\alpha)(Z_{2},Z_{3})\rangle,

which is symmetric in
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
by Codazzi equation.
Hence, to prove the flatness is enough to show that

\langle\phi(T_{1},\xi_{1}),\phi(tT_{2},\xi_{2})\rangle=\langle\phi(T_{1},\xi_{2}),\phi(T_{2},\xi_{1})\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall T_{1},T_{2}\in\Gamma,\,\forall\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L.

(12)

Notice first that the nullity of
\alpha|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
is
\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Gamma=\Delta_{g}
.
Thus,
\alpha|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}
is completely described by Theorem 2 of
[
16
]
.
Namely, there are vectors
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p-\ell}\in\Gamma\cap\Delta_{g}^{\perp}
such that
\alpha(Z_{i},Z_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
and the set

\{\rho_{i}:=\alpha(Z_{i},Z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{p-\ell}

is an orthonormal basis of
L^{\perp}
.
Given
\xi\in L
, Codazzi equation implies that

\langle\phi(Z_{i},\xi),\rho_{j}\rangle=-\langle\xi,(\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{i}}\alpha)(Z_{j},Z_{j})\rangle=\langle\xi,\nabla^{\perp}_{Z_{j}}(\alpha(Z_{i},Z_{j}))-\alpha(\nabla_{Z_{j}}Z_{i},Z_{j})-\alpha(Z_{i},\nabla_{Z_{j}}Z_{j})\rangle=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall i\neq j.

Then
\phi(Z_{i},\xi)=\lambda_{i}(\xi)\rho_{i}
for some 1-forms
\lambda_{i}:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Then (
12
) holds since
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p-\ell}\}
is a basis of
\Gamma
and

\langle\phi(Z_{i},\xi_{1}),\phi(Z_{j},\xi_{2})\rangle=\delta_{ij}\lambda_{i}(\xi_{1})\lambda_{j}(\xi_{2}),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall i,j,\,\forall\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L.

Finally, by Lemma
2.1
, we have for
Z_{0}\in\Gamma
a regular element of
\beta
that

\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{r})=n+\ell-\dim\text{Im}(\phi^{Z_{0}})=(n+\ell)-(p-\ell)=n-\dim\text{Im}(\beta^{Z_{0}})+\ell=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\ell=\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{l})+\ell.

The result now follows from Proposition
2.3
.
Notice that the second fundamental form of
\hat{g}
is the orthogonal projection of
\alpha
onto
L
, but as
\alpha(\Gamma,TM)\in L^{\perp}
then
\Gamma=\Delta_{\hat{g}}
.
∎

We can describe locally all the submanifolds
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
with
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
be a submanifold with
\Gamma\neq\Delta_{g}
.
Then, on each connected component
U
of an open dense subset of
M^{n}
, we have one of the following possibilities:

1.

\mu=\nu_{g}+1
and
g|_{U}=j\circ\hat{g}
is a composition where
\hat{g}:U\rightarrow V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
j:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
are isometric immersions with
\Gamma=\Delta_{\hat{g}}
;

2.

\mu=\nu_{g}+2
and
U
is flat.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

By Proposition
3.3
, and Theorem
1.1
, it only remains to analyze the case
\mu=n=\nu_{g}+1
.
However, this case is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Each case of Theorem
3.6
is naturally parametrizable.
For
(i)
we use the Gauss parametrization described in
[
7
]
, and Corollary 18 of
[
10
]
describes the second case.

3.3
The case
\nu_{g}=\mu-p+2

In this final subsection, we discuss the next case of Chern-Kuiper’s inequalities.
For this, we prove Theorem
3.10
which analyzes in generality the case
\ell=1
.
This result and Theorem
3.5
imply Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
3.5
describes the submanifolds that attain the first inequality of (
9
).
We now analyze when the second one does.
Namely, let us consider
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
a submanifold with
\Delta_{g}\neq\Gamma
and suppose that
L:=\mathcal{S}(\beta)^{\perp}
has rank
\ell=1
.
As before, consider
\phi=\phi_{L}
the tensor given by (
2
).
We begin with the next result.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
L
has rank
1
and

\nu_{g}\leq n-p-1
then
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
.
Furthermore, if
\alpha(\Delta_{\beta},\Delta_{\beta})\neq 0
then
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
.

Proof.

On the second Bianchi identity (
5
), take
S=Z\in\Gamma
,
T=d_{1},v=d_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta}
, and
w\in TM
to obtain

0=R_{\phi}(Z,d_{1},\alpha(U,d_{2}),w)+R_{\phi}(U,Z,\alpha(d_{1},d_{2}),w)\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z\in\Gamma,\,\forall d_{1},d_{2}\in\Gamma,\,\forall w\in TM.

(13)

In the last equation, fix
d_{1}
and choose
0\neq d_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Delta_{g}^{\perp}
such that
\hat{\alpha}(d_{1},d_{2})=0
.
This is possible since
\ell=1
and

\dim(\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Delta_{g}^{\perp})=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})-\dim(\Delta_{g})\geq n-(p-1)-(n-p-1)=2,

where the last inequality comes from Lemma
2.1
.
Let
\rho\in L
be a fixed unit generator of
L
and take
U\in TM
such that
\rho=\hat{\alpha}(U,d_{2})
to obtain

0=R_{\phi}(Z,d_{1},\rho,w)=\langle\phi(Z,w),\phi(d_{1},\rho)\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z\in\Gamma,\,\forall d_{1}\in\Delta_{\beta},\,\forall w\in TM,

but
\phi(\Gamma,TM)=\beta(TM,\Gamma)=L^{\perp}
, so
\phi(d_{1},\rho)=0
for any
d_{1}\in\Delta_{\beta}
.
Thus

\Delta_{\beta}\subseteq\Delta_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM\subseteq\Delta_{\beta}.

Finally, suppose that
\alpha(\Delta_{\beta},\Delta_{\beta})\neq 0
.
Then
\alpha(\Delta_{\beta},\Delta_{\beta})=L
by (
6
).
Take
d_{1},d_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta}
such that
\alpha(d_{1},d_{2})=\rho
, and use them in (
13
) to obtain

0=R_{\phi}(Z,U,\rho,w),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z\in\Gamma,\,\forall U,w\in TM,

which proves that
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
since
R_{\phi}(\Gamma,TM,TM,TM)=0
by Gauss equation.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Lemma
3.8
holds under the weaker assumption of
\dim(\Delta_{\beta}\cap\Delta_{g}^{\perp})\geq 2
instead of
\nu_{g}\leq n-p-1
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
be an isometric immersion with

\mu\neq\mu_{g}\leq n-p-1.

Suppose that
L:=\mathcal{S}(\alpha|_{TM\times\Gamma})^{\perp}\subseteq T^{\perp}_{g}M
has rank
1
.
Then, on each connected component
U
of an open dense subset of
M^{n}
where
\mu
,
\nu_{g}
, and
k=\dim\alpha(\Delta_{\beta},\Delta_{\beta})
are constant, we have the following possibilities

1.

k=1
and
g|_{U}
is a composition of a ruled extension
G:N^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
and an isometric embedding
\hat{g}:U\subseteq M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n+1}
.
Moreover,
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=\Gamma
,

(n+1)-(p-1)\leq\nu_{G}\leq(n+1)-(\mu-\nu_{g}),

and
\hat{g}_{*}(\Gamma)\subseteq\hat{\Gamma}
, where
\hat{\Gamma}\subseteq TN
is the nullity of
N^{n+1}
and satisfies that
\dim(\hat{\Gamma})\geq\mu-\nu_{g}+\nu_{G}
;

2.

k=0
and
g
is
\Delta_{\beta}
-ruled.
Moreover, the rank of the ruling is at least
(n-p+1)
.

Proof.

We want to use Proposition
2.3
to prove this result.
By Lemma
3.8
we know that
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
.

Suppose first that
k=1
, and so
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
by Lemma
3.8
.
This implies that the tensor
\hat{\beta}:(TM\oplus L)\times\Gamma\rightarrow L^{\perp}
given by
\hat{\beta}(v,Z)=\phi(Z,v)
is a flat extension of
\beta
.
Notice that the left nullity of
\hat{\beta}
coincides with
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
.
Indeed, let us verify the non-trivial contention.
Take
v_{0}\in TM\oplus L
such that
\hat{\beta}(\Gamma,v_{0})=0
.
Then, as
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}
, we have that

0=R_{\phi}(Z,X,v_{0},w)=\langle\phi(X,v_{0}),\phi(Z,w)\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall Z\in\Gamma,\,\forall X,w\in TM,

but
\phi(\Gamma,TM)=L^{\perp}
, and so
v_{0}\in\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
.
In particular, Lemma
2.1
for
\beta
and
\hat{\beta}
shows that the dimensions of
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
and
\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\beta}
differ by at most
1
.
However, if
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{l}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}
then
g
would be
\Delta_{\beta}
-ruled which is absurd since
k=1
.
Proposition
2.3
shows that
g
has a ruled extension
G:N^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
.
Moreover, Lemma
2.1
implies that the nullity of
G
satisfies that

\nu_{G}=\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{r})=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+1\geq n+1-(p-1).

On the other hand, using (
7
) we get that

\nu_{G}=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+1=\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)+\nu_{g}+1-\mu\leq n+1-(\mu-\nu_{g}).

The bound on the nullity of
N^{n+1}
follows from (
7
) and
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{l}\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}^{r}
since

\dim(\hat{\Gamma})=\dim(\Gamma_{\phi}^{r})\geq\dim(\Delta_{\phi}^{r}+\Gamma)=1+\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)=1+\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\mu-\nu_{g}=\nu_{G}+\mu-\nu_{g}.

Finally, suppose that
k=0
, that is,
\alpha(\Delta_{\beta},\Delta_{\beta})=0
.
Codazzi equation (
2
) for
d_{1},d_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta}
give us that

0=(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(d_{1},d_{2})-(\overline{\nabla}_{d_{1}}\phi)(X,d_{2})=\phi(X,\nabla_{d_{1}}d_{2}),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X\in TM,\,\forall d_{1},d_{2}\in\Delta_{\beta},

which proves that
\Delta_{\beta}
is totally geodesic since
\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM=\Delta_{\beta}
.
Hence,
g
is
\Delta_{\beta}
-ruled, and
Lemma
2.1
gives the desired bound on the rank of the rulings.
∎

We prove now Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
3.2
we know that
\ell\in\{1,2\}
.
The case
\ell=2
follows from Theorem
3.5
.
Assume now that
\ell=1
, so we can apply Theorem
3.10
.
Then, if
k=1
,
N^{n+1}
must be flat since

\dim(\hat{\Gamma})\geq p-2+\nu_{G}\geq p-2+(n+1)-(p-1)=n,

but
\dim(\hat{\Gamma})=n
is not possible by the symmetries of the curvature tensor, so
\hat{\Gamma}=TN
.
It remains to exclude the second possibility of that result, that is,
k=0
.
Suppose, by contradiction, that
\ell=1
and
g
is
\Delta_{g}
-ruled on an open subset of
M^{n}
.
Notice that as
\phi|_{TM\times\Gamma}=\beta
is flat and
\Delta_{\beta}=\Delta_{\phi}^{r}\cap TM
, so
\phi|_{TM\times(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)}
is flat.
However, by (
7
) and Lemma
2.1
we know that

\dim(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma)=\dim(\Delta_{\beta})+\mu-\nu_{g}\geq n-(p-1)+p-2=n-1,

so
\phi|_{TM\times TM}
must be flat.
Then, fixing a unit generator
\rho
of
L
, the shape operator
A=A_{\rho}
satisfies Gauss equation.
However, as
g
is
\Delta_{\beta}
-ruled and
A\Gamma=0
, we have that

\langle A(\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma),\Delta_{\beta}+\Gamma\rangle=0,

which implies that
\mu=\ker(A)\geq n-2
.
This is a contradiction since
\mu=\nu_{g}+p-2\leq(n-p-1)+(p-2)=n-3
.
∎

4
Final comments

In this final section, we give some observations of this work.

The results of this work suggest that there are at least two distinct families of submanifolds
g:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
with
\nu_{g}\neq\mu
.
The submanifolds with rank greater than their codimension and those that do not.
Moreover, Theorems
3.5
and
3.10
suggest that, aside from the ruled cases, any submanifold of the first class is contained in a submanifold of the second one.

In the submanifold theory, there are many works in which it is necessary to exclude compositions of the form
g=G\circ\hat{g}
where
\hat{g}:N^{n+\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
is a flat submanifold; see
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
for example.
Moreover, the notion of
honest deformation
is to exclude this type of behavior in the deformation theory; see
[
10
]
.
This concept seems to be related to our work, but we did not deal with deformations.
For this reason, it may be more appropriate a notion of honesty that depends only on the submanifold itself.

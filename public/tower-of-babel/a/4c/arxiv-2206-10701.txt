Boundary null controllability for the heat equation with dynamic boundary conditions
S. E. Chorfi, G. El Guermai, A. Khoutaibi, L. Maniar

Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with the boundary controllability of heat equation with dynamic boundary conditions. More precisely, we prove that the equation is null controllable at any positive time by means of a boundary control supported on an arbitrary subboundary. The proof of the main result combines a new boundary Carleman estimate and some regularity estimates for the adjoint system, with an explicit dependence with respect to the final time. This technique allows us to overcome a new difficulty that arises when absorbing a normal derivative term.

1.
Introduction

Let us fix
T>0
and an integer
N\geq 2
. Throughout the paper,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
of class
C^{3,1}
and
\Gamma^{0}\subset\Gamma
a nonempty relatively open subset of
\Gamma
. Let us set

E_{T}=(0,T)\times E\qquad\text{ for all }E\subset\overline{\Omega}.

Then, we consider the following heat equation with dynamic boundary conditions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}y-d\Delta y=f(t,x),&\qquad\text{ in }\Omega_{T},\\
&\partial_{t}y_{\Gamma}-\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}+d\partial_{\nu}y=g(t,x),&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&y_{\Gamma}(t,x)=y_{|\Gamma}(t,x),&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&(y,y_{\Gamma})\rvert_{t=0}=(y_{0},y_{0,\Gamma}),&\Omega\times\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(1)

where
d,\delta>0
are positive real numbers,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
and
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{T})
are the source terms,
\partial_{\nu}y:=(\nabla y\cdot\nu)_{\mid\Gamma}
is the normal derivative, where
\nu
is the outer unit normal field on
\Gamma
exterior to
\Omega
. Further, we denote by
y_{\Gamma}:=y_{\mid\Gamma}
the trace of
y
. Finally,

\Delta
is the Laplace operator,
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the Riemannian submanifold
\Gamma
(see
[
29
]
), and
y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),y_{0,\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
are initial states, which are not necessarily interrelated. The term
d\partial_{\nu}y
represents the interaction domain-boundary, while
\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}
stands for a boundary diffusion.

The aim of this paper is to prove the boundary null controllability of the system (
1
). Our main result relies on a new boundary Carleman estimate for the adjoint system associated to (
1
), with a local integral in terms of Dirichlet data localized in the arbitrary subboundary
\Gamma^{0}\subset\Gamma
.

Evolution equations with dynamic boundary conditions have attracted a considerable attention in last years, and have found applications in various mathematical models such as population dynamics and heat transfer, in particular when the diffusion process takes place in the interface between a solid and a moving fluid, see
[
30
,
12
,
25
]
and the references therein. The reader can be referred to the seminal paper
[
17
]
for the physical interpretation and derivation of such boundary conditions. We also refer to the recent paper
[
28
]
, where the derivation is given by employing the Carslaw-Jaeger relation. Many researches were done for the wellposedness and regularity results related to such equations, see
[
13
,
17
,
27
,
31
]
for the case of a scalar diffusion and without gradient terms, and recently
[
24
]
, in the presence of gradient terms. The general case of variable diffusion coefficients with gradient terms has been recently studied in
[
3
]
. Although the controllability of parabolic equations, by the Carleman estimate approach, is well studied in the case of static boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann or Fourier boundary conditions), see, e.g.,
[
8
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
, only some recent works concern the controllability and inverse problems in the dynamic boundary conditions case; see
[
1
,
2
,
7
]
.

In
[
23
,
27
]
, the authors have considered the distributed null controllability for (
1
), that is, the null controllability from an arbitrary subdomain
\omega\Subset\Omega
. The inverse source problem with interior measurements has been recently studied in a more general setting in
[
3
]
. In these works, we have adopted the interior weight function used in
[
16
]
for parabolic equations with classical boundary conditions. The aforementioned weight function is null on the boundary of the domain. This fact along with the nature of classical boundary conditions allow several boundary terms to be canceled. This is not the case in the present setting.

As for the standard (scalar) heat equation, a well-known result states that the boundary null controllability and distributed null controllability are equivalent (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
). This result is no longer valid for coupled parabolic systems. In our case, one can obtain the boundary null controllability from a distributed control by enlarging the domain
\Omega
. Nevertheless, this technique for dynamic boundary conditions requires more regular initial data than those in the Dirichlet or Neumann cases, see
[
27
, Theorem 4.5]
. More precisely, due to the use of Sobolev’s embedding and local regularity results, the authors take initial conditions such that
y_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega),\,{y_{0}}_{|\Gamma}\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
and
y_{0}\in W^{2-\frac{2}{p}}_{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1+\frac{N}{2}
. Our boundary Carleman estimate allows us to recover the boundary controllability result without assuming any further regularity assumption. Furthermore, our direct approach has the following extra advantages:

\bullet

we keep an explicit dependence of the constants in the boundary Carleman estimate with respect to the final time
T
. This fact is crucial when dealing with semilinear problems;

\bullet

we obtain an explicit constant, with respect to the final time
T
, of the boundary observability constant which is different from the interior observability constant obtained in
[
23
]
;

\bullet

we then obtain a continuous dependence of null controls and corresponding states with respect to initial data and source terms;

\bullet

our boundary Carleman estimate allows to obtain an explicit upper bound for the cost of boundary approximate controllability, i.e., an explicit bound for the size of the minimal norm control. We refer to
[
7
]
for the case of a distributed control;

\bullet

the techniques could seemingly be extended to deal with inverse problems with boundary measurements in the spirit of
[
1
,
3
]
. However, a careful analysis is needed to include a large class of source terms.

In comparison with the classical parabolic equations, where the control acts by means of the Dirichlet condition on a part of the boundary of the domain, the present Carleman inequality requires a different technique to absorb a term of Neumann type (in terms of the normal derivative) by the left-hand side of the Carleman estimate. Absorbing this term turns out to be a main difficulty that will be overcome with help of some parabolic regularity estimates with explicit constants in terms of the final time
T
(see Proposition
3
). Such regularity estimates can be very useful while doing Carleman estimates
[
5
]
. It is worth mentioning the paper
[
10
]
which is the first to deal with a Carleman inequality when a time derivative appears in the boundary condition (but without a boundary diffusion). However, it only consider the one dimensional case and point out the difficulty of the controllability in higher dimensions for such equations (see Section 4 in
[
10
]
).

In the subsequent sections, we typically use the following surface divergence theorem

\int_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}y\,z\,\mathrm{d}S=-\int_{\Gamma}\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}y,\nabla_{\Gamma}z\rangle_{\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}S,\qquad y\in H^{2}(\Gamma),\;z\in H^{1}(\Gamma),

(2)

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}
is the tangential gradient and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
is the Riemannian inner product of tangential vectors on
\Gamma

[
21
]
. To simplify notation, we will denote

\left\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f,\nabla_{\Gamma}g\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=\nabla_{\Gamma}f\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}g.

Also, we need the following identity which holds for general Riemannian manifolds. Its proof can be found in
[
6
]
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{\Gamma}f\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}g)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}f=\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}g\left(\nabla_{\Gamma}f,\nabla_{\Gamma}f\right)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma}g\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\left(\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}f\right|^{2}\right)

(3)

for
f\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
and
g\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
, where
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}
denotes the Hessian with respect to the Riemannian metric on
\Gamma
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some wellposedness and regularity results that will be useful in the sequel. In Section
3
, we prove a boundary Carleman estimate for the homogeneous adjoint system. Finally, in Section
4
, we apply the Carleman estimate to show the observability inequality, and consequently we obtain the boundary null controllability of the system (
1
).

2.
Wellposedness and regularity of the solution

In this section, we recall some wellposedness and regularity results related to system (
1
). The reader can refer to
[
27
]
for different notions of the solution as well as detailed proofs.

2.1.
Notations

First, we introduce the following real Hilbert space

\mathbb{L}^{2}:=L^{2}(\Omega,\mathrm{d}x)\times L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathrm{d}S),

equipped with the scalar product given by

\langle(y,y_{\Gamma}),(z,z_{\Gamma})\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=\langle y,z\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle y_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

where we denoted the Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
and the surface measure on
\Gamma
by
\mathrm{d}x
and
\mathrm{d}S
, respectively. We also define the spaces

\mathbb{H}^{k}:=\left\{(y,y_{\Gamma})\in H^{k}(\Omega)\times H^{k}(\Gamma)\colon y_{|\Gamma}=y_{\Gamma}\right\},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

which are Hilbert spaces when equipped with the standard product norm. Note that
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is a Hilbert space when equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\left\|\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right),\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\text{where }\left\langle\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right),\left(z,z_{\Gamma}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla y\cdot\nabla z\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}S.

Similarly,
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\left\|\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right),\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\text{where }\left\langle\left(y,y_{\Gamma}\right),\left(z,z_{\Gamma}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Delta y\Delta z\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,\mathrm{d}S.

For the regularity of the solutions, we introduce the following spaces

\mathbb{E}_{1}(t_{0},t_{1}):=H^{1}\left(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)\text{ for }t_{1}>t_{0}\text{ in }\mathbb{R},

\mathbb{E}_{2}(t_{0},t_{1}):=H^{1}\left(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)\cap H^{2}\left(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)\text{ for }t_{1}>t_{0}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}.

In particular,

\mathbb{E}_{1}:=\mathbb{E}_{1}(0,T)\;\text{ and }\;\mathbb{E}_{2}:=\mathbb{E}_{2}(0,T).

Finally, we recall that the system (
1
) is governed by the linear operator that is denoted by

\mathcal{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}d\Delta&0\\
-d\partial_{\nu}&\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}\end{array}\right),\qquad D(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{H}^{2}.

It is shown in
[
27
, Proposition 2.1]
that the operator
\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint dissipative and generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(\mathrm{e}^{t\mathcal{A}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{L}^{2}
.

2.2.
Regularity estimates

We first recall an elliptic regularity result, which will allow us to characterize the domain of
\mathcal{A}^{2}
and prove parabolic regularity later on. Consider the following elliptic problem

\begin{cases}d\Delta u=f,&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}u_{\Gamma}-d\partial_{\nu}u-u_{\Gamma}=g,&\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
u_{\Gamma}=u_{|\Gamma},&\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{cases}

(4)

The following result is a special case of
[
22
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Proposition 1
.

If
\mathcal{F}=(f,g)\in H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Gamma)
, then the solution of (
4
) satisfies
(u,u_{\Gamma})\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
and

\|(u,u_{\Gamma})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{4}}\leq C\|\mathcal{F}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Gamma)},

(5)

where
C=C(d,\delta)
is a positive constant.

Consequently, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

The domain of
\mathcal{A}^{2}
is given by

D\left(\mathcal{A}^{2}\right)=\left\{(u,u_{\Gamma})\in\mathbb{H}^{4}\colon(d\Delta u)\rvert_{\Gamma}=\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}u_{\Gamma}-d\partial_{\nu}u\right\},

which is a Hilbert space equipped with the standard inner product of
H^{4}(\Omega)\times H^{4}(\Gamma)
.

Now, let us consider the backward adjoint problem of (
1
), which is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{t}\varphi-d\Delta\varphi=0,&\qquad\text{ in }\Omega_{T},\\
&-\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}-\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}+d\partial_{\nu}\varphi=0,&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&\varphi_{\Gamma}(t,x)=\varphi_{|\Gamma}(t,x),&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&(\varphi(T,\cdot),\varphi_{\Gamma}(T,\cdot))=(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma}),&\Omega\times\Gamma\end{aligned}

(6)

for given
(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
. We recall the following result from
[
27
]
for future use.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
,
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{T})
,
Y_{0}:=(y_{0},y_{0,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
and
(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
. Then the following assertions are true.

(a)

There is a unique mild solution
Y:=(y,y_{\Gamma})\in C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})
of (
1
). Moreover,
Y
belongs to
\mathbb{E}_{1}(\tau,T)
and solves (
1
) strongly on
(\tau,T)
with initial data

, for each
\tau\in(0,T)
. This mild solution is given by

Y(t)=\mathrm{e}^{t\mathcal{A}}Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)\mathcal{A}}(f(s),g(s))\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in[0,T].

(b)

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all data the mild solution
Y
satisfies

\|Y\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leq C\left(\|Y_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{T})}\right).

(7)

(c)

If
Y_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, then the mild solution from (a) is the strong one.

(d)

The backward adjoint system (
6
) admits a unique mild solution given by
(\varphi(t),\varphi_{\Gamma}(t))=\mathrm{e}^{(T-t)\mathcal{A}}(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})
. It is the unique strong solution if
(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
.

In order to absorb the normal derivative term in the proof of the Carleman inequality and obtain an explicit observability constant in terms of
T
, we shall prove more accurate regularity estimates for the following adjoint system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hskip-5.40608pt-\partial_{t}\Phi(t)=\mathcal{A}\Phi(t)+\mathcal{F}(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
\hskip-5.40608pt\Phi(T)=(0,0),\end{array}\right.

(8)

where
\Phi:=(\varphi,\varphi_{\Gamma})
and
\mathcal{F}:=(f,g)
.

Proposition 3
.

The following assertions hold

(i)

if
\mathcal{F}\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)
, then the solution
\Phi
of (
8
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Phi\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}\right)}\leq C(1+T)\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)},

where
C>0
is independent of
T
and
\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{F}\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)
, then the solution
\Phi
of (
8
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Phi\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)\cap H^{1}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right),

(10)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}\leq C(1+T)\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)},

(iii)

if
\mathcal{F}\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)\cap H^{1}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)
and
\mathcal{F}(T)=(0,0)
, then the solution
\Phi
of (
8
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Phi\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4}\right)\cap H^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4}\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}\leq C(1+T)\left(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)}+\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}\right).

Proof.

By density, it suffices to consider a smooth source term so that the solution
\Phi
is smooth. Next, we focus on the regularity estimates.

(i)
Multiplying (
6
)
1
by
\varphi
and (
6
)
2
by
\varphi_{\Gamma}
and using Green formula and surface diverge formula, we obtain, for any
t\in(0,T)
, that

-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|\Phi(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+d\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S=\left\langle\mathcal{F}(t),\Phi(t)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

(12)

Integrating over
(t,T)
, we infer, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
t\in(0,T)
, that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathcal{F}(t),\Phi(t)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{F}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\|\Phi(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\|\Phi\|_{C\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Phi\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}.

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we find that

\|\Phi\|_{C\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}^{2}\leq C\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}.

Since

\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\leq T\|\Phi\|_{C\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}^{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\leq T\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2},

it follows that

\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leq CT\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}.

(13)

On the other hand, integrating (
12
) over
(0,T)
, we obtain

\min(d,\delta)\|(\nabla\varphi,\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathcal{F}(t),\Phi(t)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(14)

We have

2\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\mathcal{F}(t),\Phi(t)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}+\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}.

This inequality with (
13
)-(
14
), yield

\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}\leq C(1+T)\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}.

(ii)
For
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}\varphi+d\Delta\varphi)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-d\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2d\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}S+d^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}g^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}(\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}+\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}-d\partial_{\nu}\varphi)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt=\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S-\delta\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S-2d\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}S+\delta^{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle-2d\delta\int_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}S,

where we used Green’s formula and surface divergence formula. Adding up the two previous identities and integrating over
(0,T)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\delta)\left(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}^{2}+\|\varphi_{\Gamma}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2d\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}|\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}||\partial_{\nu}\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}S.

9

Fixing

Next, it suffices to estimate the normal derivative term so that

\|\partial_{\nu}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where

and C is a positive constant independent of T. Indeed, since
\Omega
is of class

, there exists
\mathbf{N}\in C^{2}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)^{N}
(see

\mathbf{N}(x)=\nu(x),\;x\in\Gamma,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|\mathbf{N}(x)|\leq 1,\;x\in\Omega.

Multiplying (
6
)
1
by
\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\Delta\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(15)

By Green’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\Delta\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{\nu}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{\nu}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{N}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{\nu}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{N}\;|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{N}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\partial_{\nu}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{N}\;|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{N}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(16)

where
\nabla\mathbf{N}=\left(\partial_{i}\mathbf{N}_{j}\right)
stands for the standard Jacobian matrix of

.

Using (

2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\nu}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{N}\;|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{N}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{\Omega}f\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{N}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Using Young’s inequality and the fact
\mathbf{N}\in C^{2}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)^{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
T
such that

\|\partial_{\nu}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

This completes the proof of
(ii)
.

(iii)
We set
\Psi=\partial_{t}\Phi
. Differentiating (
8
) in time
t
, we see that
\Psi
solves the same system (
8
) with source term
\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}
. Then by (ii), we have

\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}\leq C(1+T)\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)}.

(17)

On the other hand,
(\varphi,\varphi_{\Gamma})
solves the following problem

\begin{cases}d\Delta\varphi=-f-\partial_{t}\varphi,&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}-d\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\varphi_{\Gamma}=-g-\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}-\varphi_{\Gamma},&\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
\varphi_{\Gamma}=\varphi_{|\Gamma},&\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{cases}

Then inequality (
5
) implies that

\|\Phi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{4}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right).

Integrating over
(0,T)
and combining the resulting inequality with (
10
) and (
17
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 1

3.
The Carleman estimate

In this section, we prove a boundary Carleman estimate for the homogeneous backward adjoint problem (

In the following lemma, we recall a weight function needed for the global boundary Carleman estimate. We refer to
[
32

for a precise statement and also for the proof.

Lemma 3.1

(see
[
18
]
)
.

Given a nonempty relatively open set
\gamma\Subset\Gamma^{0}\Subset\Gamma

\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\hskip 17.07182pt\eta

\displaystyle>0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\;|\nabla\eta|

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}>0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\overline{\Omega},

\displaystyle\mathrm{(iii)}\;\,\partial_{\nu}\eta

\displaystyle\leq-c_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\setminus\gamma,

\displaystyle\mathrm{(iv)}\quad\;\,\eta

\displaystyle=0,\;\nabla_{\Gamma}\eta=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\setminus\gamma.

Let
\gamma\Subset\Gamma^{0}
be a relatively open and non-empty, and let
\eta
be as above. We then define the weight functions
\alpha
and
\xi
by

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t(T-t)},

\displaystyle t\in(0,T),

(19)

\displaystyle\alpha(t,x)

\displaystyle=\theta(t)\left(\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{\infty}}-\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\eta(x)}\right),

\displaystyle(t,x)\in\overline{\Omega}_{T},

\displaystyle\xi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\theta(t)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\eta(x)},

\displaystyle(t,x)\in\overline{\Omega}_{T},

•

On

Lemma 3.2

(Boundary Carleman estimate)
.

There exist two positive constants

that are independent of

such that the strong solution

of the backward system (

\displaystyle s^{-1}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi^{-1}\left(|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}+|\Delta\varphi|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+s\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+s^{-1}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi^{-1}\left(|\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}+|\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t+s\lambda\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi|\nabla_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t

In the following remark, we highlight a few facts about the boundary Carleman inequality (
3.2
).

Remark 2
.

It is worth to mention that:

•

In the boundary Carleman inequality for the classical heat equation, the local term

s\lambda\int_{\Gamma^{0}_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi(\partial_{\nu}\eta)|\partial_{\nu}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t

(21)

naturally appears in the right-hand side of the Carleman estimate, see, for instance,
[
9
, Théorème 3.4]
. A main difficulty in comparison with the classical boundary conditions is that, in our case, we must absorb the normal derivative term (
21
) by the left-hand side of (
3.2
). This will be done by using the regularity estimates stated in Section
2
. The underlying term has been easily transmitted to the left-hand side of (
3.2
) in the case of internal controllability, since in that case the weight function satisfies
\partial_{\nu}\eta^{0}<0
on whole
\Gamma

•

In contrast to the interior weight function used in
[
27
]
for dynamic boundary conditions, the boundary weight function
\eta
is not null on
\Gamma
, which makes the situation a bit more complicated by adding several new boundary terms in the proof of the Carleman estimate.

\displaystyle s\lambda\int_{\gamma_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi\left|\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t\leq s\lambda\int_{\Gamma_{T}^{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\chi\xi\left|\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+(4s^{2}\lambda^{2}\xi^{2}+2s\lambda^{2}\xi)(\nabla_{\Gamma}\eta\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\chi)\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi

4.
Boundary null controllability

In this section, we prove the null controllability of the following linear inhomogeneous system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}y-d\Delta y=f(t,x),&\qquad\text{ in }\Omega_{T},\\
&\partial_{t}y_{\Gamma}-\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}+d\partial_{\nu}y=v(t,x)\mathds{1}_{\Gamma^{0}}+g(t,x),&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&y_{\Gamma}(t,x)=y_{|\Gamma}(t,x),&\qquad\text{ on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&(y,y_{\Gamma})\rvert_{t=0}=(y_{0},y_{0,\Gamma}),&\Omega\times\Gamma.\end{aligned}

(58)

To this end, we shall first infer an observability estimate for the homogeneous backward system given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{t}\varphi-d\Delta\varphi=0,&\qquad\text{in }\Omega_{T},\\
&-\partial_{t}\varphi_{\Gamma}-\delta\Delta_{\Gamma}\varphi_{\Gamma}+d\partial_{\nu}\varphi=0,&\qquad\text{on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&\varphi_{|\Gamma}(t,x)=\varphi_{|\Gamma}(t,x),&\qquad\text{on }\Gamma_{T},\\
&(\varphi(T,\cdot),\varphi_{\Gamma}(T,\cdot))=(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma}),&\Omega\times\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(59)

by applying the Carleman estimate (
3.2
).

Proposition 4
.

There exists a constant
C=C\left(\Omega,\Gamma^{0}\right)>0
such that, for all
(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
, the mild solution

(\varphi,\varphi_{\Gamma})
of the backward problem (
59
) satisfies

\|\varphi(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi_{\Gamma}(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{C\left(T^{2/3}+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\int_{\Gamma^{0}_{T}}|\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t.

Moreover, for
(y_{0},y_{0,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
the mild solution
(y,y_{\Gamma})
of the forward problem (
58
) with
f=g=v=0

satisfies

\|y(T,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y_{\Gamma}(T,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{C\left(T^{2/3}+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\int_{\Gamma^{0}_{T}}|y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

The proof mainly follows from the Carleman estimate (
3.2
) with an adaptation of the same ideas in
[
23
, Proposition 5]
, so we omit the details.
∎

Remark 3
.

We emphasize that the boundary observability constant

K_{\Gamma^{0}}\left(\Omega,T\right):=\exp\left({C\left(\Omega,\Gamma^{0}\right)\left(T^{2/3}+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\right)

obtained in Proposition
4
is different from the interior observability constant

K_{\omega}\left(\Omega,T\right):=\exp\left({C\left(\Omega,\omega\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)}\right)

obtained in
[
23
, Proposition 5]
. This is merely due to the parabolic regularity estimates used in the Carleman estimate to absorb the normal derivative term.

Now, we are in position to conclude the null controllability of (
58
), where inhomogeneities with exponential decay at
t=0
and
t=T
are allowed. Recall that

\theta(t)=\frac{1}{t(T-t)},\quad\qquad t\in(0,T).

Let us introduce the
L^{2}
-weighted spaces

\displaystyle Z_{\Omega}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}):\mathrm{e}^{s\theta(t)}\theta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(t)\,f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})\right\},

\displaystyle Z_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle:=\left\{g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{T}):\mathrm{e}^{s\theta(t)}\theta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(t)\,g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{T})\right\},

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\left\{Y\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right):\mathrm{e}^{st\theta(t)}Y\in L^{2}\left(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}\right)\right\},

with the inner products

\displaystyle\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{Z_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{T}}f_{1}f_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{2s\theta}\,\theta^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{Z_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{T}}g_{1}g_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{2s\theta}\,\theta^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any initial data
Y_{0}:=(y_{0},y_{0,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
,
f\in Z_{\Omega}
and

there exists a control

such that the mild solution
(y,y_{\Gamma})
of

58

y(T,\cdot)=y_{\Gamma}(T,\cdot)=0.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
Y_{0}

(60)

Proof.

By duality, the proposition will be derived from the previous observability inequality along with the Carleman estimate. To do this, we define a bounded linear operator

\mathcal{T}:L^{2}\left(\Gamma^{0}_{T}\right)\to\mathbb{L}^{2}

\mathcal{T}v=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}}(0,-\mathds{1}_{\Gamma^{0}}v(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using the embedding

By Proposition
2
(a), the function

is the final value
(y(T),y_{\Gamma}(T))
of the mild solution
(y,y_{\Gamma})
to the inhomogeneous system (
58

\mathcal{T}^{*}(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})=-\mathds{1}_{\Gamma^{0}}\varphi_{\Gamma},

where

The adjoint operator

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}\times Z_{\Omega}\times Z_{\Gamma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi_{\Gamma}(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+C\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi^{3}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\mathrm{e}^{-2s\alpha}\xi^{3}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Gamma^{0}_{T}}|\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}S\,\mathrm{d}t=C\|\mathcal{T}^{*}(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\|_{L^{2}\left(\Gamma^{0}_{T}\right)}

for

, and hence for
(\varphi_{T},\varphi_{T,\Gamma})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
by denseness.
Theorem 15.2 of
[

implies that the range of
\mathcal{T}
contains that of
\mathcal{S}
; i.e.,
for all

,

there exists a control

(y(T),y_{\Gamma}(T))=\mathcal{S}(Y_{0},f,g)-\mathcal{T}v=(0,0)
. Finally, the estimate (

[
27
, Proposition 4.3]

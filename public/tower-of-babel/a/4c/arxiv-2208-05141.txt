Partial (co)actions of Taft and Nichols Hopf algebras on algebras Thanks: 2020 MSC: Primary 16W99; Secondary 16T99. Key words and phrases: Partial action; Partial coaction; Taft algebra; Nichols Hopf algebra.
Graziela Fonseca, Grasiela Martini and Leonardo Silva

Abstract
. In this paper, we characterize suitable partial (co)actions of Taft and Nichols Hopf algebras on algebras, and moreover we get that such partial (co)actions are symmetric. For certain algebras, these partial (co)actions obtained are, indeed, all of them. This work generalizes the results obtained by the authors in [ 16 ] .



1.
Introduction

Global and partial actions of Hopf algebras on algebras are widely studied subjects, with various interests, applications and ramifications. However, calculating and classifying such actions is a difficult task. There exist several works that calculate (global) actions of certain Hopf algebras on different types of algebras, such as fields, domains, Weyl algebras, path algebras of quivers, among others
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

In general, the works investigate actions of two classes of Hopf algebras: the semisimple and the pointed (nonsemisimple).
In particular, the Taft algebra is an important example to be considered in the pointed case.
Because of that, some works deal particularly with Taft actions on algebras
[
7
,
17
,
18
]
.

In order to understand the actions of pointed Hopf algebras, another interesting example to be considered is the Nichols Hopf algebra: it is the archetype of pointed Hopf algebras.
Indeed, finite dimensional pointed Hopf algebras (with abelian group of group-like elements
G
) are expected to be a lifting of the bosonization of a Nichols algebra by the group algebra
\Bbbk G

[
4
]
. In particular, the Nichols Hopf algebra is just the bosonization of an exterior algebra by the group algebra of the cyclic group of order 2.

For partial Hopf actions, the situation is even more unexplored, with just a few concrete examples known.
In
[
16
]
, the authors exhibit explicitly all partial actions of Taft and Nichols Hopf algebras on their base fields, obtaining that such partial actions depend only on the partial action of the group of group-like elements, a derivation and a compatibility between both, same as it happens in the global case
[
7
]
.

In this work, the authors continue the investigation of the partial actions of Taft and Nichols Hopf algebras on algebras, extending the results of
[
16
]
.
In particular, Theorem
3.9
shows that, with suitable conditions, the partial actions still depend only on the partial group action, a derivation and a compatibility between them.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the definitions of partial (co)actions of a bialgebra on an algebra and also some definitions and results about
q-combinatorics
, that will be used in the computations of the partial Taft actions.
In Section
3
, we investigate and characterize suitable partial actions of the Taft algebra on an algebra. Furthermore, we present the partial Taft coactions obtained dually from such partial actions. At last, in Section
4
, we compute some partial (co)actions of a Nichols Hopf algebra on algebras.

2.1.
Partial (co)actions

Partial actions and partial coactions of bialgebras on algebras were introduced in
[
6
]
.
Since this earlier definition does not deal with symmetric conditions, we will present as it appears in later works.
These conditions are important for some developments in the theory of partial (co)actions, such as partial (co)representations
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

A
partial action of a bialgebra
H
on an algebra
A
is a linear map
\cdot:H\otimes A\longrightarrow A
, denoted by
\cdot(h\otimes a)=h\cdot a
, such that

(PA.1)

1_{H}\cdot a=a
,

(PA.2)

h\cdot ab=(h_{1}\cdot a)(h_{2}\cdot b)
, and

(PA.3)

h\cdot(k\cdot a)=(h_{1}\cdot 1_{A})(h_{2}k\cdot a)
,

hold for all
h,k\in H
and
a,b\in A
.
In this case,
A
is called a
partial
H
-module algebra
.
A partial action is
symmetric
if in addition we have

(PA.S)

h\cdot(k\cdot a)=(h_{1}k\cdot a)(h_{2}\cdot 1_{A})
.

Note that conditions (PA.2) and (PA.3) are equivalent to the following:

(PA.2
′
)

h\cdot(a(k\cdot b))=(h_{1}\cdot a)(h_{2}k\cdot b)
.

Every
H
-module algebra is a symmetric partial
H
-module algebra. Moreover, a partial
H
-module algebra is an
H
-module algebra if and only if
h\cdot 1_{A}=\varepsilon(h)1_{A}
, for all
h\in H
.

A
(symmetric) partial coaction of a bialgebra
H
on an algebra
A
is a linear map
\rho:A\longrightarrow A\otimes H
satisfying dual conditions to the previous conditions (PA.
\_
).
In fact, the concepts of partial actions and partial coactions are dually related in
[
5
]
as follows.

Let
\langle-,-\rangle:K\otimes H\longrightarrow\Bbbk
be a dual pairing between bialgebras and
A
an algebra. If
A
is a (symmetric)
partial
K
-comodule algebra, then
A
is a (symmetric) partial
H
-module algebra.
Furthermore, if the dual pairing
\langle-,-\rangle
is non-degenerate and
A
is a rational (symmetric) partial
H
-module algebra, then
A
is a (symmetric) partial
K
-comodule algebra.
Thus, in the particular case that
H
is a finite-dimensional bialgebra with basis
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, we obtain the following characterization.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
A
is a (symmetric) partial
H
-module algebra via
h\cdot a
, then
A
is a (symmetric) partial
H^{\ast}
-comodule algebra via
\rho:A\rightarrow A\otimes H^{*}
, given by
\rho(a)=\sum_{i\in I}h_{i}\cdot a\otimes h_{i}^{\ast}
. Reciprocally, if
A
is a (symmetric) partial
H
-comodule algebra via
\rho:A\rightarrow A\otimes H
, where
\rho(a)=a^{0}\otimes a^{1}
, then
A
is a (symmetric) partial
H^{\ast}
-module algebra via
h^{\ast}\cdot a=h^{\ast}(a^{1})a^{0}
.

2.2.
q-combinatorics

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{Z},n\geq 0
, and
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. The
q-binomial coefficients
are defined recursively as follows.
First, set
{0\choose 0}_{q}=1
and
{n\choose m}_{q}=0
if
m>n
or
m<0
.
Then, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq m\leq n
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}={{n-1}\choose{m-1}}_{q}+q^{m}{{n-1}\choose m}_{q}.

It follows that
{n\choose 0}_{q}=1={n\choose n}_{q}
and
{n\choose m}_{q}={n\choose{n-m}}_{q}
.

The
q-numbers
and the
q-factorials
also are defined recursively:
(0)_{q}=0
,
(n)_{q}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}q^{\ell};
and

(0)_{q}!=1
,
(n)_{q}!=(n)_{q}(n-1)_{q}!,
respectively.

Note that, if
q\neq 1
, then
q
is an
n^{th}
root of unity if and only if
(n)_{q}=0
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
and
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. If
(n-1)_{q}!\neq 0
, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}=\dfrac{(n)_{q}!}{(n-m)_{q}!(m)_{q}!},

for all
0<m<n
.
In particular, if
q\neq 1
is a primitive
n^{th}
root of unity, then
{n\choose m}_{q}=0,

for all
0<m<n
.

Suppose
q
is a primitive
N^{th}
root of unity. For
k\geq 0
, let
k_{D}
and
k_{R}
be the integers determined by
k=k_{D}\ N+k_{R}
where
0\leq k_{R}<N
.
Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}={n_{R}\choose m_{R}}_{q}{n_{D}\choose m_{D}},

for all
0\leq m\leq n
.

Furthermore, the following equalities are well-know:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}{m\choose k}_{q}q^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}-km}=0\quad(m\geq 1),

(2.5)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}=q^{m(n-m)}{n\choose m}_{q^{-1}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle{n\choose m}_{q}={{n-1}\choose m}_{q}+q^{n-m}{{n-1}\choose{m-1}}_{q}.

These definitions and equalities are classic for
q-computations
, and one can find more details about them, for instance, in
[
20
]
.
However, to compute partial actions of Taft algebras on algebras, we are going to need some more.
We present below
q-identities
that will be used to compute such partial actions in the following section.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
16
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.3 and 3.9]

Let
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
i,j,k,s,t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, such that
k\leq j
.
Then,

(2.7)

\displaystyle{j\choose k}_{q}{{j-k}\choose{i-k}}_{q}={j\choose i}_{q}{i\choose k}_{q};

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}q^{s\ell+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}}{j\choose\ell}_{q}{{j+t-\ell}\choose{j+s}}_{q}={t\choose s}_{q};

(2.9)

\displaystyle q^{s(i-j)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}{j\choose\ell}_{q}{{j+t-\ell}\choose{i+s-\ell}}_{q}{\ell\choose i}_{q}(-1)^{i-\ell}q^{\frac{(i-\ell)(i-\ell+1)}{2}}={j\choose i}_{q}{t\choose s}_{q}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
t,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
Then,

(i)

if
0\leq\ell<t
and
(t-1)_{q}!\neq 0
, then

(2.10)

\left(1-q^{t-\ell}\right){t\choose\ell}_{q}+q^{\ell+1}{t\choose\ell+1}_{q}={t\choose\ell+1}_{q};

(ii)

for each
m\in N_{0}
, it holds

(2.11)

\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{m\choose s}_{q}{{s+t}\choose{\ell}}_{q}=q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+m(t-\ell)}{t\choose\ell-m}_{q}.

Proof.

(i)

It is straightforward using that
{t\choose\ell+1}_{q}=\frac{(t-\ell)_{q}}{(\ell+1)_{q}}{t\choose\ell}_{q}
and (
2.2
).

(ii)

The proof is done by induction on
m
. The statement is clear for
m=0
. For
m>0
, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{m+1-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(m+1)}{m+1\choose s}_{q}{{s+t}\choose{\ell}}_{q}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def_q_binomial}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{m+1-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(m+1)}{m\choose s-1}_{q}{{s+t}\choose{\ell}}_{q}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{m\choose s}_{q}{{s+t}\choose{\ell}}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-m}\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{m\choose s}_{q}{{s+(t+1)}\choose{\ell}}_{q}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{m\choose s}_{q}{{s+t}\choose{\ell}}_{q}.

So, we conclude the induction step applying the induction hypothesis in both summations, and using equality (
2.6
).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q\in\Bbbk\setminus\{1\}
be a
n^{th}
root of unity. Then,

(i)

for each
0\leq i<n
, it holds that

(2.12)

(-1)^{i}q^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}{n-1\choose i}_{q}=1;

(ii)

if
q
is a primitive root of unity, and
\ell,t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
0\leq\ell\leq t<n
, then it holds that

(2.13)

(-1)^{\ell}q^{t\ell-\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{2}}{n+\ell-t-1\choose\ell}_{q}={t\choose\ell}_{q}.

Proof.

Both items are proved by induction. For item (i) it can be used equality (
2.1
), while for item (ii) it is a computation using (

).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
q\in\Bbbk
be a primitive
n^{th}
root of unity and
k,\ell,t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
k,\ell,t<n
. Then,

(i)

if
n\leq t+\ell
, then, for each
0\leq m<t
, it holds that

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{t\choose m-s}_{q}{s\choose t+\ell-n}_{q}\\
=&\ (-1)^{m}q^{\frac{-m(m-1)}{2}-t(t+\ell-m)}{m-t+n\choose\ell}_{q};\end{split}

(ii)

if
\ell\leq k
, then it holds that

(2.15)

\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose s}_{q}={k\choose\ell}_{q};

(iii)

if
\ell\leq k
, then, for each
\ell\leq m<n
, it holds that

(2.16)

\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell-k)}{n+m-k\choose s}_{q}{n+m-s\choose n+\ell-s}_{q}={k\choose\ell}_{q}.

Proof.

We prove the statements by induction on the upper index of the summation symbol.

(i)

To prove this item, we consider two cases:
t+\ell>n
and
t+\ell=n
.

First, assume
t+\ell>n
.
For
m=0
, it is clear.
For the induction step, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(m+1)}{t\choose m+1-s}_{q}{s\choose t+\ell-n}_{q}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s+1}q^{\frac{(s+1)(s+2)}{2}-(s+1)(m+1)}{t\choose m-s}_{q}{s+1\choose t+\ell-n}_{q}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def_q_binomial}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\ -q^{-m}\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{t\choose m-s}_{q}{s\choose t+\ell-n-1}_{q}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\ q^{t+\ell-m}\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{t\choose m-s}_{q}{s\choose t+\ell-n}_{q}.

Then, one conclude the induction step using the induction hypothesis in both summations and applying (
2.1
).

Now, assume
t+\ell=n
.
Thus, identity (
2.14
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{t\choose m-s}_{q}=(-1)^{m}q^{mt-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}{m-t+n\choose m}_{q}.

Again, the case
m=0
is clear. For the induction step, assume
m\geq 0
and note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(m+1)}{t\choose m+1-s}_{q}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ {t\choose m+1}-q^{-m}\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-sm}{t\choose m-s}_{q}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\ {t\choose m+1}-q^{-m}\left((-1)^{m}q^{mt-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}{m-t+n\choose m}_{q}\right),

where the induction hypothesis was used to obtain the last equality. Then, the result follows using equalities (
2.13
) and (
2.6
).

(ii)

The statement is clear for
\ell=0
. Assume
\ell>0
and
\ell+1\leq k
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\ell+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell+1-k)}{n+\ell+1-k\choose s}_{q}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def_q_binomial}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{s=1}^{\ell+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell+1-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose s-1}_{q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{\ell+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose s}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{s+1}q^{\frac{(s+1)(s+2)}{2}-(s+1)(\ell+1-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose s}_{q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose s}_{q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell+1}q^{\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{2}-(\ell+1)(\ell-k)}{n+\ell-k\choose\ell+1}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q^{k-\ell}{k\choose\ell}_{q}+{k\choose\ell}_{q}+q^{\ell+1}{k\choose\ell+1}_{q}.

where the last equality follows from the induction hypothesis and equality (

). Hence, (

) concludes the induction step.

(iii)

The statement holds for

Now, for the induction hypothesis, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}-s(\ell-k)}{n+m+1-k\choose s}_{q}{n+m+1-s\choose n+\ell-s}_{q}

\displaystyle=

since

, because

) we obtain the result.

∎

3.
Partial (co)actions of the Taft Algebra

Taft Algebra.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and suppose that
\Bbbk
contains a primitive
n^{th}
root of unity
q
.
In particular
char(\Bbbk)\nmid n
.
The
Taft algebra of order
n
, or shortly
Taft algebra
, here denoted by
T_{n}(q)
, has the following structure:
as algebra it is generated over
\Bbbk
by two elements
g
and
x
with relations
g^{n}=1,

x^{n}=0
and
xg=qgx
.
Thus, the set
\{g^{i}x^{j}\ :\ 0\leq i,j<n\}
is the canonical basis for
T_{n}(q)
and so
dim_{\Bbbk}(T_{n}(q))=n^{2}
.
The coalgebra structure of
T_{n}(q)
is induced by setting
g
a group-like element and
x
an
(1,g)
-primitive element, that is,
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
,
\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x
,
\varepsilon(g)=1
and
\varepsilon(x)=0
.
In general, the comultiplication map is
\Delta(g^{i}x^{j})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}{j\choose\ell}_{q}g^{i+\ell}x^{j-\ell}\otimes g^{i}x^{\ell}
, for
i,j\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n-1}
.
To complete the Hopf algebra structure of
T_{n}(q)
, the antipode map
S
is defined by
S(g)=g^{n-1}
and
S(x)=-g^{n-1}x.

Furthermore, note that the group of group-like elements is
G(T_{n}(q))=\{1,g,\cdots,g^{n-1}\}
,
i.e.
,
G(T_{n}(q))=C_{n}.

3.1.
Partial actions

In this section, we characterize suitable partial actions of
T_{n}(q)
on an algebra
A
. If
A
has only trivial idempotent elements, these are all partial actions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\cdot:H\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
H
on
A
. For each
h\in G(H)
and
a\in A
:

(i)

(h\cdot 1_{A})a(h\cdot 1_{A})=(h\cdot 1_{A})a
;

(ii)

if the partial action
\cdot|_{\Bbbk G(H)\otimes A}
is symmetric, then
(h\cdot 1_{A})a=a(h\cdot 1_{A})
;

(iii)

if
h\cdot 1_{A}=0
, then
(h^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(h^{i+1}\cdot 1_{A})=0
, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Assume
h\in G(H)
and
a\in A
.

(i)

First,

(h\cdot 1_{A})(h\cdot(h^{-1}\cdot a))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(PA.2)}}{{=}}(h\cdot 1_{A})(h\cdot 1_{A})(1_{H}\cdot a)=(h\cdot 1_{A})a.

Then,

\displaystyle(h\cdot 1_{A})a(h\cdot 1_{A})

\displaystyle=(h\cdot 1_{A})(h\cdot(h^{-1}\cdot a))(h\cdot 1_{A})

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(PA.2)}}{{=}}(h\cdot 1_{A})(h\cdot((h^{-1}\cdot a)1_{A}))=(h\cdot 1_{A})a.

(ii)

Analogously to the previous item.

(iii)

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
(h^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(h^{i+1}\cdot 1_{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(PA.3)}}{{=}}h^{i}\cdot(h\cdot 1_{A})=h^{i}\cdot 0=0.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\cdot:T_{n}(q)\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
T_{n}(q)
on
A
.
If
g\cdot 1_{A}=1_{A}
, then
\cdot
is a global action.

Proof.

Suppose that
g\cdot 1_{A}=1_{A}
.
It is enough to prove that
g\cdot(h\cdot a)=gh\cdot a
and
x\cdot(h\cdot a)=xh\cdot a
, for any
h\in T_{n}(q)
and
a\in A
.
First, by (PA.2), we get

x\cdot 1_{A}=x\cdot 1_{A}^{2}=(x\cdot 1_{A})(1\cdot 1_{A})+(g\cdot 1_{A})(x\cdot 1_{A})=2(x\cdot 1_{A}),

that is,
x\cdot 1_{A}=0
. Then, by (PA.3),

x\cdot(h\cdot a)=(x\cdot 1_{A})(h\cdot a)+(g\cdot 1_{A})(xh\cdot a)=xh\cdot a.

Also by (PA.3), it follows that

g\cdot(h\cdot a)=(g\cdot 1_{A})(gh\cdot a)=gh\cdot a.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\cdot:T_{n}(q)\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
T_{n}(q)
on

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\cdot:T_{n}(q)\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
T_{n}(q)
on
A
.
If

, then

(iii)

such that

, for all
a\in A

Proof.

(i)

By definition of the partial action, we have

g^{n-1}x\cdot(gx^{n-1}\cdot a)=(g^{n-1}x\cdot 1_{A})(x^{n-1}\cdot a)

As seen in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, it holds that
g^{n-1}x\cdot a=-qa(x\cdot 1_{A})
. Then, the above equality can be rewritten as

(gx^{n-1}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})=(x\cdot 1_{A})(x^{n-1}\cdot a).

Then, by (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle q\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{n-1\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-1-k}(g^{k+1}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{n-1\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-k}(g^{k}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k}.

Now, since
g\cdot 1_{A}=0
, and so
g\cdot a=g^{-1}\cdot a=0
, for all
a\in A
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}q{n-1\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-(k+1)}(g^{k+1}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}q^{-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}q{n-1\choose n-1}_{q^{-1}}a(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n}a+\sum_{k=2}^{n-2}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{n-1\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-k}(g^{k}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k}.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{n-2}-(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}q^{k}{n-1\choose k-1}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-k}(g^{k}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k}

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{n-1\choose n-1}_{q^{-1}}a(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n}a+\sum_{k=2}^{n-2}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{n-1\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{n-k}(g^{k}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k}.

) and

, we conclude that

, for all

.

(ii)

Since

and

\displaystyle g^{i}\cdot(x\cdot 1_{A})=

\displaystyle\ (g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(g^{i}x\cdot 1_{A})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{formula_3_1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\ q^{-i}(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})((x\cdot 1_{A})(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})-(g^{i+1}\cdot 1_{A})(x\cdot 1_{A}))

\displaystyle\ q^{-i}((g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(x\cdot 1_{A})(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})-(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(g^{i+1}\cdot 1_{A})(x\cdot 1_{A}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{idempotente_comuta}(iii)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\ q^{-i}(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})(x\cdot 1_{A})(g^{i}\cdot 1_{A})

(iii)

∎

3.2.

In this section, we investigate the theory of the partial coactions of Taft algebras on algebras.

First, recall that
T_{n}(q)
is a self-dual Hopf algebra.

Lemma 3.18
.

Consider the Taft algebra
T_{n}(q)
and the linear maps:

(1)

\psi:T_{n}(q)\longrightarrow(T_{n}(q))^{*}
given by

\psi(g^{i}x^{j})=G^{i}X^{j},
where

\displaystyle G^{i}X^{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(j)_{q}!q^{-i(k+j)-jk-\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}(g^{k}x^{j})^{*};

(2)

\varphi:(T_{n}(q))^{*}\longrightarrow T_{n}(q)

\displaystyle\varphi((g^{i}x^{j})^{*})=\frac{1}{n}((j)_{q}!)^{-1}q^{ij+\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{k(i+j)}g^{k}x^{j}.

Then, both maps defined in (1) and (2) are isomorphisms of Hopf algebras.
Furthermore,
\varphi=\psi^{-1}
.

For the next result, consider
\pi
the canonic projection from

on
\Bbbk C_{n}

\rho
is a global coaction, or

is a partial coaction of

such that

(i)

\displaystyle\rho(a)=\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j}q^{-ij}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}w^{j-k}\\
\times\left(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}q^{-(i+k)t}(g^{t})^{\ast}(a^{1})a^{0}\right)w^{k}\otimes\frac{1}{n}((j)_{q}!)^{-1}q^{ij+\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}q^{\ell(i+j)}g^{\ell}x^{j}

(iii)

By Proposition

and

Therefore, considering

\displaystyle A_{1}=0

\displaystyle g\cdot 1_{A}=0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ A_{1}=1\Leftrightarrow g\cdot 1_{A}=1.

By Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
3.9
,

\displaystyle\rho(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j}(g^{i}x^{j})\cdot a\otimes\varphi((g^{i}x^{j})^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j}\sum_{k=0}^{j}q^{-ij}(-1)^{k}q^{-\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}{j\choose k}_{q^{-1}}(x\cdot 1_{A})^{j-k}(g^{i+k}\cdot a)(x\cdot 1_{A})^{k}

\displaystyle\otimes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}((j)_{q}!)^{-1}q^{ij+\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}q^{\ell(i+j)}g^{\ell}x^{j},

and

4.
Partial (co)actions of the Nichols Hopf algebra

First, we shall emphasize that Nichols Hopf algebras were introduced by Taft in
[
21
]
, but were named after the work of Nichols
[
19
]
.
Such Hopf algebras are the prototype of the theory of
Nichols algebras
.
We will present this family of Hopf algebras as it appears in
[
10
, Section 2.2]
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and suppose
char(\Bbbk)\neq 2
.
The
Nichols Hopf algebra of order
n
, or shortly
Nichols Hopf algebra
, here denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
, has the structure as follows:
as algebra it is generated over
\Bbbk
by the
n
letters
g,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}
with relations
g^{2}=1
,
x_{i}^{2}=0
,
x_{i}g=-gx_{i}
and
x_{i}x_{j}=-x_{j}x_{i}
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Thus, the set

\mathcal{B}=\{g^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}...x_{n-1}^{j_{n-1}}:j_{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1},i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n-1}\}

is the canonical basis for
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
and consequently
dim_{\Bbbk}(\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}})=2^{n}
.
To complete the Hopf algebra structure of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
, we set

\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
,
\varepsilon(g)=1
,
S(g)=g^{-1}=g,
and
\Delta(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes 1+g\otimes x_{i}
,
\varepsilon(x_{i})=0
and
S(x_{i})=-gx_{i},
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Note that
G(\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}})=\{1,g\}=C_{2}
, for any
n
.
In particular, when
n=2
, the Nichols Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}_{2^{2}}
is exactly the Sweedler’s
4
-dimensional Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}_{4}
.

Similarly to global actions of Taft’s algebra
[
7
, Proposition 4]
, it is straightforward to determine global actions of Nichols Hopf algebra completely through an automorphism and skew-derivations.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let A be an algebra, then an action of the Nichols Hopf algebra
H_{2^{n}}
on
A
is completely determinate by a choice of:

(i)

An automorphism
\alpha
of
A
of order
2
, i.e.,
\alpha^{2}=\alpha
;

(ii)

A family of
\alpha
-derivations
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}}
of
A
such that
{\delta_{i}}^{2}=0
and
\alpha\delta_{i}=-\delta_{i}\alpha
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.

Equivalently, the structure of
H_{2^{n}}
-module algebra on A is uniquely determined by a choice of:

(i)

A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
, defined by an automorphism
\alpha
;

(ii)

A family of
\alpha
-derivations
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}}
such that
{\delta_{i}}^{2}=0
,
\delta_{i}(A_{0})\subset A_{1}
and
\delta_{i}(A_{1})\subset A_{0}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.

4.1.
Partial Actions

In this section, we compute a family of examples of partial actions of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
A
.

First, we shall analyze when a partial action is global.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
A
.
If
g\cdot 1_{A}=1_{A}
, then
\cdot
is global.

Proof.

Analogously to the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
A
.
If
g\cdot 1_{A}=0
and
x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, then,
for all
a\in A
,

gx_{i}\cdot a=x_{i}\cdot a=(x_{i}\cdot 1_{A})a,

gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=0,\ \ \ s\geq 2,i_{\ell}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1},\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{s}.

Proof.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
A
with

x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, and
g\cdot 1_{A}=0
.

First, note that
g\cdot a=0
, for
a\in A
. Then, by (PA.2), we have that
x_{i}\cdot a=(x_{i}\cdot 1_{A})a
and
gx_{i}\cdot a=a(gx_{i}\cdot 1_{A})
. Moreover, considering
h=gx_{i}
and
k=g
in (PA.3), we obtain
gx_{i}\cdot 1_{A}=x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}
, and using that
x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
we conclude
gx_{i}\cdot a=x_{i}\cdot a=(x_{i}\cdot 1_{A})a
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
and
a\in A
.

Now, for
h=gx_{i}
and
k=x_{j}
we get
x_{i}x_{j}\cdot a=0
, and finally taking
h=gx_{i}
and
k=gx_{j}
we deduce that
gx_{i}x_{j}\cdot a=0
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
i\neq j
.

By induction, one can prove that
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=0
, for
s\geq 2
,
i_{\ell}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{s}
.
Indeed, we already done for
s=2
.
Assume
s\geq 2
and consider
h=gx_{i_{1}}
,
k=x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}
(resp.
h=gx_{i_{1}}
,
k=gx_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}
) in (PA.3).
Then, using the induction hypothesis, it follows that
gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=0
(resp.
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=0
). ∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\cdot:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\otimes A\longrightarrow A
be a linear map such that
1\cdot 1_{A}=1_{A}
,
g\cdot 1_{A}=0
,
x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, and for all
a\in A
,

gx_{i}\cdot a=x_{i}\cdot a=(x_{i}\cdot 1_{A})a,

gx_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}\cdot a=0,\ \ \ s\geq 2,i_{\ell}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1},\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{s}.

Then,
\cdot
is a partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
A
.

Proof.

We need to check if condition (PA.2
′
) holds.
We proceed by fixing
h\in\mathcal{B}
and verifying (PA.2
′
) for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.

First, if
h\in\{1,g,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\}
, then (PA.2
′
) holds trivially for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
.

If
h=gx_{i}
,
i\in I_{n-1}
, then (PA.2
′
) means

gx_{i}\cdot(a(k\cdot b))=(gx_{i}\cdot a)(gk\cdot b)+a(gx_{i}k\cdot b),

for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
.
Using that
x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, and considering
k
as
g^{\ell},g^{\ell}x_{j}
and
g^{\ell}x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}...x_{j_{s}}
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1},

j\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
and
s\in\mathbb{I}_{2,n-1},j_{t}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1},t\in\mathbb{I}_{s}
, it is a routine computation to get the equality.

Now, if
h=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1}
,
i_{1},i_{2}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
i_{1}<i_{2}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})=

\displaystyle\ g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\otimes g^{\ell}-g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}+g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{2}}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\ g^{\ell}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}},

and so (PA.2
′
) becomes

\displaystyle g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdot(a(k\cdot b))=

\displaystyle\ (g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}k\cdot b)-(g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\ (g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{2}}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}k\cdot b)+(g^{\ell}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}k\cdot b),

for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
.

Since
(gx_{s}\cdot a)=(x_{s}\cdot a)=(x_{s}\cdot 1_{A})a
and
(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdot a)=(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}k\cdot a)=0
, for any
s\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
k\in\mathcal{B}
, (PA.2
′
) is reduced to

(x_{i_{1}}\cdot 1_{A})a(g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}k\cdot b)=(x_{i_{2}}\cdot 1_{A})a(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}k\cdot b),

for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
.
One can verify this equality using
x_{i}\cdot 1_{A}\in Z(A)
for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
and considering
k
as
g^{t}
and
g^{t}x_{j_{1}}...x_{j_{s}}
, where
t\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1}
and
s\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
j_{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
,
k\in\mathbb{I}_{s}
,
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{s}
.

Finally, it remains to check (PA.2
′
) for
h=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}...x_{i_{s}}
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1}
and
s\geq 3
, and any
k\in\mathcal{B}
.
Write
h=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w
, for some
w\in\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\Delta(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w)

\displaystyle=\Delta(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}})\Delta(x_{i_{3}})\Delta(w)

\displaystyle=g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}w_{2}-g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}w_{2}

\displaystyle+g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}w_{2}+g^{\ell}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}w_{2}

\displaystyle+g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}+g^{\ell+2}x_{i_{1}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}

\displaystyle-g^{\ell+2}x_{i_{2}}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}+g^{\ell+1}w_{1}\otimes g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2},

and for any
k\in\mathcal{B}
, condition (PA.2
′
) means

\displaystyle g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w\cdot(a(k\cdot b))

\displaystyle=(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle-(g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+(g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+(g^{\ell}x_{i_{3}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+(g^{\ell+1}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+(g^{\ell+2}x_{i_{1}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle-(g^{\ell+2}x_{i_{2}}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}k\cdot b)

\displaystyle+(g^{\ell+1}w_{1}\cdot a)(g^{\ell}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}w_{2}k\cdot b).

Note that the above equality holds since the linear map
\cdot
evaluated in a product that contains at least two skew-primitive elements as factors is equal to zero. Therefore, the map
\cdot
as given is a partial action of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on
\Bbbk
.
∎

Considering the results presented in this section, we have characterized all partial actions

and

for all

of the Nichols Hopf algebra

\lambda_{\alpha}(gx_{1}x_{2})=0
and
\lambda_{\alpha}(x_{i})=\lambda_{\alpha}(gx_{i})=\alpha_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{2}
.

4.2.
Partial coactions

In this section, we compute some partial coactions of Nichols Hopf algebra
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
on an algebra
A
.
The setting here is the same as for the Taft algebra, since both are self-dual Hopf algebras.

Lemma 4.8
.

The linear map
\psi:\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}})^{*}

given by
\psi(g)=1^{*}-g^{*}
and
\psi(x_{i})=x_{i}^{*}-(gx_{i})^{*}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
, is an isomorphism of Hopf algebras.

From the isomorphism
\psi
in the above lemma, it follows that

\psi\left(\frac{1+g}{2}\right)=1^{*},\psi\left(\frac{x_{i}-gx_{i}}{2}\right)=x_{i}^{*}\textrm{ and }\psi\left(\frac{-(x_{i}+gx_{i})}{2}\right)=(gx_{i})^{*},

for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
.
Thus, we are able to prove the following result.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\rho:A\longrightarrow A\otimes\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
be a linear map, denoted by
\rho(a)=a^{0}\otimes a^{1}
, such that
A_{1}=(1^{\ast}-g^{\ast})(1^{1})1^{0}=0
and
w_{i}\in Z(A)
, where
w_{i}=(x_{i}^{*}-(gx_{i})^{*})(1^{1})1^{0}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
. Then,
\rho
is a partial coaction if and only if

\displaystyle\rho(a)=a\otimes\left(\frac{1+g}{2}\right)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}w_{i}a\otimes gx_{i},

for any
a\in A
.
Moreover, all these partial coactions are symmetric.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{g^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}...x_{n-1}^{j_{n-1}}:j_{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{0,1},i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n-1}\}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{H}_{2^{n}}
. By Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
4.3
,
A_{1}=0\Leftrightarrow g\cdot 1_{A}=0
and

\displaystyle\rho(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}...x_{n-1}^{j_{n-1}}\cdot a)\otimes\psi^{-1}((g^{j_{0}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}}...x_{n-1}^{j_{n-1}})^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\cdot a\otimes\psi^{-1}(1^{\ast})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\cdot a\otimes\psi^{-1}(x_{i}^{\ast})+gx_{i}\cdot a\otimes\psi^{-1}(gx_{i}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\otimes\left(\frac{1+g}{2}\right)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}w_{i}a\otimes gx_{i},

for all
a\in A
, where
\psi
is given by Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Remark.
By the correspondence
A_{1}\Leftrightarrow g\cdot 1_{A}
,
A_{1}=1_{A}
if only if
\rho
is global coaction.

Example 4.10
.

When
A=\Bbbk
, these partial coactions coincide with the partial coactions
\rho(a)=1\otimes z_{\alpha}
, where
z_{\alpha}=\frac{1+g}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}\,gx_{i}
, presented in
[
16
, Theorem 4.6]
.

Examples 4.11
.

n=2
every partial coaction such that
A_{1}=0
and
w\in Z(A)
is given by
\rho(a)=a\otimes\left(\frac{1+g}{2}\right)-wa\otimes gx,

for all
a\in A

3.24
.

and

.

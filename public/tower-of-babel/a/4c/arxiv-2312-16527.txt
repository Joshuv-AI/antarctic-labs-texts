Improved global well-posedness for mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equations on tori
Robert Schippa

Abstract
. We show new global well-posedness results for mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equations on tori in one and two dimensions. Introduction

This note is concerned with the global well-posedness of the mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on tori. The one-dimensional torus with period
2\pi\beta

\mathbb{T}_{\beta}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\beta\mathbb{Z})

The Cauchy problems for the mass-critical NLS read

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=\pm|u|^{\frac{4}{d}}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\underline{\gamma}},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\underline{\gamma}}).\end{array}\right.

The energy is given by

2.1.
Fourier transform conventions

Recall that for
\underline{\gamma}\in(1/2,1]^{d-1}
we set

\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\underline{\gamma}}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}_{\gamma_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{T}_{\gamma_{d-1}}.

For fixed
\underline{\gamma}\in(1/2,1]^{d-1}
, we consider rescaled tori
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{T}_{\gamma_{1}\lambda}\times\ldots\mathbb{T}_{\gamma_{d-1}\lambda}
with large period
\lambda\geq 1
. We suppress the notation of
\underline{\gamma}
for the rescaled tori because it will be fixed at the outset, and the estimates will be independent of
\underline{\gamma}\in(1/2,1]^{d-1}
.

Let
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable. The Lebesgue norms are given by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})}=\big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
1\leq p<\infty
with the usual modification for
p=\infty
.

The Fourier coefficients of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})
satisfy
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{Z}/\lambda\times\mathbb{Z}/(\lambda\gamma_{1})\times\ldots\mathbb{Z}/(\lambda\gamma_{d-1})
2
2

2

To be consistent, we suppress dependence on
\gamma
likewise on the frequency side.
are given by

\hat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda}}e^{-ik\cdot x}f(x)dx,

and the Fourier inversion formula reads

f(x)=\frac{1}{(\gamma_{1}\ldots\gamma_{d-1})\lambda^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}/\lambda}e^{ik\cdot x}\hat{f}(k).

The dual
(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})^{*}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}
is endowed with normalized counting measure. Let
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{C}
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}/\lambda
. We define

\int_{A}\tilde{f}(k)(dk)_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\gamma_{1}\ldots\gamma_{d-1})}\sum_{k\in A}\tilde{f}(k).

The Fourier transform conventions are supposed to ensure that Plancherel’s theorem holds true without constant depending on
\lambda
:

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\gamma_{1}\ldots\gamma_{d-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}.

Sobolev norms on
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda}
are defined by

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{d}(\gamma_{1}\ldots\gamma_{d-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}.

For
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\lambda}
we denote with
P_{A}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\lambda})
the Fourier projection to
A
. For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we denote with
P_{N}
the smooth frequency projection to frequencies of size
N
. For
N=1
, we consider frequencies of size
\lesssim 1
.

\begin{split}\|P_{\lambda I_{1}}u_{1\lambda}P_{\lambda I_{2}}u_{\lambda I_{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}([0,\frac{1}{\lambda N}]\times\mathbb{T})}&=\sum_{J}\|P_{\lambda I_{1}}u_{1\lambda}P_{\lambda I_{2}}u_{2\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}(J\times\mathbb{T})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{N\lambda}\|u_{1\lambda}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\|u_{2\lambda}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.\end{split}

The claim follows then from reversing the scaling.

If
M\lesssim N
, we can use the bilinear estimate directly:

\|P_{\lambda I_{1}}u_{1\lambda}P_{\lambda I_{2}}u_{2\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}([0,\frac{1}{\lambda N}]\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\frac{1}{M\lambda}\|u_{1\lambda}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\|u_{2\lambda}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

∎

Remark 2.11

.

Let

.

,

]
):

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\lambda\geq 1
and

If

3.
Auxiliary local well-posedness results

3.1.
Local well-posedness on the torus on an intermediate time-scale

In the following we solve the quintic NLS on the rescaled torus

(15)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{xx}u&=\pm|u|^{4}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}).\end{array}\right.

With the quintic NLS on the unit torus being locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for times
T\lesssim 1
, the quintic NLS on the rescaled torus is expected to be locally well-posed up to times
\lambda^{2}
. We solve the Cauchy problem in the following on the time-scale
\lambda/N
. Recall
\lambda\sim N^{\frac{1-s}{s}+\varepsilon}
. Thus, for
s<\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\lambda\gg N
, choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

With the
L^{6}
-Strichartz estimates in adapted function spaces at hand, we can show the following auxiliary well-posedness result:

Proposition 3.1
.

For
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, the Cauchy problem (
15
) is locally well-posed in
Y^{s}
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
for
T\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{N}
provided that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}\ll 1
. The solution satisfies

\|u\|_{Y_{T}^{s}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u\|_{Y_{T}^{s}}^{5}.

Proof.

We use the property (
6
) from Proposition
2.7
and Littlewood-Paley theory:

\big\|\chi_{[0,T)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}(|u|^{4}u)(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\big\|_{Y^{s}}\lesssim\sup_{v}\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}\sum_{M\geq 1}P_{M}(|u|^{4}u)\overline{P_{M}v}dxdt\big|.

We use an additional Littlewood-Paley decomposition of the factors to write

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{M\geq 1}\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M}(|u|^{4}u)\overline{P_{M}v}dxdt\\
&=\sum_{M\geq 1}\sum_{N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\ldots\gtrsim N_{5}}\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt.\end{split}

We omit the notation of complex conjugates above and in the following because the estimates do not depend on these.
We use Littlewood-Paley dichotomy to argue

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{M\geq 1}\sum_{N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\ldots\gtrsim N_{5}}\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt\\
&=\sum_{M\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\ldots\gtrsim N_{5}}\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt\\
&\quad+\sum_{M\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{3}\ldots\gtrsim N_{5}}\iint P_{M}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt\\
&=(I)+(II).\end{split}

We turn to the estimate of
(I)
. We use almost orthogonality and decompose
P_{M}v
and
P_{N_{1}}u_{1}
into frequency intervals of size
N_{2}
:

\begin{split}&\quad\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt\\
&=\sum_{I_{1},I_{2}}\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M,I_{1}}vP_{N,I_{2}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt.\end{split}

After estimating the above expression, the sum over
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
can be removed by an application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and property (
5
) of the
Y^{s}
-spaces.

Case
N_{2}\lesssim N
. We use the
L^{6}
-estimate from (
11
) to find

\begin{split}&\quad\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{M,I_{1}}vP_{N_{1},I_{2}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}dxdt\\
&\lesssim\|P_{M,I_{1}}v\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\|P_{N_{1},I_{2}}u_{1}\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\\
&\lesssim\|P_{M,I_{1}}v\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\|P_{N_{1},I_{2}}u_{1}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}.\end{split}

Now we can use almost orthogonality to sum over
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

Case
N_{2}\gtrsim N
. In this case we can still use the
L^{6}_{t,x}
-estimates from (
12
). We conclude

\lesssim\log(\lambda N_{2})^{18}\|P_{M,I_{1}}v\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\|P_{N_{1}}u_{1}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}.

Recalling that
\lambda\sim N^{\frac{1-s}{s}+\varepsilon}\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1-s}{s}+\varepsilon}
, we have

\lesssim\big(\frac{1}{s}\log(N_{2})\big)^{18}\|P_{M,I_{1}}v\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\|P_{N_{1},I_{2}}u_{1}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\|P_{N_{2}}u_{2}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}.

In this case we have easy summation to
\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|u\|^{5}_{Y_{T}^{s}}
.

We turn to the estimate of
(II)
: Let
N^{*}=\max(M,N_{3})
. We can again use an almost orthogonal decomposition for
u_{N_{1}}
and
u_{N_{2}}
to frequency intervals of size
N^{*}
. Again the
L^{6}_{t,x}
-estimates will be the cornerstone of the analysis:

\begin{split}&\quad\iint\sum_{M\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{3}\gtrsim\ldots\gtrsim N_{5}=(*)}P_{M}vP_{N_{1},I_{1}}u_{1}\ldots P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\\
&\leq\sum_{(*)}\|P_{M}v\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\|P_{N_{1},I_{1}}u_{1}\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\|P_{N_{2},I_{2}}u_{2}\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{L^{6}_{t,x}}.\end{split}

If
N^{*}\lesssim N
, then we can use the estimate (
11
) to find

\lesssim\sum_{(*)}\|P_{M}v\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\|P_{N_{1},I_{1}}u_{1}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}\ldots\|P_{N_{5}}u_{5}\|_{Y_{T}^{0}}.

For
M\gtrsim N^{\varepsilon}
we find from straight-forward summation the estimate

\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|u\|^{5}_{Y_{T}^{s}}.

If
N^{*}\gg N
, then we can use the
L^{6}_{t,x}
-estimate (
12
) to close the estimate.
∎

To carry out the
I
-method, we introduce notations: Let
m(\xi)
be a smooth non-negative symbol on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which equals
1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and equals
|\xi|^{-1}
for
|\xi|\geq 2
. For any
N\geq 1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let
I_{N}^{\alpha}
be defined by

(I_{N}^{\alpha}f)\widehat{(}\xi)=m\big(\frac{\xi}{N}\big)^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi).

We have the following invariance lemma
[
7
, Lemma 12.1]
:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha_{0}>0
and
n\geq 1
. Suppose that
Z
,
X_{1}
,
\ldots
,
X_{n}
are translation invariant Banach spaces and
T
is a translation invariant
n
-linear operator such that we have the estimate

\|I_{1}^{\alpha}T(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\|_{Z}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|I_{1}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|_{X_{i}}

for all
u_{1}
,
\ldots
,
u_{n}
and all
0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|I_{N}^{\alpha}T(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\|_{Z}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|I_{N}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|_{X_{i}}

with implicit constant independent of
\alpha
.

Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
yield the following result on the
I
-Cauchy problem:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
0<s<1
and
0<T\sim\lambda/N
. Let
I
be a smooth radially decreasing interpolant of

(If)\widehat{(}\xi)=\begin{cases}\hat{f}(\xi),\quad&|\xi|\leq N,\\
\big(\frac{N}{|\xi|}\big)^{1-s}\hat{f}(\xi),\quad&|\xi|>2N.\end{cases}

Then the derived Cauchy problem

(16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}Iu+\partial_{xx}Iu&=\pm I(|u|^{4}u),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\lambda},\\
Iu(x,0)&=Iu_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})\end{array}\right.

is locally well-posed in
Y_{T}^{1}
provided that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}\ll 1
.

3.2.
Auxiliary local well-posedness in two dimensions

Next, we show a local well-posedness result on the unit time scale in two dimensions.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
0<s<\frac{2}{3}
and
\lambda=N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}
. Then the
I
-system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}Iu+\Delta Iu&=\pm I(|u|^{2}u),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda},\\
Iu(0)&=Iu_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})\end{array}\right.

is locally well-posed on a time-scale
T\sim\lambda^{-\delta}
for any
\delta>0
. This means for any
C>0
and
\|Iu_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\leq C
there exists a solution
Iu\in Y^{1}_{T}
which satisfies

\|Iu\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}\leq\bar{C}(C,\delta)

independently of
N\gg 1
and which depends Lipschitz-continuously on the initial data.

Proof.

We invoke the invariance lemma
3.2
to reduce to show local well-posedness

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=\pm|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})\end{array}\right.

for
0<s<1
. By the property (
5
) of
Y^{s}
-spaces and Littlewood-Paley decomposition it suffices to show estimates for ordered dyadic frequency ranges
N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq N_{3}
:

(17)

\big|\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}P_{N}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2}P_{N_{3}}u_{3}dxdt\big|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{0-}(N_{3}^{*})^{\varepsilon}(N_{4}^{*})^{\varepsilon}\|P_{N}v\|_{Y^{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}}.

for
N\sim N_{1}\gtrsim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{3}\gtrsim N_{4}
or
N\ll N_{1}\sim N_{2}
. For the proof, decompose the highest and second to highest frequency ranges
N_{1}^{*}
,
N_{2}^{*}
into balls of size
N_{3}^{*}
by almost orthogonality. Now, for a function frequency localized in a ball
B=B(x,N_{3}^{*})
we have the following Strichartz estimates as a consequence of Galilean invariance for
4\leq q\leq\infty
:

\|P_{B}u\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\lambda N_{3}^{*})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(N_{3}^{*})^{4\big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\|u\|_{Y^{0}}.

This follows from interpolating the estimates at
q=4
and
q=\infty
.

Consequently, an application of Hölder’s inequality yields for
0\leq T\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta}
:

\|P_{B}u\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\lambda^{-\delta\big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{q}\big)+\varepsilon^{\prime}}(N_{3}^{*})^{4\big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{q}\big)+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|P_{B}u\|_{Y^{0}}.

For given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
q>4
small enough and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
small enough, such that by an application of Hölder’s inequality, we find

\big|\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}P_{N}vP_{N_{1}}u_{1}P_{N_{2}}u_{2}P_{N_{3}}u_{3}dxdt\big|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{0-}(N_{3}^{*})^{\varepsilon}\|P_{N}v\|_{Y^{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}}.

The estimate suffices to show local well-posedness for
s=2\varepsilon
by a standard application of the contraction mapping principle. The details are omitted.
∎

4.
Modified energies

4.1.
Modified energies in one dimension

With the local well-posedness on the time-scale
\lambda/N
ensured, we next analyze the growth of the modified energy. We use the resonant decomposition due to Li–Wu–Xu
[
19
]
and recall the (necessary) notations. Let
n\in 2\mathbb{N}
and we define
n
-multipliers
M_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
on

\Gamma_{n}=\{\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}=0\}.

We define

\Lambda_{n}(M_{n};f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=\int_{\Gamma_{n}}M_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{f}_{j}(t,k_{j})(d\Gamma_{n})_{\lambda}\text{ with }(d\Gamma_{n})_{\lambda}=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(dk_{j})_{\lambda}

and let
\Lambda_{n}(M_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(M_{n};u,\overline{u},\ldots,u,\overline{u})
. We find by substituting the equation (
1
) for
d=1
:

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{n}(M_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(M_{n}\alpha_{n})+i\Lambda_{n+4}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}X_{j}(M_{n})\big),

where

\alpha_{n}=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}k_{j}^{2};\quad X_{j}(M_{n})=M_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j-1},k_{j}+\ldots+k_{j+4},k_{j+5},\ldots,k_{n+4}).

We define the
I
-energy by

E_{I}^{1}(u(t))=E(Iu)=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}Iu\|^{2}_{2}\pm\frac{1}{6}\|Iu(t)\|^{6}_{L^{6}}=\Lambda_{2}(\sigma_{2})\pm\Lambda_{6}(\sigma_{6}),

where

\sigma_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}m(k_{1})k_{1}m(k_{2})k_{2};\quad\sigma_{6}=\frac{1}{6}m(k_{1})\ldots m(k_{6}).

We compute by
\alpha_{2}=0
due to convolution constraint:

(18)

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{2}(\sigma_{2})=i\Lambda_{6}\big((-1)X_{1}(\sigma_{2})+X_{2}(\sigma_{2})\big)=\frac{i}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6}(-1)^{j+1}m^{2}(k_{j})k_{j}^{2}.

Secondly,

(19)

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{6}(\sigma_{6})=\Lambda_{6}(\sigma_{6}\alpha_{6})\pm\underbrace{i\Lambda_{10}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{6}(-1)^{j}X_{j}(\sigma_{6})\big)}_{M^{1}_{10}}.

We denote the sum of (
18
) and the first term from (
19
) as
M_{6}
. In the following we focus on the defocusing case to simplify notations. Since we establish estimates of the modulus of the involved terms, the same estimates work in the focusing case.

The keypoint is to show that the estimates from Li–Wu–Xu hold on the much longer time interval
T\sim\lambda/N
compared to
T\lesssim 1
. With the energy decay being the same, we can improve on the regularity from Li–Wu–Xu
[
19
]
under small mass assumption.

Let

\Upsilon=\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\in\Gamma_{6}:|k_{1}^{*}|\sim|k_{2}^{*}|\gtrsim N\}

and denote with
\chi_{X}
the characteristic function for some set
X
.

We decompose the multiplier
M_{6}^{1}
in resonant
\tilde{A}
and non-resonant
\tilde{A}^{c}
region, which will be defined in Subsection
5.1
, after using the cancellation by conservation of energy:

\begin{split}M_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})&=\frac{i}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6}(-1)^{j+1}m^{2}(k_{j})k_{j}^{2}+\sigma_{6}\alpha_{6}\\
&=(M_{6}^{1}+M_{6}^{2})(\chi_{\Upsilon}+\chi_{\Upsilon^{c}})=(M_{6}^{1}+M_{6}^{2})\chi_{\Upsilon}\\
&=\underbrace{M_{6}^{2}\chi_{\Upsilon}+M_{6}^{1}\chi_{\tilde{A}^{c}}\chi_{\Upsilon}}_{\tilde{M}_{6}}+\underbrace{M_{6}^{1}\chi_{\tilde{A}}\chi_{\Upsilon}}_{\overline{M}_{6}}.\end{split}

For
\tilde{M}_{6}
we have a favorable estimate after integration by parts. But this cannot be carried out for
\overline{M}_{6}
. We define the modified
I
-energy
E_{I}^{2}(u(t))
by

E_{I}^{2}(u(t))=E_{I}^{1}(u(t))+\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6}),\quad\tilde{\sigma}_{6}=-\tilde{M}_{6}/\alpha_{6}.

Now it holds

\frac{d}{dt}E_{I}^{2}(u(t))=\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})+\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10}),

where

\overline{M}_{10}=i\sum_{j=1}^{6}(-1)^{j}(X_{j}(\sigma_{6})+X_{j}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})).

Below we use the following formula:

(20)

E_{I}^{1}(u(t))=E_{I}^{1}(u(0))-\big[\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})\big]_{0}^{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})+\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10})ds.

In the following we focus on the proof of the following estimates for
0<t\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{N}
and
\frac{1}{3}<s<\frac{1}{2}
:

(21)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{0}^{t}\int\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\hat{u}(s,k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(s,k_{6})d\Gamma_{6}ds\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|^{6}_{Y_{T}^{1}},

(22)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{0}^{t}\int\overline{M}_{10}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{10})\hat{u}(s,k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(s,k_{10})d\Gamma_{10}ds\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|^{10}_{Y_{T}^{1}},

(23)

\displaystyle\big|\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})(t)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|Iu(t)\|^{6}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}.

The first and second estimate translate to favorable energy estimates on the extended time-interval of length
\frac{\lambda}{N}
. Since
|\sigma_{6}|,|\tilde{\sigma}_{6}|\lesssim 1
, the proof of the second and third estimate is more straight-forward. The bulk of the work is to estimate the resonant part. Moreover, we need to establish size and regularity estimates of the multilinear Fourier multipliers. The size estimates are essentially due to Li–Wu–Xu
[
19
]
. However, the regularity was not analyzed in
[
19
]
, which will be done through Fourier series argument in the forthcoming sections. For this reason also the analysis of the size is repeated.

4.2.
Modified energies in two dimensions

We turn to the analysis in two dimensions.
The energy is given by

E^{1}(u)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}\frac{|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}}{2}dx\pm\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}\frac{|u|^{4}}{4}dx,

and we decompose the
I
-energy
E_{I}=E^{1}(Iu)=\Lambda_{2}(\sigma_{2})\pm\Lambda_{4}(\sigma_{4})
with

\sigma_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}k_{1}m(k_{1})\cdot k_{2}m(k_{2}),\quad\sigma_{4}=\frac{1}{4}m(k_{1})\ldots m(k_{4}).

Then we compute from substituting (
1
) and symmetrization

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{2}(\sigma_{2})=\Lambda_{4}(M_{4}),\quad M_{4}=\frac{i}{4}(-k_{1}^{2}m^{2}(k_{1})+k_{2}^{2}m^{2}(k_{2})-k_{3}^{2}m^{2}(k_{3})+k_{4}^{2}m^{2}(k_{4})).

Secondly,

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{4}(\sigma_{4})=\frac{i}{4}m(k_{1})\ldots m(k_{4})(-k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}-k_{3}^{2}+k_{4}^{2})\pm i\sum_{j=1}^{4}X_{j}(\sigma_{4}).

We carry out a very simple resonant decomposition, using a correction term for the first term of the above display and some part of
M_{4}
. The aim is to remain with the estimate
|\overline{M}_{4}|\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}
for the remainder term
\overline{M}_{4}
of
M_{4}
.

To this end, we suppose that
|k_{i}|\sim N_{i}
. If
N_{2},N_{4}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{3}
or
N_{1},N_{3}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{4}
,
then clearly the resonance function

\Omega_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})=k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}-k_{4}^{2},\quad(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\in\Gamma_{4}

satisfies the size estimate
|\Omega_{4}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})|\gtrsim(N_{1}^{*})^{2}
. We define the relevant region (since the multipliers become trivial in the complement due to energy conservation and convolution constraint):

\Upsilon=\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\in\Gamma_{4}:\,|k_{i}|\sim N_{i},\;N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gtrsim N\}.

We define the non-resonant region by

\tilde{A}=\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\in\Upsilon:\,|k_{1}|\sim|k_{3}|\gg N_{3}^{*}\text{ or }|k_{2}|\sim|k_{4}|\gg N_{3}^{*}\}.

With the non-resonant region given by
\tilde{A}
we define the correction term

\tilde{\sigma}_{4}=\mp\sigma_{4}-\underbrace{i\frac{k_{1}^{2}m^{2}(k_{1})-m^{2}(k_{2})k_{2}^{2}+m^{2}(k_{3})k_{3}^{2}-m^{2}(k_{4})k_{4}^{2}}{\Omega_{4}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})}\chi_{\tilde{A}}}_{\sigma_{4,2}}.

We define the modified
I
-energy by

E_{I}^{2}=E_{I}^{1}+\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4}),

and it follows

\frac{d}{dt}E_{I}^{2}=\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})+\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})

with

\overline{M}_{4}=M_{4}\cdot\chi_{\Upsilon\cap\tilde{A}^{c}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{M}_{6}=\pm i\sum_{j=1}^{4}(X_{j}(\sigma_{4})+X_{j}(\sigma_{4,2})).

We rewrite the formula for
E_{I}^{1}
by the fundamental theorem of calculus as

E_{I}^{1}(t)=E_{I}^{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})ds-[\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)-\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(0)].

We shall establish the following estimates for
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
and
0\leq T\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta}
:

(24)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})ds\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-1+}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|Iu\|^{4}_{Y^{1}_{T}},

(25)

\displaystyle\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})ds\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-2+}\|Iu\|^{6}_{Y^{1}_{T}},

(26)

\displaystyle\big|\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|Iu(t)\|^{4}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.

The main part is to show estimates for
\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})
. The estimates for
\overline{M}_{6}
and the boundary term are simpler because the multiplier is bounded.

5.
Energy growth bounds in one dimension

5.1.
Estimate of the resonant contribution

5.1.1.
Resonant decomposition

In the following we shall estimate the multiplier

M_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})=\sum_{i=1}^{6}(-1)^{i+1}m^{2}(k_{i})k_{i}^{2}

on
\Gamma_{6}
after dyadic localization
|k_{i}|\sim N_{i}\in 2^{N_{0}}
against the resonance function

\Omega_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})=\sum_{i=1}^{6}(-1)^{i+1}k_{i}^{2}.

By symmetry we suppose in the following

(27)

N_{1}\geq N_{3}\geq N_{5},\,N_{2}\geq N_{4}\geq N_{6},\text{ and }N_{1}\geq N_{2}.

By Littlewood-Paley dichotomy we have
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}
. In the following we carry out possibly finer decompositions than on the dyadic scale. We shall only consider additional decompositions into intervals with tractable summation.
Consider

A=\{\underline{k}\in\Gamma_{6}:|\Omega_{6}|\lesssim|M_{6}|\}.

We shall find
\tilde{A}\supseteq A
with
\tilde{A}
given as union of products of intervals and let

\overline{M}_{6}=M_{6}\chi_{\tilde{A}}=\sum_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{6}}M_{6}\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6}).

Here
\chi_{I}
denotes the indicator function for the interval
I
.
The contribution of
\overline{M}_{6}
will then be estimated with size and regularity of
M_{6}
on
I_{1},\ldots,I_{6}
(see Proposition
5.6
) and linear and bilinear Strichartz estimates.

It is easy to see that the following cases are non-resonant:

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
|k_{i}|\sim N_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,6
, and (
27
) holds. If

(28)

N_{2}\ll N_{1},

or

(29)

N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gtrsim N_{3}^{*}\gg N_{4}^{*},

then we are in the non-resonant case

(30)

|M_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})|\lesssim|\Omega_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})|.

Proof.

In case of (
28
), it is immediate that

|\Omega_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})|=|k_{1}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}+k_{5}^{2}-(k_{2}^{2}+k_{4}^{2}+k_{6}^{2})|\gtrsim N_{1}^{2}-N_{2}^{2}\gtrsim N_{1}^{2}.

So, when we consider (
29
), we suppose that
\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\}=\{N_{1}^{*},N_{2}^{*},N_{3}^{*}\}
or
\{N_{1}^{*},N_{2}^{*},N_{3}^{*}\}=\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{4}\}
.
We obtain from convolution constraint in the first scenario

\begin{split}\Omega_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})&=k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}+O((N_{4}^{*})^{2})=(k_{1}-k_{2})(k_{1}+k_{2})+k_{3}^{2}+O((N_{4}^{*})^{2})\\
&=(k_{2}-k_{1}-k_{3})k_{3}+O(N_{1}^{*}N_{4}^{*})\\
&=-2k_{1}k_{3}+O(N_{1}^{*}N_{4}^{*}).\end{split}

Hence, the lower bound
|\Omega_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})|\gtrsim N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}
is immediate. In the second scenario
\{N_{1}^{*},N_{2}^{*},N_{3}^{*}\}=\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{4}\}
we can argue symmetrically.

If
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{3}^{*}
, the size estimate
|M_{6}|\lesssim N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}
is trivial. If
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gg N_{3}^{*}
we have
|M_{6}|\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}
by invoking the mean value theorem to estimate
|m^{2}(k_{1})k_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(k_{2})k_{2}^{2}|\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}
(see Proposition
5.6
(i)).
∎

The following lemma yields our criterion for non-resonance in case the third highest frequency is much lower than the highest frequency.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
|k_{i}|\sim N_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,6
, and (
27
) holds. Moreover, suppose that
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gg N_{3}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}
, and
\{N_{1}^{*},N_{2}^{*}\}=\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, and
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are of different signs. If
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\sim N_{12}\gg\frac{(N_{3}^{*})^{2}}{N_{1}^{*}}
, then we have
|\Omega_{6}|\gtrsim N_{1}^{*}N_{12}\gtrsim|M_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})|
.

Proof.

We write

\Omega_{6}=(k_{1}-k_{2})(k_{1}+k_{2})+k_{3}^{2}-k_{4}^{2}+k_{5}^{2}-k_{6}^{2}=(k_{1}-k_{2})(k_{1}+k_{2})+O((N_{3}^{*})^{2}).

With
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\sim N_{1}^{*}
, we find that under our assumption
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\sim N_{12}\gg\frac{(N_{3}^{*})^{2}}{N_{1}^{*}}
, we have

|\Omega_{6}|\sim N_{12}N_{1}^{*}.

For an upper bound on
M_{6}
we write (supposing that
k_{1}>-k_{2}
, the other case is symmetric):

\begin{split}M_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})&=m^{2}(k_{1})k_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(k_{2})k_{2}^{2}+m^{2}(k_{3})k_{3}^{2}-\ldots-m^{2}(k_{6})k_{6}^{2}\\
&=\int_{-k_{2}}^{k_{1}}f^{\prime}(\xi)d\xi+O(m^{2}(N_{3}^{*})(N_{3}^{*})^{2})\\
&=(k_{1}+k_{2})f^{\prime}(k_{*})+O(m^{2}(N_{3}^{*})(N_{3}^{*})^{2})\end{split}

with
f(\xi)=m^{2}(\xi)\xi^{2}
. This yields the claimed estimate by our assumptions on
|k_{1}+k_{2}|
.
∎

Note that the above lemma amounts to introducing a bilinear Fourier localization to describe the non- resonant set. On the other hand, for a superset of the resonant set, where
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\lesssim\frac{(N_{3}^{*})^{2}}{N_{1}^{*}}
, we can localize
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
to intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
of size
\frac{(N_{3}^{*})^{2}}{N_{1}^{*}}
which summation can be carried out via almost orthogonality.

Lastly, we consider the case with four comparable high frequencies and the fifth frequency being significantly lower.

Suppose that
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{4}^{*}\gg N_{5}^{*}

(ii)

are not of the same sign. If

,

(iii)

|\Omega_{6}|\gtrsim(N_{1}^{*})^{2}

k_{i}
and

|k_{i}+k_{2}|\ll N_{1}^{*}

Ad (i): Suppose that we are in Case (

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that there is

\overline{M}^{\mathbb{R}}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6})=\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6})\text{ on }\Gamma_{6},

\big|m_{6}(\frac{\xi_{1}}{L_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\xi_{6}}{L_{6}})\big|\lesssim L_{1}\ldots L_{6}\overline{M}_{6}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{6})\langle\xi_{\max}\rangle^{-15}.

For
\xi=0

37
) for
\alpha=0

\begin{split}&\quad\int\big(\frac{L_{i}}{-i\xi_{j}}\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell_{j}}\big(e^{-i\frac{\xi_{j}\ell_{j}}{L_{j}}}\big)\big(\prod_{i\neq j}e^{-i\frac{\xi_{i}\ell_{i}}{L_{i}}}\big)\overline{M}_{6}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{6})d\ell_{1}\ldots d\ell_{6}\\
&=\frac{L_{j}}{(-i\xi_{j})}\int\big(e^{-i\frac{\xi_{1}\ell_{1}}{L_{1}}}\ldots e^{-i\frac{\xi_{6}\ell_{6}}{L_{6}}}\big)\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell_{j}}\overline{M}_{6}(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{6})\big)d\ell_{1}\ldots d\ell_{6}.\end{split}

Remark 5.5
.

We use the Fourier series expansion in the following way: We want to estimate

\int_{\Gamma_{6}\times[0,T]}\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6})\hat{u}_{1}(k_{1},t)\ldots\hat{u}_{6}(k_{6},t)d\Gamma_{6}dt.

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Gamma_{6}\times[0,T]}\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6})\hat{u}_{1}(k_{1},t)\ldots\hat{u}_{6}(k_{6},t)d\Gamma_{6}dt\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{6}\times[0,T]}\frac{1}{L_{1}\ldots L_{6}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{6}\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{6}\big(\frac{\xi_{1}}{L_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\xi_{6}}{L_{6}}\big)e^{i\frac{k_{1}\xi_{1}}{L_{1}}}\ldots e^{i\frac{k_{6}\xi_{6}}{L_{6}}}\prod_{i=1}^{6}\hat{u}_{i}(k_{i},t)d\Gamma_{6}dt\\
&=\frac{1}{L_{1}\ldots L_{6}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{6}\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{6}(\frac{\xi_{1}}{L_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\xi_{6}}{L_{6}})\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}\times[0,T]}u_{1}(x+\frac{\xi_{1}}{L_{1}},t)\ldots u_{6}(x+\frac{\xi_{6}}{L_{6}},t)dxdt.\end{split}

\begin{split}&\quad\big|\int_{\Gamma_{6}\times[0,T]}\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\chi_{I_{1}}(k_{1})\ldots\chi_{I_{6}}(k_{6})\hat{u}_{1}(k_{1},t)\ldots\hat{u}_{6}(k_{6},t)d\Gamma_{6}dt\big|\\
&\lesssim\overline{M}_{6}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{6})C(I_{1},\ldots,I_{6})\prod_{i=1}^{6}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}.\end{split}

.

We have the following size and regularity estimates for

The key decay estimate is proved in the following proposition:

Proposition 5.10
.

Suppose that

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10})ds\big|\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|_{Y_{T}^{1}}^{10}.

Proof.

With the above reductions, we can focus on the estimate

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}}P_{K_{1}}u(t,x)\ldots P_{K_{10}}u(t,x)dxds\big|\lesssim N^{-3+}(K_{1}^{*})^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{10}\|P_{K_{i}}Iu\|_{Y_{i,T}^{1}}.

K_{i}=K_{i}^{*}

\lesssim\frac{\log(N)^{2}}{N^{3}}K_{1}^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{10}\|P_{K_{i}}Iu\|_{Y^{1}_{i,T}}.

Case

Proposition 5.11
.

\big|\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})(t)\big|\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|Iu(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{6}.

6.
Energy growth bounds in two dimensions

6.1.
Estimate of the resonant contribution

The first step is to show size and regularity estimates for
\overline{M_{4}}
. The following analysis remains fairly rough. To improve on this, one should look into a refined resonant decomposition of
M_{4}
.
In the following we suppose dyadic frequency localization

|k_{i}|\sim N_{i},\quad N_{1}\geq N_{3},\quad N_{2}\geq N_{4}.

We show the following straight-forward size and regularity estimate:

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose the frequencies are in dyadic ranges like above and
N_{1}\sim N_{2}
. Then the following size and regularity estimate holds:

(57)

|\overline{M_{4}}|\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}.

Proof.

In case
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{3}^{*}
the claimed size and regularity estimate is immediate since
m^{2}(N_{i})N_{i}^{2}\lesssim m^{2}(N_{1}^{*})(N_{1}^{*})^{2}
by monotonicity of the function
m(k)k
for
k\in[1,\infty)
. In case
N_{1}^{*}\gg N_{3}^{*}
we firstly decompose the frequency ranges of
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
(corresponding to
N_{1}^{*}
and
N_{2}^{*}
) into balls of size
N_{3}^{*}
, which causes no summation loss by almost orthogonality. With
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\sim N_{1}\sim N_{2}
by our assumptions, we can use the mean-value theorem on
m^{2}(k_{1})k_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(k_{2})(-k_{2})^{2}
to estimate

|m^{2}(k_{1})k_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(-k_{2})(-k_{2})^{2}|\lesssim|\nabla f(\xi_{*})||k_{1}+k_{2}|

with
f(\xi)=m^{2}(\xi)|\xi|^{2}
. With
\xi_{*}\in\{k_{1}+t(-k_{2}-k_{1}):t\in[0,1]\}
the estimate
|\xi_{*}|\gtrsim N_{1}
is evident (because by convolution constraint
|k_{1}+k_{2}|\lesssim N_{3}^{*}\ll N_{1}
).

This gives
|\nabla f(\xi_{*})|\sim m^{2}(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}
and, in conclusion,

|m^{2}(k_{1})k_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(-k_{2})(-k_{2})^{2}|\lesssim m^{2}(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}.

Hence,

|M_{4}|\lesssim m^{2}(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}+m^{2}(N_{3}^{*})(N_{3}^{*})^{2}\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})m(N_{3}^{*})N_{1}^{*}N_{3}^{*}.

The regularity of the trivial extension

M_{4}^{\mathbb{R}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{4})=m^{2}(\xi_{1})\xi_{1}^{2}-m^{2}(\xi_{2})\xi_{2}^{2}+m^{2}(\xi_{3})\xi_{3}^{2}-m^{2}(\xi_{4})\xi_{4}^{2}

is checked like in Proposition
5.6
. For the estimates in
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
we use the additional localization in Fourier space to argue

|\partial_{\xi_{i}}^{\alpha}M_{4}|\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}(N_{3}^{*})^{-\alpha}\text{ for }i=1,2.

∎

We are ready to prove the estimate for the energy growth of the resonant part:

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
0\leq T\leq\lambda^{-\delta}
and
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
. Let
\lambda=N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}
. Then the following estimate holds for any
\varepsilon>0

(58)

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})ds\big|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{-1+\varepsilon}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|Iu\|^{4}_{Y^{1}_{T}}.

Proof.

Since the multiplier is otherwise trivial we can suppose that
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gtrsim N
. By symmetry, we suppose like above
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gg N_{3}^{*}
.

Case A
N_{3}^{*}\ll N
. In this case the size and regularity estimate holds with
|M_{4}|\lesssim m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}
. In the following suppose that
N_{3}^{*}=N_{3}
to ease notation. After using the Fourier series argument and changing to position space, we can use two bilinear Strichartz estimates (due to large frequency separation):

\begin{split}&\quad\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{4}}M_{4}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\prod_{i=1}^{4}\chi_{I_{i}}\hat{u}_{i}(k_{i})d\Gamma_{4}ds\big|\\
&\lesssim m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}}u_{1}(t,x)\ldots u_{4}(t,x)dxdt\big|\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}(\lambda N_{1})^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{3}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{4}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|P_{N_{i}}u\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.\end{split}

We analyze the expression depending on the size of
N_{1}
relative to
N_{3}
and
N_{4}
.

Case AI

N_{3}\gtrsim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Note that for
s\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}]
,
\lambda^{-1}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and therefore, by
N\lesssim N_{1}
and
N_{3}\gtrsim N^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{3}}{N_{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{N_{3}}{N}\lesssim\frac{N_{3}}{N}.

Subcase AI.i

N_{3}\sim N_{4}
. In this case we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{2})N_{2}(N^{-(1-s)}N_{2}^{-s})N_{3}\frac{N_{4}}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\varepsilon}N^{-2+}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|P_{N_{i}}u\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.\end{split}

This is sufficient.

Subcase AI.ii

N_{4}\ll N_{3}
. Then the above expression becomes

\begin{split}&\quad N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{2})N_{2}(N^{-(1-s)}N_{2}^{-s})N_{3}\big(\frac{N_{3}}{N}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\lambda^{-1}+\frac{N_{4}}{N}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\varepsilon}N^{-1+}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}N_{i}m(N_{i}).\end{split}

The ultimate step is clear if
\lambda^{-1}\geq N_{4}/N
. If
N_{4}/N\geq\lambda^{-1}
, we can verify that
(N_{4}N)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
s\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}]
.

Case AII

N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In this case we have

\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{i}}{N_{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\text{ for }i=3,4,

and we find

N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{2})N_{2}(N^{-(1-s)}N_{2}^{-s})N_{3}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{4}}}\big(\frac{N_{4}}{N}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-\varepsilon}N^{-\frac{5}{4}+}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i},

which suffices.

Case B

N_{3}^{*}\gtrsim N
and
N_{4}^{*}\ll N
. In this case we can use one bilinear Strichartz estimate involving
u_{N_{1}^{*}}
and
u_{N_{4}^{*}}
and two
L^{4}_{t,x}
-Strichartz estimates to find by the Fourier series argument:

\begin{split}&\quad\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{4}}\overline{M}_{4}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\hat{u}_{1}(k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(k_{4})d\Gamma_{4}ds\big|\\
&\lesssim m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}}u_{1}\ldots u_{4}(s,x)dxds\big|\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{N_{4}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{2})N_{2}(N^{-(1-s)}N_{2}^{-s})m(N_{3})N_{3}\frac{m(N_{4})N_{4}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\varepsilon}N^{-1+}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.\end{split}

This suffices.

Case C

N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N
. In this case we infer from
L^{4}_{t,x}
-Strichartz estimates and the estimate
(m(N_{i})N_{i})^{-1}\lesssim N^{-(1-s)}N_{i}^{-s}
:

\begin{split}&\quad\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{4}}\overline{M}_{4}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{4})\hat{u}_{1}(k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(k_{4})d\Gamma_{4}ds\big|\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{1}^{\varepsilon}m(N_{1}^{*})N_{1}^{*}m(N_{3}^{*})N_{3}^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\\
&\lesssim N^{-2+}N_{1}^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}},\end{split}

which suffices.
∎

6.2.
Estimate of the non-resonant contribution

We turn to estimates for the boundary term. Under the dyadic frequency localization
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gg N_{3}^{*}
or
N_{2}\sim N_{4}\gg N_{3}^{*}
we can use almost orthogonality to additionally localize the range of
k_{1}
and
k_{3}
to balls of size
N_{3}^{*}
. Then we can argue like above to obtain the following:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gg N_{3}^{*}
or
N_{2}\sim N_{4}\gg N_{3}^{*}
. Then it holds the size and regularity estimates:

\big|\frac{M_{4}}{\Omega_{4}}\big|\lesssim 1.

We are ready to estimate the boundary term:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
. Then it holds for
\delta=\delta(s)

(59)

|\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)|\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|Iu(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}^{4}.

Proof.

We denote
u_{i}^{*}=P_{N_{i}}u
. Using the Fourier series argument, it suffices to show

\big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}u_{1}^{*}(x)\ldots u_{4}^{*}(x)dx\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{-\varepsilon}N^{-\delta}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.

We apply Hölder’s and Bernstein’s inequality to find

\begin{split}\big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}u_{1}^{*}(x)\ldots u_{4}^{*}(x)dx\big|&\lesssim\|P_{N_{1}^{*}}u_{1}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\|P_{N_{2}^{*}}u_{2}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\|P_{N_{3}^{*}}u_{3}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\|P_{N_{4}^{*}}u_{4}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\\
&\lesssim N_{3}^{*}N_{4}^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.\end{split}

Suppose that
N_{1}^{*}\sim N_{2}^{*}\gtrsim N
and
N_{3}^{*}\lesssim N
.
Since
(m(N_{i}^{*})N_{i}^{*})^{-1}\lesssim N^{-(1-s)}(N_{1}^{*})^{-s}
for

, we find

\big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}}u_{1}^{*}\ldots u_{4}^{*}dx\big|\lesssim N^{-2+}(N_{1}^{*})^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|P_{N_{i}}u_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.

If
N_{3}^{*}\gtrsim N
and
N_{4}^{*}\ll N
, note that for
\frac{1}{3}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
, we have

N_{3}^{*}\lesssim N^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{3}m(N_{i}^{*})N_{i}^{*},

which finishes this case.

Finally, suppose that
N_{4}^{*}\gtrsim N
.
Since
m(N_{i}^{*})N_{i}^{*}=\frac{N^{1-s}}{(N_{i}^{*})^{1-s}}N_{i}^{*}=N^{1-s}(N_{i}^{*})^{s}
, we have for

(N_{i}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{s}m(N_{i}^{*})N_{i}^{*}N^{-\frac{1}{3}}(N_{i}^{*})^{0-}.

Consequently,

(N_{3}^{*})^{\frac{3}{2}}(N_{4}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{4}{3}}(N_{1}^{*})^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i}^{*})N_{i}^{*}.

The proof is complete.
∎

6.3.
Estimate for the non-resonant part

In the following we show a growth bound for the non-resonant part:

Proposition 6.5
.

For
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
the following estimate holds:

(60)

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})dt\big|\lesssim N^{-2+}\prod_{i=1}^{6}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.

Recall that

\overline{M}_{6}=\pm i(\sum_{j=1}^{4}X_{j}(\sigma_{4})+X_{j}(\sigma_{4,2})).

Since we have already showed the size and regularity estimate for

\sigma_{4,2}=\frac{M_{4}}{\Omega_{4}}\cdot\chi_{\tilde{A}},

it remains to check the size and regularity estimate for

\sigma_{4}=m(k_{1})\ldots m(k_{4}),

which is straight-forward and recorded in the following:

Lemma 6.6
.

\sigma_{4}
satisfies the size and regularity estimate:

|\sigma_{4}|\lesssim 1.

This way we can apply the Fourier series argument to
\sigma_{4}
and
\sigma_{4,2}
to reduce the estimate (

) to the following:

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{2}{3}
and

. Suppose that
u_{i}
is frequency localized at
N_{i}
and
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N_{3}\ldots\gtrsim N_{6}
with
N_{1}\gtrsim N
. Then the following estimate holds:

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}}u_{1}(t,x)\ldots u_{6}(t,x)dxdt\big|\lesssim N^{-2+}N_{1}^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{6}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
N_{5}\ll N
. Then we can use four linear Strichartz estimates to find

\begin{split}\big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}}u_{1}(t,x)\ldots u_{6}(t,x)dxdt\big|&\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\|u_{5}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|u_{6}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(\lambda N_{1})^{\varepsilon}N_{5}N_{6}\prod_{i=1}^{6}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.\end{split}

Since
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N
, we have

(m(N_{1})N_{1}m(N_{2})N_{2})^{-1}\lesssim N^{-2(1-s)}N_{1}^{-2s}

and thus,

(\lambda N_{1})^{\varepsilon}N_{5}N_{6}\prod_{i=1}^{6}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}\lesssim N^{-2+}N_{1}^{0-}\prod_{i=1}^{6}m(N_{i})N_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{Y^{0}_{T}}.

Suppose that
N_{5}\gtrsim N
or
N_{6}\gtrsim N
. We still find

N_{5}N_{6}\lesssim m(N_{5})N_{5}m(N_{6})N_{6}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{4}m(N_{i})N_{i}\big)N_{1}^{0-}N^{-2+},

which allows us to conclude like above.
∎

7.
Conclusion of the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We give a sketch of the argument.
The slow energy growth
N^{-3+}
on the time interval
\lambda/N
tells us that we can iterate the local well-posedness
N^{3-}
times until the energy has grown significantly. This gives for the rescaled solution
u_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
a time interval of existence of

\frac{\lambda}{N}\cdot N^{3-}=\lambda N^{2-}.

Scaling back we obtain that the solution
u
exists on a time scale

T=N^{2-}\lambda^{-1}=N^{-\frac{1}{s}+3-}.

Consequently, for
s>\frac{1}{3}
, we obtain global well-posedness letting
N\to\infty
.

We turn to the details. To make the continuity argument transparent, we collect the previously established estimates and implicit constants.
Firstly, there is a universal constant
C_{1}\geq 1
such that

\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{1}(E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda})(t)+\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

In the defocusing case this is clear, whereas in the focusing case this requires an application of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and the small mass assumption.
Secondly, there is a universal constant
C_{2}\geq 1
such that

E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})\leq C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

By Proposition
3.1
, there is
\bar{C}>0
such that

\|Iu_{\lambda}\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}\leq\bar{C}

for
T=\frac{\lambda}{N}
provided that

\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}\leq c\ll 1.

Let
c_{2}\leq c/(10C_{2}C_{3})
. The key bounds to control the energy growth, which were proved in Propositions
5.7
,
5.10
, and
5.11
, are presently recalled:

(61)

\begin{split}\big|\int_{0}^{t}\int\overline{M}_{6}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{6})\hat{u}(s,k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(s,k_{6})d\Gamma_{6}ds\big|&\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|^{6}_{Y_{T}^{1}},\\
\big|\int_{0}^{t}\int\overline{M}_{10}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{10})\hat{u}(s,k_{1})\ldots\hat{\overline{u}}(s,k_{10})d\Gamma_{10}ds\big|&\lesssim N^{-3+}\|Iu\|^{10}_{Y_{T}^{1}},\\
\big|\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})(t)\big|&\lesssim N^{-\delta}\|Iu(t)\|^{6}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}.\end{split}

We show the following:

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose that

\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}\leq c_{2}.

Choose
N
large enough such that

N^{-\varepsilon}C_{1}(\bar{C}^{6}+\bar{C}^{10})\leq C_{1}C_{2}c,\quad C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}(2(10C_{1}C_{2}c)^{6})\leq C_{2}c.

Then, for
T=\lambda N^{2-2\varepsilon}
, the following estimate holds:

\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{2}\leq 10C_{2}C_{3}c_{2}\ll 1.

Proof.

The proof is carried out via a continuity argument.
For times
0\leq t\leq\frac{\lambda}{N}
this follows from the auxiliary local well-posedness result. We turn to larger times: Write

\begin{split}E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda}(t))&=E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})-\big(\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6}(t))-\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})(0))\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10})ds.\end{split}

Suppose that
\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}^{2}\leq 10C_{1}C_{2}c
and
0\leq t\leq N^{2-}\lambda
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T\in[\frac{(M-1)\lambda}{N},\frac{M\lambda}{N}]
. By the above display and the bounds (
61
), we obtain:

\begin{split}\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}&\leq C_{1}(E_{I}^{1}u_{\lambda}(t)+\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\leq C_{1}(E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})-\big(\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma}_{6})(t)-\Lambda_{6}(\tilde{\sigma})(0)\big)\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{M-2}\int_{[k\lambda/N,(k+1)\lambda/N]}\big(\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})+\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10})\big)ds\\
&\quad+\int_{[\frac{(M-1)\lambda}{N},T]}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})+\Lambda_{10}(\overline{M}_{10})ds\\
&\leq C_{1}(E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})+C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}\big(\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{6}+\|Iu_{\lambda}(0)\|_{H^{1}}^{6}\big)+MN^{-3+}(\bar{C}^{6}+\bar{C}^{10})\big)\\
&\leq C_{1}(C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+MN^{-3+}(\bar{C}^{6}+\bar{C}^{10})+C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}(2(10C_{1}C_{2}c)^{6})\\
&\leq 5C_{1}C_{2}c.\end{split}

Hence, we have small
H^{1}
-norms on a timescale
N^{2-}\lambda
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>\frac{1}{3}
with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\ll 1
. We rescale
u_{0\lambda}
to the large torus
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
and obtain with the choice
\lambda=N^{\frac{1-s}{s}+\varepsilon}
:

\begin{split}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}&\leq 2(\|u_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}+N^{2(1-s)}\|u_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})})\\
&\leq 2(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+N^{2(1-s)}\lambda^{-2s}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})})\\
&\leq 2(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+N^{-2s\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})}).\end{split}

Now we can choose
N=N(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})})
large enough such that

\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}\leq c_{2}\ll 1

with
c_{2}
like in Proposition
7.1
.
By Proposition
7.1
the
I
-Cauchy problem (
16
) can be solved for times
T\sim\lambda N^{2-}
.

Scaling back, we find that the solution on
\mathbb{T}
exists for times

T^{*}\sim\lambda^{-1}N^{2-}=N^{3-\frac{1}{s}-}.

Hence, for
s>\frac{1}{3}
and letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain global well-posedness.

∎

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We collect the proved estimates and formulae for convenience.

Starting with local solutions to

(62)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u&=\pm|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\underline{\gamma}}^{2})\end{array}\right.

for
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
, we rescale to tori
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}
with large period and damp the high frequencies to consider the
I
-system

(63)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}i\partial_{t}Iu_{\lambda}+\Delta Iu_{\lambda}&=\pm I(|u_{\lambda}|^{2}u_{\lambda}),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda},\\
Iu_{\lambda}(0)&=Iu_{\lambda 0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda}).\end{array}\right.

With our choice of
\lambda=N^{\frac{1-s}{s}}
we accomplish

\|Iu_{\lambda}(0)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\lesssim\|u_{\lambda 0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})},

and we have local well-posedness of (
63
) on times
T=\lambda^{-\delta}
.

We use the
I
-energy as an almost conserved quantity:

E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda})(t)=E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda 0})-\big[\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)-\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(0)\big]+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})+\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})\big)ds.

The relevant growth bounds for
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
and
0\leq T\leq\lambda^{-\delta}
are given by Proposition
6.2
and
6.5
:

\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})ds\big|\lesssim N^{-1+}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|Iu_{\lambda}\|^{4}_{Y^{1}_{T}},\quad\big|\int_{0}^{T}\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})ds\big|\lesssim N^{-2+}\|Iu_{\lambda}\|^{6}_{Y^{1}_{T}}.

Note that the estimate for the resonant part is clearly inferior to the estimate for the non-resonant part.
The estimate for the boundary term for
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq\frac{2}{3}
is given by some
C_{\delta}
and
N^{-\delta}

\big|\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)\big|\leq C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|^{4}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.

In the defocusing case we have

(64)

\|Iu_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\leq 2E_{I}^{1}(u(t)),\quad\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\leq\|u_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}.

For the first estimate to hold in the focusing case, we require a smallness condition on
\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda})}=\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma})}\ll 1
. Then follows from the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Ladyzhenskaya inequality

\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\ll\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Consequently, we obtain as well in the focusing case:

\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq 2\big(\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}}\frac{|Iu_{\lambda}|^{4}}{4}dx\big).

We choose
N
large enough depending on the following.
Firstly, note that there is a universal constant
C_{2}
such that

E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})\leq C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Given
u_{0}
, by the auxiliary local well-posedness we find
\bar{C}
such that

\|Iv_{\lambda}\|_{Y^{1}_{T}}\leq\bar{C}\|Iv_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}

for
T\leq\lambda^{-\delta}
as long as

\|Iv_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq 10C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

Now we choose
N\gg 1
such that

N^{-\varepsilon}\big(\bar{C}^{4}(5C_{2})^{4}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}^{4}+\bar{C}^{6}(5C_{2})^{6}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}^{6})\leq\|Iu_{0}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}^{2}.

Secondly, we choose
N\gg 1
possibly larger such that

26C_{2}C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}^{4}\leq\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H_{\lambda}^{1}}^{2}.

With this choice of
N
, we show the following proposition:

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose that
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\gamma})}\ll 1
in the focusing case such that (
64
) holds. There is
N=N(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
such that for
0\leq t\leq T\lesssim N^{1-}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
the following estimate holds for the solution to (
63
):

(65)

\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\lambda})}\leq 5C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Proof.

We use a continuity argument. The claim holds for
|t|\leq\lambda^{-\delta}
by the auxiliary local well-posedness. Next suppose (
65
) holds up to
T\ll N^{1-}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Write
T=M\lambda^{-\delta}
for
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we estimate

\begin{split}E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda}(T))&=E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})-\big[\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(t)-\Lambda_{4}(\tilde{\sigma}_{4})(0)\big]+\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-\delta}}\big(\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})+\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})\big)\\
&\quad+\int_{\lambda^{-\delta}}^{2\lambda^{-\delta}}\big(\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})+\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})\big)+\ldots+\int_{(M-1)\lambda^{-\delta}}^{M\lambda^{-\delta}}\big(\Lambda_{4}(\overline{M}_{4})+\Lambda_{6}(\overline{M}_{6})\big).\end{split}

By the bootstrap assumption we have the following estimate for the boundary term:

C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}(\|Iu_{\lambda}(t)\|^{4}_{H^{1}}+\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{4})\leq C_{\delta}N^{-\delta}26C_{2}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{4}_{H^{1}}\leq\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Consequently,

\begin{split}E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda}(T))&\leq E_{I}^{1}(u_{0\lambda})+\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\\
&\quad+M\lambda^{-\delta}N^{-1+\varepsilon}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{-\varepsilon}\tilde{C}(\bar{C}^{4}(5C_{2})^{4}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{4}+\bar{C}^{6}(5C_{2})^{6}\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|^{6}_{H^{1}}).\end{split}

The final term can be bounded by
\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
and thus,

\|Iu_{\lambda}(T)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq E_{I}^{1}(u_{\lambda}(T))+\|Iu_{\lambda}(T)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 4\|Iu_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Hence, we can continue the bootstrap assumption until
T\sim N^{1-}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

With the above proposition at hand, the conclusion is now a matter of rescaling:

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We choose
N
large enough to have (
65
) from Proposition
7.2
. Reversing the scaling we find that the solution to (
62
) exists on a time

T\sim\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2}}N^{1-}\sim N^{-\frac{3}{2s}+\frac{5}{2}-}.

The exponent is positive provided that
s>\frac{3}{5}
. Hence, taking
N
to infinity for
s>\frac{3}{5}
establishes global well-posedness.
∎

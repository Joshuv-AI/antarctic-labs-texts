Extremal Process of Last Progeny Modified Branching Random Walks
Partha Pratim Ghosh, Bastien Mallein

Abstract
. We consider a last progeny modified branching random walk, in which the position of each particle at the last generation n n is modified by an i.i.d. copy of a random variable Y Y . Depending on the asymptotic properties of the tail of Y Y , we describe the asymptotic behaviour of the extremal process of this model as n → ∞ n\to\infty .

1.
Introduction

Branching random walk (or BRW for short) is a particle system on the real line constructed as follows. It starts from a single particle at position
0
forming the initial generation
0
of the process. Each particle reproduces independently of all others by creating an identically distributed point process of children around its position. We denote by
\mathcal{U}
the set of particles in the branching random walk. For all
u\in\mathcal{U}
, we write
S_{u}
for the position of particle
u
,
|u|
for the generation to which it belongs and
u_{k}
for the ancestor of
u
alive at generation
k\leq|u|
. We only consider in the present article supercritical branching random walks, satisfying the assumption

(1.1)

\E\left[\#\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=1\}\right]>1.

Under this condition, the BRW survives forever with positive probability,
or in other words

\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\#\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=n\}>0\quad\forall\,n\geq 0)>0.

Note that we do not make any assumption on the finiteness of
\#\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=1\}
, the random variable corresponding to the number of children of a given individual.

For all
\theta>0
, we denote by
\kappa(\theta)=\log\E\left[\sum_{|u|=1}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}}\right]\in(-\infty,\infty]
the log-Laplace transform of (the intensity measure of the reproduction law of) the BRW. Assuming that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
, we write

\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)=\E\left[\sum_{|u|=1}S_{u}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-\kappa(\theta)}\right]

whenever this integral is well-defined, irrespectively of the well-definition of
\kappa
in a neighbourhood of
\theta
. If
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
is well-defined, we also write

\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=\E\left[\sum_{|u|=1}(S_{u}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-\kappa(\theta)}\right]\in[0,\infty].

By Hölder’s inequality, one immediately remarks that
\kappa
is convex, therefore
\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}:\kappa(\theta)<\infty\}
forms an interval. Moreover, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, it is straightforward to verify that
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
and
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)
indeed correspond to the first and second derivative of
\kappa
for
\theta
in the interior of that interval. We also remark that
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=0
implies that almost surely, all particles in the first generation have the same position. We bar this degenerate situation from consideration by always assuming throughout this article that

(1.2)

\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\quad\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\exists u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=1,S_{u}\neq x)>0.

Branching random walks have been the subject of a large and still expanding literature. One of the most studied features of this model is the asymptotic behaviour of the position of particles at the right tip of the BRW.
Biggins (1976)
proved that the
maximal displacement

M_{n}:=\max_{|u|=n}S_{u}
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{n}}{n}=v:=\inf_{\theta>0}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}\quad\text{a.s. on the survival event of the BRW,}

as long as there exists
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
.

Addario-Berry and Reed (2009)
(see also
Bramson and Zeitouni, 2009
) showed that if there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that

(1.3)

\theta_{0}\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})-\kappa(\theta_{0})=0,

and additional integrability conditions hold, then setting
m_{n}:=nv-\frac{3}{2\theta_{0}}\log n
, the sequence
(M_{n}-m_{n})
is tight, although
Hu and Shi (2009)
proved that this sequence exhibits almost sure fluctuations on a logarithmic scale.
Aïdékon (2013)
proved the convergence in distribution of the centred maximal displacement under close to optimal integrability conditions for a BRW satisfying (
1.3
).

In order to study the joint convergence in distribution of the first few rightmost particles, one can consider the so-called
extremal process
of the BRW, defined as

\mathcal{Z}_{n}:=\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{X}_{n},

where
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{S_{u}}
is the empirical measure of the BRW and
\tau_{x}
is the operator corresponding to a shift by
x
of the point measure.
Madaule (2017)
proved that under mild conditions, the extremal process
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of the BRW converges in law for the topology of vague convergence of point measure to a limiting decorated Cox process
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
. We refer to
Shi (2015)
for an overview of branching random walks, and to
Maillard and Mallein (2021)
and
Subag and Zeitouni (2015)
for background on decorated Cox processes and their connections with branching particle systems.

In this article, we are interested in the
last progeny modified BRW
, introduced by
Bandyopadhyay and Ghosh (2025)
, which can be constructed as follows: Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
and
(Y_{u},u\in\mathcal{U})
be a collection of i.i.d. random variables of law
\nu
, which are independent of the BRW. This model is the family of point measures defined for
n\geq 0
by

\mathcal{E}_{n}=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{S_{u}+Y_{u}}.

In other words, in the process
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, the positions of particles at the last step
n
in the BRW are modified by the i.i.d. random variables
(Y_{u},|u|=n)
. This can be seen as a noisy observation of the positions of particles in the branching random walk.

Bandyopadhyay and Ghosh (2025)
considered a last progeny modified BRW with perturbation law
\nu
given by the Gumbel law of parameter
1/\theta
. Assuming that
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)\leq 0
, they showed that

(1.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{-m_{n}(\theta)}\mathcal{E}_{n}=\mathcal{Y}_{\theta}\quad\text{ in law for the topology of vague convergence},

with
\mathcal{Y}_{\theta}
a random point measure. On the one hand, if
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)<0
, then
m_{n}(\theta)=n\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}
and the limiting point measure
\mathcal{Y}_{\theta}
is a Cox process (i.e. a Poisson point process with a random intensity measure) with intensity
\theta W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x
. On the other hand, if
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)=0
, (
1.4
) holds with
m_{n}(\theta)=n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\theta}
a Cox process with intensity
\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}\theta Z_{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x
.
Here,
W_{\infty}(\theta)
and
Z_{\infty}
are the almost sure limits of the
additive martingale

(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
and the
derivative martingale

(Z_{n},n\geq 0)
, as defined in (
1.6
) and (
1.9
), respectively.
These results were extended by
Kowalski (2024)
, who studied the asymptotic behaviour of the position of the right-most atom in the last progeny modified BRW for
\nu
belonging to a larger class of probability distributions.

The main objective of the present article is to study the asymptotic behaviour of the full extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}
as
n\to\infty
, assuming that the tail of the law
\nu
is exponential, more precisely that there exists
\theta>0
and a regularly varying function
L
such that

(1.5)

\nu([x,\infty))\sim L(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\quad\text{ as $x\to\infty$.}

Let us recall that a function
L
is called regularly varying at
\infty
with parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
if for all
\lambda>0
, we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{L(\lambda x)}{L(x)}=\lambda^{\alpha}.

A function is called
regularly varying at
\infty
if there exists such a parameter
\alpha
. We refer to the book by
Bingham et al. (1987)
for background on regularly varying functions.

We endow the set of point measures on
\mathbb{R}
with the topology of vague convergence. For
\mu
a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}
and
Q
a non-negative random variable, we denote by PPP(
Q\mu
) a Cox process with intensity
Q\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, i.e., conditionally on
Q
, a Poisson point process with intensity
Q\mu
.

We show in this article that the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is mainly predicted by the parameter
\theta
in (
1.5
). More precisely, it satisfies a different asymptotic depending on whether
\theta<\theta_{0}
,
\theta=\theta_{0}
or
\theta>\theta_{0}
with
\theta_{0}
the critical parameter of the BRW defined in (
1.3
). We refer to these three asymptotic regimes as subcritical, critical and supercritical, respectively, and we present our main results in each of these three regimes in the next three sections. We end the introduction with an heuristic explanation for our results.

Remark 1.1
.

Aiming for generality of our results, we do not wish to work under the assumption that the parameter
\theta_{0}
exists except if necessary. However, by strict convexity of
\kappa
, we remark that
\theta\mapsto\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)
is increasing whenever it is well-defined. Therefore, we will determine if the last progeny modified BRW is in the subcritical, critical or supercritical regime by observing the sign of
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)
.

1.1.
Extremal process in the subcritical regime

Let
\nu
be a probability distribution satisfying (
1.5
) and
S
a BRW such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
. In the subcritical regime, we claim that the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
can be described using the so-called
additive martingale
of the BRW, defined as follows:

(1.6)

\displaystyle W_{n}(\theta):=\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}.

It is immediate from its definition that
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
is a non-negative martingale, therefore converges almost surely to a limit
W_{\infty}(\theta)
. Assuming that
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
is well-defined,
Biggins (1977)
obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for its uniform integrability (with an alternative proof by
Lyons, 1997
based on a spine decomposition argument). More precisely, if
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
is well-defined,
then the martingale
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
is uniformly integrable if and only if

(1.7)

\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)<0\text{ and }\E\left[W_{1}(\theta)\log_{+}W_{1}(\theta)\right]<\infty,

where
\log_{+}(x)=\log\max(x,1)
. If (
1.7
) does not hold, then
W_{\infty}(\theta)=0
a.s. This result was extended by
Alsmeyer and Iksanov (2009)
, who obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for the uniform integrability of
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
regardless of the well-definition of
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
.

For the last progeny modified BRW, we show that the asymptotic behaviour of its extremal process is in the subcritical regime whenever
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
is uniformly integrable, i.e., as soon as the conditions of Alsmeyer and Iksanov are satisfied. However, the more restrictive condition (
1.7
) allows us to work with a larger class of perturbation distribution
\nu
. We therefore state two versions of our results in the subcritical regime, the first one holding under minimal conditions on the BRW but with stronger assumptions on
\nu
, the second under minimal conditions for the law
\nu
but with the more restrictive conditions on the BRW.

We first consider a BRW satisfying the assumptions of
(
Alsmeyer and Iksanov, 2009
, Theorem 1.3)
and a perturbation distribution
\nu
having an exact exponential tail.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
. We assume that
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
is uniformly integrable. Let
\nu
be a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
such that there exists a constant
L\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

(1.8)

\nu([x,\infty))\sim L\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$.}

Then, writing

m_{n}=n\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta}\log L,

the extremal process
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges in law to a PPP
(\theta W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

Next, we consider a BRW satisfying (
1.7
) and a perturbation distribution
\nu
satisfying (
1.5
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
. We assume that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta+\delta)+\kappa(\theta-\delta)<\infty
and that (
1.7
) holds. Let
\nu
be a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
1.5
) with a regularly varying function
L
of index
\alpha
. Then, writing

m_{n}=n\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta}\log L(n)\quad\text{and}\quad c_{1}=\left(\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{\alpha},

the extremal process
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges in law to a PPP
(c_{1}\theta W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

Noting that a constant is a regularly varying function of index
0
, we see that these two statements are consistent. Theorem
1.2
corresponds to the case
\alpha=0
and
c_{1}=1
; however, for its conclusion to hold, it is not necessary to assume that
\kappa
is finite at any point other than
\theta
, or that
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>-\infty
, unlike in Theorem
1.3
.
The conditions we worked under in these results were chosen to obtain an explicit centring sequence
(m_{n})
. However, we believe that it is a generic result in the subcritical regime that there will exist a centring sequence such that
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges in law to a Poisson point process with intensity
W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\mathrm{d}x
.

1.2.
Extremal process in the critical regime

Let
S
be a BRW, we assume that there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that (
1.3
) holds, and that
\nu
satisfies (
1.5
) with
\theta=\theta_{0}
. We refer to this situation as the critical regime. We observe that the martingale
(W_{n}(\theta_{0}),n\geq 0)
converges to 0 almost surely, by the previously mentioned result of Biggins. However, the so-called
derivative martingale
defined for
n\geq 0
by

(1.9)

\displaystyle Z_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}(n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})-S_{u})\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{0}S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta_{0})}

works as a good replacement for this martingale. Immediate computations show that
(Z_{n},n\geq 0)
is a signed martingale, which is generally not uniformly integrable. Assuming that
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)<\infty
,
Aïdékon (2013)
obtained necessary condition that
Chen (2015)
proved to be sufficient for the convergence of
Z_{n}
to an a.s. non-negative non-degenerate limit
Z_{\infty}
. This necessary and sufficient condition is

(1.10)

\E\left[W_{1}(\theta_{0})(\log_{+}W_{1}(\theta_{0}))^{2}\right]+\E\left[\overline{W}_{1}\log_{+}(\overline{W}_{1})\right]<\infty,

where
\overline{W}_{1}=\sum_{|u|=1}(\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})-S_{u})_{+}\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{0}S_{u}-\kappa(\theta_{0})}
and
x_{+}=\max(x,0)
.

Under these conditions, as well as a slightly more stringent condition on the tail of
\nu
, we obtain a similar result to the subcritical regime, with
Z_{\infty}
playing the role of
W_{\infty}(\theta)
, and a modified centring term for the extremal process.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
and
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)<\infty
. We assume that

\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)=0,

i.e.,
\theta=\theta_{0}
and that (
1.10
) holds. Let
\nu
be a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}
such that there exists a regularly varying function
L
at
\infty
with index
\alpha\in(-2,0)
satisfying (
1.5
). Then, writing

\displaystyle m_{n}=n\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta}\log L\left(\sqrt{n}\right)-\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n\quad\text{and}\quad c_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}\left(2\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1\right),

the extremal process
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges in law to a PPP
(c_{2}\theta Z_{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.4
also holds assuming that
\nu
satisfies (
1.5
) with
L
a positive constant, using the same proof techniques as the ones we develop below. This result therefore extends the previous estimate of
Bandyopadhyay and Ghosh (2025)
for
\nu
a Gumbel distribution with parameter
\frac{1}{\theta_{0}}
.

Remark 1.6
.

We believe that up to adding a stronger integrability condition on the reproduction law of the BRW, one could prove a result similar to Theorem
1.4
assuming that
\nu
satisfies (
1.5
) with
L
a regularly varying function with parameter
\alpha\geq 0
. However, we predict a sharp phase correction around
\alpha=-2
, and that the limiting extremal process should resemble the one obtained in the supercritical regime if
\alpha<-2
.

1.3.
Extremal process in the supercritical regime

We finally turn to the case of perturbation law
\nu
satisfying (
1.5
) with
\theta>\theta_{0}
. In this situation, the perturbation has such strong tails that its asymptotic behaviour can be deduced from the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, the extremal process of the BRW. As a result, we consider here a BRW satisfying the assumptions used by
Madaule (2017)
to prove the convergence of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
. These conditions include notably (
1.10
). In this situation, it will be sufficient for the tail of
\nu
to be light enough for the convergence of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
to hold.

Theorem 1.7
.

We assume that the reproduction law of the BRW is non-lattice, and there exists
\theta_{0}>0
satisfying (
1.3
). We assume that
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0})<\infty
and that (
1.10
) holds. Let
\nu
be a probability measure such that there exist
C>0
and
\theta>\theta_{0}
verifying

(1.11)

\nu([x,\infty))\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\text{ for $x\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Then, writing

m_{n}=n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})-\frac{3}{2\theta_{0}}\log n,

the extremal process
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
converges in law to

(1.12)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\delta_{z_{i}+Y_{i}},

where
(z_{i},i\in\mathbb{N})
are the atoms of the limiting extremal process
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
of the BRW and
(Y_{i})
is an independent sequence of i.i.d. variables with law
\nu
.

1.4.
Heuristics for our results

The asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
can be explained by observing the distribution of particles at the
n
th generation of the BRW. We assume here that
\kappa(\theta)
is finite for all
\theta\geq 0
, and that there exists
\theta_{0}
satisfying (
1.3
). In this situation, for all
\theta<\theta_{0}
it is well-known that the contributions to the value of the martingale
W_{n}(\theta)
are mostly supported by particles in a neighbourhood of
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
. Otherwise stated, there are at time
n
approximately
W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-n(\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta))}
particles to the neighbourhood of
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
. Moreover, with high probability, there is no particle to the right of
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})
.

Let us now estimate the maximal position reached by
S_{u}+Y_{u}
by a particle
u
such that
S_{u}
is close to
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
. Under the assumption that
\nu
has an exponential tail with parameter
\vartheta
, we know that the maximum of
N
independent copies of
Y
will be close to
\frac{\log N}{\vartheta}
. Therefore, we expect particles in a neighbourhood of
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
to create a cloud of perturbed observations with the furthest point being found around

n\left[\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)+\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\kappa(\theta)-\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\vartheta}\log W_{\infty}(\theta).

Differentiating the linearly growing term with respect to
\theta
, we obtain
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)(1-\theta/\vartheta)
, therefore this quantity will be maximal, for large
n
, when
\theta=\min(\vartheta,\theta_{0})
.

When
\vartheta<\theta_{0}
, the extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is thus obtained by generating a large number of i.i.d. copies of
Y
. As a result, this extremal process, centred by
n\kappa(\vartheta)/\vartheta
will converge to a Poisson point process with intensity
\vartheta\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta x}\,\mathrm{d}x
, shifted by
\frac{1}{\vartheta}\log W_{\infty}(\vartheta)
to take into account the exact number of random variables. This corresponds to the situation described in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

When
\vartheta\geq\theta_{0}
, then the maximum of the last progeny modified BRW is generated by one of the leading particles, in a neighbourhood of
n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta_{0})
. This explains the shape of the limiting extremal process in the supercritical regime, which is obtained by perturbing the limiting extremal process of the BRW. In Theorem
1.7
, the tail of the perturbations is so thin that only particles very close to the edge create a large perturbation, which yields the natural form of the limiting extremal process, obtained by perturbing the limiting extremal process of the original BRW with i.i.d. random variables. In Theorem
1.4
, the main contribution near the tip of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
comes from particles at distance
\Theta(\sqrt{n})
from the leading edge of the BRW, which are counted using the derivative martingale of the BRW. This explains the extremal process, given by a Poisson point process with exponential intensity, shifted by
\frac{1}{\theta_{0}}\log Z_{\infty}
instead of
\frac{1}{\theta_{0}}\log W_{\infty}(\theta_{0})
, and the apparition of
L(\sqrt{n})
in the centring term
m_{n}
, as the typical perturbations observed at generation
n
make jumps of that size
z\in\Theta(\sqrt{n})
, with probability approximately
L(z)\mathrm{e}^{-z}
.

Our proof techniques consist in validating this heuristic computation, by identifying the set of particles in the BRW that contribute to the generation of the tip of the point measure
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, then carefully proving the convergence towards the identified extremal process.

1.5.
Organization of the article.

We introduce in the next section some estimates that allow us to study the Laplace transform of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
. We then use the convergence of this Laplace transform to prove our main theorems in Section
3
.

2.
Laplace transform of the last progeny modified branching random walk

To prove the convergence in distribution of
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
to a limiting point measure
\mathcal{Z}
in law for the topology of vague convergence, it is sufficient to show that for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}={\langle\mathcal{Z},\varphi\rangle}\quad\text{ in law,}

where we write
{\langle\mathcal{X},\varphi\rangle}=\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{X}
. As a result, it is sufficient to prove the convergence of the Laplace transform of
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
, defined as

\varphi\in\mathcal{T}\mapsto\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right],

where
\mathcal{T}
is the set of all non-negative continuous compactly supported functions, i.e., to show that for each
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, we have

(2.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\mathcal{Z},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right].

In the rest of the section, we introduce some methods and results allowing to study this Laplace transform.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using the independence between
(Y_{u},u\in\mathcal{U})
and the BRW, we observe that

(2.2)

\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{x}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(x+S_{u})\right)\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{x}\mathcal{X}_{n},g_{\varphi}\rangle}\right)\right]

where we have set

g_{\varphi}:x\mapsto-\log\int\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(x+y)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}y),

and we recall that
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is the counting measure of the BRW at time
n
.

Applying Madaule’s theorem (
Madaule, 2017
) to (
2.2
) yields Theorem
1.7
(see Section
3.3
).
To prove Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
, we first show that
M_{n}-m_{n}
converges to
-\infty
in probability, where we recall that
M_{n}:=\max_{|u|=n}S_{u}
(with

Lemma 2.3

Proof of the theorems

We prove in this section our main theorems. We first consider the asymptotic behaviour of

3.1.
BRW below the boundary case

In this section,
\theta
is a fixed positive constant, and we assume that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
and
(W_{n}(\theta),n\geq 0)
is uniformly integrable. We denote by
W_{\infty}(\theta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(\theta)
the almost sure limit of this martingale. We start by proving Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, using Lemma
2.2
, under assumption (
1.8
), for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
A>0
such that for all
x\leq-A
, we have

(3.1)

\left|g_{\varphi}(x)-c_{\varphi}(\theta)L\mathrm{e}^{\theta x}\right|\leq\varepsilon\mathrm{e}^{\theta x}.

Observe that for all
a,b\geq 0
, we have
|\mathrm{e}^{-a}-\mathrm{e}^{-b}|\leq|a-b|\wedge 1
using that
\exp
is
1
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\E\left[\left|\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{X}_{n},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}-\mathrm{e}^{-c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{n}(\theta)}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\E\left[\left|{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{X}_{n},g_{\varphi}\rangle}-c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{n}(\theta)\right|\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\E\left[\left|\sum_{|u|=n}h_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\right|\wedge 1\right],

where
h_{\varphi}(x)=g_{\varphi}(x)-c_{\varphi}(\theta)L\mathrm{e}^{\theta x}
, using that
\mathrm{e}^{-\theta m_{n}}=L^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-n\kappa(\theta)}
. By (
3.1
), we have

\E\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{M_{n}\leq m_{n}-A\right\}}\left|\sum_{|u|=n}h_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\right|\wedge 1\right]\leq\varepsilon L^{-1}\E[W_{n}(\theta)],

hence

\E\left[\left|\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{X}_{n},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}-\mathrm{e}^{-c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{n}(\theta)}\right|\right]\leq\varepsilon L^{-1}+\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(M_{n}\geq m_{n}-A).

Letting
n\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we conclude, by first using Lemma
2.1
and then using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and (
2.2
), that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-c_{\varphi}(\theta)\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\exp\left(-W_{\infty}(\theta)\int\theta\mathrm{e}^{-\theta z}(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(z)})\,\mathrm{d}z\right)\right].

To complete the proof, it is then enough to observe that this limit is the Laplace transform of the PPP(
\theta W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta z}\,\mathrm{d}z
).
∎

Theorem
1.3
follows from very similar computations. We use the extra integrability condition
\kappa(\theta+\delta)<\infty
to guarantee that under our stated assumptions,
M_{n}-m_{n}
almost surely decays linearly, and the condition
\kappa(\theta-\delta)<\infty
to control the contributions of particles far from position
m_{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We compute the asymptotic behaviour of the Laplace transform of
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
using similar computations as for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We recall that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, we have

\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\right)\right],

and that
c_{1}=\left(\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)\right)^{\alpha}
. Let
\eta>0
such that
4\eta<\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
. Using Lemma
2.2
and the regular variations of
L
at
\infty
, we deduce that almost surely, for all
n
large enough,

\displaystyle(1-\tfrac{2}{c_{1}^{1/\alpha}}\eta)^{|\alpha|}c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq n\eta\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq n\eta\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq(1-\tfrac{2}{c_{1}^{1/\alpha}}\eta)^{-|\alpha|}c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq n\eta\right\}}.

Here we use the Uniform Convergence Theorem (
Bingham et al., 1987
, Theorem 1.5.2
), which states that
\lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{L(\lambda z)}{L(z)}=\lambda^{\alpha}
holds uniformly on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
. Note that there exists
r>0
so that

[(1-\tfrac{3}{c_{1}^{1/\alpha}}\eta)^{|\alpha|},(1-\tfrac{3}{c_{1}^{1/\alpha}}\eta)^{-|\alpha|}]\subset[1-r\eta,1+r\eta].

Using Lemma
2.4
, together with the law of large numbers for the random walk
(T_{n},n\geq 0)
, we observe that

(3.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|>n\eta\right\}}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\left|T_{n}-\E[T_{n}]\right|>n\eta\right)=0.

Consequently, using the a.s. convergence of
W_{n}(\theta)
to
W_{\infty}(\theta)
, we deduce that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq n\eta\right\}}=W_{\infty}(\theta)\quad\text{ in probability.}

Hence, we conclude that

\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{|u|=n}\!g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq n\eta\right\}}\!-c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{\infty}(\theta)\right|>r\eta c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{\infty}(\theta)\right)

converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. In order to show that
\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})
converges to
c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{\infty}(\theta)
in probability and complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we now show that

(3.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)|>n\eta\right\}}=0\quad\text{ in probability.}

On the one hand, using that
\vartheta\mapsto\kappa(\vartheta)
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
on
(\theta-\delta,\theta+\delta)
, and that
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)<0
, we observe that
\kappa(\vartheta)/\vartheta
is decreasing on
[\theta,\theta+\delta]
for sufficiently small
\delta
, which implies
\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}>\frac{\kappa(\theta+\delta)}{\theta+\delta}
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}-n\left(\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}-\varepsilon\right)=-\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

As
\log L(n)/n\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we conclude that almost surely, for all
n
large enough,
M_{n}-m_{n}\leq-\varepsilon n/2
. Therefore, almost surely, for
n
large enough

\displaystyle\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta,S_{u}-m_{n}<-n\varepsilon/2\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2c_{\varphi}(\theta)\sum_{|u|=n}L(m_{n}-S_{u})\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}}{L(n)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta,S_{u}-m_{n}<-n\varepsilon/2\right\}}.

Therefore, using again the Uniform Convergence Theorem (
Bingham et al., 1987
, Theorem 1.5.2
), there exists a constant
C
depending on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
such that almost surely, for all
n
large enough

\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta\right\}}\leq C\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta\right\}}.

This, together with (
3.2
), implies that

(3.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>n\eta\right\}}=0\quad\text{ in probability.}

With similar computations, we observe that for all
B>0
, we have

(3.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<-n\eta\right\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}>-nB\right\}}=0\quad\text{ in probability.}

On the other hand, using that
\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{-1}\log L(x)=0
, we observe that for all
x
large enough, we have

g_{\varphi}(-x)\leq 2c_{\varphi}(\theta)L(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\leq 4c_{\varphi}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-(\theta-\delta/2)x}.

Therefore, for all
n
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}<-nB\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq 4c_{\varphi}(\theta)\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{(\theta-\delta/2)(S_{u}-m_{n})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}<-nB\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq 4c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{n}(\theta-\delta)\mathrm{e}^{n\kappa(\theta-\delta)-(\theta-\delta)m_{n}-\delta nB/2}.

Using that
W_{n}(\theta-\delta)
converges almost surely, we observe that for
B
large enough, we have

(3.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}<-nB\right\}}=0\quad\text{ in probability.}

Consequently, combining (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we get (
3.3
), which implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})=c_{1}c_{\varphi}(\theta)W_{\infty}(\theta)\quad\text{ in probability.}

Using the dominated convergence theorem together with (
2.2
), we conclude that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-c_{1}W_{\infty}(\theta)c_{\varphi}(\theta)\right)\right].

We can now complete the proof, observing that the right-hand side is the Laplace transform of the PPP(
c_{1}\theta W_{\infty}(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x
).
∎

3.2.
BRW in the boundary case

We turn in this section to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The proof follows a similar scheme as the one used above, with the added introduction of a shaving procedure. More precisely, for all
A>0
, we set

\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)=\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=n,\;S_{u_{k}}\leq k\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)+A\quad\forall\,k\leq n\},

the set of particles that stayed at all times below the line
x\mapsto x\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)+A
. Using that
\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)=-\infty
a.s. we observe that almost surely, for
A
large enough, we have
\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)=\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=n\}
. In other words, writing

\mathcal{S}_{A}:=\{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<A\},

we observe that on the event
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, we have
\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)=\{u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=n\}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and that
\lim_{A\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\mathcal{S}_{A})=1
.

As a first step towards the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we show that no particle in
\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)
above position
m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}
contributes to the extremal process with high probability, as soon as
\varepsilon>0
is small enough.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the conditions and notation of Theorem
1.4
, for all
B>0
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\exists u\in\mathcal{U}:|u|=n,S_{u}\geq m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2},S_{u}+Y_{u}\geq m_{n}-B)=0.

Proof.

We first recall from
(
Mallein, 2016
, Theorem 1.1)
, that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(M_{n}\geq n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{3}{2\theta}\log n+\varepsilon^{-1})=0.

Letting
a_{n}=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\log n+\log L\left(\sqrt{n}\right)\right)
, we can rewrite this as

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(M_{n}\geq m_{n}-a_{n}+\varepsilon^{-1})=0.

Moreover, we have
\lim_{A\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\mathcal{S}_{A})=1
. Therefore it is enough to prove that for all
A>0
large enough,

(3.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\exists u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A):\begin{array}[]{l}m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],\\
S_{u}+Y_{u}\geq m_{n}-B\end{array}\right)=0.

Using the Markov inequality, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\exists u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A):m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],S_{u}+Y_{u}\geq m_{n}-B\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\E\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}]\right\}}\nu([m_{n}-B-S_{u},\infty))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\E\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}]\right\}}L(m_{n}-S_{u})\mathrm{e}^{\theta(S_{u}+B-m_{n})}\right]

for all
n
large enough, where we used the independence between
Y
and
S
, and the fact that
a_{n}\to\infty
, therefore we can apply (
1.5
) to
m_{n}-S_{u}
.

We then use the formula of
m_{n}
and the many-to-one lemma to compute

\displaystyle\E\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}]\right\}}L(m_{n}-S_{u})\mathrm{e}^{\theta(S_{u}-m_{n})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=n^{1/2}\E\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A)}\frac{L(m_{n}-S_{u})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}]\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=n^{1/2}\E\left[\frac{L(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right],

where
\widehat{T}_{k}=T_{k}-k\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
and
\widehat{m}_{n}=m_{n}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
. Let
\rho\in(0,\alpha+2)
, we define
\mathfrak{L}:x\mapsto x^{\rho-\alpha}L(x)
. Observe that
\mathfrak{L}
is a regularly varying function at
\infty
with index
\rho>0
. Therefore, by the Uniform Convergence Theorem (
Bingham et al., 1987
, Theorem 1.5.2
), for all
\delta>0
, there exists
N_{\delta}
such that for all
x>N_{\delta}
,

\left|\frac{L(\lambda x)}{L(x)}-\lambda^{\alpha}\right|=\lambda^{\alpha-\rho}\left|\frac{\mathfrak{L}(\lambda x)}{\mathfrak{L}(x)}-\lambda^{\rho}\right|<\delta\lambda^{\alpha-\rho}\quad\text{ for all $\lambda\in(0,2\varepsilon]$.}

As a result, for all
n
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\frac{L(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\E\left[\left(\frac{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\right)^{\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\delta\E\left[\left(\frac{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\right)^{\alpha-\rho}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right].

We now compute this quantity using the ballot theorem to the centred random walk
(-\widehat{T}_{n})
with finite variance.

Using e.g.
(
Aïdékon and Shi, 2010
, Lemma 4.1)
, there exist
C>0
and
h>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\geq 0
and
b\geq-a
, we have

\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[b,b+h],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-a,j\leq n)\leq C\frac{\left((a+1)\wedge n^{1/2}\right)\left((a+b+1)\wedge n^{1/2}\right)}{n^{3/2}}.

Let
\gamma\in(-2,0)
. For all
n
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\left(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\right)^{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{k=1}^{{\left\lceil\varepsilon n^{1/2}/h\right\rceil}}(kh)^{\gamma}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[kh,(k+1)h],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{Ch^{\gamma}(A+1)}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{{\left\lceil\varepsilon n^{1/2}/h\right\rceil}}k^{\gamma}(A+kh+C^{\prime}\log n+1)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2Ch^{\gamma}(A+1)^{2}}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{{\left\lceil\varepsilon n^{1/2}/h\right\rceil}}k^{\gamma}(kh+C^{\prime}\log n),

where
C^{\prime}
is chosen so that
|\widehat{m}_{n}|\leq C^{\prime}\log n
for all
n
large enough.
Decomposing this sum for
k\leq\log n
and
k\geq\log n
, we obtain, for
n
large enough

\displaystyle\E\left[(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n})^{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2Ch^{\gamma}(A+1)^{2}(C^{\prime}+h)}{n^{3/2}}\left((\log n)^{\gamma+2}+\sum_{k={\left\lceil\log n\right\rceil}}^{{\left\lceil\varepsilon n^{1/2}/h\right\rceil}}k^{\gamma+1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{K_{\gamma}}{n^{3/2}}(\log n)^{\gamma+2}+\frac{K^{\prime}_{\gamma}}{n^{3/2}}\varepsilon^{\gamma+2}n^{\gamma/2+1},

for some
K_{\gamma},K^{\prime}_{\gamma}>0
since
\gamma+1>-1
. As a result, letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\exists u\in\mathcal{G}_{n}(A):\begin{array}[]{l}m_{n}-S_{u}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],\\
S_{u}+Y_{u}\geq m_{n}-B\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{\theta B}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{1/2}\E\left[\frac{L(\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\widehat{m}_{n}-\widehat{T}_{n}\in[a_{n}-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon n^{1/2}],-\widehat{T}_{j}\geq-A,j\leq n\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\mathrm{e}^{\theta B}K^{\prime}_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha+2}+2\mathrm{e}^{\theta B}\delta K^{\prime}_{\alpha-\rho}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\rho+2}.

Using that
\alpha>\alpha-\rho>-2
, we conclude that (
3.7
) holds, which completes the proof.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we write

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{S_{u}+Y_{u}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}\leq m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, together with the inequality
|\mathrm{e}^{-a}-\mathrm{e}^{-b}|\leq|a-b|\wedge 1
, we observe that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{(\varepsilon)},\varphi\rangle}}\right]\right|

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}\varphi(S_{u}+Y_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}\geq m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]=0.

It is therefore enough to study the asymptotic behaviour of
\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{(\varepsilon)},\varphi\rangle}}\right]
to identify the limiting distribution of
\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n}
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
. Using the same computations as in (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\exp\left(-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{n},\varphi\rangle}\right)\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}\leq m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}\right)\right].

We study the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}\leq m_{n}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}
as
n\to\infty
then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. By Lemma
2.2
, we get that for all
\delta>0
, for all large enough
n
,

\displaystyle(1-\delta)c_{\varphi}(\theta)n^{1/2}\sum_{|u|=n}\frac{L(m_{n}-S_{u})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq(1+\delta)c_{\varphi}(\theta)n^{1/2}\sum_{|u|=n}\frac{L(m_{n}-S_{u})}{L(n^{1/2})}\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}.

Recall that
L
is regularly varying with index
\alpha<0
. We use again the Uniform Convergence Theorem (
Bingham et al., 1987
, Theorem 1.5.2
), yielding

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda>\varepsilon/2}\left|\frac{L(\lambda x)}{L(x)}-\lambda^{\alpha}\right|=0.

As a result, for all
0<\delta<\varepsilon
, for all
n
large enough, we have

\displaystyle(1-\delta)c_{\varphi}(\theta)n^{1/2}\sum_{|u|=n}\underline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}((m_{n}-S_{u})/n^{1/2})\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq(1+\delta)c_{\varphi}(\theta)n^{1/2}\sum_{|u|=n}\overline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}((m_{n}-S_{u})/n^{1/2})\mathrm{e}^{\theta S_{u}-n\kappa(\theta)},

where
\underline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
\overline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
are continuous functions such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle(x^{\alpha}-\delta)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{[\varepsilon+\delta,\infty)\right\}}\leq\underline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)\leq(x^{\alpha}-\delta)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{[\varepsilon,\infty)\right\}}

and

\displaystyle(x^{\alpha}+\delta)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{[\varepsilon,\infty)\right\}}\leq\overline{h}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(x)\leq(x^{\alpha}+\delta)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{[\varepsilon-\delta,\infty)\right\}}.

As a result, using a combination of
(
Madaule, 2016
, Theorem 1.2)
and
(
Aïdékon and Shi, 2014
, Theorem 1.1)
, then letting
\delta\downarrow 0
we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}c_{\varphi}(\theta)Z_{\infty}\E\left[\left(R_{1}\sqrt{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}\right)^{\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{1}\sqrt{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}\geq\varepsilon\right\}}\right]\quad\text{ in probability,}

where
(R_{t},t\in[0,1])
is a Brownian meander. Since
R_{1}
has Rayleigh distribution, we observe that
\E\left[\left(R_{1}\right)^{\alpha}\right]<\infty
. Now, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we obtain by monotonicity that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{m_{n}-S_{u}\geq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\right\}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}c_{\varphi}(\theta)Z_{\infty}\E\left[\left(R_{1}\sqrt{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\quad\text{ in probability.}

Finally, we set

c_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}\E\left[\left(R_{1}\sqrt{\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}\right)^{\alpha}\right]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}}\left(2\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1\right).

We now conclude, by equation (
3.8
), that

\displaystyle\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-c_{2}c_{\varphi}(\theta)Z_{\infty}}\right]=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right].

Identifying the Laplace transform of the PPP(
c_{2}\theta Z_{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}\,\mathrm{d}x
), the proof of Theorem
1.4
is now complete.
∎

3.3.
BRW above the boundary case

We prove in this section Theorem
1.7
as a consequence of the convergence of the extremal process of the BRW observed by
Madaule (2017)
. Recall that by
(
Madaule, 2017
, Theorem 1.1)
, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]=\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},\varphi\rangle}}\right],

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}
is the limiting extremal process of the BRW, a decorated, randomly shifted Poisson point process with exponential intensity. Moreover,
(
Madaule, 2017
, Proposition 2.1)
shows that
\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta(S_{u}-m_{n})}
is tight for all
\theta>\theta_{0}
.
Using these two results and assuming (
1.11
), we show that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{n},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]=\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right],

which implies the convergence of the extremal process of the last progeny modified BRW.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
K>0
, we write
\chi_{K}
a function in
\mathcal{T}
such that

\mathbbm{1}_{[-K,K]}\leq\chi_{K}\leq\mathbbm{1}_{[-2K,2K]}.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, using (
2.2
), we can write for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]\right|\leq I_{1}(n,K)+I_{2}(n,K)+I_{3}(K),

where we have set

\displaystyle I_{1}(n,K)

\displaystyle=\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{|u|=n}\chi_{K}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\right]\right|

\displaystyle I_{2}(n,K)

\displaystyle=\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{|u|=n}\chi_{K}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},\chi_{K}g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle I_{3}(K)

\displaystyle=\left|\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},\chi_{K}g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]-\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]\right|.

Observe that
\chi_{K}g_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{T}
. Therefore, by
(
Madaule, 2017
, Theorem 1.1)
, we know that for all
K>0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{2}(n,K)=0
. Additionally,
\lim_{K\to\infty}I_{3}(K)=0
by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we now bound
I_{1}(n,K)
in
n
.

Let
\theta_{1}\in(\theta_{0},\theta)
, using the inequality
|\mathrm{e}^{-a}-\mathrm{e}^{-b}|\leq|a-b|\wedge 1
for all
a,b\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}(n,K)

\displaystyle\leq\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}(1-\chi_{K})g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\right)\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|S_{u}-m_{n}|\geq K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\leq-K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\geq K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right],

by the sub-additivity
(a+b)\wedge 1\leq(a\wedge 1)+(b\wedge 1)
for all
a,b\geq 0
. We first observe that

\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\geq K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]\leq\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}(M_{n}-m_{n}\geq K),

which converges to
0
uniformly in
n
as
K\to\infty
, by tightness of
(M_{n}-m_{n})
. Moreover, using Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\leq-K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\E\left[\left(C^{\prime}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta(S_{u}-m_{n})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\leq-K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C^{\prime}\E\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{1}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\leq K\right\}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta(S_{u}-m_{n})}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\leq-K\right\}}\right]+\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{1}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\geq K\right),

for an arbitrary
\theta_{1}\in(\theta_{0},\theta)
. As a result,

\displaystyle\E\left[\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g_{\varphi}(S_{u}-m_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{S_{u}-m_{n}\leq-K\right\}}\right)\wedge 1\right]\leq C^{\prime}K\mathrm{e}^{-K(\theta-\theta_{1})}+\operatorname{\mathbf{P}}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{1}(S_{u}-m_{n})}\geq K\right).

Using
(
Madaule, 2017
, Proposition 2.1)
, we observe that
\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\theta_{1}(S_{u}-m_{n})}
is tight, and therefore we conclude that

\lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{1}(n,K)=0.

Finally,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\tau_{-m_{n}}\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi\rangle}}\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\langle\mathcal{Z}_{\infty},g_{\varphi}\rangle}}\right]=\E\left[\exp\left(-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi(z_{i}+Y_{i})\right)\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

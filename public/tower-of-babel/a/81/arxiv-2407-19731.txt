Classification of toric surface singularities
Florin Ambro

Abstract
. We classify two-dimensional toric log germs in terms of their minimal log discrepancy. In particular, Borisov’s series of toric surface singularities with minimal log discrepancy bounded below can be described explicitely and geometrically.

1.
Introduction

Terminal, canonical, log terminal, log canonical singularities are classes of singularities that inevitably appear while running the Log Minimal Model Program. Such singularities are very special in the larger class of normal singularities, so a first hope would be to classify them
explicitely
. In low dimension, this is possible.

Consider the surface case (see
[
4
]
for example). Terminal just means smooth. Canonical singularities are the Du Val singularities, which up to analytic isomorphism have explicit equations of type A-D-E.
Log terminal singularities are analytically isomorphic to quotients
0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}/G
, where
G\subset\operatorname{GL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
is a finite group acting without reflections. Log canonical surface singularities are classified either by taking the index one cover and descending information from the Gorenstein case, or via the dual graph of exceptional curves on the minimal resolution.

In dimension three (see
[
7
]
for example) less is known, besides
a partial classification of canonical singularities and the classification of terminal
3
-fold singularities (described as cyclic covers of certain hypersurface singularities). As a consequence of classification, M. Reid
[
7
]
observed that for a terminal
3
-fold singularity
P\in X
, the general anti-canonical member
S\in|-K_{X}|
has at most Du Val singularity at
P
. This observation had two crucial consequences.
First, an analogue for the general anti-bicanonical member of a flipping contraction was the final step in establishing the Mori Program in dimension three
[
5
]
. Second, V.V. Shokurov
[
8
]
introduced the theory of complements and used them to establish the existence of
3
-fold log flips.

Since
[
8
,
9
]
, it is believed that in higher dimension a
qualitative
classification of
singularities may be possible, according to certain properties of the anti-pluricanonical members and the minimal log discrepancy (see
[
1
]
for an introduction to minimal log discrepancies and Shokurov’s conjectures).

Toric singularities are combinatorially defined, so it is natural to know more on their classification. Toric terminal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities are in bijection with lattice simplices, with one vertex at the origin, containing
no other lattice points besides vertices.
In dimension three, these are the cyclic quotient singularities of type
\frac{1}{r}(1,-1,a)
, where
r,a
are relatively prime positive integers. In dimension four, a complete classification was only recently completed
[
10
]
, after initial computer-generated explicit partial lists of series proposed in
[
6
]
. Renouncing the explicit nature of the partial lists, A. Borisov
[
2
]
obtained a qualitative classification of toric singularities with minimal log discrepancy bounded below:
given
d,\epsilon>0
, there exists only finitely many
series
of toric

{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities with
\dim X=d
and
\operatorname{mld}(X)\geq\epsilon
. The series are defined by finitely many closed subgroups of the real torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
Our motivation is to understand Borisov’s series, and to see if their combinatorial definition has a geometric equivalent, possibly with an analogue in the non-toric case.
In this note, we completely describe Borisov’s series in dimension two. An interesting new feature is that they are defined by invariant hyperplane sections.

Our setup differs slightly from Borisov’s. For a germ
P\in X
, Borisov considers the
global
minimal log discrepancy condition

\operatorname{mld}(X)\geq\epsilon
, while we consider the
local
minimal log discrepancy condition
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X)\geq t
. That is, Borisov considers all geometric valuations over
X
whose center contains
P
, while we only consider those with center exactly
P
. Borisov’s global series are just glueings of finitely many local series.

Fix a real number
0<t\leq 2
. Suppose for simplicity the boundary is zero. Every toric surface singularity
P\in X
is cyclic quotient, say of type
x=\frac{1}{r}(w_{1},w_{2})
. The complement
X\setminus T
of the torus sitting inside
X
is an invariant boundary
\Sigma=E_{1}+E_{2}
satisfying
K+\Sigma=0
and
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,\Sigma)=0
. The condition
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X)\geq t
translates as follows: for every
y\in(0,1]^{2}\cap({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}x)
, the inequality
y_{1}+y_{2}\geq t
holds. A C-program can list all
x
with this property, just like in
[
6
]
. There are infinitely many solutions. We take each solution, and investigate why it sits in the list. Our first observation is that besides finitely many exceptions,
x
sits in the list for a very simple reason:
m_{1}x_{1}+m_{2}x_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for some integers
m_{1},m_{2}\in[0,\frac{1}{t}]
, not both zero. We say in this case that
P\in X^{2}
belongs to a
codimension one
t
-lc series
.
Note that there are only finitely many codimension one
t
-lc series. Geometrically, they can be characterized in two equivalent (dual) ways:

(H)

The principal divisor
H=m_{1}E_{1}+m_{2}E_{2}
is an invariant hyperplane section of
P\in X
satisfying
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,tH)\geq 0
.

(C)

Let
n=\max(m_{1},m_{2})
and
B_{n}=\Sigma-\frac{H}{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(1-\frac{m_{i}}{n})E_{i}\geq 0
. Then

n(K+B_{n})\sim 0
and
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B_{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}\geq t
. Here
B_{n}
is an invariant
n
-complement of
K
.

Our second observation is that even the finitely many exceptions can be described explicitely, using two ”bounded, linearly independent” invariant hyperplanes (see Theorem
4.3
for the precise statement). Thus toric surface singularities
P\in X
with
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X)\geq t
belong either to (finitely many) codimension one
t
-lc series, or to finitely many isomorphism types.

Our third observation is that the above classification extends to the case when
X
is endowed with an invariant boundary
0\leq B\leq\Sigma
(see Theorem
4.1
and Section 5.2)). In the log case, the codimension one
t
-lc series are (dually) characterized as follows:

(H)

The principal divisor
H=m_{1}E_{1}+m_{2}E_{2}
is an invariant hyperplane section of
P\in X
satisfying
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B+tH)\geq 0
.

(C)

Let
B=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(1-a_{i})E_{i}
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
be minimal such that

na_{i}\geq m_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Denote
B_{n}=\Sigma-\frac{H}{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(1-\frac{m_{i}}{n})E_{i}\geq B
.
Then
n(K+B_{n})\sim 0
,
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B_{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}
, and either a)
n\leq t^{-1}
, or
b)
t^{-1}<n\leq\lceil t^{-1}\rceil
and
B_{n}\geq(1-\frac{1}{nt})\Sigma+\frac{1}{nt}B
.

The reader may check that the two equivalent conditions imply
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)\geq t
.

Our main result (Theorem
4.1
) is in fact a characterization
of the condition
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)\geq t
in terms of invariant hyperplanes.
But once the type of coefficients of
B
are fixed, Theorem
4.1

easily gives explicit forms for the series.

We outline the structure of this note. Section 2 is a preliminary on subgroups avoiding open cones inside a vector space. Section 3 is our key technical tool, a classification of subgroups of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
which do not intersect a deformation of the standard simplex (compare
[
3
]
).
In Section 4 we apply the classification in Section 3 to translate into
hyperplane sections the condition on a singularity to be
t
-log canonical. This provides a proof for the lists generated by a C-program. In Section 5, we interpret the classification in terms of hyperplane sections and anti-pluricanonical members.

2.
Preliminary

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space.
Let
V^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(V,{\mathbb{R}})
be the dual vector space. The duality pairing

V^{*}\times V\to{\mathbb{R}},\ (\varphi,v)\mapsto\langle\varphi,v\rangle=\varphi(v)

induces a canonical identification

V{\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}}(V^{*})^{*}
. For a convex cone
\sigma\subset V
, the dual cone
is

\sigma^{\vee}=\cap_{v\in\sigma}\{\varphi\in V^{*}|\langle\varphi,v\rangle\geq 0\}.

The duality theorem for convex cones states that
the closure of
\sigma
in
V
equals
(\sigma^{\vee})^{\vee}
.
Let
G\leq(V,+)
be a subgroup. The dual subgroup inside
V^{*}
is defined by

G^{*}=\{\varphi\in V^{*}|\langle\varphi,G\rangle\subset{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

The duality theorem for subgroups states that the closure of
G
in
V
equals
(G^{*})^{*}
.

If we choose a basis for
V
, and the dual basis for
V^{*}
, we may identify
V={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
V^{*}=\check{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
, with duality pairing

\langle x,\check{x}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\check{x}_{i}
.

For
v\in V
, denote
v^{*}=\{\varphi\in V^{*}|\langle\varphi,v\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, that is the subgroup dual to the subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}v\subset V
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space, let
G\leq V
be a closed subgroup, let
\sigma\subset V
be a closed convex cone such that
\dim\sigma=\dim V
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma=\emptyset
.

(2)

There exists
0\neq\varphi\in\sigma^{\vee}
such that

{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\subset G^{*}
.

Proof.

We may restate (2) as
\varphi\in G^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
. Therefore (2) is equivalent to
G^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\neq 0
.
We have
(G^{\perp})^{\perp}=G\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}=:G_{\mathbb{R}}
. By the separation theorem for convex sets,
G^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}=0
if and only if

G_{\mathbb{R}}+\sigma=V
. Since
\sigma
has an interior point, the latter is equivalent to
G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\neq 0
.
We conclude that (2) is equivalent to
G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma=0
.
It remains to show that
G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\neq\emptyset
if and only if

G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\neq\emptyset
.

From the inclusion
G\subset G_{\mathbb{R}}
,
the direct implication is clear. For the converse, let
g_{i}\in G
and
t_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\sum_{i}t_{i}g_{i}\in\operatorname{int}\sigma
. There exists
\epsilon>0

such that
\sum_{i}(t_{i}-\epsilon,t_{i}+\epsilon)g_{i}\subset\operatorname{int}\sigma
.
Then
\sum_{i}(tt_{i}-t\epsilon,tt_{i}+t\epsilon)g_{i}\subset\operatorname{int}\sigma
for
t>0
.
For
2t\epsilon>1
, there exist integers
z_{i}\in(tt_{i}-t\epsilon,tt_{i}+t\epsilon)
. Then

\sum_{i}z_{i}g_{i}\in\operatorname{int}\sigma\cap G
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space, let
G\leq V
be a closed subgroup, let
\sigma\subset V
be a closed convex cone such that
\dim\sigma=\dim V
. If
\dim V\in\{1,2\}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G\cap\sigma=0
.

(2)

There exists
\varphi\in\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}
such that
{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\subset G^{*}
.

Proof.

We may restate (2) as
\varphi\in G^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}
. By the separation theorem for convex sets,

G^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}=\emptyset
if and only if

G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma\neq 0
. Therefore (2) is equivalent to

G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma=0
. It remains to show that

G\cap\sigma=0
if and only if

G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma=0
.

Suppose
G\cap\sigma=0
. Then
G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma=\emptyset
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
0\neq\varphi\in\sigma^{\vee}

such that
G\subset\varphi^{\perp}
. If
\dim V=1
, we obtain
G=0
.
Then
G_{\mathbb{R}}=0
, hence
G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma=0
. Suppose
\dim V=2
. We may
choose an isomorphism
V\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
\sigma
corresponds to

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}
or
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. In the latter case
G=0
, and we argue as above. Suppose
\sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}
. If
\varphi\in\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}
, then
G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma\subset\sigma\cap\varphi^{\perp}=0
. Else, we may suppose
\varphi=(0,1)
. Then

G_{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}(1,0)
. Then
G\cap{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}=0
. Therefore
G=0
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Lemma
2.2
is no longer true if
\dim V\geq 3
.
For example, let
c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
,
V={\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
G={\mathbb{Z}}(0,1,-1)+{\mathbb{Z}}(1,c,-c)
,
\sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{\geq 0}
. Then
G\cap\sigma=0
,
G_{\mathbb{R}}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}|x_{2}+x_{3}=0\}
,
G_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}(1,0,0)
,
G^{\perp}={\mathbb{R}}(0,1,1)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G\leq{\mathbb{R}}
be a subgroup with dual subgroup
G^{*}\leq\check{{\mathbb{R}}}
,
let
t>0
. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

G\cap(0,t)=\emptyset
.

(2)

G^{*}\cap(0,\frac{1}{t}]\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose
y\in G^{*}\cap(0,\frac{1}{t}]
. Let
x\in G
with
x>0
.
Then
0<xy\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore
xy\geq 1
. Therefore
x\geq t
. We deduce
G\cap(0,t)=\emptyset
.

Conversely, suppose
G\cap(0,t)=\emptyset
. We may suppose
G
is closed. Then either
G=0
, or
G={\mathbb{Z}}g
for some
g\geq t
. If
G=0
,
then
G^{*}=\check{{\mathbb{R}}}
contains
(0,\frac{1}{t}]
. If
G={\mathbb{Z}}g
, then

G^{*}={\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{g}
, and
0<\frac{1}{g}\leq\frac{1}{t}
. ∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G\leq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

be a subgroup such that
G\cap(0,+\infty)^{2}\neq\emptyset
. Let
t>0
and
U=(0,t)\times(0,+\infty)
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

G\cap U=\emptyset
.

(2)

There exists
(\check{x}_{1},0)\in G^{*}
with
0<\check{x}_{1}\leq\frac{1}{t}
.

Proof.

Define
\tilde{U}=(0,t)\times{\mathbb{R}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We claim that

G\cap U=\emptyset
if and only if
G\cap\tilde{U}=\emptyset
. Indeed,
suppose by contradiction that
G\cap U=\emptyset\neq G\cap\tilde{U}
. There exists
g=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in G
with
0<x^{\prime}<t
and
y^{\prime}\leq 0
. We may enlarge
t
and suppose
t=\inf\{x|\exists\ (x,y)\in G\cap(0,+\infty)^{2}\}
.
If
(x,y)\in G\cap(0,+\infty)^{2}
then

(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime})\in G\cap(0,+\infty)^{2}
. At the limit, we obtain

t\leq t-x^{\prime}
, that is
x^{\prime}\leq 0
. Contradiction!

Consider the projection
\pi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
\pi(x,y)=x
.
Then
\pi(G)=H
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tilde{U}=\pi^{-1}((0,t))
.
Therefore (1) is equivalent to

H\cap(0,t)=\emptyset
. By Lemma
2.4
, this is equivalent to

H^{*}\cap(0,\frac{1}{t}]\neq\emptyset
. The latter is equivalent to (2).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G\leq V
and
\varphi\in V^{*}\setminus 0
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G\leq\varphi^{*}
,
G\cap\varphi^{\perp}=0
.

(2)

G=0
, or
G={\mathbb{Z}}v
with
\langle\varphi,v\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

Proof.

The implication
(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
is clear.
For the converse, consider the subgroup
\langle\varphi,G\rangle\leq{\mathbb{Z}}
. If zero, then
G=G\cap\varphi^{\perp}=0
. If non-zero, it equals
q{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
q\geq 1
. Choose
v\in G
with
\varphi(v)=q
. Then

G={\mathbb{Z}}v+G\cap\varphi^{\perp}={\mathbb{Z}}v
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G\leq V
and
\varphi\in V^{*}\setminus 0
. The following
are equivalent:

(1)

\langle\varphi,G\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}
and
G\cap\varphi^{\perp}
is dense in
\varphi^{\perp}
.

(2)

G
is a dense subgroup of
\varphi^{*}
.

Proof.

Any subgroup
G\leq\varphi^{*}
is either a
subgroup of
\varphi^{\perp}
, or of the form
G={\mathbb{Z}}v+G\cap\varphi^{\perp}
where
\varphi(v)=q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
(and then
\varphi^{*}={\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v}{q}+\varphi^{\perp}
). If
G
is dense in
\varphi^{*}
, then
\varphi(G)
is dense in
{\mathbb{Z}}
, hence

\varphi(G)={\mathbb{Z}}
. The equivalence follows.
∎

3.
Combinatorial classification

Let
V
be a
2
-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space. Let
\sigma\subset V
be a closed strongly convex
2
-dimensional cone. Let
\sigma^{\vee}\subset V^{*}
be the dual cone inside the dual vector space. Let
\psi\in\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
. Define a functional

\sigma^{\vee}\ni m\mapsto\gamma(m):=\sup\{t\geq 0|\psi-tm\in\sigma^{\vee}\}\in[0,+\infty].

Since
0
is the smallest face of
\sigma^{\vee}
,
\gamma(0)=+\infty
and

\gamma(m)<+\infty
is a maximum for every
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
.
Note
\gamma(m)=0
if and only if
m
does not belong to the smallest face of
\sigma^{\vee}
which contains
\psi
.
The reciprocal
\sigma^{\vee}\ni m\mapsto\frac{1}{\gamma(m)}\in[0,+\infty]
is convex, positively homogeneous, piecewise linear with respect to the barycentric subdivison of
\psi\in\sigma^{\vee}
.
The set

\{m\in\sigma^{\vee}|\gamma(m)\geq 1\}=\sigma^{\vee}\cap(\psi-\sigma^{\vee})

is a compact convex polytope.

Let
G\leq(V,+)
be a subgroup such that
G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\neq\emptyset
.
Define

\lambda:=\lambda(\sigma,\psi,G)=\inf\{\langle\psi,e\rangle|e\in G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\}

and suppose
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
m\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0

such that
\gamma(m)>0
.

Proof.

We may suppose
V={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
\sigma={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
and
\psi=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\check{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
with

a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0
.

Case
\psi\in\partial(\sigma^{\vee})
. Here
\psi=(a_{1},0)

and
a_{1}>0
. Then
G\cap(0,\frac{\lambda}{a_{1}})\times(0,\infty)=\emptyset
.
By Lemma
2.5
, there exists
(m_{1},0)\in G^{*}
with

0<m_{1}\leq\frac{a_{1}}{\lambda}
. Then
(m_{1},0)\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0

and
\gamma(m_{1},0)=\frac{a_{1}}{m_{1}}\geq\lambda>0
.

Case
\psi\in\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}
. Here
a_{1},a_{2}>0
.
If
G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}=\{0\}
,
Lemma
2.2
implies that there exists
e\in\operatorname{int}\sigma
with

{\mathbb{R}}e\subseteq\overline{G}
. Then
\lambda=0
, a contradiction.
Therefore there exists
m\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
.
In dual coordinates,
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
and
\gamma(m)=\min(\frac{a_{1}}{m_{1}},\frac{a_{2}}{m_{2}})>0
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

\gamma:=\gamma(\sigma^{\vee},\psi,G^{*})=\sup\{\gamma(m)|m\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0\}
.

In particular,
\gamma>0
. We abused notation by denoting by
\gamma
both a functional and a number,
but we hope the reader will easily distinguish them in context.

Lemma 3.3
.

\lambda\geq\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
m\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
. Then
\psi-\gamma(m)m\in\sigma^{\vee}
.
Let
e\in G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma
. Then
\langle m,e\rangle
is a positive integer, so
\langle m,e\rangle\geq 1
. Therefore

\langle\psi,e\rangle\geq\langle\gamma(m)m,e\rangle\geq\gamma(m)
. We
obtain

\langle\psi,e\rangle\geq\gamma(m).

Taking infimum after all
e
, and supremum after all
m
, we obtain
the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\gamma
is a maximum.

Proof.

Since
\gamma\leq\lambda
,
\gamma
is a
positive real number. Let
c>0
with
c\gamma>1
.
Let
m^{n}\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
be a sequence with

\gamma(m^{n})\uparrow\gamma
.
Then
c\gamma(m^{n})>1
for
n\gg 0
, and therefore

m^{n}\in(c\psi-\sigma^{\vee})\cap\sigma^{\vee}
for
n\gg 0
.
The latter set is compact.
Therefore the sequence
m^{n}
has a cluster point
m
. It follows that

m\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
and
\gamma(m)=\gamma
. In particular,
m\neq 0
.
∎

By Lemma
3.4
, there exists
v^{1}\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0

with
\gamma(v^{1})=\gamma
.

Lemma 3.5
.

\langle v^{1},G\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

We have
\langle v^{1},G\rangle\leq{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since
G\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\neq\emptyset
,
\langle v^{1},G\rangle\neq 0
.
Therefore
\langle v^{1},G\rangle=k{\mathbb{Z}}
for some positive integer
k
.
Then
\frac{1}{k}v^{1}\in G^{*}\cap\sigma^{\vee}\setminus 0
. We
have
\gamma(\frac{1}{k}v^{1})=k\gamma(v^{1})=k\gamma
. Then
k\gamma\leq\gamma
,
so
k=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
\psi\in\partial(\sigma^{\vee})
. Then
\lambda=\gamma
,

\psi=\gamma v^{1}
, and
\lambda
is a minimum.

Proof.

We use the notations and argument in the first part
of the proof of Lemma
3.1
. We constructed

m=(m_{1},0)\in G^{*}\cap\partial(\sigma^{\vee})
with
\gamma(m)\geq\lambda
.
But
\lambda\geq\gamma
, so
\gamma(m)=\lambda=\gamma
.
Equivalently,
\psi=\gamma m
. In particular,
\psi
is a minimum
and
m=v^{1}
(so
v^{1}
is unique maximizer in this case).
∎

We suppose from now on that
\psi\in\operatorname{int}\sigma^{\vee}
.
Consider the subgroup
G\cap(v^{1})^{\perp}\leq(v^{1})^{\perp}
.
Since

\sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}
is the standard positive cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a
2
-dimensional lattice which contains
e_{1}=(1,0),e_{2}=(0,1)
as primitive vectors.

Let
P\in(X,B)
be an invariant structure of log variety with

\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)=a\geq 0
. We have
B=b_{1}E_{1}+b_{2}E_{2}
, with
b_{1},b_{2}\in[0,1]
.
Let
\psi\in N^{*}=:M
with
\langle\psi,e_{i}\rangle=1-b_{i}
for
i=1,2
,
so that
(\chi^{\psi})+K+B=0
.
For any toric valuation
E_{e}
of
X
(here
e\in N^{prim}\cap\sigma
),
the log discrepancy of
(X,B)
in
E_{e}
is computed as follows:

a_{E_{e}}(X,B)=\langle\psi,e\rangle.

In particular,
a=\min\{\langle\psi,e\rangle|e\in N\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma\}
.
We have
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)\geq t
if and only if
N\cap\{e\in\operatorname{int}\sigma|\langle\psi,e\rangle<t\}=\emptyset
.

1)

a\in[0,2]
. Note that
a=0
if and only if
B=\Sigma
,
and
a=2
if and only if
X={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
and
B=0
. By Theorem
4.1
,
we deduce that
a\geq 1
if and only if
(X,B)
is one of the following:

•

({\mathbb{A}}^{1},b_{1}\cdot 0)\times({\mathbb{A}}^{1},b_{2}\cdot 0)
,

b_{1}+b_{2}\leq 1
(here
a=2-b_{1}-b_{2}
), or

•

B=0
and
X
is a quotient of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
of type
\frac{1}{l+1}(l,1)\ (l\geq 1)
. Here
a=1
.

2)
Denote by
|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}
the invariant hyperplane sections through
P
, or equivalently, the hyperplane sections through
P
which are supported by
\Sigma
. One of the following holds:

a)

There exists
H\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}
,
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B+aH)=0
, or

b)

There exist
H_{1},H_{2}\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
such that
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=a
and
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B+\gamma_{1}H_{1}+\gamma_{2}H_{2})=0
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\max\{\operatorname{lct}_{P}(X,B;H)|H\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}\}
. Choose

H_{1}\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}
with
(X,B+\gamma H_{1})
maximally log canonical.
We have
a\geq\gamma
. If
a=\gamma
, a) holds with
H=H_{1}
.

Suppose
a>\gamma
. Note that
(\psi)=\Sigma-B
.
We constructed
\alpha\in[0,1)
and

H_{2}\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}

with
B+\gamma_{1}H_{1}+\gamma_{2}H_{2}=\Sigma
, where

\gamma_{2}=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-\alpha},\gamma_{1}=\lambda-\gamma_{2}=\gamma-\alpha\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma.

We have a chain which strictly decreases the minimal log canonical
center near
P

(X,B)\prec(X,B+\gamma H_{1})\prec(X,B+\gamma_{1}H_{1}+\gamma_{2}H_{2})=(X,\Sigma).

Here
B+\gamma_{1}H_{1}+\gamma_{2}H_{2}=(B+\gamma H_{1})+\gamma_{2}(H_{2}-\alpha H_{1})
.
Note that
H_{2}-\alpha H_{1}
is effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier. Its index coincides
with the index of
\alpha
. If
\operatorname{mult}_{E_{1}}(B+\gamma H_{1})=0
and

l=\operatorname{mult}_{E_{1}}(H_{1})
, then
\operatorname{index}(\alpha)=l
and
\gamma=\frac{1-b_{1}}{l}
.
In particular,
l\gamma\leq 1
. We showed that

\frac{a}{2}\leq\gamma\leq a,

so
l\leq\frac{2}{a}
. Therefore
l
is bounded by
a
.
Also, the inequalities
\psi^{\prime}\leq 1
and
\alpha<1
become

a-\gamma\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\gamma.

Let
a_{1}=\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B+\gamma H_{1})
. We showed that

•

a=2\gamma
if
\alpha=0
,

•

a=\gamma+(l-l\alpha)a_{1}
if
\alpha\neq 0
.

Suppose
b_{1},b_{2}
belong to a given DCC set
{\mathcal{B}}\subset[0,1]
.
Then
a
satisfies ACC. In fact, note that
\gamma<a
and
b_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}
implies

a-\gamma\geq\epsilon(a,l,{\mathcal{B}})>0
. One shows that if
a-\gamma
is bounded away from
0
,
then
X
belongs to finitely many isomorphism types. So

a
can only accumulate to
\gamma
, from above.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

There exists
H\in|{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,P}|_{\Sigma}
with
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B+\frac{a}{2}H)\geq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose in b) that
\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}
. Then
\gamma_{1}\geq\frac{a}{2}
,
so we may take
H=H_{1}
.
∎

3)
Suppose
B
has standard coefficients and

\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)\geq\frac{p}{q}
. Then there exists
1\leq s\leq\frac{2q}{p}

and
B\leq B_{qs}\leq\Sigma
such that
qs(K+B_{qs})\sim 0
and

\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B_{qs})\geq\frac{p}{q}
.

Proof.

Case a): there exists
0\neq m\in M\cap\sigma^{\vee}

with
\psi-\frac{p}{q}m\in\sigma^{\vee}
. Set

B_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(1-\frac{pm_{i}}{q})E_{i}
. Then
B\leq B_{q}\leq\Sigma

and
(\chi^{pm})+q(K+B_{q})=0
. Moreover,
\psi_{K+B_{q}}=\frac{p}{q}m
, so

\langle\frac{p}{q}m,e\rangle\geq\frac{p}{q},\forall e\in N\cap\operatorname{int}\sigma.

Case b):
\psi=(a-\frac{a-\gamma}{1-\alpha})m^{1}+\frac{a-\gamma}{1-\alpha}m^{2}
.
In particular,

\psi\geq(\frac{p}{q}-\frac{\frac{p}{q}-\gamma}{1-\alpha})m^{1}+\frac{\frac{p}{q}-\gamma}{1-\alpha}m^{2}.

Equivalently,

q\psi\geq(p-\frac{p-q\gamma}{1-\alpha})m^{1}+\frac{p-q\gamma}{1-\alpha}m^{2}.

Since
B
has standard coefficients,
\gamma=\frac{1}{l}
for an
integer
l
with
l\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The above inequality becomes

(l-l\alpha)q\psi\geq(q-pl\alpha)m^{1}+(lp-q)m^{2}.

Let
s=l-l\alpha
. Then
sq\psi\geq z_{1}m^{1}+z_{2}m^{2}
for some

z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Set
B_{sq}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(1-\frac{z_{1}m^{1}_{i}+z_{2}m^{2}_{i}}{sq})E_{i}
.
Then
B\leq B_{sq}\leq\Sigma
and
(\chi^{z_{1}m^{1}+z_{2}m^{2}})+sq(K+B_{sq})=0
.
Thus

\langle\psi_{K+B_{sq}},e\rangle=\langle\frac{z_{1}m^{1}+z_{2}m^{2}}{sq},e\rangle\geq\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{sq}=\frac{p}{q}.

Note
s=l-l\alpha\leq l=\gamma^{-1}\leq\frac{2}{a}
.
∎

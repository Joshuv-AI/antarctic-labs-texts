An analytic criterion for the local finiteness of a countable semigroup
O. Yu. Aristov

Abstract
. We prove that a countable semigroup S S is locally finite if and only if the Arens-Michael envelope of its semigroup algebra is a ( D ​ F ) (DF) -space. This is a counterpart to a recent result of the author, which asserts that S S is finitely generated if and only if the Arens-Michael envelope is a Fréchet space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46A04, 46H99, 20M25, Secondary 46A11

It was discovered in
[
Ar20+
]
that topological properties of the Arens-Michael envelope of the semigroup algebra of a countable semigroup
S
(denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
) depends on properties of the semigroup and vice versa. Namely,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a Fréchet space if and only if
S
is finitely generated. In this note we show that
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a
(DF)
-space if and only if
S
is locally finite. We also give a direct proof of an auxiliary result, which asserts that
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a nuclear space for every countable
S
.

Recall that an
Arens-Michael algebra
is a complete Hausdorff locally convex algebra whose topology can be determined by a family of submultiplicative prenorms (i.e., satisfying
\|ab\|\leqslant\|a\|\,\|b\|
for every elements
a
and
b
).
The
Arens-Michael envelope
of a (not necessarily unital) associative algebra
A
over
\mathbb{C}
is the completion of
A
with respect to the topology determined by all possible submultiplicative prenorms. (This is a simplified version of a definition in
[
He93
]
in which locally convex algebras are considered.) For a countable semigroup
S
we denote by
\mathbb{C}S
its semigroup algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. (Note that
\mathbb{C}S
is unital if and only if
S
has an identity.) We need the following explicit description of the Arens-Michael envelope of
\mathbb{C}S
given in
[
Ar20+
]
.

We say that an
\mathbb{R}
-valued function
\ell
on
S
is a
length function
if

\ell(s_{1}s_{2})\leqslant\ell(s_{1})+\ell(s_{2})\qquad(s_{1},s_{2}\in S).

Suppose
\{s_{n}\!:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a generating subset (e.g., one can take
S
itself). For any function
F\!:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
put

\ell_{F}(s)\!:=\inf\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}F(n_{k})\!:\,s=s_{n_{1}}\cdots s_{n_{m}}\!:\,n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\,.

(1)

It is easy to see that
\ell_{F}
is a non-negative length function.
Note that any element
a
of
\mathbb{C}S
can be written as a finite sum
\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}\delta_{s}
, where
\delta_{s}
is the delta-function at
s
. It is not hard to show that

\|a\|_{F}\!:=\sum_{s\in S}|a_{s}|\,e^{\ell_{F}(s)}.

(2)

defines a submultiplicative prenorm on
\mathbb{C}S
. (Of course, one may replace here Euler’s constant by any strictly positive number.)

Proposition 1
.

(see
[
Ar20+
, Lemma 3.13]
)

Let
S
be a countable semigroup with generating subset
\{s_{n}\!:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Then the locally convex space

\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}=\Bigl\{a=\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}\delta_{s}\!:\|a\|_{F}<\infty\quad\forall F\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}\Bigr\}

endowed with the convolution and the topology determined by the family of submultiplicative prenorms
\{\|\cdot\|_{F}\!:\,F\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}\}
defined in (
2
)
is the Arens-Michael envelope of the semigroup algebra
\mathbb{C}S
.

It is worth mentioning that, by the universal property of the Arens-Michael envelope
[
He93
, Chapter 5, Definition 2.21]
, the resulting topology does not depend on enumeration of the generating subset.
Note also that we can take
F
with values in
\mathbb{N}
but, nevertheless, the corresponding defining system of submultiplicative prenorms may still be uncountable.

To prove the main result, Theorem
6
, we need the following theorem on nuclearity.

Theorem 2
.

Let
S
be a countable semigroup. Then the locally convex space
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is nuclear.

An idea of a proof of this theorem was communicated by the author to Akbarov and the corresponding argument (in the group case) can be found in
[
Ak20+
, Lemma 3.15]
. But in some points it differs from the proof given here.
In particular, Akbarov’s version is based on the Grothendieck–Pietsch nuclearity criterion and uses a terminology different from ours. The direct and relatively short proof that is presented here includes as well a concise version of a combinatorial trick essential in the argument. Note that Theorem
2
can also be deduced from
[
Ar20+
, Theorem 6.3]
.

For any
F\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
we denote by
\mathbb{C}S_{F}
the completion of
\mathbb{C}S
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{F}
.
It is obvious that for
F_{1}\leqslant F_{2}
there is a naturally defined continuous homomorphism
\theta_{F_{2}F_{1}}\!:\mathbb{C}S_{F_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}S_{F_{1}}
of Banach algebras.

Proposition 3
.

For every
F_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
there is
F_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
F_{2}>F_{1}
and
\theta_{F_{2}F_{1}}\!:\mathbb{C}S_{F_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}S_{F_{1}}
is a nuclear linear map.

Note that it is important in the proof that the generating set is infinite. For a finitely generated semigroup we must take a generating set with repetition.

We need an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
F_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Put
F_{2}(n)\!:=F_{1}(n)+n
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\sum_{s\in S}e^{\ell_{F_{1}}(s)-\ell_{F_{2}}(s)}<\infty.

(3)

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and put
C_{n}\!:=\{s\in S\!:\,\ell_{F_{2}}(s)-\ell_{F_{1}}(s)=n\}
. We first claim that

|C_{n}|\leqslant 2^{n}-1,

(4)

where
|C_{n}|
denotes the cardinality of
C_{n}
.

Indeed, let
s\in S
. It follows from the definition of
\ell_{F_{2}}
and the fact that the values of
\ell_{F_{2}}
are in
\mathbb{N}
that there are
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
s=s_{n_{1}}\cdots s_{n_{m}}
and

\ell_{F_{2}}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}F_{2}(n_{k}).

For every
s\in S
fix
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
satisfying this equality. Then we have the map
s\mapsto(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
, where
m
depends on
s
. It is obvious that the map is injective.

Since

\ell_{F_{2}}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(F_{1}(n_{k})+n_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{F_{1}}(s)\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{m}F_{1}(n_{k}),

we have
\ell_{F_{2}}(s)-\ell_{F_{1}}(s)\geqslant\sum n_{k}
for every
s
.
In particular,
|C_{n}|
does not exceed the number of finite sequences
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
in
\mathbb{N}
satisfying the inequality
n\geqslant\sum n_{k}
. It is well known that the number of compositions of
j
(ways of writing
j
as the sum
\sum n_{k}
, where
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
are arbitrary positive integers) equals
2^{j-1}
. Summing over
j
, we obtain (
4
).

Further,
S=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}C_{n}
because
\ell_{F_{2}}(s)-\ell_{F_{1}}(s)>0
for every
s
. Since
C_{n}
are disjoint, we have

\sum_{s}e^{\ell_{F_{1}}(s)-\ell_{F_{2}}(s)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|C_{n}|e^{-n}.

It follows from (
4
) that

\sum_{s}e^{\ell_{F_{1}}(s)-\ell_{F_{2}}(s)}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n}e^{-n}<\infty.

∎

Proof of Proposition
3
.

Put
F_{2}(n)\!:=F_{1}(n)+n
as in Lemma
4
. Let
\chi_{s}
denote the functional on
\mathbb{C}S_{F_{2}}
such that
\chi_{s}(\delta_{s})=1
and
\chi_{s}(\delta_{t})=0
when
t\neq s
. It is easy to see that
\|\delta_{s}\|_{F_{1}}=e^{\ell_{F_{1}}(s)}
and
\|\chi_{s}\|^{\prime}_{F_{2}}=e^{-\ell_{F_{2}}(s)}
, where the prime means that we take the norm in the dual Banach space.
Writing an arbitrary element
a
of
\mathbb{C}S_{F_{2}}
as
\sum_{s}a_{s}\delta_{s}
we have

\theta_{F_{2}F_{1}}(a)=\sum_{s\in S}\chi_{s}(a)\,\delta_{s}.

It follows from Lemma
4
that

\sum\|\chi_{s}\|^{\prime}_{F_{2}}\,\|\delta_{s}\|_{F_{1}}<\infty

and so
\theta_{F_{2}F_{1}}
is nuclear.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\{s_{n}\!:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an infinite generating subset of
S
.
It follows from Proposition
1
that
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a projective limit of the system of Banach spaces of the form
\mathbb{C}S_{F}
, where
F
runs over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, we can assume that each
F
takes values in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Then for every submultiplicative prenorm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{C}S
there is
F_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|\cdot\|\leqslant C\|\cdot\|_{F_{1}}
for some
C>0
. By Proposition
3
, for every
F_{1}
there is
F_{2}\geqslant F_{1}
such that
\theta_{F_{2}F_{1}}
is a nuclear linear map, which immediately implies that
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is nuclear.
∎

Recall that a semigroup
S
is said to be
locally finite
if every finite subset of
S
is contained in a finite subsemigroup (see, e.g.,
[
Gri95
, p. 161]
or, in the case of a group,
[
KB73
]
).

Recall also that a locally convex space is called a
(DF)
-space
if it has a fundamental sequence of bounded subsets and every strongly bounded subset of its strong dual that is a union of countably many equicontinuous sets is also equicontinuous
[
Ko69
, § 29.3]
. We do not use this definition directly but refer only to the classical results that the strong dual of a Fréchet space is a
(DF)
-space and that the strong dual of a
(DF)
-space is a Fréchet space
[
Ko69
, pp. 396–397]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
S
be a locally finite countable semigroup. Then
\mathbb{C}S
endowed with the strongest locally convex topology is an Arens-Michael algebra. As a corollary,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}S
and hence it is a
(DF)
-space.

Proof.

The strongest locally convex topology on
\mathbb{C}S
is determined by prenorms of the form
|a|_{\varphi}\!:=\sum_{s}|\alpha_{s}|\,\varphi(s)
, where
\varphi
runs over all functions on
S
with values in
[1,+\infty)
(here
a=\sum\alpha_{s}\delta_{s}
). On the other hand, topology on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is determined by the family (
\|\cdot\|_{F}
), where

F
runs over all
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{N}
(with respect to a fixed
generating subset
\{s_{n}\!:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
); see (
2
). The former family clearly includes the latter. To complete the proof we show that the topologies coincide on
\mathbb{C}S
. It suffices to show that for any
\varphi\!:S\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
there is
F
such that
\varphi(s)\leqslant e^{\ell_{F}(s)}
for all
s\in S
.

If
S
is finite, there is nothing to prove. Suppose that
S
is infinite and denote by
S_{n}
the subsemigroup generated by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
. Passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
s_{n}\notin S_{n-1}
. Since
S
is locally finite, each
S_{n}
is finite. Hence there is a non-negative increasing function
F
such that

F(n)\geqslant\max\{\ln\varphi(s)\!:\,s\in S_{n}\}\qquad\text{for every~$n$.}

(5)

Fix
s\in S
and take
n
such that
s\in S_{n}\setminus S_{n-1}
. Then for every decomposition
s=s_{n_{1}}\cdots s_{n_{m}}
there is
n_{j}
equal or greater than
n
and so we obtain
F(n)\leqslant\sum_{k}F(n_{k})
since
F\geqslant 0
. Therefore
F(n)\leqslant\ell_{F}(s)
. It follows from (
5
) that
\varphi(s)\leqslant e^{F(n)}
and so
\varphi(s)\leqslant e^{\ell_{F}(s)}
. This holds for all
s\in S
and so the topologies coincide.

Finally,
\mathbb{C}S
is isomorphic to the strong dual space of
\mathbb{C}^{S}
, which is a Fréchet space because
S
is countable. Thus
\mathbb{C}S
is a
(DF)
-space
[
Ko69
, p. 396]
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

A countable semigroup
S
is locally finite if and only if
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a
(DF)
-space.

Proof.

The necessity follows from Proposition
5
.

To verify the sufficiency suppose that
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a
(DF)
-space. We need to show that any finitely generated subsemigroup
S_{0}
of
S
is finite. Note that, by the universal property of the Arens-Michael envelope, the embedding
S_{0}\subset S
induces a continuous homomorphism
j\!:\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
. It follows from the representation of elements of these algebras as series that
j
is injective. The Hahn–Banach theorem implies that the corresponding linear map
j^{\prime}\!:(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S})^{\prime}\to(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}})^{\prime}
between their strong dual spaces is surjective. Moreover, we claim that
j^{\prime}
is open.

To prove the claim we use Pták’s open mapping theorem, which asserts that every surjective continuous linear map from a Pták space to a barreled space is open (see a proof in
[
Ko79
, § 34.2, p. 27, (3)]
and the definition of a Pták space on p. 26 in [ibid.]). Since
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a
(DF)
-space, the strong dual
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S})^{\prime}
is a Fréchet space
[
Ko69
, p. 397]
.
It follows from
[
Ko69
, § 21.10, p. 273, (5)]
that being a Fréchet space it is a Pták space. On the other hand, since
S_{0}
is finitely generated,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}}
a Fréchet space
[
Ar20+
, Proposition 3.15]
. Being also nuclear by Theorem
2
, it is a Montel space
[
Tr67
, p. 519, (50.12)]
. The strong dual of a Montel space is a Montel space
[
Ko69
, § 27.2, p. 369]
. In particular,
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}})^{\prime}
is barreled. Thus we can apply Pták’s open mapping theorem and
j^{\prime}
is open.

Finally, since
j^{\prime}
is open and
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S})^{\prime}
is a Fréchet space, so is
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}})^{\prime}
. Note that every metrizable
(DF)
-space is normable
[
CB87
, Observation 8.3.6]
. Thus
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S_{0}}
is a Banach space. Then all the norms of the form (
2
) on
\mathbb{C}S_{0}
are equivalent and so
S_{0}
is finite as desired.
∎

Remark 7
.

Note that the classes of locally finite and finitely generated semigroups are opposite in properties since their intersection contains only finite semigroups. This fact is parallel to the assertion that every
Fréchet
(DF)
-space is a Banach space (because every metrizable
(DF)
-space is normable). Indeed, if
S
is a countable semigroup,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a Fréchet space if only if
S
is finitely generated
[
Ar20+
, Proposition 3.15]
and
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a
(DF)
-space if only if
S
is locally finite (Theorem
6
). Finally,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}S}
is a Banach space if only if
S
is finite.

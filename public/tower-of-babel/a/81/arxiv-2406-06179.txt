Operator-Valued Twisted Araki–Woods Algebras
Rahul Kumar R, Melchior Wirth

Abstract
. We introduce operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras. These are operator-valued versions of a class of second quantization algebras that includes q q -Gaussian and q q -Araki–Woods algebras and also generalize Shlyakhtenko’s von Neumann algebras generated by operator-valued semicircular variables. We develop a disintegration theory that reduces the isomorphism type of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras over type I factors to the scalar-valued case. Moreover, these algebras come with a natural weight, and we characterize its modular theory. We also give sufficient criteria that guarantee that factoriality of these algebras.



1.
Introduction

Second quantization von Neumann algebras or Gaussian type von Neumann algebras are algebras generated by field operators on various versions of Fock spaces. They arise naturally both from a purely mathematical and a physics viewpoint and play an important role in the study of operator algebras. Perhaps the two most prominent second quantization von Neumann algebras are the von Neumann algebras generated by CCR and CAR relations, which provide the mathematical framework for Bosonic and Fermionic interactions.

There are interesting generalizations to these classical constructions in different directions. First to mention are the algebras of field operators on
full
Fock space introduced as a model of free semicircular variables by Voiculescu. These are at the heart of free probability theory (see
[
VDN92
]
) and generate von Neumann algebras isomorphic to the free group factors.

In the nineties, M. Bożejko and R. Speicher in
[
BS94
]
significantly generalized CCR/CAR algebras by allowing for a much more general type of ‘symmetry’ encoded in a ‘twist’ operator satisfying the Yang–Baxter equation. An important special case are the
q
-Gaussian von Neumann algebras, for which the twist is a scalar multiple
q
of the tensor flip. Voiculescu’s von Neumann algebras generated by free semicircular variables correspond to the case
q=0
in ths setting.

Fundamental properties like factoriality and amenability of the
q
-Gaussian algebras and more generally von Neumann algebras generated by field operators on a twisted Fock space have been studied extensively in the last two decades. Through the combined efforts of several groups it is now known that these algebras are non-amenable type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors (see
[
BKS97
,
Nou04
,
Ric05
,
Kró06
,
SW18
,
Bik+21
,
MS23
,
Yan23
]
).

Shlyakhtenko introduced a further generalization of Voiculescu’s construction in
[
Shl97
]
by incorporating a one-parameter unitary group but keeping the full Fock space. These algebras are the non-tracial counterparts of free group factors and are known as free Araki–Woods factors. They are non-amenable full factors of type
\mathrm{III}
whenever the associated one-parameter group of unitaries is non-trivial. Free Araki-Woods factors possess many interesting properties: For example, they lack Cartan sub-algebras, have the complete metric approximation property
[
HR11
]
, and are strongly solid
[
BHV18
]
.

In
[
Hia03
]
, Hiai introduced a
q
-deformed version of free Araki–Woods factors, which are non-tracial analogs of the
q
-Gaussian algebras. As in the tracial case, properties like factoriality, non-amenability, etc. are harder to prove for
q\neq 0
because the powerful tools from free probability do not immediately apply. After a significant number of partial results
[
Nou04
,
BM17
,
SW18
,
Bik+23
]
, a complete solution of the factoriality problem was obtained very recently by Kumar, Skalski and Wasilewski
[
KSW23
]
.

A further generalization of Hiai’s
q
-deformed Araki–Woods algebras, namely mixed
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras involving multi-scalar symmetry, was introduced in
[
BKM23
]
and studied in
[
Kum23
,
BKM24
]
.

In terms of a number of structural properties like factoriality and amenability, these deformed algebras behave either like free group factors or free Araki–Woods factors. However, not much is known about the dependence of the isomorphism class of these second quantization von Neumann algebras on the value of
q
(or more generally the twist). On the positive side, it was shown by Guionnet and Shlyakhtenko
[
GS14
]
using free monotone transport that for finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and small
q
(depending on the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space), the
q
-Gaussian algebras are isomorphic to free group factors. This result was generalized to the non-tracial case of
q
-Araki–Woods factors by
[
Nel15
]
.

The range of
q
for which this result is valid degenerates to
\{0\}
as the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space goes to infinity, and indeed it was proved by Caspers
[
Cas23
]
that infinitely generated
q
-Gaussian von Neumann algebras for
q\neq 0
do not have Akemann-Ostrand property and hence are not isomorphic to free group factors.

The dependence of the isomorphism type on the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space is even harder – already for
q=0
and finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, it reduces to the famous free group factor isomorphism problem.

Motivated by applications to quantum field theory, Correa da Silva and Lechner
[
CL23
]
(see also
[
Lec23
]
for a summary) introduced a general framework for both the tracial and non-tracial second quantization algebras mentioned in the previous paragraphs. Given a Hilbert space
H
, a standard subspace
K\subset H
in the sense of modular theory, and a bounded self-adjoint Yang-Baxter operator
T
on
H\otimes H
satisfying certain positivity condition, they constructed the
T
-twisted Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)
and von Neumann algebras
\Gamma_{T}(K\subset H)
, which they call
twisted Araki–Woods algebras
. These algebras are generated by field operators on
\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)
associated with vectors in
K\subset H
.

Correa da Silva and Lechner showed that if the twist
T
is compatible with the standard subspace
K\subset H
in a certain sense and satisfies a condition called crossing symmetry, the vacuum vector in
\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)
is cyclic and separating for
\Gamma_{T}(K\subset H)
. Depending on the choice of the standard subspace
K\subset H
and the twist
T
, the twisted Araki–Woods algebras
\Gamma_{T}(K\subset H)
take various forms and in particular generalize all the algebras mentioned earlier. It was proved by Yang
[
Yan23
]
that
\Gamma_{T}(K\subset H)
is a non-amenable factor when
2\leq\dim(H)<\infty
and in addition is isomorphic to a free Araki-Woods factor for
\lVert T\rVert
small (depending on the dimension of
H
).

In this article we introduce operator-valued versions of these twisted Araki–Woods algebras. Operator-valued (free) second quantization algebras were first considered by Shlyakhtenko in his work on operator-valued probability when he studied the von Neumann algebras generated by operator-valued semicircular variables
[
Shl99
]
and have found applications in the study of generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups
[
JRS
,
Wir22
]
. Operator-valued versions of twisted Fock spaces and specifically Bosonic Fock spaces have occurred in the mathematical physics literature in relation to quantum field theory
[
Ske98
,
Vas24
]
.

In our operator-valued setting, the underlying Hilbert space is replaced by a Hilbert bimodule (or correspondence) over a base von Neumann algebra. While it is not obvious how to define a bimodule version of standard subspaces, the equivalent formulation in terms of a one-parameter unitary group and an anti-unitary involution, which was used in Shlyakhtenko’s definition of free Araki–Woods algebras, has a bimodule analog in the so-called Tomita correspondences. Tomita correspondences were recently introduced by the second-named author in the study of generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. In contrast to Shlyakhtenko’s von Neumann algebras generated by operator-valued semicircular variables, our approach is thus basis-free. Both approaches are equivalent in the case when the twist is zero and the canonical conditional expectation defined by Shlyakhtenko in this setting is faithful, as we show in this article.

Another difficulty in the operator-valued setting arises if one wants to define analogs of the
q
-Gaussian or
q
-Araki–Woods algebras. The definition of the
q
-deformed tensor product in the scalar-valued case relies on the existence of the tensor flip
\xi\otimes\eta\mapsto\eta\otimes\xi
on
H\otimes H
or more generally the action of
S_{n}
on
H^{\otimes n}
that permutes the tensor factors. In the operator-valued case, when the tensor product of Hilbert spaces is replaced by the relative tensor product of correspondences, the naive definition of the tensor flip does not necessarily result in a bounded map, let alone a bimodule map. This difficulty in defining Bosonic or Fermionic Fock bimodules was already noted in the mathematical physics literature
[
Ske98
,
Vas24
]
. For this reason, we work in the framework of general twists, for which the structural requirements in the operator-valued case are transparent. In specific examples, we show that there are still natural operator-valued versions of
q
-Gaussian and
q
-Araki–Woods algebras.

Let us give some more details of our definition of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras. If
(M,\varphi)
is a pair consisting of a von Neumann algebra
M
and a normal semi-finite faithful weight
\varphi
on
M
, a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
is a triple
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
consisting of a correspondence
\mathcal{H}
from
M
to itself together with an anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}
and a strongly continuous one-parameter group of unitaries
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying some compatibility conditions (see Definition
2.5
). Given a Tomita correspondence
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
over
(M,\varphi)
and a contractive bimodule map
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
that satisfies certain positivity condition similar to the ones in
[
CL23
]
, we define a twisted inner product on the relative tensor powers
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
to construct the twisted Fock bimodule
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
. We define the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
to be the von Neumann algebra generated by the left action of
M
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
and the field operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi):=a_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
for
\xi
varying over an appropriate real subspace of
\mathcal{H}
that depends on
\mathcal{J}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
. The inclusion
M\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
comes with a natural faithful normal conditional expectation so that
\varphi
induces a faithful normal semifinite weight
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
. In the case when
M=\mathbb{C}1
, the algebras
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
reduces to the (scalar-valued) twisted Araki–Woods algebra considered in
[
CL23
]
.

It seems more difficult to develop a comprehensive structure theory for the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras compared to the scalar-valued case for at least two reasons: For one, the structure of the base algebra
M
comes into play. As a simple observation, when the Tomita correspondence is zero, then the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra coincides with the base algebra. But even for among non-trivial Tomita correspondences, there are examples for which
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))\cong M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
. As a second reason, while the isomorphism type of a Hilbert space is determined by its dimension, the correspondences over a given von Neumann algebra have a much richer structure. As an example, if one wants to study conjugate variables for the field operators in
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
relative to the base algebra
M
, it follows from Shlyakhtenko’s work
[
Shl00
]
that the codomain of the derivations should be chosen depending on structure of
\mathcal{H}
.

Our main contributions apart from the definition of the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras are the following. First, we develop a disintegration theory of Tomita correspondences over semi-finite von Neumann algebras. In the special case of type I factors, this leads to a complete characterization of Tomita correspondences and a characterization of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras as tensor products of the base algebra with scalar-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras. In particular, this clarifies the structure of Shlyakhtenko’s von Neumann algebras generated by
M
-valued semicircular variables if
M
is a type I factor. Second, we characterize the modular theory of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
in terms of the underlying Tomita correspondence. Third, we give sufficient criteria under which the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras are factors. Furthermore, we also show that several natural von Neumann algebraic constructions fall into the class of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras.

2.1.
Weights on Von Neumann Algebras

In this section we briefly recall some basic facts and fix the notation regarding weights, modular theory and several classes of bimodules over von Neumann algebras. Except for Subsection

]

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. A
weight
on
M
is a map
\varphi\colon M_{+}\to[0,\infty]
such that
\varphi(x+y)=\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)
and
\varphi(\lambda x)=\lambda\varphi(x)
for all
x,y\in M_{+}
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Here we use the usual convention
0\cdot\infty=0
.

For a weight
\varphi
we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\{x\in M_{+}\mid\varphi(x)<\infty\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\{x\in M\mid x^{\ast}x\in\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lin}\{x^{\ast}y\mid x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\}.

The weight
\varphi
is called
normal
if
\sup_{j}\varphi(x_{j})=\varphi(\sup_{j}x_{j})
for every bounded increasing net
(x_{j})
in
M_{+}
,

if
\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}
generates

as a von Neumann algebra, and

if
\varphi(x^{\ast}x)=0
implies

.

Every element of
\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
is a linear combination of four elements of
\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}
, and
\varphi
can be linearly extended to
\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
. This extension will still be denoted by
\varphi
.

A semi-cyclic representation of
M
is a triple
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
consisting of a normal representation of
M
on
H
and a
\sigma
-strong
∗
closed linear map
\Lambda
from a dense left ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
M
into

with dense range such that

\displaystyle\pi(x)\Lambda(y)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(xy)

for all
x\in M
and
y\in\mathfrak{n}
. We call
\mathfrak{n}
the
definition ideal
of the semi-cyclic representation
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
.

A normal semi-finite weight
\varphi
on
M
gives rise to a semi-cyclic representation
(\pi_{\varphi},L_{2}(M,\varphi),\Lambda_{\varphi})
as follows:
L_{2}(M,\varphi)
is the Hilbert space obtained from
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
after separation and completion with respect to the inner product

\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\times\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\to\mathbb{C},\,(x,y)\mapsto\varphi(x^{\ast}y),

\Lambda_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\to L_{2}(M,\varphi)
is the quotient map and
\pi_{\varphi}
is given by
\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy)
.

This semi-cyclic representation is essentially uniquely determined by

in the following sense: If
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
is another semi-cyclic representation of

with definition ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and
\langle\Lambda(x),\Lambda(y)\rangle=\varphi(x^{\ast}y)
for all

, then there exists a unitary
U\colon L_{2}(M,\varphi)\to H
such that
U\Lambda_{\varphi}=\Lambda
and
U\pi_{\varphi}(x)U^{\ast}=\pi(x)
for all

.

The operator

\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})\to L^{2}(M,\varphi),\,\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\mapsto\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})

is an anti-linear closable operator. Let
S_{\varphi}
denote its closure and
S_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}
the polar decomposition of
S_{\varphi}
. The operator
J_{\varphi}
, called the
modular conjugation
, is an anti-unitary involution and
\Delta_{\varphi}
, called the
modular operator
, is a positive non-singular self-adjoint operator. We define the right GNS embedding

\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\to L^{2}(M,\varphi),\,x\mapsto J_{\varphi}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})

and the right action

\pi_{\varphi}^{\prime}\colon M^{\mathrm{op}}\to B(L^{2}(M,\varphi)),\,x^{\mathrm{op}}\mapsto J_{\varphi}\pi_{\varphi}(x)^{\ast}J_{\varphi}.

The
modular group
associated with
\varphi
is the point-weak
∗
continuous automorphism group
\sigma^{\varphi}
on
M
given by
\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)=\pi_{\varphi}^{-1}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it})
.

for the set of all
(\varphi,\psi)
-bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}
. If the weights
\varphi
and
\psi
are clear from the context, we simply talk about left-bounded, right-bounded and bounded vectors.

If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is left
\psi
-bounded, then
L_{\psi}(\xi)
is a bounded right module map. In fact, right module maps of this form are strongly dense in the set of all bounded right module maps, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 2.1
.

The intersection of
\{L_{\psi}(\xi)\mid\xi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{N},\psi)\}
with the unit ball of
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})
is dense in the unit ball of
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})\}
with respect to the strong operator topology. Moreover, if
\psi
is finite, then
\{L_{\psi}(\xi)\mid\xi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{N},\psi)\}=\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})\}
with
\lVert T\rVert\leq 1
. Choose
x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\psi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\psi}^{\ast}
with
\lVert x_{\alpha}\rVert\leq 1
and
x_{\alpha}\to 1
strongly and let
\xi_{\alpha}=T\Lambda_{\psi}(x_{\alpha})
. If
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\psi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\psi}^{\ast}
, then

\xi_{\alpha}y=(T\Lambda_{\psi}(x_{\alpha}))y=T(\Lambda_{\psi}(x_{\alpha})y)=Tx_{\alpha}\Lambda_{\psi}^{\prime}(y).

Thus
\xi_{\alpha}
is left-bounded with
L_{\psi}(\xi_{\alpha})=Tx_{\alpha}
. Clearly,
Tx_{\alpha}\to T
in the strong operator topology.

If
\psi
is finite, we can take
x_{\alpha}=1
and get
L_{\psi}(T\Lambda_{\psi}(1))=T
.
∎

2.4.
Relative Tensor Product of Correspondences

There are three models of the relative tensor product of correspondences, which all come in handy in different situations. We describe here how they relate to each other.

Let
M
,
N
,
Q
be von Neumann algebras,
\mathcal{H}
a correspondence from
N
to
M
and
\mathcal{K}
a correspondence from
Q
to
N
. The relative tensor product
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
is the correspondence from
Q
to
M
with underlying Hilbert space obtained from the algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})\odot\mathcal{K}
after separation and completion with respect to the inner product

\langle S\otimes\xi,T\otimes\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K}}=\langle\xi,\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(S^{\ast}T)\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}.

Note that if
S,T\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})
, then
S^{\ast}T\in(N^{\prime})^{\prime}=N
so that this expression makes sense. We write
S\otimes_{N}\xi
for the image of
S\otimes\xi
in
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K}
. The left and right action are given by

x(S\otimes_{M}\xi)y=\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(x)S\otimes_{M}\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(y^{\mathrm{op}})\eta.

The relative tensor product can alternatively be described by the tensor product of the associated von Neumann bimodules in the sense of the previous subsection.

Lemma 2.2
.

The map

\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})\overline{\odot}_{N}\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(Q)_{Q},\mathcal{K}_{Q})\overline{\odot}_{Q}L^{2}(Q)\to\mathcal{H}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K},\,S\otimes_{N}T\otimes_{Q}\xi\mapsto S\otimes_{N}T\xi

is a unitary bimodule map.

Proof.

For
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})
,
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(Q)_{Q},\mathcal{K}_{Q})
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}(Q)
we have

\displaystyle\langle S_{1}\otimes_{N}T_{1}\xi_{1},S_{2}\otimes_{N}T_{2}\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{1}\xi_{1},\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(S_{1}^{\ast}S_{2})T_{2}\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},T_{1}^{\ast}\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(S_{1}^{\ast}S_{2})T_{2}\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},\langle S_{1}\otimes_{N}T_{1}|S_{2}\otimes_{N}T_{2}\rangle_{Q}\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{1}\otimes_{N}T_{1}\otimes_{Q}\xi_{1},S_{2}\otimes_{N}T_{2}\otimes_{Q}\xi_{2}\rangle.

Thus the map is isometric. Moreover, the set
\{T\xi\mid T\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(Q)_{Q},\mathcal{K}_{Q}),\,\xi\in L^{2}(Q)\}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}
by
[
Tak03
, Lemma IX.3.3]
. Hence the map has dense range. Again, the bimodule property is clear.
∎

A third description of the relative tensor product relies on the choice of a normal semi-finite faithful weight
\psi
on
N
. Let
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
be the Hilbert space obtained from
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{N},\psi)\odot\mathcal{K}
with respect to the inner product

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\eta_{1},\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi_{2}))\eta_{2}\rangle.

We write
\xi\otimes_{\psi}\eta
for the image of
\xi\otimes\eta
inside
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
. The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
becomes a correspondence from
Q
to
M
when endowed with the actions

x(\xi\otimes_{\psi}\eta)y=\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(x)\xi\otimes_{\psi}\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{K}}(y^{\mathrm{op}})\eta.

Lemma 2.3
.

The map

\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K},\,\xi\otimes_{\psi}\eta\mapsto L_{\varphi}(\xi)\otimes_{N}\eta

is a unitary bimodule map.

Proof.

For
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
left
\psi
-bounded and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
one has

\displaystyle\langle L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})\otimes_{N}\eta_{1},L_{\psi}(\xi_{2})\otimes_{N}\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{1},\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi_{2}))\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1}\otimes_{\psi}\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes_{\psi}\eta_{2}\rangle

Thus the map is isometric. As
\{L_{\psi}(\xi)\mid\xi\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{N},\psi)\}
is dense in
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},\mathcal{H}_{N})
with respect to the strong operator topology, the map also has dense range. The bimodule property is clear.
∎

One can also define the relative tensor product of a right and a left module map.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M
,
N
,
Q
be von Neumann algebras,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
correspondences from
N
to
M
and
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}
correspondences from
Q
to
N
. If
\Phi\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
is a bounded right module map and
\Psi\colon\mathcal{K}_{1}\to\mathcal{K}_{2}
is a bounded left module map, then there exists a unique bounded linear operator
\Phi\otimes_{N}\Psi\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes_{N}\mathcal{K}_{2}
such that

(\Phi\otimes_{N}\Psi)(S\otimes_{N}\xi)=\Phi S\otimes_{N}\Psi\xi

for all
S\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},(\mathcal{H}_{1})_{N})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Moreover,
\lVert\Phi\otimes_{N}\Psi\rVert\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert\lVert\Psi\rVert
and
(\Phi\otimes_{N}\Psi)^{\ast}=\Phi^{\ast}\otimes_{N}\Psi^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},(\mathcal{H}_{1})_{N})
and
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Since
\Phi\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
is a bounded right module map, one has
\Phi S_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},(\mathcal{H}_{2})_{N})
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

By definition of the relative tensor product and the left modularity of
\Psi
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Phi S_{k}\otimes_{N}\Psi\xi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\Psi\xi_{j},(S_{j}^{\ast}\Phi^{\ast}\Phi S_{k})\cdot\Psi\xi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\Psi\xi_{j},(S_{j}^{\ast}S_{k})\cdot\Psi\xi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lVert\Phi\rVert^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\Psi\xi_{j},\Psi((S_{j}^{\ast}S_{k})\cdot\xi_{k})\rangle_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\Psi\rVert^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\xi_{j},(S_{j}^{\ast}S_{k})\cdot\xi_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert\Phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\Psi\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}\otimes_{N}\xi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

Moreover, if
S_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(N)_{N},(\mathcal{H}_{i})_{N})
and
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, then

\displaystyle\langle\Phi S_{1}\otimes_{N}\Psi(\xi_{1}),S_{2}\otimes_{N}\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi(\xi_{1}),(S_{1}^{\ast}\Phi^{\ast}S_{2})\cdot\xi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},(S_{1}^{\ast}\Phi^{\ast}S_{2})\cdot\Psi^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{1}\otimes_{N}\xi_{1},\Phi^{\ast}S_{2}\otimes_{N}\Psi^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rangle.

Thus
(\Phi\otimes_{N}\Psi)^{\ast}=\Phi^{\ast}\otimes_{N}\Psi^{\ast}
.
∎

It is easy to see that in the other pictures of the relative tensor product, the relative tensor product of maps corresponds to
S\otimes_{M}T\otimes_{M}\xi\mapsto\Phi S\otimes_{M}\Psi T\otimes_{M}\xi
and
\xi\otimes_{\psi}\eta\mapsto\Phi(\xi)\otimes_{\psi}\Psi(\eta)
, respectively.

2.5.
Tomita Correspondences

Tomita correspondences were introduced in
[
Wir22
]
in the study of generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. Let us recall their definition.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
. A
Tomita correspondence
over
(M,\varphi)
is a triple
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
consisting of a correspondence
\mathcal{H}
from
M
to itself, an anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and a strongly continuous unitary group
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

•

\mathcal{J}(x\xi y)=y^{\ast}(\mathcal{J}\xi)x^{\ast}
for all
x,y\in M
,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
,

•

\mathcal{U}_{t}(x\xi y)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y)
for all
x,y\in M
,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
,

•

\mathcal{U}_{t}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In the context of this article, Tomita correspondences can be seen as bimodule versions of the data underlying Shlyakhtenko’s free Araki–Woods algebras and Hiai’s
q
-Araki–Woods algebras, namely a complex Hilbert space, an anti-unitary involution and a strongly continuous unitary group that commutes with the involution.

The primary class of examples of Tomita correspondences comes from inclusions of von Neumann algebras with expectation: If
M
is a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
and
M\subset\hat{M}
is a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras with a faithful normal expectation
E\colon\hat{M}\to M
, then
(L^{2}(\hat{M}),J_{\varphi\circ E},(\Delta_{\varphi\circ E}^{it}))
is Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
. More generally, the same is true if
E
is replaced by a normal semi-finite faithful operator-valued weight
[
Haa79
]
.

A consequence of the construction of the operator-valued free Araki–Woods algebras in
[
Wir22
, Section 4.2]
(called free Gaussian algebras there) is that in general Tomita correspondences are exactly those subbimodules of the Tomita correspondences from the previous paragraph that are invariant under the modular conjugation and modular group.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
. By Stone’s theorem, there exists a non-singular positive self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\mathcal{A}^{it}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We write
\mathcal{U}_{z}
for the (possibly unbounded) operator
\mathcal{A}^{iz}
with
z\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}_{-i}
.

By the spectral theorem,
\bigcap_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{z})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
. In fact, in the context of Tomita correspondences, more is true
[
Wir22
, Propositon 4.8]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
, then the set

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\left\{\xi\in\bigcap_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{z}):\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi\in L^{\infty}(_{M}\mathcal{H}_{M},\varphi)\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

3.
Tomita Correspondences over Type I Factors

As discussed in the introduction, the operator-valued free Araki–Woods factors we construct coincide with Shlyakhtenko’s free Araki–Woods algebras from
[
Shl97
]
in the case when the base algebra is
\mathbb{C}
. One key tool in the analysis of free Araki–Woods factors is the fact that the Hilbert space can be decomposed into a direct integral of one- and two-dimensional subspaces that are invariant under both the anti-unitary involution and the unitary group.

This decomposition does not immediately carry over to the operator-valued case as one will only get a direct integral of Hilbert spaces that does not respect the bimodule structure. This problem boils down to the fact that the unitary group in the definition of a Tomita correspondence does not consist of bimodule maps, but rather maps that respect the bimodule structure up to a twist by the modular group.

If the underlying von Neumann algebra is semi-finite, this twist can be corrected in a sense, giving rise to a direct integral decomposition of Tomita correspondences analogous to the scalar-valued case that additionally respects the bimodule structure. We combine this with the representation theory of type I factors to obtain a complete characterization of separable Tomita correspondences over type I factors.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra,
h
a non-singular positive self-adjoint operator affiliated with
M
and
\varphi=\tau(h^{1/2}\,\cdot\,h^{1/2})
. We have
\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}=h^{it}\,\cdot\,h^{-it}
and
f(h)
belongs to the centralizer
M^{\varphi}
for every bounded Borel function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Tomita correspondence from
(M,\varphi)
to itself. By definition,
\mathcal{U}_{t}\in\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(M^{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(M^{\varphi})^{\prime}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The following lemma is now immediate.

Lemma 3.1
.

The operators
\mathcal{V}_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, given by

\mathcal{V}_{t}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},\,\xi\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}\xi h^{it})

form a strongly continuous unitary group on
\mathcal{H}
.

By Stone’s theorem, there exists a unique self-adjoint operator
A
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{t}=e^{itA}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
one has
\mathds{1}_{-B}(A)=\mathcal{J}\mathds{1}_{B}(A)\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\mathcal{J}\mathcal{V}_{t}\mathcal{J}\xi=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}(\mathcal{J}\xi)h^{it})=\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}\xi h^{it})=\mathcal{V}_{t}\xi.

Thus
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{V}_{t}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{V}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Schwartz function. By the Fourier inversion formula one has

\displaystyle f(-A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(-t)e^{itA}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(-t)\mathcal{J}e^{itA}\mathcal{J}\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(t)e^{itA}\,dt\right)\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}f(A)\mathcal{J}.

Now the claim follows by approximation of
\mathds{1}_{B}
by Schwartz functions.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu(B)=0
if and only if
\mathds{1}_{B}(A)=0
and
\mu(B)=\mu(-B)
for all
B\subset\mathbb{R}
Borel.

Proof.

Let
(e_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{J}e_{j}=e_{j}
for all
j\geq 0
and define

\mu(B)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j}\langle e_{j},\mathds{1}_{B}(A)e_{j}\rangle.

The property
\mu(B)=0
if and only if
\mathds{1}_{B}(A)=0
is easy to see, while
\mu(B)=\mu(-B)
follows from the previous lemma.
∎

By the direct integral version of the spectral theorem
[
Hal13
, Theorem 10.9]
, the correspondence
\mathcal{H}
admits a direct integral decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
such that

\mathcal{V}_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}e^{i\omega t}\,d\mu(\omega).

In particular, the algebra of diagonal operators on
\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
is the von Neumann algebra generated by all operators of the form
f(A)
with
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
bounded Borel.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have
\mathcal{V}_{t}\in\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(M)^{\prime}\cap\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(M)^{\prime}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We only show the result for the left action. The proof for the right action is analogous. For
x\in M
,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\mathcal{V}_{t}(x\xi)=\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}x\xi h^{it})=\mathcal{U}_{t}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}(x)h^{-it}\xi h^{it})=x\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}\xi h^{it})=x\mathcal{V}_{t}\xi.\qed

By
[
Tak79
, Corollary IV.8.16]
it follows that the left and right action are decomposable representations in the sense that

\displaystyle\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)\,d\mu(\omega),

\displaystyle\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot)\,d\mu(\omega),

where for a.e.
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
the map
\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}
) is a normal unital
\ast
-homomorphism from
M
(resp.
M^{\mathrm{op}}
) to
B(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})
and the images of
\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}
and
\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}
commute . In other words,
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
gets the structure of a correspondence from
M
to itself and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)(\omega)=\mathcal{V}_{t}(\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{it})\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{it})\xi)(\omega)=e^{i\omega t}\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{it})\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{-it})\xi(\omega)

for every
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a.e.
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
decomposes as a direct integral

\mathcal{U}_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}e^{i\omega t}\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{it})\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(h^{-it})\,d\mu(\omega).

Thus, if we let
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}=\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-\omega}
for
\omega>0
, we get a direct integral decomposition

\mathcal{H}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}^{\oplus}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega),

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{r\in\mathbb{R}:r\geq 0\}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

With respect to the direct integral decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}^{\oplus}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
, the operator
\mathcal{J}
decomposes as
\mathcal{J}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}^{\oplus}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
such that for a.e.
\omega\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

•

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}
is an anti-unitary involution,

•

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}(x\xi y)=y^{\ast}(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}\xi)y^{\ast}
for all
x,y\in M
,
\xi\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
,

•

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})=\mathcal{H}_{-\omega}
.

Proof.

The algebra of diagonal operators with respect to the direct integral decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
coincides with the von Neumann algebra generated by all operators of the form
f(A)
with
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
an even Borel function. It follows from
Lemma

3.2
that
\mathcal{J}f(A)=f(A)\mathcal{J}
for every even Borel function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus
\mathcal{J}
is decomposable.

The first two bullet points follow from the corresponding properties of
\mathcal{J}
, while the last is a consequence of
\mathcal{J}A=-A\mathcal{J}
.
∎

The last property means that with respect to the direct sum decomposition
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}=\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-\omega}
for
\omega>0
, we can write
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}
as

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\omega}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{J}_{-\omega}\\
\mathcal{J}_{\omega}&0\end{pmatrix}

with mutually inverse anti-unitary maps
\mathcal{J}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{H}_{-\omega}
,
\mathcal{J}_{-\omega}\colon\mathcal{H}_{-\omega}\to\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
that satisfy

\mathcal{J}_{\omega}\pi_{l,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(x^{\ast})\pi_{r,\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(y^{\mathrm{op}})=\pi_{l,-\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(y^{\ast})\pi_{r,-\omega}^{\mathcal{H}}(x^{\mathrm{op}})\mathcal{J}_{\omega}.

Now we focus on the case when
M
is a type I factor. We first recall a well-known result on the structure of correspondences from
M
to itself.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
M
is a type I factor and
\mathcal{H}
is a correspondence from
M
to itself, then there exists a Hilbert space
H
such that
\mathcal{H}\cong L^{2}(M)\otimes H
with left and right action on the first tensor factor.

Proof.

Let
M=B(K)
and
F=\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M)_{M};\mathcal{H}_{M})
. Recall that the trivial bimodule
L^{2}(B(K))
can be identified with the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators on
K
. By
[
Ske01
, Example 3.3.4]
there exists a Hilbert space
H
such that
F\cong B(K,K\otimes H)
as von Neumann bimodules over
M
, where the bimodule structure on
B(K,K\otimes H)
is given by
x\cdot y\cdot z=(x\otimes 1)yz
for
x,z\in M
,
y\in B(K,K\otimes H)
.

It follows that

\mathcal{H}\cong F\overline{\odot}_{M}L^{2}(M)\cong\mathrm{HS}(K,K\otimes H)\cong\mathrm{HS}(K)\otimes H\cong L^{2}(M)\otimes H,

where
\mathrm{HS}
stands for the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators. Here the first isomorphisms follows from the connection between correspondences and von Neumann bimodules discussed in Section
2.3
, the second one is given by
x\otimes_{M}y\mapsto xy
, while the third and fourth are obvious.
∎

Therefore, if
M
is a type I factor,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
, then there exists a measurable field of Hilbert spaces
(H_{\omega})_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\cong L^{2}(M)\otimes H_{\omega}
as bimodules for a.e.
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Again, we will suppress the isomorphism and simply write
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}=L^{2}(M)\otimes H_{\omega}
. It follows that

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M)\otimes\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}H_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)

with left and right action on the first tensor factor and

\mathcal{U}_{t}=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}e^{i\omega t}\,d\mu(\omega).

Moreover, if we let
H=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{\oplus}H_{\omega}\,d\mu(\omega)
and

J\colon H\to H,\,(J\xi)(\omega)=\mathcal{J}_{-\omega}\xi(-\omega),

then
\mathcal{J}=J_{\varphi}\otimes J
.

To summarize, we have obtained the following characterization of separable Tomita correspondences over type I factors.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
M
is a type I factor,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
a separable Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
, then there exists a Hilbert space
H
, an anti-unitary involution
J\colon H\to H
, a strongly continuous unitary group
(U_{t})
on
H
satisfying
[J,U_{t}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a unitary bimodule map
V\colon\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M)\otimes H
such that
V\mathcal{J}=(J_{\varphi}\otimes J)V
and
V\mathcal{U}_{t}=(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes U_{t})V
.

4.
Braided Twists

In this section we discuss the twists we use to define our operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras. It is natural to assume that these twists are bimodule maps, which makes the class of twists more restricted compared to the scalar-valued case. This explains why there seem to be no natural fermionic and bosonic Fock bimodules (or more generally
q
-deformed Fock bimodules) without further assumptions on the underlying correspondence, as has been observed already in the mathematical physics literature
[
Ske98
,
Vas24
]
.

In our conditions on the twists, we do not aim for the highest generality, but rather for a convenient class of twists for which the modular data can be readily computed from the data of the Tomita correspondence and which includes several interesting families of examples.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a correspondence from
M
to itself. Given a bounded bimodule map
\mathcal{T}\colon\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
, we define the bounded bimodule maps
\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
and
R_{\mathcal{T},n}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{M}n}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k,n}

\displaystyle=1^{\otimes_{M}(k-1)}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{T}\otimes_{M}1^{\otimes_{M}(n-k-1)},

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},n}

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{T}_{1,n}+\mathcal{T}_{1,n}\mathcal{T}_{2,n}+\dots+\mathcal{T}_{1,n}\dots\mathcal{T}_{n-1,n}.

If
n
is irrelevant in the context, we simply write
\mathcal{T}_{k}
instead of
\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
for
n\geq k+1
.

Moreover, we define inductively,

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{T},1}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}

\displaystyle=(1\otimes_{M}P_{\mathcal{T},n})R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}.

Definition 4.1
.

A
twist
is a contractive bimodule map
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
such that
P_{\mathcal{T},n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. A
strict twist
is a twist such that
P_{\mathcal{T},n}
is additionally invertible for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A twist
\mathcal{T}
is called
braided
if it satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation

\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a self-adjoint contractive bimodule map on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
that satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation
\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a braided twist.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
BS94
, Theorem 2.2]
. Let
\pi_{i}\in S_{n}
denote the transposition between
i
and
i+1
and define a map
\Phi\colon S_{n}\to B(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{M}n})
by
\Phi(e)=1
and
\Phi(\sigma)=\mathcal{T}_{\pi_{i(1)}}\dots\mathcal{T}_{\pi_{i}(k)}
whenever
\sigma=\pi_{i(1)}\dots\pi_{i(k)}
is a reduced word. As explained in
[
BS94
, Theorem 1.1]
, this map is well-defined since
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation.

By
[
BS94
, Theorem 2.2]
the operator
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\Phi(\sigma)
is positive. Moreover,
P_{\mathcal{T},n}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\Phi(\sigma)
follows from the Coxeter decomposition of
S_{n}
as in the proof of
[
BS94
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

Given a twist
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
, we define a positive semi-definite sesquilinear form on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{M}n}
by

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{T},n}=\langle\xi,P_{\mathcal{T},n}\eta\rangle.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
denote the Hilbert space obtained from
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{M}n}
after separation and completion with respect to this inner product. Note that since
P_{\mathcal{T},n}
is a bimodule map,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
inherits the structure of a correspondence from
M
to itself from
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{M}n}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
twisted Fock bimodule

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
over
\mathcal{H}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})=L^{2}(M)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}.

In the case
\mathcal{T}=0
, this is the Hilbert completion of the Fock bimodule over the von Neumann bimodule
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M),\mathcal{H}_{M})
, which was introduced by Pimsner
[
Pim97
]
.

Remark 4.4
.

In the scalar-valued case, the most studied examples of twists in this context are the ones that produce the (mixed)
q
-Gaussian algebras. They all involve the flip map on
H\otimes H
. In the operator-valued case, a naive definition of the flip map fails: There is in general no bounded linear map on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
that maps
\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta
to
\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\xi
for all bounded vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, let alone a bimodule map.

This is one of the reasons why we work with general twists instead of trying to define an operator-valued version of (mixed)
q
-Gaussian algebras. Nevertheless, for specific
\mathcal{H}
one can still define an analog of the flip map. We will exhibit some instances of such correspondences in the examples below.

Example 4.5
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is a direct sum of copies of the trivial bimodule, say
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)
with left and right action
x(\xi\otimes e_{i})y=x\xi y\otimes e_{i}
for the canonical orthonormal basis
(e_{i})
of
\ell^{2}(I)
, we have
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}\cong L^{2}(M)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)
via

(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\otimes e_{i})\otimes_{\varphi}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)\otimes e_{j})\mapsto\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy)\otimes e_{i}\otimes e_{j}.

If
T
is a twist on
\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)
, let
\mathcal{T}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T
. If we identify
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
with
L^{2}(M)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)^{\otimes n}
analogous to the case
n=2
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{T}_{k,n}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T_{k,n}
and
P_{\mathcal{T},n}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes P_{T,n}
. Thus
\mathcal{T}
is a twist, and it is a strict twist if and only if
T
is a strict twist. Moreover,
\mathcal{T}
is braided if and only if
T
is braided.

In particular, if
q_{ij}\in[-1,1]
, we can take

T\colon\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)\to\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\ell^{2}(I),\,T(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})=q_{ij}e_{j}\otimes e_{i}.

This is the twist defining the mixed
q
-Gaussian algebras in the scalar-valued case.

Example 4.6
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space with anti-unitary involution
J
and let
\pi
be a unitary representation of a (discrete countable) group
G
on
H
such that
[\pi(g),J]=0
for all
g\in G
. In other words,
\pi
is the complexification of an orthogonal action of
G
on the real Hilbert space
H^{J}
.

One can turn
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H
into a correspondence
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
from
L(G)
to itself by defining the left and right action as

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}\cdot(\delta_{h}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=\delta_{gh}\otimes\pi(g)\xi,

\displaystyle(\delta_{h}\otimes\xi)\cdot\lambda_{g}

\displaystyle=\delta_{hg}\otimes\xi.

Inductively one can show that

V_{n}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\otimes_{\tau}n}\to\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H^{\otimes n},\,(\delta_{g_{1}}\otimes\xi_{1})\otimes_{\tau}\dots\otimes_{\tau}(\delta_{g_{n}}\otimes\xi_{n})\mapsto\delta_{g_{1}\dots g_{n}}\otimes\pi(g_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes\pi(g_{1}\dots g_{n-1})\xi_{n}

is unitary. Moreover,
V_{n}
becomes a bimodule map if
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H^{\otimes n}
is endowed with the left and right action given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}\cdot(\delta_{h}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\delta_{gh}\otimes\pi(g)\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\pi(g)\xi_{n},

\displaystyle(\delta_{h}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n})\cdot\lambda_{g}

\displaystyle=\delta_{hg}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n}.

Similar to the previous example, if
(e_{i})
is an orthonormal basis of
H
and
T
is the twist on
H\otimes H
given by
T(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})=q_{ij}e_{j}\otimes e_{i}
, then
1_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes T
is a twist on
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H\otimes H\cong\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\otimes_{\tau}2}
. More generally, instead of
T
we can take any twist
S
on
H\otimes H
that is equivariant in the sense that
[S,\pi(g)\otimes\pi(g)]=0
for all
g\in G
, and
1_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes S
is braided if
S
is braided.

Example 4.7
.

The center
\mathcal{Z}_{M}(F)
of a bimodule
F
over
M
is
\mathcal{Z}_{M}(F)=\{\xi\in F\mid x\xi=\xi x\text{ for all }x\in M\}
. A von Neumann bimodule
F
over
M
is called
centered
if
\mathcal{Z}_{M}(F)
generates
F
as a von Neumann bimodule (see
[
Ske01
, Section 3.4]
). We also call a correspondence
\mathcal{H}
from
M
to itself centered if the von Neumann bimodule
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M)_{M},\mathcal{H}_{M})
is centered. Note that the center of
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M)_{M},\mathcal{H}_{M})
is the set of all bounded bimodule maps from
L^{2}(M)
to
\mathcal{H}
.

By
[
Ske01
, Corollary 3.4.11]
, every centered correspondence from
M
to itself is of the form
\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}L^{2}(M)
with central projections
p_{i}\in M
. In particular, if
M
is a factor, then every centered correspondence from
M
to itself is a direct sum of copies of the trivial bimodule.

If
F
is a centered von Neumann bimodule, then there exists a unique unitary bimodule map
\sigma
on
F\overline{\odot}_{M}F
, called the
flip
, such that

\sigma(S\otimes T)=T\otimes S

for
S,T\in\mathcal{Z}_{M}(F)
(see
[
Ske01
, Proposition 8.1.1]
).

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a centered correspondence from
M
to itself and
F=\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M)_{M},\mathcal{H}_{M})
. In this case,
\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}(M)}
extends to a unitary bimodule map on
F\overline{\odot}_{M}F\overline{\odot}_{M}L^{2}(M)
. Under the canonical identification
F\overline{\odot}_{M}F\overline{\odot}_{M}L^{2}(M)\cong\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
discussed in Section
2.4
, one gets a unitary bimodule map
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{H}
, which we also call the flip.

The verification of the Yang–Baxter equation in this case follows along the same lines as in the scalar-valued case. Thus that
q\mathcal{T}
is a braided twist for
q\in[-1,1]
by
Lemma

4.2
. In the case
q=1
, the twisted Fock bimodule
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
coincides with the Hilbert completion of the symmetric Fock bimodule studied by Skeide (see
[
Ske98
]
,
[
Ske01
, Chapter 8]
).

In the case when
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
, we need some more compatibility conditions of the twist. In this case, it is more convenient to work with the weight-dependent formulation of the relative tensor product discussed in Section
2.4
.

With this formulation, it is not hard to see that despite they are not bimodule maps, both
\mathcal{J}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
can be extended to the relative tensor powers
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
. There exists a unique anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}^{(2)}
and a unique strongly continuous unitary group
(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)})
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{J}^{(2)}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)=\mathcal{J}\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\eta

for all left
\varphi
-bounded
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and right
\varphi
-bounded
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, and

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)=\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta

for all left
\varphi
-bounded
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
.

Moreover,
(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J}^{(2)},(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}))
is a Tomita correspondence.

Proof.

From the definition of a Tomita correspondence it is easy to see that
\mathcal{J}
maps left bounded to right bounded vectors, and
R_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\xi)=\mathcal{J}L_{\varphi}(\xi)\mathcal{J}
for left bounded
\xi
(see
[
Wir22
, Lemma 4.7]
). Moreover, since right bounded vectors are dense in
\mathcal{H}
by
[
Tak03
, Lemma IX.3.3]
, the linear span of vectors of the form
\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta
with
\xi
left bounded and
\eta
right bounded is dense in
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
.

For
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{M},\varphi)
,
\xi_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in L^{\infty}(_{M}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{J}\eta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\xi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{J}\xi_{j},L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\eta_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\eta_{k})\cdot\mathcal{J}\xi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{J}\xi_{j},\mathcal{J}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{j})^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})\mathcal{J}\cdot\mathcal{J}\xi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{J}\xi_{j},\mathcal{J}(\xi_{k}\cdot\mathcal{J}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{j})\mathcal{J})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\xi_{k}\cdot\mathcal{J}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\eta_{j})\mathcal{J},\xi_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

By
[
Tak03
, Proposition IX.3.15]
, the last line equals
\sum_{j,k}\langle\xi_{k}\otimes\eta_{k},\xi_{j}\otimes\eta_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. Thus
\mathcal{J}^{(2)}
is isometric.

For
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{j}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{j},L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{k})\cdot\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{j},\sigma_{\varphi}^{it}(L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{k}))\cdot\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\mathcal{U}_{t}\eta_{j},\mathcal{U}_{t}(L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{k})\cdot\eta_{k})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\eta_{j},L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{k})\cdot\eta_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}\otimes\eta_{k}\right\rVert^{2},

where we used the identity
L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(U_{t}\xi_{k})=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{k}))
from
[
Wir22
, Lemma 4.7]
for the second equality. Thus
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}
is isometric. The remaining claims are easy to check.
∎

As a consequence, we can inductively define
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(n)})
on the relative tensor power
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
such that
(\mathcal{H}^{(n)},\mathcal{J}^{(n)},(\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(n)}))
becomes a Tomita correspondence. If
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi\in\mathcal{H}
are bounded vectors, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(n)}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{n},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\xi_{n}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\xi_{1}.

For a twist
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
let
\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n}=1+\mathcal{T}_{n-1}+\mathcal{T}_{n-1}\mathcal{T}_{n-2}+\dots+\mathcal{T}_{n-1}\dots\mathcal{T}_{1}
. The following result is a consequence of the Coxeter representation of the symmetric group with transpositions as generators. The proof of
[
CL23
, Lemma 3.16]
carries over to our setting.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a braided twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
, then

P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}=(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1)\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

(a)

If
[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{J}^{(2)}]=0
, then
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\mathcal{T}_{k}=\mathcal{T}_{n-k}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\leq n-1
. In particular,
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n}=R_{\mathcal{T},n}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b)

If additionally
\mathcal{T}
is braided, then
[P_{\mathcal{T},n},\mathcal{J}^{(n)}]=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(a)

If
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
are bounded, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\mathcal{T}_{k}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{T}(\xi_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{k+1})\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\xi_{n}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}\mathcal{T}(\xi_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{k+1})\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\xi_{n}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{J}^{(2)}(\xi_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{k+1})\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{n-k}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n}).

Now the first claim follows from the density of bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}
. The second claim is then immediate from the definitions of
R_{\mathcal{T},n}
and
\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n}
.

(b)

We proceed by induction. Assume that the claim is true for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
are bounded, then

\displaystyle(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)

\displaystyle=(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})(\mathcal{J}\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\eta\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\xi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}P_{\mathcal{T},n}\xi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1)(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta).

Hence
(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}=\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1)
.

Now we deduce from (a) that

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1)\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is braided, the last expression coincides with
\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}
by
Lemma

4.9
.∎

The analogous result for the unitary group
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
is immediate.

Lemma 4.11
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a twist such that
[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
[P_{\mathcal{T},n},\mathcal{U}_{t}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 4.12
.

If
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence, we say that a braided twist
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
is
compatible
if
[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{J}^{(2)}]=0
and
[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a compatible twist, it follows from the previous two lemmas that
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(n)}
leave
\ker P_{\mathcal{T},n}
invariant and are isometric with respect to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathcal{T},n}
for all

. Thus they can be continuously extended to (anti-) unitary operators on

. It is then not hard to see that these extensions make

and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
into Tomita correspondences.

We write
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})
for the bounded operators on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
that act on

by

and

, respectively.

Let us discuss under which conditions the twists in the examples from the beginning of the section are compatible twists.

Example 4.13

As in Example

, where

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(2)}]

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{J}^{(2)}]

Example 4.14
.

As in Example

On

Example 4.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))

. We write

.

Let

By standard approximation techniques
[

,

is a strongly
∗
dense
\ast

, this implies that the linear span of

is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

With the canonical isomorphism between
F\overline{\odot}_{M}F\overline{\odot}L^{2}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
discussed in Section

, it is not hard to see that

\mathcal{T}(R_{1}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1})\otimes_{\varphi}R_{2}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{2}))=R_{2}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1})\otimes_{\varphi}R_{1}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1})

for

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(2)}\mathcal{T}(R_{1}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1})\otimes_{\varphi}R_{2}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x_{2}))

for all

5.
Operator-Valued Twisted Araki–Woods Algebras

In this section we introduce the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras as von Neumann algebras on the twisted Fock space generated by the left action of the base algebra and certain operator-valued analogs of the field operators. In the second part, we show that these operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras carry a natural “vacuum” weight and show how under a suitable condition on the twist, the associated modular data can be described in terms of the underlying Tomita correspondence.

5.1.
Twisted Creation and Annihilation Operators

We start with the definition of the creation operators. To define them in the twisted case, we need some operator inequalities, which will also be useful later for factoriality criteria in Section
7
. The proofs are essentially the same as in the scalar-valued case (see
[
BS94
,
Boż98
]
). In the following,
M
is a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
, then

c_{n}(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\leq P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\leq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k}\right)(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}),

where
c_{n}(q)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+q^{k})^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-q^{k})
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a braided twist, the inequalities remain true if
1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}
is replaced by
P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1
.

Proof.

The second inequality follows from the same arguments as the one used in
[
BS94
, Theorem 3.1]
in the scalar-valued case: Let
d_{n}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k}\right)
. Since
P_{\mathcal{T},n}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{2}=(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{\ast}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\leq\lVert R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\rVert^{2}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})^{2}.

By the definition of
R_{T,n+1}
and
Lemma

2.4
, we have
\lVert R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\rVert\leq d_{n}
. Thus
P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\leq d_{n}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})
.

For the first inequality, we follow the argument from
[
Boż98
, Theorem 6]
in the scalar-valued case. The inequality holds trivially when
\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert=1
since
P_{\mathcal{T},n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathrm{N}
by the definition of twist. Thus, we consider only the case when
\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert<1
. It follows by
Lemma

2.4
, the lines of
[
Boż98
, Lemma 4(b)]
that

R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-\mathcal{T}_{n}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{n-1}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{k})(1+\mathcal{T}_{n})\prod_{l=n}^{1}(1-\mathcal{T}_{n}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{l})^{-1},

which implies

R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{-1}=\prod_{l=1}^{n}(1-\mathcal{T}_{n}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{l})(1+\mathcal{T}_{n})^{-1}\prod_{k=n-1}^{1}(1-\mathcal{T}_{n}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{n-1}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{k})^{-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{-1}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k})(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{n-k+1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k})\prod_{k=2}^{n}(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k})^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1+\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k})\prod_{k=2}^{n}(1-\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k})^{-1}.

Hence,
\lVert R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{-1}\rVert\leq c_{n}(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1}
. This implies that
\lVert(R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{-1})^{\ast}(R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{-1})\rVert\leq(c_{n}(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1})^{2}
and hence
R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{\ast}\geq c_{n}(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{2}
. Now it follows from the previous calculation that

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{2}=(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}R_{\mathcal{T},n+1}^{\ast}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})\geq c_{n}(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{2}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}).

If
\mathcal{T}
is a braided twist, then
P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}=(P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1)\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}
by
Lemma

4.9
, and the proof of the inequalities with
1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}
replaced by
P_{\mathcal{T},n}\otimes_{\varphi}1
is analogous.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is left bounded and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
,then

\langle\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta,P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}(n+1)}}\leq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k}\right)\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert^{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T},n}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
d_{n}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k}\right)
. By
Lemma

5.1
we have
P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}\leq d_{n}(1\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n})
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta,P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}\langle\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta,\xi\otimes_{\varphi}P_{\mathcal{T},n}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}(n+1)}}

\displaystyle=d_{n}\langle\eta,L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi)\cdot P_{\mathcal{T},n}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}}

\displaystyle=d_{n}\langle P_{\mathcal{T},n}^{1/2}\eta,L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi)\cdot P_{\mathcal{T},n}^{1/2}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert^{2}\langle\eta,P_{\mathcal{T},n}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}}

\displaystyle=d_{n}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert^{2}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T},n}^{2},

where we used that
P_{\mathcal{T},n}
is positive and a bimodule map.
∎

As a consequence of the previous lemma, for every left bounded
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, the map

\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}/\ker P_{\mathcal{T},n}\to\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n+1}/\ker P_{\mathcal{T},n+1},\,\eta+\ker P_{\mathcal{T},n}\mapsto\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\eta+\ker P_{\mathcal{T},n+1}

extends to a bounded linear operator
a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)
from
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}
, called (twisted)
creation operator
. We further set
a_{\mathcal{T},0}^{\ast}(\xi)=L_{\varphi}(\xi)
as operator from
L^{2}(M)
to
\mathcal{H}
.

If
\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert<1
, then

\lVert a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)\rVert\leq\sqrt{d_{n}}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\leq\frac{\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert}{\sqrt{1-\lVert T\rVert}}

is bounded for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We define the (twisted)
annihilation operator
by
a_{\mathcal{T},n+1}(\xi)=a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)^{\ast}
and
a_{\mathcal{T},0}(\xi)=0
. If
\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert<1
, then the previous estimate shows that the twisted creation and annihilation operator can be extended to bounded operators on the twisted Fock bimodule, which we denote by
a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)
and
a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
, respectively.

In general however, as in the case of the scalar-valued Bosonic Fock space, one has to deal with unbounded operators. We show in the next lemma that one has unique self-adjoint realizations of the field operators even in the unbounded case.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every left-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
there exists a unique self-adjoint operator
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
such that
L^{2}(M)\subset\operatorname{dom}(s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))
,
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\eta=a_{\mathcal{T},0}^{\ast}(\xi)\eta
for
\eta\in L^{2}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}\subset\operatorname{dom}(s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\eta=(a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi))\eta
for
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
.

Proof.

For
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
left-bounded let
a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)
and
a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
denote the operators whose domain contains all finitely non-zero sequences in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
and which coincide with
a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)
and
a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)
, respectively, on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
. Since
a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)
and
a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
are mutually adjoint on their domain, the sum
s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)=a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
is symmetric and densely defined. We show that it is essentially self-adjoint using Nelson’s theorem on analytic vectors
[
Nel59
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}=L^{2}(M)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},j}
. By the previous lemma,

\displaystyle\lVert a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\leq\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert^{k}\right)^{1/2}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\leq(n+1)\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}

for all
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}
. Likewise,
\lVert a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\leq n\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}
for all
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}
.

If
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}
, then
s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k}\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n+k}
. As

\displaystyle\lVert s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k+1}\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=\lVert(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k}\eta\rVert\leq 2(n+k+1)\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k}\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}},

it follows by induction that

\lVert s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k}\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\leq\frac{2^{k}(n+k)!}{n!}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert^{k}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}.

Thus

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lVert s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)^{k}\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}}{k!}s^{k}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k}{n}(2s\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert)^{k}.

As
\binom{n+k}{n}\leq(n+k)^{n}
, this series converges for
\lvert s\rvert<(2\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert)^{-1}
. Hence
\eta
is an analytic vector for
s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)
. Since
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
, it follows from Nelson’s theorem that
s_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(\xi)
is essentially self-adjoint on
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},\leq n}
.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
and
\mathcal{T}
a compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. The operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the left action of
M
and the operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
for
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})
left bounded with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
.

If some of the operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
are unbounded, this von Neumann algebra is to be understood as the von Neumann algebra generated by the left action of
M
and resolvents of the operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
left-bounded with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
.

Remark 5.5
.

If one views
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{U}_{-i}
as analogs of the modular conjugation and modular operator in Tomita–Takesaki theory, then
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
is the analog of the Tomita operator
S
. Since
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
is an anti-linear involution, the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
of left-bounded vectors
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
is a real-linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}+i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\cap i\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}=\{0\}
. So the choice of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be seen as a bimodule analog of the choice of a real Hilbert subspace in the definition of scalar-valued twisted Araki–Woods and Gaussian algebras.

5.2.
Modular Theory

In this subsection we exhibit a natural weight on the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras that takes the role of the vacuum state in the scalar-valued theory (but it will only be a state if the reference state on the base algebra is a state). Then we show that the associated modular data can be described in terms of the underlying Tomita correspondence. For this to hold however, we need an additional condition on the twist, which we call locality. This condition ensures that the commutant of the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra on the twisted Fock bimodule also has a natural description in terms of the underlying Tomita correspondence.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compatible braided twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. Analogous to the construction of the left creation operators, we define for right bounded
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
the right creation operator
b_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)
that acts on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
by
b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)\eta=\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\xi
, and we write
b_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)
for the adjoint of
b_{\mathcal{T},n-1}^{\ast}(\xi)
. Taking
Lemma

4.9
into account, the boundedness on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
follows from similar computations as for the left creation operators. An analogous argument to the one given in Lemma
5.3
shows that there is a unique self-adjoint operator
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
that coincides with
b^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)+b_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is a braided compatible twist. If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is left bounded and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
, then
\mathcal{J}\xi
is right bounded and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\mathcal{J}\xi=\mathcal{J}\xi
and

\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)\mathcal{J}^{(n)}=b^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\mathcal{J}\xi).

Proof.

The part regarding left and right boundedness is
[
Wir22
, Lemma 4.7]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{z}=\mathcal{U}_{\bar{z}}\mathcal{J}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
follows from the definition of Tomita correspondence. Thus
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})
. This settles the first part.

Moreover, if
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
, then

\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\eta=\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}(\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\eta)=\eta\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{J}\xi=b^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\mathcal{J}\xi)\eta.\qed

For the next definition recall that two (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operators
x
and
y
are said to
commute strongly
if
f(x)
and
g(y)
commute for all bounded Borel functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. If
x
and
y
are bounded, this notion reduces to
xy=yx
.

Definition 5.7
.

We call a compatible braided twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}

local
if for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi
is left bounded and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
and
\eta
is right bounded and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\eta=\eta
, the operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
and
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)
commute strongly.

Remark 5.8
.

As shown in the proof of
Lemma

5.3
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the subspace
L^{2}(M)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},j}
of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
consists of analytic vectors for
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
for every left-bounded vector
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
. The same is true if one replaces
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
by
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)
for
\eta\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2})
right-bounded with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\eta=\eta
. Therefore, the operators
s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
and
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)
commute strongly if and only if
[a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi),b^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(\eta)+b_{\mathcal{T},n}(\eta)]=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by
[
Nel59
, Corollary 9.2]
.

Example 5.10
.

As in Example
4.13
let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M)\otimes\ell^{2}(I)
with left and right action

\mathcal{T}:=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T

We next verify that

A direct computation shows that
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
is the closure of the operator with domain
\operatorname{lin}\{\xi\otimes\eta\mid\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}),\,\eta\in\operatorname{dom}(U_{-i/2})\}
that acts as

Let
(e_{i})
be an orthonormal basis of the real Hilbert space
\ker(JU_{-i/2}-1)

\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi

\xi_{i}

\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})

self-adjoint such that

converges strongly.

An analogous argument shows that there exists an orthonormal basis
(f_{i})
of the real Hilbert space
\ker(JU_{i/2}-1)
with

such that every right-bounded vector

with
y_{i}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
self-adjoint such that

converges strongly.

For
\xi
and
\eta
as above we have

\displaystyle a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes s_{T,n}(e_{i}),

\displaystyle b_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi)+b_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}J_{\varphi}y_{i}J_{\varphi}\otimes d_{T,n}(f_{i}).

Since

for all

. Hence

\mathcal{T}
is local.

We now turn to the weight on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
induced by
\varphi
and its modular theory. We first note that
M\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is an inclusion with expectation.

Let
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
denote the inclusion map and

E_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))\to B(L^{2}(M)),\,x\mapsto\iota_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}x\iota_{\mathcal{T}}

Since
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}
is a bimodule map,
E_{\mathcal{T}}
maps bounded right module maps on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
to bounded right module maps on
L^{2}(M)
. In particular,
E_{\mathcal{T}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})))=M
and
E_{\mathcal{T}}
is a normal conditional expectation. Let
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}=\varphi\circ E_{\mathcal{T}}
on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
.

To show that
E_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
are faithful, we will use the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.13

(
[
Tak03
, Lemma IX.3.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a correspondence from M to itself. The map
L_{\varphi}
is a bijection from the set of left
\varphi
-bounded vectors in
\mathcal{K}
onto the set of all right module maps
T\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{K}
that satisfy
\varphi(T^{\ast}T)<\infty
.

Lemma 5.14

(
[
Wir22
, Lemma 4.14]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a correspondence from M to itself. If
\xi\in\mathcal{K}
is left
\varphi
-bounded, then
\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi))=\lVert\xi\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}.

Proposition 5.15
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a local braided compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
, then the map

\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}\colon n_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}),\,x\mapsto L_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})

is injective and has dense range. Moreover, if
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
, then

\langle\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x),\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(y)\rangle=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast}y)

and

\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(xy)=x\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(y).

In particular,
E_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
are faithful.

Proof.

First note that
x\iota
is a right module map from
L^{2}(M)
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
and by definition of
\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
we have

\varphi((x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})^{\ast}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}}))=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast}x)<\infty.

Thus
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}
is well-defined. Moreover,

\lVert\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\rVert^{2}=\varphi((x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})^{\ast}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}}))=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast}x).

By polarization, we obtain
\langle\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x),\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(y)\rangle=\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast}y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
.

Moreover, since
x
is a right module map,

(xL_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}}))m=x(L_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})m)=xy\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(m)

for all
m\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
. Thus
xL_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})
is left-bounded with
L_{\varphi}(xL_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}}))=xy\iota_{\mathcal{T}}
. Hence

x\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(y)=xL_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})=L_{\varphi}^{-1}(xy\iota_{\mathcal{T}})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(xy).

Injectivity and density of the range can be shown as in
[
Wir22
, Theorem 4.15]
.

If
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast}x)=0
, the
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=0
as shown above. Since
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}
is injective, it follows that
x=0
. Hence
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
and thus also
E_{\mathcal{T}}
are faithful.
∎

As a consequence, the map
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\mapsto\Lambda_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}(x)
extends to a unitary operator from
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
to
L^{2}((\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})),\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}})
. Thus
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
and the associated left Hilbert algebra is isomorphic to
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast})
with product and involution given by
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(y)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(xy)
and
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)^{\sharp}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x^{\ast})
.

We will next characterize the associated modular theory. To do so, it is convenient to introduce the dual objects corresponding to the right field operators. We continue to assume that
\mathcal{T}
is a local compatible braided twist.

Let
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be the von Neumann algebra generated by the right action of
M
and the operators
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\eta)
for
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
right-bounded with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\eta=\eta
. The locality assumption implies
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))^{\prime}
. Moreover,
E_{\mathcal{T}}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))=M^{\prime}
and
\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}=\varphi^{\prime}\circ E_{\mathcal{T}}
defines a normal semifinite weight on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
. Exactly as above one shows that
\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}
is faithful and the associated semi-cyclic representation is given by
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}),\,y\mapsto R_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})
.

Remark 5.16
.

If
\varphi
is a state and
\Omega\in L^{2}_{+}(M)\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
the cyclic vector representing
\varphi
, then
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=x\Omega
for
x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
and likewise
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y)=y\Omega
for

. Furthermore,
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}
are the restriction of the vector state
\langle\Omega,\cdot\,\Omega\rangle
to
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
, respectively. The conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{T}}
restricts to
x\mapsto L_{\varphi}(\Omega)^{\ast}xL_{\varphi}(\Omega)
on

Lemma 5.17
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is a local braided compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. If
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
and
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
, then

x\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y)=y\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x).

In particular,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y)
is a right-bounded vector for the left Hilbert algebra
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast})
and
\pi_{r}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y))=y
. Moreover,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast})\subset\operatorname{dom}(S^{\ast})
and
S^{\ast}\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y^{\ast})
for
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is semi-finite, there exists a net
(m_{\alpha}^{\prime})
of self-adjoint contractions in
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
such that
m_{\alpha}^{\prime}\to 1
strongly. Let

m_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t^{2}}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(m_{\alpha}^{\prime})\,dt.

Then
m_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\cap\operatorname{dom}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{z})
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
and

\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(m_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-z)^{2}}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(m_{\alpha}^{\prime})\,dt

for
z\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows from the dominated convergence theorem that
\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(m_{\alpha})\to 1
strongly for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Now let
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
,
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}
. If
n\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
, then

L_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})m_{\alpha}n=L_{\varphi}(L_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}}))\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(m_{\alpha}n)=x\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(m_{\alpha})\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(n).

Thus
L_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})m_{\alpha}=L_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(m_{\alpha}))=x\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(m_{\alpha})
. An analogous argument shows that
\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(m_{\alpha})R_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})=y\Lambda_{\varphi}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(m_{\alpha}))
.

As
\mathcal{T}
is assumed to be local, the operators
x
and
y
commute. Therefore,

\displaystyle y\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}yL_{\varphi}^{-1}(x\iota_{\mathcal{T}})m_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}yx\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(m_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}xy\Lambda_{\varphi}(\sigma_{-i/2}^{\varphi}(m_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}x\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(m_{\alpha})R_{\varphi}^{-1}(y\iota_{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle=x\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(y).

The remaining statements follow from standard theory of left Hilbert algebras.
∎

We are now in the position to characterize the modular theory associated with the weight
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
. For the proof recall that
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{\xi\in\bigcap_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{z}):\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi\text{ bounded for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}
, which is dense in
\mathcal{H}
by
Proposition

2.6
.

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a local braided compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. The modular operator
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}
and the modular conjugation
J_{\mathcal{T}}
associated with the left Hilbert algebra
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast})
satisfy

(a)

\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(b)

J_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})
.

Proof.

Since
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}=\varphi\circ E_{\mathcal{T}}
and
E_{\mathcal{T}}
is a faithful normal conditional expectation, we have
J_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=J_{\varphi}\xi
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\xi=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\xi
for
\xi\in L^{2}(M)
.

(a) We write
S_{\mathcal{T}}=J_{\mathcal{T}}\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{1/2}
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
and let
p_{R}(\xi)=\mathds{1}_{[-R,R]}(s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))
. Note that
p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\iota_{\mathcal{T}}=p_{R}(\xi)L_{\varphi}(\xi)
. Thus

\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}((p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))^{2})=\varphi(\lvert p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\rvert^{2})\leq\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi))=\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}

by
Lemma

5.14
.

As
p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
is self-adjoint, we have
p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{\ast}
. Moreover,

\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))=L_{\varphi}^{-1}(p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\iota_{\mathcal{T}})=p_{R}(\xi)\xi.

Hence
p_{R}(\xi)\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}})
and
S_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{R}(\xi)\xi)=p_{R}(\xi)\xi
. Since
S_{\mathcal{T}}
is closed, it follows that
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}})
and
S_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\xi
. For arbitrary
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
let
\eta=\frac{1}{2}(\xi+\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi)
and
\zeta=\frac{1}{2i}(\xi-\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi)
so that
\eta,\zeta\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\eta=\eta
,
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\zeta=\zeta
and
\xi=\eta+i\zeta
. By linearity,
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}})
and

S_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=S_{\mathcal{T}}\eta-iS_{\mathcal{T}}\zeta=\eta-i\zeta=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi.

Now let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi=\xi
and let
q_{R}(\xi)=\mathds{1}_{[-R,R]}(d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
. Arguing as above, we get

\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}((q_{R}(\xi)d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))^{2})=\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(\lvert q_{R}(\xi)d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\iota_{\mathcal{T}}\rvert^{2})\leq\varphi^{\prime}(R_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\xi))=\varphi(J_{\varphi}R_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\xi)J_{\varphi}).

By
[
Wir22
, Lemma 4.7]
, we have
\mathcal{J}R_{\varphi}(\xi)J_{\varphi}=L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\xi)
. Thus

\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}((q_{R}(\xi)d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))^{2})=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\xi))=\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}.

It follows that
q_{R}(\xi)d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\psi}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}}
. By
Lemma

5.17
, we have
q_{R}(\xi)\xi=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}(q_{R}(\xi)d_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))\in\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast})
and

S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}q_{R}(\xi)\xi=q_{R}(\xi)\xi,

and we conclude as above that
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast})
and
S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}\xi=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

If we combine these two facts, we obtain
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}S_{\mathcal{T}})
and
S_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}S_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\mathcal{U}_{-i}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. It follows by induction that
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha})
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{\alpha}\xi=\mathcal{U}_{-i\alpha}\xi
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
. Thus
z\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi
and
z\mapsto\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{iz}\xi
are analytic functions that coincide on
i\mathbb{Q}
. By the identity theorem for analytic function we obtain
\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi=\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{iz}\xi
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. As
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
is dense by
Proposition

2.6
, we conclude
\Delta^{it}_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
.

In particular, if
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
, then

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)))

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}(p_{R}(\xi)\xi)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi))\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\xi

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi))\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi))s_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)),

which implies
\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi)s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi))=\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi))s_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)
.

We prove by induction over
n
that
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\xi=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
. The base case
n=1
was settled in the previous paragraph. Assume that
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\xi=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},k}
,
k\leq n
. If
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}(s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}-a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}).

Since
a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n-1}
, we have by induction hypothesis

\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}.

Moreover, if we use the induction hypothesis on the first summand, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}(p_{R}(\xi_{1})s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi_{1})s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1}))\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sigma^{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}}_{t}(p_{R}(\xi_{1}))s_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=s_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1},

where we used in the last step that
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{(n+1)}a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\mathcal{U}_{-t}^{(n)}=a_{\mathcal{T},n}^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{1})
. If we reassemble the terms, we arrive at

\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1})=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}).

As the set
\{\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}\mid\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0},\,\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}\}
is total in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
, we conclude
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{it}\xi=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{t})\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n+1}
.

(b) It was shown in (a) that
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\operatorname{dom}(S_{\mathcal{T}})=\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{1/2})
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi
,
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{1/2}\xi=\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi
. Thus
J_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\mathcal{J}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. The same identity for arbitrary
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
follows by continuity of
\mathcal{J}
and density of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then

J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)=J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(y^{\ast})=\mathcal{J}(\xi y^{\ast})=y\mathcal{J}\xi=d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y),

where we used that
J_{\mathcal{T}}
coincides with
\mathcal{J}
on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
. Note that
J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}
is affiliated with the commutant of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
, and since
\mathcal{T}
is local, the same is true for
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)
. Thus

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(xy)

\displaystyle=J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}x\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)

\displaystyle=xJ_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)

\displaystyle=xd_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)x\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(xy).

As
\Lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}})
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
, we deduce
J_{\mathcal{T}}S_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\mathcal{T}}=d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi)
.

Now we proceed again by induction. Assume that
J_{\mathcal{T}}\xi=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},k}
,
k\leq n
, and let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}
. As above, we use that

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}-a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}.

The second summand is in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n-1}
, hence

J_{\mathcal{T}}(a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1})=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})a_{\mathcal{T},n}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

by induction hypothesis. Moreover,

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{T}}\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}.

By
Lemma

5.6
we have
d_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\xi_{1})\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)
. Hence

J_{\mathcal{T}}s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi_{1})\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}.

We have thus shown

J_{\mathcal{T}}\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n+1}.

As in (a), a density argument concludes the induction step.
∎

6.
Examples of Operator-Valued Twisted Araki–Woods Algebras

In this section we present some examples for which our operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras can be identified with known constructions of von Neumann algebras. In particular, we discuss the connection to the von Neumann algebras generated by operator-valued semicircular variables discussed by Shlyakhtenko (Subsection
6.1
), give a full description of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras over type I factors as tensor products of the base algebra with scalar-valued twisted Araki–Woods factors (Subsection
6.2
) and show that semidirect products of
q
-Gaussian algebras (or more generally twisted Araki–Woods algebras) by Gaussian actions naturally occur as operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras (Subsection
6.3
).

All these identifications hold not only on the level of von Neumann algebras, but on the level of operator-valued
W^{\ast}
-probability spaces. An operator-valued
W^{\ast}
-probability space is a pair
(M\subset N,E)
consisting of a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras
M\subset N
and a faithful normal conditional expectation
E\colon N\to M
. If
(M_{i}\subset N_{i},E_{i})
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, are operator-valued
W^{\ast}
-probability spaces, we say that
(M_{1}\subset N_{1},E_{1})
and
(M_{2}\subset N_{2},E_{2})
are isomorphic or write
(M_{1}\subset N_{1},E_{1})\cong(M_{2}\subset N_{2},E_{2})
if there exists a
\ast
-isomorphism
\alpha\colon N_{1}\to N_{2}
such that
\alpha\circ E_{1}=E_{2}\circ\alpha
. In particular, this implies
\alpha(M_{1})=M_{2}
.

6.1.
Connection to Shlyakhtenko’s von Neumann algebras Generated by Operator-Valued Semicircular Variables

Let us briefly review Shlyakhtenko’s construction from
[
Shl99
]
. Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and
I
a finite or countably infinite index set. A family
(\eta_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
of normal linear maps on
M
is called a
covariance matrix
if the map

\eta\colon M\to M\overline{\otimes}B(\ell^{2}(I)),\,x\mapsto\sum_{i,j\in I}\eta_{i,j}(x)\otimes E_{ij}

is normal and completely positive. Here
E_{ij}
denotes the canonical matrix units in
B(\ell^{2}(I))
defined by
E_{ij}\delta_{k}=\delta_{jk}\delta_{i}
.

If
\eta
is a covariance matrix, there exists a unique pair
(F,(\xi_{i})_{i\in I})
consisting of a von Neumann bimodule
F
over
M
and a family
(\xi_{i})_{i\in I}
in
F
generating
F
as von Neumann bimodule such that

\langle\xi_{i}|x\xi_{j}\rangle=\eta_{ij}(x)

for all
x\in M
,
i,j\in I
.

If
\varphi
is a faithful normal state on
M
, let
F_{\varphi}
be the completion of
F
with respect to the inner product

\langle\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle_{F_{\varphi}}=\varphi(\langle\zeta_{1}|\zeta_{2}\rangle).

Note that
F_{\varphi}
is canonically isomorphic to
F\overline{\odot}_{M}L^{2}(M,\varphi)
via the map
\xi\mapsto\xi\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}
, where
\Omega_{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{+}(M,\varphi)
is the cyclic and separating vector associated with
\varphi
. Through this isomorphism,
F_{\varphi}
becomes a correspondence from
M
to itself. Note that the right module structure on
F_{\varphi}
is not the one inherited from
F
. In the following, expressions of the form
\xi x
with
\xi\in F_{\varphi}
and
x\in M
always refer to the right module structure of
F_{\varphi}
as correspondence, so there should be no confusion.

Shlyakhtenko’s operator-valued semicircular operators with variance
\eta
are the operators
X_{i}^{\varphi}=a^{\ast}_{0}(\xi_{i})+a_{0}(\xi_{i})
on
\mathcal{F}_{0}(F_{\varphi})
. Let
\Phi(M,\eta)
denote the von Neumann algebra generated by
M
and
\{X^{\varphi}_{i}\mid i\in I\}
.

There is a normal conditional expectation
E
from
\Phi(M,\eta)
onto
M
given by
E(x)=\iota^{\ast}x\iota
, where
\iota\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
is the natural inclusion map. Without further assumptions,
E
may fail to be faithful. A necessary and sufficient condition for the faithfulness of
E
was given in
[
Shl99
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
E
is faithful, then there exists an anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}\colon F_{\varphi}\to F_{\varphi}
and a strongly continuous unitary group
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
on
F_{\varphi}
such that
(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
and
\Phi(M,\eta)=\Gamma_{0}(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
.

Proof.

Let
J
,
\Delta
denote the modular conjugation and modular operator associated with the faithful normal state
\varphi\circ E
on
\Phi(M,\eta)
. By
[
Shl99
, Lemma 5.1]
, both
J
and
\Delta^{it}
leave
F_{\varphi}
invariant and they restrict to
J_{\varphi}
and
\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}
on
L^{2}(M)
. Let
\mathcal{J}=J|_{F_{\varphi}}
,
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\Delta^{it}|_{F_{\varphi}}
. It is easy to see that
(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a Tomita correspondence.

Let
\Omega\in L^{2}_{+}(M)\subset\mathcal{F}_{0}(F_{\varphi})
denote the cyclic vector such that
\varphi=\langle\Omega,\cdot\,\Omega\rangle
. Note that

\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}=J\Delta^{1/2}X^{\varphi}_{i}\Omega=X^{\varphi}_{i}\Omega=\xi_{i}.

Therefore
\Phi(M,\eta)\subset\Gamma_{0}(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
.

For the converse inclusion we follow the argument from the proof of
[
Shl99
, Lemma 5.1]
. Let
K
be the real-linear span of all elements of the form
x^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)+y^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)
with
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2})
and
i\in I
and let
K^{\prime}
be the set of all left bounded vectors in
\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
.

By definition,
\xi_{i}\in K^{\prime}
. Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}(x^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)+y^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})y+\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})x)

\displaystyle=y^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)+x^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y),

we have
K\subset K^{\prime}
.

Note that

(x^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}y+y^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}x)\Omega=x^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}\Omega\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)+y^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}\Omega\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)=x^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)+y^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x)

and
s_{0}(\xi)\Omega=\xi
for
\xi\in K^{\prime}
. Since
\langle\Omega,\cdot\,\Omega\rangle
is a faithful normal state on
\Gamma_{0}(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
by
Proposition

5.15
, we deduce
x^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}y+y^{\ast}X_{i}^{\varphi}x=s_{0}(x^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}+y^{\ast}\xi_{i}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x))
.

As
\{\zeta\in K:\lVert L_{\varphi\circ E}(\zeta)\rVert\leq 1\}
spans
F_{\varphi}
as a complex Hilbert space, it follows that
\{\zeta\in K:\lVert L_{\varphi\circ E}(\zeta)\rVert\leq 1\}
is dense in
\{\zeta\in K^{\prime}:\lVert s(\zeta)\rVert\leq 1\}
. Hence the unit ball of
\Phi(M,\zeta)
is strongly dense in
\Gamma_{0}(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
. As they are both von Neumann algebras, we conclude
\Phi(M,\eta)=\Gamma_{0}(F_{\varphi},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

If
M
is a separable von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a faithful normal state on
M
and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a separable Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
, then there exists a countable set
I
and a covariance matrix
\eta\colon M\to M\overline{\otimes}B(\ell^{2}(I))
such that
(M\subset\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})),E_{0})\cong(M\subset\Phi(M,\eta),E)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is separable, we can choose a countable dense subset
\{\zeta_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of
\{\zeta\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2})\mid\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\zeta=\zeta,\lVert L_{\varphi}(\zeta)\rVert\leq 1\}
. Let

\eta_{ij}\colon M\to M,\,\eta_{ij}(x)=L_{\varphi}(\zeta_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(x\zeta_{j})

Clearly,
\eta
is a covariance matrix. Form the von Neumann bimodule
F
and the family
(\xi_{i})_{i\in I}
as in the definition of
\Phi(M,\eta)
. In particular,
\langle\xi_{i}|x\xi_{j}\rangle=L_{\varphi}(\zeta_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(x\zeta_{j})
. Since

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{i},x\xi_{j}y\rangle_{F_{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{i}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi},x\xi_{j}\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}y\rangle_{F\overline{\otimes}_{M}L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{\varphi},\langle\xi_{i}|x\xi_{j}\rangle\Omega_{\varphi}y\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\langle\xi_{i}|x\xi_{j}\rangle\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y))

\displaystyle=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\zeta_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(x\zeta_{j})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y))

\displaystyle=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\zeta_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(x\zeta_{j}y))

\displaystyle=\langle\zeta_{i},x\zeta_{j}y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

for
x\in M
,
y\in\operatorname{dom}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2})
and
i,j\in I
, there exists a unique unitary bimodule map
U\colon F_{\varphi}\to\mathcal{H}
such that
U\xi_{i}=\zeta_{i}
. Then it is not hard to see that
\mathcal{F}(U)X^{\varphi}_{i}\mathcal{F}(U)^{\ast}=s_{0}(\zeta_{i})
.

As
\mathcal{F}(U)
is a unitary bimodule map that leaves
L^{2}(M)
invariant, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}(U)\,\cdot\,\mathcal{F}(U)^{\ast}
is a
\ast
-isomorphism between
\Phi(M,\eta)
and
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
that leaves the copies of
M
invariant and intertwines the canonical conditional expectations.
∎

6.2.
Operator-Valued Twisted Araki–Woods Algebras over Type I Factors

In this section we give a complete description of operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras in the case when the base algebra is a type I factor (and the Tomita correspondence is separable). More precisely, we show that they decompose as a tensor product of the base algebra and a (scalar-valued) twisted Araki–Woods factor. In particular, in the light of the previous subsection, this yields a complete description of the von Neumann algebras generated by operator-valued semicircular variables over type I factors for which the canonical conditional expectation is faithful.

Theorem 6.3
.

If
M
is a type I factor,
\varphi
a normal semifinite faithful weight on
M
,
(H,\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
a separable Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
and
\mathcal{T}
a local braided compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
, then there exists a Hilbert space
H
, an anti-unitary involution
J
on
H
, a strongly continuous unitary group
(U_{t})
on
H
satisfying
[J,U_{t}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and a local braided compatible twist
T
on
H\otimes H
such that

(M\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})),E_{0})\cong(M\subset M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t})),\mathrm{id}\otimes\psi),

where
\psi
is the vacuum state on the scalar-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3.7
there exists a Hilbert space
H
, an anti-unitary involution
J\colon H\to H
, a strongly continuous unitary group
(U_{t})
on
H
satisfying
[J,U_{t}]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a unitary bimodule map
V\colon\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M)\otimes H
such that
V\mathcal{J}=(J_{\varphi}\otimes J)V
and
V\mathcal{U}_{t}=(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes U_{t})V
.

As discussed in
Example

4.5
, there is a canonical identification
(L^{2}(M)\otimes H)^{\otimes_{\varphi}2}\cong L^{2}(M)\otimes H\otimes H
, which we will tacitly make use in the following. The map
(V\otimes_{\varphi}V)\mathcal{T}(V^{\ast}\otimes_{\varphi}V^{\ast})
is a bounded bimodule map on
L^{2}(M)\otimes H\otimes H
, that is, it belongs to
(M\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{H\otimes H})^{\prime}\cap(M^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{C}1_{H\otimes H})^{\prime}
. Since
M
is a factor, this implies that there exists a bounded linear map
T
on
H\otimes H
such that
(V\otimes_{\varphi}V)\mathcal{T}(V^{\ast}\otimes_{\varphi}V^{\ast})=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T
.

A direct computation shows that
V^{\otimes_{k}\varphi}\mathcal{T}_{k}(V^{\ast})^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T_{k}
and
V^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}P_{\mathcal{T},n}(V^{\ast})^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes P_{T,n}
. Thus
T
is a twist, and a strict twist if
\mathcal{T}
is a strict twist. Moreover,
V^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
extends to a unitary bimodule map
V^{(n)}
between
H_{\mathcal{T},n}
and
L^{2}(M)\otimes H_{T,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We write
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)
for the unitary bimodule map between
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
and
L^{2}(M)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)
that restricts to the identity on
L^{2}(M)
and to
V^{(n)}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},n}
.

Furthemore,

1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T_{1}T_{2}T_{1}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{T}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)^{\ast}=1_{L^{2}(M)}\otimes T_{2}T_{1}T_{2}.

Hence
T
is braided. The compatibility of
T
follows directly from the tensor product decompositions of
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{J}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
.

To show that
T
is local, let
\xi,\eta\in H
and
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
and non-zero. A direct computation shows
a^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(V^{\ast}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\otimes\xi))=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)(x\otimes a^{\ast}_{T,n}(\xi))\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)^{\ast}
and likewise
b^{\ast}_{\mathcal{T},n}(V^{\ast}(\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)\otimes\eta))=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)(J_{\varphi}x^{\ast}J_{\varphi}\otimes b^{\ast}_{T,n}(\eta))\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)^{\ast}
. Hence the locality of
T
follows from the locality of
\mathcal{T}
, and the spatial isomorphism induced by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(V)
maps
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
onto
M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
and interwines the conditional expectations.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

If
M
is a type I factor,
I
a countable set and
\eta\colon M\to M\overline{\otimes}B(\ell^{2}(I))
a covariance matrix such that the canonical conditional expectation from
\Phi(M,\eta)
onto
M
is faithful, then
\Phi(M,\eta)
is isomorphic to a tensor product of
M
with a free Araki–Woods algebra.

Remark 6.5
.

By
[
Shl97
, Corollary 6.11]
, if
(U_{t})
is non-trivial, then
\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))
is a type III factor and in particular properly infinite. Thus
M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))\cong\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))
for every type I factor
M
. If
(U_{t})
is trivial, then
\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))\cong L(\mathbb{F}_{\dim H})
and
M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))
is an interpolated free group factor if
M
is finite.

6.3.
Gaussian Actions on Twisted Araki–Woods Algebras

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
J
an anti-unitary involution on
H
,
(U_{t})
a strongly continuous unitary group on
H
such that
[U_{t},J]=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
T
a local compatible braided twist on
H\otimes H
. We consider group actions on the twisted Araki–Woods algebra
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
that are induced by unitary actions on
H
compatible with
J
and
(U_{t})
.

More precisely, let
G
be a discrete group and
\pi
a unitary representation of
G
on
H
such that
[\pi(g),J]=0
for all
g\in G
. In other words,
\pi
is the complexification of an orthogoncal action of
G
on
H^{J}
. If
[\pi(g)\otimes\pi(g),T]=0
for all
g\in G
and
[\pi(g),U_{t}]=0
for all
g\in G
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\pi(g)^{\otimes n}
is a unitary operator on
H^{\otimes n}
that commutes with
P_{T,n}
. Hence it gives rise to a unitary operator
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\pi(g))
on
\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)
. The Gaussian action
\Gamma_{T}(\pi)
of
G
on the twisted Araki–Woods algebra
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
induced by
\pi
is given by

\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g))\colon B(\mathcal{F}_{T}(H))\to B(\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)),\,\Gamma_{T}(g)x=\mathcal{F}_{T}(\pi(g))x\mathcal{F}_{T}(\pi(g))^{\ast}.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
g\in G
and
x\in\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
, then
\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g))x\in\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
.

Proof.

Since
T
is local, it suffices to show that
\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g))(b_{T}^{\ast}(\eta)+b_{T}(\eta))\in\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))^{\prime}
for all
\eta\in\operatorname{dom}(U_{i/2})
with
JU_{i/2}\eta=\eta
. For
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in H
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g))(b_{T}^{\ast}(\eta))(\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{T}(\pi(g))(\pi(g)^{\ast}\xi_{1}\otimes\pi(g)^{\ast}\xi_{n}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes\pi(g)\eta

\displaystyle=b_{T}^{\ast}(\pi(g)\eta)(\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n}).

Hence
\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g))(b_{T}^{\ast}(\eta)+b_{T}(\eta))=b_{T}^{\ast}(\pi(g)\eta)+b_{T}(\pi(g)\eta)
. Since
\pi(g)
commutes with
J
and
(U_{t})
by assumption, we have
\pi(g)\eta\in\operatorname{dom}(U_{i/2})
and
JU_{i/2}\pi(g)\eta=\pi(g)\eta
. Thus
b_{T}^{\ast}(\pi(g)\eta)+b_{T}(\pi(g)\eta)\in\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))^{\prime}
.
∎

In the following we consider the correspondence
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
from
L(G)
to itself that was defined in
Example

4.6
. As discussed there, one can identify
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\otimes_{\tau}n}
with
\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H^{\otimes n}
, and we will tacitly do so in the following.

To fix notations, let us also briefly recall the definition of crossed products. If
N
is a von Neumann algebra,
\Gamma
a discrete group and
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{Aut}(N)
a group homomorphism, let

\alpha\colon N\to B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(N)),\,\alpha(x)(\delta_{\gamma}\otimes\eta)=\delta_{\gamma}\otimes\rho(\gamma^{-1})x\eta.

The crossed product
N\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
is the von Neumann algebra generated by
L(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{C}1
and
\alpha(N)
inside
B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(N))
.

If
\psi
is a faithful normal state on
N
that is invariant under
\rho
,
\Omega_{\psi}\in L^{2}_{+}(N)
the corresponding cyclic vector and

\iota_{\psi}\colon\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(N),\,\delta_{\gamma}\mapsto\delta_{\gamma}\otimes\Omega_{\psi},

then
E_{\psi}=\iota_{\psi}^{\ast}\,\cdot\,\iota_{\psi}
is a faithful normal conditional expectation from
N\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
onto
L(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
T
be an equivariant local compatible braided twist on
H\otimes H
. If
\mathcal{T}=1_{\ell^{2}(G))}\otimes T
,
\mathcal{U}_{t}=1_{\ell^{2}(G)}\otimes U_{t}
and
\psi
is the vacuum state on
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
, then the operator-valued
W^{\ast}
-probability spaces
(L(G)\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\mathcal{J}_{\pi},(\mathcal{U}_{t})),E)
and
(L(G)\subset\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))\rtimes_{\Gamma_{T}(\pi)}G,E_{\psi})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let

V_{n}\colon\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H_{T,n}\to\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H_{T,n},\,\delta_{g}\otimes\xi\mapsto\delta_{g}\otimes\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}\xi.

If
g,h\in G
and
\xi,\eta\in H^{\otimes n}
, then

\displaystyle\langle V_{n}(\delta_{g}\otimes\xi),V_{n}(\delta_{h}\otimes\eta)\rangle_{\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H_{T,n}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h}\langle\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}\xi,\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}\eta\rangle_{H_{T,n}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h}\langle\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}\xi,\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}P_{T,n}\eta\rangle_{H^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h}\langle\xi,P_{T,n}\eta\rangle_{H^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{g}\otimes\xi,\delta_{h}\otimes\eta\rangle_{\ell^{2}(G)\otimes H_{T,n}},

where we used that
\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}
commutes with
P_{T,n}
since
T
is equivariant. As the range of
V_{n}
is dense for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we conclude that
V_{n}
is unitary for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle V_{n}(\delta_{gh}\otimes\pi(g)^{\otimes n}\xi)=\delta_{gh}\otimes\pi(h^{-1})\xi=(\lambda_{g}\otimes 1)V_{n}(\delta_{h}\otimes\xi)

and

\displaystyle V_{n+1}((1\otimes a^{\ast}_{T}(\xi))(\delta_{g}\otimes\eta))

\displaystyle=V_{n+1}(\delta_{g}\otimes\xi\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=\delta_{g}\otimes\pi(g^{-1})\xi\otimes\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes n}\eta

\displaystyle=(1\otimes\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g^{-1}))a^{\ast}_{T}(\xi))(\delta_{g}\otimes\pi(g^{-1})^{\otimes}n\eta)

\displaystyle=(1\otimes\Gamma_{T}(\pi(g^{-1}))a^{\ast}_{T}(\xi))V_{n}(\delta_{g}\otimes\eta).

Moreover, if
\xi\in\operatorname{dom}(U_{-i/2})
, then

\mathcal{J}_{\pi}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)=\mathcal{J}_{\pi}(\delta_{e}\otimes U_{-i/2}\xi)=\delta_{e}\otimes JU_{-i/2}\xi.

Hence
\mathcal{J}_{\pi}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)=(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)
if and only if
JU_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
.

Let

V\colon\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(H)\to\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(H),\,V=1_{\ell^{2}(G)}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{n}.

Then
V
is unitary and the previous computations show

\displaystyle V(\lambda_{g}\cdot\xi)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{g}\otimes 1)V\xi

\displaystyle Vs_{\mathcal{T}}(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=1\otimes\alpha(s_{T}(\xi))V,

where
\alpha\colon\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))\to B(\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}(H))
is the representation in the definition of the crossed product. Thus
V\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\mathcal{J}_{\pi},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))V^{\ast}=\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))\rtimes_{\Gamma_{T}(\pi)}G
and
VL(G)V^{\ast}=L(G)\otimes\mathbb{C}1
. Furthermore,
V\circ\iota_{\mathcal{T}}=\iota_{\psi}
, which implies
E(\cdot)=E_{\psi}(V\,\cdot\,V^{\ast})
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In the free case
T=0
, the structure of the crossed product
\Gamma_{0}(H,J,(U_{t}))\rtimes_{\Gamma(\pi)}G
has been studied in
[
HT21
]
. In particular, the authors characterize factoriality, determine the type classification and give sufficient criteria for fullness and strong solidity for these crossed product algebras.

7.
Factoriality

In this section we study when the operator-valued Araki–Woods algebras are factors. In contrast to the scalar-valued case,
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
can fail to be a factor even for strict twists – for example, if
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
\mathcal{J}=J_{\varphi}\otimes J
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes 1
, then it is not hard to see that
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))\cong M\overline{\otimes}L(\mathbb{F}_{d})
(compare with the proof of
Theorem

6.3
), which is only a factor if
M
is a factor. However, there are also interesting cases when
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a factor even though
M
is not (see
[
Shl99
, Section 3]
or
Example

7.4
below). This already suggests that the factoriality question is more subtle than in the scalar-valued case.

Here we give two sufficient criteria for factoriality. The first is based on mixing properties of the Tomita correspondence, while the second one relies on a suitable notion of the Tomita correspondence having sufficiently large dimension as bimodule.

We start with the mixing property. Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Following the terminology used for example in
[
BBM19
]
, a correspondence
\mathcal{H}
over
M
is called
jointly mixing
if
\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{H}}(x_{n})\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}(y_{n})\to 0
in the weak
∗
topology whenever
(x_{n})
,
(y_{n})
are bounded sequences in
M
such that
x_{n}\to 0
or
y_{n}\to 0
in the weak
∗
topology.

Example 7.1
.

If
\pi
is a normal representation of
M
on a Hilbert space
H
, then
\mathrm{HS}(H)
with the left and right action given by
x\cdot R\cdot y=\pi(x)R\pi(y)
is a jointly mixing correspondence from
M
to itself. In particular, the coarse bimodule
L^{2}(M)\otimes L^{2}(M)
is jointly mixing.

Note that if
(\pi,H,(U_{t}))
is a covariant representation of
(M,\sigma^{\varphi}_{t})
, that is,
U_{t}\pi(x)U_{-t}=\pi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x))
, then one can turn
\mathrm{HS}(H)
into a Tomita correspondence by defining
\mathcal{J}(R)=R^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}(R)=U_{t}RU_{-t}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is a jointly mixing Tomita correspondence, then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},k}
is jointly mixing for all
k\geq 1
and all strict twists
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

We first prove that the relative tensor product
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{K}
is jointly mixing if
\mathcal{K}
is jointly mixing. Let
(x_{n})
,
(y_{n})
be bounded sequences in
M
such that one of them converges to
0
in the weak
∗
topology.

Since the sequences are bounded, it suffices to show that

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{1},x_{n}\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{2}y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{K}}\to 0

for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
left
\varphi
-bounded and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
.

We have

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{1},x_{n}\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{2}y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{K}}=\langle\eta_{1},\pi_{l}^{\mathcal{K}}(L_{\varphi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}x_{n}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{2}))\pi_{r}^{\mathcal{K}}(y_{n})\eta_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}.

By assumption,
L_{\varphi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}x_{n}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{2})\to 0
or
y_{n}\to 0
in the weak
∗
topology. As
\mathcal{K}
is jointly mixing, it follows that
\langle\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{1},x_{n}\xi_{2}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{2}y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{K}}\to 0
.

It follows by induction that
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
is jointly mixing for all
k\geq 1
. To prove that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T},k}
is jointly mixing, let again
(x_{n})
,
(y_{n})
be bounded sequences in
M
such that one of them converges to
0
in the weak
∗
topology. Again, by a density argument it suffices to show that

\langle\xi,x_{n}\eta y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{T},k}\to 0

for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{T},k}
is a bimodule map, we have
\langle\xi,x_{n}\eta y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{T},k}=\langle\xi,x_{n}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{T},k}\eta)y_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}}
, and the claimed convergence follows immediately from the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}k}
is jointly mixing.
∎

The following result is an adaptation of
[
KV19
, Theorem 5.1]
, where the result was proved under the assumption that
\varphi
is a trace,
\mathcal{T}=0
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
(with a slightly different mixing condition). We call an element of
M

diffuse
if it is self-adjoint and its spectral measure has no atoms.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be a jointly mixing Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
with
\varphi
finite and let
\mathcal{T}
be a local compatible braided strict twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. If the centralizer
M^{\varphi}
of
\varphi
contains a diffuse element, then

\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})))=\{z\in\mathcal{Z}(M)\mid z\xi=\xi z\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{H}\}.

In particular, if
M
is a factor, so is
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
.

Proof.

If
x\in M^{\varphi}
is diffuse, then there exists a sequence
(u_{n})
of unitaries in
\{x\}^{\prime\prime}
that converges to
0
in the weak
∗
topology. Let
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})))
and let
\Omega\in L^{2}_{+}(M)
denote the cyclic and separating vector representing
\varphi
. We have

(z-E_{\mathcal{T}}(z))\Omega=(u_{n}zu_{n}^{\ast}-E_{\mathcal{T}}(u_{n}zu_{n}^{\ast}))\Omega=u_{n}(z-E_{\mathcal{T}}(z))u_{n}^{\ast}\Omega=u_{n}(z-E_{\mathcal{T}}(z))\Omega u_{n}^{\ast}.

Note that
(z-E_{\mathcal{T}}(z))\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
. It follows from
Lemma

7.2
that
u_{n}(z-E(z))\Omega u_{n}^{\ast}\to 0
weakly. Thus
z=E_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\in M\cap M^{\prime}
.

Moreover,
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma_{M}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
implies

z\xi=zs_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\Omega=s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)z\Omega=\xi z

for all bounded vectors
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. As bounded vectors are dense in
\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
z\xi=\xi z
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
.

Conversely, if
x\in\mathcal{Z}(M)
and
x\xi=\xi x
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, then

xs_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)\Omega=x\xi=\xi x=s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)J_{\varphi}x^{\ast}\Omega=s_{\mathcal{T}}(\xi)x\Omega

for all left-bounded vectors
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi=\xi
. Therefore,
x\in\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})))
.
∎

Example 7.4
.

Let
(\pi,H,(U_{t}))
be a normal faithful covariant representation of
(M,\sigma^{\varphi})
and let
(\mathrm{HS}(H),\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))

. This correspondence is jointly mixing. Moreover, if

.

For the second factoriality criterion we need the operator inequalities from
Lemma

5.1
. For the ease of notation, we write

c(q)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{k}}{1+q^{k}},\quad d(q)=(1-q)^{-1}

for

. Note that these are the limits as

.

The following result is a direct consequence of

.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
M

\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on

\mathcal{H}
a correspondence from

\mathcal{T}

Proposition 7.6

(Spectral Gap)
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
be a Tomita correspondence over
(M,\varphi)
and assume there exists a finite family
(\xi_{i})_{i\in I}
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
such that

•

\mathcal{J}\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}
for all
i\in I
,

•

L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})=\delta_{ij}1
for all
i,j\in I
,

•

there exists a family
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
of projections in
M
such that
L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{j})=\delta_{ij}p_{i}
for all
i,j\in I
.

There exists a function
f\colon[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
such that whenever
\mathcal{T}
is a local braided compatible twist on
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
with
\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert<1
and
\lvert I\rvert>f(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)
, then there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{i}-x\xi_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}\geq\kappa^{2}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(\lvert x-E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\rvert^{2})

for all
x
in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof follows
[
Śni04
]
. First note that the assumptions imply
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}
,
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}
and
R_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})=\delta_{ij}1
,
R_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{j})=\delta_{ij}J_{\varphi}p_{i}J_{\varphi}
.

Define

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\to\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}),\,S\eta=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes(a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})-b_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}))\eta,

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\to\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}),\,T\eta=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})-b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i}))\eta,

where
(e_{i})
is the canonical orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(I)
.

Step 1:
We first show that
\lVert S\rVert\leq 2d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
.

For that purpose let
S_{l}\eta=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})\eta
and
S_{r}\eta=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes b_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})\eta
. With

\Pi_{i}\colon\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H}),\,e_{j}\otimes\eta\mapsto\delta_{ij}\eta

we have
S_{l}^{\ast}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})\Pi_{i}
and
S_{r}^{\ast}=\sum_{i\in I}b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})\Pi_{i}
.

From
Lemma

5.1
we deduce

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{l}^{\ast}\left(\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})\eta_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle=d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)\sum_{i,k\in I}\langle\eta_{i},L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{k})\eta_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)\sum_{i\in I}\lVert p_{i}\eta_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}.

It follows that
\lVert S_{l}\rVert\leq d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
. An analogous calculation shows
\lVert S_{r}\rVert\leq d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
. Thus
\lVert S\rVert\leq 2d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
.

Step 2:
We prove that
\lvert T\rvert\geq\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}-\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
.

For
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}\in\mathcal{H}
left-bounded and
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
let

\displaystyle X\sum_{k=1}^{m}\eta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\zeta_{k}=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=1}^{m}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})\zeta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{i}.

We have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=1}^{m}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})\zeta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{m}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})\zeta_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{m}\left\langle\zeta_{k},L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})^{\ast}\sum_{i\in I}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{l})\zeta_{l}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}

Since

\left(\sum_{i\in I}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i,j\in I}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{j})^{\ast}=\sum_{i\in I}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast},

we have
\sum_{i\in I}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}\leq 1
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=1}^{m}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})\zeta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k,l=1}^{m}\left\langle\zeta_{k},L_{\varphi}(\eta_{k})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta_{l})\zeta_{l}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\eta_{k}\otimes_{\varphi}\zeta_{k},\sum_{l=1}^{m}\eta_{l}\otimes_{\varphi}\zeta_{l}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}.

Thus
X
extends to a contraction from
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{H}
. It follows from
Lemma

7.5
that
\tilde{X}=j_{r}Xj_{l}^{-1}
is a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
with
\lVert\tilde{X}\rVert\leq c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1/2}d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
.

For
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
let

Y\eta=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\otimes\xi_{i}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta.

We have

\displaystyle\lVert Y\eta\rVert_{\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}=\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\xi_{i}\otimes_{\varphi}\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}=\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}=\lvert I\rvert\lVert\eta\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}.

Thus
Y
is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
to
\ell^{2}(I)\otimes\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
with norm
\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}
.
Therefore, another application of
Lemma

7.5
shows that
\tilde{Z}=Y^{\ast}(1\otimes j_{l}^{-1})

defines a bounded linear operator from
\ell^{2}(I)\otimes(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M))
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})
with norm
\lVert\tilde{Z}\rVert\leq\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1/2}
.

A direct computation shows that
\tilde{Z}T=\lvert I\rvert-\tilde{X}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
. Hence, whenever
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
, then

\displaystyle\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1/2}\lVert T\eta\rVert\geq\lVert\tilde{Z}T\eta\rVert=\lVert(\lvert I\rvert-\tilde{X})\eta\rVert\geq(\lvert I\rvert-c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{-1/2}d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2})\lVert\eta\rVert.

Therefore,

\lvert T\rvert\geq c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}-d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}.

Step 3:
We show that
\lvert S+T\rvert\geq c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}-d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}-2d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
.

If
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})\ominus L^{2}(M)
, then the inequalities from Step 1 and Step 2 combined imply

\lVert(S+T)\eta\rVert\geq\lVert T\eta\rVert-\lVert S\eta\rVert\geq(c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}-d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2})\lVert\eta\rVert-2d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lVert\eta\rVert.

Step 4:
Let
\kappa=c(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}-d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}-2d(\lVert\mathcal{T}\rVert)^{1/2}
. We show that if
x
is in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\kappa\geq 0
, then

\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{i}-x\xi_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}\geq\kappa^{2}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}(\lvert x-E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\rvert^{2}).

Since
x
is in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
E_{\mathcal{T}}
is a weight-preserving conditional expectation,
E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)
is also in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
. Hence we can assume without loss of generality
E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=0
. Moreover, the assumption
L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})=1
implies

\varphi(1)=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i})^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi_{i}))=\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}<\infty

by
Lemma

5.14
. Let
\Omega\in L^{2}_{+}(M)
denote the cyclic and separating vector representing
\varphi
. Since
x
is in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
, it follows from
Theorem

5.18
that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\Omega=x\Omega
.

Now we use
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})
commutes with
a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})
and
x
commutes with
b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+b_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i})
for all
i\in I
to deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i})\Omega

\displaystyle=(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}))\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\Omega

\displaystyle=(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi_{i}))x\Omega

and

x\xi_{i}=x(b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+b_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}))(\xi_{i})\Omega=(b_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi_{i})+b_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi_{i}))x\Omega.

Therefore,

\sum_{i\in I}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi_{i}-x\xi_{i}\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2}=\lVert(S+T)x\Omega\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H})}^{2},

and
x\Omega\in L^{2}(M)^{\perp}
because
E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)=0
. Now the claim follows from Step 3.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Under the assumptions of
Proposition

7.6
, the center of the operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebra is given by

\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t})))=\{x\in\mathcal{Z}(M)\mid\xi x=x\xi\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{H}\}.

In particular, if
M
is a factor, then
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
is a factor.

Proof.

If
x
is in the center of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
, then
x
is in the centralizer of
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathcal{T}}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle x\xi

\displaystyle=x(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi))\Omega

\displaystyle=(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi))x\Omega

\displaystyle=(a_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(\xi)+a_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi))\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\Omega

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})x^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{J})\xi.

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Thus
x=E_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\in M
by

7.6
. Hence
x\in\mathcal{Z}(M)
and
x\xi=\xi x
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
follows from the computation above by the density of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

The converse inclusion can be shown exactly as in the proof of

7.3
.
∎

8.
Application to Quantum Markov Semigroups

Tomita correspondences occur naturally in the study of generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups and their relation to derivations
[
Wir22
,
Wir24a
]
. Operator-valued twisted Araki–Woods algebras enter the picture if one wants to obtain derivations with values in a von Neumann superalgebra instead of a bimodule.

In
[
JRS
]
in the tracial case and in
[
Wir22
, Section 7]
in the general case, operator-valued free Araki–Woods algebras were employed for this purpose. One disadvantage is that they are always infinite-dimensional (unless the Tomita correspondence is trivial), even if the base algebra is finite-dimensional. In this section we used a twisted algebra to show that one can always choose the von Neumann superalgebra to be finite-dimensional if the base algebra is a matrix algebra.

Moreover, we use the disintegration theory from
Section

3
to introduce the notion of Bohr spectrum of a quantum Markov semigroup on a semi-finite von Neumann algebra in a way that it coincides with the set of Bohr frequencies occurring in Alicki’s theoremm for quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras.

Let us briefly recall the relevant terminology regarding quantum Markov semigroups. A
quantum Markov semigroup
on a von Neumann algebra
M
is a family
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of normal unital completely positive maps on
M
such that
P_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
,
P_{s}P_{t}=P_{s+t}
for all
s,t\geq 0
and
P_{t}\to 0
as
t\to 0
in the point-weak
∗
topology.

If
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
M
, then the quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
is called
GNS-symmetric with respect to
\varphi
if
\varphi\circ P_{t}\leq\varphi
and

\varphi(P_{t}(x)^{\ast}y)=\varphi(x^{\ast}P_{t}(y))

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
,
t\geq 0
.

A GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
gives rise to a strongly continuous symmetric contraction semigroup
(T_{t})
on
L^{2}(M)
characterized by
T_{t}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))=\Lambda_{\varphi}(P_{t}(x))
for
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
,
t\geq 0
. By semigroup theory,
(T_{t})
is of the form
T_{t}=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
for some positive self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}(M)
. By
[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.3]
, the set

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{L}}=\left\{x\in\bigcap_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{dom}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}):\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast},\,\Lambda_{\varphi}(\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x))\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{L}^{1/2})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

is a weak
∗
dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
M
such that
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{L}})
is a core for
\mathcal{L}^{1/2}
.

Following the terminology introduced in the tracial case by Junge, Li and LaRacuente in
[
LJL20
]
(see also
[
BGJ22
]
), a
derivation triple
for
(P_{t})
is a a triple
(M\subset\hat{M},E,\delta)
consisting of

•

a unital inclusion
M\subset\hat{M}
of von Neumann algebras,

•

a faithful normal conditional expectation
E\colon\hat{M}\to M
, and

•

a closed operator
\delta\colon\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{L}^{1/2})\to L^{2}(\hat{M})
satisfying
\delta\circ J_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi\circ E}\circ\delta
,
\delta\circ\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}=\Delta_{\hat{\varphi}}^{it}\circ\delta
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)y)=\delta(x\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(y))=x\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(y))+\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))y

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{L}}

such that
\mathcal{L}=\delta^{\ast}\delta
.

It was shown in
[
Wir22
, Corollary 7.6]
that a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup admits a derivation triple if and only if it satisfies a property called
\Gamma
-regularity. Every GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on a finite-dimensional von Neumann algebra or more generally every GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup with bounded generator is
\Gamma
-regular.

Proposition 8.1
.

If
M
is a type I factor,
\varphi
a normal semifinite faithful weight on
M
and
(P_{t})
a quantum Markov semigroup that is GNS-symmetric with respect to
\varphi
and
\Gamma
-regular, then
(P_{t})
admits a derivation triple
(M\subset\hat{M},E,\delta)
with
\hat{M}=M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
and
E=\mathrm{id}\otimes\psi
, where
\psi
is the vacuum state on
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
. Moreover, if
M
is finite, then
H
can be chosen finite-dimensional.

Proof.

Let
(M\subset N,F,\partial)
be any derivation triple for
(P_{t})
and let
\mathcal{H}
be the closed linear hull of
\{x\partial(a)y\mid x,y\in M,\,a\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{L}^{1/2})\}
. By definition of a derivation triple,
J_{\varphi\circ F}
and
\Delta^{it}_{\varphi\circ F}
leave
\mathcal{H}
invariant, making it into a Tomita correspondence
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
over
(M,\varphi)
. Moreover, if
M
is finite-dimensional, so is
\mathcal{H}
.

By
Theorem

3.7
we can write
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M)\otimes H
,
\mathcal{J}=J_{\varphi}\otimes J
,
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes U_{t}
. Taking
\delta=\partial
viewed as map into
L^{2}(M\overline{\otimes}\Gamma_{\mathcal{T}}(H,J,(U_{t}))
and
\hat{M}
,
E
as in the statement of the theorem does the job.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Every GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
admits a derivation triple
(M\subset\hat{M},E,\delta)
with
\hat{M}
finite-dimensional.

Proof.

If one takes
T
to be minus the flip map in the previous theorem, then
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
is contained in the bounded linear operators on the fermionic Fock space over
H
. In particular, if
H
is finite-dimensional, so is
\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
and then
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\Gamma_{T}(H,J,(U_{t}))
is also finite dimensional.
∎

As the previous results show, derivation triples for a given quantum Markov semigroup are not unique, even if one makes the natural minimality assumption that the range of
\delta
generate
L^{2}(\hat{M})
as a correspondence. What is unique (up to isomorphism) however is the Tomita correspondence
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
generated by the range of
\delta
with the restriction of the modular data from
\hat{M}

[
Wir22
, Theorem 6.9]
.

In particular, if
M
is semi-finite with normal faithful trace
\tau
and
\varphi=\tau(h^{1/2}\,\cdot\,h^{1/2})
, then the spectrum of
\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}\,\cdot\,h^{it})
is an invariant of
(P_{t})
. This leads us to the following definition.

Definition 8.3
.

If
(P_{t})
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup and
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
the associated Tomita correspondence, then the
Bohr spectrum
of
(P_{t})
is the set of all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
for which the operator
\mathcal{U}_{t}(h^{-it}\,\cdot\,h^{it})-e^{i\omega t}
on
\mathcal{H}
is not invertible for some (or all)
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

To give an interpretation of the Bohr spectrum, we show how it is related to the Bohr frequencies occurring in Alicki’s theorem on generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 8.4
.

If
h\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is positive definite and
P_{t}=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
with
\mathcal{L}
given by

\mathcal{L}\colon M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\,\mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{-\omega_{j}/2}(v_{j}^{\ast}[v_{j},x]-[v_{j}^{\ast},x]v_{j})

with
v_{j}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
non-zero and
\omega_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\{v_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq d\}=\{v_{j}^{\ast}\mid 1\leq j\leq d\}
and
hv_{j}h^{-1}=e^{-\omega_{j}}v_{j}
, then
(P_{t})
is a quantum Markov semigroup, which is GNS-symmetric with respect to
\mathrm{tr}(\,\cdot\,h)
, and the Bohr spectrum of
(P_{t})
is
\{\omega_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq d\}
.

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
let
j^{\ast}
denote the unique index in
\{1,\dots,d\}
such that
v_{j^{\ast}}=v_{j}^{\ast}
. We write
\varphi
for
\mathrm{tr}(h\,\cdot\,)
. As shown in
[
Wir22
, Section 8.2]
, the Tomita correspondence
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{U}_{t}))
associated with
(P_{t})
is given by
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{HS}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
\mathcal{J}=J_{\varphi}\otimes J
with
(\mathcal{J}\xi)_{j}=\overline{\xi_{j^{\ast}}}
, and
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\otimes U_{t}
with
(U_{t}\xi)_{j}=e^{i\omega_{j}t}\xi_{j}
. Thus
\mathcal{V}_{t}=1\otimes U_{t}
and the Bohr spectrum of
(P_{t})
is
\{\omega_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq d\}
.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

By Alicki’s theorem (see
[
Ali76
, Theorem 3]
,
[
CM17
, Theorem 3.1]
), every GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is of the form given in the previous theorem. In this case, the Bohr spectrum is always contained in the spectrum of
\log\Delta_{\varphi}
. It is an interesting question whether this is true for the Bohr spectrum of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on an arbitrary semi-finite von Neumann algebras.

On affine Lusztig varieties Sur les variétés de Lusztig affines
Xuhua He

Abstract
. Affine Lusztig varieties encode the orbital integrals of Iwahori–Hecke functions and serve as building blocks for the (conjectural) theory of affine character sheaves. We establish a close relationship between affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties. Consequently, we give an explicit nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula for affine Lusztig varieties in most cases. Les variétés de Lusztig affines codent les intégrales orbitales des fonctions d’Iwahori–Hecke et servent d’éléments de base pour la théorie (conjecturale) des faisceaux caractères affines. Nous établissons une relation étroite entre les variétés de Lusztig affines et les variétés de Deligne–Lusztig. En conséquence, nous donnons un modèle explicite de non-vacuité et une formule de dimension pour les variétés de Lusztig affines dans la plupart des cas.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Deligne–Lusztig varieties and Lusztig varieties

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a connected reductive group over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
H=\mathbf{H}(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\operatorname{Fr}
be the Frobenius endomorphism on
H
. Let
B
be an
F
-stable Borel subgroup of
H
and
W
be the Weyl group of
H
. The Deligne–Lusztig variety is defined by

X_{w}=\{hB;h^{-1}\operatorname{Fr}(h)\in B\dot{w}B\},

where
w\in W
and
\dot{w}
is a representative of
w
in
G
. It is the subvariety of the flag variety
H/B
consisting of points with fixed relative position with its image under the Frobenius endomorphism. Deligne–Lusztig varieties play a crucial role in the representation theory of finite groups of Lie type
[
10
]
.

Lusztig varieties were introduced in 1977 by Lusztig
[
35
]
at the
LMS symposium on representations of Lie groups
in Oxford. They are defined by replacing the Frobenius endomorphism in the definition of
X_{w}
with the conjugation action by a given regular semisimple element
h\in H
. It was shown in
[
35
]
that the character values arising from the cohomology of Deligne–Lusztig varieties are “universal invariants”, which also make sense over complex numbers. The definition of Lusztig varieties was generalized to an arbitrary element
h\in H
in Lusztig’s theory of character sheaves
[
36
]
. Character sheaves can be defined in two different ways: via “admissible complexes” and via Lusztig varieties. It is a deep result that the two definitions coincide. Abreu and Nigro
[
1
]
used Lusztig varieties in their geometric approach to characters of Hecke algebras. The homology classes of Deligne–Lusztig varieties and those of Lusztig varieties were studied by Kim
[
29
]
.

1.2.
Affine Lusztig varieties

The main subjects of this paper are the affine analogs of Lusztig varieties.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
L=\mathbf{k}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
be the field of the Laurent series (equal characteristic case) or
W(\mathbf{k})[1/p]
(mixed characteristic case) if
\mathbf{k}
is characteristic
p
, where
W(\mathbf{k})
is the ring of
p
-typical Witt vectors. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
L
. Let
\breve{G}=\mathbf{G}(L)
. Let
\mathrm{Gr}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}
be the affine Grassmannian of
\breve{G}
and
\mathrm{Fl}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{I}
be the affine flag variety. We define the affine Lusztig varieties

\displaystyle Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{\mu}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}\},

\displaystyle Y_{w}^{\mathbf{G}}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{I}\in\mathrm{Fl};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\}

in the affine Grassmannian and the affine flag variety, respectively. Here
\gamma
is a regular semisimple element in
\breve{G}
,
\mu
is a dominant coweight of
\breve{G}
, and
w
is an element in the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}
. Lusztig first introduced affine Lusztig varieties in
[
38
]
. Affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian were also studied by Kottwitz and Viehmann
[
33
]
. In the literature, affine Lusztig varieties are also called generalized affine Springer fibers or Kottwitz–Viehmann varieties.

In equal characteristic setting, affine Lusztig varieties are locally closed subschemes of the affine Grassmannian and affine flag variety, equipped with a reduced scheme structure. In mixed characteristic setting, we consider affine Lusztig varieties as perfect schemes in the sense of Zhu
[
51
]
and Bhatt–Scholze
[
4
]
, i.e., as locally closed perfect subschemes of
p
-adic flag varieties.

Affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian and the affine flag variety arise naturally in the study of orbital integrals of spherical Hecke algebras and Iwahori–Hecke algebras. Roughly speaking, based on the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula, one may interpret the orbital integrals of spherical Hecke algebras (resp. Iwahori–Hecke algebras) as point-counting problems on the associated affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian (resp. the affine flag variety). We refer to Yun’s lecture notes
[
50
]
, and recent preprint of Chi
[
9
, §5.3]
for some formulas cononecting the affine Springer fibers and orbital integrals and the thesis work of G. Wang
[
49
, Proposition 11.2.5]
for some formulas connecting the affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian and the orbital integrals.

Affine Lusztig varieties also serve as building blocks for the (conjectural) theory of affine character sheaves. Namely, we consider the map

m:\breve{G}\times^{\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\breve{G},\qquad(g,g^{\prime})gg^{\prime}g^{-1},

where
\breve{G}\times^{\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
is the quotient of
\breve{G}\times\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
by the
\mathcal{I}
-action defined by
i\cdot(g,g^{\prime})=(gi^{-1},ig^{\prime}i^{-1})
. The fibers of this map are the affine Lusztig varieties in the affine flag variety. This map is the affine analog of Lusztig’s map to define the character sheaves for reductive groups over algebraically closed fields. One may define a similar map by replacing
\mathcal{I}
with
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}
. It is interesting to realize Lusztig’s unipotent almost characters of
p
-adic groups
[
40
]
using affine Lusztig varieties.

1.3.
Main result

It is a fundamental question to determine the nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula for affine Lusztig varieties.

This question was solved for affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian of a split connected reductive group in equal characteristic case (under a mild assumption on the residue characteristic) by Bouthier and Chi
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. Their method relies on a global argument using the Hitchin fibration and does not work in mixed characteristic case (e.g., for
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)
for a ramified element
\gamma
). See
[
8
, Remark 1.2.3]
. Little is known for nonsplit groups or for the affine flag case.

The main purpose of this paper is to study affine Lusztig varieties in the affine flag variety of any (not necessarily split) connected reductive group in equal and mixed characteristic cases. Our approach is very different from previous approaches. The key idea is to establish a close relationship between affine Lusztig varieties and their Frobenius-twisted analogs, affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties. Our approach is purely local and thus works also in mixed characteristic case. We will focus on the affine flag case, since the affine Grassmannian case can be deduced from this.

We first give a quick review of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties. Let
F^{\prime}
be a non-archimedean local field and
\breve{F}^{\prime}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F^{\prime}
. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be a connected reductive group over
F^{\prime}
,
\breve{G}^{\prime}=\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(\breve{F}^{\prime})
and
\sigma
be the Frobenius endomorphism on
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
be a
\sigma
-stable Iwahori subgroup of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. Let
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
and
w
be an element in the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. The affine Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
is defined by

X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)=\{g\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Fl}^{\prime};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\}.

The notion of an affine Deligne–Lusztig variety was first introduced by Rapoport
[
44
]
. It plays an important role in arithmetic geometry and the Langlands program. Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties have been studied extensively in the past two decades, and we now have a much better understanding of these varieties than of affine Lusztig varieties. The nonemptiness pattern and the dimension formula for affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties are completely known in the affine Grassmannian case and are known for most pairs
(w,b)
in the affine flag case. We refer to the survey article
[
21
]
for a detailed discussion.

The structure of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties, in general, is quite complicated, and affine Lusztig varieties are even more complicated, partially because, in the loop group, the classification of ordinary conjugacy classes is more complicated than the classification of Frobenius-twisted conjugacy classes. In the special case where
w=1
, the associated affine Deligne–Lusztig variety is either empty or a discrete set. The associated affine Lusztig variety is the affine Springer fiber
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
and is, in general, finite-dimensional (but not zero-dimensional). The dimension formula for affine Springer fiber was obtained by Bezrukavnikov
[
2
]
in equal characteristic case and Chi
[
9
]
in mixed characteristic case.

The main result of this paper, roughly speaking, shows that the difference between affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties comes from the affine Springer fibers.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Suppose that the group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
F^{\prime}
is associated with the group
\mathbf{G}
over
L
. Let
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element of
\breve{G}
and
[b]=f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\{\gamma\})
. Then, for any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we have

(1)

Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

if
X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
, then
\dim Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)=\dim X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)+\dim Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{\gamma}
.

In particular,
Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)
is either an empty set or finite-dimensional.

The notion of associated groups is given in §
2.6
. It means that
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
is residually split and there is a natural bijection between the Iwahori–Weyl groups of
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
. If the residue characteristic of
\breve{F}
is positive, then we may take
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
\breve{F}
so that
\breve{G}=\breve{G}^{\prime}
. However, if
\mathbf{G}
is defined over
\mathbb{C}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
, then
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
has to be chosen over a different field. The isomorphism class of the affine Lusztig variety
Y_{w}(\gamma)
depends on
w
and the conjugacy class
\{\gamma\}
of
\gamma
in
\breve{G}
, and the isomorphism class of the affine Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
depends on
w
and the Frobenius-twisted conjugacy class
[b]
of
b
in
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. To match the data, we introduce in §
2.6
a surjective (but not injective) map
f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}
from the set of conjugacy classes of
\breve{G}
to the set of Frobenius-twisted conjugacy classes of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. The variety
Y_{\gamma}
is the affine Springer fiber associated with
\gamma
, introduced in §
6.3
via the Hodge–Newton decomposition for ordinary conjugation action established in Theorem
4.4
. In the case where
\gamma
is bounded,
Y_{\gamma}
is the affine Springer fiber of
\mathbf{G}
, and in general,
Y_{\gamma}
is the affine Springer fiber of some Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
.

Finally, in Theorems
6.9
and
6.10
, we provide explicit descriptions of the nonemptiness pattern and the dimension formula of affine Lusztig varieties, in most cases based on our knowledge of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties and affine Springer fibers.

One may also apply the method presented in this paper to study the irreducible components. This will be done in future work.

It is also worth pointing out that the relation between affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties is
not
constructed in a geometric way, that is, by explicitly defining a map from one type of variety to another and then computing images and fibers. One may expect there to be some close relation between the (co)homology of affine Lusztig varieties and that of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties, similar to the work of Lusztig
[
35
]
(see, e.g., the equality displayed in
[
41
, page 13]
). Another possible way to reveal the relation between affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties is via the (conjectural) connection between the cocenter of the affine Hecke category and the (conjectural) theory of affine character sheaves. The cocenter of the affine Hecke category has recently been studied by Li, Nadler, and Yun
[
34
]
.

Preliminary

2.1.
Loop groups and Iwahori–Weyl groups

Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
L=\mathbf{k}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
be the field of the Laurent series or
W(\mathbf{k})[1/p]

\mathbf{k}

, where

\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over

. Let

be the apartment of

corresponding to

. We fix an alcove

in

and let
\mathcal{I}
be the corresponding Iwahori subgroup of
\breve{G}
. The simple reflections are reflections along the wall of the base alcove

. Let
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
be the set of simple reflections of
\tilde{W}
. We choose a special vertex

of

and represent
\tilde{W}
as a semidirect product

\tilde{W}=X_{*}(\mathbf{T})_{\Gamma_{0}}\rtimes W_{0}=\{t^{\lambda}w;\lambda\in X_{*}(\mathbf{T})_{\Gamma_{0}},w\in W_{0}\},

where

under the action of

. Let
\mathbb{S}=\tilde{}\mathbb{S}\cap W_{0}
be the set of simple reflections of

. For any

, we choose a representative
\dot{w}
in
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{T})(L)
. By convention, the dominant Weyl chamber of
V=X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is opposite to the unique Weyl chamber containing
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
\Delta
be the set of relative simple roots determined by the dominant Weyl chamber. We denote by

the longest element of
W_{0}
.

A subset

of
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
is called

if the subgroup
W_{K}
of
\tilde{W}
generated by

the standard parahoric subgroup of

\mathcal{I}
and
\dot{w}
for

. By definition, a parahoric subgroup of
\breve{G}
is a finite union of double cosets of an Iwahori subgroup. Any parahoric subgroup of
\breve{G}
is conjugate to a standard parahoric subgroup
\mathcal{P}_{K}

of
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
.

For any

, we denote by
{}^{J}\tilde{W}
(resp.
\tilde{W}^{J}
) the set of minimal representatives of

(resp.
\tilde{W}/W_{J}
). For any

{}^{J}\tilde{W}\cap\tilde{W}^{K}
.

Let
\breve{G}_{\mathrm{af}}
be the subgroup of
\breve{G}
generated by all the parahoric subgroups and

be the Iwahori–Weyl group of

\tilde{S}

2.2.
Affine Lusztig varieties

The affine flag variety of
\breve{G}
is defined as
\mathrm{Fl}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{I}
. If
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}\supset\mathcal{I}
is the maximal special parahoric subgroup of
\breve{G}
corresponding to the special vertex
v_{0}
, then we also consider the affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}
. When
L
is the field of Laurent series, both the affine flag variety and the affine Grassmannian have natural ind-scheme structures. In a mixed characteristic setting, we regard the affine flag variety and the affine Grassmannian as the perfect ind-schemes in the sense of Zhu
[
51
]
and Bhatt–Scholze
[
4
]
.

Let
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}^{+}
be the set of dominant coweights. We have the decompositions

\breve{G}=\bigsqcup_{w\in\tilde{W}}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I},\qquad\breve{G}=\bigsqcup_{\mu\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}^{+}}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}.

For any
\gamma\in\breve{G}
and
w\in\tilde{W}
, the
affine Lusztig variety

Y_{w}(\gamma)
in the affine flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}
is defined by

Y_{w}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{I}\in\mathrm{Fl};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\}.

For
w\in\tilde{W}
with
\ell(w)=0
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
, we have
\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}\gamma
, and
Y_{w}(\gamma)=\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
is the affine Springer fiber.

For any dominant coweight
\mu
and
\gamma\in\breve{G}
, the
affine Lusztig variety

Y_{\mu}(\gamma)
in the affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}
is defined by

Y_{\mu}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}\in\mathrm{Gr};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}\}.

By definition,
Y_{w}(\gamma)
is a locally closed ind-subscheme of
\mathrm{Fl}
, and
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)
is a locally closed ind-subscheme of
\mathrm{Gr}
. Let
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)=\{g\in\breve{G};g^{-1}\gamma g=\gamma\}
be the centralizer of
\gamma
. It acts on the affine Lusztig varieties
Y_{w}(\gamma)
and
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)
.

Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties

Let
F^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
and
\breve{F}^{\prime}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be a connected reductive group, residually split over
F^{\prime}
and
\breve{G}^{\prime}=\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(\breve{F}^{\prime})
. Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius endomorphism on
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. We fix a
\sigma
-stable Iwahori–Weyl group
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. Let
\tilde{W}^{\prime}
be the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}^{\prime}

For
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}

\mathbf{J}_{b}

2.4.
The sets
B_{\sigma}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})

For any

, is not injective in general. We will not recall the precise definition of the maps

, but we will give explicit descriptions of the restriction of these maps to the Iwahori–Weyl groups in the next subsection.

2.5.
The straight conjugacy classes

To match the conjugacy classes of

with the

By definition, an element

Let

to

3.
Affine Springer fibers

3.1.
Bounded-modulo-center elements

In the rest of this paper, unless otherwise stated, we assume that either
\mathrm{char}(\mathbf{k})=0
or
\mathrm{char}(\mathbf{k})>0
does not divide the Weyl group
W_{0}
of
\mathbf{G}
.

In this section, we consider the group
\breve{G}
together with a group automorphism
\delta
such that
\delta(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{I}
. Then
\delta
also induces group automorphisms on
\tilde{W}
and on
\Omega
. We consider the group extensions

\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}=\breve{G}\rtimes\langle\delta\rangle,\tilde{W}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}=\tilde{W}\rtimes\langle\delta\rangle\text{ and }\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}=\Omega\rtimes\langle\delta\rangle.

An element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is called
bounded
if it is contained in a bounded subgroup. Note that if
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is of finite order, then any element in
\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
is bounded. On the other hand, if
g\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is bounded, then it has a fixed point in the affine flag variety. So it is conjugate to an element of the form
\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. By the boundedness,
\tau
is of finite order. Thus an element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is bounded if and only if it is conjugate to an element of
\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
with finite order.

Let
\breve{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}}
be the adjoint group of
\breve{G}
. We have a natural projection map
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}\Aut(\breve{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
. An element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is called
bounded-modulo-center
if its image in
\Aut(\breve{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
is bounded. We assume furthermore that the image of
\delta
in
\Aut(\breve{G}_{{\mathrm{ad}}})
is of finite order. Then an element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is bounded-modulo-center if and only if it is conjugate to an element of
\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. As a consequence, an element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is bounded-modulo-center if and only if it is conjugate to an element in
\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
and some
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)
-stable spherical subset
K
of
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
.

3.2.
Regular locus

Let
\gamma
be a bounded-modulo center element of
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. Let
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
and
K
be an
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)
-stable spherical subset of
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. We define the
affine Springer fiber
in the partial affine flag variety
\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K}
by

Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{P}_{K}\in\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}\}.

By definition,
Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\gamma
is conjugate to an element in
\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
. If we assume furthermore that
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
, then the condition that
g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
is equivalent to the condition that
\gamma(g\mathcal{P}_{K})\gamma^{-1}=g\mathcal{P}_{K}
. In other words,
Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)=(\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K})^{\gamma}
is the subscheme of fixed points of
\gamma
on
\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K}
.

Let
\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}
be the reductive quotient of
\mathcal{P}_{K}
, i.e., the quotient of
\mathcal{P}_{K}
by its pro-unipotent radical. The Dynkin diagram of
\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}
is
K
. The conjugation action of
\dot{\tau}
on
\mathcal{P}_{K}
induces a diagram automorphism on
\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}
, which we denote by
\bar{\tau}
. For any element
\gamma^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
, we denote by
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}
its image in the (not necessarily connected) reductive group
\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}\rtimes\langle\bar{\tau}\rangle
. We consider the
regular locus

Y_{K,\tau}^{{\mathrm{reg}}}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{P}_{K}\in Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma);\overline{g^{-1}\gamma g}\text{ is regular in }\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}\rtimes\langle\bar{\tau}\rangle\}.

Since the set of regular elements in any (not necessarily connected) reductive group over
\mathbf{k}
is open,
Y^{{\mathrm{reg}}}_{K,\tau}(\gamma)
is open in
Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)
. By
[
28
, §3, Proposition 1]
, the affine Springer fibers associated with the bounded-modulo-center regular semisimple elements are finite-dimensional.

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
be a bounded-modulo-center regular semisimple element. Then

(1)

the affine Springer fiber
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
in the affine flag variety is equidimensional;

(2)

for any
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
and an
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)
-stable spherical subset
K
of
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
with
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\dot{\tau}
, the regular locus
Y^{{\mathrm{reg}}}_{K,\tau}(\gamma)
is nonempty and

\dim Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)=\dim Y^{{\mathrm{reg}}}_{K,\tau}(\gamma)=\dim\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}.

3.3.
Topological Jordan decomposition

We follow
[
3
, Appendix B]
. Recall that
\mathbf{k}
is the residue field of
\breve{F}
. Let
\gamma\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
be a bounded semisimple element. We say that
\gamma
is
strongly semisimple
if for every representation of
\mathbf{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
(over an algebraic closure of
\breve{F}
), any eigenvalue of the image of
\gamma
is in
\mathbf{k}^{\times}
(in equal characteristic case), or the Teichmüller representative of an element in
\mathbf{k}^{\times}
(in mixed characteristic case). The latter condition is equivalent to the condition that any eigenvalue is of finite order prime to
p
, where
p
is the residue characteristic. We say that
\gamma
is
topologically unipotent
if for every representation of
\mathbf{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
(over an algebraic closure of
\breve{F}
), any eigenvalue
\alpha
of
\gamma
satisfies
\mathrm{val}_{\breve{F}}(\alpha-1)>0
.

The following result, which is called the
topological Jordan decomposition
, was proved by Bezrukavnikov and Varshavsky
[
3
, Lemma B.1.6]
for split groups in equal characteristic case. The same proof works for all groups in both equal and mixed characteristic cases.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
be a bounded semisimple element. Then there exists a unique decomposition
\gamma=su=us
such that
s\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is strongly semisimple and
u\in\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is topologically unipotent.

The topological Jordan decomposition can be seen explicitly as follows. Note that any bounded element in
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is conjugate to an element in
\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
of finite order. Suppose that
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
. Following the proof of
[
3
, Lemma B.1.7]
, we may represent
\mathcal{I}
as
\lim_{n}H_{n}
, where
H_{n}=\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}_{n}
is an algebraic group over
\mathbf{k}
. Here
\mathcal{I}_{n}
is the
n
th congruence subgroup of
\mathcal{I}
. The conjugation action of
\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
preserves
\mathcal{I}_{n}
and induces an action on
H_{n}
for each
n
. We consider the group extension
H^{{\mathrm{ex}}}_{n}=H_{n}\rtimes\langle\dot{\tau}\rangle
. Then
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}\dot{\tau}
corresponds to a family
\gamma_{n}\in H_{n}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. Let
\gamma_{n}=s_{n}u_{n}=u_{n}s_{n}
be the Jordan decomposition of
\gamma_{n}\in H^{{\mathrm{ex}}}_{n}
. By the uniqueness of Jordan decomposition, both
\{s_{n}\}_{n}
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
form compatible families. Let
s=\lim_{n}s_{n}\in\mathcal{I}\rtimes\langle\dot{\tau}\rangle
and
u=\lim_{n}u_{n}\in\mathcal{I}\rtimes\langle\dot{\tau}\rangle
. Then
\gamma=su=us
. By the proof of
[
3
, Lemma B.1.7]
,
s
is strongly semisimple and
u
is topologically unipotent. Then
su
is the topological Jordan decomposition of
\gamma
.

We also have the following simple criterion for topological unipotent elements. Let
U_{\mathcal{I}}
be the pro-unipotent radical of
\mathcal{I}
and
\bar{}\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}/U_{\mathcal{I}}
be the reductive quotient, which is a torus over
\mathbf{k}
. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
and
\bar{\gamma}
be the image of
\gamma
in
\bar{}\mathcal{I}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. It is easy to see that
\gamma
is topologically unipotent if and only if
\bar{\gamma}
is a unipotent element in the (disconnected) reductive group
\bar{}\mathcal{I}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. When the residue characteristic is large, the only unipotent elements in
\bar{}\mathcal{I}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
is the identity element. In this case, the topologically unipotent elements in
\mathcal{I}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
are the elements in
U_{\mathcal{I}}
, and the topologically unipotent elements of
\breve{G}
are all contained in
\breve{G}_{\mathrm{af}}
. However, this is not the case for small residue characteristic.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
and
\breve{F}=\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{2}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
. Then
\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&1\\
\varepsilon&\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}\end{bmatrix}
is a topologically unipotent regular semisimple element. However, this element is not contained in
\breve{G}_{\mathrm{af}}
.

3.4.
Equi-dimensionality

To prove Theorem
3.1
, one may reduce consideration to that of the adjoint group case. In particular, one may assume that
\gamma
is a bounded element.

Let
\gamma=su
be the topological Jordan decomposition. For any strongly semisimple element
s\in\breve{G}
, we denote by
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{s}
the simply connected covering of the derived group of the centralizer of
s
. By
[
3
, Lemma B.2.3]
, there is a universal homeomorphism between the affine Springer fiber
(\mathrm{Fl}^{\mathbf{G}})^{\gamma}
and a disjoint union of copies of the affine Springer fibers
(\mathrm{Fl}^{\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{s}})^{\gamma}
.

It suffices to prove the following:

(a)

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a semisimple, simply connected group. Let
\gamma
be a topological unipotent element in
\breve{G}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. Then the affine Springer fiber
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
is connected and equi-dimensional.

When
\mathbf{G}
is split over
\mathbb{C}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
, the result is due to Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
28
, §4, Proposition 1 and Lemma 2]
. The general case follows from their proof, by paying extra attention to the Springer fibers for unipotent elements in disconnected reductive groups (only occurring in small characteristic) as in
[
47
, Chap. II, §1]
. We give a sketch of the proof for the convenience of readers.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\breve{G}\dot{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. Let
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}_{\tau\text{-sph}}
be the set of spherical
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\tau)
-orbits on
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. For any
K\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}_{\tau\text{-sph}}
, we consider the projection map
\pi:\mathrm{Fl}\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K}
. By Steinberg’s theorem, the map
\pi
induces a surjection from
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
to
Y_{K,\tau}(\gamma)=(\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K})^{\gamma}
, and the fiber over
g\mathcal{P}_{K}
is the Springer fiber in the finite flag
\mathcal{P}_{K}/\mathcal{I}
associated with the element
\overline{g^{-1}\gamma g}
, where
\overline{g^{-1}\gamma g}
is the image of
g^{-1}\gamma g
in the (disconnected) reductive group
\bar{}\mathcal{P}_{K}\rtimes\Omega^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
. Since
\gamma
is topologically unipotent,
\overline{g^{-1}\gamma g}
is unipotent. By
[
47
, Chap. II, Lemme 1.3]
, any fiber of
\pi:\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}(\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K})^{\gamma}
is either a point or a one-dimensional curve. In the latter case, we call this one-dimensional curve a
line of type
K
.

For connectedness, we follow the proof of
[
28
, §4, Lemma 2]
. Let
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
. Let
w
be the relative position of
z
and
z^{\prime}
. Then
w
is also the relative position of
\dot{\tau}\cdot z
and
\dot{\tau}\cdot z^{\prime}
. Thus
w
commutes with
\tau
. For any
K\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}_{\tau\text{-sph}}
, we denote by
w_{K}
the longest element in the finite Weyl group
W_{K}
. Since
\mathbf{G}
is semisimple and simply connected, the Iwahori–Weyl group
\tilde{W}
is the affine Weyl group
W_{\mathrm{af}}
. By
[
37
, Theorem A.8]
, the centralizer of
\tau
in
\tilde{W}
is the affine Weyl group generated by
\{w_{K}\}_{K\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}_{\tau\text{-sph}}}
. Therefore we have a length-additive expression
w=w_{K_{1}}\cdots w_{K_{n}}
for some
K_{1},\ldots,K_{n}\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}_{\tau\text{-sph}}
. Then there exists a unique sequence
z=z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}=z^{\prime}
of elements in
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
such that for any
0<i\leqslant n
,
z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}
are in the same fiber of the projection map
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}(\breve{G}/\mathcal{P}_{K_{i}})^{\gamma}
. Hence
z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}
are connected by a line of type
K_{i}
. In particular,
z
and
z^{\prime}
are connected, and hence
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
is connected.

Finally, we prove equi-dimensionality. Let
Y
be an irreducible component of
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
. Let
Y_{0}
be an irreducible component of
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
of dimension
d
, where
d=\dim\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
. Let
z\in Y_{0}
and
z^{\prime}
be a generic point in
Y
. Let
z=z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}=z^{\prime}
be a sequence of elements in
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
such that for any
0<i\leqslant n
,
z_{i-1}
and
z_{i}
are connected by a line
\mathcal{L}_{i}
of type
K_{i}
. By
[
28
, §4, Lemma 1]
, there exists an irreducible component
Y_{i}
of
\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
such that
\dim Y_{i}=d
and
\mathcal{L}_{i}\subset Y_{i}
. In particular,
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\subset Y_{n}
. Since
z^{\prime}
is a generic point in
Y
, we must have
Y=Y_{n}
. In particular,
\dim Y=d
.

3.5.
Nonemptiness of the regular locus

When
\mathbf{G}
is split over
\mathbb{C}(\!(\varepsilon)\!)
, and
K
is hyperspecial, the result is due to Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
28
, §4, Corollaries 1 and 2]
. The general case follows from their proof, by paying extra attention to the Springer fibers for disconnected reductive groups. We give a sketch of the proof for the convenience of readers.

Let
d=\dim\mathrm{Fl}^{\gamma}
. Let
\rho:\tilde{W}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}\Aut(H_{2d}(\mathrm{Fl}))
be the natural action of
\tilde{W}^{{\mathrm{ex}}}
on
H_{2d}(\mathrm{Fl})
. Suppose that

4.1.

in

4.3.
Method of successive approximations

A key ingredient in the proof of Hodge–Newton decomposition in
[
16
]
is the method of successive approximations.

We prove the following result for minimal length elements. The statement is similar to the
\sigma
-conjugation action related to
\mathbf{P}
-alcove elements
[
16
, Lemma 6.1.1]
, but the proof is simpler. In this subsection, we simply write
{}^{g}h
for
ghg^{-1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
w
is a minimal length element in its conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{I}_{n}\subset\mathcal{I}
be the
n
th principal congruent subgroup of
\mathcal{I}
. Set
N_{n}=\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F}),N^{-}_{n}=\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}^{-}(\breve{F})
and
M_{n}=\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F})
. Then, for
m\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following:

(1)

Given
i_{+}\in N_{n}
, there exists
b_{+}\in N_{n}
such that
b_{+}i_{+}{}^{m\dot{w}}b_{+}^{-1}\in N_{n+1}
.

(2)

Given
i_{-}\in N^{-}_{n}
, there exists
b_{-}\in N^{-}_{n}
such that
{}^{(\dot{w}m)^{-1}}b_{-}i_{-}b_{-}^{-1}\in N^{-}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

We prove part (1). Part (2) is proved in the same way.

We simply write
\mathbf{M}
for
\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}
. Since
w
is a
\mathbf{P}_{\nu_{w}}
-alcove element, we have
{}^{\dot{w}}N_{n}\subset N_{n}
. Hence
{}^{m\dot{w}}N_{n}\subset N_{n}
and
{}^{(m\dot{w})^{k}}N_{n}\subset N_{n}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By §
4.2
, there exists a standard parahoric subgroup
\mathcal{P}
of
\breve{M}_{\nu_{w}}
such that
(m\dot{w})^{k}\subset\mathcal{P}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{P}
for all
k
.

Note that
\mathcal{P}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{P}=\bigsqcup_{u\in W_{M}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
, where
W_{M}
is the (finite) relative Weyl group of
\mathbf{M}
. We choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle k\nu_{w},\alpha\rangle\geqslant\max\{\ell(u);u\in W_{M}\}+1
for all roots in
\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}}=\mathcal{I}\cap\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F})
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}^{-}}=\mathcal{I}\cap\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}^{-}(\breve{F})
. We have the Iwahori decomposition
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}^{-}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}}
. Then, for any
u\in W_{M}
,

\mathcal{I}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{I}=(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}^{-}})\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}^{-}})=\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{N}^{-}}.

Thus
(m\dot{w})^{k}\in N_{0}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}N^{-}_{0}\cap\breve{M}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
. Then
(m\dot{w})^{k}=i\dot{u}\dot{w}^{k}i^{\prime}
for some
i,i^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
and
u\in W_{M}
. So
{}^{(m\dot{w})^{k}}N_{n}\subset{}^{i\dot{w}^{k}\dot{u}}N_{n}\subset{}^{i}N_{n+1}\subset N_{n+1}
.

We take
b_{+}=({}^{(m\dot{w})^{k-1}}i_{+}^{-1})\cdots({}^{m\dot{w}}i_{+}^{-1})i_{+}^{-1}
. We then have
b_{+}i_{+}{}^{m\dot{w}}b_{+}^{-1}={}^{(m\dot{w})^{k}}i_{+}\in N_{n+1}
.
∎

We now prove the following result, analogous to
[
16
, Theorem 2.1.2]
for
\mathbf{P}
-alcove elements and
\sigma
-conjugation action.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that
w
is a minimal length element in its conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}
. Let
\mathcal{I}\times^{\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
be the quotient of
\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
by the action of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
defined by
i\cdot(g,h)=(gi^{-1},ihi^{-1})
. Then
the map

\phi:\mathcal{I}\times^{\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I},\qquad(i,m)imi^{-1}

is surjective.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that in
[
16
, §§4 and 6]
. We simply write
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
for
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
. Consider the following Cartesian diagram:

\textstyle{({}^{\dot{w}}\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I})\times^{{}^{\dot{w}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}}\,\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{I}\times^{\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}}

It suffices to prove that the map
({}^{\dot{w}}\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I})\times^{{}^{\dot{w}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}}\,\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
is surjective. Following
[
16
, §6]
, a generic Moy–Prasad filtration gives a filtration
\mathcal{I}=\cup_{r\geqslant 0}\mathcal{I}[r]
with
\mathcal{I}[r]\supset\mathcal{I}[s]
for

such that

(1)

each
\mathcal{I}[r]
is normal in
\mathcal{I}
;

(2)

(a)

either there exists a positive affine root

such that the embedding

induces an isomorphism
U_{\alpha}/U_{\alpha_{+}}\cong\mathcal{I}[r]/\mathcal{I}[r^{+}]

(b)

or

.

We show that any element in
\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}[r]
is conjugate under

to an element of
\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathcal{I}[r^{+}]
and that the conjugators can be taken to be small when

If

4.4.
Hodge–Newton decomposition for ordinary conjugation

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
w
is a minimal length element in its conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}
. Then for any
\gamma\in\breve{G}
,
Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\{\gamma\}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, for
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}
, we have
Z_{\breve{M}_{\nu_{w}}}(\gamma)=Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
and

Y^{\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}}_{w}(\gamma)\cong Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma).

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}_{\nu_{w}}
. We first prove that

(a)

If
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
, and
g\in\breve{G}
with
g\gamma_{1}g^{-1}=\gamma_{2}
, then
g\in\breve{M}
.

By definition,
g\gamma_{1}^{k}g^{-1}=\gamma_{2}^{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w^{k}=t^{\lambda}
for some coweight
\lambda
. By §
4.2
(a), there exists a standard parahoric subgroup
\mathcal{P}
of
\breve{M}
such that
\gamma_{1}^{lk},\gamma_{2}^{lk}\in\mathcal{P}t^{l\lambda}=\mathcal{P}t^{l\lambda}
for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\mathcal{P}t^{l\lambda}g\cap gt^{l\lambda}\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
. Set
\breve{P}=\mathbf{P}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F})
,
\breve{N}=\mathbf{N}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F})
, and
\breve{N}^{-}=\mathbf{N}^{-}_{\nu_{w}}(\breve{F})
. We may assume that
g\in\breve{P}\dot{x}\breve{P}
for some
x\in W_{0}
. Note that
\breve{P}\dot{x}\breve{P}\cong(\breve{N}\cap\dot{x}\breve{N}^{-}\dot{x}^{-1})\times\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{P}
. We write
g
as
g_{1}g_{2}
with
g_{1}\in(\breve{N}\cap\dot{x}\breve{N}^{-}\dot{x}^{-1})
and
g_{2}\in\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{P}
. Then
gt^{l\lambda}\mathcal{P}\subset g_{1}\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{P}
and
\mathcal{P}t^{l\lambda}g\subset\mathcal{P}(t^{l\lambda}g_{1}t^{l\lambda})\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{P}
. Hence
g_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}(t^{l\lambda}g_{1}t^{-l\lambda})\breve{M}\neq\emptyset
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
g_{1}\in\breve{N}
, we have
\lim_{l\infty}t^{l\lambda}g_{1}t^{-l\lambda}=1
. Thus
g_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}(t^{l\lambda}g_{1}t^{-l\lambda})\breve{M}\neq\emptyset
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
implies that
g_{1}=1
. Hence
g\in\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{P}
. By the same argument, we have
g\in\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{M}
. We write
g
as
g=g_{3}\dot{x}g_{4}
for
g_{3},g_{4}\in\breve{M}
. By definition, any element in
\breve{M}
commutes with
t^{l\lambda}
. Then
\mathcal{P}g_{3}t^{l\lambda}\dot{x}g_{4}\cap g_{3}t^{lx(\lambda)}\dot{x}g_{4}\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
and hence
t^{l(x(\lambda)-\lambda)}\in g_{3}^{-1}\mathcal{P}g_{3}\dot{x}g_{4}\mathcal{P}g_{4}^{-1}\dot{x}^{-1}
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that for fixed
g_{3}
and
g_{4}
,
g_{3}^{-1}\mathcal{P}g_{3}\dot{x}g_{4}\mathcal{P}g_{4}^{-1}\dot{x}^{-1}
is bounded. Thus we have
x(\lambda)=\lambda
. Hence
x\in W_{\nu_{w}}
and
g\in\breve{M}\dot{x}\breve{M}=\breve{M}
.

(a) is proved.

We now prove the theorem. If
Y_{w}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
, then
\{\gamma\}\cap\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\neq\emptyset
. By Theorem
4.3
,
\{\gamma\}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\neq\emptyset
. We assume that
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
. Note that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\subset\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
. Let
\mathrm{Fl}^{\mathbf{M}}
be the affine flag variety of
\breve{M}
. The image of
Y^{\mathbf{M}}_{w}(\gamma)
under the immersion
\mathrm{Fl}^{\mathbf{M}}\mathrm{Fl}
is contained in
Y_{w}(\gamma)
.

Suppose that
g\mathcal{I}\in Y_{w}(\gamma)
. By Theorem
4.3
, up to multiplying a suitable element in
\mathcal{I}
, we may assume that
g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{M}}
. By (a),
g\in\breve{M}
. This shows that the image of
Y^{\mathbf{M}}_{w}(\gamma)
equals
Y_{w}(\gamma)
. The proof is finished.
∎

5.2.
Lusztig varieties and Deligne–Lusztig varieties

For any

and

Lusztig variety
is defined by

\mathcal{B}_{w,\gamma,\delta}=\{gB\in H/B;g^{-1}\gamma\delta(g)\in B\dot{w}B\}.

The isomorphism classes of Lusztig varieties depend on the
\delta
-twisted conjugacy class
\{\gamma\}
of

in
H
and

was introduced by Lusztig

35

[

]

Similarly, for any

is

-conjugate to

It is easy to see that

The main purpose of this section is to compare the dimension of Lusztig varieties and Deligne–Lusztig varieties for associated

and

and

and identify

with

and

as group automorphisms on

.

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 5.1

Let

and

The proof involves two parts: the base case for the minimal length elements

-conjugacy classes and the reduction procedure to the base case. We discuss these in the rest of this section.

6.7.
Explicit descriptions

Let
\leqslant
be the dominance order on the set of dominant rational coweights, that is,
\lambda_{1}\leqslant\lambda_{2}
if and only if
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}
is a non-negative rational linear combination of simple coroots.

We recall the definitions of the defect and the virtual dimension.

For
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
, the
defect
of
b
is defined to be
\operatorname{def}(b)=\rank_{F}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}-\rank_{F}\mathbf{J}_{b}
, where for a reductive group
\mathbf{H}
defined over
F
,
\rank_{F}
is the
F
-rank of the group
\mathbf{H}
. The defect may also be defined in the following way, depending only on the Iwahori–Weyl group, not the reductive group. Let
V_{w}=\{v\in V;w\cdot v=v+\nu_{w}\}
. For any straight conjugacy class
C
of
\tilde{W}
, we define the
defect
of
C
by

\operatorname{def}(C)=\dim V-\dim V_{w}\text{ for any }w\in C.

By
[
32
, §1.9]
,
\operatorname{def}(C)=\operatorname{def}(b)
, where
[b]=[\dot{w}]
for any
w\in C
.

Note that any element in
\tilde{W}
can be written in a unique way as
xt^{\lambda}y
, where
x,y\in W_{0}
,
\lambda
is a dominant coweight such that
t^{\lambda}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}_{0}}\tilde{W}
. We set
\eta(xt^{\lambda}y)=yx\in W_{0}
. For any
w\in\tilde{W}
and

, the

is defined to be

By
[
20
, Theorem 2.30]
, we have
\dim X_{w}(b)\leqslant d_{w}(b)
.

We may reformulate the virtual dimension using the straight conjugacy class
C
instead. Set

d_{w}(C)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\ell(w)+\ell(\eta(w))-\operatorname{def}(C)-\ell(C)\big).

Here

is defined as the length of any minimal length element in

if

for any

. Here,

is the half sum of positive roots.

We now state the result for the nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula for affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\mu
be a dominant coweight. Then
X_{\mu}(b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\kappa(\mu)=\kappa(b)
and
\nu_{[b]}\leqslant\mu
. In this case,

\dim X_{\mu}(b)=\langle\mu-\nu_{[b]},\rho\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{def}(b).

The “only if” part of the nonemptiness pattern is due to Rapoport and Richartz
[
45
]
and the “if” part is due to Gashi
[
12
]
. The dimension formula is proved in
[
15
]
and
[
48
]
for split groups, in
[
18
]
and
[
51
]
for unramified groups, and in
[
20
, Theorem 2.29]
in general.

Combining Theorem
6.8
with Theorem
6.7
, we obtain the following description of the nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula for affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian.

Theorem 6.9

Note that
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}
. Set

. We have the following Cartesian diagram

\textstyle{Y_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{\mu}(\gamma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}.}

Note that the fibers of the projection map

are isomorphic to

. Then we have

\dim Y_{\mu}(\gamma)=\dim Y_{1}-\dim\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}/\mathcal{I}=\dim Y_{1}-\ell(w_{0}).

Similarly, we set
X_{1}=\sqcup_{w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}}X_{w}(b)
. We have
\dim X_{\mu}(b)=\dim X_{1}-\ell(w_{0})
.

In particular,

if and only if
X_{\mu}(b)\neq\emptyset
and
Y_{1}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset

X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset

and
\nu_{[b]}\leqslant\mu

\kappa(\gamma)=\kappa(b)
and
\nu_{\{\gamma\}}=\nu_{[b]}

6.7

Y_{\mu}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
if and only

and
\nu_{\{\gamma\}}\leqslant\mu

Suppose that
\kappa(\mu)=\kappa(\gamma)
and
\nu_{\{\gamma\}}\leqslant\mu

and Theorem

\displaystyle\dim Y_{\mu}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\dim Y_{1}-\ell(w_{0})

\displaystyle=\max_{w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}}\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)-\ell(w_{0})

\displaystyle=\max_{w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}}\dim X_{w}(b)+\dim Y_{\gamma}-\ell(w_{0})

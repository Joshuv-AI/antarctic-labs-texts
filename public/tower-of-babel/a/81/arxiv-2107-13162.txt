Introduction

In his 1783 work
[
9
]
L. Euler considered the following
transcendental equation
entailed from 1758 work of J. H. Lambert
[
21
]

(1)

x^{\alpha}-x^{\beta}=(\alpha-\beta)vx^{\alpha+\beta}.

Letting
\alpha\to\beta
in (
1
), Euler obtained

(2)

\ln x=vx^{\beta}.

Next, Euler set
y=x^{\beta}
and
u=\alpha v
in (
2
), obtaining

(3)

{\ln y\over y}=u.

Letting
y=e^{w}
, equation (
3
) yields

(4)

we^{-w}=u.

Equation (
4
) gave rise to the Lambert W function, and in particular the function
W_{0}(x)
for
-e^{-1}\leq x<0
.

Denote
R_{0}=(0,1)
,
\overline{R}_{0}=(0,1]
, and
R_{1}=(1,\infty)
. Then, for each
0<u<e^{-1}
there are exactly two solutions
w
of (
4
).
Moreover, one solution is always in
R_{0}
and one solution is always in
R_{1}
. For
u=e^{-1}
,
w=1
is the only solution.
Thus, for
0<u\leq e^{-1}
, there exists exactly one solution
w
of (
4
) in
\overline{R}_{0}
.
This solution is either unique when
u=1
or is the smaller of the two solutions when
0<u<e^{-1}
.

Lambert-Euler inversion (
4
) yields the existence of function

(5)

x(t):=\min\{x>0~:~xe^{-x}=te^{-t}\},\qquad t\in(0,\infty),

with the range
\overline{R}_{0}
.
In 1960, function
x(t)
was used by P. Erdős and A. Rényi
[
7
]
for establishing formation of a giant cluster in the theory of random graphs.
In 1962, J. B. McLeod
[
24
]
used Lambert-Euler inversion and function
x(t)
in the analysis of
Smoluchowski coagulation equations

with multiplicative kernel (aka
Flory coagulation system
), observing the
gelation phenomenon
.

In this paper, we will study the multidimensional Lambert-Euler inversion problem.
Let
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
be a nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix.
For a given vector
{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, consider region

(6)

R_{0}=\left\{{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}:\,\rho\left(VD[z_{j}]\right)<1\right\}

its closure within
(0,\infty)^{k}
,

(7)

\overline{R}_{0}=\left\{{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}:\,\rho\left(VD[z_{j}]\right)\leq 1\right\},

and the complement of
\overline{R}_{0}
within
(0,\infty)^{k}
,

(8)

R_{1}=\left\{{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}:\,\rho\left(VD[z_{j}]\right)>1\right\},

where for a vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with coordinates
x_{i}
,

D[x_{i}]
denotes the diagonal matrix with entries
x_{i}
, and
\rho(M)
denotes the spectral radius of matrix
M
.

In this paper we found it convenient to us bra-ket notation of P. Dirac. Specifically,

|{\bf x}\rangle
will denote the column vector representation of vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
and
\langle{\bf x}|
will denote the row vector representation of vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
For

and
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

will represent the product

, a column vector.
Respectively,
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf y}\rangle=\langle{\bf y}|{\bf x}\rangle
will be the dot product of
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Finally, for a matrix

,
\langle{\bf x}|M|{\bf y}\rangle
will represent the product
{\bf x}^{\sf T}M{\bf y}
resulting in a scalar.

Let
{\bf e}_{j}
denote the
j
-th standard basis vector. The following theorem is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Multidimensional Lambert-Euler inversion)
.

Consider a nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
For any given
{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, there exists a unique vector
{\bf y}\in\overline{R}_{0}
such that

(9)

y_{j}e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}\rangle}=z_{j}e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}\rangle}\qquad j=1,\ldots,k.

Moreover, if
{\bf z}\in\overline{R}_{0}
, then
{\bf y}={\bf z}
. If
{\bf z}\in R_{1}
,
then
{\bf y}<{\bf z}
(
y_{i}<z_{i}

\forall i
), i.e.,
{\bf y}
is the smallest solution of (
9
).

Theorem
1.1
which we will prove in Section
2
,
yields the following multidimensional analogue of
[-e^{-1},0)
branch of Lambert
W
function
W_{0}(x)
.
Consider domain

(10)

\mathcal{D}=\left\{{\bf u}\in(0,\infty)^{k}~~:~~\exists\,{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}\quad\text{ such that }\quad|{\bf u}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}\,e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\right\}.

Then, by Theorem
1.1
,

(11)

\mathcal{D}=\left\{{\bf u}\in(0,\infty)^{k}~~:~~\exists\,{\bf z}\in\overline{R}_{0}\quad\text{ such that }\quad|{\bf u}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}\,e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\right\}

and for the mapping
\,\Psi_{V}:(0,\infty)^{k}\to\mathcal{D}\,
defined as
\,|\Psi_{V}(z)\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}\,e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\,
,
Theorem
1.1
implies that restricting the domain of
\,\Psi_{V}\,
to
\overline{R}_{0}
, makes
\,\Psi_{V}\,
a continuous bijection from
\overline{R}_{0}
to
\mathcal{D}
. Thus, we can define Lambert-Euler inversion
\,\Lambda_{V}\,
of
\,\Psi_{V}\,
as a continuous bijection from
\mathcal{D}
to
\overline{R}_{0}
.
Thus, for any given
{\bf z}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, equation (
9
)
has the minimal solution
{\bf y}=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\bf z})\in\overline{R}_{0}
, where the minimum is achieved in all coordinates.

Now, we can define a multidimensional analogue of function
x(t)
in (
5
).
For any given
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, let
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
for all
t>0
,
i.e.,
{\bf y}(t)
is the minimal solution of

(12)

y_{j}e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}\rangle}=\alpha_{j}te^{-t\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle}\qquad\forall j=1,\ldots,k.

Consequently,
\,{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
is a continuous function.

By analogy with
\,\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{x(t)\over t}=0
for

in (
5
), we will show
that
\,\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{{\bf y}(t)\over t}={\boldsymbol{0}}
. This will be done in Lemma
2.7
of Section
2
.

.

1.1.
Vector-Multiplicative Coalescent Processes

The solution to the multidimensional Lambert-Euler inversion given in Theorem
1.1
and
the function
{\bf y}(t)
defined in (
12
) will be used in the analysis of a general class of coalescent processes introduced here
that we will call the
vector-multiplicative coalescent processes
.

For a nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
and a given vector
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
,
let

(13)

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{1}[n]\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{k}[n]\end{array}\!\!\right]={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}n+o(\sqrt{n}).

Consider a system with
k
types of particles,
1,\ldots,k
,
and the coalescent process that begins with
\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]|{\bf 1}\rangle=\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|{\bf 1}\rangle n+o(\sqrt{n})
singletons
distributed between the
k
types so that for each
i
, there are
\alpha_{i}[n]=\alpha_{i}n+o(\sqrt{n})
particles of type
i
.
In this continuous time Markov process, a particle of type
i
bonds with a particle of type
j
with the rate
v_{i,j}/n
,
where
v_{i,j}=\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle
is the
(i,j)
element in the matrix
V
.
The bonds are formed independently. This process is called
vector-multiplicative coalescent
.

Formally, vector-multiplicative coalescent process describes cluster merger dynamics, where the weight of each cluster is a
k
-dimensional vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
such that
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0
.
Each cluster of weight
{\bf x}
bonds together
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
particles of corresponding types
1,\ldots,k
.
The coalescent process begins with
\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]|{\bf 1}\rangle
singletons of all
k
types, of which there are
\alpha_{i}[n]
of type
i
(for all
i=1,\ldots,k
). Each pair of clusters with respective weight vectors
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
would coalesce into a cluster of weight
{\bf x+y}
with rate
K({\bf x},{\bf y})/n
,
where

(14)

K({\bf x},{\bf y})=\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle.

The last merger will create a cluster of weight
\,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]
.

The kernel
K({\bf x},{\bf y})
defined in (
14
) will be referred to as the
vector-multiplicative kernel
.
The kernel is symmetric

K({\bf x},{\bf y})=K({\bf y},{\bf x})\quad\text{ for all vectors }~{\bf x},{\bf y}

and bilinear

K(c_{1}{\bf x}+c_{2}{\bf y},{\bf z})=c_{1}\,K({\bf x},{\bf z})+c_{2}\,K({\bf y},{\bf z})\quad\text{ for all vectors }~{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z}~~\text{ and scalars }~c_{1},c_{2}.

Notice that coalescent processes with vector-valued weights have been considered in the past. See
[
18
,
19
,
30
]
.

In the vector-multiplicative coalescent process, let
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
denote the number of clusters of weight
{\bf x}
at time
t\geq 0
.
The initial values are
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}[n]\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.
The process

{\bf ML}_{n}(t)=\Big(\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)\Big)_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0}

that counts clusters of all types in the vector-multiplicative coalescent process is the corresponding

Marcus-Lushnikov process
.
In Lemma
3.1
we will refer to the known weak limit result of T. G. Kurtz for
density dependent population processes
that yields
convergence of
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
to
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
, where
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is the solution to the
modified Smoluchowski equations
(MSE)

{d\over dt}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)=-\zeta_{\bf x}\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}\,:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf z}}

with the same initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.

In Section
3.3
we will find the unique solution
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
of the above modified Smoluchowski equations.
Specifically, for a vector
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
let

\,{\bf x}!=x_{1}!x_{2}!\ldots x_{k}!\,

and for vectors
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
let

\,{\bf a}^{\bf b}=a_{1}^{b_{1}}a_{2}^{b_{2}}\ldots a_{k}^{b_{k}}\,
whenever
a_{i}^{b_{i}}
is uniquely defined for all
i
.
Now, consider a complete graph
K_{k}
consisting of vertices
\{1,\ldots,k\}
with weights
w_{i,j}=w_{j,i}\geq 0
assigned to
its edges
[i,j]
(
i\not=j
).
Let the weight
W(\mathcal{T})
of a spanning tree
\mathcal{T}
be the product of the weights of all of its edges.
Finally, let
\tau(K_{k},w_{i,j})=\sum\limits_{\mathcal{T}}W(\mathcal{T})
denote the
weighted spanning tree enumerator
,
i.e., the sum of weights of all spanning trees in
K_{k}
.
These notations are used in the following closed form expression, that will be established in Corollary
3.11
of Section
3.3

(15)

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}{\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}.

The concept of
gelation
was studied in
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
31
]
and related papers.
For the hydrodynamic limit
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
of the Marcus-Lushnikov process with vector-multiplicative kernel,
the
gelation time

T_{gel}
is the time after which the total mass
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\,
begins to dissipate, i.e.,
while the initial total mass was
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(0)|{\bf x}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle
,

T_{gel}=\inf\Big\{t>0\,:\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle<|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\Big\}.

In Section
4.1
we will use Corollary
2.6
of the Multidimensional Lambert-Euler inversion Theorem
1.1

for establishing
gelation
in the coagulation process with the vector-multiplicative kernel (
14
) and find the value of the
gelation time
.
Specifically, we will show that the gelation time equals to

T_{gel}={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

1.2.
Application in minimal spanning trees

Another application concerns the problem of finding the asymptotic mean length of the minimal spanning tree in a graph equipped with independent random edge lengths
as studied in
[
4
,
6
,
10
,
12
]
and related research works.
Let
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
denote a graph with
\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]|{\bf 1}\rangle=\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|{\bf 1}\rangle+o(\sqrt{n})

vertices divided into
k
partitions of respective sizes

\alpha_{1}[n],\ldots,\alpha_{k}[n],

where, each vertex in the
i
-th partition is connected with each vertex in the
j
-th partition by an edge if and only if
v_{i,j}=v_{j,i}>0
.
Even within an
i
-th partition, if
v_{i,i}>0
, a pair of vertices is connected by an edge.

Now, we equip the edges in the graph
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
with edge lengths as follows.
For each edge
e
connecting a vertex in the
i
-th partition with a vertex in the
j
-th partition
we have an associated random variable
\ell_{e}\sim{\sf Beta}\left(1,v_{i,j}\right)
, distributed on
(0,1)
via
the beta probability density function

f_{i,j}(x)=v_{i,j}(1-x)^{v_{i,j}-1},\qquad 0<x<1.

Random variables
\{\ell_{e}\}_{e}
are sampled independently.
Here,
\ell_{e}
represents the length of edge
e
.

The length of a tree graph is the sum of the lengths
\ell_{e}
of its edges.
Consider the minimal spanning tree of
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
, i.e., a spanning tree of
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
with the minimal length.
Let random variable
L_{n}
denote the length of the minimal spanning tree of
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
.
We are interested in finding the limit
\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]
.
Following the approach in
[
18
]
,
\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]
will be expressed using the closed form solution
(
15
) of the modified Smoluchowski equations.

The connection between the limit
\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]\,
and
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is established using the following framework.
First, we construct a random graph process by considering a “time” parameter
p\in[0,1]
, and declaring an edge
e
“open” if
\ell_{e}\leq p

and “closed” if
\ell_{e}>p
.
Thus, at time
p
, we have a graph consisting of
\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]|{\bf 1}\rangle
vertices and all open edges.
The random graph process
G(n,p)
describes the corresponding percolation dynamics on graph
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
equipped with edge lengths
\ell_{e}
.
Process
G(n,p)
partitions
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
into clusters of vertices connected by open edges at time
p
.

Notice that under the time change
p=1-e^{-t/n}
with
t\in[0,\infty)
, for an edge
e
connecting a vertex in the
i
-th partition with a vertex in the
j
-th partition we have

P(\ell_{e}\leq 1-e^{-t/n})=1-e^{-tv_{i,j}/n}.

Thus, in
G(n,1-e^{-t/n})
, an edge adjacent to a vertex in the
i
-th partition and a vertex in the
j
-th partition would open after waiting
for an exponentially distributed arrival time with parameter
v_{i,j}/n
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between connected clusters in
G(n,p)
and vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
.
Namely, a cluster with
x_{i}
vertices in the
i
-th partition is represented by a vector
{\bf x}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
with coordinates
x_{i}
.
Consider the random graph process
G(n,1-e^{-t/n})
.
For two clusters composed of two disjoint subsets of vertices in
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
, represented by vectors
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
,
the waiting time for the clusters to connect via an open edge will be
an exponential random variable with parameter
\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle/n
.
Hence, the merger dynamics of clusters in the random graph process
G(n,1-e^{-t/n})
matches the merger dynamics of clusters in
the vector-multiplicative coalescent process. That is, if we let
\xi_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
denote the number of clusters represented by vector
{\bf x}

in
G(n,1-e^{-t/n})
, then
\,\Big(\xi_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)\Big)_{\bf x}
is distributed as the Marcus-Lushnikov process

\,{\bf ML}_{n}(t)=\Big(\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)\Big)_{\bf x}
.

As
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
, and therefore,
\xi_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
converges weakly to
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
, the approach in
[
18
]
yields

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{\!{\bf x}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0~}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\,dt.

In Corollary
5.2
, the closed form expression (
15
) of the solution
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
to the modified Smoluchowski equations
is substituted, yielding the following general asymptotic equation

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0}{(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)!\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}{\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}\,\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle^{-\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle}.

Additionally, recalling a known correspondence between the gelation time
T_{gel}
in the Marcus-Lushnikov process and the time
p_{c}
of formation
of a giant component in
G(n,p)
, we have
p_{c}\sim 1-e^{-T_{gel}/n}\sim{T_{gel}\over n}
. Hence,

p_{c}\sim{1\over n\,\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

Finally, random graph processes
G(n,1-e^{-t/n})
have many features similar to the
inhomogeneous random graphs
formalism considered in
Söderberg
[
27
]
, Bollobás et all
[
5
]
, and related papers.
We hope that the results of this current manuscript can be used in the study of inhomogeneous random graphs

We conclude the introduction by noticing that the parts of the paper on Smoluchowski coagulation equations and on spanning trees
are tied to a number of interesting Abel’s type multinomial identities such as (

Additionally, in Lemma

\,\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{{\bf y}(t)\over t}={\boldsymbol{0}}

For

Next, for a given

\displaystyle{\partial F({\bf x};{\bf z})\over\partial{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=I-D\left[z_{j}e^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\rangle}\right]V.

For a given pair of vectors

The following lemma is instrumental for establishing uniqueness in Theorem

.

If

satisfy equation (
9
), then
{\bf y}={\bf z}
.

Proof.

We will prove this statement by contradiction. Suppose there is a pair
{\bf y},{\bf z}\in\overline{R}_{0}

satisfying
{\bf y}\not={\bf z}
and (
9
). Then,
F({\bf y};{\bf z})=0
.
Consider a set of indices
\,\mathcal{I}=\{i\,:\,y_{i}<z_{i}\}
, and let
\mathcal{I}^{c}=\{i\,:\,y_{i}\geq z_{i}\}
denote its complement.

Next, consider a point
{\bf x}^{*}
with coordinates

|{\bf x}^{*}\rangle=|{\bf z}\rangle+\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}(y_{i}-z_{i})|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle

and a path
{\bf x}(t),

\,t\in[0,1]
, connecting
{\bf z}
and
{\bf y}
defined as

(17)

|{\bf x}(t)\rangle=\begin{cases}|{\bf z}\rangle+2t|{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\rangle&\text{ for }t\in[0,1/2],\\
|{\bf x}^{*}\rangle+(2t-1)|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle&\text{ for }t\in[1/2,1].\end{cases}

Now, since
F({\bf y};{\bf z})=F({\bf z};{\bf z})=0
, then from (
2
) and (
17
) we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle F({\bf y};{\bf z})-F({\bf z};{\bf z})=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\big|dF({\bf x}(t);{\bf z})\big\rangle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\partial F({\bf x};{\bf z})\over\partial{\bf x}}~\Big|\,d{\bf x}(t)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}{\partial F({\bf x};{\bf z})\over\partial{\bf x}}~\Big|\,d{\bf x}(t)\Big\rangle+\int\limits_{1/2}^{1}{\partial F({\bf x};{\bf y})\over\partial{\bf x}}~\Big|\,d{\bf x}(t)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}\Big(I-D\left[z_{j}e^{2t\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\rangle}\right]V\Big)\,\Big|\,{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\Big\rangle\,dt+2\!\!\int\limits_{1/2}^{1}\Big(I-D\left[y_{j}e^{2(t-1)\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\right]V\Big)\,\Big|\,{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\Big\rangle\,dt

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad=|{\bf y}-{\bf z}\rangle-|\mathcal{E}_{I}\rangle-|\mathcal{E}_{II}\rangle,

where

(19)

|\mathcal{E}_{I}\rangle=2\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}D\left[z_{j}e^{2t\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\rangle}\right]V\,\Big|\,{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\Big\rangle\,dt=D[z_{j}a_{j}]V|{\bf x}^{*}-{\bf z}\rangle

with

a_{j}=\begin{cases}{1-e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\over\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}&\text{ if }j\in\mathcal{I},\\
1&\text{ if }j\in\mathcal{I}^{c},\end{cases}

and similarly,

(20)

|\mathcal{E}_{II}\rangle=2\!\!\int\limits_{1/2}^{1}D\left[y_{j}e^{2(t-1)\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\right]V\,\Big|\,{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\Big\rangle\,dt=D[y_{j}b_{j}]V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle

with

b_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j\in\mathcal{I},\\
{1-e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\over\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}&\text{ if }j\in\mathcal{I}^{c}.\end{cases}

Notice that for
j\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle>0
and
\,a_{j}={1-e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\over\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\in(0,1)
.
Similarly, for
j\in\mathcal{I}^{c}
, we have
\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle>0
and
\,b_{j}={1-e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\over\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle}\in(0,1)
.
Thus, by Proposition
2.1
,

(21)

\rho\left(D[z_{j}a_{j}]V\right)\leq\rho\left(D[z_{j}]V\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho\left(D[y_{j}b_{j}]V\right)\leq\rho\left(D[y_{j}]V\right),

where the first inequality is strict if
{\bf z}\not={\bf x}^{*}
and the second inequality is strict if
{\bf y}\not={\bf x}^{*}
.

Now, since
|\mathcal{E}_{I}\rangle
in (
19
) has all nonpositive coordinates
and
|\mathcal{E}_{II}\rangle
in (
20
) has all nonnegative coordinates, we have

\langle\mathcal{E}_{I}\,|\,\mathcal{E}_{II}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}_{II}\,|\,\mathcal{E}_{I}\rangle\leq 0.

Therefore, since
\rho\left(D[y_{j}]V\right)\leq 1
and either
{\bf y}\not={\bf x}^{*}
or
{\bf z}\not={\bf x}^{*}
or both hold,
equations (
19
), (
20
), and (
21
) imply

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{E}_{I}+\mathcal{E}_{II}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\mathcal{E}_{I}\big\|^{2}+\big\|\mathcal{E}_{II}\big\|^{2}~<\rho^{2}\!\left(D[z_{j}]V\right)\big\|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\big\|^{2}+\rho^{2}\!\left(D[y_{j}]V\right)\big\|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\big\|^{2}+\big\|{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\big\|^{2}~=\big\|{\bf y}-{\bf z}\big\|^{2}

as
\,\langle{\bf y}-{\bf x}^{*}\,|\,{\bf z}-{\bf x}^{*}\rangle=0
.
The contradiction to equation (
2
) follows.
∎

Let
|{\bf 1}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
denote the vector with all of its coordinates equal to
1
, and
let
|{\bf 0}\rangle
denote the vector of zeros.
For a vector
{\bf x}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
with coordinates
x_{i}
, let
|{\bf x}^{-1}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{-1}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
denote the vector with coordinates
x_{i}^{-1}
. Also, for vectors
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we will write
{\bf a}<{\bf b}
if
a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
i
.
Similarly, for matrices
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, we will write
A<B
if the inequality holds coordinate-wise.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any given
{\bf z}\in R_{1}
, there exists a vector
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\eta_{i}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
such that

0<\eta_{i}<1\qquad\forall i,

\rho\left(VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]\right)=1
, and

(22)

VD[z_{j}]|{\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\rangle=|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle-|{\bf 1}\rangle.

Proof.

Let
{\bf u}>{\boldsymbol{0}}
(i.e.,
u_{i}>0

\forall i
) be the Perron-Frobenius eigenvector of
VD[z_{j}]
, then since
\rho\left(VD[z_{j}]\right)>1
,
we have

\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]-I|{\bf u}\rangle~>0\quad\text{ for all }~i,

and therefore

(23)

\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf u}\rangle~>u_{i}\quad\text{ for all }~i.

Consider two sequences of vectors in
(0,\infty)^{k}
,
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}
and
{\bf w}^{(k)}
evolving according to the following recursion

(24)

\eta_{i}^{(k)}={1\over 1+\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}^{(k-1)}\rangle}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf w}^{(k)}={\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}.

Let the sequences begin with
{\bf w}^{(0)}=1-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(0)}=\varepsilon{\bf u}
with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that

{\bf w}^{(0)}<{\bf 1}
, and by (
23
),

\eta_{i}^{(1)}={1\over 1+\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}^{(0)}\rangle}=1-\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}^{(0)}\rangle+O(\varepsilon^{2})~<~1-w^{(0)}_{i}=\eta_{i}^{(0)}\qquad\text{ for all }~i.

Then,
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(0)}>{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(1)}
, and by (
24
),
{\bf w}^{(1)}>{\bf w}^{(0)}
, which in turn implies

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(1)}>{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(2)}
, and so on. Recursively obtaining

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}>{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k+1)}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bf w}^{(k+1)}>{\bf w}^{(k)}

for all
k=0,1,\ldots
. Hence, the limits

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}={\boldsymbol{\eta}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}{\bf w}^{(k)}={\bf w}

satisfy

(25)

{\boldsymbol{0}}<{\boldsymbol{\eta}}={\bf 1}-{\bf w}<{\bf 1}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\eta_{i}={1\over 1+\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}\rangle}\quad\text{ for all }~i.

Equation (
25
) implies

\eta_{i}\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}\rangle=1-\eta_{i}=w_{i}\quad\text{ for all }~i.

Thus,
\eta_{i}w_{i}^{-1}=\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}]|{\bf w}\rangle^{-1}=\langle{\bf e}_{i}|VD[z_{j}w_{j}]|{\bf 1}\rangle^{-1}\,
, and

(26)

D[\eta_{j}w_{j}^{-1}]VD[z_{j}w_{j}]|{\bf 1}\rangle=|{\bf 1}\rangle,

i.e., all the rows of
D[\eta_{j}w_{j}^{-1}]VD[z_{j}w_{j}]
add up to
1
.

For the vector
|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}\eta_{i}^{-1}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
,
equation (
26
) yields

VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle=D[w_{j}\eta_{j}^{-1}]D[\eta_{j}w_{j}^{-1}]VD[z_{j}w_{j}]|{\bf 1}\rangle=D[w_{j}\eta_{j}^{-1}]|{\bf 1}\rangle=|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle.

Hence, by Perron-Frobenius theorem,
1
is the Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue of
VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]
, the spectral radius
\rho\left(VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]\right)=1
,
and
|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle
is the corresponding Perron-Frobenius eigenvector.

Therefore, as
\,{\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}={\bf w}
,

VD[z_{j}]|{\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\rangle=VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle=|{\bf w}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle=|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle-|{\bf 1}\rangle

yielding the claim in (
22
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any given
{\bf z}\in R_{1}
, there exists a unique vector
{\bf y}\in R_{0}
such that (
9
) is satisfied.
Moreover,
{\bf y}<{\bf z}
.

Proof.

For
{\bf x}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
with coordinates
x_{i}
, let
|\ln{\bf x}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\ln x_{i}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
denote the vector with coordinates
\ln x_{i}
.
Consider function
f_{z}:(0,\infty)^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined as follows:

(27)

|f_{z}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})\rangle=|\ln{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\rangle+VD[z_{i}]|{\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\rangle,\qquad{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in(0,\infty)^{k}.

For the vector
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
in Lemma
2.4
, we have

|f_{z}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle=|\ln{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\rangle+VD[z_{i}]|{\bf 1}-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\rangle=-|\ln{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle+|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{-1}\rangle-|{\bf 1}\rangle\ =-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\ln\left(\eta_{i}^{-1}e^{1-\eta_{i}^{-1}}\right)|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle\,>\,{\bf 0}

since
\eta_{i}^{-1}>1

~\forall i~
and
xe^{1-x}<1
for all positive
x\not=1
.

Now, since
|f_{z}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle>{\boldsymbol{0}}
, by continuity of
f_{z}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})
, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
sufficiently small so that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=(1-\delta){\boldsymbol{\eta}}
satisfies

|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle>{\boldsymbol{0}}.

Notice that since
\rho\left(VD[z_{j}\eta_{j}]\right)=1
, we have
\rho\left(VD[z_{j}\widetilde{\eta}_{j}]\right)=1-\delta<1
.

Next, consider a smooth curve
{\bf x}(t)
in
(0,\infty)^{k}
that begins at
{\bf x}(0)=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
and evolves according to
the following differential equations:

(28)

{d\over dt}x_{i}(t)=-x_{i}(t)\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\langle{\bf e}_{i}|\big(VD[z_{j}x_{j}(t)]\big)^{m}|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle\qquad i=1,\ldots,k.

As each
x_{i}(t)
is monotone decreasing to
0
at exponentially fast rate as
t\to\infty
, and since
\rho\big(VD[z_{j}x_{j}(0)]\big)<1
,
by Prop.
2.1
, we have
\rho\big(VD[z_{j}x_{j}(t)]\big)<1
for all
t\geq 0
. Thus,

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\big(VD[z_{j}x_{j}(t)]\big)^{m}=\big(I-VD[z_{j}x_{j}(t)]\big)^{-1}

is well-defined for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, (
28
) yields

{d\over dt}|\ln{\bf x}(t)\rangle=-\big(I-VD[z_{j}x_{j}(t)]\big)^{-1}|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle

and

\displaystyle\big|f_{z}\big({\bf x}(t)\big)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle+|\ln{\bf x}(t)\rangle-|\ln\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\rangle+VD[z_{i}]|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-{\bf x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\Big(D\big[1/x_{j}(s)\big]-VD[z_{i}]\Big)\big|d{\bf x}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\big(I-VD[z_{j}x_{j}(s)]\big)|d\ln{\bf x}(s)\rangle

(29)

\displaystyle=|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle-\int\limits_{0}^{t}|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle ds~=(1-t)|f_{z}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\rangle.

Hence, substituting
t=1
into (
2
) yields

(30)

\big|f_{z}\big({\bf x}(1)\big)\big\rangle={\boldsymbol{0}},

where
{\bf x}(1)<{\bf x}(0)=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}<{\boldsymbol{\eta}}<{\bf 1}.

Next, we let
|{\bf y}\rangle=D[z_{j}]|{\bf x}(1)\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}x_{i}(1)|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle
, then by Prop.
2.1
,

\rho\big(VD[y_{j}]\big)=\rho\big(VD[z_{j}x_{j}(1)]\big)<\rho\left(VD[z_{j}\widetilde{\eta}_{j}]\right)<1,\quad\text{ i.e., }~{\bf y}\in R_{0}.

Also,
{\bf x}(1)<{\bf 1}
yields

{\bf y}<{\bf z}.

Finally, equations (
30
) and
x_{i}(1)=y_{i}z_{i}^{-1}
imply

{\boldsymbol{0}}=\big|f_{z}\big({\bf x}(1)\big)\big\rangle=|\ln{\bf x}(1)\rangle+VD[z_{i}]\big|{\bf 1}-{\bf x}(1)\big\rangle=|\ln{\bf y}\rangle-|\ln{\bf z}\rangle+V|{\bf z}-{\bf y}\rangle

arriving at

|\ln{\bf y}\rangle-V|{\bf y}\rangle=|\ln{\bf z}\rangle-V|{\bf z}\rangle,

equivalent to equation (
9
).
∎

2.2

Corollary 2.5

If
\rho\left(VD[z_{j}]\right)=1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The statement in Theorem
1.1

2.2
and

2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)

(a)

~~~{\bf y}(t)={\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\,

(b)

~~~{\bf y}(t)<{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\,

.

Notice that
\,\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{x(t)\over t}=0
for

in (
5

is analogous to the absorption of connected components of various sizes

7

We have the corresponding multidimensional result.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any given
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, let
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
be the minimal solution of (
12

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{{\bf y}(t)\over t}={\boldsymbol{0}}.

Proof.

Let
v_{i,j}=\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle
denote the entries in matrix
V
. Recall that
V
is nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix.
Thus,
v_{i,j}=v_{j,i}\geq 0
for all
i,j
.

Recall that
\,y_{i}(t)\leq\alpha_{i}t\,
for all
i
and all
t>0
.
Equation (
12
) implies

(31)

{y_{i}(t)\over t}=\alpha_{i}e^{-t\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-{\bf y}/t\rangle}\quad\text{ for all }~i=1,\ldots,k,

where
{\bf y}={\bf y}(t)
. First, we claim that

(32)

\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}{1\over t}\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}\rangle<\langle{\bf 1}|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle.

We prove (
32
) by contradiction as follows. Suppose, not. Then, there exists a sequence
t_{m}>0

increasing to
\infty
, such that

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}{1\over t_{m}}\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}(t_{m})\rangle=\langle{\bf 1}|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle.

Hence, for all

sufficiently large,
y_{i}(t_{m})>\alpha_{i}t_{m}/2
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Thus, since
{\bf y}(t)\in\overline{R}_{0}
, the spectral radius

1\geq\rho(VD[y_{j}])\geq{t_{m}\over 2}\rho(VD[\alpha_{j}])

by Prop.
2.1
, contradicting
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}t_{m}=\infty
.
Therefore, equation (
32
) holds.

By equation (
32
), there exists
\varepsilon>0
so small that it satisfies

(33)

\varepsilon<\min\limits_{j}\alpha_{j}\quad\text{ and }\quad\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}{1\over t}\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}\rangle<\langle{\bf 1}|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle-k\varepsilon.

Next, (
33
) implies the existence of
T>0
large enough so that whenever

we have

(34)

\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}e^{-v_{i,j}\varepsilon t}>\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }~i,j

and

(35)

\forall t>T\quad\exists j^{\prime}~~\text{ such that }~~~{y_{j^{\prime}}(t)\over t}<\alpha_{j^{\prime}}-\varepsilon.

Next, for a given

\,{y_{j}(t)\over t}<\alpha_{j}-\varepsilon\,
for some

, then for all

31
) and (

) yield

{y_{i}(t)\over t}=\alpha_{i}e^{-t\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-{\bf y}/t\rangle}\leq\alpha_{i}e^{-tv_{i,j}(\alpha_{j}-y_{j}/t)}<\alpha_{i}e^{-v_{i,j}\varepsilon t}<\alpha_{i}-\varepsilon.

Hence, by (
35
) and irreducibility of
V
, for all
t>T
, we have

(36)

{y_{i}(t)\over t}<\alpha_{i}-\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }~i.

Together, equations (
31
) and (
36
) imply

{y_{i}(t)\over t}\leq\alpha_{i}e^{-tv_{i,j}(\alpha_{j}-y_{j}/t)}<\alpha_{i}e^{-v_{i,j}\varepsilon t}\quad\text{ for all }~t>T\quad\text{ and all }~i,j.

Thus, by irreducibility of
V
, we have
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}{y_{i}(t)\over t}=0
exponentially fast for each
i=1,\ldots,k
.
∎

3.
Vector-Multiplicative Coalescent Processes

In this section we will analyze Smoluchowski coagulation equations (
37
) and modified Smoluchowski equations (
39
).
In Lemma
3.1
, we will show that equations (
39
) are a hydrodynamic limit of the Marcus-Lushnikov process
for the vector-multiplicative coalescent.
Our main result is in Subsection
3.3
, where we will use tools from combinatorics and linear algebra to find a complete solution to the modified Smoluchowski system of equations (
39
).

3.1.
Vector-Multiplicative Smoluchowski Equations

Consider a vector-multiplicative coalescent process introduced in Subsection
1.1
.
Let
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
be an averaged quantity that tracks the relative number of clusters of weight
{\bf x}
at tome
t\geq 0
.
Since the process evolves according to the merger rates
\,n^{-1}\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle
,
the
Smoluchowski coagulation system
of equations for the vector-multiplicative coalescent process is written as follows:

(37)

{d\over dt}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)=-\zeta_{\bf x}\sum_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf y}}\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}\,:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf z}}

with the initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.
Functions
\zeta_{\bf x}
are indexed by all weight vectors
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
satisfying
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0
.
This is also the domain for summation, i.e.,
\sum\limits_{\bf x}f({\bf x})=\!\!\sum\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0}f({\bf x})
.

Note that the initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
yield
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(0)|{\bf x}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle
.
Equation (
37
) implies

(38)

{d\over dt}\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle=-\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\sum_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf y}}\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf z}}|{\bf y}+{\bf z}\rangle=0

whenever the second order moments of the solutions
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
of (
37
) are convergent, i.e., the matrix of all second order moments

\,A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|
has all finite entries.

If we set the total mass constant by letting

\sum_{\bf y}\zeta_{\bf y}(t)|{\bf y}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle,\qquad\text{ where }~~\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k}),

in the right hand side of (
37
), then equation (
37
) will turn into the following quasilinear system of equations

(39)

{d\over dt}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)=-\zeta_{\bf x}\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}\,:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf z}},

with the same initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
as in (
37
).
Equations of the type in (
39
) are called
modified Smoluchowski equations
(MSE) or
Flory
system of equations.

Equation (
38
) implies that the solutions of Smoluchowski coagulation equations (
37
) and modified Smoluchowski equations (
39
)
will coincide as long as the second order moments
\,A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|
of the solutions
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
of (
37
) are convergent, i.e., for all
t
between
0
and
t_{c}
, where

t_{c}=\inf\Big\{t>0~:~\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|\text{ diverges }\Big\}.

3.2.
Marcus-Lushnikov process and hydrodynamic limit

Recall that
Marcus-Lushnikov process

{\bf ML}_{n}(t)
keeps track of cluster counts in the vector-multiplicative coalescent process that begins
with
\langle{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]|{\bf 1}\rangle
singletons of
k
types with
\alpha_{i}[n]
of type
i
for all
i
. Specifically, let
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)
denote the number of connected components of weight
{\bf x}
at time
t
. Then,

{\bf ML}_{n}(t)=\Big(\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(t)\Big)_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0}

with the starting values
\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}[n]\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.

Our next lemma states that the solution to the modified Smoluchowski coagulation system (
39
) is
the hydrodynamic limit of the Marcus-Lushnikov process
{\bf ML}_{n}(t)
with cross-multiplicative kernel.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any given
T>0
and all
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
satisfying
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0
,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{s\in[0,T]}\left|n^{-1}\zeta_{\bf x}^{[n]}(s)-\zeta_{\bf x}(s)\right|=0\qquad\text{ a.s.}

where
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is the solution of the modified Smoluchowski coagulation system (
39
)
with the initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.

Proof.

The proof is an application of the weak convergence results of T. G. Kurtz for
density dependent population processes
.
Namely, Theorem 2.1 in Chapter 11 of
[
8
]
, or equivalently, Theorem 8.1 in
[
20
]
.
This lemma follows immediately from the approach in Section 5 of
[
18
]

by replacing
V=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right]

with any other nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
∎

3.3.
Solving the Modified Smoluchowski Equations

Recall that

\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}=\alpha_{1}^{x_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{x_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{k}^{x_{k}}
.
The following proposition generalizes the approach in
[
18
,
24
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider

(40)

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}S_{\bf x}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}.

with
S_{\bf x}
solving the following reccursion

(41)

S_{\bf x}(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle S_{\bf y}S_{\bf z}

with the initial conditions
S_{{\bf e}_{j}}=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
.
Then,
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is the unique solution of MSE (
39
) with the initial conditions
\zeta_{\bf x}(0)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\delta_{{\bf e}_{i},{\bf x}}
.

Proof.

First we show that
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is a solution of (
39
).
Differentiating with respect to
t
yields

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}S_{\bf x}\left[e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-2}-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}S_{\bf x}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-2}-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\zeta_{\bf x}(t),

and for
{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)\zeta_{{\bf z}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{{\bf y}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{{\bf z}}S_{{\bf y}}S_{{\bf z}}e^{-(\langle{\bf y}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\langle{\bf z}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle)t}t^{\langle{\bf y}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle+\langle{\bf z}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}S_{{\bf y}}S_{{\bf z}}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-2}.

Plugging the above two equations into (
39
) yields (
41
).

Finally, the uniqueness of solution (
40
) of (
39
) follows from quasilinearity of (
39
).
∎

Next, we complete the solution of (
39
) by finding a combinatorial expression for
S_{\bf x}
in (
40
).
First, we need the following notations.

For a given
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}
satisfying
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle>0
, let
K_{\bf x}(V)
denote a graph equipped with
edge weights such that

•

\,K_{\bf x}(V)
is a complete graph with
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle
vertices; its vertices are
partitioned into
k
groups with the number of vertices in the
i
-th partition set equal to
x_{i}
, the
i
-th coefficient of the vector
{\bf x}
;

•

\,V
is the matrix of edge weights, i.e., the weight of an edge connecting a vertex in the
i
-th partition set with a vertex in the
j
-th partition set equals
v_{i,j}=v_{j,i}
.

Finally, if
\mathcal{T}
is a spanning tree of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
, then the weight of
\mathcal{T}
is the product of the weights of all of its edges.
Let
T_{\bf x}=T_{\bf x}(V)
denote the
weighted spanning tree enumerator
of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
, i.e.,
T_{\bf x}
is the sum of weights of all spanning trees of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
.
Now, for a graph consisting of just one vertex, the weighted spanning tree enumerator is set to be equal
1
. Thus,
T_{{\bf e}_{j}}=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T_{\bf x}
be the weighted spanning tree enumerator of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
. Then,

S_{\bf x}=\frac{T_{\bf x}}{{\bf x}!}\qquad\text{ where we denote }~~{\bf x}!=x_{1}!x_{2}!\ldots x_{k}!

is the solution to the recursion equation (
41
).

Proof.

Let us count the total weight
T_{\bf x}=T_{\bf x}(V)
of all spanning trees of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
. For a given
{\bf y}
and
{\bf z}
satisfying
{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}
, there are

{x_{1}\choose y_{1}}{x_{2}\choose y_{2}}\cdots{x_{k}\choose y_{k}}

ways of splitting
K_{\bf x}(V)
into
K_{\bf y}(V)
and
K_{\bf z}(V)
.
Each of the two subgraphs,
K_{\bf y}(V)
and
K_{\bf z}(V)
has the respective weighted spanning tree enumerators
T_{\bf y}
and
T_{\bf z}
.
For any given disection of
K_{\bf x}(V)
into
K_{\bf y}(V)
and
K_{\bf z}(V)
, the total weight of the edges connecting the two subgraphs equals
\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle
.
Now, there are
\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1
edges in every spanning tree of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
, and each edge splits the tree into two spanning trees,

K_{\bf y}(V)
and
K_{\bf z}(V)
.
Hence, the total weight
T_{\bf x}
of all spanning trees in
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
satisfies

(42)

T_{\bf x}=\frac{1}{2(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}{x_{1}\choose y_{1}}{x_{2}\choose y_{2}}\cdots{x_{k}\choose y_{k}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle T_{\bf y}T_{\bf z},

where the multiple of
{1\over 2}
accounts for double counting
{\bf y}+{\bf z}
splits with
{\bf z}+{\bf y}
splits.

Equation (
42
) can be rewritten as

(43)

{T_{\bf x}\over{\bf x}!}(\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle-1)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}:{\bf y}+{\bf z}={\bf x}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle{T_{\bf y}\over{\bf y}!}{T_{\bf z}\over{\bf z}!}

with the initial conditions
T_{{\bf e}_{j}}=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
.
Therefore, by the uniqueness of the solution of the recursive equation (
41
), we have
\,S_{\bf x}=\frac{T_{\bf x}}{{\bf x}!}
.
∎

Let
L_{\bf x}=L_{\bf x}(V)
denote the
weighted Laplacian matrix
of
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
, i.e.,

L_{\bf x}=\big(l_{r,s}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle\times\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle}

is a matrix with coordinates

(44)

l_{r,s}=\begin{cases}\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf x}\rangle-v_{i,i}&\text{ if }r=s,\,\text{ where }\,i=1+\max\{m:\,s_{m}<r\},\\
-v_{i,j}&\text{ if }r\not=s,\,\text{ where }\,i=1+\max\{m:\,s_{m}<r\},~j=1+\max\{m:\,s_{m}<s\},\end{cases}

where
s_{0}=0
, and
s_{m}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
for
m=1,\ldots,k
. Schematically,
L_{\bf x}
is represented as follows

\hskip 20.2355pt\overbrace{\hskip 108.405pt}^{x_{1}}~~~\overbrace{\hskip 158.99377pt}^{x_{2}}\enskip\dots\enskip\overbrace{\hskip 108.405pt}^{x_{k}}

L_{\bf x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|ccc|c|cc}\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{1,1}&-v_{1,1}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&\dots&-v_{1,k}&-v_{1,k}\\
-v_{1,1}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{1,1}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&\dots&-v_{1,k}&-v_{1,k}\\
\hline\cr-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,1}&\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{2,2}&-v_{2,2}&-v_{2,2}&\dots&-v_{2,k}&-v_{2,k}\\
-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,2}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{2,2}&-v_{2,2}&\dots&-v_{2,k}&-v_{2,k}\\
-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,2}&-v_{2,2}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{2,2}&\dots&-v_{2,k}&-v_{2,k}\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr-v_{k,1}&-v_{k,1}&-v_{k,2}&-v_{k,2}&-v_{k,2}&\dots&\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{k,k}&-v_{k,k}\\
-v_{k,1}&-v_{k,1}&-v_{k,2}&-v_{k,2}&-v_{k,2}&\dots&-v_{k,k}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{k,k}\end{array}\right]

Notice that for each
m=1,2,\ldots,k
and each
j=s_{m-1}+1,\ldots,s_{m}
, vector
|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle-|{\bf e}_{j+1}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle}
is an eigenvector of
L_{\bf x}

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle
. Hence,
\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle
is an eigenvalue of
L_{\bf x}
of multiplicity
x_{m}-1
.

The weighted spanning tree enumerator
T_{\bf x}=T_{\bf x}(V)
can be expressed via the celebrated Kirchhoff’s Weighted Matrix-Tree Theorem
[
16
,
17
,
23
]
as stated below.

Theorem 3.4

(Weighted Matrix-Tree Theorem)
.

For any
1\leq i,j\leq\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle
,

(45)

T_{\bf x}=(-1)^{i+j}\det\big[L_{\bf x}\big]_{i,j},

where
\big[L_{\bf x}\big]_{i,j}
denotes the
(i,j)
minor of
L_{\bf x}
obtained by removing the
i
-th row and
j
-th column in
L_{\bf x}
.

Observe that for a simple graph
G
with all edge weights
w_{i,j}=1
, the weighted spanning tree enumerator
\tau(G,w_{i,j})

counts the number of spanning trees in
G
.

Example 3.5
.

In the 1-D case (
k=1
),
\,T_{n}=n^{n-2}
is the number of spanning trees in a complete graph
K_{n}
, and equation (
42
)
turns into the following well known identity

(46)

T_{n}={1\over 2(n-1)}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}{n\choose m}m(n-m)T_{m}T_{n-m}.

On the other hand, as discovered in
[
24
]
,
S_{n}={n^{n-2}\over n!}
.
Thus, validating Lemma
3.3
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
V=|{\bf 1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}|-I
.
In the context of the vector-multiplicative coalescent processes, this is the case when only the pairs of particles of different types are allowed to bond,
each such pair bonding with rate
1/n
.
Then,
T_{\bf x}=T_{\bf x}(V)
is the number of spanning trees in a complete multipartite graph
\,K_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}
.
It was shown in
[
22
]
that the number of spanning trees in the complete multipartite graph equals

(47)

T_{\bf x}=n_{\bf x}^{k-2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n_{\bf x}-x_{i})^{x_{i}-1},\quad\text{ where }\quad n_{\bf x}=\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle.

For instance, in the 2-D case (
k=2
),
\,T_{x_{1},x_{2}}=x_{1}^{x_{2}-1}x_{2}^{x_{1}-1}
is the number of spanning trees in the complete bipartite graph
K_{x_{1},x_{2}}

with the partitions of sizes
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. Also, it was shown in
[
18
]
that the solution
S_{x_{1},x_{2}}
of (
41
) equals

S_{x_{1},x_{2}}={x_{1}^{x_{2}-1}x_{2}^{x_{1}-1}\over x_{1}!x_{2}!}
. Thus, Lemma
3.3
is validated for this case as well.

Weighted Matrix-Tree Theorem (Thm.
3.4
) was enhanced in S. Klee and M. T. Stamps
[
17
]
as follows.

Lemma 3.7

(Weighted Matrix-Tree Lemma,
[
17
]
)
.

For any given vectors
~{\bf a},{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle}
such that

\langle{\bf a}|{\bf 1}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i}a_{i}\not=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\langle{\bf b}|{\bf 1}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i}b_{i}\not=0,

the weighted spanning tree enumerator equals

(48)

T_{\bf x}={\det\Big(L_{\bf x}+|{\bf a}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|\,\Big)\over\langle{\bf a}|{\bf 1}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|{\bf 1}\rangle}.

Lemma
3.7
will be used in our solution for
T_{\bf x}(V)
in Thm.
3.8
.
Notice that in
\,K_{\bf x}(V)
, the total weight of all edges connecting
vertices in the
i
-th partition with the vertices in the
j
-th partition equals
x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j}
. Thinking of the
k
partitions as
k
vertices in
the
partition graph

K_{k}
, where vertex
i
and vertex
j
, representing the corresponding partitions, are connected by an edge of weight
x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j}
,
the weighted enumerator for the spanning trees on the partition graph equals

(49)

\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})=T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big).

Notice that, by Weighted Matrix-Tree Theorem (Thm.
3.4
),

(50)

T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big)=(-1)^{i+j}\det\big[L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\big]_{i,j}\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i,j\leq k,

where the weighted Laplacian for the partition graph equals

L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})=D[x_{i}]\Big(D[\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf x}\rangle]-VD[x_{i}]\Big).

Our next result reduces the computation of
T_{\bf x}(V)
from
(\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle-1)
-dimensional determinants as in (
46
) and (
48
), where
\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle
gets arbitrarily large, to just computing the
(k-1)
-dimensional determinant in (
50
).

Theorem 3.8

(Solution for
T_{\bf x}(V)
)
.

(51)

T_{\bf x}(V)={\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}.

Proof.

Recall that we let
s_{0}=0
, and
s_{m}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
for
m=1,\ldots,k
.
Set
|{\bf a}\rangle=\!\sum\limits_{j=s_{k-1}+1}^{s_{k}}\!\!|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle
in
\mathbb{R}^{\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle}
, i.e.,

\langle{\bf a}|=\big(0,\ldots,0,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{x_{k}}\big),

and let

\langle{\bf b}|=\big(\underbrace{v_{k,1},\ldots,v_{k,1}}_{x_{1}},~\underbrace{v_{k,2},\ldots,v_{k,2}}_{x_{2}},~\ldots,~\underbrace{v_{k,k},\ldots,v_{k,k}}_{x_{k}}\big).

Then, by Lemma
3.7
,

(52)

T_{\bf x}={\det\Big(L_{\bf x}+|{\bf a}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|\,\Big)\over\langle{\bf a}|{\bf 1}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|{\bf 1}\rangle}={\det\Big(L_{\bf x}+|{\bf a}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|\,\Big)\over x_{k}\langle{\bf e}_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle}.

On the other hand,

(53)

L_{\bf x}+|{\bf a}\rangle\langle{\bf b}|=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}&&&*&*&*\\
&Q&&*&*&*\\
&&&*&*&*\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0&\langle e_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&\langle e_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\end{array}\right],

where
s_{k-1}\times s_{k-1}
matrix
Q=\Big[~L_{\bf x}~\Big]_{[1..s_{k-1}]\times[1..s_{k-1}]}
is the restriction of the Laplacian matrix
L_{\bf x}
to the first
s_{k-1}
rows and columns.

Equations (
52
) and (
53
) yield

(54)

T_{\bf x}={\langle{\bf e}_{k}|V|{\bf x}\rangle^{x_{k}-1}\over x_{k}}\det(Q).

Recall the weighted Laplacian

L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})=D[x_{i}]\Big(D[\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf x}\rangle]-VD[x_{i}]\Big).

Therefore, by Thm.
3.4
, the weighted enumerator for the spanning trees on the partition graph equals

(55)

\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})=\det\big[L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\big]_{k,k}=\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}x_{j}\right)\det(\widetilde{Q}),

where
\,\widetilde{Q}=\Big[D[\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf x}\rangle]-VD[x_{i}]\Big]_{k,k}
is the
(k,k)
minor of
D[\langle{\bf e}_{i}|V|{\bf x}\rangle]-VD[x_{i}]
.

Next, we compare matrix

\widetilde{Q}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\langle{\bf e}_{1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle-x_{1}v_{1,1}&-x_{2}v_{1,2}&\dots&-x_{k-1}v_{1,k-1}\\
-x_{1}v_{2,1}&\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle-x_{2}v_{2,2}&\dots&x_{k-1}v_{2,k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-x_{1}v_{k-1,1}&-x_{2}v_{k-1,2}&\dots&\langle{\bf e}_{k-1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle-x_{k-1}v_{k-1,k-1}\end{array}\right]

to

\hskip 19.5132pt\overbrace{\hskip 108.405pt}^{x_{1}}~~~\overbrace{\hskip 104.79128pt}^{x_{2}}\enskip\dots\enskip\overbrace{\hskip 162.6075pt}^{x_{k-1}}

Q=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc|c|cc}\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{1,1}&-v_{1,1}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&\dots&-v_{1,k-1}&-v_{1,k-1}\\
-v_{1,1}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{1,1}&-v_{1,2}&-v_{1,2}&\dots&-v_{1,k-1}&-v_{1,k-1}\\
\hline\cr-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,1}&\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{2,2}&-v_{2,2}&\dots&-v_{2,k-1}&-v_{2,k-1}\\
-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,1}&-v_{2,2}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{2}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{2,2}&\dots&-v_{2,k-1}&-v_{2,k-1}\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\hline\cr-v_{k-1,1}&-v_{k-1,1}&-v_{k-1,2}&-v_{k-1,2}&\dots&\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{k-1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{k-1,k-1}&-v_{k-1,k-1}\\
-v_{k-1,1}&-v_{k-1,1}&-v_{k-1,2}&-v_{k-1,2}&\dots&-v_{k-1,k-1}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle{\bf e}_{k-1}|V|{\bf x}\rangle\!-\!v_{k-1,k-1}\end{array}\right]

First, we observe that if
|{\bf u}\rangle=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-1})^{T}
is a right eigenvector of
\widetilde{Q}
, then

\big(\underbrace{u_{1},\ldots,u_{1}}_{x_{1}},~\underbrace{u_{2},\ldots,u_{2}}_{x_{2}},~\ldots,~\underbrace{u_{k-1},\ldots,u_{k-1}}_{x_{k-1}}\big)^{T}

is an eigenvector of
Q
corresponding to the same eigenvalue.

Next, we find all
s_{k-1}\!-\!(k\!-\!1)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}(x_{m}-1)
remaining eigenvalues.
This is easy since for each
m=1,\ldots,k-1
and each
j=s_{m-1}\!+\!1,\ldots,s_{m}
,
vector
|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\!-\!|{\bf e}_{j+1}\rangle
in
\mathbb{R}^{s_{k-1}}
is an eigenvector of
Q

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle
.
Thus,
\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle
is an eigenvalue of
Q
of multiplicity
x_{m}-1
.

Therefore,

(56)

\det(Q)=\det(\widetilde{Q})\prod\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle^{x_{m}-1}.

Together, equations (
56
) and (
55
) imply

(57)

\det(Q)=T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}x_{j}^{-1}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{k-1}\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle^{x_{m}-1}.

Finally, substituting equation (
57
) into (
54
) yields

T_{\bf x}=T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}^{-1}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{k}\langle{\bf e}_{m}|V|{\bf x}\rangle^{x_{m}-1}={T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big)\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}.

Thus, by (
49
), the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Observe that Theorem
3.8
reduces the need for calculating
T_{\bf x}(\cdot)
for every
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}

to finding an expression for
T_{\bf 1}(\cdot)
, and substituting values of
{\bf x}
into
T_{\bf 1}\big(D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]\big)
.

Example 3.10
.

For a given vector
{\bf w}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
, let

V=|{\bf w}\rangle\langle{\bf w}|-D[w_{i}^{2}].

Then,
K_{\bf x}(V)
is a complete multipartite graph with weighted edges.
Now,

D[x_{i}]VD[x_{i}]=|{\bf xw}\rangle\langle{\bf xw}|-D[x_{i}^{2}w_{i}^{2}],

where

denotes the vector with coordinates
x_{i}w_{i}
.
Thus, the weighted Laplacian of the partition graph equals

L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})=\langle{\bf x}|{\bf w}\rangle D[x_{i}w_{i}]-|{\bf xw}\rangle\langle{\bf xw}|

and, by Lemma
3.7
,

(58)

\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})={\det\Big(L(x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})+|{\bf xw}\rangle\langle{\bf xw}|\Big)\over\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf xw}\rangle^{2}}={\bf x}^{\bf 1}{\bf w}^{\bf 1}\langle{\bf x}|{\bf w}\rangle^{k-2}.

Hence, by Theorem
3.8
, substituting (
58
) into (
51
) yields

(59)

T_{\bf x}={\bf w}^{\bf 1}\langle{\bf w}|{\bf x}\rangle^{k-2}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}.

Notice that letting
{\bf w}={\bf 1}
in (
59
) yields (
47
) as a special case.

Together, Proposition
3.2
, Lemma
3.3
, and Theorem
3.8
yield the following
general solution to the modified Smoluchowski equations (
39
).

Corollary 3.11
.

(60)

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}T_{\bf x}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}\quad\text{ with }\quad T_{\bf x}={\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}

is the unique solution of MSE (
39
).

]
.

4.
Gelation

In the vector-multiplicative processes the total mass
\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
is also a vector
with each coordinate being the corresponding component-vise total mass.
Consider the matrix of all second order moments
\,A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|
.
In this section, we will analyze
\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
and
A(t)
and establish gelation and find the gelation time.

4.1.
Divergence of second order moments

Let
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
be a solution to MSE (
39
).
Then, (
39
) implies
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\geq 0
for all
{\bf x}
and all
t\geq 0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

|M_{n}(t)\rangle=\sum\limits_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf x}\rangle\leq n}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{n}(t)=\sum\limits_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf x}\rangle\leq n}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|

be the partial sums for the vector series
\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
and matrix series
\,A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|
.
Equation (
39
) yields the following inequality

\displaystyle{d\over dt}|M_{n}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf x}\rangle\leq n}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}\,:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}+{\bf z}\rangle\leq n}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}\zeta_{{\bf z}}|{\bf y}+{\bf z}\rangle

\displaystyle=-A_{n}(t)V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}\,:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}+{\bf z}\rangle\leq n}\zeta_{{\bf y}}|{\bf y}\rangle\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf z}}

\displaystyle<-A_{n}(t)V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf y}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf y}\rangle\leq n\\
{\bf z}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf z}\rangle\leq n\end{subarray}}\zeta_{{\bf y}}|{\bf y}\rangle\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf z}}

\displaystyle=-A_{n}(t)V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle+A_{n}(t)V|M_{n}(t)\rangle

(62)

\displaystyle=-A_{n}(t)V\big|\boldsymbol{\alpha}-M_{n}(t)\big\rangle,

where
\,|M_{n}(0)\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\big\rangle
.
Inequality (
4.1
) implies

|M_{n}(t)\rangle\leq|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\big\rangle\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Therefore,

\sum\limits_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf 1}|{\bf x}\rangle\leq n}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\leq\langle{\bf 1}|M_{n}(t)\rangle\leq\langle{\bf 1}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\big\rangle

and series

\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\quad\text{ is convergent for all }~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in(0,\infty)^{k}~\text{ and all }\,t\geq 0.

Let
S({\bf z})=\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf z}^{\bf x}
be the generating function of
S_{\bf x}
defined as a
k
-dimensional power series.
Notice that by (
40
) we have

(63)

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)=\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}S_{\bf x}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}={1\over t}S_{\bf x}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\right)^{\bf x}={1\over t}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x},

where
\,|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle
.

Therefore, since

\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over t}\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x}

converges for all choices of
\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in(0,\infty)^{k}\,
and all
t>0
,
the series
\,\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x}\,

converges for all
{\bf w}
in the domain
\mathcal{D}
as in (
10
).
Hence, by (
11
), open set

\mathcal{D}_{0}=\left\{{\bf u}\in(0,\infty)^{k}~~:~~\exists{\bf z}\in R_{0}\quad\text{ such that }\quad|{\bf u}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}\,e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf z}\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle\right\}.

is a subset of the domain (interior region) of convergence of
S({\bf z})=\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf z}^{\bf x}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Solutions of (
37
) and (
39
) coincide for
\,0\leq t\leq{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
Moreover,

(64)

\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\quad\text{ for all }~0\leq t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

Proof.

Observe that for all multinomials
p({\bf x})=p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, the series
\sum\limits_{\bf x}p({\bf x})S_{\bf x}{\bf z}^{\bf x}

is convergent in the domain of convergence of
S({\bf z})=\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf z}^{\bf x}
.
Thus, by (
63
), series

\sum\limits_{\bf x}p({\bf x})\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over t}\sum\limits_{\bf x}p({\bf x})S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x}\quad\text{ with }\quad|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle

converges whenever
{\bf w}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. This happens when
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\in R_{0}
, i.e., when

t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

Since
p({\bf x})
can be taken to be quadratic, the matrix of all second order moments
\,A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|

is finite when
t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
Hence, equation (
38
) implies that
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle
for all
\,0\leq t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
∎

Additionally, equation (
37
) implies

\displaystyle{d\over dt}A(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)\zeta_{{\bf z}}(t)\Big(|{\bf y+z}\rangle\langle{\bf y+z}|\Big)-\sum_{{\bf x,y}}\langle{\bf x}|V|{\bf y}\rangle\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)\Big(|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)\zeta_{{\bf z}}(t)\Big(|{\bf y}\rangle\langle{\bf z}|+|{\bf z}\rangle\langle{\bf y}|\Big)~=\sum_{{\bf y},{\bf z}}\langle{\bf y}|V|{\bf z}\rangle\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)\zeta_{{\bf z}}(t)\Big(|{\bf y}\rangle\langle{\bf z}|\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\bf y}\zeta_{{\bf y}}(t)|{\bf y}\rangle\langle{\bf y}|\right)V\left(\sum_{\bf z}\zeta_{{\bf z}}(t)|{\bf z}\rangle\langle{\bf z}|\right)~=A(t)\,VA(t),

and therefore,

(65)

{d\over dt}\big(VA(t)\big)=\big(VA(t)\big)^{2}

with the initial conditions
A(0)=D[\alpha_{i}]
.
Note that we used finiteness of some third order moments of
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
. However, they should be finite for all positive
t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}

as
p({\bf x})
can be take to be a third degree multinomial.

Naturally, equation (
65
) has the following solution

(66)

A(t)=D[\alpha_{i}]\Big(I-tVD[\alpha_{i}]\Big)^{-1}

implying the explosive behavior of the second moments matrix
A(t)
as
\,t\uparrow{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.

4.2.
Gelation via mass conservation and mass dissipation

As a consequence of multidimensional Lambert-Euler inversion,

\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
can be expressed via the minimal solution
{\bf y}(t)
of (
12
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
denote the solution to the modified Smoluchowski equation (
39
) and
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
be the minimal solution of (
12
). Then,

\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle={1\over t}|{\bf y}(t)\rangle,\quad\text{ or equivalently, }\qquad\sum_{\bf x}x_{i}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={y_{i}(t)\over t}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,k).

Proof.

Consider the generating function
\,S({\bf z})=\sum\limits_{\bf x}S_{\bf x}{\bf z}^{\bf x}\,
of
\,S_{\bf x}\,
for
\,{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

in the domain of convergence.
Recall that, by (
63
), we have the following representation

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over t}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x},\quad\text{ where }\quad|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle.

Therefore,

\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\,x_{i}={1\over t}x_{i}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x}={1\over t}w_{i}{\partial\over\partial w_{i}}S_{\bf x}{\bf w}^{\bf x},

and

\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle={1\over t}D[w_{i}]\big|\nabla S({\bf w})\big\rangle\quad\text{ with the gradient of }\,S({\bf z})\,\text{ taken at }~~{\bf w}.

Thus, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

t|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle=D[w_{i}]\big|\nabla S({\bf w})\big\rangle,\quad\text{ where }\quad|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle,

for any choice of
\,{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}>{\bf 0}\,
and
\,0<t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.

Hence, for all
{\bf y}\in R_{0}
, we have

(67)

|{\bf y}\rangle=D[w_{i}]\big|\nabla S({\bf w})\big\rangle,\quad\text{ where }\quad|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle.

Next, for a given
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}>{\bf 0}\,
and
\,t\not={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
, let
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
be the minimal solution of (
12
).
Then, we have

\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle={1\over t}D[w_{i}]|\nabla S({\bf w})\rangle,

where, by (
12
),

|{\bf w}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}te^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}e^{-\langle{\bf e}_{j}|V|{\bf y}(t)\rangle}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle

and as
{\bf y}(t)\in R_{0}
, (
67
) yields

D[w_{i}]\big|\nabla S({\bf w})\big\rangle=|{\bf y}(t)\rangle.

Therefore,

\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle={1\over t}D[w_{i}]\big|\nabla S({\bf w})\big\rangle={1\over t}|{\bf y}(t)\rangle

affirming the statement of the lemma for
\,t\not={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.

Now, equation (
4.1
) implies that the partial sums
\,|M_{n}(t)\rangle\,
of
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\,
are decreasing,
and therefore,
\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
itself is coordinate-wise nonincreasing. Thus,
\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\leq|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle
, and by continuity of
{\bf y}(t)=\Lambda_{V}\circ\Psi_{V}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}t)
, we have

|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle=\lim\limits_{~~t\downarrow{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}}{1\over t}|{\bf y}(t)\rangle=\lim\limits_{~~t\downarrow{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}}\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle~\leq~\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}\left({1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}\right)\big|{\bf x}\big\rangle~\leq~|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle.

This yields
\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle=|{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle={1\over t}|{\bf y}(t)\rangle
for
\,t={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
∎

Gelation time
T_{gel}
is defined as the time until which the mass
\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle
is conserved, and after which,
the mass begins to dissipate coordinate-wise.

Definition 4.3
.

The gelation time is the infimum

T_{gel}=\inf\Big\{t>0\,:\,\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle~<~|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\Big\}.

Together, Corollary
2.6
and Lemma
4.2
imply the following.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
be the solution to the modified Smoluchowski equation (
39
). Then, gelation time equals

T_{gel}={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

Recall another critical time introduced in Subsection
3.1

t_{c}=\inf\Big\{t>0~:~A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|\text{ diverges }\Big\}.

From Lemma
4.2
and equation (
38
) we deduce the value of
t_{c}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

For
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
solving the modified Smoluchowski equation (
39
),

t_{c}={1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}.

Proof.

Equation (
38
) yields
\sum_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle
for all
t\in[0,t_{c})
.
Thus, Lemma
4.2
implies
\,t_{c}\leq{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
On the other hand, the second moment matrix series
A(t)=\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\langle{\bf x}|
is finite
for
\,t<{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
(recall (
66
) and its derivation), yielding
\,t_{c}\geq{1\over\rho(VD[\alpha_{i}])}
.
∎

Lemmas
2.7
and
4.2
yield another important corollary,
stating that the total mass
\,\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle\,
will eventually dissipate to nothing,
corresponding to a fact that all smaller clusters will be eventually absorbed by a giant component.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
be the solution to the modified Smoluchowski equation (
39
). Then,

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sum\limits_{\bf x}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)|{\bf x}\rangle={\boldsymbol{0}}.

5.
Application in Minimal Spanning Trees

For a nonnegative irreducible symmetric matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
and a vector
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in(0,\infty)^{k}
,
let
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]
be as in (
13
). Consider the graph
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
equipped with random edge lengths
\ell_{e}

as defined in Sect.
1.2
. Recall that the length of a tree is the sum of the lengths of the tree’s edges, and
let the random variable
L_{n}
denote the length of the minimal spanning tree of
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
.
We are interested in the asymptotic mean lengths of the minimal spanning tree of
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
as
n\to\infty
.
The following theorem follows immediately from Sect. 4.3 in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

(68)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{\bf x}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\zeta_{\bf x}(t)\,dt,

where
\zeta_{\bf x}(t)
is the solution of the modified Smoluchowski coagulation system (
39
).

Proof.

Follows immediately from the proof in Section 4.3 of
[
18
]
by replacing
V=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right]

with any other nonnegative irreducible symmetric
V\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.
∎

Applying Corollary
3.11
and Theorem
5.1
together results in the following closed form expression
for the limit
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

(69)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{\bf x}{(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1)!\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}T_{\bf x}\,\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle^{-\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle}

\text{ with }\quad T_{\bf x}={\tau(K_{k},x_{i}x_{j}v_{i,j})\over{\bf x}^{\bf 1}}(V{\bf x})^{{\bf x}-{\bf 1}}.

Proof.

Substituting

\,\zeta_{\bf x}(t)={1\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}T_{\bf x}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}\,

from equation (
60
) into equation (
68
) yields

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{\bf x}{1\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}T_{\bf x}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle t}t^{\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle-1}\,dt=\sum_{\bf x}{1\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}T_{\bf x}\,\langle{\bf x}|V|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle^{-\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle}\Gamma\big(\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle\big).

∎

Now, we will use the following example to validate the general formula (
69
) in Corollary
5.2
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
V=|{\bf 1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}|-I
, i.e.,
K_{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[n]}
is a complete multipartite graph with edge lengths
\ell_{e}

uniformly distributed on
(0,1)
.
Substituting (
47
) into (
69
) yields

(70)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]=\sum_{\bf x}{(n_{\bf x}-1)!\over{\bf x}!}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\bf x}n_{\bf x}^{k-2}\,\big(n_{\bf x}\langle{\bf 1}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle-\langle{\bf x}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rangle\big)^{-n_{\bf x}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n_{\bf x}-x_{i})^{x_{i}-1},

where
\,n_{\bf x}=\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle
.
In the equipartitioned case, when
\boldsymbol{\alpha}={\bf 1}
, equation (
70
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bf x}{n_{\bf x}!\over{\bf x}!}\,n_{\bf x}^{k-n_{\bf x}-3}\,(k-1)^{-n_{\bf x}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n_{\bf x}-x_{i})^{x_{i}-1}

(71)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k-n-3}\,(k-1)^{-n}\!\!\sum\limits_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle=n}{n!\over{\bf x}!}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-x_{i})^{x_{i}-1}

Now, by the Abel’s type multinomial identity from Abramson
[
1
]
, we have

(72)

\sum\limits_{{\bf x}:\langle{\bf x}|{\bf 1}\rangle=n}{n!\over{\bf x}!}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-x_{i})^{x_{i}-1}=k(k-1)^{n-1}n^{n-k}.

Substituting (
72
) into (
5.3
), we get

(73)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]={k\over k-1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-3}={k\over k-1}\zeta(3),

where
\zeta(n)
is Riemann zeta function.
Equation (
73
) matches the general expression for the asymptotic limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{n}]
for regular graphs with i.i.d. uniformly distributed edge lengths as derived in Beveridge et al
[
4
]
,
thus, validating the general formula (
69
) in Corollary
5.2
.

Critical phase induced by Berry phase and dissipation in a spin chain
Simon Martin, Tarun Grover

Abstract
Motivated by experiments on spin chains embedded in a metallic bath, as well as closed quantum systems described by long-range interacting Hamiltonians, we study a critical SU ( N ) (N) spin chain perturbed by dissipation, or equivalently, after space-time rotation, long-range spatial interactions. The interplay of dissipation and the Wess-Zumino (Berry phase) term results in a rich phase diagram with multiple renormalization-group fixed points. For a range of the exponent that characterizes the dissipative bath, we find a second-order phase transition between the fixed point that describes an isolated critical spin chain and a dissipation-induced-ordered phase. More interestingly, for a different range of the exponent, we find a stable, gapless, nonrelativistic phase of matter whose existence necessarily requires coupling to the dissipative bath. Upon tuning the exponent, we find that the fixed point corresponding to this gapless, stable phase “annihilates” the fixed point that describes the transition out of this phase to the ordered phase. We also study a relativistic version of our model, and we identify a new critical point. We discuss the implications of our work for Kondo lattice systems and engineered long-range interacting quantum systems.

II
Model and its symmetries

We will first consider a nonrelativistic setup where dissipation induces interactions that are nonlocal only in time, analogous to the standard Hertz-Millis theory for antiferromagnets
Hertz 1976
;
Millis 1993
(the induced nonlocality in space due to dissipative bath is assumed to be subleading compared to the spatial kinetic energy term, and hence neglected
Danu et al. 2022
). We consider a system which in the absence of dissipation is described by the 1+1-D SU
(N)_{k}
WZW CFT
Wess and Zumino 1971
;
Novikov 1981
;
Witten 1983
;
Witten 1984
. The (Euclidean) action is

S[g]=S_{\text{Grad}}[g]+S_{\text{WZ}}[g]+S_{\text{Dis}}[g].

(1)

In this equation

S_{\text{Grad}}[g]=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{\tau}g\partial_{\tau}g^{-1}+\partial_{x}g\partial_{x}g^{-1}\right)

(2)

is the standard kinetic energy term for the matrix-valued field
g\in\text{SU}(N)
, transforming in the bifundamental represenation of SU
(N)_{L}

\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
.
c
is a velocity which will run under RG as discussed below. Next,

S_{\text{WZ}}[g]=\frac{ik}{12\pi}\int_{B^{3}}d\tau\,dx\,du\,\epsilon^{ijk}\tr\left(\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{i}\tilde{g}\,\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{j}\tilde{g}\,\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{k}\tilde{g}\right)

(3)

is the Wess-Zumino (WZ) Berry-phase term, defined in terms of
\tilde{g}(\tau,x,u)
which is an extension of the field
g(\tau,x)
to a three-ball
B^{3}
so that
\tilde{g}(\tau,x,u=0)=g_{0}
is any chosen reference value, and
\tilde{g}(\tau,x,u=1)=g(\tau,x)
is the physical value of
g
at
(\tau,x)
(= boundary
S^{2}
of
B^{3}
). Finally,

S_{\text{Dis}}[g]=k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr[\mathds{1}-g(\tau,x)g^{-1}(\tau^{\prime},x)]\,,

(4)

where
\gamma>0
, is the dissipation term. The kernel
K
is defined as
K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=\frac{A}{|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{3-\delta}}
with the normalization
\quad A=\frac{(\delta-2)}{16\pi\Gamma(\delta-1)\cos(\pi\delta/2)}
chosen so that the Fourier transform
\tilde{K}(\omega)
of
K(\tau)
has a simple form suited for our RG analysis. We restrict
\delta
to the range
0<\delta<2
so that the Fourier transform
\tilde{K}(\omega)
of
K(\tau)
goes to zero as
\omega\rightarrow 0
, and
1/A
is not divergent. The global continuous symmetry of this model is SU
(N)_{L}

\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
where under SU
(N)_{L}
,
g\rightarrow Ug
, and under SU
(N)_{R}
,
g\rightarrow gV
, where
U,V
are arbitrary SU
(N)
matrices. Since
S_{\text{Grad}}[g]+S_{\text{WZ}}[g]
is Lorentz invariant, after interchanging space and imaginary time, the action
S[g]
describes a nondissipative closed system with long-range spatial interactions (a dynamical exponent
z
in the dissipative system corresponds to a dynamic exponent
1/z
in its space-time interchanged counterpart).

The exponent
3-\delta
for the kernel
K(\tau)
is chosen so that
\delta=\tilde{\delta}/k\ll 1
, with
\tilde{\delta}
an
\mathcal{O}(1)
number, allows for a controlled
1/k
expansion. Relatedly, the couplings
\lambda
and
\gamma
will be of the order
1/k
at all the RG fixed points, which implies that the three terms in the action
S[g]
all scale as
k
. It will be useful to introduce the
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
couplings
\tilde{\lambda}=k\lambda
and
\tilde{\gamma}=k\gamma
. The dynamical exponent
z
will be defined as part of the RG scheme, and will deviate from unity only by
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
, and therefore we also introduce an
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
variable
\tilde{z}
such that
z=1+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}
.

Renormalization Group

To set up our RG calculation, we decompose the matrix-valued field
g
as
g=g_{s}e^{W}
, where

denotes “slow” variables, and

denotes “fast” variables

and

,

(5)

\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[(\tilde{\delta}-\tilde{z})\tilde{\gamma}-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}c\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}w\Bigg]\,,

(6)

(7)

;

;

;

2.

When
\tilde{\delta}>\tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\frac{(4C_{F}+N)^{3}}{N}}

\tilde{\gamma}

3.

Most interestingly, in the intermediate regime, namely, when
4C_{F}<\tilde{\delta}<\tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}

\otimes

(N)_{R}

\tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}

We note a technical subtlety about our RG calculation: the total action
S[g]
respects the discrete symmetry
g(\tau,x)\rightarrow g^{-1}(\tau,-x)
which rules out terms such as
\int d\tau dx\tr\left(\partial_{\tau}g\partial_{x}g^{-1}\right)
. However, the aforementioned decomposition
g=g_{s}e^{W}
“fractionalizes” the action of this discrete symmetry, and integrating out
W
can and does generate an unphysical term

\int d\tau dx\tr\left(\partial_{\tau}g_{s}\partial_{x}g_{s}^{-1}\right)
which should be discarded on symmetry grounds. One way to keep the symmetry manifest is by defining a symmetrized effective action
S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]=\frac{1}{2}\big(S_{\text{Eff}}^{(1)}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Eff}}^{(2)}[g_{s}]\big)
, where
(1)
and
(2)
correspond to the following two decompositions:
g=g_{s}e^{W}
and
g=e^{W}g_{s}
. The two decompositions yield exactly the same RG for all the physical (i.e. symmetry allowed) terms, while the aforementioned unphysical term has a relative opposite sign. Due to this,
S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]
only contains terms allowed by symmetries.

IV
A Relativistic version

As mentioned in the introduction, we also study a relativistic-invariant version of our model. The kinetic energy term and the WZW term are unchanged (we set
c=1
), while the dissipation is now chosen as Lorentz invariant

S_{\text{Dis}}=k^{2}\gamma\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r^{\prime}}\,K(|{\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r^{\prime}}|)\tr\left(\mathds{1}-g({\bf\it r})g^{-1}({\bf\it r^{\prime}})\right)\,,

(10)

where
{\bf\it r}=(\tau,x)
denotes Euclidean space-time, and the kernel is now
K(r)=\frac{B}{r^{4-\delta}}
with
B=-\frac{1}{2^{1+\delta}\pi^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(2-\delta/2)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{\delta}{2}-1\big)}
and
r=|{\bf\it r}|
. The normalization of the kernel is such that its Fourier transform is
\tilde{K}(p)=-\frac{1}{8\pi}|p|^{2-\delta}
, with
p=|{\bf\it p}|
,
{\bf\it p}=(\omega,q)
.

The RG analysis can be carried out using a scheme similar to that for the nonrelativisitic case (See Appendix
B
). It will again be useful to introduce
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
couplings
\tilde{\lambda}=k\lambda
and
\tilde{\gamma}=k\gamma
. The corresponding
\beta
functions to the leading order in
1/k
are:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(\tilde{\lambda})&=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\frac{N\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{8\pi}\Bigg(1-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})-\frac{C_{F}}{16\pi^{2}}\tilde{\lambda}^{3}\tilde{\gamma}F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\lambda}^{4}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})+\frac{N}{32\pi^{2}}\tilde{\lambda}^{3}\tilde{\gamma}F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(11)

\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\tilde{\delta}\tilde{\gamma}-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg]\,,

(12)

with
F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{8\pi}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}}
.

In contrast to the nonrelativistic case, we now find only two qualitatively different phase diagrams as a function of

, as illustrated by the RG flows in Fig.

fixed point (which presumably again corresponds to the symmetry-broken phase) by a single-parameter tuned quantum phase transition, while when
\tilde{\delta}>4C_{F}
, the WZW fixed point is unstable towards the large
\tilde{\gamma}
fixed point at infinitesimal dissipation. Furthermore, we find the following scaling dimensions for the primary field
g
and the energy density operator
\epsilon
at the dissipative fixed point:
\Delta_{g}=\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2k},\Delta_{\epsilon}=2+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{64C_{F}^{3}k}\Bigg[N\tilde{\delta}^{2}-\sqrt{N(1024C_{F}^{5}-64C_{F}^{3}\tilde{\delta}^{2}+N\tilde{\delta}^{4})}\Bigg]
. The scaling dimensions at the WZW fixed point of course match the known exact results in the large-
k
limit, namely,
\Delta_{g}=2C_{F}/k,\Delta_{\epsilon}=2+2N/k
. Analogous to the long-range Ising or O(
N
) models
Paulos et al. 2016
, it will be interesting to explore whether these theories potentially correspond to an infinite number of new conformal field theories labeled by the integers
(N,k)
.

V
Summary and discussion

We carried out an RG study of a class of (1+1)-D CFTs perturbed by long-range interactions along space and/or time, and we identified several RG fixed points (see Fig.
1
). For a range of the exponent
\delta
that characterizes long-range interactions, we found that the CFT becomes unstable towards a stable, gapless dissipative phase that exhibits non-trivial scaling both along space and time. Upon tuning
\delta
, one encounters a fixed-point annihilation between the fixed point corresponding to the aforementioned stable, gapless phase, and another dissipative fixed point with one relevant direction. Compared to relativistic systems with long-range interactions and no WZW term
Fisher et al. 1972
;
Sak 1973
;
Sak 1977
;
Bhattacharjee et al. 1982
;
Paulos et al. 2016
;
Behan et al. 2017a
;
Behan et al. 2017b
;
Defenu et al. 2017
;
Slade 2018
;
Gubser et al. 2019
;
Defenu et al. 2020
;
Chakraborty and Goykhman 2021
;
Chai et al. 2021
;
Chai et al. 2022
, the novelty here is the presence of an intermediate coupling stable phase. We characterized this critical phase via the scaling dimensions of a few prominent operators and the dynamical critical exponent. We also studied a relativistic version of our theory that shows a novel quantum critical point between the WZW CFT and a dissipation-induced symmetry-broken phase (see Fig.
4
).

It is important to ask what lattice models may give rise to the non-trivial intermediate coupling dissipative phase (Fig.
1
(b)). In our analysis we assumed SU
(N)_{L}\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
symmetry at low energies which may be difficult to achieve starting from a lattice model. Although one can certainly find fine-tuned lattice models that realize SU
(N)_{k}
CFTs for any
N,k

Babujian 1982
;
Babujian 1983
;
Takhtajan 1982
, when
k>1
there generically exist relevant terms that explicitly break the SU
(N)_{L}\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
symmetry down to the diagonal SU
(N)

Affleck and Haldane 1987
. A natural way to realize SU
(N)_{L}\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
without any fine-tuning is to consider a spin chain that corresponds to the edge mode of a 2D symmetry protected topological (SPT) phase
Liu and Wen 2013
. Further, 1+1-D models with
k=1
for any
N
are also stable (assuming translation symmetry) since anomaly-based arguments imply that under RG flow the parity of the level cannot change
Gepner and Witten 1986
;
Furuya and Oshikawa 2017
;
Yao et al. 2019
. On that note, for a single impurity coupled to a dissipative bath, one also finds a phase diagram broadly similar to our problem
Cuomo et al. 2022
;
Nahum 2022
;
Beccaria et al. 2022
;
Weber and Vojta 2023
;
Hu and Si 2022
, and although the corresponding calculation is justified only in a semiclassical limit somewhat analogous to ours (large spin
S
for a single impurity versus large level
k
for WZW CFT), numerical studies have shown that the qualitative aspects carry over even to spin-1/2 impurities
Weber and Vojta 2023
;
Hu and Si 2022
. Therefore, it will be interesting to explore the effect of long-range interactions on (1+1)-D lattice models corresponding to SU
(N)_{k}
CFTs even at
k=1
using quantum Monte Carlo (QMC)
Werner et al. 2005a
;
Laflorencie et al. 2005
;
Sperstad et al. 2012
;
Weber et al. 2022
;
Song et al. 2023
;
Zhao et al. 2023
, or in engineered systems
Toskovic et al. 2016
;
Choi et al. 2017
;
Moro-Lagares et al. 2019
;
Choi et al. 2019
;
Richerme et al. 2014
;
Jurcevic et al. 2014
;
Britton et al. 2012
;
Neyenhuis et al. 2017
;
Liu et al. 2019
. Another direction worth exploring is the potential relation to models of deconfined quantum critical points that also have WZW terms and show fixed-point annihilation in fractional dimensions
Nahum 2020
;
Ma and Wang 2020
.

Returning to the topic of hybrid-dimensionality Kondo lattice models, we speculate that the dissipative phase can potentially be a novel “fractionalized Fermi liquid” with a small Fermi surface. This is because the physics of Kondo singlet formation, and relatedly, that of a “large Fermi surface” heavy Fermi liquid phase
Oshikawa 2000
where local moments contribute to the Fermi surface volume, is non-perturbative in the Kondo coupling
J_{K}
with an effective energy scale
e^{-c/J_{K}}
, where
c
is a constant. If one imagines that our action
S[g]
was obtained by integrating out a fermionic bath, then such physics is likely not operative in the dissipative phase since the Kondo coupling
J_{K}
appears only perturbatively (with dissipation
\gamma\sim J^{2}_{K}
). In contrast to the “conventional” small Fermi surface fractionalized phases
Senthil et al. 2003
;
Senthil et al. 2004
, in such a dissipation-induced non-Fermi-liquid, here the electrons and spins do not completely decouple at low energies since non-zero dissipation must imply non-trivial entanglement between spins and electrons. At the same time, one can still inquire whether the fixed points we obtained are
perturbatively
stable against flow to a large Fermi surface phase. For example, as discussed in Ref.
Danu et al. 2020
, for a spin chain embedded in a Dirac semi-metal, the electronic bath completely decouples from the spin chain at weak Kondo coupling, resulting in a hybrid-dimensionality small-Fermi-surface fractionalized Fermi liquid
Senthil et al. 2003
;
Senthil et al. 2004
. Another example is provided by “Fermi-Bose Kondo impurity” models
Smith and Si 1999
;
Sengupta 2000
;
Vojta et al. 2000
;
Zhu and Si 2002
;
Zaránd and Demler 2002
, where one finds an intermediate dissipation fixed point which is again stable against Kondo singlet formation with the fermionic bath
Hu and Si 2022
. The existence of either of these fixed points can be inferred solely using a dissipative bosonic bath similar to our calculation
Cuomo et al. 2022
;
Nahum 2022
;
Beccaria et al. 2022
;
Weber and Vojta 2023
. In a similar vein, we expect that the WZW CFT fixed point (Fig.
1
(a)), and more interestingly, the stable, dissipative fixed point (Fig.
1
(b)) are also both stable against flow towards a large Fermi surface phase. The heuristic reasoning behind this expectation is that perturbatively, the dissipation coefficient
\gamma
is proportional to
J^{2}_{K}
, and since the RG flow at either of these fixed points is attractive along the
\gamma
direction, one expects that it will be attractive along the
J_{K}
direction as well. We leave the further exploration of this topic to the future.

Appendix A
RG analysis of the nonrelativistic theory

This Appendix presents the detailed RG calculation of the nonrelativistic theory presented in the main text.

A.1
Expanding in slow/fast modes

The RG calculation is performed by splitting
g
into slow and fast degrees of freedom:
g(\tau,x)=g_{s}(\tau,x)g_{f}(\tau,x)
, where
g_{s}
is a slow-varying background field, while
g_{f}
constitutes fast fluctuations about
g_{s}

Witten 1984
. The goal is to obtain the effective action for the slow fields
g_{s}
due to the integration of the fast modes
g_{f}
.
g_{f}
is thus expanded to quadratic order with the following decomposition:

g_{f}=e^{W}\approx\mathds{1}+W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}+...\,,

(13)

with
W(\tau,x)=iT^{a}\phi^{a}(\tau,x)
, where
T^{a}
are the
N^{2}-1
generators of
\text{SU}(N)
in the fundamental representation, which respect the algebra
[T^{a},T^{b}]=if^{abc}T^{c}
and are normalized according to
\tr(T^a T^b)=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{ab}
, while
\phi^{a}
are
N^{2}-1
real scalar fields. Below we analyze the three terms in the action
S[g]=S_{\text{Grad}}[g]+S_{\text{WZ}}[g]+S_{\text{Dis}}[g]
with such a decomposition. Overall, the main simplification in the large
k
limit is that at each order in
1/k
, there are only a finite number of Feynman diagrams that contribute to the RG flow, as explained in Appendix
A.3
.

A.1.1
Gradient term

Let us start with the gradient term. For
\mu=\tau
or
\mu=x
(no sum over
\mu
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g \partial_{\mu} g^{-1} \Big)&=\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} (g_s g_f) \partial_{\mu} (g_f^{-1} g_s^{-1}) \Big)\\
&=\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s g_f \partial_{\mu} g_f^{-1} g_s^{-1} + \partial_{\mu} g_s g_f g_f^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} + g_s \partial_{\mu} g_f \partial_{\mu} g_f^{-1} g_s^{-1} + g_s \partial_{\mu} g_f g_f^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&=\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)+\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_f \partial_{\mu} g_f^{-1} \Big)+2\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s g_f \partial_{\mu} g_f^{-1} \Big)\,,\end{split}

(14)

where we have used the fact that
g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}=g_{f}g_{f}^{-1}=\mathds{1}
, which implies that
\partial_{\mu}g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}=-g_{s}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}^{-1}
(same thing for
g_{f}
). Expanding the second term to quadratic order in
W
yields

\tr[\partial_{\mu}g_{f}\partial_{\mu}g_{f}^{-1}\big]=-\tr[\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\mu}W\big]+\mathcal{O}(W^{3})\,.

(15)

For the third term, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s g_f \partial_{\mu} g_f^{-1} \Big)&=2\tr[g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}\Big(\mathds{1}+W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}\Big)\partial_{\mu}\Big(\mathds{1}-W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big]+...\\
&=2\tr[g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{2}W\partial_{\mu}W+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}WW-W\partial_{\mu}W\Big)\Big]+\text{Terms linear in }W+\mathcal{O}(W^{2})\\
&=\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s [\partial_{\mu}W,W] \Big)+...\,,\end{split}

(16)

where the terms linear in
W
can be dropped, since these will yield vanishing contributions when computing loop diagrams over fast modes (no momentum exchange between slow and fast modes is compatible with momentum conservation).

Therefore, using the results derived above, the gradient term becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Grad}}[g_{s}g_{f}]=S_{\text{Grad}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Grad}}^{(2)}[W]+S_{\text{Int,Grad}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]\,,\end{split}

(17)

with

S_{\text{Grad}}[g_{s}]=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} + \partial_x g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1} \Bigg)\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Grad}}^{(2)}[W]&=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau} W \partial_{\tau} W + \partial_x W \partial_x W \Bigg)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\Pi^{-1}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)\,,\quad\Pi(\omega,q)=\frac{\lambda}{\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+q^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(19)

S_{\text{Int,Grad}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} g_s^{-1}\partial_{\tau} g_s [\partial_{\tau} W,W] + g_s^{-1}\partial_x g_s [\partial_x W,W] \Bigg)\,.

(20)

Note that the second term has been written in Fourier space, after having taken the trace over the generators. This term will contribute to the fast propagator.

A.1.2
WZ term

Let us now split the degrees of freedom in the WZ term. To do so, note that

g^{-1}dg=g_{f}^{-1}g_{s}^{-1}d(g_{s}g_{f}^{-1})=g_{f}^{-1}g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}g_{f}+g_{f}^{-1}dg_{f}\,.

(21)

Therefore, the trace becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tr[g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\Big]\\
&=\tr[\Big(g_{f}^{-1}g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}g_{f}+g_{f}^{-1}dg_{f}\Big)\wedge\Big(g_{f}^{-1}g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}g_{f}+g_{f}^{-1}dg_{f}\Big)\wedge\Big(g_{f}^{-1}g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}g_{f}+g_{f}^{-1}dg_{f}\Big)\Big]\,.\end{split}

(22)

Expanding this expression yields eight terms, which can be combined to give

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr[g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\Big]&=\tr[g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\Big]+3\tr[dg_{s}^{-1}\wedge dg_{s}\wedge g_{f}dg_{f}^{-1}\Big]\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-3\tr[g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge dg_{f}\wedge dg_{f}^{-1}\Big]+\mathcal{O}(W^{3})\,.\end{split}

(23)

Expanding the second term to quadratic order in
W
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}3\tr[dg_{s}^{-1}\wedge dg_{s}\wedge g_{f}dg_{f}^{-1}\Big]&\approx 3\tr[dg_{s}^{-1}\wedge dg_{s}\wedge\Big(\mathds{1}+W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}\Big)d\Big(\mathds{1}-W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big]\\
&=3\tr\Big( dg_s^{-1} \wedge dg_s \wedge\frac{1}{2} [dW,W] \Big)+\text{Linear term in }W+\mathcal{O}(W^{3})\,,\end{split}

(24)

while we get for the third term

\displaystyle\begin{split}-3\tr[g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge dg_{f}\wedge dg_{f}^{-1}\Big]=3\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} d g_s \wedge dW \wedge dW \Big)+\mathcal{O}(W^{3})\,.\end{split}

(25)

Hence, combining everything leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr[g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\wedge g^{-1}dg\Big]&=\tr[g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\Big]+\frac{3}{2}\tr\Big( dg_s^{-1} \wedge dg_s \wedge[dW,W] \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+3\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} d g_s \wedge dW \wedge dW \Big)\\
&=\tr[g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\Big]+\frac{3}{2}\tr\,d\Big(g_{s}^{-1}dg_{s}\wedge[dW,W]\Big)\,,\end{split}

(26)

where the second and the third terms have been combined in a total derivative in the last step. Hence, applying Stoke’s theorem, the WZ action becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}g_{f}]&=S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Int,WZ}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]\\
&=S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]+\frac{ik}{8\pi}\int d\tau dx\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s [\partial_{\nu}W,W] \Big)\,.\end{split}

(27)

The relativistic notation
\mu=(\tau,x)
is used here.

A.1.3
Dissipation term

Finally, we focus on the dissipation term. The trace becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g g^{\prime\, -1}\Big)&=\tr\big( \mathds{1} - g_s g_f g_f^{\prime\, -1} g_s^{\prime\, -1} \big)\\
&\approx\tr[\mathds{1}-g_{s}\Big(\mathds{1}+W+\frac{W^{2}}{2}\Big)\Big(\mathds{1}-W^{\prime}+\frac{W^{\prime 2}}{2}\Big)g_{s}^{\prime\,-1}\Bigg]\\
&=\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)-\tr\Bigg( \frac{W^2}{2} + \frac{W'^2}{2} - W W' \Bigg)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\tr[\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{\prime\,-1}g_{s}\Big)\Bigg(\frac{W^{2}}{2}+\frac{W^{\prime 2}}{2}-WW^{\prime}\Bigg)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(W^{3})\,,\end{split}

(28)

where a prime means evaluated at
(\tau^{\prime},x)
. Once again, the linear terms in
W
are dropped. In this case, the dissipation action takes the following form

S_{\text{Dis}}[g_{s}g_{f}]=S_{\text{Dis}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]+S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]\,,

(29)

with

S_{\text{Dis}}[g_{s}]=k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\tau,x) g_s^{-1}(\tau',x)\Big)\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]&=-k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dxK(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\Bigg( \frac{W^2}{2} + \frac{W^{\prime\, 2}}{2} - W W' \Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dxK(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr[\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{\prime\,-1}g_{s}\Big)\Bigg(\frac{W^{2}}{2}+\frac{W^{\prime\,2}}{2}-WW^{\prime}\Bigg)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(32)

The second term (purely fast part) can be written in Fourier space

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]&=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega,\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime}}\int_{q,q^{\prime}}\tilde{K}(\omega^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega^{\prime},q^{\prime})e^{i\omega^{\prime\prime}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}e^{i(\omega\tau+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau+q^{\prime}x)}+\frac{1}{2}e^{i(\omega\tau^{\prime}+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}+q^{\prime}x)}-e^{i(\omega\tau+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}+q^{\prime}x)}\Bigg]\\
&=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega,q}\Big(\tilde{K}(0)-\tilde{K}(-\omega)\Big)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)\,,\end{split}

(33)

where
\int_{\omega}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}
,
\int_{q}=\int\frac{dq}{2\pi}
. In the first equality, the trace over the generators has been performed, while in the second equality, integrals over momentum/frequency
\delta
functions have been carried out. The Fourier transform of the kernel is obtained using the general formula

\int d^{d}x\frac{e^{-ip\cdot x}}{|x|^{\beta}}=\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)}{\pi^{d/2}2^{\beta}\Gamma(\beta/2)}(2\pi)^{d}\frac{1}{|p|^{d-\beta}}\,,

(34)

for
d
Euclidean dimensions. In our case,
d=1
and
\beta=3-\delta
for the Fourier transform of the kernel, which yields
\tilde{K}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{8\pi}|\omega|^{2-\delta}
. This shows that
\tilde{K}(0)=0
and the fast part of the dissipation action thus becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega,q}\Bigg(-\frac{1}{8\pi}|\omega|^{2-\delta}\Bigg)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)\end{split}

(35)

A.1.4
Recap

As a recap, the action expanded at quadratic order in
W
can be grouped in three terms:
S[g_{s}g_{f}]=S[g_{s}]+S^{(2)}[W]+S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]
. The first term is simply the initial action evaluated at
g=g_{s}

\displaystyle\begin{split}S[g_{s}]&=S_{\text{Grad}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Dis}}[g_{s}]\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} + \partial_x g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1} \Bigg)+\frac{ik}{12\pi}\int_{B^{3}}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\tau,x) g_s^{-1}(\tau',x)\Big)\,.\end{split}

(36)

It contributes to the
\beta
functions only via the final rescaling step. The second contribution to the expanded action regroups the two terms which only contain fast fields:

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{(2)}[W]&=S_{\text{Grad}}^{(2)}[W]+S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau} W \partial_{\tau} W + \partial_x W \partial_x W \Bigg)-k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dxK(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\Bigg( \frac{W^2}{2} + \frac{W^{\prime\, 2}}{2} - W W' \Bigg)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,q)\Big(\Pi^{-1}(\omega,q)-k^{2}\gamma\tilde{K}(\omega)\Big)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{a}(\omega,\phi)\tilde{G}^{-1}(\omega,q)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-\omega,-q)\,,\end{split}

(37)

where we have identified the fast propagator

\tilde{G}(\omega,q)=\frac{\lambda}{q^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma|\omega|^{2-\delta}}\,.

(38)

Finally, the last piece contains all the terms mixing slow and fast modes, which are denoted as interaction terms

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]&=S_{\text{Int,Grad}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]+S_{\text{Int,WZ}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]+S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} g_s^{-1}\partial_{\tau} g_s [\partial_{\tau} W,W] + g_s^{-1}\partial_x g_s [\partial_x W,W] \Bigg)+\frac{ik}{8\pi}\int d\tau dx\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s [\partial_{\nu}W,W] \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dxK(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr[\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{\prime\,-1}g_{s}\Big)\Bigg(\frac{W^{2}}{2}+\frac{W^{\prime\,2}}{2}-WW^{\prime}\Bigg)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(39)

The first two terms can be combined into a “WZW interaction term”
S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=S_{\text{Int,Grad}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]+S_{\text{Int,WZ}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) [\partial_{\mu}W,W] \Big)\,,\end{split}

(40)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x)=g_{s}^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}\partial_{\tau}-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\partial_{x}\Big)g_{s}\,,\qquad\Phi_{x}(\tau,x)=g_{s}^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{x}+\frac{ik}{8\pi}\partial_{\tau}\Big)g_{s}\,.\end{split}

(41)

A.2
Fourier representation of interaction terms

We now express the interaction terms, which we will average over with respect to the fast propagator, in Fourier space.

A.2.1
WZW interaction term

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]&=\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) [\partial_{\mu}W,W] \Big)\\
&=i\int d\tau dx\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p,p^{\prime}}e^{i(p+p^{\prime}+p_{s})\cdot x}(p_{\mu}-p_{\mu}^{\prime})\tr[\tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_{s})\tilde{W}(p)\tilde{W}(p^{\prime})\Big]\\
&=i\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})\tr[\tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_{s})\tilde{W}(p)\tilde{W}(-p-p_{s})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(42)

where
p=(\omega,q)
is a fast 2-momentum and
p_{s}=(\omega_{s},q_{s})
is a slow 2-momentum.

A.2.2
Dissipation interaction term

To treat the dissipation interaction term
S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}
, let us define

D_{s}(\tau,\tau^{\prime},x)=\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{-1}(\tau^{\prime},x)g_{s}(\tau,x)=\int\frac{d\omega_{s}}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega^{\prime}_{s}}{2\pi}\int\frac{dq_{s}}{2\pi}\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s})e^{i(\omega_{s}\tau+\omega^{\prime}_{s}\tau^{\prime}+q_{s}x)}\,.

(43)

Therefore, by Fourier transforming, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]&=k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,\omega^{\prime},\Omega}\int_{q,q^{\prime}}\,\tilde{K}(\Omega)e^{i\Omega(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s})e^{i(\omega_{s}\tau+\omega_{s}^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}+q_{s}x)}\\
\hfil&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(\omega^{\prime},q^{\prime})e^{i(\omega\tau+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau+q^{\prime}x)}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(\omega^{\prime},q^{\prime})e^{i(\omega\tau^{\prime}+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}+q^{\prime}x)}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(\omega^{\prime},q^{\prime})e^{i(\omega\tau+qx)}e^{i(\omega^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}+q^{\prime}x)}\Bigg)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(44)

where frequencies and momenta with a subscript “
s
” are slow modes, while the others are fast modes, except for
\Omega
which is unspecified for now. The space and time integrals yield
\delta
functions over frequencies and momenta. Performing them, we arrive at

S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}\,,

(45)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{1}=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega^{\prime}_{s})\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s})\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(-\omega_{s}-\omega^{\prime}_{s}-\omega,-q-q_{s})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(46)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{2}=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s})\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s})\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(-\omega_{s}-\omega^{\prime}_{s}-\omega,-q-q_{s})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(47)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{3}=-k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s}+\omega)\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s})\tilde{W}(\omega,q)\tilde{W}(-\omega_{s}-\omega^{\prime}_{s}-\omega,-q-q_{s})\Big]\,.\end{split}

(48)

The interaction terms presented in the two previous sections can be represented diagrammatically in terms of the vertices presented in Fig.

.

, where the square corresponds to the slow object
\Phi_{\mu}
. b) Representation of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. These two terms are essentially the same, since the Fourier transform of their kernel only contains slow modes. The square depicts an insertion of the kernel from either

or

, times the purely slow object

, each vertex contains two

.

A.3
Integration of fast modes

We are now in a position to integrate the fast modes. To do so, we proceed with a cumulant expansion.

S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]\approx S[g_{s}]+\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}-\frac{1}{2}\expectationvalue{(S^{(2)}_{\text{Int}}[g_s,W])^2}_{f}^{c}+...\,,

(49)

where the expectation value is taken with respect to the fast modes, while
c
stands for connected correlation function. We perform the RG calculation at one-loop, which is controlled using a large-
k
expansion. This requires the couplings
\lambda
and
\gamma
to be of order
1/k
as well as
\delta
, which justifies the introduction of the
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
parameters
\tilde{\lambda}=k\lambda
,
\tilde{\gamma}=k\gamma
and
\tilde{\delta}=k\delta
.

With
e^{W}
expanded to quadratic order in
W
, only one-loop diagrams are generated, as we can see from the vertices of Fig.
5
. Moreover, it is clear that diagrams at order
n
in the cumulant expansion contain
n
vertices. Two-loop diagrams can be obtained by expanding to higher powers in
W
. However, these terms will be suppressed with additional powers of
1/k
. This comes from the fact that every vertex is of order
k
, but the propagator is of order
1/k
. Hence, the order in
1/k
of a diagram is given by
n_{p}-n_{v}
(respectively the number of propagators and the number of vertices). However,
n_{p}-n_{v}=n_{l}-1
, where
n_{l}
is the number of loops in a given diagram. Therefore, the order in
1/k
of a diagram is directly related to the number of loops it has.

A.3.1
Order 1 in interaction action

Let us start by evaluating the first expectation value

\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}=\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}+\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}\,.

(50)

Dissipation term:
The expectation value of the dissipation term is separated into the expectation value of its three pieces (see Eq. (
45
) above)

\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}=\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}+\expectationvalue{T_2}_{f}+\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}\,,

(51)

which can be represented by the following three Feynman diagrams

Figure 6:
one-loop Feynman diagrams contributing to
\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}
. (a), (b) and (c) correspond respectively to
\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}
,
\expectationvalue{T_2}_{f}
and
\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}
.

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}&=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega_{s}\omega_{s}^{\prime}q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s}^{\prime})\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s})\expectationvalue{\tilde{W}(\omega,q) \tilde{W}(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s)}_{f}\Big]\\
&=i^{2}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega_{s}\omega_{s}^{\prime}q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s}^{\prime})\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s})T^{a}T^{b}\Big]\expectationvalue{\tilde{\phi}^a(\omega,q) \tilde{\phi}^b(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s)}_{f}\,.\end{split}

(52)

The expectation value yields a single Wick contraction

\expectationvalue{\tilde{\phi}^a(\omega,q) \tilde{\phi}^b(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s)}_{f}=\delta^{ab}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime})\delta(q_{s})\,,

(53)

from which we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}&=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\int_{\omega_{s}}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s})\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},-\omega_{s},0)T^{a}T^{a}\Big]\\
&=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{4}\Big(N-\frac{1}{N}\Big)\int_{\omega,k}\tilde{G}(\omega,k)\int_{\omega_{s}}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s})\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},-\omega_{s},0)\Big]\\
&=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}C_{F}\,I_{1}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)\,,\end{split}

(54)

where the trace has been simplified using the SU
(N)
completeness relation
T^{a}_{ij}T^{a}_{kl}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\Big)
. We have also defined the SU
(N)
quadratic Casimir in the fundamental representation
C_{F}=\frac{N^{2}-1}{2N}
and the fast integral

I_{1}=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\lambda}{q^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma|\omega|^{2-\delta}}\,.

(55)

In the last step, the following inverse Fourier transform has been employed

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\omega_{s}}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s})\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},-\omega_{s},0)\Big]&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s}}K(\tau^{\prime\prime})\tr\Big( D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau-\tau^{\prime\prime})}\\
&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dxK(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\Big( D(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)\\
&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)\end{split}

(56)

By performing a very similar calculation, one can show that
\expectationvalue{T_2}_{f}=\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}
. For
T_{3}
, using the above result for the expectation value of the fast modes, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}&=\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}\Big(N-\frac{1}{N}\Big)\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\int_{\omega_{s}}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\,\tr[\tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_{s},-\omega_{s},0)\Big]\,.\end{split}

(57)

The kernel is now expanded to quadratic order in
\omega_{s}

\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\Bigg(|\omega|^{2-\delta}+(2-\delta)\frac{\omega}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\omega_{s}+\frac{1}{2}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\frac{\omega_{s}^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\Bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\omega_{s}^{3})\,,

(58)

Clearly, the contribution from the second term vanishes since the fast integrand is odd under
\omega\rightarrow-\omega
. Moreover, the contribution from the first term can also be shown to vanish

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\omega_{s}}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,-\omega_s,0) \Big)&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}\\
&=\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( D_s(\tau,\tau,x) \Big)\\
&=0\,,\end{split}

(59)

since
D_{s}(\tau,\tau,x)=\mathds{1}-g_{s}(\tau,x)g^{-1}_{s}(\tau,x)=0
. Hence, only the quadratic term in
\omega_{s}
survives. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}&=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{16\pi}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\tilde{G}(\omega,q)}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\int_{\omega_{s}}\omega_{s}^{2}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,-\omega_s,0) \Big)\,.\end{split}

(60)

This is proportional to
\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)
, as can be seen from the following manipulations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\omega_{s}}\omega_{s}^{2}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,-\omega_s,0) \Big)&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)\omega_{s}^{2}e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}\\
&=-\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)\partial_{\tau}^{2}e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}\\
&=-\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\int_{\omega_{s}}\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau}^2 D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}\\
&=-\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau}^2 D_s(\tau,\tau',x) \Big)\delta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\\
&=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\tr\Big( g_s^{-1}(\tau',x) \partial_{\tau}^2 g_s(\tau,x) \Big)\delta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\\
&=-\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,,\end{split}

(61)

where
\omega_{s}^{2}
has been replaced by
-\partial_{\tau}^{2}
acting on the exponential, while integration by parts has also been used twice. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}&=\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{16\pi}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\tilde{G}(\omega,q)}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(62)

WZW term:
We now move to the expectation value of the WZW interaction term, corresponding to the Feynman diagrams shown in Fig.
7
.

Figure 7:
one-loop Feynman diagram contributing to
\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}
.

The calculation of the diagram yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}&=i\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})\,\tr\Big( \ev{\Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \Tilde{W}(p) \Tilde{W}(-p - p_s)}_f \Big)\\
&=-i\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\expectationvalue{\Tilde{\phi}^a(p) \Tilde{\phi}^b(-p-p_s)}\\
&=-i\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^a \Big)(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}(p_{s})\tilde{G}(p)\\
&=-2i\int_{p}p_{\mu}\tilde{G}(p)\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(0) T^a T^a \Big)\\
&=0\,,\end{split}

(63)

where we still have
p=(\omega,q)
,
p_{s}=(\omega_{s},q_{s})
. The above expression vanishes for two reasons. First, the integral over fast modes vanishes due to an odd integrand. Secondly, simplifying the trace using the
\text{SU}(N)
completeness relation yields a trace of
\Phi_{\mu}
, which vanishes. This can be shown by writing
g=v\mathds{1}+iN^{a}T^{a}
, with
\vec{n}=(v,\vec{N})^{T}
,
\vec{n}\cdot\vec{n}=1
. In this case,
\tr\Phi_{\mu}\sim\vec{n}\cdot\partial_{\mu}\vec{n}=0
, since
\vec{n}
is perpendicular to its derivative.

Recap:
Therefore, the expectation value of the interaction action is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}&=\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}+\expectationvalue{T_2}_{f}+\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}\\
&=-k^{2}\gamma C_{F}\,I_{1}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{16\pi}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\tilde{G}(\omega,q)}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(64)

A.3.2
Order 2 in interaction action

We now move to the term quadratic in the interaction action in the cumulant expansion. There are three terms to consider

\expectationvalue{(S^{(2)}_{\text{Int}}[g_s,W])^2}_{f}^{c}=\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}+\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)} S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}}_{f}^{c}\,.

(65)

Squared WZW term:
We start with the expectation value of the squared WZW interaction term, the diagrammatic representation of which is shown in Fig.
8
.

Figure 8:
one-loop Feynman diagram contributing to
\expectationvalue{(S^{(2)}_{\text{Int,WZW}})^2}_{f}^{c}
.

The diagram corresponds to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=-\int_{p_{s},p_{s}^{\prime}}\int_{p,p^{\prime}}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})(2p^{\prime}_{\nu}+p^{\prime}_{s\,\nu})\Big\langle\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{W}(p) \tilde{W}(-p-p_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \tilde{W}(p') \tilde{W}(-p'-p_s') \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\\
&\approx-4\int_{p_{s},p_{s}^{\prime}}\int_{p,p^{\prime}}p_{\mu}p^{\prime}_{\nu}\Big\langle\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{W}(p) \tilde{W}(-p-p_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \tilde{W}(p') \tilde{W}(-p'-p_s') \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\,,\end{split}

(66)

where
p=(\omega,q)
,
p_{s}=(\omega_{s},q_{s})
,
p^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime},q^{\prime})
,
p^{\prime}_{s}=(\omega^{\prime}_{s},q^{\prime}_{s})
. Note that
p_{s\,\mu}
and
p^{\prime}_{s\,\nu}
have been dropped since the expression is already quadratic in derivatives (from the two
\Phi_{\mu}
). Slow modes, when expressed in real space, correspond to derivatives, which means even more irrelevant terms. Let us focus our attention on the expectation value

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\expectationvalue{\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{W}(p) \tilde{W}(-p-p_s) \Big) \tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \tilde{W}(p') \tilde{W}(-p'-p_s') \Big)}_{f}^{c}\\
&=\expectationvalue{\tilde{\phi}^a(p) \tilde{\phi}^b(-p-p_s) \tilde{\phi}^c(p') \tilde{\phi}^d(-p'-p_s')}_{f}^{c}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^c T^d \Big)\,.\end{split}

(67)

The expectation value is computed using Wick contractions. There are two connected pieces, denoted as
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. First, let us consider
W_{1}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{1}&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(2)}(p+p^{\prime})\delta^{(2)}(p+p^{\prime}+p_{s}+p_{s}^{\prime})\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\tilde{G}(p)\tilde{G}(p+p_{s})\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^c T^d \Big)\\
&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(2)}(p+p^{\prime})\delta^{(2)}(p+p^{\prime}+p_{s}+p_{s}^{\prime})\tilde{G}(p)\tilde{G}(p+p_{s})\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^a T^b \Big)\,.\end{split}

(68)

The traces are computed using the the completeness relation for the SU
(N)
generators which leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^a T^b \Big)&=\tilde{\Phi}^{ij}_{\mu}(p_{s})\tilde{\Phi}^{lm}_{\nu}(p_{s}^{\prime})\Big(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{jn}\delta_{km}-\frac{1}{2N}\delta_{jk}\delta_{mn}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{kl}\delta_{in}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ik}\delta_{nl}\Big)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2N}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)+\frac{1}{4}\Big(1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \Big)\,\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2N}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)\,,\end{split}

(69)

Next, consider
W_{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{2}&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(2)}(p-p^{\prime}-p_{s}^{\prime})\delta^{(2)}(p^{\prime}-p-p_{s})\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\tilde{G}(p+p_{s})\tilde{G}(p)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^c T^d \Big)\\
&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(2)}(p-p^{\prime}-p_{s}^{\prime})\delta^{(2)}(p^{\prime}-p-p_{s})\tilde{G}(p+p_{s})\tilde{G}(p)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^b T^a \Big)\,.\end{split}

(70)

Computing the traces leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') T^b T^a \Big)&=\tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}^{ij}(p_{s})\tilde{\Phi}^{lm}_{\nu}(p_{s}^{\prime})\Big(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{jl}\delta_{kn}-\frac{1}{2N}\delta_{jk}\delta_{nl}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{kn}\delta_{im}-\frac{1}{2N}\delta_{ki}\delta_{mn}\Big)\\
&=\Big(\frac{N}{4}-\frac{1}{2N}\Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)+\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \Big)\,\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)\\
&=\Big(\frac{N}{4}-\frac{1}{2N}\Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(p_s') \Big)\,.\end{split}

(71)

Combining everything and integrating over the
\delta
functions yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=-4\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}p_{\mu}\tilde{G}(p)\tilde{G}(p+p_{s})\,\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(-p_s) \Big)\Bigg[\frac{1}{2N}p_{\nu}+\Big(\frac{N}{4}-\frac{1}{2N}\Big)(p_{\nu}+p_{s\,\nu})\Bigg]\\
&\approx-N\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\tilde{G}^{2}(p)\,\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(p_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\nu}(-p_s) \Big)\\
&=-N\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}(\omega,q)_{\mu}(\omega,q)_{\nu}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\,\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) \Phi_{\nu}(\tau,x) \Big)\,,\end{split}

(72)

where slow modes have once again been neglected compared to fast modes. Note that the fast integral vanishes if
\mu\neq\nu
. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=-N\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\omega^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\,\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x) \Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x) \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-N\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}q^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\,\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \Phi_{x}(\tau,x) \Phi_{x}(\tau,x) \Big)\\
&=-NI_{2}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x) \Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x) \Big)-NI_{3}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \Phi_{x}(\tau,x) \Phi_{x}(\tau,x) \Big)\,,\end{split}

(73)

where we have defined the following fast integrals

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\omega^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\omega^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{(q^{2}+\omega^{2}/c^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma|\omega|^{2-\delta})^{2}}\\
I_{3}&=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}q^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}q^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{(q^{2}+\omega^{2}/c^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma|\omega|^{2-\delta})^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(74)

Using the expressions for
\Phi_{\tau}
and
\Phi_{x}
to simplify the traces and regrouping similar terms, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=\frac{N}{c^{4}\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{2}-\frac{k^{2}c^{4}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{3}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N}{\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{3}-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{2}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_x g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+N\frac{ik}{4\pi}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}I_{3}-\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}I_{2}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1}\Big)\,,\end{split}

(75)

The first two terms contribute to the renormalization of the gradient term in the action. However, the third term is unphysical. Indeed, as pointed out in the main text, the operator
\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1}\Big)
breaks a symmetry from the original action, since it is not invariant under
g(\tau,x)\rightarrow g^{-1}(\tau,-x)
. This term is generated due to the fact that when performing the splitting of the degrees of freedom using the decomposition
g=g_{s}g_{f}
, this symmetry is “fractionalized” between the slow and fast modes and is effectively lost when the latter are integrated out. For the purpose of the RG analysis, this unphysical term can thus be dropped from the effective action.

However, by doing the “opposite” decomposition, that is
g=g_{f}g_{s}
, one can easily show that the expanded action to quadratic order in
W
is essentially the same as the one derived above, but with the important difference that the sign of
S_{\text{Int,WZ}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]
reverses, that is

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{f}g_{s}]&=S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Int,WZ}}^{\prime\,(2)}[g_{s},W]\\
&=S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\int d\tau dx\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\tr\Big( g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} [\partial_{\nu}W,W] \Big)\,,\end{split}

(76)

which is equivalent to the replacement
k\rightarrow-k
(strictly speaking, there are also a few other minor differences, such as
g_{s}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}^{-1}
instead of
g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}
, but these do not affect the renormalization of any physical term). Hence, doing the RG with this new decomposition yields the same expression as Eq. (
75
), but with a relative negative sign in the third term. Therefore, by defining the symmetrized effective action
S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]=\frac{1}{2}\big(S_{\text{Eff}}^{(1)}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Eff}}^{(2)}[g_{s}]\big)
, where
S_{\text{Eff}}^{(1)}[g_{s}]
is obtained using
g=g_{s}g_{f}
and
S_{\text{Eff}}^{(2)}[g_{s}]
comes from using
g=g_{f}g_{s}
, the unphysical terms cancel, leaving an effective action containing only terms allowed by symmetries.

Squared dissipation term:
Let us next consider the square of the dissipation term,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=\expectationvalue{(T_1+T_2+T_3)^2}_{f}^{c}\\
&=\expectationvalue{T_1^2}_{f}^{c}+\expectationvalue{T_2^2}_{f}^{c}+\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{T_1 T_2}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{T_1 T_3}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{T_2 T_3}_{f}^{c}\,,\end{split}

(77)

which can be represented diagrammatically by Fig.
9
.

one-loop Feynman diagrams contributing to
\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}
.

From the Fourier-space expressions of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we see that the first three terms will contain two slow kernels. Therefore, terms with three time integrals and two kernels will be generated. An example of such a term is

\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}dxK(\tau)K(\tau^{\prime})\tr[\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{-1}(\tau^{\prime\prime}-\tau)g_{s}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Big)\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{-1}(\tau^{\prime\prime}+\tau^{\prime})g_{s}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Big)\Bigg]\,,

(78)

where the fields’
x
-dependence is implicit. Let us now analyze the relevance of this term compared to the terms in the initial action. To do so, we apply the rescaling
x\rightarrow bx
,
\tau\rightarrow b^{z}\tau
, where
b>0
and
z
is the dynamical critical exponent. Using this, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\sim b^{1-z}\\
&\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{x} g_s \partial_{x} g_s^{-1} \Big)\sim b^{z-1}\\
&\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\frac{1}{|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{3-\delta}}\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)\sim b^{1+z(\delta-1)}\,,\end{split}

(79)

(we can take the naive vanishing scaling dimension for the fields since
\Delta_{g}>0
makes terms even more irrelevant). By performing the same rescaling for Eq. (
78
), we see that it goes as
b^{1+z(2\delta-3)}
. Therefore, for
\delta<1
(which is required for our controlled large-
k
expansion), this term is less relevant then the terms in the initial action and thus can be neglected.

Let us now move on to the two terms
\expectationvalue{T_1 T_3}_{f}^{c}
and
\expectationvalue{T_2 T_3}_{f}^{c}
.
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
contribute with a slow kernel, while
T_{3}
gives a mixed kernel containing slow and fast modes. The mixed kernel needs to be expanded in powers of
\omega_{s}
as in the calculation of
\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}
. Therefore, the resulting contributions will be like the initial dissipation term, but with additional time derivatives. For example, at order
\omega_{s}^{2}
(the first non-vanishing order), we would have something of the form

\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,\partial_{\tau}^{2}K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s(\tau',x) g_s(\tau,x) \Big)\,,

(80)

which is of course very irrelevant and can be dropped.

Finally, let us compute the expectation value of
T_{3}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}&=k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\int_{\Omega_{s},\Omega_{s}^{\prime},l_{s}}\int_{\Omega,l}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\tilde{K}(\Omega+\Omega_{s})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Big\langle\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega'_s,q_s) \Tilde{W}(\omega,q) \Tilde{W}(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega'_s,l_s) \Tilde{W}(\Omega,l) \Tilde{W}(-\Omega-\Omega_s-\Omega_s',-l-l_s) \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\,,\end{split}

(81)

where
l
and
l_{s}
are respectively fast and slow momenta. Once again, we start by considering the expectation value

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big\langle\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega'_s,q_s) \Tilde{W}(\omega,q) \Tilde{W}(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega'_s,l_s) \Tilde{W}(\Omega,l) \Tilde{W}(-\Omega-\Omega_s-\Omega_s',-l-l_s) \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\\
&=\expectationvalue{\tilde{\phi}^a(\omega,q) \tilde{\phi}^b(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s) \tilde{\phi}^c(\Omega,l) \tilde{\phi}^d(-\Omega-\Omega_s-\Omega_s',-l-l_s)}_{f}^{c}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\tr\Big( \tilde{D}_{s}(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) T^a T^b \Big)\tr\Big( \tilde{D}_{s}(\Omega_s,\Omega_s',l_s) T^c T^d \Big)\,.\end{split}

(82)

The calculation of this expectation value is quite similar to the one performed before (see Eqs. (
67
)–(
71
)). Let us denote the two connected pieces as
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{1}&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta(\omega+\Omega)\delta(q+l)\delta(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega+\Omega_{s}+\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\delta(q+q_{s}+l+l_{s})\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2N}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega_s',l_s) \Big)+\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\Bigg)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega_s',l_s) \Big)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(83)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{2}&=(2\pi)^{4}\delta(\omega-\Omega-\Omega_{s}-\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\delta(q-l-l_{s})\delta(\Omega-\omega-\omega_{s}-\omega_{s}^{\prime})\delta(l-q-q_{s})\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\Bigg[\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(N-\frac{2}{N}\Bigg)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega_s',l_s) \Big)+\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,\Omega_s',l_s) \Big)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(84)

By adding the two Wick contractions and integrating over the
\delta
functions, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}&=k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Bigg[\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2N}\tilde{K}(\omega-\Omega_{s})+\frac{1}{4}\Big(N-\frac{2}{N}\Big)\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega_{s})\Bigg)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\Bigg(\frac{1}{4}\Big(1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\Big)\tilde{K}(\omega-\Omega_{s})+\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega_{s})\Bigg)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(85)

We now need to expand the kernels as well as the second propagator in powers of the slow modes
\omega_{s}
,
\omega_{s}^{\prime}
,
\Omega_{s}
and
q_{s}
. Instead of expanding directly, which would yield a huge number of terms, let us analyze the various possible slow contributions that can be generated. We will only focus on the term which contains
\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)
, since the structure of the other term which is proportional to
\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)
follows from a very similar analysis (in fact, it turns out that the contribution from this latter term vanishes as discussed below). At leading order in the slow mode expansion, the contribution from Eq. (
85
) to the effective action for the slow field
g_{s}
is proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\\
&=\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times e^{-i\omega_{s}\tau_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{s}^{\prime}\tau_{2}}e^{-iq_{s}x}e^{-i\Omega_{s}\tau_{s}}e^{i(\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega_{s})\tau_{4}}e^{iq_{s}y}\\
&=\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\delta(y-x)\\
&=0\,,\end{split}

(86)

which vanishes since
D_{s}(\tau,\tau,x)=0
. Next, at linear order in slow modes, all the contributions vanish, since these terms will also be linear in fast modes, which will yield an odd fast integrand. Therefore, to get a nonzero contribution, we must go to quadratic order in fast modes. There are various possible combinations. Let us analyze them. First, we could have a term with
\omega_{s}^{2}
. Its contribution to the effective action will be proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\omega_{s}^{2}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\\
&=\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\omega_{s}^{2}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times e^{-i\omega_{s}\tau_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{s}^{\prime}\tau_{2}}e^{-iq_{s}x}e^{-i\Omega_{s}\tau_{s}}e^{i(\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega_{s})\tau_{4}}e^{iq_{s}y}\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})}e^{i\omega^{\prime}_{s}(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})}e^{i\Omega_{s}(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})}e^{iq_{s}(y-x)}\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_1}^2 D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\delta(y-x)\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_1}^2 D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) D_s(\tau_3,\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})\\
&=0\,,\end{split}

(87)

where integration by parts has been used. With an identical calculation, terms with
\omega_{s}^{\prime 2}
will be the same as above, except with
\partial_{\tau_{2}}^{2}
instead of
\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}
, while terms with
\Omega_{s}^{2}
will contain
\partial_{\tau_{3}}^{2}
. Clearly, these terms also vanish for the same reason as above. Therefore, we recognize a pattern here: a term with a
T_{3}
vanishes if there is no time derivative that acts on the associated
D_{s}
. Hence, we see that terms with
\omega_{s}\omega_{s}^{\prime}
also vanish, since no derivatives will be acting on
D_{s}(\tau_{3},\tau_{4},x)
.

Let us now look at the contribution from terms with
\omega_{s}\Omega_{s}
, which will contain
\partial_{\tau_{1}}
and
\partial_{\tau_{3}}
. This will be proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\omega_{s}\Omega_{s}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_1} D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) \partial_{\tau_3} D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\delta(y-x)\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dx\tr\Big( g_s^{-1}(\tau_2,x) \partial_{\tau_1} g_s(\tau_1,x) g_s^{-1}(\tau_4,x) \partial_{\tau_3} g_s(\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\\
&=-\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} \partial_{\tau} g_s g_s^{-1} \partial_{\tau} g_s \Big)\\
&=\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(88)

The contribution from terms with
\omega_{s}^{\prime}\Omega_{s}
is quite similar

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\omega_{s}^{\prime}\Omega_{s}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dxdy\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_2} D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) \partial_{\tau_3} D_s(\tau_3,\tau_4,y) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\delta(y-x)\\
&=-\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}d\tau_{4}\int dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_2} g_s^{-1}(\tau_2,x) g_s(\tau_1,x) g_s^{-1}(\tau_4,x) \partial_{\tau_3} g_s(\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{2})\delta(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3})\\
&=-\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} g_s g_s^{-1} \partial_{\tau} g_s \Big)\\
&=-\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(89)

All the other possible quadratic terms contain at least a momentum
q_{s}
. All of these terms will vanish, since
q_{s}
will yield a space derivative, which does not prevent the two time coordinates in
D_{s}
from being the same.

Therefore, we only need to keep track of the terms with
\omega_{s}\Omega_{s}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{s}\Omega_{s}
in the expansion of Eq. (
85
). However, since these two terms have an opposite sign, any contribution from the combination
(\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime})\Omega_{s}
vanishes when expanding Eq. (
85
). Knowing this, we can set
\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}=q_{s}=0
in
\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})
as well as
\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}=0
in
\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime}+\Omega_{s})
. Moreover, for the remaining non-vanishing contributions, since each
D_{s}
becomes
g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\tau}g_{s}
(up to an integration by parts), we see that the term proportional to
\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Big)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)
also vanishes, since as argued before,
\tr(g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s)=0
. Therefore, we are left with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}&\approx k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},\Omega_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\Bigg[\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2N}\tilde{K}(\omega-\Omega_{s})+\frac{1}{4}\Big(N-\frac{2}{N}\Big)\tilde{K}(\omega+\Omega_{s})\Bigg)\\
&\hskip 199.16928pt\times\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{D}_s(\Omega_s,-\omega_s-\omega_s'-\Omega_s,-q_s) \Big)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(90)

Expanding the kernels yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\tilde{K}(\omega-\Omega_{s})&\approx-\frac{(2-\delta)^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\omega_{s}\Omega_{s}+...\,,\\
\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\tilde{K}(\omega+\Omega_{s})&\approx\frac{(2-\delta)^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\omega_{s}\Omega_{s}+...\,.\end{split}

(91)

Hence, by using Eq. (
88
), we finally get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}+...=\frac{N(2-\delta)^{2}}{4(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)+...\,,\end{split}

(92)

where the ellipsis denote irrelevant terms.

Mixed WZW-Dissipation term:
Finally, we must compute the mixed WZW-dissipation contribution

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}}_{f}^{c}=2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}T_1}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_2}_{f}^{c}+2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}\,,\end{split}

(93)

which can be represented by

one-loop Feynman diagrams contributing to
\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}}_{f}^{c}
.

Let us focus on the first term

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}T_1}_{f}^{c}&=ik^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\int_{\Omega_{s},l_{s}}\int_{\Omega,l}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s})(2\Omega+\Omega_{s},2l+l_{s})_{\mu}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Big\langle\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega'_s,q_s) \Tilde{W}(\omega,q) \Tilde{W}(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(\Omega_s,l_s) \Tilde{W}(\Omega,l) \Tilde{W}(-\Omega-\Omega_s,-l-l_s) \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\,.\end{split}

(94)

The computation of the expectation value is quite similar to the one in
\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}
, involving two connected Wick contractions. After integrating over the
\delta
functions, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}T_1}_{f}^{c}&=i\frac{N}{4}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s}^{\prime})(2\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},2q+q_{s})_{\mu}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)\,.\end{split}

(95)

Since there is a slow kernel and a derivative coming from
\Phi_{\mu}
, we can take the leading order term in the slow modes expansion

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}T_1}_{f}^{c}&\approx i\frac{N}{2}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega_{s}^{\prime})(\omega,q)_{\mu}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)=0\,.\end{split}

(96)

The expression vanishes due to the fact that the fast integrand is odd. Clearly, the exact same thing happens with
T_{2}
. Therefore, let us analyze the third term

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}&=-2ik^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\int_{\Omega_{s},l_{s}}\int_{\Omega,l}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})(2\Omega+\Omega_{s},2l+l_{s})_{\mu}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Big\langle\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega'_s,q_s) \Tilde{W}(\omega,q) \Tilde{W}(-\omega-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q-q_s) \Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(\Omega_s,l_s) \Tilde{W}(\Omega,l) \Tilde{W}(-\Omega-\Omega_s,-l-l_s) \Big)\Big\rangle_{f}^{c}\,.\end{split}

(97)

Computing the expectation value and the integrals over the
\delta
functions yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}&=-i\frac{N}{2}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})(2\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},2q+q_{s})_{\mu}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\tilde{G}(\omega+\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime},q+q_{s})\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)\,.\end{split}

(98)

This time, we need to expand to linear order in the various slow modes, since there is already a derivative in
\Phi_{\mu}
(the leading order contribution of course vanishes). As we did before, let us look at the various possibilities one encounters when expanding Eq. (
98
). First, linear terms in
\omega_{s}
yield contributions to the effective action for
g_{s}
proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\omega_{s}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)\\
&=\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dxdy\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\omega_{s}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau_3,y) \Big)e^{-i\omega_{s}\tau_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{s}^{\prime}\tau_{2}}e^{-iq_{s}x}e^{i(\omega_{s}+\omega_{s}^{\prime})\tau_{3}}e^{iq_{s}y}\\
&=i\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dxdy\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\tr\Big( D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau_3,y) \Big)\partial_{\tau_{1}}e^{i\omega_{s}(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})}e^{i\omega_{s}^{\prime}(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})}e^{iq_{s}(y-x)}\\
&=-i\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dxdy\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_1} D_s(\tau_1,\tau_2,x) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})\delta(x-y)\\
&=i\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dx\tr\Big( g_s^{-1}(\tau_2) \partial_{\tau_1} g_s(\tau_1) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})\\
&=-i\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) \Big)\,.\end{split}

(99)

For linear terms in
\omega_{s}^{\prime}
, the situation is identical, but with
\partial_{\tau_{2}}
instead of
\partial_{\tau_{1}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\omega_{s},\omega_{s}^{\prime},q_{s}}\omega_{s}^{\prime}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega_s,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)\\
&=i\int d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}d\tau_{3}\int dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau_2} g_s^{-1}(\tau_2) g_s(\tau_1) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau_3,x) \Big)\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{1})\delta(\tau_{3}-\tau_{2})\\
&=i\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) \Big)\,.\end{split}

(100)

Finally, it is clear that terms with
q_{s}
vanish, since they will be proportional to
\tr\Big( \partial_x D_s(\tau,\tau,x) \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) \Big)=0
. Hence, since the terms with
\omega_{s}
and
\omega_{s}^{\prime}
have an opposite sign, Eq. (
98
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}&\approx-iNk^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega_{s},\omega^{\prime}_{s},q_{s}}\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})(\omega,q)_{\mu}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\omega,\omega_s',q_s) \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\omega_s-\omega_s',-q_s) \Big)+...\,.\end{split}

(101)

The expansion of the kernel at linear order in
\omega_{s}
yields

\tilde{K}(\omega+\omega_{s})\approx-\frac{(2-\delta)}{8\pi}\frac{\omega}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\omega_{s}+...\,,

(102)

from which we get, using Eq. (
99
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}&=\frac{N(2-\delta)}{8\pi}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega}{|\omega|^{\delta}}(\omega,q)_{\mu}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu}(\tau,x) \Big)\\
&=\frac{N(2-\delta)}{8\pi}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\tau}(\tau,x) \Big)\,,\end{split}

(103)

where in the second equality, the fast integral is only nonzero if
\mu=\tau
. Using the expression for
\Phi_{\tau}
, this becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}&=\frac{N(2-\delta)}{8\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{c^{2}\lambda}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-i\frac{N(2-\delta)}{(8\pi)^{2}}k^{3}\gamma\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{x} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(104)

Once again, an unphysical term with mixed partial derivatives is generated. It can again be ignored for the rest of the RG calcualtion since it drops out from a symmetrized version of the RG (see the discussion right after Eq. (
75
)).

Recap:
Combining all the contributions we found above, the expectation value of the squared interaction action is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S^{(2)}_{\text{Int}}[g_s,W])^2}_{f}^{c}&=\frac{N}{c^{4}\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{2}-\frac{k^{2}c^{4}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{3}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N}{\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{3}-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{2}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_x g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N(2-\delta)^{2}}{4(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N(2-\delta)}{8\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{c^{2}\lambda}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} \Big)+...\,,\end{split}

(105)

where the ellipsis denote the unphysical terms with mixed partial derivatives (which are neglected, as justified above).

A.3.3
Higher order terms in the cumulant expansion

Higher order terms in the cumulant expansion, that is, expectation values of higher powers of the interaction action, will yield other one-loop contributions. However, only irrelevant terms with more derivatives and kernels will be generated, and we can then stop at quadratic order in the interaction action.

A.3.4
Effective action full expression

Therefore, by collecting all potentially relevant terms that have been computed above, the effective action is thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Bigg( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1} + \partial_x g_s \partial_x g_s^{-1} \Bigg)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{ik}{12\pi}\int_{B^{3}}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k^{2}\gamma\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\tau,x) g_s^{-1}(\tau',x)\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-k^{2}\gamma\,C_{F}\,I_{1}\,\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}dx\,K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\tau,x) g_s^{ -1}(\tau',x)\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{16\pi}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\,\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\tilde{G}(\omega,q)}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N}{2c^{4}\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{2}-\frac{k^{2}c^{4}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{3}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N}{2\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{3}-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{2}\Bigg)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{x} g_s \partial_{x} g_s^{-1}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-N\frac{(2-\delta)^{2}}{8(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\,\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1}\Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N(2-\delta)}{16\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{c^{2}\lambda}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\int d\tau dx\tr\Big(\partial_{\tau} g_s \partial_{\tau} g_s^{-1}\Big)\end{split}

(106)

A.4
\beta
functions calculation

Having obtained the effective action, we are now in a position to compute the
\beta
functions. There will be three of these, from the three terms that are getting renormalized:
\partial_{\tau}g\partial_{\tau}g^{-1}
,
\partial_{x}g\partial_{x}g^{-1}
and the dissipation
K(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\tr\big( \mathds{1} - g(\tau,x) g^{-1}(\tau',x) \big)
. Note that the WZ term does not get renormalized, as expected, since its coefficient
k
is quantized to be an integer.

The
\beta
functions are obtained by rescaling space and time according to

x\rightarrow bx=e^{dl}x\,,\qquad\tau\rightarrow b^{z}\tau=e^{zdl}\tau\,,

(107)

where
b=e^{dl}
, with
dl
an infinitesimal positive quantity and
z
is the dynamical critical exponent. As we will see eventually, all the terms containing fast integrals (obtained from the one-loop analysis) will be proportional to
dl
, so we only need to rescale terms coming from
S[g_{s}]
in the effective action. From a simple power-counting, the following rescaling factors are deduced for the three
\beta
functions:

Spatial derivatives term:

S_{\text{Grad, spatial}}\sim\int d\tau dx\partial_{x}^{2}\implies\text{Factor of}\hskip 14.22636ptb^{z-1}\approx 1+(z-1)dl\,,

(108)

Time derivatives term:

S_{\text{Grad, time}}\sim\int d\tau dx\partial_{\tau}^{2}\implies\text{Factor of}\hskip 14.22636ptb^{1-z}\approx 1+(1-z)dl\,,

(109)

Dissipation term:

S_{\text{Dis}}\sim\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\int dx\frac{1}{|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{3-\delta}}\implies\text{Factor of}\hskip 14.22636ptb^{1+(\delta-1)z}\approx 1+[1+(\delta-1)z]dl\,.

(110)

Therefore, after applying the rescaling, comparing the effective action with the initial action yields the following renormalized couplings:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{R}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{z-1}{\lambda}dl-\frac{N}{2\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{3}-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{2}\Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(111)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{(c^{2}\lambda)_{R}}&=\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}+\frac{1-z}{c^{2}\lambda}dl+\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{16\pi}(2-\delta)(1-\delta)\,\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\tilde{G}(\omega,q)}{|\omega|^{\delta}}-\frac{N}{2c^{4}\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{2}-\frac{k^{2}c^{4}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{3}\Bigg)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-N\frac{(2-\delta)^{2}}{8(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\,\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{2\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)-\frac{N(2-\delta)}{16\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{c^{2}\lambda}\int_{\omega,q}\frac{\omega^{2}}{|\omega|^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(\omega,q)\,,\end{split}

(112)

\displaystyle\begin{split}k^{2}\gamma_{R}=k^{2}\gamma+k^{2}[1+(\delta-1)z]\gamma dl-k^{2}\gamma\,C_{F}\,I_{1}\,.\end{split}

(113)

The
\beta
function of a a given coupling
g
is then defined to be
\beta(g)=\frac{g_{R}-g}{dl}=\frac{dg}{dl}
. Therefore, the next step is to evaluate the fast integrals over a frequency/momentum-shell.

However, before doing that, the above expressions can be greatly simplified in the context of the
1/k
expansion. Indeed, at large-
k
, all the fixed points should be located at values of
\lambda
and
\gamma
of order
1/k
, which is why we introduced the
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
couplings
\tilde{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\gamma}
. Since the Gaussian fixed point (
\lambda=\gamma=0
) is relativistic, it has
z=1
. Therefore, all the non-trivial fixed points should have
z=1+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}
. Knowing this, we see that all the terms on the right-hand-side of the above three equations, except the first one in each case, are all of order
k^{0}
(recalling that
\tilde{G}(\omega,q)\sim\lambda\sim 1/k
). Hence, since
\delta=\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{k}
, we can set
\delta=0
in all the three fast integrals
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
as well as in all the prefactors appearing in Eq. (
112
). Keeping
\delta
would simply add corrections of higher power in
1/k
to the
\beta
functions. In this case, the second equation reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{(c^{2}\lambda)_{R}}=\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}+\frac{1-z}{c^{2}\lambda}dl+\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{8\pi}\,I_{1}-\frac{N}{2c^{4}\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(I_{2}-\frac{k^{2}c^{4}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}I_{3}\Bigg)-\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\,I_{2}-\frac{N}{8\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{c^{2}\lambda}I_{2}\,.\end{split}

(114)

A.4.1
Evaluation of the fast integrals

Let us now evaluate the fast integrals. We have for
I_{1}

I_{1}=\int_{\omega,q}\tilde{G}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\lambda}{q^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma\omega^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\,.

(115)

We now rescale
\omega\rightarrow c\,\omega
, which means that the integral becomes

I_{1}=c\lambda\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{q^{2}+\omega^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma\omega^{2}}\,.

(116)

This integral is performed using polar coordinates
(\omega,q)=p(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
over the shell
b^{-1}=e^{-dl}<p<1
. Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}&=\frac{c\lambda}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{e^{-dl}}^{1}dp\frac{p}{p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma p^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\\
&=\frac{c\lambda}{4\pi^{2}}dl\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\frac{1}{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma\cos^{2}\theta}\\
&=\frac{c\lambda}{2\pi}\frac{dl}{\sqrt{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma}}\\
&=\frac{c\lambda}{2\pi}w(c^{2}\lambda\gamma)dl\,,\end{split}

(117)

where we have introduced the quantity
w(c^{2}\lambda\gamma)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma}}
. The two other integrals are computed using the same method

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=\lambda^{2}\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\big(q^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma\omega^{2}\big)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\\
&=\frac{c^{3}\lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{e^{-dl}}^{1}dp\frac{p^{3}\cos^{2}\theta}{\big(p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma p^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\big)^{2}}\\
&=\frac{c^{3}\lambda^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{dl}{\big(1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma\big)^{3/2}}\\
&=\frac{c^{3}\lambda^{2}}{4\pi}w^{3}(c^{2}\lambda\gamma)dl\,,\end{split}

(118)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3}&=\lambda^{2}\int\frac{d\omega dq}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{q^{2}}{\big(q^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma\omega^{2}\big)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\\
&=\frac{c\lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{e^{-dl}}^{1}dp\frac{p^{3}\sin^{2}\theta}{\big(p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma p^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\big)^{2}}\\
&=\frac{c\lambda^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{dl}{\sqrt{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}c^{2}\lambda\gamma}}\\
&=\frac{c\lambda^{2}}{4\pi}w(c^{2}\lambda\gamma)dl\,.\end{split}

(119)

A.4.2
\beta
functions

Using the results of the previous section, we thus get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big)=\frac{z-1}{\lambda}-\frac{Nc}{8\pi}\Bigg(w-\frac{k^{2}c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}\Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(120)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta\Big(\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}\Big)=\frac{1-z}{c^{2}\lambda}+\frac{C_{F}}{16\pi^{2}}k^{2}c\lambda\gamma w-\frac{N}{8\pi c}\Bigg(w^{3}-\frac{k^{2}c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w\Bigg)-\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{3}}k^{4}c^{3}\lambda^{2}\gamma^{2}w^{3}-\frac{N}{32\pi^{2}}k^{2}c\lambda\gamma w^{3}\,,\end{split}

(121)

\beta(\gamma)=[1+(\delta-1)z]\gamma-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}c\lambda\gamma w\,.

(122)

Using the chain rule, one can write
\beta(\lambda)=-\lambda^{2}\beta\Big(\frac{1}{k}\Big)
and
\beta(c)=-\frac{c^{3}\lambda}{2}\beta\Big(\frac{1}{c^{2}\lambda}\Big)+\frac{c\lambda}{2}\beta\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big)
. By using the
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
variables introduced previously, we finally get the three
\beta
functions in their final form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(\tilde{\lambda})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[-\tilde{z}\tilde{\lambda}+\frac{Nc\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{8\pi}\Bigg(w-\frac{c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}\Bigg)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,\end{split}

(123)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(c)=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\tilde{z}c-\frac{Nc^{2}\tilde{\lambda}}{16\pi}\Bigg(1+\frac{c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)(w-w^{3})-\frac{C_{F}}{32\pi^{2}}c^{4}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}\tilde{\gamma}w+\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{3}}c^{6}\tilde{\lambda}^{3}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}w^{3}+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{2}}c^{4}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}\tilde{\gamma}w^{3}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,\end{split}

(124)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[(\tilde{\delta}-\tilde{z})\tilde{\gamma}-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}c\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}w\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,.\end{split}

(125)

Apparently, we have four unknowns to solve for, namely, the fixed point(s) values of
\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\gamma},c
and
\tilde{z}
and only three equations. However, the fixed point value of the velocity
c
is not a universal characteristic of a fixed point, and in fact, each fixed point should be thought of as a line of fixed points labeled by a different value of the velocity
c
. This is similar to the renormalization group in other systems, e.g., see Refs.
Gamba et al. 1999
;
Lee 2007
. The fact that universal exponents do not depend on
c
can be seen by introducing the variables
x=c\tilde{\lambda}
and
y=c\tilde{\gamma}
. Their respective
\beta
function is then
\beta(x)=c\beta(\tilde{\lambda})+\tilde{\lambda}\beta(c)
,
\beta(y)=c\beta(\tilde{\gamma})+\tilde{\gamma}\beta(c)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(x)&=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\frac{Nx^{2}}{16\pi}\Bigg(1-\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)\Big(w(xy)+w^{3}(xy)\Big)-\frac{C_{F}}{32\pi^{2}}x^{3}yw(xy)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{3}}x^{4}y^{2}w^{3}(xy)+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{2}}x^{3}yw^{3}(xy)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,\end{split}

(126)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(y)&=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\tilde{\delta}y-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}xyw(xy)-\frac{N}{16\pi}xy\Bigg(1+\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)\Big(w(xy)-w^{3}(xy)\Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{C_{F}}{32\pi^{2}}x^{2}y^{2}w(xy)+\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{3}}x^{3}y^{3}w^{3}(xy)+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{2}}x^{2}y^{2}w^{3}(xy)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,\end{split}

(127)

while
\beta(c)
is unchanged.
\beta(x)
and
\beta(y)
are now independent of
c
and
\tilde{z}
and can thus be plotted in the
x-y
plane to locate the fixed points.

A.5
Fixed point analysis

A.5.1
Solving for fixed points

Let us now find the fixed points of the RG flow equations. Consider first the relativistic case, where
\tilde{\gamma}=0
. Since the theory is relativistic,
\tilde{z}=0
. For this case, it is more illuminating to work with the three
\beta
functions
\beta(\tilde{\lambda})
,
\beta(c)
and
\beta(\tilde{\gamma})
. We need to solve
\beta(\tilde{\lambda})=\beta(c)=\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=0
. The last two
\beta
functions vanish, while the condition from the first
\beta
function becomes

0=\frac{Nc\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{8\pi}\Bigg(1-\frac{c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)\,.

(128)

There are thus two relativistic fixed points, the first one being the trivial Gaussian fixed point in
\tilde{\lambda}=0
. There is also a non-trivial fixed point in
\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{8\pi}{c}
. This is in fact a line of fixed points, as argued previously. This is nothing less than the WZW fixed point, which can easily be seen by setting
c=1
.

We now move on to the case of nonrelativistic fixed points, for which
\tilde{\gamma}>0
and
\tilde{z}\neq 0
. Note that in this case, there is no fixed point for
\tilde{\lambda}=0
. Therefore, we get the expression for
\tilde{z}
from
\beta(\tilde{\lambda})=0

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{z}=\frac{Nc\tilde{\lambda}}{8\pi}\Bigg(w(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})-\frac{c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg)=\frac{Nx}{8\pi}\Bigg(w(xy)-\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}(xy)\Bigg)\,.\end{split}

(129)

By replacing the expression for
\tilde{z}
in
\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=0
, we get the cubic equation presented in the main text, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\tilde{\delta}-\frac{Nx}{8\pi}\Bigg(w(xy)-\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}(xy)\Bigg)-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}xw(xy)=\tilde{\delta}-(4C_{F}+N)u(x,y)+Nu^{3}(x,y)\,,\end{split}

(130)

where we have introduced
u(x,y)=\frac{x}{8\pi}w(xy)=\frac{x}{8\pi}\Big(1+\frac{1}{8\pi}xy\Big)^{-1/2}
. By setting
\beta(c)=0
and using the expression for
\tilde{z}
, we get the following second equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\frac{N}{16\pi}\Bigg(1-\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)\Big(w(xy)+w^{3}(xy)\Big)-\frac{C_{F}}{32\pi^{2}}xyw(xy)+\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{3}}x^{2}y^{2}w^{3}(xy)+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{2}}xyw^{3}(xy)\,.\end{split}

(131)

Hence, fixed points are solutions of the above two equations. This system of equations does not have a compact solution, and therefore, we obtain the positions of the fixed point(s) numerically (in principle, one may obtain analytical expressions for the fixed-point values of
x
and
y
, but they are very long and not particularly illuminating).

Let us now focus our attention on the second equation. By writing
xy=8\pi\Big(\frac{1}{w^{2}}-1\Big)
,
x^{2}=(8\pi)^{2}\frac{u^{2}}{w^{2}}
,
u(x,y)
can be expressed solely in terms of
w(xy)

u(x,y)=\sqrt{1-\frac{4C_{F}}{N}\frac{1-w^{2}(xy)}{1+w^{2}(xy)}}\,.

(132)

Since
x,y\geq 0
,
w(xy)
respects
0\leq w(xy)\leq 1
. From the above expression, we then see that
u(x,y)
also respects
0\leq u(x,y)\leq 1
, which puts constraints on the three solutions of Eq. (
130
). Again, the closed-form expressions are not very illuminating and therefore we don’t write them down explicitly. Nevertheless, one can easily see that one solution is always negative and is thus unphysical. The two other solutions are always non-negative, as we can see from Figure
2
in the main text, and correspond to the two possible dissipative fixed points. However, there are three different regimes, depending on the value of
\tilde{\delta}
: (i) For
0<\tilde{\delta}<4C_{F}
, one of the solutions has
u(x,y)>1
, and is therefore unphysical since the associated fixed point has
y<0
. This corresponds to the regime with only a dissipative critical point. (ii) When
\tilde{\delta}> \tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\frac{(4C_{F}+N)^{3}}{N}}
, the two solutions of the cubic equations are complex and there are thus no dissipative fixed points.
\tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}
is the value of
\tilde{\delta}
where the discriminant of the cubic equation vanishes and where the fixed point annihilation occurs. (iii) Finally, for
4C_{F}<\tilde{\delta}<\tilde{\delta}_{\text{Max}}
, the two solutions of the cubic equation are physical, which corresponds to the regime with two dissipative fixed points: the unstable dissiaptive critical point and a new stable dissipative phase.

A.5.2
Adding a magnetic field

Our goal is now to compute universal quantities at the aforementioned fixed points. To obtain the scaling dimension of the primary field
g
, a “magnetic field” term is added to the action

S_{h}[g]=h\int d\tau dx\,\tr\Big( g + g^{-1} \Big)\,,

(133)

which breaks the SU
(N)_{L}

\otimes
SU
(N)_{R}
symmetry down to it’s diagonal SU
(N)
subgroup. Splitting slow and fast modes and expanding to quadratic order in
W
, it is easy to see that

S_{h}[g]=S_{h}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Int,}h}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=h\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( g_s+g_s^{-1} \Big)+\frac{h}{2}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big((g_s + g_s^{-1}) W^2\Big)\,.

(134)

Writing the interaction action in Fourier space yields

S_{\text{Int,}h}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=\frac{h}{2}\int_{p_{s}}\int_{p}\tr\Big( \Tilde{B}_s(p_s) \Tilde{W}(p) \Tilde{W}(-p-p_s) \Big)\,,

(135)

where
B_{s}(\tau,x)=g_{s}+g_{s}^{-1}
. Let us then find the renormalization equation for
h
. At one-loop, we have

S_{h\text{,Eff}}[g_{s}]=S_{h}[g_{s}]+\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,}h}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}+...\,.

(136)

The computation of the expectation value is straightforward

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,}h}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}&=-\frac{h}{2}\int_{p_{S}}\int_{p}\tr\Big( \Tilde{B}_s(p_s) T^a T^b \Big)\expectationvalue{\Tilde{\phi}^a(p) \Tilde{\phi}^b(-p-p_s)}_{f}\\
&=-\frac{h}{2}\int_{p}\tilde{G}(p)\tr\Big( \Tilde{B}_s(0) T^a T^a \Big)\\
&=-\frac{h}{2}C_{F}\,I_{1}\int d\tau dx\tr\Big( g_s + g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(137)

After rescaling by
b^{z+1}\approx 1+(1+z)dl
and using the expression for
I_{1}
derived before, we get the following
\beta
function for
h

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(h)=\Big(2+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}\Big)h-\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}c\tilde{\lambda}h\,w(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})=\Big(2+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}\Big)h-\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}xh\,w(xy)+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\end{split}

(138)

A.5.3
Dynamical critical exponent and scaling dimensions

We are now in a position to compute universal quantities at the different fixed points. We will focus on the dynamical critical exponent
z
, the scaling dimension of
g
,
\Delta_{g}
and the scaling dimension of the energy density operator
\epsilon=\tr\Big( \frac{1}{c^2} \partial_{\tau}g \partial_{\tau}g^{-1} + \partial_{x}g \partial_{x}g^{-1} \Big)
,
\Delta_{\epsilon}
.

First, the dynamical critical exponent
z=1+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}
is obtained directly using Eq. (
129
), evaluated at the various fixed points. Next, to compute
\Delta_{g}
, we need the eigenvalue
e_{h}
, which is computed using
\beta(h)
. By replacing the expression for
\tilde{z}
in Eq. (
138
), we get

e_{h}=2+\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\frac{Nx}{8\pi}\Bigg(w(xy)-\frac{x^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}w^{3}(xy)\Bigg)-\frac{C_{F}x}{4\pi}w(xy)\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,

(139)

which needs to be evaluated at the various fixed points. The scaling dimension
\Delta_{g}
is then given by
\Delta_{g}=1+z-e_{h}=\frac{1}{k}(\tilde{z}-\tilde{e}_{h})
, where we have defined
e_{h}=2+\frac{\tilde{e}_{h}}{k}
. Finally, the calcualtion of
\Delta_{\epsilon}
requires the diagonalization of the following
2\times 2
matrix

M_{xy}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x}\beta(x)&\partial_{y}\beta(x)\\
\partial_{x}\beta(y)&\partial_{y}\beta(y)\end{pmatrix}\Big|_{(x,y)=(x^{*},y^{*})}\,.

(140)

In general, this matrix does not have vanishing entries, which means that the energy density operator
\epsilon
(associated with coupling
x
) and the dissipation operator (associated with coupling
y
) mix among themselves. Therefore, the energy density operator is a linear combination of the two scaling operators
\mathcal{O}_{+}
and
\mathcal{O}_{-}
(eigenvectors of the above matrix), which have an associated eigenvalue
e_{+}
and
e_{-}
respectively, where
e_{+}>e_{-}
. Following
Cardy 1996
, the scaling dimension of the energy density operator is then given by
\Delta_{\epsilon}=1+z-e_{+}=2+\frac{1}{k}(\tilde{z}-\tilde{e}_{+})
whith
e_{+}=\frac{\tilde{e}_{+}}{k}
.

Let us compute these quantities at the various fixed points. We start with the trivial Gaussian fixed point, which has
x^{*}=y^{*}=0
. Since it is relativistic,
z=1
(
\tilde{z}=0
). For the scaling dimensions, we get
\Delta_{g}=0
and
\Delta_{\epsilon}=2
. We now move to the WZW fixed point, located at
x=8\pi
,
y=0
. It is also a relativistic fixed point, thus
z=1
. The scaling dimensions are
\Delta_{g}=\frac{2C_{F}}{k}=\frac{N^{2}-1}{Nk}
and
\Delta_{\epsilon}=2+\frac{2N}{k}
. These two results of course agree with the large-
k
expansion of the exact expressions,
\Delta_{g}=\frac{N^{2}-1}{N(N+k)}
and
\Delta_{\epsilon}=\frac{4N+2k}{N+k}
, as they should
Witten 1984
. Moreover, note that for these two relativistic fixed points, the energy density operator is a scaling operator.

Finally, we must proceed numerically for the two dissipative fixed points since their position cannot be easily obtained analytically. Fig.
3
in the main text depicts critical exponents accurate to
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
at these two fixed points. As already mentioned, due to operator mixing, the biggest of the two eigenvalues must be selected to compute
\Delta_{\epsilon}
. The limit
\tilde{\delta}\rightarrow 4C_{F}
(when the stable dissipative fixed point approaches the WZW fixed point) is interesting since
\Delta_{\epsilon}
at the stable fixed point seemingly approaches
2
, accurate to
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
. This may seem contradictory with the fact that
\Delta_{\epsilon}=2+\frac{2N}{k}
at the WZW fixed point. The resolution of this is as follows: as
\tilde{\delta}\rightarrow 4C_{F}
, the overlap between the energy density operator
\epsilon
(associated with coupling
x
), and the scaling operator with the dominant eigenvalue (i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{+}
in our notation) approaches zero, and exactly at
\delta=4C_{F}
,
\epsilon=\mathcal{O}_{-}
. Therefore, only at
\tilde{\delta}=4C_{F}
,
\Delta_{\epsilon}=1+z-e_{-}=2+\frac{2N}{k}
, which agrees with the expression for the scaling dimension of the energy operator at the WZW fixed point.

A.6
Relation between
\eta
and
z

One can derive the relation between
\tilde{z}
and
\Delta_{g}
presented in the main text using
\beta(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\beta(h)
. Indeed, by setting
\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=0
, we get

0=\tilde{\delta}-\tilde{z}-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}c\tilde{\lambda}w(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\,.

(141)

Moreover, as illustrated in the previous section,
\beta(h)
allows to compute the eigenvalue
e_{h}
, which is itself related with the scaling dimension of
g

\Delta_{g}=1+z-e_{h}=2+\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}-2-\Bigg[\frac{\tilde{z}}{k}-\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}c\tilde{\lambda}w(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg]=\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}c\tilde{\lambda}w(c^{2}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\,.

(142)

By isolating
w
and replacing in Eq. (
141
), we arrive at the desired expression

\tilde{z}=\tilde{\delta}-2k\Delta_{g}\,.

(143)

This relation only holds at
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
. An exact expression valid to all orders can be argued for by demanding the dissipation term to be scale-invariant. By applying the rescaling
x\rightarrow bx
,
\tau\rightarrow b^{z}\tau
, the following condition must be satisfied

0=1+z(\delta-1)-2\Delta_{g}\,.

(144)

Using the fact that
e_{h}=1+z-\Delta_{g}
and
\eta=1+z+2-2e_{h}
, where
\eta
is the anomalous dimension of
g
, one arrives at

z=\frac{2-\eta}{2-\delta}\,.

(145)

Expanding Eq. (
144
) (or Eq. (
145
)) to leading order
1/k
yields Eq. (
143
).

Appendix B
RG analysis of the relativistic theory

This Appendix details the RG analysis for the relativistic theory. The calculation is very similar to the nonrelativistic case, so only the main differences and key points are discussed.

B.1
Expanding in slow and fast modes

The expansion in slow and fast modes proceeds exactly as in the nonrelativistic case. Once again, the resulting action is grouped into three terms:
S[g_{s}g_{f}]=S[g_{s}]+S^{(2)}[W]+S^{(2)}_{\text{Int}}[g_{s},W]
. The first term is the initial action evaluated at
g=g_{s}

\displaystyle\begin{split}S[g_{s}]&=S_{\text{Grad}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{WZ}}[g_{s}]+S_{\text{Dis}}[g_{s}]\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)+\frac{ik}{12\pi}\int_{B^{3}}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k^{2}\gamma\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}\,K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\vb*{r}) g_s^{-1}(\vb*{r}')\Big)\,,\end{split}

(146)

The second term is purely quadratic in
W
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{(2)}[W]&=S_{\text{Grad}}^{(2)}[W]+S_{\text{Dis}}^{(2)}[W]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} W \partial_{\mu} W \Big)-k^{2}\gamma\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\tr\Bigg( \frac{W^2}{2} + \frac{W^{\prime\, 2}}{2} - W W' \Bigg)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf\it p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{a}({\bf\it p})\Big(\Pi^{-1}({\bf\it p})-k^{2}\gamma\tilde{K}({\bf\it p})\Big)\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-{\bf\it p})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf\it p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{a}({\bf\it p})\tilde{G}^{-1}({\bf\it p})\tilde{\phi}^{a}(-{\bf\it p})\,,\end{split}

(147)

where

\Pi({\bf\it p})=\Pi(p)=\frac{\lambda}{p^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{K}({\bf\it p})=\tilde{K}(p)=-\frac{1}{8\pi}p^{2-\delta}\,,

(148)

and the fast propagator is then

\tilde{G}({\bf\it p})=\frac{\lambda}{p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma p^{2-\delta}}\,.

(149)

Note that the prime notation now stands for
W^{\prime}=W({\bf\it r}^{\prime})
, with
{\bf\it r}^{\prime}=(\tau^{\prime},x^{\prime})
. Finally, the interaction term is

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]&=S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]+S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]\\
&=\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\,\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) [\partial_{\mu} W,W] \Big)+k^{2}\gamma\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr[\Big(\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{\prime\,-1}g_{s}\Big)\Bigg(\frac{W^{2}}{2}+\frac{W^{\prime\,2}}{2}-WW^{\prime}\Bigg)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(150)

where

\Phi_{\mu}=g_{s}^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Bigg)g_{s}\,.

(151)

B.2
Fourier representation of interaction terms

The Fourier representation of the two interaction terms is almost identical to that in the nonrelativistic case

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]=i\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s}}\int_{{\bf\it p}}(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu})\tr\Big( \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(\vb*{p}_s) \Tilde{W}(\vb*{p}) \Tilde{W}(-\vb*{p}-\vb*{p}_s) \Big)\,,\end{split}

(152)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Int,Dis}}^{(2)}[g_{s},W]&=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}\\
&=k^{2}\gamma\int_{{\bf\it p}}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime}}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{W}(\vb*{p}) \Tilde{W}(-\vb*{p}-\vb*{p}_s - \vb*{p}_s') \Big)\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{K}(p_{S}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{K}(p_{s})-\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s})\Bigg)\,,\end{split}

(153)

where we have defined
D_{s}({\bf\it r},{\bf\it r}^{\prime})=\mathds{1}-g_{s}^{-1}({\bf\it r}^{\prime})g_{s}({\bf\it r})
, while
\int_{{\bf\it p}}
is a shorthand for
\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf\it p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}
.

B.3
Integration of fast modes

We proceed with the cumulant expansion as in the nonrelativistic case.

B.3.1
Order 1 in interaction action

We start with the expectation value of the interaction action. Let us focus first on the dissipative terms. The expectation values of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are essentially the same as before

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_1}_{f}=\expectationvalue{T_2}_{f}=-\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{2}C_{F}\,I_{1}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}\,K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s^{\prime\, -1} g_s \Big)\,,\end{split}

(154)

where
I_{1}=\int_{{\bf\it p}}\tilde{G}(p)
. For
T_{3}
, the main difference is the expansion of the mixed kernel. Expanding to quadratic order in
{\bf\it p}_{s}
, we get

\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s})\approx-\frac{1}{8\pi}\Bigg[p^{2-\delta}+\frac{2-\delta}{2}\frac{p_{s}^{2}}{p^{\delta}}-\frac{\delta(2-\delta)}{2}\frac{({\bf\it p}\cdot{\bf\it p}_{s})^{2}}{p^{2+\delta}}\Bigg]+...\,,

(155)

where the ellipsis denote higher order terms in
{\bf\it p}_{s}
as well as linear terms, which have a vanishing fast integral. As in the nonrelativistic case, the contribution to
\langle T_{3}\rangle_{f}
from the leading order term vanishes. Since we still need
\delta\sim 1/k
to control the expansion, the third term is of higher order in
1/k
and is thus dropped. In this case, we get

\expectationvalue{T_3}_{f}=\frac{(2-\delta)}{16\pi}C_{F}k^{2}\gamma\int_{p}\frac{\tilde{G}(p)}{p^{\delta}}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.

(156)

Naturally, we still have
\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}=0
. Hence, the expectation value of the interaction action is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int}}^{(2)}[g_s,W]}_{f}&\approx-k^{2}\gamma C_{F}\,I_{1}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}\,K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s g_s^{\prime\, -1} \Big)+\frac{k^{2}\gamma C_{F}}{8\pi}I_{1}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\,,\end{split}

(157)

where the higher order terms in
\delta
have been dropped.

B.3.2
Order 2 in interaction action

We now move to the expectation value of the square of the interaction action. We only need to focus on the same three contributions as in the nonrelativistic case, since all the other terms either vanish or are irrelevant. For the square of the WZW action, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}&=-N\int_{{\bf\it p}}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\tilde{G}^{2}(p)\,\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) \Phi_{\nu}(\vb*{r}) \Big)\\
&=-\frac{N}{2}\int_{{\bf\it p}}p^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(p)\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) \Big)\\
&=-\frac{N}{2}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) \Phi_{\mu}(\vb*{r}) \Big)\,,\end{split}

(158)

where rotational invariance has been used, while
I_{2}=\int_{{\bf\it p}}p^{2}\tilde{G}^{2}(p)
. Using the expression for
\Phi_{\mu}
, the trace yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big( \Phi_{\mu} \Phi_{\mu} \Big)&=\tr[\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g_{s}\Bigg)\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\rho}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g_{s}\Bigg)\Bigg]\\
&=\tr[\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}-\frac{ik}{8\pi k}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\Bigg(g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g_{s}+g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}\Bigg)\\
\hfil&\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{k^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\mu\rho}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g_{s}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g_{s}\Bigg]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(159)

Hence

\expectationvalue{(S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)})^2}_{f}^{c}=\frac{N}{2\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.

(160)

We now move on to the expectation value of the square of the dissipation term. As in the nonrelativistic case, only
\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}
contributes. Following the same steps as before, one finds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}&\approx k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\int_{{\bf\it p}}\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s})\tilde{G}^{2}(p)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\times\Bigg[\Bigg(-\frac{1}{2N}\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}-{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime\prime})+\Big(\frac{N}{4}-\frac{1}{2N}\Big)\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime\prime})\Bigg)\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s'',-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s'-\vb*{p}_s'') \Big)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(161)

Similarly to the nonrelativistic case, the non-vanishing contributions, when expanding in terms of the slow modes, are proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime\prime}}p_{s\,\mu}p_{s\,\nu}^{\prime\prime}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s'',-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s'-\vb*{p}_s'') \Big)=\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,,\end{split}

(162)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime\prime}}p_{s\,\mu}^{\prime}p_{s\,\nu}^{\prime\prime}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s'',-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s'-\vb*{p}_s'') \Big)=-\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(163)

From this, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{T_3^2}_{f}^{c}=\frac{N(2-\delta)^{2}}{4(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}\int_{{\bf\it p}}\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{p^{2\delta}}\tilde{G}(p)\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} \Big)=\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\,,\end{split}

(164)

where rotational invariance has been used.

Finally, the last contribution comes from the mixed term
2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}=-i\frac{N}{2}k^{2}\gamma\int_{{\bf\it p}}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime}}\tilde{K}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s})(2p_{\mu}+p_{s\,\mu}+p_{s\,\mu}^{\prime})\tilde{G}(p)\tilde{G}({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}_{s}+{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime})\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s') \Big)\,.\end{split}

(165)

The nonzero contributions when expanding to linear order in the slow modes are proportional to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime}}p_{s\,\nu}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s') \Big)=-i\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu} \Big)\,,\end{split}

(166)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\bf\it p}_{s},{\bf\it p}_{s}^{\prime}}p_{s\,\nu}^{\prime}\tr\Big( \Tilde{D}_s(\vb*{p}_s,\vb*{p}_s') \Tilde{\Phi}_{\mu}(-\vb*{p}_s-\vb*{p}_s') \Big)=i\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(167)

Performing the slow mode expansion then yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}=\frac{N(2-\delta)}{8\pi}k^{2}\gamma\int_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{p^{\delta}}\tilde{G}^{2}(p)\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu} \Big)=\frac{N}{8\pi}k^{2}\gamma I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu} \Big)+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\,,\end{split}

(168)

where rotational invariance has once again been used in the fast integral. Let us simplify the trace

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} g_s \Phi_{\mu} \Big)&=\tr[\partial_{\mu}g_{s}^{-1}g_{s}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g_{s}-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g_{s}\Bigg)\Bigg]\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)-\frac{ik}{8\pi}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \partial_{\nu} g_s \Big)\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,,\end{split}

(169)

where the second term vanishes since
\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \partial_{\nu} g_s \Big)=\tr\Big( \partial_{\nu} g_s^{-1} \partial_{\mu} g_s \Big)
. Hence

2\expectationvalue{S_{\text{Int,WZW}}^{(2)} T_3}_{f}^{c}=\frac{N}{8\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{\lambda}I_{2}\int d^{2}r\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.

(170)

Therefore, the expectation value of the square of the interaction action is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\expectationvalue{(S^{(2)}_{\text{Int}}[g_s,W])^2}_{f}^{c}&=\frac{N}{2\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)+\frac{N}{2(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{N}{8\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{\lambda}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(171)

Note that in the relativistic case, the unphysical terms with mixed partial derivatives are not generated, since these would break Lorentz invariance.

B.3.3
Effective action full expression

The effective action at one-loop is thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\text{Eff}}[g_{s}]&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\,\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{ik}{12\pi}\int_{B^{3}}\tr\Big( g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \wedge g_s^{-1} dg_s \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+k^{2}\gamma\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}\,K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr\Big(\mathds{1} - g_s(\vb*{r}) g_s^{-1}(\vb*{r}') \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-C_{F}k^{2}\gamma\,I_{1}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}d^{2}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}\,K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\,\tr\Big( \mathds{1} - g_s(\vb*{r}) g_s^{-1}(\vb*{r}') \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{C_{F}k^{2}\gamma}{8\pi}I_{1}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N}{4\lambda^{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N}{4(8\pi)^{2}}k^{4}\gamma^{2}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{N}{16\pi}\frac{k^{2}\gamma}{\lambda}I_{2}\int d^{2}{\bf\it r}\tr\Big( \partial_{\mu} g_s \partial_{\mu} g_s^{-1} \Big)\,.\end{split}

(172)

As in the nonrelativistic case, higher order terms in the cumulant expansion yield irrelevant terms which can be neglected.

B.4
\beta
functions calculation

From the effective action, we see that
\tr\big( \partial_{\mu} g \partial_{\mu} g^{-1} \big)
and
K({\bf\it r}-{\bf\it r}^{\prime})\tr\big( \mathds{1}-g(\vb*{r})g^{-1}(\vb*{r}') \big)
will be renormalized. The
\beta
functions for
\lambda
and
\gamma
are obtained by rescaling
{\bf\it r}\rightarrow b{\bf\it r}
, with
b=e^{dl}
. Once again, only terms coming from
S[g_{s}]
are rescaled, since the fast integrals will be proportional to
dl
. The gradient term is scale-invariant and does not pick up any factor of
b
, while the dissipation term picks up a factor of
b^{\delta}\approx 1+\delta dl
.

B.4.1
Fast integrals

The fast integrals are evaluated over a shell
b^{-1}=e^{-dl}<p<1
. For
I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}=\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf\it p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\lambda}{p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma p^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{e^{-dl}}^{1}dp\frac{p}{p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma p^{2}}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma}dl=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}F(\lambda\gamma)dl\,,\end{split}

(173)

where
F(\lambda\gamma)=\frac{1}{1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma}
. On the other hand,
I_{2}
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}=\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf\it p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}p^{2}}{\big(p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma p^{2}\big)^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{e^{-dl}}^{1}dp\frac{p^{3}}{\big(p^{2}+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma p^{2}\big)^{2}}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\big(1+\frac{k^{2}}{8\pi}\lambda\gamma\big)^{2}}dl=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\pi}F^{2}(\lambda\gamma)dl\,.\end{split}

(174)

B.4.2
\beta
functions

The
\beta
functions for the two couplings are obtained following the same procedure as in the nonrelativistic case. It will again be useful to introduce
\mathcal{O}(k^{0})
couplings
\tilde{\lambda}=k\lambda
and
\tilde{\gamma}=k\gamma
. One finds,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(\tilde{\lambda})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\frac{N\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{8\pi}\Bigg(1-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}^{2}}{(8\pi)^{2}}\Bigg)F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})-\frac{C_{F}}{16\pi^{2}}\tilde{\lambda}^{3}\tilde{\gamma}F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})+\frac{N}{(8\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\lambda}^{4}\tilde{\gamma}^{2}F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})+\frac{N}{32\pi^{2}}\tilde{\lambda}^{3}\tilde{\gamma}F^{2}(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,\end{split}

(175)

\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{k}\Bigg[\tilde{\delta}\tilde{\gamma}-\frac{C_{F}}{2\pi}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,

(176)

with
F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{8\pi}\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}}
.

B.5
Fixed point analysis

B.5.1
Solving for fixed points

Solving
\beta(\tilde{\lambda})=\beta(\tilde{\gamma})=0
, we find three fixed points. There is the trivial Gaussian fixed point at
\tilde{\lambda}=\tilde{\gamma}=0
and the WZW fixed point at
\tilde{\lambda}=8\pi
and
\tilde{\gamma}=0
. The fixed point of our main interest is the dissipative fixed point located at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{128\pi C_{F}^{2}\tilde{\delta}}{64C_{F}^{3}-16C_{F}^{2}N+N\tilde{\delta}^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{(16C_{F}^{2}-\tilde{\delta}^{2})N}{16C_{F}^{2}\tilde{\delta}}\,.\end{split}

(177)

Note that this fixed point only exists for
\tilde{\delta}<4C_{F}
. When
\tilde{\delta}>4C_{F}
, the WZW fixed point becomes unstable, similar to the nonrelativistic theory, the main difference being that now it becomes unstable towards a fixed point at
\tilde{\gamma}\rightarrow\infty
in contrast to the nonrelativistic case, where it became unstable towards the fixed point corresponding to the stable, dissipative phase (see Fig.
1
(b) of the main text).

B.5.2
Scaling dimensions and critical exponents

The calculation of
\Delta_{g}
is once again done by adding a magnetic field to the action. The resulting
\beta
function for
h
is

\beta(h)=2h-\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}\tilde{\lambda}hF(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})+\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})\,,

(178)

from which we get the magnetic field eigenvalue

e_{h}=2-\frac{C_{F}}{4\pi k}\tilde{\lambda}F(\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma})\,,

(179)

which must be evaluated at the various fixed points. For the scaling dimension of the energy density operator
\epsilon=\tr\big( \partial_{\mu} g \partial_{\mu} g^{-1} \big)
, we must obtain the eigenvalues of the following
2\times 2
matrix

M_{\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{\gamma}}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\beta(\tilde{\lambda})&\partial_{\tilde{\gamma}}\beta(\tilde{\lambda})\\
\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}}\beta(\tilde{\gamma})&\partial_{\tilde{\gamma}}\beta(\tilde{\gamma})\end{pmatrix}\Big|_{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\gamma})=(\tilde{\lambda}^{*},\tilde{\gamma}^{*})}\,,

(180)

At the two relativistic fixed points (Gaussian and WZW), the two scaling dimensions are identical as in the nonrelativistic theory. However, for the dissipative fixed point, we can this time obtain closed-form expressions. For the scaling dimension of
g
, we get

\Delta_{g}=\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2k}\,,

(181)

while the eigenvalues of the above
2\times 2
matrix are

e_{\pm}=\frac{-N\tilde{\delta}^{3}\pm\tilde{\delta}\sqrt{N(1024C_{F}^{5}-64C_{F}^{3}\tilde{\delta}^{2}+N\tilde{\delta}^{4})}}{64C_{F}^{3}k}\,.

(182)

As in the nonrelativistic case, the eigenvalue contributing to the scaling dimension of the energy density operator is the biggest, that is
e_{+}
. Therefore, we find

\Delta_{\epsilon}=2-e_{+}=2+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{64C_{F}^{3}k}\Bigg[N\tilde{\delta}^{2}-\sqrt{N(1024C_{F}^{5}-64C_{F}^{3}\tilde{\delta}^{2}+N\tilde{\delta}^{4})}\Bigg]\,.

(183)

Once again, as
\tilde{\delta}\rightarrow 4C_{F}
, we see that
\Delta_{\epsilon}
approaches
2\neq 2+\frac{2N}{k}
, the value at the WZW fixed point. The reason is identical to the nonrelativistic case: as
\tilde{\delta}\rightarrow 4C_{F}
, the overlap between the energy density operator and the scaling operator with the dominant eigenvalue approaches zero, and therefore, at
\tilde{\delta}=4C_{F}
the scaling dimension of the energy operator matches with what is expected for the WZW CFT, namely,
2+\frac{2N}{k}
.

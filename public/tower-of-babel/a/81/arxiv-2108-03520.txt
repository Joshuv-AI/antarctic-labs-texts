1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)e^{2\pi inz}\in S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a non-CM holomorphic cuspidal newform with trivial nebentypus, level
N
, and even integral weight
k\geq 2
. Deligne’s proof of the Weil conjectures implies the Weil–Deligne bound
|a_{f}(p)|\leq 2p^{(k-1)/2}
at all primes
p
and that
a_{f}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n
. Consequently, we may define
\theta_{p}\in[0,\pi]
such that
a_{f}(p)=2p^{(k-1)/2}\cos\theta_{p}
. It is natural to ask how the angles
\theta_{p}
are distributed. In particular, given an interval
I\subseteq[0,\pi]
, we wish to understand the behavior of
\pi_{f,I}(x):=\#\{p\leq x:p\nmid N,\ \theta_{p}\in I\}
. The Sato–Tate conjecture, now a theorem due to Barnet–Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, and Taylor
[
2
]
, asserts that

(1.1)

\pi_{f,I}(x)\sim\mu_{\ST}(I)\pi(x),

where
\mu_{\ST}(I)
is the probability measure given by
\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{I}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta
, and
\pi(x)
is the ordinary prime-counting function.

Despite the successful proof, unconditional effective error bounds remained unattainable without knowing that the symmetric power
L
-functions
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)
have particular analytic properties for any
m\geq 1
(for instance, that they have an analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfy a functional equation). By Langlands functoriality, these analytic properties would be immediately implied by the fact that the
m
-th symmetric power lift
\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\pi_{f}
corresponds to a cuspidal automorphic representation of
GL_{m+1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, where
\pi_{f}
is the automorphic representation of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
corresponding to
f
. Though it was known that any symmetric power
L
-function becomes automorphic after base change to a suitable number field, the result of
[
2
]
could not be made effective without first making this base change constructive; the proof of automorphy would remove the need for a base change altogether.

Let
N
be squarefree. Assuming the automorphy of symmetric power
L
-functions over
\mathbb{Q}
and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, Rouse and Thorner
[
24
]
proved the explicit error bound

|\pi_{f,I}(x)-\mu_{\ST}(I)\mathrm{Li}(x)|\leq 3.33x^{\frac{3}{4}}-\frac{3x^{\frac{3}{4}}\log\log x}{\log x}+\frac{202x^{\frac{3}{4}}\log((k-1)N)}{\log x}\qquad\text{for}\quad x\geq 2.

The automorphy of the symmetric power
L
-functions
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)
over
\mathbb{Q}
had long been expected for all
m
, but was known until recently only for
m\leq 8

[
4
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
. In 2019 and 2020, the result for all
m
arrived in a pair of breakthrough papers of Newton and Thorne
[
19
,
20
]
. These made unconditional a result due to Thorner
[
28
]
, which states that for fixed
f
and
I
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist effectively computable constants
c_{1,\varepsilon},c_{2,\varepsilon}>0
depending on
f
such that

\displaystyle|\pi_{f,I}(x)-\mu_{\ST}(I)\pi(x)|\leq c_{1,\varepsilon}\pi(x)(\log x)^{-\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for}\quad x\geq c_{2,\varepsilon}.

In 2021, Thorner
[
27
]
showed that there exists an effectively computable absolute constant
\Cr{c_{100}}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\pi_{f,I}(x)-\mu_{\ST}(I)\pi(x)\right|\leq\Cr{c_{100}}\pi(x)\frac{\log((k-1)N\log{x})}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\qquad\text{for}\quad x\geq 3.

In this paper, we make this constant
\Cr{c_{100}}
explicit. In particular, we prove the following theorems, which constitute the first unconditional explicit bounds on the error term in the Sato–Tate conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a non-CM holomorphic cuspidal newform with trivial nebentypus, squarefree level
N
, and even integral weight
k\geq 2
. Then for
x\geq 3
, we have

\left|\pi_{f,I}(x)-\mu_{\ST}(I)\pi(x)\right|\leq 58.1\;\pi(x)\frac{\log((k-1)N\log{x})}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be the newform corresponding to a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with arbitrary conductor
N
. Then for
x\geq 3
, the same bound as in Theorem
1.1
applies.

Remark 1.3
.

The requirement that
N
be squarefree in Theorem
1.1
is a technical inconvenience that can be avoided if one is willing to accept a weaker bound. This is discussed in Remark

Remark 1.4
.

The true order of magnitude of this error remains an open question. It is expected that a bound of the shape
c_{f,\varepsilon}x^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
is satisfied. Some convincing evidence for this is exhibited in
[
1
]
in the elliptic curve case.

For

f
corresponding to a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, a result of Elkies
[
9

yields that

Conjecture 1.5

(Atkin–Serre)

Let

, there is a

Following an argument similar to that of Gafni, Thorner, and Wong

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

be a non-CM cuspidal newform of trivial nebentypus, squarefree level
N

\frac{\#\left\{x<p\leq 2x:|a_{f}(p)|\leq 2p^{(k-1)/2}\frac{\log\log p}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right\}}{\#\{x<p\leq 2x\}}\leq 179\cdot\frac{\log((k-1)N\log{x})}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}.

The work of Gafni et al. implies that a density one subset of primes satisfies the Atkin–Serre conjecture for arbitrary

While our work closely follows that of Thorner

[

Recent results due to Newton and Thorne (

Proof of Main Theorem

We first define the following analogue of the first Chebyshev function:

\vartheta_{f,I}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\

Theorem 3.1

\displaystyle\left|\vartheta_{f,I}(x)-\mu_{\ST}(I)x\right|\leq{\textstyle\left(1+\frac{1}{36260}\right)}x.

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{\pm}\Bigg\{\left(1+\frac{4}{M+1}\right)\left|\pm\frac{1}{y}\int_{x}^{x\pm y}(\Theta_{0}(u)-u)du\right|

Lemma 6.1

Proof.

Let

\bigg|\frac{(1+\theta)^{\rho+1}-1-(\rho+1)\theta}{\rho(\rho+1)\theta^{2}}\bigg|=\bigg|\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{1}^{1+\theta}\frac{u^{\rho}-1}{\rho}du\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1}{|\rho|}|\rho|u\,du=\frac{1}{2},

where the inequality follows from the bound

\left|\frac{(x+y)^{\rho+1}-x^{\rho+1}}{y\rho(\rho+1)}\right|\leq\frac{x^{\beta+1}}{|\rho||\rho+1|}\left(\left(1+\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\beta+1}+1\right)\leq\frac{x}{|\rho||\rho+1|}(2\theta^{-1}+2+\theta).\qed

\frac{x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)}=\frac{x}{s}+x\log x-x+\cdots,\qquad\frac{x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)}=-\frac{1}{s+1}-1-\log x+\cdots,

respectively. By casework on the value of
m\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu4)
and the size of
k
, we find that
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)
has zeros at
s=-1
and
s=0
of orders
2
and
0
for
4\nmid m,k=2
; orders
1
and
1
for
4\mid m
,
k=2
; orders 1 and 0 for
4\nmid m
,
k\geq 4
; and orders 0 and 1 for
4\mid m,k\geq 4
, respectively. In each case, by expanding the series at each point and evaluating at
x+y
and at
x
, we find

\displaystyle\Res\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)\frac{(x+y)^{s+1}-x^{s+1}}{s(s+1)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda_{0}|y+\int_{x}^{x+y}\log(z)\,dz+\log\left(1+\frac{y}{x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda_{0}|y+y\log(x+y)+\frac{y}{x},

where
\lambda_{0}
is defined as either
\frac{L^{\prime}(0)}{L(0)}
or
\lim_{s\to 0}(\frac{L^{\prime}(0)}{L(0)}-\frac{1}{s})
, whichever is well-defined.

We proceed to bound
\lambda_{0}
. If
4\nmid n
, then
\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}
extends analytically to
s=0
, and so we may write

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(0,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)=\frac{\log q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}{f}}}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(0,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)+\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}.

To estimate
\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}
, we first use Corollary
4.6
, which gives us the bound

\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\overline{\rho}}\leq\frac{2}{\Cr{zfr5}}{(m+7)^{2}\log(\Cr{zfr3}(k-1)Q(m+7))}.

Hence we may bound the sum directly by

\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\frac{N(1,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)}{\Cr{zfr5}}{(m+7)^{2}\log(\Cr{zfr3}(k-1)Q(m+7))}+\sum_{|\gamma|>1}\frac{1}{\rho}.

The last sum can be estimated using results from Section 5. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}\frac{1}{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sigma_{1}-\beta}\cdot\frac{(\sigma_{1}-\beta)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}{\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}-\beta}{(\sigma_{1}-\beta)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}\right),

from which choosing
\sigma_{1}=\frac{3}{2},\ T=0
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}\frac{1}{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq 5\bigg(\Real\left(\frac{1}{2}\log q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}\right)+\Real\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}\left(\frac{3}{2},\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)+\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(\frac{3}{2},\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 5\bigg(\frac{m+1}{2}\log Q+(m+1)\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\right|+\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(\frac{3}{2},\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)\bigg).

Thus it only remains to find explicit bounds for
\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(0,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)
and
\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(\frac{3}{2},\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)
. The first may be bounded via Lemma
5.5
, as regardless of the value of
m\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu4)
, we may write

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(\frac{3}{2},\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{11(m+1)}{6}+\frac{m+1}{4}\log\left(1+\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{(m+1)(k-1)}{2}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{(m+1)\log(2\pi)}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Cr{res32}(m+1)+\frac{m+1}{2}\log\left((m+1)(k-1)\right),

where
\Cr{res32}=\frac{11}{6}+\frac{1}{2}\log(\frac{7}{4})-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\leq 1.1943
.

As for
\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(0,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)
, we note that
(j-1/2)(k-1)\geq 1
in all cases except
j=1,k=2
, so that we may use Lemma
5.5
again. As
\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})/\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})<-1.9
, we omit it by non-negativity and reach the bound

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(0,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Cr{res32}(m+1)+\frac{m+1}{2}\log\left((m+1)(k-1)\right).

In the case that
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}{f})
does not extend analytically to 0, the argument must be changed slightly. Indeed, as
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}{f})
, we may write

\Real\left(\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}\right)=\frac{\log q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}\!{f}}}{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\sigma,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)+\frac{1}{s}\right)+\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)-\frac{1}{s}\right),

where the second and third terms extend analytically to
\sigma=0
.
Thus, taking
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}{f})-\frac{1}{s}
as
s\rightarrow 0
, we see that it is indeed bounded by the same quantity, as the contributions from the poles of
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}
and
\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\gamma}
cancel. Hence in either case we may use the above bound, so that

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N(1,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)}{\Cr{zfr5}}{(m+7)^{2}\log(\Cr{zfr3}(k-1)Q(m+7))}

\displaystyle\quad+3(m+1)\log((k-1)Q(m+1))+\Cr{reslast}(m+1),

where
\Cr{reslast}:=6\Cr{res32}+|\zeta^{\prime}(\frac{3}{2})/\zeta(\frac{3}{2})|<8.6705
.
∎

7.
Application to the Atkin–Serre Conjecture

Here we apply Theorem
3.1
to the Atkin–Serre conjecture. We require the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f
be as in Theorem
1.6
. Then for
x\geq 3
,

\#\{x<p\leq 2x:p\nmid N,\ \cos\theta_{p}\in I\}\leq\Cr{c_{101}}(\pi(2x)-\pi(x))\left(\mu_{\ST}(I)+\Cr{c_{100}}\frac{\log((k-1)N\log{x})}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right),

where
\Cr{c_{101}}:=3.015
.

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequality to
\pi_{f,I}(2x)-\pi_{f,I}(x)
and using either Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
, we first arrive at the result

\pi_{f,I}(2x)-\pi_{f,I}(x)\leq(\pi(2x)+\pi(x))\left(\mu_{\ST}(I)+\Cr{c_{100}}\frac{\log((k-1)N\log{x})}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right).

Now, we use that
\pi(2x)+\pi(x)\sim 3(\pi(2x)-\pi(x))\sim 3\pi(x)
. In particular, using the bound
\frac{x}{\log{x}-1.1}<\pi(x)<\frac{x}{\log{x}-1}
for
x>60\,184
given by
[
8
]
, we see that

\displaystyle\pi(2x)+\pi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2x}{\log(2x)-1.1}+\frac{x}{\log{x}-1.1}

\displaystyle\leq\Cr{c_{101}}\left(\frac{2x}{\log(2x)-1}-\frac{x}{\log{x}-1.1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Cr{c_{101}}\left(\pi(2x)-\pi(x)\right),

where the bound holds for
x\geq 10^{100}
with
\Cr{c_{101}}
defined as in the statement of the lemma. Noting that the listed bound holds trivially for
3\leq x\leq 10^{100}
, we arrive at the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

First, note that as
a_{f}(p)=2p^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\cos\theta_{p}
and since

is decreasing in

\ell(x)\leq 1

Appendix A
Conductor of Symmetric Powers of Elliptic Curves

This section presents a short proof of the upper bound on the arithmetic conductor
q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}
of
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f)
, for
m\geq 1
stated in Section
2
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve with conductor
N=q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{1}f}
, and suppose
m\geq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}\leq N^{m+1}.

Our bound will rely crucially on the computations of
[
17
]
, and our result is a uniform sharpening of the bound
q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}\ll N^{6m}
from
[
7
, Appendix A.2]
. We write

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}=\prod_{p}p^{\epsilon_{m}(p)+\delta_{m}(p)},

where
\epsilon_{m}(p)
and
\delta_{m}(p)
are the local tame conductor and local wild conductor at
p
respectively.

Proof.

We freely use results from
[
17
, Section 3]
. If
p
is a prime of multiplicative reduction, then
\epsilon_{m}(p)=m
and
\delta_{m}(p)=0
. If
p
is of additive reduction, we know that
\epsilon_{m}(p)\leq m+1
; since
\delta_{m}(p)=0
for
p\geq 5
, it suffices to bound the wild conductors at
2
and
3
. Recall that in the case of additive reduction at
p
, we have
v_{p}(N)\geq 2
, where
v_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic valuation. Let

\displaystyle d_{2}:=\max\left\{0,\frac{v_{2}(N)}{2}-1\right\}(m+1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{3}:=\max\left\{0,\frac{v_{3}(N)}{2}-1\right\}(m+1).

By
[
17
, Table 2]
and
[
17
, Table 3]
, we see that
\delta_{m}(2)\leq d_{2}
and
\delta_{m}(3)\leq d_{3}
, respectively. Thus, we may write

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{Sym}^{m}f}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d_{2}}3^{d_{3}}\prod_{p\mid N}p^{m+1}\leq N^{m+1}.\qed

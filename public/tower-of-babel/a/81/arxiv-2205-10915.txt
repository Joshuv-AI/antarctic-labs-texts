Stable degenerations of singularities
Chenyang Xu, Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. For any Kawamata log terminal (klt) singularity and any minimizer of its normalized volume function, we prove that the associated graded ring is always finitely generated, as conjectured by Chi Li. As a consequence, we complete the last step of establishing the Stable Degeneration Conjecture proposed by Chi Li and the first named author for an arbitrary klt singularity.



1.
Introduction

The algebraic K-stability of Fano varieties has been a very active research topic in recent years. The local analogue is a stability theory for Kawamata log terminal (klt) singularities. There were two roots from earlier analytic works: the first is the work
[
33
]
, which shows that for a klt singularity
x\in X
with a torus
\mathbb{T}
action, the existence of the Sasaki-Einstein metric along a Reeb vector field
\xi
implies that
\xi
minimizes a normalized volume function defined among all Reeb vector fields; the second one is the study of the Ricci-flat metric tangent cone for a singularity on the Kähler-Einstein Fano variety which is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of Kähler-Einstein manifolds
[
12
]
.

The general algebraic local stability theory was initiated in
[
30
]
. It centers around the problem of minimizing the
normalized volume function

\widehat{\rm vol}\colon\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\},

defined on the non-archimedean link
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
(i.e. the set of valuations centered at
x
) of a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
.
The complete conjectural picture, named the Stable Degeneration Conjecture, was proposed in
[
30
,
27
]
(see
[
39
, Conjecture 4.4]
), which says for
any
klt singularity, a minimizer of
\widehat{\rm vol}
induces a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant degeneration to a K-semistable Fano cone singularity (for some torus
\mathbb{T}
); and moreover, such a degeneration is unique.

1.1.
Main theorem

After a period of intensive works, including
[
30
,
29
,
25
,
8
,
28
,
27
,
26
,
40
,
37
]
etc., the only unknown part of the Stable Degeneration Conjecture is the local higher rank finite generation, which we will address in the current paper, i.e. we confirm
[
30
, Conjecture 7.1(5)]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Local higher rank finite generation)
.

Let
x\in(X=\mathrm{Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity. Let
v
be a minimizer of
\widehat{\rm vol}
on
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
. Then the associated graded algebra
{\rm gr}_{v}R:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is finitely generated, where

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}:=\{f\in R\ |\ v(f)\geq\lambda\}\mbox{\ \ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ \ }\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}:=\{f\in R\ |\ v(f)>\lambda\}.

Here when the value monoid
\Phi
of the image
v\colon R\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
generates an abelian group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e. the rational rank of
v
is one, the finite generation property follows from
[
3
]
(see
[
8
,
28
]
). However, the case when
v
is of higher rational rank needs substantially more work.

As we mentioned above, together with the aforementioned earlier works, this completes the local stability theory of klt singularities.

Theorem 1.2

(Stable Degeneration Conjecture)
.

Let
x\in(X={\rm Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity.

(1)

Up to rescaling,
\widehat{\rm vol}
has a unique minimizer
v
in
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
which is quasi-monomial with a finitely generated associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R
, and it induces a degeneration of
(X,\Delta)
to a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity
(X_{0}={\rm Spec}({\rm gr}_{v}R),\Delta_{0};\xi_{v})
, where
\xi_{v}
is the Reeb vector field induced by the grading of
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R
.

(2)

Conversely, assume a quasi-monomial valuation
v
has a finite generated associated graded ring
{\rm gr}_{v}R
such that
\left(X_{0}={\rm Spec}({\rm gr}_{v}R),\Delta_{0}\right)
is klt where
\Delta_{0}
is the degeneration of
\Delta
and
(X_{0},\Delta_{0};\xi_{v})
is K-semistable as a log Fano cone singularity. Then
v
is a minimizer of
\widehat{\rm vol}
on
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
.

(3)

(X_{0},\Delta_{0};\xi_{v})
has a degeneration to a unique K-polystable log Fano cone singularity
(Y,\Delta_{Y};\xi)
, which admits a Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric when the ground field
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
.

We refer to
[
26
, Section 2.2]
for the definition of log Fano cone singularities and K-stability notions for them.

The above theorem combines many previous works together with Theorem
1.1
. More precisely, the normalized volume function was first introduced in
[
30
]
. The existence of a minimizer
v
was proved in
[
8
]
, which was shown to be quasi-monomial in
[
40
]
. Then
[
37
]
proved that a minimizer is unique up to rescaling. In
[
29
,
28
]
, the rest of (1) and (2), i.e. the K-semistable characterization of the minimizer, was proved for the case when the rational rank of
v
is equal to one.
Theorem
1.1
addresses the finite generation of the associated graded ring for
v
in a general case. Assuming this
[
27
]
proves the K-semistable characterization in all cases. The existence and uniqueness of a K-polystable degeneration in (3) is proved in
[
26
]
.
When
k=\mathbb{C}
, such a degeneration admits a Ricci-flat Kähler cone metric by
[
31
, Theorem 2.9]
, which in turn relies on the global finite generation theorem proved in
[
32
]
.

As a consequence we also get the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity. Then its local volume

\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta):=\inf_{v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}}\widehat{\rm vol}(v)

is an algebraic number.

1.2.
Finite generation via multiple degeneration

Higher rank finite generation plays a key role to complete the algebraic theory of K-stability. In the global setting, one needs to show that for any log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
that is not uniformly K-stable, any valuation
v
computing the
\delta
-invariant has a finitely generated associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}_{v}(R)
for
R=\bigoplus_{m\in r\cdot\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(-m(K_{X}+\Delta))
where
r>0
is a sufficiently divisible integer. This was solved in
[
32
]
, and the proof can be split into two parts. In the first part, it is shown that such a valuation
v
is a monomial lc place on a log resolution
(Y,E)
of
(X,\Delta)
, for a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
whose birational transform on
Y
contains an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-subdivisor. Such a complement
\Gamma
is called special (with respect to the log resolution
(Y,E)
). It is a key observation in
[
32
]
that when the rational rank of
v
is greater than one, one should focus on lc places of this smaller class of
\mathbb{Q}
-complements to achieve the finite generation. Then in the second part, it is shown that any
v
satisfying the above assumption indeed induces a finitely generated associated graded ring
{\rm gr}_{v}R
. One major input in the proof of the second part is to show that for any small rational perturbation
w
of
v
, the induced degeneration
X_{w}:={\rm Proj}\Big({\rm gr}_{w}(R)\Big)
yields a log Fano pair with an
\alpha
-invariant bounded from below by a constant (which does not depend on
w
), therefore belongs to a bounded family of pairs by
[
17
]
.

For the local problem, one can mimic the argument in the first step as above to construct a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement for a valuation minimizing
\widehat{\rm vol}
, and then perturbations of
v
yields degenerations to log Fano cones. However, a main issue is that the boundedness result for such log Fano cones, unlike the case for Fano varieties, is not known (see e.g. Conjecture
1.7
). Therefore, we have to develop a new approach to circumvent the usage of boundedness result. In
[
41
]
, it was proposed to use multiple degenerations over a base
\mathbb{A}^{n}
, to attack the finite generation. More precisely, if
\Gamma
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
, then
[
41
]
shows that one can use any
r
divisorial lc places of
(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
to obtain a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
-equivariant flat degeneration of
(X,\Delta)
over
\mathbb{A}^{r}
, which indeed yields a locally stable family in the sense of
[
22
]
*Definition-Theorem 4.7.

The key recipe for this strategy to work is to show that the degeneration family has irreducible fibers over all points in
\mathbb{A}^{r}
. This is in general a delicate condition. One of our main observations in this paper is that a refinement of the notion of the special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement can be used to guarantee that the degenerations induced by its lc places have irreducible fibers.
More precisely, besides the log smooth model considered in the original definition of special
\mathbb{Q}
-complements in
[
32
]
, we also consider models

\pi\colon(Y,E={\rm Ex}(\pi))\to X

such that
\pi
is an isomorphism over
X\setminus\{x\}
,
(Y,E+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)
is qdlt (Definition
2.2
), and
-(K_{Y}+E+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)
is ample.
We call this
a Kollár model
, as it is a generalization of Kollár component to the case of multiple exceptional components.
If we write
E=\sum^{r}_{i=1}E_{i}
, then a key technical result says that the multiple degenerations over
\mathbb{A}^{r}
given by
E_{i}
always have irreducible central fibers.
This is sufficient to imply the finite generation of the associated graded ring for any valuation
v\in{\rm QM}(Y,E)
, as we will see in Section
4.3
.

Then starting from any log smooth model
(Y,E)
admitting a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
and a monomial lc place

v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)\cap{\rm LC}(X,\Delta+\Gamma),

we will show that
v
is contained in
\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
for a Kollár model
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to X
. For this, we find a process to modify
(Y,E)
by first blowing up, and then running a minimal model program. See Section
3.3
.

To summarize, we show the following technical statement (Theorem
4.1
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
x\in(X={\rm Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity and let
v
be a monomial lc place of a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
with respect to some log smooth model
(Y,E)
of
(X,\Delta)

(
Definition
3.1
)
. Then the associated graded ring
{\rm gr}_{v}(R)
is finitely generated.

We remark that a special case of this statement, i.e. when
x\in(X,\Delta)
is a log Fano cone singularity (with respect to some torus
\mathbb{T}
) and
v
is
\mathbb{T}
-invariant, is proved in
[
15
]
by improving arguments in
[
32
]
.

Theorem
1.4
implies the following global version of the higher rank finite generation statement, which was first proved in
[
32
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Corollary 1.5

(
[
32
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair. Let
v
be a monomial lc place of a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
with respect to some log smooth model
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
. Then the associated graded algebra
{\rm gr}_{v}(R)
is finitely generated, where
R=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(-mr(K_{X}+\Delta))
for some
r
such that
r(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier.

Along the way of the proof, we also get the following more precise characterization for how the induced degenerations vary as we vary the valuations.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
x\in(X={\rm Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity and let
(Y,E)
be a toroidal model over
(X,\Delta)
with a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
. Then
\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
is a rational polyhedral subset of
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
, and for any two valuations
v
and
w
on the same open face of
\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R\cong\mathrm{gr}_{w}R
of the associated graded algebras.

For the definition of toroidal models (resp.
\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
), see Section
2.2.2
(resp. Definition
3.1
).

The remaining part of the proof is showing that any minimizer
v
is a monomial lc place for a log smooth model
(Y,E)
over
(X,\Delta)
with a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement. This is similar to
[
32
, Corollary 3.4]
, but we replace the basis type divisors in the global settings by the construction in
[
37
]
. See Section
3.1
.

Finally, we want to restate the following conjecture that is well known to experts.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Fix a positive integer
n
, a positive number
\delta
and a finite set
I\subseteq[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
. Then the set

\Big\{(X,\Delta)\ \Big|\begin{array}[]{l}x\in(X,\Delta)\mbox{ is a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity},\\
\dim X=n,\,{\rm Coeff}(\Delta)\subseteq I,\,\mbox{and }\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)\geq\delta\end{array}\Big\}

is bounded.

Theorem
1.2
and Conjecture
1.7
together imply that for any finite set
I
of rational numbers and any positive number
\delta
, the class of all
n
-dimensional klt singularities
x\in(X,\Delta)
with
\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)\geq\delta
and
{\rm Coeff}(\Delta)\subseteq I
is bounded up to special degeneration (i.e.
specially bounded
). They also imply that the local volumes of
n
-dimensional klt singularities with
{\rm Coeff}(\Delta)\subseteq I
satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC) and the only accumulation point is zero. This is because Theorem
1.2
yields a degeneration of
(x,X,\Delta)
to a K-semistable Fano cone singularity
(X_{0},\Delta_{0};\xi_{v})
and we know that

\widehat{\rm vol}(x,X,\Delta)=\widehat{\rm vol}_{(X,\Delta)}(v)=\widehat{\rm vol}_{(X_{0},\Delta_{0})}(\xi_{v})=\widehat{\rm vol}(X_{0},\Delta_{0};\xi_{v}),

where the second equality follows from
[
27
, Lemma 2.58]
. As we mentioned, a major difference between our current approach with the one in
[
32
]
is that we circumvent the above type of boundedness result, by considering multiple degenerations. Nevertheless, we believe Conjecture
1.7
still plays a key role for advancing our understanding of the local K-stability theory.

2.1.
Qdlt pairs

The concept of qdlt pairs is introduced in
[
10
, Definition 35]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
D\subseteq X
be a reduced divisor on a normal variety. We say
(X,D)
is
toroidal
if for any
x\in X
there exists an SNC pair
x^{\prime}\in(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
and a finite abelian group
G
acting on it preserving every irreducible component of
D^{\prime}
, such that the
G
-action is free in codimension one and Zariski locally we have

\left(x\in(X,D)\right)\cong\left(x^{\prime}\in(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\right)/G.

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
]
*Definition 35)
.

A
qdlt
pair
(X,\Delta)
is a log pair such that there exists an open set
U\subset X
such that
\Delta|_{U}
is reduced,
(U,\Delta|_{U})
is toroidal, and the log discrepancy
A_{X,\Delta}(F)>0
for any prime divisor
F
over
X
whose center on
X
is contained in
X\setminus U
. Note that when
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
is irreducible, being qdlt is equivalent to being plt. Similarly we can define
sub-qdlt
pairs by allowing negative coefficients in
\Delta
.

Qdlt pairs behave very similar to dlt pairs. We list a few properties of this kind.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
(X,\Delta)
is a qdlt pair and
X
is quasi-projective. Let
\Delta_{0}
and
\Delta_{1}
be effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
(
not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
)
. Assume that
\Delta_{0}\leq\Delta
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta_{1})
does not contain any stratum of
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
. Then there exist some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D\geq\Delta_{0}+\varepsilon\Delta_{1}
such that
(X,D)
is qdlt and
\lfloor D\rfloor=\lfloor\Delta_{0}\rfloor
. Furthermore, if
X
is projective over
S
and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample over
S
, then
D
can be chosen so that
-(K_{X}+D)
remains ample over
S
.

In particular, we can always make small perturbation of a qdlt pair to reduce the number of components with coefficient
1
.

Proof.

This is proved just as in
[
19
]
*Proposition 2.43. Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
, and let
U
be the open set as in Definition
2.2
. Since
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta_{1})
does not contain any stratum of
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
, after possibly shrinking
U
we may assume that
\Delta_{1}|_{U}=0
. Choose some
r>0
such that
r(\Delta-\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{1})
has integer coefficients and
r(\Delta-\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{1})|_{U}
is Cartier. By the following Lemma
2.4
, we may choose some
m\gg 0
such that the linear system
|mH+r(\Delta-\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{1})|
is basepoint free on
U
. Let
G_{0}\in|mH+r(\Delta-\Delta_{0}-\Delta_{1})|
be a general member and let
G=\frac{1}{r}G_{0}
. Then

K_{X}+\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}+G\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X}+\Delta+\frac{m}{r}H

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and by Bertini’s theorem
(X,\Delta+\mathrm{Supp}(G))
is toroidal on
U
. Consider the convex combination

(X,D_{\varepsilon}:=\Delta_{0}+(1-\varepsilon)(\Delta-\Delta_{0})+\varepsilon(\Delta_{1}+G))

of
(X,\Delta)
and
(X,\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}+G)
. This pair has toroidal support on
U
and
\lfloor D_{\varepsilon}\rfloor=\lfloor\Delta_{0}\rfloor
when
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. If
\pi\colon Y\to(X,\Delta+\Delta_{1}+G)
is a log resolution and
F\subseteq Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(U)
is a prime divisor, then
A_{X,D_{\varepsilon}}(F)\to A_{X,\Delta}(F)>0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus
(X,D_{\varepsilon})
is qdlt when
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
D_{\varepsilon}
gives the desired boundary. Moreover,

K_{X}+D_{\varepsilon}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X}+\Delta+\frac{m\varepsilon}{r}H,

hence
-(K_{X}+D_{\varepsilon})
remains ample if
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample. ∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a normal variety, and
D
a Weil divisor on
X
. Let
H
be an ample divisor. Then the base locus of
|mH+D|
for
m\gg 0
is the non-Cartier locus of
D
.

Proof.

This should be well-known to experts. First we show that every closed point
x\in X
in the non-Cartier locus of
D
is also in the base locus of
|mH+D|
. In fact, if there exists
G\in|mH+D|
such that
x\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(G)
then
G
is clearly Cartier at
x
, hence so is
D\sim G-mH
, a contradiction. Next we take some
m\gg 0
such that the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+mH)
is globally generated. For every
x\in X
where
D
is Cartier, we have
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+mH)\otimes k(x)\cong k(x)
, while global generation induces a surjection

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+mH))\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+mH)\otimes k(x).

Thus we may find a section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+mH))
that does not vanish at
x
; in other words, we get a divisor
G\in|mH+D|
that does not pass through
x
. As
x
is arbitrary, this implies that the base locus of
|mH+D|
is contained in the non-Cartier locus of
D
, and we are done.
∎

The next lemma describes the geometry of the lc centers of qdlt pairs.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a qdlt pair. Then the lc centers of
(X,\Delta)
are normal, and they are exactly the irreducible components of various intersections
D_{1}\cap\dots\cap D_{r}
where
D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
are irreducible components of
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
.

Proof.

By definition the lc centers intersect the toroidal locus
U
and then it is not hard to see that any lc center is given in this form. Let us show that any such intersection
D_{1}\cap\dots\cap D_{r}
is normal. Using induction on
r
it suffices to show that each component
D_{i}
is normal, since by adjunction,
(D_{1},\mathrm{Diff}_{D_{1}}(\Delta-D_{1}))
is qdlt. Without loss of generality we assume
i=1
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists some
\Delta^{\prime}\geq D_{1}
such that
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is qdlt and
\lfloor\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor=D_{1}
(which is irreducible). By definition this implies that
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is plt, thus by
[
21
]
*Theorem 4.16 we see that
D_{1}
is normal and we are done.
∎

of

reduced divisors

2.2.
Valuations and models

2.2.1.
Valuations

Let
X
be a reduced, irreducible (separated) variety defined over
\mathbbm{k}
. A
real valuation
of its function field

K(X)
is a non-constant map

v\colon K(X)^{\times}(:=K(X)\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying:

•

v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)
;

•

v(f+g)\geq\min\{v(f),v(g)\}
;

•

v(\mathbbm{k}^{\times})=0
.

We set
v(0)=+\infty
. The
rational rank

{\rm rat.rk.}(v)
of a valuation
v
is the dimension of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space in
\mathbb{R}
spanned by
v(K(X)^{\times})
. A valuation
v
gives rise
to a valuation ring

\mathcal{O}_{v}:=\{f\in K(X)\mid v(f)\geq 0\}.

We say a real valuation
v
is centered at a scheme-theoretic
point
x=c_{X}(v)\in X
if we have a local inclusion

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{v}
of local rings.
Notice that the center of a valuation, if exists,
is unique since
X
is separated. Denote by
\mathrm{Val}_{X}

the set of real valuations of
K(X)
that admits a center
on
X
. For a closed point
x\in X
, we denote by
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
the set
of real valuations of
K(X)
centered at
x\in X
. Note that a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is centered at a closed point
x\in X
if and only if
v(f)>0
for all
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{x}
.

For each valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
and any non-negative real number
\lambda
, we define the valuation ideal

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v):=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\mid v(f)\geq\lambda\}.

Then it is clear that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)
is an
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
-primary ideal for each
\lambda>0
and
x=c_{X}(v)
.

Given a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
and a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we may evaluate
\mathfrak{a}
along
v
by setting

v(\mathfrak{a}):=\min\{v(f)\mid f\in\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X,c_{X}(v)}\}.

It follows from the above definition that if
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
are nonzero ideals, then
v(\mathfrak{a})\geq v(\mathfrak{b})
.
Additionally,
v(\mathfrak{a})>0
if and only if
c_{X}(v)\in{\rm Supp}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathfrak{a})
.
We endow
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
with the weakest topology such that,
for every nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}
on
X
, the map
\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
v\mapsto v(\mathfrak{a})
is continuous.
The subset
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}\subseteq\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is endowed with
the subspace topology. In some literature, the space
\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
is called the
non-archimedean link
of
x\in X
. For more background on valuation spaces, see
[
18
, Section 4]
.

Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
a valuation, we can also define
v(D)
to be
v(f)
where
f
is a defining equation of
D
at
c_{X}(v)
. And similarly if
D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, we can define
v(D)=\frac{1}{m}v(mD)
for a sufficiently divisible positive integer
m
. For a graded sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of ideals, we define

v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\inf_{k}\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{k})}{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty,\mathfrak{a}_{k}\neq 0}\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{k})}{k}.

Let
\mu\colon Y\to X
be a proper birational morphism with
Y
a normal variety. For a prime divisor
E
on
Y
(we call any such
E
a divisor over
X
), we define a valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{E}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
that sends each rational function in
K(X)^{\times}=K(Y)^{\times}
to its order of vanishing along
E
. Note that
the center
c_{X}(\mathrm{ord}_{E})
is the generic point of
\mu(E)
.
We say that
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is a
divisorial valuation
if there exists
E
as above and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
v=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{E}
.

When
X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is affine and
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is a real valuation, we define the associated graded algebra as

\mathrm{gr}_{v}R:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}(v)

where
\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}(v)=\bigcup_{\mu>\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}(v)=\{f\in R\ |\ v(f)>\lambda\}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
R
be an integral domain and let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a graded sequence of ideals such that
\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{gr}(R,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}
is an integral domain. Then there exists some discrete valuation
v
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\mathfrak{a}_{i}(v)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

For any
0\neq f\in R
set
v(f)=\sup\{i\,|\,f\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}
. We need to show that
v
is a valuation. Since
\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,we clearly have
v(f+g)\geq\min\{v(f),v(g)\}
. Suppose that
f\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}
and
g\in\mathfrak{a}_{j}\setminus\mathfrak{a}_{j+1}
, then their reductions
\bar{f}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}
and
\bar{g}\in\mathfrak{a}_{j}/\mathfrak{a}_{j+1}
are non-zero in
\mathrm{gr}(R,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
. Since
\mathrm{gr}(R,\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is an integral domain we get
\bar{f}\bar{g}\neq 0
, which gives
fg\in\mathfrak{a}_{i+j}\setminus\mathfrak{a}_{i+j+1}
and hence
v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)
.
∎

2.2.2.
Models

A model
(Y,E)
over a pair
(X,\Delta)
consists of a projective birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
and a reduced divisor
E
on
Y
. We call it a
log smooth
(resp.
toroidal
)
model
if
(Y,\mathrm{Supp}(E+\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta))
is simple normal crossing (SNC) (resp. toroidal).

Definition 2.8
.

A valuation
v=v_{\alpha}
is called quasi-monomial if there exists a log smooth model
(Y,E=E_{1}+\dots+E_{r})\to X
and
r
nonnegative numbers
\alpha=(\alpha_{1}
,…,
\alpha_{r})
such that

(1)

\bigcap^{r}_{i=1}E_{i}\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

There exists a component
C\subseteq\bigcap^{r}_{i=1}E_{i}
, such that around the generic point
\eta
of
C
,
E_{i}
is given by the local equation
(z_{i}=0)
and

(3)

for any
f\in\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
, we may write
f=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}_{\geq 0}}c_{\beta}z^{\beta}
(where
z^{\beta}=z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{\beta_{r}}
) in
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,\eta}
so that each
c_{\beta}
is either zero or a unit,
then

v_{\alpha}(f)=\min\left\{\sum\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\,|\,c_{\beta}\neq 0\right\}.

For example, if we choose
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}_{\geq 0}
, the corresponding valuation is given by the toroidal divisor coming from the weighted blow up with weight
\alpha
.
The rational rank of the quasi-monomial valuation
v_{\alpha}
is equal to the dimension of the

\mathbb{Q}
-linear vector space

{\rm span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}.

In particular, the rational rank is always bounded above by the codimension of
\eta
. On the other hand, by further blowing up we can always choose a log smooth model so that

{\rm codim}_{Y}(\eta)={\rm rat.rk.}(v)=r,

in which case the weights
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.

In the following, we also need a slight generalization of Definition
2.8
, namely instead of assuming that
(Y,E_{1}+\dots+E_{r})
is simple normal crossing at
\eta
, we assume

(\eta\in Y,E_{1}+\dots+E_{r})\cong(\eta^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+E^{\prime}_{r})/G,

where
(\eta^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+E^{\prime}_{r})
is a semi-local SNC pair and
G
is an abelian group, i.e.
(\eta\in Y,E_{1}+\dots+E_{r})
is toroidal around
\eta
. Denote the pull back of
E_{i}
by
n_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}
. Then for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
, we can define
v_{\alpha}
to be the restriction of
v_{\alpha^{\prime}}
at
\eta^{\prime}\in(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+E^{\prime}_{r})
as in Definition
2.8
, where
\alpha^{\prime}=(n_{1}\alpha_{1},\dots,n_{r}\alpha_{r})
(the weights in
\alpha^{\prime}
are chosen so that
v_{\alpha}(E_{i})=\alpha_{i}
). The definition is independent of the SNC abelian cover
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
. We denote by
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
all such valuations with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
, and by
\mathrm{QM}^{\circ}_{\eta}(Y,E)
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{>0}
. In general, given a model
(Y,E)
, we denote by
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)\subseteq\mathrm{Val}_{X}
the union of
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,F)
where
\eta\in Y
and
F\subseteq E
is a reduced divisor such that
(Y,F)
is toroidal at
\eta
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a graded sequence of ideals. Then the function

v\to v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
is concave on
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
.

Proof.

By passing to the abelian cover, we may assume
(Y,E)
is log smooth at
\eta
.
Assume
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
are in
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
, corresponding to
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
and
v_{t}
the valuation corresponding to
\alpha_{t}:=(1-t)\alpha_{0}+t\alpha_{1}
.
For any
f
write
f=\sum c_{\beta}z^{\beta}
. Let
\beta_{0}
satisfy

c_{\beta_{0}}\neq 0\mbox{\ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ }v_{t}(f)=\langle\alpha_{t},\beta_{0}\rangle.

Then

v_{t}(f)=(1-t)\langle\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\rangle+t\langle\alpha_{1},\beta_{0}\rangle\geq(1-t)v_{0}(f)+tv_{1}(f).

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal, and
v_{t}(\mathfrak{a})=v_{t}(f)
for some
f\in\mathfrak{a}
.
Then

v_{t}(\mathfrak{a})=v_{t}(f)\geq tv_{0}(f)+(1-t)v_{1}(f)\geq tv_{0}(\mathfrak{a})+(1-t)v_{1}(\mathfrak{a}).

Since
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}v(\mathfrak{a}_{k})
for any valuation
v
, the above inequality implies

v_{t}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\geq(1-t)v_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})+tv_{1}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}).

∎

Definition 2.12

(3)

.

2.4.
Kollár components

Recall that a divisor
E
over a normal singularity
x\in X
is said to be
primitive
if there exists a projective birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
Y
is normal,
E
is a prime divisor on
Y
and
-E
is a
\pi
-ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor. We call
\pi\colon Y\to X

the associated prime blowup
(it is uniquely determined by
E
).

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
E
be a divisor over
x\in X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. Then
\mathrm{gr}_{E}R:=\mathrm{gr}_{\mathrm{ord}_{E}}R
is finitely generated if and only if
E
is primitive.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{m}=\mathfrak{a}_{m}(\mathrm{ord}_{E})
(
m\in\mathbb{N}
). By lifting generators of
\mathrm{gr}_{E}R=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}/\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}
, we see that
\mathrm{gr}_{E}R
is finitely generated if and only if
\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}
is finitely generated over
R
. We shall show that the latter is equivalent to the primitivity of
E
. If
E
is primitive and
\pi\colon Y\to X
is the associated prime blowup, then
\mathfrak{a}_{m}=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE)
. As
-E
is
\pi
-ample, there exists some
p\gg 0
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{i+jp}=\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
\mathcal{R}
is finitely generated. Conversely, if
\mathcal{R}
is finitely generated, then
[
16
]
*Corollary 3.3 implies that
E
is primitive.
∎

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be the prime blowup of a primitive divisor
E
over a normal singularity
x\in X
. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{m}:=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE)
. Then by the Rees construction we get a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant degeneration

(2.1)

p\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}={\rm Spec}(\mathbbm{k}[t])\mbox{ of $X$, \ \ \ where }\mathcal{X}:=\mathrm{Spec}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{-m}\mathfrak{a}_{m}.

By the construction, there is a natural isomorphism
\iota\colon\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})\cong X\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
.
So
\mathcal{X}
has a section
\gamma\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathcal{X}
given by the closure of
x\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
. Let
X_{0}=p^{-1}(0)
be the fiber over
0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and let
x_{0}=\gamma(0)
. Note that
X_{0}\cong\mathrm{Spec}(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{a}_{m}/\mathfrak{a}_{m+1})
. For any
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
, we denote by
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
the closure of
\Delta\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
on
\mathcal{X}
under the natural isomorphism
\iota
.

]

.

Let

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity and let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a proper birational morphism. Let
E\subseteq\pi^{-1}(x)
be a prime exceptional divisor. Assume that there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

such that
K_{Y}+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}0
and
(Y,D)
is plt. Then

is a Kollár component over

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=\pi_{*}D-\Delta\geq 0
. Since
K_{Y}+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}0
, we have
K_{Y}+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+\Gamma)
. Thus as
(Y,D)
is plt we see that
\Gamma
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of
x\in(X,\Delta)
and
E
is its unique lc place. By
[
3
]
*Corollaries 1.4.3 and 1.3.1, this implies that

is primitive and we let
\varphi\colon Z\to X
be the associated prime blowup. Then
(Z,E+\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\Delta+\Gamma))
is lc since it is the crepant pullback of

; it is also plt since it has only one lc place which is the divisor

. It follows that
(Z,E+\varphi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)
is plt as well. As
-(K_{X}+E+\varphi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\varphi}-A_{X,\Delta}(E)\cdot E

is a Kollár component.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

If
E
is a Kollár component over a klt singularity

From the assumption, we know that for any

3.

by assumption, the right hand side is
>\frac{1}{A_{X,\Delta}(v_{0})}=1

Our next goal is to reduce the study of special

-complements (and their monomial lc places) to the study of Kollár models. In this subsection, we first introduce the concept of Kollár models and discuss some of their general properties.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair that’s projective over a quasi-projective variety
U
such that
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample over
U
.
A model
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is said to be

if there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

on
Y
such that
\lfloor D\rfloor=E
,
(Y,D)
is qdlt, and
-(K_{Y}+D)
is ample over
U
.

Note that these conditions imply that
there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
of
(X,\Delta)
such that
Y
is a qdlt modification (in the sense of e.g.
[
21
]
*Theorem 1.34) of
(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
and
\pi_{*}^{-1}\Gamma
contains an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-subdivisor.

The following proposition demonstrates some of the flexibility in the construction of qdlt Fano type models.

Proposition 3.6
.

(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair projective over
U
such that
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample over
U
. Let
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
be a model of qdlt Fano type. Then:

(1)

For any reduced divisor
E_{0}\leq E
, the model
(Y,E_{0})\to(X,\Delta)
is of qdlt Fano type.

(2)

For any birational contraction
\varphi\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
over
X
that is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of

, the induced model
\pi^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}=\varphi_{*}E)\to(X,\Delta)
is of qdlt Fano type.

Proof.

Let
D
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor in Definition
3.5
. We can choose some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta_{0}
such that
\lfloor\Delta_{0}\rfloor=E_{0}
and
\Delta_{0}\geq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
. By Lemma
2.3
we get a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

such that
\lfloor D_{0}\rfloor=\lfloor\Delta_{0}\rfloor=E_{0}
,
(Y,D_{0})
is qdlt and
-(K_{Y}+D_{0})
is ample over

. This implies (1).

Let

be an ample divisor on

that is in a general position and let

. Then by the assumption on

and Bertini’s theorem we know that

does not contain any stratum of

. By Lemma

, there exists some

and some

\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor=E
,
(Y,D_{1})

-(K_{Y}+D_{1})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0\ /U.

In fact, Lemma
2.3
gives some

as above such that
-(K_{Y}+D_{1})
is ample; but then by Bertini’s theorem we can add a general ample divisor to

so that
-(K_{Y}+D_{1})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0\ /U
.

Let

. Since
\varphi
is an isomorphism at the generic points of all the strata of

, we see that
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime}_{1})
is also qdlt and
-(K_{Y^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}_{1})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0\ /U
. Since

, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

makes

a qdlt Fano type model over
(X,\Delta)
, as
\lfloor D^{\prime}\rfloor=E^{\prime}
and

-(K_{Y^{\prime}}+D^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q},U}\varepsilon G^{\prime}

is ample by construction. This gives (2).
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity. A
Kollár model
of
x\in(X,\Delta)
is a model
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
such that
\pi
is an isomorphism away from
\{x\}
,
E=\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)
,
(Y,\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
is qdlt and
-(K_{Y}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
is ample.

Clearly a Kollár model is also of qdlt Fano type (with
U=X
). The terminology comes from the following basic observations.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity. Then the plt blowup
\pi\colon Y\to X
of any Kollár component
E
is a Kollár model
(Y,E)
over
(X,\Delta)
. Conversely, if
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is a Kollár model, then every irreducible component of
E
is a Kollár component over
x\in(X,\Delta)
.

Proof.

The first part is clear from the definition. For the second part, suppose that
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is a Kollár model. Let
E_{1}
be an irreducible component of
E
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1}\geq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E_{1}
on
Y
such that
-(K_{Y}+D_{1})
is ample,
\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor=E_{1}
and
(Y,D_{1})
is qdlt. Since
\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor
is irreducible, this indeed implies the pair
(Y,D_{1})
is plt. Let
m>0
be some sufficiently divisible integer and let
G\in\frac{1}{m}|-m(K_{Y}+D_{1})|
be a general member. Then
K_{Y}+D_{1}+G\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
and
(Y,D_{1}+G)
remains plt by Bertini’s theorem. By Lemma
2.16
, we conclude that
E_{1}
is a Kollár component.
∎

Over a klt singularity, the two types of models introduced above are closely related. In fact, the following proposition shows that Kollár models are nothing but the ample models of qdlt Fano type models.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity, and let
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
be a model of qdlt Fano type such that
\pi(E)=\{x\}
. Let
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
be the ample model of
-(K_{Y}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
over
X
. Then
g
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
E
, and the induced morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}=g_{*}E)\to(X,\Delta)
is a Kollár model.

We remark that here the ample model exists since
Y
is of Fano type over
X
.

Proof.

Let us first show that
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
E
. Let
\varphi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification, which exists by
[
3
]
*Corollary 1.4.3. Note that
\varphi
is an isomorphism at the locus where
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial; in particular, this includes the generic points of all the strata of
E
. By Proposition
3.6
, we deduce that the model
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{E}=\varphi_{*}E)
is of qdlt Fano type. Since a small birational map such as
\varphi
does not change the ample model in question, we may replace
(Y,E)
by
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{E})
and assume that
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

Next we run the MMP
h\colon Y\dashrightarrow\overline{Y}
over
X
with respect to
L:=-(K_{Y}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
. We need to show that the MMP is an isomorphism at the generic points of all strata of
E
. To see this, note that if
D\geq E+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor from Definition
3.5
that makes
(Y,E)
of qdlt Fano type, then

(3.6)

L\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}-(K_{Y}+D)+(D-E-\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)=({\rm ample})+(D-E-\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)

and
\mathrm{Supp}(D-E-\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)
does not contain any stratum of
E
(otherwise
(Y,D)
would not be qdlt). Thus the MMP is an isomorphism at the generic points of all strata of
E
. By Proposition
3.6
, the model
(\overline{Y},\overline{E}=h_{*}E)
is of qdlt Fano type. Since the MMP does not change the ample model of
L
, after replacing
(Y,E)
by
(\overline{Y},\overline{E})
(and modify the corresponding divisor
D
) we may further assume that
L
is nef.

The ample model
g\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
of
L
contracts all subvarieties
W\subseteq Y
such that
L|_{W}
is not big. By (
3.6
) again, we see that the contracted locus is contained in
\mathrm{Supp}(D-E-\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta)
, which does not contain any stratum of
E
. Therefore, we conclude that
g
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
E
.

By Proposition
3.6
, it then follows that
\pi^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
is of qdlt Fano type. To show that
\pi^{\prime}
is in fact a Kollár model, we first observe that
E^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{Ex}(\pi^{\prime})
as
\pi^{\prime}(E^{\prime})=\pi(E)=\{x\}
. On the other hand,

-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\pi^{\prime-1}_{*}\Delta+E^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi^{\prime}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{X,\Delta}(E^{\prime}_{i})\cdot E^{\prime}_{i}

is ample by construction, where
E^{\prime}_{1},\dots,E^{\prime}_{r}
are the components of
E^{\prime}
. Since
A_{X,\Delta}(E^{\prime}_{i})>0
, this implies that
\mathrm{Ex}(\pi^{\prime})
is necessarily contained in
E^{\prime}
, thus
E=\mathrm{Ex}(\pi^{\prime})
. As
\pi^{\prime}(E^{\prime})=\{x\}
, we further deduce that
\pi^{\prime}
is an isomorphism away from
\{x\}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

The class of Kollár models (or more generally, qdlt Fano type models) enjoys many stronger properties that qdlt modifications of a general
\mathbb{Q}
-complement do not always have. The following result gives one example, which illuminates our later discussion.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(Y,E=\sum^{r}_{i=1}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
be a Kollár model over a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
. Then the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(E)
is a simplex, i.e. the intersections of any subsets of
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\}
is non-empty and irreducible.

This follows from the fact there is a unique minimal lc center for
(Y,D+E)
where
D
is the divisor in the definition of Kollár model.
See
[
10
, Proposition 25]
. Later we need the following slightly stronger result.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(X,D)
be an lc pair and let
f\colon X\to U
be a projective morphism such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{U}
and
-(K_{X}+D)
is
f
-ample. Assume that there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt. Then for any
u\in U
, the set of lc centers of
(X,D)
that intersects
f^{-1}(u)
has a unique minimal element with respect to inclusion. In particular, for any lc center
W
of
(X,D)
, the map
f|_{W}\colon W\to U
has connected fibers.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
2
]
*Theorem 6.6 and in fact the statement holds without assuming the existence of a klt boundary
\Delta
. We provide a direct proof in our special case for the reader’s convenience. The statement is local on
U
, thus after shrinking around
u\in U
we may assume that
L:=-(K_{X}+D)
is ample. Since any intersection of lc centers is a union of lc centers
[
21
]
*4.20, it suffices to show that the minimal lc center is unique. Suppose that there are two minimal lc centers
Z_{1}\neq Z_{2}
that intersect
f^{-1}(u)
. They are necessarily disjoint, otherwise their intersection contains smaller lc centers. Let
I_{Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}
be the ideal sheaf of
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\subseteq X
. Let
m>0
be a sufficiently large and divisible integer such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes I_{Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}
is globally generated, and let
G\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(mL)\otimes I_{Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}}|
be a general member. Fix some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Then
-(K_{X}+D+\varepsilon G)
is ample,
(X,D+\varepsilon G)
is lc away from
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}
(by Bertini’s theorem), but is not lc at the generic point of
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
. Consider the convex combination

(X,\Gamma:=c\Delta+(1-c)(D+\varepsilon G))

of
(X,D+\varepsilon G)
with the klt pair
(X,\Delta)
, where
0<c\ll 1
. Then
(X,\Gamma)
is klt away from
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}
, its non-klt locus is exactly
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}
, and
-(K_{X}+\Gamma)
is ample. Since
Z_{1}
is disjoint from
Z_{2}
, this contradicts the Kollár-Shokurov connectedness theorem
[
20
]
*17.4 Theorem. Finally, if
W
is an lc center of
(X,D)
and
W\to U
has disconnected fibers, then after replacing
U
with an étale local neighbourhood we may assume that
W
itself is disconnected (see e.g.
[
21
]
*Claim 4.38.1). But this contradicts the uniqueness of the minimal lc center.
∎

To prepare for the study of the degenerations of Kollár models in the next section, we also introduce a family version of Kollár models. Let us recall that a log canonical pair
(X,\Delta)
with a flat morphism
f\colon X\to B
to a smooth base is called
a locally stable family
(see
[
22
, Section 4.6]
), if for any simple normal crossing divisor
H_{1}+\dots+H_{r}\subseteq B
(
r=\dim B
), the pair

(X,\Delta+f^{*}(H_{1}+\cdots+H_{r}))
is log canonical.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
B\subseteq(X,\Delta)\to B
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein family of klt singularities over a smooth base
B
of dimension
r
(i.e. it is a locally stable family with a section such that
b\in(X_{b},\Delta_{b})
is klt for all
b\in B
). A
locally stable family of Kollár models
of
B\subseteq(X,\Delta)\to B
is a model
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
such that the following holds:

(1)

\pi
is an isomorphism away from
B\subseteq X
and
E=\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)
,

(2)

-(K_{Y}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
is
\pi
-ample, and

(3)

for any closed point
b\in B
, there exists some SNC divisor
H_{1}+\dots+H_{r}
with
b\in H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{r}
such that
(Y,\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E+p^{*}(H_{1}+\dots+H_{r}))
is qdlt where
p
denotes the map
Y\to B
.

When
E
is irreducible, we call
(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)\to B
a
locally stable family of Kollár components
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Using notation from Definition
3.12
, we have

(1)

Every irreducible component of
E
dominates
B
.

(2)

For any irreducible component
E_{1}
of
E
, the map
p|_{E_{1}}\colon E_{1}\to B
is equidimensional with irreducible and reduced fibers.

(3)

\pi_{b}\colon(Y_{b},E_{b})\to(X_{b},\Delta_{b})
is a Kollár model for all
b\in B
.

(4)

The component
E_{1}
can be extracted as a locally stable family of Kollár components, and the natural map

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE_{1})\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{b}}(-mE_{1})

is surjective for all
b\in B
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix some
b\in p(E_{1})
and let
D=\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
. Since all the statements are local on both
X
and
B
, we can shrink
X
and
B
around
b
if necessary. Let
H=H_{1}+\dots+H_{r}
. Since
-(K_{Y}+D+E)
is ample over
X
and
E_{1}
is an lc center of
(Y,D+E)
, by Lemma
3.11
we know that the map
p\colon E_{1}\to B\subseteq X
has connected fibers.
On the other hand, since
(Y,D+E+p^{*}H)
is qdlt, we know that the fiber

E_{1,b}=p^{-1}(b)\cap E_{1}=H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{r}\cap E_{1}

is normal by Lemma
2.5
, and

\dim E_{1,b}=\dim Y-(r+1)=\dim X_{b}-1.

It follows that
E_{1,b}
is irreducible and reduced, and the map
E_{1}\to B
is equidimensional. This gives the statement (2). It also implies that
E_{1}
dominates
B
as otherwise

\dim E_{1,b}\geq\dim E_{1}-\dim B+1\geq\dim Y-r,

a contradiction. By adjunction, we also see that
\pi_{b}\colon(Y_{b},E_{b})\to(X_{b},\Delta_{b})
is a Kollár model. Since
E_{1}
is arbitrary, we obtain the statements (1) and (3).

By Lemma
2.3
, we get a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{0}\geq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
such that
-(K_{Y}+D_{0}+E_{1})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample,
\lfloor D_{0}\rfloor=0
and
(Y,D_{0}+E_{1}+p^{*}H)
is qdlt. Let
D_{1}
be the sum of
D_{0}
with a general member of
\frac{1}{m}|-m(K_{Y}+D_{0}+E_{1})|
for some sufficiently divisible integer
m>0
. Then
D_{1}\geq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
,
\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor=0
,

K_{Y}+D_{1}+E_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}0,

and
(Y,D_{1}+E_{1}+p^{*}H)
is qdlt. Then
E_{1}
is an lc place of the pair
(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
where
\Gamma=\pi_{*}D_{1}-\Delta\geq 0
. By
[
3
]
*Corollary 1.4.3, there exists a birational contraction
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
over
X
such that
E^{\prime}=g_{*}E_{1}
is the unique exceptional divisor of
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to X
, and
-E^{\prime}
is ample over
X
. We claim that

(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to X\to B

extracts
E_{1}
as a locally stable family of Kollár components.

To see this, denote the map
Y^{\prime}\to B
by
p^{\prime}
and let
D^{\prime}=g_{*}D_{1}
. Since

K_{Y}+D_{1}+E_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}0

and
(Y,D_{1}+E_{1}+p^{*}H)
is lc, the same holds for its birational transform
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+p^{\prime*}H)
. Using
D_{1}\geq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
we then see that

(Y^{\prime},\pi^{\prime-1}_{*}\Delta+E^{\prime})\to B

is locally stable. This implies
E^{\prime}
is equidimensional over
B
with reduced fibers. In particular, it does not contain any component of
Y_{b}
. As
-E^{\prime}
is ample over
X
, the
\pi^{\prime}
-exceptional locus is supported on
E^{\prime}
, thus we also deduce that
Y^{\prime}_{b}\dashrightarrow X_{b}
is birational. By adjunction we know that
(Y^{\prime}_{b},D^{\prime}_{b}+E^{\prime}_{b})
is lc, and it is crepant to
(Y_{b},D_{1,b}+E_{1,b})
. Recall that
(Y,D_{1}+E_{1}+p^{*}H)
is qdlt and
\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor=0
, thus by adjunction
(Y_{b},D_{1,b}+E_{1,b})
is qdlt and its only lc place is
E_{1,b}
(recall that
E_{1,b}
is irreducible). Since every component of
E^{\prime}_{b}
has to be an lc place of
(Y^{\prime}_{b},D^{\prime}_{b}+E^{\prime}_{b})
, we see that
E^{\prime}_{b}
is also irreducible and it is the unique lc center of
(Y^{\prime}_{b},D^{\prime}_{b}+E^{\prime}_{b})
, which in turn gives the minimal lc center of
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+p^{\prime*}H)
. Moreover, the birational map
Y_{b}\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}_{b}
identifies

with
E^{\prime}_{b}
as divisors over

. By Lemma

, we conclude that the pair
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+p^{\prime*}H)
is also qdlt. So

is a locally stable family of Kollár model as desired, which gives the first part of the statement (4).

Finally, by Kawamata-Viehweg Vanishing Theorem and the fact that
-E^{\prime}
is
\pi^{\prime}
-ample we have
R^{1}\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}(-mE^{\prime}-p^{*}H_{i})=0
. Repeatedly cutting down by the hyperplanes
H_{i}
, we have

\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}(-mE^{\prime})\to\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}_{b}}(-mE^{\prime})

is surjective. Clearly
\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}(-mE^{\prime})=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE_{1})
as
E_{1}
and
E^{\prime}
are the same divisors over
X
. Similarly
\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}_{b}}(-mE^{\prime})=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{b}}(-mE_{1})
as the birational map
Y_{b}\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}_{b}
identifies
E_{1,b}
with
E^{\prime}_{b}
as divisors over
X_{b}
. Therefore,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE_{1})\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{b}}(-mE_{1})
is also surjective. This proves the remaining part of (4). The proof is now complete.
∎

3.3.
Construction of Kollár models

In the rest of this section, we explain how to construct Kollár models, or more generally, models of qdlt Fano type using special
\mathbb{Q}
-complements. The main goal is to prove the following statement.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair that is projective over a quasi-projective variety
U
such that
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample. Let
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
be a toroidal model, and let
\Gamma
be a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement with respect to
\pi
. Then for any rational simplex
\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)
, there exists a model
\pi^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
of qdlt Fano type such that
\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})=\sigma
as a subset of
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
.

Here we say
\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)
is a
rational simplex
if there exist divisorial valuations
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\in\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)
that are contained in the same simplicial cone of
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
such that they span an
(r-1)
-simplex and
\sigma
is the cone over it.

We divide the proof of this theorem in two major steps: a tie-breaking step, where we modify the toroidal model
(Y,E)
and the special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
while fixing the rational simplex
\sigma
so that

\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)=\mathrm{LC}(X,\Delta+\Gamma)=\sigma;

and an MMP step, where we contract all the exceptional divisors that are not contained in
E
to obtain the qdlt Fano type model. We first treat the MMP step.

Lemma 3.15
.

Theorem
3.14
holds when
\sigma=\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.

Proof.

Since in general
\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)\subseteq\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
, the assumption in particular includes that
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
is a simplex and

\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)=\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E).

Let
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
be the crepant pullback of
(X,\Delta)
. Since
\Gamma
is a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement, there exists some effective ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G\leq\pi^{-1}_{*}\Gamma
that does not contain any stratum of
E
. Then the assumption
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)=\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)
implies that
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma)
is sub-qdlt.

We first show that it suffices to find a birational contraction
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
with
Y^{\prime}
being projective over
X
, such that

(1)

g
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
(Y,E)
, and

(2)

g
contracts all the
\pi
-exceptional divisors that are not contained in
E
.

These conditions are natural in order for
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}=g_{*}E)
to be a model of qdlt Fano type: in fact condition (1) is the same as the assumption in Proposition
3.6
(2), while condition (2) guarantees that
Y^{\prime}
is of Fano type since
g
in particular contracts all the
\pi
-exceptional divisors with non-positive coefficients in
\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma
(these are the divisors that potentially prevent
Y
from being of Fano type). To see that the two conditions are sufficient for us, let
G^{\prime}_{0}
be an effective ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y^{\prime}
that is in a general position, and let
G_{0}
be its birational transform on
Y
. Then
\mathrm{Supp}(G_{0})
does not contain any stratum of
E
(by (1) and Bertini), which are also the lc centers of
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma)
. It follows that for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
(
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
) we have
G-\varepsilon G_{0}
is still ample, and
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0})
is still sub-qdlt with

\mathrm{LC}(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0})=\mathrm{QM}(Y,E).

Choose a sufficiently divisible integer
m
and take a general
G_{1}\in\frac{1}{m}|m(G-\varepsilon G_{0})|
. Then by Bertini’s theorem
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0}+G_{1})
is also sub-qdlt with

\mathrm{LC}(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0}+G_{1})=\mathrm{LC}(Y,\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0})=\mathrm{QM}(Y,E).

Moreover, as

\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0}+G_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}\Gamma

and the left hand side is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor we have

(3.7)

\pi^{*}\Gamma-G+\varepsilon G_{0}+G_{1}=\pi^{*}\Gamma^{\prime}

for some
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
(X,\Delta)
. Note that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}0
. By construction, the lc places of
(X,\Delta+\Gamma^{\prime})
are still given by
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
. Denote the induced map
Y^{\prime}\to X
by
\pi^{\prime}
. By the property (2) of the birational contraction
g\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
, the birational transform
g_{*}(\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma^{\prime})
is effective; in other words, the crepant pullback of
(X,\Delta+\Gamma^{\prime})
to
Y^{\prime}
has an effective boundary. Combined with the property (1), we see that
(Y^{\prime},g_{*}(\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma^{\prime}))
is qdlt and its lc places are given by
\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
. By (
3.7
),
{\pi_{*}^{\prime}}^{-1}\Gamma^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon G_{0}
. Let

D=g_{*}(\Delta_{Y}+\pi^{*}\Gamma)-\varepsilon G_{0}\geq{\pi_{*}^{\prime}}^{-1}\Delta.

Then
(Y^{\prime},D)
is qdlt,
\lfloor D\rfloor=E^{\prime}
and
-(K_{Y^{\prime}}+D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\varepsilon G_{0}
is ample. It follows that the model
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
is qdlt of Fano type as desired.

Thus it remains to find a birational contraction that satisfies (1)–(2). Let
F
be the sum of
\pi
-exceptional divisors that are not contained in
E
. We aim to run an MMP
Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
over
X
for some effective divisor whose support is
F
. Since
F
does not contain any stratum of
E
, the MMP process satisfies property (1). By the negativity lemma
[
19
]
*Lemma 3.39, the MMP will contract
F
hence it also satisfies property (2). Nevertheless, we need to verify that the MMP exists since
Y
may not be of Fano type over
X
. To this end, fix some
0<c\ll 1
so that
A_{X,\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma}(E_{i})<1
for every irreducible component
E_{i}
of
E
(this is possible since
E_{i}
is an lc place of
(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
) and write

\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma)=K_{Y}+\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}

where
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are effective and have no common components. Then
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta_{2})\subseteq F
. As
(X,\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma)
is klt, we also know that the coefficients of
\Delta_{1}
are less than
1
.

If
(Y,\Delta_{1})
is klt, then so does
(Y,\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon F)
when
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, and we may run the
(K_{Y}+\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon F)
-MMP
Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
over
X
by
[
3
]
*Theorem 1.2. Since

K_{Y}+\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon F\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\pi}\Delta_{2}+\varepsilon F

and the right hand side is supported on
F
, this gives the desired birational contraction.

In general, since
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, we can take a dlt modification by
[
34
]
*Theorem 1.1 of
(Y,\Delta_{1})
. This is a projective birational morphism
\rho\colon Z\to Y
with exceptional divisors
F^{\prime}_{i}
such that
Z
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial,
(Z,\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{1}+\sum F^{\prime}_{i})
is dlt, and
K_{Z}+\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{1}+\sum F^{\prime}_{i}
is
\rho
-nef. By the negativity lemma, the last condition implies that
A_{Y,\Delta_{1}}(F^{\prime}_{i})\leq 0
for all
F^{\prime}_{i}
. In particular,
\rho
is an isomorphism over the locus where
(Y,\Delta_{1})
is already klt. We claim that
\rho
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
E
, so that we can try to construct the birational contraction by running MMP on
Z
instead.

For this it suffices to show that if
\eta\in Y
is the generic point of any stratum of
E
, then
(Y,\Delta_{1})
is klt at
\eta
. But since
(Y,E+F)
is toroidal and
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta_{2})\subseteq F
, we have
\eta\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta_{2})
. Thus as the crepant pullback
(Y,\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2})
of the klt pair
(X,\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma)
is sub-klt, we deduce that
(Y,\Delta_{1})
is klt at
\eta
as desired, and therefore
\rho\colon Z\to Y
is an isomorphism at
\eta
.

Thus we may replace
Y
by
Z
and run through the above argument. Let
\varphi\colon Z\to X
be the induce map, and write

\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma)=K_{Z}+D_{1}-D_{2}

where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are effective and have no common components. Let
\widetilde{F}
be the sum of all
\varphi
-exceptional divisors that are not contained in
\rho_{*}^{-1}E
(i.e.
\widetilde{F}=\rho_{*}^{-1}F+\sum F^{\prime}_{i}
). As before, we have
\mathrm{Supp}(D_{2})\subseteq\widetilde{F}
. We claim that
(Z,D_{1})
is klt. Clearly
\lfloor D_{1}\rfloor=0
since
(X,\Delta+(1-c)\Gamma)
is klt; thus

D_{1}\leq\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{1}+(1-\varepsilon)\sum F^{\prime}_{i}

for some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Since
\lfloor\Delta_{1}\rfloor=0
and
(Z,\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{1}+\sum F^{\prime}_{i})
is dlt, we know that
(Z,\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{1}+(1-\varepsilon)\sum F^{\prime}_{i})
is klt, hence so is
(Z,D_{1})
, proving the above claim.

Now we can complete the proof. Since
(Z,D_{1}+\varepsilon\widetilde{F})
remains klt when
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we may run the
(K_{Z}+D_{1}+\varepsilon\widetilde{F})
-MMP
Z\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
over
X
. As

K_{Z}+D_{1}+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},\varphi}D_{2}+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}

and the right hand side is fully supported on
\widetilde{F}
, the MMP exactly contracts
\widetilde{F}
and thus the induced birational map
Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
is a birational contraction that satisfies the property (2). As
\widetilde{F}
does not contain any stratum of
\rho_{*}^{-1}E
and
\rho\colon Z\to Y
is an isomorphism at the generic point of every stratum of
E
, the map
Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
also satisfies the property (1). This finishes the proof.
∎

We next work towards reducing Theorem
3.14
to the special case treated above. For this we need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
(Y,E)
be a toroidal pair, and let
\Delta
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor supported on
E
such that
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor\leq 0
. Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
be toroidal divisors over
(Y,E)
such that
v_{i}=\mathrm{ord}_{F_{i}}
are in a simplicial cone of
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
and span an
(r-1)
-simplex. Then there exists a proper birational morphism
\rho\colon Z\to Y
extracting the divisors
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
such that
\mathrm{Ex}(\rho)\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}
and
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}
is ample over
Y
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon W\to Y
be a toroidal blowup that extracts the divisors
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
. Then
W
is of Fano type over
Y
. By running an MMP
g\colon W\dashrightarrow W^{\prime}
with scaling over
Y
we obtain a model such that
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot g_{*}F_{i}
is nef over
Y
and let
h\colon W^{\prime}\to Z
be the corresponding ample model over
Y
. It suffices to show that none of the divisors
F_{i}
are contracted in this process. By assumption,
D:=E-\Delta
is effective and
\mathrm{Supp}(D)=E
. Since
A_{Y,E}(F_{i})=0
, we have
A_{Y,\Delta}(F_{i})=\mathrm{ord}_{F_{i}}(D)
, thus

-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},f}f^{*}D-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{ord}_{F_{i}}(D)\cdot F_{i}=D_{W},

for some effective divisor
D_{W}
that does not contain any
F_{i}
in its support. It follows that the (
D_{W}
-)MMP
W\dashrightarrow W^{\prime}
does not contract any of the divisors
F_{i}
and hence by replacing the initial model
W
with
W^{\prime}
we may simply assume
W=W^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we have

K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},f}\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i},

thus the ample model
W\to Z
satisfies

K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}=h^{*}(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{*}F_{i}).

Here
\Delta_{Z},\Delta_{W}
denote the strict transform of
\Delta
on
Z,W
. Note that locally both
(W,\Delta_{W}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i})
and
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{*}F_{i})
have toroidal singularities. In fact, étale locally on
Y
the toroidal pair
(Y,E)
is isomorphic to a toric pair, and if we take a base change of our MMP over the toric pair, the MMP with scaling and the ample model construction are naturally torus equivariant. Also recall that
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor\leq 0
. Thus
\mathrm{LC}(W,\Delta_{W}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i})
is the
r
-dimensional simplicial cone spanned by all
F_{i}
while
\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}h_{*}F_{i})
is the simplicial cone spanned by
h_{*}F_{i}
. But as the two pairs are crepant equivalent, their dual complexes have the same dimension. In particular, the divisors
h_{*}F_{i}
also span an
(r-1)
-simplex. This implies that none of the
F_{i}
’s are contracted on the ample model.
∎

The following lemma gives the tie-breaking step for the proof of Theorem
3.14
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
be a toroidal model and let
\Gamma_{Y}
be a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement of
(X,\Delta)
with respect to
\pi
. Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
be divisors over
Y
such that
v_{i}=\mathrm{ord}_{F_{i}}\in\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma_{Y};Y,E)
are in a simplicial cone of
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
and they span an
(r-1)
-simplex. Then there exists a birational toroidal morphism
\rho\colon Z\to(Y,E)
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
of
(X,\Delta)
such that

(1)

\rho
extracts the divisors
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
and
\mathrm{Ex}(\rho)\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}
;

(2)

\Gamma
is special with respect to
(Z,F)
where
F=F_{1}+\dots+F_{r}
; and

(3)

\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)=\mathrm{LC}(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
be the crepant pullback. Then
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
is sub-klt and in particular
\lfloor\Delta_{Y}\rfloor\leq 0
. By applying Lemma
3.16
to the toroidal pair
(Y,\mathrm{Supp}(E+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)))
and the sub-boundary
\Delta_{Y}
, we deduce that there exists a toroidal birational morphism
\rho\colon Z\to Y
extracting the divisors
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
such that
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta_{Y}}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}
is ample over
Y
. Note that
A_{Y,\Delta_{Y}}(F_{i})=A_{X,\Delta}(F_{i})
, so this
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is the same as
-\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{X,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}
. To prove the lemma, we need to find a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
with respect to
(Z,F)
such that
\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)=\mathrm{LC}(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
.

First we need to introduce some more notation. Let
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
be the crepant pullback of
(X,\Delta)
, i.e.,

\rho^{*}\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)=K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z},

and let
\widetilde{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{X,\Delta}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}
. Since
\Gamma_{Y}
is a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement with respect to
(Y,E)
, we have
\pi^{-1}_{*}\Gamma_{Y}\geq G
for some effective ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G
that does not contain any stratum of
(Y,E)
. Let

D=\pi^{*}\Gamma_{Y}-G\geq 0.

Since
G
is ample on
Y
and
-\widetilde{F}
is ample over
Y
, we can choose a rational number
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
such that both
\frac{1}{2}G+\varepsilon D
and
\frac{1}{2}\rho^{*}G-\varepsilon\widetilde{F}
are ample. This guarantees that
\rho^{*}(G+\varepsilon D)-\varepsilon\widetilde{F}
is ample.

We break the pullback of
\Gamma_{Y}
to
Z
into three parts:

\rho^{*}\pi^{*}\Gamma_{Y}=(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+(\rho^{*}(G+\varepsilon D)-\varepsilon\widetilde{F}).

We claim that

(3.8)

\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F})=\mathrm{QM}(Z,F).

Once this is proved, the lemma follows almost immediately: since
\rho^{*}(G+\varepsilon D)-\varepsilon\widetilde{F}
is ample, by Bertini’s theorem we can choose some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

G^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\rho^{*}(G+\varepsilon D)-\varepsilon\widetilde{F}

such that

\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime})=\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)

still holds. In particular, the ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G^{\prime}
does not contain any stratum of
(Z,F)
in its support. Since

(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\rho^{*}\pi^{*}\Gamma_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0\,/X,

we have

(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime}=\rho^{*}\pi^{*}\Gamma

for the effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

\Gamma:=\pi_{*}\rho_{*}((1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma_{Y}

on
X
. By construction
\Gamma
is a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement with respect to
(Z,F)
and

\rho^{*}\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+\Gamma)=K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime}.

Thus

\mathrm{LC}(X,\Delta+\Gamma)=\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+(1-\varepsilon)\rho^{*}D+\varepsilon\widetilde{F}+G^{\prime})=\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)

as desired.

It remains to prove (
3.8
). The pair in question is a convex linear combination of
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\rho^{*}D)
and
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\widetilde{F})
, thus it suffices to show that:

(1)

both
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\rho^{*}D)
and
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\widetilde{F})
are sub-lc,

(2)

\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)\subseteq\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\rho^{*}D)
, and

(3)

\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\widetilde{F})=\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)
.

First,
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\rho^{*}D)
is sub-lc since it is the crepant pullback of
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+D)
, which is sub-lc as

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+D\leq\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+\Gamma_{Y}).

Moreover, all the
F_{i}
’s are lc places of
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+D)
by assumption, thus

\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)\subseteq\mathrm{LC}(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\rho^{*}D).

On the other hand, a direct computation gives

K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+\widetilde{F}=\rho^{*}(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{Y,\Delta_{Y}}(F_{i})\cdot F_{i}=K_{Z}+\left(\rho^{-1}_{*}\Delta_{Y}\vee\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i}\right),

where the notation
D_{1}\vee D_{2}
denotes the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
such that
D\geq D_{i}
for
i=1,2
. As
\lfloor\Delta_{Y}\rfloor\leq 0
, we hence see that the toroidal pair
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+\widetilde{F})
is also lc and its lc places are exactly given by
\mathrm{QM}(Z,F)
. Thus we have proved all the properties (1)–(3) above and this finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.14
.

By Lemma
3.17
, after possibly replacing the toroidal model
(Y,E)
and the corresponding special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
, we may assume that
\sigma=\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
. The result then follows from Lemma
3.15
.
∎

One of the main applications of Theorem
3.14
is the construction of Kollár models for monomial lc places of special
\mathbb{Q}
-complements.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity and let
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
be a monomial lc place of some special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
with respect to some toroidal model
(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
. Then there exists a Kollár model
\pi^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
such that
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
be the smallest simplicial face that contains
v
and choose some rational simplex

\sigma\subseteq{\rm int}(\Sigma)\cap\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)

such that
v\in\sigma
. By Theorem
3.14
, we get a model
\pi\colon(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{E})\to(X,\Delta)
of qdlt Fano type such that
\mathrm{QM}(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{E})=\sigma
. Since
v
is centered at
x
, the same holds for all valuations in
{\rm int}(\Sigma)
. In particular
\pi(\widetilde{E})=\{x\}
and thus the ample model
\pi\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
of
-(K_{\widetilde{Y}}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\widetilde{E})
is a Kollár model by Proposition
3.9
. By construction
v\in\sigma=\mathrm{QM}(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{E})=\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.
∎

4.
Kollár models and finite generation

The goal of this section is to prove the following finite generation statement, which is a more precise version of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
x\in(X=\mathrm{Spec}(R),\Delta)
be a klt singularity, and let
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
be a quasi-monomial valuation. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

The associated graded ring
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R
is finitely generated and the central fiber
(X_{v},\Delta_{v})
of the induced degeneration is klt.

(2)

The valuation
v
is a monomial lc place of a special
\mathbb{Q}
-complement
\Gamma
with respect to some toroidal model
(Y,E)

(
see Definition
3.1
)
.

(3)

There exists a Kollár model
(
see Definition
3.5
)

\pi\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
such that
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.

We explain some notation in the above theorem (c.f.
[
32
]
*Theorem 4.2). Assuming the finite generation of
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R
, then similar to the Rees construction (
2.1
) for a primitive divisor, we define
X_{v}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{gr}_{v}R)
, and let
\Delta_{v}
be the induced degeneration of
\Delta
to
X_{v}
. More precisely, suppose
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}\Delta_{i}
where
\Delta_{i}
is a prime divisor on
X
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
. Let
I_{\Delta_{i}}\subseteq R
be the ideal of
\Delta_{i}
. Let
\mathrm{in}(I_{\Delta_{i}})\subseteq\mathrm{gr}_{v}R
be the initial ideal of
I_{\Delta_{i}}
, i.e.

\mathrm{in}(I_{\Delta_{i}})=\bigoplus_{\lambda}I_{\Delta_{i},\lambda},\mbox{ where }I_{\Delta_{i},\lambda}=\{{\rm Im}(f)\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}(v)\ |\ f\in I_{\Delta_{i}}\mbox{ and }v(f)\geq\lambda\}.

Then
\Delta_{v}:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}\Delta_{v,i},
where
\Delta_{v,i}
is the divisorial part of the closed subscheme
V(\mathrm{in}(I_{\Delta_{i}}))
in
X_{v}
, i.e.
\Delta_{v,i}
and
V(\mathrm{in}(I_{\Delta_{i}}))
coincide away from a codimension
2
subset of

. When
v=\mathrm{ord}_{E}
is a divisorial valuation induced by some primitive divisor
E
over

, this coincides with the construction in (
2.1
).

Note that one of the implications in Theorem
4.1
, namely
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
, has already been established in Corollary
3.18
.

4.1.
Special degenerations come from lc places of special complements

As in
[
32
]
*Theorem 4.2, we first prove the easier part of the remaining implications, i.e.
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
, in Theorem
4.1
. We will need an auxiliary lemma which is a local analog of
[
7
]
*Theorem A.2.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a primitive divisor over a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
.
Assume that the special fiber
x_{0}\in(X_{0},\Delta_{0})
of the Rees construction (
2.1
) is lc. Then
E
is an lc place of some
N
-complement of
x\in(X,\Delta)
, where the integer
N
only depends on the dimension of
X
and the coefficients of
\Delta
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
7
]
*Propositions A.3 and A.4. To begin with, we show that the associated prime blow up
\pi\colon Y\to X
of
E
specializes to some
Y_{0}\to X_{0}
. Indeed, let
\mathfrak{a}_{j}=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-jE)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
and let
(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
be the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant family from (
2.1
). Then the flat extension of
\mathfrak{a}_{j}[t,t^{-1}]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})}
to
\mathcal{X}
is given by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j}:=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{-i}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{j})
. Since

\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j+1}\cong\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}t^{-j}(\mathfrak{a}_{j}/\mathfrak{a}_{j+1})

is finitely generated, and if we denote by

\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{Proj}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j+1}\right)\,,

then we have
\mathcal{E}\cong E\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
. This implies
\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j}
is finitely generated.
Consider the morphism

\rho\colon\mathcal{Y}:=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{j}\right)\to\mathcal{X}

over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then

\mathcal{Y}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})\cong Y\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})

and
\rho
restricts to
\pi\times\mathrm{id}
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
. The exceptional divisor of
\rho
is given by
\mathcal{E}
.
Let
\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}=\rho_{*}^{-1}\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
be the strict transform of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, and denote by
Y_{0}
the central fiber of
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

Let
\ell>0
be a sufficiently divisible integer such that
\ell E
is Cartier and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-p\ell E)=0
for all
p\geq 1
. Through the long exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-(p\ell+1)E)\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-p\ell E)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(-p\ell E)\to 0,

we get
\mathfrak{a}_{p\ell}/\mathfrak{a}_{p\ell+1}\cong H^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E}(-p\ell E))
. Let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
be the quotient by
\mu_{\ell}
, where the action is via
\mu_{\ell}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},Y^{\prime}_{0},X^{\prime}_{0}
be the induced quotients of
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{E},Y_{0},X_{0}
, where the corresponding quotient maps are still denoted by
\sigma
. Since the
\mu_{\ell}
-action is free outside
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
, we have

K_{\mathcal{X}}+\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}+X_{0}=\sigma^{*}(K_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}+X^{\prime}_{0})

for an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and

K_{\mathcal{Y}}+\mathcal{E}+Y_{0}+\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}=\sigma^{*}(K_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}_{0}+\mathcal{D}^{\prime})

for an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
on

Proof.

The argument is the same as the ones in

]
*Lemma 4.3. Let

Theorem 4.4

In the above notation, the family

. Then

Proof of
4.5

Let

, and let

(

Therefore, as

by the ample model of

4.3.
Irreducible central fiber implies finite generation

Finally, we will show that the irreducibility of the central fiber, as proven by Proposition

implies the finite generation. This was pointed out in
[
41
]
. Here we give a detailed argument in a slightly more general context.

We fix the following setup. Let
x\in X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
be a singularity and let

\pi\colon(Y,E=E_{1}+\dots+E_{m})\to X

be a model. Assume that:

•

\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
is nonempty,

•

(Y,\mathrm{Supp}(E+\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)))
is toroidal at a generic point
\eta
of
\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
, and

•

there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
F
on
Y
such that
-F
is
\pi
-ample and
E+\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)+F
has toroidal support at
\eta\in Y
.

Remark 4.7
.

The first two conditions allow us to define the simplex
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)\subseteq\mathrm{Val}_{X}
and normally it contains the valuation we want to analyze. The last condition rules out some peculiar birational maps but in the situations we consider it is often automatic. For example, if
(Y,E)
is a Kollár model of a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
, then the first condition follows from Proposition
3.10
, the second condition holds by definition, and for the last condition we can take

F=K_{Y}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E-\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta),

which is supported on
E
. In general, for any quasi-monomial valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
, we can always choose a log smooth model
\pi\colon(Y,E)\to X
such that
\mathrm{Ex}(\pi)
supports a
\pi
-ample divisor (which serves as our
-F
; by the negativity lemma
F
is effective) and
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.

Let
v_{i}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
(
i=1,\dots,r
) be divisorial valuations that all lie in
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
. There exists a natural linear map
\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\to\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
sending the
i
-th basis vector
e_{i}
to
v_{i}
. For
0\neq s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
we let
v_{s}\in\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(Y,E)
be the image of
s
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we also let
I_{s,\lambda}\subseteq R
be the ideal given by

(4.3)

I_{s,\lambda}={\rm Span}\{f\in R\,|\,s_{1}v_{1}(f)+\dots+s_{r}v_{r}(f)\geq\lambda\}.

The above expression is chosen so that
I_{s,\lambda}
“looks like” the valuation ideal of
v_{s}
. In fact, this is the case if
Y=X
(by the definition of quasi-monomial valuations). The following lemma is an algebraic reformulation (in our more general setup) of the result claimed in
[
41
]
*Lemma 3.8.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{0\}
. Assume that there exists some valuation
w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
such that
I_{s,\lambda}=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(w)
for all
\lambda
. Then
w=v_{s}
.

Geometrically, the condition that
I_{s,\bullet}
are valuation ideals is equivalent to saying that the central fiber of the induced degeneration is irreducible and reduced, i.e. the graded algebra
\bigoplus_{\lambda}(I_{s,\lambda}/I_{s,>\lambda})
is integral (see Lemma
2.7
).

In the global setting, the analogous construction for
I_{s,\lambda}
is often viewed as the “geodesic” connecting two valuations (see
[
35
]
,
[
6
]
*Section 3.1.2 or
[
5
]
*Section 4). Thus Lemma
4.8
can be interpreted as saying that whenever the geodesic lies in the valuations space, it is the obvious line in the corresponding simplex
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition of quasi-monomial valuations that
I_{s,\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})
for all
\lambda
, hence
v_{s}\geq w
on
R
. It remains to show that
w\geq v_{s}
. Let
0\neq f_{0}\in R
and let
\lambda_{0}=v_{s}(f_{0})
. Let

\mathfrak{b}=\big\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}\mid v_{s}(f)\geq\lambda_{0}\big\}

be the corresponding valuation ideal on
Y
; in particular
f_{0}\in\mathfrak{b}
. Let
\alpha_{i}=v_{s}(E_{i})
. Then we have a surjection

(4.4)

\bigoplus_{b_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\sum\alpha_{i}b_{i}\geq\lambda_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}E_{i}\right)\to\mathfrak{b}

by the definition of the quasi-monomial valuation
v_{s}
. Let
\ell>0
be a sufficiently divisible integer; in particular,

\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F)\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F)

is surjective. We claim that for a general element
f\in\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F)
, we have

w(f)=v_{s}(f)\mbox{\ \ \ and \ \ \ }w(f_{0}f)\geq v_{s}(f_{0}f),

which together implies
w(f_{0})\geq v_{s}(f_{0})
. Since
f_{0}
is arbitrary, we deduce that
w\geq v_{s}
as desired.

We now prove the claim. First observe that since
f\in\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F)
is general, we have

\pi^{*}({\rm div}(f))=\ell F+D

for some divisor
D
that does not contain any stratum of
E
. Thus

s_{1}v_{1}(f)+\dots+s_{r}v_{r}(f)=s_{1}v_{1}(\ell F)+\dots+s_{r}v_{r}(\ell F)=v_{s}(\ell F)=v_{s}(f),

where the second equality follows from the definition of the valuation
v_{s}
and the fact that the local equation of
\ell F
is given by a monomial. In particular,

f\in I_{s,v_{s}(f)}=\mathfrak{a}_{v_{s}(f)}(w),

i.e.
w(f)\geq v_{s}(f)
which together with
v_{s}\geq w
implies
w(f)=v_{s}(f)
.

Next we show that
w(f_{0}f)\geq v_{s}(f_{0}f)
. Since
\ell
is sufficiently divisible and
-F
is
\pi
-ample, the map

\bigoplus_{b_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\sum\alpha_{i}b_{i}\geq\lambda_{0}}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}E_{i}-\ell F\right)\to\pi_{*}(\mathfrak{b}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F))

induced by (
4.4
) is surjective. As

f_{0}f\in\pi_{*}(\mathfrak{b}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\ell F)),

we may write

f_{0}f=g_{1}+\dots+g_{p},

where each

g_{j}\in\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{m}b^{(j)}_{i}E_{i}-\ell F\right)

for some
b^{(j)}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
that satisfies
\sum\alpha_{i}b^{(j)}_{i}\geq\lambda_{0}
. Similar to the argument above, for any
j=1,\dots,p
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}(g_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}b^{(j)}_{i}E_{i}+\ell F\right)

\displaystyle=v_{s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}b^{(j)}_{i}E_{i}+\ell F\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}b^{(j)}_{i}+v_{s}(\ell F)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{0}+v_{s}(\ell F)=v_{s}(f_{0})+v_{s}(f)=v_{s}(f_{0}f),

where the first equality follows from the definition of the quasi-monomial valuation
v_{s}
as above. It follows that each
g_{j}\in I_{s,v_{s}(f_{0}f)}
which gives
f_{0}f\in I_{s,v_{s}(f_{0}f)}
. Therefore,
w(f_{0}f)\geq v_{s}(f_{0}f)
. This proves the claim and completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In practice, it is often hard to show directly that
I_{s,\bullet}
are valuations ideals when the weights
s_{i}
are irrational. The following auxiliary lemma allows us to only consider rational weights.

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})=I_{s,\lambda}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
s\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}_{\geq 0}\setminus 0
. Then the same holds when
s\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}\setminus 0
.

Proof.

We may write
s=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}s^{(i)}
for some
s^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
(
i=1,\dots,m
) and some
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
that are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent. Note that it suffices to prove

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})=I_{s,\lambda}\mbox{ when $\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}c_{i}$ for some }a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{N},

since these are the only places where
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})
or
I_{s,\lambda}
possibly jumps.

Fix any such
s
and
\lambda
. We claim that for any
c^{\prime}_{1},\dots,c^{\prime}_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
that are sufficiently close to
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
respectively, we have

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad I_{s,\lambda}=I_{s^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}},

where
s^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c^{\prime}_{i}s^{(i)}
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}c^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}_{\geq 0}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}})=I_{s^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
by assumption, hence the lemma follows immediately from this claim.

We now proceed to prove the above claim. Let us first check that

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}}).

Suppose that this is not the case, i.e. there exists some
0\neq f\in R
that is contained in one of
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s}),\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}})
but not the other. Since each
c^{\prime}_{i}
is sufficiently close to
c_{i}
, we may assume that
s\leq 2s^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime}\leq 2\lambda
. By the definition of the valuations
v_{s}
, we then have
v_{s}\leq 2v_{s^{\prime}}
. It follows that
v_{s}(f)<4\lambda
, otherwise

v_{s^{\prime}}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}v_{s}(f)\geq 2\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}

and thus

f\in\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}}),

a contradiction. Let
\eta\in Y
be the center of
v_{s}
. Recall that
v_{s}(f)
is the smallest weight of monomials in the local expansion of
f\in\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}
. Consider the monomials with weights at most
4\lambda
. Each monomial has weight
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}c_{i}
for some
b_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. If we perturb
c_{i}
to
c^{\prime}_{i}
, then the weight becomes
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}c^{\prime}_{i}
. As
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(1)}c_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(2)}c_{i}\mbox{\ \ if and only if \ \ }(b_{1}^{(1)},...,b_{m}^{(1)})=(b_{1}^{(2)},...,b_{m}^{(2)}).

Combined with the fact that there are only finitely many monomials with weights at most
4\lambda
, we see that if the
c^{\prime}_{i}
’s are sufficiently close to
c_{i}
, then

(4.5)

4\lambda>\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(1)}c_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(2)}c_{i}\mbox{\ \ if and only if \ \ }4\lambda>\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(1)}c^{\prime}_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(2)}c^{\prime}_{i};

for any
(b_{1}^{(1)},...,b_{m}^{(1)})
and
(b_{1}^{(2)},...,b_{m}^{(2)})
in
\mathbb{N}^{m}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}^{(j)}c_{i}<4\lambda

(j=1,2)
.
In other words, the monomials that are responsible for computing
v_{s}(f)
are also responsible for computing
v_{s^{\prime}}(f)
. In particular, we have

v_{s}(f)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}c_{i}=\lambda\mbox{\ \ if and only if \ \ }v_{s^{\prime}}(f)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}c^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda^{\prime},

hence it cannot happen that
f
belongs to one of
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s}),\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}})
but not the other. This is a contradiction. Thus
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s})=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(v_{s^{\prime}})
as desired.

The other equality
I_{s,\lambda}=I_{s^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
can be proved in a similar fashion. Suppose that
I_{s,\lambda}\neq I_{s^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
, then there exists some
f\in R
such that one of the inequalities

\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}(f)\geq\lambda,\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{r}s^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}(f)\geq\lambda^{\prime}

holds but not the other. As in the above proof, we may assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}(f)<4\lambda
. Note that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}c_{i}\mbox{\ \ and \ \ }\sum_{i=1}^{r}s^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}c^{\prime}_{i}

for some
b_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. As in (
4.5
), this implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}v_{i}(f)\geq\lambda\mbox{\ \ if and only if \ \ }\sum_{i=1}^{r}s^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}(f)\geq\lambda^{\prime},

a contradiction. Thus
I_{s,\lambda}=I_{s^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
as desired. This proves the claim and also finishes the proof of the lemma.

\Gamma

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
be a minimizer of
\widehat{\rm vol}_{X,\Delta}
. Denote by
X={\rm Spec}(R)
. By Theorem
1.1
,
{\rm gr}_{v}(R)
is finitely generated. If the rational rank of
v
is
r
, then
X_{v}={\rm Spec}({\rm gr}_{v}(R))
admits a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
-action, and
(X_{v},\Delta_{v})
is a klt singularity. Moreover, the valuation
v\colon R\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
yields a Reeb vector
\xi_{v}
and
(X_{v},\Delta_{v};\xi_{v})
is a K-semistable log Fano cone singularities. By
[
27
]
*Lemma 2.58, we have

\widehat{\rm vol}_{X,\Delta}(v)=\widehat{\rm vol}_{X_{v},\Delta_{v}}(\xi_{v}),

thus we need to show that the right hand side is an algebraic number.

This was proved in
[
11
]
, inspired by arguments in
[
33
]
. To see it, denote by
N={\rm Hom}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r})
the coweight lattice, and
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Then by
[
11
]
*Lemma 4.2, the function

\mathrm{vol}_{X_{v},\Delta_{v}}\colon\xi\mapsto\mathrm{vol}({\rm wt}_{\xi})

(where
{\rm wt}_{\xi}
is the valuation corresponding to
\xi
), defined on the Reeb cone
\mathfrak{t}_{+}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, can be extended to a rational function with rational coefficients on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, if we restrict on the hyperplane
A_{X_{v},\Delta_{v}}({\rm wt}_{\xi})=1
, then the minimizer
\xi_{v}
of
\mathrm{vol}
has algebraic numbers as coordinates in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
by
[
11
, Lemma 4.6]
. This implies
\mathrm{vol}_{X_{v},\Delta_{v}}(\xi_{v})
is an algebraic number.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By the definition of quasi-monomial valuations,
v\mapsto A_{X,\Delta}(v)
is linear on
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
and
v\mapsto v(\Gamma)
is piecewise linear with rational coefficients. The latter is also concave by Lemma
2.9
. Since
A_{X,\Delta}(v)\geq v(\Gamma)
, we see that

\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)=\{v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)\mid A_{X,\Delta}(v)=v(\Gamma)\}

is a rational polyhedral subset. We get
\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
by removing from
\mathrm{LC}(\Gamma;Y,E)
its intersection with a few faces of
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
, thus it is also polyhedral.

For the other statement, it suffices to consider the case when
v,w\in{\rm int}(\sigma)
for some rational simplex
\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
, as the general case then follows by covering
\mathrm{LC}_{x}(\Gamma;Y,E)
with rational simplices. By Theorem
3.14
, there exists a Kollár model
\pi\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
such that
\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})=\sigma
. By Corollary
4.10
, we get
\mathrm{gr}_{v}R\cong\mathrm{gr}_{w}R
. The proof is now complete.
∎

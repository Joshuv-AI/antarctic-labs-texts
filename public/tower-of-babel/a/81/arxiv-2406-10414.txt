On the Field Isomorphism Problem for the Family of Simplest Quartic Fields
David L. Pincus, Lawrence C. Washington

Abstract
. Deciding whether or not two polynomials have isomoprhic splitting fields over the rationals is the Field Isomorphism Problem. We consider polynomials of the form f n ​ ( x ) = x 4 − n ​ x 3 − 6 ​ x 2 + n ​ x + 1 f_{n}(x)=x^{4}-nx^{3}-6x^{2}+nx+1 with n ≠ 3 n\neq 3 a positive integer and we let K n K_{n} denote the splitting field of f n ​ ( x ) f_{n}(x) ; a ‘simplest quartic field’. Our main theorem states that under certain hypotheses there can be at most one positive integer m ≠ n m\neq n such that K m = K n K_{m}=K_{n} . The proof relies on the existence of squares in recurrent sequences and a result of J.H.E. Cohn [ 3 ] . These sequences allow us to establish uniqueness of the splitting field under additional hypotheses in Section 5 and to establish a connection with elliptic curves in Section 6 .

1.
Introduction

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\pm 3\}
and let
K_{n}=\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
, where
\rho
is a root of

f_{n}(x)=x^{4}-nx^{3}-6x^{2}+nx+1.

Since
K_{-n}=K_{n}
, we henceforth restrict
n
to be a positive integer.
These fields
K_{n}
are the
simplest quartic fields
of M.-N. Gras
[
4
]
. Their Galois groups are cyclic of order 4, given by the identity map and

\sigma:\rho\mapsto\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1},\qquad\sigma^{2}:\rho\mapsto\frac{-1}{\rho},\qquad\sigma^{3}:\rho\mapsto\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}.

It is natural to ask if
K_{m}=K_{n}
for distinct positive integers
m
and
n
.
Hoshi
[
5
]
gives the following examples:

K_{1}=K_{103},\qquad K_{2}=K_{22},\qquad K_{4}=K_{956},

and proves that these are the only ones with
0<m<n\leq 10^{5}
, and also these are the only ones with
0<m\leq 10^{3}
with no bound on
n
.

In the following, we take an approach different from the one used by Hoshi and prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

(a) Let
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
be a positive integer. There is at most one positive integer
m\neq n
such that
K_{m}=K_{n}
.

(b) Let
n\equiv 8\pmod{16}
be a positive integer and suppose the trace to
\mathbb{Q}
of the fundamental unit of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+16})
is odd. There is at most one positive integer
m\neq n
such that
K_{m}=K_{n}
.

(c) Let
n
be an arbitrary positive integer. There are only finitely many positive integers
m
such that
K_{m}=K_{n}
.

Remarks.
Part (c) was also proved by Hoshi, but using work of Siegel in a form different from what we use. The congruence conditions on
m
in parts (a) and (b)
are the only ones that allow the fundamental unit of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+16})
to have negative norm and odd trace, which we need in order to apply results of Cohn in Section 3.

2.
Preliminaries

Writing

f_{n}(x)/x^{2}=\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right)^{2}-n\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right)-4,

we see that the unique quadratic subfield of
K_{n}
is
k_{n}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{n^{2}+16}\right)
, and

\rho-\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{n\pm\sqrt{n^{2}+16}}{2}.

It follows that

K_{n}=k_{n}\left(\sqrt{2\left(n^{2}+16+n\sqrt{n^{2}+16}\right)}\right).

If

with

squarefree, then

for some positive integer

, and
K_{n}=k_{n}\left(\sqrt{2(y^{2}d+ny\sqrt{d}})\right)
.

Suppose now that
K_{m}=K_{n}
. Then
k_{m}=k_{n}
, so

m^{2}+16=dx^{2},\qquad n^{2}+16=dy^{2}

for some positive integers

and

. Since

k_{m}\left(\sqrt{2(x^{2}d+mx\sqrt{d}})\right)=k_{n}\left(\sqrt{2(y^{2}d+ny\sqrt{d}})\right)

and

, we obtain the following:

Lemma 1
.

With the notations above,
K_{m}=K_{n}
if and only if

\left(x^{2}d+mx\sqrt{d}\right)\left(y^{2}d+ny\sqrt{d}\right)=xyd\left(x\sqrt{d}+m\right)\left(y\sqrt{d}+n\right)\in\left(k_{n}^{\times}\right)^{2}.

Remark.
It is possible that

even though

. For example,
k_{58}=k_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
. Write
m^{2}+16=58^{2}+16=26^{2}\cdot 5
, so

. Write

, so

. Then

\displaystyle xyd\left(x\sqrt{d}+m\right)\left(y\sqrt{d}+n\right)

\displaystyle=52\cdot 5(26\sqrt{5}+58)(2\sqrt{5}+2)

\displaystyle=13(8\sqrt{5})^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}\right)^{8}

is not a square in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
since

is not a square in this field. Lemma

tells us that
K_{58}\neq K_{2}
. We could also deduce this from part (a) of
Theorem
1
since we already know that
K_{2}=K_{22}
, and there cannot be a third index giving the same field.

Theorem 2

(Cohn) Let

5.
Recurrent sequences

Let
k_{n}
be the quadratic subfield of
K_{n}
and let
\epsilon
be the fundamental unit of
k_{n}
. Let

u_{j}=\frac{\epsilon^{j}-\overline{\epsilon}^{j}}{\epsilon-\overline{\epsilon}},\qquad v_{j}=\epsilon^{j}+\overline{\epsilon}^{j}.

If

, then
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
satisfy the relations

\displaystyle u_{0}=0,\quad u_{1}=1,\quad

\displaystyle u_{j+2}=tu_{j+1}+u_{j},

\displaystyle v_{0}=2,\quad v_{1}=t,\quad

\displaystyle v_{j+2}=tv_{j+1}+v_{j}

for
j\geq 0
.

The proofs of the following relations are straightforward from the definitions of
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
:

\displaystyle 2u_{r+s}=u_{r}v_{s}+u_{s}v_{r}

\displaystyle u_{2r}=u_{r}v_{r}

\displaystyle v_{2r}=v_{r}^{2}-2(\text{Norm}(\epsilon))^{r}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
. Write
n^{2}+16=y^{2}d
and assume

\epsilon=\frac{n+y\sqrt{d}}{4}

is the fundamental unit of
k_{n}
. Let

be the smallest odd positive index for which

is divisible by
d
and suppose

dividing

such that

is a quadratic nonresidue mod
p
.
Then there is no positive integer
m\neq n
with
K_{m}=K_{n}
.

Proof.

As before, write
k_{n}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
with
d
squarefree. Then
n^{2}+16=dy^{2}
for some even integers

, and

\epsilon=\frac{n+y\sqrt{d}}{4}.

Suppose

.
From the proof of parts (a) and (b) of Theorem
1

for some

for some odd integer

. Moreover,

for some integer

Therefore,

divides
u_{r}
. Let

. Since

and

is the smallest positive index with

,

, so

and

, which implies that

is odd. Therefore, we may write

for some

.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be positive integers with

and

even. If

, then

.

Proof.

Write
r_{2}/r_{1}=2^{e}\ell
for some

and some odd

. Since

v_{r_{1}}=\epsilon^{r_{1}}+\overline{\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\text{ divides }v_{r_{1}\ell}=\epsilon^{r_{1}\ell}+\overline{\epsilon}^{r_{1}\ell},

we have

. Therefore,

.
This implies
v_{4r_{1}\ell}=v_{2r_{1}\ell}^{2}-2\equiv 2\pmod{p}
. Continuing in this way, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Returning to the proof of the theorem and working mod

, we have

\displaystyle 2u_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv 2u_{r_{0}+2\ell r_{0}}\equiv u_{r_{0}}v_{2r_{0}\ell}+u_{2r_{0}\ell}v_{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\equiv u_{r_{0}}v_{2r_{0}\ell}\equiv\pm 2u_{r_{0}}\pmod{p}

by the lemma.
Therefore,

\left(\frac{du_{r}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{\pm du_{r_{0}}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{\pm 1}{p}\right)\left(\frac{du_{r_{0}}}{p}\right)=-1

since
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and

is a quadratic non-residue mod

Therefore,
du_{r}
cannot be a square. This completes the proof.
∎

Multidegrees, prime ideals, and non-standard gradings
Alessio Caminata, Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Aldo Conca

Abstract
. We study several properties of multihomogeneous prime ideals. We show that the multigraded generic initial ideal of a prime has very special properties, for instance, its radical is Cohen-Macaulay. We develop a comprehensive study of multidegrees in arbitrary positive multigraded settings. In these environments, we extend the notion of Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals by means of a standardization technique. Furthermore, we recover or extend important results in the literature, for instance: we provide a multidegree version of Hartshorne’s result stating the upper semicontinuity of arithmetic degree under flat degenerations, and we give an alternative proof of Brion’s result regarding multiplicity-free varieties.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with several aspects of the theory of multidegrees.
The concept of multidegree provides the right generalization of the degree of a variety to a multiprojective setting, and its study goes back to seminal work by van der Waerden
[
60

in 1929.
Multidegrees have found applications in several areas (e.g., algebraic geometry, commutative algebra, combinatorics, convex geometry, and more recently, algebraic statistics), and there is a long list of more recent papers where the notion of multidegree (or mixed multiplicity) plays a fundamental role (see, e.g.,
[

The goal of this paper is twofold.
Firstly, we concentrate on studying several interesting properties that prime ideals enjoy in a multigraded setting.
Secondly, we make a comprehensive study of multidegrees in non-standard multigradings.

For organizational purposes, we divide the Introduction into two shorter subsections.

1.1.
Prime ideals

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j\leq m_{i}]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring with grading induced by setting
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, where
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
is the
i
-th standard basis vector.
Then
S
is naturally seen as the multihomogeneous coordinate ring of the product of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.

Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous prime ideal.
A fundamental goal of this paper is to study several properties of
P
.
Although our results regarding prime ideals do not involve in principle multidegrees, our proofs heavily depend on the use of this concept.
The fact that prime ideals are quite special from a multigraded point of view has already been noticed.
To highlight some of these we point out: Castillo–Cid-Ruiz–Li–Montaño–Zhang’s result that the support of the positive multidegrees of
S/P
forms a discrete polymatroid
[
7
, Theorem A]
, Brändén–Huh’s result that the volume polynomial (a different encoding of multidegrees) of
S/P
is Lorentzian
[
2
, Theorem 4.6]
, and Brion’s result on multiplicity-free varieties
[
3
]
.

Our first main result is regarding
multigraded generic initial ideals
.
Given a monomial order
>
on
S
, one may define the multigraded generic initial ideal in analogy with the singly-graded setting (see
Section 5
for details).

Theorem A

(
Theorem 5.6
)
.

(
\mathbb{k}
infinite
)

We have that
\sqrt{{\text{gin}}_{>}(P)}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal.

This result can be seen as yet another manifestation of the fact that the initial ideals of primes are “special”.
For example:

–

In
[
38
]
, Kalkbrener and Sturmfels showed that the radical of the initial ideal of a prime is equidimensional and connected in codimension one.

–

In
[
56
, Chapter 8]
, Sturmfels proved that the radical of the initial ideal of a toric ideal is Cohen-Macaulay.

–

In
[
33
]
, Hoşten and Thomas proved that the associated primes of the initial ideal of a toric ideal satisfy a saturated chain property.

On the other hand, it is not difficult to find a prime ideal
P
such that
{\text{in}}_{>}(P)
and its radical are not Cohen-Macaulay (see
Section 5
).
In this sense,
Theorem A
is a sharp result.

We also discovered an interesting (and perhaps unexpected) behavior of the minimal primary components of
{\text{gin}}_{>}(P)
.
Let
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subset[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
be a subset.
We denote by
S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the polynomial subring generated by the variables with degrees
{\mathbf{e}}_{j_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{j_{k}}
, and we write
I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}=I\cap S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
for the corresponding contraction of an
S
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
.
For an ideal
I\subset S
, let
\text{MLength}(I)
denote the maximal length of the minimal primary components of
I
.
The following theorem shows that
MLength
cannot increase under the natural projections of
{\text{gin}}_{>}(P)
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 5.9
)
.

(
\mathbb{k}
infinite
)

We have the inequality

{\rm MLength}\left({\text{gin}}_{>}\left(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)\right)\;\leq\;{\rm MLength}\left({\text{gin}}_{>}\left(P\right)\right).

Moreover, for every
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\text{Min}}_{S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}}\left(S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}/{\text{gin}}_{>}(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})})\right)
, we can find
\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Min}}_{S}\left(S/{\text{gin}}_{>}(P)\right)
such that the length of the
{\mathfrak{p}}
-primary component of
{\text{gin}}_{>}\left(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)
divides the length of the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary component of
{\text{gin}}_{>}(P)
.

To better appreciate the complexity of the process described in
Theorem B
, the reader is referred to
Section 5
.
Also,
Theorem B
may not hold for non prime ideals (see
Section 5
).

Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(S/P)\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be the integral closed subscheme corresponding to
P
.
We also consider the behavior of the multidegrees of
X
under the natural projections.
It turns out that our proof of
Theorem B
is a consequence of this study of multidegrees under projections.
The projection corresponding to
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}
is given by
\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}:{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}
.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=\dim(X)
, one says that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is the
multidegree of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
(see
Section 2
for precise definitions).
Geometrically speaking, when
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
equals the number of points (counting multiplicity) in the intersection of
X
with the product
L_{1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}L_{p}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, where
L_{i}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
is a general linear subspace of dimension
m_{i}-n_{i}
.
Let
\text{MDeg}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
be the maximum of the multidegrees of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
The next theorem shows that
MDeg
cannot increase under the natural projections.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 4.2
)
.

We have the inequality

{\rm MDeg}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)\;\leq\;{\rm MDeg}_{\mathbb{P}}(X).

Moreover, for any
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=\dim\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)
and such that
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{d}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)>0
, there exists some
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
and such that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
and
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{d}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)
divides
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
.

If we drop the condition that
P
is a prime ideal, then one can find fairly simple instances where multidegrees do increase under projections (see
Section 4
).

Finally, we also provide an alternative proof of the following remarkable result of Brion
[
3
]
regarding multiplicity-free varieties.
We say that
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
is
multiplicity-free
if
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}(X)\in\{0,1\}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
.
Let
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\text{ and }\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0\}

be the support of positive multidegrees of
X
.

Theorem D

(
Theorem 6.6
; Brion
[
3
]
)
.

If
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is multiplicity-free, then:

(i)

X
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay
(
i.e.,
S/P
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring
)
.

(ii)

X
is arithmetically normal
(
i.e.,
S/P
is a normal domain
)
.

(iii)

(
\mathbb{k}
infinite
)
There is flat degeneration of
X
to the following reduced union of multiprojective spaces

H\;=\;\bigcup_{{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\,\in\,\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{p}}\;\,\subset\;\,{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n_{i}}=\text{Proj}\left(\mathbb{k}[x_{i,m_{i}-n_{i}},\ldots,x_{i,m_{i}}]\right)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}=\text{Proj}\left(\mathbb{k}[x_{i,0},\ldots,x_{i,m_{i}}]\right)
only uses the last
n_{i}+1
coordinates.

Our proof of
Theorem D
uses techniques quite different from the ones used in
[
3
]
, and ideas related to the fiber-full scheme (
[
11
,
13
,
10
]
) play an important role.
We also point out that our proof is valid for arbitrary fields.

1.2.
Non-standard gradings

Multidegrees in non-standard multigradings are considerably more complicated as they lack a clear multiprojective geometrical content, but they are still important because of the flexibility they provide in terms of applications.
One prime example is Knutson–Miller’s result
[
41
, Theorem A]
expressing double Schubert polynomials and double Grothendieck polynomials as the multidegree polynomials and
\mathcal{K}
-polynomials, respectively, of matrix Schubert varieties with a fine grading.
This fine grading takes into account both row and column position;
we provide some examples that explore the technicalities of this fine grading (up to certain sign changes) in
Section 8
.
These fine gradings play a key role in the recent proof by Castillo–Cid-Ruiz–Mohammadi–Montaño
[
8
]
of a conjecture of Monical–Tokcan–Yong
[
49
]
stating the saturated Newton polytope property of double Schubert polynomials.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring (that is,
\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\deg(\alpha)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
).
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal.
The
multidegree polynomial

\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
of
R/I
is defined in terms of the multigraded Hilbert series of
R/I
(see
Section 2
for details).
In the standard multigraded setting it may be seen as an algebraic encoding of the multidegrees of the corresponding multiprojective scheme (see
Theorem 2.12
).

Our approach is to associate to
I
an ideal
J
in a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
S
such that
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
.
We call
J
the
standardization
of
I
(see
Section 7
).
This construction is inspired by the techniques
step-by-step homogenization
introduced by McCullough–Peeva in
[
45
]
for singly-graded settings and
standardization
introduced in
[
8
]
for certain multigraded settings.
It turns out that, there is a tight relation between
I\subset R
and its standardization
J\subset S
, as it may be witnessed from the following statements:

(i)

{\text{codim}}(I)={\text{codim}}(J)
.

(ii)

I\subset R
and
J\subset S
have the same
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded Betti numbers.

(iii)

\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
.

(iv)

R/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring if and only if
S/J
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring.

(v)

Initial ideals are compatible with standardization.

(vi)

If
I\subset R
is a prime ideal and it does not contain any variable, then
J\subset S
is also a prime ideal.

For more details, see
Theorem 7.2
.

These results allow us to deduce in
Theorem 7.5
that the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid for any prime ideal
P\subset R
, which constitutes an extension of
[
7
, Theorem A]
.

We introduce the notion of
arithmetic multidegree polynomial
, which can be seen as a generalization of the notion of arithmetic degree considered by Sturmfels–Trung–Vogel in
[
54
]
.
It is defined as follows

\mathcal{A}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(R/I)}\text{length}_{R_{P}}\left(\text{H}_{P}^{0}((R/I)_{P})\right)\,\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}}).

Motivated by the work of Hartshorne on the connectivity of Hilbert schemes
[
29
]
, we show in
Theorem 3.8
that arithmetic multidegree is upper semicontinuous under flat degenerations.
As a consequence, in the case of Gröbner degenerations, we obtain the coefficient-wise inequality

\mathcal{A}\left(R/I;{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,\leq_{c}\,\mathcal{A}\left(R/{\text{in}}_{>}(I);{\mathbf{t}}\right).

This upper semicontinuity result was obtained by Hartshorne
[
29
, Chapter 2]
in the singly-graded setting.

In
§7.1
, we extend the notion of Cartwright–Sturmfels ideals to arbitrary positive multigradings.
The original definition was given over a standard multigraded setting by Conca–De Negri–Gorla in a series of papers
[
18
,
16
,
17
,
15
,
19
]
.
This was obtained as an abstraction of previous work of Cartwright and Sturmfels
[
6
]
; thus the chosen name.

In the current arbitrary positive multigraded setting, we say that
I\subset R
is
Cartwright–Sturmfels
(CS for short) if there is a radical Borel-fixed ideal in the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
S
that has the same
\mathcal{K}
-polynomial as
I
(see
§7.1
).
It should be mentioned that
I\subset R
is CS if and only if its standardization
J\subset S
is CS (in the sense of
[
18
]
).
By utilizing the close relation between
I\subset R
and its standardization, in
Theorem 7.10
, we prove the following statements when
I
is CS:

(i)

{\text{in}}_{>}(I)
is radical and CS for any monomial order
>
on
R
; in particular,
I
is radical.

(ii)

The
\mathbb{N}
-graded Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\text{reg}(I)
is bounded from above by
p
.

(iii)

I
is a multiplicity-free ideal.

(iv)

If
P\subset R
is a minimal prime of
I
, then
P
is CS.

(v)

All reduced Gröbner bases of
I
consist of elements of multidegree
\leq(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
In particular,
I
has a universal Gröbner basis of elements of multidegree
\leq(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

This shows that CS ideals in arbitrary positive multigradings enjoy similar desirable properties as in the standard multigraded setting.

Finally, as a consequence of our work, we portray a family of multigraded Hilbert functions whose ideals have very rigid properties.
It is most convenient to enunciate this result in terms of the multigraded Hilbert scheme of Haiman and Sturmfels
[
28
]
.
Let
h:\mathbb{N}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function and consider the corresponding multigraded Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
parametrizing
R
-homogeneous ideals with Hilbert function equal to
h
.

Theorem E

(
§7.1
)
.

If
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
contains a
\mathbb{k}
-point that corresponds to a
CS
ideal and a
\mathbb{k}
-point that corresponds to a prime ideal, then any
\mathbb{k}
-point in
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
corresponds to an ideal that is Cohen-Macaulay and
CS
.

This theorem can be seen as a generalization of a previous result by Cartwright and Sturmfels
[
6
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.6]
.

Preliminaries

In this preparatory section, we briefly recall some basic concepts and we fix the notation.
In particular, we establish the initial stage regarding the main theme of this paper, that of multidegrees.
For more details on multidegrees the reader is referred to
[
48
,
14
]
.

We use a multi-index notation.
Let
p\geq 1
be a positive integer and, for each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
be the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}=\left(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0\right)
.
If
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}),\,{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
are two vectors, we write
{\mathbf{n}}\geq{\mathbf{m}}
whenever

for all
1\leq i\leq p
, and
{\mathbf{n}}>{\mathbf{m}}
whenever
n_{j}>m_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq p
.
For any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we set
{\mathbf{n}}!=n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!
.
We write
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for the vectors
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
and
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)
, respectively.

A
discrete polymatroid

\mathcal{P}
on
[p]:=\{1,\ldots,p\}
is a collection of points in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
of the following form

\mathcal{P=}\left\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\;\mid\;\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}),\;\forall{\mathfrak{J}}\subsetneq[p],\;\sum_{i\in[p]}n_{i}=r([p])\right\}

with
r:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

being a rank function on
[p]
. A
rank function on

[p]

is a function
r:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following three properties:

(i)

r(\emptyset)=0
.

(ii)

r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1})\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
if
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[p]
.

(iii)

r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})+r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\cup{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1})+r({\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
if
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[p]
.

A comprehensive discussion regarding polymatroids can be found in
[
51
]
.

.

The following statements hold:

.

\mathcal{P}_{1}
and

, then the Minkowski sum

.

[

.

(ii) See
[

.

Following
[
49
]
, we say that a polynomial

has the

2.1.
Multidegrees in positive multigradings

Here we briefly review the notion of multidegrees in positive multigradings that are not necessarily standard.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a
positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
polynomial ring (that is,
\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\deg(\alpha)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
).
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module and
F_{\bullet}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
R
-resolution

F_{\bullet}:\;\cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow F_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}

of
M
.
Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}
be variables over
\mathbb{Z}
and consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
, where the variable
t_{i}
corresponds with the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
If we write
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
with
\mathbf{b}_{i,j}=(\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
, then we define the Laurent polynomial

\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}\,:=\,\sum_{j}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}=\sum_{j}t_{1}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1}}\cdots t_{p}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p}}.

Then, the
\mathcal{K}
-polynomial
of
M
is defined by

\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\,:=\,\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}.

Remark 2.3
.

Since
R
is assumed to be positively graded, there is a well-defined notion of Hilbert series

{\text{Hilb}}_{M}({\mathbf{t}})\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[M\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\right){\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}},

and then we can write

{\text{Hilb}}_{M}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\frac{\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-{\mathbf{t}}^{\deg(x_{i})}\right)}.

In particular, this shows that
\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
does not depend on the specific free resolution
F_{\bullet}

(also, see
[
48
, Theorem 8.34]
).

Definition 2.4
.

The
multidegree polynomial
of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
is the homogeneous polynomial
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
given as the sum of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(M;1-t_{1},\ldots,1-t_{p})

having total degree
{\text{codim}}(M)
, which is the lowest degree appearing.

An
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
is said to be
multiplicity-free
if the coefficients of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})
belong to the set
\{0,1\}
.

We also have the following variation of the multidegree polynomial that was introduced in
[
15
]
.
This notion was introduced with the goal of measuring the contribution of all the minimal primes (possibly of not maximal dimension), and in the case of Borel-fixed ideals, it does precisely that (see
[
15
, Proposition 3.12]
).

Definition 2.5
.

The
\mathcal{G}
-multidegree polynomial
of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
is the polynomial
\mathcal{G}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
given as the sum of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})

whose corresponding monomial is minimal in the support of
\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})
.

For a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
, we denote by
\left[p\right]_{i}
the sum of the terms of
p
with total degree equal to
i
.
So, for any finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded module
M
with
{\text{codim}}(M)\geq i
, we obtain that
\left[\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})\right]_{i}
equals
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
if
{\text{codim}}(M)=i
and
0\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
otherwise.
We collect the following basic facts.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module.
The following statements hold:

(i)

\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
is the sum of the terms of
\mathcal{G}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
having total degree equal to
{\text{codim}}(M)
.

(ii)

If
z\in R
is a homogeneous non-zero-divisor on
M
with
\deg(z)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, then

(a)

\mathcal{K}(M/zM;{\mathbf{t}})=(1-t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{a_{p}})\,\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
,

(b)

\mathcal{C}(M/zM;{\mathbf{t}})=\langle\deg(z),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\,\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
where
\langle\deg(z),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle=a_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+a_{p}t_{p}
.

(iii) (additivity)

Let
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-modules.
Then we have the equality
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})=\left[\mathcal{K}(L;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})\right]_{i}+\left[\mathcal{K}(N;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})\right]_{i}
with
i={\text{codim}}(M)
.

(iv) (associativity formula)

\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\\
\dim(R/P)=\dim(M)\end{subarray}}\text{length}_{R_{P}}\left(M_{P}\right)\,\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}}).

(v) (positivity)

\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
is a nonzero polynomial with non-negative coefficients.

Proof.

(i) This part follows from the fact that any term in
\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})
has total degree at least
{\text{codim}}(M)
(see
[
48
, Claim 8.54]
).

(ii) It can be obtained by simple algebraic manipulations with Hilbert series (see
[
48
, Exercise 8.12]
).

(iii) It is a direct consequence of the additivity of Hilbert series and the inequalities
{\text{codim}}(L)\geq{\text{codim}}(M)
and
{\text{codim}}(N)\geq{\text{codim}}(M)
.

(iv) This is proved by a standard use of prime filtrations; see
[
48
, Theorem 8.53]
.

(v) Write
M\cong F/K
with
F
a finite rank
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
R
-module.
By considering a Gröbner degeneration we may assume that
K
is a monomial submodule (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Chapter 15]
).
Then the associativity formula (part (iv)) reduces to the case
M=R/P
and
P
a monomial prime ideal.
For a monomial prime ideal
P=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{m}})
, part (ii)(b) yields
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}})=\langle\deg(x_{i_{1}}),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\cdots\langle\deg(x_{i_{m}}),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle
, and so the result follows from the fact that
R
is positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded.
Alternatively, see
Section 7
.
∎

We introduce yet another notion of multidegree polynomial.
It can be seen as a generalization of the notion of arithmetic degree from
[
54
]
(also, see
[
29
]
).
The goal of this notion is to capture the contribution of all the associated primes.

Definition 2.7
.

The
arithmetic multidegree polynomial
of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
is given by

\mathcal{A}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\;:=\;\sum_{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)}\text{length}_{R_{P}}\left(\text{H}_{P}^{0}(M_{P})\right)\,\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}}).

2.2.
Multidegrees in standard multigradings

We now concentrate on standard multigradings.
The study of standard multigraded algebras is of utmost importance as they correspond with closed subschemes of a product of projective spaces (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
and the references therein).

Let
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a
standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
That is, the total degree of each variable
x_{i}
is equal to one (i.e., for each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\deg(x_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
1\leq k_{i}\leq p
) and
\deg(\alpha)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
.

Suppose that for each
1\leq i\leq p
there are exactly
m_{i}+1
variables
x_{j}
’s with degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
(i.e.,
m_{i}+1=\lvert\{1\leq j\leq n\mid\deg(x_{j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\}\rvert
).
Thus the corresponding multiprojective scheme is the product of projective spaces
\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
In this setting, the irrelevant ideal is given by
{\mathfrak{N}}:=\left([S]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\right)\cap\cdots\cap\left([S]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}\right)\subset S
.
An
S
-homogeneous prime ideal
P\subset S
is said to be
relevant
if
P\not\supseteq{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Relevant prime ideals are important as they are the ones that have a well-defined geometrical counterpart.
For more details on the
MultiProj
construction, the reader is referred to
[
7
,
37
]
.
The next remark shows that one can always restrict to relevant prime ideals when considering the multidegree polynomial.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
S^{\prime}=S[x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{n+p}]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring extending the grading of
S
and with
\deg(x_{n+i})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
; accordingly, we have that
\text{MultiProj}(S^{\prime})={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}+1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}+1}
.
Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous (not necessarily relevant) prime ideal, and set
P^{\prime}=PS^{\prime}
to be the extension of
P
to
S^{\prime}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

P^{\prime}\subset S^{\prime}
is a relevant prime ideal.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S^{\prime}/P^{\prime};{\mathbf{t}})
.

Proof.

(i) It is clear that
P^{\prime}
is a prime ideal, and it is relevant because
x_{n+i}\notin P^{\prime}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.

(ii) Given a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
S
-resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
S/P
, then
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
S^{\prime}
-resolution of
S^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
with the same Betti numbers.
Therefore,
\mathcal{K}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S^{\prime}/P^{\prime};{\mathbf{t}})
and consequently
\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S^{\prime}/P^{\prime};{\mathbf{t}})
.
∎

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module, and set
{\mathbb{P}}:=\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
The relevant support of
M
is given by
\text{Supp}_{++}(M):=\text{Supp}(M)\cap{\mathbb{P}}
.
There is a polynomial
P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Q}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
, called the
Hilbert polynomial
of
M
(see, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
), such that the degree of
P_{M}
is equal to
r=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}(M)\right)
and

P_{M}(\nu)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([M]_{\nu}\right)

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
\nu\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, if we write

(1)

P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}},

then
0\leq e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=r
.

Definition 2.9
.

Under the notation of
(1)
, we define the following invariants:

(i)

For
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{n}}\rvert=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}(M)\right)
,
e({\mathbf{n}};M):=e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
is the
mixed multiplicity of
M
of type
\mathbf{n}
.

(ii)

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded quotient ring of
S
and

X=\text{MultiProj}(R)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
be the corresponding closed subscheme.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{n}}\rvert=\dim(X)
,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X):=e({\mathbf{n}};R)
is the
multidegree of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Definition 2.10
.

For a multiprojective scheme
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}} \times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
, the support of positive multidegrees of
X
is denoted by
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X):=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\text{ and }\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0\}
.

Remark 2.11
.

In a multigraded setting one needs to be careful because the notions of multidegrees introduced in
§2.1
and
§2.2
may not agree.
This pathology stems from the fact that the support
\text{Supp}(M)
and the relevant support
\text{Supp}_{++}(M)
may be quite different for a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module
M
.
Nevertheless, we have the following unifying result.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module and set
r=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}(M)\right)
.
If
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{N}}}^{0}(M)=0
, then we have equality

\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r\end{subarray}}e({\mathbf{n}};M)\,t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
14
, Theorem A]
.
Also, for more details, see
[
7
, Remark 2.9]
.
∎

For a closed subscheme
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}} \times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
, we say that the
multidegree polynomial of
X
is defined as

\mathcal{C}(X;{\mathbf{t}})\;:=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\end{subarray}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\,t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}.

The above theorem implies that
\mathcal{C}(X;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(R;{\mathbf{t}})
for an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded quotient ring
R
of
S
such that
X=\text{MultiProj}(R)
and
(0:_{R}{\mathfrak{N}}^{\infty})=0
.

The following piece of notation will be useful throughout the paper.

Notation 2.13

(Natural projections)
.

Let
\mathfrak{J}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
be a subset.

(i)

Let
\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}
denote the natural projection.

(ii)

Denote by
S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the
\mathbb{N}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0\\
i_{j}=0\text{ if }j\not\in{\mathfrak{J}}\end{subarray}}{\left[S\right]}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})},

and denote by
I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the contraction
I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=I\cap S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
for any
S
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
.

Since the coefficients of the multidegree polynomial are non-negative in positive gradings, it becomes natural to address the positivity of these coefficients.
The next result characterizes the positivity of multidegrees, and it follows from
[
7
]
.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous
(
not necessarily relevant
)
prime ideal.
Then the support of
\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid.

Proof.

By
§2.2
, we may adjoin new variables to
S
and assume that
P\subset S
is a relevant prime ideal.
Consider the closed subscheme
X=\text{MultiProj}(S/P)\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
Then
Theorem 2.12
implies that

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(P)\end{subarray}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}(X){\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\in\;\mathbb{N}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

Fix
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(P)
.
From
[
7
, Theorem A]
we obtain that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}(X)>0
if and only if
\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}m_{j}-\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\leq\dim\big(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\big)
for all
{\mathfrak{J}}\subset[p]
.
We have that
r:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\,r({\mathfrak{J}}):=\dim\big(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\big)\big)
is a rank function (see
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
).
Equivalently, we get that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}(X)>0
if and only if

\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\;\leq\;s({\mathfrak{J}}):=\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}m_{j}+r([p]\setminus{\mathfrak{J}})-r([p])\quad\text{ for all \quad${\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]$},

where
s:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a rank function by
Section 2
(i).
Therefore the support of
\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid, as claimed.
∎

3.
A multidegree version of a result of Hartshorne

In this section, we extend some results of Hartshorne into the world of multidegrees.
More precisely, by extending the results of
[
29
, Chapter 2]
, we study the behavior of the arithmetic multidegree under flat degenerations (
§2.1
).
We shall see that the arithmetic multidegree is the multigraded extension of the numbers
n_{i}
’s introduced by Hartshorne in
[
29
, Chapter 2]
.

3.1.
The functor
R^{i}

Here we recall the definition of the functors
R^{i}
and some of their basic properties.
Essentially the same construction was considered by Schenzel
[
50
]
, under the name of
dimension filtration
, in his study of Cohen-Macaulay filtered modules.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.

Definition 3.1
.

For any
i\geq 0
, we set

R^{i}(M)\;:=\;\big\{m\in M\mid{\text{codim}}\left(\text{Supp}_{R}(m)\right)\geq i\big\}.

We first pause to point out some of the basic properties of the functor
R^{i}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

R^{i}(M)
is an
R
-module.

(ii)

R^{i}
is a left exact functor.

(iii)

We have the equality

R^{i}(M)\,=\,\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)}^{0}(M)

where

J^{i}(M)\,:=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\\
{\text{codim}}(P)\geq i\end{subarray}}P

is the intersection of the associated primes of
M
of codimension
\geq i
.
By convention, we set
J^{i}(M)=R
if there is no
P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)
with
{\text{codim}}(P)\geq i
.

(iv)

Let
0=\bigcap_{j}M_{j}
be an irredundant primary decomposition of the submodule
0\subset M
, and suppose that
M_{j}
is
P_{j}
-primary.
Then
R^{i}(M)
is the intersection of those
M_{j}
such that
{\text{codim}}(P_{j})<i
.

(v)

{\text{Ass}}_{R}\left(R^{i}(M)\right)=\{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\mid{\text{codim}}(P)\geq i\}
.

(vi)

{\text{Ass}}_{R}\left(M/R^{i}(M)\right)=\{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\mid{\text{codim}}(P)<i\}
.

(vii)

If
N
is an
R
-submodule of
M
supported on codimension
\geq i
, then
N\subset R^{i}(M)
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) are immediate from the definition of
R^{i}
.

(iii) It is clear that
R^{i}(M)\supseteq\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)}^{0}(M)
.
Let
m\in R^{i}(M)
.
By definition,
{\text{codim}}(\text{Ann}_{R}(m))={\text{codim}}(\text{Supp}_{R}(m))\geq i
, and so it follows that
{\text{Ass}}_{R}(R/\text{Ann}_{R}(m))\subseteq\{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\mid{\text{codim}}(P)\geq i\}
.
This implies that
J^{i}(M)\subseteq\sqrt{\text{Ann}_{R}(m)}
and consequently
m\in\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)}^{0}(M)
.
Thus we have the claimed equality.

(iv), (v) and (vi) follow from the already proved part (iii) and known behavior of the zeroth local cohomology with respect to associated primes and primary decompositions (see
[
22
, Proposition 3.13]
).

(vii) follows directly from the definition of
R^{i}
.
∎

The proposition below extends
[
29
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi:A\rightarrow B
be homomorphism of Noetherian rings which is either faithfully flat or a localization map with
B=W^{-1}A
and
W\subset A
a multiplicatively closed set.
Let
R
be a finitely generated
A
-algebra and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
Set
R_{B}=R\otimes_{A}B
and
M_{B}=M\otimes_{A}B
.
Then

R^{i}(M_{B})\,\cong\,R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}B

for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

From
§3.1
(iii) and the base change property of local cohomology (in either case
\phi
is flat), we obtain

R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}B\,=\,\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)}^{0}(M)\otimes_{A}B\,\cong\,\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)R_{B}}^{0}(M_{B}).

First, we assume that
\phi:A\rightarrow B=W^{-1}B
is a localization map.
Then, by basic properties of localizations,
{\text{Ass}}_{R_{B}}(M_{B})=\{PR_{B}\mid P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\text{ and }P\not\supset W\}
and
{\text{codim}}(PR_{B})={\text{codim}}(P)
for any
P\in\text{Spec}(R)
.
As a consequence, the equality
J^{i}(M)R_{B}=J^{i}(M_{B})
follows, and so we get
R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}B\cong\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)R_{B}}^{0}(M_{B})=\text{H}_{J^{i}(M_{B})}^{0}(M_{B})=R^{i}(M_{B})
.

Next, we assume that
\phi:A\rightarrow B
is faithfully flat.
By
[
44
, Theorem 23.2]
the associated primes of
M_{B}
are given by

{\text{Ass}}_{R_{B}}(M_{B})\,=\,\bigcup_{P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)}{\text{Ass}}_{R_{B}}\left(R_{B}/PR_{B}\right).

On the other hand, since
R\rightarrow R_{B}
is faithfully flat, it follows that
{\text{codim}}\left(J^{i}(M)\right)={\text{codim}}\left(J^{i}(M)R_{B}\right)
(see
[
43
, (4.D) Theorem 3, page 28]
and
[
43
, (13.B) Theorem 19(3), page 79]
).
This yields the equality
\sqrt{J^{i}(M)R_{B}}=\sqrt{J^{i}(M_{B})}
(see
§3.1
(iii)).
So, we obtain
R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}B\cong\text{H}_{J^{i}(M)R_{B}}^{0}(M_{B})=\text{H}_{J^{i}(M_{B})}^{0}(M_{B})=R^{i}(M_{B})
, and the result follows.
∎

We now make the connection between the functors
R^{i}
and the arithmetic multidegree introduced in
§2.1
.

Definition-Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.
For any
i\geq 0
, we define the
i
-th truncation multidegree polynomial
by

\mathcal{C}^{i}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\,:=\,\left[\mathcal{C}(R^{i}(M);{\mathbf{t}})\right]_{i}\,=\,\begin{cases}\mathcal{C}(R^{i}(M);{\mathbf{t}})&\text{ if }{\text{codim}}(R^{i}(M))=i\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then one has the equality

\mathcal{A}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{i}(M;{\mathbf{t}}).

Proof.

For any
P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)
with
{\text{codim}}(P)=i
,
§3.1
(iii) yields the following equality

R^{i}(M)_{P}\,=\,\text{H}_{P}^{0}(M_{P}).

Thus by
§3.1
(v) and the associativity formula of multidegrees (see
§2.1
(iv)), we obtain

\left[\mathcal{C}(R^{i}(M);{\mathbf{t}})\right]_{i}\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)\\
{\text{codim}}(P)=i\end{subarray}}\text{length}_{R_{P}}\left(\text{H}_{P}^{0}(M_{P})\right)\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}}).

Hence summing over
i\geq 0
gives the claimed equality.
∎

3.2.
Behavior under flat degenerations

In this subsection, we study the functor
R^{i}
under a flat degeneration.
The following setup is assumed throughout the present subsection.

Setup 3.5
.

Let
A
be a universally catenary Noetherian domain.
Let
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring over
A
.
Let
Q=\text{Quot}(A)
be the field of fractions of
A
.
For any
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
, let
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}):=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field at
{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Notice that, if
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module and
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
, then
M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded module over the positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
R\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\cong\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
The goal of this subsection is to study the behavior of
\mathcal{A}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}})
when varying
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
.
We have the following generic base change property of
R^{i}
.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
29
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module.
Then there is a dense open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\text{Spec}(A)
such that
R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\cong R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}))
for all
i\geq 0
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
.

In the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
we consider a coefficient-wise order.
Let
\geq_{c}
be the partial order on
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
such that for any two polynomials
f=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}f_{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}
and
g=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}g_{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}
in
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
we have
f\geq_{c}g
if and only if
f_{\mathbf{n}}\geq g_{\mathbf{n}}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
The next proposition shows that the arithmetic multidegree polynomial cannot decrease under flat degenerations.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module, and suppose that
M
is flat over
A
.
Then we have the coefficient-wise inequality

\mathcal{A}\left(M\otimes_{A}Q;{\mathbf{t}}\right)\;\leq_{c}\;\mathcal{A}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right)

for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
.

Proof.

Fix
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)
.
By
[
30
, Exercise II.4.11]
or
[
27
, Proposition 7.1.7]
there is a discrete valuation ring
V
of
Q
that dominates
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
; that is,
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset V
and
{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{n}\cap A_{\mathfrak{p}}
where
\mathfrak{n}
is the closed point of
\text{Spec}(V)
.
We have a field extension
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{n})
and by utilizing
§3.1
, we obtain that

R^{i}\left(M_{V}\otimes_{V}\kappa(\mathfrak{n})\right)\,\cong\,R^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\right)\otimes_{\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}\kappa(\mathfrak{n}),

where
M_{V}=M\otimes_{A}V
.
Since multigraded Hilbert functions are preserved under base field extensions, we get the equality of polynomials
\mathcal{A}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right)=\mathcal{A}\left(M_{V}\otimes_{V}\kappa(\mathfrak{n});{\mathbf{t}}\right)
.
Therefore, we can substitute
A
by
V
, and for the rest of the proof we assume that
A
is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Over the discrete valuation ring
A
, a module is
A
-flat if and only if it is
A
-torsion-free.
Since
M
is
A
-flat by assumption, we have
P\cap A=0
for all
P\in{\text{Ass}}_{R}(M)
.
Then
§3.1
(v)(vi) imply that both
R^{i}(M)
and
M/R^{i}(M)
are
A
-flat for all
i\geq 0
.
From
§3.1
, we get
R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}Q\cong R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}Q)
.
The
A
-flatness of
R^{i}(M)
yields the equalities

\mathcal{C}\left(R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}Q);{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{C}\left(R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}Q;{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{C}\left(R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right),

and so we obtain
{\text{codim}}(R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}))\geq i
.
The
A
-flatness of
M/R^{i}(M)
gives the injection

R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\hookrightarrow M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}).

Then we obtain the injection
R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\hookrightarrow R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}))
(see
§3.1
(vii)), and so the additivity and positivity of multidegree polynomials give the inequality

\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(R^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,\leq_{c}\,\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}));{\mathbf{t}}\right)

(see
§2.1
(iii)(v)).
By combining everything together we get the inequality

\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}Q;{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}Q);{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,\leq_{c}\,\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(R^{i}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}));{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{C}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right).

This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We are now ready to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
§3.2
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module, and suppose that
M
is flat over
A
.
Then the function

\text{Spec}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}],\quad{\mathfrak{p}}\mapsto\mathcal{A}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right)

is upper semicontinuous with respect to the component-wise order
\geq_{c}
on
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

E_{h}\,:=\,\left\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)\mid\mathcal{A}\left(M\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}});{\mathbf{t}}\right)\leq_{c}h\right\}

is an open subset for an arbitrary
h\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
.
Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
.
The topological Nagata criterion for openness (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 24.2]
) says that
E_{h}
is open if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

If
{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{q}\in\text{Spec}(A)
,
{\mathfrak{p}}\in E_{h}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
, then
\mathfrak{q}\in E_{h}
.

(ii)

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\in E_{h}
, then
E_{h}
contains a non-empty open subset of
V({\mathfrak{p}})\subset\text{Spec}(A)
.

Condition (i) follows from
§3.2
.
Condition (ii) is obtained by utilizing
§3.2
and Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 24.1]
).
This establishes the result of the theorem.
∎

For the sake of completeness, we restate our results in terms of Gröbner degenerations.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

4.
Multidegrees of projections

In this section, we study the multidegrees of all the possible natural projections of an integral multiprojective scheme
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
We show that the maximal multidegree of a projection is always smaller or equal than the maximal multidegree of
X
.
Even more, we prove that any multidegree of a projection must divide some multidegree of
X
.
The setup below is used throughout this section.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.
Suppose that
\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(R)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
be an integral closed subscheme and
R
be an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded quotient ring of
S
that is a domain.

For a closed subscheme
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{k}}
, we consider the following number

\text{MDeg}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}(Z)\;:=\;\max\{\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Z)\mid{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\text{ with }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(Z)\}.

The main result of this section is the theorem below.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
Section 4
.
Let
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subset[p]
and set
{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}
.
Then we have the inequality

{\rm MDeg}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)\;\leq\;{\rm MDeg}_{\mathbb{P}}(X).

Moreover, for any
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=\dim\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)
and such that
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{d}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)>0
, there exists some
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
and such that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
and
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{d}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)
divides
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
.

Before providing the proof of the theorem we need some preparatory results.

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider the natural projection
\Pi_{[p-1]}:{\mathbb{P}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p-1}}
and let
Y=\Pi_{[p-1]}(X)
be the corresponding image.
Let
\Phi:X\rightarrow Y
be the restriction of
\Pi_{[p-1]}
to
X
and
Y
.
If
\dim(X)=\dim(Y)
, then

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}},0}(X)\;=\;\deg(\Phi)\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Y)

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
;
here
\deg(\Phi)=\left[K(X):K(Y)\right]
denotes the degree of
\Phi
.

First proof.

Let
r=\dim(X)
.
We write the Hilbert polynomial of
X
as follows

P_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq r\end{subarray}}e({\mathbf{n}})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}.

Choose a positive integer
\omega
such that
P_{X}(\nu)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[R\right]_{\nu}\right)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
satisfying
\nu\geq\omega\cdot\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Let
A\subset R
be the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

A\,=\,R_{([p-1])}\,=\,\bigoplus_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1}\geq 0}\left[R\right]_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1},0}.

Consider the finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
-graded
A
-module
M=\bigoplus_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1}\geq 0}\left[R\right]_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1},\omega}
.
By construction the Hilbert polynomial of
M
is given by

P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,P_{X}({\mathbf{t}},\omega)\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1},\,m\in\mathbb{N}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|+m\leq r\end{subarray}}e({\mathbf{n}},m)\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p-1}+n_{p-1}}{n_{p-1}}\binom{\omega+m}{m}.

Regrouping the terms yields the following expression

P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq r\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{r-|{\mathbf{n}}|}e({\mathbf{n}},m)\binom{\omega+m}{m}\right)\cdot\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p-1}+n_{p-1}}{n_{p-1}}.

This forces the equality
e({\mathbf{n}};M)=e({\mathbf{n}},0)=\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}},0}(X)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r
.
Since
M
is a torsion-free module over the domain
A
, the associativity formula for mixed multiplicities gives the equalities
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}},0}(X)=e({\mathbf{n}};M)=\text{rank}_{A}(M)e({\mathbf{n}};A)=\text{rank}_{A}(M)\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Y)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r
.
As
\dim(X)=\dim(Y)
, the morphism
\Phi
is generically finite.
Let
\phi:\text{Proj}(R)\rightarrow\text{Spec}(A)
be the natural morphism obtained by considering
R
as an
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring after setting
[R]_{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1}\geq 0}[R]_{\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p-1},\mu}
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
.
Notice that
\phi
is a generically finite since
\Phi
is, and that
\deg(\Phi)=\deg(\phi)
.
Let
\eta
the be the generic point of
\text{Spec}(A)
and
Q=\text{Quot}(A)
be the field of fractions of

.
Then the generic fiber

is zero-dimensional (and thus affine, see, e.g.,
[
25
, Proposition 5.20]
) and we have the equality

\deg(\phi)\,=\,\dim_{Q}(\phi^{-1}(\eta))\,=\,\dim_{Q}\left(\text{H}^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\mu))\right)

for all
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular, this implies that
\deg(\phi)=\dim_{Q}\left(\text{H}^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\omega))\right)=\dim_{Q}\left(\left[R\otimes_{A}Q\right]_{\omega}\right)=\text{rank}_{A}(M)
, after possibly increasing

and making it large enough.
By combining everything, we obtain the claimed equality
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}},0}(X)\;=\;\deg(\Phi)\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Y)

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r
.
∎

Second proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq p-1
, let
L_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{i}^{\prime}\right)
be the line bundle on

To simplify notation, set

and let

.
From the projection formula (see
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]
) and the fact that

24

\displaystyle\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}},0}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}}c_{1}(f^{*}(L_{1}))^{n_{1}}\cdots c_{1}(f^{*}(L_{p-1}))^{n_{p-1}}\cap[X]

for all

.

∎

\Pi_{[p-1]}:{\mathbb{P}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p-1}}
.
We proceed by induction on
s=\dim(X)-\dim(Y)
.
If
s=0
, then the result follows directly from
Section 4
.

Suppose that
s>0
.
This implies that
\dim(\Pi_{p}(X))\geq 1
since the function
r({\mathfrak{J}})=\dim\left(\Pi_{{\mathfrak{J}}}(X)\right)
is a rank function (see
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
).
We substitute
X
and
Y
by
X_{\mathbb{L}}
and
Y_{\mathbb{L}}
, respectively, and we consider the generic element
y
in
\left[S_{\mathbb{L}}\right]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}
.
Let
X^{\prime}=X_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}
and
Y^{\prime}=\Pi_{[p-1]}(X^{\prime})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{\prime}
.
From
Section 4
and
Theorem 4.7
, we obtain that
X^{\prime}
is an integral scheme and the equalities

\dim\left(X^{\prime}\right)\,=\,\dim(X)-1\quad\text{ and }\quad\dim(Y^{\prime})\,=\,\min\{\dim(X)-1,\dim(Y)\}=\dim(Y).

This implies that
Y^{\prime}
and
Y_{\mathbb{L}}
have the same dimension, and thus we necessarily get
Y^{\prime}=Y_{\mathbb{L}}
because
Y^{\prime}\subset Y_{\mathbb{L}}
by construction.
Also, for all
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
and

, we have that
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}}^{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}(X^{\prime})=\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}(X)
.
Therefore, since
\dim(X^{\prime})-\dim(Y^{\prime})=s-1
, we can apply the inductive hypothesis to the scheme
X^{\prime}
and the corresponding image
Y^{\prime}=Y_{\mathbb{L}}
under the projection
\Pi_{[p-1]}
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
The multidegrees of prime ideals

In this section, we investigate several special properties that are enjoyed by the multidegrees of prime ideals.
We use the following setup throughout this section.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.

The theorem below proves that the multidegree polynomial and the
\mathcal{G}
-multidegree polynomial coincide for a prime ideal.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous prime ideal.
Then we have the equality

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\mathcal{G}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}}).

Proof.

Choose a monomial order
>
on
S
.
By using
[
38
]
, we obtain that
\sqrt{{\text{in}}(P)}
is equidimensional and connected in codimension one (see also
[
36
, Appendix 1]
).
For any monomial prime ideal
L=(x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{k}})\subset S
, we have

\mathcal{C}(S/L;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{G}(S/L;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}}

where
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
a_{i}=|\{l\mid\deg(x_{j_{l}})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\}|
(see
§2.1
).

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{h},P_{h+1},\ldots,P_{h+s}
be the associated primes of
{\text{in}}(P)
, where
P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}
are the minimal ones.
We choose a prime filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{r}=S/{\text{in}}(P)

where
M_{j}/M_{j-1}\cong S/L_{j}
and
L_{j}\subset S
is a monomial prime ideal (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Proposition 3.7]
).
All the associated primes of
{\text{in}}(P)
must appear among the
L_{j}
’s and all the
L_{j}
’s must contain a minimal prime of
{\text{in}}(P)
.
Therefore, by utilizing the additivity of Hilbert functions over this prime filtration and the above remarks, we obtain

\mathcal{C}\left(S/{\text{in}}(P);{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\mathcal{G}\left(S/{\text{in}}(P);{\mathbf{t}}\right)\,=\,\sum_{j=1}^{h}\text{length}_{S_{P_{j}}}\left((S/{\text{in}}(P))_{P_{j}}\right)\mathcal{C}(S/P_{j};{\mathbf{t}});

indeed: if
L_{k_{1}}\subset L_{k_{2}}
then
\mathcal{C}(S/L_{k_{1}};{\mathbf{t}})
divides
\mathcal{C}(S/L_{k_{2}};{\mathbf{t}})
; all the minimal
P_{j}
’s have the same codimension; and by localizing we get that the number of times that a minimal
P_{j}
appears among the
L_{k}
’s is equal to the length of the Artinian local ring
(S/{\text{in}}(P))_{P_{j}}
.
This concludes the proof because
S/P
and
S/{\text{in}}(P)
have the same multigraded Hilbert function.
∎

The remainder of this section is dedicated to multigraded generic initial ideals.
Thus we further specify the notation by fixing the following setup.

Setup 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an infinite field,
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
be a vector of positive integers, and

S=\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j\leq m_{i}\right]

be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial with
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Hence we have
\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
The group naturally acting on
S
as the group of multigraded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra isomorphisms is
G=\text{GL}_{m_{1}+1}(\mathbb{k})\times\cdots\times\text{GL}_{m_{p}+1}(\mathbb{k})
, where
\text{GL}_{m_{i}+1}(\mathbb{k})
is the group of invertible
(m_{i}+1)\times(m_{i}+1)
matrices over
\mathbb{k}
.
The
Borel subgroup of
G
is given by
B=B_{m_{1}+1}(\mathbb{k})\times\cdots\times B_{m_{p}+1}(\mathbb{k})
, where
B_{m_{i}+1}(\mathbb{k})
is the subgroup of
\text{GL}_{m_{i}+1}(\mathbb{k})
consisting of upper triangular invertible matrices.
Let
>
be a monomial order on
S
that satisfies
x_{i,0}>x_{i,1}>\cdots>x_{i,m_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.

Much as in the singly-graded case (see
[
22
, §15.9]
), we have the following definition.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous ideal.
The
multigraded generic initial ideal

{\text{gin}}_{>}(I)
of
I
with respect to
>
is the ideal
{\text{in}}_{>}(g(I))
, where
g
belongs to a Zariski dense open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset G
.

Whenever the used monomial order
>
is clear from the context, we shall write
{\text{in}}(I)
and
{\text{gin}}(I)
instead of
{\text{in}}_{>}(I)
and
{\text{gin}}_{>}(I)
, respectively.
An
S
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
is said to be
Borel-fixed
if
g(I)=I
for all
g\in B
.
Similarly to the singly-graded setting, it can be shown that
{\text{gin}}(I)
is Borel-fixed (see
[
22
, Theorem 15.20]
).

Remark 5.5
.

One can extend the known properties of Borel-fixed ideals in the singly-graded setting (see
[
22
, Chapter 15]
) to obtain the following statements:

(i)

Any Borel-fixed prime ideal in
S
is of the form

P_{\mathbf{a}}\,=\,\left(x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p\text{ and }0\leq j<a_{i}\right)

for some
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
(see
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
).

(ii)

An
S
-homogeneous Borel-fixed ideal
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal and all its associated primes are also Borel-fixed (see
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
).

The following theorem shows that the radical of the multigraded generic initial ideal of any prime is Cohen-Macaulay.
This demonstrates a very special behavior of prime ideals in a multigraded setting.

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous prime ideal.
Then
\sqrt{{\text{gin}}(P)}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal.

Proof.

Let
J=\sqrt{{\text{gin}}(P)}\subset S
.
Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}
be the minimal primes of
J
.
As a consequence of
[
38
]
, all the
P_{k}
’s have the same codimension.
Each
P_{k}
is Borel-fixed, and so we can write

P_{k}\,=\,\left(x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j<a_{k,i}\right)

for some
\mathbf{a}_{k}=(a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|\mathbf{a}_{k}|={\text{codim}}(P)
.
With this notation we now have

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\mathcal{C}(S/{\text{gin}}(P);{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{h}\text{length}_{S_{P_{k}}}\left((S/{\text{gin}}(P))_{P_{k}}\right)\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}_{k}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{h}\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}_{k}}.

The Alexander dual of
J
is a monomial ideal
K\subset S
given by

K=\left({\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{h}}\right)

where

{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq p,\,0\leq j<a_{k,i}}x_{i,j};

see
[
32
, Corollary 1.5.5]
.
The Eagon-Reiner theorem (see, e.g.,
[
32
, Theorem 8.1.9]
) yields that
J
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
K
has a linear resolution.
Thus we concentrate on proving that
K
has a linear resolution.

From
Theorem 2.14
, we obtain that the support of
\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})
, which coincides with the set of lattice points
\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{h}\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
, is a discrete polymatroid.
We construct the following monomial ideal

M=\left({\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{h}}^{\prime}\right)\quad\text{ with }\quad{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}^{\prime}=x_{1,0}^{a_{k,1}}x_{2,0}^{a_{k,2}}\cdots x_{p,0}^{a_{k,p}}

in the polynomial subring
\mathbb{k}[x_{1,0},x_{2,0},\ldots,x_{p,0}]\subset S
.
By construction and following the notation of
[
32
, §12.6]
, we say that that
M
is a polymatroidal ideal, and so the ideal
M
has a linear resolution by
[
32
, Theorem 12.6.2, Proposition 8.2.1]
.
Notice that
K
can be seen naturally as the polarization of
M
by sending
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}^{\prime}
to
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}
.
Finally, by standard properties of polarization (see
[
32
, §1.6]
), it follows that
K
also has a linear resolution.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

For an ideal

\text{MLength}(I)\;:=\;\max\big\{\text{length}_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left((S/I)_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\mid{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\text{Min}}_{S}(S/I)\big\}

-homogeneous prime ideal, the multiplicities of the minimal primary components of the multigraded generic initial ideal have a very restrictive behavior with respect to the natural projections.

Theorem 5.9
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
P\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous prime ideal.
Let
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subset[p]
be a subset.
Then we have the inequality

{\rm MLength}\left({\text{gin}}\left(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)\right)\;\leq\;{\rm MLength}\left({\text{gin}}\left(P\right)\right).

Moreover, for a given minimal prime
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\text{Min}}_{S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}}\left(S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}/{\text{gin}}(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})})\right)
of
{\text{gin}}(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})})

\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Min}}_{S}\left(S/{\text{gin}}(P)\right)

{\text{gin}}(P)
such that the length of the

-primary component of
{\text{gin}}\left(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)
divides the length of the
\mathfrak{q}
-primary component of
{\text{gin}}(P)

Proof.

As in
§2.2
, we can add new variables
x_{i,m_{i}+1}
to
S
with
\deg(x_{i,m_{i}+1})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}

is relevant.
By further specifying
x_{i,m_{i}}>x_{i,m_{i}+1}

{\text{gin}}(P)
will not involve the new variables
x_{i,m_{i}+1}

So, without any loss of generality, we assume that
P
is a relevant prime ideal.

Let
I={\text{gin}}(P)\subset S
, and

P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}
be the minimal primes of
I
.
For each
1\leq k\leq h
, we know that
P_{k}=\left(x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j<a_{k,i}\right)

for some
\mathbf{a}_{k}=(a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

(2)

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\mathcal{C}(S/I;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{h}\text{length}_{S_{P_{k}}}\left((S/I)_{P_{k}}\right)\,{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}_{k}}.

Set

it follows that

\mathcal{C}(S/P;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r\end{subarray}}\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X){\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}.

Then comparing coefficients yields the equality

\text{length}_{S_{P_{k}}}\left((S/I)_{P_{k}}\right)\,=\,\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-\mathbf{a}_{k}}(X)

for all

.

Set
{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}

By applying the same argument to the projection corresponding to

of

with

such that

\text{length}_{{\left(S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}\left(\left(S_{({\mathfrak{J}})}/{\text{gin}}(P_{({\mathfrak{J}})})\right)_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)\,=\,\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}}^{{\mathbf{d}}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right).

Therefore, the result follows as a consequence of
Theorem 4.2
.

Example 5.11
.

Here we continue using the prime ideal
P\subset S
from

, where

is the
\mathbb{N}^{3}
-graded polynomial ring

with

The corresponding multidegree polynomial is given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}2\,t_{1}^{3}t_{2}^{3}&\longleftrightarrow&\left({x}_{0},\,{x}_{1},\,{x}_{2}^{2},\,{y}_{0},\,{y}_{1},\,{y}_{2}\right)\,=:\,M_{1}\\

One can compute the whole expression of

by utilizing

[
26
]
, and one may check that
{\text{gin}}(P)
has a total of
9
primary components and that all the embedded ones have codimension
7
.
We consider the projection
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{2,3\}
.
The corresponding projected ideal is given by

We already saw that the multidegree polynomial of this projection is equal to

The correspondence between terms of

and minimal primary components of

is depicted below:

\begin{array}[]{lll}2\,t_{2}^{3}&\longleftrightarrow&\left({y}_{0},\,{y}_{1},\,{y}_{2}^{2}\right)\\

Again,

Notice that

6.
Multiplicity-free varieties

The main goal of this section is to obtain an alternative proof of the result of Brion
[
3
]
regarding multiplicity-free varieties.
For organizational purposes we divide the section into two subsections.

6.1.
Cohomology and associated primes under flat degenerations

This subsection contains some technical results that will be needed in our treatment of multiplicity-free varieties.
Here we study the behavior of cohomology and associated primes under flat degenerations.
Our approach is inspired by ideas related to the fiber-full scheme (
[
11
,
13
,
10
]
).

Proposition 6.1

(cf.
[
11
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
(A,{\mathfrak{n}},\mathbb{k})
be a Noetherian local ring with residue field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
\mathbb{Y}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}
be a closed subscheme and
\mathfrak{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
\mathbb{Y}
.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{F}
is flat over
A
.
Let
Y=\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
be the closed subscheme given as the special fiber and
F=\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}
be the corresponding coherent sheaf on
Y
.

If
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Y,F),\,\text{H}^{i+1}(Y,F)\right)=0
for all
i\geq 0
, then the following statements hold:

(i)

\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y},\mathfrak{F})
is
A
-flat for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

The natural map
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y},\mathfrak{F})\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(B),\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{A}B)
is an isomorphism for all
i\geq 0
and any
A
-algebra
B
.

Proof.

For any
A
-algebra
B
, set
\mathbb{Y}_{B}:=\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(B)
and
\mathfrak{F}_{B}:=\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{A}B
.

First, we prove that for any Artinian quotient ring
C
of
A
both statements of the proposition hold.
We proceed by induction on
\text{length}(C)
.
The base case
\text{length}(C)=1
is clear because we necessarily have
C=\mathbb{k}
.
Thus, we assume that
\text{length}(C)>1
.
We may choose an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset C
generated by a socle element of
C
to obtain a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow C\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

with
\mathfrak{a}\cong\mathbb{k}
and
\text{length}(C^{\prime})=\text{length}(C)-1
.
By tensoring with
-\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{F}
, since
\mathfrak{F}
is flat over
A
, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow F\rightarrow\mathfrak{F}_{C}\rightarrow\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0.

Consequently, we get an induced long exact sequence in cohomology

\text{H}^{i-1}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\,\xrightarrow{\delta_{i-1}}\,\text{H}^{i}(Y,F)\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C},\mathfrak{F}_{C})\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\,\xrightarrow{\delta_{i}}\,\text{H}^{i+1}(Y,F).

The inductive hypothesis yields the isomorphism
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\otimes_{C^{\prime}}\mathbb{k}\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}^{i}(Y,F)
.
Then the map
\delta_{i}
gets the following factorization

\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\otimes_{C^{\prime}}\mathbb{k}\cong\text{H}^{i}(Y,F)\;\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}\;\text{H}^{i+1}(Y,F)

where
\beta_{i}
is a
\mathbb{k}
-linear map.
The condition
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Y,F),\,\text{H}^{i+1}(Y,F)\right)=0
implies that
\beta_{i}=0
, hence
\delta_{i}=0
.
For all
i\geq 0
, we have proved that the natural map
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C},\mathfrak{F}_{C})\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})
is surjective, and the natural map
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C^{\prime}},\mathfrak{F}_{C^{\prime}})\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}(Y,F)
is surjective by induction.
Therefore, it follows that the natural map

\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{C},\mathfrak{F}_{C})\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(Y,F)

is also surjective for all
i\geq 0
.
Finally, by applying
[
30
, Theorem 12.11]
, we obtain both statements for
C
.
This establishes the proposition for any Artinian quotient ring of
A
.

For each
q\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{Y}_{q}=\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(A/{\mathfrak{n}}^{q})
and
\mathfrak{F}_{q}=\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{A}A/{\mathfrak{n}}^{q}
.
By the above step we have that
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{q},\mathfrak{F}_{q})
is flat over
A/{\mathfrak{n}}^{q}
.
The Theorem on Formal Functions (see
[
30
, Theorem III.11.1]
,
[
53
,
Tag 02OC
]
) yields the following isomorphism

\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y},\mathfrak{F})\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}\;\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\;\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}\;\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{q},\mathfrak{F}_{q}).

So by applying
[
53
,
Tag 0912
]
to the inverse system
\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y}_{q},\mathfrak{F}_{q})\right)_{q\geq 1}
, we obtain that
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y},\mathfrak{F})\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}
is a flat
A
-module.
Hence
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{Y},\mathfrak{F})
is
A
-flat for all
i\geq 0
.
This settles part (i) of the proposition.
Then part (ii) follows from (i) (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 2.6]
).
∎

Notation 6.2
.

Given projective scheme
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}=\text{Proj}(B)
over a field
\mathbb{k}
, we denote by
I_{Y}\subset B
the corresponding saturated homogeneous ideal.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}=\text{Proj}(B)
be a projective scheme over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

For
1\leq i\leq r
, the ideal
I_{Y}
has an associated prime of codimension
i
if and only if the following limit

\underset{\nu\to\infty}{\lim}\,\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{r-i}\left(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu)\right)\right)}{\nu^{r-i}/(r-i)!}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}_{+}

is a positive integer.

(ii)

Y
is Cohen-Macaulay and equidimensional if and only if
\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))=0
for all
i<\dim(Y)
and
\nu\gg 0
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{m}}=[B]_{+}
be the graded irrelevant ideal of
B
.
Set
C=B/I_{Y}
.

(i)
We recall the following known properties of Ext modules:

-

{\text{codim}}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)\geq i
;

-

a prime
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(B)
of codimension
i
is an associated prime of
C
if and only if it is a minimal prime of
\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)
;

see, e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Since
\delta_{i}=\dim\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)\leq r+1-i
, we consider the usual truncated notion of multiplicity

e_{r+1-i}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)\,=\,\underset{\nu\to\infty}{\lim}\,\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right]_{\nu}\right)}{\nu^{r-i}/(r-i)!}\,=\,\begin{cases}e\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)&\text{if }\delta_{i}=r+1-i\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(see, e.g.,
[
52
]
).
Then the graded local duality theorem (see
[
5
, Theorem 3.6.19]
) implies that

e_{r+1-i}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)\;=\;\underset{\nu\to\infty}{\lim}\,\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1-i}(C)\right]_{-\nu}\right)}{\nu^{r-i}/(r-i)!}.

There is a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow C\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\nu))\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(C)\rightarrow 0

and an isomorphism

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{j+1}(C)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{H}^{j}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\nu))\quad\text{ for all $j\geq 1$;}

see
[
22
, Theorem A4.1]
.
As a consequence, we have the equality

e_{r+1-i}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)\;=\;\underset{\nu\to\infty}{\lim}\,\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{r-i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))\right)}{\nu^{r-i}/(r-i)!}.

Finally, the result follows because
e_{r+1-i}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)\right)
is a positive integer if and only if
\text{Ext}_{B}^{i}(C,B)
has dimension
r+1-i
(i.e., when
C
has an associated prime of codimension
i
).

(ii) This is the content of the proof of
[
30
, Theorem III.7.6(b)]
.
Alternatively, we can argue as follows.
Let
y\in Y
be a point and
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(B)
the corresponding prime in
B
.
By definition we have that
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}=\big\{\frac{f}{g}\mid f,g\in C\text{ homogeneous elements},\,g\not\in{\mathfrak{p}}C,\,\deg(f)=\deg(g)\big\}
.
Let
C_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
be the localization of
C
at all the homogeneous elements not in
{\mathfrak{p}}
(we follow the notation of
[
5
, §1.5]
); this is an
∗
local ring ring with
∗
maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}C_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
.
By
[
5
, Exercise 2.1.27]
,
C_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
C_{\mathfrak{p}}
is.
Let
w\in[C]_{1}
be a linear form not in
{\mathfrak{p}}C
.
Notice that we have the equality
C_{({\mathfrak{p}})}=\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}[w,w^{-1}]
and that
w
can be seen as an indeterminate over
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
C_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

The same arguments in the proof of part (i) imply that
\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))=0
for all
i<\dim(Y),\nu\gg 0
if and only if
\text{Supp}_{B}\left(\text{Ext}_{B}^{r-i}(C,B)\right)\subseteq\{{\mathfrak{m}}\}
for all
i<\dim(Y)
.
The latter condition is equivalent to the following two conditions:

(a)

all minimal primes of
I_{Y}
have codimension equal to
r-\dim(Y)
;

(b)

C_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in V(I_{Y})\setminus\{{\mathfrak{m}}\}\subset\text{Spec}(B)
;

indeed, this follows from the local duality theorem and the depth sensitivity of local cohomology (see
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.7]
).
So, the proof of part (ii) is complete.
∎

By combining the above results we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(A,{\mathfrak{n}},\mathbb{k})
be a Noetherian local ring with residue field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
\mathbb{Y}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}
be a closed subscheme and suppose that
\mathbb{Y}
is flat over
A
.
Let
Y=\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
be the special fiber.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\text{Spec}(A)
and
Z=\mathbb{Y}\times_{\text{Spec}(A)}\text{Spec}(\kappa(\mathfrak{q}))\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}^{r}
.

If
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu)),\,\text{H}^{i+1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))\right)=0
for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\gg 0
, then the following statements hold:

(i)

Y
is Cohen-Macaulay and equidimensional if and only if
Z
is both.

(ii)

For
1\leq i\leq r
,
I_{Y}
has an associated prime of codimension
i
if and only if
I_{Z}
has one.

Proof.

By
§6.1
, we obtain the following equality

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))\right)\;=\;\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-\nu))\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\gg 0
.
Then the statements of the theorem follow from
§6.1
.
∎

6.2.
Properties of multiplicity-free varieties

Throughout this subsection we shall use the following setup.

Setup 6.5

Let
\mathbb{k}

be an
\mathbb{N}^{p}

Our next goal is to obtain an alternative proof of the following beautiful result.

Theorem 6.6

(Brion
[
3
]

Assume

.
If

is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

(iii)

(
\mathbb{k}

The proof of this theorem is divided in several steps.
We first consider the case of one-dimensional multiplicity-free varieties.

Lemma 6.7

Suppose that

The basic idea is to express

\Pi_{i}(X)\cong\text{Proj}\left(R_{(i)}\right)

By

, we have that

equals either

or
\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})

Since

That is, we have the following commutative diagram:

Proposition 6.8
.

Under the notation and assumptions of
Theorem 6.6
, we have
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\dim(X)
.

If
\dim(X)=0
, then the result is clear since we have
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})=1=P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})
.

If
\dim(X)=1
, then
X
is isomorphic to the image of a diagonal morphism
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}\rightarrow\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}\right)^{e}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
by
§6.2
.
Thus, we get
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})=1+\sum_{i\,\mid\,{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\text{MSupp}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(X)}t_{i}=P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})
.

Suppose that
d=\dim(X)\geq 2
.
Here we use the conventions of
Section 4
. Write
R=S/P
and notice that
P_{\mathbb{L}}:=P\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{L}\subset S_{\mathbb{L}}
is again a prime ideal (see
Section 4
).
One way of relating
X
and
H
is by using the multigraded generic initial ideal (see
Section 5
).
Let
>
be a monomial order on
S
that satisfies
y_{0}>\cdots>y_{\ell}
, where
y_{j}=x_{p,j}
.
Let
I={\text{gin}}(P_{\mathbb{L}})\subset S_{\mathbb{L}}
.
Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}
be the minimal primes of
I
and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{h}
be the corresponding minimal primary components.
Since
X
is assumed to be multiplicity-free,
Section 5
yields that
Q_{k}=P_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq h
.
Set
J=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{h}=\sqrt{I}\subset S_{\mathbb{L}}
.
By construction we have
H_{\mathbb{L}}:=H\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{L}\cong\text{MultiProj}(S_{\mathbb{L}}/J)
.
Let
G:=\text{MultiProj}(S_{\mathbb{L}}/I)
.
There exists a one-parameter flat family
\mathfrak{X}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
such that
X_{\mathbb{L}}
is the general fiber and
G
is the special fiber (see
[
22
, §15.8]
).
Then we write

P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,P_{G}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq d\end{subarray}}e({\mathbf{n}})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}

and

P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq d\end{subarray}}h({\mathbf{n}})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}.

Since
G_{\text{red}}=H_{\mathbb{L}}
, it follows that
\dim\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(G)\right)=\dim\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(H)\right)
for all
{\mathfrak{J}}\subset[p]
.
From
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have that
\dim\left(\Pi_{i}(X)\right)
equals the degree of
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})
in the variable
t_{i}
; and the same holds for
G
and
H
.
Suppose that
\dim\left(\Pi_{p}(X)\right)\geq 1
.
We have the equalities

P_{X_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,P_{G\cap V(y)}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq d,n_{p}\geq 1\end{subarray}}e({\mathbf{n}})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p-1}+n_{p-1}}{n_{p-1}}\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}-1}{n_{p}-1}

and

P_{H_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)}({\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|\leq d,n_{p}\geq 1\end{subarray}}h({\mathbf{n}})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p-1}+n_{p-1}}{n_{p-1}}\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}-1}{n_{p}-1}.

As no monomial of
J
involves the variable
y_{\ell}
, we obtain
J+(y)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{h}\left(Q_{k}+(y)\right)
.
This implies that

H_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)\;=\;\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\,\in\,\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\\
n_{p}\geq 1\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{n_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{L}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{n_{p-1}}\times_{\mathbb{L}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{n_{p}-1}\;\subset\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}.

On the other hand,
X_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)
is an integral scheme, and
Theorem 4.7
and
[
7
, Theorem A]
yield that
\text{MSupp}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}}\left(X_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)\right)=\{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{p}\mid{\mathbf{n}}\in\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\text{ and }n_{p}\geq 1\}
.
Therefore, the inductive hypothesis gives the equality
P_{X_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{H_{\mathbb{L}}\cap V(y)}({\mathbf{t}})
, and so we obtain that
e({\mathbf{n}})=h({\mathbf{n}})
for all
n_{p}\geq 1
.

For
1\leq i<p
, we can repeat the above arguments and consider the
i
-th component of
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
So, we conclude that
e({\mathbf{n}})=h({\mathbf{n}})
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\neq\mathbf{0}
.
Thus
c=P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})-P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})
is a constant polynomial.

We have a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow J/I\rightarrow S_{\mathbb{L}}/I\rightarrow S_{\mathbb{L}}/J\rightarrow 0
.
Hence
P_{J/I}({\mathbf{t}})=c
and the corresponding coherent sheaf
\mathfrak{K}=(J/I)^{\sim}
on
G
is supported on dimension zero.
By
Theorem 5.6
,
S_{\mathbb{L}}/J
is Cohen-Macaulay, and thus
H_{\mathbb{L}}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Let
\mathfrak{s}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{L}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{m_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{r}
be the Segre embedding, where
r=(m_{1}+1)\cdots(m_{p}+1)-1
.
Let
Z=\mathfrak{s}(X_{\mathbb{L}})
,
Y=\mathfrak{s}(G)
and
Y^{\prime}=\mathfrak{s}(H_{\mathbb{L}})
.
Let
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{s}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{1}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
, and notice that
\mathbb{Y}=\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{X})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{1}
is a one-parameter flat family with general fiber
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{r}
and special fiber
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{r}
.
We get a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathfrak{s}_{*}\mathfrak{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0
.
As
\mathfrak{s}_{*}\mathfrak{K}
is supported on dimension zero, we have
\text{H}^{i}(Y,(\mathfrak{s}_{*}\mathfrak{K})(\nu))=0
for all
i\geq 1,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand,
§6.1
(ii) yields
\text{H}^{i}(Y^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}(-\nu))=0
for all
i<d,\nu\gg 0
.
From the induced long exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(Y,(\mathfrak{s}_{*}\mathfrak{K})(-\nu))\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}^{i}(Y^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}(-\nu))\,\rightarrow\,\cdots

we obtain that
\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-\nu))=0
for all
1\leq i<d,\nu\gg 0
.
Since
\dim(Y)=d\geq 2
, it follows that the condition of
Theorem 6.4
holds for our current
Y
.
The ideal
I_{Z}
does not have an associated prime of codimension
r
because
Z
is an integral scheme, and so
Theorem 6.4
(ii) implies that
I_{Y}
does not have an associated prime of codimension
r
.
Therefore
\mathfrak{s}_{*}\mathfrak{K}=0
and
Y=Y^{\prime}
.

Finally, we have shown that
c=P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})-P_{H}({\mathbf{t}})=0
, and this concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We can now complete our proof of Brion’s result.

Proof of
Theorem 6.6
.

Let
P\subset S
be a prime ideal with
X=\text{MultiProj}(S/P)
.
Let
>
be a monomial order on
S
and assume that
\mathbb{k}
is infinite (see
Section 4
).
As in
§2.2
and the beginning of the proof of
Theorem 5.9
, we may adjoin new variables to
S
in such a way that all the associated primes of
{\text{gin}}(P)
are relevant.
Let
I={\text{gin}}(P)
and
J=\sqrt{{\text{gin}}(P)}
.
Since
X
is multiplicity-free,
Section 5
implies that
J
is the intersection of the minimal primary components of
I
.
By
§6.2
, we have the equalities
P_{S/I}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{S/P}({\mathbf{t}})
, and since all the associated primes of
I
are assumed to be relevant, we obtain
I=J
.

The result of part (iii) now follows at once (see
[
22
, §15.8]
).
Moreover, we have proved that the multigraded Hilbert function of
S/P
equals the one of
S/J
.
From
Theorem 5.6
we have that
S/J
is Cohen-Macaulay and as a consequence
S/P
also is.
This settles part (i).

Let
\overline{R}
be the integral closure of
R=S/P
in its field of fractions
\text{Quot}(R)
.
We have that
\overline{R}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
R
-module (see
[
57
, §2.3]
).
Write
\overline{R}=R[w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}]\subset\text{Quot}(R)
where
w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}
is a minimal set of homogeneous generators of
\overline{R}
as an
R
-algebra. Suppose by contradiction that each
w_{l}\not\in R
.
Let
T=S[z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}]
be an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring over
S
with a surjective

-algebra homomorphism
\psi:T\twoheadrightarrow\overline{R},\,z_{l}\rightarrow w_{l}
.
Since
\text{rank}_{R}\left(\overline{R}\right)=1
, the associativity formula for mixed multiplicities yields the equality
e({\mathbf{n}};\overline{R})=e({\mathbf{n}};R)
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
.
Hence we consider the multiplicity-free prime ideal

.
Choose a lexicographical monomial order

on

such that

for all

.
The assumption

would imply that a monomial of the form

is among the minimal generators of

However, this would contradict the fact that

(i) and
§7.1
; notice that the proof of

only depends on the fact that

So,

Finally, we restate Brion’s theorem in a more algebraic language.

.

.

is an

-homogeneous multiplicity-free prime ideal, then:

is Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

(

)

7.
Standardization of ideals

In this section, we develop a process of standardization of ideals in an
arbitrary positive (possibly non-standard) multigrading
.
Here we use a mixture of the techniques
step-by-step homogenization
introduced in
[
45
]
for singly-graded settings and
standardization
introduced in
[
8
]
for certain multigraded settings.
The following setup and construction is used throughout this section.

Setup 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\ell_{i}=|\deg(x_{i})|
be the total degree of the variable
x_{i}
.
Let

S\;=\;\mathbb{k}\left[y_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\text{ and }1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}\right]

be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring such that

\deg(x_{i})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\deg(y_{i,j})\quad\text{for all \;\;$1\leq i\leq n$.}

We define the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra homomorphism

\phi:R=\mathbb{k}[{\mathbf{x}}]\longrightarrow S=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}],\quad\phi(x_{i})=y_{i,1}y_{i,2}\cdots y_{i,\ell_{i}}.

For an
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
, we say that the extension
J=\phi(I)S
is the
standardization
of
I
, as
J\subset S
is an
S
-homogeneous ideal in the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
S
.
Let
{\mathbf{t}}=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\}
be variables indexing the
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-grading, where
t_{i}
corresponds with
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.
Given a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
and a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module
N
, by a slight abuse of notation, we consider both multidegree polynomials
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(N;{\mathbf{t}})
as elements of the same polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
.

The following theorem contains some of the basic and desirable properties that the standardization process satisfies.

Theorem 7.2
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal and
J=\phi(I)S
be its standardization.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

{\text{codim}}(I)={\text{codim}}(J)
.

(ii)

I\subset R
and
J\subset S
have the same
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded Betti numbers.

(iii)

\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
.

(iv)

R/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring if and only if
S/J
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring.

(v)

Let
>
be a monomial order on
R
and
>^{\prime}
be a monomial order on
S
which is compatible with
\phi

(
i.e., if
f,g\in R
with
f>g
, then
\phi(f)>^{\prime}\phi(g)
)
.
Then
{\text{in}}_{>^{\prime}}(J)=\phi({\text{in}}_{>}(I))S
.

(vi)

If
I\subset R
is a prime ideal and it does not contain any variable, then
J\subset S
is also a prime ideal.

Proof.

Let
T
be the polynomial ring
T=\mathbb{k}[{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{y}]\cong R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}S
with its natural
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-grading induced from the ones of
R
and
S
.
We see
R
and
S
as subrings of
T
.
We consider the quotient ring
T/IT
and notice that
\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is a regular sequence of homogeneous elements on
T/IT
.
We also have the following natural isomorphism

\frac{T}{IT+\left(\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}\right)}\;\cong\;S/J.

As the natural inclusion
R\hookrightarrow T
is a polynomial extension, we have that
\dim(T/IT)=\dim(R/I)+\dim(S)=\dim(R)+\dim(S)-{\text{codim}}(I)
and that
T/IT
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay.
So, by cutting out with the regular sequence described above, we obtain that

\dim(S/J)=\dim(T/IT)-\dim(R)=\dim(S)-{\text{codim}}(I)

and that
S/J
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
T/IT
is Cohen-Macaulay.
This completes the proofs of parts (i) and (iv).

Let
F_{\bullet}:\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}F_{0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
R
-resolution of
R/I
.
Since
\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is a regular sequence on both
T
and
T/IT
, it follows that

{\text{Tor}}_{k}^{T}\Big(T/IT,T/\Big(\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big)\Big)=0\quad\text{ for all $k>0$,}

and so
G_{\bullet}=F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}T/(\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n})
provides (up to isomorphism) a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
S
-resolution of
S/J
.
The identification of
G_{\bullet}
as a resolution of
S
-modules is the same as
\phi(F_{\bullet})
(more precisely,
G_{\bullet}
has the same Betti numbers as
F_{\bullet}
and the
i
-th differential matrix of
G_{\bullet}
is given by the substitution
\phi(f_{i})
of the differential matrix
f_{i}
).
We obtained the result of part (ii).
Therefore, by definition, we have the equalities
\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}})
that settle part (iii).

To show part (v) we can use Buchberger’s algorithm (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Chapter 15]
).
Indeed, we can perform essentially the same steps of the algorithm in a set of generators of
I
and the corresponding set of generators for
J
; for instance, for any two polynomials
f,g\in R
we have the following relation of
S
-polynomials
S(\phi(f),\phi(g))=\phi(S(f,g))
.

Lastly, we concentrate on the proof of part (vi).
Suppose that
I\subset R
is a prime ideal not containing any variable.
It then follows that
T/IT
is a domain and that
\{y_{1,2}\cdots y_{1,\ell_{1}},\,x_{1}\}
is a regular sequence on
T/IT
, and so
[
22
, Exercise 10.4]
implies that
T/(IT,x_{1}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}y_{1,j})
is a also a domain.
Finally, by repeating iteratively this argument we obtain that
T/\big(IT+\big(\{x_{i}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}y_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}\big)\big)\cong S/J

is a domain.
This concludes the proof of part (vi).
∎

Remark 7.3
.

Without too many changes the above arguments could be used to define the
standardization of a module
.
In that case, one could proceed by standardizing a presentation matrix of a module.

A direct known consequence of the above theorem is the following remark.

Remark 7.4
.

For any
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
the multidegree polynomial
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})
has non-negative coefficients.
Indeed, it follows from
Theorem 7.2
(iii) and the (well-known) fact that multidegrees are non-negative in a standard multigraded setting.

The following theorem shows that the support of the multidegree polynomial is a discrete polymatroid for prime ideals in a polynomial ring with arbitrary positive multigrading.
It is a consequence of
[
7
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
P\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous prime ideal.
Then the support of the multidegree polynomial
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{i\mid x_{i}\in P\}
be the set of indices such that the corresponding variables belong to
P
.
We consider the polynomial ring

R^{\prime}=\mathbb{k}[x_{i}\mid i\notin\mathcal{L}]\subset R.

Let
P^{\prime}\subset R^{\prime}
be the (unique) ideal that satisfies the condition
P=P^{\prime}R+\left(x_{i}\mid i\in\mathcal{L}\right)
.
By construction
P^{\prime}\subset R^{\prime}
does contain any variable.
Since
R/P\cong R^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
, it follows that
P^{\prime}
is also a prime ideal.
As a consequence of
Theorem 7.2
(vi) the standardization
Q=\phi(P^{\prime}R)\subset S
is a prime ideal.

Since the variables
x_{i}
with indices in
\mathcal{L}
form a regular sequence on
R/P^{\prime}R
, we obtain the equation

\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\prod_{i\in\mathcal{L}}\langle\deg(x_{i}),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\,\cdot\,\mathcal{C}(R/P^{\prime}R;{\mathbf{t}})

where
\langle\deg(x_{i}),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle=a_{i,1}t_{1}+\cdots+a_{i,p}t_{p}\in\mathbb{N}[{\mathbf{t}}]
after writing
(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,p})=\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

(see
§2.1
(ii)).
From
Theorem 2.14
we have that the support of
\mathcal{C}(S/Q;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid, and then
Theorem 7.2
(iii) implies that the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/P^{\prime}R;{\mathbf{t}})
is also a discrete polymatroid.
On the other hand, it is clear that the support of each linear polynomial
\langle\deg(x_{i}),{\mathbf{t}}\rangle
is a discrete polymatroid.
Finally,
Section 2
(ii) yields that the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid.
∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Assume
Section 7
with
\mathbb{k}
an infinite field.
Let
P\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous prime ideal, and
Q=\phi(P)S
be its standardization.
Then
\sqrt{{\text{gin}}(Q)}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal.

Proof.

By
Theorem 7.5
and
Theorem 7.2
(iii), the support of
\mathcal{C}(S/Q;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid.
One may notice that the proof of
Theorem 5.6
only depends on the fact that the support of the multidegree polynomial of the ideal is a discrete polymatroid.
Therefore, by using the same arguments applied to
Q
(which may not be prime), we conclude that
\sqrt{{\text{gin}}(Q)}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal.
∎

7.1.
Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals in positive multigradings

The purpose of this subsection is to extend the notion of Cartwright-Sturmfels ideals to arbitrary positive multigradings.
This family of ideals was defined and studied in a series of papers
[
18
,
16
,
17
,
15
,
19
]
over a standard multigraded setting.

Definition 7.7
.

An
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
is said to be
Cartwright-Sturmfels
(CS for short) if there exists an
S
-homogeneous radical Borel-fixed ideal
K\subset S
such that
\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S/K;{\mathbf{t}})
.

The first basic (yet important) observation that one can make is the following lemma.

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal and

Proof.

∎

Proposition 7.9
.

Let

{\rm CS}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of

Let

Notice that

Finally, we point out some interesting consequences of our work, that signal very rigid properties of certain multigraded Hilbert functions.
To that end, we use the multigraded Hilbert scheme of Haiman and Sturmfels
[
28
]
.
For a given function
h:\mathbb{N}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, the multigraded Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
parametrizes all
R
-homogeneous ideals
I\subset R
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[R/I\right]_{\nu}\right)=h(\nu)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
We have the following rigidity result that extends
[
6
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.6]
.

Corollary 7.11
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
h:\mathbb{N}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function and consider the corresponding multigraded Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
.
If
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
contains a
\mathbb{k}
-point that corresponds to a
CS
ideal and a
\mathbb{k}
-point that corresponds to a prime ideal, then any
\mathbb{k}
-point in
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
corresponds to an ideal that is Cohen-Macaulay and
CS
.

Proof.

Let
[P],[H]\in{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
be
\mathbb{k}
-points in
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
such that
P\subset R
is a prime ideal and
H\subset R
is a CS ideal.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal such that
[I]\in{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{h}
.
From the definition, it follows that
I
and
P
are both CS.
We may assume that
\mathbb{k}
is a infinite field and keep the primeness of
P
(see
Section 4
).
Let
J\subset S
and
Q\subset S
be the standardizations of
I
and
P
, respectively.
Since
J
and
Q
are CS, both
{\text{gin}}(J)
and
{\text{gin}}(Q)
are radical, and actually are equal.
Then
{\text{gin}}(J)={\text{gin}}(Q)
is Cohen-Macaulay by
Section 7
.
Finally,
J\subset S
is Cohen-Macaulay and
Theorem 7.2
(iv) implies that
I\subset R
is also Cohen-Macaulay; which concludes the proof.
∎

8.
Examples of determinantal ideals with a fine grading

The goal of this section is to present examples of multigraded ideals in non-standard positive multigraded polynomial rings that have a multiplicity-free multidegree.
The examples we selected are determinantal ideals.

We start with generic determinantal ideals with the finest multidegree structure.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and set
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in[m]\times[n]]
with
m\leq n
.
We give
R
a multigraded structure by setting
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{f}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
where
{\mathbf{e}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{e}}_{m}
and
\mathbf{f}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{f}_{n}
are the canonical bases of
\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\mathbb{N}^{n}
, respectively.
The determinantal ideal
I_{r}
of the
r
-minors of the
m\times n
generic matrix
X=(x_{i,j})
is then
R
-homogeneous.
We have:

Theorem 8.1
.

The
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R/I_{m}
has a multiplicity-free multidegree with respect to the
\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
-grading.

A combinatorial formula for the multidegree of
R/I_{r}
is described in
[
48
, Chap.15 and 16]
(up to certain sign changes).
Indeed, by
[
48
, Theorem 15.40 and Corollary 16.30]
, the multidegree of a larger family of determinantal ideals, the Schubert determinantal ideals, is given in terms of pipe-dreams (i.e., special tiling of the rectangles by crosses and elbow joints).
So one could derive
Theorem 8.1
from the results in
[
48
]
.
Nevertheless, we present below a self-contained proof of
Theorem 8.1
.
To simplify the notation, we set

\mathcal{K}_{m,n}=\mathcal{K}(R/I_{m};t_{1},\dots,t_{m},s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{C}_{m,n}=\mathcal{C}(R/I_{m};t_{1},\dots,t_{m},s_{1},\dots,s_{n}).

Indeed, we prove the following formula from which
Theorem 8.1
follows immediately.

Theorem 8.2
.

\mathcal{C}_{m,n}\,=\,\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in T}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathbf{b}}

with
T=\big\{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,\mid\,|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}|=n-m+1\big\}
.

For the proof
Theorem 8.2
we denote by
H_{m,n}
the right hand side of the equality, i.e.

H_{m,n}\,=\,\sum_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in T}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathbf{b}}

with
T=\big\{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\,\mid\,|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}|=n-m+1\big\}
.
Firstly we observe that
H_{m,n}
satisfies the following recursion:

Lemma 8.3
.

For all
n>m>1
, one has
H_{m,n}=s_{n}H_{m,n-1}+t_{m}H_{m,n-1}+H_{m-1,n-1}
.

Proof.

The first addend corresponds to the sum of all the monomials in
H_{m,n}
that contain
s_{n}
.
The second corresponds to the sum of all the monomials in
H_{m,n}
that do not contain
s_{n}
and contain
t_{m}
.
Finally the third addend corresponds to the sum of all the monomials in
H_{m,n}
that do not contain
s_{n}
and
t_{m}
. Here it is important to observe that
n-m+1=(n-1)-(m-1)+1
.
∎

Secondly we observe that also
\mathcal{C}_{m,n}
satisfies the same recursion of
Section 8
.

Lemma 8.4
.

For all
n>m>1
, one has
\mathcal{C}_{m,n}=s_{n}\mathcal{C}_{m,n-1}+t_{m}\mathcal{C}_{m,n-1}+\mathcal{C}_{m-1,n-1}
.

Proof.

The
m
-minors of
X
form a Gröbner basis of
I_{m}
with respect to the diagonal term order.
Let
J_{m,n}={\text{in}}(I_{m})
be the monomial ideal generated by the main diagonals of the
m
-minors of
X
.
Then

\mathcal{K}_{m,n}=\mathcal{K}(R/J_{m,n};t_{1},\dots,t_{m},s_{1},\dots,s_{n})

and similarly for
\mathcal{C}_{m,n}
.
We have a decomposition
J_{m,n}=x_{m,n}J_{m-1,n-1}+J_{m,n-1}
from which it follows that
J_{m,n}+(x_{m,n})=J_{m,n-1}+(x_{m,n})
and
J_{m,n}:(x_{m,n})=J_{m-1,n-1}
.
Therefore we have an induced short exact sequence

0\to R/J_{m-1,n-1}\left(-\deg(x_{m,n})\right)\to R/J_{m,n}\to R/\left(J_{m,n-1}+(x_{m,n})\right)\to 0

and hence

(3)

\mathcal{K}_{m,n}=(1-t_{m}s_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m,n-1}+t_{m}s_{n}\mathcal{K}_{m-1,n-1}.

Replacing
{\mathbf{t}}
with
\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}}
and
{\mathbf{s}}
with
\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{s}}
and extracting the homogeneous component of degree
n-m+1
we obtain

\mathcal{C}_{m,n}=(t_{m}+s_{n})\mathcal{C}_{m,n-1}+\mathcal{C}_{m-1,n-1},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove
Theorem 8.2
.

Proof of
Theorem 8.2
.

By
Section 8
and
Section 8
the polynomials
H_{m,n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{m,n}
satisfy the same recursive relation.
Hence it suffices to check that they agree when
m=1
and when
m=n
. When
m=1
the ideal
I_{m}
is generated by a regular sequence of elements of degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+\mathbf{f}_{j}
with
j=1,\dots,m
.
In this case,
\mathcal{C}_{1,n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{1}+s_{j})
which clearly coincides with
H_{1,n}
.
When
m=n
then
I_{m}
is generated by single element of degree
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{f}_{j}
, and hence
\mathcal{C}_{m,m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}
which coincides with
H_{m,m}
.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

As a consequence of
Theorem 8.1
and
§7.1
, it follows that
I_{m}\subset R
is a CS ideal.

Remark 8.6
.

By inspecting the Eagon-Northcott resolution of
I_{m}
, we may compute that the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
I_{m}
is given by
\text{reg}(I_{m})=m+n
.
Indeed, by coarsening the
\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
-grading of
R
into an
\mathbb{N}
-grading, we obtain that each variable
x_{i,j}
has degree
2
and that the ideal
I_{m}
is generated in degree
2m
.
Since the Eagon-Northcott complex has linear maps in the
x_{i,j}
’s and length
n-m
, it follows that
\text{reg}(I_{m})=n+m
.
This shows that the upper bound of
Theorem 7.10
(ii) is sharp.

Remark 8.7
.

From the recursion of
\mathcal{K}
-polynomials stated in
(3)
, we may compute the following formula for the
\mathcal{K}
-polynomial of
I_{m}
:

\mathcal{K}(R/I_{m};{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\;=\;1-t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n-m}(-1)^{j}h_{j}({\mathbf{t}})e_{m+j}({\mathbf{s}})

where

is the complete symmetric polynomial of degree
j
in
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
(sum of all monomials of degree
j
) and

is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
j
in
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
(sum of all squarefree monomials of degree
j
).
Also, the formula for
\mathcal{K}(R/I_{m};{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})

.

Finally, we point out that most determinantal varieties do not have a multiplicity-free multidegree with respect to the
\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
-grading.

Remark 8.8
.

For
1<r<m
, the
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R/I_{r}
does not have a multiplicity-free multidegree with respect to the
\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
-grading.
For
2<r<m
, this happens already with
respect to the
\mathbb{N}^{m}
(row) grading and the
\mathbb{N}^{n}
(column) grading individually, see
[
19
]
.
We have a significantly different behavior when
r=2
:
R/I_{2}
has a multiplicity free multidegree with respect to the row or column grading, but one can check by utilizing
[
48
]
or by direct computations that for

the multidegree polynomial has coefficients
>1
with respect to the
\mathbb{N}^{m}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{n}
-grading.
For example, when
m=n=3
and
r=2
the multidegree polynomial is

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(R/I_{2},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\;=\;

\displaystyle t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}t_{3}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}s_{1}+{\bf 2}t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}s_{1}+t_{1}t_{2}s_{1}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}s_{1}s_{2}+{\bf 2}t_{1}t_{2}s_{1}s_{2}+

\displaystyle{\bf 2}t_{1}s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}+t_{1}s_{1}^{2}s_{2}+s_{1}^{2}s_{2}s_{3}+s_{1}^{2}s_{2}^{2}+\cdots\mbox{ symmetric terms}.

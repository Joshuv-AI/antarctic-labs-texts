Abstract
. For a bounded Lipschitz domain with Lipschitz interface we show the following compactness theorem : Any 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -bounded sequence of vector fields with 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -bounded rotations and 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -bounded divergences as well as 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -bounded tangential traces on one part of the boundary and 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -bounded normal traces on the other part of the boundary, contains a strongly 𝖫 2 \mathsf{L}^{2} -convergent subsequence. This generalises recent results for homogeneous mixed boundary conditions in [ 2 , 4 ] . As applications we present a related Friedrichs/Poincaré type estimate , a div-curl lemma , and show that the Maxwell operator with mixed tangential and impedance boundary conditions (Robin type boundary conditions) has compact resolvents .

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Gamma
,
composed of the boundary parts
\Gamma_{\!0}
(tangential) and
\Gamma_{\!1}

In

Theorem 1.1

(compact embedding for vector fields with homogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

Let
(\Omega,\Gamma_{\!0})
be a bounded strong Lipschitz pair and let
\varepsilon
be admissible. Then

\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

Here,

– in classical terms and in the smooth case –

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\rot E

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle n\times E|_{\Gamma_{\!0}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\in\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\varepsilon E

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\dive\varepsilon E

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle n\cdot\varepsilon E|_{\Gamma_{\!1}}

\displaystyle=0.

\lVert E\rVert_{\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)}^{2}\coloneqq\lVert E\rVert_{\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}^{2}
.
The definitions of the latter Hilbert spaces need some explanations:

Definition 2.1

(
\mathsf{L}^{2}
-traces)
.

(i)

The tangential trace of a vector field
E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)

is a well-defined tangential vector field
\tau_{\Gamma}E\in\mathsf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)

generalising the classical tangential trace
\tau_{\Gamma}\widetilde{E}=-n\times n\times\widetilde{E}|_{\Gamma}

for smooth vector fields
\widetilde{E}
.
By the notation
\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
we mean, that there exists
a tangential vector field
E_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
, such that
for all vector fields
\Phi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)
it holds

\langle\rot\Phi,E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\Phi,\rot E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\Phi,E_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}.

Then we set
\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\coloneqq E_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
.
Here and in the following, the twisted tangential trace of the smooth vector field
\Phi

is given by the tangential vector field
\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma}\Phi=n\times\Phi|_{\Gamma}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)

with
\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\Phi=\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma}\Phi|_{\Gamma_{\!1}}=0

and
\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\Phi=\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma}\Phi|_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
.
Note that
\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E
is well defined as

\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)
is dense in

\mathsf{L}^{2}_{t}(\Gamma_{\!0})=\big\{v\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0}):n\cdot v=0\big\}
.

(ii)

Analogously, the normal trace of a vector field
E\in\mathsf{H}(\dive,\Omega)

is a well-defined function
\nu_{\Gamma}E\in\mathsf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)

generalising the classical normal trace
\nu_{\Gamma}\widetilde{E}=n\cdot\widetilde{E}|_{\Gamma}

for smooth vector fields
\widetilde{E}
.
Again, by the notation
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
we mean, that
for all functions
\phi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
it holds

\langle\grad\phi,E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\phi,\dive E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}.

Here, the well-known scalar trace of the smooth function
\phi

is given by
\sigma_{\Gamma}\phi=\phi|_{\Gamma}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)

with
\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\phi=\sigma_{\Gamma}\phi|_{\Gamma_{\!0}}=0

and
\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi=\sigma_{\Gamma}\phi|_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
.
Note that
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E
is well defined as

\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
.

Remark 2.2

(
\mathsf{L}^{2}
-traces)
.

Analogously to
2.1

(i)
and as

\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\widetilde{E}\cdot\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\widetilde{H}=(n\times\widetilde{E})\cdot(-n\times n\times\widetilde{H})=(n\times n\times\widetilde{E})\cdot(n\times\widetilde{H})=-\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\widetilde{E}\cdot\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\widetilde{H}

holds on
\Gamma_{\!0}
for smooth vector fields
\widetilde{E}
,
\widetilde{H}
,
we can define the twisted tangential trace

\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
of a vector field
E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)

as well by

\langle\rot\Phi,E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\Phi,\rot E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=-\langle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\Phi,\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}

for all vector fields
\Phi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)
.

4.
Compact Embeddings

Our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 4.1

(compact embedding for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
(E_{\ell})
be a bounded sequence in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)
.
By the Helmholtz decomposition in
3.3
we

\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
-orthogonally and regularly decompose

E_{\ell}=\grad u_{\grad,\ell}+E_{\mathcal{H},\ell}+\varepsilon^{-1}\rot E_{\rot,\ell}

with
u_{\grad,\ell}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
,
E_{\rot,\ell}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)
, and

E_{\mathcal{H},\ell}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, and there exists a constant
c>0
such that
independent of
E_{\dots}
and for all
\ell

\lVert u_{\grad,\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)}+\lVert E_{\mathcal{H},\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}+\lVert E_{\rot,\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}.

As
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)
is finite dimensional
we may assume (after extracting a subsequence) that
E_{\mathcal{H},\ell}
converges strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
.
Since
\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
by Rellich’s selection theorem, we may assume
that also the regular potentials
u_{\grad,\ell}
and
E_{\rot,\ell}

converge strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Moreover,
u_{\grad,\ell}|_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\rot,\ell}|_{\Gamma}
are bounded in

\mathsf{H}^{1/2}{}(\Gamma)
by the (scalar) trace theorem, and thus we may assume
by the compact embedding
\mathsf{H}^{1/2}{}(\Gamma)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
that

u_{\grad,\ell}|_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\rot,\ell}|_{\Gamma}
converge strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}{}(\Gamma)
.
In particular,
u_{\grad,\ell}|_{\Gamma_{\!1}}
and
E_{\rot,\ell}|_{\Gamma_{\!0}}

converge strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}{}(\Gamma_{\!1})
and
\mathsf{L}^{2}{}(\Gamma_{\!0})
, respectively.
After all this successively taking subsequences we obtain
(using
\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
-orthogonality and the definition
of the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
-traces of
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E_{\ell}

and the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})
-traces of
\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E_{\ell}
from
2.1
)

\displaystyle\big\lVert\grad(u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k})\big\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\grad(u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k}),E_{\ell}-E_{k}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\big\langle u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k},\dive\varepsilon(E_{\ell}-E_{k})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k}),\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon(E_{\ell}-E_{k})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+c\big\lVert(u_{\grad,\ell}-u_{\grad,k})|_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\big\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\to 0

and

\displaystyle\big\lVert\varepsilon^{-1}\rot(E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k})\big\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\varepsilon^{-1}\rot(E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k}),E_{\ell}-E_{k}\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\big\langle E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k},\rot(E_{\ell}-E_{k})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\langle\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k}),\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(E_{\ell}-E_{k})\big\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+c\big\lVert(E_{\rot,\ell}-E_{\rot,k})|_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\big\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}\to 0.

Hence,
(E_{\ell})
contains a strongly
\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
-convergent
(and thus strongly
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-convergent) subsequence.

Remark 4.2

(compact embedding for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

4.1
even holds for weaker boundary data. For this, let
0\leq s<1/2
.
Taking into account the compact embedding
\mathsf{H}^{1/2}{}(\Gamma)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{H}^{s}{}(\Gamma)

and looking at the latter proof, we see that

\big\{E\in\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\,:\,\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\in\mathsf{H}^{-s}{}(\Gamma_{\!0})\big\}\cap\big\{E\in\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{H}(\dive,\Omega)\,:\,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E\in\mathsf{H}^{-s}{}(\Gamma_{\!1})\big\}\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

5.
Applications

5.1.
Friedrichs/Poincaré Type Estimates

A first application is the following estimate:

Theorem 5.1

(Friedrichs/Poincaré type estimate for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

There exists a positive constant
c
such that for all vector fields
E
in

\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)^{\bot_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}}

it holds

c\lVert E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\rot E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\dive\varepsilon E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}+\lVert\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}.

Proof.

For a proof we use a standard compactness argument using
4.1
.
If the estimate was wrong, then there exists a sequence

(E_{\ell})\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)^{\bot_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}}

with
\lVert E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}=1
and

\lVert\rot E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\dive\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}+\lVert\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\to 0.

Thus, by
4.1
(after extracting a subsequence)

E_{\ell}\to E\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)^{\bot_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}}\qquad\mathrm{(strongly)}

and
\rot E=0
and
\dive\varepsilon E=0
(by testing).
Moreover, for all
\Phi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\Omega)
and for all

\phi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\rot\Phi,E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\Phi,\rot E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\Phi,\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}\leq c\lVert\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!0})}\to 0,

\displaystyle\langle\grad\phi,\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\phi,\dive\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\leq c\lVert\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\varepsilon E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\to 0,

cf.
2.1
, implying

\langle\rot\Phi,E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\grad\phi,\varepsilon E\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Hence,

E\in\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},0}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)

by
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
(weak and strong homogeneous boundary conditions coincide).
This shows
E=0
as
E\,\bot_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\,\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},\Gamma_{\!1},\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, in contradiction to

1=\lVert E_{\ell}\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\to\lVert E\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}=0
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Friedrichs/Poincaré type estimate for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

As in
4.2
there are corresponding generalised
Friedrichs/Poincaré type estimates for weaker boundary data, where
the
\mathsf{L}^{2}{}(\Gamma_{0/1})
-spaces and norms are
replaced by
\mathsf{H}^{-s}{}(\Gamma_{0/1})
-spaces and norms.

5.2.
A div-curl Lemma

Another immediate consequence is a div-curl-lemma.

Theorem 5.3

(div-curl lemma for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

Let
(E_{n})
and
(H_{n})
be bounded sequences
in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)
and
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)
, respectively.
Then there exist
E\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)
and
H\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)

as well as subsequences, again denoted by
(E_{n})
and
(H_{n})
, such that

E_{n}\rightharpoonup E
in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)

and
H_{n}\rightharpoonup H
in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)
as well as

\langle E_{n},H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\to\langle E,H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We follow in closed lines the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Let
(E_{n})
and
(H_{n})
be as stated.
First, we pick subsequences, again denoted by
(E_{n})
and
(H_{n})
,
and
E
and
H
, such that

E_{n}\rightharpoonup E
in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)

and
H_{n}\rightharpoonup H
in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)
.
In particular,

(1)

\displaystyle\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H\quad\text{in}\quad\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1}).

To see (
1
), let
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rightharpoonup H_{\Gamma_{\!1}}
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
.
Since for all
\phi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,H_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\leftarrow\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}=

\displaystyle\;\langle\grad\phi,H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\phi,\dive H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\;\langle\grad\phi,H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\phi,\dive H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

we get
H\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)
and
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H=H_{\Gamma_{\!1}}
.
Moreover,

\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\to\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\phi,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}
.
As
\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})

and
\big(\langle\,\cdot\,,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}\big)

is uniformly bounded with respect to
n
we obtain (
1
).

By
3.2
we have the orthogonal Helmholtz decomposition

\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\ni E_{n}=\grad u_{n}+\widetilde{E}_{n}

with
u_{n}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)

and
\widetilde{E}_{n}\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)

as
\grad\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)\subset\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},0}(\rot,\Omega)\subset\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)
.
By orthogonality and the Friedrichs/Poincaré estimate,

(u_{n})
is bounded in
\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
and hence contains
a strongly
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-convergent subsequence, again denoted by
(u_{n})
.
(For
\Gamma_{\!0}=\emptyset
we may have to add a constant to each
u_{n}
.)
Moreover, as
(u_{n}|_{\Gamma})
is bounded in

\mathsf{H}^{1/2}{}(\Gamma)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
we may assume that

(u_{n}|_{\Gamma})
converges strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
.
In particular,
(\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}u_{n})=(u_{n}|_{\Gamma_{\!1}})
converges strongly in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
.
The sequence
(\widetilde{E}_{n})
is bounded in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)

by orthogonality and since
\rot\widetilde{E}_{n}=\rot E_{n}

and
\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}\widetilde{E}_{n}=\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E_{n}
.

4.1
yields a strongly
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-convergent subsequence,
again denoted by
(\widetilde{E}_{n})
.
Hence, there exist
u\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
and

\widetilde{E}\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)

such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
and

u_{n}\to u
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and

\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}u_{n}\to\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}u
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})

as well as
\widetilde{E}_{n}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{E}
in

\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)

and
\widetilde{E}_{n}\to\widetilde{E}
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Finally, we compute

\displaystyle\langle E_{n},H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\grad u_{n},H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\widetilde{E}_{n},H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\langle u_{n},\dive H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}u_{n},\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}+\langle\widetilde{E}_{n},H_{n}\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\to-\langle u,\dive H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}u,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})}+\langle\widetilde{E},H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\grad u,\dive H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\widetilde{E},H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle E,H\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

since indeed
E=\grad u+\widetilde{E}
holds by the weak convergence.
∎

(div-curl lemma for vector fields with inhomogeneous mixed boundary conditions)
.

As in
4.2
and
5.2
there are corresponding generalised
div-curl lemmas for weaker boundary data, where
the
\mathsf{L}^{2}{}(\Gamma_{0/1})
-spaces and norms are

5.3.
Maxwell’s Equations with Mixed Impedance Type Boundary Conditions

Let
\varepsilon
,
\mu
be admissible and time-independent matrix fields,
and let
T,k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
In
I\times\Omega
with
I\coloneqq(0,T)
we consider Maxwell’s equations
with mixed tangential and impedance boundary conditions

(2a)

\displaystyle\p_{t}E-\varepsilon^{-1}\rot H

\displaystyle=F\in\mathsf{L}^{2}\big(I,\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\big),

(Ampère/Maxwell law)

(2b)

\displaystyle\p_{t}H+\mu^{-1}\rot E

\displaystyle=G\in\mathsf{L}^{2}\big(I,\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\Omega)\big),

(Faraday/Maxwell law)

(2c)

\displaystyle\dive\varepsilon E

\displaystyle=\rho,

(Gauß law)

(2d)

\displaystyle\dive\mu H

\displaystyle=0,

(Gauß law for magnetism)

(2e)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E

\displaystyle=0,

(perfect conductor bc)

(2f)

\displaystyle\nu_{\Gamma_{\!0}}H

\displaystyle=f,

(normal trace bc)

(2g)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E+k\tau^{\times}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}H

\displaystyle=0,

(impedance bc)

(2h)

\displaystyle E(0)

\displaystyle=E_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega),

(electric initial value)

(2i)

\displaystyle H(0)

\displaystyle=H_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}_{\mu}(\Omega).

(magnetic initial value)

Note that the impedance boundary condition, also called Leontovich boundary condition,
is of Robin type and that the impedance is given by
\lambda=1/k=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\mu}
,
if
\varepsilon,\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Despite of other recent and very powerful approaches
such as the concept of “evolutionary equations”, see the pioneering work of Rainer Picard,
e.g.,
[
20
,
10
]
,
one can use classical semigroup theory for solving the Maxwell system (
2
).

We will split the system (
2
) into two static systems and a dynamic system.
For simplicity we set
\varepsilon=\mu=1
and
F=G=0
.
The static systems are

(3a)

\displaystyle\rot E

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\rot H

\displaystyle=0,

(3b)

\displaystyle\dive E

\displaystyle=\rho,

\displaystyle\dive H

\displaystyle=0,

(3c)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nu_{\Gamma_{\!0}}H

\displaystyle=f,

(3d)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E

\displaystyle=-kg,

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}^{\times}H

\displaystyle=g,

where
g
is any suitable tangential vector field in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\!1})
.
For simplicity we put
g=0
, then these two systems are
solvable by
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
.
However, the same result also gives conditions for which
g\neq 0
this system is solvable.
The dynamic system is

(4a)

\displaystyle\p_{t}E

\displaystyle=\rot H,

(4b)

\displaystyle\p_{t}H

\displaystyle=-\rot E,

(4c)

\displaystyle\dive E

\displaystyle=0,

(4d)

\displaystyle\dive H

\displaystyle=0,

(4e)

\displaystyle\nu_{\Gamma_{\!0}}H

\displaystyle=0,

(4f)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E

\displaystyle=0,

(4g)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E+k\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}^{\times}H

\displaystyle=0.

The initial conditions for the dynamic system are
E(0)=E_{0}-E_{\mathrm{stat}}
and
H(0)=H_{0}-H_{\mathrm{stat}}
, where
E_{\mathrm{stat}}
and
H_{\mathrm{stat}}
are the solutions of the two static systems (
3
).
We can write (
4a
) and (
4b
) as

\displaystyle\p_{t}\begin{bmatrix}E\\
H\end{bmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0&\rot\\
-\rot&0\end{bmatrix}}_{\phantom{\A_{0}}\eqqcolon\A_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}E\\
H\end{bmatrix},

and the boundary conditions (
4f
)
and (
4g
)
shall be covered by the domain of
\A_{0}
:

\dom\A_{0}\coloneqq\left\{(E,H)\in\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma}(\rot,\Omega)\times\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\rot,\Omega)\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma_{\!0}}E=0,\;\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}E+k\tau_{\Gamma_{\!1}}^{\times}H=0\right\}.

Here, we did ignore the equations
\dive E=0
,
\dive H=0
and
\nu_{\Gamma_{\!0}}H=0
.
However,
\A_{0}
is a generator of a
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup by
[
22
, Example 8.10]

or
[
25
, Section 5]
,
where the input function is
u=0
.
(In these sources they regard boundary control systems and system nodes, respectively.
One condition of those concepts is that the system with
u=0

is described by a generator of a
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup).
The next lemma provides a tool to respect the remaining
conditions of (
4
) as well.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\T(\cdot)
be a
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
,
and let
\A
be its generator.
Then every subspace
V\supseteq\ran\A
is invariant under
\T(\cdot)
.
Moreover,
\A\big|_{V}
generates the strongly continuous semigroup

\T_{V}(\cdot)\coloneqq\T(\cdot)\big|_{V}
,
if
V
is additionally closed in
X
.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
and let
x\in V
.
Then
\ran\A\ni\A\int_{0}^{t}\T(s)x\,\mathrm{d}s=\T(t)x-x

and hence
\T(t)x\in V
.
The remaining assertion follows from
[
6
, Chapter II, Section 2.3]
.
∎

Therefore, it is left to show that the remaining conditions
establish a closed and invariant subspace under the semigroup
\T_{0}
generated
by
\A_{0}
or contains
\ran\A_{0}
.
Note that by
3.1

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{(E,H)\;\middle|\;\dive E=0,\,\dive H=0,\,\nu_{\Gamma_{\!0}}H=0\right\}

\displaystyle\;=\mathsf{H}_{0}(\dive,\Omega)\times\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},0}(\dive,\Omega)

\displaystyle\;=\big(\rot\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\times\rot\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\big)\oplus\big(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)\big).

This space is closed as the intersection of kernels of closed operators.
Clearly,
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)

is invariant under
\T_{0}
, since every

(E,H)\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)

is a constant in time solution of the system (
4
), i.e.,

\T_{0}(t)\begin{bmatrix}E\\
H\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}E\\
H\end{bmatrix}.

By

\rot\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\times\rot\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)=\begin{bmatrix}0&\rot\\
-\rot&0\end{bmatrix}\big(\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)\times\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\big)\supseteq\ran\A_{0}

and
5.5
we have that also

\rot\mathsf{H}(\rot,\Omega)\times\rot\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\rot,\Omega)

is invariant under
\T_{0}
.
Hence,
5.5
and
4.1
imply the next theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

\A\coloneqq\A_{0}\big|_{S}
is a generator of a
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup and

\dom\A\subseteq\big(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}(\dive,\Omega)\big)\times\big(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\rot,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\dive,\Omega)\big)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

Consequently, every resolvent operator of
\A
is compact.

If
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)=\{0\}
and

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)=\{0\}
,
then
0
is in the resolvent set of
\A
and
\A^{-1}
is compact. Alternatively,
we can further restrict
\A
to

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)^{\perp_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)^{\perp_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
.
This would also match our separation of static solutions and dynamic solutions,
since solutions with initial condition in

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma,\emptyset}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
are constant in time.

5.4.
Wave Equation with Mixed Impedance Type Boundary Conditions

For the scalar wave equation the situation is even simpler
since traces of
\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
-functions already belong to
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
,
even to
\mathsf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.
In
I\times\Omega
we consider the wave equation in first order form
(linear acoustics) with mixed scalar and impedance boundary conditions

\displaystyle\p_{t}w-\dive v

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\p_{t}v-\grad w

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!0}}w

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}w+k\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}v

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle w(0)

\displaystyle=w_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=v_{0}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

We write the system as

\p_{t}\begin{bmatrix}w\\
v\end{bmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0&\dive\\
\grad&0\end{bmatrix}}_{\phantom{\A_{0}}\eqqcolon\A_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}w\\
v\end{bmatrix}

with

\dom\A_{0}\coloneqq\big\{(w,v)\in\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)\times\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\;\big|\;\sigma_{\Gamma_{\!1}}w+k\nu_{\Gamma_{\!1}}=0\big\}.

As before, by
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
or
[
22
, Example 8.9]
,

\A_{0}
is a generator of
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup.
Again, we want to separate the static solutions from the dynamic system. The static solutions are given by
\ker\A_{0}
, which can be characterise by

\ker\A_{0}=\{0\}\times\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega),

where we assumed
\Gamma_{\!0}\neq\emptyset
, otherwise the first component can also be constant and the second component would be in
\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1},0}(\dive,\Omega)
.
By
3.2
, the orthogonal complement of
\ker\A_{0}
is

S\coloneqq\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\grad\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega).

Note that
S
contains
\ran\A_{0}
and is therefore (by
5.5
) an invariant subspace under the semigroup generated by
\A_{0}
. Moreover, note that
\grad\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},0}(\rot,\Omega)
and that
S
is closed.
Hence,
5.5
and
4.1
imply the next theorem.

Theorem 5.7
.

\A\coloneqq\A_{0}\big|_{S}
is a generator of
\mathsf{C}_{0}
-semigroup and

\dom\A\subseteq\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)\times\big(\widehat{\mathsf{H}}_{\Gamma_{\!1}}(\dive,\Omega)\cap\mathsf{H}_{\Gamma_{\!0},0}(\rot,\Omega)\big)\overset{\mathsf{cpt}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega).

Consequently, every resolvent operator of
\A
is compact.

Alternatively, we can also regard the classical formulation of the wave equation
and see that it is necessary for the second component in our formulation to be
in
\grad\mathsf{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{\!0}}(\Omega)
, if we want the solutions to correspond.

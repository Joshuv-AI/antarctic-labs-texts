Euler system with a polytropic equation of state as a vanishing viscosity limit
Eduard Feireisl, Christian Klingenberg, Simon Markfelder

Abstract
We consider the Euler system of gas dynamics endowed with the incomplete ( e − ϱ − p ) (e-\varrho-p) equation of state relating the internal energy e e to the mass density ϱ \varrho and the pressure p p . We show that any sufficiently smooth solution can be recovered as a vanishing viscosity – heat conductivity limit of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with a properly defined temperature. The result is unconditional in the case of the Navier type (slip) boundary conditions and extends to the no-slip condition for the velocity under some extra hypotheses of Kato’s type concerning the behavior of the fluid in the boundary layer.

Introduction

The
Euler system
describing the evolution of the density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the velocity
{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
, and the internal energy
e=e(t,x)
of a compressible inviscid fluid reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e\right]+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e\right]+p\right){\bf u}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(1.1)

The fluid is confined to a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{3}
, with
impermeable boundary
,

{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.2)

The system (
1.1
) rewritten in terms of the phase variables
(\varrho,{\bf u},e)
is symmetric hyperbolic, see e.g. Benzoni-Gavage and Serre
[
4
, Chapter 13, Section 13.2.2]
.
The problem is formally closed by prescribing a suitable
equation of state
(EOS). We consider a polytropic EOS

p=(\gamma-1)\varrho e\ \mbox{with the adiabatic exponent}\ \gamma>1.

(1.3)

The equation of state (
1.3
) is
incomplete
, in particular, the (absolute) temperature
\vartheta
is not uniquely determined. Indeed Gibbs’ law asserts

\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right),

(1.4)

where
s
is a new thermodynamic variable called
entropy
. Here
D=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\varrho},\frac{\partial}{\partial\vartheta}\right)
. Plugging (
1.3
) in (
1.4
) we obtain a first order system that can be integrated yielding

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}\right),

(1.5)

and, in accordance with (
1.3
), (
1.4
),

\displaystyle e(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta}{\gamma-1}\frac{\vartheta^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}\right),

\displaystyle s(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=S\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}\right),\ S^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\frac{\gamma P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z^{2}},

(1.6)

for an arbitrary function
P
. Thus the absolute temperature
\vartheta
is determined by
\varrho
and
e
modulo the function
P
, see Cowperthwaite
[
6
]
, Müller and Ruggeri
[
17
]
, or
[
11
, Chapters 2,3]
.

The
Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
describing the motion of a real viscous and heat conductive gas can be viewed as a viscous regularization of (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho s)+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho s{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right),\ \mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\equiv\frac{\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}}{2},

(1.7)

with the viscous stress
\mathbb{S}
given by
Newton’s rheological law

\mathbb{S}=2\widetilde{\mu}\left(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{1}{d}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\widetilde{\eta}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},

(1.8)

and the heat flux given by
Fourier’s law

{\bf q}=-\widetilde{\kappa}\nabla_{x}\vartheta.

(1.9)

The Second law of thermodynamics requires the entropy production rate

\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)

to be non–negative; whence the diffusion
transport coefficients

\widetilde{\mu}
,
\widetilde{\eta}
, and
\widetilde{\kappa}
must be non–negative.
Note that, unlike in the Euler system (
1.1
), the knowledge of the temperature
\vartheta
is necessary to determine the entropy as well as the heat flux in (
1.7
). The internal energy
e
can be evaluated in terms of
\varrho
,
\vartheta

through (
1.6
). Thus solutions of the associated Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.7
), that may be seen as a viscous regularization of the Euler system (
1.1
), depend on the choice of
P
in (
1.5
).

We consider the vanishing dissipation limit of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system, specifically, we rescale

\mathbb{S}_{n}\approx\mu_{n}\mathbb{S},\ {\bf q}_{n}\approx\kappa_{n}{\bf q},\ \mu_{n}\searrow 0,\ \kappa_{n}\searrow 0.

(1.10)

Moreover,
the existing mathematical theory of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (see
[
11
]
) is based on the augmentation of the pressure, and, accordingly, the internal energy and entropy, by the radiation component

p_{R}=\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},\ e_{R}=\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4},\ s_{R}=\frac{4a}{3\varrho}\vartheta^{3},\ a>0.

(1.11)

The parameter
a
is very small and usually neglected in the real world applications. Consistently with (
1.10
), we therefore consider

a=a_{n},\ a_{n}\searrow 0.

(1.12)

Suppose that
\gamma>1
is given and that the Euler system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) admits a smooth (
C^{1}
) solution on a time interval
[0,T]
. Our goal is to identify the function
P
in (
1.5
) in such a way that any sequence of
weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
1.7
)–(
1.9
) converges in the vanishing viscosity/radiation limit (
1.10
)–(
1.12
) to the solution of the Euler system in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.
Moreover, we show that
the convergence
is unconditional, if the boundary layer is eliminated by the choice of the complete slip boundary conditions

{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ (\mathbb{S}\cdot{\bf n})\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.13)

where
{\bf n}
denotes the outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. In the case of the no–slip boundary conditions

{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.14)

the convergence is conditioned by extra hypotheses of Kato’s type
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
identified in the compressible setting by Sueur
[
21
]
and Wang, Zhu
[
22
]
.

In comparison with the existing literature, notably
[
22
]
, our result covers all admissible values of the adiabatic coefficient
\gamma
in (
1.3
) as well as general dependence of the transport coefficients
on the temperature in the spirit of the existence theory developed in
[
11
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the necessary preliminary material concerning the weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system including the relative energy inequality that represents a crucial tool in the analysis. Section
3
contains the main results. In Section
4
we show consistency of the vanishing viscosity approximation. Specifically, the viscous stress, the heat flux as well as the radiation components of the
pressure, internal energy, and entropy along with the associated fluxes disappear in the regime specified in (
1.10
), (
1.12
). This process is “path dependent”, specifically certain relations concerning the asymptotic behaviour of
(\mu_{n},\kappa_{n},a_{n})
must be imposed in the spirit of
[
8
]
. The convergence towards the strong solution of the Euler system is shown in Section
5
.

2
Preliminary material

We recall the existing theory of weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system.

2.1
Mathematical theory of the closed system

By imposing either the complete slip (
1.13
) or the no slip boundary condition (
1.14
) we suppose the fluid is mechanically insulated. In view of our final objective, we require the fluid to be energetically
isolated, specifically

{\bf q}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(2.1)

The mathematical theory for closed systems relevant for future analysis was developed in
[
11
]
. Note that the extension to
open
systems is also available in the recent works
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
, see also the forthcoming monograph
[
9
]
.

A suitable
weak formulation
of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system augmented by the radiative terms proposed in
[
11
]
reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}(p+p_{R})

\displaystyle=\mu{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho(s+s_{R}))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho(s+s_{R}){\bf u})+\kappa\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mu\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\kappa\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho(e+e_{R})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.2)

see
[
11
, Chapter 3]
. Note that we anticipate the influence of thermal radiation represented by the extra terms

p_{R}
,
e_{R}
, and
s_{R}
in (
2.2
). In accordance with (
1.12
), these terms will vanish in the asymptotic limit. The energy balance appearing in the Euler system in (
1.1
) is replaced by
the entropy inequality supplemented with the total energy balance in (
2.2
).

2.2
Transport coefficients

In accordance with the molecular theory of gases (see e.g. Becker
[
1
]
), the transport coefficients depend on the temperature. Specifically, we assume that
\widetilde{\mu}
,
\widetilde{\eta}
,
\widetilde{\kappa}
are continuously differentiable functions of
\vartheta
satisfying

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mu}(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right),\ \alpha\geq 0,

\displaystyle\sup_{\vartheta\in[0,\infty)}|\widetilde{\mu}^{\prime}(\vartheta)|

\displaystyle<\infty,\

\displaystyle 0\leq\widetilde{\eta}(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}\left(1+\vartheta^{\alpha}\right),\ 0<\underline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}\left(1+\vartheta^{3}\right)

(2.3)

for any
\vartheta\geq 0
. Note that the cubic growth of
\kappa
is motivated by the presence of the radiation terms, see Oxenius
[
18
]
.

2.3
Equation of state

A proper choice of the equation of state for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system plays of course a crucial role in the present paper. Given
\gamma>1
, we have to identify the function
P
in (
1.5
). For
p=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
,

e=e(\varrho,\vartheta)
, we recall the
hypothesis of thermodynamic stability

\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\varrho}>0,\ \frac{\partial e(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}>0.

(2.4)

This imposes the following restrictions on
P
:

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(Z)

\displaystyle>0\ \mbox{for all}\ Z>0,

\displaystyle\gamma P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z

\displaystyle>0\ \mbox{for all}\ Z>0.

(2.5)

The following lemma shows existence of a suitable
P
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\underline{Z}>0
there exist functions
P,S\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
with properties (
1.6
), (
2.5
) and such that

P(Z)=Z\quad\text{ for all }Z\in[0,\underline{Z}].

(2.6)

Moreover
P,S
satisfy

\displaystyle P(0)

\displaystyle=0;

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}

\displaystyle\leq C\quad\text{ for all }Z>0;

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle>0;

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{Z\to\infty}S(Z)

\displaystyle=0.

(2.10)

Note that according to (
2.10
),
S
from lemma
2.1
is in accordance with the Third law of thermodynamics, namely

s(\varrho,\vartheta)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \vartheta\to 0+\ \mbox{for any fixed}\ \varrho>0,

cf. Belgiorno
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
\underline{Z}=1
. Set
P,S\in C^{1}[0,\infty)

P(Z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Z&\text{ if }Z\leq 1,\\
\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}Z^{\gamma}&\text{ if }Z>1,\end{array}\right.

and

S(Z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\log(Z)+1&\text{ if }Z\leq 1,\\
\frac{1}{Z}&\text{ if }Z>1,\end{array}\right.

It is then straightforward to check (
1.6
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
)–(
2.10
).

Let us now look at
\underline{Z}\neq 1
. We define the
P,S
constructed above as
P_{1},S_{1}
and set

P(Z):=P_{1}\left(\frac{Z}{\underline{Z}}\right),\qquad S(Z):=\frac{1}{\underline{Z}}S_{1}\left(\frac{Z}{\underline{Z}}\right).

Again straightforward computations show that the properties (
1.6
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
)–(
2.10
) follow from the corresponding property of
P_{1},S_{1}
.
∎

Note that for
Z\in[0,\underline{Z}]
, according to (
2.6
) and (
1.5
) we simply obtain the Boyle-Mariotte law

Hence the temperature for the Euler system (
1.1
) endowed with the incomplete EOS (
1.3
) can be recovered by choosing
\underline{Z}
in lemma
2.1
appropriately, see section
4.1
for details.

2.4
Relative energy

The
relative energy
for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system may be seen as a counterpart of Dafermos’ relative entropy for the (hyperbolic) Euler system, see
[
7
]
. Given a trio of “test functions”

r>0,\ \Theta>0,\ {\bf U},

(2.11)

the relative energy reads

\displaystyle E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf U}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}+H_{\Theta}(\varrho,\vartheta)-\frac{\partial H_{\Theta}(r,\Theta)}{\partial\varrho}(\varrho-r)-H_{\Theta}(r,\Theta)

(2.12)

where

H_{\Theta}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho(e(\varrho,\vartheta)-\Theta s(\varrho,\vartheta))

is the ballistic free energy. In the context of the system (
2.2
) perturbed by the radiation terms, we have

H_{\Theta}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho((e+e_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)-\Theta(s+s_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)).

The relative energy augmented by the radiation component will be denoted
E_{a}
.
We also introduce the standard energy

E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta).

The following result was proved in
[
10
]
:
Suppose that:

•

(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is a weak solution to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
2.2
) in
(0,T)\times\Omega
with the no–flux boundary conditions (
2.1
) and either the complete slip boundary conditions
(
1.13
) or the no–slip boundary condition (
1.14
).

•

(r,\Theta,{\bf U})
is a trio of continuously differentiable test functions,

r>0,\ \Theta>0\ \mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\overline{\Omega},

where
{\bf U}
satisfies either the impermeability boundary condition

{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

or the no–slip boundary condition

{\bf U}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Then the
relative energy inequality

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{\Omega}E_{a}\Big(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\big|r,\Theta,{\bf U}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\Big]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Theta}{\vartheta}\left(\mu\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\kappa\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\big((s+s_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)-(s+s_{R})(r,\Theta)\big)({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\Big(\partial_{t}{\bf U}+{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\Big)\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p+p_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\big((s+s_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)-(s+s_{R})(r,\Theta)\big)\Big(\partial_{t}\Theta+{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Theta\Big)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(1-\frac{\varrho}{r}\right)\partial_{t}(p+p_{R})(r,\Theta)-\frac{\varrho}{r}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}(p+p_{R})(r,\Theta)\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.13)

holds for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Finally, we recall the fundamental properties of the relative energy that follow from the hypothesis of thermodynamic stability (
2.4
). In accordance with hypothesis (
2.11
), fix

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\varrho}

\displaystyle<\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}r\leq\sup_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}r<\overline{\varrho},

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\vartheta}

\displaystyle<\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\Theta\leq\sup_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\Theta<\overline{\vartheta},

and define

[F]_{\rm ess}=\Phi(\varrho,\vartheta)F,\ [F]_{\rm res}=F-[F]_{\rm ess},

where

\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}(0,\infty)^{2},\ 0\leq\Phi\leq 1,\ \Phi(\varrho,\vartheta)=1\ \mbox{whenever}\ \underline{\varrho}\leq\varrho\leq\overline{\varrho}\ \mbox{and}\ \underline{\vartheta}\leq\vartheta\leq\overline{\vartheta}.

Then

\displaystyle E_{a}\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf u}\right)\geq E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left([\varrho-r]_{\rm ess}^{2}+[\vartheta-\Theta]_{\rm ess}^{2}+[{\bf u}-{\bf U}]^{2}_{\rm ess}\right)

\displaystyle E_{a}\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(1_{\rm res}+[\varrho(e+e_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)]_{\rm res}+[\varrho|(s+s_{R})(\varrho,\vartheta)|]_{\rm res}\right),

\displaystyle E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|r,\Theta,{\bf u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\left(1_{\rm res}+[\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)]_{\rm res}+[\varrho|s(\varrho,\vartheta)|]_{\rm res}\right),

(2.14)

where the constants depend on
\underline{\varrho}
,
\overline{\varrho}
,
\underline{\vartheta}
, and
\overline{\vartheta}
, see e.g.
[
11
]
for details. As a consequence of the hypothesis of thermodynamic stability (
2.4
), the relative energy expressed in terms of the
conservative entropy variables
(\varrho,{\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u},\mathcal{S}=\varrho s)
is a strictly convex function and represents to so-called Bregman distance between

(\varrho,{\bf m},\mathcal{S})
and
(r,r{\bf U},rs(r,\Theta))
, see e.g.
[
12
]
.
Note carefully that the relative energy

associated to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (

3.1
Unconditional convergence in the absence of boundary layer

(Unconditional convergence)
.

Let

1.1

\varrho_{E},\ e_{E}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}),\ {\bf u}_{E}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega;R^{3})

\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\varrho_{E}>0,\ \inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}e_{E}>0.

Then there exists a (

\varrho

and entropy

Let
(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (

), and the no–flux boundary conditions

), in the vanishing dissipation/radiation regime:

\mu_{n}\searrow 0,\ a_{n}\approx\mu_{n}^{\frac{4}{1+\alpha}},\ \frac{\kappa_{n}}{a_{n}^{\frac{3}{4}}}\to 0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\underline{\varrho}<\inf_{(0,T)\times\Omega}\varrho_{n,0}\leq\sup_{(0,T)\times\Omega}\varrho_{n,0}<\overline{\varrho}\ \mbox{uniformly in}\ n,\ \varrho_{n,0}\to\varrho_{E}(0,\cdot)\ \mbox{in}\ L^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle 0

(3.3)

4
Consistency of the vanishing dissipation/radiation approximation

As a preliminary step, we show
consistency
of the vanishing dissipation/radiation approximation.

4.1
Temperature for the Euler system

First we introduce the temperature
\vartheta_{E}
associated to the limit system. Without loss of generality, we may fix the constants
\underline{\varrho}
,
\overline{\varrho}
in (
3.2
) so that

0<\underline{\varrho}<\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\varrho_{E}\leq\sup_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\varrho_{E}<\overline{\varrho}.

(4.1)

Next, in accordance with the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
,

0<\underline{e}<\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}e_{E}\leq\sup_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}e_{E}<\overline{e}

(4.2)

for certain constants
\underline{e}
,
\overline{e}
. Let us set

\underline{Z}>\frac{\overline{\varrho}}{\left((\gamma-1)\underline{e}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}

and apply lemma
2.1
to obtain suitable functions
P,S
. Furthermore we define

\vartheta_{E}:=(\gamma-1)e_{E}.

Note that
\vartheta_{E}>(\gamma-1)\underline{e}
and hence

\frac{\varrho_{E}}{(\vartheta_{E})^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}<\underline{Z}.

By virtue of (
2.6
), we have

e(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})=e_{E}\ \mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}.

Moreover, without loss of generality, we may suppose

0<\underline{\vartheta}<\inf_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\vartheta_{E}\leq\sup_{[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}}\vartheta_{E}<\overline{\vartheta},

(4.3)

with the same constants
\underline{\vartheta}
,
\overline{\vartheta}
as in (
3.2
). From this moment on, the pressure law is fixed.

As
p
,
e
, and
s
comply with Gibbs’ relation, the smooth solution of the Euler system conserves the entropy:

\partial_{t}(\varrho_{E}s(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E}))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{E}s(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E}){\bf u}_{E})=0,

(4.4)

where
s
is given by (
1.6
).

4.2
Consistency

The Navier–Stokes–Fourier system (
2.2
) may be viewed a singular perturbation of the Euler system with the extra “error” terms

\displaystyle E^{1}_{n}

\displaystyle=p_{R}=\frac{a_{n}}{3}\vartheta^{4}_{n},

\displaystyle E^{2}_{n}

\displaystyle=\mu_{n}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n})=\mu_{n}\left(\widetilde{\mu}(\vartheta_{n})\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right)+\widetilde{\eta}(\vartheta_{n}){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right),

\displaystyle E^{3}_{n}

\displaystyle=\varrho s_{R}=\frac{4a_{n}}{3}\vartheta_{n}^{3},\ E^{4}_{n}=\varrho s_{R}{\bf u}=\frac{4a_{n}}{3}\vartheta_{n}^{3}{\bf u}_{n},\ E^{5}_{n}\equiv\kappa_{n}\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}=\kappa_{n}\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{n})\frac{\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n}}{\vartheta_{n}},\ E^{6}_{n}=\varrho e_{R}=a_{n}\vartheta_{n}^{4}.

(4.5)

We say that the approximation of the Euler system by the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system is consistent, if the above “error” terms vanish in the asymptotic limit
n\to 0
.
As a matter of fact, we need a milder form of consistency compatible with the relative energy inequality. More specifically, it is sufficient to control the “errors” by the dissipation term

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\mu_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}\left|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\mu_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{\eta}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\kappa_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}^{2}}|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

and the total energy

\mathcal{E}_{n}\equiv\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|^{2}+\varrho_{n}e(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})+a_{n}\vartheta^{4}_{n}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

For each error term
E^{i}_{n}
,
i=1,\dots,6
specified in (
4.5
) and
\varepsilon>0
, we have to find
c(\varepsilon)
such that

\|E^{i}_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\omega_{n}\ \mbox{uniformly for}\ n\to\infty,\ \omega_{n}\to 0.

(4.6)

Obviously,
E^{1}_{n}=p_{R}
,
E^{6}_{n}=\varrho e_{R}
, and
E^{3}_{n}=\varrho s_{R}
satisfy (
4.6
) (with
\varepsilon=0
), it remains to handle the viscous stress, the heat flux and the entropy convective flux term.

Moreover, we recall some basic estimates that follow directly from the hypotheses (
1.6
), (
2.5
), (
2.7
)–(
2.10
):

\displaystyle\varrho^{\gamma}+\varrho\vartheta

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta),

\displaystyle 0\leq\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\varrho\left(1+|\log(\varrho)|+[\log(\vartheta)]^{+}\right).

(4.7)

4.2.1
Viscous stress consistency

By virtue of hypothesis (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\widetilde{\mu}(\vartheta_{n})\left|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right|\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mu_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{\mu}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}\left|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c(\varepsilon)\mu_{n}\int_{\Omega}\left(1+\vartheta_{n}^{1+\alpha}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\mu_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\mu_{n}\int_{\Omega}\left[\vartheta_{n}^{1+\alpha}\right]_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\mu_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\frac{\mu_{n}}{a_{n}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{4}}}\int_{\Omega}\left[a_{n}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{4}}\vartheta_{n}^{1+\alpha}\right]_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\mu_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}\left[a_{n}\vartheta_{n}^{4}+1\right]_{\rm res}\ \,{\rm d}{x}

where the last inequality follows from hypothesis (
3.1
) and the simple fact that
x^{1+\alpha}\leq c(x^{4}+1)
. Thus we obtain the desired estimate (
4.6
). The bulk viscosity term can be handled in a similar fashion.

4.2.2
Heat flux consistency

Similarly to the preceding part,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n}|\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\varepsilon\kappa_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}^{2}}|\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c(\varepsilon)\kappa_{n}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{n})\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\kappa_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\kappa_{n}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{n}^{3}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\kappa_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\frac{\kappa_{n}}{a_{n}^{\frac{3}{4}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}a_{n}\vartheta_{n}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\kappa_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\frac{\kappa_{n}}{a_{n}^{\frac{3}{4}}}+c(\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}_{n};

(4.8)

whence (
4.6
) follows from hypothesis (
3.1
).

4.2.3
Radiation entropy convective flux consistency

To close the circle of consistence estimates, we have to handle the integral

a_{n}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{3}_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq a_{n}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;R^{3})}

that corresponds to the radiation entropy convective flux.

By virtue of Sobolev embedding theorem,

\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{W^{1,\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \mbox{as long as}\ \alpha\geq\frac{1}{3},

and, by a generalized Korn-Poincaré inequality
[
11
, Theorem 11.23]
,

\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{W^{1,\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left(\left\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right\|_{L^{\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega;R^{9})}+\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right).

Another application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right\|_{L^{\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega;R^{9})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega)}\left\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{9})}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{3}_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq a_{n}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}a_{n}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+a_{n}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega)}\left\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}_{n}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{9})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}+c(\varepsilon)\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{\mu_{n}}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega)}^{2}+ca_{n}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(4.9)

Finally, by virtue of hypothesis (
3.1
)

\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{\mu_{n}}\|\vartheta_{n}^{3}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega)}^{2}=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{\mu_{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\vartheta_{n}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{7-\alpha}{4}}=\frac{a_{n}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{4}}}{\mu_{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega}a_{n}\vartheta_{n}^{4}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{7-\alpha}{4}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\frac{7-\alpha}{4}}.

The rightmost integral in (
4.9
) can be handled in a similar fashion. Since
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{3},1]
, we have in particular
0\leq\alpha\leq 3
and the desired conclusion (
4.6
) follows from boundedness of the total energy.

5
Convergence

The proof of convergence consists in plugging the strong solution
(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E})
of the Euler system as the test functions
r=\varrho_{E}
,
\Theta=\vartheta_{E}
,
{\bf U}={\bf u}_{E}
in the relative energy inequality (
2.13
). This can be done in a direct manner in the case of the complete slip boundary conditions (
1.13
) , whereas the velocity
{\bf u}_{E}
must be modified to comply with
the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions in the case of no–slip (
1.14
). We focus on the latter case as the proof in the case of the complete slip boundary conditions can be performed in a way similar to
[
8
]
.

5.1
Velocity regularization

If the solutions of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system satisfy the no–slip boundary conditions, the velocity
{\bf u}_{E}
is not eligible for the relative energy inequality (
2.13
) as its tangential component may not vanish on
\partial\Omega
. Instead we consider

{\bf U}={\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta},

(5.1)

where the perturbation
{\bf v}_{\delta}
is given as

{\bf v}_{\delta}(t,x)=\xi\left(\frac{{\rm dist}[x,\partial\Omega]}{\delta}\right){\bf u}_{E}(t,\Pi(x)),\ \delta>0,

(5.2)

where

\xi\in C^{\infty}(R),\ \xi^{\prime}\leq 0,\ \xi(d)=1\ \mbox{if}\ d\leq 0,\ \xi(d)=0\ \mbox{if}\ \xi\geq 1,

and

\Pi(x)\in\partial\Omega\ \mbox{is the nearest point to}\ x\ \mbox{in}\ \partial\Omega.

If
\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{k}
,
k\geq 2
, then

for any

, and

5.2
Application of the relative energy inequality

As
{\bf U}={\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}
vanishes on
\partial\Omega
, the trio
(r=\varrho_{E},{\bf U}={\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta},\Theta=\vartheta_{E})
can be used as test functions in the relative energy inequality
(
2.13
). Recall that at this stage we have the following vanishing parameters:
\mu_{n}
,
\kappa_{n}
,
a_{n}
, and
\delta=\delta_{n}
.

We have

\left|E\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}\right)-E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\right)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n}-{\bf u}_{E}){\bf v}_{\delta}\right|+\varrho_{n}|{\bf v}_{\delta}|^{2}

(5.3)

{\rm ess}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|\varrho_{n}\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}+{\rm ess}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|\varrho_{n}{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

we may infer that

\int_{\Omega}\left|E\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}\right)-E\left(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\right)\right|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \delta\to 0.

(5.5)

The first rather straightforward observation
is that, under hypothesis (
3.2
) concerning the initial data,

\int_{\Omega}{E}_{a_{n}}\left(\varrho_{0,n},\vartheta_{0,n},{\bf u}_{0,n}\Big|\varrho_{E}(0,\cdot),\vartheta_{E}(0,\cdot),{\bf u}_{E}(0,\cdot)-{\bf v}_{\delta}(0,\cdot)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0\ \mbox{for}\ n\to\infty,\ \delta\to 0.

Consequently, we can write (
2.13
) in the form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E_{a_{n}}\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+{\underline{\vartheta}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\vartheta_{n}}\left(\mu_{n}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}-\kappa_{n}\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n}}{\vartheta_{n}}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}):\nabla_{x}({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\kappa_{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{E}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}\big((s+s_{R})(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})-(s+s_{R})(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})\big)(({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})-{\bf u}_{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{E}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}\Big(\partial_{t}({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})+({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\cdot\nabla_{x}({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\Big)\cdot(({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})-{\bf u}_{n})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p+p_{R})(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n}){\rm div}_{x}({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}\big((s+s_{R})(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})-(s+s_{R})(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})\big)\Big(\partial_{t}\vartheta_{E}+({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{E}\Big)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(5.6)

holds for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
, where

Our goal is to show

\int_{\Omega}E_{a_{n}}\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=h(n,\delta)\ \mbox{uniformly for a.a.}\ \tau\in(0,T),

by means of a Gronwall type argument.

5.4
Integrals independent of the boundary layer

Now, we estimate the integrals on the right–hand side of (

) that are independent of
{\bf v}_{\delta}
. First, by virtue of (
4.7

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}[s(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})+1]_{\rm res}|{\bf u}_{n}||\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{E}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right|

Next, we recall two identities that follow from the specific form of EOS (

\displaystyle=-(\gamma-1)\vartheta_{E}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{E},

\displaystyle=-\gamma p(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E}){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{E}.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\Big(p(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})-p(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{E}\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}\big(s(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})-s(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})\big)\Big(\partial_{t}\vartheta_{E}+{\bf u}_{E}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{E}\Big)\,{\rm d}{x}

5.5

5.5.1
Viscous stress

Similarly to Section

Consequently, hypothesis (
3.5
) yields the desired estimate. Note that this type of estimates forces us to consider the thinckeness
\delta
of the boundary layer asymptotically large than

\mu

\frac{\mu_{n}}{\delta_{n}}\to 0.

Alternatively, following Sueur
[
21
]

3.4
), meaning

\sqrt{\mu_{n}}\|\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\mu_{n}};R^{d})}\to 0.

Setting
\mu_{n}\approx\delta_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}):\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mu_{n}}\|\mathbb{S}(\vartheta_{n},\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\mu_{n}};R^{d})}\|\sqrt{\mu_{n}}\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\mu_{n}}\||_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\mu_{n}};R^{d})}\to 0.

(5.16)

5.5.2
Convective term

Finally, we consider

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}{\bf u}_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}{\bf u}_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\delta}.

Recall that

\displaystyle{\bf w}(t,x)

\displaystyle={\bf w}_{n}(t,x)+{\bf w}_{\tau}(t,x),

\displaystyle{\bf w}_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left({\bf w}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\rm dist}[x,\partial\Omega]\right)\nabla_{x}{\rm dist}[x,\partial\Omega],\ {\bf w}_{\tau}(t,x)={\bf w}(t,x)-{\bf w}_{n}(t,x).

Similarly, for a scalar function
F
, we decompose

\nabla_{x}F=\nabla_{n}F+\nabla_{\tau}F,\ \nabla_{n}F=\left(\nabla_{x}{\rm dist}[x,\partial\Omega]\cdot\nabla_{x}F\right)\nabla_{x}{\rm dist}[x,\partial\Omega].

In accordance with the definition of
{\bf v}_{\delta}
, we get

({\bf v}_{\delta})_{n}=0,\ \|\nabla_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\ \|\nabla_{n}{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{\delta}.

(5.17)

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\varrho_{n}{\bf u}_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x},

where, by virtue of (
5.17
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{\tau}\cdot\nabla_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}||{\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})|\,{\rm d}{x}.

In view of (
5.4
),
(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,
(\varrho_{n}{\bf u}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are equi–integrable; whence

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}||{\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})|\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ \delta\to 0

uniformly in
n
.

Thus it remains to handle the integral

\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}.

By Hölder’s inequality and (
5.17
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{17+3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta})}\left\|{\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{7-3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})},

(5.18)

where
\frac{24}{7-3\alpha}
is the critical exponent in the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality

\left\|{\bf u}_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{7-3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{9})}.

(5.19)

As
{\bf u}_{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, Korn’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf u}_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{7-3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{9})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}^{t}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right\|_{L^{\frac{8}{5-\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{9})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta})}\left\|\vartheta^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}^{t}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\mathbb{I}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{9})}

Note that the constants are independent of
\delta
as
{\bf u}_{n}
can be extended to be zero outside
\Omega
.

Thus going back to (
5.18
) we deduce

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{c(\varepsilon)}{\delta}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{17+3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\frac{c(\varepsilon)}{\delta^{2}\mu_{n}}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{24}{17+3\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\left\|\vartheta_{n}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{8}{1-\alpha}}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}

in accordance with hypothesis (
3.5
).

Finally, we consider
\alpha=1
and replace the critical exponent
\frac{24}{17+3\alpha}
in (
5.18
) by the
L^{2}
norm. Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\left\|{\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\left\|{\bf u}_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\left\|{\bf u}_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{n}}{\delta}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\right).

(5.20)

Now, replacing (
5.19
) by Hardy–Sobolev inequality, we gain multiplicative factor
\delta
,

\left\|{\bf u}_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\delta\|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta};R^{9})}.

(5.21)

Thus the final inequality reads

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\cdot\nabla_{n}({\bf v}_{\delta})_{\tau}\cdot({\bf u}_{n}-({\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta}))\,{\rm d}{x}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(\varepsilon)\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{n}}{\delta}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left\|\varrho_{n}({\bf u}_{n})_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\mathcal{D}_{n}

in accordance with (
3.6
).

Alernatively, following Sueur
[
21
]
, we may suppose (
3.4
). The proof is exactly the same as in
[
21
]
.

5.6
Strong convergence

We have established the convergence

\int_{\Omega}E_{a_{n}}\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}-{\bf v}_{\delta_{n}}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty

uniformly for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
. This obviously yields

\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n}\Big|\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E},{\bf u}_{E}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

In addition, as the energy of the initial data converges and both Euler and the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system conserve energy, we get

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{n}|{\bf u}_{n}|^{2}+\varrho_{n}e(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{E}|{\bf u}_{E}|^{2}+\varrho_{E}e(\varrho_{E},\vartheta_{E})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{1}(0,T).

This yields the desired strong convergence claimed in (
3.3
).

Abstract
. Let K / F K/F be a finite Galois extension of fields with Gal ​ ( K / F ) = Γ \textup{Gal}(K/F)=\Gamma . In an earlier work of Timothy Kohl, the author enumerated dihedral Hopf-Galois structures acting on dihedral extensions. Dihedral group is one particular example of semidirect product of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} . In this article we count the number of Hopf-Galois structures with Galois group Γ \Gamma of type G G , where Γ , G \Gamma,G are groups of the form ℤ N ⋊ ϕ ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{N}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}_{2} when N N is odd with radical of N N being a Burnside number. As an application we also find the corresponding number of skew braces.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hopf-Galois structures

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative ring with unity and let
\mathcal{H}

be an
\mathcal{R}
-bialgebra. Then
\mathcal{H}
will be called an
\mathcal{R}
-Hopf algebra
if there is an

\mathcal{R}
-module homomoprhism
\lambda:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
(the antipode map), which is
both an
\mathcal{R}
-algebra and an
\mathcal{R}
-coalgebra antihomomophism such that:

\displaystyle\lambda(h\otimes h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda(h)\otimes\lambda(h^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Delta\lambda(h)

\displaystyle=(\lambda\otimes\lambda)\tau\Delta,

where
\Delta
is the comultiplication map and
\tau
is the switch map
\tau(h_{1}\otimes h_{2})=h_{2}\otimes h_{1}
. Now assume that
\mathcal{H}
is commutative.
An
\mathcal{R}
-Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
is called a
finite algebra
if it is finitely generated and a projective
\mathcal{R}
-module. Now if
\mathcal{S}
is an
\mathcal{R}
-algebra which is an
\mathcal{H}
-module, then
\mathcal{S}
is called an
\mathcal{H}
-module algebra
if

\displaystyle h(st)=\sum h_{(1)}(s)h_{(2)}(t)\text{ and }h(1)=\epsilon(h)1

for all
h\in\mathcal{H},s,t\in\mathcal{S}
, where
\Delta(h)=\sum\limits_{(h)}h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)}\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
according to Sweedler’s (
[
Sw69
]
)
notation and
\epsilon:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
is the co-unit map.

Then
\mathcal{S}
, a finite commutative
\mathcal{R}
-algebra
is called an
\mathcal{H}
-Galois extension
over
\mathcal{R}
if
\mathcal{S}
is a left
\mathcal{H}
-module algebra
and the
\mathcal{R}
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle j:\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\textup{End}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S}),

given by
j(s\otimes h)(s^{\prime})=sh(s^{\prime})
for
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},h\in\mathcal{H}
, is an isomorphism.
Now we define a Hopf-Galois structure on a separable field extension. Assume
K/F
is a finite separable field extension. An
F
-Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
, with an action on
K
such that
K
is an
H
-module algebra
and the action makes
K
into an
\mathcal{H}
-Galois extension, will be called a
Hopf-Galois structure

on
K/F
.

1.2.
Greither-Pareigis theory
[
GP87
]
and Boytt’s translation
[
B96
]

Given a group

we define

of

as a semidirect product
G\rtimes_{\psi}\textup{Aut}(G)
, where
\psi
is the identity map. Holomorph of a group

(denoted by

(set of permutations on

) as follows

\textup{Hol}(G)=\{\eta\in\textup{Perm}(G):\eta\text{ normalizes }\lambda(G)\}

where
\lambda
is the left regular representation. Now we state some results which will help us count the number of Hopf-Galois structures on a given field extension. The following result is due to
[
GP87
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
C00
, Theorem 6.8]

Let
K/F
be a separable extension of fields. Let
L
be its normal closure,
\Gamma=\textup{Gal}(L/F),\Gamma^{\prime}=\textup{Gal}(L/K)
. Define the set
X=\Gamma/{\Gamma^{\prime}}
. Then there is a bijection between Hopf-Galois structure on
K/F
and regular subgroups
G
of
\textup{Perm}(X)
normalized by
\lambda(\Gamma)
where
\lambda
is the left regular representation.

In the proof of the above lemma, given a regular subgroup
G\leq\textup{Perm}(X)
normalized by
\lambda(\Gamma)
, the Hopf-Galois structure on

corresponding to

is

.
Here
\Gamma
acts on

by conjugation inside
\textup{Perm}(X)
and it acts on

by field automorphism, which induces an action of
\Gamma
on
K[G]
. This

is called the

of the Hopf-Galois extension.
Although Greither-Pareigis theory simplifies the problem of counting the number of
Hopf-Galois structure for a given separable extension, the size of

is large (

) in general.
The next lemma (also known as Boytt’s translation) further simplifies the problem by considering regular embedding in

, which is comparatively smaller in size.
From the proof of

B96

Definition 4.5

A left skew brace is a triple

]
,
[
CJO14
]

Lemma 4.6

[
SV18

A.3

Let

skew braces with additive group isomorphic to
\Gamma
and classes of regular subgroups of
\textup{Hol}(\Gamma)

under conjugation by elements of
\textup{Aut}(\Gamma)
.

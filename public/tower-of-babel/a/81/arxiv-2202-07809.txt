Computing binary curves of genus five
Dušan Dragutinović

Abstract
Genus 5 curves can be hyperelliptic, trigonal, or non-hyperelliptic non-trigonal, whose model is a complete intersection of three quadrics in ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} . We present and explain algorithms we used to determine, up to isomorphism over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} , all genus 5 curves defined over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} , and we do that separately for each of the three mentioned types. We consider these curves in terms of isogeny classes over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} of their Jacobians or their Newton polygons, and for each of the three types, we compute the number of curves over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} weighted by the size of their 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} -automorphism groups.



1
Introduction

A standard result is that the smooth curves of genus
5
are either hyperelliptic, trigonal, or complete intersections of three quadric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Therefore, to understand the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{5}
of smooth curves of genus 5, we should understand the subvarieties parametrizing these three kinds of smooth curves. Denote with
\mathcal{H}_{5}
the subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{5}
parametrizing hyperelliptic curves of genus 5, with
\mathcal{T}_{5}
the subvariety parametrizing trigonal curves of genus 5, and lastly, let
\mathcal{U}_{5}
be the subvariety parametrizing curves whose canonical model in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a complete intersection of three quadric hypersurfaces.
Let us write
\mathrm{Hyp}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
for the set of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
up to isomorphism (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
),
\mathrm{Trig}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
for the set of trigonal curves of genus
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
up to isomorphism (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
), and
\mathrm{ComInt}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
for the set of curves of genus
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
up to isomorphism (over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
) that are complete intersections of three quadric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
in their canonical models.

This paper aims to give algorithms for computing all
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-isomorphism classes of smooth curves of genus
5
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, to present the obtained results, and to discuss some relevant questions, such as describing the isogeny types or Newton polygons of Jacobians of dimension
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We do that separately for
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
\mathrm{Trig}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and
\mathrm{ComInt}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Furthermore, we are interested in determining the automorphism groups over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
for curves in the preceding three sets. These computations lead us to finding the moduli counts
|\mathcal{H}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|,|\mathcal{T}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|,|\mathcal{U}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|
, and thus
|\mathcal{M}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|
. Consequently, we get a piece of information about the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{5}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{5}
.

In
[
Xar20
]
, Xarles determined all curves of genus
4
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. His approach for computing the hyperelliptic curves is a universal one. We follow it closely and apply it to genus
5
hyperelliptic curves.

The algorithm for computing the representatives for the isomorphism classes of trigonal curves is based on the explicit description of their models in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and the idea of the exhaustion of all eligible equations respecting the isomorphisms; a stack count we obtained for this case matches the one by Wennink in
[
Wen20
]
, who used a partial sieve method for plane curves for that purpose.

Lastly, for the non-hyperelliptic non-trigonal curves, a similar but more subtle idea of exhaustion of the eligible triples of quadratic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y,Z,T,U]
was used. We extensively explain the steps in our reasoning preceding the final algorithm we used for this problem and mention some intermediate steps and partial results.

We implement all the algorithms and do the computations in the mathematical software
SageMath
and we collect the implementations and obtained results for non-hyperelliptic non-trigonal curves of genus 5 over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
on

https://github.com/DusanDragutinovic/Non-hyp-non-trig-genus-5-over-F_2
.

Codes and results for hyperelliptic and trigonal genus 5 curves over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
can be found on

https://github.com/DusanDragutinovic/MT_Curves

and were made for the purpose of a master’s thesis conducted at Utrecht University.

2
Hyperelliptic curves

It is known that any hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
can be represented in a standard (affine) equation

y^{2}+q(x)y=p(x),\quad\text{for }p(x),q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x],

(1)

with
2g+1\leq\max\{2\deg(q(x)),\deg(p(x))\}\leq 2g+2
.

In
[
Xar20
]
, Xarles gave the approach to compute all (smooth) curves of genus
4
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
up to isomorphism. The given algorithm for determining the hyperelliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
can be generalized to higher genera, and here, we will use it to obtain the set
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Some of the claims made in
[
Xar20
]
we can use directly, while for the other ones, we will mention the analogs in the genus
5
case.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}=\{h(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]:\deg(h(x))\leq n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and for
A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}
, define an action of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}
by

\psi_{n}(A)(q(x))=(cx+d)^{n}q\left(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}\right);

we will also use the notation
A.q(x)
for this. Further, denote the quotient set of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}
under this action by
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{n}/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

Let
H_{1}:y^{2}+q_{1}(x)y=p_{1}(x)
and
H_{2}:y^{2}+q_{2}(x)y=p_{2}(x)
be two hyperelliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, where it holds that
2g+1\leq\max\{2\deg(q_{i}(x)),\deg(p_{i}(x))\}\leq 2g
with
q_{i}(x)
monic, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Using that any isomorphism of such
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
has to be of the form

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d},\frac{r(x)+y}{(cx+d)^{g+1}}\right)

for some
A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
r(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{g+1}
, Xarles showed the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
Xar20
]
, Lemma 1)
.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be as above and suppose
H_{1}\cong H_{2}
. Then there exists
A\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
such that
q_{2}(x)=\psi_{g+1}(A)(q_{1}(x))
.

Using the lemma, when finding all non-isomorphic hyperelliptic curves
H:y^{2}+q(x)y=p(x)
of genus
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, for a polynomial
q(x)
it is enough to consider only elements of
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}}
.

For any
q(x)\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{g+1}}
, let
\mathrm{Stab}(q(x))
be the stabilizer of
q(x)
under the
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-action. We cite two more results from
[
Xar20
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Xar20
]
, Lemma 4)
.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
given by standard equations (
1
):
y^{2}+q(x)=p_{i}(x)
,
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then there are
A\in\mathrm{Stab}(q(x))
and
r(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x],\deg(r(x))\leq g+1
such that

p_{2}(x)=\psi_{2g+1}(p_{1}(x)+r(x)^{2}+q(x)r(x)).

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Xar20
]
, Lemma 5)
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Given a nonzero polynomial
q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
and a polynomial
\allowbreak{p(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]}\allowbreak
with
2g+1\leq\max\{2\deg(q(x)),\deg(p(x))\}\leq 2g+2
, the equation
\allowbreak{y^{2}+q(x)y=p(x)}\allowbreak
defines a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
if and only if

\gcd(q(x),p^{\prime}(x)^{2}+q^{\prime}(x)^{2}p(x))=1,

and either
\deg(q(x))=g+1
or
a^{2}_{2g+1}\neq a_{2g+2}b_{g}^{2}
, where
p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{2g+2}a_{i}x^{i}
and
q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{g+1}b_{i}x^{i}
.

The idea of determining
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is as follows. Initially, we should check for a pair of
p(x),q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
with

11\leq\max\{2\deg(q(x)),\deg(p(x))\}\leq 12

whether
y^{2}+q(x)y=p(x)
defines a hyperelliptic curve of genus
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Lemma
2.1
reduces that, by considering some smaller set of possible
q(x)
’s, namely, only the set of representatives for
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}
for the
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-action, called
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. In other words,
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the set of representatives of elements in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}}
. Then, using Lemma
2.2
, for fixed
q(x)\in Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, we can reduce the list of possible polynomials
p(x)
, and finally, Lemma
2.3
helps us to decide whether such pairs
(q(x),p(x))
define hyperelliptic curves of genus
5
. Therefore, it is only left to determine
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. We do that below using the same ideas as in
[
Xar20
]
, Lemma 2.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(q(x))=\{P\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}:q(P)=0\}
, let

D_{q(x)}=\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(q(x))+(6-\deg(q(x)))\cdot\infty

be the zero divisor of
q(x)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then the action of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}
naturally translates to the (standard) action of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on
\mathrm{Div}_{6}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and these actions are compatible, i.e.,
D_{A.q(x)}=A.D_{q(x)}
.

Proof.

For an arbitrary polynomial
q(x)=e_{6}x^{6}+e_{5}x^{5}+\ldots+e_{1}x+e_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}
and a matrix
A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
we compute

q_{new}(x)=A.q(x)=e_{6}(ax+b)^{6}+e_{5}(ax+b)^{5}(cx+d)+\ldots+e_{1}(ax+b)(cx+d)^{5}+e_{0}(cx+d)^{6}.

For
P=d/c,c\neq 0
, we see that
P\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(A.q(x))
if and only if
\deg(q(x))<6
, and moreover, its multiplicity as a zero of
A.q(x)
is precisely
6-\deg(q(x))
; this means that the multiplicity of
P=d/c
in
D_{A.q(x)}
is the same as the multiplicity of
\infty
in
D_{q(x)}
. Using
A^{-1}
and changing the roles of
q(x)
and
q_{new}(x)
we can similarly get the conclusion on the degree of
q_{new}(x)
when inspecting
P=\infty
. For other
P\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, we see
P\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(q(x))
if and only if
\frac{aP+b}{cP+d}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(q(x))
and the corresponding multiplicities match. From these explicit relations, we see that the asserted claim holds.
∎

The previous lemma implies that determining
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]_{6}}
(and hence
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
) is the same as determining
\text{Div}_{6}(\mathbb{F}_{2})/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Note further that if
P
is a
K
-point, for
K/\mathbb{F}_{2}
some finite extension, and
A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, then
A.P=\frac{aP+b}{cP+d}
is again a
K
-point (
\infty
is a
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-point).

Theorem 2.5
.

The set
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
consists of the following elements

:

\deg(q(x))\leq 2
:
\quad 1,x,x^{2},x(x+1),x^{2}+x+1

\deg(q(x))=3

\quad x^{3},x^{2}(x+1),(x^{2}+x+1)x,x^{3}+x+1

\deg(q(x))=4

\deg(q(x))=6

\quad(x^{2}+x+1)^{3},(x^{3}+x+1)^{2},(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{3}+x^{2}+1),(x^{4}+x+1)(x^{2}+x+1),\allowbreak x^{6}+x+1,x^{6}+x^{3}+1

For

. As we mentioned above, in order to find

Firstly, any
D_{q(x)}
that consists only of

is equal to the unique one

with
n_{1}\leq n_{0}\leq n_{\infty}
. Since
\deg(D_{q(x)})=6
, we get that all the possible triples
(n_{\infty},n_{0},n_{1})
are

\{(6,0,0),(5,1,0),(4,2,0),(4,1,1),(3,3,0),(3,2,1),(2,2,2)\}.

Using the correspondence from Lemma
2.4
, this gives us the subset of polynomials
q(x)
in
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
,

\{1,x,x^{2},x(x+1),x^{3},x^{2}(x+1),x^{2}(x+1)^{2}\}.

If
D_{q(x)}
contains only one point of degree
2
and no other points of degree
\geq 2
in its support, similarly as above, we get that
D_{q(x)}
is equal to one of

3\zeta_{2},\text{ }2\zeta_{2}+2\infty,\text{ }2\zeta_{2}+\infty+0,\text{ }\zeta_{2}+4\infty,\text{ }\zeta_{2}+3\infty+0,\text{ }\zeta_{2}+2\infty+2\cdot 0,\text{ }\zeta_{2}+2\infty+0+1.

This induces the set of polynomials in
Q_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
(we use
\zeta_{n}
as notation for any
\zeta_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
of degree

over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
):

\{(x^{2}+x+1)^{3},(x^{2}+x+1)^{2},(x^{2}+x+1)^{2}x,x^{2}+x+1,(x^{2}+x+1)x,(x^{2}+x+1)x^{2},(x^{2}+x+1)x(x+1)\},

If
D_{q(x)}
contains a point of degree
3
, then the possibilities are the following

D_{q(x)}\in\{2\zeta_{3},\zeta_{3}+\zeta_{3}^{\prime},\zeta_{3}+\zeta_{2}+\infty,\zeta_{3}+3\infty,\zeta_{3}+2\infty+0,\zeta_{3}+\infty+0+1\},

where
\zeta_{3},\zeta_{3}^{\prime}
are of degree

. Since
x\mapsto x+1
, which is induced by the action of

, translates

to
q_{2}(x)=x^{3}+x^{2}+1
, we have that
D_{q_{1}(x)}
and
D_{q_{2}(x)}
are the same in

D_{(q_{1}(x))^{2}},D_{(q_{1}(x))(x^{2}+x+1)},D_{(q_{1}(x))x(x+1)}

D_{(q_{2}(x))^{2}},\allowbreak D_{(q_{2}(x))(x^{2}+x+1)},D_{(q_{2}(x))x(x+1)}

The previously described reasoning leads to an algorithm for computing the set
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, that is practically the same as the one for computing
\mathrm{Hyp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
from
[
Xar20
]
.

Algorithm 1. Determine
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

Step 0

From the previous theorem, we get
\mathbf{list\_of\_qs}
, the list of all possible representatives for a polynomial
q(x)
.

Step 1

For each
q(x)
in
\mathbf{list\_of\_qs}
compute the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}(q(x))\subseteq\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of
q(x)
under the action defined by
\psi_{6}(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr))(q(x))=(cx+d)^{6}q(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d})
for
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

Step 2

For fixed
q(x)
in
\mathbf{list\_of\_qs}
, check whether a polynomial
p(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
, which satisfies
\allowbreak 11\leq\max\{2\deg(q(x)),\deg(p(x))\}\leq 12\allowbreak
, is such that
C:y^{2}+q(x)y=p(x)
is a (nonsingular) curve; collect all such
p(x)
’s in the list
\mathbf{q\_list\_of\_ps}
of potential
p(x)
’s for
q(x)
. The smoothness condition can be checked using Lemma
2.3
, saying that
C
is a (nonsingular) curve of genus
5
if and only if
\gcd(q(x),p^{\prime}(x)^{2}+q^{\prime}(x)^{2}p(x))=1
and either
\deg(q(x))=6
or
a^{2}_{11}\neq a_{12}b_{5}^{2}
, where
p(x)=\sum^{12}_{i=0}a_{i}x^{i}
and
q(x)=\sum^{6}_{i=0}b_{i}x^{i}.

Step 3

Fix
q(x)
in
\mathbf{list\_of\_qs}
and consider
\mathbf{q\_list\_of\_ps}
, its associated list of potential
p(x)
’s. For curves
C_{1}:y^{2}+q(x)y=p_{1}(x)
and
C_{2}:y^{2}+q(x)y=p_{2}(x)
, we write
p_{1}(x)\sim p_{2}(x)
if they are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Refine
\mathbf{q\_list\_of\_ps}
by taking only the representatives
p(x)
for this relation
\sim
. With the same argument as in Lemma
2.2
, we find that the relation
\sim
is defined as:
p_{1}(x)\sim p_{2}(x)
if and only if
(cx+d)^{12}p_{2}(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d})=p_{1}(x)+r(x)^{2}+r(x)q(x)
for some
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{Stab}(q(x))
and some
r(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]
of degree
\deg(q(x))\leq 6
.

In such a manner, using the mathematical software
SageMath
, we computed the list of all non-isomorphic hyperelliptic curves of genus
5
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. There are in total
1070
such curves, i.e.,
|\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|=1070
, and we confirmed that

|\mathcal{H}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})|=\sum_{C\in\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(C)|}=512=2^{2\cdot 5-1}.

For curves in
\mathrm{Hyp}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, we computed their numbers of points over finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{2^{N}}
, for
N\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
, and then we found their Newton polygons. In particular, all the Newton polygons but one occur for elements of this class of genus
5
(nonsingular) curves over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We mention explicitly some of the results in Section
5
.

3
Trigonal curves

Let
C
be a (projective) plane curve. We say that a singularity of
C
is of
delta invariant

1
if it is either a node (an ordinary double point), where a curve is locally of the form
xy=0
, or an ordinary cusp, so that the curve is locally
y^{2}=x^{3}
.

A standard computation using the Riemann-Roch theorem and the genus-degree formula give us a well-known fact, which we use to compute
\mathrm{Trig}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
:

Theorem 3.1
.

A curve
C
of genus
5
is trigonal if and only if it can be represented as a plane quintic with precisely one singularity of delta invariant
1
.

Any isomorphism of such curves
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
extends to an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
For a matrix
M=\begin{pmatrix}m_{11}&m_{12}&m_{13}\\
m_{21}&m_{22}&m_{23}\\
m_{31}&m_{32}&m_{33}\end{pmatrix}
in
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
q(X,Y,Z)
a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y,Z]
, the formula

M.q(X,Y,Z)=q(m_{11}X+m_{12}Y+m_{13}Z,m_{21}X+m_{22}Y+m_{23}Z,m_{31}X+m_{32}Y+m_{33}Z)

(2)

defines an action of
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on the set of all homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y,Z]
. Alternatively, we can define the mentioned action
M.q(X,Y,Z)
as
q(M.(X,Y,Z))
with

M.(X,Y,Z)=M\cdot(X,Y,Z)^{t}.

Therefore, in order to determine the list of all trigonal curves of genus
5
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, it is sufficient to find
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-representatives among all the quintic homogeneous polynomials in
X,Y,Z
that define projective plane curves with delta invariant 1.

To compute all trigonal curves of genus
5
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we have implemented the following algorithm in
SageMath

Complete intersections of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}

The remaining curves of genus

Denote the set of all the non-isomorphic representatives over

\mathbb{F}_{2}
-points of the non-hyperelliptic non-trigonal locus
\mathcal{U}_{5}

The idea behind computing curves

First, we give a set
\Sigma

defining a curve isomorphic to

. Then, we filter the set
\Sigma
to get the set
\mathrm{ComInt}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
so that no two elements of it define curves isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

\mathbb{P}^{4}
are isomorphic over

if and only if their canonical models in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
are isomorphic via some projective automorphism

The group
\mathrm{PGL}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
acts on the subset of homogeneous quadratic polynomials in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{2}[X,Y,Z,T,U]
by acting on variables
X,Y,Z,T,U
via

M.(X,Y,Z,T,U)=M\cdot(X,Y,Z,T,U)^{t}

(3)

for
M\in\mathrm{PGL}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. This induces the right action on homogeneous quadratic polynomials.

For practical work in the program

, we represent the quadrics

\displaystyle q_{P}=p_{0}X^{2}

\displaystyle+p_{1}XY+p_{2}XZ+p_{3}XT+p_{4}XU+p_{5}Y^{2}+p_{6}YZ+p_{7}YT+

\displaystyle+p_{8}YU+p_{9}Z^{2}+p_{10}ZT+p_{11}ZU+p_{12}T^{2}+p_{13}TU+p_{14}U^{2}

using the
15
-tuples

\mathrm{PGL}_{5}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
on

4.1

\mathbb{F}_{2}

An automorphism

\mathbb{F}_{2}

Therefore, for triples
(P,Q,R)

or

to

, for

,

q^{3}-q-2&\text{ if }g\equiv 1\mod 2\end{cases},

and
a_{\lambda}|_{g}=0
, if
|\lambda|
is odd. Using our data, for

and

, we computed the sums from the definition of
a_{\lambda}|_{g}
for some
\lambda
, and the outcomes agree with the mentioned formulas:

a_{[2]}|_{5}=-1025,\quad a_{[1^{2}]}|_{5}=1023,\quad a_{[1^{2},2]}|_{5}=-2367,\quad a_{[1^{2},3]}|_{5}=a_{[3,4^{2}]}|_{5}=a_{[1,2,5^{2}]}|_{5}=0.

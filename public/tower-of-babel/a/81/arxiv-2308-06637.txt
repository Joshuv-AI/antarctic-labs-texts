Novel fuzzy topologies from old through fuzzy primals
Zanyar A. Ameen, Ramadhan A. Mohammed, Tareq M. Al-shami, Baravan A. Asaad

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a novel fuzzy structure named “fuzzy primal”. We study the essential properties and discuss basic operations on it. A fuzzy operator ( ⋅ ) ⋄ (\cdot)^{\diamond} on the family of all fuzzy sets is introduced here by applying the q-neighborhood structure to a primal fuzzy topological space along with the Łukasiewicz disjunction. We explore the main characterizations of ( ⋅ ) ⋄ (\cdot)^{\diamond} . Then, we define another fuzzy operator, symbolized by C ​ l ⋄ Cl^{\diamond} , with the utilization of ( ⋅ ) ⋄ (\cdot)^{\diamond} . These fuzzy operators are studied in order to deduce a new fuzzy topology from the original one. Such a new fuzzy topology is called primal fuzzy topology. The fundamental structure, particularly a fuzzy base that generates primal fuzzy topologies, as well as many relationships between different fuzzy primals and fuzzy topologies, are also analyzed. Lastly, the concept of compatibility between fuzzy primals and fuzzy topologies is introduced, and some equivalent conditions related to this are examined. It is shown that if a fuzzy primal is compatible with a fuzzy topology, then the fuzzy base that generates the primal fuzzy topology is itself a fuzzy topology.



Key words and phrases:
Fuzzy topology, fuzzy primal, fuzzy grill, fuzzy ideal, fuzzy filter, primal fuzzy topology, fuzzy ideal topology.

Introduction

By Zadeh
[
16
]
, the fundamental idea of a fuzzy set was first presented as an extension of a classical set. Chang later proposed the idea of fuzzy topology
[
7
]
. Lowen
[
11
]
offered an alternate definition of fuzzy topology. The idea can be viewed as a natural generalization of the general topology. Since then, other authors in this field have examined and conducted various general topological characteristics in fuzzy settings.

A new weak structure can be created by omitting a particular postulate (or part of a postulate) in fuzzy topology. For instance, by dismissing the finite intersection condition, Abd-El-Monsef and Ramadan
[
1
]
appeared with the concept of fuzzy supra topology. By disregarding the union condition in fuzzy topology, Ameen et al.
[
5
]
established the concept of infra fuzzy topology. A Lowen’s version of infra fuzzy topology was appeared in
[
9
,
4
]
under the name of fuzzy infi-topology.

Other mathematical fuzzy structure like fuzzy ideals, fuzzy filters, and fuzzy grills are essential in developing fuzzy topology. The concept of a fuzzy ideal was introduced by Sarkar
[
15
]
while studying the notion of ideal fuzzy topology. Gähler
[
10
]
used fuzzy filters for producing certain types of fuzzy topologies. The structure of fuzzy grills were defined by Azad
[
6
]
for the purpose studying fuzzy approximation spaces. Fuzzy grills are generalization of (crisp) grills proposed by Choquet
[
8
]
in 1947. Mukherjee and Das
[
13
]
constructed a fuzzy topology which can be called ”grill fuzzy topology. It is worth remarking that all these fuzzy topologies are finer than the original fuzzy topologies and are normal subclasses of the set of all fuzzy topologies.

Recently, Acharjee et al.
[
2
]
defined the notion of primals as the dual of grills. Then they used primals to generate a new fuzzy topology called primal topology. Following their direction, Al-shami et al.
[
3
]
introduced soft primal and studied their basic properties. Then soft primals are applied to generate a new soft topology called primal soft topology. This proves to us constructing new topologies over a single universe via some structures is another fruitful area of study. Motivating by the latter statements, we introduce the concept of fuzzy primals and study their fundamental properties. By using the fuzzy primal structure, we build a new fuzzy topology from an old one and name it the primal fuzzy topology.

Here is the remaining content of the paper: Afterward the introduction, we review the definitions and outcomes needed to truly comprehend the material provided in this paper. After that, in Section 3, we establish the concept of fuzzy primals and demonstrate how fuzzy primals and fuzzy grills are related concepts. The fundamental operations on fuzzy primals are investigated. In Section 4, we propose the definition of a primal fuzzy topological space followed by a fuzzy topological operator
(\cdot)^{\diamond}
. Then, we study the main properties of
(\cdot)^{\diamond}
. Furthermore, we define another fuzzy operator called
Cl^{\diamond}
with the aid of
(\cdot)^{\diamond}
and show that
Cl^{\diamond}
is the Kuratowski fuzzy closure operator. It means that
Cl^{\diamond}
generates a fuzzy topology, which we call a primal fuzzy topology. The central properties of primal fuzzy topologies are studied. In Section 5, we introduce the concept of compatibility of a fuzzy primal with a fuzzy topology. Some equivalences of the this concept are investigated. In Section 6, we conclude by summarizing the major contributions and making some recommendations for the future.

3
Fuzzy primal

Definition 3.1
.

A subfamily
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathbb{I}^{Y}
is said to be a fuzzy primal on
Y
if it satisfies the following postulates:

1.

1_{Y}\notin\mathcal{P}
.

2.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
, and
\nu\subseteq\mu
, then
\nu\in\mathcal{P}
.

3.

If
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
such that
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
or
\nu\in\mathcal{P}
.

The next result is easy to prove.

Theorem 3.2
.

A subfamily
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathbb{I}^{Y}\
is a fuzzy primal on
Y
iff the following conditions are satisfied.

1.

1_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{P}
.

2.

If
\mu\not\in\mathcal{P}
and
\mu\subseteq\nu
, then
\nu\not\in\mathcal{P}
.

3.

If
\mu\not\in\mathcal{P}
and
\nu\not\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\mu\cap\nu\not\in\mathcal{P}
.

Example 3.3
.

The following families are fuzzy primals:

1.

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathbb{I}^{Y}-\{1_{Y}\}
(Trivial fuzzy primal).

2.

\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}:\lambda\leq\mu\}
, where

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}4x,&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq 1/4;\\
1,&\text{ if }1/4\leq x\leq 1.\end{cases}

3.

\mathcal{P}_{3}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y},y_{t_{0}}\notin\lambda\}
, where
y_{t_{0}}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(Y)
.

4.

\mathcal{P}_{4}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y},\lambda\text{ has a range which is subset of even numbers}\}
.

It is worth noting that the families
\mathbb{I}^{Y}
and
\{0_{Y}\}
are not fuzzy primals. The first is clear as
1_{Y}\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
. For the latter, we have
\mu\cap\nu=0_{Y}\in\{0_{Y}\}
, but neither
\mu
nor
\nu
belongs to
\{0_{Y}\}
, where

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq 1/2;\\
2x-1,&\text{ if }1/2\leq x\leq 1,\end{cases}

and

\nu(x)=\begin{cases}1-2x,&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq 1/2;\\
0,&\text{ if }1/2\leq x\leq 1.\end{cases}

Theorem 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a fuzzy grill on
Y
, then the family
\mathcal{P}=\{\nu:\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}\}
is a fuzzy primal on
Y
.

Proof.

First, it is obvious that
0_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{G}
, so
1_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{P}
. Second, let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}
and take any fuzzy subset
\mu
of
\nu
. By the way of constructing
\mathcal{P}
we have
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}
. Since
\bar{\nu}\subseteq\bar{\mu}
, it follows from the definition of fuzzy grill that
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{G}
. This automatically means that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
. Third, let
\mu,\nu
be fuzzy subsets such that
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}
. Then,
\bar{\mu}\cup\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}
. Therefore,
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{G}
or
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}
. Thus,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
or
\nu\in\mathcal{P}
. Hence, we get the desired result.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is a fuzzy primal on
Y
, then the family
\mathcal{G}=\{\nu:\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a fuzzy grill on
Y
.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are two fuzzy primals on
Y
, then
\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
is a fuzzy primal on
Y
.

Proof.

First, let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be two fuzzy primals on
Y
. Then
1_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
1_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. So that
1_{Y}\not\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
. Second, suppose that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
and let
\mu\subseteq\nu
. Then,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
or
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. This automatically leads to that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
or
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. So
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
. Third, let
\mu,\nu
be fuzzy subsets such that
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
. Then,
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
or
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
. This implies that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
or
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\mathcal{P}_{2}
, as required.
∎

The next example elaborates that the class of fuzzy primals on a set
Y
is not closed under the intersection operator in general.

Example 3.7
.

Let
Y=\{y,z\}
. Consider families
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}:0\leq\lambda(y)\leq 0.6,0\leq\lambda(z)\leq 1\}\cup\{0_{Y}\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{\eta\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}:0\leq\eta(y)\leq 1,0\leq\eta(z)\leq 0.7\}\cup\{0_{Y}\}
. Evidently,
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2}
satisfy all conditions in Definition
3.1
, so they are fuzzy primals on
Y
. If
\mu,\nu
are fuzzy sets such that
\mu(y)=0.5
,
\mu(z)=1
and
\nu(y)=1
,
\nu(z)=0.6
, then
\nu\notin\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mu\notin\mathcal{P}_{2}
. On the other hand,
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}
, but neither
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}
nor
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}
is not a fuzzy primal.

4
Primal fuzzy topology

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T})
be a fuzzy topological space and
\mathcal{P}
be a fuzzy primal on
Y
. The 3-tuple
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
is said to be a primal fuzzy topological space (briefly, PFTS).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
be a PFTS. Then a fuzzy mapping
(\cdot)^{\diamond}:\mathbb{I}^{Y}\to\mathbb{I}^{Y}
is defined as follows
\lambda^{\diamond}(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})=\{y_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(Y):\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})\}
for each fuzzy subset
\lambda
. For short, we write
\lambda^{\diamond}
or
\lambda_{\mathcal{P}}^{\diamond}
instead of
\lambda^{\diamond}(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
.

The next examples establish that the properties
\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq\lambda
and
\lambda\subseteq\lambda^{\diamond}
are generally false.

Example 4.3
.

Let the fuzzy set
\mu
be defined as in Example
3.3
. That is,

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq 1/2;\\
2x-1,&\text{ if }1/2\leq x\leq 1.\end{cases}

Consider the fuzzy topology
\mathfrak{T}=\{0_{Y},\mu,1_{Y}\}
on a set
Y
and the fuzzy primal
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{I}^{Y}-\{1_{Y}\}
.
One can check that
\mu\diamond=Cl(\mu)=1_{Y}\nsubseteq\mu
(c.f., Theorem
4.5
(ii)).

Example 4.4
.

Consider the indiscrete fuzzy topology
\mathfrak{T}=\{0_{Y},1_{Y}\}
on a set
Y
and any fuzzy primal
\mathcal{P}
. Since
1_{Y}
is the only fuzzy open q-neighborhood of all fuzzy points
y_{t}
, so for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
,

\displaystyle y_{t}\in\lambda^{\diamond}\Longleftrightarrow\bar{\lambda}\oplus\overline{1_{Y}}=\bar{\lambda}\oplus 0_{Y}=\bar{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}.

(
\blacksquare
)

Therefore, we have

\lambda^{\diamond}=\begin{cases}0_{Y},&\text{ if }\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P};\\
1_{Y},&\text{ if }\bar{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}.\end{cases}

Take
0_{Y}\neq\bar{\mu}\notin\mathcal{P}
. Thus,
\mu\nsubseteq\mu\diamond=0_{Y}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be fuzzy subsets of a PFTS
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
. Then the next statements hold true.

1.

0_{Y}^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.

2.

\lambda^{\diamond}=Cl(\lambda^{\diamond})\subseteq Cl(\lambda)
.

3.

If
\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
\lambda^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.

4.

If
\lambda\subseteq\mu
, then
\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq\mu^{\diamond}
.

5.

(\lambda^{\diamond})^{\diamond}\subseteq\lambda^{\diamond}
.

6.

(\lambda\cup\mu)^{\diamond}=\lambda^{\diamond}\cup\mu^{\diamond}
.

7.

(\lambda\cap\mu)^{\diamond}\subseteq\lambda^{\diamond}\cap\mu^{\diamond}
.

8.

If
\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P}
, then
(\lambda\cup\mu)^{\diamond}=\mu^{\diamond}
.

Proof.

1.

Since
\bar{0}_{Y}\oplus\bar{\lambda}=1_{Y}
for any fuzzy set
\lambda
and
1_{Y}\notin\mathcal{P}
, so
0_{Y}^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.

2.

If
y_{t}\in Cl(\lambda^{\diamond})
, then
\lambda^{\diamond}\prec\nu
for each
\nu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
. This means that
\lambda^{\diamond}(z)+\nu(z)>1

Cl(\lambda^{\diamond})\subseteq\lambda^{\diamond}

Cl(\lambda^{\diamond})=\lambda^{\diamond}
.

We now show that
\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq Cl(\lambda)

y_{t}\notin Cl(\lambda)

\lambda\not\prec\nu
for some
\nu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})

z\in X

\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\nu}=1_{Y}\notin\mathcal{P}

y_{t}\notin\lambda^{\diamond}

Suppose otherwise that there exists

3.2

\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\nu}\notin\mathcal{P}

\lambda^{\diamond}=0_{Y}

Assume that
\lambda\subseteq\mu

y_{t}\in\lambda^{\diamond}

\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}
for all
\nu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})

\lambda\subseteq\mu

\bar{\mu}\subseteq\bar{\lambda}

2.6
,
\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\nu}\subseteq\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\nu}

\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}

\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq\mu^{\diamond}

5.

By (ii),
\lambda^{\diamond\diamond}=Cl(\lambda^{\diamond\diamond})\subseteq Cl(\lambda^{\diamond})=\lambda^{\diamond}

6.

\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq

\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\theta}\notin\mathcal{P}

(\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\theta})\cap(\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\theta})\notin\mathcal{P}

3.2

(\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\theta})\cap(\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\theta})\subseteq(\bar{\lambda}\cap\bar{\mu})\oplus\bar{\theta}

7.

Since

8.

It follows from parts (iii) and (vi).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
be a PFTS. Then
\mu^{\diamond}=Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
for some
\lambda\notin\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

On the first hand, we want to show that
\mu^{\diamond}\subseteq Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
for some
\lambda\notin\mathcal{P}
. Let
y_{t}\notin Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
. Then there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
such that

\nu(y)+\big(\mu(y)-(1-\lambda(y))\big)\leq 1.

This implies that

\nu(y)+\mu(y)-1+\lambda(y)\leq 1,

and so

2-\big(\nu(y)+\mu(y)\big)\geq\lambda(y)\text{ for all }y\in Y.

Therefore,
\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\nu}\notin\mathcal{P}
and thus,
y_{t}\notin\mu^{\diamond}
. This proves that
\mu^{\diamond}\subseteq Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
.

On the other hand, if
y_{t}\notin\mu^{\diamond}
, then there exists
\theta\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
such that
\bar{\mu}\oplus\bar{\theta}=\lambda\notin\mathcal{P}
. We want to check that

\theta+\big(\mu-\bar{\lambda}\big)\leq 1_{Y}.

Given
y\in Y
. If
\theta(y)+\mu(y)>1
, then
(1-\theta)(y)+(1-\mu)(y)=\lambda(y)
and therefore,
1-\theta(y)+1-\mu(y)=\lambda(y)\cdots(\blacktriangle)
. Thus,
\theta(y)+\mu(y)-1+\lambda(y)=1
. If
\theta(y)+\mu(y)\leq 1
, by
(\blacktriangle)
, we get
\lambda(y)=0
. This means that
\theta(y)+\mu(y)-1+\lambda(y)\leq 1
. Both cases imply that
\theta(y)+\mu(y)-1+\lambda(y)\leq 1
means
\theta+\big(\mu-\bar{\lambda}\big)\leq 1_{Y}
. This yields that
y_{t}\notin Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
and then
Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})\subseteq\mu^{\diamond}
. Hence,
\mu^{\diamond}=Cl(\mu-\bar{\lambda})
.
∎

Definition 4.8
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
be a PFTS. Then a fuzzy mapping
Cl^{\diamond}:\mathbb{I}^{Y}\to\mathbb{I}^{Y}
is defined as follows
Cl^{\diamond}(\mu)=\mu\cup\mu^{\diamond}
for any fuzzy set
\mu
. We may use
Cl^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}}
instead of
Cl^{\diamond}
if any kind of confusion arises.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be fuzzy subsets of a PFTS
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
. Then the next statements hold true:

1.

Cl^{\diamond}(0_{Y})=0_{Y}
.

2.

\lambda\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)
.

3.

\lambda\subseteq\mu
implies
Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\mu)
.

4.

Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda\cup\mu)=Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\cup Cl^{\diamond}(\mu)
.

5.

Cl^{\diamond}(Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda))=Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

1.

Since
\overline{0_{Y}}\notin\mathcal{P}
, by Theorem
4.5
(iii),
0_{Y}^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
, and hence
Cl^{\diamond}(0_{Y})=0_{Y}\cup 0_{Y}^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.

2.

This one is easy as
\lambda\subseteq\lambda\cup\lambda^{\diamond}=Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)
.

3.

Suppose
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
with
\lambda\subseteq\mu
. By Theorem
4.5
(iv),
\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq

\mu^{\diamond}
and so
\lambda\cup\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq\mu\cup\mu^{\diamond}
. Thus,
Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\mu)
.

4.

By the same technique used in (iii) and applying Theorem
4.5
(vii), one can easily conclude that
Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda\cup\mu)=Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\cup Cl^{\diamond}(\mu)
.

5.

To show that
Cl^{\diamond}(Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda))\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)
, we implicitly use multiple statements of Theorem
4.5
. Now,

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\bigcup(Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda))^{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\bigcup(\lambda\cup\lambda^{\diamond})^{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\bigcup\lambda^{\diamond}\bigcup(\lambda^{\diamond})^{\diamond}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)\bigcup\lambda^{\diamond}\bigcup\lambda^{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda).

The converse is always true by using (ii) and (iii). Therefore,
Cl^{\diamond}(Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda))=Cl^{\diamond}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
be a PFTS. Then the family
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}=\{\mu\subseteq\mathbb{I}^{Y}:Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\mu})=\bar{\mu}\}
constitutes a fuzzy topology on
Y
.

Proof.

Since
Cl^{\diamond}(0_{Y})=0_{Y}
by Theorem
4.9
(i), so
1_{Y}\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
. By Theorem
4.9
(ii),
1_{Y}\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(1_{Y})
. Then
0_{Y}\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
. Then
Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\mu})=\bar{\mu}
and
Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\nu})=\bar{\nu}
. By Theorem
4.9
(iv),
Cl^{\diamond}(\overline{\mu\cap\nu})=Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\mu}\cup\bar{\nu})=Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\mu})\cup Cl^{\diamond}(\bar{\nu})=\bar{\mu}\cup\bar{\nu}=\overline{\mu\cap\nu}
. This implies that
\mu\cap\nu\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
.

Let
\{\mu_{s}:s\in S\}\subseteq\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
. Then
Cl^{\diamond}(\overline{\mu_{s}})=\overline{\mu_{s}}
for all
s
. Set
\sigma=\bigcap_{s\in S}\overline{\mu_{s}}
. By Theorem
4.9
(iii),
Cl^{\diamond}(\sigma)\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\overline{\mu_{s}})=\overline{\mu_{s}}
for all
s
. This implies that
Cl^{\diamond}(\sigma)\subseteq\bigcap_{s\in S}\overline{\mu_{s}}=\sigma
. By Theorem
4.9
(ii),
\sigma\subseteq Cl^{\diamond}(\sigma)
. Therefore,
Cl^{\diamond}(\bigcap_{s\in S}\overline{\mu_{s}})=\bigcap_{s\in S}\overline{\mu_{s}}
. But
\overline{\bigcap_{s\in S}\overline{\mu_{s}}}=\bigcup_{s\in S}\mu_{s}
implies
\bigcup_{s\in S}\mu_{s}\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
is a fuzzy topology on
Y
.
∎

Theorem 4.13

\spadesuit

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle>1-\beta(y)

\displaystyle\mu\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathfrak{T}}(y_{t}).

\beta\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}

\bar{\beta}

\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}

(\bar{\mu})^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\bar{\mu}

(\bar{\mu})^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}}\bigcup(\bar{\mu})^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\bar{\mu}
. By Theorem
4.7
(ii),
(\bar{\mu})^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\bar{\mu}
implies
\bar{\mu}
is fuzzy
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{G}}
-closed. Hence,
\mu\in\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{G}}
and consequently,
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}}\bigcap\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{G}}
. Thus,
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{G}}=\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{P}}\bigcap\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
∎

.

5
Compatibility of a fuzzy primal with a fuzzy topology

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
be a PFTS. Then
\mathfrak{T}
is called compatible with
\mathcal{P}
, denoted by
\mathfrak{T}\approx\mathcal{P}
, if for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
and each
y_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(Y)
with
y_{t}\in\lambda
, there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
such that
\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\mu}\notin\mathcal{P}
implies
\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
. The fuzzy set
\lambda^{\square}
is defined by

\lambda^{\square}=\{y_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(Y):y_{t}\in\lambda\text{ and }y_{t}\notin\lambda^{\diamond}\}.

Evidently,
\lambda^{\diamond}\bigcap\lambda^{\square}=0_{Y}
.

We can express any fuzzy set
\lambda
in terms of the operators
\lambda^{\diamond}
and
\lambda^{\square}
using the result that follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
,
\lambda=\lambda^{\square}\cup(\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond})
.

Proof.

Surely, by Definition
5.2
,
\lambda^{\square}\subseteq\lambda
, and
\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond}\subseteq\lambda
. Therefore,
\lambda^{\square}\cup(\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond})\subseteq\lambda
.

On the other hand, suppose
y_{t}\in\lambda
. If
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\diamond}
, then
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\diamond}\cap\lambda
. Differently,
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\square}
. Both cases imply
\lambda\subseteq\lambda^{\square}\cup(\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond})
. Thus,
\lambda=\lambda^{\square}\cup(\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond})
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
,
\lambda^{\square}\cap(\lambda^{\square})^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise that there is
y_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}(Y)
such that
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\square}\cap(\lambda^{\square})^{\diamond}
. Then
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\square}
and
y_{t}\in(\lambda^{\square})^{\diamond}
. From the fist statement, we have
y_{t}\in\lambda
and
y_{t}\notin\lambda^{\diamond}
. Form
y_{t}\in(\lambda^{\square})^{\diamond}
, we have
\overline{\lambda^{\square}}\oplus\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
. Since
\lambda^{\square}\subseteq\lambda
, so
\bar{\lambda}\subseteq\overline{\lambda^{\square}}
implies
\bar{\lambda}\oplus\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{Q}(y_{t})
. This means that
y_{t}\in\lambda^{\diamond}
, a contradiction to the consequence of the first statement. Thus, we must have
\lambda^{\square}\cap(\lambda^{\square})^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
.
∎

.

For any PFTS
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})
, the following properties are equivalent:

1.

\mathfrak{T}\approx\mathcal{P}
.

2.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
with
\lambda\cap\lambda^{\diamond}=0_{Y}
implies
\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P}
.

3.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
,
\overline{(\lambda^{\square})}\notin\mathcal{P}

4.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{I}^{Y}
, if
\lambda
includes no
\sigma
such that
0_{Y}\neq\sigma\subseteq\sigma^{\diamond}
implies
\bar{\lambda}\notin\mathcal{P}
.

For each fuzzy
\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}
-closed set

in

,
\overline{(\lambda^{\square})}\notin\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

(ii) Given

r<t

y_{r}\in\lambda^{\diamond}

(iv) Let

Theorem 5.7

Let

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{T},\mathcal{P})

\mathfrak{T}\approx\mathcal{P}

\mathfrak{T}^{\diamond}=\big\{\mu-\bar{\lambda}:\mu\in\mathfrak{T}\text{ and }\lambda\not\in\mathcal{P}\big\}

Let

Conclusion and possible lines of future work

Chang [1] proposed a fuzzy topology on a universe, which is a modified version of classical topology. Lowen [2] expanded the definition of fuzzy topology by including all constant functions in addition to zero and unit functions. These topological generalizations have developed into an intriguing field of study. Several methods for generating fuzzy topologies have been discussed in the literature. By investigating the concept of primal fuzzy topology, we have made a novel contribution to the field of fuzzy topology. This study is based on fuzzy primal, a supplementary concept of a fuzzy grill. Fuzzy primals can be thought of as a widening of fuzzy ideals. We have covered multiple fundamental operations on fuzzy primals. A primal fuzzy topological space combines a fuzzy topological space and a fuzzy primal. Then we have proposed a fuzzy operator, denoted by the symbol
(\cdot)^{\diamond}
, in regard to a fuzzy topological space. The fuzzy topological operator
(\cdot)^{\diamond}
is used to define another operator called ”
Cl^{\diamond}
.” The characteristics of
Cl^{\diamond}
have been discussed in detail. We have observed that
Cl^{\diamond}
is consistent with all of Kuratowski’s fuzzy closure operator’s axioms, therefore it generates a fuzzy topology known as primal fuzzy topology. Some examples have been provided to demonstrate the fact that primal fuzzy topologies are natural (non-trivial) fuzzy topologies. We have shown that the base of primal fuzzy consists of fuzzy sets that are fuzzy open sets from the original fuzzy topology minus the complement of some element in a fuzzy primal. Consequently, a primal fuzzy topology can be viewed as a larger fuzzy topology than the original one. We defined the term as a fuzzy primal being compatible with a fuzzy topology and examined other analogous conditions. It is demonstrated that the fuzzy base which produces the primary fuzzy topology is also a fuzzy topology if a fuzzy primal and a fuzzy topology are compatible.

The findings in this article are preliminary, and additional studies could provide additional knowledge by investigating more aspects of primal fuzzy topology, such as primal fuzzy interior, primal fuzzy limit points, and so on. Separation axioms, compactness, and connectedness of primal fuzzy topologies are also possible research directions in this area.

Infinitesimal deformations of some Quot schemes
Indranil Biswas, Chandranandan Gangopadhyay, Ronnie Sebastian

Abstract
. Let E E be a vector bundle on a smooth complex projective curve C C of genus at least two. Let 𝒬 ⁡ ( E , d ) \mathcal{Q}(E,d) be the Quot scheme parameterizing the torsion quotients of E E of degree d d . We compute the cohomologies of the tangent bundle T 𝒬 ⁡ ( E , d ) T_{\mathcal{Q}(E,d)} . In particular, the space of infinitesimal deformations of 𝒬 ⁡ ( E , d ) \mathcal{Q}(E,d) is computed. Kempf and Fantechi computed the space of infinitesimal deformations of 𝒬 ⁡ ( 𝒪 C , d ) = C ( d ) \mathcal{Q}({\mathcal{O}}_{C},d)\,=\,C^{(d)} ( [ Kem81 ] , [ Fan94 ] ).

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g_{C}
, with

g_{C}\,\geq\,2
.
Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r\,\geqslant\,1
.
Fix an integer
d\,\geqslant\,1
. Let
\mathcal{Q}\,:=\,\mathcal{Q}(E,d)
be the
Quot scheme parameterizing all torsion quotients of

E
of degree
d
. It is known that
\mathcal{Q}
is a
smooth projective variety of dimension
rd
. This Quot scheme has
various moduli theoretic interpretations,
see
[
BGL94
]
,
[
BDW96
]
,
[
BFP20
]
,

[
HPL21
]
,
[
BRa13
]
,
[
OP21
]
,
which have led to extensive studies of it. Our aim here is to compute
the cohomologies of the tangent bundle
T_{\mathcal{Q}}
, especially

H^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})
that parametrizes the infinitesimal deformations
of
\mathcal{Q}
.

The group of holomorphic automorphisms
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{Q})
of
\mathcal{Q}
is a complex Lie group
whose Lie algebra is
H^{0}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})

[
Ser06
, Lemma 1.2.6]
.
It is known that

H^{0}(\mathcal{Q}({\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus r},d),\,T_{\mathcal{Q}({\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus r},d)})\,=\,\mathfrak{sl}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\,=\,H^{0}(X,\,{\rm End}({\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus r}))/{\mathbb{C}}

for all
r\,\geq\,2

[
BDH15
]
. From this it follows that the maximal
connected subgroup of
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{Q}({\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus r},d))
is

{\rm PGL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})\,=\,\text{Aut}({\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus r})/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
More generally, if either
E
is semistable or
r\,\geqslant\,3
,
then

H^{0}(\mathcal{Q}(E,d),\,T_{\mathcal{Q}(E,d)})\,=\,H^{0}(X,\,\text{End}(E))/{\mathbb{C}}

[
Gan19
]
, and hence the maximal connected subgroup of
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{Q}(E,d))

is
\text{Aut}(E)/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Regarding the next cohomology
H^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})
, first consider the
case of
r\,=\,1
. In this case, the Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}(E,d)
is identified with the
d
-th symmetric product

C^{(d)}
of
C
. The infinitesimal deformation space

H^{1}(C^{(d)},\,T_{C^{(d)}})
was computed in
[
Kem81
]
under
the assumption that
C
is
non-hyperelliptic, and it was computed in
[
Fan94
]
when

g\,\geqslant\,3
(see also
[
Fan94
, Remark 2.6]
for the case
g=2
).

Henceforth, we will always assume that
r\,=\,{\rm rank}(E)\,\geqslant\,2
.

If
d\,=\,1
, then
\mathcal{Q}\cong\mathbb{P}(E)
. Consequently,

H^{1}(\mathcal{Q}(E,1),\,T_{\mathcal{Q}(E,1)})
can be computed easily.

Associated to the vector bundle
E
there is the Atiyah bundle
At(E)
on
C

[
Ati57
, Theorem 1]
. The infinitesimal
deformations of the pair
(C,\,E)
are parametrized by

H^{1}(X,\,At(E))

[
Che12
, Proposition 4.2]
. For the natural
homomorphisms
\mathcal{O}_{C}\,\hookrightarrow\,\text{End}(E)\,\hookrightarrow\,At(E)
, the quotients
\text{End}(E)/\mathcal{O}_{C}

and
At(E)/\mathcal{O}_{C}
are vector bundles and will be denoted by
ad(E)
and
at(E)
respectively.
Also, given any vector bundle
V
on
C
we can
construct a natural bundle called the Secant bundle

{Sec}^{d}(V)
on
C^{(d)}

(see
[
Mat65
, Proposition 1]
,
[
BL11
, Section 2]
).
In particular we have a bundle
Sec^{d}(at(E))
on
C^{(d)}
.

Recall that we have the Hilbert-Chow map
\phi:\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow C^{(d)}

(also called Quot-to-Chow morphism by some authors

[
Ric20
, Section 2.4]
). We refer to

[
GS20
, Section 2]
for the definition of this map.

We prove the following (see Theorem
9.9
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r\,=\,{\rm rank}(E)\,\geqslant\,2
. Then

(1)

Sec^{d}(at(E))\,\cong\,\phi_{*}T_{\mathcal{Q}}
and

(2)

R^{i}\phi_{*}T_{\mathcal{Q}}\,=\,0
for all
i\,>\,0
.

The cohomologies of
Sec^{d}(at(E))
can be computed easily. Therefore,
we can compute the spaces
H^{i}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})
using Theorem
1.1
.
We prove the following (see Theorem
9.10
):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\rm rank}(E),\,d\,\geqslant\,2
. Denote the genus of
C
by
g_{C}
.
The following three statements hold:

(1)

For all
d-1\,\geqslant\,i\,\geqslant\,0
,

\displaystyle H^{i}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,H^{0}(C,\,at(E))\otimes

\displaystyle\bigwedge^{i}H^{1}(C,\,\mathcal{O}_{C})

\displaystyle\bigoplus H^{1}(C,\,at(E))\otimes\bigwedge^{i-1}H^{1}(C,\,\mathcal{O}_{C})\,.

In particular,

h^{i}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,{g_{C}\choose i}\cdot h^{0}(C,\,at(E))+{g_{C}\choose{i-1}}\cdot h^{1}(C,\,at(E))\,.

(2)

When
i\,=\,d
,

H^{d}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,\bigwedge^{d-1}H^{1}(C,\,\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes h^{1}(C,\,at(E))\,.

In particular,

h^{d}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,{g_{C}\choose{d-1}}\cdot h^{1}(C,\,at(E))\,.

(3)

For all
i\,\geq\,d+1
,

H^{i}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,0.

By Theorem
1.2
when
g_{C}\geq 2
we get

H^{0}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,H^{0}(C,\,at(E))\,=\,H^{0}(C,\,ad(E))\,.

Recall that
\mathcal{Q}
is a fine moduli space, that is,
there exists a certain universal quotient on
C\times\mathcal{Q}
.
The kernel of this universal quotient, which is locally free, is denoted
by
\mathcal{A}
. We also
compute
H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\,{\mathscr{E}nd}(\mathcal{A}))
, which
is the space of all infinitesimal
deformations of
\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, the following is proved
(see Corollary
9.16
):

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\rm rank}(E),\,g_{C},\,d\,\geqslant\,2
. Then we have

H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\,{\mathscr{E}nd}(\mathcal{A}))\,=\,H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\,\mathcal{O}_{C\times\mathcal{Q}})\,=H^{1}(C,\,\mathcal{O}_{C})\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}})\,.

It can be seen using Corollary
9.1

that
H^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
.

In
[
Gan18
]
it was shown that when
E\,\cong\,\mathcal{O}^{n}_{C}

for some
n\,\geqslant\,1
, then
\mathcal{A}
is slope stable with
respect to some natural polarizations of
C\times\mathcal{Q}
.
By
[
HL10
, Corollary 4.5.2]
, the space
H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\,{\mathscr{E}nd}(\mathcal{A}))
is the tangent
space at
[\mathcal{A}]
of the Moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of sheaves on

C\times\mathcal{Q}
with the same Hilbert polynomial
(with respect to some fixed polarization on
C\times\mathcal{Q}
) as
\mathcal{A}
.
Moreover, the differential of the determinant map
\mathcal{M}\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm Pic}(C\times\mathcal{Q})
at the point
[\mathcal{A}]
is given by
the trace map

H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\,{\mathscr{E}nd}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{H^{1}(tr)}H^{1}(C\times\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{O}_{C\times\mathcal{Q}})

(see
[
HL10
, Theorem 4.5.3]
).
Note that this map is onto, since the composition of the maps
\mathcal{O}_{C\times\mathcal{Q}}\to{\mathscr{E}nd}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{tr}\mathcal{O}_{C\times\mathcal{Q}}
is an isomorphism.
Therefore, Theorem
1.3
implies
that the determinant map
\mathcal{M}\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm Pic}(C\times\mathcal{Q})

induces an isomorphism at the level of tangent spaces
at the point
[\mathcal{A}]
.

We briefly describe how the paper is organized. In Sections 2 to 5
we prove several preliminary results which we shall need later.
In these sections we show that the relative adjoint Atiyah sequence
associated to a vector bundle
V
on
C\times X
(see (
4.4
)),
restricted to
c\times X
can be obtained in three different ways.
In Section
6 we recall the construction of the space

from
[
GS20
]
and prove
a result relating to its canonical divisor. In Section
7 we prove some results related to projective bundles.
In Section 8, the results in Section 7 and Section 5
are used in computing cohomologies of some sheaves on

. These
cohomology computations are used in Section 9 to compute higher
direct images, for the Hilbert-Chow map, of some natural vector bundles
on
\mathcal{Q}
. These computations are then used to prove the main
results.

on

Lemma 5.1
.

The image of the map in (

Remark 5.2
.

We summarize what we have done above as follows.

Proposition 5.3
.

The infinitesimal deformation map of
\mathcal{K}

(see (
5.3
)) at
c
, that is,
the map in (
5.2
)

If the vector bundle
\mathcal{F}

\{a_{\alpha\beta}\}

on a variety

is given by the cocycle

7.

We collect here some facts which shall be used later.
Let

(7.1)

Lemma 7.1
.

With notation as above,

(1)

R^{i}\pi_{*}(\Omega_{\pi}(1))\,=\,0

\forall\ i
,

(2)

H^{i}(\mathbb{P}(E),\,\Omega_{\pi}(1))\,=\,0

\forall\ i

(3)

\forall\ i

(4)

\forall\ i

(5)

\forall\ i

(6)

\forall\ i

(7)

\forall\ i

(8)

\forall\ i

Proof.

Note that for any

from the Euler sequence on the projective space

for all

. By Grauert’s theorem we get that

R^{i}\pi_{*}(\Omega_{\pi}(1))\,=\,R^{i}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(-1))\,=\,0\,\ \ \ \forall\ \ i\,\geq\,0\,.

Therefore we get (1) and (3). Using the Leray spectral sequence we see
that (2) and (4) follows immediately from (1) and (3) respectively. From projection

9.4.

The following theorem is deduced using the fact that

.

be the genus of

. For all

\displaystyle H^{i}(\mathcal{Q},\,T_{\mathcal{Q}})\,=\,H^{0}(C,\,at(E))\otimes

\displaystyle\bigoplus H^{1}(C,\,at(E))\otimes\bigwedge^{i-1}H^{1}(C,\,\mathcal{O}_{C})\,.

, Proposition

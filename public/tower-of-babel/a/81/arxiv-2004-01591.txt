Relating spin squeezing to multipartite entanglement criteria for particles and modes
Matteo Fadel, Manuel Gessner

Abstract
Entanglement witnesses based on first and second moments exist in the form of spin-squeezing criteria for the detection of particle entanglement from collective measurements, and in form of modified uncertainty relations for the detection of mode entanglement or steering. By revealing a correspondence between them, we show that metrologically useful spin squeezing reveals multimode entanglement in symmetric spin states that are distributed into addressable modes. We further derive tight state-independent multipartite entanglement bounds on the spin-squeezing coefficient and point out their connection to widely-used entanglement criteria that depend on the state’s polarization. Our results are relevant for state-of-the-art experiments where symmetric entangled states are distributed into a number of addressable modes, such as split spin-squeezed Bose-Einstein condensates.

II
Mode vs particle entanglement

A collection of systems (labeled
1,...,\Xi
) is entangled if their quantum state cannot be written as

\rho=\sum_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}\rho_{\gamma}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{(\Xi)}\;,

(1)

where
\sum_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}=1
is a probability distribution, and
\rho_{\gamma}^{(i)}
are density matrices for system
i
. The local systems may refer either to the
\Xi=N
particles or to the
\Xi=M
modes that they occupy, giving rise to particle or mode entanglement, respectively. In practice, determining whether a given quantum state allows for a decomposition of the form of Eq. (
1
) is an extremely hard task. One therefore relies on entanglement witnesses or, more generally, necessary conditions that any separable state must satisfy
GuehneToth
;
HuberReview
. A violation of these criteria then represents a witness for entanglement.

II.1
Uncertainty-based mode-entanglement criterion

Criteria based on first and second moments of linear observables are powerful tools to detect entanglement

;
Raymer
;
Hofmann
;
GuehneToth
and steering

;
ReidRMP

An important class of these criteria take on the form of Heisenberg-Robertson-type uncertainty relations, with a modified lower bound on the variances that can be violated by entangled states. The most general formulation of these criteria for bipartite systems was given by Giovannetti
et al.
in Ref.

HyllusEisert
;

{\bm{S}}^{A}=\sum_{i\in A}{\bm{s}}^{(i)}/2

{\bm{s}}^{(i)}
is the spin for particle

\mathcal{G}^{2}:=\dfrac{4\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}\right]\,\text{Var}\left[S_{y}^{A}-S_{y}^{B}\right]}{\left(|\langle S_{x}^{A}\rangle|+|\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle|\right)^{2}}\geq 1\;.

(2)

The choice of observables (
e.g.
local spin components) can further be optimized to identify the most sensitive entanglement criterion for a given quantum state. These criteria can be generalized to study entanglement in specific multipartitions (with precise microscopic information about which subsystems share quantum correlations)

as well as full inseparability

;
Toscano
;
ReidTeh
;
RiedTehSpin
,
i.e.
the violation of these bounds in all possible partitions.

II.2
Spin squeezing particle-entanglement criterion

In the case of a large number of spins, it becomes challenging to address each particle individually. Nevertheless, particle entanglement among the individual spins can be detected from collective measurements of the spin components through spin-squeezing criteria. For all fully separable spin
-1/2

\xi^{2}:=\dfrac{N\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}\right]}{|\langle S_{x}\rangle|^{2}}\geq 1\;,

(3)

\xi^{2}

et al.
spin-squeezing coefficient

\xi^{2}<1

S_{x}
direction.

The spin-squeezing coefficient can be considered as a simple Gaussian approximation of the full metrological sensitivity that can be extracted from the quantum state
PS09
;
RMP
;
GessnerPRL2019
. For this reason, states with
\xi^{2}<1
can achieve a quantum enhancement beyond the standard quantum limit in metrology measurements

and the entanglement revealed by this condition is metrologically useful. This approach can be extended to fluctuating particle numbers

, multipartite entanglement

;
GessnerPRA2017
, Bell nonlocality

;
QinNPJQI2019

III
Equivalence of mode and particle entanglement: Two-mode case

In the following we prove that, if the state of the system is symmetric under the exchange of particle labels and of modes, the two criteria (
2
) and (
3
) are equivalent, namely that

\mathcal{G}^{2}=\xi^{2}\;.

(4)

To show this, imagine a system of
i=1,\dots,N
particles with an internal (spin) and an external (mode) dof. We associate to each particle the operator
s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\Pi^{I,(i)}
, where
s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}
is the spin operator along direction
\vec{u}
, and
\Pi^{I,(i)}
is the projection operator of the external dof onto one of the
M=2
modes labeled as
I=1\equiv A
and
I=2\equiv B
.
Let us now assume the following properties valid for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
, and
I,J=1,\dots,M\geq 2
:

(i)

dof’s factorize
: there are no correlations between the spin and the spatial dof,
e.g.
\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle=\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\right\rangle\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\Pi^{I,(i)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(j)}\Pi^{J,(j)}\right\rangle=\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(j)}\right\rangle\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\Pi^{J,(j)}\right\rangle
;

(ii)

particle symmetry
: the state is invariant under permutations of the particle labels,
e.g.
\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\right\rangle=\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(1)}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(j)}\right\rangle=\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(1)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(2)}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Pi^{I,(1)}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\Pi^{J,(j)}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Pi^{I,(1)}\Pi^{J,(2)}\right\rangle
, for
i\neq j
;

(iii)

symmetric splitting
: a) there is equal probability for a particle to be found in any of the modes,
i.e.
\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle=1/M
, and b) these probabilities are independent,
i.e.
\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\Pi^{J,(j)}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle\left\langle\Pi^{J,(j)}\right\rangle
.

Let us mention that these assumptions are relevant for a number of experimental systems. For example, they apply to an ensemble of identical atoms distributed symmetrically in a set of external modes, as in Refs.

;
KunkelSplitBEC
;
LangeSplitBEC

Exploiting assumptions (i) and (ii), we can now compute expectation values of collective spin observables as

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{\vec{u}}^{I}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(i)}\Pi^{I,(i)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Pi^{I}\right\rangle N\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}\right\rangle

(5)

Note that here, and in the following, we use the shorthand notation
\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(1)}\right\rangle=\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\Pi^{I,(1)}\right\rangle=\left\langle\Pi^{I}\right\rangle
.

Similarly, we obtain that correlators take the form (see Appendix
A.1
for details)

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{\vec{u}}^{I}S_{\vec{v}}^{J}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\delta_{I,J}\left\langle\Pi^{I}\right\rangle N\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(1)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(2)}\right\rangle+

\displaystyle+\left\langle\Pi^{I}\right\rangle\left\langle\Pi^{J}\right\rangle N(N-1)\left\langle s_{\vec{u}}^{(1)}s_{\vec{v}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\;.

(6)

To prove now the relation Eq. (

(7)

For the other variance in Eq. (
2

\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}\right]=\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}\right]\;.

(8)

The same holds also for the denominator, which can be written as

\left(|\langle S_{x}^{A}\rangle|+|\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle|\right)^{2}=|\langle S_{x}\rangle|^{2}\;,

(9)

since symmetry implies that
\langle S_{x}^{A}\rangle
and
\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle
have the same sign. It is now straightforward to combine the results of Eqs. (

This result highlights a correspondence of the detected mode entanglement in two addressable modes and the detected particle entanglement in fully symmetric many-body quantum states. In the following we further generalize this criterion to an arbitrary number of modes

IV
Generalization to multipartite entanglement

When considering a collection of systems, entanglement can emerge in different partitions of the ensemble,
i.e.
across any separation of the ensemble into groups of systems. Let us denote one specific partition as
\Lambda=\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k}\}
, where the
\mathcal{A}
’s are non-overlapping groups of
1\leq|\mathcal{A}_{q}|\leq\Xi
systems, such that
\sum_{q=1}^{k}|\mathcal{A}_{q}|=\Xi
. An
\Xi
-partite quantum state
\rho
is called
\Lambda
-separable if it can be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda-\mathrm{sep}}=\sum_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}\rho_{\gamma}^{(\mathcal{A}_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{(\mathcal{A}_{k})}\;,

(10)

where the
\rho_{\gamma}^{(\mathcal{A}_{q})}
are quantum states of the subsystem
\mathcal{A}_{q}
. For an overview of different classes of entangled states in multipartite systems, we refer to Appendix
B
.

IV.1
Inseparability of
M
modes

The entanglement criterion (
2
) can be generalized to yield a criterion for
\Lambda
-separable states of an
M
-mode system as follows

: Any
\Lambda
-separable state must satisfy

\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h})^{2}:=\dfrac{\,\text{Var}\left[\sum_{I=1}^{M}g_{I}S_{z}^{I}\right]\,\text{Var}\left[\sum_{I=1}^{M}h_{I}S_{y}^{I}\right]}{\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h})^{2}}\geq 1\;,

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{q=1}^{l}\left|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}g_{I}h_{I}\langle S^{I}_{z}\rangle\right|\;.

(12)

This bound holds for arbitrary choices of the coefficient vectors
\vec{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{M})
and
\vec{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{M})
, which can be optimized to obtain the strongest possible witness. A violation of Eq. (
11
) witnesses inseparability in the partition
\Lambda
.

We may further exclude separability in all partitions
\Lambda
that contain at most
k
subsystems by observing a violation of the single condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h}):=\max_{\Lambda:\>l\geq k}\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h})\geq 1,

(13)

where the maximization runs over all partitions that consist of
l\geq k
subsystems. A violation of the above bound with
k=M
, where each mode is treated as an individual subsystem, indicates that there is entanglement somewhere in the system without specifying how many subsystems are entangled. If the bound is violated for
k=2
, this means that we cannot identify even two separable groups, and we must thus consider the ensemble of all spins as a single entangled system. We remark here that this criterion analyzes each partition on a one-by-one basis, but it does not exclude arbitrary mixtures of separable models for different partitions, which is known as genuine multipartite entanglement
ReidTeh
;
HyllusEisert
;
GuehneToth
(see Appendix
B
for details).

It is evident that the computation of the bound (
13
) becomes very demanding since the number of possible partitions increases exponentially with
M
. Moreover, identifying a suitable choice for the
\{\vec{g},\vec{h}\}
introduces additional complexity.
A special case of Eq. (
11
) is obtained for the choice of
\{\vec{g},\vec{h}\}
given by

g_{I}^{\ast}=1\;,\qquad h_{1}^{\ast}=1\;,\qquad h_{J}^{\ast}=-\frac{1}{M-1}\;,

(14)

for all

and

.
With this choice we note that
g_{1}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}=1
, and
g_{I}^{\ast}h_{I}^{\ast}=-(M-1)^{-1}
for
I>1
.

Since for the symmetric spin states considered here, the variances in Eq. (

\mathcal{G}_{k}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h})=\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda_{\min}}^{M}(\vec{g},\vec{h})

is the partition that achieves the minimum

\displaystyle\beta^{M}_{k}(\vec{g},\vec{h})=\frac{|\langle S_{z}^{A}\rangle|}{2}\min_{\Lambda:l\geq k}\beta^{M}_{\Lambda}(\vec{g},\vec{h})\;,

(15)

and
\beta^{M}_{\Lambda}(\vec{g},\vec{h}):=\sum_{q=1}^{l}\left|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{A}_{q}}g_{I}h_{I}\right|
. In writing Eq. (

\{\vec{g},\vec{h}\}
. Next, we observe that all contributions of terms from sets with
I>1

whenever they appear in a partition
\Lambda

I=1

\beta^{M}_{\Lambda}(\vec{g}^{*},\vec{h}^{*})
when in a partition

I>1
) and places all other modes (including

\displaystyle\beta^{M}_{\Lambda}(\vec{g}^{*},\vec{h}^{*})

\displaystyle=|g_{1}^{\ast}h_{1}^{\ast}\underbrace{+\dots+g_{M-(k-1)}^{\ast}h_{M-(k-1)}^{\ast}}_{M-k\;\text{terms}}|

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{|g_{M-(k-2)}^{\ast}h_{M-(k-2)}^{\ast}|+\cdots+|g_{M}^{\ast}h_{M}^{\ast}|}_{k-1\;\text{terms}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2(k-1)}{M-1}\;.

In summary, the minimum bound at the denominator of Eq. (

and, using again Eqs. (

IV.3
Multipartite entanglement detection from spin squeezing

IV.3.1
State-independent multipartite entanglement bounds

Spin squeezing provides quantitative bounds on the number of entangled particles from collective measurements. Furthermore, the detected entanglement is relevant for the improvement of measurement precision in quantum metrology. To see this, recall that the quantum Fisher information

V
Relation with two-way EPR steering

For a bipartite scenario (
M=2
) the criterion Eq. (
2
) can be extended to detect also a stronger form of entanglement, namely EPR steering. Specifically, states that do not allow for steering of system
B
by
A
satisfy the condition
CavalcantiReid
;
Bowen
;
ReidRMP

\mathcal{R}^{2}:=\dfrac{4\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}^{A}+S_{z}^{B}\right]\,\text{Var}\left[S_{y}^{A}-S_{y}^{B}\right]}{|\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle|^{2}}\geq 1\;.

(31)

Therefore, a violation of Eq. (
31
) reveals steering of
B
by
A
. A similar criterion holds for steering of
A
by
B
.

In the following we will focus on the symmetric scenario, where measurements in system
A
and
B
yield the same results. In this case we have
\left\langle S_{x}^{A}\right\rangle=\left\langle S_{x}^{B}\right\rangle
which allows us to express the condition (

) equivalently as

\mathcal{R}^{2}=4\mathcal{G}^{2}=4\xi^{2}\geq 1\;.

(32)

Because of symmetry, a violation of this relation directly implies two-way steering between modes
A
and
B
.

Combined with our results from the previous section, we conclude that if we want to observe steering through Eq. (
31
), we need to satisfy the condition

\xi^{2}<1/4
, which implies entanglement of
p>6
particles. However, note that Eq. (
31
) can be generalized by including free coefficients in front of the spin operators, similarly to Eq. (
11
). This allows to detect EPR correlations with less squeezing

, but the correspondence given in Eq. (
32
) is lost.

Interestingly, a bipartite EPR criterion can also be derived from the Sørensen-Mølmer bounds Eq. (
30
). We show in Appendix
F.5
that a violation of

\,\text{Var}[S_{z}]\geq S_{B}\,F_{S_{B}}\left[\dfrac{\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle}{S_{B}}\right]

(33)

implies steering of
B
by
A
. This criterion is easier to violate than the condition
\,\text{Var}[S_{z}]\geq S\,F_{S_{B}}\left[\dfrac{\langle S_{x}^{B}\rangle}{S}\right]
that was derived in Ref.

VI
Discussions and conclusions

In this work we established relations between criteria for multipartite entanglement of particles and modes based on the measurement of first and second moments of collective spin observables. In the case of symmetric spin states, we found that the Wineland
et al.
spin-squeezing coefficient
Wineland
coincides with a witness of mode entanglement that is based on Heisenberg-Robertson-type uncertainty relations with modified bounds
Giovannetti
. This correspondence can be extended to reveal a direct relation between the spin-squeezing coefficient of symmetric spin states and a two-way EPR-steering criterion of two addressable modes.

We further revealed the relation between different multipartite entanglement criteria based on spin squeezing. The Wineland
et al.
spin-squeezing coefficient
Wineland
captures the metrological sensitivity gain and can be used to study multiparticle entanglement. The approach by Sørensen and Mølmer
SorensenMolmer
makes use of the independent knowledge of the spin polarization to derive optimized state-dependent bounds on the spin-squeezing coefficient for multipartite entangled states. Alternatively, state-independent bounds can be derived by exploiting the relation between spin squeezing and the Fisher information
PS09
, but these bounds are not saturable by Gaussian states
HyllusToth
. We addressed this limitation by deriving state-independent bounds that can be asymptotically saturated. This provides the tightest state-independent bounds on the spin-squeezing coefficient for the detection of multipartite entangled states. Interestingly, we observe that these bounds coincide with those of Sørensen and Mølmer
SorensenMolmer
in the limit of vanishing polarization.

Moreover, we identified a simple expression for the maximum spin squeezing that can be achieved locally from the splitting of a squeezed state. Our results provide bounds on the amount of addressable multimode entanglement that can be generated by distributing identical particles into external modes. For example, they apply to nonclassical states of BECs that are split into different spatial modes, as in Refs.
FadelSplitBEC
;
KunkelSplitBEC
;
LangeSplitBEC
.

D.1
Ultimate limit on spin squeezing

For a given, integer spin length

\displaystyle=|S,0\rangle+\lambda\sum_{m\neq 0}\dfrac{\langle m,S|S_{x}|S,0\rangle}{E_{0}-E_{m}}|S,m\rangle

\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{M}
and we decompose the total spin

into its local components as

\displaystyle(2S)\xi^{-2}_{\rho}=\frac{\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle_{\rho}}{\,\text{Var}{[S_{z}]}_{\rho}},

and the absence of correlations in

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I=1}^{M}\left\langle S_{x}^{I}\right\rangle_{\rho_{I}}

\displaystyle\,\text{Var}{[S_{z}]}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I=1}^{M}\,\text{Var}{[S_{x}^{I}]}_{\rho_{I}}.

(59b)

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{I=1}^{M}\left\langle S_{x}^{I}\right\rangle_{\rho_{I}}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{I=1}^{M}\,\text{Var}{[S_{z}^{I}]}_{\rho_{I}}\sum_{I=1}^{M}\frac{\left\langle S_{x}^{I}\right\rangle_{\rho_{I}}^{2}}{\,\text{Var}{[S_{z}^{I}]}_{\rho_{I}}},

\displaystyle(2S)\xi^{-2}_{\rho_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{M}}\leq\sum_{I=1}^{M}(2S_{I})\xi^{-2}_{\rho_{I}}.

(61)

E.2
Limits on spin squeezing for

Consider a separable state of the form

(62a)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}\sum_{I=1}^{M}(2S_{I})\xi^{-2}_{\rho_{\gamma}^{I}}

(62b)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}\sum_{I=1}^{M}(2S_{I})(S_{I}+1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{I=1}^{M}S_{I}^{2}+2S

Relation between the spin-squeezing entanglement witnesses of Wineland
et al.

The Wineland

F.2
Generalization of the Sørensen-Mølmer bound to non-integer

Sørensen and Mølmer proved their criterion for a decomposition of the total spin

F.3
Relation between different spin-squeezing multiparticle entanglement criteria

Since the functions
F_{S}[x]
are strictly increasing, the entanglement criterion (
30
) detects the largest number of entangled states when
\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle
is large.
If, however, the value of
\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle
is not known separately from
\,\text{Var}{[S_{z}]}/\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle^{2}
, we must assume the ‘worst-case scenario’, which consequently is given in the limit of
\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle\rightarrow 0

28
) on the Wineland
et al.

28
) and (
30

65
) and (

Note that the condition (
30

-partite entanglement can be equivalently stated as

\displaystyle\frac{N\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}\right]}{\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\,\frac{F_{S_{p}}\left[\frac{\langle S_{x}\rangle}{S}\right]}{\left(\frac{\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle}{S}\right)^{2}}.

(75)

Making use of properties 2 and 4, we further obtain that

\displaystyle 2\,\frac{F_{S_{p}}\left[\frac{\langle S_{x}\rangle}{S}\right]}{\left(\frac{\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle}{S}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\frac{p}{2}+1}.

(76)

Combining these two bounds, we can derive the multiparticle spin squeezing condition (
28
) in the ‘worst-case’ limit of the approach of Eq. (
30
).

Let us now demonstrate that the same correspondence holds for the most general formulation of the respective criteria for non-integer
N/p
. Using properties 2 and 4 in Eq. (
72
) implies that in the limits
\langle S_{x}^{(i)}\rangle\to 0
and
\langle S_{x}^{(r)}\rangle\to 0
the following condition holds for states with no more than
p

\displaystyle\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\langle S_{x}^{(i)}\rangle\right)^{2}}{N_{p}\frac{p^{2}}{2}+pN_{p}}+\frac{\langle S_{x}^{(r)}\rangle^{2}}{\frac{r^{2}}{2}+r}.

(77)

We now demonstrate that the following statement holds for arbitrary

with

\displaystyle\frac{A^{2}}{a}+\frac{B^{2}}{b}\geq\frac{(A+B)^{2}}{a+b}.

(78)

This relation follows immediately from

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{(Ab-aB)^{2}}{ab(a+b)}=\frac{A^{2}}{a}+\frac{B^{2}}{b}-\frac{(A+B)^{2}}{a+b}.

(79)

Using Eq. (

\displaystyle\,\text{Var}\left[S_{z}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\langle S_{x}\right\rangle^{2}}{N_{p}\frac{p^{2}}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{2}+N},

where we have used that

. Multiplying both sides of (

and dividing by

, we recover the condition (

.

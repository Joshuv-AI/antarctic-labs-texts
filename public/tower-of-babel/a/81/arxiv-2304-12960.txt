Restriction type estimates on general two-step stratified Lie groups Thanks: The author gratefully acknowledges the support by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) through grant MU 761/12-1.
Lars Niedorf

Abstract
. We prove restriction type estimates for sub-Laplacians on general two-step stratified Lie groups. The core of our approach is to use spectral cluster estimates to effectively control the eigenvalue distribution of a family of anisotropic twisted Laplacians.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B15, 22E25, 22E30, 43A85

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Restriction estimates

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface. The concept of Fourier restriction estimates was introduced by Stein in the seventies (for general sub-manifolds), which are a priori estimates of the form

(1.1)

\|\hat{f}|_{S}\|_{L^{q}(S,\sigma)}\leq C_{p}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

where
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
\sigma
is the surface measure on
S
, and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of Schwartz functions.
For the sphere
S=S^{n-1}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|=1\}
and
n\geq 2
, the
restriction conjecture

[
34
,
39
]
states that the restriction estimate
Equation

1.1
holds if and only if

1\leq p<\frac{2n}{n+1}\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq q\leq\frac{n-1}{n+1}p^{\prime},

where
p^{\prime}=(1-1/p)^{-1}
is the dual exponent of
p
. The restriction conjecture for the sphere is solved in dimension
n=2
, but is widely open for
n\geq 3
, although many partial results are available, including work by Bourgain
[
1
]
, Tao
[
37
]
, and Guth
[
11
]
. In the range
1\leq p\leq 2(n+1)/(n+3)
, due to the work of Stein and Tomas
[
35
,
43
]
(see also
[
36
,
10
]
), the restriction conjecture, which is then the estimate

\|\hat{f}|_{S}\|_{L^{2}(S,\sigma)}\leq C_{p}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

is known to hold for the sphere, and also for more general classes of surfaces with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature. (Note that
(n-1)p^{\prime}/(n+1)=2
for the endpoint
p=2(n+1)/(n+3)
. The
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
restriction estimate
Equation

1.1
follows from the
L^{p}
-
L^{2}
estimate via interpolation with the trivial
L^{1}
-
L^{\infty}
estimate.)

It is by now well-known that restriction estimates are closely related to many other problems in harmonic analysis and PDE, such as spectral multiplier estimates and Bochner–Riesz summability for the Laplacian, the Kakeya conjecture, and local smoothing, see
[
38
]
.

Given these close connections for the Laplacian, one hopes that many of these will carry over to elliptic or even sub-elliptic differential operators. One class of operators that has received a lot of attention in the last few decades are second-order differential operators which are a sum of squares of the form

(1.2)

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{k}^{2}),

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
are some vector fields on a smooth manifold
M
. These operators can be seen as a natural replacement for the Laplacian when passing to a sub-elliptic setting. Due to a celebrated theorem of Hörmander
[
12
]
, the operator in (
1.2
) is hypoelliptic whenever the iterated commutators

X_{j_{1}},[X_{j_{1}},X_{j_{2}}],[X_{j_{1}},[X_{j_{2}},X_{j_{3}}]],\dots\quad\text{where }j_{i}\in\{1,\dots,k\}

up to a certain order span the tangent space of the underlying manifold
M
at every point. Rothschild and Stein
[
31
]
pointed out that operators of the form (
1.2
) satisfying Hörmander’s bracket generating condition can be locally approximated by left-invariant sub-Laplacians on stratified Lie groups, after the initial vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
on
M
are lifted to vector fields
\smash{\tilde{X}_{1}},\dots,\smash{\tilde{X}_{k}}
on some larger space
\smash{\tilde{M}}
by some freeing procedure.

When asking for analogs of restriction estimates in the setting of stratified Lie groups, things change dramatically compared to the Euclidean setting. For instance, for the sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group, the only possibility of having restriction estimates is the case where
p=1
(unless one passes to mixed
L^{p}
-norms), which is due to the fact that the Heisenberg group admits a one-dimensional center
[
28
]
. However, the situation improves for left-invariant sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups with center of dimension
d_{2}>1
, where it is possible to prove restriction estimates for the Stein–Tomas range
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
, see
[
41
,
19
]
, but it is not yet known if such restriction estimates hold beyond the class of Heisenberg type groups. Attempts to prove restriction estimates for the larger class of Métivier groups where the group satisfies a certain non-degeneracy condition have unfortunately failed so far (see the remarks in
[
2
]
and
[
30
]
on
[
3
,
20
]
).

1.2.
Statement of the main results

The purpose of this paper is to establish restriction
type
estimates for the whole class of two-step stratified Lie groups.

Before stating the main results, we need to introduce some notation. Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group, that is, a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(which is the tangent space at the identity of
G
) admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
into two non-trivial subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, where
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. In the following, we refer to
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as being the
first
and
second layer
of
\mathfrak{g}
, respectively. Let

(1.3)

d_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}\geq 1,\quad d_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad d=\dim\mathfrak{g}.

Given a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we identify each element of the basis by a left-invariant vector field on
G
via the Lie derivative, and consider the associated sub-Laplacian
L
, which is the second-order differential operator

(1.4)

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

We also choose a basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the inner product rendering
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induces a norm on the dual
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
which we denote by
|\cdot|
. For
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, let
J_{\mu}
be the skew-symmetric endomorphism such that

(1.5)

\mu([x,x^{\prime}])=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle,\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Then
G
is a
Heisenberg type group
if the endomorphisms
J_{\mu}
are orthogonal for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
of length 1, which means that

J_{\mu}^{2}=-|\mu|^{2}\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}.

The restriction type estimates in
Theorem

1.1
are stated in terms of the norms

\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}=\bigg(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{K\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sup_{\lambda\in[\frac{K-1}{M},\frac{K}{M})}|F(\lambda)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\quad M\in(0,\infty)

which were introduced by Cowling and Sikora in
[
7
]
. We introduce an additional truncation along the spectrum of the operator

U=(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2}))^{1/2}.

Due to this additional truncation, we also refer to the corresponding restriction type estimates as
truncated
restriction type estimates. A similar truncation is used in
[
30
]
for sub-Laplacians in Heisenberg type groups, and in
[
29
]
in the related setting of Grushin operators.

Our truncated restriction type theorem reads as follows.
Given any Euclidean space of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we denote by

p_{n}:=\frac{2(n+1)}{n+3}

the
Stein–Tomas exponent
associated with that space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group, and, as in
Equation

1.4
, let
L
be a sub-Laplacian on
G
. Suppose that
1\leq p\leq\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
with dimensions
d_{1},d_{2}
as in (
1.3
). If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in a compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\chi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is a smooth function with compact support, then

(1.6)

\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{A,p,\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|F\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfies
1/p=(1-\theta_{p})+\theta_{p}/\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
.

As we will see later in
Remark

5.2
, one has
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)=0
for
\ell
being small enough in (
1.6
), so
Theorem

1.1
should actually be read as a statement for all
\ell\in[-\ell_{0},\infty)
, where
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is a constant depending on the matrices
J_{\mu}
, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
, and the compact subset
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
above.

Although the above theorem is valid for all two-step stratified Lie groups, the presence of the norm
\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}
in (
1.6
) imposes a slight drawback. By (3.19) and (3.29) of
[
7
]
(or alternatively Lemma 3.4 of
[
4
]
), for
s>1/2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
can be estimated by

(1.7)

\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}\leq C_{s}\big(\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}+M^{-s}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big),

which means that
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
is stronger than the
L^{2}
-norm. Ignoring for a moment the additional truncation, the restriction type estimate (
1.6
) with
\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}
replaced by
\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}
would be equivalent (see for instance
[
32
, Proposition 4.1]
) to a restriction type estimate for the Strichartz projectors
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
, which are formally given by
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}(L)
, where
\delta_{\lambda}
is the Dirac delta distribution at
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. This would recover some of the results of the (erroneous) article
[
3
]
. However, in its current form, (
1.6
) is not sufficient to push restriction estimates beyond the class of Heisenberg type groups.

On the other hand, the truncated restriction type estimates can still be used to prove spectral multiplier estimates for the whole class of Métivier groups (which is the class of two-step stratified Lie groups where the matrices
J_{\mu}
are invertible for all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
), by exploiting the fact that the dimension
d_{1}
of the first layer is in general much larger than the dimension
d_{2}
of the second layer if
G
is a Métivier group. This is done in a follow-up paper.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

In
Section

2
, we briefly sketch the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. In
Section

3
, we analyze the spectral decomposition of the sub-Laplacian
L
and the vector fields
-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}}
, which admit a joint functional calculus. In particular, we show that the sub-Laplacian
L
corresponds to a family of anisotropic twisted Laplacians by a partial Fourier transform along the second layer.

In
Section

4
, we derive spectral cluster estimates for those anisotropic twisted Laplacians, which are then subsequently exploited in
Section

5
to prove the restriction type estimates of
Theorem

1.1
.

1.4.
Notation

We let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. The indicator function of a subset
A
of some measurable space will be denoted by
\mathbf{1}_{A}
. We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for a constant
C
. If
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, we write
A\sim B
. Given two suitable functions
f
and
g
on a two-step stratified Lie group
G
, let
f*g
denote their group convolution given by

f*g(x,u)=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})g\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),\quad(x,u)\in G,

where
d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
G
. The space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
s\geq 0
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
L^{q}_{s}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
the Sobolev space of fractional order
s
.

2.
Sketch of the proof

As in
[
19
,
6
,
29
,
30
]
, the proof of the truncated restriction type estimate in
Theorem

1.1
relies on combining two restriction (type) estimates, namely an
L^{p}
-
L^{2}
estimate for the spectral projections associated with the twisted Laplacian on the first layer, and a restriction estimate for the sphere on the second layer, whence we require
1\leq p\leq\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
as a condition for the range of
p
. More precisely, conjugating the sub-Laplacian
L
by the partial Fourier transform given by

f^{\mu}(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}f(x,u)e^{-i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,du,\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}

transforms the sub-Laplacian
L
into a family
(L^{\mu})_{\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}}
of twisted Laplacians
L^{\mu}
on the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Assuming here for the sake of simplicity that
G
is a Métivier group, then, at least on some non-empty Zariski-open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, each twisted Laplacian
L^{\mu}
admits an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions associated with the eigenvalues

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},\quad\textbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}

where
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
\textbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{N}
are fixed parameters independent of
\mu
, and
\mu\mapsto\textbf{b}^{\mu}=(b_{1}^{\mu},\dots,b_{N}^{\mu})\in(0,\infty)^{N}
is a function homogeneous of degree 1, which is smooth on
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}
and extends to a continuous function on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
.

The special case where
G
is a Heisenberg type group corresponds to taking
N=1
and
\textbf{b}^{\mu}=|\mu|
. Then the eigenvalues of the twisted Laplacian
L^{\mu}
are given by
|\mu|[k]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
[k]:=2k+d_{1}/2
. In
[
19
,
30
]
(and similar in
[
6
,
29
]
), the proofs of the restriction type estimates there rely on
L^{p}
-
L^{2}
spectral projection estimates of the form

(2.1)

\|\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mu|[k]\}}(L^{\mu})\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})}\leq C_{p}\left|\mu\right|^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}[k]^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mu|[k]\}}:\mathbb{R}\to\{0,1\}
denotes the indicator function of the point
|\mu|[k]
.

Writing
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
in polar coordinates, that is,
\mu=\rho\omega
, where
\rho\in[0,\infty)
and
\omega\in S^{d_{2}-1}
, note that
\textbf{b}^{\mu}
does not depend on
\omega
in the Heisenberg type case. To adapt the approach of
[
30
]
to setting of arbitrary two-step Lie groups and handle the dependence on the parameter
\omega
, we employ spectral cluster estimates of the form

(2.2)

\lVert\mathbf{1}_{[K|\mu|,(K+1)|\mu|)}(L^{\mu})\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})}\leq C_{p}\left|\mu\right|^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}(K+1)^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}

for
K\in\mathbb{N}
. These spectral cluster estimates are inspired by the work of Sogge
[
33
, Chapter 5]
. Although the spectral projection estimates (
2.1
) and the spectral cluster estimates (
2.2
) are equivalent in the case of Heisenberg type groups, using spectral cluster estimates in the setting of arbitrary two-step Lie groups leads to restriction type estimates in terms of the Cowling–Sikora norms
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
in place of the
L^{2}
-norm.

3.
Sub-Laplacians on two-step stratified Lie groups

3.1.
Joint functional calculus

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group. Then its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which is the tangent space
T_{e}G
at the identity
e\in G
, admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, where
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Using exponential coordinates, we identify
G
with its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The group multiplication is then given by

(x,u)(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},u+u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}[x,x^{\prime}]\right),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},u,u^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

We choose bases
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
and
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, respectively. By means of these bases, we identify
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the inner product with respect to which
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}},U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
becomes an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. As usual, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
will also be identified with the Lie algebra of smooth left-invariant vector fields on
G
via the Lie derivative. Then, given a smooth function
f
on
G
, we have

\displaystyle X_{j}f(x,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}f\big((x,u)(tX_{j},0)\big)\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{j}}f(x,u)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{2}}\left\langle U_{k},[x,X_{j}]\right\rangle\partial_{u_{k}}f(x,u),

\displaystyle U_{k}f(x,u)

\displaystyle=\partial_{u_{k}}f(x,u).

The sub-Laplacian
L
associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
is given by

L=-\left(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}\right).

For
f\in L^{1}(G)
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, let
f^{\mu}
denote the
\mu
-section of the partial Fourier transform along the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
given by

f^{\mu}(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}f(x,u)e^{-i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,du,\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Up to some constant, this defines an isometry
\mathcal{F}_{2}:L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2})\to L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*})
. Given
f\in L^{2}(G)
, we also write
f^{\mu}=(\mathcal{F}_{2}f)(\cdot,\mu)
(for almost all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
) in the following.
For
f\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, we have
(X_{j}f)^{\mu}=X_{j}^{\mu}f^{\mu}
, where

X_{j}^{\mu}=\partial_{x_{j}}+\tfrac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu}(x,X_{j}),

where
\omega_{\mu}
denotes the bilinear form given by

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Moreover, let
L^{\mu}
be the second order differential operator defined by

L^{\mu}=-\left((X_{1}^{\mu})^{2}+\dots+(X_{d_{1}}^{\mu})^{2}\right).

We call
L^{\mu}
the
\mu
-twisted Laplacian
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Direct computation shows that

(Lf)^{\mu}=L^{\mu}f^{\mu}=\left(-\Delta_{x}+\tfrac{1}{4}|J_{\mu}x|^{2}-i\,\omega_{\mu}(x,\nabla)\right)f^{\mu},

where
J_{\mu}
is again the endomorphism given by

\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},

and
\omega_{\mu}(x,\nabla)
is a short-hand notation for the operator

\omega_{\mu}(x,\nabla)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\omega_{\mu}(x,X_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}.

The operators
L,-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}}
form a system of formally self-adjoint, left-invariant and pairwise commuting differential operators, whence they admit a joint functional calculus
[
25
]
. Let
U
be the vector of differential operators

\mathbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}}).

Since the joint functional calculus is compatible with unitary representation theory, the
\mu
-sections of the partial Fourier transform and the joint functional calculus of
L
and
U
are compatible as well.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function, then

(3.1)

\left(F(L,\mathbf{U})f\right)^{\mu}=F(L^{\mu},\mu)f^{\mu}

for all
f\in L^{2}(G)
and almost all
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
.

Proof.

This follows from the arguments of
[
28
, Section 1]
. Let
\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}))
denote the group of unitary operators on
L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
. Applying Proposition 1.1 of
[
28
]
to the unitary representation
\pi_{\mu}:G\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}))
given by

\big(\pi_{\mu}(x,u)\varphi\big)(x^{\prime})=e^{-i\langle\mu,u\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})}\varphi(x^{\prime}-x),\quad(x,u)\in G,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}

yields
Equation

3.1
if
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
is of the form
F(\lambda,\mu)=G(\lambda)H(\mu)
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
. By a standard density argument, we obtain
Equation

3.1
for any bounded Borel function
F
.
∎

3.2.
Decomposition into blocks of twisted Laplacians

Next, we transform the twisted Laplacian
L^{\mu}
into a more accessible form by choosing an appropriate basis with respect to which
L^{\mu}
turns into a differential operator consisting of blocks of rescaled twisted Laplacians with standard symplectic form.

Definition 3.2
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
\lambda>0
, we call the operator

\Delta^{(\lambda)}_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}|z|^{2}-i\lambda\omega(z,\nabla_{z}),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}

the
\lambda
-twisted Laplacian
on
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
, where

(3.2)

\omega(z,w)=\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}(z,w)=\langle Jz,w\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}

is the standard symplectic form induced by the
2m\times 2m
matrix

J=J_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\\
\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Given parameters
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{N})\in[0,\infty)^{N}
and

3.3.

Let again

[
28
]

\big(F\big(\Delta^{\mathbf{b}^{\mu},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\big)f\big)^{(\tau)}=F\big(|\tau|^{2}+\Delta^{\mathbf{b}^{\mu},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}\big)f^{(\tau)}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}})

and almost all

Remark 3.9

In general,

Proof.

We briefly recall the spectral properties of twisted Laplacians. We refer the reader to

for further details. For

where

and
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
, let

be the

\Phi_{\nu}^{\lambda}(\xi)=\lambda^{m/4}\prod_{j=1}^{m}h_{\nu_{j}}(\lambda^{1/2}\xi_{j}),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

where

shall denote the

-th Hermite function on

(as, for instance, defined in
[

[

that the matrix coefficients

given by

(3.14)

\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda}(z)=(2\pi)^{-m/2}\lambda^{m/2}\big(\pi_{\lambda}(z,0)\Phi_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\Phi_{\beta}^{\lambda}\big),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}

form a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions of the

-twisted Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}^{(\lambda)}\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda}=\left(2\left|\beta\right|_{1}+m\right)\lambda\,\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda}.

Thus, for

, the orthogonal projection associated with the eigenvalue

The projection
P_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}
can be written in a more explicit form via the
\lambda
-twisted convolution given by

f\times_{(\lambda,m)}g(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}f(w)g(z-w)e^{\frac{i}{2}\lambda\,\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}(z,w)}\,dw,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2m},

where
\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}
shall again denote the standard symplectic form of
Equation

3.2
. Since

\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\lambda}(z)=\lambda^{m/2}\,\Phi^{1}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}(\lambda^{1/2}z),

the identities (1.3.41) and (1.3.42) of
[
42
, pp. 21]
imply

(3.16)

\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}(z)=(2\pi)^{m/2}\lambda^{m/2}\sum_{|\nu|_{1}=k}\Phi^{\lambda}_{\nu,\nu}(z).

Hence, by (2.1.5) of
[
42
, p. 30]
,
P_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}
may be rewritten as

(3.17)

P_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}f=f\times_{(\lambda,m)}\varphi_{k}^{(\lambda,m)}.

Writing down the eigenfunctions of the anisotropic twisted Laplacian
\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}
is now immediate, as the functions

\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}=\Phi_{\nu^{(1)},(\nu^{\prime})^{(1)}}^{(b_{1})}\otimes\dots\otimes\Phi_{\nu^{(N)},(\nu^{\prime})^{(N)}}^{(b_{N})}

with
\nu=(\nu^{(1)},\dots,\nu^{(N)}),\nu^{\prime}=\big((\nu^{\prime})^{(1)},\dots,(\nu^{\prime})^{(N)}\big)\in\mathbb{N}^{r_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{N}^{r_{N}}=\mathbb{N}^{\bar{d}_{1}/2}
form a complete orthonormal system of
\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}
, with

\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\,\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}=\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big(2\,|(\nu^{\prime})^{(n)}|_{1}+r_{n}\big)b_{n}\bigg)\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}.

Hence
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}})
decomposes into eigenspaces of
\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}
. Using
Equation

3.17
on every block shows that the projections defined by

\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}f=f\times_{(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r})}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}

satisfy

\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{\bar{d}_{1}/2}}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\in A_{\textbf{k}}}\big(f,\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}\big)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}},

where
A_{\textbf{k}}
is the set of all
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{\bar{d}_{1}/2}
such that
|(\nu^{\prime})^{(n)}|_{1}=k_{n}
for all
1\leq n\leq N
. Hence
\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}
projects onto a subspace of the eigenspace of
\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}
that is associated with the eigenvalue

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n},\quad\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N},

which finishes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Convolution kernels

Recall that the sub-Laplacian

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})

and the vector
\textbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}})
of differential operators, where
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
is the chosen basis of the second layer of the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, admit a joint functional calculus which is compatible with the partial Fourier transform along
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
by
Proposition

3.1
. For suitable functions
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
, the operator
F(L,\mathbf{U})
possesses a convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}
, that is,

F(L,\mathbf{U})f=f*\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(G).

As in
[
21
, Corollary 8]
, we show that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}
can be explicitly written down in terms of the Fourier transform and rescaled Laguerre functions. To that end, recall that
Proposition

3.4
yields the spectral decomposition

-J_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(b_{n}^{\mu})^{2}P_{n}^{\mu}

for all
\mu
in the Zariski open subset
\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
of
Proposition

3.4
. We use the notation of
Proposition

3.4
in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.10
.

If
F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
is a Schwartz function, then
F(L,\mathbf{U})
possesses a convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}\in\mathcal{S}(G)
. For
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}(x,u)=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-r_{0}-d_{2}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\tau|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu},\mu)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\varphi_{k_{n}}^{(b_{n}^{\mu},r_{n})}(R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{n}^{\mu}x)\bigg]e^{i\langle\tau,R_{\mu}^{-1}P_{0}^{\mu}x\rangle}\,e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\tau\,d\mu,

where
P_{0}^{\mu}=\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\left(P_{1}^{\mu}+\dots+P_{N}^{\mu}\right)
and

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu}.

Proof.

Since
F
is a Schwartz function, we have
\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}\in\mathcal{S}(G)
due to a result of Hulanicki
[
14
]
(see also
[
24
, Proposition 4.2.1]
). The formula for
\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}
can be proved as in
[
26
, Proposition 4]
by using the Fourier inversion formula of the group Fourier transform on
G
and computing the matrix coefficients of the Schrödinger representations, but can also be derived as follows. Recall from
Proposition

3.4
that

P_{n}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}P_{n}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}\text{ and }n\in\{0,\dots,N\},

where
P_{n}
denotes the projection from
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
onto the
n
-th layer. Let
\bar{P}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}-P_{0}
and
\bar{P}^{\mu}=\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-P_{0}^{\mu}
. Then

\bar{P}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}\bar{P}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}.

Using the Fourier inversion formula,
Propositions

3.1
and
3.4
, and
Equation

3.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{r_{0}+d_{2}}F(L,\textbf{U})f(x,u)=(2\pi)^{r_{0}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}}F(L^{\mu},\mu)f^{\mu}(x)e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{r_{0}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\big(F(\Delta^{\mathbf{b}^{\mu},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}},\mu)(f^{\mu}\circ R_{\mu})\big)(R_{\mu}^{-1}x)e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\big(F(|\tau|^{2}+\Delta^{\mathbf{b}^{\mu},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}},\mu)f^{(\tau,\mu)}\big)(\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x)\,e^{i\langle\tau,P_{0}R_{\mu}^{-1}x\rangle}e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\tau\,d\mu,

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}F(|\tau|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu},\mu)\big(\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}f^{(\tau,\mu)}\big)(\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x)

(3.19)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\times e^{i\langle\tau,P_{0}R_{\mu}^{-1}x\rangle}e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\tau\,d\mu,

where we put

f^{(\tau,\mu)}=(f^{\mu}\circ R_{\mu})^{(\tau)}.

By
Proposition

3.8
, we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\big(\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}f^{(\tau,\mu)}\big)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}f^{(\tau,\mu)}(z)\,\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}(y-z)\,E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}(y,z)\,dz,

where
E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}
is given by

E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}(y,z)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\exp\big(\tfrac{i}{2}\,b_{n}\,\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}\big(y^{(n)},z^{(n)}\big)\big),

with
y=(y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(N)}),z=(z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(N)})\in\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
.

Unboxing the definition of
f^{(\tau,\mu)}
yields

(3.21)

f^{(\tau,\mu)}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}f\big(R_{\mu}(t,z),u^{\prime}\big)\,e^{-i\langle\tau,t\rangle}e^{-i\langle\mu,u^{\prime}\rangle}\,du^{\prime}dt.

Note that

\displaystyle f*\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{F(L,\mathbf{U})}\big(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}[x,x^{\prime}]\big)\,d(x,u).

Inserting
Equation

3.20
and
Equation

3.21
into the last line of
Equation

3.19
and rearranging the order of integration yields the expression

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}

\displaystyle f\big(R_{\mu}(t,z),u^{\prime}\big)\,F(|\tau|^{2}+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu},\mu)\,\,

\displaystyle\times\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{b^{\mu}},\mathbf{r}}(\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x-z)\,E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}(\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x,z)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\langle\tau,P_{0}R_{\mu}^{-1}x-t\rangle}e^{i\langle\mu,u-u^{\prime}\rangle}\,d\tau\,d\mu\,du^{\prime}dt\,dz.

Recall that
Equation

3.4
asserts

\omega_{\mu}(P_{n}^{\mu}x,P_{n}^{\mu}x^{\prime})=b_{n}^{\mu}\,\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}(P_{n}R_{\mu}^{-1}x,P_{n}R_{\mu}^{-1}x^{\prime}).

Since
\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle
and

J_{\mu}(\ran P_{n}^{\mu})\subseteq\ran P_{n}^{\mu}

by
Proposition

3.4
, we have

\omega_{\mu}(P_{n}^{\mu}x,P_{n^{\prime}}^{\mu}x^{\prime})=0\quad\text{for }n\neq n^{\prime}.

This yields

\displaystyle E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}(\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x,\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\exp\big(\tfrac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu}(\bar{P}^{\mu}x,\bar{P}^{\mu}x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=\exp\big(\tfrac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})\big).

Hence, substituting
x^{\prime}=R_{\mu}(t,z)
in the formula above, that is,

t=P_{0}R_{\mu}^{-1}x^{\prime}\quad\text{ and }\quad z=\bar{P}R_{\mu}^{-1}x^{\prime},

and using
\bar{P}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}\bar{P}
and
P_{0}^{\mu}R_{\mu}=R_{\mu}P_{0}
, we obtain
Proposition

3.10
.
∎

4.
Spectral cluster estimates

Throughout this section, we fix
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{N}
with
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and consider the anisotropic twisted Laplacians

\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}})\oplus\Delta^{(b_{1})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}}\oplus\dots\oplus\Delta^{(b_{N})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}}

for parameters
\textbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{N})
ranging in a compact subset
B
of
[0,\infty)^{N}
. In the following, we deviate slightly from the notation of the previous section (where we generally denoted coordinates on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
by
x
and coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
under the orthogonal transformation
R_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of
Proposition

3.4
by
y
) and will denote the coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
again by
x
in this section.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we again write
p_{n}=2(n+1)/(n+3)
for the Stein–Tomas threshold. The main result of this section are the following spectral cluster estimates, which are inspired by
[
33
, Chapter 5]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Spectral cluster estimates)
.

Let
\mathbf{B}\subseteq[0,\infty)^{N}
be a compact subset. If
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{1}}
, then

(4.1)

\lVert\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r},p}\left(K+1\right)^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}

for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{B}
.

We give two proofs of these estimates. The first one relies on a Mehler type formula and subordination by the heat semigroup associated with the anisotropic twisted Laplacian, but only works for the smaller range
1\leq p<p_{d_{1}-1}
.

4.1.
First proof via subordination by the heat semigroup

Proposition 4.2
.

The cluster estimate (
4.1
) holds true for
1\leq p<p_{d_{1}-1}
.

The proof of
Proposition

4.2
is essentially that of Proposition II.8 of
[
5
]
combined with a Mehler type formula. However, the uniformity of the constants is crucial here, so we give full details.

Proof.

Given
\textbf{b}\in[0,\infty)^{N}
, the heat semigroup

T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(t)=\exp(-t\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}),\quad t\geq 0

admits an analytic extension
\zeta\mapsto T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(\zeta)=\exp(-\zeta\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})
to the complex right half-plane. By
[
23
, Proposition 4]
, it can be written as a twisted convolution with the heat kernel
p^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\zeta}
, which is given by

\displaystyle p^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\zeta}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\zeta)^{d_{1}/2}}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{4\zeta}\big|x^{(0)}\big|^{2}\Big)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{S}(i\zeta b_{n})^{r_{n}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{4\zeta}\,\mathrm{T}(i\zeta b_{n})\big|x^{(n)}\big|^{2}\Big),

where
x=(x^{(0)},\dots,x^{(N)}),x^{\prime}=((x^{\prime})^{(0)},\dots,(x^{\prime})^{(N)})\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}
, and

\mathrm{S},\mathrm{T}:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{k\pi:0\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}\}\to\mathbb{C}

are the meromorphic functions given by

\mathrm{S}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\sin\zeta}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{T}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{\tan\zeta}.

Then, if

E^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}(x,x^{\prime})=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\exp\big(\tfrac{i}{2}\,b_{n}\,\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}\big(x^{(n)},(x^{\prime})^{(n)}\big)\big),

the operator
T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(\zeta)
is given by

(4.3)

T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(\zeta)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}f(x^{\prime})p^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\zeta}(x-x^{\prime})E^{(\textbf{b},\textbf{r})}(x,x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}.

Note that the parameters
b_{n}
are actually allowed to be zero, in which case the twisted convolution is just the Euclidean convolution on the
n
-th block. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{T}(i\zeta)

\displaystyle=\coth\zeta=\frac{\sinh(2\operatorname{Re}\zeta)-i\sin(2\operatorname{Im}\zeta)}{2\left|\sinh\zeta\right|^{2}},

whence

\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{\zeta}\,\mathrm{T}(i\zeta)\Big)\geq 0\quad\text{if }\operatorname{Re}\zeta\geq 0.

Thus, neglecting all oscillations of the twisted convolution above,
Equation

4.3
yields

(4.4)

\lVert T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(\zeta)f\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{1}\lVert p^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\zeta}\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\,|\zeta|^{-d_{1}/2}\lVert f\rVert_{1}

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
lying in the rectangle

R=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:0<\operatorname{Re}\zeta\leq 1\text{ and }|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|\leq\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\},

where the constant
\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
is chosen small enough such that
\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}<\pi/b_{n}
for all
\textbf{b}\in\textbf{B}
and all
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Interpolating between
Equation

4.4
and the trivial
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
estimate, we get

(4.5)

\lVert T^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}(\zeta)\rVert_{p\to p^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}|\zeta|^{-\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}\quad\text{for all }\zeta\in R\text{ and }1\leq p\leq 2.

Given
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\widehat{F_{K}}(\xi)=(1-|\xi|)_{+}^{2}\,e^{-iK\xi},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
F_{K}
is given by

F_{K}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\frac{\lambda-K-\sin(\lambda-K)}{(\lambda-K)^{3}},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular,
F_{K}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a positive function with
\supp\widehat{F_{K}}=[-1,1]
. Put

F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}(\lambda)=F_{K}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda\big)\quad\text{and}\quad G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}(\lambda)=F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}(\lambda)\,e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda/(K+1)}.

Note that there is some constant
C_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
independent of
K
such that

\inf_{\lambda\in[K,K+1)}G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}(\lambda)\geq C_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}.

This implies

C_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\,\lVert\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})f\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})f\rVert_{2}.

Using a
TT^{*}
argument, we obtain

(4.6)

C_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}^{2}\,\big\|\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\leq\big\|(G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}})^{2}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}.

Note that

\supp\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}\subseteq[-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}},\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}].

The Fourier inversion formula yields

(4.7)

(G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}})^{2}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}^{\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}*\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}(\xi)\,e^{-(\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}/(K+1)-i\xi)\lambda}\,d\xi.

Moreover, note that there is a constant
\tilde{C}_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
independent of
K
such that

(4.8)

\big\|\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}*\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\tilde{C}_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}.

Altogether,
Equation

4.6
,
Equation

4.8
and
Equation

4.5
yield

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\big\|(G_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}})^{2}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}^{\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}\big|\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}*\widehat{F_{K}^{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}}(\xi)\big|\,\big\|e^{-(\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}/(K+1)-i\xi)\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}}\big\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\alpha_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}^{2}(K+1)^{-2}+\xi^{2})^{-\frac{d_{1}}{4}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\sim_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}(K+1)^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})-1}.

Note that the last integral converges since
1\leq p<p_{d_{1}-1}
. Hence

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{p\to 2}\leq C_{\textbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\left(K+1\right)^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}},

which is (
4.1
).
∎

4.2.
Second proof via the dispersive estimates of Koch and Tataru

The proof of
Equation

4.1
for the full range
1\leq p\leq p_{d_{1}}
relies on interpolation between
p=1
and the endpoint
p=p_{d_{1}}
. For
p=1
, we just resort to our previous result. Let
\mathbf{B}\subseteq[0,\infty)^{N}
be the compact subset of
Theorem

4.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The cluster estimate (
4.1
) holds true for
p=1
, i.e.,

(4.9)

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{[K,K+1)}(\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}})\big\|_{1\to 2}\leq C_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\left(K+1\right)^{\frac{d_{1}}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}

for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{B}
.

Proof.

Note that
d_{1}=r_{0}+2\left|\textbf{r}\right|_{1}\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
. We distinguish the cases
d_{1}=2
and
d_{1}>2
. If
d_{1}>2
, then
p_{d_{1}-1}=2d_{1}/(d_{1}+2)>1
, and the statement follows directly from
Proposition

4.2
. If
d_{1}=2
, we have
r_{0}=0
,
N=1
,
\textbf{r}=1
, and
\textbf{b}=b_{1}
, so the anisotropic twisted Laplacian admits only one block

\Delta^{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}=\Delta^{(b_{1})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}.

By

\frac{\partial x^{1}}{\partial\xi}=\frac{\partial x^{t}}{\partial\xi}\bigg|_{t=1}=\tilde{a}_{\xi\xi}(0,x,\xi)+O(\sqrt{t_{0}}),

where
\tilde{a}_{\xi\xi}
denotes the Hessian matrix of
\xi\mapsto\tilde{a}(0,x,\xi)
. Now the curvature condition of (ii) yields that
\tilde{a}_{\xi\xi}(0,x,\xi)
admits a non-degenerate
(n-1-k)
-minor, whence one may locally choose coordinates
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime\prime})
with
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-1-k})
so that the matrix
\tilde{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}(0,x,\xi)
is non-degenerate. As in
[
18
, p. 242]
, estimating the integral
Equation

4.30
in
\xi^{\prime\prime}
via the support of
C\tilde{B}
and in
\xi^{\prime}
via
Equation

4.31
, we get

\int_{C\tilde{B}}(1+|x^{t}-y|)^{-N}\,d\xi\lesssim\mu^{k},

which yields
Equation

4.29
and thus
Equation

4.27
.
∎

With
Theorem

4.6
at hand, we now prove
Lemma

4.5
, which completes the proof of the spectral cluster estimates of
Theorem

4.1
. Recall that
\mathbf{B}\subseteq[0,\infty)^{N}
denotes the compact subset of
Theorem

4.1
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.5
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
denote the open balls of radius 1 and 2 centered at the origin.
We have to show that there is some
\gamma=\gamma_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}>0
such that, for all
\textbf{b}\in\textbf{B}
and all
\kappa\geq 1
, we have

(4.32)

\kappa^{2/(d_{1}+1)}\|u|_{B_{1}}\|_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\leq C_{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{r}}\,\kappa\,\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}+\kappa^{-1}\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}

whenever
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
satisfy

\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}^{\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\mathbf{b},\mathbf{r}}-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{4}\big)u=f.

Let
\kappa\geq 1
. The parameter
\gamma=\gamma_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
will be chosen later in the proof. Recall that
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{N})\in[0,\infty)^{N}
,
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and

\Delta^{\mathbf{\gamma}^{2}\kappa^{2}\textbf{b},\mathbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}-2|\textbf{r}|_{1}}})\oplus\Delta^{(\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}b_{1})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}}\oplus\dots\oplus\Delta^{(\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}b_{N})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}}.

By definition, on each block, we have

\Delta^{(\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}b_{n})}_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}\left(\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}b_{n}\right)^{2}|z|^{2}-i\left(\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}b_{n}\right)\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}(z,\nabla_{z}),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}},

where
\omega_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}(z,w)=\langle J_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}z,w\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}
is the standard symplectic form induced by

J_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{n}}}\\
\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{r_{n}}}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2r_{n}\times 2r_{n}}.

In the Weyl calculus, the symbol
\sigma^{\textbf{b}}=\sigma_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}
of
\Delta^{\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{4}
is given by

\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=\left|\xi+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}x\right|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{4},\quad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},

where

(4.33)

J^{\textbf{b}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\dots&0\\
0&b_{1}J_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{1}}}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\dots&0&b_{N}J_{\mathbb{R}^{2r_{N}}}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{1}}.

In the following, given
\lambda>0
, we use again the notation

B^{\lambda}=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}:|x|<1\text{ and }|\xi|<\lambda\}.

We decompose the phase space into a non-elliptic region, where we will apply
Theorem

4.6
, and an elliptic region, where even better estimates are available.

(1)
The non-elliptic region.

We apply
Theorem

4.6
with parameters

k=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\kappa^{2}.

Let
\eta_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\eta_{0}(x,\xi)=1
for
(x,\xi)\in B^{1}
and
\eta_{0}(x,\xi)=0
for
(x,\xi)\notin 2B^{1}
. We define
\eta
via

\eta(x,\xi)=\eta_{0}(x,\xi/\lambda).

Let
p^{\textbf{b}}
and
a^{\textbf{b}}
be the symbols given by

p^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=\lambda^{-1}\big(\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\eta\big)(x,\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad a^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=\lambda^{-2}\big(\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\eta\big)(x,\xi).

Since
\sigma^{\textbf{b}}
is a polynomial of degree 2, we have

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=0\quad\text{for }|\alpha|+|\beta|>2.

Moreover, for all
(x,\xi)\in B^{\lambda}
and
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 2
,

\lambda^{-2}\,\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\big|=\bigg|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\bigg(\Big|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}\,x\Big|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\bigg)\bigg|\\
\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},\alpha,\beta}\lambda^{-|\beta|}.

Hence, via Leibniz rule, we have in particular

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\begin{cases}\lambda^{-|\beta|}&\text{for }|\alpha|\leq 2,\\
\lambda^{\frac{|\alpha|-2}{2}-|\beta|}&\text{for }|\alpha|>2.\end{cases}

Thus,
p^{\textbf{b}}=\lambda a^{\textbf{b}}\in\lambda S_{\lambda}^{2}
, which matches the requirements of
Theorem

4.6
.

Next, to apply
Theorem

4.6
, we verify the conditions (i) and (ii) from there. We consider
\Sigma=\chr p^{\textbf{b}}\cap B^{\lambda}
. For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
, the set

\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}:\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=0\}

is a sphere of radius
\gamma\kappa^{2}=\gamma\lambda
with center
-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}x=-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\lambda J^{\textbf{b}}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. Note that
\eta|_{B^{\lambda}}=1
. Thus, by choosing
\gamma=\gamma_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
small enough, we can ensure that

\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}:\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=0\text{ and }|x|<1\}\subseteq B^{\lambda}

and that for
|x|<1
the
x
-section
\Sigma_{x}
is the whole sphere of radius
\gamma\lambda
with center
-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\lambda J^{\textbf{b}}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. For condition (i) of
Theorem

4.6
, we observe that
(x,\xi)\in\Sigma
implies that
\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=0
since
\eta|_{B^{\lambda}}=1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\xi}\,p^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\left|\left(\nabla_{\xi}\,\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\right)\eta(x,\xi)+\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\,\nabla_{\xi}\,\eta(x,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{-1}\left|\xi-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\lambda J^{\textbf{b}}x\right|=2\gamma\qquad\text{for all }(x,\xi)\in\Sigma.

On the other hand, condition (ii) of
Theorem

4.6
follows from the fact that for
|x|<1
the sphere
\Sigma_{x}
has
d_{1}-1
non-vanishing curvatures. Note that the required bound associated with the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{x}
in condition (ii) actually does not depend on
\textbf{b}\in\textbf{B}
.

Now, for
k=0
, the exponents
q
and
\rho(k)
in
Equation

4.20
are given by

q=\frac{2(d_{1}+1)}{d_{1}-1}=p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(q)=\frac{d_{1}-1}{2(d_{1}+1)}.

To show
Equation

4.32
, suppose that
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
satisfy

\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}^{\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{4}\big)u=f.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth cut-off function with
\psi|_{B_{1}}=1
and compact support in
B_{2}
. Then, applying
Theorem

4.6
with
k=0
and
\lambda=\kappa^{2}
yields

\lambda^{-\rho(q)}\lVert\chi^{w}(\psi u)\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lVert\psi u\rVert_{2}+\lVert(p^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\rVert_{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
\lambda^{1/2}
, we obtain

(4.34)

\lambda^{1/(d_{1}+1)}\lVert\chi^{w}(\psi u)\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{1/2}\|u|_{B_{2}}\|_{2}+\lambda^{1/2}\lVert(p^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\rVert_{2}.

We show that

(4.35)

\lambda^{1/2}\lVert(p^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\rVert_{2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{1/2}\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}+\lambda^{-1/2}\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Recall that
p^{\textbf{b}}=\lambda^{-1}\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\eta
. We want to use the calculus of pseudo-differential operators. We use the symbol classes
S^{m}
, which are defined as follows: Given
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
p_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},\mathbb{C})
lies in
S^{m}
if

(4.36)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}p_{0}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\alpha|}\quad\text{for all }x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}},\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d_{1}},

where
\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
. (Note that we have changed from
\smash{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}}
to
\smash{\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}}
in our notation compared to the definition of the symbol classes
S^{j}_{\lambda}
of
[
18
]
.) If
p_{0}\in S^{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
p_{0}\in S^{-\infty}
.

Note that the symbols
\sigma^{\textbf{b}}
and
\eta
satisfy the bounds

\displaystyle\lambda^{-2}\,\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)\big|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\bigg(\Big|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}x\Big|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{2-|\alpha|}\lambda^{-|\alpha|}\quad\text{for }|x|\leq 1

and

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\eta(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{-M-|\alpha|}\lambda^{-|\alpha|}\quad\text{for all }M\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular, we have
\lambda^{-2}\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\in S^{2}
and
\eta\in S^{-\infty}
. Now, the calculus of pseudo-differential operators yields

(4.37)

\lambda^{-2}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\eta)^{w}=\lambda^{-2}\eta^{w}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}+(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w},

where the symbol
\tau^{\textbf{b}}\in S^{-\infty}
satisfies the bounds

(4.38)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\tau^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},M,\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{-M-|\alpha|-1}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-1}.

For details, see Section 3 of Chapter 2 in
[
9
]
and in particular Theorem 2.49 of
[
9
, p. 107]
. The bound
Equation

4.38
actually follows from an inspection of the proof in
[
9
]
. Note that we can pass to the frequency scale
\lambda=1
by rescaling via
(x,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda x,\xi/\lambda)
.

We derive
L^{2}
bounds for the operators
\eta^{w}
and
(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
following the arguments of the proof of Corollary 2.56 of
[
9
, p. 112]
. (Note that this is essentially the statement of the Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem, but we give a direct argument here since our symbols are sufficiently regular.) Let
K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}
denote the integral kernel of
(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
, that is,

K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-d_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\tau^{\textbf{b}}(\tfrac{1}{2}(x+y),\xi)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi.

Then, via integration by parts,

(x-y)^{\alpha}K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)=C_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\tau^{\textbf{b}}(\tfrac{1}{2}(x+y),\xi)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi.

Using
Equation

4.38
, we obtain

\displaystyle|(x-y)^{\alpha}K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},M,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{-M-|\alpha|-1}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\sim\lambda^{-|\alpha|+d_{1}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\big\langle\tilde{\xi}\big\rangle^{-M-|\alpha|-1}\,d\tilde{\xi}.

Since the last integral converges if we choose
M>0
large enough, we get

|K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)|\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},M,\beta}\left(\lambda\left|x-y\right|\right)^{-\beta}\lambda^{d_{1}-1}\quad\text{for all }\beta\geq 0.

Thus, Young’s convolution inequality shows that
(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
is bounded on
L^{2}
with

(4.39)

\|(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{-1}.

Similarly,
\eta^{w}
is bounded on
L^{2}
with

(4.40)

\|\eta^{w}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 1.

Hence, the left-hand side of
Equation

4.35
can be dominated by

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/2}\|(p^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1/2}\|(\sigma^{\textbf{b}}\eta)^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1/2}\|\eta^{w}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}+\lambda^{3/2}\|(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}

(4.41)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{-1/2}\|\eta^{w}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}+\lambda^{1/2}\|u|_{B_{2}}\|_{2}.

To prove
Equation

4.35
, it remains to bound the first summand of
Equation

4.41
. Recall that
\lambda=\kappa^{2}
and
(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}u=f
by assumption, where

(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}=\Delta^{\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\textbf{b},\textbf{r}}_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}-\gamma^{2}\kappa^{4}.

We choose a smooth cut-off function
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}|_{\supp\psi}=1
and compact support in
B_{2}
. Via Leibniz rule, we obtain

(4.42)

(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)=f\psi-u\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}+i\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\langle J^{\textbf{b}}\cdot\,,\nabla\rangle\right)\psi-\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle,

where
J^{\textbf{b}}
is the
d_{1}\times d_{1}
matrix of
Equation

4.33
. For the first two summands, using that
\psi
is supported in
B_{2}
, we observe that
Equation

4.40
implies

(4.43)

\|\eta^{w}(f\psi)\|_{2}\lesssim\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}

and, since
\kappa^{2}=\lambda
, we also have

(4.44)

\big\|\eta^{w}\big(u\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}+i\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\langle J^{\textbf{b}}\cdot\,,\nabla\rangle\right)\psi\big)\big\|_{2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda\,\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Let
\tilde{u}:=u\tilde{\psi}
. Then, since
\tilde{\psi}|_{\supp\psi}=1
, we have

\eta^{w}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\eta^{w}((\partial_{j}\psi)\partial_{j}\tilde{u}).

Note that the
j
-th summand on the right-hand side is the concatenation of the pseudo-differential operators associated with the symbols
\eta
,
(x,\xi)\mapsto\partial_{j}\psi(x)
and
(x,\xi)\mapsto i\xi_{j}
. Note that
\eta\in S^{-\infty}
,
\partial_{j}\psi\in S^{0}
, and
\big((x,\xi)\mapsto i\xi_{j}\big)\in S^{1}
. Since the concatenation of pseudo-differential operators is the pseudo-differential operator associated with their twisted product, we have

\eta^{w}((\partial_{j}\psi)\partial_{j}\tilde{u})=\omega_{j}^{w}\tilde{u},

where
\omega_{j}\in S^{-\infty}
satisfies the bounds

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\omega_{j}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{-M-|\alpha|+1}\lambda^{-|\alpha|+1},

see
[
9
, Theorem 2.47]
. Similar arguments as we used for
Equation

4.39
show that

\|\omega_{j}^{w}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\lambda.

Thus, we obtain

(4.45)

\|\eta^{w}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda\,\|\tilde{u}\|_{2}\lesssim_{\tilde{\psi}}\lambda\,\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}

Combining the estimates
Equation

4.43
,
Equation

4.44
, and
Equation

4.45
, we get

\|\eta^{w}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\psi u)\|_{2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}+\lambda\,\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Together with with
Equation

4.41
, we obtain
Equation

4.35
. Then, using
Equation

4.34
together with
Equation

4.35
finally yields

\lambda^{1/(d_{1}+1)}\left\lVert\chi^{w}(\psi u)\right\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{1/2}\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}+\lambda^{-1/2}\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}.

Thus, we are done with the proof once we have verified that

(4.46)

\lambda^{1/(d_{1}+1)}\left\lVert(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\right\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{1/2}\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\rVert_{2}+\lambda^{-1/2}\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\rVert_{2}.

(2)
The elliptic region.
The estimate
Equation

4.46
follows from the ellipticity in the region of the phase space where
|x|\leq 1
and
|\xi|\geq\lambda
. Even though this estimate may not be surprising to experts, we provide a brief sketch of a proof for the convenience of the reader. The following arguments are essentially borrowed from
[
9
, Chapter 2]
, but they can also be found in other places in the literature, for instance
[
40
,
33
]
.

First, by choosing the constant
\gamma=\gamma_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}>0
smaller if necessary, we can assume that
\gamma<1/10
and

\Big|\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}x\Big|\leq\frac{1}{10}\,|x|\quad\text{for all }|x|\leq 1\text{ and }\textbf{b}\in\textbf{B}.

Then, if
|x|\leq 1
and
|\xi|\geq\lambda
, we have

|a^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)|=\bigg|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}\,\bar{x}\bigg|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\sim 1+\Big|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big|^{2}=\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{2}.

Then one can argue as follows. Note that
d_{1}=r_{0}+2\left(r_{1}+\dots+r_{N}\right)\geq 2
, so in particular
p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}<\infty
. Thus, if we put

s:=d_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}\Big)=d_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d_{1}-1}{2(d_{1}+1)}\Big)=\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}+1},

then, by Sobolev’s embedding theorem,

\displaystyle\lVert(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\rVert_{L_{s}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lVert(1-\Delta)^{s/2}(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\rVert_{2}

(4.47)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{s}\Big\lVert\Big(1-\frac{\Delta}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{s/2}(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\Big\rVert_{2}.

We can assume without loss of generality that the symbol
\chi
of
Theorem

4.6
satisfies
\chi(x,\xi)=\chi_{0}(x,\xi/\lambda)
for some smooth cut-off function
\chi_{0}
which is compactly supported in the ball
B^{1}
. Note that

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|}\lambda^{-|\alpha|},

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(1-\chi(x,\xi))|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{-|\alpha|}\lambda^{-|\alpha|}.

In particular,
\left\langle\frac{\cdot}{\lambda}\right\rangle\in S^{s}
and
1-\chi\in S^{0}
, where
S^{m}
denotes again the symbol class as defined in
Equation

4.36
. Let the symbol
\omega
be given by

\omega(x,\xi):=\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s}\left(1-\chi(x,\xi)\right)\psi(x).

Then
\omega\in S^{s}
and

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\omega(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|}\lambda^{-|\alpha|}.

Similar to
Equation

4.37
, the calculus of pseudo-differential operators yields

(4.48)

\Big(1-\frac{\Delta}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{s/2}\left(1-\chi^{w}\right)\psi=\omega^{w}+\rho_{1}^{w},

where
\rho_{1}\in S^{s-1}
is an error term with

(4.49)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\rho_{1}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|-1}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-1}.

Let
\tau^{\textbf{b}}
be the symbol given by

\tau^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)=\frac{\omega(x,\xi)}{\sigma^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)}\\
=\lambda^{-2}\,\frac{\omega(x,\xi)}{|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}J^{\textbf{b}}x|^{2}-\gamma^{2}}.

Then
\tau^{\textbf{b}}\in S^{s-2}
and

(4.50)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\tau^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|-2}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-2}.

As in the first part of the proof, let
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth cut-off function with
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}|_{\supp\psi}=1
and compact support in
B_{2}
. Moreover, we may assume that there is another smooth cut-off function
\psi_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\smash{\psi_{0}}|_{\supp\psi}=1
and
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}|_{\supp\psi_{0}}=1
. Then we have in particular
\supp\psi\subseteq\supp\psi_{0}\subseteq\supp\smash{\tilde{\psi}}
.

Then, since
\smash{\psi_{0}}|_{\supp\psi}=1
, we have
\omega\psi_{0}=\omega
and thus
\tau^{\textbf{b}}\psi_{0}=\tau^{\textbf{b}}
. Thus, again by the calculus of pseudo-differential operators, we can write

(4.51)

\omega^{w}=(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\psi_{0}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}+(\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}})^{w},

where
\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}}\in S^{s-1}
is a symbol such that

(4.52)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}}(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},\alpha,\beta}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|-1}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-1}.

Similar to
Equation

4.39
, using the bounds
Equation

4.50
, we see that the operator
(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
is bounded on
L^{2}
. However, note that

\displaystyle|(x-y)^{\alpha}K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\Big\langle\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|-2}\lambda^{-|\alpha|-2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\sim\lambda^{-|\alpha|+d_{1}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}\big\langle\tilde{\xi}\big\rangle^{s-|\alpha|-2}\,d\tilde{\xi}

and that the last integral converges if
|\alpha|>d_{1}+s-2
. Since
s=d_{1}/(d_{1}+1)<1
, we get

(4.53)

|K_{(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}}(x,y)|\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r},\beta}\left(\lambda\left|x-y\right|\right)^{-\beta}\lambda^{d_{1}-2}\quad\text{for all }\beta\geq d_{1}-1.

Altogether, by Young’s inequality, we obtain

(4.54)

\lVert(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\rVert_{2\to 2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{-2}.

Moreover, one can show that
\rho_{1}^{w}
and
(\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
are bounded on
L^{2}
with

(4.55)

\lVert\rho_{1}^{w}\rVert_{2\to 2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert(\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\rVert_{2\to 2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{-1}.

However, this does not follow immediately as in the arguments above from the bounds
Equation

4.49
and
Equation

4.52
, since we only get
Equation

4.53
for
\beta\geq d_{1}
, in which case the function
x\mapsto|x|^{-\beta}
is not locally integrable. However, since both symbols are in particular in the class
S^{0}
, they satisfy the regularity assumptions of the Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem, see for instance
[
9
, Chapter 2, Section 5]
, and the bounds
Equation

4.55
follow, for example, by applying the Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem to the operators
\lambda\rho_{1}^{w}
and
\lambda(\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}})^{w}
and by using the rescaling
(x,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda x,\xi/\lambda)
.

Let
\tilde{u}:=u\smash{\tilde{\psi}}
. Recall that
s=d_{1}/(d_{1}+1)
. Using
Equation

4.48
and
Equation

4.51
, and gathering the estimates
Equation

4.47
,
Equation

4.54
and
Equation

4.55
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/(d_{1}+1)}\lVert(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\rVert_{p_{d_{1}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\,\Big\lVert\Big(1-\frac{\Delta}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{s/2}(1-\chi^{w})(\psi u)\Big\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\,\lVert(\omega^{w}+\rho_{1}^{w})\tilde{u}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left(\lVert(\tau^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\psi_{0}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\tilde{u}\rVert_{2}+\lVert(\rho_{2}^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\tilde{u}\rVert_{2}+\lVert\rho_{1}^{w}\tilde{u}\rVert_{2}\right)

(4.56)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda^{-1}\lVert\psi_{0}(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}\tilde{u}\rVert_{2}+\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\rVert_{2}.

Similar to
Equation

4.42
, we have

(\sigma^{\textbf{b}})^{w}(\tilde{\psi}u)=f\psi-u\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}+i\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\langle J^{\textbf{b}}\cdot\,,\nabla\rangle\right)\tilde{\psi}-\langle\nabla u,\nabla\tilde{\psi}\rangle.

Since
\smash{\tilde{\psi}}|_{\supp\psi_{0}}=1
, we have
\psi_{0}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\smash{\tilde{\psi}}\rangle=0
. Thus, using similar bounds as in
Equation

4.43
and
Equation

4.44
, that is,

\|f\tilde{\psi}\|_{2}\lesssim\left\lVert f|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2}

and

\big\|u\,\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}+i\gamma^{2}\kappa^{2}\langle J^{\textbf{b}}\cdot\,,\nabla\rangle\big)\psi\big\|_{2}\lesssim_{\textbf{B},\textbf{r}}\lambda\,\left\lVert u|_{B_{2}}\right\rVert_{2},

we see that the last line of
Equation

4.56
is in particular bounded by the right-hand side of

4.46
, which finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Truncated restriction type estimates

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.1
. Recall that
L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})
denotes a sub-Laplacian on an arbitrary two-step stratified Lie group
G
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
is a basis of the first layer of the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. We write again
U=|\textbf{U}|^{1/2}
for the square root of the Laplacian on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, where
U
is the vector of differential operators
\textbf{U}=(-iU_{1},\dots,-iU_{d_{2}})
and
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The restriction type estimates of
Theorem

1.1
are stated in terms of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
given by

\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}=\bigg(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{K\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sup_{\lambda\in[\frac{K-1}{M},\frac{K}{M})}|F(\lambda)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\quad M\in(0,\infty).

By
[
4
, Lemma 3.4]
, for every bounded Borel function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,

(5.1)

\|F\|_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert F\rVert_{M,2}\leq C_{s}\big(\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}+M^{-s}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)\quad\text{for }s>1/2,

whence
\|\cdot\|_{M,2}
is stronger than the
L^{2}
-norm.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we need to show that

(5.2)

\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\rVert_{p\to 2}\leq C_{p,\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|F\|_{2}^{1-\theta_{p}}\lVert F\rVert_{2^{\ell},2}^{\theta_{p}}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}

and all
1\leq p\leq\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}=\min\{2\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+3},2\frac{d_{2}+1}{d_{2}+3}\}
, where
\theta_{p}\in[0,1]
satisfies

\frac{1}{p}=(1-\theta_{p})+\frac{\theta_{p}}{\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}}.

As our notation indicates,
Equation

5.2
is derived by interpolation between the endpoints
p=1
and
p=\min\{p_{d_{1}},p_{d_{2}}\}
. The case
p=1
, where we have only the
L^{2}
-norm on the right-hand side, follows from a Plancherel estimate for the integral kernel (see also Section III.5 of
[
5
]
).

Lemma 5.1
.

The restriction type estimate

5.2
holds true for

, i.e.,

\lVert F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)\rVert_{1\to 2}\leq C_{\chi}2^{-\ell d_{2}/2}\|F\|_{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
denote the convolution kernel of the operator
F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)
. Then

\|F(L)\chi(2^{\ell}U)f\|_{2}=\|f*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{1}\|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\|_{2}.

By
[
22
, Corollary 8]
, using the notation of
Proposition

3.10
,

(5.3)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}=(2\pi)^{|\textbf{r}|_{1}-(d_{1}+d_{2})}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\left|F(s+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\right|^{2}\\
&\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}\left[\left(b_{n}^{\mu}\right)^{r_{n}}\binom{k_{n}+r_{n}-1}{k_{n}}\right]d\sigma_{r_{0}}(s)\,d\mu,\end{split}

where

is the Dirac delta at 0 if

, and

d\sigma_{r_{0}}(s)=\frac{\pi^{r_{0}/2}}{\Gamma(r_{0}/2)}s^{r_{0}/2}\frac{ds}{s}\quad\text{if }r_{0}>0.

Recall from
Proposition

3.10
that

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(2k_{n}+r_{n}\right)b_{n}^{\mu},

where the function

is homogeneous of degree 1, see
Proposition

3.4

b_{n}^{\mu}\left(k_{n}+1\right)\leq\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}\lesssim_{A}1

whenever

, where

is the fixed compact subset of

1.1

\binom{k_{n}+r_{n}-1}{k_{n}}\sim(k_{n}+1)^{r_{n}-1},

the right-hand side of

(5.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2,r}^{*}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\left|F(s+\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu})\chi(2^{\ell}|\mu|)\right|^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\mu}\,d\mu\,d\sigma_{r_{0}}(s).

We rewrite the integral over

in polar coordinates, that is,

\mu=\rho\omega\quad\text{for }\rho\in[0,\infty)\text{ and }|\omega|=1.

Then, since

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mu}=\rho\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega}.

Thus, using
\rho=|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell}
,

5.4
is bounded by a constant times

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell(d_{2}+N)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}

\displaystyle\left|F(s+\rho\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})\chi(2^{\ell}\rho)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}^{\omega}\,\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,d\sigma_{r_{0}}(s).

Substituting

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell(d_{2}+N)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\left|F(s+\lambda)\chi(2^{\ell}(\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega})^{-1}\lambda)\right|^{2}

is compactly supported.
∎

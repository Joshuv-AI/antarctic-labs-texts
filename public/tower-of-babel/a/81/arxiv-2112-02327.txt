Cocompact imbedding theorem for functions of bounded variation into Lorentz spaces
Lin Zhao

Abstract




Abstract

We show that the imbedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
q>1
is cocompact with respect to group and the profile decomposition for
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This paper extends the cocompactness and profile decomposition for the critical space
L^{1^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to Lorentz spaces
L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
q>1
. A counterexample for
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
not cocompact is given in the last section.

Keywords
: Concentration analysis; BV spaces; Lorentz spaces; Profile decomposition

We need the following definitions and lemmas of Lorentz spaces and

spaces, see

. Lorentz spaces are known as real interpolation spaces between Lebesgue spaces and can be defined via the notion of Schwarz symmetrization. Let
u
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, whose level sets have finite measure for every level. Then the function

\displaystyle\mu(\lambda)=|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|u(x)|>\lambda\}|,~\lambda\geq 0,

(1.1)

is the distribution function of
u
and

\displaystyle u^{\sharp}(r)=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid\mu(\lambda)\leq|B_{r}|\}

(1.2)

is radially symmetric and non-increasing rearrangement of

, where

is ball centered at the origin with radius

and
|A|
is the
N
dimensional Lebesgue measure of
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We call the function

,

as Schwarz symmetrization of

. Then, we define Lorentz spaces
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

\displaystyle L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u:\text{measurable in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\|u\|_{L^{p,q}}=|B_{1}|^{\frac{q-p}{pq}}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{\frac{N}{p}}u^{\sharp}(|x|))^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{N}})^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\}.

The definition of Lorentz spaces is also given by decreasing rearrangement
u^{\ast}
as the distribution function
\mu
of
u
, namely

\displaystyle u^{\ast}(t)=\sup\{\lambda>0\mid\mu(\lambda)>t\}.

(1.4)

Then, we define the Lorentz spaces

\displaystyle L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u:\text{measurable in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\|u\|_{L^{p,q}}=(\int_{0}^{\infty}(u^{\ast}(t)t^{\frac{1}{p}})^{q}\frac{dt}{t})^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty\},

(1.5)

where (
1.5
) is a quasinorm and equivalent to (
1
). Lorentz spaces satisfy the following imbedding lemma.

Lemma 1.1
(
[

Definition 1.2
(
[
19

\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A function

is said to be of bounded variations, if the total variation of

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{\Omega}=\sup\{\int_{\Omega}u\text{div}vdx\mid v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),~\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}<+\infty,

(1.6)

where

\|v\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}(v_{k}(x))^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle BV(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\mid\|Du\|_{\Omega}<\infty\},

(1.7)

we define the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{BV(\Omega)}=\|Du\|_{\Omega}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

(1.8)

Definition 1.3.
The sequence
\{u_{n}\}

(1.10)

, where

Lemma 1.4.
Assume

is open and bounded with

(1.12)

The space of functions of bounded variation

1). Invariance. The group of operators on

2). Chain rule. Let

\displaystyle\|D\varphi(u)\|\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Du\|.

(1.17)

Definition 1.8
(
[

,

]
). Let

be a group of linear isometries of

.

Main results

Theorem 2.1.
The imbedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1<q\leq 1^{\ast}=\frac{N}{N-1}
,

, is cocompact with respect to the group
\mathcal{D}
(

), i.e. if, for any sequence

,
g[j_{k},y_{k}]u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
then
u_{k}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 2.2.
The embedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1^{\ast}<q\leq\infty
is cocompact with respect to
\mathcal{D}
, by the cocompact imbedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{1^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

. Theorem 2.1 may hold as follows by the interpolate method, since the sequence is bounded in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and we will use the estimate of Lorentz norms over lattices to prove Theorem 2.1. However, the cocompact embedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
does not hold for

and we will give a counterexample in the Section 3 for this case.

3
Counterexample

In this section, we will show an example which indicates the imbedding
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
not cocompact. In fact we shall define a bounded sequence of
\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which does not converge to zero in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. To this aim, fix a function

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&1<|x|\leq 2,\\
0,&|x|\leq 1~\text{or}~|x|>2,\\
\end{array}\right.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and obviously
\phi\in\dot{BV}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We take the function
v_{i}
given by the rescaling of
\phi
,

\displaystyle v_{i}(x)=2^{i(N-1)}\phi(2^{i}x),~2^{-i}<|x|\leq 2^{-(i-1)}.

(3.4)

For every positive integer number
n
, we choose
u_{n}
as the following function:

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(x).

(3.5)

Since the functions
v_{i}
satisfies
\|v_{i}\|_{\dot{BV}}=\|\phi\|_{\dot{BV}}
and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{BV}}=\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\|_{\dot{BV}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|v_{i}\|_{\dot{BV}}=\|\phi\|_{\dot{BV}}

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{L^{1^{\ast}}}^{1^{\ast}}=\frac{1}{n^{1^{\ast}-1}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1^{\ast}}}^{1^{\ast}}\rightarrow 0,~n\rightarrow\infty.

(3.7)

Then
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
strongly in
L^{1^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It must be clearly
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
also in
L^{1^{\ast},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1<q<1^{\ast}
, as follows by interpolation since the sequence is bounded in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We claim that
\{u_{n}\}
does not converge to 0 in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and therefore Theorem 2.1 does not hold with
q=1
. In fact, if
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
strongly in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then we have
u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{1^{\ast},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=L^{\frac{N}{N-1},1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This implies for any linear functional

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(u_{n})=0.

(3.8)

However, we can find a functional

\displaystyle f_{0}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\frac{1}{|x|}dx.

(3.9)

Setting
u=u_{n}
in (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle f_{0}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}(x)\frac{1}{|x|}dx

(3.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{i}(x)\frac{1}{|x|}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}2^{i(N-1)}\phi(2^{i}x)\frac{1}{|x|}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}2^{i(N-1)+i-Ni}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(y)\frac{1}{|y|}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(y)\frac{1}{|y|}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 1<|y|\leq 2\}}\frac{1}{|y|}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 1<|y|\leq 2\}|\neq 0,

where
|A|
is the
N

A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the last inequality. Taking

), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{0}(u_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}|\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 1<|y|\leq 2\}|\neq 0.

(3.11)

This conclusion is a contradiction.

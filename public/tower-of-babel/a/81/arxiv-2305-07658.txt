Mehdi Hassani

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

For the fixed complex number
s
the generalized omega function
\Omega_{s}(k)
is defined by
\Omega_{s}(k)=\sum_{p^{\ell}\|k}\ell^{s}
, where
p^{\ell}\|k
means that
\ell
is the largest power of
p
such that
p^{\ell}|k
. The cases
s=0
and
s=1
coincide, respectively, with the well-known number theoretic omega functions
\omega(k)=\sum_{p|k}1
, the number of distinct prime divisors of the positive integer
k
, and
\Omega(k)=\sum_{p^{\ell}\|k}\ell
, the total number of prime divisors of
k
. Duncan
[
3
]
proved that for each arbitrary integer
s\geqslant 0
,

(1.1)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leqslant n}\Omega_{s}(k)=\log\log n+M_{s}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right),

where
M_{s}
is a constant depending on
s
, given by
M_{s}=M+M^{\prime}_{s}
, with
M
referring to the Meissel–Mertens constant (see Remark
2.11
for more information), and

M^{\prime}_{s}=\sum_{p}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 2}\frac{\ell^{s}-(\ell-1)^{s}}{p^{\ell}}.

Here and through the paper
\sum_{p}
means that the sum runs over all primes. Note that
M_{0}=M
. Also, we let
M^{\prime}=M_{1}
and
M^{\prime}_{1}=M^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p(p-1)}
. Thus,
M^{\prime}=M+M^{\prime\prime}
. Approximation (
1.1
) is a generalization of the previously known result of Hardy and Ramanujan
[
5
]
concerning the average of the functions
\omega
and
\Omega
.

Based on Dirichlet’s hyperbola method and prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions with error term Saffari
[
12
]
obtained a full asymptotic expansion for the average of
\omega(n)
where
n
runs over the arithmetic progression
a
modulo
q
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
. For
a=q=1
, his result reads a follows

(1.2)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leqslant n}\omega(k)=\log\log n+M+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log^{m+1}n}\Big),

where
m\geqslant 1
is any fixed integer, and the coefficients
a_{j}
are given by

(1.3)

a_{j}=-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\log^{j-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{j}}{\mathrm{d}s^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{s}(s-1)\zeta(s)\right)_{s=1}.

Diaconis
[
2
]
reproved (
1.2
) using Dirichlet series of
\omega
, Perron’s formula and complex integration methods. One may obtain similar expansion for the average of generalized omega function
\Omega_{s}
for each fixed real
s\geqslant 0
, replacing
M
by
M_{s}
(see
[
9
, Theorem 1]
for more details).

Explicit versions of (
1.1
) for
s=0
and
s=1
is obtained in
[
8
]
and
[
6
]
, respectively, and then both improved in
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
, where it is showed that for each
n\geqslant 2
the following double sided approximation holds

(1.4)

-\frac{1.133}{\log n}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leqslant n}\omega(k)-\log\log n-M<\frac{1}{2\log^{2}n}.

Also,

(1.5)

-\frac{1.175}{\log n}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leqslant n}\Omega(k)-\log\log n-M^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2\log^{2}n},

where the left hand side is valid for each
n\geqslant 24
and the right hand side is valid for each
n\geqslant 2
.

2.
Summary of the results

2.1.
Unconditional results

In the present paper we are motivated by finding global numerical lower and upper bounds for the differences
\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)
, where
\mathcal{A}_{s}(n)
defined for any fixed complex number
s
as follows

\mathcal{A}_{s}(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\leqslant n}\Omega_{s}(k)-\log\log n.

The problem for the case
\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)
is an easy corollary of the inequalities (
1.4
). More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

For all natural numbers
n\geqslant 2
, we have

(2.1)

\alpha_{0}\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)\leqslant\beta_{0}

with the best possible constants
\alpha_{0}=\frac{45}{32}-\log\log 32
and
\beta_{0}=\frac{1}{2}-\log\log 2
, and the equality in the left hand side only for
n=32
, and in the right hand side only for
n=2
.

Similarly, to get a global numerical lower bound for
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)
, we can use the inequalities (
1.5
) to show the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all natural numbers
n\geqslant 2
, we have

(2.2)

\alpha_{1}\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)

with the best possible constant
\alpha_{1}=\frac{8}{7}-\log\log 7
and the equality only for
n=7
.

The problem of obtaining a global numerical upper bound for
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)
is quite different by the above ones. Although, computations show that
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)<\beta_{1}
for any
n\geqslant 2
with the best possible constant
\beta_{1}=M^{\prime}
, but the inequalities (
1.5
) are not enough sharp to show this fact. To deal with this difficulty, we made explicit all steps of the proof of (
1.2
) by following Saffari’s argument in
[
12
]
, and hence, we could to prove the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

For all natural numbers
n\geqslant\mathrm{e}^{14167}\approxeq 4.466\times 10^{6152}
, we have

(2.3)

\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)<\beta_{1}

with the best possible constant
\beta_{1}=M^{\prime}
. Moreover, if we assume that the Riemann hypothesis is true, then (
2.3
) holds for all natural numbers
n\geqslant 1400387903260
.

To prove Theorem
2.3
we use explicit forms of the prime number theorem with error term. Let
\pi(x)=\sum_{p\leqslant x}1
be the prime counting function, and
\mathrm{li}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\log t}\,\mathrm{d}t
be the logarithmic integral function, defined as the Cauchy principle value of the integral. By
f=O^{\ast}(g)
we mean
|f|\leqslant g
, providing an explicit version of Landau’s notation. It is known
[
15
, Theorem 2]
that

\pi(x)=\mathrm{li}(x)+O^{\ast}\left(0.2795\,x(\log x)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{(\log x)/6.455}}\right)\qquad(x\geqslant 229).

Modifying the above to the classical form, for any
x>1.2
we have

(2.4)

\pi(x)=\mathrm{li}(x)+O^{\ast}(R(x)),\qquad R(x)=x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log x}}.

This is however a weaker approximation, but it is suitable for our arguments, because of its global validity. We will use it to prove the following unconditional results.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any fixed integer
m\geqslant 1
and for any
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
we have

(2.5)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Mx+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(x,m)\right),

where

\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(x,m)=2^{m+1}m!\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}+(2^{m+1}+1)\,\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\\
+x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{6}\sqrt{\log x}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log x+3\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\log x}+21\right)+\sqrt{x}.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}^{14167}\approxeq 4.466\times 10^{6152}
we have

(2.6)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Mx-\left(1-\gamma\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{5x}{\log^{2}x}\right),

and consequently
\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)-\log\log x<M
.

In order to transfer an average result on the function
\omega
to an average result on the function
\Omega
, we may consider the average difference
\mathcal{J}(x):=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\left(\Omega(n)-\omega(n)\right)
, for which it is known
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
that for each integer
n\geqslant 1
,

(2.7)

nM^{\prime\prime}-25\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}<\mathcal{J}(n)<nM^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}\Big(2-\frac{20}{\log n}\Big).

Modifying the above approximation, we will prove in Lemma
3.4
that
\mathcal{J}(x)=M^{\prime\prime}x+O^{\ast}(\frac{33\sqrt{x}}{\log x})
for any
x\geqslant 2
. Thus, Theorem
2.4
and Corollary
2.5
transfer to the following results.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any fixed integer
m\geqslant 1
and for any
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
we have

(2.8)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)=x\log\log x+M^{\prime}x+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(x,m)\right),

where

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(x,m)=\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(x,m)+\frac{33\sqrt{x}}{\log x}.

Corollary 2.7
.

For
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}^{14167}\approxeq 4.466\times 10^{6152}
we have

(2.9)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)=x\log\log x+M^{\prime}x-\left(1-\gamma\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{6x}{\log^{2}x}\right),

and consequently
\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)-\log\log x<M^{\prime}
.

2.2.
Conditional results

As we observe in Corollary
2.5
, approximation (
2.5
), even with its initial parameter
m=1
, gives explicit bounds for
\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)
for large values of
x
. The reason is using approximation (
2.4
) with the remainder term
R(x)
, and appearing the term
x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{6}\sqrt{\log x}}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}(x,m)
. This term comes essentially from the classical zero-free regions for the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
. The situation changes as well, when we use approximations for
\pi(x)
under assuming the Riemann hypothesis (RH), which asserts that
\Re(s)>\frac{1}{2}
is a zero-free region, and indeed it is the best possible zero-free region, for
\zeta(s)
. Accordingly, it is known
[
13
, Corollary 1]
that if the Riemann hypothesis is true, then

\pi(x)=\mathrm{li}(x)+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{1}{8\pi}\sqrt{x}\log x\right)\qquad(x\geqslant 2657).

By computation, we observe that one may drop the coefficient
\frac{1}{8\pi}
and get an easy to use bound for global range
x\geqslant 2
, as follows

(2.10)

\pi(x)=\mathrm{li}(x)+O^{\ast}\left(\widehat{R}(x)\right),\qquad\widehat{R}(x)=\sqrt{x}\log x.

Note that the above approximations are close to optimal, because on one hand von Koch
[
16
]
showed that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to
\pi(x)=\mathrm{li}(x)+O(\sqrt{x}\log x)
, and on the other hand Littlewood
[
11
]
proved that letting
b(x)=\frac{\log\log\log x}{\log x}
, there are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that there are arbitrarily large values of
x
for which
\pi(x)>\mathrm{li}(x)+c_{1}\sqrt{x}\,b(x)
and that there are also arbitrarily large values of
x
for which
\pi(x)<\mathrm{li}(x)-c_{2}\sqrt{x}\,b(x)
. By using conditional approximation (
2.10
), we obtain the following analogues of Theorems
2.4
,
2.6
, and Corollaries
2.5
,
2.7
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume that the Riemann hypothesis is true. For any fixed integer
m\geqslant 1
and for any
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
we have

(2.11)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Mx+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\omega}(x,m)\right),

and

(2.12)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)=x\log\log x+M^{\prime}x+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\Omega}(x,m)\right),

where

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\omega}(x,m)=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}m!\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}+4x^{\frac{2}{3}}\log x+9x^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
+\left(\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m+1}+1\right)\,\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{x^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\log x}+15\sqrt{x}\log x,

and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\Omega}(x,m)=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\omega}(x,m)+\frac{33\sqrt{x}}{\log x}
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Assume that the Riemann hypothesis is true, and let
x_{0}=1400387903260
. Then, for
x\geqslant x_{0}
we have

(2.13)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Mx-\left(1-\gamma\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{11x}{\log^{2}x}\right),

and

(2.14)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)=x\log\log x+M^{\prime}x-\left(1-\gamma\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{12x}{\log^{2}x}\right),

and consequently
\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)-\log\log x<M
and
\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Omega(n)-\log\log x<M^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.10
.

According to partial computations we could run, it seems that the inequality
\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)<M
holds for
n\geqslant 16
, however, it fails for
n=15
. Also, as we mentioned above, the inequality
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)<M^{\prime}
holds for any integer
n\geqslant 2
. A computational challenge is to check validity of them up to
x_{0}
, hence we will get a global conditional bound under RH. More generally, we ask about finding bounds for the difference
\mathcal{A}_{s}(n)
for any fixed real
s>0
. A strategy to attack this problem is to make explicit the argument used in
[
9
]
to approximate the average difference
\mathcal{J}_{s}(n):=\sum_{k\leqslant n}\left(\Omega_{s}(k)-\omega(k)\right)
, for which it is proved that

2^{s}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}\ll nM^{\prime}_{s}-\mathcal{J}_{s}(n)\ll(2+\varepsilon)^{s}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n},

holds for each pair of fixed real numbers
s>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, and for
n
sufficiently large.

Remark 2.11
.

The Meissel–Mertens constant
M

[
4
, pp. 94–98]
is determined by

M=\gamma+\sum_{p}\left(\log\Big(1-p^{-1}\Big)+p^{-1}\right),

where
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant
[
4
, pp. 24–40]
. Also, see the impressive survey
[
10
]
for more information about
\gamma
. Among several properties of the constants
M
and
M^{\prime}
we have the following rapidly converging series

M=\gamma+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)\log\zeta(k)}{k},\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\prime}=\gamma+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(k)\log\zeta(k)}{k},

where
\mu
is the Möbus function and
\varphi
is the Euler function. Computations based on the above series representations yields that

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\approxeq 0.26149721284764278375542683860869585905156664826120,

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approxeq 1.03465388189743791161979429846463825467030798434439.

We have used these values in our numerical verifications of the results of the present paper. All of computations have been done over Maple software

3.
Proof of unconditional approximations

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Considering the left hand side of (
1.4
), we observe that the inequalities

\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)>M-\frac{1.133}{\log n}>\alpha_{0}

hold when
n>\mathrm{e}^{1.133/(M-\alpha_{0})}\approxeq 102841.56
. Thus, we obtain the left hand side of (
2.1
) for any integer
n\geqslant 102842
. By computation, it holds also for
2\leqslant n\leqslant 102841
with equality only for
n=32
. Also, considering the right hand side of (
1.4
), we observe that the inequalities

\mathcal{A}_{0}(n)<M+\frac{1}{2\log^{2}n}<\beta_{0}

hold when
n>\mathrm{e}^{1/\sqrt{2(\beta_{0}-M)}}\approxeq 2.48
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Since
\mathrm{e}^{1.175/(M^{\prime}-\alpha_{1})}\approxeq 8.23
, for any integer
n\geqslant 9
we have
n>\mathrm{e}^{1.175/(M^{\prime}-\alpha_{1})}
, or equivalently
M^{\prime}-1.175/\log n>\alpha_{1}
. By using this inequality, and the left hand side of (
1.5
) we deduce that
\mathcal{A}_{1}(n)>\alpha_{1}
holds for
n\geqslant 24
. By computation, it holds also for
2\leqslant n\leqslant 24
with equality only for
n=7
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
and their corollaries based on a series of lemmas. As in
[
12
]
, we start by using Dirichlet’s hyperbola method
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
to get the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
x
and
y
satisfying
1\leqslant y\leqslant x
, we have

(3.1)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=\sum_{p\leqslant y}\left[\frac{x}{p}\right]+\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\pi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)-\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\pi(y).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{1}(n)=1
be the unitary arithmetic function, and
\varpi(n)
be the characteristic function of primes; that is
\varpi(n)=1
when
n
is prime, and
\varpi(n)=0
otherwise. We consider Dirichlet convolution of these two functions,

\mathbf{1}\ast\varpi(n)=\varpi\ast\mathbf{1}(n)=\sum_{d|n}\varpi(d)\,\mathbf{1}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)=\sum_{d|n}\varpi(d)=\sum_{p|n}1=\omega(n).

Note that
[x]=\sum_{n\leq x}\mathbf{1}(n)
, and
\pi(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\varpi(n)
. Thus, by using Dirichlet’s hyperbola method, for any
y
satisfying
1\leqslant y\leqslant x
we deduce that

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\mathbf{1}\ast\varpi(n)=\sum_{n\leqslant y}\left[\frac{x}{n}\right]\varpi(n)+\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\pi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)-\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\pi(y).

This gives (
3.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
x
and
y
satisfying
1.2<y\leqslant x
, we have

(3.2)

\sum_{p\leqslant y}\left[\frac{x}{p}\right]=x\log\log y+Mx+O^{\ast}\left(h_{1}(x,y)\right),

where

h_{1}(x,y)=x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(6\sqrt{\log y}+19\right)+y.

Proof.

We have

\sum_{p\leqslant y}\left[\frac{x}{p}\right]=\sum_{p\leqslant y}\left(\frac{x}{p}-\left\{\frac{x}{p}\right\}\right)=x\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p}+O^{\ast}(y).

The Stieltjes integral and integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{2^{-}}^{y}\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi(t)}{t}=\frac{\pi(y)}{y}+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{li}(y)}{y}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{R(y)}{y}\right)+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The last integral is dominated by
\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{R(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t
, so it is convergent as
y\to\infty
. Thus, we have

\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{R(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right).

Note that

\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{R(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(6\sqrt{\log y}+18\right).

Also, integration by parts implies

\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{\mathrm{li}(t)}{t}\Big|_{2}^{y}+\int_{2}^{y}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t\log t}=\log\log y-\frac{\mathrm{li}(y)}{y}+\frac{\mathrm{li}(2)}{2}-\log\log 2.

Combining the above approximations, we deduce that

\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log y+C+O^{\ast}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(6\sqrt{\log y}+19\right)\right),

where

C=\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\mathrm{li}(2)}{2}-\log\log 2.

Mertens’ approximation concerning the sum of reciprocal of primes
[
14
, Theorem 1.10]
asserts that
\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p}-\log\log y\to M
as
y\to\infty
. This implies that
C=M
, and concludes the proof. Meanwhile, let us mention that the equality
C=M
also implies that

\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{\pi(t)-\mathrm{li}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=M+\log\log 2-\frac{\mathrm{li}(2)}{2}\approxeq-0.62759759779276794.

An additional output of the completed proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x
and
y
satisfy
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
and
1.2<x^{\delta}\leqslant y\leqslant x^{\Delta}<x
for some fixed
\delta,\Delta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

(3.3)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\pi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\mathrm{li}(y)+x(\log\log x-\log\log y)\\
+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(h_{2}(x,y)\right),

where

h_{2}(x,y)=\frac{m!}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}\\
+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{x^{\Delta}}{\log x}+x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(1+\log\frac{x}{y}\right).

Proof.

For
n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}
we have
\frac{x}{n}\geqslant y\geqslant x^{\delta}>1.2
. Thus, we may use the approximation (
2.4
) to get

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\pi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+O^{\ast}\left(\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}R\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right).

Since
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=-\frac{x}{t^{2}(\log x-\log t)}
, the Stieltjes integral and integration by parts gives

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\int_{1^{-}}^{\frac{x}{y}}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\mathrm{d}[t]=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\mathrm{li}(y)+x\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}\frac{[t]}{t^{2}(\log x-\log t)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We write
[t]=t-\{t\}
to get

(3.4)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\mathrm{li}(y)+x(\log\log x-\log\log y)-\mathcal{E}(x,y),

with the remainder
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
given by

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=x\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}(\log x-\log t)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Letting
g_{x}(t)=(1-\frac{\log t}{\log x})^{-1}
, we have

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,g_{x}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathcal{E}_{1}(x,y)-\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y),

with

\mathcal{E}_{1}(x,y)=\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,g_{x}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y)=\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{\frac{x}{y}}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,g_{x}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
y\geqslant x^{\delta}
, we have
1\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{x}{y}\leqslant x^{1-\delta}
, and consequently
0\leqslant\frac{\log t}{\log x}\leqslant 1-\delta<1
. We use Taylor’s formula with remainder
[
1
, Theorem 5.19]
for the function
u\mapsto(1-u)^{-1}
, which asserts that if
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1-\delta
for some fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
, as in our case, then for any given integer
m\geqslant 1
,

(3.5)

(1-u)^{-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}u^{r}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\,u^{m}\right).

Taking
u=\frac{\log t}{\log x}
in (
3.5
), we get

(3.6)

g_{x}(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\right)^{r}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\left(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\right)^{m}\right).

Thus,

\mathcal{E}_{1}(x,y)=\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\right)^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t+h_{\delta}(x),

where

\displaystyle|h_{\delta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\left(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\right)^{m}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{m}t}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{m!}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}.

Also, we have

\frac{x}{\log x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{\log t}{\log x}\right)^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{x}{\log^{j}x}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\log^{j-1}t\,\mathrm{d}t=-x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}.

Hence, the following approximation holds for any fixed integer
m\geqslant 1
, with the coefficients
a_{j}
given by (
1.3
),

(3.7)

\mathcal{E}_{1}(x,y)=-x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{m!}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}\right).

To deal with
\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y)
we note that by induction on
n\geqslant 0
, we obtain the following anti-derivative formula with the coefficients
P(n,j)={n\choose j}j!
,

(3.8)

\int\frac{\log^{n}t}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=0}^{n}P(n,j)\log^{n-j}t.

Since
y\leqslant x^{\Delta}
we get
\frac{x}{y}\geqslant x^{1-\Delta}
. Thus, for any integer
n\geqslant 0
we have

\int_{\frac{x}{y}}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,\log^{n}t\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\int_{x^{1-\Delta}}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,\log^{n}t\,\mathrm{d}t<\int_{x^{1-\Delta}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{n}t}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

By using (
3.8
), and assuming that
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{x^{1-\Delta}}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{n}t}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{n}x}{x^{1-\Delta}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}P(n,j)(1-\Delta)^{n-j}\frac{1}{\log^{j}x}

\displaystyle<\frac{\log^{n}x}{x^{1-\Delta}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}P(n,j)=\frac{\log^{n}x}{x^{1-\Delta}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{j!}<\mathrm{e}n!\,\frac{\log^{n}x}{x^{1-\Delta}}.

Thus, for any integer
n\geqslant 0
we obtain

(3.9)

\mathcal{I}_{n}(x,y):=\int_{\frac{x}{y}}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,\log^{n}t\,\mathrm{d}t<\mathrm{e}n!\,\frac{\log^{n}x}{x^{1-\Delta}}.

Applying (
3.6
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{\log x}{x}\,\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{x}{y}}^{\infty}\frac{\{t\}}{t^{2}}\,g_{x}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\log^{r}x}\,\mathcal{I}_{r}(x,y)+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}\log^{m}x}\,\mathcal{I}_{m}(x,y)\right).

Hence, by using (
3.9
) we deduce that

\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y)<\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}m!}{\delta^{m+1}}+\mathrm{e}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}r!\right)\frac{x^{\Delta}}{\log x}.

Since
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}r!\leqslant m!
, we obtain

(3.10)

\mathcal{E}_{2}(x,y)=O^{\ast}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{x^{\Delta}}{\log x}\right).

Combining (
3.4
) with approximations (
3.7
) and (
3.10
) we obtain

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\mathrm{li}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\mathrm{li}(y)+x(\log\log x-\log\log y)+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}\\
+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{m!}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{x^{\Delta}}{\log x}\right).

Now, to conclude the proof of (
3.3
) we need just to approximate the sum
\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}R\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)
. Since
n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}
we have
\frac{x}{n}\geqslant y
. Thus,

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}R\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\leqslant x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{1}{n}\leqslant x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(1+\log\frac{x}{y}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Considering the hyperbolic identity (
3.1
) and approximations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we get

(3.11)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+Mx+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(h_{3}(x,y)\right),

where

h_{3}(x,y)=h_{1}(x,y)+h_{2}(x,y)+\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\left(\mathrm{li}(y)-\pi(y)\right).

By using (
2.4
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\left(\mathrm{li}(y)-\pi(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]O^{\ast}\left(R(y)\right)

\displaystyle=O^{\ast}\left(x\,\frac{R(y)}{y}\right)=O^{\ast}\left(x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\right).

Thus, (
3.11
) holds with
h_{3}(x,y)=h_{1}(x,y)+h_{2}(x,y)+x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}

+x\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\log y}}\left(\log\frac{x}{y}+6\sqrt{\log y}+21\right)+y.

Now, we take
\delta=\Delta=\frac{1}{2}
, and hence

. Note that the assumption
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
covers
x^{\delta}=\sqrt{x}>1.2
. Thus, we obtain (
2.5
), and the proof is complete.

4.
Proof of conditional approximations

To prove conditional results, under assuming the Riemann hypothesis, we reconstruct Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, replacing
R(x)
by
\widehat{R}(x)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that the Riemann hypothesis is true. Then, for any
x
and
y
satisfying
2\leqslant y\leqslant x
, we have

(4.1)

\sum_{p\leqslant y}\left[\frac{x}{p}\right]=x\log\log y+Mx+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{y}}\,(3\log y+4)+y\right).

Proof.

Note that

\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{R}(t)}{t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{2\log y+4}{\sqrt{y}}.

Thus, following similar argument as the proof of Lemma
3.2
and by using (
2.10
), we deduce that assuming RH, for any
y\geqslant 2
we have

\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log y+M+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{3\log y+4}{\sqrt{y}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the Riemann hypothesis is true. Let
x
and
y
satisfy
x\geqslant\mathrm{e}
and
1.2<x^{\delta}\leqslant y\leqslant x^{\Delta}<x
for some fixed
\delta,\Delta\in(0,1)
. Then, we have

(4.2)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\pi\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\mathrm{li}(y)+x(\log\log x-\log\log y)\\
+x\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a_{j}}{\log^{j}x}+O^{\ast}\left(\widehat{h}_{2}(x,y)\right),

where

\widehat{h}_{2}(x,y)=\frac{m!}{\delta^{m+1}}\,\frac{x}{\log^{m+1}x}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{m+1}}\right)\mathrm{e}m!\,\frac{x^{\Delta}}{\log x}\\
+\frac{2x}{\sqrt{y}}\left(\log y+2\right)+15\sqrt{x}\log x.

Proof.

Following similar argument as the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we should approximate the sum
\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\widehat{R}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)
, for which, we have

(4.3)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\widehat{R}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\sqrt{x}\log x\,\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}-\sqrt{x}\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}.

Letting
f_{0}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
and
f_{1}(t)=\frac{\log t}{t}
, we observe that
f_{0}(t)
is decreasing for
t\geqslant 1
, and with
t_{0}=\mathrm{e}^{2}\approxeq 7.39
, the function
f_{1}(t)
is increasing for
1\leqslant t\leqslant t_{0}
and decreasing for
t\geqslant t_{0}
. Moreover,

\max_{t\geqslant 1}f_{1}(t)=f_{1}(\mathrm{e}^{2})=\frac{2}{\mathrm{e}}<1.

Thus, comparison of a sum and an integral of a monotonic function
[
14
, Theorem 0.4]
implies that there exists
\theta_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}=1+\int_{1}^{[\frac{x}{y}]}f_{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\theta_{0}\left(f_{0}\left(\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\right)-1\right).

Since
\max_{t\geqslant 1}f_{0}(t)=f_{0}(1)=1
, we get

(4.4)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}=\int_{1}^{[\frac{x}{y}]}f_{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}(3)=\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}f_{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}(4).

Also, we write

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}=\sum_{1<n\leqslant 7}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\log 8}{\sqrt{8}}+\sum_{8<n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}.

There exists
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,1]
such that

\sum_{1<n\leqslant 7}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}=\int_{1}^{7}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\theta_{1}f_{1}(7)=\int_{1}^{7}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{2}{\mathrm{e}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{8<n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle=\int_{8}^{[\frac{x}{y}]}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\theta_{2}\left(f_{1}\left(\left[\frac{x}{y}\right]\right)-f_{1}(8)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{8}^{[\frac{x}{y}]}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}\left(\frac{4}{\mathrm{e}}\right).

Thus,

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}=\int_{1}^{[\frac{x}{y}]}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}\left(\eta\right)=\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}\left(\eta+\frac{2}{\mathrm{e}}\right),

where
\eta=\frac{6}{\mathrm{e}}+f_{1}(8)+\int_{7}^{8}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\approxeq 3.68
. Since
\eta+\frac{2}{\mathrm{e}}<5
, we get

(4.5)

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}=\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+O^{\ast}(5).

By computation, we have

\sqrt{x}\log x\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}f_{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t-\sqrt{x}\int_{1}^{\frac{x}{y}}f_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\frac{2x}{\sqrt{y}}\left(\log y+2\right)-2\sqrt{x}\left(\log x+2\right).

Thus, considering the identity (
4.3
) and the approximations (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we deduce that

\sum_{n\leqslant\frac{x}{y}}\widehat{R}\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)=\frac{2x}{\sqrt{y}}\left(\log y+2\right)+O^{\ast}\left(15\sqrt{x}\log x\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

1
Introduction

Numerical semigroups and submonoids of
\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
be the set of natural numbers and
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integer numbers. A numerical semigroup is a subset
S
of
\mathbb{N}
that is closed under addition,
0\in S
, and
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is a finite set. For a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\langle A\rangle
the submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
generated by
A
:

\langle A\rangle=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}:n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},c_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\mathbf{a}_{j}\in A\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

It is known that
\langle A\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
{\gcd}(A)=1
(see
[
8
]
).

Let
M
be a nontrivial submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
. Then,
M
is finitely generated and, moreover, there exists a subset of
M
that generates
M
such that any other generating set of
M
contains it; such generating set is called the
minimal generating set
of
M
and its elements, which are called the
minimal generators
of
M
, are precisely the nonzero elements belonging to
M
that cannot be written as the sum of two nonzero elements in
M
. We denote by
e(M)
the number of minimal generators of
M
.

If
S
is a numerical semigroup, we call
e(S)
the
embedding dimension
of
S
. The maximum element in
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is called the
Frobenius number of
S
, which is denoted by
{\rm F}(S)
and the cardinality of
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is the
genus of
S
, which is denoted by
{\rm g}(S)
.

Assume that
M
is a nontrivial submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
. If
x\in M\setminus\{0\}
, the
Apéry set of
x
in
M
, denoted by
{\rm Ap}(M,x)
, is defined by
{\rm Ap}(M,x)=\{m\in M:m-x\notin M\}
. If
d=\gcd(M)
and
S
is the numerical semigroup obtained by dividing every element in
M
by
d
, then we have the relation
{\rm Ap}(M,x)=\{ds:s\in{\rm Ap}(S,x/d)\}
. If
S
is a numerical semigroup and
x\in S\setminus\{0\}
, then
{\rm Ap}(S,x)=\{w(0),\ldots,w(x-1)\}
, where
w(i)
is the least element in
S
congruent to
i
modulo
x
; we also have the following two properties:

1.

{\rm F}(S)=\max{\rm Ap}(S,x)-x
,

2.

\displaystyle{\rm g}(S)=\frac{1}{x}\left(\sum_{s\in{\rm Ap}(S,x)}s\right)-\frac{x-1}{2}
.

An integer number
x
is a
pseudo-Frobenius number
of
S
if
x\notin S
and
x+s\in S
for all
s\in S\setminus\{0\}
. The set of pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
S
is denoted by
{\rm PF}(S)
. We have
{\rm PF}(S)
is nonempty, as
{\rm F}(S)
is a pseudo-Frobenius number of
S
. A partial order
\leq_{S}
is defined in
S
as follows: for
a,b\in S
,
a\leq_{S}b
if and only if
b-a\in S
. If
x\in S\setminus\{0\}
, then Pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
S
are precisely those integers of the form
w-x
, where
w
is a maximal element in
{\rm Ap}(S,x)
with respect to
\leq_{S}
, that is

{\rm PF}(S)=\left\{w-x:w\in{\rm Maximals}_{\leq_{S}}{\rm Ap}(S,x)\right\}.

The set
{\rm PF}(S)
is finite, and its cardinality is called
the type
of
S
, which is denoted by
{\rm t}(S)
. All these concepts and results can be found in
[
8
]
.

Submonoids and numerical semigroups generated by tails of sequences.

Let us consider a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of positive integers, which we will call the
generating sequence
. For each
n\geq 1
, the
n-
tail of
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is the subsequence
(x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots)
, and we define a submonoid
S_{n}
of
\mathbb{N}
by
S_{n}=\langle\{x_{n+j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}\rangle
. As we know,
S_{n}
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
\gcd(x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots)=1
. In this way, we associate a family of submonoids of
\mathbb{N}
with a given sequence of positive integers. In recent works on numerical semigroups
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
, a particular generating sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is given, and for the numerical semigroups
S_{n}
associated with this sequence, they find

1.

the minimal generating set and the embedding dimension of
S_{n}
;

2.

the Apéry set
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},x_{n})
, the Frobenius number and genus of
S_{n}
;

3.

in most cases, the pseudo-Frobenius numbers and the type of
S_{n}
are computed.

The generating sequences considered in those works have the form
x_{n}=ca^{n}-d
, for particular values of the integers
a,c
and
d
, where
a\geq 2
and
c
is positive.

Presentation of the results.

We give arbitrary integers
a,c
and
d
, with
a\geq 2
and
c>0
. Then, we consider the generating sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
given by

x_{n}=ca^{n}-d.

One first condition on
a,c
and
d
is
d<ca
, since we require
x_{1}=ca-d>0
. This sequence satisfies the simple linear recurrence relation
x_{n+1}=ax_{n}+(a-1)d
, for all
n\geq 1
. Now, for any
n\geq 1
we define a submonoid
S_{n}
of
\mathbb{N}
given by

S_{n}=\langle\{x_{n+j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}\rangle.

To simplify notation, given
n\geq 1
, we set
\mathbf{s}_{j}=x_{n+j}
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. So,
S_{n}=\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}\rangle
. Besides, the
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s satisfy the the recurrence relation
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=a\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d
, for all
j\geq 0
.

For our purposes, it will be enough to assume that
d
is relatively prime to
a
and
c
(otherwise, we can divide by an adequate factor to reduce to this case). The following conjectures arise from the results from
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
:

1.

The minimal generating set of
S_{n}
has the form
\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}
for some
m>0
(such
m
must be equal to
e(S_{n})
).

2.

For all
n\geq 1
,
e(S_{n})\geq n
.

3.

For all
n\geq 1
,
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq e(S_{n})-1
.

In this work, we prove all these conjectures in a very general setting, and give a characterization of
e(S_{n})
that involves
repunit numbers
and the elements
\mathbf{s}_{0}
and
\mathbf{s}_{1}
.

In general, we have an upper bound for the type of a numerical semigroup
S
:
{\rm t}(S)\leq m(S)-1
, where
m(S)
is the minimal nonzero element in
S
. We also know that
e(S)\leq m(S)
. Thus, the third conjecture above establishes an improvement to the general upper bound for the type for the numerical semigroups
S_{n}
.

One of our main results is the characterization of the of
e(S_{n})
. Let
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
. The
n-
th repunit number in base
a
,
\mathbf{R}_{n}
, is defined by

\mathbf{R}_{n}=1+a+\cdots+a^{n-1}=\frac{a^{n}-1}{a-1}.

We prove that if
m
is the least positive integer such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda\leq\mathbf{R}_{m}
, and if we also have
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
, then
e(S_{n})=m
.

We show that the condition
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
is satisfied in important general cases, for instance, if
d>0
.

The characterization of the Apéry set
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
is crucial for finding the Frobenius number, the genus, the pseudo-Frobenius numbers, and the type of
S_{n}
. To give our characterization of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
, we need a special set of
r-
tuples of integers, which we describe now. Given an integer
r\geq 1
, a
residual
r-
tuple
(here, we use the terminology given in
[
5
]
for such tuples) is a tuple of integers
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
such that

1.

0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq a
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and

2.

if
\alpha_{i_{0}}=a
for some
i_{0}\in\{2,\ldots,r\}
, then
\alpha_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,i_{0}-1\}
.

The set of all residual
r-
tuples will be denoted by
A(r)
. The size of
A(r)
is
(a^{r+1}-1)/(a-1)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 12]
). An important order relation on
A(r)
is the
co-lexicographic order
\leq_{c}
in which
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\leq_{c}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
if, either,
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
or there is some
t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
\alpha_{t}<\beta_{t}
and
\alpha_{j}=\beta_{j}
for
j=t+1,\ldots,r
.

If
m=e(S_{n})
and we assume that
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
, where
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
, the we prove that there is a unique residual
(m-1)-
tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
such that
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
is equal to

\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}:(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})\in A(m-1),(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})\leq_{c}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\right\}

and
\max{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
. Moreover, we show that this residual
(m-1)-
tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
is the unique that satisfy the relation

(\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda)-1=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}.

We test our results on some generating sequences. For instance, we deal with the generating sequence
(2^{k}-1)2^{n}-1
, when
k>2^{n}
(this case was not treated in
[
4
]
); we also treat sequences of the form
(a^{k}-1)a^{n}-1
, for
a\geq 3
and
k\geq 1
.

Finally, if
m=e(S_{n})
,
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
and we assume that
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
, then
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq e(S_{n})-1
, and verify Wilf’s conjecture for the numerical semigroup
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
, under the given conditions.

2
Repunit numbers and relations between the generators of
S_{n}

In this small section we show some basic properties of repunit numbers and the generators of
S_{n}
that we will use later. For convenience we define
\mathbf{R}_{0}=0
. The sequence of repunit numbers in base
a
satisfies the recurrence relation
\mathbf{R}_{n+1}=a\mathbf{R}_{n}+1
, for all
n\geq 0
. It is easily seen that

\mathbf{R}_{n}=(a-1)\sum_{j=m}^{n-1}\mathbf{R}_{j}+\mathbf{R}_{m}+n-m,

(1)

whenever
0\leq m<n
. By using the recurrence relation satisfied by the generators of
S_{n}
, that is,

\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=a\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d
, for all
j\geq 0
, we can derive the following formula that generalizes (
1
):

\mathbf{s}_{t}=(a-1)\sum_{j=u}^{t-1}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{u}+(t-u)(a-1)d,

(2)

where
0\leq u<t
. In the following lemma we give the fundamental relations between the
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s.

The elements of
S_{n}
can be represented as linear combinations of the
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s with nonnegative coefficients, but it is convenient to consider linear combinations of the
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s with integer coefficients.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{r}
are integers, then

\alpha\mathbf{s}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}=\left(\alpha-a\sum_{j=2}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j-1}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1}

(3)

Thus, any linear combination of the
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s can be transformed into a linear combination of
\mathbf{s}_{0}
and
\mathbf{s}_{1}
. The sum of the coefficients of both sides combinations in (
3
) is the same.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that for all
j\geq 1
and integers
\alpha
and
\beta
,

\alpha\mathbf{s}_{0}+\beta\mathbf{s}_{j}=\left(\alpha-a\beta\mathbf{R}_{j-1}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\beta\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1}.

By induction on
j
, assume that the result is true for
j\geq 1
and all integers
\alpha
and
\beta
. Note that

\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=a\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d=a\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{1}-a\mathbf{s}_{0}
. Then we have

\alpha\mathbf{s}_{0}+\beta\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=(\alpha-a\beta)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\beta\mathbf{s}_{1}+a\beta\mathbf{s}_{j}.

Now, by applying the induction hypothesis to
(\alpha-a\beta)\mathbf{s}_{0}+a\beta\mathbf{s}_{j}
, we get

(\alpha-a\beta)\mathbf{s}_{0}+a\beta\mathbf{s}_{j}=\left(\alpha-a\beta-a^{2}\beta\mathbf{R}_{j-1}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(a\beta\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1},

and by the recurrence relation between the
\mathbf{R}_{j}
’s, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\mathbf{s}_{0}+\beta\mathbf{s}_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha-a\beta-a^{2}\beta\mathbf{R}_{j-1}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\beta+a\beta\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha-a\beta\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\beta\mathbf{R}_{j+1}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1}.

This ends the proof of our claim. Now, the sum of coefficients in the right hand side of (
3
) is

\displaystyle\alpha-a\sum_{j=2}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha+\beta_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{r}\beta_{j}\left(\mathbf{R}_{j}-a\mathbf{R}_{j-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha+\beta_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{r}\beta_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j},

which is the sum of the coefficients in the left hand side of (
3
).
∎

3
The embedding dimension of
S_{n}

We give a characterization of the embedding dimension
S_{n}
. First, we show that there is a unique positive integer
m
such that
\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}
is the minimal generating set of
S_{n}
, and, of course
e(S_{n})=m
. Then, we find conditions on
\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1}
and repunit numbers to determine such integer
m
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r>0
be such that
\mathbf{s}_{r}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<r\}\rangle
. Then
S_{n}=\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<r\}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
S=\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<r\}\rangle
. First, we show that
as+(a-1)d\in S
, for all
s\in S\setminus\{0\}
. In fact, if
s\in S\setminus\{0\}
, then
s=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N},i=0,\ldots,r-1
, and some
\alpha_{j}>0
. So

\displaystyle as+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=a\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=a\sum_{i=0,i\neq j}^{r-2}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}+a\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=a\sum_{i=0,i\neq j}^{r-2}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}+a(\alpha_{j}-1)\mathbf{s}_{j}+a\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=a\sum_{i=0,i\neq j}^{r-2}\alpha_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}+a(\alpha_{j}-1)\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{j+1}.

If
j<r-1
, then
j+1<r
and
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}\in S
. Now, if
j=r-1
, then
j+1=r
and
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=\mathbf{s}_{r}\in S
, by hypothesis. Thus, we see that
as+(a-1)d\in S
.

In order to prove that
S_{n}=S
, it suffices to prove that
\mathbf{s}_{j}\in S
for all
j\geq 0
. Clearly
\mathbf{s}_{0}\in S
and, if
\mathbf{s}_{j}\in S
, where
j\geq 0
, then
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}=a\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d\in S
. This ends the proof.
∎

Since
S_{n}
is finitely generated, there is some
m>0
such that
\mathbf{s}_{m}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}\rangle
and
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}\notin\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m-1\}\rangle
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that for some
m>0
it holds that
\mathbf{s}_{m}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}\rangle
but
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}\notin\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m-1\}\rangle
. Then
\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}
is the minimal generating set of
S_{n}
. In particular,
m=e(S_{n})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the set
\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}
generates
S_{n}
. If this is not the minimal generating set of
S_{n}
, then there exists
j_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<j_{0}\}\rangle
. Note that
j_{0}<m-1
since
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}\notin\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m-1\}\rangle
. Again, by Lemma
3.1
we have
S_{n}=\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<j_{0}\}\rangle
, but it follows that
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<j_{0}\}\rangle\subseteq\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m-1\}\rangle
, which is a contradiction. We conclude that
\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}
is the minimal generating set of
S_{n}
.
∎

It is clear that the positive integer
m
in Theorem
3.2
is unique, so the embedding dimension of
S_{n}
is the unique
m>0
such that
\mathbf{s}_{m}\in\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m\}\rangle
and
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}\notin\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<m-1\}\rangle
.

Remark 3.3
.

In Theorem
3.2
we only use that
S_{n}
is a submonoid of
\mathbb{N}
; we do not need
S_{n}
to be a numerical semigroup yet.

In the following proposition we characterize the conditions under which the embedding dimension of
S_{n}
is
1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
e(S_{n})=1
, then
n=1
and
ca-d
divides
c-d
. Conversely, if
ca-d
divides
c-d
, then
e(S_{1})=1
.

Proof.

If
e(S_{n})=1
, then
\{\mathbf{s}_{0}\}
generates
S_{n}
. In particular,
\mathbf{s}_{0}\mid\mathbf{s}_{1}
. So
\mathbf{s}_{0}
divides
\mathbf{s}_{1}-a\mathbf{s}_{0}=(a-1)d
, and since
\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},d)=1
, we must have
\mathbf{s}_{0}\mid a-1
. Therefore,
\mathbf{s}_{0}\leq a-1
. Since
d<ac
, we have
ca^{n}-ca<ca^{n}-d\leq a-1
, from which
ca(a^{n-1}-1)/(a-1)<1
, and this can only happen when
n=1
. Now, the relation
ca-d=c(a-1)+c-d
implies that
ca-d
divides
c-d
, since
\mathbf{s}_{0}=ca-d
divides
a-1
.

Conversely, if
ca-d
divides
c-d
, then by the relation
ca-d=c(a-1)+c-d
and the fact that
ca-d
and
c
are relatively prime, we conclude that
ca-d
divides
a-1
. Now,
ca^{2}-d=c(a^{2}-1)+c-d
, from which we see that
\mathbf{s}_{0}=ca-d
divides
\mathbf{s}_{1}=ca^{2}-d
and by Theorem
3.2
,
e(S_{1})=1
.
∎

Now, we study the sum of coefficients in representations of
\mathbf{s}_{m}
as linear combinations of
\mathbf{s}_{j}
’s,
0\leq j<m
(if such representations exist).

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that for some
m>0
, there are integers
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}
such that
\mathbf{s}_{m}=\alpha_{0}\mathbf{s}_{0}+\alpha_{1}\mathbf{s}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
. Then, there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}=1+tca^{n}.

Moreover, if the
\alpha_{j}
’s are nonnegative, then
t>0
.

Proof.

The equation
\mathbf{s}_{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
implies that

ca^{n+m}-d=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}ca^{n+j}-d\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j},

from which
d\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}-1\right)=sca^{n}
, where
s=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}a^{j}-a^{m}
. Since
d
is relatively prime to
a
and
c
, we obtain that
d
divides
s
. So,
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}=1+tca^{n}
, where
t=s/d
. Clearly, if the
\alpha_{j}
’s are nonnegative, it must be
t>0
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have
e(S_{n})\geq n
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathbf{s}_{n-1}\notin\langle\{\mathbf{s}_{j}:0\leq j<n-1\}\rangle
. If we had
\mathbf{s}_{n-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
, for some nonnegative
\alpha_{j}
’s, then by Lemma
3.5
, there is some
t>0
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\alpha_{j}=1+tca^{n}
. Hence,

\mathbf{s}_{n-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\geq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\alpha_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}=(1+tca^{n})\mathbf{s}_{0}\geq(1+ca^{n})\mathbf{s}_{0}.

Now, since
d<ca
, we have
d\leq ca-1\leq a^{n-1}(ca-1)
, from which it follows that
\mathbf{s}_{0}\geq a^{n-1}
, which is equivalent to
(1+ca^{n})\mathbf{s}_{0}>\mathbf{s}_{n-1}
. This gives us a contradiction.
∎

Theorem
3.6
is precisely our conjecture about the lower bound on the embedding dimension of
e(S_{n})
.

Now we go to our main result about the embedding dimension of
S_{n}
. After its proof, we show some consequences and examples. We need the following lemma, which is easy to verify.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
t
be a positive integer. If
\lambda
divides
\mathbf{s}_{0}
and
\mathbf{s}_{1}
, then

\mathbf{s}_{t}=\left(\frac{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\lambda}-\mathbf{R}_{t}+1\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\mathbf{R}_{t}-\frac{\mathbf{s}_{0}}{\lambda}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1}.

Before the next theorem, we prove that
\gcd(S_{n})=\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1})=\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},a-1)
. In fact, since
d
is relatively prime to
a
and
c
, we have
\mathbf{s}_{0}=ca^{n}-d
is relatively prime to
a,c
and
d
. The relation
\mathbf{s}_{1}=a\mathbf{s}_{0}+(a-1)d
implies that
\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1})=\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},(a-1)d)=\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},a-1)
, where, for the last equality, we use the fact that
\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},d)=1
. Now, using the relation
\mathbf{s}_{j}=a\mathbf{s}_{j-1}+(a-1)d
,
j=1,2,\ldots,
we find that every common divisor of
\mathbf{s}_{0}
and
a-1
divides
\mathbf{s}_{j}
for all
j\geq 0
. Thus,
\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},a-1)
divides
\gcd(S_{n})
. Clearly,
\gcd(S_{n})
divides
\gcd(\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1})
, and this ends the proof of our claim.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
. Let
m
be the smallest positive integer such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda\leq\mathbf{R}_{m}
and assume that
\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda\geq\mathbf{R}_{m}
. Then,
e(S_{n})=m
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
we must show that
\mathbf{s}_{m}
can be written as a linear combination of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
, with nonnegative integer coefficients, and also that
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
cannot be written as linear combination of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
with nonnegative integer coefficients. In fact, by Lemma
3.7
we have

\mathbf{s}_{m}=\left(\frac{\mathbf{s}_{1}}{\lambda}-\mathbf{R}_{m}+1\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}+\left(\mathbf{R}_{m}-\frac{\mathbf{s}_{0}}{\lambda}\right)\mathbf{s}_{1},

and by hypothesis, the coefficients in this representation are nonnegative integers. Let
A_{0}=\left(\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda\right)-\mathbf{R}_{m}+1
and
B_{0}=\mathbf{R}_{m}-\left(\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda\right)
, so that
\mathbf{s}_{m}=A_{0}\mathbf{s}_{0}+B_{0}\mathbf{s}_{1}
.

Now we have to show that
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
cannot be written as linear combination of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
with nonnegative integer coefficients. We make the following claim:
For any representation
\mathbf{s}_{m}=\beta_{0}\mathbf{s}_{0}+\cdots+\beta_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
, where
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1}
are nonnegative integers, there is a nonnegative integer
u
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\beta_{j}=1+\left(u+1\right)\frac{(a-1)}{\lambda}ca^{n}.

To prove the claim, note that by Proposition
2.1
, if
\mathbf{s}_{m}=\beta_{0}\mathbf{s}_{0}+\cdots+\beta_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
, where the
\beta_{j}
’s are nonnegative, then we can derive a representation
\mathbf{s}_{m}=A\mathbf{s}_{0}+B\mathbf{s}_{1}
for some integers
A,B
, and
A+B=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\beta_{j}
.
By Lemma
3.5
, there is an integer
t>0
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\beta_{j}=1+tca^{n}
, so
A+B=1+tca^{n}
. Therefore, we have
A\mathbf{s}_{0}+B\mathbf{s}_{1}=\mathbf{s}_{m}=A_{0}\mathbf{s}_{0}+B_{0}\mathbf{s}_{1}
, so there is an integer
u
such that
A-A_{0}=u\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
and
B-B_{0}=-u\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda
. Hence,

(A+B)-(A_{0}+B_{0})=u\left(\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda-\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda\right)=uc[(a-1)/\lambda]a^{n}.

It follows that

A+B=uc[(a-1)/\lambda]a^{n}+(A_{0}+B_{0})=1+(u+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n},

which implies that
t=(u+1)(a-1)/\lambda
. Since
t\geq 1
, it must be
u\geq 0
. This ends the proof of the claim.

To continue the proof of the theorem, by contradiction, we assume that there is a representation
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
, where the
b_{j}
’s are nonnegative, and
b_{j_{0}}>0
for some
j_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,m-2\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{m}

\displaystyle=a\mathbf{s}_{m-1}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}(ab_{j})\mathbf{s}_{j}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0,j\neq j_{0}}^{m-2}(ab_{j})\mathbf{s}_{j}+ab_{j_{0}}\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}}+(a-1)d

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0,j\neq j_{0}}^{m-2}(ab_{j})\mathbf{s}_{j}+a(b_{j_{0}}-1)\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}}+\mathbf{s}_{j_{0}+1}.

The sum of coefficients in this representation of
\mathbf{s}_{m}
is

\sum_{j=0,j\neq j_{0}}^{m-2}(ab_{j})+a(b_{j_{0}}-1)+1=a\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}-a+1.

By the claim there exists
u_{0}\geq 0
such that
a\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}-a+1=1+(u_{0}+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n}
. Then,
\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}=1+(u_{0}+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n-1}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.5
we have
\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}=1+t_{1}ca^{n}
for some
t_{1}>0
, so that
1+(u_{0}+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n-1}=1+t_{1}ca^{n}
. It follows that
(u_{0}+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]=t_{1}a
; but, since
a
and
(a-1)/\lambda
are relatively prime,
a
must divide
u_{0}+1
. Then,
u_{0}+1\geq a
and
\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}=1+(u_{0}+1)[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n-1}\geq 1+[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n}
.
Therefore,

\mathbf{s}_{m-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\geq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_{j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}=(1+[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n})\mathbf{s}_{0}.

Now, by hypothesis we have
\mathbf{s}_{0}>\lambda\mathbf{R}_{m-1}
, which is equivalent to
[(a-1)/\lambda]\mathbf{s}_{0}\geq a^{m-1}
, and this is equivalent to
\mathbf{s}_{m-1}<(1+[(a-1)/\lambda]ca^{n})\mathbf{s}_{0}
, so we have a contradiction. This ends the proof of Theorem
3.8
.
∎

Example 3.9
.

Let
a\geq 2
and consider the generating sequence
x_{n}=a^{n}-1
. Here,
\lambda=a-1
, and the minimal positive integer
m
such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda=(a^{n}-1)/(a-1)\leq\mathbf{R}_{m}=(a^{m}-1)/(a-1)
, is precisely
m=n
. Clearly,
\mathbf{R}_{n}<\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda=(a^{n+1}-1)/(a-1)
. By Theorem
3.8
,
e(S_{n})=n
.

The condition
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
in Theorem
3.8
is satisfied in many important cases, as in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose that
d>0
and let
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
. If
m
is the smallest positive integer such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda\leq\mathbf{R}_{m}
, then
e(S_{n})=m
.

Proof.

We have to prove that
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
. Suppose, on the contrary, that
\lambda\mathbf{R}_{m}>\mathbf{s}_{1}
. Then,

\lambda a^{m-1}=\lambda\frac{a^{m}-a^{m-1}}{a-1}=\lambda(\mathbf{R}_{m}-\mathbf{R}_{m-1})>\mathbf{s}_{1}-\mathbf{s}_{0}=(a-1)ca^{n}.

It follows that
a^{m-1}>\frac{a-1}{\lambda}ca^{n}
, so
\lambda\mathbf{R}_{m-1}\geq ca^{n}>ca^{n}-d=\mathbf{s}_{0}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Consider a generating sequence of the form
x_{n}=c2^{n}-d
, where
c>1
. If
0<d\leq 2^{n}
and
k
is the smallest positive integer such that
c\leq 2^{k}
, then
e(S_{n})=n+k
.

Proof.

Here we have
a=2
, so
\lambda=1
, since
\lambda
divides
a-1
. Write
c=2^{k}-r
, where
0\leq r<2^{k-1}
. Note that
\mathbf{s}_{0}=2^{n+k}-(r+d)\leq 2^{n+k}-1=\mathbf{R}_{n+k}
. Now, we must show that
\mathbf{R}_{n+k-1}<\mathbf{s}_{0}
. We have
c\geq 2^{k-1}+1
, so

\mathbf{s}_{0}=c2^{n}-d\geq 2^{n+k-1}+2^{n}-d\geq 2^{n+k-1}>\mathbf{R}_{n+k-1}.

This ends the proof.
∎

In a similar way we can prove the following.

Corollary 3.12
.

Consider a generating sequence of the form
x_{n}=c2^{n}-1
. If
k
is the smallest nonnegative integer such that
c\leq 2^{k}
, then
e(S_{n})=n+k
.

Example 3.13
.

1.

Fix
k\geq 2
and consider the generating sequence
x_{n}=(2^{k}-1)2^{n}-1
. By Corollary
3.12
, we have
e(S_{n})=n+k
(see
[
4
]
).

2.

For
k\geq 1
, consider the generating sequence
x_{n}=(2^{k}+1)2^{n}-(2^{k}-1)
. In this case, the smallest power of
2
greater than or equal to
c=2^{k}+1
is
2^{k+1}
, and
d=2^{k}-1
. So, we have
d\leq 2^{n}
if and only if
k\leq n
. Thus, by Corollary
3.11
, if
k\leq n
, then
e(S_{n})=n+k+1
(see
[
9
]
).

3.

Let
k\geq 1
and
x_{n}=(2^{k}+1)2^{n}-(2^{k}-1)
, where
k>n
. Here, we have
c=2^{k}+1
. In this case we have
\mathbf{R}_{n+k-1}<\mathbf{s}_{0}\leq\mathbf{R}_{n+k}
, so by Corollary
3.11
,
e(S_{n})=n+k
(see
[
9
]
).

4.

Let
a\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
, and consider the generating sequence
x_{n}=(a^{k}-1)a^{n}-1
. It is easy to see that, for all
n\geq 1
,
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})=1
. We claim that
e(S_{n})=n+k+1
. To show this, by Proposition
3.10
, we must prove that
\mathbf{R}_{n+k}<\mathbf{s}_{0}\leq\mathbf{R}_{n+k+1}
. In fact, observe first that

\mathbf{s}_{0}=a^{n+k}-a^{n}-1<a^{n+k}+a^{n+k-1}+\cdots+a+1=\mathbf{R}_{n+k+1}.

On the other hand, it is easily seen that
(a-1)\mathbf{s}_{0}\geq a^{n+k}
, from which it follows that
\mathbf{s}_{0}>(a^{n+k}-1)/(a-1)=\mathbf{R}_{n+k}
.

Now we return to the condition
\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda\geq\mathbf{R}_{m}
in Theorem
3.8
. When
d<0
, the conclusion of Theorem
3.8
may not be true, for it may happen that
\mathbf{R}_{m}>\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
. A counterexample can be found by taking
a=2
,
c=1
,
d=-33
, and
n=1
. In this case
m=6
but
e(S_{1})=7
. In the following proposition we give conditions under which
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
is satisfied, although
d<0
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Assume
d<0
. Let
k
and
l
be the only positive integers such that
\mathbf{R}_{k-1}<-d\leq\mathbf{R}_{k}
and
\mathbf{R}_{l-1}<c\leq\mathbf{R}_{l}
. If
n\geq k
and
\gcd(S_{n})=1
, then
\mathbf{s}_{0}\leq\mathbf{R}_{n+l}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}
. Moreover,
e(S_{n})=n+l
.

Proof.

There are positive integers
d_{0}
and
c_{0}
such that
-d=\mathbf{R}_{k-1}+d_{0}
and
c=\mathbf{R}_{l-1}+c_{0}
, where
d_{0}\leq a^{k-1}
and
c_{0}\leq a^{l-1}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{0}=ca^{n}+(-d)=c_{0}a^{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{l-2}a^{n+j}+d_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}a^{j}\leq a^{n+l-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{l-2}a^{n+j}+a^{k-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}a^{j}.

If
n\geq k
, then it follows that
\mathbf{s}_{0}\leq\mathbf{R}_{n+l}
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{1}

\displaystyle=c_{0}a^{n+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{l-2}a^{n+1+j}+d_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}a^{j}

\displaystyle=c_{0}a^{n+1}+\mathbf{R}_{n+l}+d_{0}-a^{k-1}(a^{n-k+1}+\cdots+1)

\displaystyle=c_{0}a^{n+1}+\mathbf{R}_{n+l}+d_{0}-a^{k-1}\mathbf{R}_{n-k+2},

and it is easy to verify that

c_{0}a^{n+1}+d_{0}\geq a^{n+1}+1\geq a^{k-1}\mathbf{R}_{n-k+2},

so
\mathbf{s}_{1}\geq\mathbf{R}_{n+l}
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathbf{s}_{0}>\mathbf{R}_{n+l-1}
, so we have
e(S_{n})=n+l
, by Theorem
3.8
.
∎

4
Residual tuples and the Apéry set
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})

In this section we describe a way of finding the maximum element of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
, and we show a characterization of the elements of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
. Throughout this section, we assume the embedding dimension of
S_{n}
is at least 2. We start with the following proposition, which is a common result found in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
. Recall that
A(m-1)
stands for the set of residual
(m-1)-
tuples.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
m=e(S_{n})
. If
x\in{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
, then there exists
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\in A(m-1)
such that
x=\alpha_{1}\mathbf{s}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
x
. For
x=0
we have the residual
(m-1)-
tuple
(0,\ldots,0)
such that
x=0\mathbf{s}_{1}+\cdots+0\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
. Suppose that
x>0
and let
j=\min\{i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}:x-\mathbf{s}_{i}\in S_{n}\}
. Observe that
j\neq 0
since
x\in{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
. By induction, there exists
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\in A(m-1)
such that
x-\mathbf{s}_{j}=\alpha_{1}\mathbf{s}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
. Hence,
x=\alpha_{1}\mathbf{s}_{1}+\cdots+(\alpha_{j}+1)\mathbf{s}_{j}+\cdots+\alpha_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1}
. To conclude the proof, we only need to show that
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{j}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\in A(m-1)
. If
\alpha_{j}+1=a+1
, then we get
(\alpha_{j}+1)\mathbf{s}_{j}=(a+1)\mathbf{s}_{j}=\mathbf{s}_{j}+a\mathbf{s}_{j}=a\mathbf{s}_{j-1}+\mathbf{s}_{j+1}
. Since
\mathbf{s}_{j+1}\in S_{n}
, we see that
x-\mathbf{s}_{j-1}\in S_{n}
, contradicting the minimality of
j
. If there exists
k>j
such that
\alpha_{k}=a
, then we obtain that
\mathbf{s}_{j}+a\mathbf{s}_{k}=a\mathbf{s}_{j-1}+\mathbf{s}_{k+1}
, so
x-\mathbf{s}_{j-1}\in S_{n}
, which contradicts the minimality of
j
. Also, by the minimality of
j
we have
\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{j-1}=0
, and consequently,
(\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{j}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\in A(m-1)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
t
be a positive integer. Then
\mathbf{R}_{t+1}>\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
for all residual
t-
tuples
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})
.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})
be a residual
t-
tuple. If
\alpha_{j_{0}}=a
for some
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{R}_{j_{0}}+(a-1)\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{t}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{R}_{j_{0}}+\left(\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{t}a^{j}-(t+1-j_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{j_{0}}-1}{a-1}+\left(a^{j_{0}}\mathbf{R}_{t+1-j_{0}}-(t+1-j_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-1}\left(a^{j_{0}}-1+a^{j_{0}}\left(a^{t+1-j_{0}}-1\right)-(a-1)(t+1-j_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a-1}\left(a^{t+1}-1-(a-1)(t+1-j_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{t+1}-(t+1-j_{0})

\displaystyle<\mathbf{R}_{t+1}

In a similar way it can be shown that if
\alpha_{j}<a
for all
j
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}<\mathbf{R}_{t+1}
.
∎

Now, we prove some properties of residual tuples. Recall the colexicographic order
\leq_{c}
on the set of residual
r-
tuples
A(r)
:
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\leq_{c}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
if and only if
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
or there exists
t\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
such that
\alpha_{t}<\beta_{t}
and
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}
for all
i>t
.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
and
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
are residual
r-
tuples, then
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})<_{c}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}<\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
.

Proof.

If
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})<_{c}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
, then there is some
t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
\alpha_{t}<\beta_{t}
and
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}
for
i=t+1,\ldots,r
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}+\alpha_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t}+\sum_{j=t+1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle<\mathbf{R}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t}+\sum_{j=t+1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t}+\sum_{j=t+1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

In the second line above we applied Lemma
4.2
. For the converse, suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}<\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
. Clearly, it cannot be
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
. Now, if
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})>(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r})
, then it would follow that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}>\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
, which is a contradiction. This ends the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For all
r\geq 1
,

\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}:(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in A(r)\right\}=\{0,1,\ldots,\mathbf{R}_{r+1}-1\}.

Moreover, there is an isomorphism of partially ordered sets
\varphi:A(r)\to\{0,\ldots,\mathbf{R}_{r+1}-1\}
given by

\varphi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j},

for all residual
r-
tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
.

Proof.

The minimum and maximum residual
r-
tuples with respect to the co-lexicographic order are
(0,\ldots,0)
and
(0,\ldots,0,a)
, respectively; these
r-
tuples correspond to the integers
0
and
a\mathbf{R}_{r}=\mathbf{R}_{r+1}-1
. So,

\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}:(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in A(r)\right\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,\mathbf{R}_{r+1}-1\}.

Since both sets have
\mathbf{R}_{r+1}
elements, they are equal.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
r\geq 1
. For each integer
t
such that
0\leq t<\mathbf{R}_{r+1}
, there exists a unique residual
r-
tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
such that
t=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\neq(0,\ldots,0,a)
be a residual
r-
tuple. We want to find the immediate successor of
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
in
A(r)
. To do this, we just have to represent

in the form

, and so

is the immediate successor of
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
. We have two cases. If
\alpha_{i}=a
for some

, then

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=1+a\mathbf{R}_{i}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{R}_{i+1}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha_{i+1})\mathbf{R}_{i+1}+\sum_{j=i+2}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}.

Therefore, the immediate successor of
(0,\ldots,0,a,\alpha_{i+1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
is
(0,\ldots,0,0,1+\alpha_{i+1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
. Similarly, if
\alpha_{j}<a
for all

is

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
be a residual
r-

tuples less than or equal to

with respect to the colexicographic order is

1+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}.

Proof.

Let

be the set of
r-

(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
with respect to the co-lexicographic order. If

. Assume the result is true for

. Let

be a residual
(r+1)-

is the disjoint union of subsets

and

, where

is the set of all residual
(r+1)-

(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r+1})
such that

and

is the set of all residual
(r+1)-

(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r+1})
such that
\beta_{r+1}=\alpha_{r+1}
. Each element in

can be represented uniquely in the form
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r+1})
, where

and

is a residual
r-

Now, we give our characterization of the Apéry set

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

,

,

(m-1)-

(4)

Proof.

Let us denote by

the set in (
4
). The size of

is precisely
1+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}=\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda

\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\equiv(a-1)d\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\ ({\rm mod}\ \mathbf{s}_{0}).

Since

Recall that
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
is a numerical semigroup and the Apéry set of
(1/\lambda)\mathbf{s}_{0}
is given by
{\rm Ap}((1/\lambda)S_{n},(1/\lambda)\mathbf{s}_{0})=(1/\lambda){\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})

Now, when

runs over the set of residual

(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})

and

, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\gamma_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\ ({\rm mod}\ \mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda).

\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
and

are less than or equal to

, so

. We have shown that the set of integers

To show that

. By Proposition

, there is a residual
(m-1)-

If we show that

cannot be congruent modulo
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda

(1/\lambda)S_{n}

Now, if
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m-1})

\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\delta_{j}(\mathbf{s}_{j}/\lambda)-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}(\mathbf{s}_{j}/\lambda)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda,&\text{if $\alpha_{i}<a$ for all $i$};\\
(\mathbf{s}_{i+1}/\lambda)-a\mathbf{(}s_{i}/\lambda),&\text{if $\alpha_{i}=a$ for some $i$}.\end{cases}

Note that

5
The Frobenius number

The following result is a consequence of Theorem

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume
d>0
. If
m=e(S_{n})
,
\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
and
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
is the unique residual
(m-1)-
tuple such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda=1+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
, then

\max{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}.

Moreover, for the numerical semigroup
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
, we have

{\rm F}((1/\lambda)S_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}(\mathbf{s}_{j}/\lambda)-\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda.

Proof.

The condition
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
is satisfied by Proposition
3.10
. Then, Theorem
4.7
applies. If
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})<_{c}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
, let
t
be the number of residual
r-
tuples
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m-1})
such that

(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})\leq_{c}(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m-1})<_{c}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}),

and
u
the number of such
r-

(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m-1})
such that
\delta_{i}=a
for some
i
. Then,

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}=(t-u)\mathbf{s}_{1}+u(\mathbf{s}_{1}-a\mathbf{s}_{0})=(t-u)\mathbf{s}_{1}+u(a-1)d>0,

since
t>0,u\leq t
and

. This proves that
\max{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
.
∎

The genus

To calculate the genus of
S_{n}
directly by means of the formula
{\rm g}(S)=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{w\in{\rm Ap}(S,x)}w-\frac{x-1}{2}
, it is useful to compute sums of the form

\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j},

where the sum is taken over all residual
r-
tuples
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
. The following result gives us a nice formula for this kind of sums.

Proposition 6.1
.

For all
r\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a\mathbf{R}_{r}\mathbf{s}_{r+1}+ra^{r+1}\mathbf{s}_{0}\right)

Proof.

First, we have

\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(a^{r+1}+a^{r+1-j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{j}.

This formula follows exactly as in the proof of Theorem 25 in
[
6
]
where, although that proof is for the generating sequence of repunit numbers
\mathbf{R}_{n}=(a^{n}-1)/(a-1)
, it works for a general generating sequence. Now,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(a^{r+1}+a^{r+1-j}\right)\mathbf{s}_{j}

\displaystyle=a^{r+1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(ca^{n+j}-d)+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a^{r+1-j}(ca^{n+j}-d)

\displaystyle=a^{r+1}\left(ca^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{r}a^{j-1}-rd\right)+\left(rca^{r+n+1}-da\sum_{j=1}^{r}a^{r-j}\right)

\displaystyle=ca^{r+n+2}\mathbf{R}_{r}-rda^{r+1}+rca^{r+n+1}-da\mathbf{R}_{r}

\displaystyle=a\mathbf{R}_{r}\mathbf{s}_{r+1}+ra^{r+1}\mathbf{s}_{0}

∎

Example 6.2
.

Consider the generating sequence
x_{n}=5\cdot 3^{n}-1
. To find the genus of
S_{n}
, first we calculate the sum
\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+2})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+2}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)
taken over all residual
(n+2)-
tuples
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+2})
which are less than or equal to
(0,\ldots,0,2,1,0)
with respect to the colexicographic order:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+2})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+2}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+1})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{n}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1}\right)+(2\mathbf{s}_{n}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1}).

In the last expression, the sums are taken over all residual tuples, without restriction. By Applying Proposition
6.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+2})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+2}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(3\mathbf{R}_{n}\mathbf{s}_{n+1}+n3^{n+1}\mathbf{s}_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(3\mathbf{R}_{n-1}\mathbf{s}_{n}+(n-1)3^{n}\mathbf{s}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{R}_{n}\mathbf{s}_{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(3\mathbf{R}_{n-1}\mathbf{s}_{n}+(n-1)3^{n}\mathbf{s}_{0}\right)+\mathbf{R}_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{n}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1})

\displaystyle+(2\mathbf{s}_{n}+\mathbf{s}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((5n+6+25\mathbf{R}_{n})3^{n}+5\mathbf{R}_{n}+1\right)\mathbf{s}_{0}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle g((1/2)S_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\mathbf{s}_{0}/2)}\sum_{(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n+2})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+2}\beta_{j}(\mathbf{s}_{j}/2)\right)-\frac{\mathbf{s}_{0}/2-1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((5n+6+25\mathbf{R}_{n})3^{n}+5\mathbf{R}_{n}+1\right)-\frac{\mathbf{s}_{0}/2-1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(5n+6+25\mathbf{R}_{n})3^{n}.

This way of computing the genus of
S_{n}
is tedious in general. A recursive way of computing the sum
\sum_{(\beta_{j})}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
over all residual
(m-1)-
tuples
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})
, less than or equal to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
in the colexicographic order, can be described as follows: Given the residual
(m-1)-
tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
under the conditions of Theorem
4.7
, define
G_{t}
for
t=1,\ldots,m-1
as follows:

G_{t}=\sum_{(\beta_{j})}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j},

where the left-most sum is taken over all residual
t-
tuples
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{t})
such that
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{t})\leq_{c}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})
. It can be shown that

G_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\mathbf{s}_{0}\frac{\alpha_{t}(\alpha_{t}-1)}{2}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}\right)+\alpha_{t}G_{t-1},

and this can be used to find
{\rm g}(S_{n})
recursively.

7
Pseudo-Frobenius numbers and type

Let
m=e(S_{n}),\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
and assume that
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
. Theorem
4.7
gives us a characterization of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
. If
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
is the only residual
(m-1)-
tuple such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda-1=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
, let
B(m-1)
be the set of all residual
(m-1)
-tuples
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})
that are less than or equal to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
with respect to the colexicographic order. Then, the elements of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
are precisely those elements in
S_{n}
of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\beta_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
, where
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})\in B(m-1)
.

To find the pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
, we must find first the maximal elements of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
, with respect to the order
\leq_{S_{n}}
given by
a\leq_{S_{n}}b
if and only if
b-a\in S_{n}
. Then, the pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
are those integers of the form
m/\lambda-\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda
, where
m
is maximal in
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
, with respect to the order
\leq_{S_{n}}
.

First, we find the maximal
(m-1)-
tuples
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})\in B(m-1)
with respect to the
product order
. We describe these
(m-1)-
tuples as follows: Let
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r}
the only indexes
j\in\{2,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
\alpha_{j}>0
. The maximal
(m-1)-
tuples in
B(m-1)
with respect to the product order can be partitioned into
r+1
subsets of
B(m-1)
,
M_{0},M_{1},\ldots,M_{r}
, as we describe next. For
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}
, let

\displaystyle M_{j}=\{

\displaystyle(a,a-1,\ldots,a-1,\alpha_{j}-1,\alpha_{j+1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}),

\displaystyle(0,a,a-1,\ldots,a-1,\alpha_{j}-1,\alpha_{j+1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}),

\displaystyle(0,0,a,a-1,\ldots,a-1,\alpha_{j}-1,\alpha_{j+1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1}),

\displaystyle\ \ \vdots

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0,a,\alpha_{j_{t}}-1,\alpha_{j_{t}+1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\},

and let
M_{0}=\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})\}
. As we see, the set
M_{0}
has 1 element and
M_{j}
has
j-1
elements for each
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}
.

For
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}
, each
(m-1)-
tuple in
M_{j}
generates an element of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
. Namely, the tuple
(0,\ldots,0,a,a-1,\ldots,a-1,\alpha_{j}-1,\alpha_{j+1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
, where the
a
is in position
i
,
1\leq i<j
, corresponds to the element

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=a\mathbf{s}_{i}+(a-1)\sum_{l=i+1}^{j-1}\mathbf{s}_{l}+(\alpha_{j}-1)\mathbf{s}_{j}+\sum_{l=j+1}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}

\displaystyle=a\mathbf{s}_{j-1}-(j-i-1)(a-1)d+(\alpha_{j}-1)\mathbf{s}_{j}+\sum_{l=j+1}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}+\sum_{l=j+1}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}-(j-i)(a-1)d

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=j}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}-(j-i)(a-1)d.

Also, the tuple
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
corresponds to

\mathbf{f}_{0}:=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}.

This way, the candidates to maximal elements of
{\rm Ap}(S_{n},\mathbf{s}_{0})
with respect to
\leq_{S_{n}}
are the elements
\mathbf{f}_{i,j}
, where
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\},1\leq i<j
, along with the element
\mathbf{f}_{0}
.

Now, assume that
1\leq t<r
. For
1\leq i<j_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{i,j_{t}}-\mathbf{f}_{i+j_{t+1}-j_{t},j_{t+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=j_{t}}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}-(j_{t}-i)(a-1)d-\sum_{l=j_{t+1}}^{m-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}+(j_{t}-i)(a-1)d

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=j_{t}}^{j_{t+1}-1}\alpha_{l}\mathbf{s}_{l}\in S_{n}.

This shows that
\mathbf{f}_{j_{t+1},i+j_{t+1}-j_{t}}\leq_{S_{n}}\mathbf{f}_{j_{t},i}
. If we set
j_{0}=1
, the candidates to pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
S_{n}
reduce to the elements
\mathbf{f}_{i,j_{t}}
,
t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
j_{t-1}\leq i<j_{t}
, along with the element
\mathbf{f}_{0}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
m=e(S_{n}),\lambda=\gcd(S_{n})
and assume that
\mathbf{R}_{m}\leq\mathbf{s}_{1}/\lambda
. If
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-1})
is the only residual
(m-1)-
tuple such that
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda-1=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}
, then

{\rm PF}(S_{n})\subseteq\{\mathbf{f}_{0}\}\cup\{\mathbf{f}_{i,j_{t}}:t\in\{1,\ldots,r\},j_{t-1}\leq i<j_{t}\}.

In particular, the type of
S_{n}
satisfies the inequality
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq e(S_{n})-1
.

Proof.

It only remains to prove that
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq e(S_{n})-1
. Now, for
t\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, there are

j_{t}-j_{t-1}
elements of the form
\mathbf{f}_{i,j_{t}}
,
j_{t-1}\leq i<j_{t}
. Then, the number of candidates to pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
S_{n}
is

1+\sum_{t=1}^{r}(j_{t}-j_{t-1})=j_{r}\leq m-1=e(S_{n})-1.

This ends the proof.
∎

The equality
{\rm t}(S_{n})=e(S_{n})-1
holds in many cases, for instance, for the semigroups considered in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Moreover, as we see in the proof of Theorem
7.1
,
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq j_{r}
, where
j_{r}
is the greatest integer
j
in
\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
\alpha_{j}>0
. For instance, in part 2 of Example
5.2
, for
\mathbf{s}_{0}=5\cdot 3^{n}-1
we found that
e(S_{n})=n+3
, while
\mathbf{s}_{0}/\lambda-1=2\mathbf{R}_{n}+\mathbf{R}_{n+1}
, which implies that
{\rm t}(S_{n})\leq n+1
. This example shows that the type of
S_{n}
can be strictly less than
e(S_{n})-1
.

Finally, a conjecture of Wilf on numerical semigroups establishes that for any numerical semigroup
S
, if
n(S)
is the number of elements in
S
less than
{\rm F}(S)
, then
{\rm F}(S)+1\leq n(S)\cdot e(S)
. This conjectures has been verified for various families of numerical semigroups, see
[
1
]
. Here, we can prove that Wilf’s conjecture is true for the numerical semigroup
(1/\lambda)S_{n}
, under the hypothesis of Theorem
7.1
. In fact, this follows by
[
2
, Theorem 20]
, which states the inequality
{\rm F}(S)+1\leq n(S)({\rm t}(S)+1)
for any numerical semigroup
S
. In our case, we have
{\rm t}(S_{n})+1\leq e(S_{n})
, so Wilf’s conjecture follows trivially for
S_{n}
, under the hypothesis of Theorem
7.1
.

Agol cycles of pseudo-Anosov 3-braids
Elaina Aceves, Keiko Kawamuro

Abstract
. An Agol cycle is a complete invariant of the conjugacy class of a pseudo-Anosov mapping class. We study necessary and sufficient conditions for equivalent Agol cycles of pseudo-Anosov 3-braids.



1.
Introduction

Nielsen-Thurston’s classification states that every surface homeomorphism is isotopic to either a periodic, reducible or pseudo-Anosov map
[
Thu88
]
.
If a map
\phi
is pseudo-Anosov, there are two transverse, singular measured foliations on the surface and a dilatation
\lambda>1
such that
\phi
stretches along one foliation by
\lambda
and the other by
1/\lambda
.

When a surface is an
n
-punctured disk
D_{n}
, the mapping class group
{\mathcal{MCG}}(D_{n})
is isomorphic to the

n
stranded braid group
B_{n}

[
Bir74
]
.
Suppose that a braid
\beta\in B_{n}\simeq{\mathcal{MCG}}(D_{n})
is a pseudo-Anosov mapping class.

By definition, the dilatation is an invariant of the conjugacy class of a pseudo-Anosov map.
The dilatation can be the first tool for the conjugacy problem since it is often easy to compute.
However, the downside of the dilatation is that it is not always an effective conjugacy class invariant as demonstrated in Theorem
8.4
:

Theorem
8.4
.

There are infinitely many integers

x,y
and
z
, such that
the braids
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{z}
and

\beta^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{z}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{-1}

belong to distinct conjugacy classes but
have the same dilatation

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-4})

where

(1.1)

\gamma=\gamma(x,y,z)={\tt{sgn}}(xyz)(-2-x-y+xz+yz+xyz).

Fortunately, there exists a stronger invariant.
The Agol cycle
[
Ago11
]
(see Definition
1.2
) is another conjugacy class invariant of a pseudo-Anosov mapping class on a surface
S
, and more importantly, it is a complete conjugacy invariant up to the center of
{\mathcal{MCG}}(S)
.

An Agol cycle is a sequence of measured train tracks generated by maximal splitting.

A measured train track is a combinatorial object encoding the transverse measured foliation of a pseudo-Anosov map.
(It is also called an invariant train track since the train track is invariant under action of the pseudo-Anosov map, and often we call it a train track without the adjective measured or invariant for simplicity.)
It is a graph and each edge is labeled by a positive number called its weight (or measure).
As depicted in Figure
1
, at each vertex three edges meet tangentially and the weights satisfy the
switch condition

a=b+c
.
For a detailed definition of a measured train track,
see Section 2 of Agol’s paper
[
Ago11
]
or Chapter 15 of Farb and Margalit’s book
[
FM12
]
.

Figure 1.
A vertex of a train track where the weights satisfy
a=b+c
.

Maximal splitting

.

[

An

\tau_{p}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup\tau_{q-1}\rightharpoonup\tau_{q}=\lambda^{-1}\beta(\tau_{p})

. The

Definition 1.3
.

We say that two Agol cycles are

Similarly, two Agol cycles are

if they are equivalent after taking mirror image.

Theorem 1.4
.

[

Every pseudo-Anosov mapping class has an Agol cycle.
Furthermore, two pseudo-Anosov maps are in the same conjugacy class up to center of
{\mathcal{MCG}}(S)
if and only if they have equivalent Agol cycles.

A known example of an Agol cycle for a pseudo-Anosov 4-braid can be found in Agol’s paper
[

In general, it is not easy to compute Agol cycles by hand.
Therefore, necessary or sufficient conditions for equivalent Agol cycles will be helpful to solve the conjugacy problem.

Our new conditions (3), (4) and (5) have different characteristics as follows:

An Agol cycle contains a large amount of information and each train track in the cycle can be very complicated with many twists. After untwisting (which is a homeomorphism operation), we will show that there are only four types of train tracks that appear in a cycle (Type I, II, I’, II’ defined in Figure
6
).
In Condition (3), we focus on the topological types (Type I, II, I’, II’) and forget the numerical data of the weights of the train track edges.

In Condition (4), we estimate a particular algebraic number
\alpha
(the MP-ratio defined in Proposition
2.2
) associated to the pseudo-Anosov braid in the Farey tessellation.
Since the number
\alpha
is irrational, there exists an infinite sequence of nested intervals (
J_{l}\supset J_{l+1}\supset J_{l+2}\supset\cdots
) in the Farey tessellation that converges to
\alpha
.
For each pair of adjacent intervals
J_{t}\supset J_{t+1}
, we may forget their exact location in the Farey tessellation, and instead focus on the relative location of the sub-interval
J_{t+1}
with respect to
J_{t}
. In the Farey tessellation,
J_{t}
splits into two sub-intervals. We record whether
J_{t+1}
is in the left-subinterval (
L
) or the right-subinterval (
R
) of
J_{t}
. This is how we generate an
{\bf LR}
-sequence.

In Condition (5), we focus on just one train track in the infinite sequence.
In contrast to (3), we forget the topological side and focus on the numerical data of the edge weights of train tracks.
Here is a useful consequence of (5): If the algebraic numbers (MP-ratios)
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
do not satisfy
{\tt A}+{\tt B}\alpha+{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt D}\alpha\alpha^{\prime}=0
for any
{\tt A},{\tt B},{\tt C},{\tt D}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the 3-braids
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are not conjugate.

A concrete example is presented in Example
5.4
.

In Theorem
7.1
, we explore the converse direction of (2)
\Rightarrow
(5) in Theorem
6.1
.

Weak version of Theorem
7.1

\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
be pseudo-Anosov 3-braids.
Suppose that there exist

4.
Triple-weight train track and Farey sequence

In this section, we establish a relationship between triple-weight train tracks and Farey sequences that we use to prove our main results Theorem
6.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n})\setminus\mathbb{Q}
be the MP-ratio of some pseudo-Anosov 3-braid.
We will define an infinite set of intervals and then construct a map
T
which associates to each interval a 4-tuple of numbers.
There is a unique sequence such that

I_{1,i_{1}}\supset I_{2,i_{2}}\supset I_{3,i_{3}}\supset\cdots\supset\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}I_{k,i_{k}}=\{\alpha\}
.
Our goal of the section is to prove Theorem
4.9
, where we relate the interval
I_{k,i_{k}}
and the 4-tuple
(x_{k},y_{k}\ ;\ z_{k},w_{k})
under the map
T
.

4.1.
Farey sequence

First, we recall the
Farey sum
,
\boxplus
.
Given two fractions,
\frac{s}{t}
and
\frac{s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}
, we define:

\frac{s}{t}\boxplus\frac{s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}:=\frac{s+s^{\prime}}{t+t^{\prime}}.

Notice that if
\frac{s}{t}<\frac{s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}
, then
\frac{s}{t}<\frac{s}{t}\boxplus\frac{s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}<\frac{s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}}
.

For
k=0,1,2,\dots
, we inductively define an ordered set
L_{k}
of fractions with cardinality
2^{k}+1
.
The set
L_{k}
is called the
Farey sequence
of order
k+1
.
Define

L_{0}=\{a_{0,1},a_{0,2}\}=\left\{\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{1}\right\}

where we are deliberately not simplifying the fractions.
Assume
L_{k}=\{a_{k,1},a_{k,2},\cdots,a_{k,2^{k}+1}\}
where

\frac{0}{1}=a_{k,1}<a_{k,2}<\cdots<a_{k,2^{k}}<a_{k,2^{k}+1}=\frac{1}{1}.

Define

L_{k+1}=\{a_{k+1,1},\cdots,a_{k+1,2^{k+1}+1}\}=L_{k}\cup\{a_{k,i}\boxplus a_{k,i+1}|i=1,2,\cdots,2^{k}\}

as a set and then reorder them to have

\frac{0}{1}=a_{k+1,1}<a_{k+1,2}<\cdots<a_{k+1,2^{k+1}+1}=\frac{1}{1}.

Example 4.1
.

\displaystyle L_{1}=\left\{a_{1,1},a_{1,2},a_{1,3}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{1}\right\}

\displaystyle L_{2}=\left\{a_{2,1},\cdots,a_{2,5}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{1}\right\}

\displaystyle L_{3}=\left\{a_{3,1},\cdots,a_{3,9}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{0}{1},\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{5},\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{5},\frac{2}{3},\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{1}\right\}

Definition 4.2
.

Let
n=\lfloor\frac{1}{\alpha}\rfloor
. For
k=0,1,\cdots
and
i=1,2,\cdots,2^{k}
, define the following open interval:

I_{k,i}:=\left(\frac{1}{n+a_{k,i+1}},\frac{1}{n+a_{k,i}}\right)

In particular, we have that
I_{0,1}=\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{1}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{0}{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right)
and

(4.1)

\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{2^{k}}I_{k,i}=\left[\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right]\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{n+a}\mid a\in L_{k}\right\}.

.

For

,

\displaystyle I_{1,2}\cup I_{1,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{1}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\cup\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{0}{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right]\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{1}},\ \frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}},\ \frac{1}{n+\frac{0}{1}}\right\}

For

,

\displaystyle I_{2,4}\cup I_{2,3}\cup I_{2,2}\cup I_{2,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{1}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{2}{3}}\right)\cup\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{2}{3}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\cup\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{3}}\right)\cup\left(\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{3}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{0}{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{n}\right]\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{1}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{2}{3}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{2}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{1}{3}},\frac{1}{n+\frac{0}{1}}\right\}

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
k=0,1,\dots

(4.2)

\displaystyle I_{k,i}\setminus\left\{\frac{1}{n+a_{k+1,2i}}\right\}

\displaystyle I_{k+1,2i}\sqcup I_{k+1,2i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n+a_{k+1,2i+1}},\frac{1}{n+a_{k+1,2i}}\right)\sqcup\left(\frac{1}{n+a_{k+1,2i}},\frac{1}{n+a_{k+1,2i-1}}\right)

A standard fact about Farey sequence is that

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

, for every

with

In particular, the sequence

6.
Necessary conditions for equivalent Agol cycles

We are ready to state the main theorem that gives a number of necessary conditions for equivalent Agol cycles. Those conditions have different characteristics: topological, number theoretic, and numerical.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
be pseudo-Anosov 3-braids.
The statements (1) and (2) are equivalent (proved by Agol).
The statement (2) implies (3), (3) implies (4), and (4) implies (5).

(1)

\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are conjugate in
B_{3}
up to a center element.

(2)

\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
have equivalent Agol cycles.

(3)

(Topological condition)
There exist
l
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}_{l})={\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m})
and the triple-weight train track sequences

\mathcal{T}_{l}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{T}_{l+1}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{T}_{l+2}\rightharpoonup\cdots\qquad(\mbox{for }\beta)

and

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m+1}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m+2}\rightharpoonup\cdots\qquad(\mbox{for }\beta^{\prime})

give the same periodic I-II-I’-II’-sequence.

(4)

(Number theoretic condition)
There exist
l
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

{\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}_{l})={\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m})
and the nested Farey interval sequences

J_{l}\supset J_{l+1}\supset J_{l+2}\supset\cdots
for
\beta
and

J^{\prime}_{m}\supset J^{\prime}_{m+1}\supset J^{\prime}_{m+2}\supset\cdots
for
\beta^{\prime}

give the same periodic
{\bf LR}
-sequences.

(5)

(Numerical condition)
There exist
l
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that the 4-ratios of
\mathcal{T}_{l}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}
are the same; namely,

\frac{x_{l}+y_{l}\alpha}{z_{l}+w_{l}\alpha}=\frac{x^{\prime}_{m}+y^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}_{m}+w^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime}}.

The same
LR
-sequence condition in (4) implies the
existence of integers
A,B,C,D
with
AD-BC=1
or
-1
(if
{\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}_{l})=-{\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m})
we have
AD-BC=-1
) such that

\frac{1}{\alpha}-n=\frac{A(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+B}{C(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+D}.

In particular, when
l=m=1
we obtain

\frac{1}{\alpha}-n=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime}.

More detail and precise numbers are given in the proof of the theorem.

A feature of condition (5) is that it is not seeing the entire infinite sequences like (3) and (4), but rather focusing on two particular train tracks
\mathcal{T}_{l}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) is due to Agol
[
Ago11
]
. See also Margalit’s talk slides
[
Mar16
]
.

The statement (2) implies (3) since Types I, II, I’ and II’ (introduced in Theorem
3.1
and illustrated in Figure
6
) are defined up to homeomorphism and these are the only homeomorphism types that appear in Agol cycles.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4):
We need to show for all
t=0,1,2,\cdots
,
J_{l+t}
and
J^{\prime}_{m+t}
correspond to the same letter, either
{\bf L}
or
{\bf R}
.

We first check the base case (
t=0
):
By Lemma
4.8
and Definition
5.2
, the condition
\epsilon_{l}={\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}_{l})={\tt{sgn}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m})=\epsilon^{\prime}_{m}
implies that the Farey intervals
J_{l}
and
J^{\prime}_{m}
correspond to the same letter, either
{\bf L}
or
{\bf R}
.

To see the next stage (
t=1
), we first note that by the assumption (3) and Corollary
3.7
,
the 4-tuples of
\mathcal{T}_{l+1}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m+1}
have the same type (Type 1 or Type 2).
Then by Corollary
4.10
,
\epsilon_{l}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{m}
implies that
\epsilon_{l+1}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{m+1}
.
The argument for the base case applies here and we can conclude that
J_{l+1}
and
J^{\prime}_{m+1}
correspond to the same letter, either
{\bf L}
or
{\bf R}
.

Inductively, for all
t=2,3,\cdots,
we can show that
J_{l+t}
and
J^{\prime}_{m+t}
correspond to the same letter, either
{\bf L}
or
{\bf R}
.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(5):
We first note that condition (4) is equivalent to the existence of a 2
\times
2 matrix

N=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
that takes the Farey interval

J_{l+t}=(\frac{u_{t}}{v_{t}},\frac{w_{t}}{s_{t}})
to

simultaneously; which means

N\begin{pmatrix}u_{t}&w_{t}\\
v_{t}&s_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u^{\prime}_{t}&w^{\prime}_{t}\\
v^{\prime}_{t}&s^{\prime}_{t}\end{pmatrix}

for each
t=0,1,\cdots
.
Since the nested sequences have convergences
\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\{J_{k}\}=\{\frac{1}{\alpha}-n\}
and
\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\{J^{\prime}_{k}\}=\{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime}\}
, it is equivalent to

\frac{1}{\alpha}-n=\frac{A(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+B}{C(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+D}.

In other words, the following vectors are parallel (the symbol
\parallel
stands for parallel):

(6.1)

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\alpha}-n\\
1\end{pmatrix}\parallel N\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime}\\
1\end{pmatrix}

The matrix
N
can be explicitly computed as follows:
By Lemma
4.7
, the Farey numbers
\frac{u_{t}}{v_{t}},\frac{w_{t}}{s_{t}},\frac{u^{\prime}_{t}}{v^{\prime}_{t}},\frac{w^{\prime}_{t}}{s^{\prime}_{t}}
for
t=0
satisfy one of the two cases (here we use the assumption
\epsilon_{l}=\epsilon^{\prime}_{m}
):

\displaystyle\bullet\quad\frac{u_{0}}{v_{0}}=\frac{c_{l}}{a_{l}},\quad\frac{w_{0}}{s_{0}}=\frac{c_{l}+d_{l}}{a_{l}+b_{l}},\quad\frac{u^{\prime}_{0}}{v^{\prime}_{0}}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{m}}{a^{\prime}_{m}},\quad\frac{w^{\prime}_{0}}{s^{\prime}_{0}}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{m}+d^{\prime}_{m}}{a^{\prime}_{m}+b^{\prime}_{m}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\epsilon_{l}=\epsilon_{m}=1,

\displaystyle\bullet\quad\frac{u_{0}}{v_{0}}=\frac{c_{l}+d_{l}}{a_{l}+b_{l}},\quad\frac{w_{0}}{s_{0}}=\frac{c_{l}}{a_{l}},\quad\frac{u^{\prime}_{0}}{v^{\prime}_{0}}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{m}+d^{\prime}_{m}}{a^{\prime}_{m}+b^{\prime}_{m}},\quad\frac{w^{\prime}_{0}}{s^{\prime}_{0}}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{m}}{a^{\prime}_{m}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\epsilon_{l}=\epsilon_{m}=-1.

In either case,

N=\begin{pmatrix}c_{l}&d_{l}\\
a_{l}&b_{l}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c^{\prime}_{m}&d^{\prime}_{m}\\
a^{\prime}_{m}&b^{\prime}_{m}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

and
\det N=(-\epsilon_{l})(-\epsilon^{\prime}_{m})=1
since
\epsilon_{l}=\epsilon_{m}
.

Next, we note that

\begin{pmatrix}a_{l}&-c_{l}\\
-b_{l}&d_{l}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}_{m}&-c^{\prime}_{m}\\
-b^{\prime}_{m}&d^{\prime}_{m}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{l}&d_{l}\\
a_{l}&b_{l}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c^{\prime}_{m}&d^{\prime}_{m}\\
a^{\prime}_{m}&b^{\prime}_{m}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=-N.

By (
6.1
) we have

(6.2)

\begin{pmatrix}a_{l}&-c_{l}\\
-b_{l}&d_{l}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\alpha}-n\\
1\end{pmatrix}\parallel\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}_{m}&-c^{\prime}_{m}\\
-b^{\prime}_{m}&d^{\prime}_{m}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Using Theorem
4.9
, we can rewrite the 4-ratio of
\mathcal{T}_{l}
as

\frac{x_{l}+y_{l}\alpha}{z_{l}+w_{l}\alpha}=\frac{a_{l}-(a_{l}n+c_{l})\alpha}{-b_{l}+(b_{l}n+d_{l})\alpha}=\frac{a_{l}(\frac{1}{\alpha}-n)-c_{l}}{-b_{l}(\frac{1}{\alpha}-n)+d_{l}},

which is equal to the slope of the left hand side vector of (

).
Likewise the 4-ratio of

\frac{x_{l}+y_{l}\alpha}{z_{l}+w_{l}\alpha}=\frac{x^{\prime}_{m}+y^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}_{m}+w^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime}}
.
∎

\Rightarrow

\beta

.

Let
\beta
and

Suppose that

and

(1)

{\tt{Type}}(\mathcal{T}_{l+1})={\tt{Type}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m+1})

(2)

{\tt{Type}}(\mathcal{T}_{l+1})={\tt{Type}}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m+1})^{\prime}

In Case (1),

\beta
and

have equivalent Agol cycles, and in Case (2) they have mirror equivalent Agol cycles.

Example 7.2
.

Related to Example

,

.

It is interesting to point out that

Below is a lemma needed to prove Theorem

.

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}_{l}
and
\mathcal{T}_{m}^{\prime}
have the same 4-ratio.
Then for all

\mathcal{T}_{l+t}

(either Type 1 or Type 2).

Proof of Lemma

Define
{\tt A},{\tt B},{\tt C},{\tt D}

{\tt A}+{\tt B}\alpha+{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt D}\alpha\alpha^{\prime}=(x_{l}+y_{l}\alpha)(z^{\prime}_{m}+w^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime})-(z_{l}+w_{l}\alpha)(x^{\prime}_{m}+y^{\prime}_{m}\alpha^{\prime}).

Since
\mathcal{T}_{l}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{m}
have the same 4-ratio

{\tt A}+{\tt B}\alpha+{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt D}\alpha\alpha^{\prime}=0.

Using Theorem
4.9
we may describe
{\tt A},{\tt B},{\tt C},{\tt D}
as follows.

\displaystyle{\tt A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}(-a_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}+b_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})

\displaystyle{\tt B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}\left((a_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})n+(c_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}-d_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle{\tt C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}\left((a_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})n^{\prime}+(a_{l}d_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}c_{m}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle{\tt D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}\left((a_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})nn^{\prime}+(a_{l}d_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}c_{m}^{\prime})n+(c_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}-d_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})n^{\prime}+(c_{l}d_{m}^{\prime}-d_{l}c_{m}^{\prime})\right)

We have:

\displaystyle{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha({\tt D}\alpha^{\prime}+B)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\tt D}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt B}}{{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt A}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}-n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{({\tt D}+n{\tt C})\alpha^{\prime}+({\tt B}+n{\tt A})}{{\tt C}\alpha^{\prime}+{\tt A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-({\tt B}+n{\tt A})(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})-({\tt D}+n{\tt C})-n^{\prime}({\tt B}+n{\tt A})}{{\tt A}(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+{\tt C}+{\tt A}n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}(d_{l}a_{m}^{\prime}-c_{l}b_{m}^{\prime})(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}(c_{l}d_{m}^{\prime}-d_{l}c_{m}^{\prime})}{\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}(-a_{l}b_{m}^{\prime}+b_{l}a_{m}^{\prime})(\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-n^{\prime})+\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{m}^{\prime}(a_{l}d_{m}^{\prime}-b_{l}c_{m}^{\prime})}

From the last fraction, we define a matrix

so that the following vectors are parallel:

(7.1)

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\alpha}-n\\

8.
Dilatation is preserved under 3-braid flypes

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

, which states that the dilatation is preserved under flype moves.
In addition, as byproducts of this section, we describe how to compute the transition matrix of a given 3-braid (thus the dilatation) in the proof of Lemma

8.1.
Birman-Menasco’s classification of 3-braids

We briefly review well-known facts on 3-braid flypes that are relevant to this paper.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\varepsilon=\pm 1
and
x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{\varepsilon}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{z}
and

then we say that
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are related by an
\varepsilon
-flype.

See Figure
12

If
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are conjugate (resp. not conjugate), then the flype is called
degenerate
(resp.
non-degenerate

Figure 12.
(Definition

Non-degenerate flypes play a significant role in knot theory.
Flypes are used in the classification of 3-braids in Birman and Menasco’s work
[

]
, in Markov’s Theorem without Stabilization
[
BM06a
]

[

Many other transversally simple knots admit a negative flype like those found by Etnyre and Honda
[

]
(cf. Matsuda and Menasco
[

[

]

In the Tait flype conjecture, Thistlethwaite and Menasco proved that two reduced alternating diagrams of an alternating link are related by a sequence of flypes
[

It is easy to see that a flype move preserves the topological link type of the braid closure.
On the contrary, the following Birman and Menasco’s 3-braid classification theorem states that a flype changes the conjugacy class in general.

Theorem 8.2
.

[
BM93
]

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a link type of braid index three. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

There exists a unique conjugacy class of 3-braid representatives of
\mathcal{L}
.

(2)

There exists two conjugacy classes of 3-braid representatives of
\mathcal{L}
.

Case
(2)
happens if and only if
\mathcal{L}
has a 3-braid representative that admits a non-degenerate flype.

Moreover, Ko and Lee determine all the non-degenerate flypes.

Theorem 8.3
.

[
KL99
, Theorem 5]

The 3-braids
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{\varepsilon}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{z}
and
\beta^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{z}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{\varepsilon}

•

Neither
x
nor
y
is equal to
0,\,\varepsilon,\,2\varepsilon
or
z+\varepsilon

•

x\neq y
, and

•

|z|\geq 2
.

Suppose that

A

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{z}
and

\beta^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}^{x}\sigma_{2}^{z}\sigma_{1}^{y}\sigma_{2}^{-1}
be pseudo-Anosov 3-braids related by a negative flype.
For large
x,y
and
z
, the transition matrices
M
and
M^{\prime}
associated to
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
respectively are the following:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\tt{sgn}}(xyz)\left(\begin{matrix}-1-y&{\tt{sgn}}(z)(x+y+xy)\\
{\tt{sgn}}(z)(1-z-yz)&-1-x+xz+yz+xyz\\
\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle M^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\tt{sgn}}(xyz)\left(\begin{matrix}-1+yz&-x-y+xyz\\
-1+z+yz&-1-x-y+xz+xyz\\
\end{matrix}\right)

Furthermore, we have
\det(M)=\det(M^{\prime})=1
and

{\rm{tr}}(M)={\rm{tr}}(M^{\prime})=\gamma(x,y,z)={\tt{sgn}}(xyz)(-2-x-y+xz+yz+xyz).

Remark 8.8
.

There is a bijection between the set of Case 3 braids and the set of Case 5 braids.
Suppose
{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt z}>0
.
Then

\textrm{(Case 3) }\sigma_{1}^{-{\tt x}}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{\tt y}\sigma_{2}^{-{\tt z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm conj}}{{\sim}}\sigma_{2}^{-{\tt z}}\sigma_{1}^{-{\tt x}}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{\tt y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm rev}}{{\sim}}\sigma_{1}^{\tt y}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{-{\tt x}}\sigma_{2}^{-{\tt z}}\textrm{ (Case 5)}

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm conj}}{{\sim}}
means conjugation and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm rev}}{{\sim}}
means reverse orientation or read the word backward.
Thus swapping
{\tt x}
and
{\tt y}
gives a bijection between the two sets.

Similarly, we can find a bijection between the sets for Case 4 and Case 6 braids by swapping
{\tt x}
and
{\tt y}
.

Remark 8.9
.

In Proposition
8.7
, we assume that
x,y,z\gg 1
.
More precisely in Case 1, for example, we require
(x-1)-\frac{1}{(y-1)-\frac{1}{z}}>\alpha
.

Let
a
and
b
be positive integers.
After scaling, we can identify W
(a,b)
with W
(\alpha,1)
for some
\alpha=\frac{a}{b}
.
We start with W
(\alpha,1)
and apply
\sigma_{1}^{-x}
.
If
x>\alpha
, we obtain

W(\alpha,1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{1}^{-x}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M(x-\alpha,1).

If
x-1>\alpha
, we then obtain

M(x-\alpha,1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{2}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}W(x-\alpha,x-1-\alpha).

If
(x-1)-\frac{1}{y-1}>\alpha
, we obtain

W(x-\alpha,x-1-\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{1}^{-y}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M((y-1)(x-1)-1-\alpha(y-1),(x-1)-\alpha).

If
(x-1)-\frac{1}{(y-1)-\frac{1}{z}}>\alpha
, we further obtain

(8.4)

\displaystyle M((y-1)(x-1)-1-\alpha(y-1),(x-1)-\alpha)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{2}^{-z}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle W((y-1)(x-1)-1-\alpha(y-1),(x-1)(zy-z-1)-z-\alpha(zy-z-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W((1-y)\alpha-x-y+xy,(1+z-yz)\alpha+(1-x-xz-yz+xyz))

The expression in (
8.4
) matches with the matrix
M_{1}
in (
8.2
).
Therefore, as long as

(8.5)

(x-1)-\frac{1}{(y-1)-\frac{1}{z}}>\alpha

holds, the transition matrix
M_{1}
is valid.

Invariant Part of Class Groups and Distribution of Relative Class Group
Weitong Wang

Abstract
. We generalize the work of Roquette and Zassenhaus on the invariant part of the class groups to the relative class groups. Thus, we can show some statistical results as follows. For abelian extensions over a fixed number field K K , we show infinite C p C_{p} -moments for the Sylow p p -subgroup of the relative class group when p p divides the degree of the extension. For sextic number fields with A 4 A_{4} -closure, we can show infinite C 2 C_{2} -moments for the Sylow 2 2 -subgroup of the relative class group when the extensions run over a fixed Galois cubic field.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the distribution of (relative) class groups of number fields.
Let us use an example to explain the notion briefly.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of quadratic number fields ordered by the absolute discriminant
d
.
Define

N_{d}(\mathcal{E},X)=\#\{K\in\mathcal{E}\mid d_{K}<X\},

which is the function that counts quadratic number fields ordered by discriminant.
Then we can define the notations of probability and moments for class groups.
Let
A
be a finite abelian group, and
p
be a rational prime.
Define the
p
-rank of
A
, denoted by
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A
, as the largest number
r
so that there exists some injective group homomorphism
C_{p}^{r}\to A
.
For each non-negative integer
r
, define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\cong A):=

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{K\in\mathcal{E}\mid d_{K}<X\text{ and }\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\cong A\}}{N_{d}(\mathcal{E},X)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r):=

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{K\in\mathcal{E}\mid d_{K}<X\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r\}}{N_{d}(\mathcal{E},X)},

and call it the probability of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\cong A
, resp.
\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r
.
Define the
A
-moment of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
to be

\mathbb{E}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{E}\\
d_{K}<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert}{N_{d}(\mathcal{E},X)}.

When
p
is an odd prime, Cohen and Lenstra
[
2
]
gives the prediction for the probability distribution of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong A
, where
A
is a finite abelian
p
-group.
When the set of fields
\mathcal{E}
is generalized to the set of Galois
G
-fields, where
G
is a finite group, Cohen and Martinet
[
4
]
gives the corresponding generalization of Cohen-Lenstra Heuristics.
The method of Cohen and Martinet could be applied to non-Galois cases and obtain the corresponding predictions.
See the author and Wood
[
18
]
.
Though this area is widely open, there are some proven results.
A famous one is obtained by Davenport and Heilbronn
[
6
]
.
In the context of distribution of class groups, we can translate their result into the following:
for quadratic number fields, the
C_{3}
-moment of class groups is exactly what is predicted by Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet Heuristics.
Another result is the work of Alex Smith
[
16
]
, where he proves that the distribution of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfies the Gerth’s Conjecture
[
9
]
when
K
runs over quadratic number fields.

We discuss in detail the case where
p=2
and
K
quadratic.
If we apply Genus Theory (see Ishida
[
10
]
for example) to quadratic number fields, then

\omega(d_{K})-1\leq\operatorname{rk}_{2}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq\omega(d_{K}),

where
\omega(n)
counts all the distinct prime factors of an integer
n
.
This implies that for each non-negative integer
r
, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{2}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r)=0,

and

\mathbb{E}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{2})\rvert)=+\infty.

We can call this phenomenon “zero-probability”, resp. “infinite moment” in short.
This means that the distribution of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is qualitatively different from other Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
.
In particular, the original Cohen-Lenstra Heuristics
cannot
be applied to this case (there is literally no prediction from the heuristics).
This is a motivation for Gerth’s Conjecture.
And we will follow this phenomenon in this paper.

Let us introduce some notations so that we can make statements clearly.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix a number field
K
.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{K}
the set of all finite primes of
K
.
When
K=\mathbb{Q}
, let
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the set of all finite rational primes.
Given a set
\mathfrak{R}
of ideals of
K
, define
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}
as the set of all primes in
\mathfrak{R}
.

An example of
\mathfrak{R}
is the set given by an ideal class of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
.
Since there are multiple ways to describe field extensions, we give the following two definitions to make terms like “the set of all non-Galois cubic number fields” precise.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group.
Fix a number field
k
.
Define a
G
-extension of
k
to be an isomorphism class of pairs
(K,\psi)
, where
K
is a Galois extension of
k
, and
\psi:G(K/k)\cong G
is an isomorphism.
An isomorphism of pairs
(\tau,m_{\tau}):(K,\psi)\to(K^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
is defined to be an isomorphism
\tau:K\to K^{\prime}
such that the map
m_{\tau}:G(K/k)\to G(K^{\prime}/k)
sending
g
to
\tau\circ g\circ\tau^{-1}
satisfies
\psi^{\prime}\circ m_{\tau}=\psi
.
We sometimes leave the
\psi
implicit, but this is always what we mean by a
G
-extension.
The
G
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
are also called
G
-fields
.

Definition 1.3
.

A transitive permutation group is defined to be a subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
such that
G
acts on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
transitively.
Let
G
be a transitive permutation group.
Fix a number field
k
.
Define
{\mathcal{E}}(G;k)
to be the isomorphic classes of pairs
(K,\psi)
such that the Galois closure
(\hat{K},\psi)
is a
G
-field and
K=\hat{K}^{G_{1}}
, where
G_{1}=\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(1)
is the stabilizer of
1
.
If the base field
k=\mathbb{Q}
, then we just omit it and write
{\mathcal{E}}(G):={\mathcal{E}}(G,\mathbb{Q})
.

There are alternative ways to define the set of fields.
See Wood
[
8
, p.292]
for their differences and connections.
Note that if
G
is abelian, and we view
G
as a transitive permutation group by its operation on itself, then
\mathcal{E}(G;k)
simply means the set of abelian
G
-extensions over
k
.
Let us give the notation of counting number fields.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
S
be a countable set with a counting function
C:S\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that for each
X>0
the set
\{a\in S\mid C(a)<X\}
is finite.
Define

N_{C}(S,X):=\#\{a\in S\mid C(a)<X\}.

For now, we have seen that the (absolute) discriminant could work as a counting function.
However, in some cases, ordering fields by discriminant will contradict what is predicted by the heuristics.
See Cohen and Martinet
[
3
]
, Bartel and Lenstra
[
1
]
for example.
See also Wood
[
19
]
for some discussions on different orderings from a field-counting point of view.
The choice of the counting function may affect the result of field-counting in a nontrivial way.
But we are not going to discuss it in detail here.
For abelian fields, we simply use the product of ramified primes as our counting function.
For more general cases, see Definition
4.3
.

When
p\mid\lvert G\rvert
, the work of the author
[
17
]
shows the following result for abelian fields.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group with a subgroup
H
.
Define
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(G)
.
For each
K\in\mathcal{E}
, define
C:=\sqrt{(d_{K})}
where
\sqrt{}
means the radical, i.e., the product of ramified primes.
Let
q
be a prime number such that
p\mid\lvert G/H\rvert
, and
\Omega:=\{g\in G\mid r_{g}\equiv 0\bmod{p}\}
, where
r_{g}
is the order of
g
.
We have that

\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{H})\leq r)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{H}),C_{p})\rvert)=+\infty,

where the notation
\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{H})
means the relative class group.

And we can see that the zero probability of
\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{H})\leq r
and the infinite
C_{p}
-moment are qualitatively different from what is predicted from the Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet Heuristics for the case when
p\nmid\lvert G\rvert
,
which generalizes the quadratic case.
On the other hand, for non-Galois fields, the method of the author
[
17
]
could not be applied directly.
Let us use an example to explain this.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
L/K
be an extension of the number fields.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}
, define

e(\mathfrak{p},L/K):=\gcd(e(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}},

where
\mathfrak{P}
runs over primes of
L
above
\mathfrak{p}
.

The result of Roquette and Zassenhaus
[
15
]
says the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
15
, Theorem 1]

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
p
be a finite rational prime.
We have

\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\geq\#\{q\in\mathcal{P}\mid e(q,K/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 0\bmod{p}\}-2(n-1).

This is the algebraic preliminary for results like Theorem
1.5
.
Also, this shows the connection between specific ramified primes and the
q
-rank of class groups.
However, let
G
be the image of
A_{4}
in
S_{6}
, and consider the set of fields
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(G)
.
It means that
L\in\mathcal{E}
if and only if
[L:\mathbb{Q}]=6
and the Galois closure
\hat{L}
is an
A_{4}
-field (or just sextic
A_{4}
-field for short).
One can check the ramification table of all tamely ramified primes in
L/\mathbb{Q}
and obtain the following result:

Proposition 1.8
.

For each sextic
A_{4}
-field
L
, we have

\#\{p\nmid 6:e(p,L/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 0\bmod{2}\}=0.

If we apply Theorem
1.7
to a sextic
A_{4}
-field
L
, then it always says that

\operatorname{rk}_{2}\operatorname{Cl}_{L}\geq-10,

which is trivial.
On the other hand, the structure of
G
(or
A_{4}
) tells us that it is likely that there are some nontrivial subgroups of
\operatorname{Cl}_{L}[2^{\infty}]
(the Sylow
2
-subgroup) associated to the ramified primes.
So we need some generalization of Theorem
1.7
in the sense that the base field is not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let us present it here as follows.

Theorem 1.9
.

Fix a rational prime
p
.
Let
L/K
be a finite extension of number fields with Galois closure
N/K
.
Denote
[N:K]
by
n
.
We have

\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}_{L}\geq\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\mid e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)\equiv 0\bmod{p}\}-n^{2}.

Clearly if the base field
K=\mathbb{Q}
, then we go back to the case of Theorem
1.7
with a coarser lower bound on the
p
-rank in general, thus it is indeed a version of generalization.
See Section
3
for more details.
As for the distribution of class groups, we have the following.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
K
be a fixed number field.
For each finite Galois extension
L/K
, define
C(L/K):=\mathfrak{N}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{d}_{L/K}})
,
where
\mathfrak{d}
is the relative discriminant,
\sqrt{}
is the radical, and
\mathfrak{N}
is the absolute norm.
Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and set
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(G,K)
.
For each
p\mid\lvert G\rvert
, and for each non-negative integer
r
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r)=0.

In addition, we have

\mathbb{E}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(L/K),C_{p})\rvert)=+\infty.

See Section
6
for the proof.
For our “prototype”, the sextic
A_{4}
-fields, we have the following.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of sextic
A_{4}
-fields, and
d
the absolute discriminant.
For each non-negative integer
r
, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{2}\operatorname{Cl}_{L}\leq r)=0.

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}_{d,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{L},C_{2})\rvert)=+\infty.

Similarly, for cubic fields with
S_{3}
-closure, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of non-Galois cubic fields.
For each
K_{3}\in\mathcal{E}
, let
K_{6}
be its Galois closure with a unique quadratic subfield
K_{2}
.
Fix an integer
n\geq 2
.
Define the counting function
C
by

C(K_{3}):=d_{K_{3}}d_{K_{2}}^{n},

where
d
is the absolute discriminant.
For each non-negative integer
r
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{3}\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{3}}\leq r)=0.

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{3}},C_{3})\rvert)=+\infty.

Note that for absolute discriminant, we have

d_{K_{6}}=d_{K_{2}}d_{K_{3}}^{2}=d_{K_{2}}^{3}\operatorname{Nm}_{K_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{K_{6}/K_{2}}),

where
\mathfrak{d}
means the relative discriminant.
So the counting function
C
, compared to the discriminant, assigns different “weights” to
\mathfrak{d}_{K_{6}/K_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{K_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}=d_{K_{2}}
,
and we could call such counting functions “generalized discriminant”.
See Section
7
for more discussions.

1.1.
Outline

In Section
3
, we first show some nontrivial algebraic structure of the relative class group, including the proof of Theorem
1.9
.
This is also the algebraic preliminary to prove Theorem
1.10
and
1.11
.
In Section
4
and
5
, we prepare some technical notations and tools for the proof of the distribution of class groups.
They could be used to translate the arithmetic properties of the extensions of number fields into the information of a generating series together with its analytic properties.
Section
4
focuses on defining local specifications and counting functions in an appropriate context,
and Section
5
focuses on studying the analytic properties of certain series.
Then, we prove statistical results for abelian extensions, resp. for some special non-abelian fields, in Section
6
, resp. in Section
7
.
The key idea of the proof is the following.
We first construct a suitable generating series to estimate the result of counting fields with a special specification ordered by the counting function.
Then study its analytic properties, especially its analytic continuation and the pole behaviour.
By Tauberian Theorem
[
12
, Appendix II Theorem I]
, we obtain the asymptotic behaviour.
Finally, algebraic results like Theorem
1.9
together with the asymptotic behaviour of the field-counting will imply the desired “zero-probability” and “infinite moment” as stated in Theorem
1.10
,
1.11
, and
1.12
.

2.
Basic notations

In this section we introduce some of the notations that will be used in the paper.
We use some standard notations coming from analytic number theory.
For example, write a complex number as
s=\sigma+it
.
Denote the Euler’s phi function by
\phi(n)
.
Let
\omega(n)
count the number of distinct prime divisors of
n
and so on.

We also follow the notations of inequalities with unspecified constants from Iwaniec and Kowalski
[
11
, Introduction, p.7]
.
Let us just write down the ones that are important for us.
Let
X
be some space (usually some region of
\mathbb{C}
in our paper), and let
f,g
be two complex functions defined on
X
.
Then
f(x)\ll g(x)
for
x\in X
means that
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq Cg(x)
for some constant
C\geq 0
.
Any value of
C
for which this holds is called an implied constant.
We use
f(x)\asymp g(x)
for
x\in X
if
f(x)\ll g(x)
and
g(x)\ll f(x)
both hold with possibly different implied constants.
We say that
f=o(g)
as
x\to x_{0}
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists some (unspecified) neighbourhood
U_{\epsilon}
of
x_{0}
such that
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq\epsilon g(x)
for
x\in U_{\epsilon}
.
Finally,
f\sim g
as
x\to x_{0}
if we can write
f=g+o(g)
.

3.
Estimate of class group

In this section we give an estimate of class group following the idea of Roquette and Zassenhaus
[
15
]
.
The most important goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.9
stated in Section
1
.
Here we generalize it a little, so that it could also give an estimate for the higher torsion part of the class group.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{K}
means the set of finite primes of
K
, and recall the notation
e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)
from Definition
1.6
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix a number field
K
, and let
L/K
be a finite extension with its Galois closure
N/K
.
There exists some constant
c
, depending on
[N:\mathbb{Q}]
, such that for each rational prime
p
, and for each non-negative integer
l
, we have

\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}_{L}\geq\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\mid e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{l+1}}\}-c.

One can take
c=[N:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}
.

For our convenience, let us give an equivalent definition of
p
-rank for a finite abelian group, so that it also works for finitely generated abelian groups.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a finite abelian group, then

\operatorname{rk}_{p}A=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A/pA.

Proof.

Recall that for a finite abelian group
A
, its
p
-rank
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A
is defined as the largest number
r
such that there exists some injective homomorphism
C_{p}^{r}\to A
.

Since the
p
-part
A_{(p)}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\otimes A
of
A
is a direct summand, we see that if
C_{p}^{l}\to A
is any group homomorphism, then its image is included in
A_{(p)}
.
Moreover it is clear that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A/pA=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A_{(p)}/pA_{(p)}.

So we may assume without loss of generality that
A
is a finite abelian
p
-group.
According to the structure theorem of finite abelian group, we see that there exists some non-negative integers
l
and
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\cdots\leq n_{l}
such that

A\cong\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{i}}\mathbb{Z}.

Let
x_{i}
be a generator of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\}
gives a basis of
A/pA
.
In other words,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A/pA=l.

And the set
\{p^{n_{i}-1}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
generates an elementary
p
-group in
A
that is isomorphic to
C_{p}^{l}
.
This shows that

\operatorname{rk}_{p}A\geq l.

Conversely, let
F\cong\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^{l}
be the free
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-module with the set of generators
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{l}\}
.
There is a surjective map
f:F\to A
induced by
e_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
.
If
B\cong C_{p}^{l^{\prime}}
is any elementary
p
-group included in
A
, then
f^{-1}(B)
is a submodule of
F
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
is a principal ideal domain, we know that
f^{-1}(B)
is also free and its rank as a free module (or the size of a set of basis) is at most
l
.
Therefore
B
is generated by at most
l
elements, i.e.,
l^{\prime}\leq l
.
This shows that
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A\leq l
.
And we are done.
∎

From now on, given any abelian group
A
, we define

\operatorname{rk}_{p}A=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A/pA.

The most important property of this definition is the following.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
,
B
, and
C
be abelian groups.

(1)

If
A\subseteq B
then

\operatorname{rk}_{p}A\leq\operatorname{rk}_{p}B.

(2)

If
B\to C
is a surjective homomorphism, then

\operatorname{rk}_{p}B\geq\operatorname{rk}_{p}C.

(3)

Given a short exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0,

we have that

\operatorname{rk}_{p}B\leq\operatorname{rk}_{p}A+\operatorname{rk}_{p}C.

The inequalities hold in the following sense.
Take (3) for example.
If
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A
and
\operatorname{rk}_{p}C
are both finite, then
\operatorname{rk}_{p}B
must also be finite and less than
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A+\operatorname{rk}_{p}C
.
Else if
\operatorname{rk}_{p}B
is infinite, then the rank
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A
or
\operatorname{rk}_{p}C
must also be infinite.

Proof.

(1):
For an abelian group
A
, we have

A=A_{t}\oplus A_{f},

where
A_{t}
is the torsion part and
A_{f}
is the torsion-free part.
Clearly if
A\subseteq B
, then
A_{t}\subseteq B_{t}
and
A_{f}\subseteq B_{f}
.
For the torsion-free part, this reduces to the property that
\mathbb{Z}
is a principal ideal domain and the number of generators of
A_{f}
cannot exceed the one of
B_{f}
.
For the torsion part, we can use the equivalent definition that
\operatorname{rk}_{p}A_{t}
is the largest number
r
such that there exists an injective homomorphism
C_{p}^{r}\to A_{t}
.
Composing with
A_{t}\to B_{t}
, we obtain an injective homomorphism
C_{p}^{r}\to B_{t}
, which implies that
\operatorname{rk}_{p}B_{t}\geq r
.
And we are done.

(2) and (3):
Assume that we have a short exact sequence

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0.

Tensoring with
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain the following exact sequence

A/pA\to B/pB\to C/pC\to 0.

Now that the abelian groups in the sequence are also
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces, we see that the map
B/pB\to C/pC
splits, and

B/pB=C/pC\oplus\operatorname{im}(A/pA).

As vector spaces, we clearly have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}B/pB\geq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}C/pC\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{im}(A/pA)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A/pA.

And the result just follows.
∎

Let us make some notations.
Fix a number field
K
.
Denote its group of fractional ideals by
I_{K}
and its group of principal ideals by
P_{K}
.
Let
N/K
be a finite Galois extension with Galois group
G=G(N/K)
.
For any subextension
L/K
of
N/K
, we can embed
I_{L}
into
I_{N}
via
L\to N
.
The subgroup
I_{L}
of
I_{N}
in general is not a
G
-submodule of
I_{N}
.
Still we can consider the subgroup of
I_{L}
fixed by
G
, i.e., define

I_{L}^{G}:=I_{L}\cap I_{N}^{G}=\{I\in I_{L}\mid g\cdot I=I\text{ for all }g\in G\}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{L}^{G}:=P_{L}\cap I_{N}^{G},

Let us call it
G
-
invariant part
of
I_{L}
, resp.
P_{L}
.
Note that when
L=N
, or more generally if
L/K
is Galois, then
I_{L}
, resp.
P_{L}
, is a
G
-module, and the so-called
G
-invariant part coincides with the usual notion defined by the Galois action.
Since we have a chain of extensions, let us denote the primes of
K
by
v
, the primes of
L
by
w
, and the primes of
N
by
x
.
For each
v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}
, and for each
w\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
such that
w\mid v
, define

n_{w/v}:=\frac{e(w/v)}{e(v,L/K)}.

Then define

(3.1)

\mathfrak{a}_{v}:=\prod_{w\mid v}w^{n_{w/v}}\in I_{L}.

Recall from Definition
1.6
that
e(v,L/K)\mid e(w/v)
for all
w\mid v
.
In other words,
\mathfrak{a}_{v}\in I_{L}
is the integral ideal such that
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{e(v,L/K)}=v
in
I_{L}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The group
I_{L}^{G}
is a free abelian group generated by

\{\mathfrak{a}_{v}\mid v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\}.

Proof.

This is a generalization of Roquette and Zassenhaus
[
15
, (6)]
to the case when the base field is changed from
\mathbb{Q}
to
K
.
First of all, for any (finite) prime
v
of
K
, the ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{v}
(see (
3.1
)) viewed as an element of
I_{N}
could be decomposed by prime ideals of
N
as follows:

\mathfrak{a}_{v}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{P}_{L}\\
w\mid v\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\\
x\mid w\end{subarray}}x^{e(x/w)n_{w/v}}.

Note that
N/K
is Galois.
For each
x\mid w
, we have

e(x/w)e(w/v)=e(x/v)=e(v,N/K).

Therefore
e(x/w)n_{w/v}=e(x/v)e(v,L/K)^{-1}
, and we have

\mathfrak{a}_{v}=\prod_{x\mid v}x^{e(v,N/K)e(v,L/K)^{-1}}.

It is fixed by the action of
G
, for all the exponents are the same.
This shows that
\mathfrak{a}_{v}\in I_{L}^{G}
for each
v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}
.

Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{v}\mid v
.
If
v\neq v^{\prime}
, then clearly
\mathfrak{a}_{v}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{v^{\prime}}
are coprime, hence linearly independent in
I_{L}
.
This shows that
\{\mathfrak{a}_{v}\}_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}
is a set of linearly independent ideals in
I_{L}
.

Finally, if
\mathfrak{b}\in I_{L}^{G}
, and
\mathfrak{b}=w_{1}^{l_{1}}\dots w_{n}^{l_{n}}
is the decomposition of
\mathfrak{b}
in
L
, then all the conjugates of
w_{1}
must show up in the decomposition of
\mathfrak{b}
.
So we can assume without loss of generality that
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
all lie above a fixed prime
v
of
K
.
Again, let us decompose
\mathfrak{b}
in
N
:

\mathfrak{b}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{x\mid w_{i}}x^{e(x/w_{i})l_{i}}.

Since all the primes
x
of
N
above
v
are conjugate to each other, we see that if
x_{1}\mid w_{i}
and
x_{2}\mid w_{j}
, then

\displaystyle e(x_{1}/w_{i})l_{i}=

\displaystyle e(x_{2}/w_{j})l_{j}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{e(v,N/K)}{e(w_{i}/v)}l_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{e(v,N/K)}{e(w_{j}/v)}l_{j}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow l_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{e(w_{i}/v)}{e(w_{j}/v)}l_{j}

Since
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
are integers, we see that for each
1\leq j\leq n
, we have that

n_{w_{j}/v}=\frac{e(w_{j}/v)}{\gcd(e(w_{1}/v),\dots,e(w_{n}/v))}\mid l_{j}.

And this implies that
\mathfrak{a}_{v}\mid\mathfrak{b}
, i.e.,
\{\mathfrak{a}_{v}\}_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}
generates
I_{L}^{G}
.
∎

An immediate corollary is the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

The quotient group

is a finite abelian group, and we have

I_{L}^{G}/I_{K}\cong\prod_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}\mathbb{Z}/e(v,L/K)\mathbb{Z}

where
v
runs over all finite primes of
K
.

Proof.

This is a generalization of Roquette and Zassenhaus
[
15
, (8)]
to the case when the base field is a number field
K
instead of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)}=v
.
So
I_{K}
is a free abelian subgroup generated by
\{\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)}\}_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}
, and

I_{L}^{G}/I_{K}=\langle\mathfrak{a}_{v}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{v}^{e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)}=1\rangle\cong\prod_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{K}}\mathbb{Z}/e(\mathfrak{p},L/K)\mathbb{Z}.

∎

Definition 3.6

Let

Proposition 3.7

Let
L/K
be a subextension of
N/K

\operatorname{D}_{L}
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}_{L}
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{Cl}_{N}^{G}/\operatorname{D}_{N}

Proof.

For
L

The group
P_{K}
stays inside

as a subgroup, and let us give the quotient subgroup

an estimate by the following two results.

Proposition 3.8
.

Fix a rational prime

We have

P_{N}^{G}/P_{K}\cong H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*}),

where

is the ring of integers of

Let us consider the following short exact sequence

x\mapsto(x)
for all

.
Taking the

-invariant part, we have the following long exact sequence:

Note that by additive Hilbert’s 90, we have
N^{G}=K
, as additive modules.
But the actions of
G
on

and

are the same.
In other words, as
G
-sets,
N^{*}
is a
G
-subset of

.
So, we know that

.

Claim:
H^{0}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})=\mathscr{O}_{K}^{*}

It is clear that
\mathscr{O}_{K}^{*}\subseteq(\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})^{G}

On the other hand, if

This shows that

According to Hilbert’s 90, we know that

So
H^{0}(G,P_{N})=P_{N}^{G}\to H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})
is surjective with kernel equal the image of

The map

is induced by

And

So

And we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

The cohomology group
H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})
is a finite abelian group that is annihilated by
\lvert G\rvert
.
Moreover, we have the following estimate

\operatorname{rk}_{p}H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\leq\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Map}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*}),

for each rational prime

.
Here
\operatorname{Map}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})
is the abelian group consisting of morphisms between sets.
In particular,

is a finite abelian group that could be generated by

is the rank of
\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*}

Proof.

According to the interpretation of cohomolology group

\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*}

On the other hand, as a group

.
Therefore we have the following exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\to\operatorname{Map}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})

\displaystyle Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\to H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\to 0.

By Proposition
3.3
, we see that

\operatorname{rk}_{p}H^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\leq\operatorname{rk}_{p}Z^{1}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*})\leq\operatorname{rk}_{p}\operatorname{Map}(G,\mathscr{O}_{N}^{*}).

Note that every object here is a finitely generated abelian group.
Then, let us construct a set that generates

If
\{\alpha_{0}:=\zeta_{N},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r_{N}}\}
is a basis of

Clearly for each

The size of

.

∎

3.2.
Connections with distribution of class groups

We start with a generalization of the hypothesis in the author
[
17
]
.

In this section, let
G
be a transitive permutation group in
S_{n}
.
Fix a number field
K
.
Define
\mathcal{E}
as a subset of
\mathcal{E}(G;K)
.
Let
C
be a counting function on
\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{E}
is ordered by

.

In concrete examples, we may exclude the fields
L
that contain a strictly larger group of roots of unity than
K
.
In other words, a field

is contained in
\mathcal{E}
if and only if

\mu(K)=\mu(L),

where
\mu(K)
means the group of roots of unity in
K
.
In other cases, one may consider the fields that satisfy some local conditions.
For example, define
\mathcal{E}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{E}(C_{2};\mathbb{Q})
such that a quadratic
L
is contained in
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
G_{\infty}(L/\mathbb{Q})=C_{2}
, where
G_{\infty}
means the decomposition group at infinity.
In other words,
\mathcal{E}
is the set of all the imaginary quadratic number fields.

Let
\Omega
be a subset of
G
.
We say that
\Omega
is closed under conjugation if for each

is included in
\Omega
,
i.e.,
x\in\Omega\Rightarrow G\cdot x\subseteq\Omega
.
We say that
\Omega
is closed under invertible powering if for each

such that
\langle x\rangle=\langle y\rangle
we have

if and only if
y\in\Omega
.
Here,
\langle x\rangle
is the subgroup of
G
generated by

.

Definition 3.11
.

Fix a number field
K
with a nonempty set

of prime ideals.
Let
G
be a transitive permutation group,
and
\Omega
a nonempty subset of

For each

is a generator of the inertia subgroup

for some

.
If

For each non-negative integer

,

to be the subset of

by

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}:=\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid\#\mathcal{P}_{\Omega,L}(T)=\gamma\}.

Note that when

So its inertia subgroup (up to conjugate) is cyclic and admits a generator.
Due to our conditions on

or which one of the generators of the inertia subgroup

is picked up to do the computation.
In other words, the notation

.

as

.
In this case, we say that Hypothesis 2 holds for
((\mathcal{E},C),\Omega,T)
.

It is not hard to show that Hypothesis 1 implies 2, and we just leave it to the reader.

Example 3.13
.

Though in this paper, we will study the questions under a suitable set-up, so that we could prove that the hypothesis is true.
But there are “counter-examples”.
Let
G:=S_{3}
.
Then
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(G;\mathbb{Q})
means the non-Galois cubic fields.
Let
\Omega:=\{(123),(132)\}\subseteq G
, and
T
be the set of all rational primes.
The notation
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}
means all non-Galois cubic fields

such that there are exactly

totally ramified primes

.
According to Davenport-Heilbronn

, if we order cubic fields by (absolute) discriminant, denoted by

such that

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{d}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,0}^{T},X)}{N_{d}(\mathcal{E},X)}=c.

In other words, the hypothesis fails if we order the cubic fields by the discriminant.
To explain the above phenomena, note that the discriminant of a cubic field could be written as
df^{2}
where

is the discriminant of the associated quadratic number field, while

means the product of all totally ramified primes.
Therefore, if we order fields by the discriminant, then the properties of quadratic number fields may affect the statistical results in a nontrivial way.
Of course, this is just a philosophical reasoning, not a proof.
See also Bartel and Lenstra
[
1
]
.

Then we show the connection between the estimate of class groups and the distribution of class groups.

Theorem 3.14
.

Fix a rational prime
p
and a non-negative integer
l
.

(1)

Assume
e_{G}\notin\Omega
to be a nonempty subset of
G
closed under invertible powering and conjugation such that there exists some constant
c\geq 0
for each

, we have

\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\geq\#\mathcal{P}_{T}(\Omega,L)-c.

If Hypothesis
3.12
(2) holds for
((\mathcal{E},C),\Omega,T)
, then for each non-negative integer

, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r)=0.

(2)

If there exists a constant
0\leq a<1
such that for each non-negative integer
r
, we have
\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r)\leq a
, then

\mathbb{E}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K),C_{p})\rvert)=+\infty.

Proof.

We first show (1).
By the condition, for each non-negative integer
\gamma
and for each
m>c
if
L\in\mathcal{E}^{T}_{\Omega,\gamma+m}
then

\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)>\gamma.

So by Hypothesis
3.12
(2), for each non-negative integer
r
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r)\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\gamma=0}^{r+c}N_{C}(\mathcal{E}^{T}_{\Omega,\gamma},X)}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma=0}^{r+c}\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N_{C}(\mathcal{E}^{T}_{\Omega,\gamma},X)}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}=0.

Then we show (2).
For each small real number
0<\epsilon<1-a
, and for each real number
M>0
, there exists some integer
r_{M}\geq 1
such that for all
r\geq r_{M}
we have

p^{r}(1-a-\epsilon)>M.

Recall that

\mathbb{P}_{C,\mathcal{E}}(\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid C(L)<X\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r\}}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}.

So for the same
\epsilon
, there exists

such that for all

we have

\frac{\#\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid C(L)<X\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\leq r_{M}\}}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}<a+\epsilon.

Then for each

we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{C(L)<X}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K),C_{p})\rvert}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{p^{r_{M}}\cdot\#\{C(L)<X\mid\operatorname{rk}_{p}p^{l}\operatorname{Cl}(L/K)\geq r_{M}\}}{N_{C}(\mathcal{E},X)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle p^{r_{M}}(1-a-\epsilon)>M.

This shows that the infinite moment holds. And we are done for the whole proof.

Finally we explain the condition in the theorem by the following result.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
G\subseteq S_{n}
be a transitive permutation group with
G_{1}=\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(1)
.
Let
N/K
be a Galois
G
-extension with
L:=N^{G_{1}}
.
Fix a finite prime
v
of
K
, and let
x
be a prime of
N
above
v
with the decomposition group
G_{x}\subseteq G
.

(1)

Define
\mathcal{P}_{v}(L):=\{w\in\mathcal{P}_{L}:w\mid v\}
.
The morphism of sets

G_{1}\backslash G/G_{x}\to\mathcal{P}_{v}(L),\quad G_{1}\sigma G_{x}\mapsto\sigma x|_{L}

is bijective.

(2)

If
w=x|_{L}
, then

[L_{w}:K_{v}]=\#G_{x}\cdot 1\quad\text{and}\quad e(w/v)=\#I_{x}\cdot 1,

where
I_{x}
is the inertia subgroup, and
G_{x}\cdot 1
, resp.
I_{x}\cdot 1
, is the orbit.

(3)

We have

e(v,L/K)=\gcd(\#I_{v}\cdot i)_{i=1}^{n},

where
I_{v}\subseteq G
is the inertia subgroup of
v
defined up to conjugacy.

Proof of the lemma.

The first statement is a standard result from algebraic number theory.
See Neukirch
[
13
, p.54-55]
for an explanation.

Proof of (2)
.
We move on to the second one.
We have the following chain of extensions of local fields:

K_{v}\subseteq L_{w}\subseteq N_{x}.

Since
G_{x}\cong G(N_{x}/K_{v})
, we have that

L_{w}=N_{x}^{G_{x}\cap G_{1}}.

To see why this is true, it is clear that the fixed subgroup
G(N_{x}/L_{w})
is a subgroup of
G_{x}\cap G_{1}
, for
G(N_{x}/L_{w})
must fix
L
.
For the other direction, note that
L_{w}
could be defined by the completion of
L
with the valuation
w
.
So for each
g\in G_{x}\cap G_{1}
, it fixes all the
(L,w)
-Cauchy sequences, hence fixing the field
L_{w}
.
This first implies that

[L_{w}:K_{v}]=[G_{x}:G_{x}\cap G_{1}].

By definition of the inertia subgroup, the short exact sequence

1\to I_{x}\to G_{x}\to G(\kappa_{x}/\kappa_{v})\to 1

induces the short exact sequence

1\to I_{x}\cap G_{1}\to G_{x}\cap G_{1}\to G(\kappa_{x}/\kappa_{w})\to 1.

In other words, we have

e(w/v)=[I_{x}:I_{x}\cap G_{1}].

Then let us translate it into the context of permutation groups.
The right cosets
G_{1}\backslash G
form a right
G
-set, i.e.,
(G_{1}\sigma)\cdot g\mapsto G_{1}(\sigma g)
.
And the
G
-action induces an injective homomorphism
\varphi:G\to S_{n}
which preserves
G_{1}
, i.e.,
\varphi(G_{1})=G_{1}
.
So we see that
G
and
\varphi(G)
are both transitive and their actions on
\{1,\dots,n\}
are determined by
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(1)=G_{1}=\operatorname{Stab}_{\varphi(G)}(1)
,
which implies that
G
and
\varphi(G)
together with their actions on
\{1,\dots,n\}
are permutation isomorphic.

So the group index
[G_{x}:G_{x}\cap G_{1}]
is the same thing as
\#G_{x}\cdot 1
,
i.e., this number is independent of such a map
\varphi:G\to S_{n}
that preserves
G_{1}
.
And similarly we have that
e(w/v)=\#I_{x}\cdot 1
.
This means that the size of the orbit of
1
under the action of
G_{x}
is the degree
[L_{w}:K_{v}]
, and the ramification index is given by the size of the orbit induced by
I_{x}
.

Proof of (3)
.
Finally, let us prove the identity

e(v,L/K)=\gcd(\#I_{v}\cdot i)_{i=1}^{n}.

Note that the double cosets are in one-to-one correspondence with the orbits
\{(G_{1}\sigma)\cdot G_{x}\mid\sigma\in G\}
.
So we also have a bijective map
\psi:\{G_{x}\cdot i\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}\to\mathcal{P}_{v}(L)
.
Note that the number of
different
orbits may be less than
n
in general.
Fix some
1\leq i\leq n
.
Define
w^{\prime}:=\psi(G_{x}\cdot i)
, and let
x^{\prime}
be a prime of
N
that lies above it.
By applying the method in the proof of (2) to
K_{v}\subseteq L_{w^{\prime}}\subseteq N_{x^{\prime}}
, we see that

[L_{w^{\prime}}:K_{v}]=\#G_{x^{\prime}}\cdot 1\quad\text{and}\quad e(w^{\prime}/v)=\#I_{x^{\prime}}\cdot 1.

Clearly
G_{x}
and
G_{x^{\prime}}
are conjugate in
G
.
So there exists some
\sigma\in G
such that
G_{x^{\prime}}=\sigma G_{x}\sigma^{-1}
.
Note that our
G
-action on
G_{1}\backslash G
is on the right.
By identifying the stabilizers
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\}
and the right cosets
G_{1}\backslash G
and
n
objects in general
\{1,\dots,n\}
as
G
-sets, we have

\sigma^{-1}G_{1}\sigma=G_{1}\cdot\sigma=\sigma\cdot 1.

Since
G_{1}\sigma G_{x}\mapsto\sigma x|_{L}=w^{\prime}
, we see that
\sigma^{-1}G_{1}\sigma=G_{i}
, the stabilizer of
i
.
So we have that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sigma G_{x}\sigma^{-1}:\sigma G_{x}\sigma^{-1}\cap G_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[G_{x}:G_{x}\cap G_{i}]=\#G_{x}\cdot i.

Similar method shows that
e(w^{\prime}/v)=\#I_{x}\cdot i
.
The choice of
x\mid v
only affects the labeling.
So we finally reach the identity

e(v,L/K)=\gcd(e(w/v))_{w\mid v}=\gcd(\#I_{v}\cdot i)_{i=1}^{n}.

∎

4.
Local specifications

In this section, we establish the fundamental notions for our proof of the main results.
Fix a number field
K
, i.e., the base field of this section.
For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Define

\displaystyle U_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\{x\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}:\lvert x\rvert_{\mathfrak{p}}=1\}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is finite};\\
&\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is infinite real};\\
&\mathbb{C}^{*}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is infinite complex}.\end{aligned}\right.

In other words,
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the unit group of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Let
J_{K}
be the group of idèles of
K
, and
\operatorname{C}_{K}
the idèles class group.
Let
S
be a finite set of primes containing the ones at infinity.
Define

J_{K}^{S}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}U_{\mathfrak{p}}

be the
S
-idèles.
Define
K^{S}:=J_{K}^{S}\cap K^{*}
to be the
S
-units of
K
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
F
be a fixed field.
An étale
F
-algebra
A
of degree
\lvert G\rvert
is called a
G
-structured

-algebra

if there is an inclusion
\varphi_{A}:G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(A)
such that
G
acts transitively on the idempotents of
A
.
If
A
and
B
are two
G
-structured
F
-algebras, then an isomorphism
f:A\to B
is an isomorphism of
F
-algebras such that the induced map
f_{*}:\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(A)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{F}(B)
restricted to
G
is the identity map, i.e.,
for each
g\in G
, we have

f_{*}(\varphi_{A}(g))=\varphi_{B}(g).

The most important example to keep in mind is the following.
If
M/K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an extension of local fields with an injective map
G(M/K_{\mathfrak{p}})\hookrightarrow G
, then
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}M
is a
G
-structured
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra, where
H
is the image of
G(M/K_{\mathfrak{p}})
in
G
.
Roughly speaking, this is another way to state the local-global principle.

If
F
is a local field, then we define the decomposition group and the inertia subgroup of
A
over
F
(up to conjugate) as follows.
For a fixed centrally primitive idempotent
e
of
A
, the field
eA
corresponds to a field extension
eA/F
with the corresponding extension of valuations
w/v
.
So, if
g\in G
fixes the component
eA
, then it is clearly an element in the decomposition group
G_{w}\subseteq G
.
It also admits the inertia group
I_{w}
which comes from the exact sequence

1\to I_{w}\to G_{w}\to G(\lambda/\kappa)\to 1,

where
\lambda
, resp.
\kappa
, is the residue field of
eA
, resp.
F
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
K
be a number field, and
G
be a finite group.

(1)

Fix a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
.
Define a specification at
\mathfrak{p}
(with respect to
G
) to be a
G
-structured
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
which is considered up to isomorphism, such that there exists a subgroup
H\subseteq G
and an
H
-extension

with
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}F\cong\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We say that a
G
-extension
L/K
satisfies the specification
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
at
\mathfrak{p}
if
L_{\mathfrak{p}}=L\otimes K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(2)

Define a local specification of
K
(with respect to
G
) to be a collection of specifications
\Sigma=(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that for all but finitely many primes the specification
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
is unramified.
We say that a
G
-extension
L/K
satisfies the local specification
\Sigma
if for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
the extension

satisfies the specification
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Remark
.

Note that a
G
-structured algebra
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an étale algebra.
Its ramification is well-defined via its components.
In particular, since it admits a transitive
G
-action, the ramification could be defined using a single component.
For example, if

is one of the component and

is tamely ramified, then every component is tamely ramified over

, and we can talk about the inertia generator (up to conjugate).

When

is abelian, specifications at a prime

are literally

the
G
-structured

-algebras by the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G
is a finite abelian group, then we have the one-to-one correspondence between the following two sets

\{\text{isomorphism classes of }G\text{-structured }F\text{-algebra}\}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(G_{F},G).

Proof.

See Wood
[

for example.
∎

Note that when

and

.
This means that every specification at

corresponds to a continuous map

and vice versa.
Globally we can replace

by the idèles class group

, and the surjective maps

-extensions

.

Let

Define

(1)

(2)

of

.
We can generalize the counting function

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be a finite group.
For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of

, there exists some continuous homomorphism

.

Using the fact that

simply comes from a choice of

.
∎

When

, there exists an algebra

such that the corresponding map

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.

.
Given functions

Proof.

For each

, denoted by
L\sim\Sigma
,
if
L_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in T
.

(3)

Let
\Phi
be a set of specifications at
T
.
We write
L\in\Phi
if there exists some
\Sigma\in\Phi
such that
L\sim\Sigma
.

The definition may not work well for number fields, especially when
T
is infinite.
For example, for each
p\in\mathfrak{P}
, let
\Sigma_{p}
be some unramified local specification, and define
\Sigma:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\Sigma_{p}
.
Clearly when
G
is nontrivial, there is no
G
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
that satisfies
\Sigma
.

Then we generalize it to the “specifications of morphisms”.
Let
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
be a homomorphism.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, the information of
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:U_{\mathfrak{p}}\to G
is not enough to give a fixed
G
-structured
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra.
So, if we want a notation of “specifications for morphisms”, then the idea is to consider only the set of specifications “defined by the inertia generators”.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Fix a number field
K
with a finite set
S
of primes containing the ones at
\lvert G\rvert\infty
.

(1)

For each prime
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\lvert G\rvert\infty
, let
\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
be a set of specifications at
\mathfrak{p}
.
We say that
\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
is defined by inertia generators if for each specifications
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p},1}
and
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p},2}
at
\mathfrak{p}
with the same inertia subgroup we have
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p},1}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
if and only if
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p},2}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
.

(2)

Let
T
be a set of primes of
K
.
For each prime
\mathfrak{p}\in T
, let
\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
be a set of specifications at
\mathfrak{p}
and let

\displaystyle\Phi:=

\displaystyle\{\Sigma=(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}\mid\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{p})\text{ and }\Sigma\text{ is a specification at }T\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}\Phi(\mathfrak{p}),

where
\prod^{\prime}
means the restricted product whose restriction is given by the set of unramified specifications at
\mathfrak{p}
.
We say that
\Phi
is defined by inertia generators if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in T\backslash S
the set
\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
is defined by inertia generators.

(3)

Let
\Phi=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
be the set of specifications at
T
that is defined by inertia generators.
For each map
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
, we say that
\chi
satisfies
\Phi
, denoted by
\chi\in\Phi
, if for each
\mathfrak{p}
and for each algebra
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}
whose local map extends
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have that
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
.

It is clear that if
L/K
is an abelian
G
-extension corresponding to the map
\chi:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to G
, then the induced map
J_{K}^{S}\to G
satisfies
\Phi
if and only if
L\in\Phi
, where
\Phi
is a set of specifications defined by inertia generators.
So, this is indeed a generalization of the specifications for abelian extensions of
K
to the morphisms
J_{K}^{S}\to G
.

We give a description of the “specifications defined globally” showing up in Hypothesis
3.12
in terms of the definitions above.
Let
G
be a transitive permutation group, and
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of
G
that is closed under conjugation and invertible powering.
Fix a number field
K
, define
\mathcal{E}
as a subset of
\mathcal{E}(G;K)
.
Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
K
containing the ones at
\lvert G\rvert\infty
,
and
T\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{K}
be a set of prime ideals.
Recall from Definition
3.11
that for each non-negative integer
\gamma
, we have defined the notation
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an integral ideal of
K
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}
.

(1)

We say that
\mathfrak{a}
is square-free if
\mathfrak{p}^{2}\nmid\mathfrak{a}
for each finite prime
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.,
\mu(\mathfrak{a})\neq 0
where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

(2)

Define
\omega(\mathfrak{a})
to be the number of distinct prime factors of
\mathfrak{a}
.

(3)

For a set
T
of prime ideals, define

I^{+,\mu}_{T}:=\{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}:\mu(\mathfrak{a})\neq 0\text{ and }\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{p}\in T\}.

Using these notations, we can express
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}
in terms of local specifications.

Proposition 4.9
.

Fix a non-negative integer
\gamma
.
For each prime
\mathfrak{p}\in T
, define

\Omega^{+}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mid y_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Omega\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega^{-}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mid y_{\mathfrak{p}}\notin\Omega\},

where
y_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a generator of the inertia subgroup of
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
For each
\mathfrak{a}\in I^{+,\mu}_{T}
, define

\Phi(\mathfrak{a}):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\Omega^{+}_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in T\\
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}\Omega^{-}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

We have

(4.1)

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\in I^{+,\mu}_{T}\\
\omega(\mathfrak{a})=\gamma\end{subarray}}\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid L\in\Phi(\mathfrak{a})\}.

Proof.

By definition, for any
G
-extension
L/K
, the inertia generator of
\mathfrak{p}
is contained in
\Omega
if and only if
L_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Omega^{+}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
This implies that if
L
is contained in one of the sets on the right-hand side of (
4.1
), then it must be contained in
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}
.

By definition, if
L\in\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{T}
, then there are exactly
\gamma
primes
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in T
and that the inertia generator of
L_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}/K_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
(up to conjugate) is contained in
\Omega
.
Define
\mathfrak{a}:=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma}
.
And we see that
\mathfrak{a}\in I^{+,\mu}_{T}
, and
\omega(\mathfrak{a})=\gamma
, and
L\in\Phi(\mathfrak{a})
.

It suffices to show that the union is disjoint.
When
\gamma=0
, we see that the right-hand side becomes a single set.
So we assume without loss of generality that
\gamma>0
and let
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
be two different integrals of
I^{+,\mu}_{T}
with
\omega(\mathfrak{a})=\gamma=\omega(\mathfrak{b})
such that

\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid L\in\Phi(\mathfrak{a})\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid L\in\Phi(\mathfrak{b})\}

are nonempty.
Then first of all, there exists some
\mathfrak{p}\in T
such that
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}
but
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{b}
.
Second, if
L_{1}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{a})
and
L_{2}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{b})
, then
L_{1,\mathfrak{p}}
has inertia generator contained in
\Omega
while the inertia generator of
L_{2,\mathfrak{p}}
is contained in
G\backslash\Omega
.
So
L_{1}\neq L_{2}
belong to different isomorphism classes of field extensions over
K
.
This shows that the union on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) must be disjoint.
∎

Let us conclude this section by some applications of these notations.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
Fix a number field
K
with a finite set
S
of primes containing the ones at
\lvert G\rvert\infty
.
We have that

\operatorname{Hom}(J_{K}^{S},G)\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}\operatorname{Hom}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*},G)+\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\operatorname{Hom}(U_{\mathfrak{p}},G).

Proof.

For topological groups
A
and
B
, the notation
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
always means the group of continuous homomorphisms.
So for any (continuous) homomorphism
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
, we see that the kernel
\ker\chi
must be both open and closed.

Claim: given local continuous homomorphisms
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:U_{\mathfrak{p}}\to G
for all
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
such that
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is trivial for all but finitely many primes, and continuous homomorphisms
\chi:K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to G
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
, the product

\chi:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}

is a morphism of topological groups.
It suffices to show that
\ker\chi
is open.
For a fixed
\mathfrak{p}
, the kernel
\ker\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an open and closed subgroup of
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
or
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
.
And
\ker\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}=U_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}
.
So if
x=(x_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\in J_{K}^{S}
is contained in the kernel of
\chi
, then

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}x_{\mathfrak{p}}\ker\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}

is an open neighbourhood of
x
in
J_{K}^{S}
and is included in
\ker\chi
.
This shows that
\ker\chi
is open.

Conversely, if
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
is continuous, then there exists a finite set of primes
S_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(K)\backslash S
such that

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}W_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{0}}V_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S\cup S_{0}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\ker\chi,

where
W_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an open and closed neighbourhood of
1
in
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
and
V_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an open and closed neighbourhood of
1
in
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
This implies that
\chi
factors through the quotient
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which reduces to the finite product of the morphisms
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over primes in
S\cup S_{0}
.
So
\chi
is actually an element of the restricted product of
\operatorname{Hom}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*},G)
and
\operatorname{Hom}(U_{\mathfrak{p}},G)
with respect to the trivial map, where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over all primes of
K
,
i.e., the direct sum.
And we are done for the proof.
∎

Based on the structure of
\operatorname{Hom}(J_{K}^{S},G)
, we abstractly obtain a generating series as follows.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
C
be a counting function defined as in Definition
4.3
, and
S
a finite set of primes containing the ones at
\lvert G\rvert\infty
.
Define
f
to be a complex-valued function defined on morphisms
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
such that
f(\chi)=1
if
\chi
is trivial and

f(\chi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}f(\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Let
T
be a set of primes.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in T
, let
\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
be a set of local specifications at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Define
\Phi:=\prod^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}\Phi(\mathfrak{p})
as the restricted product with respect to unramified specifications.
If
\Phi
is defined by inertia generators, then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G\\
\chi\in\Phi\end{subarray}}f(\chi)C(\chi)^{-s}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in T}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:U_{\mathfrak{p}}\to G\\
\chi\in\Phi(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}f(\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})(\mathfrak{Np})^{-c(\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})s}\Bigr)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin T}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:W_{\mathfrak{p}}\to G\\
\chi\notin\Phi(\mathfrak{p})\end{subarray}}f(\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})\mathfrak{Np}^{-c(\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})s}\Bigr),

where
W_{\mathfrak{p}}=U_{\mathfrak{p}}
if
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
and
W_{\mathfrak{p}}=K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
otherwise.

Proof.

Since the identity holds in the sense of the Riemann sum, by Lemma
4.10
, this reduces to the fact that
C
and
f
are multiplicative with respect to the group of ideals of
K
.
∎

This statement also explains why we prefer the morphisms
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
.
In summary, we give a definition of local specification in terms of
G
-structured algebra and apply it to the morphisms
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to G
so that we can construct generating series for them.
In the next section, we are going to study the analytic properties of such series.

5.
Analytic continuation for generating series

In this section we present some analytic results related to the generating series used in this paper.
Throughout the section fix a number field
K
.
Let us introduce the notion called ray class group.
See also Neukirch
[
13
, VI.1.7]
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a module (an integral ideal) of
K
.
Define
I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
as the fractional ideals that are relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}
, and
P_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
as the group of all principal ideals
(a)\in P_{K}
such that

a\text{ totally positive}\quad\text{and}\quad a\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{m}}.

The latter condition means that there exist integers
b,c\in\mathscr{O}_{K}^{*}
that are relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}
such that
a=b/c
and
b\equiv c\bmod{\mathfrak{m}}
.
The ray class group mod
\mathfrak{m}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, is the quotient
I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}/P_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Let
h_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}\rvert
be the size of the ray class group.

Using the language of idèles, we can give an equivalent definition for the ray class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
, we define the higher unit groups as follows.
First, set
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(0)}:=U_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see also the beginning of Section
4
).
Then, for each positive integer
n
, define

U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(n)}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+\mathfrak{p}^{n}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is finite};\\
&\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is infinite real};\\
&\mathbb{C}^{*}\quad&\text{if }\mathfrak{p}\text{ is infinite complex}.\end{aligned}\right.

Write
\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{n_{\mathfrak{p}}}
as a replete ideal, where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over all primes of
K
.
In particular,
n_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\mid\infty
.
Define
J_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(n_{\mathfrak{p}})}
.
The subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}:=J_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}K^{*}/K^{*}
of the idèles class group
\operatorname{C}_{K}
is called the
congruence subgroup

\bmod{\mathfrak{m}}
.
The map

J_{K}\to I_{K},\quad\alpha\mapsto(\alpha)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}\mathfrak{p}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})}

induces an isomorphism
\operatorname{C}_{K}/\operatorname{C}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}\to\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
See Neukirch
[
13
, VI.1.9]
for details.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a module of
K
, and
\mathfrak{R}
be a set of ideals representing an element of the ray class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Define

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s):=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}}\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}},

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}
means the primes in the class
\mathfrak{R}
.
The series
l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)
defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
.
It could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
Moreover, there exists a holomorphic function
f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s)
at
s=1
such that

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)=f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s)+\frac{1}{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}\log\frac{1}{s-1}

near
s=1
.
Here
h_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the size of the ray class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}\to S^{1}
be a character.
Define

L(\chi,s):=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I^{\mathfrak{m}}_{K}}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a})}{(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{a})^{s}}

as the corresponding Hecke
L
-series.
When
\chi
has conductor other than
1
, it actually admits a holomorphic continuation to the whole plane.
Moreover, the Hecke
L
-series
L(\chi,s)
admits a zero-free region in a neighbourhood
U
of
\sigma\geq 1
with at most one real and simple zero inside
U
(the so-called exceptional zero).
See Coleman
[
5
]
for example.
On the other hand, Neukirch
[
13
, VII.13.3]
shows that if
\chi
is a nontrivial (irreducible) character of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}=I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}/P_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, then the
L
-series
L(\chi,s)
satisfies

L(\chi,1)\neq 0.

So we could simply say that there exists a neighbourhood
V
of
\sigma\geq 1
(dependent on
K
and
\chi
) such that
L(\chi,s)
is nonzero in
V
.
Since
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
is a finite abelian group, there are only finitely many characters, so we can take such a neighbourhood
V
that works for all
\chi
.
By Definition
1.1
, let
\mathcal{P}_{I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}}
be the set of primes in
I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
For each ideal class
\mathfrak{R}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, denote the set of primes in
\mathfrak{R}
by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}
.
In the open half-plane, we have

\log L(\chi,s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\chi(\mathfrak{p})^{n}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-ns}.

Note that for each
\delta>0
, it is absolutely and uniformly convergent in the half-plane
\sigma>1+\delta
.
So given any class
\mathfrak{R}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi}\bar{\chi}(\mathfrak{R})\log L(\chi,s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\chi}\bar{\chi}(\mathfrak{R})\chi(\mathfrak{p})^{n}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-ns}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\mathfrak{m}}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}}\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{s}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{I_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}}\\
\chi(\mathfrak{p}^{n})=\chi(\mathfrak{R})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{ns}}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle h_{\mathfrak{m}}(l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)+f(s)).

By taking the absolute value, we see that
f(s)
is holomorphic in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.
The identity already gives the holomorphic continuation of
l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)
to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
, because
L(\chi,s)
are all holomorphic functions in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
that could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
And they are nonzero in some neighbourhood
V
of the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
, as mentioned above.
Since
V
and
V\backslash\{s=1\}
could be chosen to be simply connected, we see that the notation
\log L(\chi,s)
also makes sense in the corresponding region.

The behaviour of
l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)
at
s=1
is given by the pole behaviour of
L(\chi,s)
.
To be precise, if
\chi
has conductor other than
1
, then
L(\chi,s)
is holomorphic at
s=1
with
L(\chi,1)\neq 0
.
Else if
\chi_{0}
is the principal character, then
L(\chi_{0},s)
differs
\zeta_{K}(s)
by a finite number of factors (in the sense of Euler form).
So there exists some holomorphic function
g(s)
at
s=1
such that

\log L(\chi_{0},s)=\log\frac{1}{s-1}+g(s).

And this implies the identity near
s=1
:

\displaystyle l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}\log\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{1}{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}g(s)-f(s)+\frac{1}{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}L(\chi,s)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}\log\frac{1}{s-1}+f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s).

∎

Then we generalize the lemma in the following way.
Recall that for each integral ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{l}^{r_{l}}
of
K
, where
r_{i}
is a positive integer for
i=1,\dots,l
, denote the number of distinct prime factors by
\omega(\mathfrak{a}):=l
.
We call
\mathfrak{a}
square-free if
r_{1}=r_{2}=\cdots=r_{l}=1
.
Recall that for a class of ideals
\mathfrak{R}
(e.g., a subset of the ray class group), denote the prime ideals in
\mathfrak{R}
by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
k
be a fixed number field, and
\mathfrak{R}
be a set of ideals.
Write
I^{+,\mu}_{\mathfrak{R}}:=I^{+,\mu}_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}}
(see Definition
4.8
).
If the series

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s):=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s}

defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
, where
\sigma_{0}
is a fixed positive real number,
and it could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
except at
s=\sigma_{0}
with an identity

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)=f(s)+f_{0}(s)\log\frac{1}{s-\sigma_{0}},

near
s=\sigma_{0}
, where
f(s),f_{0}(s)
are holomorphic at
s=\sigma_{0}
with
f_{0}(\sigma_{0})\neq 0
,
then for each non-negative integer
\gamma
, the series

l_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{R},s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\in I^{+,\mu}_{\mathfrak{R}}\\
\omega(\mathfrak{a})=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s}

also defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
that could be extended to the line
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
except at
s=\sigma_{0}
.
And there exists holomorphic functions
g_{0}(s)
,
\dots
,
g_{\gamma}(s)
at
s=\sigma_{0}
such that

l_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{R},s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\gamma}g_{i}(s)(\log\frac{1}{s-\sigma_{0}})^{i}

near
s=\sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
\gamma
.
The statement is true when
\gamma=1
by the condition.
Now assume that it is true for the series
l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s),\dots,l_{\gamma-1}(\mathfrak{R},s)
, where
\gamma\geq 2
.
One can check the following identity:

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)\cdot l_{\gamma-1}(\mathfrak{R},s)=\gamma l_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{R},s)+\sum_{j=2}^{\gamma}(-1)^{j}l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},js)l_{\gamma-j}(\mathfrak{R},s),

where
l_{0}(s):=1
as a notation.
Then the analytic properties of
l_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{R},s)
follows from the induction assumption.
∎

Based on these two lemmas, we can further give a result on the analytic property of Euler product

(5.1)

F(\mathfrak{R},s):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{R}}(1-(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a module of
K
, and
\mathfrak{R}
be a set of ideals representing an ideal class in
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Then the Euler product
F(\mathfrak{R},s)
defined as in (
5.1
) gives a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
that could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
And there exists some holomorphic function
f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s)
at
s=1
such that

F(\mathfrak{R},s)=f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s)(s-1)^{-1/h_{\mathfrak{m}}}

near
s=1
.

Proof.

First of all, by comparing with
\zeta_{K}(s)
, we see that
F(\mathfrak{R},s)
is absolutely and uniformly convergent in
\sigma>1+\delta
for any
\delta>0
.

It is clear that
F(\mathfrak{R},s)
is nonzero in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
.
So let us consider the difference

l_{1}(\mathfrak{R},s)-\log F(\mathfrak{R},s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{R}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{p})^{-ns}.

This defines a holomorphic function in the half-plane
\sigma>1/2
.
So we see that
F(\mathfrak{R},s)
admits a holomorphic continuation to
\sigma\geq 1
except at
s=1
.
In addition, we see that
F(\mathfrak{R},s)^{h_{\mathfrak{m}}}
has a simple pole at
s=1
.
Since
F(\mathfrak{R},s)
is nonzero in a neighbourhood of
s=1
, the existence of
f_{\mathfrak{R}}(s)
is guaranteed.
∎

A corollary is the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a module of
K
, and let
\mathfrak{R}
be a set of ideals representing an ideal class in
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Define

G(\mathfrak{R},s):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{R}}}(1+n\mathfrak{Np}^{-s}),

where
n
is a fixed positive integer.
Then
G(\mathfrak{R},s)
defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
that could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
And there exists a holomorphic function
g(s)
at
s=1
such that

G(\mathfrak{R},s)=g(s)(s-1)^{-n/h_{\mathfrak{m}}}

near
s=1
.

Proof.

By comparing
G(\mathfrak{R},s)
with
F(\mathfrak{R},s)^{n}
, we see that
G(\mathfrak{R},s)
is holomorphic in the open half-plane.
Since
G
is non-zero in this region, we can take the logarithm and the difference

E(s):=\log G(\mathfrak{R},s)-n\log F(\mathfrak{R},s)

is a holomorphic function in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.
Therefore we have the identity

G(\mathfrak{R},s)=\exp(E(s))\cdot F(\mathfrak{R},s)^{n}

which gives the analytic continuation of
G(\mathfrak{R},s)
described in the statement.
∎

7.1.
Algebraic Theory

We need some algebraic preliminaries.
First of all, Class Field Theory tells us the following:
for a fixed number field
K
, there exists a one-to-one correspondence

\{L\text{ is an }H\text{-extension over }K\}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{C}_{K},H).

But we also require that
L
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
.
So we need to study the Galois action in the extensions of idèles.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
A
be a finite group, and
M
be a finite abelian group.
Fix an
A
-field
K
.
Let
L/K
be an
M
-extension with the Artin reciprocity map

\chi:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to M.

The field extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
is also Galois if and only if the kernel
N_{L}:=\ker\chi
is an
A
-submodule of
\operatorname{C}_{K}
.
In addition, if
M
is an
A
-module and
\chi
is an
A
-morphism, then the action of
A
on
M
is the same as the one induced by the short exact sequence

1\to M\to G(L/\mathbb{Q})\to A\to 1,

i.e.,
G(L/\mathbb{Q})
is an element of
H^{2}(A,M)
.

Proof.

If
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, then
N_{L}=\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}\operatorname{C}_{L}
is a
A
-submodule of
\operatorname{C}_{K}
.
This shows that the induced isomorphism
\operatorname{C}_{K}/N_{L}\cong M
is an
A
-morphism, where the action of
A
on
M
is induced from its action on
\operatorname{C}_{K}
.

Conversely, we can assume for contradiction that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is not Galois.
Since
K
is Galois, we see that for any
g
in the absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have that

gK=K\text{ and }gK\subseteq gL.

This implies that if
L^{\prime}
is any conjugate of
L
, then
K\subseteq L\cap L^{\prime}
.
In particular,
N_{L}
and
N_{L^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Nm}_{L^{\prime}/K}\operatorname{C}_{L^{\prime}}
are conjugate with each other in the
A
-module
\operatorname{C}_{K}
.
In other words,
N_{L}
cannot be
A
-invariant.
So the contradiction implies that
L/\mathbb{Q}
must be Galois.

When
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, then clearly we have the short exact sequence

1\to M\to G(L/\mathbb{Q})\to A\to 1.

Since
M
is abelian, we see that
A
acts on
M
by conjugation.
We need to verify that this action coincides with the
A
-module
M
.
By Neukirch
[
13
, IV.5.8]
(functorial behaviour of the reciprocity map), for an automorphism
\sigma
of
L
, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(L/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{C}_{K}/N_{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(L/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{C}_{K}/N_{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}

where
\sigma^{*}
means the group conjugation, and
\sigma
on the right means the action induced by
\sigma
acting on
\operatorname{C}_{K}
.
Note that
G(L/K)\cong\operatorname{C}_{K}/N_{L}
via the Artin reciprocity map
\chi
.
This commutative diagram shows that all the actions are compatible.
And we are done.
∎

Note that there is a similar result for local extensions.
Let us write down the statement.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
A
be a finite group, and
M
be a finite abelian group.
Fix a finite rational prime
p
and an
A
-extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Let
L/K
be a finite abelian extension with the reciprocity map
\chi:K^{*}\to M
.
The extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Galois if and only if
N_{L}:=\ker\chi
is
A
-invariant.
In addition, if
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Galois, then
G(L/\mathbb{Q})
is an element of
H^{2}(A,M)
.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
7.3
and we just leave it to the reader.

Lemma 7.5
.

If
A
is a finite group and
M
a finite
A
-module such that
\gcd(\lvert A\rvert,\lvert M\rvert)=1
, then
M
is cohomologically trivial, that is, for each subgroup
B\subseteq A
and for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

H^{n}(B,M)=0.

Proof.

Since multiplying by
\lvert B\rvert
is an automorphism of
M
by our condition, by Neukirch and Schmidt and Wingberg
[
14
, I.1.6.2]
, we know that
H^{n}(B,M)
is trivial for each positive integer
n
.
∎

Note that this implies that
G
is the only group that fits into the short exact sequence (
7.1
).
To be precise, we have the following.

Proposition 7.6
.

Fix a
\bar{G}
-field
K
.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between the following two sets

\operatorname{Sur}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{C}_{K},H)\leftrightarrow\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid K\subseteq L\}.

Proof.

If
K
is a
\bar{G}
-field and
\chi:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to H
is a surjective
\bar{G}
-morphism, then the class field
L/K
must be Galois by Lemma
7.3
.
In addition, the Galois group
G(L/\mathbb{Q})
is an element of
H^{2}(\bar{G},H)
.
Since the cohomology group is trivial, we see that
G(L/\mathbb{Q})
must be isomorphic to
G
.
In other words, the
\bar{G}
-morphism
\chi
corresponds to a field

.

If
L\in\mathcal{E}
, then
L
is a
G
-field, so

is a
\bar{G}
-field.
This implies that there is an Artin reciprocity map
\chi:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to H
that corresponds to

.
By Lemma
7.3
again, the map
\chi
must be a
\bar{G}
-morphism, and we are done.
∎

For a
\bar{G}
-field
K
, we know that
G
-fields above

are given by surjective
\bar{G}
-morphisms
\chi:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to H
.
And of course, if

must be a
\bar{G}
-field.
Now we want to show how to compute the value

(see Definition

in theory.
After all, the map

is defined for specifications at
p
.
So we need to recover the information of specifications at
p
from this chain and the Artin reciprocity map.
We give the statements in steps.

Lemma 7.7
.

If we choose a splitting map
s:\bar{G}\to G
and treat
\bar{G}
also as a subgroup of
G
,
then for each subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G
, there exists
g\in G
, and
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
and
\bar{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\bar{G}
such that
\bar{G}^{\prime}
acts on
H^{\prime}
via the action of
\bar{G}
on
H
, and

gG^{\prime}g^{-1}=H^{\prime}\bar{G}^{\prime}.

Proof.

Let
\pi:G\to\bar{G}
be the projection map with the section
s:\bar{G}\to G
.
Since
G^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
G
, there is a short exact sequence induced by
\pi
, i.e.,

1\to H^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}\to\bar{G}^{\prime}\to 1,

where
\bar{G}^{\prime}
is the image of
G^{\prime}
under the map
\pi
, and
H^{\prime}=G^{\prime}\cap H
is the kernel of this map.
In other words
H^{\prime}
is a normal subgroup of
G^{\prime}
such that
H^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
have coprime orders.
By Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem, we see that
H^{\prime}
admits a complement in
G^{\prime}
, i.e., there exists some subgroup
\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}
such that
H^{\prime}\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}=G^{\prime}
.
In particular, the restriction
\pi:\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}\to\bar{G}^{\prime}
induces a group isomorphism.
This implies that
\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\bar{G}^{\prime})=H\bar{G}^{\prime}
and it is one of the complements of
H
in
H\bar{G}^{\prime}
, for

H\cap\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}=\{e_{G}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{-1}(\bar{G}^{\prime})=HG^{\prime}=HH^{\prime}\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}=H\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}.

By Schur-Zassenhaus theorem again, we see that the complements of
H
in
H\bar{G}^{\prime}
are all conjugate to each other.
So if
g\in H\bar{G}^{\prime}
is some element such that
g\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}g^{-1}=\bar{G}^{\prime}
, then we have that

gG^{\prime}g^{-1}=gH^{\prime}g^{-1}g\bar{G}^{\prime\prime}g^{-1}=H^{\prime}\bar{G}^{\prime}.

And we are done.
∎

Recall that a specification

Lemma 7.8

7.2.
Estimate of field-counting

In this subsection, if we obtain the estimate

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0},X)\ll X^{1/(m+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)\asymp\frac{X^{1/m}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1},

where
\gamma
is a positive integer,
then Lemma
7.1
is proved immediately.
For this purpose (estimate of field-counting), let us prove the following results one-by-one.

Lemma 7.9
.

(1)

Fix an abelian
\bar{G}
-field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and define
\mathcal{E}_{K}:=\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid K\subseteq L\}
.
We have

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K},X)\leq\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert\cdot\#\{\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}/K^{S_{\infty}},H)\mid C(\chi)<X\},

where
S_{\infty}
is the set of all infinite primes of
K
.

(2)

Let
A
and
B
be two finite abelian
p
-groups.
We have

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\rvert\leq\lvert A\rvert^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}B}.

(3)

Fix a finite discrete
\bar{G}
-module
A
.
There exist positive constant
c
and
N
depending only on
\bar{G}
and
A
such that for each abelian
\bar{G}
-field
K
, we have

(7.3)

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert\leq c\sqrt{d_{K}}^{N},

where
\sqrt{d_{K}}
is the radical of the absolute discriminant (product of ramified primes).

Proof.

(1):
Recall that
S_{\infty}
is the set of infinite primes of
K
.
For a finite set
S
of primes including the ones at infinity, we have the notions of
S
-idèles
J_{K}^{S}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
, and
S
-units
K^{S}=K^{*}\cap J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}
(inside
J_{K}
).
In addition, there is a short exact sequence

1\to J_{K}^{S}/K^{S}\to\operatorname{C}_{K}\to\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{S}\to 1.

When
S=S_{\infty}
, we have
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{S_{\infty}}=\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
.
By taking the homomorphisms that are
\bar{G}
-equivariant to
H
, we have an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{C}_{K},H)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}/K^{S_{\infty}},H).

This implies first that if
\chi:J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}/K^{S_{\infty}}\to H
admits a preimage, then the number of
\tilde{\chi}:\operatorname{C}_{K}\to H
such that its restriction is exactly
\chi
is given by the size of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)
.
In particular, this shows that the original proof of Lemma 7.10 is flawed.
That is, when
\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)
is non-trivial, there must be multiple
G
-fields
L/\mathbb{Q}
including
K
as a subfield with exactly the same local specifications.

On the other hand, we have shown that
\mathcal{E}_{K}
and
\operatorname{Sur}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{C}_{K},H)
corresponds to each other bijectively (see Proposition 7.6).
And we see in Section 7.1 that the counting function
C
could be generalized to
\chi:J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}\to H
(that factors through
K^{S_{\infty}})
.
Putting everything together, we have

\displaystyle N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K},X)=

\displaystyle\#\{\tilde{\chi}\in\operatorname{Sur}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{C}_{K},H)\mid C(\tilde{\chi})<X\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert\cdot\#\{\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}/K^{S_{\infty}},H)\mid C(\chi)<X\}.

And we are done for (1).

(2):
For finite cyclic
p
-groups, we see that

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{a}\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/p^{b}\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{\min\{a,b\}}\mathbb{Z}.

In general, write
A\cong\prod_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}A}\mathbb{Z}/p^{a_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
and
B\cong\prod_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}B}\mathbb{Z}/p^{b_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}A}\prod_{j=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}B}\mathbb{Z}/p^{\min\{a_{i},b_{j}\}}\mathbb{Z}.

This implies that

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\rvert\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}B}\prod_{i=1}^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}A}p^{a_{i}}=\lvert A\rvert^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}B}.

And we are done for (2).

(3):
By (2), we have

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K}[p^{\infty}],A[p^{\infty}])\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{K}[p^{\infty}]\rvert^{\operatorname{rk}_{p}A}\leq\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rvert^{r_{A}},

where
r_{A}=\sup_{p}\operatorname{rk}_{p}A
.
By Minkowski bound of the size of the class group, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c=c(\lvert\bar{G}\rvert,\varepsilon)
such that

\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rvert^{r_{A}}\leq cd_{K}^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}r_{A}},

where
d_{K}
is the absolute discriminant of
K/\mathbb{Q}
.
Since the Galois group
\bar{G}
is fixed, we see that there exists
N=N(\bar{G},\varepsilon,A)
such that
(\sqrt{d_{K}})^{N}>d_{K}^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}r_{A}}
, where
\sqrt{d_{K}}
means the radical of the absolute discriminant (or just the product of ramified primes), that is,

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},A)\rvert\leq c\sqrt{d_{K}}^{N},

and we are done for the proof.
∎

Let us first give an upper bound.

Lemma 7.10

(Correction of the original Lemma 7.10)

Clearly

\bar{G}
-morphism

\chi\in\Omega_{p}^{\pm}

And we can define

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}:=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\{\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}},H)\mid\chi\in\Phi(d)\}.

The relation between
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}

\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma},X)\leq\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert N_{C}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma},X).

Let us define a generating series for
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}

\displaystyle F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}}(s):=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*},H)}1\Bigr)\prod_{p\mid\lvert G\rvert}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*},H)}p^{-c(\chi)s}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid d}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}U_{\mathfrak{p}},H)\\
\chi(y_{p})\in\Omega\end{subarray}}p^{-c(\chi)s}\Bigr),

where

.
Note that the map
c_{G}:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

.
Then we obtain a counting function

\bar{G}

Note that for each prime

\bar{G}

\chi

In particular, it takes into account the ramification of

.
So, for each
\bar{G}

\chi:J_{K}^{S_{\infty}}\to H

\bar{G}
-morphism

We get a generating series as follows:

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}(s):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{E}(\bar{G})}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert C^{\prime}(K)^{-s}F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}}(s).

We want to reduce it to a single Euler product so that we could deduce the analytic properties of the series.
Let us first apply Lemma
7.9
here.
By (
7.3
), for each abelian
\bar{G}
-field extension

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert\leq d\sqrt{d_{K}}^{N}.

We may assume without loss of generality that

is an integer, and obtain the following series

E_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}(s)=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{E}(\bar{G})}d\sqrt{d_{K}}^{N}C^{\prime}(K)^{-s}F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}}(s).

Apply Proposition 7.7 here, and we obtain a positive integer
a
for each
\bar{G}
-field
K
and for each
p\in\mathcal{P}
as an upper bound for
\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}K^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}},H)\rvert
,
and
\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}U_{\mathfrak{p}},H)\rvert
.
Because

\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}U_{\mathfrak{p}},H)\rvert\leq\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*},H)\rvert\leq\#\{\text{Specifications at }p\text{ w.r.t }G\}.

Or equivalently one can see the existence of such a number
a
by the structure of

as a topological group.
In particular, the number
a
could be chosen so that it is uniform for all
\bar{G}
-fields
K
.
Let
M:=\max_{g\in G}c_{G}(g)
.
For each non-negative integer
\gamma
, define

D_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}(s):=d\prod_{p\mid\lvert G\rvert\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\to\bar{G}}1+\sum_{\Sigma_{p}}p^{-c(\Sigma_{p})s}\Bigr)\prod_{p\nmid\lvert G\rvert\infty}(1+ap^{N}\sum_{n=m^{\prime}}^{M}p^{-ns})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid d}ap^{-ms},

where
\Sigma_{p}

p
with respect to
G
.

\sum_{n}b_{n}n^{-s}:=d\prod_{p\mid\lvert G\rvert\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\to\bar{G}}1\Bigr)\prod_{p\nmid\lvert G\rvert\infty}(1+ap^{N}\sum_{n=m^{\prime}}^{M}p^{-ns}).

a\sum_{n=m^{\prime}}^{M}p^{-ns}
in the local factor over counts the number of nontrivial elements in

when

, we have

\displaystyle b_{n}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{C^{\prime}(K)=n}d\sqrt{d_{K}}^{N}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{C^{\prime}(K)=n}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{G}}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},H)\rvert.

Similarly if we write

\sum_{n}c_{n}n^{-s}:=\prod_{p\mid\lvert G\rvert\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\Sigma_{p}}p^{-c(\Sigma_{p})s}\Bigr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid d}ap^{-ms}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n}c_{K,n}n^{-s}:=F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}}(s),

So, we have obtained a uniform Euler product to describe an upper bound estimate for field-counting as promised.
Therefore, it suffices to consider the analytic properties of
\sum_{n}a_{n}n^{-s}:=D_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}(s)
.

First consider the case when
m^{\prime}-N>m
and
\gamma>0
.
The series

defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane

By Lemma 5.2 and Proposition 5.3, the function

, and there exist holomorphic functions

The Tauberian Theorem
[

\sum_{n<X}a_{n}\sim A\frac{X^{1/m}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1},

If
\gamma=0
, or

.
Let

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)=N_{C^{l}}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X^{l}).

The method above could be applied to the case when the counting function

If

If

D_{C^{l},\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma}}(s)=d\prod_{p\mid\lvert G\rvert\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\to\bar{G}}1+\sum_{\Sigma_{p}}p^{-lc(\Sigma_{p})s}\Bigr)\prod_{p\nmid\lvert G\rvert\infty}(1+ap^{N}\sum_{n=lm^{\prime}}^{lM}p^{-ns}).

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0},X)=N_{C^{l}}(\mathcal{E}_{0},X^{l})\ll X^{1/(m^{\prime}-N/l)}(\log X)^{a-1}\ll X^{1/(m^{\prime}-\varepsilon)}.

And we are done for the proof.
∎

Then we prove a lower bound for
N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)
.

Lemma 7.11
.

Fix a
\bar{G}
-field
K
, and define

\mathcal{E}_{K}:=\{L\in\mathcal{E}\mid K\subseteq L\}.

For each positive integer
\gamma
, let
\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}:=\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
.
Then we have

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma},X)\gg\frac{X^{1/m}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1},

as
X\to\infty
.

Proof.

Step 1:

Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
K
such that

(1)

S
is a
\bar{G}
-set, and there exists a surjective
\bar{G}
-morphism
\varphi:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to H
;

(2)

J_{K}^{S}/K^{S}\cong\operatorname{C}_{K}
, i.e.,
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}^{S}
is trivial;

(3)

for each finite prime
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
,
\mathfrak{p}\nmid d_{K}\lvert H\rvert\infty
, where
d_{K}
means the (absolute) discriminant of
K
.

Such a set
S
exists.
Because we can first take a set
S^{\prime}
such that
J_{K}^{S^{\prime}}/K^{S^{\prime}}\cong\operatorname{C}_{K}
(see also Neukirch
[
13
, VI.1.4]
).
Then replace
S^{\prime}
by a larger set of primes if necessary, there must be a surjective
G
-morphism
\varphi:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S^{\prime}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to H
.
So take
S
to be a
\bar{G}
-set that includes
S^{\prime}
and
\{\mathfrak{p}:\mathfrak{p}\mid d_{K}\lvert H\rvert\infty\}
, then
S
will satisfy these three conditions simultaneously.

Since
S
is a
\bar{G}
-set, we say that a rational prime
p\in S
if for some
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
we have
\mathfrak{p}\in S
.
Clearly this definition does not depend on the choice of
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.,
p\in S
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\in S
for each
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
.

Recall that for each
\bar{G}
-morphism
\chi_{p}:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\to H
, we could extend it to some
\bar{G}
-morphism
\tilde{\chi}_{p}:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to H
(the choice is not unique).
And such a map
\tilde{\chi}_{p}
corresponds bijectively to a specification
\Sigma_{p}
at
p
with respect to
G
.
Define

\Omega_{p}^{+}:=\{\Sigma_{p}\mid y_{p}\in\Omega\}

to be the set of specifications at
p
such that its inertia generator is contained in
\Omega
.
Since the definition of
\Omega_{p}^{+}
only depends on the inertia generators, the notation
\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}
is well-defined.
For a
\bar{G}
-morphism
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to H
, we say that
\chi\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}
if there are exactly
\gamma
primes
p\notin S
such that
\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}
.
Recall also that if
\chi\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}
is surjective and factors through
J_{K}^{S}/K^{S}
, then the corresponding field
L
is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}
.
In this case we simply write
\chi\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}
.

Step 2:

Then we follow the idea of Wood
[
19
]
to construct the generating series and so on.
Using the structure theorem of finite abelian groups, write
H=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{Z}/n_{j}\mathbb{Z}
, where
n_{j}\mid n_{j+1}
for each
j=1,\dots,l-1
.
Define
\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{j=1}^{l}K^{S}/(K^{S})^{n_{j}}
.
For each continuous homomorphism
\chi:J_{K}^{S}\to H
, define
\chi_{j}:J_{K}^{S}\to\mathbb{Z}/n_{j}\mathbb{Z}
to be the projection.
And for each
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{l})\in\mathcal{A}
, define

\dot{\chi}(\epsilon):=\prod_{j=1}^{l}\zeta_{n_{j}}^{\chi_{j}(\epsilon_{j})}.

For the computation of this notation
\dot{\chi}(\epsilon)
, we refer to Wood
[
19
, Lemma 2.16 and 2.17]
for details.
Another result (or a corollary) we cite from this paper is the following.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\lvert G\rvert\infty
be a prime of
K
.
If
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to H
is a continuous map such that the tame inertia generator of the corresponding
H
-structured
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra is
g\in H
, then Wood
[
19
, Lemma 2.17]
implies that there exists some Hecke character
\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}
of
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
such that

\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{P})=\frac{\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\epsilon^{g})}{\epsilon^{g}}=\dot{\chi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\epsilon)

where
\mathfrak{P}
is any prime of
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
above
\mathfrak{p}
, and
\epsilon^{g}
is defined in Wood
[
19
, Lemma 2.17]
.
Since the value is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{P}
, by abuse of notation, let us just write it as
\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{p})
.
But keep in mind that the Hecke character is defined for
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
.

Step 3:

Now let
\varphi:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\to H
be a surjective
\bar{G}
-morphism.
For each
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, define

F_{C,\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}\\
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}},\forall\mathfrak{p}\in S\end{subarray}}C(\chi)^{-s}.

For simplicity, it suffices to consider the following specifications and the corresponding generating series.
For each
p\notin S
, define

\Omega_{p}^{0}:=\{\Sigma_{p}\mid y_{p}=e_{G}\}

where
\Sigma_{p}
is a specification at
p
with respect to
G
, and
y_{p}
is its inertia generator.
Recall again that for each
\bar{G}
-morphism
\chi_{p}:\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\to H
, the notations
\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}
and
\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{0}
are well-defined because
\Omega^{+}_{p}
and
\Omega_{p}^{0}
are defined by inertia generators.
Let

\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G}):=\{p\text{ rational prime}\mid p\notin S\text{ and }p\text{ totally splits in }K/\mathbb{Q}\},

where
e_{G}
means that the Frobenius is trivial.
By Čebotarev density Theorem, we know that this set is infinite.
To abbreviate the notations, write
I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}:=I^{+,\mu}_{\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G})}
.
For each integer
d\in I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}
, define

\Phi(d):=\prod_{p\mid d}\Omega_{p}^{+}\times\prod_{p\notin S,p\nmid d}\Omega_{p}^{0}.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}:=

\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\{\chi\in\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}\mid\chi\in\Phi(d)\}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}:=

\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\{\chi\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{K,\gamma}\mid\chi\in\Phi(d)\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}
is a subset of
\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}
.
We have the generating series

F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}\\
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}},\forall\mathfrak{p}\in S\end{subarray}}C(\chi)^{-s}.

If we write
F_{C,\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)=\sum_{n}a_{n}n^{-s}
and
F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)=\sum_{n}b_{n}n^{-s}
, then
b_{n}\leq a_{n}
for all positive integer
n
.
For each
\epsilon\in\mathcal{A}
, we have the following:

\displaystyle F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}\\
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}},\forall\mathfrak{p}\in S\end{subarray}}\dot{\chi}(\epsilon)C(\chi)^{-s}C(\varphi)^{-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\varphi)^{-s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid d}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}}\dot{\chi}_{p}(\epsilon)p^{-ms}\Bigr).

The relation between
F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)
and
F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)
is given by the expression:

F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{A}}F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s).

That is, a field
L\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}
is represented by a unique surjective map
\chi\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}
that factors through
K^{S}
.
For two functions
f(s)
and
g(s)
defined in the open half-plane
\sigma>1/m
, we say that
f(s)\approx_{m}g(s)
if the ratio
f/g
admits an analytic continuation to the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1/m
.
For
\gamma=1
, we do the computation as follows.

\displaystyle F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)=

\displaystyle C(\varphi)^{-s}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G})}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}}\dot{\chi}_{p}(\epsilon)p^{-ms}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\varphi)^{-s}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G})}p^{-ms}\Bigl(\sum_{\chi_{p}\in\Omega_{p}^{+}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{K}\\
\mathfrak{p}\mid p\end{subarray}}\dot{\chi}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\epsilon)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\approx_{m}

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\Omega}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G})\\
p\equiv 1\bmod{r_{g}}\end{subarray}}p^{-ms}\prod_{x\in\bar{G}}\theta_{\epsilon^{g_{x\mathfrak{p}}}}(x\mathfrak{p}),

where
\mathfrak{p}
is some prime of
K
above
p
, and

is the inertia generator at
x\mathfrak{p}
.
We use a trick to express this product in the last row into a single Hecke character.
For each

, we know that
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Therefore we may treat the inertia generator

as an element of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
such that
y_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv\zeta_{p-1}\bmod{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\zeta_{p-1}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
is a fixed generator.
Note that
x:K_{\mathfrak{p}}\to K_{x\mathfrak{p}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-isomorphism.
We see that
xy_{\mathfrak{p}}
also satisfies
xy_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv\zeta_{p-1}\bmod{\mathfrak{p}}
, for
\zeta_{p-1}
could be represented by an integer which is fixed by any Galois action, that is,
xy_{\mathfrak{p}}=y_{x\mathfrak{p}}
.
So, the product of the last row also defines a Hecke character of
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
by

\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}:=\prod_{x\in\bar{G}}\theta_{\epsilon^{xg}}\circ\tilde{x},

where
\tilde{x}
is a preimage of
x
in
G(K(\zeta_{r_{g}})/\mathbb{Q})
.
Since the choice of
\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p}
in the extension
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})/K
does not affect the value of
\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}
, we see that this is a well-defined product of Hecke characters, hence still a Hecke character.
Note also that
p
totally splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r_{g}})/\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
p\equiv 1\bmod{r_{g}}
.
So, we can write

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)\approx_{m}\sum_{g\in\Omega}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{K(\zeta_{r_{g}})/\mathbb{Q}}(e)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{K(\zeta_{r_{g}})}\\
\mathfrak{P}\mid p\end{subarray}}p^{-ms}\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{P}).

Then let us deal with the condition that
p\in\mathcal{P}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e_{G})
so that everything could be rearranged as a combination of
L
-functions.
Note that an unramified finite prime
p
splits completely in
K/\mathbb{Q}
if and only if the Frobenius at
p
is trivial.
So, by orthogonality of characters, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{K(\zeta_{r_{g}})/\mathbb{Q}}(e)}p^{-ms}\sum_{\mathfrak{P}\mid p}\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\text{ unr}}p^{-ms}\sum_{\mathfrak{P}\mid p}\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{P})\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert C_{\bar{G}}(e_{\bar{G}})\rvert}\sum_{\chi\in\hat{\bar{G}}}\bar{\chi}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{\phi(r_{g})}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/r_{g})^{*}}}\bar{\psi}(p)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(r_{g})\lvert\bar{G}\rvert}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\hat{\bar{G}}\\
\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/r_{g})^{*}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{p\text{ unr}}\bar{\chi}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})\bar{\psi}(p)p^{-ms}\sum_{\mathfrak{P}\mid p}\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}(\mathfrak{P}).

Note that
\bar{\chi}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is also a Dirichlet character.
To be precise, there exists a Dirichlet character
\psi_{\chi}
such that
\psi_{\chi}(p)=\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
for all unramified
p
.
Lift the characters to
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
, that is, for each
(\chi,\psi)\in\widehat{\bar{G}}\times\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/r_{g})^{*}}
, we define a character
\rho_{g,\chi,\psi}
by

\rho_{g,\chi,\psi}:=(\psi_{\chi}\cdot\psi)\circ\operatorname{Nm}_{K(\zeta_{r_{g}})/\mathbb{Q}}.

Recall that a Hecke
L
-series
L(\psi,s)
admits a zero-free region (Coleman
[
5
]
).
So, we have

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)\approx_{m}\sum_{g\in\Omega}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\hat{\bar{G}}\\
\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/r_{g})^{*}}\end{subarray}}\log L(\rho_{g,\chi,\psi}\cdot\psi_{\epsilon^{g}},ms).

The relation
\approx_{m}
holds because the comparison of the series is mainly determined by the totally split primes, that is, they have the same coefficient for each
p^{-ms}
.
Note that
\widehat{\bar{G}}
and
\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/r_{g})^{*}}
admit a trivial character.
As for
\psi_{\epsilon^{g}}
, i.e.,
\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}
, if the element
\epsilon^{g}
defined as in Wood
[
19
, Lemma 2.17]
is contained in
K(\zeta_{r_{g}})
, then
\theta_{\epsilon^{g}}
is trivial.
And this condition is automatically satisfied when
\epsilon
is taken to be the identity of
\mathcal{A}
.
Recall that if a Hecke character
\psi
is nontrivial, then
L(\psi,ms)
is holomorphic in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1/m
.
Else if
\psi
is principal, then
L(\psi,s)
and
\zeta_{K(\zeta_{r_{g}})}(s)
differ by finitely many local factors, and
L(\psi,ms)
admits a holomorphic continuation to the line
s=1/m
except at
s=1/m

In particular, near the point
s=1/m
, we have

\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{\log L(\psi,ms)}{\log\bigl(\frac{1}{s-1/m}\bigr)}=1,

which is exactly the order of the pole of
L(\psi,ms)
at
s=1/m
.
This shows that for each
\epsilon\in\mathcal{A}
, the series
F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)
is a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1/m
,
and it admits a holomorphic continuation to the line
\sigma=1/m
except at
s=1/m
.
In addition, there exist holomorphic functions
f_{1,1}(s),f_{1,0}(s)
at
s=1/m
such that
f_{1,1}(1/m)
is positive real and such that

F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,s)=f_{1,1}(s)\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{s-1/m}\Bigr)+f_{1,0}(s)

near
s=1/m
.
By Tauberian Theorem
[
12
, Appendix II Theorem I]
we see that

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K,1},X)\geq N_{C}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,1},X)\sim b_{0}\frac{X^{1/m}}{\log X}

where
b_{0}
is a positive real number.
This proves the statement when
\gamma=1
.
The case for general
\gamma
follows from Lemma
5.2
, for if we write

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,1}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)=C(\varphi)^{-s}\sum_{p}b_{p,\epsilon}p^{-ms},

then

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)=C(\varphi)^{-s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in I^{+,\mu}_{e_{G}}\\
\omega(d)=\gamma\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\prod_{p\mid d}b_{p,\epsilon}p^{-ms}\Bigr).

So Lemma
5.2
implies the analytic continuation of
F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,\epsilon,s)
, hence also of
F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)
.
In particular, the case when
\epsilon=e_{\mathcal{A}}
shows that
F_{C,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)
could not be extended analytically to
s=1/m
, and gives the desired analytic behaviour near
s=1/m
.
To be precise, there exist holomorphic functions
f_{\gamma,0},f_{\gamma,1},\dots,f_{\gamma,\gamma}
at
s=1/m
such that
f_{\gamma,\gamma}(1/m)
is positive real and that

F_{C,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma}}(\varphi,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma}f_{\gamma,i}(s)\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{s-1/m}\Bigr)^{j}.

Then Tauberian Theorem
[
12
, Appendix II Theorem I]
gives the desired estimate similar to the case when
\gamma=1
:

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma},X)\geq N_{C}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{K,\gamma},X)\sim b_{\gamma}\frac{X^{1/m}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1},

where
b_{\gamma}
is a positive real number.
∎

We are finished with the proof of all the main statements of this section.
In summary, Lemma
7.10
shows an upper bound of
N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)
.
while Lemma
7.11
shows an lower bound by

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},C)\geq N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{K,\gamma},X).

So, we’ve proven that the Hypothesis
3.12
(1) is true for
((\mathcal{E},C),\Omega,\mathcal{P})
.
To be precise, for each non-negative integer
\gamma
we have

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)=o(N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma+1},X)).

7.3.
Examples

In this section let us discuss some applications of the main results of this section via examples, including the proof of Theorem
1.11
and
1.12
.

Example 7.12

(Sextic fields with
A_{4}
-closure)
.

This is the prototype of Section
7
and even the whole paper.
Let
A_{4}\subseteq S_{4}
be the alternating group of order
4
.
Consider the map
f:A_{4}\hookrightarrow S_{6}
induced by
(12)(34)\mapsto(1)(2)(34)(56)
, and
(123)\mapsto(135)(246)
, and let
G
be the image of
A_{4}
.
It is a transitive permutation group in
S_{6}
.
The stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(1)
in
G
of
1
is a cyclic group of order
2
, generated by the image of
(12)(34)
.
Let
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(G)
be the set of sextic fields with
A_{4}
-closure.
Clearly
G
admits the following short exact sequence

1\to V_{4}\to G\to C_{3}\to 1,

where
C_{3}
acts on
V_{4}
by permuting the generators.
Define

\Omega:=\{g\in G\mid r_{g}=2\},

which is equivalent to the image of
V_{4}\backslash\{e_{G}\}
in
G
.
For any non-negative integer
\gamma
, write
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{\mathcal{P}}
.
In other words,
L\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
means that there are exactly
\gamma
primes
p\nmid 6
such that its inertia generator is contained in
\Omega
.

Though it seems that we cannot apply the results of this section to it directly.
Note that every field
L\in\mathcal{E}
admits a Galois closure
M
which is a Galois
A_{4}
-field.
The study of
\mathcal{E}
and the study of
A_{4}
-fields are essentially the same once we give the definition of the counting function
C
.
The (absolute) discriminant of the sextic field
L\in\mathcal{E}
could be written as

d_{L}=d_{K}^{2}\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{L/K})

where
K
is the Galois cubic subfield of
L
, and
\mathfrak{d}_{L/K}
is the relative discriminant.

Let
p\geq 5
be a rational prime that is ramified in
L/\mathbb{Q}
.
Its inertia subgroup
I_{p}
is cyclic, so it is either isomorphic to
C_{2}
or
C_{3}
.
If
I_{p}\cong C_{3}
, then the normalizer of
I_{p}
is itself.
So,
I_{p}=G_{p}
, and
L/K
is unramified.
This implies that
p^{2}\|d_{K}
and
p\nmid\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{L/K})
.

If
I_{p}\cong C_{2}
, then the normalizer of
I_{p}
is isomorphic to
V_{4}
.
However, whether we have
G_{p}\cong V_{4}
or
G_{p}=I_{p}
, the prime
p\mathscr{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}\mathfrak{p}_{3}
must split in
K/\mathbb{Q}
.
Because of the action of
C_{3}
on
V_{4}
, there are exactly
2
of the primes, say
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, ramified in
L/K
.
Therefore,
p\nmid d_{K}
and
p^{2}\|\operatorname{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{L/K})
.

This shows that
d_{L}
could be realized as a counting function.
To be precise, let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of Galois
A_{4}
-fields, and define
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{F}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{\mathcal{P}}
.
The map
c_{G}:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is defined by
c_{G}(g)=2
for
g\in\Omega
and
c_{G}(g)=4
for
g\in G\backslash(\Omega\cup\{e_{G}\})
.
This induces a counting function
C
for
\mathcal{F}
.
And for each
M\in\mathcal{F}
with a subfield
L\in\mathcal{E}
, we see that
C(M)=d_{L}
up to wildly ramified primes.
So, we have a nontrivial example that satisfies the set-up of the main results of this section, and Theorem
1.11
is proved immediately.
Note that Definition
1.3
defines the set of fields up to isomorphism classes instead of fields in a fixed algebraic closure.
So, by Lemma
7.1
, for each
0<\varepsilon<1
, we have

N_{d}(\mathcal{E}_{0},X)=N_{C}(\mathcal{F}_{0},X)\ll X^{1/(3-\varepsilon)}.

For each positive integer
\gamma
, we have

N_{d}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)=N_{C}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma},X)\asymp\frac{X^{1/2}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1}.

This implies that Hypothesis
3.12
(1) is true for
((\mathcal{E},d),\Omega,\mathcal{P})
.
So, Theorem
3.1
and
3.14
imply that Theorem
1.11
is true.

Finally, let us apply the method of this section to the non-Galois cubic fields, i.e., the proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Example 7.13

(Cubic fields)
.

Let
G=S_{3}
, and
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(G)
be the set of non-Galois cubic fields.
The group
G
admits the short exact sequence

1\to C_{3}\to G\to C_{2}\to 1

where
C_{2}
acts on
C_{3}
in a nontrivial way.
Let
\Omega:=C_{3}\backslash\{e_{G}\}
.
For each non-negative integer
\gamma
, write
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega,\gamma}^{\mathcal{P}}
.
In other words, a cubic field
K_{3}\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
if there are exactly
\gamma
primes
p\nmid 6
such that its tame inertia generator is contained in
\Omega
.
The condition simply means that
p
is totally ramified in
K_{3}
.
The counting functions discriminant and product of ramified primes do not satisfy our conditions.
So we need to define a special one so that the method of this section works.

For each non-Galois cubic field
K_{3}
, let
K_{6}
be its Galois closure and
K_{2}:=K_{6}^{C_{3}}
be the associated quadratic number field.
Note that if we write
d_{K_{3}}=d_{K_{2}}f^{2}
, where
p\mid f
implies that
p
is totally ramified in
K_{3}
.
Or equivalently
f^{4}=\operatorname{Nm}_{K_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{d}_{K_{6}/K_{2}})
where
\mathfrak{d}
means the relative discriminant.
Define

C(K_{3}):=d_{K_{3}}d_{K_{2}}^{2}=d_{K_{2}}^{3}f^{2}.

One can check that this counting function could also be realized by Definition
4.3
in the following sense.
Let
c_{G}:G\to\mathbb{Z}
be the map induced by
c_{G}((12))=3
and
c_{G}((123))=2
.
For
p\mid 6
, let
c_{p}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-valued map on the set of specifications at
p
.
Then we obtain a counting function
C^{\prime}
for
S_{3}
-fields induced by
c_{G}
and
c_{2},c_{3}
.
Since
K_{3}=K_{6}^{\langle(23)\rangle}
, the function
C^{\prime}
induces a counting function for
K_{3}\in\mathcal{E}
, still denoted by
C^{\prime}
.
In particular whenever
K_{3}
is tamely ramified,
C^{\prime}(K_{3})=C(K_{3})
.
In other words, they differ only at
2
and
3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of Galois
S_{3}
-fields.
Now we can apply Lemma
7.1
and obtain for each positive integer
\gamma
the following estimate

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{\gamma},X)=N_{C}(\mathcal{F}_{\gamma},X)\asymp\frac{X^{1/2}}{\log X}(\log\log X)^{\gamma-1}.

Also, the work of Davenport and Heilbronn
[
6
]
implies that there exists some real number
c_{0}>0
such that

N_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0},X)\sim c_{0}X^{1/3},

for there is no totally ramified primes and for each
K\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
we have
C(K)=d_{K}^{3}
, where
d
is the usual discriminant.
This also shows that in general the lower bound estimate from Lemma
7.1
could not be improved in the sense of the main term, unless we have more conditions on the counting function
C
.
By Theorem
3.1
, there exists some constant
c=c(6)
, for each
K_{3}\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

\operatorname{rk}_{3}\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{3}}\geq\#\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid p\text{ is totally ramified}\}-c=\#\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Omega,K_{3})-c.

By Theorem
3.14
, we obtain the following:
for each non-negative integer
r
, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{C}(\operatorname{rk}_{3}\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{3}}\leq r)=0,

and

\mathbb{E}_{C}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{3}},C_{3})\rvert)=+\infty.

Moreover, we can see that the statement remains true for all counting functions

C(K_{3}):=d_{K_{3}}d_{K_{2}}^{n},

where the integer
n\geq 2
.
This proves Theorem
1.12
.

Slope inequality for an arbitrary divisor
Houari Benammar Ammar

Abstract
Let f : S ⟶ C f:S\longrightarrow C be a surjective morphism with connected fibers from a smooth complex projective surface S S to a smooth complex projective curve C C with general fiber F F . In this paper, we develop a more general version of the slope inequality for data ( D , ℱ ) (D,\mathcal{F}) , where D D is an arbitrary relatively effective divisor on S S and ℱ \mathcal{F} is a locally free sub-sheaf of f ∗ ​ 𝒪 S ​ ( D ) f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D) . We see how the speciality of D D , restricted to the general fiber, plays a role in the results. Moreover, we compute some natural examples and provide applications.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

Let

be a fibred surface. Consider the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
, where

is a relatively effective divisor and

is not semi-stable. Here, we set:

-\infty&\mbox{otherwise}\text{.}\par\end{array}\right.

(2).

Recently, in

, the authors proved a slope inequality for a data

In this section, we study the Harder-Narasimhan filtration within the context of fibred
surfaces.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a vector bundle over a smooth projective curve

C
. There exists a unique sequence of vector sub-bundles of
\mathcal{F}
:

0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{1}\subsetneq...\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{k}=\mathcal{F}\text{,}

that satisfies the following conditions:

(1).

For
i=1,...,k
,
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
is a
semi-stable vector bundle.

(2).

For any
i=1,...,k
, setting
\mu_{i}:=\mu(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\frac{\operatorname{deg}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})}{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})}
, we have:

\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>...>\mu_{k}\text{.}

In the context of Proposition
3.1
above, the filtration is called the
Harder-Narasimhan filtration
of
\mathcal{F}
.
We set
\mu_{f}=\mu_{k}
and call it the
final slope
of
\mathcal{F}
.
The following elementary lemma is important in what follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r_{i}
be the rank of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Then

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+r_{k}\mu_{k}\text{.}

Proof.

Indeed, we consider the exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{k}/\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\longrightarrow 0\text{.}

From the additivity of degree, we have

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k}=\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k-1}+\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k}/\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\text{.}

Similarly, we have

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k-1}=\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k-2}+\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k-1}/\mathcal{F}_{k-2}\text{.}

And so, by induction, we can conclude that

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\text{.}

From the definition of slope, for every
i=1,..,k
we have:

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}=\mu_{i}(r_{i}-r_{i-1})\text{.}

Thus, we obtain the
desired formula.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Compare with
[
21
, Corollary IV.3.8]
)
.

\forall i;1\leq i\leq k,\hskip 5.69046ptN_{i}
are nef divisors on

Proof.

Fix

\mathbb{Q}
-twisted vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{i}\langle-\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})}{\ rk(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{k-1})}\rangle
is nef.

We define the following quotient bundles and
\mathbb{Q}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\\
\delta_{i}=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{i})}{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{G}_{i})}.\end{array}\right.

However,

is a nef vector bundle by
[

\operatorname{deg}\delta_{i}=\mu_{i}

-\operatorname{deg}\delta_{1}<-\operatorname{deg}\delta_{2}<...<-\operatorname{deg}\delta_{k}.

Using the following exact sequence of vector bundles:

we next prove that
\mathcal{F}_{i}\langle-\delta_{i}\rangle
is nef by induction,
\forall i;1\leq i\leq k

\mathcal{F}_{1}\langle-\delta_{1}\rangle=\mathcal{G}_{1}\langle-\delta_{1}\rangle
which is nef. Now, assume that
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\langle-\delta_{i-1}\rangle
is a nef bundle. Then
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\langle-\delta_{i}\rangle
is
\mathbb{Q}
-ample. Using the above exact sequence and since
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\langle-\delta_{i}\rangle
and
\mathcal{G}_{i}\langle-\delta_{i}\rangle

Thus,

Lemma 3.8

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
d_{i}=\operatorname{deg}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})=N_{i}.F
such that

. Then,

d_{k}\geq d_{k-1}\geq...\geq d_{1}\geq 0.

Proof.

Since
F
is a fiber,

. Then,

=\lambda_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{C}(\mathcal{F}_{i})}(1)).F=(\epsilon^{*}(D-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i}})-E).F

But

Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\geq Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i+1}}\text{.}

Thus,

D-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i+1}}=D-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}+(Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{i+1}})\text{.}

Therefore,

d_{i+1}\geq d_{i}\text{.}

Proposition 3.10
.

Continuing with the setting as above, we define the following constant for the data

\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{max}\{i|\hskip 2.84544pt{N_{i}}_{|_{F}}\text{is special}\}\\

, then
{N_{j}}_{|_{F}}
is special for

and
{N_{j}}_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial for
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1\leq j\leq k
. Otherwise,
{N_{j}}_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial,
\forall j;1\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

Recall that

Z_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\geq...\geq Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}.

Also, we identified the general fiber of
S
with the general fiber of
\hat{S}
. Let
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1\leq j\leq k-1

\dots\longrightarrow H^{0}(F,{N_{j+1}}_{|_{F}}/{N_{j}}_{|_{F}})\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,{N_{j}}_{|_{F}})\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,{N_{j+1}}_{|_{F}})\longrightarrow 0.

\frac{d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1}\leq...\leq\frac{d_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}}{h^{0}({N_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}}_{|_{F}})-1}.

–

\forall i;\hat{s}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\leq i\leq\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}
:

\frac{d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1}\geq 2\text{.}

Proof.

For
(1)
, if
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=-\infty
, then by definition of
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}
,
{N_{i}}_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial
\forall i;1\leq i\leq k
. Thus, by applying the Riemann-Roch formula:

h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})=d_{i}+1-g(F)\text{.}

Since

h^{0}({N_{i+1}}_{|_{F}})=h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})+d_{i+1}-d_{i}\text{,}\hskip 8.5359pt

it follows that
\forall i;\hat{s}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\leq i\leq k-1
:

\frac{d_{i+1}}{h^{0}({N_{i+1}}_{|_{F}})-1}=\frac{d_{i}+d_{i+1}-d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})+d_{i+1}-d_{i}-1}\leq\frac{d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1}\text{.}

Moreover,
D_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial, and then by the Riemann-Roch formula we have

h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})=D.F+1-g(F)\text{.}

Thus,

h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})=h^{0}(F,{N_{i}}_{|_{F}})+D.F-d_{i}\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\forall i;1\leq i\leq k\text{.}

Then, we deduce the desired lower bound:

\frac{d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1}\geq 1+\frac{g(F)}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})-1}\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\forall i;\hat{s}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\leq i\leq k.

This proves
(1)
.

For
(2)
, if
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1\leq i\leq k
, then argue as in
(1)
. Suppose now that

{N_{i}}_{|_{F}}
is special, that is
\hat{s}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\leq i\leq\hat{n}
. Then by Clifford Theorem
[
1
]
, we have

d_{i}\geq 2(h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1)

which implies

\frac{d_{i}}{h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1}\geq 2\text{.}

∎

Remark 3.18
.

If we compare Proposition
3.9
and Theorem
3.17
, then we deduce that
\forall i;\hat{s}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\leq i\leq k
:

d_{k}\geq d_{k-1}\geq...\geq d_{\hat{s}}\geq 1\text{.}

4
Slope inequalities

Now, we are ready to present the technical lemma in the method, we called it the
Modified Xiao Lemma
. Note that it is a more general form of Xiao
[
29
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
Modified Xiao Lemma
)
.

Let
\hat{f}:\hat{S}\to C
be a fibred surface with
F
its general fiber,
\hat{D}
be a divisor on
\hat{S}
, and suppose that there
exist a sequence of effective
divisors:

\mathcal{Z}_{1}\geq\mathcal{Z}_{2}\geq...\geq\mathcal{Z}_{j}\text{,}

and a sequence of rational numbers:

\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq...\geq\mu_{j}\text{,}

such that for every
i\in\{1,...,j\}
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{i}:=\hat{D}-\mathcal{Z}_{i}-\mu_{i}F

are nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors. Then,

\hat{D}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+2\hat{D}.\mathcal{Z}_{j}-\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{2}+2\mu_{j}d_{j}\text{,}

where
d_{i}=\mathcal{N}_{i}.F
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\mathcal{N}_{1}^{2}\geq 0
by nefness. However,

\mathcal{N}_{i}^{2}=\mathcal{N}_{i}(\mathcal{N}_{i-1}+(\mathcal{Z}_{i-1}-\mathcal{Z}_{i})+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})F)

\geq\mathcal{N}_{i}(\mathcal{N}_{i-1}+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})F)

\geq(\mathcal{N}_{i-1}+(\mathcal{Z}_{i-1}-\mathcal{Z}_{i})+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})F)(\mathcal{N}_{i-1}+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})F)

\geq\mathcal{N}_{i-1}^{2}+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})(2\mathcal{N}_{i-1}F+(\mathcal{Z}_{i-1}-\mathcal{Z}_{i})F)

=\mathcal{N}_{i-1}^{2}+(\mu_{i-1}-\mu_{i})(d_{i-1}+d_{i})\text{.}

So, by induction we have

\mathcal{N}_{j}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})\text{.}

Hence,

(\hat{D}-\mathcal{Z}_{j}-\mu_{j}F)^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})\text{.}

But,

(\hat{D}-\mathcal{Z}_{j}-\mu_{j}F)^{2}=(\hat{D}-\mathcal{Z}_{j})^{2}-2\mu_{j}(\hat{D}-\mathcal{Z}_{j})F

=\hat{D}^{2}-2\hat{D}.\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{2}-2\mu_{j}d_{j}\text{.}

Thus,

\hat{D}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+2\hat{D}.\mathcal{Z}_{j}-\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{2}+2\mu_{j}d_{j}\text{.}

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The term
2\hat{D}.\mathcal{Z}_{j}-\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{2}+2\mu_{j}d_{j}
describes the negativity of
\hat{D}
.

Example 4.3
.

In the setting of Lemma
4.1
, suppose that
j=k
,
\hat{D}
is nef, and
\mu_{k}\geq 0
. Set
\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}=0
and
\mu_{k+1}=0
. Then apply Lemma
4.1
to the sequence of effective divisors:

\mathcal{Z}_{1}\geq\mathcal{Z}_{2}\geq...\geq\mathcal{Z}_{k+1}=0\text{,}

and a sequence of rational numbers:

\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq...\geq\mu_{k+1}=0\text{.}

So, we conclude the original result of Xiao
[
29
, Lemma 2]
:

\hat{D}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}).

Prior to stating the main results, we apply Lemma
4.1
to the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
where
\mathcal{F}\subseteq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
. Let
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq k}
be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{F}
, and
(Z_{i},M_{i},N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
be the triple of fixed parts, moving parts, and the Miyaoka divisors respectively (Definition
3.4
). Then, we deduce the following inequalities.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface with general fiber
F
. Consider the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
where
D
is a relatively effective divisor on
S
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is a locally free sub-sheaf on
C
with
\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{F}\geq 2
. Then we have the following three cases:

(1).

If
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=-\infty
, then

D^{2}\geq 2\beta_{D}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}-\beta_{D}\mu_{1}+(\beta_{D}-2\beta_{D}r_{k}+2d_{k})\mu_{k}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(2).

If
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=k
, then

D^{2}\geq 4\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}-2\mu_{1}+2(1-2r_{k}+d_{k})\mu_{k}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(3).

If
1\leq\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}<k
, then

D^{2}\geq 4\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+2\beta_{D}\sum_{i=\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}^{k-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})

-2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1})-\beta_{D}(\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}-\mu_{k})+(\beta_{D}+2)r_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}(\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1})

+2\epsilon^{*}D.{Z}_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}+2\mu_{k}d_{k}\text{.}

Here
\epsilon
is constructed as in Proposition
3.3
and
\beta_{D}
is as in Theorem
3.17
.

Proof.

Recall that
\hat{f}=f\circ\epsilon:\hat{S}\to C
is a fibred surface with
F
its general fiber and
\hat{D}:=\epsilon^{*}D
is a relatively effective divisor on
\hat{S}
. Consider
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq k}
the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{F}
, let
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
be the sequence of slopes and
(Z_{i},M_{i},N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
the triple of fixed parts, moving parts, and the Miyaoka divisors respectively, recall also that
\hat{D}=Z_{i}+M_{i},\forall i;1\leq i\leq k
, and

Z_{1}\geq Z_{2}\geq\dots\geq Z_{k}\geq 0,

\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\dots>\mu_{k}\text{.}

Therefore,
N_{i}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-nef divisors on
\hat{S}
, where
d_{i}=N_{i}.F
. Now, by Lemma
4.1
, we have the following inequality:

\hat{D}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+2\hat{D}.{Z}_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}+2\mu_{k}d_{k}\text{.}

(4.1)

Recall that
\epsilon:\hat{S}\to S
is a proper birational morphism, hence
\hat{D}^{2}=D^{2}
. By Theorem
3.17
, we have the following natural three cases:

(1).

If
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=-\infty
, then
{N_{i}}_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial
\forall i;1\leq i\leq k
, and

d_{i}\geq\beta_{D}(h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1)\geq\beta_{D}(r_{i}-1)\text{.}

The right hand side inequality follows from Lemma
3.8
, where
r_{i}=\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F}_{i}),\forall i;1\leq i\leq k
, we also recall that
r_{i+1}\geq r_{i}-1,\forall i;1\leq i\leq k-1
, hence

d_{i+1}\geq\beta_{D}r_{i}\text{.}

We substitule all these information into (
4.1
):

D^{2}\geq 2\beta_{D}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})-\beta_{D}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{k})+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}+2\mu_{k}d_{k}\text{.}

By Lemma
3.2
, we deduce the desired inequality:

D^{2}\geq 2\beta_{D}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}-\beta_{D}\mu_{1}+(\beta_{D}-2\beta_{D}r_{k}+2d_{k})\mu_{k}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(2).

If
\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=k
, then
{N_{i}}_{|_{F}}
is special
\forall i;1\leq i\leq k
and

d_{i}\geq 2(h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1)\geq 2(r_{i}-1)\text{,}

however

d_{i+1}\geq 2r_{i}\text{.}

This implies

D^{2}\geq 4\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}-2\mu_{1}+2(1-2r_{k}+d_{k})\mu_{k}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(3).

If
1\leq\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}<k
, then

\forall i;\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1\leq i\leq k,\hskip 11.38092ptd_{i}\geq\beta_{D}(h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1)\geq\beta_{D}(r_{i}-1)\text{,}

and

\forall i;1\leq i\leq\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})},\hskip 11.38092ptd_{i}\geq 2(h^{0}({N_{i}}_{|_{F}})-1)\geq 2(r_{i}-1)\text{.}

So, we decompose the right hand side of (
4.1
) into three parts:

D^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+\sum_{i=\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}^{k-1}(d_{i}+d_{i+1})(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})

+(d_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}+d_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1})(\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1})

+2\hat{D}.{Z}_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}+2\mu_{k}d_{k}\text{.}

Which implies

D^{2}\geq 4\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})+2\beta_{D}\sum_{i=\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}^{k-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})

-2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})-\beta_{D}(\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}-\mu_{k})+(\beta_{D}+2)r_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}(\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-\mu_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1})

+2\hat{D}.{Z}_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}+2\mu_{k}d_{k}\text{,}

since

d_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}\geq 2(r_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-1)\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptd_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}\geq\beta_{D}(r_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}-1)\geq\beta_{D}(r_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})\text{.}

∎

Remark 4.5
.

The lower bound that we obtain for
D^{2}
in Theorem
4.4
for
1\leq\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}<k
is given by a sum of three parts. The first part,

4\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})

describes the effect of special Miyaoka divisors. The second part,

2\beta_{D}\sum_{i=\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+1}^{k-1}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1})

explains the impact of the nonspecial Miyaoka divisors in the sequence
(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
. The third part is a transition from special to nonspecial Miyaoka divisors. In general, we could give a lower bound for
D^{2}
by

4\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}},\hskip 5.69046pt2\beta_{D}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}_{\hat{n}_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}

and other terms, but to formulate the slope inequality for the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
, it turns out that we should first start by taking
\operatorname{min}(2,\beta_{D})
and write, for instance,

d_{i}\geq\operatorname{min}(2,\beta_{D})(r_{i}-1)\text{.}

Following this set-up, we will obtain in the next corollary a lower bound for
D^{2}
by

2\operatorname{min}(2,\beta_{D})\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}

and other terms that we will explore in the upcoming paragraphs.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface,
F
its general fiber. Consider the data

\{\mu_{1},\mu_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}\}
. Thus,

D^{2}\geq d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})+d_{1}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}^{2}\text{.}

(4.4)

Since
d_{1}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})\geq 0
, and by Remark
3.18
, we have
d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}\geq 1
. Then we can divide by
d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}
, thus the following inequality follows:

-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})\geq\frac{-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}}{d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}}D^{2}+2\frac{\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}}{d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}}\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-\frac{\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}}{d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}}Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}^{2}\text{.}

Combining this last inequality and the inequality (
4.3
), we deduce the desired result:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}}{d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}+\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}^{2}

\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}}{d_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}+\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}})}}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}-Z_{t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}^{2}\text{.}

(3).

If
t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}=-\infty
, so both
\mu_{1},\mu_{k}<0
. By the fact that

\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k}+2d_{k}\geq-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\text{,}

we conclude that

(\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k}+2d_{k})\mu_{1}\leq-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\mu_{1}\text{.}

We replace this last inequality in (
4.2
) for the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
, thus

D^{2}\geq 2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}+(\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k}+2d_{k})(\mu_{1}+\mu_{k})+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

We set

A:=\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}-2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k}+2d_{k}\text{.}

To obtain the desired result, we discuss the above inequality depending on whether
A\leq 0
or
A>0
.

(a).

If
A\leq 0
, then we apply Lemma
4.1
to the sequences
\{Z_{1},Z_{k}\}
and
\{\mu_{1},\mu_{k}\}
:

D^{2}\geq d_{k}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{k})+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(4.5)

Combining the two last inequalities above, we deduce

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}d_{k}}{-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k}-d_{k}}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(b).

If
A>0
, we note that

\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}\leq\mu_{1}+\mu_{k}\text{.}

Then,

D^{2}\geq(3\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}+2d_{k}-2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}r_{k})\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

Now, we prove the last point of the theorem. If
d_{k}=\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}(r_{k}-1)
, we apply inequality (
4.2
) for
m=k
:

D^{2}\geq 2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}-\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{k})+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

Combining this last inequality and inequality (
4.5
), we deduce the desired result:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2d_{k}}{r_{k}}\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface, and let
D
be a relatively effective and relatively nef divisor on
S
. Consider the data
(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))
. Then the following number is nonnegative:

\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}\geq 0\text{.}

Proof.

We recall that
Z_{k}=\epsilon^{*}Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}+E
, where
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is the fixed part of
|D+f^{*}A|
and
A
is a sufficiently very ample divisor on
C
. Thus,
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is an effective divisor supported on fibers. By assumption,
D
is relatively nef, then we conclude the desired nonnegativity:

\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}=\epsilon^{*}D.(\epsilon^{*}Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}+E)=\epsilon^{*}D.\epsilon^{*}Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}=D.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\geq 0\text{.}

∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
D
be a relatively effective divisor. Consider the data
(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))
. Then
Z_{k}^{2}\leq 0
.

Proof.

Our aim is to prove that
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}\leq 0
. By contradiction, we assume that
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}>0
. Then by the Hodge index theorem, either

\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{0}(nZ_{\mathcal{F}_{k}})=+\infty\hskip 5.69046pt\text{or}\hskip 5.69046pt\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{0}(K_{S}-nZ_{\mathcal{F}_{k}})=+\infty\text{.}

However, the divisor
K_{S}-nZ_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is never effective if
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is not zero and
n\gg 0
. Thus, the only possibility is

\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{0}(nZ_{\mathcal{F}_{k}})=+\infty\text{.}

This contradicts the fact that
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is not movable. Therefore, we deduce
Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}\leq 0
. Consequently, by the definition of
Z_{k}
:

Z_{k}=\epsilon^{*}Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}+E\text{,}

we conclude the nonpositivity of
Z_{k}^{2}
; in other words,
Z_{k}^{2}\leq 0
.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
D
be a relatively effective and relatively nef divisor on
S
. Consider the data
(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))
.

(1).

If
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is semi-stable, then:

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

(2).

If
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is not semi-stable, we have the following cases:

(a).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=k
:
D
is nef and

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}D.F}{D.F+\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

(b).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=1
:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2d_{1}}{r_{1}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{2}\text{.}

(c).

If
1<t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}<k
:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}})}d_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}}{d_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}+\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}})}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}-Z_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}^{2}\text{.}

(d).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=-\infty
:

D^{2}\geq C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}.\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

In addition, if
D.F=\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}(h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-1)
, then independently of
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}
we have:

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

Proof.

First, if
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is semi-stable, then by Remark
4.8
we have:

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.{Z}_{1}-Z_{1}^{2}\text{.}

Since
2\epsilon^{*}D.{Z}_{1}-Z_{1}^{2}\geq 0
by Proposition
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
, where
d_{1}=D.F
and
r_{1}=\operatorname{rk}(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))=h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})
, we deduce the desired inequality:

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

Now, if
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is not semi-stable, then we apply Theorem
4.9
and we have the following cases:

(a).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=k
:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}d_{k}}{d_{k}+\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

(b).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(S))}=1
:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2d_{1}}{r_{1}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{1}-Z_{1}^{2}\text{.}

(c).

If
1<t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}<k
:

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}})}d_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}}{d_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}+\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F}_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}})}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}-Z_{t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}^{2}\text{.}

(d).

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=-\infty
:

D^{2}\geq C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}.\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)+2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

By Proposition
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
, we have that
2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\geq 0
. Furthermore,
d_{k}=D.F
. So we deduce the desired result:

•

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=k
, equivalently
\mu_{k}\geq 0
, then we have

D^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}D.F}{D.F+\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

•

If
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=-\infty
, it is equivalent to
\mu_{1}<0
. Then

D^{2}\geq C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}.\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

Also, if
\mu_{k}\geq 0
, then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is nef on
C
, so
M_{k}
is nef on
\hat{S}
. Since

\hat{D}=Z_{k}+M_{k}\text{,}

Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}
is supported in the fibers, and by assumption
D
is relatively nef, thus we deduce that
D
is nef.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

In general, if
D
is nef, we do not necessarily have
\mu_{k}\geq 0
.

Example 4.14
.

In
[
27
, Thoerem 5]
, the authors proved that if
D
is a relatively nef divisor on
S
such that
D_{|{{}_{F}}}
is generated by global sections on a general fiber
F
of
f
,
D_{|{{}_{F}}}
is a special divisor on
F
, and

2h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-D.F-1>0\text{,}

(P)

then

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

First, we remark that the condition (
P
) is equivalent to

2h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-D.F-1=1,

since by Clifford’s Theorem:

D.F\geq 2(h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-1)\text{.}

Thus, we can assume
D_{|{{}_{F}}}
has a section and is not necessarily generated by global sections since we can always eliminate the horizontal fixed part of
D
. By Corollary
4.12
, we see that whether
D_{|{{}_{F}}}
is special or nonspecial, we proved the same inequality:

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{,}

if the following more general condition holds:

D.F=\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}(h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-1)\text{.}

(Q)

Theorem 4.15
.

let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber. If
D
is a relatively effective and relatively nef divisor,
D_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial with
h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})>g
, then

D^{2}\geq 2\frac{D.F}{h^{0}(F,{D}_{|_{F}})}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

Proof.

By assumption,
D_{|_{F}}
is nonspecial and
h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})>g
. Thus, we have
\beta_{D}\leq 2
and
\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=\beta_{D}
. Then the condition
(Q)
is satisfied:

D.F=\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}(h^{0}(F,D_{|_{F}})-1)\text{.}

Finally, the desired inequality follows from Example
4.14
.
∎

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
D
be a nef divisor on
S
. Consider the data
(D,\mathcal{F})
. Then, if
t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\geq 1
:

2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{i}-Z_{i}^{2}\geq 0,\hskip 5.69046pt\forall i;1\leq i\leq t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}\text{.}

Proof.

Recall that
\epsilon^{*}D=M_{i}+Z_{i}
, thus
2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{i}-Z_{i}^{2}=(\epsilon^{*}D+M_{i})Z_{i}
. However,
M_{i}
and
\epsilon^{*}D
are nef,
\forall i;1\leq i\leq t_{(D,\mathcal{F})}
. So, we deduce the nonnegativity of
2\epsilon^{*}D.Z_{i}-Z_{i}^{2}
.
∎

5
Examples and applications

Example 5.1
.

Let
D=K_{S/C}
be the relative canonical divisor of a fibred surface
f\colon S\rightarrow C
with
g(F)\geq 2
. Thus, by
[
10
]
,
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}
is a nef vector bundle on
C
. Also,
\hat{n}_{(K_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C})}=k
since
D_{|_{F}}=K_{F}
is a special divisor on
F
. This implies
\alpha_{(K_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C})}=2
. We also have
t_{(K_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C})}=k
, where
k
is the length of the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}
, and
d_{k}=2g-2
where
g=g(F)
. Then, by Remark
4.8
and Theorem
4.9
, we have:

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq 4\frac{g-1}{g}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}+2\epsilon^{*}K_{S/C}.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}\text{.}

Recall that

2\epsilon^{*}K_{S/C}.Z_{k}-Z_{k}^{2}=2K_{S/C}.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}-E^{2}\text{,}

where
E
is the exceptional divisor of
\epsilon
(Proposition
3.3
). Here
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\mathcal{F}=f_{*}\omega_{S/C}
. In
[
2
, Example 2.1]
,
the authors considered the case where
f
is a relatively minimal nodal fibration. They calculate the term

2K_{S/C}.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}-E^{2}\text{,}

and prove that if
n\geq 1
is the total number of
disconnecting nodes contained in the fibres, we have

2K_{S/C}.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}-E^{2}\geq n\text{.}

Therefore,

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq 4\frac{g-1}{g}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}+n\text{.}

This implies if
f
is relatively minimal and

K_{S/C}^{2}=4\frac{g-1}{g}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{,}

then
f
is never a relatively minimal nodal fibration with at least
1
disconnected node.

Furthermore, we remark that if
f
is relatively minimal, then by Corollary
4.12
,
K_{S/C}
is nef and therefore

2K_{S/C}.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}-E^{2}\geq 0\text{.}

Moreover, to have

K_{S/C}^{2}=4\frac{g-1}{g}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{,}

it must be that

2K_{S/C}.Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}-Z_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}^{2}-E^{2}=0\text{.}

Then
|K_{S/C}+f^{*}\mathcal{O}(A)|
has no fixed part and is base point free for a sufficiently ample divisor on
C
.

In general, if
f
is relatively minimal, we always have the original Xiao’s result
[
29
]
:

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq 4\frac{g-1}{g}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

Example 5.2
.

Let
D=mK_{S/C}
be the relative pluricanonical divisor of a fibred surface
f\colon S\rightarrow C
, with
g=g(F)\geq 2
, and
m\geq 2
. It is well known that
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is a nef vector bundle on
C
, see
[
28
, Theorem 1.3]
for instance. If
K_{S/C}
is relatively nef, then by Corollary
4.12
, we have the following cases:

•

If
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is semi-stable:

m^{2}K_{S/C}^{2}\geq\frac{4m}{2m-1}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{,}

since

\operatorname{rk}(f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})=(2m-1)(g-1)\text{.}

Therefore,

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq\frac{4}{m(2m-1)}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{.}

•

If
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is not semi-stable, then using the fact that
mK_{F}
is a nonspecial divisor, we see that

\hat{n}_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}<k\text{,}

and

\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}=1+\frac{g}{h^{0}(F,mK_{F})-1}\text{,}

since

h^{0}(F,mK_{F})>g\text{.}

Moreover,

t_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}=k\text{,}

because
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is a nef vector bundle on
C
. Then,

m^{2}K_{S/C}\geq\frac{2\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}d_{k}}{d_{k}+\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{.}

Recall that
d_{k}=2m(g-1)
and
h^{0}(F,mK_{F})=(2m-1)(g-1)
, thus

\frac{2\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}d_{k}}{m^{2}(d_{k}+\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})})}=\frac{4}{m(2m-1)}\text{.}

Therefore,

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq\frac{4}{m(2m-1)}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{.}

In this computations, we see that whether
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is semi-stable or not, we have the same lower bound. The reason is that

d_{k}=\alpha_{(mK_{S/C},f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})}.(r_{k}-1)\text{,}

as explained in the last item of Corollary
4.12
. However, in general, for data
(D,\mathcal{F})
where
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
, the constant
\frac{2\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}d_{k}}{d_{k}+\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}}
is different from
\frac{2d_{k}}{r_{k}}
because the inequality
d_{k}>\alpha_{(D,\mathcal{F})}(r_{k}-1)
can well happen.

Example 5.3
.

Now, let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber with
g(F)\geq 2
. We take
D=K_{S/C}+L
such that
L
is nef and relatively big with
L.F>1
. We know that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is a nef vector bundle on
C
with

\operatorname{rk}(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))=g-1+L.F\neq 0\text{.}

If
D
is a relatively nef divisor, then by Corollary
4.12
, we have the following lower bound for
(K_{S/C}+L)^{2}
:

(K_{S/C}+L)^{2}\geq 2\left(1+\frac{g-1}{g-1+L.F}\right)\operatorname{deg}f_{*}(\omega_{S/C}\otimes\mathcal{O}(L))\text{,}

because we know the following information for the data
(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))
:

d_{k}=\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}(r_{k}-1),\hskip 5.69046pt\hat{n}_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}<k\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=1+\frac{g}{g-2+L.F}\text{,}

also the quantity
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}
is maximal, this means
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=k\text{.}

Example 5.4
.

We give a trivial example in which we can see that
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=-\infty
can happen.
Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber with
g(F)=2
. Assume that
K_{S/C}
is a nef divisor and the second Fujita decomposition (Theorem
1.2
) of
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}
is not trivial. Then there exists an ample line bundle
\mathcal{A}
on
C
and a flat line bundle
\mathcal{U}
such that

f_{*}\omega_{S/C}=\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{U}\text{.}

Now, let
D=K_{S/C}+f^{*}\mathcal{M}

for a sufficiently negative divisor
\mathcal{M}
on
C
such that
\operatorname{deg}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M}))<0
.
In this case,
D
is a relatively nef divisor on
S
such that
D_{|_{F}}=K_{F}
and the bundle
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
decomposes into two parts:

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathcal{M}\text{.}

The Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)
is the following:

0\subsetneq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{M}\subsetneq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

We consider the data
(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))
. Since
D_{|_{F}}=K_{F}
, we see that
D_{|_{F}}
is special,
\hat{n}_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=2
, and
\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=2
. Since
g(F)=2
, then we have
d_{2}=2
. Also, clearly, we have
t_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=-\infty
. Applying Corollary
4.12
, we deduce the following inequality:

D^{2}\geq C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

We know that
C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}=2
, so we replace
C_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}
by
2
in the above inequality:

D^{2}\geq 2\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D)\text{.}

Additionally, we remark that
d_{k}=\alpha_{(D,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(D))}(r_{k}-1)
. Thus, again by Corollary
4.12
or Example
4.14
, we deduce the same lower bound.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber with
g(F)\geq 2
. Then

K_{S/C}^{2}=\frac{2}{m(m-1)}\left(\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\right),\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

Remark 5.6
.

In the setting of Proposition
5.5
, in particular:

K_{S/C}^{2}=\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes 2}-\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

Proof.

Recall that if
S\to C
is a fibred surface, then for any line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{S}(L)
on
S
, we have the following Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formula
[
1
, page 333]
:

\operatorname{deg}f_{!}\mathcal{L}=\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\mathcal{L}-\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{L}=\frac{L(L-K_{S/C})}{2}+\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

In particular, we apply the above formula to the relative pluricanonical bundle
\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
, thus we obtain

\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}=\frac{m(m-1)K_{S/C}^{2}}{2}+\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

But
\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}=0
because
\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is known to be torsion free and

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}=h^{1}(F,\omega_{F}^{\otimes m})=h^{0}(F,\omega_{F}^{\otimes(1-m)})=0,

since
g(F)\geq 2
by assumption. Thus, we obtain the desired formula:

K_{S/C}^{2}=\frac{2}{m(m-1)}\left(\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\right),\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

∎

Remark 5.7
.

Using Noether’s Formula, Xiao
[
29
, Theorem 2]
remarked that

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq 12\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber with
g(F)\geq 2
. Then

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq\frac{12}{6m(m-1)+1}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

Proof.

We recall that in Proposition
5.5
, we proved that

K_{S/C}^{2}=\frac{2}{m(m-1)}\left(\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\right),\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2

which implies

\frac{m(m-1)}{2}K_{S/C}^{2}=\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

Thus,

\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}=\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}K_{S/C}^{2},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

(5.1)

Now, by Remark
5.7
, we have

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq 12\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}\text{.}

Combining this inequality with equality (
5.1
), it follows that

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq 12\left(\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}K_{S/C}^{2}\right),\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{,}

which implies

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq 12\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}-6m(m-1)K_{S/C}^{2},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

Therefore,

(6m(m-1)+1)K_{S/C}^{2}\leq 12\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{,}

which further simplifies to the desired inequality:

K_{S/C}^{2}\leq\frac{12}{6m(m-1)+1}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{.}

∎

Remark 5.9
.

If
K_{S/C}^{2}>0
and
g(F)\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}>0
,
\forall m\geq 2

.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface,
F
its general fiber with
g(F)\geq 2
, and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
with
\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}\geq 0
,
m\geq 2
. Then

\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{6m}{(6m(m-1)+1)(g-1)}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{,}

where
\mu(\mathcal{F})=\frac{\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}}{\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

We consider the data
(mK_{S/C},\mathcal{F})
and let
\mathcal{F^{{}^{\prime}}}
be the maximal destabilizing vector sub-bundle of
\mathcal{F}
. We apply Lemma
4.1

to the sequences
\{Z(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}),0\}
and
(\mu(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}),0)
. Then, we have

K_{S/C}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{m}(g-1)\mu(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})\text{.}

Here,
Z(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})
is the fixed part of the sub-bundle
\mathcal{F^{{}^{\prime}}}
and
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})=\frac{\operatorname{deg}\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}}
. Since
\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}}
is the maximal destabilizing vector sub-bundle of
\mathcal{F}
, we have

\mu(\mathcal{F}^{{}^{\prime}})\geq\mu(\mathcal{F})\text{.}

Combining the last two inequalities and Proposition
5.8
, we deduce the desired inequality:

\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\frac{6m}{(6m(m-1)+1)(g-1)}\operatorname{deg}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\text{.}

∎

Now, we state the First Fujita decomposition for the relative pluricanonical bundle and adjoint canonical bundle in the case of a fibred surface.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
its general fiber, let
L
be a semi-ample line bundle on
S
. Then

f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}=\mathcal{N}_{m}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{m}},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall m\geq 2\text{,}\text{ and }f_{*}(\omega_{S/C}\otimes L)=\mathcal{N}_{L}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{L}}\text{.}

Here,
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{N}_{m}^{\vee})=0
and
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{N}_{L}^{\vee})=0\text{.}

Proof.

Note
p_{m}:=h^{0}(C,(f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})^{\vee})=h^{0}(C,\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)})
, since
(f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m})^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)}
. We take
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{p_{m}}\}
as a basis of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)})\simeq Hom(\mathcal{O}_{C},\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)})
. Then
s_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus s_{p_{m}}
defines a map:

s_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus s_{p_{m}}:\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{m}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)}

which yields the following short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{m}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{1}f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes(1-m)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{m}\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathcal{Q}_{m}
is the quotient bundle. By duality, we have

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{m}^{\vee}\longrightarrow f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{m}}\longrightarrow 0\text{.}

We deduce from
[
12
, Theorem 26.4]
that the last exact sequence splits since the bundle
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
admits a singular hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature and verifies the minimal extension property, see
[
12
]
for more details. Hence, the first Fujita decomposition follows:

f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}=\mathcal{N}_{m}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus p_{m}}

Here,
\mathcal{N}_{m}=\mathcal{Q}_{m}^{\vee}
. and
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{Q}_{m})=0
.

For the adjoint case, we note
p_{L}:=h^{0}(C,(f_{*}(\omega_{S/C}\otimes L))^{\vee})
, the proof is the same as in the relative pluricanonical case, since
L
admits a smooth hermitian metric
h
with semi-positive curvature and a trivial multiplier ideal sheaf. Thus,
f_{*}(\omega_{S/C}\otimes L)
admits a singular hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature and verifies the minimal extension property.
∎

Theorem 5.12

)
.

Let

In the following paragraphs, we derive some explicit consequences for the direct image of relative pluricanonical bundles.

Example 5.13
.

If
f
is not isotrivial, it is known that
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is ample if not zero
\forall m\geq 2
. This implies that
the trivial part in the First Fujita decomposition is zero, and
the flat part in the Catanese-Fujita-Kawamata decomposition is zero.

Example 5.14
.

If
L
is an ample line bundle on

, it is well known that
f_{*}(\omega_{S/C}\otimes L)
is an ample vector bundle on
C
. Equivalently, the trivial part in the First Fujita decomposition is zero, and
the flat part in the Catanese-Fujita-Kawamata decomposition is zero.

Recall an important result on the direct image of pluricanonical sheaf over a curve due to Viehweg
[

and

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
f:S\to C
be a fibred surface and
F
be its general fiber. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1).

For all
m\geq 2
, the vector bundle
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is ample if not zero.

(2).

There exist some
m\geq 1
such that
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}

(3).

There exist some
m\geq 1

.

Moreover, if
f
is semi-stable, then the conditions
(1),(2)
, and
(3)
are equivalent to

(4).

f
is not isotrivial.

[

, Corollary 5.2]
)
.

Let

be a fibred surface. Then, either
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}

\forall m\geq 2
when
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}\neq 0
, or
f_{*}\omega_{S/C}^{\otimes m}
is hermitian flat
\forall m\geq 2
.

Proof.

5.15

If

.

, we observe that

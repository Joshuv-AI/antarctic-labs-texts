Brylinski-Radon transformation and generic projections
Yongqiang Liu, Laurentiu Maxim, Botong Wang

Abstract
. Using the Brylinski-Radon transformation, we prove that under a generic surjective linear map F : ℂ n → ℂ m F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m} , the pushforward of a perverse sheaf is perverse, modulo shifts of constant sheaves.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32S22, 32S60.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth affine algebraic variety
Y\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is well-known that for a generic linear function
l:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, the restriction
l|_{Y}
is a holomorphic Morse function with no critical points along infinity. Moreover, if
Y
is singular, the same statement holds in the stratified sense (see
[
STV05
, Lemma 3.1]
for the precise formulation). One can use this fact to relate the Chern-Mather Euler characteristics and Chern-Mather classes of affine varieties to the polar degrees (
[
STV05
,
ST10
]
). Partially motivated by these results, we investigate the direct pushforward of perverse sheaves under generic surjective linear maps.

We work with constructible complexes and perverse sheaves over a commutative ring
S
which is Noetherian and of finite cohomological dimension. The perverse
t
-structure is defined by using the middle perversity
p
, and we denote by
p^{+}
the dual perverse
t
-structure, which in general is not defined by a perversity function. Roughly speaking, a bounded constructible complex
\mathcal{P}
is in the heart of the dual
t
-structure
p^{+}
if its Verdier dual
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P})
is a perverse sheaf with respect to
p
. See, e.g.,
[
MS22
, Section 10.2.2]
for an introduction to perverse sheaves, and also
[
MS22
, Section 10.2.3]
for a brief account on the dual perverse
t
-structure.

The main result of this note is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a perverse sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and let
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a generic linear projection with
1\leq m\leq n-1
. Then, up to (shifts of) constant sheaves on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
RF_{*}(\mathcal{P})
is a perverse sheaf. More precisely, the perverse cohomology sheaf
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(RF_{*}(\mathcal{P}))
is the shift of a constant sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
for all
k\neq 0
. Moreover, the statement also holds if we replace the standard perverse
t
-structure
p
by the dual perverse
t
-structure
p^{+}
.

Applying Verdier duality, we also obtain analogous statements for the direct image with compact support.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the above notations, the perverse cohomology sheaf
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(RF_{!}(\mathcal{P}))
is the shift of a constant sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
for all
k\neq 0
. Moreover, the statement also holds if we replace the standard perverse
t
-structure
p
by the dual perverse
t
-structure
p^{+}
.

Our initial motivation for Theorem
1.1
was to study the topology of hyperplane arrangement complements. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{d}\}
be an affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and let
U_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus(H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{d})
be its complement. Then under any surjective linear map
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
, the fiber of
F|_{U_{\mathcal{A}}}\colon{U_{\mathcal{A}}}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
is either empty or a hyperplane arrangement complement of smaller dimension. This structure allows us to induct on dimension to study the topology of
{U_{\mathcal{A}}}
. See
[
LMW22
]
for some results in this direction.

For proving Theorem
1.1
, we make use of the Brylinski-Radon transformation, e.g., see
[
Bry86
]
. But since we use a more general form of this transformation (over a more general ring instead of a field) than the one appearing in
[
Bry86
]
, we reproduce here Brylinski’s arguments in this more general form. In a private communication, Jörg Schürmann informed us that, when
m=1
, our main result from Theorem
1.1
can also be proved by using stratified Morse theory. This fact opens the door for further exploration of the seemingly deep relation between the Brylinski-Radon transformation and stratified Morse theory.

2.
Brylinski-Radon transformation

In this section, we recall the definition of Brylinski-Radon transformation and its well-known properties.
Let
G(n+1,r+1)
be the Grassmannian parametrizing
r
-dimensional projective subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The universal family
\mathbf{V}
of
r
-dimensional projective subspaces is defined by

\mathbf{V}=\{(x,V)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)\mid x\in V\}.

Note that
\dim\mathbf{V}=n+r(n-r).

Let
{\mathbf{U}}={\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)\setminus\mathbf{V}
. Let
i_{\mathbf{V}}\colon\mathbf{V}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)
and
j_{\mathbf{U}}\colon\mathbf{U}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)
be the inclusion maps. Let
p_{1}\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
p_{2}\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)\to G(n+1,r+1)
be the two projection maps.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Brylinski-Radon transformation
is defined by

\mathrm{Rad}(-):D^{b}_{c}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},S)\to D^{b}_{c}\big(G(n+1,r+1),S\big),\quad\mathcal{F}\mapsto Rp_{2*}Ri_{\mathbf{V}*}i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)].

A well-known fact about the Brylinski-Radon transformation is its exactness with respect to the perverse
t
-structure, modulo (shifts of) constant sheaves. Here, instead of working over a field as in Brylinski’s original paper, we need to work over a more general ring.
For completeness, we provide here the full proof of this result, which serves both as a review of Brylinski’s arguments and as an explanation of how to adapt his arguments to our setting.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Bry86
, Théorème 5.5]

Modulo (shifts of) constant sheaves, the Brylinski-Radon transformation is exact with respect to the perverse
t
-structure. More precisely, for any perverse sheaf
\mathcal{P}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
,
the perverse cohomology sheaf
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))
is the shift of a constant sheaf on
G(n+1,r+1)
for any
k\neq 0
. Additionally, if
\mathcal{P}
is in the heart of the dual perverse
t
-structure
p^{+}
, then
{}^{p^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))
is also the shift of a constant sheaf on
G(n+1,r+1)
for any
k\neq 0
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We first work with the perverse
t
-structure
p
. The adjunction triangle

(1)

j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\to p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\to Ri_{\mathbf{V}*}i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{+1},

induces a distinguished triangle

(2)

Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)]\to Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)]\to\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{+1}.

Notice that the composition
p_{2}\circ j_{\mathbf{U}}\colon\mathbf{U}\to G(n+1,r+1)
is a fiber bundle with fiber
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
, where
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is realized as a
r
-dimensional projective subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Projecting from
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
defines another fiber bundle map
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{r}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n-r-1}
with fiber the affine space
\mathbb{C}^{r+1}
. This bundle structure on every fiber of
p_{2}\circ j_{\mathbf{U}}
implies that
p_{2}\circ j_{\mathbf{U}}
factors through two fiber bundle maps: one is an affine bundle map with fiber
\mathbb{C}^{r+1}
and the other is a projective bundle map with fiber
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-r-1}
. Since for an affine map
f
,
Rf_{!}
preserves half of the perverse
t
-structure (e.g., see
[
MS22
, Theorem 10.3.69]
) and for a proper map
f
of fiber dimension
n-r-1
,
Rf_{*}=Rf_{!}
also preserves half of the perverse
t
-structure up to degree
n-r-1
(e.g., see
[
MS22
, Corollary 10.3.30]
), we have

(3)

Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}=Rp_{2!}j_{\mathbf{U}!}=R(p_{2}\circ j_{\mathbf{U}})_{!}:\,^{p}D^{\geq\bullet}_{c}(\mathbf{U},S)\to\,^{p}D^{\geq\bullet-(n-r-1)}_{c}(G(n+1,r+1),S).

Now, given a perverse sheaf
\mathcal{P}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, since
G(n+1,r+1)
has dimension
(r+1)(n-r)
,
p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{P}[(r+1)(n-r)]
is a perverse sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,r+1)
, e.g., see
[
MS22
, Corollary 10.2.25]
. Since
j_{\mathbf{U}}
is an open embedding,
j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}
is exact with respect to the perverse
t
-structure. Therefore,

j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[(r+1)(n-r)]\in\,^{p}D^{\geq 0}_{c}(\mathbf{U},S).

Thus, by (
3
) we have

Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[(r+1)(n-r)]\in\,^{p}D^{\geq-(n-r-1)}_{c}(G(n+1,r+1),S),

or equivalently

Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\in\,^{p}D^{\geq r(n-r)+1}_{c}(G(n+1,r+1),S).

Taking the perverse cohomology long exact sequence of (
2
), we have

\cdots\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+r(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+r(n-r)+1}(Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))\to\cdots

By the above arguments, we know that
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+r(n-r)+1}(Rp_{2*}j_{\mathbf{U}!}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))=0
for
k<0
. Hence,
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))\cong\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+r(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))
for
k<0
. Since all cohomology sheaves of
Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})
are shifts of local systems, it follows that
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+r(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))\cong\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))
is also the shift of a local system for any
k<0
. This is due to the fact that the standard
t
-structure and the perverse
t
-structure differ in this case just by a shift. Moreover, since
G(n+1,r+1)
is simply-connected, all these local systems are constant sheaves.

To show that
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))
are shifts of local systems for
k>0
, we can dualize the above arguments. The analogous distinguished triangle of (
1
) is

(4)

i_{\mathbf{V}!}i_{\mathbf{V}}^{!}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\to p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\to Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\xrightarrow{+1}.

Since
i_{\mathbf{V}}
is a closed embedding, we have
i_{\mathbf{V}!}=Ri_{\mathbf{V}*}
. Since
p_{1}
and
p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}}
are submersions of relative dimensions
(r+1)(n-r)
and
r(n-r)
, respectively, we have
p_{1}^{!}=p_{1}^{*}[2(r+1)(n-r)]
and (e.g., see
[
MS22
, Corollary 10.2.25]
)

i_{\mathbf{V}}^{!}\,p_{1}^{!}=(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{!}=(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}[2r(n-r)]=i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}[2r(n-r)].

Thus,

i_{\mathbf{V}}^{!}p_{1}^{*}=i_{\mathbf{V}}^{!}p_{1}^{!}[-2(r+1)(n-r)]=i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}[-2(n-r)].

So the distinguished triangle (
4
) can also be written as

Ri_{\mathbf{V}*}i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[-2(n-r)]\to p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\to Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\xrightarrow{+1}.

By applying
Rp_{2*}
, we get the following distinguished triangle analogous to (
2
),

\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P})\to Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[(r+2)(n-r)]\to Rp_{2*}Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[(r+2)(n-r)]\xrightarrow{+1},

and the perverse sheaf cohomology long exact sequence is of the form

\cdots\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+(r+2)(n-r)-1}(Rp_{2*}Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))\to\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+(r+2)(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))\to\cdots

We can use similar arguments as before to show that

j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})[(r+1)(n-r)]\in\,^{p}D^{\leq 0}_{c}(\mathbf{U},S)

and

Rp_{2*}Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}=R(p_{2}\circ j_{\mathbf{U}})_{*}:\,^{p}D^{\leq\bullet}_{c}(\mathbf{U},S)\to\,^{p}D^{\leq\bullet+n-r-1}_{c}(G(n+1,r+1),S).

Thus,

Rp_{2*}Rj_{\mathbf{U}*}j_{\mathbf{U}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\in\,^{p}D^{\leq(r+2)(n-r)-1}_{c}(\mathbf{U},S),

and together with the above long exact sequence, we have

\,{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))\cong\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+(r+2)(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))

for any
k>0
. Since, as before,
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k+(r+2)(n-r)}(Rp_{2*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{P}))
are shifts of local systems (hence of constant sheaves), we finished the proof of Theorem
2.2
for perverse sheaves.

To prove the assertion about the dual perverse
t
-structure
p^{+}
, we need to show that the transformation
\mathrm{Rad}
is self-dual, i.e.,

\mathrm{Rad}=\mathbb{D}_{G(n+1,r+1)}\circ\mathrm{Rad}\circ\mathbb{D}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}},

where
\mathbb{D}_{G(n+1,r+1)}
and
\mathbb{D}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}
denote the Verdier dual functors on
G(n+1,r+1)
and
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, respectively. In fact, we recall that

\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{F})=Rp_{2*}Ri_{\mathbf{V}*}i_{\mathbf{V}}^{*}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)]=R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{*}(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)].

Since both
p_{2}
and
i_{\mathbf{V}}
are proper maps, so is
p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}}
, and hence
R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{*}
is self-dual. On the other hand, since
p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}}:\mathbf{V}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is a submersion of relative dimension
r(n-r)
, we have (e.g., see
[
MS22
, Corollary 10.2.25]
)

\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{V}}\circ(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}=(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{!}=(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}[2r(n-r)].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\circ\mathrm{Rad}\circ\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}\big(R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{*}(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})[r(n-r)]\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{*}(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{F})[-r(n-r)]

\displaystyle=R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{!}(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{!}(\mathcal{F})[-r(n-r)]

\displaystyle=R(p_{2}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})_{*}(p_{1}\circ i_{\mathbf{V}})^{*}(\mathcal{F})[2r(n-r)-r(n-r)]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{F}).

Now, suppose
\mathcal{P}
is in the heart of the dual perverse
t
-structure
p^{+}
. Then
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P})
is a perverse sheaf. By the first part of the theorem, we know that

{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P})))
is the shift of a constant sheaf for
k\neq 0
. Since
\mathrm{Rad}=\mathbb{D}\circ\mathrm{Rad}\circ\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{D}\circ\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}\circ\mathbb{D}=\,^{p^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{-k}
, we have

{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P})))=\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{D}\,\mathrm{Rad}\,\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{P})))=\,^{p}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbb{D}\,\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))=\mathbb{D}(\,^{p^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{-k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P})))

is the shift of a constant sheaf for
k\neq 0
. Since Verdier duality preserves constant sheaves up to a shift, it follows that
{}^{p^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{-k}(\mathrm{Rad}(\mathcal{P}))
is the shift of a constant sheaf for
k\neq 0
. This proves the second part of the theorem.
∎

3.
Some transversality results

Given a linear space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we consider its standard projective compactification
\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
Fix a linear subspace
N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\dim N=n-m
. Every element
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}/N
corresponds to an affine subspace
\tilde{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The closure of
\tilde{\alpha}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is a projective subspace of dimension
n-m
. Thus,
\mathbb{C}^{n}/N
parametrizes a family of
(n-m)
-dimensional projective subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. By the universality of the Grassmannian, we have an injective map
\iota_{N}:\mathbb{C}^{n}/N\to G(n+1,n-m+1)
. Denote the image of
\iota_{N}
by
I_{N}
.

Let
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
be the general affine group of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The natural action of
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
extends to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. In fact,
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
can be identified with the subgroup of
\mathrm{PGL}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
fixing the hyperplane at infinity. Thus, the natural
\mathrm{PGL}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
-action on
G(n+1,n-m+1)
restricts to a natural
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
-action on
G(n+1,n-m+1)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The above
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
-action on
G(n+1,n-m+1)
has two orbits. The open orbit is equal to

\bigcup_{N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}}I_{N}
,
where the union is over all
(n-m)
-dimensional linear subspaces
N
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

Denote by
A
the open subset of
G(n+1,n-m+1)
corresponding to
(n-m)
-dimensional projective subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
not contained in the hyperplane at infinity
H_{\infty}
. Denote by
B
the closed subset of
G(n+1,n-m+1)
corresponding to
(n-m)
-dimensional projective subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
contained in
H_{\infty}
. Clearly, the
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
-action preserves the subsets
A
and
B
. Moreover,
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
acts transitively on both
A
and
B
.

Every element in
A
is the closure of some
(n-m)
-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and hence is in the image
I_{N}
for some
N
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f\colon Z\to G(n+1,n-m+1)
be an algebraic map from a smooth algebraic variety
Z
. Then for a general
(n-m)
-dimensional linear subspace
N
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
f^{-1}(I_{N})
is smooth and
\mathrm{codim}_{G(n+1,n-m+1)}I_{N}=\mathrm{codim}_{Z}f^{-1}(I_{N})
.

Proof.

Let
Z_{A}=f^{-1}(A)
, where
A
is the open
\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
-orbit in
G(n+1,n-m+1)
. Since
I_{N}\subset A
, without loss of generality we can replace
G(n+1,n-m+1)
by
A
and replace
Z
by
Z_{A}
. Notice that for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{GA}(n,\mathbb{C})
,
\sigma\cdot I_{N}=I_{\sigma(N)}
. We need to show that fixing a
(n-m)
-dimensional subspace
N_{0}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
f^{-1}(\sigma(I_{N_{0}}))
is smooth and of expected dimension. This follows from Kleiman’s transversality theorem (
[
Kle74
]
[Theorem 2]).
∎

In the special case when
f
is the identity map, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

The image
I_{N}
is a locally closed smooth subvariety of
G(n+1,n-m+1)
.

Remark 3.4
.

The above corollary can also be derived from a stronger statement that under an appropriate choice of coordinates,
I_{N}
is equal to a Schubert cell of
G(n+1,n-m+1)
. In fact, if we consider
G(n+1,n-m+1)=\mathrm{GL}(n+1,\mathbb{C})/P
with the parabolic subgroup
P
, then
I_{N}
is the orbit of the left action of a Borel subgroup of
G(n+1,n-m+1)
contained in
P
. This implies that
I_{N}
is a Schubert cell.

4.
Generic projection

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a constructible complex on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Fix
m\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, and let
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a general surjective linear map. We will first express
RF_{*}(\mathcal{F})
in terms of the Brylinski-Radon transformation and, as an application, complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
j_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the inclusion map. Recall that any
(n-m)
-dimensional linear subspace
N
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defines a map
\iota_{N}:\mathbb{C}^{n}/N\to G(n+1,n-m+1)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the above notations, if
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}/N=\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the natural generic projection, then we have an isomorphism

(5)

RF_{*}(\mathcal{F})[m(n-m)]\cong\iota_{N}^{*}\,\mathrm{Rad}(Rj_{\mathbb{C}^{n}*}(\mathcal{F})).

Proof.

We use the notations of Section
2
with
r=n-m
. Recall that
\mathbf{V}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,n-m+1)
is the universal family of projective subspaces, and
\mathbf{U}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times G(n+1,n-m+1)
is its complement. Denote the two projections by
q_{1}:\mathbf{V}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
q_{2}:\mathbf{V}\to G(n+1,n-m+1)
.
In other words, these projections are defined so that we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{V}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{1}}
\scriptstyle{q_{2}}
\scriptstyle{i_{\mathbf{V}}}

Methods on compositeness and related aspects
\firstname José Antonio \lastname Oller \fnsep

Abstract
In many physical applications, bound states and/or resonances are observed, which raises the question whether these states are elementary or composite. Here we elaborate on several methods for calculating the compositeness X X of bound states and resonances in Quantum Mechanics, and in Quantum Field Theory by introducing particle number operators. For resonances X X is typically complex and we discuss how to get meaningful results by using certain phase transformations in the S S matrix.



1
Introduction

We start by reviewing a few basic aspects of the problem of compositeness in hadron physics
Weinberg:1962hj
;
Weinberg:1963zza
;
Weinberg:1965zz
. The Hamiltonian
H
is first split in the free
H_{0}
and interaction
V
parts,

\displaystyle H=H_{0}+V~.

(1)

Both
H
and
H_{0}
share the same spectrum. For
H_{0}
, one has the bare eigenstates,

\displaystyle H_{0}|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha}|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle~,~\text{ continuum spectrum}~,

\displaystyle H_{0}|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{B_{n}}|\phi_{n}\rangle~,~\text{ discrete spectrum}~,

(2)

where
|\phi_{n}\rangle
is a bare “elementary” state, and
|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle
is made up by the direct product of free particles
(Greek letters are used as subscripts to refer to quantum numbers, among which one has the momentum, spin, etc). The physical spectrum is comprised by the eigenstates of
H
,

\displaystyle H|\psi^{\pm}_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha}|\psi^{\pm}_{\alpha}\rangle~,~\text{ continuum spectrum}~,

\displaystyle H|\psi_{B_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{B_{n}}|\psi_{B_{n}}\rangle~,~\text{ discrete spectrum}~.

(3)

Here, the
|\psi_{B_{n}}\rangle
are the bound states of the theory and the
|\psi^{\pm}_{\alpha}\rangle
are the scattering in/out states, respectively. Within this framework the definitions of compositeness
X
and elementariness
Z
of a bound state
|\psi_{B}\rangle
are as follows
Weinberg:1962hj
.
Let us consider the linear decomposition of this state in the basis of eigenstates of
H_{0}
,

\displaystyle|\psi_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\langle\phi_{n}|\psi_{B}\rangle|\phi_{n}\rangle+\int d\alpha\langle\varphi_{\alpha}|\psi_{B}\rangle|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle~.

(4)

Then, the Parseval identity implies

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{B}|\psi_{B}\rangle=1

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{n}|\langle\phi_{n}|\psi_{B}\rangle|^{2}}_{\equiv Z}+\underbrace{\int d\alpha|\langle\varphi_{\alpha}|\psi_{B}\rangle|^{2}}_{\equiv X}~,

(5)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=Z+X~.

A new interpretation based on the use of the number operators in the interaction or Dirac picture was introduced in Ref.
Oller:2017alp
, where more details can be found. The basic idea is
to take two free particles of types
A
and
B
(
H_{0}|AB_{\gamma}\rangle=E_{\gamma}|AB_{\gamma}\rangle
). The standard creation and annihilation operators of the two free particles are

a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}
,
b^{\dagger}_{\beta}
,
b_{\beta}
, respectively.
In terms of them, the number operator
N_{D}
for the total number of these free particles is

\displaystyle N_{D}=\int d\alpha a_{\alpha}^{\dagger}a_{\alpha}+\int d\beta b_{\beta}^{\dagger}b_{\beta}=N_{D}^{A}+N_{D}^{B}~,

(6)

with
N_{D}^{A}
and
N_{D}^{B}
the number operators for each species of particles separately. Here, the subscript
D
refers to the Dirac picture used.
In nonrelativistic Quantum Field Theory (QFT) one can express these operators using the fields
\psi_{A}
and
\psi_{B}
as
Thirring:book1

\displaystyle N_{D}=\int d^{3}x\left[\psi_{A}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{A}(x)+\psi_{B}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{B}(x)\right]~,~x=(t,{\mathbf{x}})~,

(7)

Let us notice that in the interaction picture
[H_{0},N_{D}]=0
, so that
N_{D}
is actually time independent,
N_{D}(t)=N_{D}(0)
. In terms of
N_{D}
one can give an alternative definition for the compositeness
X
as
Oller:2017alp

\displaystyle X=\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi_{B}|N_{D}|\psi_{B}\rangle~.

(8)

The factor 1/2 is introduced because we are considering that only two-particle states made out of
A
and
B
may contribute to
|\psi_{B}\rangle
. Let us now probe the equivalence between the new definition of
X
and the previous one in Eq. (
5
). From the linear decompositions of the bound-state
|\psi_{B}\rangle
in states
|AB_{\gamma}\rangle
and
|\phi_{n}\rangle
(eigenstates of
H_{0}
) we have

\displaystyle|\psi_{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int d\gamma C_{\gamma}|AB_{\gamma}\rangle+\sum_{n}C_{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle~,

(9)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi_{B}|N_{D}^{A}+N_{D}^{B}|\psi_{B}\rangle=\int d\gamma|C_{\gamma}|^{2}~.

This definition is specially suitable for Effective Field Theories (EFTs), like e.g. ChPT and hadron physics in general. The point is that in a low-energy EFT written in terms of the (pseudo-)Goldstone bosons as the only degrees of freedom, it is valuable to define
X
as in Eq. (
8
), since it does not require to have explicit bare “elementary” states (fields) in the theoretical set up.

This new definition is also the most adequate for the treatment in QFT since, as deduced in
Oller:2017alp
, it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle X=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int d^{4}x\langle\varphi_{B}|P\left[e^{-i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}V_{D}(t^{\prime})}\sum_{i}\psi_{A_{i}}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{A_{i}}(x)\right]|\varphi_{B}\rangle~,

(10)

by expressing the number operators in terms of the nonrelativistic fields
\psi_{A_{i}}
. Compared with Eq. (
9
) here
A\equiv A_{1}
and
B\equiv A_{2}
.
Making use of the LSZ formalism one can directly express
X
in terms of
S
-matrix elements,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\!=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{E\to E_{B}}\frac{(E-E_{B})^{2}}{g_{\alpha}(k_{B})^{2}}\lim_{T\to+\infty}\!\frac{1}{T}\int\!d^{4}x\langle\varphi_{\alpha}|P\left[e^{-i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}V_{D}(t^{\prime})}\sum_{i}\psi_{A_{i}}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{A_{i}}(x)\right]|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle~,

(11)

with
g_{\alpha}(k_{B})
the coupling of the bound state to the continuum in/out states. Of course, the previous equation clearly shows that
X
is an observable in nonrelativistic QFT since it is expressed in terms of
S
-matrix elements in the presence of a local source term, made up by the number-operator density.

Explicit formulas

\sidecaption

for a bound state represented by the double lines. The two filled dots correspond to insertions of

The Feynman diagrams appropriate for the calculation of Eq. (
10
) are depicted in Fig.
1
, where the insertion of the number-operator density in the propagator of every particle is represented by the double dots. The resulting expression for
X
in terms of the off-shell coupling to
A
and
B
is

\displaystyle X_{\ell S}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g_{\ell S}^{2}(k)}{(k^{2}/2\mu-E_{B})^{2}}~,

(12)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell S}X_{\ell S}~,

with
\mu
the reduced mass. The subscripts
\ell
and
S
in Eq. (
12
) refer to particular two-particle partial wave amplitude (PWAs) coupled to the bound state (e.g.
{}^{3}S_{1}
and
{}^{3}D_{1}
for the deuteron in nucleon-nucleon (
NN
) scattering).
If in the integral of Eq. (
12
) we assume a shallow bound state then the coupling
g_{\ell S}(k)
can be factorized out with
k=0
, or with its on-shell value (which is valid at this level of accuracy). Thus,
X_{\ell S}
becomes

\displaystyle X_{\ell S}

\displaystyle=g_{\ell S}^{2}\frac{d}{dE}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{k^{2}/2\mu-E_{B}}=g_{\ell S}^{2}\frac{i\mu^{2}}{2\pi k_{B}}~,

(13)

with the binding momentum
k_{B}=\sqrt{2\mu E_{B}}
. This is one of the results of Ref.
Weinberg:1965zz
. The equation for
g_{\ell S}(k)
is obtained from the Lippmann-Schwinger equation
T=V+VGT
in the limit
E\to E_{B}
and by taking the residue at the pole, so that

\displaystyle g_{\ell S}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{k^{\prime}}^{2}dk^{\prime}V_{\ell S}(k,k^{\prime})\frac{1}{{k^{\prime}}^{2}/2\mu-E_{B}}g_{\ell S}({k^{\prime}})~.

(14)

For the case of energy-independent potentials, which corresponds to
pure potential scattering
, it was shown in Ref.
Oller:2017alp
that the compositeness is exactly equal to 1 for any bound state, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}X_{\alpha}=1~,

(15)

with
n
the number of coupled PWAs.
We refer to
Oller:2017alp
for the interesting detailed analysis, which is skipped here to avoid becoming too technical.
This result has interesting implications like e.g. in the case of the deuteron, studied within ChPT up to N
4
LO in terms of energy-independent potentials with an estimated error in the reproduction of its properties of around a 4%
RodriguezEntem:2020jgp
;
Epelbaum:2014sza
. Therefore, one would conclude from Eq. (
15
) that for this bound state
X=0.96-1.0
.

We now discuss within similar terms the case of resonances following the basic lines of scattering theory and the use of number operators
Oller:2017alp
. A resonance stems from the analytic continuation in energy of in states with energy
E+i\varepsilon
, and out states with energy
E-i\varepsilon
(
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
). In this way, when evaluating an
S
-matrix element
\langle\psi_{\beta}^{-}|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle
both ket and bra involve an energy of
E+i\varepsilon
. In performing the analytical continuation towards the pole position of the resonance in the complex
E
-plane one has to cross the real axis along
E>0
, borrowing in the second Riemann sheet (RS) where the resonance pole lies with
\Im E<0
.
For the calculation of
X
for a resonance in QFT we can then proceed similarly as for the case of a bound state by isolating the double pole residue of
S
-matrix elements with the external source
\sum_{i}\psi_{A_{i}}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{A_{i}}(x)
. The corresponding formula is

\displaystyle X=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{E\to E_{R}}\frac{(E-E_{R})^{2}}{g_{\alpha}(k_{R})^{2}}\lim_{T\to+\infty}\!\frac{1}{T}\int\!d^{4}x\langle\varphi_{\alpha}|P\left[e^{-i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dt^{\prime}V_{D}(t^{\prime})}\sum_{i}\psi_{A_{i}}^{\dagger}(x)\psi_{A_{i}}(x)\right]|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle~,

(16)

with
E_{R}
and
k_{R}
the resonance pole position in energy and momentum, respectively. Of course, one can also write down a formula similar to Eq. (
12
) for a bound state, corresponding to analogous Feynman diagrams as those in Fig.
1
with the replacement
|\psi_{B}\rangle\to|\psi_{R}\rangle
. The result is

\displaystyle X_{\ell S}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{g_{\ell S}^{2}(k)}{(k^{2}/2\mu-E_{n}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{i\mu^{2}}{\pi k_{n}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial k}k\,g_{\ell S}^{2}(k)\right]_{k=k_{n}}~,

(17)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell S}X_{\ell S}~.

The extrapolation of the integral to the second RS is responsible for the additional last term as compared with Eq. (
12
).

In pure potential scattering it was proved in Ref.
Oller:2017alp
that
X=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}X_{\alpha}=1
, see also Ref.
Hernandez:1984zzb
. For instance, this would imply that
X
for the virtual or antibound state in the
{}^{1}S_{0}

NN
scattering should be very close to 1, since
NN
scattering data can be described very precisely with energy-independent potentials derived from ChPT
RodriguezEntem:2020jgp
;
Epelbaum:2014sza
. If for the deuteron, with a binding momentum of around 45 MeV, the possible uncertainty was bounded to a 4%, for the virtual state in the
{}^{1}S_{0}
PWA one would expect it to be smaller because
|k_{R}|\approx 10
MeV
\ll 45
MeV.

3
Compositeness in the Heisenberg picture

Let us denote by
N_{H}(t)
the number operator in the Heisenberg picture. The scattering states
|\psi^{\pm}\rangle
are eigenstates of
H
that behave as free states when acted with operators at
\tau\mp\infty
, respectively
Weinberg:book1
. Then,

\displaystyle\exp(-iH\tau)N_{H}(\tau)|\psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rangle\to\exp(-iH_{0}\tau)N_{D}(\tau)|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle~,

(18)

for
\tau\to\mp\infty
. Taking into account in the previous equation that
|\psi^{\pm}\rangle=U_{D}(0,\tau)|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle=\exp(iH\tau)\exp(-iH_{0}\tau)|\varphi_{\alpha}\rangle
, with
\tau\to\mp\infty
, we have the equality at the operational level

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\pm}\exp(-iH\tau)N_{H}(\tau)\exp(iH\tau)=N_{D}(\tau)~.

(19)

Since
H|\psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rangle=E_{\alpha}|\psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rangle
we also have that

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|N_{D}(\tau)|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle=\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|N_{H}(\tau)|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle~,~\tau\to\pm\infty.

(20)

Furthermore, as
dN_{H}(t)/dt=i[H,N_{H}(t)]
it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|N_{H}(t)|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle=0~,

(21)

so that both expectation values in Eq. (
20
) are time independent, and we can simply write that

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|N_{D}(t)|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle=\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|N_{H}(t^{\prime})|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle~,

(22)

for arbitrary
t
and
t^{\prime}
.
The equality between the expectation values of
N_{D}
and
N_{H}
is also clear by taking into account the QFT expressions above for
X
in Eqs. (
11
) and (
16
), since they correspond to the
S
-matrix elements
\displaystyle{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{-}|\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt\,N_{H}(t)|\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\rangle}
when calculated in the interaction picture. Afterwards, the residue of the double pole is isolated.

Now, the idea would be to use an appropriate operator normalized in lattice QCD to mimic the number operators. For instance, if the operator in the Heisenberg picture is
O(t)
and we consider a bound state made out of nucleons, like the deuteron, we could take the normalization constant to be
\langle N|O(t)|N\rangle
, which indeed is time independent (to conclude this one can follow an analogous reasoning as used for Eq. (
21
)).
Of course, extra discussions about the final adequate choice for the operator
O(t)
in lattice QCD are still required, and this should be the object of further work.

4
Sum rule

From a generic formula for two-particle PWAs one can deduce a sum rule for compositeness and elementariness. We follow here Ref.
Guo:2015daa
, which employs relativistic kinematics.
Two-body unitarity along the right-hand cut, and above the thresholds of channels
i
and
j
, can be expressed as
Oller:2019rej

\displaystyle\Im\left.T^{-1}\right|_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\rho_{i}~,

(23)

where
\rho_{i}
is the phase-space factor
\rho_{i}=p_{i}/8\pi\sqrt{s}
, with
p_{i}
the center of mass (CM) momentum for the channel
i
and
s
the CM total energy squared (the usual Mandelstam variable). From this equation one can resum the right-hand or unitarity cut and obtain a general expression for a PWA in coupled channels. In matrix notation it reads
Oller:2019opk
;
Oller:2019rej

\displaystyle T(s)=\left[{\cal K}(s)^{-1}+G(s)\right]^{-1}~,

(24)

where
{\cal K}
is an interacting kernel and the unitary loop function
G_{i}(s)
is given by

\displaystyle G_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left[a_{i}(\Lambda)+\log\frac{m_{i1}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}-x_{+}\log\frac{x_{+}-1}{x_{+}}-x_{-}\log\frac{x_{-}-1}{x_{-}}\right]~,

(25)

\displaystyle x_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{s+m_{i2}^{2}-m_{i1}^{2}}{2s}\pm\frac{1}{2s}\sqrt{(s+m_{i2}^{2}-m_{i1}^{2})^{2}-4s(m_{i2}^{2}-i0^{+})}~,

where
m_{1i}
and
m_{2i}
are the masses of the two particles in the channel
i
, and
a_{i}(\Lambda)
is a subtraction constant (the combination
a_{i}(\Lambda)-\log\Lambda^{2}
is
\Lambda
independent).
Taking the derivative of Eq. (
24
) with respect to
s
, in the limit
s\to s_{R}
the residue of the double pole implies that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\sum_{i}g_{i}^{2}\left.\frac{dG_{i}(s)}{ds}\right|_{s_{B}}}_{X_{i}}+\underbrace{g^{T}G(s_{B})\left.\frac{dK(s)}{ds}\right|_{s_{B}}G(s_{B})g}_{Z}~.

(26)

The expression for
X_{i}
does not depend on
a_{i}
or
\Lambda
, since they disappear in the derivative of
G_{i}(s)
. It is the same expression as in Eq. (
13
) for shallow bound states, and also for separable potentials
Sekihara:2014kya
;
Aceti:2012dd
.

5
Resonances

For the case of resonances one should consider the unitarity loop function in the 2nd RS for those channels in which the transit to this sheet is involved to find the pole position at
s_{R}
. We indicate this by
G^{II}(s_{R})
. Then, Eq. (
26
) implies

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle=-g_{i}^{2}\left.\frac{dG_{i}^{II}(s)}{ds}\right|_{s_{R}}~.

(27)

However, for resonances
X_{i}
is typically complex.
We argue next that one should actually take its absolute value
|X_{i}|
.
This based on certain phase transformations of the
S
-matrix in PWAs, driving to a phase redefinition of the couplings
Guo:2015daa
.
Let us first consider a narrow resonance
\Gamma\ll M_{R}-m_{\rm th}
, with
\Gamma
,
M_{R}
the resonance mass and width, and
m_{\rm th}
the nearest threshold. Later on we generalize these results for wider resonances. The
S
matrix is split in a pole term plus
S_{0}(s)
, which is assumed to be smooth in a certain domain around the resonance pole. That is,

\displaystyle S(s)=\frac{R}{s-s_{R}}+S_{0}(s)~,

(28)

with
R
the residue matrix of the resonance pole at
s_{R}
.
Then, we impose unitarity of the
S
matrix in PWAs,

\displaystyle S(s)S(s)^{\dagger}=I~.

(29)

The solution of this equation with
S(s)
given by Eq. (
28
), with constant
S_{0}
for
s
near
s_{R}
, is

\displaystyle S(s)

\displaystyle={\cal O}\underbrace{\left(I+\frac{i\lambda{\cal A}}{s-s_{R}}\right)}_{S_{R}(s)}{\cal O}^{T}~,

(30)

\displaystyle{\cal O}{\cal O}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=I~.

Here
{\cal A}
is a rank one symmetric projection operator and

S_{R}(s)
is the pure resonant
S
matrix. The disposition of operators in Eq. (
30
) clearly indicates the corrections to
S_{R}
due to initial- and final-state interactions from
S_{0}
.

Isoscalar scalar
\pi\pi
phase shifts,
J^{PC}=0^{++}
. For the experimental references see
Oller:1997ti
.

The dressing to
S_{R}
by
S_{0}
typically modifies the phases of the resonance couplings in
S_{R}
. This is because the moduli of the couplings
g_{i}
to the different channels have physical meaning, since they provide the partial decay widths of a narrow resonance
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
,
\Gamma_{i}={|g_{i}|^{2}}/({8\pi M_{R}^{2}})
. Then, the
S
-matrix phase transformation only change the phases of the resonance couplings

\displaystyle S_{\cal O}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv{\cal O}S(s){\cal O}^{T}~,

(31)

\displaystyle{\cal O}

\displaystyle={\rm diag}(e^{i\phi_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\phi_{n}})~,

\displaystyle g_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\to g_{i}^{2}e^{2i\phi_{i}}~.

As an example of this mechanism let us consider
\pi\pi-K\bar{K}
scattering in
J=0
and
I=0
(
I
is the total isospin). As the author checked in the calculations for Ref.
Oller:1997ti
, the coupling to
\pi\pi
of the
f_{0}(980)
is a positive number times a phase-factor
i
, which stems from the phase-shifts of
\pi\pi
rescattering due to the
f_{0}(500)
resonance at the rise of the
f_{0}(980)
. This is clearly seen from Fig.
2
.

Therefore,
X_{i}
, Eq. (
27
), only changes its phase under the phase transformations of Eq. (
31
), and they can be chosen so that it becomes a real positive number. Hence,

\displaystyle X_{i}\to

\displaystyle|X_{i}|\geq 0~.

(32)

Let us now generalize Eq. (
32
) for non-narrow resonances. The point is to require the validity of the Laurent series expansion around the resonance pole up to physical values of
s
above threshold, so that the resonance couplings still directly impact physical energies. This implies that
s_{{\rm th};n}<\Re s_{R}<s_{{\rm th};n+1}
, where
s_{{\rm th};n}
is the threshold of channel
n
.

6
Effective range expansion

We assume now that a bound or resonance pole lies close enough to a relevant threshold so that the well-known effective range expansion (ERE) can be applied. We follow here the developments in Ref.
Kang:2016ezb
. Up to and including the scattering length
a
and the effective range
r
the
S
-wave PWA can be written as

\displaystyle T(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{2}rk^{2}+G(k)}~,~~~G(k)=-ik~.

(33)

Then, if
T(k)
has a resonance pole at
E=E_{R}=M_{R}-i\Gamma/2
(energy is measured from
m_{\rm th}
),
a
and
r
can be given in terms of the mass and width of the resonance as

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\frac{2k_{i}}{|k_{R}|^{2}}~~,~~k_{R}=\sqrt{2\mu E_{R}}=k_{r}-i\,k_{i}~,

(34)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k_{i}}~~,~~\text{so that }\frac{r}{a}>2~.

By applying Eq. (
27
) we can express the compositeness as

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=-\gamma^{2}\frac{dG}{ds}=-\gamma_{k}^{2}\frac{dG}{dk}=i\frac{k_{i}}{k_{r}}=i\tan\frac{\phi}{2}~,

(35)

with
\gamma_{k}^{2}
the residue at the pole position in the complex
k
-plane.
The simplicity of the ERE up to including
r
perfectly illustrates the condition on the mass of the resonance, discussed at the end of Sec.
5
, because

\displaystyle|X|\leq 1\leftrightarrow\phi\in[0,\pi/2]\leftrightarrow M_{R}\geq 0~,

(36)

with
|X|=1
for
M_{R}=0
and
\Gamma>0
.
Furthermore, since
X
is purely imaginary in Eq. (
35
) if its real part is taken to end with a real compositeness, as advocated in Ref.
Aceti:2014ala
, then from the ERE
X=0
. Of course, this result is not meaningful in such generality. The expression for
|X|
can also be rewritten as
|X|=\left(\frac{2r}{a}-1\right)^{-1}
. In the subsequent we drop the sign of modulus on
X
and directly consider by this symbol the compositeness calculated within the ERE.

7
CDD poles. Track of elementariness

While the ERE is a smooth expansion around
k=0
of the combination
t(E)^{-1}+ik
, which has no right-hand cut, more structure can be accounted for by allowing poles in
t(E)^{-1}
. These are the so-called CDD poles
Castillejo:1955ed
.
In this way, a once-subtracted dispersion relation (DR) for
t(E)^{-1}
accounting for a pole in
t(E)^{-1}
at
M_{Z}
gives

\displaystyle t(E)=\frac{1}{\frac{\lambda}{E-M_{Z}}+\beta-ik}~,

(37)

with
\lambda
the residue at the CDD pole and
\beta
a subtraction constant.
A consequence of this pole is that the ERE or a Flatté parameterization break down for
|k|\geqslant\sqrt{2\mu|M_{Z}|}
.
A near-threshold CDD pole contributes to
a
and
r
as

\displaystyle\delta a

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{Z}}{\lambda}~,~~~\delta r=-\frac{\lambda}{mM_{Z}^{2}}~.

(38)

Therefore,
\delta a\to 0
and
\delta r\to-\infty
(unless
\lambda=0
) for
M_{Z}\to 0
. As a result,
a large negative value of
r
is a hint for a nearby CDD pole and for elementariness. Indeed, if we calculate
X
from the ERE formula
X=\left(\frac{2r}{a}-1\right)^{-1}\to 0
for
M_{Z}\to 0
.
Nonetheless, one should keep in mind that having a near CDD pole to a resonance pole is sufficient but not necessary for the resonance to be elementary. For instance, as analyzed in Ref.
Oller:1998zr
, the CDD pole associated to the
\rho(770)
resonance is located at
s=M_{\rho}/(1-g_{V}^{2})
and tends to infinity because of the KSFR relation, which requires
g_{V}^{2}=1
. This is so despite the resonance
\rho(770)

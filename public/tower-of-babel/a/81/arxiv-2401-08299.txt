Towards a Complete Local-Global Principle
Nikola Kuzmanovski

Abstract
Ahlswede and Cai proved that if a simple graph has nested solutions (NS) under the edge-isoperimetric problems, and the lexicographic (lex) order produces NS for its second cartesian power, then the lex order produces NS for any finite cartesian power. Under very general assumptions, we prove that if a graph and its second cartesian power have NS, then so does any finite cartesian power. Harper asked if this is true without any restriction. We also conjecture that it is. All graphs studied in the literature for which the lex order is optimal are regular. This lead Bezrukov and Elsässer to conjecture that if the lex order is optimal for the second cartesian power, then the original graph is regular. A counterexample to this conjecture is provided.

2
Uniqueness of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
in
G^{2}

Suppose that
G
and
H
admit optimal orders.
For
A\subseteq V_{H}\times V_{G}
denote
A_{i}(a)=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in A\bigm|x_{i}=a\}
.
We say that
A
is
compressed
if

A_{i}(a)=\{0,1,...,|A_{i}(a)|-1\}
for
i=1,2
and any
a
.
A proof of the following folklore result can be found in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
A_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{n}\subseteq V_{H\square G}
,
then there exist compressed sets
A_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{n}^{\prime}
such that
|A_{i}|=|A_{i}^{\prime}|
and
|I_{H\square G}(A_{i})|\leq|I_{H\square G}(A_{i}^{\prime})|
,
for all
i\in[n]
.

Thus, if
H\square G
has NS, then
H\square G
has NS
A_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{|V|}
such that each
A_{i}
is compressed.
These NS give an order on
H\square G

such that each initial segment is a compressed set.
We will call such orders
compressed optimal orders
.
Note that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
are compressed optimal orders.
A very useful insight for the proof of Theorem
2.3
is the following result.

Lemma 2.2

(Bezrukov
[
5
]
,1999)
.

If
A\subseteq V_{H\square G}
is compressed then

\displaystyle|I(A)|=\sum_{(x,y)\in A}(\delta_{H}(x)+\delta_{G}(y)).

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
G
and
G^{2}
have NS,
and let
\mathcal{O}
be a compressed optimal order on
G^{2}
.
If
G
is
\delta
-dense then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{L}
or
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Take a chain of compressed optimal sets
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{|V_{H}||V_{G}|}
,
such that
|A_{i}|=i
.
We always have
A_{1}=\{(0,0)\}
, since
A_{1}
is compressed.
The claim is trivial for
|V_{G}|=1
, so suppose that
|V_{G}|>1
.
Also, because
A_{2}
is compressed we have
A_{2}=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}
or
A_{2}=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}
.
If
A_{2}=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}
then Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
give us
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{L}
.
If
A_{2}=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}
then symmetry and the same argument gives us
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{C}
.
We will now prove these lemmas.
The following inequality is useful for proving Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
k\in\{3,\dots,|V_{G}|\}
then
\delta(k)-\delta(2)>0
.

Proof.

Since
G
is
\delta
-dense we have
\delta_{G}(k)-\delta_{G}(2)\geq\min_{n\geq 2}s_{G,n}-\delta_{G}(2)\geq 2-1>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
A_{2}=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}
then
A_{|V_{G}|}=\{(0,0),(0,1),\dots,(0,|V_{G}|-1)\}
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that this is not the case.
Then there is some
k\geq 3
such that
A_{k}\neq\{(0,0),\dots,(0,k-1)\}
,
and for all
k^{\prime}<k
we have
A_{k^{\prime}}=\{(0,0),\dots,(0,k^{\prime}-1)\}
.
Thus,
A_{k}=\{(0,0),\dots,(0,k-2),(1,0)\}
,
since
A_{k}
is compressed and
A_{k-1}=\{(0,0),\dots,(0,k-2)\}
.
However now we have a contradiction with the optimality of
A_{k}
,

\displaystyle|I(\{(0,0),(0,1),\dots,(0,k-1)\})|-|I(A_{k})|=\delta_{G}(k)-\delta_{G}(2)>0,

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
A_{|V_{G}|}=\{(0,0),(0,1),\dots,(0,|V_{G}|-1)\}
then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Assume for the purposes of a contradiction that the claim does not hold.
Let
k
be minimal such that
A_{k}
is not an initial segment of the lex order.
Also, let
(x,y)
be the unique vertex in
A_{k-1}\setminus A_{k-2}
,
and
(w,z)
be the unique vertex in
A_{k}\setminus A_{k-1}
.
We must have

,
since otherwise

is an initial segment of lex and
A_{k}
is compressed.
This forces

.

Case 1:
If
y>0
then we get a contradiction with the optimality of
A_{k}
,

\displaystyle|I(A_{k-1}\cup\{(x,y+1)\})|-|I(A_{k})|

\displaystyle=\delta(x+1)+\delta(y+2)-\delta(x+2)-\delta(1),

\displaystyle=\delta(x+1)+\delta(y+2)-\delta(x+2),

\displaystyle\geq\delta(x+1)+\delta(y+2)-\delta(x+1)-1,

\displaystyle=\delta(y+2)-\delta(2),

\displaystyle>0.

Case 2:
Suppose
y=0
.
So, there exist
t\geq 1
such that for
s=k+t
we have

\displaystyle A_{s}=A_{k-1}\cup\{(x+1,0),(x+2,0),\dots,(x+t,0)\}\cup\{(x,1)\}.

Let

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\{(x,0),(x,1),\dots,(x,t)\},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\{(x,0),(x+1,0),\dots,(x+t,0)\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=A_{k-2}\cup C\cup\{(x,t+1)\}.

One has,

\displaystyle|I(B\setminus\{(x,t+1)\})|-|I(A_{s}\setminus\{(x,1)\})|

\displaystyle=|I(C)|-|I(R)|+|I(A_{k-2},C)|-|I(A_{k-2},R)|,

\displaystyle\geq|I(A_{k-2},C)|-|I(A_{k-2},R)|,

\displaystyle=(t+1)|I(A_{k-2},\{(x,0)\})|-\sum_{j=0}^{t}|I(A_{k-2},\{(x+j,0)\})|

\displaystyle\geq(t+1)|I(A_{k-2},\{(x,0)\})|-\sum_{j=0}^{t}|I(A_{k-2},\{(x,0)\})|

\displaystyle=0.

Thus, we a contradiction with the optimality of
A_{s}
, since from the above inequality we have

\displaystyle|I(B)|-|I(A_{s})|

\displaystyle=\delta(x+1)+\delta(t+2)-\delta(x+1)-\delta(2)\geq\delta(t+2)-\delta(2)>0,

Therefore, the claim holds, since in both cases we get a contradiction.
∎

As mentioned at that start of the proof we are done after the proof of lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
.
∎

We can now prove Theorem

.

Suppose that

There exists a compressed optimal order on

Towards a Complete Local-Global Principle

We will first provide a counterexample to Conjecture
1.5
.
Let

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=(\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\},\{\{x_{0},x_{1}\},\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\{x_{3},x_{4}\}\}),\mbox{ two disjoint paths of sizes $3$ and $2$},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=(\{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4},y_{5}\},\{\{y_{0},y_{1}\},\{y_{0},y_{2}\},\{y_{1},y_{2}\},\{y_{3},y_{4}\},\{y_{3},y_{5}\},\{y_{4},y_{5}\}\}),

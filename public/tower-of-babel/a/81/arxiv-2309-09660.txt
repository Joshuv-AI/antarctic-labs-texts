A family of stabilizer-free virtual elements on triangular meshes
Xuejun Xu, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. A family of stabilizer-free P k P_{k} virtual elements are constructed on triangular meshes. When choosing an accurate and proper interpolation, the stabilizer of the virtual elements can be dropped while the quasi-optimality is kept. The interpolating space here is the space of continuous P k P_{k} polynomials on the Hsieh-Clough-Tocher macro-triangle, where the macro-triangle is defined by connecting three vertices of a triangle with its barycenter. We show that such an interpolation preserves P k P_{k} polynomials locally and enforces the coercivity of the resulting bilinear form. Consequently the stabilizer-free virtual element solutions converge at the optimal order. Numerical tests are provided to confirm the theory and to be compared with existing virtual elements. Key words. virtual element, stabilizer free, elliptic equation, Hsieh-Clough-Tocher macro-triangle, triangular mesh. AMS subject classifications. 65N15, 65N30

For solving

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f&&\hbox{\rm{in }}\ \Omega,\\

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\nabla u,\nabla v)&=(f,v)&&\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{aligned}

Let

For

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{h}=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\tilde{v}|_{\partial K}\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial K),\Delta\tilde{v}|_{K}\in P_{k-2}(K)\},

where
P_{-1}=\{0\}

\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial K)=\{v\in C^{0}(\partial K):v|_{e}\in P_{k}(e)\ \forall e\subset K\}
.
In computation, the standard
interpolated virtual finite element space on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is defined by

(1.4)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v}\ :\ v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}(K),\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h};\ \tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{h}\},

\displaystyle\langle v_{h}-\tilde{v},w_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}=0,\ \hbox{\rm{ and }}\ (\nabla(v_{h}-\tilde{v}),\nabla w_{h})_{K}=0.

The stabilizer-free virtual element equation reads: Find
u_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}\in V_{h}
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{h}=(f,v_{h})\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{h},\ v_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v},

where
(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{h}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{K}

2.
The well-posedness

We show in this section that the stabilizer-free virtual element equation has a unique solution.

Lemma 2.1
.

The interpolation operator

\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}
is well defined in (
1.8
) and it preserves
P_{k}
polynomials,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v}=\tilde{v}\quad\ \hbox{\rm{if }}\ \tilde{v}\in P_{k}(K).

Proof.

Because
\tilde{v}|_{\partial K}\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(\partial K)
,

v_{h}
can assume the boundary
condition
v_{h}=\tilde{v}
exactly on
\partial K
. The linear system of equations in (
1.8
)
is a finite dimensional square system. The existence is implied by the uniqueness.
To show the uniqueness, we let
\tilde{v}=0
in (
1.8
).
Letting
w_{h}=v_{h}
in (
1.8
), we get

\displaystyle\nabla v_{h}={\bf 0}\quad\hbox{\rm{ on }}\ K.

Thus
v_{h}=c
is a constant on
K
. As
v_{h}
is continuous on edges,
v_{h}=c
is
a global constant on the whole domain. By the boundary condition, we get
0=\tilde{v}|_{\partial\Omega}=v_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=c
.
Hence
v_{h}=0
and (
1.8
) has a unique solution.

If
\tilde{v}\in P_{k}(K)\subset\mathbb{V}_{k}(K)
, defined in (
1.4
), then the solution
of (
1.8
) says, letting
w_{h}=v_{h}-\tilde{v}
,

\displaystyle\nabla(v_{h}-\tilde{v})={\bf 0}.

Thus
v_{h}-\tilde{v}
is a global constant which must be zero as it vanishes at all
\partial K
.
(
2.1
) is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The stabilizer-free virtual element equation (
1.6
) has a unique solution,
where the interpolation
\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}
is defined in (
1.8
).

Proof.

As both
\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{h}
,
(
1.6
) is a finite square system of linear equations. The uniqueness of solution
implies the existence.
To show the uniqueness, we let
f=0
and
\tilde{v}=\tilde{u}
in (
1.6
).
It follows that

\displaystyle|\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}|_{1,h}=0.

Thus
\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}=c
is constant on each
K
. But
\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}
is continuous on the whole
domain. By the boundary condition, we get
0=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=c
. That is,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}=0.

On one triangle
K=\cup_{i=1}^{3}K_{i}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}=0\quad\ \hbox{\rm{on }}\ \partial K.

Inside the triangle, by (
1.8
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and
integration by parts, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle(-\Delta\tilde{u},w_{h})=(\nabla\tilde{u},\nabla w_{h})=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(K)\cap\mathbb{V}_{k}(K).

By the space
\tilde{V}_{h}
definition (
1.3
), we denote

(2.5)

\displaystyle p_{k-2}=-\Delta\tilde{u}\in P_{k-2}(K).

Let the
w_{h}
in (
2.4
) be

(2.6)

\displaystyle w_{h}=p_{k-2}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})\in H^{1}_{0}(K)\cap\mathbb{V}_{k}(K),

where
\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})
is the
P_{1}
Lagrange basis function at
node
{\bf x}_{0}
, cf. Figure
2
.
That is,
\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})|_{K_{i}}=\lambda_{i,0}
is the barycentric coordinate at

x_{0}
on triangle
K_{i}
,
i.e., a linear function which assumes value 1 at
{\bf x}_{0}
and vanishing on the
line
e_{i}
, cf. Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
A Hsieh-Clough-Tocher macro-triangle
K=\cup_{i=1}^{3}K_{i}

With the
w_{k}
in (
2.6
), we get from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\int_{K}p_{k-2}^{2}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})d{\bf x}=0.

As
\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})>0
inside
K
, it follows that

\displaystyle p_{k-2}^{2}=0\ \hbox{\rm{ and }}\ p_{k-2}=0\ \hbox{\rm{ on }}\ K.

By (
2.3
) and (
2.5
),
\Delta\tilde{u}=0
in
K
and
\tilde{u}=0
on

\partial K
. Thus, by the unique solution of the Laplace equation,
\tilde{u}=0
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

3.
Convergence

We prove the optimal order convergence of the stabilizer-free virtual element solutions in this
section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the exact solution of (
1.2
).
Let
u_{h}
be the stabilizer-free virtual element solution of (
1.6
). It holds that

(3.1)

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{1}\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}.

Proof.

Since
w_{h}\in V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we subtract (
1.6
) from
(
1.2
) to get

\displaystyle(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla w_{h})_{h}=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h}.

Applying the Schwarz inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla(u-I_{h}u))

\displaystyle\leq|u-u_{h}|_{1}|u-I_{h}u|_{1}\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}|u-u_{h}|_{1},

where
I_{h}u
is the Scott-Zhang interpolation on
quasi-uniform triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}

[
30
]
.
The proof is complete.
∎

To get the optimal order
L^{2}
error bound, we need a full regularity of the dual
equation that the solution of the equation,

\displaystyle-\Delta w

\displaystyle=g\quad\hbox{\rm{ in }}\ \Omega,

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=0\quad\hbox{\rm{ on }}\ \partial\Omega,

satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle|w|_{2}\leq C\|g\|_{0}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the exact solution of (
1.2
).
Let
u_{h}
be the stabilizer-free virtual element solution of (
1.6
). Assuming
(
3.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}.

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the dual solution,

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla w,\nabla v)

\displaystyle=(u-u_{h},v),\ \forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus, by (
3.3
), (
3.2
) and (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla w,\nabla(u-u_{h}))=(\nabla(w-w_{h}),\nabla(u-u_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq Ch|w|_{2}h^{k}|u|_{k+1}\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}\|u-u_{h}\|_{0},

where
w_{h}
is the virtual element solution to the equation (
3.3
).
We obtain the
L^{2}
error bound.
∎

The standard
H^{1}
interpolation is defined by
\Pi_{h}^{1}\tilde{u}\in P_{k}(K)
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\nabla\Pi_{h}^{1}\tilde{u},\nabla p)_{K}&=-(\tilde{u},\Delta p)_{K}+\langle\tilde{u},\nabla p\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial K}\quad\forall p\in P_{k}(K)\setminus P_{0}(K),\\
\langle\Pi_{h}^{1}\tilde{u},p\rangle_{\partial K}&=\langle\tilde{u},p\rangle_{\partial K}\quad\forall p\in P_{0}(K),\end{aligned}

where
{\bf n}
is the unit outer normal vector.
\tilde{u}
is defined by the degrees of
freedom on triangle
K={\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}{\bf x}_{3}
,
\{F_{i},i=1,\dots,N_{K}\}
, cf.
[
4
]
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle F_{i}(\tilde{u})=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}({\bf x}_{j}),&j=1,2,3,\\
\tilde{u}(\frac{l{\bf x}_{j}+(k-l){\bf x}_{\hbox{\rm{\tiny mod}}(j,3)+1}}{k}),&l=1,\dots,k-1;\ j=1,2,3,\\
\frac{\int_{K}\tilde{u}(x-x_{0})^{j}(y-y_{0})^{l}d{\bf x}}{\int_{K}1d{\bf x}},&0\leq j+l\leq k-2,\end{cases}

where
{\bf x}_{0}=(x_{0},y_{0})
is the barycenter of
K
. The standard stabilizer
in
[
4
]
is defined as

(4.4)

\displaystyle S(\tilde{u}-\Pi^{1}_{h}\tilde{u},\tilde{v}-\Pi^{1}_{h}\tilde{v})_{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{K}}F_{i}(\tilde{u}-\Pi^{1}_{h}\tilde{u})F_{i}(\tilde{v}-\Pi^{1}_{h}\tilde{v}).

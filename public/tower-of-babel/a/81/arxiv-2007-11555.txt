Conformal field theory complexity from Euler-Arnold equations
Mario Flory, and Michal P. Heller

Abstract
Defining complexity in quantum field theory is a difficult task, and the main challenge concerns going beyond free models and associated Gaussian states and operations. One take on this issue is to consider conformal field theories in 1+1 dimensions and our work is a comprehensive study of state and operator complexity in the universal sector of their energy-momentum tensor. The unifying conceptual ideas are Euler-Arnold equations and their integro-differential generalization, which guarantee well-posedness of the optimization problem between two generic states or transformations of interest. The present work provides an in-depth discussion of the results reported in arXiv:2005.02415 and techniques used in their derivation. Among the most important topics we cover are usage of differential regularization, solution of the integro-differential equation describing Fubini-Study state complexity and probing the underlying geometry.

Introduction

The interplay between quantum field theory (QFT) and geometry has a long and rich history. Recently, a new and promising instance has emerged, which concerns geometrization of state preparation or representations of, primarily, the time evolution operator in systems described by quantum fields. In this approach one seeks to find a way of preparing a state or an operator of interest minimizing the use of what one regards as more primitive operators or states by relating it to a geodesic problem in an auxiliary geometry. This introduces then a relation between different operators or states according to their

Conformal transformations

In order to make our paper more self-contained and establish our notation, in this section we briefly review conformal transformations and the Virasoro group following the outline of the preceding works
Caputa:2018kdj
;
Erdmenger:2020sup

For this, we consider the unit circle

parametrized by an angular coordinate

\displaystyle f:\sigma\rightarrow f(\sigma)

(7)

that map the circle to itself, hence they need to satisfy the periodicity condition

\displaystyle(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})(\sigma)\equiv f_{1}\circ f_{2}(\sigma)=f_{1}(f_{2}(\sigma)).

(8)

The inverse of a group element is simply the inverse function, and the identity element is the map
f(\sigma)=\sigma
.

The Virasoro group is obtained as the central extension of the group of diffeomorphisms on the circle, thus an element in the Virasoro group is given by a function

and a number

\displaystyle(f_{1}(\sigma),\beta)\cdot(f_{2}(\sigma),\alpha)=(f_{1}\circ f_{2},\alpha+\beta+\mathfrak{C}(f_{1},f_{2})).

(9)

Herein,

is the Bott-cocycle which is explicitly given, for example, in

;
Oblak:2017ect

3.4
Degeneracy of the metric

Equations of motion and their solutions

4.1
Variational Principle

When deriving the geodesic equation in ordinary Riemannian geometry, it is a well known trick based on reparametrization invariance that we can use either the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}\dot{X}^{\mu}\dot{X}^{\nu}}
or
\mathcal{L}_{sq}=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}\dot{X}^{\mu}\dot{X}^{\nu}
. Specifically, it can be shown that the Euler-Lagrange-equations following from a variation of
\mathcal{L}_{sq}
are equivalent to those following from

if

\mathcal{L}_{1}

\mathcal{L}_{1}

\mathcal{L}_{sq}

The benefit in using
\mathcal{L}_{sq}
instead of
\mathcal{L}_{1}

The reason for the important role of affineness in this is that both Lagrangians do not depend explicitly on the variable
\tau

\tau\rightarrow\tau+\delta\tau

6
Comparison to KdV and other Euler-Arnold equations

6.1
Euler-Arnold equations as wave equations

So far, we have defined a form of geodesic motion on the infinite-dimensional Virasoro group-manifold via the functional (
21
) and the equations of motion (
34
). As we explained, these equations can be readily recognized by any general relativist as a generalisation of geodesic motion to infinite dimensions via the geodesic analogy of section
3.2
. Especially the fact that summation over discrete indices is replaced by integration over a continuous variable (
22
) is important as it leads to the integro-differential nature of the equation (
34
).
The concept of geodesic motion on group-manifolds including the Virasoro-group is not new and has been studied in detail to date, see
khesin2008geometry
;
LeeThesis
;
Vizman2008
;
arnold2013vladimir
for overviews. Such equations are often referred to as Euler-Arnold equations, due to the seminal contributions of Arnold
arnold2013vladimir
, who amongst other achievements showed that the Euler equations of fluid dynamics can be phrased as a geodesic motion on a diffeomorphism group
Arnold2014b
.

The usual procedure herein is to define an inner product on the Lie-algebra of the group, which is extended to the tangent-space at every group-element by requiring right- or left-invariance. For example, on the Virasoro-group with
(v(\tau,\sigma)\partial_{\sigma}\,,b^{(v)}(\tau))
being a two-component object consisting of the Lie-algebra element
v(\tau,\sigma)\partial_{\sigma}
of the group of diffeomorphisms and the real-valued function function
b^{(v)}(\tau)
associated with the central extension
Oblak:2016eij
, the inner product

\displaystyle\left\langle(v(\tau,\sigma)\partial_{\sigma}\,,b^{(v)}(\tau)),\,(w(\tau,\sigma)\partial_{\sigma}\,,b^{(w)}(\tau))\right\rangle\equiv\int\left(\alpha\,v\,w+\beta\,v^{\prime}\,w^{\prime}\right)\,d\sigma+b^{(v)}\cdot b^{(w)}

(69)

leads to the equations of motion

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\dot{v}+3v\,v^{\prime}\right)-\beta\left(\dot{v}^{\prime\prime}+2v^{\prime}v^{\prime\prime}+v\,v^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)-b^{(v)}v^{\prime\prime\prime}=0,

(70)

\displaystyle\dot{b}^{(v)}=0.

(71)

A number of famous integrable equations follow from this, such as the Camassa-Holm equation (
\alpha=\beta=1
), the Hunter-Saxton equation (
\alpha=0,\beta=1
), the Korteweg-de Vries equation (
\alpha=1,\beta=0
) and the inviscid Burgers equation (

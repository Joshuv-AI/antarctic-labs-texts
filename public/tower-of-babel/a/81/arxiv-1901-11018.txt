Inequalities for eigenvalues of fourth order elliptic operators in divergence form on Riemannian manifolds
Shahroud Azami

Abstract
. In this paper, we study eigenvalue of linear fourth order elliptic operators in divergence form with Dirichlet boundary condition on a bounded domain in a compact Riemannian manifolds with boundary (possibly empty) and find a general inequality for them. As an application, by using this inequality, we study eigenvalues of this operator on compact domains of complete submanifolds in a Euclidean space.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, let
(M,\langle,\rangle)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded connected domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
in
M
. Denote by
\Delta
the Beltrami-Laplace operator on
M
. The study of the spectrum of geometric operator is an important topic and many works have been done in this area. The clamped plate problem or the Dirichlet biharmonic operator for a connected bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=\lambda u,&\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\\
u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal vector field of
\partial\Omega
. Suppose that
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is eigenvalues of the problem (
1.1
). Payn et’al proved in paper
[
4
]
that

\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k=1,2,....

In 1984, Hile and Yeh
[
2
]
generalized and showed

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{n^{2}k^{\frac{3}{2}}}{8(n+2)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k=1,2,....

In 1990, Hook
[
3
]
obtained the following inequality

\frac{n^{2}k^{2}}{8(n+2)}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k=1,2,....

In 2006, Cheng and Yang
[
1
]
obtained the inequality

\lambda_{k+1}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq\left(\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k=1,2,....

In 2007, Wang and Xia
[
5
]
, proved universal bounds for eigenvalues of the biharmonic operator on Riemannian manifolds, for instance they showed that, when
\Omega
is a compact domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\lambda_{k+1}\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}+\left\{\frac{64}{n^{2}k^{2}}(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}})(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{3}{2}})-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i})^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,k=1,2,....

The aim of the present work is to study the eigenvalues of linear fourth order elliptic operator in divergence form on Riemannian manifolds. In special case, this operator is the biharmonic operator. We prove some general inequalities for them. By using these inequalities, we obtain, when
\Omega
is a compact domains of complete submanifolds in a Euclidean space.

Let
T
be symmetric positive definite
(1,1)
-tensor on
M
and
\Omega\subset M
be a compact domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
in
M
. We will studying the eigenvalue problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}^{2}u=\lambda u,&\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\\
u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{L}u=div(T(\nabla u))
and
\nabla
is the gradient operator of
M
. If
T
be the identity tensor the
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
.
The main results of this paper are as follow

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
(M,\langle,\rangle)
isometrically immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\lambda_{i}
be the
i
th eigenvalue of (
1.2
) and
u_{i}
be the corresponding orthonormal real-valued eigenfunction, that is

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}^{2}u_{i}=\lambda_{i}u_{i},&\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\\
u_{i}=\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\\
\int_{\Omega}u_{i}u_{j}dm=\delta_{ij}&\forall i,j=1,2,....\end{cases}

Then for any positive constant
\delta
and any positive integer
k
, we have

(1.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}tr(T)dm\leq\delta\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}A_{i}+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})B_{i}

where

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathcal{L}u_{i}\langle tr(\alpha\circ T)+tr(\nabla T),I\rangle dm+2\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\langle T(\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i}),I\rangle dm

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}(||tr(\alpha\circ T)||^{2}+|tr(\nabla T)|^{2})dm+4\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\langle T(\nabla u_{i}),tr(\nabla T)\rangle dm

\displaystyle+4\int_{\Omega}|T(\nabla u_{i})|^{2}dm+2\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{i}\langle T(\nabla u_{i}),I\rangle dm,

and

B_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\left\{|T(\nabla u_{i})|^{2}+u_{i}\langle T(\nabla u_{i}),tr(\nabla T)\rangle+\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{4}(||tr(\alpha\circ T)||^{2}+|tr(\nabla T)|^{2})\right\}dm,

where
I(x)=(x_{1},...,x_{m})
for any
x=(x_{1},...,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
||f||^{2}=\int_{\Omega}f^{2}dm
,
dm
is the volume form on
\Omega
,
\alpha
is the fundamental form of
M
and
\alpha\circ T=\alpha(T(.),.)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

\Omega
be a domain in an

,
T_{0}=\sup_{\bar{\Omega}}|tr(\nabla T)|
and

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we describe the necessary tools about tensor
T
and problem (
1.2
) which enable us to prove our results. Throughout the paper, for any vector fields
X,Y
, we denote
\langle T(X),Y\rangle
with
T(X,Y)
. For any
u,v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, straightforward computation implies that

\mathcal{L}(uv)=v\mathcal{L}u+u\mathcal{L}v+2T(\nabla u,\nabla v).

Let
d\mu
be the volume form on the boundary induced by the outward normal vector field
\nu
on
\partial\Omega
. The divergence theorem for operator
\mathcal{L}
as follows

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u\,dm=\int_{\partial\Omega}T(\nabla u,\nu)d\mu,

then the integration by parts yields

\int_{\Omega}v\mathcal{L}u\,dm=-\int_{\Omega}T(\nabla u,\nabla v)\,dm+\int_{\partial\Omega}vT(\nabla u,\nu)d\mu.

Hence, the operators
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{2}
are self-adjoint operator in the space of all function in
L^{2}(\Omega,dm)
that vanish on
\partial\Omega
. Therefore the eigenvalues of problem (
1.2
) are real and discrete.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in a an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
(M,\langle,\rangle)
,
\lambda_{i}
be the
i
th eigenvalue of (
1.2
) and
u_{i}
be the corresponding orthonormal real-valued eigenfunction. Then for any
h\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cup C^{3}(\partial\Omega)
and any positive integer
k
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}w_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||p_{i}||^{2},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}w_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}\mathcal{L}h}{2}||^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||p_{i}||^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}\mathcal{L}h}{2}||^{2},

where
\delta
is any positive constant,

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}p_{i}\,dm,

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}h\mathcal{L}u_{i}+2T(\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i})+\mathcal{L}(u_{i}\mathcal{L}h)+2\mathcal{L}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}),

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}T(\nabla h,\nabla h)\,dm.

Proof.

For each
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
, consider the functions
\phi_{i}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.4)

\phi_{i}=hu_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j}

where
a_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}u_{j}\,dm
. We have
\phi_{i}|_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and

\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}u_{r}\,dm=\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}u_{r}\,dm-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\int_{\Omega}u_{r}u_{j}d\mu=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall i,r=1,2,...,k.

Then by the inequality of Rayleigh-Ritz, we get

(2.5)

\lambda_{k+1}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\mathcal{L}^{2}\phi_{i}dm}{\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}^{2}dm},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall i=1,2,...,k.

Since

\mathcal{L}\phi_{i}=\mathcal{L}(hu_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\mathcal{L}u_{j}=h\mathcal{L}u_{i}+u_{i}\mathcal{L}h+2T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\mathcal{L}u_{j},

we obtain

\mathcal{L}^{2}\phi_{i}=\mathcal{L}h\mathcal{L}u_{i}+\lambda_{i}hu_{i}+2T(\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i})+\mathcal{L}(u_{i}\mathcal{L}h)+2\mathcal{L}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{j},

therefore we get

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\mathcal{L}^{2}\phi_{i}dm=\lambda_{i}||\phi_{i}||^{2}+\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}p_{i}\,dm-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}r_{ij},

where
r_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}p_{i}u_{j}dm
and

p_{i}=\mathcal{L}h\mathcal{L}u_{i}+2T(\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i})+\mathcal{L}(u_{i}\mathcal{L}h)+2\mathcal{L}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}).

Using integration by parts, we deduce that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})dm+\int_{\Omega}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i})dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=-\int_{\Omega}T(\nabla u_{j},\nabla T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i}))dm-\int_{\Omega}div(u_{j}T\nabla h)\mathcal{L}u_{i}dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})dm-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{i}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{j})dm-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}h\mathcal{L}u_{i}dm.

On the other hand

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})dm-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{i}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{j})dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=-\int_{\Omega}h\,div(\mathcal{L}u_{j}T\nabla u_{i})+\int_{\Omega}h\,div(\mathcal{L}u_{i}T\nabla u_{j})

\displaystyle\,\,\,=-\int_{\Omega}\langle h\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{j},T\nabla u_{i}\rangle dm+\int_{\Omega}\langle h\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i},T\nabla u_{j}\rangle dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}div(hT\nabla u_{j})dm-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}div(hT\nabla u_{i})dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}h\mathcal{L}^{2}u_{j}-u_{j}h\mathcal{L}^{2}u_{i})\,dm+\int_{\Omega}\left(\langle u_{i}T\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{j}\rangle-\langle u_{j}T\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i}\rangle\right)\,dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})a_{ij}-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})\,dm+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{i}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{j})\,dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathcal{L}u_{j}\mathcal{L}h\,dm+\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}u_{i}\mathcal{L}h\,dm,

which implies that

(2.9)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})dm-2\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}u_{i}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{j})dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})a_{ij}-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathcal{L}u_{j}\mathcal{L}h\,dm+\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}u_{i}\mathcal{L}h\,dm.

Substituting (
2.7
) into (
2.9
), we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})dm+2\int_{\Omega}u_{j}T(\nabla h,\nabla\mathcal{L}u_{i})dm

\displaystyle\,\,\,=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})a_{ij}-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathcal{L}u_{j}\mathcal{L}h\,dm-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}u_{i}\mathcal{L}h\,dm.

Moreover

(2.11)

\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\mathcal{L}(u_{i}\mathcal{L}h)\,dm=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathcal{L}u_{j}\mathcal{L}h\,dm.

Combining (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and
r_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}p_{i}u_{j}dm
, we can write

(2.12)

r_{ij}=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})a_{ij}.

It follows from (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) that

(2.13)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||\phi_{i}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\mathcal{L}^{2}\phi_{i}dm-\lambda_{i}||\phi_{i}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm=w_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})a_{ij}^{2},

where
w_{i}=\int_{\Omega}hu_{i}p_{i}\,dm
. We use that
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}u_{j}\,dm=0
again to get

(2.14)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}))\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}(p_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}u_{j}\,dm\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||\phi_{i}||^{2}\left(||p_{i}||^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm\right)\left(||p_{i}||^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}^{2}\right),

this implies that

(2.15)

(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm\right)\leq||p_{i}||^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}^{2}.

Multiplying (
2.15
) by
(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})
and summing on
i
from
1
to
k
, we obtain

(2.16)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||p_{i}||^{2}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}a_{ij}^{2}.

Multiplying (
2.13
) by
(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}
, summing on
i
from
1
to
k
and
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
, we infer

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}p_{i}\,dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}w_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})a_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}w_{i}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}a_{ij}^{2},

then

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}w_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})||p_{i}||^{2},

which shows that (
2.1
) is true. In order to prove (
2.2
), we set

(2.19)

b_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left(T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}}{2}\mathcal{L}h\right)dm.

Observe that

\displaystyle b_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}div(u_{j}T\nabla h)dm+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}u_{j}\mathcal{L}hdm

\displaystyle\leq\delta(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{3}||\phi_{i}||^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\int_{\Omega}(T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}}{2}\mathcal{L}h-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}u_{j})dm

\displaystyle\leq\delta(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{3}||\phi_{i}||^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})(||T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}}{2}\mathcal{L}h||^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}),

hence (

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}(v_{i}+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}b_{ij})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}(w_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})a_{ij}^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})(||T(\nabla h,\nabla u_{i})+\frac{u_{i}}{2}\mathcal{L}h||^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}).

Now, summing over

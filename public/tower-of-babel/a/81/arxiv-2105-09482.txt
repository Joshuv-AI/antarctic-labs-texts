Translating solutions for a class of quasilinear parabolic initial boundary value problems in Lorentz-Minkowski plane ℝ 1 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}_{1} Thanks: ∗ \ast Corresponding author
Ya Gao, Jing-Hua Li, Jing Mao ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the evolution of spacelike curves in Lorentz-Minkowski plane ℝ 1 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}_{1} along prescribed geometric flows (including the classical curve shortening flow or mean curvature flow as a special case), which correspond to a class of quasilinear parabolic initial boundary value problems, and can prove that this flow exists for all time. Moreover, we can also show that the evolving spacelike curves converge to a spacelike straight line or a spacelike Grim Reaper curve as time tends to infinity.



Keywords
: Mean curvature flow,
spacelike curves, Lorentz-Minkowski plane, Neumann boundary
condition.

2.1.
The gradient estimate

Let

Lemma 2.1

Let

\displaystyle\inf_{I_{d}}u(x,0)\leq u(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}u(x,0),\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

Using the maximum principle, we can obtain that

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Then we can obtain the

Lemma 2.2

Let
u

\displaystyle\inf_{I_{d}}u_{t}(x,0)\leq u_{t}(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}u_{t}(x,0),\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

Set

Differentiating both sides of the first evolution equation of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{1-u^{2}_{x}}\Phi_{xx}+\frac{2u_{x}u_{xx}}{(1-u^{2}_{x})^{2}}\Phi_{x}\qquad&&in~I_{d}\times[0,T],\\[1.42262pt]
&\Phi_{x}(i,t)=0\qquad&&t\in[0,T],~i=-d,d\\[1.42262pt]
&\Phi(\cdot,0)=\Phi_{0}\qquad&&u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(I_{d}).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Using the maximum principle and Hopf’s Lemma, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{I_{d}}u_{t}(x,0)\leq u_{t}(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}u_{t}(x,0),\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T],

\hfill\Box

The gradient estimate can be obtained as follows:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle|u_{x}|(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}|u_{x}|(x,0)<1\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

Set
\varphi=\frac{|u_{x}|^{2}}{2}

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}&=&\displaystyle u_{x}u_{xt}\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle u_{x}\left(\frac{u_{xxx}}{1-u^{2}_{x}}+\frac{2u_{x}u^{2}_{xx}}{(1-u_{x}^{2})^{2}}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle\frac{u_{x}u_{xxx}}{1-u^{2}_{x}}+\frac{2u_{x}^{2}u^{2}_{xx}}{(1-u_{x}^{2})^{2}}.\end{array}

Since

Our proof is finished.

u_{t}
in (
2.4
) yields

-\int|u_{xt}|dx+\frac{\int u_{t}dx}{\int dx}\leq u_{t}\leq\int|u_{xt}|dx+\frac{\int u_{t}dx}{\int dx},

Since

u_{t}=\frac{u_{xx}}{1-u_{x}^{2}}=\left(\mathrm{artanh}(u_{x})\right)_{x}
,
we have

and then

-\int|u_{xt}|dx+A(\theta_{-d},\theta_{d},d)\leq u_{t}\leq\int|u_{xt}|dx+A(\theta_{-d},\theta_{d},d).

This completes the proof.

3.
A general case: a class of quasilinear parabolic initial boundary value problems

In this section, the proof of Theorem
1.2
will be shown in
details.

3.1.
The gradient estimate

Denote by
v=v(u_{x})
and

v^{\prime}=v^{\prime}(u_{x})=\frac{d}{du_{x}}v(u_{x})
. From

u_{t}=(v)_{x}=v^{\prime}u_{xx}
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{x}=v^{\prime}u_{xxx}+v^{\prime\prime}u^{2}_{xx},

and

(3.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v=v^{\prime}v_{xx}.

Hence, the NBC on
u_{x}
and the above calculations yield

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v(u_{x}(i,t))=v(\theta_{i})\qquad&&for~i=-d,d\\
&v_{t}(u_{x}(i,t))=v_{tt}(u_{x}(i,t))=v_{ttt}(u_{x}(i,t))=\ldots=0\qquad&&for~i=-d,d.\end{aligned}\right.

As before, let
0<\alpha<1
and
T^{\ast}
be the maximal time such
that there exists some

\displaystyle u\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I_{d}\times[0,T^{*}))\cap C^{\infty}(I_{d}\times(0,T^{*}))

which solves (
1.5
). Next, we shall prove a priori estimates
for those admissible solutions on
[0,T]
, with
T<T^{\ast}
. First,
we can obtain the following gradient estimate:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle|u_{x}|(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}|u_{x}|(x,0)<1\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Set
\varphi=\frac{|u_{x}|^{2}}{2}
. By differentiating
\varphi
, we
have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}&=&\displaystyle u_{x}u_{xt}\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle u_{x}(v^{\prime}u_{xxx}+v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}^{2})\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}^{2}u_{x}+v^{\prime}u_{xxx}u_{x}.\end{array}

Since

\varphi_{x}=u_{x}u_{xx},~\varphi_{xx}=u^{2}_{xx}+u_{x}u_{xxx},

one has
u_{x}u_{xxx}=\varphi_{xx}-u^{2}_{xx}
, and then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}&=&\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}\varphi_{x}+v^{\prime}(\varphi_{xx}-u_{xx}^{2})\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle v^{\prime}\varphi_{xx}+v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}\varphi_{x}-v^{\prime}u_{xx}^{2}\\[8.53581pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle v^{\prime}\varphi_{xx}+v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}\varphi_{x}.\end{array}

Therefore, we can obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\leq v^{\prime}\varphi_{xx}+v^{\prime\prime}u_{xx}\varphi_{x}\qquad&&in~I_{d}\times[0,T],\\
&\varphi(i,t)=\frac{\theta_{i}^{2}}{2}\qquad&&t\in[0,T],~i=-d,d\\
&\varphi(\cdot,0)=\frac{|u_{x}(\cdot,0)|^{2}}{2}\qquad&&u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(I_{d})\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Using the maximum principle to the above system, one has

|\varphi|(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}|\varphi|(x,0),\qquad\quad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

So

|u_{x}|(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}|u_{x}|(x,0),\qquad\quad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T].

Since
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\{(x,u(x,0))|x\in I_{d}\}
is a spacelike
graph in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{1}
, it follows that

|u_{x}|(x,t)\leq\sup_{I_{d}}|u_{x}|(\cdot,0)<1,\qquad\forall x\in I_{d},~t\in[0,T],

which completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Applying the above gradient estimate and the IBVP (
1.5
), it is
not hard to get the following estimates.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.5
). Then we have:

(1)
0<c_{5}\leq v^{\prime}(t)\leq c_{6}
holds for some positive
c_{5}
,
c_{6}
only depending on
u_{x}(x,0)
;

(2)
v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime\prime}
and all higher derivatives have uniform bounds
depending only on
u_{x}(x,0)
.

3.2.
The long-time existence

Combing the system of IBVP (
1.5
) and an almost same argument
to that of
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

Furthermore, the following integral estimates for high-order

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

(3.3)

-\int|v_{xx}|dx+\widetilde{A}(\theta_{-d},\theta_{d},d)\leq v_{x}\leq\int|v_{xx}|dx+\widetilde{A}(\theta_{-d},\theta_{d},d),

(3.4)

\sup\left(\frac{\partial^{k}v}{\partial t^{k}}\right)^{2}\leq 2d\int\left|\frac{\partial^{k}v_{x}}{\partial t^{k}}\right|^{2}dx\qquad for\quad k\geq 1,

and

(3.5)

\sup\left(\frac{\partial^{k}v_{x}}{\partial t^{k}}\right)^{2}\leq 2d\int\left|\frac{\partial^{k}v_{xt}}{\partial t^{k}}\right|^{2}dx\qquad for\quad k\geq 1.

Proof.

Similar to Lemma
2.6
, the first inequality (
3.3
)
follows from the estimate

\inf g-\sup g+\frac{\int gdx}{\int dx}\leq g\leq\sup g-\inf g+\frac{\int gdx}{\int dx},

and the replacement of
v_{x}
to

course, in this process the NBC in (
1.5
) has been used.

Now,
take
h=\frac{\partial^{k}v}{\partial t^{k}}
and

h(x_{0})=\max_{I_{d}}h
. In order to show (
3.4
) and
(
3.5

|h(x_{0})|\leq(2d)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int h^{2}_{x}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By (

\begin{array}[]{lll}|h(x_{0})|&=&\displaystyle|h(x_{0})-h(-d)|\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle\int_{-d}^{x_{0}}h_{x}dx\\[8.53581pt]
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{-d}^{d}h_{x}dx\\[8.53581pt]
&=&\displaystyle||h_{x}||_{L^{1}}.\end{array}

Together with the fact

||h_{x}||_{L^{1}}\leq||1||_{L^{2}}||h_{x}||_{L^{2}},

it follows that

|h(x_{0})|\leq||1||_{L^{2}}||h_{x}||_{L^{2}},

which is (
3.4

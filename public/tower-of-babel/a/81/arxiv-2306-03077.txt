Modified metrics of acoustic black holes: A review
M. A. Anacleto, F. A. Brito, E. Passos

Abstract
In this brief review, we will address acoustic black holes arising from quantum field theory in the Lorentz-violating and non-commutative background. Thus, we consider canonical acoustic black holes with effective metrics for the purpose of investigating Hawking radiation and entropy. We show that due to the generalized uncertainty principle and the modified dispersion relation, the Hawking temperature is regularized, that is, free from the singularity when the horizon radius goes to zero. In addition, we also find logarithmic corrections in the leading order for entropy.

II
Acoustic Black Hole

In this section we review the steps to obtain the relativistic acoustic metric from the Lagrangian density of the charged scalar field. Here we will follow the procedure adopted in
Ge:2010wx
.

II.1
Relativistic Acoustic Metric

In order to determine the relativistic acoustic metric, we start by considering the following Lagrangian density:

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\ast}\partial^{\mu}\phi+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}-b|\phi|^{4}.

(1)

Now, we decompose the scalar field as
\phi=\sqrt{\rho(x,t)}\exp{(iS(x,t))}
, such that

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\rho\partial_{\mu}S\partial^{\mu}S+m^{2}\rho-b\rho^{2}+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\rho}}(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu})\sqrt{\rho}.

(2)

Moreover, from the above Lagrangian, we find the equations of motion for
S
and
\rho
given respectively by

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left(\rho\partial^{\mu}{S}\right)=0,

(3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}{\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\sqrt{\rho}}+\partial_{\mu}S\partial^{\mu}{S}+m^{2}-2b\rho=0,

(4)

where the Eq. (
3
) is the continuity equation and Eq. (
4
) is an equation describing a hydrodynamical fluid, and the term,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}{\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\sqrt{\rho}}
, called the quantum potential can be neglected in the hydrodynamic region.

Now, by performing the following perturbations on equations of motion (
3
) and (
4
):

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{0}+\epsilon\rho_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle S=S_{0}+\epsilon\psi+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(6)

We obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left(\rho_{1}u_{0}^{\mu}+\rho_{0}\partial^{\mu}\psi\right)=0,

(7)

and

\displaystyle u_{0}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-b\rho_{1}=0,

(8)

where we have defined
u_{0}^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}S_{0}
.
Hence, solving (
8
) for
\rho_{1}
and substituting into (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[u^{\mu}_{0}u^{\nu}_{0}+b\rho_{0}g^{\mu\nu}\right]\partial_{\nu}\psi=0.

(9)

We can also write the above equation as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-1-\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2{\omega}^{2}_{0}}\right]\partial_{t}\psi-{\omega}^{2}_{0}\frac{v^{i}_{0}}{{\omega}_{0}}\partial_{i}\psi\right\}

\displaystyle+\partial_{i}\left\{{-\omega}^{2}_{0}\frac{v^{i}_{0}}{{\omega}_{0}}\partial_{t}\psi+{\omega}^{2}_{0}\left[-\frac{v^{i}_{0}v^{j}_{0}}{{\omega}^{2}_{0}}+\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2{\omega}^{2}_{0}}\delta^{ij}\right]\partial_{j}\psi\right\}=0,

(10)

where
\omega_{0}=-\partial^{t}S_{0}
and
v_{0}^{i}=\partial_{i}S_{0}
(the local velocity field).
In addition, we define

c^{2}_{s}=b\rho_{0}/2\omega^{2}_{0}
to be the speed of sound
and
v^{i}=v^{i}_{0}/\omega_{0}
.
However, the equation (
II.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}^{2}}\left[\left(-1-c_{s}^{2}\right)\partial_{t}\psi-v^{i}\partial_{i}\psi\right]\right\}+\,\partial_{i}\left\{\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}^{2}}\left[-v^{i}\partial_{t}\psi+\left(-v^{i}v^{j}+c_{s}^{2}\delta^{ij}\right)\partial_{j}\psi\right]\right\}=0.

(11)

In this way, the above equation can be written as a Klein-Gordon equation in (3+1) dimensional curved space as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\right)\psi=0,

(12)

where

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c^{2}_{s}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1-c^{2}_{s}&\vdots&-v^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}&\vdots&c^{2}_{s}\delta^{ij}-v^{i}v^{j}\end{array}\right).

Hence, by determining the inverse of
g^{\mu\nu}
, we find the relativistic acoustic metric given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{1+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}+v^{2}&\vdots&-v^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}&\vdots&(1+c^{2}_{s})\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right).

The metric depends on the density
\rho_{0}
, the local sound speed in the fluid
c_{s}
, the velocity of flow
\vec{v}
.
This is the acoustic black hole metric for high
c_{s}
and
\vec{v}
speeds.
Note that, in the non-relativistic limit, up to a overall factor, the metric found by Unruh is obtained.

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-c^{2}_{s}+v^{2}&\vdots&-v^{i}\\
\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{j}&\vdots&\delta^{ij}\end{array}\right).

The relativistic acoustic metric (
II.1
) has also been obtained from the Abelian Higgs model
Ge:2010wx
.

II.2
The Dispersion Relation

Here we aim to examine the dispersion relation.
Hence, we will adopt the notation written below

\displaystyle\psi\sim\mbox{Re}\left[e^{i{\omega}t-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\right],\qquad{\omega}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t},\qquad\vec{k}=\nabla\psi.

(25)

So we can write the Klein-Gordon equation (
11
) in terms of momentum and frequency as follows:

\displaystyle(1+c^{2}_{s})\,{\omega}^{2}+2(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{k})\,{\omega}-\left(c_{s}^{2}-v^{2}\right)k^{2}=0.

(26)

Now, by making
k^{i}=\delta^{i1}
, we have

\displaystyle{\omega}=\frac{-v_{1}k\,\pm c_{s}k\sqrt{1+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}_{1}}}{(1+c^{2}_{s})}=\frac{-v_{1}k\,\pm c_{s}k\sqrt{1+(c_{s}-v_{1})(c_{s}+v_{1})}}{(1+c^{2}_{s})},

(27)

In the limit of small
v_{1}
, we find the modified dispersion relation

\displaystyle\omega\approx E\left(1+\frac{v_{1}}{2}\right),

(28)

where
E=c_{s}\,k
is the linear dispersion relation.

III
Modified Acoustic Black Hole

In this section we review the derivation of the relativistic acoustic metric from the Abelian Higgs model in the background violating-Lorentz and noncommutative.

III.1
The Lorentz-Violating Model

At this point, we consider the Abelian Higgs model with Lorentz symmetry breaking that has been introduced as a change in the scalar sector of the Lagrangian
Bazeia:2005tb
.
Moreover, the relativistic acoustic metric violating Lorentz has been found in
Anacleto:2010cr
.
Then, the corresponding Lagrangian for the abelian Higgs model in the Lorentz-violating background is written as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}-b|\phi|^{4}+k^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}\phi^{\ast}D_{\nu}\phi,

(29)

being
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
the field intensity tensor,
D_{\mu}\phi=\partial_{\mu}\phi-ieA_{\mu}\phi
the covariant derivative and
k^{\mu\nu}
a constant tensor implementing the Lorentz symmetry breaking,
given by
Anacleto:2010cr

k_{\mu\nu}=\left[\begin{array}[]{clcl}\beta&\alpha&\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha&\beta&\alpha&\alpha\\
\alpha&\alpha&\beta&\alpha\\
\alpha&\alpha&\alpha&\beta\end{array}\right],\quad(\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3),

(30)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are real parameters.

Next, following the steps taken in the previous section to derive the relativistic acoustic metric from quantum field theory, we consider
\phi=\sqrt{\rho(x,t)}\exp{(iS(x,t))}
in the Lagrangian above. Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\rho\partial_{\mu}S\partial^{\mu}S-2e\rho A_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}S+e^{2}\rho A_{\mu}A^{\mu}+m^{2}\rho-b\rho^{2}

(31)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle k^{\mu\nu}\rho(\partial_{\mu}S\partial_{\nu}S-2eA_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}S+e^{2}A_{\mu}A_{\nu})+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\rho}}(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\partial}^{\mu})\sqrt{\rho},

where
\tilde{\partial}^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}+k^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}
. The equations of motion for
S
and
\rho
are:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[\rho u^{\mu}+\rho k^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}\right]=0,

(32)

and

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\partial}^{\mu})\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}+u_{\mu}u^{\mu}+k^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+m^{2}-2b\rho=0,

(33)

where we have defined
u^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}S-eA^{\mu}
.
Now, by linearizing the equations above around the background
(\rho_{0},S_{0})
, with

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{0}+\epsilon\rho_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(34)

\displaystyle S=S_{0}+\epsilon\psi+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}),

(35)

and keeping the vector field
A_{\mu}
unchanged, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[\rho_{1}\left(u_{0}^{\mu}+k^{\mu\nu}u_{0\nu}\right)+\rho_{0}\left(g^{\mu\nu}+k^{\mu\nu}\right)\partial_{\nu}\psi\right]=0,

(36)

and

\displaystyle\left(u_{0}^{\mu}+k^{\mu\nu}u_{0\nu}\right)\partial_{\mu}\psi-b\rho_{1}=0,

(37)

by solving (
37
) for
\rho_{1}
and replacing into equation (
36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left[u^{\mu}_{0}u^{\nu}_{0}+k^{\mu\lambda}u_{0\lambda}u_{0}^{\nu}+u_{0}^{\mu}k^{\nu\lambda}u_{0\lambda}+b\rho_{0}\left(g^{\mu\nu}+k^{\mu\nu}\right)\right]\partial_{\nu}\psi=0.

(38)

Hence, we find the equation of motion for a linear acoustic disturbance
\psi
given by a Klein-Gordon equation in a curved space

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu})\psi=0,

(39)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the relativistic acoustic metrics.

For
\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha=0
, we have
Anacleto:2010cr

g_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{b\rho_{0}\tilde{\beta}_{-}^{1/2}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}}\left[\begin{array}[]{clcl}-\left(\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}\right)&\vdots&\quad\quad\quad\quad-v^{j}\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots&\cdot&\quad\quad\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
-v^{i}&\vdots&f_{\beta}\delta^{ij}+\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{i}v^{j}\end{array}\right],

(40)

where
{\cal Q}=1+\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}

and
f_{\beta}=\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}+\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}
.

The acoustic line element in the Lorentz-violating background can be written as follows

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b\rho_{0}\tilde{\beta}_{-}^{1/2}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{\cal{Q}}}\left[-\left(\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}\right)dt^{2}-2\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x}dt+\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}(\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x})^{2}+f_{\beta}d\vec{x}^{2}\right].

(41)

Now changing the time coordinate as
d\tau=dt+\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{+}\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x}}{c^{2}_{s}-\tilde{\beta}_{-}v^{2}}
,
we find the acoustic metric in the stationary form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b\rho_{0}\tilde{\beta}_{-}^{1/2}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{{\cal Q}}}\left[-\left(\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}\right)d\tau^{2}+{\cal F}\left(\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}v^{i}v^{j}}{c^{2}_{s}-\tilde{\beta}_{-}v^{2}}+\frac{f_{\beta}}{{\cal F}}\delta^{ij}\right)dx^{i}dx^{j}\!\right]\!\!.

(42)

where
{\cal F}=\left(\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}+\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}-\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}v^{2}\right)
.
For
\tilde{\beta}=1
we recover the result found in Ref.
Ge:2010wx
.

Next, for
\beta=0
and
\alpha\neq 0
, we have
Anacleto:2010cr

g_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left[\begin{array}[]{clcl}g_{tt}&\vdots&g_{tj}\\
\cdots&\cdot&\cdots\\
g_{it}&\vdots&g_{ij}\end{array}\right],

(43)

where

\displaystyle g_{tt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[(1+\alpha)c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}+\alpha^{2}(1-v)^{2}],

(44)

\displaystyle g_{tj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})v^{j},

(45)

\displaystyle g_{it}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})v^{i},

(46)

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})^{2}+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}\right]\delta^{ij}+v^{i}v^{j},

(47)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)[(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})^{2}+c^{2}_{s}]-v^{2}+\alpha^{2}(1-v)^{2}\left[1+(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})^{2}c^{-2}_{s}\right].

(48)

Thus, the acoustic line element in the Lorentz-violating background can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left[g_{tt}dt^{2}-2(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})(\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x})dt+(\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x})^{2}+f_{\alpha}d\vec{x}^{2}\right],

(49)

where
f_{\alpha}=(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})^{2}+c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}
. Now changing the time coordinate as

d\tau=dt+\frac{(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})(\vec{v}\cdot d\vec{x})}{[(1+\alpha)c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}+\alpha^{2}(1-v)^{2}]},

(50)

we find the acoustic metric in the stationary form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{b\rho_{0}}{2c_{s}\sqrt{f}}\left[g_{tt}d\tau^{2}+\Lambda\left(\frac{-v^{i}v^{j}}{g_{tt}}+\frac{f_{\alpha}\delta^{ij}}{\Lambda}\right)dx^{i}dx^{j}\right]\!.

(51)

where
\Lambda=(1-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{v})^{2}-g_{tt}
.
For
\alpha=0
, the result found in
Ge:2010wx
is recovered.

III.2
Noncommutative Acoustic Black Hole

The metric of a noncommutative canonical acoustic black hole has been found by us in
Anacleto:2011bv
.
Here, starting from the noncommutative Abelian Higgs model, we briefly review the steps to generate the relativistic acoustic metric in the noncommutative background.
Thus, the Lagrangian of the Abelian Higgs model in the noncommutative background is given by
Ghosh:2004wi

\displaystyle\hat{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa_{+}}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\kappa_{-}\left(|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}+m^{2}|\phi|^{2}-b|\phi|^{4}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\mu}\left[(D_{\beta}\phi)^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\phi+(D^{\mu}\phi)^{\dagger}D_{\beta}\phi\right],

(52)

being
\kappa_{\pm}=1\pm\theta^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}/2
,
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
the field intensity tensor
and
D_{\mu}\phi=\partial_{\mu}\phi-ieA_{\mu}\phi
the covariant derivative.
The parameter
\theta^{\alpha\beta}
is a constant, real-valued antisymmetric

- matrix in

-dimensional spacetime with dimensions of length squared.

Now, we use
\phi=\sqrt{\rho(x,t)}\exp{(iS(x,t))}

such that
Anacleto:2011bv
.

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa_{+}}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\rho\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}{\cal D}_{\mu}S{\cal D}_{\nu}S+\tilde{\theta}m^{2}\rho-\tilde{\theta}b\rho^{2}+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\rho}}\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\sqrt{\rho},

(53)

where
{\cal D}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-eA_{\mu}/S
,
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}=\tilde{\theta}g^{\mu\nu}+\Theta^{\mu\nu}

and

(56)

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\theta}u_{0}^{\mu}+\tilde{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}u_{0\nu}\right)\partial_{\mu}\psi-b\tilde{\theta}\rho_{1}=0.

(57)

Then, by manipulating the above equations, we obtain the equation of motion for a linear acoustic disturbance

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu})\psi=0,

(58)

where

given in the form
Anacleto:2011bv

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{tt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[(1-3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})c^{2}_{s}-(1+3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})v^{2}+2(\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{v})(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{v})-(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})\cdot\vec{v}],

(59)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{tj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})^{j}(c^{2}_{s}+1)-\left[2(1+2\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})-(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})\cdot\vec{v}\right]\frac{v^{j}}{2}+\frac{B^{j}}{2}(\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{v})+\frac{\theta^{j}}{2}(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{v}),

(60)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{it}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})^{i}(c^{2}_{s}+1)-\left[2(1+2\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})-(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})\cdot\vec{v}\right]\frac{v^{i}}{2}+\frac{B^{i}}{2}(\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{v})+\frac{\theta^{i}}{2}(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{v}),

(61)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(1+\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})(1+c^{2}_{s})-(1+\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})v^{2}-(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})\cdot\vec{v}]\delta^{ij}+(1+\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})v^{i}v^{j}.

(62)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(1-2\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})(1+c^{2}_{s})-(1+4\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})v^{2}]-3(\vec{\theta}\times\vec{E})\cdot\vec{v}+2(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{v})(\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{v}).

(63)

Setting
\theta=0
, the acoustic metric above reduces to the acoustic metric obtained in Ref.
Ge:2010wx
.

IV
Modified canonical acoustic black hole

In this section, we shall address the issue of Hawking temperature in the regime of low velocities
for the previous cases with further details. Now we consider an incompressible fluid with spherical symmetry. In this case the density
\rho
is a position independent quantity and the continuity equation implies that
v\sim\frac{1}{r^{2}}
. The sound speed is also a constant.
In the following we examine the Hawking radiation and entropy of the usual canonical acoustic black hole, as well as, in the Lorentz-violating and noncommutative background.

IV.1
Canonical Acoustic Metric

In this case the line element of the acoustic black hole is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(v_{r})d\tau^{2}+\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{f(v_{r})}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(64)

where the metric function,
f(v_{r})
takes the form

f(v_{r})={c^{2}-v^{2}_{r}}\quad\longrightarrow\quad f(r)=c^{2}_{s}\left(1-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\right).

(65)

Here we have defined

v_{r}=c_{s}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}
,
being
r_{h}
the radius of the event horizon.

In this case we compute the Hawking temperature using the following formula:

T_{H}=\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{4\pi}=\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{\pi r_{h}}.

(66)

By considering the above result for the Hawking temperature and applying the first law of thermodynamics, we can obtain the entropy (entanglement entropy
Anacleto:2019rfn
) of the acoustic black hole as follows

\displaystyle S=\int\frac{dE}{T}=\int\frac{dA}{4\pi r_{h}T_{H}}=\frac{A}{4c^{2}_{s}},

(67)

being
A=4\pi r^{2}_{h}
the horizon area of the canonical acoustic black hole.

IV.2
Canonical Acoustic Metric with Lorentz Violation

In the limit
c^{2}_{s}\ll 1
and
v^{2}\ll 1
can be written as a Schwarzschild metric type.
Thus, for
\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha=0
and up to an irrelevant position-independent factor,
we have
Anacleto:2010cr

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(v_{r})d\tau^{2}+\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\beta}_{-}\tilde{\beta}_{+}}f(v_{r})}dr^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\beta_{+}}}{\tilde{\beta}_{-}}}r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(68)

where

f(v_{r})=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\beta_{-}}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}}\left[\frac{c^{2}_{s}-\tilde{\beta}_{-}v^{2}_{r}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}\right]\rightarrow f(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde{\beta_{-}}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}}\left[\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{\tilde{\beta}_{+}}\left(1-\tilde{\beta}_{-}\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}\right)\right].

(69)

The Hawking temperature is given by

T_{H}=\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{4\pi}=\frac{c^{2}_{s}(1-\beta)^{3/2}}{(1+\beta)^{3/2}\pi r_{h}}=\frac{c^{2}_{s}(1-3\beta)}{\pi r_{h}}.

(70)

Therefore, the temperature is decreased when we vary the parameter
\beta
.
For
\beta=0
the usual result is obtained.
Hence, from the above temperature, we have the following result for the entropy of the acoustic black hole in the background violating Lorentz.

\displaystyle S=\frac{(1+3\beta)}{4c^{2}_{s}}A.

(71)

Now, for
\beta=0
and
\alpha\neq 0
, we find for
\alpha
sufficiently small we have up to first order

\displaystyle f(v_{r})=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}c^{2}_{s}-v^{2}_{r}]}{\sqrt{\tilde{\alpha}(1-2\alpha v_{r})}},

(72)

where
\tilde{\alpha}=1+\alpha
. For
v_{r}=c_{s}r^{2}_{h}/r^{2}
with
c_{s}=1
, the metric function becomes

f(r)\simeq\tilde{\alpha}^{-1/2}\left[\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{r_{h}^{4}}{r^{4}}\left(1+\alpha\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\alpha\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right].

(73)

In the present case there is a richer structure such as charged and rotating black holes.
The event horizon of the modified canonical acoustic black hole is obtained from the following equation:

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{r_{h}^{4}}{r^{4}}\left(1+\alpha\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\alpha\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{r^{2}}=0,

(74)

which can also be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle r^{6}+\alpha\,r^{2}_{h}\,r^{4}-\tilde{\alpha}^{-1\,}r^{4}_{h}\,r^{2}-\alpha\,r^{6}_{h}=0,

(75)

we can also write

\displaystyle r^{2}(r^{2}-r^{2}_{+})(r^{2}-r^{2}_{-})-\alpha\,r^{6}_{h}=0,

(76)

where

\displaystyle r^{2}_{\pm}=r^{2}_{h}\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\alpha}}}\right).

(77)

Now, arranging the above equation (
76
), we have

\displaystyle r^{2}=r^{2}_{+}+\frac{\alpha r^{6}_{h}}{r^{2}(r^{2}-r^{2}_{-})}.

(78)

Therefore, we can find the event horizon by solving the above equation perturbatively.
So, up to the first order in
\alpha
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{r}^{2}_{+}\approx r^{2}_{+}+\frac{\alpha r^{6}_{h}}{r^{2}_{+}(r^{2}_{+}-r^{2}_{-})}=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)r^{2}_{h}+\cdots.

(79)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{+}=r_{h}\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\cdots.

(80)

For the Hawking temperature, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{1}{\pi\tilde{r}_{+}}\left(1+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right).

(81)

In terms of
r_{h}
, we have

T_{H}=\left(1+\frac{7\alpha}{4}\right)\frac{1}{(\pi r_{h})}.

(82)

In this situation the temperature is increased when we vary the parameter
\alpha
.
For
\alpha=0
one recovers the usual result.

In this case for entropy, we find

\displaystyle S=\left(1-\frac{7\alpha}{4}\right)\frac{A}{4}.

(83)

IV.3
Noncommutative Canonical Acoustic Metric

The noncommutative acoustic metric can be written as a Schwarzschild metric type, up to an irrelevant
position-independent factor, in the nonrelativistic limit as follows
Anacleto:2011bv
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{{\cal F}}(v_{r})d\tau^{2}+\frac{[v_{r}^{2}\Gamma+\Sigma+\tilde{{\cal F}}(v_{r})\Lambda]}{\tilde{\cal F}(v_{r})}dr^{2}+\frac{r^{2}(d{\vartheta}^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta d\phi^{2})}{\sqrt{f}},

(84)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{{\cal F}}(v_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal F}(v_{r})}{\sqrt{f(v_{r})}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(v_{r})}}\left[(1-3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})c^{2}_{s}-(1+3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B})v^{2}_{r}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}{v}_{r}+2(\theta_{r}B_{r}v^{2}_{r})\right],

(85)

\displaystyle f(v_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-2\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-3\theta{\cal E}_{r}v_{r},

(86)

\displaystyle\Lambda(v_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}v_{r},

(87)

\displaystyle\Gamma(v_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+4\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-2\theta{\cal E}_{r}{v}_{r},

(88)

\displaystyle\Sigma(v_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\theta{\cal E}_{r}-({B}_{r}{v}_{r})\theta_{r}-({\theta}_{r}{v}_{r})B_{r}\right]v_{r},

(89)

being
\theta{\cal E}_{r}=\theta(\vec{n}\times\vec{E})_{r}
.
Now, by applying the relation
v_{r}=c_{s}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}
, where
r_{h}
is the radius of the event horizon and making
c_{s}=1
and so, the metric function of the noncommutative canonical acoustic black hole becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal F}(r)=\left[1-3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-(1+3\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-2\theta_{r}B_{r})\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right]\left[1-2\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}-3\theta{\cal E}_{r}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right]^{-1/2}.

(90)

Next, we will do our analysis considering the pure magnetic sector first and then we will investigate the pure electric sector.

Hence, for
\theta_{r}=0
,
\vec{\theta}\cdot\vec{B}=\theta_{3}B_{3}\neq 0
,
\theta{\cal E}_{r}=0
(or
E=0
) with small
\theta_{3}B_{3}
,

T_{H}=\frac{(1+3\theta_{3}B_{3})}{\sqrt{1-2\theta_{3}B_{3}}}\frac{1}{(\pi r_{h})}=\frac{\left(1+4\theta_{3}B_{3}\right)}{(\pi r_{h})}.

(91)

For
\theta=0
the usual result is obtained.
Here the temperature has its value increased when we vary the parameter
\theta
.

However, for the temperature in (
91
) we can find the entropy given by

\displaystyle S=\int\frac{dE}{T}=\int\frac{dA}{4\pi r_{h}T_{H}}=\frac{(1-4\theta_{3}B_{3})}{4}A,

(92)

where
A=4\pi r^{2}_{h}
is the horizon area of the canonical acoustic black hole.

At this point, we will consider the situation where
B=0
and
\theta{\cal E}_{r}\neq 0
.
So, from (
90
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal F}(r)=\left[1-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right]\left[1-3\theta{\cal E}_{r}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}\right]^{-1/2}.

(93)

For this metric the event horizon is obtained by solving the equation below

\displaystyle 1-\frac{r^{4}_{h}}{r^{4}}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{r^{2}}=0,

(94)

or

\displaystyle r^{4}-\theta{\cal E}_{r}{r^{2}_{h}}\,r^{2}-r^{4}_{h}=0.

(95)

So, solving the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle r_{+}=\left(1+\frac{\theta\mathcal{E}_{r}}{4}\right)r_{h}.

(96)

For the Hawking temperature, we find

\displaystyle T_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[1-\theta{\cal E}_{r}/2\right]}{\sqrt{1-3\theta{\cal E}_{r}}}\frac{1}{\pi r_{+}}=\frac{\left(1+\theta{\cal E}_{r}\right)}{\pi r_{+}},

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1+3\theta{\cal E}_{r}/4\right)}{\pi r_{h}}.

(98)

We also noticed that the temperature is increased when we vary the
\theta
parameter.

For entropy we have

\displaystyle S=\left(1-\theta{\cal E}_{r}\right)\frac{A}{4},

(99)

where
A=4\pi r_{+}
.

V
Quantum-corrected Hawking temperature and entropy

In this section, we implement quantum corrections in the Hawking temperature and entropy calculation arising from the generalized uncertainty principle and modified dispersion relations.

V.1
Result using GUP

At this point, we introduce quantum corrections via the generalized uncertainty principle (GUP) to determine the Hawking temperature and entropy of the canonical acoustic black hole in the Lorentz-violating and noncommutative background.
So, we will adopt the following GUP
Das:2008kaa
;
Das:2009hs
;
Ali:2011fa
;
Ali:2009zq
;
Casadio:2014pia
;
Kempf:1994su
;
Garay:1994en
;
Amelino-Camelia:2000cpa
;
Scardigli:1999jh
;
Scardigli:2003kr
;
Scardigli:2014qka
;
Scardigli:2016pjs

\displaystyle\Delta x\Delta p\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda l_{p}}{\hbar}\Delta p+\frac{\lambda^{2}l^{2}_{p}}{\hbar^{2}}(\Delta p)^{2}\right),

(100)

where
\alpha
is a dimensionless positive parameter and
l_{p}
is the Planck length.

In sequence, without loss of generality, we will adopt the natural units
G=c=k_{B}=\hbar=l_{p}=1
and by
assuming that
\Delta p\sim E

and following the steps performed in
Anacleto:2019rfn
we can obtain the following relation for the corrected energy of the black hole

\displaystyle E_{gup}\geq E\left[1-\frac{\lambda}{2(\Delta x)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}+\cdots\right].

(101)

Thus, applying the tunneling formalism using the Hamilton-Jacobi method, we have the following result for the probability of tunneling with corrected energy
E_{gup}
given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\simeq\exp[-2{\rm Im}({\cal I})]=\exp\left[\frac{-4{\pi}E_{gup}}{\kappa}\right],

(102)

where
\kappa
is the surface gravity.
Comparing with the Boltzmann factor,
e^{-E/T}
, we obtain the following result for the Hawking temperature with quantum corrections

\displaystyle T\leq{T}_{H}\left[1-\frac{\lambda}{2(\Delta x)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}+\cdots\right]^{-1}.

(103)

So, by applying it to temperature (
66
), we have the following result

\displaystyle T=\frac{c^{2}_{s}}{\pi\left[\displaystyle r_{h}-\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8r_{h}}+\cdots\right]}.

(104)

Therefore, when
r_{h}=0
the singularity is removed and the temperature is now zero.
Next, we analyze the effect of GUP in the Lorentz-violating and noncommutative cases.

For this case we can calculate the entropy which is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{A}{4c^{2}_{s}}-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\lambda\sqrt{A}}{4c^{2}_{s}}+\frac{\pi\lambda^{2}\ln A}{8c^{2}_{s}}+\cdots.

(105)

So due to the GUP we get a logarithmic correction term for the entropy.

V.1.1
Violation-Lorentz Case

In the situation where
\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha=0
, the corrected temperature due to GUP is

\displaystyle T={T}_{H}\left[1-\frac{\lambda}{4r_{h}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8r^{2}_{h}}+\cdots\right]^{-1}.

(106)

where

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{(1-3\beta)}{\pi r_{h}}.

(107)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle T=\frac{(1-3\beta)}{\pi\left[\displaystyle r_{h}-\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8r_{h}}+\cdots\right]}.

(108)

Note that when
r_{h}\rightarrow 0
the Hawking temperature tends to zero,
T\rightarrow 0
.
In the absence of the GUP the temperature,
T_{H}
, diverges when
r_{h}=0
. Therefore, we observe that the GUP has the effect of removing the singularity at
r_{h}=0
in the Hawking temperature of the acoustic black hole.

Now computing the entropy, we find

\displaystyle S=\left(1+3\beta\right)\left[\frac{A}{4}-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\lambda\sqrt{A}}{4}+\frac{\pi\lambda^{2}\ln A}{8}+\cdots\right].

(109)

For
\beta=0
and
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle T=\frac{(1+3\alpha/2)}{\pi\left[\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{+}-\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8\tilde{r}_{+}}+\cdots\right]}.

(110)

In terms of
r_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle T=\frac{(1+3\alpha/2)}{\pi\left[\displaystyle{r}_{h}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)-\frac{\lambda}{4}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8{r}_{h}}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)+\cdots\right]}.

(111)

In this situation, we can also verify the effect of the GUP on the temperature that goes to zero
when
r_{h}\rightarrow 0

(\tilde{r}_{+}\rightarrow 0)
.
In addition, we note that in both cases the Hawking temperature reaches a maximum value before going to zero,
as we can see in Fig.

VI
conclusions

In summary, in this work, we have reviewed the steps to generate relativistic acoustic metrics in the Lorentz-violating
and noncommutative background.
In particular, we have considered the modified canonical acoustic metric due to the contribution of terms violating Lorentz symmetry and noncommutativity; to examine Hawking radiation and entropy.
Moreover, we have verified, in the calculation of the Hawking temperature, that due to the presence of the GUP and the modified dispersion relation, the singularity is removed. In addition, we have shown that in these cases, the temperature reaches a maximum value and then vanishes when the horizon radius goes to zero.
Furthermore, entropy has been computed, and we show that logarithmic correction terms are generated due to the GUP and also the modified dispersion relation.
Therefore, the presented results show a behavior similar to what happens in the case of the Schwarzschild black hole.

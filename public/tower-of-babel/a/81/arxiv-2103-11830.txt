High-Dimensional Covariance Shrinkage for Signal Detection
Benjamin D. Robinson, Robert Malinas, Alfred O. Hero III

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the problem of determining the presence of a given signal in a high-dimensional observation with unknown covariance matrix by using an adaptive matched filter. Traditionally such filters are formed from the sample covariance matrix of some given training data, but, as is well-known, the performance of such filters is poor when the number of training data n n is not much larger than the data dimension p p . We thus seek a covariance estimator to replace sample covariance. To account for the fact that n n and p p may be of comparable size, we adopt the “large-dimensional asymptotic model” in which n n and p p go to infinity in a fixed ratio. Under this assumption, we identify a covariance estimator that is asymptotically detection-theoretic optimal within a general shrinkage class inspired by C. Stein, and we give consistent estimates for conditional false-alarm and detection rate of the corresponding adaptive matched filter.



I
Introduction

In this paper we consider the problem of determining signal presence in a high-dimensional observation with unknown covariance matrix.
To take advantage of the observation’s high-dimensionality, we embed the problem
in a sequence of hypothesis testing problems indexed by
n
that are of non-decreasing
dimension
p_{n}
. We assume that the signal
\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times 1}
is known modulo scalar multiplication
and that the test observation
y_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times 1}
is given,
but
that the only other data available in the
n^{\text{th}}

problem are
n
training data
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times 1}
such that
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},(y_{n}-\mathbb{E}y_{n})
are i.i.d.
This formulation is relevant to high-dimensional detection problems that arise in radar/sonar beamforming
[
38
]
, climatology
[
33
]
, and hyperspectral imaging
[
45
]
, among other places.

The simplest possibility is that
p_{n}
is constant,
\mu_{n}=\mu
is constant, and
y_{n}=y
is independent of
n
and distributed as a multivariate Gaussian with Hermitian positive-definite covariance matrix
R
. In this case, to goal is to determine whether
y
has mean
a\mu
for
a=0
(signal absent) or
a\neq 0
(signal present). More precisely, we wish to decide between the hypotheses

\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{0}:&y=d\\
\mathcal{H}_{1}:&y=a\mu+d,\qquad(a\neq 0)\end{cases}

where
d
is a mean-zero Gaussian random vector with covariance matrix
R
.
If
R
is known, a popular test is the Generalized Likelihood Ratio (GLR) test
[
35
, Equation 7]
:
|T(\mu,R,y)|^{2}\gtrless t
, where

T(\mu,R,y)=\mu^{\prime}R^{-1}y/(\mu^{\prime}R^{-1}\mu)^{1/2}

(1)

and
(\cdot)^{\prime}
denotes the (conjugate) transpose.
Suppose more realistically that the covariance matrix
R

is unknown but
n\gg p_{n}
. In this case, a popular replacement for the GLR test is the traditional Adaptive Matched Filter (AMF) test
[
35
, Equation 8]
:
|T(\mu,S_{n},y)|^{2}\gtrless t
, where

S_{n}
is the
p_{n}\times p_{n}
sample covariance matrix of the training data:

S_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}x_{j}^{\prime}.

This detector is asymptotically optimal in the sense that it attains the power of the GLR test in the limit as
n\to\infty
. In other words, for any fixed conditional false-alarm rate
\Pr[|T(\mu,S_{n},y)|^{2}>t\mid\mathcal{H}_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
, the conditional detection rate
\Pr[|T(\mu,S_{n},y)|^{2}>t\mid\mathcal{H}_{1},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
converges almost surely to the detection rate of the GLR test. (This follows, for example, from
[
37
, Section 2]
and the law of large numbers applied to
S_{n}
.)

In the modern statistical context, though, the assumption that
n\gg p_{n}
is often not realistic
[
20
]
. Instead, we follow many authors in assuming the large-dimensional asymptotic limit, in which
n,p_{n}\to\infty
and
p_{n}/n
converges to a fixed constant
\gamma\in(0,\infty)

[
50
,
10
,
15
]
.
This assumption permits any nontrivial limiting ratio of
p_{n}
and
n
—even ones that correspond to the “sample-starved” case (
n<p_{n}
), which is important in applications such as linear discriminant analysis
[
19
]
, principal component analysis
[
20
]
, space-time adaptive processing
[
48
]
, and other applications such as financial time-series analysis
[
24
]
.
In this context, we reformulate the hypothesis test above as a sequence of hypothesis tests of increasing dimension:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n}:&y_{n}=d_{n}\\
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{n}:&y_{n}=a\mu_{n}+d_{n},\qquad(a\neq 0),\end{cases}

(2)

where
d_{n}
has
p_{n}\times p_{n}
covariance matrix
R_{n}
.
The goal is, then, to asymptotically optimize and analyze the “plug-in” AMF test
|T(\mu_{n},\hat{R}_{n},y_{n})|^{2}\gtrless t
, where
\hat{R}_{n}
is some estimator of
R_{n}
. More precisely, we would like to asymptotically analyze the conditional probability of detection

\displaystyle p_{1}^{n}(\hat{R}_{n},t)

\displaystyle:=\Pr[|T(\mu_{n},\hat{R}_{n},y_{n})|^{2}>t\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}^{n},\mu_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]

and the conditional probability of false-alarm

\displaystyle p_{0}^{n}(\hat{R}_{n},t)

\displaystyle:=\Pr[|T(\mu_{n},\hat{R}_{n},y_{n})|^{2}>t\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n},\mu_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}],

and asymptotically maximize the former subject to a constraint on the latter.
(Here, we condition on
\mu_{n}
because we allow the possibility that
\mu_{n}
may be governed by a probability distribution.) It turns out that simply choosing
\hat{R}_{n}=S_{n}
can be highly sub-optimal due to the dispersed nature of the eigenvalues of

.

Stein
[
42
,
43
]
suggested that for many applications a significant improvement over

can be obtained by “eigenvalue shrinkage”: the process of modifying
S_{n}
’s eigenvalues but keeping its eigen-space decomposition.
This could be as simple as the “diagonal loading” estimators of
[
23
,
17
,
46
]
, which are of the form
\alpha(x_{1},\dots x_{n})S_{n}
plus the product of
\beta(x_{1},\dots x_{n})
and the
p_{n}\times p_{n}
identity matrix
I_{p_{n}}
for some scalar-valued functions
\alpha,\beta>0
, but could also involve modifying the eigenvalues in a much more complicated, nonlinear way, as in
[
11
]
and
[
25
]
. Shrinkage estimators have been used in Tikhonov regression
[
46
]
and in signal processing
[
17
,
36
,
1
,
8
,
9
]
, mathematical finance
[
23
,
25
]
, and many other fields
[
49
,
31
,
18
,
34
,
30
,
13
,
12
,
3
,
21
,
26
,
28
]
.

Many authors working on high-dimensional statistical problems have considered the so-called
spiked model

[
20
]
. This is the assumption that the population covariance’s eigenvalues are all equal except for finitely many “spikes” that are larger. The advantage of this model is that properties of
S_{n}
, such as its eigenvalue- and eigenvector biases have simple asymptotic forms that hold almost surely
[
29
]
—forms which are useful in areas such as principle component analysis.
Recently, several authors have applied these forms to construct shrinkage estimators that are in a sense asymptotically optimal
[
11
,
37
]
.
But the spiked assumption does not always hold in detection-theoretic applications
[
39
]
, in which case these estimators are less relevant.

In this paper, we consider the case where the spiked assumption may not hold. In this case, Ledoit and Wolf describe a shrinkage estimator they call
\tilde{S}_{n}
which
is shown to asymptotically minimize a detection-theoretically relevant loss function called MV loss among a reasonable class of estimators
[
25
, Corollary 4.1]
.
Our main accomplishments are twofold. First, we show, under fairly general assumptions, that Ledoit and Wolf’s estimator is asymptotically optimal among shrinkage estimators when plugged into the AMF. Second, we give consistent estimates (Theorem
1
(i) and (ii)) for the resulting AMF, enabling the practitioner to better understand its performance. Such results exist in the spiked case
[
37
, Theorems 3 and 4]
, but as far as we know, no one has yet successfully extended them to the nonspiked case until now.

II
Assumptions and Main Result

Throughout this paper
\mathbf{1}
will denote the indicator function, and we make the following assumptions:
X_{n}=R_{n}^{1/2}W_{n}
,
where

(H1)

The components of
W_{n}
are i.i.d. real (or complex) random variables with zero
mean, unit variance, and have
16^{\text{th}}
absolute central moment
bounded by a constant
C
independent of
n
and
p_{n}
;

(H2)

The population covariance matrix
R_{n}
is a nonrandom
p_{n}\times p_{n}

Hermitian positive definite matrix independent of
W_{n}
;

(H3)

p_{n}/n\to\gamma\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
as
n\to\infty
;

(H4)

\tau_{n1}\leq\tau_{n2}\leq\dots\leq\tau_{np_{n}}
is a system of eigenvalues of
R_{n}
, and
the empirical spectral distribution function (e.s.d.)
of the population covariance given by
H_{n}(\tau)=p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{[\tau_{nj},\infty)}(\tau)

converges a.s. to a nonrandom limit
H(\tau)
at all points of continuity of
H
;

(H5)

The support of
H
is a finite union of compact intervals and there exists a compact interval
[\underline{T},\overline{T}]\subset(0,\infty)
such that the eigenvalues
\{\tau_{nj}\}_{j=1}^{p_{n}}
are all contained in this interval for large enough
n
. Further, we assume either
T_{0}=0
or
T_{0}
is some known positive number for which
T_{0}\leq\underline{T}
.

Assumptions (H1)-(H5) come from
[
22
,
25
]
. The
16^{\text{th}}
-moment assumption is likely excessive: Ledoit and Wolf have performed simulations that indicate that a
4^{\text{th}}
-moment assumption suffices for much of what follows
[
25
]
.
Assumption (H3), introduced earlier, implies that
p_{n}\to\infty
whenever
n\to\infty
, and so whenever we write
n\to\infty
it will be implied that
p_{n}\to\infty
as well. The term “asymptotically”
will always refer to the limit as
n\to\infty
(and

).

Formally, we define a shrinkage estimator as follows. Throughout this paper
X_{n}=[x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n}]
will be the

matrix formed from
n
i.i.d. columns with mean 0 and covariance

,

will be the
p_{n}\times p_{n}
sample covariance matrix,

is the matrix of non-decreasing eigenvalues of

, and

will be a random orthogonal (unitary) matrix such that
S_{n}=U_{n}\Lambda_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}
. (If
n<p_{n}
, for example, then
U_{n}
is non-unique.)

Definition 1
.

We say that

\hat{R}_{n}:\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times n}\to\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times p_{n}}
, taking values in the cone of
p_{n}\times p_{n}
Hermitian positive-definite matrices, is a
shrinkage estimator
if and only if
\hat{R}_{n}=U_{n}\hat{D}_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}
, where
U_{n}
is a (possibly random) matrix of column eigenvectors of
S_{n}
.

For ease of analysis, we will consider only a certain type of shrinkage estimators, described by the following definition.

Definition 2
.

Let
\hat{R}_{n}=U_{n}\hat{D}_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}
be a shrinkage estimator. Then
\hat{R}_{n}
is
asymptotically admissible
if

(i)

\hat{D}_{n}
is a random diagonal matrix such that the random variables
\limsup_{n}\left\|\hat{D}_{n}\right\|
and
\limsup_{n}\left\|\hat{D}_{n}^{-1}\right\|
are almost surely bounded, where
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|
denotes the spectral norm, and

(ii)

(
Limiting shrinkage function
) Letting
\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}
denote convergence in probability as
n,p_{n}\to\infty
, there exists a nonrandom continuous function
\hat{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

p_{n}^{-1}\left\|\hat{R}_{n}-U_{n}\hat{\delta}(\Lambda_{n})U_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{\text{Fro}}^{2}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0.

(3)

The continuity mentioned in (ii) is desirable since it ensures that small perturbations to eigenvalues (such as numerical errors) do not overly affect the estimator.
We also wish to consider continuous shrinkage estimators because they slightly generalize the class of shrinkage estimators satisfying
[
25
, Assumption 4]
, and newly include such famous estimators such as the Fast Maximum Likelihood (FML) estimator of
[
44
]
and Anderson’s estimator
[
2
]
. We note that by (i) and (ii), we may without loss of generality assume
\hat{\delta}
is bounded above and below by finite nonzero numbers, and in what follows we will always do so.

Back to the context of signal detection, the detectors we consider in this paper are threshold tests on generalized AMFs: more precisely, on filters of the form
|T(\mu_{n},\hat{R}_{n},y_{n})|^{2}
, where
\hat{R}_{n}
is allowed to be any shrinkage estimator, as opposed to just the sample covariance matrix.
The performance of such detectors depends in general on
\mu_{n}
,
and may be quite poor for some values of
\mu_{n}
. As a result, we
assume
\mu_{n}
is distributed uniformly at random on the unit sphere and
will seek to make statements about the such detectors’ performance conditioned on
\mu_{n}
that
hold with high probability over the sphere. We encode
this information as another assumption

(H6)

\mu_{n}
is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}}

and is independent of
X_{n}
.

For our main result we need a couple of definitions regarding asymptotic comparisons of random variables.

Definition 3
.

Suppose
a_{n}
and

are real random variables. Then

(i)

We say

is

(ii)

We say that

is
i.p. asymptotically equivalent to

, written

(i.p.), if both
a_{n}\gtrsim b_{n}
and
b_{n}\gtrsim a_{n}
(i.p.)

This paper is mainly concerned with detection-theoretic properties of a particular shrinkage estimator devised by

25
]
and referred to as

from here on. We reproduce the definition here for convenience.

Definition 4

Let
\lambda_{n1}\leq\lambda_{n2}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{np_{n}}
be the eigenvalues of

, and let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n}=(\lambda_{n1},\dots\lambda_{np_{n}})

\displaystyle\times\left[1-\frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{\lambda_{ni}-\lambda_{nj}}{h_{nj}}\right)^{2}\right]\left.\log\left|\frac{\sqrt{5}h_{nj}-\lambda_{ni}+\lambda_{nj}}{\sqrt{5}h_{nj}+\lambda_{ni}-\lambda_{nj}}\right|\right\}

\sum_{j=[p-n]^{+}+1}^{p}\frac{3}{4\sqrt{5}h_{nj}}\left[1-\frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{\lambda_{ni}-\lambda_{nj}}{h_{nj}}\right)^{2}\right]^{+},

where the summands are defined to be zero when

\zeta_{nj}=\pi\min\{n,p_{n}\}^{-1}(a(\lambda_{nj},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n})+\sqrt{-1}b(\lambda_{nj},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n})),

\tilde{d}_{nj}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{nj}}{|1-p_{n}/n-p_{n}/n\lambda_{nj}\zeta_{nj}|^{2}},&\text{if $\lambda_{nj}>0$}\\
\frac{1}{\pi(p_{n}/n-1)a(0,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n})/n},&\text{if $\lambda_{nj}=0$}\end{cases}

(4)

Define
\hat{\delta}_{nj}
by

\begin{cases}\lambda_{np_{n}}/(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2},&\text{if $\tilde{d}_{nj}>\lambda_{np_{n}}/(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}$}\\
T_{0},&\text{if $\tilde{d}_{nj}<T_{0}$}\\
\tilde{d}_{nj},&\text{else},\end{cases}

where

is defined in (H5) and

.
Then the
Ledoit-Wolf nonlinear estimator

Code implementing the above estimator, modulo a few modifications, can be found in the supplement to

Our main theorem, below, regards optimal shrinkage estimation for signal detection.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose (H1)-(H6) hold. Let
Z
be an
\mathbb{F}
-valued random variable with standard real (complex) normal distribution. Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then if
\hat{R}_{n}
is the Ledoit-Wolf nonlinear estimator, the following hold as
n\to\infty
,
p_{n}\to\infty
, and
p_{n}/n\to\gamma
.

(i)

(False-alarm rate)
p_{0}^{n}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
converges in probability to
\Pr[|Z|^{2}>t]
.

(ii)

(Detection rate)
p_{1}^{n}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to the random variable

\Pr\left[\left|Z+a(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\hat{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}\right|^{2}>t\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}\right].

(iii)

(Optimality) If
\tilde{R}_{n}
be any asymptotically admissible shrinkage estimator, and
t_{n}
and
t
are real numbers satisfying the asymptotic constraint
p^{n}_{0}(\tilde{R}_{n},t_{n})\lesssim p^{n}_{0}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
(i.p.), we have
p^{n}_{1}(\tilde{R}_{n},t_{n})\lesssim p^{n}_{1}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
(i.p.).

Remark 1
.

The theorem above can be easily extended to the case of real scalars. The complex case is relevant to radar beamforming, whereas the real case is relevant to sonar beamforming and hyperspectral signature detection.

Proofs

Throughout this section and the rest of the paper, we assume (H1)-(H6), that

is the Ledoit-Wolf nonlinear estimator, and that

Our first proposition provides an approximation that will be useful throughout this paper. First we need a lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mu
be a random column vector that is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere
S^{p-1}
in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Let also
A
be a real
p\times p
matrix. Then there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
p
and
A
such that for all
\epsilon>0
we have

\Pr\left[\left|\mu^{\prime}A\mu-\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{tr}A\right|\geq\epsilon\right]\leq\exp\left(-cp\epsilon^{2}/\left\|A\right\|^{2}\right).

Proof.

By
[
47
, Theorem 5.1.4]
if
f
is Lipschitz on the
\sqrt{p}
-sphere (
\sqrt{p}S^{p-1}
) and
t>0
, there exists
c^{\prime}>0
independent of
f
and
t
such that

\Pr\left[\left|f(u)-\mathbb{E}f(u)\right|\geq t\right]\leq\exp(-c^{\prime}t^{2}/L_{f}^{2}),

where
u
is uniformly distributed on
\sqrt{p}S^{p-1}
and
L_{f}
is a Lipschitz constant of
f
on
\sqrt{p}S^{p-1}
. By the rotation-invariance of the distribution of
u
, we have that
\mathbb{E}[uu^{\prime}]=I_{p}
, the
p\times p
identity matrix.
Thus,

\mathbb{E}u^{\prime}Au=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{tr}(Auu^{\prime})=\mathrm{tr}\left(A\mathbb{E}[uu^{\prime}]\right)=\mathrm{tr}A.

Further, since the gradient of
f(u)=u^{\prime}Au
is
2Au
, a Lipschitz constant of
f
is easily seen to be
2\left\|A\right\|\left\|u\right\|
, which is equal to
2\sqrt{p}\left\|A\right\|
on the sphere in question. Taking
\mu=u/\sqrt{p}
, then, we get

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\left|\mu^{\prime}A\mu-\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{tr}A\right|\geq\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\left|u^{\prime}Au-\mathrm{tr}A\right|\geq p\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\left|f(u)-\mathbb{E}f(u)\right|\geq p\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-c^{\prime}(p\epsilon)^{2}/(2\sqrt{p}\left\|A\right\|)^{2}\right).

The result follows by taking
c=c^{\prime}/4
.
∎

Proposition 1
.

Let
A_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{n}\times p_{n}}
be a sequence of independent Hermitian positive-definite random matrices such that the random variable given by the
\limsup
of

Furthermore, assume that the sequences

and

are independent.
Then

\left|\mu_{n}^{\prime}A_{n}\mu_{n}-\frac{1}{p_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(A_{n})\right|\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}0.

Proof.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, it suffices to prove that

\Pr\left[\left|\mu^{\prime}_{n}A_{n}\mu_{n}-\frac{1}{p_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(A_{n})\right|\geq\epsilon\right]

(5)

is summable for every
\epsilon>0
.
By the definition of conditional probability, the above probability is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\Pr\left[\left.\left|\mu^{\prime}_{n}A_{n}\mu_{n}-\frac{1}{p_{n}}\mathrm{tr}(A_{n})\right|\geq\epsilon\ \right|A_{n}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\exp(-cp_{n}\epsilon^{2}/\left\|A_{n}\right\|^{2})\right).

(6)

Let
\Omega_{n}=\{\left\|A_{n}\right\|>C\}
where

is greater than the essential sup of the
\limsup
of

\Pr(\Omega_{n})+\exp(-cp_{n}\epsilon^{2}/C^{2}).

Since the second term above is summable, it suffices to show that
\Pr(\Omega_{n})
is summable.

For this, we first note that
\Pr(\limsup_{n}\Omega_{n})=0
since the
\limsup
of
\left\|A_{n}\right\|
is almost surely less than or equal to

. The second Borel-Cantelli lemma (see
[
16
, Proposition 10.10.b]
), together with the fact that the

’s are independent, then implies that

is summable, as desired.

, we have

\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})-\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{-2}R_{n})}{\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1})}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}0

(ii)

With
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n}):=\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{n}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n}}{(\mu^{\prime}_{n}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}}
, we have

\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})-\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1})}{\sqrt{p}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{-2}R_{n})^{1/2}}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}0.

When
y_{n}
is Gaussian, the conditional detection probability
p_{1}^{n}(\tilde{R}_{n},t)
is monotonic in
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})

[
32
]
. This motivates the question of how large
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})
can be.

To answer this question, we must use some results from random matrix theory. Throughout the rest of this document we will assume that
\lambda_{n1},\lambda_{n2},\dots,\lambda_{np_{n}}
are the eigenvalues of
S_{n}
in increasing order. Let
F_{n}(x)=p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda_{nj},\infty)}(x)
. The Marčenko-Pastur theorem
[
27
,
40
]
states that under Assumptions (H1)-(H5) of this paper, there exists a Borel measure
F
such that, almost surely,
F_{n}
converges weakly to
F
. Further,
E:=\mathrm{supp}(F)
is a disjoint union of finitely many compact intervals
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Almost sure weak convergence means
\int h\,dF_{n}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\int h\,dF
for any function
h
that is bounded and continuous on
E
, which by the portmanteau theorem
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
means that
F_{n}(I)\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}F(I)
for every interval with
F(\partial I)=0
. The Stieltijes transform of a Borel measure
G
is given for
z
in the upper half plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}
as

m_{G}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dG(t)}{t-z}.

It is well known that
m_{G}
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
. It is also well known that a sequence of Borel measures
G_{n}
converges weakly to
G
if and only if
m_{G_{n}}(z)
converges pointwise to
m_{G}(z)
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, relating the latter notion to the relatively simple notion of pointwise convergence of analytic functions. Further, if
F
is as above and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
the limit
\breve{m}_{F}(\lambda)=\lim_{z\to\lambda}m_{F}(z)
exists and is continuous
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
. The following is an elaboration of the statement and proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 4

(An upper limit for continuous shrinkage estimators)
.

Assume (H1)-(H6) hold and in particular that
\gamma=\lim p_{n}/n
. Suppose
\tilde{R}_{n}=U_{n}\tilde{D}_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}
is a asymptotically admissible shrinkage estimator. Then

\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})\lesssim\left(\int(1/\delta)\,dF\right)^{1/2},\qquad\mathrm{(i.p.)}

(7)

where
\delta
is the extended real-valued function given by

\delta(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda}{|1-\gamma-\gamma\lambda\breve{m}_{F}(\lambda)|^{2}},&\textrm{if $\lambda>0$}\\
\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\breve{m}_{F}(0)},&\text{if $\lambda=0$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

First,
\delta
is bounded on
E^{o}
—the interior of
E
. To see this, suppose for a contradiction there is
\epsilon>0
,
\lambda\in E^{o}
such that
\delta(\lambda)\geq\overline{T}+\epsilon
, where
\overline{T}
is the bound specified in (H5). By continuity of
\delta
on
E
, there is a closed interval
I
not containing zero such that every element of
\delta(I)
exceeds
\overline{T}+\epsilon/2
,. Let
d^{*}_{nj}:=u_{nj}^{\prime}R_{n}u_{nj}
, where
u_{nj}
is the
j^{\text{th}}
column vector of
U_{n}
.
We have

\displaystyle\overline{T}F_{n}(I)

\displaystyle\geq p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}\in I}d^{*}_{nj}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\int_{I}\delta\,dF

\displaystyle\geq(\overline{T}+\epsilon/2)F(I),

where the convergence follows from
[
22
, Theorem 1.4]
. Taking the limit implies
\lambda\notin E
, a contradiction of the initial assumption that
\delta(\lambda)\geq\overline{T}+\epsilon
. A similar argument applies to
\lambda=0
, the boundary of
E
, and obtaining the lower bound of
\underline{T}
for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Assume
\tilde{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous function such that

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}((\tilde{D}_{n})_{jj}-\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj}))^{2}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0,

(8)

as in Definition
2
(ii). (Recall that we assume
\tilde{\delta}
is bounded above and below by finite nonzero constants.) It follows from Cauchy-Schwarz and (
8
) that

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}|(\tilde{D}_{n})_{jj}-\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj})|\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0.

(9)

By Proposition
3
(ii) the numerator of
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})
is a.s. asymptotically equal to to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}(\tilde{D}_{n})_{jj}^{-1}.

(10)

Using the cyclic permutation property of trace, the square of the denominator of
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})
is a.s. asymptotically equal to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}(\tilde{D}_{n})_{jj}^{-2}d^{*}_{nj},

(11)

where
d^{*}_{nj}
is as before.
By (
9
), (
10
) is i.p. asymptotically equal to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj})^{-1}

(12)

and (
11
) is i.p. asymptotically equal to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj})^{-2}d^{*}_{nj}.

(13)

Let
G_{n}
be the random Borel measure given by
G_{n}(E)=p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j:\lambda_{nj}\in E}d^{*}_{nj}
. By
[
22
, Theorem 1.4]
,
G_{n}
converges weakly almost surely to the Borel measure
G
given by
dG=\delta\,dF
.
If
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded and continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and
U
is a continuity set of
G
, a generalization of the portmanteau theorem says that

\int_{U}h\,dG_{n}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\int_{U}h\,dG.

(14)

Using
h=\tilde{\delta}^{-2}
and
U=\mathbb{R}
gives that (
13
) converges a.s. to

\int\tilde{\delta}^{-2}\delta\,dF.

(15)

In addition another application of portmanteau yields that (
12
) converges a.s. to

\int(1/\tilde{\delta})\,dF.

Now, applying Cauchy-Schwarz to
\int\left((1/\tilde{\delta})\delta^{1/2}\right)\delta^{-1/2}\,dF
yields

\left(\int(1/\tilde{\delta})\,dF\right)^{2}\leq\int\tilde{\delta}^{-2}\delta\,dF\int(1/\delta)\,dF.

Putting it all together

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\int(1/\tilde{\delta})\,dF}{\left(\int\tilde{\delta}^{-2}\delta\,dF\right)^{1/2}}\qquad\text{(i.p.)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int(1/\delta)\,dF\right)^{1/2},

with equality if and only if
\tilde{\delta}
is a constant times
\delta
on
E
.
∎

Motivated by the above, we make the following definition.

Definition 5
.

If
\tilde{R}_{n}=(U_{n},\tilde{D}_{n})
is a shrinkage estimator with

p_{n}^{-1}\left\|\tilde{D}_{n}-c\delta(\Lambda_{n})\right\|_{\text{Fro}}^{2}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0

for some
c>0
then we say
\tilde{R}_{n}
is
asymptotically shrinkage-optimal
. In other words,
\tilde{R}_{n}
is asymptotically shrinkage-optimal if and only if its limiting shrinkage function is
c\delta
.
If
c=1
, we say that
\tilde{R}_{n}
is
normalized
.

Our next result describes one of the main properties of the Ledoit-Wolf nonlinear estimator
\hat{R}_{n}
.

Proposition 5
.

\hat{R}_{n}
is a normalized asymptotically shrinkage-optimal estimator.

Proof.

We assume
\gamma>1
. (The case where
\gamma<1
is similar.)
Showing that
\hat{R}_{n}
satisfies Definitions
2
(i) and (ii) is trivial. We thus seek a continuous function
\tilde{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that satisfies Definition
2
(iii) and agrees with
\delta
on
E
, the support of
F
. It turns out any continuous bounded function that is equal to
\delta
on
E
will do. To show this, we split
p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}(\hat{\delta}_{nj}-\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj}))^{2}
into

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:\\
\lambda_{nj}\in E^{c}\end{subarray}}(\hat{\delta}_{nj}-\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj}))^{2}

(16)

and

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j:\\
\lambda_{nj}\in E\end{subarray}}(\hat{\delta}_{nj}-\tilde{\delta}(\lambda_{nj}))^{2}

(17)

Consider (
16
). Let
\epsilon=(1/2)\underline{T}(1-\sqrt{1/\gamma})^{2}
. We claim that
\epsilon
is an almost sure lower bound for the liminf of the smallest nonzero sample eigenvalue. The inverse of this eigenvalue is
\left\|(W_{n}^{\prime}R_{n}W_{n})^{-1}\right\|
, which is bounded above by
\underline{T}^{-1}\left\|(W_{n}^{\prime}W_{n})^{-1}\right\|
. By
[
5
]
, the latter has almost sure limit of
2\epsilon
. Equation (
16
) then becomes

\displaystyle\lesssim CF_{n}(E^{c}\cap(\epsilon,\infty)),

for some constant
C
. But this converges to zero since
E^{c}\cap(\epsilon,\infty)
is a continuity set of
F
.

For (
17
), we use the result
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]
, which states that

\sup_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}\in E}|\tilde{d}_{nj}-\delta(\lambda_{nj})|\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0.

(18)

Since
\delta
is bounded above and below on
E
by the spectral upper and lower bounds
\left\|R_{n}\right\|
and
\underline{T}
(as discussed in Proposition
4
), and since
\left\|R_{n}\right\|\lesssim\lambda_{np_{n}}/(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}
almost surely (see
[
5
]
), (
18
) holds equally well if the
\tilde{d}_{nj}
’s are replaced by the
\hat{\delta}_{nj}
’s:

\sup_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}\in E}|\hat{\delta}_{nj}-\delta(\lambda_{nj})|\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}0.

(19)

The result follows.

∎

Before we prove our main theorem, we need a couple of final lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\tilde{R}_{n}
is a normalized asymptotically shrinkage-optimal estimator. Then

(i)

\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\to}1

(ii)

\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})
is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
\left(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n}\right)^{1/2}

Proof.

We consider here the case where
\gamma>1
. (The case where
\gamma<1
is similar.) Let
\epsilon
be as in the proof of Proposition
5
.

We first establish that

\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n}

(20)

converges i.p. to
\int\delta^{-1}\,dF
. Let
\tilde{R}_{n}=(U_{n},\tilde{D}_{n})
with limiting shrinkage function
\delta
. By Proposition
2
(ii), (
20
) is a.s. asymptotically equivalent to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}(\tilde{D}_{n})_{jj}^{-2}d^{*}_{nj},

where
d^{*}_{nj}
is as before. By Definition
2
(iii), this is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{n}}\delta(\lambda_{nj})^{-2}d^{*}_{nj},

We divide the above into two sums:

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}\geq\epsilon}\delta(\lambda_{nj})^{-2}d^{*}_{nj}

(21)

and

p_{n}^{-1}\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}<\epsilon}\delta(\lambda_{nj})^{-2}d^{*}_{nj}.

(22)

Consider the first expression. Since
U=[\epsilon,\max E]
is a continuity set of the measure
G
from Proposition
4
, (
14
) implies that (
21
) converges almost surely to
\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\delta^{-1}\,dF
.

Consider the expression (
22
). Since
\epsilon
is an almost sure lower bound on the liminf of the smallest nonzero sample eigenvalue, there is, almost surely, a number
N
such that
n>N
implies this eigenvalue is greater than
\epsilon
. Assuming
n
exceeds this number
N
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}<\epsilon}\delta(\lambda_{nj})^{-2}d^{*}_{nj}

\displaystyle=\delta(0)^{-2}\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{nj}<\epsilon}d^{*}_{nj}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}\delta(0)^{-2}\int_{-\infty}^{\epsilon}\delta\,dF

\displaystyle=\delta(0)^{-1}F(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\epsilon}(1/\delta)\,dF.

where the third-to-last relation follows from
[
25
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Putting the two together yields (
20
) is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
\int(1/\delta)\,dF
, as claimed.

Similarly,
\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n}
is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
\int(1/\delta)\,dF
. This proves (i). For (ii), it follows from what we have shown so far that
\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})
is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
\left(\int(1/\delta)\,dF\right)^{1/2}
. Since the latter is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}
, we are done.
∎

The following lemma analyzes properties of the linear functional
T(\mu_{n},\hat{R}_{n},\,\cdot\,)
(without the modulus-square applied to it).

Lemma 3
.

If
\tilde{R}_{n}
is a normalized asymptotically shrinkage-optimal estimator and
E
is an open disk in
\mathbb{C}
, the following hold:

(i)

With
T_{n}=T(\mu_{n},\tilde{R}_{n},y_{n})
, the random variable
\Pr[T_{n}\in E\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n},\mu_{n},X_{n}]
converges in probability to
\Pr[Z\in E]
, where
Z
is a standard circularly symmetric complex normal random variable.

(ii)

The random variable
\Pr[T_{n}\in E\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}^{n},\mu_{n},X_{n}]
is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to

\Pr\left[Z\in E-a(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}\right].

Proof.

Consider assertion (i). We first show that
\Pr[T_{n}\in E\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n},\mu_{n},X_{n}]
converges is almost surely asymptotically equivalent to

\int_{E/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}}\phi\,dz,

(23)

where
\phi
is the standard circularly symmetric complex Gaussian.
For this, let
y_{n}=R_{n}^{1/2}z_{n}\sim\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n}
and let
w_{n}^{\prime}
be the vector
\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}^{1/2}/(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}
. Then

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[T_{n}\in E\mid\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n},\mu_{n},X_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\frac{w_{n}^{\prime}z_{n}}{\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}}\in\frac{1}{\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}}E\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\frac{w_{n}^{\prime}z_{n}}{\left\|w_{n}\right\|}\in\frac{1}{\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}}E\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}\right].

By the Berry-Esseen theorem
[
6
,
14
]
, the difference of the latter from (
23
) is bounded by

\frac{C_{1}}{\left\|w_{n}\right\|}\max_{1\leq i\leq p_{n}}\frac{\rho_{n,i}}{w_{n,i}^{2}},

for some absolute constant
C_{1}
, where
\rho_{n,i}=\mathbb{E}[|w_{n,i}|^{3}|z_{n,i}|^{3}]\leq C|w_{n,i}|^{3}
. Using Definition
2
(ii), it is straightforward to show that, asymptotically, the quantity
\left\|w_{n}\right\|
is bounded below. Thus, we wish to show that
\max_{i}|w_{n,i}|
goes to zero rapidly in probability.

For this, observe that
|w_{n,i}|=(w_{n,i}\overline{w_{n,i}})^{1/2}
can be written as

(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}^{1/2}e_{n,i}e_{n,i}^{\prime}R_{n}^{1/2}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}=:\epsilon_{n,i}^{1/2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\Pr[\max_{i}|w_{n,i}|>\epsilon]

\displaystyle=\Pr[\max_{i}\epsilon_{n,i}>\epsilon^{2}]

\displaystyle=\Pr[\exists i:\ \epsilon_{n,i}>\epsilon^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p_{n}}\Pr[\epsilon_{n,i}>\epsilon^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p_{n}}\Pr[|\epsilon_{n,i}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{n,i}|>\epsilon^{2}/2]+\sum_{i=1}^{p_{n}}\Pr[\tilde{\epsilon}_{n,i}>\epsilon^{2}/2],

(24)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}_{n,i}

\displaystyle=p_{n}^{-1}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}^{1/2}e_{n,i}e_{n,i}^{\prime}R_{n}^{1/2}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1})

\displaystyle=p_{n}^{-1}e_{n,i}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}e_{n,i}

\displaystyle\leq p_{n}^{-1}\left\|R_{n}\right\|\left\|\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\right\|^{2},

Thus, with
Y_{n}=\left\|\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\right\|^{2}
, the second term of (
24
) is asymptotically bounded above by

p_{n}\Pr[p_{n}^{-1}Y_{n}>\epsilon^{\prime}]

(25)

for
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{2}/(2\underline{T})
. By Chernoff’s inequality, this is bounded above by

p_{n}\exp(-\epsilon^{\prime}p_{n})\mathbb{E}\exp(Y_{n}).

But since
\limsup_{n}Y_{n}
is almost surely bounded (see Definition
2
(i)), say by
C
, we have that the above is bounded above by

p_{n}\exp(-\epsilon^{\prime}p_{n})\exp(2C)

for
n
large enough.
Thus, the second term in (
24
) goes to zero exponentially fast. Further the first term in (
24
) converges to zero exponentially fast by the proof of Proposition
1
with
A_{n}
equal to
\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}R_{n}^{1/2}e_{n,i}e_{n,i}^{\prime}R_{n}^{1/2}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}
. By Borel-Cantelli and the continuous mapping theorem, this proves the almost sure asymptotic equivalence asserted.

An upper bound for the difference between (
23
) and
\int_{E}\phi\,dz
is
\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty}
times

\lambda\left((E/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2})\Delta E\right),

(26)

where
\lambda
is Lebesgue measure and
\Delta
is the symmetric difference. Using continuity of the above inset expression in
\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}
and
Lemma
2
, (
26
) converges in probability to zero. The proof of part (i) is complete.

Now consider (ii). If
\tilde{R}_{n}
is any shrinkage estimator, then

p_{1}^{n}(\tilde{R}_{n},t)
is a.s. asymptotically equivalent to

\int_{E/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}-a\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})}\phi\,dz.

(27)

This follows since
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{n}
data are just
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{n}
data shifted by
\mu_{n}
and the fact that

\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})=(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}.

Applying a similar continuity argument to the one applied to (
26
), we get that (
27
) is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to

\int_{E-a(\mu_{n}^{\prime}\tilde{R}_{n}^{-1}\mu_{n})^{1/2}}\phi\,dz,

as desired.
∎

We now prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For (i) and (ii) simply use Lemma
3
with
\tilde{R}_{n}=\hat{R}_{n}
and disks centered at the origin.

Next consider (iii).
Suppose
t_{n}
and
t
and
\tilde{R}_{n}
are such that
p_{0}(\tilde{R}_{n},t_{n})\lesssim p_{0}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
. By the Berry-Esseen arguments above, this means

\Pr\left[|Z|^{2}/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})>t_{n}\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}\right]\lesssim\Pr[|Z|^{2}>t]\qquad\text{(i.p.)}.

This implies that
t_{n}\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})\gtrsim t
. Thus,

\displaystyle p_{1}^{n}(\tilde{R}_{n},t_{n})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Pr[|T(\mu_{n},\tilde{R}_{n},y_{n})|^{2}>t/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})\mid\mathcal{H}_{1}^{n},\mu_{n},X_{n}]\qquad\text{(i.p.)},

which is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to

\Pr[|Z/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})^{1/2}+a\nu_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})|^{2}>t/\xi_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})\mid\mu_{n},X_{n}].

Using the shrinkage-optimality of
\hat{R}_{n}
, Lemma
2
(i), and another dominated convergence argument, the inset expression immediately above is i.p. asymptotically less than or equal to

\Pr[|Z+a\nu_{n}(\hat{R}_{n})|^{2}>t],

which by part (ii) of this theorem is i.p. asymptotically equivalent to
p_{1}^{n}(\hat{R}_{n},t)
.
The proof is complete.

∎

Long-time behavior of solutions to the M 1 M_{1} model with boundray effect
Nangao Zhang, Changjiang Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of solutions of
M_{1}
model on quadrant
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
From this model, combined with damped compressible Euler equations, a more general system is introduced.
We show that the solutions to the initial boundary value problem of this system globally exist and tend time-asymptotically to the corresponding nonlinear parabolic equation governed by the related Darcy’s law.
Compared with previous results on compressible Euler equations with damping obtained by Nishihara and Yang in
[
24
]
, and Marcati, Mei and Rubino in
[
16
]
, the better convergence rates are obtained. The approach adopted is based on the technical time-weighted energy estimates together with the Green’s function method.

Key Words:

M_{1}
model, nonlinear parabolic equation, time-weighted energy estimates, asymptotic behavior.

AMS Subject Classification:
85A25, 35L65, 35B40.

1
Introduction

Radiation transport is involved in many fields, such as climate, medicine and so on. In recent years, a great effort has been made to develop approximate models that solve the radiative transfer equations at a low cost. In this context, the
M_{1}
model is an interesting choice (cf.
[
2
,
3
]
). Here we just consider the scattering part and we omit the role played by the temperature, then the corresponding simplified model reads
(cf.
[
1
,
6
]
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\rho+c\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+c\nabla\cdot P(\rho,u)=-c\sigma\rho u.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here, the unknown function
\rho=\rho(x,t)\geq 0
and
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~(1\leq n\leq 3)
denote respectively the radiative energy and normalized radiative flux. The positive constants
c
and
\sigma
denote the speed of the light and the opacity.
Concerning the radiative pressure
P(\rho,u)
, it is given by

P(\rho,u)=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-\chi(u))\mathbb{I}_{n}+(3\chi(u)-1)\frac{u\otimes u}{|u|^{2}}\right)\rho,

(1.2)

with

\chi(u)=\frac{3+4|u|^{2}}{5+2\sqrt{4-3|u|^{2}}},

(1.3)

where
\mathbb{I}_{n}
is the identity matrix of order
n
and
\left|u\right|\leq 1
.

In this paper, we shall restrict ourselves to the one-dimensional case. We set
c=1
without loss of generality, then (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\left(\rho u\right)_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\left(\rho u\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{\rho}{3}\right)_{x}+\left(\frac{2\rho u^{2}}{2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}\right)_{x}=-\sigma\rho u.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

We are interested in the large time behavior of solutions to (
1.4
) on quadrant. Suppose that
\rho\geq C>0
, then it is more convenient to use the Lagrangian coordinates to explore this system. We consider the coordinate transformation as follows:

x\Rightarrow\int_{(0,0)}^{(x,t)}\rho(y,s)\mathrm{d}y-(\rho u)(y,s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\Rightarrow\tau,

and we still denote the Lagrangian coordinates by
(x,t)
for simplicity.

Let
v=\frac{1}{\rho}
, then the system (
1.4
) can be transformed as the following form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u_{t}+\left(\frac{1}{3v}\right)_{x}-\left(\frac{u^{2}\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}{v(2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}})}\right)_{x}=-\sigma u,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

with the following initial data

(v,u)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},u_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad v_{+}>0\textsterling\textlnot\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\infty,

(1.6)

and the Dirichlet boundary condition

u(0,t)=0.

(1.7)

Now let’s briefly review the previous work on
M_{1}
model and introduce the main concerns of this paper. For the Cauchy problem, the global existence of smooth solutions with small initial data has been studied in
[
6
]
, Zhang and Zhu in
[
27
]
showed that the solution of (1.5) tended time-asymptotically to the nonlinear diffusion waves, and obtained its optimal convergence rate.
However, the large time behavior of solutions to
M_{1}
model on quadrant is never dealt with as we know.
Therefore, the target in this paper is to study the large time behaviors of smooth solutions to (
1.5
)–(
1.7
).
It is noted that in physics, the damping effects make that the dynamical system possesses diffusion phenomena like nonlinear diffusion equations, so we are mainly focus on the nonlinear diffusive phenomena of this system.

Noting the relationship between (
1.5
) and the compressible Euler equations with damping, we hope to obtain more general results involving the two systems. So in this paper, we tend to consider the following more general system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=-\alpha u+(g(u)f(v))_{x},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

with initial data

(v,u)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},u_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad\mbox{as}\quad x\rightarrow\infty,

(1.9)

and with the Dirichlet boundary condition

u|_{x=0}=0.

(1.10)

Here
u=u(x,t)~\mbox{and}~v=v(x,t)>0:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are unknown,
p
is a smooth function of
v
with
p>0
,
g
and
f
are smooth function of
u
and
v
, respectively, physical coefficients
\alpha>0
.
v_{+}>0
and
u_{+}
are constants.

Let’s review some relevant mathematical investigations. When
(g(u)f(v))_{x}\equiv 0
, the system (
1.8
) reduces to compressible Euler equations with linear damping

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=-\alpha u.\end{array}\right.

(1.11)

For the Cauchy problem to (
1.11
), there is a huge literature on the investigations of global existence and large time behaviors of smooth solutions (see
[
21
,
26
,
29
]
and references therein).
Hsiao and Liu in
[
7
]
firstly obtained the solutions
(v,u)(x,t)
to the corresponding Cauchy problem of (
1.11
) tend time-asymptotically to the nonlinear self-similar diffusion wave solutions
(\bar{\bar{v}},\bar{\bar{u}})(x,t)
of the porous media equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{\bar{v}}_{t}-\bar{\bar{u}}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{\bar{v}})_{x}=-\alpha\bar{\bar{u}},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{array}\right.

in the sense
\left\|(v-\bar{\bar{v}},u-\bar{\bar{u}})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{1}{2}},t^{-\frac{1}{2}})
.
Then, by taking more detailed energy estimates, Nishihara in
[
20
]
successfully improved the convergence rates
as
\left\|(v-\bar{\bar{v}},u-\bar{\bar{u}})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{3}{4}},t^{-\frac{5}{4}})
.
Furthermore, by constructing an approximate Green function with the energy method together, Nishihara, Wang and Yang in
[
23
]
completely improved the convergence rates as
\left\|(v-\bar{\bar{v}},u-\bar{\bar{u}})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-1},t^{-\frac{3}{2}})
, which is optimal in the sense comparing with the heat equation.
These conclusions require that both the initial disturbance and the wave strength around a particular diffusion wave are suitably small, some of these restrictions were later partially relaxed by Zhao in
[
28
]
.
For the Cauchy problem to (
1.11
) with nonlinear damping or vacuum, and so on, we can refer to these interesting works
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
30
]
and references therein.

For the initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) on the quarter plane
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
to (
1.11
), the global existence and the asymptotic behavior of the solution have been investigated by several authors (see
[
14
,
15
]
and references therein). Nishihara and Yang in
[
24
]
considered the asymptotic behavior of solution to (
1.11
) with the Dirichlet boundary condition (
1.10
), and they got the convergence rates in form of
\left\|(v-\tilde{v},u-\tilde{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-\frac{3}{4}},t^{-\frac{5}{4}})
by perturbing the initial value around the linear diffusion waves
(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})(x,t)
which satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{v}_{t}-\tilde{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p^{\prime}(v_{+})\tilde{v}_{x}=-\alpha\tilde{u},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\tilde{u}|_{x=0}=0,\quad(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})|_{x=\infty}=(v_{+},0),\end{array}\right.

when the initial perturbation belonged to
H^{3}\times H^{2}
. Observing that the decay rates can be better by eliminate the slower decay term
(p^{\prime}(\bar{v})-p^{\prime}(v_{+}))\bar{v}_{x}
in
[
24
]
, Marcati, Mei and Rubino in
[
16
]
chose a new asymptotic profile as the nonlinear diffusion waves
(\bar{v},\bar{u})(x,t)
which satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}_{t}-\bar{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{v})_{x}=-\alpha\bar{u},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{u}|_{x=0}=0,\quad(\bar{v},\bar{u})|_{x=\infty}=(v_{+},0),\end{array}\right.

and they improved the convergence rates as
\left\|(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u})(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(t^{-1},t^{-\frac{3}{2}})
when the initial perturbation is small belonging to
(H^{3}\times H^{2})\cap(L^{1}\times L^{1})
.
Such smallness assumptions on the initial perturbation were then partially relaxed by Jiang and Zhu in
[
12
]
.
For other studies related to the initial-boundary value problem to (
1.11
), we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
13
]
and references therein.

When
\alpha=\sigma
,
p(v)=\frac{1}{3v},g(u)=\frac{u^{2}\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}{2+\sqrt{4-3u^{2}}}
and
f(v)=\frac{1}{v}
, the system (
1.11
) reduces to
M_{1}
model (
1.5
) which we will study in the next.
Motivated by these preceding results, in the present paper, we will consider the convergence to nonlinear diffusion waves for solutions to the IBVP (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) on quadrant, and we will obtain a sharper result which indeed improves those in Nishihara and Yang
[
24
]
, and Marcati, Mei and Rubino
[
16
]
.

According to Darcy’s law, we expect the asymptotic profile of the Dirichlet IBVP (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}_{t}-\bar{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
p(\bar{v})_{x}=-\alpha\bar{u},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{u}|_{x=0}=0,\quad(\bar{v},\bar{u})|_{x=\infty}=(v_{+},0),\end{array}\right.

(1.12)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{xx},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\bar{u}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{x},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\bar{v}_{x}|_{x=0}=0,\quad(\bar{v},\bar{u})|_{x=\infty}=(v_{+},0).\end{array}\right.

(1.13)

Taking the limits to system of (
1.8
) as
x\rightarrow\infty
, and noting that
p(v)_{x}
and
(g(u)f(v))_{x}
will be vanishing, then we find that
u(\infty,t)
satisfies formally the following ODEs:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}v(\infty,t)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}u(\infty,t)=-\alpha u(\infty,t),\\[5.69054pt]
(v,u)(\infty,0)=(v_{0},u_{0})(\infty)=(v_{+},u_{+}).\end{array}\right.

(1.14)

By direct calculation, we have

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}(v,u)(x,t)=(v,u)(\infty,t)=(v_{+},u_{+}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}).

(1.15)

To eliminate the value of
u(x,t)
at
x=\infty
, we introduce the following auxiliary functions as in
[
24
,
16
,
12
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{v}(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{u_{+}}{-\alpha}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}m_{0}(x),\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{u}(x,t)=u_{+}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}m_{0}(y){\rm d}y,\end{array}\right.

(1.16)

where
m_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\infty}m_{0}(x){\rm d}x=1.

Then one can immediately obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{v}_{t}-\hat{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{u}_{t}=-\alpha\hat{u},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{u}|_{x=0}=0,\quad(\hat{v},\hat{u})|_{x=\infty}=(0,u_{+}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t}).\end{array}\right.

(1.17)

Combining (
1.8
), (
1.12
) and (
1.17
), we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(v-\bar{v}-\hat{v})_{t}-(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})_{t}+(p(v)-p(\bar{v}))_{x}+\alpha(u-\bar{u}-\hat{u})=-\bar{u}_{t}+(g(u)f(v))_{x}.\end{array}\right.

(1.18)

Integrating
\eqref{1.11}_{1}
over
[0,\infty)\times[0,t]
, and noticing that
\bar{v}(x,0)=v_{+}+\delta_{0}\phi_{0}(x)
in the next section as in
[
16
]
, we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}(v-\bar{v}-\hat{v})(y,t){\rm d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(v_{0}(x)-v_{+}\right)dx-\delta_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{0}(x)dx+\frac{u_{+}}{\alpha}=0.

(1.19)

Thus, we reach the setting of perturbations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V(x,t)=-\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\left(v-\bar{v}-\hat{v}\right)(y,t){\rm d}y,\\[5.69054pt]
z(x,t)=u(x,t)-\bar{u}(x,t)-\hat{u}(x,t),\end{array}\right.

(1.20)

then after the integration of
\eqref{1.11}_{1}
over
(x,\infty)
, we have the reformulated problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{t}-z=0,\\[5.69054pt]
z_{t}+\left(p(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v})-p(\bar{v})\right)_{x}+\alpha z=(g(z+\bar{u}+\hat{u})f(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v}))_{x},\end{array}\right.

(1.21)

and the linearized problem around
\bar{v}
gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{t}-z=0,\\[5.69054pt]
z_{t}+\left(p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x}+\alpha z=F_{1}+F_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(V,z)|_{t=0}:=(V_{0},z_{0})(x),\\[5.69054pt]
V|_{x=0}=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.22)

or

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{tt}+\left(p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x}+\alpha V_{t}=F_{1}+F_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(V,V_{t})|_{t=0}:=(V_{0},z_{0})(x),\\[5.69054pt]
V|_{x=0}=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.23)

where

{F}_{1}=\frac{1}{\alpha}p(\bar{v})_{xt}-\left(p(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v})-p(\bar{v})-p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}\right)_{x},

(1.24)

{F}_{2}=\left(g(V_{t}+\bar{u}+\hat{u})f(V_{x}+\bar{v}+\hat{v})\right)_{x},

(1.25)

\left(V_{0},z_{0}\right)(x)=\left(-\int_{x}^{\infty}\left(v_{0}(y)-\bar{v}(y,0)-\hat{v}(y,0)\right){\rm d}y,~~u_{0}(x)-\bar{u}(x,0)-\hat{u}(x,0)\right).

(1.26)

Notations.
In the following,
C
and
c
denote the generic positive constants depending only on the initial data, but independent of the time. For function spaces,
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})~(1\leq p\leq\infty)
is an usual Lebesgue space with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}|f(x)|^{p}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},~~~1\leq p<\infty~~~\mbox{and}~~~\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}|f(x)|.

For any integer
l\geq 0
,
H^{l}
denotes the usual
l
-th order Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with its norm

\|f\|_{l}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{l}\left\|\partial_{x}^{j}f\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~~\quad\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{0}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}.

In order to state our main results, we assume that the following assumptions hold:

\displaystyle p,f\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),~~p^{\prime}<0~\mbox{for}~\mbox{any}~v>0,

(1.27)

\displaystyle g\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}),~~~g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0.

(1.28)

The followings are the main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that (
1.27
)–(
1.28
) hold,
\|V_{0}\|_{H^{3}}+\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}}
and
\delta:=\|v_{0}-v_{+}\|_{L^{1}}+|u_{+}|
are sufficiency small. Then, there exists a unique time-global solution
V(x,t)
of (
1.22
), which satisfies

V(x,t)\in C^{k}(0,\infty;H^{3-k}),~~~k=0,1,2,3;\quad z(x,t)\in C^{k}(0,\infty;H^{2-k}),~~~k=0,1,2,

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}},\qquad~~~~~~~~1\leq k\leq 3,

(1.29)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{j}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-j-1},\qquad 0\leq k+j\leq 2,~0\leq j\leq 1,

(1.30)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

(1.31)

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we assume further that
(V_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}z_{0})(x)\in L^{1}
, then solution
V(x,t)
obtained in Theorem
1.1
satisfies the following improved decay rates

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}},\qquad~~~~~0\leq k\leq 3,

(1.32)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{j}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-j-\frac{5}{4}},\qquad 0\leq k+j\leq 2,~0\leq j\leq 1,

(1.33)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}}.

(1.34)

Moreover, if
u_{+}=0
,
\int_{0}^{\infty}(V_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}z_{0})(x){\rm d}x=0
and
W_{0}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}(V_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}z_{0})(y){\rm d}y\in L^{1}
, then faster decay rates hold:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{3}{4}},\qquad~~~~~0\leq k\leq 3,

(1.35)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{j}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}-j-\frac{7}{4}},\qquad 0\leq k+j\leq 2,~0\leq j\leq 1,

(1.36)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}z(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{13}{4}}.

(1.37)

Remark 1.1
.

As we can see from Theorem 2.1 in Marcati, Mei and Rubino
[
16
]
, the authors required that
\|V_{0}\|_{H^{3}}+\|z_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|v_{0}-v_{+}\|_{L^{1}}+|u_{+}|+\|V_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|z_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
be small enough to get the decay estimates
\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}-l}
in the case of
0\leq k\leq 2
or
0\leq l\leq 1
, but estimates for other cases have not been elucidated. In contrast, our conditions are weaker, and the conclusions are sharper.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, except for
\|z_{tt}(t)\|
, the decay estimates obtained in Theorem
1.2
are all optimal. As for
\|z_{tt}(t)\|
, we can also use the similar way to obtain an extra time-decay
(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
when
\left(V_{0},z_{0}\right)(x)
is small belonging to
H^{4}\times H^{3}
.

Remark 1.3
.

As for compressible Euler equations with damping, the condition
u_{+}=0
in Theorem
1.2
can be removed. One can see (
3.2
) in Section
3
for more details.

Before concluding this section, we point out the main difference between the study in this paper and the related results in the previous works. Similar arguments used in
[
24
,
16
]
, we will prove Theorem
1.1
by deriving the key
a priori
energy estimates.
But for the way to obtain the decay estimates (
1.32
)–(
1.34
), our analyses is quite different from
[
16
]
.
From the dissussion in
[
16
]
, we can see that once they got the existence and decay rates of the solution in the
L^{2}
-framework, they used Green’s function to get an integral representation of the solution, and only analyzed the integral expression of the solution to obtain the improved decay rates
\|\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}-l}
in the case of
0\leq k\leq 2
or
0\leq l\leq 1
, but other cases cann’t be clarified.
Unlike their approach, by analyzing the integral representation of the solution, we firstly obtain
\|V(t)\|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}
. With all these preparations, by using the time-weighted energy estimates, we can get the desired estimates (
1.32
)–(
1.34
). Similar analysis ideas can also deduce (
1.35
)–(
1.37
). See Section
3
for more detials.
This technique is quite useful, it has been successfully used in
[
5
]
. We think this approach has at least two advantages: one is that we can get the desired decay rates without putting additional regularity on the initial data, and the other is that the calculation process is much simpler and clearer.
Finally, we want to point out that for the Newman boundary problem, due to the difficulties in dealing with some boundary terms, we have not found a suitable method, but some ideas in this paper provide some key clues, and we will continue to study this problem in the future.

The arrangement of the present paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we prepare some preliminaries, which are useful in the proof of theorems. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of our main theorems.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some known results which will be used for the proof of our main results in next section.

Firstly, as for the solution
(\bar{v},\bar{u})(x,t)
of (
1.13
), we can obtain that

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
12
,
16
]
)
Let
\bar{v}(x,t)
be a solution of (
1.13
) with the initial data
\bar{v}(x,0)=v_{+}+\delta_{0}\phi_{0}
, where
\delta_{0}
is a constant satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[v_{0}(x)-v_{+}\right]dx-\delta_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{0}(x){\rm d}x+\frac{u_{+}}{\alpha}=0,

(2.1)

and
\phi_{0}
is a given smooth function satisfying

\phi_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})~~~~\mbox{and}~~~~\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{0}{\rm d}x\neq 0.

Then the solution
(\bar{v},\bar{u})(x,t)
of (
1.13
) globally and uniquely exists and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial^{k}_{x}\partial^{j}_{t}(\bar{v}-v_{+})(t)\|\leq C|\delta_{0}|(1+t)^{-\frac{4j+2k+1}{4}},

\begin{split}J_{5}=&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x,t-s;y)\left[(p^{\prime}(v_{+})-p^{\prime}(\bar{v}))V_{y}\right]_{y}(y,s){\rm d}y{\rm d}s\\
=&\left(-\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}-\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{y}(x,t-s;y)[(p^{\prime}(v_{+})-p^{\prime}(\bar{v}))V_{y}](y,s){\rm d}y{\rm d}s:=J_{5}^{1}+J_{5}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.138)

By using (

), (

) and (

), we have

\begin{split}\left\|J_{5}^{1}(t)\right\|&\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left\|G_{y}(t-s)\right\|\|V_{x}(s)\|\|(\bar{v}-v_{+})(s)\|{\rm d}s\\
&\leq C\delta M(t)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\rm d}s\leq C\delta M(t)t^{-\frac{3}{4}},\end{split}

(3.139)

and

\begin{split}\left\|J_{5}^{2}(t)\right\|&\leq\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left\|G_{y}(t-s)\right\|_{L^{1}}\|V_{x}(s)\|\|(\bar{v}-v_{+})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}{\rm d}s\\
&\leq\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{5}{4}}{\rm d}s\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{4}}.\end{split}

(3.140)

Furthermore, since
u_{+}=0
, then it follows that

in view of (

),
\eqref{1.6}_{2}

\displaystyle\left\|J_{9}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}G(x,t-s;y)_{y}(gf)(y,s){\rm d}y{\rm d}s\right\|\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left\|G_{y}(t-s)\right\|\left\|(gf)(s)\right\|_{L^{1}}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left\|G_{y}(t-s)\right\|\left\|(V_{s}+\bar{u})(s)\right\|^{2}{\rm d}s\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{4}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\rm d}s\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(3.141)

Consequently,

\|V(t)\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{9}\|J_{i}(t)\|\leq C(1+\delta M(t))(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(3.142)

As introduced in the introduction, the condition

(3.144)

Addition of

,

By applying the same idea as the upper lemma, integrating

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+s)^{\epsilon_{0}+\frac{3}{2}}\left(V_{t}^{2}-\lambda p^{\prime}(\bar{v})V_{x}^{2}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}\tau

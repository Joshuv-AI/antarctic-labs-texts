The common range of co-analytic Toeplitz operators on the Drury-Arveson space Thanks: M.H. was partially supported by a GIF grant. Thanks: J.M. was partially supported by National Science Foundation Grant DMS 2054199
Alexandru Aleman, Michael Hartz, John E. M c Carthy, Stefan Richter

Abstract
. We characterize the common range of the adjoints of cyclic multiplication operators on the Drury–Arveson space. We show that a function belongs to this common range if and only if its Taylor coefficients satisfy a simple decay condition. To achieve this, we introduce the uniform Smirnov class on the ball and determine its dual space. We show that the dual space of the uniform Smirnov class equals the dual space of the strictly smaller Smirnov class of the Drury-Arveson space, and that this in turn equals the common range of the adjoints of cyclic multiplication operators.

Primary 32A35, 46A13, 47B35

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Known results on the disk

Let
H^{2}
denote the Hardy space on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, and let
H^{\infty}
denote the bounded analytic
functions on
\mathbb{D}
. If
m\in H^{\infty}
we let
T_{m}:H^{2}\to H^{2},\ f\mapsto mf
denote the multiplication operator. We call its adjoint
T_{m}^{*}
a co-analytic Toeplitz operator. In
[
15
]
, H. Helson asked

Question 1.1
.

What functions are in the range of
T_{m}^{*}
for every outer function
m
in
H^{\infty}
?

He was led to this question from the following considerations.
The Nevanlinna class of
\mathbb{D}
is defined as

(1.2)

N(\mathbb{D})\ =\ \Big\{f:f\ {\rm holomorphic\ on\ }\mathbb{D},\ \sup_{0<r<1}\int\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta})|)d\theta<\infty\Big\}.

The Smirnov class
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
is a subset of functions in
N(\mathbb{D})
:

(1.3)

N^{+}(\mathbb{D})\ =\ \Big\{f\in N(\mathbb{D})\ :\ \lim_{r\uparrow 1}\ \int\log[1+|f(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})|]d\theta\ =\ 0\Big\},

where
f(e^{i\theta})
is the non-tangential limit, which exists a.e.
[
13
, Thm. II.5.3]
.
It can be shown (see
e.g.
[
13
, Sec. II.5]
) that

(1.4)

\displaystyle N(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\ =\

\displaystyle\{f=g/m:\ g\in H^{2},\ m\in H^{\infty},m\neq 0{\rm\ on\ }\mathbb{D}\}

(1.5)

\displaystyle N^{+}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\ =\

\displaystyle\{f=g/m:\ g\in H^{2},\ m\ {\rm outer\ }\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f=q/m:\ q\in H^{\infty},m\ {\rm outer\ }\}.

We shall use
outer
throughout the note to mean an outer function in
H^{\infty}
.
Helson considered
the locally convex inductive limit topology
{\mathcal{I}}
on
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
that comes from viewing it
as

N^{+}(\mathbb{D})\ =\ \bigcup\Big\{\frac{1}{m}H^{2}\ :\ m\ {\rm outer\ }\Big\}.

First we put a norm on each
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}
by declaring

\|g\|_{\frac{1}{m}H^{2}}\ :=\ \|mg\|_{H^{2}},

(so dividing by
m
is a unitary from
H^{2}
onto
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}
).
Then
{\mathcal{I}}
is the translation-invariant topology that has a neighborhood base at zero
consisting of all absolutely convex sets
\Omega
with the property that

\Omega\cap\frac{1}{m}H^{2}
is open for each outer function
m

(this is the same as saying that
m\Omega\cap H^{2}
is open in
H^{2}
for every outer

m
).
In other words,
\mathcal{I}
is the finest locally convex topology
on
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
that makes all inclusions
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}\hookrightarrow N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
continuous.
Helson proved in
[
15
]
the following theorem:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n}
be in
H^{2}
. Then the functional

\Gamma:\sum b_{n}z^{n}\ \mapsto\ \sum b_{n}\overline{\gamma_{n}}

is a continuous linear functional on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),{\mathcal{I}})
if and only if

h
is in the range of
T_{m}^{*}
for every outer function
m
.

He asked for an intrinsic characterization of such
h
. One can view the condition of Question
1.1
in some ways as a smoothness condition.
Indeed, if
m(z)=1-z
and
T_{m}^{*}f=h,
then denoting the
k
-th Taylor coefficient of a holomorphic
function
g
by
\widehat{g}(k)
,
we have

\widehat{h}(k)=\widehat{f}(k)-\widehat{f}(k+1)
. So
h
has a telescoping Taylor series when evaluated at
1
, and
by Abel’s theorem
\lim_{r\uparrow 1}h(r1)
exists. Similarly looking at symbols that are powers
of
(e^{i\theta}-z)
, one can show that if
h
is in
\bigcap_{m\ {\rm outer}}{\rm ran}(T_{m}^{*})
, all its derivatives have radial limits everywhere on the unit circle.

There is another topology on
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
. One can define a metric on
N(\mathbb{D})
by

\rho(f,g)\ =\ \sup_{0<r<1}\int\log(1+|[f-g](re^{i\theta})|)d\theta.

The Nevanlinna class with the metric
\rho
is not a topological vector space, because
scalar multiplication is not continuous
[
27
]
. On the smaller Smirnov class,

\rho
makes it into a topological vector space with a complete translation invariant metric
(however it is not locally convex). The dual of
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)
had been found by N. Yanagihara
[
29
]
:

Theorem 1.7
.

The functional

\Gamma:\sum b_{n}z^{n}\ \mapsto\ \sum b_{n}\overline{\gamma_{n}}

is a continuous linear functional on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)
if and only if
there exist constants
M,c>0
so that

(1.8)

|\gamma_{n}|\ \leq\ Me^{-c\sqrt{n}}\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

In
[
17
]
it was shown that the inclusion map from
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)
to
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),{\mathcal{I}})

is continuous, and moreover
{\mathcal{I}}
is the finest locally convex topology such that inclusion is continuous.
Consequently, both spaces have the same dual, and hence Question
1.1
was answered.

Theorem 1.9
.

A function
h=\sum\hat{h}(n)z^{n}
in
H^{2}
is in the range of
T_{m}^{*}
for every outer function
m
in
H^{\infty}
if and only if
there exist
M,c>0
so that

(1.10)

|\hat{h}(n)|\ \leq\ Me^{-c\sqrt{n}}\quad\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Remark 1.11

If
m
is any non-zero function in
H^{\infty}
, the range of
T_{m}^{*}
depends only on its outer part.
Indeed, factoring
m=um_{o}
where
u
is inner and
m_{o}
is outer, we have that

T_{m}^{*}uf=T_{m_{o}}^{*}f

and
T_{m}^{*}
annihilates
(uH^{2})^{\perp}
.
So Question
1.1
is the same
as asking for a characterization of

(1.12)

\bigcap\{{\rm ran}(T_{m}^{*})\ :\ m\in H^{\infty},\ {\rm not\ identically\ }0\}.

Remark 1.13

Functions satisfying the smoothness condition (
1.8
) come up in multiple places.
For example, in
[
9
]
they are shown to be the functions that are the multipliers of every de Branges-Rovnyak space
H(b)
when
b
is not an extreme point of the ball of
H^{\infty}
; see

[
25
]
for a treatment of these spaces. In
[
7
]
, the authors give bounds on the number
of zeroes such functions can have in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, and discuss how the class coincides with a certain class of Cauchy transforms.

1.2.
New results on the ball

Let
d
be a positive integer, and
\mathbb{B}_{d}
denote the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Let us define the Drury-Arveson space
H^{2}_{d}
to be the Hilbert space of holomorphic functions
on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
with reproducing kernel given by

k(z,\lambda)\ =\ \frac{1}{1-\langle z,\lambda\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}.

We let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the natural numbers (including
0
).
The monomials
\{z^{\alpha}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}\}
form an orthogonal basis for
H^{2}_{d}
. Their norms will come up
frequently, so we shall define

(1.14)

\omega_{\alpha}\ :=\ \|z^{\alpha}\|_{{H^{2}_{d}}}\ =\ \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{1}!\dots\alpha_{d}!}{(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d})!}}.

For many questions regarding multivariable operator theory, the Drury–Arveson space turns
out to be the right generalization of
H^{2}
to the unit ball; see
[
6
,
26
]
for background on
H^{2}_{d}
.

We shall let
{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d})
denote the multiplier algebra of
H^{2}_{d}
. For each
m
in
{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d})
, we shall
let

T_{m}:f\mapsto mf

denote the multiplication operator, and we shall call its adjoint
T_{m}^{*}
a co-analytic Toeplitz operator on
{H^{2}_{d}}
. A multiplier
m
is called cyclic if
{\rm ran}(T_{m})
is dense in
H^{2}_{d}
. Our goal is to answer the
d
-dimensional version of Question
1.1
.

Question 1.15
.

What functions are in the range of
T_{m}^{*}
for every cyclic
m
in
{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d})
?

In analogy to (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), we define the Nevanlinna and Smirnov classes
of
H^{2}_{d}
by

\displaystyle N(H^{2}_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f=g/m:\ g\in H^{2}_{d},m\in{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d}),m\neq 0{\rm\ on\ }\mathbb{B}_{d}\}

\displaystyle N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f=g/m:\ g\in H^{2}_{d},m\in{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d}),m\ {\rm cyclic\ }\}.

In
[
3
]
we proved that
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
is equal to

\{f=\phi/m:\ \phi\in{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d}),m\in{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d}),m\ {\rm cyclic\ }\}.

As before, let us define
the locally convex inductive limit topology
{\mathcal{I}}
on
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
.
We norm each
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}
by

\|g\|_{\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}}\ :=\ \|mg\|_{H^{2}_{d}}.

Then
{\mathcal{I}}
is the translation-invariant topology that has a neighborhood base at zero
consisting of all absolutely convex sets
\Omega
with the property that

\Omega\cap\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}
is open for each cyclic multiplier
m
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{I}
is the finest locally convex topology on
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})

that makes all inclusions
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}\hookrightarrow N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
continuous.
The polynomials are dense in
(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
, and
Helson’s theorem remains true; we prove this in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.16
.

The function
h\in H^{2}_{d}
is in
\bigcap\{{\rm ran}(T_{m}^{*}):m\ {\rm cyclic}\}
if and only if
the functional

\Gamma_{h}:f\mapsto\langle f,h\rangle_{{H^{2}_{d}}}

extends to be a continuous linear functional on
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
.

To characterize these functions by their Taylor coefficients, we need to study an appropriate version
of (
1.3
).
In
[
22
]
W. Rudin defined the Nevanlinna and Smirnov classes on the unit ball

\mathbb{B}_{d}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
analogously to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), replacing integration over the circle with
integration over the sphere. He conjectured that the two classes were equal for
d\geq 2
, but
the solution of the inner function problem on the ball showed that this was not true. See
[
23
]
.

In this note we shall study holomorphic functions on the ball that are uniformly in the Smirnov class
on every disk through the origin. So we shall let

N_{u}
denote the holomorphic functions on the ball
\mathbb{B}_{d}
such that

(1.17)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\ :=\ \sup_{\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}\sup_{0<r<1}\int\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta}\zeta)|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

is finite. This is not a norm, but we can define a metric by

\rho(f,g)={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\sup_{\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}\sup_{0<r<1}\int\log(1+|[f-g](re^{i\theta}\zeta)|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

We define the uniform Smirnov class
N^{+}_{u}
by

(1.18)

N^{+}_{u}\ =\ \{f\in N_{u}:\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\rho(f_{r},f)=0\},

where
f_{r}(z):=f(rz)
.
We shall show in Proposition
2.5
that
(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
is an F-space,
i.e.
a topological vector space in which
the topology comes from a complete translation invariant metric, and that the polynomials are dense in
(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
. Moreover we show that multiplication is continuous, so
(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
is a topological algebra.
(It can be shown that when
d=1
the definitions of the Smirnov class from (
1.3
) and (
1.18
)
coincide. We were unable to find a convenient reference, so we include a proof in Proposition
B.1
.)

In Theorem
3.6
we prove that
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
embeds in

(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
, but the inclusion is not continuous, and is proper whenever
d\geq 2
.
Indeed, in Section
7
we prove that
N(H^{2}_{d})
does not even contain the ball algebra.

Theorem 1.19
.

For all
d\geq 2
there is a holomorphic function
f

on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
that is continuous up to the boundary, but is not in
N(H^{2}_{d})
.

Next, we find the dual of
(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
. We prove in Section
4
:

Theorem 1.20
.

The functional

(1.21)

\Gamma:f\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\overline{\gamma_{\alpha}}

is in
(N^{+}_{u},\rho)^{*}
if and only if there exist constants
M,c>0
so that

(1.22)

|\gamma_{\alpha}|\ \leq\ M\omega_{\alpha}e^{-c\sqrt{|\alpha|}}\quad\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

In this case, the series in (
1.21
) converges absolutely for every
f\in N_{u}^{+}
.

Since
N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}})\subsetneq N^{+}_{u}
, and the inclusion is not even continuous,
Theorem
1.20
would not seem strong enough to answer
Question
1.15
. Nevertheless, our most surprising result is that it is, and
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
and

(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
have the same duals.
Putting all these results together we get our principal result.

Theorem 1.23
.

Let
h\in H^{2}_{d}
and let
\Gamma=\langle\cdot,h\rangle_{{H^{2}_{d}}}
be the associated
linear functional. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\Gamma\in(N^{+}_{u},\rho)^{*}
,

(ii)

\Gamma\in(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})^{*}
,

(iii)

h\in{\rm ran}(T_{m}^{*})
for every cyclic
m\in\Mult(H^{2}_{d})
,

(iv)

there exist
M,c>0
so that

(1.24)

|\widehat{h}(\alpha)|\omega_{\alpha}\leq Me^{-c\sqrt{|\alpha|}}

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

In this case, the functional
\Gamma
on
N_{u}^{+}
or on
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
is given
by

\Gamma(f)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle f_{n},h_{n}\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\omega_{\alpha}^{2}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\overline{\widehat{h}(\alpha)},

where both sums converge absolutely, and
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}

and
h=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}
are the homogeneous expansions of
f
and
h
, respectively.

Note: The reason
\omega_{\alpha}
appears on the left in (
1.24
) and on the right in (
1.22
)
is the way we define
\Gamma
. In Theorem
1.23
we use the
{H^{2}_{d}}
inner product, which
is the form we will use in our proofs, and
in Theorem
1.20
we used the
\ell^{2}
inner product on the coefficients, just to make the statement of the theorem more succinct.
This means that
\gamma_{\alpha}=\omega_{\alpha}^{2}\widehat{h}(\alpha)
.

We prove Theorem
1.23
in Sections
5
and
6
.

In Section
8
, we prove that
(N^{+}_{u},{\mathcal{I}})
can be realized as a dense subspace
of a Fréchet space, i.e. a complete locally convex metrizable space, so in particular
the topology
{\mathcal{I}}
on
N^{+}_{u}
is metrizable.
In Section
A
we have an appendix on inductive limit topologies.

2.
Basic properties of
N^{+}_{u}

The following lemma is proved in
[
21
, II.3.1 and II.11.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g\in N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
. Then for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(2.2)

|g(z)|\ \leq\ e^{\frac{2{\left|\kern-0.5382pt\left|\kern-0.5382pt\left|g\right|\kern-0.5382pt\right|\kern-0.5382pt\right|}}{1-|z|}}-1.

Moreover, its Taylor coefficients satisfy

(2.3)

|\widehat{g}(n)|\ \leq\ e^{\sqrt{8n{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|g\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}}(1+\varepsilon_{n})},

where
\varepsilon_{n}
is
o(1)
, depends only on
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
, and decreases as
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
decreases.

For
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
, let

i_{\zeta}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}_{d},\quad z\mapsto z\zeta.

If
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
, then
f\circ i_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})

is the slice function along the direction
\zeta
.
Clearly, we have
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\circ i_{\zeta}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
.
So if
f\in N^{+}_{u}
, then
f\circ i_{\zeta}\in N(\mathbb{D})
and
\lim_{r\to 1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\circ i_{\zeta}-(f\circ i_{\zeta})_{r}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
,
which implies that
f\circ i_{\zeta}\in N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
by Proposition
B.1
.
So
we get from (
2.2
) that

(2.4)

f\in N^{+}_{u}\ \Rightarrow\ |f(w)|\ \leq\ e^{\frac{2{\left|\kern-0.5382pt\left|\kern-0.5382pt\left|f\right|\kern-0.5382pt\right|\kern-0.5382pt\right|}}{1-|w|}}-1,\qquad\forall\ w\in\mathbb{B}_{d}.

Proposition 2.5
.

(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
is an F-space, and multiplication is continuous.
Moreover, the polynomials are dense.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
\rho
defines a metric on
N_{u}^{+}
.
Observe that if
f\in N_{u}
,

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
|\lambda|\leq n<|\lambda|+1
, then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\lambda f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|nf\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq n{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq(|\lambda|+1){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

by the triangle inequality.
From this, it easily follows that
N^{+}_{u}
is closed under scalar multiplication, and hence is a subspace of
N_{u}
.

To see density of the polynomials, note that if
f\in N^{+}_{u}
, then for each
r<1
, the function

f_{r}
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
and hence a uniform limit of polynomials on
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
.
Thus each
f_{r}
and therefore
f
belongs to the
\rho
-closure of the polynomials.

Continuity of addition on
N^{+}_{u}
follows from the triangle inequality.
To see continuity of multiplication, from which scalar multiplication follows as a special case,
note that
if
f,g,f_{0},g_{0}\in N^{+}_{u}
then

fg-f_{0}g_{0}=(f-f_{0})(g-g_{0})+f_{0}(g-g_{0})+g_{0}(f-f_{0}).

Using the triangle inequality together with the inequality
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|FG\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|F\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|G\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
,
we obtain

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|fg-f_{0}g_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-f_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g-g_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{0}(g-g_{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g_{0}(f-f_{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

So it suffices to prove that multiplication is separately continuous.

Since
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|fg\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq(\|f\|_{\infty}+1){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
, we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f(g-g_{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle\ \leq\

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(f-f_{r})(g-g_{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{r}(g-g_{0})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-f_{r}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g-g_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+(\|f_{r}\|_{\infty}+1){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g-g_{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be a homogeneous polynomial of degree

(a)

\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\|h\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\lesssim(n+1)^{(d-1)/2}\|h\|_{\infty}
.

(b)

\max_{\alpha}|\widehat{h}(\alpha)|\omega_{\alpha}\leq\|h\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\lesssim(n+1)^{(d-1)/2}\max_{\alpha}|\widehat{h}(\alpha)|\omega_{\alpha}

Here, the implied constants only depend on

Proof.

(a) We have

\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\|h\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}=\|h\|_{H^{2}_{d}},

since

is homogeneous (see e.g.
[

, Proposition 6.4]

if

, denotes the Hardy space on the ball, then the formula for the norm
of a monomial in

shows that

\|h\|_{H^{2}_{d}}^{2}=\binom{d-1+n}{d-1}\|h\|_{H^{2}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{d})}^{2}\leq\binom{d-1+n}{d-1}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim(n+1)^{d-1}\|h\|_{\infty}^{2}.

so the inequality on the left is obvious, and the inequality on the right follows
from the fact that there are

variables of degree

.
∎

3.
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
is contained in
N^{+}_{u}

Our goal is to show that
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})\subset N^{+}_{u}
. We begin with two lemmata.

Lemma 3.1
.

H^{2}_{d}\subset N^{+}_{u}
and

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\|f\|_{H^{2}_{d}}
for all
f\in H^{2}_{d}
.

Proof.

The inequality
\log(1+x)\leq x
shows that for all
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
, we have

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta}\zeta)|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\leq\|f\circ i_{\zeta}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|f\circ i_{\zeta}\|_{H^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{2}_{d}}.

Hence
H^{2}_{d}\subset N_{u}
, and the inequality in the statement holds.
Moreover,
\rho(f_{r},f)\leq\|f-f_{r}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\to 0
as
r\to 1
, so
f\in N^{+}_{u}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space and let
\psi\in\Mult(\mathcal{H})
with
\|\psi\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
.
Then for all
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\Big\|\frac{1-\psi}{1-r\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq\frac{2}{1+r}.

Proof.

By von Neumann’s inequality,

\Big\|\frac{1-\psi}{1-r\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq\sup_{|z|=1}\Big|\frac{1-z}{1-rz}\Big|.

Straightforward calculus shows that the supremum on the right is attained for
z=-1
and hence
equal to
\frac{2}{1+r}
.
∎

A normalized complete Pick space can be defined to be a Hilbert function space on a set
X
with a reproducing kernel
of the form

k(x,y)\ =\ \frac{1}{1-\langle b(x),b(y)\rangle},

where
b
is some function from
X
into the unit ball of a Hilbert space such that
b(x_{0})=0

for some base point
x_{0}\in X
.
See
[
1
,
2
]
for how this is equivalent to having the complete Pick property.
The multiplier algebra
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
can be identified with a WOT-closed subalgebra of
B(\mathcal{H})
.
In this way,
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
becomes a dual space. On bounded subsets of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
, the resulting
weak-
*
topology agrees with the topology of pointwise convergence on
X
.
Indeed, this follows from density of the linear span of all kernel functions in
\mathcal{H}
.

It follows from a result of Davidson, Ramsay and Shalit, see
[
8
, Corollary 2.7]
, that there is a 1-to-1 correspondence
between multiplier invariant subspaces of a complete Pick space
and weak-
*
closed ideals of the multiplier algebra.
In particular, if
m
is a cyclic multiplier, then
m\Mult(\mathcal{H})
is weak-
*
dense
in
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
. We require the following Kaplansky density type refinement of this fact.
If
f\in\mathcal{H}
, we shall let
[f]
denote the closure in
\mathcal{H}
of
\{mf:m\in\Mult(\mathcal{H})\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a normalized complete Pick space and let
m\in\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.
If
\varphi\in\Mult(\mathcal{H})\cap[m]
with
\|\varphi\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
,
then there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})
in
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
so that

(1)

(\varphi_{n}m)
converges to
\varphi
in the weak-
*
topology, and

(2)

\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi\in[m]
, there exists a sequence
(q_{n})
in
\Mult(\mathcal{H})

such that

(3.4)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|n^{2}(q_{n}m-\varphi)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1.

In this setting,
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
yields a sequence
(\psi_{n})
in
\Mult(\mathcal{H})

forming a contractive column multiplier and a multiplier

\psi\in\Mult(\mathcal{H})
with
\|\psi\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
and
\psi\neq 1
so that

n^{2}(q_{n}m-\varphi)=\frac{\psi_{n}}{1-\psi}\quad\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

In particular,
\|\psi_{n}\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
and thus

(3.5)

\|(1-\psi)(q_{n}m-\varphi)\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Let
r_{n}=1-\frac{1}{n}
and set

\varphi_{n}=\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}q_{n}\in\Mult(\mathcal{H}).

Then

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}q_{n}m\Big\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}(q_{n}m-\varphi)\Big\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}+\Big\|\frac{(1-\psi)\varphi}{1-r_{n}\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}.

We estimate the first summand using (
3.5
) and the second summand using
Lemma
3.2
to find that

\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}(1-r_{n})}+\frac{2}{1+r_{n}}=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{2}{2-\frac{1}{n}},

hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
.

Moreover, since
(q_{n}m)
tends to
\varphi
pointwise, we see that
(\varphi_{n}m)
tends to
\varphi
pointwise
and hence in the weak-
*
topology. Replacing
\varphi_{n}
with
t_{n}\varphi_{n}
for a suitable
sequence of scalars
(t_{n})
in
(0,1)
converging to
1
, we obtain the desired sequence.
∎

Remark 3.11

5.

First we show that Helson’s theorem is true for

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
m\in{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d})
by cyclic.
The function

is in the range of
T_{m}^{*}
if and only if the functional

(5.2)

f\ \mapsto\ \sum\omega_{\alpha}^{2}\widehat{f}(\alpha)\overline{\widehat{h}(\alpha)}=\langle f,h\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}

extends from

to be
a continuous linear functional on
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}

Proof.

Suppose

|\langle f,h\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}|\leq M\ \|mf\|_{H^{2}_{d}}.

Conversely, suppose (

{H^{2}_{d}}
in
\frac{1}{m}{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\ =\

\displaystyle\langle mf,mk\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,T_{m}^{*}g\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}

The function

)

to be

(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),{\mathcal{I}})

6.
Proof of necessity of (

Recall that
P_{\zeta}(z)=\langle z,\zeta\rangle

To prove that (ii) implies (iv) in Theorem

by studying the slice functionals

First, we observe that outer functions in

lift to cyclic mulipliers of
H^{2}_{d}

Lemma 6.1
.

If
\phi

H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, then for every
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
, the function

\phi\circ P_{\zeta}

{\rm Mult}(H^{2}_{d})

\|\phi\circ P_{\zeta}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}

Multiplication by
\langle w,\zeta\rangle

the norm of multiplication by
\phi(\langle w,t\zeta\rangle)

bounded by the norm of
\phi
in
H^{\infty}
. As

, these multiplication operators converge in

\phi(\langle w,\zeta\rangle)

Now suppose

is outer. Then there are polynomials

in

.
Therefore
\phi(\langle w,\zeta\rangle)p_{n}(\langle w,\zeta\rangle)\to 1
in

is cyclic.
∎

As a consequence, we show that the Smirnov class in one variable embeds continuously into
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For

(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),{\mathcal{I}})\to(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),{\mathcal{I}}),\quad f\mapsto f\circ P_{\zeta},

Let

be the map in the statement of the lemma.
Recall that by the universal property of the inductive limit topology,

of
H^{2}
, the restriction of

to

is continuous.
But

is a cyclic multiplier of

Since the inclusion
\frac{1}{m\circ P_{\zeta}}H^{2}_{d}\subset N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})

∎

In

, it was shown that the dual spaces of

Let

The key step in proving (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) in Theorem

is to show that
the slice functionals
\Gamma_{\zeta}
are not only individually continuous, but equicontinuous on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)

To this end, we require some more preliminary results.
For cyclic multipliers that arise as in Lemma
6.1
,

3.3
can be refined to conclude that
(\varphi_{n})
are cyclic.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\psi\in H^{\infty}
be outer, let
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
and let
m=\psi\circ P_{\zeta}
.
Then there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})

of cyclic multipliers of
H^{2}_{d}
so that

(1)

(\varphi_{n}m)
converges to
1
in the weak-
*
topology of
\Mult(H^{2}_{d})
, and

(2)

\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\|\psi\|_{\infty}=1
.
Since
\psi
is outer,

\psi(z)=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{i\theta}+z}{e^{i\theta}-z}\log|\psi(e^{i\theta})|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Big)

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Define

\psi_{n}(z)=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{i\theta}+z}{e^{i\theta}-z}\log(\min(n,|\psi(e^{i\theta})|^{-1}))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Big).

Then
\psi_{n}\in H^{\infty}
is outer,
\|\psi_{n}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1

, the dominated

\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\psi(z)}

\varphi_{n}=\psi_{n}\circ P_{\zeta}
.
Then each

Since

\Mult(H^{2}_{d})
.
∎

On bounded subsets of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
, the weak-
*
topology coincides with the topology of pointwise convergence.
The following simple lemma shows that it sometimes suffices to establish convergence at a single point.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a normalized complete Pick space on
X

and let
(\varphi_{n})
be a sequence in the closed unit ball of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.
If there exists a point

so that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{0})=1
,
then
(\varphi_{n})
converges to
1
in the weak-
*
topology of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

By renormalizing the kernel (cf.
[
2
, Section 2.6]
), we may assume that the kernel is normalized at

.
In this setting, we find that

\|1-\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=1+\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-2\mathrm{Re\,}\varphi_{n}(x_{0})\leq 2-2\mathrm{Re\,}\varphi_{n}(x_{0})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

In particular,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)=1
for all

.

We will make use of infinite products of multipliers.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a normalized complete Pick space on
X
and let
(\psi_{n})
be a sequence in the closed unit ball
of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
. Assume that there exists
x_{0}\in X
so that
\sum_{n}|1-\psi_{n}(x_{0})|<\infty
.

(a)

The infinite product
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}
converges in the weak-
*
topology of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.

(b)

If each
\psi_{n}
is cyclic, then
\psi=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}
is cyclic as well.

Proof.

(a)
For
M\geq N
, let
\Psi_{N,M}=\prod_{n=N}^{M}\psi_{n}
, which belongs to the closed unit ball of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.
The assumption implies that
\Psi_{N,M}(x_{0})
converges to
1
as
M\geq N\to\infty
,
hence
\Psi_{N,M}(x)
converges to
1
as
M\geq N\to\infty
for all

by Lemma

.
From this, we deduce that the partial products

converge pointwise on

and hence
in the weak-

topology of
\Mult(\mathcal{H})
.

(b) Since
\psi_{1}
is cyclic, there is a sequence
(f_{k})
in
\mathcal{H}

so that
\psi_{1}f_{k}\to 1
. Therefore
\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\psi_{n}\in[\psi]
.
Iterating, we get for every

that
\prod_{n=N}^{\infty}\psi_{n}\in[\psi]
.
As
N\to\infty
,
\prod_{n=N}^{\infty}\psi_{n}
converges weakly to

in
\mathcal{H}
by part (a),
so
\psi
is cyclic.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

It is in general not true that non-zero weak-

limits of cyclic multipliers are cyclic. For instance,
if
f\in H^{\infty}
is a singular inner function, then
f_{r}
is outer for each
0<r<1
and
f_{r}
converges
to
f
in the weak-
*
topology of
H^{\infty}
as
r\to 1
.

The following is the key lemma needed to prove equicontinuity of the slice functionals.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
f\in N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
. Then the map

\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}\to(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I}),\quad\zeta\mapsto f\circ P_{\zeta},

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
(\zeta_{n})
be a sequence in
\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
that tends to
\zeta
.
It suffices to show that a subsequence of
(f\circ P_{\zeta_{n}})
converges to
f\circ P_{\zeta}
in
(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
.
Let
F=f\circ P_{\zeta}\in N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
.
It is elementary to construct a sequence
(U_{n})
of

unitaries tending to

so that
U_{n}\zeta_{n}=\zeta
for all

.

Then
P_{\zeta}\circ U_{n}=P_{\zeta_{n}}
, so we have to show that a subsequence of
(F\circ U_{n})
tends to
F
in
(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
.

We will construct a cyclic multiplier
M
so that a subsequence of
(F\circ U_{n})
tends to
F
in
\frac{1}{M}H^{2}_{d}
.
Continuity of the inclusion
\frac{1}{M}H^{2}_{d}\subset(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
then shows that the subsequence also
converges to
F
in
(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
, as desired.
To construct

, write
f=\frac{g}{u}
for some
g\in H^{2}
and some outer function
u\in H^{\infty}
.
Let
G=g\circ P_{\zeta}
and
m=u\circ P_{\zeta}
, so
F=\frac{G}{m}
.
The multiplier
M
will essentially be an infinite product of multipliers
of the form

, suitably corrected to ensure convergence.

First, by Lemma
6.4
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})

of cyclic multipliers of
H^{2}_{d}
so that
\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 1
for all

and so that
|1-\varphi_{n}(0)m(0)|\leq 2^{-n}

Next, since
m\circ U_{n}
converges to

in the norm of

, we may pass to a subsequence of
(U_{n})
to achieve that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{4n}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}^{2}\|m-m\circ U_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{d}}\leq 1.

Then
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
yields a sequence
(\psi_{n})
in
\Mult(H^{2}_{d})
forming
a contractive column multiplier and a contractive multiplier
\psi\in\Mult(H^{2}_{d})
with
\psi(0)=0

so that

2^{2n}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}(m-m\circ U_{n})=\frac{\psi_{n}}{1-\psi};

whence

(6.4)

\|(1-\psi)(m-m\circ U_{n})\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 2^{-2n}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}^{-1}.

Let
r_{n}=1-2^{-n}
and let

\eta_{n}=\varphi_{n}(m\circ U_{n})\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi},

which is a product of cyclic multipliers and hence cyclic
(cyclicity of
1-\psi
was shown in
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
).
Then

\|\eta_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq\Big\|\varphi_{n}(m\circ U_{n}-m)\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}+\Big\|\varphi_{n}m\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}.

We estimate the first summand using (
6.4
) and the second
summand using Lemma
3.2
combined with the bound
\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 1
to find that

\|\eta_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 2^{-2n}\frac{1}{1-r_{n}}+\frac{2}{1+r_{n}}\leq 1+2^{-n+1}.

Let
t_{n}=1-2^{-n+1}
. Then
\|t_{n}\eta_{n}\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 1
and
t_{n}\eta_{n}
is cyclic for
n\geq 2
.
Moreover, since
\psi(0)=0
, we have
\eta_{n}(0)=\varphi_{n}(0)m(0)
.
Since
|1-\varphi_{n}(0)m(0)|\leq 2^{-n}
, it follows that
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|1-t_{n}\eta_{n}(0)|<\infty
.
Thus, Lemma
6.6
shows that the infinite product of the
t_{n}\eta_{n}
converges
to a cyclic multiplier. Let

M=m^{2}\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}t_{n}\eta_{n}.

Then
M
is a cyclic multiplier.

We finish the proof by showing that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|M(F\circ U_{n}-F)\|_{H^{2}_{d}}=0
.
Indeed, since
F=\frac{G}{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\|M(F\circ U_{n}-F)\|_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m^{2}\eta_{n}(F\circ U_{n}-F)\|_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\varphi_{n}m\frac{1-\psi}{1-r_{n}\psi}\Big\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\|m(m\circ U_{n})(F\circ U_{n}-F)\|_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|m(G\circ U_{n})-(m\circ U_{n})G\|_{H^{2}_{d}},

where in the last estimate, we used again that
\|\varphi_{n}m\|_{\Mult(H^{2}_{d})}\leq 1

and Lemma
3.2
. Since
G\circ U_{n}
tends to
G
in
H^{2}_{d}
and the sequence of multipliers

(m\circ U_{n})
tends to
m
in the strong operator topology,
we conclude that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|M(F\circ U_{n}-F)\|_{H^{2}_{d}}=0
, as desired.
∎

We are ready to prove equicontinuity of the slice functionals
\Gamma_{\zeta}
defined
by
\Gamma_{\zeta}(f)=\Gamma(f\circ P_{\zeta})
.

Lemma 6.9
.

If
\Gamma\in(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})^{*}
, then the functionals
\{\Gamma_{\zeta}:\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}\}

are equicontinuous on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.3
, each
\Gamma_{\zeta}
is continuous on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)
.
By the uniform boundedness principle for F-spaces (see, e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 2.6]
), it therefore suffices to show that the functionals
\Gamma_{\zeta}

are pointwise bounded.
If
f\in N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
, then
\{f\circ P_{\zeta}:\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}\}

is a compact set in
(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})
by Lemma
6.8
and compactness of
\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}
.
Hence,

\sup_{\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}|\Gamma_{\zeta}(f)|=\sup_{\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}}|\Gamma({f\circ P_{\zeta}})|<\infty,

establishing pointwise boundedness.
∎

Finally, we can finish the proof of Theorem
1.23
.

Proof of (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) in Theorem
1.23
.

If
\Gamma=\langle\cdot,h\rangle\in(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\mathcal{I})^{*}
,
then the slice functionals
\{\Gamma_{\zeta}:\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{d}\}
are equicontinuous on
(N^{+}(\mathbb{D}),\rho)

by Lemma
6.9
. Thus, an application of Lemma
4.3

shows that the homogeneous components
h_{n}
of
h
satisfy the decay condition
\|h_{n}\|_{\infty}=O(e^{-c\sqrt{n}})
for some
c>0
.
From Corollary
2.7
, we get (
1.24
), for some
c>0
.
∎

7.
N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}})
vs.
N_{u}^{+}

The goal of this section is to construct functions in the ball algebra
A(\mathbb{B}_{d})
(and hence in
N_{u}^{+}
)
that
do not belong to
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
. Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\tau:\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}},\quad z\mapsto d^{d/2}z_{1}z_{2}\ldots z_{d}.

It follows from the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means that
\tau
maps
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}

onto
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{d}
onto
\mathbb{D}
. Let

a_{n}=\|\tau^{n}\|^{-2}_{H^{2}_{d}}

and let
\mathcal{K}_{d}
be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
\mathbb{D}
with reproducing kernel

K(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(z\overline{w})^{n}.

Then
\mathcal{K}_{d}
embeds isometrically into
H^{2}_{d}
. For a proof of the following lemma,
see
[
5
, Lemma 7.1]
. The weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
is the reproducing
kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
with reproducing kernel
(1-z\overline{w})^{-1/2}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The map

V:\mathcal{K}_{d}\to H^{2}_{d},\quad f\mapsto f\circ\tau,

is an isometry whose range
is the space of all functions in
H^{2}_{d}
that are power series in
z_{1}z_{2}\ldots z_{d}
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is the weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
, and

\mathcal{K}_{d}\subset A(\mathbb{D})
for
d\geq 4
.

The following proposition makes it possible to construct functions in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})

that do not belong to
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
. In fact, the resulting functions are not even quotients
of multipliers with non-vanishing denominators.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be a function with the property that

g\cdot f\notin\mathcal{K}_{d}\quad\text{ for all }g\in\mathcal{K}_{d}\setminus\{0\}.

Then
f\circ\tau\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
, but
f\circ\tau\notin N(H^{2}_{d})
.

We first consider an example showing that the hypothesis of the proposition can be satisfied.

Example 7.3
.

Let
d\geq 4
and let
f
be a Blaschke product whose zeros accumulate at every point of
\mathbb{T}
.
Since
\mathcal{K}_{d}\subset A(\mathbb{D})
by Lemma
7.1
, we find that

gf\notin\mathcal{K}_{d}
for all
g\in\mathcal{K}_{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus,
f\circ\tau\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
,
but
f\notin N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
by the proposition.

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

Let
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be as in the statement of Proposition
7.2
.
Since
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, we have
f\circ\tau\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{d})
.
Suppose towards a contradiction that
f\circ\tau=\frac{\varphi}{\psi}
for
functions
\varphi,\psi\in H^{2}_{d}
and
\psi
non-vanishing, hence

\varphi=\psi(f\circ\tau)
.

We will show that we can achieve that
\varphi
and
\psi
belong to the
range of the isometry
V
of Lemma
7.1
.
To this end, we decompose
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}

and
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
into holomorphic functions so that
\varphi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}

are power series in
z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{d}
and so that no monomial
of the form
(z_{1}z_{2}\ldots z_{d})^{n}
occurs in
\varphi_{2}
or
\psi_{2}
.
Since
f\circ\tau
is a power series in
z_{1}z_{2}\ldots z_{d}
,
it follows that

(7.5)

\varphi_{1}=(f\circ\tau)\psi_{2}.

Moreover, since
\varphi,\psi\in H^{2}_{d}
, we also have
\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}\in H^{2}_{d}

and
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi(0)\neq 0
.
Hence,
\varphi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}
belong
to the range of the isometry
V
of Lemma
7.1
,
so there exist

g,h\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
such that
\varphi_{1}=h\circ\tau
and
\psi_{1}=g\circ\tau
,
and clearly
g(0)=\psi_{1}(0)\neq 0
.

From (
7.5
), we infer that
(h\circ\tau)=(f\circ\tau)\cdot(g\circ\tau)
.
As
\tau
maps
\mathbb{B}_{d}
onto
\mathbb{D}
, this means that
h=f\cdot g
, contradicting the assumption on
f
.
Thus,
f\circ\tau\notin N(H^{2}_{d})
.
∎

The construction of Example
7.3
can be refined to work for all
d\geq 2

and to yield a function in the ball algebra
A(\mathbb{B}_{d})
.

Theorem

1.19

A(\mathbb{B}_{d})\not\subset N(H^{2}_{d})
for all natural numbers
d\geq 2
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
d=2
. We will use Lemma
7.1
to embed the weighted
Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
into
H^{2}_{2}
.

A sequence
(z_{n})
in
\mathbb{D}
is called a zero set for
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
if there exists
a function
f\in\mathcal{D}_{1/2}\setminus\{0\}
that vanishes precisely on
\{z_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
By
[
20
, Theorem 2]
, there exists a Blachke sequence
(z_{n})
that is not a zero
set for
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
and whose only cluster point is
1
.
Let
B
the Blaschke product with zeros
(z_{n})
and let
f(z)=B(z)(1-z)
. Then
f\in A(\mathbb{D})
.
Since
(z_{n})
is not a zero set for
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
, it is a uniqueness
set for
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
by
[
2
, Proposition 9.37]
,
meaning that any function in
\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
vanishing on
\{z_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

is identically zero.
Thus,

g\cdot f\notin\mathcal{D}_{1/2}
for any
g\in\mathcal{D}_{1/2}\setminus\{0\}
. In this
setting, Lemma
7.1
and Proposition
7.2
imply that
f\circ\tau\notin N(H^{2}_{2})
,
but clearly
f\circ\tau\in A(\mathbb{B}_{2})
.

The case of an arbitrary natural number
d\geq 2
is easily deduced from the case
d=2
.
Indeed,
let
F\in A(\mathbb{B}_{2})
but
F\notin N(H^{2}_{2})
, let

P:\mathbb{B}_{d}\to\mathbb{B}_{2}
be the projection onto the first
2
coordinates and define
G=F\circ P
.
Then
G\circ P\in A(\mathbb{B}_{d})
. If
G
were of the form
G=\varphi/\psi
for
\varphi,\psi\in\Mult(H^{2}_{d})

and
\psi
non-vanishing, then

F=G\big|_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}=\frac{\varphi\big|_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}}{\psi\big|_{\mathbb{B}_{2}}}

would be a quotient of two functions in
\Mult(H^{2}_{2})
, a contradiction. Thus,
G\notin N(H^{2}_{d})
.
∎

Theorem
1.19
also implies the following fact about topologies
on the Smirnov class
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a natural number and let
\tau
be a topology on
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
that
gives
N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
the structure of a topological vector space. Suppose that

(1)

point evaluations at points in
\mathbb{B}_{d}
are
\tau
-continuous, and

(2)

(N^{+}(H^{2}_{d}),\tau)
is sequentially complete.

Then there exists a sequence
(f_{n})
of functions analytic in a neighborhood of

\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
such that
(f_{n})
tends to zero uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
, but is not null
with respect to
\tau
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.19
, there exists
f\in A(\mathbb{B}_{d})
but
f\notin N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

g_{n}(z)=f((1-\tfrac{1}{n+1})z),

so that
(g_{n})
is analytic in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
and
converges to
f
uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
. Since
f\notin N^{+}(H^{2}_{d})
, the first assumption on
\tau
implies
that
(g_{n})
does not converge with respect to
\tau
, and hence it is not
\tau
-Cauchy by the second assumption.
Therefore, there exist strictly increasing sequences
(n_{k})
and
(m_{k})
of natural numbers and a
\tau
-neighborhood
U
of the origin such that
g_{n_{k}}-g_{m_{k}}\notin U
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
f_{k}=g_{n_{k}}-g_{m_{k}}
.
Then
(f_{k})
is not
\tau
-null, but converges to zero uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{d}}
.
∎

8.
The containing Fréchet space of
N^{+}_{u}

In
[
30
]
, Yanagihara described a Fréchet space
F^{+}
that contained the Smirnov class, a sort of “locally convex completion”. In this section, we shall do the same for
N^{+}_{u}
. Throughout this section, we shall assume that
f
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
and that
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}
is its decomposition into homogeneous polynomials.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f
be analytic on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
, and for
0\leq r<1
write
M(r,f)=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle{\limsup_{r\to 1}(1-r)\log M(r,f)\leq 0,}

(ii)

\displaystyle{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq 0,}

(iii)

\displaystyle{\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|f_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq 0,}

(iv)

\displaystyle{\limsup_{|\alpha|\to\infty}\frac{\log|\hat{h}(\alpha)|\omega_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{|\alpha|}}\leq 0.}

Proof.

The equivalence of (ii), (iii), and (iv) follows from Lemma
2.6
. If
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
w\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
, then
f(zw)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}(w)z^{n}
. Thus the equivalence of (i) and (ii) follows from Lemma 1 of
[
30
]
, p. 96.
∎

Let
F^{+}_{u}
be the collection of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
that satisfy any of the conditions of Lemma
8.1
. For a positive constant
c
and
f\in F^{+}_{u}
set

(8.2)

\|f\|^{2}_{c}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{2}_{d}}\ e^{-c\sqrt{n}},

and define a locally convex topology on
F^{+}_{u}
by the family of semi-norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{c}\}_{c>0}
. Since
\|f\|^{2}_{c}
is increasing as
c
decreases to 0, it is clear that
F^{+}_{u}
is metrizable, and a translation invariant metric is given by

(8.3)

d_{F}(f,g)\ =\ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\frac{\|f-g\|_{1/k}}{1+\|f-g\|_{1/k}}.

Furthermore, one easily checks that for each
f=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}
, the partial sums
p_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}
converge to
f
in
\|\cdot\|_{c}
for each
c>0
, hence the polynomials are dense in
F^{+}_{u}
. Moreover, a routine argument shows that
F^{+}_{u}
is complete with respect to the metric
d_{F}
. Therefore
F^{+}_{u}
is a Fréchet space.

Proposition 8.4
.

(N^{+}_{u},\rho)
embeds continuously into
F^{+}_{u}
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
2.8
and the closed graph theorem for
F
-spaces,
see, e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 2.15]
.
∎

The next result, combined with Theorem
4.4
and Corollary
2.7
,
shows that
F_{u}^{+}
has the same dual space as
N_{u}^{+}
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
h
be analytic on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
, let
h=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}
be its expansion into homogeneous polynomials, and assume that there are
M,c>0
such that
\|h_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\leq Me^{-c\sqrt{n}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each
f\in F^{+}_{u}
, the series

\Gamma_{h}(f)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\langle f_{n},h_{n}\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\omega_{\alpha}^{2}\hat{f}(\alpha)\overline{\hat{h}(\alpha)}

converges absolutely, and
\Gamma_{h}
defines a continuous linear functional on
F^{+}_{u}
.

Moreover, every continuous linear functional on
F^{+}_{u}
is of this form.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies that

\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\omega_{\alpha}^{2}|\hat{f}(\alpha)\hat{h}(\alpha)|\leq\|f_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\|h_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}\leq M\|f_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}e^{-c\sqrt{n}}.

Hence, by another application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we see that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\omega_{\alpha}^{2}|\hat{f}(\alpha)\hat{h}(\alpha)|\ \leq\ M\|f\|_{c}\ \sqrt{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-c\sqrt{n}}}

holds for each
f\in F^{+}_{u}
. This implies that
\Gamma_{h}
is continuous on
F^{+}_{u}
.

The converse follows from the continuous inclusion
(N_{u}^{+},\rho)\subset F_{u}^{+}
in Proposition
8.4
and our earlier description of the dual space of
(N_{u}^{+},\rho)
in Theorem
4.4
.
We provide a direct argument.

If
\Phi
is a continuous linear functional on
F^{+}_{u}
,
then it follows from the definition of the topology on
F^{+}_{u}
in terms of the seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{c}
that there exist
c>0
and
M\geq 0
so that
|\Phi(f)|\leq M\|f\|_{c}

for every
f\in F_{u}^{+}
.
To see this from the metric
d_{F}
, notice that by continuity of
\Phi
, there is
\delta>0
such that
|\Phi(f)|<1
, whenever
f\in F^{+}_{u}
with
d_{F}(f,0)<\delta
. Choose
N
such that
2^{-N}<\delta
and set
\varepsilon=\frac{\delta}{2N}
. Then for each
f\in F^{+}_{u}
with
\|f\|_{1/N}<\varepsilon
we have

\displaystyle d_{F}(f,0)

\displaystyle\ \leq\

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}2^{-k}\|f\|_{1/k}+2^{-N}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle N\|f\|_{1/N}+\delta/2

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\delta.

This implies that
|\Phi(f)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{1/N}
for every polynomial
f
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{N}
denote the Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{d}
with norm given
by (
8.2
) with
c=\frac{1}{N}
.
Then

\Phi
extends to define a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{H}_{N}
.
By the Riesz representation theorem, there is
g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}
such that
\|g\|_{1/N}\leq 1/\varepsilon
and

\Phi(f)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{N}}\langle f_{n},g_{n}\rangle_{H^{2}_{d}}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
set
h_{n}=e^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{N}}g_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\|h_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\ =\

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{N}}\|g_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{d}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2N}}\|g\|_{1/N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2N}}}{\varepsilon},

and
\Phi=\Gamma_{h}
.
∎

From Proposition
8.4
, we get that
N^{+}_{u}
embeds continuously into
F^{+}_{u}
,
and by Theorem
3.6
, so does
\frac{1}{m}H^{2}_{d}
for each cyclic multiplier
m
.
Since
F_{u}^{+}
is locally convex, it follows that
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
embeds continuously into
F_{u}^{+}
.
So if we use the metric
d_{F}
from (
8.3
), we get that the identity map is continuous from

(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
to
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
. Moreover, since the polynomials are dense in
F^{+}_{u}
, we have that
N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}})
is dense
in
F^{+}_{u}
. Therefore
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
has the same dual as
F^{+}_{u}
, and by Theorem
1.23
and Proposition
8.5
, this is the
same as the dual of
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a locally convex vector space with dual
{\mathcal{V}}^{*}
, there is a finest
topology on
\mathcal{V}
with this dual, called the Mackey topology for the pair.
Certain conditions on a topology force it to coincide with the Mackey topology; these include being metrizable (as
d_{F}
is) or being barrelled (as
{\mathcal{I}}
is)
[
28
, 10-1.9]
.
Putting these facts together, we get the following conclusion.

Theorem 8.6
.

On
N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}})
, the inductive limit topology
{\mathcal{I}}
and the metric topology
d_{F}
coincide.

For the convenience of the reader, here is a direct proof.

Proof.

As the by Theorem
1.23
and Proposition
8.5
identity map is continuous from

(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
to
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
by the discussion preceding the theorem, we need to prove that the identity map from

(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
to
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
is also continuous. To do this, it suffices to show that for each continuous semi-norm
p
on
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
, the inclusion
(N^{+},d_{F})
to
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
is continuous, since the topology on any locally convex space is give by its continuous semi-norms (see e.g.
[
11
, 1.10.1]
).

So let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
tending to zero. We claim that
(f_{n})
tends to zero weakly in
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
. Indeed, if
\Gamma
is a continuous linear functional on
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
, then
\Gamma
is continuous on
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),{\mathcal{I}})
and hence on
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})
by Theorem
1.23
and Proposition
8.5
.
Thus,
\Gamma(f_{n})
tends to
0
for all
\Gamma
, establishing weak convergence. The uniform boundedness principle for the semi-normed space
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
implies that
\sup_{n}p(f_{n})<\infty
. (This follows from the uniform boundedness principle for normed spaces by taking a quotient.) So we have shown that if
d_{F}(f_{n},0)\to 0
, then
p(f_{n})
is bounded. But if
d_{F}(f_{n},0)\to 0
, then by passing to a subsequence, we may achieve that
d_{F}(nf_{n},0)\to 0
, hence
p(nf_{n})
is bounded, so
(f_{n})
tends to
0
in
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
. This proves continuity of
(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),d_{F})\to(N^{+}({H^{2}_{d}}),p)
.
∎

Appendix A
Inductive limits

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a topological vector space, and for each
\alpha
in some index set
A
let

X_{\alpha}
be a topological vector space that embeds continuously in
\mathcal{V}
. For example,
\mathcal{V}
could be all holomorphic functions on some domain
\Omega
, with the topology of locally uniform convergence, and each
X_{\alpha}
could be some Banach space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
.

Let
Y=\cup_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
. There are two different ways to define an inductive limit topology on
Y
. The first, which we shall call the locally convex inductive limit topology and denote
{\mathcal{I}}
,
has as a neighborhood basis at
0
all absolutely convex sets
U
with the property that
U\cap X_{\alpha}
is open
for every
\alpha\in A
. A basis for the topology is sets of the form
y+U
, where
y\in Y
and
U

is a neighborhood of
0
.
The topology
{\mathcal{I}}
has advantages:

•

It is a vector space topology (addition and scalar multiplication are continuous).

•

The TVS space
(Y,{\mathcal{I}})
is locally convex.

But

•

It can be hard to describe what a general open set is.

•

The topology may be trivial, with
Y
the only non-empty open set (see Example
A.1
).

A second topology, which we shall call the non-locally convex inductive limit topology and denote
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc}
,
has as a basis all sets
U
with the property that
U\cap X_{\alpha}
is open
for every
\alpha\in A
.
In general, the topologies
{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc}
are distinct, though G. Edgar points out in

[
10
]
that even Choquet did not realize this.
Topology
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc}
has advantages

•

It is much simpler to describe the open sets.

•

It frequently turns out to be given by a complete metric (eg. for
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})

[
17
]
, or Example
A.1
).

However

•

It may not be a vector space topology — addition may not be continuous
[
10
]
, where the example
is all continuous functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
, viewed as the union of the continuous functions
supported on
[-n,n]
.

It is immediate that
(Y,{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc})
embeds continuously in
(Y,{\mathcal{I}})
.
Moreover, if
\Gamma
is a linear functional on
Y
, one can check its continuity just by looking at the
preimage of convex open sets in
\mathbb{C}
, so the continuous linear functionals are the same on both spaces:

(Y,{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc})^{*}\ =\ (Y,{\mathcal{I}})^{*}.

Example A.1
.

An illuminating example is to
let

\mathcal{V}
denote the space of all measurable functions on
[0,1]
, identifying those that are zero a.e.
with respect to Lebesgue measure.
For
w
any integrable function that is positive a.e., let
L^{2}(w)=\{f:\int|f|^{2}w<\infty\}
.
Then
\mathcal{V}=\cup_{w}L^{2}(w)
.
The topology
{\mathcal{I}}
is the trivial topology, and the topology
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc}
is the topology of convergence in measure,
which is given by a complete metric.

Indeed, let us define a metric on
\mathcal{V}
by

d_{L}(f,g)\ =\ \int_{0}^{1}\min(1,|f(x)-g(x)|)dx.

This is complete, and gives the topology of convergence in measure.
It can be shown that the topologies given by
d_{L}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rm nlc}
coincide.

Appendix B
Smirnov class on the disk

On the disk, the following three definitions
of the Smirnov class
N^{+}(\mathbb{D})
are equivalent.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
f\in N(\mathbb{D})
. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|f(re^{i\theta})|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|f(e^{i\theta})|\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
.

(ii)

\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta})-f(e^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=0
.

(iii)

\lim_{r\to 1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-f_{r}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
It is known that (i) implies that
\{\log^{+}|f_{r}|:0<r<1\}
is uniformly integrable
over
\mathbb{T}
; see
[
12
, Theorem A.3.7]
.
Hence
\{\log(1+|f_{r}|):0<r<1\}
is uniformly integrable.
Moreover, if
g\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})
, then
\log(1+|g|)
is subharmonic
because
t\mapsto\log(1+e^{t})
is increasing and convex. Thus,
by Vitali’s convergence theorem, we find that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-f_{r}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(se^{i\theta})-f(rse^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(e^{i\theta})-f(re^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

Applying Vitali’s convergence theorem again, we see that the last expression tends to
0
as
r\to 1
, so (iii) holds.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) By Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta})-f(e^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<s<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(sre^{i\theta})-f(se^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f-f_{r}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\xrightarrow{r\to 1}0,

so (ii) holds.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) From the elementary inequality
|\log^{+}a-\log^{+}b|\leq\log(1+|a-b|)
for
a,b\geq 0
,
we deduce that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\log^{+}|f(re^{i\theta})|-\log^{+}|f(e^{i\theta})||\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log(1+|f(re^{i\theta})-f(e^{i\theta})|)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

so (ii) implies that
\log^{+}|f_{r}|
tends to
\log^{+}|f|
as
r\to 1
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
In particular, (i) holds.
∎

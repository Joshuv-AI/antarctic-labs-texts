State-based Modal Logics for Free Choice
Maria Aloni, Aleksi Anttila, and Fan Yang label=e1]m.d.aloni@uva.nl label=e2]aleksi.i.anttila@helsinki.fi label=e3]fan.yang.c@gmail.com

Abstract
We study the mathematical properties of bilateral state-based modal logic (BSML), a modal logic employing state-based semantics (also known as team semantics), which has been used to account for free choice inferences and related linguistic phenomena. This logic extends classical modal logic with a nonemptiness atom which is true in a state if and only if the state is nonempty. We introduce two extensions of BSML and show that the extensions are expressively complete, and develop natural deduction axiomatizations for the three logics.

Bilateral State-based Modal Logic

Definition 2.2
.

A

Proposition 2.4

(Replacement w.r.t. strong equivalence)
.

For any formulas

, and

\theta[\varphi/p]\equivclosed\theta[\psi/p]

Proof.

By induction on

\theta=\mnDiamond\eta

\mnNeg\mnOslash\eta[\varphi/p]\equiv\mnNeg\eta[\varphi/p]\equiv\\

Throughout the paper, we make extensive use of the following properties:

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a formula
\varphi

–

is
downward closed
, provided
[M,s\models\varphi\text{ and }t\subseteq s]\implies M,t\models\varphi
;

–

is
union closed
, provided
[M,s\models\varphi\text{ for all }s\in S\neq\emptyset]\implies M,\bigcup S\models\varphi
;

–

has the
empty state property
, provided
M,\emptyset\models\varphi\text{ for all }M
;

–

is
flat
, provided
M,s\models\varphi\iff M,\{w\}\models\varphi\text{ for all }w\in s
.

It is easy to check that a formula is flat if and only if it is downwards closed, union closed, and has the empty state property. A simple induction shows:

Fact 2.7
.

If a formula
\varphi
does not contain
ne
, then it is downward closed and has the empty state property. If
\varphi
does not contain
\intd
, then it is union closed.

In particular, formulas of
\mathrm{BSML}
and
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
are union closed; formulas of
\mathrm{ML}
are flat (as they are downward and union closed, and have the empty state property).

It is also easy to verify that
\mnDiamond\varphi
and
\mnBox\varphi
are always flat. It follows from the flatness of
\mathrm{ML}
-formulas that the state-based semantics for
\mathrm{ML}
correspond with the standard (single-world) Kripke semantics in the sense of the following fact:

Fact 2.8
.

For any formula
\alpha\in\mathrm{ML}
:

\displaystyle M,s\models\alpha

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle M,\{w\}\models\alpha\text{ for all }w\in s

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle M,w\models\alpha\text{ for all }w\in s.

Proof.

The first equivalence follows from flatness, and the second from
\{w\}\models\alpha
iff

(proved by an easy induction).
∎

As an immediate consequence of Fact

of
\mathrm{ML}
-formulas,

in the state-semantics sense iff
\Delta\models\alpha
in the usual single-world-semantics sense. For this reason, when discussing
\mathrm{ML}
-formulas, we simply use the notation
\models
to refer to both state-based and world-based entailment (similarly for
\equiv
).

3
Expressive Power and Normal Forms

In this section, we study the expressive power of
\mathrm{BSML}
and its extensions. We measure the expressive power of the logics in terms of the
properties
—sets of pointed models—they can express. It is well-known that the expressive power of classical modal logic
\mathrm{ML}
is characterized by
bisimulation-invariance
: the properties expressible in
\mathrm{ML}
are invariant (closed) under bisimulation. This also holds in the state-based modal setting: our logics are invariant under
state bisimulation
. In Section
3.1
, we introduce state bisimulation and prove bisimulation-invariance; in Section
3.2
, we apply this notion to show expressive completeness results for our logics.

3.1
Bisimulation

We recall briefly some standard results concerning bisimulation for classical modal logic and standard Kripke semantics; for more comprehensive discussion, we refer the reader to, for instance,
[

23
]

Throughout this section, we fix a finite set
\mathsf{X}\subseteq\mathsf{Prop}
of propositional variables. We omit mention of
\mathsf{X}
whenever doing so does not result in confusion in order to keep our notation light. A
pointed model
(over

(M,w)

M

\mathsf{X}

Definition 3.1

(Bisimilarity)
.

For any

and any
(M,w)
(over
\mathsf{Y}\supseteq\mathsf{X}
) and
(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})
(over
\mathsf{Y}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathsf{X}

M,w\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
(or simply

(M,w)
and
(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})
are
\mathsf{X},k

–

M,w\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{0}M,w:\iff

M^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models p
.

–

M,w\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k+1}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}:\iff M,w\rightleftharpoons_{0}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
and

–

[forth] for all
v\in R[w]
there is a
v^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}[w^{\prime}]

M,v\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},v^{\prime}
;

–

[back] for all
v^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}[w^{\prime}]
there is a
v\in R[w]
such that
M,v\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},v^{\prime}
.

The
modal depth

md(\varphi)
of a formula
\varphi
is defined recursively as:

–

md(p)=md(\textsc{ne})=0

–

md(\mnNeg\varphi)=md(\mnOslash\varphi)=md(\varphi)

–

md(\varphi\land\psi)=md(\varphi\vee\psi)=md(\varphi\intd\psi)=max\{md(\varphi),md(\psi)\}

–

md(\mnDiamond\varphi)=md(\varphi)+1

We say that
(M,w)
and
(M,w^{\prime})
are
{\mathsf{X}},k
-equivalent
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
), written
M,w\equiv^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
(or simply

), if for all
\alpha({\mathsf{X}})\in\mathrm{ML}
with
md(\alpha)\leq k
:
M,w\models\alpha\iff M^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models\alpha
.

Definition 3.2

(Hintikka formulas)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(M,w)
be a pointed model over
\mathsf{Y}\supseteq\mathsf{X}
. We define the
k
-th Hintikka formula

\chi^{\mathsf{X},k}_{M,w}
(or simply
\chi^{k}_{w}
) of
(M,w)
recursively as:

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathsf{X},0}_{M,w}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigwedge\{p\;|\;p\in\mathsf{X},w\in V(p)\}\land\bigwedge\{\mnNeg p\;|\;p\in\mathsf{X},w\notin V(p)\}

\displaystyle\chi^{{\mathsf{X}},k+1}_{M,w}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\chi^{k}_{w}\land\underset{v\in R[w]}{\bigwedge}\mnDiamond\chi^{k}_{v}\land\mnBox\underset{v\in R[w]}{\bigvee}\chi^{k}_{v}

It is easy to see that there are only finitely many non-equivalent
k
-th Hintikka formulas for a given finite
\mathsf{X}
—this is why we may assume that the conjunction and the disjunction in
\chi^{\mathsf{X},k+1}_{M,w}
are finite and hence that
\chi^{\mathsf{X},k+1}_{M,w}
is well-defined.

Theorem 3.3
.

(See, for instance,
[
12
,
23
]
.
)

\displaystyle w\equiv_{k}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle w^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{w}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\chi^{k}_{w}\equiv\chi^{k}_{w^{\prime}}

In the context of standard Kripke semantics, we measure the expressive power of a logic in terms of the classes of pointed models expressible in the logic:

Definition 3.4
.

A
property
(over
\mathsf{X}
) is a class of pointed models over
\mathsf{X}
. Each formula
\alpha

expresses
a property (over
\mathsf{X}\supseteq\mathsf{P}(\varphi)

\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathsf{X}}:=\{(M,w)\text{ over $\mathsf{X}$}\;|\;M,w\models\alpha\}.

We say that a logic
L
is
expressively complete
for a class of properties
\mathbb{P}
, written
\llbracket L\rrbracket=\mathbb{P}
, if for each finite
\mathsf{X}
, the class
\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{X}}
of properties over
\mathsf{X}
in
\mathbb{P}
is precisely the class of properties over
\mathsf{X}
expressible by formulas of
L
with , that is, if

\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{X}}=\llbracket L\rrbracket_{\mathsf{X}}:=\{\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathsf{X}}\;|\;\alpha\in L\}.

A property
\mathcal{P}
is
invariant under

\mathsf{X},k
-bisimulation if
(M,w)\in\mathcal{P}
and
M,w\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
imply
(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}
. A property
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathsf{X}
is

if
\mathcal{P}
is invariant under
\mathsf{X},k
-bisimulation for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
3.3
allows us to prove a world-based expressive completeness theorem for
\mathrm{ML}
:
\mathrm{ML}
can express all world-based properties that are invariant under bounded bisimulation.

Theorem 3.5

(World-based expressive completeness of
\mathbf{ML}
)
.

\llbracket\mathrm{ML}\rrbracket=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

Proof.

For the inclusion
\subseteq
, for any
\alpha\in\mathrm{ML}
, we have that
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket
is invariant under
k
-bisimulation for

by Theorem

. For the converse inclusion

\mathcal{P}
invariant under

-bisimulation, it follows easily from Theorem

that

M^{\prime},w^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{(M,w)\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{w}\iff(M^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}\quad\text{(i.e., }\mathcal{P}=\llbracket\bigvee_{(M,w)\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{w}\rrbracket\in\llbracket\mathrm{ML}\rrbracket).

Note that since there are only finitely many non-equivalent Hintikka formulas

for a given finite

∎

The formula

We now introduce state-based analogues of the preceding notions and results. A

(over
\mathsf{X}

(M,s)
where

is a model over
\mathsf{X}
and

is a state on

,
(M,s)
and
(M,s^{\prime})
are
\mathsf{X},k
-equivalent
(in a logic

State-based bisimulation (first introduced in
[
24
,
34
]
) is a natural generalization of the world-based notion: two pointed state models are bisimilar if for each world in one there is a bisimilar world in the other, and vice versa.

Definition 3.6

(State bisimilarity)
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
(M,s)
(over
\mathsf{Y}\supseteq\mathsf{X}
) and
(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})
(over
\mathsf{Y}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathsf{X}
) are
\mathsf{X},k
-bisimilar
, written
M,s\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}M^{\prime},s^{\prime}
(or simply
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}

–

[forth] for each
w\in s
there is some
w^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}
such that
M,w\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
;

–

[back] for each
w^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}
there is some
w\in s
such that
M,w\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},w^{\prime}
.

State bisimulation is clearly a conservative extension of world-based bisimulation in that
\{w\}\rightleftharpoons_{k}\{w^{\prime}\}
iff
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}
. We thus use the same symbol
\rightleftharpoons_{k}
for both world-based and state based bisimulation, and we sometimes write simply

for

. It is easy to see that for any

, then

for all

(for

similarly implies
w\rightleftharpoons_{n}w^{\prime}
for all
n<k
).

We list below some useful properties of state bisimulation; see
[
24
]
for a proof.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
s\rightleftharpoons_{k+1}s^{\prime}
, then:

(i)

R[s]\rightleftharpoons_{k}R^{\prime}[s^{\prime}]
;

(ii)

for all

such that

there are

such that
s^{\prime}=t^{\prime}\cup u^{\prime}
,
t\rightleftharpoons_{k+1}t^{\prime}
, and
u\rightleftharpoons_{k+1}u^{\prime}
.

-bisimilarity implies

Theorem 3.8

(Bisimulation invariance)

Proof.

For any

3.7

t\neq\emptyset
and
t\rightleftharpoons_{k-1}t^{\prime}
, we also have
t^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Hence,
s^{\prime}\models\mnDiamond\psi
.

\varphi=\mnBox\psi
. Suppose
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
for
k=md(\mnBox\psi)=md(\psi)+1
, and
s\models\mnBox\psi
. Let
w^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}
. By
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, there is a
w\in s
such that
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}
. Since
s\models\mnBox\psi
, we have
R[w]\models\psi
. By
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}
we obtain
R[w]\rightleftharpoons_{k-1}R^{\prime}[w^{\prime}]
by Lemma
3.7

(i)
. Then by induction hypothesis,
R^{\prime}[w^{\prime}]\models\psi
, so
s^{\prime}\models\mnBox\psi
.
∎

3.2
Expressive completeness

We move on to the main results concerning the expressive power of our logics. In the classical world-based setting we measured the expressive power of a logic in terms of the classes of pointed models expressible in the logic; we now use classes of pointed state models.

Definition 3.9
.

A
state property
(over
\mathsf{X}
) is a class of pointed state models over
\mathsf{X}
. Each formula
\varphi

expresses
a property (over
\mathsf{X}\supseteq\mathsf{P}(\varphi)
)

||\varphi||_{\mathsf{X}}:=\{(M,s)\text{ over }\mathsf{X}\;|\;M,s\models\varphi\}.

We say that a logic
L
is
expressively complete
for a class of properties
\mathbb{P}
, written
||L||=\mathbb{P}
, if for each finite
\mathsf{X}
, the class
\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{X}}
of properties over
\mathsf{X}
in
\mathbb{P}
is precisely the class of properties over
\mathsf{X}
expressible by formulas of
L
, that is, if

\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{X}}=||L||_{\mathsf{X}}:=\{||\varphi||_{\mathsf{X}}\;|\;\varphi\in L\}.

We say that a state property
\mathcal{P}
is
invariant under
\mathsf{X},k
-bisimulation (
k\in\mathbb{N}
)
if

[M,s\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}M^{\prime},s^{\prime}]\implies(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P},

and that
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathsf{X}
is
invariant under bounded bisimulation
if
\mathcal{P}
is invariant under
\mathsf{X},k
-bisimulation for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{P}
is
union closed
if

[(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}\text{ for all }s\in S\neq\emptyset]\implies(M,\bigcup S)\in\mathcal{P},

and
\mathcal{P}
is
flat
if

(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}\iff(M,\{w\})\in\mathcal{P}\text{ for all }w\in s.

Clearly,
\mathcal{P}
is flat iff
\mathcal{P}
is union closed and
downward closed
(i.e.,
(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}
and
t\subseteq s
imply
(M,t)\in\mathcal{P}
), and has the
empty state property
(i.e.,
(M,\emptyset)\in\mathcal{P}
for all
M
).

We aim to show three results. First,
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
is expressively complete for

\mathbb{B}:=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

Second,
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
is complete for

\mathbb{U}:=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is union closed and invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

Third, although the properties expressed by formulas of
\mathrm{BSML}
are union closed and invariant under bounded bisimulation,
\mathrm{BSML}
is not complete for
\mathbb{U}
. Using the notions we have introduced, it is also easy to show that
\mathrm{ML}
is complete for

\mathbb{F}:=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is flat and invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

We include below a brief proof of this folklore result as seeing it alongside the other expressive completeness theorems is instructive. Writing
||L_{1}||\subset||L_{2}||
if
||L_{1}||_{\mathsf{X}}\subseteq||L_{2}||_{\mathsf{X}}
for all finite
\mathsf{X}
and
||L_{2}||_{\mathsf{Y}}\not\subseteq||L_{1}||_{\mathsf{Y}}
for some finite
\mathsf{Y}
, we therefore have:

||\mathrm{ML}||\subset||\mathrm{BSML}||\subset||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}||\subset||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}||.

We begin by using Hintikka formulas
\chi^{k}_{w}\in\mathrm{ML}
for pointed models to construct Hintikka formulas
\chi^{k}_{s}\in\mathrm{ML}
and
\theta^{k}_{s}\in\mathrm{BSML}
for pointed state models.

Definition 3.10

(Hintikka formulas for states)
.

For any pointed state model
(M,s)
over
\mathsf{Y}\supseteq\mathsf{X}
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-th Hintikka formula

\chi^{\mathsf{X},k}_{M,s}
(or simply
\chi_{s}^{k}
) and the
k
-th strong Hintikka formula

\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{M,s}
(or simply
\theta_{s}^{k}
) of
(M,s)
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathsf{X},k}_{M,s}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\bigvee_{w\in s}\chi^{k}_{w};

\displaystyle\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{M,s}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\bigvee_{w\in s}(\chi^{k}_{w}\land\textsc{ne}).

Recall that we stipulate that
\bigvee\emptyset=\bot
. Since there are only finitely many non-equivalent
\chi^{k}_{w}
for a given finite
\mathsf{X}
, we may assume that the disjunctions in
\theta^{k}_{s}
and
\chi^{k}_{w}
are finite, and hence that the formulas are well-defined. These formulas function as characteristic formulas for states in the sense of the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

(i)

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{s}\iff
there is a state
t\subseteq s
such that
M,t\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},s^{\prime}
.

(ii)

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\theta^{k}_{s}\iff M,s\rightleftharpoons_{k}M^{\prime},s^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We prove the two items simultaneously. If
s=\emptyset
, then
\theta^{k}_{\emptyset}=\chi^{k}_{\emptyset}=\bot
, and

s^{\prime}\models\bot\iff s^{\prime}=\emptyset\iff s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}\iff\exists t\subseteq s:t\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
. Now assume that
s\neq\emptyset
.

\Longleftarrow
: We first prove
(i)
. Suppose
t\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
for some
t\subseteq s
. Then for any
w^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}
, there exists
w\in t\subseteq s
such that
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}
, which, by Theorem
3.3
, implies that
w^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{w}
and so
w^{\prime}\models\chi_{s}^{k}
. Since the classical formula
\chi_{s}^{k}
is flat (Fact
2.7
), we conclude that
s^{\prime}\models\chi_{s}^{k}
.

For
(ii)
, suppose
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
. First observe that the back condition for
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
implies that there is some
t\subseteq s
such that
t\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
. Thus, by
(i)
, we have
s^{\prime}\models\chi_{s}^{k}
, which means that for every
w\in s
, there exists
s^{\prime}_{w}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{w\in s}s^{\prime}_{w}
and
s^{\prime}_{w}\models\chi_{w}^{k}
. Meanwhile, by the forth condition for
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, we know that for every
w\in s
, there exists
w^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}
such that
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}w^{\prime}
, which implies
w^{\prime}\models\chi_{w}^{k}
by Theorem
3.3
. Thus, we may w.l.o.g. assume that each
s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
(for we can simply include the world
w^{\prime}
in
s_{w}^{\prime}
), giving that
s_{w}^{\prime}\models\chi_{w}^{k}\wedge\textsc{ne}
. Hence,
s^{\prime}\models\theta_{s}^{k}
.

\Longrightarrow
: For
(i)
, suppose
s^{\prime}\models\chi_{s}^{k}
. Then, for each
w\in s
, there exists a subset
s_{w}^{\prime}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{w\in s}s_{w}^{\prime}
and
s_{w}^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{w}
. By the empty state property of classical formulas and Theorem
3.3
, we have that either
s_{w}^{\prime}=\emptyset
or
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}s_{w}^{\prime}
. Let
t=\{w\in s\mid s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq s
. Clearly, the forth condition for
t\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
is satisfied. To verify the back condition, observe that for any
v^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{w\in s}s_{w}^{\prime}
, there exists
w\in s
such that
v^{\prime}\in s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
w\in t
and
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}_{w}
, whereby
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}v^{\prime}
.

For
(ii)
, suppose
s^{\prime}\models\theta_{s}^{k}
. Then, for every
w\in s
, there exists
s_{w}^{\prime}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{w\in s}s_{w}^{\prime}
and
s_{w}^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{w}\wedge\textsc{ne}
. We then have
s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
w\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
(by Theorem
3.3
again). Analogous to the above proof for
(i)
, we define
t=\{w\in s\mid s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq s
and obtain
t\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
by the same argument. But since now each
s_{w}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, we have actually
t=s
and thus
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
.
∎

We now give the proofs of our expressive completeness results in order. We start with the folklore result that
\mathrm{ML}
is complete for
\mathbb{F}
. Hereafter, we often abbreviate a pointed state model
(M,s)
in a property
\mathcal{P}

.

Definition 3.12

(Characteristic formulas for properties in
\mathbb{F}
)
.

For any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathsf{X}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-th characteristic formula

\nu^{\mathsf{X},k}_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathrm{ML}
of
\mathcal{P}
is defined as follows:

\nu^{\mathsf{X},k}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{s}

As before, the formula is clearly well-defined given a specific finite
\mathsf{X}
.

Proposition 3.13

(
\mathbf{ML}
is expressively complete for
\mathbf{\mathbb{F}}
)
.

||\mathrm{ML}||=\mathbb{F}:=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is flat and invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from Theorem
3.8
and Fact
2.7
. For the converse inclusion
\supseteq
, we show that for any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{F}
invariant under
k
-bisimulation:

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{s}\iff(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}\quad\text{(i.e., $\mathcal{P}=||\nu^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||\in||\mathrm{ML}||$).}

\Longleftarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
. Since
s^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, we have
s^{\prime}\models\chi^{k}_{s^{\prime}}
by Proposition
3.11
(i)
, which implies
s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{s}
by the empty state property of
\chi_{s}^{k}
.

\Longrightarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\chi^{k}_{s}
. Then for each
s\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
t_{s}^{\prime}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{P}}t_{s}^{\prime}
and
t_{s}^{\prime}\models\chi_{s}^{k}
. The latter means, by Proposition
3.11
(i)
, that there exists
t\subseteq s
such that
t_{s}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}t
. Since
\mathbb{F}
is downward closed and invariant under
k
-bisimulation, we have
t_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
s\in\mathcal{P}
. Finally, since
\mathcal{P}
is union closed, and also using the fact that since
\mathcal{P}
has the empty state property, it is not the empty property, we conclude
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{P}}t_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

The theorem also yields an alternative normal form for formulas in
\mathrm{ML}
: For an arbitrary
\mathrm{ML}
-formula
\alpha
, since
||\alpha||
is invariant under
k
-bisimulation for
k=md(\alpha)
, we have
||\alpha||=||\nu_{||\alpha||}^{md(\alpha)}||
, that is,
\alpha\equiv\bigvee_{s\in||\alpha||}\chi^{md(\alpha)}_{s}
.

Next, we show the first of our main results:
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
is complete for
\mathbb{B}
, and is thus the expressively strongest logic. The proof is similar to the one given for
\mathrm{ML}
and
\mathbb{F}
, but the addition of
ne
and
\intd
allow us to fully characterize states and to capture all properties invariant under bounded bisimulation.

Definition 3.14

(Characteristic formulas for properties in
\mathbb{B}
)
.

For any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{X}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-th characteristic formula

\xi^{\mathsf{X},k}_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
of
\mathcal{P}
is defined as follows:

\xi^{\mathsf{X},k}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\bigintdd_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{s}.

Recall that we stipulate that
\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}\emptyset=\Mnupmodels
.

Theorem 3.15

(
\mathbf{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
is expressively complete for
\mathbb{B}
)
.

||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}||=\mathbb{B}=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}

Proof.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from Theorem
3.8
. For the nontrivial inclusion
\supseteq
, we show that for any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{B}
invariant under
k
-bisimulation:

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\bigintdd_{(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{s}\iff(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}\quad\text{(i.e., }\mathcal{P}=||\xi^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||\in||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}||).

If
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
, then
\xi^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}=\Mnupmodels
, and thus
\mathcal{P}=||\Mnupmodels||=||\xi^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||
. Now, suppose
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
. If
s^{\prime}\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{s}
, then
s^{\prime}\models\theta^{k}_{s}
for some
s\in\mathcal{P}
. By Proposition
3.11
(ii)
, we have
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, which implies
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
by invariance under
k
-bisimulation. Conversely, if
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
, since
s^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, we have
s^{\prime}\models\theta^{k}_{s^{\prime}}
by Proposition
3.11
(ii)
and so
s^{\prime}\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{s}
.
∎

As above, this also provides us with a disjunctive normal form: for each
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
-formula
\varphi
:
||\varphi||=||\xi^{md(\varphi)}_{||\varphi||}||
, that is,
\varphi\equiv\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in||\varphi||}\theta^{md(\varphi)}_{s}
.

The expressive completeness of
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
for
\mathbb{U}
is proved in a similar way, but there is a small twist in this case. First define:

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ has the empty state property}\};

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ does not have the empty state property}\}.

So that
\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}\cup\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
. Note that if
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
so that for some
M
we have
(M,\emptyset)\notin\mathcal{P}
, then in fact by invariance under
k
-bisimulation
(M^{\prime},\emptyset)\notin\mathcal{P}
for all
M^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.16

(Characteristic formulas for properties in
\mathbb{U}
)
.

For any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}_{\mathsf{X}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}.

If
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
, the
characteristic formula
of
\mathcal{P}
is
\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}
. If
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
, the
characteristic formula
of
\mathcal{P}
is
\textsc{ne}\land\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Theorem 3.17

(
\mathbf{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
is expressively complete for
\mathbb{U}
)
.

||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}||=\mathbb{U}=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is union closed and invariant under bounded bisimulation}\}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from Fact
2.7
and Theorem
3.8
. For the nontrivial inclusion
\supseteq
, we first show that for any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
invariant under
k
-bisimulation,

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\iff(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}\quad\text{(i.e., $\mathcal{P}=||\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||\in||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}||$).}

\Longleftarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
. Since
s^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s^{\prime}
, by Proposition
3.11

(ii)
, we have
s^{\prime}\models\theta^{k}_{s^{\prime}}
, whereby
s^{\prime}\models\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s^{\prime}}
. Hence,
s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
by the empty state property of
\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
.

\Longrightarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
. Then for each
s\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
t_{s}^{\prime}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{P}}t_{s}^{\prime}
and
t_{s}^{\prime}\models\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
. The latter implies, by Proposition
3.11

(ii)
, that
t_{s}^{\prime}=\emptyset
or
t_{s}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s
. Since
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
has the empty state property, and since it is invariant under
k
-bisimulation, we have
t_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
s\in\mathcal{P}
. Finally, since
\mathcal{P}
is union closed, and also using the fact that since
\mathcal{P}
has the empty state property, it is not the empty property, we conclude
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{P}}t_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.

Next, we show that for any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
invariant under
k
-bisimulation,

M^{\prime},s^{\prime}\models\textsc{ne}\land\bigvee_{(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\iff(M^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}\quad\text{(i.e., $\mathcal{P}=||\textsc{ne}\land\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||\in||\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}||$).}

If
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
, then
\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}=\bot
, and thus
\mathcal{P}=||\Mnupmodels||=||\textsc{ne}\land\bot||=||\textsc{ne}\land\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}||
. Now, suppose
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
.

\Longrightarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
. By the same argument as in the
\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
-case, we obtain
s^{\prime}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}^{\textsc{ne}}
, we have
s^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
so
s^{\prime}\models\textsc{ne}
.

\Longleftarrow
: Suppose
s^{\prime}\models\textsc{ne}\land\bigvee_{s\in P}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
. As in the
\mathbb{U}^{\emptyset}
-case, for each
s\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
t_{s}^{\prime}\subseteq s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{P}}t_{s}^{\prime}
and
t_{s}^{\prime}=\emptyset
or
t_{s}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s
. Let
\mathcal{Q}:=\{s\in\mathcal{P}\;|\;t_{s}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}s\}
so that
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}t_{s}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is invariant under
k
-bisimulation, we have
t^{\prime}_{s}\in\mathcal{P}
for each
s\in\mathcal{Q}
, and since
s^{\prime}\models\textsc{ne}
, we have
s^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
so that
\mathcal{Q}\neq\emptyset
. Finally, since
\mathcal{P}
is union closed and
\mathcal{Q}\neq\emptyset
, we conclude
s^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}t_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Again the proof yields a disjunctive normal form: for any
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
-formula
\varphi
, either
\varphi\equiv\zeta^{md(\varphi)}_{||\varphi||}
or
\varphi\equiv\textsc{ne}\land\zeta^{md(\varphi)}_{||\varphi||}
.

Finally, we remark that while
\mathrm{BSML}
is union closed and invariant under bisimulations, it is not complete for
\mathbb{U}
. To prove this, we show first that for
\mathrm{BSML}
-formulas, the empty state property implies the downward closure property. Note that while converse might also appear to hold, it does not—for instance, the formula
\Mnupmodels
is downward closed but does not have the empty state property.

Lemma 3.18
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathrm{BSML}
, if
\varphi
has the empty state property, then
\varphi
is downward closed.

Proof.

By induction on
\varphi
(assumed to be in negation normal form). We only show the nontrivial cases.

Let
\varphi=\psi\land\chi
. If
M,\emptyset\models\psi\land\chi
, then
M,\emptyset\models\psi
and
M,\emptyset\models\chi
. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis,
\psi
and
\chi
are downward closed. So if
s\models\psi\land\chi
and
t\subseteq s
, then
t\models\psi
and
t\models\chi
, and therefore
t\models\psi\land\chi
.

Let
\varphi=\psi\vee\chi
. If
M,\emptyset\models\psi\vee\chi
, then clearly
M,\emptyset\models\psi
and
M,\emptyset\models\chi
. So by the induction hypothesis,
\psi
and
\chi
are downward closed. If
s\models\psi\vee\chi
, and
t\subseteq s
, then there are
s_{1},s_{2}
such that
s=s_{1}\cup s_{2}
,
s_{1}\models\psi
and
s_{2}\models\chi
. By downward closure we have
t\cap s_{1}\models\psi
and
s\cap t_{2}\models\chi
and therefore
t=(t\cap s_{1})\cup(t\cap s_{2})\models\psi\vee\chi
.
∎

Fact 3.19

(
\mathbf{BSML}
is not expressively complete for
\mathbb{U}
)
.

||\mathrm{BSML}||\subset\mathbb{U}=\{\mathcal{P}\;|\;\mathcal{P}\text{ is invariant under bounded bisimulation and union closed}\}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from Fact
2.7
and Theorem
3.8
. For inequality, let

\mathcal{P}:=||(p\land\textsc{ne})\lor(\mnNeg p\land\textsc{ne})||\cup||\bot||.

Clearly
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}
. Assume for contradiction that
||\varphi||=\mathcal{P}
for some
\varphi
in
\mathrm{BSML}
. Then
\varphi
has the empty state property so it is downward closed by Lemma
3.18
. Let
(M,s)\in||(p\land\textsc{ne})\vee(\mnNeg p\land\textsc{ne})||\subseteq\mathcal{P}
. Then there are
t,u
such that
s=t\cup u
,
t\models p\land\textsc{ne}
, and
u\models\mnNeg p\land\textsc{ne}
. By
||\varphi||=\mathcal{P}
, we have
s\models\varphi
. By downward closure,
t\models\varphi
, so
(M,t)\in\mathcal{P}
. But

t\nvDash(p\land\textsc{ne})\vee(\mnNeg p\land\textsc{ne})
and
t\nvDash\bot
; a contradiction.
∎

The property
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{U}
in the above proof is clearly expressible in the expressively complete logic
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
as
\mathcal{P}=||\mnOslash((p\land\textsc{ne})\lor(\mnNeg p\land\textsc{ne}))||
.

We conclude this section by noting some consequences of the expressive power results. First, it is shown in
[
20
]
that any state-based modal logic with the locality property and which is also
forgetting
enjoys
uniform interpolation
. Each of our logics has the locality property, and it follows from our expressive completeness results that
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
and
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
are forgetting. Therefore
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
and
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
enjoy uniform interpolation. (See
[
20
]
for details; for the locality requirement, see
[
55
]
.)

Second, the finite model property for our logics follows from that
for
\mathrm{ML}
, by a simple argument that makes use of disjoint unions of models. The
disjoint union

\biguplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
of the models
\{M_{i}\;|\;i\in I\neq\emptyset\}
is defined as usual. Recall in particular that the domain of the disjoint union is defined as
\bigcup_{i\in I}(W_{i}\times\{i\})
. We also extend the notion to state properties in a natural way: the
disjoint union
of a nonempty property
\mathcal{P}=\{(M_{i},s_{i})\;|\;i\in I\}
is
\biguplus\mathcal{P}:=(\biguplus_{i\in I}M_{i},\biguplus_{i\in I}s_{i})
, where
\biguplus_{i\in I}s_{i}:=\bigcup_{i\in I}(s_{i}\times\{i\})
. To simplify notation, we define the disjoint union
\biguplus\emptyset
of the empty property to be
(M,\emptyset)
for some fixed
M
. We refer to an element
(w,i)
in the disjoint union as simply
w
, and similarly for states. The following is standard (see, for example,
[
12
,
23
]
):

Proposition 3.20
.

For all
i\in I,w\in W_{i},k\in\mathbb{N},\mathsf{X}\subseteq\mathsf{Prop}:M_{i},w\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}\biguplus_{i\in I}M_{i},w
. Consequently, for all
s\subseteq W_{i}:M_{i},s\rightleftharpoons_{k}\biguplus_{i\in I}M_{i},s
.

Proposition 3.21

(Finite model property)
.

If
\nvDash\varphi
, then there is some finite model
M
and state
s
such that
M,s\nvDash\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
(M,s)
be such that
M,s\nvDash\varphi
and let
\mathsf{X}=\mathsf{P}(\varphi)
and
k=md(\varphi)
. If
s=\emptyset
, we clearly have
M^{\prime},s\nvDash\varphi
for all finite
M^{\prime}
. Now assume
s\neq\emptyset
. Since there are only finitely many
\mathsf{X},k
-bisimilarity types (or Hintikka formulas), we can pick a finite
t\subseteq s
such that
t\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}}_{k}s
. Clearly for any
w\in t
we have
\chi^{k}_{w}\notequiv\bot
; therefore, by the finite model property for
\mathrm{ML}
, there is some finite
(M_{w},v_{w})
such that
M_{w},v_{w}\models\chi^{k}_{w}
. Then by Theorem
3.3
,
M_{w},v_{w}\rightleftharpoons_{k}M,w
. By Proposition
3.20
,
\biguplus_{w\in t}M_{w},v_{w}\rightleftharpoons_{k}M_{w},v_{w}
for all
w\in t
; then also
\biguplus_{w\in t}M_{w},v_{w}\rightleftharpoons_{k}M,w
for all
w\in t
, so
\biguplus_{w\in t}M_{w},\{v_{w}\;|\;w\in t\}\rightleftharpoons_{k}M,t
, whence
\biguplus_{w\in t}M_{w},\{v_{w}\;|\;w\in t\}\nvDash\varphi
by Theorem
3.8
, where
\biguplus_{w\in t}M_{w}
is clearly finite.
∎

Together with the completeness results to be proved in Section
4
, this implies that our logics
\mathrm{BSML}
,
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
, and
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
are decidable.

4

Theorem 4.3

(Soundness of

If

By induction on the length

[

\vee\mathsf{E}

and

We now move on to prove the completeness theorem for the system. Our strategy consists in showing that each formula is provably equivalent to one in disjunctive normal form—as stated in the following lemma—and then making use of the semantic and proof-theoretic properties of formulas in this form.

, then
\Phi\models\psi
.

To provide a simple demonstration of the new rules, we derive the useful fact that
\mnOslash
cancels out the effects of appending
\land\textsc{ne}
to classical formulas:

Lemma 4.26
.

\alpha\dashv\vdash\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})
.

Proof.

\dashv
:
\bot\vdash\alpha
by
\mnNeg\mathsf{I}
and
\mnNeg\mathsf{E}
, and
\alpha\land\textsc{ne}\vdash\alpha
so
\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})\vdash\alpha
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
.
\vdash
:
\alpha\vdash\alpha\land\mnOslash\textsc{ne}
by
\mnOslash\textsc{ne}\mathsf{I}
. We have
\alpha\land\mnOslash\textsc{ne}[\textsc{ne}/\mnOslash\textsc{ne}]=\alpha\land\textsc{ne}
, and
\alpha\land\textsc{ne}\vdash\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{I}
. On the other hand,
\alpha\land\mnOslash\textsc{ne}[\bot/\mnOslash\textsc{ne}]=\alpha\land\bot
, and
\alpha\land\bot\vdash\bot\vdash\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{I}
. Therefore
\mnOslash\textsc{ne}\land\alpha\vdash\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
.
∎

Our strategy for proving completeness is similar to that used in Section
4.1
. We again require a crucial normal form provable equivalence result. This is stated in the next lemma; the proof is once more withheld until the end of the section.

Lemma 4.27
.

For each
\varphi\in\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
and each
k\geq md(\varphi)
, there is some property
\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle\varphi\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}

or

\displaystyle\varphi\dashv\vdash\textsc{ne}\land\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}

A number of lemmas which were proved specifically for
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
in Section
4.1
also hold for
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
, and their
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
-proofs are similar. In particular, Lemma
4.4
(Replacement) follows by a similar inductive argument, where the new case
\theta=\mnOslash\eta
is proved by applying
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
and
\mnOslash\mathsf{I}
. Proposition
4.5
(Negation normal form) is proved by a similar argument, using the additional equivalence
\mnNeg\mnOslash\varphi\dashv\vdash\mnNeg\varphi
(by
\mnNeg\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
).

Now, observe that a formula in
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
-normal form can be converted into an equivalent formula in the normal form of the expressively stronger logic
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
, as stated in the following lemma (we omit the easy proof).

Lemma 4.28
.

\displaystyle\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\equiv\bigintdd_{\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus Q}
.

While the global disjunction
\intd
is not in the language of
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
, we show in the next lemma that the formulas
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
and
\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus Q}
are also proof-theoretically interchangeable in the sense that if rules for both
\mnOslash
and
\intd
were available, the two formulas would derive the same consequences and be derivable from the same premises. Item
(i)
below corresponds to the entailment
\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus Q}\models\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
, and item
(ii)
simulates the other direction
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus Q}
.

Lemma 4.29
.

(i)

For any
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, we have
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
.

(ii)

Suppose
\displaystyle\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
is
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive in
\varphi
. If

\displaystyle\Phi,\varphi[\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}}/\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}]\vdash\psi
for all
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, then
\Phi,\varphi\vdash\psi
.

Proof.

(i)
Clearly,
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}}\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\theta^{k}_{s}
. Next, we derive
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\theta^{k}_{s}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{I}
and
\vee\mathsf{Mon}
. Since
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vee\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\bot
(by
\lor\mathsf{I}
), we obtain
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vee\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta_{t}^{k}
by
\mnOslash\mathsf{I}
—that is,
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
. Putting all these together, we conclude
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}}\vdash\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}
.

(ii)
Let
\mathcal{P}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
, and thus
\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}=\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}=\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\lor\ldots\lor\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}
. Observe that for every
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, we have
\theta_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}}^{k}\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{s_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}}\theta_{s_{i}}^{k}\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
, where
\tau_{i}=\theta_{s_{i}}^{k}
if
s_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}
, and
\tau_{i}=\bot
if
s_{i}\notin\mathcal{Q}
, by
\vee\mathsf{I}
and
\bot\mathsf{E}
. Now, by assumption, we have
\Phi,\varphi[\theta_{\biguplus\emptyset}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi
and
\Phi,\varphi[\theta_{\biguplus\{s_{n}\}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi
. Since
\theta_{\biguplus\emptyset}^{k}\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\bot
and
\theta_{\biguplus\{s_{n}\}}^{k}\dashv\vdash(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-1}\bot)\vee\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}
, we obtain

\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\bot\vee\bot/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi\text{ and }\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\bot\vee\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi.

Then, by
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
, we obtain

\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\bot\vee\mnOslash\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi.

(4)

Next, by assumption again,
\Phi,\varphi[\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\{s_{n-1}\}}/\zeta_{\mathcal{P}}^{k}]\vdash\psi
and
\Phi,\varphi[\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\{s_{n-1},s_{n}\}}/\zeta_{\mathcal{P}}^{k}]\vdash\psi
, which imply

\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\theta_{s_{n-1}}^{k}\vee\bot/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi\text{ and }\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\theta_{s_{n-1}}^{k}\vee\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi.

Again, by
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
, we obtain

\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\theta_{s_{n-1}}^{k}\vee\mnOslash\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi.

(5)

Applying
\mnOslash\mathsf{E}
to (
4
) and (
5
), we obtain
\Phi,\varphi[(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n-2}\bot)\vee\mnOslash\theta_{s_{n-1}}^{k}\vee\mnOslash\theta_{s_{n}}^{k}/\zeta^{k}_{\mathcal{P}}]\vdash\psi
. Repeating this argument, we eventually get
\Phi,\varphi\vdash\psi
, as required.
∎

Given this proof-theoretic correspondence between the normal forms, we can show completeness for
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
using an argument that is similar to that used for
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
. This time we first establish weak completeness, and then derive the strong completeness theorem as a corollary of weak completeness and compactness. (The strategy we employed for the direct proof of the strong completeness of
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
does not work for
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
due to the structure of the
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
-normal form.)

Theorem 4.30

(Weak completeness of
\mathbf{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
)
.

If
\varphi\models\psi
, then
\varphi\vdash\psi
.

Proof.

Let
k=max\{md(\varphi),md(\psi)\}
. By Lemma
4.27
,

\displaystyle\varphi\dashv\vdash\eta_{\varphi}\land\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}

and

\displaystyle\psi\dashv\vdash\eta_{\psi}\land\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{t},

(6)

where each of
\eta_{\varphi}
and
\eta_{\psi}
is either
ne
or (for notational convenience)
\Mndownmodels
. Since
\varphi\models\psi
,
\eta_{\varphi}=\Mndownmodels
implies
\eta_{\psi}=\Mndownmodels
. Each of the remaining possibilities—that is, (a)
\eta_{\varphi}=\eta_{\psi}=\Mndownmodels
; (b)
\eta_{\varphi}=\eta_{\psi}=\textsc{ne}
; (c)
\eta_{\varphi}=\textsc{ne}
and
\eta_{\psi}=\Mndownmodels
—implies by (
6
) and soundness,

\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\models\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{t},

so that by Lemma
4.28
,

\quad\bigintdd_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\models\bigintdd_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}.

(7)

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
, and
u\models\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
. By (
7
), we have
u\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}}\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}
. Thus,

for some
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
. By Proposition
3.11
(ii)
,
\biguplus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons_{k}u\rightleftharpoons_{k}\biguplus\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.
Then by Lemma
4.10
,
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\vdash\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
4.29
(i)
,
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}}\vdash\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{t}
, so also
\theta^{k}_{\biguplus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\vdash\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{t}
. Hence, by Lemma
4.29
(ii)
,
\bigvee_{s\in\mathcal{P}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{s}\vdash\bigvee_{t\in\mathcal{Q}}\mnOslash\theta^{k}_{t}
, and therefore
\varphi\vdash\psi
.
∎

Corollary 4.31

(Strong completeness of
\mathbf{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
)
.

If
\Phi\models\psi
, then
\Phi\vdash\psi
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.30
and compactness.

\eta_{i}=\textsc{ne}
, then
f_{i}\neq\emptyset
so
\theta^{k}_{f_{i}}
is not
\bot
. We have
\theta^{k}_{f_{i}}=\bigvee_{w\in f_{i}}(\chi^{k}_{w}\land\textsc{ne})\vdash\textsc{ne}
by Lemma
4.17
. If
\eta_{i}=\alpha\in\mathrm{ML}
, we clearly have that
\theta^{k}_{f_{i}}\vdash\chi^{k}_{f_{i}}
. Since
f_{i}\models\alpha
, we have
f_{i}\subseteq\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket
by flatness, which implies
\chi_{f_{i}}^{k}\models\chi_{\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket}^{k}\equiv\alpha
. Therefore,
\chi^{k}_{f_{i}}\vdash\alpha
by Proposition
4.8
.
∎

To prove item
(ii)
of Lemma
4.36
, we first show that for a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive sequence
\mathbf{a}=\langle\eta\rangle
of length
1
, the entailment
\varphi[\eta]\models\varphi[\Mndownmodels\land\eta/\eta]\models\varphi[\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}\land\eta/\eta]
can be simulated in our system in the sense of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.37
.

Let
[\eta]
be
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive in
\varphi
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathsf{X}\subseteq\mathsf{Prop}
be finite. If
\Phi,\varphi[\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}\land\eta/\eta]\vdash\psi
for all
s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}
, then
\Phi,\varphi\vdash\psi
.

Proof.

Consider
\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}:=\bigvee_{w\in\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket_{\mathsf{X}}}\chi_{w}^{k}
, where
\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket_{\mathsf{X}}=\{w\;|\;w\models\Mndownmodels\}
. Since there are only finitely many non-equivalent
k
-th Hintikka formulas
\chi_{w}^{k}
over
\mathsf{X}
, we may assume w.l.o.g.
\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}=\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\lor\ldots\lor\chi^{k}_{w_{n}}
, for some worlds
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
from some models
M_{1},\dots,M_{n}
. Clearly,
\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}\equiv\Mndownmodels
, which implies
\vdash\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}
by Proposition
4.8
. Thus, we have
\varphi\vdash\varphi[\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}\land\eta/\eta]
by Lemma
4.4
.

Now, for any
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}\in\{\textsc{ne},\bot\}
, consider the state
s=\{w_{i}\mid\tau_{i}=\textsc{ne}\}
. By applying
\bot\mathsf{E}
and
\vee\mathsf{I}
, we derive
\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}(\chi_{w_{i}}^{k}\land\tau_{i})\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{w_{i}\in s}(\chi^{k}_{w_{i}}\land\textsc{ne})=\theta^{k}_{s}
. Thus, we have
\varphi[\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}(\chi_{w_{i}}^{k}\land\tau_{i})\land\eta/\eta]\dashv\vdash\varphi[\theta_{s}^{k}\land\eta/\eta]
. Therefore, by assumption we have

\Phi,\varphi[\big((\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\bot)\vee\ldots\vee(\chi^{k}_{w_{n}}\land\bot)\big)\wedge\eta/\eta]\vdash\psi

\text{ and }\Phi,\varphi[\big((\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\bot)\vee\ldots\vee(\chi^{k}_{w_{n}}\land\textsc{ne})\big)\wedge\eta/\eta]\vdash\psi,

from which
\Phi,\varphi[\big((\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\bot)\vee\ldots\vee(\chi^{k}_{w_{n-1}}\land\bot)\vee\chi^{k}_{w_{n}}\big)\land\eta/\eta]\vdash\psi
follows by
\bot\textsc{ne}\mathsf{Trs}
. Repeating this argument, we finally get
\Phi,\varphi[\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}\land\eta/\eta]\vdash\psi
, whence
\Phi,\varphi\vdash\psi
.
∎

Next, in
(i)
of the following lemma (
(ii)
is used later) we show that for a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive sequence
\mathbf{a}=\langle\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\rangle
of arbitrary length, we can simulate the entailment

\varphi[\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X},\mathbf{a}}}\varphi^{k,f}.

Lemma 4.38
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive sequence of
\varphi
. Then:

(i)

for any
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}

(ii)

for any
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}
there are some
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}

\varphi[\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\vdash\varphi^{k,f}.

Proof.

(i)

. Since

4.4

s_{1}=\emptyset

4.2

\varphi[\theta_{s_{1}}^{k}\wedge\alpha/\alpha]\vdash\varphi^{f}

\varphi[\theta_{s_{1}}^{k}\wedge\alpha/\alpha]\vdash\varphi[\theta_{r}^{k}/\alpha]

\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\alpha\vdash(\theta^{k}_{r}\lor\theta^{k}_{s_{1}\backslash r})\land\alpha\vdash\theta^{k}_{r}\lor(\theta^{k}_{s_{1}\backslash r}\land\alpha)\vdash\theta^{k}_{r}\lor(\chi_{s_{1}\backslash r}^{k}\land\alpha).

\chi_{s_{1}\backslash r}^{k}\models\mnNeg\alpha

\chi_{s_{1}\backslash r}^{k}\vdash\mnNeg\alpha

\theta^{k}_{r}\lor(\chi_{s_{1}\backslash r}^{k}\land\alpha)\vdash\theta^{k}_{r}\lor(\mnNeg\alpha\land\alpha)\vdash\theta^{k}_{r}\lor\bot\vdash\theta_{r}^{k}

.

Now let
\mathbf{a}=\langle\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\rangle
with

and

. Applying what we just proved for

and

for some

. Repeating this argument
n

such that
\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\vdash\varphi^{f}
.

(ii)
Define
(M_{i},s_{i}):=f_{i}
. Since
\theta_{s_{i}}^{k}\wedge\eta_{i}\vdash\theta_{s_{i}}^{k}
for each

, we derive by Lemma
4.4
that
\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\vdash\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}/\eta_{n}]=\varphi^{f}
.
∎

Putting together Lemmas
4.37
and
4.38

(i)
, we can simulate
\varphi\models\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}}\varphi^{f}
; that is, we can prove Lemma
4.36

(ii)
.

Proof of Lemma
4.36

(ii)
.

Assume
\Phi,\varphi^{f}\vdash\psi
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}
. By multiple applications of Lemma
4.37
it suffices to show
\Phi,\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\vdash\psi
for all

. This follows by Lemma

(i)
and our assumption.
∎

We have now settled that relative to
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive sequences, each formula is proof-theoretically interchangeable with the global disjunction of all its realizations. For a formula
\varphi
that can be constructed solely using the occurrences in such a sequence
\mathbf{a}
, the realizations over
\mathbf{a}
correspond with (and are, in fact, provably equivalent to) the disjuncts
\theta^{k}_{s}
in the normal form
\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\,\mathop{{{\mathbin{{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}\mspace{-4mu}{\rotatebox[origin]{5.0}{${\backslash}$}}}\hskip-1.23997pt\rotatebox[origin]{-5.0}{${/}$}}}}$}_{s\in||\varphi||}\theta^{k}_{s}
for
\varphi
. We must now show that every formula is provably equivalent to some formula of this form. Formally, we say that a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive sequence
\mathbf{a}=\langle\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\rangle
of a formula is in addition a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive partition
of
\varphi
if
\varphi
is of the form
[\eta_{1}]\medcircle_{1}[\eta_{2}]\medcircle_{2}\ldots\medcircle_{n-1}[\eta_{n}]
where each
\medcircle_{i}
is either
\wedge
or
\vee
. We show:

Lemma 4.39
.

For all
\varphi
there is a
\varphi^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}
has a partition and
\varphi\dashv\vdash\varphi^{\prime}
.

Before we prove this lemma, let us confirm that realizations over
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive partitions are indeed provably equivalent to strong Hintikka formulas
\theta_{s}^{k}
. Hereafter, to simplify notation, we write
\theta^{k}_{\mathbf{0}}:=\Mnupmodels
and view
\mathbf{0}
as a null state such that
\mathbf{0}\models\Mnupmodels
.

Lemma 4.40
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive partition of
\varphi
. Then for all
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X},\mathbf{a}}
, we have that
\varphi^{k,f}\dashv\vdash\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}
for some
s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}
or
s=\mathbf{0}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for all states
s
and
t
(including the null state),
\theta^{k}_{s}\land\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{u}
and
\theta^{k}_{s}\lor\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{v}
for some (null or non-null) states
u
and
v
. If one of
s
and
t
is
\mathbf{0}
, then
\theta^{k}_{s}\land\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{\mathbf{0}}
and
\theta^{k}_{s}\lor\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{\mathbf{0}}
by Lemma
4.2
and
\Mnupmodels\mathsf{Ctr}
. If neither state is
\mathbf{0}
, then
\theta^{k}_{s}\lor\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{s\uplus t}
. For
\theta^{k}_{s}\land\theta^{k}_{t}
, if
s\nrightleftharpoons_{k}t
, then
\theta^{k}_{s}\land\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{\mathbf{0}}
by Lemmas
4.11
and
4.2
; if
s\rightleftharpoons_{k}t
, then
\theta^{k}_{s}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{t}
by Lemma
4.10
, so clearly
\theta^{k}_{s}\land\theta^{k}_{t}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{s}
.
∎

. This result is, as it turns out, an easy corollary of the fact that all formulas of the form

or

are provably equivalent to classical formulas. To establish this fact, we first recall that in Lemma

, we proved that formulas of the form

depends on Lemma

, which were given

. We have already shown Lemma

Proof of Lemma

(in

).

By

,
\mnDiamond\varphi\vdash\mnDiamond(\varphi\land\textsc{ne})\lor\mnDiamond(\varphi\land\bot)

We also need the following modal analogue of Lemma

:

Lemma 4.41
.

Let
[\eta]
be
\intd\hskip-1.0pt
-distributive in
\varphi
. For any

and any finite

\displaystyle\mnDiamond\varphi\vdash\bigvee_{s\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\mnDiamond\varphi[\theta^{\mathsf{X},k}_{s}\land\eta/\eta]

. Put
\varphi^{\prime}:=\varphi[\chi^{k}_{\llbracket\Mndownmodels\rrbracket}\land\eta/\eta]

\mnDiamond\bot\textsc{ne}\mathsf{Trs}

\displaystyle\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\vdash

\displaystyle\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}[\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\textsc{ne}/\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}]\lor\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}[\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\bot/\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}]

\displaystyle\vdash

\displaystyle\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}[\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\textsc{ne}/\chi^{k}_{w_{1}},\chi^{k}_{w_{2}}\land\textsc{ne}/\chi^{k}_{w_{2}}]\lor\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}[\chi^{k}_{w_{1}}\land\textsc{ne}/\chi^{k}_{w_{1}},\chi^{k}_{w_{2}}\land\bot/\chi^{k}_{w_{2}}]\lor

\displaystyle\dots

We can now show that formulas of the form
\mnDiamond\varphi
or
\mnBox\varphi
are provably equivalent to classical formulas, and then derive the partition lemma as a corollary.

Lemma 4.42
.

\mnDiamond\varphi
and
\mnBox\varphi
are provably equivalent to classical formulas.

Proof.

We prove the two results simultaneously but only give the details for
\mnDiamond\varphi
; the details for
\mnBox\varphi
are similar. We first prove that the result holds in case
\varphi
has a partition
\mathbf{a}
. Let
k\geq md(\varphi)
, and let
\mathsf{X}\supseteq\mathsf{P}(\varphi)
be finite. We first show that

\mnDiamond\varphi\dashv\vdash\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X},\mathbf{a}}}\mnDiamond\varphi^{k,f}.

For the direction
\dashv
, for a given
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}
, we have
\varphi^{f}\vdash\varphi
by Lemma
4.36

(i)
, which implies
\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}\vdash\mnDiamond\varphi
; therefore
\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}}\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}\vdash\mnDiamond\varphi
follows by
\vee\mathsf{E}
. For the converse direction
\vdash
, by repeated applications of Lemma
4.41
, we have

\mnDiamond\varphi\vdash\bigvee_{s_{1}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\ldots\bigvee_{s_{n}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\mnDiamond\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}].

By Lemma
4.38

(i)
and
(ii)
together with
\vee\mathsf{Mon}
and
\mnDiamond\mathsf{Mon}
, we derive

\bigvee_{s_{1}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\ldots\bigvee_{s_{n}\in||\Mndownmodels||_{\mathsf{X}}}\mnDiamond\varphi[\theta^{k}_{s_{1}}\land\eta_{1}/\eta_{1},\ldots,\theta^{k}_{s_{n}}\land\eta_{n}/\eta_{n}]\vdash\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}}\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}.

It now suffices to show that
\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{a}}}\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}
is provably equivalent to a classical formula. We show this by showing that each disjunct
\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}
is so. By Lemma
4.40
, we have
\varphi^{f}\dashv\vdash\theta^{k}_{s}
for some state
s
. Thus, also
\mnDiamond\varphi^{f}\dashv\vdash\mnDiamond\theta^{k}_{s}
. If
s=\mathbf{0}
, then
\mnDiamond\theta^{k}_{s}=\mnDiamond\Mnupmodels\dashv\vdash\mnDiamond\bot\dashv\vdash\bot
by Proposition
4.8
and Lemma
4.21
. If
s\neq\mathbf{0}
, then by Lemma
4.23

(i)
, we know that
\mnDiamond\theta^{k}_{s}
is provably equivalent to a classical formula.

We now show the general case by induction on the modal depth of
\varphi
. Note that by
\mnNeg\textsc{ne}\mathsf{E}
, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
\varphi=\varphi(\bot/\mnNeg\textsc{ne})
. If
md(\varphi)=0
, there is clearly a partition of
\varphi
, so the result follows from what we just proved. If
md(\varphi)=n+1
, then for all subformulas of
\varphi
of form
\mnDiamond\psi
or
\mnBox\psi
, we have
md(\psi)\leq n
, and thus by induction hypothesis
\mnDiamond\psi\dashv\vdash\alpha_{\mnDiamond\psi}
and
\mnBox\psi\dashv\vdash\alpha_{\mnBox\psi}
for some
\alpha_{\mnDiamond\psi},\alpha_{\mnBox\psi}\in\mathrm{ML}
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\varphi\dashv\vdash\varphi^{\prime}:=\varphi[\alpha_{\medcircle_{1}\psi_{1}}/\medcircle_{1}\psi_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\medcircle_{n}\psi_{n}}/\medcircle_{n}\psi_{n}]
, where
[\medcircle_{1}\psi_{1}],\ldots,[\medcircle_{n}\psi_{n}]
are all the subformula occurrences of form
\mnDiamond\psi
or
\mnBox\psi
in
\varphi
such that no
[\medcircle_{i}\psi_{i}]
is a suboccurrence of any other occurrence of the form
\mnDiamond\psi
or
\mnBox\psi
in
\varphi
. The formula
\varphi^{\prime}
clearly has a partition, so
\mnDiamond\varphi^{\prime}\dashv\vdash\alpha\in\mathrm{ML}
; then also
\mnDiamond\varphi\dashv\vdash\alpha
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.39
.

Apply Lemma
4.42
,
\mnNeg\textsc{ne}\mathsf{E}
, and Lemma
4.4
to replace all subformula occurrences of form
\mnDiamond\psi
,
\mnBox\psi
, or
\mnNeg\textsc{ne}
in
\varphi
with classical formulas.
∎

We are now ready to prove the completeness theorem for the
\mathrm{BSML}
-system.

Theorem 4.43

(Completeness of
\mathbf{BSML}
)
.

If
\Phi\models\psi
, then
\Phi\vdash\psi
.

Proof.

Assume
\Phi\nvdash\psi
. We show
\Phi\nvDash\psi
.
Let
\Phi=\{\varphi_{i}\;|\;i\in I\}
. By Lemma
4.39
, we may assume that each
\varphi_{i}
has a partition
\mathbf{a}_{i}
and
\psi
has a partition
\mathbf{a}
. For each
i
, let
k_{i}:=max\{md(\varphi_{i}),md(\psi)\}
and
\mathsf{X}_{i}:=\mathsf{P}(\varphi_{i})\cup\mathsf{P}(\psi)
. Now, for an arbitrary
i\in I
, since
\Phi\backslash\{\varphi_{i}\},\varphi_{i}\nvdash\psi
, by Lemma
4.36

(ii)
, we know there is a realizing function
f_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{i}}
such that
\Phi\backslash\{\varphi_{i}\},\varphi_{i}^{f}\nvdash\psi
. Continuing to argue in a similar way, one can find for each
i\in I
a realizing function
f_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{i}}
such that
\{\varphi_{i}^{k_{i},f_{i}}\;|\;f_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{i}},i\in I\}\nvdash\psi
. By Lemma
4.40
, each
\varphi_{i}^{k_{i},f_{i}}
is provably equivalent to
\theta_{s_{i}}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}
for some
s_{i}
. Thus,
\Phi^{\prime}=\{\theta_{s_{i}}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}\;|\;i\in I\}\nvdash\psi
as well.

Observe that
\Phi^{\prime}\nvDash\Mnupmodels
, since otherwise either
s_{i}=\mathbf{0}
for some
i\in I
, or
s_{i}\nrightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{M}}_{m}s_{j}
for some
i,j\in I
and for
m=min\{k_{i},k_{j}\}
and
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{X}_{i}\cap\mathsf{X}_{j}
. In the former case,
\theta_{s_{i}}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}=\Mnupmodels
and thus
\Phi^{\prime}\vdash\psi
; a contradiction. In the latter case,
\theta^{{\mathsf{M},m}}_{s_{i}},\theta^{{\mathsf{M},m}}_{s_{j}}\vdash\Mnupmodels
by Lemma
4.11
, so that by Lemma
4.14
,
\theta^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}_{s_{i}},\theta^{\mathsf{X}_{j},k_{j}}_{s_{j}}\vdash\Mnupmodels
. Thus,
\Phi^{\prime}\vdash\psi
; a contradiction again.

Now, let
t
be such that
t\models\Phi^{\prime}
. For each
i\in I
, we have
t\models\theta_{s_{i}}^{k_{i}}\dashv\vdash\varphi_{i}^{f_{i}}
, and thus
t\models\varphi_{i}^{f_{i}}
by soundness. By Lemma
4.35
, we further conclude
t\models\varphi_{i}
, whereby
t\models\Phi
. To show
\Phi\nvDash\psi
, it then suffices to show
t\nvDash\psi
. Assume otherwise. Take
i\in I
. By Lemma
4.35
,
t\models\psi^{k_{i},g}
for some
g\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathbf{a}}
. In view of Lemma
4.40
, we have
t\models\theta_{r}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}
for some (non-null) state
r
with
\psi^{k_{i},g}\dashv\vdash\theta_{r}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}
. On the other hand, since
t\models\Phi^{\prime}
, we have
t\models\theta_{s_{i}}^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}
. Then, by Proposition
3.11
(ii)
, we have
r\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}_{i}}_{k_{i}}t\rightleftharpoons^{\mathsf{X}_{i}}_{k_{i}}s_{i}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.10
,
\theta^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}_{s_{i}}\vdash\theta^{\mathsf{X}_{i},k_{i}}_{r}\vdash\psi^{k_{i},g}
. As
\psi^{k_{i},g}\vdash\psi
(by Lemma
4.36

(i)
), we are forced to conclude that
\Phi^{\prime}\vdash\psi
, which is a contradiction.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented natural deduction axiomatizations for
\mathrm{BSML}
and the two extensions we introduced,
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
(
\mathrm{BSML}
with the global/inquisitive disjunction
\intd
) and
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
(
\mathrm{BSML}
with the novel emptiness operator
\mnOslash
). We also proved the expressive completeness of the two extensions:
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
is expressively complete for the class of all state properties invariant under bounded bisimulation;
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\mnOslash}
for the class of all union-closed state properties invariant under bounded bisimulation. We conclude by noting an additional preliminary result known to us, and listing possible directions for further investigation.

We saw that
\mathrm{BSML}
is union closed but not expressively complete for union-closed properties. It appears, however, that one can find a different natural class of state properties for which
\mathrm{BSML}

is
complete: according to a very recent unpublished result
[
33
]
,
\mathrm{BSML}
is complete for the class of all properties
\mathcal{P}
such that
\mathcal{P}
is invariant under bounded bisimulation, union-closed, and also
convex
, where
\mathcal{P}
is convex if
[(M,s)\in\mathcal{P}
and
(M,t)\in\mathcal{P}
and
t\subseteq u\subseteq s]
implies
(M,u)\in\mathcal{P}
.

The semantics of the quantifiers in first-order dependence logic are analogous to the semantics of the modalities used in modal dependence logic, which are distinct from the
\mathrm{BSML}
-modalities
\mnDiamond
and
\mnBox
. Defining first-order quantifiers whose semantics are analogous to those of
\mnDiamond
and
\mnBox
may yield first-order state-based logics with interesting properties.

In inquisitive semantics, the global disjunction
\intd
is used to model the meanings of questions. Similarly, in the context of the extension
\mathrm{BSML}^{\hskip-2.0pt\intd}
, this disjunction might allow one to model questions and their interaction with free choice phenomena
5
, and to arrive at a full account of examples of overt free choice cancellation
5
, which were left open in
[
3
]
.

\ex

. Can I have coffee or tea?
\mnDiamond(c\vee t)\intd\mnNeg\mnDiamond(c\vee t)

Ẏes
\leadsto
you can have coffee and you can have tea
.̱ No
\leadsto
you cannot have either

\ex

. You may have coffee or tea, I don’t know which.

Finally, given that the emptiness operator
\mnOslash
is a natural counterpart to
ne
and that it can be used to cancel out the effects of pragmatic enrichment in the sense that
\mnOslash(\alpha\land\textsc{ne})\equiv\alpha
, investigating the applications of this operator in formal semantics may yield some interesting results.

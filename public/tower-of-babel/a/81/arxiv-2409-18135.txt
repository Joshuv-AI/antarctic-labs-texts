Norm of an operator with numerical range in a sector
Chi-Kwong Li and Kuo-Zhong Wang

Abstract
. We refine a recent result of Drury concerning the optimal ratio between the norm and numerical radius of a bounded linear operator T T with numerical range lying in a sector of a circular disk. In particular, characterization is given to the operators attaining the optimal ratio, and properties of such operators are explored.



Keywords. Norm, numerical radius.

AMS classification. 15A60, 47A12.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
be the set of bounded linear operators acting on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
of dimension larger than 1.
If
n
has dimension
n
,
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is identified with the set
M_{n}
of
n\times n
complex matrices.
For
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, its numerical range and numerical radius are defined by

W(T)=\{\langle Tx,x\rangle:x\in{\mathcal{H}},\|x\|=1\}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad w(T)=\sup\{|\mu|:\mu\in W(T)\},

respectively.
It is known that the numerical radius is a norm on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that

w(T)\leq\|T\|\leq 2w(T).

Let
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]
and

(1.1)

S(\alpha)=\{a+ib\in{\mathbb{C}}:a,b\in{\mathbb{R}},{|b|}\leq a\tan\alpha\}.

Suppose
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
.
In
[
2
]
the author proved that

(1.2)

\|T\|\leq w(T)\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}

and showed that the equality is attainable. This improves the result in
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
asserting
\|T\|\leq w(T)\sqrt{1+2\sin^{2}\alpha}

for any
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
Moreover, it was shown that the bound is attained by the matrix
A\in M_{2}
equal to

\frac{1}{1+2\sin^{2}\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha\cos^{2}\alpha}+i\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha+\sin^{4}\alpha}&2\sin^{2}\alpha\cr 0&\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha\cos^{2}\alpha}-i\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha+\sin^{4}\alpha}\cr\cr\end{pmatrix}.

(Note that there is a small typo in the description of
A
in the paper.)
The key step of the proof in
[
2
]
used
a result concerning the characterization of a matrix
A\in M_{n}
such that
w(A)\leq 1
in
[
1
]
,
and some intricate computational arguments after reducing the problem to the
2\times 2
case.

In this paper, we will give an elementary proof for the optimal inequality (
1.2
) for
2\times 2
matrices.
Moreover, we show that a matrix
T\in M_{2}
with
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
attaining equality in (
1.2
) if
and only if
T/\|T\|
is unitarily similar to the matrix
A
described in the preceding paragraph. These will be done in
Section 2.
As shown in
[
2
]
, one can deduce (
1.2
) for a general operator
T
once we have the result
for matrices in
M_{2}
. In Section 3, we study and characterize those operators
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
with
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)

attaining the equality in (
1.2
).

For
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]
, we always assume that
S(\alpha)
is defined as in (
1.1
) and

(1.3)

\tau(\alpha)=\sup\{\|T\|/w(T):T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}),T\neq 0,W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)\}.

If
\alpha=0
and
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
satisfies
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
,
then
W(T)\subseteq[0,\infty)
is positive semidefinite,
and we always have
\|T\|=w(T)
so that
{\tau}(0)=1
.
We will exclude this case in our discussion.

We shall always assume that

S(\alpha)
and
\tau(\alpha)
are defined as
in (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) with
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2]
.
It is easy to see that if we write
T=H+iG
with
H=(T+T^{*})/2
and
G=i(T^{*}-T)/2
,
then
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
is equivalent to

|\cos\alpha\langle Gx,x\rangle|\leq\sin\alpha\langle Hx,x\rangle

for all unit vectors
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
, i.e.,

\sin\alpha H\pm\cos\alpha G
is positive semidefinite.

2.
The two-by-two case

The goal of this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2]
and
T\in M_{2}
be nonzero with
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
. Then

\|T\|\leq w(T)\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}.

The equality holds if and only if
T/\|T\|
is unitarily
similar to the matrix

(2.1)

\frac{1}{1+2s}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1+s-s^{2}}+i\sqrt{s+s^{2}}&2s\cr 0&\sqrt{1+s-s^{2}}-i\sqrt{s+s^{2}}\cr\cr\end{pmatrix}\ \hbox{ with }\ s=\sin^{2}\alpha.

We will use the following basic facts about numerical range and numerical radius; see
[
6
]
.

(1)

Let
T\in M_{2}
. Then
W(T)
is an elliptical disk
with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
as foci, and length of minor axis equal to

\sqrt{{\rm tr}\,(TT^{*})-|\lambda_{1}|^{2}-|\lambda_{2}|^{2}}
.

(2)

Denote by
\sigma(X)
the spectrum of
X\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
If
T=H+iG\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, where
H
and
G
are self-adjoint,
then
w(T)=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\max\sigma(\cos\theta H+\sin\theta G)
.

To determine
\tau(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2]
, we let

(2.2)

\hat{\tau}(\alpha)=\sup\{\|A\|/w(A):A\in R(\alpha)\},

where
R(\alpha)
is the set of
A\in M_{2}
such that

\det(A)>0
and
A/\det(A)^{1/2}
is unitarily similar to a matrix of the form

(2.3)

\begin{pmatrix}re^{i{\theta}}&2c\cr 0&e^{-i{\theta}}/r\cr\end{pmatrix}\quad\hbox{ with }r\geq 1,\ \theta\in[0,\alpha],\ c=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta}.

As we shall see in Lemma
2.4
,
\hat{\tau}(\alpha)
is attainable.
For
A\in R(\pi/2)
it is easy to check that

A+A^{*}
is positive semidefinite so that
W(A)\subseteq S(\pi/2)
.
For
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)
and
A\in M_{2}
be nonzero.
Then
A\in R(\alpha)
or
A^{*}\in R(\alpha)
if and only if
W(A)\subseteq S(\alpha)

and touches each of the two boundary rays of
S(\alpha)
at a nonzero point,
i.e., there are
r_{1},r_{2}>0
such that
r_{1}e^{i\alpha},r_{2}e^{-i\alpha}\in W(A)
.

Proof.
For the sufficiency, let

A=H+iG
with
H=H^{*}
and
G=G^{*}
. We may replace

A
by
V^{*}AV
for a suitable unitary
V\in M_{2}
and assume that
H={\rm diag}\,(a_{1},a_{2})
with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq 0
.
We claim that
a_{2}>0
.
If not, then either
W(A)
is a non-degenerate elliptical disk touching the imaginary axis so that

W(A)\not\subseteq S(\alpha)
, or
W(A)
is a line segment with
0
as an endpoint so that

W(A)
cannot touches the boundary rays of
S(\alpha)
at two nonzero points. So,
a_{1}\geq a_{2}>0
.
Let

\tilde{A}=H^{-1/2}AH^{-1/2}=I_{2}+iH^{-1/2}GH^{-1/2}

also satisfies
W(\tilde{A})\subseteq S(\alpha)
and touches the two boundary rays of
S(\alpha)
.
This follows readily from the observation that

(2.4)

\{v^{*}\tilde{A}v:v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},v\neq 0\}=\{v^{*}H^{1/2}AH^{1/2}v:v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},v\neq 0\}=\{\tilde{v}^{*}A\tilde{v}:\tilde{v}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},\tilde{v}\neq 0\}.

Since the normal matrix
\tilde{A}
touches the boundary rays of
S(\alpha)
at two nonzero points,
it has eigenvalues
1\pm i\tan\alpha
.
As a result,
\det(\tilde{A})=1+\tan^{2}\alpha
, and

\det(A)=(1+\tan^{2}\alpha)/(a_{1}a_{2})>0
.
We may replace
A
by
\gamma U^{*}AU
with
\gamma=\det(A)^{-1/2}
and a suitable unitary
U\in M_{2}
and assume that

A
is in the form (
2.3
) with
r>0
. We may assume that
r\geq 1
. Else, replace
A
by
A^{*}
.
Now, let

A=\tilde{H}+i\tilde{G}
with
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}^{*}
and
\tilde{G}=\tilde{G}^{*}
.
Then
\tilde{H}^{-1/2}\tilde{G}\tilde{H}^{-1/2}
has eigenvalues
\pm\tan\alpha
. Thus,

-\tan^{2}\alpha=\det(\tilde{H}^{-1/2}\tilde{G}\tilde{H}^{-1/2})=\det(\tilde{G})/\det(\tilde{H})=-(c^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta)/(\cos^{2}\theta-c^{2})

so that

\tan^{2}\alpha=\frac{c^{2}-\cos^{2}\theta+1}{\cos^{2}\theta-c^{2}}=-1+\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\theta-c^{2}}\quad\hbox{ i.e., }\quad\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}=1+\tan^{2}\alpha=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}{\theta}-c^{2}}.

Hence,
c^{2}=\cos^{2}\theta-\cos^{2}\alpha=\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta.

Conversely, suppose
A
or
A^{*}
belongs to
R(\alpha)
. Assume
A\in R(\alpha)
. The other case can be treated similarly.
Then
A/\det(A)^{1/2}
is unitarily similar to a matrix of the form (
2.3
).
We simply assume that
A
has the form (
2.3
), and let

A=\tilde{H}+i\tilde{G}
with
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}^{*}
and
\tilde{G}=\tilde{G}^{*}
.
Then
\tilde{H}
has positive diagonal entries and
\det(\tilde{H})=\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\alpha=\cos^{2}\alpha>0
.
So,
\tilde{H}
is positive definite. Moreover,

\tilde{H}^{-1/2}\tilde{G}\tilde{H}^{-1/2}
has the same eigenvalues as
\tilde{H}^{-1}\tilde{G}
, which equals

\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta/r&-c\cr-c&r\cos\theta\cr\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}r\sin\theta&-ic\cr ic&-\sin\theta/r\cr\end{pmatrix}
having trace 0 and
determinant
-\tan^{2}\alpha
.
As a result,
I_{2}+i\tilde{H}^{-1/2}\tilde{G}\tilde{H}^{-1/2}
has numerical range lying in
S(\alpha)

and touches the boundary rays of
S(\alpha)
at two nonzero points.
The matrix
A=\tilde{H}+i\tilde{G}
has the same property
by observation (
2.4
).

We will show that the value

\hat{\tau}(\alpha)
defined in (
2.2
) equals
\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}
in the next two lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2]
. If
A\in M_{2}
has the form
(
2.3
)

with
r>1
and
\theta>0
, then
there is
\hat{A}\in R(\alpha)
with
\|A\|/w(A)<\|\hat{A}\|/w(\hat{A})
.

Proof.
Suppose
A
has the form (
2.3
) with

r>1
and
\theta>0
.
Then
\|A\|^{2}+1/\|A\|^{2}=r^{2}+1/r^{2}+4(\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta)
.
Consider
(\hat{r},\hat{\theta})
with
\hat{r}\in(1,r)
and
\hat{\theta}\in(0,\theta)
such that

(2.5)

r^{2}+1/r^{2}+4(\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta)=\hat{r}^{2}+1/\hat{r}^{2}+4(\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}).

Let
\hat{c}=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}}
and

A(\hat{r})=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{r}e^{i\hat{\theta}}&2\hat{c}\cr 0&e^{-i\hat{\theta}}/\hat{r}\cr\end{pmatrix}=H(\hat{r})+iG(\hat{r}),

where
H(\hat{r})=H(\hat{r})^{*}
and
G(\hat{r})=G(\hat{r})^{*}
. By (
2.5
),

\|A\|^{2}+1/\|A\|^{2}=\|A(\hat{r})\|^{2}+1/\|A(\hat{r})\|^{2}
so that
\|A\|=\|A(\hat{r})\|
.
We will show that
there is
A(\hat{r})
with
1<\hat{r}<r
and
0<\hat{\theta}<\theta

such that
w(A(\hat{r}))<w(A)
.
It will then follow that
\|A\|/w(A)<\|A(\hat{r})\|/w(A(\hat{r}))
.

To achieve our goal, let
\lambda_{1}(X)
be the largest eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix

X\in M_{n}
.
Then

w(A(\hat{r}))=\max\{\lambda_{1}(\Re(e^{-i\phi}A(\hat{r}))):\phi\in[0,\pi/2]\}

and

\lambda_{1}(\Re e^{-i\phi}A(\hat{r}))
is the larger zero of the polynomial

\displaystyle f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det(\lambda I_{2}-\cos\phi H(\hat{r})-\sin\phi G(\hat{r}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}-(\hat{r}\cos(\hat{\theta}-\phi)+\cos(\hat{\theta}+\phi)/\hat{r})\lambda+\cos(\hat{\theta}-\phi)\cos(\hat{\theta}+\phi)-\hat{c}^{2}.

Since

\cos(\hat{\theta}-\phi)\cos(\hat{\theta}+\phi)-\hat{c}^{2}=\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos^{2}\phi-\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}\sin^{2}\phi-\sin^{2}\alpha+\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}=\cos^{2}\phi-\sin^{2}\alpha
,

f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-(\hat{r}\cos(\hat{\theta}-\phi)+\cos(\hat{\theta}+\phi)/\hat{r})\lambda+\cos^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\phi.

Let
\hat{\phi}\in(0,\pi/2)
be such that

w(A(\hat{r}))
equal to the larger zero
\lambda_{1}(\hat{r})
of

f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda)
.
That is,
w(A(\hat{r}))
equals the larger zero of

f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-(\hat{r}\cos(\hat{\theta}-\hat{\phi})+\cos(\hat{\theta}+\hat{\phi})/\hat{r})\lambda+\cos^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\hat{\phi}.

We
claim
that there is
\phi\in(\hat{\phi},\pi/2)
such that
the larger zero
\lambda_{1}(r)
of

f_{r}(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-(r\cos(\theta-\phi)+\cos(\theta+\phi)/r)\lambda+\cos^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\phi

satisfies
\lambda_{1}(r)>\lambda_{1}(\hat{r})
.
Since
\lambda_{1}(r)=\lambda_{1}(\Re(e^{-i\phi}A))\leq w(A)
, we have

w(A(\hat{r}))=\lambda_{1}(\hat{r})<w(A)
as desired.

Note that our claim is valid if there is
\phi\in(\hat{\phi},\pi/2)
satisfies

r\cos(\theta-\phi)+\cos(\theta+\phi)/r\geq\hat{r}\cos(\hat{\theta}-\hat{\phi})+\cos(\hat{\theta}+\hat{\phi})/\hat{r},

i.e.,

(2.6)

(r+1/r)\cos\theta\cos\phi+(r-1/r)\sin\theta\sin\phi\geq(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})\cos\hat{\theta}\cos\hat{\phi}+(\hat{r}-1/\hat{r})\sin\hat{\theta}\sin\hat{\phi}

for the following reason.
If
\phi\in(\hat{\phi},\pi/2)
satisfies (
2.6
),
then
f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda)=f_{r}(\lambda)+\xi_{1}\lambda+\xi_{2}
with
\xi_{1}\geq 0
and
\xi_{2}>0
.
Evidently,
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(\hat{r})>0
and

f_{r}(\lambda_{0})=f_{\hat{r}}(\lambda_{0})-\xi_{1}\lambda_{0}-\xi_{2}<0
. Thus,
\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}(r)
.

By (
2.5
), our choice of
(\hat{r},\hat{\theta})
always gives

r^{2}+1/r^{2}-4\sin^{2}\theta=\hat{r}^{2}+1/\hat{r}^{2}-4\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}

so that

\displaystyle(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})^{2}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}-(r+1/r)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{2}+1/\hat{r}^{2}-(r^{2}+1/r^{2})+(r+1/r)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})^{2}\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}-4\sin^{2}\theta+(r+1/r)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})^{2}\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-1/r)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-(\hat{r}-1/\hat{r})^{2}\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}>0

The last inequality above holds because
1<\hat{r}<r
and
0<\hat{\theta}<\theta<\pi/2
.
Thus we have

(2.7)

(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})\cos\hat{\theta}>(r+1/r)\cos\theta

and

(2.8)

(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})^{2}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}+(\hat{r}-1/\hat{r})^{2}\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}=(r+1/r)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+(r-1/r)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

Now, we can prove (
2.6
).
Let
a=(r+1/r)\cos\theta,b=(r-1/r)\sin\theta,\hat{a}=(\hat{r}+1/\hat{r})\cos\hat{\theta},
and
\hat{b}=(\hat{r}-1/\hat{r})\sin\hat{\theta}
.
By (
2.8
),
a^{2}+b^{2}=\hat{a}^{2}+\hat{b}^{2}\equiv s
. There are
\eta,\hat{\eta}\in(0,\pi/2)
such that

\cos\eta=a/\sqrt{s}
and
\cos\hat{\eta}=\hat{a}/\sqrt{s}
. Hence
\sin\eta=b/\sqrt{s}
and
\sin\hat{\eta}=\hat{b}/\sqrt{s}
.
By (
2.7
), we have
\cos\eta<\cos\hat{\eta}
, i.e.,
\hat{\eta}<\eta
.
Now, given
\hat{\phi}\in(0,\pi/2)
, we consider two cases.

Case 1.

\hat{\phi}<\eta
: In the case, we choose
\phi=\eta
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{a}\cos\hat{\phi}+\hat{b}\sin\hat{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\hat{a}^{2}+\hat{b}^{2}}=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}=\sqrt{s}=a^{2}/\sqrt{s}+b^{2}/\sqrt{s}=a\cos\phi+b\sin\phi.

Case 2.

\hat{\phi}\geq\eta
:
Let
g(t)=\cos\eta\cos t+\sin\eta\sin t-\cos\hat{\eta}\cos t-\sin\hat{\eta}\sin t
for
\eta\leq t<\pi/2
.
Then
g(\eta)=1-\cos\hat{\eta}\cos\eta-\sin\hat{\eta}\sin\eta>0
and

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cos\eta\sin t+\sin\eta\cos t+\cos\hat{\eta}\sin t-\sin\hat{\eta}\cos t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin t(\cos\hat{\eta}-\cos\eta)+\cos t(\sin\eta-\sin\hat{\eta})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0\qquad(\mbox{ since }\cos\hat{\eta}>\cos\eta).

Hence
g
is increasing in
[\eta,\pi/2)
and then
g(t)>0
for
t\in[\eta,\pi/2)
.
Thus,

g(\hat{\phi})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}(a\cos\hat{\phi}+b\sin\hat{\phi}-\hat{a}\cos\hat{\phi}-\hat{b}\sin\hat{\phi})>0.

By continuity, there exists
\phi\in(\hat{\phi},\pi/2)
such that

a\cos\phi+b\sin\phi-\hat{a}\cos\hat{\phi}-\hat{b}\sin\hat{\phi}>0.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2]
and
A\in R(\alpha)
.
Then
\|A\|\leq w(A)\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}
. The equality holds if and only if
A/\|A\|
is unitarily similar to the matrix
in
(
2.1
)
.

Proof.
Let
A\in M_{2}
satisfy the hypothesis. We may assume that
A
is in the form (
2.3
).
Then the product of the two singular values of
A
equals
|\det(A)|=1
.
So,
A
has singular values
\|A\|
and
1/\|A\|
. Thus,

\|A\|^{2}+1/\|A\|^{2}={\rm tr}\,(A^{*}A)=r^{2}+1/r^{2}+4c^{2}
and

\|A\|\pm 1/\|A\|=\sqrt{(r\pm 1/r)^{2}+4c^{2}}
. Thus,

(2.9)

\|A\|=\frac{1}{2}[\sqrt{(r+1/r)^{2}+4c^{2}}+\sqrt{(r-1/r)^{2}+4c^{2}}].

We will show that
\|A\|\leq w(A)\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}
.
The equality holds if and only if
A/\|A\|
is unitarily similar to the matrix in (
2.1
).
By Lemma
2.3
, we can focus on
A\in R(\alpha)
of the form (
2.3
) with
\theta=0
and
r=1
.

Case 1
Suppose
\theta=0
.
Then
A=\begin{pmatrix}r&2\sin\alpha\cr 0&1/r\end{pmatrix}
so that

W(A)
is an elliptical disk with foci
1/r,r
and minor axis of length
2\sin\alpha
.
Hence, the major axis is the real line segment with endpoints

\frac{1}{2}[(r+1/r)\pm\sqrt{(r-1/r)^{2}+4\sin^{2}\alpha}]\ \ \hbox{ so that }\ \ w(A)=\frac{1}{2}[(r+1/r)+\sqrt{(r-1/r)^{2}+4\sin^{2}\alpha)}].

By (
2.9
) with
c=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta}=\sin\alpha
,
and the fact that
{1\geq 4/(r+1/r)^{2}}
,

w(A)\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}-\|A\|=\frac{1}{2}\{(r+1/r)[\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}-\sqrt{1+4{\sin^{2}\alpha}/(r+1/r)^{2}}]

\ \hskip 93.95122pt+(\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}-1)\sqrt{(r-1/r)^{2}+4\sin^{2}\alpha}\}>0.

Thus,
\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha}>\|A\|/w(A)
.

Case 2
Suppose
\theta>0
and
r=1
so that

A=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta}&2c\cr 0&e^{-i\theta}\cr\end{pmatrix}
. Then
the elliptical disk
W(A)
has foci
e^{i\theta}
and
e^{-i\theta}
,
semi-minor axis of length
c=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta}
.
The major axis has endpoints of the form
\cos\theta\pm ib
, where
\pm b
are the eigenvalues of

i(A^{*}-A)/2
so that
b=\sin\alpha
.
The boundary of
W(A)
is the set

\{(\cos\theta+c\cos\phi)+i\sin\alpha\sin\phi:\phi\in[0,2\pi)\}
.
To determine
w(A)
, we can focus on
x=\cos\phi\in[0,1]
and consider

f(x)=(\cos\theta+cx)^{2}+\sin^{2}\alpha(1-x^{2})\quad\hbox{ with }\quad f^{\prime}(x)=2c(\cos\theta+cx)-2x\sin^{2}\alpha.

Note that
\sin\theta\neq 0
. Then
f^{\prime}(x)=0
implies

x=\frac{c\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\alpha-c^{2}}=\frac{c\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\qquad\hbox{ as }\qquad c^{2}=\sin^{2}\alpha-\sin^{2}\theta.

We will consider two subcases depending according to

c\cos\theta/\sin^{2}\theta>1
or

c\cos\theta/\sin^{2}\theta\leq 1
.
Let
s=\sin^{2}\alpha
. Then
\sin^{2}\theta=s-c^{2}
and
\cos^{2}\theta=1-s+c^{2}
.
Since
c\geq 0

Equivalently,

Case 2.a.
Assume

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2c(\cos\theta+cx)-2xs>2(\sin^{2}\theta+c^{2}x-xs)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\sin^{2}\theta-x\sin^{2}\theta)\geq 0.\qquad\qquad\qquad(\hbox{since }x\in[0,1]).

Hence

By (

g(c)=\frac{\|A\|}{w(A)}=\frac{\|A\|}{\sqrt{f(1)}}=\frac{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}+c}{\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\theta}+c}=\frac{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}+c}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\alpha+c^{2}}+c}=\frac{\sqrt{1/c^{2}+1}+1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\alpha/c^{2}+1}+1}.

Set

with

.
Then

\displaystyle(\ln g(y))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{(\sqrt{y+1}+1)\sqrt{y+1}}-\frac{\cos^{2}\alpha}{(\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}+1)\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1+\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}-(y+1)\cos^{2}\alpha-\sqrt{y+1}\cos^{2}\alpha}{(\sqrt{y+1}+1)\sqrt{y+1}(\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}+1)\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-\cos^{2}\alpha+\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}-\cos^{2}\alpha\sqrt{y+1}}{(\sqrt{y+1}+1)\sqrt{y+1}(\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}+1)\sqrt{y\cos^{2}\alpha+1}}>0.

for

if

, where

.
We have

Thus,

in this case.

Case 2.b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta+cx)^{2}+s(1-x^{2})=s\left(s\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{4}\theta}+\left(1-\frac{c^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{4}\theta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\left(s\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{4}\theta}+1-\frac{(s-\sin^{2}\theta)\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{4}\theta}\right)=s\left(1+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right)=\frac{s}{\sin^{2}\theta}.

Then

g^{\prime}(c)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\left\{\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}+1\right)\sqrt{s-c^{2}}-(\sqrt{1+c^{2}}+c)\frac{c}{\sqrt{s-c^{2}}}\right\}

=\frac{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}+c}{\sqrt{s}\sqrt{s-c^{2}}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}}\left((s-c^{2})-c\sqrt{1+c^{2}}\right)=0

is the maximum. Thus, we have shown that

in this case.

Using the fact that

\sin^{2}\theta=\frac{s+s^{2}}{1+2s},\ \ \cos^{2}\theta=\frac{1+s-{s^{2}}}{1+2s},\ \hbox{ and }\ \|A\|=\sqrt{1+c^{2}}+c=\frac{1+s}{\sqrt{1+2s}}+\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+2s}}=\sqrt{1+2s}

-iy_{1}=y_{2}\neq 0
. As a result,

(1,1)^{t}
is a multiple of

\begin{pmatrix}-iy_{1}\cr y_{2}\cr\end{pmatrix}=\frac{(\mu+d)i}{2}w_{1}+\frac{\mu-d}{2}w_{2}\ \hbox{ with }\ w_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}{-x_{2}}/{d_{1}}\cr{x_{1}}/{(d_{2}\tan\alpha)}\cr\end{pmatrix}\ \hbox{ and }\ w_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}{x_{2}}/{(d_{1}\tan\alpha)}\cr{x_{1}}/{d_{2}}\cr\end{pmatrix}.

Equivalently,
\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}\cr d_{2}\cr\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\cos\theta+c}\cr\sqrt{\cos\theta-c}\cr\end{pmatrix}

is a multiple of
(\mu+d)i\tilde{w}_{1}+(\mu-d)\tilde{w}_{2}

\tilde{w}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}{\cos\theta-\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}\cr\cos\alpha\cr\end{pmatrix}\ \hbox{ and }\ \tilde{w}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}{(\sqrt{1+c^{2}}-\cos\theta)}/{\tan\alpha}\cr\sin\alpha\cr\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mu=\mu_{1}+i\mu_{2}
. Comparing real part and imaginary part, we see that

(d_{1},d_{2})^{t}
is a multiple of

(\mu_{1}+d+i\mu_{2})i\tilde{w}_{1}+(\mu_{1}-d+i\mu_{2})\tilde{w}_{2}=-\mu_{2}\tilde{w}_{1}+(\mu_{1}-d)\tilde{w}_{2}+i((\mu_{1}+d)\tilde{w}_{1}+\mu_{2}\tilde{w}_{2}).

So,
(\mu_{1}+d)\tilde{w}_{1}+\mu_{2}\tilde{w}_{2}=0
and
(d_{1},d_{2})^{t}
is a multiple of

-\mu_{2}\tilde{w}_{1}+(\mu_{1}-d)\tilde{w}_{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{d^{2}-\mu_{1}^{2}-\mu_{2}^{2}}{\mu_{2}}\tilde{w}_{1}&\hbox{ if }\mu_{2}\neq 0\cr\quad 2\mu_{1}\tilde{w}_{2}&\hbox{ if }\mu_{2}=0.\cr\end{cases}

Equivalently,

\frac{\cos\theta+c}{\cos\alpha}=\frac{\sqrt{\cos\theta+c}}{\sqrt{\cos\theta-c}}=\frac{\cos\theta-\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}{\cos\alpha}\hbox{ or }\frac{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}-\cos\theta}{\cos\alpha},

which is impossible because

2\cos\theta=\frac{2\sqrt{1+\sin^{2}\alpha\cos^{2}\alpha}}{\sqrt{1+2\sin^{2}\alpha}}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\sin^{2}\alpha}}=\sqrt{1+c^{2}}-c.

Hence
d=0
, which is a contradiction. Similarly, if
T_{12}
is nonzero, we may apply the argument above with

v=0
and
u=\mu(x_{2},x_{1})^{t}
to derive a contradiction.

Thus,
\tilde{T}=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
. Clearly, we have
W(T_{2})\subseteq S(\alpha)
.
Moreover, we have
w(T_{2})\leq 1/\tau(\alpha)
. Otherwise,
\|T\|/w(T)=1/w(T_{2})>\tau(\alpha)
.

Clearly, if
{\mathcal{H}}
has a finite dimension, then
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
always attains its norm at a unit vector so that
the Theorem
3.4
is valid. In the infinite dimensional case,
there may not exist a unit vector
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\|T\|=\|T(x)\|

so that the necessity part of Theorem
3.4
may fail.
For example, we may
let
T_{n}\in M_{2}
with
W(T_{n})\subseteq S(n\alpha/(n+1))
and
\|T_{n}\|/w(T_{n})=1/w(T_{n})=\tau(n\alpha/(n+1))
.
We let
T
be the (infinite) direct sum of
T_{n}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
, so that
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
and

\|T\|/w(T)=1/\lim w(T_{n})=\tau(\alpha)
.
However, one can check that
T
is not unitarily
similar to
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
with
T_{1}\in M_{2}
satisfying
\|T_{1}\|/w(T_{1})=\tau(\alpha)
.
Nevertheless, we have the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
satisfies
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
and
\|T(x)\|\neq\|T\|
for any unit vector
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
If
\|T\|/w(T)=\tau(\alpha)
,
then there is a sequence of unitary operators
\{U_{m}\}
in
B({\mathcal{H}})
such that

U_{m}^{*}TU_{m}/\|T\|=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}(m)&T_{12}(m)\cr T_{21}(m)&T_{22}(m)\cr\end{pmatrix}\quad\hbox{ for }m=1,2,\dots

with

T_{11}(m)\in M_{2}
converging to the matrix
B/\|B\|
in
(
3.3
)
,
T_{12}(m)
and
T_{21}(m)
converging to the zero operators.

Proof.
Suppose
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
satisfies
\|T\|/w(T)=\tau(\alpha)
. We may replace
T
by
T/\|T\|
and assume that
\|T\|=1
.
There will be a sequence of unit vectors
\{x_{m}\}
such that
\|Tx_{m}\|
converges to
\|T\|=1
.
There is a sequence of unitary operators
\{U_{m}\}
such that

U_{m}^{*}TU_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}(m)&T_{12}(m)\cr T_{21}(m)&T_{22}(m)\cr\end{pmatrix}
with

T_{11}(m)\in M_{2}
satisfying
\|T_{11}(m)\|\rightarrow 1
.
Suppose
T_{21}(m)
does not converge to 0. Then we may assume that the leading
3\times 3
submatrix of

U_{m}^{*}TU_{m}
has the form

\tilde{B}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}(m)&u_{m}\cr v_{m}^{*}&b_{m}\cr\end{pmatrix}
so that

\|u_{m}\|>\varepsilon
for all positive integer
m
, where
\varepsilon
is a positive number.
Now,
\{\tilde{B}_{m}\}
is a bounded sequence, and has a convergent subsequence with limit of the form

\tilde{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B/\|B\|&u\cr v^{*}&b\cr\end{pmatrix}
such that
B/\|B\|
has norm 1
and
w(B/\|B\|)=1/\tau(\alpha)
,
\|u\|>\varepsilon
,
W(B/\|B\|)\subseteq W(\tilde{B})\subseteq S(\alpha)
and

\|\tilde{B}\|/w(\tilde{B})=\tau(\alpha)
. This contradicts Theorem
3.4
.
Similarly, we can show that
T_{21}(m)
converges to the zero operator.
Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
T_{11}(m)
converges to
T_{1}
, which satisfies

W(T_{1})\subseteq S(\alpha)
and
\|T_{1}\|/w(T_{1})=\tau(\alpha)
by Theorem
3.4
, we have the conclusion.

Now, we turn to the case when
\alpha=\pi/2
.
Note that
W(T)\subseteq S(\alpha)
simply means that
T+T^{*}
is positive semidefinite, and

\tau(\pi/2)=\sqrt{2}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
T
be a
3\times 3
nonzero matrix. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

W(T)\subseteq S(\pi/2)
and
\|T\|/w(T)=\sqrt{2}
,

(b)

T/\|T\|
is unitarily similar to a
3\times 3
matrix of the form

(3.4)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{2}{3}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}&d\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}&0&\sqrt{3}d\\
d&-\sqrt{3}d&b_{1}+ib_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)

with

(3.5)

d\geq 0,\quad b_{1}\geq 3d^{2}/2,\quad\hbox{ and }\quad 18d^{2}+\sqrt{2(12d^{2}+b_{1})^{2}+2b_{2}^{2}}\leq 1.

Moreover, if condition (b) holds with
d>0
, then
T
is unitarily irreducible.

Proof.
Suppose
W(T)\subseteq S(\pi/2)
and
\|T\|/w(T)=\sqrt{2}
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we may assume that
T/\|T\|
is unitarily similar to a matrix
of the form
\tilde{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}&\mu v\\
d\tilde{v}^{*}&b_{1}+ib_{2}\\
\end{array}\right)
, where
B_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2/3&1/\sqrt{3}\\
-1/\sqrt{3}&0\\
\end{array}\right),v=(1,\sqrt{3})^{t},\tilde{v}=(1,-\sqrt{3})^{t},d\geq 0
, and
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
We have
\|B_{1}\|=1
and
w(B_{1})=1/\sqrt{2}
.
If
x=(\sqrt{3},-i)^{t}/2
, then
\Re(e^{i\pi/4}B_{1})x=1/\sqrt{2}x
.
Since
w(\tilde{T})=\|\tilde{T}\|/\sqrt{2}=1/\sqrt{2}
, we have

\Re(e^{i\pi/4}\tilde{T})\hat{x}=1/\sqrt{2}\hat{x}
, where
\hat{x}=(x^{t},0)^{t}
.
This implies that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{\mu}e^{-i\pi/4}+de^{i\pi/4})-i(\bar{\mu}e^{-i\pi/4}-de^{i\pi/4})=de^{i\pi/4}(1+i)+\bar{\mu}e^{-i\pi/4}(1-i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}de^{i\pi/2}+\sqrt{2}\bar{\mu}e^{-i\pi/2}=\sqrt{2}i(d-\bar{\mu}).

Thus
\bar{\mu}=d\geq 0
and then

\Re(e^{i\theta}\tilde{T})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{2}{3}\cos\theta&i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}&d\cos\theta\\
-i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}&0&i\sqrt{3}d\sin\theta\\
d\cos\theta&-i\sqrt{3}d\sin\theta&b_{1}\cos\theta-b_{2}\sin\theta\\
\end{array}\right).

Since
W(\tilde{T})\subseteq S(\pi/2)
,
\Re(\tilde{T})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2/3&0&d\\
0&0&0\\
d&0&b_{1}\\
\end{array}\right)
is positive semidefinite. Equivalently,
b_{1}\geq 3d^{2}/2
.

On the other hand,
w(\tilde{T})\geq w(B_{1})=1/\sqrt{2}
and
so that
w(\tilde{T})=1/\sqrt{2}
if and only if
M_{\theta}=I_{3}/\sqrt{2}-\Re(e^{i\theta}\tilde{T})

is positive semidefinite for all
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
.
Clearly, the leading
1\times 1
and
2\times 2
principal submatrices of
M_{\theta}
are positive definite for all

\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{\pm\pi/4\}
.
Hence by continuity, this is also equivalent to
\det(M_{\theta})\geq 0
for all
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{\pm\pi/4\}
.
Let

\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-b_{1}\cos\theta+b_{2}\sin\theta
.
By expanding cofactors along the third column of
M_{\theta}
, we see that
\det(M_{\theta})
equals

\displaystyle\gamma\left|\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\cos\theta&-i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}\\
i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{matrix}\right|+id\sqrt{3}\sin\theta\left|\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\cos\theta&-i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}\\
-d\cos\theta&id\sqrt{3}\sin\theta\end{matrix}\right|-d\cos\theta\left|\begin{matrix}i\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{3}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
-d\cos\theta&id\sqrt{3}\sin\theta\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\cos\theta-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{3})-d^{2}\sqrt{3}\sin^{2}\theta(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}-\sqrt{3}\cos\theta)-d^{2}\cos\theta(-\sin^{2}\theta+\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{3}(\cos\theta-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}-d^{2}3(1-\cos^{2}\theta)(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)+d^{2}\cos\theta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)(\sqrt{2}+\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{3}(\cos\theta-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}+(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)4d^{2}(\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\cos\theta-\frac{3}{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{3}(\cos\theta-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}+(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)^{2}4d^{2}(-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\cos\theta)^{2}(\sqrt{2}-18\sqrt{2}d^{2}-\cos\theta(24d^{2}+2b_{1})+2b_{2}\sin\theta).

Let
f(\theta)=\sqrt{2}-18\sqrt{2}d^{2}-\cos\theta(24d^{2}+2b_{1})+2b_{2}\sin\theta
. We need to determine the condition on

(d,b_{1},b_{2})
such that

f(\theta)
is always nonnegative. Clearly,

f(\theta)
is minimum when
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
is chosen such that

\cos\theta(24d^{2}+2b_{1})-2b_{2}\sin\theta=\|(24d^{2}+2b_{1},-2b_{2})\|
. Hence, we need

1-18d^{2}-\sqrt{2(12d^{2}+b_{1})^{2}+2b_{2}^{2}}\geq 0\quad\hbox{ i.e., }\quad 18d^{2}+\sqrt{2(12d^{2}+b_{1})^{2}+2b_{2}^{2}}\leq 1.

Conversely, we can follow the above derivation and show that if
T/\|T\|
is unitarily similar to the matrix of the form (
3.4
),
then
T+T^{*}
is positive semidefinite and
\|T\|/w(T)=\sqrt{2}
.
So,
(a)
and
(b)
are equivalent.

Now, suppose that
d>0
and that
T
is unitarily reducible. Let
T
be unitarily similar to
T_{1}\oplus[r]\equiv C
, where
T_{1}
is a
2\times 2
and
r\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then
\|T_{1}\|=\|T\|
, otherwise
w(T)=|r|=\|T\|
, this is a contradiction. This implies that

W(T_{1})\subseteq S(\pi/2)
and

\|T\|=\|T_{1}\|\leq\sqrt{2}w(T_{1})\leq\sqrt{2}w(T).

Hence
\|T_{1}\|=\sqrt{2}w(T_{1})
. By Theorem
2.1
,
T_{1}
is unitaruly similar to
1/3\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+i\sqrt{2}&2\\
0&1-i\sqrt{2}\\
\end{array}\right)
, and
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2/3&1/\sqrt{3}\\
-1/\sqrt{3}&0\\
\end{array}\right)

as well. Thus,
r=b_{1}+ib_{2}
. But then
{\rm tr}\,(T^{*}T)={\rm tr}\,(C^{*}C)<{\rm tr}\,(\tilde{T}^{*}\tilde{T})
, which is impossible. Hence,
T
is
unitarily irreducible.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
B
has the form
(
3.4
)
with
d\in(0,1/\sqrt{45})
.
Then there is a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}B&0\\
0&0_{n-3}\\
\end{array}\right)+(\varepsilon+3d^{2}/2-b_{1}-ib_{2})E_{33}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}\varepsilon^{k}(E_{(k+2)(k+3)}+E_{(k+3)(k+3)})

satisfies
W(T)\subseteq S(\pi/2)
,
\|T\|/w(T)=\sqrt{2}
, and
is unitarily irreducible.

Proof.
We may assume that
\varepsilon<1
.
Let
\hat{B}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2}{3}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}&d\cr-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}&0&\sqrt{3}d\cr d&-\sqrt{3}d&3d^{2}/2\cr\end{pmatrix}
, and

T=T_{1}+T_{2}
with

T_{1}=\hat{B}\oplus 0_{n-3}\ \ \hbox{ and }\ \ T_{2}=\varepsilon E_{33}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}\varepsilon^{k}(E_{(k+2)(k+3)}+E_{(k+3)(k+3)}).

Since
d\in(0,1/\sqrt{45})
, we can choose

\varepsilon>0
such that

\hat{B}+{\rm diag}\,(0,0,\varepsilon)\equiv B_{\varepsilon}

satisfies
\|B_{\varepsilon}\|/w(B_{\varepsilon})=1/w(B_{\varepsilon})=\sqrt{2}
by Theorem
3.6
.
Note that
\Re(T)=\Re(T_{1})+\Re(T_{2})
.
Now,
\Re(T_{1})=\Re(\hat{B})\oplus 0_{n-3}

and

\Re(T_{2})=\varepsilon E_{33}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}\varepsilon^{k}E_{(k+3)(k+3)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-3}\frac{\varepsilon^{k}}{2}(E_{(k+2)(k+3)}+E_{(k+3)(k+2)})
.
Clearly,
\Re(T_{1})
is positive semidefinite;

\Re(T_{2})
is also positive semidefinite because its (real) eigenvalues
lie in the region
\cup_{k=1}^{n-3}\{z:|z-\varepsilon^{k}|\leq\varepsilon^{k}/2+\varepsilon^{k+1}/2\}\cup\{0\}

by the Gershgorin theorem; e.g., see
[
5
]
. Thus,
\Re(T)
is positive semidefinite and
W(T)\subseteq S(\pi/2)
.

Now, for any
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
,
I_{n}/\sqrt{2}-\Re(e^{i\theta}T)=R_{1}(\theta)+R_{2}(\theta)
,
where

R_{1}(\theta)=[I_{3}/\sqrt{2}-\Re(e^{i\theta}\hat{B})-\delta E_{33}]\oplus 0_{n-3}\equiv Q(\theta)\oplus 0_{n-3}

and

R_{2}(\theta)=({\rm diag}\,(0,0,\delta)\oplus I_{n-3}/\sqrt{2})-\Re(e^{i\theta}T_{2}),

where
\delta>0
is chosen so that
the determinants of the leading
1\times 1
and
2\times 2
submatrices of

Q(\theta)
are positive and

\det(Q(\theta))\geq 0
for all
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{\pm\pi/4\}
.
The existence of such a
\delta
is ensured by the proof of Theorem 3.6.
Hence
Q(\theta)
is positive semidefinite and so is
R_{1}(\theta)
.
We may adjust
\varepsilon
so that
2\varepsilon<\min\{\delta,1/\sqrt{2}\}
.
Then the
(j,j)
entry of
R_{2}(\theta)
is larger than the sum of the absolute values of the off-diagonal entries
in the
j
-th row
for
j=3,\dots,n
. Hence,
R_{2}(\theta)

is positive semidefinite by Gershgorin theorem.
Thus,
w(T)\leq 1/\sqrt{2}
.
Since the leading
2\times 2
submatrix of
T
has norm 1 and numerical radius
1/\sqrt{2}
, we see that
w(T)=1/\sqrt{2}
, and hence

\|T\|/w(T)=\sqrt{2}
.

Next, we claim that
T
is unitarily irreducible. In this case,
0\in\partial W(B_{\varepsilon})
and
0\notin\sigma(B_{\varepsilon})
.
Let
a_{\varepsilon}=\min\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(B_{\varepsilon})\}
.
Hence
a_{\varepsilon}>0
and

a_{\varepsilon}\to\min\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(\hat{B})\}>0\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

We may assume that
\varepsilon<\min\{1,a_{\varepsilon}\}
.
Assume that
T
is unitarily similar to a direct summand as
\hat{T}_{1}\oplus\hat{T}_{2}
on
H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
.
For
k=4,\ldots,n
, let

x_{k}=e_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}e_{k+1}+\cdots+\frac{\varepsilon^{(1+n-k)(n-k)/2}}{(1-\varepsilon)\cdots(1-\varepsilon^{n-k})}e_{n}.

Then it is straightforward to verify that
T^{*}x_{k}=\varepsilon^{k-3}x_{k}
and
\mbox{span}\{x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}\}=\mbox{span}\{e_{4},\ldots,e_{n}\}\equiv V_{1}
.
We have
\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle>0
for all
4\leq i,j\leq n
.
Hence
V_{1}
must be in
H_{1}
or
H_{2}
, and suppose it is in
H_{1}
.
We obtain that
H_{2}\subseteq\mbox{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}\equiv V_{2}

and
\{\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon^{n-3}\}\subseteq\sigma(\hat{T}_{1})
.
Assume that
x_{0}
is an eigenvector of
T
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\varepsilon
.
Since
\varepsilon<a_{\varepsilon}=\min\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(B_{\varepsilon})\}
,
the matrix
B_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon I_{3}
is invertible.
Hence the eigenvector

can be set as
(u^{t},-1,0)^{t}
, where

.
Thus

and
x_{0}+e_{4}=(u^{t},0)^{t}\in V_{2}
, which implies that
H_{1}\cap V_{2}\neq\{0\}
.
As a result,
B_{\varepsilon}
is unitarily reducible, which is a contradiction.
Hence
T
is unitarily irreducible.

Theorem 3.8
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{H}}
has dimension at least
3
and
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
is such that
T+T^{*}
is positive semidefinite.
Suppose
T
attains its norm at a unit vector

, i.e.,

Then

if and only if the compression of the operator

.

Proof.
To prove the necessity, assume that
T
has the said properties. Suppose

\tilde{T}=T/\|T\|
has operator matrix
\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}\cr T_{21}&T_{22}\cr\end{pmatrix}
,
where

is a compression of
\tilde{T}
onto

.
Then

is positive semidefinite,
\|T_{11}\|=1
, and

.

\sqrt{2}\geq\|T_{11}\|/w(T_{11})\geq\|\tilde{T}\|/w(\tilde{T})=\sqrt{2}
.

T_{11}

by Lemma

.

Conversely, suppose the compression of
\tilde{T}
onto

. Then
\|\tilde{T}\|=\|B\|=1
.
Since
1/\sqrt{2}\geq w(\tilde{T})\geq w(B)=1/\sqrt{2}
, we have
\|\tilde{T}\|/w(\tilde{T}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}=}\|B\|/w(B)=\sqrt{2}
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose
T\in B({\mathcal{H}})
is nonzero such that
T+T^{*}
is positive semidefinite. If
\|T\|/w(T)=\tau(\pi/2)
,
then there is a sequence of unitary operators
\{U_{m}\}
in

such that

U_{m}^{*}TU_{m}/\|T\|=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}(m)&T_{12}(m)\cr T_{21}(m)&T_{22}(m)\cr\end{pmatrix}\quad\hbox{ for }m=1,2,\dots

Reply to the Comment on “Thermal, quantum antibunching and lasing thresholds from single emitters to macroscopic devices”
Mark Anthony Carroll, Giampaolo D’Alessandro, Gian Luca Lippi, Gian-Luca Oppo, Francesco Papoff

Abstract
We deconstruct and address a comment to Carroll et al. [Phys Rev Lett 126, 063902 (2021)] (PRL) that has been posted on arXiv appearing as two versions [arXiv:2106.15242v1] and [arXiv:2106.15242v2]. This comment claimed that a term in the model presented in the PRL had been incorrectly omitted and that, hence, the laser threshold predicted by the model in the PRL is unattainable. We show that the term in question was correctly neglected because it represents collective effects that are not observable in the devices modelled in the PRL. Moreover, even if this term were to be included, the laser threshold would still be present, contrary to what was claimed in the comment. We conclude that the model presented in PRL is correct and that its results are innovative and of wide application in laser physics and quantum optics.

Here we show that: 1) The threshold exists, persists and is
attainable
even with the wrong assumptions of
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
;
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
; 2) When correctly taking into account terms of the order of
\sum_{n\neq l}\delta\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle
and the sum’s spatial nonlocality, the CIM provides accurate values of the laser threshold.

We consider first the term
\sum_{n\neq l}\delta\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle
: since different emitters are in different positions, the sum does not correspond to the modulus squared of the macroscopic polarization
P(\boldsymbol{x})
, a local function of position
\boldsymbol{x}
in Maxwell’s equations. Instead, it corresponds to the integral
\int_{|\boldsymbol{y-x}|>\epsilon}P(\boldsymbol{x})P^{*}(\boldsymbol{y})d\boldsymbol{y}
, where
|\boldsymbol{y-x}|>\epsilon
eliminates single emitter terms as does
n\neq l
in the sum. Furthermore,
|P|^{2}
does not correspond to
\sum_{n,l}\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle
as incorrectly suggested in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
. Imposing operator normal ordering gives

\sum_{n,l}\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle=\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle-\sum_{n,l}\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{l}c_{n}\rangle\neq|P|^{2}

where
\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle
is the excited state population and
\sum_{n,l}\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{l}c_{n}\rangle
the sum of the expectation values of the product of polarisations between QDs placed at different positions: a spatially nonlocal term. This decomposition proves the point. The term that corresponds to the amplitude squared of the local polarization from Maxwell’s equations is instead the term
|\langle v^{\dagger}c\rangle|^{2}
, present in the CIM (see Eq. (2) in
Carroll et al. 2021
) but arbitrarily and inconsistently removed from Eq. (1) in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
;
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
.

In nanolasers, terms like
\sum_{n\neq l}\delta\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle
are normally neglected. They represent collective effects , like superradiance, usually not observable in the presence of strong polarisation dephasing due to high carrier density screening
Gies et al. 2007
. It is also shown in
Protsenko et al. 2021
that in the limit of strong polarisation dephasing
(\gamma_{c}/\gamma)\ll 1
(where
\gamma_{c}
and
\gamma
are the cavity decay and dephasing rates, respectively) the correlations describing collective effects are negligible and that they become important only in low-Q cavities where (
\gamma_{c}/\gamma)\geq 1
. The CIM
Carroll et al. 2021
matches the parameters of (GaAs-based) Quantum Dots most commonly used in nanolasers, with very rapid decays of the intrinsic polarization
Sugawara et al. 2000
and negligible polarization correlations. Therefore the elimination of this term in the CIM
Carroll et al. 2021
is perfectly justified.

On the contrary, the term incorrectly identified with
|P|^{2}
in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
;
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
is physically not realistic. To verify this we use the cluster expansion to write the dynamical equation for
\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{l}c_{n}\rangle
. This is

\displaystyle d_{t}\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{l}c_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=-(2\gamma+i\Delta\varepsilon)\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{l}c_{n}\rangle

(1)

\displaystyle+g_{ls}^{*}\big[\langle b_{s}^{\dagger}v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle(1-2\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle)

\displaystyle-2\langle v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle\langle b_{s}^{\dagger}c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle+2\langle b_{s}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle\langle v_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle\big]

\displaystyle+g_{ns}\big[\langle b_{s}c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}\rangle(1-2\langle c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle)

\displaystyle-2\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}\rangle\langle b_{s}c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle+2\langle b_{s}\rangle\langle c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}\rangle\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}v_{l}\rangle\big],

where the coefficients
g_{ns}
depend on the cavity-mode field at the QDs positions

the spatial nonlocality, Eq. (

g_{ls}

corresponding to different QDs. Neglecting these phase

Having now established the validity of the CIM
Carroll et al. 2021
and pointed out the errors in the model proposed in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
;
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
, we correct and clarify the outstanding claims and statements made in the Comment. The term
\delta\langle b^{\dagger}b\rangle
is not the cavity field as stated in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
.

b^{\dagger}b
is the photon number operator and its expectation value
\langle b^{\dagger}b\rangle
corresponds to the intensity of the resonant cavity mode,

not to the cavity field
. The term corresponding to the (complex) amplitude of
the coherent laser mode is
\langle b\rangle
.
From the cluster expansion
\langle b^{\dagger}b\rangle=\delta\langle b^{\dagger}b\rangle+\langle b^{\dagger}\rangle\langle b\rangle
, one sees
that the term
\delta\langle b^{\dagger}b\rangle
is the difference
between the total photon number and the coherent fraction which possesses a
collective phase.

Contrary to a claim made in
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021a
, the
model presented in
Carroll et al. 2021
is not a “Maxwell-Bloch” model. It is a quantum model derived directly from a quantum Hamiltonian and naturally describes spontaneous emission. On the contrary, the Comment
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
follows a semiclassical perspective when including, in an
ad hoc
manner, quantum fluctuations to mimic spontaneous emission. Thus, its approach
Vyshnevyy and Fedyanin 2021b
is not self consistent.

It is important to remark that neglecting

\delta\langle b^{\dagger}bc^{\dagger}c\rangle
and
\delta\langle b^{\dagger}bv^{\dagger}v\rangle
is standard procedure with cluster expansions
Fricke 1996
at the two-particle level
Gies et al. 2007
and that Ref.
Carroll et al. 2021
follows the traditional approach of previous publications on this issue.

\displaystyle\langle c_{l}^{\dagger}c_{l}\rangle_{th}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{c}\gamma}{2N|g|^{2}}\Bigg[1+\Big(\frac{\Delta\nu}{\gamma_{c}+\gamma}\Big)^{2}\Bigg]

(2)

where the nonlasing state is stable for
\langle c^{\dagger}c\rangle<\langle c^{\dagger}c\rangle_{th}
and unstable if

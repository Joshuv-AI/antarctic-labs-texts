An error estimate for the Gauss-Jacobi-Lobatto quadrature rule
Concetta Laurita

Abstract
An error estimate for the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature formula for integration over the interval [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] , relative to the Jacobi weight function w α , β ​ ( t ) = ( 1 − t ) α ​ ( 1 + t ) β w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)=(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta} , α , β > − 1 \alpha,\beta>-1 , is obtained. This estimate holds true for functions belonging to some Sobolev-type subspaces of the weighted space L w α , β 1 ​ ( [ − 1 , 1 ] ) L_{w^{\alpha,\beta}}^{1}([-1,1]) .

Introduction

For a Jacobi weight function
w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)=(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
, on the interval
[-1,1]
we consider the
(n+2)
-point Gauss-Lobatto rule

\int_{-1}^{1}f(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt=w_{0}f(-1)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}f(t_{k})+w_{n+1}f(1)+e_{n}(f)

(1)

which is exact for polynomials of degree at most
2n+1
, i.e.

e_{n}(f)=0,\qquad\forall f\in\mathbb{P}_{2n+1}

being
\mathbb{P}_{n}
the set of all algebraic polynomials on
[-1,1]
of degree at most
n
.

It is well known that the interior quadrature nodes
t_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, are the zeros of the Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
orthonormal with respect to the Jacobi weight

w^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)=(1-t)^{2}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)
. The weights of the formula (
1
) are given by

w_{k}=\int_{-1}^{1}l_{k}(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n+1

(2)

where, setting from now on
t_{0}=-1
and
t_{n+1}=1
,
l_{k}(t)
denotes the
(k+1)
-th Lagrange fundamental polynomial associated to the system of nodes
\left\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t_{n+1}\right\}
.

By standard arguments, it can be easily proved that the quadrature error
e_{n}(f)
satisfies the following estimate

|e_{n}(f)|\leq 2\left(\int_{-1}^{1}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)\,dt\right)E_{2n+1}(f)_{\infty},\qquad\forall f\in C([-1,1]),

where

E_{n}(f)_{\infty}=\inf_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\|f-P\|_{\infty}

denotes the error of best approximation of a function
f\in C([-1,1])
by means of polynomials of degree at most
n
with respect to the uniform norm.

The aim of the present paper is to provide a new error estimate for less regular functions belonging to some Sobolev-type subspaces of the weighted space
L_{w^{\alpha,\beta}}^{1}([-1,1])
. A similar estimate is proved in
[
3
]
for the classical Gauss-Jacobi quadrature formula, in
[
1
]
for the Gauss-Lobatto rule with respect to the Legendre weight
w^{0,0}
and, more recently, in
[
2
]
for the Gauss-Radau formula with respect to a general Jacobi weight
w^{\alpha,\beta}
.

2
Notation and preliminary results

2.1
Notation

For a general weight function
w(t)
on
[-1,1]
and
1\leq p<+\infty
, let
L^{p}_{w}
denote the weighted space of all real-valued measurable functions
f
on
[-1,1]
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}=\|fw\|_{p}=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|f(t)w(t)|^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty,

and let
W_{r}^{p}(w)
be the following weighted Sobolev-type subspaces of
L^{p}_{w}

W_{r}^{p}(w)=\left\{f\in L^{p}_{w}:\ f^{(r-1)}\in AC(-1,1),\ \|f^{(r)}\varphi^{r}w\|_{p}<+\infty\right\},

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\geq 1
,
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}
and
AC(-1,1)
is the collection of all functions which are absolutely continuous on every closed subset of
(-1,1)
, equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W_{r}^{p}(w)}=\|fw\|_{p}+\|f^{(r)}\varphi^{r}w\|_{p}.

For a function
f\in L_{w}^{p}
, the error of the best approximation of
f
in
L_{w}^{p}
by polynomials of degree at most
n
is defined as

E_{n}(f)_{w,p}=\inf_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{n}}\|f-P\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

Fixed a Jacobi weight
w^{\gamma,\delta}(t)=(1-t)^{\gamma}(1+t)^{\delta}
,
\gamma,\delta>-1
, we will denote by
x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}
and
\lambda_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, the nodes and coefficients of the corresponding
n
–point Gauss-Jacobi quadrature rule on
[-1,1]
and by

p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(t)=\gamma_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}t^{n}+\mathrm{lower\,degree\,terms},\qquad\gamma_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}>0,

the Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
orthonormal w.r.t.
w^{\gamma,\delta}(t)
having positive leading coefficient.

In the sequel
{\mathcal{C}}
will denote a positive constant which may assume different values in different formulas. We write
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(a,b,...)
to say that
{\mathcal{C}}
is dependent on the parameters
a,b,....
and
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(a,b,...)
to say that
{\mathcal{C}}
is independent of them.
Moreover, we will write
A\sim B
, if there exists a positive constant
{\mathcal{C}}
independent of the parameters of
A
and
B
such that

1/{\mathcal{C}}\leq A/B\leq{\mathcal{C}}
.

2.2
Preliminary results

It is well known that for functions
f
belonging to
W_{1}^{p}(w)
, the following Favard inequality

E_{n}(f)_{w,p}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n}E_{n-1}(f^{\prime})_{\varphi w,p},

(3)

is fulfilled for a positive constant
{\mathcal{C}}
independent of
n
and
f
(see, for example,
[
3
, (2.5.22), p. 172]
). By iteration of inequality (
3
), it follows that, for
f\in W_{r}^{p}(w)
,
r\geq 1
, the estimate

E_{n}(f)_{w,p}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n^{r}}E_{n-r}(f^{(r)})_{\varphi^{r}w,p},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f)

(4)

holds true.

Let us recall that the knots
x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}
and Christoffel numbers
\lambda_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}
of the Gaussian quadrature formula corresponding to the Jacobi weight
w^{\gamma,\delta}
satisfy the following properties (see, for instance,
[
3
, (4.2.4), p. 249]
)

x_{n,k+1}^{\gamma,\delta}-x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\sim\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}{n},\qquad x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\leq t\leq x_{n,k+1}^{\gamma,\delta},

(5)

and (see
[
6
, (14), p.673]
)

\lambda_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\sim\frac{\sqrt{1-\left(x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\right)^{2}}}{n}w^{\gamma,\delta}(x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta})

(6)

uniformly for
1\leq k\leq n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, for the orthonormal polynomials
\left\{p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(t)\right\}_{n}
one has that (see
[
7
, (12.7.2), p. 309]
)

\frac{\gamma_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}}{\gamma_{n-1}^{\gamma,\delta}}\sim 1,\qquad\mathrm{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

(7)

and (see, for instance,
[
5
, p. 170]
) the equality

\frac{1}{p_{n-1}^{\gamma,\delta}\left(x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\right)}=\frac{\gamma_{n-1}^{\gamma,\delta}}{\gamma_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}}\lambda_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\left(p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}\right)^{\prime}\left(x_{n,k}^{\gamma,\delta}\right)

(8)

holds true. Furthermore (see
[
5
, Corollary 9.34, p. 171]

p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(1)\sim n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\left|p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(-1)\right|\sim n^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}},

(9)

uniformly for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and, more generally,
[
3
, (4.4.49)-(4.2.30), p. 255]
)

\left|p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(t)\right|\sim n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad 1-\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n^{2}}\leq t\leq 1,

(10)

\left|p_{n}^{\gamma,\delta}(t)\right|\sim n^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad-1\leq t\leq-1+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n^{2}}.

(11)

3
Main results

Lemma 3.1
.

The nodes and the weights of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature formula (
1
) satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle w_{0}\displaystyle

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{C}}\,\Delta t_{0}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{1}),

(12a)

\displaystyle w_{k}\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Delta t_{k}\,w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k}),&&k=1,\ldots,n-1\\
\displaystyle\Delta t_{k-1}\,w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k})&&k=n\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle w_{n+1}\displaystyle

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{C}}\,\Delta t_{n}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n}),

(12d)

where
\Delta t_{k}=t_{k+1}-t_{k}
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n
and
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n)
.

Proof.

First, let us observe that the weights of the formula (
1
), given in (
2
), have the following alternative representation

\displaystyle w_{0}\displaystyle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t_{0}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})}w^{\alpha+1,\beta}(t)dt,

(13a)

\displaystyle w_{k}\displaystyle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(1-t_{k}^{2}\right)}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{\left(t-t_{k}\right)\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{k})}w^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)dt,\qquad k=1,\ldots,n,

(13b)

\displaystyle w_{n+1}\displaystyle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+t_{n+1}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{n+1})}w^{\alpha,\beta+1}(t)dt.

(13c)

For
k=1,\ldots,n
, since

w_{k}=\frac{\lambda_{n,k}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}}{1-\left(x_{n,k}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{2}},

using (
6
) and (
5
) for
\gamma=\alpha+1
and
\delta=\beta+1
, we can deduce that

w_{k}\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k})\Delta t_{k}&&k=1,\ldots,n-1\\
\displaystyle w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k})\Delta t_{k-1}&&k=n\end{array}\right.

i.e. (
12
). Now, in order to prove (
12a
), let us observe that, since

\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})}-\left(\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})}\right)^{2}\right]w^{\alpha+1,\beta}(t)dt=0,

we can rewrite the first coefficient
w_{0}
in (
13a
) as follows

w_{0}\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{1})}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})}\right]^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{(t-t_{1})\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{1})}\right]^{2}\frac{(t-t_{1})^{2}}{1+t}w^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)dt.

Being
(t-t_{1})^{2}/(1+t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}

and using (
8
) (with
\gamma=\alpha+1
and
\delta=\beta+1
) for
k=1
(we recall that in our notation
x_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\equiv t_{1}
), we get

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\left[\frac{\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{1})}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})}\right]^{2}\lambda_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{\left[p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{0})\right]^{2}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}}{\gamma_{n-1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\left[p_{n-1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\left(t_{1}\right)\right]^{2}}.

Now, in virtue of (
6
), it is

\lambda_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\sim\frac{\left(1-x_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}\left(1+x_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\beta+\frac{3}{2}}}{n}\sim\frac{1}{n^{2\beta+4}}.

(14)

Taking into account (
14
), and (
7
), (
9
) and (
11
) (all applied with
\gamma=\alpha+1
and
\delta=\beta+1
), we deduce the following estimate of
w_{0}

w_{0}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\frac{n^{2\beta+4}}{n^{4\beta+6}}=\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n^{2\beta+2}}.

(15)

On the other hand,

\Delta t_{0}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{1})=\left(1-x_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+x_{n,1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\beta+1}\sim\frac{1}{n^{2\beta+2}}

which, combined with (
15
), gives (
12a
). In order to prove (
12d
), proceeding in an analogous way, we start writing
w_{m+1}
as follows

w_{m+1}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{n})}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{n+1})}\right]^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\frac{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)}{(t-t_{n})\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{n})}\right]^{2}\frac{(t-t_{n})^{2}}{1-t}w^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t)dt.

Then, using
(t-t_{n})^{2}/(1-t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}
, (
6
) and (
8
) for
\gamma=\alpha+1
,
\delta=\beta+1
and
k=n
, (
7
), (
9
) and (
10
) also with
\gamma=\alpha+1
and
\delta=\beta+1
, we deduce the estimate

\displaystyle w_{n+1}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left[\frac{\left(p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\prime}(t_{n})}{p_{n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(t_{n+1})}\right]^{2}\lambda_{n,n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\sim\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha+2}}

with
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n)
. Since

\Delta t_{n}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n})\sim\left(1-x_{n,n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\alpha+1}\left(1+x_{n,n}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)^{\beta}\sim\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha+2}}

we can, finally, conclude that also (
12d
) holds true.
∎

Using the previous lemma we are able to prove our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
f\in W_{r}^{1}(w^{\alpha,\beta})
,
r\geq 1
, the error of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature formula (
1
) satisfies the following estimate

|e_{n}(f)|\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{(2n)^{r}}E_{2n+1-r}(f^{(r)})_{\varphi^{r}w^{\alpha,\beta},1}

(16)

where
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(f,n)
is a positive constant.

Proof.

We start by proving the estimate (
16
) in the case
r=1
. First, we are going to show that

\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}|f(t_{k})|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fw^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n}\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{\prime}(t)|\varphi(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt.

(17)

Taking into account (
12a
), (
12
) and (
12d
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}|f(t_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\left[\right.\Delta t_{0}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{1})|f(t_{0})|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta t_{k}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k})|f(t_{k})|+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Delta t_{n-1}\,w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n})|f(t_{n})|+\Delta t_{n}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n})|f(t_{n+1})|\left.\right].

In virtue of the following inequality

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}(b-a)|f(a)|\\
(b-a)|f(b)|\end{array}\right\}\leq\int_{a}^{b}|f(t)|dt+(b-a)\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(t)|dt,

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}|f(t_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\left[w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{1})\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|f(t)|dt+\Delta t_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|dt\right)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{k})\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|f(t)|dt+\Delta t_{k}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|dt\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n})\left(\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|f(t)|dt+\Delta t_{n-1}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|f^{\prime}(t)|dt\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle w^{\alpha,\beta}(t_{n})\left(\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|f(t)|dt+\Delta t_{n}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|dt\right),

from which, being for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
,

1\pm t_{k}\sim 1\pm t\sim 1\pm t_{k+1},\qquad t_{k}\leq t\leq t_{k+1},\quad

(18)

and, taking into account (
5
) and also that

\displaystyle\Delta t_{0}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}{n},\qquad-1<t\leq t_{1},

\displaystyle\Delta t_{n}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}{n},\qquad t_{n}\leq t<1,

we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}|f(t_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|f(t)|w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt+\frac{1}{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|\varphi(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|f(t)|w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt+\frac{1}{n}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|\varphi(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|f(t)|w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt+\frac{1}{n}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|f^{\prime}(t)|\varphi(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\left[\int_{-1}^{1}|f(t)|w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt+\frac{1}{n}\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{\prime}(t)|\varphi(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right]

i.e. (
17
). Since the
(n+1)
quadrature formula (
1
) is exact for any polynomial of degree at most
2n+1
, for
P\in\mathbb{P}_{2n+1}
we have

\displaystyle|e_{n}(f)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|e_{n}(f-P)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-1}^{1}(f-P)(t)w^{\alpha,\beta}(t)dt\right|+\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}(f-P)(t_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(f-P)w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}w_{k}\left|(f-P)(t_{k})\right|.

Now, combining the previous inequality with (
17
) and the following relation (see, for instance,
[
3
, p. 339]
,
[
4
, p. 286]
)

\|(f-P)^{\prime}\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}\leq{\mathcal{C}}(2n+2)\|(f-P)w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}+{\mathcal{C}}_{1}E_{2n}(f^{\prime})_{\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta},1},

we can deduce

\displaystyle|e_{n}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|(f-P)w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n}\|(f-P)^{\prime}\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|(f-P)w^{\alpha,\beta}\|_{1}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}}{n}E_{2n}(f^{\prime})_{\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta},1}.

Taking the infimum over
P\in\mathbb{P}_{2n+1}
and using the Favard inequality (
4
) we get

|e_{n}(f)|\leq{\mathcal{C}}E_{2n+1}(f)_{w^{\alpha,\beta},1}+\frac{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}}{n}E_{2n}(f^{\prime})_{\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta},1}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{2n}E_{2n}(f^{\prime})_{\varphi w^{\alpha,\beta},1}.

(19)

The estimate (
16
) for
r>1
can be deduced from (
19
) by iterating the application of the Favard inequality.
∎

A Bessel Analog of the Riesz Composition Formula
Christoph Fischbacher, Fritz Gesztesy, Roger Nichols

Abstract
. We provide an elementary derivation of the Bessel analog of the celebrated Riesz composition formula and use the former to effortlessly derive the latter.

1.
Introduction

This note aims at a pedagogical introduction into Riesz and Bessel potentials and some of their basic properties and connections with Bessel functions. In particular, we focus on offering an elementary and straightforward approach.

Given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the celebrated Riesz composition formula

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x^{\prime}\,|x-x^{\prime}|^{2\alpha-n}|x^{\prime}-y|^{2\beta-n}=k_{\alpha,\beta,n}|x-y|^{2\alpha+2\beta-n},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;x\neq y,

\displaystyle\hskip 213.39566pt\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\in(0,n/2),

(1.1)

where

k_{\alpha,\beta,n}=\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha)\Gamma((n/2)-\beta)}=\frac{\gamma_{\alpha,n}\gamma_{\beta,n}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta,n}},

(1.2)

and

\gamma_{\alpha,n}=\pi^{n/2}2^{2\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)/\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha),

(1.3)

is cited at numerous places, but rarely proved in all details. Indeed, the sources

[
5
, eq. (11.2)]
,
[
21
, eq. (1.1.3)]
,
[
32
, Sect. V.1]
,
all mention (
1
), (
1.2
), but only du Plessis
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
provides a detailed (and not-so-short) proof.

From the point of view of operator theory, the Riesz potential operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
, given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}({\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}f)(x)=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}f(y)\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$},&\\
\alpha\in(0,n/2),\;f\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),&\end{split}

(1.4)

formally (and, admittedly, somewhat naively) corresponds to the
-\alpha
-th power of the Laplacian in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
,
(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}\big|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
,
\alpha\in(0,n/2)
, with
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
a Lizorkin space, see Section
3
for details. Hence, on a formal level (to be made precise later, see, e.g., (
3.8
), (
3.22
)), the Riesz composition formula (
1
) corresponds to the semigroup property
1
1

1

The reader should keep in mind that what we denote by
(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,n/2)
, is often denoted by

,
\beta\in(0,n)
.

(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}(-\Delta)^{-\beta}=(-\Delta)^{-\alpha-\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta,(\alpha+\beta)\in(0,n/2).

(1.5)

We will derive (

) via first principles from an analogous composition formula for Bessel potentials, namely, given

,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x^{\prime}\,\frac{\lambda^{(n-2\alpha)/4}K_{(n/2)-\alpha}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x-x^{\prime}|\big)\lambda^{(n-2\beta)/4}K_{(n/2)-\beta}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x^{\prime}-y|\big)}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{(n/2)-\alpha}|x^{\prime}-y|^{(n/2)-\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad=\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,n}\lambda^{(n-2\alpha-2\beta)/4}\frac{K_{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x-y|\big)}{|x-y|^{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 69.70915pt\alpha,\beta,\lambda\in(0,\infty),\;x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;x\neq y,

where

\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,n}=\frac{(2\pi)^{n/2}2^{-1}\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}=\frac{\eta_{\alpha,n}\eta_{\beta,n}}{\eta_{\alpha+\beta,n}},

(1.7)

and

\eta_{\alpha,n}=(2\pi)^{n/2}2^{\alpha-1}\Gamma(\alpha).

(1.8)

Restricting
\alpha,\beta\in(0,n/2)
with
\alpha+\beta<n/2
, and applying the limit

in (
1.6
) results in the Riesz composition formula (
1
), (
1.2
).

From an operator theory point of view, the (bounded) Bessel potential operator
{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha,n,z}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
, given by

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha,n,z}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\eta_{\alpha,n}^{-1}(-z)^{(n-2\alpha)/4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{\alpha-(n/2)}

\displaystyle\quad\times K_{(n/2)-\alpha}\big((-z)^{1/2}|x-y|\big)f(y)\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt\alpha\in(0,\infty),\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty),\;f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),

coincides with the
-\alpha
-th power of the Helmholtz Laplacian

in

,

{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha,n,z}=(-\Delta-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha},\quad\alpha\in(0,\infty),\ z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty),

(1.10)

\begin{split}&(-\Delta-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}(-\Delta-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\beta}\\
&\quad=(-\Delta-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha-\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta\in(0,\infty),\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty).\end{split}

(1.11)

We conclude this section by proving that

converges to

in norm resolvent sense as

. The proof of this result relies on the following explicit characterization of the norm of a bounded multiplication operator.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
. The operator of multiplication
{\mathcal{M}}_{h}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
defined by
{\mathcal{M}}_{h}:f\mapsto hf
,
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
is a bounded linear operator and

\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(2.33)

Proof.

Let
h\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
the operator of multiplication by
h
in the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
. It is clear that
{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
is a linear operator and that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)},\quad f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x).

(2.34)

Hence,
\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}\leqslant\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}
. Therefore, to establish (
2.33
), it suffices to show that

\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}.

(2.35)

To this end, define the sets

A_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,|\,|h(x)|\geqslant\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\varepsilon\},\quad\varepsilon>0.

(2.36)

It follows from the definition of the essential supremum that

\text{$A_{\varepsilon}$ has positive Lebesgue measure for every $\varepsilon>0$}.

(2.37)

For each
\varepsilon>0
, choose a subset
B_{\varepsilon}\subseteq A_{\varepsilon}
such that
B_{\varepsilon}
has positive and finite Lebesgue measure. Then
\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}}\in L^{2}(({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))
,
\varepsilon>0
, and

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}d^{n}x\,|h(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\big[\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\varepsilon\big]^{2}\|\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}^{2},\quad\varepsilon>0.

(2.38)

As a result,
\|{\mathcal{M}}_{h}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}\geqslant\|h\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and (
2.35
) follows.
∎

If
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
, then
f(H_{0})\in{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))

f(H_{0})={\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{M}}_{f(|\cdot|^{2})}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1},

(2.39)

where

is the operator of multiplication by

is furnished by the spectral theorem and the functional calculus for self-adjoint operators; see, e.g.,

and
[

. The approach via the spectral theorem has the advantage that it applies to arbitrary self-adjoint operators, but in the case of

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)\in(0,\infty)
and suppose

converges in norm resolvent sense to

as

; that is,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}\Big\|\big((H_{0}+\lambda I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}\\
&\hskip 22.76219pt-\big(H_{0}^{-\alpha}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}=0,\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash S_{\alpha},\end{split}

(2.40)

where

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\sigma\big((H_{0}+\lambda I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}\big)}

\displaystyle=\big\{(\mu+\lambda)^{-\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,\mu\in[0,\infty),\,\lambda\in(0,1]\big\}\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle=\big\{\mu^{-\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,\mu\in(0,\infty)\big\}\cup\{0\}.

(2.41)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)\in(0,\infty)
. The equation
\mu^{-\alpha}=-1
has no solutions for
\mu\in(0,\infty)
. Therefore,
-1\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash S_{\alpha}
. Recalling the identity (see, e.g.,
[

, p. 178]
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(T_{1}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}-(T_{2}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}=(T_{1}-z_{0}I_{{\mathcal{H}}})(T_{1}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}\\
&\quad\times\big[(T_{1}-z_{0}I_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}-(T_{2}-z_{0}I_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}\big](T_{2}-z_{0}I_{{\mathcal{H}}})(T_{2}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1},\\
&\hskip 200.59201ptz\in\rho(T_{1})\cap\rho(T_{2}),\end{split}

(2.42)

where

,

, are closed operators in a complex, separable Hilbert space

with

, one may exploit boundedness of the factors
(T_{j}-z_{0}I_{{\mathcal{H}}})(T_{j}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}=I_{{\mathcal{H}}}+(z-z_{0})(T_{j}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}
,

, to conclude that it suffices to take

in (

, one obtains:

\displaystyle\Big\|\big((H_{0}+\lambda I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\;\;-\big(H_{0}^{-\alpha}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big\|{\mathcal{F}}\Big[\big((H_{0}+\lambda I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\big(H_{0}^{-\alpha}+I_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)^{-1}\Big]{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Big\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}

\displaystyle\quad=\underset{\mu\in(0,\infty)}{\rm ess.sup}\,\Big|\big((\mu+\lambda)^{-\alpha}+1\big)^{-1}-\big(\mu^{-\alpha}+1\big)^{-1}\Big|

\displaystyle\quad=\underset{\mu\in[0,\infty)}{\rm ess.sup}\,|f_{\alpha}(\mu+\lambda)-f_{\alpha}(\mu)|,\quad\lambda\in(0,1],

(2.43)

where

f_{\alpha}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\big(\mu^{-\alpha}+1\big)^{-1},&\mu\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\mu=0.\end{cases}

(2.44)

is continuous on

since

for all
\mu\in(0,\infty)
and
\lim_{\mu\downarrow 0}f_{\alpha}(\mu)=0=f_{\alpha}(0)
. In fact, since
\lim_{\mu\to\infty}f_{\alpha}(\mu)=1
, the function
f_{\alpha}
is actually uniformly continuous on
[0,\infty)
. In particular,

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
\delta(\varepsilon)\in(0,1/2)
such that

\underset{\mu\in[0,\infty)}{\rm ess.sup}\,|f_{\alpha}(\mu+\lambda)-f_{\alpha}(\mu)|<\varepsilon,\quad\lambda\in(0,\delta(\varepsilon)).

(2.45)

In consequence,

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}\Bigg[\underset{\mu\in[0,\infty)}{\rm ess.sup}\,|f_{\alpha}(\mu+\lambda)-f_{\alpha}(\mu)|\Bigg]=0,

(2.46)

and hence (
2.40

follows from (
2.43
) and (
2.46

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)\in(0,n/2)
,

, and introduce the Riesz potential operator

as follows:

\displaystyle({\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}f(y)

\displaystyle=\big(g_{\alpha,n}^{\vee}(0,\,\cdot\,)*f\big)(x),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hskip-46.94687pt\gamma_{\alpha,n}=\pi^{n/2}2^{2\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)/\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha),

for appropriate functions

(

)
.

(i)
Let

and

)

(ii)
Let

and

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant C_{\alpha,n,q,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)},\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x);

{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}\in{\mathcal{B}}\big(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)\big).

(iii)

\displaystyle\big[|\,\cdot\,|^{-2\alpha}f^{\wedge}\big]^{\vee}(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{2\alpha-n}f(y)

(3.4)

Moreover, the right-hand side in (

(iv)
If

h_{f}:=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}|\,\cdot\,|^{2\alpha-n}\ast f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)

(3.5)

and

|\,\cdot\,|^{-2\alpha}f^{\wedge}=(h_{f})^{\wedge}.

(3.6)

In particular, if

\big[|\,\cdot\,|^{-2\alpha}f^{\wedge}\big]^{\vee}=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}|\,\cdot\,|^{2\alpha-n}\ast f={\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha,n}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x).

(3.7)

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-\alpha}f=\big[|\,\cdot\,|^{-2\alpha}f^{\wedge}\big]^{\vee},

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\dom\big(H_{0}^{-\alpha}\big)

\alpha\in(0,n/4)

|\,\cdot\,|^{-2\alpha}f^{\wedge}

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
if and only if
\|f^{\wedge}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=0
; that is, if and only if
f\equiv 0
. As a result, no sign-definite function in
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\backslash\{0\}
belongs to
\dom\big(H_{0}^{-\alpha}\big)

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\eta:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a measurable function and let
M_{\eta}
be the maximal operator of multiplication by
\eta
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(M_{\eta}f)(\xi)=\eta(\xi)f(\xi)\,\text{ for a.e.~$\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$},\\
&f\in\dom(M_{\eta})=\big\{g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)\,\big|\,\eta g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)\big\}.\end{split}

(3.13)

Then, any dense subset
{\mathcal{C}}
of
\dom(M_{\eta})
is a core for
M_{\eta}
.

Proof.

First, note that
M_{\eta}^{*}=M_{\overline{\eta}}
and
M_{\overline{\eta}}^{*}=M_{\eta}
. Now, define
M_{{\eta},0}:=M_{{\eta}}\upharpoonright_{\mathcal{C}}
. We claim that
M_{\eta,0}^{*}=M_{\overline{\eta}}
. Since
M_{\eta,0}\subseteq M_{\eta}
, it immediately follows that
M_{\overline{\eta}}\subseteq M_{\eta,0}^{*}
. Now, suppose that
f\in{\mathcal{D}}(M_{\eta,0}^{*})
, which means that there exists
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)
such that for all
g\in{\mathcal{C}}
:

\begin{split}&(f,M_{\eta,0}g)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=\big(\tilde{f},g\big)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)}\\[2.84526pt]
&\quad\text{if and only if }\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}\xi\,\overline{\left(f(\xi)\overline{\eta(\xi)}-\tilde{f}(\xi)\right)}g(\xi)=0.\end{split}

(3.14)

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is dense, this implies
\overline{\eta}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)
. Consequently,
f\in{\mathcal{D}}(M_{\overline{\eta}})
and
M_{\eta,0}^{*}f=M_{\overline{\eta}}f
. This shows
M_{\eta,0}^{*}\subseteq M_{\overline{\eta}}
and therefore
M_{\eta,0}^{*}=M_{\overline{\eta}}
. Similarly, it can be shown that
M_{\overline{\eta},0}^{*}=M_{\eta}
. Therefore, one obtains
\overline{M_{\eta,0}}=M_{\eta,0}^{**}=M_{\eta}
, which proves that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a core for
M_{\eta}
. ∎

4.
Recovering the Riesz Composition Formula

from a Bessel Analog

The fact that

\begin{split}(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha}(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\beta}=(H_{0}-zI_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)})^{-\alpha-\beta},&\\
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha),\text{\rm Re}(\beta),\in(0,\infty),\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty),&\end{split}

(4.1)

combined with (
2.5
) and (
2.23
) thus instantly yields the following result, a Bessel (resp., Helmholtz) analog of the Riesz composition formula (see (
4.5

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha),\text{\rm Re}(\beta)\in(0,\infty)
,

z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x^{\prime}\,\frac{K_{(n/2)-\alpha}\big((-z)^{1/2}|x-x^{\prime}|\big)K_{(n/2)-\beta}\big((-z)^{1/2}|x^{\prime}-y|\big)}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{(n/2)-\alpha}|x^{\prime}-y|^{(n/2)-\beta}}\\
&\quad=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,n}}{(-z)^{n/4}}\frac{K_{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}\big((-z)^{1/2}|x-y|\big)}{|x-y|^{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}},\quad x,y,\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;x\neq y,\end{split}

(4.2)

where

\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,n}=\frac{(2\pi)^{n/2}2^{-1}\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}.

(4.3)

We call (
4.2
) a Bessel composition formula as fractional powers of the Laplacian,
-\Delta
, in connection with the Riesz potential and the Riesz composition formula (
1
), are now replaced by those of
-\Delta-z
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty)
in connection with the Bessel potential. Formula (
4.2
) is mentioned, for instance, in
[
3
, p. 338]
,
[
4
, eq. (2.5)]
,
[
6
, eq. (4, 7)]
,
[
32
, p. 135]
. The next result, the celebrated Riesz composition formula, is proved in detail by du Plessis
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
. We present an elementary and short argument based on the Bessel composition formula next. Indeed, taking
\lambda\downarrow 0
in (
4.2
) and using the limiting form for

K_{\nu}(\zeta)\underset{\zeta\rightarrow 0}{\sim}2^{-1}\Gamma(\nu)(\zeta/2)^{-\nu},\quad\text{\rm Re}(\nu)>0

(4.4)

(see, e.g.,
[
1
, No. 9.6.9]
), one can now easily recover the Riesz composition formula for all relevant values of
\alpha
and
\beta
from the Bessel composition formula (
4.2
). In particular, one recovers the constant

in (

[

Theorem 4.2

(The Riesz Composition Formula)
.

Suppose that
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\text{\rm Re}(\alpha),\text{\rm Re}(\beta),\text{\rm Re}(\alpha+\beta)\in(0,n/2)
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x^{\prime}\,|x-x^{\prime}|^{2\alpha-n}|x^{\prime}-y|^{2\beta-n}=k_{\alpha,\beta,n}|x-y|^{2\alpha+2\beta-n},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;x\neq y,

(4.5)

where

k_{\alpha,\beta,n}=\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha)\Gamma((n/2)-\beta)}=\frac{\gamma_{\alpha,n}\gamma_{\beta,n}}{\gamma_{\alpha+\beta,n}},

(4.6)

and

\gamma_{\alpha,n}=\pi^{n/2}2^{2\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha)/\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha).

(4.7)

Proof.

Fixing
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\{1\}
, Theorem

implies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x^{\prime}\,\lambda^{(n-\alpha-\beta)/2}\frac{K_{(n/2)-\alpha}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x-x^{\prime}|\big)K_{(n/2)-\beta}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x^{\prime}-y|\big)}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{(n/2)-\alpha}|x^{\prime}-y|^{(n/2)-\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad=\kappa_{\alpha,\beta,n}\lambda^{(n-2\alpha-2\beta)/4}\frac{K_{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}\big(\lambda^{1/2}|x-y|\big)}{|x-y|^{(n/2)-\alpha-\beta}},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\hskip 69.70915pt\alpha,\beta,\lambda\in(0,\infty),\;x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;x\neq y.

Restricting

, and using the estimate (

), an application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem together with the

) in (

) then yields (

)–(

).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Following the kind suggestion by the anonymous referee, we now sketch an alternative and elementary proof of the Riesz composition formula: One starts with the identity,

\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,s^{(n/2)-\alpha-1}e^{-s},\quad 0<\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)<n/2,

(4.10)

makes the substitution

,

,

\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha)|x|^{2\alpha-n}=4^{\alpha-(n/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{\alpha-(n/2)-1}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4t)},\quad 0<\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)<n/2.

(4.11)

Introducing the Gauss–Weierstrass integral kernel

W(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4t)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;t\in(0,\infty),

(4.12)

this results in

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}|x|^{2\alpha-n}=\frac{\Gamma((n/2)-\alpha)}{4^{\alpha}\pi^{n/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}|x|^{2\alpha-n}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{\alpha-1}W(x,t),&\\
0<\text{\rm Re}(\alpha)<n/2,\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.&\end{split}

(4.13)

Identity (
4.13
) is also called
Bochner’s subordination formula
for the Riesz potential kernel, see
[
29
, Theorem 2.34]
.

Next, introduce

I=\gamma_{\alpha,n}^{-1}\gamma_{\beta,n}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|y|^{2\alpha-n}|x-y|^{2\beta-n}\quad\alpha,\beta\in(0,n/2).

(4.14)

By (
4.13
) and Fubini’s theorem for nonnegative functions one obtains

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,t^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}(W(\,\cdot\,,t)*W(\,\cdot\,,s))(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,t^{\alpha-1}t^{\beta-1}W(x,t+s),

(4.15)

employing
(W(\,\cdot\,,t)*W(\,\cdot\,,s))(x)=W(x,t+s)
. Substituting

,
s=u(1-v)
,
u\in(0,\infty)
,

, one arrives at

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}udu\int_{0}^{1}dv\,(uv)^{\alpha-1}[u(1-v)]^{\beta-1}W(x,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,u^{\alpha+\beta-1}W(x,u)\int_{0}^{1}dv\,v^{\alpha-1}(1-v)^{\beta-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,u^{\alpha+\beta-1}W(x,u)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\alpha+\beta}^{-1}|x|^{2\alpha+2\beta-n},\quad\alpha,\beta\in(0,n/2),\;\alpha+\beta\in(0,n/2),

employing the
\beta
function integral and then Bochner’s subordination formula (

This computation extends to

\diamond

Appendix A
Some Background on Convolution-type Operators

We start by denoting the unitary Fourier transform and its inverse in

by

\displaystyle({\mathcal{F}}f)(\xi)\equiv f^{\wedge}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\LIM_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|\leqslant R}d^{n}x\,f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi},\quad f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),

\displaystyle\big({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g\big)(x)\equiv g^{\vee}(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\LIM_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|\xi|\leqslant R}d^{n}\xi\,g(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x},\quad g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi),

(A.1)

where
\LIM_{R\rightarrow\infty}
denotes the limit in the norm of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}\xi)
, respectively, in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
.

At this point we recall the following result from
[
27
, Theorem IX.29]
:

Theorem A.1
.

Assume
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
and suppose that either

(i)

g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
,

or,

(ii)

g^{\vee}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
.

Then, in either case, one introduces

(g(-i\nabla)f)(x):=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,g^{\vee}(x-y)f(y),\quad f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),

(A.2)

where the integral on the right-hand side of (
A.2
) converges for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in case
(i)

and for
(
Lebesgue
)
a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in case
(ii)
.

In this context it is convenient to also recall Young’s (convolution) inequality (see, e.g.,
[
27
, p. 29]
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|g*h\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\|h\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)},\\
&\,g\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),\;h\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),\;1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty,\,p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1+r^{-1}.\end{split}

(A.3)

Here
f\ast g
denotes the convolution of the functions
f
and
g
defined by (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Sect. 2.15]
and
[
27
, p. 6]
)

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,f(x-y)g(y)\,\text{ for a.e.~$x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$},

(A.4)

provided that
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
and
g\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
for some
p,q\in[1,\infty)
such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}\geqslant 1
.

If
p\in[1,\infty)
and
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
are fixed, then the operator in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
given by convolution with the function
g
,

A_{g}f=f\ast g,\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),

(A.5)

is a bounded operator by Young’s inequality (
A.3
) (with
q=1
and
r=p
) and one obtains the following estimate for the operator norm:

\|A_{g}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.6)

If
g
is nonnegative a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then equality actually holds in (
A.6
). We did not find a statement of the following (likely, well-known) result in the literature, so we include its proof for completeness.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\in(1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, and
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
with
g\geqslant 0
a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The operator
A_{g}:L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
defined by

A_{g}f=f\ast g,\quad f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x),

(A.7)

is bounded and

\|A_{g}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}=\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.8)

In particular,
\|A_{g}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}
does not depend on
p\in(1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
.

Proof.

If
p=\infty
, then Young’s inequality (
A.3
) implies

\|A_{g}f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)},\quad f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x).

(A.9)

Equality holds in (
A.9
) for
f=\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
(here and throughout
\chi_{\Omega}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) as

\displaystyle\|A_{g}\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}

\displaystyle=\underset{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{\rm ess.sup}\,\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(x-y)g(y)\bigg|

(A.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,g(y)=\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

Since
\|\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=1
, (
A.8
) follows for
p=\infty
.

Next, let
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
, then Young’s inequality (
A.3
) implies
A_{g}f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
and

\|A_{g}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=\|f\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.11)

Therefore,
A_{g}
is bounded with

\|A_{g}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}\leqslant\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.12)

If
\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=0
, then (
A.8
) is a trivial consequence of (
A.12
). To establish (
A.8
) in the nontrivial case
\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}>0
, it suffices to find a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
such that
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=1
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.13)

Consider the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
defined by

f_{j}=(2j)^{-n/p}\chi_{[-j,j]^{n}},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.14)

By inspection,
\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=1
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Furthermore, the reflection symmetry of the hypercube
[-j,j]^{n}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with respect to the origin
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in each of the
n
coordinate directions implies:

\displaystyle\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,f_{j}(x-y)g(y)\bigg|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,(2j)^{-n/p}\chi_{[-j,j]^{n}+x}(y)g(y)\bigg|^{p}

\displaystyle=(2j)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg[\int_{[-j,j]^{n}+x}d^{n}y\,g(y)\bigg]^{p},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}},

(A.15)

where in obvious notation

[-j,j]^{n}+x=\big\{y+x\,\big|\,y\in[-j,j]^{n}\big\},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.16)

Let
\varepsilon\in\big(0,\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)
. The condition that
g
is nonnegative a.e. on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
implies

\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,g(y),

(A.17)

so it is possible to choose
j(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\int_{[-j(\varepsilon),j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}y\,g(y)\geqslant\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(A.18)

For each
j>j(\varepsilon)
, one verifies that

[-j(\varepsilon),j(\varepsilon)]^{n}\subseteq[-j,j]^{n}+x\,\text{ whenever $x\in[-(j-j(\varepsilon)),j-j(\varepsilon)]^{n}$}.

(A.19)

Combining (
A.15
) and (
A.19
), one obtains:

\displaystyle\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(2j)^{-n}\int_{[-(j-j(\varepsilon)),j-j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg[\int_{[-j,j]^{n}+x}d^{n}y\,g(y)\bigg]^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(2j)^{-n}\int_{[-(j-j(\varepsilon)),j-j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg[\int_{[-j(\varepsilon),j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}y\,g(y)\bigg]^{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant(2j)^{-n}\int_{[-(j-j(\varepsilon)),j-j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}x\,\bigg[\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigg]^{p}

\displaystyle=(2j)^{-n}\bigg[\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigg]^{p}\int_{[-(j-j(\varepsilon)),j-j(\varepsilon)]^{n}}d^{n}x

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n}\bigg[\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigg]^{p},\quad j>j(\varepsilon),\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.20)

As a result,

\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\geqslant\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}\bigg[\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bigg],\quad j>j(\varepsilon),\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.21)

The above analysis shows that for each fixed
\varepsilon\in\big(0,\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)
, there exists
j(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geqslant\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\bigg[\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}-1\bigg]\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\cdot\,\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}}_{\leqslant 1}

\displaystyle\geqslant\bigg[\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}-1\bigg]\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad j>j(\varepsilon),\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.22)

Since

\lim_{j\to\infty}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}-1\bigg]\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}=0,

(A.23)

there exists
N(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
N(\varepsilon)>j(\varepsilon)
such that

\bigg[\bigg(\frac{j-j(\varepsilon)}{j}\bigg)^{n/p}-1\bigg]\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}>-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad j>N(\varepsilon),\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.24)

Thus, (
A.22
) and (
A.24
) yield the following result: For each
\varepsilon\in\big(0,\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\big)
, there exists
N(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

-\varepsilon<\|f_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}-\|g\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}<\varepsilon,\quad j>N(\varepsilon),\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(A.25)

Hence, (
A.13
) holds.
∎

Remark A.3
.

As a complement to Theorem
A.2
, we recall the following result for the product of a (pointwise) multiplication operator with a convolution operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
. For
p\in(2,\infty)
, let
K_{f,g}
denote the integral operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
with integral kernel

k_{f,g}(x,y)=f(x)g(x-y)\,\text{ for a.e.~$(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$}.

(A.26)

An application of the Hausdorff–Young inequality and
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
implies that
K_{f,g}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{p}\big(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)\big)
and

\|K_{f,g}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x))}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)}.

(A.27)

For further details, as well as extensions to more general integral operators, we refer to the remark given in
[
31
, p. 40]
.

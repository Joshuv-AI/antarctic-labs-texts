Permutation-Equivariant Quantum K-Theory X. Quantum Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch in Genus 0 This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Algebra, Topology, and Dynamics in Interaction in honor of Dmitry Fuchs. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Fuchs.html
Alexander GIVENTAL

Abstract
We extract genus 0 0 consequences of the all genera quantum HRR formula proved in Part IX. This includes re-proving and generalizing the adelic characterization of genus 0 0 quantum K-theory found in [Givental A., Tonita V., in Symplectic, Poisson, and Noncommutative Geometry, Math. Sci. Res. Inst. Publ. , Vol. 62, Cambridge University Press, New York, 2014, 43–91]. Extending some results of Part VIII, we derive the invariance of a certain variety (the “big J-function”), constructed from the genus 0 0 descendant potential of permutation-equivariant quantum K-theory, under the action of certain finite difference operators in Novikov’s variables, apply this to reconstructing the whole variety from one point on it, and give an explicit description of it in the case of the point target space.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that the graph of each
{\mathbf{f}}_{r}
for fixed values of the parameters
{\mathbf{t}}_{2r},{\mathbf{t}}_{3r},\dots
is a Lagrangian variety in
(\mathcal{K},\Omega)
(where
\mathcal{K}
is the space of
K
-valued Laurent polynomials in

), and the whole family of such Lagrangian varieties for, say,

determines
\mathcal{L}_{X}
. However, a value of
{\mathbf{f}}_{1}

, does not “remember” the values of the parameters
{\mathbf{t}}_{2},{\mathbf{t}}_{3},\dots
. One reason to introduce
\mathcal{L}_{X}
parameterized by all the

is that from a point
{\mathbf{f}}=({\mathbf{f}}_{1},{\mathbf{f}}_{2},\dots)\in\mathcal{L}_{X}

\mathcal{K}^{\infty}_{-}

\mathcal{L}_{X}
to be Lagrangian in
(\mathcal{K}^{\infty},\Omega^{\infty})
.

We will give an
adelic characterization
of

in terms of

genus-0 K-theoretic GW-invariants of

\chi^{\rm fake}(X_{g,n,d};V):=\int_{[X_{g,n,d}]}\operatorname{td}(T_{X_{g,n,d}})\operatorname{ch}(V),

where

is the virtual fundamental class, and

is the virtual tangent bundle. The genus-0 descendant potential
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\rm fake}
is defined as a function of

\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\rm fake}(t)=\sum_{n,d}\frac{Q^{d}}{n!}\langle t(L),\dots,t(L)\rangle_{0,n,d}^{\rm fake},

\big\langle\phi_{\alpha_{1}}L^{m_{1}},\dots,\phi_{\alpha_{n}}L^{m_{n}}\big\rangle_{0,n,d}:=\chi^{\rm fake}\big(X_{0,n,d};\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{ev}_{i}^{*}(\phi_{\alpha_{i}})L_{i}^{m_{i}}\big)

The graph of the differential

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}=({\mathbf{f}}_{1},{\mathbf{f}}_{2},\dots,{\mathbf{f}}_{r},\dots)
be a sequence of rational functions of
q
with values in
K=K^{0}(X)\otimes\Lambda
considered as an element of the symplectic loop space

(\mathcal{K}^{\infty},\Omega^{\infty})

\mathcal{L}_{X}\subset\mathcal{K}^{\infty}
of the “big J-function” of permutation-equivariant quantum K-theory of
X
if and only if it satisfies the following
criteria:

(i)

For each
r=1,2,\dots
, the Laurent series expansion
{\mathbf{f}}_{r}^{(1)}
of
{\mathbf{f}}_{r}(q)
near
q=1
lies in the Lagrangian submanifold
\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\rm fake}\subset\big(\mathcal{K}^{\rm fake},\Omega^{\rm fake}\big)
representing the graph of the differential of the genus
0
descendant potential
\mathcal{F}_{X}^{\rm fake}
of fake quantum K-theory of
X
.

(ii)

For each
r
, and each root of unity
\zeta\neq 1
, the Laurent series expansion
{\mathbf{f}}_{r}^{(\zeta)}
of
{\mathbf{f}}_{r}\big(q^{1/m}/\zeta\big)
near
q=1
, where

is the primitive order of
\zeta
, lies in the Lagrangian subspace

\triangle_{\zeta}\Psi^{m}\big(T_{{\mathbf{f}}_{rm}^{(1)}}\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\rm fake}\big)\otimes_{\Psi^{m}(\Lambda)}\Lambda,

where the operator of multiplication
\triangle_{\zeta}
is given by the formula

\triangle_{\zeta}:=\exp\left\{\sum_{k>0}\left(\frac{\Psi^{k}(T^{*}_{X}-1)}{k(1-\zeta^{-k}q^{k/m})}-\frac{\Psi^{km}(T^{*}_{X}-1)}{k(1-q^{km})}\right)\right\}.

Theorem
1.2
generalizes several previously known results. Setting
{\mathbf{t}}_{k}=0
for all

we obtain the result of
[
8
]
which characterizes the version of genus-0 quantum K-theory which does not involve permutations of the marked points. Setting all
{\mathbf{t}}_{k}

equal to each other, we obtain an adelic characterization of permutation-equivariant J-function studied in Part VII and
Part VIII
[
5
,

]
.

In fact one can prove Theorem
1.2
by generalizing the arguments in the genus-
0
paper
[
8
]
by the author and Valentin Tonita. Our goal here is, however,
is to derive this genus-
0
result from the all-genera quantum HRR theorem established in Part IX of this series. That all-genera result is a formula expressing a quantum state (in the Fock space of
(\mathcal{K}^{\infty},\Omega^{\infty})
), which represents the appropriate total descendant potential of the theory, in terms of its adelic counterpart described via the “fake” quantum K-theory. What seems peculiar at the first sight is that Theorem
1.2
above is not an
adelic formula
, but merely an
adelic characterization
of points on
\mathcal{L}_{X}
(in terms of their localizations at the roots of unity). How can the quasi-classical limit at
\hbar=0
(and this is how the genus-
0
information is supposed to be extracted from the quantum-mechanical description of the all-genera data) fail to yield a formula too? The answer to this puzzle lies in the unusual feature of the quantum-mechanical formalism of the permutation-equivariant quantum K-theory, where the Adams operations act non-trivially on the Planck constant:
\Psi^{r}\big(\hbar^{\pm 1}\big)=\hbar^{\pm r}
. Consequently, the quasi-classical limit loses its familiar form of a single Lagrangian variety, and acquires a more intriguing structure.

1.2

Furthermore, to verify the criterion (ii), take
\zeta\neq 1

{\mathbf{f}}_{r}^{(\zeta)}=\Psi^{1/r}\underline{{\mathbf{f}}}_{r}^{(\zeta)}

How is the space
T

T_{f}\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\rm fake}

\big[\Psi^{M}(f)\big]_{+}

[\underline{{\mathbf{f}}}_{mr}]_{+}^{(1)}

Ultimately we conclude that if
{\mathbf{f}}=({\mathbf{f}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{f}}_{r},\dots)

\mathcal{L}_{X}

1.2

(i)\ \ {\mathbf{f}}_{r}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\rm fake},\qquad\text{and}\qquad(ii)\ \ {\mathbf{f}}_{r}^{(\zeta)}\in\triangle_{\zeta}\Psi^{m}\big(T_{{\mathbf{f}}_{rm}^{(1)}}\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\rm fake}\big)\otimes_{\Psi^{m}(\Lambda)}\Lambda

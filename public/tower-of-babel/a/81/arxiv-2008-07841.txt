On the Convergence of Consensus Algorithms with Markovian Noise and Gradient Bias
Hoi-To Wai

Abstract
This paper presents a finite time convergence analysis for a decentralized stochastic approximation (SA) scheme. The scheme generalizes several algorithms for decentralized machine learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning. Our proof technique involves separating the iterates into their respective consensual parts and consensus error. The consensus error is bounded in terms of the stationarity of the consensual part, while the updates of the consensual part can be analyzed as a perturbed SA scheme. Under the Markovian noise and time varying communication graph assumptions, the decentralized SA scheme has an expected convergence rate of O ⁡ ( log ⁡ T / T ) {\mathcal{}O}(\log T/\sqrt{T}) , where T T is the iteration number, in terms of squared norms of gradient for nonlinear SA with smooth but non-convex cost function. This rate is comparable to the best known performances of SA in a centralized setting with a non-convex potential function.

Introduction

Decentralized algorithm has become a core tool for control, optimization and machine learning in the increasingly connected world. Among others, a common setting is multi-agent optimization, where a group of agents on a connected graph seek to minimize a sum of local functions using a common parameter. For convex optimization problems, consensus-based algorithms have been developed in
[
1
]
for deterministic optimization, and the recent results have extended the latter to stochastic/non-convex optimization
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

With new machine learning inspired-models such as the training of neural networks and reinforcement learning, researchers have focused on developing decentralized methods for
non-convex
stochastic optimization
[
6
]
. However, methods developed so far for this scenario are limited to using
unbiased
gradient estimates
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, requiring i.i.d. data samples and easy-to-compute gradients. The latter requirements may be an obstacle in deploying the methods in sophisticated problems.

The focus of this paper is to relax the unbiased gradient estimate assumption of decentralized stochastic optimization methods. We consider a decentralized stochastic approximation (DSA) scheme whose local update directions are computed from samples of a Markov chain, and the update directions converge asymptotically to a non-gradient mean field, i.e., it is a biased DSA scheme. Our setting is relevant to a decentralized stochastic gradient method with ergodic data as well as multi-agent reinforcement learning.
Our contributions are:

•

For non-convex stochastic optimization, we prove that the DSA scheme with updates from Markov samples converge to a consensual and stationary point at a rate of
{\mathcal{}O}(\log T/\sqrt{T})
, where
T
is the iteration number.

•

To analyze the convergence of biased DSA, we develop a decoupling procedure to split the DSA iterates into their consensual part and consensus error, which has a similar favor to the analysis in
[
2
,
10
,
11
]
. Such separation allows us to (i) bound the consensus error explicitly; and subsequently (ii) analyze the recursion of the consensual part as a
perturbed
biased SA scheme through adopting prior analysis, e.g.,
[
12
]
.

•

We show that the biased DSA scheme also converges for time varying topology under a standard bounded connectivity assumption.

For biased DSA scheme with Markov samples, the closest work related to ours include
[
2
]
which considered a convex optimization related setting; and
[
13
]
which proposed an algorithm that may not achieve exact consensus.
To the best of our knowledge, this paper provides the first finite-time convergence analysis for DSA scheme with biased updates relying on Markov samples. In addition, this work is related to the recent works on non-asymptotic analysis of SA schemes
[
14
,
15
,

]

2
Decentralized SA Scheme

Consider a network of
n
agents which have the goal of obtaining a common (a.k.a. consensual) solution,
\overline{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is a stationary point to the optimization problem:

V^{\star}=\min_{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}~V(\bm{\theta})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}(\bm{\theta}),\vskip-2.84544pt

(1)

where
V_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth (but possibly non-convex) function and is lower bounded. Conceptually, the
i
th function
V_{i}(\bm{\theta})
can be interpreted as the local potential/cost function held by the
i
th agent.
The agents communicate through an undirected, connected simple graph
G=(V,E)
where
V=\{1,...,n\}
is the node set, and
E\subseteq V\times V
is the edge set which includes self-loops.
The graph is endowed with a symmetric, weighted adjacency matrix
{\bm{A}}
. We assume

H​​ 1
.

The matrix
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
satisfies:

1.

A_{ij}=0
whenever
(i,j)\notin E
.

2.

{\bm{A}}{\bf 1}={\bm{A}}^{\top}{\bf 1}={\bf 1}
.

3.

\|{\bm{U}}^{\top}{\bm{A}}{\bm{U}}\|_{2}\leq 1-\bar{\rho}
, where
\bar{\rho}\in(0,1]
and
{\bm{U}}
is a projection matrix such that
{\bm{I}}-\frac{1}{n}{\bf 1}{\bf 1}^{\top}={\bm{U}}{\bm{U}}^{\top}
.

Note that condition 3) is equivalent to requiring that
\max\{|\lambda_{2}({\bm{A}})|,|\lambda_{n}({\bm{A}})|\}\leq 1-\bar{\rho}
. Such a weighted adjacency matrix
{\bm{A}}
exists if
G
is connected, e.g.,
[
1
]
.

At iteration
t
, each agent holds a local solution
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)}
and for simplicity, we assume the initialization
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(0)}=\bm{\theta}_{j}^{(0)}
for any
i,j
. Let
X^{t+1}\in{\sf X}
be a random sample, where
{\sf X}
is a (discrete or continuous) state space. The decentralized SA (DSA) scheme performs the recursion at all agents
i=1,...,n
,

\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t+1)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\bm{\theta}_{j}^{(t)}-\gamma_{t+1}{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X^{t+1}),\vskip-2.84544pt

(2)

where the first term corresponds to an average consensus step among the neighbors of agent
i
, and
{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X^{t+1})
is a local stochastic update computed from
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)}
,
X^{t+1}
. Furthermore, we denote

{\mathcal{}F}_{t}=\sigma\{\{\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(0)}\}_{i=1}^{n},X^{s},~s=0,1,2,...,t\}\vskip-2.84544pt

(3)

as the filtration of random elements up to iteration
t
. Note that
\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)}
is measurable w.r.t.
{\mathcal{}F}_{t}
. In the special case of
n=1
, (
2
) is reduced to the classical SA scheme
[
17
]
; for general
n>1
, (
2
) is related to a matrix momentum SA scheme studied in
[
18
]
.

We consider a
biased
DSA scheme in this paper. To describe the setup,
the random samples
\{X^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
forms form a Markov chain (MC) with the kernel
\mathbb{P}
satisfying:

H​​ 2
.

The Markov kernel
\mathbb{P}:{\sf X}\times{\sf X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
generating
\{X^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has a unique stationary distribution
\mu:{\sf X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and it is irreducible, aperiodic.

For any measurable function
f
on
{\sf X}
, with a slight abuse of notation we define

\mathbb{P}f(X^{t})=\mathbb{E}[f(X^{t+1})|X^{t}]=\int_{\sf X}f(x)\mathbb{P}(X^{t},dx)
. The mean field of
{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};X)
is

\textstyle h_{i}(\bm{\theta}):=\int_{\sf X}{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)\,\mu(dx).

(4)

Importantly, the
averaged
mean field

\textstyle\overline{h}(\bm{\theta})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}(\bm{\theta})

(5)

is related to Problem (
1
) through:

H​​ 3
.

For any
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
d_{0},c_{0}>0
such that

\begin{split}&\langle\overline{h}(\bm{\theta})\,|\,{\nabla}V(\bm{\theta})\rangle\geq c_{0}\|\overline{h}(\bm{\theta})\|^{2},~d_{0}\|\overline{h}(\bm{\theta})\|^{2}\geq\|{\nabla}V(\bm{\theta})\|^{2},\end{split}

where
\langle{\bm{x}}\,|\,{\bm{y}}\rangle={\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{y}}
denotes Euclidean inner product.

The constants
c_{0},d_{0}
characterize the multiplicative
bias
of the mean field in view of a stationary solution to (
1
). It allows the local stochastic update to be
quasi-gradient
, which is relevant when the gradient of
V_{i}(\bm{\theta})
is hard to obtain.
Besides, the transient of stochastic update is biased under H
2
with
\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X^{t+1})|{\mathcal{}F}_{t}]\neq h_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)})
for finite
t
. To this end, we assume:

H​​ 4
.

For any
i=1,...,n
,
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
x\in{\sf X}
, there exists a measurable function
\widehat{\mathcal{}H}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\sf X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)-\mathbb{P}\widehat{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)={\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)-h_{i}(\bm{\theta}).\vskip-2.84544pt

(6)

The measurable function in H
4
is a solution to the Poisson equation. Such function exists under H
2
and additional conditions on the MC. For example, we may assume
\mathbb{P}
is uniformly ergodic, i.e., for a constant
K
,

\textstyle\sup_{x\in{\sf X}}\|\mathbb{P}^{t}(x,\cdot)-\mu(\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}\leq K\lambda^{-t},~\forall~t\geq 0,

(7)

such that
\lambda\in[0,1)
characterizes the mixing time of
\mathbb{P}
.
H
4
is also satisfied under more relaxed conditions, e.g., geometric ergodicity, see
[
19
, Ch. 21.2]
,
[
20
]
.

We assume that both the local stochastic updates and potential functions are smooth w.r.t.
\bm{\theta}
:

H​​ 5
.

For any
i
, the local stochastic update
{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)
is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
\bm{\theta}
, i.e., for any
\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\sup_{x\in{\sf X}}\|{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)-{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}^{\prime};x)\|\leq L_{h}\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\|.

(8)

Consequently, the mean field map
h_{i}(\bm{\theta})
is
L_{h}
-Lipschitz such that
\|h_{i}(\bm{\theta})-h_{i}(\bm{\theta}^{\prime})\|\leq L_{h}\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\|
,
\forall~\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

H​​ 6
.

The potential function
V(\bm{\theta})
is
L_{V}
-smooth such that
\|{\nabla}V(\bm{\theta})-{\nabla}V(\bm{\theta}^{\prime})\|\leq L_{V}\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\|
for any
\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lastly, we assume the following on
{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)
:

H​​ 7
.

For any
\bm{\theta}=(\bm{\theta}_{1},...,\bm{\theta}_{n})
, there exists
\sigma_{o}
such that

\begin{split}&\textstyle\sup_{x\in{\sf X}}\|{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i};x)-{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}}{\mathcal{}H}_{j}(\bm{\theta}_{j};x)\|\\
&\leq\sigma_{o}\{{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c})\|+\|\bm{\theta}_{i}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\|\},\end{split}

(9)

for any
i=1,...,n
, where we have
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}={\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}}\bm{\theta}_{j}
.

H​​ 8
.

For any
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
\sigma_{h}
such that

\begin{split}&\textstyle\sup_{x\in{\sf X}}\big\|{\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}}{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c};x)-\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c})\big\|\leq\sigma_{h}.\end{split}

(10)

In particular,
\sigma_{o}
quantifies the
heterogeneity
of the stochastic updates, while
\sigma_{h}
plays a similar role as the variance of
(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)-h_{i}(\bm{\theta})\}
.
Under H
5
, we observe H
7
can be satisfied if the l.h.s. of (
9
) is upper bounded by
{\mathcal{}O}(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|h_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i})\|)
. Our condition H
7
is considerably weaker than the heterogeneity assumption required by
[
7
]
, as we allow the heterogeneity between the local updates to grow with the norm of mean field.

Notice that
H
7
, H
8
are
uniform bounds
on the norms of error for all
x\in{\sf X}
. They are considerably stronger than those for decentralized stochastic algorithms with i.i.d. data, e.g.,
[
21
,
7
]
. However, we remark that this is a caveat for the prior works on SA with Markov noise as well, e.g.,
[
22
,
13
,
12
,
23
]
. We discuss two applications.

2.1
Decentralized SGD with Ergodic Data

In this case, the
i
th potential function is taken as the following stochastic objective function:

V_{i}(\bm{\theta}):=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}}\big[V_{i}(\bm{\theta};X_{i})\big].\vskip-2.84544pt

(11)

The local stochastic update is given by

{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X_{i}^{t+1})={\nabla}V_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X_{i}^{t+1}),\vskip-2.84544pt

(12)

such that
\{X_{i}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Markov chain with the kernel
\mathbb{P}_{i}:{\sf X}_{i}\times{\sf X}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a unique stationary distribution
\mu_{i}:{\sf X}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Consequently, the mean field of
{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};X_{i}^{t+1})
is the gradient
h_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)})={\nabla}V_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)})
.

Eq. (
11
), (
12
) generalize the vanilla decentralized SGD method
[
7
]
to scenarios with non-i.i.d. (a.k.a. ergodic) data. As discussed in
[
24
,
22
]
, the latter is important to applications where data samples are not obtained independently. For example, the data samples are generated using a Markov chain Monte carlo method.

To see that (
11
), (
12
) fit the assumptions of this paper, we take
{\sf X}={\sf X}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\sf X}_{n}
and form
\mathbb{P}
by concatenating the local Markov kernels.
Clearly, H
3
is satisfied with
c_{0}=d_{0}=1
; H
2
, H
4
depend on the Markov chain; H
5
, H
6
are related to the smoothness of
V_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)
w.r.t.
\bm{\theta}
; H
7
can be satisfied with more homogeneous objective function; H
8
bounds the noise in estimating the mean field by averaging the local stochastic updates.

2.2
Decentralized TD(0) Learning

We consider the policy evaluation problem in a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) setting. Our aim is to compute the value function under a policy
\pi
for an unknown Markov decision process (MDP) using linear function approximation
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
.

Consider the MDP at state
x
with the reward of
{\rm R}(x)
. The agents only observe a local reward
{\rm R}_{i}(x)
satisfying
{\rm R}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm R}_{i}(x)
. We aim at approximating the value function as
V(x)=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{s}{\rm R}(x_{s})|x_{0}=x]\approx\bm{\theta}^{\top}\Phi(x)
, where
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the function parameter and
\Phi(x)
is a ‘feature’ vector.
To find
\bm{\theta}
, the decentralized TD(0) learning algorithm
[
26
,
27
]
deploys (
2
) with the following local stochastic update:

{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)};x)=\Phi(x)\big\{{\rm R}_{i}(x)+(\gamma{\Phi}(x^{\prime})-\Phi(x))^{\top}\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)}\big\}

where
x^{\prime}\in{\sf X}
denotes the next state drawn from the MDP when the current state is
x
. The terms inside the curly bracket is the temporal difference error.
We observe that the resultant algorithm is a
linear DSA
scheme.

To discuss the performance of TD(0), we take
V_{i}(\bm{\theta})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{\theta}-\bm{\theta}^{\star}\|^{2}
, where
\bm{\theta}^{\star}
solves the Bellman equation:

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\Phi(x)(\gamma{\Phi}(x^{\prime})-\Phi(x))^{\top}]\bm{\theta}^{\star}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\Phi(x){\rm R}(x)].

Most of our assumptions can be satisfied by the linear DSA.
Using
[
28
, Lemma 3 & 4]
, H
3
is satisfied with
c_{0}=\frac{1-\gamma}{4}
, and we can show that
d_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\|\Phi(x)(\gamma\Phi(x^{\prime})-\Phi(x))^{\top}\|_{2}]^{2}
. H
2
, H
4
are conditions on the Markov chain; H
5
–H
7
can be satisfied with a bounded
\Phi(x)
,
{\rm R}(x)
.
Lastly, H
8
can be relaxed in the analysis as the resultant DSA scheme is linear. In the interest of space, we leave the development of the latter case to a future work.

3
Finite-Time Analysis of DSA

For general smooth cost function
V(\bm{\theta})
, we consider a random terminating time
\tau(T)
such that

\textstyle{\rm Pr}(\tau(T)=t)=\gamma_{t+1}/\sum_{s=0}^{T}\gamma_{s+1},~t=0,...,T,

where
\tau(T)\in\{0,...,T\}
is selected independently and
T
is the maximum number of iterations. This is a common stopping criterion proposed in
[
21
]
. For DSA, it can be decided by the agents with a simple consensus protocol before the iterations.
Our main result is summarized as:

Theorem 1
.

Under H
1
–H
8
, suppose the step size satisfies

\textstyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\gamma_{t}\leq\min\Big\{1,\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2\sigma_{o}},\frac{c_{0}}{2\widetilde{C}^{\sf mk}}\Big\}

(13)

and there exists
\hat{a}
such that
0\leq\gamma_{t}-\gamma_{t+1}\leq\hat{a}\gamma_{t}^{2}
. For any
T\geq 0
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(\tau(T))})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{{\rm C}^{\sf tot}}{(c_{0}/2)\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}},~~\max_{i=1,...,n}\hskip-5.69046pt\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(\tau(T))}\hskip-2.84544pt-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(\tau(T))}\|]\leq\frac{\frac{1}{c_{0}}{\rm C}^{\sf tot}+\frac{3\sigma_{o}}{2\bar{\rho}}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}}{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}},

(14)

where we have defined
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{\theta}_{i}^{(t)}
,

\textstyle{\rm C}^{\sf tot}:=V(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{(0)}}{n})-V^{\star}+{\rm C}_{0}^{\sf mk}+\overline{\rm C}^{\sf mk}\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2},

and the constants
\widetilde{C}^{\sf mk}
,
{\rm C}_{0}^{\sf mk}
,
\overline{\rm C}^{\sf mk}
will be specified in Section
3.1
. The expectation is taken w.r.t. the terminating iteration
\tau(T)
and the Markovian randomness.

For the best convergence rate, we may take
\gamma_{t}=a_{0}/\sqrt{t+a_{1}}
for some
a_{0},a_{1}>0
. In this case, the theorem shows that the squared norm of mean field and the consensus error converge at the rate of
{\mathcal{}O}(\log T/\sqrt{T})
. By H
3
and (
14
), we have that
\mathbb{E}[\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(\tau(T))})\|^{2}]
converges at
{\mathcal{}O}(\log T/\sqrt{T})
, i.e., the DSA scheme finds a stationary point to problem (
1
). Note that this is a standard rate for non-convex stochastic optimization
[
6
]
.
Compared to existing works, our convergence rate is similar to a centralized SA scheme, e.g.,
[
12
]
, and it strengthens that of
[
13
]
for DSA with Markov noise, as we provide a convergence rate for
exact consensus
.

As will be derived later, the constant
{\rm C}^{\sf tot}
is proportional to
{\mathcal{}O}(\bar{\rho}^{-1})
, i.e., related to the spectral gap of the weighted adjacency matrix [cf. H
1
] and the magnitude
\sup_{x,\bm{\theta}}\|\widehat{H}_{i}(\bm{\theta};x)\|
in H
4
. In the case of uniform MC, the latter is in the order of
{\mathcal{}O}(\frac{1}{1-\lambda})
such that it is related to the mixing time of the Markov chain. Our bound also highlights on the initialization
V({\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{i}^{(0)}}/{n})
.

Instead of analyzing the convergence of the DSA scheme with a single potential function, in the analysis that follows, we adopt a divide-and-conquer approach similar to
[
2
,
10
,
11
]
, where we first decompose the DSA iterate
\bm{\theta}_{1}^{(t)},...,\bm{\theta}_{n}^{(t)}
into its consensual part and consensus error.
By observing the individual update formulas, we bound the consensus error
separately
as the latter depends on stationarity of the averaged iterate. Subsequently, the consensual part can be analyzed using similar technique as a centralized SA scheme.
Due to space limitation, we only provide the analysis for general nonlinear DSA under H
1
–H
8
.

Proof of Theorem

\bm{\theta}^{(t)}=({\bf 1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d})\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}+({\bm{U}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d})\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{o}^{(t)}.

\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{o}^{(t+1)}

Lemma 1

Assume H
1

The above lemma shows that the consensus error can be upper bounded by the convolution between an exponential term

and the norm of mean field weighted by the step size as
\gamma_{s+1}\{1+\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(s)})\|\}

Importantly,

\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|\rightarrow 0

Next, we focus on the convergence of the consensual component
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}

\begin{split}&({\textstyle\frac{1}{n}{\bf 1}^{\top}}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d}){\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}^{(t)};X^{t+1})\\

where

Extension to Time-varying Graph

Our analysis can be extended to scenarios when the communication graph is time varying. Let
G^{(t)}=(V,E^{(t)})
be a simple, undirected graph which is potentially not connected, where
E^{(t)}\subseteq E
, and the graph is associated with a weighted adjacency matrix
{\bm{A}}^{(t)}
.
We replace
{\bm{A}}
by
{\bm{A}}^{(t)}
in the DSA scheme (
2
) at iteration
t
, and H
1
is updated with the following assumption

H​​ 9
.

For any
t\geq 0
, the matrix
{\bm{A}}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfies:

1.

A_{ij}^{(t)}=0
whenever
(i,j)\notin E^{(t)}
.

2.

{\bm{A}}^{(t)}{\bf 1}=({\bm{A}}^{(t)})^{\top}{\bf 1}={\bf 1}
.

3.

\exists B\geq 0

The last condition can be guaranteed under the ‘bounded communication’ setting

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the convergence of a biased decentralized stochastic approximation (DSA) scheme. The scheme is a multi-agent optimization algorithm relying on biased, stochastic updates that approximate the gradient of a smooth cost function. Here, the biasednesses stem from taking Markov samples and quasi-gradients in the updates. We prove that DSA finds a consensual and stationary point to the cost function at a rate of
{\mathcal{}O}(\log T/\sqrt{T})
, where
T
is the maximum iteration number. Future works include extending to asynchronous, gradient tracking DSA, state-controlled Markov chain, etc..

&\leq\big(2+\frac{L_{h}}{2n}+\frac{4\sigma_{o}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\big)\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}+\big(\sigma_{h}^{2}+\frac{4\sigma_{o}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\big)\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}.\end{split}

(30)

To analyze the last two terms
A_{3}^{i}
,
A_{4}^{i}
, we denote

\bm{\mathcal{E}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{n}}{\bf 1}^{\top}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d},~~\bm{\theta}_{c}^{(t)}:=({\bf 1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d})\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)},

(31)

such that
\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}_{c};x)=(\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}_{1}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c};x);\cdots;\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}_{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c};x))
.

Thirdly, we observe that from
[
29
, Lemma 4.2]
, under H
2
, H
4
, H
8
, it can be shown for any
\bm{\theta}_{c}=({\bf 1}\otimes{\bm{I}}_{d})\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}
, with
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
x\in{\sf X}
that:

\|\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}_{c};x)\|\leq K_{\mathbb{P}}.

(32)

Here,
K_{\mathbb{P}}
depends on the mixing time of the Markov chain, e.g., it is proportional to
\frac{1}{1-\lambda}
under the uniform ergodicity condition (
7
).
Therefore,

\begin{split}\textstyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{3}^{i}&=\gamma_{1}\langle{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(0)})\,|\,\bm{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}_{c}^{(0)};X^{0})\rangle\\
&\leq\gamma_{1}K_{\mathbb{P}}\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(0)})\|.\end{split}

(33)

Fourthly, using
|\gamma_{t+2}-\gamma_{t+1}|\leq\hat{a}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}
, we have

{\small\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{4}^{i}&\leq\hat{a}d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)})\|\|\bm{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}_{c}^{(t)};X^{t+1})\|+L_{V}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}\|\|\bm{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}(\bm{\theta}_{c}^{(t)};X^{t+1})\|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\gamma_{T+1}\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(T)})\|\|\bm{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{P}\hat{\mathcal{}H}_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{c}^{(T)};X^{T+1})\|\\[2.84544pt]
&\leq K_{\mathbb{P}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big\{\hat{a}d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)})\|+L_{V}\gamma_{t+1}\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}\|\big\}+\gamma_{T+1}K_{\mathbb{P}}\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(T)})\|.\end{split}}

To bound
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{4}^{i}
, we observe that

\begin{split}&\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)})\|\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\big\{1+\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)})\|^{2}\big\},\end{split}

the latter can be further simplified as

\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)})\|^{2}\leq a^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}.

Moreover,

{\small\begin{split}&\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t+1)}-\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)}\|=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})+{\bm{e}}_{0}^{(t)}+{\bm{e}}_{1}^{(t)}\|\\
&\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\big\{\sigma_{h}+\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|+{\textstyle\frac{L_{h}}{n}}\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{o}^{(t)}\|\}\\
&\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\big\{\sigma_{h}+\frac{1+\frac{L_{h}}{n}}{2}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}+{\textstyle\frac{L_{h}}{2n}}\|\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{o}^{(t)}\|^{2}\}\\
&\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\big\{{\textstyle\frac{\bar{\rho}n(1+2\sigma_{h})+L_{h}+4L_{h}\sigma_{o}^{2}}{2\bar{\rho}n}}+{\textstyle\frac{\bar{\rho}n+4L_{h}\sigma_{o}^{2}}{2\bar{\rho}n}}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}\}.\end{split}}

We observe the crude upper bound

\begin{split}&\gamma_{T+1}\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(T)})\|\leq d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\gamma_{T+1}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(T)})\|\\
&\leq\frac{d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\big(1+\gamma_{T+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(T)})\|^{2}\big)\leq\frac{d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}+\frac{d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}.\end{split}

(34)

Define the constants

\begin{split}{\rm C}_{0}^{\sf mk}&:=K_{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{\frac{d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}+\gamma_{1}\|{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(0)})\|\Big\},\\
{\rm C}_{1}^{\sf mk}&:=K_{\mathbb{P}}L_{V}\frac{\bar{\rho}n(1+2\sigma_{h})+L_{h}(1+4\sigma_{o}^{2})}{2\bar{\rho}n}+d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bar{L}_{h}\sigma_{h}^{2}+\bar{L}_{h}\frac{4\sigma_{o}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}}+K_{\mathbb{P}}\hat{a}\big),\\
{\rm C}_{2}^{\sf mk}&:=d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bar{L}_{h}\big(2+\frac{L_{h}}{2n}+\frac{4\sigma_{o}^{2}}{\bar{\rho}}\big)+K_{\mathbb{P}}\frac{d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}+K_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\hat{a}a^{2}d_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+L_{V}\frac{\bar{\rho}n+4L_{h}\sigma_{o}^{2}}{2\bar{\rho}n}\Big).\end{split}

(35)

Combining the terms yields

\begin{split}&\textstyle\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}\langle{\nabla}V(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\,|\,{\bm{e}}_{0}^{(t)}\rangle\big|\big]\leq{\rm C}_{0}^{\sf mk}+{\rm C}_{1}^{\sf mk}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}+{\rm C}_{2}^{\sf mk}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma_{t+1}^{2}\|\overline{h}(\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}_{c}^{(t)})\|^{2}.\end{split}

This is the desired result for the lemma.

Appendix D
Auxiliary Lemma

Lemma 4
.

Let
\{a_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
be an arbitrary sequence of non-negative number and
\rho\in(0,1)
be a constant. For any
T\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}(1-\rho)^{t-s}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\sum_{t=0}^{T}a_{t}^{2}.

(36)

Proof.

We begin by expanding the summation on the l.h.s. of (
36
) and observing the following upper bound:

\begin{split}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\sum_{q=0}^{t}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{2t-q-s}&\leq\sum_{s=0}^{T}\sum_{q=0}^{s}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{-q-s}\sum_{t=s}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\\
&\quad+\sum_{q=0}^{T}\sum_{s=0}^{q}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{-q-s}\sum_{t=q}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\end{split}

(37)

As
\sum_{t=s}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\leq\frac{(1-\rho)^{2s}}{\rho}
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{s=0}^{T}\sum_{q=0}^{s}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{-q-s}\sum_{t=s}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\sum_{s=0}^{T}\sum_{q=0}^{s}\big\{a_{s}^{2}+a_{q}^{2}\big\}(1-\rho)^{s-q}\end{split}

(38)

Observe that

\sum_{s=0}^{T}a_{s}^{2}\sum_{q=0}^{s}(1-\rho)^{s-q}=\sum_{s=0}^{T}a_{s}^{2}\sum_{q=0}^{s}(1-\rho)^{q}\leq\sum_{s=0}^{T}\frac{a_{s}^{2}}{\rho}

\sum_{s=0}^{T}\sum_{q=0}^{s}a_{q}^{2}(1-\rho)^{s-q}=\sum_{q=0}^{T}a_{q}^{2}\sum_{s=q}^{T}(1-\rho)^{s-q}\leq\sum_{s=0}^{T}\frac{a_{s}^{2}}{\rho}

This shows

\begin{split}&\sum_{s=0}^{T}\sum_{q=0}^{s}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{-q-s}\sum_{t=s}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\leq\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\sum_{s=0}^{T}a_{s}^{2}\end{split}

By symmetry, we have
\sum_{q=0}^{T}\sum_{s=0}^{q}a_{s}a_{q}(1-\rho)^{-q-s}\sum_{t=q}^{T}(1-\rho)^{2t}\leq\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\sum_{s=0}^{T}a_{s}^{2}
. Adding this two bounds yields the desired result in (
36
).
∎

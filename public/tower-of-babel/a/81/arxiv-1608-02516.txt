Higher order mean curvatures of SAC half-lightlike submanifolds of indefinite almost contact manifolds
Fortuné Massamba*, Samuel Ssekajja**

Abstract
. We introduce higher order mean curvatures of screen almost conformal (SAC) half-lightlike submanifolds of indefinite almost contact manifolds, admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection, and use them to generalize some known results of [ 6 ] . Also, we derive a new set of integration formulae via the divergence of some special vector fields tangent to this submanifold. Several examples are also included to illustrate the main concepts.

Primary 53C25; Secondary 53C40, 53C50

1.
Introduction

Null (or lightlike) subspaces exist naturally in semi-Riemannian spaces and they play a central role in general relativity. More precisely, in the study of black holes (small volumes of spacetime with infinite density). In fact, they are subspaces whose induced metrics are singular (or simply with vanishing determinants). Differential geometry of these subspaces was introduced by Duggal and Bejancu in their book
[
4
]
, which was later updated by Dugal and Sahin to
[
5
]
. Their approach was later adopted by many other researchers, including but not limited to;
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
14
]
. From the above pieces of work, we can see the theory of lightlike geometry rests on a number of operators, including, shape, Ricci, etc., together with functions constructed from them, like mean curvature, scalar curvature, etc. However, the most important of such functions are the ones derived from algebraic invariants of their respective operators. For instance, trace, determinant, and in a more general sense the
r^{th}
symmetric functions,
\sigma_{r}
. These functions play a central role in studying higher order mean curvatures in differential geometry of both Riemannian and semi-Riemannian manifolds. In fact, for any given point in a manifold, the
r^{th}
symmetric function
\sigma_{r}
coincides with the
r^{th}
mean curvature
S_{r}
. A great deal of work has been done for
r=1
(see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
and references therein). But the case
r>1
is strictly non-linear and complicated. The most efficient way of studying this case is the use of Newton transformations,
T_{r}
, of a given operator
A
(or a system of operators) which, in some way, linearizes
S_{r}
. That is to say
(-1)^{r-1}rS_{r}=\mathrm{tr}(A\circ T_{r-1})
.

Let
M^{m+1}
be a half lightlike submanifold of an indefinite contact manifold
\overline{M}^{m+3}
admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection. Then,
M
carries three shape operators
A_{E}^{*}
,
A_{N}
and
A_{W}
, where
E
,
N
and
W
are respectively vector fields in its radical distribution, lightlike transversal bundle and screen transversal bundle. When the structure vector field
\xi
is tangent to
M
but not necessarily in its screen distribution, then
A_{E}^{*}
is a self-adjoint operator on
TM
while
A_{N}
and
A_{W}
are generally not self-adjoint. If we suppose that
M
is a screen almost conformal (SAC)
[
12
]
half-lightlike submanifold , then the operator
A_{N}
becomes self-adjoint on
TM
and therefore diagonalizable on
TM
and hence we can investigate its higher order mean curvatures. With such mean curvatures, one can also investigate integration geometry on such submanifolds. Integration geometry is fundamentally important as it provide obstructions to the existence of foliations whose leaves enjoy some special geometric properties-totally geodesic (or totally umbilical), minimal, constant mean curvature and many more. Also, it provides a way of minimizing volume (of submanifolds) as well as energy defined from smooth vector fields on manifolds (see
[
15
]
and references therein).

In this paper, we consider SAC half lightlike submanifold
M
of an indefinite contact manifold
\overline{M}
, admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection. We derive equations relating the
r^{th}
mean curvatures and Newton transformations of
A_{N}
and
A_{E}^{*}
. We generalize some known results for
r=1
and also, derive new integration formulas by computing the divergence of some vector fields on in the tangent bundle of
M
. The rest of the paper is arranged as follows. Section
2
outlines the basic preliminary concepts needed in other parts of the paper. Section
3
introduces Newton transformations of
A_{E}^{*}
. In Section
4
we show that the
r^{th}
mean curvatures and Newton transformations of
A_{N}
and
A_{E}^{*}
are in partial variation (see Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
). Also, we derive generalized differential equations for
r^{th}
mean curvatures (Theorem
4.10
). In Section
5
we present special integration formulas by computing the divergence of some vector fields (see Theorem
5.7
and its corollaries).

Preliminaries

Let
\overline{M}
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold endowed with an almost contact structure
(\overline{\phi},\xi,\eta)

\overline{\phi}

(1,1)

\overline{\phi}^{2}=-\mathbb{I}+\eta\otimes\xi,\;\;\eta(\xi)=1,\;\;\eta\circ\overline{\phi}=0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\overline{\phi}(\xi)=0.

(2.1)

Then
(\overline{\phi},\xi,\eta,\,\overline{g})
is called an indefinite almost contact metric structure on
\overline{M}
if
(\overline{\phi},\xi,\eta)
is an almost contact structure on
\overline{M}
and
\overline{g}
is a semi-Riemannian metric on
\overline{M}
such that
[
3
]
, for any vector field
\overline{X}
,
\overline{Y}
on
\overline{M}
,

\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}\,\overline{X},\overline{\phi}\,\overline{Y})=\overline{g}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})-\eta(\overline{X})\,\eta(\overline{Y}),

(2.2)

It follows that, for any vector
\overline{X}
on
\overline{M}
,

\eta(\overline{X})=\overline{g}(\xi,\overline{X}).

We denote by
\Gamma(\Xi)
the set of smooth sections of the vector bundle
\Xi
.

A connection
\overline{\nabla}
on
\overline{M}
is called a
semi-symmetric non-metric connection

[
8
,
14
]
if

and its corresponding torsion tensor

satisfy the following equations;

\displaystyle(\overline{\nabla}_{\overline{X}}\overline{g})(\overline{Y},\overline{Z})

\displaystyle=-\eta(\overline{Y})\overline{g}(\overline{X},\overline{Z})-\eta(\overline{Z})\overline{g}(\overline{X},\overline{Y}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\;\;\overline{T}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})

\displaystyle=\eta(\overline{Y})\overline{X}-\eta(\overline{X})\overline{Y},

(2.4)

for all
\overline{X}
,
\overline{Y}
and
\overline{Z}
vector fields on
\overline{M}
.

Let
(\overline{M},\overline{g})

(m+n)

Take

The submanifold

of

is said to be

-lightlike submanifold

We say that

From (

Let

[

\overline{g}(N,N)=\overline{g}(N,Z)=\overline{g}(N,W)=0

Let

be complementary (but not orthogonal) vector bundle to

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(TM)

\displaystyle=l\mathrm{tr}(TM)\perp S(TM^{\perp}),

\displaystyle T\overline{M}

\displaystyle=S(TM)\perp S(TM^{\perp})\perp\{\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus l\mathrm{tr}(TM)\}.

Note that the distribution

[

Let

The Gauss and Weingarten formulas are given by

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y),\;\;\forall\,X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}V=-

\displaystyle A_{V}X+\nabla_{X}^{t}V,\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM),\;\;V\in\Gamma(\mathrm{tr}\,(TM)).

Notice that

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}N=-A_{N}X+\tau(X)N+\rho(X)W,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}W=-A_{W}X+\phi(X)N,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}PY=\nabla^{*}_{X}PY+C(X,PY)E,

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}E=-A^{*}_{E}X-\delta(X)E,

,

and

\displaystyle=B(X,Y)\lambda(Z)+B(X,Z)\lambda(Y)

(2.23)

\displaystyle\delta(X)=\tau(X)-b\lambda(X),\quad\forall\,X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

(2.24)

\displaystyle+\tau(X)B(Y,Z)-\tau(Y)B(X,Z)+\phi(X)D(Y,Z)-\phi(Y)D(X,Z)\}N

\displaystyle+\{(\nabla_{X}D)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}D)(X,Z)+\rho(X)B(Y,Z)-\rho(Y)B(X,Z)\}W,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\overline{R}(X,Y)N

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{X}(A_{N}Y)+\nabla_{Y}(A_{N}X)+A_{N}[X,Y]

\displaystyle+\tau(X)A_{N}Y-\tau(Y)A_{N}X+\rho(X)A_{W}Y

\displaystyle-\rho(Y)A_{W}X+\{B(X,A_{N}X)-B(X,A_{N}Y)

\displaystyle+\rho(X)\tau(Y)-\rho(Y)\tau(X)\}W,\;\;\;\forall\,X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

(2.26)

A half lightlike submanifold

of an indefinite almost contact manifold
\overline{M}
, with

where

\overline{M}
admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection if and only if

A_{N}X=\varphi A^{*}_{E}X-aX,\;\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(2.29)

Furthermore, if
M
is SAC half-lightlike submanifold of an almost contact manifold
\overline{M}
admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection then from (
2.29
), the operator
A_{N}
is self-adjoint on
TM
and thus diagonalizable on
TM
. Also, from (
2.29
) we have that any SAC half-lightlike submanifold of an almost contact manifold admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection, with the structure vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(S(TM))
is screen conformal.

3.
Newton transformations of
A_{E}^{*}

Let
\overline{M}
be an
(n+3)
-dimensional almost contact metric manifold admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection and
(M,g,S(TM))
be a codimension two SAC half-lightlike submanifold of
\overline{M}
. Since
A^{*}_{E}
is a self-adjoint operator, from (
2.20
) and (
2.29
) we can see that
A_{N}
is a self-adjoint linear operator on
TM
. Thus,
A_{E}^{*}
and
A_{N}
are diagonalizable. Hence,
A_{E}^{*}
has
(n+1)
real eigenvalues
\kappa_{0}^{*}=0,\kappa_{1}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{*}
(the principal curvatures) corresponding to a set of quasi-orthonormal frame field of eigenvector fields
\{Z_{0}=E,Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}\}
. By the SAC condition (
2.29
) it is easy to see that
-a,(\varphi\kappa_{1}^{*}-a),\cdots,(\varphi\kappa_{n}^{*}-a)
are eigenvalues of
A_{N}
. Moreover the matrix of
A_{N}
has the form

A_{N}=\mathrm{diag}(-a,\varphi\kappa_{1}^{*}-a,\cdots,\varphi\kappa_{n}^{*}-a).

Associated to the shape operator
A_{E}^{*}
are
(n+1)
algebraic invariants

S_{r}^{*}=\sigma_{r}(\kappa_{0}^{*},\kappa_{1}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{*}),

where
\sigma_{r}:M^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, for
r=0,1,\cdots,n+1
, are symmetric functions given by

\sigma_{r}(\kappa_{0}^{*},\kappa_{1}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{*})=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots\kappa_{i_{r}}^{*}.

(3.1)

Let denote by
\mathbb{I}
the identity map in
\Gamma(TM)
. Then, the characteristic polynomial of
A_{E}^{*}
is given by

Q(t)=\det(A_{E}^{*}-t\mathbb{I})=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{\alpha}S_{r}^{*}t^{n+1-\alpha}.

The normalized
r
-th mean curvature
H_{r}^{*}
of
M
is defined by

H_{r}^{*}=\dbinom{n+1}{r}^{-1}S_{r}^{*}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;H_{0}^{*}=1.\;\;\mbox{(a constant function 1)}.

In particular, when
r=1
, then
H_{1}^{*}=\frac{1}{n+1}\mathrm{tr}(A_{E}^{*})
which is called the
mean curvature
of the half-lightlike submanifold
M
. On the other hand,
H_{2}^{*}
relates directly with the (intrinsic) scalar curvature of
M
. Often times,
H_{r}^{*}
instead of
S_{r}^{*}
, is called the
r
-th mean curvature
[
1
,
2
]
. Moreover, the functions
S_{r}^{*}
(
H_{r}^{*}
respectively) are smooth on the whole
M
and, for any point
p\in M
,
S_{r}^{*}
coincides with the
r
-th mean curvature at
p
. Throughout this paper, we shall use
S_{r}^{*}
instead of
H_{r}^{*}
.

The Newton transformations
T_{r}^{*}:\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
, for
r=0,1,\cdots,n+1
, of a SAC half-lightlike submanifold
M
of an
(n+3)
-dimensional almost contact metric manifold
\overline{M}
with respect to
A_{N}
are given by

T_{r}^{*}=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{*}A^{*r-\alpha}_{E},

(3.2)

or equivalently by the inductive formula

T_{0}^{*}=\mathbb{I},\quad T_{r}^{*}=(-1)^{r}S_{r}^{*}\mathbb{I}+A_{E}^{*}\circ T_{r-1}^{*},\quad 1\leq r\leq n,

(3.3)

Notice that, by Cayley-Hamiliton theorem, we have
T_{n+1}^{*}=0
. Moreover,
T_{r}^{*}
are also self-adjoint and commutes with
A_{E}^{*}
.

It is important to note that the operators
T^{*}_{r}
depend on the choice of the transversal bundle
\mathrm{tr}(TM)
and the screen distribution
S(TM)
. Suppose a screen distribution
S(TM)
changes to another screen
S(TM)^{\prime}
. The following are some of the local transformation equations due to this change (see
[
4
, p. 87]
for more details):

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{i}^{j}\left(W_{j}-\epsilon_{j}c_{j}E\right),

(3.4)

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(X)

\displaystyle=N-\frac{1}{2}g(W,W)E+W,

(3.5)

\displaystyle A_{E}^{{}^{\prime}*}X

\displaystyle=A_{E}^{*}X+B(X,N-N^{\prime})E,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}Y+B(X,Y)\{\frac{1}{2}g(W,W)E-W\},

(3.7)

for any
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TM|_{\mathcal{U}})
, where
W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}W_{i}
,
\{W_{i}\}
and
\{W_{i}^{\prime}\}
are the local orthonormal bases of
S(TM)
and
S(TM)^{\prime}
with respective transversal sections
N
and
N^{\prime}
for the same null section
B
. Here
c_{i}
and
W_{i}^{j}
are smooth functions on
\mathcal{U}
and
\{\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{n}\}
is the signature of the basis
\{W_{1},\cdots,W_{n}\}
. Denote by
\omega
is the dual
1
-form of
W
, characteristic vector field of the screen change, with respect to the induced metric
g=\overline{g}|_{M}
, that is,

\omega(X)=g(X,W),\quad\forall\;X\in\Gamma(TM).

(3.8)

Consider an orthogonal basis
\{Z_{i}\}
, for
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, which diagonalizes
A_{E}^{{}^{\prime}*}
and
A_{E}^{*}
. Let
k_{i}^{\prime}
and
k_{i}
be the eigenvalues corresponding to eigenvector
Z_{i}
. Then, from (
3.6
) we have
(k_{i}^{\prime}-k_{i})Z_{i}=-B(Z_{i},W)E
, which shows that the eigenvalues changes under the change of the screen distribution. Since the generalized expansion
\Theta_{r}
depends on on the eigenvalues
k_{i}
, i.e.
\Theta_{r}=(-1)^{r}S_{r}^{*}=(-1)^{r}\sigma_{r}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})
, then a change of
N
will cause a change in it. Now, let
\{\Theta,T^{*}_{r}\}
and
\{\Theta^{\prime},T^{*^{\prime}}_{r}\}
be two sets of the above objects under a change in
N
. Applying recurrence relation (
3.3
) and the fact that
T_{r}Z_{i}=(-1)^{r}S_{r}^{*i}Z_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle T^{*^{\prime}}_{r}Z_{i}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{r}^{\prime}\mathbb{I}+(-1)^{r-1}S_{r-1}^{*i^{\prime}}A_{E}^{*^{\prime}}Z_{i},

(3.9)

\displaystyle T^{*}_{r}Z_{i}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{r}\mathbb{I}+(-1)^{r-1}S_{r-1}^{*i}A_{E}^{*}Z_{i}.

(3.10)

Subtracting the second relation in (
3.9
) from the first and using relation (
3.6
) with
X=Z_{i}
, we deduce that the operators
T^{*}_{r}
and
T^{*^{\prime}}_{r}
are related by the following equation.

\displaystyle T^{*^{\prime}}_{r}=T_{r}+(\Theta_{r}^{\prime}-\Theta_{r})\mathbb{I}+\theta_{r}A_{E}^{*}+B(T^{*^{\prime}}_{r-1},N-N^{\prime})E,

(3.11)

where
\theta_{r}:=(-1)^{r-1}(S_{r-1}^{*i^{\prime}}-S_{r-1}^{*i})
. It is easy to see that the tensor
T^{*}_{r}
is unique if and only if the null hypersurface
M
is totally geodesic. For more details on Newton transformations and their properties, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
and many more references therein.

Let
S^{*\beta}_{r}=\sigma_{r}(\kappa_{0}^{*},\kappa_{1}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{\beta-1}^{*},\kappa_{\beta+1}^{*},\cdots\kappa_{n}^{*})
for
1\leq\beta\leq n
. A half-lightlike submanifold
M
of an
(n+3)
-dimensional almost contact metric manifold
\overline{M}
is called
r
-umbilical
(resp.
r
-maximal
)
[
1
]
if for all
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we have

S^{*i}_{r}=S^{*j}_{r},\quad\mbox{(resp. $H_{r}^{*}=0$)},\quad 1\leq r\leq n.

(3.12)

Then, the following algebraic properties of
T_{r}^{*}
are well-known (see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
and references therein for details).

\displaystyle T_{r}^{*}Z_{\beta}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}S^{*\beta}_{r}Z_{\beta},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(T_{r}^{*})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}(n+1-r)S_{r}^{*},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(A_{E}^{*}\circ T_{r-1}^{*})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r-1}rS_{r}^{*},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(A_{E}^{*2}\circ T_{r-1}^{*})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}(-S_{1}^{*}S_{r}^{*}+(r+1)S_{r+1}^{*}),

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(T_{r-1}^{*}\circ\nabla_{X}A_{E}^{*})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r-1}X(S_{r}^{*}),\quad\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(3.17)

Next, we define the divergence of the operator
T_{r}^{*}:\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(TM)
as the vector field
\mathrm{div}^{\nabla}(T_{r}^{*})\in\Gamma(TM)
and given by

\mathrm{div}^{\nabla}(T_{r}^{*})=\mathrm{tr}(\nabla T_{r}^{*})=\sum_{\beta=0}^{n}(\nabla_{Z_{\beta}}T_{r}^{*})Z_{\beta}.

(3.18)

In line with (
3.12
), we can see that the SAC half-lightlike submanifold given in Example
2.1
is
r
-minimal with
1\leq r\leq 6
.

4.
Fundamental SAC equations of
A_{N}

In this section, we derive SAC equations of
A_{N}
from those of
A_{E}^{*}
. We use some of them to generalize some known results of
[
6
]
.
Let
TM=\mathrm{span}\{Z_{0}=E,Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}\}
and
S(TM)=\mathrm{span}\{Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a SAC half-lightlike submanifold of an almost contact manifold
\overline{M}
admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection. Let
S_{1}
and
S_{1}^{*}
be the
1^{st}
order mean curvatures corresponding to the two shape operators
A_{N}
and
A_{E}^{*}
respectively. Then,

S_{1}=\varphi S_{1}^{*}-an.

(4.1)

Proof.

From
H_{1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\mathrm{tr}(A_{N})
and
H_{1}=\dbinom{n+1}{1}^{-1}S_{1}
, we have
S_{1}=\mathrm{tr}(A_{N})
. From the last equation and the fact that
M
is SAC half-lightlike submanifold, we have
S_{1}=\mathrm{tr}(A_{N})|_{S(TM)}=\varphi\mathrm{tr}(A_{E}^{*})-an
which completes the proof.
∎

In the next proposition, we generalize Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M
be a SAC half-lightlike submanifold of an almost contact manifold
\overline{M}
admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection. Let
S_{r}
and
S_{r}^{*}
be the
r
-th mean curvatures corresponding to the two shape operators
A_{N}
and
A_{E}^{*}
respectively. Then, for all
r\geq 1
we have

S_{r}=\varphi^{r}S_{r}^{*}+J_{r}^{*}(a,\varphi),

(4.2)

where for a given
A_{E}^{*}
,
J_{r}^{*}
are smooth functions in
a
and
\varphi
given by

\displaystyle J_{r}^{*}(a,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}(-1)^{r}a^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}(-1)^{r+j}e_{j}(\kappa_{i1}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{ir}^{*})a^{r-j}\varphi^{j}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{0}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{n}^{*}
be the eigenvalues (principal curvatures) of
A_{E}^{*}
and
consider a linear factorization of a
k^{th}
-degree monic polynomial in
t
below;

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(t-X_{i})=\sum_{s=0}^{k}(-1)^{s}e_{s}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})t^{k-s},

(4.4)

where

denotes the
s^{th}
-degree symmetric function in variables
X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}
. Thus, if
S_{r}
is the
r^{th}
mean curvatures of
A_{N}
, then we have from the definition of
S_{r}
and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle S_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{r}}=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}(\varphi\kappa_{i_{j}}^{*}-a)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{r}\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}e_{r}(\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{i_{r}}^{*})+\sum_{0<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}(-1)^{r}a^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}(-1)^{r}\{-e_{1}(\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{ir}^{*})a^{r-1}\varphi+\cdots

\displaystyle+(-1)^{r-1}e_{r-1}(\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{i_{r}}^{*})a\varphi^{r-1}\}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{r}S_{r}^{*}+\sum_{0<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}(-1)^{r}a^{r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}(-1)^{r+j}e_{j}(\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*},\cdots,\kappa_{i_{r}}^{*})a^{r-j}\varphi^{j},

which proves (
4.2
) and (
4.2
), hence the proof.
∎

Notice that
J_{r}^{*}(0,\varphi)=0
, which is the case when the structure vector field
\xi
belongs to
S(TM)
. From (
4.2
), we have
S_{1}=\varphi S_{1}^{*}+J_{1}^{*}(a,\varphi)
and from (
4.2
), we can see that
J_{1}^{*}(a,\varphi)=-an
. Thus,
S_{1}=\varphi S_{1}^{*}-an
, which is Proposition
4.1
.

In what follows, we construct a SAC half-lightlike submanifold in which
\xi\in\Gamma(S(TM))
of an indefinite almost contact manifold
\overline{M}=\mathbb{R}_{2}^{9}
, admitting a semi-symmetric non-metric connection. Notice from (

and therefore the corresponding SAC half-lightlike submanifold is actually screen conformal.

Theorem 4.4

Let

\overline{M}

be the
r

T_{r}=\varphi^{r}T_{r}^{*}+\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}(a,\varphi),

(4.5)

where
\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{r}^{*}(a,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}\{J_{\alpha}^{*}(a,\varphi)\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}-a\mathbb{I}\right)^{r-\alpha}

\displaystyle+\varphi^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{*}\sum_{k=1}^{r-\alpha}(-1)^{k}\dbinom{r-\alpha}{k}\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}\right)^{r-\alpha-k}(a\mathbb{I})^{k}\}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\dbinom{r}{j}(\varphi A_{E}^{*})^{r-j}(a\mathbb{I})^{j}.

(4.6)

Proof.

Using the fact that
M
is SAC half-lightlike submanifold, the definition of
T_{r}
, (

) and Proposition

\displaystyle T_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}A^{r-\alpha}_{N}=A_{N}^{r}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}A^{r-\alpha}_{N}

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}-a\mathbb{I}\right)^{r}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}\left(\varphi^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{*}+J_{\alpha}^{*}(a,\varphi)\right)\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}-a\mathbb{I}\right)^{r-\alpha}.

Applying the binomial theorem, the above equation leads to

\displaystyle T_{r}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{r}A_{E}^{*r}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}\dbinom{r}{j}(\varphi A_{E}^{*})^{r-j}(a\mathbb{I})^{j}+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}\{\varphi^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{*}+J_{\alpha}^{*}(a,\varphi)\}

\displaystyle\times\{\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}\right)^{r-\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-\alpha}(-1)^{k}\dbinom{r-\alpha}{k}\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}\right)^{r-\alpha-k}(a\mathbb{I})^{k}\}.

(4.7)

Expanding the two brackets in (
4

\displaystyle T_{r}=\varphi^{r}A_{E}^{*r}+\varphi^{r}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{*}A_{E}^{*r-\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}(-1)^{\alpha}\{J_{\alpha}^{*}(a,\varphi)\left(\varphi A_{E}^{*}-a\mathbb{I}\right)^{r-\alpha}

Representation theory of graded algebras given by locally finite quivers
Zetao Lin, Shiping Liu

Abstract
. This paper aims to study graded modules over a graded algebra Λ \it\Lambda given by a locally finite quiver with homogeneous relations. By constructing a graded Nakayama functor, we discover a novel approach to establish Auslander-Reiten formulas, from which we derive almost split sequences in the category of all graded Λ \it\Lambda -modules. In case Λ \it\Lambda is locally left (respectively, right) bounded, the category of finitely presented graded modules and that of finitely copresented graded modules both have almost split sequences on the left (respectively, right). We shall also obtain existence theorems for almost split triangles in various derived categories of graded Λ \it\Lambda -modules. In case Λ \it\Lambda is locally bounded, an indecomposable complex in the bounded derived category of finite dimensional graded modules is the starting (respectively, ending) term of an almost split triangle if and only if it has a finite graded projective resolution (respectively, injective coresolution); and consequently, this bounded derived category has almost split triangles on the right (respectively, left) if and only if every graded simple module is of finite graded projective (respectively, injective) dimension. Finally, we specialize to the existence of almost split sequences and almost split triangles for graded representations of any locally finite quiver.

Introduction

Graded algebras play an essential role in many domains such as commutative algebra, Lie theory, algebraic geometry and algebraic topology; see, for example,

The representation theory of locally finite dimensional graded algebras have been studied thoroughly by numerous researchers; see, for example,
[

,

,

Motivated by the application of the covering technique; see
[

,
17

Almost split sequences in abelian categories, introduced by Auslander and Reiten; see
[

In the classical setting, the existence of almost split sequences for graded modules was studied first by Gordon and Green; see
[

[

[

In the locally finite dimensional case, Martinez-Villa established an Auslander-Reiten formula by the classical approach of tensor product and adjunction isomorphism, and he obtained an existence theorem for almost split sequences ending with finitely presented graded modules in the category of locally finite dimensional graded modules. In this paper, we shall provide a novel approach to establish an Auslander-Reiten formula for finitely presented graded modules and a generalized Auslander-Reiten formula for finitely copresented graded modules, which enable us to obtain an existence theorem in the category of all graded modules for almost split sequences ending with finitely presented graded modules or starting with finitely copresented graded modules.

Determining which categories have almost split sequences or almost split triangles has long been a key research topic; see, for example,
[
15
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
35
]

[
7
,
16
]
. Our results yield many interesting abelian categories of graded modules having almost split sequences on one or two sides, and derived categories of graded modules having almost split triangles on one or two sides. The content of the paper is outlined section by section as follows.

In Section 1, we shall lay down the foundation of the paper. In Section 2, we study some generalities about the category
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

\it\Lambda

{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has enough projective objects and enough injective objects; see (

In Section 3, we study the existence of almost split sequences for graded

Preliminaries

The objective of this section is to lay down the foundation of this paper. We shall fix some terminology and notation which will be used throughout this paper and collect some preliminary results.

1.1.
Linear algebra.

k

Given

In case

(2)

There exists a natural

,

and

1.2.
Quivers

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is the set of arrows between vertices. Given an arrow
\alpha:x\to y
in
Q_{1}
, we call
x
the
starting point
and
y
the
end point
of
\alpha
; and write
s(\alpha)=x
and
e(\alpha)=y
. For each vertex
x\in Q_{0}
, one associates a
trivial path

\varepsilon_{x}
with
s(\varepsilon_{x})=e(\varepsilon_{x})=x
. A
path
of positive length
n
is a sequence
\rho=\alpha_{n}\cdots\alpha_{1}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
such that
s(\alpha_{i+1})=e(\alpha_{i})
, for all
1\leq i<n
. One says that
Q
is
locally finite
provided, for any
x\in Q_{0}
, that the set
Q_{1}(x,-)
of arrows
\alpha
with
s(\alpha)=x
and the set
Q_{1}(-,x)
of arrows
\beta
with
e(\beta)=x
are both finite. Moreover,
Q
is
strongly locally finite
provided, for any
x,y\in Q_{0}
, that
the set
Q(x,y)
of paths from
x
to
y
is finite.

The
opposite
quiver
Q^{\rm o}
of
Q
is defined in such a way that
(Q^{\rm o})_{0}=Q_{0}
and
(Q^{\rm o})_{1}=\{\alpha^{\rm o}:y\to x\mid\alpha:x\to y\in Q_{1}\}
. A non-trivial path
\rho=\alpha_{n}\cdots\alpha_{1}
in
Q(x,y)
, where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
, corresponds to a non-trivial path
\rho^{\rm o}=\alpha_{1}^{\rm o}\cdots\alpha_{n}^{\rm o}
in
Q^{\rm o}(y,x)
. For convenience, the trivial path in
Q^{\rm o}
at a vertex
x
will be identified with the trivial path in
Q
at
x
.

1.3.
Algebras given by quivers with relations

In this paper, a
k
-algebra does not necessarily have an identity.
Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a locally finite quiver. We shall write
kQ
for the path algebra of
Q
over
k
and
kQ^{+}
for the two-sided ideal in
kQ
generated by
Q_{1}
. A two-sided ideal in
kQ
is called a
relation ideal
if it is contained in
(kQ^{+})^{2}
. An element in
kQ
is called
homogeneous
if it is a linear combination of paths of the same length, and a relation ideal in
kQ
is called
homogeneous
if it is generated by some homogeneous elements.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
, where
R
is a relation ideal of
kQ
. We fix some notation for
\it\Lambda
, which will be used throughout this paper. Write
\bar{\hskip-1.0pt\gamma}=\gamma+R\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
for
\gamma\in kQ
, and
e_{x}=\bar{\varepsilon}_{x}
for
x\in Q_{0}
. Then,

\{e_{x}\hskip-1.5pt\mid\hskip-1.5ptx\in Q_{0}\}
is a complete set of pairwise orthogonal idempotents in
\it\Lambda
. Note that the opposite algebra of
\it\Lambda
is
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}=kQ^{\rm o}/R^{\rm o},
where
R^{\rm o}=\{\rho^{\rm o}\mid\rho\in R\}
. We shall write
\bar{\gamma\hskip 1.8pt}^{\hskip-1.0pt\rm o}=\gamma^{\rm o}+R^{\rm o}
for
\gamma\in kQ
, but
e_{x}=\varepsilon_{x}+R^{\rm o}
for
x\in Q_{0}
. In this way, we have an algebra anti-isomorphism
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}:\bar{\gamma\hskip 1.5pt}\to\bar{\gamma\hskip 1.5pt}^{\hskip-1.0pt\rm o}
.

Noetherian algebras play an important role in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. In our context, noetherianness will be replaced by local noetherianness as follows:
\it\Lambda
is called
locally left noetherian
if the left
\it\Lambda
-modules
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
with
x\in Q_{0}
are noetherian;
locally right noetherian
if the right
\it\Lambda
-modules
e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
x\in Q_{0}
are noetherian. As examples, recall that
\it\Lambda
is called
special multi-serial
provided, for any
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, that there exists at most one
\beta\in Q_{1}
such that
\beta\alpha\notin R
and at most one
\gamma\in Q_{1}
such that
\alpha\gamma\notin R
. In this case, for any
x\in Q_{0}
, both
\sum_{\alpha\in Q_{1}(x,-)}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\alpha}
and

are finite sums of uniserial modules; see

23
,
18
]
. Then, it follows that
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
and
e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

Finally, we shall say that
\it\Lambda
is
locally left bounded
if the
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
with
x\in Q_{0}
are finite dimensional,
locally right bounded
if the

1.4.
Additive categories

Throughout this paper, all categories are additive
k
-categories in which morphisms are composed from the right to the left. All functors between additive
k
-categories are additive. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive
k
-category. A full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
is called
strictly full
if it is closed under isomorphisms. An object in
\mathcal{A}
is called
strongly indecomposable
if it has a local endomorphism algebra. One says that
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}

[
32

.

A morphism

provided that every morphism

[
26
]
and its dual, we obtain the following well-known statement.

Proposition 1.4.1.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Krull-Schmidt
k
-category.

(1)

A nonzero morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{A}
is left minimal if and only if
pf\neq 0
, for any nonzero retraction
p:Y\to N.

(2)

A nonzero morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{A}
is right minimal if and only if
fq\neq 0
, for any nonzero section
q:M\to X.

Finally, a morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{A}
is called
left almost split
if
f
is not a section and every non-section morphism
g:X\to Z
factors through
f
; and
minimal left almost split
if it is left minimal and left almost split. Dually, one defines
right almost split morphisms
and
minimal right almost split morphisms
in
\mathcal{A}
; see
[
5
]
.

1.7.
Derived categories

Clearly,

K(\mathfrak{A}).
The inclusion functors
q:K^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to K(\mathfrak{A});

i:K^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to K^{b}(\mathfrak{A})
and
j:K^{b}(\mathfrak{A})\to D(\mathfrak{A})
induce triangle-exact functors
q^{D}:D^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to D(\mathfrak{A});

i^{D}:D^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to D^{b}(\mathfrak{A})
and
j^{D}:D^{b}(\mathfrak{A})\to D(\mathfrak{A})
such that
q^{D}=j^{D}\circ i^{D}
. It is well-known that
j^{D}
is fully faithful; see
[
37
, (III.3.4.5)]
. If
\mathcal{A}
has enough
\mathfrak{A}
-projective objects, then
q^{D}
is fully faithful; see
[
9
, (1.11)]
, and so is
i^{D}
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

2.
Categories of graded modules

The objective of this section is to study generalities concerning graded modules over graded algebras given by locally finite quivers with homogeneous relations. The results obtained in this section will be needed not only in the following sections of this paper but also in future study of Koszul algebras given by locally finite quivers.

Throughout this section let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
, where
Q
is a locally finite quiver and
R
is a homogeneous relation ideal of
kQ
. Then
\it\Lambda
is a positively graded
k
-algebra with grading
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}
, where
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}=\{\bar{\gamma\hskip 1.6pt}\mid\gamma\in kQ_{i}\}
. For convenience, set
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}=0
for
i<0
. Write
J=\oplus_{i\geq 1}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}
, which is a graded two-sided ideal of
\it\Lambda
.
The opposite algebra
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
is also positively graded as
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}^{\rm o}
, where
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}^{\rm o}=\{\bar{\gamma\hskip 1.8pt}^{\hskip-1.0pt\rm o}\mid\gamma\in kQ_{i}\}
, for all
i\geq 0
. Note that this grading for the opposite algebra is different from the classical one; see
[
38
, (1.2.4)]
.

2.1.
Graded modules.

A left
\it\Lambda
-module
M
is
unitary
if

M=\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}e_{x}M
and
graded
if
M=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
, where the
M_{i}
are
k
-spaces such that
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}M_{i}\subseteq M_{i+j}
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
M
be a graded unitary left
\it\Lambda
-module. Then
M=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z};x\in Q_{0}}\,M_{i}(x)
as a
k
-space, where
M_{i}(x)=e_{x}M_{i},
called the
(i,x)
-
piece
of
M
. Given
u\in e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{x}
, we shall write
M(u):M_{i}(x)\to M_{i+j}(y)
for the
k
-linear map given by the left multiplication by
u
. An element
m\in M
is called
homogeneous
of degree
i
if
m\in M_{i}
and
pure
if
m\in M_{i}(x)
for some
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. A
\it\Lambda
-submodule
N
of
M
is called
graded
if
N=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(M_{i}\cap N).
In this case,
N
is graded as
N=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{i},
where
N_{i}=M_{i}\cap N
, such that if
m=\sum_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}m_{i,x}\in N

with
m_{i,x}\in M_{i}(x)
, then
m_{i,x}\in N
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}.

Let
M,N
be graded unitary left
\it\Lambda
-modules. A
\it\Lambda
-linear morphism
f:M\to N
is
graded
if
f(M_{i})\subseteq N_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. In this case,
f
restricts to
k
-linear maps
f_{i}:M_{i}\to N_{i}
and
f_{i,x}:M_{i}(x)\to N_{i}(x)
such that
f=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i}=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}f_{i,x}
.
Conversely, given
k
-linear maps
f_{i,x}:M_{i}(x)\to N_{i}(x)
with
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
, the
k
-linear map
f=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}f_{i,x}:M\to N
is graded
\it\Lambda
-linear if and only if
uf_{i,x}(m)=f_{i+j,y}(um)
, for
m\in M_{i}(x)
and
u\in e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{x}
with
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in Q_{0}
.

The graded unitary left
\it\Lambda
-modules together with the graded
\it\Lambda
-linear morphisms form an
abelian
k
-category, which will be written as
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. The morphism spaces and the extension groups in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
will be written respectively as
{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M,N)
and
{\rm GExt}^{i}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M,N)
. Moreover, we put
{\rm GEnd}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M)={\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M,M).
The following statement is evident; compare
[
38
, page 20]
.

Lemma 2.1.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
\{M_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}
is a family of modules in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, then

(1)

there exists a direct sum
M=\oplus_{\sigma\in\it\Sigma}M_{\sigma},
defined by
M_{i}(x)=\oplus_{\sigma\in\it\Sigma}(M_{\sigma})_{i}(x)
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
.

(2)

there exists a product
N=\Pi_{\sigma\in\it\Sigma}M_{\sigma},

defined by

N_{i}(x)=\Pi_{\sigma\in\it\Sigma}(M_{\sigma})_{i}(x),
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
.

Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
M=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z};x\in Q_{0}}M_{i}(x)
. One says that
M
is
bounded above
if
M_{i}=0
for
i\gg 0,

bounded below
if
M_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
and
bounded
if
M_{i}=0
for all but finitely many
i\in\mathbb{Z}.
The full subcategories of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
of bounded below modules and of bounded above modules will be written as

{\rm GMod}^{+}\hskip-3.6pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm GMod}^{-}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, respectively. Moreover,

M
is called
locally finite dimensional
if
M_{i}
is finite dimensional for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
piecewise finite dimensional
if
M_{i}(x)
is finite dimensional for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}.
We shall denote by
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

the full subcategory of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
of piecewise finite dimensional modules.

Let
V\in{\rm Mod}k
. Setting
(M\otimes V)_{i}=M_{i}\otimes V
, we obtain
a graded module
M\otimes V=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(M\otimes V)_{i}\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.
Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
grading

s
-
shift

M\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts\hskip-0.3pt\rangle
of
M
is defined by
M\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts\hskip-0.3pt\rangle_{i}=M_{i+s}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For a morphism
f:M\to N
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, the
grading

s
-
shift

f\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts\hskip-0.5pt\rangle:M\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts\hskip-0.5pt\rangle\to N\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts\hskip-0.5pt\rangle
of
f
is defined by
f\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts\hskip-0.5pt\rangle_{i}=f_{i+s},
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It is clear that
(M\otimes V)\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts\hskip-0.3pt\rangle=M\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\otimes V,
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
V\in\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}k
.

2.2.
The duality
\mathfrak{D}

In the classical graded setting, there exists a duality for locally finite dimensional graded modules given by applying componentwise the functor
D
; see
[
36
, page 70]
. In our setting, we shall apply the functor
D
piecewise in order to obtain a duality for piecewise finite dimensional graded modules.

Given
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, we define
\mathfrak{D}M=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}\,D(M_{-i}(x))\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
, whose left
\it\Lambda
-multiplication is such, for
\varphi\in D(M_{-i}(x))
and
u\in e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{y}
, that
u^{\rm o}\cdot\varphi=\varphi\circ M(u),
that is,
(u^{\rm o}\hskip-0.5pt\cdot\hskip-0.5pt\varphi)(m)=\varphi(um),
for
m\in M_{-i-j}(y).

In particular,
(\mathfrak{D}M)_{i}=\oplus_{x\in Q_{0}}D(M_{-i}(x))
and
(\mathfrak{D}M)_{i}(x)=D(M_{-i}(x))
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in Q_{0}
. Given a morphism
f:M\to N
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, we define a morphism
\mathfrak{D}f:\mathfrak{D}N\to\mathfrak{D}M
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
by setting
(\mathfrak{D}f)_{i,x}=D(f_{-i,x}),
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. This clearly yields a contravariant functor
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}.

Lemma 2.2.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Consider
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
V\in{\rm Mod}k
.

(1)

Given
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\mathfrak{D}(M\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts\hskip-0.5pt\rangle)=(\mathfrak{D}M)\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle
.

(2)

There exists a natural monomorphism
\rho:M\to\mathfrak{D}^{2}\!M
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
,
which is an isomorphism in case
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.

(3)

There exists a binatural morphism

\theta:\mathfrak{D}M\otimes DV\to\mathfrak{D}(M\otimes V)
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
,
which is an isomorphism in case
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
or
V\in{\rm mod}k
.

Proof.
Statement (1) is evident. For
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
, we have a canonical
k
-linear monomorphim
\rho_{i,x}:M_{i}(x)\to D^{2}(M_{i}(x))=(\mathfrak{D}^{2}M)_{i}(x)
.
Given
m\in M_{i}(x)
,
u\in e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{x}
and
f\in D(M_{i+j}(y))
, we have

\rho_{i+j,y}(um)(f)=f(um)=(u^{\rm o}\cdot f)(m)=\rho_{i,x}(m)(u^{\rm o}\cdot f)=(u\cdot\rho_{i,x}(m))(f).\vskip-3.0pt

That is,
\rho_{i+j,y}(um)=u\,\rho_{i,x}(m)
. Thus,
\rho=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}\rho_{i,x}:M\to\mathfrak{D}^{2}\hskip-1.0ptM
is a monomorphism in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, which is clearly natural in
M
. If
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, then
M_{i}(x)\in{\rm mod}k
, and hence,
\rho_{i,x}
is a
k
-linear isomorphism, for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}.
That is,
\rho
is an isomorphism. This establishes Statement (2).

Next, given
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0},
we have
(\mathfrak{D}M\otimes DV)_{i}(x)=D(M_{-i}(x))\otimes DV
and
(\mathfrak{D}(M\otimes V))_{i}(x)=D(M_{-i}(x)\otimes V).
Let
\theta_{i,x}:D(M_{-i}(x))\otimes DV\to D(M_{-i}(x)\otimes V)
be the
k
-linear map as defined in
Corollary
1.1.2
(2).
Given
g\in D((M_{-i}(x))
,
f\in DV
and
u\in e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{y}
, it is easy to verify that

(u^{\rm o}\cdot\theta_{i,x}(g\otimes f))(m\otimes v)=\theta_{i+j,y}(u^{\rm o}\,(g\otimes f))(m\otimes v),\mbox{ for }m\in M_{-i-j}(y),v\in V.\vskip-2.0pt

That is,
u^{\rm o}\cdot\theta_{i,x}(g\otimes f)=\theta_{i+j,y}(u^{\rm o}\,(g\otimes f))
. And consequently, we have
a morphism
\theta=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}\theta_{i,x}:\mathfrak{D}M\otimes DV\to\mathfrak{D}(M\otimes V)
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
. It is a routine verification that
\theta
is natural in
M
and
V
. Finally, if
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
or
V\in{\rm mod}k
then, by Corollary
1.1.2
(2),
\theta_{i,x}
is a
k
-linear isomorphism for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}.
That is,
\theta
is an isomorphism in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

As a consequence of Lemma
2.2.1
(2), we obtain our promised duality as follows.

Proposition 2.2.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. The contravariant functor

\mathfrak{D}:{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\hskip-0.3pt\rm o}\hskip-2.0pt
is exact and restricts to a duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\hskip-0.3pt\rm o}\hskip-2.0pt
.

The following statement says that
\mathfrak{D}
converts direct sums into direct products.

Proposition 2.2.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Given
M_{\sigma}\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have
\mathfrak{D}(\oplus_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}M_{\sigma})\cong\Pi_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}\hskip 0.6pt\mathfrak{D}(M_{\sigma}).

Proof.
Let
M_{\sigma}\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Write
M=\oplus_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}M_{\sigma}
and
N=\Pi_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}\mathfrak{D}(M_{\sigma}).

Fix
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. Then
M_{i}(x)\hskip-0.5pt=\hskip-0.5pt\oplus_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}(M_{\sigma})_{i}(x)
and
N_{i}(x)\hskip-0.5pt=\hskip-0.5pt\Pi_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}D(\hskip-0.5pt(M_{\sigma})_{-i}(x)\hskip-0.5pt)
. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, denote by
q_{\hskip 0.4pt\sigma}:(M_{\sigma})_{i}(x)\to M_{i}(x)
the canonical injection. Then, we have a canonical
k
-linear isomorphism

\Phi_{i,x}:(\mathfrak{D}M)_{i}(x)=D(\oplus_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}(M_{\sigma})_{-i}(x))\to\Pi_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}D((M_{\sigma})_{-i}(x))=N_{i}(x)\vskip-2.0pt

such that
\Phi_{i,x}(f)=(f\circ q_{\hskip 0.4pt\sigma})_{\sigma\in\mathit{\Sigma}}
for all
f\in(\mathfrak{D}M)_{i}(x)
. Given
f\in(\mathfrak{D}M)_{i}(x)
and
u\hskip-0.5pt\in\hskip-0.5pte_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{y}
, it is easy to verify that
u^{\rm o}\cdot\Phi_{i,x}(f)=\Phi_{i+j,y}(u^{\rm o}\cdot f)
. This yields an isomorphism
\Phi=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}\Phi_{i,x}:\mathfrak{D}M\to N
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
The proof of the proposition is completed.

2.3.
Graded projective modules.

A projective object in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called
graded projective.
For each
a\in Q_{0}
, we put
P_{a}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{a}=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{i}e_{a}
. Since
Q
is a locally finite,
P_{a}
is locally finite dimensional with
(P_{a})_{i}=0
for all
i<0
. To describe the graded morphisms starting from these modules,
we fix some notation. Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.
Given a pure element
m\in M_{s}(a)
with
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in Q_{0}
, the right multiplication by
m
yields a graded
\it\Lambda
-linear morphism
M[m]:P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\to M.
By definition,
M[m](e_{a})=e_{a}m=m.

Proposition 2.3.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Consider
P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle
with
(s,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
and
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Then we have a natural
k
-linear isomorphism

\eta:{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle,M)\to M_{s}(a):f\mapsto f(e_{a}),

whose inverse is given by
\varphi:M_{s}(a)\to{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle,M):m\mapsto M[m].

Proof.
Let
f\in{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle,M).
Observing that
e_{a}\in P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle_{s}(a)
, we see that
m=f(e_{a})\in M_{s}(a)
such that
f=M[m]
. It is easy to verify that
\eta^{-1}=\varphi
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

The following statement is an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.3.1
.

Corollary 2.3.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Then,
P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\otimes V
is a graded projective module, for
(s,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
and
V\in{\rm Mod}\hskip 0.4ptk
.

In the sequel, we shall denote by
{\rm GProj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
the strictly full additive subcategory of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
generated by the
P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\otimes V
with
(s,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
and
V\in{\rm Mod}\hskip 0.4ptk
, and by
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
the one generated by the
P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.8pt\rangle
with
(s,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. We shall describe the morphsims in
{\rm GProj}\,\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; compare
[
9
, (7.6)]
. Given
u\in e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{s-t}e_{b}=P_{b}\hskip 0.3pt\hskip-0.4pt\langle\hskip 0.4pt-t\hskip 0.0pt\rangle_{s}
, in order to simplify the notation, we shall write the right multiplication by
u
as

P[u]:P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\to P_{b}\hskip 0.3pt\hskip-0.4pt\langle\hskip 0.4pt-t\hskip 0.0pt\rangle:v\mapsto vu.\vskip-2.0pt

Note that this notation does not distinguish
P[u]
from its grading shifts.

Proposition 2.3.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Consider
P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\otimes V
and
P_{b}\hskip 0.3pt\hskip-0.4pt\langle\hskip 0.4pt-t\hskip 0.0pt\rangle\otimes W
with
(s,a),(t,b)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
and
V,W\in\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}k
. We have a
k
-linear isomorphism

\varphi:e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{s-t}e_{b}\otimes\hskip-0.5pt{\rm Hom}_{k}(V,W)\hskip-0.5pt\to\hskip-0.5pt{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\hskip-0.5pt\otimes V,P_{b}\hskip 0.3pt\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-t\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\otimes W)\hskip-0.5pt:\hskip-0.5ptu\otimes f\hskip-0.5pt\mapsto\hskip-0.5ptP[u]\otimes f.\vskip-2.0pt

Proof.
Clearly, we have a
k
-linear map
\varphi
as stated in the proposition. Choose a
k
-basis
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of
e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{s-t}e_{b}
. Consider
\omega\in{\rm Ker}(\varphi)
. Then,
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes f_{i}
, where
f_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{k}(V,M)
. Given
v\in V
, we have
\varphi(\omega)(e_{a}\otimes v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes f_{i}(v)=0
, and hence,
f_{i}(v)=0
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Hence,
\omega=0.
So,
\varphi
is a monomorphism.

On the other hand, let
f\in{\rm GHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\hskip-0.5pt\otimes\hskip-0.5ptV,P_{b}\hskip 0.3pt\hskip-0.4pt\langle\hskip 0.4pt-t\hskip 0.0pt\rangle\hskip-0.5pt\otimes\hskip-0.5ptW)
. Given
v\in V
, observing that
e_{a}\otimes v\in P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.3pt\rangle_{s}\otimes V
, we have
f(e_{a}\otimes v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes w_{i,v},
for some unique
w_{i,v}\in W
. This yields
k
-linear maps
f_{i}:V\to W:v\mapsto w_{i,v}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, such that
f=\varphi(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes f_{i})
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

\it\Lambda

Proposition 2.4.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R

First, we have a

-linear isomorphism
\theta_{a}\!:\hskip-0.5pt{\rm Hom}_{k}(e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}_{0}e_{a},V)\!\to\!V\!\!:\!g\!\mapsto\!g(e_{a}).

Proposition 2.5.1.

Proposition 2.5.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R

. If

, and hence,

um\neq 0

top-element

m\in M_{n}(a)\backslash JM
for some
(n,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. In this case, we can find a
k
-subspace
L_{n,a}
of
M_{n}(a)
, containing
M_{n}(a)\cap JM
, such that
M_{n}(a)=L_{n,a}\oplus km
. Setting
L_{i,x}=M_{i}(x)
for
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
with
(i,x)\neq(n,a),
we obtain a
k
-subspace
L(m)=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}L_{i,x}
of
M

m\not\in L(m)
.

Lemma 2.6.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Consider a module
M
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. A graded submodule
L
of
M
is graded maximal if and only if
L=L(m)
for some top-element
m\in M;
and in this case,
JM\subseteq L.

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Consider a top-element
m\in M_{n}(a)
with
(n,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. We claim that
L(m)
is a
\it\Lambda
-submodule of
M
. Otherwise, there exist some
m^{\prime}\in L_{i,x}
and
u\in e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}e_{x}
with
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in Q_{0}
such that
um^{\prime}\in M_{i+j}(y)\backslash L_{i+j,y}.
By definition,
(i+j,y)=(n,a)
. If
j=0
, then
(i,x)=(n,a)
and
u\in e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{0}e_{a}=ke_{a}
, so
um^{\prime}\in L_{i,x}=L_{i+j,y}
, absurd. If
j>0
, then
um^{\prime}\in M_{n}(a)\cap JM\subseteq L_{n,a}=L_{i+j,y}
,
a contradiction. This establishes our claim. Being of codimension one,
L(m)
is graded maximal in
M
. Since
M_{n}(a)\cap JM\subseteq L_{n,a}
, we see that
JM\subseteq L(m)
.

Let
L
be a graded maximal submodule of
M
. Then, we have a graded simple module
M/L=\oplus_{(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}}(M_{i}(x)+L)/L
. By Proposition
2.5.1

for some
(n,a)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. Therefore,
(M_{n}(a)+L)/L=k(m+L)
for some top element
m\in M_{n}(a)\backslash L_{n}(a)
, and
L_{i}(x)=M_{i}(x)
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
with
(i,x)\neq(n,a)
. As a consequence,
M_{n}(a)=L_{n}(a)+km
and
M_{n}(a)\cap JM\subseteq L_{n}(a)
. Since
m\not\in L_{n}(a)
, we see that
M_{n}(a)=L_{n}(a)\oplus km
. In view of the above construction,
L=L(m)
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

The
graded radical

{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M
of
M
is the intersection of all graded maximal submodules of
M
.
A graded submodule
N
of

is called

in

if

for any proper graded submodule

of

.

Proposition 2.6.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If

, then
{\rm rad}M=JM

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. By Lemma
2.6.1
,

Consider

. Write

, where
m_{i,x}\in M_{i}(x)
. Then,
m_{n,a}\not\in JM
for some

. By Lemma
2.6.1
, we have a graded maximal submodule

of

. Then,

{\rm rad}M=JM
. Suppose that

Remark.
In case

{\rm GMod}^{-}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

Let

.

We have a sufficient condition for

Proposition 2.6.4.

Let

2.7.
Finitely generated modules

Note that every finitely generated module in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is generated by finitely many pure elements. This fact leads to the following notion.

Definition 2.7.1 .

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\!=\!kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Given
M\hskip-0.5pt\in\hskip-0.5pt{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, a set
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\}
of pure elements in

is called a
top
-
basis
if
\{m_{1}+{\rm rad}M,\ldots,m_{r}+{\rm rad}M\}
is a
k
-basis of
{\rm top}M
and

Proposition 2.7.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Given
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, the following statements are equivalent
\,:

(1)

M
is finitely generated
\,;

(2)

M
admits a finite top-basis
\,;

(3)

M
is bounded below and
{\rm top}M
is finite dimensional.

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
be nonzero. Assume that Statement (1) holds. Clearly,
M\in{\rm GMod}^{+}\hskip-3.5pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. By Corollary
2.6.5
,
{\rm top}M
is graded semisimple. Being finitely generated,
{\rm top}M
is finite dimensional; see (
2.5.1
). Thus, Statement (3) holds.

Suppose that Statement (3) holds. We may choose pure elements
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
in
M
such that

\{m_{1}+{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M,\ldots,m_{r}+{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M\}
is a
k
-basis of
{\rm top}M
. In particular,

M/{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M=(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}m_{i}+{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M)/{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M.

Since
{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M
is graded superfluous in
M
; see (
2.6.4
),
M=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}m_{i}
. Thus, Statement (2) holds, and so does Statement (1). The proof of the proposition is completed.

2.8.
Graded projective cover

A superfluous epimorphism in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called
graded superfluous
, and a projective cover of a module in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called a
graded projective cover.

Lemma 2.8.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

An epimorphism
f:M\to N
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is graded superfluous if and only if
{\rm Ker}(f)
is graded superfluous in
M;
and in this case,
f^{-1}({\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}N)={\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}M
.

(2)

An epimorphism
f:P\hskip-0.5pt\to\hskip-0.5ptM
in
{\rm GMod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
P
graded projective is a graded projective cover of
M
if and only if
{\rm Ker}(f)\subseteq{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}P.

Proof.
Statement (1) is easily adapted from the non-graded setting; see, for example,
[
2
, (5.15)]
. Consider an epimorphism
f:P\hskip-0.5pt\to\hskip-0.5ptM
in
{\rm GMod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
P
graded projective. By Propositions
2.6.2
and
2.6.4
,
{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}P
is the largest superfluous graded submodule of
P
. So, every graded submodule of
{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}P
is superfluous in
P
. Now, Statement (2) follows from Statement (1). The proof of the lemma is completed.

Example.
Given
a\in Q_{0}
, the canonical projection
p_{a}:P_{a}\to S_{a}
is a graded projective cover of
S_{a}
.

We are ready to construct a graded projective cover for every finitely generated graded module; compare
[
30
, (1.1)]
.

Proposition 2.8.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. A module

admits a graded projective cover

f\hskip-0.5pt:\hskip-0.5ptP_{a_{\hskip-0.8pt1}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s_{\hskip-0.6pt1}\hskip-0.5pt\rangle\hskip-0.5pt\oplus\cdots\oplus\hskip-0.5ptP_{a_{\hskip-0.5ptr\hskip-0.5pt}}\hskip-1.0pt\langle\hskip-0.4pt-s_{\hskip-0.6ptr}\hskip-0.5pt\rangle\hskip-0.5pt\to\!M:e_{a_{i}}\mapsto m_{i}\vskip-2.0pt

\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
with
m_{i}\in M_{s_{i}}(a_{i})

Proof.
Let
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
be a top-basis for
M
, where

with

and

. In view of Proposition
2.3.1
, we obtain a graded epimorphism

Since

is

-linearly independent, it follows that
{\rm Ker}(f)\subseteq{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}(\oplus_{i=1}^{r}P_{a_{\hskip-0.8pt1}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s_{\hskip-0.6pt1}\hskip-0.5pt\rangle).

So

is a graded projective cover of

; see (
2.8.1
).

Suppose that

has a graded projective cover as stated in the proposition. Then,

and

Let

, where

), there exists some

Since

2.9.
Graded socle.

Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. The
graded socle

{\rm soc}M
of
M
is the sum of all graded simple submodules of
M
.
A graded submodule
N
of
M
is called
graded essential
in
M
if
N\cap L\neq 0
for any nonzero graded submodule
L
of
M.

Lemma 2.9.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, then
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is contained in every essential graded submodule of
M
such that
({\rm soc}\hskip 0.3ptM)_{i}(x)=\{m\in M_{i}(x)\mid Jm=0\}
for all
(i,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
.

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Assume that
L
is an essential graded submodule of
M
. If
S
is a graded simple submodule of
M
, then
S=L\cap S\subseteq L
. Thus,
{\rm soc}M\subseteq L.
The second part of the statement follows from Proposition
2.5.2
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

In general,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is not necessarily graded essential in
M
. Nevertheless, we have the following sufficient condition for this to happen.

Lemma 2.9.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
M\in{\rm GMod}^{-}\hskip-2.5pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, then
{\rm soc}M
is graded essential in
M
.

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}^{-}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Consider a nonzero graded submodule
N
of
M
. Choose
0\neq m\in N_{i}
for some integer
i
. Since
N\in{\rm GMod}^{-}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, there exists some
j\geq 0
such that
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}m\neq 0
but
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j+1}m=0
. By Lemma
2.9.1
,
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{j}m\subseteq{\rm soc}M
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Example.
Consider
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
, where

Q:
1
\alpha

\beta

2

and

.
Then,
P_{1}=k\{e_{1},\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}^{2}\hskip-0.5pt,\cdots\}
with
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}P_{1}=k\bar{\beta}
. Observe that

is a graded submodule of

such that

. So,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}P_{1}
is not graded essential in

.

The next statement describes the graded socle for modules in
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Corollary 2.9.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with

a locally finite quiver. If

then
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a}=ke_{a}^{\star}
, which is graded essential in
I_{a}
.

Proof.
Fix

. Note that

(I_{a})_{0}=D(e_{a}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{0}^{\rm o}e_{a})=ke^{\star}_{a}.
Since
(I_{a})_{i}=0
for

2.9.1
,

. So,
({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a})_{0}=ke^{\star}.
Consider

for some

. Then,

for some

, that is,

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a}=({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a})_{0}=ke_{a}^{\star}

,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a}

. The proof of the corollary is completed.

{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

Definition 2.10.1 .

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M

Finitely cogenerated graded modules are characterized as follows.

Proposition 2.10.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with

a locally finite quiver. Given

, the following statements are equivalent

(1)

\,;

(2)

(3)

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M

Proof.
Let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is finitely generated and graded semisimple. By Proposition
2.5.1
,
{\rm soc}M
is finite dimensional. Thus,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
has a
k
-basis

\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
, where
m_{i}\in M_{s_{i}}(a_{i})
with
(s_{i},a_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. Set
s=s_{1}+\dots+s_{r}
. Then
M_{j}\cap\hskip 0.5pt{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M=0
for
j>s
. Suppose that there exists
0\neq m\in M_{p}
for some
p>s
. Since
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is graded essential in
M
, there exists some
u\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}_{t}
with
t\geq 0
such that
0\neq um\in M_{t+p}\cap{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
, a contradiction. Hence, Statement (3) holds.

Suppose that Statement (3) holds. Then,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
has a
k
-basis

\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
, where the
m_{i}
are pure elements in
M
. Since
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is graded essential in
M
, by Lemma
2.9.2
,
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
is a soc-basis for
M
. Hence, Statement (2) holds. The proof of the proposition is completed.

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.10.2
and Corollary
2.9.3
(3), we obtain
the following statement.

Corollary 2.10.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Then, every module in
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is finitely cogenerated, and every graded submodule of a finitely cogenerated graded module is finitely cogenerated.

2.11.
Graded injective envelope.

An essential monomorphism in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called
graded essential
, and an injective envelope of a module in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called a
graded injective envelope
.

Lemma 2.11.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

A monomorphism
f:M\to N
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is graded essential if and only if
{\rm Im}(f)
is graded essential in
N;
and in this case,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}N=f({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M)
.

(2)

A monomorphism
f:M\to I
in
{\rm GMod}^{+}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
I
graded injective is a graded injective envelope of
M
if and only if
{\rm soc}I\subseteq{\rm Im}(f)
.

Proof.
Statement (1) is easily adapted from the non-graded setting; see, for example,
[
2
, (5.13)]
. By Lemmas
2.9.1
and
2.9.2
,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I
is the smallest essential graded submodule of
I
. Thus, every graded submodule of
I
containing
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I
is graded essential in
I
. Now, Statement (2) follows from Statement (1). The proof of the lemma is completed.

Example.
Given
a\in Q_{0}
, by Lemma
2.4.4
, we have a graded monomorphism
q_{a}:S_{a}\to I_{a}
, sending
e_{a}+Je_{a}
to
e_{a}^{\star}
. By Corollary
2.9.3
,
{\rm Im}(q_{a})={\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{a}
, and by Lemma
2.11.1
,
q_{a}
is a graded injective envelope of
S_{a}
.

We are ready to construct a graded injective envelope for every finitely cogenerated graded module.

Proposition 2.11.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. A module
M
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
admits a graded injective envelope

g\hskip-0.5pt:\!M\!\to\!I_{a_{1}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.5pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts_{1}\hskip-0.8pt\rangle\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3pt\cdots\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3ptI_{a_{r}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.5pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts_{r}\hskip-0.8pt\rangle:m_{i}\mapsto e_{a_{i}}^{\star}

if and only if
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
with
m_{i}\in M_{-s_{i}}(a_{i})
is a soc-basis for
M.

Proof.
Suppose that
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
is a soc-basis for
M,
where
m_{i}\in M_{-s_{i}}(a_{i})
. Then,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M=km_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus km_{r}.
By Lemma
2.4.4
, we have a graded monomorphism

q:{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M\to I_{a_{1}}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts_{1}\hskip-0.3pt\rangle\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3pt\cdots\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3ptI_{a_{r}}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pts_{r}\hskip-0.3pt\rangle=I:m_{i}\mapsto e_{a_{i}}^{\star}.

By Corollary
2.9.3
,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I=ke_{a_{1}}^{\star}\!\!\oplus\cdots\oplus ke_{a_{r}}^{\star}={\rm Im}(q).
Since the inclusion map
h:{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M\to M
is graded essential; see (
2.11.1
), we have a graded monomorphism
g:M\to I
such that
g\circ h=q
. Since
{\rm soc}I={\rm Im}(q)\subseteq{\rm Im}(g),
by Lemma
2.11.1
(2),
g
is a graded injective envelope of
M
.

Suppose that
g:M\to I_{a_{1}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.5pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts_{1}\hskip-0.8pt\rangle\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3pt\cdots\hskip 0.3pt\oplus\hskip 0.3ptI_{a_{r}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.5pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts_{r}\hskip-0.8pt\rangle=I
is a graded injective envelope of
M
. By Lemma
2.11.1
,
ke_{a_{1}}^{\star}\!\!\oplus\cdots\oplus ke_{a_{r}}^{\star}=g({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M),
where
e_{a_{i}}^{\star}\in I_{a_{i}}\hskip-0.5pt\hskip-0.5pt\langle\hskip-0.4pts_{i}\hskip-0.8pt\rangle_{-s_{i}}
. Thus,
e_{a_{i}}^{\star}=g(m_{i})
for some
m_{i}\in({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M)_{-s_{i}}(a_{i})\subseteq M_{-s_{i}}(a_{i})
. Since
g
is a monomorphism,
M
is bounded above and
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
is a
k
-basis of
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
. By Lemma
2.9.2
,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is graded essential in
M
.
The proof of the proposition is completed.

A module
M
in
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called
finitely copresented
if it admits a
graded injective copresentation
over
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, that is an exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{\hskip 0.4pt0}}
\textstyle{I^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{1}}
\textstyle{I^{1},}

in
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
I^{0},I^{1}\in{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Such a graded injective copresentation is called
minimal
if
d^{\hskip 0.4pt{}^{0}}
and
d^{1}
are both left minimal. Applying Corollary
2.10.3
, Proposition
2.11.2
and the dual of Schanuel’s Lemma, we obtain the following statement.

Lemma 2.11.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Every finitely copresented module in
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
admits a minimal graded injective copresentation over
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, which is unique up to isomorphism.

2.12.
Krull-Schmidt subcategories

In this subsection, we provide several Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt subcategories of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, which will play an important role in our later study of almost split sequences and almost split triangles.

Lemma 2.12.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
P\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
I\in{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, then
{\rm GHom}_{\Lambda}(P,M)
and
{\rm GHom}_{\Lambda}(M,I)
are finite dimensional, for all
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proof.
We shall only prove the first part of the statement. Let
P\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Then
P\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}P_{a_{i}}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s_{i}\hskip-0.3pt\rangle
, where
(s_{i},a_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}\times Q_{0}
. Given
M\in{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, by Proposition
2.3.1
,
{\rm GHom}_{\Lambda}(P,M)\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}M_{s_{i}}(a_{i}).
The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following statement exhibits some particular feature of the graded setting.

Lemma 2.12.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

The category
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt and contains all finitely generated graded projective modules in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

(2)

The category
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt and contains all finitely cogenerated graded injective modules in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). By Lemma
2.12.1
,
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite. Given
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a\in Q_{0}
, by Proposition
2.3.3
,
{\rm GEnd}_{\Lambda}(P_{a}\hskip-0.6pt\langle\hskip-0.2pt-s\hskip-0.8pt\rangle)\cong ke_{a}
. Thus,
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Krull-Schmidt. If
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is finitely generated and graded projective, then it has a graded projective cover
f:P\to M
with
P\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
2.8.2
), and hence,
M\cong P
.
The proof of the lemma is completed.

We write
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
,
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
for the full subcategories of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
of finitely generated modules, of finitely cogenerated modules, and of finite dimensional modules, respectively.
Clearly, they are all subcategories of
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Lemma 2.12.3.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

The restricted functor
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
is a duality.

(2)

Both
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and

{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt extension-closed subcategories of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, whose intersection is
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proof.
(1) Let
M\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Then, we have a graded epimorphism
f:P\to M
with
P\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Applying the duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
; see (
2.2.2
), we obtain a graded monomorphism
\mathfrak{D}f:\mathfrak{D}M\to\mathfrak{D}P
with
\mathfrak{D}P
in
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
. By Corollary
2.10.3
,
\mathfrak{D}M\in{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
. Dually, if
N\in{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
, then
\mathfrak{D}N\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

(2) Clearly
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is closed under direct summands and extensions, and by Lemma
2.12.1
, it is Hom-finite. So,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Krull-Schmidt. Then, by Statement (1),
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt and extension-closed in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Finally, let
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
be finitely generated and finitely cogenerated. By Propositions
2.12.4
and
2.10.2
,
M
is bounded. Since the modules in
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are locally dimensional, so are those in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. As a consequence,
M\in{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

In view of Lemma
2.12.3
(2),
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and

{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are exact
k
-categories, which are not abelian in general. A module
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is called
noetherian
if every graded submodule of
M
is finitely generated. Note that this is equivalent to
M
being noetherian as a ungraded
\it\Lambda
-module; see
[
38
, (5.4.7)]
.

Proposition 2.12.4.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. Then
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\hskip 1.0pt
or
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\hskip 0.5pt
is abelian if and only if
\it\Lambda
is locally left or right noetherian
;
and in this case,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
or
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Ext-finite, respectively.

Proof.
Suppose that
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian. Since
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is closed under graded quotients, it is closed under graded submodules. In particular,
\it\Lambda
is locally left noetherian.
Conversely, suppose that
\it\Lambda
is locally left noetherian. Then, the modules in
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are noetherian, and so are those in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Therefore,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian. As a consequence, every module in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
admits a graded projective resolution over
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. In view of Lemma
2.12.1
, we see that
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Ext-finite.

Finally,
\it\Lambda
is locally right noetherian if and only if
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
is locally left noetherian. In view of the duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}\to{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
2.12.3
), we see that the second part of the statement holds. The proof of the proposition is completed.

Next, we shall study the full subcategories
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
of finitely presented modules and of finitely copresented modules, respectively.

Lemma 2.12.5.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

The duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
restricts to two mutually quasi-inverse functors

\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
and
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}\to{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

(2)

Both
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and

{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt extension-closed subcategories of
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, whose intersection is
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb}\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proof.
(1)

Given
M\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$},
it admits a graded projective presentation

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0ptP^{-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}
where
P^{-1}\!,P^{0}\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Applying
\mathfrak{D}
yields a graded injective copresentation

\textstyle{\!0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{D}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{D}P^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{D}P^{-1}}
with
\mathfrak{D}P^{0}\hskip-2.0pt,\hskip 0.4pt\mathfrak{D}P^{-1}\!\hskip-0.5pt\in\hskip-0.5pt{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}\!.
That is,
\mathfrak{D}M\in{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
. Dually, if
N\in{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
, then
\mathfrak{D}N\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
In view of Proposition
2.2.2
, we have a duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}
.

(2) By Lemma
2.12.3
,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite, and by Proposition 2.1 in
[
3
]
, it is extension-closed in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Assume that
M\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
M=M^{1}\oplus M^{2}
. By Lemma
2.11.3
,
M
admits a graded projective cover
f:P\to M
with
{\rm Ker}(f)
finitely generated. Being finitely generated,
M^{i}
has a graded projective cover
f^{i}:P^{i}\to M^{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then,
{\rm Ker}(f)\cong{\rm Ker}(f^{1})\oplus{\rm Ker}(f^{2})
. In particular,
{\rm Ker}(f^{i})
is finitely generated, and hence,
M^{i}\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
for
i=1,2
. Thus,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is closed under direct summands. So,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Krull-Schmidt. Then, by Statement (1),
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is also Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt and extension-closed in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Finally, assume that
M\in{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
M_{i}=0
for
i\geq t
. By Proposition
2.8.2
,
M
has a graded projective cover
f:P\to M
with
P\in{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Write
L={\rm Ker}(f)
. Then
L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
, where
L_{i}\subseteq P_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
L_{i}=P_{i}
for all
i\geq t.
Given
i>t
, we see that
L_{i}=P_{i}=J_{i-t}P_{t}\subseteq{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}L.
This implies that
{\rm top}L=\oplus_{i\leq t}(L_{i}+{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}L)/{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}L
. On the other hand, since
P
is locally finite dimensional,

\oplus_{i\leq t}P_{i}
is finite dimensional, and so is
\oplus_{i\leq t}L_{i}
. Thus,
{\rm top}L
is finite dimensional. Since
L\in{\rm GMod}^{+}\hskip-3.5pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, by Proposition
2.7.2
(3),
L
is finitely generated. So,
M\in{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Dually,
M\in{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
The proof of the lemma is completed.

It is evident that the projective objects in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are the modules in
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$};
and the injective objects in
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are the modules in
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proposition 2.12.6.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. The finitely cogenerated injective objects in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are the finite dimensional modules in
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$};
and the finitely generated projective objects in
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are the finite dimensional modules in
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.

Proof.
We shall only prove the first part of the statement. Given
M\in{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we see that
M_{\geq n}=\oplus_{i\geq n}M_{i}
is a graded submodule of
M
. Let
L
be an injective object in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, which is finitely cogenerated. Being an essential monomorphism in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
2.11.1
), the inclusion map
j:{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}L\to L
is an injective envelope of
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}L
in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. On the other hand, by Proposition
2.11.2
,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}L
admits an injective envelope
q:{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}L\to I
in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
with
I\in{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Since
L
is finite dimensional; see (
2.12.5
),
L=L_{\geq n}
for some

. Fix arbitrarily

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I\cong{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}L,

{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}(I_{\geq t})={\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I
. Hence,

in
{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (

). Since

is finite dimensional and
L
is injective in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, there exists a graded morphism

such that

. Since
q_{\geq t}
is graded essential,

is a monomorphism. And since

, we have

is finite dimensional. In particular,

Applying Proposition

, we obtain the following interesting statement.

Proposition 2.12.7.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with

a locally finite quiver. The following statements hold.

(1)

Every

with

We shall only prove Statement (1). Clearly,

in

3.
Graded almost split sequences

The objective of this section is to study the existence of almost split sequences for graded modules.
We shall first construct a graded Nakayama functor, which allows us to establish directly a graded Auslander-Reiten formula for finitely presented graded modules and a generalized Auslander-Reiten formula for finitely copresented graded modules. From these formulas we derive two existence theorems for almost split sequences in the category of all graded modules, one for finitely presented graded modules and one for finitely copresented graded modules. Finally, we shall study when the category of finitely presented graded modules and that of finitely copresented graded modules have almost split sequences.

3.1.
Graded transpose

In the locally finite dimensional graded case, Marinez-Villa has introduced the transpose of a graded
\it\Lambda
-module; see
[
35
, (1.4)]
. In our setting, we need to take more caution in the construction. We start with defining a contravariant functor
(-)^{t}:{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\rm o}

Given

Let

We shall denote by

Moreover, put

and
\overline{\rm\hskip-0.5ptGEnd}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M)=\overline{\rm\hskip-0.5ptGHom}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M,M).

We are ready to obtain the promised Auslander-Reiten formulae
as follows.

3.5.
Graded almost split sequences

To the best of our knowledge, there exists no existence theorem for almost split sequences starting with finitely copresented (graded) modules in the category of all (graded) modules.
over a general (graded) algebra.
Using our generalized Auslander-Reiten formula and the result in
[
31
, (3.7)]
; see also
[
32
, (2.3)]

Theorem 3.5.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

4.
Graded almost split triangles

The objective of this section is to study the existence of almost split triangles in various derived categories of graded modules. The graded Nakayama functor constructed in Section 3 allows us to apply the results in
[
31
, Section 5]
for this purpose. Some of our results are analogous
to those of Happel for finite dimensional ungraded algebras in
[
21
,
22
]
. Throughout this section let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra, where
Q
is a locally finite quiver and
R
is a relation ideal in
kQ
.

4.1.
The existence in general

The bounded complexes of projective objects and those of injective objects in an abelian category play an essential role in the study of almost split triangles in their derived categories; see
[
21
,
22
,
31
]
.

Lemma 4.1.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver.

(1)

The categories
K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
are Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt.

(2)

The Nakayama functor induces two mutually quasi-inverse triangle equivalences
\nu:K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})\to K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\nu^{-}\!:K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})\to K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}).

Proof.
(1) Since
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is Hom-finite and Krull-Schmmidt; see (
2.12.2
),
C^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is a Hom-finite additive subcategory of
C^{b}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
, which is closed under direct summands. Therefore,
C^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is Krull-Schmidt, and consequently,
K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt; see
[
29
, page 431]
. Similarly,
K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt.

(2) The Nakayama functor
\nu:{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
induces two mutually quasi-inverse equivalences
\nu:{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
\nu^{-}:{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
3.2.1
). Applying them component-wise, we obtain two mutually quasi-inverse triangle equivalences
\nu:K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})\to K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\nu^{-}\!:K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})\to K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}).
The proof of the lemma is completed.

It is well-known that
\!K^{b}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\!K^{b}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})

are full triangulated subcategories of
\!D^{b}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\!D^{b}({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
; see
[
39
, (10.4.7)]
, while
\!D^{b}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\!D^{b}({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
are full triangulated subcategories of
\!D({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\!D({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
respectively; see
[
37
, (III.3.4.5)]
. Note, however, that
D({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is not necessarily a triangulated subcategory of
\!D({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
.

Theorem 4.1.2.

Let

\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\!\in\hskip-0.5ptK^{b}\hskip-0.5pt({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is indecomposable,

then

D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}),\!

D({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}),\!

D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}(\hskip-0.5pt{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
D(\hskip-0.5pt{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
each have an almost split triangle

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0pt\nu\hskip-0.5ptP^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\hskip 0.3pt[-\hskip-0.5pt1]\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hskip-1.0ptL^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-2.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\hskip-2.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\hskip-1.0pt.}

Proof.
Let
P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\in K^{b}\hskip-0.5pt({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
be indecomposable. By Lemma
4.1.1
,
P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}
and
\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}
are strongly indecomposable. Considering the Nakayama functors
\nu\hskip-0.5pt:\hskip-1.0pt{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\!\to\!{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
\nu\hskip-0.5pt:\hskip-1.0pt{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\!\to\!{\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
3.2.1
), we deduce from Theorem 5.8 in
[
31
]
a desired almost split triangle in each of
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
,
\!D({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
,
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
\!D({\rm GMod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

4.2.
The existence in the locally noetherian case

The bounded derived category of finitely generated graded modules over noetherian graded algebras is important in geometry; see, for example,
[
11

Lemma 4.2.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a graded algebra with
Q
a locally finite quiver. If
\it\Lambda
is locally left or right noetherian, then
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})

Proof.
Assume that

{\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is an Ext-finite abelian subcategory of
{\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
; see (
2.12.4
). By Corollary B in
[
27
]
,
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt. Since
{\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has enough graded projective modules; see (
2.8.2
),
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is
a full triangulated subcategory of
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
; see (
1.7.1
). The proof of the lemma is completed.

In case
\it\Lambda
is locally left and right noetherian,

both
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip-0.5pt+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip-0.5pt-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
are full triangulated subcategories of
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
.

Theorem 4.2.2.

Let

(1)

If

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0ptN^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[1]\hskip-2.0pt}

If
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\in\!\hskip-0.5ptD^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip-0.5pt-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is indecomposable,

then
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[1]}

admits a finite graded injective coresolution over
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\hskip 0.5pt;
and in this case,
N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\in D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip-0.5pt+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})

has a graded projective resolution

,

has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{\hskip-3.0pt\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[-\hskip-0.5pt1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4.3.
Existence in the locally bounded case

In this subsection, we shall concentrate on the bounded derived category of finite dimensional graded
\it\Lambda
-modules. Although our results are analogous to those of Happel in
[
21
,
22
]
, they do exhibit some particular features of the locally bounded graded setting.

Proposition 4.3.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a locally bounded graded algebra, where
Q
is a locally finite quiver.
If
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\in D^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.3pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
is indecomposable, then

(1)

D^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.3pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{\!N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt[1]\!}

if and only if
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt
has a finite graded projective resolution over
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\hskip 0.5pt;

(2)

D^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.3pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{\!M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\hskip-1.0pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt[1]\!}

if and only if
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt
has a finite graded injective coresolution over
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
.

Proof.
Since
\it\Lambda
is locally bounded,
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are contained in
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Thus,
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt abelian
k
-category with enough projective objects and enough injective objects. In view of Theorem
3.2.1
, we have a Nakayama functor
\nu:{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\to{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Now, the result follows directly from Theorem 5.12 in
[
31
]
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

We are ready to obtain the sufficient and necessary conditions for the bounded derived category of finite dimensional graded modules to have almost split triangles.

Theorem 4.3.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
be a locally bounded graded algebra, where
Q
is a locally finite quiver. Then

D^{\hskip 0.5ptb}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has almost split triangles on the right
(
respectively, left
)
if and only if every graded simple module in
{\rm mod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is of finite graded projective
(
respectively, injective
)
dimension.

Proof.
Since
\it\Lambda
is locally bounded,

{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt abelian
k
-category with enough projective objects and enough injective objects.

So, every complex in
C^{b}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has a graded projective resolution in
C^{-}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
and a graded injective coresolution in
C^{+}({\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
; see
[
19
, (7.5)]
. Then, it is not hard to see that every complex in
C^{b}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has a finite graded projective resolution over
{\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
if and only if every module in
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is of finite graded projective dimension, or equivalently, every graded simple module in
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is of finite graded projective dimension. Dually, every complex in
C^{b}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has a finite graded injective coresolution over
{\rm ginj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
if and only if every simple module in
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is of finite graded injective dimension. Now, the statement follows immediately from Proposition
4.3.1
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

Remark.
The bounded derived category of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional ungraded algebra has almost split triangles on either side if and only if it has almost split triangles on both sides; see
[
22
, (1.5)]
. As shown below, this is not the case in the locally bounded graded setting.

Example.
Consider the locally bounded graded algebra
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R
, where

\textstyle{Q:}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{n}}
\textstyle{n-1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{2}}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{1}}
\textstyle{0}

and
R=k\langle\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+1}\mid i\geq 1\rangle
. Given
n\geq 0
, we see that
S_{n}
is of graded projective dimension
n
and of infinite graded injective dimension. By Theorem
4.3.2
,
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
has almost split triangles on the right but not on the left.

5.
Graded representations of quivers

In this section, we shall specialize to graded representations of an arbitrary locally finite quiver
Q
. In this case, we shall strengthen some of the results obtained in Sections 3 and 4, which are analogous to those for ungraded representations of strongly locally finite quivers stated in
[
10
]
.

5.1.
Graded almost split sequences

We shall restrict our attention to finitely presented graded representations and finitely co-presented graded representations.

Lemma 5.1.1.

Let
Q
be a locally finite quiver. Then
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5pt+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
and
{\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5pt-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
are hereditary abelian
k
-categories, which are Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt.

Proof.
By Lemma
2.12.5
,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp}kQ
and
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti}kQ
are Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt and extension-closed in
{\rm GMod}kQ
. Note that the category of all unitary left
kQ
-modules is hereditary; see
[
15
, (8.2)]
. In particular,
{\rm GExt}^{2}_{kQ}(M,N)=0
for all
M,N\in{\rm GMod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ
. So,
{\rm GMod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ
is hereditary. Since
{\rm GMod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ
has enough projective objects; see (
2.4.5
), a subobject of a projective object in
{\rm GMod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ
is projective.

Consider a morphism
f:P\to P^{\prime}
in
{\rm gproj}\hskip 0.6ptkQ
. Since
{\rm Im}(f)
is graded projective,
P\cong{\rm Ker}(f)\oplus{\rm Im}(f)
. So,
{\rm Ker}(f)\in{\rm gproj}\hskip 0.6ptkQ
. By Proposition 2.1 in
[
3
]
,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
is closed under kernels and cokernels, and consequently, it is abelian. Since
{\rm GMod}\hskip 0.6ptkQ
is hereditary, so is
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.6ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ
. Finally, by Lemma
2.12.5
(1),
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.6pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
is also hereditary and abelian. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Note that
kQ
is locally left bounded if and only if
Q
has no infinite path with a starting point, and it is locally right bounded if and only if
Q
has no infinite path with an end point.

Theorem 5.1.2.

Let
Q
be a locally finite quiver. The following statement holds.

(1)

The abelian category
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
has almost split sequences on the left if and only if
Q
contains no infinite path with a starting point.

(2)

The abelian category
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
has almost split sequences on the right if and only if
Q
contains no infinite path with an end point.

(3)

Both
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp}kQ
and
{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
have almost split sequences if and only if
Q
contains no infinite path.

Proof.
The sufficiency of Statement (1) follows from Theorem
3.6.1
.
Suppose that
Q
has an infinite path with a starting point, say it starts with an arrow
a\to b
. Then,
P_{b}
is infinite dimensional and not graded injective. By Theorem
3.5.2
(1),
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
has no almost split sequence starting with
P_{b}
.
This establishes Statement (1). And Statement (2) follows dually. Finally, the necessity of Statement (3) follows from Statements (1) and (2). If
Q
contains no infinite path, then
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp}kQ={\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ={\rm gmod}^{b}kQ
, which has almost split sequences by Theorem
3.6.2
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

5.2.
Graded almost split triangles

First of all, by Lemma
5.1.1
and Proposition
1.7.1
, both
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
and
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
are Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt full triangulated subcategories of
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}kQ)
.

Theorem 5.2.1.

Let
Q
be a locally finite quiver. The following statements hold.

(1)

If

M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\in\!D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
is indecomposable, then
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ)
has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{\hskip-2.0ptN^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[1],\hskip-2.0pt}
where
N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\in D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{\hskip-0.5pt-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
.

(2)

If
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\in\!\hskip-0.5ptD^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4pti}kQ)
is indecomposable,

then
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hskip 0.5ptkQ)
has an almost split triangle
\textstyle{M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\hskip-0.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\!\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[1],}

where
N^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\in D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Let
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\in D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.8pt}({\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
be indecomposable. Since
{\rm gmod}^{+\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
is hereditary; see (
5.1.1
),
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt
has a graded projective resolution
P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\in C^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gproj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
. Since
M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\cong P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}
in
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
, by Theorem
4.1.2
, there exists an almost split triangle
\textstyle{\hskip-3.0pt\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[-\hskip-0.5pt1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 2.5pt\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\hskip-2.0pt}
in
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$})
, where
\nu P^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 2.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}[-\hskip-0.5pt1]\!\in\!D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm gmod}^{-\hskip-0.5pt,\hskip 0.4pti\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

The following statement is analogous to Theorem 7.11 in
[
10
]
, which contains a partial converse of

Theorem 5.2.2.

Let
Q
be a locally finite quiver. Then, the following statements are equivalent
\,:

(1)

D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.8pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles
(\hskip-1.0pt
on the left, on the right
\hskip 0.8pt);

(2)

D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-1.0pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles
(\hskip 0.4pt
on the left, on the right
);

(3)

Q
has no infinite path
(\hskip-1.0pt
with a starting point, with an end point
\hskip 0.8pt).

Proof.
In view of the duality
\mathfrak{D}:{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ^{\rm o}\to{\rm gmod}^{-,\hskip 0.5pti\hskip-1.0pt}kQ
, we shall only prove the equivalence of Statements (1) and (3). Suppose first that
D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles on the left. By Corollary 7.3(1) in
[
10
]
,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
has almost split sequences on the left; and by Theorem
5.1.2
(1),
Q
has no infinite path with a starting point. Suppose conversely that
Q
has no infinite path with a starting point. Then,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ={\rm gmod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
; and by Theorem
5.1.2
(1),
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ
has almost split sequences on the left. Moreover, by Proposition
2.12.6
, every indecomposable injective object in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ
is isomorphic to a finite dimensional module
I_{x}
in
{\rm ginj}\hskip 0.5ptkQ
for some
x\in Q_{0}
, whose socle
S_{x}
is simple and admits a projective cover
P_{x}
in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-1.0pt}kQ
. By Corollaries 2.2(2) and 7.3(1) in
[
10
]
,
D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles on the left.

Next, suppose that
D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles on the right. By Corollary 7.3(1) in
[
10
]
, every
S_{x}
with
x\in Q_{0}
admits an injective envelope in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
. By Proposition
2.12.7
(1),
Q
has no infinite path with an end point. Suppose conversely that
Q
has no infinite path with an end point. By Proposition
2.12.7
,
every
S_{x}
with
x\in Q_{0}
has an injective envelope
I_{x}
in
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
, and by Theorem
3.6.1
,
{\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ
has almost split sequence on the right. By Corollary 7.3(2) in
[
10
]
,
D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles on the right. Finally, combining what has been shown, we see that
D^{b}({\rm gmod}^{+,\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.5pt}kQ)
has almost split triangles if and only if
Q
has no infinite path. The proof of the theorem is completed.

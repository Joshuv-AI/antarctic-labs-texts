Pinwheel solutions to Schrödinger systems
Mónica Clapp, Angela Pistoia

Abstract
We establish the existence of positive segregated solutions for competitive nonlinear Schrödinger systems in the presence of an external trapping potential, which have the property that each component is obtained from the previous one by a rotation, and we study their behavior as the forces of interaction become very small or very large. As a consequence, we obtain optimal partitions for the Schrödinger equation by sets that are linearly isometric to each other. 1 1 1 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J50 (35J47, 35B06, 35B07, 35B40) 2 2 2 Keywords: Schrödinger systems, segregated solutions, phase separation, optimal partition.

Introduction

Consider the nonlinear Schrödinger system

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}+V_{i}(x)u_{i}=|u_{i}|^{2p-2}u_{i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad u_{i}>0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
N\geq 2
,
p>1
and
p<\frac{N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
,
\beta_{ij}=\beta_{ji}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
V_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For the cubic nonlinearity (
p=2
) in dimensions
N=2,3
this system arises in the study of Bose-Einstein condensation for a mixture of
\ell
different states which overlap in space. It has been widely studied in the last two decades. Most work has been done in the autonomous case (i.e., for constant
V_{i}
). We refer the reader to the recent paper
[
11
]
where the authors provide an exhaustive list of references. The non-autonomous case turns out to be much more difficult. Some results have been recently obtained by Peng and Wang
[
13
]
, Pistoia and Vaira
[
14
]
, and Li, Wei and Wu
[
11
]
.

The system (
1.1
) for a more general subcritical nonlinearity in higher dimensions has been much less studied. Even if it does not have an immediate physical motivation, finding a solution in this general setting is a quite interesting and challenging problem from a mathematical point of view. To our knowledge, the only result so far is that by Gao and Guo
[
10
]
who proved the existence of infinitely many solutions for the system of only two equations (
\ell=2
) when the coupling parameter
\beta_{12}
is negative, and both equations have a common potential
V_{1}=V_{2}
, which does not enjoy any symmetry properties, but satisfies suitable decay assumptions at infinity. However, nothing is said about the sign of the solutions.

Here we study (
1.1
) in a fully symmetric setting, namely we consider the nonlinear Schrödinger system

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}+V(x)u_{i}=|u_{i}|^{2p-2}u_{i}+\beta\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad u_{i}>0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
N\geq 4
,
1<p<\frac{N}{N-2}
,
\beta<0
and
V\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies the following assumptions for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(V_{1})

V
is radial.

(V_{2})

0<\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)
and
V(x)\to V_{\infty}>0
as
|x|\to\infty
.

(V_{3}^{n})

There exist
C_{0},R_{0}>0
and
\lambda\in(0,2\sin\frac{\pi}{\ell n})
such that

V(x)\leq V_{\infty}-C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}|x|}\qquad\text{for every \ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ \ with \ }|x|\geq R_{0}.

We look for
fully nontrivial
solutions to (
1.2
), i.e., solutions with all components
u_{i}
different from zero.
Set

\|u\|^{2}_{V}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}).

We prove the following results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
V
satisfies
(V_{1})
,
(V_{2})
and
(V_{3}^{n})
. Then the system (
1.2
) has a fully nontrivial solution
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
satisfying

(1.3)

\begin{cases}u_{1}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}z,\theta y)=u_{1}(z,y)&\text{for every \ }\theta\in O(N-2),\\
u_{j+1}(z,y)=u_{1}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n}z,y)&\text{for every \ }j=1,\ldots,\ell-1,\end{cases}

and every
(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This solution has least energy among all nontrivial solutions satisfying (
1.3
). Furthermore, the energy of each component satisfies

\frac{p-1}{2p}\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}<n\,\mathfrak{c}_{\infty},

where
\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}
is the ground state energy of the Schrödinger equation

(1.4)

-\Delta u+V_{\infty}u=|u|^{2p-2}u,\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

As usual,
O(N-2)
denotes the group of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
. The symmetries (
1.3
) of the solutions given by Theorem
1.1
suggests calling them
pinwheel solutions
.

Since the potential
V
is assumed to be radial, using the compactness of the embedding of the subspace of radial functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and following the argument given in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, it is easy to see that the system (
1.2
) has a solution all of whose components are radial. Note however that, if
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) and some component
u_{i}
is radial, then
u_{1}=\cdots=u_{\ell}=:u
and
u
is a nontrivial solution of the equation

-\Delta u+V(x)u=(1+(\ell-1)\beta)|u|^{2p-2}u,\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Therefore, if
1+(\ell-1)\beta\leq 0
, a nontrivial solution to the system (
1.2
) satisfying (
1.3
) cannot be radial. In fact, more can be said. The following result, combined with Theorem (
1.1
), yields multiple positive nonradial solutions when the assumption
(V_{3}^{n})
is satisfied for large enough
n
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\beta\leq-\frac{1}{\ell-1}
and, for some
m,q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\boldsymbol{u}_{m},\boldsymbol{u}_{q}
be solutions to (
1.2
) satisfying (
1.3
) with
n=\ell^{m}
and
n=\ell^{q}
respectively. If
m\neq q
, then
\boldsymbol{u}_{m}\neq\boldsymbol{u}_{q}
.

One may wonder if the solution given by Theorem
1.1
for
\beta\in(-\frac{1}{\ell-1},0)
is radial or not. The following result gives a partial answer in terms of the nonautonomous Schrödinger equation (
1.5
). Namely, if the least energy solutions to this equation that satisfy (
1.6
) are nonradial, then the solutions to the system (
1.2
) satisfying (
1.3
) are nonradial for
\beta
close enough to
0
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
V
satisfies
(V_{1})
,
(V_{2})
and
(V_{3}^{n})
. Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}=(u_{k,1},\ldots,u_{k,\ell})
be a least energy fully nontrivial solution to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) with
\beta=\beta_{k}
. Assume that
\beta_{k}<0
and
\beta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Then, after passing to a subsequence,
u_{k,j}\to u_{0,j}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{0,j}\geq 0
,
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}=(u_{0,1},\ldots,u_{0,\ell})
satisfies (
1.3
),
u_{0,j}
is a nontrivial solution to the equation

(1.5)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=|u|^{2p-2}u,\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and
u_{0,j}
has least energy among all solutions to (
1.5
) satisfying

(1.6)

u(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}z,\theta y)=u(z,y)\quad\text{for every \ }\theta\in O(N-2),\ (z,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Furthermore,

\frac{p-1}{2p}\|u_{0,j}\|^{2}_{V}<n\,\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}.

Next, we describe the behavior of the solutions given by Theorem
1.1
as
\beta\to-\infty
. As shown by Conti, Terracini and Verzini
[
8
,
9
]
and Chang, Lin, Lin and Lin
[
2
]
, there is a connection between variational elliptic systems with strong competitive interaction and optimal partition problems.

We shall call an
\ell
-tuple
(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{\ell})
of nonempty open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
an
(n,\ell)
-pinwheel partition
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
if
\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega_{j}=\emptyset
whenever
i\neq j
and it satisfies following two symmetry conditions:

(S_{1})

\Omega_{j+1}=\{(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}:(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n}z,y)\in\Omega_{1}\}
for each
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.

(S_{2})

If
(z,y)\in\Omega_{1}
then
(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}z,\theta y)\in\Omega_{1}
for every
\theta\in O(N-2)
.

We denote the set of all
(n,\ell)
-pinwheel partitions by
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\ell}
. If
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying
(S_{2})
, a minimizer for

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}}\frac{p-1}{2p}\|u\|_{V}^{2}=:\mathfrak{c}_{\Omega}

on the Nehari manifold

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}:=

\displaystyle\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):u\neq 0,\ \|u\|_{V}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2p},\text{ \ and \ }

\displaystyle u(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}z,\theta y)=u(z,y)\text{ \ for all \ }\theta\in O(N-2)\text{ \ and \ }(z,y)\in\Omega\},

is a
least energy solution
to the problem

(1.8)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u=|u|^{2p-2}u,\qquad u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
u(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}z,\theta y)=u(z,y)\quad\text{for every \ }\theta\in O(N-2),\ (z,y)\in\Omega.\end{cases}

We say that
(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{\ell})
is an
optimal
(n,\ell)
-pinwheel partition
for equation (
1.5
) if
\mathfrak{c}_{\Omega_{j}}
is attained on
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega_{j}}
and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{c}_{\Omega_{j}}=\inf_{(\Theta_{1},\ldots,\Theta_{\ell})\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{c}_{\Theta_{j}}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
V
satisfies
(V_{1})
,
(V_{2})
and
(V_{3}^{n})
. Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}=(u_{k,1},\ldots,u_{k,\ell})
be a least energy fully nontrivial solution to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) with
\beta=\beta_{k}
. Assume that
\beta_{k}\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Then, after passing to a subsequence,

(i)

u_{k,j}\to u_{\infty,j}
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{\infty,j}\geq 0
,
u_{\infty,j}\neq 0
,
u_{\infty,i}u_{\infty,j}=0
if
i\neq j
,
\boldsymbol{u}_{\infty}=(u_{\infty,1},\ldots,u_{\infty,\ell})
satisfies (
1.3
), and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{k}u_{k,j}^{p}u_{k,i}^{p}\to 0\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{whenever }i\neq j.

(ii)

u_{\infty,j}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,  the restriction of
u_{\infty,j}
to the open set
\Omega_{j}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:u_{\infty,j}(x)>0\}
is a least energy solution to the problem (
1.8
) in
\Omega_{j}
, and
(\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{\ell})
is an optimal
(n,\ell)
-pinwheel partition for equation (
1.5
).

(iii)

\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\Omega_{j}=\mathscr{R}\cup\mathscr{S}
, where
\mathscr{R}\cap\mathscr{S}=\emptyset
,
\mathscr{R}
is an
(m-1)
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathscr{S}
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Hausdorff measure
\leq m-2
. Furthermore, if
\xi\in\mathscr{R}
, there exist
i,j
such that

\lim_{x\to\xi^{+}}|\nabla u_{i}(x)|=\lim_{x\to\xi^{-}}|\nabla u_{j}(x)|\neq 0,

where
x\to\xi^{\pm}
are the limits taken from opposite sides of
\mathscr{R}
and, if
\xi\in\mathscr{S}
, then

\lim_{x\to\xi}|\nabla u_{j}(x)|=0\qquad\text{for every \ }j=1,\ldots,\ell.

(iv)

If
\ell=2
, then
u_{\infty,1}-u_{\infty,2}
is a sign-changing solution to equation (
1.5
) satisfying (
1.6
).

Note that
(iii)
implies that the partition exhausts
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\overline{\Omega}_{j}
. Therefore, every
\Omega_{j}
is unbounded.

The regularity properties of optimal partitions have been established, in different settings, for instance in
[
1
,
4
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
and some of the references therein.

Theorem
1.4
establishes the existence of optimal partitions having an additional property: each set of the partition is obtained from any other by means of a linear isometry. Pinwheel partitions are an example of this type of partitions, but others are conceivable. In Section
2
we present a general symmetric variational setting for the system (
1.2
) that produces other examples.

The existence of sign-changing solutions to equation (
1.5
) having the additional property that their negative part is obtained from the positive one by means of a linear isometry and a change of sign has been established in
[
5
]
. This includes those given by Theorem
1.4
(iv)
. The tool for producing this type of solutions is a homomorphism from some group of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto the group with two elements. As shown is Section
2
this tool also serves to get positive solutions of the system (
1.2
) for
\ell=2
with the property that each component is obtained from the other by composition with a linear isometry. The general tool for obtaining a similar result for the system (
1.2
) of
\ell
equations is a homomorphism into the group of permutations of a set of
\ell
elements.

Rather than search for results in the general setting of Section
2
, we decided, for the sake of clarity, to look at pinwheel solutions only. The solutions found in
[
13
,
14
]
for
N=2,3
and
p=2
were of this type. Peng and Wang
[
13
]
focused on the case where the potential
V
is greater than its limit at infinity and, for a system of two equations, they established the existence of pinwheel solutions for
\beta
sufficiently negative. Pistoia and Vaira raised the question of whether solutions exist when
V
is below its limit at infinity and showed in
[
14
]
that the system (
1.2
) has a solution satisfying (
1.3
) for
\beta
close enough to
0
. The energy of each component approaches
n\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}
as
\beta\to 0
.

Our results can be easily extended to dimension
N=2
. In contrast, the dimension
N=3
requires a more delicate analysis because compactness can also be lost by the presence of solutions to the autonomous system (with
V=V_{\infty}
) that travel to infinity; see Remark
3.3
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present the general variational framework and in Section
3
we study the behavior of minimizing sequences of pinwheel solutions for the system (
1.2
). In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2
. Section
5
is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

2
The symmetric variational setting

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of the group
O(N)
of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and for
\ell\geq 2
let
S_{\ell}
be the group of permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
in the obvious way, i.e.,

\sigma(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})=(u_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,u_{\sigma(\ell)})\text{ \ for every \ }\sigma\in S_{\ell},\ (u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.

Let
\phi:G\to S_{\ell}
be a continuous homomorphism of groups.
A function
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
will be called
\phi
-equivariant
if

(2.1)

\boldsymbol{u}(gx)=\phi(g)\boldsymbol{u}(x)\text{ \ for all \ }g\in G,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Note that, if
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is
\phi
-equivariant, then
\boldsymbol{u}
is
K_{\phi}
-invariant, where
K_{\phi}:=\ker(\phi)
.

These data define a
G
-action on
\mathcal{H}:=(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{\ell}
as follows:

(g\boldsymbol{u})(x):=\phi(g)\boldsymbol{u}(g^{-1}x)\text{ \ for every \ }g\in G,\ \boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{H}.

For
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we set

\langle u,v\rangle_{V}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+V(x)uv)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{V}:=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle_{V}}.

The solutions to the system (
1.2
) are the positive critical points of the functional
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{2p}-\frac{\beta}{2p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p},

which is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. Its
i\text{-th}
partial derivative is

\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})v=\langle u_{i},v\rangle_{V}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{2p-2}u_{i}v-\beta\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}v

for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H},\ v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The functional
\mathcal{J}
is
G
-invariant, i.e.,

\mathcal{J}(g\boldsymbol{u})=\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})\quad\text{ \ for every \ }g\in G,\ \boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{H}.

So, by the principle of symmetric criticality
[
17
, Theorem 1.28]
, the critical points of the restriction of
\mathcal{J}
to the
G
-fixed point space of
\mathcal{H}
,

\mathcal{H}^{\phi}:=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H}:g\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{u}\ \forall g\in G\}=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H}:\boldsymbol{u}\text{ is }\phi\text{-equivariant}\},

are critical points of
\mathcal{J}
, i.e., they are the solutions to the system (
1.2
) satisfying (
2.1
). We denote by
\mathcal{J}^{\phi}
the restriction of
\mathcal{J}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\phi}
. Note that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{J}^{\phi})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{v}=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})v_{i}\qquad\text{for any \ }\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi}.

The fully nontrivial critical points of
\mathcal{J}^{\phi}
belong to the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\phi}:=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi}:u_{i}\neq 0,\ \partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})u_{i}=0\ \forall i=1,\ldots,\ell\}.

Observe that

\mathcal{J}^{\phi}(\boldsymbol{u})=\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}\qquad\text{if \ }\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}.

Set

c^{\phi}:=\inf_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}}\mathcal{J}^{\phi}(\boldsymbol{u}).

We consider also the single equation

(2.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=|u|^{2p-2}u,\qquad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G},

where
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u\text{ is }G\text{-invariant}\}
, and we denote by
J:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{M}^{G}
energy functional and the Nehari manifold associated to it, i.e.,

(2.3)

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2p}

and

\mathcal{M}^{G}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G}:u\neq 0,\ \|u\|_{V}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2p}\right\}.

Similarly, we denote by
J_{\infty}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
be the energy functional and the Nehari manifold associated to (
1.4
). Set

(2.4)

\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}}J_{\infty}(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{c}^{G}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}^{G}}J(u).

We shall focus our attention on the following example.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{m}:=\{\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/m}:j=0,\ldots,m-1\}
act on
\mathbb{C}
by complex multiplication and
G_{m}:=\mathbb{Z}_{m}\times O(N-2)
act on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as

\displaystyle\alpha x:=

\displaystyle(\alpha z,y)

\displaystyle\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{m},

\displaystyle\theta x:=

\displaystyle(z,\theta y)

\displaystyle\forall\theta\in O(N-2),\quad x=(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Let
\sigma_{1}\in S_{\ell}
be the cyclic permutation
\sigma_{1}(i):=i+1\mod\ell
, and
\phi_{n}:G_{\ell n}\to S_{\ell}
be the homomorphism given by
\phi_{n}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/\ell n},\theta):=\sigma_{1}
for any
\theta\in O(N-2)
. Then
\boldsymbol{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is
\phi_{n}
-equivariant iff

(u_{1}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/\ell n}z,\theta y),\ldots,u_{\ell}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/\ell n}z,\theta y))=(u_{2}(z,y),\ldots,u_{\ell}(z,y),u_{1}(z,y))

for every
(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
and
\theta\in O(N-2)
, i.e., iff (
1.3
) hold true. Note that every
u_{j}
is
G_{n}
-invariant.

3
The behavior of minimizing sequences

From now on we fix
n
and we take
G_{n}
and
\phi_{n}:G_{\ell n}\to S_{\ell}
as in Example
2.1
. Then, for any
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})v_{i}=\ell\,\partial_{j}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})v_{j}\qquad\text{for any \ }j=1,\ldots,\ell,

and the set
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
is the usual Nehari manifold associated to the functional
\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}:\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}:\boldsymbol{u}\neq 0,\ (\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{u}=0\}.

It has the following properties.

Proposition 3.1
.

(a)

\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}\neq\emptyset
.

(b)

c^{\phi_{n}}\geq\ell\mathfrak{c}^{G_{n}}>0
.

(c)

\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
is a closed
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-submanifold of codimension
1
of
\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
, and a natural constraint for
\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}
.

(d)

If
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
is such that, for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{2p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}>0,

then there exists a unique
s_{\boldsymbol{u}}\in(0,\infty)
such that
s_{\boldsymbol{u}}\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
. Furthermore,

\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(s_{\boldsymbol{u}}\boldsymbol{u})=\max_{s\in(0,\infty)}\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(s\boldsymbol{u}).

(e)

c^{\phi_{n}}\leq\ell n\mathfrak{c_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

The proof is easy. We give the details for the sake of completeness.

(a):
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nontrivial radial function such that
\|\varphi\|_{V}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi|^{2p}
. Set
\xi_{i,j}:=(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(i+\ell j)/\ell n},0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}
and define

\displaystyle u_{R,i+1}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varphi(x-R\xi_{i,j}),\qquad i=0,\ldots,n-1,

where
R>0
is taken large enough so that
u_{R,i}
and
u_{R,j}
have disjoint supports for every
i\neq j
. Then,
\boldsymbol{u}_{R}:=(u_{R,1},\ldots,u_{R,1})\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
.

(b):
Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
. As
\beta<0
we have

0<\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}=\|u_{1}\|_{V}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{1}|^{2p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i}|^{2p}\qquad\forall i=2,\ldots,\ell.

Hence, there exists
s\in(0,1]
such that
su_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{G_{n}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. Therefore,

\ell\mathfrak{c}^{G_{n}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}J(s_{i}u_{i})=\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|s_{i}u_{i}\|^{2}_{V}\leq\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{V}=\mathcal{J}^{\phi}(\boldsymbol{u}).

It follows that
\ell\mathfrak{c}^{G_{n}}\leq c^{\phi_{n}}
.

(c):
The function
\Psi:\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}\smallsetminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\Psi(\boldsymbol{u}):=(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{u}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}=\Psi^{-1}(0)
. It follows from
(b)
that
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
. As

\Psi^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{u}=(2-2p)\ell\,\|u_{1}\|_{V}^{2}\neq 0,

we have that
0
is a regular value of
\Psi
. This shows that
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-submanifold of codimension
1
of
\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
. It also shows that
\boldsymbol{u}\not\in\ker\Psi^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})=:T_{\boldsymbol{u}}\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
, the tangent space of
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
at
\boldsymbol{u}
. Hence,

\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}=T_{\boldsymbol{u}}\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}\oplus\mathbb{R}\boldsymbol{u}.

Since, by definition,
(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{u}=0
for every
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
, we infer that a critical point of the restriction of
\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}
to
\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
is a critical point of
\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}
.

(d):
The proof is straightforward. The number
s_{\boldsymbol{u}}
is

s_{\boldsymbol{u}}=\left(\frac{\|u_{1}\|_{V}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{1}|^{2p}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq 1\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{1}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}}\right)^{1/(2p-2)}.

(e):
Let
\omega
be the least energy positive radial solution to (
1.4
) and set
\xi_{i,j}=(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(i+\ell j)/\ell n},0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Define

\displaystyle w_{R,i+1}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega(x-R\xi_{i,j}),\qquad i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Then
\boldsymbol{w}_{R}=(w_{R,1},\ldots,w_{R,\ell})\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
. If
R
is sufficiently large, statement
(d)
yields
s_{R}\in(0,\infty)
such that
s_{R}\boldsymbol{w}_{R}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
and
s_{R}\to 1
as
R\to\infty
. Using assumption

we obtain

\displaystyle c^{\phi_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(s_{R}\boldsymbol{w}_{R})=\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|s_{R,i}w_{R,i}\|_{V}^{2}\to\ell n\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}\quad\text{as \ }R\to\infty.

This shows that

, as claimed.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}=(u_{k,1},\ldots,u_{k,\ell})\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
be such that
\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\to c^{\phi_{n}}
and
u_{k,i}\geq 0
. Then, after passing to a subsequence, either
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\to\boldsymbol{u}
strongly in
\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
with
u_{i}\geq 0
, or there are points
(z_{k},0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
|z_{k}|\to\infty
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Big\|u_{k,1}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\omega\big(\ \cdot\ -\,(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}z_{k},0)\big)\Big\|=0,

and
c^{\phi_{n}}=\ell n\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}
, where
\omega
is the least energy positive radial solution to (
1.4
).

Proof.

Invoking Ekeland’s variational principle
[
17
, Theorem 8.5]
we may assume that
(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\to 0
in
(\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}
.

Since
\beta<0
, Proposition
3.1
(b)
yields
c_{0}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}>c_{0}\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By Lions’ lemma
[
17
, Lemma 1.21]
there exist
\delta>0
and
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that, after passing to a subsequence,

\int_{B_{1}(x_{k})}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}>\delta\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

For
(x_{k})
we fix a sequence
(\xi_{k})
and a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
|x_{k}-\xi_{k}|\leq C_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying one of the alternatives stated in Lemma
3.2
. Then,

(3.2)

\int_{B_{C_{0}+1}(\xi_{k})}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}\geq\int_{B_{1}(x_{k})}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}>\delta\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

It follows that, either
\xi_{k}=0
, or
\xi_{k}=(\zeta_{k},0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
and
\zeta_{k}\to\infty
. Otherwise, by Lemma
3.2
, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
there would exist
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\in O(N-2)
such that
|\gamma_{i}\xi_{k}-\gamma_{j}\xi_{k}|\geq 2(C_{0}+1)
if
i\neq j
for large enough

and, as
u_{k,1}
is
G_{n}
-invariant, we would have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B_{C_{0}+1}(\gamma_{i}\xi_{k})}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}=m\int_{B_{C_{0}+1}(\xi_{k})}|u_{k,1}|^{2p}>m\delta,

for all

. This is impossible because
(u_{k,1})
is bounded in
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Next, we distinguish two cases.

Case 1.

\xi_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since the sequence
(u_{k,1})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, passing to a subsequence, we have that
u_{k,1}\rightharpoonup u_{1}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{k,1}\to u_{1}
in
L^{2p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{k,1}\to u_{1}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence,
u_{1}\geq 0
and it follows from (
3.2
) that
u_{1}\neq 0
. Note that, as
u_{k,1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G_{n}}
,
u_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G_{n}}
. Set
u_{j+1}(z,y):=u_{1}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n}z,y)
for
(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
,
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
, and
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
. Then,
u_{k,j+1}\rightharpoonup u_{j+1}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and, as
(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\to 0
in

, we derive from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{k\to\infty}\partial_{1}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\varphi=\partial_{1}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u})\varphi\qquad\text{for every \ }\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{G_{n}}.

Hence,
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{n}}
and

\displaystyle c^{\phi_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(\boldsymbol{u})=\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{k,i}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})=c^{\phi_{n}}.

Therefore,
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}\to\boldsymbol{u}
strongly in
\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}
. This shows that, in Case 1, the first alternative stated in Theorem
3.4
holds true.

Case 2.

\xi_{k}=(\zeta_{k},0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
and
\zeta_{k}\to\infty
.

Set

w_{k,i}(x):=u_{k,i}(x+\xi_{k}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell.

Note that
w_{k,i}
is
O(N-2)
-invariant. Since the sequence
(w_{k,i})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, a subsequence satisfies
w_{k,i}\rightharpoonup w_{i}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{O(N-2)}
,
w_{k,i}\to w_{i}
in

and
w_{k,i}\to w_{i}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence,
w_{i}\geq 0
. To simplify notation, set
\alpha:=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}
. Note that, as

if

, we have that

weakly in

. Hence, setting
V_{k}(x):=V(x+\xi_{k})
, Lemma
A.1
gives

\displaystyle\|w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-m}\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|w_{k,i}\circ\alpha^{-m}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{n-1}(w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-j})(\,\cdot\,-\alpha^{j}\xi_{k}+\alpha^{m}\xi_{k})\Big\|_{V_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\Big\|w_{k,i}\circ\alpha^{-m}-\sum_{j=m}^{n-1}(w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-j})(\,\cdot\,-\alpha^{j}\xi_{k}+\alpha^{m}\xi_{k})\Big\|_{V_{k}}^{2}+o(1).

Since

is
G_{n}
-invariant, the change of variable

yields

\displaystyle\Big\|u_{k,i}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{n-1}(w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-j})(\,\cdot\,-\alpha^{j}\xi_{k})\Big\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big\|u_{k,i}-\sum_{j=m}^{n-1}(w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-j})(\,\cdot\,-\alpha^{j}\xi_{k})\Big\|_{V}^{2}+\|w_{i}\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}+o(1),

and iterating this identity we obtain

(3.3)

\|u_{k,i}\|_{V}^{2}=\Big\|u_{k,i}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(w_{i}\circ\alpha^{-j})(\,\cdot\,-\alpha^{j}\xi_{k})\Big\|_{V}^{2}+n\|w_{i}\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}+o(1).

On the other hand, for any given

Recalling that

-invariant and performing the translation

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\widehat{v}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})(v_{k}\circ\alpha^{j})=n\,\partial_{i}\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})v_{k}

\displaystyle=n\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla w_{k,i}\cdot\nabla v+V_{k}(x)w_{k,i}v)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{k,i}|^{2p-2}w_{k,i}v

\displaystyle\qquad-\beta\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{k,j}|^{p}|w_{k,i}|^{p-2}w_{k,i}v\Big).

Note that
\widehat{v}_{k}
is
G_{n}
-invariant. As
(\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}})^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{u}_{k})\to 0
, invoking (
3.1
) and assumption

, and passing to the limit as

we get

(3.4)

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla w_{i}\cdot\nabla v+V_{\infty}w_{i}v)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{i}|^{2p-2}w_{i}v-\beta\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

for every

and

. Since, by (

we see that
w_{1}\neq 0
. Furthermore, equation (

(3.5)

\|w_{1}\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{1}|^{2p}+\beta\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

Corollary 3.5
.

If

, the system (

) has a least energy fully nontrivial solution satisfying (

).

4
Existence of a solution

We define the set of
weak
(n,\ell)
-pinwheel partitions
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{n}_{\ell}:=\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{H}^{\phi_{n}}:u_{i}\neq 0,\,\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}=|u_{i}|_{2p}^{2p},\,u_{i}u_{j}=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ if }i\neq j\},

and set

\widehat{c}^{\phi_{n}}:=\inf_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{W}^{n}_{\ell}}\,\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|u_{i}\|_{V}^{2}.

Our next goal is to give an upper estimate for
\widehat{c}^{\phi_{n}}
. To this end, we choose
\varepsilon\in\big(0,\frac{d_{\ell n}-\lambda}{d_{\ell n}+\lambda}\big)
and a radial function
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq 1-\varepsilon
and
\chi(x)=0
if
|x|\geq 1
. Let
\omega
be the positive least energy radial solution to (
1.4
). For each
r>0
define

\omega_{r}(x):=\chi\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)\omega(x).

Lemma 4.1
.

As
r\to\infty
,

\left|\|\omega\|^{2}-\|\omega_{r}\|^{2}\right|=O(\mathrm{e}^{-2(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{V_{\infty}}r}),\qquad\left||\omega|^{2p}_{2p}-|\omega_{r}|^{2p}_{2p}\right|=O(\mathrm{e}^{-2p(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{V_{\infty}}r}),

where
|\,\cdot\,|_{2p}
denotes the norm in
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

This statements follow easily from the well known estimates
|\omega(x)|=O(|x|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{V_{\infty}}|x|})
and
|\nabla\omega(x)|=O(|x|^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{V_{\infty}}|x|})
, as in
[
7
, Lemma 2]
.
∎

Set
\varrho:=\frac{d_{\ell n}+\lambda}{4}
, and for
R>1
define

\widehat{w}_{1,R}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega_{\varrho R}(x-R(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n},0))\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{1,R}:=t_{R}\widehat{w}_{1,R},

where
t_{R}\in(0,\infty)
is such that
\|w_{1,R}\|_{V}^{2}=|w_{1,R}|_{2p}^{2p}
. Note that
t_{R}\to 1
as
R\to\infty
,
w_{1,R}
is
G_{n}
-invariant and

\text{supp}\big(\omega_{\varrho R}(\,\cdot\,-R(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n},0))\big)\subset\overline{B_{\varrho R}(R(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n},0))}.

Set
w_{j+1,R}(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/\ell n}z,y):=w_{1,R}(z,y)
for
(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
and
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
. Since
\varrho<\frac{d_{\ell n}}{2}
we have that
\text{supp}(w_{i,R})\cap\text{supp}(w_{j,R})=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
. Hence,
\boldsymbol{w}_{R}=(w_{1,R},\ldots,w_{\ell,R})\in\mathcal{W}^{n}_{\ell}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exist
C_{1},R_{1}>0
such that

\frac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|w_{i,R}\|_{V}^{2}=\mathcal{J}^{\phi_{n}}(\boldsymbol{w}_{R})\leq\ell n\mathfrak{c}_{\infty}-C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}R}\quad\text{for all \ }R\geq R_{1}.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{w}_{R}=(w_{1,R},\ldots,w_{\ell,R})\in\mathcal{W}^{n}_{\ell}
, the equality holds true. To prove the inequality note that that
t_{R}\in[\frac{1}{2},2]
for
R
large enough. Assumption
(V_{3}^{n})
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(x)-V_{\infty})|t_{R}\omega_{\varrho R}(x-R(1,0))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{|x|\leq\varrho R}\big(V(x+R(1,0))-V_{\infty}\big)|t_{R}\omega_{\varrho R}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{C_{0}}{4}\int_{|x|\leq\varrho R}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}\,|x+R(1,0)|}|\omega(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-\frac{C_{0}}{4}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}|x|}|\omega(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}R}=:-2C\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\sqrt{V_{\infty}}R}.

Using Lemma
4.1
, for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{k,i}|^{2}\leq(1+s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u_{k,i}\cdot\nabla(u_{k,i}\min\{u^{2s}_{k,i},L^{2}\})

Appendix A
An auxiliary result

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfies
|\xi_{k}|\to\infty
and
V\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies
(V_{2})
. Set
V_{k}(x):=V(x+\xi_{k})
. Then,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{V_{k}}^{2}-\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}-v\|_{V_{k}}^{2}=\|v\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}.

Proof.

As
v_{k}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
one has

\displaystyle\|v\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\|v_{k}\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}-\|v_{k}-v\|_{V_{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v_{k}\|_{V_{k}}^{2}-\|v_{k}-v\|_{V_{k}}^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}-V_{k})v_{k}v-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}-V_{k})v^{2}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
R>0
large enough so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus B_{R}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v|^{2}\leq 2\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus B_{R}}|v|^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Now, take
k_{0}
such that

|V_{\infty}-V(x+\xi_{k})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2|v|_{2}^{2}}=:\delta\quad\text{for every \ }x\in B_{R}\text{ \ and \ }k\geq k_{0}.

Then, for
k\geq k_{0}
we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v|^{2}\leq\int_{B_{R}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus B_{R}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v|^{2}<\varepsilon

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(V_{\infty}-V_{k})v_{k}v|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{\infty}-V_{k}||v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof.
∎

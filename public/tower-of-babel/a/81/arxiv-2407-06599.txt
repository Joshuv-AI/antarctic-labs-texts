Quantum coherence measures for generalized Gaussian wave packets under a Lorentz boost
Arnab Mukherjee, Soham Sen, Sunandan Gangopadhyay

Abstract
In this paper we consider a single particle, spin-momentum entangled state and measure the effect of relativistic boost on quantum coherence. The effect of the relativistic boost on single-particle generalized Gaussian wave packets is studied. The coherence of the wave function as measured by the boosted observer is studied as a function of the momentum and the boost parameter. Using various formulations of coherence, it is shown that in general the coherence decays with the increase in momentum of the state, as well as the boost applied to it. A more prominent loss of coherence due to relativistic boost is observed for a single particle electron than that of a neutron. The analysis is carried out with generalized Gaussian wave packet of the form 𝒩 ​ p n ​ exp ⁡ ( − p 2 σ 2 ) \mathcal{N}p^{n}\exp(-\frac{p^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}) with n n being the “generalization parameter” and 𝒩 \mathcal{N} denoting the appropriate normalization constant. We also obtain a range for parameter n n appearing in the wave packet. The upper bound is found to have a dependence on the mass of the particle and the width of the Gaussian wave packet. We have obtained the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence, l 1 l_{1} and l 2 l_{2} norm measure of coherence, and relative entropy of coherence for a (1+1) and (3+1)-dimensional analysis. Corresponding to each of the cases, we observe that the l 1 l_{1} norm measure of coherence is equal to the Frobenius norm measure of coherence. We have analyzed the scenario for which such a beautiful coincidence can occur. Finally, we have plotted different measures of coherence for the electron as well as the neutron for different values of the width of the wave-function σ \sigma , boost parameter β \beta , and generalization parameter n n .

Now as

\implies C_{F}(\rho)&=2\sqrt{\rho_{\Lambda_{01}}\rho_{\Lambda_{10}}}~.\end{split}

The

III.4
Relative entropy of coherence

Another, quite simple analytical measure of coherence is often given by the relative entropy of coherence which is defined as
[
27
]

C_{\text{Rel}}(\rho_{\Lambda})=S(\rho_{\text{diag}})-S(\rho)

(58)

where
\rho_{\text{diag}}=\sum_{i}\rho_{ii}|i\rangle\langle i|
with
S(\rho_{\text{diag}})
being defined as

\begin{split}S(\rho_{\text{diag}})&=-\text{tr}\left[\rho_{\text{diag}}\ln\left[\rho_{\text{diag}}\right]\right]\\
&=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{i}\ln\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{i}\\
&=-(\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{1}\ln\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{1}+\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{2}\ln\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{2})\end{split}

(59)

where
\lambda^{\text{diag}}_{i}
(for
i\in\{1,2\}
) denotes the eigenvalues of the diagonal reduced density matrix. The von-Neumann entropy corresponding to the reduced density matrix
\rho
takes the form

\begin{split}S(\rho)&=-\text{tr}[\rho\ln\rho]\\
&=-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\ln\lambda_{i}\\
&=-\left(\lambda_{1}\ln\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\ln\lambda_{2}\right)\end{split}

(60)

where
\lambda_{i}
(for
i\in\{1,2\}
) denotes the eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix. For the reduced density matrix in eq.(
40
), we obtain the eigenvalues corresponding to the diagonal reduced density matrix (
\rho_{\Lambda}^{\text{diag}}
) as

\lambda_{1}^{\text{diag}}=\lambda_{2}^{\text{diag}}=\frac{1}{2}~.

(61)

The eigenvalues corresponding to the reduced density matrix in eq.(
40
) as

\lambda_{1}=\mathcal{F},~\lambda_{2}=1-\mathcal{F}~.

(62)

In our analysis,
\mathcal{F}\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}\right)
. We can therefore obtain, the two entropies as

\begin{split}S\left(\rho_{\Lambda}^{\text{diag}}\right)&=-\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right)\\
&=-\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
\implies S^{\text{diag}}&=\ln 2\end{split}

(63)

and

\begin{split}S(\rho_{\Lambda})&=-\mathcal{F}\ln\mathcal{F}-(1-\mathcal{F})\ln(1-\mathcal{F})\\
&\simeq-\mathcal{F}\ln\mathcal{F}-(1-\mathcal{F})(-\mathcal{F})\\
\implies S(\rho_{\Lambda})&\simeq\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{F}\ln\mathcal{F}~.\end{split}

(64)

The relative entropy of coherence using eq.(
58
) then reads

\begin{split}C_{\text{Rel}}(\rho_{\Lambda})&=S\left(\rho^{\text{diag}}_{\Lambda}\right)-S(\rho_{\Lambda})\\
&=\ln 2+\mathcal{F}\ln\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{F}~.\end{split}

(65)

Substituting the analytical form of
\mathcal{F}
in the above expression, we can write down the relative entropy of coherence as

C_{\text{Rel}}(\rho_{\Lambda})=\ln 2+\frac{2n+1}{8}\left(\frac{\cosh\alpha-1}{\cosh\alpha+1}\right)\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}\ln\left[\frac{2n+1}{8}\left(\frac{\cosh\alpha-1}{\cosh\alpha+1}\right)\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}\right]-\frac{2n+1}{8}\left(\frac{\cosh\alpha-1}{\cosh\alpha+1}\right)\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}~.

(66)

IV
Different measure of coherence for spin 1/2 particles under Lorentz boost in (3+1)-dimensions

In this section we shall investigate spin-1/2 particles under Lorentz boost in (3+1)-dimensions. We consider that the particle can move in any arbitrary direction. In this scenario, the representation of the Wigner’s little group takes the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p})&=\frac{(p^{0}+m)\cosh\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)+(\mathbf{p}.\hat{e}))\sinh\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{(p^{0}+m)(p^{0}\cosh\alpha+(\mathbf{p}.\hat{e})\sinh\alpha+m)}}\\

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p}))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}&&\mathcal{E}-i\mathcal{F}\\

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}&\equiv p^{0}+m,~\mathcal{B}\equiv p^{0}\cosh\alpha+p_{z}\sinh\alpha+m,\\

\mathcal{E}&=p_{x}\sinh\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right),~\mathcal{F}=p_{y}\sinh\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right).\end{split}

VI
Discussion and Conclusion

In this work, we consider a single particle entangled state and its quantum coherence under Lorentz boost. At first, we considered a generalized form of the Gaussian wave packet (with its peak at the origin), where the general Gaussian wave function (in momentum space) is multiplied by
n
-th power of the momentum
p
. For a given boost in one direction (
(1+1)
-dimensional consideration) we have obtained different measures of coherence with a dependence on the boost parameter
\beta
, Gaussian width
\sigma
, the mass of the fermion, and the generalization parameter
n
. At first, we have calculated the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence and then we have calculated the
l_{1}
norm measure of coherence for the system. We observe that
C_{F}(\rho_{\Lambda})
comes out to be exactly similar to
C_{l_{1}}(\rho_{\Lambda})
. We find out that such a coincidence can occur if and only if the diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix are
\frac{1}{2}
and the off-diagonal elements are complex-conjugate of each other. In our case the off-diagonal elements are real and equal to each other which results in such a beautiful coincidence. Next, we make use of the condition that these two measures of coherence neither can be greater than unity nor can be less than zero in the
\beta\rightarrow 1
limit. Implementation of these two condition provided us with a bound on the “generalization parameter ”
n
with
0.5
being the lower-bound and
2\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}
in the (1+1) dimensional scenario. Next, we have investigated the
l_{2}
norm measure of coherence and relative entropy of coherence for the above case in consideration. In the next section (section (
IV
)), we have used a
(3+1)
-dimensional model and investigated the similar measures of coherence. Comparing the form of the reduced density matrix in eq.(
87
) with eq.(
40
) we observe that for
n=0
, the two density matrices are identical which is a very important finding in our work. The reduced density matrix is again obtained with equal and real off-diagonal terms and with diagonal terms equal to
\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, according to the equality condition of the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence and
l_{1}
-norm measure of coherence obtained for the (1+1)-d case, this reduced-density matrix again lead us to same analytical forms corresponding to the two measures of coherence. Again claiming that the coherence-measures has to be less than or equal to 1 and greater than or equal to zero, we obtain a range for the
n
-parameter. We find out from eq.(
92
), that the lower bound for
n
is -1.5 whereas the minimum value of the upper bound is equal to
6\left(\frac{\sigma}{m}\right)^{2}-\frac{3}{2}
. It is interesting to see that both of the bounds for
n
for the (3+1)-dimensional case is exactly three-times to the bounds of
n
in the (1+1)-dimensional scenario. The physical reason behind this phenomenon is that the degrees of freedom corresponding to each particle (while considering the spatial components) has exactly tripled in the (3+1)-dimensional scenario than the (1+1)-scenario. We have then obtained the analytical form of the
l_{2}
norm of coherence and relative entropy of coherence. Finally, in section (
V
), we plot different measures of coherence against the width of the Gaussian wave-packet. Primarily, we have considered the results obtained in section (
III
) for the electron and the results in section (4) for the case of neutron. From Figure(
1

V

We also observe that for a relativistic observer there will be a significant amount of loss in the measure of coherence depending on the value of the Lorentz boost where the loss becomes more and more significant for increase in
\beta
. Due to the Wigner rotation
[
41
]
of a single-particle entangled state under Lorentz boost, the spin and momentum degrees of freedom couples with each other which results in loss of coherence when boost in non-zero. We also plot this measure of coherence with respect to the Gaussian width parameter for a fixed value of Lorentz boost and different values of the generalization parameter
n
in the case of an electron in Fig.(

where the

\Lambda\mathbf{p}
is the spatial component of the Lorentz transformed four-momentum.

To determine the structure of the
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}
and the effect of the Lorentz boosts
\Lambda
on a quantum state
|p,\sigma\rangle
, we use two invariant quantities, namely

\displaystyle p^{2}\equiv p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}-E^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m^{2}

\displaystyle p^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E

(101)

Hence, we can use these two invariant quantities

p^{2}

p^{0}

to choose a ‘standard’ 4-momentum
k^{\mu}
that identifies a specific class of quantum states
[
41

k^{\mu}=(m,0,0,0)
. Then, any 4-momenta
p^{\mu}
can be expressed in terms of the standard

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=(L(p)k)^{\mu}=L^{\mu}_{\hskip 5.69046pt\nu}(p)k^{\nu}

(102)

where
L(p)
is a Lorentz transformation that depends on
p^{\mu}
and takes
k^{\mu}\rightarrow p^{\mu}
. Therefore, quantum states
|\mathbf{p},\sigma\rangle
can be defined in terms of the standard momentum state

as

\displaystyle|\mathbf{p},\sigma\rangle=N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

(103)

where
N(p)
is a normalization constant and
\mathbf{k}
is the spatial component of the standard 4-momentum.

Applying a Lorentz boost
\Lambda
on

, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)|\mathbf{p},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\,\mathcal{U}(L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(I)\,\mathcal{U}(\Lambda L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}^{-1}(L(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}(\Lambda L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}(L^{-1}(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}(\Lambda L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}(L^{-1}(\Lambda p)\Lambda L(p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda p))\,\mathcal{U}(W(\Lambda,p))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p}))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle=\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p}))|\mathbf{k},\sigma^{\prime}\rangle

where

On the other hand,
\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda\mathbf{p}))
takes

without

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)|\mathbf{p},\sigma\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda\mathbf{p}))\,\mathcal{U}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p}))|\mathbf{k},\sigma\rangle

(106)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(p)\,\mathcal{U}(L(\Lambda\mathbf{p}))\,\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(W(\Lambda,\mathbf{p}))|\mathbf{k},\sigma^{\prime}\rangle

Free boundary regularity of vacuum states for incompressible viscous flows in unbounded domains
Christophe Prange, Jin Tan

Abstract
. In the well-known book of Lions [ Mathematical topics in fluid mechanics. Incompressible models , 1996], global existence results of finite energy weak solutions of the inhomogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (INS) were proved without assuming positive lower bounds on the initial density, hence allowing for vacuum. Uniqueness, regularity and persistence of boundary regularity of density patches were listed as open problems. A breakthrough on Lions’ problems was recently made by Danchin and Mucha [The incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in vacuum, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. , 72 (2019), 1351–1385] in the case where the fluid domain is either bounded or the torus. However, the case of unbounded domains was left open because of the lack of Poincaré-type inequalities. In this paper, we obtain regularity and uniqueness of Lions’ weak solutions for (INS) with only bounded and nonnegative initial density and additional regularity only assumed for the initial velocity, in the whole-space case ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , d = 2 d=2 or 3 3 . In particular, our result allows us to study the evolution of a vacuum bubble embedded in an incompressible fluid, as well as a patch of a homogeneous fluid embedded in the vacuum, which provides an answer to Lions’ question in the whole-space case.

Introduction

In the present paper, we are concerned with the Inhomogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (INS) in the whole-space

(with

)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\

in the presence of vacuum.
The unknowns are the velocity field

The initial values are prescribed as follows:

It will be assumed that the initial density

[In the case of a density patch

Uniqueness in the absence of vacuum

Let us recall some recent developments on the unique solvability of the inhomogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (
INS

[
34

in the bounded domain case, whenever initial velocity and density are smooth enough and away from vacuum.

After these early works, a number of papers were devoted to the study of strong solutions to system (

(\rho_{0},u_{0})

Concerning strong solutions allowing vacuum, local well-posedness was proved by Choe and Kim

Finally, let us briefly recall the main ideas from

It appears that the only difficult term is

More precisely, if the density contains regions of vacuum one does not have obvious control of
\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}

and this also reveals the lack of lower-order bound for the velocity. However, by taking advantage of the following Desjardins interpolation inequality (see

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\rho|u|^{4}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\cdot\ln^{1/2}\left(e+\frac{\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}}{M^{2}}+\frac{\rho_{*}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}}{\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}^{2}}\right),

which is an improvement of the well-known Ladyzhenskaya inequality, it turns out that the

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|u-\bar{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\quad{\rm with}\quad\bar{u}(t):=\frac{1}{|{\mathbb{T}}^{2}|}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}u(t,x)\,dx,

in our setting, especially for the far-field vacuum in the two-dimensional case. Moreover, the roughness of the density causes additional difficulties for the uniqueness issue. Indeed, we can only perform estimates in low regularity spaces for the following degenerate equation

\underbrace{\rho(\partial_{t}\delta\!u+u\cdot\nabla\delta\!u)}_{\rm degenerate~when~there~is~vacuum}+\nabla\delta P-\Delta\delta\!u=\underbrace{-\delta\!\rho\,\dot{\bar{u}}}_{\rm loss~of~one~derivative~already}-\rho\delta\!u\cdot\nabla\bar{u};

see more remarks in the Subsection

1.3.
A few remarks on the main results

Main novelties

First, our results settle the uniqueness question for the difficult two-dimensional system in the presence of far-field vacuum. Notice that this is a novelty of the whole-space case because in the bounded domain and Torus case treated previously by Danchin and Mucha
[
16
]
there is no far-field vacuum. Notice that the far-field vacuum case is not accessible by the methods of
[
16
]
. Indeed, our work seems to be the first unique solvability result concerning (
INS
) in unbounded domains supplemented with merely bounded densities that can contain vacuum. Moreover, the strategy followed in the current paper maybe adapted to the half-space case
[
19
]
, which remains an interesting problem in the field.

Second, let us stress that in Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, the density is bounded but does not satisfy any smoothness assumption. Notice that this is not the case of other works concerned with the whole-space. For example, in
[
43
]
the initial density satisfies the condition

\bar{x}^{\alpha}\rho_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\quad p>2,

while in
[
9
]
the authors assume that
\rho_{0}\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, with
p>d
,
d=2
or
3
.
These conditions do not allow for non smooth densities with jump discontinuities like patches or vacuum bubbles.

Third, we emphasize that in both theorems, Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, we construct finite-energy weak solutions in the sense of Definition
1.1
. These solutions have additional regularity properties that follow from the assumption that the initial velocity belongs to the spaces
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
or
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. In this framework, we can prove the uniqueness of the solutions that we construct. Notice that we do not rely on Lions’ theorem of existence of finite-energy weak solutions (Theorem
Theorem
above), but prove the existence of the solutions with the properties stated above, see Section
2
for the existence part of Theorem
B
in the three-dimensional case, and see Section
3
for the existence part of Theorem
A
in the two-dimensional case. Finally, let us also remark that condition (
FFV-1
) is not needed neither in 2D nor 3D. In 2D, the far-field vacuum condition (
FFV-1
) is replaced by the condition (
FFV-2
) and (
Compa
).

Some further estimates

We state here certain further boundedness properties for the velocity that can be obtained from the estimates of the paper.

In 3D under (
aNV
) or (
VB
), we already know, see Theorem
B
and Remark
1.2
that
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
on the life-span of the solution. This in combination with the techniques of the paper and the following compatibility assumption

(Compa-3D)

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{0}+\nabla P_{0}=\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g,\quad{\rm for}~g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

gives that
u\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}
on the life-span of the solution.

In 2D under (
aNV
) or (
VB
), we already know, see Theorem
A
and Remark
1.2
that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
. Combining this with the assumption (
Compa
) gives that
u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. In the case when neither (
aNV
) nor (
VB
), we get at best the weighted-boundedness of the velocity

(1.10)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{x}^{-b}{u}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty,

see Proposition
3.8
under (
FFV-2
) and (
Compa
).

1.4.
Main difficulties and strategy for the proofs

First of all, let us point to the main difficulties. Those emerge from the facts that: (i) the density is rough (our analysis includes density patches and vacuum bubbles) and (ii) the density may vanish on some part of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Dealing with the vacuum

If there is vacuum at initial time, the velocity equation is degenerate and it becomes particularly difficult to gain control of the velocity itself. It is important to note here that there is an essential difference between the two- and three-dimensional cases. In the three-dimensional case, see Theorem
B
, we can propagate the assumption that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and hence show that
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{0});\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. Hence
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{0});L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
by Sobolev’s embedding. In the two-dimensional case in the presence of vacuum, the quantity that is naturally controlled by the energy estimate (
1.3
) is
\sqrt{\rho}u
, which generally does not ensure that the velocity
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for some
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Hence, one needs to find a way to control
u
from
\|\sqrt{\rho}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
and
\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
. Such estimates can be proved, see Appendix
A
, under specific assumptions that control the size of the vacuum region and the behavior of the density near the vacuum region. In the most favorable case, i.e. condition (
aNV
) or (
VB
), one can show a direct interpolation estimate for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity in terms of
\|\sqrt{\rho}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
and
\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
, see Proposition
A.6
. In the case of the far-field vacuum assumption, which is the most difficult case handled in our work, one can only show an estimate for weighted
L^{m}
norms of the velocity,
m\geq 2
, see Proposition
A.4
.

The compatibility condition for the far-field vacuum case

A compatibility condition in the spirit of (
Compa
) first appeared in the works
[
9
,
10
]
. Roughly speaking, such a condition boils down to assuming that
\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
at initial time. The compatibility condition was removed in the recent papers
[
36
,
43
]
. In these works the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions is proved. However, without a compatibility condition, one is unable to infer certain information, such as continuity in time, on the velocity near initial time. One barely has time weighted estimates on the velocity.

To handle the two-dimensional far-field vacuum (Theorem
A
(Case 2)), which is the most difficult case, we show persistence of the initial condition (
FFV-2
). Doing so, we can only obtain bounds for
\rho u
and time-weighted estimates of
u
.
Condition (
Compa
) is then crucial to get the space-weighted boundedness of the velocity near initial time, see (
1.10
) and Proposition
3.8
, which in turn is key for the uniqueness in Subsection
4.2
.

The compatibility condition (
Compa
) creates important difficulties when trying to approximate the initial data to construct an approximate sequence of smooth solutions. Our construction in Subsubsection
3.2.1
is inspired by
[
10
]
. However, since we apply existence results for smooth data, we need to further regularize the right-hand-side
\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g
of (
Compa
). We then require that
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
so as to be able to show the appropriate convergence and boundedness properties of the constructed sequence of approximate data.

Outline of the proofs

The general strategy is that of proofs à la Hoff
[
30
]
.

We first get lower-order estimates on the velocity. We give propagate the
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
or the
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
regularity of the initial data for the velocity. In the three-dimensional case, this estimate directly yields a
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{6}_{x}
bound on
u
. In the two-dimensional case, these estimates, in combination with the interpolation estimate of Proposition
A.6
(Case 1 of Theorem
A
) or the weighted interpolation estimate of Proposition
3.8
(Case 2 of Theorem
A
), lead to estimates of the velocity itself.

The next step is to get higher-order estimates on the velocity. We carry out time-weighted estimates of
\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}
. Finally we transfer (shift) the integrability of
\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}
to second-order derivatives of the velocity via maximal regularity estimates for the stationary Stokes system. This enables us to get the crucial Lipschitz estimate for the velocity.

The Lipschitz estimate for the velocity is key to the uniqueness proofs. To show the uniqueness, we carry out a duality proof, which is a new approach in this context. In particular our approach differs from the Lagrangian approach used in
[
16
]
.

1
.

2.
Proof of the existence results in the three-dimensional case, Theorem
B

This section mainly concerns the proof of the existence part in Theorem
B
(3D case).
The idea is to take advantage of classical results to construct smooth approximate solutions without vacuum. After that, we show persistence of
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
regularity and
some time-weighted estimates for time derivatives of the velocity independent of the lower bounds of the density. Those estimates will enable us to shift the regularity to obtain
L^{1}_{t}Lip_{x}
estimates for the velocity, which is important for our proof of uniqueness. Finally, we pass to the limit via classical compactness argument.

Recall that the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfies conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.7
), and that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0.

Thus, we consider smoothed-out initial data

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})~\,\,{\rm with}~\,\,\varepsilon\leq\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\leq 2\rho_{*}u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})~\,\,{\rm with}~\,\,\nabla\cdot u_{0}^{\varepsilon}=0

such that as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

\displaystyle u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}\quad{\rm in}~~\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\quad\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}}u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\,\rightharpoonup\,\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\quad{\rm in}~~L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})

and

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\rho_{0}\quad{\rm weak*~in}~~L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Then, by the strong solution theory (e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 0.2]
), we know that there exists a local-in-time strong solution
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
to (
INS
).

2.1.
Uniform estimates

In what follows, we focus on uniform estimates for the approximate solutions
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
.
We often use the following Sobolev embedding

(2.1)

\|z\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\quad{\rm for}~~z\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

with a universal positive constant
C
. For notational simplicity, we omit the superscript
\varepsilon
and write the solution
(\rho,u)
.

2.1.1.
Persistence of
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
regularity for the velocity

Proposition 2.1

(gradient estimate)
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be a smooth enough solution to system (
INS
) on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.
There exist positive universal constants
c_{0},C
such that if

(2.2)

\rho_{*}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq c_{0}

then it holds for all
t\in[0,T_{*})

(2.3)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|(\sqrt{\rho}{u}_{t},\Delta u)(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\|P(s,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\\
\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\exp(C\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}).

If (
2.2
) is not satisfied, then (
2.3
) holds true on
[0,T]
provided that

(2.4)

T\leq\frac{c}{\rho_{*}^{3}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}}\quad{\rm for~some~universal~constant}~c.

Proof.

In order to estimate the second derivative of
u
and the gradient of the pressure, we rewrite the momentum equation of (
INS
) in the form

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\nabla P=-\rho\dot{u}\hskip 33.28966pt{\rm in}~(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\
&\nabla\cdot u=0\hskip 85.07388pt{\rm in}~(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\\
&u\to 0,\quad{\rm as}~|x|\to\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying the standard
L^{2}
-estimate to (
2.5
) yields that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq C\Big({\rho_{*}}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\rho_{*}^{2}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\Big).

Testing the momentum equation of (
INS
) against
{u_{t}}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\rho u\cdot\nabla u,u_{t}\rangle.

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\rho_{*}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

Putting the above inequality together with (
2.6
) implies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho_{*}}\|(\sqrt{\rho}{u}_{t},\Delta u,\nabla P)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\rho_{*}\|u\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\rho_{*}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{3}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\rho_{*}^{3}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{6}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{*}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2},

in which inequality (
2.1
) and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
A.1
) were used.
Therefore, we see that whenever
T
satisfies
C\rho_{*}^{3}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds<1,
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq\frac{\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}}{1-C\rho_{*}^{3}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds}\quad{\rm for~all}~t\in[0,T].

Now, the energy inequality (
1.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2C\rho_{*}^{3}}\quad{\rm for~any}~t\geq 0

provided that
(\sqrt{\rho_{*}})^{3}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq c_{0}
for small enough
c_{0}.
Using inequality (
2.1
) again, we get (
2.3
).

In the case when the smallness condition is not satisfied, we get from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}\leq\frac{\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}}{1-2C\rho_{*}^{3}t\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}},

which eventually enables us to finish the proof of the second statement.
∎

Remark 2.2

(boundedness in space of the velocity)
.

Notice that thanks to inequality (
2.3
) we also have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}\exp(C\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}).

2.1.2.
Estimates of the time derivative

Here, we want to bound time derivative
\sqrt{\rho t}{u}_{t}
in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
and
\sqrt{t}\nabla{u}_{t}
in the space
L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),

since it is an important step towards the proof of existence and higher-order spatial estimates for the velocity.

Proposition 2.3

(time derivative estimates)
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be a smooth enough solution to system (
INS
) on
[0,T_{*})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.
Then for all
T\in[0,T_{*})
it holds

(2.8)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sqrt{\rho t}{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\sqrt{t}{u}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\,ds\leq C_{0,T},

where
C_{0,T}
is a constant depending only on
T,\rho_{*}
and norms
\|\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Proof.

At first, applying the time derivative
\partial_{t}
to the momentum equation in system (
INS
) and multiplying the resulting equation by
\sqrt{t}
yields

(2.9)

\rho\big(\partial_{t}(\sqrt{t}{u}_{t})+u\cdot\nabla(\sqrt{t}{u}_{t})\big)-\Delta(\sqrt{t}{u}_{t})+\nabla(\sqrt{t}{P}_{t})\\
=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}}\rho u_{t}-\sqrt{t}\rho_{t}u_{t}-\sqrt{t}\rho_{t}u\cdot\nabla u-\sqrt{t}\rho u_{t}\cdot\nabla u.

Taking the
L^{2}
scalar product with
\sqrt{t}{u}_{t}
, we get

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\sqrt{\rho t}{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}{u}_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=A_{1}+\dots+A_{4}

where

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2},

\displaystyle A_{2}:=-\langle t\rho_{t},|u_{t}|^{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle A_{3}:=-\langle\sqrt{t}\rho_{t}u\cdot\nabla u,\sqrt{t}{u}_{t}\rangle,

\displaystyle A_{4}:=-\langle\sqrt{t}\rho u_{t}\cdot\nabla u,\sqrt{t}{u}_{t}\rangle.

In order to estimate
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},A_{4},
we proceed as follows.
Noticing that

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}(\dot{u}-u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+\rho_{*}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}.

Using the equation
\dot{\rho}=\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,
we write

\displaystyle A_{2}=-\langle t\rho u,\nabla(|u_{t}|^{2})\rangle

and

\displaystyle|A_{2}|\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\rho_{*}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+C\rho_{*}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}.

Similarly, we write

\displaystyle A_{3}=-\langle t\rho u,\nabla[(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot u_{t}]\rangle.

and decompose

\displaystyle|A_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\langle|t\rho u|,|\nabla u|^{2}\,|u_{t}|\rangle+\langle|t\rho u|,|u|\,|\nabla^{2}u|\,|u_{t}|\rangle+\langle|t\rho u|,|u|\,|\nabla u|\,|\nabla u_{t}|\rangle

\displaystyle=:A_{31}+A_{32}+A_{33}.

Thanks to inequality (
2.1
) and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle A_{31}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{*}\sqrt{T}\|\sqrt{t}u_{t}\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{*}\sqrt{T}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+C\rho_{*}^{2}{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}.

As for
A_{32},A_{33}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{32}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\rho_{*}T}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}+\rho_{*}T\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}

and

\displaystyle A_{33}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{*}\sqrt{T}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+C\rho_{*}^{2}{T}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{4}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}.

To handle
A_{4},
we use inequality (
2.1
) and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
A.1
) to get

\displaystyle|A_{4}|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{*}}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\sqrt{t}u_{t}\|_{L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{*}}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\|\nabla(\sqrt{t}u_{t})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}+C{\rho_{*}}\|\sqrt{\rho t}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

By putting all the above estimates into (
2.10
), we find that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq\|t^{-1/2}\delta\!\rho\bar{x}^{\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\|\sqrt{t}\nabla(\bar{x}^{-\beta}\dot{\bar{u}}\cdot v)\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{D}(T)\|\sqrt{t}\nabla(\bar{x}^{-\beta}\dot{\bar{u}}\cdot v)\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}.

Using inequalities (
3.30
), (
3.31
) and
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla(\bar{x}^{-\beta}\dot{\bar{u}}\cdot v)|\leq C(|\nabla\dot{\bar{u}}||\bar{x}^{-\beta}v|+|\dot{\bar{u}}\bar{x}^{-\beta}||\nabla v|+|\dot{\bar{u}}\bar{x}^{-\beta}||v\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\beta}|),

which implies that

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{t}\nabla(\bar{x}^{-\beta}\dot{\bar{u}}\cdot v)\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{t}\nabla\dot{\bar{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\|\bar{x}^{-\beta}v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}+\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\bar{u}}\bar{x}^{-\beta}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\quad+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sqrt{t}\dot{\bar{u}}\bar{x}^{-\beta}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\|v\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}

So by estimate (
4.31
) we can bound the right-hand-side of (
4.32
) and get

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\delta\!u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leq CT^{\frac{\beta}{6}}\big(\bar{D}(T)\|\sqrt{\rho}\delta\!u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\delta\!u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\big).

Clearly, the above inequality implies that, if
T
is small enough then

(4.33)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}\delta\!u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq C\bar{D}(T)T^{\frac{\beta}{6}}.

Putting the above inequality into (
4.30
), we get for
T
small enough

\displaystyle\bar{D}(T)\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\bar{D}(t)\left(\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\bar{u}\langle x\rangle^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+|\ln\bar{D}(t)|-\ln t\right)\,dt\right).

From Osgood’s lemma
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
, we then infer that
\bar{D}(t)\equiv 0
on
[0,T],
and thus
\sqrt{\rho}\delta\!u\equiv 0,~\delta\!\rho\equiv 0
on
[0,T]
thanks to inequality (
4.33
)
and equation (
4.19
), respectively.
Finally,
\delta\!u\equiv 0
and
\delta\!\rho\equiv 0
on
[0,\infty)
can be concluded similarly as previous subsection. In particular, one needs to use the functional inequality (
3.28
).
∎

To complete the proof of uniqueness part of Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
, it suffices to observe that all the assumptions in Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
are satisfied by those solutions constructed in Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
.∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
C

We remark that the general strategy is the same in dimensions
d=2
and
3
.
Assume that
\partial\Omega_{0}
corresponds to the level set
\{f_{0}=0\}
of some
\mathcal{C}^{1,\gamma}
function
f_{0}:\Omega_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}.
Then we know that
\partial\Omega_{t}=X(t,f^{-1}_{0}(\{0\}))
corresponds to the level set
\{f_{t}=0\}
with
f_{t}:=f_{0}\circ(X(t,\cdot))^{-1},
where
(X(t,\cdot))^{-1}
is the inverse function of
X(t,\cdot)
. Indeed, as the flow is incompressible, the Jacobian of
X
is identically equal to
1
. Hence the classical inverse function theorem ensures the existence and regularity of
X^{-1}.

Now, fix some
T>0.

In the 2D case, according to Theorem
A
, for all
r\in[2,\infty)

we can find
q>2
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq\|\nabla^{2}(\sqrt{t}u)\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}\|{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(0,T)}\leq C_{0,T}

and by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
A.1
) and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))})\leq C_{0,T}.

By Sobolev’s embedding, one has
\nabla u\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathcal{C}^{0,\gamma})
for all
\gamma\in(0,1).
Consequently, the flow
X(t,\cdot)
is
\mathcal{C}^{0,\gamma}
and so is
f_{t}.

For the 3D case, Theorem
B
ensures that for all
r\in[2,6)
,
\nabla u\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
and we can find
q>2
such that
\nabla^{2}(\sqrt{t}u)\in{L^{q}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}.
Thus
\nabla u\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).
This finally implies that
f_{t}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{0,\gamma}
function, if
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}.
∎

Appendix A
Functional spaces and inequalities

For the reader’s convenience, we here recall a few results
that are used repeatedly in the paper. Let us first recall
the definitions of homogeneous Sobolev spaces.

Definition A.1

(homogeneous Sobolev space)
.

Let
s
be in
\mathbb{R}.
The homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of tempered distributions
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{d},
with Fourier transform in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
satisfying

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}:=\||\xi|^{s}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\infty.

We often use the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities.

Proposition A.2

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities;
[
26
, Lemma II.3.3]
)
.

If
(q,r)\in(1,\infty)^{2}
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
q
and
r
such that

(A.1)

\|z\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla z\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\theta}\|z\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-\theta}

with
\frac{1}{p}=\theta\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{d}\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q},~0\leq\theta\leq 1.

Let us now recall some properties of the spaces
\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
that are used in the paper. For more details, see Appendix A and B of
[
42
]
.
The first one is the following weighted estimate for elements of the space
\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

Lemma A.3

(
[
42
, Theorem B.1]
)
.

For
m\in[2,\infty)
and
\ell\in(1+\frac{m}{2},\infty),
there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
m
and
l
such that for all
z\in\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

(A.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|z|^{m}}{{\langle x\rangle}^{2}}\,(\log\langle x\rangle)^{-\ell}\,dx\right)^{1/m}\leq C\left(\|z\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right).

Then one has the following Proposition due to Li and Xin
[
37
]
, which is a combination of Lemma
A.3
with the Poincaré inequality; see
[
37
]
, (2.6) and (2.8) in the proof of Lemma 2.4 therein.

Proposition A.4

(
[
37
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
m\in[2,\infty)
and
\ell\in(1+\frac{m}{2},\infty)
. Let
z\in\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\eta\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Assume that
\sqrt{\eta}z\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta\leq\eta^{*},\quad M\leq\int_{B_{R}}\eta\,dx,

for positive constants
\eta^{*},M,R.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
m,\ell,\eta^{*},M,R
such that

(A.3)

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|z|^{m}}{{\langle x\rangle}^{2}}\,(\log\langle x\rangle)^{-\ell}\,dx\right)^{1/m}\leq C(\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}).

We also needed the following conditional
L^{2}
bound for elements of the space
\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

Lemma A.5

(
[
42
, Lemma B.1 and Remark B.1]
)
.

\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is a Hilbert space for the scalar product
\langle\nabla z,\nabla w\rangle+\langle\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}}\,z,\,w\rangle
and an equivalent norm is given by
\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+|\int_{B_{1}}z\,dx|.
Moreover, we have
\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap(L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})+L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\hookrightarrow H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),
more precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
z\in\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
satisfying
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
with
z_{1}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\,z_{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

(A.4)

\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\leq C\left(\|z_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|z_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right).

A similar result holds for all
z\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}).
There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
z\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfying
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
with
z_{1}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\,z_{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})

(A.5)

\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\left(\|z_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{2}{5}}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{5}}+\|z_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right).

Then based on Lemma
A.5
, we have

Proposition A.6

(interpolation estimate)
.

Let
d=2,\ 3
. For all
z\in\widetilde{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
or
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and all non-negative function
\eta\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that satisfies either

(A.6)

(1/\eta)\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad{\rm for~some}~\delta_{0}>0,

or

(A.7)

(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad{\rm for~some}~\underline{\eta}\in(0,\infty),\,\,p\in(d/2,\infty),

there exists a constant
C_{*}
depending on
d
,
\delta_{0}
and
\|({1}/{{\eta}})\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{1}}
in the case when (
A.6
) is satisfied,
p
,
d
,
\bar{\eta}
and
\|(\bar{\eta}-\eta)_{+}\|_{L^{p}}
in the case when (
A.7
) is satisfied,
such that

(A.8)

\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C_{*}(\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}).

Proof.

We first consider
\eta
satisfying (
A.7
). It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\underline{\eta}}\leq\sqrt{(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}}+\sqrt{\eta}.

We then write by Hölder’s inequality, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in Proposition
A.2
and Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\underline{\eta}}\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}}\,z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|z\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}+\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-\frac{d}{2p}}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{d}{2p}}+\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\underline{\eta}-\eta)_{+}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{d}{p}}\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\frac{\sqrt{\underline{\eta}}}{2}\|z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

which enables us to obtain (
A.8
).

If (
A.6
) is satisfied, we decompose

\displaystyle z=z\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}+z\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta\geq\delta_{0}}

and write that

\displaystyle\|z\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta\geq\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}\|\sqrt{\eta}z\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta\geq\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|z\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|({1}/{\sqrt{\eta}})\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\eta}z\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\|({1}/{{\eta}})\,\mathbf{1}_{\eta<\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We complete the proof by using Lemma
A.5
and Young’s inequality.∎

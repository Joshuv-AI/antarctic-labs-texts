Characterization and Analysis of Generalized Grey Incomplete Gamma Noise
W. Bock, L. Cristofaro, [.3cm] Rheinland-Pfälzische Technische Universität Kaiserslautern-Landau,Fachbereich Mathematik,Gottlieb-Daimler-Straße 48,67663 Kaiserslautern, GermanyE-Mail: bock@mathematik.uni-kl.de, [.2cm] Sapienza University of Rome, Italy E-Mail: lorenzo.cristofaro@uniroma1.it

Abstract
The grey incomplete gamma distributions was established by one of the authors in a previous publication. In this article we use the Kondratiev characterization theorem to identify those via a suitable Laplace transform with holomorphic functions with suitable properties. We establish theorems for the integration and convergence of sequences of these distributions. As direct applications of these analytic tools we give the examples of Donsker’s delta function, the local time, identify the time-derivative of the process as a suitable distribution and define the Gamma grey Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

By the results in

P1

For

L^{1}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, as in Lemma
3.1
.

We can apply dominated convergence to the
T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}
-transform of
\Phi_{n}
, so that for
\xi\in U_{p,q}
we have

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-isa}\Gamma(\rho,\theta+S(s,\eta,\xi))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-isa}\Gamma(\rho,\theta+\frac{1}{2}(s^{2}\langle\eta,\eta\rangle+\langle\xi,\xi\rangle+2s\langle\eta,\xi\rangle))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\rho,\theta}(r)e^{-\frac{1}{2}r\langle\xi,\xi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}rs^{2}\langle\eta,\eta\rangle-s(r\langle\xi,\eta\rangle+ia)}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\rho,\theta}(r)r^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}r\langle\xi,\xi\rangle}e^{\frac{r\langle\xi,\eta\rangle^{2}}{2\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}+\frac{ia\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}{\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}-\frac{a^{2}}{2\langle\eta,\eta\rangle r}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{ia\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}{\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rk}f_{\rho,\theta}(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2r\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{ia\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}{\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}\Gamma(1-\rho)\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-rk-\frac{a^{2}}{2\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}r^{-1}}r^{-3/2}\frac{e^{-\theta r}}{(r-1)^{\rho}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\overset{*}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{ia\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}{\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}\Gamma(1-\rho)\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(u+1)(k+\theta)-\frac{a^{2}}{2\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}(u+1)^{-1}}(u+1)^{-3/2}u^{-\rho}\mathrm{d}u

where
k=\frac{1}{2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle-\frac{\langle\xi,\eta\rangle^{2}}{2\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}\geq 0
and with
u=r-1
.

We use the analyticity of the function
\exp(k_{1}(u+1)^{-1})
for
u>0
in order to represent
T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))(\xi)
as a series.

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}k_{1}^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u(k+\theta)}(u+1)^{-n-3/2}u^{-\rho}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\overset{**}{=}

\displaystyle k_{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}k_{1}^{n}}{n!}\Psi(1-\rho,\frac{1}{2}-n-\rho;k+\theta).

where we use 1 of 2.1.3 in
[
33
]
in
**
and
\Psi(a,b;z)
is the Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function,
k_{1}=\dfrac{a^{2}}{2\|\eta\|^{2}}
and
k_{2}=\dfrac{e^{-k-\theta}e^{\frac{ia\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}{\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}
.

For
\xi=0
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\frac{a^{2n}}{2^{n}\|\eta\|^{2n}}\Psi(1-\rho,\frac{1}{2}-n-\rho;\theta).

For
\xi=0
, we can represent the expectation of Donsker’s Delta as:

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(\delta_{a}(\langle\cdot,\eta\rangle))(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\rho,\theta}(r)r^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2r\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2r\langle\eta,\eta\rangle}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\eta,\eta\rangle r}}f_{\rho,\theta}(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta_{a}(x)p(x;\eta,\rho,\theta)\mathrm{d}x

where
p(x;\eta,\rho,\theta)
is the density of a centered Gaussian r.v. with variance
R\|\eta\|^{2}
and
R
is a non negative random variable with density
f_{\rho,\theta}
.
∎

Indeed, for
\rho=1
, we have that
\Psi(0,\frac{1}{2}-n;\theta)=1
(see Equation 13.6.3 in
[
10
]
) and
\Gamma(1,\theta)=e^{-\theta}
.

4
Grey Incomplete Gamma Noise

In order to present the Gamma Grey Brownian Motion, we introduce the fractional operator
M_{-}^{\alpha/2}
defined, for
any
f\in\mathcal{S}
, as

M_{-}^{\alpha/2}f:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sqrt{C_{\alpha}}D_{\_}^{(1-\alpha)/2}f,\qquad\alpha\in(0,1)\\
f,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\alpha=1\\
\sqrt{C_{\alpha}}I_{\_}^{(\alpha-1)/2}f,\qquad\alpha\in(1,2)\end{array}\right.,

where

D_{\_}^{\beta}\,f(x):=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}x}\int_{x}^{\infty}f(t)(t-x)^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;\beta\in(0,1),

is the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative and

I_{\_}^{\beta}\,f(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{x}^{\infty}f(t)(t-x)^{\beta-1}\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;\beta\in(0,1),

is the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral, see
[
11
]
.

In a similar way, we define the fractional operator
M_{+}^{\alpha/2}
, which is defined by means of the lower Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative and the lower Riemann-Liouville fractional integral.

The tempered
\Gamma
-grey Brownian motion (hereafter
\Gamma

-GBM) is defined as
B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t,\omega):=\left\langle\omega,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\right\rangle
for
t\geq 0
and a.e.-
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
where
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\rho\in(0,1]
and
\theta>0
.

Thus, for
\xi\in\mathcal{U}_{\theta/2}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\theta}
, we can apply the
S
-transform on it and obtain

\displaystyle S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t,\cdot))(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\langle\omega,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle e^{\langle\omega,\xi\rangle}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{-1}_{\nu}(\xi)\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}e^{\langle\omega,\xi\rangle+s\langle\omega,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle}\Big|_{s=0}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{-1}_{\nu}(\xi)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{\langle\omega,\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\Big|_{s=0},

(9)

where we can exchange the integral and the derivative due fact that the integral in line (
7
) is finite due to Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,
\Gamma

-GBM is in
L^{2}(\nu_{\rho,\theta})
and the Laplace transform (
2
) is finite for
\xi\in\mathcal{U}_{\theta/2}
.

We have that
\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{\langle\omega,\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}\Gamma(\rho,\theta+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\|^{2})
, such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{\langle\omega,\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(d\omega)

\qquad\qquad=-e^{-(\theta+1/2\|\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\|^{2})}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}\Big(s\|M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\|^{2}+\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle\Big),

thus

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}e^{\langle\omega,\xi+sM_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle}\nu_{\rho,\theta}(d\omega)\Big|_{s=0}=-e^{-(\theta+\|\xi\|^{2})}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}\Big(\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle\Big).

Hence, we get

S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t,\cdot))(\xi)

\qquad\qquad=\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}(-e^{-(\theta+\|\xi\|^{2})})\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}\Big(\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle\Big).

(10)

Note that for
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle=\langle M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi,\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi(x)dx
, see (5.17) in
[
34
]
.

Now we establish that the
\Gamma
-GBM is the differentiable in
(\mathcal{S})^{-1}_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}
and the existence of its noise in
(\mathcal{S})^{-1}_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}
is guaranteed applying Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\rho\in(0,1]
and
\theta>0
.
We define the Grey Incomplete Gamma Noise in
(\mathcal{S})^{-1}_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}
as,

N_{t}^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(\omega):=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t+h,\omega)-B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t,\omega)}{h},\quad\text{a.e.-}\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

and for every
\xi\in U_{\theta}
we have

S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(N_{t}^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}\big)(\xi)=-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-\theta-\|\xi\|^{2}}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}(M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi)(t).

Proof.

For
t\geq 0
, we define the sequence

\mathscr{N}_{t,n}(\omega):=\frac{B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t+h_{n},\omega)-B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t,\omega)}{h_{n}}\in(\mathcal{S})^{-1}_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}

for a.e.-
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are such that
h_{n}\to 0
for
n\to\infty
.

We apply the
S
-transform for
\xi\in U_{\theta}

\displaystyle S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(\mathscr{N}_{t,n}\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{n}}\Big(S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t+h_{n}))(\xi)-S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}(B_{\alpha,\rho}^{\theta}(t))(\xi)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-\theta-\|\xi\|^{2}}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}\frac{\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[t+h_{n},t)}\rangle}{h_{n}}.

By the fact that

h_{n}^{-1}|\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[t+h_{n},t)}\rangle|\leq h_{n}^{-1}\int_{t}^{t+h_{n}}|M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi(x)|dx\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(|M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi(x)|)

and that for
\xi\in U_{\theta}

|-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-\theta-\|\xi\|^{2}}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}|<e^{-\theta}\theta^{\rho-1}

we have that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\xi\in U_{\theta}

|S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(\mathscr{N}_{t,n}\big)(\xi)|<e^{-\theta}\theta^{\rho-1}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(|M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi(x)|)<\infty,

we note that
M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}
.
So we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(\mathscr{N}_{t,n}\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-\theta-\|\xi\|^{2}}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}\frac{\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[t+h_{n},t)}\rangle}{h_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-\theta-\|\xi\|^{2}}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}(M_{+}^{\alpha/2}\xi)(t).

Thus,
\{S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(\mathscr{N}_{t,n}\big)(\xi)\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence for each
\xi
.

By the fact that
S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\big(\mathscr{N}_{t,n}\big)(\xi)
is holomorphic on
U_{\theta}
and it is finite for each
\xi\in U_{\theta}
, we can apply Theorem
2.4
for the convergence of
\mathscr{N}_{t,n}
to
\mathscr{N}_{t}
.
∎

5
Grey Incomplete Gamma Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process

We now compute the solution of Langevin Equation driven by Gamma Grey Brownian motion:

dX^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)=-\lambda X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)dt+\kappa dB^{\theta}_{\alpha,\rho}(t),\quad X_{0}^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}=x_{0},

(11)

where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in\ \mathbb{R}
.

We consider the weak integral form of Equation (
11
):

X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)=x_{0}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(s)ds+\kappa B^{\theta}_{\alpha,\rho}(t)

(12)

and we solve it applying the S-transform for
\xi\in U_{\theta}
and using Equation (
10
):

S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\Big(X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)\Big)(\xi)=x_{0}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\Big(X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(s)\Big)(\xi)ds+\kappa C_{\rho,\theta}(\xi)\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle

where

C_{\rho,\theta}(\xi):=-\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2})}e^{-(\theta+\|\xi\|^{2})}\Bigg(\theta+\frac{\|\xi\|^{2}}{2}\Bigg)^{\rho-1}.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have that
x(s):=S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\Big(X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(s)\Big)(\xi)
is an holomorphic function, so we can differentiate it:

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=-\lambda x(t)+kC_{\rho,\theta}(\xi)\frac{d}{dt}\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle

and solve it:

\displaystyle S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\Big(X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)\Big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{0}e^{-\lambda t}+\kappa C_{\rho,\theta}(\xi)\Big(\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\rangle-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\langle\xi,M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[0,s)}\rangle ds\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{*}{=}

\displaystyle x_{0}e^{-\lambda t}+\kappa C_{\rho,\theta}(\xi)\langle\xi,h_{\alpha,t}\rangle,

where we used the linearity of inner product and changing the order of integration in
*
and

h_{\alpha,t}(u)=\left(M_{-}^{\alpha/2}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\right)(u)-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\left(M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[0,s)}\right)(u)ds.

By considering that
S_{\nu_{\rho,\theta}}\Big(\langle\cdot,f\rangle\Big)(\xi)=\langle\xi,f\rangle
for
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
and that we can express
h_{\alpha,\lambda}
as the limit of functions in Schwartz for density, we can invert the S-transform and we get:

X^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t,\omega)=x_{0}e^{-\lambda t}+\kappa B^{\theta}_{\alpha,\rho}(t,\omega)-\lambda\kappa\langle\omega,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}M_{-}^{\alpha/2}1_{[0,s)}ds\rangle.

Definition 5.1
.

The solution of the Langevin Equation (
11
) is called ”Grey Incomplete Gamma Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process” or
\Gamma
-gOU and its characteristic function for
t\geq 0
is

\mathbb{E}\big(e^{iwX^{\alpha,\rho,\theta}(t)}\big)=\exp^{iwx_{0}e^{-\lambda t}}\frac{\Gamma(\rho,\theta+\frac{w^{2}}{2}\|h_{\alpha,t}\|^{2})}{\Gamma(\rho,\theta)}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1)
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Re(z)>0
, then

|\Gamma(\rho,z)|<\frac{1}{\cos(\arg(z))^{\rho}}\Gamma(\rho,\Re(z)).

Proof.

By definition in
[
10
]
, we have that

\Gamma(\rho,z)=\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\rho-1}dt

such that the integral domain does not cross the real negative axis.

We choose as integral domain the line
\mathcal{L}_{z}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}|w=re^{i\arg(z)},\,r\in(|z|,+\infty)\}
.

Applying
u=te^{-i\arg(z)}
in what follows we have

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\rho,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}|e^{-ue^{i\arg(z)}}||(ue)^{i\arg(z)}|^{\rho-1}du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z|}^{\infty}|e^{-u\cos(\arg(z))}||u|^{\rho-1}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos(\arg(z))^{\rho}}\int_{|z|\cos(\arg(z))}^{\infty}e^{-y}|y|^{\rho-1}dy,

where in the last equality we use
y=u\cos(\arg(z))
.

To conclude, we note that
\Re(z)=\Re(|z|e^{i\arg(z)})=|z|\cos(\arg(z))
.
∎

We note that for
\eta\in L^{2}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in U_{p,q}
,

\Re(\theta+S(s;\eta,\xi))=\theta+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{1}+s\eta\|^{2}-\|\xi_{2}\|^{2}>0.

Hence, we can apply the above Lemma and note that

\arg(\theta+S(s;\eta,\xi))=\arctan\left(\frac{2\langle\xi_{1}+s\eta,\xi_{2}\rangle}{\theta+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{1}+s\eta\|^{2}-\|\xi_{2}\|^{2}}\right)\subset[-M,M],

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
where
M\in(0,\pi/2)
. So we have that there exists
C_{\rho}>0
such that

\frac{1}{\cos(\arg(z(s)))^{\rho}}<C_{\rho},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

where
z(s)=\theta+S(s;\eta,\xi)
. We can also use
C_{\rho}:=\frac{1}{\cos(M)^{\rho}}
.

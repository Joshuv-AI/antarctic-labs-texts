Generic Properties of Hitchin Representations
Hongtaek Jung

Abstract
. Let G G be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint group whose Weyl group contains − 1 -1 , let λ \lambda be the Jordan projection of G G , and let S S be a closed orientable surface of genus at least 2. For a G G -Hitchin representation ρ \rho , we define the set J ⁡ ( ρ ) := { λ ⁡ ( ρ ⁡ ( x ) ) | x ∈ π 1 ​ ( S ) ∖ { 1 } } J(\rho):=\{\lambda(\rho(x))\,|\,x\in\pi_{1}(S)\setminus\{1\}\} . Choose any hyperplane H H in the maximal abelian subalgebra of the Lie algebra of G G . Our main result shows that, for a generic G G -Hitchin representation ρ \rho , we have J ⁡ ( ρ ) ∩ H = ∅ J(\rho)\cap H=\emptyset . As an application, we prove that generic orbifold Hitchin representations are strongly dense. This extends the result of Long, Reid, and Wolff for the Hitchin representations of surface groups. Our theorem also shows that the split real forms of many simple adjoint Lie groups contain strongly dense orbifold fundamental groups, partially generalizing the work of Breuillard, Guralnick, and Larsen.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group and let
S
be a closed orientable surface of genus at least 2. The
G
-Hitchin component, introduced by Hitchin
[
Hit92
]
, has been intensively studied as one of the prime examples of higher Teichmüller spaces. More generally, one can think of the
G
-Hitchin component for an orientable orbifold
O
of negative Euler characteristic. For instance, Thurston
[
Thu22
]
considered the Teichmüller space for orbifolds and Choi and Goldman
[
CG05
]
explored the orbifold
\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
-Hitchin components as the deformation space of convex projective geometry. More recently, Alessandrini, Lee and Schaffhauser
[
ALS23
]
defined the orbifold
G
-Hitchin components in full generality. In this note, we explore two properties that generic
G
-Hitchin representations of orbifold groups have.

Let
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
be the set of
G
-Hitchin representations and let
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O):=\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)/G
. When
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we simply write
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{n}(O):=\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}(O)
and
\operatorname{Hit}_{n}(O):=\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{n}(O)/\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We also denote by
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(S)
and
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(S)
the space of
G
-Hitchin representations and the
G
-Hitchin component for a surface
S
.

The first generic property is about eigenvalues. For an element
[\rho]
in the Fuchsian locus of
\operatorname{Hit}_{2k-1}(S)
, we have
\lambda_{k}(\rho(x))=0
for all
x\in\pi_{1}(S)
, where
\lambda_{k}
is the
\log
of the absolute value of the
k
th largest eigenvalue. On the other hand, as we move away from the Fuchsian locus, we would expect to encounter Hitchin representations
\rho
breaking the condition that
\lambda_{k}(\rho(x))=0
for all
x\in\pi_{1}(S)
. Therefore, a descent question to ask is the following:

Question 1.1
.

Is there a Hitchin representation
[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{2k-1}(S)
such that
\lambda_{k}(\rho(x))\neq 0
for all
x\in\pi_{1}(S)\setminus\{1\}
?

We will study Question
1.1
in the orbifold setting. For orbifold Hitchin components, Question
1.1
is a subtle problem even for
k=2
; as observed in
[
LR24
, Proposition 1.1]
, there is an infinite order element, say
x
, in some
orbifold group

\Delta
such that
\lambda_{2}(\rho(x))=0
for all
\rho\in\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{3}(\Delta)
. Yet, our main result gives an affirmative partial answer to Question
1.1
.

We will actually show more a generally statement under a restricted setting. Recall that any element
[\rho]
in the Fuchsian locus of
\operatorname{Hit}_{n}(O)
satisfies the relation

(1)

\lambda_{i}(\rho(x))+\lambda_{n-i+1}(\rho(x))=0

for all
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
. Presumably, this equation does not hold once we move away from the Fuchsian locus. However, there is a group theoretic obstruction to break (
1
). We say that an element
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
is
regular
if the normalizer of
x
is the infinite cyclic generated by
x
itself. If
x
is not regular,
x
and
x^{-1}
are conjugate and this forces
\lambda_{i}(\rho(x))+\lambda_{n-i+1}(\rho(x))=0
hold for any Hitchin representations
\rho
. We prove that, restricted to ones that survive under the abelianization, not being regular is the only obstruction to which
\lambda_{i}(\rho(x))+\lambda_{n-i+1}(\rho(x))=0
fails.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
O
be a closed orientable non-elementary 2-orbifold of negative Euler characteristic. Assume that
O
is neither
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,2,2,p)
,
p\geq 2
nor
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,p,q)
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<2
. Then for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, the following set

\{[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{n}(O)\,|\,\lambda_{i}(\rho(x))+\lambda_{n-i+1}(\rho(x))\neq 0\text{ for all regular }x\notin\ker(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)^{\mathrm{ab}})\}

is generic in
\operatorname{Hit}_{n}(O)
.

Here, we say that a subset of an analytic manifold
M
is
\emph{generic}
if it is the complement of the union of countably many proper analytic subvarieties. Also
\mathbb{S}^{2}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{k})
is an orbifold whose underlying space is the 2-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with cone points of order
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}
.

The second generic property that we want to explore is closely related to the notion of strongly dense subgroup introduced by Breuillard, Green, Guralnick, and Tao
[
BGGT12
]
. We first recall some definitions. We say that a subgroup
H
of
G
is
strongly dense
if for any pair of non-commuting elements
x,y\in H
, the subgroup of
G
generated by
x
and
y
is Zariski dense. Analogues to the Tits alternative, every finitely generated subgroup of a strongly dense subgroup of
G
is either very simple (i.e., abelian) or very complicated (i.e., Zariski dense in
G
). Motivated from this, we say that a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to G
of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
into
G
is
strongly dense
if
\rho(x)
and
\rho(y)
generate a Zariski dense subgroup of
G
for any pair of non-commuting elements
x,y\in\Gamma
.

One may observe that every Fuchsian representation of a surface group into
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is strongly dense. For a higher rank
G
, on the other hand,
we can produce many Zariski dense
G
-Hitchin representations which are not strongly dense by amalgamating a Zariski dense Hitchin representation and a Fuchsian representation along a separating simple closed curve on a surface. Despite this, it is reasonable to believe that generic
G
-Hitchin representations are strongly dense. Indeed, when
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, Long, Reid, and Wolff
[
LRW25
]
recently proved that the set of strongly dense
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-Hitchin representations of a surface
S
is generic in
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{n}(S)
. We prove a similar result for Hitchin representations of orbifold groups.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
O
be a closed orientable non-elementary orbifold of negative Euler characteristic. Assume that
O
has at most one even order cone point. Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group such that the Weyl group of
G
contains
-1
. Then the set of strongly dense
G
-Hitchin representations is generic in
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
.

The restriction on underlying orbifolds is essential; if
O
has two even order cone points, one can find a pair of non-commuting elements
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
whose orders are even, say,
2n
and
2m
respectively. Then
s_{1}^{n}
and
s_{2}^{m}
are order two non-commuting elements in
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
. However,
\langle\rho(s_{1}^{n}),\rho(s_{2}^{m})\rangle
is always isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for any Hitchin representation
\rho
, which cannot be Zariski dense in
G
.

Theorem
1.3
shows that split real forms of types
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{2n}
,
E_{7}
,
E_{8}
,
F_{4}
and
G_{2}
contain plenty of strongly dense discrete orbifold subgroups of various Euler characteristics. This can be seen as a partial generalization of
[
BGGT12
, Theorem 4.5]
and
[
BGL24
, Corollary 8.3]
, where they constructed strongly dense non-abelian free subgroups and closed surface subgroups in the complexification
G^{\mathbb{C}}
respectively. We remark that our result cannot be derived from
[
BGL24
, Theorem 8.1 and Theorem 8.5]
because the representation variety
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O),G^{\mathbb{C}})
is not irreducible in general.

Our theorem does not cover Lie groups of types
A_{n}
,
E_{6}
, and
D_{2n+1}
. However we still believe that these Lie groups also contain strongly dense orbifold fundamental groups.

Proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are based on the following Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
O
be a closed orientable non-elementary orbifold of negative Euler characteristic other than
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,2,2,p)
,
p\geq 2
or
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,p,q)
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<2
. Let
G
be the split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group. Let
\lambda:G\to\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
be the Jordan projection.

(i)

For an arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the following set

\{[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)\,|\,\alpha(\lambda(\rho(x)))\neq c\text{ for all regular }x\notin\ker(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)^{\mathrm{ab}})\}

is generic in
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
.

(ii)

Assume furthermore that
\alpha
satisfies
\alpha=-w_{0}\alpha
for the longest element
w_{0}
of the Weyl group of
G
. Then

\{[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)\,|\,\alpha(\lambda(\rho(x)))\neq c\text{ for all regular }x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\setminus\{1\}\}

is generic in
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
.

For the definition of the Jordan projection, consult Section
2
. We also remark that the condition in (ii) is always satisfied if
G
is of type
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{2n}
,
E_{7}
,
E_{8}
,
F_{4}
, or
G_{2}
as for these cases the longest element of the Weyl group of
G
acts as
-1
on
\mathfrak{a}
.

Once we have Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.2
immediately follows since, as illustrated in Example
2.2
, the Jordan projection
\lambda(\rho(x))
of a
\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-Hitchin representation
\rho
is more or less taking the log of the diagonal matrix that is conjugate to
\rho(x)
.

The milestone of this paper is to use the Goldman product formula as a variation formula. This new perspective, combined with the flow lifting theorem (Theorem
4.1
), paves the way to our main results.

Organization

Section

provides a quick review on Hitchin representations and relevant Lie theory. In Section

. Finally, in Section

Orbifolds

An

together with a collection of quadruples
\{(\widetilde{U}_{i},\Gamma_{i},\phi_{i},V_{i})\}

•

•

\widetilde{U}_{i}

We say that an orbifold
O
is
orientable
if the group actions associated to orbifold charts and all transition maps are orientation preserving;
O
is
closed
if
O
does not have the orbifold boundary and the underlying space
|O|
is compact.

For each point
x
of a 2-orbifold
O
, one can find a local chart of the form
\mathbb{R}^{2}/G_{x}
where
G_{x}
is a finite group of isometries on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case,
G_{x}
is called the
local group
of
x
. We say
x\in O
is
regular
if
G_{x}=\{1\}
and
singular
otherwise. The set of singular and regular points of
O
will be denoted by
O_{\mathrm{sing}}
and
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
respectively.

In this paper, we will only focus on closed orientable 2-orbifolds of negative Euler characteristics. Such an orbifold
O
has the underlying space
|O|
a closed orientable surface and there are finitely many cone points in
|O|
of order
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}
. Since the Euler characteristic of
O
is negative, the integers
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}
satisfy

\chi(|O|)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)<0.

Such an orbifold will be denoted by
|O|(p_{1},\cdots,p_{k})
. All closed orientable 2-orbifolds of negative Euler characteristics are hyperbolic; they can be realized as a quotient orbifold
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
possibly with torsion.

Elementary orbifolds
are ones that cannot be decomposed further along a 1-suborbifold into orbifolds of negative Euler characteristics. The regular set of any non-elementary closed orientable orbifold of negative Euler characteristic contains an essential simple closed curve.

Every orbifold
O
can be covered by its universal orbifold
\widetilde{O}
. The group of deck transformation groups of the universal covering
\widetilde{O}\to O
is the
orbifold fundamental group

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
.

In what follows, an
oriented closed curve
in a 2-orbifold
O
is a smooth immersion
\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to|O|
such that
|\gamma^{-1}(O_{\mathrm{sing}})|
is finite and each point
t\in\gamma^{-1}(O_{\mathrm{sing}})
is given a prescribed local lift of
\gamma|[t-\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon]
to the universal cover of
O
. We denote by
|\gamma|
the image of an oriented closed curve
\gamma
.

Let
\gamma
be an oriented curve in
O
. Let
p\in|\gamma|
. If
p\in O_{\mathrm{reg}}
, choose an orbifold chart
U
around
p
with the trivial local group and a subinterval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gamma([a,b])\subset U
. Then we homotope
\gamma|[a,b]
inside
U
relative to the end-points
\{\gamma(a),\gamma(b)\}
. If
p\in O_{\mathrm{sing}}
, we homotope the prescribed lift of
\gamma
in the universal cover fixing the end-points and project it down to
O
. This process gives a new oriented curve which is said to be
elementary homotopic
to
\gamma
. We say that two oriented closed curves are
homotopic
if they are connected by a sequence of elementary homotopies. If the domain of
\gamma
is given a distinguished base-point, we assume that every elementary homotopy of
\gamma
fixes the base-point.

Similar to usual fundamental groups, it is known that
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
is homeomorphic to the group of homotopy classes of oriented based closed curves. For a oriented closed curve
\gamma
with a base-point
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\gamma_{t_{0}}
an element of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O,\gamma(t_{0}))
represented by the oriented closed curve
\gamma
with the base-point
t_{0}
. We say that an oriented closed curve
\gamma
on a closed 2-orbifold
O
is
essential
if it is not homotopic in
O
to a point or an orbifold boundary component of
O
.

Since
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a subspace of
|O|
, we have a natural homomorphism
\pi_{1}(O_{\mathrm{reg}})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
that is known to be surjective. Therefore it is sometimes convenient to regard an element
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
as an oriented curve in
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

Let
\gamma
and
\eta
be two oriented closed curves on
O
. We define the set

\gamma\sharp\eta=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\,|\,\gamma(t)=\eta(s)\text{ is a transverse intersection point}\}.

By abusing language, we call each element of
\gamma\sharp\eta
an
intersection point
between
\gamma
and
\eta
. Also it will be convenient to employ the following convention: given an element
p=(t_{0},s_{0})\in\gamma\sharp\eta
, we write
\gamma_{p}:=\gamma_{t_{0}}
and
\eta_{p}:=\eta_{s_{0}}
. Note that both are elements of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O,\gamma(t_{0}))=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O,\eta(s_{0}))
. If the orbifold
O
is oriented, we define
\operatorname{sign}p\in\{1,-1\}
for an intersection point
p=(t_{0},s_{0})\in\gamma\sharp\eta
by the sign of the ordered bases
\{\dot{\gamma}(t_{0}),\dot{\eta}(s_{0})\}
of
\operatorname{T}_{\gamma(t_{0})}O
. The
algebraic intersection number

\operatorname{i}(\gamma,\eta)
is defined to be the signed sum

\operatorname{i}(\gamma,\eta):=\sum_{p\in\gamma\sharp\eta}\operatorname{sign}p.

We say that an element
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)

regular
is if its normalizer
N_{\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)}(x)
is an infinite cyclic group generated by
x
. An oriented closed curve in
O
is regular if it represents a regular element of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
.

Assume that
O
is equipped with a hyperbolic structure. If an oriented closed curves
\gamma
represents an infinite order element of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
, then
\gamma
is freely homotopic to a closed geodesic or a geodesic full 1-suborbifold (see
[
CG05
]
for the definition). We say that two closed curves
\gamma
and
\eta
on
O

intersect essentially
if their geodesic representatives have non-empty intersection. This definition in fact does not depend on the choice of a hyperbolic structure on
O
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
O
be a closed non-elementary hyperbolic orbifold. Assume that
O
is neither
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,2,2,p)
,
p\geq 2
nor
\mathbb{S}^{2}(2,2,p,q)
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<2
.

(i)

Any regular essential oriented simple closed curve can be homotoped inside
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
to a regular oriented simple closed geodesics contained in
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

(ii)

An infinite order element
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
is not regular if and only if there are order two elements
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
such that
x=s_{1}s_{2}
.

(iii)

Let
\gamma
be a regular oriented closed curve. Then there is a regular simple closed curve
\eta
intersecting
\gamma
essentially.

Proof.

As
O
is given a hyperbolic structure, we may regard
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
as a discrete subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
.

(i) has been addressed in many places; see for instance
[
CG05
, Theorem 4.3]
,
[
TWZ06
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
BK23
, Lemma 5.8]
.

(ii) Since
x
has infinite order it is a hyperbolic element in
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
. Note that elements in the normalizer of
x
preserve the fixed points of
x
in
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
. By the discreteness of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
, we know that each element
y
in the normalizer of
x
is either a power of
x
or an order two element preserving two fixed points of
x
in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Hence, if
x
is not regular, the normalizer of
x
contains an order two element, say
s_{1}
. We then know that
s_{1}xs_{1}=x^{-1}
. Let
s_{2}=s_{1}x
. Since
s_{2}^{2}=s_{1}xs_{1}x=x^{-1}x=1
, the forward implication is proved. The converse direction is a straightforward computation.

(iii) It suffices to find a collection of simple regular closed geodesics whose complement consists of disks, annuli and disks with one cone point.

If
O
is a sphere with six order two cone points, we take three simple regular closed curves as in Figure
1
. By (ii), these curves are regular.

For other 2-orbifolds
O
, we first construct
O^{\prime}
by splitting
O
along pairwise disjoint geodesic full-1-suborbifolds joining pairs of order two cone points so that the
O^{\prime}
contains at most one order two cone point. The resulting orbifold
O^{\prime}
has totally geodesic boundary and its underlying space contains essential simple closed curves. It is a standard fact that one may find a collection of essential simple closed curves
\mathcal{S}
in
(O^{\prime})_{\mathrm{reg}}
such that
(O^{\prime})_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\mathcal{S}
consists of disks, once punctured disks and annuli. Again, (ii) shows that these curves are regular and each component of
O\setminus\mathcal{S}
is a disk, annulus or a disk with one cone point.
∎

2.2.
Lie theory and Hitchin representations

Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple Lie group
G^{\mathbb{C}}
of adjoint type and let
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra. In the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, we choose a regular nilpotent element
E
. Then, we can always complete
E
to a triple
\{E,F,H\}
satisfying the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-relations:
[H,E]=2E
,
[H,F]=-2F
and
[E,F]=H
. Such a principal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple induces a representation
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}
which can be integrated to a Lie group representation
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to G^{\mathbb{C}}
. By taking the real points, one can find a Lie group representation
\iota_{G}:\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to G
.

We call a representation
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\to G
a
Fuchsian representation
if it is of the form
\iota_{G}\circ\rho_{hyp}
for some discrete faithful representation
\rho_{hyp}:\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\to\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. A representation
\rho:\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\to G
is a
G
-Hitchin representation
if
\rho
can be continuously deformed to a Fuchsian one. The set of
G
-Hitchin representations will be denoted by
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
. We also define
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O):=\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)/G
, the space of conjugacy classes of
G
-Hitchin representations. We will use the square bracket
[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
to denote the conjugacy class of
\rho\in\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
. Topological properties of orbifold Hitchin components are studied in
[
ALS23
]
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
admits a
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}
. We have the Cartan involution
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, which makes the symmetric bilinear form

\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}_{\theta}(\cdot,\cdot):=-\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\cdot,\theta(\cdot))

positive definite on
\mathfrak{g}
. The Cartan involution
\theta
decomposes
\mathfrak{g}
into the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the +1 eigenspace of
\theta
and
\mathfrak{p}
is the
-1
eigenspace of
\theta
. Hence,
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}
is positive definite on
\mathfrak{p}
and negative definite on
\mathfrak{k}
.

Choose a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{p}
. Then we have the restricted root space decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}+\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}

where
\Pi\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
is the set of restricted roots and

\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\,|\,[A,X]=\beta(A)X\text{ for all }A\in\mathfrak{a}\}.

If
G
is real split, we have

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\,|\,[A,X]=0\text{ for all }A\in\mathfrak{a}\}=\mathfrak{a}.

This decomposition is orthogonal with respect to
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}_{\theta}
.

We now define the Jordan projection. Choose an ordering on
\Pi
and let
\Delta
be the set of simple roots with respect to this order. Let

\mathfrak{a}^{+}=\{A\in\mathfrak{a}\,|\,\beta(A)>0\text{ for all }\beta\in\Delta\}

and let
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
be the closure of
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Any element
x\in G
can be uniquely written as the product
x=x_{k}x_{h}x_{u}
of commuting elements where
x_{k}
is conjugate into the analytic subgroup of
\mathfrak{k}
,
x_{h}
is in the conjugacy class of
\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
and
x_{u}
is unipotent. We know that the conjugacy class of
x_{h}
intersects
\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
at a unique point. The
Jordan projection
of
x
is a unique element
\lambda(x)\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
such that
\exp\lambda(x)
is conjugate to
x_{h}
. This defines a map
\lambda:G\to\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
.

We say that an element
x\in G
is
loxodromic
if
\lambda(x)\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Let
Z_{K}(\mathfrak{a})
be the centralizer of
\mathfrak{a}
in the analytic subgroup of
\mathfrak{k}
. Then
x\in G
is loxodromic if and only if
x
is conjugate into
y\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
for some
y\in Z_{K}(\mathfrak{a})
. We say
x\in G
is
purely loxodromic
if
x
is conjugate into
\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
.

Example 2.2
.

As a concrete example, take
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the Cartan involution
\theta
and the invariant symmetric bilinear form
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}
may be taken to be
X\mapsto-X^{\operatorname{T}}
and
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(XY)
respectively. An element
x\in\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is loxodromic if, for some lift
x^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
of
x
, the eigenvalues of
x^{\prime}
are real and their absolute values are all distinct.
x\in\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is purely loxodromic if
x
can be lifted to
x^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
whose eigenvalues are positive and distinct. For an element
x\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\varepsilon_{i}(x)
be (complex) eigenvalues of
x
such that
|\varepsilon_{1}(x)|\geq|\varepsilon_{2}(x)|\geq\cdots\geq|\varepsilon_{n}(x)|
. The Jordan projection of a loxodromic element
x
is
\operatorname{diag}(\log|\varepsilon_{1}(x^{\prime})|,\cdots,\log|\varepsilon_{n}(x^{\prime})|)
where
x^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a lift of
x
.

3.
The Goldman Product Formula

In this section we introduce the Goldman product formula which computes the Atiyah-Bott-Goldman symplectic form on Hamiltonian vector fields of a certain kind.

Let
G
be a real semi-simple Lie group. and let
U\subset G
be a non-empty open set which is invariant under conjugation in the sense that
xUx^{-1}\subset U
for all
x\in G
. A differentiable function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
is
invariant
if
f(yxy^{-1})=f(x)
for all
x\in U
and
y\in G
. We say that an invariant function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
is
homogeneous
if
f(x^{k})=kf(x)
for any
x\in U
and any positive integer
k
with
x^{k}\in U
.

Given an invariant function
f
we define the
Goldman function

\widehat{f}:U\to\mathfrak{g}
which associates to
x\in U
a unique element
\widehat{f}(x)\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(x),Y)=\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f(x\exp tY)

for all
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
be an invariant function such that the domain
U
contains all purely loxodromic elements and let
O
be a closed orientable orbifold of negative Euler characteristic. For a given infinite order element
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\setminus\{1\}
, we define the differentiable function
f_{x}:\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)\to\mathbb{R}
by
f_{x}([\rho])=f(\rho(x))
. This function is well-defined because
\rho(x)
is always purely loxodromic; see Lemma
5.2
. Since
f
is an invariant function, we know that
f_{x}
depends only on the conjugacy class of
x
. Hence, we may say that
f_{x}
is defined for the homotopy class of oriented essential closed curves representing
x
.

We are now ready to state the product formula by Goldman
[
Gol86
, Theorem 3.5]
. The original statement is about symplectic geometry. At this moment, however, we only need the existence of Hamiltonian vector fields, which one may take it for granted. We will explain the construct of these Hamiltonian flows in Section
4
.

Theorem 3.1

(Goldman Product formula)
.

Let
S
be a closed oriented surface of genus
>1
. Let
f
and
g
be invariant functions. Let
x
and
y
be oriented essential closed curves in
S
. Assume that all intersections points of
x
and
y
are transverse double points. Let
\mathbb{X}_{g_{y}}
be the Hamiltonian vector field associated to
g_{y}
with respect to the Atiyah-Bott-Goldman symplectic form. Then, at any point
[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(S)
, we have

\mathbb{X}_{g_{y}}(f_{x})|_{[\rho]}=\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(\rho(x_{p})),\widehat{g}(\rho(y_{p}))).

We remark that the transverse double intersection condition in the theorem is not essential. If
x
and
y
have a multiple intersection, we can always resolve it to a collection of double intersections by perturbing
x
and
y
near the intersection point.

Goldman’s motivation for promoting Theorem
3.1
was to compute the Atiyah-Bott-Goldman symplectic form between two Hamiltonian vector fields. Besides its origin, we will focus on the aspect that Theorem
3.1
provides the first variation formula for functions along Hamiltonian flows. This viewpoint sheds light on the way toward our main theorems.

In the remaining part of this section, we compute the Goldman function for a certain class of invariant functions.

We begin with a simple observation:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\widehat{f}:U\to\mathfrak{g}
be the Goldman function of an invariant function
f
. Then for any
x\in U
and
y\in G
, we have
\widehat{f}(yxy^{-1})=\operatorname{Ad}_{y}\widehat{f}(x)
.

Let
\lambda:G\to\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
be the Jordan projection. By definition, we have
\lambda(yxy^{-1})=\lambda(x)
for all
x,y\in G
. Hence, given a non-trivial linear functional
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, the function
\alpha\circ\lambda:G\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\alpha\circ\lambda(yxy^{-1})=\alpha\circ\lambda(x)
for all
x,y\in G
.

We are interested in the case when the domain
U\subset G
is given by the set of purely loxodromic elements. Defined on
U
, we are concerned with the function
\alpha\circ\lambda
. To say that
\alpha\circ\lambda
is an invariant function, we need to verify that
U
is open and that
\lambda
is at least differentiable. As we could not find an explicit statement from existing literature, we decide to give their proofs in Appendix
A
. Here we just state the results.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group. Let
\lambda:G\to\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
be the Jordan projection and let
U
be the set of purely loxodromic elements. Then

(i)

U
is open,

(ii)

\lambda
is real analytic on
U
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group. Let
U
be the set of purely loxodromic elements in
G
. Let
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
f:=\alpha\circ\lambda
. Then,

(i)

f
is a real analytic invariant function,

(ii)

f
is homogeneous,

(iii)

for an element
x\in\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
, we have

\widehat{f}(x)=\alpha^{\vee},\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{f}(x^{-1})=w_{0}\alpha^{\vee}.

where
\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{a}
is a unique element in
\mathfrak{a}
satisfying
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\alpha^{\vee},Y)=\alpha(Y)
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{a}
and
w_{0}
is the longest element of the Weyl group of
G
.

Proof.

(i) This follows from Lemma
3.3
.

(ii) Let
x\in U
. One can find
y\in G
such that
yxy^{-1}=\exp X
for some
X\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. For any positive integer
k\neq 0
, we observe

\lambda((\exp X)^{k})=kX.

Thus, we have

\lambda(x^{k})=\operatorname{Ad}_{y^{-1}}\lambda((\exp X)^{k})=k\operatorname{Ad}_{y^{-1}}\lambda(\exp X)=k\lambda(x).

(iii) Write
x^{\pm 1}=\exp\pm X
for some
X\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Let
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
be an arbitrary element. By the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, we have

(2)

x^{\pm 1}\exp tY=\exp\pm X\exp tY=\exp\left(\pm X+t\frac{\operatorname{ad}_{\pm X}}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{ad}_{\pm X}}}Y+O(t^{2})\right).

Let

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}

be the restricted root space decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. Since
G
is a split real form we have
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{a}
. Decompose
Y
into the sum of restricted root vectors;
Y=Y_{0}+\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}Y_{\beta}
. Since
X\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
, we have
[X,Y_{\beta}]=\beta(X)Y_{\beta}
for every restricted root
\beta\in\Pi
. Hence, the argument of the rightmost exponential in (
2
) becomes

\pm X+tY_{0}+t\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}\frac{\beta(\pm X)}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-\beta(\pm X)}}Y_{\beta}+O(t^{2}).

Let

Z_{\pm}:=Y_{0}+\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}\frac{\beta(\pm X)}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-\beta(\pm X)}}Y_{\beta}.

For a small enough
t
, (
2
) remains purely loxodromic. Also by Lemma
3.3
, for all sufficiently small
t
, we can find an analytic path
g_{t}\in G
such that
g_{t}(x\exp tY)g_{t}^{-1}\in\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. In the case of
x^{-1}
, there is a longest element
w_{0}
in the Weyl group
N_{\mathfrak{k}}(\mathfrak{a})/Z_{\mathfrak{k}}(\mathfrak{a})
such that
g_{t}w_{0}(x^{-1}\exp tY)w_{0}^{-1}g_{t}^{-1}\in\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Since
t\mapsto g_{t}
is analytic with
g_{0}=1
, we expand
g_{t}
near
t=0
and write
g_{t}=\exp(tQ+O(t^{2}))
for some
Q\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have

\displaystyle g_{t}(x\exp tY)g_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{t}}(X+tZ_{+}+O(t^{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(X+tZ_{+}+t[Q,X]+O(t^{2})\right)

and

\displaystyle g_{t}w_{0}(x^{-1}\exp tY)w_{0}^{-1}g_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{t}}\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}(-X+tZ_{-}+O(t^{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X+t\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}Z_{-}-t[Q,\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X]+O(t^{2})\right).

Recall that both
X
and
-\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X
are members of
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. By the construction of
g_{t}
, the first order terms,

tZ_{+}+t[Q,X],\quad\text{ and }\quad t\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}Z_{-}-t[Q,\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X]

should be contained in
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Since
X
and
\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X
belong to
\mathfrak{a}
, both
[Q,X]
and
[Q,\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X]
do not have
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-factors in the restricted root space decomposition. This forces

\displaystyle[Q,X]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}\frac{\beta(X)}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-\beta(X)}}Y_{\beta}

\displaystyle[Q,\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}\frac{\beta(-X)}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-\beta(-X)}}\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}Y_{\beta}.

Therefore, we know that

\displaystyle\lambda(x\exp tY)

\displaystyle=X+tY_{0}+O(t^{2})

\displaystyle\lambda(x^{-1}\exp tY)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}X+t\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}Y_{0}+O(t^{2})

and that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f(x\exp tY)

\displaystyle=\alpha(Y_{0})

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f(x^{-1}\exp tY)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\operatorname{Ad}_{w_{0}}Y_{0}).

Since the decomposition
Y=Y_{0}+\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}Y_{\beta}
is orthogonal with respect to the positive definite pairing
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}_{\theta}
, we get

\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\alpha^{\vee},Y)=-\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\theta\alpha^{\vee},Y)=\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}_{\theta}(\alpha^{\vee},Y)=\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}_{\theta}(\alpha^{\vee},Y_{0})=\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\alpha^{\vee},Y_{0})=\alpha(Y_{0}).

Therefore,
\widehat{f}(x)=\alpha^{\vee}
. Similarly, we have
\widehat{f}(x^{-1})=w_{0}\alpha^{\vee}
.
∎

4.
Lifting Goldman Flows

In this section, we investigate how Goldman flows behavior under a finite covering.

Let
O
be a closed orientable non-elementary 2-orbifold with negative Euler characteristic and let
f:U\to G
be an invariant function. Let
x
be an oriented essential simple closed curve in
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Through the natural map
\pi_{1}(O_{\mathrm{reg}})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
,
x
represents an infinite order element in
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
.

Goldman
[
Gol86
]
constructed an explicit Hamiltonian flow, called a
Goldman flow
, associated to a given Hamiltonian vector field of the form
\mathbb{X}_{f_{x}}
. In
[
CJK20
,
CJ23
]
, a multi-curve generalization of Goldman flows using graph of groups was studied. As we need Goldman flows along a multi-curve, let us quickly recall them. One may refer to
[
Ser80
]
for more details on graph of groups.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\}
be a set of pairwise non-isotopic oriented essential simple closed curves in
O_{\mathrm{reg}}
. We also regard
\mathbf{x}
as a collection of oriented closed curves in
O
via the inclusion
O_{\mathrm{reg}}\to O
. Let
O_{1},\cdots,O_{d}
be the completions of connected components of
O\setminus(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}|)
. We first construct a graph of groups
\mathcal{G}
. As a directed graph,
\mathcal{G}
has the vertex set
V(\mathcal{G})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{d}\}
where
v_{i}\in O_{i}
are regular points in
O_{i}
. We join
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
(possibly
i=j
) by an edge
e_{k}
if and only if
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
are glued along some
|x_{k}|
. We orient
e_{k}
so that it passes
|x_{k}|
from the left-side of
|x_{k}|
to the right-side of
|x_{k}|
. Note that we can distinguish the left-side and the right-side of
|x_{k}|
since
x_{k}
is oriented and
O
is orientable. Assign to each vertex
v_{i}
the group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O_{i},v_{i})
. For each edge
e_{k}
, we give the group
\langle x_{k}\rangle
. We regard
\langle x_{k}\rangle
as a subgroup of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O_{i},v_{i})
by conjugating
x_{k}
with the arc
e_{k}\cap O_{i}
if
v_{i}
is the head of the edge
e_{k}
.

Choose a maximal tree
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{G}
such that
v_{1}\in V(\mathcal{T})
. Then it is a standard fact that
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O,v_{1})
is isomorphic to the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{T})
of the graph of groups. Recall that
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{T})
is generated by
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O_{1},v_{1}),\cdots,\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O_{k},v_{k})
together with edges in
E_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{G}):=E(\mathcal{G})\setminus E(\mathcal{T})
. The isomorphism
\mathfrak{j}:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{T})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O,v_{1})
maps
\gamma\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O_{i},v_{i})\subset\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{T})
to the element
\mathfrak{j}(\gamma)=e\gamma\overline{e}
where
e
is a unique edge path in
\mathcal{T}
joining
v_{1}
and
v_{i}
with its reversal
\overline{e}
. Also it maps
c\in E_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{G})
to the element
gch
where
g
is an edge path in
\mathcal{T}
from
v_{1}
to the tail of
c
and
h
is an edge path in
\mathcal{T}
from the head of
c
to
v_{1}
.

To define the Goldman flow, we use the following notations. For
e\in E(\mathcal{T})
, let
H(e)
be the head segment of

\displaystyle\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathbb{X}_{f_{\widetilde{x}_{j}}},\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{X}_{g_{\widetilde{y}_{i}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega(\mathbb{X}_{f_{\widetilde{x}_{j}}},\mathbb{X}_{g_{\widetilde{y}_{i}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{p\in\widetilde{x}_{j}\sharp\widetilde{y}_{i}}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathsf{B}(\widehat{f}(\rho(q_{*}((\widetilde{x}_{j})_{p}))),\widehat{g}(\rho(q_{*}((\widetilde{y}_{i})_{p}))))

By our choice of lifts, the signs of intersections
(t_{1},u_{1}),(t_{2},u_{2})\in\widetilde{x}_{j}\sharp\widetilde{y}_{i}
are the same if
q(\widetilde{x}_{j}(t_{1}))=q(\widetilde{x}_{j}(t_{2}))=q(\widetilde{y}_{i}(u_{1}))=q(\widetilde{y}_{i}(u_{2}))
. It follows that for each of a fixed
j
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{p\in\widetilde{x}_{j}\sharp\widetilde{y}_{i}}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathsf{B}(\widehat{f}(\rho(q_{*}((\widetilde{x}_{j})_{p}))),\widehat{g}(\rho(q_{*}((\widetilde{y}_{i})_{p}))))

\displaystyle=l\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(\rho(x_{p})^{l}),\widehat{g}(\rho(y_{p})^{m}))

\displaystyle=l\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(\rho(x_{p})),\widehat{g}(\rho(y_{p}))).

Therefore,

(9)

\displaystyle\omega\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathbb{X}_{f_{\widetilde{x}_{j}}},\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{X}_{g_{\widetilde{y}_{i}}}\right)

\displaystyle=l\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(\rho(x_{p})),\widehat{g}(\rho(y_{p})))

\displaystyle=|\Delta|\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathsf{B}(\widehat{f}(\rho(x_{p})),\widehat{g}(\rho(y_{p}))).

By combining (
8
) and (
9
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

For a fixed infinite order
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the condition
\alpha\circ\lambda(\rho(x))=c
is not algebraic. However, we still expect that the set
\{[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)\,|\,\alpha(\lambda(\rho(x)))=c\}
is almost negligible.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)\setminus\{1\}
be an infinite order element. Define

U_{x,\alpha,c}:=\{[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)\,|\,\alpha(\lambda(\rho(x)))\neq c\}.

Then for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
,
U_{x,\alpha,c}
is generic provided it is non-empty.

Proof.

Choose a finite manifold covering
\widetilde{O}\to O
. One can identify
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
as a submanifold of
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(\widetilde{O})
fixed by the action of the deck group of
\widetilde{O}\to O
.

It is well-known
[
FG06
, Theorem 1.13]
that for a Hitchin representation
\rho
,
\rho(z)
is purely loxodromic for any
z\in\pi_{1}(\widetilde{O})\setminus\{1\}
. Since
x
has infinite order,
x^{k}
is a non-trivial element in
\pi_{1}(\widetilde{O})
for some integer
k
. Therefore,
\rho(x^{k})
is purely loxodromic. This implies that
\rho(x)
is also purely loxodromic. Thus, if
U_{x,\alpha,c}
is not empty, then it is the complement of the zero locus of the non-constant real analytic function
\rho\mapsto\alpha(\lambda(\rho(x)))
.
Since Hitchin components are analytic connected manifold,
U_{x,\alpha,c}
is open and dense in
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
.
∎

In the above proof, we implicitly showed that
\rho(x)
is purely loxodromic provided
x
has infinite order.

The following result was essentially observed by Goldman in his own proof for Wolpert’s cosine formula
[
Gol86
, Theorem 3.9]
. For readers’ convenience, we reformulate it as a lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\rho:\pi^{\mathrm{orb}}_{1}(O)\to\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a Fuchsian representation and let
x,y
be infinite order elements in
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
. Represent
x,y
as parameterized closed geodesics in
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\rho(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O))
. For each intersection
p=(t,s)\in x\sharp y
, let
\varphi_{p}([\rho])
be the counter-clock angle from
\dot{x}(t)
to
\dot{y}(s)
at
x(t)
. Let
f:=\alpha\circ\lambda
and
g=\beta\circ\lambda
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

(\operatorname{sign}p)\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\widehat{f}(\iota_{G}\circ\rho(x_{p})),\widehat{g}(\iota_{G}\circ\rho(y_{p})))=\begin{cases}\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\alpha^{\vee},\operatorname{Ad}_{R(\varphi_{p}([\rho]))}\beta^{\vee})&\text{ if }\operatorname{sign}p=1\\
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}(\alpha^{\vee},-\operatorname{Ad}_{R(\varphi_{p}([\rho]))}w_{0}\beta^{\vee})&\text{ if }\operatorname{sign}p=-1\end{cases}

where

R(\varphi_{p}([\rho]))=\iota_{G}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\varphi_{p}([\rho])/2)&\sin(\varphi_{p}([\rho])/2)\\
-\sin(\varphi_{p}([\rho])/2)&\cos(\varphi_{p}([\rho])/2)\end{pmatrix}\right).

Proof.

Take the lifts
\widetilde{x}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
\widetilde{y}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
of
x
and
y
in the upper-half plane model
\mathbb{H}^{2}
such that
\widetilde{x}(0)
and
\widetilde{y}(0)
descend to the intersection point
x(t)=y(s)
. By taking a suitable
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-conjugation, we may assume that
\widetilde{x}
is the ray from
0
to
\infty
and the intersection point
\widetilde{x}(0)=\widetilde{y}(0)
is situated at
\sqrt{-1}
. Then we know that
\rho(x_{p})=\operatorname{diag}(\exp(\ell),\exp(-\ell))
,
\ell>0
, and that the axis of
\rho(y_{p})
passes through
\sqrt{-1}
. Hence,
\iota_{G}\circ\rho(x_{p})\in\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. Moreover, conjugating
\rho(y_{p})
by

\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\varphi_{p}([\rho])}{2}&-\sin\frac{\varphi_{p}([\rho])}{2}\\
\sin\frac{\varphi_{p}([\rho])}{2}&\cos\frac{\varphi_{p}([\rho])}{2}\end{pmatrix}

brings
\rho(y_{p})
into
\operatorname{diag}(\exp(\ell^{\prime}),\exp(-\ell^{\prime}))
if
\operatorname{sign}p=1
and into
\operatorname{diag}(\exp(-\ell^{\prime}),\exp(\ell^{\prime}))
if
\operatorname{sign}p=-1
for some
\ell^{\prime}>0
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Ad}_{R(\varphi_{p}(\rho))}\iota_{G}\circ\rho(y_{p})
is in
\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
if
\operatorname{sign}p=1
and in
\exp-\mathfrak{a}^{+}
if
\operatorname{sign}p=-1
. Therefore, the conclusion follows from Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
.
∎

\rho
be any Fuchsian representation. Let

be the 1-parameter family of Fuchsian representations obtained by applying the twisting flow along

Claim 5.4

\max_{p\in x_{t}\sharp y_{t}}\varphi_{p}([\rho_{t}])

Let

be a hyperbolic structure on

\displaystyle<\varepsilon

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Following
[
LRW25
]
, let
\operatorname{Bad}(x,y)
be the set of
G
-Hitchin representations
\rho
such that
\rho(x)
and
\rho(y)
do not generate a Zariski dense subgroup of
G
. Since
\operatorname{Bad}(x,y)
is an analytic subvariety of
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)

[
BGGT12
, Theorem 4.1]
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Bad}(x,y)
is proper for every non-commuting pair
\{x,y\}
.

By mean of contradiction, suppose that there are non-commuting
x,y\in\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(O)
such that
\operatorname{Bad}(x,y)=\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
.

Choose a finite index torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma
of
\langle x,y\rangle
and take a covering
\widetilde{O}
of
O
corresponding to
\Gamma
. Let
\eta
be in the Fuchsian locus of
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)
. Because
\eta|\Gamma=\eta|\pi_{1}(\widetilde{O})
is the holonomy of the lifted hyperbolic structure on
\widetilde{O}
, we know that
\eta|\Gamma
is a positive representation in the sense of Fock and Goncharov
[
FG06
]
. Since positivity is an open condition, one can find a small open set
V\subset\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
around
[\eta]
such that for any
[\rho]\in V
,
[\rho|\Gamma]
is positive. Let
\overline{V}
be the preimage of

under the projection
\overline{\operatorname{Hit}}_{G}(O)\to\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
.

For
\rho\in\overline{V}
, let
\rho_{\Gamma}:=\rho|\Gamma:\Gamma\to G
and let
H_{\rho_{\Gamma}}
be the Zariski closure of
\rho(\Gamma)
. By assumption, we know that
H_{\rho_{\Gamma}}\neq G
for all
\rho\in\overline{V}
.

Consider the semisimplification
\rho_{\Gamma}^{ss}:\Gamma\to H_{\rho_{\Gamma}}^{ss}
of the representation
\rho_{\Gamma}
. Note that

is a proper semi-simple Lie subgroup of

. It is known
[
GGKW17
, Lemma 2.40]
that
\lambda(\rho_{\Gamma}^{ss}(z))=\lambda(\rho_{\Gamma}(z))=\lambda(\rho(z))
for all

.

By Sambarino
[
Sam24
, Theorem B]
, there are only finitely many semi-simple subalgebras
\mathfrak{h}_{1},\mathfrak{h}_{2},\cdots,\mathfrak{h}_{k}
of
\mathfrak{g}
whose real ranks are strictly smaller than the real rank of
G
such that, apart from the Zariski dense case, the Lie algebra of
H_{\rho_{\Gamma}}^{ss}
is conjugate to one of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
. Therefore, one can find finitely many elements
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
in

such that for any
\rho\in\overline{V}
and

. This means that the set

avoids the non-empty open set

.

On the other hand, because

has at most one even order cone point,

contains a regular

in

. By the proof of Theorem

is an open dense subspace of
\operatorname{Hit}_{G}(O)
. This leads us to a contradiction.
∎

Further Discussions and Questions

In this section, we focus on the
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
case and present explicit formulas computing the first variation of eigenvalues along Fuchsian locus. In what follows we abbreviate
\operatorname{Hit}_{3}(S):=\operatorname{Hit}_{\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(S)
. We also use the trace as our non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\mathbf{\mathsf{B}}
.

Let
c
be an oriented essential simple closed curve
c
.
In
\operatorname{Hit}_{3}(S)
, we write
\ell:=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3}
. As before,
\lambda_{i}
denotes the logarithm of the
i
th largest eigenvalue. The quantity
\ell_{c}([\rho])=\lambda_{1}(\rho(c))-\lambda_{3}(\rho(c))
,
[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{3}(S)
records the Hilbert length of
c
with respect to the convex real projective geometry corresponding to
[\rho]
.

There are two types of fundamental Hamiltonian flows. One is the twisting flow
\mathcal{T}^{c}_{t}
along
c
which is the same as the classical twisting flow on the Teichmülle space. The other flow is so-called the bulging flow
\mathcal{B}^{c}_{t}
along
c
. The bulging flow allows us to move away from the Fuchsian locus. It is known that
\mathcal{T}^{c}_{t}
and
\mathcal{B}^{c}_{t}
are Hamiltonian flows corresponding to the vector fields
\mathbb{X}_{\ell_{c}}
and
\mathbb{X}_{(\lambda_{2})_{c}}
respectively.

Recall that one possible embedding
\iota_{\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}:\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
is given by

(10)

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}&ab&b^{2}\\
2ac&ad+bc&2bd\\
c^{2}&cd&d^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

A straightforward computation using (
10
) and Lemma
5.3
yields the following.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
x
be an oriented essential closed curve and let
y
be an oriented essential simple closed curve. Let
[\rho]\in\operatorname{Hit}_{3}(S)
be a Fuchsian representation. Then we have

(11)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\ell_{x}(\mathcal{T}^{y}_{t}([\rho]))

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}2\cos\varphi_{p}([\rho])

(12)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}(\lambda_{2})_{x}(\mathcal{B}_{t}^{y}([\rho]))

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in x\sharp y}\frac{3}{2}(\operatorname{sign}p)(1+3\cos 2\varphi_{p}([\rho]))

(13)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\ell_{x}(\mathcal{B}_{t}^{y}([\rho]))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}}{\operatorname{d}t}\right|_{t=0}(\lambda_{2})_{x}(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{y}([\rho]))=0,

where
\varphi_{p}([\rho])
is the counter-clock angle at
p
from geodesic representatives of
x
to
y
measured in the hyperbolic structure corresponding to
[\rho]
.

In Corollary
6.1

[
Wol83
,
Ker83
]
. When
x
is simple, (

) was manifested in
[

.

Given a closed orientable surface
S
with genus
>1
, there are infinitely many pairs of non-conjugate elements

such that
\operatorname{length}_{X}(x)=\operatorname{length}_{X}(y)
for all hyperbolic structures

]
. By (

Corollary 6.2

There are infinitely many pairs

There are infinitely many pairs of non-conjugate elements

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(x))=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(y))
for all quasi-Fuchsian
\rho:\pi_{1}(S)\to\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Motivated from Theorem

In view of Corollary

Let

Appendix A
Analyticity of the Jordan Projection

In this appendix we prove Lemma
3.3
. We restate the claim first.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
G
be a split real form of a complex simple adjoint Lie group. Let
\lambda:G\to\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
be the Jordan projection and let
U
be the set of purely loxodromic elements. Then,

(i)

U
is open,

(ii)

\lambda
is real analytic on
U
.

Proof.

(i) Consider the adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}:G\to\operatorname{SL}(\mathfrak{g})
. The Jordan projection is equivariant in the sense that
\lambda(\operatorname{Ad}_{x})=\operatorname{ad}_{\lambda(x)}
for all
x\in G
. The image
C
of
\partial\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
under the representation
\operatorname{ad}
is a linear subspace of

\{\operatorname{diag}(0,0,\cdots,0,\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{d})\,|\,\beta_{i}\geq 0\text{ and }\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{d}=0\}

where
d=\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim\mathfrak{a}
. In particular,
C
is a closed set. Therefore we only need to show that

D:=\{x\in\operatorname{SL}(\mathfrak{g})\,|\,\lambda(x)\in C\}

is closed. This can be proven by using the fact that the roots of a polynomial depend continuously on the coefficients. In particular, if
g_{i}
is a given sequence converging to
g
with
\lambda(g_{i})\in C
, then because the eigenvalues of
g_{i}
converges to that of
g
, we have
\lambda(g_{i})\to\lambda(g)
as described in Example
2.2
. Since
C
is closed, we know that
\lambda(g)\in C
. Thus,
D
is closed. Since
U
is the complement of the closed set
\operatorname{Ad}^{-1}(D)
,
U
is open.

(ii) Let
A^{+}=\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+}
. It is enough to show that
\lambda
is analytic at a neighborhood of each point of
A^{+}
. Consider the analytic map
F:A^{+}\times G\to U
defined by

(a,x)\mapsto xax^{-1}.

Let
a_{0}:=\exp A_{0}\in A^{+}\subset U
be arbitrary. Take exponential charts containing
(a_{0},1)\in A^{+}\times G
and
a_{0}\in U
. If we express
F
in these charts, we would get

F:(A,X)\mapsto e^{\operatorname{ad}_{X}}A

where
A\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
e^{\operatorname{ad}_{X}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{k}
. Recall that we have the restricted root space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
. Let
\Delta=\{H_{1},\cdots,H_{r}\}
be elements in
\mathfrak{a}
dual to simple roots. Write
\Pi=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{d}\}
and choose a non-zero element
E_{i}
from each
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta_{i}}
. Then
\Delta\cup\{E_{i}\}_{i=1,2,\cdots,d}
forms a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
. With respect to this basis, we write
A
and
X
as coordinates

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=A_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}y_{i}H_{i},

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}H_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}E_{i},

\displaystyle F(A,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}(A,X)H_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}g_{i}(A,X)E_{i}.

Now we compute the Jacobian of
F
at
(a_{0},1)
:

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}=0,

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}=0,\qquad\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}=0,

and

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}=0,\qquad\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}=-\delta_{ij}\beta_{i}(A_{0}),

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Since
A_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
, we have
\beta_{i}(A_{0})\neq 0
for all
i
. Therefore,
F
has the full rank at
(a_{0},1)
and by the analytic implicit function theorem, we can find an analytic local section
s:O\to A^{+}\times G
of
F
in a neighborhood
O
of
a_{0}
. The logarithm of the
A^{+}
-factor of
s(a)
is precisely the Jordan projection of
a\in O
. Thus
\lambda
is analytic.
∎

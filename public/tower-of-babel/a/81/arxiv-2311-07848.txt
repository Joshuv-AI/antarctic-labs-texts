Abstract
. In this paper, first we give a weak version of Ikeda’s conjecture on the period of the Ikeda-Miyawaki lift. Next, we confirm this conjecture rigorously in some cases.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate Ikeda’s conjecture on the period of the Ikeda-Miyawaki lift.
Let
k,n
be positive integers such that
k\equiv n+1\mod 2
and
k>n
.
For a Hecke eigenform
h
in the Kohnen plus subspace of weight
k+1/2
for
\varGamma_{0}(4)
, and a primitive form
g
of weight
k+n+1
for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, Ikeda
[
17
]
constructed a cusp form
\mathcal{F}_{h,g}
of weight
k+n+1
for
Sp_{2n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
with the following property:

If
\mathcal{F}_{h,g}
is not identically
0
, it is a Hecke eigenform and its standard
L
-function
L(s,\mathcal{F}_{h,g},\mathrm{St})
is expressed as

L(s,\mathcal{F}_{h,g},\mathrm{St})=L(s,g,\mathrm{St})\prod_{i=1}^{n}L(s+k+n-i,f),

where
f
is the primitive form of weight
2k
for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
corresponding to
h
via the Shimura correspondence, and
L(s,f)
is Hecke’s
L
-function of
f
.
The existence of such a Hecke eigenform was conjectured by Miyawaki
[
29
]
. Therefore we call
\mathcal{F}_{h,g}
the Ikeda-Miyawaki lift of
h
and
g
.
To ensure the non-vanishing of
\mathcal{F}_{h,g}
, Ikeda
[
17
]
proposed the following conjecture concerning the period (or the Petersson norm ) of
\mathcal{F}_{h,g}
:

2^{(2n-1)k+4n}\dfrac{\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g},{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g}\rangle}{\displaystyle\langle g,g\rangle\langle h,h\rangle}=\displaystyle\Lambda(k+n,\mathrm{St}(g)\otimes f)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\xi}(2i)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\Lambda}(2i+1,f,\mathrm{St})

(cf. Conjecture
3.1
). As for the definitions of several
L
-values in question, see (
2.1
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
).
Ikeda also confirmed the conjecture approximately in some cases. But, as far as we know, there is no examples that confirms this conjecture rigorously.

In this paper, first we deduce a weak version of the above conjecture. More precisely, put

C_{h,g}=\dfrac{\displaystyle\langle g,g\rangle\langle h,h\rangle}{\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g},{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g}\rangle}\displaystyle\Lambda(k+n,\mathrm{St}(g)\otimes f)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\xi}(2i)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\Lambda}(2i+1,f,\mathrm{St}).

Then we prove that
C_{h,g}
belongs to
\mathbb{Q}(f)\mathbb{Q}(g)
, where
\mathbb{Q}(f)
and
\mathbb{Q}(g)

are the Hecke fields of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
and
\mathbb{Q}(g)
, respectively (cf. Theorem
3.6
). As a result
we prove the algebraicity of
\dfrac{\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g},{\mathcal{F}}_{h,g}\rangle}{\displaystyle\langle g,\ g\rangle^{2}\langle f,\ f\rangle^{n-1}\langle h,h\rangle\Omega_{-}(f)}
, where
\Omega_{-}(f)
is Manin-Shimura period for
f
, which will be defined in Section 1 (cf. Theorem
3.8
).
Our proof relies on the algebraicities of critical values of the product of the Hecke
L
-functions and the triple product
L
-functions of elliptic cusp forms and the standard
L
-functions of Siegel cusp forms (cf.
[
7
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
).

Next, we confirm Ikeda’s conjecture rigorously in some cases (cf. Theorems
6.4
and
6.8
). The idea is to compute the special values of
L
-functions stated above rigorously. We have an algorithm for computing the special values of the triple product
L
-functions of elliptic cusp forms (cf.
[
13
, Theorem 4.9]
). We also have an algorithm for computing the special vales of the product of the Hecke
L
-functions elliptic cusp forms
(cf. Proposition
5.1
). One of key ingredients in the proof of main examples is to compute the standard
L
-functions of Siegel cusp forms for
Sp_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
using the pullback formula of the Siegel Eisenstein series. This method has been implicitly used in
[
13
, Section 5]
. However, there we used the pull-back formula without differential operator. In this paper, we use the pullback formula with differential operator (cf. Theorem
4.8
), which allows us to compute the values in question more efficiently (cf. Remark
4.9
).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce several
L
-functions and review their algebraicity. In Section 3, we review the Ikeda-Miyawaki lift and Ikeda’s conjecture on its period, and prove a weaker version of Ikeda’s conjecture.
In Section 4, we review the pullback of the Siegel-Eisenstein series. Moreover, we give an algorithm for computing the special value of the standard
L
-function
L(s,F,\mathrm{,}{St})
at
s=k-3
when
F
is a Hecke eigenform of weight
k+2
for
Sp_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. In Section 5, we give an algorithm for computing the special value of the product of Hecke
L
-functions of primitive forms for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Finally, in Section 6, we confirm Ikeda’s conjecture in some cases using the results in Sections 5, 6, and
[
13
, Theorem 4.9]
.

3.
Ikeda-Miyawaki lift

Throughout this section, fix positive integers
k,n
such that
k\equiv n+1\mod 2

and

.
We denote by
S_{k+1/2}^{+}(\varGamma_{0}(4))
the Kohnen plus subspace of weight
k+1/2
for
\varGamma_{0}(4)
.
For the detail of the Kohnen plus subspace, see
[
25

Pullback of Siegel Eisenstein series

To prove our main result, we give an algorithm for computing the standard
L
-value for a cuspidal Hecke eigenform for
\operatorname{Sp}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
(cf. Theorem
4.8
). This type of algorithm was given in the case
F
is a cuspidal Hecke eigenform for
\operatorname{Sp}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
with

(cf.
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
21
]
,
[

]
).
To give such an algorithm, first we express a certain modular form arising from the pullback of Siegel Eisenstein series as a linear combination of Hecke eigenforms with their standard
L
-values as coefficients (cf. Theorem
4.3
).
We have carried it out in
[
23
]
, and here
we give its refinement.
For a non-negative integer
m
, put

\Gamma_{m}(s)=\pi^{m(m-1)/4}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(s-{i-1\over 2}).

Let
n,l
be positive integers such that
l
is even. Define the Eisenstein series
E_{n,l}(Z,s)
by

E_{n,l}(Z,s)=\bigl(\det\mathrm{Im}(Z)\bigr)^{s}\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}j(\gamma,Z)^{-l}|j(\gamma,Z)|^{-2s},

where
\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})_{\infty}=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0_{n}&D\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\Big\}
.
Then
E_{n,l}(Z,s)
converges absolutely as a function of
s
if the real part of
s
is large enough.
It has a meromorphic continuation to the whole
s
-plane, and it belong to

M_{l}^{\infty}(\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Moreover it is holomorphic and finite at
s=0
, which will be denoted by
E_{n,l}(Z)
.
In particular, if
E_{n,l}(Z)
belongs to
M_{l}(\operatorname{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})),
we put

E_{n,l}^{*}(Z)=Z(n,l)E_{n,l}(Z),

where

Z(n,l)=\zeta(1-l)\prod_{i=1}^{[n/2]}\zeta(1+2i-2l).

From now on, for an element
A\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})_{\geq 0}
, we denote by
c_{n.l}(A)
the
A
-th Fourier coefficient
c_{E_{n,l}^{*}}(A)
of
E_{n,l}^{*}(Z)
.
To determine the Fourier coefficient of
E_{n,l}^{*}(Z)
, we define a polynomial attached to local Siegel series.
For a prime number
p
, let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the field of
p
-adic numbers and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

p
-adic integers. For an element

where
{\bf e}_{p}
is the additive character of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
{\bf e}_{p}(m)={\bf e}(m)
for

, and

\nu_{p}(R)=[R\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}+\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}]
.
We define
\chi_{p}(a)
for

as follows:

\chi_{p}(a):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}+1&\text{ if }\ {\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\sqrt{a})={\mathbb{Q}}_{p},\\
-1&\text{ if }\ {\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\sqrt{a})/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\text{ is quadratic unramified},\\
0&\text{ if }\ {\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\sqrt{a})/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\text{ is quadratic ramified}.\end{array}\right.

For an element

n

For a nondegenerate half-integral matrix

over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

\gamma_{p}(B,X)
in
X

\gamma_{p}(B,X):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-X)\prod_{i=1}^{n/2}(1-p^{2i}X^{2})(1-p^{n/2}\xi_{p}(B)X)^{-1}&\text{ if }n\text{ is \ even},\\
(1-X)\prod_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}(1-p^{2i}X^{2})&\text{ if }\ n\text{ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

Then it is well known that there exists a unique polynomial
F_{p}(B,X)
in
X
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with constant term

[
20
]

For any

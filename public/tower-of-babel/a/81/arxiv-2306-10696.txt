On the functional equations of Siegel Eisenstein series of an odd prime level p p
Keiichi Gunji

Abstract
. Let p p be an odd prime. In this paper we write down the functional equations of Siegel Eisenstein series of degree n n , level p p with quadratic or trivial characters. First we study the U ⁡ ( p ) U(p) action in the space of Siegel Eisenstein series to get U ⁡ ( p ) U(p) -eigen functions. Secondly we show that the functional equations for U ⁡ ( p ) U(p) -eigen Eisenstein series are quite simple and easy to write down. Consequently the matrix that represents functional equations of Siegel Eisenstein series are given by the products of simple matrices.



1.
Introduction

Functional equations are one of the most important result in the theory of Eisenstein series. The general theories for functional equations in terms of the automorphic representations are already established by Langlands (
[
8
]
).

In terms of the classical notation, first Kaufold got the result in degree
2
case(
[
6
]
). Kalinin gave the functional equation for any degree case, just rewriting the Langlands or Harish-Chandra’s results in classical terminologies (
[
4
]
).
After that Mizumoto (
[
10
]
) gave another proof for functional equations by computing the constant term of the Fourier expansions and unfolding methods.

We shall introduce their results. Let
\varGamma^{n}=\mathit{Sp}(n,\mathbb{Z})
be the symplectic modular group of rank
n
, matrix-size
2n
and
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})
be its subgroup consisting of the elements whose lower left
(n\times n)
-blocks are zero matrices. For
s\in\mathbb{C}
, an even integer
k
and
Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
(Siegel upper half space) we define

E_{k}^{n}(Z,s)=\det(Y)^{s-k/2}\sum_{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\varGamma^{n}}\det(CZ+D)^{-k}|\det(CZ+D)|^{-2s+k}.

The right-hand side converges absolutely for
\re(2s)>n+1
and it continues meromorphically to whole
s
-plane.
We put

\mathbb{E}_{k}^{n}(Z,s):=\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}(s)}\xi(2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)E_{k}^{n}(Z,s),

here
\Gamma_{n}(s)=\pi^{n(n-1)/4}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(s-i/2)
and
\xi(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
is the completed Riemann zeta function, that satisfies
\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 2]
,
[
10
, Corollary 6.6]
)
.

Let
\kappa_{n}=(n+1)/2
. Under the above notations, the functional equation

\mathbb{E}_{k}^{n}(Z,s)=\mathbb{E}_{k}^{n}(Z,\kappa_{n}-s)

holds.

The aim of this paper is to consider functional equations for level
p
case, with an odd prime number
p
. Let

\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathit{Sp}(n,\mathbb{Z})\biggm|C\equiv 0\bmod p\right\}.

As is well-known that the space of Siegel Eisenstein series of degree
n
with respect to
\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)
is
(n+1)
-dimensional, thus our functional equations are written in vector valued form. In elliptic modular case, i.e. the case of
n=1
, the explicit forms of functional equations are already known (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 4.4.2]
), but in the higher degree case it is predicted to be quite complicated.

Our basic strategy is investigating
U(p)
-operator on the space of Siegel Eisenstein series. We explain it more precisely. Let
\psi=\chi_{0}
or
\chi_{p}
be the Dirichlet character modulo
p
, here
\chi_{0}
stands for the trivial character and
\chi_{p}
stands for the quadratic character. Let
k
be an integer such that
\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
.
We can take
\{w_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq n}
as a representative set of the double co-set
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\varGamma^{n}/\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)
, where
w_{j}
is given in (
2.1
). Then the Siegel Eisenstein series corresponding to
w_{j}
is defined by

E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{j};Z,s)=\det(Y)^{s-k/2}\sum_{\gamma}\psi^{*}(\gamma)\det(CZ+D)^{-k}|\det(CZ+D)|^{-2s+k},

here
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)
runs through the set
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})w_{j}\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p)
,
\psi^{*}
is defined in (
2.2
). It also converges for
\re(2s)>n+1
and continues meromorphically to whole
s
-plane.
We set
\mathcal{E}_{k,s}^{n}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space spanned by
\{E_{k,\psi}^{n}(w_{j};Z,s)\mid 0\leq j\leq n\}
. Then the eigenvalues of
U(p)
-operator on
\mathcal{E}^{n}_{k,s}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
are given by
\{p^{l(s,j)}\mid 0\leq j\leq n\}
with
l(s,j)=n(k/2-s)+2sj-j(j+1)/2
(Corollary
2.5
). Let
\kappa_{n}=(n+1)/2
. By an easy calculation one can find

l(s,j)=l(\kappa_{n}-s,n-j),

thus we see the following observation. Let
E^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)\in\mathcal{E}^{n}_{k,s}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
be the suitably normalized
U(p)
-eigenfunction with the eigenvalue
p^{l(s,\nu)}
; it is defined in Proposition
2.10
, explicitly. Then we may expect the existence of the functional equation of the form

E^{n,(\nu)}_{n,\psi}(Z,s)=C(s)E^{n,(n-\nu)}_{n,\psi}\left(Z,\kappa_{n}-s\right)

with some function
C(s)
in
s
. In the case of
\psi=\chi_{0}
, it actually follows from Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand the case of
\psi=\chi_{p}
, we can prove it by using the general theory of Langlands. Now we state our main theorem. Let
\delta_{p}=0
or
1
according as
p\equiv 1
or
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. We define

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{0},s)\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}(s)}\,\xi(2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{p},s)\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)}\,\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s),

here,

\xi(\chi_{p},s)=\left(\frac{p}{\pi}\right)^{(s+\delta_{p})/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\delta_{p}}{2}\right)L(\chi_{p},s)

is the completed Dirichlet
L
-function, that satisfies
\xi(\chi_{p},1-s)=\xi(\chi_{p},s)
; the factor
\gamma_{\nu}(\psi,s)
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{0},s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-p^{-2s}}{1-p^{\nu-2s}}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{1-p^{2i-4s}}{1-p^{2\nu+1-2i-4s}},

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{p},s)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{p}p^{-1/2})^{\nu}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{1-p^{2i-4s}}{1-p^{2\nu+1-2i-4s}},

with
\varepsilon_{p}=1
or
\sqrt{-1}
according as
p\equiv 1
or
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
4.8
)
.

We have the functional equation

\mathbb{E}^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)=\mathbb{E}^{n,(n-\nu)}_{k,\psi}(Z,\kappa_{n}-s).

In terms of the basis
\{E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{\nu};Z,s)\}
of
\mathcal{E}^{n}_{k,s}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
, the functional equations are written as follows. Set

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}(s)}\,\xi(2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)}\,\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s).

Let
B_{\psi}(s)
be the representative matrix of the eigenfunctions
E^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\psi}(Z,s)
using the basis
E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{j};Z,s)
, that is defined in (
2.10
). Note that
B_{\psi}(s)
is strict upper triangular.
We denote by
T_{\psi}(s)=(t_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
the anti-diagonal matrix of size
n+1
, whose
(n-\nu,\nu)
-component
(0\leq\nu\leq n)
is given by
\gamma_{\nu}(\psi,s)\gamma_{n-\nu}(\psi,\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.4
, Theorem
4.9
)
.

We have the functional equation

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{0};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{n};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\end{pmatrix}=B_{\psi}(\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}T_{\psi}(s)B_{\psi}(s)\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{0};Z,s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{n};Z,s)\end{pmatrix}.

Thus we can write the representative matrix of the functional equations by the product of upper triangular matrices and the anti-diagonal matrix.

We explain the contents of this paper. In section
2
we study the action of
U(p)
-operator on the space of Siegel Eisenstein series. The results are already known by Walling (
[
14
]
), however we use the different methods from
[
14
]
. We can regard the space of modular forms as functions in
\mathit{GSp}(\mathbb{A})
in a standard manner, then the problem becomes to study the action of
U(p)
-operator on the space of degenerate principal series. This action is purely
p
-local and simple calculations induce our results. In section
3
we study the Fourier expansions of Siegel Eisenstein series. The constant teams play crucial roles in studying functional equations. In section
4
we prove our main results.
Finally in section
5
we compute the example in the case of degree
2
with the quadratic character.

We denote by
\mathbf{1}_{n}
the identity matrix of size
n
. We put
w_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathbf{1}_{n}\\
\mathbf{1}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}
. For a commutative ring

, we define

\displaystyle\mathit{GSp}(n,R)

\displaystyle=\{g\in\mathit{GL}(2n,R)\mid{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}\!gw_{n}g=\mu(g)w_{n},\ \mu(g)\in R^{\times}\},

\displaystyle\mathit{Sp}(n,R)

\displaystyle=\{g\in\mathit{GSp}(n,R)\mid\mu(g)=1\}.

For

,
(n\times n)
-matrices
A_{g}
,
B_{g}
,
C_{g}
and

are defined by

C_{g}&D_{g}\end{pmatrix}

\mathit{GSp}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})=\{g\in\mathit{GSp}(n,\mathbb{R})\mid\mu(g)>0\}
.

We define the automorphic factor
j(g,Z)
for
g\in\mathit{GSp}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
by

j(g,Z)=\mu(g)^{-n/2}\det(C_{g}Z+D_{g}),

and for the function

on
\mathbb{H}_{n}

Note that for a scalar matrix

.

. We put

For a Dirichlet character
\psi

, let

A_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)=\{f\colon\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}\mid f|_{k}\gamma(Z)=\psi(\det D_{\gamma})f(Z)\ \text{for $\gamma\in\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)$}\},

M_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)=\{f\in A_{k}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)\mid\text{$f$ is holomorphic}\},

that is the space of holomorphic Siegel modular forms of degree

, level

and character
\psi
. If

\psi=\chi_{0}
or
\chi_{p}
, here
\chi_{0}
stands for the trivial character modulo

\chi_{p}
stands for the quadratic character modulo

.

The set of symmetric matrix of size
n
with entries in

is denoted by
\Sym^{n}(R)
. We put
\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}
the set of half integral matrices. For

2.
The action of
U(p)
-operator on the space of Siegel Eisenstein series

In this section we study the action of
U(p)
-operator on the space of Siegel Eisenstein series. It is already investigated by Walling (
[
14
, Section 4]
), that works for any square-free level.
Here we introduce another method. We only consider the level
p
case, however we can avoid complicated calculation by lifting modular forms of classical notations to modular forms of adelic notations.

2.1.
U(p)
-operator in classical notation

Let
\tau_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{n}&0\\
0&p\mathbf{1}_{n}\end{pmatrix}
.
For
f\in A_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
, we define
U(p)
-operator on the space
A_{k}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
by

\bigl(U(p)f\bigr)(Z)=p^{nk/2-n(n+1)/2}\sum_{\gamma\in\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)\backslash\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)\tau_{p}\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)}\psi(\det A_{\gamma})f|_{k}\gamma.

It is well-known that a representative set of
\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)\backslash\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)\tau_{p}\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)
is given by

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{n}&S\\
0&p\mathbf{1}_{n}\end{pmatrix}\biggm|S\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p)\right\},

thus we have

(U(p)f)(Z)=p^{-n(n+1)/2}\sum_{S\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p)}f\left(\frac{Z+S}{p}\right).

Because of the above, the
U(p)
-action becomes quite simple in terms of Fourier coefficients.
For
f\in M_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
, if the Fourier expansion of
f
is given by

f(Z)=\sum_{T\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}}C(T)\mathbf{e}(TZ),

then

\bigl(f|U(p)\bigr)(Z)=\sum_{T\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}}C(pT)\mathbf{e}(TZ).

Next we define the space of Siegel Eisenstein series. For
0\leq r\leq n
, we put

w_{n,r}=w_{r}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0_{r}&&-\mathbf{1}_{r}&\\
&\mathbf{1}_{n-r}&&0_{n-r}\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{1}_{r}&&0_{r}&\\
&0_{n-r}&&\mathbf{1}_{n-r}\end{array}\right).

(2.1)

We set

\displaystyle P_{n}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\mu A&B\\
0&{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}\!A^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathit{GSp}(n,\mathbb{R})\right\},

\displaystyle P_{0,n}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}\!A^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathit{Sp}(n,\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Then a representative set of
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathit{Sp}(n,\mathbb{Z})/\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)
is given by
\{w_{r}\}_{0\leq r\leq n}
.

Assume that
\psi=\chi_{0}
or
\chi_{p}
. We define the function
\psi^{*}
on
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})w_{r}\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p)
by

\psi^{*}(\gamma)=\psi(\det D_{\eta})\psi(\det D_{\kappa}),\quad\gamma=\eta w_{r}\kappa,\ \eta\in P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z}),\ \kappa\in\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p).

(2.2)

One can check that
\psi^{*}
is well-defined.
Let
k
be an integer such that
\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. For
Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
, we define the Siegel Eisenstein series of degree
n
, weight
k
, level
p
with character
\psi
corresponding to the cusp
w_{r}
by

E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)=\det(Y)^{s-k/2}\sum_{\gamma}\psi^{*}(\gamma)\,j(\gamma,Z)^{-k}\,|j(\gamma,Z)|^{-2s+k},

here
\gamma
runs thorough the set
P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash P_{0,n}(\mathbb{Z})w_{r}\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p)
.
The right hand side converges when
2\re(s)>n+1
. As is well-known, it continues meromorphically in whole
s
-plane (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 1]
and
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
). Note that if
\psi
is neither trivial nor quadratic, then
E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)
is defined only when
r=0
or
n
. We have
E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)\in A_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
and
E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,k/2)\in M_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
.
If the the Fourier expansion is given by

E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)=\sum_{T\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}}b(T,Y,s)\mathbf{e}(TX),

then

U(p)E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)=\sum_{T\in\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}}b(pT,p^{-1}Y,s)\mathbf{e}(TX).

(2.3)

We define the space of Siegel Eisenstein series by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k,s}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)

\displaystyle=\langle E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{r};Z,s)\mid 0\leq r\leq n\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}.

This space is closed under the action of
U(p)
-operator.

2.2.
U(p)
-operator in adelic notatoion

In order to investigate the action of
U(p)
-operator, it is convenient to consider it in adelic notation. Let
\mathbb{A}
be the adele ring of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
G=\mathit{GSp}_{n}
and
G_{\infty}^{+}=\mathit{GSp}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})
.
We define the compact group
\prod_{v\leq\infty}K_{v}\subset G(\mathbb{A})
by

K_{v}=\begin{cases}\mathit{Sp}(n,\mathbb{R})\cap\mathit{O}(2n)&\text{if $v=\infty$,}\\
\mathit{GSp}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{q})&\text{if $v=q\neq p$ is a finite place,}\\
\{\gamma\in\mathit{GSp}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\mid C_{\gamma}\equiv 0\bmod p\}&\text{if $v=p$.}\end{cases}

Though
\prod_{v}K_{v}
is not a maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A})
, we have a decomposition

G(\mathbb{A})=G(\mathbb{Q})G_{\infty}^{+}\prod_{\text{$q$:prime}}K_{q}.

(2.4)

The Dirichlet character
\psi
modulo
p
can be lifted to the idele character
\omega
as follows. Let
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
be the idele group of
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, then we have

\quad\mathbb{A}^{\times}=\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\prod_{\text{$q$:prime}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times},

with the set
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
of positive real numbers. For
g=\gamma\delta\alpha\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{q})_{q}\in\prod\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}
as above decomposition, we define the character
\omega\colon\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

\omega(g)=\psi(\varpi(\alpha_{p}))^{-1}

under the natural surjection
\varpi\colon\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
. We denote by
\omega_{v}
the
v
-component of
\omega
, i.e.
\omega_{v}\colon\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{\times}\xrightarrow{\omega}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Written explicitly, if
q\neq p
be a finite place then

\omega_{q}(q^{m}\alpha)=\psi(q)^{m}\quad\text{with $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}$},

and

\omega_{p}(p^{m}\alpha)=\psi(\varpi(\alpha))^{-1}\quad\text{with $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}$.}

For the infinite place
v=\infty
,

\omega_{\infty}(d)=\psi\left(\frac{d}{|d|}\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\psi(-1)=1$}\\
\mathrm{sign}(d)&\text{if $\psi(-1)=-1$}.\end{cases}

For
f\in A_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
we define the
\mathbb{C}
-valued function
\Lambda(f)
on
G(\mathbb{A})
as follows. Let
\mathbf{i}_{n}=\sqrt{-1}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{n}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
.
For
g=\gamma g_{\infty}\kappa\in G(\mathbb{A})
as in (
2.4
), we define

\Lambda(f)(g)=\omega_{p}(\det D_{\kappa_{p}})j(g_{\infty},\mathbf{i}_{n})^{-k}f(g_{\infty}\langle\mathbf{i}_{n}\rangle).

This is well-defined since
f\in A_{k}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\psi)
. Then
\Phi=\Lambda(f)
satisfies the following properties.

\begin{split}&\Phi(\gamma g)=\Phi(g)\quad\text{for}\quad\gamma\in G(\mathbb{Q}),\\
&\Phi(gu)=\Phi(g)j(u,\mathbf{i}_{n})^{-k}\quad\text{for}\quad u\in K_{\infty},\\
&\Phi(g\kappa)=\Phi(g)\omega_{p}(\det D_{\kappa_{p}})\quad\text{for}\quad\kappa=(\kappa_{q})\in\prod_{q}K_{q}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Conversely for a function
\Phi\colon G(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
2.5
), we define the function
\Pi(\Phi)\colon\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\Pi(\Phi)(Z)=\Phi(g_{\infty})j(g_{\infty},\mathbf{i}_{n})^{k},

here
g_{\infty}\in\mathit{GSp}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})
is taken so that
g_{\infty}\langle\mathbf{i}_{n}\rangle=Z
. Then
\Pi(\Phi)\in A_{k}(\varGamma_{0}^{n}(p),\psi)
, and the maps
\Lambda
and
\Pi
are inverse of each other.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A})
be the space of the smooth functions on
G(\mathbb{A})
, that is
C^{\infty}
-class in infinite place and locally constants in finite places. We consider the
U(p)
-operator on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A})
. Put

H_{p}=K_{p}\begin{pmatrix}p\mathbf{1}_{n}&0\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{n}\end{pmatrix}K_{p}

and we define the function
\widetilde{\omega}_{p}\colon H_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
by

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Proof.

For simplicity we write

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that

We put

\displaystyle W^{l}_{m}(\chi_{0})

Fourier expansion of the Siegel Eisenstein series

In order to write down the functional equations explicitly, we consider the Fourier expansion of the Siegel Eisenstein series.
We put for

\mathbb{Z}_{\rm prim}^{(n,r)}=\{A\in M_{n,r}(\mathbb{Z})\mid\exists B\in M_{n,n-r}(\mathbb{Z})\ \text{such that}(A,B)\in\mathit{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\}.

For matrices
A

Then by a similar argument of
[

we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

\displaystyle E^{n}_{k,\psi}(w_{\nu};Z,s)

\displaystyle=(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\sum_{r=\nu}^{n}\sum_{N\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}}\mathscr{S}_{r}^{\nu}(\psi,N,2s)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{Z}^{(n,r)}_{\mathrm{prim}}/\mathit{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})}\xi_{r}\Bigl(Y[Q],N,s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\Bigr)\mathbf{e}(N[{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}\!Q]X).

Here

is the confluent hypergeometric function investigated in
[
12
]
;
\mathscr{S}_{r}^{\nu}(\psi,N,s)
is a kind of Siegel series defined as follows. Let

\mathcal{M}_{r}=\left\{(C,D)\in M_{r,2r}(\mathbb{Z})\Bigm|\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$(C,D)$ is symmetric and co-prime,}\\
\det C>0\end{array}\right\}.

(C,D)

. Then the map

\mathit{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{r}\to\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q}),\quad(C,D)\mapsto C^{-1}D

is bijective. For
T=C^{-1}D\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
we put
\delta(T)=\det C
.
For

set

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\nu}_{r}

\displaystyle=\{(C,D)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}\mid\rank(C\bmod p)=\nu\},

\displaystyle\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\{R=C^{-1}D\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})\mid(C,D)\in\mathcal{M}^{\nu}_{r}\}.

We define the function
\widetilde{\psi}
on
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}
as follows. For
R=C^{-1}D\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}
with
(C,D)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}^{\nu}
, there exists
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in P_{0,r}(\mathbb{Z})w_{r,\nu}\varGamma^{r}_{0}(p)
.
Then we put
\widetilde{\psi}(R)=\psi^{*}(\gamma)
, where
\psi^{*}
is defined in (
2.2
). Under the above notation we define

\mathscr{S}^{\nu}_{r}(\psi,N,s)=\sum_{R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{(\nu)}}\widetilde{\psi}(R)\delta(R)^{-s}\mathbf{e}(RN),

here we write
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{(\nu)}
instead of
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}\bmod\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We can show that the Siegel series
\mathscr{S}_{r}^{\nu}(\psi,N,s)
has an Euler product expression.
For a prime number
q
, let
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})_{q}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{q^{i}}\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
. Any
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
has a decomposition
R=R_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}R_{i}
with

and
R_{i}\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})_{q_{i}}
, where

are mutually distinct primes. This decomposition is unique up to
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then we have

\delta(R)=\delta(R_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{s}\delta(R_{i}).

We put
\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}_{p}=\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}\cap\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})_{p}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
is written as
R=R_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}R_{i}
with
R_{0}\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})_{p}
and
R_{i}\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})_{q_{i}}
.
Then
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}
if and only if
R_{0}\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}_{p}
,
and in that case we have

\widetilde{\psi}(R)=\widetilde{\psi}(R_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\psi(\delta(R_{i})).

Proof.

For
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
, there exists
U,V\in\mathit{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

R=U\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}/\delta_{1}\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{r}/\delta_{r}\end{pmatrix}V,\quad\delta_{i}>0,\ \delta_{i}\mid\delta_{i+1},\ (\delta_{i},\lambda_{i})=1,

by the elementary divisor theorem. Put

C=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{1}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\delta_{r}\end{pmatrix}U^{-1},\quad D=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\lambda_{r}\end{pmatrix}V,

then
(C,D)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
and
R=C^{-1}D
.
Assume that
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}
for some
\nu
, then
(p,\delta_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq\nu
and
p\mid\delta_{i}
for
\nu+1\leq i\leq r
. Since
(CU,D\,{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}U^{-1})
is also symmetric and co-prime, if we put
W=V\,{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=8.0pt}^{t}U^{-1}
and write

W=\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}&W_{2}\\
W_{3}&W_{4}\end{pmatrix}\quad W_{1}\in M_{\nu}(\mathbb{Z}),\ W_{4}\in M_{r-\nu}(\mathbb{Z}),

then
W_{3}\equiv 0\bmod p
. In this case we can show

\widetilde{\psi}(R)=\psi(\det W_{4})\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\psi(\delta_{i})\prod_{i=\nu+1}^{r}\psi(\lambda_{i}).

Next we consider
R_{0}
. Put
\delta=\delta(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\delta_{i}
. Let
e=\ord_{p}\delta
and
\delta=p^{e}\delta^{\prime}
. If we write
1/\delta=m/p^{e}+l/\delta^{\prime}
, then we can take
R_{0}=\delta^{\prime}mR
. We also put
e_{i}=\ord_{p}\delta_{i}
for
\nu+1\leq i\leq r
and write
\delta_{i}=p^{e_{i}}\delta^{\prime}_{i}
. Then
R_{0}=C_{0}^{-1}D_{0}
with

C_{0}=\diag(1,\ldots,1,p^{e_{\nu+1}},\ldots,p^{e_{r}})U^{-1}
and

&m\delta^{\prime}\lambda_{\nu+1}/\delta^{\prime}_{\nu+1}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&m\delta^{\prime}\lambda_{r}/\delta_{r}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}V.

Thus we have proved that
R\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}
if and only if
R_{0}\in\Sym^{r}(\mathbb{Q})^{(\nu)}_{p}
. Moreover, since
m\delta^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod p^{e}
by definition, we have

\widetilde{\psi}(R_{0})=\psi(\det W_{4})\prod_{i=\nu+1}^{r}\psi(\lambda_{i})\prod_{i=\nu+1}^{r}\psi(\delta^{\prime}_{i})^{-1}.

Since
\delta^{\prime}=\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\delta_{i}\bigr)\bigl(\prod_{i=\nu+1}^{r}\delta^{\prime}_{i}\bigr)
, we have
\widetilde{\psi}(R)=\widetilde{\psi}(R_{0})\psi(\delta^{\prime})
, which proves the lemma.

(0\leq j\leq n)

is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(Y,s)

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{-1})^{-\nu k}2^{\nu(\nu+3)/2-2\nu s}\pi^{\nu(\nu+1)/2}\dfrac{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}(s)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\dfrac{\zeta(2s-\nu)}{\zeta(2s)}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\dfrac{\zeta(4s+2i-2\nu-1)}{\zeta(4s-2i)}\xi(2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}\left(Y,2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right).

Proposition 4.2
.

We have

\Lambda_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(Y,s)=\Lambda_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(n-\nu)}(Y,\kappa_{n}-s).

Together with Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
, our main result follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

We have a functional equation

\mathbb{E}^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\chi_{0}}(Z,s)=\mathbb{E}^{n,(n-\nu)}_{k,\chi_{0}}(Z,\kappa_{n}-s).

We can also write down the functional equations in terms of the basis
\{E^{n}_{k,\chi_{0}}(w_{i};Z,s)\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
of the space
\mathcal{E}^{n}_{k,s}(\varGamma^{n}_{0}(p),\chi_{0})
. We define

\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s)=\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}(s)}\,\xi(2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
T_{\chi_{0}}(s)
be the anti-diagonal matrix of size
n+1
, whose

(n-\nu,\nu)
-component is
\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{0},s)\gamma_{n-\nu}(\chi_{0},\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}
for
0\leq\nu\leq n
. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{0}}(w_{0};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{0}}(w_{n};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\end{pmatrix}=B_{\chi_{0}}(\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}T_{\chi_{0}}(s)B_{\chi_{0}}(s)\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{0}}(w_{0};Z,s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{0}}(w_{n};Z,s)\end{pmatrix},

here
B_{\chi_{0}}(s)
is the upper triangular matrix defined in (
2.10
).

In order to prove the Proposition
4.2
we quote two lemmas from
[
10
]
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
10
, Lemma 6.1]
)
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\frac{\Gamma_{m}(s)}{\Gamma_{m}(s+r)}=(-1)^{mr}\frac{\Gamma_{m}(\kappa_{m}-r-s)}{\Gamma_{m}(\kappa_{m}-s)}.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
10
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

For
0\leq\nu\leq n
, we have

\zeta^{(n)}_{n-\nu}(Y,s)=\det(Y)^{\nu/2-s}\,\frac{\prod_{j=n-\nu}^{n-1}\,\xi(2s-j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\,\xi(2s-j)}\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}\left(Y,\frac{n}{2}-s\right).

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

We put
\lambda=n-\nu
.
Multiplying suitable power of
\pi
and certain Gamma functions in order to change zeta functions into completed zeta functions, we have

\Lambda^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\chi_{0}}(s)=(\sqrt{-1})^{-\nu k}2^{\nu(\nu+3)/2-2\nu s}\pi^{\nu(\nu+1)/4}G_{\nu}(s)Z_{\nu}(s)

(4.1)

with

G_{\nu}(s)=\dfrac{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}(s)}\cdot\dfrac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma\left(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{\Gamma(2s-i)}{\Gamma\left(2s+i-\dfrac{2\nu+1}{2}\right)}

and

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\xi(2s-\nu)\prod_{j=1}^{[\nu/2]}\xi(4s+2j-2\nu-1)\prod_{j=[\nu/2]+1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta^{(n)}_{\nu}\left(Y,2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right).

First we treat
Z_{\nu}(s)
. We have

\prod_{j=1}^{[\nu/2]}\xi(4s+2j-2\nu-1)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
\text{even}\end{subarray}}^{\nu}\xi(4s+j-2\nu-1)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\nu-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j),

the latter is obtained by the translation
j\mapsto\nu-j
. Further

\prod_{j=[\nu/2]+1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu+1\\
\text{even}\end{subarray}}^{n}\xi(4s-j)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\not\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j),

thus we can rewrite
Z_{\nu}(s)
as follows. If
\nu\leq\lambda
then

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\xi(2s-\nu)\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu\\
j\not\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta^{(n)}_{\nu}\left(Y,2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right),

(4.2)

and if
\nu\geq\lambda
then

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\xi(2s-\nu)\prod_{j=0}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\lambda\\
j\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\nu-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta^{(n)}_{\nu}\left(Y,2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right).

(4.3)

By Lemma
4.6
we have

\displaystyle(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta^{(n)}_{\nu}\left(Y,2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=\nu}^{n-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)}{\prod_{j=0}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)}(\det Y)^{\kappa_{n}-s-k/2}\zeta_{\lambda}^{(n)}\left(Y,2(\kappa_{n}-s)-\frac{\lambda+1}{2}\right).

In the case of
\nu\leq\lambda
, decompose
\prod_{j=0}^{\lambda-1}=\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\cdot\prod_{j=\nu}^{\lambda-1}
then we have

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\xi(2s-\nu)\prod_{j=\lambda}^{n-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu\\
j\not\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{\kappa_{n}-s-k/2}\zeta_{\lambda}^{(n)}\left(Y,2(\kappa_{n}-s)-\frac{\lambda+1}{2}\right).

We have
\xi(2s-\nu)=\xi\bigl(2(\kappa_{n}-s)-\lambda\bigr)
and

\displaystyle\prod_{j=\lambda}^{n-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=\lambda}^{n-1}\xi(2-4s+\nu+j)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=\lambda}^{n-1}\xi\bigl(4(\kappa_{n}-s)-\lambda-n+j\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{\nu-1}\xi\bigl(4(\kappa_{n}-s)-\lambda-1-j\bigr),

the final equality is obtained by
j\mapsto n-1-j
. Similarly we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu\\
j\not\equiv\nu\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\lambda-1}\xi(4s-\nu-1-j)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=\nu\\
j\equiv\lambda\,(2)\end{subarray}}^{\lambda-1}\xi\bigl(4(\kappa_{n}-s)-\lambda-1-j),

therefore by comparing (
4.3
) we have
Z_{\nu}(s)=Z_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n}-s)
. Also in the case of
\nu\geq\lambda
, one can show that
Z_{\nu}(s)=Z_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n}-s)
holds similarly.

Next we consider
G_{\nu}(s)
. For an integer
r
, we denote
\widetilde{r}=r(r-1)/4
for simplicity.

If
\nu
is even then by
i\mapsto\nu/2-i
we have

\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\Gamma\left(2s+i-\frac{2\nu+1}{2}\right)=\prod_{i=0}^{\nu/2-1}\Gamma\left(2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}-i\right),

thus

\displaystyle\quad\ \Gamma_{\nu}\left(2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{\Gamma(2s-i)}{\Gamma\left(2s+i-\dfrac{2\nu+1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\pi^{\widetilde{\nu}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0\\
\text{odd}\end{subarray}}^{\nu-1}\Gamma\left(2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\nu/2}\Gamma(2s-i)=\pi^{\widetilde{\nu}}\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\Gamma(2s-i).

By a similar calculation the above identity holds also in the case of
\nu
is odd.
thus we have

G_{\nu}(s)=\pi^{\widetilde{\nu}}\frac{\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\,\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\Gamma(2s-i)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}(s)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma\left(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}\right)}.

By Legendre’s duplication formula we have

\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\Gamma(2s-i)=\pi^{-\nu/2}2^{2s\nu-\nu(\nu+3)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\left\{\Gamma\Bigl(s-\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\Bigl(s-\frac{i}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\right\},

therefore

G_{\nu}(s)=\pi^{-\nu(\nu+1)/4}2^{2s\nu-\nu(\nu+3)/2}\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Bigl(\Gamma_{\nu}(s)\Bigr)^{2}}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}(s)}.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}=\pi^{\widetilde{n}-\widetilde{\nu}}\prod_{i=\nu}^{n-1}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\right)=\pi^{\widetilde{n}-\widetilde{\nu}-\widetilde{\lambda}}\,\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{k}{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)}{\Gamma_{n}(s)}=\pi^{-\widetilde{n}+\widetilde{\nu}+\widetilde{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}\right)}.

As a consequence we have the following.
Let

G^{*}_{\nu}(s)=(\sqrt{-1})^{-\nu k}2^{\nu(\nu+3)/2-2\nu s}\pi^{\nu(\nu+1)/4}G_{\nu}(s),

then by (
4.1
) we have
\Lambda_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(s)=G^{*}_{\nu}(s)Z_{\nu}(s)
and

G^{*}_{\nu}(s)=(-1)^{-\nu k/2}\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}\,\frac{\Gamma_{\lambda}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}\Bigr)}.

We remark that
k
is even since
\chi_{0}(-1)=1
. By Lemma
4.5
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}(s)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\nu k/2}\frac{\Gamma_{\nu}\Bigl(\kappa_{n}-s-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\Bigl(\kappa_{n}-s-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\Bigr)},

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\lambda}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}+\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\Bigl(s-\dfrac{\nu}{2}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lambda k/2}\frac{\Gamma_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n}-s)}{\Gamma_{\lambda}\Bigl(\kappa_{n}-s-\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)},

which shows that
G^{*}_{\nu}(s)=G^{*}_{\lambda}(\kappa_{n}-s)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
The case of the quadratic character

Finally we consider the case of
\psi=\chi_{p}
. In this case we cannot induce the functional equations of
E^{n}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{\nu};Z,s)
from Theorem
1.1
.
However we also have the following.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a functional equation of the form

E^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,s)=C(s)E^{n,(n-\nu)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,\kappa_{n}-s).

Proof.

The desired assertion follows from the general theorem due to Langlands (
[
8
]
). Using the notation in subsection
2.3
, put

I^{(\nu)}(s,\omega)_{K}=\bigotimes_{v}I^{(\nu)}_{v}(s,\omega)_{K_{v}}

for
\nu=0
or
1
, and
I(s,\omega)_{K}=I^{(0)}(s,\omega)_{K}\oplus I^{(1)}(s,\omega)_{K}
. We define the intertwining operator
M(s)\colon I(s,\omega)_{K}\to I(n+1-s,\omega)_{K}
by

M(s)f(g)=\int_{\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{A})}f\left(w_{n}\begin{pmatrix}1&X\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}g\right)\,dX.

For
f\in I(s,\omega)_{K}
, the Eisenstein series
E(f,g,s)
is defined by

E(f,g,s)=\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{Q})}f(\gamma g).

Then Langlands showed that the functional equation

E(f,g,s)=E(M(s)f,g,\kappa_{n}-s)

holds (cf.
[
1
, Part I, Main Theorem (a), (ii)]
,
[
11
, VI.2.1. Theorem (1)]
). We denote by
\varphi(\nu,2s)
the
U(p)
-eigenfunction in
I_{p}(2s,\omega)_{K_{p}}
with the eigenvalue
p^{l(s,\nu)}
. Since the intertwining operator
M(s)
is compatible with
U(p)
-operator given in (
2.7
), we have
M(2s)\mathbf{f}_{\varphi(\nu,2s)}=C(s)\mathbf{f}_{\varphi(n-\nu,n+1-2s)}
for a certain function
C(s)
, for
l(s,\nu)=l(\kappa_{n}-s,n-\nu)
. This proves our assertion.
∎

We define the completed Dirichlet
L
-function as follows. For a Dirichlet character
\psi
we put
\delta_{\psi}=(1-\psi(-1))/2
. For simplicity we denote
\delta_{p}=\delta_{\chi_{p}}
, then
\delta_{p}=0
or
1
according as
p\equiv 1
or
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, equivalently
k
is even or odd, for
\chi_{p}(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. The completed Dirichlet
L
-function is now defined by

\xi(\chi_{p},s)=\left(\frac{p}{\pi}\right)^{(s+\delta_{p})/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\delta_{p}}{2}\right)L(\chi_{p},s),

that satisfies
\xi(\chi_{p},s)=\xi(\chi_{p},1-s)
.
We put
\varepsilon_{p}=1
or
\sqrt{-1}
according as
p\equiv 1
or
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. For

\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{p},s)=(\varepsilon_{p}p^{-1/2})^{\nu}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{1-p^{2i-4s}}{1-p^{2\nu+1-2i-4s}},

we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s)=\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{p},s)\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)}\,\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{0}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s).

Then the constant term of the Fourier expansion of
\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(Z,s)
is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(Y,s)=(\sqrt{-1})^{-\nu k}2^{\nu(\nu+3)/2-2\nu s}\pi^{\nu(\nu+1)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\varepsilon_{p}p^{-1/2})^{\nu}\dfrac{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{a}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\dfrac{L(\chi_{p},2s-\nu)}{L(\chi_{p},2s)}\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\dfrac{\zeta(4s+2i-2\nu-1)}{\zeta(4s-2i)}\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta_{\nu}^{(n)}\left(Y,2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right).

The following similar results as the case of trivial character holds.

Theorem 4.8
.

We have a functional equation

\mathbb{E}^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,s)=\mathbb{E}^{n,(n-\nu)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,\kappa_{n}-s).

We put

\mathbb{E}_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,}(w_{\nu};Z,s)=\dfrac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)}\,\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)\,E_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n}(w_{\nu};Z,s).

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
T_{\chi_{p}}(s)
be the anti-diagonal matrix of size
n+1
, whose

(n-\nu,\nu)
-component is
\gamma_{\nu}(\chi_{p},s)\gamma_{n-\nu}(\chi_{p},\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}
for
0\leq\nu\leq n
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{0};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{n};Z,\kappa_{n}-s)\end{pmatrix}=B_{\chi_{p}}(\kappa_{n}-s)^{-1}T_{\chi_{p}}(s)B_{\chi_{p}}(s)\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{0};Z,s)\\
\vdots\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{n};Z,s)\end{pmatrix}.

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

By Lemma
4.7
it suffices to show the functional equation of the constant term;
\Lambda_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(\nu)}(s)=\Lambda_{k,\chi_{p}}^{n,(n-\nu)}(\kappa_{n}-s)
.
The proof is quite similar to the proof of Proposition
4.2
. We only consider the case
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, equivalently to the case that
k
is odd.
By multiplying suitable power of
\pi
and Gamma functions we have

\Lambda^{n,(\nu)}_{k,\chi_{0}}(s)=(\sqrt{-1})^{-\nu(k-1)}2^{\nu(\nu+3)/2-2\nu s}\pi^{\nu(\nu+1)/4}G_{\nu}(s)Z_{\nu}(s)

with

\displaystyle G_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(2s-\dfrac{\nu+1}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}\cdot\dfrac{\Gamma\left(s+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(s-\dfrac{\nu-1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{[\nu/2]}\frac{\Gamma(2s-i)}{\Gamma\left(2s+i-\dfrac{2\nu+1}{2}\right)},

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\xi(\chi_{p},2s-\nu)\prod_{j=1}^{[\nu/2]}\xi(4s+2j-2\nu-1)\prod_{j=[\nu/2]+1}^{[n/2]}\xi(4s-2j)

\displaystyle\quad\times(\det Y)^{s-k/2}\zeta^{(n)}_{\nu}\left(Y,2s-\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right).

as (
4.2
) or (
4.3

\xi(n-\nu)

Also the term

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{n}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{\nu}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}=\pi^{\widetilde{n}-\widetilde{\nu}-\widetilde{\lambda}}\,\Gamma_{\lambda}\left(s-\frac{\nu-1}{2}+\frac{k-1}{2}\right),

In this section we calculate the example in the case of degree

N\in\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}

\det(Y)^{s-k/2}\mathscr{S}^{(1)}_{2}(\chi_{p},N,2s)\xi_{2}\left(Y,N,s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right).

[

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\left(Y,N,s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right)

On the other hand
\mathscr{S}_{2}^{(1)}(\chi_{p},N,s)
is written as follows. Let
D_{N}
be the absolute value of the discriminant of the quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\det(2N)})

\det(2N)=D_{N}\mathfrak{f}^{2}

f_{q}=\ord_{q}\mathfrak{f}
for each prime
q
. We set
\chi_{N}
the quadratic character associated with

\chi_{N}(-1)=-1
and for a prime number
q

(q,D_{N})=1

\chi_{N}(q)=\left(\dfrac{-D_{N}}{q}\right)

\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)=\begin{cases}0&a\equiv 0\bmod 2,\\
1&a\equiv\pm 1\bmod 8,\\
-1&a\equiv\pm 5\bmod 8.\end{cases}

Let
N[\gamma]=p^{m}(\alpha,p^{t}\beta)
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
for some
\gamma\in\mathit{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Then
p\mid D_{N}
if and only if
t
is odd. If
t
is even then the Dirichlet character
\chi_{N}\chi_{p}
is primitive, on the other hand if
t
is odd then we have
\chi_{N}\chi_{p}=\chi_{N}^{\prime}\chi_{0}
for a certain primitive character
\chi_{N}^{\prime}
. We put
\chi_{N}^{*}=\chi_{N}\chi_{p}
or
\chi_{N}^{\prime}
according as
t
is even or odd respectively. We put
l_{N}=f_{p}-m+\ord_{p}D_{N}

or
(t+1)/2
according as
t
is even or odd respectively. Then by
[
6
, Hifrssatz 10]
we can write

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{2}^{(1)}(\chi_{p},N,2s)

\displaystyle=S_{2}^{(1)}(\chi_{p},N,s)_{p}\frac{L(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)(1-\chi_{N}^{*}(p)p^{1-2s})}{L(\chi_{p},2s)\zeta(4s-2)(1-p^{2-4s})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{q\neq p}F_{N}^{(q)}(\chi_{p}(q)q^{-2s}),

Here
F_{N}^{(q)}(X)
is a certain polynomial in
X
, that equals to
1
unless
q\mid f_{q}
. It satisfies the functional equation

F_{N}^{(q)}(q^{-3}X^{-1})=(q^{3}X^{2})^{-f_{q}}F_{N}^{(q)}(X).

(5.1)

The term
S_{2}^{(1)}(\chi_{p},N,2s)
is computed in
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
. We have

\displaystyle S_{2}^{(1)}(\chi_{p},N,2s)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{p}\chi_{p}(\alpha)p^{(2-2s)m+3/2-2s}\frac{1-p^{2-4s}}{(1-p^{3-4s})(1-\chi_{N}^{*}(p)p^{1-2s})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigl(1-p^{(3-4s)l_{N}}-\chi_{N}^{*}(p)p^{1-2s}(1-p^{(3-4s)(l_{N}-1)})\bigr).

Therefore the Fourier coefficient
C(N,s)
of

\mathbb{E}^{2,(1)}_{k,\chi_{p}}(Z,s)=\varepsilon_{p}p^{-1/2}\frac{\Gamma_{2}\left(s+\dfrac{k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{2}\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)}\xi(\chi_{p},2s)\xi(4s-2)E^{2}_{k,\chi_{p}}(w_{1};Z,s)

at
N
is given by

\displaystyle C(N,s)=2^{2k+2}\pi^{-s+k+(1-\delta_{p})/2}\chi_{p}(\alpha)p^{s+(\delta_{p}-1)/2}\frac{\Gamma(2s-1)}{\Gamma\left(s+\dfrac{\delta_{p}-1}{2}\right)}L(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)

\displaystyle\times\det(N)^{s-3/2+k/2}\omega_{2}\left(2\pi Y,N;s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right)F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})\prod_{q\neq p}F_{N}^{(q)}(\chi_{p}(q)q^{-2s})

with

F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})=\frac{p^{(2-2s)m+3/2-2s}}{1-p^{3-4s}}\bigl(1-p^{(3-4s)l_{N}}-\chi_{N}^{*}(p)p^{1-2s}(1-p^{(3-4s)(l_{N}-1)})\bigr).

(5.2)

We denote the absolute value of the conductor of
\chi_{N}^{*}
by
D_{N}^{*}
. Then

D_{N}^{*}=\begin{cases}pD_{N}&\text{if $(p,D_{N})=1$,}\\
p^{-1}D_{N}&\text{if $p\mid D_{N}$.}\end{cases}

Since
\chi_{N}^{*}(-1)=-\chi_{p}(-1)
,we have
\delta_{\chi_{N}^{*}}=(1-\chi_{N}^{*}(-1))/2=1-\delta_{p}
.
The completed Dirichlet
L
function
\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)
is given by

\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)=\left(\frac{D_{N}^{*}}{\pi}\right)^{s-\delta_{p}/2}\Gamma\left(s-\dfrac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)L(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1).

Since we can show that the Gauss sum of
\chi_{N}^{*}
coincides with
(\sqrt{-1})^{1-\delta_{p}}\sqrt{D_{N}^{*}}
, the functional equation
\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},s)=\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},1-s)
holds.
For both cases
\delta_{p}=0
or
1
we have

\Gamma\left(s+\frac{\delta_{p}-1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(s-\frac{\delta_{p}}{2}\right)=\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{2-2s}\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(2s-1),

thus we can write

\displaystyle C(N,s)=\chi_{p}(\alpha)2^{k+3}\pi^{k-\delta_{p}}p^{s+(\delta_{p}-1)/2}(D_{N}^{*})^{-s+\delta_{p}/2}\det(2N)^{s-3/2+k/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)\omega_{2}\left(2\pi Y,N;s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right)F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})\prod_{q\neq p}F_{N}^{(q)}(\chi_{p}(q)q^{-2s}).

Let
d=\ord_{p}D_{N}\in\{0,1\}
. Then

\ord_{p}\det(2N)=2f_{p}+b
and
D_{N}^{*}=D_{N}^{\prime}p^{1-d}
with
(D_{N}^{\prime},p)=1
. Since
\det(2N)=p^{2f_{p}+d}D_{N}^{\prime}\prod_{q\neq p}q^{2f_{q}}
we can write by using a constant
A
that is independent of
s
,

\displaystyle C(N,s)

\displaystyle=A\,p^{2(f_{p}+d)s}\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)\omega_{2}\left(2\pi Y,N;s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})\prod_{q\neq p}q^{2sf_{q}}F_{N}^{(q)}(\chi_{p}(q)q^{-2s}).

By
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
and (
5.1
),

\xi(\chi_{N}^{*},2s-1)\omega_{2}\left(2\pi Y,N;s+\frac{k}{2},s-\frac{k}{2}\right)\prod_{q\neq p}q^{2sf_{q}}F_{N}^{(q)}(\chi_{p}(q)q^{-2s})

is invariant under
s\mapsto 3/2-s
.
Note that
2(f_{p}+d)=2m+2l_{N}
and one can check frome (
5.2
) that
p^{2(m+l_{N})s}F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})
is also invariant
s\mapsto 3/2-s
, i.e.

F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{2s-3})=p^{(4s-3)(m+l_{N})}F_{N}^{(p)}(p^{-2s})

holds. As a consequence we have
C(N,s)=C(N,3/2-s)
, that is the evidence of our functional equation.

We remark that the concrete form of the functional equation (Theorem
4.8
) is formulated so that the constant terms of the Fourier expansion coincide each other. However we have shown the equality of the Fourier coefficients at
N>0
.

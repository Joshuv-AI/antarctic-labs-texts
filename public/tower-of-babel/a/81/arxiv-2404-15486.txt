1.
Introduction

Let
a,b,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q,r>1
, in this paper we consider the following inequality:

(1)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x+\alpha\left|\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\text{d}x\right|^{\frac{p}{r-1}}\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(a,b)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{s.t.}\ u(a)=u(b)=0.\end{split}

The optimal constant in (
1
) corresponds to the value realizing the minimum in the following eigenvalue problem

(2)

\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u],\;u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(a,b),\,u\not\equiv 0\right\},

where

(3)

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]:=\dfrac{\dint_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}dx+\alpha\left|\dint_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\,dx\right|^{\frac{p}{r-1}}}{\left(\dint_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}.

This kind of problems lead in general to nonstandard associated Euler-Lagrange equations, known in literature as nonlocal, because they depend on the value that the unknown function assumes on the whole domain throughout the integral over
(a,b)
. Specifically,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}y^{\prime})^{\prime}+\alpha|\gamma|^{\frac{p}{r-1}-2}\gamma|y|^{r-2}=\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\,||y||_{q}^{p-q}|y|^{q-2}y\ \text{in}\ ]a,b[\\[8.5359pt]
y(a)=y(b)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma=\int_{a}^{b}|y|^{r-2}y\ dx
, except for some trivial cases detailed in
Section

3
.

This type of nonlocal problems date back at least to the 1837 papers by Duhamel
[
Du
]
and Liouville
[
Lio
]
on thermo-elasticity. Since then, such problems have been studied in various contexts, including reaction-diffusion equations modeling chemical processes (see
[
F2
,
S
]
) or Brownian motion with random jumps (see
[
Pin
]
). They have been the object of extensive research over the last thirty years
[
F1
,
F2
,
FV
]
, particularly through the study of variational problems involving a nonlocal one-parameter functional, both in the
n
-dimensional (
[
BFNT
]
) and the one-dimensional (
[
DPP1
,
DPP2
]
) setting.

In higher dimensions, problem (
2
) has been treated (
[
BFNT
]
) only in the case when
p=q=r=2
. In that setting, the authors identify a
saturation phenomenon
under a volume constraint; they show that, among all domains with fixed measure, the optimal shape is the ball (for values of the parameter
\alpha
below a critical one) and a union of two equal balls for supercritical values. A similar result holds when the Euclidean metric is replaced by a Finsler one
[
Pis
]
. A related but distinct line of research, not investigated in this paper, concerns the study of symmetry-breaking phenomena, where the symmetry of optimal domains is lost below or above certain threshold values; see for instance
[
BDNT
,
BCGM
,
N2
]
.

In one dimension, the analysis of the nonlocal problem (
2
) relies on the study of a generalized Wirtinger inequality, which was started by the pioneering work of Dacorogna, Gangbo, and Subia
[
DGS
]
in the case
q\leq 2p
and
r=2
. Subsequently, in
[
E
,
BKN
,
BK
,
N1
,
CD
,
GN
]
various parameter ranges have been analyzed. The question of symmetry/non symmetry of minimizers has been been completely settled in
[
GGR
]
. Specifically, minimizers are symmetric when
q\leq(2r-1)p
, while no odd function can be a minimizer when
q>(2r-1)p
.

Our aim is to investigate the symmetry properties of the minimizers of (
2
) and, as a consequence, to provide some informations on
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
. To explore the full range of the exponents
p,q,r>1
, we recall that, to the best of our knowledge, the nonlocal problem (
2
) has previously been studied only in the case
p=q=2
with
2\leq r\leq 3
in
[
DPP1
]
, and for
p=q\geq 2
with
\frac{p}{2}+1\leq r\leq\frac{p}{2}
in
[
DPP2
]
. In these regimes, the minimizers of (
2
) are symmetric (either even or odd), and a saturation phenomenon occurs. In particular, for subcritical values of the parameter
\alpha
, the minimizers are even functions with constant sign, while for supercritical values, the minimizers are odd sign-changing. It is therefore natural to ask in which regions of the parameter space symmetry breaking is expected to occur.
In the present paper, we extend the range of treatable exponents and establish a new saturation phenomenon.

Throughout this paper, for the sake of simplicity, we will study the problem in the interval
(-1,1)
instead of
(a,b)
. The general case can be easily recovered since the nonlocal eigenvalue admits the following rescaling:

\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r;(a,b))=\left[\left(\frac{2}{b-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}}\right]^{p}\ \lambda_{\tilde{\alpha}}\left(p,q,r;(-1,1)\right),

with
\tilde{\alpha}=\left(\frac{b-a}{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{r-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)p}\alpha
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p,q,r>1
be such that
\frac{2p}{p+2}\leq q\leq p
and
\frac{q}{2}+1\leq r\leq q+\frac{q}{p}
. Then there exists a positive number
\alpha_{C}=\alpha_{C}(p,q,r)
such that:

(i)

if
\alpha<\alpha_{C}
, then
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)<\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
,

(ii)

If
\alpha\geq\alpha_{C}
, then
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
,

where

\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(a,b)\\
\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\ \text{d}x=0\\
u\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}.

In addition, we prove the following symmetry results for the solutions of problem (
2
). We refer to
Section

3
for the definition of the generalized trigonometric function
\sin_{p,q}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p,q,r>1
be such that
\frac{2p}{p+2}\leq q\leq p
and
\frac{q}{2}+1\leq r\leq q+\frac{q}{p}
.

(i)

If
\alpha<\alpha_{C}
, then any minimizer

of
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
is an even function with constant sign in
(-1,1)
.

(ii)

If
\alpha>\alpha_{C}
, the function
y(x)=\sin_{p,q}(\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)x)
,

, is the unique minimizer, up to a multiplicative constant, of
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
. Hence it is an odd function,
\int_{-1}^{1}|y|^{r-2}y\,dx=0
, and
\overline{x}=0
is the only point in
(-1,1)
such that
y(\overline{x})=0
.

(iii)

If
\alpha=\alpha_{C}
, then
\lambda_{\alpha_{C}}(p,q,r)
admits both a positive minimizer and the minimizer

, up to a multiplicative constant. Moreover, if

any minimizer has constant sign or it is odd.

2.
A unified treatment of Poincaré, Wirtinger and Twisted inequalities

One of the main aims of this paper is to unify and extend the study of Poincaré, Wirtinger, and Twisted inequalities, through the introduction of a penalization term. In this Section, we describe each of these classical inequalities in detail, explaining their connections with the nonlocal inequality under investigation.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, inequality (
1
) is a nonlinear generalization of the celebrated one-dimensional inequality

(4)

\lambda\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{2}\text{d}x\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\text{d}x\quad\forall u\in C^{1}(a,b);

that is the Poincaré inequality when

(5)

u(a)=u(b)=0

and that is the Wirtinger inequality when

(6)

\int_{a}^{b}u\ \text{d}x=0.

The best constant
\lambda
in both Poincaré (
4
)-(
5
) and Wirtinger inequality (
4
)-(
6
) is obtained for

\lambda=\left(\frac{\pi}{b-a}\right)^{2}.

The generalized Poincaré inequality (see e. g.
[
GGR
]
and references therein) states that there exists a constant
\lambda_{P}(p,q)
such that

(7)

\lambda_{P}(p,q)\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(a,b)\ \text{s.t.}\ u(a)=u(b)=0.

It is easily seen that, when
\alpha=0
, the nonlocal inequality (
1
) is the Poincaré inequality (
7
).
When
p=q=2
, we come back to the classical Poincaré inequality (
4
)-(
5
). Moreover, the optimal constant in (
7
) is also the minimum for the variational problem

\lambda_{P}(p,q)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(a,b)\\
u\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

and the minimizing functions are even functions with constant sign. It is easily seen that
\lambda_{P}(p,q)
is a homogeneous
(p,q)-
Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue (see e. g.
[
LE
, Th. 3.3]
).

On the other hand, the generalized Wirtinger inequality states that there exists a constant
\lambda_{W}(p,q,r)
such that

(8)

\lambda_{W}(p,q,r)\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(a,b)\ \text{s.t.}\ \int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\ \text{d}x=0.

When
p=q=r=2
, we come back to the classical Wirtinger inequality (
4
)-(
6
).
Moreover, the optimal constant in (
8
) is also the minimum for the variational problem

\lambda_{W}(p,q,r)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}(a,b)\\
\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\ \text{d}x=0\\
u\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

and the minimizing functions are odd functions. For the exact value of
\lambda_{W}(p,q,r)
see
[
GN
]
. It is easily seen that
\lambda_{W}(p,q,r)
is a Neumann Laplacian eigenvalue (see e.g.
[
LE
, Th. 3.4]
).

When both Dirichlet (
5
) and Neumann (
6
) boundary conditions hold, we speak of twisted boundary conditions
[
BB
,
FH
]
. The best constant in the Twisted inequality (
4
)-(
5
)-(
6
) is equal to

\lambda_{T}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{b-a}\right)^{2}.

The generalized Twisted inequality states that there exists a constant
\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
such that

(9)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(a,b)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{s.t.}\ u(a)=u(b)=0\ \text{and}\ \int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\text{d}x=0.\end{split}

It is easily seen that, meanwhile when
\alpha\to+\infty
, inequality (
1
) tends to the Twisted inequality (
9
).
When
p=q=r=2
, we come back to the classical Twisted inequality (
4
)-(
5
)-(
6
).

Moreover, the optimal constant in (
9
) is also the minimum for the variational problem

(10)

\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(a,b)\\
\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{r-2}u\ \text{d}x=0\\
u\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\text{d}x}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u|^{q}\text{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}

and the minimizing functions are odd functions. See
[
CD
, Thm. 1.1]
for the exact value of
\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
, and note that there is no dependence on the parameter
r
.

3.
The eigenvalue problem

In this Section, we firstly recall some results on the generalized trigonometric functions and then some properties of the eigenvalue problem (
2
).

3.1.
The
(p,q)-
circular functions

We briefly summarize some properties of the
p
-trigonometric functions for any fixed
1<p<+\infty
(refer e. g.
[
LE
,
Lin
,
Pe
]
). These functions generalize the familiar trigonometric functions and coincide with them when
p=2
.

Let us consider the function
F_{p}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

F_{p}(x)=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle x$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{dt}{\left(1-{t^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Denote by
z(s)
the inverse function of
F
which is defined on the interval
\left[0,\frac{\pi_{p}}{2}\right]
, where

\pi_{p}=2\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{dt}{\left(1-{t^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Therefore, the
p
-sine function
\sin_{p}
is defined as the following periodic extension of
z(t)
:

\sin_{p}(t)=\left\{\begin{split}&z(t)&&\text{if}\ \ t\in\left[0,\frac{\pi_{p}}{2}\right],\\
&z(\pi_{p}-t)&&\text{if}\ \ t\in\left[\frac{\pi_{p}}{2},\pi_{p}\right],\\
&-\sin_{p}(-t)\ &&\text{if}\ \ t\in\left[-\pi_{p},0\right].\\
\end{split}\right.

It is extended periodically to all
\mathbb{R}
, with period
2\pi_{p}
. Furthermore, the
p
-cosine function is defined by

\cos_{p}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\sin_{p}\left(t\right)

and is a
2\pi_{p}
-periodic and odd function.

To further extend the definitions of trigonometric functions, let us consider
p,q>1
and set

\pi_{p,q}:=2\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{1}{(1-t^{q})^{\frac{1}{p}}}dt=\frac{2}{q}B\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},\frac{1}{q}\right)=\frac{2}{q}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}\right)},

where
B
and
\Gamma
are the beta and the gamma function, respectively.

This definition coincides with
\pi_{p}
when
p=q
. Therefore the function
\sin_{p,q}
is defined on the interval
[0,\frac{\pi_{p,q}}{2}]
as the inverse of
F_{p,q}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

F_{p,q}(x)=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle x$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{1}{(1-t^{q})^{\frac{1}{p}}}dx

and extended to the real line by the usual process involving the symmetry and the
2\pi_{p,q}
periodicity.

Finally, we recall from
[
LE
, Thm. 3.3]
, that any eigenvalue and eigenfunction of the
1
-dimensional Dirichlet
(p,q)-
Laplacian eigenvalue problem:

(11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}y^{\prime})^{\prime}=\lambda|y|^{q-2}y&\text{in}\ ]-1,1[\\[11.38092pt]
y(-1)=y(1)=0.\end{array}\right.

are of the form

\lambda_{n}=c_{1}\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{n\pi_{p,q}}{2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad y_{n}(x)=c_{2}\sin_{p,q}\left(\frac{n\pi_{p,q}}{2}(x+1)\right)\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

respectively, for
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly, when
p=q
, we fall in the case of the
p
-Laplacian problem.

3.2.
The eigenvalue problem

We firstly show some properties of the solution of the eigenvalue problem (
2
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q,r>1
be such that
r\leq p+1
. Then, problem (
2
) admits a solution in
W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
and any minimizer
y
of (
2
) is a solution of the following Dirichlet homogeneous problem

(12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}y^{\prime})^{\prime}+\alpha|\gamma|^{\frac{p}{r-1}-2}\gamma|y|^{r-2}=\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\,||y||_{q}^{p-q}|y|^{q-2}y&\text{in}\ ]-1,1[\\[11.38092pt]
y(-1)=y(1)=0,\end{array}\right.

where

\gamma=\begin{cases}0\text{ if both }r=p+1\text{ and }\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|y|^{r-2}y\ dx=0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|y|^{r-2}y\ dx\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
y,y^{\prime}|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}\in C^{1}[-1,1]
.

Proof.

Standard methods of Calculus of Variations prove the existence of a minimizer.
If
p>r-1
, the functional
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[\cdot]
in (
3
) is differentiable and hence the associated Euler-Lagrange equation leads to (
12
); meanwhile, when
p=r-1
, the problem (
2
) coincides with problem (
10
); hence
\gamma=0
and we get the conclusion.

Finally, the fact that
y,y^{\prime}|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}\in C^{1}[-1,1]
is easily seen from (
12
).
∎

At this stage, we analyze the monotonicity and asymptotic properties of the eigenvalue (
2
) with respect to the parameter
\alpha
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any fixed
p,q,r>1
such that
r\leq p+1
, the function
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
is Lipschitz continuous, nondecreasing with respect to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and

\lim_{\alpha\to-\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{T}(p,q,r).

Proof.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
, then by using the Hölder inequality with coniugate exponents
\frac{q}{r-1}
and
\frac{q}{q-r+1}
, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha+\varepsilon}[u]\leq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]+\varepsilon\frac{\left(\dint_{-1}^{1}|u|^{r-1}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{{r-1}}}}{\left(\dint_{-1}^{1}|u|^{q}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]+2^{\frac{p(q-r+1)}{q(r-1)}}\varepsilon.

Therefore, we gain the following chain of inequalities

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]\leq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha+\varepsilon}[u]\leq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]+2^{\frac{p(q-r+1)}{q(r-1)}}\varepsilon\quad\forall\ \varepsilon>0.

By taking the minimum for any
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
, we have

\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{\alpha+\varepsilon}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)+2^{\frac{p(q-r+1)}{q(r-1)}}\varepsilon\quad\forall\varepsilon>0,

that implies the desired Lipschitz continuity and monotonicity.

Now, let us fix a positive admissible function
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(-1,1)
. Then, we have that
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[\varphi]\to-\infty\quad\text{as }\alpha\to-\infty
and, since
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\leq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[\varphi]
, we have that

\lim_{\alpha\to-\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=-\infty.

Finally, let us consider a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to+\infty
.
Since
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
is nondecreasing with respect to
\alpha
, we have that
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us denote
u_{n}=u_{\alpha_{n}}
the normalized (
||u_{n}||_{L^{q}}=1
) minimizer in
W_{0}^{1,p}
of (
2
) when the value of the parameter is
\alpha_{n}
; we have that

\lambda_{\alpha_{n}}(p,q,r)=\int_{-1}^{1}|u^{\prime}_{n}|^{p}\ dx+\alpha_{n}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|u_{n}|^{r-2}u_{n}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}\leq\lambda_{T}(p,q,r).

This implies that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}
strongly converges in
L^{p}(-1,1)
and weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
to a function
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
such that
\|u\|_{L^{q}}=1
. Furthermore, we have that

\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|u_{n}|^{r-2}u_{n}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ n\rightarrow+\infty

which means that
\int_{-1}^{1}|u|^{r-2}u\ dx=0
. On the other hand, since
u
is an admissible function for (
10
), by using the lower semicontinuity of the integral, we have that

\begin{split}\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)\leq\int_{-1}^{1}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\ dx&\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left[\int_{-1}^{1}|u^{\prime}_{n}|^{p}\ dx+\alpha_{n}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|u_{n}|^{r-2}u_{n}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}\right]\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{\alpha_{n}}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)\end{split}

and hence the conclusion follows.∎

4.
The auxiliary function
H

In this Section, we study the behavior of an auxiliary function on which is based the proof of the main results (
Theorem

1.1

H(m,p,q,r):(m,p,q,r)\in[0,1]\times]1,+\infty[\times]1,+\infty[\times]1,+\infty[\mapsto\mathbb{R},

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

such that
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q}{2}+\frac{q}{2p}\leq r\leq q+\frac{q}{p}
. For any fixed

and

•

for any fixed

•

for

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps: in the first step we compute the expression of the derivative of

with respect to

and

; in the second step we study the sign of the aforementioned derivative; in the third step we analyze the cases excluded by the previous steps. From now on, for the sake of simplicity, we will set

Step 1 (The derivative of

).
Let us start by considering the case when

and

.
Differentiating

with respect to

, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{r}h(m,p,q,r,y)=&-\frac{1}{p}\frac{(1-y^{r-1})\partial_{r}R+R\,y^{r-1}\log y}{\left[1-R(1-y^{r-1})-y^{q}\right]^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}+\\[5.69046pt]
&-\frac{m}{p}\frac{[-(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})\partial_{r}R-R\,m^{r-1}y^{r-1}(\log m+\log y)]}{\left[1-R(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})-m^{q}y^{q}\right]^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}.\end{split}

Therefore, in order to compute the derivative of

with respect to

\partial_{r}R=-\frac{1-m^{q}}{(1+m^{r-1})^{2}}m^{r-1}\log m

&+m^{r}\frac{(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})\log m-(1+m^{r-1})y^{r-1}(\log m+\log y)}{\left[1-R(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})-m^{q}y^{q}\right]^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Step 2 (The monotonicity of

).

It is easily seen that the numerator of the first ratio, in the curly brackets of (

+m^{r}\frac{(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})\log m-(1+m^{r-1})y^{r-1}(\log m+\log y)}{\left[1-R(1+m^{r-1}y^{r-1})-m^{q}y^{q}\right]^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}\bigg\}.

Hence, by setting

+\bigg[(y^{r-1}-1)\log m+(1+m^{r-1})y^{r-1}\log y\bigg]m^{r-\frac{q(p+1)}{p}},

To prove the positivity of

, we will show that

\begin{split}\partial_{y}g(m,p,q,r,y)&=\bigg[(r-1)y^{r-2}m^{r-1}\log m-(r-1)y^{r-2}(1+m^{r-1})\log y-y^{r-2}(1+m^{r})\bigg]\\
&\ \quad+\bigg[(r-1)y^{r-2}\log m+(1+m^{r-1})((r-1)y^{r-2}\log y+y^{r-2})\bigg]m^{r-\frac{q(p+1)}{p}}\\

\begin{split}f(m,p,q,r)&=\left(m^{r-1}+m^{r-\frac{q(p+1)}{p}}\right)\log\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{r-1}\left(1+m^{r-1}-m^{r-\frac{q(p+1)}{p}}-m^{2r-1-\frac{q(p+1)}{p}}\right)\\

because

Hence, the positivity of

Step 3 (The trivial cases).

Lemma 4.2

between the unique maximum and the unique minimum point of

\begin{split}\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)&=\left[\left(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{2}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}q\right]^{p}\pi_{p,q}^{p}=\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\pi_{p,q}^{p}\\

At this stage, we are in a position to prove the main Theorems of the paper.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

When
\alpha\leq 0
, the minimizers of (
2
) have constant sign; indeed

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[u]\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}[|u|],

with equality if and only if
u\geq 0
or
u\leq 0
.

In order to prove the main result, we will show that there exists
\alpha>0
for which problem (
2
) admits a minimizer
y
that changes sign. Suppose, by contradiction, that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a divergent sequence
\alpha_{k}
, with a corresponding sequence of nonnegative eigenfunctions
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
associated to
\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}(p,q,r)
, such that
\|y_{k}\|_{q}=1
.

By
Proposition

3.2
, we have that
\lambda_{\alpha_{k}}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
and hence, it holds that

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|y_{k}^{\prime}|^{p}\ dx+\alpha_{k}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}y_{k}^{r-1}\ dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}\leq\lambda_{T}(p,q,r).

Therefore,
y_{k}
converges (up to a subsequence) to a function
y\in W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
, strongly in
L^{p}(-1,1)
and weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(-1,1)
. Moreover
\|y\|_{p}=1
and
y
is not identically zero. As a consequence,
\|y\|_{r-1}>0
and, letting
\alpha_{k}\rightarrow+\infty
in (
37
), we have a contradiction. We have thus proved that there exists a positive value of
\alpha
such that the minimization problem (
2
)
admits an eigenfunction
y
satisfying
\int_{-1}^{1}|y|^{r-2}y\ dx=0
. In this case,
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
and, up to a multiplicative constant,
y=\sin_{p,q}(\pi_{p,q}x)
.

Since, by
Proposition

3.1
,
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
is nondecreasing and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\alpha
, we can define

\alpha_{C}=\min\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\colon\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)\}=\sup\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\colon\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)<\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)\}.

It is easily seen that this critical value of the parameter is strictly positive.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

If
\alpha<\alpha_{C}
, then all minimizers corresponding to
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)
have constant sign; otherwise, when
\alpha<\alpha_{C}
, we have
\lambda_{\alpha}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)
.

Let us now consider the case
\alpha>\alpha_{C}
, and suppose by contradiction that there exists
\bar{\alpha}>\alpha_{C}
and a minimizer
\bar{y}
such that

\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{y}|^{r-2}\bar{y}\,dx>0,\quad\|y\|_{p}=1,\mathcal{Q}_{\bar{\alpha}}[\bar{y}]=\lambda_{\bar{\alpha}}(p,q,r).

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\bar{\alpha}-\varepsilon}[\bar{y}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{\bar{\alpha}}[\bar{y}]-\varepsilon\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{y}|^{r-2}\bar{y}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\bar{\alpha}}(p,q,r)-\varepsilon\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|\bar{y}|^{r-2}\bar{y}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{r-1}}<\lambda_{\bar{\alpha}}(p,q,r).

This implies that

\lambda_{T}(p,q,r)=\lambda_{\alpha_{C}}(p,q,r)\leq\lambda_{\bar{\alpha}-\varepsilon}(p,q,r)<\lambda_{\bar{\alpha}}(p,q,r),

which contradicts the definition of
\alpha_{C}
. Hence, for all
\alpha>\alpha_{C}
, any minimizer
y
satisfy

\int_{-1}^{1}|y|^{r-2}y\,dx=0.

Hence, by
Proposition

5.2
, the claims
(i)
and
(ii)
follows. Regarding
(iii)
, it is not difficult to show, using suitable approximating sequences, that
\lambda_{\alpha_{C}}(p,q,r)
admits both a nonnegative minimizer and a minimizer with vanishing
r
-average.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

To analyze the behavior of the solutions when
r=p+1
, we consider the case
\alpha=\alpha_{C}(p,q,p+1)
. In this setting, the corresponding positive minimizer

is a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(|y^{\prime}|^{p-2}y^{\prime})^{\prime}+\lambda_{T}(p,q,p+1)||y||_{q}^{p-q}y^{q-1}=\alpha_{C}(p,q,p+1)y^{q-1}&\text{in }]-1,1[\\

The positivity of the eigenfunction ensures (see also (

\lambda_{T}(p,q,p+1)||y||_{q}^{p-q}-\alpha_{C}(p,q,p+1)=\lambda_{0}(p,q,p+1)||y||_{q}^{p-q}=\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\left(\frac{\pi_{p,q}}{2}\right)^{p}.

As a consequence, we deduce the explicit expression

\alpha_{C}(p,q,p+1)=\frac{2^{p}-1}{2^{p}}\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\pi_{p,q}^{p}.

Neutron skin in 48 Ca determined from p+ 48 Ca and 48 Ca+ 12 C scattering
Shingo Tagami, Tomotsugu Wakasa, Maya Takechi, Jun Matsui, Masanobu Yahiro

Abstract
Background In our previous paper, we determined r skin 208 ​ ( exp ) = 0.278 ± 0.035 r_{\rm skin}^{208}({\rm exp})=0.278\pm 0.035 fm from measured reaction cross sections σ R \sigma_{\rm R} for p+ 208 Pb scattering, using the Kyushu (chiral) g g -matrix folding model with the densities calculated with the Gogny-D1S Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov (D1S-GHFB) with the angular momentum projection (AMP). The value agrees with that of PREX2. Reaction cross sections σ R \sigma_{\rm R} are available for p+ 48 Ca scattering, whereas interaction cross sections σ I ( ≈ σ R ) \sigma_{\rm I}(\approx\sigma_{\rm R}) are available for 48 Ca + 12 C scattering. As for 48 Ca, the high-resolution E ​ 1 E1 polarizability experiment ( E ​ 1 E1 pE) yields r skin 48 ​ ( E ​ 1 ​ pE ) = 0.14 ∼ 0.20 ​ fm r_{\rm skin}^{48}(E1{\rm pE})=0.14\sim 0.20~{\rm fm} . Purpose We determine r skin 48 ​ ( exp ) r_{\rm skin}^{48}({\rm exp}) from the data on σ R \sigma_{\rm R} for p+ 48 Ca scattering and from the data on σ I \sigma_{\rm I} for 48 Ca+ 12 C scattering. Methods We use the Kyushu g g -matrix folding model with the densities calculated with the D1M-GHFB+AMP densities. The D1M-GHFB+AMP proton and neutron densities are scaled so as to reproduce the data under the condition that the radius r p r_{\rm p} of the scaled proton density equals the data r p ​ ( exp ) r_{\rm p}({\rm exp}) determined from the electron scattering. We deduce skin values r skin = r n ​ ( exp ) − r p ​ ( exp ) r_{\rm skin}=r_{\rm n}({\rm exp})-r_{\rm p}({\rm exp}) from the resulting r n ​ ( exp ) r_{\rm n}({\rm exp}) and the r p ​ ( exp ) r_{\rm p}({\rm exp}) determined from electron scattering. The same procedure is taken for D1S-GHFB+AMP. Results We regard r skin 48 ​ ( E ​ 1 ​ pE ) r_{\rm skin}^{48}(E1{\rm pE}) as a reference skin value. Using the reference skin value and taking D1M-GHFB+AMP, we determine r skin 48 ​ ( exp ) = 0.158 ± 0.025 r_{\rm skin}^{48}({\rm exp})=0.158\pm 0.025 fm for p+ 48 Ca scattering and 0.160 ± 0.058 0.160\pm 0.058 fm for 48 Ca + 12 C scattering. Conclusion We take the weighted mean and its error for the two skin values. The result is r skin 48 ​ ( exp ) = 0.158 ± ( 0.023 ) exp ± ( 0.012 ) th ​ fm r_{\rm skin}^{48}({\rm exp})=0.158\pm(0.023)_{\rm exp}\pm(0.012)_{\rm th}~{\rm fm} .

II.2
Scaling of proton and neutron densities

We consider proton and neutron densities calculated with of D1S-GHFB+AMP and D1M-GHFB+AMP as the original density
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
.
The scaled density
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is determined
from the original density
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
as

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\equiv\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha),~~{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\rm scaling}\equiv{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha

(21)

with a scaling factor

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle_{\rm scaling}}{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle}}.

(22)

In Eq. (
21
), we have replaced
r
by
{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}/\alpha
in the original density.
Eventually,
r
dependence
of
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is different from that of
\rho({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
.
We have multiplied the original density by
\alpha^{-3}

in order to normalize the scaled density.
The symbol means
\sqrt{\langle{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}^{2}\rangle_{\rm scaling}}
is the root-mean-square
radius of
\rho_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
.

For later convenience, we refer to the proton (neutron) radius of the scaled proton (neutron) density
\rho^{\rm p}_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})

(
\rho^{\rm n}_{\rm scaling}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
) as
r_{\rm p}({\rm scaling})
(
r_{\rm n}({\rm scaling})
).

When we scale the original D1S proton and neutron densities to the scaled densities base on

r_{\rm skin}^{48}(E1{\rm pE})=0.14\sim 0.20~{\rm fm}
,
the value of
\alpha
is 0.9992 for neutron and 0.9899 for proton.
These values are quite close to 1.

II.3.1
GCM and PNVAP

In PNVAP, the total energy is defined by

\displaystyle E_{NZ}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\langle\Phi|\hat{H}\hat{P}_{Z}\hat{P}_{N}|\Phi\rangle}{\langle\Phi|\hat{P}_{N}\hat{P}_{Z}|\Phi\rangle}

(23)

with

\displaystyle\delta E_{NZ}

\displaystyle=0,

(24)

where
P_{N}
(
P_{Z}
) is the number projector for neutron (proton) and the intrinsic ground-state

|\Phi\rangle

satisfies
{\hat{a}_{k}}|\Phi\rangle=0
for the annihilation operator
{\hat{a}_{k}}
of quasi-particle for any
k
.

In GCM, we consider the following Hamiltonian and norm matrices as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}{\cal H}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\cr{\cal N}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\end{matrix}\right\}

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\Phi_{n}|\left\{\begin{matrix}\hat{H}\cr 1\end{matrix}\right\}\hat{P}^{I}_{KK^{\prime}}|\Phi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm with}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle|\Phi_{n}\rangle=|\Phi(q_{20,n})\rangle

\displaystyle,\langle\Phi(q_{20,n})|\hat{Q}_{20}|\Phi(q_{20,n})\rangle=q_{20,n};

(26)

in actual calculations, the
\{q_{20,n}\}
are taken from -60 fm
2
to 140 fm
2
at an interval of 20 fm
2
.
The total energy with the total spin
I
is

\displaystyle E_{I}=\frac{\sum_{KnK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}g^{*}_{IKn}{\cal H}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}g_{IK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}}{\sum_{KnK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}g^{*}_{IKn}{\cal N}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}g_{IK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}}

(27)

\displaystyle{\rm under~the~conditions}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\frac{\delta E_{I}}{\delta g^{*}_{IKn}}

\displaystyle=0,

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\left[{\cal H}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}g_{IK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}-E_{I}{\cal N}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\right]g_{IK^{\prime}n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=0.

(29)

II.3.2
GFHB+AMP

In GHFB+AMP, the total wave function
|\Psi^{I}_{M}\rangle
with the AMP is defined by

|\Psi^{I}_{M}\rangle=\sum_{K,n=0}^{N}g^{I}_{Kn}\hat{P}^{I}_{MK}|\Phi_{n}\rangle,

(30)

where
\hat{P}^{I}_{MK}
is the angular-momentum-projector and the

|\Phi_{n}\rangle
for
n=0,1,\cdots,N
are mean-field (GHFB) states,
where
N+1
is the number of the states.
The coefficients
g^{I}_{Kn}
are obtained by solving the Hill-Wheeler equation

\sum_{K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}{\cal H}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\ g^{I}_{K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}=E_{I}\,\sum_{K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}{\cal N}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\ g^{I}_{K^{\prime}n^{\prime}},

(31)

with the Hamiltonian and norm kernels defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}{\cal H}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\\
{\cal N}^{I}_{Kn,K^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\end{array}\right\}=\langle\Phi_{n}|\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{H}\\
1\end{array}\right\}\hat{P}_{KK^{\prime}}^{I}|\Phi_{n^{\prime}}\rangle.

(32)

For even nuclei, there is no blocking state, i.e.,
N=0
in the Hill-Wheeler equation.
We can thus perform GHFB+AMP.
However, we have to find the value of
\beta
at
which the ground-state energy becomes minimum.
In this step, the AMP has to be performed for any
\beta
, so that the Hill-Wheeler calculation is heavy.
In fact, the AMP is not taken for mean field calculations in many works.
The reason why we do not take into account
\gamma
deformation is
that the deformation does not affect
\sigma_{\rm R}

Sumi et al. 2012b
.

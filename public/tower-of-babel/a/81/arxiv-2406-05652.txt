Abstract
Millimeter wave (mmWave) cell-free massive MIMO (CF mMIMO) is a promising solution for future wireless communications. However, its optimization is non-trivial due to the challenging channel characteristics. We show that mmWave CF mMIMO optimization is largely an assignment problem between access points (APs) and users due to the high path loss of mmWave channels, the limited output power of the amplifier, and the almost orthogonal channels between users given a large number of AP antennas. The combinatorial nature of the assignment problem, the requirement for scalability, and the distributed implementation of CF mMIMO make this problem difficult. In this work, we propose an unsupervised machine learning (ML) enabled solution. In particular, a graph neural network (GNN) customized for scalability and distributed implementation is introduced. Moreover, the customized GNN architecture is hierarchically permutation-equivariant (HPE) , i.e., if the APs or users of an AP are permuted, the output assignment is automatically permuted in the same way. To address the combinatorial problem, we relax it to a continuous problem, and introduce an information entropy-inspired penalty term. The training objective is then formulated using the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) . The test results show that the realized sum-rate outperforms that of the generalized serial dictatorship (GSD) algorithm and is very close to the upper bound in a small network scenario, while the upper bound is impossible to obtain in a large network scenario.

Introduction

Cell-free massive MIMO (CF mMIMO)
is a promising solution for next-generation wireless communications.
By deploying
access points (APs)
in a high density and
enabling cooperation between
APs
to serve the same user,

CF mMIMO
can realize high
received signal strength (RSS)
,
low interference thanks to the cooperation between

,
and high reliability due to the resulting macro diversity
[
1
]
.
These properties make

suitable for
millimeter wave (mmWave)

which enjoys a large bandwidth
but suffers from low

due to high propagation loss and low amplifier output power.
In addition, weak diffraction at high frequencies
[
2
]
makes blockage a serious problem.
By using the
mmWave
spectrum and cooperation of multiple
APs
in
CF mMIMO
,
the
RSS
is enhanced and the blockage problem is mitigated through macro-diversity.

Given the hardware and channel properties, optimizing
mmWave

CF mMIMO
is primarily an assignment problem between
APs
and users.
Sophisticated precoding techniques,
such as
zero-forcing (ZF)
and
minimum mean square error (MMSE)
,
are neither feasible (due to the immature hardware)
nor indispensable (the spectral efficiency is not crucial given a large bandwidth).
Instead, conjugate precoding
is more feasible with simple hardware,
realizes high antenna gain given many antennas,
and reduces interference significantly because multi-user channels become asymptotically orthogonal as the number of antennas increases
[
3
]
.
Moreover,
transmit power control is not necessary because
the optimal transmit power is always the maximum available power
given the high path loss
for typical objectives.

In the literature,
a mixed-integer programming problem for
CF mMIMO
fronthaul resource allocation is considered in
[
4
]
.

APs
and pilots are assigned in a rule-based, distributed way in
[
5
]
.
An alternative selection method is proposed for
AP
-user assignment in
[
6
]
.
The block-sparsity norm
l_{2,1}
is applied to optimize
AP
-user assignment and power control simultaneously in
[
7
]
.
The pilot assignment problem is considered based on serving set in
[
8
]

and graph coloring in
[
9
]
.

It can be concluded from the above that the assignment problem in
CF mMIMO
optimization
is difficult to solve due to its non-differentiability.
The existing algorithms are either heuristic or do not scale well.
In recent years,

machine learning (ML)
has been proposed to solve combinatorial problems in
[
10
]
.
Such methods train a
neural network (NN)
on massive amounts of data to find an optimized mapping from problem representation to solution.
In the literature,
an encoder-decoder like

is applied to optimize

[
11
]
.
In
[
12
]
, a

?
meta-algorithm
?
is proposed to generate a greedy policy for combinatorial problems by joining

and graph embedding.

is applied in
[

to optimize pilot assignment in a decentralized way.
However, if we do not have

correct
?

since it has a poor scalability due to the

On the other hand, if the objective is known, model-based optimization is a better choice because it does not rely on sampling solutions through trial-and-error.
Therefore, the dimensionality has only a moderate impact.

Among different contributions to

Despite its significant potential,

We propose a model-based

using a customized

Compared to existing works,

Our main contributions are summarized below.

III
General Approach of the Unsupervised Machine Learning for Optimization

We present the general approach of unsupervised
ML
for optimization as follows.
Given effective channel gains
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times N}
,
with element
g_{kn}
in row
k
and column
n
as defined in (
1
),
we look for a solution
\mathbf{S}
that maximizes objective
f
defined in (
2a
),
which is fully determined by
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{S}
, and can be written as

f(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{S})
.
We define an

\theta

i.e.,
\theta
contains all trainable weights and biases of
N_{\theta}
.

N_{\theta}

maps from
\mathbf{G}

to
\mathbf{S}
,
i.e.,
\mathbf{S}=N_{\theta}(\mathbf{G})
.
The objective can then be written as
f(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{S})=f(\mathbf{G},N_{\theta}(\mathbf{G});\theta)
,
where it is emphasized that

depends on
\theta
.
We collect massive amounts of realizations of

in a training set
\mathcal{D}

and formulate the problem as

\max_{\theta}J=\sum_{\mathbf{G}\in\mathcal{D}}f(\mathbf{G},N_{\theta}(\mathbf{G});\theta).

(3)

In this way,

is optimized for the ensemble of
\mathbf{G}\in\mathcal{D}
(
training
) using gradient ascent:

\theta\leftarrow\theta+\eta\nabla_{\theta}J,

(4)

where
\eta
is learning rate.
If
N_{\theta}
is well trained,

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}=N_{\theta}(\mathbf{G^{\prime}})
is also a good solution for
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{D}
(
testing
),
like a human uses experience to solve new problems of the same type
2
2

2

A complete retraining is only required when the input is fundamentally changed, e.g., when there is a change of deployment environment.

[
20
]
.

Compared to analytical methods,
which optimize
\mathbf{S}
itself given
\mathbf{G}
,

ML
optimizes the mapping from
\mathbf{G}
to
\mathbf{S}
.
This has the following two advantages:

1.

Low complexity in operation:

If analytical optimization is used, it is performed after receiving
\mathbf{G}
.
If its complexity is high, a real-time application may be difficult.
In contrast, with
ML
-based optimization,
N_{\theta}
is trained prior to operation.
During operation, using the trained

to make inferences on a new

is much less complex than training or analytical optimization.

2.

Scalability:

As will be described in
section IV
,
the same mapping is applied to all
APs
and users.
This is a significant advantage in large network scenarios with many
APs
and users.
By optimizing the mapping

instead of

,
the complexity becomes independent of the numbers of
APs
and users.
This achieves a high scalability,
which is a major challenge in
CF mMIMO
.

Formulation (
3
) presents a general approach.
In the following sections,
we propose specialized
GNN
architecture and
objective function according to the problem properties described in
subsection II-B
.

V
Training with Augmented Lagrangian Method

In this section,
we propose a training process for Problem (
2
) using
ALM
.
Unlike conventional applications of
ALM
,
which tune optimization variables directly,
we use
ALM
to formulate the objective,
then use the Adam optimizer to optimize the

parameter
\theta

During training,

s_{kn}
is relaxed to a continuous variable between 0 and 1.
Constraint (
2c
) is guaranteed by the recurrent process described in
section IV
.
Constraints (
2b
) and (
2d
)
appear in the objective as penalty terms.
Using

the objective function is defined as

\begin{split}g&=f-\lambda_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{ReLU}\left(L-\sum_{n=1}^{N}s_{kn}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}\\
&\qquad-\nu_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{ReLU}\left(L-\sum_{n=1}^{N}s_{kn}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\nu_{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}^{2},\end{split}

(8)

where

\lambda_{j}
and
\nu_{j}
are the factors of the linear and

respectively (
j=1,2
),

is the penalty term of

n
for (

) inspired by the information entropy:

p_{n}=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_{kn}\log(s_{kn}).

(9)

Note that

is equal to 0 if

or
1

Otherwise

,

VII
Conclusion

Due to challenging
mmWave
channel and hardware characteristics,
the
mmWave

CF mMIMO
optimization problem is primarily an assignment problem between
APs
and users.
In this work,
we have proposed an unsupervised
ML
approach.
In particular, we have utilized a customized
GNN
to achieve scalability, distributed inference, hierarchical permutation-equivariance,
and reduced data traffic in fronthaul.
To solve the combinatorial assignment problem,
we first relaxed it to a continuous problem.
Next, we introduced a penalty inspired by information entropy and use
ALM
to enforce discreteness.
Testing results indicate that the proposed method
outperforms both random assignment and
GSD
algorithm.
In a small network scenario, its performance is very close to the upper bound found via exhaustive search.
In a large network scenario, where exhaustive search is not feasible due to memory and time limitations, the proposed method still achieves good performance.
The proposed method could be generalized to other large-scale combinatorial problems in the future.
The combination of
ML
and analytical
ALM
can also be applied to other constrained optimization problems.

Code and data of this work will be made public on Github if the paper is accepted.

Ergodicity of explicit logarithmic cocycles over IETs
P. Berk, F. Trujillo, C. Ulcigrai

Abstract
We prove ergodicity in a class of skew-product extensions of interval exchange transformations given by cocycles with logarithmic singularities. This, in particular, provides explicit examples of ergodic ℝ \mathbb{R} -extensions of minimal locally Hamiltonian flows with non-degenerate saddles in genus two. More generally, given any symmetric irreducible permutation, we show that for almost every choice of lengths, the skew-product built over the associated IET using a cocycle with symmetric logarithmic singularities that is odd when restricted to each of the exchanged intervals is ergodic.



1
Introduction

This paper gives a contribution to the study of
skew-products
over interval exchange transformations, an infinite measure preserving setting in which fundamental questions such as ergodicity are still wide open.

Let
I:=[0,1]
denote the unit interval and
T:I\to I
be an
interval exchange transformation
(or IET for short), i.e., a piecewise isometry of the interval with finitely many discontinuities (see §
2.1.1
for the formal definition).

Given a real-valued measurable function
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
, the
\mathbb{R}
-extension of
T
given by
\varphi
is the skew-product automorphism
T_{\varphi}
of the strip
I\times\mathbb{R}
given by

T_{\varphi}(x,y)=(T(x),y+\varphi(x)),\qquad x\in I,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

One can then see that
T_{\varphi}
preserves a natural infinite invariant measure, namely, the restriction
\lambda
to the strip
I\times\mathbb{R}
of the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Iterates of
T_{\varphi}
have the form

T^{n}_{\varphi}(x,y)=(T^{n}(x),y+S_{n}\varphi(x)),

where
S_{n}\varphi
denotes the
Birkhoff sums
of
\varphi
over
T
, which are given by

S_{n}\varphi:=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varphi\circ T^{j},&\text{if}\ n>0,\\
0,&\text{if}\ n=0,\\
\sum_{j=n}^{-1}\varphi\circ T^{-j},&\text{if}\ n<0.\\
\end{cases}

(2)

Since Birkhoff sums define a
\mathbb{R}
-
cocycle
over
T
(i.e.,
S_{n+m}\varphi=S_{n}\varphi+S_{m}\varphi\circ T^{n}
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
)
the function
\varphi
is also called a
cocycle
. It is well known that
T_{\varphi}
is recurrent if and only if
\int_{I}\varphi(x)dx=0
(see
[
5
]
). Recall that
T_{\varphi}
is called
recurrent
if for every
A\subseteq I
of positive Lebesgue measure and any
\epsilon>0
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
T_{\varphi}^{n}(A\times\{0\})\cap A\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\neq\emptyset
.

Skew-products of the form (
1
) where
T:I\to I
is a
rotation
(i.e., the map
T(x)=x+\alpha\mod 1
) are one of the most studied examples in infinite ergodic theory (see for example the monographs
[
1
,
33
]
and the references therein). In particular, when the cocycle
\varphi
is of the form
\varphi=\chi_{[0,\beta]}-\beta
(where
\chi_{[a,b]}
denotes the characteristic function of the interval
[a,b]
and
0<\beta<1
) there is a long history of results on it, starting from ergodicity (see for example
[
34
,
11
,
3
,
27
,
6
,
2
]
). Interval exchange transformations are a natural generalization of rotations. However, the problem of ergodicity of skew-product extensions over IETs is still widely open despite being actively researched; see, for example,
[
20
,
10
,
16
,
13
,
19
,
30
,
31
,
9
]
for some results in particular settings. In particular, some specific results for skew-products over IETs with
d>2
, when the cocycle
\varphi
is piecewise constant or piecewise absolutely continuous, were proved, for example, in
[
10
,
16
,
25
]
.

1.1
Ergodicity of cocycles with logarithmic singularities

In this paper, we consider the special case when the cocycle
\varphi:I\to

\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\}
has
logarithmic singularities
, namely, if
\{u^{t}_{i},1\leq i\leq d-1\}
denote the discontinuities
T
, it blows up at a (nonempty subset) of
\{u^{t}_{i},1\leq i\leq d-1\}\cup\{u^{t}_{0}:=0,u^{t}_{d}:=1\}
as
C_{i}^{\pm}|\log(x-u^{t}_{i})|
when
x\to(u^{t}_{i})^{\pm}
, for some constants
C_{i}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
(not all equal to 0) (see Definition
2.1
).
One of the reasons for the interest in this type of cocycle is that skew-products over IETs under such cocycles arise naturally when taking Poincaré sections of extensions of locally Hamiltonian flows on surfaces of genus
g\geq 1
(see §
2.2
and also the introduction of
[
17
]
and the references therein), a class of flows whose study goes back to the work of Poincaré
[
28
]
(see also the work of Krygin
[
23
]
).

When
d=2
, i.e.,
T=R_{\alpha}
is a rotation, ergodicity of skew-products as in (
1
) for cocycles with logarithmic singularities was previously studied for cocycles with one
symmetric
logarithmic singularity (see Definition
2.3
), such as

\varphi(x)=C\log(x)+C\log(1-x)+2C,

by Frączek and Lemańczyk in
[
14
]
, and for cocycles with one
asymmetric
logarithmic singularity (see Definition
2.3
), such as

{\varphi(x)=C\log(1-x)+C,}

(3)

by Fayad and Lemańczyk in
[
12
]
(notice that the constants
2C
and
C
are added so that the cocycles have zero mean). In both cases, it was proved that
T_{\varphi}
is ergodic for almost every rotation number.

Cocycles with logarithmic singularities over IETs were recently investigated in
[
15
,
17
]
. This paper considers a special class of cocycles with logarithmic singularities over IETs in which one can exhibit explicit examples of ergodic extensions. We comment below on the relation between this result and the existing ones in the literature. More precisely, we consider cocycles with non-trivial logarithmic singularities (see Definition
2.1
), which are
odd
when restricted to each of the intervals
I_{i}:=(a_{i},b_{i})
exchanged by the IET, i.e., for
i=1,\dots,d
, the cocycle
\varphi
satisfies

\varphi(a_{i}+b_{i}-x)=-{\varphi(x)},\qquad\text{for\, all}\ x\in(a_{i},b_{i}).

See Definition
2.4
and §
2.1.2
for more details. Furthermore, we consider IETs whose permutations reverse the order of the intervals, so-called
symmetric
permutations (see §
2.1.1
).

Theorem 1.1

(Ergodicity of odd cocycles with logarithmic singularities)
.

For any
d\geq 3
, for almost every IET
T:I\to I
with a symmetric permutation on
d
symbols, and for any cocycle
\varphi
of class
C^{2}
which:

1.

has non-trivial symmetric logarithmic singularities (see §
2.1.2
) at the discontinuities of
T
(in the sense of Definition
2.1
);

2.

is
odd
on each continuity interval of
T
(in the sense of Definition
2.4
),

the associated skew-product
T_{\varphi}
is ergodic with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
I\times\mathbb{R}
.

We remark that the assumption
d\geq 3
is necessary, since for
d=2
, for any rotation
R_{\alpha}
by
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
,
a cocycle
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the assumptions of the theorem is necessarily of the form

{\varphi(x)=C\big(\log x+\log(1-x)-\log(x+\alpha\mod 1)-\log(1-\alpha-x\mod 1)\big)},

which is a
coboundary
with respect to
R_{\alpha}
, i.e., it can be
expressed in the form
\psi-\psi\circ R_{\alpha}
for some measurable function

\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
(in this case,
\psi=C(\log x+\log(1-x))
, and thus the
associated skew-product is not ergodic (see
[
1
, Chapter
8]
).

For cocycles with logarithmic singularities over IETs, the only results about ergodicity were proved by Frączek and the last author (see
[
17
]
, which generalizes previous work in
[
15
]
in the particular case of periodic-type IETs). In
[
17
]
, the
existence
of ergodic skew-products over IETs given by cocycles with symmetric logarithmic singularities is proved for any
d\geq 2
and any combinatorics, but the class of cocycles considered is not explicit. In particular, it is shown that for almost every IET and every cocycle
\varphi
with symmetric logarithmic singularities, there exists a piecewise constant function
\chi
, constant on the intervals exchanged by
T
, called
correction
, such that the skew-product
T_{\varphi-\chi}
given by the corrected cocycle
\varphi-\chi
is ergodic. While this provides a large class of cocycles with symmetric logarithmic singularities whose
\mathbb{R}
-extension is ergodic (indeed, corrected cocycles form a subspace of finite codimension), it is not an
explicit
class (since it is not clear how to check whether a cocycle is indeed
corrected
). On the other hand, the assumption we make on the roof, namely being odd with respect to the involution, is explicit and checkable.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we build ergodic extensions of locally Hamiltonian flows in genus two (see §
1.2
). We state the precise result in the next section (see Theorem
1.2
).

1.2
Ergodicity of locally Hamiltonian flows extensions

Let
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth area-preserving flow (or equivalently, a locally Hamiltonian flow, see for example
[
17
]
) on a compact closed surface
M
. Given a continuous function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
, the associated extension flow on
M\times\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
(\phi_{t}^{f})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and given by

\phi_{t}^{f}(x,a)=\left(\phi_{t}(x),a+\int_{0}^{t}f(\phi_{s}(x))ds\right),

(4)

for any
x\in M
, and any
a,t\in\mathbb{R}.
Notice that the flow
\phi^{f}_{\mathbb{R}}
has a skew-product form, i.e., it
projects
on the
M
coordinate to the flow
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}
, and that the motion in the
\mathbb{R}
fiber is determined by the oscillations of the
ergodic integrals
of
f
along
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}
. These flows arise in the work of Poincaré
[
29
]
by coupling
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}
with the solutions of the differential equation on
\mathbb{R}

\frac{\mathrm{d}\,\!y}{\mathrm{d}\,\!t}=f(\psi_{t}(p)),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R},\ \quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

For surfaces of
genus one
, the existence of ergodic extensions was first discovered by Krygin
[
23
]
in the case where the flow
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}
has no singularities.
For flows on the torus with singularities, a much-studied class of locally Hamiltonian flows first studied by Arnold in
[
4
]
, and, in particular, for an
Arnold flow
(namely restriction to the minimal component of a locally Hamiltonian flow in genus one with one saddle and one center), ergodicity of extensions was proved for a full measure set of rotation numbers by Fayad and Lemańczyk in
[
12
]
(as a corollary of the ergodicity of skew-products over rotations under an asymmetric cocycle
\varphi
of the form (
3
) (the reduction is also explained in §
2.2
below)).

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
we can produce explicit examples of ergodic extensions of locally Hamiltonian flows in genus two. Let us recall that minimal locally Hamiltonian flows with only saddles are (singular) time-reparametrizations of linear flows on translation surfaces (an observation which goes back to Zorich, see
[
43
]
,
[
32
]
). More precisely, for a given locally Hamiltonian flow
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, there exists a translation structure on
M
and a non-negative function
\alpha:M\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, which is as regular as the vector field associated to
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and vanishes at saddle points, such that if
X_{v}
is the vertical vector field with respect to the translation structure,
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is generated by the rescaled vector field

\alpha X_{v}
.

We will say that
\alpha
is the
generator
of the time-change
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Let us also recall that every translation surface in genus two admits a
hyperelliptic involution
, i.e., a continuous map
\mathcal{I}:M\to M
such that
\mathcal{I}\neq\id_{M}
,
\mathcal{I}^{2}
is equal to the identity, and verifies

v_{{-t}}(\mathcal{I}(x))=\mathcal{I}(v_{t}(x)),

(5)

for any
x\in M
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
for which the flows above are well-defined.
We will refer to it as the
underlying hyperelliptic involution
.

We will prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

For a typical Hamiltonian flow
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of class
C^{2}
on a surface
M
of genus
2
with two isomorphic saddles, the extension
(\phi^{f}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
given by a
C^{2}
function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is ergodic if
the function
\alpha f
, where
\alpha
is the generator of the time-change which gives
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, is
odd
with respect to the underlying hyperelliptic involution
\mathcal{I}
on
M
, i.e.,

\alpha f\circ\mathcal{I}=-\alpha f.

(6)

The notion of
typical
is measure-theoretical and refers to the measure class induced by the Katok fundamental class (see e.g.
[
36
]
).
In
[
17
]
, the existence of ergodic extensions of typical minimal locally Hamiltonian flows with non-degenerate saddles was proved in any genus by Frączek and the third author (extending the previous work
[
15
]
by the same authors treating only a measure zero class of such flows) under the assumption that the observable
f
belongs to a finite codimension (but still infinite dimensional) subspace (which corresponds to the subspace of
corrected
cocycles mentioned in the previous section §
1.1
). The nature of this class, though, is mysterious, and it is unclear how to effectively check that a given cocycle is indeed
corrected
and hence induces an ergodic extension. On the other hand, as explained below, Theorem
1.2
allows defining an infinite dimensional space of functions that induce explicit examples of ergodic extensions.

In §
2.2
, we show how Theorem
1.2
can be reduced to an application of Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, we will show that, by taking a suitable Poincaré section, the study of the ergodicity of extension flows
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in the statement of Theorem
1.2
can be reduced to the study of ergodicity of skew-products of the form (
19
).
We will show more generally that
there exists a class
\mathcal{F}_{odd}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{2}(M)
of observables (that we call “
odd
”) such that for any
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
in
\mathcal{F}_{odd}

the extension
(\phi^{f}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is ergodic (see Remark
1
) and that this space
\mathcal{F}_{odd}
contains the infinite-dimensional space of functions
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
which are odd with respect to the underlying hyperelliptic involution in the sense of (
6
).

1.3
Outline of the proof

Let us conclude the introduction by presenting some of the ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To show ergodicity, we make use of a criterion (stated here below as Proposition
1.4
) for the ergodicity of skew-products introduced and used in
[
15
,
24
]
. Let us first recall the definition of partial rigidity sets.

Definition 1.3

(Partial rigidity sets)
.

Given a probability preserving transformation
T:(X,\mu)\to(X,\mu)
, we say that a sequence of Borel subsets
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
is a sequence of
partial rigidity sets
if there exists an increasing sequence of natural numbers
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, called
rigidity times
, and
\delta>0
such that, as
n\to\infty
:

(i)

\mu(\Xi_{n})\to\delta>0
,

(ii)

\mu(\Xi_{n}\Delta T(\Xi_{n}))\to 0
,

(iii)

\sup_{x\in\Xi_{n}}d(x,T^{h_{n}}x)\to 0
.

We then say that
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
\delta
-partial rigidity sets along the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of rigidity times.

In particular, this definition guarantees that the iterates
T^{h_{n}}
, when restricted to
\Xi_{n}
, converge pointwise to the identity. The following criterion shows that ergodicity follows from the existence of partial rigidity sets
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and rigidity times
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the
Birkhoff sums

(S_{h_{n}}\varphi(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

are
tight
(Assumption
1
in Proposition
1.4
) when restricted restricted to
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
while having enough
oscillation
(Assumption
2
in Proposition
1.4
).

Proposition 1.4

(Ergodicity criterion,
[
15
,
24
]
)
.

Let
T:(X,\mu)\to(X,\mu)
be an automorphism of a compact metric space
X
preserving an ergodic probability measure
\mu
and let
\varphi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
. Suppose there exist
\delta
-partial rigidity sets
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some
\delta>0
(in the sense of Definition
1.3
) and corresponding rigidity times
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the following two assumptions hold:

1.

BS Tightness Assumption
:
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Xi_{n}}|S_{h_{n}}\varphi(x)d\mu(x)|<+\infty
.

2.

BS Oscillations Assumption
:
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{\Xi_{n}}e^{2\pi kiS_{h_{n}}\varphi(x)}d\mu(x)\right|<\delta
.

Then the associated skew-product
T_{\varphi}
is ergodic.

The criterion stated above is proved in [Proposition 2.3,
[
15
]
] (see also [Proposition 12,
[
24
]
). Assumption
1
guarantees that the sequence of distributions of Birkhoff sums
(S_{h_{n}}\varphi|_{\Xi_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, when seen as random variables where the variable
x
is uniformly distributed on
\Xi_{n}
, is
tight
, which guarantees
tightness of the Birkhoff sums
along partial rigidity sets. Assumption
2
can be verified by showing that the Birkhoff sums
S_{h_{n}}\varphi
display sufficient oscillations; from this, the name
Birkhoff sums oscillations
.

In our case, the sets
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the criterion will be given by appropriate Rohlin towers, which we obtain as subsets of the towers provided by the Rauzy-Veech induction procedure. By picking the induction times appropriately, the measure of these towers will be bounded from below (so that Condition (
i
) of Definition
1.3
holds), and they will be rigid (in the sense of Conditions (
ii
), (
iii
) of Definition
1.3
). Furthermore, one can guarantee that iterates of points in the tower up to rigidity times do not come too close to singularities.

To verify the Assumption
1
, it is crucial to exploit that the logarithmic singularities are
symmetric
(otherwise, when the singularities are asymmetric, there cannot be tightness, see, e.g.,
[
37
,
32
,
35
]
). To prove tightness, we rely on the cancellations proved in
[
38
]
, which guarantee that Birkhoff sums of the derivatives can be controlled far from singularities. We also need to control some values of the cocycle (at midpoints of each continuity interval). The latter control is achieved by exploiting the symmetry of the permutation combined with the assumption that the cocycle is
odd
.

Finally, to prove that together with tightness (i.e., Assumption
1
) there are also oscillations (i.e., Assumption
2
holds), which are crucial to guarantee ergodicity (since cocycles which are coboundaries are trivially tight, but do not produce ergodicity), it will be helpful to have a quantitative control
from below
of the derivative of Birkhoff sums on the sets
(\Xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. This can be achieved (see Theorem
3.15
) guaranteeing that the orbits up to the rigidity time of the points in the rigidity set
\Xi_{n}
do get close (although in a controlled fashion) to discontinuities on which the cocycle
\varphi
has a non-trivial logarithmic singularity.

2
Preliminaries and reduction

First, let us define several notions we informally mentioned in the introduction (particularly the class of cocycles we consider). We then show in §
2.2
how to deduce the result about extensions of locally Hamiltonian flows in genus two from the result on skew-products.

2.1
Basic definitions

2.1.1
Symmetric interval exchange maps

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
d
-element alphabet. An interval exchange transformation
T=(\pi,\lambda)
is a piecewise isometry of the interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
determined by a pair
\pi=(\pi_{0},\pi_{1})
(to which we refer to as a
permutation
) of bijections
\pi_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{A}\to\{1,\ldots,d\}
, for
\varepsilon=0,1
, and a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
. For any
\lambda=(\lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
, let

|\lambda|=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha},\quad I=\left[0,|\lambda|\right),

and define

I_{\alpha}=[l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}),\qquad\text{ where }\qquad l_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\beta)<\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta},\;\;\;r_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\beta)\leq\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta}.

With these notations,
|I_{\alpha}|=\lambda_{\alpha}
. We consider only
irreducible
permutations, that is, only pairs of bijections
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:\mathcal{A}\to\{1,\dots,d\}
for which
\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{0}^{-1}\{1,\dots,k\}=\{1,\ldots,k\}
implies
k=d
. We denote by
S^{\mathcal{A}}
the set of all irreducible permutations. Denote by
\Omega_{\pi}
the matrix

[\Omega_{\alpha\,\beta}]_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}
given by

\Omega_{\alpha\,\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}+1&\text{ if
}\pi_{1}(\alpha)>\pi_{1}(\beta)\text{ and
}\pi_{0}(\alpha)<\pi_{0}(\beta),\\
-1&\text{ if }\pi_{1}(\alpha)<\pi_{1}(\beta)\text{ and
}\pi_{0}(\alpha)>\pi_{0}(\beta),\\
0&\text{ in all other cases.}\end{array}\right.

(7)

Then, the IET
T_{(\pi,\lambda)}
associated with
(\pi,\lambda)
is given by
T_{(\pi,\lambda)}x=x+w_{\alpha}
, for any
x\in I_{\alpha}
, where
w=\Omega_{\pi}\lambda
.

Let
End(T)
stand for the set of endpoints of the intervals
\{I_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
. A pair
{(\pi,\lambda)}
satisfies the
Keane condition
if
T_{(\pi,\lambda)}^{m}(l_{\alpha})\neq l_{\beta}
, for all
m\geq 1
, and for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}
with
\pi_{0}(\beta)\neq 1
. Keane
[
21
]
showed that an IET with an irreducible permutation that satisfies this condition is minimal.

We often consider the case of normalized IETs, that is when
|I|=1
. For this purpose, we denote by
\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}
the positive simplex of all vectors
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{A}}_{>0}
such that
|\lambda|=1
.

Symmetric permutations.
In this paper, we will be mostly concerned with
symmetric combinatorics
(which fully reverse the order of the intervals), i.e., such that

\pi_{1}\circ{\pi_{0}^{-1}}(i)={d}-i+1,

for every
1\leq i\leq d
.
Notice that
when
\pi
is the symmetric permutation, the discontinuities of
T
and
T^{-1}
, which we denote by
0=u_{0}^{t}<u_{1}^{t}<\dots<u_{d}^{t}=1
and
0=u_{0}^{b}<u_{1}^{b}<\dots<u_{d}^{b}=1
, respectively, are given by

u_{k}^{t}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\alpha)<k}\lambda_{\alpha},\quad\quad u_{k}^{b}=\sum_{\pi_{1}(\alpha)<k}\lambda_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\alpha)\geq d+1-k}\lambda_{\alpha}{=1-u_{d-k}^{t}}.

(8)

Thus, for any
{1}\leq k\leq d
, we have that

T(l_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(k)})=T(u_{k-1}^{t})=u_{d-k}^{b}.

(9)

2.1.2
Odd cocycles with logarithmic singularities

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alphabet of
d\geq 3
elements and consider a partition
I=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha}
of
I
into a finite number of subintervals. Throughout this work, we will be interested in the partition
I=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha}
given by the intervals exchanged by an IET.
We denote by
C^{r}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
the space of functions
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi\mid_{I_{\alpha}}
is of class
C^{r}
, for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Similarly, we denote by
AC(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
(resp.
BV(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
) the space of functions whose restrictions to each interval are absolutely continuous (resp. of bounded variation).

Symmetric logarithmic singularities.

We will consider the following class of singular cocycles:

Definition 2.1

(Logarithmic cocycles)
.

We say that the cocycle
\varphi\in C(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
has
logarithmic singularities
if there exist constants
C_{\alpha}^{+},C_{\alpha}^{-}\in\mathbb{R}
, for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, and a function
g_{\varphi}\in AC(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
such that
g_{\varphi}^{\prime}\in BV(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and

\varphi(x)=g_{\varphi}(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{\alpha}^{+}\log\left(|I|\left\{\frac{(x-l_{\alpha})}{|I|}\right\}\right)+C_{\alpha}^{-}\log\left(|I|\left\{\frac{(r_{\alpha}-x)}{|I|}\right\}\right).

(10)

Furthermore, we say that the logarithmic singularities of
\varphi
are
non-trivial
if

C_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(1)}^{+}+C_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)}^{+}\neq 0\quad\text{ or }\quad C_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(k)}^{+}\neq 0,\text{ for some }1<k<d.

The non-triviality condition guarantees that at least one branch has a nontrivial logarithmic singularity. The reason for the formulation (in particular, the first of the above conditions that asks for a
sum
of two constants to be non-zero) is technical (and related to the special role played by the preimage of the endpoint
0
as discontinuity). Still, it is easy to check in the class cocycles with a geometric origin (i.e., appear from the reduction of an extension of a locally Hamiltonian flow to a skew-product).

Definition 2.2
.

We denote by
LS^{r}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
the space of functions in
C^{r}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
having
non-trivial
logarithmic singularities.

Definition 2.3

(Symmetric logarithmic cocycles)
.

Given
\varphi\in LS^{r}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
as in (
10
), we say that its logarithmic singularities are
symmetric
if

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{\alpha}^{-}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}C_{\alpha}^{+}.

(11)

We denote by
LSS^{r}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
the space of functions with logarithmic symmetric singularities. Otherwise, we say that the logarithmic singularities are
asymmetric
.

Note that in the locally Hamiltonian flows literature (see e.g.
[
38
,
18
]
), when logarithmic singularities are considered, it is usually for roofs of special flows, so that the constants

are all positive (or non-negative). We stress that in this definition for
cocycles
(as in
[
15
,
18
]
), the constants

are real numbers, not necessarily positive.

Odd cocycles.

Let us now define the class of cocycles that we call
odd on each interval
. Given an interval

, we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{I}:I\to I
the involution given by

\mathcal{I}_{I}(x)=a+b-x,\qquad\text{for\, all}\ x\in I.

(12)

One can check , by a direct calculation, that any symmetric IET

verifies

T^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{I}=\mathcal{I}_{I}\circ T.

(13)

Definition 2.4

(Cocycles odd on each continuity interval)
.

A cocycle
\varphi
in

is
odd with respect to each continuity interval
if the cocycle
\varphi
restricted to each
I_{\alpha}
is an odd symmetric function with respect to the middle point of the restriction interval, i.e.,

\varphi(x)=-\varphi(\mathcal{I}_{I_{\alpha}}(x))\qquad\text{for\ all}\ x\in I_{\alpha},\,\alpha\in\mathcal{A}.

(14)

Given an IET
T:I\to I
with a

permutation, the oddness condition stated above can be equivalently rephrased as a request that the cocycle has the following behavior under the

\mathcal{I}_{I}
of the domain of definition

Lemma 2.5

(Oddness and involution, see also

)

Let

be an IET with a symmetric permutation. A cocycle

is odd on each continuity interval of

if and only if

\varphi\circ T^{-1}=-\varphi\circ\mathcal{I}_{I}.

(15)

The proof is based on a simple calculation, which also appeared in
[

]
, and we include here to underline the role of symmetry.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in I_{\alpha}
. Let us first show that since
T
has a symmetric permutation

\mathcal{I}_{I_{\alpha}}(x)=\mathcal{I}_{I}\circ T(x).

(16)

Indeed, if
0\leq i<d
is such that
I_{\alpha}=[u^{t}_{i},u^{t}_{i+1})
,
T
maps linearly
I_{\alpha}
onto
[|I|-u^{t}_{i+1},|I|-u^{t}_{i})

for all

Thus, one can verify directly that (

\mathcal{I}_{I}(T(x))=|I|-T(x)=|I|-x-|I|+u^{t}_{i}+u^{t}_{i+1}=u^{t}_{i}+u^{t}_{i+1}-x=\mathcal{I}_{I_{\alpha}}(x),

where the last equality follows by the definition (

\mathcal{I}_{I_{\alpha}}

We now claim that a measurable function

is odd on each interval if and only if it satisfies (

can be equivalently rewritten as

Indeed, this expression

2.1.3
Rauzy-Veech induction notation

The classical induction procedure for IETs, known as the

Notation for iterates.
Given an IET

of

intervals corresponding to

and verifying Keane’s condition, one can define, for any

, the iterates of

on a suitably chosen sequence of nested subintervals shrinking towards the left endpoint of

:=I

For any

{I^{n}:=}I^{n}(T)
the

the IET obtained inducing

on

, i.e., the first return map of

to
I^{n}\subseteq I
. We denote by
\{{I^{n}_{\alpha}:=}I^{n}_{\alpha}(T)\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
the intervals exchanged by
T_{n}
and by

the length of the inducing interval. The
n
-th iterate of Rauzy-Veech renormalization of

is then given by

\mathcal{RV}^{n}(T)(x):=\frac{T_{n}({|I^{n}|}x)}{{|I^{n}|}},\qquad\forall x\in I.

Finally we denote by
q^{n}=q^{n}(T)=(q^{n}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
the vector in
\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{A}}
whose entries
q^{n}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, give the
return time
of
I^{n}_{\alpha}
to
I^{n}
under the action of
T
. If there is no risk of confusion, we will omit the explicit dependence on
T
in all of the above notations.

Any finite sequence of permutations which can be obtained as successive permutations of an orbit of the Rauzy-Veech algorithm is called a
Rauzy path

\gamma
of combinatorial length
|\gamma|
(where the combinatorial length is the number of permutations minus
1
). With every Rauzy path

we can associate a non-negative
d\times d
-matrix

, called

, which satisfies the following: if
(\pi,\lambda)
is such that
(\pi^{i})_{i=0}^{|\gamma|-1}=\gamma
, then
\lambda=A_{\gamma}\lambda^{n}
. An important use of this matrix comes from the fact that if
A_{\gamma}
is a positive matrix for some Rauzy path
\gamma
, then there exists a constant
\rho(\gamma)>0
such that, for any IET
(\pi,\lambda)
verifying Keane’s condition and any
n\geq|\gamma|
such that
(\pi^{i})_{i=n-|\gamma|+1}^{n}=\gamma
, we have

\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{q_{\alpha}^{n}}{q_{\beta}^{n}}<\rho(\gamma).

(17)

Polygons and zippered rectangles.

Given an IET
(\pi,\lambda)
one can add a
suspension datum

\tau\in\Theta^{\pi}
, where

\Theta^{\pi}:=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{A}}\,\left|\,\quad\sum_{\pi_{1}(\alpha)<k}\tau_{\alpha}<0<\sum_{\pi_{0}(\alpha)<k}\tau_{\alpha},\ \text{ for every }1<k\leq d\right\}\right..

By drawing two broken line segments starting at 0 in

, one created by taking the consecutive line segments

\lambda_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(i)}+i\tau_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(i)}

the space of all polygonal representations. Notice that some of these polygonal representations may have self-intersections. However, in this paper, we will only use polygonal representations associated with triples for which this does not happen. We also consider the space of
normalized
polygonal representations

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{M}}:=\{(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\in{\mathcal{M}}\ |

\displaystyle\ \ |\lambda|=1\text{ and }Area((\pi,\lambda,\tau))=1\},

\displaystyle{\text{where}\ Area((\pi,\lambda,\tau)):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{\pi_{0}(\beta)<\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\tau_{\beta}\right).}

(\pi,\lambda,\tau)

(see

42

[
41
]
or
[
42
]
.

On the space
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
of triples
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
one can define the
extended Rauzy-Veech induction

\mathcal{R}:\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, which provides an invertible extension of
\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
. At the level of polygonal representations, the action of

extended Rauzy-Veech induction

\mathcal{R}
consists of cutting a
properly chosen triangle of the polygon
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
and gluing it to
one of the intervals corresponding to the second copy of one of its sides. In this case, both polygons, before and after induction, represent the same translation surface. We denote by
\mathcal{R}^{n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)=(\pi^{n},\lambda^{n},\tau^{n})

One can also consider the

as follows. Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathcal{A}}
, let us denote by

. Then

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(\pi,\lambda,\tau):=\left(\pi^{1},\frac{\lambda^{1}}{|\lambda^{1}|},|\lambda^{1}|\cdot\tau^{1}\right).

The normalized extended Rauzy-Veech induction is invertible and extends the
normalized Rauzy-Veech induction

. The importance of this extension comes from a classical theorem
by Masur
[

]
and Veech

states that this transformation is ergodic with respect to a probability
measure equivalent to a product of the counting measure on
S^{\mathcal{A}}
and
the Lebesgue measure on
\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Theta^{\pi}
.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\alpha f(v_{-t}\circ v_{-\rho(x)}\circ\mathcal{I}\circ T(x))\,dt=\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\alpha f(v_{-t}\circ\mathcal{I}(x))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\alpha f(\mathcal{I}\circ v_{t}(x))\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\alpha f(v_{t}(x))\,dt

\displaystyle=-\varphi(x).

Therefore,
\varphi
verifies (
15
).

Thus, we proved that the skew-product
T_{\varphi}
that we obtain in this setting satisfies the assumptions of Theorem

and hence it is ergodic if

belongs to a full measure set of IETs. Since the extension

is ergodic if (and only if) its Poincaré map

is ergodic, Theorem
1.2
now follows from the fact that the set of locally Hamiltonian flows whose Poincaré map on a given section
\mathscr{S}
(when seen as translation surfaces as described above) belongs to a full measure set of IETs has full measure with respect to the measure class given by the Katok fundamental class.
∎

3.1
Construction of good base dynamics

Let us start by showing how to construct, for any IET
T
with a sufficiently small
\delta
-drift
(see Definition
3.2
) and sufficiently balanced lengths, subintervals with nice rigidity properties, whose distance to the discontinuities of
T
is explicitly controlled.

In the following, if
\pi
is a symmetric permutation on
\mathcal{A}
, let
\alpha\mapsto\overline{\alpha}
be the involution of the alphabet, which inverts the order of the letters from left to right in the interval, namely for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{\alpha}

\pi
is a symmetric combinatorial datum, we define
\overline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}

to be

\overline{\alpha}:=\pi_{0}^{-1}(\pi_{1}(\alpha))=\pi_{0}^{-1}(d+1-\pi_{0}(\alpha)),\qquad\text{for each}\,\alpha\in\mathcal{A}.

Remark that if all subintervals had equal length, i.e.,
\lambda_{\alpha}=1/d
for all

(which is not compatible with the Keane condition) then, because of the symmetry assumption on the permutation,

would be the identity and the intervals
I_{\alpha}
would be mapped to
I_{\overline{\alpha}}
by

, and vice versa.

The IETs which we consider to build good dynamics (and that later we will use as base dynamics for Rohlin towers given by renormalization) are just a perturbation of this order two periodic behavior, chosen so that the iterates of a subinterval of
F_{\alpha}\subseteq I_{\alpha}
when moving to
I_{\overline{\alpha}}
and then back in
I_{\alpha}
,

to the right by a proportion
\delta>0
of

. To define the class of IETs more precisely, let us give two definitions.

Definition 3.1

(Balanced lengths)
.

Given an IET
T=(\pi,\lambda)
and
\nu>0
, we say that
T
is
\nu
-balanced

\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{\lambda_{\beta}}<\nu.

In particular,

\frac{{|I|}}{\nu d}\leq\lambda_{\beta}\leq\frac{\nu{|I|}}{d},\quad\text{for all }\beta\in\mathcal{A}.

The second definition characterizes the parameters which give the drifting phenomenon.

Definition 3.2

(

Given an IET

Good renormalization times

We now define, in §

3.2.1
Discontinuities and derivative control

Let us introduce a few definitions used in describing good renormalization times.
It is rather classical to consider times when the Rauzy-Veech induction is
balanced
in the following sense.

Definition 3.4

(Balanced times)
.

Given an IET
T=(\pi,\lambda)
and
\nu>0
, we say that
n\geq 0
is a
\nu
-balanced time for
T
if
\lambda^{n}
and
q^{n}
are
\nu
-balanced, i.e.,

\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{\lambda^{n}_{\alpha}}{\lambda^{n}_{\beta}}<\nu,\qquad\max_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{q^{n}_{\alpha}}{q^{n}_{\beta}}<\nu.

(34)

Thus, both the lenght vector
\lambda^{n}
and the height vector
q^{n}
are
\nu
-balanced in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

The next condition guarantees that the location of the discontinuities in Rohlin towers is controlled.

Definition 3.5

(Well-positioned discontinuities)
.

Given an IET
T=(\pi,\lambda)
with a symmetric permutation, we say that
T
has
well-positioned singularities at time
n
if
\pi^{n}=\pi
and, for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
with
1<\pi_{0}(\alpha)<d
, there exists an integer

such that

\tfrac{q^{n}_{\alpha}}{4}<i_{\alpha}^{n}<q^{n}_{\alpha}\quad\text{and }\quad l_{\alpha}=T^{i^{n}_{\alpha}}(l_{\alpha}^{n}).

(35)

Combining balanced times with
\delta
-drift for the induced IET (see
Definition
3.2
), we will be able to obtain good rigidity properties
for the Rohlin towers associated with the induction procedure (see Lemma

3.8
). The last ingredient crucial
for tightness is the existence of times, which we call
good derivative
control times
, where the following control of the Birkhoff sums
of the first derivative (which is not an integrable function) holds (whose
existence was proved in the work of the last author, see

[
38
]
).

Definition 3.6

(Good derivative control times)
.

Given an IET
T:I\to I
, a cocycle
\varphi\in LSS^{2}(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
of the form (
10
), and

, we say that

is a

-good derivative control time
for

if, for any

and any

,

\left|S_{r}{\varphi^{\prime}}(z)-\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{C_{\alpha}^{+}}{z_{\alpha}^{+}}+\frac{C_{\alpha}^{-}}{z_{\alpha}^{-}}\right)\right|\leq Mr,

where
z_{\alpha}^{+}
and
z_{\alpha}^{-}
are the closest visits of

and
r_{\alpha}
respectively, among the first
r

\{T^{i}z,0\leq i<r\}
of

.

We remark that since
{\varphi^{\prime}}
has singularities of type

, which are not integrable, the conclusion of Birkhoff ergodic theorem does not apply to
{\varphi^{\prime}}
and the existence of good derivative control times as the above can only be proved when the logarithmic singularities are
symmetric
and under a (full measure) delicate Diophantine-like condition for the IET (see
[
38

or the survey
[
36

for further details), while it typically fails when the singularities are asymmetric (see
[
37
,
32
]
).

For a.e. IET

3.4

Cancellations.

We can now use the oddness assumption on the cocycle, together with the location of the centers, to show cancellations. For this purpose, we will use the following consequence of the following result (first proved in

Lemma 3.13

(Lemma 4.6 in
[
8

Let

Proof.

Recall that symmetry of

\displaystyle S_{n}\varphi(T^{-n}(\mathcal{I}_{I}(x)))

∎

3.6

In this section, we conclude the proof of the main result of this article, namely Theorem

Let us first explain the strategy. To prove ergodicity, we need to verify its assumptions of the ergodicity criterion given by Proposition

To check Condition (

To check Condition (

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\psi:[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a

0<\eta<1

\rho=\min_{x\in[0,\eta]}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|>0,\quad\quad\textup{Var}(\psi^{\prime})|^{\eta}_{0}\leq V.

\left|\int_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi ki\psi(x)}d\textup{Leb}(x)\right|\leq 1-\eta+\frac{C}{k}.

Let us first conclude the strategy overview and then give the proof of the lemma above.
To apply Lemma

Proof of Lemma

Let us assume WLOG that

From the definition of variation, using that for any interval

one can show that

Using this estimate and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{2\pi ki\psi(x)}\textrm{d}\textup{Leb}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\eta}\frac{\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{2\pi ki\psi(x)}\right)}{2\pi ki\psi^{\prime}(x)}\textrm{d}\textup{Leb}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi k}\left|\left[\frac{e^{2\pi ki\psi(x)}}{\psi^{\prime}(x)}\right]_{0}^{\eta}-\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{{2\pi ki\psi(x)}}\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime}(x)}\right)\textrm{d}{\textup{Leb}(x)}\right|

\displaystyle{\leq\frac{2}{2\pi k\,{\min_{0\leq x\leq\eta}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|^{2}}}+\frac{1}{2\pi k}\frac{V}{\rho^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{\pi\rho}\left(1+\frac{V}{2\rho}\right)}

which concludes the proof. ∎

\{\Xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partial rigidity sequence with partial rigidity times
\{2h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, in the sense of Definition
1.3
. We now proceed to verify the two conditions of the ergodicity criterion.

Tightness of Birkhoff sums.
To show tightness, we will compare the Birkhoff sums
S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x)
of any
x\in\Xi_{n}
with the Birkhoff sums
S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x_{\alpha}^{n})
, where the point
x^{n}_{\alpha}
belongs to the tower over
I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}
given by Proposition
3.10
. Recall that, in particular, this explicit Birkhoff sum is either equal to 0 or it is very close to 0.

To study the difference
|S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x)-S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x_{\alpha}^{n})|
, we can split both the orbit segments of
x
and
x_{\alpha}^{n}
of length
2h_{n}
into two orbit segments and pair them so that we can apply the mean value theorem to the differences of the corresponding Birkhoff sums as follows.

Assume, for example,
k:=j-i\geq 0
(the other case is similar). Since
x=T^{i}(x_{0})
, we have that
T^{k}(x)=T^{j-i}(T^{i}(x_{0}))=T^{j}(x_{0})
, so
T^{k}(x)
and
x^{n}_{\alpha}=T^{j}(y)
, where
y\in I_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}
, belong to the same floor of the Rohlin tower over
F^{n}_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, by the good dynamics, if
J_{1}
denotes the interval between them,
T^{\ell}
restricted to
J_{1}
is as an isometry (and in particular continuous) for
0\leq\ell<h_{n}-k
(by the good dynamics given by Lemma
3.8
, see in particular
2
). Hence, we can apply the mean value theorem so that, for some
z_{1}\in J_{1}
,

\left|S_{h_{n}-{k}}\varphi(T^{k}(x))-S_{h_{n}-{k}}\varphi(x^{n}_{\alpha})\right|\leq\left|S_{h_{n}-{k}}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{1})\right||J_{1}|.

(60)

Similarly, we have that
T^{2h_{n}-k}(x^{n}_{\alpha})
and
x=T^{i}(x_{0})
belong to the same floor of the tower over
I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}
(since we can write
T^{2h_{n}-k}(x^{n}_{\alpha})=T^{i-j}T^{2h_{n}}(T^{j}(y))=T^{i}(T^{2h_{n}}(y))
where
y\in F^{n}_{\alpha}
and, by the good dynamics,
T^{2h_{n}}(y)
is again in
I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}
). Thus, if
J_{2}
denotes the interval between them,
T^{\ell}
restricted to
J_{2}
is an isometry for
0\leq\ell<k
. Thus, as above, by the mean value theorem, there exists
z_{2}\in J_{2}
such that

\left|S_{k}\varphi(x)-S_{{k}}\varphi(T^{2h_{n}-k}x^{n}_{\alpha})\right|\leq\left|S_{{k}}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{2})\right||J_{2}|.

(61)

Let us now estimate the RHS of (
60
) and (
61
).
By the
M
-good derivative control (see Definition
3.6
) and the control on the closest visit given by Conditions
3
or
4
of Lemma
3.8
, recalling that we chose
\eta<\delta
, we can estimate

\left|S_{{k}}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{2})\right|\leq Mk+\frac{(2d-1)C_{max}}{\delta|I^{m_{n}}|}+\frac{C^{+}_{\gamma(\alpha)}}{\eta|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|}\leq Mq^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}+\frac{2dC_{max}}{\eta|I^{m_{n}}|}.

(62)

Similarly, writing
z_{1}=T^{k}(z_{0})
for
z_{0}\in F^{n}_{\alpha}
and seeing the RHS of (
60
) as a difference, using Proposition
3.9
and an estimate similar to the above, we get

\left|S_{2h_{n}-k}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{1})\right|=\left|S_{2h_{n}}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{0})-S_{k}\varphi^{\prime}(z_{0})\right|\leq(D+M)q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}+\frac{(2d+1)C_{max}}{\eta|I^{m_{n}}|}.

(63)

Combining all these estimates (i.e., adding up (
60
) and (
61
) and applying (
62
) and (
63
)), using balance (which gives
1/|I^{m_{n}}|\leq d\nu q^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}
) and recalling that
|J_{i}|\leq|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|
, for
i=1,2
, we get

\displaystyle\big|S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x)-S_{2h_{n}}\varphi(x_{\alpha}^{n})\big|

\displaystyle\left|\left(S_{k}\varphi(x)+S_{2h_{n}-k}\varphi(T^{k}x)\right)-\left(S_{2h_{n}-k}\varphi(x_{\alpha}^{n})+S_{k}\varphi(T^{2h_{n}-k}x_{\alpha}^{n})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|S_{k}\varphi(x)-S_{{k}}\varphi(T^{2h_{n}-k}x^{n}_{\alpha})\right|+\left|S_{2h_{n}-{k}}\varphi(T^{k}x)-S_{2h_{n}-{k}}\varphi(x^{n}_{\alpha})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\overline{D}q^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|\leq\overline{D},\quad\text{where}\ \overline{D}:=D+2M+\frac{\nu(2d^{2}+d)C_{max}}{\eta},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\Xi_{n}
. This, together with (
49
), shows that
the sets
\{\Xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy Condition
1
in
Proposition
1.4
.

Oscillations of Birkhoff sums.
We now proceed to show that Condition
2
in Proposition
1.4
is also satisfied.
The proof differs slightly depending on whether the assumption (
57
) on the constants or the assumption (
58
) is satisfied. However, the only real difference comes from the fact that in the second case, namely (
57
) (when only the sum of two constants is known to be non-zero), we approach two discontinuities of
f
at once (since there is one in 0 and one in the pre-image of 0 via
T
), while in the case (
58
) we approach only one singularity.

We will give the argument only in the case (
58
), leaving the details of the other case to the reader. In this case,
let
\widetilde{F}^{n}_{\alpha}
be the left-hand side initial interval of
F^{n}_{\alpha}
(namely, an interval with the same left endpoint) of length
\eta\cdot|F^{n}_{\alpha}|\leq\eta/{q_{\alpha}^{{m_{n}}}}
, and consider the subtower of
\Xi_{n}
above it (namely, a Rohlin tower of height
1/4
th of the whole tower), i.e.,

\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\lceil q^{{m_{n}}}_{\alpha}/4\rceil}T^{i}\big(\widetilde{F}^{n}_{\alpha}\big)\subseteq\Xi_{n}.

Note that each of the intervals
T^{i}\big(\widetilde{F}^{n}_{\alpha}\big)
is again the left subiterval of
T^{i}\big(F^{n}_{\alpha}\big)
. Recalling that the left endpoint of
F^{n}_{\alpha}
(and hence
\widetilde{F}^{n}_{\alpha}
) is
l^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}+\eta|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|
and
\widetilde{F}^{n}_{\alpha}
has length
\eta|I^{m_{b}}_{\alpha}|
, for any
x\in\tilde{\Xi}_{n}
, the closest visit to a singularity (given by (
43
)), as discussed in §
3.3.2
) is not closer than
2\eta|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|
. Thus, Proposition
3.9
and balance (which guarantees that
|I^{m_{n}}|q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}\geq\min_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}}\left(|I^{m_{n}}_{\beta}|q_{\beta}^{m_{n}}\right)\geq 1/d\nu
) give

|S_{2h_{n}}{\varphi^{\prime}}(x)|\geq\frac{|C^{+}_{\gamma(\alpha)}|}{|x_{0}-l^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|}-Dq_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}\geq\frac{|C^{+}_{\gamma(\alpha)}|}{2\eta|I^{m_{n}}|}-D\,q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}>\left(\frac{d\nu|C^{+}_{\gamma(\alpha)}|}{2\eta}-D\right)q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}\geq\rho\,q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}},

(64)

where the last inequality follows by choice of
\eta
and definition of
\rho
, see (
59
).
On the other hand, Proposition
3.9
, Lemma
3.8
and balance give

|S_{2h_{n}}{\varphi^{\prime}}(x)|\leq Dq^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}+\frac{|C^{+}_{\alpha}|q^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}}{|x_{0}-l^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|}\leq Dq^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}+\frac{|C^{+}_{\alpha}|}{\eta|I^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}|}\leq\left(D+\frac{d\nu|C^{+}_{\alpha}|}{\eta}\right)q^{m_{n}}_{\alpha}.

(65)

The inequalities (
64
) and (
65
) imply that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
i\in\{0,\lceil q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}/4\rceil\}
, the function
\psi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
\psi(x):=S_{2h_{n}}{\varphi}(|F_{\alpha}^{n}|x+T^{i}l_{\alpha}^{m_{n}})
, satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.16
. Indeed, by (
64
), for every
x\in[0,\eta]
,

\psi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}S_{2h_{n}}{\varphi}(|F_{\alpha}^{n}|x+T^{i}l_{\alpha}^{m_{n}})\geq|F_{\alpha}^{n}|\rho q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}\geq\rho/d\nu,

where the last inequality follows from the balance.
Moreover, by (
65
), we get

Var(\psi^{\prime}|_{[0,\eta]})=\int_{0}^{\eta}|\psi^{\prime}(x)|\,dx\leq|F_{\alpha}^{n}|\left(D\eta+d\nu|C_{\alpha}^{+}|\right)q_{\alpha}^{m_{n}}\leq\left(D\eta+d\nu|C_{\alpha}^{+}|\right).

Thus, by Lemma
3.16
, there exists a constant
C>0
, which does not depend on
n
, such that, for every
0\leq i\leq{q_{\alpha}^{n}}
and
k{>}0
,

\left|\int_{T^{i}F^{n}_{\alpha}}e^{2\pi ki\varphi(x)}\,d\textup{Leb}(x)\right|\leq Leb(F^{n}_{\alpha})\left(1-\eta+\frac{C}{k}\right).

Hence, for
k
sufficiently large,

\left|\int_{\Xi_{n}}e^{2\pi ki\varphi(x)}\,d\textup{Leb}(x)\right|<\textup{Leb}(\Xi_{n}),

which shows that the sets
\{\Xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy also Condition
2
in Proposition
1.4
.

Thus, having verified all assumptions of Proposition
1.4
, we can apply it to conclude that the skew-product
T_{\varphi}
under consideration is ergodic.

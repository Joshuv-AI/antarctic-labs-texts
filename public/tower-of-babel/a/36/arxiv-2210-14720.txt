Fractional Fourier series on the torus and applications
Chen Wang, Xianming Hou, Qingyan Wu, Pei Dang and Zunwei Fu

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the fractional Fourier series on the fractional torus and proceed to investigate several fundamental aspects. Our study includes topics such as fractional convolution, fractional approximation, fractional Fourier inversion, and the Poisson summation formula. We also explore the relationship between the decay of fractional Fourier coefficients and the smoothness of a function. Additionally, we establish the pointwise convergence of the fractional Fourier series using the properties of the fractional Féjer kernel. Finally, we demonstrate the practical applications of the fractional Fourier series, particularly in the context of solving fractional partial differential equations with periodic boundary conditions, and showcase the utility of approximation methods on the fractional torus for recovering non-stationary signals.

2.
Fractional Fourier series on the torus

In this section, we begin by introducing the definition of fractional Fourier series in the setting
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. Subsequently, we establish some basic facts of fractional Fourier analysis. Additionally, we will give the fractional convolution and obtain fractional approximation in
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\neq\pi\mathbf{Z}
. Set
e_{\alpha}(x):=e^{\pi i|x|^{2}\cot\alpha}
and
e_{\alpha}f(x):=e_{\alpha}(x)f(x)
for a function
f
on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We say that a function
f
on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
lies in the space
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
if

\displaystyle f(x)=e_{-\alpha}g(x),\quad g\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})

and satisfies
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}<\infty
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a complex-valued function
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, we define

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx,&\alpha\neq\pi\mathbf{Z},\\
f(m),&\alpha=2\pi\mathbf{Z},\\
f(-m),&\alpha=2\pi\mathbf{Z}+\pi,\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(m,x):=A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(x)e_{\alpha}(m,x)e_{\alpha}(m),

here
A_{\alpha}=\sqrt{1-i\cot\alpha}
and
e_{\alpha}(m,x)=e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}
.
We call
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)
the
m
-th fractional Fourier coefficient of order
\alpha
of
f
.

In order to state our results, we recall some notations. The spaces
C^{k}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
,
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
, are defined as the sets of functions
\phi
for which
\partial^{\beta}\phi
exist and are continuous for all
|\beta|\leq k
. When
k=0
we set
C^{0}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})=C(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
to be the space of continuous functions on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. Let

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}):=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}C^{k}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}).

Definition 2.3
.

For
0\leq k\leq\infty
. The space
e_{-\alpha}C^{k}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
is defined to be the space of functions
f
on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle f(x)=e_{-\alpha}g(x),\quad g\in C^{k}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}).

Notice that the spaces
e_{-\alpha}C^{k}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
are contained in
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

We denote by
\overline{f}
the complex conjugate of the function
f
, by
\tilde{f}
the function
\tilde{f}(x)=f(-x)
, and by
\tau_{y}(f)(x)
the function
\tau_{y}(f)(x)=f(x-y)
for all
y\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. Since
\alpha=\pi\mathbf{Z}
, we get
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}=\{0\}
. Hence, throughout this paper, for
\alpha\neq\pi\mathbf{Z}
, we always assume
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}=[-|\sin\alpha|/2,|\sin\alpha|/2]^{n}
. Next, we give some elementary properties of fractional Fourier coefficients.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f,g
be in
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Then for all
m,k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
y\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
, we have

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f+g)(m)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)+\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(g)(m)
;

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\lambda f)(m)=\lambda\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)
;

(3)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\overline{f})(m)=\overline{{\mathcal{F}_{-\alpha}}(f)(m)}
;

(4)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{f})(m)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(-m)
;

(5)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\tau_{y}(e_{\alpha}f)](m)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e_{\alpha}(m,y)
;

(6)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}(k,\cdot)f](m)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot k)\cot\alpha}e_{\alpha}({k})=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m-k)
;

(7)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(0)=A_{\alpha}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{-\alpha}f(x)dx
;

(8)

\sup_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq|\csc\alpha|^{n/2}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}
;

(9)

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)](m)=(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m),
whenever
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\beta}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

It is obvious that properties (1)-(4) and (7) hold. We now pay attention to (5). Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\tau_{y}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)](m)=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}(e_{\alpha}f)(x-y)e_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}-y}(e_{\alpha}f)(x^{\prime})e_{\alpha}(m,x^{\prime}+y)dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e_{\alpha}(m,y),

where we make the variable change
x^{\prime}=x-y
in the second equality, and the third equality follows from the periodicity of function.

Next, we deal with (6).

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m-k)=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(x)e_{\alpha}(x)e_{\alpha}(m-k)e_{\alpha}(m-k,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\alpha}(k)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot k)\cot\alpha}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{-\alpha}(k,x)f(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}(k,\cdot)f](m)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot k)\cot\alpha}e_{\alpha}(k).

It follows from the fact
|K_{\alpha}(m,x)|\leq|\csc\alpha|^{n/2}
that (8) holds.

Now we turn to (9). By the periodicity and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)](m)=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}f(x)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose
f_{1}\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n_{1}}_{\alpha})
and
f_{2}\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n_{2}}_{\alpha})

(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})

is in
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}_{\alpha})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\big)(m_{1},m_{2})=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f_{1})(m_{1})\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f_{2})(m_{2}),

where
m_{1}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n_{1}}
and
m_{2}\in\mathbf{Z}^{n_{2}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

For
\alpha\neq n\pi
, a trigonometric polynomials of order
\alpha
on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
is a function of the form

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}c_{m,\alpha}K_{-\alpha}(m,x),

where
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
and
c_{m,\alpha}
is a constant depending on
m
and
\alpha
. The degree of
P_{\alpha}
is the largest number
|m_{1}|+\cdots+|m_{n}|
such that
c_{m,\alpha}
is nonzero. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)=c_{m,\alpha}.

For
x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
,
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, the fractional Fourier series of order
\alpha
of
f
is the series

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

Now, we wonder (
2.2
) converges in which sense? The convergence of the fractional Fourier series is the main topic in this paper. Next, we introduce a kind of the fractional convolution of order
\alpha
to study the convergence of fractional Fourier series.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n})
. Define the fractional convolution of order
\alpha
as follows:

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}g)(x)=|\csc\alpha|^{n}{e_{-\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(y)f(y)(\delta^{\alpha}g)(x-y)dy},

where
(\delta^{\alpha}g)(x)=g(x\csc\alpha)
.

Let
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{|m|\leq N}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|m|\leq N}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(y)K_{\alpha}(m,y)dyK_{-\alpha}(m,x)

\displaystyle=|\csc\alpha|^{n}e_{-\alpha}(x)\sum_{|m|\leq N}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(y)f(y)e^{2\pi im\cdot(x-y)\csc\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=|\csc\alpha|^{n}e_{-\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(y)f(y)\sum_{|m|\leq N}e^{2\pi im\cdot(x-y)\csc\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=:\big(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{D}_{N}^{n}\big)(x),

where
{D}^{n}_{N}(x)
is the classical multidimensional Dirichlet kernel.

Noting that one-dimensional Dirichlet kernel
{{D}}_{N}^{1}
is not an approximate identity. This fact led Cesàro and independently Fejér to consider the arithmetic means of the Dirichlet kernel in dimension
1
as follows

\displaystyle{F}^{1}_{N}(x):=\frac{1}{N+1}\big[{D}_{0}^{1}(x)+{D}_{1}^{1}(x)+\cdots+{D}_{N}^{1}(x)\big].

By Proposition 3.1.7 in
[
13
]
, we have the following equivalent way to write the kernel
{F}^{1}_{N}
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle{{F}}^{1}_{N}(x)=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|j|}{N+1}\Big)e^{2\pi ijx}={\frac{1}{N+1}\Big(\frac{\sin\big((N+1)\pi x\big)}{\sin(\pi x)}\Big)^{2}}.

The function
{{F}}^{1}_{N}
given by (
2.3
) is called the Fejér kernel.
Let

{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x):=|\csc\alpha|F^{1}_{N}(x\csc\alpha).

We have the following identity for the kernel
{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}
:

Proposition 2.8
.

For every nonnegative integer
N
, the following identity holds

(2.4)

\displaystyle{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x)=|\csc\alpha|\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|j|}{N+1}\Big)e^{2\pi ijx\csc\alpha}={\frac{|\csc\alpha|}{N+1}\Big(\frac{\sin\big((N+1)\pi x\csc\alpha\big)}{\sin(\pi x\csc\alpha)}\Big)^{2}}

for all
x\in\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}
. Thus

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(e_{-\alpha}{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}\big)(m)=A_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(m)\Big(1-\frac{|m|}{N+1}\Big)

if
|m|\leq N
and zero otherwise.

Proof.

Note that
{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(e_{-\alpha}{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}\big)(m)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}e_{-\alpha}(x){{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}|\csc\alpha|e_{\alpha}(m)\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|j|}{N+1}\Big)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(m,x)e_{-\alpha}(j,x)dx

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(m)\Big(1-\frac{|m|}{N+1}\Big),

where

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|j|}{N+1}\Big)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(m,x)e_{-\alpha}(j,x)dx=0,\qquad m\neq j,

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|j|}{N+1}\Big)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(m,x)e_{-\alpha}(j,x)dx=1-\frac{|m|}{N+1},\qquad m=j.

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

Let
N
be a nonnegative integer. The function
{{F}}^{1,\alpha}_{N}
on
\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}
given by
(\ref{eq2.3})

The Fejér kernel

Remark 2.10
.

For all
N\geq 0
, by
(\ref{eq2.3})
, we get

\displaystyle{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|^{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n},|m_{j}|\leq N}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{|m_{j}|}{N+1}\Big)e^{2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\csc\alpha|^{n}}{(N+1)^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{\sin\big((N+1)\pi x_{j}\csc\alpha\big)}{\sin(\pi x_{j}\csc\alpha)}\Big)^{2}.

Proposition 2.11
.

The family of fractional Fejér kernels
\{{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}\}_{N=0}^{\infty}
is an approximate identity on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|{F}^{n,\alpha}_{N}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}=\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}{F}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(x)dx=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}{F}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x_{j})dx_{j}=1.

By noting that
1\leq{|t|}{/|\sin t|}\leq{\pi}{/2}
when
|t|\leq{\pi}{/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{F}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\csc\alpha|}{N+1}\min\Big(\frac{(N+1)|\pi x\csc\alpha|}{|\sin(\pi x\csc\alpha)|},\frac{1}{|\sin(\pi x\csc\alpha)|}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\csc\alpha|}{N+1}\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\min\Big(N+1,\frac{1}{|\pi x\csc\alpha|}\Big)^{2},

where
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2|\csc\alpha|}
. For
\delta>0
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\delta\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2|\csc\alpha|}}{F}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x)dx\leq\frac{|\csc\alpha|}{N+1}\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\int_{\delta\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2|\csc\alpha|}}\frac{1}{|\pi\delta\csc\alpha|^{2}}dx\leq\frac{1}{4\delta^{2}}\frac{1}{N+1}\rightarrow 0

as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

In higher dimensions, given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in[-{|\sin\alpha|}/{2},{|\sin\alpha|}/{2}]^{n}
with
|x|\geq\delta,
there exists a
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

|x_{j}|\geq\delta/\sqrt{n}
and thus

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq\delta}{F}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(x)dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|x_{j}|\geq\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{n}}}{F}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x_{j})dx_{j}\prod_{k\neq j}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}{F}^{1,\alpha}_{N}(x_{k})dx_{k}\leq\frac{n}{4\delta^{2}}\frac{1}{N+1}\rightarrow 0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\neq\pi\mathbf{Z}
.

(1) If
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}-f\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}=0.

(2) If
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
is uniformly continuous on a subset
K
of
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}-f\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}=0.

.

Proof.

We first deal with the case
1\leq p<\infty
. It is easy to check that

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N})(x)-f(x)=

\displaystyle e_{-\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}f(t){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(x-t)\mathrm{d}t-\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(x){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{-\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big((e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Now approximate a given
e_{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
by a continuous function with compact support to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-h)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|^{p}dx\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad h\rightarrow 0.

For given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-h)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|^{p}dx<\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{2^{p}},\quad h\in[-\delta,\delta].

Then

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N})(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=e_{\alpha}(x)\int_{|t|\leq\delta}\big((e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+e_{\alpha}(x)\int_{|t|>\delta}\big((e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)dt

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

By Minkowski’s integral inequality and (
2.6
), we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|t|>\delta}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big|\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x-t)-\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x)\big|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f\|_{p}\int_{|t|>\delta}{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)dt\rightarrow 0\qquad as\quad N\rightarrow\infty.

In addition, by
(\ref{eq2.2})
, we get

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|t|\leq\delta}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big|\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x-t)-\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x)\big|^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)dt<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

This together with (
2.8
) implies the required conclusion.

Now, we turn to conclusion
(2)
. Let
e_{\alpha}f
be a bounded function on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
that is
uniformly continuous on K. Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a neighborhood
V
of
0
such that

\displaystyle\big|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-h)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\rm{\;for\;all\;}}h\in V\;{\rm{and}}\;x\in K.

Applying this along with
(\ref{eq1.5})
, we get that as
N\to\infty

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in K}\big|(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N})(x)-f(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|t|\leq\delta}\sup_{x\in K}\big|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{|t|>\delta}\sup_{x\in K}\big|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)-(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|{{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

The set of trigonometric polynomials of order
\alpha
is dense in
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we claim that
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}
is also a trigonometric polynomial of order
\alpha
.
In fact,

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N})(x)=

\displaystyle\underset{|m_{j}|\leq N}{\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{|m_{j}|}{N+1}\Big)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

It follows from Theorem
2.12
that
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{\mathcal{F}}^{n}_{N}
converges to
f
as
N\to\infty
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

(Weierstrass approximation theorem for trigonometric polynomials of order
\alpha
) Every continuous function on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
is a uniform limit of trigonometric polynomials with order
\alpha
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
is a compact set. By Theorem
2.12
(2), we know that
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{\mathcal{F}}^{n}_{N}
converges uniformly to
f
as
N\to\infty
. Since
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{\mathcal{F}}^{n}_{N}
is a trigonometric polynomial of order
\alpha
, we conclude that every continuous function on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
can be uniformly approximated by trigonometric polynomials of order
\alpha
.
∎

3.
Reproduction of Functions from Their Fractional Fourier Coefficients

In this section, we establish the fractional Fourier inversion on
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and study basic properties of
e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Moreover, to explore the connections between fractional Fourier analysis on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and fractional Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we give the fractional Poisson summation formula.

3.1.
Fractional Fourier inversion

We now define the partial sums of fractional Fourier series.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\neq\pi\mathbf{Z}
. The expressions

(3.1)

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{F}_{N}^{n})(x)=

\displaystyle\underset{|m_{j}|\leq N}{\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{|m_{j}|}{N+1}\Big)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)

are called the fractional Fejér means (or fractional Cesàro means) of
f
.

In the following propositions, we obtain that the fractional Fourier series uniquely determine the function.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
f,g\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
satisfy
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(g)(m)
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, then
f=g
a.e.

Proof.

By linearity of
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
, we can set
g=0
. If
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)=0
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, we know from (
3.1
) that
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{F}_{N}^{n}=0
for all
N\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
. By Theorem
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}-f\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\rightarrow 0

as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\leq\big\|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}-f\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}+\big\|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\rightarrow 0,

from which we conclude that
f=0
a.e.
∎

Whether the partial sums of the fractional Fourier series converge to the function as
N\rightarrow\infty
? We give a firm answer to this question.

Corollary 3.3
.

(Fractional Fourier inversion) Suppose
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and

\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|<\infty.

Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)\quad a.e.,

and therefore
f
is almost everywhere equal to a continuous function.

Proof.

For
m\neq n
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle K_{-\alpha}(m,x)K_{\alpha}(n,x)=|\csc\alpha|^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m)e_{\alpha}(n)e_{\alpha}(n-m,x).

This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}K_{-\alpha}(m,x)K_{\alpha}(n,x)dx=0,\quad m\neq n.

Meanwhile

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}K_{-\alpha}(m,x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx=

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|^{n}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}dx=1.

Set

G(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(j)K_{-\alpha}(j,x).

It is obvious that
G\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(G)(m)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(j)K_{-\alpha}(j,x)\Big)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m).

By Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)\quad a.e.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Fractional Fourier series of square summable functions

Now consider the space
e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
with inner product

\displaystyle\langle f|g\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(t)\overline{g(t)}dt.

It is easy to check that

\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}K_{-\alpha}(m,x)\overline{K_{-\alpha}(n,x)}dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&when\;m=n,\\
0,&when\;m\neq n.\end{array}\right.

This implies that the sequence
\{K_{-\alpha}(m,\cdot)\}
is orthonormal. Meanwhile, for all
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle f|K_{-\alpha}(m,\cdot)\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(y)\overline{K_{-\alpha}(m,y)}dy=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m).

If
\langle f|K_{-\alpha}(m,\cdot)\rangle=0
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, we know from Proposition
3.2
that
f=0
a.e. Therefore, the completeness of the sequence
\{K_{-\alpha}(m,\cdot)\}
holds.

Proposition 3.4
.

The following are valid for
f,g\in e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and

(1) (Plancherel’s identity)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|^{2}.

(2) The function
f(x)
is a.e. equal to the
e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
limit of the sequence

\displaystyle\sum_{|m|\leq M}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

(3) (Parseval’s relation)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(t)\overline{g(t)}dt=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(g)(m)}.

(4) The map
f\mapsto\big\{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)\big\}
is an isometry from
e_{-\alpha}L^{2}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
onto
l^{2}
.

(5) For all
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big[e_{\alpha}(\cdot)fg\big](m)={A_{\alpha}^{n}}e_{\alpha}^{2}(m)\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(j)e^{-2\pi i(j\cdot m)\cot\alpha}{\mathcal{F}_{-\alpha}(e_{\alpha}^{2}(\cdot)g)(j-m)}.

Proof.

The proofs of (1), (2), (3) and (4) are quite similar to those in the case of Fourier series, so we omit them.
Now we turn to (5). Let
\overline{G(x)}=e_{\alpha}(x)g(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)
. From (3), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big[e_{\alpha}(fg)\big](m)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}(x)f(x)g(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}f(x)\overline{G(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(j)\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(G)(j)}.

In view of Proposition
2.4
(6), we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(G)(j)}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\overline{e_{\alpha}(x)g(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)}K_{\alpha}(j,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{A_{\alpha}^{n}}e_{-\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\big[\overline{e^{2}_{\alpha}(x)g(x)}e^{2\pi imx\csc\alpha}\big]K_{\alpha}(j,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{A_{\alpha}^{n}}e_{-\alpha}(m)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(\overline{e_{\alpha}^{2}g}e_{-\alpha}(m,\cdot)\big)(j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{A_{\alpha}^{n}}e_{-\alpha}^{2}(m)e^{2\pi i(j\cdot m)\cot\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(\overline{e^{2}_{\alpha}g}\big)(j-m).

By Proposition
2.4
(3), we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(G)(j)}=

\displaystyle{A_{\alpha}^{n}}e^{2}_{\alpha}(m)e^{-2\pi i(j\cdot m)\cot\alpha}\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(\overline{e^{2}_{\alpha}g}\big)(j-m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{A_{\alpha}^{n}}e^{2}_{\alpha}(m)e^{-2\pi i(j\cdot m)\cot\alpha}{\mathcal{F}_{-\alpha}\big(e^{2}_{\alpha}g\big)(j-m)}.

∎

3.3.
The fractional Poisson summation formula

In this subsection, we establish an important connection between fractional Fourier analysis on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(Fractional Poisson summation formula)
Suppose that
f
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If there exist
C,\delta>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{n+\delta}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and the fractional Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(e_{-\alpha}f)
restricted on
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\big|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(e_{-\alpha}f\big)(m)\big|<\infty.

Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(e_{-\alpha}f\big)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)=e_{-\alpha}(x)\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}f(x+k|\sin\alpha|),

and in particular

\displaystyle A_{-\alpha}^{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\big(e_{-\alpha}f\big)(m)e_{-\alpha}(m)=\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}f(k|\sin\alpha|).

Proof.

Suppose

F(x)=e_{-\alpha}(x)\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}f(x+k|\sin\alpha|).

It is easy to check that
F\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. We show that the sequence of the fractional Fourier coefficients of
F
coincides with the restriction of the fractional Fourier transform of
f
on
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
. In fact, for any
k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle|k|\sin\alpha|+x|\geq|k\sin\alpha|-|x|\geq|k\sin\alpha|-\frac{|\sin\alpha|}{2}\sqrt{n}

for all
x\in[-|\sin\alpha|/2,|\sin\alpha|/2]^{n}
. This together with (
3.3
) implies

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\big|f(x+k|\sin\alpha|)\big|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\frac{C_{n,\delta}}{(1+|k\sin\alpha|)^{n+\delta}}<\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(F)(m)=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}f(x+k|\sin\alpha|)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}f(x+k|\sin\alpha|)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\sum_{k\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}+k|\sin\alpha|}e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(e_{-\alpha}f)(m),

where the second equality follows from (
3.6
). Meanwhile, we also obtain that
F
is continuous. It follows that (
3.4
) holds with
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(F)|
in place of
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(e_{-\alpha}f)|
. By Corollary
3.3
, we obtain that (
3.5
) holds for
x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and then by periodicity, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

4.
Decay of Fractional Fourier coefficient

In this section, we study the decay of the fractional Fourier coefficients associated with the smoothness of a function.

4.1.
Decay of fractional Fourier coefficients of integrable function

Proposition 4.1
.

(Riemann-Lebesgue lemma)
Suppose
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\to 0\quad as\quad|m|\to\infty.

Proof.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P_{\alpha}
such that
\|f-P_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}}<\varepsilon
. If
|m|>\text{degree}(P_{\alpha})
, then we have
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)=0
. Hence

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|=|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)-\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)|\leq\|f-P_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}}<\varepsilon.

This implies that
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\to 0
as
|m|\to\infty
.
∎

For
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we claim that
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|
may tend to zero arbitrarily slowly. More precise, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(d_{m})_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers with
d_{m}\to 0
as
|m|\to\infty
. Then there exists a function
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
such that
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\geq d_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
. In other words, given any rate of decay, there exists an integrable function on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
whose absolute value of fractional Fourier coefficients have slower rate of decay.

Proof.

We first deal with
n=1
. Let
\{a_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}
be a sequence of positive numbers that converges to zero as
|m|\to\infty
. Apply Lemma 3.3.2 in
[
13
]
to the sequence
\{a_{m}+a_{-m}\}_{m\geq 0}
to find a convex sequence
\{c_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
that dominates
\{a_{m}+a_{-m}\}_{m\geq 0}
and decreases to zero as
|m|\to\infty
. Extend
c_{m}
for
m<0
by taking
c_{m}=c_{|m|}
. Set

(4.1)

\displaystyle f(x)=e_{-\alpha}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)(c_{j}+c_{j+2}-2c_{j+1})F_{j}^{1,\alpha}(x).

Using Lemma 3.3.3 in
[
13
]
with
s=0
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)(c_{j}+c_{j+2}-2c_{j+1})\|F_{j}^{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})}=c_{0}<\infty,

since
\|F_{j}^{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})}=1
for all
j
. Therefore, we obtain that
f\in{e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})}
by noting that the series in (
4.1
) converges in
{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})}
. For
m\in\mathbf{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)(c_{j}+c_{j+2}-2c_{j+1})\big|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(e_{-\alpha}F_{j}^{1,\alpha})(m)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=|m|}^{\infty}(j+1)(c_{j}+c_{j+2}-2c_{j+1})\Big|A_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(m)\Big(1-\frac{|m|}{j+1}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|^{1/2}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(r+1)(c_{r+|m|}+c_{r+|m|+2}-2c_{r+|m|+1})

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\csc\alpha|^{1/2}c_{|m|}=|\csc\alpha|^{1/2}c_{m},

where the second equality follows from Proposition
2.8
and the third equality is due to Lemma 3.3.3 in
[
13
]
with
s=|m|
.

Next, we turn to
n\geq 2
. Let
(d_{m})_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers with
d_{m}\to 0
as
|m|\to\infty
. There exists a positive sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}
such that
a_{m_{1}}\cdots a_{m_{n}}\geq d_{({m_{1}},\ldots,{m_{n}})}
and
a_{j}\to 0
as
|j|\to\infty
. Set

\mathbf{f}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{1})\cdots f(x_{n}),

where
f
is defined as in (
4.1
) such that
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\geq a_{m}
. This together with (
2.1
) implies
|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{f})(m)|\geq d_{m}
.
∎

4.2.
Decay of Fractional Fourier coefficients of smooth functions

In this subsection, we are devoted to studying the relationship between the decay of fractional Fourier coefficients and the smoothness of a function.

Definition 4.3
.

For
0<\gamma<1
, the homogeneous Lipschitz space of order
\gamma
on
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
is defined by

\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})=\{f:\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}\to C\;with\;\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}:=\underset{h\neq 0}{\sup_{x,h\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|}{|h|^{\gamma}}.

Next, we discuss the decay of fractional Fourier coefficients of Lipschitz functions.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
s\in\mathbf{Z}
and
s\geq 0
.

(a) Suppose that
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{s}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Then

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi|\csc\alpha|}\Big)^{s}\frac{\max\limits_{|\beta|=s}{|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)](m)|}}{|m|^{s}}

and thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|(1+|m|^{s})\to 0

as
|m|\to\infty
.

(b) Suppose that
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{s}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and whenever
|\beta|=s
,
\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)
are in
\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
for some
0<\gamma<1
. Then

(4.5)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq\frac{(\sqrt{n})^{s+\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{s}|\csc\alpha|^{s+\gamma+n-1}2^{\gamma+1}}\frac{\max\limits_{|\beta|=s}{\|\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}}}{|m|^{s+\gamma}},\qquad m\neq 0.

Proof.

For fixed
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, there exists a
j
such that
|m_{j}|=\sup_{1\leq k\leq n}|m_{k}|
. Therefore,
|m|\leq\sqrt{n}|m_{j}|
. For
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{s}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, integrating by parts
s
times with respect to the variable
x_{j}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\frac{e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}}{(2\pi im_{j}\csc\alpha)^{s}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi im_{j}\csc\alpha)^{s}}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}e_{-\alpha}(x)\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

(4.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi im_{j}\csc\alpha)^{s}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)](m).

Then

|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi|\csc\alpha|}\Big)^{s}\frac{\max\limits_{|\beta|=s}{|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}[e_{-\alpha}\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)](m)|}}{|m|^{s}}.

We now turn to conclusion (b). Let
e_{j}=(0,...,|\sin\alpha|,...,0)
be the element of the torus
\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
whose
j
th coordinate is
|\sin\alpha|
and all the others are zero. By noting that
e^{\pi i}=e^{-\pi i}=-1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx=-\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\Big(x-\frac{e_{j}}{2m_{j}}\Big)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial_{j}^{s}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\Big[\partial_{j}^{s}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x)-\partial_{j}^{s}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)\Big(x-\frac{e_{j}}{2m_{j}}\Big)\Big]e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx.

Notice that

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)(t)-\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\Big(t-\frac{e_{j}}{2m_{j}}\Big)\Big|\leq\frac{\|\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}}{(2|m_{j}\csc\alpha|)^{\gamma}}.

This combined with (
4.2
) leads to

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)\big|=

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{A_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)}{(2\pi im_{j}\csc\alpha)^{s}}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\partial_{j}^{s}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)(x)e^{-2\pi im\cdot x\csc\alpha}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi|\csc\alpha||m|}\Big)^{s}\frac{|A_{\alpha}^{n}|}{2|\csc\alpha|^{n}}\frac{\|\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}}{(2|m_{j}\csc\alpha|)^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi|\csc\alpha||m|}\Big)^{s}\frac{1}{2|\csc\alpha|^{n/2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2|\csc\alpha||m|}\Big)^{\gamma}{\|\partial_{j}^{s}\big(e_{\alpha}f\big)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{n})^{s+\gamma}}{(2\pi)^{s}|\csc\alpha|^{s+\gamma+n/2}2^{\gamma+1}}\frac{\max\limits_{|\beta|=s}{\|\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}}}{|m|^{s+\gamma}},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary is a consequence of Theorem
4.4
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
s\in\mathbf{Z}
with
s\geq 0
.

(a) Suppose that
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{s}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq c_{n,s,\alpha}\frac{\max(\|f\|_{1},\max_{|\beta|=s}{\|\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{1}})}{(1+|m|)^{s}}

(b) Suppose that
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{s}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and whenever
|\beta|=s
,
\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)
are in
\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
for some
0<\gamma<1
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq

\displaystyle c_{n,s,\alpha}\frac{\max(\|f\|_{1},\max_{|\beta|=s}{\|\partial_{j}^{s}(e_{\alpha}f)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}})}{(1+|m|)^{s+\gamma}}.

The following proposition provides a partial converse to Theorem
4.4
. We
denote below by
[[s]]
the largest integer strictly less than a given real number s. Then
[[s]]
is equal to the integer part
[s]
of
s
, unless
s
is an integer, in which case
[[s]]=[s]-1
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
s>0
. If
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and satisfies

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\leq C(1+|m|)^{-s-n}

for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
. Then
e_{\alpha}f
has partial derivatives of all orders
|\beta|\leq[[s]]
. Moreover,
\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)\in{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}
for all multi-indices
\beta
satisfying
|\beta|=[[s]]
with
0<\gamma<s-[[s]]
.

Proof.

Using (
4.7
) and Corollary
3.3
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)

for almost all
x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. Let
f_{m}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)
. If a series
g=\sum_{m}g_{m}
satisfies

\sum_{m}\|\partial^{\beta}g_{m}\|_{\infty}<\infty
for all
|\beta|\leq M
, we obtain
g\in C^{M}
and
\partial^{\beta}g=\sum_{m}\partial^{\beta}g_{m}
. Then, we need to check

\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f_{m})(x)|<\infty.

Notice that

\displaystyle\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f_{m})(x)=

\displaystyle A_{-\alpha}^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}e^{2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}.

For
|\beta|\leq[[s]]
, by
(\ref{eq3.11})
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\big|\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f_{m})(x)\big|\leq

\displaystyle|A_{-\alpha}^{n}|\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|\sup_{x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}|(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}e_{-\alpha}(m,x)|<\infty.

Hence
e_{\alpha}f\in C^{[[s]]}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, and

\displaystyle\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)=

\displaystyle e_{\alpha}(x)\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

For
|\beta|=[[s]]
and
0<\gamma<s-[[s]]

\displaystyle|e^{2\pi i(m\cdot h)\csc\alpha}-1|\leq\min(2,2\pi|m||h||\csc\alpha|)\leq 2^{1-\gamma}(2\pi)^{\gamma}|m|^{\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}|\csc\alpha|^{\gamma}.

This together with (

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)(x+h)-\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)(x)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|A_{-\alpha}^{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e_{-\alpha}(m)e_{-\alpha}(m,x)\big(e^{2\pi i(m\cdot h)\csc\alpha}-1\big)(2\pi im\csc\alpha)^{\beta}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\frac{C|\csc\alpha|^{n/2}}{(1+|m|)^{s+n}}(2\pi)^{[[s]]}|m|^{[[s]]}|\csc\alpha|^{[[s]]}\big|e^{2\pi i(m\cdot h)\csc\alpha}-1\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\frac{C|\csc\alpha|^{n/2}}{(1+|m|)^{s+n}}(2\pi)^{[[s]]}|m|^{[[s]]}|\csc\alpha|^{[[s]]}2^{1-\gamma}(2\pi)^{\gamma}|m|^{\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}|\csc\alpha|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}(2\pi)^{[[s]]+\gamma}|\csc\alpha|^{[[s]]+n/2+\gamma}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\frac{C|m|^{[[s]]+\gamma}}{(1+|m|)^{s+n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}(2\pi)^{s}|\csc\alpha|^{[[s]]+n/2+\gamma}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\frac{C|m|^{[[s]]+\gamma}}{(1+|m|)^{s+n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{1-\gamma}(2\pi)^{s}|\csc\alpha|^{[[s]]+n/2+\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}.

Therefore,
\partial^{\beta}(e_{\alpha}f)\in{\dot{\Lambda}_{\gamma}}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

In this section, we will discuss the pointwise convergence of fractional Fejér means. For the convergence of other means, such as Bochner-Riesz means, one can refer to

5.1.
Pointwise Convergence of the fractional Fejér Means

Using properties of the fractional Fejér kernel, we obtain the following 1-dimensional result regarding the convergence of the fractional Fejér means:

Theorem 5.1
.

If
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha})
has both left and right limits at a point
x_{0}
, denoted by
f(x_{0-})
and
f(x_{0+})
, respectively, then

(5.1)

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{1}_{N})(x_{0})\to\frac{1}{2}\big(f(x_{0+})+f(x_{0-})\big)\quad as\quad N\to\infty.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}=[-{|\sin\alpha|}/{2},{|\sin\alpha|}/{2}]
. For fixed
\varepsilon>0
, take
\delta\in(0,{|\sin\alpha|}/{2})
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}+t)+(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}-t)}{2}-\frac{e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0+})+e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0-})}{2}\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

where
0<t<\delta
. Follows form Proposition
2.11
, there exists
N>N_{0}
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\delta\leq t\leq{|\sin\alpha|/2}}{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(t)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}_{N}^{1})(x_{0})-f(x_{0+})=e_{-\alpha}(x_{0})\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}\big[(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}+t)-e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0+})\big]{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(t)dt,

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}_{N}^{1})(x_{0})-f(x_{0-})=e_{-\alpha}(x_{0})\int_{\mathbf{T}^{1}_{\alpha}}\big[(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}-t)-e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0-})\big]{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(t)dt.

Using the fact that the integrand is even, we have

\displaystyle(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}_{N}^{1})(x_{0})-\frac{f(x_{0+})+f(x_{0-})}{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\frac{|\sin\alpha|}{2}}\Big[\frac{(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}+t)+(e_{\alpha}f)(x_{0}-t)}{2}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0+})+e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0-})}{2}\Big]{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(t)dt.

We divided the integral in (
5.1
) into two parts, the integral over
[0,\delta)
and the integral over
(\delta,{|\sin\alpha|}/{2})
, which are denoted by
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
, respectively. Using (
5.2
), we get
I_{3}\leq\varepsilon
. For
N\geq N_{0}
, by (
5.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}\leq\varepsilon\big(\|e_{\alpha}f-e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0_{+}})\|_{L^{1}}+\|e_{\alpha}f-e_{\alpha}(x_{0})f(x_{0_{-}})\|_{L^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle=:\varepsilon c(f,x_{0},\alpha),

where
c(f,x_{0},\alpha)
is a constant depending on
f
,
x_{0}
and
\alpha
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Almost everywhere convergence of the fractional Fejér Means

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
.

(a) Let

\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}f:=\sup_{N\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}}|f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}|.

Then
\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}
maps
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
for
1<p\leq\infty
.

(b) For any function
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we get that as
N\to\infty

\displaystyle f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}\to f\quad a.e.

Proof.

Using the fact that
1\leq\frac{|t|}{|\sin t|}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
when
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(x)|=

\displaystyle\frac{|\csc\alpha|}{N+1}\Big|\frac{\sin\big((N+1)\pi x\csc\alpha\big)}{\sin(\pi x\csc\alpha)}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(N+1)|\csc\alpha|}{4}\Big|\frac{\sin\big((N+1)\pi x\csc\alpha\big)}{(N+1)x\csc\alpha}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(N+1)|\csc\alpha|}{4}\min\Big\{\pi^{2},\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2}|x\csc\alpha|^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\frac{(N+1)|\csc\alpha|}{1+(N+1)^{2}|x|^{2}}.

where
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2|\csc\alpha|}
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\varphi(t)=(1+t^{2})^{-1}
and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi(t/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon>0
. For
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\varepsilon>0
, let

\Phi(x)=\varphi(x_{1})\cdots\varphi(x_{n})

and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)={\varepsilon^{-n}}\Phi(t/\varepsilon)
. Therefore, for
|x|\leq{|\sin\alpha|/2}
we get
|{{F}}_{N}^{1,\alpha}(x)|\leq{\pi^{2}}|\csc\alpha|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)
with
\varepsilon=(N+1)^{-1}
. Meanwhile, for
y\in[-|\sin\alpha|/2,{|\sin\alpha|/2}]^{n}
, we have

|{{F}}_{N}^{n,\alpha}(y)|\leq\pi^{2n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(y),

where
\varepsilon=(N+1)^{-1}
.

Now let
e_{\alpha}f
be an integrable function on
{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}
and let
f_{0}
denote its periodic extension on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
x\in[-|\sin\alpha|/2,{|\sin\alpha|/2}]^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}f(x)=

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}}\Big|e_{-\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}(e_{\alpha}f)(x-t){{F}}^{n,\alpha}_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi^{2n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}|(e_{\alpha}f)(x-t)|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 10^{n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}{|f_{0}(x-t)|}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 10^{n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{|(f_{0}\chi_{Q})(x-t)|}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:10^{n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\mathfrak{F}(f_{0}\chi_{Q})(x),

where
Q
is the cube
[-|\sin\alpha|,|\sin\alpha|]^{n}
and
\mathfrak{F}
is the operator on the set of integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined by

\mathfrak{F}(h)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}|h|\ast\Phi_{\varepsilon}.

By Lemma 3.4.5 in
[
13
]
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\leq 10^{n}|\csc\alpha|^{n}\|\mathfrak{F}(f_{0}\chi_{Q})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,\alpha}\|f_{0}\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}.

Meanwhile, it is easy to see

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\leq C_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}.

By the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, we obtain the
L^{p}
conclusion.

Now we turn to conclusion (b). Since the sequence
\{{{F}}_{N}^{n,\alpha}\}_{N=0}^{\infty}
is an approximate identity and
e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}})
is dense in
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}})
, we have

f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}\to f,\quad f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}),

uniformly on
{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}
as
N\to\infty
. Since
\mathcal{{H}}_{\alpha}
maps
e_{-\alpha}L^{1}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}})
and using Theorem 2.1.14 in
[
13
]
, we obtain that for
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{1}({\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}})
,
f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{{F}}^{n}_{N}\to f
a.e.
∎

5.3.
Norm convergence of the fractional Bochner-Riesz Means

Theorem 5.3
.

For
R>0
and
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, let
a(m,R)
be complex numbers such that

(1) For every
R>0
there is a
q_{R}
such that
a(m,R)=0
if
|m|>q_{R}
;

(2) There is an
M_{0}<\infty
such that
|a(m,R)|\leq M_{0}
for all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
and all
R>0
;

(3) For all
m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, the limit of
a(m,R)
exists as
R\to\infty
and
lim_{R\to\infty}a(m,R)=a_{m}
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. For
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
and
x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
, define

\displaystyle S_{R}f(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}a(m,R)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

For
h\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, define

\displaystyle Ah(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

Then for all
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
the sequence
S_{R}f
converges in
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
as
R\to\infty
if and only if there exists a constant
K<\infty
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{R>0}\|S_{R}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq K.

Furthermore, if (
5.5
) holds, then for the same constant
K
and
h\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\neq 0}\|Ah\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq K,

and the
A
extends to a bounded operator
\widetilde{A}
from
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
; moreover, for every
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
we have
S_{R}f\to\widetilde{A}f
in
L^{p}
as
R\to\infty
.

Proof.

If
S_{R}f
converges to in
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, then
\|S_{R}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{f}
, where constant
C_{f}
depends on
f
. Moreover, each
S_{R}f
is a bounded operator from

e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
to
L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
with norm at most
\#\{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}:|m|\leq q_{R}\}M_{0}
. Therefore, we obtain that
\{S_{R}\}_{R>0}
is a family of
L^{p}
bounded linear operators that satisfy
\sup_{R>0}\|S_{R}\|_{L^{p}(e_{-a}\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})\to L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})}\leq C_{f}
for each
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. It follows form the uniform boundedness theorem that the operator norms of
S_{R}
are bounded uniformly in
R
. This proves
(\ref{eq5.5})
.

Conversely, assume
(\ref{eq5.5})
. For
h\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, by property (ii) and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}a(m,R)\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

Fatou’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\|Ah\|_{L^{p}}=\|\lim_{R\to\infty}S_{R}h\|_{L^{p}}\leq K\|h\|_{L^{p}},

hence (
5.6
) holds. Thus
A
extends to a bounded operator
\widetilde{A}
on
e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
by density.

For
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we need to prove
S_{R}f\to\widetilde{A}f
in
L^{p}
as
R\to\infty
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P_{\alpha}
such that
\|f-P_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon
. Notice that
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)=0
for
|m|>\text{degree}(P_{\alpha})
. Then there is an
R_{0}>0
such that for all
R>R_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|m_{1}|+\cdots+|m_{n}|\leq\text{degree}(P_{\alpha})}|a(m,R)-a_{m}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)\leq\varepsilon.

For
R>R_{0}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|S_{R}P_{\alpha}-AP_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{R}P_{\alpha}-AP_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|m_{1}|+\cdots+|m_{n}|\leq\text{degree}(P_{\alpha})}|a(m,R)-a_{m}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(P_{\alpha})(m)\lesssim\varepsilon.

Then

\displaystyle\|S_{R}f-\widetilde{A}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{R}f-S_{R}P_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}}+\|S_{R}P_{\alpha}-\widetilde{A}P_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\widetilde{A}P_{\alpha}-\widetilde{A}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2K+1)\varepsilon

for
R>R_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

The Bochner-Riesz means of order
\gamma\geq 0
is the operator

\displaystyle B^{\gamma}_{R}(f)(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n},|m|\leq R}\Big(1-\frac{|m|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

In fact, suppose
a(m,R)=\Big(1-\frac{|m|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}
for
|m|\leq R
. It is easy to check that the sequence
\Big(1-\frac{|m|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)^{\gamma}
satisfy the properties in Theorem
5.3
. Hence, we have following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\gamma\geq 0
. For
f\in e_{-\alpha}L^{p}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\|B^{\gamma}_{R}(f)-f\|_{L^{p}}=0\Longleftrightarrow\sup_{R>0}\|B^{\gamma}_{R}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}.

Applications to Partial Differential Equations

It is well known that Fourier theory play an important role in the solution of differential equations, see

,

,

,

,

]
and the references therein for more details. In this section, we apply the fractional Fourier series to get solutions of the fractional heat equation and fractional Dirichlet problem.

Definition 6.1
.

For fixed
k>0
, define the fractional heat kernel of order
\alpha
:

H_{t}^{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}e^{-4\pi^{2}|m|^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}e^{2\pi im\cdot x},

where
t>0
. Note that
H_{t}^{\alpha}
is absolutely convergent for any
t>0
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
k>0
be fixed and
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Then the fractional heat equation of order
\alpha

(6.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)=k\triangle_{x}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big),\quad t\in(0,\infty),x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha},

under the initial condition

(6.2)

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(x,0)=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha},

has a unique solution which is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and
C^{\infty}
on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
given by

(6.3)

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(x,t)=(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{H_{t}^{\alpha}})(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-4\pi^{2}|m|^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

Proof.

For
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
, it is easy to see that the series in (
6.3
) is rapidly convergent in
m
and we obtain that
F_{\alpha}(x,t)
is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. In addition, the series can be differentiated term by term with
t>0
, and thus it produces a
C^{\infty}
function on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. By Corollary
3.3
,
F_{\alpha}
satisfies the initial condition. Next, it remains to check (
6.1
).

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big[\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-4\pi^{2}|m|^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}A_{-\alpha}^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m)e_{-\alpha}(m,x)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\Big[\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-4\pi^{2}m^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}A_{-\alpha}^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}^{2}}}e_{-\alpha}(m,x)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}^{2}}}\Big[\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-4\pi^{2}m^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}A_{-\alpha}^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m)e_{-\alpha}(m,x)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\triangle_{x}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big),

where the last equality follows from the convergence of the series.

Finally, assume that there is another solution
G_{\alpha}(x,t)
, which is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}
and
C^{\infty}
on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}
. Then
G_{\alpha}(x,t)
can be expanded in fractional Fourier series as follows:

G_{\alpha}(x,t):=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}c_{m}^{\alpha}(t)K_{-\alpha}(m,x),

where

c_{m}^{\alpha}(t)=\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}G_{\alpha}(y,t)K_{\alpha}(m,y)dy.

Then
c_{m}^{\alpha}(t)
is a smooth function on
(0,\infty)
since
G_{\alpha}
is
C^{\infty}
on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}
.
By equation (
6.1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{c}_{m}^{\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)G_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)e_{-\alpha}(x)K_{\alpha}(m,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kA_{\alpha}^{n}e_{\alpha}(m)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}^{2}}}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)G_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)e^{-2\pi i(m\cdot x)\csc\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi^{2}|m|^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}k{c}_{m}^{\alpha}(t),

where the last equality follows from an integration by parts in which the boundary terms cancel each other in view of the periodicity of the integrand in
x
.
In addition,
{c}_{m}^{\alpha}(0)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)
. Therefore, we get

{c}_{m}^{\alpha}(t)=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-4\pi^{2}|m|^{2}|\csc\alpha|^{2}kt}.

Thus
G_{\alpha}=F_{\alpha}
on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
f\in e_{-\alpha}C^{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha})
. Then the fractional Dirichlet problem of order
\alpha

(6.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}}^{2}}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)=0;\;t\in(0,\infty),x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha},\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;F_{\alpha}(x,0)=f(x);\quad x\in\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha},\end{cases}

has a solution which is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and
C^{\infty}
on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
given by

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(x,t)=(f\overset{\alpha}{\ast}{P_{t}^{\alpha}})(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-2\pi|m|{|\csc\alpha|}t}K_{-\alpha}(m,x).

Proof.

It is easy to see that
F_{\alpha}(x,t)
is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
and
C^{\infty}
on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\alpha}
. By Corollary
3.3
,
F_{\alpha}
satisfies the initial condition. It suffices to verify (
6.4
). Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-2\pi|m|{|\csc\alpha|}t}A_{-\alpha}^{n}e_{-\alpha}(m^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}^{2}}}e_{\alpha}(m,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x_{j}^{2}}}\Big[e_{\alpha}(x^{2})\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)e^{-2\pi|m|{|\csc\alpha|}t}K_{-\alpha}(m,x)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\triangle_{x}\Big(e_{\alpha}(x)F_{\alpha}(x,t)\Big).

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Applications to non-stationary signals

In this section, we will demonstrate the use of Corollary
3.3
and Theorem
5.2
in the recovery of non-stationary signals. For example, suppose

g(x)=\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x,&-\frac{1}{4}<x\leq\frac{1}{4},\\
\frac{1}{4},&x=-\frac{1}{4},\end{array}\right.

and
g(x+1/4)=g(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. By taking
\alpha=\pi/6
, we have

f(x)=e^{-\pi ix^{2}\cot\pi/6}g(x)=\begin{cases}xe^{-\sqrt{3}\pi ix^{2}},&-\frac{1}{4}<x\leq\frac{1}{4},\\
\frac{1}{4}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{16}\pi i},&x=-\frac{1}{4}.\end{cases}

It is easy to see
f\in e_{-\pi/6}L^{1}(\mathbf{T}^{1}_{-\pi/6})
. Note that

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cc}\displaystyle iA_{\alpha}^{1}e^{\sqrt{3}\pi im^{2}}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{8\pi m},&m\neq 0,\\
\displaystyle 0,&m=0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the series
\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}|\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(f)(m)|
is not convergent and we can not recover the signal
f(x)
by fractional Fourier inversion. Meanwhile, the approximating method (Theorem
5.2
) can give another way to recover the non-stationary signal
f(x)
. In fact, for
N\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle(f\overset{\pi/6}{\ast}{F}_{N}^{1})(x)=

\displaystyle ie^{-\sqrt{3}\pi ix^{2}}\sum_{m=-N}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{|m|}{N+1}\Big)\frac{(-1)^{m}}{4\pi m}e^{4\pi imx}.

We prove that the number of terms contributing a nonzero value to the multiplicity of the positive root μ = α i + α i + 1 + ⋯ + α j \mu=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j} with 1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ r 1\leq i\leq j\leq r in L ⁡ ( α ~ ) L(\tilde{\alpha}) is given by the product F i ⋅ F r − j + 1 F_{i}\cdot F_{r-j+1} , where F n F_{n} is the n th n^{\text{th}} Fibonacci number. Using this result, we show that the q q -multiplicity of the positive root μ = α i + α i + 1 + ⋯ + α j \mu=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j} with 1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ r 1\leq i\leq j\leq r in the representation L ⁡ ( α ~ ) L(\tilde{\alpha}) is precisely q r − h ⁡ ( μ ) q^{r-h(\mu)} , where h ⁡ ( μ ) = j − i + 1 h(\mu)=j-i+1 is the height of the positive root μ \mu . Setting q = 1 q=1 recovers the known result that the multiplicity of a positive root in the adjoint representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 r + 1 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C}) is one.

Introduction

For
r\geq 1
, the type
A_{r}
Lie algebra, also known as the special linear algebra, is denoted
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})=\{X\in M_{r+1}(\mathbb{C}):\text{Tr}(X)=0\}
. We recall that
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})

is a complex vector space with a bilinear product called the Lie bracket, defined by the commutator bracket
[X,Y]=XY-YX
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
, which is skew-symmetric and satisfies the Jacobi identity.
Given
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{r+1}
, the standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{r+1}
, for each
i\in[r]
, we define

\alpha_{i}=e_{i}-e_{i+1}
.
We let
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
be the set of simple roots,
\Phi^{+}=\Delta\cup\{\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq r\}
be the set of positive roots, and
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.

We recall that the Weyl group
W
is the group generated by reflections, denoted as
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}
, through hyperplanes which are orthogonal to the simple roots
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
, respectively.
The Weyl group of type
A_{r}
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{r+1}
.
If
\sigma\in W
, then
\ell(\sigma)=k
is the length of
\sigma
, which means that
k
is the smallest nonnegative integer such that
\sigma
can be expressed (not necessarily uniquely) as a product of
k
reflections.

One area of interest in combinatorial representation theory is the
computation involved in determining the multiplicity of a weight
\mu
in a highest weight representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})

with highest weight
\lambda
.
One way to compute this multiplicity is via Kostant’s weight multiplcity formula.
For weights
\lambda
and
\mu
, let the
Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula
to be

\displaystyle m(\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{\sigma\in W}(-1)^{\ell(\sigma)}\wp(\sigma(\lambda+\rho)-\mu-\rho)

(1)

where
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha
and

\wp(\xi)
is Kostant’s partition function,
which counts the number of ways the weight
\xi
can be written as a nonnegative integral sum of positive roots (Theorem on page 589
in
[
14
]
).

When calculating the Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula (
1
), every element in the Weyl group contributes a term to the multiplicity.
We know the order of the Weyl group increases factorially as the rank of the Lie algebra increases, and it has been observed that many of the terms contribute trivially (i.e. give a zero term) to the computation
[
5
]
.
To formulate a more efficient way to compute Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula, Harris gave the following definition.

Definition 1.1

(Definition 1.1 in
[
9
]
)
.

For
\lambda
and
\mu
integral weights of
\mathfrak{g}
, let the
Weyl alternation set
to be

\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\mu)=\{\sigma\in W~|~\wp(\sigma(\lambda+\rho)-\mu-\rho)>0\}.

(2)

This set tells us the elements of the Weyl group for which
\sigma(\lambda+\rho)-\mu-\rho
can be written as a nonnegative integral sum of positive roots.
Knowing the Weyl alternation set allows one to reduce the weight multiplicity computation by only needing to consider those Weyl group elements that contribute nonzero terms to this computation.

An additional challenge in computing weight multiplicities is that there are no general formulas for Kostant’s partition function nor for its
q
-analog.
We recall the following result of Lusztig, which defines the
q
-analog of Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula.

Definition 1.2

(Proposition 9.2 in
[
16
]
)
.

The
q
-analog of Kostant’s partition function
is the polynomial-valued function

\displaystyle\wp_{q}(\xi)=c_{0}+c_{1}q+c_{2}q^{2}+\cdots+c_{k}q^{k},

(3)

where
c_{i}
equals the number of ways to write
\xi
as a sum of exactly
i
positive roots.
Then the
q
-analog of Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula
is given by

\displaystyle m_{q}(\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{\sigma\in W}(-1)^{\ell(\sigma)}\wp_{q}(\sigma(\lambda+\rho)-\mu-\rho).

(4)

Observe that evaluating equation (
4
) at
q=1
recovers the multiplicity in equation (
1
).

Work on characterizing and enumerating the Weyl alternation sets for certain pairs of weights,
(\lambda,\mu)
, has gathered some attention in the literature.
This includes the following.
If
\lambda
and
\mu
are integral weights of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
, then the sets
\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\mu)
are known in general
[
6
,
15
]
.
Harris considers
\tilde{\alpha}
, the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
with

, and
\mu=0
and establishes the cardinality of the Weyl alternation set,
|\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},0)|
, is a Fibonacci number
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Harris also states that if
\mu
is a nonzero dominant weight,
then the Weyl alternation set is either
\{1\}
when
\mu=\tilde{\alpha}
or empty otherwise.
Chang, Harris, and Insko extend these results and show that when
\mu=0
and
\lambda
is the sum of all the simple roots of the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C})
,
the cardinality of the Weyl alternation set
\mathcal{A}(\lambda,0)
is given by a Fibonacci number
[
4
, Corollary 2.1 and 3.1]
.
While in the Lie algebras

\mathfrak{sp}_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{so}_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
, the cardinalities of the analogous Weyl alternation sets
\mathcal{A}(\lambda,0)
are given by a multiple of a Lucas number
[
4
, Corollary 4.1 and 5.1]
.
Harris, Insko, and Williams consider
\tilde{\alpha}
, the highest root of the classical Lie algebras, and
\mu=0
and show that
|\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},0)|
satisfies homogeneous linear recurrence relations
[
11
]
.
In the type
B
case, if
\mu=\varpi_{1}
denotes the first fundamental root, then
|\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\varpi_{1})|

is a Fibonacci number
[
11
, Corollary 7.4]
.
They also consider the case where
\mu
is a dominant positive root and give the multiplicity of these weights in the adjoint representation of the classical Lie algebras.
Berenshteĭn and Zelevinskiĭ
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
provide a comprehensive list of pairs of weights
(\lambda,\mu)
of the classical Lie algebras and the exceptional Lie algebra
G_{2}
for which
m(\lambda,\mu)=1
.
Harris, Rahmoeller, Schneider, and Simpson consider certain such pairs,
determine
\mathcal{A}(\lambda,\mu)
, and show that the
q
-multiplicity is a power of
q

[
12
]
.
For all pairs of weights that Berenshteĭn and Zelevinskiĭ provide,
Harris et. al conjecture that

m_{q}(\lambda,\mu)
is a power of
q

[
12
, Conjecture 7]
, and as of yet, this conjecture remains an open problem.

As noted above, past work fixed
\lambda
to be the highest root or the sum of the simple roots of a classical Lie algebra, while
\mu
was either
0
or a dominant positive root.
In this work, we focus our study on

\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
,
fix
r\geq 1
, let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root, and consider
\mu\in\Phi^{+}
.
In this way,
\mu
may be a nondominant weight.
It is known that whenever
\mu
is a root,
m(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)=1
.
Hence, when
\mu
is dominant, the pair
(\lambda,\mu)
is given by Berenshteĭn and Zelevinskiĭ
[
1
]
.
Moreover, we recall that the multiplicity is invariant under the action of the Weyl group.
Namely,
m(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)=m(\tilde{\alpha},\sigma(\mu))
for all
\sigma\in W

[
7
, Proposition 3.1.20]
.
However, when
\mu\in\Phi^{+}
is nondominant, a characterization and enumeration of the elements in
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
was unknown.
This paper contributes to this literature by proving the following results.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
and let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})

if and only if

\sigma=1
or
\sigma=s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
for some collection of nonconsecutive integers

2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1
and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
.

From the characterization in Theorem
2.1
, we are able to provide an enumeration for the cardinality of the Weyl alternation set.

Corollary 2.5
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
and let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then

|\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})|=F_{i}\cdot F_{r-j+1},

where
F_{n}
denotes the
n^{\text{th}}
Fibonacci number

[
17
,
A000045
]
.

Given the characterization and enumeration of the terms contributing nontrivially to the
q
-analog of Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula, we prove the following result using purely combinatorial techniques.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
and let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then

m_{q}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)=q^{r-h(\mu)},

where

h(\mu)=j-i+1
denotes the height of
\mu
.

Observe that setting
q=1
in Theorem
4.1
recovers the known result that the multiplicity of a
positive root in the adjoint representation is equal to one
[
7
, Theorem 2.4.1]
.

We remark that the work in
[
2
,
3
,
13
]
, together imply that when
\tilde{\alpha}
is the highest root,
\mu
is a dominant weight, and

is a Weyl group element, the jump polynomial associated to the weight

is equal to a power of

times the

-analog of Kostant’s weight multiplicity formula

.
In such a case,

, and
m_{q}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
is a power of

.
Thus, providing an alternative proof of Theorem
4.1
.
However, the proofs presented in the current are purely combinatorial.

This work is organized as follows:
In Section

2.1

In Section

Weyl Alternation Sets

In
[
4
]
, they consider the case where
\lambda
is the highest root and
\mu=0
.
We still let
\lambda
be the highest root and denote it as
\tilde{\alpha}
.
However, we let
\mu\in\Phi^{+}
.
The following result characterizes the elements in the set
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
.
The following theorem is closely related to Theorem 2.1 in
[
4
]
, and we adapt the proof techniques for our purpose.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
and let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})

if and only if

\sigma=1
or
\sigma=s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
for some collection of nonconsecutive integers

2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1
and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) We proceed by contradiction and assume
\sigma\neq 1
or
\sigma\neq s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
for some collection of nonconsecutive integers
2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1
and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
.
Then
\sigma
must contain either
s_{1}
,
s_{r}
,
s_{y}
with
i\leq y\leq j
, or some product of
s_{n}
and
s_{n+1}
for some integer
1\leq n\leq r
.

Let
\sigma=s_{1}
.
Then

\displaystyle\wp(s_{1}(\tilde{\alpha}+\rho)-\rho-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp(\tilde{\alpha}-\alpha_{1}+\rho-\alpha_{1}-\rho-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp(\tilde{\alpha}-2\alpha_{1}-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp(-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{i-1}+\alpha_{j+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle=0

since
-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{i-1}+\alpha_{j+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}
cannot be written as a sum of positive roots since the coefficient for
\alpha_{1}
is negative.
Thus, we get a contradiction, and
s_{1}\notin\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
.
By
[
11
, Proposition 3.4]
, if
\sigma\notin\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
, then neither is any
\sigma^{\prime}\in W
that contains
\sigma
in its reduced word expression.
Thus,
\sigma\in W
containing
s_{1}
in its reduced word expression cannot be in
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
.
This reaches a contradiction to the assumption that
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
.

A similar argument is made for when
\sigma
contains either
s_{r}
,
s_{y}
with
i\leq y\leq j
, or
some product of
s_{n}
and
s_{n+1}
for some integer
1\leq n\leq r
.

We have shown that if
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
, then
\sigma
cannot contain
s_{1}
,
s_{r}
,
s_{k}
with
i\leq k\leq j
, nor any product of consecutive
s_{n}
and
s_{n+1}
with
1\leq n\leq r-1
.
Thus, the only remaining possibilities are that
\sigma=1
or that
\sigma=s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
for some collection of nonconsecutive integers

2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1
and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
as desired.

(
\Leftarrow
) It suffices to consider the following two cases.

Case 1
: If
\sigma=1
, then

\displaystyle\wp(1(\tilde{\alpha}+\rho)-\rho-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp(\tilde{\alpha}-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i-1}+\alpha_{j+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle>0

since
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i-1}+\alpha_{j+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}
can be written as a sum of positive roots.
Thus,

\sigma=1\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
.

Case 2
: Let
\sigma=s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
where for some collection of nonconsecutive integers

2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1

and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
.
Then

\displaystyle\wp(s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}

\displaystyle(\tilde{\alpha}+\rho)-\rho-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}))

\displaystyle=\wp\left(\tilde{\alpha}+\rho-\sum_{h=1}^{t}\alpha_{\ell_{h}}-\rho-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\wp\left(\tilde{\alpha}-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})-\sum_{h=1}^{t}\alpha_{\ell_{h}}\right).

(5)

Notice that

\{i,i+1,\ldots,j\}\cap\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{t}\}=\emptyset
.
If
x\in\{i,i+1,\ldots,j\}\cup\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{t}\}
, then in equation (
5
), the coefficient of
\alpha_{x}
is exactly 0.
If
x\notin\{i,i+1,\ldots,j\}\cup\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{t}\}
, then in equation (
5
), the coefficient of
\alpha_{x}
is exactly 1.
Hence,
\tilde{\alpha}-(\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})-\sum_{h=1}^{t}\alpha_{\ell_{h}}
, can be written as a sum of positive roots.
Therefore,
s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
.
∎

Having characterized the elements of
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})
for all
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
, we now consider the cardinality of the set. To do so requires the following definition and enumerative result.

We begin by defining the sequence of numbers commonly referred to as Fibonacci
[
18
]
but was first introduced in the literature by Pingala
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any
n\geq 3
, define the
Fibonacci number
, denoted
F_{n}
, by the recursive formula:

F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2},

with seed values
F_{1}=F_{2}=1
.

We utilize the following enumerative result in later results.

Proposition 2.3
.

The number of ways to select a subset of
[n]:=\{1,2,3,\ldots,n\}
consisting of nonconsecutive numbers is given
F_{n+2}
, where
F_{i}
is the
i^{\text{th}}
Fibonacci number.

Proof.

We proceed by induction.
For
n=1
, the only options are to select no integers or to select
1
.
There are two such choices and note
F_{1+2}=F_{3}=2
, and more over
F_{3}=F_{2}+F_{1}
, as claimed.
Assume that for all
k\leq n
the number of ways to select a subset of
[k]
consisting of only nonconsecutive integers is given by
F_{k+2}
.
Now let
k=n+1
.
Note that for any subset of
[n-1]
consisting of nonconsecutive integers, we can include the integer
n+1
to get a subset of
[n+1]
consisting of nonconsecutive integers.
Then by induction, there are
F_{n-1+2}=F_{n+1}
many such subsets, all of which contain the integer
n+1
.
Moreover, any subset of
[n]
consisting of nonconsecutive integers is also a subset of
[n+1]
with the same property and does not contain
n+1
.
By induction hypothesis, there are
F_{n+2}
such subsets.
As these subsets are disjoint (since they either contain
n+1
or not), then we have shown that the number of subsets of
[n+1]
consisting of nonconsecutive integers is given by
F_{n+2}+F_{n+1}
, which is equal to
F_{n+3}
, as desired.
∎

In the following section, we use the enumerative result Harris gives in
[
10
]

which counts subsets of
k
nonconsecutive integers from the set
[n]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The number of ways to select
k
nonconsecutive integers from the set
[n]
is

\binom{n+1-k}{k}.

Together, Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
imply the combinatorial identity

\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{n+1-k}{k}=F_{n+2}.

We are now able to enumerate the elements of
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
for any
\mu\in\Phi^{+}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
and let
\tilde{\alpha}
be the highest root of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then

|\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})|=F_{i}\cdot F_{r-j+1},

where
F_{n}
denotes the
n^{\text{th}}
Fibonacci number.

Proof.

Let
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
.
By Theorem
2.1
, we know
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})

if and only if

\sigma=1
or
\sigma=s_{\ell_{1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{k}}s_{\ell_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\ell_{t}}
for some collection of nonconsecutive integers

2\leq\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{k}\leq i-1
and
j+1\leq\ell_{k+1},\ldots,\ell_{t}\leq r-1
.

Selecting a subset consisting of nonconsecutive integers from the set
\{2,\ldots,i-1\}

is the same as selecting a subset consisting of nonconsecutive integers from the set
\{1,\ldots,i-2\}
.
By Proposition
2.3
, there are
F_{i}
such subsets.
The same process can be done for
selecting a subset consisting of nonconsecutive integers from the set
\{j+1,\ldots,r-1\}
to get
F_{r-j+1}
such subsets.

The choices for subsets

\{2,\ldots,i-1\}
and
\{j+1,\ldots,r-1\}

are disjoint and independent of each other.
Thus, the total number of ways to construct an element of
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})

is given by the product
F_{i}\cdot F_{r-j+1}
.
Therefore,
|\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})|=F_{i}\cdot F_{r-j+1}
.
∎

The
q
-Analog of Kostant’s Partition Function

Now we are going to give formulas for the value of the
q
-analog of Kostant’s partition function when the input is
\sigma(\tilde{\alpha}+\rho)-\rho-\mu
with
\mu\in\Phi^{+}
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
.
To begin, we give some counts for the number of
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\alpha},\mu)
with a fixed length.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\mu=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}
with

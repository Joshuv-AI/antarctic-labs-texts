[
2

be an integer and
k=A(m)
, denote by

the smallest number
l
for which there are integers
b_{1},\cdots,b_{l}
such that the numbers

a_{i}+b_{j},1\leqslant i\leqslant k,1\leqslant j\leqslant l

contain every residue modulo
m

L(m)\geqslant m/k
. Ruzsa also proved in
[
2
]
that:

Theorem A.
If

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n+1}}{na_{n}}\rightarrow\infty,

then
A
has an exact complement.

Theorem B.
Let
A
be a set satisfying
\frac{A(2x)}{A(x)}\rightarrow 1
. The following are equivalent:

(a)
A
has an exact complement;

(b)
A(m)L(m)/m\rightarrow 1
;

(c) there is a sequence
m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots
of positive integers such that
A(m_{i+1})/A(m_{i})\rightarrow 1
and
A(m_{i})L(m_{i})/m_{i}\rightarrow 1
.

In 2017, Ruzsa
[
3
]
further considered exact additive complements, that is:

Theorem C.
For exact additive complements with
\frac{A(2x)}{A(x)}\rightarrow 1
, we have

\displaystyle A(x)B(x)-x\geqslant(1+o(1))\frac{a^{*}(x)}{A(x)}\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{as}\hskip 8.53581ptx\rightarrow+\infty.

In 2019, Chen and Fang

Theorem D.

There exist exact additive complements

with

such that

\displaystyle A(x)B(x)-x\leqslant(1+o(1))\frac{a^{*}(x)}{A(x)}

In this paper, under the condition (

Theorem 1.1

For exact additive complements
A,B
with (
1.1

\displaystyle A(x)B(x)-x\geqslant\frac{a^{*}(x)}{A(x)}+o\left(\frac{a^{*}(x)}{A(x)^{2}}\right)\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{as}\hskip 8.53581ptx\rightarrow+\infty.

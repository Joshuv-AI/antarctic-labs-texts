Erratum to “The combined effect in one space dimension beyond the general theory for nonlinear wave equations”
Katsuaki Morisawa, Takiko Sasaki, Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
Our previous paper has a wrong citation of the result on the general theory for nonlinear wave equations which causes a slightly different conjecture on a possibility to improve the theory, related to the title of the paper.



In Morisawa, Sasaki and Takamura
[
3
]
,
there is a wrong citation of the result on the general theory for nonlinear wave equations
in one space dimension by
[
1
,
2
]
at the last case in (2.24).
This error causes a slightly different conjecture on a possibility
to improve the general theory.
So that, the descriptions from (2.24) to (2.26) in
[
3
]
on pp. 1634-1635
should be modified as follows.

In fact, (2.24) in
[
3
]
should be replaced with

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha/2}&\mbox{in general},\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha(1+\alpha)/(2+\alpha)}&\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0,\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\min\{\beta_{0}/2,\alpha\}}&\mbox{if $\partial_{u}^{\beta}F(0)=0$
for $1+\alpha\leq\forall\beta\leq\beta_{0}$}.\end{array}\right.

If one applies it to (2.25) in
[
3
]
,
one has the following estimates in each cases.

•

When
p<q
,

then, we have to set
\alpha=p-1
and
\beta_{0}=q-1
which yield that

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/2}&\mbox{in general},\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)/(p+1)}&\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0,\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\min\{(q-1)/2,p-1\}}&\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{if $\partial_{u}^{\beta}F(0)=0$}\\
\mbox{for $p\leq\forall\beta\leq q-1$}.\end{array}\end{array}\right.

We note that the third case is available for (2.25) in
[
3
]
.
Therefore, for
p\leq(q+1)/2
, we obtain that

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq c{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}

whatever the value of
\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx
is.
On the other hand, for
(q+1)/2<p
, i.e.

\frac{q-1}{2}<p-1,

we obtain

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-(q-1)/2}&\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx\neq 0,\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\max\{(q-1)/2,p(p-1)/(p+1)\}}&\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

•

When
p\geq q
,

then, similarly to the case above, we have to set
\alpha=q-1
, which yields that

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-(q-1)/2}&\mbox{in general},\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-q(q-1)/(q+1)}&\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0,\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\min\{\beta_{0}/2,(q-1)\}}&\mbox{if $\partial_{u}^{\beta}F(0)=0$ for $q\leq\forall\beta\leq\beta_{0}$}.\end{array}\right.

We note that the third case does not hold for (2.25) in
[
3
]
by
\partial_{u}^{q}F(0)\neq 0
.

In conclusion, for the special nonlinear term in (2.25) in
[
3
]
,
the result of the general theory is

\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}&\mbox{for}\ p\leq\displaystyle\frac{q+1}{2},\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-(q-1)/2}&\mbox{for}\ \displaystyle\frac{q+1}{2}\leq p\end{array}\right.\mbox{if}\ \int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx\not=0\\

and

\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}&\mbox{for}\ p\leq\displaystyle\frac{q+1}{2},\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-\max\{(q-1)/2,p(p-1)/(p+1)\}}&\mbox{for}\ \displaystyle\frac{q+1}{2}\leq p\leq q,\\
c{\varepsilon}^{-q(q-1)/(q+1)}&\mbox{for}\ q\leq p\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0.\end{array}

Therefore a part of our results in
[
3
]
,

\begin{array}[]{l}T({\varepsilon})\sim C{\varepsilon}^{-p(q-1)/(q+1)}\\
\quad\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0\ \mbox{and}\ \frac{q+1}{2}<p<q,\end{array}

is better than the lower bound of
\widetilde{T}({\varepsilon})
.
So we have a possibility to improve the general theory at least
to include the case above.

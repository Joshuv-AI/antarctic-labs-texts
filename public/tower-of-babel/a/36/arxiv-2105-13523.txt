Synthesis and study of fcc-Co derived from isostructural Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N}
Seema, Dileep Kumar, U. P. Deshpande and Mukul Gupta

Abstract
This work demonstrates synthesis and study of fcc-Co derived from an isostructural Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} . Diffusion measurements carried out in this work, reveal that N self-diffusion is the swiftest in Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} compared to other transition metal nitrides or even the mononitride CoN. By the application of a high substrate temperature ( T s \mathrm{T_{s}} ) growth or thermal annealing temperature ( T a \mathrm{T_{a}} ); N diffuses out from the fcc- Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} above 573 K leaving behind a high purity fcc-Co phase. Generally, Co grows in a hcp structure and a (partial) hcp → \rightarrow fcc-Co transformation takes place around 700 K or above 70 GPa. The proposed route through nitridation and diffusion of N not only bring down the phase transition temperature, an impurity present in the form of hcp-Co can be avoided altogether. Oriented Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} (111) thin films were grown using a CrN(111) template on a quartz substrate using a dc magnetron sputtering. Samples were grown at different T s \mathrm{T_{s}} or room temperature grown Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} samples were annealed at different T a \mathrm{T_{a}} . Analysis using x-ray diffraction, N K-edge x-ray absorption, x-ray photoelectron and secondary ion mass spectroscopy confirmed the formation of fcc- Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} or fcc-Co phases. It was found that Co-N bonding and N concentration get significantly reduced at a high T s \mathrm{T_{s}} or T a \mathrm{T_{a}} . Magnetization measurements combining ex-situ and in-situ magneto-optical Kerr effect showed differences in anisotropy and coercivity of Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} and fcc-Co samples. Combining structural, electronic and magnetization measurements, it has been observed that a high purity fcc-Co can be conveniently derived from the isostructural Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} aided by an exceptionally high N self-diffusion in Co 4 ​ N \mathrm{Co_{4}N} .



I
Introduction

Cobalt (Co) is well-known to be formed in three allotropes namely (i) anisotropic high-coercivity (H
c
) hcp-Co (ii) pseudo-cubic bcc-Co and (iii) symmetric low H
c
fcc-Co. Generally, under ambient conditions, Co crystallizes in a hcp structure and transforms into fcc structure above 700 K or 70 GPa
Lizárraga et al. 2017
;
Matar et al. 2007
. Among the three allotropes, fcc-Co phase has been proposed to be used in soft magnetic applications involving power electronics and biomedicine. It has been reported that soft fcc-Co phase with high saturation magnetization and reasonably low H
c
is a good candidate for medical diagnostic and therapy applications
Zeisberger et al. 2007
. Recently, some theoretical as well as experimental study on the structural and magnetic transformations in bulk Co had been performed suggesting that as Co transforms from hcp to fcc phase at high pressure and the magnetization vanishes at 150 GPa
Boccato et al. 2019
;
Torchio et al. 2016
. Various synthesis methods have been utilized to produce nanocrystalline fcc-Co with a suitable control of the particle size, stability, and magnetic properties. These methods involve chemical routes, solvothermal process, air synthesis as well as thin film deposition techniques
Yoo et al. 1998
;
Grass and Stark 2006
;
Zhang et al. 2009
;
Gajbhiye et al. 2008
;
Kumar and Gupta 2007
;
Banu et al. 2019
. In a study reporting Fischer-Tropsch synthesis of Co particles, it was observed that hcp-Co phase partially exist even at 850 K
Andreev et al. 2015
. Recently, Kumar et al.
Kumar et al. 2019
studied biphasic Co in C matrix using pyrolysis synthesis and found fcc-Co formation dominating at high heating rates.

In thin film form, usually high temperature film deposition or annealing has been carried out for the hcp to fcc transformation
Kumar and Gupta 2007
. Even by high temperature annealing at
\approx
773 K, partial transformation to fcc Co has been observed and hcp+fcc phase were found to coexist. Surprisingly, Banu et al.
Banu et al. 2017
;
Banu et al. 2018
reported the formation of high-density non-magnetic fcc-Co layer at the top in polycrystalline Co thin films under ambient conditions. Among thin films, mostly hcp(0001) or fcc(111) oriented films have been studied along with very few reports of meta stable bcc Co
Ohtake et al. 2011
. In polycrystalline films,
generally stacking faults exist parallel to the closed packed plane of fcc and hcp structure and even small volume of crystals with mixed orientation exist
Longo et al. 2014
;
Seema et al. 2021
. However, once fcc-Co formed, reverse transformation from fcc to hcp phase is not favorable because of energetics involved in the process
Tolédano et al. 2001
.

Nitrides of Co have been recently explored extensively in search of materials for metal-air batteries in energy storage. Tetra cobalt nitride (
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
) possess multiple magnetic functionalities and has been proposed as an interesting material for spintronic devices. They are also an active catalytic agent in oxygen evolution reaction and reduction reactions
Kang et al. 2018
. The formation of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
phase takes place when all the bcc sites in fcc-Co are occupied with N. The theoretical value of lattice parameter of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
is about 3.7 Å but the highest observed experimental value was 3.65 Å
Pandey et al. 2017
, and it tends to decrease when
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
thin film samples were deposited at higher
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or annealed at high
\mathrm{T_{a}}

Fang et al. 2004
;
Gupta et al. 2015
;
Pandey et al. 2017
. Literature reports on N self-diffusion measurements in mononitride phases of transition metals suggests that the N diffuses faster in CoN as compared to FeN and other early transition metal nitrides
Tayal et al. 2014a
;
Pandey et al. 2019a
. This trend has been observed to follow the order of enthalpy of formation of these compounds. Since
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
has been predicted to have a similar enthalpy of formation (
\approx
0 kJ/mole
Imai et al. 2014
) as that of CoN, it is anticipated that N-diffusion might inhibit the formation
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
phase at higher temperatures. Also, a recent study focused on the process of N incorporation in Co lattice and found it minimal at temperature more than 623 K using NH
3
plasma
van Straaten et al. 2020
. Therefore, this phenomenon of N self-diffusion at higher temperatures can be utilized for obtaining fcc-Co form
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
anticipating a complete out diffusion of N.

Therefore, in this work, we studied the phase formation of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
at different
\mathrm{T_{s}}
and characterized resulting samples. For comparison purpose, we also annealed
\mathrm{T_{s}}
=300 K deposited
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
samples at different temperatures and compared them with those deposited at different
\mathrm{T_{s}}
. We characterized crystal and electronic structure as well as the magnetic properties of deposited and annealed samples. We also quantified N self-diffusion in
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
phase for the first time. This work provides an alternate pathway for the formation fcc-Co phase derived from the isostructural
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
.

IV
Discussion

From the detailed study carried out in this work by measuring the structure, depth profiles, N K-edge XAS and N 1s XPS on samples deposited at different
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or annealed at different
\mathrm{T_{a}}
it becomes clear that N diffuses out from
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}

\approx
600 K and the resulting sample is fcc-Co. N self-diffusion measurements further confirm that it is exceptionally high in
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
as compared to other metal nitrides or even the mononitride CoN. From these measurements, a mechanism leading to the formation of fcc-Co from the isostructural
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
can be understood. Under ambient conditions, Co crystallizes in a hcp structure but at high pressure (70 GPa) or high temperature (700 K) it transform into fcc-Co albeit some amount of hcp-Co still remains. The route followed in this work is to first synthesize fcc-
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
via a reactive nitrogen sputtering of hcp-Co and thereafter N out-diffusion (by
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or
\mathrm{T_{a}}
) from
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
leads to precipitation of fcc-Co above 573 K. Once formed, the reverse transformation from fcc-Co to hcp-Co is not possible due to energetically unfavorable conditions
Tolédano et al. 2001
. Though such transformation of fcc-Co from the isostructural
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
appears convincingly simplistic, it is to be noted that the Curie temperature (
\mathrm{T_{C}}
) of Co is quite high at 1400 K. Since the transformation is taking place well below the
\mathrm{T_{C}}
, the local moments of Co are expected to play a deterministic role similar to those leading to hcp
\rightarrow
fcc transformations in Co
Lizárraga et al. 2017
. Comparing the observed behavior with a similar compound
\mathrm{Fe_{4}N}
, it appears that upon high temperature (above 800 K) annealing of fcc-
\mathrm{Fe_{4}N}
converts it into bcc-Fe
Chatbi et al. 1997
instead of fcc-Fe. Here, it is to be noted that the
\mathrm{T_{C}}
of Fe is much lower at around 1000 K and the transformation from fcc-
\mathrm{Fe_{4}N}
\rightarrow
bcc-Fe is also taking place near around the
\mathrm{T_{C}}
. Therefore, the role of local moments will be entirely different as compared to that in Co
Tolédano et al. 2001
. Another effect that seems to be playing a deterministic role is the self-diffusion of N in these compounds. Among late transition metal nitrides (viz. FeN, CoN, Ni-N), N self-diffusion in CoN was found to be fastest
Pandey et al. 2020
and in case of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
N self-diffusion was even faster than the mononitride CoN. In this scenario, it becomes favorable for fcc-
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
to transform into fcc-Co at a moderate temperature of about 600 K.

Conclusion

Through this work, we demonstrate synthesis of (111) oriented
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
films on a buffer layer of CrN (111) at a substrate temperature,
\mathrm{T_{s}}
of 300 K. Keeping all deposition conditions identical to the growth of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
but at
\mathrm{T_{s}}

>
573 K resulting films resembles to fcc-Co (instead of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
). Also, thermal annealing of
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
film results into a similar transformation from
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}

\rightarrow
Co when the annealing temperature
\mathrm{T_{a}}
, exceeds 573 K. These observations reveal that at high enough
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or
\mathrm{T_{a}}
, N diffuses out from fcc-
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
, leaving behind the isostructural Co. Chemical depth profiling using SIMS and local structural and bonding analysis using XAS and XPS showed almost negligible N content in samples with
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or
\mathrm{T_{a}}
higher than 573 K. Systematic changes in the magnetic properties of samples can be seen with the application of
\mathrm{T_{s}}
or Ta. Though the variation in magnetic anisotropy are quite similar in both cases, the magnitude of coercivity was found to be higher in annealed samples. N self-diffusion measurements in
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
phase were performed for the very first time and it was found that the diffusivity of N in
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
is exceptionally high as compared to neighboring transition metal or even with cobalt mononitride phase. Such a high diffusivity of N provides an alternative pathway for the formation of fcc-Co from the isostructural
\mathrm{Co_{4}N}
phase at a moderate temperature of about 600 K.

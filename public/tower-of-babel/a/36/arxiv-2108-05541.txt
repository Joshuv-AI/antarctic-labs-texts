Abstract
. We consider the eigenvalues of symplectic elliptic Ginibre matrices which are known to form a Pfaffian point process whose correlation kernel can be expressed in terms of the skew-orthogonal Hermite polynomials. We derive the scaling limits and the convergence rates of the correlation functions at the real bulk/edge of the spectrum, which in particular establishes the local universality at strong non-Hermiticity. Furthermore, we obtain the subleading corrections of the edge correlation kernels, which depend on the non-Hermiticity parameter contrary to the universal leading term. Our proofs are based on the asymptotic behaviour of the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble due to Lee and Riser as well as on a version of the Christoffel-Darboux identity, a differential equation satisfied by the skew-orthogonal polynomial kernel.

1.
Introduction and main results

What has been understood about non-Hermitian random matrices with symplectic symmetry?
To name a few, we should start with the celebrated work of Ginibre
[
23
]
.
There, he introduced Gaussian random matrices with quaternion entries (now known as the symplectic Ginibre ensemble) and derived the joint probability distribution function
{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
for their eigenvalues
\bm{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
It is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d{\mathbf{P}}_{N}(\bm{\zeta})&=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}\lvert\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{k}\rvert^{2}\lvert\zeta_{j}-\overline{\zeta}_{k}\rvert^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\lvert\zeta_{j}-\overline{\zeta}_{j}\rvert^{2}e^{-NQ(\zeta_{j})}\,dA(\zeta_{j}),\end{split}

where
Z_{N}
is the partition function and
dA(\zeta):=\tfrac{1}{\pi}d^{2}\zeta
.
Here
Q(\zeta)=|\zeta|^{2}
, but one may consider an arbitrary potential
Q:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
as long as it satisfies suitable potential theoretic assumptions.
From the statistical physics point of view, this eigenvalue distribution (
1.1
) can be interpreted as a two-dimensional Coulomb gas with an additional complex conjugation symmetry, see e.g.
[
19
, Section 15.9]
and
[
20
,
29
]
.

Regarding the macroscopic (global) properties of the symplectic Ginibre ensemble, it is well known that as
N\to\infty
, the eigenvalues tend to be uniformly distributed on the centered disc with radius
\sqrt{2}
, known as the circular law.
Such a statement was extended to the ensemble (
1.1
) with general
Q
in
[
12
]
, where it was shown that as
N\to\infty
, the system tends to minimise the weighted logarithmic energy functional
[
38
]
, the continuum limit of its discrete Hamiltonian.
In particular, in the large system, the eigenvalues condense in a compact set
S\subset\mathbb{C}
called the droplet.
Moreover, due to a well-known fact from the logarithmic potential theory
[
38
]
, it follows that the density inside the droplet with respect to the area measure
dA
is given by
\Delta Q/2
, where we use the convention
\Delta=\partial{\bar{\partial}}
.

Turning to the microscopic (local) properties, contrary to the complex Ginibre ensemble whose bulk scaling limit was obtained already in Ginibre’s original work
[
23
]
, the equivalent level of analysis for the symplectic Ginibre ensemble was obtained long after.
To our knowledge, it first appeared in the second edition of Mehta’s book
[
32
]
, where the bulk scaling limit was derived at the origin of the spectrum.
More precisely, it is known that the particle system (
1.1
) forms a Pfaffian point process whose correlation functions can be expressed in terms of a certain
2\times 2
matrix-valued kernel (see (
1.6
) below), and the large-
N
limit of the kernel was computed in
[
32
]
. (See also
[
24
]
for the scaling limits of the products of Ginibre matrices at the singular point, the origin.)

Beyond the radially symmetric potentials, an elliptic generalisation of the symplectic Ginibre ensemble was studied by Kanzieper
[
25
]
.
Such an extension is called the
elliptic Ginibre ensemble
and is associated with the potential

(1.2)

Q(\zeta):=\tfrac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}(|\zeta|^{2}-\tau\operatorname{Re}\zeta^{2}),\qquad\tau\in[0,1).

(cf. see
[
26
]
for a different type of elliptic extension of planar point processes.)
The associated droplet
S
is given by the elliptic disc

(1.3)

S:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)})^{2}+(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2}(1-\tau)})^{2}\leq 1\},

which is also known as the elliptic law, see the top part of
Figure
1
.
In
[
25
]
, Kanzieper introduced the formalism of skew-orthogonal polynomials to construct the correlation kernel and showed that the elliptic Ginibre ensemble can be exactly solved using the Hermite polynomials.
Furthermore, based on this formalism, he obtained the scaling limits at the origin in the maximally non-Hermitian regime where
\tau=0
as well as in the almost-Hermitian regime where
1-\tau=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
.
The analogous result for any fixed
\tau\in[0,1)
was obtained in
[
6
]
. We also refer to
[
2
]
for the chiral counterpart of such a result, which can be solved using the generalised Laguerre polynomials.

Figure 1
.
A single sample of eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre ensemble. Here,
N=4096
and
\tau=1/2
.

While the above-mentioned works mainly focused on various scaling limits at the origin, recently, there have been several studies on the scaling limits of the symplectic ensembles away from the origin as well.
One remarkable result in this direction was due to Akemann, Kieburg, Mielke, and Prosen
[
7
]
, where the authors showed that away from the real axis, the eigenvalue statistics of the symplectic and complex Ginibre ensembles are equivalent in the large-
N
limit, see also
[
36
]
for a similar statement on the fluctuations of the maximal modulus.
Moreover very recently, the edge scaling limit of the symplectic Ginibre ensemble at the right/left endpoint of the spectrum was obtained independently in
[
4
]
and
[
27
,
31
]
.
These works can be thought of as the maximally non-Hermitian analogue of the previous work
[
8
]
, which investigated the edge scaling limits of the elliptic Ginibre ensemble in the almost-Hermitian regime where
1-\tau=\mathcal{O}(N^{-\frac{1}{3}})
.
However at strong non-Hermiticity with general (fixed)
\tau\in[0,1)
, the scaling limits along the real axis as well as their convergence rates have not been discovered yet and we aim to contribute to these problems.
(After this work, the derivation of scaling limits at weak non-Hermiticity was extended to the entire bulk and edge along the real axis in
[
14
]
.)

Let us now be more precise in introducing our main results.
We consider the matrix model

(1.4)

X=\sqrt{1+\tau}\,S+\sqrt{1-\tau}\,T,\qquad S=\frac{1}{2}\Big(G_{1}+G_{1}^{\dagger}\Big),\ T=\frac{1}{2}\Big(G_{2}-G_{2}^{\dagger}\Big)

where
G^{\dagger}
denotes the conjugate transpose and
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are independent random matrices from the symplectic Ginibre ensemble (see
[
23
]
and
[
32
, Section 15.2]
), i.e. they have the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-\overline{B}&\overline{A}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{2N\times 2N},\qquad A_{ij},B_{ij}\sim{\mathcal{N}}\Big(0,\frac{1}{2N}\Big)+i{\mathcal{N}}\Big(0,\frac{1}{2N}\Big)

Note that due to this form our matrix
X
has
2N
eigenvalues that come in complex conjugate pairs.
However, only one value from each pair is needed to characterise the spectrum of
X
.
We used a single realisation of such a matrix
X
to create Figure
1
where we plotted all
2N
eigenvalues.

The matrix probability density is given by

d{\mathbf{P}}(X)=\exp\Big(-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\big[\operatorname{Tr}(XX^{\dagger})-\tau\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(X^{2})\big]\Big)\,dX.

Then diagonalizing
X
as in Ginibre’s paper
[
23
]
or writing it in Schur form (compare
[
32
, Appendix A.33]
) we can derive the eigenvalue distribution (
1.1
) with (
1.2
).
We remark that for general potentials
Q
it is not clear how to construct a matrix model that leads to the eigenvalue distribution (
1.1
).
Apart from the symplectic elliptic Ginibre ensemble and its chiral version
[
2
]
other known cases include the truncated unitary symplectic ensemble
[
28
]
and products of symplectic Ginibre matrices
[
24
]
.

In the sequel, we shall focus on the ensemble (
1.1
) with the elliptic potential (
1.2
).
It is well known (see e.g.
[
25
]
) that the
k
-point correlation function

(1.5)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{k}):=\frac{N!}{(N-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-k}}{\mathbf{P}}_{N}(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N})\prod_{j=k}^{N}\,dA(\zeta_{j})

has the structure

(1.6)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{\zeta}_{j}-\zeta_{j}){\textup{Pf}}\Big[e^{-NQ(\zeta_{j})/2-NQ(\zeta_{l})/2}\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\zeta_{j},\zeta_{l})&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\zeta_{j},\bar{\zeta}_{l})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\bar{\zeta}_{j},\zeta_{l})&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\bar{\zeta}_{j},\bar{\zeta}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\Big]_{j,l=1,\cdots,k}.

Here the two-variable function
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}
is called the
(skew) pre-kernel
.

To describe the local statistics at a given point
p\in\mathbb{C}
, it is convenient to define the rescaled point process
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
as

(1.7)

z_{j}:=e^{-i\theta}\sqrt{N\delta}\cdot(\zeta_{j}-p),\qquad\delta:=\dfrac{\Delta Q(p)}{2}=\frac{1}{2(1-\tau^{2})},

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
is the angle of the outward normal direction at the boundary if
p\in\partial S
(and otherwise
\theta=0
).
Here, the rescaling factor
\sqrt{N\delta}
is chosen according to the macroscopic density of the ensemble at the point
p
.
See Figure
1
for an illustration of the rescaled process.
The correlation function
R_{N,k}
of the process
\bm{z}
is given by

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N,k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})&:=\frac{1}{(N\delta)^{k}}\mathbf{R}_{N,k}(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{k}).\end{split}

(In the end, this rescaling makes
R_{N,1}
close to
1
in the complex bulk.)
In particular, if
p\in\mathbb{R}
, it can be written as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{N,k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})&=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\bar{z}_{j}-z_{j}){\textup{Pf}}\Big[e^{-\frac{N}{2}(Q(p+\frac{z_{j}}{\sqrt{N\delta}})+Q(p+\frac{z_{l}}{\sqrt{N\delta}}))}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{N}(z_{j},z_{l})&\kappa_{N}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{l})\\
\kappa_{N}(\bar{z}_{j},z_{l})&\kappa_{N}(\bar{z}_{j},\bar{z}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\Big]_{j,l=1,\cdots k},\end{split}

where the rescaled pre-kernel
\kappa_{N}
reads

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa_{N}(z,w):=\frac{1}{(N\delta)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta).\end{split}

In particular, the one-point function
R_{N}\equiv R_{N,1}
for the rescaled point process has the form

(1.11)

R_{N}(z)=(\bar{z}-z)e^{-NQ(p+\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{N\delta}})}\,\kappa_{N}(z,\bar{z}).

Recall that the
k
-th Hermite polynomial
H_{k}
is given by

(1.12)

H_{k}(z):=(-1)^{k}e^{z^{2}}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}e^{-z^{2}}=k!\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!(k-2m)!}(2z)^{k-2m}.

Then it follows from
[
25
]
that the pre-kernel
\kappa_{N}
has the canonical representation

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa_{N}(z,w)&=\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w\Big)\\
&\quad-\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big).\end{split}

See Subsection
2.1
for further details.

In our first result Proposition
1.1
, we obtain a differential equation satisfied by the pre-kernel
\kappa_{N},
which can be recognised as a version of the Christoffel-Darboux formula, cf.
[
30
,
4
,
13
,
16
]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For each
N
and for any
p\in\mathbb{C}
, the (canonical) pre-kernel
\kappa_{N}
satisfies the differential equation

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{z}\kappa_{N}(z,w)&=\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{2(1-\tau)N}{1+\tau}}\,p+2(1-\tau)z\Big)\kappa_{N}(z,w)\\
&\quad+2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big)H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w\Big)\\
&\quad-2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w\Big).\end{split}

Remark
.

It follows from

(\tfrac{\tau}{2})^{k/2}H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big)\sim(\sqrt{N}p+\sqrt{2}z)^{k},\qquad(\tau\to 0)

that for
\tau=0
, the equation (
1.14
) reduces to

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{z}\kappa_{N}(z,w)&=(\sqrt{2N}p+2z)\kappa_{N}(z,w)\\
&\quad+2\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\frac{(\sqrt{N}p+\sqrt{2}z)^{k}(\sqrt{N}p+\sqrt{2}w)^{k}}{k!}-2\frac{(\sqrt{N}p+\sqrt{2}z)^{2N}}{(2N-1)!!}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\sqrt{N}p+\sqrt{2}w)^{2l}}{(2l)!!}.\end{split}

This differential equation was utilized in
[
4
]
. See also
[
1
]
for a related statement in the other extremal case when
\tau=1
.

It is worth pointing out that the inhomogeneous term in the second line of (
1.14
) corresponds to the (holomorphic) kernel of the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble with dimension
2N
. Such a relation has been observed in other models as well, which include the Laguerre
[
2
,
35
,
3
]
and the Mittag-Leffler ensembles
[
4
,
11
,
17
]
.

Let us remark that the counterpart of such a differential equation for the kernel of the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble was obtained in
[
30
, Proposition 2.3]
. (See also
[
16
, Subsection 3.1]
for an alternative derivation in a more general framework.)
As it allows to perform a suitable asymptotic analysis, this formula has turned out to be very useful in various situations, see e.g.
[
9
,
15
,
37
]
.
In a similar spirit, we emphasise that Proposition
1.1
can also be applied to other cases including the almost-Hermitian regime, see
[
14
]
.
(We also mention that a different approach for the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble was recently developed in
[
5
]
.)

In our main result Theorem
1.2
below, we establish the large-
N
asymptotic of the kernel. This is based on the asymptotic analysis of the differential equation (
1.14
), see Corollary
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
.
Recall that by (
1.3
), we have
S\cap\mathbb{R}=[-\sqrt{2}(1+\tau),\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)].

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then there exists a pre-kernel
\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}
such that

(1.16)

R_{N,k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\bar{z}_{j}-z_{j})\,{\textup{Pf}}\Big[e^{-|z|^{2}-|w|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(z_{j},z_{l})&\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{l})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(\bar{z}_{j},z_{l})&\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(\bar{z}_{j},\bar{z}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\Big]_{j,l=1,\cdots k}

and that satisfies the following asymptotic behaviours as
N\to\infty
.

•

(The real bulk)
For
|p|<\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
, there exists an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(1.17)

\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)=\kappa^{\mathbb{R}}_{\textup{bulk}}(z,w)+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),

where

(1.18)

\kappa^{\mathbb{R}}_{\textup{bulk}}(z,w):=\sqrt{\pi}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(z-w).

•

(The real edge)
For
p=\pm\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
and an arbitrary
{\varepsilon}>0
it holds

(1.19)

\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)=\kappa^{\mathbb{R}}_{\textup{edge}}(z,w)+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\kappa^{\mathbb{R},1/2}_{\textup{edge}}(z,w)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}}),

where

(1.20)

\kappa^{\mathbb{R}}_{\textup{edge}}(z,w):=e^{2zw}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-s^{2}}\sinh(2s(w-z))\operatorname{erfc}(z+w-s)\,ds

and

(1.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa^{\mathbb{R},1/2}_{\textup{edge}}(z,w)&:=\tfrac{1}{12\sqrt{2}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{3/2}\,e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big[\Big((2z^{2}+\tfrac{1-2\tau}{1+\tau})e^{-2z^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)+2\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}w\,e^{-2(z^{2}+w^{2})}\Big)-\Big(z\leftrightarrow w\Big)\Big].\end{split}

•

(The real axis outside the droplet)
For
|p|>\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
, there exists an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(1.22)

\widetilde{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

In all three cases the error terms are uniform for
z,w
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.

Since the leading terms of the pre-kernels are independent of
\tau
and
p
(for the real bulk case), Theorem
1.2
affirms the local bulk/edge universality along the real axis.
For the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble, the analogous result on the fine asymptotic behaviours of the kernels were obtained by Lee and Riser
[
30
]
. We also refer to
[
21
]
for similar results on Gaussian symplectic ensemble when

.

Remark
.

As noted in
[
4
]
, the limiting pre-kernels in Theorem
1.2
can be written in the following unified way

(1.23)

\kappa_{a}^{\mathbb{R}}(z,w):=\frac{e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{a}e^{-2(z-u)^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}(w-u))-(z\leftrightarrow w)\,du,\qquad a=\begin{cases}\infty&\textup{if}\quad|p|<\sqrt{2}(1+\tau),\\
0&\textup{if}\quad|p|=\sqrt{2}(1+\tau),\\

For an arbitrary
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the pre-kernel
\kappa_{a}^{\mathbb{R}}
can be obtained by rescaling the process at the

-dependent point

(1.24)

p=\pm\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)\mp a\sqrt{\tfrac{2(1-\tau^{2})}{N}}.

(See also
[

,

]
for further motivations on zooming the point process at a moving location.)

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain that the density
R_{N}
when

for

in the bulk along the real line is given by

(1.25)

R_{N}(x+iy)=4yF(2y)+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad F(z):=e^{-z^{2}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{t^{2}}\,dt.

(This easily follows from

For
p
at the real edge we have

(1.27)

R_{N}(z)=R(z)+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}R^{(1/2)}(z)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}}),

where

R^{(1/2)}(z)=\tfrac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}\operatorname{Im}(z)\,e^{-4\operatorname{Im}(z)^{2}}\operatorname{Im}\Big[(2z^{2}+\tfrac{1-2\tau}{1+\tau})\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}\bar{z})\,e^{-2z^{2}}+2\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\,\bar{z}\,e^{-4\operatorname{Re}z^{2}}\Big].

Since the first subleading term is of order
1/\sqrt{N}
, the convergence of
R_{N}(z)
to the limit
R(z)
is rather slow.
To estimate the limit from values at finite
N
we use the following method: for given
z\in\mathbb{C}
we first compute
R_{N}(z)
for
N=2000,3000,4000,5000
numerically, then we do a least squares fit of the function
N\mapsto a+b/\sqrt{N}+c/N
to this data to estimate the constants
a,b,c
.
Since we know that the density has the series representation
R_{N}(z)=R(z)+R^{(1/2)}(z)/\sqrt{N}+R^{(1)}(z)/N+\cdots
the fitted value of
a
is our best guess for the limit
R(z)
.
We apply the same method to estimate the limit of the first correction
\sqrt{N}(R_{N}(z)-R(z))
.

We remark that for each
x
, as
y\to\infty,

R(x+iy)\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(2x),\qquad R^{(1/2)}(x+iy)\sim-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-4x^{2}}y^{2}.

2
is devoted to recalling the skew-orthogonal polynomial formalism and to proving Proposition
1.1
.
In Section
3
, we perform the asymptotic analysis of the inhomogeneous terms in (
1.14
) (Proposition
3.1
) and complete the proof of Theorem

. In Appendix

.

2.
Generalised Christoffel-Darboux identity

In this section, we derive a version of the Christoffel-Darboux formula.
For this we will use an explicit expression of the pre-kernels in terms of the Hermite polynomials.

2.1.
Skew-orthogonal polynomials

Following
[
25
]
we express the pre-kernel, and by extension all correlation functions, in terms of skew-orthogonal polynomials.
Define the skew-symmetric form

\langle f,g\rangle_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Big(f(\zeta)g(\bar{\zeta})-g(\zeta)f(\bar{\zeta})\Big)(\zeta-\bar{\zeta})e^{-NQ(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta).

Let
q_{m}
be a family of monic polynomials of degree
m
that satisfy the following skew-orthogonality conditions with positive skew-norms
r_{k}>0
: for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}

(2.1)

\langle q_{2k},q_{2l}\rangle_{s}=\langle q_{2k+1},q_{2l+1}\rangle_{s}=0,\qquad\langle q_{2k},q_{2l+1}\rangle_{s}=-\langle q_{2l+1},q_{2k}\rangle_{s}=r_{k}\,\delta_{k,l}.

Here
\delta_{k,l}
is the Kronecker delta.
Such polynomials exist if
\int\lvert\zeta\rvert^{m}e^{-NQ(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta)<\infty
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, see e.g.
[
6
]
.
Then the pre-kernel
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}
is written as

(2.2)

{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{q_{2k+1}(\zeta)q_{2k}(\eta)-q_{2k}(\zeta)q_{2k+1}(\eta)}{r_{k}}.

For the elliptic potential
Q
in (
1.2
), the associated skew-orthogonal polynomials
q_{k}
and their skew-norms
r_{k}
are given by

q_{2k}(\zeta)=\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{k}k!\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\zeta\Big),\qquad q_{2k+1}(\zeta)=\Big(\frac{\tau}{2N}\Big)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\zeta\Big),

and

r_{k}=2(1-\tau)^{3/2}(1+\tau)^{1/2}\frac{(2k+1)!}{N^{2k+2}},

see
[
25
]
.
Thus the pre-kernel
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}
of the ensemble
\bm{\zeta}
has the expression

\begin{split}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)&=\Big(\frac{N}{2(1-\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{1+\tau}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{(2k+1)!!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}\\
&\quad\times\Big[H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\zeta\Big)H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\eta\Big)-H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\eta\Big)H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\zeta\Big)\Big].\end{split}

Therefore by (
1.10
), the rescaled pre-kernel
\kappa_{N}
at the point
p\in\mathbb{R}
is given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\kappa_{N}(z,w)=\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)\Big(F(z,w)-F(w,z)\Big),

where

(2.4)

F(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w\Big).

2.2.
Derivation of a differential equation

With the expression (
2.4
), we show the Christoffel-Darboux identity (Proposition
1.1
) for the kernel.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Throughout the proof, let us write

(2.5)

\zeta=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}z,\qquad\eta=\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}p+\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}w

to lighten notations.
It is well known that the Hermite polynomial
H_{j}
satisfies the following differentiation rule and the three term recurrence relation:

(2.6)

H_{j}^{\prime}(z)=2jH_{j-1}(z),\qquad H_{j+1}(z)=2zH_{j}(z)-H_{j}^{\prime}(z).

Using these formulas, we have

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k-1)!!}H_{2k}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k-1)!!}H_{2k}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)+\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k-1)!!}(2\zeta H_{2k-1}(\zeta)-H_{2k-1}^{\prime}(\zeta))\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)+\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\,\zeta\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k+1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle\quad-\tau\partial_{z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k+1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)+\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}.

By rearranging the terms,

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{k+1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=F(z,w)-\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N-1}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k+\frac{3}{2}}}{(2k+2)!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)H_{2k+2}(\eta).

Therefore we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}F(z,w)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\,\zeta F(z,w)-\tau\partial_{z}F(z,w)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2N-1)!!}\Big(2\zeta H_{2N-1}(\zeta)-H_{2N-1}^{\prime}(\zeta)\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k+\frac{3}{2}}}{(2k+2)!}\Big(2\zeta H_{2k+1}(\zeta)-H^{\prime}_{2k+1}(\zeta)\Big)H_{2k+2}(\eta)+\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}.

We now use the three term recurrence relation again and obtain

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{z}F(z,w)=2\sqrt{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\,\zeta F(z,w)-\tau\partial_{z}F(z,w)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}\Big[\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)-\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k}}{(2k)!}H_{2k}(\zeta)H_{2k}(\eta)\Big].

This leads to

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\partial_{z}F(z,w)=\Big(\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\tau)N}{1+\tau}}p+2(1-\tau)z\Big)F(z,w)\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}}{1+\tau}\Big[\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta)-\frac{\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}}{1+\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k}}{(2k)!}H_{2k}(\zeta)H_{2k}(\eta)\Big].\end{split}

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{z}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\eta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\eta)\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l-2)!!}H_{2l-1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\eta)\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l-2)!!}\Big(2\zeta H_{2l-2}(\zeta)-H_{2l-2}^{\prime}(\zeta)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-\tau^{2})\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\eta)\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}\Big(2\zeta H_{2l}(\zeta)-H_{2l}^{\prime}(\zeta)\Big).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}F(w,z)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-\tau^{2})\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{(2k+1)!!}H_{2k+1}(\eta)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}\Big(2\zeta H_{2l}(\zeta)-H_{2l}^{\prime}(\zeta)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)H_{2k+1}(\eta).

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{z}F(w,z)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})N}p+2(1-\tau^{2})z\Big)F(w,z)-\tau\partial_{z}F(w,z)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)H_{2k+1}(\eta),

which leads to

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{z}F(w,z)&=\Big(\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\tau)N}{1+\tau}}p+2(1-\tau)z\Big)F(w,z)-\frac{\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}}{1+\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}H_{2k+1}(\zeta)H_{2k+1}(\eta).\end{split}

Combining all of the above, we conclude

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\Big(F(z,w)-F(w,z)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\tau)N}{1+\tau}}p+2(1-\tau)z\Big)\Big(F(z,w)-F(w,z)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}}{1+\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(\zeta)H_{k}(\eta)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{2(1-\tau^{2})}}{1+\tau}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}(\zeta)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(\eta).

Now Proposition
1.1
follows from the relation (
2.3
).

∎

To analyse the large-
N
limit of the pre-kernel, let us introduce

(2.9)

\widehat{\kappa}_{N}(z,w):=\omega_{N}(z,w)\kappa_{N}(z,w),

where

(2.10)

\omega_{N}(z,w)=\exp\Big[\tau\,\Big(p\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2(1-\tau^{2})}}+z\Big)^{2}+\tau\,\Big(p\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2(1-\tau^{2})}}+w\Big)^{2}-\Big(p\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{2}z\Big)\Big(p\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{2}w\Big)\Big].

Let us write

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E^{1}_{N}(\xi,\omega)&:=2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\exp\Big[N\Big(\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})-\xi\omega\Big)\Big]\\
&\quad\times\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big),\end{split}

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E^{2}_{N}(\xi,\omega)&:=2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\exp\Big[-N\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})\Big]\\
&\quad\times\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big).\end{split}

Then as an immediate consequence of Proposition
1.1
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

We have

(2.13)

\partial_{z}\widehat{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)=2(z-w)\widehat{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)+r_{N}(z,w),

where

(2.14)

r_{N}(z,w)=E_{N}^{1}\Big(\tfrac{p}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,\tfrac{p}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}w\Big)-e^{(z-w)^{2}}E_{N}^{2}\Big(\tfrac{p}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,\tfrac{p}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}w\Big).

The asymptotic expansion of
r_{N}
will be addressed in the next section.

3.
Asymptotic analysis for the correlation kernels

In this section, we analyse the large-
N
asymptotics of the differential equation (
2.13
) and prove Theorem
1.2
.

3.1.
Asymptotics of the inhomogeneous terms

As indicated by Lemma
3.10
below, the key ingredient to prove Theorem
1.2
is the large-
N
behaviour of the function
r_{N}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following asymptotics hold as
N\to\infty
.

•

(The real bulk)
For
|p|<\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
, there exists an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that

r_{N}(z,w)=2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

•

(The real edge)
For
p=\pm\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
and an arbitrary
{\varepsilon}>0
, it holds

(3.1)

r_{N}(z,w)=r(z,w)+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}r^{(1/2)}(z,w)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}}),

where

(3.2)

r(z,w):=\operatorname{erfc}(z+w)-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{(z-w)^{2}-2z^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}r^{(1/2)}(z,w)&:=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{(z-w)^{2}-2z^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\tfrac{4}{3}z^{2}-\tfrac{4}{3}zw+\tfrac{2}{3}w^{2}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau})e^{-2w^{2}}-(\tfrac{2}{3}z^{3}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau}z)\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)\Big].\end{split}

•

(The real axis outside the droplet)
For
|p|>\sqrt{2}(1+\tau)
, there exists an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(3.4)

r_{N}(z)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

In all three cases the error terms are uniform for
z,w
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.

Combining Corollary
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
, our main result Theorem
1.2
can be easily derived by solving the associated differential equations, see Subsection
3.5
.
The proof of each statement in Proposition
3.1
is given in the following subsections.

Remark
.

For the special case when
p=0
, the limiting kernel was obtained in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
using an integral representation of the Hermite polynomials.
From the viewpoint of Proposition
3.1
, it also follows from the convergence
\lim_{N\to\infty}r_{N}=2
.
We remark that this convergence can be alternatively obtained using the classical Mehler-Hermite formula (see e.g.
[
34
, Eq.(18.18.28)]
).

For the reader’s convenience, let us outline the strategy for the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

•

We rewrite
E_{N}^{1},E_{N}^{2}
as

(3.5)

E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)=E_{N}^{1}(\xi_{0},\omega)+\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi}\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}(t,\omega)\,dt,\qquad E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)=E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega_{0})+\int_{\omega_{0}}^{\omega}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,t)\,dt.

•

Using a version of the Christoffel-Darboux identity, we express
\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}
and
\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}

).

•

Using the strong asymptotics of the Hermite polynomials (Lemma

and

We end this subsection by introducing the main ingredients for the proof of Proposition

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
N
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)&=\sqrt{2\tau}\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{2N-1}\frac{\sqrt{N}}{(2N-1)!}\exp\Big[N\Big(\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})-\xi\omega\Big)\Big]\\
&\quad\times\Big[\tau H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)H_{2N-1}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big)-H_{2N-1}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big)\Big]\end{split}

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)&=-\sqrt{2\tau}(1-\tau)\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{2N-1}\frac{\sqrt{N}}{(2N-1)!}\exp\Big(-N\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})\Big)\\
&\quad\times H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)H_{2N-1}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

This lemma immediately follows from the Christoffel-Darboux identity for the kernel of complex elliptic Ginibre ensembles found in
[
30
, Proposition 2.3]
. (See
[
16
, Section 3]
for the derivation of such identities in a more general setup.)

To be more precise, let

(3.8)

S_{N}(\zeta,\eta):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(\zeta)H_{k}(\eta).

Then by (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), one can express
E_{N}^{1}
and
E_{N}^{2}
in terms of
S_{N}
. In particular, for
E_{N}^{2}
, we use the realisation

\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}\frac{(i\sqrt{\tau}/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega\Big)H_{k}(0),

which follows from
H_{2l}(0)=(-1)^{l}(2l)!/l!
and
H_{2l+1}(0)=0
.
Now the Christoffel-Darboux identity

(3.9)

\begin{split}\partial_{\zeta}S_{N}(\zeta,\eta)&=\frac{2\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(\eta-\tau\zeta)S_{N}(\zeta,\eta)+\frac{2}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{N}\frac{\tau H_{N}(\zeta)H_{N-1}(\eta)-H_{N-1}(\zeta)H_{N}(\eta)}{(N-1)!}\end{split}

completes the proof.
∎

The strong asymptotics of the Hermite polynomials can be found for instance in
[
30
,
39
]
.
(See also
[
33
,
18
]

[

.

T>0

(3.10)

l:=T\Big(\log\frac{T}{1-\tau^{2}}-1\Big)

(3.11)

g(z):=\log\Big(z+\sqrt{z^{2}-F_{0}^{2}}\Big)+\frac{z}{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-F_{0}^{2}}}-\log 2-\frac{1}{2},\qquad F_{0}:=2\sqrt{T\tfrac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}}.

We also write

(3.12)

\psi(z):=\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{F_{0}}\,\Big(z+\sqrt{z^{2}-F_{0}^{2}}\Big).

Lemma 3.3
.

(Strong asymptotics of Hermite polynomials)

For
T>0
and
R\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

(3.13)

\Big|H_{TN+R}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}z\Big)\Big|=\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{TN+R}{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{(TN+R)!}{N}}\,\Big|e^{N(Tg(z)-l/2)}\Big|\,\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{5}{12}}),

where the
\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{5}{12}})
-term is uniform for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{TN+R}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}z\Big)&=\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\frac{\tau}{2}\Big)^{-\frac{TN+R}{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{(TN+R)!}{N}}\\
&\quad\times\psi(z)^{R}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}e^{N(Tg(z)-l/2)}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\big),\end{split}

where the
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
-term is uniform for any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\setminus[-F_{0},F_{0}]
.

In the sequel, it is convenient to write

(3.15)

K_{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\tfrac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}(\operatorname{Re}z)^{2}+\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}(\operatorname{Im}z)^{2}\leq T\},\qquad\varkappa_{T}:=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{T}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}},

and

(3.16)

\Omega(z):=\lvert z\rvert^{2}-\tau\operatorname{Re}z^{2}-2T\operatorname{Re}g(z)+l.

In the rest of this section, we will mainly consider the case
T=2
.

3.2.
Inside the bulk

In this section we prove the first part of Proposition
3.1
. By (
2.14
), it immediately follows from the next lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert x_{0}\rvert<\sqrt{2\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
. Then there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}
of
x_{0}
and an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that the following estimates hold uniformly for
\xi,\omega\in U
:

E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)=2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
and the asymptotic (
3.13
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq CN^{5/6}\exp\Big(-N\operatorname{Re}\Big[\xi\omega-\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})-2g(\xi)-2g(\omega)+l\Big]\Big),

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq CN^{5/6}\exp\Big(-N\operatorname{Re}\Big[\xi\omega-\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})+\tfrac{(\xi-\omega)^{2}}{2}-2g(\xi)-2g(\omega)+l\Big]\Big)

for a sufficiently large
N
and
\xi,\omega\in\mathbb{C}
.

By
[
30
, Lemma D.1]
, we have that
\Omega(z)\geq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
and that
\Omega(z)=0
only for
z\in\partial K_{T}
.
Here,
\Omega
is given by (
3.16
).
Notice that
x_{0}\in\mathring{K}_{2}
(i.e. (
3.15
) with
T=2
).
Hence there exists a compact set
V\subset\mathring{K}_{2}
that contains an open neighbourhood of
x_{0}
and
\Omega(z)>2{\varepsilon}
for all
z\in V
with some
{\varepsilon}>0
.
Consequently we obtain that for
\xi,\omega\in V
with
\lvert\xi-\bar{\omega}\rvert<\sqrt{2{\varepsilon}}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Big[\xi\omega-\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})-2g(\xi)-2g(\omega)+l\Big]=-\frac{1}{2}\lvert\xi-\bar{\omega}\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\omega)>{\varepsilon}.

Let us take

U^{1}\subset V\cap\{z\mid\lvert z-x_{0}\rvert<\sqrt{{\varepsilon}/2}\}.

Then we have the uniform estimate

(3.17)

\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}(t,\omega)=\mathcal{O}(N^{5/6}e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad(t,\omega\in U^{1}).

To analyse
E_{N}^{2}
, notice that for
\xi,\omega\in V
with
(\operatorname{Im}\xi)^{2},(\operatorname{Im}\omega)^{2}<{\varepsilon}/2
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Big[\xi\omega-\tfrac{\tau}{2}(\xi^{2}+\omega^{2})+\tfrac{(\xi-\omega)^{2}}{2}-2g(\xi)-2g(\omega)+l\Big]=-(\operatorname{Im}\xi)^{2}-(\operatorname{Im}\omega)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\omega)>{\varepsilon}.

Let
U^{2}\subset V\cap\{z\mid\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert<\sqrt{{\varepsilon}/2}\}
.
Then

(3.18)

\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,t)=\mathcal{O}(N^{5/6}e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad(\xi,t\in U^{2})

holds uniformly.

Next, we analyse
E_{N}^{1}(\xi_{0},\omega)
and
E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega_{0})
for suitable choices of
\xi_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
.
For
E_{N}^{1}
, let us choose
\xi_{0}=\bar{\omega}
.
Then
E_{N}^{1}(\bar{\omega},\omega)
corresponds to the density of the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble with
2N
eigenvalues in the bulk.
(See the next subsection for further details.)
In particular, by
[
30
, Lemma D.4]
, we have the uniform estimate

(3.19)

E_{N}^{1}(\bar{\omega},\omega)=2+\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

We now claim that

(3.20)

E_{N}^{2}(\xi,0)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}/2}N^{\frac{1}{6}}),\qquad(\xi\in U^{2})

holds uniformly.
By (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\quad\Big|\exp\Big(-N\frac{1-\tau}{2}\xi^{2}\Big)\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{(2N)!!}{(2N-1)!!\,N}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\Big(-N\operatorname{Re}\Big[\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}\xi^{2}-2g(\xi)+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]\Big)\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{5}{12}}).

We also have that for
\xi\in V
with
(\operatorname{Im}\xi)^{2}<{\varepsilon}
,

\operatorname{Re}\Big[\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}\xi^{2}-2g(\xi)+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Im}\xi)^{2}>\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2}.

Notice also that Stirling’s formula yields

\Big(\frac{(2N)!!}{(2N-1)!!\,N}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-\frac{1}{4}}).

Finally, due to a well-known Taylor series, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}(0)=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\Big(-\frac{\tau}{4}\Big)^{l}\,\frac{(2l)!}{(l!)^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tau}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\tfrac{\tau^{N}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)

Combining all of the above with (
2.12
), we obtain (
3.20
).

We now choose
U\subset U^{1}\cap U^{2}=V\cap\{z\mid\lvert z-x_{0}\rvert<\sqrt{{\varepsilon}/2}\}
.
Then using (
3.17
), (
3.19
) for
E_{N}^{1}
and (
3.18
), (
3.20
) for
E_{N}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)

\displaystyle=E_{N}^{1}(\bar{\omega},\omega)+\int_{\bar{\omega}}^{\xi}\partial_{\xi}E_{N}^{1}(t,\omega)\,dt=2+\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{5}{6}}e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)

\displaystyle=E_{N}^{2}(\xi,0)+\int_{0}^{\omega}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,t)\,dt=\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-\frac{N{\varepsilon}}{2}}),

where the error terms are uniform for
\xi,\omega\in U
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
At the edge

In this subsection, we prove the second assertion of Proposition
3.1
.

Let us define

(3.22)

{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(z,\overline{w}):=N\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}e^{-N(z\overline{w}-\tfrac{\tau}{2}(z^{2}+\overline{w}^{2}))}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{j}}{j!}H_{j}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}z\Big)\overline{H_{j}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}w\Big)}.

Notice that the function
{\mathcal{K}}_{2N}
is same as
E_{N}^{1}
in (
2.11
) up to a prefactor.

It is well known that
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
corresponds to the pre-kernel of the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble with
n
points, which is known to form a determinantal point process, see e.g.
[
22
]
.
As
n,N\to\infty
while keeping
n/N=T
fixed, the elliptic Ginibre ensemble condensates to the droplet
K_{T}
in (
3.15
).
Moreover, the asymptotic behaviour of (
3.22
) on the diagonal was extensively studied in
[
30
]
. Along the same lines as the proof of
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
, one can obtain the following proposition.
(See also
[
37
, Corollary 8.12]
for the leading term of the proposition.)

Proposition 3.5
.

(Cf.
[
30
, Theorem 3.10]
)

Given
T>0
and
z_{0}\in\partial K_{T}
, let
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}(z_{0})\in\mathbb{C}
be the outer unit normal vector of
K_{T}
at
z_{0}
.
Then as
n,N\to\infty
while keeping
n/N=T
fixed and for an arbitrary
{\varepsilon}>0
, we have

(3.23)

\frac{1}{N}{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\Big(z_{0}+\tfrac{\xi\mathbf{n}}{\sqrt{N}},\overline{z_{0}+\tfrac{\omega\mathbf{n}}{\sqrt{N}}}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\Big(\frac{\xi+\overline{\omega}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\varkappa(z_{0})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\xi^{2}-\xi\overline{\omega}+\overline{\omega}^{2}-1}{3}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\xi+\overline{\omega})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}}),

where
\varkappa(z_{0})
is the curvature of
\partial K_{T}
at
z_{0}
.
Here the error bound is uniform for bounded
\xi,\omega
and over
z_{0}\in\partial K_{T}
.

We shall need the following elementary estimates. We provide the proof in Appendix
A.1
.
Recall that
l,g,\psi
and
\varkappa_{T}
are given by (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.15
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T>0
and
x_{0}=\sqrt{T\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
.
As
N\to\infty
, we have the following:

•

for
0\leq\alpha<1/2
, we have

(3.24)

\operatorname{Re}\Big[\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}s^{2}-Tg(s)+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]\geq N^{-1+2\alpha},\qquad s\in[0,x_{0}-\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}N^{\alpha}];

•

for
0\leq\nu<1/6
, we have

(3.25)

\exp\Big(-N\Big[\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)^{2}-Tg\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]\Big)=e^{-2z^{2}}\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\varkappa_{T}\frac{z^{3}}{\sqrt{N}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+6\nu})\Big)

uniformly for
z=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
;

•

for
0\leq\nu<1/6
, we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)}&=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{T(1-\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}\tau}{1+\tau}\varkappa_{T}\frac{z}{\sqrt{N}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+2\nu})\Big),\\
\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)}\frac{\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}s\Big)}}{\psi\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}s\Big)}&=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{T(1-\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+\tau}\varkappa_{T}\frac{s+\tau z}{\sqrt{N}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+2\nu})\Big)\end{split}

uniformly for
z,s=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
.

We now prove the following lemma, which gives the second assertion of Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
x_{0}=\pm\sqrt{2\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
.
Then as
N\to\infty
and for arbitrary
{\varepsilon}>0
, we have

(3.27)

E_{N}^{1}\Big(x_{0}\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}w\Big)=\operatorname{erfc}(z+w)+\tfrac{\sqrt{2}\varkappa_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-(z+w)^{2}}\tfrac{2z^{2}-2zw+2w^{2}-1}{3\sqrt{\pi}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}})

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}w\Big)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-2z^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)\\
&+\tfrac{\varkappa_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-2z^{2}}\Big[(\tfrac{2}{3}z^{3}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau}z)\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\tfrac{1}{1+\tau}-\tfrac{1+2w^{2}}{3})e^{-2w^{2}}\Big]+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+{\varepsilon}}).\end{split}

The error terms are uniform for bounded
z,w
.

Proof.

We shall consider the case
x_{0}=+\sqrt{2\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
as the other case works analogously.

The expansion (
3.27
) for
E_{N}^{1}
immediately follows from Proposition
3.5
using the relation

E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)=\tfrac{2}{N}{\mathcal{K}}_{2N}(\xi,\omega).

Thus it suffices to show the expansion (
3.28
) for
E_{N}^{2}
.
To analyse
E_{N}^{2}
, choose
0<\alpha<\nu<1/6
and let us write

(3.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}w\Big)=E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,0\Big)\\
&+\int_{0}^{x_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}N^{\alpha}}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,s\Big)\,ds+\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\int_{-N^{\alpha}}^{w}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}s\Big)\,ds.\end{split}

We first show that for
z=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
uniformly,

(3.30)

E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,0\Big)=\sqrt{2}\,e^{-2z^{2}}\Big[1+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}\varkappa_{2}(\tfrac{2}{3}z^{3}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau}z)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+6\nu})\Big].

Note that by (
3.14
) and Stirling’s formula, we have

\begin{split}\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N-1)!!}H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\xi\Big)&=\frac{1}{(2\pi(1-\tau^{2})N)^{1/4}}\frac{\sqrt{(2N)!}}{(2N-1)!!}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(\xi)}e^{N(2g(\xi)-l/2)}\Big(1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\Big)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2(1-\tau^{2}))^{1/4}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(\xi)}e^{N(2g(\xi)-l/2)}\Big(1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\Big),\end{split}

where the
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
-term is uniform for
\xi
in a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\setminus[-F_{0},F_{0}]
.
Applying the estimates (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), the desired asymptotic behaviour (
3.30
) follows from the expression (
2.12
) and the summation formula (
3.21
).

Next, we show that for
z=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
uniformly,

(3.31)

\int_{0}^{x_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}N^{\alpha}}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,s\Big)\,ds=e^{-2z^{2}}\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-N^{2\alpha}}).

For this, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of
\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,s)
using Lemma
3.2
.
Note that by (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we have

(3.32)

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}(\xi,s)\rvert

\displaystyle=2(1-\tau)(\tfrac{2}{\pi(1-\tau^{2})})^{\frac{1}{4}}\lvert\psi^{\prime}(\xi)\rvert

(3.33)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\Big(N\operatorname{Re}\Big[-\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}\xi^{2}+2g(\xi)-\tfrac{l}{2}-\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}s^{2}+2g(s)-\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]\Big)\,\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{2}{3}}).

Moreover, it follows from (
3.24
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) that

(3.34)

\Big|\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,s\Big)\Big|=e^{-2\operatorname{Re}z^{2}}\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-N^{2\alpha}})

uniformly for
0\leq s\leq x_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}N^{\alpha}
and
z=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
.
Now by using the triangle inequality and integrating this asymptotic, we obtain (
3.31
).

Finally, we show that for
z,w=\mathcal{O}(N^{\nu})
uniformly,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}\int_{-N^{\alpha}}^{w}\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}s\Big)\,ds=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-2z^{2}}(1+\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}w))\\
&\quad-\tfrac{\varkappa_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-2z^{2}}\Big[(\tfrac{2}{3}z^{3}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau}z)(1+\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{2}w))+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\tfrac{1}{1+\tau}-\tfrac{1+2w^{2}}{3})e^{-2w^{2}}\Big]\\
&\quad+e^{-2z^{2}+2(\operatorname{Im}w)^{2}}\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+7\nu}).\end{split}

Similarly as above, we use Lemma
3.2
and the asymptotics (
3.14
), (
3.25
), which leads to

\partial_{\omega}E_{N}^{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z,x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}s\Big)=-\sqrt{\tfrac{2N}{\pi}}e^{-2(z^{2}+s^{2})}\Big[1+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}\,\varkappa_{2}\big(\tfrac{2}{3}(z^{3}+s^{3})-\tfrac{s+\tau z}{1+\tau}\big)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+6\nu})\Big].

Here
\varkappa_{2}
is given by (
3.15
) with
T=2
. By integration, (
3.35
) follows from elementary asymptotic behaviour of the error function (see e.g.
[
34
, Eq.(7.12.1)]
).

Combining (
3.29
) with the asymptotics (
3.30
), (
3.31
) and (
3.35
) and using that
z,w
are bounded (but
\nu>0
), we obtain the desired expansion (
3.28
) for
E_{N}^{2}
. Choosing
{\varepsilon}>7\nu
completes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Outside the bulk

This subsection is devoted to proving the last statement of Proposition
3.1
. Again, this is an immediate consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert x_{0}\rvert>\sqrt{2\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
. Then there exists a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}
of
x_{0}
and an
{\varepsilon}>0
such that the following estimates hold uniformly for
\xi,\omega\in U
:

E_{N}^{1}(\xi,\omega)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad E_{N}^{2}(\xi,\omega)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}).

In the proof of Lemma
3.8
we shall use an estimate for Hermite polynomials.

Lemma 3.9
.

For all
\tau\in(0,1)
,
0\leq l\leq N-1
and
\lvert\omega_{0}\rvert\geq\sqrt{2\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}}
, we have

(3.36)

\Big|\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega_{0}\Big)\Big|\leq\Big|\frac{(\tau/2)^{l+1}}{(2l+2)!!}H_{2l+2}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}\omega_{0}\Big)\Big|.

The proof of this lemma is given in Appendix
A.2
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

The proof is merely same as that of Lemma
3.4
except the fact that in the representation (
3.5
), we choose
\xi_{0}
,
\omega_{0}
outside of the droplet.
In particular, the estimates of the terms involving the derivatives of
E_{N}^{1}
or
E_{N}^{2}
are the same as those in Lemma
3.4
. Thus all we need to analyse are the initial values.

Let us choose a compact subset
V
of
K^{\mathsf{c}}_{2}
.
For
E_{N}^{1}
, it again follows from
[
30
, Lemma D.4]
that for an
{\varepsilon}>0,

E_{N}^{1}(\bar{\omega},\omega)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),\qquad(\omega\in V).

It remains to analyse
E_{N}^{2}
in (
2.12
).
For the
\xi
-dependent part of
E_{N}^{2}
, the estimates above work, but the
\omega
-dependent part of
E_{N}^{2}
requires special care.
Let us choose
\omega_{0}=x_{0}
.
Then by combining Lemma
3.9
and the asymptotics (
3.13
), we have

(3.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big|e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}N(1-\tau)x_{0}^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}x_{0}\Big)\Big|&\leq e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}N(1-\tau)x_{0}^{2}}N\frac{(\tau/2)^{N}}{(2N)!!}\Big|H_{2N}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}x_{0}\Big)\Big|\\
&=e^{-N\operatorname{Re}[\frac{1}{2}(1-\tau)x_{0}^{2}-2g(x_{0})+\frac{l}{2}]}\sqrt{\tfrac{(2N-1)!!}{(2N)!!\,N}}\,\mathcal{O}(N^{1+\frac{5}{12}}).\end{split}

Since
x_{0}
is real, we have

\mathrm{Re}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\tau)x_{0}^{2}-2g(x_{0})+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(x_{0})>{\varepsilon}.

Then by Stirling’s formula, we obtain

\exp\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}N(1-\tau)x_{0}^{2}\Big)\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{(\tau/2)^{l}}{(2l)!!}H_{2l}\Big(\sqrt{N\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}}x_{0}\Big)=\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-N{\varepsilon}}),

which leads to

E_{N}^{2}(\xi,x_{0})=2\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\,\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{1}{6}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}N{\varepsilon}})\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-N{\varepsilon}})=\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{5}{6}}e^{-\frac{3}{2}N{\varepsilon}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.5.
Proof of the main theorem

In this subsection, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
By Corollary
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
, it remains to solve the resulting differential equations. For this, we first need the following lemma, which readily follows from the basic theory of ordinary differential equations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
a,r_{N}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be analytic functions,
f_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
let
f_{N}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be the solution to

f_{N}^{\prime}=a\,f_{N}+r_{N},\qquad f_{N}(z_{0})=f_{0}.

If
r_{N}\to r
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, then the limit
f(z):=\lim_{N\to\infty}f_{N}(z)
exists for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
, is analytic and satisfies

f^{\prime}=a\,f+r,\qquad f(z_{0})=f_{0}.

Moreover suppose that as
N\to\infty
, the inhomogeneous term
r_{N}
has the asymptotic expansion

r_{N}=r+r^{(\gamma)}/N^{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(1/N^{\widetilde{\gamma}}),\qquad(\widetilde{\gamma}>\gamma),

where
\mathcal{O}(1/N^{\widetilde{\gamma}})
-term is uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
Then

f_{N}=f+f^{(\gamma)}/N^{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(1/N^{\widetilde{\gamma}})

also holds uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
and the subleading term
f^{(\gamma)}
satisfies

f^{(\gamma)\prime}=a\,f^{(\gamma)}+r^{(\gamma)},\qquad f^{(\gamma)}(z_{0})=0.

Proof.

Recall that the solution of our linear inhomogeneous differential equation is given by the variation-of-constants formula

f_{N}(z)=U(z,z_{0})f_{0}+\int_{z_{0}}^{z}U(z,t)r_{N}(t)\,dt,\qquad U(z,t):=\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{z}a(s)\,ds\Big).

For any compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}
, there exists
\rho_{K}>0
such that

\lvert r(z)+r^{(\gamma)}(z)/N^{\gamma}-r_{N}(z)\rvert\leq\rho_{K}/N^{\widetilde{\gamma}}.

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}.

Set
f(z)=U(z,z_{0})f_{0}+\int_{z_{0}}^{z}U(z,t)r(t)\,dt
and
f^{(\gamma)}(z)=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}U(z,t)r^{(\gamma)}(t)\,dt
.
Then one can show that the following inequality holds for all
z\in K
:

\begin{split}\lvert f(z)+f^{(\gamma)}(z)/N^{\gamma}-f_{N}(z)\rvert&\leq\lvert z-z_{0}\rvert\cdot\max_{t\in K}\lvert U(z,t)\rvert\cdot\max_{t\in K}\lvert r(t)+r^{(\gamma)}(t)/N^{\gamma}-r_{N}(t)\rvert\\
&\leq\operatorname{diam}(K)\,e^{A\cdot\operatorname{diam}(K)}\rho_{K}/N^{\widetilde{\gamma}},\end{split}

where
A:=\max_{s\in K}\operatorname{Re}a(s)<\infty
.
Hence
f_{N}
has the same rate of convergence as
r_{N}
.
∎

Using Lemma
3.10
with
\gamma=1/2
and
\widetilde{\gamma}=1
, we can now derive the large-
N
asymptotics of
\widehat{\kappa}_{N}(z,w)
.
For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we recall a notion of the equivalent kernels. (See also
[
4
]
.)

Remark
.

(Equivalent kernels)
Consider a
2k\times 2k
skew-symmetric matrix
A=(a_{j,l})_{j,l}
and let
g_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
for
1\leq j\leq 2k
.
The new matrix
B=(g_{j}g_{l}a_{j,l})_{j,l}
is again skew-symmetric and we have
{\textup{Pf}}(B)=\prod_{j=1}^{2k}g_{j}\,{\textup{Pf}}(A)
.
Furthermore, if
g_{2l}=1/g_{2l-1}
for
1\leq l\leq k
, then
{\textup{Pf}}(B)={\textup{Pf}}(A)
.
This implies that different kernels can give rise to the same correlation functions.
In particular, we call two pre-kernels
{\bm{\varkappa}}
and
\widetilde{{\bm{\varkappa}}}

equivalent
if there exists a unimodular function
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
with
g(\overline{\zeta})=1/g(\zeta)
such that
\widetilde{{\bm{\varkappa}}}(\zeta,\eta)=g(\zeta)g(\eta){\bm{\varkappa}}(\zeta,\eta)
.
Also, we call the combination
c(\zeta,\eta):=g(\zeta)g(\eta)
a
cocycle
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that by definition (
2.9
), we have
\kappa_{N}=\widehat{\kappa}_{N}/\omega_{N}
.
Note that

e^{-\frac{N}{2}(Q(p+\frac{z}{\sqrt{N\delta}})+Q(p+\frac{w}{\sqrt{N\delta}}))}\frac{1}{\omega_{N}(z,w)}=e^{-\lvert z\rvert^{2}-\lvert w\rvert^{2}+2zw}\,\frac{1}{c_{N}(z,w)},

where the cocycle
c_{N}(z,w)
is given by

(3.38)

c_{N}(z,w)=\exp\Big(-i\sqrt{2N\tfrac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}}\,p\,\operatorname{Im}z-i\sqrt{2N\tfrac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}}\,p\,\operatorname{Im}w+i\tau\operatorname{Im}z^{2}+i\tau\operatorname{Im}w^{2}\Big).

Therefore as
N\to\infty
, the equivalent kernels have the uniform limit

(3.39)

\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{N}(z,w)e^{-\frac{N}{2}(Q(p+\frac{z}{\sqrt{N\delta}})+Q(p+\frac{w}{\sqrt{N\delta}}))}\kappa_{N}(z,w)=e^{-\lvert z\rvert^{2}-\lvert w\rvert^{2}+2zw}\,\widehat{\kappa}(z,w).

By Proposition
3.1
, one can see that for the leading order, the differential equation (
2.13
) coincides with the one in
[
25
]
(for the real bulk case) and in
[
4
]
(for the real edge case). Moreover, it was shown that the kernels (
1.18
) and (
1.20
) are the associated solution in each case. (Here the uniqueness follows from the anti-symmetry of the pre-kernel.)
On the other hand, for the case outside the droplet the differential equation is trivial.
Therefore only the derivation of
\kappa_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}
in (
1.21
) remains.

By Corollary
2.1
, Proposition
3.1
, Lemma
3.10
and (
3.39
), the function

\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w):=e^{-2zw}\,\kappa_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w)

satisfies the differential equation

(3.40)

\partial_{z}\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w)=2(z-w)\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w)+r^{(1/2)}(z,w).

This differential equation can be easily solved by virtue of an integrating factor.
Furthermore, the anti-symmetry
\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w)=-\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(w,z)
determines the solution as

\begin{split}\widehat{\kappa}_{\textup{edge}}^{\mathbb{R},1/2}(z,w)&=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{(z-w)^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\int_{w}^{z}e^{-2t^{2}}\Big[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\tfrac{4}{3}t^{2}-\tfrac{4}{3}tw+\tfrac{2}{3}w^{2}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau})e^{-2w^{2}}-(\tfrac{2}{3}t^{3}-\tfrac{\tau}{1+\tau}t)\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)\Big]\,dt\\
&=\tfrac{1}{12\sqrt{2}}(\tfrac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{(z-w)^{2}}\Big[\Big((2z^{2}+\tfrac{1-2\tau}{1+\tau})e^{-2z^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}w)+2\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\,we^{-2(z^{2}+w^{2})}\Big)-\Big(z\leftrightarrow w\Big)\Big],\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

A.1.
Proof of Lemma
3.6

The proof of Lemma
3.6
merely follows from the computations given in
[
30
]
.

First, we prove (
3.24
).
By definitions (
3.11
), (
3.16
), we have that for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\operatorname{Re}\Big[\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}s^{2}-Tg(s)+\tfrac{l}{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(s).

Then
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
implies the existence of a threshold
\delta>0
such that the approximation

\Omega(s)=2\lvert s-x_{0}\rvert^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\lvert s-x_{0}\rvert^{3})

holds uniformly for
s\in[x_{0}-\delta,x_{0}+\delta]
.
Additionally,
recall that
\Omega(s)\geq 0
for all
s
and
\Omega(s)=0
only for
s
on the boundary of the droplet, i.e.
s=x_{0}
.
Therefore we have an
{\varepsilon}>0
with
\frac{1}{2}\Omega(s)\geq{\varepsilon}
for all
s\in[0,x_{0}-\delta]
.
For
s\in[x_{0}-\delta,x_{0}-\sqrt{2/N}N^{\alpha}]
we can use the quadratic approximation from above to derive

(A.1)

\frac{1}{2}\Omega(s)\geq 2N^{-1+2\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2+3\alpha})\geq N^{-1+2\alpha}.

Here the last inequality holds for large enough
N
if
\alpha<1/2
.

Next, we show the second assertion (
3.25
).
By
[
30
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have the expansion

Tg\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)=\frac{1-\tau}{2}x_{0}^{2}+\frac{l}{2}+(1-\tau)x_{0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}z-\frac{1+\tau}{N}z^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\varkappa_{T}}{3}\frac{z^{3}}{N^{3/2}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{z^{4}}{N^{2}}\Big).

Thus we obtain

\exp\Big(-N\Big[-\tfrac{1-\tau}{2}\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)^{2}+Tg\Big(x_{0}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{N}}z\Big)-\frac{l}{2}\Big]\Big)=e^{-2z^{2}}\exp\Big[\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\varkappa_{T}\tfrac{z^{3}}{\sqrt{N}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\tfrac{z^{4}}{N}\Big)\Big],

which leads to (
3.25
).

For the last assertion (
3.26
), we use Taylor expansion around
x_{0}
analogous to
[
30
, Lemma 3.5]
.
Note that for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(A.2)

\psi(x_{0})=1,\qquad\psi^{\prime}(x_{0})=\sqrt{\frac{1+\tau}{T(1-\tau)}},\qquad\psi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=-\frac{2\tau(1+\tau)}{T(1-\tau)^{2}}.

By direct computations, this gives rise to (
3.26
) using the convention
\varkappa_{T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}(\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau})^{3/2}

A.2.
Proof of Lemma
3.9

It is well known that the zeros of the Hermite polynomial of degree
n
lie in the interval
(-\sqrt{2n+1},\sqrt{2n+1})
, see e.g.
[
34
, Section 18.16]
.
Thus we have excluded all zeros.
Additionally since the Hermite polynomials are positive as
\omega_{0}\to\pm\infty
, we can omit the absolute values.

We simplify the inequality (
3.36
) by multiplying both sides with
(2l)!!\,(2/\tau)^{l}
and applying the three term recurrence relation (
2.6
) to
H_{2l+2}
.
Let us rewrite the argument of the polynomials as

\sqrt{N(1-\tau^{2})/(2\tau)}\,\omega_{0}=(1+\tau)y/\sqrt{\tau}.

Then Lemma
3.9
follows from the fact that for
\tau\in(0,1)
,
0\leq l\leq N-1
and
y\geq\sqrt{N}
,

\Big(1+\frac{2l+1}{2l+2}\tau\Big)H_{2l}\Big(\tfrac{1+\tau}{\sqrt{\tau}}y\Big)\leq\frac{\tau(1+\tau)}{2(l+1)}y\,H_{2l+1}\Big(\tfrac{1+\tau}{\sqrt{\tau}}y\Big).

Let us show the slightly stricter inequality

(A.3)

H_{2l}\Big(\tfrac{1+\tau}{\sqrt{\tau}}y\Big)\leq\frac{\tau}{2(l+1)}y\,H_{2l+1}\Big(\tfrac{1+\tau}{\sqrt{\tau}}y\Big)

by an induction argument.
For
l=0
, the inequality (
A.3
) is obvious since
H_{0}(x)=1
and
H_{1}(x)=2x
.

From now on, we shall write
a_{l}=\tau y/(2l+2)
and
x=(1+\tau)y/\sqrt{\tau}
to lighten the notations.
Due to the three term recurrence relation (
2.6
), it suffices to show that

(A.4)

H_{2l}(x)\leq a_{l}H_{2l+1}(x)=a_{l}\Big[2xH_{2l}(x)-4lH_{2l-1}(x)\Big].

Again, by the three term recurrence relation, this is equivalent to

(A.5)

H_{2l-2}(x)\leq\frac{2a_{l}(x^{2}-l)-x}{(2l-1)(2a_{l}x-1)}H_{2l-1}(x).

By the induction hypothesis, we have
H_{2l-2}(x)\leq a_{l-1}H_{2l-1}(x)
.
Therefore to complete the induction step it is enough to show that

(A.6)

a_{l-1}\leq\frac{2a_{l}(x^{2}-l)-x}{(2l-1)(2a_{l}x-1)},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\frac{\sqrt{\tau}}{2l}\leq\frac{(1+\tau)^{2}y^{2}-\tau l-(1+\tau)(l+1)}{\sqrt{\tau}(2l-1)\big((1+\tau)y^{2}-(l+1)\big)}.

This inequality (
A.6
) follows from the straightforward computations using elementary algebra and we leave it to the interested readers.

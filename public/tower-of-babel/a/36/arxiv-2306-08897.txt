Minima Nonblockers and Blocked Sets of a Continuum Journal: a journal.
C. Piceno, H. Villanueva

Abstract
Given a continuum X X and an element x ∈ X x\in X , π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) is the smallest set that contains x x and does not block singletons, and B ⁡ ( x ) B(x) is the set of all elements blocked by x {x} . We prove that for each x ∈ X x\in X , B ⁡ ( x ) B(x) is connected, B ⁡ ( x ) ⊂ π ⁡ ( x ) B(x)\subset\pi(x) , and that if B ⁡ ( x ) B(x) is closed, then B ⁡ ( x ) = π ⁡ ( x ) B(x)=\pi(x) . Among other results, we prove that if X X is a Kelley continuum and π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) is proper, then B ⁡ ( x ) = π ⁡ ( x ) B(x)=\pi(x) . Finally, we prove that for a certain class of dendroids, the family of minima non-blockers coincides with the family of connected non-blockers.

Minima nonblockers and blocked sets

In this section we study the blocked sets and their relation with minima nonblockers. We start with the following easy result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a continuum and
x\in X
such that
B(x)
is closed.
Then,
B(x)=\pi(x)
.

Proof.

By Observation
2.1
, it suffices to show that
\pi(x)\subset B(x)
. It is clear that
B(x)
does not block any element outside of
B(x)
. Since
B(x)
is closed, we have that
x\in B(x)\in\mathcal{NB(F}_{1}(X))
, and therefore
\pi(x)\subset B(x)
.
∎

It is not always true that
B(x)
is closed, as Example
3.10
shows; however,
B(x)
is always connected.

Theorem 3.2
.

For every continuum
X
and every
x\in X
,
B(x)
is connected.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and suppose that
B(x)
is not connected. Let
D
be the component of
B(x)
containing
x
and let
C
be a component of
B(x)
such that
C\neq D
. If
{\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)\cup D
is connected, then there exists
y\in{\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)-B(x)
. Therefore, there exists an order arc
\alpha_{1}:[0,1]\to C(X)
such that
\alpha_{1}(0)=\{y\}
and
x\notin\alpha_{1}(t)
for all
t<1
. Now, if
c\in C
, by Theorem 1.8 of
[
9
]
, there exists an order arc
\alpha_{2}:[0,1]\to C(X)
such that
\alpha_{2}(0)=\{c\}
and
\alpha_{2}(1)={\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)
. Hence,

\alpha(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{2}(t),&\text{ if }t\in[0,1]\\
\alpha_{1}(t-1)\cup{\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right),&\text{ if }t\in[1,2]\end{array}\right.

is a path from
c
to
X
avoiding
x
, a contradiction.
On the other hand, suppose that
{\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)\cup D
is not connected.
Then, there exists an open set
U
such that
{\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)\subset U
and

.
Now, Theorem 1.8 of
[
9
]
implies there exists an order arc
\gamma:[0,1]\to C(X)
such that
\gamma(0)={\operatorname{cl}}_{X}\!\left(C\right)
and
\gamma(1)=X
. By continuity, we can take

such that
\gamma(t)\subset U
. Thus, there exists

such that

. Since

, we can construct a path as above and conclude that

does not block

. Again, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.3

Let

\Lambda_{X}
is a decomposition which is neither upper nor lower semi-continuous.

4
Minima nonblockers in dendroids

As we know, all minima nonblockers of a continuum
X
are connected. An interesting question is when the set of connected nonblockers coincides with the set of minima nonblockers. Corollary
3.15
give us sufficient conditions to ensure that
\Lambda_{X}=\mathcal{NB(F}_{1}(X))
. It is clear that if either
X
is a continuum such that
\mathcal{S}(X)
is finite or
X
is a finite graph, then
\Lambda_{X}=\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))
. In this section, we study the case when
X
is a dendroid such that
X-\mathcal{S}_{1}(X)\neq\emptyset
.

We say that a continuum
X
is
unicoherent
if
A\cap B
is connected for each subcontinua
A
and
B
such that
A\cup B=X
; and
X
is said to be
hereditarily unicoherent
if every subcontinuum of
X
is unicoherent.
A
dendroid
is an arcwise connected hereditarily unicoherent continuum.
A point
p
in a dendroid
D
is called a
center
for
D
if there exist two points
b
and
c
in
D
, called
basin points
, such that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a continuum
C
containing
p
of diameter less than
\epsilon
, called a
bottleneck
, and there are open sets
U
and
V
, containing
b
and
c
, respectively, called
basins
, such that every arc from
U
to
V
intersects
C
. In this case, we also say that
p
is a
bc
-
center
.
A point
p
in a dendroid
D
is a
strong center
if there exist open sets
U
and
V
such that every arc in
D
from
U
to
V
contains
p
.

Note 4.1
.

If
X
is a dendroid and
A\in 2^{X}
, then
A\in\mathcal{NB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
if and only if
A
has empty interior and each arc component of
X-A
is dense in
X
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a dendroid such that
X-\mathcal{S}_{1}(X)\neq\emptyset
and
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, then
A\in\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))
if and only if
A
has empty interior and
X-A
is arc-connected.

Proof.

It is clear that if
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
has empty interior and
X-A
is arc-connected, then
A\in\mathcal{CNB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
.

Now, let
A\in\mathcal{NB(F}_{1}(X))
.
By Theorem 3.6 of
[
7
]
, Theorem 1 of
[
11
]
and Theorem 3.2 of
[
3
]
, we have that there exists one center
o\in X-A
. Let
b
and
c
be two points of
X
such that
o
is a
bc
-center. Let
C\in C(X)
a bottleneck such that
o\in C
and
C\cap A=\emptyset
. Let
U
and
V
be basins for
b
and
c
respectively. Since
{\operatorname{int}}_{X}\!\left(A\right)=\emptyset
, we have that
U-A\neq\emptyset
and
V-A\neq\emptyset
. Hence, if
x\in X-A
and
\alpha
is an order arc
\alpha:[0,1]\to C(X)
from
\{x\}
to
X
avoiding
A
, there exists
t\in[0,1)
such that
\alpha(t)\cap(U-A)\neq\emptyset
and
\alpha(t)\cap(V-A)\neq\emptyset
. Since
\alpha(t)\in C(X)
,
\alpha(t)
is arc-connected and therefore
\alpha(t)\cap C\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
X-A
is arc-connected.
∎

Since no strong center is a shore point, by Theorem
4.2
we have the next corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

Given a dendroid
X
with a strong center and
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, then
A\in\mathcal{CNB}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(X))
if and only if
A
has with empty interior and
X-A
is arc-connected.

Moreover, by
[
7
, Theorem 3.11]
, every planar dendroid contains a single point bottleneck and no point bottleneck is a shore point. Hence, Theorem
4.2
implies the following.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
X
is a planar dendroid and
A\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, then,
A\in\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))
if and only if
A
has empty interior and
X-A
is arc-connected.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a dendroid such that
X-\mathcal{S}_{1}(X)\neq\emptyset
.
Then,
\Lambda_{X}=\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))\subset\Lambda_{X}
. Let
A\in\mathcal{CNB(F}_{1}(X))
. Let
p\in A
,
q\in X-A
and
R
the arc from
p
to
q
. By compactness, we can take
a\in R
such that
aq\cap A=\{a\}
. If
\pi(a)\neq A
, we can take
s\in A-\pi(a)
. Since
\pi(a)
does not block
s
there exists a continuum
B
such that
s\in B
and
B\cap(X-A)\neq\emptyset
,
B\cap\pi(A)=\emptyset
. By Theorem
4.2
, there exists an arc from
q
to any point of
B-A
avoiding
A
. Hence, there is an arc from
s
to
q
avoiding
\pi(a)
. On the other hand, we can construct an arc from
s
to
q
passing trough
a
. This implies that
X
contains a closed curve, which is a contradiction. Therefore
\pi(a)=A
.
∎

Evaluating ChatGPT-4 Vision on Brazil’s National Undergraduate Computer Science Exam Journal: TOCE CCS: Applied computing E-learning CCS: Computing methodologies Natural language processing CCS: Computing methodologies Computer vision
Nabor C. Mendonça

Abstract
. The recent integration of visual capabilities into Large Language Models (LLMs) has the potential to play a pivotal role in science and technology education, where visual elements such as diagrams, charts, and tables are commonly used to improve the learning experience. This study investigates the performance of ChatGPT-4 Vision, OpenAI’s most advanced visual model at the time the study was conducted, on the Bachelor in Computer Science section of Brazil’s 2021 National Undergraduate Exam (ENADE). By presenting the model with the exam’s open and multiple-choice questions in their original image format and allowing for reassessment in response to differing answer keys, we were able to evaluate the model’s reasoning and self-reflecting capabilities in a large-scale academic assessment involving textual and visual content. ChatGPT-4 Vision significantly outperformed the average exam participant, positioning itself within the top 10 best score percentile. While it excelled in questions that incorporated visual elements, it also encountered challenges with question interpretation, logical reasoning, and visual acuity. A positive correlation between the model’s performance in multiple-choice questions and the performance distribution of the human participants suggests multimodal LLMs can provide a useful tool for question testing and refinement. However, the involvement of an independent expert panel to review cases of disagreement between the model and the answer key revealed some poorly constructed questions containing vague or ambiguous statements, calling attention to the critical need for improved question design in future exams. Our findings suggest that while ChatGPT-4 Vision shows promise in multimodal academic evaluations, human oversight remains crucial for verifying the model’s accuracy and ensuring the fairness of high-stakes educational exams. The paper’s research materials are publicly available at https://github.com/nabormendonca/gpt-4v-enade-cs-2021 .



1.
Introduction

The advent of large language models (LLMs), such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT
(
OpenAI 2023a
)
and Google’s Gemini
(
Google 2023
)
, has ushered in a new era in natural language processing. These models exhibit human-like performance in tasks requiring complex reasoning, including abstract thinking, problem-solving across diverse knowledge domains, coding, and comprehending human emotions and motives
(
Zhao et al. 2023b
;
Bubeck et al. 2023
)
. The recent integration of visual capabilities into these models
(
OpenAI 2023b
)
has the potential to play a pivotal role in advancing their use in educational settings, particularly in the science and technology fields, where visual elements such as diagrams, charts, and tables are commonly used to offer a more comprehensive and engaging learning experience
(
Evagorou et al. 2015
)
.

Using LLMs like ChatGPT and Gemini in education presents novel opportunities and challenges for students, educators, and standardized exam creators
(
Li et al. 2023
;
Kasneci et al. 2023
)
. These models offer students innovative tools for exam preparation, potentially reshaping study habits and learning methodologies
(
Dai et al. 2023
)
. For educators, LLMs serve as a resource to enrich teaching strategies and help tailor exam content to better suit learning outcomes
(
Zhou et al. 2024
)
. Meanwhile, standardized exam creators could utilize these AI tools to refine their questions, ensuring clarity, fairness, and appropriate difficulty levels. However, the use of these models also introduces concerns around accuracy, reliability, and potential for misuse, highlighting the need for careful evaluation of visual LLMs in academic settings
(
Meyer et al. 2023
;
Zhou et al. 2024
)
.

This study evaluates the accuracy of ChatGPT-4 Vision
(
OpenAI 2023c
)
, OpenAI’s most advanced visual model at the time the study was conducted, in answering the Bachelor in Computer Science (BCS) section of Brazil’s 2021 National Undergraduate Exam (ENADE)
(
Inep 2023
)
. Unlike most prior evaluations of ChatGPT and other LLMs in academic assessments in Computer Science (e.g.,
(
Joshi et al. 2023
;
Richards et al. 2024
;
Jalil et al. 2023
;
Bordt and von Luxburg 2023
)
) and other fields (e.g.,
(
Nori et al. 2023
;
Frenkel and Emara 2023
;
Pursnani et al. 2023
)
), we presented all ENADE BCS exam questions to ChatGPT-4 Vision in their original image formats as seen by human participants. This method allowed a comprehensive evaluation of the model’s multimodal reasoning abilities, challenging its linguistic and visual interpretation capabilities in a particular education domain.

Additionally, we explored the model’s capacity for self-reflection by prompting it to reassess its initial responses when presented with differing answer keys. When ChatGPT-4 Vision’s answers diverged from the answer keys after reassessment, we assembled an independent panel of computer science researchers from various Brazilian universities to evaluate the accuracy of the model’s responses. This additional layer of expert review provided insights into the model’s reasoning accuracy and the exam questions’ quality regarding clarity and objectivity.

We further correlated the model’s performance with the difficulty and discrimination levels of the multiple-choice questions, as reported by ENADE organizers. This analysis aimed to provide insights into how the model’s proficiency compared to that of the human participants, offering a more nuanced understanding of the model’s capabilities and limitations in a real-world academic assessment context.

Our study aims to answer the following quantitative research questions:

RQ1::

How well does ChatGPT-4 Vision perform in answering ENADE 2021 BCS open questions?

RQ2::

How well does ChatGPT-4 Vision perform in answering ENADE 2021 BCS multiple-choice questions?

RQ3::

How often did ChatGPT-4 change or maintain its response when confronted with a disagreeing official answer?

RQ4::

What is ChatGPT-4 Vision’s ENADE 2021 BCS exam final score, and how does it compare with the exam participants?

In addition, we examined the reasoning, accuracy, and reflective analysis of ChatGPT-4 Vision, and the independent expert assessments to answer the following qualitative research question:

RQ5::

What are the main challenges ChatGPT-4 Vision faced in answering ENADE 2021 BCS open and multiple-choice questions?

In summary, our contributions are as follows:

•

We have evaluated ChatGPT-4 Vision’s multimodal reasoning abilities on the ENADE 2021 BCS exam, a large-scale computer science academic assessment test involving textual and visual content, providing all questions in image format.

•

We have explored ChatGPT-4 Vision’s capacity for self-reflection by allowing it to reassess its initial responses when confronted with differing answer keys.

•

We have correlated ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance with the difficulty and discrimination levels of the exam’s multiple-choice questions, offering insights into how the model’s proficiency compares to that of the human participants.

•

We have involved an independent panel of computer science researchers from various Brazilian universities to evaluate the accuracy of ChatGPT-4 Vision’s responses that conflicted with the official answer, providing further insights into the model’s limitations and the exam questions’ design quality.

Overall, our results reveal ChatGPT-4 Vision’s impressive yet limited performance in reasoning about multimodal content in computer science academic assessments. In particular, ChatGPT-4 Vision significantly outperformed the average exam participant, achieving the top 10 best score percentile. However, the model faced several challenges with question interpretation, logical reasoning, and visual acuity. These findings underscore the continued need for human oversight in verifying LLMs’s accuracy in solving complex reasoning tasks. Moreover, some disagreements between the model, the expert panel, and the official answer raise concerns about the quality of some exam questions. Given the significant impact of ENADE on Brazil’s undergraduate academic rankings, our findings call attention to the critical need for improved question design and quality control in future ENADE exams to ensure a fair educational assessment process.

To facilitate the replication and extension of our study, we have made our research materials publicly available at
https://github.com/nabormendonca/gpt-4v-enade-cs-2021
. This repository contains the exam questions, response standards, answer keys, ChatGPT-4’s full conversations, and experts’ evaluations, in Portuguese and English.

Related Work

Our work touches on several areas of related research, including LLM evaluations in general logical reasoning tasks
(
Bubeck et al. 2023
;

;
Mao et al. 2024
;
Espejel et al. 2023
)
, using LLMs in educational assessments in computer science
(
Joshi et al. 2024
;
Richards et al. 2024
;
Jalil et al. 2023
;
Bordt and von Luxburg 2023
;
Mahon et al. 2023
)
and other domains
(
Nori et al. 2023
;
Toyama et al. 2023
;
Frenkel and Emara 2023
;
Pursnani et al. 2023
)
, and recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs)
(
Zhang et al. 2024a
;
Mayne 2024
;
Wu et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2024b
)
. Also of interest are related works that evaluated LLMs’ effectiveness in answering Brazil’s ENEM
(
Nunes et al. 2023
;
Pires et al. 2023
;
Santos and Campelo 2023
)
—a national multidisciplinary high-school-level exam widely used as an admission test in Brazilian universities—and ENADE
(
Almeida et al. 2024
)
. We briefly discuss these studies below to provide a comprehensive backdrop against which the contributions of our work can be contextualized and compared.

2.1.
LLMs Evaluation in Logical Reasoning

Bubeck et al. 2023
showed that GPT-4 exhibited significant logical reasoning capabilities in various domains, including professional and academic benchmarks, where it performs at the human level. The study also highlighted the model’s struggles with planning and creative problem-solving due to its reliance on a sequential prediction paradigm. Similarly,
Liu et al. 2023
focused on logical reasoning in reading comprehension and natural language inference, finding that despite strong performances on known datasets, GPT-4’s effectiveness diminishes with new, out-of-distribution data. This observation points to a crucial gap in GPT-4’s capacity to handle unfamiliar data types and logical inferences beyond its training data.

Mao et al. 2024
provided a more holistic view by surveying assessments across various capabilities of ChatGPT and GPT-4, indicating strong language understanding and generation skills but also limitations in tasks requiring multi-step reasoning and domain-specific knowledge.
Finally,
Espejel et al. 2023
compared reasoning abilities across LLMs including GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and BARD, finding that while GPT-4 generally outperforms its predecessors and competitors, all models show limitations in inductive, mathematical, and multi-step reasoning tasks.

Building on these foundational studies, our study further explores the capabilities of ChatGPT-4 Vision by evaluating its performance on the ENADE 2021 BCS exam, which includes complex reasoning tasks in a multimodal context. This extends the current understanding of LLMs’ capabilities by testing them on more challenging, real-world academic assessments.

2.2.
LLMs Evaluation in Computer Science Assessments

Joshi et al. 2024
evaluated the performance of ChatGPT-3.5 across a variety of computer science exercises, including true/false, multiple-choice, short and long answers, and coding-related questions. The study demonstrated ChatGPT-3.5’s inconsistent accuracy across subjects and question types, particularly in numerical-based problems. Similarly,
Richards et al. 2024
provide an in-depth analysis of ChatGPT-3.5’s performance in a university computer science curriculum. The study involved a blind marking exercise in which the ChatGPT-generated answers were directly compared with those of human students. The findings showed that barring two outliers, every ChatGPT-3.5 answer achieved at least a passing grade. The study also highlights that while ChatGPT-3.5 is capable of producing adequate solutions in most cases, its performance varies across subjects and question types, with ChatGPT-3.5’s effectiveness diminishing as the complexity of the questions increases.

Jalil et al. 2023
explored ChatGPT-3.5’s performance in answering 31 questions from a popular software testing textbook. The study found that ChatGPT-3.5 provided correct or partially correct answers and explanations for over half of the questions tested, showcasing the model’s potential as an educational tool. However, the study also noted ChatGPT’s inconsistent accuracy and poor self-assessment, especially in questions that involved both coding and conceptual understanding.

Bordt and von Luxburg 2023
investigated ChatGPT-3.5 and 4’s performance in an undergraduate computer science exam focusing on algorithms and data structures. ChatGPT-3.5 narrowly passed the exam with 20.5 out of 40 points, a result that was slightly below the performance of the average student. ChatGPT-4 scored about 17% higher, reaching the average student’s level. The study underlined the model’s challenges with exercises requiring a detailed understanding of algorithmic processes, particularly involving structured output that was not in pseudo-code format.
Finally,
Mahon et al. 2023
investigated the capabilities of ChatGPT-4 (no vision) on the UK A-Level and Irish Leaving Certificate computer science examinations. They found that ChatGPT-4 achieved high marks on both exams but struggled with questions involving symbols or images and cascading questions where initial inaccuracies led to further errors. This work underscores the strengths and weaknesses of generative AI in educational assessments and has implications for exam design and marking schemes.

Our research builds on these findings by not only evaluating ChatGPT-4 Vision on a comprehensive academic assessment in Computer Science but also examining its performance relative to question difficulty and discrimination levels. This provides a more nuanced understanding of the model’s capabilities and limitations compared to the human participants, which is extremely important in educational contexts.

2.3.
LLMs Evaluation in Other Education Domains

Nori et al. 2023
presents a comprehensive evaluation of ChatGPT-4’s performance on the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) and the MultiMedQA medical benchmark. The study found that GPT-4, without any specialized prompt crafting, surpassed the passing score on the USMLE by over 20 points and outperformed previous models like ChatGPT-3.5 and specialized medical models. The study also explored ChatGPT-4’s calibration, crucial in high-stakes domains like medicine, and found it to be significantly better than its predecessor. Similarly,
Toyama et al. 2023
investigated the performance of ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4, and Bard on the official board examination of the Japan Radiology Society. The study found that ChatGPT-4 significantly outperformed ChatGPT-3.5 and Bard by over 20 percentage points, achieving an accuracy of 65% in the exam’s multiple-choice questions. Both studies underscore the potential utility of AI models in assisting professionals in high-stakes domains like medicine, while also emphasizing the need for cautious application due to the models’ limitations in accuracy and safety-critical tasks.

In the field of engineering,
Frenkel and Emara 2023
examined ChatGPT-3.5 and 4’s performance on the United States Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) Mechanical Engineering Exam. The study found that ChatGPT-4 significantly outperforms ChatGPT-3.5, achieving 76% correct answers compared to 51% by GPT-3.5. A similar study was conducted by
Pursnani et al. 2023
where they evaluated GPT-3.5-Legacy, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and GPT-4’s performance across different sections of the FE Environmental Exam. The study also found that GPT-4 outperformed GPT-3.5-Legacy and GPT-3.5-Turbo by a significant margin, achieving 75% accuracy with slightly refined prompts. However, both studies confirmed previous findings about ChatGPT’s error types and pitfalls, noting its inconsistency across question types and subjects and its tendency to confidently produce incorrect answers.

Our study contributes to this body of work by evaluating ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance on a high-stakes academic exam (ENADE), involving both textual and visual content. By allowing the model to reassess its responses with differing answer keys, we explore its self-reflective capabilities, a novel aspect not covered in previous education-centered studies.

2.4.
Recent Advances in VLMs

Zhang et al. 2024a
conducted a comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art VLMs, including GPT-4 Vision, on tasks requiring visual deductive reasoning using Raven’s Progressive Matrices (RPMs). Their findings revealed significant challenges in models’ abilities to perform multi-hop relational and deductive reasoning based solely on visual cues, highlighting perception issues and difficulties in understanding abstract patterns.
Mayne 2024
proposed improvements to GPT-4 Vision’s performance on visual reasoning tasks through enhanced prompt engineering techniques. By incorporating specific instructions in prompts, such as paying attention to details and describing items with positional specificity, Mayne demonstrated substantial improvements in the model’s accuracy on visual puzzles.
Wu et al. 2024
introduced the Visualization-of-Thought prompting technique, which aims to elicit spatial reasoning in LLMs by visualizing their reasoning traces. This technique enhances spatial reasoning tasks, such as natural language navigation, visual navigation, and visual tiling, by guiding subsequent reasoning steps through mental image visualization. Finally,
Zhang et al. 2024b
presented
MathVerse
, a specialized benchmark designed to evaluate the visual mathematical reasoning skills of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
MathVerse
includes a diverse set of 2,612 visual math problems across three primary areas: plane geometry, solid geometry, and functions. The study found that most existing MLLMs struggle to understand math diagrams and often rely more on textual questions than visual inputs, emphasizing the need for better visual encoding capabilities.

Our work differs from these related studies in several ways but also stands to benefit significantly from their insights and methodologies. While
Zhang et al. 2024a
provide a comprehensive evaluation of VLMs on visual deductive reasoning tasks, our focus is on assessing ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance in the context of the ENADE exam, which involves a broader range of multimodal reasoning tasks beyond visual deductive reasoning. The visual-enhanced prompt engineering techniques proposed by
Mayne 2024
and
Wu et al. 2024
could be particularly beneficial in improving ChatGPT-4 Vision’s handling of spatial reasoning tasks within the ENADE exam, guiding the model through intermediate steps via mental image visualization. Furthermore, our work could benefit from the development of a
MathVerse
-like fine-grained multimodal benchmark based on ENADE questions with visual content to better evaluate and understand ChatGPT-4 Vision’s capabilities in real-world educational assessments.

2.5.
LLMs Evaluation in Brazil’s ENEM and ENADE

Nunes et al. 2023
studied GPT-3.5 and GPT-4’s performance on answering multiple-choice ENEM questions, using different prompt strategies, including Chain-of-Thought (CoT). GPT-4, especially with CoT, achieved up to 87% accuracy on the 2022 exam, significantly outperforming GPT-3.5.

Pires et al. 2023
also examined the performance of ChatGPT-3.5 and 4, the latter with and without Vision capabilities, on the 2022 and 2023 editions of ENEM. The study showed a clear advancement in performance from ChatGPT-3.5 to ChatGPT-4, with GPT-4 models significantly outperforming their predecessors. Specifically, ChatGPT-4 (no Vision) and ChatGPT-4 Vision achieved accuracies of about 73% and 79% in the 2022 exam, and 80% and 86% in the 2023 exam, respectively. Interestingly, when provided with human-generated captions transcribing the questions’ visual content, ChatGPT-4 with no Vision capabilities improved its accuracies to 81% and 91% in the 2022 and 2023 exams, respectively, outperforming ChatGPT-4 Vision with images and no captions.

Santos and Campelo 2023
investigated the performance of quantized open-source LLaMA-based models on a custom-built benchmark containing over one thousand ENEM questions, showing that the best models achieved an accuracy of 46% for the original texts of the Portuguese questions and 49% on their English translations. Despite the modest computational requirements of these open-source models, allowing them to run on low-end hardware, their limited accuracy compared to that of closed models indicates significant room for improvement.
Finally, in a recent study,
Almeida et al. 2024
introduced Sabiá-2, a new family of LLMs specifically trained on Portuguese texts and evaluated its performance on a diverse range of exams, including a multidisciplinary ENADE benchmark containing only multiple-choice questions with no images from the 2022 and 2023 exams. The study revealed that Sabiá-2, particularly the Sabiá-2 Medium model, matched or surpassed GPT-4 Turbo’s performance in several ENADE disciplines and significantly outperformed GPT-3.5 in all but one discipline. The model’s lowest scores were achieved in domains related to engineering and economics, with a particularly lower score compared to GPT-4 Turbo in Computer Engineering.

Our work extends this area of research by evaluating ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance on the ENADE BCS exam, which includes both open and multiple-choice multimodal questions. Additionally, by involving an independent panel of computer science researchers, we provide a more rigorous assessment of the model’s accuracy and limitations in answering ENADE questions, in addition to the questions’ overall quality.

3.
Background

3.1.
ENADE

Established in 2004 by the Brazilian Ministry of Education, the
Exame Nacional de Desempenho dos Estudantes
(ENADE) is a federal government exam that assesses the academic performance of undergraduate students in Brazil, fostering an environment of continuous improvement in educational standards
(
Inep 2023
)
. ENADE conducts annual assessments targeting students who are nearing the completion of their academic programs. The exam committee defines the knowledge areas to be evaluated each year, with individual areas having their graduating students assessed every three years.

The exam covers various disciplines—from computer science and mathematics to history and philosophy—and encompasses bachelor, teaching, and technology degrees. The exam consists of two primary components: the first, the
general education component
, common across various disciplines, assesses competencies and skills developed by students and their general knowledge relating to Brazilian and global realities. The second component, the
specific knowledge component
, is tailored to each discipline, evaluating the mastery of knowledge and skills expected for the discipline’s professional profile
(
Inep 2023
)
. Our study focuses on the specific knowledge component of the ENADE 2021 BCS exam
(
Inep 2022
)
.

3.2.
2021 Bachelor in Computer Science Exam

ENADE’s BCS exam evaluates core computer science principles and concepts such as algorithms, programming, operating systems, software engineering, artificial intelligence, and distributed systems. At the time of writing, the BCS exam had last been conducted in November 2021 with 10,119 participants from over 300 institutions across all five regions of Brazil
(
Inep 2022
)
.

The facility level classifies the percentage of correct answers for each multiple-choice question into a hierarchy of levels, from Very Easy (
\geq 0.86

(

. A higher point-biserial correlation for a question indicates effective discrimination between students who score high overall and those who score low. Questions with very low or negative point-biserial correlation values may be seen as excessively difficult or potentially flawed, as they do not align with the general performance trends of the examinees and could be considered for exclusion from scoring.

Lastly, the mean and median scores for the final score were 36.7 and 35.8, respectively, with a standard deviation of 15.8. These numbers indicate that the exam’s open questions were particularly challenging for the participants. In contrast, the multiple-choice questions were relatively more accessible, even though the average score in this section was still below the 50% threshold. This result aligns with the observation that nearly half of the 19 scored multiple-choice questions were classified as Hard or Very Hard, as shown in Table
2
.

4.
Study Method

Our study evaluates ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance on the specific knowledge component of the ENADE 2021 BCS exam. To this end, we provided the model with the exam’s questions in image format by taking a high-resolution screen capture of each question’s page from the exam’s official PDF document. Since the exam’s questions and response standard are in Portuguese, we conducted the evaluation experiment in Portuguese, including the model’s prompts and responses, and the expert assessments of conflicting answers. We translated all materials into English for this paper. Although we have not replicated the entire experiment using the English translations of the questions, our experience with ChatGPT-Vision and its successors is that these OpenAI models are as good at answering questions in Portuguese as they are in English.

4.1.
ChatGPT Versions Used in the Study

We used ChaGPT-4 Vision
(
OpenAI 2023c
)
to answer ENADE’s 2021 BCS exam questions and ChatGPT-4 Turbo
(
OpenAI 2023d
)
, to reflect on ChaGPT-4 Vision’s performance on each question. The reason we have not used ChatGPT-4 Turbo from the start is that it was only released after we completed the question-answering phase of the research using ChatGPT-4 Vision.

ChatGPT-4 Vision, released in September 2023, expands upon the text-based capabilities of ChatGPT-4 by incorporating image analysis and understanding
(
OpenAI 2023c
)
. It features a context window of 32k tokens and an updated knowledge base until January 2022. Since the ENADE 2021 exam was administered on November 14th, 2021, and the exam key and response standard were made publicly available on March 18th, 2022, it is reasonable to assume ChatGPT-4 Vision’s knowledge base did not include any information from the exam.

ChatGPT-4 Turbo, which was launched in November 2023, features a significantly larger context window (128k tokens) compared to GPT-4, allowing it to handle complex and lengthy prompts with greater accuracy and coherence
(
OpenAI 2023d
)
. Additionally, ChatGPT-4 Turbo incorporates Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)
(
Lewis et al. 2020
)
, which integrates external information to enhance the model’s responses. Furthermore, ChatGPT-4 Turbo boasts DALL-E 3 integration for text-to-image generation, text-to-speech capabilities with multiple voice options, and an updated knowledge base extending to April 2023.

4.2.
Evaluation Procedure

Figure 1.
Question-answering evaluation procedure.

Our evaluation procedure for each exam question involved a multi-step process, as illustrated in the activity diagram shown in Figure
1
. Initially, we captured an image of the question from the exam’s PDF file. We then provided this image along with a simple (zero-shot) Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt
(
Wei et al. 2022
)
to ChatGPT-4 Vision, asking the model to answer the question depicted in the image and to detail its reasoning step by step. This prompting approach was chosen because it enables the model to demonstrate its ability to understand and solve complex problems without prior specific training on the exam’s content, relying instead on its general pre-trained knowledge base. Moreover, by incorporating CoT prompts, the evaluation process gains transparency and insight into the model’s reasoning, allowing the assessment not just of the correctness of the final answers but also of the logical processes used to arrive at them. This is especially valuable in an educational context, where understanding the steps of problem-solving is as important as the solution itself. Appendix
A
shows our study’s zero-shot CoT prompt templates for the ENADE 2021 BCS open and multiple-choice exam questions.

After providing ChatGPT-4 Vision with the appropriate prompt, we compared the model’s generated response against the exam’s official answer standard (for open questions) or answer key (for multiple-choice questions). When the model’s response did not align with the official answer, we prompted the model to reassess its response in light of the official answer and self-reflect on its possible reasoning mistakes. Essentially, our reassessment prompts inform the model that its initial answer is inconsistent with the official answer and instruct it to ponder whether to maintain or change its initial answer given the new information. Appendix
A
shows our reassessment prompt templates for open and multiple-choice questions. We interactively analyzed the model’s subsequent responses and prompted it for further clarification when the model’s decision on the correct answer was unclear or incoherent.

Following the clarification stage, if discrepancies persisted between ChatGPT-4 Vision’s responses and the official answer, the conflicting answers were escalated to an independent expert panel for review. The panel comprised two or more computer science researchers from multiple Brazilian universities with known expertise on the question’s subject. We provided each panel member with the question, official answer, and ChatGPT-4 Vision’s responses. They were individually asked to assess the correctness of the model’s responses and provide their reasoning for their decisions without knowing the identity and opinions of their peers. The panel members were also asked to indicate if the question was unclear or had multiple or no possible correct answers. We then compiled and classified the panel’s assessments as conclusive or inconclusive. Conclusive assessments were those where all panel members agreed on the question’s correct answer. Inconclusive assessments were those where the panel members disagreed on the correctness of the question’s answer or indicated that the question was unclear or had no possible answer.

In the final evaluation step, we provided ChatGPT-4 Turbo with the question image, ChatGPT-4 Vision’s prompts and responses, and the expert assessments (when available) for each question in which the model did not initially agree with the official response and prompted it to reflect on the entire question-answering process. In particular, we asked the model to identify the possible challenges the AI faced in answering the question, the cause of its errors, and whether these errors could be mitigated by using more sophisticated prompt engineering techniques or would be inherent to the limitations of current LLMs. This final reflective assessment aimed to provide further insights into the AI’s reasoning and decision-making processes, contributing to a deeper understanding of the model’s multimodal capabilities and limitations.

4.3.
Question Scoring

We scored ChatGPT-4 Vision’s responses to the three open questions using the exam’s official answer standard as a reference. We assigned scores from 0 to 100 to each response, where 0 indicated an utterly incorrect response, and 100 indicated an entirely correct response. We scored partially correct responses proportionally, based on the number of correct items or assertions they contained. For example, a response with two correct assertions out of three would be scored 66.6.

We scored ChatGPT-4 Vision’s responses to the multiple-choice questions proportionally to the number of responses that agreed with the exam’s answer key. For reference, we scored all 25 valid multiple-choice questions, including the six questions the exam committee excluded from the final score. However, we only consider the 19 valid non-excluded questions for computing the model’s final score.

We only scored the model’s initial response to each question and ignored its subsequent reassessments or the expert panel’s reviews in determining the score value. For example, if the model initially answered a question incorrectly but later changed its response after reassessment, we only considered the initial incorrect response for scoring purposes.

4.4.
Experimental Setup

During the question-answering sessions, we manually interacted with both ChatGPT-4 Vision and ChatGPT-4 Turbo using their respective web interfaces. We did not use an automated script for this task because ChatGPT-4 Vision’s model was not available via API at the time the experiment was conducted. We used the models’ default settings with no parameter fine-tuning. We saved all the model’s prompts and responses and the expert assessments as text files in their original Portuguese version. We translated them into English using the GPT-4 Turbo API via a customized Python script with minor manual revisions and improvements. We also translated the exam’s official answer standard for open questions into English using the same script.

We applied Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient
(
Zar 2005
)
to test this hypothesis by calculating the correlation between the AI model’s performance and each question’s facility and discrimination levels. This method was chosen for its robustness in handling non-parametric data and its effectiveness in measuring the strength and direction of association between two ranked variables. The AI model’s answers were numerically coded to facilitate the analysis: correct answers as 1 and 0 otherwise. The facility level and the discrimination level were numerically represented by the percentage of correct answers and the Point-Biserial correlation values, respectively.

Our Spearman’s rank correlation analysis indicated a coefficient of 0.555 (
p=0.004
) between the AI model’s performance and the facility level. The analysis also yielded a Spearman’s correlation coefficient of 0.584 (
p=0.0022
) when comparing the AI model’s performance with the Point-Biserial Correlation values. These results reveal statistically significant positive correlations (
p<0.05
) between the AI model’s performance and the exam questions’ facility and discrimination levels, as evidenced by the
p
-values reported. Thus, the correlation results support our claim that ChatpGPT-4 Vision is more adept at answering questions that are both easier for students and more discriminative regarding student performance.

Based on our analysis of ChatGPT-4 Vision’s initially incorrect responses, the expert assessments (when available), and ChatGPT-4 Turbo’s responses, we identified three challenges categories:
Question Interpretation
,

, and

. We further classified the most common errors generated by the model when facing each of these challenges into eight error types, as described and illustrated in Table

. Question interpretation errors are caused by the model’s inability to correctly interpret the questions’ statements. Logical reasoning errors arise from the model’s flawed reasoning processes when answering the assertions in the questions. Finally, visual acuity errors are caused by the model’s inability to correctly identify and process the questions’ visual elements. These challenge categories and error types are not mutually exclusive, as the manifestation of errors related to one challenge may lead to the manifestation of other errors related to another challenge. For example, the model’s misidentification of a visual element in a question may trickle down to an incorrect multi-step reasoning process.

The last column of Table

reveals that the most common error types generated by ChatGPT-4 Vision in answering the ENADE 2021 BCS questions were related to logical reasoning, particularly incorrect multi-step reasoning (in five questions), insufficient domain knowledge (in four questions), and evaluation leniency (in three questions). Inconsistent responses and misidentification of visual elements (in three questions each) were also common error types. Other less frequent error types included incorrect algorithmic reasoning and non-compliance with guidelines (in two questions), and lack of domain-specific visual output (in one question). Appendix

shows the model’s conversations for the three open and six multiple-choice questions illustrating most of these challenges categories and error types. The model’s full conversations for all the questions are available in the paper’s companion repository.

These results provide several insights into ChatGPT-4 Vision’s multimodal reasoning capabilities. For example, the model’s frequent incorrect multi-step reasoning and insufficient domain knowledge suggest its ability to reason about complex, multi-step problems in computer science is still limited. Moreover, the model’s multiple evaluation leniency issues indicate its ability to evaluate the validity of assertions is not yet as strict as that of the human experts. Finally, the model’s tendency to misidentify visual elements and lack of domain-specific visual output show that its visual acuity is not yet as developed as its text-based reasoning capabilities. This limitation is particularly critical given the frequent use of visual data in academic assessments in computer science and other science and engineering fields.

Another relevant result was the model’s struggle with vague or ambiguous statements, particularly in questions where the expert panel did not agree on the correct answer, such as in questions 13, 18, and 25. This result suggests that some of the exam’s questions may have been poorly constructed, leading to confusion and disagreement among the model and the human experts. Indeed, some experts explicitly mentioned the low quality of some ENADE questions, with one expert being even more forthright when assessing the quality of one particular question: “I’m sad that such a poorly formulated question was used to evaluate computer science students.”

It is worth noting that these challenges did not manifest uniformly across the open and multiple-choice questions. For example, the model’s issues with incorrect multi-step reasoning were more frequent in the multiple-choice questions with indirect answers. At the same time, the lack of domain-specific visual output is only an issue for open questions requiring visual responses. Moreover, some challenges were not uniformly distributed across questions with similar characteristics. For example, the model’s issues with misidentifying visual elements did not occur in all questions containing figures or diagrams. These results suggest that the model’s challenges are highly context-dependent and may require tailored solutions to address them effectively.

6.
Discussion

Given the results presented in the previous section, we discuss the implications of ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance in the ENADE 2021 BCS exam and the insights gained from the model’s challenges in answering the exam’s questions. We also discuss the limitations of our study and future research directions.

6.1.
Implications of ChatGPT-4 Vision’s Performance

The performance of ChatGPT-4 Vision on the ENADE 2021 BCS exam highlights significant advancements in AI’s ability to engage with complex, multimodal academic content. The AI’s overall score, comparable to that of the top-scoring human participants, particularly its superior performance in open questions and its competitive edge in multiple-choice questions with visual elements, underscores the potential of multimodal AI in educational settings. These results indicate the evolving capabilities of AI systems to process and analyze information in a manner previously thought to be exclusive to human cognition
(
Zhai et al. 2024
)
. Such capabilities could be harnessed to support various educational tasks, such as developing advanced tutoring systems, aiding educational assessment, and providing personalized learning experiences
(
Li et al. 2023
)
. The model’s ability to reason about complex, multimodal problems in computer science is particularly promising, given the growing demand for AI systems that can understand and reason about multimodal data in this education domain
(
Zhou et al. 2024
)
.

6.2.
Insights from ChatGPT-4 Vision’s Challenges

The challenges faced by ChatGPT-4 Vision, particularly in logical reasoning, question interpretation, and visual acuity, provide valuable insights into the current limitations of AI in fully understanding and responding to complex multimodal academic problems. The model’s difficulties with incorrect multi-step reasoning and insufficient domain knowledge reinforce similar findings reported by prior studies (e.g.,
(
Bubeck et al. 2023
;
Mao et al. 2023
;
Espejel et al. 2023
)
). Nevertheless, these insights provide valuable guidance for future improvements to the model’s reasoning capabilities, perhaps through more sophisticated training datasets or advanced reasoning algorithms
(
Tyen et al. 2024
)
. The model’s challenges with visual acuity also highlight the importance of developing AI systems that can accurately interpret and integrate multi-modal inputs
(
Zhao et al. 2023a
)
. Furthermore, the model and expert panel’s struggle with vague or ambiguous statements could inform educational assessment design, helping improve the clarity and precision of questions during exam elaboration. Finally, the finding that the model’s performance is correlated with the difficulty and discriminative power of the exam’s multiple-choice questions suggests that ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance could be used as a proxy for question quality and complexity, thus providing a valuable tool for exam creators to test and refine their questions before administering the exam.

6.3.
Study Limitations and Future Directions

Our study has certain limitations that are important to acknowledge. First, the reliance on zero-shot CoT prompts may have limited the AI’s ability to fully engage with the multi-step reasoning required by some exam questions, potentially underestimating its true reasoning capabilities. Future studies should experiment with advanced prompting techniques like Self-Consistency
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, Tree-of-Thoughts
(
Yao et al. 2023
)
, and Visualization-of-Thought
(
Wu et al. 2024
)
, which may enable the AI to navigate complex reasoning sequences more effectively, potentially revealing deeper insights into its problem-solving abilities.
The fact that ChatGPT-4 Vision was able to reassess and correct its initial response to several questions during our experiment suggests that new prompting techniques tailored for the exam’s format and style have the potential to further enhance the model’s accuracy.
Second, our evaluation protocol involved asking the AI model to answer each question only once. This design choice limits our ability to analyze the consistency of the AI’s performance or its variability in responding to the same question across multiple attempts. A more comprehensive evaluation could involve multiple attempts to answer each question, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the AI’s performance and the potential for improvement over time
(
Chen et al. 2023
)
.
Third, our study did not compare ChaGPT-4 Vision’s ENADE 2021 BCS exam performance with other multimodal generative AI models like Google’s recently released Gemini family of models
(
Gemini Team 2023
)
. A systematic comparative analysis involving other multimodal generative AI models could provide valuable insights into the unique capabilities and limitations of each model in handling educational content. This approach could also help identify areas for cross-model improvements and innovations
(
Qi et al. 2023
)
.
Finally, our study did not incorporate traditional exam validation methods, such as pre-assessment moderation, where human experts review and validate exam questions to ensure their quality, fairness, and alignment with educational objectives
(
Bloxham et al. 2016
)
. Incorporating such traditional methods could provide a valuable benchmark against which the AI’s performance can be measured. Future research could explore this comparison to better understand the potential and limitations of AI in educational assessments.

7.
Conclusion

ChatGPT-4 Vision’s performance on Brazil’s ENADE 2021 Bachelor in Computer Science exam offers a compelling glimpse into the future of generative AI technologies in educational assessment. ChatGPT-4 Vision not only matched but, in some cases, exceeded the top human scores, particularly in handling questions with visual components and complex problem-solving scenarios. While showcasing the model’s impressive multimodal reasoning capabilities, the results particularly underline the need for improvements in the AI’s handling of complex visual data interpretations and its ability to apply domain-specific knowledge accurately. Future research should focus on refining multimodal generative AI models to enhance their interpretive, reasoning, and vision capabilities. This includes exploring adaptive prompt techniques that dynamically adjust based on the AI’s response quality, aiming to steer AI models more effectively through complex reasoning pathways. Additionally, employing AI models in simulated testing environments can help identify biases, ambiguities, or inconsistencies in exam questions, thereby enhancing their fairness and effectiveness. Successfully addressing these challenges will not only advance AI’s capabilities in educational assessments but also pave the way for broader applications in personalized learning, automated tutoring systems, and interactive educational content.

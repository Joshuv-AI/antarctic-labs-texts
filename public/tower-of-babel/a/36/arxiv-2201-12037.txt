Abstract
Algebraic dichotomy is a generalization of an exponential dichotomy (see Lin [ 1 ] ). This paper gives a version of Hartman-Grobman linearization theorem assuming that linear system admits an algebraic dichotomy, which generalizes the Palmer’s linearization theorem. Besides, we prove that the homeomorphism in the linearization theorem is Hölder continuous (and has a Hölder continuous inverse). Comparing with exponential dichotomy, algebraic dichotomy is more complicate. The exponential dichotomy leads us to the estimates ∫ − ∞ t e − α ⁡ ( t − s ) ​ 𝑑 s \int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\alpha(t-s)}ds and ∫ t + ∞ e − α ⁡ ( s − t ) ​ 𝑑 s \int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(s-t)}ds which are convergent. 1.2
Motivation and novelty

In this paper, we pay particular attention to the effect of the algebraic dichotomy imposing on the linearization of the differential equations.
Palmer’s linearization theorem requires two essential conditions: (i) the nonlinear term
f
is uniformly bounded and Lipschitzian; (ii) the linear system

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)

(1.2)

possesses an exponential dichotomy.
In the present paper, we try to reduce the second condition. Motivated by Lin’s algebraic dichotomy (see Lin
[
1
]
) and the works of Palmer
[
32
]
and Zhang et al
[
12
]
, we study the
C^{0}
linearization with the algebraic dichotomy. Further more, we prove that the homeomorphism and its inverse are Hölder continuous under the assumption of the algebraic dichotomy. When the algebraic dichotomy reduces to the exponential dichotomy, our results generalize and improve the previous ones.
Comparing with exponential dichotomy, algebraic dichotomy is more general. The exponential dichotomy leads to the estimates
\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\alpha(t-s)}ds
and
\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha(s-t)}ds
which are convergent. However, the algebraic dichotomy will leads us to
\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}ds
or
\int_{t}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}ds
, whose the convergence is unknown in the sense of Riemann. This brings more difficulties to our research.

The structure of our paper as follows. In Section 2, we give our main results. In Section 3, we give some preliminary results. In Section 4, we give rigorous proofs to show the regularity of the equivalent function
H(t,x)
and
G(t,y)
. Finally, we give an example to illustrate our linearization theorem.

Statement of main results

Consider the following two non-autonomous systems

x^{\prime}=A(t)x+f(t,x)

(2.1)

and

x^{\prime}=A(t)x,

(2.2)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
A(t)
is a
n\times n
continuous and bounded matrix defined on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
T(t,s)
be the evolution operator of system (
2.2
) satisfying
T(t,s)x(s)=x(t)
,
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
, for any solution
x(t)
of system (
2.2
). Clearly,
T(t,t)=Id
and

\displaystyle T(t,\tau)T(\tau,s)=T(t,s),\ \ \ t,s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.3)

An increasing function
\mu:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
is said to be a growth rate if
\mu(0)=1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\mu(t)=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mu(t)=+\infty.

(2.4)

In the following, we always assume that
\mu(t)
is growth rate.

Definition 2.1
.

[
1
]
Linear system (
2.2
) is said to admit an algebraic dichotomy, if there exists a projection
P(s)
and constants
K>0
,
\alpha>0
such that

\begin{split}T(t,s)P(s)&=P(t)T(t,s),\end{split}

(2.5)

\begin{split}||T(t,s)P(s)||&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha},\quad\mathrm{if}\quad t\geq s,\\
||T(t,s)Q(s)||&\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha},\quad\mathrm{if}\quad t\leq s\end{split}

(2.6)

hold, where
P(s)+Q(s)=I
.

Obviously,
\mu(t)=e^{t}
yields an exponential dichotomy.

To show its generality, we give an example of algebraic dichotomy.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the differential equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

\begin{split}\begin{cases}x^{\prime}_{1}=-\eta_{1}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)x_{1},\\
x^{\prime}_{2}=\eta_{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)x_{2},\end{cases}\end{split}

(2.7)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are positive constants.

From system (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle x_{1}=\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\eta_{1}}V_{1},\quad x_{2}=\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{\eta_{2}}V_{2},

where
V_{1},V_{2}
are constants.

Taking
P(s)=diag\{1,0\}
,
Q(s)=diag\{0,1\}
.
Then, we have

T(t,s)P(s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\eta_{1}}&0\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\mathrm{for}\quad t\geq s,

(2.8)

T(t,s)Q(s)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\eta_{2}}\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\mathrm{for}\quad t\leq s.

(2.9)

Taking
\eta_{3}=\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}
. It follows that

\displaystyle||T(t,s)P(s)||\leq\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\eta_{3}},\quad\mathrm{for}\quad t\geq s,

\displaystyle||T(t,s)Q(s)||\leq\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\eta_{3}},\quad\mathrm{for}\quad t\leq s.

This implies that equation (
2.7

\displaystyle K=1,\quad\alpha=\eta_{3}.

Remark 2.3

Particularly, if

Preliminary results

We split the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

If linear system (

Proof
.
Let

\displaystyle x(t)=T(t,s)x(s).

From (

\displaystyle x(t)=T(t,s)P(s)x(s)+T(t,s)Q(s)x(s).

If

, consider the case

. We have

\displaystyle||x(t)||=||T(t,s)P(s)x(s)+T(t,s)Q(s)x(s)||\geq||T(t,s)P(s)x(s)||-||T(t,s)Q(s)x(s)||.

From (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle||P(s)x(s)||

\displaystyle=||T(s,s)P(s)T(s,t)T(t,s)P(s)x(s)||

\displaystyle\leq||T(s,t)P(t)||\ ||T(t,s)P(s)x(s)||

\displaystyle\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\ ||T(t,s)P(s)x(s)||.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle||T(t,s)P(s)x(s)||\geq\frac{1}{K}\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{\alpha}||P(s)x(s)||.

From (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle||T(t,s)Q(s)x(s)||\leq K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}||x(s)||.

Hence,

\displaystyle||x(t)||\geq\frac{1}{K}\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{\alpha}||P(s)x(s)||-K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}||x(s)||.

From (

), when

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}||x(t)||=+\infty.

This contradicts with the boundedness of

,

Lemma 3.2
.

For any given

, system

For any given

\displaystyle||Z_{0}(t)||

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds+\int_{t}^{+\infty}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds

For any given

Lemma 3.3
.

For any given

\displaystyle||\mathscr{F}z||\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds+\int_{t}^{+\infty}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds\leq 2K\beta\alpha^{-1}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)||g_{0}-g||ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{+\infty}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)||g_{0}-g||ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4K\gamma\alpha^{-1})(4K\beta\alpha^{-1}).

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle||g_{m}(t,(\tau,\xi))-g(t,(\tau,\xi))||\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}||T(t,s)P(s)(f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g_{m-1})

\displaystyle-f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g))||ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{+\infty}||T(t,s)Q(s)(f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g_{m-1})

\displaystyle-f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g))||ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)||g-g_{m-1}||ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{+\infty}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)||g-g_{m-1}||ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4K\gamma\alpha^{-1})^{m}(4K\beta\alpha^{-1}).

Since
4K\gamma\alpha^{-1}<1
,
g_{m}(t,(\tau,\xi))\to g(t,(\tau,\xi))
, as
m\to+\infty
.
From the uniqueness of the limit, we know that
\bar{g}(t,(\tau,\xi))=g(t,(\tau,\xi))
. Thus,

\lim_{m\to+\infty}g_{m}(t,\tau,\xi)=g(t,\tau,\xi).

Next, we prove
g_{m}(t,(\tau,\xi))=g_{m}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi)))
. Since

Y(t,(\tau,\xi))=Y(t,t,Y(t,\tau,\xi)).

g_{1}(t,(\tau,\xi))=\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))ds.

\begin{split}g_{1}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi)))=&\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)))ds\\
&-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)))ds\\
=&\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))ds.\end{split}

Thus,
g_{1}(t,(\tau,\xi))=g_{1}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi))).

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle g_{m+1}(t,(\tau,\xi))=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g_{m}(s,\tau,\xi))ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g_{m}(s,\tau,\xi))ds,

\displaystyle g_{m+1}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi)))=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))+g_{m}(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)))ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))+g_{m}(s,s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi)+g_{m}(s,\tau,\xi))ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)f(s,Y(s,\tau,\xi))ds.

Thus,
g_{m+1}(t,(\tau,\xi))=g_{m+1}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi))).

From the mathematical induction, we get

g_{m}(t,(\tau,\xi))=g_{m}(t,(t,Y(t,\tau,\xi))).

Similarly, we take sufficiently large constant
\tilde{M_{1}}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\frac{1}{M+\gamma}\ln\frac{1}{||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||}
, such that

\displaystyle\tilde{M_{1}}\geq\max\{[\log_{\frac{\mu(t-\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}}{||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||}]+1,[\log_{\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}}{||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||}]+1,\alpha,M\}.

Finally, taking constants
\lambda>0
is sufficiently large, and
q^{\prime}>0
is sufficiently small, satisfing
\lambda>\frac{4K\beta\alpha^{-1}+2K\gamma\alpha^{-1}}{1-2K\gamma\alpha^{-1}e}
,
q^{\prime}\leq\min\{\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{M_{1}}},\frac{\gamma}{M+\gamma},\frac{\alpha}{M\tilde{\tau}}\ln\frac{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}\}
, where
\ln\frac{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}<\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

Furthermore, we prove if
||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||<1
, for any non-negative integer
m
, we have

\displaystyle||g_{m}(t,(t,\eta))-g_{m}(t,(t,\eta^{\prime}))||\leq\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}.

(4.1)

When
m=0
, (
4.1
) obviously holds. Now we induce that hypothesis (
4.1
) holds. From (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle g_{m+1}(t,(t,\eta))-g_{m+1}(t,(t,\eta^{\prime}))=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)[f(s,Y(s,t,\eta)+g_{m}(s,t,\eta))

\displaystyle-f(s,Y(s,t,\eta^{\prime})+g_{m}(s,t,\eta^{\prime}))]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,s)Q(s)[f(s,Y(s,t,\eta)+g_{m}(s,t,\eta))

\displaystyle-f(s,Y(s,t,\eta^{\prime})+g_{m}(s,t,\eta^{\prime}))]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{2}.

Now divide
J_{1},J_{2}
into two parts:

\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{-\infty}^{t-\tilde{\tau}}+\int_{t-\tilde{\tau}}^{t}=J_{11}+J_{12},

\displaystyle J_{2}=\int_{t}^{t+\tilde{\tau}}+\int_{t+\tilde{\tau}}^{+\infty}=J_{21}+J_{22}.

Then, by using (
2.6
) and (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle||J_{11}||

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t-\tilde{\tau}}||T(t,s)P(s)||2\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t-\tilde{\tau}}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}2\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq 2K\beta\alpha^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(t-\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2K\beta\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{M_{1}}}},

and

\displaystyle||J_{22}||

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t+\tilde{\tau}}^{+\infty}||T(t,s)Q(s)||2\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t+\tilde{\tau}}^{+\infty}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}2\beta\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq 2K\beta\alpha^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2K\beta\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{M_{1}}}}.

Furthermore, it follows from (
2.6
) , (
2.11
) and lemma
3.9
that

\displaystyle||J_{12}||

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-\tilde{\tau}}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)(||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M(s-t)}+\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}e^{Mq^{\prime}(s-t)})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-\tilde{\tau}}^{t}K\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)(||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M\tilde{\tau}}+\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}e^{Mq^{\prime}\tilde{\tau}})ds

\displaystyle\leq K\gamma\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M\tilde{\tau}}+K\gamma\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(t-\tilde{\tau})}\right)^{\alpha}\ln\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(t-\tilde{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq K\gamma\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{\frac{\gamma}{M+\gamma}}+K\gamma\lambda\alpha^{-1}e||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq(K\gamma\alpha^{-1}+K\gamma\lambda\alpha^{-1}e)||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}},

and

\displaystyle||J_{21}||

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t+\tilde{\tau}}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)(||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M(s-t)}+\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}e^{Mq^{\prime}(s-t)})ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t+\tilde{\tau}}K\left(\frac{\mu(s)}{\mu(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\gamma\mu^{\prime}(s)\mu^{-1}(s)(||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M\tilde{\tau}}+\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}e^{Mq^{\prime}\tilde{\tau}})ds

\displaystyle\leq K\gamma\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||e^{M\tilde{\tau}}+K\gamma\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}\right)^{\alpha}\ln\frac{\mu(t+\tilde{\tau})}{\mu(t)}

\displaystyle\leq K\gamma\alpha^{-1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{\frac{\gamma}{M+\gamma}}+K\gamma\lambda\alpha^{-1}e||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq(K\gamma\alpha^{-1}+K\gamma\lambda\alpha^{-1}e)||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle||g_{m+1}(t,(t,\xi))-g_{m+1}(t,(t,\xi^{\prime}))||

\displaystyle\leq||J_{11}||+||J_{12}||+||J_{21}||+||J_{22}||

\displaystyle\leq 4K\beta\alpha^{1}||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{M_{1}}}}+2(K\gamma\alpha^{-1}+K\gamma\lambda\alpha^{-1}e)||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(4K\beta\alpha^{-1}+2K\gamma\alpha^{-1}+2K\gamma\alpha^{-1}\lambda e\right)||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}.

This means that for any non-negative integer
m
, there is

\displaystyle||g(t,(t,\eta))-g(t,(t,\eta^{\prime}))||\leq\lambda||\eta-\eta^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}.

From the definition of
G(t,y)
, if
0<||y-y^{\prime}||<1
, we have

\displaystyle||G(t,y)-G(t,y^{\prime})||\leq||y-y^{\prime}||+\lambda||y-y^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}\leq(1+\lambda)||y-y^{\prime}||^{q^{\prime}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.8
.

5
An example

Consider the differential equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\begin{split}\begin{cases}x^{\prime}=-\eta_{1}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)x+\varepsilon\sin(x+t),\\
y^{\prime}=\eta_{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)y+\varepsilon\cos(x+t),\end{cases}\end{split}

(5.1)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\varepsilon
are positive constants and
\mu(t)=\frac{2}{\pi}e^{t}(\frac{\pi}{2}+\arctan t)
. Denoting

z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x\\
y\\
\end{array}\right),\ A(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\eta_{1}\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)&0\\
0&\eta_{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)\\
\end{array}\right),\ f(t,z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon\sin(x+t)\\
\varepsilon\cos(x+t)\\
\end{array}\right).

From example
2.2
, we know that the linear part of equation (
5.1
) admits an algebraic dichotomy.
Then,

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(t)\mu^{-1}(t)=1+\frac{1}{(\frac{\pi}{2}+\arctan t)(1+t^{2})}.

We can easily get
1<\mu^{\prime}(t)\mu^{-1}(t)<1+\frac{2}{\pi}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle||f(t,z)||\leq\varepsilon||\sin(x+t)+\cos(x+t)||\leq 2\varepsilon\leq 2\varepsilon\mu^{\prime}(t)\mu^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle||f(t,z_{1})-f(t,z_{2})||\leq 2\varepsilon||z_{1}-z_{2}||\leq 2\varepsilon\mu^{\prime}(t)\mu^{-1}(t).

Hence, equation (
5.1
) satisfies the condition of Theorem
2.7
if
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\eta_{3}}{8}
, where
\eta_{3}=\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}
. Therefore, Eq. (
5.1
) is topologically conjugated to its linear part.

Abstract
In this paper, our main aim is to investigate the strong convergence for a neutral McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation with super-linear delay driven by fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) H\in(1/2,1) . After giving uniqueness and existence for the exact solution, we analyze the properties including boundedness of moment and propagation of chaos. Besides, we give the Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme and show that the numerical solution converges strongly to the exact solution. Furthermore, a corresponding numerical example is given to illustrate the theory. Keywords : Neutral McKean-Vlasov SDEs; super-linear delay; fractional Brownian motion; strong convergence rate; EM scheme

Introduction

The McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations (SDEs) have received a lot of attention since they were proposed by McKean
[
21
]
. The concept of chaos propagation is defined in terms of relative entropy. Bossy and Talay
[
3
,
4
]
chose to approximate the McKean-Vlasov
limit by replicating the behavior with a system of
n
weakly interacting particles, and studied the numerical approximation of the solutions to the McKean-Vlasov SDEs. Lacker
[
15
]
showed how to considerably strengthen the usual mode of convergence of
an
n
-particle system to its McKean-Vlasov limit, known as propagation of chaos. Hammersley et al.
[
11
]
investigated the McKean-Vlasov SDEs with common noise, and developed an appropriate definition of
weak solutions for such equations. The existence and uniqueness of the exact solutions as well as the propagation of chaos of McKean-Vlasov SDEs, have been intensively studied, for example in
[
7
,
24
]
and references therein.

Some studies have considered the situation where the coefficients of McKean-Vlasov SDEs exhibit super-linear growth. Dos Reis et al.
[
6
]
presented two fully probabilistic Euler schemes for the simulation of McKean-Vlasov SDEs: one explicit and one implicit. These schemes are designed for McKean-Vlasov SDEs under the one-sided Lipschitz drift assumption and the polynomial growth in diffusion. Reisinger and Stockinger
[
22
]
introduced adaptive EM schemes for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with one-sided Lipschitz condition and Polynomial growth condition for the drift, and proved strong convergence rate of
1/2
. Gao et al.
[
10
]
studied the numerical scheme of highly nonlinear neutral multiple-delay stochastic McKean-Vlasov equations, and established the tamed EM
scheme for the corresponding particle system, and obtained the convergence rate in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
sense. Cui et al.
[
5
]
studied numerical methods for approximating solutions to a sort of McKean-Vlasov neutral SDEs, where the drift coefficient exhibits super-linear growth, and constructed the strong convergence rate of the tamed EM scheme.

Generally, let
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},t\in[0,T]\}
be a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent
H\in(0,1)
defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Then,
B^{H}
is a centered Gaussian process
with the covariance function

R_{H}(t,s)=\mathbb{E}(B_{t}^{H}B_{s}^{H})=\frac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}),\ \ \forall t,s\in[0,T].

The fractional Brownian motion
\{B_{t}^{H}\}_{t\geq 0}
corresponds to a standard Brownian motion when
H=1/2
. This process was
introduced by Kolmogorov and studied by Mandelbrot and Van Ness
[
18
]
. The self-similarity and long memory properties make fractional Brownian motion a suitable input noise for various models. It is said that
B^{H}
is neither a Markov process nor a semimartingale unless
H=1/2
. However, it has been shown that many basic tools in ordinary stochastic calculus have their counterparts for fractional Brownian motion
[
26
]
. He et al.
[
13
]
proposed a truncated EM scheme for SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion with super-linear drift coefficient and obtained the convergence rate of the numerical method. For more details about SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion, we can refer to
[
14
,
17
,
25
,
27
]
and the related references therein.

Recently, fractional Brownian motion has been applied to financial time series, hydrology, and telecommunications. To develop these applications, numerous equations have been established regarding fractional Brownian motion, including McKean-Vlasov SDEs. However, compared to SDEs driven by standard Brownian motion, the numerical issues of SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion have not been well studied. Among them, the research on McKean-Vlasov SDEs is even scarcer. To name a few, Bauer and Meyer-Brandis
[
2
]
studied McKean-Vlasov equations on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with irregular drift and additive fractional noise. Shen et al.
[
23
]
established the existence and uniqueness theorem for solutions of the McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion and standard Brownian motion. Fan et al.
[
8
]
studied a McKean-Vlasov SDE driven by fractional Brownian motion as follows:

(1.1)

{\rm d}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}t+\sigma(t,\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}B_{t}^{H},

where the coefficients
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
, here,
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
\theta
-th moment. They gave the well-posedness of (
1.1
) and established the Bismut formula by Malliavin calculus. He et al.
[
12
]
studied the EM scheme for (
1.1
) where the coefficients are
\mathbb{W}_{\theta}
-Lipschitz.

Since stochastic differential delay equations have been widely discussed in many researches to describe the dependence of past behavior, for example
[
20
,
16
]
. Neutral McKean-Vlasov SDDEs have also been discussed, such as
[
5
]
,
[

]
. However, there are almost no studies on neutral McKean-Vlasov SDEs with delay driven by fractional Brownian motion, and even fewer that address the case where the coefficients exhibit nonlinear growth as well. Motivated by the above discussion, we consider neutral McKean-Vlasov SDEs with super-linear delay driven by fractional Brownian motion, as an extension of (
1.1
)

{\rm d}\big(X_{t}-D(X_{t-\tau})\big)=b(X_{t},X_{t-\tau},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}B_{t}^{H},

where
D(\cdot)
is a neutral term and
\tau>0
is the delay.

The structure of the rest of the paper is as follows. In Section 5, the EM scheme is established and the strong convergence rate is given. Finally, a numerical example is presented in Section 6.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we work on a filtered probability space
\{\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}\}
satisfying the usual
conditions. The canonical process
\{B^{H}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion defined on the probability space with Hurst exponent
H\in(0,1)
. Let
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,|\cdot|)
be the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
. For a matrix, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
the Euclidean norm. In addition, we use
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to denote the family of all probability measures on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the Borel
\sigma
-field over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
\tau>0
, let
\mathcal{C}([-\tau,0];\mathbb{R})
be the family of all continuous functions
\varphi
from
[-\tau,0]
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|\varphi\|:=\sup\limits_{-\tau\leq\rho\leq 0}|\varphi(\rho)|
. Define the subset of probability measures with finite
\theta
-th moment by

\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigg\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{\theta}\mu({\rm d}x)<\infty\bigg\},

and for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

\mathcal{W}_{\theta}(\mu)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{\theta}\mu({\rm d}x)\right)^{1/\theta}.

As a metric on the space, we use the Wasserstein distance. For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\theta\geq 1
, the Wasserstein
distance between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as

\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)=\bigg(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{\theta}\pi({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)\bigg)^{1/\theta},

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
stands for all the couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
, i.e.,
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\pi(\cdot,\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(\cdot)
and
\pi(\mathbb{R}^{d},\cdot)=\nu(\cdot)
. For
p\geq 1
, let
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variables
V
that satisfies
\parallel V(x)\parallel_{L^{p}}:=(\mathbb{E}|V(x)|^{p})^{1/p}<\infty.

It is worth noting that there are two major obstacles depending on the properties of
\{B^{H}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. Firstly, the fractional Brownian motion
is not a semimartingale except in the case of standard Brownian motion (
H=1/2
), hence the classical Itô calculus based on semimartingales cannot be directly applied to the fractional case. Secondly, there is no martingale representation theorem with respect to the fractional Brownian motion.

In this paper, we consider the following
d
-dimensional neutral McKean-Vlasov SDDE driven by fractional Brownian motion as follows:

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}\big(X_{t}-D(X_{t-\tau})\big)=b(X_{t},X_{t-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}){\rm d}B^{H}_{t},\ \ t>0,\\
X_{t}=\xi,\ \ t\in[-\tau,0],\end{array}\right.

where
D:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma:\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}
denotes the law of
X_{t}
and
H\in(1/2,1)
, the initial data
\xi\in L^{\overline{p}}(\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
with
\overline{p}\geq\theta\geq 1
. We impose the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

D(0)=0
. And there exist positive constants
L
and
l\geq 1
,
0<\lambda<1
, such that

\displaystyle|D(x)-D(y)|\leq\lambda|x-y|,

\begin{array}[]{ll}|b(x,y,\mu)-b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)|\leq L\left[|x-\overline{x}|+(1+|y|^{l}+|\overline{y}|^{l})|y-\overline{y}|+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)\right],\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\parallel\sigma(\mu)-\sigma(\nu)\parallel\leq L\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu),\end{array}

for all
x,y,\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. And for initial experience distribution
\delta_{0}
,

|b(0,0,\delta_{0})|\vee\parallel\sigma(\delta_{0})\parallel\leq L.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. There exists a positive constant
L
such that

|b(x,y,\mu)|\leq L\left(1+|x|+|y|^{l+1}+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\delta_{0})\right),

and

\parallel\sigma(\mu)\parallel\leq L\left(1+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\delta_{0})\right).

Proof.

We omit it since it is obvious.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

[
19
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
p>1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p}\leq\bigg(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg)^{p-1}\bigg(|x|^{p}+\frac{|y|^{p}}{\varepsilon}\bigg).

Especially for
\varepsilon=((1-\lambda)/\lambda)^{p-1}
, where
p\geq 2
and
0<\lambda<1
, we get

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p-1}}|x|^{p}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p-1}}|y|^{p}.

3
Existence and Uniqueness

In stead of the usual Picard iteration, we use the Carathéodory approximation to show the existence and uniqueness for (
2.1
). Since for the Picard iteration, one can not be able to establish a Cauchy sequence in case of equations with super-linearly growing coefficients. We first give the definition of Carathéodory approximate as follows. For every integer
n\geq 2/\tau
, define
X_{t}^{n}
on
[-\tau,T]
by

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}=\xi(t),\ \ t\in[-\tau,0],

and for
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})=\xi(0)+D(\xi(-\tau))+\int_{0}^{t}b\left(X^{n}_{s-1/n},X^{n}_{s-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s-1/n}^{n}}\right){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\left(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s-1/n}^{n}}\right){\rm d}B_{s}^{H}.

It is important to note that each
X_{t}^{n}
can be determined explicitly by the stepwise iterated Itô integrals over the intervals
[0,1/n]
,
(1/n,2/n]
etc. To obtain the desired results, it is also necessary to establish the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Then for
\overline{p}>1/H
and
n\geq 2/\tau
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{-\tau\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
\overline{p},H,\xi,T,L
.

Proof.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, by Assumption
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
, we immediately get

\displaystyle\lvert X_{t}^{n}\rvert^{\overline{p}}=

\displaystyle\lvert D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})+X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})\rvert^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{\overline{p}-1}}|D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}-1}}|X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda|X_{t-\tau}|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}-1}}|X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}},

then,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\lambda\parallel\xi\parallel^{\overline{p}}+\lambda\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}-1}}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{n}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\parallel\xi\parallel^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}}}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{n}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}}.

By the elementary inequality, the Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.2
, we derive from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{n}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3^{\overline{p}-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi(0)-D(\xi(-\tau))|^{\overline{p}}+3^{\overline{p}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}b\left(X_{r-1/n}^{n},X_{r-\tau}^{n},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+3^{\overline{p}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma\left(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+|X_{r-1/n}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}+|X_{r-\tau}^{n}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}+\left(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}},\delta_{0})\right)^{\overline{p}}\right]{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg).

For the last term, we use the techniques of Theorem 3.1 in
[
8
]
. Denote
\int_{s}^{t}(t-\kappa)^{-\kappa}(r-s)^{\kappa-1}{\rm d}r:=C(\kappa)
. Since for
\overline{p}H>1
, we can take some
\kappa
such that
1-H<\kappa<1-1/{\overline{p}}
, then by the stochastic Fubini theorem, the Hölder inequality, and combining with Lemma
2.2
, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(\kappa)^{-{\overline{p}}}}{({\overline{p}}-1-\kappa{\overline{p}})^{{\overline{p}}-1}}t^{{\overline{p}}-1-\kappa{\overline{p}}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{\kappa-1}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg|^{{\overline{p}}}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{{\overline{p}}-1-\kappa{\overline{p}}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{\frac{\kappa-1}{H}}\parallel\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\parallel^{\frac{1}{H}}{\rm d}r\right)^{\overline{p}H}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\overline{p}H-1}\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\parallel^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\overline{p}H-1}L^{\overline{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}},\delta_{0})\right]^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r.

Referring to the proof in
[
7
, Proposition 3.4]
, for the
\theta
-Wasserstein metric, we get

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}},\delta_{0})\right)^{\overline{p}}=\left(\mathcal{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\right)^{\overline{p}}\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{r-1/n}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}.

To sum up, by the Gronwall inequality, we get

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{n}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{r-\tau}^{n}|^{(l+1)\overline{p}}{\rm d}r.

Taking the skills of
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
as a reference, to get the assertion, we first set up a finite sequence
\{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{[T/\tau]+1}\}
where
u_{i}=([T/\tau]+2-i)\overline{p}(l+1)^{[T/\tau]+1-i}
, and
[T/\tau]
denotes the integer part of
T/\tau
. It is easy to see that
u_{i}\geq\overline{p}
,
u_{[T/\tau]+1}=\overline{p}
and
(l+1)u_{i+1}<u_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,[T/\tau]
.
For
s\in[0,\tau]
, we immediately get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}|X_{s}^{n}|^{u_{1}}\bigg)\leq C

since
\xi\in L^{\overline{p}}(\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
. For
s\in[0,2\tau]
, by (
3.3
) and the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\underset{s\in[0,2\tau]}{\sup}|X_{s}^{n}|^{u_{2}}\bigg)\leq C+C\int_{0}^{2\tau}\left[\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,r]}|X_{s-\tau}^{n}|^{u_{1}}\bigg)\right]^{\frac{(l+1)u_{2}}{u_{1}}}{\rm d}r\leq C.

By induction, the desired result is obtained.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Then for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
with
t-s<1
, and
p>1/H
,
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
,
n\geq 2/\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{n}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\leq C[(t-s)^{p}+(t-s)^{pH}],

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
p,H,\xi,T,L
but independent of
s
and
t
.

Proof.

Similarly to Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle|X_{t}^{n}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}|X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})-X_{s}^{n}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{p}.

By (
3.1
), we may compute

\displaystyle X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})-X_{s}^{n}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}b\bigg(X^{n}_{r-1/n},X^{n}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\bigg){\rm d}r+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}.

By the elementary inequality, the Hölder inequality, Lemma
2.2
and similar skills as (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{n}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{n})-X_{s}^{n}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}b\left(X_{r-1/n}^{n},X_{r-\tau}^{n},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{p}+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(t-s)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}L^{p}\left[1+|X_{r-1/n}^{n}|+|X_{r-\tau}^{n}|^{l+1}+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}},\delta_{0})\right]^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}C(t-s)^{pH-1}\int_{s}^{t}L^{p}\left[1+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}},\delta_{0})\right]^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t-s)^{p-1}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{u\in[-\tau,r]}|X_{u}^{n}|^{p}\bigg){\rm d}r+C(t-s)^{p-1}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{u\in[-\tau,r]}|X_{u}^{n}|^{(l+1)p}\bigg){\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C(t-s)^{pH-1}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{u\in[-\tau,r]}|X_{u}^{n}|^{p}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Since
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, we can applying Lemma
3.1
to obtain the assertion.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Then for
p>1/H
and
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, (
2.1
) admits a global unique solution.

Proof.

We split the proof into two steps.

Step 1. Existence.
For
m>n\geq 2/\tau
and
t\in[0,T]
, by Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle|X_{s}^{m}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}|X_{s}^{m}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{m})-X_{s}^{n}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{p}.

By (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{m}_{s}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{m})-X_{s}^{n}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{n})|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\bigg[b\left(X^{m}_{r-1/m},X^{m}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}}\right)-b\left(X^{n}_{r-1/n},X^{n}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right)\bigg]{\rm d}r\bigg\lvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\bigg(\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\bigg){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:I_{1}+I_{2}.

By the Hölder inequality and Assumption
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\bigg[b\left(X^{m}_{r-1/m},X^{m}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}}\right)-b\left(X^{n}_{r-1/n},X^{n}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right)\bigg]{\rm d}r\bigg\lvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|b\left(X^{m}_{r-1/m},X^{m}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}}\right)-b\left(X^{n}_{r-1/n},X^{n}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}\right)\right|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/m}-X^{n}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left[(1+|X^{m}_{r-\tau}|^{l}+|X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{l})|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|\right]^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/m}-X^{m}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/n}-X^{n}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(|1+|X^{m}_{r-\tau}|^{l}+|X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{l})^{p}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{p}{\rm d}r.

Since for
l\geq 1
, we have
2pl\leq(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, then by the Hölder inequality, together with Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/m}-X^{n}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/n}-X^{n}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\left[\mathbb{E}(1+|X^{m}_{r-\tau}|^{l}+|X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{l})^{2p}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{pH}\right]+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{m}_{r-1/n}-X^{n}_{r-1/n}|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r.

By Assumption
2.1
, Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
and the same techniques as (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\bigg(\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\bigg){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}})\parallel^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}L^{p}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/m}^{m}}-\mathcal{L}_{X_{r-1/n}^{n}}))^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{r-1/m}^{m}-X_{r-1/n}^{n}|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{r-1/m}^{m}-X_{r-1/n}^{m}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{r-1/n}^{m}-X_{r-1/n}^{n}|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{pH}\right]+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{r-1/n}^{m}-X_{r-1/n}^{n}|^{p}{\rm d}r.

Combining
I_{1},I_{2}
and using the Gronwall inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{m}_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{pH}\right]+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r.

Define a sequence
\{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{[T/\tau]+1}\}
where
u_{i}=([T/\tau]+2-i)p2^{[T/\tau]+1-i}
, and
[T/\tau]
denotes the integer part of
T/\tau
. It is easy to see that
u_{i}\geq p
,
u_{[T/\tau]+1}=p
and
2u_{i+1}<u_{i}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,[T/\tau]
.
For
s\in[0,\tau]
, we see

(3.4)

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}|X^{m}_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{u_{1}}\bigg)\to 0,\ {\rm for}\ m,n\to\infty.

For
s\in[0,2\tau]
, by the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\underset{s\in[0,2\tau]}{\sup}|X^{m}_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{u_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{pH}\right]+C\int_{0}^{2\tau}\left[\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,r]}|X_{s-\tau}^{m}-X_{s-\tau}^{n}|^{u_{1}}\bigg)\right]^{\frac{u_{2}}{u_{1}}}{\rm}dr.

We derive from (
3.4
) that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\underset{s\in[0,2\tau]}{\sup}|X^{m}_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{u_{2}}\bigg)\to 0,\ {\rm for}\ m,n\to\infty.

By induction, we can see that for
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.5)

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{m}_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\bigg)\to 0,\ {\rm for}\ m,n\to\infty.

Consequently,
(X^{n})_{n\geq 2/\tau}
is a Cauchy sequence for
p>1/H
. Denote the limit of
(X^{n})
by
X
. Therefore, letting
m\to\infty
in (
3.5
), we conclude

(3.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{n}|^{p}\bigg)=0.

Next, we need to show that the
X_{t}
is a solution to (
2.1
). For
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}-X^{n}_{t-1/n}|^{p}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}-X^{n}_{t}+X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{t-1/n}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}-X^{n}_{t}|^{p}+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{t-1/n}|^{p}.

By Lemma
3.2
and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}-X^{n}_{t-1/n}|^{p}\to 0,\ \ {\rm as}\ \ n\to\infty.

By taking
n\to\infty
in (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle X_{t}-D(X_{t-\tau})=\xi(0)+D(\xi(-\tau))+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s},X_{s-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H},\ \ t\in[0,T],

this indicates
X_{t}
is a solution to (
2.1
).

Step 2. Uniqueness.
Let
X_{t}
and
Y_{t}
be two solutions of (
2.1
) with
X_{t}=Y_{t}=\xi(t),\ \ t\in[-\tau,0]
. For
t\in[0,T]
, by the elementary inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-D(X_{s-\tau})-Y_{s}+D(Y_{s-\tau})|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}(b(X_{r},X_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}})-b(Y_{r},Y_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{Y_{r}})){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{Y_{r}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{1}+W_{2}.

By Assumption
2.1
, the Hölder inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle W_{1}=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}(b(X_{r},X_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}})-b(Y_{r},Y_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{Y_{r}})){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|b(X_{r},X_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}})-b(Y_{r},Y_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{Y_{r}})|^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}-Y_{r}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\mathbb{E}(1+|X_{r-\tau}|^{l}+|Y_{r-\tau}|^{l})^{2p}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}-Y_{r}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r.

By Assumption
2.1
again, we get

\displaystyle W_{2}=2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{Y_{r}}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}-Y_{r}|^{p}{\rm d}r.

Recalling that

\displaystyle|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}|X_{s}-D(X_{s-\tau})-Y_{s}+D(Y_{s-\tau})|^{p}.

Combining
W_{1}
-
W_{2}
and using the Grownall inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{m}_{r-\tau}-X^{n}_{r-\tau}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r.

In the same way as in Step 1, it is easy to see that
X(t)=Y(t),t\in[0,T],\mathbb{P}-a.s.
We complete the proof of uniqueness.
∎

4
Propagation of Chaos

Since (
2.1
) is distribution-dependent, we exploit stochastic interacting particle systems to approximate it. We use a system of
N
interacting particles,

(4.1)

{\rm d}\big(X^{i,N}_{t}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{i,N})\big)=b(X^{i,N}_{t},X^{i,N}_{t-\tau},\mu^{X,N}_{t}){\rm d}t+(\mu^{X,N}_{t}){\rm d}B^{H,i}_{t},\ \ i=1,2,\cdots,N,

with the initial
X_{0}^{i,N}=\xi^{i}\in L^{\overline{p}}(\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
, where the empirical measures is defined by

\mu^{X,N}_{t}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{j,N}}(\cdot),

here,
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure at point
x
. We quote Lemma 3.2 in
[
12
]
as the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For two empirical measures
\mu_{t}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}\limits^{N}\delta_{Z^{1,j,N}_{t}}
and
\nu_{t}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}\limits^{N}\delta_{Z_{t}^{2,j,N}}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg\lvert Z_{t}^{1,j,N}-Z_{t}^{2,j,N}\bigg\rvert^{\theta}\bigg)^{1/\theta}.

In order to prove that the particle approximation is useful, we provide the path propagation of the chaos, and consider a system of non-interacting particles as follows:

(4.2)

{\rm d}\big(X_{t}^{i}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{i})\big)=b(X_{t}^{i},X_{t-\tau}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}^{i}}){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}^{i}}){\rm d}B^{H,i}_{t},\ \ X_{0}^{i}=X_{0}^{i,N}=\xi^{i},\ \ i=1,2,\cdots,N.

We remark that particles
\{X_{t}^{i},i=1,2,\cdots,N\}
are mutually independent whereas particles
\{X_{t}^{i,N},i=1,2,\cdots,N\}
are not independent but identically distributed.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. For
{\overline{p}}>1/H
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[-\tau,T]}|X_{t}^{i}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[-\tau,T]}|X_{t}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C,\ \ i=1,2,\cdots,N,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
{\overline{p}},T,L,H,\xi
.

Proof.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, by Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\|\xi\|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{\overline{p}}.

Referring to (
3.2
), by the elementary inequality, the Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi^{i}(0)-D(\xi^{i}(-\tau))|^{\overline{p}}+3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}b(X_{r}^{i,N},X_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\mu_{r}^{X,N}){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\mu_{r}^{X,N}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+(3t)^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}L^{\overline{p}}\bigg[1+|X_{r}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}+|X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}+(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{r}^{X,N},\delta_{0}))^{\overline{p}}\bigg]{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+3^{{\overline{p}}-1}C\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\sigma(\mu_{r}^{X,N})\parallel^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{r}^{X,N},\delta_{0}))^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X_{r}^{j,N}|^{\theta}\bigg)^{{\overline{p}}/\theta}{\rm d}r,

where for the last inequality we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{s}^{X,N},\delta_{0})=\mathcal{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{s}^{X,N})=\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{j,N}|^{\theta}\bigg)^{1/\theta}.

Since all
j
are identically distributed, by the Minkowski inequality and Lemma
4.1
, for the last term, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{j,N}|^{\theta}\bigg)^{{\overline{p}}/\theta}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\parallel|X_{s}^{j,N}|^{\theta}\parallel_{L^{{\overline{p}}/\theta}}\bigg)^{{\overline{p}}/\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{j,N}|^{\overline{p}})^{\theta/{\overline{p}}}\bigg)^{{\overline{p}}/\theta}=\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\leq

\displaystyle C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{s}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}s+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}{\rm d}s.

By the same technique as in Lemma
3.1
, we can show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. For
p>1/H
and
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i}-X_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ \ p>\frac{d}{2},\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N),\ p=\frac{d}{2},\\
N^{-\frac{p}{d}},\ p\in[2,\frac{d}{2}).\end{array}\right.

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
p,T,H,L,\theta
but independent of
N
.

Proof.

Based on the previous discussion, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i})-X_{s}^{i,N}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, by (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we may compute

\displaystyle X_{t}^{i}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{i})-X_{t}^{i,N}+D(X_{t-\tau}^{i,N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left[b(X_{s}^{i},X_{s-\tau}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})-b(X_{s}^{i,N},X_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\mu_{s}^{X,N})\right]{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left[\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})-\sigma(\mu_{s}^{X,N})\right]{\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i}.

By the elementary inequality and the Hölder inequality, Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
4.2
, and the same technique with (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i})-X_{s}^{i,N}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(X_{r}^{i},X_{r-\tau}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}})-b(X_{r}^{i,N},X_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\mu_{r}^{X,N})\right]{\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\left[\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}})-\sigma(\mu_{r}^{X,N})\right]{\rm d}B_{r}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}t^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\lvert b(X_{r}^{i},X_{r-\tau}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}})-b(X_{r}^{i,N},X_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\mu_{r}^{X,N})\rvert^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}})-\sigma(\mu_{r}^{X,N})\parallel^{1/H}{\rm d}r\bigg]^{pH}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2t)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}L^{p}\bigg[|X_{r}^{i}-X_{r}^{i,N}|+(1+|X_{r-\tau}^{i}|^{l}+|X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{l})|X_{r-\tau}^{i}-X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|

\displaystyle+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N})\bigg]^{p}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}^{i}-X_{r}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{r-\tau}^{i}-X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{p}{\rm d}r,

where we have used the fact that
\overline{p}>1/H
. Noticing that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N})\leq\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X})+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{r}^{X},\mu_{r}^{X,N}),

by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{p}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{\theta}]^{p/{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(2^{\theta-1}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}))^{\theta}+2^{\theta-1}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{r}^{X},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{\theta}\bigg)^{p/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}))^{p}+C\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{r}^{X},\mu_{r}^{X,N}))^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X_{r}^{j}-X_{r}^{j,N}|^{\theta}\bigg)^{p/\theta}+C\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}))^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}|X_{r}^{i}-X_{r}^{i,N}|^{p}+C\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}))^{p}.

Since
\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)\leq\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)
for
p\geq\theta
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{r}^{i}-X_{r}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{r-\tau}^{i}-X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}))^{p}{\rm d}r.

Applying the Gronwall inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{r-\tau}^{i}-X_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\mu_{s}^{X}))^{p}{\rm d}s.

Since
(\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\mu_{s}^{X}))^{p}
is controlled by the Wasserstein distance estimate(
[
9
]
, Theorem 1), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\mu_{s}^{X}))^{p}\leq

\displaystyle C(\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{i}|^{\overline{p}})^{p/{\overline{p}}}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{{\overline{p}}-p}{{\overline{p}}}},\ \ p>\frac{d}{2},{\overline{p}}\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N)+N^{-\frac{{\overline{p}}-p}{{\overline{p}}}},\ p=\frac{d}{2},{\overline{p}}\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{p}{d}}+N^{-\frac{{\overline{p}}-p}{{\overline{p}}}},\ p\in[2,\frac{d}{2}),{\overline{p}}\neq\frac{d}{d-p}.\end{array}\right.

Since we have
2p\leq(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, then, by Lemma
4.2
, the Gronwall inequality, and the same techniques as in Lemma
3.3
, the result can be shown.
∎

5
Strong Convergence Rate

In this section, we consider the EM approximate solution of (
4.1
). Assume that for any time
T>0
, there exist positive constants
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta=\frac{\tau}{m}=\frac{T}{M}
, where
\Delta\in(0,1)
is the step-size. Consequently, for the time grid
t_{k}=t_{0}+k\Delta
, it holds that
t_{k+1}-\tau=t_{k+1-m}
. For
k=-m,\cdots,0,
we set
Y_{t_{k}}=\xi
and
t_{0}=0
. We now define the discrete EM scheme for (
4.1
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}=\xi^{i}(t_{k}),\ \ \ k=-m,-m+1,\cdots,0,\\
Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t_{k+1-m}}^{i,N})=Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t_{k-m}}^{i,N})+b(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},Y_{t_{k-m}}^{i,N},\mu_{t_{k}}^{Y,N})\Delta\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sigma(\mu_{t_{k}}^{Y,N})\Delta B_{t_{k}}^{H,i},\ \ k=0,1,\cdots,M,\end{array}\right.

where the empirical measures
\mu_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{Y_{t_{k}}^{j,N}}(\cdot)
and
\Delta B_{t_{k}}^{H,i}=B_{t_{k+1}}^{H,i}-B_{t_{k}}^{H,i}
. Let

\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\xi^{i}(t),\ \ -\tau\leq t\leq 0,\\
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{[T/\Delta]}Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}{\rm I}_{[k\Delta,(k+1)\Delta)}(t),\ \ t\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

and the continuous EM solution writes as

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{t}^{i,N}=\xi^{i}(t),\ \ -\tau\leq t\leq 0,\\
Y_{t}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t-\tau}^{i,N})=Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t_{k-m}}^{i,N})+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}s+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i},\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

where
\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\overline{Y}_{t}^{j,N}}(\cdot)
. For
t_{k}\in[-\tau,0]
,
Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}=\xi^{i}
, we rewrite (
5.1
) as

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t-\tau}^{i,N})=\xi^{i}(0)-D(\xi^{i}(-\tau))+\int_{0}^{t}b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i},\ \ t\geq 0.

We observe
Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}=\overline{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N}
for
k=-m,-(m+1),\cdots,M
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Then, for
{\overline{p}}>1/H
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[-\tau,T]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
{\overline{p}},H,T,\xi,L
.

Proof.

As previous, for
t\in[0,T]
, we know that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|Y_{s}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\|\xi^{i}\|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|Y_{s}^{i,N}-D(Y_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{\overline{p}}

By the elementary inequality, the Hölder inequality, (
5.1
), Lemma
2.2
, and using the techniques of (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|Y_{s}^{i,N}-D(Y_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}|\xi^{i}(0)-D(\xi^{i}(-\tau))|^{\overline{p}}+3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}b(\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N}){\rm d}r\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+3^{{\overline{p}}-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{r}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s}^{i,N}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1){\overline{p}}}{\rm d}r.

The desired assertion can be archived by the same steps as in Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. For
p>1/H
and
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{k\in[-m,M]}\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\Delta^{pH},

where
C
is a positive constant that depends on
H,p,L,\lambda
but independent of
\Delta
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, for any
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t-\tau}^{i,N})-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}+D(\overline{Y}_{t-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}

For
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
, we may compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-D(Y_{t-\tau}^{i,N})-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}+D(\overline{Y}_{t-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\bigg\lvert\int_{t_{k}}^{t}b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}s+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\bigg\lvert\int_{t_{k}}^{t}b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}s\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)+2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\bigg\lvert\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\bigg\lvert\int_{t_{k}}^{t}b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}s\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg),

\displaystyle I_{2}=2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\bigg\lvert\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}){\rm d}B_{s}^{H,i}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg).

By Lemma
2.2
and the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\Delta^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|b(\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N})|^{p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\Delta^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}L^{p}(1+|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}+|\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1)p}+(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N},\delta_{0}))^{p})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CL^{p}\Delta^{p}+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1)p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N},\delta_{0}))^{p}{\rm d}s.

Similar with Lemma
3.1
, by the Hölder inequality, the Kahane-Khintchine formula, the Fubini theorem and Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\lambda)^{-p}(p-1)^{p-1}}{(p-1-\lambda p)^{p-1}}\Delta^{p-1-\lambda p}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{(\lambda-1)/H}\parallel\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N})\parallel^{1/H}{\rm d}s\bigg)^{pH}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{p-1-\lambda p}\Delta^{p(\lambda+H-1)}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\parallel\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N})\parallel^{p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}(1+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N},\delta_{0}))^{p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{pH}+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N},\delta_{0}))^{p}{\rm d}s.

Combining
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, and using Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CL^{p}\Delta^{p}+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1)p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+C\Delta^{p-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s+C\Delta^{pH}+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta^{pH}+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1)p}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta^{pH}+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sup_{t_{k}\leq u\leq s}\mathbb{E}|Y_{u}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}s+C\Delta^{pH-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\sup_{t_{k}\leq u\leq s}\mathbb{E}|Y_{u-\tau}^{i,N}|^{(l+1)p}{\rm d}s.

Since
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, we immediately derive from Lemma
5.1
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}|Y_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\Delta^{pH}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. For
p>1/H
and
(l+1)p\leq\overline{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[-\tau,T]}|X_{t}^{i,N}-Y_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\Delta^{pH},

where
C
is a positive constant that depends on
H,p,L,\lambda
but independent of
\Delta
.

Proof.

Similarly, by Lemma
2.3
, we derive that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-Y_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{p}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})-Y_{s}^{i,N}+D(Y_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}.

By the elementary inequality, the Hölder inequality, the stochastic Fubini theorem and the Kahane-Khintchine formula and Assumption
2.1
, we may compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{i,N})-Y_{s}^{i,N}+D(Y_{s-\tau}^{i,N})|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}(b(X^{i,N}_{r},X^{i,N}_{r-\tau},\mu^{X,N}_{r})-b(\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})){\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{s}(\sigma(\mu^{X,N}_{r})-\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})){\rm d}B^{H,i}_{r}\bigg\rvert^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}t^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\lvert b(X^{i,N}_{r},X^{i,N}_{r-\tau},\mu^{X,N}_{r})-b(\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N},\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N},\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})\bigg\rvert^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}C\int_{0}^{t}\parallel\sigma(\mu^{X,N}_{r})-\sigma(\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})\parallel^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[|X^{i,N}_{r}-\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N}|+(1+|X^{i,N}_{r-\tau}|^{l}+|\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{l})|X^{i,N}_{r-\tau}-\overline{Y}_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu^{X,N}_{r},\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})\bigg]^{p}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\overline{\mu}_{s}^{Y,N}))^{p}{\rm d}s.

Noticing that
X_{r}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N}=X_{r}^{i,N}-Y_{r}^{i,N}+Y_{r}^{i,N}-\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu^{X,N}_{r},\overline{\mu}_{r}^{Y,N})=\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{i,N}_{r}-\overline{Y}_{r}^{i,N}|^{\theta}\bigg)^{1/\theta},

then, by Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
,
5.1
,
5.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}^{i,N}-Y_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta^{pH}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{i,N}_{r}-Y_{r}^{i,N}|^{p}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(|X^{i,N}_{r-\tau}-Y_{r-\tau}^{i,N}|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}r.

Applying the Gronwall inequality and using the techniques as in Theorem
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i,N}-Y_{t}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\Delta^{pH}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let Assumption
2.1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{i}-Y_{t}^{i.N}|^{p}\bigg)\leq C\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\Delta^{pH},\ \ p>\frac{d}{2},\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N)+\Delta^{pH},\ p=\frac{d}{2},\\

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{j,N})\bigg){\rm d}B_{t}^{H,i},

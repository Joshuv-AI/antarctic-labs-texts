Second cohomology space of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) acting on the space of n n -ary differential operators on ℝ \mathbb{R}
Mabrouk Ben Ammar

Abstract
We consider the spaces ℱ λ \mathcal{F}_{\lambda} of polynomial weighted densities of weight λ \lambda ( λ \lambda -densities) on the line as 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -modules and then we compute the second cohomological spaces H diff 2 ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) , 𝒟 λ ¯ , μ ) \mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu}) , where μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in\mathbb{R} , λ ¯ = ( λ 1 , … , λ n ) ∈ ℝ n \underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n} and 𝒟 λ ¯ , μ \mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu} is the space of n n -ary differential operators from ℱ λ 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ ℱ λ n \mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{n}} to ℱ μ \mathcal{F}_{\mu} .

1
Introduction

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we consider the space of
\lambda
-densities:

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}=\big\{fdx^{\lambda}~|~f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\big\}.

The Lie algebra of vector fields on the line:

\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{X_{f}=f\frac{d}{dx}~|~f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right\}

acts on
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
by the
Lie derivative

L^{\lambda}
:

X_{h}\cdot(fdx^{\lambda})=L_{X_{h}}^{\lambda}(fdx^{\lambda}):=(hf^{\prime}+\lambda h^{\prime}f)dx^{\lambda}.

(1.1)

For
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu}
the space of
n
-ary
differential operators
A
from
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{n}}
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. The Lie algebra
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})

acts on the space
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu}
of these differential operators by:

X_{h}\cdot A:=L_{X_{h}}^{\underline{\lambda},\mu}(A)=L_{X_{h}}^{\mu}\circ A-A\circ L_{X_{h}}^{\underline{\lambda}}

(1.2)

where
L_{X_{h}}^{\underline{\lambda}}
is the Lie derivative on
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{n}}
defined by the Leibnitz rule.
The spaces
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu}
can be also viewed as
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-modules, where
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
is realized as
a subalgebra of
\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R})
:

\mathfrak{sl}(2)=\mathrm{Span}(X_{1},\,X_{x},\,X_{x^{2}}).

We are interested in the computation of the differential cohomology spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
where
the subscript
\mathrm{diff}
means that we consider only differential cocycles.
For
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
are computed by Gargoubi
[
7
]

and Lecomte
[
8
]
, while the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-relative cohomology spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R}),\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
are computed by
Bouarroudj and Ovsienko
[
4
]
and the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathrm{Vect}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
are computed by
Feigen and Fuchs
[
5
]
.
For
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
are computed by
Bouarroudj
[
3
]
. For
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})

are computed by O. Basdouri and N. Elamine
[

]
. For

2
Cohomology

Let
V
be
\mathfrak{g}
-module where
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra.
We consider the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
of
n
-cochains of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
V
:

C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V):=\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda^{n}(\mathfrak{g}),V)

and the
coboundary operator

\partial:C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
defined by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(u_{0},\dots,u_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}u_{i}f(u_{0},\dots,\hat{\imath},\dots,u_{n})+

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}f([u_{i},u_{j}],u_{0},\dots,\hat{\imath},\dots,\hat{\jmath},\dots,u_{n}).

The kernel of
\partial|_{C^{n}}
, denoted
Z^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, is the space of
n
-
cocycles
.
The space of
n
-
coboundaries
is
B^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\partial(C^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g},V))
. Since
\partial^{2}=0
then we have

B^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\subset Z^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and then we can define the
n^{th}
cohomology space as the quotient space:

\mathrm{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=Z^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)/B^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V).

Here we consider only cochains that are given by differential operators and then we compute the differential cohomology spaces

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
.

3
The spaces
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})

We consider the spaces

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})
where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and

\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let

\delta=\mu-|\underline{\lambda}|\quad\text{where}\quad|\underline{\lambda}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}.

The space
\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu}
is spanned, as
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
-module, by the operators
\Omega^{\alpha}
,
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, defined by

\Omega^{\alpha}(f_{1}dx^{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}dx^{\lambda_{n}})=f_{1}^{(\alpha_{1})}\dots f_{n}^{(\alpha_{n})}dx^{\mu}.

Let
A_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and consider the elementary operator
A_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha}
, then we have

X_{h}\cdot A_{\alpha}\Omega^{\alpha}=A^{\prime}_{\alpha}h\Omega^{\alpha}+(\delta-|\alpha|)A_{\alpha}h^{\prime}\Omega^{\alpha}-{1\over 2}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i}-1)A_{\alpha}h^{\prime\prime}\Omega^{\alpha-\varepsilon_{i}},

(3.1)

where
(\varepsilon_{1},\,\dots,\,\varepsilon_{n})
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Therefore the operators
x^{m}\Omega^{\alpha}
are eigenvectors of
X_{x}
:

X_{x}\cdot\Omega^{\alpha}=(m+\delta-|\alpha|)\Omega^{\alpha}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Any 2-cocycle

f\in\mathrm{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})

has the following general form

\begin{array}[]{lll}f(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}(h_{1}h^{\prime}_{2}-h_{2}h^{\prime}_{1})\Omega^{\alpha}+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}B_{\alpha}(h_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h_{2}h^{\prime\prime}_{1})\Omega^{\alpha}\\[10.0pt]
&&+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}C_{\alpha}(h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2})\Omega^{\alpha},\end{array}

(3.2)

where
A_{\alpha},\,B_{\alpha}
and
C_{\alpha}
are, a priori, polynomial functions satisfying the following cocycle condition:

C^{\prime}_{\alpha}+(\delta-|\alpha|-1)B_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})A_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0.

(3.3)

Proof. 1) Any 2-cocycle on
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
should retains the general form expressed in (
3.2
). Consider the 2-cocycle condition:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial f(X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})&=&X_{h_{0}}\cdot f(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})-X_{h_{1}}\cdot f(X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{2}})+X_{h_{2}}\cdot f(X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{1}})\\[10.0pt]
&&-f([X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{1}}],X_{h_{2}})+f([X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{2}}],X_{h_{1}})-f([X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}}],X_{h_{0}})=0,\end{array}

which can be expressed as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial f(X_{h_{0}},X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})={\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}}\Big(C^{\prime}_{\alpha}+(|\alpha|-\delta+1)B_{\alpha}-{1\over 2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})A_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}\Big)\varphi(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2})\Omega^{\alpha},\end{array}

where
\varphi(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2})=(h_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime\prime}_{1}h_{2})h^{\prime}_{0}+(h^{\prime\prime}_{0}h_{2}-h_{0}h^{\prime\prime}_{2})h^{\prime}_{1}+(h_{0}h^{\prime\prime}_{1}-h^{\prime\prime}_{0}h_{1})h^{\prime}_{2}
. Thus, the cocycle condition is

C^{\prime}_{\alpha}+(|\alpha|-\delta+1)B_{\alpha}-{1\over 2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})A_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0.

(3.4)

\Box

Proposition 3.1
.

1)

If
\delta\notin\mathbb{N}
then
{\rm H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=0
.

2)

If
\delta=k\in\mathbb{N}
then, up to a coboundary, any 2-cocycle

f\in\mathrm{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})

can be expressed as follows:

f(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})=\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k-1}C_{\alpha}(h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2})\Omega^{\alpha},

(3.5)

where the coefficients
C_{\alpha}
are constants.

Proof.
Consider a 1-cochain

b(X_{h_{1}})=\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}h_{1}\Omega^{\alpha}+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}h^{\prime}_{1}\Omega^{\alpha}+\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}h^{\prime\prime}_{1}\Omega^{\alpha},

where
U_{\alpha}
,
V_{\alpha}
and
W_{\alpha}
are, a priori, polynomial functions. Then we have

\partial b(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})=X_{h_{1}}\cdot b(X_{h_{2}})-X_{h_{2}}\cdot b(X_{h_{1}})-b([X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}}])

So, according to (
3.1
), we can write

\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial b(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\big((|\alpha|-\delta)U_{\alpha}+V^{\prime}_{\alpha}\big)(h^{\prime}_{1}h_{2}-h_{1}h^{\prime}_{2})\Omega^{\alpha}\\[10.0pt]
&+&{\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})U_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}-W^{\prime}_{\alpha}\Big)(h_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h_{2}h^{\prime\prime}_{1})\Omega^{\alpha}\\[10.0pt]
&+&{\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}+(\delta-|\alpha|-1)W_{\alpha}\Big)(h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2})\Omega^{\alpha}.\end{array}

(3.6)

1) If
\delta\notin\mathbb{N}
then we have
(\delta-|\alpha|)\neq 0
and
(\delta-|\alpha|-1)\neq 0
because
|\alpha|\in\mathbb{N}
. So, we can choose

V_{\alpha}=0,\quad U_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\frac{A_{\alpha}}{\delta-|\alpha|}\quad\text{ and }\quad W_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\delta-|\alpha|-1}.

Therefore, according to the cocycle conditions (
3.3
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})U_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}-W^{\prime}_{\alpha}=B_{\alpha},

indeed,
U_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=\displaystyle\frac{A_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}}{\delta-|\alpha|-1}.

Therefore we have

f=\partial b

and then

{\rm H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=0.

2) If
\delta=k\in\mathbb{N}
then, as in the previous case, using (
3.6
), we can first annihilate
the coefficients
A_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|\neq k
and the coefficients
B_{\alpha}
and
C_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|\neq k-1
by adding to
f
a suitable coboundary. Next, we can cancel all coefficients
A_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|=k
and the coefficients
B_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|=k-1
by adding a coboundary
\partial b
where

b(X_{h_{1}})=-\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k}V_{\alpha}h^{\prime}_{1}\Omega^{\alpha}+\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k-1}W_{\alpha}h^{\prime\prime}_{1}\Omega^{\alpha},

such that
V^{\prime}_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}
and
W^{\prime}_{\alpha}=B_{\alpha}
. Thus, up to coboundary, any 2-cocycle can be expressed as in (
3.5
).

\Box

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\delta\notin\mathbb{N}
or
\delta=k\in\mathbb{N}
but
-2\underline{\lambda}\notin\{0,\,\dots,\,k-1\}^{n}
then

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=0.

Proof. If
\delta=k\in\mathbb{N}
and
-2\underline{\lambda}\notin\{0,\,\dots,\,k-1\}^{n}
then, we proved in the previous Proposition that any 2-cocycle
f
can be expressed as in (
3.5
). Now, we prove that any 2-cocycle
f
is a coboundary. Indeed, let

b(X_{h_{1}})=\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k}V_{\alpha}h^{\prime}_{1}\Omega^{\alpha},

where the coefficients
V_{\alpha}
are constants. Then we have

\partial b(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})=\frac{1}{2}{\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k-1}}\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}(h^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{2})\Omega^{\alpha}.

Thus, we have to solve the following system of linear equations:

\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k-1}\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=C_{\alpha}

(3.7)

Without loss of generality, assume that
-2\lambda_{1}\notin\{0,\,\dots,\,k-1\}
. Choose arbitrarily the coefficients
V_{(0,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
.
Since
-2\lambda_{1}\notin\{0,\,\dots,\,k-1\}
then
(\alpha_{1}+2\lambda_{1})\neq 0
since
|\alpha|=k-1
, therefore the coefficients
V_{(1,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
are uniquely defined by (
3.7
)
in function of the corresponding
C_{(0,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
and the
V_{(0,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})+\varepsilon_{i}}
for
i\geq 2
.
Similarly, by (
3.7
), we define the coefficients
V_{\alpha}
with
\alpha_{1}=2
, in function of those with
\alpha_{1}=1
and the corresponding coefficients

C_{(1,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
. So, step by step, we define all the coefficients
V_{\alpha}
so that

f=\partial b.

\Box

4
Singular cases

Now assume that

\delta=k\quad\text{ and }\quad-2\underline{\lambda}=(t_{1},\,t_{2},\,\dots,\,t_{n})\in\{0,\,1,\,\dots,\,k-1\}^{n},

and let

\sigma_{n}=t_{1}+\dots+t_{n}.

Denote by

\Gamma_{n}^{k}=\begin{pmatrix}n+k-1\\
k\end{pmatrix},

Lemma 4.1
.

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=\ell,

where
\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\ell
is the rank of the system

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=k-1}\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0

(4.1)

Proof.
The system (
4.1
) is with
\Gamma_{n}^{k}
unknowns and
\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}
equations. Indeed, any equation is coming from a given

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
with
|\alpha|=k-1
, while the unknowns are the
V_{\alpha}
with
|\alpha|=k
. The rank of (
4.1
) is then less or equal to

\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}
.
If the system (
4.1
) is of rank
(\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\ell)
then, in (
3.7
) we can annihilate
(\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\ell)
parameters

by adding suitable coboundary.
Therefore,

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-(\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\ell)=\ell.

\Box

According to Lemma
4.1
, in this section we have only to compute the rank
\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\ell
of (
4.1
).
Let us decompose (
4.1
) into the following two subsystems
(S_{1})
and
(S_{2})
:

(S_{1}):\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0,\quad\alpha_{1}\neq t_{1}.

(4.2)

(S_{2}):\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0,\quad\alpha_{1}=t_{1}.

(4.3)

If
t_{1}>0
we extract from
(S_{1})
the following system
(S^{\prime}_{1})

(S^{\prime}_{1}):\quad\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+2\lambda_{i})V_{\alpha+\varepsilon_{i}}=0,\quad\alpha_{1}=t_{1}-1.

(4.4)

.

The subsystem
(S_{1})
is always with maximal rank, it is with rank:

\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\Gamma_{n-1}^{k-t_{1}-1}.

Proof.
In fact, since
\alpha_{1}\neq t_{1}
then
(\alpha_{1}+2\lambda_{1})\neq 0
, therefore, as in Theorem
3.2
, we easily prove that
(S_{1})
is of maximal rank.
It is with rank
\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}-\Gamma_{n-1}^{k-t_{1}-1}
where
\Gamma_{n}^{k-1}
is the number of equations of (
4.1
) and
\Gamma_{n-1}^{k-t_{1}-1}

is the number of equations of
(S_{2})
.

\Box

Lemma 4.3
.

If
t_{1}=0
then the spaces spanned respectively by the equations of
(S_{1})
and
(S_{2})
are supplementary. That is:

rank\eqref{e1}=rank(S_{1})+rank(S_{2}).

In fact there are no common unknowns. Indeed, the unknowns
V_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
of
(S_{1})
are all with
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
,
while those of
(S_{2})
are all with
\alpha_{1}=0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\sigma_{n}<k-1
, then

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathfrak{sl}(2),\mathcal{D}_{\underline{\lambda},\mu})=0.

Proof. This is equivalent to the fact that, in this case, (
4.1
) is of maximum rank even if
-2\underline{\lambda}=(t_{1},\,t_{2},\,\dots,\,t_{n})\in\{0,\,1,\,\dots,\,k-1\}^{n}
provided that
\sigma_{n}<k-1
(as in Theorem
3.2
).
We proceed by recurrence. It is true for
n=2
(see
[
3
]
).
Assume that is true for
n-1
. By Lemma
4.2

(S_{1})
is with maximal rank, and by the recurrence hypothesis
(S_{2})
is also of maximal rank. Indeed,
(S_{2})
is related
to the
(n-1)
-tuple
(\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})
and satisfies the recurrence hypothesis:

\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=k-1-t_{1}=k^{\prime}-1\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma_{n-1}=t_{2}+\cdots+t_{n}<k-t_{1}-1=k^{\prime}-1.

The system
(S^{\prime}_{1})
is also with maximal rank, indeed it is also related to the
(n-1)
-tuple
(\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})
and
satisfies the recurrence hypothesis:

\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=k-t_{1}=k^{\prime\prime}-1\quad\text{

Now, we look to the rank of (

) by combining

and
(S_{2})

f

) is of rank

.

[

Abstract
. Recently, Juyal, Maji and Sathyanarayana have studied a Lambert series associated with a cusp form over the full modular group and the Möbius function. In this paper, we investigate the Lambert series ∑ n = 1 ∞ [ a f ​ ( n ) ​ ψ ​ ( n ) ∗ μ ⁡ ( n ) ​ ψ ′ ​ ( n ) ] ​ exp ⁡ ( − n ​ y ) , \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny), where a f ​ ( n ) a_{f}(n) is the n n th Fourier coefficient of a cusp form f f over any congruence subgroup, and ψ \psi and ψ ′ \psi^{\prime} are primitive Dirichlet characters. This extends the earlier work to the case of higher level subgroups and also gives a character analogue.



1.
Introduction

Let
{a(n)}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The Lambert series is defined by
F(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}
for
|q|<1
. By setting
b(n)=\sum_{d|n}a(d)
, we have
F(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b(n)q^{n}
. Over the years, this family of Lambert series has been studied by many mathematicians
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
. Hardy and Ramanujan
[
8
]
used the behaviour of certain Lambert series as
q\rightarrow 1
to derive an asymptotic expansion for a general partition function.

Let
\Delta(z):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)\exp(2\pi inz)
be the Ramanujan cusp form of weight 12. In 1981, Zagier
[
15
, p. 417]
predicted that the constant term of the automorphic form
y^{12}|\Delta(z)|^{2}
, that is, the Lambert series

a_{0}(y):=y^{12}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{2}(n)\exp({-4\pi ny})

has an asymptotic expansion
of the form

a_{0}(y)\sim C+\sum_{\rho}C_{\rho}y^{1-\frac{\rho}{2}}\quad\textrm{as}\,\,y\rightarrow 0^{+},

(1.1)

where
C
and
C_{\rho}
are some constants and the sum over
\rho
runs through all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.
Further, he plotted the graph of
a_{0}(y)
and found an oscillatory behaviour as
y\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Under the assumption of the Riemann hypothesis, (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle a_{0}(y)\sim C+y^{3/4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\cos\left(\phi_{n}+\frac{t_{n}\log(y)}{2}\right),\quad{\rm as}\,\,y\rightarrow 0^{+},

where
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\pm it_{n}
and
a_{n}
,
\phi_{n}
are some constants.
This asymptotic expansion indicates that
a_{0}(y)
has an oscillatory behaviour as
y\rightarrow 0^{+}
due to the presence of cosine functions.
Although this conjecture was overlooked by mathematicians for almost two decades, it was eventually settled in 2000 by Hafner and Stopple
[
7
]
. Later, Chakraborthy et al.
[
5
]
observed a similar phenomenon for any Hecke eigen form over the full modular group and they
[
6
]
generalized it for congruence subgroups.

Recently, Juyal, Maji and Sathyanarayana
[
10
]
studied the Lambert series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_{f}*\mu)(n)\exp(-ny)
, where
a_{f}(n)
is the
n
th Fourier coefficient of a cusp form
f
of weight
k
over
\textrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function. They derived the following exact formula in terms of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
. In
[
11
]
, they have also studied a similar Lambert series associated with the symmetric square
L
-function.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
10
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
a_{f}(n)
be the
n
th Fourier coefficient of the cusp form
f\in S_{k}(SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Assume that all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then for any positive
y
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)*\mu(n)]e^{-ny}

\displaystyle=2\,\Gamma(k)\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}^{*}(n)}{n^{k}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k}{2},\frac{k+1}{2};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{y^{2}}{4n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}(y),

where

A_{f}^{*}(n):=\left(a_{f}*\mu_{k}\right)(n),\quad{\rm with}\quad\mu_{k}(n)=\mu(n)n^{k-1},

(1.2)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(y)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{L(f,\rho)\Gamma(\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\frac{1}{y^{\rho}},

(1.3)

the sum over
\rho
which runs through all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, involves bracketing the terms so that the terms corresponding to
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are included in the same bracket if they satisfy

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})-\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})|<\exp\left(-C\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})}{\log(\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1}))}\right)+\exp\left(-C\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})}{\log(\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2}))}\right),

(1.4)

where
C
is some positive constant.

In this paper, we investigate the following Lambert series:

\displaystyle A_{f}(y):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny),

(1.5)

where
a_{f}(n)
is the
n
th Fourier coefficient of a cusp form
f
of weight
k
, level
Q
and Nebentypus
\chi
, and
\psi,\psi^{\prime}
are primitive Dirichlet characters. We derive an exact formula for the above Lambert series (
1.5
) involving non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
and a generalized hypergeometric function, and there by generalize Theorem
1.1
in two different directions. On the one hand, our work generalize Theorem
1.1
for congruence subgroups and on the other hand, we also get a character analogue. As an application, we also derive an asymptotic expansion and establish an oscillatory behavior of
y^{1/2}A_{f}(y)
as
y\rightarrow 0^{+}.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
k
and
Q
be two positive integers. Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
Q
and the Gauss sum
\epsilon_{\chi}
is defined by
\epsilon_{\chi}:=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\chi(j)\exp\left({\frac{2\pi ij}{Q}}\right)
.
Define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(Q):=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c c}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):{c}\equiv 0\pmod{Q}\right\}.

We denote
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Q),\chi)
to be the space of cusp forms of weight
k
, level
Q
, and Nebentypus character
\chi
. Consider
f(z)\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Q),\chi)
with the Fourier series expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)\exp(2\pi inz),\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{H}.

(2.1)

The L-function associated to
f
is defined by
L_{f}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}
, which is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>\frac{k+1}{2}
. The analytic extension and functional equation for
L_{f}(s)
was proved by Hecke.
It is known that for a positive integer
r
such that
(Q,r)=1
and a primitive Dirichlet character
\psi
modulo
r
, the
\psi
-twist of
f
defined by

f_{\psi}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)\psi(n)\exp(2\pi inz),

is a element of
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi\psi^{2})
, where
N=Qr^{2}
.
Hence it is natural to consider the following Dirichlet series:

L_{f}(s,\psi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\psi(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)>\frac{k+1}{2}.

It is known as the
\psi
-twist of
L_{f}(s)
. We can analytically extend
L_{f}(s,\psi)
into an entire function and the completed
L
-function
\Lambda_{f}(s,\psi)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)
satisfies the following functional equation
[
12
, p. 131]

\Lambda_{f}(s,\psi)=\frac{i^{k}\epsilon_{\chi}^{2}}{r}\chi(r)\psi(Q)\Lambda_{g}(k-s,\overline{\psi}),

(2.2)

where

g(z)=Q^{k/2}(Qz)^{-k}f\left(-\frac{1}{Qz}\right):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{g}(n)\exp(2\pi inz)\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Q),\overline{\chi})).

Now we introduce two well-known special functions which are essential in this work.
Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}
be complex numbers such that
b_{j}\neq 0,-1,-2,...
for
1\leq j\leq q
. The generalized hypergeometric series
{}_{p}F_{q}\left(a_{1},\cdots,a_{p};\,b_{1},\cdots,b_{q};\,z\right)

[
13
, p. 404, Equation 16.2.1]
is defined by

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left(a_{1},\cdots,a_{p};\,b_{1},\cdots,b_{q};\,z\right):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}\cdots(a_{p})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}\cdots(b_{q})_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(2.3)

where
(a)_{n}:=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}
. This series converges for all complex values of
z
if
p\leq q
. For
p=q+1
, it converges for
|z|<1
an further it can be analytically continued to the complex plane with a branch cut from
1
to
+\infty
.

Let
m,n,p,q
be non-negative integers such that
0\leq m\leq q
,
0\leq n\leq p
. Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}
be complex numbers such that
a_{i}-b_{j}\not\in\mathbb{N}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
. Then the Meijer
G
-function
[
13
, p. 415, Definition 16.17]
is defined by the line integral:

\displaystyle G_{p,q}^{\,m,n}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}-s)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)z^{s}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}+s)\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}-s)}\mathrm{ds}.

(2.4)

Here we note that the line of integration
L
, goes from
-i\infty
to
+i\infty
, separates the poles of the factors
\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)
from the factors
\Gamma(b_{j}-s)
. This integral converges if
p+q<2(m+n)
and
|\arg(z)|<(m+n-\frac{p+q}{2})\pi
. Slater’s theorem
[
13
, p. 415, Equation 16.17.2]
gives a relation between Meijer
G
-function and generalized hypergeometric functions as follows:
If
p\leq q
, and
b_{j}-b_{k}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for
j\neq k
,
1\leq j,k\leq m
, then

\displaystyle G_{p,q}^{\,m,n}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{p,q,k}^{m,n}(z){}_{p}F_{q-1}\left(\begin{matrix}1+b_{k}-a_{1},\cdots,1+b_{k}-a_{p}\\
1+b_{k}-b_{1},\cdots,\star,\cdots,1+b_{k}-b_{q}\end{matrix}\Big|(-1)^{p-m-n}z\right),

where
\star
suggests that the term
1+b_{k}-b_{k}
is excluded and

\displaystyle A_{p,q,k}^{m,n}(z):=\frac{z^{b_{k}}\prod_{j=1,j\neq k}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}-b_{k})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1+b_{k}-a_{j})}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1+b_{k}-b_{j})\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}-b_{k})}.

3.
Statement of Results

For a fixed natural number
k
, we define
\mu_{k}(n):=\mu(n)n^{k-1}
.
Let
\psi^{\prime}
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
M
, and

a:=\begin{cases}0,&\psi^{\prime}(-1)=1,\\
1,&\psi^{\prime}(-1)=-1.\end{cases}

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Q),\chi)
be a cusp form with the
n
th Fourier coefficient as
a_{f}(n)
. Let
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
be primitive Dirichlet characters of modulus
r
and
M
respectively. Assume that all non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
are simple.
For any positive
y
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny)=2N^{k/2}\Gamma(k+a)\left(\frac{yN}{M}\right)^{a}\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{k+a}\frac{\chi(r)\psi(Q)\epsilon_{\psi}^{2}}{r\epsilon_{\psi^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[a_{g}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)\overline{\psi^{\prime}}(n)]}{n^{k+a}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-\frac{N^{2}y^{2}}{4M^{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-(1-a)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\mathcal{R}(y)+R_{0},

where
N=Qr^{2}
, and the terms
R_{0}
and
\mathcal{R}(y)
are defined as

\displaystyle R_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{L_{f}^{\prime}(0,\psi)}{L^{\prime}(0,\psi^{\prime})},&{\rm if}\,a=0\,{\rm and}\,M>1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{R}(y)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{L_{f}(\rho,\psi)\Gamma(\rho)}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\psi^{\prime})}\frac{1}{y^{\rho}},

(3.1)

where the sum over
\rho
in
\mathcal{R}(y)
, runs through all non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
, involves bracketing the terms so that the terms corresponding to
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are included in the same bracket if they satisfy

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})-\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})|<\exp\left(-\frac{C|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})|}{\log(|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})|+3)}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{C|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})|}{\log(|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})|+3)}\right),

(3.2)

where
C
is some positive constant.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
3.1
can be extended analytically for
\operatorname{Re}(y)>0.
Also, by substituting
Q=r=M=1
in Theorem
3.1
, one can immediately recover Theorem
1.1
.

As a special case of Theorem
3.1
, by taking
M=r=1
, we get a higher level analogue of Theorem
1.1
. We note this special case as a corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Q),\chi)
be a cusp form. Assume that all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple.
Then for
y>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)*\mu(n)]e^{-ny}

\displaystyle=\frac{i^{k}\Gamma(k)Q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{2^{k-1}\pi^{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[a_{g}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)]}{n^{k}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k}{2},\frac{k+1}{2};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{Q^{2}y^{2}}{4n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{R}(y),

where
\mathcal{R}(y)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{L_{f}(\rho)\Gamma(\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\frac{1}{y^{\rho}}
, the sum over
\rho
runs through all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
involving bracketing as in (
3.2
).

On the other hand, if we let
Q=1,M=r,
and
\psi=\psi^{\prime}
in Theorem
3.1
, we get a character analogue of Theorem
1.1
. Mainly, we obtain the following identity.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\rm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
be a cusp form. Let
\psi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
r
. Assume that all non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n)[a_{f}(n)*\mu(n)]\exp(-ny)=R_{0}+\mathcal{R}(y)+2y^{a}r^{k+a-1}\epsilon_{\psi}\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{k+a}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\psi}(n)[a_{f}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)]}{n^{k+a}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-\frac{r^{2}y^{2}}{4n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-(1-a)\right],

where
R_{0}
is defined as in Theorem
3.1

and
\mathcal{R}(y)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{L_{f}(\rho,\psi)\Gamma(\rho)}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\psi)}\frac{1}{y^{\rho}}

Now letting
Q=r=1
in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
f\in S_{k}(\rm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[a_{f}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)\overline{\psi^{\prime}}(n)]}{n^{k+a}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-\frac{y^{2}}{4M^{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-(1-a)\right],

.

4.
Proofs of main results

We begin by collecting a few well-known results which will be essential for the proof of the main theorem. First, we state an important asymptotic expansion of the gamma function, which is popularly known Stirlng’s formula.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
s=\sigma+iT
in a vertical strip
\alpha\leq\sigma\leq\beta
,

|\Gamma(\sigma+iT)|=\sqrt{2\pi}|T|^{\sigma-1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi|T|}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|T|}\right)\right)\quad{\rm as}\quad|T|\rightarrow\infty.

(4.1)

We refer
[
9
, p. 151]
for the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any complex number
z
, we have

\Gamma(2z)=\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2})\,2^{2z}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}.

(4.2)

This is the duplication formula for the gamma function. The next result gives an important bound for the inverse of the Dirichlet
L
-function.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume there exist a sequence of positive numbers
T
with arbitrary large absolute value satisfying
|T-\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|>\exp(-A|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|/\log(|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|))
for every non-trivial zero
\rho
of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
, where
A
is some suitable positive constant. Then,

\frac{1}{|L(\sigma+iT,\psi^{\prime})|}<e^{BT},

for some suitable constant
0<B<\pi/4
.

The proof of this lemma can be found in Titchmarsh
[
14
, p. 219]
.
∎The next result says that any
L
-function associated to a cusp form can be bounded by a suitable polynomial in a vertical strip.

Lemma 4.4
.

In any vertical strip
\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma\leq b
,
there exist a
constant
C(\sigma_{0})
, such that

|L_{f}(\sigma+iT,\psi)|\ll|T|^{C(\sigma_{0})}

as
|T|\rightarrow\infty.

One can see this result in
[
9
, p. 97, Lemma 5.2]
.
∎Next, we state the functional equation for the Dirichlet
L
-function.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\psi^{\prime}
be a Dirichlet character of Modulo
M
. Then the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\psi^{\prime})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(n)}{n^{s}}
analytically extends to the whole complex plane and satisfies the functional equation:

\left(\frac{\pi}{M}\right)^{-\frac{s+a}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+a}{2}\right)L(s,\psi^{\prime})=\frac{\epsilon_{\psi}^{\prime}}{i^{a}\sqrt{M}}\left(\frac{\pi}{M}\right)^{-\frac{1-s+a}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s+a}{2}\right)L(1-s,\overline{\psi^{\prime}}).

We refer
[
9
]
for the proof.
∎Now we are ready to give the proof of the main theorem.

First, we note that the Lambert series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny)

converges absolutely and uniformly for any
y>0
.
Now using inverse Mellin transform for
\Gamma(s)
, one can write

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{(ny)^{s}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s,

(4.3)

here interchange of summation and integration is possible only when we start with
\operatorname{Re}(s)=c>\frac{k+1}{2}
.
Next, to simplify this line integral we shall take help of contour integration and use Cauchy’s residue theorem.
Consider the contour
\mathcal{C}_{T}
determined by the line segments
[c-iT,c+iT],[c+iT,\lambda+iT],[\lambda+iT,\lambda-iT]
, and
[\lambda-iT,c-iT]
, where
T
is some large positive real number and
-1<\lambda<0
. Before using Cauchy’s residue theorem, let us identify the poles of the integrand function. From (
2.2
), it follows that
\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)
has no poles since
\Lambda_{f}(s,\psi)
is an entire function.
Hence poles of the integrand are only due to the zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
. Note that, if
\psi^{\prime}
is an even character of modulus
M>1
, then
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
has trivial zeros at
0,-2,-4,\cdots
. And if
\psi^{\prime}
is an odd character, then
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
has trivial zeros at
-1,-3,-5,\cdots
.
Again, we know that the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
lie in the strip
0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1
. Therefore, applying Cauchy’s residue theorem, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{T}}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s=\mathcal{R}_{T}(y)+R_{0},

(4.4)

where
\mathcal{R}_{T}(y)
denotes the residual function, which includes finitely many terms contributed by the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
with
|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|<T
and
R_{0}
is the residue at
s=0
.
Now, we can write

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{T}}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s=\left(\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{\lambda+iT}+\int_{\lambda+iT}^{\lambda-iT}+\int_{\lambda-iT}^{c-iT}\right)\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s.

(4.5)

Next, utilizing Lemma
4.1
,
4.3
and
4.4
, one can show that both of the horizontal integrals

\displaystyle H_{1}(T,y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+iT}^{\lambda+iT}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s,\,\,H_{2}(T,y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma(s)\lambda-iT}^{c-iT}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s,

tend to zero as
T\rightarrow\infty
through those values of
T
which satisfy
|T-\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|>\exp(-A|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|/\log(|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|))
.
Therefore, letting
T\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.4
) and in view of (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), we arrive at

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]e^{-ny}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\lambda-i\infty}^{\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s+\mathcal{R}(y)+R_{0},

(4.6)

where the contribution of the residual term
R_{0}
will be taken into account only when
\psi^{\prime}
is an even character with modulus
M>1
. Therefore, we have

R_{0}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}s\,\Gamma(s)\frac{L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}=\begin{cases}\frac{L_{f}^{\prime}(0,\psi)}{L^{\prime}(0,\psi^{\prime})},&{\rm if}\,\,a=0,M>1,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

The function
\mathcal{R}(y)
is the sum of the residual terms coming from the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
. This term can be evaluated in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(y)=\sum_{\rho}\lim_{s\rightarrow\rho}(s-\rho)\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\Gamma(\rho)L_{f}(\rho,\psi)}{L^{\prime}(\rho,\psi^{\prime})y^{-\rho}},

(4.8)

where the summation runs over the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
. Here we note that we have used the assumption that all non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
are simple. Even if we do not assume the simplicity hypothesis, then also one can figure out this residual term.
Now we shall try to evaluate the left vertical integral

V(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\lambda-i\infty}^{\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}y^{-s}{\rm d}s,

(4.9)

where
-1<\lambda<0
. The functional equation (
2.2
) of
L_{f}(s,\psi)
suggests that

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)L_{f}(s,\psi)=i^{k}\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}\right)^{k-2s}\frac{\epsilon_{\psi}^{2}}{r}\chi(r)\psi(Q)\Gamma(k-s)L_{g}(k-s,\overline{\psi}).

(4.10)

Again, Lemma
4.5
yields that

\frac{1}{L(s,\psi^{\prime})}=\frac{i^{a}\sqrt{M}}{\epsilon_{\psi^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{\pi}{M}\right)^{\frac{1-2s}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s+a}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1-s+a}{2})}\frac{1}{L(1-s,\overline{\psi^{\prime}})}.

(4.11)

Now substituting (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) in (
4.9
) and simplifying, we get

V(y)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}i^{k+a}N^{\frac{k}{2}}\epsilon_{\psi}^{2}\chi(r)\psi(Q)}{2^{k}\pi^{k}r\epsilon_{\psi^{\prime}}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\lambda-i\infty}^{\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s+a}{2})\Gamma(k-s)}{\Gamma(\frac{1-s+a}{2})}\frac{L_{g}(k-s,\overline{\psi})}{L(1-s,\overline{\psi^{\prime}})}\left(\frac{Ny}{4\pi M}\right)^{-s}{\rm d}s.

After a change of variable from
s
to
k-s
,
V(y)
takes the following form:

V(y)=C_{k,\chi,\psi,\psi^{\prime}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{k-\lambda-i\infty}^{k-\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{k-s+a}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s-k+1+a}{2}\right)}\frac{L_{g}(s,\overline{\psi})}{L(s-k+1,\overline{\psi^{\prime}})}\left(\frac{Ny}{4\pi M}\right)^{s}{\rm d}s,

(4.12)

where

C_{k,\chi,\psi,\psi^{\prime}}:=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}i^{k+a}\epsilon_{\psi}^{2}\chi(r)\psi(Q)}{r\epsilon_{\psi^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{2M}{y\sqrt{N}}\right)^{k}.

(4.13)

Note that the Dirichlet series expansion of
L_{g}(s,\overline{\psi})
and
L(s-k+1,\overline{\psi^{\prime}})
are absolutely convergent on the line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=k-\lambda
since
k-\lambda>k
as
-1<\lambda<0
. Therefore, on the line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=k-\lambda
, with the help of the Dirichlet series expansion, one can write

\frac{L_{g}(s,\overline{\psi})}{L(s-k+1,\overline{\psi^{\prime}})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{g}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)\overline{\psi^{\prime}}(n)}{n^{s}}.

(4.14)

Now, plugging (
4.14
) in (
4.12
) and then taking summation outside of integration, we get

V(y)=C_{k,\chi,\psi,\psi^{\prime}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{g}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)\overline{\psi^{\prime}}(n)]\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{k-\lambda-i\infty}^{k-\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{k-s+a}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s-k+1+a}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{Ny}{4\pi Mn}\right)^{s}{\rm d}s.

(4.15)

To simplify
V(y)
further, we shall concentrate on the following integral:

U_{n,a}(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{k-\lambda-i\infty}^{k-\lambda+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{k-s+a}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s-k+1+a}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{Ny}{4\pi Mn}\right)^{s}{\rm d}s,

(4.16)

by doing a change of variable from
s
to
2s
and invoking Lemma
4.2
, it takes the shape as

U_{n,a}(y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{k-\lambda}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{k-\lambda}{2}+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1-k+a}{2}\right)}z^{s}{\rm d}s,

(4.17)

where
z=\left(\frac{Ny}{2\pi Mn}\right)^{2}
. To make more comprehensible form for
U_{n,a}(y)
, we must employ the definition of the Meijer
G
-function. Unfortunately, by analysing the poles of the integrand of
U_{n,a}(y)
we can verify that the line of integration
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{k-\lambda}{2}
does not distinguish all the poles of
\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)
from the poles of
\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Note that
\frac{k}{2}<\frac{k-\lambda}{2}<\frac{k+1}{2}
as
-1<\lambda<0
. Thus, we shift the line of integration
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{k-\lambda}{2}
to the line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=c^{\prime}
where
\frac{k}{2}-1<c^{\prime}<\frac{k}{2}
. Now we can see that the line of integration
\operatorname{Re}(s)=c^{\prime}
does separate the poles. At this moment, we construct a new rectangular contour
\mathfrak{C}
joining the line segments
[c^{\prime}-iT,\frac{k-\lambda}{2}-iT],\left[\frac{k-\lambda}{2}-iT,\frac{k-\lambda}{2}+iT\right],\left[\frac{k-\lambda}{2}+iT,c^{\prime}+iT\right],
and
[c^{\prime}+iT,c^{\prime}-iT]
and employing Cacuchy’s residue theorem, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathfrak{C}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1-k+a}{2}\right)}z^{s}{\rm d}s=R_{\frac{k}{2}},

(4.18)

where
R_{\frac{k}{2}}
is the residue at
s=\frac{k}{2}
, and it can be evaluated as

\displaystyle R_{\frac{k}{2}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+a\right)}z^{\frac{k}{2}}&{\rm if}\,\,a=0,\\
0,&{\rm if}\,\,a=1.\end{cases}

(4.19)

Now using Stirling’s formula for gamma function, one can show that the contribution of the horizontal integrals vanish as
T\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, letting
T\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.18
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{k-\lambda}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{k-\lambda}{2}+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1-k+a}{2}\right)}z^{s}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c^{\prime}-i\infty}^{c^{\prime}+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1-k+a}{2}\right)}z^{s}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+R_{\frac{k}{2}}.

(4.20)

Now utilizing the definition (
2.4
) of the Meijer
G
-function and verifying all the necessary conditions, one can show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c^{\prime}-i\infty}^{c^{\prime}+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1-k+a}{2}\right)}z^{s}{\rm d}s=G_{2,2}^{\,1,2}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}1,\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{1+k-a}{2}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right).

(4.21)

At this situation, we shall invoke Slater’s theorem (
2.5
) to simplify Meijer
G
-function in terms of hypergeometric functions. Thus after a significant simplification, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{2,2}^{\,1,2}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}1,\frac{1}{2}\\
\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{1+k-a}{2}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right)=z^{\frac{k+a}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+a}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+k+a}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+a\right)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-z\right)

(4.22)

Now substituting
z=\left(\frac{Ny}{2\pi Mn}\right)^{2}
in (
4.22
) and in view of (
4.19
), (
4
) and (
4.21
), the integral
U_{n,a}(y)
becomes

\displaystyle U_{n,a}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{Ny}{2\pi Mn}\right)^{k+a}\frac{\Gamma(k+a)}{2^{k}}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-z\right)-(1-a)\right].

(4.23)

Plugging the above expression of
U_{n,a}(y)
in (
4.15
), the final expression for the left vertical integral
V(y)
reduces to

\displaystyle V(y)

\displaystyle=2N^{\frac{k}{2}+a}\Gamma(k+a)\left(\frac{y}{M}\right)^{a}\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{k+a}\frac{\chi(r)\psi(Q)\epsilon_{\psi}^{2}}{r\epsilon_{\psi^{\prime}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[a_{g}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)*\mu_{k}(n)\overline{\psi^{\prime}}(n)]}{n^{k+a}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\times\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-\frac{N^{2}y^{2}}{4M^{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)-(1-a)\right].

(4.24)

Using the definition (
2.3
) of the hypergeometric series one can show that the above infinite series is indeed convergent.
Finally, combining (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
4
), we finish the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{k+a}{2},\frac{k+1+a}{2};\frac{1+2a}{2};-\frac{N^{2}y^{2}}{2^{2}M^{2}n^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M^{\prime}-1}\frac{\left(\frac{k+a}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{k+1+a}{2}\right)_{m}}{\left(\frac{1+2a}{2}\right)_{m}m!}\left(\frac{-Ny}{2Mn\pi}\right)^{2m}

5.
Final remarks

In 2018, Banerjee and Chakraborty
[
3
]
established an asymptotic expansion for the Lambert series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)\overline{\lambda_{g}(n)}\exp(-ny)
, where
\lambda_{f}(n)
and
\lambda_{g}(n)
are
n
th Fourier coefficient of Hecke Mass cusp forms
f
and
g
respectively. Recently, the same Lambert series corresponding to the Fourier coefficents of Hilbert modular forms has been studied by Agnihotri
[
2
]
.
In this paper, we have established an exact formula the Lambert series

A_{f}(y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[a_{f}(n)\psi(n)*\mu(n)\psi^{\prime}(n)]\exp(-ny),
in terms of the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\psi^{\prime})
, where
a_{f}(n)
is the
n
th Fourier coefficient of a cusp form
f
over a congruence subgroup, and
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
are primitive Dirichlet characters. Interestingly, we have observed that
y^{1/2}A_{f}(y)
has an oscillatory behaviour as
y\rightarrow 0^{+}
. It would be desirable to study similar Lambert series associated to the
n
th Fourier coefficient of other automorphic forms.

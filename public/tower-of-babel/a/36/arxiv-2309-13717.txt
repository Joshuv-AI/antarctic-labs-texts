On the Petrov Type of a 4-manifold
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. On an oriented 4-manifold, we examine the geometry that arises when the curvature operator of a Riemannian or Lorentzian metric g g commutes, not with its own Hodge star operator, but rather with that of another semi-Riemannian metric h h that is a suitable deformation of g g . We classify the case when one of these metrics is Riemannian and the other Lorentzian by generalizing the concept of Petrov Type from general relativity; the case when h h is split-signature is also examined. The “generalized Petrov Types” so obtained are shown to relate to the critical points of g g ’s sectional curvature, and sometimes yield unique normal forms. They also carry topological information independent of the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality, and yield a direct geometric formulation of “almost-Einsten” metric via the Ricci or sectional curvature of g g .



1.
Introduction

The Petrov classification of 4-dimensional spacetimes, due to A. Petrov
[
Pet69
]
, is a foundational result in gravitational physics, a classification of Lorentzian metrics by their Weyl curvature tensors; see
[
O’N95
,
SKM+09
]
for modern treatments. Somewhat less well known is its reformulation due to J. Thorpe
[
Tho69a
]
, who showed that a spacetime’s “Petrov Type” is in fact the Lorentzian analogue of the “normal form” of an oriented 4-dimensional Riemannian manifold. The latter refers to those Riemannian 4-manifolds
(M,g)
whose curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
is determined by knowledge of just the critical points and values of its sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
, when the latter is viewed as the quadratic form of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
. The most well known examples of such 4-manifolds, discovered independently by M. Berger
[
Ber61
]
and I. Singer & Thorpe
[
ST69
]
, are Einstein metrics; i.e., those Riemannian metrics
g
whose Ricci tensor
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
satisfies
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}=\lambda g
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The reason for this is that in dimension 4, the Einstein condition arises precisely when
g
’s Hodge star operator
*\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
commutes with
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
:

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*=*\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}.

As shown in
[
Tho69a
]
, this commutativity is also the origin of Petrov Type, but with the Weyl curvature in place of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
. Given this common origin, the purpose of this article is to generalize (
1
) by examining the geometry that arises when
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
and
*
arise from
different metrics
on
M
; i.e., when the curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
of a Riemannian or Lorentzian metric commutes, not with its own Hodge star
*
, but rather with that of another semi-Riemannian metric
h
that is a suitable deformation of
g
. Semi-Riemannian here means that
h
can be a Riemannian, a Lorentzian, or a “split-signature” metric; i.e., a metric of signature
(2,2)
. As we will show, in each of these cases there is geometric and sometimes even topological information contained in the symmetric or “Weyl-like part” of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
, that is to say, in that portion of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
that commutes with
h
’s Hodge star operator
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}
:

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\pm*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}})\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}=*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}\circ\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}.

(If
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}
were replaced by
*
here, then
\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}=\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}+\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}}{12}I
, where
\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
is the Weyl curvature operator of
g
and
\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}
is its scalar curvature.) What we mean by “suitable deformation of
g
” is simply that
h
has orthonormal frames in common with
g
. For such
h
, we prove the following:

i.

When
g
is Riemannian and
h
Lorentzian, we take the latter to be of the form
h\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g-2T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat}
with
T
a
g
-unit length vector field on
M
. For such
h
, we then proceed to generalize the notion of Petrov Type by classifying the
\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
’s in (
2
) analogously to
[
Tho69a
]
. As shown in Theorems
3
and
5
, there will be three Petrov Types, given by the Jordan normal forms of
\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
when the latter is viewed as a complex-linear map via the complex multiplication on
\Lambda^{2}
provided by
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}^{2}=-1
.

ii.

The best case is when
\hat{S}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
(i.e., when
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
itself commutes with
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{h}}}
). It yields a notion of “almost-Einstein” metric generalizing
\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}=\lambda g
, whether
h
is Lorentzian or split-signature. While the latter case is more direct (Theorem
6
), the former is also noteworthy (Theorems
1
and
2
), because “almost-Einstein” is the best one can do if
M
is compact: There are no non-flat Einstein metrics when
M
has Euler characteristic zero
[
Ber61
]
, as it must if it is to admit a Lorentzian metric. Even more, Theorems
1
and
4
show that when
h
is Lorentzian,
g
’s Petrov Type is related to the
number
of critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
, and that there is a further topological obstruction: The signature of
M
must be zero, too. (This is derived independently of the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality
[
Tho69b
,
Hit74
]
; i.e., without assuming the existence of an Einstein metric on
M
.)

iii.

Finally, we examine the case when
g
is Lorentzian and
h\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g+2T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat}
is Riemannian, or when both are Riemannian of any form. These yield the best results, provided one uses a variant of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
that is self-adjoint with respect to
h
. With this modification, Theorem
7
shows that there is a
unique
normal form (Petrov Type), that it is determined entirely by the critical points and values of a certain quadratic form, and that it yields an “almost-Einstein” as in ii. above.

2.
The Hodge star, normal forms, and Petrov Types

In this section we provide a brief overview as well as some historical remarks regarding normal forms and the Hodge star operator in both the Riemannian and Lorentzian settings. The study of “normal forms” for curvature operators is motivated by a well known fact from linear algebra: On any finite-dimensional inner product space
(V,\langle\,,\rangle)
, a self-adjoint linear transformation
T\colon V\longrightarrow V
is determined by the critical points and values of its
quadratic form
function, defined on the unit sphere of
V
as follows:

(3)

\displaystyle v\mapsto\langle Tv,v\rangle\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt|v|=1.

The connection to Riemannian geometry is that the sectional curvature of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
can also be realized as a quadratic form. First, fix
p\in M
and observe that the second exterior product
\Lambda^{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Lambda^{2}(T_{p}M)
inherits an inner product
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}
from
g
as follows: For all
v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in T_{p}M
,

(4)

\displaystyle\langle{v_{1}\wedge w_{1}},{v_{2}\wedge w_{2}}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{det}\begin{bmatrix}g(v_{1},v_{2})&g(v_{1},w_{2})\\
g(w_{1},v_{2})&g(w_{1},w_{2})\end{bmatrix}\cdot

With this in hand, we may express the Riemann curvature 4-tensor
R
as a linear map
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
called the
curvature operator
, whose action
v\wedge w\mapsto\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w)
is defined to be the unique 2-vector satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w)},{x\wedge y}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-R(v,w,x,y)\hskip 14.45377pt\text{for all $x,y\in T_{p}M$}.

Owing to the symmetry
R_{ijkl}=R_{klij}
, observe that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
is
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}
-self-adjoint. It is with respect to
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
that the sectional curvature of
g
is a quadratic form. Indeed, for any orthonormal pair
v,w\in T_{p}M
, the sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
of the 2-plane
v\wedge w
is

(6)

\displaystyle\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=R(v,w,w,v)=\underbrace{\,\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w)},{v\wedge w}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}\,}_{\text{``$\langle Tv,v\rangle$"}}.

(Our sign convention is
R(a,b,c,d)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g(\nabla_{\!a}{\!\nabla_{\!b}{\,c}}-\nabla_{\!b}{\!\nabla_{\!a}{c}}-\nabla_{\![a,b]}{c},d)
.) Given this analogy with (
3
), it is thus natural to study
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
by studying the critical point behavior of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
— the goal being to classify those curvature tensors which are determined by knowledge of just the critical point structure of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
. Such metrics are then said to have a “normal form.” (Observe that this problem is more difficult than (
3
) because not all unit 2-vectors in
\Lambda^{2}
correspond to 2-planes in
T_{p}M
. Rather, only those unit 2-vectors
\xi
that are decomposable do; i.e., those that can be written as
\xi=v\wedge w
for
v,w\in T_{p}M
.) As shown in
[
ST69
]
, if one restricts attention to the class of Einstein metrics on a smooth 4-manifold
M
, then such metrics are indeed determined solely by the critical point structure of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
. As mentioned in the Introduction, what makes this beautiful result possible is that, in dimension 4, Einstein metrics
g
are exactly those that satisfy (
1
), where the Hodge star operator
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}
is defined as sending any 2-plane
v\wedge w
to its
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}
-orthogonal complement. The precise definition is

(7)

\displaystyle\xi\wedge*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}\eta\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\langle{\xi},{\eta}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}\,dV\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\xi,\eta\in\Lambda^{2},

where
dV
is the orientation form in the 1-dimensional space
\Lambda^{4}(T_{p}M)
. (As we’ll see below, these definitions would not change if we replaced the Riemannian metric
g
with a Lorentzian metric
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
.) Thanks to
*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}
, there is in fact another way to express the Einstein condition in dimension 4 (see
[
ST69
]
):

(8)

g
is Einstein
\iff\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w)=\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}(v\wedge w))
.

I.e.,
g
is Einstein if and only if the sectional curvature of each 2-plane is equal to that of its orthogonal complement. With these preliminaries out of the way, let us now comment briefly on what else has been done regarding normal forms in the Riemannian setting. Continuing the study of the pointwise sectional curvature function
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
, Thorpe analyzed its zero set when
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\geq 0
in
[
Tho71
]
, and also gave a condition for a 4-manifold to have
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}>0
, in
[
Tho72
]
. Dimension 4 is particularly rich: Aside from Einstein metrics, the class of Kähler 4-manifolds with positive Ricci curvature also possesses a normal form, as shown in
[
Joh80a
]
. In fact Johnson also found normal forms for 6-dimensional Kähler manifolds with positive sectional curvature, in
[
Joh80b
]
. As one may imagine, however, in higher dimensions the behavior of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
becomes more difficult to analyze in general, as shown in
[
Zol79
]
; indeed, aside from the Kähler case in dimension 6, the author knows of only one other higher-dimensional result, in
[
Zol80
]
, who found normal forms for a special class of curvature operators in dimension 5. Nevertheless, the study of sectional curvature related to and inspired by these works continues to the present day; e.g., on a variant of
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
known as the
curvature operator of the second kind
(see, e.g., the recent works
[
CGT23
,
Li23
]
). So, too, does the quest for normal forms — even in dimension 4. Indeed,
[
CT16
, Prop. 2.4]
recently showed that gradient Ricci 4-solitons, which satisfy

\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}+\text{Hess}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}f=\lambda g

for some smooth function
f
on
M
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, also possess a normal form with respect to the operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}+\hat{H}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
, where
\hat{H}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Hess}_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}f{\footnotesize\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}\,g
.

Now we turn to Lorentzian geometry. First, recall that a
Lorentzian metric

g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
on a smooth manifold
M
is a smooth nondegenerate metric with signature
(-\!+\!+\!\cdots+)
. The absence of positive-definiteness implies that all nonzero vectors
X\in TM
come in three flavors:

\displaystyle\text{$X$ is}~\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\text{``spacelike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(X,X)>0$},\\
\text{``timelike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(X,X)<0$},\\
\text{``lightlike"}&\text{if}&\text{$g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(X,X)=0$}.\end{array}\right.

The Hodge star operator
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
of a Lorentzian 4-manifold
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})
is defined in perfect analogy with that of a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
; however, owing to the different signature, let us analyze
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
with some care. To begin with, in order to have orthonormal frames in common with
g
, our
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
’s will not be chosen arbitrarily, but rather as follows: For a suitable choice of
g
-unit length vector field
T
, let us form the metric

(13)

\displaystyle g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g-2T^{\flat}\otimes T^{\flat},

where
T^{\flat}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=g(T,\cdot)
is the one-form
g
-metrically equivalent to
T
. Notice that
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(T,T)=-1
, so that
T
is unit
timelike
with respect to
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
.
Note also that any
g
-orthonormal basis containing
T
is also a
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthonormal basis, and vice-versa
— a “deformation of
g
” as we called it in the Introduction. Generally speaking, the more “distinguished”
T
is — e.g., if it is closed,
dT^{\flat}=0
, or if it is a Killing vector field,
\mathfrak{L}_{T}g=0
— the more similar the properties of
g
and
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
will be; see
[
Ole14
]
for a careful treatment. In any case, whatever the choice of
T
, if we take an oriented local
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
with
e_{1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=T
, then the 2-vectors

(14)

\displaystyle\{e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\,,\,e_{1}\wedge e_{3}\,,\,e_{1}\wedge e_{4}\,,\,e_{3}\wedge e_{4}\,,\,e_{4}\wedge e_{2}\,,\,e_{2}\wedge e_{3}\}

will comprise an orthonormal basis for
\Lambda^{2}
with respect to the Lorentzian inner product
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
on
\Lambda^{2}
, which is defined as
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}
was in (
4
):

(15)

\displaystyle\langle{v_{1}\wedge w_{1}},{v_{2}\wedge w_{2}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{det}\begin{bmatrix}g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(v_{1},v_{2})&g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(v_{1},w_{2})\\
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(w_{1},v_{2})&g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(w_{1},w_{2})\end{bmatrix}\cdot

(Note that the first three basis elements in (
14
) are all timelike,

\langle{e_{1}\wedge e_{i}},{e_{1}\wedge e_{i}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=-1\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pti=2,3,4,

so that
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
has signature
(-\!-\!-\!+\!++)
.) Now we can define the Hodge star operator
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
with respect to
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
, in perfect analogy with (
7
):

\xi\wedge*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\eta\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\langle{\xi},{\eta}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}\,dV\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\xi,\eta\in\Lambda^{2}.

Bearing in mind that
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{1},e_{1})=-1
, observe that the action of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
on the basis (
14
) is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=-e_{3}\wedge e_{4},\\
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{3})=-e_{4}\wedge e_{2},\\
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{4})=-e_{2}\wedge e_{3},\end{array}\right.\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{3}\wedge e_{4})=e_{1}\wedge e_{2},\\
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{4}\wedge e_{2})=e_{1}\wedge e_{3},\\
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{2}\wedge e_{3})=e_{1}\wedge e_{4},\end{array}\right.

or in block matrix form,

(23)

\displaystyle*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}=\begin{bmatrix}O&I\\
-I&O\end{bmatrix},

where
I
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix. By contrast, the Riemannian Hodge star (
7
), with respect to the same basis (
14
), is

(24)

\displaystyle*_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{g}}}=\begin{bmatrix}O&I\\
I&O\end{bmatrix}\cdot

The difference is that, while
*
is an involution, splitting
\Lambda^{2}
into the direct sum
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}\oplus\Lambda^{-}
of
self-dual
(
*\xi=\xi
) and
anti-self-dual
(
*\xi=-\xi
) eigenvenctors,
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is not (even though
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
-self-adjoint). Indeed,
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}^{2}=-1.
However, the complex multiplication on
\Lambda^{2}
that the latter defines will be crucial to our construction of “Riemannian Petrov Type” below — just as it was in the construction of Petrov Type in
[
Tho69a
]
(see also
[
Bes07
, pp. 98ff.]
and
[
O’N95
, Chapter 5]
). Let us describe this now. Because
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}^{2}=-1
, one loses the self-dual/anti-self-dual splitting of
\Lambda^{2}
, but gains a complex structure in the process, via
i\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
. Then the commuting condition

\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}=*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}},

where
\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
is the Weyl curvature operator of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
— which, being trace-free, automatically commutes with
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
— turns
\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
into a
complex-linear
map on
\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
. The “Petrov Type of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
” is then precisely the complex eigenstructure of
\hat{W}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
. This eigenstructure was shown in
[
Tho69a
]
to coincide with the
number
of critical points of
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
’s sectional curvature function. (E.g., the Petrov Type of vacuum black holes is equivalent to their sectional curvature functions having exactly one “spacelike” critical point at any point on
M
.) Therefore, by having a Riemannian
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
commute with a Lorentzian
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
, we can realize complex eigenstructures for
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
and thus “Riemannian Petrov Types.” (Cf. with
[
AG97
]
, wherein it was shown that an oriented Einstein 4-manifold will admit a (locally) Hermitian structure if and only if its self-dual Weyl tensor
{\hat{W}}^{+}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{W}+\hat{W}\circ*)
has at least two of its three eigenvalues equal.) As we do so, the following is helpful to keep in mind:

Definition 1
.

Let
(M,g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}})
be an oriented Lorentzian 4-manifold. At any
p\in M
, let
P
denote an oriented 2-dimensional subspace of
T_{p}M

The “intermediate metrics” whose geometry we will be studying fit within the following larger class of “almost-Einstein” metrics:

Definition 2

(almost-Einstein metric)
.

An oriented Riemannian 4-manifold
(M,g)
is

T
on
M

\{T,X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}

(29)

\displaystyle\emph{\text{$\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}$}}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&\psi_{1}&\psi_{2}&\psi_{3}\\

\psi_{2}&0&\lambda&0\\
\psi_{3}&0&0&\lambda\end{bmatrix}\cdot

Here

are smooth functions.

Einstein metrics are locally, if not always globally, almost-Einstein. Almost-Einstein metrics therefore deform the Einstein condition “in the direction of a fixed vector field

.” Our motivation for considering them is that, on the 4-manifolds we’ll be considering, non-flat Einstein metrics don’t even exist, leaving metrics “close” to Einstein as the only possibility:

Lemma 1
.

Let
g
be an almost-Einstein metric on a closed 4-manifold. If
g
is Einstein, then it is flat.

Proof.

If the closed 4-manifold
M
globally admits a nowhere vanishing vector field
T
, then it must have Euler characteristic zero:
\chi(M)=0
. But by Berger
[
Ber61
]
, on such a 4-manifold any Einstein metric
g
must be flat; indeed, by the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula in dimension 4,

\chi(M)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}|W_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\cancelto{0}{\mathring{\text{Ric}}}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{24}|\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}|^{2},

where
\mathring{\text{Ric}}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Ric}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}-\frac{\text{scal}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}}{4}g
vanishes identically for Einstein metrics.
∎

On the other hand, let us observe in passing that almost-Einstein metrics certainly do exist locally:

Lemma 2
.

Let
\lambda,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}
be smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{4}

Proof.

The 2-tensor (

\lambda\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\lambda\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt\lambda\pm(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}+\psi_{3}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}+\psi_{3}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big|_{\bf 0}\neq\lambda\big|_{\bf 0}\neq 0

, in which case the 4-tensor defined in the coordinate frame on

by (

whose Ricci tensor is, with respect to the coordinate frame

∎

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}
defined on each
G_{+}(p)\cup G_{-}(p)\subseteq T_{p}M
by

(39)

\displaystyle\text{\emph{$\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}P},{P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}$}},

is called the
L
-
sectional curvature of
g
.

Observe that
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}
is not defined for
degenerate
2-planes
P
(i.e., those 2-dimensional subspaces
P\subseteq T_{p}M
on which
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}|_{P}
is degenerate). On the other hand, there are 2-planes
P
at which
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}
and
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
agree:

Lemma 3
.

If
P\in G_{+}(p)\cup G_{-}(p)
contains
T
or is orthogonal to
T
, then
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(P)=\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)
.

Proof.

If
P\in G_{+}(p)\cup G_{-}(p)
contains
T
, then we may write
P=T\wedge w
for some
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-unit
w
that is
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthogonal to
T
(
w
must be spacelike; see, e.g.,
[
O’N83
, Lemma 26, p. 141]
). Now form a
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
-orthonormal basis (
14
) for
\Lambda^{2}
with
T\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}
and
w\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{2}
, and note that

\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=\langle{e_{1}\wedge e_{2}},{e_{1}\wedge e_{2}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=-1.

Since this basis is also
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!\scalebox{0.7}{\emph{g}}}
-orthonormal by construction, we have that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=-R_{1212}\,e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+\cdots
. Then

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})},{e_{1}\wedge e_{2}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=-R_{1212}=R_{1221}\overset{\eqref{sec:v0}}{=}\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(P).

If
P
is orthogonal to
T
, then choose a basis (
14
) with

and

, where, in this case,
\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)=\langle{e_{2}\wedge e_{3}},{e_{2}\wedge e_{3}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=+1
. Writing
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{2}\wedge e_{3})=\cdots-R_{2323}\,e_{2}\wedge e_{3}+\cdots
, we have

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{2}\wedge e_{3})},{e_{2}\wedge e_{3}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=-R_{2323}=R_{2332}=\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(P),

which completes the proof.
∎

The virtue of working with
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}
is that it recovers something of the
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
-Einstein characterization (
8
), thus providing an alternative notion of “almost-Einstein” metric (in what follows, note the crucial role played by the self-adjointness of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
with respect to
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
):

Theorem 2

(almost-Einstein metric, Lorentzian case)
.

If an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold
(M,g)
is
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein
, then

\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})=\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)

for all
P\in G_{+}(p)\cup G_{-}(p)
.

Proof.

Our proof follows that of
[
Tho69a
, Theorem, p. 5]
(which Theorem is with respect to the Lorentzian curvature operator, not the Riemannian one that we are dealing with here; see our remark after the proof). Suppose that
g
is a
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein metric with respect to some choice of unit length vector field
T
on
M
. Noting that
\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})=-\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)
(this follows from the self-adjointness of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
), we have that

(40)

\displaystyle\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}P)},{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqn:p00}}{=}

\displaystyle\!\!-\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}P)},{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}P},{P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P),

where in (
41
) we used the self-adjointness of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
once again.
∎

It’s natural to ask whether the converse is true — as it is in the fully Riemannian
[
ST69
]
or fully Lorentzian
[
Tho69a
]
cases. In fact it is not true here. Indeed, suppose that
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})=\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)
for all nondegenerate 2-planes
P
, and that we would like to conclude from this that
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}=\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
(equivalently, that
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}=-\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
). Using the self-adjointness of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
once again, we would have

(42)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,-\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\!P},{P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}\,}_{\text{$\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P^{\perp_{\scalebox{0.35}{\emph{L}}}})$, via \eqref{eqn:secsec}}}\hskip-5.78172pt\,\,=\underbrace{\,\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(P)\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}P},{P}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}\,}_{\text{$\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}(P)$}}.

This implies that the operators
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
and
-\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
have the same
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
-quadratic forms on all nondegenerate 2-planes
P
. Now, if two curvature operators both satisfy the algebraic Bianchi identity and have equivalent quadratic forms, then by a standard argument they must be equal (even if their quadratic forms are equal only on
nondegenerate
2-planes, by a continuity argument they would still be equal on all 2-planes; see
[
Tho69a
]
). However,
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
does not satisfy the algebraic Bianchi identity with respect to
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
, hence neither does
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}\circ\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\circ*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
. Moving on, we are now ready to classify
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein metrics, by defining a Riemannian version of Petrov Type:

Definition 5

(Petrov Type of
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein metric)
.

Let
(M,g)
be
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein, with complex-linear curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\colon\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
. Then
g
has
Petrov Type I, II, or III at
p\in M
if
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
has, respectively, 3, 2, or 1 independent complex eigenvectors at
p
.

Three distinct eigenvalues is the generic case; the remaining cases are “algebraically special.” Jordan normal-form theory now yields the following matrix characterization of Petrov Type (cf.
[
Tho69a
, Theorem, p. 3]
):

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein. At each
p\in M
, its complex-linear curvature operator
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}\colon\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\scalebox{0.5}{\emph{$\mathbb{C}$}}}^{2}
will be similar to one of

(43)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\,\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&0&\lambda_{3}\end{bmatrix}\,}_{\emph{\text{Type I}}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\underbrace{\,\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&1\\
0&0&\lambda_{2}\end{bmatrix}\,}_{\emph{\text{Type II}}}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\underbrace{\,\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&1&0\\
0&\lambda_{1}&1\\
0&0&\lambda_{1}\end{bmatrix}\,}_{\emph{\text{Type III}}},

with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
not necessarily distinct.

Proof.

If
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
has three linearly independent eigenvectors (Type I) at
p\in M
, then it is diagonalizable, with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
not necessarily distinct; this is the leftmost matrix. If
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
has two linearly independent eigenvectors (Type II), then it is not diagonalizable and splits into two cases: 1) Two of its eigenvalues are equal, or 2) all three are equal. Since in Type II the total geometric multiplicity is 2, either case leads to two Jordan blocks and thus to the middle matrix above (if all three eigenvalues are equal, then
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
). The final case, with only one linearly independent eigenvector (Type III), has geometric multiplicity 1, hence one Jordan block.
∎

We would now like to relate a
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-Einstein metric’s Petrov Type to its sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
; before doing so, however, we will first prove a more general result by characterizing the critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}
(the reason for taking such a path will appear in Theorem
5
below). As we do so, recall once again that
\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}
is not self-adjoint with respect to
\langle{\,},{}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}
. In practice, this means that in an arbitrary
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
with timelike direction
e_{1}
(we don’t assume that
T=e_{1}
), the components

(44)

\displaystyle K_{ijkl}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\langle{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})},{e_{k}\wedge e_{l}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}},

while satisfying
K_{jikl}=-K_{ijkl}
and
K_{ijlk}=-K_{ijkl}
, are however not pairwise symmetric in general:
K_{ijkl}\neq K_{klij}
. But if
e_{1}=T
, then we can say more:

Lemma 4
.

With respect to any oriented
g_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}
-orthonormal basis of the form
\{T\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, the components (

(45)

\displaystyle\text{\emph{$K_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}\,\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})$}}.

Hence in such a basis,

(46)

\displaystyle\text{\emph{$K_{ijkl}=K_{klij}\iff\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})$}}.

Proof.

The key is that such a basis is also
g
-orthonormal, hence by (
5
),

K_{ijkl}=-\langle{\!\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{\hat{R}_{\scalebox{0.6}{\emph{g}}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})}_{\text{$-R_{ijkl}\,e_{k}\wedge e_{l}+\cdots$}}\!\!\!\!\!\!},{e_{k}\wedge e_{l}}\rangle_{\!g_{\scalebox{0.3}{\emph{L}}}}=R_{ijkl}\,\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{k}\wedge e_{l}).

Since
R_{ijkl}=R_{klij}
, it’s clear that
K_{ijkl}=K_{klij}
if and only if
\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\epsilon_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}(e_{k}\wedge e_{l})
.
∎

Now we are in a position to characterize the critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{$L$-$\hat{R}$}}}

(M,g)

\displaystyle K_{34ij}=-K_{ij34}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{$(i,j)\neq(3,4)$ or $(1,2)$},

\displaystyle K_{12ij}=-K_{ij12}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{$(i,j)\neq(3,4)$ or $(1,2)$}.

We employ the same coordinate chart

[

, though with minor index changes. Given a critical 2-plane

for two spacelike

for

is
*_{\scalebox{0.4}{\emph{L}}}

Theorem 4
.

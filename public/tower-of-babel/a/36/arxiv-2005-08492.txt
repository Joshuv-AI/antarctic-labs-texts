Properties of R ⁡ ( X ) , R ⁡ ( a , X ) ​ and ​ R ​ W ​ ( a , X ) R(X),R(a,X)\mbox{ and }RW(a,X)
Tim Dalby

Abstract
. In the context of metric fixed point theory in Banach spaces three moduli have played an important role. These are R(X), R(a, X) and RW(a, X). This paper looks at some of their properties. Also investigated is what happens when they take on the value of 1. The situation where these moduli are set in dual space is also considered.

46B10, 47H09, 47H10

1.
Introduction

The Banach space moduli of
R(X),R(a,X)\mbox{ and }RW(a,X)
were introduced within the context of fixed point theory. This area of research looks at whether, in an infinite dimensional real Banach space, every nonexpansive mappings on every weak compact convex nonempty set has a fixed point. If this is so then the space is said to have the weak fixed point property (w-FPP). It can be shown that this propery is separably determined so in this paper the Banach space is assumed to be separable.

If the sets are not necessarily weak compact but closed then the property is called the fixed point property (FPP).

Another aspect of this topic is that it involves weakly convergent sequences and usually a translation is employed so that the sequence being studied is weakly convergent to zero. Inherent in most results is the interplay between weak null sequences and the norm. Much more background can be found in Goebel and Kirk [14] and Kirk and Sims [18].

These moduli all measure a relationship between weak null sequences and the norm and they have similar definitions. These definitions are given in the next section. Because of the close similarity of the definitions it is natural to ask how the three
Rs
are related, do they share similar properties and, if they take on certain values, what does mean for that Banach space. This paper explores these questions.

2.
Definitions

Definition 2.1
.

[García-Falset, 10]

R(X)=\sup\{\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|:\|x\|\leqslant 1,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\}.

So
1\leqslant R(X)\leqslant 2
.

In [10] García-Falset showed that if a Banach space,
X
, has the nonstrict Opial property and
R(X)<2
then
X
has the w-FPP. About the same time Prus [24] produced a similar result. Later García-Falset [11] was able to drop the nonstrict Opial condition.

Domínguez-Benavides in [7] created a new version of
R(X)
this time with a parameter and an extra condition on the weak null sequences.

Definition 2.2
.

[Domínguez-Benavides, 7]

\mbox{ For }a\geqslant 0\mbox{ let }R(a,X)=\sup\{\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|:\|x\|\leqslant a,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0

\mbox{ where }D[(x_{n})]=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x_{m}\|\right)\leqslant 1\}.

In [7] Domínguez-Benavides showed that if
R(a,X)<1+a
for some
a\geqslant 0
then
X
has the w-FPP. In addition, it was shown that there is a link between
R(a,X)
and the modulus of near uniform smoothness,
\Gamma(t)
, in reflexive Banach spaces. This link was explored further in [12] where the third one of the
Rs
was defined.

Definition 2.3
.

[García-Falset, Llorens-Fuster and Mazcuñan-Navarroa, 12]

\mbox{ For }a>0\mbox{ let }RW(a,X)=\sup\{\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\wedge\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|:\|x\|\leqslant a,

\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\}.

In all three definitions
\liminf
can be replaced by
\limsup.

Again there is a link between
RW(a,X)
and
\Gamma(t)
. The authors exploited this link with
\Gamma(t)
to show that uniformly nonsquare Banach spaces have the FPP and thus solved a long standing conjecture.

In [12] it was shown that
R(a,X)\leqslant RW(a,X)\mbox{ for all }a>0.
Using this inequality and the definitions it is clear that
R(1,X)\leqslant RW(1,X)\leqslant R(X).

So
RW(a,X)
is the newest modulus and sits in the “middle” between
R(a,X)
and
R(X).
It is probably the most useful of the three because of its links to
\Gamma(t)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}(t).
This is discussed in Section 4.

The following results are motivated by corollary 4.3 of [12].

3.
Properties of
R(a,X)

It is straightforward to see that

1\vee a\leqslant R(a,X)\leqslant 1+a

and

\mbox{ if }0\leqslant a\leqslant b\mbox{ then }R(a,X)\leqslant R(b,X).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then there exist
a>0
such that
R(a,X)=1+a
if and only if
R(b,X)=1+b
for all
b>0.

Proof.

This proof is based on part of the proof of corollary 4.3 of [12].

Assume there exist
a>0
such that
R(a,X)=1+a.
Let
\eta\in(0,1)
then there exists

x\in X,\|x\|\leqslant a,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n\mbox{ and }\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x_{m}\|\right)\leqslant 1

such that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|>1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1).\qquad\mbox{ \# }

Let
x_{n}^{*}\in X^{*},\|x_{n}^{*}\|=1,x_{n}^{*}(x_{n}+x)=\|x_{n}+x\|\mbox{ for all }n.

Because
X
is separable the dual unit ball,
B_{X^{*}}
, is weak* sequentially compact so without loss of generality
x_{n}^{*}
can be assumed to converge weak* to
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}.

Now
x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})\leqslant\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1,x^{*}(x)\leqslant\|x\|\leqslant a.

From #

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n}+x)

\displaystyle>1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})+x^{*}(x)

\displaystyle>1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)\qquad\mbox{ \#\# }

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})

\displaystyle>1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)-x^{*}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant 1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)-a

\displaystyle=1-\eta(a\wedge 1)

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-\eta.

Next, from ##

\displaystyle x^{*}(x)

\displaystyle>1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})

\displaystyle\geqslant 1+a-\eta(a\wedge 1)-1

\displaystyle=a-\eta(a\wedge 1)

\displaystyle\geqslant a-a\eta

\displaystyle=a(1-\eta).

Now let
b>0
then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}+\frac{b}{a}x\right\|

\displaystyle\geqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n}+\frac{b}{a}x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})+\frac{a}{b}x^{*}(x)

\displaystyle>1-\eta+\frac{b}{a}\times a(1-\eta)

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)(1+b).

Note that
\left\|\frac{b}{a}x\right\|\leqslant\frac{b}{a}\times a=b
so

R(b,X)\geqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}+\frac{b}{a}x\right\|>(1-\eta)(1+b).

Allowing
\eta\rightarrow 0
gives
R(b,X)\geqslant 1+b.

But
R(b,X)\leqslant 1+b\mbox{ for all }b>0.
Therefore
R(b,X)=1+b.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then there exist
a>0
such that
R(a,X)<1+a
if and only if
R(b,X)<1+b
for all
b>0.

Proof.

Only one of the implications needs to be considered. Assume there exist
a>0
such that
R(a,X)<1+a
and also assume there exists a
b>0
such that
R(b,X)=1+b.
Then by proposition 3.1
R(a,X)=1+a.
This is a contradiction.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then the following are equivalent.

(a)

There exists
a>0
such that
R(a,X)<1+a
;

(b)

R(a,X)<1+a
for all
a>0
;

(c)

R(1,X)<2.

Remarks

1.

The equivalence of (b) and (c) was mentioned by Prus in his talk at the 12th International Conference on Fixed Point Theory and Its Applications [25].

2.

It would appear that the introduction of the parameter
a
does not create a great advantage in fixed point theory. The real advantage is using
D[(x_{n})]
in the definition of
R(a,X).

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then the following are equivalent.

(a)

There exists
a>0
such that
R(a,X)=1+a
;

(b)

R(a,X)=1+a
for all
a>0
;

(c)

R(1,X)=2.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then either

R(a,X)=1+a\mbox{ for all }a>0\mbox{ or }R(a,X)<1+a\mbox{ for all }a>0.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then either

R(a,X)=1+a\mbox{ for all }a>0\mbox{ or }R(1,X)<2.

So if
R(a,X)
takes on its largest possible value then some interesting conclusions can be drawn. At the other end of the scale, when
R(a,X)
takes on one of its possible minimum values,
a
, there is also an interesting result. The case when the
Rs
equal 1 when
a=1
is explored further in a later section.

Another point of interest is the rate of increase of
R(a,X).
The following proposition looks into that and then the consequences are investigated.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then
0<a\leqslant b
implies
aR(b,X)\leqslant bR(a,X).

Proof.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space and
a\mbox{ and }b
be real numbers such that
0<a\leqslant b.

Consider

x\in X,\|x\|\leqslant b,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n\mbox{ and }\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x_{m}\|\right)\leqslant 1.

Then

\left\|\frac{a}{b}x\right\|\leqslant\frac{a}{b}\times b=a,\frac{a}{b}x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\left\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}\right\|\leqslant\frac{a}{b}\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n\mbox{ and }

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}-\frac{a}{b}x_{m}\right\|\right)=\frac{a}{b}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{m}\right\|\right)\leqslant\frac{a}{b}\leqslant 1.

Thus

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{a}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}+\frac{a}{b}x\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{b}{a}R(a,X).

Therefore
R(b,X)\leqslant\frac{b}{a}R(a,X).

∎

This means that if
0<a\leqslant b
then

\frac{R(b,X)}{b}\leqslant\frac{R(a,X)}{a}.

In other words
\frac{R(a,X)}{a}
is nonincreasing. The consequences of this property deserves further investigation.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then if there exists
a>0
such that
R(a,X)=a
then
R(b,X)=b
for all
b\geqslant a.

Proof.

Assume there exists an
a>0
such that
R(a,X)=a.
Let
b\geqslant a.
Then by proposition 3.7
R(b,X)\leqslant\frac{b}{a}R(a,X)=\frac{b}{a}\times a=b
but
R(b,X)\geqslant b\mbox{ for all }b>0.

So
R(b,X)=b.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then
R(a,X)=a\mbox{ for all }\newline
a\geqslant 1\mbox{ if and only if }R(1,X)=1.

Proof.

Only one implication needs to be considered. Let
a\geqslant 1
then proposition 3.7 means
1\times R(a,X)\leqslant a\times R(1,X)=a
but
R(a,X)\geqslant a\mbox{ for all }a>0.

So
R(a,X)=a.

∎

4.
Properties of
RW(a,X)

It is straightforward to see that

1\vee a\leqslant RW(a,X)\leqslant 1+a

and

\mbox{ if }0\leqslant a\leqslant b\mbox{ then }RW(a,X)\leqslant RW(b,X).

RW(a,X)
is closely related to
\Gamma_{X}(t)
and
\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}(t)
in reflexive Banach spaces.

The modulus of nearly uniform smoothness is

\Gamma_{X}(t)=\sup\left\{\inf_{n>1}\left(\frac{\|x_{1}+tx_{n}\|+\|x_{1}-tx_{n}\|}{2}-1\right)\right\},

where the supremum is taken over all basic sequences
(x_{n})
in
B_{X}.

If
X
is reflexive then

\Gamma_{X}(t)=\sup\left\{\inf_{n>1}\left(\frac{\|x_{1}+tx_{n}\|+\|x_{1}-tx_{n}\|}{2}-1\right):(x_{n})\mbox{ in }B_{X},x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\right\}.

The definition of
\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}(t)
is

\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\Gamma_{X}(t)}{t}.

In [21] and [12] it was shown that, in a reflexive Banach space, there exists
a>0
such that
RW(a,X)<1+a
if and only
\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}(0)<1
if and only if there exists
t>0
such that
\Gamma_{X}(t)<t.

The first condition on
RW(a,X)
will be looked at below.

Not surprisingly these properties are similar to those of
R(a,X)
and the proofs are similar. To prevent boredom a slightly different approach is taken.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then the following are equivalent.

(a)

RW(a,X)<1+a
for all
a>0
;

(b)

There exists
a>0
such that
RW(a,X)<1+a
;

(c)

RW(1,X)<2.

Proof.

Both (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) and (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) are straightforward.

\bf{(b)\Rightarrow(a):}
Assume that (a) does not hold then there exists
b>0
such that
RW(b,X)=1+b.
Then
RW(c,X)=1+c\mbox{ for all }c>0.
This result formed part of the proof of corollary 4.3 of [12]. So there is a contradiction.

\bf{(c)\Rightarrow(a):}
This is proved in a similar fashion.

∎

Another point of interest is the rate of increase of
RW(a,X)
and not surprisingly it is the same as for
R(a,X).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then
0<a\leqslant b
implies
aRW(b,X)\leqslant bRW(a,X).

Proof.

Consider
x\in X,\|x\|\leqslant b,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n.

Note that
\left\|\frac{a}{b}x\right\|\leqslant\frac{a}{b}\times b\leqslant a,\frac{a}{b}x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}\|\leqslant\frac{a}{b}\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n.

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\wedge\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{a}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}+\frac{a}{b}x\|\wedge\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\frac{a}{b}x_{n}-\frac{a}{b}x\|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{b}{a}RW(a,X).

Therefore
RW(b,X)\leqslant\frac{b}{a}RW(a,X).

∎

This means that if
0<a\leqslant b
then

\frac{RW(b,X)}{b}\leqslant\frac{RW(a,X)}{a}.

In other words
\frac{RW(a,X)}{a}
is nonincreasing.

There is a tantalising similarity to a property of
\Gamma_{X}(t).
In [12] it was shown that
\frac{\Gamma_{X}(t)}{t}
is nondecreasing. These properties of
RW(a,X)
and
\Gamma_{X}(t)
combined with the two inequalities linking them in theorem 4.1 of [12] provide fertile ground for further research.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space then the following are equivalent.

(a)

There exists
a>0
such that
RW(a,X)=a
and

has Kadec-Klee norm;

(b)

X
has the Schur property.

Proof.

(b)

\bf{(a)\Rightarrow(b):}
Suppose that
X

x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n\mbox{ with }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|=\alpha>0.

Consider
x\in X,\|x\|=a,RW(a,X)=a.
Then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\wedge\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|\leqslant\|x\|.

(1)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|

So
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\leqslant\|x\|.

Weak lower semicontinuity of the norm means

\|x\|\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|.

Thus
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|=\|x\|
but
x_{n}+x\rightharpoonup x.

The Kadec-Klee norm implies
x_{n}+x\rightarrow x
which means
x_{n}\rightarrow 0.

This contradicts
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|=\alpha>0.

(2)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|

The proof follows the same form as in (1) and results in a contradiction.

Therefore
X
has the Schur property.

∎

RW(1,X)\leqslant J(X)
where
J(X)
is the James constant.

Proof.

J(X):=\sup\{\{\|x+y\|\wedge\|x-y\|\}:\|x\|,\|y\|\leqslant 1\}.

Let
\eta>0
then there exists

But

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\frac{\|x+y\|^{2}+\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\left(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\right)}:\|x\|+\|y\|\not=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{\|x+y\|^{2}+\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\left(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\right)}:\|x\|=1,\|y\|\leqslant 1\right\}.

Following the same path as in the previous proof let
\eta>0
then there exists

x\in X,\|x\|\leqslant 1,x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n

such that

\displaystyle\mbox{ Therefore }C_{NJ}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\|x_{n}+x\|^{2}+\|x_{n}-x\|^{2}}{2\left(1+1\right)}\mbox{ for all }n.

\displaystyle 4C_{NJ}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|x_{n}+x\|^{2}+\|x_{n}-x\|^{2}\mbox{ for all }n

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|^{2}+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|^{2}

X

\mbox{ if }x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\mbox{ then }\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\mbox{ for all }x.

If the sequence,
(x_{n})
, is in the dual,
X^{*}
, and the convergence is weak* then the condition is called the nonstrict *Opial condition.

Dhompongsa and Kaewkhao in [6] improved the result from [2] by showing that
R(X)=1
if and only if

has property(
m_{\infty}
).

Here property(
m_{\infty}
) is if

and

This property was introduced in [16] and formally named as such in [17]. In
X^{*}
, property(
m_{\infty}^{*}
) is defined in the same way but with weak* null sequences.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space with
R(X)=1
then
c_{0}\hookrightarrow X.

Proof.

In [2] it was shown that if
X
is a separable Banach space with Property(
M
) then
c_{0}\hookrightarrow X
if and only if there exists
x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|=1
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x+tx_{n}\|=1\vee t

for all
t\geqslant 0,\mbox{ and all }x,\mbox{ with }\|x\|=1.

Property(
m_{\infty}
) ensures that this condition is satisfied for all weak null sequences.

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If

be a separable Banach space with
RW(1,X)=1
and satisfies the nonstrict Opial condition then

has Property(
M

Proof.

Assume
X
has the nonstrict Opial condition and
RW(1,X)=1.
Consider
x_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\mbox{ and }x\in X.
Using the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm we have

\|x\|\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\pm x\|.

The nonstrict Opial condition leads to

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\pm x\|.

Combining these two inequalities gives

\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\pm x\|.

Let
a=\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|.
If
a=0,
then
m_{\infty}
equation is trivially satisfied. So assume
a\not=0
then

\left\|\frac{x}{a}\right\|\leqslant 1,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{x_{n}}{a}\right\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ and }\frac{x_{n}}{a}\rightharpoonup 0.

By taking subsequences if necessary, we may assume that
\left\|\frac{x_{n}}{a}\right\|\leqslant 1\mbox{ for all }n.

Using
RW(1,X)=1
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{x_{n}}{a}+\frac{x}{a}\right\|\wedge\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{x_{n}}{a}-\frac{x}{a}\right\|\leqslant 1.

Then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\wedge\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|\leqslant\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|.

But
\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\pm x\|
leading to

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|\wedge\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|=\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|.

Then either

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}+x\|=\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|\mbox{ or }\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x\|=\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|.

Since this is true for all
x\in X
changing
x
into
-x
means

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\pm x\|=\|x\|\vee\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}\|.

Thus
X
has property(
m_{\infty}
).

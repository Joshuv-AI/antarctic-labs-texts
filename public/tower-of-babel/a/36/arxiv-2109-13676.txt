Semi-stable representations as limits of crystalline representations
Anand Chitrao, Eknath Ghate, Seidai Yasuda

Abstract
We construct an explicit sequence V k n , a n V_{k_{n},a_{n}} of crystalline representations of exceptional weights converging to a given irreducible two-dimensional semi-stable representation V k , ℒ V_{k,\mathcal{L}} of Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ p / ℚ p ) \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}) . The convergence takes place in the blow-up space of two-dimensional trianguline representations studied by Colmez and Chenevier. The process of blow-up is described in detail in the rigid analytic setting and may be of independent interest. Also, we recover a formula of Stevens expressing the ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariant as a logarithmic derivative. Our result can be used to compute the reduction of V k , ℒ V_{k,\mathcal{L}} in terms of the reductions of the V k n , a n V_{k_{n},a_{n}} . For instance, using the zig-zag conjecture we recover (resp. extend) the work of Breuil-Mézard and Guerberoff-Park computing the reductions of the V k , ℒ V_{k,\mathcal{L}} for weights at most p − 1 p-1 (resp. p + 1 p+1 ), at least on the inertia subgroup. In the cases where zig-zag is known, we are further able to obtain some new information about the reductions for small odd weights. Finally, we explain some apparent violations to local constancy in the weight of the reductions of crystalline representations of small weight.

Let

In particular, we show how information about the reductions of the former representations

has applications to computing deformation rings, to the weight part of Serre’s conjecture, to the Breuil-Mézard conjecture
and to modularity lifting theorems.

1.2
Limits of crystalline representations

Colmez
[
Col08
]
and Chenevier
[
Che13
]
have constructed a moduli space of non-split trianguline

(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules of rank
2
over the Robba ring (assuming that the
quotient of the characters occurring in the triangulation is not of a certain kind). The first goal
of this paper is to construct for
k\geq 3
an explicit sequence of crystalline representations converging in this space to the (dual of the) semi-stable representation
V_{k,\mathcal{L}}
for a prescribed
\mathcal{L}
-invariant
\mathcal{L}
. We will use this in conjunction with a local constancy result to study the reductions of semi-stable representations.

In order to state our result, let us recall the definition of the rigid-analytic space constructed by Colmez and Chenevier. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the parameter space for characters of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
. Let
x:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
be the identity character. Let
\chi:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character, sending
p
to
1

and such that
\chi|_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}}
is the identity character. We call the characters
x^{i}\chi
for
i\geq 0

exceptional
.
For each
i\geq 0
, let
F_{i}
and

be the closed analytic subvarieties of
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}
such that for every finite extension
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}F_{i}(E)&=\{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in\mathcal{T}(E)\times\mathcal{T}(E)\text{ }|\text{ }\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{-1}=x^{i}\chi\},\\
F_{i}^{\prime}(E)&=\{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in\mathcal{T}(E)\times\mathcal{T}(E)\text{ }|\text{ }\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{-1}=x^{-i}\}.\end{array}

Let

and

.
The Colmez-Chenevier space

is the blow-up of
(\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T})\setminus F^{\prime}
along

in the category of rigid-analytic spaces.
Our first main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
r=k-2
and
\mathcal{L}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(E)
. For
n\geq 1
, let

(k_{n},a_{n})=\begin{cases}(k+p^{n}(p-1),\>p^{r/2}(1+\mathcal{L}p^{n}(p-1)/2)),&\text{if $\mathcal{L}\not=\infty$}\\
(k+p^{n^{2}}(p-1),\>p^{r/2}(1+p^{n})),&\text{if $\mathcal{L}=\infty$}.\end{cases}

(1)

Then

V^{*}_{k_{n},a_{n}}\to V^{*}_{k,\mathcal{L}},

i.e., the sequence of points in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
associated to the crystalline representations
V^{*}_{k_{n},a_{n}}

converges to the point in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
associated to the semi-stable representation
V^{*}_{k,\mathcal{L}}
.

1.4
Reductions of semi-stable representations

In
[

suppose

This approach to computing the reduction of semi-stable representations using crystalline representations is of some importance because the reductions of these two classes of
representations are nowadays largely studied by completely different methods: the crystalline case uses the compatibility of reduction between
the
p
-adic and mod
p
Local Langlands correspondences, or computes the reduction of the corresponding Wach module, whereas the reductions in the semi-stable case are
determined by studying the reductions of the corresponding strongly divisible modules.
In our experience, the former methods, while quite intricate,
are not as complicated as the latter method. Thus, in view of the remarks above,
the techniques used in the crystalline case may be brought to bear on the study of the reductions of semi-stable representations. We note, however, that the former
method is only available for two-dimensional representations of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})

representations of

of any dimension (though in practical terms only for those of small Hodge-Tate weights).

1.5
Relation with local constancy

In
[
Ber12
]
, Berger proved the following theorem on the local constancy in the weight
of the semi-simplification of the reduction of crystalline representations.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Ber12
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let
a_{p}\not=0
and
k>3v_{p}(a_{p})+\alpha(k-1)+1
, where
\alpha(j)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{n-1}(p-1)}\right\rfloor
. Then there exists
m=m(k,a_{p})
such that
\overline{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}=\overline{V}_{k,a_{p}}
, if
k^{\prime}\geq k
and
k^{\prime}-k\in p^{m-1}(p-1)\mathbb{Z}
.

This local constancy result does not extend to small weights
k
in the sense that the bound in the theorem
is sharp. This was noticed in
[
Gha21
]
using the following examples.

•

Let
k=4
and
a_{p}=p\geq 5
. Then
4\not>3(1)+1
and the crystalline representation
V_{k,a_{p}}
does not satisfy Berger’s bound on the weight. Now let
k^{\prime}=4+p^{n}(p-1)
, for large
n
. If the above local constancy result were to hold for
V_{k,a_{p}}
, then we would have
\overline{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\sim\overline{V}_{k,a_{p}}
. However,
\overline{V}_{k,a_{p}}
is irreducible as
k
belongs to the range
[2,p+1]
treated by Fontaine and Edixhoven
[
Edi92
]
, but
\overline{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\sim\mu_{3}\>\omega^{2}\oplus\mu_{3^{-1}}\>\omega
is reducible by
[
BGR18
, Theorem 1.1]
.

•

Let
k=5
and
a_{p}=p^{3/2}
, for
p\geq 7
. Then
5\not>3(1.5)+1
and the crystalline representation
V_{k,a_{p}}
again does not satisfy the bound on the weight. Now let
k^{\prime}=5+p^{n}(p-1)
, for large
n
. If the above local constancy result were to hold for
V_{k,a_{p}}
, then we would have
\overline{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\sim\overline{V}_{k,a_{p}}
. However,
\overline{V}_{k,a_{p}}\sim\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2}^{4})
since
k
belongs to the Fontaine-Edixhoven range, but
\overline{V}_{k^{\prime},a_{p}}\sim\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2}^{3+p})
by
[
GR23
, Theorem 1.1]
.

However, we have the following
corollary to Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.7
.

For
k\geq 3
, the sequence of crystalline representations
V^{*}_{k+p^{n}(p-1),p^{r/2}}
converges to the semi-stable representation
V^{*}_{k,\mathcal{L}}
for
\mathcal{L}=0
.

Therefore, for large
n
the reductions of the crystalline representations
V_{k+p^{n}(p-1),p^{r/2}}
in the examples above should be isomorphic
to the reduction of the semi-stable representation
V_{k,\mathcal{L}}
with
\mathcal{L}=0
, and not necessarily to
the reduction of the crystalline representation
V_{k,p^{r/2}}
(though all may be the same, as is the case when
k=3
). Indeed, one checks that

•

if
k=4
and
p\geq 5
, then by
[
BM02
, Theorem 1.2]
,
the reduction of the semi-stable representation
V_{4,\mathcal{L}}
for
\mathcal{L}=0
is
\mu_{3}\>\omega^{2}\oplus\mu_{3^{-1}}\>\omega
, and,

•

if
k=5
and
p\geq 7
, then by
[
GP19
, Theorem 5.0.5]
(or, better, by Theorem
1.5
), the reduction of the semi-stable representation
V_{5,\mathcal{L}}
for
\mathcal{L}=0
is

\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2}^{3+p})
.

Thus, there is no apparent contradiction to local constancy in the weight for the reductions of the crystalline representations above when
k
is small if one
works in the Colmez-Chenevier space which includes semi-stable representations of weight
k
.

The blow-up of

In this section, we provide details about blow-ups in the rigid analytic setting which may be of independent
interest. This is inspired by the work of Schoutens (cf.
[
Sch95
]
), but we carefully work out the details and also do not work over algebraically
closed fields. For background on rigid analytic geometry, see
[
BGR84
]
.
We also recall the important Proposition
2.5
, which for each
finite extension
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
establishes a bijection between the
E
-valued points of
\widetilde{U}_{r}
that lie above
(p^{r},(1+p)^{k-1}-1)
and the tangent directions in
U_{r}
at
the exceptional point.

2.1
The blow-up as a rigid analytic variety

The blow-up of
U_{r}
consists of a rigid analytic variety
\widetilde{U}_{r}
and a map
\pi:\widetilde{U}_{r}\to U_{r}
satisfying the properties given in
[
Sch95
, Definition 1.2.1]
. In this subsection, we will construct
\widetilde{U}_{r}
.

Recall that
U_{r}=\text{Sp }\mathcal{O}(U_{r})
, where

\mathcal{O}(U_{r})=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle S_{1},S_{2},T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\rangle/(p^{r}T_{1}-S_{1},1-T_{1}T_{2},pT_{3}-S_{2}).

The
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-valued point
(p^{r},(1+p)^{k-1}-1)
corresponds to the maximal ideal
m=(f_{1},f_{2})
of
\mathcal{O}(U_{r})
, where
f_{1}=S_{1}-p^{r}
and
f_{2}=S_{2}-((1+p)^{k-1}-1)
. By the blow-up of
U_{r}
at
(p^{r},(1+p)^{k-1}-1)
, we mean the blow-up at the maximal ideal
m
.

We will construct
\widetilde{U}_{r}
by a patching argument. Let
Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{1}^{\prime}
and
Q_{2}^{\prime}
be indeterminates. For
i=0,1,\cdots
, consider the affinoid algebra

A_{i}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{1}\rangle/(f_{2}-Q_{1}f_{1}),

which describes the subset of
U_{r}
where
|f_{2}|\leq p^{i}|f_{1}|
. Sending
p^{i+1}Q_{1}
to
p\cdot p^{i}Q_{1}
and
X
to
p^{i}Q_{1}
induces an isomorphism
A_{i+1}\langle X\rangle/(pX-p^{i+1}Q_{1})\cong A_{i}
, showing that
\text{Sp }A_{i}
is the affinoid subdomain (hence an open subvariety) of
\text{Sp }A_{i+1}
consisting of maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
such that
|Q_{1}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}|\leq p^{i}
.
We therefore get a sequence of inclusions
\text{Sp }A_{0}\to\text{Sp }A_{1}\to\text{Sp }A_{2}\to\cdots
associated to the sequence of affinoid algebra homomorphisms
\cdots\to A_{2}\to A_{1}\to A_{0}
. Using
[
BGR84
, Proposition
9.3.2/1
]
we paste together the
\text{Sp }A_{i}
for
i\geq 0
to get a rigid analytic variety
\widetilde{V}_{1}
.
The same proposition also states that
\{\text{Sp }A_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is an admissible cover of
\widetilde{V}_{1}
.
Similarly, for
i\geq 0
, we define

B_{i}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{2}\rangle/(f_{1}-Q_{2}f_{2})

and glue the corresponding affinoid spaces to get a rigid analytic variety
\widetilde{V}_{2}
. The blow-up
\widetilde{U}_{r}
is the rigid analytic variety obtained by
glueing
\widetilde{V}_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}
along certain open subvarieties.

We describe these open subvarieties now. For each
i\geq 0
, consider the affinoid algebra

A_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{1},p^{i}Q_{1}^{\prime}\rangle/(f_{2}-Q_{1}f_{1},1-Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\prime}),

which describes a Laurent subdomain
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}
of
\text{Sp }A_{i}
given by the condition
|Q_{1}|\geq p^{-i}
. Since we have an isomorphism
A_{i+1}^{\prime}\langle X,Y\rangle/(pX-p^{i+1}Q_{1},pY-p^{i+1}Q_{1}^{\prime})\cong A_{i}^{\prime}
(sending
X
to
p^{i}Q_{1}
and
Y
to
p^{i}Q_{1}^{\prime}
), we see that
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}
is an affinoid subdomain (and hence an open subvariety) of
\text{Sp }A_{i+1}^{\prime}
. Using
[
BGR84
, Proposition
9.3.2/1
]
, we paste together
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}
for
i\geq 0
to get a rigid analytic variety
\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}
. Moreover, using
[
BGR84
, Proposition
9.3.3/1
]
, we see that the canonical inclusions
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}\to\text{Sp }A_{i}
induce an inclusion
\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}\xhookrightarrow{}\widetilde{V}_{1}
, identifying
\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}
as a subvariety of
\widetilde{V}_{1}
. Similarly, we construct a subvariety
\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime}
of
\widetilde{V}_{2}
by glueing all the
\text{Sp }B_{i}^{\prime}
together, where

B_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{2},p^{i}Q_{2}^{\prime}\rangle/(f_{1}-Q_{2}f_{2},1-Q_{2}Q_{2}^{\prime}).

It turns out that
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}
and
\text{Sp }B_{i}^{\prime}
are the intersections of
\text{Sp }A_{i}
and
\text{Sp }B_{i}
in the blow-up
\widetilde{U}_{r}
that we will soon construct.
The spaces above are summarized by the following picture:

\widetilde{V}_{1}

\widetilde{V}_{2}
corresponds to the surface of the sphere except the north pole.

We claim that
\widetilde{V}_{j}^{\prime}
is an open subvariety of
\widetilde{V}_{j}
, for
j=1,2
. Without loss of generality, assume
j=1
. To prove this claim, we need to show that
\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\text{Sp }A_{i}
is an admissible open subset of
\text{Sp }A_{i}
for each
i\geq 0
. Write

, then
\text{Sp }A_{k}^{\prime}\cap\text{Sp }A_{i}
is the set of maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of

satisfying
|Q_{1}\text{ mod }\mathfrak{m}|\geq p^{-k}

\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\text{Sp }A_{i}

\mathfrak{m}
of

. In other words, it is the complement of the vanishing set of

in
\text{Sp }A_{i}

To glue
\widetilde{V}_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}
, we define an isomorphism
\phi:\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}\to\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime}
as follows. Recall that for
i\geq 0

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{1},p^{i}Q_{1}^{\prime}\rangle/(f_{2}-Q_{1}f_{1},1-Q_{1}Q_{1}^{\prime}),\\

Consider an isomorphism of affinoid algebras
B_{i}^{\prime}\to A_{i}^{\prime}
given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{i}Q_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{i}Q_{1}^{\prime},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{i}Q_{2}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{i}Q_{1}.}

This isomorphism gives rise to an isomorphism
\phi_{i}:\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}\to\text{Sp }B_{i}^{\prime}
of the corresponding affinoid spaces. For
i,j\geq 0
, these maps clearly restrict to the same map on
\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}\cap\text{Sp }A_{j}^{\prime}
. Therefore applying
[
BGR84
, Proposition
9.3.3/1
]
, we get an isomorphism of rigid analytic varieties
\phi:\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}\to\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime}

Define
\widetilde{U}_{r}
to be the rigid analytic variety obtained by glueing
\widetilde{V}_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}
along
\widetilde{V}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime}

We see this by computing its
E

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Since
\widetilde{U}_{r}
is covered by
\widetilde{V}_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}

\widetilde{V}_{1}(E)
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}(E)

A_{i}=\mathcal{O}(U_{r})\langle p^{i}Q_{1}\rangle/(f_{2}-Q_{1}f_{1}).

We identify the set of

\text{Sp }A_{i}

\{(s_{1},s_{2},q_{1})\in U_{r}(E)\times E\text{ }\big|\text{ }(1+s_{2}-(1+p)^{k-1})=q_{1}(s_{1}-p^{r}),\>|q_{1}|\leq p^{i}\}.

. For
i\geq j
, the glueing map

induces the canonical inclusion map on

-valued points
\text{Sp }A_{j}(E)\to\text{Sp }A_{i}(E)
. Therefore, the set of
E
-valued points of
\widetilde{V}_{1}
is the union of all the

, i.e.,

\widetilde{V}_{1}(E)=\{(s_{1},s_{2},q_{1})\in U_{r}(E)\times E\text{ }\big|\text{ }(1+s_{2}-(1+p)^{k-1})=q_{1}(s_{1}-p^{r})\}.

We similarly have

\widetilde{V}_{2}(E)=\{(s_{1},s_{2},q_{2})\in U_{r}(E)\times E\text{ }\big|\text{ }(s_{1}-p^{r})=q_{2}(1+s_{2}-(1+p)^{k-1})\}.

\widetilde{U}_{r}(E)
.
If
P
is an
E
-valued point of
\widetilde{V}_{1}\cap\widetilde{V}_{2}
, it is an

-valued point of
\text{Sp }A_{i}\cap\text{Sp }B_{i}=\text{Sp }A_{i}^{\prime}
, for some

. Being a point of
\text{Sp }A_{i}
, it is of the form
(s_{1},s_{2},q_{1})
, for some

. Since the glueing map between

and

takes

to
S_{1}

to

and

to the inverse of

in

represents

\text{Sp }B_{i}
(the condition

under both of these identifications. As a result of this discussion, we see that

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{r}(E)=\{(s_{1},s_{2},\>\xi_{1}:\xi_{2})\in U_{r}(E)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}(E)\text{ }\big|\text{ }(s_{1}-p^{r})\xi_{2}=(1+s_{2}-(1+p)^{k-1})\xi_{1}\}

(5)

exactly as in the classical algebraic geometry setting.

2.2
The blow-up map
\pi:\widetilde{U}_{r}\to U_{r}

In this subsection, we define a candidate for the blow-up map
\pi:\widetilde{U}_{r}\to U_{r}
using
[
BGR84

]

To define
\pi
, we first define its restrictions
\pi_{i}
to
\widetilde{V}_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
We first define the map
\pi_{1}:\widetilde{V}_{1}\to U_{r}
.
For each
i\geq 0
, consider the map
\text{Sp }A_{i}\to U_{r}

These maps clearly restrict to the same map on
\text{Sp }A_{i}\cap\text{Sp }A_{j}
, for

. So using
[
BGR84

]

\pi_{1}:\widetilde{V}_{1}\to U_{r}
of rigid analytic varieties. The other map
\pi_{2}:\widetilde{V}_{2}\to U_{r}
is constructed similarly.

Now, to get a map
\pi:\widetilde{U}_{r}\to U_{r}
, we have to check that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
agree on the intersection of
\widetilde{V}_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}_{2}

(\cong\widetilde{V}_{2}^{\prime})
, we have to check that the following diagram commutes

In this subsection, we prove that the map

at
the maximal ideal

To prove this, we need to check that

satisfies the two properties stated in
[

We first show that the ideal sheaf on

corresponding to the ideal

of

extends to an invertible ideal sheaf on

in the sense of
[

Let us prove this statement for

mA_{i}
on

of

Since

Lemma 2.1
.

f_{1}
is not a zero divisor in

Proof.

Recall that

So let us now prove that

is not a zero divisor in

Assume that there exists

divides

-distinguished of degree

[
BGR84
, Definition 5.2.1/1]
). Applying the Weierstrass division theorem (cf.
[

, Theorem 5.2.1/2]
) to

and

does not appear in

is of degree

in

) such that

divides

implies that

divides

divides

in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle S_{1},S_{2},Q_{1}\rangle/(f_{2}-Q_{1}f_{1})
.
∎

2.4
Points in the exceptional fiber as tangent directions

The following standard fact allows us to realize

(p^{r},(1+p)^{r+1}-1)
as tangent directions
in

at this point. Recall that the maximal ideal

corresponding to this exceptional point is the ideal generated by

Proposition 2.5
.

There is a bijection

\pi^{-1}(p^{r},(1+p)^{r+1}-1)(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hom}(m/m^{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}E,E))

between the

over

, which sends a point

to the class in
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hom}(m/m^{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}E,E))
represented by
v
, where

v(\overline{f_{1}}\otimes 1)=a\text{ and }v(\overline{f_{2}}\otimes 1)=b,

with bar denoting image modulo
m^{2}
.

Proof.

Let

be an
E
-valued point in the fiber above the exceptional point. Assume

. Write

.
From the construction of
\widetilde{U}_{r}
, we see that

.
Therefore

is an
E
-valued point of
\text{Sp }B_{i}
, for some

. Let

be the homomorphism

associated with

. The discussion at the end of Section

implies that

is defined by

,

and

. Let

be the kernel of

. The map

induces

-linear surjection

\displaystyle\overline{f_{2}}\otimes(a/b)

\displaystyle\overline{f_{2}}\otimes 1

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\overline{f_{2}}\otimes 1,

3
Explicit bases of
H^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi))
and
\mathcal{L}
-invariants

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and think of the cyclotomic character
\chi
as a character
\chi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
.
In this section, we study two explicit bases of the first Fontaine-Herr cohomology group

H^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi))
and the corresponding formulas for the
\mathcal{L}
-invariant.
Here
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi)
is the Robba ring over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in the variable
T
,
with the standard actions of
\varphi
and
\Gamma

twisted by
x^{r}\chi
.

Colmez has constructed two power series
G(|x|,r+1)
and
G^{\prime}(|x|,r+1)
in
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
which he uses to define a basis of
H^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi))

[
Col08
, Proposition 2.19]
. In the first subsection, we explicitly compute
G(|x|,r+1)
. In the second subsection, we give a partial description of
G^{\prime}(|x|,r+1)
. In the third subsection, we state Colmez’s formula for the
\mathcal{L}
-invariant of a non-zero element of
H^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi))
expressed as a linear combination in this basis. In the same subsection, we describe another basis of
H^{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(x^{r}\chi))
studied by Benois
[
Ben11
, Proposition 1.5.4]
. Finally, we find the change of basis matrix between these two bases and restate the formula for the
\mathcal{L}
-invariant in terms of Benois’ basis (Definition
3.6
).

We first recall that the Fontaine-Herr cohomology groups
H^{i}(D)
of a
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
D
over
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
for
i=0,1,2

are defined to be the cohomology groups of the complex

\displaystyle 0\to D\to D\oplus D\to D\to 0,

(6)

where the second map is
x\mapsto((\varphi-1)x,(\gamma-1)x)
and the third map is
(y,z)\mapsto(\gamma-1)y-(\varphi-1)z
,
for
\gamma
a fixed topological generator of
\Gamma
.
We note that this complex is as in
[
Che13
, Section 2.1]
and is a bit different from the one in
[
Col08
, Section 2.1]
.

We now recall some standard facts about Robba rings. For
M>0
, let
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
be the set of bidirectional power series in the variable
T
with coefficients in
E
that converge on the elements of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with valuation belonging to
(0,M]
. More precisely, it is the set of the series
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n}
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}v_{p}(a_{n})+ns\to\infty,\text{ for all }0<s\leq M.

For each
0<s\leq M
, there is a valuation
v_{p}(\>\>,s)
on
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
defined by
v_{p}(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n},s)=\inf\{v_{p}(a_{n})+ns\text{ }|\text{ }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Any sequence in
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
that is Cauchy with respect to
v_{p}(\>\>,s)
, for each
0<s\leq M
is convergent in
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
(see
[
Ked06
, Definition 2.5.1]
). Moreover, choosing a sequence
0<r_{l}\leq M
converging to
0
with
r_{1}=M
, we get a countable family of valuations
v_{p}(\>\>,r_{l})
on
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
. Note that if
r_{l}\leq s\leq M
, for some
l\geq 1
, then
v_{p}(f(T),s)\geq\inf\{v_{p}(f(T),r_{l}),v_{p}(f(T),M)\}
. Therefore to check if a sequence in
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
is convergent, we only need to check that it converges to a common limit with respect to the valuations
v_{p}(\>\>,r_{l})
, for
l\geq 1
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
is a Fréchet space with respect to the valuations
v_{p}(\>\>,r_{l})
. For the space
\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}
, we set
r_{l}=\dfrac{1}{\phi(p^{l})}
, where
\phi
is Euler’s totient function.

The Robba ring over
E
is defined to be
\mathcal{R}_{E}=\cup_{M>0}\mathcal{E}_{E}^{]0,M]}
. It is endowed with commuting actions of an operator
\varphi
and the group
\Gamma
via
\varphi T=(1+T)^{p}-1
and
\gamma T=(1+T)^{\chi(\gamma)}-1
, for
\gamma\in\Gamma
, respectively.

We recall some facts from
[
Laz62
]
. Let
\phi_{n}(T)
for
n\geq 1
be the
p^{n}
-th cyclotomic polynomial defined by

\phi_{n}(T)=\frac{(1+T)^{p^{n}}-1}{(1+T)^{p^{n-1}}-1}=1+(1+T)^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+(1+T)^{(p-1)p^{n-1}}.

(7)

The polynomials
\phi_{n}(T)
are
\frac{1}{\phi(p^{n})}
-extremal in the sense of
[
Laz62
, Definition 2.7]
. This means that
v_{p}(\phi_{n}(T),1/\phi(p^{n}))
is attained at the constant and leading terms:

v_{p}(a_{0})=v_{p}\left(\phi_{n}(T),\frac{1}{\phi(p^{n})}\right)=v_{p}(a_{p^{n-1}(p-1)})+\frac{p^{n-1}(p-1)}{\phi(p^{n})}=1,

(8)

where
\phi_{n}(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}T+\cdots+a_{p^{n-1}(p-1)}T^{p^{n-1}(p-1)}
, with
a_{0}=p
and
a_{p^{n-1}(p-1)}=1
. For
n\geq 1
, we also have

\varphi(\phi_{n}(T))=\phi_{n+1}(T).

(9)

Let
t=\log{(1+T)}\in\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}
. The following formula relates
t
and the cyclotomic polynomials:

t=T\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\phi_{n}(T)}{p}.

(10)

We also have

\varphi(t)=pt,

(11)

and

\gamma(t)=\chi(\gamma)t,

(12)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Let
r\geq 0
. By
[
Col08
, Proposition 2.16 (i)]
, there is an isomorphism

\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}/t^{r+1}\to\prod_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}/\phi_{n}^{r+1}.

(13)

By
[
Col08
, Proposition 2.16 (ii)]
, for
n\geq 1
, there is a
\Gamma
-equivariant isomorphism

\iota_{n}:\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}/\phi_{n}^{r+1}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{n}})[t]/t^{r+1}

obtained by sending
T
to
\zeta_{p^{n}}e^{t/p^{n}}-1
. Thus the
\Gamma
-equivariant homomorphism

\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}/t^{r+1}\to\prod_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p^{n}})[t]/t^{r+1}

(14)

induced by the maps
\iota_{n}
for
n\geq 1
is an isomorphism.

For the rest of the paper, we fix a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
such that
\chi(\gamma)=\zeta_{p-1}^{a}(1+p)
for some fixed integer
a
.

3.1

Let

. The first basis vector of

constructed by Colmez is represented by the element

(for the untwisted action of

and

),
see

, Proposition 2.19]
. Here

is any

\mathcal{E}^{]0,\frac{1}{p-1}]}
mapping to

under the map (

In other words,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f(T)&\equiv&\dfrac{1}{p}\text{ }\mod{\phi_{1}(T)^{r+1}}\\[8.0pt]
f(T)&\equiv&\dfrac{1}{p^{2}}\mod{\phi_{2}(T)^{r+1}}\\
&\vdots&\\

&\vdots&\\
\end{array}

The goal of this section is to write down

Definition 3.1
.

For each

and

G_{n,\,r+1}(T):=\left[1-\left(\frac{\phi_{n}(T)}{p}\right)^{r+1}\right]^{r+1}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\prod_{i>n}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{\phi_{i}(T)}{p}\right)^{r+1}\right]^{r+1}.

To check

is well defined, we need to show that the infinite product converges. We prove that for any
n,r\geq 1
, the product
\prod_{i>n}(1-(1-(\phi_{i}(T)/p))^{r+1})
converges.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
c=1,2,\cdots,p-1
and any
j=1,2,\cdots,cp^{i-1}
, we have

v_{p}\left({cp^{i-1}\choose j}\right)=i-1-v_{p}(j).

Proof.

Exercise.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
i\geq 1
, we have
v_{p}\left(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{i})}\right)=0
and for
l\geq 1
, we have
v_{p}\left(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{l})}\right)>i-l-1
.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{i}(T)
is
\frac{1}{\phi(p^{i})}
-extremal, we see that by (
8
),
v_{p}\left(\phi_{i}(T),\frac{1}{\phi(p^{i})}\right)=1
. Since all the terms except the constant terms of the polynomials
\phi_{i}(T)
and
\phi_{i}(T)-p
are the same, we get
v_{p}\left(\phi_{i}(T)-p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{i})}\right)=1
. Therefore
v_{p}\left(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{i})}\right)=0
.

Write
\phi_{i}(T)-p=a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\cdots+a_{\phi(p^{i})}T^{\phi(p^{i})}
. By expanding the powers of
(1+T)
in
\phi_{i}(T)
, we see that

a_{j}={p^{i-1}\choose j}+{2p^{i-1}\choose j}+\cdots+{(p-1)p^{i-1}\choose j}.

By Lemma
3.2
,

v_{p}(a_{j})+\frac{j}{\phi(p^{l})}\geq i-1-v_{p}(j)+\frac{p^{v_{p}(j)}}{\phi(p^{l})}=i-1-v_{p}(j)+\frac{p^{v_{p}(j)-(l-1)}}{p-1}>i-1-v_{p}(j)+v_{p}(j)-(l-1)=i-l.

Therefore
v_{p}\left(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{l})}\right)=v_{p}\left(\phi_{i}(T)-p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{l})}\right)-1>i-l-1
.
∎

We now prove that the product

(15)

for each

. Fix

and pick

such that

. As
(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p)^{r+1}
contains no negative power of

, we have

v_{p}((1-\phi_{i}(T)/p)^{r+1},s)\geq(r+1)v_{p}\left(1-\phi_{i}(T)/p,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{l_{s}})}\right)>(r+1)(i-l_{s}-1),

,

) holds.

Our candidate for

. To see that the infinite sum is well defined, we prove the following

Theorem 3.4

For any positive integer

v_{p}\left(\quad,\frac{1}{\phi(p^{l})}\right)

Proof.

Fix

. We need to show

as

Consider

Lemma 6.2

Recall that for integer

Lemma 6.3
.

We have

\lim_{l\to k}\frac{y(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{l-k}=2a_{p}(k)a_{p}^{\prime}(k).

Proof.

The statement generalizes that of Lemma
6.1
in the case
\mathcal{L}\neq\infty
and
we give a slightly different proof.
Note that

\displaystyle y(l)^{2}-p^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2a_{p}(l)^{2}+2a_{p}(l)\sqrt{a_{p}(l)^{2}-4p^{l-1}}-4p^{l-1}}{4}-p^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{p}(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{2}+\frac{a_{p}(l)^{2}\sqrt{1-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1}}-p^{r}}{2}-p^{l-1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{y(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{l-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{p}(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{2(l-k)}+\frac{a_{p}(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{2(l-k)}\sqrt{1-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1}}+p^{r}\frac{\sqrt{1-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1}}-1}{2(l-k)}-\frac{p^{l-1}}{l-k}.

By the definition of the derivative, the first and the second summands in the equation above converge to
a_{p}(k)a_{p}^{\prime}(k)
as
l\to k
. The last summand clearly converges to
0
. The third summand also converges to
0
. Indeed,
as
l\to k

a_{p}(l)
becomes
r/2
. Therefore for such

we can write

\sqrt{1-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{1/2\choose i}(-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1})^{i}.

Using the fact that
\frac{p^{l-1}}{l-k}
converges to

, we see that

\lim_{l\to k}\frac{\sqrt{1-4a_{p}(l)^{-2}p^{l-1}}-1}{2(l-k)}=0.

Putting everything together, we see that

\lim_{l\to k}\frac{y(l)^{2}-p^{r}}{l-k}=2a_{p}(k)a_{p}^{\prime}(k).\qed

Lemma 6.4
.

We have

\lim_{l\to k}\frac{(1+p)^{l-1}-(1+p)^{k-1}}{l-k}=(1+p)^{k-1}\log(1+p).

This is the same as Lemma

\displaystyle(1+p)^{k-1}\frac{[(1+p)^{l-k}-1]}{l-k}

\displaystyle(1+p)^{k-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i}{l-k-1\choose i-1}p^{i}\right].

\lim_{l\to k}\frac{(1+p)^{l-1}-(1+p)^{k-1}}{l-k}=(1+p)^{k-1}\log(1+p).\qed

Now rewrite the point (

p
-adic
expansions of the
binomial coefficients appearing in the zig-zag conjecture.

H_{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{v_{+}}\frac{1}{i}
. Similarly,

{k+p^{n}(p-1)-2-v_{+}\choose v_{-}}=1+p^{n}(p-1)H_{-}+\text{ terms involving }p^{2n},

for
H_{-}=\sum_{i=1}^{v_{-}}\frac{1}{i}
(if
v_{-}\geq 1
;
H_{-}=0
if
v_{-}=0
).
Substituting, we get

\displaystyle\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r+v_{p}((1+\mathcal{L}p^{n}(p-1)/2)^{2}-[1+p^{n}(p-1)(H_{-}+H_{+})+\text{ terms involving }p^{2n}])

\displaystyle\qquad-v_{p}(p^{1+r/2}(1+\mathcal{L}p^{n}(p-1)/2))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r+v_{p}(\mathcal{L}p^{n}(p-1)-(H_{-}+H_{+})p^{n}(p-1)+\text{terms involving }p^{2n})

\displaystyle\qquad-(1+r/2)-v_{p}(1+\mathcal{L}p^{n}(p-1)/2).

Thus, for large
n
,

\tau_{n}=r/2-1+n+v_{p}(\mathcal{L}-H_{-}-H_{+}),

if
\mathcal{L}\neq H_{-}+H_{+}
(and
\tau_{n}\geq r/2-1+2n+c
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
independent of
n

if
\mathcal{L}=H_{-}+H_{+}
).

As mentioned above, the zig-zag conjecture involves another parameter
t
which for
V_{k_{n},a_{n}}
we call
t_{n}
. We have

t_{n}=v_{p}(k_{n}-2-r)=n.

These formulas for
\tau_{n}
and
t_{n}
show that for large
n
the parameter
\tau_{n}-t_{n}
lies, independently of
n
, in
one of the intervals (which may possibly be a point) that appear in
the statement of
[
Gha21
, Conjecture 1.1]
. This interval is determined by the size of
\nu=v_{p}(\mathcal{L}-H_{{-}}-H_{{+}})

(and is the rightmost one when
\nu=\infty
).
The conjecture accordingly specifies the exact shape of the reductions of the crystalline representations
V_{k_{n},a_{n}}
for large
n
in terms of the size of
\nu
.
Since taking duals commutes with taking reduction, we obtain the reductions of the
V^{*}_{k_{n},a_{n}}
for large
n

in terms of the size of
\nu
.
Using
Theorem
1.1
and local constancy for the reduction,
we get the reduction of the limiting semi-stable
representation
V^{*}_{k,\mathcal{L}}
in terms of the size of
\nu
.
Finally, taking duals again, we obtain Theorem
1.3
for
\mathcal{L}
finite.

Now assume we are in the case
\mathcal{L}=\infty
. Then a computation similar to the one above shows that
\tau_{n}=r/2-1+n
and
t_{n}=n^{2}
, so that
for large
n
, we have
\tau_{n}<t_{n}
(and so
\tau_{n}-t_{n}
is in the leftmost interval appearing in the conjecture).
Using
[
Gha21
, Conjecture 1.1]
and Theorem
1.1
again, we see that

\overline{V}_{k,\infty}\sim\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2}^{k-1})
, at least on the inertia subgroup
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.
Thus Theorem
1.3
also holds for
\mathcal{L}=\infty
as
\nu=-\infty
(but as remarked after the theorem,
the result in this case is classical by
[
Edi92
]
and even holds without assuming zig-zag!).

8
Bounded Hodge-Tate weights

In this section, we use the techniques of this paper to give a proof of the fact that the limit of a sequence of two-dimensional (irreducible) crystalline representations
V_{n}
for
n\geq 1
with Hodge-Tate weights in an interval
[a,b]
such that the difference of the Hodge-Tate weights is at least two infinitely often is also (irreducible) crystalline with Hodge-Tate weights in the interval
[a,b]
.
However, note that Berger
[
Ber04
, Théorème 1]
has proved more generally
that the limit of subquotients of crystalline representations with bounded Hodge-Tate weights belonging to
[a,b]
is also crystalline with Hodge-Tate weights in
[a,b]
.

Twisting by a fixed power of the cyclotomic character, we may assume that infinitely often the Hodge-Tate weights of
V_{n}
are
(0,k_{n}-1)
with
k_{n}\geq 3
.
This sequence of integers
k_{n}
is not to be confused with the one defined in (
1
).
Then there exists a sequence of unramified characters
\mu_{n}
for
n\geq 1
such that
V_{n}\simeq V_{k_{n},a_{n}}\otimes\mu_{n}
,
for some
a_{n}
with

v_{p}(a_{n})>0
.
For each
n\geq 1
, consider the ordered pair of characters
(\delta_{1,n},\delta_{2,n})
associated with
V_{n}^{*}
under triangulation. We have
\delta_{1,n}=\mu_{y_{n}}\cdot\mu_{n}^{-1}
and
\delta_{2,n}=\mu_{1/y_{n}}\chi^{1-k_{n}}\cdot\mu_{n}^{-1}
, where
y_{n}
is as in (
3
). The assumption that the sequence
V_{n}
converges means that the associated sequence of points in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
converge to a point in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
. Say that the sequence
(\delta_{1,n},\delta_{2,n})
converges to
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
in
(\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T})\setminus F^{\prime}
.

First assume that
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{-1}
is not of the form
x^{r}\chi
for
r\geq 0
. Then, by convention, the
\mathcal{L}
-invariant associated to the limit point is
\infty
.
Using the convergence of (the unramified parts of)
\delta_{1,n}\delta_{2,n}
and
\delta_{1,n}\delta_{2,n}^{-1}
, we see that
\mu_{n}^{-2}
converges to
\mu_{\alpha^{2}}
for some
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{*}
and
\mu_{y_{n}^{2}}
converges to
\mu_{y^{2}}
for some
y\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{*}
. Therefore
\delta_{1,n}=\mu_{y_{n}}\cdot\mu_{n}^{-1}
converges to
\delta_{1}=\mu_{\pm\alpha y}
. Similarly,
\delta_{2,n}
converges to
\delta_{2}=\mu_{\pm\alpha/y}\chi^{1-k}
for some
k
. So the sequence
V_{n}^{*}
converges to
V(\mu_{\pm\alpha y},\mu_{\pm\alpha/y}\chi^{1-k},\infty)
, for some
k
, in the notation of
[
Col08
]
. Now
k_{n}
is a bounded sequence of integers converging to
k
. Therefore
k_{n}=k
for large
n
. In particular,
k\geq 3
.
But
V(\mu_{\pm\alpha y},\mu_{\pm\alpha/y}\chi^{1-k},\infty)
is equal to the crystalline representation
V_{k,y+p^{k-1}/y}^{*}\otimes\mu_{\pm\alpha}
.
Taking duals, we see that the sequence
V_{n}
converges to the crystalline representation
V_{k,y+p^{k-1}/y}\otimes\mu_{\pm\alpha}^{-1}
. Untwisting by the fixed power of the cyclotomic character, the original sequence
V_{n}
also converges to a crystalline representation with Hodge-Tate weights in
[a,b]
.

Now assume that
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{-1}=x^{r}\chi
, for some
r\geq 0
. This means that
\delta_{1,n}\delta_{2,n}^{-1}=\mu_{y_{n}^{2}}\chi^{k_{n}-1}
converges to
x^{r}\chi
in the neighborhood
U_{r}
of
x^{r}\chi
in
\mathcal{T}
. Let
k=r+2
.
Since
k_{n}
is a bounded sequence of integers, the convergence implies that

k_{n}=k
for all but finitely many
n
. In particular, we have
k\geq 3
.
The point corresponding to the character
\mu_{y_{n}^{2}}\chi^{k-1}
in the blow-up
\widetilde{U}_{r}
of
U_{r}
is

(y_{n}^{2},\>(1+p)^{k-1}-1,\>y_{n}^{2}-p^{r}:0)=(y_{n}^{2},\>(1+p)^{k-1}-1,\>1:0),

at least if
y_{n}^{2}\neq p^{r}
(if
y_{n}^{2}=p^{r}
, then it is also the last point since this is the only crystalline point in the fiber above
x^{r}\chi
).
Since
y_{n}^{2}
converges to
p^{r}
, this sequence converges to the point
(p^{r},\>(1+p)^{k-1}-1,1:0)
in
\widetilde{U}_{r}
. By Theorem
5.2
, we see that the
\mathcal{L}
-invariant associated with this limit point is
\infty
. So the limit of the sequence
V_{n}^{*}
is

V(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\infty)
in the notation of
[
Col08
]
.

Now
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{-1}=x^{r}\chi=\mu_{p^{r/2}}\cdot(\mu_{1/p^{r/2}}\chi^{1-k})^{-1}
implies that there is a character
\delta
such that
\delta_{1}=\delta\cdot\mu_{p^{r/2}}
and
\delta_{2}=\delta\cdot\mu_{1/p^{r/2}}\chi^{1-k}
.
Using
[
Col08
, Proposition 4.4 (i)]
, we see that
(\delta_{1}\delta_{2})(p)
is a unit. Hence
\delta(p)
is a unit. Consequently,
V(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\infty)\simeq V(\mu_{p^{r/2}},\mu_{1/p^{r/2}}\chi^{1-k},\infty)\otimes\delta
. Therefore the limit of the crystalline representations
V^{*}_{n}
is the crystalline representation
V_{k,\infty}^{*}\otimes\delta
. Taking duals, we see that the limit of the
V_{n}
is
V_{k,\infty}\otimes\delta^{-1}
. Note that the
\delta_{1,n}=\mu_{y_{n}}\cdot\mu_{n}^{-1}
are unramified characters converging to
\delta_{1}=\delta\cdot\mu_{p^{r/2}}
. This forces
\delta
to be unramified and hence crystalline. Therefore
V_{k,\infty}\otimes\delta^{-1}
is crystalline. The last representation has Hodge-Tate weights
(0,k-1)
.
Untwisting by the fixed power of the cyclotomic character, we see that the Hodge-Tate weights of the limit representation of the original sequence again lie in
[a,b]
.

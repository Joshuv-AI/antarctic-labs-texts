DISCRETE AND CONTINUOUS WELCH BOUNDS FOR BANACH SPACES WITH APPLICATIONS
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in a finite dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
d
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
(dual of
\mathcal{X}
) such that
f_{j}(\tau_{j})=1
,
\forall 1\leq j\leq n
. Let

and
\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X})
be the Banach space of symmetric m-tensors. If the operator

is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are all non negative, then we prove that

(1)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|^{2m}\geq\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

When
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space, and
f_{j}
is defined by

(where
\mathbb{K}
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
),
\forall 1\leq j\leq n
, then Inequality (
1
) reduces to Welch bounds. Thus Inequality (
1
) improves 48 years old result obtained by Welch [
IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 1974
]. We also prove the following continuous version of Inequality (
1
). Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\Omega,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
be a continuous Bessel family for a finite dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
d
such that
f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})=1
,
\forall\alpha\in\Omega
. If the diagonal
\Delta\coloneqq\{(\alpha,\alpha):\alpha\in\Omega\}
is measurable in the measure space
\Omega\times\Omega
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\beta)<\infty,

1.
Introduction

Given a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of unit vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

(3)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq 1.

The natural question which comes immediately is that whether there is any lower bound for the quantity max in Inequality (
3
). In his celebrated paper
[
70
]
, L. Welch proved the following result in 1974.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
70
]
(
Welch bounds
)
Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of unit vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2m}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\text{({Higher order Welch bounds})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\text{({First order Welch bound})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

Theorem 1.3

Let

be a measure space and

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\alpha\neq\beta}|\langle\tau_{\alpha},\tau_{\beta}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(\mu\times\mu)((\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta)}\left[\frac{\mu(\Omega)^{2}}{d}-(\mu\times\mu)(\Delta)\right].

Theorem 1.4
.

[

(Modular Welch bounds)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital commutative C*-algebra and
\mathcal{A}^{d}

\mathcal{A}
. Let

. If

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d}}.

\displaystyle\text{({Higher order modular Welch bounds})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\text{({First order modular Welch bound})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

.

(Modular Welch bounds)

Let
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{A}

\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
45
]

(
Continuous modular Welch bounds
)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative
\sigma
-finite W*-algebra or a commutative AW*-algebra. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
of rank
d
. Let
(G,\mu_{G})
be a Lie group and
\{\tau_{g}\}_{g\in G}
be a continuous modular Bessel family for
\mathcal{E}
such that
\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{g}\rangle=1,\forall g\in G
. If the diagonal
\Delta\coloneqq\{(g,g):g\in G\}
is measurable in
G\times G
, then

\displaystyle\int_{G\times G}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2m}\,d(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(g,h)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2m}\,d\mu_{G}(g)\,d\mu_{G}(h)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{G\times G}\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{h},\tau_{g}\rangle^{m}\,d(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(g,h)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{h},\tau_{g}\rangle^{m}\,d\mu_{G}(g)\,d\mu_{G}(h)\geq\frac{\mu_{G}(G)^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\int_{G\times G}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2}\,d(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(g,h)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2}\,d\mu_{G}(g)\,d\mu_{G}(h)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{G\times G}\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\langle\tau_{h},\tau_{g}\rangle\,d(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(g,h)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\langle\tau_{h},\tau_{g}\rangle\,d\mu_{G}(g)\,d\mu_{G}(h)\geq\frac{\mu_{G}(G)^{2}}{d}.

Further, we have the
higher order continuous modular Welch bounds

\displaystyle\sup_{g,h\in G,g\neq h}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})((G\times G)\setminus\Delta)}\left[\frac{\mu_{G}(G)^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(\Delta)\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular, we have the
first order continuous modular Welch bound

\displaystyle\sup_{g,h\in G,g\neq h}\|\langle\tau_{g},\tau_{h}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})((G\times G)\setminus\Delta)}\left[\frac{\mu_{G}(G)^{2}}{d}-(\mu_{G}\times\mu_{G})(\Delta)\right].

Even after 48 years of work of Welch, to the best of our knowledge, there are no Welch bounds or their twins or relatives for Banach spaces. In this paper we derive both discrete and continuous Welch bounds for Banach spaces. We ask several open problems for further research.

2.
Discrete Welch bounds for Banach spaces

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{X}
denotes a finite dimensional Banach space and
\mathcal{X}^{*}
denotes its dual.
I_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes the identity operator on
\mathcal{X}
. We use
\mathbb{K}
to denote
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite dimensional Banach space. Given a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}
and a collection
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
,

(i)

The
frame operator
is defined as

\displaystyle S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

The
analysis operator
is defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\coloneqq(f_{j}(x))_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}.

(iii)

The
synthesis operator
is defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\mathbb{K}^{n}\ni(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}.

Following proposition follows easily.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}
and a collection
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, the frame operator factors as
S_{f,\tau}=\theta_{\tau}\theta_{f}
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
29
,
65
]

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}.
The pair
(\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})
is said to be an
approximate Schauder frame (ASF)
for
\mathcal{X}
if the map

\displaystyle S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}.

is invertible. If
S_{f,\tau}=\lambda I_{\mathcal{X}}
, for some non zero scalar
\lambda
, then
(\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})
is called as a
tight ASF
for
\mathcal{X}
.

Following theorem says that we can recover the trace of frame operator using ASFs.

Theorem 2.4
.

Given a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}
and a collection
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S_{f,\tau})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{2}_{f,\tau})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j}).

Proof.

From the expression of
S_{f,\tau}
and from the definition of trace of operator in Banach space (see
[
38
]
) we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S_{f,\tau})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j}).

Now

\displaystyle S_{f,\tau}^{2}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)(S_{f,\tau}\tau_{j}),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Again using the definition of trace we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{2}_{f,\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(S_{f,\tau}\tau_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(\tau_{j})\tau_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j}).

∎

Now we can derive the first important result of the paper.

Theorem 2.5
.

(First order Welch bound for Banach spaces)
Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in a finite dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
d
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}.
Let
n\geq d
. If the operator

S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are all non negative, then

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})\geq\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}.

and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n},

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n}}.

Further, equality holds in Inequality (
4
) if and only if
(\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})
is tight ASF for
\mathcal{X}
.
In particular, if
f_{j}(\tau_{j})=1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, then

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}=\frac{n-\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})(n-1)}

and we have
first order (discrete) Welch bound for Banach spaces

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\geq\sqrt{\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}}=\sqrt{\frac{n-\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})(n-1)}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
be eigenvalues of
S_{f,\tau}
. Then
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\geq 0
and using the diagonalizability of
S_{f,\tau}
we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{Tra}(S_{f,\tau}))^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\leq d\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}^{2}

(5)

\displaystyle=d\operatorname{Tra}(S^{2}_{f,\tau})=d\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j}).

For the second inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}+\sum_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}+(n^{2}-n)\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|

which gives

(6)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|\geq\frac{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n}.

Now let
1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{k}(\tau_{j})|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|=\left(\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\right)^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|\leq\left(\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\right)^{2}

Using Inequality (
6
) we now get

(7)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\geq\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{2}}{n^{2}-n}}.

Whenever
f_{j}(\tau_{j})=1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, Inequality (
6
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|\geq\frac{\frac{n^{2}}{d}-n}{n^{2}-n}=\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

and Inequality (
7
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\geq\sqrt{\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}}.

Equality holds in Inequality (
2
) if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}1\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)

if and only if

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=a,\text{ for some }a>0,\forall 1\leq k\leq n

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in a finite dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
d
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}.
Let
n\geq d
. If the operator

S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are all non negative, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{r}_{f,\tau})\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{r}}{d^{r-1}},\quad\forall r\in[1,\infty)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{r}_{f,\tau})\leq\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})\right)^{r}}{d^{r-1}},\quad\forall r\in(0,1).

In particular, if
f_{j}(\tau_{j})=1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{r}_{f,\tau})\geq\frac{n^{r}}{d^{r-1}},\quad\forall r\in[1,\infty)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tra}(S^{r}_{f,\tau})\leq\frac{n^{r}}{d^{r-1}},\quad\forall r\in(0,1).

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
be eigenvalues of
S_{f,\tau}
. Let
r\in[1,\infty)
. Using Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\tau_{j})}{d}\right)^{r}=\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tra}(S_{f,\tau})}{d}\right)^{r}=\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}}{d}\right)^{r}\leq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}^{r}}{d}=\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{Tra}(S^{r}_{f,\tau})

which gives the first part. Second part again follows from Jensen’s inequality.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in a finite dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
d
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}.
Let
2<p<\infty
. If the operator

S_{f,\tau}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto S_{f,\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(x)\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are all non negative, then

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\geq n(n-1)\left(\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+n.

Proof.

Define
r\coloneqq 2p/(p-2)
and
q
be the conjugate index of
p/2
. Then
q=r/2
. Using Theorem
2.5
and Holder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{d}-n

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|\leq\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}1\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}(n^{2}-n)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}(n^{2}-n)^{\frac{2}{r}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}(n^{2}-n)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

which gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n^{2}}{d}-n\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\left(\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)(n^{2}-n)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle n(n-1)\left(\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+n

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n^{2}-n)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{d}-n\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+n

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{j}(\tau_{j})f_{j}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})f_{k}(\tau_{j})|^{\frac{p}{2}}.

∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Grassmannian frames exist in every dimension for every Banach space.

Proof.

Our arguments are motivated from the arguments given in

6

for Hilbert spaces which mainly uses compactness and continuity. We give arguments only for real Banach spaces and complex case follows by considering real and imaginary parts (of the linear functionals). Define

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{X}}^{n}\coloneqq\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X},\|x_{1}\|=\cdots=\|x_{n}\|=1\},

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{X^{*}}}^{n}\coloneqq\{(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}):\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{*},\|\phi_{1}\|=\cdots=\|\phi_{n}\|=1\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\{((x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})):((x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}))\in S_{\mathcal{X}}^{n}\times S_{\mathcal{X^{*}}}^{n},\phi_{j}(x_{j})=1,\forall 1\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle\quad\quad(\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})\text{ is an ASF for }\mathcal{X}\text{ and the frame operator }S_{\phi,x}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\text{is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are all non negative}\},

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{W}\to[0,1],\quad\Phi((x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}))\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}).

We re norm

\displaystyle\|((x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}))\|\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\|x_{j}\|+\|\phi_{j}\|)

and consider

which proves the theorem. Let
(\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})

Now for any given

\displaystyle\|((\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}),(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}))-((\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}),(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}))\|<\delta,

then

\displaystyle|\Phi((\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}),(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}))-\Phi((\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}),(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}))|=\left|\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|g_{j}(\omega_{k})|-\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\big||g_{j}(\omega_{k})|-|f_{j}(\tau_{k})|\big|\leq\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\big|g_{j}(\omega_{k})-f_{j}(\tau_{k})\big|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\big|(f_{j}+h_{j})(\tau_{k}+\rho_{k})-f_{j}(\tau_{k})\big|=\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\big|f_{j}(\tau_{k})+f_{j}(\rho_{k})+h_{j}(\tau_{k})+h_{j}(\rho_{k})-f_{j}(\tau_{k})\big|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|f_{j}(\rho_{k})|+\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|h_{j}(\tau_{k})|+\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|h_{j}(\rho_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|f_{j}\|\|\rho_{k}\|+\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|h_{j}\|\|\tau_{k}\|+\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|h_{j}\|\|\rho_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq R\cdot\delta+\delta\cdot R+\delta^{2}\leq R\cdot\delta+\delta\cdot R+R\delta^{2}<\varepsilon.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite dimensional Banach space of dimension
d
. Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a finite measure space such that there are measurable subsets
\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Omega_{n},\quad\Omega_{j}\cap\Omega_{k}=\emptyset,\quad\forall 1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq n.

If
n\geq d
, then there exists a continuous ASF
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
for
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Let
(\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})
be an ASF for
\mathcal{X}
(they exist. Infact, any spanning collection can be turned to an ASF or a pair of basis for the space and its dual works, see
[
46

44
]
). Define

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{g_{j}}{\sqrt{\mu(\Omega_{j})}},\quad\forall\alpha\in\Omega_{j},\forall 1\leq j\leq n,\quad\tau_{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{\omega_{j}}{\sqrt{\mu(\Omega_{j})}},\quad\forall\alpha\in\Omega_{j},\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{j}}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{j}}\frac{g_{j}(x)}{\sqrt{\mu(\Omega_{j})}}\frac{\omega_{j}}{\sqrt{\mu(\Omega_{j})}}\,d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}(x)\omega_{j},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is a continuous ASF for
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Like discrete case, we can get trace of frame operator using continuous ASFs.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
be a continuous approximate Bessel family for
\mathcal{X}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{f,\tau})=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha),

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{f,\tau}^{2})=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta).

Proof.

Let
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
be any basis for
\mathcal{X}
, where
d
is the dimension of
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
be the dual basis associated with
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
. Then
S_{f,\tau}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}(x)(S_{f,\tau}\omega_{j})
which gives

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{f,\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}(S_{f,\tau}\omega_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\omega_{j})\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\omega_{j})\zeta_{j}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}(\tau_{\alpha})\omega_{j}\right)\,d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)

and

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S^{2}_{f,\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}(S^{2}_{f,\tau}\omega_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(S_{f,\tau}\omega_{j})\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(S_{f,\tau}\omega_{j})\zeta_{j}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}(\tau_{\alpha})S_{f,\tau}\omega_{j}\right)\,d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(S_{f,\tau}\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)=\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})\tau_{\beta}\,d\mu(\beta)\right)\,d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta).

∎

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta)

(11)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{\text{dim}(\mathcal{X})}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{2}-\int_{\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{2}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\alpha)}{(\mu\times\mu)((\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta)}}.

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\alpha\neq\beta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})|

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\alpha\neq\beta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})|^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{(\mu\times\mu)((\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta)}\left[\frac{\mu(\Omega)^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-(\mu\times\mu)(\Delta)\right]}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{(\mu\times\mu)((\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta)}\left[\frac{\mu(\Omega)^{2}}{\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X}))}-(\mu\times\mu)(\Delta)\right]}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X}))}
be eigenvalues of

. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{\otimes m}(\tau_{\alpha}^{\otimes m})\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{2}=(\operatorname{Tra}(S_{f,\tau}))^{2}=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X}))}\lambda_{l}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X}))\sum_{l=1}^{\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{X}))}\lambda_{l}^{2}={d+m-1\choose m}\operatorname{Tra}(S^{2}_{f,\tau})

\displaystyle={d+m-1\choose m}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}^{\otimes m}(\tau_{\beta}^{\otimes m})f_{\beta}^{\otimes m}(\tau_{\alpha}^{\otimes m})\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta)

\displaystyle={d+m-1\choose m}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})^{m}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta).

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{d+m-1\choose m}}\left(\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})^{m}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d\mu(\alpha)\,d\mu(\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})^{m}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})^{m}f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})^{m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\beta)+\int_{\Delta}f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})^{2m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\beta)+\int_{\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{2m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq(\mu\times\mu)((\Omega\times\Omega)\setminus\Delta)\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,\alpha\neq\beta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\beta})f_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{m}+\int_{\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\tau_{\alpha})|^{2m}\,d(\mu\times\mu)(\alpha,\alpha).

1.
Introduction

An overpartition of a positive integer
n
is a nonincreasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
in which the first occurrence of a number
may be overlined,
\overline{p}(n)
denotes the number of overpartitions of
n
, and we define
\overline{p}(0)=1
. For example, there are
8
overpartitions of
3
enumerated by
3,\overline{3},2+1,\overline{2}+1,2+\overline{1},\overline{2}+\overline{1},1+1+1,\overline{1}+1+1
. A thorough study of the overpartition function started with the work of Corteel and Lovejoy
[
4
]
, although it has been studied under different nomenclature that dates back to MacMahon. Similar to the Hardy-Ramanujan-Rademacher formula for the partition function (cf.
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
), Zuckerman’s
[
15
]
formula for
\overline{p}(n)
states that

\overline{p}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\underset{2\nmid k}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}}\sqrt{k}\underset{(h,k)=1}{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}}\dfrac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\dfrac{d}{dn}\biggl(\dfrac{\sinh\frac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr),

(1.1)

where

\omega(h,k)=\text{exp}\Biggl(\pi i\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\dfrac{r}{k}\biggl(\dfrac{hr}{k}-\biggl\lfloor\dfrac{hr}{k}\biggr\rfloor-\dfrac{1}{2}\biggr)\Biggr)

for positive integers
h
and
k
. Similarly as Lehmer
[
10
]
obtained an error bound for the partition function
p(n)
, Engel
[
6
]
determined an error term for
\overline{p}(n)
and found that

\overline{p}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\underset{2\nmid k}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}}\sqrt{k}\underset{(h,k)=1}{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}}\dfrac{\omega(h,k)^{2}}{\omega(2h,k)}e^{-\frac{2\pi inh}{k}}\dfrac{d}{dn}\biggl(\dfrac{\sinh\frac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{k}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr)+R_{2}(n,N),

(1.2)

where

\bigl|R_{2}(n,N)\bigr|<\dfrac{N^{5/2}}{\pi n^{3/2}}\sinh\biggl(\dfrac{\pi\sqrt{n}}{N}\biggr).

(1.3)

A positive sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
\log
-concave if for all
n\geq 1
,

a^{2}_{n}-a_{n-1}a_{n+1}\geq 0.

Engel
[
6
]
proved that
\{\overline{p}(n)\}_{n\geq 2}

\log
-concave by using the asymptotic formula (
1.2
) with
N=3
followed by (
1.3
). Prior to Engel’s work on overpartitions, the
\log
-concavity of the partition function

and its associated inequalities has been studied in a wider spectrum, details can be found in
[
1
]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
. Liu and Zhang
[

proved a family of inequalities for the overpartition function.

Chen, Guo and Wang
[

]
introduced the notion of ratio

-convexity of a sequence and established that ratio
\log
-convexity implies
\log
-convexity under a certain initial condition. A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq k}
is called ratio

-convex if
\{a_{n+1}/a_{n}\}_{n\geq k}
is
\log
-convex or, equivalently, for
n\geq k+1
,

\log a_{n+2}-3\log a_{n+1}+3\log a_{n}-\log a_{n-1}\geq 0.

Let

be the difference operator defined by
\Delta f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n)
. Similar to the work done by Chen et al.
[

]
for

Theorem 1.1

[

.

[

For each

such that for all

where
\left(\alpha\right)_{r}:=\alpha\cdot(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)

They raised the following question:

[

Does there exist a positive number A such that

“

\displaystyle\dfrac{2r}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}r^{m+1}\Bigl(\dfrac{r}{n}\Bigr)^{m}\ \Biggl(\text{as},\ \binom{r+m}{r-1}\leq r^{m+1}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2r^{2}}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\dfrac{r^{2}}{n}\Bigr)^{m}\leq\dfrac{4r^{2}}{n}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\geq 2r^{2}.

From (
2.20
) and (
2.21
), it follows that for
n\geq 2r^{2}
,

\displaystyle H_{r}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)_{r-1}\dfrac{1}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)_{r-1}\dfrac{4r^{2}}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{n^{r}}

(2.22)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\dfrac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)_{r-1}\dfrac{1}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\Bigl((r-1)!+2\pi r^{2}\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)_{r-1}\Bigr)\dfrac{1}{n^{r}}.

This finishes the estimation of the lower bound for
H_{r}
.

For the upper bound estimation of
H_{r}
, we start with (
2.19
) in the following way

\displaystyle H_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{(n+r)^{r}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k\pi^{k}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{k}{2}}}

(2.23)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{(2n)^{r}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k\pi^{k}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{k}{2}}}\ \ \Bigl(\text{since},\ \dfrac{1}{(n+r)^{r}}>\dfrac{1}{(2n)^{r}}\ \forall\ n>r\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{(2n)^{r}}+\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2r-2}\dfrac{1}{(k+1)\pi^{k+1}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k+1}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}^{k}}+\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{k=2r}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k\pi^{k}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{(2n)^{r}}+\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}\underset{:=\widehat{C_{2}}(r)}{\underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{2r-2}\dfrac{1}{(k+1)\pi^{k+1}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k+1}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{r^{k}}}}+\dfrac{r}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}\underset{:=S(r)}{\underbrace{\sum_{k=2r}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k\pi^{k}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}\dfrac{1}{r^{k}}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt\Bigl(\text{since},\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}^{k}}\leq\dfrac{1}{r^{k}}\forall\ n\geq r^{2}\Bigr).

In order to estimate the infinite series
S(r)
, we need to give an upper bound of
\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}
by rewriting as

\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)_{r}=\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)^{r}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\Bigl(1+\dfrac{2i}{k}\Bigr):=\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)^{r}P(r,k).

Now,

\log P(r,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\log\Bigl(1+\dfrac{2i}{k}\Bigr)<\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\dfrac{2i}{k}=\dfrac{r(r-1)}{k}\ \implies P(r,k)<e^{\frac{r(r-1)}{k}}.

(2.24)

Using (
2.24
), we obtain

S(r)<\sum_{k=2r}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k\pi^{k}}\Bigl(\dfrac{k}{2}\Bigr)^{r}e^{\frac{r(r-1)}{k}}\dfrac{1}{r^{k}}\leq\dfrac{e^{\frac{r-1}{2}}}{2^{r}}\sum_{k=2r}^{\infty}\dfrac{k^{r-1}}{(\pi r)^{k}}\ \ \Bigl(\text{since},e^{\frac{r(r-1)}{k}}\leq e^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\forall\ k\geq 2r\Bigr).

(2.25)

Moreover,
k^{r-1}<r^{k}
for all
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 2r
. To observe this fact, we first note that to prove
k^{r-1}<r^{k}
, it is equivalent to show

\dfrac{r-1}{\log r}<\dfrac{k}{\log k}.

(2.26)

Define
f(x):=\dfrac{x}{\log x}
and observe that
f(x)
is strictly increasing for all
x>e
. As
k\geq 2r\geq 4>e
, it follows that
f(k)>f(2r)
and the fact that
f(2r)>\dfrac{r-1}{\log r}
for
r\geq 2
, we conclude (
2.26
).

Applying (
2.26
) in (
2.25
), we get

S(r)<\dfrac{e^{\frac{r-1}{2}}}{2^{r}}\sum_{k=2r}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{\pi^{k}}=\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{e}(\pi-1)}\Bigl(\dfrac{\sqrt{e}}{2\ r^{2}}\Bigr)^{r}<\dfrac{1}{10^{r}}.

(2.27)

Hence, by (
2.27
) and (
2.23
), we obtain for all
n\geq r^{2}
,

H_{r}<\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{2^{r}\ n^{r}}+\dfrac{\widehat{C_{2}}(r)}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}+\dfrac{r}{10^{r}\ n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}=\dfrac{C(r)}{n^{r-\frac{1}{2}}}-\dfrac{(r-1)!}{2^{r}\ n^{r}}+\underset{=C_{2}(r)}{\underbrace{\Bigl(\widehat{C_{2}}(r)+\dfrac{r}{10^{r}}\Bigr)}}\dfrac{1}{n^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(2.28)

∎

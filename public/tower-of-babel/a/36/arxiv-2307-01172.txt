The weak noise theory of the O’Connell-Yor polymer as an integrable discretisation of the nonlinear Schrodinger equation
Alexandre Krajenbrink, Pierre Le Doussal

Abstract
We investigate and solve the weak noise theory for the semi-discrete O’Connell-Yor directed polymer. In the large deviation regime, the most probable evolution of the partition function obeys a classical non-linear system which is a non-standard discretisation of the nonlinear Schrodinger equation with mixed initial-final conditions. We show that this system is integrable and find its general solution through an inverse scattering method and a novel Fredholm determinant framework that we develop. This allows to obtain the large deviation rate function of the free energy of the polymer model from its conserved quantities and to study its convergence to the large deviations of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation. Our model also degenerates to the classical Toda chain, which further substantiates the applicability of our Fredholm framework.

I
Model and observable

In this Supp. Mat. we will consider a slight generalization of the partition sums (

corresponds to adding drifts to the Brownian weights

It is convenient to define

Using the rules of Ito calculus it is easy to see that these partition sums satisfy the following coupled stochastic equations

where the

The observable of interest is the partition function at one point

and its probability distribution function

. It is useful to also introduce its logarithm
H_{N}=\log z_{N}(t=1)
and its PDF

.
These PDF’s exhibit, in the weak noise limit

{\cal P}_{N}(z)\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\sim}e^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\hat{\Phi}_{N}(z)}\quad,\quad{\cal P}_{N}(H)\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\sim}e^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Phi_{N}(H)}

(S48)

To compute these rate functions, which differ only by a change of variable,
\hat{\Phi}_{N}(z)=\Phi_{N}(H=\log z)

we will first compute the following generating function of the cumulants of

\overline{e^{-\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon}z_{N}(t=1)}}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\sim}e^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Psi_{N}(\Lambda)}

(S49)

and the rate function

II
Dynamical field theory and saddle point equations

We now use the standard path integral representation using source fields
j_{1}(t),\dots,j_{N}(t)

\displaystyle\overline{e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t\tiny{\sum}_{n=1}^{N}j_{n}(t)z_{n}(t)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iiint\mathcal{D}\eta\mathcal{D}\tilde{z}\mathcal{D}ze^{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,\sum_{n=1}^{N}[-\frac{\tilde{z}_{n}}{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}z_{n}-z_{n-1}+z_{n}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}z_{n}\eta_{n}-a_{n}z_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{n}^{2}+j_{n}z_{n}]}

\displaystyle\iint\mathcal{D}\tilde{z}\mathcal{D}ze^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}S[z,\tilde{z},j]}

(S53)

The source-less action reads

S_{0}[z,\tilde{z}]=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,\sum_{n=1}^{N}[\tilde{z}_{n}(\partial_{t}z_{n}-z_{n-1}+z_{n}-a_{n}z_{n})-\frac{1}{2}z_{n}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n}^{2}]

In the limit

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}&=z_{n-1}-z_{n}+\frac{g}{2}z_{n}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n}+a_{n}z_{n}\\

We have added for convenience the parameter

Note that a priori the above saddle point equations hold for

for all

\tilde{z}_{n}(t)=0

In practice we also take

except if

It is useful to note the following exact symmetry of the solution of the system (
S55

with the boundary conditions (

Hence the PDF

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\vec{v}_{n}&=U_{n}\vec{v}_{n}\\

In our case,

\frac{g}{2}\tilde{z}_{n}&\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad L_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\lambda}&\frac{z_{n}}{\lambda}\\

(S88)

where

(S89)

so that the contribution of the optimal partition function

(S90)

and

\psi_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}\lambda^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\psi}_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}\lambda^{-n}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(S91)

Since there can be only two independent solutions, there exists a linear combination relating the two sets which defines the scattering amplitudes

\begin{split}\phi_{n}&=a(\lambda,t)\bar{\psi}_{n}+b(\lambda,t)\psi_{n}\\
\bar{\phi}_{n}&=-\tilde{a}(\lambda,t)\psi_{n}+\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)\bar{\psi}_{n}\end{split}

(S92)

equivalent to implying the following asymptotic conditions at
+\infty

\phi_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda,t)\lambda^{-n}\\
b(\lambda,t)\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\phi}_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)\lambda^{-n}\\
-\tilde{a}(\lambda,t)\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix}

(S93)

In practice, the two independent solutions
\{\vec{v}_{n}\}
verifying the time evolution equation will be chosen as

\vec{v}_{n}=e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}\phi_{n}\,\quad\text{and}\quad\vec{v}_{n}=e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}\bar{\phi}_{n}

(S94)

VI
Time dependence of the scattering amplitudes

Inserting the solutions (
S94
) into the time equation of the Lax pair (
S85
) and evaluating it at
n=+\infty
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}a(\lambda,t)=0\\
&\partial_{t}\tilde{a}(\lambda,t)=0\\
&\partial_{t}b(\lambda,t)=(1-\lambda^{2})b(\lambda,t)\\
&\partial_{t}\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)=(\lambda^{2}-1)\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)\end{split}

(S95)

Hence the scattering amplitudes are either time-independent (for
a(\lambda)
and
\tilde{a}(\lambda)
) or have a Gaussian time dependence

\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)=\tilde{b}(\lambda)e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t},\quad b(\lambda,t)=b(\lambda)e^{(1-\lambda^{2})t}

(S96)

The opposite sign in the time evolution of
b(\lambda)
and
\tilde{b}(\lambda)
reflects the fact that the saddle point equations describe simultaneously forward and backward evolutions in time. This result is universal as long as the fields vanish at
\pm\infty
.

VII
Wronskian of the solution

On top of the time evolution of the scattering amplitudes, we now determine a normalisation relation using the Wronskian of the problem. For the two solutions of the Lax problem
\{\phi_{n},\bar{\phi}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the Wronskian as

W_{n}=W(\phi_{n},\bar{\phi}_{n})=\phi_{n}^{(1)}\bar{\phi}_{n}^{(2)}-\phi_{n}^{(2)}\bar{\phi}_{n}^{(1)}

(S97)

From the evolutions (
S85
),
We start with the time derivative and the index increment

\begin{split}\partial_{t}W_{n}&={{\rm Tr}}(U_{n})W_{n}=0,\\
W_{n+1}&=\mathrm{Det}(L_{n})W_{n}=(1-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})W_{n}\end{split}

(S98)

The Wronskian of
\{\phi_{n},\bar{\phi}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
at both infinities read

W_{n}\underset{n\to-\infty}{\sim}-1,\quad W_{n}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}-(a\tilde{a}+b\tilde{b})

(S99)

and we have also have from Eq. (
S98
)

W_{+\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}(1-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})W_{-\infty}

(S100)

We then deduce that

a(\lambda)\tilde{a}(\lambda)+b(\lambda)\tilde{b}(\lambda)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}(1-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})

(S101)

This normalisation relation is universal. Contrary to the Ablowitz-Ladik integrable system
ablowitz2004discrete
;
footnote-ablowitz-ladik
, the normalisation of the scattering amplitudes (
S101
) does not depend on the fields
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}
.

VIII
Conserved quantities

We derive in this Section the conserved quantities of the integrable system (
S55
). As we shall see, there exist an infinite amount of conserved quantities, which is standard for such models. To obtain the conserved quantities, we require three ingrediens:

1.

The Ricatti equation of the Lax pair;

2.

A continuity equation arising from the compatibility of the Lax equations expressed with log derivatives and the Ricatti variables and a relation between the continuity equation and the scattering amplitudes;

3.

A suitable Taylor expansion of
\log a(\lambda)
or Laurent expansion of
\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)
as a function of the spectral parameter.

VIII.1
Ricatti equation

Let us first define the Ricatti variable
\Gamma
and its inverse
\tilde{\Gamma}
as

\Gamma_{n}=\frac{v_{n}^{(2)}}{v_{n}^{(1)}},\quad\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}}

(S102)

Dividing the two equations of the space part of the Lax pair, we obtain the following recursions for the Ricatti variable and its inverse

(\Gamma_{n+1}+\tilde{z}_{n})(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})=\Gamma_{n}(\lambda^{2}-a_{n})

(S103)

and

(1+\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1})(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})=\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1}(\lambda^{2}-a_{n})

(S104)

As we shall see subsequently, we need to expand these equations to obtain the Taylor and Laurent series of the Ricatti variables.

VIII.2
Continuity equations

The continuity equations will be obtained as the compatibility of the dynamics of
\log v_{n}^{(1)}
and
\log v_{n}^{(2)}
respectively.

First continuity equation

From the Lax pair system, we obtain the pair of equations for
\log v_{n}^{(1)}

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\log v_{n}^{(1)}=\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}-z_{n-1}\Gamma_{n}\\
\log(\frac{v^{(1)}_{n+1}}{v^{(1)}_{n}})=\Delta^{+}\log v^{(1)}_{n}=\log(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{z_{n}}{\lambda}\Gamma_{n})\end{cases}

(S105)

where we introduced the following notation for the finite difference
\Delta^{+}f_{n}=f_{n+1}-f_{n}
. We rewrite the above system for convenience as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\log(v_{n}^{(1)}\lambda^{n}e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t})=-z_{n-1}\Gamma_{n}\\
\\
\log\left(\frac{v^{(1)}_{n+1}\lambda^{n+1}e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}}{v^{(1)}_{n}\lambda^{n}e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}}\right)=\Delta^{+}\log\left(v^{(1)}_{n}\lambda^{n}e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}\right)=\log(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})\end{cases}

(S106)

The compatibility is obtained by the commutation relation

\partial_{t}\Delta^{+}=\Delta^{+}\partial_{t}

(S107)

which yields the first compatibility equation

\partial_{t}\log(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})=-\Delta^{+}(z_{n-1}\Gamma_{n})

(S108)

We therefore interpret
J_{n}^{(1)}=z_{n-1}\Gamma_{n}
as a generalised current and
\varrho_{n}^{(1)}=\log(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})
as a generalised density. In particular, since the potentials
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}
vanish at infinities, we have the conservation law

\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varrho_{n}^{(1)}\right)=0

(S109)

With the particular choice of
\vec{v}_{n}=e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}\phi_{n}
and summing the second equation of (
S106
) over integers in
\mathbb{Z}
we obtain the expected relation between the scattering amplitude and this set of conserved charges as

\log a(\lambda)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\log(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad a(\lambda)=\prod_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}(1+z_{n}\Gamma_{n})

(S110)

Second continuity equation

We now repeat the same exercise for
\log v_{n}^{(2)}
and first obtain

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\log v_{n}^{(2)}=\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{2}+\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}\\
\log(\frac{v^{(2)}_{n+1}}{v^{(2)}_{n}})=\Delta^{+}\log v^{(2)}_{n}=\log(-\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+\lambda-\frac{1}{\lambda}z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda})\end{cases}

(S111)

which is also rewritten for convenience as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\log(-v_{n}^{(2)}\lambda^{-n}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t})=\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}\\
\\
\log\left(\frac{-v^{(2)}_{n+1}\lambda^{-n-1}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}}{-v^{(2)}_{n}\lambda^{-n}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}t}}\right)=\log(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{n}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})\end{cases}

(S112)

The compatibility in this case reads

\partial_{t}\log(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{n}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})=-\Delta^{+}(-\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n})

(S113)

We also interpret
J_{n}^{(2)}=-\tilde{z}_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
as a generalised current and
\varrho_{n}^{(2)}=\log(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{n}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})
as a generalised density. In particular, since the potentials
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}
vanish at infinities, we have the conservation law

\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varrho_{n}^{(2)}\right)=0

(S114)

With the particular choice of
\vec{v}_{n}=e^{\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{2}t}\bar{\phi}_{n}
and summing the second equation of (
S112
) over integers in
\mathbb{Z}
we obtain the expected relation between the scattering amplitude and this set of conserved charges as

\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\log(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{n}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\tilde{a}(\lambda)=\prod_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}(1-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{n}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}+z_{n})-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})

(S115)

VIII.3
Conserved charges

We now complete the determination of the conserved charges in the system by proceeding to the suitable expansion of the continuity equations. We now take all drifts equal to 0, i.e.
a_{n}=0
, to simplify the expressions.

Taylor expansion of
a(\lambda)

Since
\log a(\lambda)
is analytic close to the origin, we choose to expand Eqs. (
S103
)-(
S104
)-(
S110
) in a Taylor series

\log a(\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\ell}C_{\ell},\quad\Gamma_{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\ell}\Gamma_{n}^{(\ell,0)},\quad\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\ell}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}^{(\ell,0)}

(S116)

and

\varrho_{n}^{(1)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\ell}\varrho^{(1)}_{n,\ell},\quad J_{n}^{(1)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\ell}J^{(1)}_{n,\ell},\quad C_{\ell}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varrho^{(1)}_{n,\ell}

(S117)

Formally, there exists two solutions to the Taylor expansion of Eqs. (
S103
)-(
S104
):

•

The first one is physical as it consistent with a Taylor expansion of
\log a(\lambda)
and it provides the expected conserved quantities;

•

The second one is unphysical as it would impose that
\log a(\lambda)
behaves as
\log\lambda^{2}
for small spectral parameter and therefore that the solution would include a zero-mode, incompatible with the decay of
z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}
at infinities. Nonetheless formally, this expansion yields additional conserved quantities, which conservation can also be checked by hand, and for completeness we will provide them.

The first solution, the ’physical’ expansion, yields for its first two terms

1.

For
\ell=0

\varrho^{(1)}_{n,0}=\log(1-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-1}),\quad J^{(1)}_{n,0}=-z_{n-1}\tilde{z}_{n-1}

(S118)

2.

For
\ell=1

\varrho^{(1)}_{n,1}=-\frac{z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-2}}{\left(1-z_{n-1}\tilde{z}_{n-2}\right)\left(1-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-1}\right)},\quad J^{(1)}_{n,1}=1-\frac{1}{1-z_{n-1}\tilde{z}_{n-2}}

(S119)

The conserved charges are then

\begin{split}C_{0}&=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\log(1-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-1})\\
C_{1}&=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}-\frac{z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-2}}{\left(1-z_{n-1}\tilde{z}_{n-2}\right)\left(1-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n-1}\right)}\end{split}

(S120)

and one can verify using the dynamical equations for
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}\eqref{eq:YO-polymer-saddle}
that for any solution we obtain

\partial_{t}\varrho^{(1)}_{n,\ell}=J^{(1)}_{n,\ell}-J^{(1)}_{n+1,\ell}

(S121)

The second solution, the ’unphysical’ expansion, yields for its first two terms (we use again the same notation for simplicity)

1.

For
\ell=0

\varrho^{(1)}_{n,0}=\log\left(\frac{z_{n+1}}{z_{n}\left(1-z_{n+1}\tilde{z}_{n}\right)}\right),\quad J^{(1)}_{n,0}=-\frac{z_{n-1}}{z_{n}}

(S122)

2.

For
\ell=1

\varrho^{(1)}_{n,1}=\frac{\frac{z_{n+2}}{z_{n+2}\tilde{z}_{n+1}-1}+\frac{z_{n+1}^{2}}{z_{n}}}{z_{n+1}\left(z_{n+1}\tilde{z}_{n}-1\right)},\quad J^{(1)}_{n,1}=-\frac{z_{n-1}z_{n+1}}{z_{n}^{2}\left(z_{n+1}\tilde{z}_{n}-1\right)}

(S123)

Laurent expansion of
\tilde{a}(\lambda)

Since
\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)
is analytic for large
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}
, we choose to expand Eqs. (
S103
)-(
S104
)-(
S115
) as Laurent series

\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{C}_{\ell}}{\lambda^{2\ell}},\quad\Gamma_{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma_{n}^{(\ell,\infty)}}{\lambda^{2\ell}},\quad\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}^{(\ell,\infty)}}{\lambda^{2\ell}}

(S124)

and

\varrho_{n}^{(2)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varrho^{(2)}_{n,\ell}}{\lambda^{2\ell}},\quad J_{n}^{(2)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{J^{(2)}_{n,\ell}}{\lambda^{2\ell}},\quad\tilde{C}_{\ell}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varrho^{(2)}_{n,\ell}

(S125)

Formally, there exists two solutions to the Laurent expansion of Eqs. (
S103
)-(
S104
):

•

The first one is physical as it consistent with a Laurent expansion of
\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)
and it provides the expected conserved quantities;

•

The second one is unphysical as it would impose that
\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)
behaves as
\log\lambda^{2}
for large spectral parameter whereas we seek a solution for which
\log\tilde{a}(\lambda)\to 0
for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\to\infty
. Nonetheless, formally this expansion yields additional conserved quantities, which conservation can also be checked by hand, and for completeness we will provide them.

The first solution, the ’physical’ expansion, yields for its first two terms

1.

For
\ell=1

\varrho^{(2)}_{n,1}=-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n},\quad J^{(2)}_{n,1}=-z_{n-1}\tilde{z}_{n}

(S126)

2.

For
\ell=2

\varrho^{(2)}_{n,2}=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{z}_{n}\left(z_{n}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n}+2z_{n-1}\right),\quad J^{(2)}_{n,2}=-\tilde{z}_{n}\left(z_{n-1}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n-1}+z_{n-2}\right)

(S127)

The conserved charges are then

\begin{split}\tilde{C}_{1}&=-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}\\
\tilde{C}_{2}&=-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\tilde{z}_{n}\left(z_{n}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n}+2z_{n-1}\right)\end{split}

(S128)

and one can verify using the dynamical equations for
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}\eqref{eq:YO-polymer-saddle}
that for any solution we obtain

\partial_{t}\varrho^{(2)}_{n,\ell}=J^{(2)}_{n,\ell}-J^{(2)}_{n+1,\ell}

(S129)

The second solution, the ’unphysical’ expansion, yields for its first two terms (we use again the same notation for simplicity)

1.

For
\ell=1

\varrho^{(2)}_{n,1}=\log\left(\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+1}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}\right),\quad J^{(2)}_{n,1}=z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}+\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+1}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}

(S130)

2.

For
\ell=2

\varrho^{(2)}_{n,2}=z_{n+1}\tilde{z}_{n+1}-\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+1}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}+\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+2}}{\tilde{z}_{n+1}},\quad J^{(2)}_{n,2}=\frac{z_{n+1}\tilde{z}_{n+1}^{2}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}-\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+1}^{2}}{\tilde{z}_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{z}_{n+2}}{\tilde{z}_{n}}

(S131)

VIII.4
Discussion on Generalized Gibbs Ensembles and other flows generated by the conserved charges

We first comment on the fact that the quantity
(z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n})
is at the same a local density and a local current as

-z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}=J_{n,0}^{(1)}=\varrho_{n,1}^{(2)}

(S132)

Such equality also arises in other models and has
"surprising consequences"
, see Ref.
(
spohn2021hydrodynamic
, Sec. 2.1)
in the context of the Toda lattice. It would be interesting in the present context to analyse the consequence of this identity.

What is more, from the knowledge of the conserved quantities, it is possible to formally define a Generalized Gibbs measure on the fields
z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}
that is conserved by the dynamics (
10
). Such construction was done for a variety of semi-discrete integrable models in recent works, see Refs.
grava2023discrete
;
mazzuca2023equilibrium
;
spohn2020generalized
;
spohn2021hydrodynamic
;
spohn2022hydrodynamic
. To this aim, we select a subset of conserved quantities, e.g.
C_{0},\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
, and a set of conjugated "temperature" (in this example
\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
) and construct the measure

\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{d}z_{n}\mathrm{d}\tilde{z}_{n}\exp(-\beta_{0}C_{0}-\beta_{1}\tilde{C}_{1}-\beta_{2}\tilde{C}_{2})

(S133)

If the initial conditions of the OY system are chosen randomly according to the measure (
S133
), we then expect this distribution of
z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}
to be stationary as the dynamics will preserve
C_{0},\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
. Such measure can a priori be extended to arbitrary number of conserved quantities and for the usual semi-discrete integrable models, this measure was related to measures appearing in Random Matrix Theory
grava2023discrete
;
mazzuca2023equilibrium
;
spohn2020generalized
;
spohn2021hydrodynamic
;
spohn2022hydrodynamic
, we expect such a connexion to hold in this case as well.

Finally, we address a last comment about the conserved charges which is the flow induced by these. We can recast the original OY system (
10
) as

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}&=\frac{\delta}{\delta\tilde{z}_{n}}(\tilde{C}_{1}-\tilde{C}_{2})\\
-\partial_{t}\tilde{z}_{n}&=\frac{\delta}{\delta z_{n}}(\tilde{C}_{1}-\tilde{C}_{2})\end{split}

(S134)

Similarly to Ref.
merola1994novel
, one could define other dynamics by replacing in (
S134
) the term
\tilde{C}_{1}-\tilde{C}_{2}
by any linear combination of conserved quantities. The differential system induced would remain integrable with the same space Lax matrix
L_{n}
in (
S88
) but the time Lax matrix
U_{n}
would have to be modified.

The Hamiltonian system (
S134
) can also be rewritten in a symplectic form by introducing the suitable Poisson bracket. To this aim, introduce the field gradient

\nabla_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\delta}{\delta z_{n}}\\
\dfrac{\delta}{\delta\tilde{z}_{n}}\,,\end{pmatrix}

(S135)

and rewrite (
S134
) as

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}z_{n}\\
\tilde{z}_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\nabla_{n}(\tilde{C}_{1}-\tilde{C}_{2})\,.

(S136)

Then by introducing the Poisson bracket

\langle F,G\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\nabla_{n}(F)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\nabla_{n}(G)\,,

(S137)

we have that if
I[z,\tilde{z}]
is a functional of
z,\tilde{z}
, its dynamic is given by

\partial_{t}I[z,\tilde{z}]=\langle I[z,\tilde{z}],\tilde{C}_{1}-\tilde{C}_{2}\rangle\,.

(S138)

In the present case, the canonical Poisson bracket (
S137
) is the natural structure behind the dynamics but other Poisson brackets could appear in different semi-discrete integrable models as is the case for continuous systems where the Poisson bracket is not the same for the nonlinear Schrodinger equation
ablowitz2004discrete
and the derivative nonlinear Schrodinger equation
kaup1978exact
.

IX
Solution of the scattering problem for arbitrary
g

We now obtain the solution of the scattering problem associated to the non-linear system for arbitrary
g
, for the
specific initial and final conditions considered in this paper, that we now recall

z_{n}(t=0)=\delta_{n,1}\quad,\quad\tilde{z}_{n}(t=1)=-\Lambda\delta_{N,n}

(S139)

and we recall that
z_{n\leqslant 0}(t)=0
and
\tilde{z}_{n\geqslant N+1}(t)=0
.
The scattering problem is defined by the recursion, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\phi_{n+1}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{(1)}_{n+1}\\
\phi^{(2)}_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\lambda}&\frac{z_{n}}{\lambda}\\
-\frac{g}{2\lambda}\tilde{z}_{n}&\lambda-\frac{g}{2\lambda}z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{(1)}_{n}\\
\phi^{(2)}_{n}\end{pmatrix}

(S140)

together with the same equation for
\bar{\phi}_{n}
, with the following boundary conditions at
n\to\pm\infty

\displaystyle\phi_{n}\sim\lambda^{-n}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\phi}_{n}\sim\lambda^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-1\end{pmatrix},\quad n\to-\infty

(S141)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}\sim\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda)\lambda^{-n}\\
b(\lambda)e^{(1-\lambda^{2})t}\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\phi}_{n}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}(\lambda)e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t}\lambda^{-n}\\
-\tilde{a}(\lambda)\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad n\to+\infty

(S142)

We will now consider the scattering problem successively at the initial time
t=0
and at the final time
t=1
.
In each case we consider it first for
\phi
and then for
\bar{\phi}
.

IX.1
Scattering problem at
t=0

A.1 For
\phi

One must solve, from (
S140
) and the initial condition (
S139
)

\begin{split}&\phi^{(1)}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\phi^{(1)}_{n}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\delta_{n,1}\phi^{(2)}_{n}\\
&\phi^{(2)}_{n+1}=-\frac{g}{2\lambda}\tilde{z}_{n}\phi^{(1)}_{n}+(\lambda-\frac{g}{2\lambda}\tilde{z}_{n}\delta_{n,1}-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda})\phi^{(2)}_{n}\end{split}

(S143)

Using (
S141
), the first equation implies that

\begin{split}\phi_{n}^{(1)}&=\lambda^{-n},\qquad n\leqslant 1\\
\phi_{n}^{(1)}&=\lambda^{-n}+\lambda^{1-n}\phi_{1}^{(2)},\qquad n\geqslant 2\\
\end{split}

(S144)

In particular we obtain from (
S142
) that

a(\lambda)=1+\lambda\phi_{1}^{(2)}

(S145)

The second equation in (
S143
) then leads to (we recall that
a_{n}=0
for
n<1
and for
n>N
)

\begin{split}\phi^{(2)}_{n+1}&=-\frac{g}{2}\tilde{z}_{n}\lambda^{-n-1}+\lambda\phi^{(2)}_{n},\quad n<1\\
\phi^{(2)}_{n+1}&=-\frac{g}{2}\tilde{z}_{n}\lambda^{-n-1}(1+\lambda\phi_{1}^{(2)})+\lambda(1-\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda^{2}})\phi^{(2)}_{n},\quad n>1\\
\end{split}

(S146)

which implies using (
S141
)

\displaystyle\phi^{(2)}_{n}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}-\frac{g}{2}\tilde{z}_{k}\lambda^{n-2-2k},\quad n\leqslant 1

(S147)

\displaystyle\phi^{(2)}_{2}=-\frac{g}{2\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{1}+(\lambda-\frac{g}{2\lambda}\tilde{z}_{1}(t=0){-\frac{a_{1}}{\lambda}})\phi^{(2)}_{1},\quad n=2

(S148)

\displaystyle\phi^{(2)}_{n}=\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\bigg(-\frac{g}{2}\tilde{z}_{k}\lambda^{n-2-2k}(1+\lambda\phi_{1}^{(2)})\prod_{\ell=k+1}^{n-1}(1-\frac{a_{\ell}}{\lambda^{2}})\bigg)+\lambda^{n-2}\prod_{\ell=2}^{n-1}(1-\frac{a_{\ell}}{\lambda^{2}})\phi^{(2)}_{2},\quad n\geqslant 3

(S149)

We can simplify the
n=2
term as

\begin{split}\phi^{(2)}_{2}&=-\frac{g}{2\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{1}(t=0)+(\lambda-\frac{g}{2\lambda}\tilde{z}_{1}(t=0){-\frac{a_{1}}{\lambda}})\frac{a(\lambda)-1}{\lambda}\\
&=a(\lambda)(1-\frac{g}{2\lambda^{2}}\tilde{z}_{1}(t=0){-\frac{a_{1}}{\lambda^{2}}})-1+{\frac{a_{1}}{\lambda^{2}}}\\
\end{split}

(S150)

We finally obtain
a(\lambda)
from (
S145

Recalling the definition (

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{n}=\oint_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert w\right\rvert}=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}w^{2n-1}\log(1+\Lambda w^{-2N}e^{w^{2}-1}),\quad n\in[1,\infty]

This is the result in the absence of soliton. In the presence of soliton there is an additional additive contribution
so that the values of the conserved quantities are now

\displaystyle\log(\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2n}-\lambda_{-1}^{2n}}{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta\tilde{C}_{n}}{\lambda^{2n}}

Similarly from (
S192

\displaystyle\log(\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}})=\log(\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda_{-1}^{2}})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2n}\frac{\lambda_{-1}^{-2n}-\lambda_{0}^{-2n}}{n}=\Delta C_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2n}\Delta C_{n}

This leads to

\begin{split}\Delta C_{0}&=\log(\lambda_{0}^{2})-\log(\lambda_{-1}^{2}),\\

Consider here the case without soliton.

Remark XI.1

XII
Convergence to KPZ

XII.1
Convergence of the large deviation rate function

We will now show that the large deviation result for the OY polymer

\overline{\exp\left(-\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon}z_{N}(\tau=1)\right)}\sim\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Psi_{N}(\Lambda)\right)

(S222)

converges to the similar result for the short time KPZ equation as
N\to+\infty
under
a proper rescaling. We will consider successively the right hand side of (
S222
) (the large deviation form)
and its left hand side (the observable) and show that each side converges to the corresponding
one for the KPZ equation.

Consider the r.h.s. of (
S222
). Let us consider first the main branch. One recalls that

\Psi(\Lambda)=-\oint_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v\right\rvert}=N}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(-\Lambda v^{-N}e^{v-1})\\

(S223)

Note that since
\Lambda^{*}_{N}
grows very fast with
N
we need only to
consider the case
\Lambda<\Lambda^{*}_{N}
where the contour is a circle.
We parametrize the circle of radius
N
as
v=Ne^{\mathbf{i}k}
, and for
large
N
we expand around
k=0
as

v-N\log v=N-N\log N-N\frac{k^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(Nk^{3})

(S224)

Inserting this expansion into (
S223
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\simeq-N\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}e^{\mathbf{i}k}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(-\Lambda e^{N-1-N\log N}e^{-Nk^{2}/2})

(S226)

\displaystyle\simeq-\sqrt{2N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{2\pi}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda_{c}}e^{-q^{2}})

where we recall that
\Lambda_{c}=-e^{1-N}N^{N}
. We can then make the connection on the right hand
side of the equation (
S222
) with the known result for the short time KPZ
equation with droplet initial conditions
le2016exact
;
krajenbrink2021inverse
as

\exp\left(-\frac{\Psi_{N}(\Lambda)}{\varepsilon}\right)\to\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\rm KPZ}}}\Psi_{\rm KPZ}(z)\right)\quad,\quad\Psi_{\rm KPZ}(z)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(-ze^{-q^{2}})

(S227)

with the identification

z=-\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda_{c}}\quad,\quad\frac{\sqrt{2N}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\rm KPZ}}}

(S228)

Hence the "KPZ time"
T_{\rm KPZ}
must be identified with the weak noise parameter
\varepsilon^{2}/(2N)
.

In the case of the second (solitonic) branch we need to study the large
N
limit of
\Delta_{N}(\Lambda)

given by Eq. (
S221
). We recall the expansions of the Lambert functions for
0<x<1
and
N\to+\infty

\displaystyle W_{0}(-e^{-1}x^{1/N})=-1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\sqrt{\log(1/x)}+\frac{2}{3N}\log x+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

(S229)

\displaystyle W_{-1}(-e^{-1}x^{1/N})=-1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\sqrt{\log(1/x)}+\frac{2}{3N}\log x+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{N^{3/2}})

(S230)

From theses expansions we obtain from (
S220
)

\Lambda\Delta_{N}^{\prime}(\Lambda)=-2\sqrt{2N}\sqrt{\log(\Lambda_{c}/\Lambda)}+\mathcal{O}(1/N^{1/2})

(S231)

Integrating over
\Lambda
one finds, at large
N

\Delta_{N}(\Lambda)=\sqrt{2N}\frac{4}{3}(\log(\Lambda_{c}/\Lambda))^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(1/N^{1/2})

(S232)

which has precisely the form

\frac{\Delta_{N}(\Lambda)}{\varepsilon}\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\rm KPZ}}}\Delta_{\rm KPZ}(z)\quad,\quad\Delta_{\rm KPZ}(z)=\frac{4}{3}(-\log(-z))^{3/2}

(S233)

which agrees with the result for the KPZ equation in Refs.
le2016exact
;
krajenbrink2021inverse
.

Now we need to consider the left hand side of (
S222
), and identify the observables in the two models.
For this we will use the result from Ref.
(
BorodinMacdo
, Section 5.4.1 Formula (5.9))
, which
we first state in the notations of that paper. There is it shown that the OY
partition sum denoted there
e^{F_{N}(T)}
converges for
N\to+\infty
to the
continuum directed polymer partition sum
Z(X,T)
(in the notations of
BorodinMacdo
) as

e^{F_{N}(\sqrt{TN}+X)}\simeq e^{N+\frac{\sqrt{NT}+X}{2}+X\sqrt{\frac{N}{T}}+\frac{N}{2}\log(TN)-N\log N}Z(X,T)

(S234)

On the other hand the partition sums defined here are related to those in that paper via

z_{N}(t)=e^{-t(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}Z_{N}(t)\quad,\quad Z_{N}(t)=\varepsilon^{1-N}e^{F_{N}(\varepsilon t)}

(S235)

We can choose
X=0
, and we obtain the convergence

z_{N}(t=1)=e^{-(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}\varepsilon^{1-N}e^{F_{N}(\varepsilon)}\simeq\varepsilon e^{N-1-N\log N}Z(0,T)=-\frac{\varepsilon}{\Lambda_{c}}Z(0,T)

(S236)

where we have identified
\varepsilon=\sqrt{TN}
, i.e. we set
T=\varepsilon^{2}/N
.
Let us now recall that in our previous work
krajenbrink2021inverse
we define the
continuum directed polymer partition
Z_{\rm KPZ}(X,T_{\rm KPZ})
as the solution of the SHE

\partial_{t}Z_{\rm KPZ}=\partial_{x}^{2}Z_{\rm KPZ}+\sqrt{2}\eta(x,t)Z_{\rm KPZ}

(S237)

where
\eta(x,t)
is the standard (unit variance) space-time white noise. Comparing with
Ref.
BorodinMacdo
we see that we need to write

Z(X,T)=Z_{\rm KPZ}(X,T_{\rm KPZ})\quad,\quad 2T_{\rm KPZ}=T

(S238)

Thus we have the large
N
behavior

z_{N}(t=1)\simeq-\frac{\varepsilon}{\Lambda_{c}}Z_{\rm KPZ}(0,T_{\rm KPZ}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2N})

(S239)

Hence the l.h.s of (
S222
) becomes

\overline{\exp\left(-\frac{\Lambda}{\varepsilon}z_{N}(t=1)\right)}\simeq\overline{\exp\left(-zZ_{\rm KPZ}(0,T_{\rm KPZ})\right)}=\overline{\exp\left(-\frac{ze^{H(T_{\rm KPZ})}}{\sqrt{T_{\rm KPZ}}}\right)}

(S240)

where we recall that
z=-\Lambda/\Lambda_{c}
and that for the droplet solution
of the KPZ solution we have defined
H(T_{\rm KPZ})=\log Z_{\rm KPZ}(0,T_{\rm KPZ})+\frac{1}{2}\log T_{\rm KPZ}
in Ref.
krajenbrink2021inverse
.

XII.2
Convergence of the dynamical system (
10
) to the nonlinear Schrodinger equation

In this Section, we investigate how to take the limit of the WNT of the O’Connell-Yor polymer (
10
) to the WNT equations of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation directly. To this aim, we define a scaling variable
x_{0}
, which can be interpreted as a lattice length, and and introduce the rescaled space and time as

X=x_{0}(t-n),\quad T=\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}t

(S241)

We then define the partition function
Z
as

z_{n}(t)=Z(x_{0}t-x_{0}n,\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}t)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad Z(X,T)=z_{\frac{2T}{x_{0}^{2}}-\frac{X}{x_{0}}}(\frac{2T}{x_{0}^{2}})

(S242)

and similarly for
\tilde{z}_{n}
which we relate to a response field
\tilde{Z}
. Definition (
S242
) implies the differential relations

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}(t)&=x_{0}\partial_{X}Z+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}\partial_{T}Z\\
z_{n+1}-z_{n}&=-x_{0}\partial_{X}Z+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}\partial_{X}^{2}Z+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}^{3})\\
z_{n-1}-z_{n}&=x_{0}\partial_{X}Z+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}\partial_{X}^{2}Z+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}^{3})\end{split}

(S243)

where we have assumed a small
x_{0}
expansion in the last two equations. In order to keep only one dominant term in the expansion, we consider the following combination

\partial_{t}z_{n}+z_{n+1}-z_{n}=\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}(\partial_{T}Z+\partial_{X}^{2}Z)+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}^{3})

(S244)

Upon convergence, we find that the original dynamical system (
10
) reads at leading order

\begin{split}x_{0}^{2}\partial_{T}Z&=x_{0}^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}Z+2Z^{2}\tilde{Z}\\
-x_{0}^{2}\partial_{T}\tilde{Z}&=x_{0}^{2}\partial_{X}^{2}\tilde{Z}+2Z\tilde{Z}^{2}\\
\end{split}

(S245)

To obtain a final system independent on
x_{0}
, we finally rescale the fields
Z
and
\tilde{Z}
as

Q_{\rm KPZ}(X,T)=\frac{1}{x_{0}}Z(X,T),\qquad P_{\rm KPZ}(X,T)=\frac{1}{x_{0}}\tilde{Z}(X,T)

(S246)

to obtain the
\{P,Q\}
system studied in the context of the WNT of the KPZ equation
krajenbrink2021inverse
.

XII.3
Convergence of the Lax pair (
13
) to the Lax pair of the nonlinear Schrodinger equation

Recalling the driftless Lax pair for the O’Connell-Yor system

\partial_{t}{v}_{n}=U_{n}{v}_{n},\quad{v}_{n+1}=L_{n}{v}_{n},\quad U_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}&-z_{n-1}\\
\tilde{z}_{n}&\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad L_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\lambda}&\frac{z_{n}}{\lambda}\\
-\frac{1}{\lambda}\tilde{z}_{n}&\lambda-\frac{1}{\lambda}z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}\,.\end{pmatrix}

(S247)

From (
S244
) and (
S243
) we observe that in order to study the convergence of the Lax pair, we need to consider the following combination

\partial_{t}v_{n}+v_{n+1}-v_{n}=(U_{n}+L_{n}-I_{2})v_{n}

(S248)

as well as the first order in
x_{0}
of the equations
\partial_{t}v_{n}=U_{n}v_{n}
and
v_{n+1}-v_{n}=(L_{n}-I_{2})v_{n}
where
I_{2}
is the identity matrix. Indeed, in the continuum we want to transform the Lax pair (
S247
)
into a continuous version

\partial_{X}V(X,T)=U_{1}V(X,T),\quad\partial_{T}V(X,T)=U_{2}V(X,T)

(S249)

Equation (
S244
) together with Eq. (
S248
) provides a convergence to the operator
(\partial_{T}+\partial_{X}^{2})V
which in terms of the continuous Lax matrices (
S249
) reads
(U_{2}+U_{1}^{2}+\partial_{X}U_{1})V
.

To study the convergence of the Lax pair, we choose the following scaling at leading order (see also (
S243
))

\begin{split}&\lambda(x_{0})=e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{\lambda}{2}x_{0}},\\
&\tilde{z}_{n}(t)=x_{0}\tilde{Z}(X,T),\quad\tilde{z}_{n-1}(t)=x_{0}\tilde{Z}(X,T)+x_{0}^{2}\partial_{X}\tilde{Z}(X,T)+\frac{x_{0}^{3}}{2}\partial^{2}_{X}\tilde{Z}(X,T)\\
&z_{n}(t)=x_{0}Z(X,T),\quad z_{n-1}(t)=x_{0}Z(X,T)+x_{0}^{2}\partial_{X}Z(X,T)+\frac{x_{0}^{3}}{2}\partial^{2}_{X}Z(X,T)\end{split}

(S250)

Under this scaling, a straightforward replacement gives

\begin{split}L_{n}&\to I_{2}-x_{0}U_{1}+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2}(U_{1}^{2}+\partial_{X}U_{1})+x_{0}^{2}(U_{2}-W)+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}^{3})\\
U_{n}&\to x_{0}U_{1}+x_{0}^{2}W+\mathcal{O}(x_{0}^{3})\end{split}

(S251)

where

U_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\mathbf{i}\lambda}{2}&-Z(X,T)\\
\tilde{Z}(X,T)&-\frac{\mathbf{i}\lambda}{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(S252)

U_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}Z(X,T)\tilde{Z}(X,T)-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}&-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{X}Z(X,T)-\frac{\mathbf{i}\lambda}{2}Z(X,T)\\
-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{X}\tilde{Z}(X,T)+\frac{\mathbf{i}\lambda}{2}\tilde{Z}(X,T)&\frac{\lambda}{4}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}Z(X,T)\tilde{Z}(X,T)\\
\end{pmatrix}

(S253)

W=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}&-\partial_{X}Z(X,T)\\
0&\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(S254)

We recognize in Eqs. (
S252
)-(
S253
) the matrices
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of the continuous nonlinear Schrodinger equation obtained in the weak noise theory of the KPZ equation
krajenbrink2021inverse
. In particular we see the convergence

U_{n}+L_{n}-I_{2}\to x_{0}^{2}(U_{2}+U_{1}^{2}+\partial_{X}U_{1})

(S255)

as expected. This concludes the convergence of the Lax pairs of the discrete problem towards the continuous problem.

XIII
Triangular representation of the solution to the Lax problem

Here we set
g=2
and the drifts
\{a_{\ell}\}=0
and we recall the asymptotics

\phi_{n}\sim\lambda^{-n}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

-1\end{pmatrix},\quad n\to-\infty

(S256)

and

\psi_{n}\sim\lambda^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\psi}_{n}\sim\lambda^{-n}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad n\to+\infty

(S257)

and

\phi_{n}\sim\begin{pmatrix}a(\lambda,t)\lambda^{-n}\\
b(\lambda,t)\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{\phi}_{n}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)\lambda^{-n}\\
-\tilde{a}(\lambda,t)\lambda^{n}\end{pmatrix},\quad n\to+\infty

(S258)

We have that
\lambda^{n}\phi_{n}
and
\lambda^{-n}\psi_{n}
are analytic inside the circle
|\lambda|<\mathtt{R}

and that
\lambda^{-n}\bar{\phi}_{n}
and
\lambda^{n}\bar{\psi}_{n}
are analytic outside of the circle
|\lambda|>\mathtt{R}
.
(for simplicity we consider the case
\Lambda<\Lambda^{*}_{N}
but the same manipulations extent to

\Lambda>\Lambda^{*}_{N}
, the contour being a deformed circle).

One introduces the triangular representations

\psi_{n}=\lambda^{n}\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{p}\begin{pmatrix}K_{1}(n,n+p)\\
K_{2}(n,n+p)\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{n+p}K(n,n+p),\quad\phi_{n}=\lambda^{-n}\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{p}\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}(n,n+p)\\
M_{2}(n,n+p)\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}<\mathtt{R}

(S259)

\bar{\psi}_{n}=\lambda^{-n}\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{-p}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{K}_{1}(n,n+p)\\
\bar{K}_{2}(n,n+p)\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{-n-p}\bar{K}(n,n+p),\quad\bar{\phi}_{n}=\lambda^{n}\sum_{p\geqslant 0}\lambda^{-p}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{M}_{1}(n,n+p)\\

As in Ref.

XIV
Gelfand-Levitan-Marchenko equations

XIV.1
Notations and intermediate objects

To obtain the explicit solutions
\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}
as a function of the scattering data, one needs to obtain the Gelfand-Levitan-Marchenko equations of the problem. To this aim, we start by rewriting the linear relation between
\phi,\bar{\phi}
and
\psi,\bar{\psi}
and we further insert the triangular representation of
\psi_{n}
and
\bar{\psi}_{n}
obtained in Eqs. (
S259
)–(
S260
).

\begin{split}\frac{1}{a(\lambda)}\phi_{k}&=\bar{\psi}_{k}+\frac{b(\lambda,t)}{a(\lambda)}\psi_{k}\\
\frac{1}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}\bar{\phi}_{k}&=-\psi_{k}+\frac{\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}\bar{\psi}_{k}\end{split}

(S284)

The properties we will require are that

•

\lambda^{n}\phi_{n}
,
\lambda^{-n}\psi_{n}
and
a(\lambda)
are analytic inside the circle
|\lambda|<\mathtt{R}
,

•

\lambda^{-n}\bar{\phi}_{n}
,
\lambda^{n}\bar{\psi}_{n}
and
\tilde{a}(\lambda)
are analytic outside of the circle
|\lambda|>\mathtt{R}
.

The analyticity properties of the various function will be used to proceed to a contour integration of Eq. (
S284
) using that

1.

For a function analytic inside the circle of radius
\mathtt{R}
, we have that

\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\frac{1}{\lambda-\xi_{1}}f(\lambda)=f(\xi_{1})\Theta(\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi_{1}\right\rvert}<\mathtt{R})

(S285)

2.

For a function analytic outside the circle of radius
\mathtt{R}
, we have that

\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\frac{1}{\lambda-\xi_{1}}f(\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}f(\lambda)-f(\xi_{1})\Theta(\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi_{1}\right\rvert}>\mathtt{R})

(S286)

where
\Theta
denotes the Heaviside function. Furthermore, we use a contour integral representation of the Kronecker delta function

\delta_{m,n}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{m-n-1}\,.

(S287)

We define the Fourier transform on the circle of the reflection coefficients. If the scattering data
\{a,\tilde{a}\}
do not have any zero, it reads

\begin{split}F(n)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{n-1}\frac{b(\lambda,t)}{a(\lambda)}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{n-1}\frac{b(\lambda)}{a(\lambda)}e^{(1-\lambda^{2})t}\\
\tilde{F}(n)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{-n-1}\frac{\tilde{b}(\lambda,t)}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{-n-1}\frac{\tilde{b}(\lambda)}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t}\end{split}

(S288)

In the case of the presence of soliton, i.e. the scattering data having zeroes as in the current problem, we first proceed to the replacement

a(\lambda)\to a(\lambda)\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}\quad,\quad\tilde{a}(\lambda)\to\tilde{a}(\lambda)\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}

(S289)

If the scattering data have simple zeroes, e.g.
a(\pm\lambda_{0})=0
and
\tilde{a}(\pm\lambda_{-1})=0
, then the expressions of
F(n)
and
\tilde{F}(n)
are modified to take into account the respective poles as

\begin{split}F(n)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{n-1}\frac{b(\lambda)}{a(\lambda)}\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}e^{(1-\lambda^{2})t}+\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{2\lambda_{0}}\left[\lambda^{n-1}\frac{b(\lambda)}{a(\lambda)}e^{(1-\lambda^{2})t}\right]_{-\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{0}}\\
\tilde{F}(n)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi}\lambda^{-n-1}\frac{\tilde{b}(\lambda)}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t}+\frac{\lambda_{-1}^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{2\lambda_{-1}}\left[\lambda^{-n-1}\frac{\tilde{b}(\lambda)}{\tilde{a}(\lambda)}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t}\right]_{-\lambda_{-1}}^{\lambda_{-1}}\end{split}

(S290)

where the notation
[f(\lambda)]_{a}^{b}
stands for
f(b)-f(a)
.

Inserting the precise value of the scattering data, i.e.

\tilde{b}(\lambda)=-\lambda^{2},\;b(\lambda)=\Lambda\lambda^{-2N-2}e^{\lambda^{2}-1},\;a(\lambda)=e^{\varphi(\lambda)},\;\tilde{a}(\lambda)=e^{-\varphi(\lambda)}

(S291)

we then obtain in the absence of soliton

\begin{split}F(n)&=\Lambda\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi\lambda}\lambda^{n-2-2N}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-t)-\varphi(\lambda)}\\
\tilde{F}(n)&=-\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi\lambda}\lambda^{-(n-2)}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t+\varphi(\lambda)}\end{split}

(S292)

and in the presence of solitons

\begin{split}F(n)&=\Lambda\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi\lambda}\lambda^{n-2-2N}\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-t)-\varphi(\lambda)}+\Lambda\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}{2\lambda_{0}}\left[\lambda^{n-3-2N}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-t)-\varphi(\lambda)}\right]_{-\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{0}}\\
\tilde{F}(n)&=-\oint_{|\lambda|=\mathtt{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda}{2\mathbf{i}\pi\lambda}\lambda^{-(n-2)}\frac{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{-1}^{2}}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t+\varphi(\lambda)}-\frac{\lambda_{-1}^{2}-\lambda_{0}^{2}}{2\lambda_{-1}}\left[\lambda^{-n+1}e^{(\lambda^{2}-1)t+\varphi(\lambda)}\right]_{-\lambda_{-1}}^{\lambda_{-1}}\end{split}

(S293)

Remark XIV.1

Note that the two Fourier transforms verify the discrete linear evolution equations

\begin{split}\partial_{t}F(2n)&=F(2n)-F(2(n+1))\\

\end{split}

XIV.5
Check of the time derivative of
z_{n}

In this Section, we verify by an algebraic method that
z_{n}
indeed verifies the system of Eqs. (
10
). To this aim, we require three prelimiary results. From Equations (
S294
), we recall the time derivatives of the Fourier operators

\begin{split}\partial_{t}F_{n}&=F_{n}-F_{n+1}\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{F}_{n}&=\tilde{F}_{n-1}-\tilde{F}_{n}\\
\end{split}

(S340)

In particular, the combination of the two for any
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
leads to

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(F_{n}\tilde{F}_{m})&=(F_{n}-F_{n+1})\tilde{F}_{m}+F_{n}(\tilde{F}_{m-1}-\tilde{F}_{m})\\
&=F_{n}\tilde{F}_{m-1}-F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{m}\\
&=F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{m}\end{split}

(S341)

which is a rank-one operator. Furthermore, from the definition of the product of the operators
F_{n}
and
\tilde{F}_{n}
from Eq. (
S315
), we obtain the following relation for the finite difference

F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}=F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{n+1}+F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}\Longrightarrow\Delta^{+}F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}=-F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}

(S342)

where
\Delta^{+}f_{n}=f_{n+1}-f_{n}
so that the finite difference is a rank-one operator. More generally this relation remains valid for different indices

F_{n}\tilde{F}_{m}=F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{m+1}+F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{m+1}\Longrightarrow\Delta^{+}F_{n}\tilde{F}_{m}=-F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{m+1}

(S343)

The following derivative of the resolvant appearing in the expression for
z_{n}
is obtained as follows

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}&=-\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\partial_{t}(F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n})\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\\
&=-\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\\
\end{split}

(S344)

Equipped with these results, we calculate the time derivative of
z_{n}

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}&=-\partial_{t}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}\\
&=-\bra{\delta}\partial_{t}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\partial_{t}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}\\
&=-z_{n}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n-1}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}+\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}\\
&=-z_{n}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n-1}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}z_{n}\\
\end{split}

(S345)

Using that
F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}=F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}-F_{n}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}
alongside the Sherman-Morrison formula on the second term of the right hand side, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}&=-z_{n}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n-1}\frac{1}{1+F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}-F_{n}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}}\ket{\delta}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}z_{n}\\
&=-z_{n}+z_{n-1}-\frac{\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n-1}\frac{1}{1+F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}}F_{n}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}}\ket{\delta}}{1-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}}F_{n}\ket{\delta}}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}z_{n}\\
\end{split}

(S346)

Using Eq. (
S330
), we recognise in the right hand side the expression of
z_{n}
and we use Eq. (
S337
) to express the remaining difference as the product
z_{n}\tilde{z}_{n}
and obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}z_{n}&=-z_{n}+z_{n-1}+\left(\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n-1}\frac{1}{1+F_{n-1}\tilde{F}_{n-1}}F_{n}\ket{\delta}-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n}\frac{1}{1+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\right)z_{n}\\
&=-z_{n}+z_{n-1}+z_{n}^{2}\tilde{z}_{n}\\
\end{split}

(S347)

XIV.6
Discrete Fredholm framework for the solution of the non-linear system

The operator manipulations done so far can be related to a more fundamental object which is the Fredholm determinant of the
F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}
defined as

D_{n}=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n})_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{*})}

(S348)

and we expect the operators to live to act on
\ell^{2}(0,\infty)
. Let us develop some algebraic framework for such object similarly to what was done in Refs.
krajenbrink2020painleve
;
bothner2022riemann
for the Fredholm determinant of products of continuous Hankel operators.

The finite difference relation (
S342
) implies a multiplicative recursion for the Fredholm determinant
D_{n}
. Using the matrix determinant lemma, we have that

\begin{split}D_{n+1}=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{n+1})&=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}-F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1})\\
&=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n})(1-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}\frac{I}{I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta})\\
&=D_{n}\left(1-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}\frac{I}{I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\right)\end{split}

(S349)

This recursion can also be written backwards

\begin{split}D_{n}=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n})&=\mathrm{Det}(I+F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{n+1}+F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1})\\

(S350)

(S351)

The multiplicative recursion between implies that a suitable object to study is actually the logarithm of the Fredholm determinant and thus we obtain that

\begin{split}\Delta^{+}\log D_{n}&=\log\left(1-\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}\frac{I}{I+F_{n}\tilde{F}_{n}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\right)\\
&=-\log\left(1+\bra{\delta}\tilde{F}_{n+1}\frac{I}{I+F_{n+1}\tilde{F}_{n+1}}F_{n+1}\ket{\delta}\right)\end{split}

(S352)

We also have from Eq. (
S326

\{z_{n},\tilde{z}_{n}\}
of our problem.

\log\frac{D_{n+1}}{D_{n}}=\Delta^{+}\log D_{n}=\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{\infty}\log(1-\tilde{z}_{\ell}z_{\ell+1})

(S353)

which implies that the logarithmic second-order finite-difference of the Fredholm determinant reads

\begin{split}(\Delta^{+})^{2}\log D_{n}&=\Delta^{+}\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{\infty}\log(1-\tilde{z}_{\ell}z_{\ell+1})\\
&=-\log(1-\tilde{z}_{n+1}z_{n+2})\\
\end{split}

(S354)

Exponentiating this identity, we obtain

\frac{D_{n+1}^{2}}{D_{n+2}D_{n}}=1-\tilde{z}_{n+1}z_{n+2}

(S355)

This identity is akin to one obtained in the continuous setting of the weak noise theory of KPZ

XV
Limit to the classical Toda system

The weak noise theory of the O’Connell-Yor polymer can be seen as a generalisation of the classical Toda system
toda1967vibration
. This can be seen from the Lax pair of the model as in Ref.
kuznetsov2000quantum
but also directly from the dynamical system (
10
). To see this, consider the following Cole-Hopf type change of variable

\begin{split}z_{n}(t)&=\alpha e^{h_{n}(t)+\alpha^{2}t}\\
\tilde{z}_{n}(t)&=e^{-h_{n}(t)-\alpha^{2}t}(\alpha+p_{n}(t))\end{split}

(S358)

Note that the Jacobian of this change of variable is a constant. Then, the dynamics verified by the two fields
\{h_{n},p_{n}\}
is

\begin{split}\partial_{t}h_{n}&=-1+e^{h_{n-1}-h_{n}}+\alpha p_{n}+a_{n}\\
\partial_{t}p_{n}&=e^{h_{n-1}-h_{n}}(\alpha+p_{n})-e^{h_{n}-h_{n+1}}(\alpha+p_{n+1})\end{split}

(S359)

Rescaling
t=\frac{\tau}{\alpha}
, and
a_{n}=\alpha\hat{a}_{n}
, the action
S_{0}
of the O’Connell-Yor polymer
(
S54
)
reads in terms of the new variables

\displaystyle S_{0}=S[h,p]=\int\mathrm{d}\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigg[\alpha\left(1-e^{h_{n-1}(\tau)-h_{n}(\tau)}-\frac{1}{2}p_{n}(\tau)^{2}+p_{n}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}h_{n}(\tau)-\hat{a}_{n}p_{n}(\tau)\right)

(S360)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\partial_{\tau}h_{n}(\tau)+\left(1-e^{h_{n-1}(\tau)-h_{n}(\tau)}\right)p_{n}(\tau)+\frac{\alpha^{3}}{2}-\alpha^{2}\hat{a}_{n}\bigg]

(S361)

Note that the terms proportional to
\alpha^{2}
and
\alpha^{3}
are respectively is a total derivative
and a constant, hence they do not contribute to the equations of motion.
Taking
\alpha\to\infty
we find the classical Toda action, and the associated equations of motion (i.e. saddle point equations)

\begin{split}\partial_{\tau}h_{n}&=p_{n}+\hat{a}_{n}\\
\partial_{\tau}p_{n}&=e^{h_{n-1}-h_{n}}-e^{h_{n}-h_{n+1}}\end{split}

(S362)

This result brings a few comments:

1.

Since we have solved the weak noise theory of the O’Connell-Yor polymer using the discrete Fredholm determinant framework in Section
XIV
, our result is also extended for the Toda lattice.

2.

Note that our Lax pair formulation has allowed us to include drifts
\hat{a}_{n}
in the Toda system. It would be interesting
to understand their role in the dynamics.

3.

Finally, the Toda system has been related historically to the QR decomposition in linear algebra, see e.g. Refs.
symes1982qr
;
deift1983ordinary
. It would be extremely interesting to relate the O’Connell-Yor dynamics (
10
) as an extension of the flow of the QR decomposition. Furthermore, as we expect the existence of an integrable time discretisation of the O’Connell-Yor dynamics and thus of the Toda model, understanding the discrete flow could also shed some new light on linear algebra algorithms. We leave these questions open for a future work. Furthermore, the interpretation of the drifts
a_{n}
in these algorithms have to be understood.

The Lax pair we have studied so far involved a two-dimensional vector with two complementary fields. In this section for notation reasons, we will denote these components as

Regular solutions for nonlinear elliptic equations, with convective terms, in Orlicz spaces
Giuseppina Barletta

Abstract
We establish some existence and regularity results to the Dirichlet problem, for a class of quasilinear elliptic equations involving a partial differential operator, depending on the gradient of the solution. Our results are formulated in the Orlicz Sobolev spaces and under general growth conditions on the convection term. The sub and supersolutions method is a key tool in the proof of the existence results.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary. We consider the following quasilinear elliptic problem involving the
A
-laplacian operator

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{A}u=f(x,u,\nabla u)&{\rm in}\,\,\,\Omega\\
u=0&{\rm on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\,,\end{cases}

where
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a convex function, vanishing at
0
,
A\in C^{2}((0,+\infty))
,
and
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function. The
A
-laplacian operator is defined by
\Delta_{A}u={\rm div}\left(A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)
. The properties of the function
A
guarantee that
\Delta_{A}u
makes sense also when
|\nabla u|=0
.

A wide class of operators can be incorporated in (
1.1
). The
p
-Laplacian and the
(p,q)
-Laplacian, for which
A(t)=t^{p}
and
A(t)=t^{p}+t^{q}
,
t\geq 0
, respectively, are the most known, but we can also consider functions like
A(t)=(\sqrt{1+t^{2}}-1)^{\gamma}
, for
t\geq 0
and
\gamma>1
or
A(t)=t^{p}\lg(1+t)
, for
t\geq 0
and
p>1
. All the
\Delta_{A}
corresponding to the functions
A
considered above appear in many physical contests, like nonlinear elasticity and plasticity theory.

The presence of the gradient in the nonlinear term, called convection term, makes variational methods not applicable. Among the techniques used to study problems with a convection term, we cite: topological degree method (
[
BF
,
R
]
), theory of pseudomonotone operators (
[
GW
]
), fixed point theorems (
[

,

]
), sub and super solution methods (
[
FMMT
,

,
G
,
NS
]
), approximation methods (
[
T
]
), or a combination of the techniques above (
[
BaTo1
,
FMP
,
MW
]
).

We deal with existence, regularity and sign of the solutions to (
1.1
). Results in this direction can be found in
[
BF
,
FMMT
,
FMP
,
MW
,
NS
,
R
,
T
,
Z
]
. In the papers above there are various growth conditions on
f
, with respect to each variable, which make it necessary to use different methods to approach the problem, depending on the behavior of the convective term.

In all the papers cited above, the abstract framework is the classical Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and the growth conditions with respect
(s,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
are of polynomial type. By contrast, we work in Orlicz spaces and take into account a class of operators which, although they depend only on the gradient, cannot be treated in the Sobolev spaces. Furthermore, this allows for
f
a wider choice than that seen above. Roughly speaking, for a problem with the
p
-Laplacian, a function
f(x,s,\xi)=-c+\frac{|s|^{p^{*}-1}}{\lg(1+|s|)}+a(|s|)|\xi|^{p}
(see Theorem
3.3
) is allowed. This does not happen if we consider standard growths.

In
[
BF
,
FMP
,
R
,
Z
]
the authors establish the existence and the regularity of positive solutions for a problem involving the
p
-Laplacian. In
[
BF
,

]

the convection term is a continuous, nonnegative function with subcritical growth with respect to
u
and growth less then
p
with respect to
\nabla u
. In
[
FMP
]
the growth of the convection term is at most
p-1
with respect to
u
and
\nabla u
, while in
[

]
the convection term is superlinear for

and its growth is at most
p
with respect to
u
and strictly less than
p
with respect to
\nabla u
.
An existence and regularity result for the

-Laplacian with a convection term that can be singular at

[

]
. The existence of a suitable pair of sub and supersolutions plays a crucial role in their proof. In general, sub and supersolution methods allow to study also the case of a singular convection term, provided the interval of sub and supersolutions does not contain the singular point.

In
[
MW
]
the authors give also sign information on the solutions. They use sub and supersolution methods, in combination with variational techniques, for an operator that can be treated as the
(p,q)
-laplacian.

Existence and regularity results, for a general operator
A(x,\nabla u)
can be found in
[
NS
,
T
]
, and in
[
FMMT
]
for

. In
[

]
the convection term is a continuous function with growth less than
p-1
with respect to
u
and
|\nabla u|
, while in
[
FMMT
,
NS
]
the growth is at most

In Theorem

Preliminaries

In this Section we give the main definitions on Young functions and define the Orlicz Sobolev spaces that we use in the sequel. For a comprehensive treatment of Young functions and Orlicz spaces we refer the reader to
[
Chl
,
KrRu
,
RR1

Definition 2.1

A function
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is called a Young function if it is convex, vanishes at
0
, and is neither identically equal to
0
, nor to infinity (in
(0,+\infty)
).

For Young functions

(2.1)

A(\lambda t)\leq\lambda A(t)\quad\hbox{for $\lambda\leq 1$ and $t\geq 0$.}

Definition 2.2

The Young conjugate of a Young function
A
is the Young function
\widetilde{A}
defined as

\widetilde{A}(s)=\sup\{st-A(t):\ t\geq 0\}\quad\hbox{for $s\geq 0$.}

Definition 2.3

A Young function
A
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition near infinity (briefly
A\in\Delta_{2}
near infinity) if it is finite valued and there exist two constants
K\geq 2
and
M\geq 0
such that

(2.2)

A(2t)\leq KA(t)\quad\hbox{for }\ t\geq M\,.

Definition 2.4

The function
A
is said to satisfy the
\nabla_{2}
-condition near infinity (briefly
A\in\nabla_{2}
near infinity) if there exist two constants
K>2
and
M\geq 0
such that

(2.3)

A(2t)\geq KA(t)\quad\hbox{for }\ t\geq M\,.

If (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) holds with
M=0
, then
A
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition (globally), or the
\nabla_{2}
-condition (globally), respectively.
Given a Young function
A\in C^{1}([0,+\infty))
, define the quantities

(2.4)

p_{A}=inf_{t>0}\frac{t\cdot A^{\prime}(t)}{A(t)},\ \hbox{and}\quad q_{A}=sup_{t>0}\frac{t\cdot A^{\prime}(t)}{A(t)}\,.

The conditions

p_{A}>1\ \hbox{and}\quad q_{A}<+\infty

are equivalent to the fact that
A\in\nabla_{2}\cap\Delta_{2}
(globally).

We give basic definitions and the main properties on the Orlicz spaces.
Let
\Omega
be a measurable set in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, with
n\geq 1
. Given a Young function
A
, the Orlicz space
L^{A}(\Omega)
is the set of all measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}A\big(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}|u|\big)\,dx\leq 1\bigg\}

is finite. The functional
\|\cdot\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
L^{A}(\Omega)
, and the latter is a Banach space (see
[
Ad
]
).

If
A
is a Young function, then a generalized Hölder inequality

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega}|uv|\,dx\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{A}}(\Omega)}

holds for every
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{\widetilde{A}}(\Omega)
.

If
A\in\Delta_{2}
globally (or
A\in\Delta_{2}
near infinity and
\Omega
has finite measure) then

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}A(k|u|)dx<+\infty\ \hbox{for all}\ u\in L^{A}(\Omega),\ \hbox{all}\ k\geq 0\,.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
with
|\Omega|<\infty
. The isotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined as

\displaystyle W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}:

the continuation of
u
by
0
outside
\Omega

\displaystyle\hbox{is weakly differentiable in ${{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}$, \, |u|,\, $|\nabla u|\in L^{A}(\Omega)$}\}.

The space
W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)}=\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}.

is a Banach space. This norm is equivalent to the standard one

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}+\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\,.

For the Young function
A
in (
1.1
), we assume:

[ A1

]
A\in C^{2}(]0,+\infty[)
(this implies
A^{\prime}\in C^{1}([0,+\infty[)
);

[ A2

] there exist two positive constants
\delta
,
g_{0}>0
such that

(2.7)

\delta\leq\frac{tA^{\prime\prime}(t)}{A^{\prime}(t)}\leq g_{0}\quad\hbox{for}\ t>0\,.

We point out that, (
2.7
) guarantees that
A^{\prime}(0)=0
and
A\in\nabla_{2}\cap\Delta_{2}
globally. In fact integrating (
2.7
),

\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}\right)^{\delta}\leq\frac{A^{\prime}(t)}{A^{\prime}(t_{0})}\leq\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}\right)^{g_{0}}\quad\hbox{for}\ t>t_{0}>0\,.

Choosing
t=2t_{0}

2^{\delta}A^{\prime}(t_{0})\leq A^{\prime}(2t_{0})\leq 2^{g_{0}}A^{\prime}(t_{0})\quad\hbox{for}\ t>t_{0}>0\,.

Thus

A(2t)=\int_{0}^{2t}A^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau=2\int_{0}^{t}A^{\prime}(2s)ds\leq 2^{g_{0}+1}\int_{0}^{t}A^{\prime}(s)ds=2^{g_{0}+1}A(t)\quad\hbox{for all}\;t>0,

and

A(2t)=\int_{0}^{2t}A^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau=2\int_{0}^{t}A^{\prime}(2s)ds\geq 2^{\delta+1}\int_{0}^{t}A^{\prime}(s)ds=2^{\delta+1}A(t)\quad\hbox{for all}\;t>0.

3
Main results

In this section first we give two existence and regularity results (Theorems
3.2
and
3.3

(x,s,\xi)\in\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

Here is the definition of weak solution to (

Definition 3.1

A function

is a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) if

\int_{\Omega}A^{\prime}(|\nabla u|)\cdot\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\cdot\nabla vdx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u,\nabla u)vdx

for all

.

For the first two Theorems, we assume

({\cal{H}}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a:[0,+\infty[\to[0,+\infty[\ \hbox{is a locally essentially bounded function};\\
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:\Omega\to[0,+\infty[\ \hbox{are two measurable functions},\ \rho_{1},\,\rho_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ \hbox{and}\\
\rho_{2}(x)>0\ \hbox{on a set of positive measure};\\

\hbox{and there exist}\ s_{0}>0,\,k_{1}\in\left]0,\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right[,\ \hbox{such that}\ g_{1}(|s|)|s|\leq A(k_{1}|s|)\ \hbox{for all}\ |s|\geq s_{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

Remark 3.4

We say that

The local condition on

Theorem 3.5

Let

Proof
. Let

and the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{A}(u)=f_{R}(x,u,\nabla u)&{\rm in}\,\,\,\Omega\\

In view of the choice of

\underline{u}
and

we deduce that

, for a.e.
x\in\Omega

, all
\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

[

]

The functions

Remark 3.6

When the solution
u
has constant sign, it should be interest to verify if it is positive (or negative) in
\Omega

[

A quite standard situation occurs when

Corollary 3.7

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.5
, assume that

satisfies

(3.4)

f(x,s,\xi)\geq-aA^{\prime}(|\xi|)-b(s)\ \hbox{for all}\;x\in\Omega,\ s>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;|\xi|\leq 1,

where

,

is a function increasing in

(for some
\overline{\delta}>0

, and

for

and some

Then any nonnegative, nontrivial solution to (

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative, nontrivial solution to (
1.1
). Theorem
3.5
ensures that

in
\Omega

[
PS
]

Conditions
(A1)^{\prime}
and

of the Theorem cited above hold, because

is strictly increasing. Conditions
(F2)
and
(B1)
are satisfied too.

It remains to verify condition
(1.1.5)
of Theorem 5.3.1 of
[
PS
]
.
Put

. Due to the monotonicity of
\frac{A(t)}{t}
, for

, it holds

B(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{A(kt)}{t}dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}\frac{A(ks)}{s}dt=A(ks)\,.

If
h\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
k<2^{h}
then, in view of (
2.2
)

A(ks)\leq K^{h}A(s)\quad\hbox{for all}\;s\geq 0\,.

Let
b_{1}=\max\{p_{A}-1,K^{h}\}
.
Then, for

, using (

) and the inequality above

\displaystyle H(s)=sA^{\prime}(s)-A(s)

\displaystyle\geq(p_{A}-1)A(s)=\frac{p_{A}-1}{b_{1}}b_{1}A(s)

\displaystyle\geq b_{1}A\left(\frac{(p_{A}-1)s}{b_{1}}\right)\geq K^{h}A\left(\frac{(p_{A}-1)s}{b_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq A\left(\frac{k(p_{A}-1)s}{b_{1}}\right)\geq B\left(\frac{(p_{A}-1)s}{b_{1}}\right)\,,

or equivalently

H\left(\frac{b_{1}s}{p_{A}-1}\right)\geq B(s)\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<s<\frac{b_{1}\overline{\delta}}{p_{A}-1}\,.

is increasing, so

\frac{b_{1}s}{p_{A}-1}\geq H^{-1}(B(s))\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<s<\frac{b_{1}\overline{\delta}}{p_{A}-1}\,.

Finally

(3.5)

\frac{1}{H^{-1}(B(s))}\geq\frac{p_{A}-1}{b_{1}s}\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<s<\frac{b_{1}\overline{\delta}}{p_{A}-1}\,.

Integrating (
3.5

of Theorem 5.3.1 of

in
\Omega

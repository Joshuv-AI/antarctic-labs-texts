Introduction

Let
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}
(
k\geq 2
) be a set of distinct integers with
c_{i}>1
(
i=1,\dots,k
).
For a given non-negative integer
\ell
, let
S_{\ell}(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
(written as
S_{\ell}
for shorthand), which is called the
\ell
-numerical semigroup, with
\gcd(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})=1
be the set of all the elements
n
whose number of solutions to
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}=n
is more than
\ell
. Then for the set of non-negative integer
\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash S_{\ell}
is the set of all the elements of
n
whose number of solutions to
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}=n
(
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) is less than or equal to
\ell
. In
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, the properties of the
\ell
-numerical semigroups and explicit forms of crucial numbers are discussed

In
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
and more references, the terminology of
p
-numerical semigroup is frequently used. However, in this paper, we mainly deal with prime numbers, so we do not use
p
or
q
, but instead use
\ell
.
.

The set
\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash S_{\ell}
is finite if and only if
\gcd(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})=1
. Then, there exists the largest element
g_{\ell}(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
, which is called the
\ell
-Frobenius number
. When
\ell=0
,
g(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})=g_{0}(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
is the original Frobenius number, which is the central topic on the classically well-known linear Diophantine problem of Frobenius.
In general, to find an explicit closed form of
g_{\ell}(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
is very difficult for
k\geq 3
, but when
k=2
, the
\ell
-Frobenius number can be given explicitly:
for any non-negative integer
\ell

g_{\ell}(c,d)=(\ell+1)cd-c-d

(1.1)

(see
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
for more general formulas and related concepts).

For integers
c,d
with
1<c<d
and
\gcd(c,d)=1
, let
\pi_{\ell,c,d}
be the number of primes in

\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(c,d):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}|n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d),~n\in S_{\ell}(c,d)\}\,,

and
\pi(x)
denotes the number of all primes up to
x
.

Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba
[
6
]
proved that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant
k_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\pi_{0,c,d}\geq k_{\varepsilon}\frac{g_{0}(c,d)}{\bigl(\log g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)^{2+\epsilon}}\,,

and conjectured the following:

\pi_{0,c,d}\sim\frac{\pi(g_{0}\bigl(c,d)\bigr)}{2}\quad(\text{as}~c\to\infty)\,.

(1.2)

Ding
[
1
]
made some progress on the above conjecture (
1.2
). More precisely, for all but at most

O\bigl(N(\log N)^{1/2}(\log\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\bigr)

pairs
c
and
d
, one has

\pi_{0,c,d}=\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)}{2}+O\left(\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)}{\bigl(\log\log(cd)\bigr)^{\varepsilon}}\right)\,.

Since

\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)}{2}+O\left(\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)}{\bigl(\log\log(cd)\bigr)^{\varepsilon}}\right)\sim\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{0}(c,d)\bigr)}{2}\quad(\text{as}~c\to\infty)

and the total number of the pairs
(c,d)
with
1<c<d
,
\gcd(c,d)=1
and
cd\ll N
is
\gg N\log N
, Ding’s result provided an almost all version of (
1.2
).

Though it seemed to be out of reach
[
6
]
, very recently Ding, Zhai and Zhao
[
2
]
completely proved (
1.2
).

The main purpose of this paper is to show a more general result of (
1.2
) as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer.
For integers
c
and
d
with
1<c<d
and
\gcd(c,d)=1
, we have

\pi_{\ell,c,d}\sim\frac{\pi\bigl(g_{\ell}(c,d)\bigr)}{2\ell+2}\quad(\text{as}~c\to\infty)\,.

Remark.

When
\ell=0
, this is reduced to the main result in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.
The result itself is still never obvious because the speed of convergence is very slow.

2.
Preliminaries

From now on,
c
and
d
will always denote two positive integers satisfying that
1<c<d
and
\gcd(c,d)=1
. Below, the first lemma is quite trivial.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
cx+dy=cx^{\prime}+dy^{\prime}
with
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

c|(y-y^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad d|(x-x^{\prime}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that the number of solutions to

n=cx+dy\quad(x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0})

is exactly
\ell
, then we have

n=(\ell-1)cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}

for some
0\leq x_{0}\leq d-1
and
0\leq y_{0}\leq c-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\ell
solutions are given as

n=cx_{1}+dy_{1}=cx_{2}+dy_{2}=\cdots=cx_{\ell}+dy_{\ell}\,.

Without loss of generality, assume that

x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{\ell}.

So,
y_{1}>y_{2}>\dots>y_{\ell}
. Now, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

x_{2}=x_{1}+d,\quad x_{3}=x_{1}+2d,\quad\dots,\quad x_{\ell}=x_{1}+(\ell-1)d

with
0\leq x_{1}\leq d-1
and
0\leq y_{\ell}\leq c-1
. Thus,

n=c\bigl(x_{1}+(\ell-1)d\bigr)+dy_{\ell}=(\ell-1)cd+cx_{1}+dy_{\ell}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\pi_{\ell,c,d}
be defined as the one in introduction, then we have

\pi_{\ell,c,d}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell cd<n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)\\
n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\in\mathcal{P}\\
0\leq x_{0}\leq d;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c\end{subarray}}1\,.

Proof.

If
n
has more than
\ell+1
solutions with the form
cx+dy
(
x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
), then by Lemma
2.2
, we have

n=(\ell+k)cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\qquad(0\leq x_{0}\leq d-1;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c-1)\,.

for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. For these
n
, we will have

n\geq(\ell+1)cd>g_{\ell}(c,d).

So, if
n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)
with more than
\ell
solutions, then
n
has exactly
\ell+1
solutions (expressions). Employing again Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that

n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\qquad(0\leq x_{0}\leq d-1;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c-1)\,,

provided that
n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)
.

Furthermore, if
n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)
with

0\leq x_{0}\leq d\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq y_{0}\leq c,

then we clearly have
x_{0}\leq d-1
and
y_{0}\leq c-1
.

Now, the lemma follows from the trivial fact that
\ell cd\not\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

As usual, the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda(n)
is defined as

\Lambda(n)=\begin{cases}\log p&\text{if $n=p^{\alpha}$ ($\alpha>0$)};\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we shall firstly provide the following weighted version whose proof will be put on the next section.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer.
For integers
c
and
d
with
1<c<d
and
\gcd(c,d)=1
, we have

\psi_{\ell,c,d}\sim\frac{g_{0}(c,d)}{2}\quad(\text{as}~c\to\infty)\,,

where

\psi_{\ell,c,d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell cd<n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)\\
n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\\
0\leq x_{0}\leq d;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)\,.

3.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Our proof follows from the argument of
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
with some adjustments. By the same shorthand in
[
2
]
, we write
g_{0}(c,d)=cd-c-d
briefly as
g
. Throughout the proof, the integer
c
is supposed to be sufficiently large.

By definitions of
\psi_{\ell,c,d}
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell cd<n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)\\
n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\\
0\leq x_{0}\leq d;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq g\\
n=cx_{0}+dy_{0}\\
0\leq x_{0}\leq d;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c\end{subarray}}\Lambda(\ell cd+n)+O_{\ell}(\log g)\,.

For any real
\alpha
, let

\displaystyle f(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq n\leq g}\Lambda(\ell cd+n)e(\alpha n)\,,

\displaystyle h(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq x\leq d\atop 0\leq y\leq c}e\bigl(\alpha(cx+dy)\bigr)\,,

where
e(t)
denotes
e^{2\pi it}
for any number
t
as usual.
Then by the orthogonal relation, we have

\psi_{\ell,c,d}=\int_{0}^{1}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha\,.

(3.1)

We are in a position to introduce the Hardy–Littlewood method to evaluate the above integral.

Let
Q<c^{1/3}
denote a parameter depending only on
c
and
d
which shall be decided later. Define the major arcs to be

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(Q)=\bigcup_{1\leq q\leq Q}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\alpha:\left|\alpha-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leq\frac{Q}{qg}\right\}.

By our assumption, we have
Q<g^{1/6}
, from which it follows trivially that the above subsets are pairwise disjoint (see e.g.
[
2
, Section 2]
).
In addition, we note that

\mathfrak{M}(Q)\subseteq\left[\frac{1}{Q}-\frac{Q}{qg},1+\frac{Q}{qg}\right]\subseteq\left[\frac{Q+1}{g},1+\frac{Q+1}{g}\right].

The minor arcs are then defined to be

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(Q)=\left[\frac{Q+1}{g},1+\frac{Q+1}{g}\right]\setminus\mathfrak{M}(Q).

From Eq. (
3.1
), it is plain that

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{Q+1}{g}}^{1+\frac{Q+1}{g}}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha+\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha.

(3.2)

3.1.
Estimates of the minor arcs

Note that

\displaystyle\left|f(\alpha)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{0\leq n\leq g}\Lambda(\ell cd+n)e(\alpha n)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq\ell cd+g}\Lambda(m)e(\alpha m)e(-\alpha\ell cd)\right|\quad(m=\ell cd+n)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq\ell cd+g}\Lambda(m)e(\alpha m)\right|.

By a remarkable theorem of Vinogradov (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
) as well as the Dirichlet approximation theorem (see e.g.
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
), we can obtain that

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)}\left|\sum_{m}\Lambda(m)e(\alpha m)\right|\ll_{\ell}\frac{g(\log g)^{4}}{Q^{1/2}}+g^{4/5}(\log g)^{4}\,

(see
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
), which means that

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)}|f(\alpha)|\ll_{\ell}\frac{g(\log g)^{4}}{Q^{1/2}}+g^{4/5}(\log g)^{4}\,.

By using the estimate in
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]

\int_{0}^{1}|h(-\alpha)|d\alpha\ll(\log g)^{2}\,,

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(Q)}|f(\alpha)|\int_{\mathfrak{m}(Q)}|h(-\alpha)|d\alpha

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\left(\frac{g(\log g)^{4}}{Q^{1/2}}+g^{4/5}(\log g)^{4}\right)\int_{0}^{1}|h(-\alpha)|d\alpha

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\frac{g(\log g)^{6}}{Q^{1/2}}+g^{4/5}(\log g)^{6}\,.

(3.3)

3.2.
Calculations of the major arc

We now calculate the integral on the major arcs.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq q\leq Q}\sum_{1\leq a\leq q\atop\gcd(a,q)=1}\int_{\frac{a}{q}-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{a}{q}+\frac{Q}{qg}}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)d\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq q\leq Q}\sum_{1\leq a\leq q\atop\gcd(a,q)=1}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}f\left(\theta+\frac{a}{q}\right)h\left(-\theta-\frac{a}{q}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{Q}{g}}^{\frac{Q}{g}}f(\theta)h(-\theta)d\theta+\mathcal{R}\,,

(3.4)

where

\mathcal{R}=\sum_{1<q\leq Q}\sum_{1\leq a\leq q\atop\gcd(a,q)=1}\int_{-\frac{Q}{qg}}^{\frac{Q}{qg}}f\left(\theta+\frac{a}{q}\right)h\left(-\theta-\frac{a}{q}\right)d\theta.

We shall later prove that
\mathcal{R}
still contributes to the ‘error term’.

For any real
\theta
, we have

\displaystyle f(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq n\leq g}\Lambda(n+\ell cd)e(\theta n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq g+\ell cd}\Lambda(m)e\bigl(\theta(m-\ell cd)\bigr)\quad(m=n+\ell cd)

\displaystyle=e(-\theta\ell cd)\widetilde{f(\theta)}\,,

where

\widetilde{f(\theta)}=\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq g+\ell cd}\Lambda(m)e(\theta m)\,.

Let
\rho(m)=\Lambda(m)-1
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{f(\theta)}-\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq g+\ell cd}e(\theta m)=\sum_{\ell cd<m\leq g+\ell cd}\rho(m)e(\theta m)+O_{\ell}\left(\log g\right)\,.

By partial summation, we have

\sum_{\ell cd<m\leq g+\ell cd}\rho(m)e(\theta m)=e\big((\ell cd+g)\theta\bigr)\sum_{m\leq\ell cd+g}\rho(m)\\
-e(\ell cd\theta)\sum_{m\leq\ell cd}\rho(m)-2\pi i\theta\int_{\ell cd}^{\ell cd+g}\Bigg(\sum_{m\leq t}\rho(m)\Bigg)e(t\theta)dt\,.

(3.5)

Using the prime number theorem, there exists some absolute constant
\kappa_{1}>0
so that

\sum_{m\leq t}\rho(m)=\psi(t)-t\ll te^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log t}}.

Inserting this into Eq. (
3.5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell cd<m\leq g+\ell cd}\rho(m)e(\theta m)

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}ge^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}+|\theta|\int_{\ell cd}^{\ell cd+g}te^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log t}}dt

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}ge^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}+|\theta|e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\int_{\ell cd}^{\ell cd+g}t~dt

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}g(1+|\theta|g)e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\,.

Thus,

\widetilde{f(\theta)}=\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq g+\ell cd}e(\theta m)+O_{\ell}\bigl(g(1+|\theta|g)e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\bigr)\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle f(\theta)

\displaystyle=e(-\theta\ell cd)\sum_{\ell cd\leq m\leq g+\ell cd}e(\theta m)+O_{\ell}\bigl(g(1+|\theta|g)e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<n\leq g}e(\theta n)+O_{\ell}\bigl(g(1+|\theta|g)e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\bigr)\,.

By the estimates of
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\theta|\leq\frac{Q}{g}}f(\theta)h(-\theta)d\theta=\frac{g}{2}+O_{\ell}\left(\frac{g}{Q}(\log g)^{2}+gQ^{2}e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}\right)\,.

(3.6)

For
Q<c^{1/3}
, by
[
2
, Lemma 4.5]
we also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\sum_{2\leq q\leq Q}\sum_{1\leq a\leq q\atop\gcd(a,q)=1}\int_{|\theta|\leq\frac{Q}{qg}}f\left(\frac{a}{q}+\theta\right)h\left(-\frac{a}{q}-\theta\right)d\theta\ll_{\ell}dQ^{3}\,.

(3.7)

Bringing together (
3.4
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we conclude that for
Q<c^{1/3}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}(Q)}f(\alpha)h(-\alpha)=\frac{g}{2}+O_{\ell}\left(\frac{g}{Q}(\log g)^{2}+gQ^{2}e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}+dQ^{3}\right).

(3.8)

3.3.
The asymptotic formula

It can be concluded from (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.8
) that for
Q<c^{1/3}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}=\frac{g}{2}+O_{\ell}\left(\frac{g}{Q}(\log g)^{2}+gQ^{2}e^{-\kappa_{1}\sqrt{\log g}}+dQ^{3}+\frac{g(\log g)^{6}}{Q^{1/2}}+g^{4/5}(\log g)^{6}\right)\,.

We now choose
Q=(\log g)^{14}
. Then we can obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}=\frac{g}{2}+O_{\ell}\left(\frac{g}{\log g}\right),

(3.9)

provided that
c\geq(\log g)^{43}
.

For
c\leq(\log g)^{43}
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell cd<n\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)\\
n=\ell cd+cx_{0}+dy_{0}\\
0\leq x_{0}\leq d;\,0\leq y_{0}\leq c\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m\leq g\\
m=cx+dy\\
x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(\ell cd+m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq y\leq c\atop\gcd(y,c)=1}\sum_{m+\ell cd\equiv dy\!\!\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ c)\atop dy\leq m+\ell cd\leq g}\Lambda(m+\ell cd)+O_{\ell}(\log g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq y\leq c\atop\gcd(y,c)=1}\sum_{n\equiv dy\!\!\!\!\!\ ({\rm{mod}}\ c)\atop dy+\ell cd\leq n\leq g+\ell cd}\Lambda(n)+O_{\ell}(\log g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq y\leq c\atop\gcd(y,c)=1}\bigl(\psi(g+\ell cd;c,dy)-\psi(dy+\ell cd;c,dy)\bigr)+O_{\ell}(\log g).

Since
c\leq(\log g)^{43}\ll(\log d)^{43}
, by the Siegel–Walfisz theorem we have

\displaystyle\psi(g+\ell cd;c,dy)-\psi(dy+\ell cd;c,dy)=\frac{g-dy}{\varphi(c)}+O_{\ell}\left(ge^{-\kappa_{2}\sqrt{\log g}}\right)\,,

where
\kappa_{2}>0
is an absolute constant.
Thus, for
c\leq(\log g)^{43}
we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell,c,d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq y\leq c\atop\gcd(y,c)=1}\frac{g-dy}{\varphi(c)}+O_{\ell}\left(g(\log g)^{43}e^{-\kappa_{2}\sqrt{\log g}}\right)

\displaystyle=g-\frac{1}{2}cd+O_{\ell}\left(g(\log g)^{43}e^{-\kappa_{2}\sqrt{\log g}}\right)

\displaystyle=g/2+O_{\ell}\left(g/c+g(\log g)^{43}e^{-\kappa_{2}\sqrt{\log g}}\right).

(3.10)

Therefore, from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we know that

\psi_{\ell,c,d}\sim g/2\quad(\text{as}~c\rightarrow\infty).

This completes the proof of proposition
2.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

From now on, the symbol

will always denote primes. For
\ell cd<p\leq g_{\ell}(c,d)

\vartheta_{\ell,a,b}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell cd<p\leq t\\

\vartheta_{\ell,a,b}(t)\leqslant\sum_{p\leqslant t}\log p\ll t,

\pi_{\ell,a,b}=\frac{\psi_{\ell,a,b}}{\log g_{\ell}(c,d)}+O_{\ell}\left(\frac{\sqrt{g}}{\log g}+\frac{g}{(\log g)^{2}}\right)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{g}{\log g_{\ell}(c,d)},

(4.4)

as

. Recall that

\pi(g_{\ell}(c,d))\sim\frac{g_{\ell}(c,d)}{\log g_{\ell}(c,d)}\sim\frac{(\ell+1)g}{\log g_{\ell}(c,d)}\quad(\text{as~}c\rightarrow\infty).

(4.5)

Now, Theorem

) and (

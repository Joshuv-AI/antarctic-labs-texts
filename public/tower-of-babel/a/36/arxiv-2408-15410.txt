Quantitative Level lowering for weight two Hilbert modular forms
Mohamed Moakher

Abstract
. We generalize a result of Ribet and Takahashi on the parametrization of elliptic curves by Shimura curves to the Hilbert modular setting. In particular, we study the behaviour of the parametrization of modular abelian varieties by Shimura curves associated to quaternion algebras D D over a totally real field F F , as we vary D D . As a consequence, we obtain that on these Shimura curves, the cohomological congruence module is equal to the ring theoretic congruence module even in cases where we do not have multiplicity one, thereby extending results of Manning and Böckle-Khare-Manning.

1.
Introduction

In
[
RT97
]
, Ribet and Takahashi established a formula comparing the degrees of modular parametrizations by Shimura curves of the isogeny class of an elliptic curve. More precisely, let
p
be a prime number,
N
be a square-free integer, and let
f
be a weight two newform of level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients. We can associate to
f
a Galois representation
\rho_{f}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
which corresponds to an isogeny class of elliptic curves
\mathcal{E}
. Consider a decomposition
N=N^{+}Q
such that
Q
has an even number of prime factors. Then there exists (up to isomorphism) a unique indefinite quaternion algebra
D_{Q}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with discriminant
Q
, which gives rise to a Shimura curve
X_{0}^{Q}(N^{+})
. The Eichler–Shimura construction gives us an elliptic curve
E_{Q,f}\in\mathcal{E}
, and a modular parametrization

X_{0}^{Q}(N^{+})\longrightarrow E_{Q,f}.

The degree of this covering map is referred to as the Shimura degree of
E_{Q,f}
by
X_{0}^{Q}(N^{+})
and is denoted by
\delta_{Q}
. Now suppose
Q=\overline{Q}q_{1}q_{2}
for distinct prime numbers
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
. Then there is an indefinite quaternion algebra
D_{\overline{Q}}
of discriminant
\overline{Q}
, and applying the same procedure to
\overline{Q}
produces another integer
\delta_{\overline{Q}}
. The result of Ribet–Takahashi can then be stated as follows.

Theorem
.

[
RT97
, Theorem 1]

Assume that
\overline{\rho}_{f}=(\rho_{f}\bmod p)^{\text{ss}}
is absolutely irreducible and that
N^{+}
is not a prime, then we have the equality

(1.1)

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\delta_{\overline{Q}})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\delta_{Q})+c_{q_{1}}+c_{q_{2}},

where
c_{q_{i}}
is the highest power of
p
such that
\rho_{f}|_{G_{q_{i}}}\bmod p^{c_{q_{i}}}
is unramified.

Subsequently in
[
Tak01
]
, Takahashi removed the condition on
N^{+}
.

The Shimura degree
\delta_{D}
is known to be closely related to the congruence number
\eta_{f}(N^{+},Q)
which measures the congruences between
f
and other forms of weight two and level
N
that are
Q
-new. For instance, the order at
p
of both these numbers agree under certain multiplicity one results (see
[
ARS12
]
).
This suggests the following conjecture
[
PW11
, Conjecture 1.4]
which can purely be stated in terms of
f
:

(
PW
)

\begin{split}\text{If }Q=\widetilde{Q}q&\text{ with }q\text{ a prime number,}\text{ and if }\overline{\rho}_{f}\text{ is absolutely irreducible,}\text{ then }\\
&\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\eta_{f}(N^{+}q,\widetilde{Q}))=c_{q}+\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\eta_{f}(N^{+},Q)).\end{split}

Pollack and Weston proved this under certain conditions (see
[
PW11
, Theorem 6.11]
), notably requiring
\overline{\rho}_{f}
to be ramified at
q
whenever
q\mid Q
and
q\equiv\pm 1\bmod p
. They coined the term
quantitative level lowering
to describe this type of result. Under favorable assumptions ensuring multiplicity one results and that certain Hecke algebras are complete intersection, Kim and Ota (
[
KO23
]
) prove this conjecture for modular forms of higher weight.

In
[
BKM24
]
, Böckle, Khare, and Manning offered a new and more solid perspective on the result of Ribet and Takahashi. They gave a new proof of one of the inequalities in (
1.1
) using only the Taylor–Wiles–Kisin patching method. They also obtained a stronger result (
[
BKM24
, Theorem 7.4]
) towards
(\textbf{PW})
in cases where the relevant Hecke algebras are not complete intersection (in fact, the failure of being complete intersection occurs whenever the restriction of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
at the decomposition group of
q
is trivial for some
q\mid Q
with
q\equiv 1\bmod p
).

In this article, we generalize the results of Ribet–Takahashi and Böckle–Khare–Manning, concerning classical modular forms, to the weight two Hilbert modular case. Now let
F
be a totally real field with
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d
, and fix an odd prime number
p
which does not ramify in
F
. Let
\mathbf{f}
be a Hilbert cuspidal newform of parallel weight
2
, level
\mathfrak{n}
prime to
p
, and nebentypus
\psi
. We fix a finite extension
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, uniformizer
\varpi
, and residue field
k
. We assume that
\mathcal{O}
is large enough so that the Galois representation associated to
\mathbf{f}
is of the form

\rho_{\mathbf{f}}\colon G_{F}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}).

Let
Q
be a finite set of places dividing

and such that
\mathbf{f}
is Steinberg at these places. Then we can associate to
Q
a quaternion algebra
D_{Q}
, and a space of automorphic forms

endowed with an action of a localized Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}^{Q}

Let

Theorem A
.

[

Assume that

\overline{\rho}:=(\rho_{\mathbf{f}}\bmod\varpi)^{\text{ss}}

(a)

The image
\overline{\rho}

\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

Moreover if

\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
, then the kernel of

does not fix

.

(b)

If

is a finite place of
F
such that
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{v}}
is ramified and
q_{v}\equiv-1\bmod p
, then either
\overline{\rho}|_{I_{v}}
is irreducible, or
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{v}}
is absolutely reducible (we exclude vexing primes, see
[
Dia97a
]
).

Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathbb{T}^{Q}
is Gorenstein,

2.

M_{Q}
is free over
\mathbb{T}^{Q}
,

3.

Q
does not contain any trivial place for
\overline{{\rho}}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that hypothesis
(a)
implies the usual Taylor–Wiles condition that the restriction
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{F(\zeta_{p})}}
is absolutely irreducible (see
[
Kis09
, (3.2.3)]
). We make this more restrictive hypothesis in order to use Ihara’s lemma for Shimura curves over
F
, as established in
[
MS21
]
.

Therefore, when
Q
contains trivial places, we distinguish between two congruence modules: the
cohomological
congruence module
\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q})
, associated to the space of automorphic forms, and the
ring-theoretic
congruence module
\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathbb{T}^{Q})
, intrinsic to the Hecke algebra. In practice, the cohomological congruence module can be related to special values of automorphic
L
-functions, while the ring-theoretic one encodes congruences between
\mathbf{f}
and other forms. Thus, it is interesting to ask whether these
two cyclic modules are isomorphic. Although one always has
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathbb{T}^{Q}))\geq\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q}))
, there is no apparent reason for equality to hold in general.

In some cases, the patching method alone suffices to establish an isomorphism between these two modules. Notably, in the minimal case, Manning showed that
\mathbb{T}^{Q}\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{T}^{Q}}(M_{Q})
(
[
Man21
, Theorem 1.2]
), which implies the desired equality. For non-optimal levels, however, this equality is more challenging to prove, and this is precisely our contribution. Our approach combines the arithmetic geometry ideas of Ribet and Takahashi with the new techniques of Böckle, Khare, and Manning to prove the following result.

Theorem B

(
Corollaries

3.26
and
5.7
)
.

Assume that the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}=(\rho_{\mathbf{f}}\bmod\varpi)^{\text{ss}}
satisfies the same assumptions
(1)
and
(2)
of
Theorem

A
.

Let
Q_{0}=\{t\}
if
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
is even, and
Q_{0}=\{t,s\}
if

is odd, where
t
and

are finite places of
F
that are not trivial for
\overline{\rho}_{\mathbf{f}}
and at which
\mathbf{f}
is Steinberg.
If
Q
is a finite set of finite places at which
\mathbf{f}
is Steinberg and

, then

\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathbb{T}^{Q})\cong\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q}),

and if

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{\overline{Q}}))=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q}))+c_{v}.

Our theorem can be seen as a direction towards the analogue of conjecture
(PW)
in the Hilbert modular setting. Even in the case

, it yields new results, notably in the case where
K_{\mathbf{f}}\neq\mathbb{Q}
. Nevertheless, we note that the assumption on the existence of the set
Q_{0}
is

to our method, and so it excludes the case where all the places in

are trivial for
\overline{\rho}
.

.

Let

Strategy of the proof

We first start by proving
Theorem

A
. The implication
3.\Rightarrow 2.
is done using the patching method by upgrading a freeness result at the minimal level. We use Ihara’s lemma proven in our context in
[
MS21
]
and
[
Tay06
]
to give a quantitative version of level raising as in
[
Dia97b
]
. We then use an improved version of Wiles’ numerical criterion given in
[
BIK22
]
to conclude. This is the purpose of
Section

3
. To our knowledge, this case of level raising in this level of generality did not appear elsewhere in the literature. For the equivalence
1.\Leftrightarrow 3.
, we introduce a measure of Gorensteinness defect similar to that of
[
WWE21
]
and
[
KW08
]
. We prove that it remains invariant when
quotienting by regular sequences and that it is multiplicative under completed tensor products. This allows us to conclude in
Corollary

3.32
.

By assumption on
Q_{0}
and by
Theorem

A
, we have that
M_{Q_{0}}
is free of rank one over
\mathbb{T}^{Q_{0}}
, which gives us that
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathbb{T}^{Q_{0}}))=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q_{0}}))
. Our idea is to propagate this equality to
Q
by generalizing the method of Ribet and Takahashi to modular abelian
varieties over totally real fields. As in their setting, we work with the Jacobian
J_{Q}
of the Shimura curve associated to an indefinite quaternion algebra
D_{Q}
. We write
T^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{m}}J_{Q}
for the localized contravariant
p
-adic Tate module of
J_{Q}
, which satisfies
T^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{m}}J_{Q}\cong M_{Q}^{2}
. We also extract from
J_{Q_{0}}
an auxiliary abelian variety with a Hecke action, that we denote by
J^{\min}
, and that satisfies
T^{\vee}_{\mathfrak{m}}J^{\min}\cong(\mathbb{T}^{Q})^{2}
.

Since we do not assume that
K_{\mathbf{f}}=\mathbb{Q}
, we can no longer work with Shimura degrees. Indeed,
\mathbf{f}
might have congruences with forms that are Galois conjugate to it, yet geometrically,

\mathbf{f}
and its Galois conjugates are indistinguishable. Therefore, we introduce in
Section

4.3
a modified version of the Shimura degree, which we call the
\lambda
-Shimura degree
\delta_{Q}
.

Similarly to what is done in
[
Kha03
]
(except that we allow
K_{\mathbf{f}}
to be ramified at
p
), we relate the
\lambda
-Shimura degree to the size of the cohomological congruence module by working with the localized character group of the toric part of
J_{Q}
at a place

, which we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{t}(J_{Q})
. A key result in this setting is
Theorem

4.4
, which is proved following an argument of
[
Hel07
]
. Conditionally on the freeness of
\mathcal{X}_{t}(J^{\min})
over
\mathbb{T}^{Q}
, this theorem allows us to show that

\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q})\cong\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathcal{X}_{t}(J_{Q})).

Moreover, by a study of the
p
-adic uniformization of abelian varieties in
Section

4
, we
also deduce in
Lemma

4.10
that

\delta_{Q}=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}\big(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(\mathcal{X}_{t}(J_{Q}))\big)=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\mathbf{f}}}(M_{Q})).

The main ingredient in the proof
is Ribet’s exact sequence realizing the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence on the
character groups of the toric parts of Jacobians, thereby allowing us to compare
\lambda
-Shimura degrees. This is explained in
Section

5.4
, where we show using a delicate level-raising argument that

with

gives us that

2.3.1.

We recall a few facts about the Artin conductor of

(see
[

Since

(resp.

\mathbf{(II)}

\mathbf{(III)}

Remark 2.4
.

If

The level-raising argument in

There exists a Hilbert cuspdial newform

Applying the level lowering results of

2.4.
Jacobian of Shimura curves

Let
Q
be a finite set of places of
F
of indefinite type, and
U\in\mathcal{J}_{Q}
. We consider the Jacobian associated to the Shimura curve

,

J_{Q}(U)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X_{Q}(U)/F),

which is an abelian variety defined over
F
. Since
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(-/F)
is both covariant and contravariant for finite flat morphisms of proper smooth curves over
F
, for each

and

, we obtain a morphism of abelian varieties

[UgU^{\prime}]\colon J_{Q}(U^{\prime})\longrightarrow J_{Q}(U).

Therefore, the algebra
\mathbb{T}^{S}
(resp.
\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{S,S^{\prime}}
) acts on
J_{Q}(U)
, and we denote by
\mathbb{T}^{Q}(U)
(resp.
\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{Q}(U)
) its image in the endomorphism algebra of
J_{Q}(U)
.

The Jacobian
J_{Q}(U)
carries a canonical principal polarization
\theta\colon\widehat{J_{Q}}(U)\xrightarrow{\sim}J_{Q}(U)
, and the corresponding Rosati involution
(-)^{\dagger}
satisfies

[UgU]^{\dagger}=[Ug^{-1}U],

for
g\in G_{Q}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{f})
.

If
U
is a congruence subgroup, then the Atkin–Lehner involution
W_{U}
on
X_{Q}(U)
induces an involution on
J_{Q}(U)
, which we denote by the same symbol. Subsequently, we can define a new principal polarization

\varphi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W_{U}\circ\theta\colon\widehat{J_{Q}}(U)\longrightarrow J_{Q}(U)

that is defined over a finite extension of

. This polarization has the advantage of being
\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{Q}(U)
-equivariant.

3.

, and Gorensteinness

The goal of this section is to prove the freeness of

3.1.
Congruence modules

We introduce congruence modules and the Wiles defect, and collect some of their properties that will be used throughout the paper.

Notation 3.1
.

Given an
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
, we write
M_{\operatorname{tors}}
for its
\varpi
-torsion submodule, and
M^{\operatorname{tf}}
for its
\varpi
-torsion free quotient. We also set

M^{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(M,\mathcal{O}).

Recall that
\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
is the category of complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
k
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
and an augmentation
\lambda\colon R\to\mathcal{O}
, we define the cotangent module of
R
with respect to
\lambda
to be
\Phi_{\lambda,R}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2})
, where
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ker(\lambda)
. We also let
\Phi_{\lambda,R}^{\operatorname{tors}}
to be its torsion part.

Given a map
\pi\colon S\to R
in
\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
, we define the relative cotangent module
\Phi_{\lambda,S/R}
to be the kernel of the surjection
\Phi_{\lambda\circ\pi,S}\twoheadrightarrow\Phi_{\lambda,R}
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
S\twoheadrightarrow R
is surjective with
S
and
R
being of relative dimension
d
and generically formally smooth at
\lambda
, then we can write
\Phi_{\lambda,R}\cong\mathcal{O}^{d}\oplus\Phi_{\lambda,R}^{\operatorname{tors}}
, and similarly for
S
(see
[
BIK22
, Lemma 2.7]
). Therefore, the cotangent module is the kernel of the surjective map

\Phi_{\lambda,S/R}=\ker\big(\Phi_{\lambda\circ\pi,S}^{\operatorname{tors}}\twoheadrightarrow\Phi_{\lambda,R}^{\operatorname{tors}}\big).

Let
R
be a reduced finite flat local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. This means that the map
R\to R\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E=R[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
is injective and
R[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
is a product of finite extensions of
E
. Let us fix an augmentation
\lambda\colon R\to\mathcal{O}
, and set

I_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}].

Then we have a decomposition
R[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]=\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]\oplus I_{\lambda}[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
. We also note that
\Phi_{\lambda,R}
is of finite length.

We adopt the definition of the congruence module over such an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
R
given in
[
IKM24
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
M
be an
\mathcal{O}
-torsion free finitely generated
R
-module. The
congruence module
of
M
with respect to
\lambda
is defined to be

\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{coker}\left(M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]\longrightarrow(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)^{\operatorname{tf}}\right).

The
Wiles defect
of
M
is the integer

\delta_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])\cdot\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda,R})-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(M)).

Example 3.5
.

If
M=R
, then
\Psi_{\lambda}(R)=\mathcal{O}/\lambda(I_{\lambda})
.

Remark 3.6
.

If
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]=1
, then the inequality
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(M))\leq\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(R))
always holds (see
[
BKM21
, Theorem 3.12]
).

The Wiles defect gives us a criterion to prove freeness of modules over Gorenstein rings as illustrated by the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7

(
[
BIK22
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Suppose that
R
is Gorenstein and that
M
is an
\mathcal{O}
-torsion free finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]=1
. If

\delta_{\lambda}(M)=\delta_{\lambda}(R),

or equivalently

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(M))=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(R)),

and if
M
has rank at most one at each generic point of
R
, then
M
is free over
R
.

Remark 3.8
.

Note that the last condition in the statement of the theorem holds for example if
M[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
is free of rank one over
R[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
.

Remark 3.9
.

Throughout this paper, we will extensively use the fact that if
R
is a finite flat local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
R^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(R,\mathcal{O})
is a free
R
-module of rank one (see
[
Eis95
, Theorem 21.15]
).

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M
be an
\mathcal{O}
-torsion free finitely generated
R
-module, then we have the following canonical isomorphisms

(M/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M)^{\operatorname{tf}}\cong M/(M[I_{\lambda}])\quad\text{ and, }\quad(M/I_{\lambda}M)^{\operatorname{tf}}\cong M/(M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]).

We also have canonical isomorphisms

(M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])^{*}\cong M^{*}/(M^{*}[I_{\lambda}])\quad\text{ and, }\quad M^{*}[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}]\cong(M/\mathfrak{p_{\lambda}}M)^{*}.

Proof.

The first two isomorphisms follow from the fact that
M[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]=\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]\oplus I_{\lambda}M[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
.
For the third isomorphism, let us consider the exact sequence

0\to(M/M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])^{*}\to M^{*}\to(M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])^{*}\to 0.

We have
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M^{*}\subseteq(M/M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])^{*}\subseteq M^{*}[I_{\lambda}]
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}M^{*}
and
M^{*}[I_{\lambda}]
have the same
\mathcal{O}
-rank, we find that
(M/M[\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}])^{*}=M^{*}[I_{\lambda}]
since both are saturated submodules of
M^{*}
.
∎

The article
[
BKM24
]
extends the notion of Wiles defect to the higher dimensional setting. More specifically, let
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
, which is Cohen–Macaulay and flat over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
d
, and let

be an augmentation such that

is formally smooth at the point corresponding to

(we retain this notation for the rest of this section). By
[
BKM24
, Proposition 3.3]
, there exists
\widetilde{R}\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
which is complete intersection, flat and equidimensional over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension

, together with a continuous surjection of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

\widetilde{R}[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
is formally smooth at
\widetilde{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lambda\circ\pi
. We set

. Given this data, the Wiles defect of

is defined as follows.

Definition 3.11
.

Consider the following quantities

•

D_{1,\lambda}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widehat{\operatorname{Der}}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}}(R,E/\mathcal{O})

BKM24

Then the
Wiles defect
of

\delta_{\lambda}(R)=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}\left(D_{1,\lambda}(R)\right)-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}\left(c_{1,\lambda}(R)\right),

and is independent of the choice of

.

We record the following important generalization of Wiles’ numerical criterion, which shows that the Wiles defect precisely measures the failure of being complete intersection.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
BKM24
, Proposition 3.28]
)
.

R
is a complete intersection if and only if
\delta_{\lambda}(R)=0
. In particular,
\delta_{\lambda}(\mathcal{O}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]])=0
for any
m\geq 1
, and augmentation
\mathcal{O}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]]\to\mathcal{O}
.

The Wiles defect enjoys two further important properties that we will use extensively later. The first is its invariance under quotienting by a regular sequence, and the second is its additive property on completed tensor products. For completeness, we record both properties below.

Proposition 3.13

(
[
BKM24
, Theorem 3.29]
)
.

Assume that
(\varpi,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{i})
is a regular sequence in
R
, with
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{i}\in\ker\lambda
. Let
R_{\theta}=R/(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{i})
equipped with the induced augmentation
\lambda_{\theta}
. Then

\delta_{\lambda}(R)=\delta_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta}).

Proposition 3.14

(
[
BKM24
, Proposition 3.30]
)
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
be Cohen-Macaulay and flat over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Let
\lambda_{i}\colon R_{i}\to\mathcal{O}
be augmentations such that
R_{i}[\tfrac{1}{\varpi}]
is formally smooth at the point corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
. Let
R=R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}
, and
\lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lambda_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\lambda_{2}\colon R\to\mathcal{O}
, then we have the equality

\delta_{\lambda}(R)=\delta_{\lambda_{1}}(R_{1})+\delta_{\lambda_{2}}(R_{2}).

For the remainder of this paper, we will not use the formula in
Definition

3.11
for the Wiles defect. We will instead introduce an alternative one, which is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.15
.

With the same notation as above, we have

\delta_{\lambda}(R)=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}\big(\mathcal{O}/\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta}[I_{\theta}])\big)-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda,\widetilde{R}_{\theta}/R_{\theta}}),

where
I_{\theta}=\ker(\widetilde{R}_{\theta}\to R_{\theta}).

Proof.

Suppose that
(\varpi,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})
is a regular sequence in
R
, with
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}\in\ker\lambda
. Then the algebra
R_{\theta}=R/(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})
is finite over
\mathcal{O}
, and we write
\lambda_{\theta}
for the induced augmentation. By
Proposition

3.13
, we have

\delta_{\lambda}(R)=\delta_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta})=\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda_{\theta},R_{\theta}})-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta})).

By
[
BKM24
, Lemma 3.7]
, the elements
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}
lift to elements
\widetilde{\theta}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\theta}_{d}
such that
(\varpi,\widetilde{\theta}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\theta}_{d})
is a regular sequence on
\widetilde{R}
. Letting
\widetilde{R}_{\theta}=\widetilde{R}/(\widetilde{\theta}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\theta}_{d})
with its augmentation
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}
, we get by
Proposition

3.12
that
\delta_{\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta})=0
, and so

\delta_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta})=\delta_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta})-\delta_{\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta})=\big(\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda_{\theta},R_{\theta}})-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda_{\theta},\widetilde{R}_{\theta}})\big)-\big(\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta}))-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta}))\big).

By
[
FKR21
, Lemma A.10]
, we have that
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\lambda_{\theta}}(R_{\theta}))-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Psi_{\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta}))=-\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\widetilde{\lambda}_{\theta}(\widetilde{R}_{\theta}[I_{\theta}]))

∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Let

be an augmentation such that the corresponding representation of

\mathcal{O}
and

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Phi_{\lambda,R^{\square}_{v}/R_{v}^{\operatorname{unr}}})=\operatorname{ord}_{\varpi}\big((1-q_{v})((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-\alpha\beta q_{v}^{-1}(1+q_{v})^{2})\big).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{O}[[a,b,c,d,u,w,t,s]]
, and let
A,B\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
be the matrices

A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha+u&t+\gamma\\
s&\beta+w\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}1+a&b\\
c&1+d\end{pmatrix},

with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{O}^{\times},\gamma\in\mathcal{O}
, and
\alpha\beta=\epsilon_{p}\cdot\psi_{0,\iota}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\square}_{v}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{6})\subset\mathcal{R}
be the ideal generated by the relations

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=\det(A)-\alpha\beta=\beta u+\alpha w+uw-st-\gamma s,

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=\det(B)-1=a+d+ad-bc,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}r_{3}&r_{4}\\
r_{5}&r_{6}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=AB-B^{q_{v}}A.

Then
R^{\square}_{v}=\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{I}^{\square}_{v}
, with universal representation is given by
\rho^{\square}_{v}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})=A\bmod\mathcal{I}^{\square}_{v}
, and
\rho^{\square}_{v}(\sigma_{v})=B\bmod\mathcal{I}^{\square}_{v}
. Moreover, we have
R^{\operatorname{unr}}_{v}=\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{unr}}_{v}
, where
\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{unr}}_{v}=(r_{1},a,b,c,d)
.

For
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
equipped with an augmentation
\lambda\colon R\to\mathcal{O}
, let
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{O}}
denote the module of continuous Kähler differentials (see
[
BKM21
, §7.1]
). By
[
BKM21
, Lemma 7.3]
, it satisfies
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}\cong\Phi_{\lambda,\mathcal{R}}
.

Consider the augmentation
\lambda
given by sending
a,b,c,d,u,w,t,s
to
0
. As in the proof of
[
BKM21
, Proposition 7.9]
, the module
\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}
is free over
\mathcal{O}
with basis
\operatorname{d}a|_{\lambda},\dots,\operatorname{d}s|_{\lambda}
. Let
\Lambda^{\square}
(resp.
\Lambda^{\operatorname{unr}}
) be the image of
\mathcal{I}^{\square}_{v}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{unr}}_{v}
) under the
\mathcal{O}
-linear derivation map
\mathcal{R}\to\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}
. Then

\Phi_{\lambda,R^{\square}_{v}/R^{\operatorname{unr}}_{v}}=\Lambda^{\operatorname{unr}}/\Lambda^{\square}.

Let
N=B-\operatorname{id}
so that
N|_{\lambda}=0
. We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}r_{1}|_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\beta\operatorname{d}u+\alpha\operatorname{d}w-\gamma\operatorname{d}s,

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}r_{2}|_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{d}a+\operatorname{d}d,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{d}r_{3}|_{\lambda}&\operatorname{d}r_{4}|_{\lambda}\\
\operatorname{d}r_{5}|_{\lambda}&\operatorname{d}r_{6}|_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{d}\left(AN-\sum_{i=1}^{q_{v}}\binom{q_{v}}{i}N^{i}A\right)|_{\lambda}=(A\operatorname{d}N-q_{v}\operatorname{d}NA)|_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha(1-q_{v})\operatorname{d}a+\gamma\operatorname{d}c&(\alpha-q_{v}\beta)\operatorname{d}b+\gamma\operatorname{d}d-q_{v}\gamma\operatorname{d}a\\
(\beta-q_{v}\alpha)\operatorname{d}c&\beta(1-q_{v})\operatorname{d}d-q_{v}\gamma\operatorname{d}c\end{pmatrix}.

One can verify that

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&1&0\\

Computing the determinant of this matrix yields

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda^{\operatorname{unr}}/\Lambda^{\square})=\operatorname{ord}_{\varpi}\big((1-q_{v})(\beta-q_{v}\alpha)(\alpha-q_{v}\beta)\big).

The result then follows from the equality

∎

Lemma 3.19

Proof.

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\

\displaystyle=\det(\rho_{v}^{\square}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v}))-q_{v}\alpha^{2}=\alpha^{2}q_{v}(u+w+uw),

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=\det(\rho_{v}^{\square}(\sigma_{v}))-1=a+d+ad-c(\gamma+b),

∎

3.3.
Global deformation ring and
R=\mathbb{T}

We now define the relevant global deformation rings and record the consequences of the Taylor–Wiles–Kisin patching method, as worked out in
[
BKM24
]
. This gives isomorphisms between these rings and the relevant Hecke algebras, and a presentation in terms of the local deformation rings.

We define the functor
\mathcal{D}\colon\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\operatorname{Sets}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{\Sigma}\colon\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\operatorname{Sets}
) of deformations (resp.
\Sigma
-framed liftings) of
\overline{\rho}
, which associates to every
A\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
the set of strict equivalence classes of liftings
\rho
(resp. tuples
(\rho,\{t_{v}\}_{v\in\Sigma})
), where

•

\rho\colon G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
is a lift of
\overline{\rho}
which is unramified outside of
\Sigma
, finite flat at every place
v\mid p
and such that
\det\rho=\epsilon_{p}\cdot\psi_{0,\iota}\circ\operatorname{Art}_{F}^{-1}
.

•

t_{v}
is an element of
\ker(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k))
.

Here, two liftings
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
(resp. two
\Sigma
-framed liftings
(\rho,\{t_{v}\}_{v\in\Sigma})
and
(\rho^{\prime},\{t_{v}^{\prime}\}_{v\in\Sigma})
) are said to be strictly equivalent if there exists an element
a\in\ker(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(k))
such that
\rho^{\prime}=a\rho a^{-1}
(resp.
\rho^{\prime}=a\rho a^{-1}
and
t^{\prime}_{v}=at_{v}
for each
v\in\Sigma
).

It is well known that the functors
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\Sigma}
are represented by an element of
\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
that we denote by
R
and
R^{\Sigma}
(see
[
Kis09
, (3.2.1)]
). Moreover, there is a non-canonical isomorphism
R^{\Sigma}\cong R[[x_{1},\dots,x_{4|\Sigma|-1}]]
, and so we can see
R
as a quotient of
R^{\Sigma}
.

Now consider the ring

\displaystyle R_{\text{loc}}^{\square}

\displaystyle=\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\text{fl}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\square}\Big).

The natural transformation
(\rho,\{t_{v}\}_{v\in\Sigma})\mapsto(t_{v}^{-1}\rho|_{G_{v}}t_{v})_{v\in\Sigma}
induces a morphism
R_{\text{loc}}^{\square}\to R^{\Sigma}
.

Let us also consider the following rings

\displaystyle R_{\text{loc}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\text{fl}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\min}}R_{v}^{\min}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}}R_{v}^{\operatorname{st}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}}R_{v}^{\operatorname{uni}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\square}\cup\Sigma_{\triangle}}R_{v}^{\square}\Big),

\displaystyle R_{\text{loc}}^{\min}

\displaystyle=\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\text{fl}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}}R^{\operatorname{st}}_{v}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}\cup\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}}R_{v}^{\min}\Big),

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{\text{loc}}^{\min}

\displaystyle=\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\text{fl}}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}}R^{\operatorname{st}}_{v}\Big)\widehat{\otimes}\Big(\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}\cup\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}}\overline{R}_{v}^{\min}\Big),

which depend on
Q
since
\Sigma_{\operatorname{st}}=Q
. These allow us to define the global deformation rings with prescribed local properties:

R^{Q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R\widehat{\otimes}_{R^{\square}_{\text{loc}}}R_{\text{loc}}\quad\quad\text{and,}\quad\quad R^{Q,\min}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R\widehat{\otimes}_{R^{\square}_{\text{loc}}}R_{\text{loc}}^{\min}.

By
[
Man21
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
BKM24
, Lemmma 6.3]
, the Galois representation in
Proposition

2.2
induces surjective maps

R^{Q}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{Q}_{\mathcal{O},\psi_{0}}(U^{\mathbf{f}})_{\mathfrak{m}}\quad\text{ and, }\quad R^{Q,\min}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{Q}_{\mathcal{O},\psi_{0}}(U^{\min})_{\mathfrak{m}},

such that for

, the induced map

3.4.
Freeness of some Hecke modules

We turn to proving the freeness of
M_{Q}(U)
over the corresponding Hecke algebra, assuming
Q
contains no trivial places. The argument follows
[
Dia97b
, §3.2]
closely, using the augmentation associated to the form
\mathbf{g}
of
Lemma

2.5
(of nebentypus
\psi_{0}
) to level raise from the minimal level.

For a place
v\in\Sigma_{\square}\cup\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}
, let
(A_{v},B_{v})
be the roots of the polynomial
X^{2}-\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(T_{v})X+\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})q_{v}
such that
B_{v}\equiv q_{v}A_{v}\bmod\varpi
(we need to make a choice if
q_{v}\equiv\pm 1\bmod p
). For
v\in\Sigma_{\triangle}
, we note that
\mathbf{g}
is Steinberg at
v
.

We define a map
\gamma_{v}\colon M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\min})\rightarrow M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\mathbf{f}})
given for
v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}
by

\gamma_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{v}\big[\Gamma_{0}(v)\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\big]-\Big[\Gamma_{0}(v)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{v}\end{pmatrix}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Big],

for
v\in\Sigma_{\triangle}
by

\gamma_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})q_{v}\big[\Gamma_{0}(v^{2})\Gamma_{0}(v)\big]-\Big[\Gamma_{0}(v^{2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{v}\end{pmatrix}\Gamma_{0}(v)\Big]\mathbf{U}_{v},

and for
v\in\Sigma_{\square}
by

\gamma_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})q_{v}\big[\Gamma_{0}(v^{2})\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\big]-\Big[\Gamma_{0}(v^{2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{v}\end{pmatrix}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Big]T_{v}+\Big[\Gamma_{0}(v^{2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{v}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Big].

The level-raising map is then the product

\gamma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}\cup\Sigma_{\square}\cup\Sigma_{\triangle}}\gamma_{v}\colon M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\text{min}})\rightarrow M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\mathbf{f}}).

There is a unique oldform
\mathbf{g^{\prime}}
of level
\mathfrak{n}
and nebentypus
\psi_{0}
characterized by the fact that
T_{v}\mathbf{g^{\prime}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(T_{v})\mathbf{g^{\prime}}
for
v\nmid p\mathfrak{n}
,
\mathbf{U}_{v}\mathbf{g^{\prime}}=0
for
v\in\Sigma_{\triangle}
,
\mathbf{U}_{v}\mathbf{g^{\prime}}=0
for
v\in\Sigma_{\square}
, and
\mathbf{U}_{v}\mathbf{g^{\prime}}=A_{v}\mathbf{g^{\prime}}
for
v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}
.

The Hecke-equivariant perfect pairing of
Section

2.1
descends to the
\psi_{0,\iota}
-part, giving a perfect pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{U}\colon M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U)\times M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U)\to\mathcal{O},\quad U^{\min}\subseteq U\subseteq U^{\mathbf{f}}

(see
[
Man21
, Lemma 4.11]
). Similarly to
[
DFG04
, Proposition 1.4]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.23
.

Write
M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\min})[\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}]=\mathcal{O}\cdot x
. Then

M^{\psi_{0}}_{Q}(U^{\mathbf{f}})[\lambda_{\mathbf{g^{\prime}}}]=\mathcal{O}\cdot\gamma(x).

Moreover, let
\gamma^{t}
be the adjoint of
\gamma
with respect to the Hecke-equivariant pairing on both modules, then

\displaystyle\gamma^{t}\circ\gamma(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\square}}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})q_{v}(1-q_{v})(\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(T_{v})^{2}-(1+q_{v})^{2}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v}))\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}}(A_{v}-B_{v})(A_{v}^{2}-\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v}))

\displaystyle\times\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\triangle}}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})^{2}q_{v}(1-q_{v}^{2})\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{U}_{v})\cdot x.

Proof.

For the first assertion, we need to show that
\gamma
is injective with
\mathcal{O}
-torsion free cokernel. We therefore verify that
\gamma_{v}\bmod\varpi
is injective for all
v
. For
v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}}
, this follows from the injectivity of

\displaystyle(M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U^{\min})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k)^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U^{\min}_{0}(v))\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k

\displaystyle(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\big[\Gamma_{0}(v)\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\big]f_{1}+\Big[\Gamma_{0}(v)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{v}\end{pmatrix}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Big]f_{2},

which is (essentially) the statement of Ihara’s lemma, proved in the definite case in
[
Tay06
, Proof of Lemma 3.1]
and in the indefinite case in
[
MS21
]
.

For
v\in\Sigma_{\triangle}
, it is shown in the proof of
[
Tay06
, Lemma 3.1]
that the kernel of the map
(M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U^{\min})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k)^{2}\to M_{Q}^{\psi_{0}}(U^{\min}_{0}(v^{2}))\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}k
, defined by the same formula as above, consists of forms unramified at

. Since
\overline{\rho}
is ramified at

by assumption, this kernel is necessarily trivial. Finally, the case
v\in\Sigma_{\square}
is handled in
[
Tay06
, Lemma 3.1]
.

For the second assertion, we note that for a congruence subgroup
U
, and
diagonal

g\in G_{Q}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{a,\infty})
, we have that

\langle[U_{0}(v)gU]-,-\rangle_{U_{0}(v)}=\langle-,[Ug\begin{pmatrix}\varpi_{v}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}U_{0}(v)]-\rangle_{U}.

Therefore, we get that for
v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{uni}},

\gamma_{v}^{t}=A_{v}\left[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\begin{pmatrix}\varpi_{v}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Gamma_{0}(v)\right]-\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})\left[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Gamma_{0}(v)\right],

and so,

\displaystyle\gamma_{v}^{t}\circ\gamma_{v}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(A_{v}^{2}\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(T_{v})-2A_{v}\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})(q_{v}+1)+\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})\lambda_{\mathbf{g}}(T_{v})\big)(x)

\gamma^{t}_{v}\circ\gamma_{v}(x)=\psi_{0,\iota}(\varpi_{v})^{2}q_{v}(1-q_{v}^{2})\mathbf{U}_{v}(x).

Finally, the case of

follows from the same calculations as in
[
Tay06

3.5.
Non-Gorensteinness of the Hecke algebra

In this section, we propose a definition of a Gorensteiness defect similar to the one given in
[
WWE21
]
and
[
KW08
]
. We show that it is invariant under quotienting by a regular sequence, and is multiplicative on completed tensor products. These properties are analogous to those in
Proposition

3.13
and
Proposition

3.14
of the Wiles defect. Consequently, we deduce that the Hecke algebra
\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{Q}_{\mathcal{O}}(U^{\mathbf{f}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is

Gorenstein if

contains trivial places. An immediate consequence is that the module

is not free over
\overline{\mathbb{T}}{}^{Q}_{\mathcal{O}}(U^{\mathbf{f}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
, given that it is self-dual.

Notation 3.27
.

Let

, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. We denote by

the minimal number of generators of

.

Definition 3.28
.

Let

which is Cohen–Macaulay and flat over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
d
. Let
\omega_{R}
be the dualizing module of

. We define the type of

to be the integer

Remark 3.29
.

We found this terminology in
[
BH98
, 3.6.13]
.

Note that

if and only if

is Gorenstein, and thus

is a measure of the defect of Gorensteinness. Moreover, the type is invariant under quotienting by regular sequences (see
[
Eis95
, §21.3]
). From this, we get the following lemma.

.

Let
(\varpi,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})
be a regular sequence in

, and let
\overline{R}_{\theta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R/(\varpi,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})

Proof.

By
[
Eis95
, Theorem 21.15]
,
\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\overline{R}_{\theta},k)
is the dualizing module of
\overline{R}_{\theta}
.
∎

Lemma 3.31
.

Let

be Cohen-Macaulay and flat

-algebras of relative dimension

. Then we have

Proof.

First, let us note that if

is a regular sequence in

[

, Lemma 4.4]

is Cohen–Macaulay of relative dimension

over

, and its quotient by this sequence is a finite

. By

Corollary 3.32
.

The set

contains a trivial place if and only if the algebra

Proof.

Suppose

,

4.
Recollection of facts on abelian varieties

In this section, we develop the algebra necessary for proving our main theorem in
Section

5
. We start by setting the notation and recalling some useful facts on abelian varieties with semistable reduction. Specifically, we introduce the monodromy pairing on the character group of the toric part of the abelian variety, which plays an essential role in the proof. Moreover, we recall a few results on the
p
-adic uniformization of abelian varieties.

In the second part of this section, following the approach of
[
Hel07
]
, we study the abstract situation of a faithful action of a finite flat
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
\mathbb{T}
acting on an abelian variety. The central result we prove here is

Let

is a smooth commutative algebraic group over

. For an integer

, we write

for the algebraic subgroup of

T_{p}H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}H[p^{n}](\overline{F})
, and the

-adic

T^{\vee}_{p}H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}(H[p^{\infty}](\overline{F}),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)).

Both
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules equipped with a

-action, and they are related by a duality pairing

which is perfect if

is an abelian variety.

4.1.
Abelian varieties with semistable reduction

be a finite extension with ring of integers

Let

Let

\widetilde{\theta}_{1}=t-z

\widetilde{\theta}_{2}=b-z

. Given

\mathrm{in}(f)
to be its greatest term with respect to
\prec

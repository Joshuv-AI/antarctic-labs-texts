Nonconvex Matrix Factorization is Geodesically Convex: Global Landscape Analysis for Fixed-rank Matrix Optimization From a Riemannian Perspective
Yuetian Luo, Nicolás García Trillos

Abstract
In this paper we study the landscape of a general matrix optimization problem with a fixed-rank positive semidefinite (PSD) constraint. We perform the Burer-Monteiro factorization, i.e., factorize a PSD matrix 𝐗 {\mathbf{X}} as 𝐘𝐘 ⊤ {\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top} , and consider a particular Riemannian quotient geometry in a search space that has a total space equipped with the Euclidean metric. When the original objective f f satisfies standard restricted strong convexity and smoothness properties, we characterize the global landscape of the factorized objective under the Riemannian quotient geometry. In particular, we show that the entire search space can be divided into three regions: ( ℛ 1 {\cal R}_{1} ) the region near the target parameter of interest, where the factorized objective is geodesically strongly convex and smooth; ( ℛ 2 {\cal R}_{2} ) the region containing neighborhoods of all strict saddle points; ( ℛ 3 {\cal R}_{3} ) the remaining regions, where the factorized objective has a large gradient. Our results cover both noisy and noiseless settings in applications of interest. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first global landscape analysis of the Burer-Monteiro factorized objective under the Riemannian quotient geometry. Our results provide a fully geometric explanation for the superior performance of vanilla gradient descent under the Burer-Monteiro factorization. When f f satisfies a weaker restricted strict convexity property, we show there exists a neighborhood near local minimizers such that the factorized objective is geodesically convex. To prove our main results we provide a comprehensive landscape analysis of a matrix factorization problem with a least squares objective, which serves as a critical bridge in establishing the results in the general setting. Our conclusions are also based on a result of independent interest stating that the geodesic ball centered at 𝐘 {\mathbf{Y}} with a radius one-third of the least singular value of 𝐘 {\mathbf{Y}} is a geodesically convex set under the Riemannian quotient geometry, a result that, as a corollary, also implies a quantitative bound of the convexity radius in the Bures-Wasserstein space. The convexity radius obtained in this paper is sharp up to constants.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following optimization problem

\min_{{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{S}^{p\times p}\succcurlyeq 0,{\rm rank}({\mathbf{X}})=r}f({\mathbf{X}}),\quad 0<r\leq p.

These works take a big step in demystifying the observed performance of the Burer-Monteiro factorization, but they are also restrictive in the sense that they mainly focus on the landscape analysis of the objective near the global minimum or at stationary points. There have been a few attempts to study the global geometry of related optimization problems by characterizing their landscapes in the whole search space, rather than solely in regions near the global minimizer or near stationary points. For example,

]
. Based on the existing results in the literature, we ask the central question that we explore in this paper:

Can we provide a more intuitive and geometric explanation for why the Burer-Monteiro factorization works?

In addition, except for the landscape analysis for the special noisy matrix trace regression with a least squares objective in
[
LLA+19
, Section IV.B]
, the analyses in
[
LLA+19
,
ZLTW21
]

[

Can we analyze the global optimization landscape of (

Finally, the previous results for landscape analysis in the literature are restricted to the setting where
f
is well-conditioned, and thus we wonder:

Can we study the landscape of (
2
) under a weaker assumption on

?

In this work, we provide affirmative answers to the above three questions. First, we note that, under the Euclidean formulation, if
{\mathbf{Y}}
is a stationary point/local minimizer of (
2
), then
{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{O}}
is also a stationary point/local minimizer for any
{\mathbf{O}}\in\mathbb{O}_{r}
. This ambiguity makes

unavoidably nonconvex in any neighborhood of a stationary point

,
ZLTW21
]
for providing a more intuitive landscape analysis. To tackle this difficulty, we resort to tools from Riemannian optimization
[

,
Bou20
]
and consider a particular Riemannian quotient geometry on (
2
)
[
JBAS10
]
; see Section
1.4
for a brief introduction of Riemannian optimization on quotient manifolds. Specifically, we encode the invariance mapping, i.e.,
{\mathbf{Y}}\mapsto{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{O}}
, in an abstract search space by defining the equivalence classes
[{\mathbf{Y}}]=\{{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{O}}:{\mathbf{O}}\in\mathbb{O}_{r}\}
. Since the invariance mapping is performed via the Lie group
\mathbb{O}_{r}
smoothly, freely and properly, we have
{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}:=\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}/\mathbb{O}_{r}
is a quotient manifold of
\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}:=\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}_{*}

[
Lee13
, Theorem 21.10]
. Moreover, we equip
T_{\mathbf{Y}}\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}
with the metric
\bar{g}_{\mathbf{Y}}(\eta_{\mathbf{Y}},\theta_{\mathbf{Y}})={\rm tr}(\eta_{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}\theta_{\mathbf{Y}})
for any
\eta_{\mathbf{Y}},\theta_{\mathbf{Y}}\in T_{\mathbf{Y}}\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}
, where
T_{\mathbf{Y}}\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}=\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}
is the tangent space of
\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}
at
{\mathbf{Y}}
. Since
\bar{h}({\mathbf{Y}})
is invariant along the equivalence classes of
\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}
, (
2
) induces the following optimization problem on the quotient manifold
{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}
:

\min_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]\in{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}}h([{\mathbf{Y}}]):=\bar{h}({\mathbf{Y}})

(3)

Our main contribution is on providing a purely geometric and intuitive explanation of the success of vanilla GD in the Burer-Monteiro factorization via performing landscape analysis of (
3
) under the Riemannian quotient geometry. Our results cover various scenarios of
f
and allow noise as well. An informal statement of our main results (Theorems
2
-
6
and Corollary
1
) is provided in the following Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1

(Informal Results)
.

(a)

When
f
satisfies restricted strong convexity and smoothness properties (see Definition
1
), and the noise level at the rank
r
matrix parameter of interest
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
is controlled, then the global landscape of (
3
) is benign in the following sense: given any
{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}_{*}
, one of the following three properties holds:

(i) the magnitude of Riemannian gradient of
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
is large;

(ii) the Riemannian Hessian of
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
has a large negative eigenvalue and there is an explicit escaping direction;

(iii) when
{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}
is close to
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
,
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
is geodesically strongly convex and smooth. Moreover, the distance between the global minimizer and
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
is bounded by a quantity that depends on the noise level of the problem or equivalently on the magnitude of
\nabla f({\mathbf{X}}^{*})
.

(b)

When
f
satisfies a weaker restricted strict convexity property (see Definition
2
), there exists a neighborhood around a local minimizer such that
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
is geodesically convex.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first landscape analysis for the Burer-Monteiro factorization under the Riemannian quotient geometry. Thanks to this Riemannian formulation, in either setting of
f
we have that there exists a local region in which the factorized objective is either geodesically strongly convex or geodesically convex. Such results are novel and can not be obtained under the frameworks of
[
LLA+19
,
ZLTW21
]
as
\bar{h}({\mathbf{Y}})
is in essence nonconvex under the Euclidean geometry. Moreover, we also provide the global landscape analysis of (
3
) when
f
is well-conditioned. The set of three properties described in Theorem
1
(a) is known as the robust strict saddle property in the literature
[
GHJY15
,
JGN+17
,
SQW15
]
and many algorithms are guaranteed to achieve fast global convergence when these properties hold
[
SFF19
,
CB19
,
GHJY15
,
JGN+17
,
SQW18
]
. Finally, since the horizontal lift of the Riemannian gradient of
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
is the same as the Euclidean gradient (see the forthcoming Lemma
3
) of
\bar{h}({\mathbf{Y}})
, the gradient descent algorithms under the Riemannian quotient geometry and the Euclidean geometry are exactly the same from a computational point of view. Thus, our geometric landscape results give a fully geometric and intuitive explanation for the superior performance of vanilla gradient descent under the Burer-Monteiro factorization.

To prove our main results, we first show a novel geodesic convexity property of the Riemannian quotient manifold
{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}
which is also of independent interest. Specifically, we show the geodesic convexity radius of
{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}
at
[{\mathbf{Y}}]\in{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}
is of order at least
\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}})/3
; this radius is sharp up to the constant
1/3
. In addition, we base our global landscape analysis of (
3
) when
f
is well-conditioned on the global landscape analysis of the following optimization problem:

\min_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]\in{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}}H([{\mathbf{Y}}]):=\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}-{\mathbf{X}}^{*}\|_{\rm F}^{2},

(4)

where
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
is the rank
r

4

1
(a). Moreover, the optimization landscape of (

satisfies the restricted strong convexity and smoothness properties. When

satisfies a weaker restricted strict convexity property, we show that the Riemannian Hessian of

is positive definite at a local minimizer, and there exists a neighborhood around the local minimizer such that
h([{\mathbf{Y}}])
is geodesically convex.

1.4
Riemannian Optimization Under Quotient Geometries

In this section, we first give a brief introduction to Riemannian optimization and then discuss how to perform Riemannian optimization under quotient geometries.

Riemannian optimization concerns optimizing a real-valued function
f
defined on a Riemannian manifold
{\cal M}
. The calculations of Riemannian gradients and Riemannian Hessians are key ingredients to perform continuous optimization over the Riemannian manifold. Suppose
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\cal M}
,
g_{{\mathbf{X}}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Riemannian metric, and
T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M}
is the tangent space of
{\cal M}
at
{\mathbf{X}}
. Then the
Riemannian gradient
of a smooth function
f:{\cal M}\to\mathbb{R}
at
{\mathbf{X}}
is defined as the unique tangent vector,
{\rm grad}\,f({\mathbf{X}})\in T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M}
, such that
g_{\mathbf{X}}({\rm grad}\,f({\mathbf{X}}),\xi_{\mathbf{X}})={\rm D}\,f({\mathbf{X}})[\xi_{\mathbf{X}}],\forall\,\xi_{\mathbf{X}}\in T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M}
, where
{\rm D}f({\mathbf{X}})[\xi_{\mathbf{X}}]
is the differential of
f
at point
{\mathbf{X}}
along the direction
\xi_{\mathbf{X}}
. The
Riemannian Hessian
of
f
at
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\cal M}
is a linear mapping
{\rm Hess}\,f({\mathbf{X}}):T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M}\to T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M}
defined as

{\rm Hess}\,f({\mathbf{X}})[\xi_{\mathbf{X}}]=\widebar{\nabla}_{\xi_{\mathbf{X}}}{\rm grad}\,f\in T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M},\,\quad\forall\xi_{\mathbf{X}}\in T_{\mathbf{X}}{\cal M},

(5)

where
\widebar{\nabla}
is the
Riemannian connection
on
{\cal M}
, which is a generalization of the directional derivative along a vector field to Riemannian manifolds
[
AMS09
, Section 5.3]
. We say
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\cal M}
is a
Riemannian first-order stationary point (FOSP)
of
f
if
{\rm grad}f({\mathbf{X}})={\mathbf{0}}
and call a Riemannian FOSP a
strict saddle
if the Riemannian Hessian evaluated at this point has a strict negative eigenvalue. Given a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
{\cal M}
, we call
f:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}

geodesically convex
if
\mathcal{S}
is geodesically convex (i.e. any two points in
\mathcal{S}
can be connected by a geodesic that is completely contained in
\mathcal{S}
) and
{\rm Hess}f({\mathbf{X}})\succcurlyeq 0
for all
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathcal{S}
; we call
f:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}

\mu
-geodesically strongly convex
if
\mathcal{S}
is geodesically convex and
{\rm Hess}f({\mathbf{X}})\succcurlyeq\mu{\rm Id}
for all
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Next, we provide more details on how to perform Riemannian optimization on quotient manifolds. Quotient manifolds are often defined via an equivalence relation “
\sim
” that satisfies symmetric, reflexive and transitive properties
[
AMS09
, Section 3.4.1]
. To be specific, suppose
\widebar{{\cal M}}
is an embedded submanifold equipped with an equivalence relation
\sim
. The
equivalence class
(or
fiber
) of
\widebar{{\cal M}}
at a given point
{\mathbf{X}}
is defined by the set
[{\mathbf{X}}]=\{{\mathbf{X}}_{1}\in\widebar{{\cal M}}:{\mathbf{X}}_{1}\sim{\mathbf{X}}\}
. The set
{\cal M}:=\widebar{{\cal M}}/\sim=\{[{\mathbf{X}}]:{\mathbf{X}}\in\widebar{{\cal M}}\}
is called a
quotient
of
\widebar{{\cal M}}
by
\sim
. The mapping
\pi:\widebar{{\cal M}}\to\widebar{{\cal M}}/\sim
,
{\mathbf{X}}\mapsto[{\mathbf{X}}]
is called the
quotient map
or
canonical projection
and the set
\widebar{{\cal M}}
is called the
total space
of the quotient
\widebar{{\cal M}}/\sim
. If
{\cal M}
further admits a smooth manifold structure and
\pi
is a smooth submersion, then we call
{\cal M}
a
quotient manifold
of
\widebar{{\cal M}}
.

Due to the abstractness of equivalence classes, the tangent space
T_{[{\mathbf{X}}]}{\cal M}
of
{\cal M}
at
[{\mathbf{X}}]
calls for a representation in the tangent space
T_{{\mathbf{X}}}\widebar{{\cal M}}
of the total space
\widebar{{\cal M}}
. By the equivalence relation
\sim
, the representation of elements in
T_{[{\mathbf{X}}]}{\cal M}
should be restricted to the directions in
T_{\mathbf{X}}\widebar{{\cal M}}
without inducing displacement along the equivalence class
[{\mathbf{X}}]
. This can be achieved by decomposing

Denote
T_{1}S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
as the unit tangent bundle restricted to geodesic sphere
S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
, that is:

T_{1}S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])=\left\{([{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}):[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]\in S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}]),\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}\in T_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}{\cal M}_{r+}^{q},g_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}(\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]},\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})=1\right\}.

Let
\gamma:I\times T_{1}S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])\to{\cal M}_{r+}^{q}
,
I=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
for some small enough
\epsilon>0
, be a differentiable map such that
t\mapsto\gamma(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})
is the geodesic that at the instant
t=0
passes through
[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]
with velocity
\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}
, and
\|\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}}\|_{\rm F}=1
. Since
[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]\in S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
and
\rho<\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}})
, by Lemma
9
,
{\mathbf{Y}}^{{}^{\prime}\top}{\mathbf{Y}}
is nonsingular, so we can define
u(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})={\rm Log}_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}(\gamma(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}))
. In addition, let
F:I\times T_{1}S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])\to\mathbb{R}
be

\begin{split}F(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})&=g_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}(u(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}),u(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}))\\
&=\bar{g}_{\mathbf{Y}}\left(\overline{{\rm Log}_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}(\gamma(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}))},\overline{{\rm Log}_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}(\gamma(t,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}))}\right)\\
&\overset{\text{Lemma }\ref{lm: logarithm-map}}{=}\bar{g}_{\mathbf{Y}}\left(({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}+t\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}}){\mathbf{Q}}_{t}-{\mathbf{Y}},({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}+t\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}}){\mathbf{Q}}_{t}-{\mathbf{Y}}\right)\\
&=\|({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}+t\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}}){\mathbf{Q}}_{t}-{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\rm F}^{2}\\
&=t^{2}+2t\langle{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime},\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}}\rangle-2{\rm tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{t})+\|{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\|_{\rm F}^{2}+\|{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\rm F}^{2},\end{split}

(26)

where
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}+t\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}})
has SVD
{\mathbf{Q}}_{Ut}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{t}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}^{\top}
and
{\mathbf{Q}}_{t}={\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Ut}^{\top}
. Geometrically,
F
measures the square distance from
[{\mathbf{Y}}]
to points along the geodesic
\gamma
. We now discuss how the function
F
behaves around
t=0
.

We have both

and

are differentiable and
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=2g_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}\left(\partial u/\partial t,u\right)
. If a geodesic
\gamma

S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
at the point
[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]=\gamma(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})

[

, Chapter 3, Lemma 3.5]

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})=2g_{[{\mathbf{Y}}]}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}),u(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})\right)=0.

This means
(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})
is a stationary point of

at
t=0
for fixed
[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]
on
S_{r}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
and fixed
\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]}
with unit norm. If we can show that we have
\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial t^{2}}(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})>0
, then we would be able to conclude that

is a local minimizer of

. Given the geometric interpretation of the function

, this would further imply that there exists a neighborhood of
[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]
such that the geodesic
\gamma
stays out of the geodesic ball
B_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
. We thus focus on proving the positivity of the second derivative.

Let
{\mathbf{Q}}_{Ut}=[{\mathbf{q}}^{t}_{u1},\ldots,{\mathbf{q}}^{t}_{ur}]
,
{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}=[{\mathbf{q}}^{t}_{v1},\ldots,{\mathbf{q}}^{t}_{vr}]
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{t}={\rm diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
. From (
26

([{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})\in T_{1}S_{\rho}([{\mathbf{Y}}])
we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial t^{2}}(0,[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],\xi_{[{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}]})&=2-2\frac{d^{2}{\rm tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{t})}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\\
&=2-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{i}(t)}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\\
&\overset{\text{Lemma }\ref{lm: variation-formula-for-singular-values}}{=}2-2\left\langle{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}},\frac{d(\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbf{q}}^{t}_{ui}{\mathbf{q}}^{t\top}_{vi})}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\right\rangle\\
&=2-2\left\langle{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}},\frac{d({\mathbf{Q}}_{U_{t}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}^{\top})}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(27)

Since
{\mathbf{Q}}_{Ut}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}^{\top}
forms a smooth path in the space of
\mathbb{O}_{r}
, we must have that
\frac{d({\mathbf{Q}}_{U_{t}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}^{\top})}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\in T_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{U0}{\mathbf{Q}}_{V0}^{\top}}\mathbb{O}_{r}

for some skew-symmetric matrix
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=-\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\top}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\left|\left\langle{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}},\frac{d({\mathbf{Q}}_{U_{t}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{Vt}^{\top})}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\right\rangle\right|&=\left|\langle{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}\xi_{{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}},{\mathbf{Q}}_{U0}{\mathbf{Q}}_{V0}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\rangle\right|\\

\end{split}

(28)

here (a) is because

, and by Lemma

is a symmetric matrix, denoted by
{\mathbf{S}}

\boldsymbol{\Omega}=-\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\top}

{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}

Combining (

8
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we have studied the optimization landscape of the Burer-Monteiro factorized objective for a general fixed-rank PSD matrix optimization problem under the Riemannian quotient geometry. When
f
satisfies the restricted strong convexity and smoothness properties, we show the landscape of the factorized objective is benign by characterizing its geometry in the entire domain. When
f
satisfies a weaker restricted strict convexity property, we show there exists a neighborhood near the local minimizer such that the factorized objective is geodesically convex.

There are many interesting extensions to the results in this paper to be explored in the future. First, the current requirement on
\delta
to guarantee the benign global landscape of (
3
) in Corollary
1
may not be sharp and it would be interesting to explore whether we can establish similar landscape results with a sharper dependence on
\delta
. Second, it is well known that geometry plays a central role in Riemannian optimization. Picking a proper metric that maximizes the geodesic convexity radius of the objective is favorable. As we have mentioned in Remark
4
, it would be interesting to explore under what geometries the geodesic strong convexity radii of (
3
) and (
4
) are maximized. Third, the landscape of the Burer-Monteiro factorized objective can be more complicated when
f
does not satisfy RSC and RSM, see
[
YZLS22
]
. An interesting future research direction is to explore under what assumptions on
f
will the Burer-Monteiro factorization continue to work for efficient optimization. Finally, in this work we have mainly focused on the exact-parameterization setting, i.e., the number of columns of
{\mathbf{Y}}
is equal to the rank of the parameter of interest
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
. It would be very interesting to try to characterize the landscape of (
3
) in the over-parameterized setting, i.e., when the number of columns of
{\mathbf{Y}}
is greater than the rank of
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
. In that case, the optimality conditions for
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
need to be carefully developed as
{\mathbf{X}}^{*}
is merely a boundary point of the working manifold.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{A}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}\times p_{2}}
depends smoothly on a time variable
t
, so that the singular value
\sigma_{i}(t)=\sigma_{i}({\mathbf{A}}(t))
and left (right) singular vectors
{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t)={\mathbf{u}}_{i}({\mathbf{A}}(t))
(
{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)={\mathbf{v}}_{i}({\mathbf{A}}(t))
) also depend smoothly on
t
. Then for every
i=1,\ldots,p_{1}\wedge p_{2}
:

\begin{split}\dot{\sigma}_{i}(t)=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle\quad\text{ and }\quad\ddot{\sigma}_{i}(t)=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\ddot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\left\langle\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t),\frac{d({\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)^{\top})}{dt}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Here for a smooth function
\phi
of
t
,
\dot{\phi}(t):=\frac{d\phi}{dt}
,
\ddot{\phi}(t):=\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dt^{2}}
.

Proof.

First notice
\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{A}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle=\sigma_{i}(t)
. Differentiating with respect to
t
on both sides yields

\begin{split}\dot{\sigma}_{i}(t)&=\langle\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{A}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{A}}(t)\dot{{\mathbf{v}}}_{i}(t)\rangle\\
\Longrightarrow\dot{\sigma}_{i}(t)&=\sigma_{i}(t)\langle\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\sigma_{i}(t)\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{v}}}_{i}(t)\rangle\\
\overset{(a)}{\Longrightarrow}\dot{\sigma}_{i}(t)&=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle.\end{split}

(35)

Here (a) is because
\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t)\rangle=1
, so
\langle\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{i}(t),{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t)\rangle=0
. Similarly we have
\langle{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{v}}}_{i}(t)\rangle=0
.

Differentiating with respect to
t
on both sides of
\dot{\sigma}_{i}(t)=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle
, we get

\begin{split}\ddot{\sigma}_{i}(t)&=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\ddot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t)\dot{{\mathbf{v}}}_{i}(t)\rangle\\
\Longrightarrow\ddot{\sigma}_{i}(t)&=\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t),\ddot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle\frac{d({\mathbf{u}}_{i}(t){\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)^{\top})}{dt},\dot{{\mathbf{A}}}(t)\rangle.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 9
.

Given any
{\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}_{*}
such that
d([{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],[{\mathbf{Y}}])<\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}})
. Let
{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}=\mathop{\rm arg\min}_{{\mathbf{O}}\in\mathbb{O}_{r}}\|{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}-{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\rm F}
. Then
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is nonsingular and
\det({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}})))>0
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathbf{U}}
spans the top
r
eigenspace of
{\mathbf{Y}}{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}
. By Lemma
2
, we know
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{Y}}\widebar{{\cal M}}_{r+}^{q}
, so we can represent
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}
by
{\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})^{-1}{\mathbf{S}}+{\mathbf{U}}_{\perp}{\mathbf{D}}
for some
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathbb{S}^{r\times r},{\mathbf{D}}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p-r)\times r}
by Lemma
1
.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose there exists a
t^{*}\in[0,1]
such that
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t^{*}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is singular. We show next that this implies
d([{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],[{\mathbf{Y}}])=\|{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\rm F}\geq\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}})
, thus contradicting our assumption.

Since
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t^{*}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is singular, the matrix
{\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})^{-1}{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t^{*}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))={\mathbf{Y}}+t^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})^{-1}{\mathbf{S}}
is singular also. It follows that

\begin{split}\|{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}\|^{2}_{\rm F}=\|{\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})^{-1}{\mathbf{S}}\|^{2}_{\rm F}+\|{\mathbf{U}}_{\perp}{\mathbf{D}}\|_{\rm F}^{2}\geq\|t^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})^{-1}{\mathbf{S}}\|^{2}_{\rm F}\geq\min_{\widetilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}_{*}}\|\widetilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}-{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{\rm F}\overset{(a)}{=}\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}}).\end{split}

Here (a) is by the Schmidt-Mirsky theorem, see
[
Ste98
, Chapter 1, Theorem 4.32]
. We thus conclude that
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is nonsingular for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Using now the continuity of
\det({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}})))
with respect to
t
, the fact that
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is nonsingular for all
t\in[0,1]
, and the fact that at
t=0
we have
\det({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Y}})>0
, we deduce that
\det({\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}})))>0
for all
t\in[0,1]
. This completes the proof.
∎

The fact that the matrix
{\mathbf{Y}}^{\top}({\mathbf{Y}}+t({\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}-{\mathbf{Y}}))
is nonsingular for all
t\in[0,1]
has been proved in
[
MA20
, Proposition 6.2]
. Here we have presented the proof again for completeness, emphasizing the fact that the determinant does not change sign. This fact will be needed in the next lemma.

Lemma 10

(Condition Number for the Perturbation of Orthogonal Procrustes Problem)
.

Let
{\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}_{*}
such that
d([{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}],[{\mathbf{Y}}])<\sigma_{r}({\mathbf{Y}})

(36)

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|\frac{d{\mathbf{O}}_{t}}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\right\|_{\rm F}=\left\|\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{{\mathbf{O}}_{t}-{\mathbf{O}}_{0}}{t}\right\|_{\rm F}=\left\|\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{{\mathbf{O}}_{0}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}_{t}-{\mathbf{O}}_{0}{\mathbf{O}}^{\prime}_{0}}{t}\right\|_{\rm F}\\

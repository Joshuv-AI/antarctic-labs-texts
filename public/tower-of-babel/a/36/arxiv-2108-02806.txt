Affine Harish-Chandra bimodules and Steinberg–Whittaker localization
Justin Campbell, Gurbir Dhillon

Abstract
. We construct categories of Harish-Chandra bimodules for affine Lie algebras analogous to Harish-Chandra bimodules with infinitesimal characters for simple Lie algebras, addressing an old problem raised by I. Frenkel and Malikov. Under an integrality hypothesis, we establish monoidal equivalences between blocks of our affine Harish-Chandra bimodules and versions of the affine Hecke category. To do so, we introduce a new singular localization onto Whittaker flag manifolds. The argument for the latter specializes to a somewhat alternative treatment of regular localization, and identifies the category of Lie algebra representations with a generalized infinitesimal character as the parabolic induction, in the sense of categorical representation theory, of a Steinberg module.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

The study of Harish-Chandra bimodules with generalized infinitesimal characters has been of central importance in the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras. As an early triumph, it identified categories of principal series representations of complex Lie groups with categories of highest weight representations of semisimple Lie algebras
[
Dix77
]
,
[
Duf77
]
,
[
Jos79
]
,
[
BG80
]
, and thereafter with Hecke categories and Soergel bimodules
[
BB81
]
,
[
Soe92
]
,
[
BG99
]
.

The representation theory of affine Lie algebras is by now a mature subject, with well understood theories of highest weight representations, affine Hecke categories, and affine Soergel bimodules. However, a corresponding theory of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules has been elusive. This absence has been felt by multiple groups of experts for at least a quarter century, notably in the work and conjectures of I. Frenkel and Malikov
[
FM97
]
,
[
FM99
]
.

1.2.

In this paper we introduce such a category of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules, and in the process exhibit a partial substitute for infinitesimal characters for Kac–Moody Lie algebras. To show our category of bimodules has the sought-for properties, we provide a new type of singular localization onto Whittaker flag manifolds. Our method of proof for localization, which uses techniques from categorical representation theory, lifts the fundamental identification of the Grothendieck group of a block of Category
\mathscr{O}
with an induced representation of the Hecke algebra to an equivalence of the block itself with an induced representation of the Hecke category.

In the remainder of the introduction, we describe this all in more detail; after giving the definition of the category of bimodules considered in Section
1.3
, we then discuss our main Theorems
1.4.2
and
1.5.2
.

1.3.
Affine Harish-Chandra bimodules

1.3.1.

There are two basic difficulties in giving an analogue of Harish-Chandra bimodules with generalized infinitesimal characters in the affine setting. We now review these, and explain the approach taken in the present work to addressing them.

1.3.2.

The first problem is to give a good notion of a general affine Harish-Chandra bimodule. To see the difficulty, fix an almost simple, simply connected algebraic group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that a Harish-Chandra bimodule is a
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
-module on which the diagonal action of
\mathfrak{g}
integrates to an action of
G
.

Let us now pass to the loop group
G(\!(z)\!)
, whose Lie algebra is the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)
. One may again consider the abelian category of smooth
\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)
-modules on which the diagonal action is integrable. However, as
G(\!(z)\!)
has no nontrivial smooth representations, a short argument shows that this implies that all of
\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)
acts trivially. So the obtained category is equivalent to vector spaces, and is too small to be of use.

When one passes from the loop algebra to an affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
, i.e. one introduces the central charge of level
\kappa
, problems of a similar nature arise.

1.3.3.

To obtain more objects, we follow a proposal of Gaitsgory
[
Gai07
]
. Namely, if one tries to produce more objects by passing to the derived category of bimodules, one encounters the same degeneracy as in the abelian case. However, E. Frenkel and Gaitsgory introduced in
[
FG09a
]
a certain enlargment of the derived category, which we denote below by

(1.1)

\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)\operatorname{-mod}.

This dg-category, sometimes called the
renormalized
derived category, contains the usual unbounded derived category as a full subcategory, and carries a
t
-structure for which the bounded below objects agree with the usual bounded below derived category. Said informally, one adjoins objects concentrated in cohomological degree
-\infty
. The role of such objects and such a category had been long anticipated in the rich folklore around semi-infinite mathematics, with important contributions by Arkhipov, Feigin, Finkelberg, E. Frenkel, Positselski, Voronov, and others; see
[
Vor93
]
,
[
Pos10
]
for a partial indication.
1

As a striking example, the reader may wish to compare Voronov’s final remarks in
[

]
with Krause’s renormalization of the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a Noetherian scheme
[
Kra05
]
, i.e. the category of ind-coherent sheaves
[

.

One then takes the category of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules to be the
G(\!(z)\!)
-equivariant objects in the enlarged category (
1.1
). To make sense of this requires some categorical representation theory of groups. Briefly, the diagonal adjoint action of
G(\!(z)\!)
on
\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)\oplus\mathfrak{g}(\!(z)\!)
induces an action of the category of D-modules
D(G(\!(z)\!))
, viewed as a monoidal dg-category under convolution, on (
1.1
). A general procedure then attaches to any cocomplete dg-category
\mathscr{C}
equipped with an action of
D(G(\!(z)\!))
its category of equivariant objects. If one runs the above instead for the finite dimensional group
G
, this recovers the usual (derived) category of Harish-Chandra bimodules.

1.3.4.

At the time of Gaitsgory’s proposal, the formalism for passing to equivariant objects was not yet available. Plainly, one would like to compute such a category as the totalization of a cobar resolution akin to the simplicial presentation of the equivariant derived category of sheaves on a quotient stack. However, this uses inverse limits of categories, which cannot be sensibly defined working at the triangulated level. The requisite categorical representation theory has been developed over the ensuing fifteen years by Gaitsgory, with significant contributions by Beraldo, Ben-Zvi–Nadler, and Raskin, and building on earlier work of Beilinson and Drinfeld
[
BD
]
,
[
FG06
]
,
[
BZN09
]
,
[

,
[

]
,
[

,
[
Gai20
]
.

The above category of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules, and its analogues at other central charges, figures prominently in the main conjectures of the local quantum Langlands correspondence, cf.
[
Gai07
]
,
[
Gai18

1.3.5.

We now turn to the second basic difficulty. Recall that in finite type to obtain categories of Harish-Chandra bimodules equivalent to Hecke categories and categories of highest weight representations, one fixes a strict or generalized infinitesimal character for the underlying
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
-module.

This forces us to confront a fundamental issue in the representation theory of affine algebras, which experts have contended with since the early days of the subject. Namely, the center of the completed enveloping algebra
U(\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa})
is trivial for all levels
\kappa
excepting the critical level
\kappa_{c}
. Nonetheless, it has long been apparent that its category of modules behaves in certain respects as though there is a nontrivial center. Indeed, in the literature one finds various categories of representations whose linkage principles all take the same form; see
[
DGK82
]
,
[
LZ93
]
,
[
Yak07
]
,
[
ALZ16
]
. Namely, each time at a noncritical level
\kappa
they are indexed by orbits in the dual abstract Cartan
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
of the level
\kappa
dot action of the affine Weyl group.

So, we must look for an alternative way to isolate, for any noncritical level
\kappa
and affine Weyl group orbit
\chi
, a full subcategory of modules

(1.2)

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

which acts as though it were the category of modules with ‘generalized infinitesimal character
\chi
’.

1.3.6.

Our approach to this second problem, which is perhaps the basic new idea of the present work, is again via categorical representation theory.

Recall that, given an algebra
A
and a module
M
, from any element
m
of
M
one can form the
A
-submodule generated by
m
. Similarly, given a monoidal category and a module category, to any object of the latter one can associate the submodule generated by it.

In this way, we define (
1.2
) to be the
D_{\kappa}(G(\!(z)\!))
-submodule of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
generated by the Verma module of highest weight
\lambda
, for any element
\lambda
of the orbit
\chi
; the resulting submodule is independent of the choice. If one runs this definition for the finite dimensional group
G
, it recovers the usual derived category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with generalized infinitesimal character that of the corresponding Verma module.
So,
we use the categorical action to bypass the absence of infinitesimal characters.

Combining this and the construction (
1.1
), we obtain for appropriate pairs of weights
\chi,\phi
and levels
\kappa,\kappa_{\circ}
the desired categories of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules.

The term ‘appropriate’ here means that, just as in finite type, the difference between
(\chi,\kappa)
and
(\phi,\kappa_{\circ})
should be integral (or the categories would vanish).
Namely, we again pass to
G(\!(z)\!)
-equivariant objects to obtain the submodule

(1.3)

\operatorname{HCh}_{\chi,\phi}:=(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{\circ}}\operatorname{-mod}_{\phi})^{G(\!(z)\!)}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{\circ}}\operatorname{-mod}^{G(\!(z)\!)}.

1.3.7.

We spend the majority of the paper verifying that the categories of Harish-Chandra bimodules (
1.3
) introduced above behave in the desired ways. This is not completely transparent, since in the most important cases every single object of
\operatorname{HCh}_{\chi,\phi}
is a homological phantom, i.e. is concentrated in cohomological degree
-\infty
.

1.3.8.

Before discussing the bimodules, let us briefly describe some features of the categories of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules appearing in (
1.2
). As
\chi
runs through the set of affine Weyl group orbits, the subcategories (
1.2
) contain all parabolic inductions of modules with infinitesimal characters from proper Levi factors of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
. Moreover, they satisfy the linkage principle

(1.4)

\underset{\chi}{\oplus}\hskip 2.84526pt\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

i.e. for distinct orbits
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
the categories
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi_{1}}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi_{2}}
are left and right orthogonal to one another. Combining these points confirms a linkage principle for positive energy representations predicted by Yakimov
[
Yak07
]
, cf. Section
8
for more details.

If
\kappa
is an integral level, under the (conjectural) local geometric Langlands correspondence, one can associate to
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
a sheaf of categories
\mathscr{S}
on the moduli space of
\check{G}
-local systems on the punctured disk, where
\check{G}
denotes the Langlands dual of
G
. The inclusion (
1.4
) should correspond to the subcategories of
\mathscr{S}
supported on formal neighborhoods of local systems with regular singularities. In particular, the inclusion (
1.4
) does not see the wildly ramified part of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
. However, for our purposes, namely to have the sought-for relations with affine Hecke categories, this is a necessary feature, and not a bug. We turn to these relations next.

1.4.
Equivalences with affine Hecke categories

1.4.1.

In the next theorem we identify our categories of bimodules (
1.3
) with variants of the affine Hecke category under an integrality assumption. This result strongly suggests that our definition is the correct one.

To state it, we need some notation. Let
\kappa,\kappa_{\circ}
be noncritical integral levels. At such levels, the dot action of the affine Weyl group preserves the weight lattice. Denote by
\chi
and
\phi
a pair of orbits of weights at levels
\kappa
and
\kappa_{\circ}
, respectively. Fix a pinning of the loop group, and in particular an Iwahori subgroup

I\subset G(\!(z)\!),

whose prounipotent radical we denote by
\mathring{I}\subset I
. By a standard procedure, one may then associate to
\chi
and
\phi
character sheaves on
\mathring{I}
, which we denote by
\psi
and
\omega
, respectively. Loosely, as the orbit
\chi
grows more singular the character
\psi
grows more nondegenerate, cf. Section
7.1
for the details.

We also need one more definition, which may be new. For a character sheaf
\psi
as above, consider the corresponding category of Iwahori–Whittaker D-modules on the loop group

(1.5)

D\big(G(\!(z)\!)/\mathring{I},-\psi\big).

This is a naturally a
D(G(\!(z)\!))
-module under left convolution. We may therefore consider the submodule generated by the Iwahori–equivariant objects. This is a categorical analogue of the (degenerate) Steinberg module of a finite group of Lie type, and is a colocalization of (
1.5
), i.e. its inclusion admits a right adjoint.

Since (
1.5
) corepresents the functor of taking the Iwahori–Whittaker equivariant objects
\mathscr{C}^{\mathring{I},\psi}
of a categorical representation
\mathscr{C}
of the loop group, its Steinberg submodule determines a corresponding full subcategory of
Steinberg–Whittaker
equivariant objects

\mathscr{C}^{\mathring{I},\psi,\operatorname{s}}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\mathring{I},\psi}.

For example, if
\psi
is the trivial character sheaf, the Steinberg–Whittaker invariants are the Iwahori monodromic objects of
\mathscr{C}
. Finally, we note that the Steinberg–Whittaker invariants are also introduced in forthcoming work of Arinkin and Bezrukavnikov.

With these preparations, we may state our basic theorem on affine Harish-Chandra bimodules.

Theorem 1.4.2
.

Let
\chi,\phi
and
\psi,\omega
be as above. There are canonical equivalences

\operatorname{HCh}_{\chi,\phi}\simeq D\big(\hskip 0.85358pt(\mathring{I},\psi,\operatorname{s}\hskip 0.0pt)\backslash\hskip 0.28453ptG(\!(z)\!)\hskip 0.28453pt/\hskip 0.85358pt(\mathring{I},-\omega,\operatorname{s})\hskip 0.85358pt\big)\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa_{\circ}}\operatorname{-mod}_{\phi}^{\mathring{I},\psi}.

We now make a few remarks about Theorem
1.4.2
.

Remark 1.4.3
.

The analogues of the above equivalences for the finite dimensional group
G
are fundamental results, due in various amounts of generality to Bernstein–Gelfand, Beilinson–Bernstein, Milicic–Soergel, and more recently Ginzburg, Webster, and Losev
[
BG80
]
,
[
BB81
]
,
[
MS97
]
,
[
Gin09
]
,
[
Web11
]
,
[
Los12
]
. However, as far as we are aware, in the case of
\chi
and
\phi
both singular, it was only a folklore expectation that there should be a realization as appropriate bi-Whittaker sheaves.

For context, we note that the analogous categories of bi–Whittaker sheaves of general Kac–Moody groups should be Koszul dual to bi-parabolic Hecke categories, and admit equivalences with singular Soergel bimodules.

We should also mention that in the sheaf–theoretic realization, the Steinberg condition on the Whittaker invariants appearing on one side in fact implies it holds on the other. For this reason, in the literature one does not find the Steinberg–Whittaker invariants explicitly introduced except in the case of the trivial character.

Finally, we emphasize that, to us, the main point of Theorem
1.4.2
is that our category of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules is really the sought-for one, i.e., it behaves like its finite dimensional counterpart. Unfortunately, to prove that this is so, we cannot mimic the existing arguments. Namely, much of the above work relies on favorable properties of Harish-Chandra bimodules with infinitesimal characters. We only obtain their affine analogues
a posteriori
as consequences of the theorem.

Remark 1.4.4
.

The bimodule and sheaf-theoretic categories appearing above have natural convolution structures. Namely, given affine Harish-Chandra bimodules
\mathscr{M}_{1}
and
\mathscr{M}_{2}
at appropriate levels, one may compose them by forming the semi-infinite homology

\mathscr{M}_{1}\underset{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}{\overset{\frac{\infty}{2}}{\otimes}}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathscr{M}_{2}.

By construction the equivalence of Theorem
1.4.2
exchanges this with the convolution of the corresponding D-modules on the loop group, cf. Remark
10.4.2
for the details.

Remark 1.4.5
.

We also introduce the analogues of categories with strict infinitesimal characters. To see how, recall that in finite type, the category
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}
is acted on by a completion of
Z(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e. the log of monodromy. We extend this to affine type by recognizing this as the
equivariant Hochschild cohomology
of
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}
. We may then set the monodromy to zero, cf. Section
9
for the details.

1.5.
Theorem
1.4.2
via Steinberg–Whittaker localization

1.5.1.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.4.2
is simple. First, some abstract nonsense: if
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are categorical loop group representations, such that
\mathscr{C}
is
dualizable
as an abstract cocomplete dg-category, i.e. as an object of
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
, we have canonical identifications

(1.6)

\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G(\!(z)\!))\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})^{G(\!(z)\!)}\simeq(\mathscr{C}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{D})^{G(\!(z)\!)},

cf. Section
2
below for the precise definitions of
D(G(\!(z)\!))\operatorname{-mod}
and
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
.

We apply this as follows. For Kac–Moody representations, the operation of taking semi-infinite cohomology yields a canonical duality

(1.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{\vee}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa+2\kappa_{c}}\operatorname{-mod},

where as before
\kappa_{c}
denotes the critical level. Writing
\rho
for the half sum of the positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, this restricts, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, to a duality

(1.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}^{\vee}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\kappa+2\kappa_{c}}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\lambda-2\rho}.

In particular, by combining (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), we recognize our categories of Harish-Chandra bimodules as spaces of intertwining operators between categorical representations.

With this, Theorem
1.4.2
follows formally from rewriting our categories of Kac–Moody representations as categories of D-modules. We do so in the following theorem, which we call
Steinberg–Whittaker localization
.

Theorem 1.5.2
.

Let
\chi,\kappa,
and
\psi
be as in Theorem
1.4.2
. Then there is a canonical
D(G(\!(z)\!))
-equivariant equivalence

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\simeq D\big(G(\!(z)\!)/\mathring{I},\psi,\operatorname{s}\hskip-1.42262pt\big).

Let us discuss the contents of Theorem
1.5.2
in several cases.

1.5.3.

If
\chi
is regular of negative level, then
\psi
is trivial, and Theorem
1.5.2
recovers the usual Kashiwara–Tanisaki localization theorem on the enhanced affine flag variety
[
KT95
]
.

1.5.4.

By contrast, if
\chi
is singular of negative level, to our knowledge the theorem and its analogue for a reductive group
G
are both new, and are one of the main results of the present work.

Let us focus on the maximally singular case, e.g. for
G
with
\chi=-\rho
. Here, the theorem says that localization onto the nondegenerate Whittaker flag manifold yields an equivalence

(1.9)

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\rho}\simeq D\big(G/N,\psi,\operatorname{s}).

To get a feel for what is going on, recall that in the regular case, i.e. Beilinson–Bernstein localization, the underlying quasicoherent sheaves of the obtained D-modules on
G/N
descend to the flag variety
G/B
. This homogeneous space parametrizes maximal unipotent subalgebras
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, and the fibres of the obtained sheaf store (a generalized weight space in) the corresponding
\mathfrak{n}
-homology of the initial
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

In the maximally singular case (
1.9
), the underlying quasicoherent sheaves of the obtained D-modules on
G
descend to
G/Z_{G}N
, where
Z_{G}
denotes the center of
G
. This homogeneous space parametrizes maximal unipotent subalgebras
\mathfrak{n}
equipped with a generic additive character
\Psi
, and the fibres of the obtained sheaf store the corresponding
\Psi
-twisted
\mathfrak{n}
-homology of the initial
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Since the work of Beilinson–Bernstein, the problem of singular localization has been studied by various authors to various ends, with important contributions by Backelin–Kremnizer, Beilinson–Ginzburg, Bezrukavnikov–Mirkovic–Rumynin, and Kashiwara
[
BK15
]
,
[
BG99
]
,
[
BMR06
]
,
[
Kas89
]
.

To our knowledge (
1.9
) may be the first nontrivial identification of
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\rho}
with a category of D-modules. Indeed, for any singular generalized infinitesimal character, localization onto the enhanced flag variety
G/N
is not fully faithful. A fully faithful localization functor for singular categories was introduced by Backelin–Kremnizer in the interesting work
[
BK15
]
. As they note, however, for maximally singular blocks it is a special case of the tautological equivalence between all
\mathfrak{g}
-modules and all weakly
G
-equivariant D-modules on
G
.

1.5.5.

Finally, unlike at negative level, where one can show the equivalences are
t
-exact, the equivalences for
\chi
at positive level are of infinite cohomological amplitude, and make essential use of the renormalization of the underlying triangulated categories. They may be considered an affine analogue of usual localization composed with the long intertwining operator.

1.6.
Theorem
1.5.2
via categorical parabolic induction

Finally, we discuss the proof of Steinberg–Whittaker localization.

1.6.1.

The basic idea is as follows. Many categories in representation theory are well known to ‘look like’ induced representations at the level of Grothendieck groups.

In the example at hand, blocks of Category
\mathscr{O}
‘look like’ antispherical modules for the Hecke category, i.e. inductions of sign characters. This point of view has been emphasized by Soergel and his school, and in particular plays a key role in the recent breakthroughs of Williamson and others in the modular representation theory of reductive groups.

As we show in this article,
the induction picture of the Grothendieck group underlies an induction at the level of categories
. Given this, the localization theorems are easy consequences.

1.6.2.

Let us formulate the contents of the preceding paragraph precisely. The discussion below is for
G
, the case of the loop group being similar.

Fix an antidominant weight
\chi
, and write
P
for the standard parabolic corresponding the stabilizer of
\chi
under the dot action of the Weyl group. Write
N_{P}
for the unipotent radical of
P
, and
L
for its Levi quotient, with corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
. Note that
\chi
is a maximally singular weight for
L
.

Consider the functor of parabolic induction of Lie algebra representations

\operatorname{pind}:\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}^{N_{P}}.

By adjunction, this induces a functor

(1.10)

D(G)\underset{D(P)}{\otimes}\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi},

and we show in Theorem
7.2.5
that this is an equivalence.

Remark 1.6.3
.

On
B
-equivariant objects, (
1.10
) yields a canonical equivalence

D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash P/B)}{\otimes}\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}^{B}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}^{B}.

That is, blocks of Category
\mathscr{O}
are inductions of sign representations at the level of categories. Similarly, in geometry we find that convolution induces an equivalence

D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash P/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash P/N,\psi)\simeq D(B\backslash G/N,\psi),

where
\psi
is a generic additive character of
N/N_{P}
.

1.6.4.

Let us turn to localization theory. In the maximally singular case (
1.9
), after introducing a functor between the two sides, by abstract nonsense it suffices to check the obtained map on
B
-equivariant categories is an equivalence. In this case, both sides are equivalent to the category of vector spaces, so this is an easy check. The general case then follows formally by parabolically inducing (
1.9
). A similar argument also recovers the singular localization of Backelin–Kremnizer.

Remark 1.6.5
.

In the case of regular
\chi
, one can ask how this treatment of localization compares to earlier ones. The construction of the functors is somewhat different, especially so in affine type. As for the proof, the reduction to an equivalence on Borel-equivariant objects first appeared in work of E. Frenkel–Gaitsgory
[
FG09b
]
. From there, they rely on an explicit nontrivial calculation by Kashiwara–Tanisaki, while we note it follows directly from the compatibility with the Hecke action.

Remark 1.6.6
.

One can also ask about the strict logical dependence of the arguments in the present paper on prior results.
3
3

3

Aside from facts in categorical representation theory (of the flavor of abstract nonsense), our proofs of the main Theorems

and

are self-contained except for some basic facts about the Schubert decomposition of flag varieties and Category

for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

After this, we do use two nontrivial results from representation theory. To obtain the block decompositions for our categories of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules and bimodules, we use Kac–Kazhdan’s calculation of the Shapovalov determinant
[
KK79

. In addition, to obtain the linkage principle for positive energy representations predicted by Yakimov, we need to identify our construction of
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}
with the usual description via infinitesimal characters. This is deduced from Beilinson–Bernstein’s calculation of global twisted differential operators on the finite flag manifold
[
BB81
]
.

1.6.7.

We would like to finish with a couple comments which lie outside the scope of the article.

First, the presented arguments adapt,
mutatis mutandis
, to the case of Kac–Moody groups and blocks containing a simple Verma module with finite Weyl group stabilizer (or the dual of such a block). It would be very interesting to find a similar approach to localization in positive characteristic and for quantum groups.

Second, while we address the question raised by I. Frenkel–Malikov in
[
FM97
]
and
[
FM99
]
of finding the desired category of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules, we do not address some striking conjectures of
loc. cit.
on the relation with Kazhdan–Lusztig fusion. There indeed is a monoidal functor of the anticipated form from the Kazhdan–Lusztig category to the present category of bimodules. This, along with the closely related theory of projective functors in affine type and principal series Harish-Chandra bimodules, will be discussed in a sequel to the present work.

Third, we would like to mention that forthcoming work of Losev produces categories of Harish-Chandra bimodules for quantum groups. We expect that one has equivalences between these and the categories considered in this paper, compatible with the Kazhdan–Lusztig equivalence.

1.7.
Organization of the paper

Throughout the first half of the paper, we alternate between generalities in categorical representation theory, and their applications to the problem at hand.

In Section 2, we gather preliminary material and set notation.

In Section 3, we study the basic adjunctions between representations of Hecke categories and groups. This is then used in Section 4, where we introduce the categories of Lie algebra representations studied in this paper.

In Section 5, we prove the localization theorems in the maximally singular case. We then study the functor of parabolic induction for categorical representations, and crucially its relation with induction for Hecke categories, in Section 6. This is used in Section 7 to deduce the general case of the localization theorems.

The remaining sections then apply the previous material to various problems in representation theory, and may be read independently of one another. In Section 8, we obtain a linkage principle for positive energy representations of affine Lie algebras suggested by Yakimov. In Section 9, we introduce equivariant Hochschild cohomology and the analogues of strict infinitesimal characters. Finally, in Section 10, we obtain the main theorems on affine Harish-Chandra bimodules.

D(G)

D(G)
-module

(2.9)

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}:=\underset{\kappa}{\oplus}\hskip 2.84526pt\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

where
\kappa
runs over the set
\mathscr{K}
of noncritical integral levels, i.e.

\mathscr{K}=(\mathbb{Z}\cdot\kappa_{b})\setminus\kappa_{c}.

Similarly, let us write
\Lambda
for the set of highest weights for modules in
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B}
. Plainly, we have

\Lambda\simeq\Lambda_{f}\times\mathscr{K},

where the pair
(\lambda_{f},\kappa)
corresponds to the Verma module for
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}
of highest weight
\lambda_{f}
. Recall that at an integral level
\kappa
, the dot action of the affine Weyl group
W
preserves
\Lambda_{f}
. In particular, it makes sense to speak of the dot action of
W
on
\Lambda
.

As a final piece of notation related to our definition of
\Lambda
, let us write
\rho_{f}\in\Lambda_{f}
for the half sum of the positive roots of
\mathfrak{h}
, and
\rho
for the element
(\rho_{f},\kappa_{c})
of
\Lambda_{f}\times\mathbb{Z}\cdot\kappa_{b}
. With this, we have the involution

\lambda\mapsto-\lambda-2\rho:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda,

which plays a basic role in cohomological duality. Explicitly, this sends a pair
(\lambda_{f},\kappa)
to
(-\lambda_{f}-2\rho_{f},-\kappa+2\kappa_{c})
.

2.5.4.

It will be important for us that the usual functors of induction and restriction of Lie algebra representations are maps of categorical group representations. Since we are unsure if this already appears in the literature, we include proofs.

Suppose
K_{1},K_{2}
are a pair of groups as in Section
2.5.2
, with corresponding Tate Lie algebras
\mathfrak{k}_{1},\mathfrak{k}_{2}
. Suppose
h:K_{1}\rightarrow K_{2}
is a homomorphism of group ind-schemes and
\mathfrak{k}_{2,\kappa}
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{k}_{2}
such that it and its pullback
\mathfrak{k}_{1,\kappa}
to
\mathfrak{k}_{1}
are
k
-multiples of the Tate extension.

Lemma 2.5.5
.

The associated restriction functor

\operatorname{Res}:\mathfrak{k}_{2,\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathfrak{k}_{1,\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}

carries a canonical datum of
D_{\kappa}(K_{1})
-equivariance.

Proof.

Following the notation of Section 9 of
[
Ras20
]
, restriction identifies with the composition

\mathfrak{k}_{2,\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq D^{!}_{\kappa}(K_{2})^{K_{2},w}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Oblv}}D^{!}_{\kappa}(K_{2})^{K_{1},w}\xrightarrow{h^{!}}D^{!}_{\kappa}(K_{1})^{K_{1},w}\simeq\mathfrak{k}_{1,\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

and the two intermediate functors carry canonical data of
D_{\kappa}(K_{1})
-equivariance. ∎

By passing to left adjoints, we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.5.6
.

If
K_{1}
is a compact open subgroup of
K_{2}
, the functor of induction

\operatorname{ind}:\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathfrak{k}_{2,\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}

carries a canonical datum of
D(K_{1})
-equivariance.

2.5.7.

As for D-modules, the appearing categories of Lie algebra representations are canonically self-dual. Namely, for any finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, the functor of Lie algebra homology

\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect},\quad\quad M\boxtimes N\mapsto M\underset{\mathfrak{k}}{\otimes}N

is a perfect pairing. In particular, one has a canonical equivalence of monoidal
\infty
-categories

\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}}(\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod},\mathfrak{k}\operatorname{-mod}),

cf. (
2.1
). Plainly, this exchanges the tensor product of bimodules with the composition of endofunctors.

Let us turn to the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}
, cf. Section
2.2
. Consider the functor of semi-infinite homology, which we normalize with respect to the pro-Lie algebra of its Iwahori subgroup,

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-\kappa+2\kappa_{c}}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect},\quad M\boxtimes N\mapsto M\underset{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}{\overset{\frac{\infty}{2}}{\otimes}}\hskip 1.42262ptN.

It was shown by Arkhipov–Gaitsgory that this is a perfect pairing
[
AG15
]
; for an alternative proof cf. Section 5 of
[
Dhi21
]
. By Section 9 of
[
Ras20
]
this functor carries a canonical datum of
D_{\kappa}(G)
-equivariance, i.e. the duality is one of categorical representations.

Remark 2.5.8
.

We remind that the Lie algebra of the Iwahori only appears to normalize the functor; any other compact open subalgebra would do, and the resulting functor would differ by tensoring with a cohomologically graded line.

One again has a canonical equivalence of monoidal
\infty
-categories

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-\kappa+2\kappa_{c}}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{-\kappa+2\kappa_{c}}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod});

this exchanges the semi-infinite tensoring of bimodules with the composition of endo-functors.

By summing over the noncritical integral levels
\mathscr{K}
, and recalling that
\kappa_{c}
is integral, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}
is self-dual, and that one has a canonical equivalence

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}}(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod},\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}).

Concretely, to compose bimodules

M\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{1}}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{2}}\operatorname{-mod},\quad\quad N\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{3}}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{4}}\operatorname{-mod},

one tensors them if the anomaly vanishes, and otherwise sets their composition to zero, i.e.

M\star N:=\begin{cases}M\underset{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}{\overset{\frac{\infty}{2}}{\otimes}}\hskip 1.42262ptN\in\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{1}}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa_{4}}\operatorname{-mod}&\operatorname{if}\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3}=2\kappa_{c},\\
0&\operatorname{otherwise.}\end{cases}

3.
Hecke algebras and invariant vectors

In this section we record, in the present categorical context, the fundamental adjunctions between representations of a group and its Hecke
algebras, as well as their basic properties.

The statements and proofs in this section have the flavor of abstract nonsense. The reader mostly interested in representation-theoretic applications may wish to glance at the statements of Theorem
3.1.5
, Definition
3.2.2
, and Corollary
3.2.4
before proceeding directly to Section
4
.

As with later sections containing abstract nonsense, many of the basic results are known to experts.

3.0.1.

We first
recall the analogous theory
for actions of groups on vector spaces; the reader may wish to skip this and proceed directly to Section
3.1
.

Suppose that
H
is a finite group, and write
H\operatorname{-mod}
for its category of representations
over a
field of characteristic zero. Given a subgroup
K
of
H
, one can consider the associated functor
of
K
-invariants. This is co-represented by the space of
functions
\operatorname{Fun}(H/K)
, whose endomorphisms are the convolution algebra
\operatorname{Fun}(K\backslash H/K).

In particular, we obtain an adjunction

(3.1)

\operatorname{Fun}(H/K)\underset{\operatorname{Fun}(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}-:\operatorname{Fun}(K\backslash H/K)\operatorname{-mod}\rightleftarrows H\operatorname{-mod}:\operatorname{inv}^{K}.

The left adjoint in (
3.1
) is fully faithful, with essential
image generated by the simple representations which
have nontrivial
K
invariants. In particular, for a representation
\pi
of

H
, the co-unit

(3.2)

\operatorname{Fun}(H/K)\hskip 14.22636pt\underset{\hskip-14.22636pt\operatorname{Fun}(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\pi^{K}\rightarrow\pi

is an embedding, with essential image the corresponding isotypic components of

\pi
, i.e. the submodule generated by
K
invariants.

We now turn to the analogous results for categorical representations.

Remark 3.0.2
.

For a
p
-adic group
H
with a compact open subgroup
K
, an adjunction similar to (
3.1
) plays a fundamental role in its representation theory. While this is a more direct analogue of our study of affine Hecke categories and loop groups, the categorical theory behaves more similarly to the case of a finite group, including crucially the fully faithfulness of (
3.2
).

3.1.
The basic adjunctions

3.1.1.

Suppose
K\rightarrow H
is a homomorphism of group placid ind-schemes, cf. Section
2.3.1
. For

D(H)
-modules, the functor of
K
invariants is given by

\operatorname{Hom}_{D(K)\operatorname{-mod}}(\operatorname{Vect},-)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H)\operatorname{-mod}}(D(H)_{K},-).

In particular, such invariants carry a right action by

\mathscr{H}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H)\operatorname{-mod}}(D(H)_{K},D(H)_{K}),

and we obtain a tensor-hom adjunction

(3.3)

-\underset{\mathscr{H}}{\otimes}\hskip 1.42262ptD(H)_{K}:\operatorname{mod-}\hskip-1.42262pt\mathscr{H}\rightleftarrows D(H)\operatorname{-mod}:-^{K}.

In this generality, the right adjoint in (
3.3
) need not be
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linear, nor commute with colimits.

3.1.2.

Let us further suppose that
K
is
a affine group subscheme of
H
whose
prounipotent radical is of finite codimension. In this
case, one has a canonical identification of invariants and coinvariants with respect to
K
, cf. Section
2.4.3
. In particular, we may canonically identify
\mathscr{H}
with
the convolution algebra of bi-
K
-equivariant D-modules

\mathscr{H}\simeq D(K\backslash H/K).

Inversion on
H
induces a canonical isomorphism between
\mathscr{H}
and
its opposite algebra, and in particular between its categories of
left and right
modules. We use both of these identifications implicitly going forwards.

Proposition 3.1.3
.

Under our assumption on
K
, the left
adjoint in
(
3.3
) is fully faithful.

Proof.

By its identification with
K
coinvariants, the functor of
K
invariants
commutes with
colimits and is
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linear. It follows, e.g. via
using the bar resolution, that for any
\mathscr{H}
-module
\mathscr{C}
the
unit
identifies with the composition

\mathscr{C}\simeq D(K\backslash H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}\simeq\big(D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}\big)^{K}.

∎

3.1.4.

We continue to assume that
K
is as in
Section

3.1.2
, and make the further assumption that
H/K
is
ind-proper (and in particular, of ind-finite type). This yields the following property of the counit, which will be
important in what follows.

Theorem 3.1.5
.

With our assumptions on
K
, for any

D(H)
-module

\mathscr{C}
, the counit

(3.4)

D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}^{K}\xrightarrow{\hskip 5.69054ptc\hskip 5.69054pt}\mathscr{C}

is fully faithful and admits a (continuous)
D(H)
-equivariant right adjoint

c^{R}
.

Informally, the theorem allows one to reconstruct a piece of the entire module from its
K
invariants, which are often easier to analyze directly. The result and its proof are similar to work of Ben-Zvi–Gunningham–Orem
[
BZGO18
]
; for a generalization see also the interesting paper of Yang
[
Yan21
]
.

Before giving the proof, we mention an important special case.

Example 3.1.6
.

Suppose that
K=H
. In this case we refer to the
obtained category as the monodromic invariants, i.e.

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-mon}}

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{C}^{K}\underset{D(K\backslash K/K)}{\otimes}D(K/K)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathscr{C}^{K}\underset{D(\operatorname{pt}\hskip-1.42262pt/K)}{\otimes}\operatorname{Vect}.

Theorem
3.1.5
identifies this with the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
generated under colimits by the essential
image of the forgetful functor

\operatorname{Oblv}:\mathscr{C}^{K}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}.

So, in this case Theorem
3.1.5
reconstructs a ‘formal
neighborhood’ of

\mathscr{C}^{K}
inside of
\mathscr{C}
using the action of
D(\operatorname{pt}\hskip-1.42262pt/K)
on
the former. This may be thought of as a version of de-equivariantization.

Proof of Theorem
3.1.5
.

The claimed equivariance of such a right adjoint is automatic for
D(H)
-modules, cf. Section
2.4.1
.

By the
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linearity and continuity of the appearing functors, it suffices to show that the
D(H)\otimes D(H)
-equivariant
functor

(3.7)

D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}D(K\backslash H)\rightarrow D(H)

is fully faithful and admits a
right
adjoint. Namely, the general case follows by tensoring over
D(H)
with
\mathscr{C}
.

We will prove the assertions for (
3.7
) by an analysis of the situation on zero-simplices in the bar resolution, along
with
a monadicity argument. Consider the
convolution
correspondence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Since
p
is a
K
-torsor, the functor
p_{*}
admits a
left adjoint
p^{*}

(see

[
Ras15
]
,
Proposition 6.18.1). With
this, by definition the
D(H)\otimes D(H)
-equivariant
composition

D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)\rightarrow D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}D(K\backslash H)\rightarrow D(H)

identifies equivariantly with the composition

(3.8)

m:D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D(H\overset{K}{\times}H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D(H).

Since
q
is
ind-proper of ind-finite type, the
D(H)\otimes D(H)
-equivariant functor

q_{*}
admits a right
adjoint

q^{!}
. By composition, it follows that
m
admits a right adjoint
m^{R}
.

Let us now consider the situation after tensoring over
D(K\backslash H/K)
. We first claim that the insertion

\operatorname{ins}:D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)\rightarrow D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}D(K\backslash H)

is monadic. More precisely, we claim it admits a conservative right adjoint

\operatorname{ins}^{R}
.

To see this, consider the presentation of
D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}D(K\backslash H)
via the semisimplicial bar resolution

(3.9)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H).}

It follows from the preceding paragraph that each face map admits a right adjoint. Under the canonical equivalence of the colimit with the limit over its right adjoints,
\operatorname{ins}^{R}

identifies with evaluation on zero-simplices, which yields the desired
claim.

In term of the bar resolution, (
3.7
) is given by the
augmented semisimplicial
dg-category

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(H).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{*}p^{*}}

Thus the functor (
3.7
)
amounts to a morphism of monads

\operatorname{ins}^{R}\operatorname{ins}\longrightarrow p_{*}q^{!}q_{*}p^{*}.

We will prove
that this is an isomorphism, which is equivalent to the fully faithfulness of
(
3.7
).

Corollary C.2.3 in
[
Gai15
]
says that the
monad
\operatorname{ins}^{R}\operatorname{ins}
identifies with

\displaystyle D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{id}_{D(H/K)}\otimes\operatorname{act}_{D(K\backslash H)}^{R}}D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\operatorname{act}_{D(H/K)}\otimes\text{id}_{D(K\backslash H)}}D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)

as an endofunctor. Thus it is given by pull-push along the correspondence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H/K\times K\backslash H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H/K\times K\backslash H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H.}

But the endofunctor
p_{*}q^{!}q_{*}p^{*}

is given by pull-push along the isomorphic correspondence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\overset{K}{\times}H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H/K\times K\backslash H,}

and a diagram chase shows that the resulting natural isomorphism

\operatorname{ins}^{R}\operatorname{ins}\tilde{\to}p_{*}q^{!}q_{*}p^{*}

identifies
with the aforementioned morphism of monads. This completes the proof of the
fully faithfulness of (
3.7
).

It remains to furnish (
3.7
) with a right adjoint
\eqref{e:univcase}^{R}
.
To see this, recall that the morphism
m
identifies with the
composition

D(H/K)\otimes D(K\backslash H)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ins}}D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}D(K\backslash H)\xrightarrow{\eqref{e:univcase}}D(H).

The desired continuity of
\eqref{e:univcase}^{R}
, which exists as a discontinuous functor by the adjoint functor theorem, follows via the conservativity
and
continuity of

\operatorname{ins}^{R}
from the continuity of
m^{R}
.
∎

3.1.7.

Let us deduce a useful consequence of Theorem

3.1.5
.
In particular, we keep its assumptions on
K
.

Corollary 3.1.8
.

For
K
as in Theorem
3.1.5
, the functor

D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}-

is also right adjoint
to the functor of
K
invariants, with unit

\mathscr{C}\xrightarrow{\hskip 2.84526ptc^{R}\hskip 2.84526pt}D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}^{K}.

Proof.

For a
D(H)
-module
\mathscr{C}
and
D(K\backslash H/K)
-module

\mathscr{M}
, we have a map

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D(K\backslash H/K)\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathscr{C}^{K},\mathscr{M})

\displaystyle\overset{\ref{p:fftensoringup}}{\simeq}\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H)\operatorname{-mod}}\big(D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}^{K},D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{M}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H)\operatorname{-mod}}\big(\mathscr{C},D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{M}\big).

By Theorem
3.1.5
, the latter arrow is a full embedding. Its
essential surjectivity follows from the naturality of
c^{R}
, as it is an
equivalence when applied to
D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{M}
.
∎

Remark 3.1.9
.

Note that Corollary
3.1.8
can be rephrased as a natural isomorphism of functors

(3.10)

D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}(-)\hskip 5.69054pt\simeq\hskip 5.69054pt\operatorname{Hom}_{D(K\backslash H/K)\operatorname{-mod}}(D(H/K),-).

3.2.
Generation

3.2.1.

Let
H
and
K
be as in Theorem
3.1.5
. In the remainder of this section, we collect some tools which allow one to control a categorical representation from its
K
invariants. The basic definition is the following.

Definition 3.2.2
.

For a
D(H)
-module
\mathscr{C}
, we write
\mathscr{C}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}
for the
submodule of
\mathscr{C}

generated by its

K
invariants
, i.e. the essential image of the counit (
3.4
):

D(H/K)\underset{D(K\backslash H/K)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}^{K}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathscr{C}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}\hskip 2.84526pt\subset\hskip 2.84526pt\mathscr{C}.

We say that
\mathscr{C}
is
generated by its

K
invariants
if
\mathscr{C}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}=\mathscr{C}
, i.e. if the inclusion is an equivalence.

3.2.3.

Suppose
{\Theta}:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}
is a morphism of

D(H)
-modules, and consider the associated
morphism of
K
-invariant categories

{\Theta}^{K}:\mathscr{C}^{K}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{K}.

If
{\Theta}
is fully faithful, it formally follows that
{\Theta}^{K}
is as well. Indeed, given a general monoidal category
\mathscr{M}
, a fully faithful map of

\mathscr{M}
-modules
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}
, and another
\mathscr{M}
-module
\mathscr{S}
, the map

(3.11)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{M}\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathscr{S},\mathscr{C})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{M}\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathscr{S},\mathscr{D})

is a fully faithful embedding.

The following partial converse, which we will make significant use of in what follows, allows one to check fully faithfulness, and in particular equivalences, on
‘highest weight vectors’.

Corollary 3.2.4
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is generated by
K
invariants and
{\Theta}^{K}
is fully faithful, then
{\Theta}
also is fully faithful.

Proof.

By our assumption on
\mathscr{C}
, we obtain a canonical factorization of
\Theta
as a composition

(3.12)

\mathscr{C}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{K\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}.

By Theorem
3.1.5
the second arrow of (
3.12
) is fully
faithful, so it is enough to show the first arrow of (
3.12
) is also fully
faithful. However, as in Remark
3.1.9
we may rewrite this as

\operatorname{Hom}_{D(K\backslash H/K)\operatorname{-mod}}\big(D(H/K),\mathscr{C}^{K}\big)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(K\backslash H/K)\operatorname{-mod}}\big(D(H/K),\mathscr{D}^{K}\big),

hence its fully faithfulness follows from (
3.11
). ∎

Let us mention two other consequences of Theorem
3.1.5
, namely the favorable interaction of generation by
K
invariants with passing to subcategories and gluing. Since we do not use these in what follows, we omit their formal deductions.

Corollary 3.2.5
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is generated by
K
invariants, and
\mathring{\mathscr{C}}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
is a fully faithful embedding of
D(H)
-modules, then

\mathring{\mathscr{C}}
is generated by
K
invariants.

Corollary 3.2.6
.

Suppose
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
,
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}
are
D(H)
-modules fitting
into a
recollement

\mathscr{M}^{\prime}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{M}\rightleftarrows\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}.

Then
\mathscr{M}

is generated by
K
invariants if and only if
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime\prime}
are.

3.2.7.

Finally, let us note the compatibility of
K
generation and
duality. Recall that if
\mathscr{C}
is a
D(H)
-module which is dualizable as an
object of
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
, its dual
\mathscr{C}^{\vee}
carries a canonical structure of

D(H)
-module.

Corollary 3.2.8
.

For
H
and
K
as in Theorem
3.1.5
, and a dualizable

D(H)
-module
\mathscr{C}
, there are canonical isomorphisms

5.
Localization I: The sign representation and the Steinberg module

In this section, we prove a localization theorem which embeds a maximally
singular

5.0.1.

6.
parabolic induction for groups and Hecke categories

To prove the general case of the localization theorem, we will reduce to the
maximally singular case using parabolic induction for categorical
representations. In this section, we first collect
basic definitions and properties of parabolic induction functors for groups and
Hecke algebras. We then establish a basic compatibility between these induction
functors, and deduce a useful corollary on submodules generated by
Borel-invariant vectors.

The reader mostly interested in representation-theoretic applications may wish to skim Section
6.1
for the basic definitions and read the comments following Corollary
6.3.3
before proceeding on to Section
7
.

6.1.
Definitions and first properties

6.1.1.

Let
G
and
B
be as in Section
2
. Fix a standard
parabolic subgroup
B\subset P\subset G
.
8
8

8

Note that for a loop group
G
, these are sometimes called instead
parahoric
subgroups.

Denote its
prounipotent radical and Levi quotient by
N_{P}
and
L
respectively. One then has the standard correspondence of groups

L\leftarrow P\rightarrow G.

Definition 6.1.2
.

The functor of
parabolic induction
is given by the
composition

(6.1)

\operatorname{pind}_{P}^{G}:D(L)\operatorname{-mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{L}^{P}}D(P)\operatorname{-mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ind}_{P}^{G}}D(G)\operatorname{-mod}.

By its definition, parabolic induction is left adjoint to the
Jacquet
, or

parabolic restriction

functor

(6.2)

\operatorname{pres}_{G}^{P}:D(G)\operatorname{-mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{G}^{L}}D(P)\operatorname{-mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{inv}^{N_{P}}}D(L)\operatorname{-mod}.

Remark 6.1.3
.

Parabolic induction also right adjoint to the
Jacquet functor,
although in affine type
this depends on the choice of a dimension theory on
G

to normalize the equivalence
D(G)\simeq D(G)^{\vee}
, cf. Section
2.3.2
.

When
P
and
G
are clear from context, we will refer to the functors simply
as
\operatorname{pind}
and
\operatorname{pres}
. It is straightforward to see that both functors
are

\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linear and commute with limits and colimits. Similarly,
\operatorname{pres}

canonically commutes with duality, and the same holds for
\operatorname{pind}
with the
caveat for
G
of affine type as in Remark
6.1.3
.

6.1.4.

Let us pass to Hecke categories. Writing
B_{L}\simeq B/N_{P}

for the induced Borel subgroup of
L
, one has a correspondence

B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L}\leftarrow B\backslash P/B\rightarrow B\backslash G/B.

This induces a correspondence of monoidal functors

D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})\leftarrow D(B\backslash P/B)\rightarrow D(B\backslash G/B),

wherein the leftward arrow, by the prounipotence of
N_{P}
, is an equivalence, and the rightward arrow, as it is a pushforward along a closed embedding, is fully faithful.

Definition 6.1.5
.

The functor of
parabolic induction
is the
composition

(6.3)

\operatorname{pind}_{\mathscr{H}_{L}}^{\mathscr{H}_{G}}:D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})\operatorname{-mod}\simeq D(B\backslash P/B)\operatorname{-mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ind}}D(B\backslash G/B)\operatorname{-mod}.

As before, we have that
\operatorname{pind}_{\mathscr{H}_{L}}^{\mathscr{H}_{G}}
is left adjoint to the

parabolic restriction
functor

(6.4)

\operatorname{pres}_{\mathscr{H}_{G}}^{\mathscr{H}_{L}}:D(B\backslash G/B)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}D(B\backslash P/B)\operatorname{-mod}\simeq D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})\operatorname{-mod}.

Remark 6.1.6
.

It follows from Corollary
3.1.8
that
\operatorname{pind}_{\mathscr{H}_{L}}^{\mathscr{H}_{G}}
is also canonically right
adjoint to
\operatorname{pres}_{\mathscr{H}_{G}}^{\mathscr{H}_{L}}
.

When it is clear from context that we are referring to the Hecke categories associated to
P
and
G
, we will refer to the functors simply as
\operatorname{pind}
and
\operatorname{pres}
. It is straightforward to see that
\operatorname{pind}
and
\operatorname{pres}
are

\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linear, and commute with limits, colimits, and duality.

6.2.
Commutation of parabolic induction and Borel invariants

6.2.1.

Having now discussed parabolic induction functors for groups
and Hecke categories, we now establish their compatibility. Informally, the
following theorem states that taking Borel invariant vectors commutes with
parabolic induction.

Theorem 6.2.2
.

The following diagram commutes up to natural
equivalence:

(6.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(L)\operatorname{-mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})\operatorname{-mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(B\backslash G/B)\operatorname{-mod}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{inv}^{B_{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{pind}_{\mathscr{H}_{L}}^{\mathscr{H}_{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{inv}^{B}}

Proof.

Note that the appearing functors are
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
-linear and commute with
colimits. In particular, the composition
\operatorname{pind}_{\mathscr{H}_{L}}^{\mathscr{H}_{G}}\circ\operatorname{inv}^{B_{L}}
is
given by tensoring over
D(L)
with the
D(B\backslash G/B)\otimes D(L)
-module

D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash P/B)}{\otimes}D(B_{L}\backslash L)\simeq D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash P/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash P/N_{P}).

Similarly, the composition
\operatorname{inv}^{B}\circ\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}
is given by tensoring
over
D(L)
with the
D(B\backslash G/B)\otimes D(L)
-module

D(B\backslash G)\underset{D(G)}{\otimes}D(G/N_{P})\simeq D(B\backslash G/N_{P}).

Therefore, it is enough to show the natural convolution morphism

(6.6)

D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash P/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash P/N_{P})\rightarrow D(B\backslash G/N_{P}).

is an equivalence. As this identifies with the counit map for the
action of
D(L)

on

D(B\backslash G/N_{P})
via right convolution, by Theorem

3.1.5
it is fully faithful.

It remains to prove
the essential
surjectivity of (
6.6
). For this, let
W
denote
the
Weyl group of
G
, and for each
w\in W
consider the Schubert cell

C_{w}:=BwB\subset G.

Write
j_{w}:B\backslash C_{w}\rightarrow B\backslash G
for the associated locally
closed embedding. In view of the
Cousin filtration and
the
already established fully faithfulness, it
suffices to
show that
j_{w,*}\mathscr{M}
belongs to the essential image of
(
6.6
) for any

w\in W
and
any
\mathscr{M}
in
D(B\backslash C_{w}/N_{P})
.

In fact, we will obtain all such
\mathscr{M}
by convolution from
D-modules on a single Schubert cell of
L
, as follows. Write
w=uy

where
y\in W_{L}
and
u
is the minimal length
representative
of the coset
wW_{L}
. Then, writing
\ell
for the standard length function on the Coxeter group
W
,
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(y)
and convolution
yields an isomorphism

(6.7)

C_{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\times}}C_{y}\longrightarrow C_{w}.

In addition, consider the tautological projection

\pi:C_{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\times}}C_{y}\rightarrow B\backslash C_{y}.

By our assumption on
u
and
y
, this induces an isomorphism after
quotienting by
B
and
N_{P}
, i.e.

(6.8)

\pi:B\backslash C_{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\times}}C_{y}/N_{P}\simeq B\backslash C_{y}/N_{P}.

Finally, note the resulting composition

(6.9)

D(B\backslash C_{y}/N_{P})\overset{\eqref{e:step2}}{\simeq}D(B\backslash C_{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\times}}C_{y}/N_{P})\overset{\eqref{e:step1}}{\simeq}D(B\backslash C_{w}/N_{P})\xrightarrow{j_{w,*}}D(B\backslash G/N_{P})

is given by convolution on the left with the costandard object
j_{u,*}
. In particular, the image of (
6.9
) lies in the
image of (
6.6
), as desired. ∎

Remark 6.2.3
.

By passing to the right
adjoints of the functors appearing in
(
6.5
), one obtains a similar commutative diagram for parabolic restriction.

6.3.
Compatibility with generation by Borel invariants

6.3.1.

We now record a consequence of Theorem

6.2.2
which
will be of use in what follows. Recall that to a
D(L)
-module
\mathscr{C}
or a

D(G)
-module
\mathscr{D}
one has the associated submodules generated by their Borel
invariants

\mathscr{C}^{B_{L}\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{D}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}},

respectively, cf. Section

for their definition.

6.3.2.

The following corollary, said informally, asserts that the
operation of taking
the submodule generated by Borel invariants commutes with parabolic induction
and restriction.

Corollary 6.3.3
.

There is a natural isomorphism of functors

D(L)\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow D(G)\operatorname{-mod}
:

(6.10)

\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathscr{C}^{B_{L}\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}})\simeq(\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}\mathscr{C})^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}.

Similarly, there is a natural isomorphism of functors
D(G)\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow D(L)\operatorname{-mod}
:

(6.11)

\operatorname{pres}_{G}^{L}(\mathscr{D}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}})\simeq(\operatorname{pres}_{G}^{L}\mathscr{D})^{B_{L}\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}.

Proof.

We begin with (
6.10
). Note that the left hand side of
(
6.10
) is given by tensoring over
D(L)
with the
D(L)\otimes D(G)
-module

D(G)\underset{D(P)}{\otimes}D(L/B_{L})\underset{D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})}{\otimes}D(B_{L}\backslash L)\simeq D(G/B)\underset{D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})}{\otimes}D(B_{L}\backslash L).

Similarly, the right hand side is given by tensoring
with

\displaystyle D(G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash G/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash G)\underset{D(G)}{\otimes}D(G/N_{P})

\displaystyle\simeq D(G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash G/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G}}{{\times}}G/N_{P})

\displaystyle\simeq D(G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash G/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash G/N_{P}).

To identify the two, we may apply

to further rewrite the right hand side as

\displaystyle{\simeq}D(G/B)\underset{D(B\backslash G/B)}{\otimes}D(B\backslash G/B)\underset{D(B_{L}\backslash L/B_{L})}{\otimes}D(B_{L}\backslash L)

j_{w,!}\star M_{\lambda}^{L}\simeq M_{\lambda}^{L}
for

,
cf. Lemma

. In particular, we may consider the objects

\mathscr{M}_{w}:=j_{w,!}\star\mathscr{M}_{e},\quad\quad\text{for }w\in W/W_{P}.

Corollary 7.1.8

The objects
\mathscr{M}_{w}

, compactly generate
(

(7.5)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{M}_{w},\mathscr{M}_{e})\simeq\begin{cases}k&\text{if }w=e,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Proposition
7.1.5
and Remark

yield an

\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda})^{B}\simeq D(B\backslash G/N,\psi).

By construction this identifies

7.2.
The parabolic induction of the Steinberg module and Lie algebra
representations

7.2.1.

To prove the localization theorems for
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}
, it
is enough to identify it with
\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda})
. We now observe that
there is a canonical functor in one direction.

7.2.2.

Indeed, note that parabolic induction of Lie algebra
representations is a
D(L)
-equivariant functor

(7.6)

\operatorname{pind}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{N_{P}},

cf. Section
2.5.4
for the equivariance data.

Remark 7.2.3
.

In the affine case, if
\lambda
is a weight of level
\kappa
, by this we mean the functor of level
\kappa
parabolic induction

\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{N_{P}}\hskip 2.84526pt\subset\hskip 2.84526pt\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}.

By adjunction, (
7.6
) induces a

D(G)
-equivariant functor

\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}.

As parabolic induction sends Verma modules to Verma modules, this restricts to
a
D(G)
-equivariant functor

(7.7)

\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}.

7.2.4.

We now show that the above yields the sought-for identification.

Theorem 7.2.5
.

The functor (
7.7
) is an equivalence.

Proof.

As both sides of (
7.7
) are generated by their Borel
invariants (cf. Corollary
6.3.3
for the left hand side), it is enough
to show the obtained
D(B\backslash G/B)
-equivariant functor

\gamma:\operatorname{pind}_{L}^{G}(\mathfrak{l}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda})^{B}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}^{B}

is an equivalence.

We first claim it admits an equivariant right adjoint
\gamma^{R}
. Indeed, note
that for any
w\in W/W_{P}
we have

(7.8)

\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{w})\simeq\gamma(j_{w,!}\star\mathscr{M}_{e})\simeq j_{w,!}\star\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{e})\simeq j_{w,!}\star M_{\lambda}\overset{\eqref{e:stb1}}{\simeq}M_{w\cdot\lambda}.

In particular,
\gamma
sends compact objects to compact objects, and hence
admits a continuous right adjoint, which is equivariant by the semi-rigidity of

D(B\backslash G/B)
.

We next check that
\gamma
is fully faithful. As
\mathscr{M}_{e}
generates
(
7.4
) as a
D(B\backslash G/B)
-module, it is enough to see
the canonical map

(7.9)

\mathscr{M}_{e}\rightarrow\gamma^{R}\circ\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{e})

is an equivalence. Indeed, for any
w\in W/W_{P}
, we unwind

(7.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{M}_{w},\gamma^{R}\circ\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{e}))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{w}),\gamma(\mathscr{M}_{e}))\overset{\eqref{e:mgoestom}}{\simeq}\operatorname{Hom}(M_{w\cdot\lambda},M_{\lambda}).

Recalling that
M_{\lambda}
is simple, and in particular coincides
with its
contragredient dual
A_{\lambda}
, we may continue

(7.11)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(M_{w\cdot\lambda},A_{\lambda})\simeq\begin{cases}k&\text{if }w=e\\
0&\text{otherwise;}\end{cases}

where the last vanishing is a (standard) consequence of the cofreeness of

A_{\lambda}
over
N
. Comparing with (
7.5
), it follows that
(
7.9
) is an equivalence, as desired.

It remains to see that
\gamma
is essentially surjective. However, as the

M_{w\cdot\lambda}
compactly generate the target, cf. Proposition

4.0.7
, we are done by (
7.8
).
∎

7.2.6.

Concatenating Theorem
7.2.5
and Proposition

7.1.5
, we have shown the following.

Theorem 7.2.7
.

There are canonical
D(G)
-equivariant
equivalences

(7.12)

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}\simeq D(G/N,\psi,\operatorname{s})\simeq D(G/N_{P})^{L,w,-\lambda-2\rho_{L}}.

Let us explain how to deduce localization theorems at positive

Corollary 7.2.9

There are canonical

-equivariant
equivalences

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\lambda-2\rho}\simeq D(G/N,-\psi,\operatorname{s})\simeq D(G/N_{P})^{L,w,\lambda}.

Proof.

Recall that in Proposition

we saw that

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}^{\vee}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\lambda-2\rho}.

∎

Remark 7.2.10
.

Consider the action of
T
(supplemented with the

\mathbb{G}_{m}

8.
The linkage principle for positive energy representations

The goal of this section is to prove a linkage principle for
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}
and specialize it to a linkage principle for positive energy representations proposed by Yakimov in
[
Yak07
]
.

The proofs of this section do not rely on localization theory, but do use the definitions and results of Sections
3
,
4
, and
6
. The only material from this section used in its sequels is Theorem
8.1.6
, which will be applied to obtain the block decomposition for affine Harish-Chandra bimodules. So, the reader may wish to examine Theorem
8.1.6
and then proceed directly to Section
9
.

8.1.
Positive energy representations

8.1.1.

Let
H
be an almost simple, simply connected algebraic group,
H[\hskip-0.56905pt[z]\hskip-0.56905pt]
its arc group, and
H(\!(z)\!)

its loop group. Passing to their Lie algebras, let us write
\mathfrak{h}
for the
corresponding semisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}[\hskip-0.56905pt[z]\hskip-0.56905pt]
for the corresponding arc
algebra, and
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}
for the corresponding affine Lie algebra
at a fixed noncritical integral level. Recall that
\Lambda_{f}
denotes the character lattice of the abstract Cartan of
H
.

8.1.2.

Associated to this data is the following category of
positive energy representations
. To introduce it, write
K
for the first
congruence
subgroup of
H[\hskip-0.56905pt[z]\hskip-0.56905pt]
, i.e. its prounipotent radical, and consider the abelian category

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{K,\heartsuit}

of smooth
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}
-modules on which the action of the Lie algebra of
K
is integrable, i.e. locally nilpotent. Within it, consider the cocomplete Serre subcategory

\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{K,\heartsuit}

generated by parabolic inductions of
\mathfrak{h}
-modules with integral infinitesimal characters. Explicitly, if we write
Z(\mathfrak{h})
for the center of
U(\mathfrak{h})
,
\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}
may be described as follows.

Lemma 8.1.3
.

For a module
M
in
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{K,\heartsuit}
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

The object
M
lies in
\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}
.

(2)

For any subquotient
S
of
M
,
Z(\mathfrak{h})
acts on
S^{K}
locally finitely with integral eigenvalues.

(3)

For any splitting of
\mathfrak{h}
into
\mathfrak{h}[\hskip-0.56905pt[z]\hskip-0.56905pt]
,
Z(\mathfrak{h})
acts on
M
locally finitely with integral eigenvalues.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that (1) implies (3), and (3) implies (2). To see that (2) implies (1), consider the chain of submodules

(8.1)

0=M^{0}\subset M^{1}\subset M^{2}\subset\cdots\subset M

where for
i\geqslant 1
,
M^{i}
is defined inductively as the submodule of
M
generated by vectors which are
K
-invariant modulo
M^{i-1}
. By assumption (2), each successive quotient
M^{i}/M^{i-1}
lies in
\operatorname{Pos}_{\kappa}
, and hence so does each
M^{i}
. It is therefore enough to show that the filtration is exhaustive, i.e.

\varinjlim_{i}M^{i}\simeq M.

To see this, note that by the
K
-integrability of
M
, any element of
M
lies in a finite dimensional
K
-submodule, and by the prounipotence of
K
, the latter is a finite successive extension of the trivial module.
∎

Remark 8.1.4
.

Note that the Sugawara energy operator
L_{0}
acts locally finitely on objects of
\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}
, with spectrum locally bounded from below, cf. the proof of Proposition
8.4.2
below for a precise formulation. For
\mathfrak{h}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, and after relaxing the integrality assumption on the infinitesimal characters, this also characterizes
\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}
.

8.1.5.

Let us write
W_{f}
and
W
for the finite and
affine Weyl groups of
H
, and consider the level
\kappa
dot action of the latter on
\Lambda_{f}
. For an orbit of weights
\chi\in W\backslash\Lambda_{f}
, and a infinitesimal character
\chi_{f}
of
\mathfrak{h}
, let us say that
\chi_{f}

lies in

\chi
if the corresponding finite Weyl group orbit is
contained in
\chi
. For an orbit
\chi
, consider the Serre subcategory

\operatorname{Pos}_{\chi}\subset\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}

consisting of modules
\mathscr{M}
with the property that, for every subquotient
\mathscr{S}

of
\mathscr{M}
, the generalized eigenvalues of
Z(\mathfrak{h})
on
\mathscr{S}^{K}
lie in
\chi
.

The following linkage principle was proposed by Yakimov and
will be proven in this section.

Theorem 8.1.6
.

The direct sum of inclusions over
\chi\in W\backslash\Lambda_{f}

yields an equivalence of cocomplete
abelian categories

\underset{\chi}{\oplus}\hskip 1.42262pt\operatorname{Pos}_{\chi}\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}.

I.e., every object
\mathscr{M}
of
\operatorname{Pos}_{\hskip 0.56905pt\kappa}
canonically decomposes as
\underset{\chi}{\oplus}\hskip 1.42262pt\mathscr{M}_{\chi}
, where
\mathscr{M}_{\chi}
is its maximal submodule lying in
\operatorname{Pos}_{\chi}
.

Remark 8.1.7
.

The
statement of Theorem
8.1.6
and argument below can be directly adapted to cover
arbitrary infinitesimal
characters at any noncritical level. To do so, one works also with
(B,\lambda)
-equivariant objects and the corresponding twisted affine Hecke categories, where
\lambda
varies over character sheaves pulled back from the abstract Cartan; see for example
[
LY20
]
or
[
CDR20
]
for further discussion of such Hecke categories.

8.1.8.

This section is structured as follows. We will first prove a
linkage principle for all of
\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}}
. We will then, in finite type,
identify the latter category with
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with integral generalized
infinitesimal characters. Finally, we will combine these two steps to deduce
Theorem
8.1.6
.

8.2.
The linkage principle

8.2.1.

In this subsection,

will be as in Section

Proposition 8.2.2
.

The map (

Proof.

We claim it is enough to show the obtained map

\underset{\chi}{\oplus}\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}^{B}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B}

is an equivalence.

Indeed, if so we have

Let us show (

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B}}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N})\simeq 0.

Let us first assume that

is either of finite type or

are at a negative level. In these cases, by a theorem

of Kac–Kazhdan on

As

and

Finally, the case of
G

.
∎

8.3.
The relation with infinitesimal characters

8.3.1.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group. Recall that we

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\lambda}\subset\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}

as the

Remark 8.3.2

Of course, the hypothesis that

Let us write
\chi

Some applications

In this section we develop two applications of Steinberg–Whittaker localization. Namely, we explain how to deduce some familiar equivalences between highest weight categories of representations and D-modules, and how to extend the action of the center of

on
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with generalized infinitesimal characters to affine type. The latter allows one to form categories of representations with ‘strict infinitesimal characters’.

This material will not be used in our analysis of affine Harish-Chandra bimodules, and the reader may wish to directly proceed to Section

9.1.1.

9.2.
Monodromy via equivariant Hochschild cohomology

9.2.1.

For a category
\mathscr{C}
, recall its

is the endomorphisms of its identity functor, i.e.

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\mathscr{C}):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C})}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathscr{C}},\operatorname{id}_{\mathscr{C}}).

Note that this would perhaps be more precisely termed its Hochschild cochains, i.e. we consider the dg-algebra itself and not simply its cohomology.

Recall that if

is of finite type, there is a tautological

If

at the

9.2.3.

The goal of this subsection is to witness a similar action of
‘monodromy operators’ in the affine case despite the triviality of the center.
Even in finite type, this gives an formulation of these operators independent
of the realization of the category as Lie algebra representations or as

9.2.4.

The main new player, in addition to the previous contents of
the article, is the following.

\displaystyle\operatorname{HCh}_{\phi,\chi}\simeq(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\phi}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi})^{G}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\phi-2\rho},\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}).

By Theorem
7.2.7
and Corollary
7.2.9
we have canonical

D(G)
-equivariant equivalences

(10.4)

\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{-\phi-2\rho}\simeq D(G/N,-\psi,\operatorname{s})\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}\simeq D(G/N,\omega,\operatorname{s}).

In addition, note that for any
D(G)
-module
\mathscr{S}
generated by its

B
invariants, we have by adjunction and Corollary
6.3.3

(10.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}(D(G/N,-\psi,\operatorname{s}),\mathscr{S})\simeq\mathscr{S}^{N,\psi}

(10.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathscr{S},D(G/N,\omega,\operatorname{s}))\simeq\mathscr{S}_{N,-\omega}\simeq\mathscr{S}^{N,-\omega}.

Substituting the equivalences of (
10.4
) into
(
10.3
) and then using
(
10.5
)-(
10.6
)
yields the four
desired equivalences.
∎

We finish this subsection with a few remarks.

Remark 10.4.2
.

Note that, in the special case of
\chi=-\phi-2\rho
, the
proof of Theorem
10.4.1
gives a monoidal equivalence

\operatorname{HCh}_{\phi,-\phi-2\rho}\simeq D(N,\psi,\operatorname{s}\hskip-1.70717pt\backslash G/N,-\psi,\operatorname{s})

If
G
is of finite type and
\phi
a regular
orbit, this is essentially due to Beilinson–Ginzburg by a different
argument, cf. Section 5 of
[
BG99
]
and compare with Remark

7.2.10
. More generally, Theorem
10.4.1
yields a monoidal equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{HCh}^{tame}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}},\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}^{B\hskip-1.70717pt\operatorname{-gen}})

\displaystyle\simeq\underset{\phi,\chi}{\oplus}\hskip 2.84526pt\operatorname{Hom}_{D(G)\operatorname{-mod}}(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\phi},\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi})

\displaystyle\simeq\underset{\phi,\chi}{\oplus}\hskip 2.84526ptD(N,\psi,\operatorname{s}\hskip-1.70717pt\backslash G/N,\omega,\operatorname{s}),

i.e. of all tame Harish-Chandra bimodules with an algebroid of bi–Whittaker sheaves.

Remark 10.4.3
.

One may use Remark
9.2.10
to construct categories of
Harish-Chandra bimodules with ‘strict infinitesimal characters’. For example, if we
set

\operatorname{HCh}_{\overline{\phi},\chi}:=(\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\overline{\phi}}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi})^{G},

then if
\phi
is a regular orbit a variant of the proof of Theorem

10.4.1
shows that

\operatorname{HCh}_{\overline{\phi},\chi}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\operatorname{-mod}_{\chi}^{B}.

If
G
is of finite type, this recovers an important theorem due to
Bernstein–Gelfand
[
BG80
]
.

Remark 10.4.4
.

Suppose
\phi
and
\chi
are at levels of opposite sign, so that they correspond to intertwining operators between categories of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules at levels of the same sign.

Under this hypothesis, Theorem
10.4.1
exchanges an appropriately defined category of projective functors, i.e. Harish-Chandra bimodules built from tensoring with integrable modules for the affine Lie algebra, with the category of free-monodromic tilting bi-Whittaker sheaves. This, as well its relation to the Kazhdan–Lusztig fusion and some conjectures of Frenkel–Malikov, will be explained in a sequel to this paper.

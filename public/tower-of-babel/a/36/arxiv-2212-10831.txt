Padovan numbers which are concatenations of three repdigits
Kisan Bhoi, Prasanta Kumar Ray

Abstract
Padovan sequence is a ternary recurrent sequence defined by the recurrence relation P n + 3 = P n + 1 + P n P_{n+3}=P_{n+1}+P_{n} with initial terms P 0 = P 1 = P 2 = 1 . P_{0}=P_{1}=P_{2}=1. In this study it is shown that 114,151,200,265,351 , 465 , 616 , 816 , 3329 , 4410 , 7739 , 922111 114,151,200,265,351,465,616,816,3329,4410,7739,922111 are the only Padovan numbers which are concatenations of three repdigits.



Keywords
:Padovan numbers, concatenations, repdigits, linear forms in logarithms, reduction method.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
11B39; 11J86; 11D61.

1
Introduction

A natural number
N
is called a repdigit if it has only one distict digit in its decimal expansion. That is,
N
is of the form
N=a\left(\frac{10^{m}-1}{9}\right)=\overline{\underbrace{a\ldots a}_{m~\text{times}}}
for
0\leq a\leq 9
and
m\geq 1.
A natural number of the form

N=\overline{\underbrace{a\ldots a}_{l~\text{times}}\underbrace{b\ldots b}_{m~\text{times}}\underbrace{c\ldots c}_{k~\text{times}}}

can be viewed as concatenation of three repdigits where
a,l,m,k\geq 1
and
0\leq a,b,c\leq 9.

Let
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the Padovan sequence given by

P_{n+3}=P_{n+1}+P_{n}~\text{for all}~n\geq 1,

with
P_{0}=P_{1}=P_{2}=1.
The first few terms of this sequence are

1,1,1,2,2,3,4,5,7,9,12,16,21,28,37,49,65,86,114,151,200,265,351,\ldots.

In recent times, concatenation of repdigits in different linear recurrence sequences has been studied by many researchers. For example, Alahmadi et al.
[
1
]
studied the problem of finding all Fibonacci numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits. They examined the same problem for
k
-generalised Fibonacci sequence in
[
2
]
. Ddamulira searched all Padovan and Tribonacci numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits in
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, respectively. Batte et al.
[
3
]
proved that the only Perrin numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits are
\{10,12,17,22,29,39,51,68,90,119,277,644\}
. In
[
14
]
, Rayguru and Panda showed that
35
is the only balancing number which is concatenation
of two repdigits. Recently, Siar and Keskin
[
13
]
found that
12,13,29,33,34,70,84,88,89,228
, and
233

are the only
k
-generalized Pell numbers, which are concatenations of two repdigits with at least two digits. Erduvan and Keskin
[
9
]
, in their work obtained Lucas numbers which are concatenations of two repdigits. In
[
10
]
, the same authors looked into Lucas numbers which are concatenations of three repdigits. They proved that the only Lucas numbers which are concatenations of three repdigits are
123,199,322,521,843,2207,5778
. For the proof, they used lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms and Baker-Davenport reduction method due to Dujella and Pethő
[
8
]
.
In this paper, we find all Padovan numbers that are concatenations of three repdigits. More precisely, we completely solve the Diophantine equation

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\underbrace{a\ldots a}_{l~\text{times}}\underbrace{b\ldots b}_{m~\text{times}}\underbrace{c\ldots c}_{k~\text{times}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\underbrace{a\ldots a}_{l~\text{times}}}\cdot 10^{m+k}+\overline{\underbrace{b\ldots b}_{m~\text{times}}}\cdot 10^{k}+\overline{\underbrace{c\ldots c}_{k~\text{times}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\left(\frac{10^{l}-1}{9}\right)10^{m+k}+b\left(\frac{10^{m}-1}{9}\right)10^{k}+c\left(\frac{10^{k}-1}{9}\right),

that is,

P_{n}=\frac{1}{9}\left(a10^{l+m+k}-(a-b)10^{m+k}-(b-c)10^{k}-c\right).

(1.1)

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

The only Padovan numbers that are concatenations of three repdigits are
114,151,200,265,351,465,616,816,3329,4410,7739,922111.

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we run a program in
Mathemtica
and search all solutions of (
1.1
) with
n\leq 560
. Then, we take
n>560
and write (
1.1
) in three different ways to get three linear forms in logarithms. We apply lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms to obtain an upper bound on
n.
To reduce the bound, we use Baker-Davenport reduction method de Weger
[
16
]
. As a result, we get
n\leq 546,
a contradiction. In this way, we complete the proof of our main result.

We need some preliminary results which are discussed in the following section.

2
Preliminary Results

2.1
Some properties of Padovan sequence

The characteristic equation of
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
f(x)=x^{3}-x-1=0
which has one real root
\alpha
and two complex conjugate roots
\beta
and
\gamma
given by

\alpha=\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{6},~~\beta=\bar{\gamma}=\frac{-r_{1}-r_{2}+i\sqrt{3}(r_{1}-r_{2})}{12},

where

r_{1}=\sqrt[3]{108+12\sqrt{69}}
and
r_{2}=\sqrt[3]{108-12\sqrt{69}}
. Binet’s formula for Padovan sequence is given by

P_{n}=C_{\alpha}\alpha^{n}+C_{\beta}\beta^{n}+C_{\gamma}\gamma^{n},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}=\frac{(1-\beta)(1-\gamma)}{(\alpha-\beta)(\alpha-\gamma)}=\frac{\alpha+1}{-\alpha^{2}+3\alpha+1},

\displaystyle C_{\beta}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\gamma)}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta-\gamma)}=\frac{\beta+1}{-\beta^{2}+3\beta+1},

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}{(\gamma-\alpha)(\gamma-\beta)}=\frac{\gamma+1}{-\gamma^{2}+3\gamma+1}.

The minimal polynomial of
C_{\alpha}
over
\mathbb{Z}
is
23x^{3}-23x^{2}+6x-1
with all its zeros of unit modulus.
Numerically, we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle 1.32<\alpha<1.33,

\displaystyle 0.86<|\beta\rvert=|\gamma|=\alpha^{-1/2}<0.87,

\displaystyle 0.72<C_{\alpha}<0.73,

\displaystyle 0.24<|C_{\beta}|=|C_{\gamma}|<0.25.

Let
e(n):=P_{n}-C_{\alpha}\alpha^{n}=C_{\beta}\beta^{n}+C_{\gamma}\gamma^{n}.
Then
|e(n)|<\frac{1}{\alpha^{n/2}}
for all
n\geq 1.

Using induction, one can prove that

\alpha^{n-3}\leq P_{n}\leq\alpha^{n-1}~\text{holds for all}~n\geq 1.

(2.2)

2.2
Linear forms in Logarithms

Baker’s theory plays an important role in reducing the bounds concerning linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers.
Let
\eta
be an algebraic number with minimal primitive polynomial

\displaystyle f\left(X\right)=a_{0}x^{d}+a_{1}x^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{d}=a_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(X-\eta^{(i)})\in{\mathbb{Z}\left[X\right]},

where the leading coefficient
a_{0}>0
, and
\eta^{(i)}
’s are conjugates of
\eta
. Then, the
logarithmic height
of
\eta
is given by

\displaystyle h(\eta)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\text{max}\{0,\log|\eta^{(j)}|\}\right).

The following are some properties of the logarithmic height function (see
[
15
, Property 3.3]
), which will be used to calculate the heights of the algebraic integers

\displaystyle h(\eta+\gamma)\leq h(\eta)+h(\gamma)+\log 2,

\displaystyle h(\eta\gamma^{\pm 1})\leq h(\eta)+h(\gamma),

\displaystyle h(\eta^{k})=|k|h(\eta),k\in\mathbb{Z}.

With these notations, Matveev (see
[
12
]
or
[
4
, Theorem 9.4]
) proved the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{l}
be positive real algebraic integers in a real algebraic number field
\mathbb{L}
of degree
d_{\mathbb{L}}
and
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{l}
be non zero integers. If

\Gamma=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\eta_{i}^{b_{i}}-1
is not zero, then

\displaystyle\log|\Gamma|>-1.4\cdot 30^{l+3}l^{4.5}d_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}(1+\log d_{\mathbb{L}})(1+\log D)A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{l},

where
D=\text{max}\{|b_{1}|,|b_{2}|,\ldots,|b_{l}|\}
and

A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{l}
are positive real numbers such that

\displaystyle A_{j}\geq\text{max}\{d_{\mathbb{L}}h\left(\eta_{j}\right),|\log\eta_{j}|,0.16\}~~\text{for}~j=1,\ldots,l.

2.3
Reduction method

The following Baker-Davenport reduction method due to de Weger
[
16
]
is used to reduce the bounds of the variables which are too large.
Let
\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2},\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be given and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
be unknowns.
Suppose

\displaystyle\Lambda=\beta+x_{1}\vartheta_{1}+x_{2}\vartheta_{2}.

(2.3)

Set

X=\max\{|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\}
and
X_{0},Y
be positive.
Assume that

\displaystyle X\leq X_{0},

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle|\Lambda|<c\cdot\exp(-\delta\cdot Y),

(2.5)

where
c,\delta
be positive constants.
When
\beta\neq 0
in (
2.3
), put
\vartheta=-\vartheta_{1}/\vartheta_{2}
and
\psi=\beta/\vartheta_{2}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{\vartheta_{2}}=\psi-x_{1}\vartheta+x_{2}.

Let
p/q
be a convergent of
\vartheta
with
q>X_{0}.
For a real number
x,
we let

\|x\|=\min\{|x-n|,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be the distance from
x
to the nearest integer. We have the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
)
Suppose that

\displaystyle\|q\psi\|>\frac{2X_{0}}{q}.

Then, the solutions of (
2.5
) and (
2.4
) satisfy

\displaystyle Y<\frac{1}{\delta}\log\left(\frac{q^{2}c}{|\vartheta_{2}|X_{0}}\right).

We state and prove the following lemma which gives a relation between
n
amd
l+m+k
of (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.3
.

All solutions to the Diophantine equation (
1.1
) satisfy

(l+m+k)\log 10-3<n\log\alpha<(l+m+k)\log 10+1.

Proof.

From (
1.1
) and (
2.2
) we get

\alpha^{n-3}\leq P_{n}<10^{l+m+k}.

Taking logarithm on both sides, we have

(n-3)\log\alpha<(l+m+k)\log 10,

which leads to

n\log\alpha<(l+m+k)\log 10+3\log\alpha<(l+m+k)\log 10+1.

On the other hand, for the lower bound, (
1.1
) implies that

10^{l+m+k-1}<P_{n}\leq\alpha^{n-1}.

Taking logarithm on both sides, we get

(l+m+k-1)\log 10<(n-1)\log\alpha,

which leads to

(l+m+k-1)\log 10-3<(l+m+k-1)\log 10+\log\alpha<n\log\alpha.

∎

The following result will also be used in our proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\lvert e^{z}-1\rvert<y<\frac{1}{2}
for real values of
z
and
y
, then
\lvert z\rvert<2y
.

Proof.

If
z\geq 0,
we have
0\leq z\leq e^{z}-1=\lvert e^{z}-1\rvert<y.
If
z<0,
then
\lvert e^{z}-1\rvert<\frac{1}{2}.
From this, we get
e^{\lvert z\rvert}<2,
and therefore
0<\lvert z\rvert<e^{\lvert z\rvert}-1=e^{\lvert z\rvert}\lvert e^{z}-1\rvert<2y
. so, in both cases, we get
\lvert z\rvert<2y.

∎

The following lemma will be used in our proof. It is seen in

Lemma 2.5

and

\displaystyle L<2^{r}H(\log H)^{r}.

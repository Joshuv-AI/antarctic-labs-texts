1.
Introduction

Throughout this article
C
denotes a commutative ring with unity, unless otherwise stated and
T
a
C
-algebra. Recall that a map
D:T\rightarrow T
is called a
Jordan derivation
if it is
C
-linear and
D(a^{2})=D(a)a+aD(a)
, for all
a\in T
. This is said to be a
derivation
if
D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)
, for all
a,b\in T
and an
antiderivation
if
D(ab)=D(b)a+bD(a)
, for all
a,b\in T
. Derivations and antiderivations are the trivial examples of Jordan derivations. But not every Jordan derivation is a derivation (Example of
[
11
]
). A derivation
D:T\rightarrow T
is said to be an
inner derivation
if there exists
a_{0}\in T
such that
D(a)=a_{0}a-aa_{0}
, for all
a\in T
.

The study of Jordan derivation was initiated by Herstein in 1957. In
[
9
]
, he had shown that there is no proper Jordan derivation for a prime ring of characteristic not 2. In 1975, Cusack extended Herstein’s result in
[
7
]
. Later on, in 1988 Bre
\check{s}
ar
[
4
]
proved that every Jordan derivation from a 2-torsion free semiprime ring into itself is a derivation. Note that a ring
R
is
2-torsion free
if for
a\in R
such that
2a=0_{R}
, then
a=0_{R}
. The problem whether every Jordan derivation of a ring or algebra into itself is a derivation was discussed by many mathematicians in
[
3
,
13
,
10
]
.

Now, suppose
A
and
B
are unital algebras over
C
, and
M
a unital
(A,B)
-bimodule which is faithful as a left
A
-module and as a right
B
-module. Then the
C
-algebra

\text{Tri}(A,B,M)=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&m\\
0&b\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0pta\in A,\,b\in B,\,m\in M\right\}

under the usual matrix operations is said to be a triangular algebra. In last few years Jordan derivation over triangular algebras has invited attention of many mathematicians. In 2005, Benkovi
\check{c}

[
2
]
proved that every Jordan derivation from an upper triangular matrix algebra
\mathcal{A}
into its arbitrary bimodule
\mathcal{M}
is the sum of a derivation and an antiderivation, where
\mathcal{M}
is 2-torsion free. In 2006, Zhang and Yu
[
14
]
proved that every Jordan derivation from
U
into itself is a derivation, where
U=\text{Tri}(A,B,M)
is a triangular algebra and
C
is 2-torsion free. Note that above result is not true if
C
is not
2
-torsion free. In this connection an example of a Jordan derivation over
\text{Tri}(A,B,M)
is given which is not a derivation. The construction of the example is same as in Example 8 by Cheung in
[
6
]
, but in other perspective.

Example 1.1
.

Let
C=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
A=B=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&b\\
0&a\end{array}\right)|\kern 5.0pta,~b\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\right\}
and
M=\mathcal{T}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}_{2}})
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
, a field with two elements and
\mathcal{T}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}_{2}})
is the algebra of
2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is not a
2
-torsion free ring. Define
D:\text{Tri}(A,B,M)\rightarrow\text{Tri}(A,B,M)
as

\displaystyle D(X)=a_{34}e_{12}+a_{24}e_{13}+a_{13}e_{24}+a_{12}e_{34}~\text{for all}~X\in\text{Tri}(A,B,M),

\displaystyle\text{where}~X=a_{11}(e_{11}+e_{22})+a_{33}(e_{33}+e_{44})+a_{12}e_{12}+a_{13}e_{13}+a_{14}e_{14}+a_{24}e_{24}

\displaystyle+a_{34}e_{34},~a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Here
e_{ij}
represents the
n\times n
matrix with
1
at
(i,j)
th position and
0
elsewhere. Then
D
is a Jordan derivation over
\text{Tri}(A,B,M)
. Let
X=e_{11}+e_{13}+e_{14}+e_{22}+e_{33}+e_{34}+e_{44}
and
Y=e_{13}+e_{14}+e_{33}+e_{34}+e_{44}
. In this case,
D(XY)=0\neq e_{12}=D(X)Y+XD(Y)
. Therefore,
D
is not a derivation over
\text{Tri}(A,B,M)
.

Motivated by above, in Section 2, we prove that a Jordan derivation from the algebra of upper triangular matrices
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
into itself is a derivation, without assuming
C
to be
2
-torsion free (Theorem
2.1
). This is towards the furtherance of Theorem 2.1 of
[
14
]
. Moreover, it has been proved that derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
is an inner derivation (Theorem
2.2
). In 2007
[
8
]
, Ghosseiri proved that if
R
is a
2
-torsion free ring,
n\geq 2
, and
D
is a Jordan derivation on the upper triangular matrix ring
\mathcal{T}_{n}(R)
, then
D
is a derivation. As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
, a remark is provided on Ghosseiri’s result that Jordan derivation over
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
, where
F=\{0,1\}
is not
2
- torsion free, is a derivation (Corollary
2.1
).

In 2009
[
1
]
, Alizadeh proved that if
A
is a unital associative ring and
M
is a
2
-torsion free
A
-bimodule, then every Jordan derivation from
\mathcal{M}_{n}(A)
into
\mathcal{M}_{n}(M)
is a derivation. In Section 3, we prove that there is no such proper Jordan derivations on the full matrix algebra
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
, without assuming
C
is
2
-torsion free (Theorem
3.1
). Further, it is proved that every derivation on
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
is an inner derivation (Theorem
3.2
).

Before stating the main results, we have the following:

Let
T
be an algebra,
0_{T}
and
1_{T}
represent zero and identity of
T
respectively. Similarly
0
and
1
represent the zero and identity of
C
respectively.
e_{ij}
denotes the square matrix
(e_{ij})_{n\times n}
with
1
at
(i,j)
th position and
0
elsewhere. Let
D:T\rightarrow T
be a Jordan derivation. Then
D(x^{2})=D(x)x+xD(x)
for all
x\in T
. Replacing
x
by
x+y
, we have
D((x+y)^{2})=D(x+y)(x+y)+(x+y)D(x+y)
, which implies for all
x,y\in T
,

(1.1)

D(xy+yx)=D(x)y+xD(y)+D(y)x+yD(x).

Since
D
is additive,

(1.2)

D(0_{T})=0_{T}.

Also,
D(1_{T})=D(1_{T}^{2})=D(1_{T})1_{T}+1_{T}D(1_{T})
, therefore

(1.3)

D(1_{T})=0_{T}.

2.
Jordan Derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)

In this section, we discuss about Jordan derivation which is an inner derivation over upper triangular matrix algebras and towards this we prove the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
C
be a commutative ring with unity and
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
be an algebra of
n\times n
upper triangular matrices over
C
. Then every Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
,
n\geq 2
into itself is a derivation.

Proof.

Let
T=\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
and
D
be a Jordan derivation from
T
into itself. Let

(2.1)

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}a_{jk}^{(ii)}e_{jk}~,~\text{where}~a_{jk}^{(ii)}\in C.

Since
D
is a Jordan derivation,
D(e_{ii})=D(e_{ii}^{2})=D(e_{ii})e_{ii}+e_{ii}D(e_{ii})
. Now, by using (
2.1
) and equating the coefficients from both sides, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1i}+a_{2i}^{(ii)}e_{2i}+a_{3i}^{(ii)}e_{3i}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,i}+a_{i,i+1}^{(ii)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ii)}e_{in}

\displaystyle\text{for all}~i\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}.

Also, from (
1.3
), we have
D(1_{T})=0_{T}
. Therefore, by using (
2.2
), we have
\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{ij}^{(ii)}+a_{ij}^{(jj)})e_{ij}=0_{T}
. Hence,

(2.3)

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{(ii)}+a_{ij}^{(jj)}=0~,~\text{for}~1\leq i<j\leq n.

In order to find
D(e_{ij})
, when
1\leq i<j\leq n
, let
D(e_{ij})=\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq l\leq n}a_{kl}^{(ij)}e_{kl}
, for
a_{kl}^{(ij)}\in C
and
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Now, by applying (
1.1
) on
e_{ij}=e_{ii}e_{ij}+e_{ij}e_{ii}
, we have
D(e_{ij})=D(e_{ii})e_{ij}+e_{ii}D(e_{ij})+D(e_{ij})e_{ii}+e_{ij}D(e_{ii})
. Also, by (
2.2
), we get,

(2.4)

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})

\displaystyle=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1j}+a_{2i}^{(ii)}e_{2j}+a_{3i}^{(ii)}e_{3j}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,j}

\displaystyle+a_{1i}^{(ij)}e_{1i}+a_{2i}^{(ij)}e_{2i}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ij)}e_{i-1,i}+a_{i,i+1}^{(ij)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ij)}e_{in}.

Since
e_{ij}=e_{ij}e_{jj}+e_{jj}e_{ij}
. Therefore, by using (
1.1
) and putting the value of
D(e_{ij})
from (
2.4
) and
D(e_{jj})
from (
2.2
), we have

(2.5)

D(e_{ij})=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1j}+a_{2i}^{(ii)}e_{2j}+a_{3i}^{(ii)}e_{3j}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,j}+a_{ij}^{(ij)}e_{ij}+a_{j,j+1}^{(jj)}e_{i,j+1}+\cdots+a_{jn}^{(jj)}e_{in}.

Now from the assumption of
D(e_{ij})
and (
2.5
),

(2.6)

D(e_{ij})=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1j}+a_{2i}^{(ii)}e_{2j}+a_{3i}^{(ii)}e_{3j}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,j}+a_{ij}^{(ij)}e_{ij}+a_{i,j+1}^{(ij)}e_{i,j+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ij)}e_{in}.

Now, we establish

(2.7)

D(e_{ij}e_{kl})=D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})

for all
i,j,k,l\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
. Since (
2.7
) is equivalent to

(2.8)

D(e_{kl}e_{ij})=D(e_{kl})e_{ij}+e_{kl}D(e_{ij}).

Therefore, proof of (
2.7
) is sufficient to justify (
2.8
) and vice-versa. During the proof, we frequently use (
2.2
) and (
2.6
).

Case 1:
Let
i\neq j
. Without loss of generality, let
i>j
. Since from (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})e_{jj}+e_{ii}D(e_{jj})=0_{T}.

Therefore,
D(e_{ii}e_{jj})=D(e_{ii})e_{jj}+e_{ii}D(e_{jj})
.

Case 2:
Let
j<k
. In this case we want to establish
D(e_{ii}e_{jk})=D(e_{ii})e_{jk}+e_{ii}D(e_{jk})
for all
i,j,k\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
.

If
i<j
, then by using (
2.3
),

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})e_{jk}+e_{ii}D(e_{jk})=(a_{ij}^{(ii)}+a_{ij}^{(jj)})e_{ik}=0_{T}.

If
i=j
, then

\displaystyle D(e_{ik})e_{ii}+e_{ik}D(e_{ii})=0_{T}.

If
i>j
, then

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})e_{jk}+e_{ii}D(e_{jk})=0_{T}.

Case 3:
Let
i<j
and
k<l
. Now, we prove
D(e_{ij}e_{kl})=D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})
for all
i,j,k,l\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
.

For
j<k
,

\displaystyle D(e_{kl})e_{ij}+e_{kl}D(e_{ij})=0_{T}.

If
j=k
, then

\displaystyle D(e_{kl})e_{ik}+e_{kl}D(e_{ik})=0_{T}.

Also, for
j>k
,

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})=0_{T}.

Now to prove
D
is a derivation on
T
, let
A
,
B

\in T
, where
A=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}A_{ij}e_{ij}
and
B=\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq l\leq n}B_{kl}e_{kl}
for some
A_{ij},B_{kl}\in C
. Since
D
is a Jordan derivation on
T
and a derivation on
e_{ij}
’s. Hence

\displaystyle D(A)B+AD(B)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq l\leq n}A_{ij}B_{kl}[D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq l\leq n}A_{ij}B_{kl}D(e_{ij}e_{kl})

\displaystyle=D(\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq l\leq n}C_{il}e_{il})

\displaystyle=D(AB),

where
C_{il}=\sum_{j=i}^{l}A_{ij}B_{jl}
. Thus,
D
is a derivation on
T
.
∎

Now as a corollary, we describe Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
, where
n\geq 2
is a positive integer and
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
is considered as a ring. In this case, we relax the linearity condition of the map.

Corollary 2.1
.

If
F
is a field with two elements, then every Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
,
n\geq 2
, into itself is a derivation.

Proof.

Let
D
is a Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
. Since
F=\{0,1\}
,
D
is
F
-linear. Hence
D
is a derivation by Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Every derivation over
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C),~n\geq 2,
is an inner derivation.

Proof.

Let
D
be a derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
. Since every derivation is a Jordan derivation, all the identities in the proof of Theorem
2.1
hold.

Let
i<j<k
. Since
e_{ik}=e_{ij}e_{jk}
and
D
is a derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
, we have

D(e_{ik})=D(e_{ij})e_{jk}+e_{ij}D(e_{jk})
. By (
2.6
), equating the coefficient of
e_{ik}
from both sides,

(2.9)

a_{ik}^{(ik)}=a_{ij}^{(ij)}+a_{jk}^{(jk)}.

Let
X=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}x_{jk}e_{jk}
, where
x_{jk}\in C
. Now, by using (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.6
) and (
2.9
),
D(X)=BX-XB
, where

\displaystyle B=\sum_{l=2}^{n}(-a_{1l}^{(1l)})e_{ll}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}^{(jj)}e_{ij}.

So,
D
is an inner derivation.

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
and
2.2
, we have the following:

Corollary 2.2
.

Every Jordan derivation over
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C),~n\geq 2,
is an inner derivation.

Let
D
be a Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
. The question is whether there exists a unique
B\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
so that
D(X)=BX-XB
, for all
X\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(C)
. The answer is given by the following example.

Example 2.3
.

Define
D:\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by

D\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}\\
0&x_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{12}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\\
~\text{where}~x_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z},~\text{the ring of integers}.

By easy computation,
D
is a Jordan derivation. Also, by Corollary
2.2
,
D
is inner. Note that
D(X)=BX-XB
for
B=e_{11}
or
B=-e_{22}
. Hence, we have more than one choices in this case for
B
.

3.

\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C
be a commutative ring with unity and
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
be the full matrix algebra over
C
. Then every Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
,
n\geq 2
into itself is a derivation.

Proof.

Let
T=\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
,
D
be a Jordan derivation from
T
into itself. Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{kl}^{(ii)}e_{kl}~,~\text{for}~a_{kl}^{(ii)}\in C.

Since
D
is a Jordan derivation,
D(e_{ii})=D(e_{ii}^{2})=D(e_{ii})e_{ii}+e_{ii}D(e_{ii})

3.1
) and equating the coefficients from both sides, we obtain,

(3.2)

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})

\displaystyle=a_{i1}^{(ii)}e_{i1}+\cdots+a_{i,i-1}^{(ii)}e_{i,i-1}+a_{i,i+1}^{(ii)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ii)}e_{in}

Also,

(3.3)

\displaystyle D(1_{T})=0_{T}

\displaystyle\implies\mathop{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}}_{k\neq l}(a_{kl}^{(kk)}+a_{kl}^{(ll)})e_{kl}=0_{T}

In order to find

(3.7)

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})

\displaystyle=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1j}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,j}+a_{i+1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i+1,j}+\cdots+a_{ni}^{(ii)}e_{nj}

\displaystyle+a_{i1}^{(ij)}e_{i1}+\cdots+a_{i,i-1}^{(ij)}e_{i,i-1}+a_{ii}^{(ij)}e_{ii}+a_{i,i+1}^{(ij)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ij)}e_{in}

\displaystyle+a_{1i}^{(ij)}e_{1i}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ij)}e_{i-1,i}+a_{ii}^{(ij)}e_{ii}+a_{i+1,i}^{(ij)}e_{i+1,i}+\cdots+a_{ni}^{(ij)}e_{ni}

Now, from (

\displaystyle a_{ii}^{(ij)}=2a_{ii}^{(ij)}+a_{ji}^{(ii)}.

Therefore, from (

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})

\displaystyle=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1j}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,j}+a_{i+1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i+1,j}+\cdots+a_{ni}^{(ii)}e_{nj}

) and vice-versa. During the proof, we frequently use (
3.2
) and (
3.11
).

Case 1:
Let
i\neq j
. Since from (
3.2
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})e_{jj}+e_{ii}D(e_{jj})=(a_{ij}^{(ii)}+a_{ij}^{(jj)})e_{ij}=0_{T}.

Therefore,
D(e_{ii}e_{jj})=D(e_{ii})e_{jj}+e_{ii}D(e_{jj})
.

Case 2:
Let
j\neq k
. In this case we establish
D(e_{ii}e_{jk})=D(e_{ii})e_{jk}+e_{ii}D(e_{jk})
for all
i,j,k\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
.

If
i\neq j
, then by using (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.11
),

\displaystyle D(e_{ii})e_{jk}+e_{ii}D(e_{jk})=(a_{ij}^{(ii)}+a_{ij}^{(jj)})e_{ik}=0_{T}.

If
i=j
, then by using (
3.14
),

\displaystyle D(e_{ik})e_{ii}+e_{ik}D(e_{ii})=(a_{ii}^{(ik)}+a_{ki}^{(ii)})e_{ii}=0_{T}.

Case 3:
Let
i\neq j
and
k\neq l
. Now, our goal is to prove
D(e_{ij}e_{kl})=D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})
for all
i,j,k,l\in\{1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
.

Let
j\neq k
. Then by Case 2 and (
1.1
),

(3.17)

D(e_{ij}e_{kk})=0_{T}\implies D(e_{ij})e_{kk}+e_{ij}D(e_{kk})=0_{T}\implies a_{ik}^{(ij)}+a_{jk}^{(kk)}=0.

Also, by using (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})e_{kl}+e_{ij}D(e_{kl})=(a_{ik}^{(ij)}+a_{jk}^{(kk)})e_{il}=0_{T}.

Let
j=k
and
i\neq l
. Replacing
(i,j,k)
by
(j,l,i)
in (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle D(e_{jl})e_{ij}+e_{jl}D(e_{ij})=(a_{ji}^{(jl)}+a_{li}^{(ii)})e_{jj}=0_{T}.

Let
j=k
and
i=l
. Interchanging
i
and
j
in (
3.12
), we get

\displaystyle D(e_{ij})e_{ji}+e_{ij}D(e_{ji})

\displaystyle=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1i}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,i}+a_{i+1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i+1,i}+\cdots+a_{ni}^{(ii)}e_{ni}

\displaystyle+a_{j1}^{(ji)}e_{i1}+\cdots+a_{j,i-1}^{(ji)}e_{i,i-1}+a_{j,i+1}^{(ji)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{jn}^{(ji)}e_{in}

\displaystyle=a_{1i}^{(ii)}e_{1i}+\cdots+a_{i-1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i-1,i}+a_{i+1,i}^{(ii)}e_{i+1,i}+\cdots+a_{ni}^{(ii)}e_{ni}

\displaystyle+a_{i1}^{(ii)}e_{i1}+\cdots+a_{i,i-1}^{(ii)}e_{i,i-1}+a_{i,i+1}^{(ii)}e_{i,i+1}+\cdots+a_{in}^{(ii)}e_{in}

. Thus,

.
∎

To find an example of Jordan derivation over
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
, we get only inner derivation. Towards this, we have the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
C
be a commutative ring with unity and
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
be the algebra of all
n\times n
matrices over
C
. Then every derivation of
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C),~n\geq 2
is an inner derivation.

Proof.

Let
D
be a derivation on
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
. Since every derivation is a Jordan derivation, all the identities in proof of the Theorem
3.1
hold.

Let
i\neq j\neq k
. Since
e_{ik}=e_{ij}e_{jk}
and
D
is a derivation on
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
, we have

D(e_{ik})=D(e_{ij})e_{jk}+e_{ij}D(e_{jk})
. By (
3.11
), equating the coefficient of
e_{ik}
from both sides,

(3.18)

a_{ik}^{(ik)}=a_{ij}^{(ij)}+a_{jk}^{(jk)}.

For
i\neq j
, from
e_{ii}=e_{ij}e_{ji}
, we have

D(e_{ii})=D(e_{ij})e_{ji}+e_{ij}D(e_{ji})
. By (
3.2
) and (
3.11
), equating the coefficient of
e_{ii}
from both sides,

(3.19)

a_{ij}^{(ij)}+a_{ji}^{(ji)}=0.

Let
X=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}x_{kl}e_{kl}
, where
x_{kl}\in C
. Now, by using (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.11
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
),
D(X)=BX-XB
, where

\displaystyle B=\sum_{l=2}^{n}(-a_{1l}^{(1l)})e_{ll}+\mathop{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}}_{i\neq j}a_{ij}^{(jj)}e_{ij}.

Thus,
D
is an inner derivation.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.1
.

Every Jordan derivation over
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C),~n\geq 2
is an inner derivation.

Let
D
be a Jordan derivation on
\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
. The question is whether there exists a unique
B\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
such that
D(X)=BX-XB
, for all
X\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(C)
. The answer is given by the following example.

Example 3.3
.

Define
D:\mathcal{M}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
by

D\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}&x_{14}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}&x_{24}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}&x_{34}\\
x_{41}&x_{42}&x_{43}&x_{44}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{12}&-x_{12}+x_{13}&x_{14}\\
-x_{21}+x_{31}&x_{32}&-x_{22}+x_{33}&x_{34}\\
-x_{31}&0&-x_{32}&0\\
-x_{41}&0&-x_{42}&0\end{pmatrix},\\
~\text{where}~x_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then it is easy to see that
D
is a Jordan derivation. By Corollary
3.1
,
D
is an inner derivation. Moreover,
D(X)=BX-XB
, for
B=e_{11}+e_{23}
or
B=2e_{11}+e_{22}+e_{33}+e_{44}+e_{23}
. Therefore, for
B
, we have multiple choices.

Some Identities of Ramanujan’s q-Continued Fractions of Order Fourteen and Twenty-Eight, and Vanishing Coefficients
Shraddha Rajkhowa and Nipen Saikia

Abstract




Abstract:
We deduce
q
-continued fractions
S_{1}(q)
,
S_{2}(q)
and
S_{3}(q)
of order fourteen, and continued fractions
V_{1}(q)
,
V_{2}(q)
and
V_{3}(q)
of order twenty-eight from a general continued fraction identity of Ramanujan. We establish some theta-function identities for the continued fractions and derive some colour partition identities as applications. Some vanishing coefficients results arising from the continued fractions are also offered.

Keywords and phrases:

q
-continued fraction; theta-functions; colour partition of integer; vanishing coefficient.

1.
Introduction

For any complex numbers
\lambda
and
q
, define the
q
-product
(\lambda;q)_{\infty}
as

\left(\lambda;q\right)_{\infty}:=\prod_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\lambda q^{t}\right),\qquad|q|<1.

(1.1)

For brevity, we often write

(\lambda_{1};q)_{\infty}(\lambda_{2};q)_{\infty}(\lambda_{3};q)_{\infty}\cdots(\lambda_{m};q)_{\infty}=\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots,\lambda_{m};q\right)_{\infty}.

The Ramanujan’s general theta-function
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

[
2
, p. 34]
is defined as

\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathfrak{a}^{{t(t+1)/2}}\mathfrak{b}^{{t(t-1)/2}},\qquad|\mathfrak{ab}|<1.

(1.2)

In terms of
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
, Jacobi’s triple product identity
[
2
, p. 35, Entry 19]
can be stated as

\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=(-\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{ab})_{\infty}(-\mathfrak{b};\mathfrak{ab})_{\infty}(\mathfrak{ab};\mathfrak{ab})_{\infty}=(-\mathfrak{a},-\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{ab};\mathfrak{ab})_{\infty}.

(1.3)

Three useful special cases of
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
are the theta-functions
\phi(q)
,
\psi(q)
and
f(-q)

[
2
, p. 36, Entry 22(i)-(iii)]
given by

\displaystyle\phi(q)

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{f}(q,q)=\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{t^{2}}=\frac{(-q;-q)_{\infty}}{(q;-q)_{\infty}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\psi(q)

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{f}(q,q^{3})=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}q^{t(t+1)/2}=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}},

(1.5)

\displaystyle f(-q)

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{f}(-q,-q^{2})=\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{t}q^{t(3t-1)/2}=(q;q)_{\infty}.

(1.6)

Ramanujan also defined the function
\chi(q)

[
2
, p. 36, Entry 22(iv)]
as

\chi(q)=(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}.

(1.7)

One of the Ramanujan’s remarkable contribution is in the field of
q
-continued fractions. Ramanujan recorded many continued fractions in his notebooks and most famous among is the Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction
R(q)
defined by

R(q):=q^{1/5}\frac{(q,q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}}{(q^{2},q^{3};q^{5})_{\infty}}=q^{1/5}\frac{\mathfrak{f}\left(-q,-q^{4}\right)}{\mathfrak{f}\left(-q^{2},-q^{3}\right)}=\frac{q^{1/5}}{1+\dfrac{q}{1+\dfrac{q^{2}}{1+\dfrac{q^{3}}{1+\cdots}}}},\qquad|q|<1.

(1.8)

The Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction
R(q)
is often referred as the continued fraction of order five. Ramanujan also offered some theta-function identities and modular relations for the continued fraction
R(q)
. An account of these can be found in
[
3
]
. Ramanujan also recorded some general continued fraction identities in his notebook. For example, Ramanujan recorded the following general continued fraction identity
[
2
, p. 24, Entry 12]
:
Suppose that
a
,
b
and
q
are complex numbers with
|ab|<1
and
|q|<1
, or that
a=b^{2m+1}
for some integer
m
. Then

\frac{(a^{2}q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}(b^{2}q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(a^{2}q;q^{4})_{\infty}(b^{2}q;q^{4})_{\infty}}=\dfrac{1}{1-ab+\dfrac{(a-bq)(b-aq)}{(1-ab)(q^{2}+1)+\dfrac{(a-bq^{3})(b-aq^{3})}{(1-ab)(q^{4}+1)+\cdots}}}.

(1.9)

By specialising the values of
a
and
b
, and taking suitable powers of
q
, one can obtain
q
-continued fractions of particular order which satisfy theta-function identities analogous to those of
R(q)
.

In this paper, we deal with the
q
-continued fractions of order fourteen and twenty-eight. By replacing

in (

\hskip-62.59596pt=\dfrac{q^{3}(1-q)}{(1-q^{7})+\dfrac{q^{7}(1-q^{6})(1-q^{8})}{(1-q^{7})(1+q^{14})+\dfrac{q^{7}(1-q^{20})(1-q^{22})}{(1-q^{7})(1+q^{28})+\cdots}}},

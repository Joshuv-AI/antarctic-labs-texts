Study of fractional evolution equations involving Hilfer fractional derivative of order 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 and type 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1 0\leq\delta\leq 1


Abstract
. In this paper we investigate fractional differential equations with Hilfer fractional derivative of order 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 and type δ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \delta\in[0,1] in a Banach space. We introduce a family of general fractional cosine operator functions of order 1 < γ < 2 1<\gamma<2 and type δ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \delta\in[0,1] and discuss their properties to give a suitable definition of mild solution of the semilinear evolutin equation. In last section an example is presented. MSC 2010: 34G10, 34A08, 34G20, 34A12. Keywords: Hilfer fractional derivative, strong solution, mild solution, solution operator, fixed point theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic definitions related to fractional derivatives and integrals and some results of measure of non-compactness.

Definition 1
.

The Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\gamma>0
is defined by

J_{t}^{\gamma}\omega(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}\omega(s)ds,\;t>0.

(2.1)

Definition 2
.

Let
\gamma>0,m=[\gamma]
and
\delta\in[0,1]
. The Hilfer fractional derivative of order
\gamma
and type
\delta
is defined by

D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega(t)=J_{t}^{\delta(m-\gamma)}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(m-\gamma)}\omega(t),\;t>0.

(2.2)

For
\delta=0
, it gives the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative and for
\delta=1
, it gives the Caputo fractional derivative.

2.1.
Measure of Non-compactness

For any bounded subset
\mathfrak{B}
of the Banach space
X
, the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness
\psi
is defined by

\psi(\mathfrak{B})=\inf\bigg\{\epsilon>0:\mathfrak{B}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(x_{j})\>\mbox{where}\>x_{j}\in X,k\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\},

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(x_{j})
is a ball of radius
\leq\epsilon
centered
at
x_{j},j=1,2,\ldots,k
.

The another measure of noncompactness
\phi
by introduced Kurtawoski for bounded subset
\mathfrak{B}

of
X
is given as

\phi(\mathfrak{B})=\inf\bigg\{\epsilon>0:\mathfrak{B}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}M_{j}\>\mbox{and}\>diam(M_{j})\leq\epsilon\bigg\},

(2.4)

where
diam(M_{j})
is the diameter of
M_{j}
.

The well known properties are:-

(i)

\psi(\mathfrak{B})=0\Longleftrightarrow\mathfrak{B}
is relatively compact in
X
;

(ii)

For any bounded subsets
\mathfrak{B}_{1},\mathfrak{B}_{2}
of
X
,
\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{1})\leq\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{2})
if
\mathfrak{B}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{B}_{2}
;

(iii)

\psi(\{x\}\cup\mathfrak{B})=\psi(\mathfrak{B})
;

(iv)

\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{B}_{2})\leq\max\{\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{1}),\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{2})\}
;

(v)

\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{1}+\mathfrak{B}_{2})\leq\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{1})+\psi(\mathfrak{B}_{2})
, where

\mathfrak{B}_{1}+\mathfrak{B}_{2}=\{x+y:x\in\mathfrak{B}_{1},y\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}\}
;

(vi)

\psi(r\mathfrak{B})\leq|r|\psi(\mathfrak{B}),

r\in\mathbb{R}
.

For any
\Phi\subset C(I,X)
, we define

\Phi(s)=\big\{\omega(s)\in X:\omega\in\Phi\big\}\;\mbox{and}\;\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s)ds=\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\omega(s)ds:\omega\in\Phi\bigg\},\mbox{for}\;t\in I.

Proposition 1
.

[
8
]

If
\Phi\subset C(I,X)
is equicontinuous and bounded, then
t\rightarrow\psi(\Phi(t))
is continuous on
I
, and

\psi\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s)ds\bigg)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\psi(\Phi(s))ds\;\mbox{for}\;t\in I,\;\psi(\Phi)=\max_{s\in I}\psi(\Phi(s)).

Proposition 2
.

[
9
]

Let
\{\omega_{n}:I\rightarrow X\}
be a sequence of Bochner integrable functions with
\left\|\omega_{n}(t)\right\|\leq m(t)
a.e.
t\in I
and every
n\geq 1
, where
m\in L^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Then the function

\phi(t)=\psi(\{\omega_{n}(t)\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
belongs to

L^{1}(I,\mathbb{R}^{+})
and

\psi\bigg(\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{n}(\tau)d\tau:n\geq 1\bigg\}\bigg)\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(\tau)d\tau.

Proposition 3
.

[
11
]

If
\Phi
is bounded, then for
\epsilon>0
, there is a sequence

\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\Phi
satisfying

\psi(\Phi)\leq 2\psi(\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})+\epsilon.

3.
Linear Problem

We consider a real valued linear FDE

D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega(t)=c\omega(t)+\eta(t),\;t>0

(3.1)

(g_{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}*\omega)(0)=x,(g_{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}*\omega)^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=y,

(3.2)

where
c
is a real constant,
\eta:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g_{\gamma}(t)=\frac{t^{\gamma}-1}{\Gamma(\gamma)},\gamma>0
.

The next two Lemmas give the insight about the structure of solution.

Lemma 1
.

If
f\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, then

\omega(t)=t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}(ct^{\gamma})x+t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})y+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds

is solution of the problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

Proof.

We define

\omega_{1}(t)=t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}(ct^{\gamma})x+t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})y
and

\omega_{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds.

Then

\displaystyle J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma((1-\delta)(2-\gamma))}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)-1}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(\tau-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)dsd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma((1-\delta)(2-\gamma))}\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)-1}(\tau-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(\tau-s)^{\gamma})d\tau ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,2-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega_{2}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,2-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}u^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(cu^{\gamma})\eta(t-u)du.

(3.3)

Now using (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega_{2}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}u^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(cu^{\gamma})\eta(t-u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}u^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(cu^{\gamma})f^{\prime}(t-u)du+t^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})\eta(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-u)^{\gamma})f^{\prime}(\omega)du+t^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})\eta(0).

Then as per definition

\displaystyle D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega_{2}(t)=J_{t}^{\delta(2-\gamma)}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\eta(t)-E_{\gamma}(ct^{\gamma})\eta(0)+c\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-u)^{\gamma})\eta(\omega)du+E_{\gamma}(ct^{\gamma})\eta(0)

\displaystyle=\eta(t)+c\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-u)^{\gamma})\eta(\omega)du.

Hence,

\displaystyle D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega_{1}(t)+D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}(ct^{\gamma})x+t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})y

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-u)^{\gamma})\eta(\omega)du)+\eta(t)=c\omega(t)+\eta(t),t\in(0,T].

Clearly,

\displaystyle J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega(t)

\displaystyle=E_{\gamma,1}(ct^{\gamma})x+tE_{\gamma,2}(ct^{\gamma})y+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,2-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds\rightarrow x

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J_{t}^{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}\omega(t)

\displaystyle=ct^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(ct^{\gamma})x+E_{\gamma,1}(ct^{\gamma})y+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds\rightarrow y

as
t\rightarrow 0,
by dominated convergence theorem.

Hence
\omega(t)=t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}(ct^{\gamma})x+t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)}(ct^{\gamma})y+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds
is the solution of the problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 2

\omega_{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds.

Then using (
3

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{t}^{\delta(2-\gamma)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\delta(2-\gamma)}E_{\gamma,1-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{t}^{\delta(2-\gamma)}\frac{d}{dt}I.

Using Theorem

]

\displaystyle\frac{dI}{dt}=c\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma-\delta(2-\gamma)-1}E_{\gamma,\gamma-\delta(2-\gamma)}(c(t-s)^{\gamma})\eta(s)ds+\frac{d}{dt}J_{t}^{1-\delta(2-\gamma)}\eta(t)

(3.4)

4.
Linear Abstract Hilfer fractional Cauchy problem

Now, in this section we discuss the following type of Cauchy problem

D_{0}^{\gamma,\delta}\omega(t)=\mathcal{A}\omega(t)

(4.1)

(g_{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}*\omega)(0)=x,(g_{(1-\delta)(2-\gamma)}*\omega)^{{}^{\prime}}(0)=0

(4.2)

in a Banach space
X
, where
\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset X\rightarrow X
is a linear densely defined operator on
X
.

Next, we generalize the properties of
\{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}E_{\gamma,\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1}(ct^{\gamma})\}_{t>0}
to introduce a family of bounded linear operators.

Definition 3
.

We define general fractional cosine operator functions
\{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\}_{t>0}
of order
\gamma\in(1,2)
and type
0\leq\delta\leq 1\;
as a family of bounded linear operators such that

(i)

C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)
is strongly continuous;

(ii)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x}{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}=\frac{x}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}
;

(iii)

C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)=C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)
for all
t,s>0
.

(iv)

\displaystyle C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)-J_{s}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)=\frac{s^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)-

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}J_{s}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(s),\;\;t,s>0.

(4.3)

Definition 4
.

The generator of a general fractional cosine operator function of order
1<\gamma<2
and type
\delta\in[0,1]
on Banach space
X
as a linear operator with domain
D(\mathcal{A})=\bigg\{x\in X:\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\bigg(\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x-\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}x}{t^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}\bigg)\;\mbox{exists}\bigg\}
and defined as

\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset X\rightarrow X
with

\mathcal{A}x=\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\bigg(\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x-\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}x}{t^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}\bigg).

Proposition 4
.

Let
\{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\}_{t>0}
be a general fractional cosine operator function of order
1<\gamma<2
and type
\delta\in[0,1]
and
\mathcal{A}
being its generator, then

a)

C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)
maps
D(\mathcal{A})
to
D(\mathcal{A})
and
C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\mathcal{A}x=\mathcal{A}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x,\;\;x\in D(\mathcal{A});

b)

\forall x\in X,t>0\;J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x\in D(\mathcal{A})
and

C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x=\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}x+\mathcal{A}J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x;

c)

For
x\in D(\mathcal{A})

C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x=\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}x+J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\mathcal{A}x;

d)

\mathcal{A}
is a closed and densely defined operator.

e)

\mathcal{A}
corresponds to at most one general fractional cosine operator function of order
1<\gamma<2
and type
\delta\in[0,1]
.

f)

C_{\gamma,\delta}(\cdot)x\in C^{1}((0,\infty);X)
if
x\in D(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

a)

For
x\in D(\mathcal{A})
and all
t,s>0
, we have

\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x-\frac{s^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x}{t^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}=\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\bigg(C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)x-\frac{s^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}x\bigg)}{t^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}.

Since
C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)
is bounded linear operator, we have

\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\frac{C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x-\frac{s^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x}{t^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}=C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\mathcal{A}x.

Hence
C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x\in D(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{A}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x=C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)\mathcal{A}x.

b)

Let
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\frac{\bigg(C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)-\frac{s^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}\bigg)J_{t}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x}{s^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\frac{J_{s}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)\bigg(C_{\gamma,\delta}(t)x-\frac{t^{\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}x}{\Gamma(\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)}\bigg)}{s^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}

(4.4)

Now we claim that
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\frac{J_{s}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)x}{s^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}=x.

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\Gamma(2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-1)\frac{J_{s}^{\gamma}C_{\gamma,\delta}(s)x}{s^{2\gamma+\delta(2-\gamma)-2}}-x\bigg\rVert

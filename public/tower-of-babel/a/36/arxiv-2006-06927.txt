Classical inequalities for pseudo-integral


Abstract
In this paper, we have derived certain classical inequalities, namely, Young’s, Hölder’s, Minkowski’s and Hermite-Hadamard inequalities for pseudo-integral (also known as g g -integral). For Young’s, Hölder’s, Minkowski’s inequalities, both the cases p > 1 p>1 as well as p < 1 , p ≠ 0 p<1,\,p\neq 0 have been covered. Moreover, in the case of Hermite-Hadamard inequality, a refinement has also been proved and as a special case, g g -analogue of geometric-logarithmic-arithmetic inequality has been deduced.

2
The Pseudo Setting

In this section, we collect some basic algebraic operations, elementary functions, derivative and integral in the framework of pseudo algebra. Most of these notions are already known, however, we define absolute value and logarithm formally here.

2.1
The algebraic operations

Let
\oplus
denote the pseudo-addition, a function
\oplus:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which is commutative, nondecreasing in each component, associative and has a zero element. We shall assume that
\oplus
is strict pseudo-addition which means that
\oplus
is strictly increasing in each component and continuous. By Aczel’s Theorem
[
6
]
, for each strict pseudo-addition, there exists a monotone function
g
(called the generator for
\oplus
),
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

x\oplus y=g^{-1}\left(g(x)+g(y)\right).

Similarly, we denote by
\otimes
, the pseudo-multiplication which is a function
\oplus:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which is commutative, nondecreasing in each component, associative and has a unit element. The operation
\otimes
is defined by

x\otimes y=g^{-1}\left(g(x)\cdot g(y)\right).

Now onwards, the set
\mathbb{R}
equipped with the pseudo operations
\oplus
and
\otimes
with the corresponding generator
g
will be denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{g}
. The zero and the unit elements of
\mathbb{R}_{g}
will be denoted, respectively, by
0_{g}
and
1_{g}
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors considered that the generator function
g:\mathbb{R}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly monotone (either strictly increasing or strictly decreasing), onto,
g(0_{g})=0
,
g^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, for all
x
,
g\in C^{2}
and
g^{-1}\in C^{2}
. Using this map, the following well defined operations have been defined:

x\ominus y=g^{-1}\left(g(x)-g(y)\right),\quad x\otimes^{-1}y=g^{-1}\left(\frac{g(x)}{g(y)}\right),\,\,{\rm provided}\,\,y\neq 0_{g}.

The order relation in
\mathbb{R}_{g}
, denoted by
\leq_{g}
, satisfies the following:

x\leq_{g}y\iff x\ominus y\leq 0_{g}.

If
x\leq_{g}y
, we can also write it as
y\geq_{g}x
. If
x\leq_{g}y
and
x\neq y
, we shall write it as
x<_{g}y
, or equivalently,
y>_{g}x
.

In order to make
\mathbb{R}_{g}
a linear space over the field
\mathbb{R}
, we define the pseudo-scalar product:

n\odot x=g^{-1}(ng(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{g},\,\,n\in\mathbb{R}.

It was pointed out in
[
2
]
that the operations
\odot
and
\otimes
are different. There was a need to define the scalar product
\odot
since the compatibility condition
1\odot x=x
is not satisfied by
\otimes
.

Remark 2.1
.

(\mathbb{R}_{g},\oplus,\otimes,\leq_{g})
is an ordered and complete algebra.

2.2
Differentiation and integration

The pseudo-derivative or more commonly called
g
-derivative of a suitable function
f:[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
is defined by

D^{\oplus}f(x):=\frac{d^{\oplus}f(x)}{dx}=g^{-1}\left((g\circ f)^{\prime}(x)\right).

In
[
7
]
(see also
[
2
]
), a more general
g
-derivative was defined as

D_{g}f(x):=\frac{d_{g}f(x)}{dx}=\lim_{h\to 0}\left[f(x\oplus h)\ominus f(x)\right]\otimes^{-1}h

and it was shown that

D_{g}f(x)=g^{-1}\Big((g\circ f)^{\prime}(x)/g^{\prime}(x)\Big).

The pseudo-integral or the
g
-integral of a suitable function
f:[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
is defined by

\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(t)\otimes dt=g^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)\,dx\right).

(2.1)

Similar to the notion of the
g
-derivative, in
[
7
]
, a more general
g
-integral was defined which is given by

\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f(t)\otimes dt=g^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\right).

Remark 2.2
.

The
g
-derivative
D^{\oplus}
and the
g
-integral
\displaystyle\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}
can be obtained as special cases of, respectively,
D_{g}
and
\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}
. Since the justification requires notions which are beyond the scope of this paper, we refer to
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
for details. Unless specified otherwise, in this paper, the
g
-derivative and
g
-integral will be used as represented by
D_{g}
and
\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}
, respectively.

Some of the properties of
g
-integral are mentioned below:

(a)

\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}(f\oplus h)\otimes dt=\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f\otimes dt\oplus\int^{g}_{[a,b]}h\otimes dt

(b)

\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}(\lambda\otimes f)\otimes dt=\lambda\otimes\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f\otimes dt

(c)

\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}(\lambda\odot f)\otimes dt=\lambda\odot\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f\otimes dt

(d)

\displaystyle f\leq_{g}h\Rightarrow\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f\otimes dt\leq_{g}\int^{g}_{[a,b]}h\otimes dt

2.3
The exponent

Define the set

\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}:0_{g}\leq_{g}x\}.

In view of the operation
\otimes
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

x^{(n)}:=\underbrace{x\otimes x\otimes\ldots\otimes x}_{n-{\rm times}}=g^{-1}(g^{n}(x)).

This notion of exponent can be extended for a general
p\in(0,\infty)
which is defined (see
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
) for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
as

x^{(p)}=g^{-1}(g^{p}(x)).

It can further be generalized to cover negative powers as well: For
p\in(0,\infty)
, we define

x^{(-p)}=1_{g}\otimes^{-1}x^{(p)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}.

For
p=0
, we define
x^{(0)}=1_{g}
.
It is easy to check that for
p,q\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
, the following laws of exponent hold:

(i)

x^{(p)}\otimes x^{(q)}=x^{(p+q)}

(ii)

\left(x^{(p)}\right)^{(q)}=x^{(pq)}

(iii)

(x\otimes y)^{(p)}=x^{(p)}\otimes y^{(p)}

(iv)

(x\otimes^{-1}y)^{(p)}=x^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}y^{(p)}

(v)

(\alpha\odot x)^{(p)}=\alpha^{p}\odot x^{(p)}

2.4
Absolute value

For
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
, we define its
g
-absolute value as follows:

|x|_{g}:=\begin{cases}x,&{\rm if}\,0_{g}\leq_{g}x\\
-x,&{\rm if}\,x<_{g}0_{g}.\end{cases}

It can be seem that

|x|_{g}=g^{-1}\left(|g(x)|\right).

Note that

|x\oplus y|_{g}\leq_{g}|x|_{g}\oplus|y|_{g}

if
g
is increasing and the inequality is reversed if
g
is decreasing.

2.5
Exponential and logarithm functions

The
g
-exponential function for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
as defined in
[
2
]
is given by

E^{(x)}=g^{-1}(e^{g(x)}),

where
e^{g(x)}
is the standard exponential function. It is natural to define
g
-logarithm function by

{\rm Ln\,}x=g^{-1}\left(\ln{g(x)}\right),

where
\ln g(x)
is the standard logarithm function.

Remark 2.3
.

Unlike in the standard case, we may have that
E^{(x)}<_{g}0_{g}
. In fact, if the generator
g
is monotonically decreasing, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
,
E^{(x)}\leq_{g}0
, since
g^{-1}
is also monotonically decreasing. This suggests that in the pseudo case, logarithm can be defined for ”negative” numbers, which in fact is true if, again, the generator
g
is decreasing.

Through the following proposition, we provide several properties of
E^{(x)}
and
{\rm Ln\,}x
, the proofs of which can be worked out easily.

Proposition 2.4
.

The following hold:

(i)

D_{g}(E^{(x)})=E^{(x)}

(ii)

\displaystyle\int^{g}E^{(x)}\otimes dx=E^{(x)}

(iii)

E^{(x)}\otimes E^{(y)}=E^{(x\oplus y)}

(iv)

E^{({\rm Ln\,}x)}=x

(v)

{\rm Ln\,}E^{(x)}=x

(vi)

{\rm Ln\,}(x\otimes y)={\rm Ln\,}x\oplus{\rm Ln\,}y

3
Inequalities

In this section, we shall prove
g
-analogue of Young’s, Hölder’s and Minkowski’s inequalities. Here and throughout, for any
p\in\mathbb{R},\,p\neq 0
,
p^{\prime}
will denote the conjugate index to
p
, i.e.,
{1\over p}+{1\over{p^{\prime}}}=1
.

3.1
Young’s inequality

The classical Young’s inequality asserts that for
1<p<\infty
and
a,b>0
, it holds:

ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}

whereas the inequality gets reversed if
p<1,\,p\neq 0
. We prove the
g
-analogue of this inequality below:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.

(a)

If the generator
g
is increasing then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
, the following Young’s type inequality holds:

a\otimes b\leq_{g}\left(a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)\right)\oplus\left(b^{(p^{\prime})}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p^{\prime})\right).

(3.1)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
, the inequality (
3.1
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a) In view of Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle a\otimes b

\displaystyle=E^{{\rm Ln\,}(a\otimes b)}

\displaystyle=E^{({\rm Ln\,}a\oplus{\rm Ln\,}b)}.

(3.2)

Note that for any
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle{\rm Ln\,}a^{(p)}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left(\ln g^{p}(a)\right)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(p\ln g(a))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\Big(g(g^{-1}(p))\cdot g(g^{-1}(\ln g(a)))\Big)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(p)\otimes g^{-1}(\ln g(a))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(p)\otimes{\rm Ln\,}a

which gives that

{\rm Ln\,}a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)={\rm Ln\,}a.

Thus

\displaystyle{\rm Ln\,}a

\displaystyle={\rm Ln\,}a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\Big(\ln g^{p}(a)\Big)\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left(\frac{\ln g^{p}(a)}{p}\right).

(3.3)

Similarly, since
1<p^{\prime}<\infty
, we have

{\rm Ln\,}b=g^{-1}\left(\frac{\ln g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}{p^{\prime}}\right).

(3.4)

By using (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) in (
3.1
), using a known inequality and increasingness of
g^{-1}
(since
g
is so), we get

\displaystyle a\otimes b

\displaystyle=E^{\left(g^{-1}\big(\frac{\ln g^{p}(a)}{p}\big)\oplus g^{-1}\big(\frac{\ln g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}{p^{\prime}}\big)\right)}

\displaystyle=E^{\left(g^{-1}{\left({1\over p}\ln g^{p}(a)+{1\over p^{\prime}}\ln g^{p^{\prime}}(b)\right)}\right)}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left(e^{\left({1\over p}\ln g^{p}(a)+{1\over p^{\prime}}\ln g^{p^{\prime}}(b)\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{g}g^{-1}{\left({1\over p}e^{\ln g^{p}(a)}+{1\over p^{\prime}}e^{\ln g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}\right)}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}{\left({1\over p}{g^{p}(a)}+{1\over p^{\prime}}{g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}\right)}=:A.

(3.6)

Further, we find that

a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)=g^{-1}(g^{p}(a))\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)=g^{-1}\left(\frac{g^{p}(a)}{p}\right)

and similarly

b^{(p^{\prime})}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p^{\prime})=g^{-1}(g^{p}(a))\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)=g^{-1}\left(\frac{g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}{p^{\prime}}\right)

so that

a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)\oplus b^{(p^{\prime})}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p^{\prime})=g^{-1}\left(\frac{g^{p}(a)}{p}+\frac{g^{p^{\prime}}(b)}{p^{\prime}}\right)=:B.

(3.7)

The assertion now follows in view of (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) since
A\ominus B=0_{g}
.

(b) Since the generator
g
is decreasing and consequently
g^{-1}
is so, the inequality (
3.5
) gets reversed and the assertion follows.
∎

Below we prove
g
-Young’s inequality for the case
p<1,\,p\neq 0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p<1,\,p\neq 0
.

(a)

If the generator
g
is increasing then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
, the following Young’s type inequality holds:

a\otimes b\geq_{g}\left(a^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)\right)\oplus\left(b^{(p^{\prime})}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p^{\prime})\right).

(3.8)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
, the inequality (
3.8
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a) Without any loss of generality, we assume that
0<p<1
so that
p^{\prime}<0
for otherwise we can interchange the roles of
p
and
p^{\prime}
.

Take
r=1/p
and
s=-p^{\prime}/p
. Then
1<r,s<\infty
and
1/r+1/s=1
. Set

x=(a\otimes b)^{(p)}\quad{\rm and}\quad y=b^{(-p)}.

We apply inequality (
3.1
) on
x,y
and with exponents
r,s
and obtain

x\otimes y\leq_{g}\left(x^{(r)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(r)\right)\oplus\left(y^{(s)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(s)\right).

(3.9)

Now, it can be calculated that

x\otimes y=a^{(p)},\quad x^{(r)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(r)=(a\otimes b)\otimes g^{-1}(\frac{1}{p}),\quad y^{(s)}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(s)=b^{(p^{\prime})}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(\frac{-p^{\prime}}{p})

which on substituting in (
3.9
) and rearranging the terms give the result.

(b) This can be obtained using the similar arguments and applying Theorem
3.1
(b).
∎

3.2
Hölder’s inequality

Let
1<p<\infty
. We denote by
L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, the Lebesgue space which consists of all measurable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{p,\mathbb{R}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

By a weight function, we mean a function which is measurable, positive and finite almost everywhere (a.e) on
\mathbb{R}
. For a weight function
w
, we denote by
L_{p,w}(\mathbb{R})
, the weighted Lebesgue space which consists of all measurable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{p,w,\mathbb{R}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

The spaces
L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
L_{p,w}(\mathbb{R})
are both Banach spaces.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be a mesurable function and
p\in\mathbb{R},\,p\neq 0
. We define

[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes dx\right)^{({1\over p})}\quad{\rm and}\quad[f]_{p,g,\mathbb{R}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\right)^{{1\over p}}.

(3.10)

Remark 3.3
.

The expression
[f]_{p,g,\mathbb{R}}
in (
3.10
) is not a norm of some weighted Lebesgue space unless
1<p<\infty
and
g^{\prime}
qualifies to be a weight function which can be a case if, e.g.,
g
is an increasing function and consequently
g^{\prime}>0
. In such a case,
[f]_{p,g,\mathbb{R}}
will enjoy all the properties of a norm.

As an independent interest, it is easy to observe that the expressions
[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}
and
[f]_{p,g,\mathbb{R}}
are connected. Precisely, we prove the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be a measurable function and
p\in\mathbb{R},\,p\neq 0
. Then

[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}=g^{-1}\Big([g\circ f]_{p,g,\mathbb{R}}\Big).

Proof.

Let
p>0
. We have

|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}=\Big(g^{-1}(|g(f(x))|)\Big)^{(p)}=g^{-1}\Big(|g(f(x))|^{p}\Big)

so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes dx=g^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(f(x))|^{p}g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\right)

which gives that

[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}=g^{-1}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(f(x))|^{p}g^{\prime}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}\right)

and we are done in this case. Similar arguments can be employed to prove the assertion for
p<0
.
∎

An element
x\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
is said to be finite, written
x<_{g}\infty
, if there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}_{g}
such that
x<_{g}c
.

We prove the following Hölder’s type inequality:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.

(a)

Let the generator
g
be increasing and
f,h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be measurable functions such that
[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}<_{g}\infty
and
[h]_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}<_{g}\infty
. Then
[f\otimes h]^{g}_{1,\mathbb{R}_{g}}<_{g}\infty
and the following Hölder’s type inequality holds:

[f\otimes h]^{g}_{1,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[h]^{g}_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

(3.11)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then the inequality (
3.11
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a) We shall be using Theorem
3.1
for appropriate
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{g}^{+}
. Choose

a=|f(x)|_{g}\otimes^{-1}[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}

(3.12)

and

b=|h(x)|_{g}\otimes^{-1}[h]^{g}_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

(3.13)

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}a\otimes b\otimes dx=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f(x)\otimes h(x)|_{g}\otimes dx\right)\otimes^{-1}\left([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[h]^{g}_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right).

(3.14)

Further, it can be seen that

a^{(p)}=|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}\Big([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(p)}

so that

{a^{(p)}}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p)}=\left\{|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes^{-1}\Big([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(p)}\right\}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p)}

which on
g
-integrating gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}{a^{(p)}}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p)}\otimes dx

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f(x)|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes dx\right)\otimes^{-1}[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right\}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p)}

\displaystyle=1_{g}\otimes^{-1}g^{-1}(p)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\Big(\frac{g(1_{g})}{p}\Big).

Similarly, one can obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}{b^{(p^{\prime})}}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p^{\prime})}\otimes dx=g^{-1}\Big(\frac{g(1_{g})}{p^{\prime}}\Big)

which together with the last equation gives

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}{a^{(p)}}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p)}\otimes dx\right)\oplus\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}{b^{(p^{\prime})}}\otimes^{-1}{g^{-1}(p^{\prime})}\otimes dx\right)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\Big(\frac{g(1_{g})}{p}\Big)\oplus g^{-1}\Big(\frac{g(1_{g})}{p^{\prime}}\Big)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\Big(\frac{g(1_{g})}{p}+\frac{g(1_{g})}{p^{\prime}}\Big)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}(g(1_{g}))

\displaystyle=1_{g}.

(3.15)

Now, the inequality (
3.11
) follows in view of (
3.14
) and (
3.2
) if we take
a
and
b
given, respectively, by (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) in Theorem
3.1
and take
g
-integral on both the sides of the resulting inequality.

(b) This follows since the inequality (
3.1
) gets reversed for decreasing
g
.
∎

A generalized version of Theorem
3.5
is the following:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p,q,r<\infty
be such that
{1\over p}+{1\over q}={1\over r}
.

(a)

Let the generator
g
be increasing and
f,h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be measurable functions such that
[f]_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}<_{g}\infty
and
[h]_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}<_{g}\infty
. Then
[f\otimes h]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}<_{g}\infty
and the following Hölder’s type inequality holds:

[f\otimes h]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[h]^{g}_{q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

(3.16)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then the inequality (
3.16
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a)
Write
P=p/r
,
Q=q/r
so that
{1\over P}+{1\over Q}=1
. Applying the inequality (
3.11
) for the functions
F:=f^{(r)},\,H:=h^{(r)}
and with the exponents
P,Q
, we obtain

[F\otimes H]^{g}_{1,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}[F]^{g}_{P,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[H]^{g}_{Q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

(3.17)

It can be calculated that

\displaystyle[F]^{g}_{P,\mathbb{R}_{g}}

\displaystyle=\left([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(r/p)},

\displaystyle[H]^{g}_{Q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}

\displaystyle=\left([h]^{g}_{q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(r/q)}

and

[F\otimes H]^{g}_{1,\mathbb{R}_{g}}=\left([f\otimes h]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(r)}

using which in (
3.17
) and adjusting the exponents, (
3.16
) follows.

(b) This is an immediate consequence of the fact that
g
is decreasing.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.6
, we prove the following:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<p,q,r<\infty
be such that
{t\over p}+{{1-t}\over q}={1\over r}
, where
0<t<1
.

(a)

If the generator
g
is increasing and for all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
,

[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}<_{g}\infty\quad{and}\quad[f]^{g}_{q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}<_{g}\infty,

then
[f]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}<_{g}\infty
and the following inequality holds:

[f]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}\Big([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(t)}\otimes\Big([f]^{g}_{q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(1-t)}.

(3.18)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then the inequality (
3.18
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a) Applying Theorem
3.6
for the functions
f^{(t)},\,f^{(1-t)}
and with the exponents
p\over t
,
q\over{1-t}
, we obtain that

[f^{(t)}\otimes f^{(1-t)}]^{g}_{r,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}[f^{(t)}]^{g}_{p/t,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[f^{(1-t)}]^{g}_{q/{1-t},\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

Now, since it can be worked out that

[f^{(t)}]^{g}_{p/t,\mathbb{R}_{g}}=\Big([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(t)}

and

[f^{(1-t)}]^{g}_{q/{1-t},\mathbb{R}_{g}}=\Big([f]^{g}_{q,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\Big)^{(1-t)},

the assertion follows.

(b) This is an immediate consequence of the fact that
g
is decreasing.
∎

3.5

3.3
Minkowski’s inequality

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.

(a)

If the generator
g
is increasing then for all measurable functions
f,h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
, the following Minkowski’s type inequality holds:

[f\oplus h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\leq_{g}[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\oplus[h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

(3.20)

(b)

If the generator
g
is decreasing then the inequality (
3.20
) holds in the reverse direction.

Proof.

(a)
Since
g
is increasing, we have

|f\oplus h|_{g}\leq_{g}|f|_{g}\oplus|h|_{g}

(3.21)

and consequently

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f\oplus h|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f\oplus h|_{g}^{(p-1)}\otimes|f\oplus h|_{g}\otimes dx

\displaystyle\leq_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f\oplus h|_{g}^{(p-1)}\otimes|f|_{g}\otimes dx\oplus\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f\oplus h|_{g}^{(p-1)}\otimes|h|_{g}\otimes dx

\displaystyle=:I_{1}\oplus I_{2}.

(3.22)

We apply Hölder’s inequality (
3.11
) and obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq_{g}[(f\oplus h)^{(p-1)}]^{g}_{p^{\prime},\mathbb{R}_{g}}\otimes[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}

\displaystyle=\left([f\oplus h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(p)\otimes^{-1}(p^{\prime})}\otimes[f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}.

Similarly

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq_{g}\left([f\oplus h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(p)\otimes^{-1}(p^{\prime})}\otimes[h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}

so that (
3.3
) gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{g}}^{g}|f\oplus h|_{g}^{(p)}\otimes dx\leq_{g}\left([f]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\oplus[h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)\otimes\left([f\oplus h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}\right)^{(p)\otimes^{-1}(p^{\prime})}.

Now, adjusting the powers of the factor
[f\oplus h]^{g}_{p,\mathbb{R}_{g}}
, the assertion follows.

(b) This follows immediately since the inequality (
3.21
) and Hölder’s inequality both hold in the reverse direction in this case.
∎

4
Hermite-Hadamard Inequality

In this section, we shall consider the special case of the integral
\displaystyle\int^{g}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx
given by (
2.1
).

The classical Hermite-Hadamard inequality asserts that if
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function then the following holds:

f\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\leq{1\over{b-a}}\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\leq\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}

and the inequalities are reversed if
f
is concave. The aim of this section is to derive a
g
-analogue of this inequality. First, we define the following:

Definition 4.1
.

A function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
is said to be pseudo-convex on
[a,b]
if for all
x,y\in[a,b]
and all
0\leq\lambda\leq 1

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq_{g}\lambda\odot f(x)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(y).

We shall denote

\sigma:=\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta:=\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be a pseudo-convex function. Then the following Hermite-Hadamard inequality holds:

f\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\leq_{g}\left(1\over{b-a}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(b)\big).

(4.1)

Proof.

Since
f
is pseudo-convex, we have

\displaystyle f\left(\sigma+\delta\over 2\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot(f(\sigma)\oplus f(\delta))

\displaystyle\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\Big(\lambda\odot f(b)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(a)\oplus\lambda\odot f(a)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(b)\Big)

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(b)\big).

Thus since
\sigma+\delta=a+b
, it follows that

f\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot(f(\sigma)\oplus f(\delta))\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(b)\big).

(4.2)

Now, making variable substitution
\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a=x
, we find that

\displaystyle\int^{\oplus}_{[0,1]}f(\sigma)\otimes d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int^{\oplus}_{[0,1]}f(\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a)\otimes d\lambda

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(g\circ f)(\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a)\,d\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left({1\over{b-a}}\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\left({1\over{b-a}}g\left(g^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)\,dx\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle={1\over{b-a}}\odot g^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle={1\over{b-a}}\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx.

Consequently, taking
g
-integral throughout (
4.2
) with respect to
\lambda
over
[0,1]
, the inequality (
4.1
) follows. ∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
u,v>_{g}0_{g},\,u\neq v
and
g
be increasing. Then the following inequalities hold:

(u\otimes v)^{(1/2)}\leq_{g}\frac{1}{g({\rm Ln\,}u)-g({\rm Ln\,}v)}\odot(u\ominus v)\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot(u\oplus v).

(4.3)

Proof.

Clearly, for an increasing function
g
,
f(x)=E^{g^{-1}(x)}
is pseudo-convex. After some calculations, it can be worked out that by taking
f(x)=E^{g^{-1}(x)}
in Theorem
4.2
, the inequalities (
4.1
) become

\left(E^{g^{-1}(a)}\otimes E^{g^{-1}(b)}\right)^{(1/2)}\leq_{g}\frac{1}{b-a}\odot\left(E^{g^{-1}(b)}\ominus E^{g^{-1}(a)}\right)\leq_{g}\frac{1}{2}\odot\left(E^{g^{-1}(a)}\oplus E^{g^{-1}(b)}\right).

(4.4)

Now, put

E^{g^{-1}(a)}=u\quad{\rm and}\quad E^{g^{-1}(b)}=v

so that

a=g({\rm Ln\,}u)\quad{\rm and}\quad b=g({\rm Ln\,}v).

The inequalities (
4.3
) now follow with these transformations.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The inequalities (
4.3
) are the
g
-analogue of the standard geometric-logarithmic-arithmatic mean inequality

\sqrt{uv}\leq\frac{u-v}{\ln u-\ln v}\leq\frac{u+v}{2},\quad u,v>0,\,u\neq v

which can be obtained by taking the generator
g
as the identity function in (
4.3
)

A refinement of the Hermite-Hadamard inequality has recently been given in (
[
4
]
, Theorem 1.1), We prove below its
g
-analogue which is a refinement of the inequality (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
be a pseudo-convex function. Then for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, the following inequalities hold:

f\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\leq_{g}\ell(\lambda)\leq_{g}\left(1\over{b-a}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx\leq_{g}L(\lambda)\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(b)\big),

(4.5)

where

\ell(\lambda):=\lambda\odot f\left({\lambda b+(2-\lambda)a\over 2}\right)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f\left({1+\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a\over 2}\right)

and

L(\lambda):={1\over 2}\odot\Big(f(\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a)\oplus\lambda\odot f(a)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(b)\Big).

Proof.

Recall
\sigma=\lambda b+(1-\lambda)a
. Clearly
a<\sigma<b
. We apply the inequality (
4.1
) on the interval
[a,\sigma]
, with
\lambda\neq 0
and get

f\left({a+\sigma\over 2}\right)\leq_{g}\left(1\over{\sigma-a}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,\sigma]}f(x)\otimes dx\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(\sigma)\big).

(4.6)

Similarly applying again (
4.1
) on the interval
[\sigma,b]
with
\lambda\neq 1
, we get

f\left({\sigma+b\over 2}\right)\leq_{g}\left(1\over{b-\sigma}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[\sigma,b]}f(x)\otimes dx\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(\sigma)\oplus f(b)\big).

(4.7)

Clearly

\lambda\odot f\left({a+\sigma\over 2}\right)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f\left({\sigma+b\over 2}\right)=\ell(\lambda).

(4.8)

Also, we find that

\displaystyle\lambda\odot\left(1\over{\sigma-a}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,\sigma]}f(x)\otimes dx\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot\left(1\over{b-\sigma}\right)\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[\sigma,b]}f(x)\otimes dx

\displaystyle\quad={1\over{b-a}}\odot\left(\int^{\oplus}_{[a,\sigma]}f(x)\otimes dx\oplus\int^{\oplus}_{[\sigma,b]}f(x)\otimes dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad={1\over{b-a}}\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx

(4.9)

and

\displaystyle\lambda\odot{1\over 2}\odot(f(a)\oplus f(\sigma))\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot{1\over 2}\odot(f(\sigma)\oplus f(b)

\displaystyle\quad={1\over 2}\odot\Big(\lambda\odot f(a)\oplus f(\sigma)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(b)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=L(\lambda).

(4.10)

Now, taking the
\odot
product of (
4.6
) with
\lambda
and (
4.7
) with
1-\lambda
and using (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.10
), we obtain

\ell(\lambda)\leq_{g}{1\over{b-a}}\odot\int^{\oplus}_{[a,b]}f(x)\otimes dx\leq_{g}L(\lambda).

(4.11)

Writing
\displaystyle a+b\over 2
as

{a+b\over 2}={\lambda\over 2}(\lambda b+(2-\lambda)a)+\big(\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\big)(1+\lambda)b+(1-\lambda)a

and using the pseudo-convexity, we get

f\left({a+b\over 2}\right)\leq_{e}\ell(\lambda)

(4.12)

and from the pseudo-convexity applied on
L(\lambda)
, we get

L(\lambda)\leq_{g}{1\over 2}\odot\big(f(a)\oplus f(b)\big).

(4.13)

Now, the inequalities (
4.5
) follow from (
4.12
) and (
4.13
).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Similar to the pseudo-convexity, one can define pseudo-concavity: A function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{g}
is said to be pseudo-concave on
[a,b]
if for all
x,y\in[a,b]
and all
0\leq\lambda\leq 1

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\geq_{g}\lambda\odot f(x)\oplus(1-\lambda)\odot f(y).

Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
can be formulated and proved with pseudo-convexity relaced by pseudo-concavity.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, the classical inequalities, namely, Young’s, Hölder’s, Minkowski’s and Hermite-Hadamard inequalities have been derived in the framework of
g
-calculus. The entire range of index
p\in\mathbb{R},\,p\neq 0
has been covered.

Remark 5.1
.

In
[
10
]
(see also
[
8
]
), Mesiar introduced a generalized
g
-integral

\int_{[a,b]}^{g,h}f(x)\otimes dx=g^{-1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}(g\circ f)(x)h(x)\,dx\right),

where
h
is a non-negative integrable real function and the corresponding
g
-derivative

D_{g,g}f(x)=g^{-1}\Big((g\circ f)^{\prime}(x)/h(x)\Big).

The results of Section 3 can easily be formulated and proved in terms of the above integral.

Remark 5.2
.

It is of interest if the inequalities in Section 4 could be obtained for the generalized integrals
\displaystyle\int_{[a,b]}^{g}
or
\displaystyle\int_{[a,b]}^{g,h}
instead of
\displaystyle\int_{[a,b]}^{\oplus}
.

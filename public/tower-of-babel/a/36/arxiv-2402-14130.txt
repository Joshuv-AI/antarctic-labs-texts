1.
Introduction

A Sylvester rank function on a ring
R
is a function taking non-negative real values on matrices over
R
and satisfying a series of conditions (see Paragraph
2.4
) resembling the conditions of the rank function of matrices over a field. The value of a Sylvester rank function
\rk
on a matrix
A
over
R
is bounded from above by its inner rank
\rk(A)\leq\irk_{R}(A)
. If
\irk_{R}
is itself a Sylvester rank function, then the ring
R
is called a
Sylvester domain
. This notion appeared first implicitly in the works of P. Cohn and was explicitly defined by W. Dicks and E. Sontag
[
DS78
]
. One remarkable property of a Sylvester domain
R
is that it has a universal division ring of fractions
\mathcal{D}_{R}
such that for every matrix
A
over
R
, its rank over
\mathcal{D}_{R}
is equal to its inner rank
\irk_{R}(A)
.

In the case
R=K[\Gamma]
is a group algebra of a free group
\Gamma
over a field
K
, it was proven by P. Cohn
[
Coh06
, Theorem 7.11.8, Proposition 5.5.1]
that
K[\Gamma]
is a Sylvester domain. In
[
HLA22
]
, F. Henneke and D. López-Álvarez considered the case where
\Gamma
is a free-by-
\mathbb{Z}
group and showed that
K[\Gamma]
is a Sylvester domain if and only if every left finitely generated projective
K[\Gamma]
-module is free.

We would like to notice that, for an abstract group
\Gamma
, if
K
is a field of characteristic 0 and
K[\Gamma]
is a Sylvester domain, then
\irk_{K[\Gamma]}
coincides with the von Neumann Sylvester rank function
\rk_{\Gamma}
(for the definition of
\rk_{\Gamma}
, see
[
JZ19b
, Section 2]
).

In this paper we want to understand for which pro-
p
groups
G
the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain. Often, the results in combinatorial group theory and combinatorial pro-
p
group theory run in parallel; although their proofs are quite different. For example, the theorem of J. Stallings (
[
Sta68
]
) saying that a torsion-free and virtually free group is itself free was inspired by the previous and analogous result on free pro-
p
groups due to J.-P. Serre in
[
Ser65
]
. Later in

[
Lub82
]
, A. Lubotzky described how the classical theorems of M. Hall, L. Greenberg and A. Howson about finitely generated subgroups of free groups all have analogues for free pro-
p
groups. More recently, a new proof of the Hanna Neumann Conjecture (first proven independently by J. Friedman and I. Mineyev in 2011) was given by the first author in
[
Jai17
]
using homological methods that apply both to the abstract and pro-
p
versions of the conjecture. This result was later extended to non-solvable pro-
p
Demushkin groups and their discrete counterparts, the hyperbolic surface groups, in
[
JZS19
]
and
[
AJ22
]
respectively.
In the case of a pro-
p
group
G
there exists a Sylvester function on
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, which is an analogue of the von Neumann Sylvester rank function and we also denote it by
\rk_{G}
(see Example
2.5
for the definition). Its definition resembles the Lück approximation
[
Lüc94
,
JZ19b
]
.

If
F
is a finitely generated free pro-
p
group, then
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![F]\!]
is isomorphic to a ring of non-commutative formal power series over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and so from

[
Coh74
]
we know that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![F]\!]
is a Sylvester domain.
In
[
JZ20
]
, the first author proved that in this case
\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![F]\!]}=\rk_{F}
.
In this paper we extend this result to finitely generated free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free pro-
p
group containing an open free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
subgroup. Then
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain and, moreover,
\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}=\rk_{G}
.

We do not know whether the last conclusion of the theorem holds always if
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain. We leave this as a question.

Question 1
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group. Assume that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain. Does
\rk_{G}
coincide with the the inner rank of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
?

One striking consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that
\rk_{G}
takes only integer values. This provides new cases for which the pro-
p
Atiyah conjecture holds (see
[
JZ19b
, Conj. 11.1]
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free pro-
p
group containing an open free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
subgroup. Then
\rk_{G}
takes only integer values.

Recall that the usual Atiyah conjecture for torsion-free abstract groups
\Gamma
states that
\rk_{\Gamma}
takes only integer values (see
[
JZ19b
]
for more details). Notice that the pro-
p
Atiyah conjecture implies also pro-
p
Kaplansky conjecture:
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
does not have non-trivial zero divisors if
G
is torsion-free. Previously the pro-
p
Atiyah conjecture was only known for pro-
p
groups which are residually-(torsion-free
p
-adic analytic) (
[
JZS19
, Cor. 5.5]
). This class includes all infinite Demushkin groups, which are the pro-
p
groups that satisfy Poincaré duality in dimension
2
. Infinite Demushkin
G
groups comprise an important class of free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
groups, and while the Atiyah conjecture for them was previously known, the statement that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain is new. In Paragraph
2.2
we present examples of finitely generated torsion-free pro-
p
groups containing an open free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
subgroup but which are not free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
themselves.

Let us describe the structure of the paper and the main ideas behind the proof of Theorem
1.1
comparing it with the proof of the main result in
[
HLA22
]
. We let
d(G)
denote the cardinality of a minimal set of topological generators of a pro-
p
group
G
.

On one hand, all projective
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-modules are free for the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, and so, in contrast to the case of abstract free-by-cyclic groups considered in
[
HLA22
]
, there is no difference for
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
to be a pseudo-Sylvester domain or a Sylvester domain.

On the other hand, if
N
is a normal subgroup of an abstract group
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma/N\cong\mathbb{Z}
, then the group algebra
K[\Gamma]
is isomorphic to the crossed product
K[N]*\mathbb{Z}
, which allows easily to construct an embedding of the group algebra of a free-by-cyclic group into a division ring. This decomposition as a crossed product does not exists in the pro-
p
situation and we substitute it by
presenting of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, when

G
is a finitely generated free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
group with free pro-
p
kernel
N
,
as a skew power series ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!][\![s;\sigma,\delta]\!]
for the natural conjugation automorphism
\sigma\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]
and the inner
\sigma
-derivation
\delta
(see Paragraph
2.3
). In Section
2
we discuss also preliminary results and definitions about Sylvester matrix rank functions and universal embeddings that we will use to prove Theorem
1.1
.

In Section
3
we investigate pro-
p
groups
G
for which
\rk_{G}=\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
or, more generally,
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain. In particular, we show that if
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain then
G
is of cohomological dimension 2 and the properties
\rk_{G}=\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
being a Sylvester domain are commensurability invariants for torsion-free pro-
p
groups. Thus, it is enough to prove Theorem
1.1
for an open subgroup.

Let
\Lambda=\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\rangle
be the free associative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with a universal division
\Lambda
-ring of fractions
\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}
.
Now assume that
G
is a finitely generated free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
group having a mild flag presentation in the sense of Definition
4.5
, a condition that we show is virtually satisfied for every finitely generated free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
group (Lemma
4.6
). In this case we show that the maps
\sigma
and
\delta
extend through the series of embeddings

\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]\hookrightarrow\Lambda[\![t]\!]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}[\![t]\!]

in such a way that they induce embeddings of the respective skew power series rings.
We also show that the ring
\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}[\![t]\!][\![s;\sigma,\delta]\!]
is a Noetherian domain and therefore has a classical ring of fractions
\mathcal{Q}
given by the Ore localization of its non-zero elements.
This provides an embedding of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
into the
division ring
\mathcal{Q}
(Section
5
).

For an arbitrary finitely generated virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
group
G
, this construction provides an embedding of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
in an Artinian ring
\mathcal{Q}
. The next step is to show that under this embedding all full matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
become invertible over
\mathcal{Q}
. The main tool to prove this is Theorem
2.8
proven in
[
JZ20
]
. We achieve this in Section
6
. This shows that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain.

The last step is to show that
\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}=\rk_{G}
, and we prove it in Section
7
. As a corollary we obtain the pro-
p
Atiyah conjecture for finitely generated torsion-free virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
groups.

In Section
8
we discuss some applications of our result to the Lück approximation in positive characteristic.

We finish the paper with an appendix where we show that if
R
is a profinite ring and
R_{\Sigma}
is a localization of
R
with respect to a collection
\Sigma
of a square matrices over
R
, then
R_{\Sigma}
admits a Hausdorff ring topology such that the map
R\to R_{\Sigma}
is continuous. This is used only in the proof of Claim
3.2
(see also the remark afterwards).

2.
The setup

2.1.
General notation

If
G
is a pro-
p
group, we denote the
Frattini subgroup
of
G
by
\Phi(G)=G^{p}[G,G]
and the
lower
\bm{p}
-central series
of
G
by
G_{1}=G
and
G_{i}=G_{i-1}^{p}[G_{i-1},G]
, so that
G_{2}=\Phi(G)
. We stack the commutators
[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
on the right:

[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{n}]=[[\cdots[[a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3}],\cdots],a_{n}]\,.

If
I_{G}
denotes the augmentation ideal of
G
in the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, the induced filtration

D_{i}(G)=\{g\in G\mid g-1\in I_{G}^{i}\}

on
G
is called the
dimension series
mod
p
(or also the
\bm{p}
-Zassenhaus filtration
). We remark that
G_{i}\leq D_{i}(G)
for every
i\geq 1
.

When we say that a pro-
p
group
G
is finitely generated, or that
X
is a generating set for
G
, it will always mean generation in the topological sense. We recall that if
w_{0}(G)
denotes the smallest cardinality of a fundamental system of neighborhoods of
1
in
G
– it’s local weight – then
w_{0}(G)=\max\{d(G),|\mathbb{N}|\}
(
[
RZ10
, Cor. 2.6.3]
). In particular, every closed subgroup of a finitely generated pro-
p
group is at most countably generated.

2.2.
Torsion-free virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
groups

There are many abstract torsion-free groups which are virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}
but not free-by-
\mathbb{Z}
themselves. One explicit family of such groups is given by the presentations
\Gamma_{e}=\langle a,b,x,y\mid[a,b]^{e}=[x,y]^{e}\rangle
for
e>1
(see
[
BFMT09
, Example 7.2]
). More examples arise amongst 3-manifold groups such as fundamental groups of knot complements with Alexander polynomial
1
. One family of such knots is given by the Pretzel links
L(2m+1,2n+1,2q+1)
satisfying
(m+n+1)(m+p+1)=m(m+1)
, such as
L(-3,5,7)
. The condition on the Alexander polynomial implies that
[G,G]
is perfect, and such groups virtually fibre over
\mathbb{Z}
by being virtually special as a combined consequence of the works of I. Agol, P. Przytycki and D. Wise(
[
Ago08
,
Ago13
,
PW18
]
).

In the pro-
p
case we can construct examples of torsion-free pro-
p
groups which are virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
but not free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
themselves using the ideas of
[
BFMT09
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be the pro-
p
completion of the abstract group

\Gamma=\langle a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\mid[a_{1},a_{2}]^{p}=[b_{1},b_{2}]^{p}\rangle\,.

Then
G
is a torsion-free pro-
p
group that is virtually free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
but not free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
itself.

Proof.

Write
G
as the proper free pro-
p
product with amalgamation
G=A\amalg_{C}B
of the free pro-
p
groups
A=F(a_{1},a_{2})
,
B=F(b_{1},b_{2})
and cyclic subgroup
C=\langle[a_{1},a_{2}]^{p}\rangle\simeq\langle[b_{1},b_{2}]^{p}\rangle
(
[
RZ10
, Exer. 9.2.6a)]
). Since both
A
and
B
are torsion-free,
G
is also torsion-free by
[
RZ00
, Thm. 4.2(b)]
.

Let
F=F(x_{1},x_{2})
be another free pro-
p
group on two generators and consider the surjective homomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to F
defined by
\varphi(a_{i})=\varphi(b_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Claim 2.2
.

There exists an open subgroup
V\leq F
and a homomorphism
\psi\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\psi(x^{-1}[x_{1},x_{2}]x)=1
for every
x\in F(x_{1},x_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
V=\Phi(F)
. Then, it is clear that
[x_{1},x_{2}]
is not contained in
[V,V]
and hence is a non-trivial element of
M=V/[V,V]\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
. Observe that
[x_{1},x_{2}]+[V,V]
has finitely many
F
-conjugates which are linearly independent modulo
[V,V]
, and so one can find a homomorphism
\psi_{0}\colon M\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\psi_{0}(x^{-1}[x_{1},x_{2}]x+[V,V])=1
for all
x\in F
. The desired
\psi
is then the composition of the natural projection
V\to M
with
\psi_{0}
.
∎

Let
U=\varphi^{-1}(V)
. We claim that
U
is free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. More specifically, that if
\theta\colon U\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the composition
\psi\circ\varphi
, then:

Claim 2.3
.

K=\ker\theta
is free pro-
p
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G},X)
be the graph of pro-
p
groups consisting of two vertices
A
and
B
and an edge
C
connecting them such that
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},X)\simeq G
, and let
Y
be the standard
p
-tree associated with
(\mathcal{G},X)
(
[
RZ00
, Sec. 4]
). We recall that:

V(Y)=G/A\bigsqcup G/B\,,

E(Y)=G/C\,,

d_{0}(gC)=gA\,,d_{1}(gC)=gB\,.

In particular,
G
acts on
Y
on the left and
G\backslash Y\simeq X
, the vertex stabilizers are conjugate to
A
or
B
and the edge stabilizers are conjugate to
C
.

Let
Z=K\backslash Y
and
\mathcal{Z}
be the associated graph of pro-
p
groups on
Z
. The edge stabilizers of the
K
action on
Y
are the intersections
K\cap gCg^{-1}
for
g\in G
, which are trivial by construction. Since the vertex stabilizers are all free pro-
p
, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{Z},Z)
is also free pro-
p
.

First, observe that
U
is a normal subgroup of
G
and
G/U\simeq F/\Phi(F)
. Hence,
UA=UB=G
. Therefore,
G/A=UA/A\simeq U/(U\cap A)
and similarly for
G/B
. Since
K
is normal in
U
with quotient
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we get homeomorphisms:

V(Z)\simeq K\backslash U/(U\cap A)\bigsqcup K\backslash U/(U\cap B)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\theta(U\cap A)\bigsqcup\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\theta(U\cap B)\,.

Given that
[a_{1},a_{2}]=[b_{1},b_{2}]
is an element of both
U\cap A
and
U\cap B
that is not trivial under
\theta
,
V(Z)
is finite (and in fact consists of two elements).

Note that
C
is contained in
U
, and since it generates the image of
\theta
we have
U\simeq K\rtimes C
. Also observe that by the construction of
\theta
and
K
we have
[G,U]\leq K
. Combining this information with the homeomorphism
K\backslash G\simeq K\backslash U\times U\backslash G
, we get that the induced right action of
C
in
K\backslash U\times U\backslash G
is given by

(Kc,Ug)c^{\prime}=(Kcc^{\prime},Ug)

for
c,c^{\prime}\in C
and
g\in G
. Therefore:

E(Z)=K\backslash G/C\simeq K\backslash U/C\times U\backslash G\simeq U\backslash G

and
Z
is finite. From
[
Rib17
, Thm. 6.6.1]
, we conclude that the induced map
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{Z},Z)\to K
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lastly, observe that
G
itself cannot be free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. By
[
KM93
, Corollary 3.5]
,
\Gamma
is residually-
p
, and so it embeds into
G
. Since
[a_{1},a_{2}]
and
[b_{1},b_{2}]
are distinct elements in
[\Gamma,\Gamma]\subseteq[G,G]
with the same
p
-th power, the group
[G,G]
cannot be free pro-
p
.
∎

2.3.
Skew power series rings

Let
R
be a topological ring and
\sigma
a continuous automorphism of
R
. We define the continuous map
\delta=\sigma-\operatorname{id}
and observe that
\delta
is a right
\sigma
-derivation,
that is,
\delta
is an additive map and for all
a,b\in R
we have:

\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+\sigma(a)\delta(b)\,.

Moreover,
\sigma
and
\delta
commute. A
(right) skew power series ring

S=R[\![s;\sigma,\delta]\!]
over
R
with automorphism
\sigma
and derivation
\delta
consists of the topological abelian group of all formal power series

\sum_{i\geq 0}s^{i}a_{i}\,,\quad\text{with }a_{i}\in R

together with a multiplication map defined by the rule:

(1)

\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}s^{i}a_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}s^{j}b_{j}\right)=\sum_{m\geq 0}s^{m}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}\sum_{k\geq n}\binom{k}{n}\delta^{k-n}(\sigma^{n}(a_{m-n}))b_{k}\right)\,.

While we will not use them, one can define a left skew power series ring analogously. When
R
is a Noetherian pseudocompact ring, our definition agrees with the one in
[
SV06
]
by seeing
S
simultaneously as a right and left skew power series ring in the unique compatible way (see
[
SV06
, Sec. 1]
).

For the ring
R[\![s;\sigma,\delta]\!]
to exist, one needs to ensure that the infinite sums on the right hand side of (
1
) converge for every possible choice of elements
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
. If this is the case, then (
1
) defines a continuous multiplication on
S
which makes it into a topological
R
-algebra satisfying

as=s\sigma(a)+\delta(a)\,,\quad\text{ for every }a\in R\,.

In this paper, the ring
R
will either be the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of a free pro-
p
subgroup
N
of a pro-
p
group
G
or a power series ring
\Lambda[\![t]\!]
over a discrete
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\Lambda
for a fixed prime
p
. In both cases,
R
is a complete ring whose topology is induced by a Hausdorff filtration
R_{k}
, where
R_{k}
is either the intersection of the
k
-th power
I_{G}^{k}
of the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]
or the principal ideal generated by
t^{k}
of
\Lambda[\![t]\!]
. Following the nomenclature of
[
Laz65
,
Lab06
]
, we have a filtration function

w(a)=p^{-\sup\{k\geq 0\mid a\in R_{k}\}}\,,

which is submultiplicative, satisfies the ultrametric inequality and is such that
a_{i}\to 0
if and only if
w(a_{i})\to 0
. In the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]
case, this filtration function is called a valuation in
[
EJZ13
, Sec. 2.2]
, though we shall reserve the name “valuation” for a stronger class of filtration functions (see Section
4
).

Hence, we restrict ourselves to complete Hausdorff rings with a submultiplicative filtration function
w
. We say that the derivation
\delta=\sigma-\operatorname{id}
is
topologically nilpotent
if
\delta^{k}\to 0
pointwise, that is,
w(\delta^{k}(a))\to 0
for any
a\in R
. If
\delta
is topologically nilpotent, then each infinite sum

\sum_{k\geq n}\binom{k}{n}\delta^{k-n}(\sigma^{n}(a_{m-n}))b_{k}

appearing in (
1
) is convergent for any choices of
a_{i}
and
b_{j}
and therefore the ring
R[\![s;\sigma,\delta]\!]
exists. One of our preliminary results is that the completed
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group algebra of any free-by-
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
pro-
p
group
G
with free kernel
N
is isomorphic to a right skew power series ring over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N]\!]
.

2.4.
Universal division ring of fractions

All ring homomorphisms in this paper are assumed to preserve the multiplicative identity
1
. A subring
S
of
R
is
division closed
if for every unit
x\in S\cap R^{\times}
one has
x^{-1}\in S
. The
division closure
of a subring
S
of
R
is the smallest division closed subring of
R
containing
S
.

Let
f:R\to S
be a ring homomorphism. We say that
f
is
epic
if for every ring
Q
and homomorphisms
\alpha,\beta:S\to Q
, the equality
\alpha\circ f=\beta\circ f
implies
\alpha=\beta
.

As in
[
Coh06
, Chap. 7.2]
, we define an
epic division
S
-ring
as a division ring
\mathcal{D}
together with an epic homomorphism
\varphi\colon S\to\mathcal{D}
. The condition on
\varphi
to be epic is equivalent to the condition that the division closure of
\varphi(S)
is equal to
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
\varphi^{\prime}\colon S\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be another epic division
S
-ring. A subhomomorphism of epic division
S
-rings is a homomorphism
\psi\colon K\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, where
K
is a local subring of
\mathcal{D}
containing
\varphi(S)
with maximal ideal
\ker\psi
, such that
\psi\circ\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
. Two subhomomorphisms are equivalent if their restriction to the intersection of their domains coincide and are again subhomomorphisms. An
specialization

\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
of epic division
S
-rings is an equivalence class of subhomomorphisms. The archetypal examples are
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
for
S=\mathbb{Z}
, with the local subrings
K
being the localization of
\mathbb{Z}
at a prime
p
.

The
universal
division
S
-ring is an epic division
S
-ring
\mathcal{D}
such that for every other epic division
S
-ring
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
there exists a specialization
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. It is unique up to
S
-isomorphism and we will denote it by
\mathcal{D}_{S}
. If the associated map
\varphi\colon S\to\mathcal{D}_{S}
is injective, we say that
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is the
universal division
S
-ring of fractions
. In general, we will say that
S\to\mathcal{D}
is
universal
if the division closure of the image of
S
in
\mathcal{D}
is isomorphic (as a
S
-ring) to
\mathcal{D}_{S}
.

The universal division
S
-ring
\mathcal{D}_{S}
need not exist in general, even if
S
is a domain (
[
Pas82
]
). One class of rings that possess a universal division ring of fractions is the class of Sylvester domains (
[
Coh06
, Thm. 7.5.13]
). To define a Sylvester domain, we consider Sylvester matrix rank functions and the inner rank. A
Sylvester matrix rank function

\rk
on an unital ring
S
is a function defined on the set
\Mat(S)
of all matrices over
S
satisfying:

(SMat1)

\rk(A)=0
if
A
is a zero matrix and
\rk(1)=1
;

(SMat2)

\rk(AB)\leq\max\{\rk(A),\rk(B)\}
for any pair of matrices
A
and
B
that can be multiplied;

(SMat3)

\rk\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}=\rk(A)+\rk(B)
for any matrices
A
and
B
;

(SMat4)

\rk\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\geq\rk(A)+\rk(B)
for any matrices
A
,
B
and
C
of appropriate sizes.

Since
\rk(\operatorname{Id_{n}})=n
by (SMat3), one concludes that
\rk(A)\leq\min\{n,m\}
for every matrix
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(S)
by property (SMat2) and the identities
A=\operatorname{Id}_{n}A=A\operatorname{Id}_{m}
. We include a proof of the following general fact for which we could find no reference:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
B
be any submatrix of a matrix
A\in\Mat(S)
and
\rk
be any Sylvester matrix rank function on
S
. Then,
\rk(B)\leq\rk(A)
.

Proof.

Any submatrix
B
of
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(S)
can be obtained by removing rows or columns of
A
. Hence, it suffices to prove the claim for
A=\begin{pmatrix}B&a\end{pmatrix}
and for
A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}B\\
a^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
for arbitrary columns
a\in\Mat_{n\times 1}(S)
and rows
a^{\prime}\in\Mat_{1\times m}(S)
. Since

B=\begin{pmatrix}B&a\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{n-1}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{m-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}B\\
a^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\,,

the lemma follows from property (SMat2).
∎

Every Sylvester matrix rank function defines a dimension function
\dim
for finitely presented
S
-modules
M\simeq S^{m}/AS^{n}
with
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(S)
through
\dim M=m-\rk(A)
. This dimension function is an example of a
Sylvester module rank function
(see
[
JZ20
, Sec. 2.1]
), and this correspondence gives a bijection between the sets of Sylvester matrix rank functions and Sylvester module rank functions.

Example 2.5
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and
G>U_{1}>U_{2}>\ldots
be a sequence of open normal subgroups of
G
such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}=\{1\}
. Then, for any matrix
A
over the completed group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, if
A_{i}
denotes its reduction modulo
U_{i}
, the limit

\rk_{G}(A)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\rk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}A_{i}}{|G\colon U_{i}|}

exists and defines a Sylvester matrix rank function on
S=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
that does not depend on the choice of the chain
U_{i}
(
[
JZ19b
, Prop. 11.2]
). We denote the associated Sylvester module dimension function by
\dim_{G}
. Observe that for a finitely presented
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
M
we have

\dim_{G}M=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]}M}{|G\colon U_{i}|}.

If a ring
S
has a universal division ring
u:S\to\mathcal{D}_{S}
, we denote by
\rk_{S}
the induced Sylvester rank function:

\rk_{S}(M)=\rk_{\mathcal{D}_{S}}(u(M))\ (M\textrm{\ is a matrix over\ }S).

It is characterized uniquely by the following universal property: for every division
S
-ring
\gamma:S\to\mathcal{E}
and every matrix
M
over
S
,

\rk_{\mathcal{E}}(\gamma(M))\leq\rk_{S}(M)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a ring and
u:S\to\mathcal{D}_{S}
its universal division ring. Let
\alpha:S\to S
be an automorphism. Then there exists a unique
\widetilde{\alpha}:\mathcal{D}_{S}\to\mathcal{D}_{S}
such that
u\circ\alpha=\widetilde{\alpha}\circ u
.

Proof.

The universal property of
\rk_{S}
implies that
\rk_{S}=\rk_{S}\circ\alpha
. Hence
u\circ\alpha:S\to\mathcal{D}_{S}
is also universal. Thus,
u
and
u\circ\alpha
are
S
-isomorphic. This implies the existence of
\widetilde{\alpha}
.
∎

The
inner rank

\irk_{S}(A)
of a non-zero matrix
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(S)
is the smallest non-negative integer
k
such that
A
factors as a product
BC
with
B\in\Mat_{n\times k}(S)
and
C\in\Mat_{k\times m}(S)
. If the inner rank of a square
n\times n
matrix
A
is
n
, we call
A
a
full matrix
. A ring
S
is called a
Sylvester domain
if the inner rank
\irk
is a Sylvester matrix rank function on
S
. Note that
\irk
always satisfies the conditions (SMat1) and (SMat2), and every Sylvester matrix rank function
\rk
on
S
satisfies
\rk(A)\leq\irk(_{S}A)
by (SMat2). Every Sylvester domain
S
possesses a universal division ring of fractions
\mathcal{D}_{S}
which satisfies another universal property:

Proposition 2.7

(cf.
[
Coh06
, Thm. 7.5.13(e)]
)
.

If
S
is a Sylvester domain and
\psi\colon S\to R
is a ring homomorphism such that the image through
\psi
of every full matrix
A
over
S
is invertible over
R
, then
\psi
extends uniquely to a map
\psi\colon\mathcal{D}_{S}\to R
.

If
\psi\colon S\to R
is a homomorphism of rings and
\rk
is a Sylvester matrix rank function on
R
, then the precomposition with
\psi
defines a Sylvester matrix rank function on
S
, denoted
\psi^{\#}\rk
. Given a Sylvester matrix rank function
\rk^{\prime}
on
S
, we say that
R
is an envelope for
\rk
if there exists
\psi
and
\rk
as above such that
\rk^{\prime}=\psi^{\#}\rk
.

We recall that a ring
\mathcal{U}
is von Neumann regular if for every
a\in\mathcal{U}
there exists
b\in\mathcal{U}
such that
aba=a
. In particular, every division ring is von Neumann regular. In
[
JZ20
]
, the first author proved the following:

Theorem 2.8

(
[
JZ20
, Cor. 2.5]
)
.

Let
S
be a ring and
\rk
be a Sylvester matrix rank function on
S
with an envelope
\phi\colon R\to\mathcal{U}
which is a von Neumann regular ring. Assume that:

(1)

\Tor_{1}^{S}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U})=0
, and

(2)

for any finitely generated left or right
S
-submodule
M
of
\mathcal{U}
and any exact sequence
0\to I\to S^{n}\to M\to 0
,
I
is a free
S
-module.

Then
\rk=\irk
. In particular,
S
is a Sylvester domain and the division closure of
\phi(S)
in
\mathcal{U}
is
S
-isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}_{S}
, the universal division
S
-ring of fractions.

In the same paper it is shown the following converse to Theorem
2.8
:

Proposition 2.9

(
[
JZ20
, Prop. 2.2]
)
.

Let
S
be a Sylvester domain and
\mathcal{D}
it’s universal division
S
-ring of fractions. Then:

(1)

For any left (resp. right)
S
-submodule
M
(resp.
N
) of
\mathcal{D}^{r}
, we have that
\Tor_{1}^{S}(N,M)=0
.

(2)

For any finitely generated left or right
S
-submodule
M
of
\mathcal{D}^{r}
and any exact sequence
0\to I\to S^{n}\to M\to 0
,
I
is a direct union of submodules isomorphic to
S^{k}
where

k=n-\dim M=\dim I\,,

where
\dim
is the Sylvester module rank function associated to the inner rank in
S
.

We can see Theorem
2.8
as a homological criterion to determine whether
S
is a Sylvester domain and
\phi\colon S\to\mathcal{U}
is an universal embedding for
S
.

3.
Sylvester domains and completed group algebras

In this section we study pro-
p
groups
G
for which
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain or
\rk_{G}=\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
.
It is clear that the second condition is stronger, but we believe, that in fact they are equivalent.
The main objective of this section is to show that both conditions are commensurability invariants.
As far as the authors are aware, no analogue of this results is known for abstract group rings.
We start with a key lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group and suppose that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain with universal division ring of fractions
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
. Then, for every finitely generated left (right)
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-submodule
M
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}^{m}
and short exact sequence

0\to I\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}\to M\to 0\,,

the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
I
is free of finite rank.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for left submodules, as the proof for right submodules is analogous.
The main step of our proof is the following claim.

Claim 3.2
.

The submodule
I
is closed in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}

Remark
.

We provide two proofs of the claim. The first one is much easier but requires an additional condition that
\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}=\rk_{G}
. We notice that this is the case needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The second proof of Claim
3.2
does not require any additional hypothesis but uses a non-trivial result, proved in the appendix, that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
admits a Hausdorff ring topology such that the embedding
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
is continuous.

First proof of Claim
3.2
.

In this proof we not only assume that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain but that
\irk_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}=\rk_{G}
.
Let

G>N_{1}>N_{2}>\ldots

be a chain of normal open subgroups of
G
with trivial intersection.
By
[
JZ24
, Proposition 2.15]
,
for every finitely generated left
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
N
,

\dim_{G}N=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N_{i}]\!]}N}{|G\colon N_{i}|}.

If
\overline{I}
denotes the closure of
I
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}
, let
\overline{M}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}/\overline{I}
. Since the image
J_{i}
of
I
and
\overline{I}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/N_{i}]^{n}
coincide, we get

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\overline{M})

\displaystyle=\dim_{G}\overline{M}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N_{i}]\!]}\overline{M}}{|G\colon N_{i}|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/N_{i}]^{n}/J_{i}}{|G\colon N_{i}|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![N_{i}]\!]}M}{|G\colon N_{i}|}

\displaystyle=\dim_{G}M=\dim_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M)\,.

The embedding
M\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}^{m}
factors through the map
M\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M
given by
m\mapsto 1\otimes m
, so the latter must also be injective. Hence, any
x\in\overline{I}
not in
I
is such that
1\otimes x
is a non-zero element of

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M
. This element lies in the kernel of the surjection

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\overline{M}
, which is an isomorphism by comparing dimensions. Therefore,
I=\overline{I}
is closed in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}
.
∎

Second proof of Claim
3.2
.

We can identify
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
with the universal localization of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
at the set of all full matrices. Thus,
by Theorem
A.1
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
admits a Hausdorff ring topology such that the embedding
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
is continuous. In particular,
I
is closed in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}
.
∎

We recall that by Proposition
2.9
the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
I
is the direct union of submodules
I_{i}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{k}
. We claim that
I
is itself isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{k}
.

Let
J=I/I_{G}I
, where
I_{G}
is the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
. Since
I
is the direct union of submodules isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{k}
, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
J
must be isomorphic to the direct union of subspaces of dimension at most
k
. Therefore
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}J\leq k
and there exists
k
such that
J=I_{k}+I_{G}I
. Since
I
is closed, it is finitely generated.
Hence, by Nakayama’s lemma,
I
must be equal to
I_{k}
.
∎

The following observation is an immediate consequence of the previous lemma:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group. If
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a Sylvester domain, then
G
is finitely presented and
\cd G\leq 2
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.1
with
M
being the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
.
∎

Before we proceed, we recall the definition of a crossed product between a ring and a group.
Let
R
be an associative and unital ring and
G
be a group. We say that
S
is a

of

and
G
if
S=\oplus_{g\in G}S_{g}
such that for every
g,h\in G

S_{g}S_{h}\subseteq S_{gh}
,
S_{1}=R
and for every
g\in G
there exists a unit
u_{g}\in S_{g}
. We will write
S\cong R*G
.
It is clear that the multiplication is uniquely determined by the rules

u_{g_{1}}u_{g_{2}}=u_{g_{1}g_{2}}\tau(g_{1},g_{2})\,,\\
ru_{g_{1}}=u_{g_{1}}\sigma(g_{1})(r)\,,

where
\tau\colon G\times G\to R^{\times}
and
\sigma\colon G\to\Aut(R)
are functions satisfying the identities

(2)

\begin{gathered}\tau(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})\sigma(g_{3})(\tau(g_{1},g_{2}))=\tau(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})\tau(g_{2},g_{3})\,,\\
\sigma(g_{2})(\sigma(g_{1})(r))=\tau(g_{1},g_{2})^{-1}\sigma(g_{1}g_{2})(r)\tau(g_{1},g_{2})\,,\end{gathered}

for every
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
and

– this follows directly from the associativity of the product in

(

). Since

are invertible in

\{u_{g}\mid g\in G\}
is a free basis of

as a left

Let
G
be a pro-

T

in

. For every

be it’s representative in

. In this case the maps

and

can be explicitly described as follows:

\tau(g_{1}U,g_{2}U)=(\overline{g_{1}g_{2}})^{-1}\cdot\overline{g_{1}}\cdot\overline{g_{2}}\,.

The conjugation action of

For a fixed transversal

\mathcal{Q}*G/U

Claim 3.5

We have that

Claim 3.6

Proof.

\alpha_{1}=\ldots=\alpha_{n}=0

5.
The embeddings of the group algebra

We keep the notation of Section
4

We set

is the free pro-

in the same number of variables

and consider the power series ring

in a variable

sending

to

Lemma 5.1
.

The map
\iota
is an embedding of topological rings that preserves the valuation.

Proof.

We must show that
r\in R
belongs to

if and only if it’s image
\iota(r)\in\Lambda[\![t]\!]
belongs to
t^{k}\Lambda[\![t]\!]
. Since we’ve shown in Corollary

that the valuation

induced by the filtration

is a weight function, we see that the valuation of

expressed as a non-commutative power series equals the valuation of its monomial of least total weighted degree (with the weight of

being

). Hence, it suffices to check this condition only for monomials
x_{i_{1},j_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{m},j_{m}}

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Both
\sigma
and
\delta
extend from

to
\Lambda[\![t]\!]
in such a way that

,

\sigma(a_{i})=\frac{\iota(\sigma(y_{i}))}{t^{w_{i}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta(a_{i})=\frac{\iota(\delta(y_{i}))}{t^{w_{i}}}\,.

\delta(\Lambda[\![t]\!])\subseteq t\Lambda[\![t]\!]
and therefore it is also topologically nilpotent on
\Lambda[\![t]\!]

Once we set that

(and thus

), we can define

arbitrarily on the generators

of

, and for this map to extend the

. From Corollary

and Lemma

, combined with with the fact that

and

thus defined commute, we conclude that

and thus the successive compositions

and

in

∎

As we stated in Section

Proposition 5.3
.

The maps
\sigma
and
\delta

to

. Moreover,

If

If we consider

-module

)

(

To obtain free resolutions in the category of finitely generated filtered

\Omega
-modules

We already have exactness up to

Let

Hence, by taking

Claim 7.3
.

There exists a division ring extension
\mathcal{Q}\leq\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
and an extension

of the valuation

to
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}

Proof.

Consider the polynomial ring

and define

to be

w^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}q_{i}z^{i}\right)=\max\{w(q_{i})p^{-i}\}\,.

We check that

is an additive non-archimedian valuation on
\mathcal{Q}[z]
, for it is immediate that it is discrete.

Let

and
r^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}^{\prime}z^{i}
be two arbitrary elements in
\mathcal{Q}[z]

if and only if all of the

have valuation

, that is, if and only if

for all

. Hence,

. Suppose the minimal

valuation on

and

is attained at the terms
q_{m}z^{m}
and

with

and

minimal for this properties. Then, for all

w(q_{m})p^{-m}\geq w(q_{i})p^{-i}\,,

w(q^{\prime}_{n})p^{-n}\geq w(q^{\prime}_{j})p^{-j}\,.

Multiplying both inequalities implies:

w(q_{m}q^{\prime}_{n})p^{-m-n}\geq w(q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j})p^{-i-j}\quad\forall i\,,j\,.

In particular, for every

w(q_{m}q^{\prime}_{n})p^{-n-m}\geq\max\{w(q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j})\mid i+j=k\}p^{-k}\geq w\left(\sum_{i+j=k}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j}\right)p^{-k}\,.

Suppose now, by contradiction, that there exists

. By the minimality of

. This implies:

w(q_{i})/w(q_{m})<p^{i-m}=p^{n-j}\leq w(q^{\prime}_{n})/w(q^{\prime}_{j})\,,

and thus
w(q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j})<w(q_{m}q^{\prime}_{n})
, a contradiction. Therefore, if

, we have

w(q_{m}q^{\prime}_{n})p^{-n-m}=w\left(\sum_{i+j=m+n}q_{i}q_{j}\right)p^{-m-n}\,.

We have

rr^{\prime}=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\left(\sum_{i+j=k}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j}\right)z^{k}\,.

Since

w\left(\sum_{i+j=k}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j}\right)p^{-k}\leq w(q_{m}q^{\prime}_{n})p^{-n-m}=w\left(\sum_{i+j=m+n}q_{i}q^{\prime}_{j}\right)p^{-n-m}\text{ by~(\ref{note7-eq1}) and~(\ref{note7-eq2})}\,,

the minimal

At last, we must show that

Since

Now

is a Noetherian domain and thus we can construct the division ring of rational functions

∎

Claim 7.5

Claim 7.6
.

Proof.

Let us first recall the statement of the Lück approximation.
Let
\Gamma
be a group and let
K
be a field. For every matrix

of finite index let us define

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\phi_{\Gamma/N}^{A}:&K[\Gamma/N]^{n}&\to&K[\Gamma/N]^{m}\\
&(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&\mapsto&(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})A\end{array}.

This is a
K
-linear map between two finite-dimensional
K
-vector spaces. Thus, we can define a Sylvester rank function of
K[\Gamma]
by means of

(14)

\rk_{\Gamma/N}(A)=\frac{\dim_{K}\im\phi_{\Gamma/N}^{A}}{|\Gamma:N|}=n-\frac{\dim_{K}\ker\phi_{\Gamma/N}^{A}}{|\Gamma:N|}.

Conjecture 2

(The Lück approximation conjecture)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group,
K
be a field and
\Gamma>N_{1}>N_{2}>\ldots
a descending chain of normal subgroups of
\Gamma

A
be a matrix over

. Then the following holds.

(1)

The sequence
\{\rk_{\Gamma/N_{i}}(A)\}_{i\geq 1}
converges.

(2)

The limit
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{\Gamma/N_{i}}(A)
does not depend on the chain
\Gamma>N_{1}>N_{2}>\ldots
.

(3)

If moreover
\Gamma
is locally indicable, then there exists a universal embedding
K[\Gamma]\to\mathcal{Q}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{\Gamma/N_{i}}(A)=\rk_{K[\Gamma]}(A).

If
K
is of characteristic 0, the parts (1) and (2) of Conjecture
2
are known to be true
[
JZ19a
]
, and for part (3) we know that there exists an embedding

into a division ring

such that

[
JZLA20
]
, but we still do not know whether

is universal in general
[
JZ21
]
. If

Appendix A
Localization of profinite rings

Let
R
be an associative ring and
\Sigma
a collection of square matrices over
R
. The universal localization of
R
with respect to
\Sigma
is a ring
R_{\Sigma}
that comes with a ring homomorphism
\lambda\colon R\to R_{\Sigma}
such that
\lambda(A)
is invertible for every
A\in\Sigma
, and
R_{\Sigma}
is universal for that property.

We denote by
\widetilde{\Sigma}
the set of all square matrices over
R
that become invertible under
\lambda
. In general,
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is greater than
\Sigma
. However
R_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
is
R
-isomorphic to
R_{\Sigma}
. We say that
\Sigma
is
complete
if
\Sigma=\widetilde{\Sigma}
.

It is clear that a complete collection of square matrices is
multiplicative
, that is it contains

\GL_{n}(R)
for every
n
, is closed under products when defined and if
A\,,B\in\Sigma
then
\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\in\Sigma
for any matrix
C
of the appropriate size. It also has the following property regarding diagonal summands on
\Sigma
:

(DS)

\text{If }\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\in\Sigma\text{, then }A\,,B\in\Sigma\,.

J. Beachy has shown in
[
Bea93
]
that
R_{\Sigma}
and
\lambda
can be constructed as follows. From now on we assume that
\Sigma
is complete.
Let
R^{n}
denote the rows of
n
elements in
R
,
{}^{n}R
the columns of
n
elements in
R
and
\Sigma_{n}
denote the subset of
n
-by-
n
matrices in
\Sigma
. Consider the set
T_{\Sigma}
given by the disjoint union of all the products
R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}
, that is, the triples
(a,C,x)
where
a
is a row matrix over
R
of length
n
,
C\in\Sigma_{n}
and
x
is a column matrix over
R
of height
n
.
We define two binary operations of sum and product in
T_{\Sigma}
as follows:

(a,C,x)+(b,D,y)=\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}C&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\right)\,,

(a,C,x)\cdot(b,D,y)=\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}C&-xb\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}0\\
y\end{pmatrix}\right)\,,

where the matrices make sense because
\Sigma
is multiplicative,

is an

matrix and

is an

matrix.

We define an equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{1}\subset T_{\Sigma}\times T_{\Sigma}
on

by means of
(a,C,x)\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(b,D,y)
if there exists invertible matrices

over
R
such that

,

and
D=VCU
. Observe that in particular
C
and
D
must have the same size if
(a,C,x)\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(b,D,y)
. We denote the quotient
T_{\Sigma}/\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}
by
\Sigma^{-1}R
and the equivalence class of a triple
(a,C,x)
in
\Sigma^{-1}R
by
(a\colon C\colon x)
.

The operations of sum and product are well defined on the equivalence classes and descend to a sum and a product on the quotient
T_{\Sigma}
. With the sum, it becomes a commutative semigroup. To obtain an abelian additive group, we further introduce a new equivalence relation.

Let
\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
be the subsemigroup of
\Sigma^{-1}R
generated by all the elements of the form
(a\colon C\colon 0)
and
(0\colon C\colon y)
. It is shown in
[
Bea93
]
that all the elements of
\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
are either of that form or of the form
\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}\colon\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\colon\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x\end{pmatrix}\right)
for blocks of the appropriate sizes. This subsemigroup allows us to define the congruence relation
\mathcal{R}_{2}\subseteq\Sigma^{-1}R\times\Sigma^{-1}R
on
\Sigma^{-1}R
, where two elements
\overline{p}\,,\overline{q}\in\Sigma^{-1}R
are equivalent if there exists
\overline{z}_{1}\,,\overline{z}_{2}\in\Sigma_{0}^{-1}R
such that
\overline{p}+\overline{z}_{1}=\overline{q}+\overline{z}_{2}
.

The quotient space
\Sigma^{-1}R/\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}
inherits the sum and product, under which it becomes an associative ring with trivial element being the class
[(1:1:0)]
and the identity element being the class
[(1:1:1)]
. The additive inverse of
[(a\colon C\colon x)]
is
[(a\colon C\colon-x)]
. One of the main results of
[
Bea93
]
is that this quotient space is precisely the universal localization
R_{\Sigma}
, where the map
\lambda\colon R\to R_{\Sigma}
is given by
x\mapsto[(1\colon 1\colon x)]
.

Suppose now that
R
is a topological ring. In that case, all the spaces
{}^{n}R
and
R^{n}
have a natural product topology, and
\Sigma_{n}
also becomes a topological space as it is contained in
\Mat_{n}(R)\simeq R^{n^{2}}
. Hence,
T_{\Sigma}
becomes a topological space endowed with the direct union topology on which the operations of sum and product defined are continuous. Under the quotient topology, those operations are still continuous on
\Sigma^{-1}R
and
R_{\Sigma}
, so that
R_{\Sigma}
becomes a topological ring. We call this topology structure on
R_{\Sigma}
the
induced
topological structure from
R
. It is clear that
\lambda\colon R\to R_{\Sigma}
is a continuous ring homomorphism.
The main result of the appendix is the following theorem.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
R
be a profinite ring and
\Sigma
a collection of square matrices over
R
. Then
R_{\Sigma}
admits a Hausdorff ring topology such that
\lambda\colon R\to R_{\Sigma}
is continuous. In particular, every finitely generated
R
-submodule of
R_{\Sigma}
is profinite.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
\Sigma
is complete.
Since
R
is profinite, then
T_{\Sigma}
is a Hausdorff space under the induced topology.

Claim A.2
.

We have that
\Sigma^{-1}R
is Hausdorff.

Proof.

We must show that the equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is a closed subspace of
T_{\Sigma}\times T_{\Sigma}
. Since
T_{\Sigma}
is given the union topology and each equivalence class under
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is entirely contained in one of the subsets
T_{n}=R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}
, it suffices to prove that
X_{n}=(\mathcal{R}_{1}\cap(T_{n}\times T_{n}))
is closed in
T_{n}\times T_{n}
.

Consider the continuous maps
\psi\colon T_{n}\times T_{n}\times\GL_{n}(R)^{2}\to R^{n}\times\Mat_{n}(R)\times{{}^{n}R}
and
\pi\colon T_{n}\times T_{n}\times\GL_{n}(R)^{2}\to T_{n}\times T_{n}
given by

\psi(a,C,x,b,D,y,U,V)=(aU-b\,,VCU-D\,,Vx-y)\,,

\pi(a,C,x,b,D,y,U,V)=(a,C,x,b,D,y)\,.

It is clear that
X_{n}=\pi(\psi^{-1}(0,0,0))
. Since
\GL_{n}(R)^{2}
is compact,
\pi
is a closed map, so we are done.
∎

The kernel of
\Sigma^{-1}R\to R_{\Sigma}
is the subsemigroup

\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R-\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R=\{\overline{p}\in\Sigma^{-1}R\,\mid\,\exists\overline{z}\in\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R\text{ such that }\overline{p}+\overline{z}\in\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R\}\,,

which contains
\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
but it might be strictly larger.

Claim A.3
.

We have that
\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
is closed in
\Sigma^{-1}R
.

Proof.

It again suffices to consider
\Sigma^{-1}R_{n}=(R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R})/\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}
and show that
Y_{n}=\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R\cap\Sigma^{-1}R_{n}
is closed in
\Sigma^{-1}R_{n}
, as we have a homeomorphism between
\Sigma^{-1}R
and the disjoint union
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Sigma^{-1}R_{n}
.

Decompose
Y_{n}=Z_{I}\cup Z_{II}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{III,i}
where

Z_{I}=\{(a\colon C\colon 0)\in Y_{n}\}\,,

Z_{II}=\{(0\colon C\colon x)\in Y_{n}\}\,,

Z_{III,i}=\left\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}\colon\begin{pmatrix}C&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\colon\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x\end{pmatrix}\right)\in Y_{n}\,\mid\,\begin{array}[]{l}a\in R^{i}\,,x\in{{}^{n-i}R},\\
C\in\Sigma_{i}\,,D\in\Sigma_{n-i}\end{array}\right\}\,.

We only need to show that each one of the
Z_{I}
,
Z_{II}
and
Z_{III,i}
is closed in
\Sigma^{-1}R_{n}
.

The inverse image of
Z_{I}
in
R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}
is the set of triples of the form
(a,C,0)
, which immediately shows that it is closed. An analogous description holds for the inverse image of
Z_{II}
, hence it is also closed. The only case requiring a finer description is that of
Z_{III,i}
.

The inverse image of
Z_{III,i}
in
R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}
is

\left\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}U\,,V\begin{pmatrix}C&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}U\,,V\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x\end{pmatrix}\right)\,\mid\,U\,,V\in\GL_{n}(R)\right\}\,.

Define

Z_{i}=\left\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}C&0\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}\,,\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x\end{pmatrix}\right)\right\}\subseteq R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}\,.

Then, the inverse image in question is the image of
Z_{i}\times(\GL_{n}(R))^{2}
under the continuous right group action map
\mu\colon(R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R})\times(\GL_{n}(R))^{2}\to R^{n}\times\Sigma_{n}\times{{}^{n}R}
given by

\mu(b,A,y,U,V)=(bU,V^{-1}AU,V^{-1}y)\,.

Since
\Sigma
satisfies property (
DS
), the set
Z_{i}
is closed, and because
(\GL_{n}(R))^{2}
is compact the map
\mu
is closed. Hence,
\mu(Z_{i}\times(\GL_{n}(R))^{2})
and
Z_{III,i}
are both closed.
∎

Claim A.4
.

We have that
\{[(1\colon 1\colon 0)]\}
is closed in
R_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

We must show that
\Sigma_{0}^{-1}R-\Sigma_{0}^{-1}R
is a closed subset of
\Sigma^{-1}R
. Consider the map
\varphi\colon\Sigma^{-1}R\times\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R\to\Sigma^{-1}R
given by
\varphi(\overline{p},\overline{z})=\overline{p}+\overline{z}
. The inverse image
\varphi^{-1}(\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R)
is closed in the product by Claim
A.3
. Since this inverse image is precisely
(\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R-\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R)\times\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
, it proves that
\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R-\Sigma^{-1}_{0}R
is closed in
\Sigma^{-1}R
.
∎

By Claim
A.4
, the ring topology constructed on
R_{{\Sigma}}
is such that
\{0\}
is closed. Since
(R_{{\Sigma}},+)
is a topological abelian group, it must also be Hausdorff.

For the last statement of the theorem, take one such
M\leq R_{\Sigma}
and observe that the kernel
I
of any surjection
R^{n}\to M
must be closed in
R^{n}
. Therefore,
M\simeq R^{n}/I
is profinite.
∎

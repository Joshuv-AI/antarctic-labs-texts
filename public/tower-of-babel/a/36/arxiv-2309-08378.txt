Analysis of the C 16 ​ ( d , p ) 17 {}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17} C reaction from microscopic C 17 {}^{17}{\rm C} wave functions
Le Hoang Chien, P. Descouvemont

Abstract
We present a semi-microscopic study of the C 16 ​ ( d , p ) 17 {}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17} C transfer reaction. The C 17 {}^{17}{\rm C} overlap integrals and spectroscopic factors are obtained from a microscopic cluster model, involving many C 16 + n {}^{16}{\rm C}+n configurations. This microscopic model provides a fair description of the C 17 {}^{17}{\rm C} bound-state energies. The C 16 + d {}^{16}{\rm C}+d scattering wave functions are defined in the CDCC method, where the deuteron breakup is simulated by pseudostates. The transfer cross sections are in good agreement with recent data. We confirm the C 16 ​ ( 2 + ) + n {}^{16}{\rm C}(2^{+})+n structure of the ground state, and show that deuteron breakup effects have a significant influence on the cross sections. We study the C 17 ​ ( p , d ) 16 {}^{17}{\rm C}(p,d)^{16} C reverse reaction and suggest that the cross section to the 2 + 2^{+} state should be large. A measurement of the ground-state cross section would provide a strong test of the microscopic wave functions.



I
Introduction

The physics of exotic nuclear is one of the main interests in modern nuclear physics
Tanihata et al. 2013
;
Otsuka et al. 2020
.
Exotic nuclei are located near the drip lines and therefore present a low breakup threshold,
and a small number of bound states. They can be seen as a core nucleus (which may be in an excited state)
surrounded by one or two nucleon(s). The recent development of radioactive beams
Blumenfeld et al. 2013

provides helpful information about the structure of exotic nuclei. The analysis of these
data require models for the structure of the nucleus, as well as for the reaction process.

Over the last 20 years, the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
nucleus has been investigated in several works. From a Coulomb
breakup experiment, Datta Pramanik
\sl et\ al.

Datta Pramanik et al. 2003
concluded that the ground state has
a spin
3/2^{+}
and present a
{}^{16}{\rm C}(2^{+})+n
structure. The study of excited states was performed by
Elekes
\sl et\ al.

Elekes et al. 2005
by inelastic scattering on a proton target, and by Bohlen
\sl et\ al.

Bohlen et al. 2007
who used a three-neutron transfer reaction at high energies. Negative-parity states
were observed from
\beta
-delayed neutron emission of
17
B
Ueno et al. 2013
. Satou
\sl et\ al.

Satou et al. 2008

observed several unbound states from proton inelastic scattering, and concluded on the existence of
narrow
7/2^{+}
and
9/2^{+}
resonances are low energies. The lifetime of excited states was
investigated by Smalley
\sl et\ al.

Smalley et al. 2015
from a one-neutron knockout reaction on
{}^{18}{\rm C}
.
Very recently, this technique was also used by Kim
\sl et\ al.

Kim et al. 2023
who concluded
on the existence of a
5/2^{+}_{2}
state, and suggested low-energy negative parity resonances.

On the theoretical side, various techniques have been used to describe the spectroscopy of

{}^{17}{\rm C}
, in particular the multi channel algebraic scattering (MCAS) model
Amos et al. 2012
, the no-core
shell model
Smalley et al. 2015
or large-scale shell model calculations
Kim et al. 2023
. In Refs.
Descouvemont 2000
;
Timofeyuk and Descouvemont 2010
,
the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
nucleus was described in the Resonating Group Method (RGM, see Refs.
Wildermuth and Tang 1977
;
Horiuchi 1977
).
In that approach, the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
wave functions are obtained from a microscopic Hamiltonian, with
the cluster approximation. In this way, the 17-nucleon antisymmetrization is exactly taken into account.
This technique is well adapted to weakly bound nuclei, where the description of the relative
wave function at large distances is a fundamental issue. The RGM provides the overlap integrals
and the spectroscopic factors, which are necessary ingredients to the
(d,p)
transfer cross sections

Baye and Timofeyuk 1992
.
No further parameter or renormalization factor is necessary, in contrast with the
Distorted Wave Born Approximation (DWBA)
method
Satchler 1983
, where the overlap integral is approximated from the simple potential model, and
where the spectroscopic factor is an adjustable parameter.

An efficient tool to investigate the spin of nuclear states is provided by
(d,p)
reactions, where a
neutron is transferred from the deuteron to the target
Timofeyuk and Johnson 2020
. The cross sections are known to be very
sensitive to the target+neutron angular momentum
Satchler 1983
. This technique was used recently by
Pereira-López
\sl et\ al.
in a
{}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}
C
experiment
Pereira-López et al. 2020
. The authors measured the transfer cross sections
to the
{}^{17}{\rm C}

1/2^{+}
and
5/2^{+}
excited states, as well as the sum of the three bound states.
Owing to its dominant
{}^{16}{\rm C}(2^{+})+n
structure, the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
ground state presents a small spectroscopic
factor in the
{}^{16}{\rm C}(0^{+})+n
channel, and therefore the corresponding cross section could not be separated.

In the present work, our goal is to analyze the
{}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}
C
reaction by using microscopic

{}^{17}{\rm C}
wave functions. In the standard DWBA treatment of
(d,p)
reactions
Satchler 1983
,
the residual nucleus is described by a potential-model wave function, which is renormalized by a
spectroscopic factor to include missing effects, such as the antisymmetrization or the influence of
core excited states. This semi-microscopic approach to transfer reactions
has been developed in Ref.
Descouvemont 2022
, and we refer to this reference for more detail.

As the deuteron projectile is weakly bound, breakup effects in the

{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
scattering wave functions are expected to be important. This problem is addressed by
using the Continuum Discretized Coupled Channel (CDCC) method (see Ref.
Yahiro et al. 2012
for a review),
where the three-body continuum is simulated by pseudostates in the
p+n
system. This technique is
well known, and has been used for many systems. It is particularly well adapted to exotic nuclei,
where the breakup threshold energy is low, and where breakup effects are expected to be important
(see, for example, Refs.
Gomez-Ramos and Moro 2017
;
Descouvemont 2017
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we present a brief outline of the model,
which is divided in two parts. In the first part, we discuss the RGM
{}^{17}{\rm C}
wave functions, in particular
the overlap integrals and the spectroscopic factors. The second part is focused on the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
scattering
wave functions, defined in the CDCC framework. We also provide information on the calculation of
the transfer cross sections. The results on the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
spectroscopy and on the
{}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}
C
cross sections
are presented in Sec.
III
. Conclusions and outlook are discussed in Sec.
IV
.

II
The model

II.1
The Resonating Group Method

Our goal is to describe the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
wave functions in a microscopic approach. In a
A
-nucleon system
(
A=17
), the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H_{17}=\sum_{i=1}^{A}t_{i}+\sum_{i<j=1}^{A}v_{ij},

(1)

where
t_{i}
is the kinetic energy of nucleon
i
, and
v_{ij}
is a nucleon-nucleon interaction,
including central and spin-orbit components. The Coulomb force is treated exactly.
In the Resonating Group Method, the wave functions are defined at the cluster approximation and
involve internal wave functions of the clusters, as well as a relative function. The internal
wave functions are defined in the shell model. For the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
nucleus, the RGM wave function
associated with Hamiltonian
H_{17}
reads

\displaystyle\Psi_{17}^{JM\pi}={\cal A}\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{c}\varphi^{JM\pi}_{c}g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho),

(2)

where
{\cal A}
is the
A
-nucleon antisymmetrizor,
\boldsymbol{\rho}=(\rho,\Omega_{\rho})
is the
relative coordinate between
{}^{16}{\rm C}
and the neutron, and
g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
are the radial wave functions.
The channel functions
\varphi^{JM\pi}_{c}
are given by

\displaystyle\varphi^{JM\pi}_{c}=\biggl[\bigl[\phi^{I_{1}}_{16}\otimes\phi_{n}\bigr]^{I}\otimes Y_{\ell}(\Omega_{\rho})\biggr]^{JM},

(3)

where
\phi^{I_{1}}_{16}
are shell-model wave functions of
{}^{16}{\rm C}
(with an oscillator
parameter
b
, chosen here as
b=1.6
fm),
\phi_{n}
is a neutron spinor, and index
c

stands for
c=(\ell,I_{1},I)
. We adopt the same conditions as in Ref.
Timofeyuk and Descouvemont 2010
, where
{}^{16}{\rm C}
is described
by all Slater determinants involving four protons in the
p
shell, and two neutrons in the
sd
shell.
This leads to 990 Slater determinants to describe the ground state and several excited
states (see Ref.
Timofeyuk and Descouvemont 2010
for detail).

In the calculation of transfer cross sections, the relevant quantities are the overlap integrals

Timofeyuk 2014
;
Timofeyuk 2020
, defined as

\displaystyle I^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)=\frac{1}{\rho}\bigl\langle\varphi^{JM\pi}_{c}|\Psi_{17}^{JM\pi}\bigr\rangle,

(4)

which are defined for each channel
c
. This definition provides the spectroscopic factor from

\displaystyle S^{J\pi}_{c}=\int\left[I^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)\right]^{2}d\rho.

(5)

The relative functions
g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
must be determined from the Schrödinger equation
associated with Hamiltonian (
1
). In practice, we use the Generator Coordinate Method (GCM,
see Refs.
Horiuchi 1977
;
Descouvemont and Dufour 2012
), where
g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
is expanded as

\displaystyle g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)=\int dR\,f^{J\pi}_{c}(R)\,\Gamma_{\ell}(\rho,R).

(6)

In this equation,
R
is the generator coordinate, and the projected Gaussian function

\Gamma_{\ell}(\rho,R)
is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\ell}(\rho,R)=\left(\frac{\mu}{\pi b^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\mu}{2b^{2}}(\rho^{2}+R^{2})\right)i_{\ell}\left(\frac{\mu\rho R}{b^{2}}\right),

(7)

\mu
being the reduced mass and
i_{\ell}(x)
a spherical Hankel function.
With expansion (
6
), the total wave function (
2
) can be written as a superposition of
projected Slater determinants
Descouvemont and Dufour 2012
, well adapted to systematic numerical calculations.
In the GCM, the calculation of the radial functions
g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
is therefore replaced by the calculation of
the generator functions
f^{J\pi}_{c}(R)
.

The Gaussian asymptotic behaviour (
7
) is corrected with the microscopic
R
-matrix
method
Descouvemont and Baye 2010
for scattering states as well as for bound states. This issue is important
for weakly bound states, where the wave function presents a slow decrease at large distances.
The technique to derive the overlap integrals in the RGM is explained, for example, in Refs.
Descouvemont 2022
;
Descouvemont 2023
.
It is based on the calculation of the overlap kernels between GCM basis functions.

The Asymptotic Normalization Coefficient (ANC)
C^{J\pi}_{c}
in channel
c
is defined
from the long-range limit of the overlap integral as

\displaystyle I^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)\longrightarrow C^{J\pi}_{c}W_{-\eta_{c},\ell+1/2}(2k_{c}\rho),

(8)

where
k_{c}
and
\eta_{c}
are the wave number and Sommerfeld parameter in channel
c
, and
W_{ab}(x)

is the Whittaker function
DLMF
. The ANC depends on the wave function at
large distances, whereas the spectroscopic factor probes the inner part of the wave function.
Both quantities are therefore complementary. Notice that the asymptotic forms of the relative wave
functions
g^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
and of the overlap integrals
I^{J\pi}_{c}(\rho)
are identical.

II.2
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
and
{}^{17}{\rm C}+p
scattering wave functions

In the entrance channel, the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
scattering wave functions are defined in the CDCC formalism,
which simulates breakup effects of the deuteron by
p+n
pseudostates
Yahiro et al. 2012
.
The three-body Hamiltonian is defined as

\displaystyle H=H_{0}(\boldsymbol{r})+T_{R}+V_{pC}+V_{nC},

(9)

where
H_{0}
is the
p+n
Hamiltonian,
T_{R}
the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
kinetic energy, and
V_{pC}
and
V_{nC}
are
optical potentials between the nucleons and
{}^{16}{\rm C}
. In Eq. (
9
),
\boldsymbol{r}
is the
p+n

relative coordinate, and
\boldsymbol{R}
is associated with the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
system.

In the CDCC method,
p+n
wave functions are obtained from

\displaystyle H_{0}\phi^{\ell m}_{k}(\boldsymbol{r})=E^{\ell}_{k}\phi^{\ell m}_{k}(\boldsymbol{r}),

(10)

where
k
is the excitation level, and
\ell
the angular momentum. This equation provides one
bound state
(E^{0}_{1}<0)
, associated with the deuteron, and positive-energy states
(E^{\ell}_{k}>0)
,
referred to as pseudostates, which represent approximations of the continuum. The
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
wave
functions are then defined as

\displaystyle\Psi^{JM\pi}_{i}=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{\gamma}u^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R)\left[\phi^{\ell}_{k}(\boldsymbol{r})\otimes Y_{L}(\Omega_{R})\right]^{JM},

(11)

where
L
is the relative orbital momentum between
{}^{16}{\rm C}
and
d
, and where index
\gamma
stands for
\gamma=(\ell,k,L)
.
The summation over the pseudostates
(\ell,k)
must be truncated at some
\ell_{\rm max}
and
E_{\rm max}
values,
which are chosen large enough so that the expansion (
11
), and the associated cross sections converge.

The radial functions
u^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R)
and the associated scattering matrices at a center-of-mass
(c.m.) energy

E
are obtained
from the standard coupled-channel system

\displaystyle\bigl(T_{L}(R)+E^{\ell}_{k}-E\bigr)u^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R)+\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}}V^{J\pi}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}(R)u^{J\pi}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(R)=0,

(12)

with

\displaystyle T_{L}(R)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\biggl(\frac{d^{2}}{dR^{2}}-\frac{L(L+1)}{R^{2}}\biggr).

(13)

The coupling potentials
V^{J\pi}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}(R)
are obtained from matrix elements of the
optical potentials
V_{pC}+V_{nC}

Yahiro et al. 2012
. Finally, system (
12
) is solved with the
R
-matrix
method
Descouvemont and Baye 2010
;
Burke 2011
on a Lagrange mesh
Descouvemont 2016
which provides the scattering matrices and the radial wave functions
for all
J\pi
values.

For the
{}^{17}{\rm C}+p
exit channel, the scattering wave functions are defined from an optical potential as

\displaystyle\Psi^{JM\pi}_{f}=\frac{1}{R^{\prime}}u^{J\pi}_{f}(R^{\prime})\left[\bigl[\Psi_{17}\otimes\phi_{p}\bigr]^{I_{f}}\otimes Y_{L_{f}}(\Omega_{R^{\prime}})\right]^{JM},

(14)

where
\boldsymbol{R}^{\prime}
is the
{}^{17}{\rm C}+p
relative coordinate, and where
L_{f}
and
I_{f}
are the angular momentum
and the channel spin in the exit channel. Function
u^{J\pi}_{f}(R^{\prime})
is obtained from the Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\bigl(T_{L_{f}}(R^{\prime})+V_{\rm opt}(R^{\prime})-E_{f}\bigr)u^{J\pi}_{f}(R^{\prime})=0

(15)

which involves the optical potential
V_{\rm opt}(R^{\prime})
, associated with
{}^{17}{\rm C}+p
(
E_{f}
is the scattering energy in this channel).

II.3
Transfer scattering matrices

We follow the method of Ref.
Descouvemont 2022
, and refer the reader to this reference for detail.
The
(d,p)
transfer scattering matrix from an initial state
i
to a final state

f
is defined by

\displaystyle U^{J\pi}_{i,f}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\Psi^{J\pi}_{f}|V_{pn}+\Delta V|\Psi_{i}^{J\pi}\rangle,

(16)

where we use the post representation, and where
\Delta V
is the remnant potential
Satchler 1983
;
Timofeyuk and Johnson 2020
. A
difficulty associated with definition (
16
) is that the coordinates
(\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{r})
in
the entrance channel are different from those in the exit channel
(\boldsymbol{R}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime})
.
In practice,
\boldsymbol{r}
and
\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}
are expressed from
\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{R}^{\prime}

(see, for example, Ref.
Shubhchintak and Descouvemont 2019
for detail). Using definitions (
11
) and (
14
)
of the scattering wave functions, the transfer scattering matrix (
16
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle U^{J\pi}_{i,f}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{\gamma}\int u^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R)K^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R,R^{\prime})u^{J\pi}_{f}(R^{\prime})RR^{\prime}dRdR^{\prime}.

(17)

For a given
{}^{17}{\rm C}
state, the transfer kernel is given by

\displaystyle K^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R,R^{\prime})={\cal J}\sum_{c}

\displaystyle\langle\bigl[\phi_{k}^{\ell}(\boldsymbol{r})\otimes Y_{L_{i}}(\Omega_{R})\bigr]^{J}|V_{pn}+\Delta V|\bigl[I_{c}(\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime})\otimes Y_{L_{f}}(\Omega_{R^{\prime}})\bigr]^{J}\rangle,

(18)

where
{\cal J}
is the Jacobian, and
I_{c}(\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime})
are the overlap functions defined in
Eq. (
4
). In Eq. (
17
), the summation over index
\gamma
arises from the
CDCC expansion of the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d

scattering wave functions. The use of a Lagrange mesh to define the radial functions

u^{J\pi}_{\gamma}(R)
makes the numerical calculations of the double integrals (
17
)
rather fast
Shubhchintak and Descouvemont 2019
. The
R
-matrix channel radius is chosen large enough to guarantee the
convergence.

III.2
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
elastic-scattering cross sections

The calculation of the
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
scattering wave functions is the first step for the transfer cross sections.
The elastic cross section also provides an excellent test of the model. For
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
, elastic cross
sections have been measured at
\mbox{$E_{\rm lab}$}=24\ {\rm MeV/nucleon}
(
\mbox{$E_{\rm c.m.}$}=42.67

, which
is slightly higher than the energy of the
{}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}
C

Pereira-López et al. 2020

\mbox{$E_{\rm c.m.}$}=30.58
MeV).
However, this energy difference is not expected to modify the conclusions on the reliability of the model.

The calculation is performed within the CDCC framework, with the Minnesota potential
Thompson et al. 1977

for the
p+n
interaction. We use a Laguerre basis to describe the
deuteron ground state, as well as the
p+n
pseudostates (see Ref.
Druet et al. 2010
for detail).
Pseudostates up to
\mbox{$E_{\rm max}$}=20
MeV and

\mbox{$\ell_{\rm max}$}=4
are included. The
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
scattering
matrices are computed with the
R
-matrix formalism
Descouvemont and Baye 2010
;
Descouvemont 2016
. Several tests of the
numerical conditions (
E_{\rm max}
,
\ell_{\rm max}
,
R
-matrix radius,
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
basis, etc.) have been performed to
check the stability of the cross section. As
16
C + nucleon optical potentials, we use the
global parametrizations of Ref.
Koning and Delaroche 2003
and of Ref.
Varner et al. 1991
, referred to as KD and
CH, respectively.

The
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d

\mbox{$E_{\rm c.m.}$}=42.67

the experimental data of Jiang
\sl et\ al.

IV
Conclusion

We have analyzed recent data on the
{}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}
C
reaction
Pereira-López et al. 2020
by using microscopic
{}^{17}{\rm C}
wave
functions. In the microscopic RGM formalism, the wave functions are fully antisymmetric. The

{}^{16}{\rm C}+n
cluster structure includes many
{}^{16}{\rm C}
states, which provides a realistic description of
the low-lying states. The long-range behavior is treated by the
R
-matrix methods, for bound states
as well as for scattering states. The
{}^{16}{\rm C}+d
wave functions are obtained within the
CDCC approach, which includes deuteron breakup effects. We have shown that these effects are not
negligible in the transfer cross sections.

The transfer cross sections are in fair agreements with experiment, without any adjustable parameter.
We have confirmed that the
\mbox{${}^{16}{\rm C}(d,p)^{17}$C}({\rm gs})
cross section is small, owing to the dominant
{}^{16}{\rm C}(2^{+})+n

structure of the
{}^{17}{\rm C}
ground state. We have determined the reverse
{}^{17}{\rm C}(p,d)^{16}
C
cross section,
and shown that
{}^{16}{\rm C}
would be essentially populated in the
2^{+}
first excited state. A
measurement of the cross sections, and in particular of the branching ratio, should provide a
strong test of the RGM wave function.

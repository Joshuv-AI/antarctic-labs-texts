Classification and nonexistence for t t -structures on derived categories of schemes
Alexander Clark, Pat Lank, Kabeer Manali Rahul, Chris J. Parker

Abstract
. Given a suitable Noetherian scheme, we classify tensor t t -structures on the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves and its variants with prescribed support. Furthermore, we show that the existence of such t t -structures restricting to perfect complexes detects regularity, recovering a theorem of Neeman in the affine case by different methods. Our tools establish local-to-global principles for tensor t t -structures.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
What is known

The existence and classification of
t
-structures on triangulated categories arising in algebraic geometry has given new ways to extract geometric information from the homological data. A notable application of
t
-structures is a conjecture of Antieau–Gepner–Heller
[
3
]
. It predicts that
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
for a Noetherian scheme
X
admits a bounded
t
-structure if, and only if,
X
is regular. This conjecture has now been resolved: first in the affine case by Smith
[
28
]
, and in full generality by Neeman
[
26
]
.

The classification problem for
t
-structures on triangulated categories associated to schemes is subtle. In this work, we consider categories such as
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
,
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
,
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
, and their variants with support in a closed subset. A complete classification of all
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is out of reach even in the affine case
[
30
]
. Instead, one is naturally led to restrict attention to well-behaved classes of
t
-structures. Key advances in this direction include the classification of compactly generated
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
for affine schemes
[
1
,
14
]
.

Passing to the global (nonaffine) setting introduces additional difficulties. In particular, many techniques from the affine case no longer apply directly. A robust class of
t
-structures in this context are those that are both
compactly generated
and
tensor compatible
. The classification of such
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
for the global case has been achieved in
[
8
,
20
]
. These results establish a bijective correspondence between compactly generated tensor
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
and Thomason filtrations of
X
.

1.2.
What we do

The technical core of the paper concerns the local-to-global behavior of compactly generated tensor
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
. A central role is played by the classification of such
t
-structures in terms of Thomason filtrations. Our approach shows that one can effectively study
t
-structures under passage to open subschemes, local rings, and to subcategories defined by support conditions. This allows us to reduce global questions to local conditions on stalks and topological conditions. See
Section

3
for details on the tools that we develop.

1.2.1.
Relative weak Cousin

Our first result concerns compactly generated tensor
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
that restrict to
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
and its variants with prescribed support. We give a topological characterization, in terms of Thomason filtrations, for when such restrictions occur. In the affine setting, related classification results were established in
[
1
]
for schemes admitting a dualizing complex, and later in
[
31
]
for affine CM-excellent schemes of finite Krull dimension.

We now state our first main result.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose
X
is a quasi-compact CM-excellent scheme of finite Krull dimension. A Thomason filtration
\phi
on
X
is weak Cousin across
Z
if, and only if, its associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc},Z}(X)
restricts to
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z}(X)
.

Proposition

1.1
provides a characterization which applies to complexes supported on a fixed closed subset. This feature is absent from the affine results in the references above. A key ingredient in the proof is a relative form of the weak Cousin condition for Thomason filtrations, extending the notion of
[
1
]
. See
Section

4
. In the affine absolute case (i.e.
Z=X=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
),
[
13
, Corollary 5.6]
says that these results and their hypothesis of CM-excellence are optimal. It is natural to ask whether a global version of loc. cit. holds.

The intuition behind the proof of
Proposition

1.1
begins with studying how Thomason filtrations on
X
behave upon restriction to a closed subset. In particular, being relative weak Cousin over
Z
simply means that the restricted Thomason filtration satisfies the weak Cousin condition when viewed as a Thomason filtration on
Z
.

Our next result yields a classification of all tensor
t
-structures on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact CM-excellent scheme of finite Krull dimension. For any closed
Z\subseteq X
, there is a one-to-one correspondence:

\displaystyle\{\otimes\textrm{-aisle on }

\displaystyle D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z}(\mathcal{X})\}\iff\{\textrm{Thomason filtrations on }X\textrm{ that are weak Cousin across }Z\}.

A key ingredient to proving this result is
Theorem

1.2
. Furthermore,
Theorem

1.2
is quite applicable, e.g. Noetherian schemes admitting dualizing complexes
Example

4.6
. Its ramifications extend to perverse sheaf theory. Particularly, our result can be viewed as an effective conclusion to classifying
t
-structures on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
obtained from perversity functions on
X
. See
[
4
]
,
[
10
, Theorem 9.1]
,
[
16
, Theorem 5.9]
, and
[
27
]
.

1.2.2.
Restriction to perfects

We next study
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc},Z}(X)
that restrict to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
for a fixed closed subset
Z\subset X
. A key point is that restrictability implies a support theoretic constraint in terms of the regular locus (see
Proposition

5.6
). This leads to the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

A nonempty closed subset
Z
of a Noetherian scheme
X
is contained in the regular locus of
X
if, and only if, there is a Thomason filtration on
X
whose associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
and is not constant on each connected component
Z_{t}
of
Z
.

This recovers
[
26
, Theorem 0.1]
in the affine case and extends
[
28
]
to arbitrary Krull dimension. Using a result of Hochster
[
11
]
, it also applies to explicit examples of Noetherian integral domains of infinite Krull dimension that are not regular (see
Example

5.10
). The proof uses local algebra techniques rather than the methods of
[
26
]
involving approximable triangulated categories.

As a consequence, we obtain the following classification of
t
-structures on
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact CM-excellent scheme of finite Krull dimension. Suppose
Z
is a nonempty closed subset of
X
with connected components
Z_{t}
. A Thomason filtration
\phi
on
X
has an associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
which restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
if, and only if,
\phi\cap Z_{t}
satisfies weak Cousin for
Z_{t}
contained in the regular locus of
X
and
\phi\cap Z_{t}
is constant on
Z_{t}
otherwise.

; similarly for the other subcategories. If

and

. Note that

\operatorname{Perf}(X)
for all ideals
I
in
R
. The
n
-th cohomology of an object

in

is denoted by

. Additionally, if
X
is affine, then we write this instead as
H^{n}(E)
. There are the following triangulated equivalences of

with

, and

with

,

]

2.2.
t-structures

We discuss
t
-structures and related constructions within triangulated categories. See
[
5
]
for further details. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category equipped with a shift functor
[1]\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
, and assume it admits all small coproducts. Recall a subcategory is ‘strictly full’ if it is a full subcategory which is closed under isomorphisms. If
\mathcal{S}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
, then
\operatorname{Coprod}(\mathcal{S})
is the smallest strictly full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{S}
that is closed under extensions and small coproducts.

A pair of strictly full subcategories
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
in
\mathcal{T}
is called a
t
-structure
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=0
for all
A
in
\mathcal{A}
and
B
in
\mathcal{B}[-1]

(2)

\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are respectively closed under positive and negative shifts

(3)

For any object
E
in
\mathcal{T}
, there is a distinguished triangle with
A
in
\mathcal{A}
and
B
in
\mathcal{B}[-1]
:

A\to E\to B\to A[1].

A
preaisle
on
\mathcal{T}
is a strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{A}
that is closed under positive shifts and extensions; whereas an
aisle
on
\mathcal{T}
is a preaisle whose inclusion
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{T}
admits a right adjoint. If the aisle exists, denote by
\overline{\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle}^{(-\infty,0]}
the smallest cocomplete aisle of
\mathcal{T}
containing a subcategory
\mathcal{S}
. A
t
-structure
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
on
\mathcal{T}
is said to
restrict
to a triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{T}
if the pair
(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{S},\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{S})
is a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{S}
. In a similar vein, we can speak of aisles restricting if their associated
t
-structure does so.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated triangulated category which admits all small coproducts. Denote the triangulated subcategory of compact objects by
\mathcal{T}^{c}
. Then any
t
-structure on
\mathcal{T}^{c}
is the restriction of a compactly generated
t
-structure on
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
24
, Lemma 1.8 & Proposition 1.9]
coupled with the fact that aisles are closed under direct summands (see
[
2
, Corollary 1.4]
). Indeed, for any aisle
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{T}^{c}
, one can lift to
\operatorname{Coprod}(\mathcal{A})
which is a compactly generated aisle on
\mathcal{T}
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.6]
for details). In fact, we have that
\overline{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(-\infty,0]}=\operatorname{Coprod}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

The distinguished triangles in the datum of a
t
-structure are called the
truncation triangles
, and are unique up to unique isomorphism of triangles. If
A\to E\to B\to A[1]
is a truncation triangle, then we typically define
\tau^{\leq 0}E:=A
and
\tau^{\geq 1}:=B
respectively as the ‘connective’ and ‘coconnective’ components of
E
. In fact, these assignments give us well-defined functors
\tau^{\leq 0}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{A}
and
\tau^{\geq 1}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}
, which are respectively called the
connective
and
coconnective truncation functors
. Given a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is an aisle. On the other hand, for an aisle
\mathcal{A}
, there is a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
where
\mathcal{B}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}^{\perp}[1]
, see
[
18
]
. A
t
-structure may also be denoted by
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
, and occasionally we use this convention. If
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
is a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{T}
and
n
is an integer, then the pair
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq n},\mathcal{T}^{\geq n})
is also a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{T}
where
\mathcal{T}^{\leq n}:=\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq n}:=\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-n]
.

Let

.

Let

2.3.
Thomason filtrations

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. We briefly discuss Thomason filtrations on such spaces. Recall that
Thomason subset
of
X
is an arbitrary union of subsets that are both closed and have a quasi-compact complement. The fact
X
is Noetherian ensures that this condition is equivalent to being a specialization closed subset of
X
. Let
\operatorname{Spcl}(X)
be the collection of specialization closed subsets of
Y
. A function
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Spcl}(X)
is called a
Thomason filtration
on
X
if for each integer
n
we have that
\phi(n)
is a Thomason subset of
X
, and
\phi(n+1)
is contained in
\phi(n)
. It is possible to ‘shift’ a Thomason filtration
\phi
by an integer
s
as follows:
\phi[s]
is determined by assignment
n\mapsto\phi(n+s)
.

Example 2.5
.

For each Thomason filtration
\phi
on
X
and for each integer
k
one can associate another Thomason filtration
\phi^{\geq k}
which is constant at
\phi(k)
for
i\leq k
as follows:

\phi^{\geq k}(i):=\begin{cases}\phi(k),&\text{for }i\leq k,\\
\phi(i),&\text{for }i\geq k\end{cases}

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a subset of
Y
and
\phi
be a Thomason filtration on
Y
.

(1)

\phi
is
eventually vanishing
if the intersection of all
\phi(n)
is empty.

(2)

The
restriction
of
\phi
to
S
, denoted
\phi\cap S
, is given by
n\mapsto\phi(n)\cap S
.

(3)

\phi
is
constant
on
S
if
(\phi\cap S)(n)
is
S
for all
n
or
\emptyset
for all
n
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
. The standard
t
-structure on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z}(X)
is determined by the Thomason filtration on
X
given
n\mapsto Z
for
n\leq 0
and
n\mapsto\emptyset
for

. This is a Thomason filtration which is nonconstant over

. We denote this by
\phi_{\operatorname{st}}
in the special case

.

Definition 2.8
.

(1)

A generalization
p\rightsquigarrow q
(i.e.
q\in\overline{\{p\}}
) is
direct
if
p\not=q
and there does not exist a point
s
in
Y
such that
p\rightsquigarrow s\rightsquigarrow q
where
p\not=s
.

(2)

A Thomason filtration
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Spcl}(Y)
is said to satisfy the
weak Cousin condition
if for every integer

, the set
\phi(n-1)
contains all direct generalization s of each point in
\phi(n)
(i.e. for each direct generalization
p\rightsquigarrow q
with

\phi(n)
, one has
p
is in

).

Reminder 2.9
.

There is a bijective correspondence between the collection of compactly generated tensor
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
and the collection of Thomason filtrations on
X
. Initially, this was proven for Noetherian affine schemes
[
1
, Theorem 3.11]
, and later extended to Noetherian schemes
[
8
, Theorem 5.11]
. However, it has been fully generalized to affine schemes in
[
14
, Theorem 5.6]
, and recently extended to algebraic stacks
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
using loc. cit. with methods independent than that of
[
8
]
. We briefly recall the assignments.

Let
\phi
be a Thomason filtration on
X
. The associated category
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is a compactly generated
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
, for which we will denote the corresponding compactly generated tensor
t
-structure by
(\mathcal{U}_{\phi},\mathcal{V}_{\phi})
and the truncation functors by
\tau_{\phi}^{\leq 0}
and
\tau_{\phi}^{\geq 1}
. Specifically,
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is the strictly full subcategory of
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
consisting of objects
E
such that
\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{i}(E))
is contained in
\phi(i)
for all integers
i
. The associated aisle
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is compactly generated by the following subcategory:

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cap D_{\operatorname{qc},\phi(n)}^{\leq n}(X)\big).

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-aisle on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
that is compactly generated by a collection
\mathcal{S}
. The associated Thomason filtration, denoted by
\phi_{\mathcal{U}}
, is given by the following:

\phi_{\mathcal{U}}(i)=\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{S}}\bigcup_{j\geq i}\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(P)).

We may impose a partial ordering on
\operatorname{Spcl}(X)
, which naturally induces a partial ordering on the collection of Thomason filtrations on
X
. Moreover, there is a partial ordering on the collection of compactly generated tensor
t
-structures on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
by inclusion on the aisles. With respect to these orders, the bijective correspondence is order preserving.

.

Let
\phi
be a Thomason filtration on

. We at times call the corresponding compactly generated tensor

-structure on

, and vice-versa for the

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. Then an aisle
\mathcal{A}
on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
being tensor is equivalent to it being closed under the tensor action by
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cap D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cap D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)\subseteq D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
, it follows that
\mathcal{A}
being tensor aisle on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
implies

(\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cap D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(X))\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}.

To see the converse, let
E\in D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathcal{A}
where
\mathcal{A}
is an aisle on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
which is closed under the tensor action by
\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cap D^{\leq 0}_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
. Consider the truncation triangle of
E
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
on
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
,

\tau^{\leq 0}E\to E\to\tau^{\geq 1}E\to\tau^{\leq 0}E.

By
[
12
, Corollary 3.4 & Theorem 3.10]
,
\overline{\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{(-\infty,0]}_{\otimes}
exists and

Proposition 3.2

4.
Relative weak Cousin

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. We need a relative version of a notion in
[
1
, §4]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Z
be a subset of
X
. A Thomason filtration
\phi
on
X
is said to be
weak Cousin across
Z
if for any direct generalization
p\rightsquigarrow q
with
p,q
in
Z
, if
q
is in
\phi(n)
, then
p
is in
\phi(n-1)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
Z
be a subset of
X
. A Thomason filtration
\phi
on
X
is weak Cousin across
Z
if, and only if,
\phi\cap Z
is weak Cousin on
Z
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
. If
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Spcl}(X)
is Thomason filtration on
X
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\phi
satisfies the weak Cousin condition across
Z

(2)

\phi\cap U
satisfies the weak Cousin condition across
Z\cap U
for any affine open subscheme
U
of
X
where
Z\cap U\not=\emptyset

(3)

\phi_{s}
satisfies the weak Cousin condition across
Z\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,s})
for all
s
in
Z
.

Proof.

To start, we show
(1)\implies(2)
. Suppose
\phi
satisfies the weak Cousin condition across
Z
. Let
j\colon U\to X
be an open immersion such that
Z\cap U\not=\emptyset
. Fix an integer
n
. Consider a direct generalization
p\rightsquigarrow q
with
q
in
(\phi\cap U)(n)
and
p
in
Z\cap U
. Then
q
is in
\overline{\{p\}}
(i.e. the closure of
p
) in
X
, which ensures that
p
is in
\phi(n-1)
, and so,
p
belongs to
(\phi\cap U)(n-1)
.

Next, we show
(2)\implies(3)
. Let
s
be a point of
Z
. Choose an affine open neighbourhood
U
of
s
in
X
. Fix an integer
n
. Suppose
p\rightsquigarrow q
is a direct generalization in
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,s})
with
p,q
in
Z\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,s})
and
q
is in
\phi_{s}(n)
. It follows that this gives us a direct generalization of points in
Z\cap U
as open sets are generalisation closed and
s
is in
U
. Thus, the hypothesis implies
p
is in
(\phi\cap U)(n-1)
, ensuring that
p
belongs to
\phi_{s}(n-1)
as desired.

Lastly, we show
(3)\implies(1)
. Fix an integer
n
. Let
p\rightsquigarrow q
be a direct generalization in
Z
where
q
is in
\phi(n)
. Then
p\rightsquigarrow q
is a direct generalization in
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,q})
where
q
is in
\phi_{q}(n)
and
p
is in
Z\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,q})
. It follows that
p
is in
\phi_{q}(n-1)
, and so,
p
is in
\phi(n-1)
.
∎

Reminder 4.4
.

We recall
[
7
, Definition 1.2]
. A locally Noetherian scheme
X
is
CM-quasi-excellent
if the following are satisfied:

(1)

the formal fibers of the local rings of
X
are Cohen-Macaulay

(2)

every integral closed subscheme contains a nonempty Cohen-Macaulay open subscheme.

Additionally, if
X
is universally catenary, then we say it is
CM-excellent
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
be a locally Noetherian scheme. The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is CM-excellent

(2)

there exists an open covering of
X
all of whose members are CM-excellent

(3)

for every affine open
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
of
X
, the ring
R
is CM-excellent

(4)

there exists an affine open covering of
X
of spectrums of CM-excellent rings.

There exists a similar statement for the case of CM-quasi-excellence.

Proof.

The property of being universally catenary for a locally Noetherian scheme is equivalent to such conditions in the desired claim, see
[
29
,
Tag 02J9
]
. Moreover, the property that formal fibers of local rings are Cohen-Macaulay is local, and so the conditions of the desired claim are equivalent in this case. We are left to verify that
(2)
of
Reminder

4.4
is equivalent to the conditions of the claim. It is evident that
(1)\implies(2)
in this setting.

Suppose there exists an open covering of
X
, say
\cup_{i}U_{i}
, all of whose members satisfy
(2)
of
Reminder

4.4
. We want to show that every open affine subscheme of
X
satisfies
(2)
of
Reminder

4.4
. Let
j\colon V\to X
be an open immersion from an affine scheme. Let
Z
be a closed integral subscheme of
V
with generic point
\xi
. We can cover by open subscheme (in
Z
) of the form
Z\cap U_{i}
. The only such
Z\cap U_{i}
we are concerned are those that are nonempty, so without of generality, assume this is the case for each
i
. We know that
Z\cap U_{i}
corresponds to an integral closed subscheme of
U_{i}
, which is CM-excellent, so there exists a nonempty Cohen-Macaulay open subscheme of
Z\cap U_{i}
, say
W_{i}
. Taking the union
W_{i}
gives us a nonempty Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of
Z
, yielding the implication
(2)\implies(3)
.

The case where
(3)\implies(4)
follows from the fact that a scheme admits an affine open cover, and
(4)\implies(1)
can be inferred by a similar argument above.
∎

Proof of

Proof of

.

First, we show for any
\otimes
-aisle
\mathcal{A}
on

5.
Restriction to perfects

Our primary goal in the remainder of this section is to establish
Theorem

1.3
. We start with a few lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring. Consider an object

in
D_{\operatorname{qc}}^{\geq n^{\prime}}(R)\cap D_{\operatorname{qc}}^{\leq n}(R)
whose support has positive dimension. Suppose the dimension of
\operatorname{supp}(F)
and
\operatorname{supp}(H^{n}(F))
coincide. Then
H^{n+r}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F)
is not a finitely generated over
R
where
r:=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{supp}(F)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
n^{\prime}=0
as

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F)
=
H^{i+j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F[-j])
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F)
is the
i
-th-cohomology of the following complex:

F\overset{\mathbf{L}}{\otimes}\big(R\to\prod_{i_{0}}R_{f_{i_{0}}}\to\prod_{i_{0}<i_{1}}R_{f_{i_{0}}f_{i_{1}}}\to R_{f_{1}\cdots f_{r}}\big)

where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
is a minimal set of generators for
\mathfrak{m}
, see
[
29
,
Tag 0A6R
]
. Denote by
A
the complex above that we are tensoring

with. Note that
A
is
K
-flat. It is possible to calculate the cohomology of the tensor product using the spectral sequence coming from the double complex. As the double complex is concentrated in the first quadrant, the spectral sequence converges. Just to fix our convention, we make explicit the zeroth page of the spectral sequence:
E_{0}^{\ast,j}=F\otimes A^{j}
for all
j
where
A^{j}
is the
j
-th component of
A
. The differentials in the first page correspond to the vertical differentials of the double complex. It is easy to see that
E_{2}^{i,j}=H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{i}(F))
. Now, as
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
R
-module
M
and

, we get that

if

or

, and so, it follows that:

H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{i}(F))=E_{2}^{n,r}=E_{\infty}^{n,r}=H^{n+r}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F).

Thus,
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{i}(F))
is not a finitely generated
R
-module, see
[
28
, Proposition A.2]

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a non-regular Noetherian local ring,

be a closed subset of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, and

be a Thomason filtration on

which is not constant on the empty set. If the t-structure associated to

restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(R)
, then

contains the constant filtration on
\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, let us suppose that
\phi
does not contain the constant filtration on
\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\phi
is not the constant filtration on the the empty set, and does not contain the constant filtration on
\mathfrak{m}
, there is a largest integer

such that

and
\phi(n+1)=\emptyset
. Since
\phi(n)
is specialization closed and non-empty, it follows that

. Denote by

the Koszul complex associated to some sequence of elements generating

in

. It follows from

3.3
(and

) that one has a string of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\tau^{\geq 0}_{\phi}(K(\mathfrak{m})[-n])

\displaystyle\cong\tau^{\geq n}_{\operatorname{st}}(K(\mathfrak{m})[-n])

\displaystyle\cong\tau^{\geq 0}_{\operatorname{st}}(K(\mathfrak{m}))[-n]

However, this tells us that the residue field

-structure of
\phi
restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{\overline{\{\mathfrak{q}\}}}(R)
. Additionally, if
\phi
is not a constant filtration, then
R
is regular.

Proof.

We can impose
\phi
be nonempty. Consider the Koszul complex
K(\mathfrak{q})
associated to
\mathfrak{q}
in

. Note that
K(\mathfrak{q})
is cohomologically concentrated some the interval

. Our hypothesis that the associated to a
t
-structure of
\phi

\operatorname{Perf}_{\overline{\{\mathfrak{q}\}}}(R)

\operatorname{Perf}(R)
for all

3.4
, that we cannot have
\phi(-n-m)=\phi(-m+1)=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
for all integers

We check the second claim. Assume the contrary, i.e.

5.2

\phi

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
Z
be a connected closed subset of a Noetherian scheme
X
. Suppose that
\phi
is a Thomason filtration on
X
whose associated
t
-structure restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
. Consider a direct generalization
q\rightsquigarrow p
in
Z
.

(1)

If
q
is not in
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
, then
p
is not in
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
.

(2)

If
p
is in
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
, then
q
is in
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
.

Proof.

We check the fist claim. Consider the Thomason filtration
\psi:=(\phi\cap\overline{\{q\}})
on
X
. Note that
q
not belonging to
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
ensures
\psi
is not constant. It follows from
Lemma

3.5
, coupled with our hypothesis on
\phi
, that
\psi
has an associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
which restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{\overline{\{q\}}}(X)
. Set
Z_{p}:=\overline{\{q\}}\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
. Then
Proposition

3.19
tells us that
\psi_{p}
has an associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc},Z_{p}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
which restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z_{p}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
. Hence, by
Proposition

5.3
, we have that
p
does not belong to
\psi_{p}(n)
for
n\gg 0
. This tells us
p
cannot belong to
(\phi\cap Z)(n)
for
n\gg 0
as desired.

The second claim can be checked by arguing in a similar by proving its contrapositive. Specifically, in such a case,
\phi\cap\overline{\{q\}}
is not constant from the hypothesis, and one appeals to Lemma
5.2
and
Proposition

5.3
to furnish the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
Z
be a connected closed subset of a Noetherian scheme
X
. Suppose that
\phi
is a Thomason filtration on
X
whose associated
t
-structure restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
. Then
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
is either
\emptyset
or
Z
, and similarly for
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)
.

Proof.

As
Z
is connected, any pair of irreducible components
Z_{s_{1}},Z_{s_{2}}
enjoy the property that there is a sequence of irreducible components
Z_{v_{1}}:=Z_{s},Z_{v_{2}},\ldots,Z_{v_{k}}:=Z_{t}
for

such that
Z_{v_{b}}\cap Z_{v_{b+1}}\not=\emptyset
. Then the claim follows from
Lemma

5.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
Z
be a connected closed subset of a Noetherian scheme
X
. Suppose
\phi
is a Thomason filtration on
X
whose associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
restricts to a
t
-structure on
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
. Then
(\phi\cap Z)(n)
is contained in the regular locus of
X
for all
n
if
\phi
is not constant on

.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. Assume there is an
n
and
p
in
(\phi\cap Z)(n)
which is not contained in the regular locus of
X
. It follows from
Lemma

5.2
that
p
is in
(\phi\cap Z)(t)
for all
t
as
(\phi\cap Z)_{p}
is not constant on
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
and has an associated
t
-structure which restricts to
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
by
Proposition

3.19
. Then
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)

Lemma

5.5
that
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\phi\cap Z)(n)=Z
. However, we see that
\phi
must be constant on

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
Z
be a connected closed subset of a Noetherian scheme
X
. Suppose
\phi
is a Thomason filtration on
X
whose associated
t
-structure on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
restricts to a
t
-structure on
\operatorname{Perf}_{Z}(X)
. Then
\phi
is weak Cousin across
Z
.

Proof.

The case
\phi
is constant on
Z
is obvious, and so, we can impose this is not the case.
It follows by
Proposition

5.6
that
(\phi\cap Z)(n)
is contained in the regular locus of
X
for all
n
. Choose

that
\phi\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})

-structure on

\operatorname{Perf}_{Z\cap\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
Z
be a closed subset of a Noetherian scheme. Denote the connected components of
Z
by
Z_{t}
. Suppose
\phi
is a Thomason filtration on
X
. Then
\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi}(E)
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{t}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E
and
\tau^{\geq 1}_{\phi}(E)
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{t}\tau^{\geq 1}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E
if
E
is an object of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z}(X)
.

Proof.

We know that the connective and coconnective truncation functors commute with finite coproducts. It follows by
[
15
, Lemma 3.9]
that there are objects
E_{t}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh},Z_{t}}(X)
such that
E
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{t}E_{t}
. There is a string of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi}(E)

\displaystyle\cong\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi}(\bigoplus_{t}E_{t})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{t}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi}E_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{t}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E_{t}

\displaystyle(\textrm{by \lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{prop:truncations_preserve_support}}).

It remains to show
\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E
is isomorphic to
\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E_{t}
for each
t
. We have another string of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}(E)

\displaystyle\cong\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}(\bigoplus_{s}E_{s})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{s}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}}E_{s}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{s}\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}\cap Z_{s}}E_{s}

\displaystyle(\textrm{by \lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{prop:truncations_preserve_support}}).

Note that
Z_{s}\cap Z_{t}=\emptyset
for
s\not=t
, and so,
\tau^{\leq 0}_{\phi\cap Z_{t}\cap Z_{s}}E_{s}=0
in such cases. Hence, the desired claim for connective truncations has been shown, and a similar argument gives the claim for coconnective truncations.
∎

1.
Introduction

Let
q
be a prime power. We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the finite field of order
q
. For any given positive integer
m
, let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
denote the extension field of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
. The multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
is cyclic and a generator of this group is called a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is said to be normal if the set of all its conjugates with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is, if the set
\{\alpha,\alpha^{q},\ldots,\alpha^{q^{m-1}}\}
forms a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The trace of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, denoted by Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)
, is the sum of all conjugates of
\alpha
with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is, Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=\alpha+\alpha^{q}+\ldots+\alpha^{q^{m-1}}
.

An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is said to be a primitive normal if it is both primitive and normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We refer the readers to
[
11
]
for the existence of primitive and normal elements in finite fields. In
[
10
]
, Lenstra and Schoof first established the simultaneous existence of both primitive and normal elements in finite fields. Later on, by implementing sieving techniques, Cohen and Huczynska
[
5
]
gave a computer-free proof of it.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, we call a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
to be a primitive normal pair if both
\alpha
and
\beta
are primitive and normal elements over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For any rational function
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
, the existence of primitive pairs and also primitive normal pairs
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
has been an area of much research interest and a significant amount of work has been published in this direction
[
18
,
17
,
8
,
15
]
.

In 1999, Cohen and Hachenberger
[
4
]
proved the existence of a primitive normal element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a prescribed trace. In 2001, Chou and Cohen
[
1
]
proved the existence of an element
\alpha
such that both
\alpha
and
\alpha^{-1}
have zero traces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In 2018, Gupta, Sharma and Cohen
[
7
]
, proved the existence of a primitive pair
(\alpha,\alpha+\alpha^{-1})
with Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=a
, for any prescribed
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and for all
q
and
n\geq 5
. Recently, R. K. Sharma et al.
[
16
]
proved the existence of a primitive pair
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
such that
\alpha
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=a
, for a prescribed
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
is a rational function with some exceptions.

In this article, we consider a primitive normal element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
explore the existence of a primitive normal pair
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
such that Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=a
, where
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
is a rational function with some minor restrictions. We consider rational functions
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
which belong to the set
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
as defined below.

Definition 1.1
.

The set
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
is the collection of rational functions
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
with simplest form
f_{1}/f_{2}
, where
n_{1},n_{2}
are the degrees of
f_{1},f_{2}
respectively, with
n_{1}+n_{2}=n
i.e. degree sum of
f
is
n
, such that

(i)

f\neq cx^{i}g^{d}
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
and
d(>1)
divides
q^{m}-1
.

(ii)

f_{2}
is monic such that
f_{2}\neq g^{q^{m}}
for any
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
.

We note that if
f(x)=g(x)^{d}
for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
and
d>1
which divides
q^{m}-1
, then
f(\alpha)
(for any primitive element
\alpha
) is necessarily a
d^{\text{th}}
power and hence it cannot be primitive. Also, part
(ii)
of the above definition forces that
n_{2}\geq 1
. For the case
n_{2}=0
, i.e. for polynomials
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, the existence of a primitive normal element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
f(\alpha)
is also a primitive normal element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with prescribed
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
has been studied in
[
14
]
.

Furthermore, from
[
1
]
we have that for
m\leq 2
, there does not exist any primitive element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=0
. Therefore, throughout the article we shall assume that
m\geq 3
. Next we introduce some sets and notations which play an important role in this article. For
q=p^{k}
, we denote by
\mathbb{F}
the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\mathfrak{T}
denote the set of pairs
(q,m)
such that, for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f\in\mathfrak{E}_{n}
there exists an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=a
, for which (
\alpha
,

) is a primitive normal pair in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each

, we denote by
\omega(n)
and

, the number prime divisors of

and the number of square-free divisors of

respectively. Also for

, we denote by
\omega(r)
and

-divisors of

-divisors of

More precisely, in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain an asymptotic condition for the existence of the desired pair

. Next, in Sections

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and results and provide some preliminary notations which are used to prove the main results of this article.

In our work, the characteristic functions of primitive and normal elements play an important role. To represent those functions, the idea of character of finite abelian group is necessary.

Definition 2.1

(Character)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a finite abelian group. A character
\chi
of the group
\mathbb{G}
is a homomorphism from
\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
is the multiplicative group of complex numbers of unit modulus, i.e.
\chi(a_{1}a_{2})=\chi(a_{1})\chi(a_{2})
, for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
.

The characters of
\mathbb{G}
form a group under multiplication defined by
(\chi_{\alpha}\chi_{\beta})(a)=\chi_{\alpha}(a)\chi_{\beta}(a)
, called dual group or character group of
\mathbb{G}
denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
, which is isomorphic to the group
\mathbb{G}
. If the order of an element (i.e. a character) of the group
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
is
d
, then characters of order
d
is denoted by
(d)
. Further, since
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}}\cong\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}}
is cyclic and for any divisor
d
of
q^{m}-1
there are exactly
\phi(d)
characters of order
d
in
\widehat{\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}}
. The special character
\chi_{1}:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
defined as
\chi_{1}(a)=1
, for all
a\in\mathbb{G}
is called the trivial character of
\mathbb{G}
.

In a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
there are two group structures, one is the additive group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and another is the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
. Therefore we have two types of characters pertaining to these two group structures, one is the additive character for
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
denoted by
\psi
and the another one is the multiplicative character for
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
denoted by
\chi
. The multiplicative characters associated with
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
are extended from
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
by the rule

\chi(0)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\chi\neq\chi_{0},\\
1,&\text{if }\chi=\chi_{0}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2

(
e
-free element)
.

Let
e\mid q^{m}-1
, then an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
is called
e
-free, if
d\mid e
and
\alpha=y^{d}
, for some
y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
implies
d=1
.

It is clear from the definition that an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
is primitive if and only if it is a
(q^{m}-1)
-free element. The
p
-free of a natural number
r
is denoted by
r_{0}
, where
r=r_{0}p^{k}
such that
\mathrm{gcd}(r_{0},p)=1
. It is clear from the definition that
q^{m}-1
can be freely replaced by its
p
-free part,
q_{0}
.

For any
e\mid q^{m}-1
, the characteristic function for the subset of
e
-free elements of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}
is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\rho_{e}:\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{m}}\rightarrow\{0,1\};\alpha\mapsto\theta(e)\sum_{d\mid e}\left(\frac{\mu(d)}{\phi(d)}\sum_{(d)}\chi_{d}(\alpha)\right),

where
\theta(e):=\phi(e)/e
,
\mu
is the Möbius function and
\chi_{d}
stands for any character of
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}}
of order
d
.

The additive group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-module under the rule

f\circ\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\alpha^{q^{i}}
; for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}\thinspace\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order of
\alpha
is the monic
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-divisor
g
of
x^{m}-1
of minimal degree such that
g\circ\alpha=0
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order
of an additive character
\chi_{g}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
is the monic
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-divisor
g
of
x^{m}-1
of minimal degree such that
\chi_{g}\circ g
is the trivial character of
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
, where
\chi_{g}\circ g(\alpha)=\chi_{g}(g\circ\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Definition 2.3

(
g
-free element)
.

For
g\mid x^{m}-1
, an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called
g
-free element if
\alpha=h\circ\beta
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
h\mid g
imply
h=1
, where if
h=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, then
h\circ\beta=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\beta^{q^{i}}
.

From the definition, it is obvious that an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is normal if and only if it is
(x^{m}-1)
-free. It is clear that
x^{m}-1
can be freely replaced by its
p
-radical
g_{0}:=x^{m_{0}}-1
, where
m_{0}
is such that
m=m_{0}p^{a}
, here
a
is a non-negative integer and
\mathrm{gcd}(m_{0},p)=1
.

For any
g\mid x^{m}-1
, the characteristic function for the subset of
g
-free elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\eta_{g}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\rightarrow\{0,1\};\alpha\mapsto\Theta(g)\sum_{f\mid g}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(f)}{\Phi(f)}\sum_{(f)}\psi_{f}(\alpha)\right),

where
\Theta(g):=\Phi(g)/{q^{deg(g)}}
,
\psi_{f}
stands for any character of
\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order
f
and
\mu^{\prime}
is the analogue of the Möbius function defined as follows

\mu^{\prime}(g)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{s},&\text{if $g$ is the product of $s$ distinct irreducible monic polynomials},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

For each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
the characteristic function for the subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
consisting of elements with Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=a
is defined as follows:

(2.3)

\tau_{a}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\rightarrow\{0,1\};\alpha\mapsto\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}\psi\left(\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)-a\right).

The additive character
\psi_{1}
defined by
\psi_{1}(\beta)=e^{{2{\pi}i\text{Tr}(\beta)}/p}
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, where Tr is the absolute trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
is called the
canonical additive character
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and every additive character
\psi_{\beta}

for
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
can be expressed in terms of the canonical additive character
\psi_{1}
as

\psi_{\beta}(\gamma)=\psi_{1}(\beta\gamma)
, for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Thus we have that

\displaystyle\tau_{a}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}\left(\text{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(u\alpha)-ua\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\hat{\psi_{1}}(u\alpha)\psi_{1}(-ua),

where
\hat{\psi_{1}}
is the lift of
\psi_{1}
, that is, the additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
defined by
\hat{\psi_{1}}(\alpha)=\psi_{1}(\text{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha))
. In particular,
\hat{\psi_{1}}
is the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

The following results are due to Wan and Fu

]
and are crucial for proving our sufficient condition.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 5.5]

Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
be a rational function. Write
f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}(x)^{r_{j}}
, where
f_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
are irreducible polynomials and

are non zero integers. Let
\chi
be a non-trivial multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of precise square-free order

(a divisor of

). Suppose that

is not of the form

\left|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}},f(\alpha)\neq 0,\infty}\chi(f(\alpha))\right|\leq\left({\sum_{j=1}^{k}deg(f_{j})}-1\right)q^{m/2}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
6
, Theorem 5.6]

Let

, where

,

, and

be the sum of degrees of those irreducible polynomials dividing the denominator of

. Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of

is not of the form

3.
The Main Result

In this section, we present a sufficient condition for the existence of elements
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that both
\alpha
and
f(\alpha)
are simultaneously primitive normal elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=a
, where

.

Let
e_{1},e_{2}
be such that
e_{1},e_{2}\mid q^{m}-1
and
g_{1},g_{2}
be such that
g_{1},g_{2}\mid x^{m}-1
. Denote by
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})
be the number of
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, such that
\alpha
is both
e_{1}
-free and
g_{1}
-free and
f(\alpha)
is
e_{2}
-free and
g_{2}
-free with Tr
{}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha^{-1})=a
; where
f(x)\in\mathfrak{E}_{n}
. Let
P
be the set containing
0
, and zeros and poles of
f
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\geq 3
. Suppose
e_{1},e_{2}
divide
q^{m}-1
and
g_{1},g_{2}
divide

. Then we have that

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})\geq\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})\Theta(g_{1})\Theta(g_{2})q^{m/2}(q^{m/2}-(2n+1)W(e_{1})W(e_{2})W(g_{1})W(g_{2})).

In particular, we have
(q,m)\in\mathfrak{T}
provided that

(3.1)

q^{m/2}>(2n+1)W(q^{m}-1)^{2}W(x^{m}-1)^{2}.

Proof.

To prove this result, it is enough to show that
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})>0
for every
f\in\mathfrak{E}_{n}
and prescribed

. From the definition we have,

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\rho_{e_{1}}(\alpha)\rho_{e_{2}}(f(\alpha))\eta_{g_{1}}(\alpha)\eta_{g_{2}}(f(\alpha))\tau_{a}(\alpha^{-1}).

Now using (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})\Theta(g_{1})\Theta(g_{2})}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid e_{1},d_{2}\mid e_{2}\\
h_{1}\mid g_{1},h_{2}\mid g_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2})\mu^{\prime}(h_{1})\mu^{\prime}(h_{2})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2})\Phi(h_{1})\Phi(h_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{d_{1}},\chi_{d_{2}}\\
\psi_{h_{1}},\psi_{h_{2}}\end{subarray}}\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vartheta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\mid e_{1},d_{2}\mid e_{2}\\
h_{1}\mid g_{1},h_{2}\mid g_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2})\mu^{\prime}(h_{1})\mu^{\prime}(h_{2})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2})\Phi(h_{1})\Phi(h_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{d_{1}},\chi_{d_{2}}\\
\psi_{h_{1}},\psi_{h_{2}}\end{subarray}}\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2}),

where
\vartheta=\theta(e_{1})\theta(e_{2})\Theta(g_{1})\Theta(g_{2})/{q}
and

\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{d_{1}}(\alpha)\chi_{d_{2}}(f(\alpha))\psi_{h_{1}}(\alpha)\psi_{h_{2}}(f(\alpha))\hat{\psi}_{1}(u\alpha^{-1}).

Further, there exists
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
\psi_{h_{i}}(\alpha)=\widehat{\psi}_{1}(u_{i}\alpha)
, for

where
\hat{\psi}_{1}
is the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Hence

(3.3)

\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{d_{1}}(\alpha)\chi_{d_{2}}(f(\alpha))\hat{\psi}_{1}(u_{1}\alpha+u_{2}f(\alpha)+u\alpha^{-1}).

Now, for
d_{2}=1

\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{d_{1}}(\alpha)\hat{\psi}_{1}(u_{1}\alpha+u_{2}f(\alpha)+u\alpha^{-1}).

If

for any
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}(x)
then using Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})|\leq

\displaystyle(n+1)q^{m/2+1}+(|S|-1)q

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}.

Suppose

(3.4)

u_{1}x+u_{2}f(x)+ux^{-1}=\mathfrak{R}(x)^{q^{m}}-\mathfrak{R}(x),

for some
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}(x)
. We will show that this is feasible only if
u_{1}=u_{2}=u=0
. Write
f={f_{1}}/{f_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{R}={r_{1}}/{r_{2}}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}
are co-prime polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
,
r_{1},r_{2}
are co-prime polynomials over
\mathbb{F}[x]
and
f_{2},r_{2}
are both monic. Then we have that

(3.5)

r_{2}^{q^{m}}(u_{1}x^{2}f_{2}+u_{2}xf_{1}+uf_{2})=xf_{2}(r_{1}^{q^{m}}-r_{1}r_{2}^{q^{m}-1}).

Suppose
u_{2}\neq 0
.
Since
\gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1
, we get that
r_{2}^{q^{m}}\mid xf_{2}
. Further, since
\gcd(f_{1},f_{2})=1
, we get that
f_{2}\mid r_{2}^{q^{m}}\Rightarrow r_{2}^{q^{m}}=\kappa f_{2}
. It follows that,
r_{2}^{q^{m}}=f_{2}
or
r_{2}^{q^{m}}=xf_{2}
. The former case implies that
f\not\in\mathfrak{T}_{n}
, a contradiction, thus,

(3.6)

r_{2}^{q^{m}}=xf_{2}.

By combining, (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

u_{1}x^{2}f_{2}+u_{2}xf_{1}+uf_{2}=r_{1}^{q^{m}}-r_{1}r_{2}^{q^{m}-1}.

However, notice that (
3.6
) implies that
x\mid f_{2}
and
x\mid r_{2}
and, plugging in the above to (
3.7
), one obtains
x\mid r_{1}
, which contradicts
\gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1
. It follows that
u_{2}=0
. Now, (
3.4
) becomes
u_{1}x+ux^{-1}=\mathfrak{R}(x)^{q^{m}}-\mathfrak{R}(x)
, which can be easily lead to a contradiction if we further assume that
u_{1}\neq 0
or
u\neq 0
, by comparing degrees.

Therefore, when
u_{1}x+u_{2}f(x)+ux^{-1}=\mathfrak{R}(x)^{q^{m}}-\mathfrak{R}(x)
for
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}(x)
then
u_{1}=u_{2}=u=0
. This implies

|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})|\leq|S|q\leq(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}.

Now suppose
d_{2}>1
. Let
d
be the least common multiple of
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Then there exists a character
\chi_{d}
of order
d
such that
\chi_{d_{2}}=\chi_{d}^{d/d_{2}}
. Again there is an integer
0\leq k\leq q^{m}-1
such that
\chi_{d_{1}}=\chi_{d}^{k}
. Substituting these in (
3.3
), we have the following expression,

(3.8)

\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{d}(\alpha^{k}f(\alpha)^{d/d_{2}})\hat{\psi}_{1}(u_{1}\alpha+u_{2}f(\alpha)+u\alpha^{-1}).

Now let us first suppose that
u_{1}x+u_{2}f(x)+ux^{-1}\neq\mathfrak{R}(x)^{q^{m}}-\mathfrak{R}(x)
. Then Lemma
2.2
gives

|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})|\leq(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}.

Again if
u_{1}x+u_{2}f(x)+ux^{-1}=\mathfrak{R}(x)^{q^{m}}-\mathfrak{R}(x)
for some
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}(x)
, then
u_{1}=u_{2}=u=0
. Substituting
u_{1}=u_{2}=u=0
in (
3.8
) we get

(3.9)

\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{0}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{d}(\alpha^{k}f(\alpha)^{d/d_{2}}).

In this case if
x^{k}f(x)^{d/d_{2}}\neq c\mathfrak{R}(x)^{d}
for any polynomial
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, then Lemma
2.1
implies that

|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})|\leq nq^{m/2+1}\leq(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}.

Next suppose that
x^{k}f(x)^{d/d_{2}}=c\mathfrak{R}(x)^{d}
for some polynomial
\mathfrak{R}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}(x)
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Then we have that
f\notin\mathfrak{E}_{n}
.

Summarizing all the above cases we observe that

|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})|\leq(2n+1)q^{m/2+1},

when
(d_{1},d_{2},h_{1},h_{2})\neq(1,1,1,1)
.

Therefore from (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})\geq

\displaystyle\vartheta[q^{m}-(n+1)q-((2n+1)q^{m/2+1})(W(e_{1})W(e_{2})W(g_{1})W(g_{2})-1)]

(3.10)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\vartheta[q^{m}-(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}W(e_{1})W(e_{2})W(g_{1})W(g_{2})].

Hence
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})>0
if
q^{m/2}>(2n+1)W(e_{1})W(e_{2})W(g_{1})W(g_{2})
.

It is clear from the above discussion that
(q,m)\in\mathfrak{T}
, when
e_{1}=e_{2}=q^{m}-1
and
g_{1}=g_{2}=x^{m}-1
, that is provided

q^{m/2-1}>(2n+1)W(q^{m}-1)^{2}W(x^{m}-1)^{2}.\qed

4.
The prime sieve technique

In this section we apply the sieving inequality established by Kapetanakis in
[
9
]
to give a modified form of (
3
).

Lemma 4.1

(Sieving Inequality)
.

Let
d
be a divisor of
q^{m}-1
and
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
be the remaining distinct primes dividing
q^{m}-1
. Furthermore, let
g
be a divisor of
x^{m}-1
such that
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{s}
are the remaining distinct irreducible factors of
x^{m}-1
. Abbreviate
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(q^{m}-1,q^{m}-1,x^{m}-1,x^{m}-1)
to
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}
. Then

(4.1)

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(p_{i}d,d,g,g)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,p_{i}d,g,g)+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g_{i}g,g)\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g_{i}g)-(2r+2s-1)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g).

An upper bound for some differences that will be needed later is provided by the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d,m,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be such that q is a prime power,
m\geq 3
,
d\mid q^{m}-1
and
g\mid x^{m}-1
. Let
e
be a prime number which divides
q^{m}-1
but not
d
, and
h
be an irreducible polynomial dividing
x^{m}-1
but not
g
. Then we have the following bounds:

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(ed,d,g,g)-\theta(e)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|

\displaystyle\leq(2n+1)\theta(e)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,ed,g,g)-\theta(e)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|

\displaystyle\leq(2n+1)\theta(e)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,hg,g)-\Theta(h)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|

\displaystyle\leq(2n+1)\Theta(h)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,hg)-\Theta(h)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|

\displaystyle\leq(2n+1)\Theta(h)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2}.

Proof.

From the definition, we have

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(ed,d,g,g)-\theta(e)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)\\
=\frac{\vartheta}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\mid d_{1}\mid ed,\ d_{2}\mid d\\
g_{1}\mid g,\ g_{2}\mid g\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d_{1})\mu(d_{2})\mu^{\prime}(g_{1})\mu^{\prime}(g_{2})}{\phi(d_{1})\phi(d_{2})\Phi(g_{1})\Phi(g_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{g_{1}},\chi_{g_{2}}\\
\psi_{h_{1}},\psi_{h_{2}}\end{subarray}}\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},g_{1},g_{2}).

Now, using
|\chi_{f,a}(d_{1},d_{2},g_{1},g_{2})|\leq(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}
, we get

|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(ed,d,g,g)-\theta(e)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|\\
\leq\frac{\theta(e)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}}{q}(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}W(d)\left(W(ed)-W(d)\right)W(g)^{2}.

Since
W(ed)=W(e)W(d)=2W(d)
, we have

|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(ed,d,g,g)-\theta(e)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)|\leq\frac{\theta(e)\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}}{q}(2n+1)q^{m/2+1}W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}.

We obtain the remaining bounds in a similar fashion.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
d,m,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be such that q is a prime power,
m\geq 3
,
d\mid q^{m}-1
and
g\mid x^{m}-1
. Let
d
be a divisor of
q^{m}-1
and
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r}
be the remaining distinct primes dividing
q^{m}-1
. Furthermore, let
g
be a divisor of
x^{m}-1
such that
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{s}
are the remaining distinct irreducible factors of
x^{m}-1
. Define

\lambda:=1-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{p_{i}}-2\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{q^{{\mathrm{d}eg}(g_{i})}},\ \lambda>0

and

\Lambda:=\frac{2r+2s-1}{\delta}+2.

Then
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}>0
if

(4.2)

q^{m/2-1}>(2n+1)W(l)^{2}W(g)^{2}\Lambda.

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(p_{i}d,d,g,g)-\theta(p_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)\right\}+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,p_{i}d,g,g)-\theta(p_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)\right\}+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g_{i}g,g)-\theta(g_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)\right\}+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g_{i}g)-\theta(g_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g)\right\}+\lambda\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(d,d,g,g).

Now using Lemma
4.2
, we have the following expression

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}\geq\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}[\left(2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta(p_{i})+2\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Theta(g_{i})\right)\left(-(2n+1)W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2}\right)\\
+\lambda\left\{q^{m-1}-(n+1)-(2n+1)q^{m/2}(W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}-1)\right\}],

thus,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}\geq

\displaystyle\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}\Bigg[\lambda\left(\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta(p_{i})+2\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Theta(g_{i})+1}{\lambda}\right)\left(-(2n+1)W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\{q^{m-1}-(n+1)-(2n+1)q^{m/2}\right\}\Bigg]

We note that
\lambda=2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta(p_{i})+2\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Theta(g_{i})-(2r+2s+1)
. Then the above becomes

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}\geq

\displaystyle\theta(d)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}\lambda\left[\left(-(2n+1)W(d)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2}\Lambda\right)+\left\{q^{m-1}-(n+1)-(2n+1)q^{m/2}\right\}\right],

where
\Lambda={2r+2s-1}/{\lambda}+2
.

Therefore when
\lambda>0
, (
4.2
) implies
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}>0
.
∎

The technique of applying the Prime Sieve criterion given by Theorem
4.3
is to include all the small primes in
q^{m}-1
in
d
keeping as many as possible in
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
so long as
\lambda
is positive.

5.
The Modified Prime Sieve Technique

In this section we present a modified sieving criterion which is more effective than Theorem
4.3
, that was first introduced in
[
3
]
. To proceed further we need the following notations and conventions which we adopt from
[
3
]
. Let Rad(
q^{m}-1
)=
kPL
, where
k
is the product of the smallest prime divisors of
q^{m}-1
,
L
is the product of the large prime divisors of
q^{m}-1
denoted by
L=l_{1}.l_{2}\ldots l_{t}
, and the rest of the prime divisors of
q^{m}-1
are in
P
and denoted by
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Similarly we have Rad(
x^{m}-1
)=
gGH
, where
g
is the product of irreducible factors of
x^{m}-1
of least degree. Irreducible factors of large degree are factors of
H
denoted by
h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{u}
and the rest of the factors of
x^{m}-1
lie in
G
and are denoted by
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{s}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
m,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q
is a prime power and
m\geq 3
. With notations as above, let Rad(
q^{m}-1
)=
kPL
, Rad(
x^{m}-1
)=
gGH
. Define

\lambda:=1-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{p_{i}}-2\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{q^{{\mathrm{d}eg}(g_{i})}},\lambda>0,\epsilon_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{1}{l_{i}},\epsilon_{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{u}\frac{1}{q^{{\mathrm{d}eg}(h_{i})}}

and let

\lambda\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}-(2\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2})>0.

Then

(5.1)

q^{\frac{m}{2}-1}>(2n+1)\bigg[\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(k)^{2}W(g)^{2}(2r+2s-1+2\lambda)\\
+\frac{(n+2)(t-\epsilon_{1})+(n+3)(u-\epsilon_{2})}{(2n+1)}-\frac{n}{(2n+1)q^{m/2}}(t+u-(2\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2}))\bigg]\\
/\bigg[\lambda\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}-(2\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2})\bigg].

Proof.

It is clear that

(5.2)

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(q^{m}-1,q^{m}-1,x^{m}-1,x^{m}-1)=\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(kPL,kPL,gGH,gGH)\\
\geq\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(kP,kP,gG,gG)+\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(L,L,H,H)-\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1).

Now using Lemma
4.1
we have,

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(kP,kP,gG,gG)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(kp_{i},k,g,g)-\theta(p_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,g)\right\}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,kp_{i},g,g)-\theta(p_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,g)\right\}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,gg_{i},g)-\theta(g_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,g)\right\}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,gg_{i})-\theta(g_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,g)\right\}+\lambda\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(k,k,g,g).

Further, by Lemma
4.2
and (
3
)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(kP,kP,gG,gG)\geq

\displaystyle\lambda\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}\left[q^{m-1}-(2n+1)q^{m/2}W(k)^{2}W(g)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-(2n+1)\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}W(k)^{2}W(g)^{2}q^{m/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta(p_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\theta(g_{i})\right)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\theta(k)^{2}\Theta(g)^{2}\left[\lambda.q^{m-1}-q^{m/2}(2n+1)W(k)^{2}W(g)^{2}\left(2r+2s-1+2\lambda\right)\right]

Now using Lemma
4.1
, we arrive at

(5.4)

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(L,L,H,H)-\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(l_{i},1,1,1)+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,l_{i},1,1)+\\
\sum_{i=1}^{u}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,h_{i},1)+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,h_{i})-(2t+2u)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\\

Thus

(5.5)

\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(L,L,H,H)-\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(l_{i},1,1,1)-\theta(l_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\right\}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,l_{i},1,1)-\theta(l_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,h_{i},1)-\Theta(h_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\right\}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{u}\left\{\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,h_{i})-\Theta(h_{i})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\right\}-(2t+2u)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1).

Let
l
be a prime divisor of
q^{m}-1
. By the definition of
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(e_{1},e_{2},g_{1},g_{2})
we have the following

|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(l,1,1,1)-\theta(l)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)|=\frac{\theta(l)}{q}\left|\sum_{\psi_{\ell}}\chi_{f,a}(l,1,1,1)\right|,

where

\left|\chi_{f,a}(l,1,1,1)\right|=\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{l}(\alpha)\hat{\psi}_{1}(u\alpha^{-1})\right|\leq q^{m/2+1}+nq.

Therefore
|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(l,1,1,1)-\theta(l)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)|\leq\theta(l)(q^{m/2}+n)
. Similarly we get,

\left|\chi_{f,a}(1,l,1,1)\right|=\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\chi_{l}(f(\alpha))\hat{\psi}_{1}(u\alpha^{-1})\right|\leq(n+1)q^{m/2+1}

This implies that
|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,l,1,1)-\theta(l)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)|\leq\theta(l)(n+1)q^{m/2}
.

Again, for an irreducible divisor
h
of
x^{m}-1
,

\displaystyle\left|\chi_{f,a}(1,1,h,1)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\psi_{h}(\alpha)\hat{\psi}_{1}(u\alpha^{-1})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\hat{\psi}_{1}(u_{1}\alpha+u\alpha^{-1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~2q^{m/2+1}+nq.

Therefore,
|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,h,1)-\Theta(h)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)|\leq\Theta(h)(2q^{m/2}+n)
.

Again, similarly we have

\displaystyle\left|\chi_{f,a}(1,1,1,h)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\psi_{h}(f(\alpha))\hat{\psi}_{1}(u\alpha^{-1})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi_{1}(-au)\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus P}\hat{\psi}_{1}(u_{2}f(\alpha)+u\alpha^{-1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~(n+1)q^{m/2+1}.

We then have
|\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,h)-\Theta(h)\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)|\leq\Theta(h)(n+1)q^{m/2}
.

Using these bounds in (
5.5
) we get,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(L,L,H,H)-\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\geq

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\theta(l_{i})(q^{m/2}+n)-\sum_{i=1}^{t}\theta(l_{i})(n+1)q^{m/2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{u}\Theta(h_{i})(2q^{m/2}+n)-\sum_{i=1}^{u}\Theta(h_{i})(n+1)q^{m/2}

\displaystyle-(2\epsilon_{1}+2\epsilon_{2})\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1).

Now, for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
we have
\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\leq q^{m-1}
. Also
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\theta(l_{i})=t-\epsilon_{1}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{u}\Theta(h_{i})=u-\epsilon_{2}
, which imply

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(L,L,H,H)-\mathfrak{N}_{f,a}(1,1,1,1)\geq

\displaystyle-(q^{m/2}+n)(t-\epsilon_{1})-(n+1)q^{m/2}(t-\epsilon_{1})

\displaystyle-(2q^{m/2}+n)(u-\epsilon_{2})-(n+1)q^{m/2}(u-\epsilon_{2})

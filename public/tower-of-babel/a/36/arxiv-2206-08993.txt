A bijection between K K -Kohnert diagrams and reverse set-valued tableaux
Jianping Pan, Tianyi Yu

Abstract
. Lascoux polynomials are K K -theoretic analogues of the key polynomials. They both have combinatorial formulas involving tableaux: reverse set-valued tableaux ( 𝖱𝖲𝖵𝖳 \mathsf{RSVT} ) rule for Lascoux polynomials and reverse semistandard Young tableaux ( 𝖱𝖲𝖲𝖸𝖳 \mathsf{RSSYT} ) rule for key polynomials. Furthermore, key polynomials have a simple algorithmic model in terms of Kohnert diagrams, which are in bijection with 𝖱𝖲𝖲𝖸𝖳 \mathsf{RSSYT} . Ross and Yong introduced K K -Kohnert diagrams, which are analogues of Kohnert diagrams. They conjectured a K K -Kohnert diagram rule for Lascoux polynomials. We establish this conjecture by constructing a weight-preserving bijection between 𝖱𝖲𝖵𝖳 \mathsf{RSVT} and K K -Kohnert diagrams.

2.
Background

2.1.
\mathsf{RSSYT}(\alpha)
and
\mathsf{RSVT}(\alpha)

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{\ell}\geqslant 0)
, a
Young diagram
of shape
\lambda
is a finite collection of boxes, aligned at the left, in which the
i^{th}
row has
\lambda_{i}
boxes.
We use English convention for our Young diagrams and tableaux,
so the first row is the highest row.

A
reverse semistandard Young tableau
of shape
\lambda
is a filling of the Young diagram
\lambda
with positive number such that

(i)

each box contains exactly one number,

(ii)

the entries in each row weakly decrease from left to right, and

(iii)

the entries of each column strictly decrease from top to bottom.

Let
\mathsf{RSSYT}_{\lambda}
be the set of all the reverse semistandard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
.

Following
[
B:Gr
]
,
we introduce another set of tableaux
where a box might have more than one number.
A
reverse set-valued tableau
of shape
\lambda
is a filling of the Young diagram
\lambda
with positive numbers such that

(i)

each box contains a finite and non-empty set of positive integers,

(ii)

if a set
A
is to the left of a set
B
in the same row, then
\min(A)\geqslant\max(B)
, and

(iii)

if a set
C
is below a set
A
in the same column, then
\min(A)>\max(C)
.

Let
\mathsf{RSVT}_{\lambda}
be all the reverse set-valued tableaux of shape
\lambda
.

Let the
weight vector
for
T
be the weak composition whose
i^{th}
component is the the total number of appearance of
i
in
T
, denoted by
\mathsf{wt}(T)
. Given any weak composition
\alpha
, let
|\alpha|=\sum_{i\geqslant 1}\alpha_{i}
. Given
T\in\mathsf{RSVT}_{\lambda}
, define
\mathsf{L}(T)
to be an element in
\mathsf{RSSYT}_{\lambda}
constructed by only keeping the largest number in each box of
T
. We call these numbers the
leading numbers
of
T
. Any number in
T
that is not a leading number is called a
extra number
. Let the
excess
of
T
be the number of extra numbers in
T
, so we can denote it by
\mathsf{ex}(T)=|\mathsf{wt}(T)|-|\lambda|
.

Next we give the definition of
left key
of
T
, denoted by
\mathsf{K_{-}}(T)
, where
T
is a
\mathsf{RSSYT}
. It was first given in
[
W
, Section 5]
. We give the description as in
[
SY
, Definition 3.11]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
C_{1},C_{2}
be two adjacent columns from a
\mathsf{RSSYT}
with
C_{1}
on the left.
We may view
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
as sets.
We define
C_{1}\lhd C_{2}
as follows. Assume
C_{2}=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{m}\}
. Start by finding the smallest
b_{1}\in C_{1}
such that
b_{1}\geqslant a_{1}
. Then find the smallest
b_{2}\in C_{2}
such that
b_{2}\geqslant a_{2}
and
b_{2}>b_{1}
. Continue until we find all
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{m}
. Then
C_{1}\lhd C_{2}:=b_{1}<b_{2}<\dots b_{m}
. Let
C_{1},\dots C_{k}
be
k
columns in a
\mathsf{RSSYT}
, then we can define recursively,

C_{1}\lhd C_{2}\dots\lhd C_{k}:=C_{1}\lhd(C_{2}\lhd\dots\lhd C_{k}).

Given a reverse semistandard Young tableau
T
with columns
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{n}
. Then its left key
\mathsf{K_{-}}(T)
is a
\mathsf{RSSYT}
constructed by taking
C_{1}\lhd C_{2}\dots\lhd C_{k}
as its
k^{th}
column.

Given a reverse set-valued tableau
T
,
its left key
K_{-}(T)
is defined as
K_{-}(\mathsf{L}(T))
.

In this subsection, we introduce another perspective

A

is a finite subset
of
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
.
We may represent a diagram by putting a box at row
r

and column

(c,r)
in the diagram.
We adopt the convention where columns begin at
1

1
from the bottom.
The

of a diagram

,
denoted as
\mathsf{wt}(D)

is a weak composition
whose
i^{th}
entry is the number of boxes
in its
i^{th}
row.

A

is
an ordered pair
D=(D_{1},D_{2})

such that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are disjoint diagrams.
We may represent
D
by putting a box
at
(c,r)
for each
(c,r)\in D_{1}
and putting a box
with label
\mathsf{X}
at
(c,r)
for each
(c,r)\in D_{2}
.
Cells in
D_{1}
are called
Kohnert cells
.
Cells in
D_{2}
are called
ghost cells
.
The
weight
of
D
,
denoted as
\mathsf{wt}(D)
,
is a weak composition
whose
i^{th}
entry is the number of Kohnert cells and ghost cells
in its
i^{th}
row.
Let the
excess
of

,
denoted by
\mathsf{ex}(D)
, be
|D_{2}|
.

\mathsf{RSVT}

,
we send it to
(L,E)
.
The set

(resp.

Now we may view each

We write

Inner bounding the quantum entropy cone with subadditivity and subsystem coarse grainings
Temple He, Veronika E. Hubeny, Massimiliano Rota

Abstract
We show via explicit construction that all the extreme rays of both the three-party quantum entropy cone and the four-party stabilizer entropy cone can be obtained from subsystem coarse grainings of specific higher-party quantum states, namely extreme states characterized by saturating a (non-trivial) maximal set of instances of subadditivity. This suggests that the study of the “subadditivity cone”, and the set of its extreme rays realizable in quantum mechanics, provides a powerful tool for deriving inner bounds for the quantum and stabilizer entropy cones, as well as constraints on new inequalities for the von Neumann entropy.

I
Introduction

The derivation of the fundamental inequalities satisfied by the von Neumann entropy is an important problem in quantum information theory. Unfortunately, it is also notoriously difficult. Since the proof of strong subadditivity (SSA) by Lieb and Ruskai half a century ago
Lieb and Ruskai 1973a
;
Lieb and Ruskai 1973b
, no new
unconstrained
inequality has been found. New inequalities involving four or more parties were discovered in
Linden and Winter 2005
;
Cadney et al. 2012
;
Christandl et al. 2023
, but they are
constrained
, meaning that they only apply to particular density matrices which saturate other entropic constraints. In the classical case, i.e., for the Shannon entropy, new inequalities have been derived in
Zhang and Yeung 1998
;
Makarychev et al. 2002
;
Zhang 2003
;
Matús 2007
, and
Cadney et al. 2012
speculated that they might hold also in quantum mechanics. However, to our knowledge, at present there is no strong argument that new unconstrained inequalities for the von Neumann entropy should exist.

The main goal of this paper is to show that a seemingly much simpler problem, the
quantum marginal independence problem
(QMIP)
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2019
, originally motivated by an approach
Hubeny et al. 2018
;
Hubeny et al. 2019
;
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2022
to the study of entropy inequalities in quantum gravity
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
;
Hayden et al. 2013
;
Bao et al. 2015
, might provide a powerful tool for the derivation of constraints on the existence of new inequalities and their structure. Specifically, we will show that already from a partial solution to the “extremal” version of the QMIP up to nine parties, we can derive definite
inner bounds
for the quantum entropy cone, which in the case of three parties coincide with the full cone
Pippenger 2003
, and in the case of four parties coincide with the entropy cone of stabilizer states
Linden et al. 2013
. We stress that even though we are not in this paper deriving new bounds on quantum entropy cones for four or more parties, our method introduces a conceptual idea that may prove fruitful to explore further.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In §
II
and §
III
, we review the main definitions concerning entropy cones, the QMIP, and its extremal version. In §
IV
, we formulate the inner bound to the quantum entropy cone for an arbitrary number of parties. In §
V
, we review the definition of hypergraph models from
Bao et al. 2020a
and the main result from
Walter and Witteveen 2021
about their realizability by stabilizer states. We then use this technology in §
VI
to derive the main result of this paper, and conclude in §
VII
with a list of open questions.

II
The quantum entropy cone

A convenient framework to study entropy inequalities (and their relations) was introduced in
Pippenger 2003
, following the analogous work of
Yeung 1997
for the Shannon entropy. Let
[{\sf N}]=\{1,2,\ldots,{\sf N}\}
, and consider an
{\sf N}
-party density matrix
\rho_{{\sf N}}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{{\sf N}}
. The
entropy vector
of
\rho_{{\sf N}}
is the vector in
\mathbb{R}^{\sf{D}}
, with
\sf{D}=2^{{\sf N}}-1
, given by

\vec{{\sf S}}=\{{\sf S}_{\mathscr{I}},\;\forall\,\mathscr{I}\subseteq[{\sf N}]\},\qquad{\sf S}_{\mathscr{I}}:={\sf S}(\rho_{\mathscr{I}}),

(1)

where
\rho_{\mathscr{I}}
is the reduced density matrix for the parties in
\mathscr{I}
. Denoting by
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}^{*}
the set of entropy vectors for all
{\sf N}
-party quantum states, its topological closure
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
is a convex cone
Pippenger 2003
known as the
quantum entropy cone
(QEC).

For any
{\sf N}
, an
outer bound
of
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
is given by the
polyhedral
cone obtained via the intersection of the half-spaces specified by all instances of subadditivity (SA) and strong subadditivity (SSA) of the von Neumann entropy, and we will denote this cone by
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}

Pippenger 2003
. For
{\sf N}\leq 3
it is easy to show that
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}=\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
by constructing quantum states that realize entropy vectors belonging to the extreme rays of
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}

Pippenger 2003
. On the other hand, for
{\sf N}\geq 4
, the constrained inequalities found in
Linden and Winter 2005
;
Cadney et al. 2012
imply that this construction is no longer possible, since there are regions on the boundary of
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
which are inaccessible to quantum states. This however is not enough to conclude that
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}\subset\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
(strictly), since there might be quantum states that approximate the entropy vectors in these regions arbitrarily well.

III
Quantum marginal independence

An interesting and well-known problem in quantum mechanics is the
quantum marginal problem

Schilling 2014
. Given density matrices
\rho_{\mathscr{I}}
for some subsystems, it asks whether there exists a global density matrix
\rho
such that all the given
\rho_{\mathscr{I}}
can be obtained from
\rho
as marginals. In this context, entropy inequalities provide necessary conditions for the existence of a solution
Carlen et al. 2013
.

The quantum marginal
independence
problem (QMIP)
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2019
can be interpreted as a simplified version of the quantum marginal problem, where instead of fixing the marginals for some subsystems, one only demands that certain subsystems are correlated and others are not. Specifically, the QMIP asks the following question: Given an
{\sf N}
-party system and a complete specification of the presence of correlation (or conversely, the lack thereof) among the various subsystems, is there a density matrix that satisfies these constraints? As for the case of the marginal problem, entropy inequalities can be used to constrain the space of solutions for the QMIP. However, unlike its parent version, the knowledge of the quantum entropy cone is sufficient to solve the problem completely. To see this, let us formulate the QMIP more precisely.

The
{\sf N}
-party
subadditivity cone
(SAC) is defined as the polyhedral cone in entropy space carved out by all instances of SA for that
{\sf N}
. Consider a face
\mathscr{F}
of the SAC and a vector
\vec{\sf S}\in\text{int}(\mathscr{F})
. Notice that the collection of SA instances which are saturated by
\vec{\sf S}
is independent from the specific choice of this vector. The saturation of SA is equivalent to the vanishing of the mutual information, which occurs if and only two subsystems are independent. We can then interpret
\text{int}(\mathscr{F})
to correspond to a specification of which subsystems are correlated and which are independent, while remaining agnostic about the specific values of the entropies. In its original formulation
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2019
, the QMIP asked for which faces does
\text{int}(\mathscr{F})
contain at least one entropy vector that can be realized by a quantum state. We now slightly generalize this question by replacing “realized” with “approximated arbitrarily well.” In this generalized version, the QMIP can then be trivially solved if
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
is known.

In this paper we proceed in the opposite direction and attempt to extract information about
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
from the solution to the QMIP for different values of
{\sf N}^{\prime}\geq{\sf N}
. In particular, we will be interested in the extremal version of the QMIP, which we denote as EQMIP, where we only focus on the one-dimensional faces of the SAC, i.e., its extreme rays. Denoting by
\widehat{\mathscr{R}}_{{\sf N}}
the set of all extreme rays of the
{\sf N}
-party SAC, the solution
\mathscr{R}_{{\sf N}}
to the EQMIP is then

\mathscr{R}_{{\sf N}}:=\widehat{\mathscr{R}}_{{\sf N}}\cap\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}.

(2)

We will now explain how to construct inner bounds for
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
from the knowledge of
\mathscr{R}_{{\sf N}^{\prime}}
for some
{\sf N}^{\prime}\geq{\sf N}
.

IV
Inner bounds from extremal marginal independence

IV.1
Subsystem coarse grainings

Given a density matrix
\rho_{{\sf N}^{\prime}}
, we want to consider a coarse graining of the
{\sf N}^{\prime}
parties into
{\sf N}
composite ones and to relate the entropy vectors before and after the coarse graining. We will also consider purifications of

\llbracket{\sf N}\rrbracket=\{0,1,\ldots,{\sf N}\}

\displaystyle\begin{split}f:\;&\llbracket{\sf N}^{\prime}\rrbracket\rightarrow\;\llbracket{\sf N}\rrbracket\\
&\ell^{\prime}\;\mapsto\;\ell=f(\ell^{\prime}),\end{split}

(3)

\llbracket{\sf N}\rrbracket
parties it belongs to.

Given a density matrix

with entropy vector
\vec{{\sf S}}^{\prime}

same density matrix
after the coarse graining are then given by

{\sf S}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{I}}={\sf S}_{f^{-1}(\mathscr{I})},

where
f^{-1}(\mathscr{I})

IV.2
Inner bounds for
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}

Using this construction, we can now derive an inner bound for
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
as follows. Suppose that
\mathscr{R}_{{\sf N}^{\prime}}
is known for some
{\sf N}^{\prime}\geq{\sf N}

{\sf N}

Hypergraph models

The
hypergraph models
of entanglement were introduced in

Given an

VI
Constructions for small
{\sf N}

Having reviewed the necessary tools from hypergraph models, we now use this technology to construct inner bounds for
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
for
{\sf N}\leq 4
. The logic will be as follows: we will construct hypergraphs that realize certain extreme rays of the SAC which, by
1
, are automatically solutions to the EQMIP. Even without a full solution to the EQMIP, we will then show via particular coarse grainings that we can obtain entropy vectors for a smaller number of parties, which give powerful inner bounds.

VI.1
Bounds for
{\sf N}=2

This case is trivial, but we include it for completeness. The outer bound
\Sigma_{2}
to
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{2}
is the SAC, and its extreme rays are realized by Bell pairs. We then have

\Delta_{2}^{2}=\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{2}=\Sigma_{2}.

(8)

VI.2
Bounds for
{\sf N}=3

As we mentioned in §
II
, it was already shown in
Pippenger 2003
that
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3}=\Sigma_{3}
. This equivalence follows from the fact that the extreme rays of
\Sigma_{3}
contain entropy vectors realized by Bell pairs, the four-party GHZ state, and the four-party absolutely maximally entangled state (also known as the four-party perfect tensor)
Helwig and Cui 2013
. With the only exception of the four-party GHZ, all these states also generate extreme rays of the three-party SAC and are hence elements of
\mathscr{R}_{3}
. Their conical hull is
\Delta_{3}^{3}
, which coincides with the three-party holographic entropy cone
Bao et al. 2015
.

To show that the sequence in Eq. (
6

\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3}
, we need to consider

for

. The sets
\mathscr{R}_{4}
and
\mathscr{R}_{5}
were already found in

. Hence, we need

results in a hypergraph model of the GHZ state. In summary, we have

\Delta_{3}^{3}=\Delta_{3}^{4}=\Delta_{3}^{5}\subset\Delta_{3}^{6}=\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3},

which by Eq. (

\Delta_{3}^{6+i}=\Delta_{3}^{6}
for all

VI.3
Bounds for
{\sf N}=4

As mentioned in §
II
, the QEC for
{\sf N}=4
is not known. However, we will show that our construction, even with a relatively small value of
{\sf N}^{\prime}
, suffices to derive an inner bound that coincides with the subset of
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{4}
of entropy vectors realized by stabilizer states. This subset, which we denote by
\overline{\Lambda}_{4}^{*}
, was shown in

to coincide with its outer bound
\Lambda_{4}
, defined as the polyhedral cone specified by SA, SSA, and Ingleton’s inequality

. Our goal is to show that there is some

\Delta_{4}^{4+i}=\overline{\Lambda}_{4}^{*}.

(11)

To see this, we can use a result from

. It is then enough to show that each of these models (seven in total) can be obtained

{\sf N}^{\prime}
parties (for some

), whose corresponding entropy vector is an extreme ray of the
{\sf N}^{\prime}
-party SAC. Four out of these seven cases are trivial, since they correspond to elements of
\mathscr{R}_{4}
. For the remaining three non-trivial cases (which require

\Delta_{4}^{4}=\Delta_{4}^{5}\subset\Delta_{4}^{6}\subset\overline{\Lambda}_{4}^{*}\subseteq\Delta_{4}^{9}\subseteq\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{4}\subseteq\Sigma_{4},

VII
Discussion

We conclude by commenting on a few open questions regarding the strength of our bound, which we believe to be particularly interesting.

For the bounds that we have explicitly constructed in this paper, we relied on the hypergraph models of
Bao et al. 2020a
and the result of
Walter and Witteveen 2021
. However, it was shown in
Bao et al. 2020b
that for
{\sf N}\geq 5
the inclusion of the entropy cone of hypergraphs in the cone of stabilizer states is strict. This implies that hypergraph models might not be sufficient to derive the most stringent possible bounds. On the other hand, we cannot rule out the possibility that hypergraph models do suffice to solve the EQMIP for arbitrary
{\sf N}
, in which case our bounds would not converge to the full QEC for any
{\sf N}\geq 4
. To resolve this, one direction is to look for elements in
\mathscr{R}_{{\sf N}}
that can be realized by stabilizer states but violate the “hypergraph” inequality found in
Bao et al. 2020b
at
{\sf N}=5
.

A similar limitation might also affect stabilizer states, since it is possible that for some value of
{\sf N}
, the solution to the EQMIP requires non-stabilizer quantum states. This question is related to the most stringent bound that can be realized at
{\sf N}=4
. Indeed, any
\Delta_{4}^{4+i}
such that
\overline{\Lambda}_{4}^{*}\subset\Delta_{4}^{4+i}
requires an element of
\mathscr{R}_{4+i}
that cannot be realized by a stabilizer state, since it has to violate Ingleton’s inequality. Such violations have already been investigated for classical probability distributions
Mao and Hassibi 2009
, and another question for the future is to determine if for some
{\sf N}
there exist extreme rays of the SAC that violate Ingleton’s inequality while being realizable by quantum states (at least approximately).

More generally, deep structural properties of the QEC might be extractable by exploring the behavior of the sequence of approximations given in Eq. (
6
).
As we already mentioned at the end of §
IV
, one possibility (for any given
{\sf N}
) is that there exists some
\widehat{i}
such that the sequence does not change beyond this point (see Eq. (
7
)). This is the case for
{\sf N}=3
, where we have shown that the approximation is exact, and the sequence already converges to the full
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3}
at
i=3
(see Eq. (
10
)). However, in principle this behavior is possible even if the approximation is not exact. For example, this would be the case for
{\sf N}=4
if stabilizer states were sufficient to solve the EQMIP. Another possibility is that (at least for some
{\sf N}
) the sequence continues to grow indefinitely and no such
\widehat{i}
exists. In that case, it would be important to understand if it converges, and how its limit is related to
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
.

These possibilities are also related to the question about the polyhedrality of the QEC. For
{\sf N}=3
, the exact approximation of
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3}
at finite
\widehat{i}
is possible only because
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{3}
is a polyhedral cone, since any
\Delta_{{\sf N}}^{{\sf N}^{\prime}}
is polyhedral by construction. For
{\sf N}>3
, it is currently not known whether
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
is polyhedral. If it is not, as is the case for the subset of
\overline{\Sigma}^{*}_{{\sf N}}
corresponding to classical probability distributions
Matús 2007
, the sequence in Eq. (
6
) might still converge, but only at infinite
i
.

Finally, let us comment on the role of the outer bound
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
, and in particular SSA. As we mentioned in the introduction, to our knowledge there is currently no strong evidence against the possibility that the QEC also coincides with
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
for
{\sf N}>3
. To explore this possibility, a natural way to proceed is to examine the gap between our inner bound and
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
. In general, an extreme ray of
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}
is the intersection of facets corresponding to multiple instances of both SA and SSA, and it may not be an extreme ray of the
{\sf N}
-party SAC. Thus, one may wonder if such an extreme ray can be obtained from coarse graining an extreme ray of the SAC involving more parties and realizable (at least approximately) in quantum mechanics. It is intriguing that an example of this is already present at
{\sf N}=3
. In this case, one of the extreme rays of
\Sigma_{3}
, namely the one corresponding to the four-party GHZ state described in §
VI.2
, does not saturate any instance of SA, and nevertheless, it can be obtained from our construction via the coarse graining in Eq. (
9
) of the element of
\mathscr{R}_{6}
shown in
Figure

1
. This suggests that further explorations of the gap between our inner bound and the outer bound
\Sigma_{{\sf N}}

Bi-Lipschitz embeddings of the space of unordered m m -tuples with a partial transportation metric
David Bate, Ana Lucía García Pulido

Abstract
Let Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} be non-empty, open and proper. This paper is concerned with W ​ b p ​ ( Ω ) Wb_{p}(\Omega) , the space of p p -integrable Borel measures on Ω \Omega equipped with the partial transportation metric introduced by Figalli and Gigli that allows the creation and destruction of mass on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . Alternatively, we show that W ​ b p ​ ( Ω ) Wb_{p}(\Omega) is isometric to a subset of Borel measures with the ordinary Wasserstein distance, on the one point completion of Ω \Omega equipped with the shortcut metric δ ⁡ ( x , y ) = min ⁡ { ‖ x − y ‖ , dist ⁡ ( x , ∂ Ω ) + dist ⁡ ( y , ∂ Ω ) } . \delta(x,y)=\min\{\|x-y\|,\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\}. In this article we construct bi-Lipschitz embeddings of the set of unordered m m -tuples in W ​ b p ​ ( Ω ) Wb_{p}(\Omega) into Hilbert space. This generalises Almgren’s bi-Lipschitz embedding theorem to the setting of optimal partial transport.

Introduction

A striking variety of problems in geometry, analysis, combinatorics and a vast number of applications can be neatly formulated in terms of measures and their comparison using transportation metrics.
The prototypical transportation metric is the
p
-Wasserstein distance

Ambrosio et al. [2021] ©2021
.
This is defined between two Borel measures of the same total mass on a metric space
(X,d)
by

W_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma}\left(\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(1)

where
p\geq 1
and the infimum is taken over all measures
\gamma
on
X\times X
with coordinate projections
\pi_{1}\gamma=\mu
and
\pi_{2}\gamma=\nu
.
The resulting metric space of
p
-integrable probability measures equipped with
W_{p}
is denoted by
W_{p}(X)
(see
Definition

2.1
).

A drawback of the Wasserstein distance is the requirement that the compared measures must have the same total mass.
Recently emerging theories of
optimal partial transport
pertain to the transportation of measures without a mass constraint
Liero et al. 2018
;
Chizat et al. 2018
;
Piccoli and Rossi 2014
.
This article concerns the following formulation due to Figalli and Gigli
Figalli and Gigli 2010
.

Let
\Omega
be an open non-empty proper subset of
X
.
For
measures

\mu
and
\nu
on
\Omega
, one defines
Wb_{p}(\mu,\nu)
as in (
1
), but the infimum is taken over measures
\gamma
on
\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}
with

\pi_{1}\gamma|_{\Omega}=\mu\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{2}\gamma|_{\Omega}=\nu.

The resulting metric space of
p
-integrable measures, equipped with
Wb_{p}
, will be denoted by
Wb_{p}(X)
(see
Definition

3.1
).

The key property of
Wb_{p}
is that
\partial\Omega
can be used to destroy or create mass, at a cost of transporting it to or from
\partial\Omega
.
This allows measures of different total masses to be compared and hence one can construct a metric space consisting of
all
measures, instead of restricting to probability measures.
Understanding the interplay between transportation metrics and
\partial\Omega
is motivated by solving evolution equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions from gradient flows
Figalli and Gigli 2010
;
Profeta and Sturm 2020
.
The metric
Wb_{p}
has found further applications such as obtaining new comparison principles for viscosity solutions
Guillen et al. 2019
.

A natural approach to study a metric space is to embed it into a well known space, such as a Euclidean or Banach space, as this allows the metric space to inherit geometric properties of the ambient space.
Recall that the
distortion
of an injective map
f
between two metric spaces is
\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\operatorname{Lip}(f^{-1})
, where
\operatorname{Lip}(f)
is the Lipschitz constant of
f
;

is

if it has finite distortion.
Since bi-Lipschitz embeddings preserve relative distances, they are central to analysis and metric geometry
Naor 2018
and have applications to algorithm design
Indyk 2001
.

Due to the prominence of the Wasserstein spaces in various areas of mathematics, their embeddability has attracted much attention.
The non-embeddability (into
L^{1}
) of
W_{1}
over various discrete metric spaces
Baudier et al. 2023
such as the planar grid
Naor and Schechtman 2007
and Hamming cube

is known, as is the non-embeddability of

for

Andoni et al. 2018
.
The interest in bi-Lipschitz embeddings of the Wasserstein spaces dates back to the work of Almgren

De Lellis and Spadaro 2011

forming the foundations of his celebrated partial regularity theorem for area minimising currents.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete and separable metric space.
We write
\mathcal{M}(X)
for the set of Borel measures on
X
and
\mathcal{P}(X)
for the set of Borel probability measures on
X
.
The Wasserstein space is defined as follows

;
Ambrosio et al. 2008
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
define

W_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma}\left(\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the infimum is taken over all
couplings

\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(X\times X)
with coordinate projections
\pi_{1}\gamma=\mu
and
\pi_{2}\gamma=\nu
.
Note that
W_{p}(\mu,\nu)<\infty
only if
\mu(X)=\nu(X)
as otherwise there does not exist a
\gamma
as in
Definition

2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
be those
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
with

\int_{X}d(x,x_{0})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty

for some (equivalently all)

.
Then

defines a metric on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
.
Analogous statements hold for the case
p=\infty
, where the
L^{p}
integral is replaced by an essential supremum.
We write
W_{p}(X)
for the set
\mathcal{P}_{p}(X)
equipped with
W_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define the space of

m
-tupes

\mathcal{A}_{m}(X)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\![x_{i}]\!]:x_{i}\in X\ \forall 1\leq i\leq m\right\},

.
Note that, on

A fundamental step in Almgren’s study of area minimising currents was the following bi-Lipschitz embedding.

(Almgren, Theorem 2.1

)
.

For every

Optimal Partial Transport and the Shortcut Metric

The transportation metric
Wb
introduced by Figalli and Gigli
Figalli and Gigli 2010
is defined between two Borel measures.
Originally defined for open and bounded
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we state the natural generalisation of
Wb
to complete and separable metric spaces
(X,d)
(the proof of the triangle inequality is identical).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be proper and non-empty.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
define

Wb_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma}\left(\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the infimum is taken over all
couplings

\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(X\times X)
with
\pi_{1}\gamma|_{\Omega}=\mu
and
\pi_{2}\gamma|_{\Omega}=\nu
.
Then
Wb_{p}
defines a metric on

\mathcal{M}b_{p}(\Omega):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega):Wb_{p}(\mu,0)<\infty\}.

Analogous statements hold for the case
p=\infty
, where the
L^{p}
integral is replaced by an essential supremum.

We write
Wb_{p}(\Omega)
for the set
\mathcal{M}b_{p}(\Omega)
equipped with
Wb_{p}
.
We also write
Wb^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
for the set of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}b_{p}(\Omega)
with
\mu(\Omega)\leq 1
, equipped with
Wb_{p}
.

The first step in our proof of
Theorem

1.1
is to show an equivalence between
Wb^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
and
W_{p}(\Omega^{*})
, for
\Omega^{*}
the
shortcut
metric space, defined as the one point completion of
\Omega
via its complement.

Definition 3.2
.

For
\Omega\subset X
non-empty and proper, let
\Omega^{*}=\Omega\cup\{\partial\}
.
For
x,y\in\Omega
define

\delta(x,y)=\min\{\|x-y\|,\operatorname{dist}(x,X\setminus\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(y,X\setminus\Omega)\}

and
\delta(x,\partial)=\operatorname{dist}(x,X\setminus\Omega)
.
Then
\delta
defines a metric on
\Omega^{*}
.

Profeta and Sturm
(
Profeta and Sturm 2020
, Remark 1.9)
mention that
Wb_{1}^{1}(\Omega)
isometrically embeds into
W_{1}(\Omega^{*})
, and give an example showing that their embedding is not an isometry for
p>1
.
We show that there exists an isometric embedding of
Wb_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
into
2W_{p}(\Omega^{*})
for any
p\geq 1
.
Here we write
2W_{p}(\Omega^{*})
for the space of measures with total mass equal to 2.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a separable metric space and
\Omega\subset X
be non-empty and proper.
For any
p\geq 1
, the map

\displaystyle Wb_{p}^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to 2W_{p}(\Omega^{*})

\displaystyle\iota(\mu)

\displaystyle=\mu+(2-\mu(\Omega))[\![\partial]\!],

is an isometric embedding.

Proof 3.4
.

Given a coupling for
\mu,\nu
we use it to construct a coupling for
\iota(\mu),\iota(\nu)
and vice versa.

First let
\mu,\nu\in Wb_{p}^{1}(\Omega)
and suppose that
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(X\times X)
is a coupling for
\mu
and
\nu
in
Wb_{p}(\Omega)
.
Let
\pi_{\partial}(x)=\partial
for all
x\in X
and define
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega^{*}\times\Omega^{*})
as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\gamma|_{\Omega\times\Omega}+(\pi_{\partial}\times\operatorname{id})_{\#}\gamma|_{X\setminus\Omega\times\Omega}+(\operatorname{id}\times\pi_{\partial})_{\#}\gamma|_{\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+(2-[\gamma(\Omega\times\Omega)+\gamma(X\setminus\Omega\times\Omega)+\gamma(\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega)])[\![(\partial,\partial)]\!].

For notational convenience, we let
\kappa
denote the coefficient of
[\![(\partial,\partial)]\!]
in this expression.
Then

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(\gamma|_{\Omega\times\Omega})+\gamma(X\setminus\Omega\times\Omega)[\![\partial]\!]+\pi_{1}(\gamma|_{\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega})+\kappa[\![\partial]\!]

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(\gamma|_{\Omega\times\Omega})+\pi_{1}(\gamma|_{\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega})+(2-[\gamma(\Omega\times\Omega)+\gamma(\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega)])[\![\partial]\!]

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(\gamma|_{\Omega\times X})+(2-\gamma(\Omega\times X))[\![\partial]\!]

\displaystyle=\mu+(2-\mu(X))[\![\partial]\!]=\iota(\mu).

Similarly, by symmetry,
\pi_{2}\gamma^{\prime}=\iota(\nu)
.
Thus
\gamma^{\prime}
is a coupling of
\iota(\mu)
and
\iota(\nu)
in
W_{p}(\Omega^{*})

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int\delta(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\delta(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)+\int_{X\setminus\Omega\times\Omega}\delta(\partial,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega}\delta(x,\partial)^{p}d\gamma(x,y)+\kappa\delta(\partial,\partial)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)+\int_{X\setminus\Omega\times\Omega}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega\times X\setminus\Omega}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y).

(3)

Therefore,

Conversely, let
\gamma

\iota(\mu)
and
\iota(\nu)

Define the closed set

\displaystyle\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\prime}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}d(x,y)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)+\int_{(\Omega\times\Omega^{*})\setminus E}d(x,c(x))^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in\Omega
with
\delta(y_{i},y_{j})=\epsilon

for each
i\neq j
.
In particular,
\Omega^{*}
is not doubling.

Proof 3.11
.

Let
x\not\in\overline{\Omega}
and
y\in\Omega
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in\Omega
lie on the circle centred on
x
of radius
\|x-y\|
(such points exist since
\Omega
is open).
For each
1\leq i\leq N
, let
l^{\prime}_{i}
be the line segment connecting
y_{i}
to
x
and let
l_{i}
be the connected component of
l^{\prime}_{i}\cap\Omega
containing
y_{i}
.
Since
x\not\in\overline{\Omega}
, there exists
\eta>0
such that

\inf\{\|z-z^{\prime}\|:z\in l_{i},\ z^{\prime}\in l_{j},\ i\neq j\}>\eta.

Now,
\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)
is continuous on each
l_{i}
and converges to 0 as one travels along
l_{i}
towards
\partial\Omega
.
Therefore, for each sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
and each
1\leq i\leq N
, there exists
z_{i}\in l_{i}
with
\operatorname{dist}(z_{i},\partial\Omega)=\epsilon/2
.
In particular, if
\epsilon<\eta
, then
\delta(z_{i},z_{j})=\epsilon
for each
1\leq i\neq j\leq N
.

Finally, we see that
y_{i}\in B(y_{1},\epsilon)
for each
1\leq j\leq N
, but we require at least
N
balls of radius
\epsilon/4
to cover
B(y_{1},\epsilon)
.
Since
N\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary,
\Omega^{*}
cannot be doubling.

Remark 3.12
.

Lemma

3.10
is sharp in the following sense.
If
\Omega=(-1,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
, then
\Omega^{*}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a Euclidean circle.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\Omega^{*}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In both of these cases, the conclusion of
Lemma

3.10
fails.

Note that each Euclidean space is doubling and that the doubling property is preserved under taking subsets and bi-Lipschitz images.
Therefore, if a metric space is bi-Lipschitz embeddable into some Euclidean space, it must necessarily be doubling.

Corollary 3.13
.

For
n\geq 2
let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be non-empty and open such that
\overline{\Omega}
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then
\Omega^{*}
is not bi-Lipschitz embeddable into any Euclidean space.

.

4
A bi-Lipschitz description of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
in terms of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

To construct the bi-Lipschitz embedding from
Theorem

1.1
, it would be natural to adapt the techniques from the proof of
Theorem

2.3
to our setting.
However, the proof of
Theorem

2.3
strictly depends on both, the linear structure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(in particular the existence of projections), and the compactness of the unit ball.
Although
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a set,
\delta
bears no relationship to the linear structure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and this fact prohibits the direct use of Almgren’s techniques.
On the other hand, whilst it is possible to find a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
\Omega^{*}
into
\ell_{2}
to gain a linear structure, this comes at the expense of compactness of the unit ball.
Thus it is not possible to modify Almgren’s proof to our setting.

In order to prove
Theorem

1.1
we will use a Whitney decomposition
\mathcal{C}
of
\Omega
into cubes

\Omega=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}Q

(see
Proposition

4.2
) such that, within each
Q
,
\delta
is given by
\|\cdot\|
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q,\delta)=\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q,\|\cdot\|)
.

Theorem

2.3
then gives a bi-Lipschitz embedding of each
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q,\delta)
into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and it would be favourable to use these embeddings as “coordinate projections” to construct a global embedding into Hilbert space.
Of course, the union of the
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q)
does not cover
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
and therefore we cannot simply define coordinate projections by taking restrictions to each
Q
.
Nevertheless, the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q,\delta)=\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q,\|\cdot\|)
enables us to construct a map
\phi^{*}_{Q}\colon\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})\to\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
which, roughly speaking, acts as a smooth projection to
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q)
.

The main result of this section shows that the
\phi^{*}_{Q}
can be combined to define a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
into the following metric space.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a countable set and define

\mathcal{T}:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

to be the
\ell_{2}
-sum of copies of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.
That is,
\mathcal{T}
consists of sequences

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}a_{Q}

of elements of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for which

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}^{2}(a_{Q},0)<\infty,

where
0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\![0]\!]
,
equipped with the metric

\sqrt{\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}^{2}(a_{Q},a^{\prime}_{Q})}.

Once we have an embedding into
\mathcal{T}
, we will show that it is possible to find an embedding into
\ell_{2}
.
Indeed, in
Section

5
, we apply
Theorem

2.3
to each term in the definition of
\mathcal{T}
to obtain a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
\mathcal{T}
into
\ell_{2}
.

4.1
A Whitney decomposition of
\Omega

To construct the embedding into
\mathcal{T}
, we will use a Whitney decomposition of
\Omega
.
For a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
l(Q)
denote the side length of
Q
.

Proposition 4.2

(Appendix J
Grafakos 2014
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be non-empty, open and proper.
There exists a family of closed cubes
\mathcal{C}
such that

1.

\cup\mathcal{C}=\Omega
and the elements of
\mathcal{C}
have disjoint interiors.

2.

\sqrt{n}l(Q)\leq\operatorname{dist}(Q,\partial\Omega)\leq 4\sqrt{n}l(Q)
for all
Q\in\mathcal{C}
.

3.

If
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and
Q\cap Q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
then

\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{l(Q)}{l(Q^{\prime})}\leq 4.

We say that
Q,Q^{\prime}
are
neighbours
.

4.

Each
Q\in\mathcal{C}
has at most
12^{n}
neighbours.

A Whitney decomposition of
\Omega
estimates which quantity attains the minimum in the definition of
\delta
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Whitney decomposition of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and
x\in Q
and
y\in Q^{\prime}
.
Then

\sqrt{n}l(Q)\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq 5\sqrt{n}l(Q).

(6)

If
Q,Q^{\prime}
are neighbours then

\delta(x,y)=\|x-y\|.

(7)

If
Q,Q^{\prime}
are not neighbours then

\frac{l(Q)+l(Q^{\prime})}{8}\leq\delta(x,y)\leq 5\sqrt{n}(l(Q)+l(Q^{\prime})).

(8)

Proof 4.4
.

The first inequality in (
6
) is implied by
\sqrt{n}l(Q)\leq\operatorname{dist}(Q,\partial\Omega)
.
The second follows from the triangle inequality:

\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\operatorname{dist}(Q,\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{diam}(Q)\leq 4\sqrt{n}l(Q)+\sqrt{n}l(Q).

Now suppose
Q,Q^{\prime}
are neighbours and let
z\in Q\cap Q^{\prime}
.
Then by (
6
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{n}(l(Q)+l(Q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\geq\|x-z\|+\|z-y\|\geq\|x-y\|,

giving (
7
).
On the other hand, suppose that
Q,Q^{\prime}
are not neighbours and
l(Q)\geq l(Q^{\prime})
.
Then
\|x-y\|\geq l(Q^{\prime\prime})
for
Q^{\prime\prime}
a neighbour of
Q
.
In particular

\|x-y\|\geq l(Q^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{l(Q)}{4}\geq\frac{l(Q)+l(Q^{\prime})}{8},

giving the first inequality in (
8
).
The second inequality follows from (
6
).

For the remainder of the paper we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
non-empty, open and proper and
\mathcal{C}
a Whitney decomposition of
\Omega
as in
Proposition

4.2
.
We also fix
\mathcal{T}
as in
Definition

4.1
.

4.2
Constructing a coordinate system

To construct a bi-Lipschitz embedding of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
into
\mathcal{T}
,
we define projections

\phi^{*}_{Q}\colon\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})\to\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

that serve as a coordinate system for
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
.
The embedding into
\mathcal{T}
will then be defined as the
\ell_{2}
-sum of the
\phi^{*}_{Q}
(see
Definition

4.8
).

We begin with the construction of a function
\phi_{Q}
that approximates the identity within a given

, is supported on the neighbours of
Q
, and maintains bi-Lipschitz bounds with
\delta
.
For
Q\in\mathcal{C}
and

, we write

for the
closed

r
-neighbourhood of

.

Lemma 4.5
.

For each
Q\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a map

\phi_{Q}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

such that

1.

\phi_{Q}
is
9\sqrt{n+1}
-Lipschitz;

2.

\phi_{Q}(x)=0
for all
x\not\in B(Q,l(Q)/4)
.
In particular,
\phi_{Q}
is supported on the neighbours of
Q
;

3.

\|\phi_{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n+1}l(Q)
;

4.

For all
x,y\in B(Q,l(Q)/8)
,

\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|=\|x-y\|;

5.

The extension of
\phi_{Q}
to
\Omega^{*}
, defined by
\phi_{Q}(\partial)=0
, is
9\sqrt{n+1}
-Lipschitz with respect to
\delta
;

6.

If
x\in B(Q,l(Q)/8)
and
y\in\Omega^{*}
, then

\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|\geq\min\left\{\frac{\|x-y\|}{2\sqrt{n}},l(Q)\right\}.

Proof 4.6
.

Fix
Q\in\mathcal{C}
and let
c
be the centre of
Q
.
For each
x\in\Omega
, let

\eta(x)=\max\left\{1-\operatorname{dist}\left(x,B\left(Q,\frac{l(Q)}{8}\right)\right)\frac{8}{l(Q)},0\right\}.

That is,
\eta
is an
8/l(Q)
-Lipschitz function with
\|\eta\|_{\infty}=1
that equals 1 on
B(Q,l(Q)/8)
and 0 on
\Omega\setminus B(Q,l(Q)/4)
.
We also set

\varphi(x)=(x-c,l(Q))\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

a 1-Lipschitz function satisfying
\|\varphi(x)\|\leq\sqrt{n+1}l(Q)
for all
x
in the support of
\eta
.

Define
\phi_{Q}=\eta\varphi
.
Since
\phi_{Q}
is a product of Lipschitz functions,
the Lipschitz constant of
\phi_{Q}
is bounded above by

\operatorname{Lip}\varphi\|\eta\|_{\infty}+\sup\{\|\varphi(x)\|:x\in\operatorname{spt}\eta\}\operatorname{Lip}\eta\leq 1+\sqrt{n+1}l(Q)\frac{8}{l(Q)}\leq 9\sqrt{n+1}.

This demonstrates
item

1
.

Items

2
,
3
and
4
are immediate.

To see
item

5
, first let
x\in\Omega^{*}
be such that
\phi_{Q}(x)\neq 0
.
Then by
item

2
,
x\in Q^{\prime}
for
Q^{\prime}
a neighbour of
Q
, so that
l(Q^{\prime})\geq l(Q)/4
.
Therefore, by
item

3
,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{Q}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n+1}\ l(Q)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{n+1}\ l(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 8\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega),

using
eq.

6
for the final inequality.
Thus

\|\phi_{Q}(x)\|\leq 8\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)

holds for any
x\in\Omega^{*}
(including
x=\partial
).
Therefore, by the triangle inequality, for any
x,y\in\Omega^{*}
,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|\leq 8\left(\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\right).

Combining this inequality with
item

1
shows that
\phi_{Q}
is
9\sqrt{n+1}
-Lipschitz with respect to
\delta
on
\Omega^{*}
.

Finally, to see
item

6
, first suppose that
y\not\in B(Q,l(Q)/4)
.
Then by
item

2
,

\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|=\|\varphi(x)\|\geq l(Q),

so that
item

6
holds in this case.

In the case

we will show that

\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|\geq\frac{\|x-y\|}{2\sqrt{n}},

(9)

completing the proof of
item

6
.
To this end, note that

\|y-c\|\leq\sqrt{n}\frac{l(Q)}{2}+\frac{l(Q)}{4}\leq\sqrt{n}l(Q).

Therefore, by considering the first component of
\phi_{Q}
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|(x-c)-\eta(y)(y-c)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|x-y\|-(1-\eta(y))\|y-c\|

\displaystyle\geq\|x-y\|-\sqrt{n}(1-\eta(y))l(Q).

Thus, if

\sqrt{n}(1-\eta(y))l(Q)\leq\frac{\|x-y\|}{2},

(10)

then (
9
) holds.
On the other hand, if (
10
) does not hold, then by considering the final component of
\phi
, we have

\|\phi_{Q}(x)-\phi_{Q}(y)\|\geq(1-\eta(y))l(Q)\geq\frac{\|x-y\|}{2\sqrt{n}},

giving (
9
).

The pushforwards under each

define our coordinate projections on

.

Definition 4.7
.

For every
Q\in\mathcal{C}

\phi^{*}_{Q}

That is,

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{Q}\colon\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})

Recall the construction of
\mathcal{T}
from

.

Definition 4.8
.

Define the embedding

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\phi^{*}_{Q}

4.3
\phi^{*}
is bi-Lipschitz

In this section we show that
\phi^{*}
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding, beginning by showing that it is Lipschitz.

For
p\in(\Omega^{*})^{m}
and

, let

p^{-1}(S)=\{1\leq k\leq m:p_{k}\in S\}.

From now on we use the notation
\sigma q
to denote the element of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
arising from the natural action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{m}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}

(\sigma q)_{i}=q_{\sigma(i)}

Lemma 4.9
.

For any

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2}\leq c_{0}W_{2}^{2}(p,q),

Proof 4.10
.

Fix
p,q\in(\Omega^{*})^{m}
and let
Q\in\mathcal{C}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}
.
Set

J_{Q}^{\sigma}=p^{-1}(B(Q,l(Q)/4))\cup(\sigma q)^{-1}(B(Q,l(Q)/4)),

so that, by
Lemma

4.5

item

2
,

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma(k)})\|^{2}=\sum_{k\in J_{Q}^{\sigma}}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma(k)})\|^{2}.

Applying
Lemma

4.5

item

5

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma(k)})\|^{2}\leq 9^{2}(n+1)\sum_{k\in J_{Q}^{\sigma}}\delta(p_{k},q_{\sigma(k)})^{2}.

Therefore

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma(k)})\|^{2}\leq 9^{2}(n+1)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}\sum_{k\in J_{Q}^{\sigma}}\delta(p_{k},q_{\sigma(k)})^{2}.

Further,

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}\sum_{k\in J_{Q}^{\sigma}}\delta(p_{k},q_{\sigma(k)})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{k\in J_{Q}^{\sigma}}\delta(p_{k},q_{\sigma(k)})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}2\cdot 12^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta(p_{k},q_{\sigma(k)})^{2},

since

is contained within the union of the neighbours of

.
The result follows for

.

R
implies

\|p_{k}-q_{\sigma_{R}(k)}\|<\frac{l(R)}{24m}\leq\frac{l(Q)}{24m}

and so (
12
) implies
q_{\sigma_{R}(k)}\in\widehat{Q}
.
The similar argument with
p
and
\sigma_{R}q
exchanged gives (
17
).

By carefully partitioning
E
using the
\widehat{Q}
, we use
Lemma

4.12
to construct the desired permutation on
p^{-1}(E)
.

Proposition 4.14
.

There exists a bijection
\tau\colon p^{-1}(E)\to q^{-1}(E)
such that

\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(E)}\|p_{k}-q_{\tau(k)}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2}.

Proof 4.15
.

Let

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\{Q\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:p^{-1}(\widehat{Q})\neq\emptyset\}.

Note that, by (
15
),
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
can equivalently be defined as the set of
Q\in\mathcal{C}
with
q^{-1}(\widehat{Q})\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
is finite, we enumerate it as

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\{Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{j}\}

in such a way that

l(Q_{1})\geq l(Q_{2})\geq\ldots\geq l(Q_{j}).

Then, for
1\leq i\leq k\leq j
, applying
Lemma

4.12
with
Q=Q_{k}
and
R=Q_{i}
gives

p^{-1}\left(\widehat{Q}_{i}\cap\widehat{Q}_{k}\right)=(\sigma_{Q_{k}}q)^{-1}\left(\widehat{Q}_{i}\cap\widehat{Q}_{k}\right)\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq k\leq j.

(19)

Let
B_{1}=\widehat{Q}_{1}
and for each
2\leq k\leq j
define

B_{k}:=\widehat{Q}_{k}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\widehat{Q}_{i}=\widehat{Q}_{k}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\widehat{Q}_{i}\cap\widehat{Q}_{k}.

Then (
19
) implies that
\sigma_{Q_{k}}
is a permutation between
p^{-1}(B_{k})
and
\sigma_{Q_{k}}q^{-1}(B_{k})
for each
1\leq k\leq j
.
Therefore, we define a bijection

\tau\colon p^{-1}(E)\to q^{-1}(E)

by setting
\tau
to equal
\sigma_{Q_{k}}
on
D_{k}:=p^{-1}(B_{k})
for each
1\leq k\leq j
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(E)}\|p_{k}-q_{\tau(k)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{k\in D_{i}}\|p_{k}-q_{\tau(k)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{k\in D_{i}}\|p_{k}-q_{\sigma_{Q_{i}}(k)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{k\in D_{i}}\|\phi_{Q_{i}}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q_{i}}(q_{\sigma_{Q_{i}}(k)})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma_{Q}(k)})\|^{2},

using (
16
) for the third equality.
Finally (
11
) completes the proof.

Next we consider the points outside
E
for which we use the distance to
\partial\Omega
to estimate
\delta
.

Lemma 4.16
.

For any bijection

\sigma\colon p^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)\to q^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)

we have

\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)}(\operatorname{dist}(p_{k},\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(q_{\sigma(k)},\partial\Omega))^{2}\leq m^{3}c_{2}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2},

for
c_{2}\geq 1
that depends only upon
n
.

Proof 4.17
.

For a moment fix
k\in p^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)
and let
Q\in\mathcal{C}
contain
p_{k}
.
Then necessarily
Q\not\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
Therefore (
13
) and (
6
) imply

W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))\geq\frac{c_{1}}{m}l(Q)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{5\sqrt{n}m}\operatorname{dist}(p_{k},\partial\Omega).

Since each
Q\in\mathcal{C}
contains at most
m
such points
p_{k}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)}\operatorname{dist}(p_{k},\partial\Omega)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{25m^{2}n}{c_{1}^{2}}m\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2}.

The same estimate for
\sigma q
gives the desired inequality for
c_{2}=4\cdot 25n/c_{1}^{2}
.

We combine our previous results to show that
\phi^{*}_{Q}
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding.

Theorem 4.18
.

For any
p,q\in\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
,

\frac{W_{2}(p,q)^{2}}{c_{3}m^{3}}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2}\leq c_{3}W_{2}(p,q)^{2},

where
c_{3}\geq 1
depends only upon
n
.

Proof 4.19
.

The right hand inequality is given by
Lemma

4.9
.

For the left hand inequality, let
\tau
be the bijection obtained from
Proposition

4.14
and arbitrarily extend it to a bijection of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(\phi^{*}_{Q}(p),\phi^{*}_{Q}(q))^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma_{Q}(k)})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{Q\not\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\phi_{Q}(p_{k})-\phi_{Q}(q_{\sigma_{Q}(k)})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(E)}\|p_{k}-q_{\tau(k)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{c_{2}m^{3}}\sum_{k\in p^{-1}(\Omega\setminus E)}(\operatorname{dist}(p_{k},\partial\Omega)+\operatorname{dist}(q_{\tau(k)},\partial\Omega))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}m^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta(p_{k},q_{\tau(k)})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}m^{3}}W_{2}(p,q)^{2},

using
Proposition

4.14
and
Lemma

4.16
for the first inequality.

5
The embedding into Hilbert space

In this section we conclude the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.
Let
\xi\colon\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the embedding given by
Theorem

2.3
.
We write

\ell_{2}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{R}^{N}

as a direct
l_{2}
-sum over
\mathcal{C}
.
Recall the construction of
\mathcal{T}
from
Definition

4.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The function
\xi^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\ell_{2}
defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{A}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\to\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\xi

is well defined.
Moreover, for any
a,b\in\mathcal{T}
,

\frac{1}{cm^{2n+2}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(a_{Q},b_{Q})^{2}\leq\|\xi^{\prime}(a)-\xi^{\prime}(b)\|^{2}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(a_{Q},b_{Q})^{2},

for
c\geq 1
depending only upon
n
.

Proof 5.2
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{T}
, so that

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(p_{Q},0)^{2}<\infty.

Since
\xi
is 1-Lipschitz this implies that

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\|\xi(p_{Q})\|^{2}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\|\xi(p_{Q})-\xi(0)\|^{2}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(p_{Q},0)^{2}<\infty.

Hence,
\xi^{\prime}
is well defined.
Moreover, using that
\xi
is 1-Lipschitz again, we have, for any
b\in\mathcal{T}
,

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\|\xi(a_{Q})-\xi(b_{Q})\|^{2}\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(a_{Q},b_{Q})^{2},

so that
\xi^{\prime}
is also 1-Lipschitz.
Finally,
Theorem

2.3
gives

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\|\xi(a_{Q})-\xi(b_{Q})\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{cm^{2n+2}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}W_{2}(a_{Q},b_{Q})^{2}.

Theorem 5.3
.

There exists a bi-Lipschitz embedding
\zeta\colon\mathcal{B}_{m}(\Omega)\to\ell_{2}
with distortion at most
cm^{n+5/2}
, for
c\geq 1
depending only upon
n
.
That is, for any
p,q\in\mathcal{B}_{m}(\Omega)
,

\frac{W_{2}(p,q)}{cm^{n+5/2}}\leq\|\zeta(p)-\zeta(q)\|\leq cW_{2}(p,q).

Proof 5.4
.

First isometrically embed
\mathcal{B}_{m}(\Omega)
into
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
via
Corollary

3.15
.
One then applies
Theorem

4.18
to bi-Lipschitz embed
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
into
\mathcal{T}
.
Finally,
Lemma

5.1
bi-Lipschitz embeds
\mathcal{T}
into
\ell_{2}
, as required.

Remark 5.5
.

For
n\geq 3
, the distortion of any embedding of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
into
\ell_{2}
converges to
\infty
as
m
increases.
In particular,
Wb_{2}(\Omega)
does not bi-Lipschitz embed into
\ell_{2}
.

Indeed, by
Equation

7
we see that
\mathcal{A}_{m}(\Omega^{*})
contains an isometric copy of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q)
for some cube
Q
.
Thus, the distortion of any embedding into
\ell_{2}
is at least that of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q)
.
For
n\geq 3
, Andoni, Naor and Nieman
(
Andoni et al. 2018
, Theorem 7)
prove that
W_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
does not coarsely, in particular bi-Lipschitz, embed into any Banach space of non-trivial type, namely Hilbert space.
Since the set of discrete measures is dense in
W_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, a scaling argument shows that the distortion of any bi-Lipschitz embedding of
\mathcal{A}_{m}(Q)
must converge to
\infty
as
m
does.

The same conclusion can be made for
n=2
using an unpublished result of Austin and Naor announced in
(
Andoni et al. 2018
, Remark 8)
, which states that
W_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
does not bi-Lipschitz embed into
L_{1}
and, hence, does not bi-Lipschitz embed into
\ell_{2}
.

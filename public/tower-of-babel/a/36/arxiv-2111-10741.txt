Dilation operator has been investigated on many function spaces, including the Besov spaces. We state the following results summarizing the behavior of dilation operator on Besov spaces.

In

For

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Local Fields

Let
K
be a field and a topological space. If the additive group
K^{+}
and multiplicative group
K^{*}
of
K
both are locally compact Abelian group, then

is called
locally compact topological field
, or
local field.

If
K
is any field and is endowed with the discrete topology, then
K
is a local field. Further, if
K
is connected, then
K
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. If
K
is not connected, then it is totally disconnected. So, here local fields means a field
K
which is locally compact, non-discrete and totally disconnected. If
K
is of characteristic zero, then K is either a
p
-adic field for some prime number
p
or a finite algebraic extension of such a field. If
K
is of positive characteristic, then
K
is either a field of formal Laurent series over a finite field of characteristic
p
or an algebraic extension of such a field.

Let
K
be a local field. Since
K^{+}
is a locally compact abelian group, we choose a Haar measure
dx
for
K^{+}
. If
\alpha\neq 0,\penalty\ \alpha\in K
, then
d(\alpha x)
is also a Haar measure and by the uniqueness of Haar measure we have
d(\alpha x)=|\alpha|dx
. We call
|\alpha|
the
absolute value
or
valuation
of
\alpha
which is, in fact, a natural non-Archimedian norm on
K
. A mapping
|\cdot|\penalty\ :K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies:

(a)

|x|=0
if and only if
x=0
;

(b)

|xy|=|x||y|
for all
x,y\in K
;

(c)

|x+y|\leq\text{max}\{|x|,|y|\},
for all
x,y\in K
,

The property
(c)
is called the
ultrametric inequality
. It follows that

|x+y|=\text{max}\{|x|,|y|\}\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ |x|\neq|y|.

The set
\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq 1\}
is called the ring of integers in
K
. It is the unique maximal compact subring of
K
. The set
\mathfrak{P}=\{x\in K:|x|<1\}
is called the prime ideal in
K
. It is the unique maximal ideal in
\mathfrak{D}
. It can be proved that
\mathfrak{D}
is compact and open and hence
\mathfrak{P}
is also compact and open. Therefore, the residue space
\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{P}
is isomorphic to a finite field

, where
q=p^{c}
for some prime number

and

.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a fixed element of maximum absolute value in,
\mathfrak{P}
and it is called a
prime element
of
K
. For a measurable subset
E
of
K
, let
|E|=\int_{K}\mathbb{1}_{E}(x)dx
, where
\mathbb{1}
is the indicator function of

and
dx
is the Haar measure of
K
normalized so that
|\mathfrak{D}|=1
. Then it is easy to prove that
|\mathfrak{P}|=q^{-1}
and
|\mathfrak{p}|=q^{-1}
(see
[
13
]
).

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{q-1}
be any fixed full set of coset representative of
\mathfrak{P}
in,
\mathfrak{D}
then each

can be written uniquely as

x=\sum\limits_{l=k}^{\infty}c_{l}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
, where
c_{l}\in\mathcal{U}
.
If

then
|x|=q^{k}
for some

Let
\mathfrak{D}^{*}=\mathfrak{D}\setminus\mathfrak{P}=\{x\in\mathfrak{D}:|x|=1\}
;
\mathfrak{D}^{*}
is the group of units in

. If
x\neq 0
, we can write as

, with
x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}^{*}
. Let
\mathfrak{P}^{k}=\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq q^{-k}\}\penalty\ ,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. These are called

.

The set
\{\mathfrak{P}^{k}\subset K:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfies the following:

(i)

\{\mathfrak{P}^{k}\subset K:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a base for neighborhood system of identity in
K
, and
\mathfrak{P}^{k+1}\subset\mathfrak{P}^{k},\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z};

(ii)

\mathfrak{P}^{k},\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z},
is open, closed and compact in
K
;

(iii)

K=\bigcup\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{P}^{k}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \{0\}=\bigcap\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{P}^{k}.

Let
\Gamma
be the character group of the additive group
K^{+}
of

. There is a nontrivial character
\chi\in\Gamma
which is trivial on
\mathfrak{D}
but is non-trivial on
\mathfrak{P}^{-1}

\chi
be a non-trivial character on

, then the corresponding relationship

and the topological isomorphism is established for

For

be the annihilator of

that is,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}=\{\chi\in\Gamma:\forall x\in\mathfrak{P}^{k}\implies\chi(x)=1\}.

The locally compact group

can be endowed with the non-Archimedian norm as

For the Haar measure

be the Haar measure on

The set

satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\Gamma^{k}\subset\Gamma^{k+1},\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z}
, are increasing sequence in

which is open, closed and compact;

(ii)

\Gamma=\bigcup\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\Gamma^{k}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \{I\}=\bigcap\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\Gamma^{k},
where

(iii)

is a base of the unit

.

2.2.
Function classes

In this subsection we will introduce some function classes:

Test function class
S(K)
:
It is the linear space in which functions have the form

\phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}^{j}}(x-h_{j}),\penalty\ c_{j}\in\mathbb{C},h_{j}\in K,j\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N},

is the characteristic function of

The space

is an algebra of continuous functions with compact support that separates points. Consequently,

is dense in

as well as

Similarly, the test function class

on
\Gamma
can be defined. However, since

is isomorphic to
\Gamma

and
S(\Gamma)
can be regarded as equivalent with respect to absolute value or valuation.

The space
S(K)
is equipped with a topology as a topological vector space as follows: Define a null sequence in
S(K)
as a sequence
\{\phi_{n}\}
of functions on

in such a way that each
\phi_{n}
is constant on cosets of
\mathfrak{P}^{l}
and is supported on
\mathfrak{P}^{k}
for a fixed pair of integers

and

and the sequence converges to zero uniformly. The space

is complete and separable and is called the

.

Since

is dense in

, thus the Fourier transformation of

is defined by

\widehat{\phi}(\xi)\equiv(\mathcal{F}\phi)(\xi)=\int_{K}\phi(x)\bar{\chi}_{\xi}(x)dx,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \xi\in\Gamma,

and the inverse Fourier transformation of

is defined by the formula

\widecheck{\phi}(x)\equiv(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi)(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\phi(\xi)\chi_{x}(\xi)d\xi,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x\in K.

S^{\prime}(K)
, the

, is a collection of continuous linear functional on

.
S^{\prime}(K)
is also a complete topological linear space. The action of

in

on an element

in

is denoted by

. The distribution space

is given the weak
\ast
topology. Convergence in

is defined in the following way:

converges to

if
\langle f_{k},\phi\rangle
converges to

The Fourier transformation

of a distribution

is defined by

\langle\widehat{f},\phi\rangle\penalty\ =\penalty\ \langle f,\widehat{\phi}\rangle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ all}\penalty\ \phi\in S(K),

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\Delta_{j}f-f\bigg)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{j}f)(\xi)-\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\phi_{j}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)-\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\phi_{j}-1\bigg)\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi).

Since
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}=1.
Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(S_{n}f-f)(\xi)=0,

and therefore,

\displaystyle f=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}f.

∎

Definition 2.11

(B-type space)

For

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{sjt}\bigg\{\int_{K}|\Delta_{j}f|^{r}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{t}{r}}\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{t}}.

The Hasimoto Transformation for a Finite Length Vortex Filament and its Application
Masashi A iki

Abstract
We consider two nonlinear equations, the Localized Induction Equation and the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger Equation, and prove that the solvability of certain initial-boundary value problems for each equation is equivalent through the generalized Hasimoto transformation. As an application, we prove the orbital stability of plane wave solutions of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation based on stability estimates obtained for the Localized Induction Equation by the author in a paper in preparation. As far as the author knows, this is the first time that the analysis of the Localized Induction Equation, along with the Hasimoto transformation, provided new insight for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

Introduction and Problem Setting

A vortex filament is a space curve on which the vorticity of the fluid
is concentrated.
Vortex filaments are used to model very thin vortex structures such as vortices that
trail off airplane wings or propellers.
The model equation we consider in this paper is the
Localized Induction Equation (LIE) given by

\displaystyle\mbox{$x$}_{t}=\mbox{$x$}_{s}\times\mbox{$x$}_{ss}

where
\mbox{$x$}(s,t)={}^{t}(x_{1}(s,t),x_{2}(s,t),x_{3}(s,t))
is the
position vector of the vortex filament parametrized by
its arc length
s
at time
t
,

\times
is the exterior product in the three-dimensional Euclidean space,
and subscripts
s
and
t
are differentiations with the respective variables.

The LIE, which is derived by applying the localized induction approximation to the
Biot–Savart integral, was first derived by
Da Rios
[
13
]
in 1906 and was re-derived twice independently by
Murakami et al.
[
30
]
in 1937 and by
Arms and Hama
[
4
]
in 1965. Since then, many researches have been done on
the LIE and many results have been obtained.
Nishiyama and Tani
[
32
,
33
]
proved the unique solvability of initial and
initial-boundary value problems in Sobolev spaces.
The author
[
1
]
and the author and Iguchi
[
3
]
proved the unique solvability
of initial-boundary value problems in Sobolev spaces with different boundary
conditions.
In the above papers, the equation for the tangent vector,

\mbox{$v$}:=\mbox{$x$}_{s}
, given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{$v$}_{t}=\mbox{$v$}\times\mbox{$v$}_{ss}

is introduced in the analysis and plays an important role.
Equation (
1.1
) is sometimes called the Vortex Filament Equation (VFE).

Koiso
[
29
]
considered a geometrically generalized setting in which he
rigorously proved the
equivalence of the solvability of the initial value problem for the
VFE and the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation.
This equivalence was first shown by
Hasimoto
[
23
]
in which he studied the formation of solitons
on a vortex filament. He defined a transformation of variable known as the
Hasimoto transformation to transform the VFE into a
nonlinear Schrödinger equation.
The Hasimoto transformation is a change of variable given by

q(s,t)=\kappa(s,t)\exp\left({\rm i}\int^{s}_{0}\tau(r,t)\,{\rm d}r\right),

where
{\rm i}
is the imaginary unit,

\kappa
is the curvature, and
\tau
is the torsion of the filament.
Defined as such, it is well known that
q
satisfies the
nonlinear Schrödinger equation given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\rm i}q_{t}=q_{ss}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert q\rvert^{2}q.

The original transformation proposed by Hasimoto uses the torsion of the filament
in its definition, which means that the transformation is undefined at points
where the curvature of the filament is zero.
Koiso
[
29
]
constructed a transformation, sometimes referred to as the
generalized Hasimoto transformation, and gave a mathematically rigorous
proof of the equivalence of the VFE and (
1.2
).
More precisely, Koiso proved that the solvability of initial value problems
for the VFE and (
1.2
) are equivalent.
More recently,
Banica and Vega
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
and Gutiérrez, Rivas,
and Vega
[
22
]
utilized the generalized Hasimoto transformation
to construct and analyze
a family of self-similar solutions of the LIE which forms a corner in finite time.
In Chang, Shatah, and Uhlenbeck
[
12
]
and
Nahmod, Shatah, Vega, and Zeng
[
28
]
, they considered the
Schrödinger maps and employed a Hasimoto type transformation to
prove the correspondence between the solution of the Schrödinger maps and the
solution of a nonlinear Schrödinger type equation. The Schrödinger maps
are a generalization of the Heisenberg model for a ferromagnetic spin system given by

\displaystyle\mbox{$m$}_{t}=\mbox{$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{$m$},

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and the
unknown variable
m
takes values in

\mathbf{S}^{2}
. The Heisenberg model can be seen as a multi-dimensional
version of the
VFE.

All of the results mentioned above which utilizes the Hasimoto transformation
consider either an infinitely long filament defined on the whole line or
a closed filament defined on the torus.
As far as the author knows, the rigorous justification and application of the
Hasimoto transformation for problems describing the
motion of filaments with end-points have not been done.
In this paper, we justify the Hasimoto transformation and prove the equivalence of the
solvability of the following initial-boundary value problems.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$v$}_{t}=\mbox{$v$}\times\mbox{$v$}_{ss},&s\in I_{L},\ t>0,\\[8.53581pt]
\mbox{$v$}(s,0)=\mbox{$v$}_{0},&s\in I_{L},\ t>0,\\[8.53581pt]
\mbox{$v$}(0,t)=\mbox{$e$}_{1},\ \mbox{$v$}(L,t)=\mbox{$b$},&t>0,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\rm i}q_{t}=q_{ss}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert q\rvert^{2}q,&s\in I_{L},t>0,\\[8.53581pt]
q(s,0)=q_{0}(s),&s\in I_{L},\\[8.53581pt]
q_{s}(0,t)=q_{s}(L,t)=0,&t>0.\end{array}\right.

Problem (
1
) is an initial-boundary value problem for the VFE
which describes the
motion of a vortex filament on a slanted plane, considered in a previous paper by the
author
[
1
]
. Here,
L>0
is the length of the initial filament,

I_{L}\subset\mathbf{R}
is the interval
(0,L)
,

\mbox{$e$}_{1}={}^{t}(1,0,0)
, and

\mbox{$b$}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}

is an arbitrary constant vector with unit length. The boundary datum at

s=0
was chosen as
\mbox{$e$}_{1}
without loss of
generality, because the VFE is invariant under rotation.
Problem (
1
) is an initial-boundary value problem for the focusing cubic
nonlinear Schrödinger equation, where
q=q(s,t)
is a complex-valued function.

As an application of this equivalence, we will prove the orbital stability
of the plane wave solution

q_{R}

of (
1
) given in the form

\displaystyle q_{R}(t)=-\frac{1}{R}\exp\left\{-\frac{{\rm i}t}{2R^{2}}\right\}

for
R>L/\pi
in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(I_{L})
. This will be done by
considering problem (
1
) with appropriate
initial and boundary data, utilizing energy estimates for the solution

(

[

]
, and transferring
the estimates for

As far as the author knows, the results of this paper is the first time
the generalized Hasimoto transformation is utilized to give new insight on
the nonlinear Schrödinger equation from known facts about the
VFE. All of the preceding works utilizing the generalized
Hasimoto transformation did so to analyze the solution of the VFE
utilizing known facts about the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

The initial value problem for equation (
1.2
) on the torus,
explicitly given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\rm i}u_{t}=u_{ss}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2}u,&s\in\mathbf{T},t>0,\\[8.53581pt]
u(s,0)=u_{0}(s),&s\in\mathbf{T},\end{array}\right.

where

(

1
) can be reduced to
the solvability of problem (
1
) by reflection and periodic extension.

It is known by Zakharov and Shabat
[
38
]
that
equation (
1.2
) is completely integrable, and
the solution to problem
(
1
) posses infinitely many conserved quantities.
This in turn implies that solutions in the Sobolev space

H^{m}
for
m\in\mathbf{N}

is bounded in
H^{m}
for all time. The solvability of problem (
1
) in
Lebesgue or Sobolev spaces are known, for example, by
Bourgain
[
9
]
.
Hence, it is natural to ask if particular types of solutions are stable
in Sobolev spaces.
Namely, the stability of plane wave solutions of equation (
1.2
) has
been studied by many researchers.

Zhidkov
[
39
]
gives a detailed analysis of the plane wave solutions for
the initial value problem on the whole space
\mathbf{R}
.
The stability of plane wave solutions and periodic wave solutions for
problem (
1
) was investigated by
Rowlands
[
35
]
,
Gallay and Hărăguş
[
18
,
19
]
,
Faou, Gauckler, and Lubich
[

]
, and

[
37
]
.

In particular, Gallay and Hărăguş
[
18
,
19
]
considered
problem (
1
) and
proved the orbital stability of periodic wave solutions in
H^{1}_{per}
,
i.e., the orbital stability in
H^{1}
with the perturbation restricted to
periodic perturbations with the same period as the periodic wave solution.
By definition, periodic wave solutions include plane wave solutions and hence,
the stability results in
[
18
,
19
]
are valid for plane wave solutions as well.

Faou, Gauckler, and Lubich
[
14
]
considered the
initial value problem on the torus with general dimension
d\geq 1
,
and proved the long-time orbital stability of plane wave solutions
in
H^{r}
. By long-time they mean
stability up to time of order
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{N}})
where

\varepsilon>0
is the size of the perturbation in
H^{r}
and

N\in\mathbf{N}
.
The index
r>0
must be chosen sufficiently large, depending on
N
and
the
L^{2}
norm of the perturbation.

Wilson
[
37
]
considered the stability problem of plane wave solutions
for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with general power nonlinearities,
and obtained similar results as
[
14
]
.

Other results on the
initial–boundary value problems for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation have been
obtained by Holmer
[
24
]
,
Fokas and Its
[
15
]
, Fokas, Its, and Sung
[
17
]
,
Lenells and Fokas
[
25
,
26
]
,
Bona, Sun, and Zhang
[
8
]
,
and
Fokas, Himonas, and Mantzavinos
[
16
]
.
These results prove the well-posedness of
initial-boundary value problems under various boundary conditions as well as
obtain representation formulas for boundary values which represent
unknown boundary values of a solution by known boundary values, but
do not address the problem of stability of specific solutions.

In summary, we see that up until this paper, the stability of
plane wave solutions in higher order Sobolev spaces is
only partially known. Specifically, the regularity for which
the stability is proved in
[
14
,
37
]
is not given explicitly, and
the time-span for which the stability holds is not global.
On the other hand, the results in this paper give the time-global orbital stability
of plane wave solutions in Sobolev space
H^{2}
.
This is possible greatly due to the fact that the method of the proof
given in this paper is vastly different from the preceding works.

The methods utilized to prove the stability of solutions in

[
39
,
35
,
18
,
19
,
14
,
37
]
can be broadly categorized into two types.
Variational methods and methods utilizing the Hamiltonian structure of the
equation.
The variational approach was utilized in
Cazenave and Lions
[
11
]
to prove orbital stability of
standing waves for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.
The approach utilizing the Hamiltonian structure of the equation was
introduced by Grillakis, Shatah, and Strauss
[
20
,
21
]
for a broad range of
equations having a Hamiltonian structure.
These methods are widely adopted to approach stability problems for a
wide variety of dispersive equations.
In contrast, the method employed in this paper is tailor-made specifically
for problem (
1
) and (
1
). Hence,
we are able to obtain more information for our
specific problem, but the method is not as widely applicable to other problems
compared to traditional methods.

The contents of the rest of the paper are as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce basic notations and define

) and
(

In Section 3, we prove our main theorems.

The stability estimates for arc-shaped solutions are essentially derived

3.3
Proof of Theorem
2.5

We divide the proof of Theorem
2.5
into five steps.

We first establish that perturbations to the initial datum of
problem (
1
) correspond to perturbations to the initial datum
of problem (
1
). Furthermore, we derive estimates that
show in what norms the perturbations translate.

Secondly, we show that

corresponds to a particular type of
initial datum of problem (
1
). Solutions of problem (
1
)
with this type of initial datum will be referred to as an
arc-shaped solution of problem (

Then, we prove stability estimates for
arc-shaped solutions of problem (

),
which is related to the stability problem for plane wave solutions of
problem (
1

are already utilized by the author in
[
2
]
, but we reiterate it here for completeness.

Finally, we combine the results of the previous steps to prove that
the plane wave solution

is orbitally stable. More precisely,
we show that the stability estimates for problem (
1
)
yield stability estimates for problem (
1
) through the
generalized Hasimoto transformation.

\displaystyle\qquad+(q^{0}_{2}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})_{s}\mbox{$w$}^{0}-q^{0}_{2}(q^{0}_{2}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})\mbox{$v$}_{0}+\tilde{q}^{0}_{2s}\mbox{$W$}+\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}\big((\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}-q^{0}_{2})\mbox{$v$}_{0}-(\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})\mbox{$V$}\big)

\displaystyle=-\big((q^{0}_{1}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{1})^{2}+(q^{0}_{2}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})^{2}\big)\mbox{$v$}_{0}+(q^{0}_{1}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{1})_{s}\mbox{$e$}^{0}+(q^{0}_{2}-\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})_{s}\mbox{$w$}^{0}

\displaystyle\qquad+\tilde{q}^{0}_{1s}\mbox{$E$}+\tilde{q}^{0}_{2s}\mbox{$W$}-\big((\tilde{q}^{0}_{1})^{2}+(\tilde{q}^{0}_{2})^{2}\big)\mbox{$V$},

from which we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$V$}_{ss}\|\leq C\big(\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{1}-q^{0}_{1}\|_{1}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}-q^{0}_{2}\|_{1}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{1}-q^{0}_{1}\|^{2}_{1}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}-q^{0}_{2}\|^{2}_{1}\big).

In a similar fashion, we can derive the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$V$}_{sss}\|\leq C\big(\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{1}-q^{0}_{1}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}-q^{0}_{2}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{1}-q^{0}_{1}\|^{3}_{2}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}_{2}-q^{0}_{2}\|^{3}_{2}\big).

In summary, we have the estimate

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mbox{$v$}_{0}}-\mbox{$v$}_{0}\|_{3}\leq C\big(\|\tilde{q}_{0}-q_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{q}_{0}-q_{0}\|^{3}_{2}\big),

where
C>0
depends on
\|\tilde{q}_{0}\|_{2}+\|q_{0}\|_{2}
.
We also see that
C>0
is non-decreasing with respect to

\|\tilde{q}_{0}\|_{2}+\|q_{0}\|_{2}
.
Inequality (
3.19
) shows that
H^{2}
-perturbations of
q_{0}

correspond to
H^{3}
-perturbations of
\mbox{$v$}_{0}
.

We summarize the conclusion of this section
in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
q_{0}\in H^{2}(I_{L})
and
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(I_{L})
which
satisfy the
0
-th order compatibility condition for
(
1
)
,
let
\mbox{$v$}_{0}
and
\tilde{\mbox{$v$}}_{0}

be initial data constructed by the generalized Hasimoto transformation
from
q_{0}
and
q_{0}+\varphi_{0}
, respectively.
Then,
\mbox{$v$}_{0},\tilde{\mbox{$v$}}_{0}\in H^{3}(I_{L})
and satsify

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mbox{$v$}_{0}}-\mbox{$v$}_{0}\|_{3}\leq C\big(\|\tilde{q}_{0}-q_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{q}_{0}-q_{0}\|^{3}_{2}\big),

where
C>0
depends on
\|q_{0}\|_{2}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{2}
,
and is non-decreasing with respect to
\|q_{0}\|_{2}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{2}
.

3.3.3
Stability of Arc-Shaped Solutions of Problem (

We derive stability estimates for arc-shaped solutions of problem (

).
These estimates are already derived by the author in

For
R>0
,
\mbox{$v$}^{R}
is the solution of
problem (

\displaystyle\mbox{$v$}_{0}(s)=\mbox{$v$}^{R}(s)={}^{t}(\cos(s/R),-\sin(s/R),0),

\displaystyle\mbox{$b$}={}^{t}(\cos(L/R),-\sin(L,/R),0).

Recall that
\mbox{$v$}^{R}
is a stationary solution.
We consider perturbations of the arc-shaped solution

\mbox{$v$}^{R}
.
Let,
\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\in H^{3}(I_{L})

be the initial perturbation satisfying the following.

Assumption 3.4
.

For the initial perturbation
\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}
,
we assume the following.

(A1)

\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{0}(s)+\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}(s)\rvert=1

for all
s\in I_{L}
.

(A2)

\mbox{$v$}_{0}+\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}

and
b
satisfy the compatibility conditions
for problem
(
1
)
up to order

1
.

Note that because
\mbox{$v$}_{0}
and

b

satisfy the
0
-th order compatibility condition,
assumption (A2) implies
\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\rvert_{s=0}=\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\rvert_{s=L}=\mbox{$0$}
.
Let
\mbox{$v$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
be the solution of problem (
1
) with
initial datum
\mbox{$v$}_{0}+\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}

and boundary datum
b
. For convenience,
we refer to this solution
v
as the perturbed
arc-shaped solution. Set

\mbox{$\varphi$}=\mbox{$v$}-\mbox{$v$}^{R}
.
Then,
\varphi
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{$\varphi$}_{t}=\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}+\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss}+\mbox{$v$}^{R}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss},&s\in I_{L},t>0,\\[8.53581pt]
\mbox{$\varphi$}(s,0)=\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}(s),&s\in I_{L},\\[8.53581pt]
\mbox{$\varphi$}(0,t)=\mbox{$\varphi$}(L,t)=\mbox{$0$}.&\end{array}\right.

Note that

\mbox{$\varphi$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
.
We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\mbox{$\varphi$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
, set

\displaystyle E(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)):=\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|^{2}.

Then, if
\varphi
satisfies

(
3.3.3
)
,

E(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))=E(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})
for all
t>0
.
In other words,
E
is a conserved quantity.

Proof.

From the first equation in (
3.3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|^{2}=2(\mbox{$\varphi$},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{t})=2(\mbox{$\varphi$},\mbox{$v$}^{R}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})=-2(\mbox{$\varphi$},\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}),

where integration by parts was used. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2}=2(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{st})=-2(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{t})

\displaystyle=-2(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss})

\displaystyle=2(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{sss})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$\varphi$},\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}),

where we substituted
\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{sss}=-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}
.
Combining the two equalities show that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))=0
,
and this proves the lemma.
∎

From Lemma
3.5
, the following Corollary immediately follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

For
\mbox{$\varphi$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
satisfying

(
3.3.3
)
, if
R>L/\pi
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|_{1}\leq C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}

holds for all
t>0
, where
C>0
depends on
L
and
R
.

Proof.

Since
\mbox{$\varphi$}\rvert_{s=0}=\mbox{$\varphi$}\rvert_{s=L}=\mbox{$0$}
,
the Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|\leq\frac{L}{\pi}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|,

is applicable and hence

\displaystyle E(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))\geq\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\right)^{2}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|^{2}=c_{0}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|^{2}

holds for all
t>0
, where

c_{0}=1-\frac{L^{2}}{R^{2}\pi^{2}}\in(0,1)
. This with
Lemma
3.5
yields

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}(t)\|^{2}\leq CE(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})\leq C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1},

where
C>0
depends on
L
and
R
.
Applying the Poincaré inequality once more, we have

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|_{1}\leq C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}

for all
t>0
.

∎

Next, we show higher-order estimates.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\mbox{$\varphi$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
satisfying
(
3.3.3
)
,
there exists
C>0
depending only on
L
and
R
such that
if
R>L/\pi
,
\varphi
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}(t)\|_{1}\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}^{3}\big)

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

We make use of conserved quantities for problem (
1
), which was
also utilized in
[
1
]
. Properties of solutions of
problem (
1
) that we make use of here are either well known, or proved in
[
1
]

and hence, we will use them without proof.
The perturbed arc-shaped solution

v
satisfies

\lvert\mbox{$v$}(s,t)\rvert=1
for all
s\in I_{L}
and

t>0
. Hence,

\displaystyle 1=\lvert\mbox{$v$}(s,t)\rvert^{2}=\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}(s)+\mbox{$\varphi$}(s,t)\rvert^{2}

for all
s\in I_{L}
and
t>0
.
Since
\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}(s)\rvert=1
, we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle 2\mbox{$v$}^{R}(s)\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}(s,t)=-\lvert\mbox{$\varphi$}(s,t)\rvert^{2}

for all
s\in I_{L}
and
t>0
.
We also have the following conserved quantities.

(3.27)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg\{\|\mbox{$v$}_{ss}\|^{2}-\frac{5}{4}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}\rvert^{2}\|^{2}\bigg\}=0,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg\{\|\mbox{$v$}_{sss}\|^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}\rvert\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{ss}\rvert\|^{2}-14\|\mbox{$v$}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$v$}_{ss}\|^{2}+\frac{21}{8}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}\rvert^{3}\|^{2}\bigg\}=0.

These quantities are well known, and the above equality can be verified by
direct calculation.

First we set

\displaystyle E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}(t)):=\|\mbox{$v$}_{ss}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{5}{4}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}(t)\rvert^{2}\|^{2}.

For
t\geq 0
we substitute

\mbox{$v$}=\mbox{$v$}^{R}+\mbox{$\varphi$}
and decompose as follows.

\displaystyle E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}(t))=E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R})+E_{1}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))+R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)),

where
R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))

is given by

\displaystyle R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))

\displaystyle=2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})-5(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}^{R}_{s})-5((\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{5}{2}(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})-5(\lvert\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})

On the other hand, since
E_{1}
is conserved, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}(t))=E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}_{0})

\displaystyle=E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}+\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})

\displaystyle=E_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R})+E_{1}(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})+R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}),

and combining the two equalities, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))=E_{1}(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})-R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$})+R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}).

We estimate
R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$})

in detail and omit the details for

R_{1}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})

since they are the same.
The terms that are linear with respect to

\varphi
can be estimated as follows.
First we have

\displaystyle 2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})=-\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})-\frac{2}{R^{2}}\big[\mbox{$v$}^{R}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\big]^{L}_{s=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$})-\frac{2}{R^{2}}\big[\mbox{$v$}^{R}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\big]^{L}_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{R^{4}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$})-\frac{2}{R^{2}}\big[\mbox{$v$}^{R}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\big]^{L}_{s=0}.

Integrating equation (
3.26
) with respect to
s
shows that

(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$})=-\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|^{2}
.
Furthermore, differentiating equation (
3.26
) with respect to

and taking the trace

yields

\mbox{$v$}^{R}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\rvert_{s=0,L}=0
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle 2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})=-\frac{1}{R^{4}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle-5(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})=-\frac{5}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})=\frac{5}{2R^{4}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2},

where
\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert\equiv\frac{1}{R}
was
substituted.
Taking into account the above two equalities, we have

\displaystyle\lvert 2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})-5(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})\rvert\leq C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|_{1}^{2}\leq C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}^{2},

where Corollary
3.6
was applied in the last inequality.

For the other terms, we have

\displaystyle\big\lvert-5((\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})-\frac{5}{2}(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})

\displaystyle-5(\lvert\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|_{1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}^{3}\big)

where
C>0
depends only on
R
.
Corollary
3.6
and the interpolation inequality yield

\displaystyle E_{1}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))

\displaystyle\geq\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}\|^{2}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}\|\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{3}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}\|^{2}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{6}

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}\|^{2}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}^{2}-C\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{1}^{6}

where
c_{1}>0
depends only on
L
and
C>0
depends on
L

and
R
. Combining all of the obtained estimates yields

(3.29)

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}(t)\|^{2}\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{2}^{6}\big)

for all
t>0
. Here,
C>0
depends on
L
and
R
.

Similarly, if we set

\displaystyle E_{2}(\mbox{$v$}(t))=\|\mbox{$v$}_{sss}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}(t)\rvert\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{ss}(t)\rvert\|^{2}-14\|\mbox{$v$}_{s}(t)\cdot\mbox{$v$}_{ss}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{21}{8}\|\lvert\mbox{$v$}_{s}(t)\rvert^{3}\|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle E_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}+\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})

\displaystyle=E_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R})+E_{2}(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})+R_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}),

\displaystyle E_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}+\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))

\displaystyle=E_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R})+E_{2}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))+R_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)),

and the conservation of
E_{2}
implies that the above two are equal.
Hence,

\displaystyle E_{2}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))=E_{2}(\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})+R_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0})-R_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)).

Here,

\displaystyle R_{2}(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))

\displaystyle=2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{sss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss})+2(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})+(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad+8(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{4}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})+N(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)),

where

are
terms that are nonlinear with respect to
\varphi
.
We estimate as follows. First we have

\displaystyle 2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{sss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss})=-\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss})=-\frac{2}{R^{2}}[\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}]^{L}_{s=0}+\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})

Taking the exterior product of

\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}
from the left with the first equation in
problem (
3.3.3
), we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{t}=\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times(\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})+\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times(\mbox{$\varphi$}\times\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss})+\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\times(\mbox{$v$}^{R}\times\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}).

Since
\mbox{$\varphi$}_{t}\rvert_{s=0,L}=\mbox{$0$}
,

in (

) yields

\displaystyle(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})\mbox{$v$}^{R}\rvert_{s=0,L}=\mbox{$0$}.

\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}\rvert\equiv 1
further implies that

\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\cdot\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss}\rvert_{s=0,L}=0
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle 2(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{sss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss})=\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})=-\frac{1}{R^{6}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|^{2}.

Next we estimate

\displaystyle 2(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{R^{2}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{ss})=-\frac{1}{R^{6}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|^{2},

\displaystyle(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{ss}\rvert^{2}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})+8(\lvert\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s}\rvert^{4}\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{9}{R^{4}}(\mbox{$v$}^{R}_{s},\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s})=-\frac{9}{2R^{4}}\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{s}\|^{2},

and finally,

\displaystyle\lvert N(\mbox{$v$}^{R},\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))\rvert\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}\|_{2}^{5}\big).

Additionally,

\displaystyle E_{2}(\mbox{$\varphi$}(t))\geq\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss}(t)\|^{2}-C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}(t)\|_{2}^{6}\big)

along with all the estimates previously obtained shows that

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{sss}(t)\|^{2}\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}^{2}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}^{6}\big)

holds for all
t>0
, where
C>0
depends on
L
and
R
.
Estimates (
3.29
) and (
3.31
)
combined proves Lemma
3.7
.
∎

In summary, we have the following.

Proposition 3.8
.

For
R>L/\pi
, the arc-shaped solution

\mbox{$v$}^{R}
of problem
(
1
)
is
stable in the following sense.
For
\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\in H^{3}(I_{L})

satisfying Assumption

, the perturbed arc-shaped solution

\mbox{$v$}\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{3}(I_{L})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(I_{L})\big)
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mbox{$v$}(t)-\mbox{$v$}^{R}\|_{3}\leq C\big(\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}+\|\mbox{$\varphi$}_{0}\|_{3}^{3}\big)

for all

. Here,

and

.

Let,
\mbox{$v$}_{1}

Tridendriform algebras on hypergraph polytopes
Pierre-Louis Curien †, Bérénice Delcroix-Oger ∗, Jovana Obradović ‡

Abstract
. We extend the works of Loday-Ronco and Burgunder-Ronco on the tridendriform decomposition of the shuffle product on the faces of associahedra and permutohedra, to other families of hypergraph polytopes (or nestohedra), including simplices, hypercubes and some new families. We also extend the shuffle product to take more than two arguments, and define accordingly a new algebraic structure, that we call polydendriform , from which the original tridendriform equations can be crisply synthesized.



1.
Introduction

In 1998, Loday-Ronco introduced a Hopf algebra on the linear
span of rooted planar binary trees
[
11
]
.
This Hopf algebra is closely related to the Malvenuto-
Reutenauer Hopf algebra on permutations

Planar binary trees and permutations label the vertices of

Associahedra and permutohedra are instances of polytopes called

[
6
]
, which are obtained by truncating some faces of simplices, and are also known as

[
14

]
provides an adapted framework to extend the setting of Loday-Ronco and Burgunder-Ronco to other families of polytopes.

We find it convenient to work in an “unbiased” setting, where our operations may have any finite arity (think of the product

The underlying (binary) associative product that we obtain coincides with the associative product defined by Ronco
[
15
]
on graph associahedra
[
3
]
, which are a special type of hypergraph polytopes where the associated hypergraphs have only hyperedges of cardinality two. Our results apply also to other families of hypergraph polytopes such as simplices, hypercubes and erosohedra.

Therefore, with respect to
[
15
]
, our extension is two-fold: we describe not only an associative product, but a tridendriform splitting of it, and our framework applies in situations that are not covered by graph associahedra.

The article is organized as follows.
In §

In §

In §

\{x_{0}\}(\{x_{1},\ldots x_{k+1}\}(\{y_{1}\},\ldots,\{y_{p}\}))

4.
shuffle product

In our main section, we unify the above mentioned works of Burgunder, Loday and Ronco into a notion that we call shuffle product of constructs (defined in an unbiased style, cf. §
1
). Towards achieving this goal, in §
4.1
we introduce a general framework based on the formalism of hypergraph polytopes of §
3
, which will serve as “carrier” of an algebraic structure that we define by induction in §
4.3
. We show that the structure satisfies an equation that we call polydendriform, and derive an associative product from it. We show that associahedra and permutohedra fit in this framework, as well as all families of restrictohedra, which we define and study in §
4.2
. We illustrate the notions introduced with the example of friezohedra. In §
4.4
, we give an alternative non-recursive definition of the associated associative product.
In §
4.5
, we further enlarge the framework to cover more examples.

4.1.
Strict teams, clans and delegations

We first specify a collection (or
universe
)
\mathfrak{U}
of connected hypergraphs. Note that contrary to
[
15
]
, some universes may contain several hypergraphs on the same set of vertices. It is for instance the case for erosohedra, see Example

A

for all

living in

Lemma 4.3
.

A preteam
(\{{\bf H}_{a}\mid a\in A\},{\bf H})
is strict iff, for all
a\in A
and
e\in{\bf H}_{a}
,
e
is connected in
{\bf H}
. Also, in the above definition of strict team, it holds that
H_{\tilde{a}}
is connected in
{\bf H}
, for all
\tilde{a}\in\tilde{A}
.

Proof.

We shall prove the equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
, where (1) is the definition of strict team given above, (2) is the characterization claimed in the statement, and (3) is the definition of team above enhanced with the additional property claimed in the statement.

•

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. If
(\{{\bf H}_{a}\mid a\in A\},{\bf H})
is a strict team in the sense of the definition given above, then, in particular, for each
a\in A
and
e\in{\bf H}_{a}
, taking
B=A
,
X_{b}=H_{b}
for
b\neq a
and
X_{a}=(H_{a}\backslash e)
, we get that
{\bf H}_{a},X_{a}\leadsto{\bf H}_{e}
, and hence that
e
is included in a connected component
K
of
{\bf H}\backslash(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})
. But for our choice of
B
, we have

H\backslash(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})=e
, hence this forces
K=e
, and a fortiori
e
is connected in
{\bf H}
.

•

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Let
\tilde{a}\in\tilde{A}
and
\tilde{e}\in{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
. Then a fortiori
\tilde{e}\in{\bf H}_{\pi(a)}
, where
\pi:\tilde{A}\rightarrow A
is defined by
\pi(\tilde{a})=\tilde{a}
if
\tilde{a}\in A\backslash B
and
\pi{(b,i)}=b
. Since we assume (2), we have that
\tilde{e}
is connected in
{\bf H}
. Thus, all hyperedges of
{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
are connected in
{\bf H}
. By standard connectedness arguments, this, together with the fact that
{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
is connected,
implies that
H_{\tilde{a}}
is connected in
{\bf H}
: informally, every path of hyperedges of
{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
witnessing the connectedness of
{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
, for arbitrary chosen vertices in
H_{\tilde{a}}
, can be turned into a path of hyperedges of
{\bf H}
witnessing the connectedness of
H_{\tilde{a}}
in
{\bf H}
for the same chosen vertices.

•

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Obvious.

∎

Our main provision of strict clans comes from the universes of restrictohedra, that we define next.

Proposition 4.7
.

For all
{\bf R}
,
\Xi_{\bf R}
is an associative clan. If
{\bf R}
is order-friendly, then the restriction of
\Xi_{\bf R}
(still denoted by
\Xi_{\bf R}

Proof.

We first note that
every preteam
(\{\mathbf{R}_{V_{a}}|a\in A\},\mathbf{R}_{V})
satisfies
\bigcup_{a\in A}\mathbf{R}_{V_{a}}\subseteq\mathbf{R}_{V}
by definition, and hence, by Lemma
4.3

4.1
)

\tau\backslash\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b}\;\uline{\leadsto}\;\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n_{B}},
we have to prove that
\tau_{i}\in\Xi_{\bf R}
for all
i
. This follows from the fact that, for any
V
and
W\subseteq V
,
({\bf R}_{V})\backslash W={\bf R}_{V\backslash W}
and that, for all
X\subseteq R
, the connected components of
{\bf R}_{X}
are all of the form
{\bf R}_{Y}
for some

. Finally, the clan is associative since
\Xi_{\bf R}
includes all “possible” preteams in the sense that for any
X\subseteq R
and any partition
\{X_{a}\mid a\in A\}
of

, we have
(\{\mathbf{R}_{X_{a}}|a\in A\},\mathbf{R}_{X})\in\Xi_{\bf R}
if and only if
\mathbf{R}_{X}
and
\mathbf{R}_{X_{a}}
(for all

) are connected.

Suppose now that
{\bf R}
is moreover order-friendly. Then it is immediate that
\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
is ordered. Since we limit ourselves to ordered preteams
(({\bf H}_{1},\ldots,{\bf H}_{m}),{\bf R}_{V})
with

and

for all

, and since

is order-friendly, then for all

there is an induced order on

such that, if

, then
V_{\tilde{a_{1}}}<V_{\tilde{a_{2}}}
, where

. This in turn implies that
(\varphi^{B}_{\tau})^{-1}(1)
,…,
(\varphi^{B}_{\tau})^{-1}(n_{B})
form successive intervals of
\tilde{A}
, and hence that each
\tau_{i}
is ordered.
∎

The following family of graphs provides examples of order-friendly graphs (and hence of ordered associative clans).

Proposition 4.8
.

For all

, the following graph is order-friendly:

\mathbb{\Gamma}^{k}:=\{\{a\}\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\{a,a+l\}\mid a\in\mathbb{Z},l\in\mathbb{N},1\leq l\leq k\}.

Proof.

A subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is not connected in
\mathbb{\Gamma}^{k}
if and only if there is a set
X
of at least
k

]\min(V);\max(V)[
, which does not intersect
V
.
If
X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}
are the sets of such maximal sequences of consecutive integers, then the interval
[\min(V),\max(V)]
in
\mathbb{Z}
is the union of consecutive intervals

, and the connected components of

(\mathbb{\Gamma}^{k})_{V\cap I_{0}},\ldots,(\mathbb{\Gamma}^{k})_{V\cap I_{p}}

.

(for
X
interval of
\mathbb{Z}
) and
\mathbb{\Gamma}^{\infty}_{X}={\bf P}^{X}
(for finite
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
), respectively.
The teams are of the form
(\{\mathbb{\Gamma}^{1}_{X_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{\Gamma}^{1}_{X_{p}}\},\mathbb{\Gamma}^{1}_{\bigcup X_{i}})
(where the
X_{i}
are adjacent intervals) and
(\{\mathbb{\Gamma}^{\infty}_{X_{1}},\ldots,\mathbb{\Gamma}^{\infty}_{X_{p}}\},\mathbb{\Gamma}^{\infty}_{\bigcup X_{i}})
(where
X_{i}<X_{i+1}
for all
i<p
), respectively. For
k=2
,
we have
\mathbb{\Gamma}^{2}={\bf F}
, and hence we recover also friezohedra as a special case.

We end the section with a characterization of universes arising as restrictohedra.

Proposition 4.9
.

A universe
\mathfrak{U}
is of the form
\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
, for some hypergraph
{\bf R}
, if and only if it satisfies the following four conditions:

(1)

For any hypergraphs
{\bf H}_{1}
and
{\bf H}_{2}
in
\mathfrak{U}
, if
H_{1}=H_{2}
, then
{\bf H}_{1}={\bf H}_{2}
.

(2)

If
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
and
e\in{\bf H}
, if
{\bf G}\in\mathfrak{U}
is such that
e\subseteq G
, then
e\in{\bf G}
.

(3)

If
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
, and if
X\subseteq H
is such that
{\bf H}_{X}
is connected, then there exists
{\bf G}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
G=X
.

(4)

If
{\bf H}_{1},{\bf H}_{2}\in\mathfrak{U}
are such that
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
is non-empty, then there exists
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
{\bf H}_{1},{\bf H}_{2}\subseteq{\bf H}
.

Proof.

We first check that any universe of the form
\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
satisfies the conditions in the statement. Condition (1) is immediate. Conditions (2), (3) and (4) follow immediately from the observations that, by definition, for arbitrary
X
, we have
e\in{\bf R}_{X}
if and only if
e\in{\bf R}
and
e\subseteq X
, that

({\bf R}_{H})_{X}={\bf R}_{X}
, and that the union of two connected sets with a non-empty intersection is connected.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathfrak{U}
satisfies the four conditions of the statement. We set
{\bf R}=\bigcup\{{\bf H}\mid{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}\}
. We shall show the following two properties, which (together with (1)) imply immediately that
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
.

(a)

If
X
is a finite set such that there exists a hypergraph
{\bf H}
such that
H=X
and
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{R}
, then there exists a hypergraph

{\bf H}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf K}}
such that
H^{\prime}=X
and
{\bf H}\subseteq{\bf H}^{\prime}
.

(b)

If
X
is a finite set such that there exists a hypergraph
{\bf H}^{\prime}
such that
H^{\prime}=X
and
{\bf H}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
, then there exists a hypergraph

{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
H=X
and
{\bf H}^{\prime}\subseteq{\bf H}
.

For (a), we note that
{\bf H}\subseteq{\bf R}
by definition of
{\bf R}
, hence
{\bf H}\subseteq{\bf R}_{H}
, so we can set

{\bf H}^{\prime}:={\bf R}_{H}
, noticing that
{\bf R}_{H}
is connected since it contains a connected hypergraph (namely
{\bf H}
) with the same set of vertices.

We now proceed to prove (b). By definition of
\mathfrak{U}_{{\bf R}}
, the assumptions of (b) can be rephrased as saying that
{\bf H}^{\prime}={\bf R}_{X}
is connected. Also,
by definition of
{\bf R}
, for each
e\in{\bf R}_{X}
, there exists a hypergraph
{\bf H}^{e}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
e\in{\bf H}^{e}
.
So we have
{\bf R}_{X}\subseteq\bigcup\{{\bf H}^{e}\mid e\in{\bf R}_{X}\}
, this union being finite since
X
is. Suppose that
{\bf R}_{X}
has more than one hyperedge and pick
e_{0}\in{\bf R}_{X}
. We claim that there exists
e_{1}\in{\bf R}_{X}
such that

{\bf H}^{e_{0}}\cap{\bf H}^{e_{1}}
is non-empty. If it were not the case, then
{\bf R}_{X}
would be the disjoint union of
{\bf R}_{X}\cap{\bf H}^{e_{0}}
and of
{\bf R}_{X}\bigcap(\bigcup\{{\bf H}^{e}\mid e\neq e_{0}\})
, which would contradict the connectedness of
{\bf R}_{X}
.
We can thus replace
\{{\bf H}^{e}\mid e\in{\bf R}_{X}\}
by
\{{\bf H}^{e}\mid e\neq e_{0},e_{1}\}\cup\{{\bf H}_{01}\}
, where
{\bf H}_{01}\in\mathfrak{U}
is obtained from
{\bf H}^{e_{0}}
and
{\bf H}^{e_{1}}
by applying (4). By iterating this, we obtain a hypergraph
{\bf H}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
{\bf R}_{X}\subseteq{\bf H^{\prime}}
. Note that we can write this as well as

{\bf R}_{X}\subseteq{\bf H^{\prime}}_{X}
, and, as above, we have that the connectedness of
{\bf R}_{X}
implies the connectedness of

{\bf H^{\prime}}_{X}
.

Our next (independent) observation is that in the presence of (2), condition (3) can be reinforced as follows. If
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
and if
X\subseteq H
is such that
{\bf H}_{X}
is connected, then there exists
{\bf G}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that
G=X
and
{\bf H}_{X}\subseteq{\bf G}
. Indeed, let
{\bf G}
be obtained by applying (3), and let
e\in{\bf H}_{X}
: then this latter assumption reads as
e\subseteq G
, and hence
e\in{\bf G}
by (2).

Coming back to the proof of (b), we can apply the reinforced version of (3) to deduce the existence of a hypergraph
{\bf H}\in\mathfrak{U}
such that

H=X
and
{\bf H^{\prime}}_{X}\subseteq{\bf H}
. We thus have
{\bf R}_{X}\subseteq{\bf H^{\prime}}_{X}\subseteq{\bf H}
, which concludes the proof.

∎

4.3.
Shuffle product of delegations of strict clans

We now define the shuffle product
\ast(\delta)
, for a
\Xi
-delegation
\delta
, where
\Xi
is a strict clan. Until §
4.5
, we shall omit the adjective “strict” for brevity, but its presence is understood.

A
linear construct
of a hypergraph
{\bf H}
is an element of the vector space spanned by all the constructs of
{\bf H}
. We shall denote linear constructs with bold capital letters, e.g.,
{\bf C}=\Sigma_{i\in I}{\lambda_{i}}C_{i}
, where
C_{i}:{\bf H}
, for each
i\in I
, and the notation
{\bf C}:{\bf H}
will mean that
{\bf C}
is a linear construct of
{\bf H}
. We then define
X({\bf C}_{1},\dots,\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}C^{i}_{j},\dots,{\bf C}_{n})
as
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}X({\bf C}_{1},\dots,C^{i}_{j},\dots,{\bf C}_{n})
. A
rooted linear construct
is a linear construct of the form
{\bf C}=X\{{\bf C}_{a}\mid a\in A\}
, and we write
\mbox{root}({\bf C})=X
.

The
shuffle product
(or product) of a delegation
\delta=(\{C_{a}:{\bf H}_{a}\,|\,a\in A\},{\bf H})
, with
\mbox{root}(C_{a})=X_{a}
for all
a\in A
, is the linear construct of
{\bf H}
defined recursively as follows (with
\delta^{B}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{B}_{n_{B}}
as in (
4.2
)):

(4.3)

\ast(\delta)=\sum_{\emptyset\subsetneq B\subseteq A}q^{{|B|}-1}\ast_{B}(\delta),\quad\mbox{where}\;\ast_{B}(\delta)=(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})(\ast(\delta^{B}_{1}),\dots,\ast(\delta^{B}_{n_{B}})).

The instantiations of this shuffle product to associahedra and permutohedra are the ones recalled in §
2
. We detail the case of permutohedra in the next example.

Example 4.10
.

We restrict ourselves to teams with only two participating hypergraphs (which corresponds to the usual binary product on permutohedra). Then
the shuffle product of a delegation

\delta=(\{C_{1}:{\bf P}^{Y},C_{2}:{\bf P}^{Z}\},{\bf P}^{X}),\;\mbox{where}\;X=Y\cup Z,C_{1}=X_{1}(C^{\prime}_{1}),\;\mbox{and}\;C_{2}=X_{2}(C^{\prime}_{2}),

rewrites as:

\displaystyle\ast(\delta)

\displaystyle=\ast_{\{1\}}(\delta)+\ast_{\{2\}}(\delta)+q\ast_{\{1,2\}}(\delta),\;\mbox{where}

\displaystyle\ast_{\{1\}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=X_{1}(\ast(\{C^{\prime}_{1}:{\bf P}^{Y\backslash X_{1}},C_{2}:{\bf P}^{Z}\},{\bf P}^{X\backslash X_{1}})),

\displaystyle\ast_{\{2\}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=X_{2}(\ast(\{C_{1}:{\bf P}^{Y},C^{\prime}_{2}:{\bf P}^{Z\backslash X_{2}}\},{\bf P}^{X\backslash X_{2}})),

\displaystyle\ast_{\{1,2\}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\left(X_{1}\cup X_{2}\right)(\ast(\{C^{\prime}_{1}:{\bf P}^{Y\backslash X_{1}},C^{\prime}_{2}:{\bf P}^{Z\backslash X_{2}}\},{\bf P}^{X\backslash\left(X_{1}\cup X_{2}\right)})).

Writing
\ast_{\{1\}}\!=\>\prec
,
\ast_{\{2\}}\!=\>\succ
and
\ast_{\{1,2\}}\!=\!\,{\bm{\cdot}}\,
, and using an infix notation, the formula for the shuffle product on permutohedra on two constructs
C_{1}=X_{1}(C^{\prime}_{1})
and
C_{2}=X_{2}(C^{\prime}_{2})
writes as:

\displaystyle C_{1}\ast C_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{1}\prec C_{2}+C_{1}\succ C_{2}+q(C_{1}\,{\bm{\cdot}}\,C_{2}),\;\mbox{where}

\displaystyle C_{1}\prec C_{2}

\displaystyle=X_{1}(C^{\prime}_{1}\ast C_{2}),

\displaystyle C_{1}\succ C_{2}

\displaystyle=X_{2}(C_{1}\ast C^{\prime}_{2}),

\displaystyle C_{1}\,{\bm{\cdot}}\,C_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(X_{1}\cup X_{2}\right)(C^{\prime}_{1}\ast C^{\prime}_{2}),

with the convention that if
C^{\prime}_{1}
or
C^{\prime}_{2}
is the empty construct, then its shuffle product with another construct
C
is
C
. It can be checked by direct induction that this definition coincides with the one given in §
2
.

Remark 4.11
.

Let us now explain in a few words why we found convenient to consider permutohedra on a given
X
which is not necessarily the usually considered set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
). Consider the basic example:

(4.4)

(1,2)\,{\bm{\cdot}}\,(2,1)=(1,2,2,1)+(1,3,3,2)+(2,3,3,1).

In terms of constructs, this rewrites as:

(4.5)

\{2\}(\{1\})\,{\bm{\cdot}}\,\{3\}(\{4\})=\{2,3\}(\{1\})\ast\{4\}).

Equation
4.5
invites us to compute products of constructs in the complete graph on
\{1,4\}
, rather than doing some renamings.
This is naturally in phase with the general philosophy of species. The assignment that maps
X
to the set of constructs of
{\bf P}^{X}

is functorial (with respect to finite sets and bijections), giving rise to a species in the sense of Joyal.

Example 4.12
.

As a second example, we consider friezohedra. Consider the delegation

\delta=(\{2(1)\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{1,2\}}\,,\,3(4)\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{3,4\}},\}\,,\,{\bf F}_{\{1,\ldots,4\}}).

The associated shuffle product is given by:

\displaystyle\ast(\delta)=

\displaystyle\;2(1(3(4)))+2(3(1,4))+2(13(4))+3(4(2(1)))

\displaystyle+3(2(1,4))+3(24(1))+q23(1,4).

Consider now the delegation

(\{3(1,5)\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{1,3,5\}}\,,\,2(4)\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{2,4\}}\,,\,678\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{6,7,8\}}\}\,,\,{\bf F}_{\{1,\ldots,8\}}).

of Example
4.6
.
The associated shuffle product is too big to be written here. Let us focus on the term associated with
B=\{1,2\}
.
We have
\ast_{B}(\delta)=23(1,\ast(\delta_{2}^{B}))
, with
\delta_{2}^{B}=(\{4\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{4\}}\,,\,5\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{5\}}\,,\,678\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{6,7,8\}}\}\,,\,{\bf F}_{\{4,\ldots,8\}})
(as already seen in that example). By definition, we can express
\ast(\delta_{2}^{B})
as a sum over
\emptyset\subsetneq B^{\prime}\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
. Let us again make a focus, say on
B^{\prime}=\{3\}
. We get

\ast_{B^{\prime}}(\delta_{2}^{B})=678(\ast(\{4\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{4\}}\,,\,5\!:\!{\bf F}_{\{5\}}\}\,,\,{\bf F}_{\{4,5\}}))=678(4(5))+678(5(4))+q\,678(45).

When dealing with the associativity of the product in Theorem
4.15
below, we shall have to take products of (delegations made of) linear constructs, which is not a problem, as the above definitions of
\ast
,
\ast_{B}
of course extend by linearity (with the notion of delegation accordingly extended to linear constructs).
The following lemmas show two situations in which the linear extension of
\ast_{B}
still satisfies its “defining” equation
4.3

(now a property!). To see the need for such lemmas, note that the definitions of the delegations
\delta^{B}_{i}
do depend on the root of the constructs
C_{b}
(
b\in B
), which no longer exists if
C_{b}
is replaced by a linear construct that is not rooted.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(\{{\bf H}_{a}\mid a\in A\},{\bf H})
be a strict team, and suppose that we are given rooted linear constructs
{\bf C}_{a}
for each
a\in A
with root
X_{a}
, forming a delegation
\delta
(in the extended sense). Let
\emptyset\subset B\subseteq A
and let
X_{B}=\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b}
. Then we have
\ast_{B}(\delta)=(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})(\ast(\delta^{B}_{1}),\dots,\ast(\delta^{B}_{n_{B}}))
, with the same
definition of
\delta^{B}_{i}
as above.

Proof.

We first notice that we can indeed still define
\delta_{i}^{B}
as before, since the only information used on constructs are their roots.
Let us assume for simplicity that only one of the
{\bf C}_{a}
, say
{\bf C}_{b_{0}}
, is a rooted linear construct, all the others being plain constructs, and that
b_{0}\in B
, as Lemma
4.14
will a fortiori cover the case where
b_{0}\not\in B
. We shall also assume for simplicity that

{\bf C}_{b_{0}}=X_{b_{0}}\{{\bf C}_{b_{0},i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n_{b_{0}}\}
, where only one of the
{\bf C}_{b_{0},i}
, say

{\bf C}_{b_{0},i_{0}}=\Sigma_{k\in K}\lambda_{k}C_{b_{0},i_{0},k}
, is a linear construct, all the others being plain constructs (and we write then

{\bf C}_{a}=C_{a}
for
a\neq b_{0}
and
{\bf C}_{b_{0},i}=C_{b_{0},i}
for
i\neq i_{0}
). Then, by “outward” linearity, we can write

{\bf C}_{b_{0}}=\Sigma_{k\in K}\lambda_{k}C_{b_{0},k}
, where

C_{b_{0},k}=X_{b_{0}}(\{{C}_{b_{0},i}\mid i\neq i_{0}\}\bigcup\{C_{b_{0},i_{0},k}\})
. We have

\ast_{B}(\{C_{a}\mid a\in A\backslash\{b_{0}\}\}\bigcup\{{\bf C}_{b_{0}}\},{\bf H})=\Sigma_{k\in I}\lambda_{k}\ast_{B}(\{C_{a}\mid a\in A\backslash\{b_{0}\}\}\bigcup\{C_{b_{0},k}\},{\bf H}).

By definition,
we have

\ast_{B}(\{C_{a}\mid a\in A\backslash\{b_{0}\}\}\bigcup\{{\bf C}_{b_{0}}\},{\bf H})=(\Sigma_{k\in K}\lambda_{k}X_{k}(\ast((\delta^{k})^{B}_{1}),\dots,\ast((\delta^{k})^{B}_{n_{B}}))),

where for all
j\neq j_{0}=\varphi_{\tau}(b_{0},i_{0})
, all
(\delta^{k})^{B}_{j}
are equal to
\delta^{B}_{j}
, and where the
(\delta^{k})^{B}_{j_{0}}
differ only in one (and the same) position (the one indexed by
(b_{0},i_{0})
), filled with
C_{b_{0},i_{0},k}
. Then we conclude by applying “inward” linearity.

∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
(\{{\bf H}_{a}\mid a\in A\},{\bf H})
be a strict team, and let
a_{0}\in A
, and suppose that we are given constructs
C_{a}:{\bf H}_{a}
with root
X_{a}
for all
a\neq a_{0}
, and a linear construct
{\bf C}_{a_{0}}
. Let
B\subseteq A\backslash\{a_{0}\}
, and let
X_{B}=\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b}
.
Then we have
\ast_{B}(\delta)=(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})(\ast(\delta^{B}_{1}),\dots,\ast(\delta^{B}_{n_{B}}))
, with the same
definition of the teams
\delta^{B}_{i}
as above.

Proof.

The proof goes like in Lemma
4.13
. The only difference is that, under the assumption that
a_{0}\not\in B
, no information at all is required on
{\bf C}_{a_{0}}=\Sigma_{k\in K}\lambda_{k}C_{a_{0},k}
.
∎

So far, we have a magmatic unbiased notion of product.
The following theorem establishes the associativity of the product for strict associative clans.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\Xi
be an associative clan, and suppose that
\tau=(\{{\bf H}_{a}\mid a\in A\},{\bf H})\in\Xi
,
a_{0}\in A
, and
\tau^{\prime}=(\{{\bf H}_{(a_{0},a^{\prime})}\mid a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\},{\bf H}_{a_{0}})\in\Xi
,
and that we are given constructs
C_{a}:{\bf H}_{a}
for all
a\in A\backslash\{a_{0}\}

and constructs
C_{(a_{0},a^{\prime})}:{\bf H}_{(a_{0},a^{\prime})}
for all all
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
. Taking
\tau^{\prime\prime}
to be the grafting of
\tau^{\prime}
to
\tau
along
a_{0}
and setting
A^{\prime\prime}:=(A\backslash\{a_{0}\})\cup\{(a_{0},a^{\prime})\,|\,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\}
, denote the corresponding delegations by
\delta^{\prime\prime}=(\tau^{\prime\prime},\{C_{a^{\prime\prime}}\,|\,a^{\prime\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}\})
and

\delta^{\prime}=(\tau^{\prime},\{C_{(a_{0},a^{\prime})}\,|\,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\})
.
We then have that, for each
\emptyset\neq B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A^{\prime\prime}
, the following polydendriform equation holds:

\ast^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}_{B^{\prime\prime}}(\delta^{\prime\prime})=\begin{cases}\ast^{\tau}_{B^{\prime\prime}}(\{C_{a}\,|\,a\in A\backslash\{a_{0}\}\}\cup\{\ast^{\tau^{\prime}}(\delta^{\prime})\}),&\mbox{ if }B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A\backslash\{a_{0}\}\\
\ast^{\tau}_{B}(\{C_{a}\,|\,a\in A\backslash\{a_{0}\}\}\cup\{\ast^{\tau^{\prime}}_{B^{\prime}}(\delta^{\prime})\}),&\mbox{ if }B^{\prime\prime}\not\subseteq A\backslash\{a_{0}\}\end{cases},

where the superscripts record the respective support teams, and where, in the second case,
B=(B^{\prime\prime}\cap(A\backslash\{a_{0}\}))\cup\{a_{0}\}
,
B^{\prime}=\{a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\,|\,(a_{0},a^{\prime})\in B^{\prime\prime}\}
(both non-empty). Moreover, the polydendriform equation implies the following associativity equation:

\ast^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}(\delta^{\prime\prime})=\ast^{\tau}(\{C_{a}\,|\,a\in A\backslash\{a_{0}\}\}\cup\{\ast^{\tau^{\prime}}(\delta^{\prime})\}).

Remark 4.17

One could formulate the polydendriform structure as an algebra over a colored operad, where the colors are hypergraphs, the operations are teams, and the carrier of the algebra for the color

4.5.
Extending the framework

In this subsection, we enlarge the coverage of our formalism of teams and clans, and we adapt the product accordingly, in order to cover other families of polytopes like simplices, hypercubes, or erosohedra.

A preteam
\tau=(\{{\bf H}_{a}\,|\,a\in A\},{\bf H})
is called a
semi-strict team
if for each choice of a subset
\emptyset\neq B\subseteq A
and of a subset
\emptyset\neq X_{b}\subseteq H_{b}
for each

\tilde{a}\in\tilde{A}

(1)

is included in a connected component of
{\bf H}\backslash(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})

(2)

, and, for all

,

is a connected component of
{\bf H}\backslash(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})
,

where
\tilde{A}
is as in §
4.1
. When (2) applies (and vacuously when

), we say that
{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}
is
dissolved
in
{\bf H}\backslash(\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b})
.
Let us denote with

the set of elements
\tilde{a}
of
\tilde{A}
such that case (2) applies.
We define

by removing from
\tilde{A}
all elements

of
\tilde{A}_{d}
and replacing them by the elements of

(thus expressing the atomisation of

\overline{A}:=(\tilde{A}\backslash\tilde{A}_{d})+\cup_{\tilde{a}\in\tilde{A}_{d}}H_{\tilde{a}}
.
The whole situation determines a partition
\overline{A}=\overline{A}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\overline{A}_{n_{B}}
, and

is defined on the new elements

.
We still use the notation

\tau,\bigcup_{b\in B}X_{b}\;\uline{\leadsto}\;\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n_{B}}
.
The definition of clan is unchanged, except that a clan now consists of semi-strict teams and not of strict teams.
The definition of the product is adapted as follows. We assign a construct

of
{\bf H}_{\overline{a}}
for all

(1\leq i\leq n_{B})

), but setting

(see below).

We can still define a function

to

\delta^{B}_{i}=(\{C_{\tilde{a}}:{\bf H}_{\tilde{a}}\,|\,\tilde{a}\in\tilde{A}\mbox{ and }\varphi_{\tau}^{B}(\tilde{a})=i\},{\bf H}_{i}^{B}),

noticing that the participating hypergraphs of

that are not the hypergraphs

Note however that our abuse of notation is not as innocent as it seems, since the convention relies on the fact that a singleton hypergraph

Lemma 4.25

5.
Open questions

In this section, we list some directions for future work. We already mentioned the task of finding a nice combinatorial interpretation of the constructs of friezohedra. Here are some other questions we would like to address.

\bullet
The tridendriform algebras in our examples often satisfy more equations than the tridendrifom ones. Can we make a landscape of the corresponding operad structures?

\bullet

Hopf algebra structures are known for associahedra and permutohedra, see
[
2
,
11
]
. Can we find sufficient conditions for such structures to exist on a family of polytopes?

\bullet
We would like to explore the “flip” order obtained by exchanging the order of elements when seeing constructs as posets, aiming at extending results from
[
15
]
.

\bullet
We also seek comparison results, in the spirit of
[
11
]
: given two hypergraphs, one included in the other, what are the relations between the associated polytopes and between the associated algebras?

Glossary

Below, we sum up the vocabulary, accompanying each term with an example (in green) coming from the simplices.

A
preteam
(like
(\{{\bf S}^{1,3},{\bf S}^{2,4}\},{\bf S}^{1,2,3,4})
) is the ground for a product. The elements multiplied in the product are constructs of some ”participating” hypergraphs and the result is a sum of constructs of the ”coordinating hypergraph”. A preteam is a pair made of:

•

a set of coordinating hypergraphs,

•

a participating hypergraph.

A
strict team
(like
(\{{\bf S}^{1,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}3}},{\bf S}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2,4}}\},{\bf S}^{1,2,3,4})
) is a preteam satisfying some strictness properties (the coordinating hypergraph is more connected than the coordinating hypergraphs in the sense that removing some vertices in some participating hypergraph disconnects it if the same action disconnects the coordinating hypergraph).

A
strict clan

is a set of strict teams satisfying a closure property. It encompasses all the coordinating and participating hypergraphs considered, in any products.

Forming a
delegation
consists in picking a strict team and in choosing a construct for every participating hypergraph. These constructs are the objects multiplied in the product.

Coherent-Constructible Correspondence for Toric Fibrations
Yuxuan Hu, Pyongwon Suh

Abstract
. Let Σ \Sigma be a fan inside the lattice ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} , and ℰ : ℤ n → Pic ⁡ S \mathcal{E}:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}{S} be a map of abelian groups. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Mirror symmetry and coherent-constructible correspondence

Homological Mirror Symmetry (HMS) is an equivalence between two categories of seemingly different geometric origins. One of these categories is the derived category of coherent sheaves on a complex variety
X
. The other is the Fukaya-type category of another seemingly unrelated variety
\check{X}
or a potential function
W:Y\to\mathbb{C}
, whose objects are roughly Lagrangian submanifolds and whose morphisms are their intersections. The composition of morphisms in the latter category is defined by counting pseudo-holomorphic curves.

On the other hand, it has been known that the Fukaya category of an exact symplectic manifold is sheaf theoretic in nature. For example, the Nadler-Zaslow theorem
[
NZ09
,
Nad09
]
states that there is an equivalence between the infinitesimal Fukaya category of the cotangent bundle of a real analytic manifold
Z
and the category of constructible sheaves on
Z
. Kontsevich
[
Kon09
]
conjectured that for a Weinstein manifold, there is a (co)sheaf of categories on its Lagrangian skeleton whose global section is equivalent to the Fukaya category of the Weinstein manifold. Nadler’s work proposed that this (co)sheaf of categories should be modeled by the category of microlocal sheaves or a wrapped version of them
[
Nad16
]
. This is now a theorem for many cases due to the work of Ganatra-Pardon-Shende
[
GPS20a
,
GPS19
,
GPS20b
]
.

The Coherent-Constructible Correspondence (CCC) is a version of HMS for toric varieties which intertwines with the sheaf theoretic description of the Fukaya category. Originally conjectured by Bondal
[
Bon06
]
, the equivariant version was proved by Fang-Liu-Treumann-Zaslow
[
FLTZ11
]
. Some special cases of the non-equivariant version were proved in
[
Tre10
,
SS16
,
Kuw17
]
, and now it is a theorem which can be proved for toric stacks
[
Kuw20
,
Zho19
]
, which we describe as follows.

1.2.
CCC in the relative case

In the microlocal sheaf theory of Kashiwara-Schapira
[
KS90
]
, there is a sheaf of categories
\mu sh_{\Lambda}
on
T^{*}M
called the Kashiwara-Schapira stack whose category of global sections recovers the category of constructible sheaves
[
STW16
, Proposition 3.5]
:

\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)\simeq\Gamma(\Lambda,\operatorname{\mu Sh}_{\Lambda}).

By taking ind-completion, the coherent-constructible correspondence can be stated as

\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(X_{\Sigma})\simeq\Gamma(\Lambda_{\Sigma},\operatorname{\mu Sh}_{\Lambda_{\Sigma}}).

If we consider the product of the toric variety with
another variety
S
, by the Künneth formula, we have

\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(X_{\Sigma}\times S)\simeq\Gamma(\Lambda_{\Sigma},\operatorname{\mu Sh}_{\Lambda_{\Sigma}})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(S)\simeq\Gamma(\Lambda_{\Sigma},\operatorname{\mu Sh}_{\Lambda_{\Sigma}}\otimes_{k}\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(S)}),

where
\underline{\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(S)}

sheaf of categories with stalk
\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(S)

Here the tensor products are tensor products of (sheaves of)
k
-linear
stable
\infty
-categories, see
2.1.3
.

The topic of this paper is to generalize this equivalence from the case of a Cartesian product
X_{\Sigma}\times S
to that of a
principal toric fibration
(see

S

Given any fan datum
\Sigma
and sequence of line bundles

associated to the fan
\Sigma

The line bundles

give rise to a local system of categories

In the setting of principal toric fibrations, we will show that the equivalence continues to hold provided the constant sheaf is replaced with the local system of categories

\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(S)

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem

3.
Principal toric fibrations

3.1.
Stacky principal toric fibrations

In this paper, we consider a special case of fibrations
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow Y
with toric fibers,
which we call
stacky principal toric fibrations
.
A stacky principal toric fibration is a relative version of a toric stack

[
BCS05
,
Iwa09
,
FMN10
,
Tyo12
]

(more precisely, a toric stack in the sense of
[
Tyo12
]
.)

We recall the notion of a stacky fan
used to define a toric stack.

Definition 3.1
.

A
stacky fan

[
BCS05
]
denoted by
(\hat{\Sigma},\beta)
is the following data

•

a map
\beta\colon L\rightarrow N
between finite rank free abelian groups with finite cokernel,

•

a fan
\Sigma
inside
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
consisting of rational strictly convex cones,

•

a fan
\hat{\Sigma}
inside
L_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}

consisting of rational strictly convex cones.

We assume, in addition, as in
[
Kuw20
]
,
that

Assumption 3.2
.

\beta_{\mathbb{R}}
induces an isomorphism between two fans
\hat{\Sigma}
and
\Sigma
.

Now
\beta
induces a surjective morphism between (algebraic) tori

(3.1)

\beta_{k^{\times}}:L_{k^{\times}}\rightarrow N_{k^{\times}}.

We set
G_{\beta}:=\ker{\beta_{k^{\times}}}
.
The information of a stacky fan
allows one to define a toric stack

X_{\Sigma,\beta}=\left[\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{\hat{\sigma}\in\hat{\Sigma}}k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap L^{\vee}]/G_{\beta}\right],

where
\hat{\sigma}^{\vee}:=\{v\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}:v(x)\geq 0,\forall x\in\sigma\}
is the dual cone.

In order to generalize this construction
to the relative case, one needs to specify
how the fibers vary over the the base.
The case we want to consider is when
the monodromy can be encoded
by the following data.

Definition 3.3
.

An
Ikari

7
7

7

Meaning “anchor(s)”.
is a morphism of abelian groups
\mathcal{E}:L^{\vee}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
.

Remark 3.4
.

An Ikari is non-canonically equivalent to the choice of
n=\operatorname{rank}L
line bundles (order considered) on
Y
.

Given any sub-monoid
S
of
L^{\vee}
, one has an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-algebra

(3.2)

\mathcal{E}[S]:=\oplus_{s\in S}\mathcal{E}(s),

where the algebra structure is given by the obvious morphism
\mathcal{E}(s_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{E}(s_{2})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{E}(s_{1}+s_{2})
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
S=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\subset L^{\vee}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{n})
. Then
\mathcal{E}[S]\simeq\mathcal{S}ym_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n})
, and
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}pec}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\mathcal{E}[S])=E(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\vee}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\vee})
, the total space of
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\vee}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\vee}
.

We then define

(3.3)

X_{\hat{\Sigma},\mathcal{E}}:=\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{\hat{\sigma}\in\hat{\Sigma}}\operatorname{\mathcal{S}pec}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{E}[\hat{\sigma}^{\vee}\cap L^{\vee}].

Note that the algebra
\mathcal{E}[S]
carries a canonical
G_{\beta}
-action described as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

G_{\beta}
is canonically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}k[\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})]
.

Proof.

Note that

\beta_{k^{\times}}:L_{k^{\times}}=\operatorname{Spec}k[L^{\vee}]\rightarrow N_{k^{\times}}=\operatorname{Spec}k[N^{\vee}].

\ker\beta_{k^{\times}}
is then given by the fiber at identity, which is

\operatorname{Spec}k[L^{\vee}/N^{\vee}]=\operatorname{Spec}k[\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})].

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

\mathcal{E}[S]
admits a canonical
G_{\beta}
-action given by

\mathcal{E}[S]\rightarrow\mathcal{E}[S]\otimes k[\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})],

where the map on the second factor is induced by
S\hookrightarrow L^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})
.

Remark 3.8
.

Alternatively, one can view
\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})
as the character lattice of
G_{\beta}
.
Then the weight of the
G_{\beta}
-action
is given by the projection
S\rightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\beta^{\vee})
.

Definition 3.9
.

The
stacky principal toric fibration
associated to the stacky fan
(\hat{\Sigma},\beta)

and the Ikari
\mathcal{E}
is the quotient stack

(3.4)

\mathcal{X}_{\hat{\Sigma},\beta,\mathcal{E}}:=\left[X_{\hat{\Sigma},\mathcal{E}}/G_{\beta}\right]=\left[\operatornamewithlimits{colim}_{\hat{\sigma}\in\hat{\Sigma}}\operatorname{\mathcal{S}pec}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{E}[\hat{\sigma}^{\vee}\cap L^{\vee}]/G_{\beta}\right].

3.2.
The coefficient system
\Phi(\mathcal{E})

Associated to an Ikari
\mathcal{E}
,
we introduce a local system
\Phi(\mathcal{E})
of (
k
-linear stable
\infty
-)categories on the topological torus
M_{T}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
. Recall the following fact.

Lemma 3.10
.

For any (topological) torus
T
, there is a canonical equivalence
T\simeq B\pi_{1}(T)
.
∎

Applying this equivalence to
M_{T}
, we get
M_{T}\simeq B\pi_{1}(M_{T})\simeq BM
. Recall that we have maps

M\xrightarrow{\beta^{\vee}}L^{\vee}\xrightarrow{-\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Pic}(Y),

inducing maps on classifying spaces

BM\rightarrow BL^{\vee}\rightarrow B\operatorname{Pic}(Y).

On the other hand, we consider the action of Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
on the category of sheaves. Consider the groupoid
B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
and the following assignment

\ast\mapsto\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(Y),

\ast\xrightarrow{\alpha}\ast\mapsto\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(Y)\xrightarrow{\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(Y),

where
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is the line bundle in
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
corresponding to the morphism
\alpha
in
B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
.

Proposition 3.11
.

This assignment underlies a functor

B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}at_{k}.

Proof.

In the same way, we have a functor of 1-categories

B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\rightarrow dgCat_{k},

which sends the unique object to the dg category of coherent sheaves
\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
.
Note that by restricting to the full-subcategory whose objects consist of exactly one for each isomorphism class, one can make everything strictly commutative and thus avoid any 2-categorical issues.
This functor is therefore well-defined.
Now the composite

B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\rightarrow N({dgCat_{k}})\rightarrow N(dgCat_{k})[W^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal{C}at_{k}

is the desired functor.
∎

Definition 3.12
.

The coefficient system
\Phi(\mathcal{E})
associated to
\mathcal{E}

is given by the composite functor

M_{T}\simeq BM\rightarrow BL^{\vee}\xrightarrow{B(-\mathcal{E})}B\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}at_{k}.

Remark 3.13
.

Informally, this is the local system given by the representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M_{T})\simeq M\xrightarrow{\beta^{\vee}}L^{\vee}\xrightarrow{-\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\xrightarrow{\otimes}\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}(Y))
.

3.3.
Main theorem

With these technical ingredients in place,
we may now state our main theorem.

Theorem 3.14
.

There is an equivalence of
k
-linear stable
\infty
-categories,

\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Coh}\mathcal{X}_{\hat{\Sigma},\beta,\mathcal{E}}\simeq\Gamma\left(\Lambda_{\hat{\Sigma},\beta},\operatorname{\mu Sh}_{\Lambda_{\hat{\Sigma}}}^{\diamond}\otimes_{k}\Phi(\mathcal{E})\right).

Since the sheaf is supported on

\Lambda_{\hat{\Sigma},\beta}
, over
T^{*}M_{T}

6.
Descent

In this section, we are going to prove
our main theorem in the general case using

Such a proof goes as follows: locally a

next, we will show that we can further

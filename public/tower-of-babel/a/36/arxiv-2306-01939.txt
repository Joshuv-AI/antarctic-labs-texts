Uncertainty principle for the Poly-axially L α 2 L_{\alpha}^{2} -multiplier operators
Belgacem Selmi , Rahma Chbeb

Abstract
In this article, the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle and Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle are obtained for the Poly-axially L 2 L^{2} multiplier operators.



Key words:
Poly-axially operator,
L^{2}
-multiplier
operators, Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle, Donoho-
Stark’s uncertainty principle.

1
Introduction

Uncertainty principles are an essential restriction in
Fourier analysis. They state that a function
f
and its Fourier
transform
\widehat{f}
cannot be at the same time simultaneously
and sharply localized. That is, it is impossible for a non-zero
function
and its Fourier transform to be simultaneously small. Many mathematical formulations of this general fact can be
found in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

The classical uncertainty principle was established by Heisenberg
[
4
]

bringing a fundamental problem in quantum mechanics. The
mathematical formulation was established by Kennard
[
5
]
and
Weyl (who attributes results to Pauli)
[
6
]
in the late
1920’s. This leads to this quantitative formulation in the form of a
lower bound of the product of the dispersions of a nonzero function

f
and its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
:

\Big\|\|x\|\,f\Big\|_{L^{2}(I\!\!R^{n})}\Big\|\|y\|\,\widehat{f}\Big\|_{L^{2}(I\!\!R^{n})}\geq\,\frac{n}{2}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(I\!\!R^{n})}^{2},

Where

\widehat{f}(\lambda)=(2\pi)^{\frac{-n}{2}}\int_{I\!\!R^{n}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\lambda\rangle}dx;\,f\in L^{1}(I\!\!R^{n})\cap L^{2}(I\!\!R^{n}).

The Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl inequality
for the Fourier-Bessel transform (one dimensional case) (see
[
7
,
8
]
) is given by: for every
f\in L_{\nu}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+})=L^{2}(I\!\!R_{+},x^{2\nu+1}dx/2^{\nu}(\Gamma(\nu+1)))
(
\nu>\frac{-1}{2}
)

\Big\|\|x\|\,f\Big\|_{L_{\nu}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+})}\Big\|\|y\|\,{\cal{F}}(f)\Big\|_{L_{\nu}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+})}\geq\,(\nu+1)\,\|f\|_{L_{\nu}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+})}^{2},

where
{\cal{F}}(f)
is the Fourier-Bessel defined by

{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)j_{\nu}(\xi x)\frac{x^{2\nu+1}}{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}dx;\,\xi>0.

Here,
j_{\nu}
is the
normalized Bessel function of the first kind and order
\nu>\frac{-1}{2}
which is given by

j_{\nu}(x)=\Gamma(\nu+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}(\frac{x}{2})^{2k}.

(1)

Recently, the analogue of the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl inequality is established for the Poly-axially operator (see
[
9
]
) and it is given by:
for all
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})

\|f\|_{\alpha,2}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big\|\|x\|\,f\Big\|_{\alpha,2}\Big\|\|y\|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)\Big\|_{\alpha,2}.

(2)

In the present paper, we consider the Poly-axially operator (n-dimensional
Bessel operator)
\Delta_{\alpha}
(see
[
10
,
13
]
) defined for

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})\in I\!\!R^{n}
such that

\alpha_{i}>-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
for
i=1,...,n
by

\Delta_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha_{i}+1}{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

(3)

One can remark that:

\bullet
If
\alpha_{i}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
for

i=1,...,n
, then
\Delta_{\alpha}
coincides with the Laplace
operator
\Delta_{n}
on
I\!\!R^{n}

\Delta_{\alpha}=\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}.

\bullet
If
\alpha_{i}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
for
i=1,...,n-1
and

\alpha_{n}>-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
, then
\Delta_{\alpha}
coincides with
the Weinstein operator defined on
I\!\!R^{n}
(see

[
11
]
,
[
12
]
)

\Delta_{W}=\Delta_{n-1}+L_{\alpha_{n}},

where
L_{\alpha_{n}}
is the Bessel operator for the last variable
defined by

L_{\alpha_{n}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha_{n}+1}{x_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}.

Now, let m be a function in
L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
and let

\sigma
be a positive real number. The Poly-axially
L^{2}
-Multiplier
operators
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}
is defined (see
[
13
]
) for smooth functions

\varphi
on
I\!\!R_{+}^{n}
, by

T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}\varphi(x)={\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{-1}\Big(m_{\sigma}{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\Big)(x),\;x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n},

where the function
m_{\sigma}
is given by

m_{\sigma}(x)=m(\sigma x),\;x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n},

and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
is the n-dimensional Bessel Fourier transform
defined for suitable function
f
by

{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)(\lambda)=\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}f(x)\prod_{i=1}^{n}j_{\alpha_{i}}(\lambda_{i}x_{i})d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

where
d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=c_{\alpha}\,\displaystyle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}}x_{i}^{2\alpha_{i}+1}dx_{i},

c_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{|\alpha|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}
, (
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{n}
) and
j_{\alpha_{i}}
is the
normalized Bessel function of the first kind and order
\alpha_{i}>\frac{-1}{2}
given by
(\ref{bfunction})
.

As well

T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}\varphi(x)={\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{-1}(m_{\sigma})*_{\alpha}\varphi(x),\;x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n},

where

{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{-1}(m_{\sigma})(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2|\alpha|+n}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}^{-1}(m)(\frac{x}{\sigma}).

The Poly-axially
L^{2}
-Multiplier
operators
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}
are a generalization of the usual
linear multiplier operator
T_{m}

where
m
is a bounded function and
{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi)
denotes the ordinary transform on
I\!\!R^{n}
.

Our aim is to establish an analogue of the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl
uncertainty principle for these operators
. That is, we are going to show for
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
that

\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\leq\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big\|\|y\|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)\Big\|_{\alpha,2}\Big\|\|x\|\,T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\Big\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2};\,x,y\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n},

provided
m\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{\infty}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})

satisfying the admissibility condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}|m_{\sigma}(x)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}=1\,;\;x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n}.

Then, based on the techniques of Donoho-Stark
[
14
]
, we show
uncertainty principle of concentration type for the Poly-axially
L^{2}
-Multiplier
operators
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}
.

Let
E
be a measurable subset of
I\!\!R^{n}
,
S
be a measurable subset of
(0,\infty)\times I\!\!R^{n}
and

f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
. If
f
is

\varepsilon
-concentrated on E and
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f
is

\delta
-concentrated on S, then

\|m\|_{\alpha,1}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 1-(\varepsilon+\delta),

provided
m\in L_{\alpha}^{1}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})

satisfying the precedent admissibility condition

The contents of this work is the following. In section 2, we
recall some basic harmonic analysis results related to the Poly-axially
operator
\Delta_{\alpha}
on
I\!\!R^{n}
. In section 3 and section 4,
we establish respectively the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty
principle and the Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle for the
Poly-axially
L_{\alpha}^{2}
-multiplier operators
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}
.

3
Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle

This section is devoted to establish
Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle for the Poly-axially
L^{2}
-
multiplier operators
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}
.

Theorem 3.1

Let m be a function in
L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{\infty}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
satisfying the following
admissibility condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}|m_{\sigma}(x)|^{2}\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}=1\,;\;x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n}.

(12)

Then, for
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
, we have

\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\leq\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big\|\|y\|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)\Big\|_{\alpha,2}\Big\|\|x\|\,T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\Big\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}.

(13)

Proof:

Let
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
. Assume that

\Big\|\|y\|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)\Big\|_{\alpha,2}<\infty
and
\Big\|\|x\|\,T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\Big\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}<\infty.

According to inequality
(
\ref{hpw})
and relation (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|x\|^{2}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|y\|^{2}|m(\sigma y){\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)(y))|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Integrating over
[0,\infty[
with respect to the measure

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|x\|^{2}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|y\|^{2}|m(\sigma y){\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)(y))|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}.

Using (
[
13
]
Theorem 2.4 (i)) and Schwarz’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|x\|^{2}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|y\|^{2}|m(\sigma y){\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)(y))|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, by Fubini-Tonneli’s theorem and the admissibility condition
(
12
), we get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2|\alpha|+n}\,\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|x\|^{2}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{I\!\!R_{+}^{n}}\|y\|^{2}|{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}(f)(y))|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

4
Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle

Definition 4.1

(see
[
17
,
18
]
)

(i)

Let E be a measurable subset of
I\!\!R_{+}^{n}
, we say that the function
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
is

\varepsilon
-concentrated on E if

\|f-\chi_{E}f\|_{\alpha,2}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{\alpha,2},

(17)

where
\chi_{E}
is the indicator function of the set E.

(ii)

Let S be a measurable subset of
(0,\infty)\times I\!\!R_{+}^{n}
and let
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
. We say that
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f
is a

\delta
-concentrated on S if

\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f-\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}\leq\delta\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}.

(18)

Where
\chi_{S}
is the indicator function of the set S.

Theorem 4.2

Let
f
be a function in
L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
and
m\in L_{\alpha}^{1}\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
satisfying the
admissibility condition (
12
). If
f
is

\varepsilon
-concentrated on E and
T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f
is

\delta
-concentrated on S, then

\|m\|_{\alpha,1}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 1-(\varepsilon+\delta).

(19)

Proof:

Let
f\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
and
m\in L_{\alpha}^{1}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})

satisfying (
12
). Assume that
\mu_{\alpha}(E)<\infty
and

\displaystyle\int\displaystyle\int_{S}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)<\infty.

According to relations (
17
) and (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f-\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma,\alpha,m}f-\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}+\|\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(f-\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}+\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(f-\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}.

And by (
[
13
]
Theorem 2.4 (i)), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f-\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\|f\|_{\alpha,2}+\|f-\chi_{E}f\|_{\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\delta+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\alpha,2}.

Then, using the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}+\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f-\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}+(\delta+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\alpha,2}.

Next, since
m
,
\chi_{E}f\in L_{\alpha}^{1}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})\bigcap L_{\alpha}^{2}(I\!\!R_{+}^{n})
, thus, using Lemma
2.11
, we have

\displaystyle|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2|\alpha|+n}}\|m\|_{\alpha,1}\,\|\chi_{E}f\|_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2|\alpha|+n}}\|m\|_{\alpha,1}\,\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\,(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, by the fact that

\|\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}=\Big(\displaystyle\int\displaystyle\int_{S}|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)(x)|^{2}d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we get

\|\chi_{S}T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}(\chi_{E}f)\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}\leq\|m\|_{\alpha,1}\,\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\,(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\displaystyle\frac{d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore

\|T_{\alpha,m,\sigma}f\|_{\sigma,\alpha,2}\leq\|m\|_{\alpha,1}\|f\|_{\alpha,2}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\displaystyle\frac{d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\delta+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\alpha,2}.

By (
[
13
]
,theorem 2.4 (i)), it follows

\|f\|_{\alpha,2}\leq\|m\|_{\alpha,1}\|f\|_{\alpha,2}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\displaystyle\frac{d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\delta+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\alpha,2}.

Then

\|m\|_{\alpha,1}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\int_{S}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2(2|\alpha|+n)}}d\Omega_{\alpha}(\sigma,x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 1-(\varepsilon+\delta),

which is the desired result.

Remark 4.3

If

S\subset\{(\sigma,x)\in(0,\infty)\times I\!\!R_{+}^{n}:\,\sigma\geq\xi\}

for some
\xi>0
, one suppose that

\varrho=\max\{\frac{1}{\sigma}:\,(\sigma,x)\in S\,for\,some\,x\in I\!\!R_{+}^{n}\}.

Then by the previous theorem, we conclude that

\varrho^{2|\alpha|+n}\|m\|_{\alpha,1}(\mu_{\alpha}(E))^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega_{\alpha}(S))^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 1-(\varepsilon+\delta).

On correlators in T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed conformal field theories
D. Menskoy

Abstract
This paper is our contribution to the study of T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformations. We consider the effect of T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformation of conformal field theories in perturbation theory. We use dimensional regularization scheme to perturbatively calculate the effect of the T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformation on the correlation functions of the CFT’s primary fields. We provide the renormalization of the fields for the case of two-point correlation function.

2
T\bar{T}
-deformation and critical phenomena

Let us consider a two-dimensional statistical system in the vicinity of the critical temperature
T_{c}
.
T\bar{T}
-deformation describes the nature of subleading singularities in statistical physics. The free energy of such a system behaves as

\displaystyle F=F_{0}+a(T-T_{c})^{2\nu}+a^{\prime}(T-T_{c})^{\Xi}+...\>,

(4)

where
\nu
and
\Xi
are some exponents, connected with relevant and least irrelevant operators in effective action,
F_{0}
is a function regular for
T\to T_{c}
, and ”…” denotes less singular contributions. In this case, the behavior of the correlation radius
R_{c}=M^{-1}
is as follows:

\displaystyle M=b(T-T_{c})^{\nu}+b^{\prime}(T-T_{c})^{\eta}+...

(5)

The nature of singularities can be understood in terms of the Wilson renormalization group. The behavior directly at the critical point is determined by the fixed point of the renormalization group (conformal field theory). In the neighborhood of a fixed point, the system is described by the formal action

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{CFT}+\mu\int d^{2}x\>\Phi_{\Delta}(x)+\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\int d^{2}x\>O_{i}(x),

(6)

where
\mathcal{A}_{CFT}
is the action of conformal field theory,
2\Delta<2
and, therefore, the perturbation
\mu\int d^{2}x\>\Phi_{\Delta}(x)
is relevant, the remaining terms are irrelevant contributions, with the dimensions of the corresponding operators
d_{i}>2
. For simplicity, the case of one relevant perturbation is considered. It is also assumed that there are no marginal operators.

It is important that the coupling constants are regular functions of the temperature. Also, since
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a relevant perturbation, then
\mu
should vanish at
T=T_{c}
:

\displaystyle\mu(T)

\displaystyle=\mu^{\prime}(T-T_{c})+\mu^{\prime\prime}(T-T_{c})^{2}+...

(7)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{(0)}+\alpha_{i}^{\prime}(T-T_{c})+...

(8)

That means that there is a “canonical curve” in the space of quasilocal actions
1
1

1

By quasilocal action we mean such actions

\mathcal{A}=\int d^{2}x\>\mathcal{L}(\Phi(x),\partial_{\mu}\Phi(x),\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\Phi(x),...),

(9)

that the Lagrangian density is a convergent functional for all fields, whose Fourier expansion consists of modes with
|k|<\Lambda
only with
\Lambda
being a UV-cutoff.
that describes the dependence of the microscopic theory on the temperature. For
T=T_{c}
, the renormalization group flow drives the thoery from a point on this curve strictly to the fixed point. For
T-T_{c}\ll T_{c}
the renormalization group flow first flows similarly towards a fixed point, then stays for long ’RG time’ in the vicinity of a fixed point and then flows away towards some other fixed point. All critical singularities are formed in the vicinity of a fixed point, and irrelevant contributions die out there. Renormalization group equations at this stage

\displaystyle\derivative{\mu}{l}=(2-2\Delta)\mu,\>\derivative{\alpha_i}{l}=(2-d_{i})\alpha_{i}.

(10)

And since the correlation length behaves as
R_{c}(l)\sim e^{-l}
with

\displaystyle R_{c}\sim\mu^{-\nu}\sim\absolutevalue{T - T_c}^{-\nu},\>\nu=\frac{1}{2-2\Delta}.

(11)

Amplitudes

[

]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{CFT}+\mu\int d^{2}x\>\Phi_{\Delta}(x).

(12)

This phenomenon is known as the universality of the leading critical singularity.

The following subleading singularities reflect the influence of the least irrelevant operator, that is, one such that

\displaystyle d_{1}<d_{i},\>i\geq 2.

(13)

In many systems (for example in Ising model), such an operator, respecting all symmetries of the system, is
T\bar{T}

\displaystyle\Xi=d_{1}\nu,\>\eta=(d_{1}-1)\nu.

(14)

In the case of

\displaystyle d_{T\bar{T}}=4,\>\Xi=4\nu,\>\eta=3\nu.

(15)

For amplitudes

\frac{a}{b}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}.

(16)

We can derive that as follows. From the equation (

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}=\frac{F(0)}{1+\alpha F(0)},\>F(0)=F_{\alpha=0}=a\mu^{2\nu},

Then to first order in

(19)

\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{j}}{(z-x_{j})^{2}}+\frac{1}{z-x_{j}}\partialderivative{x_j}\right)\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}_{k}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{x}_{k})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}_{k}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}_k}\right)\expectationvalue{\Phi_1(x_1)...\Phi_N(x_N)}_{0},

3.1.1
Renormalization

Now we can perform the renormalization. Namely: let’s combine fields in such linear combinations

\Phi^{R}_{i}(x_{i})=\Phi_{i}(x_{i})+\alpha A(\epsilon)\partialderivative{x_i}\partialderivative{\bar{x}_i}\Phi_{i}(x_{i})

\expectationvalue{\Phi_1^R(x_1)...\Phi_N^R(x_N)}_{def}=\expectationvalue{\Phi_1^R(x_1)...\Phi_N^R(x_N)}_{0}-\alpha\int d^{2}z\expectationvalue{T\Bar{T}(z) \Phi_1^R(x_1)... \Phi_N^R(x_N)}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})

In order for the correlation function for

. Then in first order we get the answer

\expectationvalue{\Phi_1^R(x_1)...\Phi_N^R(x_N)}_{def}=\biggl(1+\alpha K\biggr)\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)...\Phi(x_N)}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}),

(37)

where

K=\sum_{j,k\neq j}^{N}\biggl[-\frac{\pi}{\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{k}}\bar{\Delta}_{k}\partial_{j}+\frac{\pi}{x_{j}-x_{k}}\Delta_{j}\bar{\partial}_{k}-2\pi\log{|x_j- x_k|\partial_j \bar{\partial}_k}\biggr].

(38)

)

\begin{split}\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\epsilon}\sum_{i,k,j\neq i,l\neq k}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\bar{\partial}_{j}\partial_{l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\expectationvalue{X}-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\epsilon}\sum_{i,k,j\neq i,l\neq k}\frac{\bar{\Delta}_{i}}{\bar{x}_{i}-\bar{x}_{j}}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\expectationvalue{X}-\\
-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\epsilon}\sum_{i,j\neq i}\sum_{l,k\neq l}\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}_{k}}{\bar{x}_{l}-\bar{x}_{k}}\partial_{l}-\frac{\Delta_{l}}{x_{l}-x_{k}}\bar{\partial}_{k}-4\log(m|x_l - x_k|)\partial_{l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\right)\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\expectationvalue{X}\\
+\left(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}\sum_{i;j\neq i;l\neq i,k;k\neq j}^{N}\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\partial_{l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\expectationvalue{X}\end{split}

(48)

There can also be divergences from the unfactorized part:

\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\>\biggl[\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}_{i}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{x}_{i})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}_{i}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}_i}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{i}}{(z-x_{i})^{2}}+\frac{1}{z-x_{i}}\partialderivative{x_i}\right)\frac{1}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2}}\biggr]\\
\times\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{l}}{(w-x_{l})^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-x_{l}}\partialderivative{x_l}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}_{k}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x}_{k})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}_{k}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}_k}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(49)

The integrals that arise here, which we need to calculate, are as follows:

I_{ac}^{bd}(x,y,t)=\int d^{2}z\>d^{2}w\frac{1}{(z-w)^{a}}\frac{1}{(\bar{z}-\bar{x})^{b}}\frac{1}{(w-y)^{c}}\frac{1}{(\bar{w}-\bar{t})^{d}},

(50)

where
a,\>b,\>c,\>d
take values 1 and 2, and
a
and
b
cannot both be equal to 1 at the same time. One can see that the case when
y=x
or
t=x
does not lead to new integrals (they can be calculated by the first-order methods). One can note that for a two-point function (
N=2
), integrals with all different points
x,\>y
and
t
do not arise. We leave the analysis of the divergent part of the integral (
50
) for a future publication. Here we consider only the case of two-point function.

3.3
Second order correction for the case of a two-point function

Let us introduce the notation:

\partial=\partial_{x}=\partialderivative{x},\>\partial_{y}=\partialderivative{y},\>\Delta=\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{2},\>\bar{\Delta}=\bar{\Delta}_{1}=\bar{\Delta}_{2}.

(51)

Also in the case of two-point function we have

\partial_{x}\expectationvalue{\Phi(x) \Phi(y)}_{0}+\partial_{y}\expectationvalue{\Phi(x) \Phi(y)}=0.

(52)

Let us write down the divergences in the case of a two-point function. Second order pole:

\left(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}\bar{\partial}_{y}^{2}+2\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\bar{\partial}_{x}\bar{\partial}_{y}+\partial_{y}^{2}\bar{\partial}_{x}^{2})\expectationvalue{X}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}(\partial_{x}\bar{\partial}_{y}+\partial_{y}\bar{\partial}_{x})^{2}\expectationvalue{X}=4\left(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}\expectationvalue{X}

(53)

We write down here all the poles in the factorized part of the BB-term

4\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\log(m|x-y|)\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}\expectationvalue{X}+4\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{\bar{y}-\bar{x}}\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}\expectationvalue{X}+4\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\Delta}{y-x}\partial\bar{\partial}^{2}\expectationvalue{X}-\\
-2\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\Delta}{(y-x)^{2}}\bar{\partial}^{2}-2\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{y}-\bar{x})^{2}}\partial+4\left(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}\expectationvalue{X}.

(54)

Let us consider the unfactorized part of the BB-term. Essentially we need to consider the following integral

\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{x})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{y})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)\\
\times\left(\frac{\Delta}{(w-x)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(w-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-x}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{w-y}\partialderivative{y}\right)\\
\times\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{y})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)\expectationvalue{X}.

(55)

We introduce the notations of differential operators that constitutes the required integral.

\displaystyle D(w|x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta}{(w-x)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(w-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-x}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{w-y}\partialderivative{y}

(56)

\displaystyle\bar{D}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{y})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}

(57)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{3}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}+1}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{2}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative[2]{\bar{x}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{y})^{2}}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{x}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}

(58)

Then the integral can be divided into three parts and take the form:

I_{1}+I_{x}+I_{y},

(59)

where we introduced the notation

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{x})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{z}-\bar{y})^{2}}\right)D(w|x,y)\bar{D}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})\expectationvalue{X},

(60)

\displaystyle I_{x}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}}D(w|x,y)\bar{D}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})\expectationvalue{X},

(61)

\displaystyle I_{y}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}}D(w|x,y)\bar{D}_{\bar{y}}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})\expectationvalue{X}.

(62)

The main idea is to take integrals directly in
d=2
, and if they lead to divergences, substitute the integrals by their expansion at
d\to 2
. Acting according to this scheme, we conclude that
I_{1}
can lead only to terms finite in
d\to 2
limit. That can be easily seen as far as integral over
z
is of the form
I_{2}^{2}
, which behaves like
\mathcal{O}(d-2)
, while the integral over
w
cannot produce divergences higher than
\frac{1}{d-2}
.

Let us move further and calculate the
I_{x}
integral

\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{x}}D(w|x,y)\bar{D}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})\expectationvalue{X}\sim\\
\sim\int d^{2}w\>\frac{1}{w-x}\left(\frac{\Delta}{(w-x)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(w-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-x}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{w-y}\partialderivative{y}\right)\\
\times\left(\frac{2\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{3}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}+1}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{2}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative[2]{\bar{x}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{y})^{2}}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{x}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)

(63)

When integrating over
w
, the divergences give only the terms with different external points of the form

\frac{1}{(w-x)(\bar{w}-\bar{y})},

(64)

and one can note that both
w
and
\bar{w}
should have exactly degree
-1
. Thus, of the terms
D(w|x,y)
, only the 2nd and 4th ones can potentially give divergences. Further, from
\bar{D}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{w}|\bar{x},\bar{y})
only the 3rd and 5th terms can give divergences. Leaving only potentially divergent parts, we write

-\pi\int d^{2}w\>\frac{1}{w-x}\left(\frac{\Delta}{(w-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-y}\partialderivative{y}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative[2]{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{x}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(65)

To integrate one needs to decompose the holomorphic part into simple terms using the formulas

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(w-x)(w-y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x-y)(w-x)}-\frac{1}{(x-y)(w-y)}

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(w-x)(w-y)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2}(w-x)}-\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2}(w-y)}-\frac{1}{(x-y)(w-y)^{2}}

(67)

and leave only the terms of degrees
-1
both in
w
and in
\bar{w}
, but with different points. We have

-\pi\int d^{2}w\>\biggl(\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}(w-x)}-\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}(w-y)}-\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)(w-y)^{2}}+\\
+\frac{1}{(x-y)(w-x)}\partialderivative{y}-\frac{1}{(x-y)(w-y)}\partialderivative{y}\biggr)\\
\times\left(\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative[2]{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{x}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(68)

One can obtain the result

\left(-\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}^2}+\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon(x-y)}\partialderivative{\bar{x}^2}\partialderivative{x}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(69)

Completely analogous procedure for
I_{y}

leads to an answer

\left(-\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}^2}-\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon(x-y)}\partialderivative{\bar{x}^2}\partialderivative{x}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(70)

The total divergent contributions of
I_{x}
and
I_{y}
are equal

-2\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}^2}\expectationvalue{X}

(71)

A similar contribution will be complex conjugate to this one. It comes from the term in the original integral

\int d^{2}w\>d^{2}z\left(\frac{\Delta}{(z-x)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(z-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{z-x}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{z-y}\partialderivative{y}\right)\frac{1}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2}}\\
\times\left(\frac{\Delta}{(w-x)^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{(w-y)^{2}}+\frac{1}{w-x}\partialderivative{x}+\frac{1}{w-y}\partialderivative{y}\right)\\
\times\left(\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{x})^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{w}-\bar{y})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{x}}\partialderivative{\bar{x}}+\frac{1}{\bar{w}-\bar{y}}\partialderivative{\bar{y}}\right)\expectationvalue{X}

(72)

This contribution will simply be the complex conjugate of (
71
)

-2\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{x}-\bar{y})^{2}}\partialderivative{x^2}\expectationvalue{X}.

(73)

3.3.1
Renormalization

In order to remove all divergences, it is necessary to renormalize the fields. We will look for it in the form

\Phi^{R}(x)=\Phi(x)+\alpha A(\epsilon)L_{-1}\bar{L}_{-1}\Phi(x)+\alpha^{2}B_{11}(\epsilon)L_{-1}^{2}\bar{L}_{-1}^{2}\Phi(x)+\alpha^{2}B_{21}(\epsilon)L_{-2}\bar{L}_{-1}^{2}\Phi(x)+\\
+\alpha^{2}B_{12}(\epsilon)L_{-1}^{2}\bar{L}_{-2}\Phi(x)+\alpha^{2}B_{22}(\epsilon)L_{-2}\bar{L}_{-2}\Phi(x)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3})

(74)

In fact, since we consider a two-point correlation function, and also since the correlation functions of primary fields with quasi-primary ones are equal to zero, then it is correct to look for renormalizations among the descendants of the form

D_{-1}^{2}\Phi,\>D_{-2}\Phi,

(75)

where

\displaystyle D_{-1}

\displaystyle=L_{-1},

(76)

\displaystyle D_{-2}

\displaystyle=L_{-2}+\mu L_{-1}^{2},\>\mu=-\frac{3}{2(2\Delta+1)}

(77)

Thus,
D_{-2}\Phi
is the quasi-primary field. One can expand the renormalization to the second order using two bases:

B_{11}L_{-1}^{2}\bar{L}_{-1}^{2}+B_{21}L_{-2}\bar{L}_{-1}^{2}+B_{12}L_{-1}^{2}\bar{L}_{-2}+B_{22}L_{-2}\bar{L}_{-2}=\\
=\tilde{B}_{11}D_{-1}^{2}\bar{D}_{-1}^{2}+\tilde{B}_{21}D_{-2}\bar{D}_{-1}^{2}+\tilde{B}_{12}D_{-1}^{2}\bar{D}_{-2}+\tilde{B}_{22}D_{-2}\bar{D}_{-2}

(78)

Correspondence of the expansion coefficients is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{11}

\displaystyle=B_{11}-\mu B_{12}-\mu B_{21}+\mu^{2}B_{22}

(79)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{12}

\displaystyle=B_{12}-\mu B_{22}

(80)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{21}

\displaystyle=B_{21}-\mu B_{22}

(81)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{22}

\displaystyle=B_{22}

(82)

In this case, the renormalization will look this way

\Phi^{R}(x)=\Phi(x)+\alpha A(\epsilon)L_{-1}\bar{L}_{-1}\Phi(x)+\alpha^{2}\tilde{B}_{11}D_{-1}^{2}\bar{D}_{-1}^{2}\Phi(x)

(83)

Other terms, containing
D_{-2}
or
\bar{D}_{-2}
cannot be determined considering the two-point correlation function.

Let us calculate the correlation function of such fields in the perturbed theory. Let’s remember that

\expectationvalue{L_{-2} \Phi(x) \Phi(y)}_{0}=\expectationvalue{ \Phi(x) L_{-2}\Phi(y)}_{0}=\left(\frac{\Delta}{(x-y)^{2}}-\frac{1}{x-y}\partial_{x}\right)\expectationvalue{\Phi(x) \Phi(y)}_{0}

(84)

\expectationvalue{\bar{L}_{-2} \Phi(x) \Phi(y)}_{0}=\expectationvalue{ \Phi(x) \bar{L}_{-2} \Phi(y)}_{0}=\left(\frac{\Delta}{(\bar{x}-\bar{y})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}\bar{\partial}_{x}\right)\expectationvalue{\Phi(x) \Phi(y)}_{0}

(85)

and use this when calculating the correlation functions:

\expectationvalue{\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{def}=\expectationvalue{\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{0}-\alpha\int d^{2}z\>\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{0}\\
+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int d^{2}z\>d^{2}w\>\biggl(\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)T\bar{T}(w)\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{0}-\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)T\bar{T}(w)}_{0}\expectationvalue{\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{0}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})\\
=\expectationvalue{X}_{0}+2\alpha A(\epsilon)\partial\bar{\partial}\expectationvalue{X}_{0}+\alpha^{2}A^{2}(\epsilon)(\partial\bar{\partial})^{2}\expectationvalue{X}_{0}+2\alpha^{2}\tilde{B}_{11}(\epsilon)(\partial\bar{\partial})^{2}\expectationvalue{X}_{0}\\
-\alpha\int d^{2}z\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)X}_{0}-2\alpha^{2}A(\epsilon)\int d^{2}z\>\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}\\
+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int d^{2}z\>d^{2}w\>\biggl(\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)T\bar{T}(w)\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}-\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)T\bar{T}(w)}_{0}\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})

(86)

Now the problem is as follows: select such coefficient
\tilde{B}_{11}(\epsilon)
, that in the final expression for
\expectationvalue{\Phi^R(x_1)\Phi^R(x_2)}_{def}
all the divergences are cancelled. The divergences at the order
\alpha
were mutually cancelled by the choice

A(\epsilon)=\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}+\pi\log{m^2}

(87)

In order to find out the divergences at
\alpha^{2}
let us calculate the integral

\int d^{2}z\>\expectationvalue{T\bar{T}(z)\partial\bar{\partial} \Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}=\\
=2\partialderivative{x_1}\partialderivative{\bar{x}_1}\left[\biggl(\frac{\pi}{\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2}}\bar{\Delta}\partial+\frac{\pi}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\Delta\bar{\partial}+\biggl(\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}+2\pi\log{(|x_1-x_2|m)}\biggr)\partial\bar{\partial}\biggr)\expectationvalue{X}_{0}\right]=\\
=2\pi\biggl(\frac{2}{\epsilon}\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}+2\log(m|x_1-x_2|)\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}+\frac{\Delta+1}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\partial\bar{\partial}^{2}+\frac{\bar{\Delta}+1}{\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2}}\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}-\\
-\frac{\Delta}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}\bar{\partial}^{2}-\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{(\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2})^{2}}\partial^{2}\biggr)\expectationvalue{X}_{0}

(88)

A remarkable fact is that divergences of the form

4\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\epsilon}\log(m|x_1-x_2|)\partial^{2}\bar{\partial}^{2}\expectationvalue{X}

(89)

are cancelled completely.

In the following let us remind that

\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}=\frac{C}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2\Delta+2\bar{\Delta}}},

(90)

and hence follows the relations

\displaystyle\partial\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}

\displaystyle=(-2\Delta)\cdot\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0},

(91)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}

\displaystyle=(-2\Delta)\cdot(-2\Delta-1)\cdot\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0},

(92)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Delta}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\partial_{x}\right)\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}

\displaystyle=3\Delta\cdot\expectationvalue{\Phi(x_1)\Phi(x_2)}_{0}.

(93)

Then, matching the coefficients at different poles and different powers of
\Delta
and
\bar{\Delta}
, we obtain that

\tilde{B}_{11}=\frac{1}{2}A(\epsilon)^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2\Delta+1}+\frac{1}{2\bar{\Delta}+1}\right)

(94)

The term
\frac{1}{2}A(\epsilon)^{2}
is a second-order pole and is consistent with that obtained in
[
7
]
.

4
Conclusion and discussion

In this study, we have computed the effect of
T\bar{T}
-deformation on correlation functions of primary fields in conformal field theory. We provide the full calculation of first order correction in perturbation theory. In first order, we see that primary fields are mixed with their Laplacians in the process of the renormalization.

In the second order we succeeded to calculate the case of two-point functions. We see the highest divergence of order
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
, which is exactly
\frac{1}{2}A(\epsilon)^{2}
, is the term coming from the renormalization term

\Phi^{R}(x)=e^{\alpha A(\epsilon)\partial\bar{\partial}}\Phi(x).

(95)

Note that due to the difference in renormalization scheme in order to compare this expression with the one given in
[
7
]
one needs to replace the divergence
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
with
\log\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
– is the excluded hard-disc radius. We have also succeeded in deriving other than this ”exponential” renormalization terms, that are less divergent and are of order
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
. One can note that this correction is not universal for all fields but essentially depends on conformal and anti-conformal dimensions.

Appendix A
First order integrals

Useful formulas for calculations:

\begin{split}\int d^{d}V\frac{(V^{2})^{a}}{(V^{2}+D)^{b}}&=\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(b-a-d/2)\Gamma(a+d/2)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(d/2)}\cdot\frac{1}{D^{b-a-d/2}}\\
\frac{1}{A^{a}B^{b}}&=\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{1}du\frac{u^{a-1}(1-u)^{b-1}}{(Au+B(1-u))^{a+b}}\end{split}

(96)

A.1
General m, n

I_{m}^{n}(x,y)=\int d^{2}z\frac{1}{(z-x)^{m}(\bar{z}-\bar{y})^{n}}=\int d^{2}z\frac{(\bar{z}-\bar{x})^{m}(z-y)^{n}}{|z-x|^{2m}|z-y|^{2n}}=\\
=\lim_{d\to 2}\int d^{d}z\frac{((Z-X)\nu_{+})^{m}((Z-Y)\nu_{-})^{n}}{|Z-X|^{2m}|Z-Y|^{2n}},

(97)

where
\nu_{+}=(1,-i,0,0,...)
and
\nu_{-}=(1,i,0,0,...)
are
d
-dimensional projectors, and
Z
,
X
,
Y
are some
d
-dimensional vectors, such that
Z\cdot\nu_{+}=\bar{z}
,
Z\cdot\nu_{-}=z
etc. Let us make the substitution
W=Z-Y
,
A=X-Y
. Then the integral will take the form

\int d^{d}W\frac{((W-A)\nu_{+})^{m}(W\nu_{-})^{n}}{|W-A|^{2m}|W|^{2n}}.

(98)

Using the Feynman parameter and formula

\frac{1}{B^{a}C^{b}}=\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{1}du\frac{u^{a-1}(1-u)^{b-1}}{(Bu+C(1-u))^{a+b}}

(99)

with
B=|W-A|^{2}
,
C=|W|^{2}
the denominator in (
99
) can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{(|W-A|^{2})^{m}(|W|^{2})^{n}}=\int_{0}^{1}du\frac{u^{m}(1-u)^{n}}{((W-Au)^{2}+u(1-u)A^{2})^{m+n}}.

(100)

Next, one can make the replacement
V=W-uA
and
D=A^{2}u(1-u)
. As a result, the integral takes the form

\int_{0}^{1}du\>u^{m}(1-u)^{n}\int d^{d}V\frac{((V-A(1-u))\nu_{+})^{m}((V+uA)\nu_{-})^{n}}{(V^{2}+D)^{m+n}}.

(101)

Let us work with the numerator

((V-A(1-u))\nu_{+})^{m}((V+uA)\nu_{-})^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\biggl(V^{\alpha_{i}}-A^{\alpha_{i}}(1-u)\biggr)\biggl(V^{\beta_{j}}+uA^{\beta_{j}}\biggl)\nu_{+\alpha_{i}}\nu_{-\beta_{j}}.

(102)

Since this term is under the integral over
V
, the denominator of the integrand is invariant under the change
V\to-V
, then only the terms with an even number of
V^{\alpha}
will give a non-zero contribution to the integral. One can expand the brackets in (
102
) to obtain

\begin{split}((V-A(1-u))\nu_{+})^{m}((V+uA)\nu_{-})^{n}&=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0,\>\\
l+k\in 2\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}^{n}(-1)^{m-k}C_{m}^{k}C_{n}^{l}u^{n-l}(1-u)^{m-k}\\
\cdot V^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots V^{\alpha_{k}}V^{\beta_{1}}\ldots V^{\beta_{l}}A^{\alpha_{k+1}}\ldots A^{\alpha_{m}}&A^{\beta_{l+1}}\ldots A^{\beta_{n}}\nu_{+\alpha_{1}}\ldots\nu_{+\alpha_{m}}\nu_{-\beta_{1}}\ldots\nu_{-\beta_{n}},\end{split}

(103)

where the emerging scalar product of the vectors should be treated as follows:

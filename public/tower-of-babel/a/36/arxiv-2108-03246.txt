Signatures of a Majorana-Fermi surface in the Kitaev magnet Ag 3 LiIr 2 O 6
Joshuah T. Heath, Faranak Bahrami, Sangyun Lee, Roman Movshovich, Xiao Chen, Fazel Tafti, Kevin S. Bedell

Abstract
Detecting Majorana fermions in experimental realizations of the Kitaev honeycomb model is often complicated by non-trivial interactions inherent to potential spin liquid candidates. In this work, we identify several distinct thermodynamic signatures of massive, itinerant Majorana fermions within the well-established analytical paradigm of Landau-Fermi liquid theory. We find a qualitative and quantitative agreement between the salient features of our Landau-Majorana liquid theory and the Kitaev spin liquid candidate Ag 3 LiIr 2 O 6 . Our study presents strong evidence for a Fermi liquid-like ground state in the fundamental excitations of a honeycomb iridate, and opens new experimental avenues to detect itinerant Majorana fermions in condensed matter systems.



I. Introduction

Kitaev honeycomb materials
are among the most promising
means of realizing itinerant Majorana fermions in a condensed matter setting

Hermanns et al. 2018
;

In the quantum spin liquid (QSL) phase, localized spins on the 2D hexagonal lattice fractionalize, allowing us to map the original spin degrees of freedom onto a Hamiltonian describing itinerant fermionic quasiparticles and localized

Unfortunately, real materials are more complex than Kitaev’s original formulation, and are often characterized by additional interactions such as

Jackeli and Khaliullin 2009

Due to the ubiquitous nature of these non-Kitaev interactions in real-world honeycomb materials, the full effect of off-diagonal contributions to the underlying Majorana excitations is an important question in the study of topological matter. Recent work on

As a consequence, such a Majorana-Fermi surface might be induced in a Kitaev material whenever spins exhibiting AFM order are coupled to those in the QSL phase via some exchange interaction.

II. Results and Discussion

II A. Theory of the Majorana-Fermi surface

The many-body Majorana model we consider is built off of a modification of fermionic combinatorics, where self-annihilation results in a modulo-2 correction to the traditional
“stars and bars”
argument of the statistical weight
Heath and Bedell 2019
. At low temperatures, the resulting distribution function resembles a Fermi-Dirac distribution, albeit with a much more sharply-defined discontinuity in the thermodynamic limit. More specifically, the
“Majorana-Fermi surface”
discussed in
Heath and Bedell 2019
retains a strong step function-like behavior in momentum space even at finite temperature, whereas the traditional Fermi surface of complex Dirac fermions is characterized by some finite
“smearing”
as we raise the temperature. This sharp Majorana-Fermi surface has been found to be a universal many-body feature of independent self-conjugate particles, and as a result
the Fermi-Dirac Sommerfeld coefficient is modified by a multiplicative factor dependent on the dimensionality
Heath and Bedell 2019
.
At
T=0
, the ground state of the Majorana-like system is a filled Fermi sea and, hence, the Fermi energy
\epsilon_{F}
and the Fermi temperature
T_{F}
are identical to that of a weakly correlated Fermi liquid.
Nevertheless, the sharp Majorana-Fermi surface results in a suppression of quasihole excitations
Heath and Bedell 2020
, and thus we expect important deviations from traditional Fermi liquid behavior.

In a conventional Fermi liquid, correlations between quasiparticles and quasiholes at finite temperature result in
long-wavelength fluctuations of the statistical quasiparticle energies
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
;
Baym and Pethick 2004
. Here,
“statistical”
quasiparticle energies obey the same equations as the quasiparticle energies defined by Landau, and are therefore different from the
“dynamical”
quasiparticle energies corresponding to the poles of the propagator. Long-wavelength fluctuations of these energies lead to a non-analytic scattering amplitude, and as a consequence apparent logarithmic divergences emerge in low-temperature quantities of physical interest
Anderson 1965
;
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
;
Carneiro and Pethick 1975
;
Chubukov et al. 2006
.
Such a phenomenon stems from
the total energy contribution of a quasiparticle of momentum
p
interacting with other quasiparticles of momentum
p+q
, given as
\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}=\sum_{q}f_{p\sigma,\,p+q\sigma^{\prime}}n_{p+q,\,\sigma^{\prime}}
,
where
f_{p\sigma,\,p+q}=\partial\epsilon_{p}/\partial n_{p+q}
is the Landau parameter and
n_{p+q,\,\sigma}
is the equilibrium distribution function.
The underlying
q
-dependence of the Landau parameter results in the non-analytic behavior seen in
\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}
and the specific heat.
From a k-matrix analysis of these scattering events, a Landau parameter
f^{\lambda}_{p,\,p+q}=f^{\lambda}(0)+b^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}+...
is found, where
\lambda=s\,(a)
for the spin (anti-)symmetric channel and
b^{\lambda}
is a function of the
k
-matrix
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
;
Baym and Pethick 2004
.

Theoretically, we find the presence of a sharply-defined Majorana-Fermi surface modifies the
q
-dependence of the Landau parameter
SUP
, which now goes as

f^{\lambda}_{p,\,p+q}=f^{\lambda}(0)+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})+b^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}+...
, with coefficients
b^{\lambda}
and
\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
(Cyrillic b) related to the scattering amplitude. As seen in the explicit calculation of
b^{\lambda}
and
\textrm{b}^{\lambda}

SUP
, both parameters have strong dependence on the value of the
“Majorana parameter”

\alpha
,
which controls how
“sharp”
the distribution function is at the Fermi energy. More explicitly and as introduced in
Heath and Bedell 2020
,
\alpha
is a phenomenological parameter in the number distribution which quantifies the affect fermionic self-conjugation has on collective, many-body behavior. As
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
, we recover the Fermi-Dirac distribution, with

\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\rightarrow 0
and a value of
B^{\lambda}
identical to the standard Fermi liquid result
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
. As
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, the fermionic number distribution retains a more step-function like behavior, and as such the Fermi surface becomes more sharply defined against external perturbations. As a consequence, terms in the Landau parameter proportional to
\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
become more prominent. Intermediate values of
\alpha
correspond to systems in which the statistical effects of self-conjugation are subjugated either by repulsive interactions, external effects, or other similar behavior.

With the full form of the
q
-dependent Landau parameter derived, the change in the quasiparticle energy
\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}
from quasiparticle-quasihole scattering in a Majorana liquid can easily be calculated via standard phase space integrals.
Note that the final form of
\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}
is found by only considering interactions between quasiparticles of momentum
p
within a cutoff
q_{c}
; i.e.,
\mid p-p_{F}\mid\ll q_{c}\ll p_{F}
, thereby restricting ourselves
to a
“window”
around the Fermi surface where Fermi liquid theory remains valid.
The resulting change to the specific heat is then found to be

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta C_{v}}{nT}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{4}}{1152}w^{\lambda}\log(2)\frac{k_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon_{F}}\frac{T_{\textrm{cut}}}{T_{M}}\frac{T}{T_{F}}(A_{0}^{\lambda})^{3}\left(1+\frac{13\pi^{2}}{20}\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{13\pi^{4}}{160}w^{\lambda}\frac{k_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon_{F}}\frac{T^{2}}{T_{F}^{2}}\log\mid\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\mid(A_{0}^{a})^{2}

(1)

where we have defined the cut-off temperature to be
T_{\textrm{cut}}=\hbar v_{F}q_{c}/2k_{B}
, the
“Majorana temperature”
to be
T_{M}=\hbar v_{F}\alpha/2k_{B}
, and we focus on a single scattering channel
\lambda
. The cutoff temperature
T_{\textrm{cut}}
is equivalent to the cutoff temperature given in
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
for the traditional Landau-Fermi liquid system, and corresponds to
v_{F}q_{c}
in temperature units. The Landau-Fermi liquid approach discussed in this article remains applicable only for temperatures
T<T_{c}
. The first term in Eqn. (
1
) is a unique feature in the specific heat explicitly connected to the presence of Majorana-like behavior in the underlying fermionic excitations
Heath and Bedell 2019
;
Heath and Bedell 2020
, and is a direct result of particle-hole scattering near the more sharply-defined Majorana-Fermi surface. The Majorana temperature (i.e., the parameter
v_{F}\alpha
in units of temperature) quantifies the multiplicative constant defining the first term in Eqn. (
1
). As such, in the limit of
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
, the leading-order expression in Eqn. (
1
) disappears, as expected in a conventional Fermi liquid with a traditional Fermi surface.
Note that the specific heat now has strong dependence on three new parameters:
T_{\textrm{cut}}
,
T_{M}
, and
A_{0}^{\lambda}
. The leading-order term in
C_{v}/T
is therefore greatly enhanced if
T_{M}/T_{\textrm{cut}}\ll 1
. More specifically, we see that the first-order term in Eqn. (
1
) dominates over the logarithmic term if
{T^{2}}/{T_{M}T_{F}}>>{T^{2}}/{T_{F}^{2}}\log\mid T/T_{\textrm{cut}}\mid
, or, equivalently, if
T_{\textrm{cut}}\exp(T_{F}/T_{M})>>T
. As such, we see that if
T_{M}
is very small, there is a large temperature range where the logarithmic term in Eqn. (
1
) is negligible compared to the leading-order term. As such, we will proceed in the next section to fit the zero-field data to a quadratic polynomial, and from the fit extract the parameters of physical interest.

II D. Possible magnon contribution to the specific heat

Before we precede, it is important to bring to light the possible issue of a magnonic contribution to the specific heat measurements. Recent powder inelastic neutron scattering data of the parent compound
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO

suggest magnonic excitations at a few meV
Choi et al. 2012
;
Choi et al. 2019
, however direct detection of magnons in the lithium iridates is often challenging as Ir is a strong neutron absorber. Recent RIXS data on the parent compound,
\alpha
-Li
2
IrO
3
, have shown a broad continuum of magnetic excitations that persist to 90 K, well above
T_{N}=15

, with similar magnetic excitations observed in Ag

2

6

in the low-temperature regime
T<T_{N}
, as a finite Sommerfeld coefficient cannot be explained by magnonic excitations and
a linear
T
-dependence in
\gamma(T)
would imply an unconventional magnon dispersion of
\omega\sim k^{3/2}
unfounded in the iridates
Vladimirov et al. 2016
;
Pan et al. 2013
;
Winter et al. 2017
.

We now turn to the possibility of a magnonic contribution to the specific heat data at high magnetic fields. Theoretically, the magnonic spectrum in an extended Kitaev-Heisenberg system has been studied in the context of nonlinear spin wave theory
McClarty et al. 2018
, in which case a finite Kitaev exchange ensures a nonzero gap in the magnonic spectrum at high external magnetic fields. The gap,
\Delta
, in the bosonic spectrum would then manifest as an exponential supression
e^{-\Delta/k_{B}T}
to the bosonic specific heat. At temperatures on the order of the gap, the gap itself would affect the magnonic dispersion so that
C_{V}/T\sim T^{1/2}
with a positive coefficient of proportionality, which is a feature we do not see in our data. As a result, due the ubiquitous nature of a gap in the magnonic spectrum of the Kitaev-Heisenberg model at finite magnetic field, we conclude that the magnonic contribution to our specific heat measurements are negligible compared to the clear Fermi liquid-like signatures seen in Figs.
3
and
5
. Agreement between the zero and finite magnetic field estimates of
T_{\textrm{cut}}
provide further confirmation for a negligible magnon contribution to the zero-field specific heat data.

Note that our theory of the Landau-Majorana liquid does not completely rule out the presence of magnons in a generic Kitaev magnet. Rather, our theoretical analysis and experimental results suggest that, were magnons leading to a dominant contribution to the specific heat, those magnonic excitations must have very unusual dispersions inconsistent with current theories. In contrast, our data appears to nicely follow the behavior expected from a Landau-Majorana liquid. For future work, it would be helpful to obtain a similarly complete set of temperature and field-dependent specific heat data for other Kitaev spin liquid candidates, and follow a similar analysis as explained here.

IV B. Electronic and nuclear Schottky contributions to the specific heat

The specific heat was obtained via a quasi-adiabatic method. The heater was mounted on one side of the sapphire stage, and the pressed pellet of the polycrystalline sample mounted on the other side with GE varnish. A ruthenium oxide resistance thermometer was glued to the free side of the sample pellet, and the weak link to the bath was attached with GE varnish directly to the sample. A heat pulse was delivered to heat capacity stage at
t_{0}
, and the temperature of the thermometer was measured as a function of time. The thermometer was directly attached to the sample, and since the heat capacity of the thermometer is negligible in comparison to that of the sample, the heat flowing between the thermometer and the sample is negligible. The thermometer is therefore in good thermal equilibrium with the sample (its lattice, to be precise), and there is no traditional
\tau_{2}
effect. The multi-exponential relaxation of thermometer’s temperature is due to internal equilibration processes, as described below.

At temperatures below roughly 100 to 150 mK, depending on the magnetic field of the measurement, the raw temperature decay curves display two regimes: initial fast relaxation followed by a very long relaxation, as displayed in Fig.
6
.

The short time scale temperature decay may reflect both the electronic and nuclear degrees of freedom (so called spin-lattice relaxation). We argue below that the nuclear contribution is reflected in the long-time tail of the temperature relaxation curve. At that time, the system (electrons and lattice) is slowly decaying down to the bath temperature. The long-time decay, therefore, reflects the total (electronic plus nuclear) specific heat of the sample, and the short time decay reflects the electronic specific heat only.

The analysis of the zero field data is particularly instructive. The electronic specific heat is determined from the fast decay. The nuclear specific heat can be approximated by the extrapolation of the long time tail of the data back to
t=0
, fitting the last 25% of the temperature trace to either slow exponential or (perhaps better) linear dependence. A linear fit may be more reliable due to the small part of the temperature curve used for extended extrapolation to
t_{0}
. Perhaps the strongest indication of the separate contribution from the nuclei is the offset of the short decay exponential fit’s equilibrium temperature from the initial temperature (before heat pulse is applied). This offset is clearly present in the data below roughly 120 mK, as seen in the inset of Fig.
6
.

is the density of states. As discussed in the main text, the
“Majorana parameter”

\alpha
is an experimentally-determined constant which controls how
“sharp”
the distribution function is at the Fermi energy
Heath and Bedell 2020
.

To derive the form of the
q
-dependent Landau parameter, we must first consider the scattering of a quasiparticle-quasihole pair in a regular Fermi liquid, the scattering process is dictated by the following equations for the
t
- and
k
- matrices:

\displaystyle t_{p\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega)=f_{p\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}+2\sum_{p_{2}}f_{p\,p_{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{n_{p_{2}}^{0}-n_{p_{2}+q}^{0}}{\omega-\epsilon_{p_{2}+q}+\epsilon_{p_{2}}+i\eta}t_{p_{2}\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega)

(4)

\displaystyle k_{p\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega)=f_{p\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}+2\mathscr{P}\sum_{p_{2}}f_{p\,p_{2}}^{\lambda}\frac{n_{p_{2}}^{0}-n_{p_{2}+q}^{0}}{\omega-\epsilon_{p_{2}+q}+\epsilon_{p_{2}}}k_{p_{2}\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega)

(5)

where
f_{p\,p_{2}}^{\lambda}
is the Landau parameter for the
\lambda
scattering channel and
n_{p}^{0}
is the equilibrium distribution function.
In the case of particle-particle scattering, the interaction energy is given by the equation

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle=K(E)\left\{1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\left(\pi\delta(E-H_{0})K(E)\right)^{2n}\right\}

\displaystyle=K(E)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}K(E)\delta(E-H_{0})K(E)\delta(E-H_{0})K(E)+...

(6)

where
K(E)
is some general k-matrix which describes mutual particle collisions. For a Fermi liquid, however, we need to consider the quasiparticle-quasihole version of the above, and thus we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=k_{pp}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega_{q\,q})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}(2)^{2}\sum_{p_{1}p_{2}}k_{p\,p_{1}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega_{p\,q})[n_{p_{1}}^{0}-n_{p_{1}+q}^{0}]\delta(\omega_{p\,q}-\omega_{p_{1}\,q})

\displaystyle\phantom{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}(2)^{2}\sum_{p_{1}p_{2}}}\times k_{p_{1}\,p_{2}}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega_{p\,q})[n_{p_{2}}^{0}-n_{p_{2}+q}^{0}]\delta(\omega_{p\,q}-\omega_{p_{2}\,q})k_{p_{2}\,p}^{\lambda}(q,\,\omega_{p\,q})+...

(7)

where we have defined
\omega_{p\,q}=\epsilon_{p+q}-\epsilon_{p}
, where
\epsilon_{p}
is the quasiparticle energy.
The interaction energy of the quasiparticle and quasihole singlet spin state is given by
2\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{s}
, while the triplet state is given by
2\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{a}
. Note that the specific heat is dependent on the spin-symmetric part of the interaction. Given that

\displaystyle f_{\uparrow\uparrow}(p,\,p+q)=-(\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{s}+\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{a})

(8)

\displaystyle f_{\uparrow\downarrow}(p,\,p+q)=-2\omega_{p\,q}^{a}

(9)

we can write the symmetric Landau parameter in the form

\displaystyle f_{p,\,p+q}^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{\uparrow\uparrow}(p,\,p+q)+f_{\uparrow\downarrow}(p,\,p+q)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta\omega_{pq}^{s}+3\Delta\omega_{pq}^{a}\right)

(10)

In the Majorana liquid we consider in the text, the form of the k-matrix differs from that of a traditional Fermi liquid.
To see this, recall from
Heath and Bedell 2019
;
Heath and Bedell 2020
the form of the distribution function
\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}
of the Majorana system:

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}=\Theta(p_{F}-p^{\prime})+\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}

(11)

That is, for
p^{\prime}>p_{F}
, we have
\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}=n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}
, while for
p^{\prime}<p_{F}
we have
n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}=1
. As such, we see

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}+q}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}+q\cdot\frac{\partial\widetilde{n}^{0}}{\partial p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}+q\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\prime}}\Theta(p_{F}-p^{\prime})+n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}q\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\prime}}\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})+\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})q\cdot\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial p^{\prime}}

(12)

We will now take the approximation that
p^{\prime}
is slightly above (but very close) to the Fermi surface, eliminating the second term. Now, for the remaining terms, we have

\displaystyle n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}q\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\prime}}\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})+\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})q\cdot\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial p^{\prime}}=n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}|q|\delta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})+\Theta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})q\cdot\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial p^{\prime}}

(13)

The first term on the right-hand side of the above is the direct result of the underlying Majorana statistics, which yields a sharp Fermi surface. However, in the case discussed in
Heath and Bedell 2019
, we have particle-hole suppression isotropically across the entire Fermi surface, as the smearing from thermal excitations is isotropic (i.e., there is no thermal gradient over
p=p_{F}
). In this case, the
“smearing”
is the direct result of a perturbation along the
\hat{q}
-direction; i.e., in the direction of particle-hole propagation. As such, the divergent term we will see is the direct result of an
“eternally sharp”
point in the distribution along the direction of particle-hole propagation only, and thus the first of the above will be independent of the relative angle between
\hat{q}
and
\hat{p}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}+q}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}+n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}|q|\delta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})+q\cdot v_{p^{\prime}}\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p^{\prime}}}

(14)

and therefore

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}-\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}+q}^{0}

\displaystyle=-n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}|q|\delta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})-q\cdot v_{p^{\prime}}\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p^{\prime}}}

(15)

Letting the Heaviside step function be approximated by a generalized Fermi-Dirac-type function
f
, we find that

\displaystyle n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}\delta(p^{\prime}-p_{F})|q|

\displaystyle=n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}\frac{f(1-f)|q|}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{|q|}{4\alpha}

(16)

Once again, in the above we have assumed that we are close to the Fermi surface. Otherwise, this term disappears. We are left with

\displaystyle\widetilde{n}^{0}_{p^{\prime}}-\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}+q}^{0}=-\frac{|q|}{4\alpha}-q\cdot v_{p^{\prime}}\frac{\partial n_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p^{\prime}}}

(17)

Performing the relevant phase space integrals, we find that

\displaystyle\int p^{\prime 2}dp^{\prime}(\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}}^{0}-\widetilde{n}_{p^{\prime}+q}^{0})\delta(\omega_{pq}-\omega_{p^{\prime}q})

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\nu(0)\left\{s-\frac{p_{F}}{12\alpha}\right\}\delta(s-\hat{p}^{\prime}\cdot\hat{q})

(18)

Hence, for the Majorana liquid,

\displaystyle\nu(0)\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q},\,\phi=0)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}\right)^{2}-\frac{p_{F}}{48\alpha}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}+\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{576\alpha^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\bigg\{}\hskip 28.45274pt\times\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{1}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi_{2}}{2\pi}\kappa^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q},\,\phi_{1})\kappa^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q},\,\phi_{2})\kappa^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q},\,\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})+...

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}\right)^{2}-\frac{p_{F}}{48\alpha}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}+\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{576\alpha^{2}}\right\}[\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})]^{3}+...\right\}

(19)

The fact that
\Delta\omega_{pq}
has linear and quadratic terms in
\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}
means that the Landau parameter
F^{\lambda}_{p,\,p+q}
will also have linear and quadratic terms in
\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}
. We can now write

\displaystyle F^{\lambda}_{p,\,p+q}=F^{\lambda}(0)+\textrm{B}^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})+B^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}+...

(20)

Our goal now is to find the value of
\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\equiv\nu(0)\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
. This is done by expanding the k-matrices in the expression for
\nu(0)\Delta\omega_{pq}^{\lambda}
about
s\equiv\omega/qv_{F}=0
:

\displaystyle\nu(0)\Delta\omega_{p\,q}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg\{\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}(0)+\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}\frac{d\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{d^{2}\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}}{ds^{2}}\bigg|_{s=0}\right]-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\bigg\{\left(\frac{1}{2}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg\{}-\frac{p_{F}}{48\alpha}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}+\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{576\alpha^{2}|q|^{2}}\bigg\}[\kappa_{m}^{\lambda}(0)]^{3}+...\bigg\}

(21)

The term proportional to
(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}
is identical to the similar term seen in a regular Landau-Fermi liquid
Pethick and Carneiro 1973
;
Carneiro and Pethick 1975
. The term linear in
\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}
is given by

\displaystyle\textrm{B}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}w_{\lambda}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{d\widetilde{\kappa}_{m}^{\lambda}}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{144}\frac{p_{F}}{\alpha}[\widetilde{\kappa}_{m}^{\lambda}(0)]^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\frac{p_{F}}{\alpha}\left\{(A_{0}^{s})^{2}\left[\frac{A_{0}^{s}}{36}-\frac{1}{p_{F}^{3}V}\right]+3(A_{0}^{a})^{2}\left[\frac{A_{0}^{a}}{36}-\frac{1}{p_{F}^{3}V}\right]\right\}

(22)

where we have expanded the k-matrix in terms of partial waves, taken the
\ell=0
channel as dominant, and used the fact that
1
1

1

Throughout this derivation, note that we have neglected the additional energy unit and taken
\hbar=1
.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{\lambda}(0)=A_{0}^{\lambda}

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{d\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{\lambda}(s)}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}=-(A_{0}^{\lambda})^{2}\left\{\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\alpha p_{F}^{2}V}\right\}

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{\lambda}(s)}{ds^{2}}\bigg|_{s=0}=2(A_{0}^{\lambda})^{2}+2(A_{0}^{\lambda})^{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\alpha p_{F}^{2}V}\right)^{2}

(25)

Without loss of generality, we can use the same underlying mathematics to calculate
B^{s}
for the Majorana liquid:

\displaystyle B^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(A_{0}^{s})^{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}A_{0}^{s}\right)+3(A_{0}^{a})^{2}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}A_{0}^{a}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{=-\frac{1}{2}\bigg\{}-\frac{\pi^{4}}{32}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha p_{F}^{2}V}\right)^{2}\left\{\left(A_{0}^{s}\right)^{3}+3\left(A_{0}^{a}\right)^{3}\right\}

(26)

This completes our derivation of the Landau parameter
F_{p,\,p+q}^{\lambda}=F^{\lambda}(0)+\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}+B^{\lambda}(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}+...
.
So how does this effect the specific heat? We know that the total contribution to the quasiparticle energy coming from
\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\epsilon_{p\sigma}=\sum_{q}f_{p\sigma,\,p+q\sigma^{\prime}}\widetilde{n}_{p+q,\,\sigma^{\prime}}^{0}

(27)

We will deal with the term linear in
(\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q})
first. Defining
\textrm{B}^{\lambda}=\nu(0)\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
and writing
\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}=\frac{\hat{p}\cdot\vec{q}}{|q|}=\frac{q_{\|}}{\sqrt{q_{\|}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}}}
,
the change in the quasiparticle energy from this term is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}\int_{0}^{q_{c}^{2}-q_{\|}^{2}}\pi d(q_{\perp}^{2})\frac{q_{\|}}{\sqrt{q_{\|}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}}}\widetilde{n}_{p+q}^{0}(T)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}q_{c}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}q_{\|}\widetilde{n}^{0}_{p+q}(T)-\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}q_{\|}^{2}\widetilde{n}_{p+q}^{0}(T)

(28)

where we have utilized the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{q_{c}^{2}-q_{\|}^{2}}d(q_{\perp}^{2})\frac{q_{\|}}{\sqrt{q_{\|}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=2q_{\|}\sqrt{q_{\|}^{2}+x}\bigg|_{0}^{q_{c}^{2}-q_{\|}^{2}}=2q_{\|}q_{c}+2q_{\|}^{2}

(29)

We then find the change in the quasiparticle energy is given by

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)

\displaystyle=-q_{c}\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}q_{\|}\widetilde{n}^{0}_{p+q}(T)-\frac{b^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}q_{\|}^{2}\log\mid\frac{q_{\|}}{q_{c}}\mid\widetilde{n}_{p+q}^{0}(T)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-q_{c}}^{q_{c}}dq_{\|}q_{\|}^{2}\widetilde{n}_{p+q}^{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{-\frac{3}{2}(p-p_{F})^{2}v_{F}^{2}\left\{\frac{3}{2}q_{c}\textrm{b}^{\lambda}-(p-p_{F})\left(b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{}\hskip-28.45274pt+3\log(2)(k_{B}T)(p-p_{F})v_{F}\left(q_{c}\textrm{b}^{\lambda}-(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{}\hskip-28.45274pt+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}(k_{B}T)^{2}(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}\bigg\}

(30)

where we found the above by solving Fermi-Dirac type integrals of the form

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\int_{-p+p_{F}}^{q_{c}}n_{q+p}^{\prime}(q+p-p_{F})^{m}dq

\displaystyle=\int_{-p+p_{F}}^{q_{c}}\frac{e^{\beta(q+p-p_{F})v_{F}}}{(e^{\beta(q+p-p_{F})v_{F}}+1)^{2}}(\beta(q+p-p_{F}))^{m}dq

\displaystyle=-(\beta v_{F})^{-m}\int_{0}^{\beta(q_{c}+p-p_{F})v_{F}}\frac{e^{x}}{(e^{x}+1)^{2}}x^{m}dx,\quad x=\beta(q+p-p_{F})v_{F}

(31)

We will now derive the change in thermodynamic entropy for the Majorana system. The change in the entropy can be simplified to an integral equation:

\displaystyle\Delta S

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)\frac{\partial\widetilde{n}_{p}}{\partial T}\approx-\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{T\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)\left(p-p_{F}\right)dn_{p}

(32)

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{n}_{p}}{\partial T}=\frac{e^{\frac{(p-p_{F})v_{F}}{k_{B}T}}}{\left(e^{\frac{(p-p_{F})v_{F}}{k_{B}T}}+1\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{(p-p_{F})}{k_{B}T^{2}}v_{F}\right)\Theta(p-p_{F})\approx\frac{\partial n_{p}}{\partial T}

(33)

Now, we can run through every term in the above equation for
\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)
and subsequently find the total change in the entropy. These integrals are easy to perform, and are left as an exercise for the reader. The end result is

\displaystyle\Delta S

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{T\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\epsilon_{p}(T)(p-p_{F})dn_{p}

\displaystyle=-\frac{13}{240}\pi^{4}nk_{B}\left(\frac{T}{T_{F}}\right)^{3}\left(B^{\lambda}\log|\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}|+\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{q_{c}}{8p_{F}}\log(2)\pi^{2}nk_{B}\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{T}{T_{F}}\right)^{2}

(34)

The change in the specific heat
\Delta C_{v}
can now be readily calculated from
\Delta S
:

\displaystyle\Delta C_{v}

\displaystyle=T\frac{\partial\Delta S}{\partial T}\bigg|_{V}

\displaystyle=-\frac{13\pi^{4}}{80}nk_{B}\left(B^{\lambda}\log|\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}|+\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\right)\left(\frac{T}{T_{F}}\right)^{3}-\frac{\log(2)\pi^{2}}{4}\frac{q_{c}}{p_{F}}nk_{B}\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{T}{T_{F}}\right)^{2}

(35)

Hence, simplifying and using our previous expressions for the factors
\textrm{B}^{\lambda}
and
B^{\lambda}
, and limiting ourselves to a single scattering channel
\lambda
, we find the specific heat to be

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta C_{v}}{nT}\approx\frac{\pi^{4}}{384}\log(2)\frac{k_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon_{F}}\frac{T_{\textrm{cut}}}{T_{M}}\frac{T}{T_{F}}(A_{0}^{a})^{3}\left(1+\frac{13\pi^{2}}{20}\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)+\frac{39\pi^{4}}{160}\frac{k_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon_{F}}\frac{T^{2}}{T_{F}^{2}}\log\mid\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\mid(A_{0}^{a})^{2}

(36)

where we have defined the Majorana temperature as

\displaystyle T_{M}=\frac{\alpha v_{F}}{2k_{B}}\rightarrow\alpha=\frac{2k_{B}T_{M}}{v_{F}}

(37)

in analogy to the Fermi temperature

\displaystyle T_{F}=\frac{\epsilon_{F}}{k_{B}}=\frac{p_{F}^{2}}{2k_{B}m}=\frac{p_{F}v_{F}}{2k_{B}}

(38)

and defined the cutoff temperature to be

\displaystyle T_{\textrm{cut}}=\frac{\hbar v_{F}q_{c}}{2k_{B}}

(39)

This completes the zero-field expression for
\Delta C_{v}/nT
given in the text.

IV D. Non-analytic contributions to the specific heat in a Majorana liquid II: Finite external magnetic field

Building off of our previous derivation of the specific heat, we will now consider the effects of turning on some finite external magnetic field.
Recall the change in the quasiparticle energy due to Majorana quasiparticle interactions:

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{-\frac{3}{2}(p-p_{F})^{2}v_{F}^{2}\left\{\frac{3}{2}q_{c}\textrm{b}^{\lambda}-(p-p_{F})\left(b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{}\hskip-34.1433pt+3\log(2)(k_{B}T)(p-p_{F})v_{F}\left(q_{c}\textrm{b}^{\lambda}-(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{}\hskip-34.1433pt+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}(k_{B}T)^{2}(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}\bigg\}

(40)

The effects of a magnetic field
M
on the specific heat can be found by first calculating the dependence of
M
on the density of states
\nu(T)
and Landau parameter
f_{0}^{a}
, given as

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\nu(T)\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}H-f_{0}^{a}M\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\hbar\sum_{pp^{\prime}}\Delta f\frac{\partial n}{\partial\epsilon}\delta n_{p}

(41)

where
g
is the gyromagnetic ratio
Carneiro and Pethick 1977
;
Misawa 1983
. By using the fact that
M=\chi_{H}H
, the temperature-dependence of the susceptibility is contained within the change of the density of states at finite field. Finally, using the Maxwell relation

\displaystyle H\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial T^{2}}=\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial H}

(42)

the
H
-field dependence of the specific heat
\gamma\equiv C(H,\,T)/T
can be extracted.
Note that the integral over
dp
in the calculation of the density of states will yield zero for any term proportional to
(p-p_{F})^{2}
or higher. Hence, we only care about the following terms:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{3\log(2)(k_{B}T)(p-p_{F})v_{F}q_{c}\textrm{b}^{\lambda}

(43)

\displaystyle\phantom{\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}\bigg\{}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}(k_{B}T)^{2}(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}\bigg\}

Simplifying, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\epsilon_{p}\approx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12v_{F}^{2}}(k_{B}T)^{2}(p-p_{F})\left\{b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{\max((p-p_{F}),\,T)}{q_{c}}\mid+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}\right\}

The new, temperature-dependent density of states
\nu^{\prime}(T)
is therefore

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}(T)

\displaystyle=\nu(0)+\sum_{p}\frac{\partial n^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p}^{0}}\frac{\partial\Delta\epsilon_{p}}{\partial\epsilon_{p}^{0}}-\sum_{pp^{\prime}}\Delta f_{pp^{\prime}}\frac{\partial n^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p}^{0}}\frac{\partial n^{0}}{\partial\epsilon_{p^{\prime}}^{0}}

\displaystyle\sim\nu(0)\left\{1+aT^{2}b^{\lambda}\log\mid\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\mid+\beta\textrm{b}^{\lambda}T^{2}+\zeta\textrm{b}^{\lambda}TT_{\textrm{cut}}-G[b^{\lambda},\,\textrm{b}^{\lambda}]\right\}

where

,

and

are unitful constants, and

is a function of

summed with
\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
. The susceptibility is then

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}\nu^{\prime}(T)}{1+f_{0}^{\lambda}\nu^{\prime}(T)}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}\nu(0)\bigg\{\frac{(-1+G)}{-1+F_{0}^{\lambda}(-1+G)}+\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}TT_{\textrm{cut}}\zeta}{(-1+F_{0}^{\lambda}(-1+G))^{2}}

\displaystyle\phantom{.............}+\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}(-\beta-a\log(T/T_{\textrm{cut}})+F_{0}^{s}(-1+G)(\beta+a\log(T/T_{\textrm{cut}}))+\textrm{b}^{\lambda}T_{\textrm{cut}}^{2}\zeta^{2})T^{2}}{(-1+F_{0}^{\lambda}(-1+G))^{3}}\bigg\}

We will now take the second derivative of

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial T^{2}}\approx\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}\nu(0)\frac{\textrm{b}^{\lambda}}{(-1+F_{0}^{\lambda}(-1+G))^{3}}

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt\phantom{\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial T^{2}}\approx}\times\left\{-3a-2\beta+2\textrm{b}^{\lambda}T_{\textrm{cut}}^{2}\gamma^{2}-2a\log\left(\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)+(-1+G)\left(3a+2\beta+2a\log\left(\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)\right)F_{0}^{\lambda}\right\}

Taking

and assuming
\textrm{b}^{\lambda}
is very large, the above becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial T^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}}{(-1+F_{0}^{\lambda}(-1+\delta(B^{\lambda}+\textrm{B}^{\lambda}))^{3}}\bigg\{2\textrm{b}^{\lambda}T_{\textrm{cut}}^{2}\gamma^{2}+F_{0}^{\lambda}\left(3a+2\beta+2a\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\log\left(\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{\approx}\hskip 142.26378pt\times\left(-2+\delta(B^{\lambda}+\textrm{B}^{\lambda})\right)\bigg\}

(48)

For small values of
\textrm{B}^{\lambda}
,
\partial^{2}\chi/\partial T^{2}
becomes temperature independent. For larger values, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{\partial T^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}\frac{2a}{(F_{0}^{\lambda})^{2}\delta^{3}\textrm{B}^{\lambda}}\log\left(\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)

(49)

Therefore, the specific heat should go as

\displaystyle\gamma(H)=\gamma(0)+H^{2}A\left(\frac{1}{2}g\hbar\right)^{2}\frac{1}{(F_{0}^{\lambda})^{5}}\frac{\alpha}{p_{F}}\log\left(\frac{T}{T_{\textrm{cut}}}\right)

\textrm{B}^{\lambda}\sim\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\frac{p_{F}}{\alpha}(F_{0}^{\lambda})^{3}

\alpha

F_{0}^{\lambda}

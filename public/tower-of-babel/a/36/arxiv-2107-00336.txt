Introduction

In this article, we establish the following weighted anisotropic Sobolev inequality,

[formula]

S\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}f\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p}w\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall\,u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w),

where
S
is the Sobolev constant,
0<q<1<p<\infty
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

\big(N\geq 2\big)
is a bounded smooth domain. Here
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
is a Finsler-Minkowski norm, i.e.
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the hypothesis from
(H0)-(H4)
given by

(H0)

\mathcal{F}(x)\geq 0
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(H1)

\mathcal{F}(x)=0
, if and only if
x=0
.

(H2)

\mathcal{F}(tx)=|t|\mathcal{F}(x)
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H3)

\mathcal{F}\in C^{\infty}\Big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\Big)
.

(H4)

the Hessian matrix
\nabla_{\eta}^{2}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{F}^{2}}{2}\Big)(x)
is positive definite for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

We assume the weight function
w
in a class of
p
-admissible weights to be discussed in Section
2
and
f\in L^{m}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is some nonnegative function. Our main emphasis is the case of
0<q<1
in
\mathcal{(P)}
and we observe that

[formula]

\mu(\Omega):=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}\Bigg\{\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p}w\,dx:\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}f\,dx=1\Bigg\},

is associated with the following type of weighted singular anisotropic
p
-Laplace equation:

[formula]

-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u=\frac{f(x)}{u^{\delta}}+\frac{g(x)}{u^{\gamma}}\text{ in }\Omega,\,u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega

where
0<\delta,\gamma<1
,
(f,g)\neq(0,0)\in L^{m}(\Omega)
are nonnegative functions. Here,

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u:=\text{div}\left(w\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)\right),

is the weighted anisotropic
p
-Laplace operator, where
\nabla_{\eta}
denotes the gradient with respect to
\eta
. Further, we prove existence and regularity results for the exponential singular problem,

[formula]

-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u=h(x)e^{\frac{1}{u}}\text{ in }\Omega,\,u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
h\in L^{t}(\Omega)
is nonnegative for some
t\geq 1
.

Before proceeding further, let us discuss some examples of
\mathcal{F}
.

Examples:
Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(i)

Then for

(1.2)

\mathcal{F}_{t}(x):=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(ii)

For

For

2.
Preliminaries and main results

Throughout the rest of the article, we assume
1<p<\infty
, unless otherwise mentioned. We say that a function
w
belong to the class of
p
-admissible weights
W_{p}
, if
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
0<w<\infty
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\mu}
such that

\mu(2B)\leq C_{\mu}\,\mu(B),

where

\mu(E)=\int_{E}w\,dx

for a measurable subset
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
d\mu(x)=w(x)\,dx
, where
dx
is the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

(ii)

If
D
is an open set and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}(D)
is a sequence of functions such that

\int_{D}|\phi_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu\to 0\text{ and }\int_{D}|\nabla\phi_{i}-v|^{p}\,d\mu\to 0

as
i\to\infty
, where
v
is a vector valued measurable function in
L^{p}(D,w)
, then
v=0
.

(iii)

There exist constants
\kappa>1
and
C_{1}>0
such that

(2.1)

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\phi|^{\kappa p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa p}}\leq C_{1}r\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

whenever
B=B(x_{0},r)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
.

(iv)

There exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(2.2)

\int_{B}|\phi-\phi_{B}|^{p}\,d\mu\leq C_{2}r^{p}\int_{B}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,d\mu,

whenever
B=B(x_{0},r)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(B)
is bounded. Here

\phi_{B}=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}\phi\,d\mu.

The conditions (i)-(iv) are important in the theory of weighted Sobolev spaces, one can refer to
[
43
]
for more details.

are

Definition 2.1
.

(Weighted Spaces)
Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in W_{p}
. Then the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega,w)
is the class of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that the norm of
u
given by

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

The weighted Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
is the class of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.4)

\|u\|_{1,p,w}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

If
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},w)
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, then we say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,w)
. The weighted Sobolev space with zero boundary value is defined as

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)=\overline{\big(C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{1,p,w}\big)}.

Using the Poincaré inequality from
[
43
]
, the norm defined by (
2.4
) on the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
is equivalent to the norm given by

(2.5)

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}w\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Moreover, the space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)
is a separable and uniformly convex Banach space, see
[
43
]
.

Next, we state an embedding result which is crucial for us. To this end, for
1<p<\infty
, we define the set

(2.6)

I=\Big[\frac{1}{p-1},\infty\Big)\cap\Big(\frac{N}{p},\infty\Big).

Consider the following subclass of
W_{p}
given by

(2.7)

W_{p}^{s}=\Big\{w\in W_{p}:w^{-s}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\,\,\text{for some}\,\,s\in I\Big\}.

Then for
s\in I
, the weight

w(x)=|x|^{\nu}\in W_{p}^{s}\text{ for any }\nu\in\Big(-N,\frac{N}{s}\Big).

Following the lines of the proof of
[
34
, Theorem
2.6
]
based on
[
23
]
the following embedding result holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in W_{p}^{s}
for some
s\in I
. Then the following continuous inclusion maps hold

W^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\hookrightarrow W^{1,p_{s}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\begin{cases}L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{for }p_{s}\leq t\leq p_{s}^{*},\text{ if }1\leq p_{s}<N,\\
L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{ for }1\leq t<\infty,\text{ if }p_{s}=N,\\
C(\overline{\Omega}),&\text{ if }p_{s}>N,\end{cases}

where
p_{s}=\frac{ps}{s+1}\in[1,p)
.
Moreover, the second embedding above is compact except for
t=p_{s}^{*}=\frac{Np_{s}}{N-p_{s}}
, if
1\leq p_{s}<N
. Further, the same result holds for the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
.

Remark 2.3
.

We note that if
0<c\leq w\leq d
for some constants

, then

and thus by the Sobolev embedding, Lemma

and

with

The following result follows from Farkas-Winkert
[

]
and Xia

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(A)

x\cdot\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)=\mathcal{F}(x)
.

(B)

\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(tx)=\text{sign}(t)\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)
.

(C)

|\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)|\leq C
, for some positive constant
C
.

(D)

\mathcal{F}
is strictly convex.

Next, the classical algebraic inequality holds from Peral
[
58
, Lemma A.0.5]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
, such that

(2.8)

\langle|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b,a-b\rangle\geq\begin{cases}C|a-b|^{p},\text{ if }2\leq p<\infty,\\
C\frac{|a-b|^{2}}{(|a|+|b|)^{2-p}},\text{ if }1<p<2.\end{cases}

More generally, we state the Finsler algebraic inequality from
[
9
, Lemma
2.5
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Then, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
, such that

(2.9)

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{F}(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)-\mathcal{F}(y)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(y),x-y\rangle&\geq C\mathcal{F}(x-y)^{p}.\end{split}

Remark 2.7
.

When
2\leq p<\infty
and
\mathcal{F}(x)=\mathcal{F}_{2}(x)=|x|
as given by (
1.2
), Lemma
2.6
coincides with Lemma
2.5
.

Corollary 2.8
.

From Lemma
2.4
and Remark

we have

(2.10)

\mathcal{F}(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)\cdot x=\mathcal{F}(x)^{p}\geq C_{1}|x|^{p},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.11)

\big|\mathcal{F}(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)\big|\leq C_{2}|x|^{p-1},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and}

(2.12)

\mathcal{F}(tx)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(tx)=|t|^{p-2}t\mathcal{F}(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.6
, we have

(2.13)

\langle\mathcal{F}(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(x)-\mathcal{F}(y)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(y),x-y\rangle>0,\quad\forall\,x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Throughout the rest of the article, we assume
1<p<\infty
and
w\in W_{p}^{s}
for some
s\in I
unless otherwise mentioned. We recall that by
\|u\|
we denote the norm
\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}
as defined in (
2.5
). For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we investigate the following approximated problem

(3.1)

\begin{split}-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u=\frac{f_{n}}{(u^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}+\frac{g_{n}}{(u^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
f_{n}(x)=\min\{f(x),n\}
and
g_{n}(x)=\min\{g(x),n\}
, provided
(f,g)\big(\neq(0,0)\big)\in L^{m_{\delta}}(\Omega)\times L^{r_{\gamma}}(\Omega)
is nonnegative, where
m_{\delta}
and
r_{\gamma}
is given by Theorem
2.11
. First we prove the following result that is important to obtain the existence and further qualitative properties of solutions for the problem (
3.1
) as shown in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
and
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
be nonnegative in
\Omega
. Then there exists a unique solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
of the problem

(3.2)

\begin{split}-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u=\xi\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\end{split}

such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}
, satisfying
u\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Existence:
To prove the existence, we define the energy functional
I:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
by

I(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p}w\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\xi u\,dx.

Since
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, using Lemma
2.2
, we have

(3.3)

\begin{split}I(u)&\geq\frac{\|u\|^{p}}{p}-C|\Omega|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|,\end{split}

where
C>0
is the Sobolev constant. Thus
I
is coercive, since
p>1
. Next, we observe that
I
is also convex. Indeed, let us define the energy functional
I_{1}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
by

I_{1}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p}w\,dx,

and
I_{2}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
by

I_{2}(u):=-\int_{\Omega}\xi u\,dx.

By the property
(D)
of Lemma
2.4
, we have
\mathcal{F}^{p}
is convex and hence
I_{1}
is convex. It is easy to see that
I_{2}
is linear and thus
I
is convex. Also,
I
is a
C^{1}
functional. Therefore,
I
is weakly lower semicontinous. As a consequence of coercivity, weak lower semicontinuity and convexity,
I
has a minimizer, say
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
which solves the equation

(3.4)

-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}u=\xi\text{ in }\Omega.

Uniqueness:
Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
solves the problem (
3.4
). Therefore,

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}w(x)\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\xi\phi\,dx,

and

(3.6)

\int_{\Omega}w(x)\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\xi\phi\,dx

holds for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
. We choose
\phi=u_{1}-u_{2}
and then subtracting (
3.5
) with (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega}w(x)\left\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{1})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u_{2})\right\}\nabla\big((u_{1}-u_{2})\big)\,dx=0.

By Lemma
2.6
, we get

\int_{\Omega}\big|\mathcal{F}\big(\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})\big)\big|^{p}w\,dx=0.

Thus
u_{1}=u_{2}
in
\Omega
. Hence the uniqueness follows.

Boundedness:
From Lemma
2.2
, noting the continuity of
X\hookrightarrow L^{l}(\Omega)
for some
l>p
and then proceeding analogous to the proof of
[
9
, Lemma
3.1
]
, we obtain

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C
depending on
\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Positivity:
Choosing
u_{-}:=\min\{u,0\}
as a test function in (
3.4
) and since
\xi\geq 0
, by (
2.10
) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}\big(\nabla u_{-}\big)^{p}w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)\nabla u_{-}w\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\xi u_{-}\,dx\leq 0,\end{split}

which gives,
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Further
g\neq 0
gives
u\neq 0
in
\Omega
. Noting Corollary
2.8
, we apply
[
43
, Theorem 3.59]
so that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
c_{\omega}
such that
u\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\Omega
. Thus
u>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

Let

. Then,

(A)

(Existence) for every

(D)

(Uniform boundedness)

Proof.

(A)

Let

be fixed. By Lemma

, for every

, there exists a unique positive solution

We define the operator
S:L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)
by

where

solves (

). Choosing

as a test function in (

), for some positive constant

Now applying Schauder’s fixed point theorem as in

in
\omega

(B)

Choosing

as a test function in (
3.1
) we have

\begin{split}J&=\langle-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}(u_{n})+\mathcal{F}_{p,w}(u_{n+1}),(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\rangle\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}-\frac{f_{n+1}}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}+\frac{g_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}-\frac{g_{n+1}}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\gamma}}\Big\}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\\
&:=J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

Using the inequalities
f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n+1}(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}-\frac{f_{n+1}}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}\Big\}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{n+1}\Big\{\frac{1}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\delta}}\Big\}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Similarly, using

J_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\frac{g_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\gamma}}-\frac{g_{n+1}}{\big(u_{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}\big)^{\gamma}}\Big\}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Hence, we have
J\leq 0
. Noting this fact and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big|\mathcal{F}\big(\nabla(u_{n}-u_{n+1})^{+}\big)\big|^{p}w\,dx=0.

where in the final step above, we have employed Lemma

Proof.

We only prove the result under the hypothesis in

1\leq p_{s}<N
and

for

as a test function in (

), first using Hölder’s inequality with the exponents

and then, by Young’s inequality with exponents

and

, we obtain

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|\phi_{k}\|^{p}&=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(\nabla\phi_{k})^{p}w\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\phi_{k}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{g_{n}}{\big(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\big)^{\delta}}\phi_{k}\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{A(k)}f(x)\phi_{k}\,dx+\int_{A(k)}g(x)\phi_{k}\,dx\\
&\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{k}^{p_{s}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*}}}+\left(\int_{A(k)}g^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{k}^{p_{s}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*}}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p_{s}^{*}{{}^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}\|\phi_{k}\|+C\left(\int_{A(k)}g^{p_{s}^{*}{{}^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}\|\phi_{k}\|\\
&\leq\epsilon\|\phi_{k}\|^{p}+C(\epsilon)\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}+C(\epsilon)\left(\int_{A(k)}g^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}.\end{split}

Here,
C
is the Sobolev constant from Lemma
2.2
and
C(\epsilon)>0
is some constant depending on
\epsilon\in(0,1)
but are independent of
n
. Note that
q>\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p_{s}^{*}-p}
gives
q>p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}
. Therefore, fixing
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and again using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}
and
\big(\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}
, for some constant
C>0
which is independent of
n
, we obtain

(3.12)

\begin{split}\|\phi_{k}\|^{p}&\leq C\left(\int_{A(k)}f^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}+C\left(\int_{A(k)}g^{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}}\\
&\leq C\Bigg\{\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}g^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}\Bigg\}|A(k)|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Let
h>0
be such that
1\leq k<h
. Then,
A(h)\subset A(k)
and for any
x\in A(h)
, we have
u_{n}(x)\geq h
. So,
u_{n}(x)-k\geq h-k
in
A(h)
. Combining these facts along with (
3.12
) and again using Lemma
2.2
for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
), we arrive at the estimate below

\displaystyle(h-k)^{p}|A(h)|^{\frac{p}{p_{s}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(h)}(u_{n}-k)^{p_{s}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{s}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}(u_{n}-k)^{p_{s}^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{s}^{*}}}\leq C\|\phi_{k}\|^{p}\leq C|A(k)|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.

Thus, for some constant
C>0
(independent of
n
), we have

|A(h)|\leq\frac{C}{(h-k)^{p_{s}^{*}}}|A(k)|^{\alpha},

where

\alpha={\frac{p_{s}^{*}p^{\prime}}{pp_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\big(\frac{q}{p_{s}^{*^{\prime}}}\big)^{\prime}}}.

Due to the assumption,
q>\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p_{s}^{*}-p}
, we have
\alpha>1
. Hence, by
[
45
, Lemma B.1]
, we have

||u_{n}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some positive constant
C>0
independent of
n
.

We end this section by establishing the following properties of
u_{n}
that are very important to prove the weighted anisotropic Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 3.7
.

The solutions
u_{n}
of the problem (
3.1
) found in Lemma
3.3
has the following properties:

(a)

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
. Then we have

(3.13)

\|u_{n}\|^{p}\leq\|\phi\|^{p}+p\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{n}-\phi)}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}f_{n}\,dx+p\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{n}-\phi)}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}g_{n}\,dx.

(b)

(Monotonicity in norm) For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{n+1}\|
.

(c)

(Strong convergence) Upto a subsequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges strongly to
u_{\delta,\gamma}
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
.

(d)

Further,
u_{\delta,\gamma}
is a minimizer of the energy functional
I_{\delta,\gamma}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

(3.14)

\displaystyle I_{\delta,\gamma}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\delta}f\,dx-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\gamma}g\,dx.

Proof.

(a)

Let us fix
\xi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
of the problem

(3.15)

-\mathcal{F}_{p,w}v=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}+\frac{g_{n}(x)}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}},\,v>0\text{ in }\Omega,\,v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Also
v
is a minimizer of the functional
J:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J(\phi):=\frac{1}{p}\|\phi\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\phi\,dx.

Therefore, for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, we have
J(v)\leq J(\phi)
which gives

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}v\,dx\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{p}\|\phi\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{f_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\delta}}\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g_{n}}{(\xi^{+}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\phi\,dx.\end{split}

Setting
v=\xi=u_{n}
in the inequality (
3.16
), the estimate (
3.13
) follows.

(b)

By Lemma
3.3
, we have
u_{n}\leq u_{n+1}
. Then choosing
\phi=u_{n+1}
in (
3.13
), we obtain
\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{n+1}\|
.

(c)

We choose
\phi=u_{\delta,\gamma}
in (
3.13
) and then using the property
u_{n}\leq u_{\delta,\gamma}
from Remark
3.5
, we have
\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{\delta,\gamma}\|
. Hence using the norm monotonicity property
\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{n+1}\|
from
(b)
, we have

(3.17)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|\leq\|u_{\delta,\gamma}\|.

Moreover since
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{\delta,\gamma}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, we get

(3.18)

\|u_{\delta,\gamma}\|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|.

Thus from (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), the result follows.

(d)

It is enough to show that

(3.19)

I_{\delta,\gamma}(u_{\delta,\gamma})\leq I_{\delta,\gamma}(v),\quad\forall\,v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w).

Let us define the auxiliary functional
I_{n}:W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\to\mathbb{R}
by

I_{n}(v):=\frac{1}{p}\|v\|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(v)f_{n}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}H_{n}(v)g_{n}\,dx,

where

G_{n}(t):=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\Big(t^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{1-\delta}-\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{-\delta}t^{-},

and

H_{n}(t):=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\Big(t^{+}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{1-\gamma}-\Big(\frac{1}{n}\Big)^{-\gamma}t^{-}.

Then we observe that
I_{n}
is
C^{1}
, bounded below and coercive. As a consequence,
I_{n}
has a minimizer at some
v_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
. Therefore, it follows that
I_{n}(v_{n})\leq I_{n}(v_{n}^{+})
, which gives
v_{n}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Noting that
<I_{n}{{}^{\prime}}(v_{n}),\phi>=0
for all
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, we conclude that
v_{n}
solves (
3.1
). By the uniqueness property from Lemma
3.3
, we have
u_{n}=v_{n}
. Hence
u_{n}
is a minimizer of
I_{n}
. Therefore, we obtain

(3.20)

I_{n}(u_{n})\leq I_{n}(v+),\quad\forall\,v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w).

Now we pass the limit as
n\to\infty
in (
3.20
) to prove our claim (
3.19
).
Firstly, by Remark
3.5
using the fact
u_{n}\leq u_{\delta,\gamma}
along with the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

(3.21)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(u_{n})f_{n}\,dx&=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(u_{\delta,\gamma})^{1-\delta}f\,dx,\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}H_{n}(u_{n})g_{n}\,dx&=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}(u_{\delta,\gamma})^{1-\gamma}g\,dx.\end{split}

Furthermore, by the strong convergence property
(c)
above, we have

(3.22)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|=\|u_{\delta,\gamma}\|.

Hence, using (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), we have

(3.23)

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{n}(u_{n})=I_{\delta,\gamma}(u_{\delta,\gamma}).

Moreover, for any
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, we have

(3.24)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G_{n}(v^{+})f_{n}\,dx&=\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\delta}f\,dx,\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}H_{n}(v^{+})g_{n}\,dx&=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{1-\delta}g\,dx.\end{split}

Finally, letting
n\to\infty
in (
3.20
) and then employing the estimates (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and that
\|v^{+}\|\leq\|v\|
, the claim (
3.19
) follows.

and also lie in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
due to the property
(D)
from Lemma
4.1
. As a consequence of our definition, the diagonal subsequence
\{v_{n_{l}}\}:=\{v_{n_{l}}^{l}\}
satisfies

(5.15)

\begin{split}v_{n_{l}}&\to v\text{ in }W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega_{l},w),\\
v_{n_{l}}&\to v\text{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega_{l}),\\
v_{n_{l}}&\to v\text{ almost everywhere in }\Omega_{l}.\end{split}

We claim that
\{v_{n_{l}}\}
converges strongly to
v
in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega_{l},w)
. To see this, let
\omega\Subset\Omega
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
in
\Omega
,
\phi\equiv 1
in
\omega
and suppose
l_{1}\geq 1
such that
\Omega^{\prime}:=\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset\Omega_{l_{1}}
. Then, by a direct computation for every
l,m\geq 1
, we have

(5.16)

\begin{split}&\int_{\omega}w(x)\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})\}\nabla(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}w(x)\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})\}\nabla\big(\phi(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\big)\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega_{l_{1}}}w(x)(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})\}\nabla\phi\,dx\\
&:=I-J.\end{split}

Estimate of
I
:
We choose
\phi(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})
as a test function in (
4.1
) and obtain for
j=l,m
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w(x)\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{j}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{j}})\nabla\big(\phi(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}h_{n}e^{\frac{1}{\big(v_{n_{j}}+\frac{1}{j}\big)}}|v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})},

for a constant
c
independent of
l,m
. Therefore, using (
5.15
), the last quantity in the above estimate goes to zero as
l,m\to\infty
. As a consequence, we arrive at

I=\int_{\Omega}w(x)\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})\}\nabla\big(\phi(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\big)\,dx\to 0

as
l,m\to\infty
.

Estimate of
J
:
Using (
2.11
) and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{l_{1}}}w(x)(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})\nabla\phi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w|\nabla v_{n_{l}}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}w|v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Noting (
5.15
) and
[
50
, Theorem
2.14
]
, the last quantity in the above estimate goes to zero as
l,m\to\infty
. Therefore,

J=\int_{\Omega_{l_{1}}}w(x)(v_{n_{l}}-v_{n_{m}})\{\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})-\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{m}})\}\nabla\phi\,dx\to 0

as
l,m\to\infty
. Using the above estimates on
I
and
J
, applying the Finsler algebraic inequality from Lemma
2.6
in (
5.16
), the sequence
\{v_{n_{l}}\}
converges strongly to
v
in
W^{1,p}(\omega,w)
. Now we pass to the limit in (
4.1
) and prove that
v
is our required solution. To this end, assume that
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset\Omega_{l_{1}}
for some
l_{1}\geq 1
. Then by the strong convergence of
v_{n_{l}}
to
v
in
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,w)
, we have

(5.17)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}w(x)\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v_{n_{l}})\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}w(x)\mathcal{F}(\nabla v)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla v)\nabla\phi\,dx.

Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\left|h_{n_{l}}e^{\frac{1}{\big(v_{n_{l}}+\frac{1}{n_{l}}\big)}}\phi\right|\leq ch\in{L^{1}(\Omega)},

for some constant
c>0
independent of
l
. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have

(5.18)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}h_{n}(x)e^{\frac{1}{\big(v_{n_{l}}(x)+\frac{1}{n_{l}}\big)}}\phi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}h(x)e^{\frac{1}{v(x)}}\phi(x)\,dx.

Thus, from (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), we conclude that
v\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega,w)
is a weak solution of the problem
\mathcal{(R)}
. Further, it can be easily seen that
\{(v_{n_{l}}-\epsilon)^{+}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
for every
\epsilon>0
and hence
(v-\epsilon)^{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
. Since by Lemma
4.1
, we have
v_{n_{l}}\geq c_{\omega}>0
for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, we obtain
v\geq c_{\omega}>0
in
\omega
. Hence
v>0
in
\Omega
and the result follows. ∎

Proof of Theorem
2.22
:
Noting Lemma
2.5
along with Remark
4.2
and then following the same proof of Theorem
2.21
, the result follows. ∎

Comment on “Approximation algorithms for quadratic programming” Thanks: This research was supported by the Beijing Natural Science Foundation under grant Z180005, and the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grants 12171021 and 11822103.
Tongli Zhang, Yong Xia

Abstract
The radius of the outer Dikin ellipsoid of the intersection of m m ellipsoids due to Fu et al. (J. Comb. Optim., 2, 29-50, 1998) is corrected from m m to m 2 + m \sqrt{m^{2}+m} . The approximation bound for the general convex quadratic constrained nonconvex quadratic program is correspondingly corrected.



∎

1
Corrections

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the intersection of
m
ellipsoids:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~g_{i}(x):=d_{i}-c_{i}^{T}x-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Q_{i}x\geq 0,~i=1,\cdots,m\right\},

where
Q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is symmetric and positive semidefinite,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. The logarithmic barrier function for
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

L(x):=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\log(g_{i}(x))
with gradient and Hessian being given by

\nabla L(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{c_{i}+Q_{i}x}{g_{i}(x)},~\nabla^{2}L(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\left(c_{i}+Q_{i}x\right)\left(c_{i}+Q_{i}x\right)^{T}}{g_{i}^{2}(x)}+\frac{Q_{i}}{g_{i}(x)}\right).

The analytic center of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the unique minimizer of
L(x)
, denoted by
\bar{x}
. The Dikin ellipsoid is defined as the following ellipsoid centered at
\bar{x}
with radius
r>0
:

\displaystyle E\left(\bar{x};r\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left(x-\bar{x}\right)^{T}\nabla^{2}L\left(\bar{x}\right)\left(x-\bar{x}\right)\leq r^{2}\right\}.

It was proved in
Nesterov1993
that

E(\bar{x};1)\subseteq\mathcal{F}.

As presented in Theorem
3
in
Fu1998
, Fu et al. claimed that

\mathcal{F}\subseteq E(\bar{x};m).

(1)

It serves as a fundamental theorem in developing approximation algorithms
Fu1998
for the convex quadratic constrained quadratic programming problem:

{\rm(QP)}~~{\rm min}_{x\in\mathcal{F}}~q(x):=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Qx+c^{T}x.

With regret, a typo in the proof makes (
1
) incorrect. It can be seen from the following simple example.

Example 1

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x^{2}\leq 1\right\}
.
The logarithmic barrier function is

L(x)=-\log(1-x^{2})
and
\nabla^{2}L(x)=2(1+x^{2})/(1-x^{2})^{2}
.
It is not difficult to verify that the analytic center of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is
0
and
\nabla^{2}L(0)=2
. Then we have

E(0;1)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~2x^{2}\leq 1\}.

Clearly,
E(0;1)\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}\nsubseteq E(0;m)=E(0;1)
.

Fortunately, (
1
) can be corrected as

Theorem 1.1

It holds that

\mathcal{F}\subseteq E(\bar{x};\sqrt{m^{2}+m}).

The proof is very similar to that of Theorem
3
in
Fu1998
. For completeness, we leave it in the appendix.

Let us reconsider Example
1
. We can verify that
\mathcal{F}_{1}=E(0;\sqrt{2})
, which implies that the corrected Theorem
1.1
is tight for this example.

For (QP) and
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, we call
x\in\mathcal{F}
an
\epsilon
-minimizer if it satisfies that

\frac{q(x)-\underline{z}}{\overline{z}-\underline{z}}\leq\epsilon,

where
\underline{z}
and
\overline{z}
are the minimum and maximum of
q(x)
over
\mathcal{F}
, respectively.

The approximation bound of Fu et al.’s approximation algorithm for solving (QP) (see Theorem 4 in
Fu1998
) is correspondingly corrected as follows.

Theorem 1.2

For all
\epsilon\in(0,1-1/\sqrt{2}]
and all
m\geq 2
, there exists a polynomial-time approximation algorithm for computing an
(1-\frac{1-\epsilon}{c(m)(1+\epsilon)^{2}})
-minimizer of
q(x)
over
\mathcal{F}
, where
c(m)=m^{2}+m
.

When
m=1
, (QP) is known as the trust-region subproblem (TRS) that can be solved in polynomial time
Fu1998
.
Celis-Dennis-Tapia (CDT) subproblem is a notable special case of (QP) with

m=2
. Theorem
1.2
implies that an
\left(1-(1-\epsilon)/(6(1+\epsilon)^{2})\right)
-minimizer for (CDT) can be computed in polynomial time.

2
Appendix

Proof of Theorem
1.1
. Denote
g_{i}(x)
,
g_{i}(\bar{x})
and
\nabla L(\bar{x})
by
g_{i}
,
\bar{g}_{i}
and
\nabla\bar{L}
, respectively. Let

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(x-\bar{x})^{T}Q_{i}(x-\bar{x}),~i=1,\cdots,m.

By Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle-(c_{i}+Q_{i}\bar{x})^{T}(x-\bar{x})=g_{i}-\bar{g}_{i}+\Delta_{i}.

(2)

Since
\bar{x}
is the analytic center of
\mathcal{F}
, it follows from
\nabla L(\bar{x})=0
and (
2
) that

0=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(c_{i}+Q_{i}\bar{x})^{T}(x-\bar{x})}{\bar{g}_{i}}=m-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g_{i}}},

which further implies that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}=m-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{g_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\leq m,

\displaystyle m^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\right)^{2}+2\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{g_{j}+\Delta_{j}}{\bar{g}_{j}}\right)\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\right)^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(x-\bar{x})^{T}\nabla^{2}L(\bar{x})(x-\bar{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{((c_{i}+Q_{i}\bar{x})^{T}(x-\bar{x}))^{2}}{g_{i}^{2}(\bar{x})}+\frac{(x-\bar{x})^{T}Q_{i}(x-\bar{x})}{g_{i}(\bar{x})}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}-1\right)^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{2\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\right)^{2}-2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{g_{i}+\Delta_{i}}{\bar{g}_{i}}\right)+1\right\}+2m

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m^{2}-2m+m+2m=m^{2}+m.

The proof is completed.
\Box

We remark that the authors in
Fu1998
mistook
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}2\Delta_{i}/\bar{g}_{i}
for
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{i}/\bar{g}_{i}
in (
3
), which leads to the incorrect result.

Parabolic Anderson model with rough noise in space and rough initial conditions
Raluca M. Balan, Le Chen, Yiping Ma

Abstract
In this note, we consider the parabolic Anderson model on ℝ + × ℝ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R} , driven by a Gaussian noise which is fractional in time with index H 0 > 1 / 2 H_{0}>1/2 and fractional in space with index 0 < H < 1 / 2 0<H<1/2 such that H 0 + H > 3 / 4 H_{0}+H>3/4 . Under a general condition on the initial data, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the mild solution and obtain its exponential upper bounds in time for all p p -th moments with p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 .

Remark 1.2
.

When the initial condition is a bounded function or a constant, the well-posedness was obtained by
X. Chen; see Theorem 1.2 of
[
7
]
. The extension of X. Chen’s work to rough
initial conditions is highly nontrivial. As we will see in the proof of Theorem
1.1
below,
to estimate the moments of the solution, we need to compute a spatial integral and a time integral.
In
[
7
]
, X. Chen uses the Laplace transform to handle the time integral first
and then computes the spatial integral. This method does not work for the rough initial data. In a
nutshell, moving from bounded initial data to rough initial data, one essentially changes the
underlying motion from the Brownian motion to the Brownian bridge. The time increments no longer
take the linear difference form; see Lemma
2.1
. To overcome this difficulty, we compute
the spatial integral first and then use Lemma
A.1
to compute the time integral. This leads
to a complicated expression which contains a product
\gamma_{n}
of ratios of gamma functions; see
(
2.9
). The most delicate part is to estimate
\gamma_{n}
. For this, we develop some novel
combinatorial techniques.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{1}>-1
,
\beta_{i}>-1
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1+\alpha_{k+1}>0\quad\mbox{for all}\ k=1,\ldots,n-1.

(A.1)

Then by setting
t_{n+1}=t
,
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
and

|\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle(t,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}):=\int_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}(t_{i+1}-t_{i})^{\beta_{i}}\mathrm{d}{\bf t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{i}+1)}{\Gamma\big(|\alpha|+|\beta|+n+1\big)}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1+\alpha_{k+1}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i})+k+1\big)}t^{|\alpha|+|\beta|+n}.

(A.2)

Proof.

The lemma is proved by induction. For
n=1
, we have:

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\alpha,\beta)=\int_{0}^{t}t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(t-t_{1})^{\beta_{1}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)\Gamma(\beta_{1}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+2)}t^{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1}.

For the induction step, we use the fact that

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})=\int_{0}^{t}t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}(t-t_{n})^{\beta_{n}}I_{n-1}(t_{n},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})\mathrm{d}t_{n}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For any
a>0
, the function
z\mapsto\Gamma(z+a)/\Gamma(z)
is non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=\Gamma(z+a)/\Gamma(z)
. Note that
f^{\prime}(z)=f(z)\left(\psi(z+a)-\psi(z)\right)
, where

\psi(z):=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
is the
psi function
; see 5.2.2 on p. 136 of

[
12
]
. By the following expression

\displaystyle\psi(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+z}\right)\quad\mbox{for any}\quad z>0,

with
\gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-1}-\ln n)\approx 0.5772
being the Euler’s
constant (see, e.g., 5.7.6 on p. 139
ibid.
), we see that
\psi
is a nondecreasing function
on
(0,\infty)
. Hence,
f^{\prime}(z)\geq 0
for all
z>0
, which implies the desired result.
∎

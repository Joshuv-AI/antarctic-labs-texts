Meromorphic mappings into projective varieties with arbitrary families of moving hypersurfaces
Si Duc Quang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a general second main theorem for meromorphic mappings into a subvariety V V of ℙ N ​ ( ℂ ) {\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}}) with an arbitrary family of moving hypersurfaces. Our second main theorem generalizes and improves all previous results for meromorphic mappings with moving hypersurfaces, in particular for meromorphic mappings and families of moving hypersurfaces in subgeneral position. The method of our proof is different from that of previous authors used for the case of moving hypersurfaces.

Introduction

In 1926, R. Nevanlinna
[
9
]
initially established a second main theorem for meromorphic functions and fixed values in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
. Later on, in 1930 H. Cartan
[
1

extended the result of Nevanlinna to the case of linearly nondegenerate meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with hyperplanes in general position. By introducing the notion of Nochka’s weights, E. Nochka
[
10
]
established the second main theorem for such mappings with families of hyperplanes in subgeneral position.

In some few recent years, the second main theorem for hypersurfaces (fixed or moving) is studied intensively by many authors. Firstly, In 1992, A. E. Eremenko and M. L. Sodin
[
5
]
proved a second main theorem for arbitrary holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
intersecting
q
hypersurfaces
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
in general position. In 2004 M. Ru
[
17
]
considered the case of algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with families of hypersurfaces in general position by using the filtration of the vector space of homogeneous polynomial of P. Corvaja and U. Zannier
[
2
]
. Then, in
[
18
]
he generalized his results to the case of algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mapping into a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
by using the method in Diophantine approximation proposed by J. Evertse and R. Ferretti
[
6
]
. His results are generalized by S. D. Quang for the case of families of hypersurfaces in subgeneral position in
[
14
]
, where he proposed the replacing hypersurfaces method to avoid using Nochka’s weights.

On the other hand, the results of M. Ru also generalized to the case of moving hypersurfaces by G. Dethloff and T. V. Tan
[
3
,
4
]
. In oder to establish the second main theorem for the case of moving hypersurfaces in general position, G. Dethloff and T. V. Tan have constructed a new filtration instead of the filtration of Corvaja and Zannier. Also in 2018, S. D. Quang
[
13
]
established the second main theorem for the case of moving hypersurfaces in subgeneral position. Recently Q. Yan and G. Yu
[

]
used the methods of
[

,
14
,
4
]
to give a second main theorem for nonconstant meromorphic mappings, but their second main theorem does not give the normal form of Cartan’s theorem as usual. Here, we will state here the normal form of the second main theorems given by Dethloff-Tan and Quang as follows.

We recall the following. Denote by

the field of all meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. We call a moving hypersurface of degree

a homogeneous polynomial

of the form:

Q(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}(z){\bf x}^{I},

where

is the set of all

with

are not all zeros,

,

for all
I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
.
For each

and is not a common zero of all

, we denote by

the support of

Q(z)^{*}=\{(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{N})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}(z){\bf x}^{I}=0\}.

We may consider

{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{N+d}{N}-1}({\mathbb{C}})
with a reduced representation
(\cdots:ha_{I}:\cdots)

The moving hypersurface

is said to be slow with respect to a meromorphic mapping

from

into

if
\|T_{a_{I}}(r)=o(T_{f}(r))\ \forall I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}

\|T_{Q}(r)=o(T_{f}(r))
. Here the notion “
\|P

outside a finite Borel measure set.

Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
,
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}
. Assume that

Q_{i}(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d_{i}}}a_{iI}(z){\bf x}^{I}.

Denote by
{\mathcal{K}}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
the smallest subfield of
\mathcal{M}
(the field of all meromorphic functions on

) which contains

Let

of dimension

is in weakly general position in

if there exists

such that all
a_{iI}\ (1\leq i\leq q,\ I\in\mathcal{I})
are holomorphic at

V\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{n}Q_{i_{j}}(z)^{*}=\varnothing.

(see
[

]
).

Let

{\mathbb{C}}

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}(\mathbf{C})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q).
Let
n_{f}
be the algebraic dimension of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
and let
\Delta_{f}
be the distributive constant of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
with respect to
f
. Suppose that
f
has finite algebraic degree over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\|\ (q-\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d_{i}}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

Hence, if
\Delta_{f}=1
, i.e.,
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is in weakly general position with respect to
f
, we will get the sharp second main theorem

\|\ (q-(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d_{i}}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

If we assume further that
f
comes into a subvariety
V
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
n
, and

is algebraically nondegenerate over

, then

,
\Delta_{f}=\Delta_{V}
, the algebraic degree of
f
over
\mathcal{K}
is equal to the degree of
V
. Hence, we have the following corrolary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into a subvariety
V
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}(\mathbf{C})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces with
\deg Q_{j}=d_{j}\ (1\leq i\leq q).
Assume that
f
is algebraically nondegenerate over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
. Let
\Delta_{V}
be the distributive constant of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
with respect to
V
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\|\ (q-\Delta_{V}(\dim V+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d_{i}}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

We see that, if
\Delta_{V}=1
(i.e.,

3.
Distributive constant and lemma on replacing moving hypersurfaces

Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be
q
moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
given by

Q_{i}(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d_{i}}}a_{iI}(z){\bf x}^{I},

where
{\bf x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})
,
{\bf x}^{I}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}
for

. Denote simply by
\mathcal{K}
the field
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}}
the set of all non-negative functions

, which are of the form

h=\dfrac{|g_{1}|+\cdots+|g_{l}|}{|g_{l+1}|+\cdots+|g_{l+k}|},

where
k,l\in\mathbb{N},\ g_{1},....,g_{l+k}\in\mathcal{K}\setminus\{0\}
and

, which may depend on

Lemma 3.8

V

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
be
n+1
integers such that
1=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}
, and let
\Delta={\mathrel{\mathop{{\max}}\limits_{{1\leq s\leq n}}}}\dfrac{t_{s}-t_{0}}{s}

with

Let
s
be an index,
1\leq s\leq n
such that
\Delta=\dfrac{t_{s}-t_{0}}{s}
.

•

•

\Delta=\dfrac{t_{s}-t_{0}}{s}\geq\dfrac{t_{s+k}-t_{0}}{s+k}

\Delta=\dfrac{t_{s}-t_{0}}{s}\geq\dfrac{t_{s+k}-t_{0}-(t_{s}-t_{0})}{(s+k)-s}=\dfrac{t_{s+k}-t_{k}}{k},\text{ i.e., }t_{s+k}-t_{s}\leq k\Delta.

and define

\displaystyle m_{n-1}

\displaystyle=t_{n}-t_{n-1}+\max\{0,m_{n}-\Delta\},

\displaystyle m_{n-2}

\displaystyle=t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}+\max\{0,m_{n-1}-\Delta\},

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=t_{1}-t_{0}+\max\{0,m_{1}-\Delta\}.

is not empty since

. We set

Suppose that

, and hence we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle m_{u-1}

\displaystyle=t_{u}-t_{u-1}>\Delta,

\displaystyle m_{u-2}

\displaystyle=t_{u-1}-t_{u-2}+m_{u-1}-\Delta=t_{u}-t_{u-2}-\Delta>\Delta,

\displaystyle m_{u-3}

\displaystyle=t_{u-2}-t_{u-3}+m_{u-2}-\Delta=t_{u}-t_{u-3}-2\Delta>\Delta,

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=t_{s+1}-t_{s}+m_{s+1}-\Delta=t_{u}-t_{s}-(u-s-1)\Delta>\Delta.

This implies that

t_{u}-t_{s}>(u-s)\Delta.

This is a contradiction. Therefore

, and hence

.

We also have the following estimate:

\displaystyle m_{s-1}

\displaystyle=t_{s}-t_{s-1}\geq\Delta,

\displaystyle m_{s-2}

\displaystyle=t_{s-1}-t_{s-2}+m_{s-1}-\Delta=t_{s}-t_{s-2}-\Delta\geq 2\Delta-\Delta=\Delta,

\displaystyle m_{s-3}

\displaystyle=t_{s-2}-t_{s-3}+m_{s-2}-\Delta=t_{s}-t_{s-3}-2\Delta\geq 3\Delta-2\Delta=\Delta,

\displaystyle\ldots

) moving hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then for every
\epsilon>0,

\biggl\|\ \int_{S(r)}\max_{K}\log\left(\prod_{j\in K}\frac{\|{\bf f}\|}{|H_{j}({\bf f})|}\right)\sigma_{m}\leq(N+1+\epsilon)T_{f}(r),

where the maximum is taken over all subsets
K\subset\{1,...,q\}
such that
\{H_{j}\ ;\ j\in K\}
is linearly independent over the field
\mathcal{M}
.

The version of this theorem for fixed hyperplanes is firstly proved by M. Ru in
[
16
, Theorem 2.3]
. For our purpose in this paper, we need this version for moving hyperplanes.

Proof.

By adding more fixed hyperplanes if necessary, we will prove that

\biggl\|\ \int_{S(r)}\max_{K}\log\left(\prod_{j\in K}\frac{\|{\bf f}\|}{|H_{j}({\bf f})|}\right)\sigma_{m}\leq(N+1+\epsilon)T_{f}(r),

where the maximum is taken over all subsets
K\subset\{1,...,q\}
such that
\sharp K=N+1
and
\{H_{j}|j\in K\}
is linearly independent over the field
\mathcal{M}
of all meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

H_{i}(z)(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})=\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{ij}(z)x_{j},

where all
a_{ij}
are small functions with respect to
f
and
a_{i0}=1
.
We put
\Psi=\{a_{ij}\}
. By Lemma
3.3
there exists a positive integer
p
such that

\frac{\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p+1))}{\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p))}\leq 1+\dfrac{\epsilon}{N+1}.

We put

s=\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p)),\qquad t=\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p)+1)).

Let
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\}
be a base of
\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p))
and
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\}
be a base of
\mathcal{L}(\Psi(p+1))
. Then
\dfrac{t}{s}\leq 1+\epsilon
and

\{b_{j}f_{k}(1\leq i\leq t,0\leq k\leq N)\}
is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
K=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N+1}\}
be a set such that
\{H_{j}|j\in K\}
is linearly independent over
\mathcal{M}
, where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{N+1}\leq q
. It is easy to see that
\{b_{j}H_{i_{v}}({\bf f})(1\leq j\leq s,1\leq v\leq N+1)\}
is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then we may choose
\beta^{kl}_{mj}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that there is
C_{K}\in GL((N+1)t;{\mathbb{C}})
such that

\displaystyle\det(b_{j}H_{i_{v}}({\bf f})(1\leq j\leq s,1\leq v\leq N+1),h_{ul}(s+1\leq l\leq t,0\leq u\leq N))

\displaystyle=C_{K}\det(b_{j}f_{k}(1\leq j\leq t,0\leq k\leq N)),

where
h_{ul}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq t,0\leq l^{\prime}\leq N}\beta^{kl^{\prime}}_{uj}b_{k}f_{l^{\prime}}(s+1\leq j\leq t,0\leq u\leq N)
, and
C_{K}
is a nonzero constant.

Let
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{(N+1)t})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m})^{(N+1)t}

be the admissible set such that

W\equiv\det\bigl(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha_{w}}b_{j}f_{k}(1\leq j\leq t,0\leq k\leq N)\bigl)_{1\leq w\leq(N+1)t}\not\equiv 0.

Note that
|\alpha_{i}|\leq(N+1)t-1,\forall 1\leq i\leq(N+1)t
.
Set

W_{K}\equiv\det\bigl(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha_{w}}b_{j}H_{i_{v}}({\bf f}),\mathcal{D}^{\alpha_{w}}h_{ul}\bigl),

where
1\leq j\leq t,1\leq v\leq N+1
,
s+1\leq l\leq t,0\leq u\leq N
, and
1\leq w\leq(N+1)t
.
It is easy to see that
W_{K}=C_{K}\cdot W
.

This implies that, for
K=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N+1}\}
,

\displaystyle s\log\left(\prod_{j\in K}\frac{\|{\bf f}\|}{|H_{j}({\bf f})|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(N+1)t\log\|{\bf f}\|+\log\left(\frac{1}{(\prod_{j\in K}|H_{j}({\bf f})|)^{s}\|{\bf f}\|^{(N+1)(t-s)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(N+1)\log\|{\bf f}\|+\sum_{K}\log^{+}\left(\frac{|W_{K}|}{(\prod_{j\in K}|H_{j}({\bf f})|)^{s}\|{\bf f}\|^{(N+1)(t-s)}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\log|W|+O(1).

Integrating both sides of the above of the above inequality over
S(r)
and applying the lemma on logarithmic derivative, we get

\displaystyle\biggl\|\ \int_{S(r)}\max_{K}\log\left(\prod_{j\in K}\frac{\|{\bf f}\|}{|H_{j}({\bf f})|}\right)\sigma_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(N+1)t}{s}T_{f}(r)-\dfrac{1}{s}N_{W}(r)+o(T_{f}(r))

\displaystyle\leq(N+1+\epsilon)T_{f}(r).

The theorem is proved.
∎

where

(4.4)

\displaystyle c_{i,j,z}:=\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}\|P_{i,j}(z)\|}{|P_{i,j}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n_{0}\text{ and }j=0,\ldots,n_{f}.

We see that
c_{i,j,z}\geq 0
for all
i
and
j
. By the definition of the Hilbert weight, there are
{\bf a}_{1,z},\ldots,{\bf a}_{\xi_{u}-p,z}\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
with

{\bf a}_{i,z}=(a_{i,1,0,z},\ldots,a_{i,1,n_{f},z},\ldots,a_{i,n_{0},0,z},\ldots,a_{i,n_{0},n_{f},z}),

where
a_{i,j,s,z}\in\{1,\ldots,\xi_{u}\},
such that the residue classes modulo
(I_{Y})_{u}
of
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{1,z}},\ldots,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{\xi_{u}-p,z}}
form a basic of
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}]_{u}/(I_{Y_{z}})_{u}
and

(4.5)

\displaystyle S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi_{u}-p}{\bf a}_{i,z}\cdot{\bf c}_{z}.

We see that
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{i,z}}\in\mathcal{T}
. Then as above
\{\tilde{R}_{1}(z),\ldots,\tilde{R}_{p}(z),{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{1,z}},\ldots,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{\xi_{u}-p,z}}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}]
. Therefore,
\{{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{1,z}},\ldots,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{\xi_{u}-p,z}}\}
is a basis of
\dfrac{\mathcal{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}]_{u}}{I(Y)_{u}}
. Then, we may write

{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{i,z}}=L_{i,z}(v_{0},\ldots,v_{H_{Y}(u)})\ \ \ \text{modulo }I(Y)_{u},

where
L_{i,z}\ (1\leq i\leq\xi_{u}-p)
are independent linear forms with coefficients in
\mathcal{K}
.
We have

\displaystyle\log\prod_{i=1}^{\xi_{u}-p}|L_{i,z}({\bf F}(z))|

\displaystyle=\log\prod_{i=1}^{\xi_{u}-p}\prod_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}\limits^{1\leq t\leq n_{0}}}}}|P_{t,j}({\bf f}(z))|^{a_{i,t,j,z}}

\displaystyle=-S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})+du(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf f}(z)\|+O(u(\xi_{u}-p)).

It follows that

\displaystyle\log\prod_{i=1}^{\xi_{u}-p}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L_{i,z}\|}{|L_{i,z}({\bf F}(z))|}=

\displaystyle S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})-du(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf f}(z)\|

\displaystyle+(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|+O(u(\xi_{u}-p)).

Note that the number of these linear forms
L_{i,z}
is finite. Denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of all
L_{i,z}
occurring in the above inequalities. We have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})\leq&\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf f}(z))|}+du(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf f}(z)\|\\
&-(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|+O(u(\xi_{u}-p)),\end{split}

where the maximum is taken over all subsets
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}
with
\sharp\mathcal{J}=H_{Y}(u)
and
\{L|L\in\mathcal{J}\}
is linearly independent over
\mathcal{K}
.
From Theorem
2.3
we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{u(\xi_{u}-p)}S_{Y_{z}}(u,{\bf c}_{z})\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}e_{Y_{z}}({\bf c}_{z})-\frac{(2n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}{u}\max_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}c_{i,j,z}

We chose an index
i_{0}
such that
z\in S(i_{0})
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}c_{i,j,z}\leq\sum_{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}\|P_{i_{0},j}\|}{|P_{i_{0},j}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}+O(1),

where the term
O(1)
does not depend on
z
and
i_{0}
.
Combining (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and the above remark, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}e_{Y_{z}}({\bf c}_{z})\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{u(\xi_{u}-p)}\left(\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}-(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|\right)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&+d\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|+\frac{(2n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}{u}\max_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}c_{i,j,z}+O(1/u)\\
\leq&\dfrac{1}{u(\xi_{u}-p)}\left(\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}-(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|\right)\\
&+d\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|+\frac{(2n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}{u}\sum_{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}\|P_{i_{0},j}\|}{|P_{i_{0},j}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}+O(1/u).\end{split}

Since
\{P_{i_{0},0}(z)=\cdots=P_{i_{0},n_{f}}(z)=0\}\cap V_{z}=\varnothing
, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle e_{Y_{z}}({\bf c}_{z})\geq(c_{i_{0},0,z}+\cdots+c_{i_{0},n_{f},z})\cdot\delta_{z}=\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}\|P_{i_{0},j}(z)\|}{|P_{i_{0},j}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}\right)\cdot\delta_{z},

for all
z\not\in\mathcal{S}
.

Then, from (
4.4
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{f}}\log\prod_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}}{|Q_{i}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{n_{f}+1}{u(\xi_{u}-p)}\left(\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}-(\xi_{u}-p)\log\|{\bf F}(z)\|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta_{z}}{u}\sum_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}\log\frac{\|{\bf f}(z)\|^{d}\|P_{i,j}(z)\|}{|P_{i,j}(z)({\bf f}(z))|}

\displaystyle+d(n_{f}+1)\log\|{\bf f}(z)\|+\frac{1}{\Delta_{f}}\log C(z)+O(1)

for generic points
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, where the term
O(1)
does not depend on
z
.
Integrating both sides of the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{q}m_{f}(r,Q_{i})\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)}{du(\xi_{u}-p)}\left(\int\limits_{S(r)}\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}\sigma_{m}-(\xi_{u}-p)T_{F}(r)\right)

(4.10)

\displaystyle+\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)T_{f}(r)+\frac{\Delta_{f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta_{f}}{du}\sum_{{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}}m_{f}(r,P_{i,j})

(note that
\delta_{z}\leq d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}
for all
z\not\in\mathcal{S}
). On the other hand, by Theorem
4.2
, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\biggl\|\ \int\limits_{S(r)}\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}&\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}\sigma_{m}-(\xi_{u}-p)T_{F}(r)\\
&\leq\dfrac{\epsilon(\xi_{u}-p)}{2\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)}T_{F}(r)\leq\dfrac{du\epsilon(\xi_{u}-p)}{2\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)}T_{f}(r).\end{split}

Combining (
4.10
) with (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|\ (q-\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)\bigl)T_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r)+\frac{\Delta_{f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta_{f}}{ud}\sum_{{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n_{f}}}}}}}m_{f}(r,P_{i,j})

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}T_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r)+\epsilon T_{f}(r)\ \ (\text{ by choosing $u$ large enough}).

The theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

(a) By the proof of Theorem
4.2
, the inequality (
4.11
) can be replaced by

\biggl\|\ \int\limits_{S(r)}\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|{\bf F}(z)\|\cdot\|L\|}{|L({\bf F}(z))|}\sigma_{m}-(\xi_{u}-p)T_{F}(r)\leq\dfrac{1}{s}N_{W(F)}(r)+\dfrac{\epsilon(\xi_{u}-p)}{2\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)}T_{F}(r).

where
W(F)
is a general Wronskian of
\{b_{j}v_{i}(\tilde{\Phi}\circ{\bf f});1\leq j\leq t,0\leq i\leq n_{u}\}
;
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}\}
is a basis of
\Psi(q^{\prime}+1)
with a positive integer
q^{\prime}
,
\Psi
is the set of all coefficients of
Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
and
s=\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(q^{\prime}))
.
Then, the second main theorem can be obtained as follows:

\|\ (q-\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon\bigl)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r)-\dfrac{1}{s}N_{W(F)}(r).

Since the number
q^{\prime}
and the dimensions
s=\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(q^{\prime})),t=\dim\mathcal{L}(\Psi(q^{\prime}+1))
can be explicitly estimated as (
4.1
) (bound above by an function of
q
and
\epsilon
), the right hand side of the above inequality will be bounded above by a sum of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[L(q,\epsilon)]}_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r)
, where
L(q,\epsilon)
is explicitly estimated. Hence, we may get the second main theorem with truncation level for counting functions as follows.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}(\mathbf{C})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q).
Let
n_{f}
be the algebraic dimension of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
and let
\Delta_{f}
be the distributive constant of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
with respect to
f
. Suppose that
f
has finite algebraic degree over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
L(q,\epsilon)
such that

\|\ (q-\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[L(q,\epsilon)]}_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

(b) If the image of
f
is contained in a subvariety
V
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
, then
1\leq n_{f}\leq\dim V
and by Remark
3.5
we have
\Delta_{f}\leq(\dim V-n_{f}+1)\Delta_{V},
and hence

\|\ (q-\Delta_{V}(\dim V-n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[L(q,\epsilon)]}_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

Since
(\dim V-n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\leq\left(\dfrac{\dim V}{2}+1\right)^{2}
, we get the second main theorem

\biggl\|\ \left(q-\Delta_{V}\left(\dfrac{\dim V}{2}+1\right)^{2}-\epsilon\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[L(q,\epsilon)]}_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

(c) With the help of Lemma
3.8
, using the construction method for the new filtralation of G. Dethloff and T. V. Tan
[
4
]
(see also
[
20
, Remark 3.1]
) and repeating the routine way in the proof of second main theorem, we may get the following theorem without the condition on the algebraic degree of
f
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}(\mathbf{C})
with a reduced representation
{\bf f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
. Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q).
Let
n_{f}
be the algebraic dimension of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}}
and let
\Delta_{f}
be the distributive constant of
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
with respect to
f
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\|\ (q-\Delta_{f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N_{Q_{i}({\bf f})}(r).

However, by this method, the truncation level
[L(q,\epsilon)]
can not be given.

Almost global solutions of 1D nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations with small weakly decaying initial data
Hou Fei, Tao Fei, Yin Huicheng[12pt] 1. Department of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210093, China2. School of Science, Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications,Nanjing 210023, China3. School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute, Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing 210023, China

Abstract
It has been known that if the initial data decay sufficiently fast at space infinity, then 1D Klein-Gordon equations with quadratic nonlinearity admit classical solutions up to time e C / ϵ 2 e^{C/\epsilon^{2}} while e C / ϵ 2 e^{C/\epsilon^{2}} is also the upper bound of the lifespan, where C > 0 C>0 is some suitable constant and ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 is the size of the initial data. In this paper, we will focus on the 1D nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations with weakly decaying initial data. It is shown that if the H s H^{s} -Sobolev norm with ( 1 + | x | ) 1 / 2 + (1+|x|)^{1/2+} weight of the initial data is small, then the almost global solutions exist; if the initial H s H^{s} -Sobolev norm with ( 1 + | x | ) 1 / 2 (1+|x|)^{1/2} weight is small, then for any M > 0 M>0 , the solutions exist on [ 0 , ϵ − M ] [0,\epsilon^{-M}] . Our proof is based on the dispersive estimate with a suitable Z Z -norm and a delicate analysis on the phase function. Keywords: 1D Klein-Gordon equation, weakly decaying initial data, dispersive estimate, Z Z -norm. 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification. 35L70, 35L72.

) roughly comes down to
that of
\|(1+|x|)^{1+}\Lambda^{\upsilon}V\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}
for some suitable number
\upsilon>0
.
To this end, two kinds of regions for
|x|\geq s^{\theta}
and
|x|\leq s^{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1)

are divided, respectively. For
|x|\leq s^{\theta}
, the authors in
[
9
]
obtain that for
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\begin{split}\|(1+|x|)^{1+}\Lambda^{\upsilon}V(t)\|_{L^{2}(|x|\leq s^{\theta})}&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\theta^{+}}\|U(s)\|^{2}_{W^{N^{\prime},\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\|U(s)\|_{H^{N}}ds+\mathrm{other~terms}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{3}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-2+\theta^{+}}ds+\mathrm{other~terms}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{3}+\mathrm{other~terms},\end{split}

(1.28)

which yields the smallness estimate of
\|V(t)\|_{Z_{1}}
when
|x|\leq s^{\theta}
.
However, in our problem (
1.3
), if taking the case of
\alpha=1/2
as an instance,
by
\|U(t)\|_{W^{N^{\prime},\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\leq C(1+t)^{-1/2}\|V(t)\|_{Z_{1/2}}

and
\|V(t)\|_{Z_{1/2}}\leq C\|(1+|x|)^{1/2+}\Lambda^{\upsilon}V\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}
, then similarly to (
1.28
), one has
that for
\theta>0
,

\begin{split}\|(1+|x|)^{1/2+}\Lambda^{\upsilon}V(T_{1/2,\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(|x|\leq s^{\theta})}&\leq C\varepsilon^{3}\int_{0}^{T_{1/2,\varepsilon}}(1+s)^{\theta^{+}/2-1}ds+\mathrm{other~terms}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{3}(1+T_{1/2,\varepsilon})^{\theta^{+}/2}+\mathrm{other~terms}.\end{split}

(1.29)

This means that
T_{1/2,\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\theta^{+}}}
holds in order to guarantee the smallness of

\|V(t)\|_{Z_{1}}
, which is too crude by comparison with
T_{1/2,\varepsilon}\sim e^{\kappa_{0}/\varepsilon^{2}}
in (
1.5
) of Theorem
1.1
.
This is the reason that we have to give more delicate analysis on the related phase
\Psi_{bad}
in (
1.24
).

Based on all the above analysis, the estimate of the
Z_{\alpha}
-norm of the
cubic nonlinearity in (
1.23
) will be finished.
On the other hand, the treatments for the quartic nonlinearity and other terms in (
1.23
)
are much easier. Finally, the bootstrap assumption of

\|V(t)\|_{Z_{\alpha}}
can be closed and then Theorem
1.1
is proved.

.

Preliminaries

2.1
Littlewood-Paley decomposition and definition of
Z_{\alpha}
-norm

For the integral function
f(x)
on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
, its Fourier transformation is defined as

\hat{f}(\xi):=\mathscr{F}_{x}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)dx.

Choosing a smooth cut-off function
\psi:\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\rightarrow[0,1]
, which equals 1 on
[-5/4,5/4]
and vanishes outside
[-8/5,8/5]
,
we set

\begin{split}&\psi_{k}(x):=\psi(|x|/2^{k})-\psi(|x|/2^{k-1}),\quad k\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,k\geq 0,\\
&\psi_{-1}(x):=1-\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi_{k}(x)=\psi(2|x|),\quad\psi_{I}:=\sum_{k\in I\cap\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\cap[-1,\infty)}\psi_{k},\end{split}

where
I
is any interval of
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
.
Let
P_{k}
be the Littlewood-Paley projection onto frequency
2^{k}

\mathscr{F}(P_{k}f)(\xi):=\psi_{k}(\xi)\mathscr{F}f(\xi),\quad k\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,k\geq-1.

For any interval
I
,
P_{I}
is defined by

P_{I}f:=\sum_{k\in I\cap\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\cap[-1,\infty)}P_{k}f.

Introducing the following dyadic decomposition in the Euclidean physical space
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits

(Q_{j}f)(x):=\psi_{j}(x)f(x),\qquad j\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits,j\geq-1.

Inspired by
[
9
]
, we define the
Z_{\alpha}
-norm of
f
as

\|f\|_{Z_{\alpha}}:=\sum_{j,k\geq-1}2^{j\alpha+N_{1}k}\|Q_{j}P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)},\qquad\alpha\in(0,1/2],\ N_{1}=12.

(2.1)

Let

Z_{\alpha}:=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits):\|f\|_{Z_{\alpha}}<\infty\}

and

\|(g,h)\|_{Z_{\alpha}}:=\|g\|_{Z_{\alpha}}+\|h\|_{Z_{\alpha}}
.

Through the whole paper, for non-negative quantities
f
and
g
,

f\lesssim g
and
f\gtrsim g
mean
f\leq Cg
and
f\geq Cg
with
C>0
being a generic constant.

2.2
Linear dispersive estimate

Lemma 2.1

(Linear dispersive estimate)
.

For any function
f
, integer
k\geq-1
and
t\geq 0
, it holds that

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{3k/2}(1+t)^{-1/2}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

(2.2)

Moreover, for
\beta\in[0,1/2]
and
j\geq-1
, one has

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{k/2+2k\beta+j\beta}(1+t)^{-\beta}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Note that

\psi_{k}(x)=\psi_{k}(x)\psi_{[[k]]}(x),

(2.4)

where
[[k]]:=[k-1,k+1]
.
Then one has

\begin{split}P_{k}e^{it\Lambda}f(x)&=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}\mathcal{K}_{k}(t,x-y)P_{k}f(y)dy,\\
\mathcal{K}_{k}(t,x)&:=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}e^{i(x\xi+t\langle{\xi}\rangle)}\psi_{[[k]]}(\xi)d\xi.\end{split}

(2.5)

According to Corollary 2.36 and 2.38 in
[
13
]
, for any
t\geq 1
, it holds that

\|\mathcal{K}_{k}(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{3k/2}t^{-1/2}.

(2.6)

For
0\leq t\leq 1
, we easily have

\|\mathcal{K}_{k}(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}\psi_{[[k]]}(\xi)d\xi\lesssim 2^{k}.

This, together with (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and Young’s inequality, leads to

\|P_{k}e^{it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{K}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{3k/2}(1+t)^{-1/2}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

In addition, the estimate of
\|P_{k}e^{-it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}
is analogous.
Thus, (
2.2
) is achieved.

Next we turn to the proof of (
2.3
).
It follows from the Bernstein inequality such as
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]

and the unitarity of
e^{\pm it\Lambda}
that

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{k/2}\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{k/2}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

On the other hand, (
2.2
) implies

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{3k/2}(1+t)^{-1/2}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim 2^{3k/2+j/2}(1+t)^{-1/2}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}&=(\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)})^{1-2\beta}(\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)})^{2\beta}\\
&\lesssim(2^{k/2})^{1-2\beta}(2^{3k/2+j/2}(1+t)^{-1/2})^{2\beta}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\\
&\lesssim 2^{k/2+2k\beta+j\beta}(1+t)^{-\beta}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any function
f
, integer
k\geq-1
,
t\geq 0
and
p\in[2,+\infty]
, it holds that

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{3k+j}}{1+t}\Big)^{1/2-1/p}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Note that

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}=\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}.

(2.8)

Applying the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem to (
2.2
) and (
2.8
) yields

\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{3k/2}}{\sqrt{1+t}}\Big)^{1-2/p}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)},

where
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1-\frac{1}{p}
.
Therefore, we achieve from (
2.4
) that

\begin{split}\|P_{k}e^{\pm it\Lambda}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}&\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{3k}}{1+t}\Big)^{1/2-1/p}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{3k}}{1+t}\Big)^{1/2-1/p}\|\psi_{[[j]]}Q_{j}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\frac{2^{3k}}{1+t}\Big)^{1/2-1/p}\|\psi_{[[j]]}\|_{L^{2p/(p-2)}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\|Q_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\\

which derives (
2.7
).
∎

(A.12)

For

) and (

) and (

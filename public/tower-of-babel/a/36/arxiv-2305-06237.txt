Weyl laws for interacting particles
Ngoc Nhi Nguyen

Abstract
. We study grand-canonical interacting fermionic systems in the mean-field regime, in a trapping potential. We provide the first order term of integrated and pointwise Weyl laws, but in the case with interaction. More precisely, we prove the convergence of the densities of the grand-canonical Hartree-Fock ground state to the Thomas-Fermi ground state in the semiclassical limit ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 . For the proof, we write the grand-canonical version of the results of [S. Fournais, M. Lewin, J.P. Solovej, The semi-classical limit of large fermionic systems , Calculus of Variations and Partial Differential Equations, 57, p.105 (2018)], and [J.G. Conlon, Semi-classical Limit Theorems for Hartree-Fock Theory , Communications in Mathematical Physics, 88, p.133 (1983)].



1.
Introduction

For a continuous
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
which is
confining
, i.e. such that
V(x)\to+\infty
as
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to+\infty
, and an energy
E\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)
the spectral projector corresponding to eigenvalues less or equal to
E\in\mathbb{R}
of the Schrödinger operators
P_{\hbar}:=-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V
and by
(x,y)\mapsto{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)(x,y)
its associated integral kernel. The
Weyl law
provides asymptotics of this integral kernel at the semiclassical limit
\hbar\to 0
. Let us recall two particular forms and their associated first order terms.

•

The
integrated
Weyl law is the version that provides the asymptotics of the number of eigenvalues of
P_{\hbar}
in the interval
(-\infty,E]
. This counting function can be written as

N_{\hbar}(E):=\tr_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)(x,x)dx.

Then, we have (see for instance
[
5
]
or
[
22
, Chap.6]
for smooth confining potential
V
, and
[
8
, Thm. 4.28]
for continuous compact
V
) that

(1.1)

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}N_{\hbar}(E)=\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[(E-V(x))_{+}]^{d/2}dx.

•

The
pointwise
Weyl law is the version that provides the pointwise asymptotics of the integral kernel
{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)(x,y)
. For instance, we have (see
[
4
, Thm. I.1]
for confining locally smooth potentials
V
) that

(1.2)

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)(x,x)=\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}[(E-V(x))_{+}]^{d/2}.

These asymptotics can be interpreted as the spatial equidistribution of free fermions trapped in a potential
V
and associated to energies less or equal to
E
. Indeed, the fermionic ground state of non-interacting, grand-canonical fermions at zero temperature and energy
E
in the external potential
V
is uniquely characterized by its one-body density matrix
{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)
, and the function
x\mapsto{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)(x,x)
then represents the spacial density of such a system.

Having this many-body picture in mind, we are interested in generalizing these Weyl laws to the case where interactions between fermions are introduced. We choose to work in the Hartree-Fock approximation where the state of the system is still characterized by its one-body density matrix
\gamma
, a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
. The ground state of the system is then a minimizer of the
\hbar
-Hartree-Fock energy
functional

(1.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V-E,w}(\gamma):=&\hbar^{d}\tr_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V-E)\gamma)\\
&\quad+\frac{\hbar^{2d}}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\gamma}(x)\rho_{\gamma}(y)w(x-y)dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\gamma(x,y)\right\rvert^{2}w(x-y)dxdy\right),\end{split}

where
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the pair interaction potential,
\gamma(x,y)
denotes the integral kernel of
\gamma
at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho_{\gamma}(x):=\gamma(x,x)
its associated density. Notice that since we work in the grand-canonical setting, we are interested in global minimizers of this functional (that is, without any constraint on
\tr_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(\gamma)
). Notice also that in the case without interactions (
w=0
),
\gamma={\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq E\right)
is indeed a minimizer.
The Hartree-Fock functional is scaled with respect to the semiclassical parameter
\hbar
in such a manner that the kinetic term and the potential terms are of the same order. To achieve this, one could consider trial operators
\gamma_{\hbar}
with a semiclassical structure, for instance the Weyl quantization
\gamma_{\hbar}={\rm Op}^{\rm w}_{\hbar}(a)
of phase-space function
a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with a sufficient decay at infinity
[
22
]
(heuristically the Wigner transform
[
21
]
of
\gamma_{\hbar}
, up to some error as
\hbar\to 0
). Its integral kernel
\gamma_{\hbar}(x,y)
and its density
\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}
are of order
\hbar^{-d}
. The linear term
\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma_{\hbar})
is of order
\hbar^{-d}
. The interaction term is quadratic in
\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}
and
\gamma_{\hbar}(x,y)
, therefore is of order
\hbar^{-2d}
. That is the reason why we retrieve an additional factor
\hbar
in front of the direct and exchange terms. A further choice has been made in order to have
\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})
of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
for (approximate) minimizers
\gamma_{\hbar}
, which are expected to have almost a semiclassical structure and to be of order
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{-d})
.

As is well-known since the works of Lieb-Simon
[
14
,
15
]
in the case of Coulomb systems, minimizers of the Hartree-Fock functional should be related as
\hbar\to 0
to minimizers of the
Thomas-Fermi functional

(1.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V-E,w}(\rho)&=\frac{d}{d+2}c_{\rm TF}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)^{1+2/d}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(x)-E)\rho(x)dx\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)dxdy,\end{split}

on a set of non-negative densities
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with additional good properties. Here,

c_{\rm TF}:=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert^{2/d}}.

Minimizers of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V-E,w}
must satisfy, when they exist, the Thomas-Fermi integral equation

c_{\rm TF}\rho_{\rm TF}(x)^{2/d}=(E-V(x)-w\ast\rho_{\rm TF}(x))_{+},

i.e.

(1.5)

\rho_{\rm TF}(x)=\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}(E-V(x)-w\ast\rho_{\rm TF}(x))_{+}^{d/2}.

The assumption on the coercivity of the potential
V
implies that the density
\rho_{\rm TF}
has compact support.
In the case
w=0
, one recovers the limiting density in the Weyl law (
1.2
) and we will see that
\rho_{\rm TF}
is its natural generalization in the case with interactions. This formula also reflects the typical fact that mean-field particles behave as free particles in the mean-field effective potential
V+w*\rho_{\rm TF}
.

Our main result (see Theorems
3
and
5
below for a more precise formulation) is then

Theorem 1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Under suitable assumptions on
V
and
w
(in particular, assuming that
w
is repulsive), any minimizer
\gamma_{\hbar}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar,V-E,w}^{\rm HF}
satisfies (up to a subsequence) the integrated Weyl law

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}\tr_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(\gamma_{\hbar})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\rm TF}(x)dx,

as well as the pointwise Weyl law

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)=\rho_{\rm TF}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\rho_{\rm TF}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V-E,w}
.

In the case where the minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{V-E,w}^{\rm TF}
is unique (which is for instance the case when
\hat{w}\geq 0
, that implies the convexity of the functional), the subsequence can be dropped and the convergence holds for the whole limit
\hbar\to 0
.

To prove this result, we follow the approach of Fournais-Lewin-Solovej
[
6
]
who treated the canonical case (that is, the asymptotics as
N\to\infty
of a system of
N
particles). The grand canonical case is similar, with the following differences: while building test functions is simpler due to the absence of a trace constraint (in the canonical setting, one assumes that
\tr\gamma=N
), one has to use the confinement of the potential together with the repulsiveness of the interactions to infer that for minimizers, the trace has the right behaviour in
\hbar
(that is,
\tr\gamma_{\hbar}\lesssim\hbar^{-d}
). Let us notice that
[
6
]
also proves the above convergences for full many-body ground states (that is, without the Hartree-Fock approximation). The grand canonical case requires a more significant change in the full many-body proof, see Section
7
. Another notable difference with
[
6
]
is that we obtain a pointwise convergence as well, and for this we use an argument due to Conlon
[
3
]
.

1.1.1.
Hartree-Fock setting

Let us recall the Hartree-Fock functional expression
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar,V,w}^{\rm HF}
(c.f. (
1.3
)) and let us explain each term is defined

\mathcal{E}_{\hbar,V,w}^{\rm HF}(\gamma)=\hbar^{d}\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)+\frac{\hbar^{2d}}{2}\left(D_{w}(\rho,\rho)-{\rm Ex}_{w}(\gamma)\right).

Here,
D_{w}
and
{\rm Ex}_{w}
respectively denote the
direct term
and
exchange term
defined by

D_{w}(\rho,\tilde{\rho}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)\tilde{\rho}(y)w(x-y)dxdy\text{ and }{\rm Ex}_{w}(\gamma):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\gamma(x,y)\right\rvert^{2}w(x-y)dxdy.

Let
\Sigma>0
large enough such that
V+\Sigma\geq 0
, the expression
\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)
means that

\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma):=\tr(\sqrt{-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+\Sigma}\gamma\sqrt{-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+\sigma})-\Sigma\tr(\gamma).

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}^{1}
the set of trace-class operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is endowed by the norm

\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}^{1}}:=\tr(\sqrt{\gamma^{*}\gamma}).

We introduce the definition set of
\mathcal{E}_{\hbar,V,w}^{\rm HF}
for a good enough
w

(1.6)

\mathcal{X}:=\{\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}\>:\>\left\lVert(-\Delta+V+1)^{1/2}\gamma(-\Delta+V+1)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}^{1}}<+\infty\},

endowed with the norm

\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{\mathcal{X}}:=\left\lVert(-\Delta+V+1)^{1/2}\gamma(-\Delta+V+1)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}^{1}}.

By abuse of notation, without any possible conflict, we sometimes denote by
\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar}
the Hartree-Fock energy, instead of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}
. We look at the variational problem for a fixed
\hbar>0

(1.7)

\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma)=:e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w},

on the convex closed subset of
\mathcal{X}

(1.8)

\mathcal{K}:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}\>:\>0\leq\gamma\leq 1\}.

where we minimize the Hartree-Fock functional.
We denote by
e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar}
this ground state energy (we explain in Sections
2.1.2
and
2.1.4
under what conditions on the considered potentials
V
,
w
this problem is well-posed).

We will show that the integrated Weyl law’s asymptotics remain true if we consider even density matrices
\gamma_{\hbar}\in\mathcal{K}
that approach minimizers of the Hartree-Fock functional.
We call
almost-minimizers
or
approximate minimizers
, operators
\gamma_{\hbar}\in\mathcal{K}
such that there exists
\varepsilon_{\hbar}=o_{\hbar}(1)
such that

(1.9)

\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})=e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}+\varepsilon_{\hbar}.

We will prove semiclassical limits (Theorems
2
and
3
) on these objects.

At the first glance, the interest in working with such objects is not obvious when the problems admit minimizers. This is the case in this paper, which deals only with external confining potentials. A reasonable perpective to this paper is to extend these semiclassical limits to more general external potentials and to excited states. This is far from being obvious and the question remains open for now. The existence of Hartree-Fock minimizers is indeed more subtle for unconfined potentials (see for instance
[
18
]
). Even in this case, there are always approximate minimizers, and they would naturally be the objects to consider.

In the semiclassical limit
\hbar\to 0
, the exchange term of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar}
becomes negligible (see for instance
[
1
]
who proved it in a canonical setting. It is what we prove in Lemma
2.7
). Therefore, it will therefore be easier to work with the
reduced Hartree-Fock
functional
\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar}
which is the Hartree-Fock functional without its exchange term. We denote by
e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar}
its associated ground state energy.

1.1.2.
Thomas-Fermi and Vlasov settings

The Thomas-Fermi energy functional
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}
(c.f. (
1.4
)) is defined on the set of trial states

(1.10)

\mathcal{X}^{\rm TF}_{V}:=\left\{\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>\rho\geq 0:\>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\rho(x)dx<\infty\right\}.

We denote by
e^{\rm TF}
its ground state energy of the Thomas-Fermi functional, defined by the formula (
1.4
)

(1.11)

e^{\rm TF}_{V,w}:=\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{X}^{\rm TF}_{V}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho).

Let us introduce also the
Vlasov energy
functional

(1.12)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}(m)&:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}m(x,\xi)dxd\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\rho_{m}(x)dx\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{m}(x)w(x-y)\rho_{m}(y)dxdy,\end{split}

defined on the phase-space densities set

(1.13)

\mathcal{K}^{\rm Vlas}_{V}:=\inf\left\{m\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>0\leq m\leq 1,\>\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))m(x)dx<\infty\right\},

for

\rho_{m}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(x,\xi)d\xi.

We define its ground state energy

(1.14)

e^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}:=\inf_{m\in\mathcal{K}^{\rm Vlas}_{V}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}(m).

As we will see in Section
2.2
, Vlasov and Thomas-Fermi functionals are closely related. In particular, the Vlasov energy is used to study the Hartree-Fock ground state and thus relate it to the Thomas-Fermi model.

Assumptions on the potentials

Without loss of generality, let us assume assume here that

Assumption 1.1
.

V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous non-negative potential such that
V(x)\to+\infty
as
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to+\infty
.

We detail below definitions of repulsivity on the interaction potential.

Assumption 1.2

(Repulsive potential)
.

Let
w
be an even real-valued function
L^{1}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\forall\rho\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}_{+})\quad D_{w}(\rho,\rho)\geq 0.

Remark 1.3
.

Assumption
1.2
ensures that the lower semicontinuous Thomas-Fermi functional is non-negative on
\mathcal{X}_{V}^{\rm TF}
. In particular, the associated ground state energy should be finite.
Note that when
w
does not satisfy Assumption
1.2
, the associated Thomas-Fermi ground state energy is not bounded from below for dimensions
d\geq 3
where the contribution of the direct term is more important than the one of the kinetic one

e^{\rm TF}_{V,w}=-\infty.

For instance, by choosing
\rho
such that
D_{w}(\rho,\rho)<0
, and defining
\rho_{n}:=n\rho
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the energy
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho_{n})\to-\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
In this case, if we prove that the ground state behaves as the Thomas-Fermi model at the first order term, then the ground state of the system diverges.

We have restricted our study to repulsive potentials since we treat grand-canonical systems and then we want to bound by below the Thomas-Fermi ground state energy. It would also be interesting to understand what happens in the attractive case, since that is what we do in dimensions 1 and 2.

Assumption 1.4

(Alternative of attractive potential for dimensions
d=1,2
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
w\in L^{1}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
an even real-valued function such that

(1.15)

\lVert(\hat{w})_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}(2\sqrt{\pi})^{-1}&\text{if}\ d=1,\\
(2\pi)^{-1}C_{\rm LT}^{-2}&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{cases}

Here
C_{\rm LT}=C_{{\rm LT},d}>0
denotes the constant in the kinetic Lieb-Thirring inequality (see for instance
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
17
]
and see
[
7
, Prop. 4]
for an uppper bound)
that holds for any operator
\gamma\geq 0

(1.16)

\lVert\rho_{\gamma}\rVert_{L^{1+2/d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{{\rm LT,}d}\tr((-\Delta)\gamma)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{d+2}}.

Remark 1.5
.

When the potential
w
is non-negative, the direct term
D_{w}(\rho_{\gamma},\rho_{\gamma})
is always non-negative.
Moreover,
D_{w}(\rho_{\gamma},\rho_{\gamma})\geq{\rm Ex}_{w}(\gamma)
and
V
is trapping.
Thus, since
V
is bounded from below, there exists such that for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}
and any
\hbar>0
,

\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma)\geq\hbar^{d}\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)\geq\hbar^{d}\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)_{-})\geq-C.

Therefore, the energy
\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V-E,w}(\gamma)
is always bounded from below for any
\hbar>0
.
We will show that it remains true under more general assumptions. We prove it in Lemma
2.3
of Section
2.1
.

In the statement of our results, there appears also the following assumption

Definition 1.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
.
A function
w\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
w_{1}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

such that
\lVert w_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon
and

.

We will see that it is needed for the lower bound on the limit of the ground state energy in Theorem
4

We will state later conditions on

2.1
).

Statement of the results

The integral and pointwise Weyl laws actually rely on a weaker version.

Theorem 2

(Weak semiclassical limit of the density)
.

, defined in (

Then, there exist
\rho_{\rm TF}
a minimizer of the Thomas-Fermi energy
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}
and a decreasing subsequence

1.2.1.
Whole many-body grand-canonical setting

A grand-canonical ensemble is composed of
\mathbb{N}
identical systems of finite particles with a fixed chemical potential
E
, which is shared with the other particles and the energy.
When the particles are fermionic, the system of grand-canonical states is described by the
fermionic Fock space

\mathcal{F}:=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dN}),

composed of sequences
\Psi=(\Psi_{0},\Psi_{1},\cdots,\Psi_{N},\cdots)
such that
\Psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. This Fock space
\mathcal{F}
is usually endowed by the scalar product

\left\langle\Psi,\Phi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\Psi_{N},\Phi_{N}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})}.

Moreover, the quantum Hamiltonian is

\mathbf{P}=\bigoplus_{N=1}^{\infty}P_{N},

where
P_{N}
is the Hamiltonian on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
defined by

P_{N}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{x_{j}}+V(x_{j}))+\hbar^{d}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}w(x_{i}-x_{j}).

The ground state energy
e_{\hbar,V,w}
of

per particle

(1.19)

e_{\hbar,V,w}:=\hbar^{d}\inf\spec(\mathbf{P})

is also defined as a function of the ground state energies of the canonical Hamiltonians
P_{N}
, up to a factor
\hbar^{d}

e_{\hbar,V,w}=\inf_{N\geq 0}e_{N,V,w}=:\hbar^{d}\inf_{N\geq 0}\spec(P_{N}).

If we add a regularity condition of

, one can prove the grand-canonical equivalent of Proposition
[
6
, Prop. 3.5]
:

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}e_{\hbar,V,w}=e^{\rm TF}_{V,w},

that we deduce easily from Theorem
4
since we always have the upper bound

e_{\hbar,V,w}\leq e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}.

2.1.
About the Hartree-Fock functional

2.1.1.
Setting and well-posedness of the Hartree-Fock energy

By definition of

We make explicit the conditions on

Proposition 2.1

(Bound on the direct term)
.

Let

\{1\}&\text{for}\ d=2,\\

(ii)

and any

-C_{\gamma,\hbar}(d)\lVert(\hat{w})_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\hbar^{d}D_{w}(\rho_{\gamma},\rho_{\gamma})\leq C_{\gamma,\hbar}(d)\lVert(\hat{w})_{+}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

2.1.2.
Coercivity of the Hartree-Fock energy

First, we prove that for any fixed

is finite and all minimizing sequences of

We also often use this relation of coercivity to give a semiclassical bound on the almost-minimizers of
\hbar
-(reduced)-Hartree-Fock functional and the whole system density matrices of trial states. This bound is crucial since we use it in several proofs below.

Lemma 2.3

Let

•

•

Otherwise, a second alternative for

Remark 2.4

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Since we have Assumption
1.2
, we only need to control the linear and the exchange term of the Hartree-Fock energy.
Moreover, Assumption
1.15
for

ensures that the direct term to be controlled by the linear term. Indeed,

\displaystyle\hbar^{d}\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)-\frac{\hbar^{d}}{2}\left\lvert{\rm Ex}_{w}(\gamma)\right\rvert-\frac{\hbar^{d}}{2}\lVert\hat{w}_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}C_{\gamma,\hbar}(d)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\hbar^{d}}{2}\left\lvert{\rm Ex}_{w}(\gamma)\right\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V-1))\gamma)&\text{if}\ d=1,\\

Now, let us provide a lower bound of the Hartree-Fock energy without its direct term and exchange term. For convenience, let us treat only

(we recover the other cases by adding multiplicative constants and changing the value of E).
For any

by

\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}:={\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V>M\right),\quad\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-}:={\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V\leq M\right).

Notice that
\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}+\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-}=1
.
Furthermore, for any
\gamma\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}\gamma)

\displaystyle=\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V-M)\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}\gamma)+M\tr((1-\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-})\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq M\tr(\gamma)-M\tr(\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-}).

By the min-max principle and the integrated Weyl law (see
[
8
, Thm. 4.28]
), applied to the continuous and compactly supported potential
-(V-M)_{-}

\displaystyle\tr(\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-})\leq\tr

\displaystyle({\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta-(V-M)_{-}\leq 0\right))\leq\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(M-V(x))_{-}^{d/2}.

Thus, there exists
C_{M}\geq 0

(2.6)

\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}\gamma)\geq M\tr(\gamma)-\hbar^{-d}C_{M}.

On the one hand,

\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)=\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+1)\gamma)-\tr(\gamma).

On the other hand, by the Weyl law and (

\displaystyle\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)

\displaystyle=\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{+}\gamma)+\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-}\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq M\tr(\gamma)-\hbar^{-d}C_{M}\>+(\min V)\tr(\Pi_{M,\hbar}^{-}\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq M\tr(\gamma)-\hbar^{-d}C_{M}.

We obtain the lower bound of the linear term

\displaystyle\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V)\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+1)\gamma)+\frac{1}{2}(M-1)\tr(\gamma)-\hbar^{-d}C_{M}.

Furthermore, taking into account the bound on the exchange term (

Let
\{\gamma_{\hbar}\}\subset\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})\leq 0
.
In particular,
\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma)\leq C
for any
C>0
and any
\hbar>0
.
By the coercivity bound (Lemma
2.3
),
\{\gamma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
satisfies Assumption
2.6
. Then, by Lemma
2.7

\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})=\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})+o_{\hbar}(1)\geq e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}+o_{\hbar}(1).

Minimizing the left-hand term on all
\gamma
, one has

e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}\geq e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}+o_{\hbar}(1).

Conversely, the inequality holds if we exchange
e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}
and
e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}
.
∎

As a consequence, almost-minimizers of Hartree-Fock functional are the ones of the reduced-Hartree-Fock functional and conversely.

2.2.
Links between the Vlasov and the Thomas-Fermi energy functionals

We give in this section some relations between Vlasov and Thomas-Fermi energy functionals. Their minimization problems are equivalent

e^{\rm TF}_{V,w}=e^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}.

This can be proved the bathtub principle
[
13
, Theorem 1.14]
. We state below, the main steps of the proof.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\rho
be a trial function for the Thomas-Fermi energy
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}
. Then,
m(x,\xi):={\mathds{1}}\left(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\leq c_{\rm TF}\rho(x)^{2/d}\right)
is a trial function for the Vlasov energy
\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}
and we have the following equalities

\rho=\rho_{m}

and

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}m(x,\xi)dxd\xi=\frac{d}{d+2}c_{\rm TF}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)^{1+2/d}dx.

As a consequence,

\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho)=\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}({\mathds{1}}\left((x,\xi)\>:\>\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\leq c_{\rm TF}\rho(x)^{2/d}\right)),

and we have the lower bound of the Thomas-Fermi energy

e^{\rm TF}_{V,w}\geq e^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
m
an admissible state for the Vlasov energy functional
\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}
. Then
\rho:=\rho_{m}
is an admissible state for the Thomas-Fermi energy functional
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}
.
Moreover, setting

\tilde{m}(x,\xi):={\mathds{1}}\left((x,\xi)\>:\>\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}\leq c_{\rm TF}\rho(x)^{2/d}\right),

we have

\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}(m)\geq\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}(\tilde{m})=\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho),

and the equality holds if and only if
m=\tilde{m}
. In particular, the ground state energies

e^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}=e^{\rm TF}_{V,w},

and if
m
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}
, then

and
\rho_{m}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}
.

Then,

\bullet

if

\rho_{m_{\hbar}}\rightharpoonup\rho
weakly on

,

\bullet

if

\rho_{m_{\hbar}}\rightharpoonup\rho
weakly-
\ast

.

3.
Proof of the semiclassical integrated Weyl law (Theorem
3
)

Assume that we have Theorem
2
and let us explain how to deduce Theorem
3
.

Let
\{\gamma_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar>0}
be a sequence of almost-minimizers of
\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}
. By Lemma
2.5
), there exists
C>0
such that for any
\hbar\in(0,\hbar_{0}]
,

\tr\left((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+1)\gamma_{\hbar}\right)\leq C\hbar^{-d}.

Let us write

\displaystyle h^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\rm TF}(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{B_{R}}(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)-\rho_{\rm TF}(x))dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)-\rho_{\rm TF}(x))dx.

Let us explain why we can restrict ourselves to the limit of the integral on a ball.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
V
is confining, we can chose
R>0
large enough so that
V(x)\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
for any
x
outside of
B_{R}
. Then, for any
\hbar>0

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)-\rho_{\rm TF}(x))dx\right\rvert=\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathds{1}}\left(\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq R\right)V(x)^{-1}\>V(x)(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)-\rho_{\rm TF}(x))dx\right\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\varepsilon\lVert V(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}-\rho_{\rm TF})\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon.

Morerover, Theorem
2
yields that
\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}\rightharpoonup\rho_{\rm TF}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which implies that

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}(\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)-\rho_{\rm TF}(x))dx=0.

Thus, we deduce the integrated Weyl law

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\rm TF}(x)dx.

This proves Theorem
3
.

4.
Proof of the semiclassical limit of the Hartree-Fock ground state energy (Theorem
4
)

We prove in this section the convergence of the Hartree-Fock ground state energy to the Thomas-Fermi ground state energy.

4.1.
Reduction to the reduced Hartree-Fock energy

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
4
consists in getting back to the reduced Hartree-Fock ground state. Indeed, we can deal the asymptotics in the semiclassical limit. Here, we do not fix the trace as in
[
6
]
, but it is relevant to consider operators that satisfy the Assumption
2.6

2.5
, this is the case for the Hartree-Fock almost-minimizers.

4.2.
The upper bound

We bound by above the
\hbar
-Hartree-Fock functional by the
\hbar
-reduced Hartree-Fock functional up to an error depending on
h
, and
take a suitable element
\tilde{\gamma}_{\hbar}\in\mathcal{K}

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\hbar})=\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho)
. Then, one has

\displaystyle\limsup_{\hbar\to 0}e^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}\leq\limsup_{\hbar\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\rm HF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\hbar})\leq\limsup_{\hbar\to 0}(\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\hbar})+o_{\hbar}(1))=\mathcal{E}^{\rm TF}_{V,w}(\rho).

Hence, one obtains the upper bound by minimizing on all the trial states
\rho
.

This is essentially the same proof as in
[
6
, Sec. 3.1]
, by taking
\tilde{\gamma}_{\hbar}
the extension by 0 of the spectral projector
{\mathds{1}}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{C_{R}}-c_{\rm TF}\rho(x)^{2/d}\leq 0\right)

is such that it contains

.
More explicitly,

of eigenfunctions of

4.3.
The lower bound

By Corollary
2.8
, we only have to prove the semiclassical limit of the reduced Hartree-Fock ground state energy to the Thomas-Fermi ground state energy

(4.1)

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}=e^{\rm TF}_{V,w}.

For any
\hbar>0

\gamma_{\hbar}\in\mathcal{K}

\hbar

By Lemma
2.5
, one has
h_{0}>0
such that for any

, the operator
\gamma_{\hbar}
satisfies the uniform bound
\tr((-\hbar^{2}\Delta+V+1)\gamma_{\hbar})<C

Let

be a coherent state defined above with the additional assumption

and even. Denote by

the Husimi measure associated to
\gamma_{\hbar}
and

.

Claim 4.1
.

The sequence
\{m_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
is a bounded sequence of trial functions for the Vlasov energy

There exists
C>0

Proof of Claim
4.1
.

By the Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x)+1)m_{\hbar}(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle\quad=\hbar^{d}\tr(-\hbar^{2}\Delta\gamma_{\hbar})+\hbar^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+V(x)+(V\ast|f^{\hbar}|^{2})(x))\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)dx+\hbar^{d+1}\tr(\gamma_{\hbar})\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Let us prove that there exist

and the other on

for

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{y\in\supp f}\left\lvert V(x)-V(x-\sqrt{h}y)\right\rvert\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in B_{R}}\omega_{x}(\sqrt{\hbar}R_{0},V)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hbar^{d}\rho_{\gamma_{\hbar}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{x\in B_{R}}\omega_{x}(\sqrt{\hbar}R_{0},V).

Since

is uniformly continuous on

The right hand-side of the inequality is uniformly bounded by

Eventually, by taking the limit

4.1

∎

To obtain it, we have to prove that

\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w}(\gamma_{\hbar})=\mathcal{E}^{\rm Vlas}_{V,w}(m_{\hbar})+\varepsilon_{\hbar},

5.
Proof of the weak semiclassical limit of the density (Theorem
2
)

Before proving Theorem
2
, let us state and prove a crucial ingredient: the weak lower semi-continuity of the Vlasov functional.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

and

Proof of Lemma

We treat the linear and the quadratic term separately as for the proof for the Hartree-Fock functional.

We first show that the kinetic energy

Let us introduce a radial decreasing function

and for any

.
On the one hand,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{+}m_{\hbar}(x,\xi)dxd\xi

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{R}(x,\xi)(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{+}m_{\hbar}(x,\xi)dxd\xi.

By taking first the limit

\liminf_{\hbar\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{+}m_{\hbar}(x,\xi)dxd\xi\geq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{+}m(x,\xi)dxd\xi.

On the other hand, since

is a confining potential, the function

weakly-
\ast

\displaystyle\lim_{\hbar\to 0}

\displaystyle\left(-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{-}m_{\hbar}(x,\xi)dxd\xi\right)=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{2}+V(x))_{-}m(x,\xi)dxd\xi.

Finally, we recover the lower semi-continuity by adding the two limits

Then, for any

We will see later in Lemma

that the proof can be adapted to the case where

depends on the semiclassical parameter

Lemma 6.1

(Hardy-Littlewood Tauberian theorem
[
20
, Thm. 10.3]
)
.

Let
E\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{m_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
and
m_{\infty}
be a non-negative measures on
[-E,+\infty)
such that their Laplace transform is well-defined

\forall\gamma\in(0,+\infty],\quad\int_{-E}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha u}dm_{\gamma}(u)<\infty,

and such that for any
\alpha>0

(6.1)

\lim_{\gamma\to+\infty}\int_{-E}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha u}dm_{\gamma}(u)=\int_{-E}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha u}dm_{\infty}(u).

Then, one has

(6.2)

\lim_{\gamma\to+\infty}dm_{\gamma}([-E,0])=dm_{\infty}([-E,0]).

Lemma 6.2

(
[
3
, Lem. 3.5]
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous and such that

as

. Then, one has for all

(6.3)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{-t\big(-\Delta+(1/t)W(x)\big)}(x,x)=e^{-W(x)}.

Let us recall Conlon’s proof of Lemma
6.2
, which is exactly what we extend to the interacting case. It uses results on Brownian motions (see (
6.4
) and (

) below).

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

The main argument is the use of the Feynman-Kac formula. It states that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
t>0

(6.4)

(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{-t(-\Delta+(1/t)W)}(x,y)=(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{-t\Delta}(x,y)\int\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,y,t}(\beta).

Here,
e^{-t\Delta}(x,y)
is the integral kernel of the propagator
e^{-t\Delta}
of the heat equation

e^{-t\Delta}(x,y)=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4t}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}},

and
d\mu_{x,y,t}
is the conditional Wiener measure on the continuous path
\beta:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\beta(0)=x
and
\beta(t)=y
(see for instance
[
20
, Chap.2]
).
When
x=y
, we can restrict our study to

\int\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta).

Conlon’s idea is to estimate this integral in the localized set of path
N_{\delta}
defined for any
\delta>0

(6.5)

N_{\delta}=\{\beta\in\mathcal{C}([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{d}),\>\beta(0)=x=\beta(t),\quad\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\lvert\beta(s)-x\right\rvert\leq\delta\}

and its complementary
N_{\delta}^{c}
. We then write

\displaystyle\int\exp

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)

\displaystyle=e^{-W(x)}d\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta})

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-W(x)}\int_{N_{\delta}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(W(\beta(s))-W(x))ds\right)-1\right]d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{N_{\delta}^{c}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta).

On the one hand, one has the bounds

e^{-W(x)}\leq e^{\lVert W_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}},\quad d\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta})\leq 1,

and for any
\delta>0

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}d\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta})=1.

On the other hand, denoting by
\omega_{x}(\delta,W)
the modulus of continuity of
w
at
x

\omega_{x}(\delta,W):=\sup_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>:\>\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq\delta\}}\left\lvert W(x)-W(y)\right\rvert,

and using that
d\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta})\leq 1
, one has

\displaystyle e^{-W(x)}

\displaystyle\int_{N_{\delta}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(W(\beta(s))-W(x))ds\right)-1\right]d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lVert W_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}\int_{N_{\delta}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert W(\beta(s))-W(x)\right\rvert ds\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert(W(\beta(s))-W(x))\right\rvert ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lVert W_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}\omega_{x}(\delta,W)e^{\omega_{x}(\delta,W)}.

Furthermore, let us explain more precisely
why
there exists
C>0
such that for any
t\in(0,1]

(6.6)

\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta}^{c})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8t}}.

The bound (
6.6
) provided below is a bit more precise than
[
3
, Eq. (3.44)]
in term of dependance on the parameter
\delta
. However, it does not matter to have
\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta}^{c})=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\alpha/t})
with
\alpha=\alpha_{\delta}>0
regardless of the dependance in
\delta
. In fact, we eventually apply the limit
t\to 0^{+}
before
\delta\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

Let is call
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the Wiener process with values in the Euclidian space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and on a probability space with probability
\mathds{P}
. The continuous probability measure
\mathds{P}_{x}
of the process that starts at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is associated to the density
y\mapsto e^{-\frac{t}{2}\Delta}(x,y)=(2\pi t)^{d/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2t}\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert^{2}}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
The measure
d\mu_{x,x,t}
corresponds to the law of the modified Brownian bridge
\{B_{2s}\}_{s\in[0,t]}
which is the normalized Wiener process (admitting the centered Gaussian of covariance
2t
as law) starting and finishing at the point
x
, i.e. with the conditioning
B_{0}=B_{2t}=x

\forall\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\mu_{x,x,t}(\Omega)=\mathds{P}\left(\{B_{2s}\}_{0\leq s\leq t}\in\Omega\right).

In our case,
\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta}^{c})
is equal to
\mathds{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\lvert B_{2s}-x\right\rvert\geq\delta\right)
. This quantity is bounded by
\mathds{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\lvert X_{2s}-x\right\rvert\geq\delta\right)
.
The reflection principle (see for instance
[
20
, Thm. 3.6.5 and Eq. (7.6’)]
) states that for any
t>0
,

\mathds{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\lvert X_{2s}-x\right\rvert\geq\delta\right)\leq 2\mathds{P}_{x}(\left\lvert X_{2t}-x\right\rvert\geq\delta).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathds{P}_{x}(\left\lvert X_{2t}-x\right\rvert\geq\delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq\delta}e^{-\frac{1}{4t}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}}dy\leq e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8t}}\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\int_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\geq\delta}e^{-\frac{1}{8t}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}}dy\right)\leq e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8t}}.

As a consequence, one has the uniform bound
(
6.6
) for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Then, we can bound the last term

\int_{N_{\delta}^{c}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)\leq e^{\lVert W_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}\mu_{x,x,t}(N_{\delta}^{c})\leq Ce^{\lVert W_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8t}}.

Eventually,

\displaystyle(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{-t(-\Delta+(1/t)W)}(x,x)

\displaystyle=e^{-W(x)}(1+o_{t\to 0^{+}}(1))+\mathcal{O}\left(\omega_{x}(\delta,W)e^{\omega_{x}(\delta,W)}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8t}}\right).

We obtain the desired limit by letting first
t\to 0^{+}
, then
\delta\to 0^{+}

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{t(-\Delta+(1/t)W)}(x,x)=e^{-W(x)}.

This ends the proof of Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Let us explain now how Lemmata

and

imply the desired asymptotics.
Recall that we have that

for any

Let us fix

).
To do so, we introduce the sequence of measures

In particular, one has for any

Furthermore, since

and

for any

and any

.
We can write

If

If

\displaystyle=(-W(x))_{+}^{d/2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}(v+1)_{+}^{d/2-1}dv=\frac{2}{d}(-W(x))_{+}^{d/2}.

As a consequence,

Let us now rewrite (

) by computing the Laplace transforms of

and

Indeed, for any
\gamma>0

Furthermore

\displaystyle\int_{-E}^{+\infty}e^{-\alpha u}dm_{\infty}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\alpha u}(u-W(x))_{+}^{d/2-1}du

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(0,1)\right\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\alpha^{-d/2}e^{-\alpha W(x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-w}w_{+}^{d/2-1}du

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\alpha^{-d/2}e^{-\alpha W(x)}.

Since we want to prove (

) for any
\alpha>0
, by taking

and replacing

, it is equicalent to prove that

Since, this is what we have assumed, this concludes the proof of Theorem

Interacting case

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Assume that we have the decomposition

such that

•

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Let us explain how the proof of Lemma
6.2
can be adapted in this framework, with
W_{\hbar}=V_{\rm trap}+V_{\hbar}-E
instead of

. Let us show that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W_{t}(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)=e^{-W_{0}(x)}.

Recall that
t=\hbar^{2}/\alpha
. By assumption, one has the uniform limit

on any compact of

, as

as

.
On the other hand, for any continuous path

such that

and any

, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert W_{t}(\beta(s))-W_{t}(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert W_{0}(\beta(s))-W_{0}(x)\right\rvert+\left\lvert W_{t}(\beta(s))-W_{0}(\beta(s))\right\rvert+\left\lvert W_{t}(x)-W_{0}(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{x}(\delta,W_{0})+2\lVert W_{t}-W_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,\delta)\right)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle e^{-W_{t}(x)}

\displaystyle\int_{N_{\delta}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W_{t}((\beta(s))-W_{t}(x))ds\right)-1\right]d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lvert e^{-W_{t}(x)}-e^{-W_{0}(x)}\right\rvert+e^{\lVert(W_{0})_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\omega_{x}(\delta,W_{0})+2\lVert W_{t}-W_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,\delta)\right)}\right)e^{\omega_{x}(\delta,W_{0})+2\lVert W_{t}-W_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,\delta)\right)}}.

The last term is bounded by

\int_{N_{\delta}^{c}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}W_{t}(\beta(s))ds\right)d\mu_{x,x,t}(\beta)\leq\left(\left\lvert e^{-W_{t}(x)}-e^{-W_{0}(x)}\right\rvert+e^{\lVert(W_{0})_{-}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}\right)e^{-\alpha/t}.

Once again, by

, then

) and then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(4\pi t)^{d/2}e^{t(-\Delta+(1/t)W_{t})}(x,x)=e^{-W_{0}(x)}.

By Lemma

\begin{split}\tilde{P}_{N}:=&\frac{N}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{x_{m}}+V(x_{m})\right)+\hbar^{d}\frac{N(N-1)}{M(M-1)}\sum_{1\leq m\leq m^{\prime}\leq M}w_{1}(x_{m}-x_{m^{\prime}})\\
&\quad+\hbar^{d}\frac{N(N-1)}{L(L-1)}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq L}w_{2}(y_{\ell}-y_{\ell^{\prime}})-\hbar^{d}\frac{N(N-1)}{LM}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{2}(x_{m}-y_{\ell}),\end{split}

is associated to the fixed variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{L}
.
The problem is reduced to a
M
-particle problem.

(2)

Then, we bound from below the right-hand term of the previous inequality, uniformly in the variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with a one-particle functional. For any
\tilde{\Psi}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dM})

(7.4)

\left\langle\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{P}_{N}\tilde{\Psi}\right\rangle\geq\frac{N}{M}\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}\big(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\big),

where
\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N}
is the effective chemical potential

(7.5)

\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N}:=\frac{w_{1}(0)}{2}\frac{N-1}{M-1}\hbar^{d}+\frac{w_{2}(0)}{2}\frac{N-1}{L-1}\hbar^{d}.

(3)

Finally, we link the reduced Hartree-Fock ground state energy asymptotics to the Thomas-Fermi ground state energy.

Remark 7.1

(What changes here)
.

In our case, the limit
\hbar\to 0
is not coupled to
N\to+\infty
. Indeed, we recall that instead of considering

\liminf_{\underset{\hbar N^{1/d}\to 1}{\hbar\to 0}}e_{N,V,w}=\liminf_{\underset{\hbar N^{1/d}\to 1}{\hbar\to 0}}\inf_{\underset{\lVert\Psi_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}{\Psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})}}\frac{\left\langle\Psi_{N},P_{N}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle}{N},

we look at

\liminf_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}\inf_{N\geq 0}e_{\hbar,N,V,w}=\liminf_{\hbar\to 0}\hbar^{d}\inf_{N\geq 0}\inf_{\underset{\lVert\Psi_{N}\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}{\Psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})}}\left\langle\Psi_{N},P_{N}\Psi_{N}\right\rangle.

Thus, it is more convenient to take
M:=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)N\rfloor
with a well-chosen parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
that we let go to
0
, instead of taking
M:=N-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
.
Indeed, in the proof of
[
6
]
, the reduced Hartree-Fock ground state energy
e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,N,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},V,w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}
has a finite mean-field
limit (
N\to+\infty
,
\hbar\to 0
, with
\hbar=N^{-1/d}
), which is
e^{\rm TF}_{V,w,1}
.
Moreover, that is always true that the term
\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}\big(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\big)
of (
7.4
) is uniformly bounded by the ground state energy
e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,N,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}
. This proves the lower bounds.

Actually, the highest order term of this reduced Hartree-Fock energy in (
7.5
) is carried by
-\frac{w_{2}(0)}{2}\frac{N-1}{L-1}\hbar^{d}\sim-\sqrt{N}\hbar^{d}
.
This convergence was possible for the choice
M=N-\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
with the relation
\hbar^{d}=1/N
, so that
\frac{w_{2}(0)}{2}\frac{N-1}{L-1}\hbar^{d}\sim\sqrt{N}\hbar^{d}\to 0
at the coupled mean-field and semiclassical limit. But it does not work with this choice of
M
in our grand-canonical setting, because this term diverges to
-\infty
as
n\to+\infty
for any fixed
\hbar>0
, so that

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},\frac{N-1}{\lfloor N\rfloor-1}}=-\infty.

Therefore the proof slightly changes with the new expression of
M
in Step 3.

Step 3.

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}e_{\hbar,N,V,w}

\displaystyle\geq\hbar^{d}\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\inf_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\>\inf_{\underset{\left\langle\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{P}_{N}\tilde{\Psi}\right\rangle\leq-C_{0}\hbar^{-d}}{\tilde{\Psi}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dM}),\>\lVert\tilde{\Psi}\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}}\left\langle\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{P}_{N}\tilde{\Psi}\right\rangle.

Instead of dealing directly with ground states energies and therefore infimum, it is more conveniant to write bounds on the reduced Hartree-Fock functional.
Hence, by (
7.4
), for
M=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)N\rfloor
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
to be precised and any normalized

(recall that its associated one-body density matrix satisfies always

)

\displaystyle\hbar^{d}\left\langle\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{P}_{N}\tilde{\Psi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{E}_{\hbar,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}^{\rm rHF}\left(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\right)\geq\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w,1}\left(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\right)+r_{\hbar,N,\varepsilon}\left(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w,1}+r_{\hbar,N,\varepsilon}\left(\gamma_{\tilde{\Psi}}^{(1)}\right),

where
r_{\hbar,N,\varepsilon}
denotes the error defined in the set
\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle r_{\hbar,N,\varepsilon}(\gamma):=\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V-\tilde{E}_{\hbar,N},w,\frac{N-1}{M-1}}(\gamma)-\mathcal{E}^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w,1}(\gamma).

Recall that

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}e^{\rm rHF}_{\hbar,V,w,1}=e^{\rm TF}_{V,w,1}.

Thus, it remains to check that for
\varepsilon
small enough, we have the lower bound of the liminf: there exists

(7.6)

for any almost-minizers
\tilde{\Psi}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{dM})
of the ground state energy of
\tilde{P}_{N}
. The conclusion stems from it.

We begin by discussing conditions on

,
\varepsilon
and

so that

and the avoid cases like

N\geq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}

.
Furthermore, we impose also that

ensures

under the condition (
7.7

Let us now explain why for
\hbar>0

), i.e. for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)

In other words, it means that the ground state energy cannot be reached for

.
First,

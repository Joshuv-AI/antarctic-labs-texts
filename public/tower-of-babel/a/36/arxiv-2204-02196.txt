3 3 -post-Lie algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras
Shuai Hou, Yunhe Sheng, Yanqiu Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, first we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras and 3 3 -post-Lie algebras. A 3-post-Lie algebra consists of a 3-Lie algebra structure and a ternary operation such that some compatibility conditions are satisfied. We show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight induces a 3 3 -post-Lie algebra naturally. Conversely, a 3 3 -post-Lie algebra gives rise to a new 3-Lie algebra, which is called the subadjacent 3-Lie algebra, and an action on the original 3-Lie algebra. Then we construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight. Consequently, we obtain the twisted L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra that controls deformations of a given relative Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight on 3-Lie algebras. Finally, we introduce a cohomology theory for a relative Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight on 3 3 -Lie algebras and use the second cohomology group to classify infinitesimal deformations.

Introduction

Rota-Baxter associative algebras originated from the probability
study of G. Baxter
[
7
]
. In the Lie algebra context, Kupershmidt introduced the notion of an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator (also called
a relative Rota-Baxter operator)
in
[

]
to better understand the classical Yang-Baxter equation.
(Relative) Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and associative algebras have important applications in various fields, such as the classical Yang-Baxter equation and integrable systems
[
2
,
26
,
33
]
, splitting of operads
[
3
,
31
]
, double Lie algebras
[

]
, Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization
of quantum field theory
[

]
, etc.
See the book
[

]
for more details. Recently, the deformation and cohomology theories of relative Rota-Baxter operators on both Lie and associative algebras were studied in
[

,
35
,
39
]
.
Post-Lie algebras, as natural generalizations of pre-Lie algebras
[
8
]
, were introduced by Vallette in
[
37
]
, and have important applications in geometric numerical integration and mathematical physics
[
5
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
29
]
. In particular, a relative Rota-Baxter operator of nonzero weight on Lie algebras induces a post-Lie algebra.

The notion of
3
-Lie algebras and more generally,

-Lie algebras was introduced by Filippov in
[

]
, which appeared naturally in various areas of theoretical and mathematical physics
[

,

,

. See the review article

,

Indeed, the

-Lie algebra is the algebraic structure corresponding to the Nambu mechanics
[

]
. In
[
6
]
, R. Bai et al. introduced the notion of Rota-Baxter operators of weight

on
3
-Lie algebras and give various constructions from Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and pre-Lie algebras. Then the notion of an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator on a
3
-Lie algebra with respect to a representation was introduced in
[

]
to study solutions of the
3
-Lie classical Yang-Baxter equation. In particular, when the representation is the adjoint representation, an

-operator is exactly a Rota-Baxter operator of weight

{\mathcal{O}}
-operators on 3-Lie algebras were also used to study matched pairs of 3-Lie algebras and 3-Lie bialgebras in

The first purpose of this paper is to study relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on 3-Lie algebras and associated structures. For this purpose, first we study actions of a 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, which is totally different from the case of Lie algebra actions. We introduce the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight

from a

-Lie algebras. Note that relative Rota-Baxter operators and Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight introduced in

The second purpose is to investigate the

-ary generalizations of post-Lie algebras and analyze the relation with the aforementioned relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on 3-Lie algebras. We introduce a new algebraic structure, which is called a

-post-Lie algebra. A

-post-Lie algebra naturally gives rise to a new

-Lie algebra and an action on the original 3-Lie algebra such that the identity map is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1. We show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
induces a
3
-post-Lie algebra structure naturally. We will explore further applications of 3-post-Lie algebras along the line of applications of post-Lie algebras in future works.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

In Section

In this paper, we work over an algebraically closed filed

2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras

In this section, we first introduce the notion of action of 3-Lie algebras, which give rise to the semidirect product 3-Lie algebras. Then we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras, which can be characterized by the graphs of the semidirect product 3-Lie algebras. Finally we establish the relation between relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras and Nijenhuis operators on 3-Lie algebras. A class of examples are given via certain projections.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
19
]
)

A
3-Lie algebra

is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a skew-symmetric linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, such that for
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq 5
, the following
Fundamental Identity
holds:

(1)

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

For
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
, define
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x:=[x_{1},x_{2},x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}
is a derivation, i.e.

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},x_{4},\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
25
]
)

A
representation
of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a vector space
V
is a linear
map:
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, such that for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathfrak{g},
the following equalities hold:

(2)

\displaystyle~\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2});

(3)

\displaystyle~\rho(x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{2})-\rho(x_{2},x_{4})\rho(x_{1},x_{3})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4}).

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. The linear map
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
defines a representation
of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, which is called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
19
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. Then the subalgebra
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]_{\mathfrak{g}}
is called the
derived algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and denoted by
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1}
. The subspace

{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}~|~[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,~\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}\}

is called the
center
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two
3
-Lie algebras.
Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be a representation of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the vector space
\mathfrak{h}
.
If
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h},

(4)

\displaystyle{}\rho(x,y)u\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{h}),

(5)

\displaystyle{}\rho(x,y)[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,

then
\rho
is called an
action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}.

We denote an action by
(\mathfrak{h};\rho).
Note that (
4
) and (
5
) imply that
\rho(x,y)
is a derivation.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. If
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1}\subset{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{g}),
then the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}).

A linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
from a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
to a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to an action
\rho
if

(6)

\displaystyle\qquad[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Remark 2.7
.

The notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on a
3
-Lie algebra given above is a natural generalization of the
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator introduced in
[
4
]
. While it is not consistent with the Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
introduced in
[
6
]
.
Therefore, there are two different theories for relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
introduced above and Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras introduced in
[
6
]
. We will see that relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras can be characterized by Maurer-Cartan elements of the controlling

-algebras, and naturally related to
3
-post-Lie algebras and Nijenhuis structures. These facts can be viewed as justifications of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
on
3
-Lie algebras being interesting structures.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
from a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
to a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
\rho
. A
homomorphism
from
T
to

consists of
3
-Lie algebra homomorphisms
\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

(7)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{h}},

(8)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x,y)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

In particular, if both
\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}
are invertible,
(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}},\psi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is called an
isomorphism
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
.

Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}).
Define a skew-symmetric bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
by

(9)

\displaystyle{}[x+u,y+v,z+w]_{\rho}=[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u+\rho(z,x)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 2.9
.

With above notations,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho})
is a
3
-Lie algebra, which is called the semidirect product of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to the action
\rho
, and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

It follows from straightforward computations, and we omit details.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}).
Then a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
if and only if the graph

Gr(T)=\{Tu+u|u\in\mathfrak{h}\}

is a subalgebra of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map.
For all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h},
we have

\displaystyle[Tu+u,Tv+v,Tw+w]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}},

which implies that the graph
Gr(T)=\{Tu+u|u\in\mathfrak{h}\}
is a subalgebra of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}
if and only if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

which means that
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
.
∎

Since the graph
Gr(T)
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{h}
as a vector space, there is an induced
3
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let

from a

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
to a

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})

. Then

-Lie algebra, called

[u,v,w]_{T}=\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Moreover,

is a
3
-Lie algebra homomorphism from

to

.

In the sequel, we give the relationship between relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
and Nijenhuis operators.
Recall from
[
27
]
that a Nijenhuis operator on a

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a linear map

satisfying

(11)

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\Big([Nx,Ny,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Ny,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Nx,y,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-N[Nx,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N[x,Ny,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N[x,y,Nz]_{\mathfrak{g}}

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}).
Then a linear map

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight

if and only if

\overline{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{{\rm{Id}}}&T\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right):\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},

-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h},
on the one hand, we have

\displaystyle[\overline{T}(x+u),\overline{T}(y+v),\overline{T}(z+w)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Tv,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+[x,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,Tv,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

On the other hand, since

we have

\displaystyle\overline{T}\Big([\overline{T}(x+u),\overline{T}(y+v),z+w]_{\rho}+[\overline{T}(x+u),y+v,\overline{T}(z+w)]_{\rho}+[x+u,\overline{T}(y+v),\overline{T}(z+w)]_{\rho}\Big)

\displaystyle-\overline{T}^{2}\Big([\overline{T}(x+u),y+v,z+w]_{\rho}+[x+u,\overline{T}(y+v),z+w]_{\rho}+[x+u,y+v,\overline{T}(z+w)]_{\rho}\Big)

\displaystyle+\overline{T}^{3}\Big([x+u,y+v,z+w]_{\rho}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Tv,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,Tv,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\lambda[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

which implies that

if and only if (

At the end of this section, we show that certain projections provide a class of examples of relative Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra such that the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})

is an action of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on itself. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an abelian
3
-Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
\mathfrak{g}^{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0.

Let
\mathfrak{k}
be a complement of
\mathfrak{h}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
as a vector space. Then the projection
P:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
onto the subspace
\mathfrak{h}
, i.e.
P(k,u)=u
for all
k\in\mathfrak{k},~u\in\mathfrak{h}
,
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the adjoint action
\mathrm{ad}
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},
denote by
\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z}
their images under the projection
P
. Since
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian and
\mathfrak{g}^{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0,
we have

\displaystyle[P(x),P(y),P(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big([P(x),P(y),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,P(y),P(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[P(x),y,P(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\lambda[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big([\overline{x},\overline{y},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\overline{x},y,\overline{z}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\overline{y},\overline{z}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\lambda[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that the projection
P
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the adjoint action
\mathrm{ad}
.
∎

Example 2.14

on itself. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

given by

In this section, given an action of 3-Lie algebras, we construct an

Definition 4.1
.

An

-algebra is a

-graded vector space

\displaystyle l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-1)},x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n}),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}}\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0.

\displaystyle+f\Big(\rho(Tu_{2},Tu_{3})u_{1}+\rho(Tu_{3},Tu_{1})u_{2}+\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{3}+\lambda[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Theorem 5.9

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{n},u_{n+1})

\displaystyle[\pi+\rho+\lambda\mu,f]_{\mathsf{R}}(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{n},u_{n+1})

\displaystyle\Big((\pi+\rho+\lambda\mu)\circ f-(-1)^{n-1}f\circ(\pi+\rho+\lambda\mu)\Big)(\mathfrak{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{U}_{n},u_{n+1})

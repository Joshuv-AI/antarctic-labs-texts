Theory of weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors and its applications to 2D stochastic Euler equations driven by multiplicative noise
Kush Kinra, Manil T. Mohan

Abstract
The two dimensional stochastic Euler equations (EE) perturbed by a linear multiplicative noise of Itô type on the bounded domain 𝒪 \mathcal{O} have been considered in this work. Our first aim is to prove the existence of global weak (analytic) solutions for stochastic EE when the divergence free initial data 𝒖 ∗ ∈ ℍ 1 ​ ( 𝒪 ) \boldsymbol{u}^{*}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}) , and the external forcing 𝒇 ∈ L loc 2 ​ ( ℝ , ℍ 1 ​ ( 𝒪 ) ) \boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})) . In order to prove the existence of weak solutions, a vanishing viscosity technique has been adopted. In addition, if curl ​ 𝒖 ∗ ∈ L ∞ ​ ( 𝒪 ) \mathrm{curl}\ \boldsymbol{u}^{*}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}) and curl ​ 𝒇 ∈ L loc ∞ ​ ( ℝ , L ∞ ​ ( 𝒪 ) ) \mathrm{curl}\ \boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})) , we establish that the global weak (analytic) solution is unique. This work appears to be the first one to discuss the existence and uniqueness of global weak (analytic) solutions for stochastic EE driven by linear multiplicative noise. Secondly, we prove the existence of a pullback stochastic weak attractor for stochastic non-autonomous EE using the abstract theory available in the literature. Finally, we propose an abstract theory for weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors. Then we consider the 2D stochastic EE perturbed by a linear multiplicative noise as an example to discuss that how to prove the weak asymptotic autonomy for concrete stochastic partial differential equations. As EE do not contain any dissipative term, the results on attractors (deterministic and stochastic) are available in the literature for dissipative (or damped) EE only. Since we are considering stochastic EE without dissipation, all the results of this work for 2D stochastic EE perturbed by a linear multiplicative noise are totally new.



1
Introduction

The motion of gases and liquids are described by fluid dynamics. If the material density of the fluid is a constant (that is, the divergence free condition is satisfied by the fluid velocity), then we call it as an incompressible fluid. The Motion of a viscous incompressible fluid flow is described by the well-known Navier-Stokes equations. In the case of inviscid flow (zero viscosity), the motion of the ideal incompressible fluid in a bounded domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is described by the Euler equations (EE)

(1.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{u}}{\partial t}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p&=\boldsymbol{f},\ \ \ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}&=0,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times[\tau,\infty),\\
\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{u}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
stands for the velocity field,
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure field and
\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an external forcing, for all
(x,t)\in\mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty)
.

1.1
The stochastic model

This work aims to study the dynamics of the stochastic counterpart of the system (
1.1
) on a bounded domain. Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with
\mathrm{C}^{2}
-boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following non-autonomous 2D stochastic EE on
\mathcal{O}
:

(1.2)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/t&=\boldsymbol{f}\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\sigma\boldsymbol{u}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W},\ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}&=0,\hskip 65.44133pt\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times[\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}&=0,\hskip 65.44133pt\text{ on }\ \partial\mathcal{O}\times[\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{u}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau},\hskip 59.75078pt\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O},\\
\end{aligned}}}\right.

where the coefficient
\sigma>0
is known as the
noise intensity
and
\boldsymbol{n}
is the outward normal to
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Here, the stochastic integral is understood in the sense of Itô and
\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{W}(t,\omega)
is a one-dimensional two-sided Wiener process defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{P})
(see Subsection
3.2
below).

1.3
Motivations

In order to apply the classical theory of global/pullback attractors (deterministic or random), one needs to define a semigroup (or evolution process) on a metric space
(\mathrm{X},\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathrm{X}})
which is continuous on
\mathrm{X}
. In this work, we are considering a stochastic DS associated with stochastic EE perturbed by a white noise which is neither dissipative nor has regularizing effects. We show the existence of a unique global weak (analytic) solution (see Definition
3.2
below) with initial data from space
\mathcal{W}=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}:\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})\}
(see Subsection
3.1
for functional spaces). Furthermore, the stochastic flow is neither continuous nor compact with respect to the topology
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{u}-\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}
. But the stochastic flow is compact with respect to an another topology
\delta(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}
on
\mathcal{W}
which is weaker than
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
in some sense. For such kind of stochastic flows, the author in
[
6
]
came up with a notion of attractor with respect to a pair of topologies
(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{W}},\delta)
known as
stochastic weak attractor
(SWA) where the attractor is
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact (see Theorem
2.5
below). As the uniqueness holds for the stochastic EE when the initial data is in
\mathcal{W}
, the stochastic flow generated by stochastic EE satisfies the evolution property. But it does not enjoys the continuity in phase space
\mathcal{W}
which is used to prove the invariance property of attractors in the classical theory of global/pullback random attractors. Despite of continuity in phase space
\mathcal{W}
, the author in
[
6
]
considered an another assumption which assures the invariance and the existence of a
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact SWA (see Hypothesis
2.1
below). Moreover, this abstract theory was applied to prove the existence of SWA for
2D stochastic dissipative EE driven by additive noise
in a bounded domain (
[
6
, Theorem 4.6]
). We use the abstract theory developed in
[
6
]
to prove the existence of a minimal SWA for
2D stochastic Euler equations perturbed by a linear multiplicative noise
(see Theorem
4.6
below).

The effect of time-dependent forcing creates the non-autonomous feature of dynamical systems which could be the most important feature distinguishing from autonomous. Fundamentally, if the forcing
\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)
(time-dependent) in (
1.2
) asymptotically approaches to a time-independent forcing
\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}
in some sense, then the random dynamics of non-autonomous system converges to the random dynamics of autonomous system. This phenomena is called the
asymptotic autonomy
or
asymptotically autonomous robustness of random dynamics
. From now onward, we use the terminology
pullback deterministic (stochastic) weak attractors
for non-autonomous deterministic (stochastic) dynamical systems and
deterministic (stochastic) weak attractors
for autonomous deterministic (stochastic) dynamical systems. Our ambition is to establish the asymptotically autonomous robustness of pullback stochastic weak attractors of (
1.2
) when the non-autonomous forcing
\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}
satisfies the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1.1
.

\boldsymbol{f}(\cdot)
converges to
\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}
in the following sense:

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\boldsymbol{f}(t+\tau)-\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\/\mathrm{d}\/t=0.

Hypothesis 1.2
.

For the time-dependent external forcing
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\emph{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
, there exist two numbers
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in[0,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\geq\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg[e^{\delta_{1}t}\|\boldsymbol{f}(t+s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+e^{\delta_{1}t}\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(t+s)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+e^{\delta_{2}t}\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(t+s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\bigg]\/\mathrm{d}\/t<+\infty,

where
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}=\mathrm{curl}\boldsymbol{f}
.

Remark 1.3
.

1. An example of Hypotheses
1.1
and
1.2
is

\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)=\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}(x)e^{t}+\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}(x)
with
\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}_{\infty}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
.

2. Hypothesis
1.1
implies that
\sup\limits_{s\geq\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{kt}\|\boldsymbol{f}(t+s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\/\mathrm{d}\/t<+\infty
for all
k>0
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
65
, Lemma 4.1]
). Therefore the first term in the integral is finite for
\delta_{1}\in(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})
under Hypothesis
1.1
.

The concept of asymptotically autonomous robustness of pullback random attractors has been considered in several articles, see
[
42
,
49
,
63
,
66
,
68
,
71
]
, etc. for 2D Navier-Stokes equations, 2D MHD equations,
g
-Navier-Stokes equations and Brinkman-Forchheimer equations etc. As we have already discussed, the classical theory for pullback random attractors depends on the continuity of the flow map in the phase space
\mathcal{W}
(cf.
[
61
]
). But the flow map of the system (
1.2
) is not continuous in
\mathcal{W}
. Motivated from the work
[
6
]
, we introduce a notion of asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors relative to a pair of
(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{W}},\delta)
topologies or
weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors
. By the weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors, we means that the minimal pullback stochastic weak attractors
\{\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega}
(
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact) converges towards the minimal stochastic weak attractor
\{\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
(
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact) in the following sense:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\mathscr{A}(t,\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega))=0,\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.e. }\omega\in\Omega,

where,
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Hausdorff semi-distance between two non-empty subsets of the Banach space
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the metric
\delta
, that is, for non-empty sets
A,B\subset\mathcal{W}

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\delta(a,b).

We prove that three sufficient conditions guarantee the weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors, see Theorem
2.5
below. Then we apply this abstract theory to 2D stochastic EE driven by a linear multiplicative noise to show how to check the sufficient conditions for concrete stochastic partial differential equations.

1.4
Novelties

To the best of our knowledge, there are no works available in the literature for the random dynamics of 2D stochastic EE. Most of the works available in the literature related to the random dynamics and asymptotic analysis are for 2D deterministic/stochastic dissipative EE, cf.
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
19
,
20
,
72
]
etc. Since we are not considering any kind of damping term in stochastic EE, the linear multiplicative structure of the Itô type noise coefficient helps us to prove the results of this work. This work appears to be the first one concerning the random dynamics of 2D stochastic EE perturbed by a linear multiplicative noise (both Itô and Stratonovich noise). The major aims and novelties of this work are:

(i)

Existence and uniqueness of the global weak (analytic) solution of stochastic Euler system (
1.2
) by using a vanishing viscosity method and compactness arguments.

(ii)

Existence of a minimal pullback stochastic weak attractor for the non-autonomous and a minimal stochastic weak attractor for the autonomous stochastic Euler system (
1.2
). We achieve this goal by using the abstract theory developed in
[
6
]
.

(iii)

Weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors of the stochastic Euler system (
1.2
). In order to obtain this, we first establish an abstract theory of weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors in Section
2
and then apply this to the system (
1.2
).

1.5
Difficulties and approaches

Since Euler equations do not have any regularizing term, the existence of global weak (analytic) solutions has been proved by using a vanishing viscosity method. Firstly, we convert the stochastic Euler system (
1.2
) into an equivalent pathwise deterministic system (see (
3.5
) below) with the help of an stationary change of variable (see (
3.4
) below). Next, we consider a Navier-Stokes type system (see system (
3.11
) below) and obtain the existence of global weak (analytic) solutions to the Euler system (
1.2
) as viscosity coefficient approaches to zero. We prove the existence of global weak (analytic) solutions of the stochastic Euler system (
1.2
) when the divergence free initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
, and external forcing
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
. In order to obtain the uniqueness of weak solutions, we need more regular divergence free initial data and external forcing. We establish the existence of a unique global weak (analytic) solution when divergence free initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
,
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
,
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}))
.

For the existence of pullback stochastic weak attractors, we borrowed the abstract theory introduced in
[
6
]
. The linear structure of Itô type multiplicative noise plays a crucial role to show the existence of
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact absorbing set. On contrast to the works available in the literature
[
8
,
9
]
, the presence of linear multiplicative noise helped us to prove the results for the system (
1.2
) without a linear damping term. Since the weak asymptotic autonomy concept is not available in the literature, we develop an abstract theory in Section
2
and then apply it to show the weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors for the system (
1.2
).

2
Abstract theory

The abstract theory of weak global attractors for autonomous dynamical systems was developed in
[
8
]

[
6

]

2.1
Pullback stochastic weak attractors and stochastic weak attractors

Let
(\mathcal{W},\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}})
be a complete metric space,
\delta
be an another metric on
\mathcal{W}
and
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Let
\{S(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}
be a family of mappings which satisfies the following hypothesis:

Hypothesis 2.1
.

Let
\{S(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\ \omega\in\Omega}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}
be a family of mappings, satisfying for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega,
the following two properties:

(i)

If a sequence
\{\boldsymbol{w}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-convergent to
\boldsymbol{w}
in
\mathcal{W},
then
S(t,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}_{n}
is
\delta
-convergent to
S(t,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}
, for all
t\geq\tau
.

(ii)

Evolution property
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle S(t,r,\omega)S(r,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}=S(t,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w},\text{ for all }\tau\leq r\leq t\text{ and for all }\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{W}.

Let
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{W})
be the
\sigma
-algebra of the metric space
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{H}
be a separable metric space such that
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{H}
with continuous embedding. The metric
\delta
is the metric endowed on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{W}
is closed in
\mathcal{H}
. Let us assume that the following hypothesis holds:

Hypothesis 2.2
.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{W}
, the mapping
(\tau,\omega)\mapsto S(t,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}
is measurable from
((-\infty,t]\times\Omega,\mathscr{B}((-\infty,t])\times\mathscr{F})
to
(\mathcal{W},\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\cap\mathcal{W})
.

Definition 2.3
.

For given
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, a bi-parametric set
\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)
is called
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded absorbing set
at time
t
if for all
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded set
B\subset\mathcal{W}
, there exists a time
\tau_{0}(B)
depending on
B
such that

\displaystyle S(t,\tau,\omega)B\subset\mathcal{B}(t,\omega),\ \ \text{ for all }\ \tau\leq\tau_{0}.

Definition 2.4
.

A family of mappings
\{S(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}
is called
asymptotically
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}/\delta
-compact
if we can find a measurable set
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\Omega})=1
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}
, there exists a
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-absorbing set
\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)
with
\delta
-compactness.

]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let Hypotheses
2.1
and
2.2
hold. Assume that for each

, there exists a measurable set
\Omega_{t}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{t})=1

\mathbb{P}
-a.e.

, the set

\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)=\overline{\bigcup_{B\subset\mathcal{W}}\Omega^{\delta}(B,t,\omega)}^{\delta},\ \ \text{ where }\ \ \Omega^{\delta}(B,t,\omega)=\bigcap_{r<t}\overline{\bigcup_{\tau<r}S(t,\tau,\omega)B}^{\delta},

is a measurable

, that is,

(i)

\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
is non-empty, it is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and

-compact,

(ii)

\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
is invariant under the mapping

, that is,

(iii)

For every
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded set

,

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\delta(S(t,\tau,\omega)B,\mathscr{A}(t,\omega))=0,\ \text{ for all }\ t\geq\tau,

is measurable with respect to the

-completion of

.

(v)

\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
is the minimal
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and

-closed set with property
\mathrm{(iii)}
, that is, if

is an another

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}(t,\omega)

\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded sets
B\subset\mathcal{W}
. Since
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}(t,\omega)
is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-closed,
\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)\subset\widehat{\mathscr{A}}(t,\omega)
.
∎

Let us now discuss the abstract theory for stochastic weak attractors.

Hypothesis 2.6
.

There exists a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure preserving transformations of
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})

such that

\displaystyle T(t,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}=T(t-\tau,0,\theta_{\tau}\omega)\boldsymbol{w},\ \ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

for all
t\geq\tau
and
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{W}
.

If a family of mappings
\{T(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
, then the attractor does not depend on the time parameter. The following theorem assures the existence of a measurable
stochastic weak attractor
when Hypothesis
2.6
is satisfied (see
[
23
, Theorem 2.2]
also).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let Hypotheses
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.6
hold. Assume that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, there exists a
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact absorbing set at time
0
. Then for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, the set

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)=\overline{\bigcup_{B\subset\mathcal{W}}\Omega^{\delta}(B,\omega)}^{\delta},\ \ \text{ where }\ \ \Omega^{\delta}(B,\omega)=\bigcap_{r<0}\overline{\bigcup_{\tau<r}T(0,\tau,\omega)B}^{\delta},

is a measurable
stochastic weak attractor
, that is,

(i)

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
is non-empty, it is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact,

(ii)

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
is invariant under the mapping
T(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
, that is,

(iii)

For every
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded set
B\subset\mathcal{W}
,

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\delta(T(t,\tau,\omega)B,\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\theta_{t}\omega))=0,\ \text{ for all }\ t\geq\tau,

(iv)

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
is measurable with respect to the
\mathbb{P}
-completion of
\mathscr{F}
.

(v)

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
is the minimal
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-closed set with property
\mathrm{(iii)}
, that is, if
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{\infty}(\omega)
is an another
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-closed set with the property
\mathrm{(iii)}
, then
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)\subset\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{\infty}(\omega)
.

2.2
Weak asymptotic autonomy

In this subsection, our aim is to establish an abstract theory for the
weak asymptotic autonomy
of
pullback stochastic weak attractors
. In applications to stochastic evolution equations perturbed by white noise, the sample space
\Omega
in the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
is

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega=\{\omega\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{Y}):\omega(0)=0\}=:\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{Y}),

where
\mathrm{Y}
is some Banach space. In this case, there exists a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure preserving transformations of
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
which is given by (
[
1
,
23
]
)

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\theta_{t}\omega)(s)=\omega(t+s)-\omega(t),\ \ \ \text{ for all }\ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

In fact, we are proposing an abstract theory on those probability spaces where the existence of a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure preserving transformations is known.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that if the existence of a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure preserving transformations is known, it implies the existence of an
\Omega_{t}
in Theorem
2.5
. In this case, one can consider
\Omega_{t}
as the set
\theta_{t}\Omega
.

Let
\{\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega}
and
\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)
be a
minimal pullback stochastic weak attractor
and
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact absorbing set
at time
t
, respectively, associated with the non-autonomous stochastic dynamical system
\{S(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}
on
\mathcal{W}
. Also, let
\{\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\omega)
be a
minimal stochastic weak attractor
and
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact absorbing set
, respectively, associated with the autonomous stochastic dynamical system
\{T(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}
on
\mathcal{W}
. We show that
\{\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega}
upper semicontinuously forward converges to
\{\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
with respect to the metric
\delta
.

Remark 2.9
.

For the sample space
\Omega=\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{Y})
, as the existence of a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure preserving transformations of
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
is known, it is remarkable that the autonomous stochastic dynamical system
\{T(t,\tau,\omega)\}_{t\geq\tau,\omega\in\Omega}
over
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
.

The following theorem provides the abstract result for the weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\{\mathscr{A}(\tau,\omega)\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R},\omega\in\Omega}
and
\{\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
are
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact. Suppose that the following properties hold.

(i)

\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t\geq\tau}\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)}^{\delta}
is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact.

(ii)

The mappings
S(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
and
T(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
satisfy,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\tau\to+\infty}\delta(S(t+\tau,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}_{\tau},T(t+\tau,\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{w}_{0})=0,\ \text{ for all }t\geq 0\text{ and }\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega},

whenever
\{\boldsymbol{w}_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
d_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded sequence,
\boldsymbol{w}_{0}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\lim\limits_{\tau\to+\infty}\delta(\boldsymbol{w}_{\tau},\boldsymbol{w}_{0})=0,
where
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
is a
\theta_{t}
-invariant full measure set.

(iii)

There exists a
t_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{B}_{t_{0}}(\omega):=\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)}^{\delta}
is
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact set.

Then, the
weak asymptotic autonomy
follows for
\mathscr{A}
, that is,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\mathscr{A}(t,\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega))=0,\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.e. }\omega\in\Omega,

where,
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Hausdorff semi-distance between two non-empty subsets of the Banach space
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the metric
\delta
, that is, for non-empty sets
A,B\subset\mathcal{W}

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\delta(a,b).

Proof.

In order to prove the convergence given in (
2.5
), it is enough to show that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})=1
, where

\Omega_{0}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{A}(t,\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)=0}}\right\}.

Let us assume that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})<1,
then
\mathbb{P}(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0})>0
. Let
\Omega_{1}=(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}
, where
\widetilde{\Omega}
is the
\theta_{t}
-invariant full measure set given in (
2.4
). Then
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})>0
and
\theta_{s}\Omega_{1}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
be fixed. Since
\omega\notin\Omega_{0}
, we can find
\varepsilon>0
and
t_{n}\geq 2t_{0}>0
(WLOG) with
t_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{A}(t_{n},\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)\geq 3\varepsilon,\ \ \text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In view of
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-boundedness and
\delta
-compactness of
\mathscr{A}(t_{n},\omega)
, there exists
\boldsymbol{w}_{n}\in\mathscr{A}(t_{n},\omega)
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\boldsymbol{w}_{n},\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{A}(t_{n},\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)\geq 3\varepsilon,\ \ \text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

By the invariance property of pullback stochastic weak attractors, we also have that there exists
\boldsymbol{x}_{n}\in\mathscr{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right)
such that
S(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega)\boldsymbol{x}_{n}=\boldsymbol{w}_{n}.

Now by condition
(\mathrm{i})
, there exists an element
\boldsymbol{x}\in\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t_{n}\geq 2t_{0}}\mathscr{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right)}^{\delta}
with

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\delta(\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{x})=0.

From (
2.7
) and condition
(\mathrm{ii})
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}_{n},T\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)=0,

which confirms the existence of an
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}_{n},T\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \ \text{ for all }\ n\geq N_{1}.

By the invariance property of
\mathscr{A}
and the absorption property of absorbing set
\mathcal{B}
, there exists a
\tau<0
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right)\ =\ S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\tau,\omega}}\right)\mathscr{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tau,\theta_{\tau-t_{n}}\omega}}\right)\ \subset\ \mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right),

which gives

(2.9)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}\in\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t_{n}\geq 2t_{0}}\mathscr{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right)}^{\delta}\ \subset\ \overline{\bigcup\limits_{t_{n}\geq 2t_{0}}\mathcal{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2},\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right)}^{\delta}=\mathcal{B}_{t_{0}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\theta_{-\frac{t_{n}}{2}}\omega}}\right).

By condition
(\mathrm{iii})
, (
2.9
) and the attracting property of
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}
, there exists
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x},\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \ \text{ for all }\ n\geq N_{2}.

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.10
), we deduce for all
n\geq N:=\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{A}(t_{n},\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\boldsymbol{w}_{n},\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}_{n},T\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x}}}\right)+\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{n},\frac{t_{n}}{2},\omega}}\right)\boldsymbol{x},\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon,

which contradicts (
2.6
).
∎

3
Global Solvability of 2D stochastic Euler equations driven by multiplicative noise

We first provide the necessary function spaces needed for the further analysis. Then, we define a transformation which helps to convert our stochastic system into an equivalent pathwise deterministic system. Finally, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the 2D stochastic Euler system (
1.2
).

3.1
Function spaces

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, the scalar as well as vector valued Lebesgue spaces will be denoted by
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, respectively. For
s>0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the usual scalar and vector valued Sobolev spaces will be represented by
\mathrm{W}^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{W}^{s,p}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbb{W}^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{W}^{s,p}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, respectively. For
p=2
, the Hilbertian Sobolev spaces will be denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{W}^{s,2}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{O})=\mathbb{W}^{s,2}(\mathcal{O})
. Let us define

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0\ \text{ and }\ \boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{O}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0\ \text{ and }\ \boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\},

where
\boldsymbol{n}
is the unit outward normal to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and
\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}
should be understood in the sense of trace in
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\mathcal{O})
(cf.
[
58
, Chapter 1, Theorem 1.2]
).
The spaces
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{V}
are endowed with the norms

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x\ \text{ and }\ \|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[|\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{2}+|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{2}}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

respectively. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the inner product in the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represent the induced duality between the spaces
\mathbb{V}
and its dual
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
. Note that
\mathbb{H}
can be identified with its dual
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
. Furthermore, we have the Gelfand triple
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
with dense and continuous embedding, and the embedding
\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
is compact.

3.2
Equivalent pathwise deterministic system

In the system (
1.2
),
\mathrm{W}(t,\omega)
is the standard scalar Wiener process on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}),
where

\Omega=\{\omega\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}):\omega(0)=0\}=:\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}),

endowed with the compact-open topology given by the complete metric

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m}}\frac{\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|_{m}}{1+\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|_{m}},\ \text{ where }\ \|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|_{m}:=\sup_{-m\leq t\leq m}|\omega(t)-\omega^{\prime}(t)|,

and
\mathscr{F}
is the Borel sigma-algebra induced by the compact-open topology of
(\Omega,d_{\Omega}),

\mathbb{P}
is the two-sided Wiener measure on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
. Also, define
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
by

\theta_{t}\omega(\cdot)=\omega(\cdot+t)-\omega(t),\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{ and }\ \omega\in\Omega.

Hence,
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
is a metric dynamical system.

Let us now consider

(3.1)

\displaystyle y(\theta_{t}\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-(t-\xi)}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}(\xi),\ \ \omega\in\Omega,

which is the stationary solution of the one dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck equation

(3.2)

\displaystyle\/\mathrm{d}\/y(\theta_{t}\omega)+y(\theta_{t}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/t=\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}(t).

It is known from
[
30
]
that there exists a
\theta
-invariant subset
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
of full measure such that
y(\theta_{t}\omega)
is continuous in
t
for every
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega},
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{|t|}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{|y(\theta_{t}\omega)|}{|t|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\delta t}|y(\theta_{-t}\omega)|=0,

for all
\delta>0
, where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. For further analysis of this work, we do not distinguish between
\widetilde{\Omega}
and
\Omega
.

Define a new variable
\boldsymbol{v}
by

(3.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})\ \ \ \text{ with }\ \ \ \boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{\tau}\omega)}\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau},

where
\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})
and
y(\theta_{t}\omega)
are the solutions of (
1.2
) and (
3.2
), respectively. Then
\boldsymbol{v}(\cdot):=\boldsymbol{v}(\cdot;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})
satisfies:

(3.5)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\boldsymbol{v}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}+e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\nabla p&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{f},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}&=0,\hskip 48.36967pt\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}&=0,\hskip 48.36967pt\text{ on }\ \partial\mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{v}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\hskip 42.67912pt\text{ in }\mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

in the distributional sense, where
\sigma>0
. It can be easily seen from (
3.5
) that in
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}

(3.6)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\boldsymbol{v}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{f},\ \ \ \ \ t>\tau,\\
\boldsymbol{v}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

Next, we define the vorticity

\varrho:=\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{\partial v_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}.

Then the vorticity equation corresponding to (
3.5
) is given by

(3.7)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\varrho}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\varrho+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla\varrho)&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f},\ \ \ \ \ t>\tau,\\
\varrho|_{t=\tau}&=\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\end{aligned}}}\right.

in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})
, since for the 2D flows the vortex stretching term is zero.

Remark 3.1
.

One can use an another transformation to change the system (
1.2
) into an equivalent pathwise deterministic system. For a given
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\hat{z}(t,\omega)=e^{-\sigma\omega(t)}
. Then,
\hat{z}
satisfies the equation

(3.8)

\displaystyle\/\mathrm{d}\/\hat{z}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\hat{z}\/\mathrm{d}\/t-\sigma\hat{z}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}.

Let
\boldsymbol{v}
be a new random variable given by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})=\hat{z}(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})\ \ \ \text{ with }\ \ \ \boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}=\hat{z}(\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau},

where
\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})
and
\hat{z}(t,\omega)
are the solutions of (
1.2
) and (
3.8
), respectively. Then the new random variable
\boldsymbol{v}(\cdot;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})
satisfies the following system:

(3.10)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\boldsymbol{v}+\frac{1}{\hat{z}(t,\omega)}\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{v}\big)+\hat{z}(t,\omega)\nabla p&=\hat{z}(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{f},\quad t>\tau,\\
\boldsymbol{v}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\qquad\quad x\in\mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

which is a pathwise deterministic system and is equivalent to the system (
1.2
). Even though the system (
3.10
) is easier to handle than the system (
3.5
), there is a technical difficulty in using the transformation (
3.9
) in the context of attractors. Since
\omega(t)
is identified with
\mathrm{W}(t,\omega)
, the change of variable given by (
3.9
) involves the Wiener process explicitly. Therefore,
\hat{z}(t,\omega)
is not a stationary process and the change of variable given by (
3.9
) is not stationary. This fact creates troubles if one uses the change of variable given by (
3.9
) in order to have conjugated random dynamical systems. Since
z(t,\omega)=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}
is stationary, the change of variable is given by a homeomorphism (also called conjugation) which transforms one random dynamical system into an another equivalent one (cf.
[
31
, Proposition B.9]
). For this reason, it is appropriate to use the change of variable mentioned in (
3.4
). We also point out here that the authors in
[
33
]
used the transformation given in (
3.8
) to study the global (or local) existence of smooth pathwise solutions of 2D (or 3D) Euler equations. But they are not investigating any qualitative properties of the solutions (cf.
[
55
]
also). The authors in
[
43
]
used the transformation given in (
3.4
) to study the random dynamics of 2D SNSE on the whole space.

Here, the solvability of stochastic Euler system (
1.2
) has been proved by a vanishing viscosity method. Let us consider the following
Navier-Stokes type equations
:

(3.11)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}&+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\\
+e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\nabla p_{\nu}&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{f},\text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}&=0,\ \ \ \text{ in }\ \mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}&=0,\ \ \ \text{ on }\ \partial\mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}&=0,\ \ \ \text{ on }\ \partial\mathcal{O}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\ \ \text{ in }\mathcal{O},\end{aligned}}}\right.

in the distributional sense, where
\sigma>0
,
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
with
\nu_{0}
sufficiently small (will be specified later) and

\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}=\frac{\partial v_{\nu,2}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial v_{\nu,1}}{\partial x_{2}}.

3.3
Bilinear form and linear operator

Let the space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{div}}(\mathcal{O})
be defined by

\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{div}}(\mathcal{O}):=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})\},

endowed with the inner product
(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{div}}}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})+(\mathrm{div\ }\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\mathrm{div\ }\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})
. Let us define the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})

\displaystyle:=\nu\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(x):\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x-\nu\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(\rho)\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(\rho))\cdot\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}(\rho))\/\mathrm{d}\/\rho

(3.12)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

for all
\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{V}
, where
\kappa(\cdot)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{O})
is the curvature and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{div}}(\mathcal{O});\mathrm{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{O}))\cap\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{O});\mathrm{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{O})),
for
s>0
is the trace operator (cf.
[
2
,
8
,
46
]
). We claim that the bilinear form (
3.3
) is well defined. Indeed, consider

\displaystyle|a(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})|

\displaystyle\leq\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+C\nu\|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{O})}\|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{O})}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\nu,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}}}\right\}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}+C\nu\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}},

which is finite for all
\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{V}
, where we have used the Trace theorems
[
58
, Theorem 1.2, p.7]
and
[
53
, Theorem 3, p.206]
. Moreover, by the definition of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x-\nu\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(\rho)|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(\rho))|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\rho.

By using the Trace theorem, and interpolation, Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(\rho)|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}(\rho))|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\rho}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+C_{\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Therefore, from (
3.14
), we immediately have

(3.16)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu})\geq\frac{\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\nu,\sigma^{2}}}\right\}}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}-C\nu\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

which implies that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is
\mathbb{V}
-coercive
(cf.
[
34
, page 352]
). We set

\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma})=\big\{\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}):(\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})|_{\partial\mathcal{O}}=0\big\},

and define the linear operator
\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}:\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma})\to\mathbb{H}
, as

(3.17)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\rangle=a(\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}),\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{V}.

There is also an extension
\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
of the operator
\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}=-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{\nu}
by an application of the Lax-Milgram lemma.

3.4
Trilinear form and bilinear operator

Let us define the
trilinear form

b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
by

b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})=\int_{\mathcal{O}}(\boldsymbol{u}(x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}(x)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(x)\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}_{j}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x.

If
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}
are such that the linear map
b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbb{V}
, the corresponding element of
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})
. We also denote
\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v})=\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})=(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}
. An integration by parts implies (cf.
[
60
]
)

(3.18)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle&=0,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V},\\
\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle&=-\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{V}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

3.5
Abstract formulation and existence

We can rewrite the system (
1.2
) in the following abstract form in
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
:

(3.19)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{f}+\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu},\ \ \ \ \ t>\tau,\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\sigma>0
,
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
.

Let us now show that there exists a global in time solution to the system (
1.2
) in the following sense:

Definition 3.2
.

A stochastic process
\boldsymbol{u}(t):=\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})
is a
\emph{globalweak(analytic)solution}
of the system (
1.2
) for
t\geq\tau
and
\omega\in\Omega
if for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(\cdot)\in\mathrm{C}([\tau,\infty);\mathbb{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau,\infty;\mathbb{V})

and

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{\psi})+\int_{\tau}^{t}b(\boldsymbol{u}(\xi),\boldsymbol{u}(\xi),\boldsymbol{\psi})\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi=(\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi})+\int_{\tau}^{t}(\boldsymbol{f}(\xi),\boldsymbol{\psi})\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi+\sigma\int_{\tau}^{t}(\boldsymbol{u}(\xi),\boldsymbol{\psi})\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}(\xi),

for every
t\geq\tau
and for every
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\mathbb{V}
.

In order to prove the existence of a solution to the system (
1.2
), it is enough to show that there exists a solution to the system (
3.5
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{V}
,
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
and
\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
. Then, there exists at least one global weak (analytic) solution
\boldsymbol{u}
of the system (
1.2
). In addition,
\boldsymbol{u}(\cdot)
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\bigg[\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}e^{-2\sigma y(\theta_{\tau}\omega)}e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\int_{\xi}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}e^{-2\sigma y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi\bigg],

for all
t\geq\tau
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n},\ldots\}\subset\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
be a complete orthonormal system in
\mathbb{H}
and let
\mathbb{H}_{n}
be the
n
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{H}
spanned by
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. Let us denote the projection of the space
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
into
\mathbb{H}_{n}
by
\mathrm{P}_{n}
, that is,
\mathrm{P}_{n}\boldsymbol{x}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\langle\boldsymbol{x},e_{j}\rangle e_{j}
. As we know that every element
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{H}
induces a functional
\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}
by the formula
\langle\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle=(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}),\;\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}
, then
\mathrm{P}_{n}\big|_{\mathbb{H}}
, the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{H}
onto
\mathbb{H}_{n}
is given by
\mathrm{P}_{n}\boldsymbol{x}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(\boldsymbol{x},e_{j})e_{j}
. Clearly,
\mathrm{P}_{n}
is the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{H}
onto
\mathbb{H}_{n}
. Let us consider the following finite-dimensional system:

(3.21)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\mathrm{P}_{n}\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu})&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\mathrm{P}_{n}\boldsymbol{f}+\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\ \ \ \ \ t>\tau,\\
\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}|_{t=\tau}&=\mathrm{P}_{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{n}\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},e_{i})e_{i}.

The existence of a local solution to the finite-dimensional system (
3.21
) follows by an application of Carathéodory’s existence theorem. Taking the inner product by
\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(\cdot)
in the first equation of (
3.21
), and making use of (
3.3
) and (
3.18
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\quad=\nu\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(\rho)|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t,\rho))|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\rho+e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{f}(t),\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)),

for a.e.
t\geq\tau
. Similar to (
3.3
), we get

(3.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(\rho)|\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(\rho))|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\rho\leq C\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+C_{\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

where
C_{\frac{1}{2}}>0
is a constant. An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities yields

(3.24)

\displaystyle e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}|(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu})|\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\frac{e^{-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}{\sigma^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}.

Combining (
3.5
)-(
3.24
), we obtain (for
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
)

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-2\nu C_{\frac{1}{2}}-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2e^{-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}{\sigma^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{f}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})},

for a.e.
t\geq\tau
. Making use of Gronwall’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-C^{*}(\nu,\sigma)+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}+\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\int_{\xi}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-C^{*}(\nu,\sigma)+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}z^{2}(\xi,\omega)\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi}}\right],

for all
t\geq\tau
,
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
and
C^{*}(\nu,\sigma)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-2\nu C_{\frac{1}{2}}>0
(we choose
\nu_{0}
such that
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-2\nu C_{\frac{1}{2}}>0,
for all
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
). Since
y(\cdot)
is continuous,
\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}\|_{\mathbb{H}}
and
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O}))
, we infer from (
3.5
) that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau+T]}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C<\infty,

where the constant
C
is independent of
n
. Integrating (
3.5
) over
(\tau,\tau+T)
and using (
3.27
), we attain
\{\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V}).

Now, for
\boldsymbol{\psi}(\cdot)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V})
, we consider

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t},\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\right\rangle}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\mathrm{A}_{\nu,\sigma}\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu})-e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\boldsymbol{f}+y(\theta_{t}\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\right\rangle}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\nu|(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi})|+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}}\kappa(y)\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}(t,y))\cdot\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{\psi}(t,y))\/\mathrm{d}\/y}}\right|+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+\sigma|y(\theta_{t}\omega)|}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times|(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\boldsymbol{\psi})|+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}|b(\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu},\boldsymbol{\psi})|+e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}|(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\psi})|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+|y(\theta_{t}\omega)|}}\right]\big(\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu,\tau}\|_{\mathbb{V}}+|y(\theta_{t}\omega)|\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\big)\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{\mathbb{V}},

which immediately gives
\{\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V}^{\prime})

\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V})
such that (as

)

(3.29)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\to

\displaystyle\ \boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\ \text{ in }\ \mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{H})\ \ \text{ strongly},

(3.30)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{n,\nu}\xrightharpoonup{w}

\displaystyle\ \boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\ \text{ in }\ \mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V})\ \ \text{ weakly}.

With the help of the convergences (
3.29
)-(
3.30
) (see
[
58
]
), one can pass the limit

in (

satisfies (
3.19

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\in\mathrm{C}([\tau,\tau+T];\mathbb{H})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(\tau,\tau+T;\mathbb{V}),\ \text{ for all }\ T>0.

\nu
for the
\mathbb{V}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\varrho_{\nu}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}-\nu\Delta\varrho_{\nu}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\varrho_{\nu}+e^{\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}(\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\cdot\nabla\varrho_{\nu})&=e^{-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}+\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)\varrho_{\nu},\ \ \ t>\tau,\\
\varrho_{\nu}|_{t=\tau}&=\nabla\land\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

Taking the inner product with
\varrho_{\nu}
in the first equation of (

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\varrho_{\nu}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\|\varrho_{\nu}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+2\nu\|\nabla\varrho_{\nu}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{\nu}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|\varrho_{\nu}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}+\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\int_{\xi}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/r}e^{-2\sigma y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)}\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi}}\right],

for all
t\geq\tau
. Since

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}&=-\nabla^{\perp}\varrho_{\nu},\ \ \text{ on }\ \mathcal{O},\\

where

\displaystyle(\Delta\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu})=-(\nabla^{\perp}\varrho_{\nu},\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}).

\displaystyle\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

Combining (
3.5
) and (

3.5

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}

Lemma 4.1

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t+2\sigma\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r+2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)\leq-\delta_{1}t,\ \ \ \text{ for all }\ \ \ t\geq t_{1},

and

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t+2\sigma\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)\leq-\delta_{2}t,\ \ \ \text{ for all }\ \ \ t\geq t_{2}.

Taking
t_{0}=\max\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
, we have for
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle L_{1}(t,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\delta_{1}t}\bigg[\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\delta_{1}\xi}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-t_{0}}^{t_{0}}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi+2\sigma\int_{\xi}^{0}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r-2\sigma y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{-\infty}^{-t_{0}}e^{\delta_{1}\xi}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[4\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\delta_{1}\xi}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi+t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi+t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi

\displaystyle\qquad+2e^{-\delta_{1}t}\int_{-t_{0}}^{t_{0}}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi+2\sigma\int_{\xi}^{0}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r-2\sigma y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)}\big\{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}+\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\big\}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi\bigg],

and

\displaystyle L_{2}(t,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[4\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\delta_{2}\xi}\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi+t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi

\displaystyle\qquad+2e^{-\delta_{2}t}\int_{-t_{0}}^{t_{0}}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi+\sigma\int_{\xi}^{0}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r-2\sigma y(\theta_{\xi}\omega)}\|\nabla\land\boldsymbol{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi\bigg].

We infer from Hypothesis
1.2
that
\sup\limits_{t\geq t_{0}}L_{1}(t,\omega)
and
\sup\limits_{t\geq t_{0}}L_{2}(t,\omega)
are bounded. Hence,
\bigcup\limits_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)
is a
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded set. Since
\mathbb{V}
is compactly embedded in
\mathbb{H}
, then arguing similarly as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, one can conclude that
\mathcal{B}_{t_{0}}(\omega):=\overline{\bigcup\limits_{t\geq t_{0}}\mathcal{B}(t,\omega)}^{\delta}
is a
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded set.
∎

Now, we are ready to reveal the main result of this section, that is, weak asymptotic autonomy of pullback stochastic weak attractors.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that Hypotheses
1.1
and
1.2
are satisfied. Then the
\/\mathrm{d}\/_{\mathcal{W}}
-bounded and
\delta
-compact pullback stochastic weak attractor
\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
of the system (
1.2
) and stochastic weak attractor
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
of the system (
4.23
) satisfy the
weak asymptotic autonomy
, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\mathscr{A}(t,\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega))=0,\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.e. }\omega\in\Omega,

where
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Hausdorff semi-distance between two non-empty subsets of some Banach space
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the metric
\delta
, that is, for non-empty sets
A,B\subset\mathcal{W}

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\delta(a,b).

Proof.

Lemma
5.1
, Proposition
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
demonstrate that conditions
\mathrm{(i)}-\mathrm{(iii)}
of Theorem
2.10
are satisfied. Hence, an application of Theorem
2.10
completes the proof.
∎

Preservation of 3 {}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu He ion polarization after laser-plasma acceleration Preprint: APS/123-QED
C. Zheng, P. Fedorets, R. Engels, I. Engin, H. Glückler, C. Kannis, N. Schnitzler, H. Soltner, Z. Chitgar, P. Gibbon, L. Reichwein, A. Pukhov, B. Zielbauer, M. Büscher

Abstract
The preservation of nuclear spin alignment in plasmas is a prerequisite for important applications, such as energy production through polarized fusion or the acceleration of polarized particle beams. Although this conservation property has been the basis of numerous theoretical papers, it has never been experimentally confirmed. Here, we report on first experimental data from a polarized 3 {}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu He target heated by a PW laser pulse, showing evidence for persistence of the nuclear polarization after acceleration to MeV energies. The finding also validates the concept of using pre-polarized targets for experiments at high-power laser facilities.



Spin dynamics in plasmas is an emerging field of research that studies how the spin of particles such as electrons, ions, and positrons evolves in plasma environments, especially under high-energy conditions such as in plasma accelerators or fusion reactors. Key aspects of spin dynamics include generating spin polarization, understanding spin precession and the effects of intense electromagnetic fields in plasma environments. All potential applications critically depend on the ability to maintain the spin alignment of particles (i.e. their polarization) in the plasma for a sufficiently long period of time compared to the typical timescales involved
Thomas et al. 2020
.

The worldwide strategic processes
CERN Yellow Reports: Monographs 2022
;
Fuchs et al. 2022
;
Anderle et al. 2021
aiming at the realization of next generation’s particle accelerators point at the importance of polarized beams (leptons, protons, and ions) for fundamental research and various applications. Dozens of theoretical papers have been published in recent years on the feasibility of polarized beam generation in plasma accelerators (see e.g. Ref.
Büscher et al. 2020
for an overview).

Almost a century ago, M. Goldhaber inferred that fusion cross sections depend on the relative spin orientation of the participating nuclei (i.e. the ’fuel’)
Goldhaber 1934
. Typical enhancement factors, e.g. for the total
d+t\rightarrow\alpha+n
cross section, amount to roughly 50 %
Paetz gen. Schieck 2010
;
Baylor et al. 2023
. In their seminal 1982 paper
Kulsrud et al. 1982
, Kulsrud et al. predicted time scales for polarization loss in a plasma of a magnetic fusion reactor to be much longer than the characteristic fuel burn-up period. A research project to measure the lifetime of spin-polarized fuel at the DIII-D reactor in San Diego is finally under way
Baylor et al. 2023
;
Heidbrink et al. 2024
. Further advantages of polarized fusion are a facilitated neutron management and an increased tritium burn efficiency that may significantly reduce the startup tritium inventory
Parisi et al. 2024
. Note that all these advantages should also apply to inertial fusion
Temporal et al. 2012
; a more comprehensive discussion can be found in Refs.
Paetz gen. Schieck 2010
;
Ciullo et al. 2016
.

The typical association of nuclear spin alignment is with low temperatures, making it counter-intuitive that it could endure in a
10^{8}
Kelvin plasma long enough to have practical applications. On the other hand, a theoretical study
Thomas et al. 2020
of the scaling laws for the depolarization times indicated the feasibility of polarized particle acceleration in strong plasma fields. Experimental tests on the polarization conservation are challenging
Raab et al. 2014
;
Büscher et al. 2020
;
Baylor et al. 2023
;
Heidbrink et al. 2024
and have not been carried out up to now.

Here, we report the findings of an experimental campaign using a nuclear-spin polarized
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He gas jet as a target
Fedorets et al. 2022
for a Petawatt laser pulse.
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He – the isospin partner of
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
H – has the advantage that it is not radioactive, can be polarized at room temperature, and stored over a long time in moderate (mT) magnetic holding fields. Laser-generated
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He plasmas, heated to the fusion resonance energies, are therefore an ideal testbed for polarized fusion. We present the first experimental evidence that the initial nuclear polarization is essentially preserved during the laser-induced heating and ionization and the subsequent acceleration of the ions from the plasma to MeV energies.

Experiment
—The laser pulses were provided by the PHELIX Petawatt laser at GSI Darmstadt
Bagnoud et al. 2010
and contained about 50 J energy each. The optimal pulse duration of 2.2 ps for the acceleration of
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He
1+,2+
ions had been found during a previous measurement with unpolarized gas
Engin et al. 2019

Mrozik et al. 2011

{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He gas was operated at Forschungszentrum Jülich, located roughly 250 km from GSI. Unfortunately, due to small amounts of oxygen leaking into the polarizer, the initial gas polarization did not exceed
\sim
50 %, as opposed to values of
75

Fedorets et al. 2022

1
) every morning. With the help of a non-magnetic gas compressor this pressure can be enhanced before each laser shot to a maximum value of 30 bar. The jet of 1 mm diameter is formed with the help of a titanium de-Laval nozzle mounted on a non-magnetic valve, both located on top of the pressure booster. This leads to super-Gaussian distributed jet densities of a few times
10^{19}
cm
-3
which are required for the acceleration of the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions to MeV energies
Engin et al. 2019
. During the gas compression and jet formation the final
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He polarization decreased to half of the initial value
Fedorets et al. 2022
.

Discussion
—The observed asymmetries are larger than the naively expected
\varepsilon=P\cdot A<0.08
, where the initial gas polarization
P
did not exceed 50 % and the analyzing power
|A|
is smaller than 0.16 (for a maximum
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ion energy of about 4 MeV)
Zheng et al. 2022
;
Engin et al. 2019
. One possible explanation is provided by simulations with the
geant4
code on the effect of asymmetric beam profiles in the polarimeter. These show that the count-rate asymmetries
\varepsilon(\phi)
can be caused not only by the beam polarization but also by a beam profile with an irregular shape in the transverse phase space. Although the observed Rutherford scattering distributions exhibit only small fluctuations in the beam profiles, these can partially account for the observed
\phi
asymmetries and thus mimic a higher polarization value.

In order to gain a better understanding of the prevalent acceleration mechanisms, we have conducted particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations with the
epoch

Arber et al. 2015
and the
vlpl
codes
Pukhov 1999
;
Pukhov 2016
both with the inclusion of the T-BMT equation
Bargmann et al. 1959
describing spin precession. The assumed initial spin direction is that of laser propagation, i.e.
s_{X}=\hbar/2
. When the laser pulse irradiates the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He target, its ponderomotive force expels the electrons mainly towards the front and at right angles to laser propagation, leaving behind a plasma channel with a strong magnetic field (few
10^{3}
T). The charge separation then induces a Coulomb explosion, leading to the acceleration of the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions at a
\pm 90^{\circ}
angle. The same acceleration mechanism was identified in the study of the unpolarized
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He and
{}^{4}\mkern-2.0mu
He targets
Engin et al. 2019
. At the falling edge of the gas jet, a displacement between the electronic and ionic plasma components leads to an acceleration process in forward direction similar to Target Normal Sheath Acceleration
Willingale et al. 2006
;
Lifschitz et al. 2014
. These forward accelerated ions, however, make up only a small part of the total amount of accelerated
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions for our laser-target configuration. If shorter targets were utilized, more ions in forward direction would be expected
Gibbon et al. 2022
. Both simulation codes show that the ions gain only small
s_{Y},s_{Z}
components in their spin vector due to a precession around the strong magnetic field of the plasma channel which is fully compatible with the observed polarization conservation.

{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He
2+

\vec{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{\mathrm{i}}}{m_{\mathrm{i}}}(\vec{v_{\mathrm{i}}}\times\vec{B})

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{\mu_{\mathrm{i}}}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(\vec{\mu_{\mathrm{i}}}\times\vec{B})\,,

(2)

where

We assume a simple model of the magnetic field

around the 1 mm long plasma channel induced by the laser pulse with a focus spot of

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{q_{\mathrm{i}}B}{m_{\mathrm{i}}}

\displaystyle=0.641\times 10^{8}\,B\,[\mathrm{T}]\,\mathrm{rad/s}

Conclusion
—We present results of a first experiment using a polarized target at a high-power laser facility and demonstrate their feasibility for the acceleration of polarized particles. Most notably we observe an angular asymmetry of the
\alpha
particles in our polarimeter which we can only explain by a transverse polarization of the accelerated
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions. It is therefore shown that the transverse polarization of the nuclear spins is at least partially conserved (according to PIC simulations to more than 99%) during plasma heating and acceleration to MeV energies.

An absolute polarization measurement of the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions is not feasible because the values of the
{}^{2}\mkern-2.0mu
H(
\vec{{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu{\mathrm{He}}}
,
{}^{4}\mkern-2.0mu
He)
{}^{1}\mkern-2.0mu
H analyzing power are not known for the full energy range of the accelerated
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions, and the energy distributions of the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions reaching the polarimeters are not precisely known. Our measurements were also limited by the low (
\lesssim 20
% in the jet) initial polarization of the target gas due to vacuum leaks in our
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He polarizer. We note that our conclusions are based on relative measurements (like the change of sign of angular asymmetries in Fig.
2
b) and are averaged over 5–6 laser shots, making them robust to fluctuations of the laser intensity or target density, and plasma filamentation
Engin et al. 2019
.

It is planned to continue the experiments at PHELIX at higher gas polarization, and using a narrower (0.5 mm instead of 1.0 mm) gas-jet target. This would have the advantage that the
{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He ions should be dominantly emitted under
0^{\circ}

;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Wu et al. 2020
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Fan et al. 2022
;
Yan et al. 2022
;
Reichwein et al. 2022

Sofikitis et al. 2018

{}^{3}\mkern-2.0mu
He in order to further study options for polarized fusion with high-power lasers.

Self-embeddings of models of arithmetic; fixed points, small submodels, and extendability
Saeideh Bahrami bahrami.saeideh@gmail.com

Abstract
In this paper we will show that for every cut I I of any countable nonstandard model ℳ \mathcal{M} of I ​ Σ 1 \mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1} , each I I -small Σ 1 \Sigma_{1} -elementary submodel of ℳ \mathcal{M} is of the form of the set of fixed points of some proper initial self-embedding of ℳ \mathcal{M} iff I I is a strong cut of ℳ \mathcal{M} . Especially, this feature will provide us with some equivalent conditions with the strongness of the standard cut in a given countable model ℳ \mathcal{M} of I ​ Σ 1 \mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1} . In addition, we will find some criteria for extendability of initial self-embeddings of countable nonstandard models of I ​ Σ 1 \mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1} to larger models.

\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a submodel of
\mathcal{M}
such that
M_{0}=\{(a)_{i}:\ i\in I\}
for some

(1)

If

, then
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is small.

(2)

If
I
is strong in
\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is
I
-small.

Proof.

First, we will inductively define the following
\Delta_{0}
-function (with parameters) in
\mathcal{M}
:

g(0):=(a)_{0}
,

and

g(x+1):=y

\exists r<\mathrm{Len}(a)\ \left(\begin{array}[]{c}y=(a)_{r}\ \wedge\\
r=\mu_{z}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\exists u<z\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g(x)=(a)_{u}\ \wedge\\
\forall w<z\ ((a)_{w}\neq(a)_{z}\wedge\exists v\leq u((a)_{w}=(a)_{v}))\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right)
.

Note that by the way we defined

, its domain is an initial segment of
\mathcal{M}
, and
{\mathrm{Dom}(g)\leq\mathrm{Len}(a)}
. Moreover, since
I
and
M_{0}
are not
\Delta_{0}
-definable in
\mathcal{M}
, then
I\subsetneq\mathrm{Dom}(g)
. So by
\Sigma_{1}
-induction in
\mathcal{M}
, we can find some

such that
(d)_{i}=g(i)
for every
i\in I
. Clearly,
(d)_{i}\neq(d)_{j}
for every distinct

, and
{M_{0}\subseteq\{(d)_{i}:\ i\in I\}}
. Now, in each case of the statement of theorem we will prove that
\{(d)_{i}:\ i\in I\}\subseteq M_{0}
:

(1)

Suppose

. If
\{(d)_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}\nsubseteq M_{0}
, then there exists the least number

such that
(d)_{n}\notin M_{0}
. So by the definition of

, there exist some

and some

{(d)_{n-1}=(a)_{m}}
and
(d)_{n}=(a)_{r}
. Therefore, by the definition of

, it holds that

, which is a contradiction.

(2)

In the general case with the extra assumption that
I
is strong in
\mathcal{M}
, consider the following partial
\Delta_{0}
-function in
\mathcal{M}
:

h(x):=\mu_{r}((d)_{x}=(a)_{r})
.

Since
I
is strong and
I\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(h)
(because
g
is well-defined on
I
), there exists some
e\in M
such that
h(i)\in I
iff
h(i)<e
, for all
i\in I
. Moreover, by the definition of
d
,
g
and
h
, for every
i\in I
it holds that
(d)_{i}=(a)_{h(i)}
. So it suffices to prove that

for every
i\in I
. Suppose not; so there exists some

which is the least element of

such that

. Now, by the way we defined

and

, it holds that:

\mathcal{M}\models\forall i<h(i_{0})\ ((a)_{i}\neq(a)_{h(i_{0})}\wedge\exists j\leq h(i_{0}-1)((a)_{i}=(a)_{j}))
.

Therefore,
M_{0}=\{x\in M:\ \mathcal{M}\models\exists i\leq h(i_{0}-1)\ x=(a)_{i}\}
. So
M_{0}
is
\Delta_{0}
-definable in
\mathcal{M}
, which is a contradiction.

∎

In the following theorem, we will show that when

is strong, the basic properties which hold for small submodels, also hold for

-small ones.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1}
be nonstandard, and

be a strong cut of
\mathcal{M}
. Then:

(1)

For every

,
\mathrm{K}^{1}(\mathcal{M};I\cup\{a\})
is
I
-small.

(2)

If
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is an

-small submodel of
\mathcal{M}
, then

.

(3)

If
\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{PA}
is countable and recursively saturated, then there exists some
I
-small elementary submodel of
\mathcal{M}
which is not of the form of
\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{M};I\cup\{a\})

.

Proof.

(1)

First fix some
arbitrary
s>I
. So by using strong
\Sigma_{1}
-Collection in
\mathcal{M}
for the formula
\mathrm{Sat}_{\Delta_{0}}(\delta_{r}(i,a,z))
, we will find some
b\in M
such that:

\mathcal{M}\models\forall\langle r,i\rangle<s\ ([f_{r}(i,a)\downarrow]\rightarrow[f_{r}(i,a)\downarrow]^{<b})
.

Then, by using
\Sigma_{1}
-induction we observe that
\mathcal{M}\models\exists y\ \forall\langle r,i\rangle<s\ \varphi(y,r,i,a,b)

\Delta_{0}

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}([f_{r}(i,a)\downarrow]^{<b}\rightarrow(y)_{\langle r,i\rangle}=f_{r}(i,a))\ \wedge\ (\neg[f_{r}(i,a)\downarrow]^{<b}\rightarrow(y)_{\langle r,i\rangle}=0)\end{array}\right)

As a result, if

\mathrm{K}^{1}(\mathcal{M};I\cup\{a\})=\{(d)_{i}:\ i\in I\}.

So by Lemma 3.1,
\mathrm{K}^{1}(\mathcal{M};I\cup\{a\})
is
I
-small in
\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

The exact argument used in
[
4
, Thm. 4.5.1]
works here: let
{M_{0}=\{(a)_{i}:\ i\in I\}}
for some

such that
(a)_{i}\neq(a)_{j}
for all distinct

. Then put:

{Z:=\{\langle y,z\rangle\in M:\ \mathcal{M}\models(a)_{y}=z\}}.

Since

-definable in

, then

. As a result, because

is strong in
\mathcal{M}
,
(I,X)\models\mathrm{PA}^{*}
.
Now, suppose

. So
(I,X)\models\exists x\ (\forall y\ \langle y,x\rangle\notin X)
. Let

. Therefore,
\textbf{x}_{0}\notin M_{0}
. So since
\textbf{x}_{0}\neq 0
, and by the definition of
\textbf{x}_{0}
, we conclude that
\textbf{x}_{0}-1\in M_{0}
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a submodel of
\mathcal{M}
.

(3)

We will generalize the method used in
[
13
, Pro. 2.10]
: let

be a nonstandard inductive satisfaction class for

such that

is recursively saturated. Put
\mathcal{M}^{*}:=(\mathcal{M};S)
, and
\mathcal{N}:=\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{M}^{*};I\cup\{s\})
for some

. First, note that

is
I
-small in
\mathcal{M}
: since
\mathcal{M}^{*}
is a countable recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}^{*}
, so it also possesses an inductive satisfaction class. Moreover,

is also strong in
\mathcal{M}^{*}
. Therefore, by repeating the argument used in the proof of part (1) of this theorem, and Lemma 3.1(2), we can show that

is
I
-small in
\mathcal{M}
.

Moreover, on one hand, it is easy to see that

is a nonstandard satisfaction class for the
\mathcal{L}_{A}
-structure
\mathcal{N}
. So
\mathcal{N}
is also a recursively saturated model of
\mathrm{PA}
. On the other hand,

is a proper initial segment of
\mathcal{N}
(because

). Therefore,
\mathcal{N}
is of the form of
\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{M};I\cup\{a\})
for no

.

∎

The following lemma will be useful in the proof of the main theorem of this section:

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{I}\Sigma_{1}
,

, and

Theorem 3.4

-elementary submodel of

and all

.

Then, by using back-and-forth method we will inductively build finite functions

, and

;

\mathrm{Q}(\bar{u},\bar{v})\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}[f(\bar{u},(a)_{i})\downarrow]\wedge[f(\bar{v},(a)_{i})\downarrow]^{<b}\wedge\\

(1):\ \

-definable in

-definable function in

, and

iff

. We will show that

,

. So by statement

(\ast_{k})

is not strong; i.e. there exists some coded function
f
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
I\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}(f)
, and the set
{D:=\{f(i):\ i\in I\wedge I<f(i)\}}
is downward cofinal in
M\setminus I
.

Let
b\in M\setminus M_{0}
and
g:=j(f)
. For every
k\in M
, we put:

A_{k}:=\{\langle r,y\rangle<k:\ \mathcal{M}\models\mathrm{Sat}_{\Delta_{0}}(\delta_{r}((a)_{y},b))\}
.

Since
A_{k}
is bounded and
\Delta_{1}
-definable, it is coded by some

in
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, the function

is
\Delta_{1}
-definable in
\mathcal{M}
. Now, we define:

.

So note that:

(I)

For every
k>I
, we have
\mathrm{Th}_{\Delta_{0}}(\mathcal{M};b,\{(a)_{i}\}_{i\in I})\subseteq\mathrm{Th}_{\Delta_{0}}(\mathcal{M};h(k),\{(a)_{i}\}_{i\in I})
.

(II)

For every
i\in I
,
h(i)
is well-defined and inside
M_{0}=\mathrm{Fix}(j)
; the reason behind this statement is that for every
i\in I
we consider the following set:

.

Then, by Lemma 3.3,
B_{i}
is coded by some

. So it holds that:

\mathcal{M}\models h(i)=\mu_{x}(\forall\langle r,\epsilon\rangle\mathrm{E}\alpha_{i}\ (\mathrm{Sat}_{\Delta_{0}}(\delta_{r}(\epsilon,x)))
.

As a result, since
\mathcal{M}_{0}\prec_{\Sigma_{1}}\mathcal{M}
, statement (II) holds.

Now, let

. So for all
i\in I
, and all

such that

h^{\prime}(g(u))=j(h)(j(f)(u))=j(h)(j(f)(j(u)))=j(h(f(u)))=h(f(u)).

Therefore, for all

, where

is the following
\Delta_{1}
-formula:

\forall u<i\ (f(u)<i\rightarrow h(f(u))=h^{\prime}(g(u)))

So by
\Sigma_{1}
-Overspill in
\mathcal{M}
, there exists some

such that:

(\spadesuit):\ \

\forall u<s\ (f(u)<s\rightarrow h(f(u))=h^{\prime}(g(u)))
.

Since
D
is downward cofinal in
M\setminus I
, there is some
i_{0}\in I
such that
I<f(i_{0})<s
. Let

. On one hand, by (I),
\mathrm{Th}_{\Delta_{0}}(\mathcal{M};b,\{(a)_{i}\}_{i\in I})\subseteq\mathrm{Th}_{\Delta_{0}}(\mathcal{M};c,\{(a)_{i}\}_{i\in I})

, we have

j(c)=j(h(f(i_{0})))=j(h)(j(f)((j(i_{0})))=h^{\prime}(g(i_{0}))=h(f(i_{0}))=c

As a result,

\mathcal{M}
.

∎

(2)

(1)

By Theorem 2.2(2), and part (1) of this lemma, it suffices to prove that

\Pi_{1}
-definable in

. Suppose not; i.e.

∎

Definition 2

First, in the next lemma we will show that the condition in the above definition, i.e. preserving coded subsets, is a necessary condition for extendability of an initial embedding:

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{M}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

Put

, and let

be arbitrary. If
{A=I\cap(\alpha_{\mathrm{E}})^{\mathcal{M}}}
for some

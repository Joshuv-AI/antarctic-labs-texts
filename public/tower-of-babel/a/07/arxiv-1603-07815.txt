Concatenation Theorems for Anti-Gowers-Uniform Functions and Host-Kra Characteristic Factors
Tamar Ziegler

Abstract
We establish a number of “concatenation theorems” that assert, roughly speaking, that if a function exhibits “polynomial” (or “Gowers anti-uniform”, “uniformly almost periodic”, or “nilsequence”) behaviour in two different directions separately, then it also exhibits the same behavior (but at higher degree) in both directions jointly. Among other things, this allows one to control averaged local Gowers uniformity norms by global Gowers uniformity norms. In a sequel to this paper, we will apply such control to obtain asymptotics for “polynomial progressions” n + P 1 ​ ( r ) , … , n + P k ​ ( r ) n+P_{1}(r),\dots,n+P_{k}(r) in various sets of integers, such as the prime numbers.

1.1
Concatenation of polynomiality

Suppose

Proposition 1.2

(Concatenation theorem for polynomials)

Proof.

The claim is trivial if

P(x+h_{1})=P(x)+P_{h_{1}}(x)

(3)

for all
x\in G

P_{h_{2}}\colon G\to K
of degree
{<}d_{2}-1
along
H_{2}
such that

P(x+h_{2})=P(x)+P_{h_{2}}(x)

(4)

for all
x\in G

and combining with (

P(x+h_{1}+h_{2})=P(x)+P_{h_{1},h_{2}}(x)

{<}d_{2}

{<}d_{2}

{<}d_{1}+d_{2}-2

{<}d_{1}+d_{2}-2

The proofs of these theorems will broadly follow the same induction strategy that appeared in the above proof of Proposition

1.3
Concatenation of anti-uniformity

Now we turn to a more non-trivial variant of Proposition
1.2
, in which the property of being polynomial in various directions is replaced by that of being
anti-uniform
in the sense of being almost orthogonal to Gowers uniform functions. To make this concept precise, and to work at a suitable level of generality, we need some notation. Recall that a
finite multiset
is the same concept as a finite set, but in which elements are allowed to appear with multiplicity.

Definition 1.6

(Sumset and averaging)
.

If
A\coloneqq\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
and
B\coloneqq\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}
are finite non-empty multisets in an additive group
G=(G,+)
, we define the sumset
A+B\coloneqq\{a_{i}+b_{j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m\}
and difference set
A-B\coloneqq\{a_{i}-b_{j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m\}
, where multiplicity is counted; thus for instance we always have
|A+B|=|A-B|=|A||B|
, where
|A|
denotes the cardinality of
A
, counting multiplicity. Note that
A+B
or
A-B
may contain multiplicity even if
A
and
B
does not, for instance
\{1,2\}+\{1,2\}=\{2,3,3,4\}
. If
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
is a function, we use the averaging notation

\mathbb{E}_{a\in A}f(a)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}f(a)

where the sum
\sum_{a\in A}
is also counting multiplicity. For instance,
\mathbb{E}_{a\in\{1,2,2\}}a=\frac{5}{3}
.

Definition 1.7

(
G
-system)
.

Let
G=(G,+)
be an at most countable additive group. A
G
-system

(\mathbf{X},T)
is a probability space
\mathbf{X}=(X,{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
, together with a collection
T=(T^{g})_{g\in G}
of invertible measure-preserving maps
T^{g}\colon X\to X
, such that
T^{0}
is the identity and
T^{g+h}=T^{g}T^{h}
for all
g,h\in G
. For technical reasons we will require that the probability space
\mathbf{X}
is countably generated modulo null sets (or equivalently, that the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbf{X})
is separable). Given a measurable function
f\colon\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{C}
and
g\in G
, we define
T^{g}f\coloneqq f\circ T^{-g}
. We shall often abuse notation and abbreviate
(\mathbf{X},T)
as
\mathbf{X}
.

Remark 1.8
.

As it turns out, a large part of our analysis would be valid even when
G
was an uncountable additive group (in particular, no amenability hypothesis on
G
is required); however the countable case is the one which is the most important for applications, and so we shall restrict to this case to avoid some minor technical issues involving measurability. Once the group
G
is restricted to be countable, the requirement that
\mathbf{X}
is countably generated modulo null sets is usually harmless in applications, as one can often easily reduce to this case. In combinatorial applications, one usually works with the case when
G
is a finite group, and
\mathbf{X}
is
G
with the uniform probability measure and the translation action
T^{g}x\coloneqq x+g
, but for applications in ergodic theory, and also because we will eventually apply an ultraproduct construction to the combinatorial setting, it will be convenient to work with the more general setup in Definition
1.7
.

Definition 1.9

(Gowers uniformity norm)
.

Let
G
be an at most countable additive group, and let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system. If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
,
Q
is a non-empty finite multiset and
d
is a positive integer, we define the
Gowers uniformity (semi-)norm

\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}
by the formula

\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{h_{1},\dots,h_{d},h^{\prime}_{1},\dots,h^{\prime}_{d}\in Q}\int_{X}\Delta_{h_{1},h^{\prime}_{1}}\dots\Delta_{h_{d},h^{\prime}_{d}}f\ d\mu

(7)

where
\Delta_{h,h^{\prime}}
is the nonlinear operator

\Delta_{h,h^{\prime}}f\coloneqq(T^{h}f)\overline{(T^{h^{\prime}}f)}.

(8)

More generally, given
d
non-empty finite multisets
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}
, we define the
Gowers box (semi-)norm

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
by the formula

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{h_{i},h^{\prime}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\int_{X}\Delta_{h_{1},h^{\prime}_{1}}\dots\Delta_{h_{d},h^{\prime}_{d}}f\ d\mu,

so in particular

\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}=\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q,\dots,Q}(\mathbf{X})}.

Note that the
\Delta_{h,h^{\prime}}
commute with each other, and so the ordering of the
Q_{i}
is irrelevant.

It is well known that
\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}
and
\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
are indeed semi-norms; see
[
23
, Appendix B]
. We will be primarily interested in the Gowers norms for a specific type of multiset

, namely the
coset progressions
that arise in additive combinatorics (see
[
35
]
). The original Gowers uniformity norms from
[
12
,
13
]
correspond to the case when

is a finite cyclic group, and
\mathbf{X}=G
is equipped with the uniform probability measure and the translation action of

.

Definition 1.10

(Coset progression)
.

A (symmetric)

is a (multi-)subset
2
2

2

Strictly speaking, one should refer to the tuple

Our first main theorem is analogous to Proposition

Theorem 1.11

(Concatenation theorem for anti-uniformity norms)
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}
be coset progressions of ranks
r_{1},r_{2}
respectively in an at most countable additive group
G
, let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system, let
d_{1},d_{2}
be positive integers, and let
f
lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
. Let
c_{1},c_{2}\colon(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
be functions such that
c_{i}(\varepsilon)\to 0
as

for

(i)

\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{Q_{1}}(\mathbf{X})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq c_{1}(\varepsilon)

\varepsilon>0

(ii)

\|f\|_{U^{d_{2}}_{Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq c_{2}(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon>0

Then there exists a function

, such that

\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{Q_{1}+Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq c(\varepsilon)

\varepsilon>0

Heuristically, hypothesis (i) (resp. (ii)) is asserting that

1.4
Concatenation of characteristic factors

Analogues of the above results can be obtained for characteristic factors of the Gowers-Host-Kra seminorms
[
30

in ergodic theory. To construct these factors for arbitrary abelian group actions (including uncountable ones), it is convenient to introduce the following notation (which should be viewed as a substitute for the machinery of Følner sequences that does not require amenability). Given an additive group

, we consider the set
{\mathcal{F}}[H]
of non-empty finite multisets

in

a directed set by declaring

if one has

; note that any two

have a common upper bound

. One can then define convergence along nets in the usual fashion: given a sequence of elements

of a Hausdorff topological space indexed by the non-empty finite multisets

\lim_{Q\to H}x_{Q}=x

\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{k})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{k})}x_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{k}}

ranges over finite non-empty multisets in

, using the product directed set
{\mathcal{F}}[H_{1}]\times\dots\times{\mathcal{F}}[H_{d}]
. Thus for instance
\lim_{(Q_{1},Q_{2})\to(H_{1},H_{2})}x_{Q_{1},Q_{2}}=x
if, for every neighbourhood

in

respectively such that

and

. If the

or

take values in

1.5
Globalizing uniformity

We have seen that anti-uniformity can be “concatenated”, in that functions which are approximately orthogonal to functions that are locally Gowers uniform in two different directions are necessarily also approximately orthogonal to more globally Gowers uniform functions. By duality, one then expects to be able to decompose a globally Gowers uniform function into functions that are locally Gowers uniform in different directions. For instance, in the ergodic setting, one has the following consequence of Theorem

:

L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{1}}_{H_{1}}(\mathbf{X}))
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{2}}_{H_{2}}(\mathbf{X}))
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{H_{1}+H_{2}}(\mathbf{X}))
are orthogonal, and the claim follows.
∎

One can similarly use Theorem
1.16
to obtain

Corollary 1.21
.

Suppose that
G
is the sum of a finitely generated group and a profinite group. Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system, let
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
be positive integers, and let
H_{1,1}
,
\dots
,
H_{1,d_{1}}
,
H_{2,1}
,
\dots
,
H_{2,d_{2}}
be subgroups of
G
. If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
is orthogonal to
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{<d_{1}d_{2}}_{(H_{1,i}+H_{2,j})_{1\leq i\leq d_{1},1\leq j\leq d_{2}}}(\mathbf{X}))
, then one can write
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
is orthogonal to

L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{<d_{1}}_{H_{1,1},\dots,H_{1,d_{1}}}(\mathbf{X}))
and
f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
is orthogonal to
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{<d_{2}}_{H_{2,1},\dots,H_{2,d_{2}}}(\mathbf{X}))
; furthermore,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are orthogonal to each other.

We can use the orthogonality in Corollary
1.20
to obtain a Bessel-type inequality:

Corollary 1.22

(Bessel inequality)
.

Let
G
be an at most countable additive group. Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system, let
(H_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite family of subgroups of
G
, and let
(d_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of positive integers.
Then for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
, we have

\sum_{i\in I}\|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}}_{H_{i}}(\mathbf{X}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}\left(\sum_{i,j\in I}\|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}+d_{j}-1}_{H_{i}+H_{j}}(\mathbf{X}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(14)

Proof.

Write
f_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}(f|\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}}_{H_{i}}(\mathbf{X}))
. We can write the left-hand side of (
14
) as

\left\langle f,\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\right\rangle

which by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is bounded by

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}\left|\sum_{i,j\in I}\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle\right|^{1/2}.

But from Corollary
1.20
,
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}}_{H_{i}}(\mathbf{X}))
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{j}}_{H_{j}}(\mathbf{X}))
are orthogonal on the orthogonal complement of

L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}+d_{j}-1}_{H_{i}+H_{j}}(\mathbf{X}))
, hence

\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle=\left\|\mathbb{E}(f|\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d_{i}+d_{j}-1}_{H_{i}+H_{j}}(\mathbf{X}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}

and the claim follows.
∎

One can use Corollary
1.21
to obtain an analogous Bessel-type inequality involving finitely generated subgroups
H_{i,k}
,
k=1,\dots,d_{i}
, which we leave to the interested reader.

Returning now to the finitary Gowers norms, one has a qualitative analogue of the Bessel inequality involving the Gowers uniformity norms:

Theorem 1.23

(Qualitative Bessel inequality for uniformity norms)
.

Let
(Q_{i})_{i\in I}
be a finite non-empty family of coset progressions
Q_{i}
, all of rank at most
r
, in an additive group
G
. Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system, and let
d
be a positive integer. Let
f
lie in the unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
, and suppose that

\mathbb{E}_{i,j\in I}\|f\|_{U^{2d-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{i}+\varepsilon Q_{j}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\varepsilon

(15)

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{i\in I}\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q_{i}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq c(\varepsilon)

(16)

where
c\colon(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a function such that
c(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Furthermore,
c
depends only on
r
and
d
. (In particular,
c
is independent of the size of
I
.)

We prove this theorem in Section
8
. We remark that the theorem is only powerful when the cardinality of the set
I
is large compared to
\varepsilon
, otherwise the claim would easily follow from considering the diagonal contribution
i=j
to (
15
).

Theorem
1.23
has an analogue for the Gowers box norms:

Theorem 1.24

(Qualitative Bessel inequality for box norms)
.

Let
d
be a positive integer. For each
1\leq j\leq d
, let
(Q_{i,j})_{i\in I}
be a finite family of coset progressions
Q_{i,j}
, all of rank at most
r
, in an additive group
G
. Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system. Let
f
lie in the unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
, and suppose that

\mathbb{E}_{i,j\in I}\|f\|_{\Box^{d^{2}}_{(\varepsilon Q_{i,k}+\varepsilon Q_{j,l})_{1\leq k\leq d,1\leq l\leq d}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{i\in I}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{i,1},\dots,Q_{i,d}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq c(\varepsilon)

where
c\colon(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a function such that
c(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Furthermore,

depends only on
r
and

.

Remark 1.25
.

In view of Theorem
1.23
, one may ask if the smallness of
\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{1}+\varepsilon Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})}
implies the smallness of
\min(\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{Q_{1}}(\mathbf{X})},\|f\|_{U^{d_{2}}_{Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})})
(or, taking contrapositives, the largeness of
\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{Q_{1}}(\mathbf{X})}
and
\|f\|_{U^{d_{2}}_{Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})}
implies some non-trivial lower bound for
\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{1}+\varepsilon Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})}
). Unfortunately this is not the case; a simple counterexample is provided by a function
f
of the form
f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}
is a bounded function with large
U^{d_{1}}_{Q_{1}}(\mathbf{X})
norm but small
U^{d_{2}}_{Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})
and
U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{1}+\varepsilon Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})
norm (which is possible for instance if
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are large subgroups of
G
,
\mathbf{X}=G
with the translation action, and
f_{1}
is constant in the
Q_{1}
direction but random in the
Q_{2}
direction), and vice versa for
f_{2}
.

1.6
A sample application

In a sequel to this paper
[
36
]
, we will use the concatenation theorems proven in this paper to study polynomial patterns in sets such as the primes. Here, we will illustrate how this is done with a simple example, namely controlling the average

A_{N,M}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[M]}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x+nm)f_{3}(x+nk)f_{4}(x+nm+nk)

for functions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\colon\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
on a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, where
[M]\coloneqq\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\leq M\}
. The natural range of parameters is when
M
is comparable to
\sqrt{N}
. In that case we will be able to control this expression by the global Gowers
U^{3}
norm:

Proposition 1.26
.

Let
N\geq 1
be a natural number, let
M
be such that
C^{-1}\sqrt{N}\leq M\leq C\sqrt{N}
for some
C>0
, and let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\colon\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
be functions bounded in magnitude by
1
. Suppose that

\|f_{i}\|_{U^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}\leq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
and
i=1,\dots,4
, where we give
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
the uniform probability measure. Then

|A_{N,M}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})|\leq c(\varepsilon)

for some quantity
c(\varepsilon)
depending only on
\varepsilon
and
C
that goes to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

For instance, using this proposition (embedding
[N]
in, say,
\mathbb{Z}/5N\mathbb{Z}
) and the known uniformity properties of the Möbius function
\mu
(see
[
22
]
) we can now obtain the asymptotic

\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[\sqrt{N}]}\mu(x)\mu(x+nm)\mu(x+nk)\mu(x+nm+nk)=o(1)

as
N\to\infty
; we leave the details to the interested reader. As far as we are able to determine, this asymptotic appears to be new. In the sequel
[
36
]
to this paper we will consider similar asymptotics involving various polynomial averages such as
\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{n\in[\sqrt{N}]}f(x)f(x+n^{2})f(x+2n^{2})
, and arithmetic functions such as the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda
.

We prove Proposition
1.26
in Section
9
. Roughly speaking, the strategy is to observe that
A_{N,M}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})
can be controlled by averages of “local” Gowers norms of the form
U^{2}_{Q}
, where
Q
is an arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
of length comparable to
M
. Each individual such norm is not controlled directly by the
U^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}
norm, due to the sparseness of
Q
; however, after invoking Theorem
1.23
, we can control the averages of the
U^{2}_{Q}
norms with averages of
U^{3}_{Q+Q^{\prime}}
norms, where
Q,Q^{\prime}
are two arithmetic progressions of length comparable to
M
. For typical choices of
Q,Q^{\prime}
, the rank two progerssion
Q+Q^{\prime}
will be quite dense in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, allowing one to obtain the proposition.

One would expect that the
U^{3}
norm in Proposition
1.26
could be replaced with a
U^{2}
norm. Indeed this can be done invoking the inverse theorem for the
U^{3}
norm
[
21
]
as well as equidistribution results for nilsequences
[
26
]
:

Proposition 1.27
.

The
U^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
norm in Proposition
1.26
may be replaced with
U^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

This result can be proven by adapting the arguments based on the arithmetic regularity lemma in
[
25
, §7]
; we sketch the key new input required in Section
10
.
In the language of Gowers and Wolf
[
15
]
, this proposition asserts that the
true complexity
of the average
A_{N,M}
is
1
, rather than
2
(the latter being roughly analogous to the “Cauchy-Schwarz complexity” discussed in
[
15
]
). This drop in complexity is consistent with similar results established in the ergodic setting in
[
4
]
, and in the setting of linear patterns in
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
25
]
, and is proven in a broadly similar fashion to these results. In principle, Proposition
1.27
is purely an assertion in “linear” Fourier analysis, since it only involves the
U^{2}
Gowers norm, but we do not know of a way to establish it without exploiting both the concatenation theorem and the inverse
U^{3}
theorem.

2
The ergodic theory limit

In this section we show how to obtain the main ergodic theory results of this paper (namely, Theorem
1.15
and Theorem
1.16
) as a limiting case of their respective finitary results, namely Theorem
1.11
and Theorem
1.13
. The technical hypothesis that the group
G
be the sum of a finitely generated group and a profinite group will only be needed near the end of the section. Readers who are only interested in the combinatorial assertions of this paper can skip ahead to Section
4
.

We first develop some fairly standard material on the convergence of the Gowers-Host-Kra norms, and on the existence of characteristic factors. Given a
G
-system
(\mathbf{X},T)
, finite non-empty multisets
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}
of
G
, and elements
f_{\omega}
of
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
for each
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, define the
Gowers inner product

\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\int_{X}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|}T^{h^{\omega_{1}}_{1}+\dots+h^{\omega_{d}}_{d}}f_{\omega}\ d\mu

(17)

where
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})
,
|\omega|\coloneqq\omega_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}
, and
{\mathcal{C}}\colon f\mapsto\overline{f}
is the complex conjugation operator; the absolute convergence of the integral is guaranteed by Hölder’s inequality. Comparing this with Definition
1.9
, we see that

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}=\langle(f)_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}.

We also recall the
Cauchy-Schwarz-Gowers inequality

|\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}|\leq\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}

(18)

(see
[
20
, Lemma B.2]
). By setting
f_{\omega}
to equal
f
when
\omega_{d^{\prime}+1}=\dots=\omega_{d}=0
, and equal to
1
otherwise, we obtain as a corollary the monotonicity property

\|f\|_{\Box^{d^{\prime}}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d^{\prime}}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

(19)

for all
1\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
and
f\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
.

We have the following convergence result:

Theorem 2.1

(Existence of Gowers-Host-Kra seminorm)
.

Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a
G
-system, let
d
be a natural number, and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
be subgroups of
G
. For each
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, let
f_{\omega}
be an element of
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
. Then the limit

\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{H_{1},\dots,H_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

exists. In particular, the limit

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{H_{1},\dots,H_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

exists for any
f\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
.

It is likely that one can deduce this theorem from the usual ergodic theorem, by adapting the arguments in
[
30
]
, but we will give a combinatorial proof here, which applies even in cases in which
G
or
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
are uncountable.

Proof.

By multilinearity we may assume that the
f_{\omega}
are all real-valued, so that we may dispense with the complex conjugation operations. We also normalise the
f_{\omega}
to lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
.

If
\vec{f}\coloneqq(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}
is a tuple of functions
f_{\omega}\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
and
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
, we say that
\vec{f}
is
d^{\prime}
-symmetric
if we have
f_{\omega}=f_{\omega^{\prime}}
whenever
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})
and
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\omega^{\prime}_{d})
agree in the first
d^{\prime}
components (that is,
\omega_{i}=\omega^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d^{\prime}
). We will prove Theorem
2.1
by downward induction on
d^{\prime}
, with the
d^{\prime}=0
case establishing the full theorem.

Thus, assume that
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
and that the claim has already been proven for larger values of
d^{\prime}
(this hypothesis is vacuous for
d^{\prime}=d
).
We will show that for any given (and sufficiently small)
\varepsilon>0
, and for sufficiently large
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})
(in the product directed set
{\mathcal{F}}[H_{1}]\times\dots\times{\mathcal{F}}[H_{d}]
), the quantity
\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
only increases by at most
\varepsilon
if one increases any of the
Q_{i}
, that is to say

\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{i-1},Q_{i}+R,Q_{i+1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}+\varepsilon

(20)

for any
i=1,\dots,d
and any finite non-empty multiset
R
. Applying this once for each
i
, we see that the limit superior of the
\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
does not exceed the limit inferior by more than
d\varepsilon
, and sending
\varepsilon\to 0
(and using the boundedness of the
\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
, from Hölder’s inequality) we obtain the claim.

It remains to establish (
20
). There are two cases, depending on whether
i\leq d^{\prime}
or
i>d^{\prime}
. First suppose that
i\leq d^{\prime}
; by relabeling we may take
i=1
. Using (
17
) and the
d^{\prime}
-symmetry (and hence
1
-symmetry) of
\vec{f}
, we may rewrite
\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
as

\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=2,\dots,d}\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}\in Q_{1}}T^{h_{1}}f_{h^{0}_{2},h^{1}_{2},\dots,h^{0}_{d},h^{1}_{d}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}

where

\displaystyle f_{h^{0}_{2},h^{1}_{2},\dots,h^{0}_{d},h^{1}_{d}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{d}\in\{0,1\}}T^{h^{\omega_{2}}_{2}+\dots+h^{\omega_{d}}_{d}}f_{(0,\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{d})}

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{d}\in\{0,1\}}T^{h^{\omega_{2}}_{2}+\dots+h^{\omega_{d}}_{d}}f_{(1,\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{d})}.

From the unitarity of shift operators and the triangle inequality, we have

\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}\in Q_{1}+R}T^{h_{1}}f_{h^{0}_{2},h^{1}_{2},\dots,h^{0}_{d},h^{1}_{d}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}\in Q_{1}}T^{h_{1}}f_{h^{0}_{2},h^{1}_{2},\dots,h^{0}_{d},h^{1}_{d}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}

for any finite non-empty multiset
R
in
H_{1}
. This gives (
20
) (without the epsilon loss!) in the case
i\leq d^{\prime}
.

Now suppose
i>d^{\prime}
. By relabeling we may take
i=d
. In this case, we rewrite
\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
as

\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\left\langle\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}},\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{1}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}

(21)

where

f^{\omega_{d}}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\coloneqq\prod_{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d-1}\in\{0,1\}}T^{h^{\omega_{1}}_{1}+\dots+h^{\omega_{d-1}}_{d-1}}f_{(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})}.

Note from Hölder’s inequality that the
f^{\omega_{d}}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}
all lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{2}(\mathbf{X})
.

A similar rewriting shows that the quantity

\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}

is the Gowers product
\langle(f_{(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d-1},0)})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}
. This tuple of functions is
d^{\prime}+1
-symmetric after rearrangement, and so by induction hypothesis this expression converges to a limit as
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})
. In particular, for
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}+\{0,n\}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{10}

for any
n\in H_{d}
. By the parallelogram law, this implies that

\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}+\{n\}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}-\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon}{10}

for all
n\in H_{d}
, which by the triangle inequality implies that

\mathbb{E}_{h^{0}_{i},h^{1}_{i}\in Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d-1}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}+R}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}-\mathbb{E}_{h_{d}\in Q_{d}}T^{h_{d}}f^{0}_{h^{0}_{1},h^{1}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{d-1},h^{1}_{d-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}^{2}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon}{10}

for any finite non-empty multiset
R
in
H_{d}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz and (
21
), this implies (for
\varepsilon
small enough) that

\left|\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d-1},Q_{d}+R}(\mathbf{X})}-\langle\vec{f}\rangle_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\right|\leq\varepsilon

which gives (
20
).
∎

We observe that the above argument (specialised to the
d
-symmetric case) also gives that

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{H_{1},\dots,H_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

(22)

whenever
Q_{i}
are finite non-empty multisets in

for any subgroup

of

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a

We can similarly define the local dual operators

in

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a
G
-system, let

be subgroups of
G
, and let

{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{H_{1},\dots,H_{d}}((f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}})

(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})
.

Proof.

The claim is trivially true for

, so assume

. By multilinearity we may assume the

are real. By a limiting argument using Hölder’s inequality, we may assume without loss of generality that

all lie in

and not just in

, in which case the
{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}((f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}})
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
. Theorem
2.1
already gives weak convergence, so it suffices to show that the limit

\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}((f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}})

topology.

By the cosine rule (and the completeness of

), it suffices to show that the joint limit

\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d},Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d},H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\left\langle{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}((f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}),{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d}}((f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}

exists. But the expression inside the limit can be written as an inner product

\langle(\tilde{f}_{\omega^{\prime}})_{\omega^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{2d}}\rangle_{\Box^{2d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}}

where
\tilde{f}_{(0,\omega)}=\tilde{f}_{(\omega,0)}\coloneqq f_{\omega}
for
\omega\coloneqq\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}
, and
\tilde{f}_{\omega^{\prime}}\coloneqq 1
for all other

, and the claim then follows from Theorem
2.1
(with
d
replaced by
2d
).
∎

In the

{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{H}f=\lim_{Q\to H}\mathbb{E}_{a\in Q}T^{a}f

in the strong topology of

for

. From this definition it is easy to see that

is

{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{H}f=\mathbb{E}(f|\mathbf{X}^{H})

In particular, we have

for all

. For

A key property concerning dual functions is that they are closed under multiplication after taking convex closures:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a
G
-system, and let

be subgroups of

. Let

Proof.

We may assume

F,F^{\prime}
are themselves dual functions, thus

and

for some

in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
.

By Proposition
2.2
, we can write

as

\displaystyle\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\lim_{(Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in Q_{i}-Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in Q^{\prime}_{i}-Q^{\prime}_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}(T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}f_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}k_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}k_{d}}f^{\prime}_{\omega})

where the limits are in the

topology.
For any given

and averaging over

is larger than the progression

\displaystyle\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\lim_{(Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in Q_{i}-Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in Q^{\prime}_{i}-Q^{\prime}_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}(T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}f_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}(h_{1}+k_{1})+\dots+\omega_{d}(h_{d}+k_{d})}f^{\prime}_{\omega}).

By Proposition
2.2
(with

\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d},Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d},H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}

\displaystyle\lim_{(Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\lim_{(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in Q_{i}-Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in Q^{\prime}_{i}-Q^{\prime}_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}(T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}f_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}(h_{1}+k_{1})+\dots+\omega_{d}(h_{d}+k_{d})}f^{\prime}_{\omega}).

\lim_{(Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{d})\to(H_{1},\dots,H_{d})}\mathbb{E}_{k\in Q^{\prime}_{i}-Q^{\prime}_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{H_{1},\dots,H_{d}}\left((f_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}k_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}k_{d}}f^{\prime}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\right).

5
Taking ultraproducts

To prove our main combinatorial theorems rigorously, it is convenient to use the device of ultraproducts to pass to a nonstandard analysis formulation, in order to hide most of the “epsilon management”, as well as to exploit infinitary tools such as countable saturation, Loeb measure and conditional expectation. The use of nonstandard analysis to analyze Gowers uniformity norms was first introduced by Szegedy
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
, and also used by Green and the authors in
[
28
]
.

We quickly set up the necessary formalism. (See for instance
[
11
]
for an introduction to the foundations of nonstandard analysis that is used here.) We will need to fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\alpha\in\beta\mathbb{N}\backslash\mathbb{N}
on the natural numbers, thus
\alpha
is a collection of subsets of natural numbers such that the function
A\mapsto 1_{A\in\alpha}
forms a finitely additive
\{0,1\}
-valued probability measure on
\mathbb{N}
, which assigns zero measure to every finite set. The existence of such a non-principal ultrafilter is guaranteed by the axiom of choice. We refer to elements of
\alpha
as
\alpha
-large sets
.

We assume the existence of a
standard universe

{\mathfrak{U}}
- a set that contains all the mathematical objects of interest to us, in particular containing all the objects mentioned in the theorems in the introduction. Objects in this universe will be referred to as
standard
objects. A
standard set
is a set consisting entirely of standard objects, and a
standard function
is a function whose domain and range are standard sets. The standard universe will not need to obey all of the usual ZFC set theory axioms (though one can certainly assume this if desired, given a suitable large cardinal axiom); however we will at least need this universe to be closed under the ordered pair construction
x,y\mapsto(x,y)
, so that the Cartesian product of finitely many standard sets is again a standard set.

A
nonstandard object
is an equivalence class of tuples
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
of standard objects indexed by an
\alpha
-large set
A
, with two tuples
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
,
(y_{n})_{n\in B}
equivalent if they agree on an
\alpha
-large set. We write
\lim_{n\to\alpha}x_{n}
for the equivalence class associated with a tuple
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
, and refer to this nonstandard object as the
ultralimit
of the
x_{n}
. Thus for instance a
nonstandard natural number
is an ultralimit of standard natural numbers, a
nonstandard real number
is an ultralimit of standard real numbers, and so forth. If
(X_{n})_{n\in A}
is a sequence of standard sets indexed by an
\alpha
-large set
A
, we define the
ultraproduct

\prod_{n\to\alpha}X_{n}
to be the collection of all ultralimits
\lim_{n\to\alpha}x_{n}
, where
x_{n}\in X_{n}
for an
\alpha
-large set of
n
. An
internal set
is a set which is an ultraproduct of standard sets. We use the term
external set
to denote a set of nonstandard objects that is not necessarily internal. Note that every standard set
X
embeds into the nonstandard set
{}^{*}X\coloneqq\prod_{n\to\alpha}X
, which we call the
ultrapower
of
X
, by identifying every standard object
x
with its nonstandard counterpart
\lim_{n\to\alpha}x
. In particular, the standard universe
{\mathfrak{U}}
embeds into the
nonstandard universe

{}^{*}{\mathfrak{U}}
of all nonstandard objects.

If
X=\prod_{n\to\alpha}X_{n}
and
Y=\prod_{n\to\alpha}Y_{n}
are internal sets, we have a canonical isomorphism

X\times Y\equiv\prod_{n\to\alpha}(X_{n}\times Y_{n})

which (by abuse of notation) allows us to identify the Cartesian product of two internal sets as another internal set. Similarly for Cartesian products of any (standard) finite number of internal sets. We will implicitly use such identifications in the sequel without further comment.

An
internally finite set
is an ultraproduct of finite sets (such sets are also known as
hyperfinite
sets in the literature). Similarly with “set” replaced by “multiset”. (The multiplicity of an element of an internally finite multiset will of course be a nonstandard natural number in general, rather than a standard natural number.)

Given a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in A}
of standard functions
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n}
indexed by an
\alpha
-large set
A
, we define the
ultralimit

\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}\colon\prod_{n\to\alpha}X_{n}\to\prod_{n\to\alpha}Y_{n}
to be the function

(\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n})(\lim_{n\to\alpha}x_{n})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}(x_{n}).

This is easily seen to be a well-defined function. Functions that are ultralimits of standard functions will be called
internal functions
. We use the term
external function
to denote a function between external sets that is not necessarily an internal function. We will use boldface symbols such as
\mathbf{f}
to refer to internal functions, distinguishing them in particular from functions
f
that take values in the standard complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
rather than the nonstandard complex numbers
{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
.

Using the ultralimit construction, any ultraproduct
X=\prod_{n\to\alpha}X_{n}
of structures
X_{n}
for some first-order language
{\mathcal{L}}
, will remain a structure of that language
{\mathcal{L}}
; furthermore, thanks to the well-known theorem of Łos, any first-order sentence will hold in
X
if and only if it holds in
X_{n}
for an
\alpha
-large set of
n
. For instance, if
G_{n}
is an additive group for an
\alpha
-large set of
n
, then
\prod_{n\to\alpha}G_{n}
will also be an additive group.

A crucial property of internal sets for us will be the following compactness-like property of internal sets.

Theorem 5.1

(Countable saturation)
.

(i)

Let
(X^{(i)})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable sequence of internal sets. If the finite intersections
\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}X^{(i)}
are non-empty for every (standard) natural number
N
, then
\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}X^{(i)}
is also non-empty.

(ii)

Let
X
be an internal set. Then any countable cover of
X
by internal sets has a finite subcover.

Proof.

It suffices to prove (i), as the claim (ii) follows from taking complements and contrapositives. Write
X^{(i)}=\prod_{n\to\alpha}X^{(i)}_{n}
. Since
\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}X^{(i)}
is non-empty, there is an
\alpha
-large set
A_{N}
such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}X_{n}^{(i)}
is non-empty for all
n\in A_{N}
. By shrinking the
A_{N}
as necessary, we may assume that the
A_{N}
are non-increasing in
N
. If we then choose
x_{n}
for any
n
in
A_{N}\backslash A_{N+1}
to lie in
\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}X_{n}^{(i)}
for all
N
, the ultralimit
\lim_{n\to\alpha}x_{n}
lies in
\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}X^{(i)}
, giving the claim.
∎

A nonstandard complex number
z\in{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
is said to be
bounded
if one has
|z|\leq C
for some standard
C
, and

|z|\leq\varepsilon
for all standard

when

is bounded and

when
z
is infinitesimal. By modifying the proof of the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem, we see that every bounded

can be uniquely written as
z={\operatorname{st}}z+o(1)
for some standard complex number
{\operatorname{st}}z

of

.

Countable saturation has the following important consequence:

Corollary 5.2

(Overspill/underspill)
.

Let
A
be an internal subset of
{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
.

(i)

If
A
contains all standard natural numbers, then
A
also contains an unbounded natural number.

(ii)

If all elements of
A
are bounded, then
A
is contained in
\{z\in{}^{*}\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq C\}
for some standard
C>0
.

(iii)

If all elements of
A
are infinitesimal, then
A
is contained in
\{z\in{}^{*}\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq\varepsilon\}
for some infinitesimal
\varepsilon>0
.

(iv)

If
A
contains all positive standard reals, then
A
also contains a positive infinitesimal real.

Proof.

If (i) failed, then we would have
\mathbb{N}=A\cap{}^{*}\mathbb{N}
, and hence
\mathbb{N}
would be internal, which contradicts Theorem
5.1
(ii). The claim (ii) follows from the contrapositive of (i) applied to the internal set
\{n\in{}^{*}\mathbb{N}:|z|\geq n\hbox{ for some }z\in A\}
. The claim (iii) similarly follows from (i) applied to the internal set
\{n\in{}^{*}\mathbb{N}:|z|\leq 1/n\hbox{ for all }z\in A\}
. Finally, the claim (iv) follows from (i) applied to the internal set
\{n\in{}^{*}\mathbb{N}:1/n\in A\}
.
∎

An internal function
\mathbf{f}\colon X\to{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
is said to be
bounded
if it is bounded at every point, or equivalently (thanks to overspill or countable saturation) if there is a standard
C
such that
|f(x)|\leq C
for all
x\in X
, and we denote this assertion by
\mathbf{f}=O(1)
. Similarly, an internal function
\mathbf{f}\colon X\to{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
is said to be
infinitesimal
if it is infinitesimal at every point, or equivalently (thanks to underspill or countable saturation) if there is an infinitesimal
\varepsilon>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq\varepsilon
for all
x\in X
, and we denote this assertion by
\mathbf{f}=o(1)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{n}=(X_{n},{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\mu_{n})
be a sequence of standard probability spaces, indexed by an
\alpha
-large set
A
. In the ultraproduct
X\coloneqq\prod_{n\to\alpha}X_{n}
, we have a Boolean algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
of
internally measurable sets
- that is to say, internal sets of the form
E=\prod_{n\to\alpha}E_{n}
, where
E_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
for an
\alpha
-large set of
n
. Similarly, we have a complex *-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
of bounded internally measurable functions - functions
\mathbf{f}\colon X\to{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
that are ultralimits of measurable functions

, and which are bounded. We have a finitely additive nonstandard probability measure
{}^{*}\mu\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}\mu_{n}\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{}^{*}[0,1]
, and a finitely additive nonstandard integral

\int_{X}\mathbf{f}\ d{}^{*}\mu\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}\int_{X_{n}}f_{n}\ d\mu_{n}

defined for bounded internally measurable functions
\mathbf{f}=\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
. The standard part
\mu\coloneqq{\operatorname{st}}{}^{*}\mu
of the nonstandard measure
{}^{*}\mu
is then an (external) finitely additive probability measure on
{\mathcal{B}}
. From Theorem
5.1
(ii), this finitely additive measure is automatically a premeasure, and so by the Carathéodory extension theorem it may be extended to a countably additive measure
\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\to[0,1]
to the
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{L}}
generated by
{\mathcal{B}}
. The space
\mathbf{X}\coloneqq(X,{\mathcal{L}},\mu)
is then known as the

associated to the standard probability spaces
\mathbf{X}_{n}
. By construction, every Loeb-measurable set

can be approximated up to arbitrarily small error in

by an internally measurable set. As a corollary, for any (standard)
1\leq p<\infty
, then any function in
L^{p}(\mathbf{X})
can be approximated to arbitrary accuracy in
L^{p}(\mathbf{X})
norm by the standard part of a bounded internally measurable function, that is to say
{\operatorname{st}}{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
is dense in

. Indeed, we can say a little more:

Lemma 5.3
.

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
, then there exists
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
such that
f={\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}
almost everywhere.

Proof.

We may normalise
f
to lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
. By density, we may find a sequence
\mathbf{f}_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
bounded in magnitude by
1
, such that
\|f-{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{X})}<1/n
for all

. In particular,
\|\mathbf{f}_{N}-\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{{}^{*}L^{1}(\mathbf{X})}\leq 2/n
for all

\mathbf{f}
bounded in magnitude by

\|\mathbf{f}-\mathbf{f}_{n}\|_{{}^{*}L^{1}(\mathbf{X})}\leq 2/n

∎

By working first with simple functions and then taking limits, we easily establish the identity

\int_{X}{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}\ d\mu={\operatorname{st}}\int_{X}\mathbf{f}\ d{}^{*}\mu

for any
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
. In particular we have

\|{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{X})}={\operatorname{st}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}L^{p}(\mathbf{X})}

for any
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
and

and

\langle{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f},{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{g}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}={\operatorname{st}}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{{}^{*}L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}

for any
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}L^{p}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\left(\int_{X}|\mathbf{f}|^{p}\ d{}^{*}\mu\right)^{1/p}

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq{}^{*}\mu\!-\!\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in X}|\mathbf{f}(x)|

If

Lemma 5.4

Let
H
be an internally finite non-empty multiset, and let

be an internal map from
H

{\operatorname{st}}\mathbb{E}_{h\in H}\mathbf{f}_{h}
lies in the closed convex hull (in

) of
\{{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}_{h}:h\in H\}

If this were not the case, then by the Hahn-Banach theorem there would exist

and

such that

\operatorname{Re}\langle{\operatorname{st}}\mathbb{E}_{h\in H}\mathbf{f}_{h},g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}>\operatorname{Re}\langle{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}_{h},g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}+\varepsilon

5.1
Translation to nonstandard setting

We now translate Theorems

to a nonstandard setting.

Let
G=\prod_{n\to\alpha}G_{n}
be a nonstandard additive group, that is to say an ultraproduct of standard additive groups
G_{n}
.
Define a
internal coset progression

Q
in
G
to be an ultraproduct
Q=\prod_{n\to\alpha}Q_{n}
of standard coset progressions
Q_{n}
in
G_{n}
. We will be interested in internal coset progressions of bounded rank, which is equivalent to
Q_{n}
having bounded rank on an
\alpha
-large set of
n
. Given a internal coset progression
Q
, we define the (external) set
o(Q)
as
o(Q)\coloneqq\bigcap_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\varepsilon Q)
, that is to say the set of all
x\in G
such that
x\in\varepsilon Q
for all standard
\varepsilon>0
. Here we are interpreting
\varepsilon Q
and

as sets rather than multisets.

Given a sequence
(\mathbf{X}_{n},T_{n})
of standard
G_{n}
-sytems, we can form a
G
-system
(\mathbf{X},T)
by setting
\mathbf{X}
to be the Loeb probability space associated to the
\mathbf{X}_{n}
, and

to be the ultralimit of the

for any

. It is easy to verify that this is a

-system; we will refer to such systems as

-systems
.

Let

be a standard positive integer, and let
Q=\prod_{n\to\alpha}Q_{n}
be an internal coset progression. If
\mathbf{f}=\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
is a bounded internally measurable function, we can define the internal Gowers norm

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}\|f_{n}\|_{U^{d}_{Q_{n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})}.

From the Hölder and triangle inequalities one has

\|f_{n}\|_{U^{d}_{Q_{n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})}\leq\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})}

for each
n
, and hence on taking ultralimits

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

and then on taking standard parts

{\operatorname{st}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}.

We can thus uniquely define the
external Gowers seminorm

U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})
on
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
such that

\|{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}={\operatorname{st}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}

(38)

for all
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
, and

\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

for all
f\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
.

In a similar fashion, if
Q^{(i)}=\prod_{n\to\alpha}Q^{(i)}_{n}
are internal coset progressions, we can define the internal box norms

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}\|f_{n}\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)}_{n},\dots,Q^{(d)}_{n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})}

for
\mathbf{f}=\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
, and the external box seminorm
\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})
on
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
such that

\|{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}={\operatorname{st}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}

for all
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
, and

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

for all
f\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
Q
be a internal coset progression of bounded rank in a nonstandard additive group
G
, and let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a Loeb
G
-system. Then for any standard
d\geq 1
and any
f\in L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})
,
the limit

\|f\|_{U^{d}_{o(Q)}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{U^{d}_{\varepsilon Q}(\mathbf{X})}

exists. Furthermore one has

\displaystyle\|f\|_{U^{d}_{o(Q)}(\mathbf{X})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}:\varepsilon>0}\|f\|_{U^{d}_{\varepsilon Q}(\mathbf{X})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\varepsilon=o(1):\varepsilon>0}\|f\|_{U^{d}_{\varepsilon Q}(\mathbf{X})}.

More generally, given internal coset progressions
Q^{(1)},\dots,Q^{(d)}
of bounded rank in
G
, the limit

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q^{(1)}),\dots,o(Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}

exists, with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q^{(1)}),\dots,o(Q^{(d)})}(\mathbf{X})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}:\varepsilon>0}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\varepsilon=o(1):\varepsilon>0}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the second claim. By limiting arguments we may assume that
f
lies in
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
; by Lemma
5.3
we may then assume
f={\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}
for some
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
, which we may normalize to be bounded in magnitude by
1
.

Suppose that we can show that

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}+o(1)

(39)

whenever
\varepsilon>0
is standard and
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
is infinitesimal. This clearly implies

\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}:\varepsilon>0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\inf_{\varepsilon^{\prime}=o(1):\varepsilon^{\prime}>0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}+\delta

for any standard
\delta>0
; on the other hand from underspill we cannot have

\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}:\varepsilon>0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}>c>\inf_{\varepsilon^{\prime}=o(1):\varepsilon^{\prime}>0}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}

for any standard

. Taking standard parts, we conclude that

\sup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}:\varepsilon>0}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}=\inf_{\varepsilon^{\prime}=o(1):\varepsilon^{\prime}>0}\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}.

Denoting this quantity by
\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q^{(1)}),\dots,o(Q^{(d)})}(\mathbf{X})}
, we see that for every standard
\delta>0
, one has

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon Q^{(d)}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q^{(1)}),\dots,o(Q^{(d)})}(\mathbf{X})}+\delta

for all infinitesimal
\varepsilon>0
, and hence (by underspill) for all sufficiently small standard
\varepsilon>0
as well. The claim then follows.

It remains to prove (
39
). By (
7
), the left-hand side expands as

\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in\varepsilon Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\int_{X}\Delta_{0,h_{1}}\dots\Delta_{0,h_{d}}\mathbf{f}\ d{}^{*}\mu,

where the nonlinear operators
\Delta_{h,h^{\prime}}
were defined in (
8
).
We can shift each
\varepsilon Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}
by an element of
\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}
while only affecting this expression by
o(1)
. Averaging, we can thus write this expression as

\mathbb{E}_{h^{\prime}_{i}\in\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(i)}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in\varepsilon Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}+h^{\prime}_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\int_{X}\Delta_{0,h_{1}}\dots\Delta_{1,h_{d}}\mathbf{f}\ d{}^{*}\mu+o(1).

Writing
h_{i}=h^{\prime}_{i}+k_{i}
, this becomes

\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in\varepsilon Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\mathbb{E}_{h^{\prime}_{i}\in\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(i)}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\int_{X}\Delta_{0,h^{\prime}_{1}+k_{1}}\dots\Delta_{1,h^{\prime}_{d}+k_{d}}\mathbf{f}\ d{}^{*}\mu+o(1).

This can be rewritten in turn as

\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in\varepsilon Q^{(i)}-\varepsilon Q^{(i)}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\langle(T^{\omega_{1}k_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}k_{d}}\mathbf{f})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\rangle_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(1)},\dots,\varepsilon^{\prime}Q^{(d)}}}+o(1).

The claim (

18

∎

From this lemma we obtain seminorms
\|\|_{U^{d}_{o(Q)}(\mathbf{X})}
and
\|\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q^{(1)}),\dots,o(Q^{(d)})}(\mathbf{X})}
on

.

Theorem 5.6

(Concatenation theorem for anti-uniformity norms, nonstandard version)
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}
be internal coset progressions of bounded rank in a nonstandard additive group
G
, let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a Loeb
G
-system, let
d_{1},d_{2}
be standard positive integers, and let
f
lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
. We make the following hypotheses:

(i)

f
is orthogonal (in
L^{2}(\mathbf{X})
) to
\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X}):\|g\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{o(Q_{1})}(\mathbf{X})}=0\}
.

(ii)

f
is orthogonal to
\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X}):\|g\|_{U^{d_{2}}_{o(Q_{2})}(\mathbf{X})}=0\}
.

Then
f
is orthogonal to
\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X}):\|g\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{o(Q_{1}+Q_{2})}(\mathbf{X})}=0\}
.

Let us assume this theorem for the moment, and show how it implies Theorem
1.11
. It suffices to show that for any
d_{1},d_{2},r_{1},r_{2},c_{1},c_{2}

as in Theorem
1.11
, and any
\delta>0
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
, such that

\|f\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{Q_{1}+Q_{2}}(\mathbf{X})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq\delta

whenever

are standard objects obeying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.11
with the given values of
d_{1},d_{2},r_{1},r_{2},c_{1},c_{2}
.

Suppose this were not the case. Then there exists
d_{1},d_{2},r_{1},r_{2},c_{1},c_{2},\delta
as above, together with a sequence
G_{n}
of standard additive groups, sequences
Q_{1,n},Q_{2,n}
of standard coset progressions in
G_{n}
of ranks
r_{1},r_{2}
respectively, a sequence
(\mathbf{X}_{n},T_{n})
of
G_{n}
-systems, and a sequence
f_{n}
of functions in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
such that

\|f_{n}\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{Q_{1,n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq c_{1}(\varepsilon)

(40)

and
\|f_{n}\|_{U^{d_{2}}_{Q_{2,n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})^{*},\varepsilon}\leq c_{2}(\varepsilon)

for all (standard)
\varepsilon>0
, but such that

\|f_{n}\|_{U^{d_{1}+d_{2}-1}_{\varepsilon_{n}Q_{1,n}+\varepsilon_{n}Q_{2,n}}(\mathbf{X}_{n})^{*},\varepsilon_{n}}>\delta

(41)

for some
\varepsilon_{n}>0
that goes to zero as

.

, internal coset progressions

of bounded rank in
G
, a Loeb
G
-system
(\mathbf{X},T)
, and a bounded internally measurable function
\mathbf{f}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\alpha}f_{n}
. Since the

, we see that
{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}
lies in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
.

Now suppose that
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
is such that
\|g\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{o(Q_{1})}(\mathbf{X})}=0
; we claim that
{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}
is orthogonal to
g
. We may normalise
g
to lie in the closed unit ball of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
. By Lemma
5.5
one has

\|g\|_{U^{d_{1}}_{\varepsilon Q_{1}}(\mathbf{X})}=0

for all standard
\varepsilon>0
. We can write
g
as the limit in

of
{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{g}^{(i)}
as

\mathbf{g}^{(i)}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]

\varepsilon>0

\alpha

We now develop some nonstandard analogues of the machinery in Section

Let
(\mathbf{X},T)
be a Loeb
G
-system for some nonstandard abelian group
G
, and let
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}
be internal coset progressions of bounded rank in
G
for some standard

. Given bounded internally measurable functions
\mathbf{f}_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
for
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}

internal dual function

{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}
to be the bounded internally measurable function

{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}=\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}\in Q_{1}-Q_{1},\dots,h_{d}\in Q_{d}-Q_{d}}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}\mathbf{f}_{\omega}

where
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})

{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{0}()=1

{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]^{2^{d}-1}
to
{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]

{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{{}^{*}\Box^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{X})}^{2^{d}}=\langle\mathbf{f},{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\rangle_{{}^{*}L^{2}(\mathbf{X})}

(42)

for any
\mathbf{f}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
.

From Hölder’s inequality and the triangle inequality we see that

\|{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\|_{{}^{*}L^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\|\mathbf{f}_{\omega}\|_{{}^{*}L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}.

dual operator

{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}\colon L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})^{2^{d}-1}\to L^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}(\mathbf{X})
such that

\|{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\|_{L^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\|f_{\omega}\|_{L^{2^{d}}(\mathbf{X})}

for all

, and such that

{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}({\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}={\operatorname{st}}{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}

(43)

whenever
\mathbf{f}_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]
for
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}
.

An important fact about dual functions, analogous to Theorem
2.4
, is that the dual operator maps factors to characteristic subfactors for the associated Gowers norm:

Theorem 6.1

(Dual functions and characteristic factors)
.

Let
(\mathbf{Y},T)
be a factor of
(\mathbf{X},T)
, and let
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}
be internal coset progressions of bounded rank. Let

be the linear span of the space

\{{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{\varepsilon Q_{1},\dots,\varepsilon Q_{d}}(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}:f_{\omega}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y});\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R};\varepsilon>0\},

L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})
(for various dilates
\varepsilon Q_{1},\dots,\varepsilon Q_{d}
of
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{d}
). Then there exists a unique subfactor
(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y}),T)

(\mathbf{Y},T)
with the following properties:

(i)

V
is contained in
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y}))
.

(ii)

Conversely, every element of
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y}))
is the limit (in
L^{2^{d}/(2^{d}-1)}

V

L^{\infty}
norm.

(iii)

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})

\|f\|_{\Box^{d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{X})}=0

f
is orthogonal (in the
L^{2}
sense) to
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y}))

We now prove this theorem. From (i) and (ii) it is clear that
\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y})
is unique; the difficulty is to establish existence.
We first observe

Lemma 6.2
.

We have
V\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})
.

Proof.

It suffices by linearity to show that

{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{\varepsilon Q_{1},\dots,\varepsilon Q_{d}}(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})

whenever
f_{\omega}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})

43

{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{\varepsilon Q_{1},\dots,\varepsilon Q_{d}}(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}

{\operatorname{st}}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}F_{\omega}=\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|\omega|-1}T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}f_{\omega}

for various

, and the claim follows.
∎

Next, we observe that
V
is almost closed under multiplication (cf. Proposition

Lemma 6.3
.

If

, then

is the limit (in

Proof.

By linearity and density, as well as Lemma
5.3
and (

f={\operatorname{st}}{}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{d}_{\varepsilon Q_{1},\dots,\varepsilon Q_{d}}(\mathbf{f}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}

for some standard
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and some
\mathbf{f}_{\omega},\mathbf{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{A}}[\mathbf{X}]

We can thus write

ff^{\prime}={\operatorname{st}}\mathbb{E}_{h_{i}\in\varepsilon Q_{i}-\varepsilon Q_{i},h^{\prime}_{i}\in\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}-\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}\forall i=1,\dots,d}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}T^{\omega_{1}h_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h_{d}}\mathbf{f}_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+\omega_{d}h^{\prime}_{d}}\mathbf{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}.

\varepsilon_{*}>0
be infinitesimal. We can shift
\varepsilon Q_{i}-\varepsilon Q_{i}
or
\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}-\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}
by an element of
\varepsilon_{*}Q_{i}-\varepsilon_{*}Q_{i}
while only affecting the above average by

. We conclude that

\displaystyle ff^{\prime}

\displaystyle={\operatorname{st}}\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}\in\varepsilon_{*}Q_{i}-\varepsilon_{*}Q_{i},h_{i}\in\varepsilon Q_{i}-\varepsilon Q_{i},h^{\prime}_{i}\in\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}-\varepsilon^{\prime}Q_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{d}\backslash\{0\}^{d}}T^{\omega_{1}(h_{1}+k_{1})+\dots+\omega_{d}(h_{d}+k_{d})}\mathbf{f}_{\omega}T^{\omega_{1}(h^{\prime}_{1}+k_{1})+\dots+\omega_{d}(h^{\prime}_{d}+k_{d})}\mathbf{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}.

Corollary 6.4

(Localisation)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be a Loeb
G
-system for some nonstandard additive group
G
,
let
\mathbf{Y}
be a factor of
\mathbf{X}
, and let

be internal coset progressions of bounded rank in

\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y})\equiv\mathbf{Y}\wedge\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{X}).

Proof.

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{Y}))
, then by Theorem
6.1

f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})
, and

can be approximated by finite linear combinations of dual functions, which then also lie in
L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{X}))
, giving one inclusion. To obtain the other inclusion, observe that if
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Y})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{{<}d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{X}))
, then by Theorem
6.1

f
is orthogonal to all functions in

with vanishing
\Box^{d}_{o(Q_{1}),\dots,o(Q_{d})}(\mathbf{X})
norm, and by second application of Theorem

we thus have

.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

Following the informal sketch in Section

, functions that are measurable with respect to

represents some additional structure, perhaps along some directions unrelated to

then guarantees that this additional structure is preserved when one performs such operations as orthogonal projection to

For the purpose of concatenation theorems, the following special case of Corollary

\Box^{{<}d_{1}-1}_{o(Q_{1,2}),\dots,o(Q_{1,d_{1}})}

{\operatorname{st}}\mathbf{c}_{n,h}
is measurable with respect to

. This argument was established for

is simply

A similar argument starting from (

is also measurable with respect to

Proof of qualitative Bessel inequality

We now prove Theorem

1.22

It will be convenient to reduce to a variant of this theorem in which the index set

has bounded size. More precisely, we will derive Theorem
1.23
from

.

Let
M\geq 1
, and let
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{M}
be a finite sequence of coset progressions

, all of rank at most
r
, in an additive group
G

\mathbf{X}
be a
G

be a positive integer. Let

, and suppose that

\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\|f\|_{U^{2d-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{i}+\varepsilon Q_{j}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\varepsilon

for some

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|f\|_{U^{d}_{Q_{i}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq 2M^{-1/2^{d}}.

To see how Theorem
8.1

1.23
, we use a random sampling argument. Let
I,Q_{i},r,G,\mathbf{X},T,d,f,\varepsilon
be as in Theorem
1.23
. Let

be a positive integer to be chosen later, and select

uniformly and independently from

\mathbb{E}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\|f\|_{U^{2d-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{a_{i}}+\varepsilon Q_{a_{j}}}(\mathbf{X})}=\frac{M(M-1)}{2}\frac{1}{|I|^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in I}\|f\|_{U^{2d-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{a_{i}}+\varepsilon Q_{a_{j}}}(\mathbf{X})}

and hence by (
15

\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\|f\|_{U^{2d-1}_{\varepsilon Q_{i}+\varepsilon Q_{j}}(\mathbf{X})}\leq\varepsilon

(53)

with probability greater than

and

obeying both (
53

54

It remains to prove Theorem

. As in the proof of Theorems

, a Loeb system
(\mathbf{X},T)
, a function

From (

Controlling a multilinear average

In this section we establish Proposition
1.26
. Let
N,M,C,f_{1},\dots,f_{4}
be as in that proposition. By linearity we may take
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}
to be real-valued. Suppose that

|A_{N,M}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})|\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
. It will suffice to show that

\|f_{i}\|_{U^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}\geq\varepsilon

for all
i=1,2,3,4
and some
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
\delta,C
.

Fix
i
. We have

\left|\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[M]}\int_{X}f_{1}T^{-nm}f_{2}T^{-nk}f_{3}T^{-n(m+k)}f_{4}\ d\mu\right|\geq\delta.

where
X
is
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
with uniform measure
\mu
and the usual shift map
T
. Let
Q
be the coset progression
Q\coloneqq\{a:|a|\leq\kappa\sqrt{N}\}
for some small
\kappa>0
to be chosen shortly. If
\kappa
is small enough depending on
\delta,C
, we can shift
m
and
k
in the above average by any elements
a^{\prime}-a,b^{\prime}-b
of
Q-Q
without changing the average by more than
\delta/2
. From this and some rearranging and averaging we see that

|\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[M]}\mathbb{E}_{a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in Q}\int_{X}T^{na+nb}f_{1}T^{-nm+na+nb^{\prime}}f_{2}T^{-nk+na^{\prime}+nb}f_{3}T^{-n(m+k)+na^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}}f_{4}\ d\mu|\geq\delta/2.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz-Gowers inequality (
18
) we have

|\mathbb{E}_{a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in Q}\int_{X}T^{na+nb}f_{1}T^{-nm+na+nb^{\prime}}f_{2}T^{-nk+na^{\prime}+nb}f_{3}T^{-n(m+k)+na^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}}f_{4}\ d\mu|\leq\|f_{i}\|_{U^{2}_{n\cdot Q}(\mathbf{X})}

for any

, where

. We thus have

\mathbb{E}_{n\in[M]}\|f_{i}\|_{U^{2}_{n\cdot Q}(\mathbf{X})}\geq\delta/2.

For the rest of this section, we adopt the asympotic notation
X\ll Y
or
Y\gg X
to denote the bound
X\leq C^{\prime}(C,\delta)Y
for some
C^{\prime}(C,\delta)
depending on
C,\delta
(or on
\kappa
, since
\kappa
can be chosen to depend only on
\delta
and

). Applying Theorem
1.23
in the contrapositive, we thus have

\mathbb{E}_{n,m\in[M]}\|f_{i}\|_{U^{3}_{n\cdot Q+m\cdot Q}(\mathbf{X})}\gg 1.

(57)

If
A
is a sufficiently large quantity depending on
\delta
, we may thus find

with greatest common divisor
(n,m)
at most
A
such that

(58)

since the contribution to (

) of all other pairs

can be shown to be

.

Fix this choice of
n,m
. By hypothesis, the multiset
n\cdot Q+m\cdot Q
has cardinality
\gg N
(counting multiplicity), and has multiplicity
\ll 1
at each point, and hence
(n\cdot Q+m\cdot Q)-(n\cdot Q+m\cdot Q)
has cardinality
\gg N^{2}
and multiplicity
\ll N
at each point. From (

) we thus have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{x,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\left(\prod_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\in\{0,1\}}f_{i}(x+\omega_{1}a+\omega_{2}b+\omega_{3}c)\right)\nu_{2}(a)\nu_{2}(b)\nu_{2}(c)\right|\gg 1

at

Reducing the complexity

In this section we sketch how the

The situation here is closely analogous to that of

That theorem was proven by combining an “arithmetic regularity lemma” that decomposes an arbitrary function into an “irrational” nilsequence, plus a uniform error, together with a “counting lemma” that controls the contribution of the irrational nilsequence. The part of the proof of
[
25

that involves the regularity lemma goes through essentially unchanged when establishing Proposition
1.26
; the only difficulty is to establish a suitable counting lemma for the irrational nilsequence, which in this context can be taken to a nilsequence of degree
\leq 2

Let
(G/\Gamma,G_{\bullet})
be a filtered nilmanifold of degree
\leq 2
and complexity
\leq M
(as defined in
[
25

-irrational polynomial sequence adapted to

(as defined in
[
25

M

for all

for some

. Then

\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[M_{1}]}F((g(x),g(x+nm),g(x+nk),g(x+n(m+k)))\Gamma^{4})=o_{A\to\infty;C,M}(1)+o_{N\to\infty;C,M}(1),

using the asymptotic notation conventions from
[
25
]
.

By repeating the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 7.1]

[
25

1.27

\leq 2

has essentially mean zero along
G_{(2)}
, in the sense that
\int_{g_{4}G_{(2)}/\Gamma_{(2)}}F
is close to zero for most
g_{4}
. By modifying
F
slightly we may assume that
F
has
exactly
mean zero, in which case one can use Proposition
10.1
to conclude the argument.

It remains to establish Proposition
10.1
. Suppose that

|\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{n,m,k\in[M_{1}]}F((g(x),g(x+nm),g(x+nk),g(x+n(m+k)))\Gamma^{4})|\geq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
; our objective is to obtain a contradiction when
A
and
N
are sufficiently large depending on
\varepsilon,C,M
. In this section we use
X\ll Y
,
Y\gg X
, or
X=O(Y)
to denote the bound
|X|\leq C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,C,M)Y
for some
C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,C,M)
depending on
\varepsilon,C,M
. As the quantity
|\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{m,k\in[M_{1}]}F((g(x),g(x+nm),g(x+nk),g(x+n(m+k)))\Gamma^{4})|
is
O(1)
for any
n
, we have

|\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{m,k\in[M_{1}]}F((g(x),g(x+nm),g(x+nk),g(x+n(m+k)))\Gamma^{4})|\gg 1

(60)

for
\gg M_{1}
choices of
n\in[M_{1}]
. On the other hand, using the linear forms
\Psi\colon(x,m,k)\mapsto(x,x+m,x+k,x+m+k)
, one can compute (in the notation of
[
25
, Definition 1.10]
) that the Leibman group
G^{\Psi}
(which is also the second Host-Kra group of
G
) contains
G_{(2)}^{4}
, basically because the quadratic polynomials
x^{2}
,
(x+m)^{2}
,
(x+k)^{2}
,
(x+m+k)^{2}
are linearly independent, and so the linear space
\Psi^{[2]}
appearing in
[
25
, Definition 1.10]
is all of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Applying (
59
), we conclude that

\int_{g_{4}G^{\Psi}/\Gamma^{\Psi}}F=0

for any
g_{4}\in G^{4}
. From this and a change of variables, we see from (
60
) and
[
25
, Theorem 1.11]
that for
\gg M_{1}
choices of
n\in[M_{1}]
, there exists an integer
a
such that the map
g_{n,a}\colon m\mapsto g(nm+a)
is
not

(A^{\prime},M_{1})
-irrational, for some
A^{\prime}=O(1)
. Unpacking the definition from
[
25
, Definition A.6]
, this means that there is an
i=1,2
and an
i
-horizontal character
\xi_{i}\colon G_{(i)}\to\mathbb{R}
of complexity
O(1)
such that

\|\xi_{i}(g_{n,a,i})\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll M_{1}^{-i}

for
\gg M_{1}
choices of
n\in[M_{1}]
,
where
g_{n,a,i}
is the
i^{\operatorname{th}}
Taylor coefficient of
g_{n,a}
. But we have the identity

\xi_{i}(g_{n,a,i})=n^{i}\xi_{i}(g_{i})

(see the proof of
[
25
, Lemma A.8]
)
and hence

\|n^{i}\xi_{i}(g_{i})\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll M_{1}^{-i}

for
\gg M_{1}
choices of
n\in[M_{1}]
. Applying the Vinogradov lemma (see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
), we conclude that there exists a natural number
q=O(1)
such that

\|q\xi_{i}(g_{i})\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll M_{i}^{-2i}\ll N^{-i}.

But this contradicts the
(A,N)
-irrationality of
g
, and the claim follows.

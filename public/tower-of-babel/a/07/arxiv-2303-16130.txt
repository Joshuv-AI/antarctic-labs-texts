Spectral GRID invariants and Lagrangian cobordisms
Mitchell Jubeir, Ina Petkova, Noah Schwartz, Zachary Winkeler, C.-M. Michael Wong

Abstract
. We prove that the filtered GRID invariants of Legendrian links in link Floer homology, and consequently their associated invariants in the spectral sequence, obstruct decomposable Lagrangian cobordisms in the symplectization of the standard contact structure on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , strengthening a result by Baldwin, Lidman, and the fifth author.

An interesting and difficult problem in contact and symplectic geometry is to

\Lambda_{-}\subset(Y_{-},\xi_{-}),\qquad\Lambda_{+}\subset(Y_{+},\xi_{+}),

1.1.
The spectral GRID invariants

We briefly recall the invariants of Legendrian links in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})

defined by Ozsváth, Szabó, and Thurston
[
OSzT08
]
using the
combinatorial grid-diagram formulation
[
MOS09
,
MOSzT07
]
of knot Floer
homology; for more details, see
Section 2.2
below. For ease of
exposition, we focus on the
tilde
flavor, which computes a stabilized
version of knot Floer homology. Let
\mathbb{G}
be a grid diagram of size
n
that
represents a Legendrian link
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})
; then there are

canonical generators

\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})
and
\mathbf{x}^{-}(\mathbb{G})
, which are cycles
of the
grid chain complex

\widetilde{\GC}(\mathbb{G})
, and thus give rise to homology
classes

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}(\mathbb{G}),\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}(\mathbb{G})\in\widetilde{\GH}(\mathbb{G})\cong\widehat{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)\otimes V^{n-\ell},

where
V
is a vector space of dimension
2
, and
\ell
is the number of
components of
\Lambda
. (On the chain level,
\widetilde{\GC}(\mathbb{G})
corresponds to a
stabilized version of
\mathrm{g}\widehat{\CFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)
, whose homology is
\widehat{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)
.) Ozsváth, Szabó, and Thurston prove that
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}
are preserved
by the homomorphisms on
\widetilde{\GH}
induced by Legendrian isotopy, which implies
that they are Legendrian isotopy invariants; they are referred to as the

GRID invariants
. Similar statements hold for the
minus
flavor of
the theory.

As explained in
[
MOSzT07
,
OSSz15
]
, one may in fact define a filtered
chain complex
\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})
with the
Alexander filtration
, such that
the associated graded object of
\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})
is
\widetilde{\GC}(\mathbb{G})
. This
corresponds to the filtered chain complex
\widehat{\CFL}
, whose associated graded
object is
\mathrm{g}\widehat{\CFL}
. The filtered chain homotopy type of
\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})
is an
invariant of the smooth link type of
\Lambda
and the grid size of
\mathbb{G}
(which
controls the amount of stabilization).
More precisely,
\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})
is filtered chain homotopy equivalent to
\widehat{\CFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)\otimes V^{n-\ell}
. (This can be seen by extending
[
OSSz15
, Section 5.7]
to the filtered context
[
OSSz15
, Section 13.3]
and setting all formal variables to zero.) Noting that
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\widehat{\CFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda))
is simply
\widehat{\HF}(-S^{3})
, we see that
each page of the associated spectral sequence

(1.1)

\widetilde{\GH}(\mathbb{G})\rightrightarrows\mathrm{H}_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}))

is isomorphic to each corresponding page of the spectral sequence

\widehat{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)\otimes V^{n-\ell}\rightrightarrows\widehat{\HF}(-S^{3})\otimes V^{n-\ell},

a stabilized version of the spectral sequence

\widehat{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda)\rightrightarrows\widehat{\HF}(-S^{3}),

which is an invariant of the smooth link type of
\Lambda
.

The idea of the spectral GRID invariants is to consider the homology classes
that
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})
represent in the spectral sequence in (
1.1
).
These may be defined recursively. For concreteness, let
(E^{i},\delta_{i})
be
the
i^{\text{th}}
page in the spectral sequence, such that
\widetilde{\GH}(\mathbb{G})
is
the
E^{1}
-page, and let
\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{1}(\mathbb{G})=\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}(\mathbb{G})
. Suppose that

\delta_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\mathbb{G}))=0
; then we obtain a homology class

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i+1}(\mathbb{G})=[\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\mathbb{G})]\in E^{i+1}
. If at any stage

\delta_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\mathbb{G}))\neq 0
, then we may instead record the integer

i
, which we will call
n^{+}(\mathbb{G})\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and the process terminates. Let

n^{+}(\mathbb{G})=\infty
if
\delta_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i})=0
for all
i\geq 1
.
Considering the invariant
\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}(\mathbb{G})=[\mathbf{x}^{-}(\mathbb{G})]
, we likewise
obtain invariants
n^{-}(\mathbb{G})
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}_{i}(\mathbb{G})
. With some work, that

n^{\pm}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}
are indeed invariants of the Legendrian link
\Lambda

can be deduced by extending certain proofs in
[
OSzT08
]
, which we will carry
out later:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
are two grid diagrams that represent the
same Legendrian link
\Lambda\subset(S^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})
. Then
n^{+}(\mathbb{G})=n^{+}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})
and
n^{-}(\mathbb{G})=n^{-}(\mathbb{G})
, and there exist filtered chain
homomorphisms

\Phi\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}),\qquad\Phi^{\prime}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}),

such that the induced maps

\Phi_{i}\colon E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}))\to E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))

and

\Phi^{\prime}_{i}\colon E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))\to E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}))

on the pages of the associated spectral sequences satisfy

\Phi_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}))=\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}),\qquad\Phi^{\prime}_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}))=\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}),

for each
1\leq i\leq n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})
.
The fact that
n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})=n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})
means that we have invariants
n^{\pm}(\Lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\mathchoice{\left\{\infty\right\}}{\{\infty\}}{\{\infty\}}{\{\infty\}}
of the Legendrian link type of
\Lambda
.
Moreover, since the (non)vanishing of
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G})
depends only on
\Lambda
, we may view these also as invariants of the Legendrian link type of
\Lambda
, and slightly abuse notation to denote them by
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\Lambda)\in E^{i}(\widetilde{\CFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda))
.

.

Versions of the maps
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
first appear in

For technical reasons, our formulation of

Remark 1.4
.

The idea to obtain refined information about a contact-geometric invariant in
knot Floer homology by considering its homology classes in a spectral

[

Remark 1.5
.

As shown in
[

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}(\Lambda)

1.2.
Obstructions

Baldwin, Lidman, and the fifth author
[
BLW22
]
prove that, if there
exists a decomposable Lagrangian cobordism
L\colon\Lambda_{-}\to\Lambda_{+}
, then
there exists a homomorphism
\Phi_{L}\colon\widetilde{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda_{+})\to\widetilde{\HFL}(-S^{3},\Lambda_{-})
that sends
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}(\Lambda_{+})
to
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}(\Lambda_{-})
. In particular:

Theorem 1.6

(
[
BLW22
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{-}
and
\Lambda_{+}
are Legendrian links in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})
,
such that

•

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}(\Lambda_{+})=0
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}(\Lambda_{-})\neq 0
; or

•

\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}(\Lambda_{+})=0
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}(\Lambda_{-})\neq 0
.

Then there does not exist a decomposable Lagrangian cobordism from
\Lambda_{-}

to
\Lambda_{+}
.

As mentioned above, the main goal of this article is to prove the following
extension of this result:

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{-}
and
\Lambda_{+}
are Legendrian links in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})
,
such that

•

n^{+}(\Lambda_{+})>n^{+}(\Lambda_{-})
; or

•

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\Lambda_{+})=0
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\Lambda_{-})\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq\min\{n^{+}(\Lambda_{-}),n^{+}(\Lambda_{+})\}
; or

•

n^{-}(\Lambda_{+})>n^{-}(\Lambda_{-})
; or

•

\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}_{i}(\Lambda_{+})=0
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}_{i}(\Lambda_{-})\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq\min\{n^{+}(\Lambda_{-}),n^{+}(\Lambda_{+})\}
.

Then there does not exist a decomposable Lagrangian cobordism from
\Lambda_{-}

to
\Lambda_{+}
.

Remark 1.8
.

Restricting to decomposable Lagrangian concordances (i.e. the case that the
genus
g
is zero), the hypotheses involving
\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{1}
are covered by

[
BS18
,
BS21
]
and
[
GJ19
, Corollary 1.4]
. In addition, for
g=0
,
[
GJ19
, Corollary 1.5]
covers the hypothesis

Specializing to the case where
\Lambda_{-}
is the undestabilizable Legendrian
unknot, we obtain the following:

Corollary 1.9
.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a Legendrian link in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})
, such that

\widetilde{\lambda}^{+}_{i}(\Lambda)=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n^{+}(\Lambda)
, or
\widetilde{\lambda}^{-}_{i}(\Lambda)=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n^{-}(\Lambda)
. Then there does not exist a
decomposable Lagrangian filling of
\Lambda
.

Functoriality

Analogous to

Theorem 1.10
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{G}_{-}
and
\mathbb{G}_{+}
are two grid diagrams that represent Legendrian links

and

in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\xi_{\mathrm{std}})

\Psi\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{+})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{-})\left\llbracket-\chi(L),\frac{\mathchoice{\left\lvert\Lambda_{+}\right\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}-\mathchoice{\left\lvert\Lambda_{-}\right\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}-\chi(L)}{2}\right\rrbracket,

such that the induced maps

\Psi_{i}\colon E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{+}))\to E^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{-}))

on the pages of the associated spectral sequences satisfy

\Psi_{i}(\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}_{+}))=\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G}_{-}),

for each

. Here,
\mathchoice{\left\lvert\Lambda_{\pm}\right\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{\pm}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{\pm}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{\pm}\rvert}

Throughout the paper, we denote by

\mathcal{C}\llbracket M,A\rrbracket_{(0,0)}=\mathcal{C}_{(-M,-A)}

As in
[

Proposition 1.11

Let

•

[\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})]=0\in\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{A+i-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})/\mathcal{F}_{A-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G})).

1.5.
Effectiveness

In

\mathit{tb}(\Lambda_{+})-\mathit{tb}(\Lambda_{-})=2g

While not directly stated in
[
GJ19
]
, their result on
n^{+}
also implies
that there is no decomposable Lagrangian cobordism
L\colon\Lambda_{1}\to-\Lambda_{1}
.

The above is recovered by
Theorem 1.7
. In addition, by considering

Theorem 1.7
also implies that there is no decomposable
Lagrangian cobordism
L\colon-\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{1}
or

Finally, note that none of the obstructions above can be obtained by
considering only linearized Legendrian contact homology (LCH), since

\Lambda_{1}
,

, and

all have the same linearized LCH. This
eliminates the most tractable approach to using the Chekanov–Eliashberg DGA

1.6.
Organization

In
Section 2
, we provide the necessary background on Legendrian
links, Lagrangian cobordisms, link Floer homology, the GRID invariants, and
filtered chain complexes and their associated spectral sequences. Next, in

Section 3
, we define the spectral invariants in detail, and
provide a detailed proof that they are preserved by grid commutation and
(de)stabilization, which implies that they are Legendrian invariants. In

Section 4
, we then show that the spectral invariants are preserved
under (the reverses of) pinches and births, proving

.
Finally, we prove

in

, establishing an algorithm to compute the spectral
invariants directly from the filtered chain complex.

Knot Floer homology and the GRID invariants

In this section, we review some basics of grid homology, following the

A

(or simply a

)
\mathbb{G}
is an
m\times m
grid on the plane, along with two sets of markers

\mathbb{O}=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}\},\qquad\mathbb{X}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\},

such that there is exactly one
O
and exactly one

in each row, as well as in each column, and no square of the grid contains more than one marking. The number

is called the

of
\mathbb{G}

A grid diagram
\mathbb{G}
specifies a link
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
as follows. Draw oriented segments connecting
X
’s to
O
’s in each column, and
O
’s to
X
’s in each row, and require that vertical segments cross above horizontal ones. We say
\mathbb{G}
is a grid diagram for
L
. Conversely, every link
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be represented by a grid diagram.
By a theorem of Cromwell
[
Cro95
]
, two grid diagrams represent the same link if and only if they are related by a sequence of commutations, in which two adjacent rows or columns are switched if the corresponding segments in them connecting the
X
’s and the
O
’s are either nested or disjoint, stabilizations, in which a
1\times 1
square with an
O
(resp.
X
) marker is replaced by a
2\times 2
square with two diagonal
O
markers and an
X
marker (resp. two diagonal
X
markers and an
O
marker), creating a new row and a new column, and destabilizations, the inverse operations. Following
[
OSSz15
]
, we classify (de)stabilizations by the marker type and the location of the empty cell in the
2\times 2
square; for example, a stabilization of type
X:SE
results in a
2\times 2
square with an empty southeastern cell, an
O
in the northwestern cell, and
X
’s in the northeastern and southwestern cells.

\mathbb{G}

invariant of the isotopy type of

. Before we do this, we introduce a bit
more notation. First, we will think of a grid diagram as a diagram on a torus,
by identifying the left and right edges, as well as the top and bottom edges of
the grid. The horizontal arcs of the grid result in a set of circles
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
,
indexed from left to right.

10

10

For any state

where

Given a planar grid
\mathbb{G}
, we construct a Legendrian link in the following

\mathbb{G}
. The

\Lambda(\mathbb{G})

and

\mathbb{G}

2.3.
Filtered chain complexes

The main algebraic structures that we study in this paper are filtered chain complexes, and the spectral sequences they induce.

A
filtration
on a chain complex
C
is a sequence of subcomplexes
(\mathcal{F}_{i}C)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
C
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}C\subset\mathcal{F}_{j}C
whenever
i\leq j\in Z
. To be more precise, this is the definition of an “increasing” filtration. We will assume that our filtrations are
bounded
, which means that
\mathcal{F}_{s}C=0
and
\mathcal{F}_{t}C=C
for some
s\leq t\in\mathbb{Z}
. A
filtered complex

\mathcal{C}=(C,\mathcal{F})
is a complex
C
equipped with a filtration
\mathcal{F}
of
C
. When the filtration is clear from context, we will often omit it.

A
map of filtered complexes
is a chain map
f:C\to C^{\prime}
that respects the filtration in the sense that
f(\mathcal{F}_{i}C)\subset\mathcal{F}_{i}C^{\prime}
.

Given a filtered complex
\mathcal{C}
, we may construct the
associated graded complex

\gr(\mathcal{C})
, which is defined to be

\gr(\mathcal{C})=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\gr_{p}(\mathcal{C})

where

\gr_{p}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{F}_{p}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C\,.

A map
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of filtered complexes induces a map
\gr(f)\colon\gr(\mathcal{C})\to\gr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
of associated graded complexes in a natural way.

While the underlying modules of filtered complexes and their associated graded
complexes are isomorphic (over a field), the same is not true in general when
one considers their differentials. For example, it is not true in general that

H_{*}(\gr(\mathcal{C}))\cong H_{*}(C)
. Instead, what we can say is that there is a
spectral sequence from the former group to the latter. A
spectral
sequence
is a sequence of chain complexes
(E^{r})_{r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}
and isomorphisms
H_{*}(E^{r})\cong E^{r+1}
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. The complex
E^{r}
comes equipped with a bigrading
E^{r}=\bigoplus_{p,q}E^{r}_{p,q}
.

Given a filtered complex
\mathcal{C}
, there is an induced spectral sequence with

E^{1}_{p,q}\cong H_{p+q}(\gr_{p}(\mathcal{C}))
and
E^{\infty}_{p,q}\cong\gr_{p}(H_{p+q}(C))
. We will summarize the relevant details, using the
conventions from
[
Sta18
,
Section
012K
]
adjusted for homological (vs. cohomological) gradings. The
filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
C
induces a grading on each page
E^{r}
of the spectral
sequence as a module; this is the grading corresponding to
p
in
E^{r}_{p,q}
.
Similarly,
q
corresponds to the original homological grading on
C
; we will
often suppress this grading for simplicity. As modules, we define

E^{r}_{p}(\mathcal{C})\cong\frac{Z^{r}_{p}(\mathcal{C})}{B^{r}_{p}(\mathcal{C})}\,,

where

\displaystyle Z^{r}_{p}(\mathcal{C})\cong\frac{\mathcal{F}_{p}C\cap\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{p-r}C)+\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C}{\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C}\quad\text{ and }\quad B^{r}_{p}(\mathcal{C})\cong\frac{\mathcal{F}_{p}C\cap\partial(\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C)+\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C}{\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C}\,.

The differential
d^{r}:E^{r}_{p}\to E^{r}_{p-r}
is induced by
\partial
, where

E^{r}
is understood as a quotient of subquotients of
C
as in the definition
above.
The only part of the spectral sequence structure induced by
\mathcal{F}
that we
have not defined is the isomorphism
H_{*}(E^{r})\cong E^{r+1}
; this is mostly
tedious but straightforward algebra. For more details, we refer the reader to

[
Wei94
]
or another book on homological algebra.

Since we are interested in the behavior of particular elements under spectral
sequences, we want to define what it means to talk about the “class of
x\in C
on the
r
-th page”. Given any nonzero
x\in C
, let
p
be the value for which
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C\setminus\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
(note that this is
the difference as sets and not the quotient). We may think of
p
as the
“filtration level” of
x
; such an integer always exists since
\mathcal{F}
is
assumed to be bounded. Then, if
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C\cap\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{p-r}C)+\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
, we define
[x]^{r}
to be the class of
x
in
E^{r}_{p}
.
Note that, given some
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C
, it may not be the case that
[x]^{r}

is defined for all
r
. However, if
[x]^{r}
is defined, then
[x]^{s}
is also
defined for all
s\leq r
, since
\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{p-s}C)\supseteq\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{p-r}C)
. In fact,
[x]^{r+1}\in E^{r+1}_{p}
is defined if and only if
d^{r}[x]^{r}=0
, since
\ker d^{r}_{p}=\partial^{-1}(B^{r}_{p-r})\cap Z^{r}_{p}\cong Z^{r+1}_{p}
. In
Section 5
, we will introduce an alternative way
of thinking about the (non)vanishing of the class of an element in
E^{r}

that lends itself nicely to certain computations.

In later sections, our strategy to relate elements in the spectral sequences associated to two filtered complexes will be to relate representatives of those elements in the filtered chain complexes. Thus, we need the following lemma, which explains how a filtered chain map induces maps on the spectral sequence:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(C,\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=(C^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
be two filtered chain
complexes, and let
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a filtered chain map. Then for
each
r\geq 1
, the map
f
induces a chain map

E^{r}(f)\colon E^{r}(\mathcal{C})\to E^{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}).

Furthermore, if
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C
is an element with a well-defined class

[x]^{r}\in E^{r}(\mathcal{C})
, such that
f(x)
can be (non-uniquely) written as

f(x)=y_{1}+y_{2}

where
y_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}^{\prime}C^{\prime}
and
y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}^{\prime}C^{\prime}
, then
[y_{1}]^{r}
is
well-defined, and

E^{r}(f)([x]^{r})=[y_{1}]^{r}.

Proof.

First, assuming we are given a map
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, we will describe the map
E^{r}(f)
. Since
E^{r}(\mathcal{C})
and
E^{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
are sub-quotients of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
respectively, one can check that
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
induces a map
E^{r}(f):E^{r}(\mathcal{C})\to E^{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. On elements,
E^{r}(f)
is defined such that

E^{r}(f)([x]^{r})=[f(x)]^{r}\,.

Note that
f
is a filtered chain map and therefore commutes with the original differentials, i.e.
f\circ\partial=\partial^{\prime}\circ f
. Therefore, we get that
E^{r}(f)
also commutes with the induced differentials, i.e.
E^{r}(f)\circ d^{r}=(d^{\prime})^{r}\circ E^{r}(f)
. It can be shown further that
E^{r}(f)
is also the map induced by
E^{r-1}(f)
on homology (a property shared by all morphisms of spectral sequences).

Next, assume
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C
is an element with a well-defined class
[x]^{r}\in E^{r}(\mathcal{C})
, and that

f(x)=y_{1}+y_{2}

for some
y_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C^{\prime}
and
y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C^{\prime}
. Then

E^{r}(f)([x]^{r})=[f(x)]^{r}=[y_{1}+y_{2}]^{r}=[y_{1}]^{r}

by the definition of
E^{r}(f)
and the fact that
y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C^{\prime}
is in the denominator of
Z^{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
.
∎

3.
Spectral GRID invariants

3.1.
Definition of the spectral GRID invariants

Now, we have the necessary background to define our invariants.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{G}
is a grid diagram, and let
A=A(\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G}))
. We define
n^{+}(\mathbb{G})
to be the smallest integer
i
for which
d^{i}_{A}[\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})]^{i}\neq 0\in E^{i}_{A}
, or
\infty
if
d^{i}_{A}[\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})]^{i}=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. We define
n^{-}(\mathbb{G})
analogously, replacing
\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})
by
\mathbf{x}^{-}(\mathbb{G})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{G}
is a grid diagram. For each
1\leq i\leq n^{+}(\mathbb{G})
, we define

\lambda^{+}_{i}(\mathbb{G})=[\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G})]^{i}\in E^{i}_{A(\mathbf{x}^{+}(\mathbb{G}))},\qquad\lambda^{-}_{i}(\mathbb{G})=[\mathbf{x}^{-}(\mathbb{G})]^{i}\in E^{i}_{A(\mathbf{x}^{-}(\mathbb{G}))}.

For the rest of this section, we will focus on proving
Theorem 1.2
, which
states the invariance of

n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(\mathbb{G})
under the choice of
\mathbb{G}
.
This will allow us to denote them by
n^{\pm}(\Lambda)
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}(\Lambda)
.

In
[
OSzT08
,
OSSz15
]
, invariance of
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}
is proven by considering
the isomorphisms, on the homology level, associated to commutation and
destabilization, and showing that they carry
[\mathbf{x}^{\pm}]
to
[\mathbf{x}^{\pm}]
. For filtered
invariants, the isomorphisms are shown to be covered by filtered
quasi-isomorphisms on the chain level. We take a very similar approach, but
with two differences:

•

We work directly on the filtered chain level, and show that
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}
is
carried by the filtered chain map either to
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}
, or to
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}+\mathbf{y}
,
where
\mathbf{y}
belongs to a lower filtration; and

•

We do not require our destabilization maps to be isomorphisms on
homology (of the associated graded object); this is in line with the
philosophy of viewing these maps as maps of decorated link cobordisms.
(See, for example,
[
Zem19
]
.) Accordingly, we also separately consider
stabilization (and not just destabilization) maps.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathbb{G}_{+}
is obtained from
\mathbb{G}_{-}
by an
O

\mathcal{P}_{O}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{+})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{-})\left\llbracket 1,\frac{\mathchoice{\left\lvert\Lambda_{+}\right\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{+}\rvert}-\mathchoice{\left\lvert\Lambda_{-}\right\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}{\lvert\Lambda_{-}\rvert}+1}{2}\right\rrbracket

such that

\mathcal{P}_{O}(\mathbf{x}^{\pm}(\mathbb{G}_{+}))=\mathbf{x}^{\pm}(\mathbb{G}_{-}).

Proof.

Given
\mathbf{x}\in S(\mathbb{G}_{+})
and
\mathbf{y}\in S(\mathbb{G}_{-})
, let
\Tri(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
be the set of
triangles in the combined diagram whose vertices are points in
(\mathbf{x}\triangle\mathbf{y})\cup\{b\}
, with the following conditions.
First,
\Tri(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is empty unless
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
agree at
m-1
points. An element
p\in\Tri(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is an embedded triangle with non-reflex angles whose boundary lies on the horizontal and vertical circles (including
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
) and whose vertices are points in
(\mathbf{x}\triangle\mathbf{y})\cup\mathchoice{\left\{b\right\}}{\{b\}}{\{b\}}{\{b\}}
, such that
\partial(\partial p\cap\boldsymbol{\beta})=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}
.
Note that a triangle is automatically empty, in the sense that
\Int(p)\cap\mathbf{x}=\emptyset
. Let
\Tri_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
be the subset of
p\in\Tri(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
such that
t\cap\mathbb{O}=\emptyset
. See
[
Won17
, Figure 4]
for some examples of triangle domains in a similar context.
Define

\mathcal{P}_{O}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{+})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{GC}}(\mathbb{G}_{-})

to be the linear map that counts
these triangles:

\mathcal{P}_{O}(\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in S(\mathbb{G}_{-})}\sum\limits_{t\in\Tri_{\mathbb{O}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\mathbf{y}.

The proof that
\mathcal{P}_{O}
is a chain map is analogous to the proof for
\mathcal{P}_{X}
. This time, we consider concatenations of rectangles and triangles, rather than rectangles and pentagons; see, for example,
[
Won17
, Lemma 3.4]
for details. The proof that
\mathcal{P}_{O}
, with the shifts in the statement of the lemma, respects the Maslov and Alexander filtration, is also a direct computation similar to the proof for
\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Finally, by
[
BLW22
, Theorems 3.7 and 3.8]
, we know that when

’s and

under

is
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}(\mathbb{G}_{-})
. We now allow
X
’s to be unblocked, but in fact, no
X
’s can be in a triangle! Thus our proof is complete.

is a filtered chain map with no filtration level shift.

5.
Computations

While our invariants have nice definitions in terms of the classes of
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})
in sub-quotients of our complex, for computational purposes we would like to rephrase these definitions in terms of a particular kind of sub-quotient: the homology of a complex. Essentially, given a grid diagram
\mathbb{G}
and an integer
r\geq 1
, we would like to be able to answer the questions

(1)

Is
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}(\mathbb{G})
well-defined?

(2)

Is
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}(\mathbb{G})=0
for a given
r
?

by computing the class of some element in the homology of some complex. This
would allow us to use the techniques of
[
NOT08
]
to compute the homology
class of such an element by doing local searches and reductions to make
computations more efficient and feasible for larger knots.

We will tackle the second question first. It turns out that the question of whether
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}
is zero or not is equivalent to the question of whether or not
\mathbf{x}^{\pm}
is null-homologous in a particular sub-quotient complex.

Proposition 5.1
.

Given
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C
with
\partial x\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have that
[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}
if and only if
[x]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C)
.

Proof.

To start, assume
[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}
. Therefore,
[x]^{r}\in B^{r}_{p}
, so we have that
x+z=\partial y
, where
y\in\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C
and
z\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
. Therefore,
\partial y=x+z\equiv x\pmod{\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C}
, so
\partial[y]=[x]\in\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
, thus
[x]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C)
. This proves one direction.

To prove the other implication, assume
[x]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C)
. Then
x=\partial y+z
, for
y\in\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C
and
z\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
. Therefore,
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C\cap\partial(\mathcal{F}_{p+r-1}C)+\mathcal{F}_{p-1}
, thus
[x]^{r}\in B^{r}_{p}
and
[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}_{p}
.
∎

Now, we will attempt to answer the first question in a similar way.

Proposition 5.2
.

Given
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p}C
with
\partial x\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have that
d^{r}[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}
if and only if
[\partial x]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C)
.

Proof.

To start, assume
d^{r}[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}
. Therefore,
[x]^{r+1}\in Z^{r+1}_{p}
is defined, so we know that
\partial(x+z)=y
for some
y\in\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C
and
z\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
. Rearranging gives us that
\partial x=\partial z+y
, so
\partial x\equiv\partial z\pmod{\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C}
, thus
[\partial x]=[\partial z]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C)
.

To prove the other direction, assume
[\partial x]=0\in H_{*}(\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C/\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C)
. This means that
\partial x=\partial z+y
for some
z\in\mathcal{F}_{p-1}C
and
y\in\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C
. Rearranging gives us that
\partial(x+z)=y\in\mathcal{F}_{p-r-1}C
, so
[x]^{r+1}\in Z^{r+1}_{p}
is defined, thus
d^{r}[x]^{r}=0\in E^{r}
.
∎

Given a grid diagram
\mathbb{G}
, these two techniques allow us to efficiently compute our invariants
n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G})
as follows:

(1)

Let
r=1
. We know that
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{1}(\mathbb{G})
is always well-defined.

(2)

Use
Proposition 5.1
to check if

\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}(\mathbb{G})=0
. If so, then we are done, and we can conclude that

n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})=\infty
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G})=0
for
i>r
.

(3)

Otherwise, use
Proposition 5.2
to check if
d^{r}\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}(\mathbb{G})=0
. If not, then we are done, and we can conclude that
n^{\pm}(\mathbb{G})=r
and
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{i}(\mathbb{G})
is undefined for
i>r
.

(4)

If
d^{r}\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r}(\mathbb{G})=0
, then
\widetilde{\lambda}^{\pm}_{r+1}(\mathbb{G})
is well-defined, so we
may increment
r
by
1
and repeat the process from (
2
).

This algorithm is implemented in
[
JPS+24
]
, which was used to produce the results in
Section 1.5
.

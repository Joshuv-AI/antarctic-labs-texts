A note on a question of Garth Dales: Arens regularity as a three space property
Mahmoud Filali, Jorge Galindo

Abstract
. Garth Dales asked whether a Banach algebra 𝒜 \mathscr{A} with an Arens regular closed ideal 𝒥 {\mathcal{J}} and an Arens regular quotient 𝒜 / 𝒥 \mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}} is necessarily Arens regular. We prove in this note that, for a class of Banach algebras including the standard algebras in harmonic analysis, Garth’s conditions force the algebra to be even reflexive. We also give examples of Banach algebras with Garth’s conditions, that are not Arens regular.

Primary 22D15; Secondary 43A46, 43A60,47C15

1.
Introduction

Before Garth Dales sadly passed away, he asked the authors
[
7
]
, and presumably other colleagues, whether Arens regularity was a three-space property, that is, whether a Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}
with an Arens regular closed ideal
{\mathcal{J}}
yielding an Arens regular quotient
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is necessarily Arens regular.

In this note, we first consider weakly sequentially complete Banach algebras
\mathscr{A}
which are ideals in their second dual
\mathscr{A}^{**}
and have a contractive approximate identity.
We call such algebras
wscai algebras
.
When such a Banach algebra is a direct summand of its multiplier algebra, i.e., when
M(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{A}\oplus B
where
B
is a closed subalgebra of

M(\mathscr{A})
,
we prove that Garth’s question has a positive answer, forcing
\mathscr{A}
to be even reflexive.

We then provide examples demonstrating that, in general, Arens regularity fails the three-space property. We see that such examples can be found in algebras that exhibit two of the following properties: (1)
\mathscr{A}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
, (2)
\mathscr{A}
is weakly sequentially complete and (3)
\mathscr{A}
has an (approximate) identity.

Following
[
2
,
3
]
, the first Arens product on
\mathscr{A}^{**}
is defined through the following three stages. For
f\in\mathscr{A}^{*},

\varphi,\psi\in\mathscr{A}
and
m,n\in\mathscr{A}^{**},

define

(1)

\langle f\cdot\varphi,\psi\rangle=\langle f,\varphi\psi\rangle,\quad\langle n\cdot f,\varphi\rangle=\langle n,f\cdot\varphi\rangle\quad\langle mn,f\rangle=\langle m,n\cdot f\rangle.

In an analogous way, the
second Arens product
in
\mathscr{A}^{**}

is defined via the following relations for
f\in\mathscr{A}^{*},

\varphi,\psi\in\mathscr{A}
and
m,n\in\mathscr{A}^{**},

(2)

\langle\varphi\cdot f,\psi\rangle=\langle f,\psi\varphi\rangle,\quad\langle f\cdot m,\varphi\rangle=\langle m,\varphi\cdot f\rangle,\quad\langle m\diamond n,f\rangle=\langle n,f\cdot m\rangle.

With either of these products, the Banach space
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
is made into a Banach algebra containing
\mathscr{A}
as a subalgebra.

It is clear from the way the products are defined that the translations

m\mapsto mn\quad\text{and}\quad m\mapsto n\diamond m:\mathscr{A}^{**}\to\mathscr{A}^{**}

are weak
∗
-weak
∗
-continuous for
any
n\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
,
and the translations

(3)

m\mapsto nm\quad\text{and}\quad m\mapsto m\diamond n:\mathscr{A}^{**}\to\mathscr{A}^{**}

are weak
∗
-weak
∗
-continuous for any
n\in\mathscr{A}
.
The algebra
\mathscr{A}
is
Arens regular
when one of the latter translations (and then both are) is weak
∗
-weak
∗
-continuous not only for the elements
n\in\mathscr{A}
but for every

n\in\mathscr{A}^{{\ast\ast}}
. This is the same as having
mn=m\diamondsuit n
, for every
m,n\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
.
When the continuity of each of the two translations in (
3
) holds for no
n\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\setminus\mathscr{A}
, we say that
\mathscr{A}
is
strongly Arens irregular
.

It may be worth remarking that an algebra
\mathscr{A}
may be both Arens regular and strongly Arens irregular.
This will be the case if and only if
\mathscr{A}
is reflexive.

The relevance of the space
WAP(\mathscr{A})
of
weakly almost periodic functionals
on
\mathscr{A}
was first noticed, independently, by Pym and Young in
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
. This is the space of all
f\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
such that the linear
map

\varphi\mapsto\varphi\cdot f:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}^{*}

is weakly compact. A consequence of Grothendieck’s criterion is that
WAP(\mathscr{A})
is actually the subspace of
\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
which consists of those
f\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
such that

\langle mn,f\rangle=\langle m\diamondsuit n,f\rangle\quad\text{for every}\quad m,n\in\mathscr{A}^{**}.

So,
\mathscr{A}
is
Arens regular if and only if
WAP(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{A}^{*}
.

By a bounded right approximate identity (
brai
for short), we mean a bounded net
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
in
\mathscr{A}
such that for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
,
\varphi e_{\alpha}\to\varphi
in
\mathscr{A}
.
A bounded left
approximate identity (blai) is defined similarly. A bounded approximate
identity (bai) is a net which is both a blai and a brai. When the terms of an approximate identity are all contained in the unit ball of
\mathscr{A}
, we call it
contractive
. The accumulation points of a brai
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
are right identities in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
for the first Arens product, and the accumulation points of a blai are left identities in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
for the second Arens product. So, the accumulation points of bais are right identities for the first Arens product and a left identities for the second, such identities are
known as mixed identities.

Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
is a weakly sequentially complete Banach algebra that is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
and contains a contractive approximate identity and let
e
be a mixed identity of
\mathscr{A}^{**}
, i.e.,
\mathscr{A}
is a wscai algebra.
The second dual
\mathscr{A}^{**}
of
\mathscr{A}
has then the direct sum decomposition

\mathscr{A}^{**}=e\mathscr{A}^{**}\oplus WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp},

with

\displaystyle WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\colon\langle\,m,f\,\rangle=0,\mbox{ for any $f\in WAP(\mathscr{A})$}\right\}

\displaystyle=(1-e)\mathscr{A}^{**}.

The subspace
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
is actually a closed ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
and

\mathscr{A}^{**}\,WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}=WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}\diamondsuit\mathscr{A}^{**}=\{0\}.

The Banach algebras

e\mathscr{A}^{**}
,
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{*}
and
the multiplier algebra
M(\mathscr{A})
of
\mathscr{A}
are isometrically isomorphic. Nevertheless, it may be worthwhile to recall the educating remark
put by Baker, Lau and Pym in
[
4
, page 190]
, that
e\mathscr{A}^{**}
and
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{*}
(and so
M(\mathscr{A})
) need not be isomorphic
when they carry their natural weak
∗
topologies induced, respectively from
\mathscr{A}^{*}
and
WAP(\mathscr{A})
.
For the details, see
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
.

As noted in
[
12
]
, if
{\mathcal{J}}
is a closed ideal in
\mathscr{A},
then
{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
admits an analogous decomposition. Denoting by
i^{\ast}\colon\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\to{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast}
the restriction map, adjoint to the inclusion map
i\colon{\mathcal{J}}\to\mathscr{A}
, one has

(4)

{\mathcal{J}}^{**}=e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}\oplus i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}=e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}\oplus(\mathscr{A}\cdot{\mathcal{J}}^{*})^{\perp},

where now

\displaystyle i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{m\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}\colon\langle\,m,i^{*}(f)\,\rangle=0,\mbox{ for every $f\in WAP(\mathscr{A})$}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{m\in\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}\colon\langle\,m,f\,\rangle=0,\mbox{ for every $f\in WAP(\mathscr{A})$}\right\},

and
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}
denotes the weak
∗
-closure of
{\mathcal{J}}
in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
.
The right ideal
e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
may be identified with
\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{w}
, the weak
∗
-closure of

{\mathcal{J}}
in
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{*}
. The properties of this decomposition, discussed in
[
12
]
, are summarized below

(i)

\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{w}
is a closed ideal of
M(\mathscr{A}),

(ii)

i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
is a closed ideal of
{\mathcal{J}}^{**},

(iii)

\overline{{\mathcal{J}}}^{w}
is isometrically isomorphic to both
e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
and
{\mathcal{J}}^{**}\diamondsuit e,

(iv)

i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}=(1-e){\mathcal{J}}^{**}={\mathcal{J}}^{**}\diamondsuit(1-e),

(v)

{\mathcal{J}}^{**}\,i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}=i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\diamondsuit{\mathcal{J}}^{**}=\{0\}.

1.1.
Some special ideals of
L^{1}(G)

During the following discussion
G
will denote a compact Abelian group with Haar measure
\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G$}}
and
\widehat{G}
the group of all continuous homomorphisms of
G
into the multiplicative group
\mathbb{T}
of all complex numbers of modulus 1. If
M(G)
denotes the space of bounded Borel measures on
G
and
\mu\in M(G)
, the
Fourier-Stieltjes transform
of
\mu
is the bounded function
\widehat{\mu}\colon\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\widehat{\mu}(\chi)=\int_{{}_{G}}\chi(t^{-1})d\mu(t)\mbox{ for each $\chi\in\widehat{G}$.}

If
X
is a linear subspace of
M(G)
and
E\subset\widehat{G}
, we denote by
X_{E}
the subspace of
X
consisting of all elements in
X
with Fourier-Stieltjes transform supported in
E
, that is

X_{E}=\{\mu\in X:\widehat{\mu}(\chi)=0~\text{for}~\chi\in\widehat{G}\setminus E\}.

We will always regard
L^{1}(G)
as an ideal in
M(G)
through the embedding
f\mapsto f\cdot d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G$}}
.

In Harmonic Analysis, there has been a great interest in subsets of
\widehat{G}
making
X_{E}=Y_{E}
for some subspace
Y
of
X.

For instance
Riesz sets
are those for which
M_{E}(G)=L^{1}_{E}(G)
,
and
Rosenthal sets
are those for which
L^{\infty}_{E}(G)=C_{E}(G)
. We will say that
E\subseteq\widehat{G}
is a
co-Rosenthal set
when its complement
\widehat{G}\setminus E
is a Rosenthal set,
that is, when,
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
and
\widehat{f}(E)=\{0\}
imply that
f
is continuous.

We will be assuming that
L^{\infty}(G)
is defined so that
L^{1}(G)^{\ast}=L^{\infty}(G)
, through the duality

\langle\,f,\varphi\,\rangle=\int\varphi(x)\overline{f(x)}d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G$}}(x),\quad f\in L^{\infty}(G),\;\varphi\in L^{1}(G).

Since
\widehat{f}(\chi)=\langle\,f,\chi\,\rangle
, for every
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}
,
and since
E\subseteq L^{1}_{E}(G)
, it is clear that
L^{1}_{E}(G)^{\perp}\subseteq L^{\infty}_{\widehat{G}\setminus E}(G)
. On the other hand, we may
L^{1}
-approximate
the functions
\varphi
in
L^{1}_{E}(G)
by trigonometric polynomials
\varphi_{n}
spanned by characters from
E
. Since
\langle\,f,\varphi_{n}\,\rangle=0\quad\text{ for every}\quad f\in L^{\infty}_{\widehat{G}\setminus E}(G),

we see that
\langle\,f,\varphi\,\rangle=0\quad\text{ for every}\quad f\in L^{\infty}_{\widehat{G}\setminus E}(G)
.
As a consequence,

L^{1}_{E}(G)^{\perp}=\{f\in L^{\infty}(G):\langle f,\varphi\rangle=0\;\text{for all}\;\varphi\in L^{1}_{E}(G)\}=L_{\widehat{G}\setminus E}^{\infty}(G),

and, accordingly,
E
is a co-Rosenthal set if and only if

L^{1}_{E}(G)^{\perp}\subseteq C(G).

Now, it happens that a functional on
L^{1}(G)
is weakly almost periodic if and only if it is continuous (one direction is a theorem of Ülger
[
19
]
valid for any locally compact group, the other only works for compact groups and can be deduced from Grothendieck’s double limit criterion), so

L^{1}_{E}(G)^{\perp}\subseteq WAP(L^{1}(G)),

whenever
E
is a co-Rosenthal set.

1.2.
Riesz and co-Rosenthal ideals

Closed ideals of
L^{1}(G)
are always of the form
L^{1}_{E}(G)
for some subset
E

in
\widehat{G}
(see
[
15
, Theorem 38.7]
). We may therefore describe the ideals of
L^{1}(G)
through the properties of the corresponding set in
\widehat{G}

and may rightfully speak of Riesz or co-Rosenthal
ideals
of
L^{1}(G)
.
Under this point of view, bearing in mind the discussions earlier in this section, the following definitions
are
genuine extensions of the concepts of Riesz and co-Rosenthal set.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra and let
{\mathcal{J}}
be a closed ideal of
\mathscr{A}
.

–

We say that
{\mathcal{J}}
is
co-Rosenthal
if
{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A}).

–

Assume in addition that
\mathscr{A}
has a mixed identity
e\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
, we say that
{\mathcal{J}}
is a
Riesz ideal
if
e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}={\mathcal{J}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The definition of Riesz ideal does not depend on the choice of the mixed identity of
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
. Indeed, once one knows that
e{\mathcal{J}}^{**}={\mathcal{J}}
, for some right identity
e\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}
, then
f{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}=fe{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}=f{\mathcal{J}}={\mathcal{J}}
for any other mixed identity
f\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}
.

When
\mathscr{A}
has a contractive approximate identity,
{\mathcal{J}}
being Riesz turns to be equivalent to being weak
∗
-closed in the multiplier algebra
M(\mathscr{A})
, see the identifications of
M(\mathscr{A})
with
e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
and
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\ast}
described in the Introduction. So, if
G
is a compact group and
\mathscr{A}=L^{1}(G)
,
{\mathcal{J}}=L_{E}^{1}(G)
,
E\subseteq\widehat{G}
, is Riesz when
{\mathcal{J}}
is weak
∗
-closed in
M(G)
, and so
L_{E}^{1}(G)=M_{E}(G)
. In the same vein, when
G
is a discrete amenable group and
E\subseteq G
, an ideal
A_{E}(G)=\{u\in A(G)\colon u(G\setminus E)=\{0\}\}
is Riesz if it is weak
∗
-closed in
B(G)
, and so
A_{E}(G)=B_{E}(G)
.

Lemma 1.3
.

For a closed ideal
{\mathcal{J}}
in
\mathscr{A}
, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathcal{J}}
is co-Rosenthal.

(ii)

i^{**}\bigl(i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\bigr)=WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
.

(iii)

The quotient Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is Arens regular.

Proof.

Let
m\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
.
Then

\langle i^{**}(m),f\rangle=\langle\,m,i^{*}(f)\,\rangle=0,\quad\text{for every}\quad f\in WAP(\mathscr{A}),

i.e.,
i^{**}(m)\in WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}.
Hence, the inclusion
i^{\ast\ast}\bigl(i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\bigr)\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
always holds.

Assume now that
{\mathcal{J}}
is co-Rosenthal and let
m\in WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
. Then
m\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp\perp}=i^{**}({\mathcal{J}}^{**})
, so that
m=i^{**}(n)

for some
n\in{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
and

\langle n,i^{*}(f)\rangle=\langle m,f\rangle=0\quad\text{for every}\quad f\in WAP(\mathscr{A}).

We see that
n\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}

and so

m\in i^{**}\bigl(i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp})\bigr)
. Therefore, (i) implies (ii).

To check that (ii) implies (i), suppose otherwise that
{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}
is not contained in
WAP(\mathscr{A})
. Then there is some element
m\in WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
which is not in
{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp\perp}=i^{**}({\mathcal{J}}^{**})
.
In particular,
m
is not in
i^{**}(i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}),
and so (ii) fails to hold.

Let now
\pi:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
be the quotient map, and recall that
{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}

and
(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{*}
are identified via
f=\widetilde{f}\circ\pi,

where
f\in{\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}
and
\widetilde{f}\in(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{*}
.
Then for any two nets

(\varphi_{\alpha})
and
(\psi_{\beta})
in
\mathscr{A}
we find

\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha}\psi_{\beta}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{f},\pi(\varphi_{\alpha})\pi(\psi_{\beta})\rangle.

Since
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is Arens regular if and only if
WAP(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})={\mathcal{J}}^{\perp}
, this quickly shows that Statements (i) and (iii) are equivalent.
∎

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Abelian group and
E\subseteq\widehat{G}
. The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\widehat{G}\setminus E
is a Rosenthal set, i.e.,
E
is a co-Rosenthal set.

(ii)

The quotient Banach algebra
L^{1}(G)/L_{E}^{1}(G)
is Arens regular.

We refer to
[
12
, Lemma 4.5]
for the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be wscai Banach algebra. Let
m\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
be such that
\mathscr{A}^{*}\cdot m\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A})
or
m\cdot\mathscr{A}^{*}\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A})
. Then
m=\varphi_{0}+r
for some
\varphi_{0}\in\mathscr{A}
and
r\in WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
.

We record here the following elementary facts for later use.

Lemma 1.6
.

For
f\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
,
e\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
and
m\in{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
, we have

(i)

m\cdot i^{*}(f)=i^{*}(m\cdot f)
.

(ii)

(em)\cdot f=e\cdot(m\cdot f)
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a wscai Banach algebra and
{\mathcal{J}}
a closed ideal in
\mathscr{A}
.
If
{\mathcal{J}}
is co-Rosenthal and Arens regular, then it is Riesz.

Proof.

Let
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a contractive approximate identity in
\mathscr{A}
and
e
be a mixed identity in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
associated with

(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
.

Let
m\in{\mathcal{J}}^{**}
and fix
f\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
. Given any
r\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
, using Lemma
1.6
, one has

\displaystyle\langle r,i^{*}((em)\cdot f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle i^{**}(r),(em)\cdot f\rangle=\langle i^{**}(r),e\cdot(m\cdot f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle i^{**}(r)e,m\cdot f\rangle=\langle i^{**}(r),m\cdot f\rangle=\langle r,i^{*}(m\cdot f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle r,m\cdot i^{*}(f)\rangle=\langle rm,i^{*}(f)\rangle.

Taking into account that
{\mathcal{J}}
is assumed to be Arens regular, we find

\langle rm,i^{*}(f)\rangle=\langle r\diamondsuit m,i^{*}(f)\rangle=0.

Therefore,
\langle\,r,i^{\ast}((em)\cdot f)\,\rangle=0
. Since this equality holds for every
r\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
, we conclude that
i^{\ast}(em\cdot f)\in\overline{i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))}
.

Let then
(g_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
WAP(\mathscr{A})
such that

\lim_{n}i^{*}(g_{n})=i^{*}((em)\cdot f)
.
Since the restriction mapping
i^{\ast}
is a quotient map, one can find (see, e.g., the proof of
[
6
, Theorem4.2(b)]
) a sequence
(h_{n})
in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
and
h_{0}\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N},i^{*}(g_{n})=i^{*}(h_{n})
, and
\lim_{n}h_{n}=h_{0}
.
As
i^{\ast}(g_{n})=i^{*}(h_{n})
, one finds that
g_{n}-h_{n}\in J^{\perp}\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A})
and, hence, that
h_{n}\in WAP(\mathscr{A})
as well. As a consequence,
h_{0}\in WAP(\mathscr{A})
. The equality
i^{*}(em\cdot f)=i^{\ast}(h_{0})
implies that
em\cdot f\in h_{0}+J^{\perp}\subseteq WAP(\mathscr{A})
.

Since
em\cdot f\in WAP(\mathscr{A})
for every
f\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
, it follows from Lemma
1.5
that
em=\varphi+r
for some
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}
and
r\in WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}
. Taking into account that
e\mathscr{A}^{**}\cap WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}=\{0\}
, we must have
em=\varphi
, which means that
em\in\mathscr{A}\cap{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}
. As elements in
\mathscr{A}\cap{\mathcal{J}}^{\ast\ast}
can be obtained as weak
∗
-limits of nets in
{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
is weakly closed in
\mathscr{A}
, we conclude that
em\in{\mathcal{J}}
.

The ideal,
{\mathcal{J}}
is thus a Riesz ideal.
∎

To state our main theorem we require the following technical condition.

Definition 1.8
.

We say that a Banach algebra is a
standard
wscai algebra, if it is in the wscai class and
e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
contains a closed vector subspace
\mathscr{B}
such that
e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}=\mathscr{A}\oplus_{1}\mathscr{B}
.

As already remarked,
e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
is isometrically isomorphic to the multiplier algebra
M(\mathscr{A})
. The Lebesgue decomposition theorem and its extension to the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra, see
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
, show that group algebras on compact groups and Fourier algebras on discrete amenable groups are both standard wscai algebras.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
have a bai and be an ideal in its second dual and let
{\mathcal{J}}
be a closed ideal in
\mathscr{A}
. Assume that
e\mathscr{A}^{**}=\mathscr{A}\oplus_{1}B
. If
{\mathcal{J}}
is Riesz and co-Rosenthal, then
\mathscr{A}
is regular.

Proof.

We prove first that the quotient
algebra
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is weakly sequentially complete. Note to begin with that,
{\mathcal{J}}
being both co-Rosenthal and Riesz, Lemma
1.3
yields

\displaystyle(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{**}=\mathscr{A}^{**}/i^{**}({\mathcal{J}}^{**})

\displaystyle=\frac{e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\oplus WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}}{{i^{**}(e{\mathcal{J}}^{**})\oplus i^{**}(i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}}{i^{**}(e{\mathcal{J}}^{**})}=\frac{e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}}{{\mathcal{J}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{A}\oplus_{1}B}{{\mathcal{J}}}=\frac{\mathscr{A}}{{\mathcal{J}}}\oplus_{1}B.

Define

S\colon(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{**}\to(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{**}
by

S\bigl((u,v)\bigr)=(u,-v)\quad\text{with}\quad u\in\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}\;\text{ and}\;v\in B.

Then
S
is an isometry and, if
I
denotes the identity map on
(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{\ast\ast}
, then
\ker(S-I)=\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
. By
[
14
, Corollary II.2]
,
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is weakly sequentially complete.

Since
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
has a bai, namely
(e_{\alpha}+{\mathcal{J}})
, where
(e_{\alpha})
is a bai in
\mathscr{A},
and
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is clearly a closed ideal
in
(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{**}
.
By
[
4
, Theorem 2.1(iii)]
, this implies that
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is strongly Arens irregular.
Since
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is Arens regular by Lemma
1.3
,
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
must be reflexive.
Therefore,

\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}=(\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}})^{**}=\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}\oplus_{1}B,

and so
B
must be trivial. Thus
e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}=\mathscr{A}
so that
\mathscr{A}
is a Riesz ideal in itself. By
[
12
, Theorem 5.2]
(see, as well,
[
20
]
and
[
11
]
for the particular case
\mathscr{A}=L^{1}(G)
)

\mathscr{A}
is Arens regular, as required.
∎

We can now answer Garth’s question for wscai algebras.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a standard wscai algebra and
{\mathcal{J}}
be a closed ideal in
\mathscr{A}
.
If both
{\mathcal{J}}
and
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
are Arens regular, then
\mathscr{A}
is reflexive.

Proof.

If
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is Arens regular, then by Lemma
1.3
,
{\mathcal{J}}
is co-Rosenthal.
If in addition
{\mathcal{J}}
is Arens regular, then by Lemma
1.7
,
{\mathcal{J}}
is Riesz.
So Theorem
1.9
shows that
\mathscr{A}
is Arens regular. But since
\mathscr{A}
is a wscai algebra,

[
4
, Theorem 2.1(iii)]
implies that
\mathscr{A}
is sAir. Thus,
\mathscr{A}
is reflexive.
∎

Next Corollary follows directly from the preceding one.

Corollary 1.11
.

A non-reflexive standard wscai Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}
cannot contain a closed ideal
{\mathcal{J}}
such that both
{\mathcal{J}}
and the quotient
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
are Arens regular.

Remarks 1.12
.

(a) Ülger asked in
[
20
]
whether Arens regular closed ideals in the group algebra of a compact abelian group are necessarily Riesz ideals. The question was re-stated in
[
12
]
for any wscai algebra.
If the algebra is not weakly sequentially complete, then examples for which this is not true are easily found, for example

c_{0}
is a closed ideal in
\ell_{\infty},
has a bai, is Arens regualar. But
c_{0}
is not a Riesz ideal in
\ell_{\infty}

(b)
It is known
[
9
]
that in the dual
\widehat{G}
of a compact Abelian group

, the union of a Riesz set and a Rosenthal set is a Riesz set. Since
\widehat{G}
itself is not Riesz unless

is finite, a Riesz subset of an infinite group can never be co-Rosenthal.

By this reason, Lemma
1.7
can be restated in this context.

Corollary 1.13
.

A co-Rosenthal ideal of
L^{1}(G)
,
G
compact infinite, is never Arens regular.

One can but wonder whether this result holds true for more general wscai algebras.

Corollary
1.11
can be recast in the light of Corollary
1.13
.

Corollary 1.14
.

Let
G
be an infinite compact Abelian group.
If

is such that

is Arens regular, then
L_{E}^{1}(G)
is not Arens regular.

2.
Examples

In the previous section, we proved that
if a Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}
satisfies the following properties

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is a closed two-sided ideal in
A^{**},

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
contains a contractive approximate identity,

(iii)

\mathscr{A}
is weakly sequentially complete,

(iv)

for a mixed identity
e\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
, the space
e\mathscr{A}^{**}
is a direct sum
\mathscr{A}\oplus\mathscr{B}
for some closed vector subspace
\mathscr{B}
,

(v)

\mathscr{A}
contains a closed ideal
{\mathcal{J}}
such that both
{\mathcal{J}}
and
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
are Arens regular, i.e.,
\mathscr{A}
satisfies
Garth’s conditions,

then
\mathscr{A}
is reflexive, that is,
\mathscr{A}
agrees strongly with Garth’s conclusion.

In this section, we give examples of Banach algebras that satisfy Garth’s conditions (v) but are not Arens regular. Of course, each of the algebras lacks some of the properties (i)–(iv), we have tried to keep as many of them as possible.

Our examples are built on certain
semidirect
products constructed through the natural right and left dual actions of a Banach algebra
\mathscr{B}
on its dual
\mathscr{B}^{*}
. For
x\in\mathscr{B}^{*}
and
\varphi,\>\psi\in\mathscr{B}
, these actions are given by

(Right action)

\displaystyle\langle x\varphi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x,\varphi\psi\rangle\quad\quad\mbox{ and }

(Left action)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi x,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x,\psi\varphi\rangle.

Following the general procedure described by Arens in
[
2
]
for forming adjoints of bilinear maps,
the right and left module actions of
\mathscr{B}
on
\mathscr{B}^{*}
extend to right and left module actions of
\mathscr{B}^{**}
on
\mathscr{B}^{***}.

The extensions are reached after three steps, akin to those leading the Arens products.

For any
p\in\mathscr{B}^{***}
and

m\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}
,
pm
is defined after defining
mf\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}
,
f\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}
and
fx\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
,
x\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
:

\displaystyle\langle\,pm,f\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,p,mf\,\rangle,

(5)

\displaystyle\langle\,mf,x\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,m,fx\,\rangle,\;

\displaystyle\langle\,fx,\varphi\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,f,x\varphi\,\rangle,\quad\varphi\in\mathscr{B}.

The action and
mp\in\mathscr{B}^{***}
is defined in the same vein

\displaystyle\langle\,mp,f\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,m,pf\,\rangle,

(6)

\displaystyle\langle\,pf,x\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,p,fx\,\rangle,\;

\displaystyle\langle\,fx,\varphi\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,f,\varphi x\,\rangle,\quad\varphi\in\mathscr{B}.

We now define the Banach algebras that constitute our examples.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a Banach algebra with dual
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
. We define three different Banach algebras with underlying Banach space
\mathscr{B}^{*}\oplus_{1}\mathscr{B}
. The multiplications are defined as follows, for
x,y\in\mathscr{B}^{*}
, and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathscr{B}
.

(i) The algebra
\mathbf{\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}}
:

(x,\varphi)(y,\psi)=(x\psi,\varphi\psi)
, if
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
is regarded as a right
\mathscr{B}
-module.

(ii) The algebra
\mathbf{{}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B}}
:

(x,\varphi)(y,\psi)=(\varphi y,\varphi\psi)
, if
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
is regarded as a left
\mathscr{B}
-module.

(iii) The algebra
\mathbf{{}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}}
:

(x,\varphi)(y,\psi)=(x\psi+\varphi y,\varphi\psi)
, if
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
is regarded as a
\mathscr{B}
-bimodule.

For
p,q\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast\ast}
and
m,n\in\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}
, the first Arens product in these algebras is then given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{(\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}})^{\ast\ast}}:(p,m)(q,n)

\displaystyle=(pn,mn)

\displaystyle\mathbf{(_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B})^{\ast\ast}}:(p,m)(q,n)

\displaystyle=(mq,mn)

\displaystyle\mathbf{(_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}})^{\ast\ast}}:(p,m)(q,n)

\displaystyle=(pn+mq,mn),

where
mn
is the first Arens product of
m
and
n
in
\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}
and
pn
and
mq
are given in (
5
) and (
6
), respectively.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{B}=c_{0}
with pointwise product. The Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}=_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\!\!\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}
has the following properties:

(i)

\mathscr{A}
is not weakly sequentially complete.

(ii)

\mathscr{A}
is not standard.

(iii)

\mathscr{A}
has a contractive approximate identity.

(iv)

\mathscr{A}
is a two-sided ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
.

(v)

\mathscr{A}
satisfies Dales’s conditions.

(vi)

\mathscr{A}
is not Arens regular.

Proof.

The algebra
c_{0}
has a contractive approximate identity
(e_{n})
, where
e_{n}=\mbox{\large${\mathbf{1}}$}_{\{1,...,n\}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is a closed two-sided ideal in its second conjugate
\ell_{\infty}
. The module action of
c_{0}

on
\ell_{1}
turns out to be pointwise multiplication, and so
\mathscr{A}
is a commutative Banach algebra.

The sequence
(0,e_{n})

is then a contractive approximate identity in
\mathscr{A}
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\|xe_{n}-x\|_{1}=\lim_{n}\|\varphi e_{n}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=0\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\ell_{1},\varphi\in c_{0}.

To see that
\mathscr{A}
is a left ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast},
recall that

(p,m)(x,\varphi)=(p\varphi+mx,m\varphi)\quad\text{for}\quad(x,\varphi)\in\mathscr{A},\;(p,m)\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast},

and note that

mx\in\ell_{1}
and
m\varphi\in c_{0}
since
c_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
are ideals in
\ell_{\infty}
(under pointwise multiplication).
So we only need
to check that
p\varphi\in\ell_{1}
for every
p\in\ell_{1}^{\ast\ast}
and
\varphi\in c_{0}.

We decompose
p=x_{0}+p_{0}
with
x_{0}\in\ell_{1}
and
p_{0}\in c_{0}^{\perp}
. To see that
p\varphi\in\ell_{1}

we consider a net
(f_{\alpha})
in
\ell_{\infty}
that converges to 0 in the
\sigma(\ell_{\infty},\ell_{1})
-topology. Then, using that

\varphi f_{\alpha}\in c_{0}
and that
x_{0}\varphi\in\ell_{1}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\langle\,p\varphi,f_{\alpha}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}\langle\,p,\varphi f_{\alpha}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha}\langle\,x_{0}\varphi,f_{\alpha}\,\rangle+\lim_{\alpha}\langle\,p_{0},\varphi f_{\alpha}\,\rangle=0.

It follows that
p\varphi\in\ell_{1}
,
as wanted.
Since
\mathscr{A}
is commutative, this shows that
\mathscr{A}
is also a right ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{**}.

To see that
\mathscr{A}
satisfies Dales’ conditions, let
{\mathcal{J}}=\ell_{1}\times\{0\}.

Then
{\mathcal{J}}
is a closed two-sided ideal in
\mathscr{A}
since
(x,0)(y,\varphi)=(x\varphi,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(y,\varphi)(x,0)=(\varphi x,0)
,
and is clearly Arens regular since the operation in
{\mathcal{J}}
is trivial.

The quotient
\mathscr{A}/{\mathcal{J}}
is isometrically isomorphic to
c_{0}
. It is, therefore, Arens regular.

As is always the case with the dual action of a non-reflexive algebra with a brai
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, the action of
c_{0}
on
\ell_{1}
is not regular, and therefore, neither is
\mathscr{A}
.

Since
c_{0}
is not weakly sequentially complete, neither is the algebra
\mathscr{A}
.

Let now
e=(0,\mathbf{1})
,
\mathbf{1}
standing for the constant 1-sequence in
\ell_{\infty}
, be the accumulation point of the approximate identity
(0,e_{n})
in
\mathscr{A}^{{\ast\ast}}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
is complemented in
e\mathscr{A}^{{\ast\ast}}
and let
P\colon e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\to\mathscr{A}
be the corresponding projection. If
\pi_{2}\colon\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}^{\ast}\oplus\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}
is the second projection and we consider the embedding
i\colon\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}\to e\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}
given by
i(x)=(0,x)=(0,\mathbf{1})(0,x)
, then the composition of the following functions

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}^{\ast\ast}\overset{i}{\to}e\mathscr{A}^{{\ast\ast}}\overset{P}{\to}\mathscr{A}\overset{\pi_{2}}{\to}\mathscr{B}

is a bounded projection
\ell_{\infty}\to c_{0}
, whose existence contradicts the Phillips-Sobczyk theorem, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.5.5]
.∎

Remark 2.3
.

If we consider the algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}
or
{}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B}
, then the Banach algebra
\mathscr{A}
is not commutative, and satisfies all the properties in Example
2.2
but the third one. It has just a contractive right (resp., left) approximate identity.

The element
(0,1)
is just a right identity in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
, and
(0,1)\mathscr{A}^{**}=\{0\}\oplus_{1}\ell_{\infty}
cannot be decomposed as
\mathscr{A}\oplus_{1}B
since
c_{0}
is not complemented in
\ell_{\infty}.
So in this case,
\mathscr{A}
is not a standard algebra either. The rest of the statements follow in the same manner.

To make Banach algebras modelled on spaces
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}\oplus_{1}\mathscr{B}
weakly sequentially complete, one needs nonreflexive Banach algebras that are weakly sequentially complete and have
weakly sequentially complete duals. This is not easily achieved. Bourgain and Delbaen managed to construct such a Banach space in
[
5
]
. One can define a Banach algebra structure on this Banach space in a standard, albeit quite trivial, way. The algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}
and
{}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}\mathscr{B}
are then Banach algebras with one-sided identity which are one-sided ideals in their biduals. These algebras satisfy Garth’s conditions, but they are not Arens
regular.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be the weakly sequentially complete non-reflexive Banach space defined in
[
5
, Section 4]
whose dual
\mathscr{B}^{*}
is weakly sequentially complete as well.
Fix
x_{0}\in\mathscr{B}^{*}
and
e\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\langle x_{0},e\rangle=1.

We introduce an operation on
\mathscr{B}
by

(7)

\varphi\psi=\langle x_{0},\psi\rangle\varphi

turning it into a Banach algebra.

Then the following properties are easily verified.

(i)

e
is a right identity for
\mathscr{B}
.

(ii)

\mathscr{B}
cannot possess a blai
(e_{\alpha})
since
e_{\alpha}\varphi=\langle x_{0},\varphi\rangle e_{\alpha}.

(iii)

\mathscr{B}
is a right ideal but not a left ideal in its second dual.

(iv)

\mathscr{B}
is Arens regular.

The natural right module action of
\mathscr{B}
on
\mathscr{B}^{*}
is now given by

x\varphi=\langle x,\varphi\rangle x_{0}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathscr{B}^{*},\;\varphi\in\mathscr{B}.

Let now
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}
. The operation in
\mathscr{A}
simplifies to

(x,\varphi)(y,\psi)=(x\psi,\varphi\psi)=(\langle x,\psi\rangle x_{0},\langle x_{0},\psi\rangle\varphi),

and so the first Arens product in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
is given by

\displaystyle(p,m)(q,n)

\displaystyle=(pn,mn)=(\langle p,n\rangle x_{0},\langle n,x_{0}\rangle m).

From this identity, we see that
\mathscr{A}
is a right but not a left ideal in its second dual.
Note also that there is no net
(e_{\alpha})
in
\mathscr{B}
which acts as a brai for the right
\mathscr{B}
-module
\mathscr{B}^{*}
,
since
\|xe_{\alpha}-x\|=\|\langle x,e_{\alpha}\rangle x_{0}-x\|
converges to zero only if
x
is a multiple of
x_{0}.

This implies that
\mathscr{A}
does not possess a brai.
The algebra
\mathscr{A}
does not posses a blai either (use the observation (ii) above or argue directly).
And as in Example
2.2
, one sees that
\mathscr{A}
satisfies Dales conditions.
Finally, since
\mathscr{B}
is not reflexive, it follows from
[
10
]
, that the natural right module action is not Arens regular, and so
the algebra
\mathscr{A}
is not Arens regular.
We summarize these observations in our second example.

Example 2.4

Let
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}_{\mathscr{B}^{\ast}}

\mathscr{B}

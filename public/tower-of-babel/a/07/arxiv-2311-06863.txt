Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, our work is devoted to studying Volterra type McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations with singular kernels. Firstly, the well-posedness of Volterra type McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations are established. And then propagation of chaos is proved with explicit
estimate of the convergence rate. Finally, We also propose an explicit Euler scheme for an interacting particle system associated with the Volterra type
McKean-Vlasov equation.

Key Words:
Stochastic Volterra equations, McKean-Vlasov SDEs, Well-posedness, Propagation of chaos, Euler schemes.

(2020):
60H20; 45D05; 60H35.

Introduction

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) are mathematical models that characterize complex phenomena including physical, chemical, and biological sciences. Among them, Volterra-type stochastic differential equations are important and interesting class of research objects with a large number of applications in mathematics finance, biology, etc. In particular, when the kernel
K(t,s)=K^{H}(t,s)
, where
K^{H}(t,s)
is defined in Remark
3.2
, Volterra-type SDEs is reduced to the classical SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion.
If the kernel
K(t,s)=(t-s)^{-\alpha}
with suitable index
\alpha
, Volterra-type SDEs is reduced to Caputo fractional SDEs, see e.g.
[
3
]
.

Stochastic Volterra integral equations are more complex models compared with usual SDEs. On the one hand, these equations are in general neither Markovian nor semi-martingales, and include fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index smaller than
1/2
as a special case. On the other hand, possibly singular kernels need to be controlled carefully, which causes fundamental technical difficulties. In the past decade, stochastic Volterra integral equations have been intensively investigated, e.g. the well-posedness
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
31
,
32
,
33
,
35
]
, the asymptotic properties such as the
large deviation
[
15
,
10
,
19
,
34
,
35
]
, moderate deviation
[
16
,
10
]
and central limit theorem
[
27
]
, and numerical methods
[
17
,
26
,
34
]
.

McKean-Vlasov SDEs (also called distribution dependent SDEs
[
24
]
or mean-field SDEs), it was first discussed by Kac in literatures
[
12
,
13
]
, which describe limiting behaviors of individual particles in an interacting particle system of mean-field type when the number of particles goes to infinity (so called propagation of chaos) and widely used in various fields. One can refer to
[
18
]
and the references therein for more details. Volterra-type McKean-Vlasov SDEs first appeared in the paper
[
28
]
, which
studied the well-posedness and Pontryagin’s type maximum principle of a class
mean-field backward stochastic Volterra integral equations. Recently, there has been an growing interest in the
study of mean-field forward (backward) SDEs. In
[

, the authors extended

by considering the well-posedness and Pontryagin’s type

To the best of our knowledge, it seems that there are few works on forward Volterra type McKean-Valsov SDEs. For the regular kernel, Jie et al.
[
11

studied one-dimensional McKean–Vlasov stochastic Volterra
equations with Hölder diffusion coefficients. One can also refer Theorem

More precisely, we first consider the well-posedness and propagation of chaos for Volterra type McKean-Vlasov SDEs under Lipschitz condition in the sense of distribution measure. Very recently, we remark that Prömel and Scheffels

Preliminary

2.1
Notations

Throughout this paper, we denote
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
to be a given complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is a nondecreasing family of sub-
\sigma
fields of
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the usual conditions. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the space of all probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right):\mu\left(|\cdot|^{2}\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty\right\}.

It is well known that
\mathcal{P}_{2}
is a Polish space under the Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}|x-y|^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),

where
\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)
is the set of couplings for
\mu
and
\nu
; that is,
\pi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\pi\left(\cdot\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=\mu
and
\pi\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\cdot\right)=\nu
.

For a measurable function
K
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and measurable function
L
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the convolutions
K*L
and
L*K
are defined by

(K*L)(t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}K(t,u)L(u,s){\mathrm{d}}u,

(L*K)(t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}L(t,u)K(u,s){\mathrm{d}}u,

for almost all
(t,s)\in\Delta
, where
\Delta:=\{t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:s\leq t\}.

2.2
Growall’s inequality of Volterra type

We first recall the following lemma due to Girpenberg
[
9
]
(Theorem
1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K:\Delta\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a measurable function. Assume that for any
T>0
,

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\in L^{\infty}(0,T)

and

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\Big\|\int_{\cdot+\varepsilon}^{\cdot}K(\cdot+\varepsilon,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}<1.

Define

\displaystyle R_{1}(t,s):=K(t,s),\quad R_{n+1}(t,s):=\int_{s}^{t}K(t,u)R_{n}(u,s){\mathrm{d}}u,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.1)

Then for any
T>0
, there exist constants
C_{T}>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that

\Big\|\int_{0}^{\cdot}R_{n}(\cdot,s)ds\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C_{T}n\gamma^{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular, the series

\displaystyle R(t,s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}R_{n}(t,s)

(2.2)

converges for almost all
(t,s)\in\Delta
, and

\displaystyle R(t,s)-K(t,s)=(K*R)(t,s)-(R*K)(t,s)

(2.3)

and for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}R(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\in L^{\infty}(0,T).

(2.4)

The function
R
defined by (
2.2
) is called the resolvent of the kernel
K
. All the functions
K
in Lemma
2.1
will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}
. We also denote by
\mathcal{K}_{>1}
the set of all nonnegative measurable functions
K
on
\Delta
with the property that for any
T>0
and some
\beta>1
,

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}K^{\beta}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\in L^{\infty}(0,T).

It is clear that
\mathcal{K}_{>1}\subset\mathcal{K}.

We now present Gronwall’s lemma of Volterra type due to Zhang
[
35
]
(Lemma
2.2
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
and
R_{n}
and
R
be defined by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively. Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be two measurable functions satisfying that for any
T>0
and some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}R_{n}(t,s)f(s){\mathrm{d}}s\in L^{\infty}(0,T)

and for almost all
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\int_{0}^{t}R(t,s)g(s){\mathrm{d}}s<\infty.

If for almost all
t\in(0,\infty)
,

f(t)\leq g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)f(s){\mathrm{d}}s,

then for almost all
t\in(0,\infty)
,

f(t)\leq g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}R(t,s)g(s){\mathrm{d}}s.

3
Well-posedness for Volterra type McKean-Vlasov SDEs

3.1
Global existence and uniqueness

Consider the following Volterra type McKean-Vlasov SDEs:

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s},

(3.1)

where
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
b:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are Borel measurable functions, and
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an m-dimensional standard Brownian motion definied on the classical Wiener space.

Now, we introduce the hypothesis under which
we will be able to prove the global existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
3.1
).

\mathbf{(H1)}
Integrability condition:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\Big[K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)+K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s)\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T},\quad t\in[0,T].

where
K_{i},i=1,2,3,4
are from
\mathbf{(H2)}
and
\mathbf{(H3)}
below.

\mathbf{(H2)}
Lipschitz condition:
There exist positive functions
K_{1}(t,s)\in\mathcal{K}
and
K_{2}(t,s)\in\mathcal{K}
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t,s\in[0,T]

\displaystyle|b(t,s,x,\mu)-b(t,s,y,\nu)|

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}(t,s)\Big(|x-y|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\Big),

\displaystyle||\sigma(t,s,x,\mu)-\sigma(t,s,y,\nu)||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}(t,s)\Big(|x-y|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\Big).

\mathbf{(H3)}
Linear growth condition:
There exist positive functions
K_{3}(t,s)\in\mathcal{K}
and
K_{4}(t,s)\in\mathcal{K}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t,s\in[0,T]

\displaystyle|b(t,s,x,\mu)|

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}(t,s)\Big(1+|x|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})\Big),

\displaystyle||\sigma(t,s,x,\mu)||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{4}(t,s)\Big(1+|x|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})\Big).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
X_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},P)
and assumptions
\mathbf{(H1)}
-
\mathbf{(H3)}
hold, Then there exists a unique measurable
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted process
X_{t}
such that for almost all
t\geq 0
,

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s},t\in[0,T],P\text{-}a.s.,

and for any
T>0
some constant
C_{p,T}

\displaystyle E|X_{t}|^{p}\leq C_{p,T}(E|X_{0}|^{p}+1).

(3.2)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We utilize the successive approximation scheme to prove Theorem
3.1
. Define recursively
(X^{n})_{n\geq 1}
as follows:
X^{1}_{t}:=X_{0}
,
t\in[0,T]
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle X^{n+1}_{t}:=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s},t\in[0,T].

(3.3)

By Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathscr{L}_{X},\mathscr{L}_{Y})\leq[E|X-Y|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathbf{(H1)}
,
\mathbf{(H3)}
we have

\displaystyle E\Big(|X_{t}^{n+1}|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+3^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle+3^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+3^{p-1}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\big|1+|X^{n}_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\delta_{0})\big|{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}||\sigma(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(1+|X^{n}_{s}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle+C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{4}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(1+|X^{n}_{s}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot E(1+|X_{s}^{n}|^{p}){\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{4}(t,s)\cdot E(1+|X_{s}^{n}|^{p}){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{p,T}+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|X_{s}^{n}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|X_{s}^{n}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s.

(3.4)

Let

f_{m}(t):=\sup_{n=1,\cdots,m}E|X_{s}^{n}|^{p},

then we get

f_{m}(t)\leq C_{p,T}(E|X_{0}|^{p}+1)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{3,4}(t,s)\cdot f_{m}(s){\mathrm{d}}s,\quad\forall m\geq 1,

where
K_{3,4}:=C_{p,T}\cdot(K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s))
and the constant
C_{p,T}
is independent of
m
.

Let
R^{K_{3,4}}
be defined by (
2.2
) in terms of
K_{3,4}
. Note that by (
2.1
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and
\mathbf{(H1)}
, when
n=1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}R_{1}^{K_{3,4}}(t,s)f_{m}(s){\mathrm{d}}s=\int_{0}^{T}K_{3,4}(t,s)f_{m}(s){\mathrm{d}}s<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}R^{K_{3,4}}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{0}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}K_{3,4}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{0}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{s}^{t}R^{K_{3,4}}(t,u)K_{3,4}(u,s){\mathrm{d}}u\Big)\cdot E|X_{0}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}K_{3,4}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot E|X_{0}|^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}R^{K_{3,4}}(t,u)\Big(\int_{0}^{u}K_{3,4}(u,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big){\mathrm{d}}u\cdot E|X_{0}|^{p}<\infty.

Hence, Combining (
2.3
), (
2.4
),
\mathbf{(H1)}
and Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}(t)\leq C_{p,T}(E|X_{0}|^{p}+1)+\int_{0}^{t}R^{K_{3,4}}(t,s)\cdot(E|X_{0}|^{p}+1){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}(E|X_{0}|^{p}+1).

(3.5)

On the other hand, we set

Z_{n,m}(t):=X^{n}_{t}-X^{m}_{t}

and

f(t):=\limsup_{n,m\to\infty}E|Z_{n,m}(t)|^{p}.

Applying BDG’s inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1)}
and
\mathbf{(H2)}
we obtain

\displaystyle E|Z_{n+1,m+1}(t)|^{p}

\displaystyle=E|X^{n+1}_{t}-X^{m+1}_{t}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}|b(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})-b(t,s,X^{m}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})|{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big(\sigma(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})-\sigma(t,s,X^{m}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot\big(\big|X^{n}_{s}-X^{m}_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}||\sigma(t,s,X^{n}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}})-\sigma(t,s,X^{m}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})||^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(\big|X^{n}_{s}-X^{m}_{s}\big|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle+C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(\big|X^{n}_{s}-X^{m}_{s}\big|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{n}_{s}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{m}_{s}})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{n}-X_{s}^{m}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{n}-X_{s}^{m}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|Z_{n,m}(t)|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s.

By (
3.1
),
\mathbf{(H1)}
and applying Fatou’s lemma, we deduce that

f(t)\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot f(s){\mathrm{d}}s.

Using Gronwall’s inequality of Volterra type Lemma
2.2
, we get for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

f(t)=\limsup_{n,m\to\infty}E|Z_{n,m}(t)|^{p}=0.

Hence, there exists a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted process
X_{t}
such that for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}E|X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}|^{p}=0.

Now taking limits for (
3.3
) gives the existence. The uniqueness follows from a similar discussion.
∎

3.2
Path continuity of solutions

In this subsection, in order to study path continuity of solution to (
3.1
), in addition to
(\mathbf{H2})
-
(\mathbf{H3})
, we also assume that:

\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}

There exists a positive constant
\gamma>0
such that for any
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]

\int_{0}^{t}|K_{i}(t^{\prime},s)-K_{i}(t,s)|{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma},\quad t\in[0,T],

\int_{0}^{t}|K_{j}(t^{\prime},s)-K_{j}(t,s)|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\gamma},\quad t\in[0,T],

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}|K_{i}(t,s)|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\gamma},\quad t\in[0,T],

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}|K_{j}(t,s)|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\gamma},\quad t\in[0,T].

where
i=1,3,j=2,4
.

\mathbf{(H4)}
For all
t,t^{\prime},s\in[0,T]
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|b(t^{\prime},s,x,\mu)-b(t,s,x,\mu)|

\displaystyle\leq F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s)\Big(1+|x|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})\Big),

\displaystyle||\sigma(t,s,x,\mu)-\sigma(t,s,y,\mu)||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s)\Big(1+|x|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})\Big),

where
F_{i}(t^{\prime},t,s),i=1,2,
are positive function on
[0,T]\times[0,T]\times[0,T]
, and satisfy for some
\gamma>0

\int_{0}^{t}F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma},\quad\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\leq C_{T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2\gamma}.

Remark 3.1

It is clear that
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
implies
K_{k}\in\mathcal{K},k=1,2,3,4

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}K_{k}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\in L^{\infty}(0,T)

Remark 3.2
.

Assume
K_{1}=K_{2}=K_{3}=K_{4}=K
, let us list some examples of kernels that satisfy
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
:

\bullet
fractional Brownian motion kernel. The fractional Brownian motion has the integral representation in law:

\displaystyle B_{t}^{H}=\int_{0}^{1}K^{H}(t,s)dW_{s},

where
W
is a standard Brownian motion and
K^{H}
is the square integral kernel:

\displaystyle K^{H}(t,s)=K(t,s)=c_{H}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}+c_{H}(\frac{1}{2}-H)\int_{s}^{t}(\theta-s)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1-(\frac{s}{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\Big)\mathrm{d}\theta,

with

c_{H}=\Big(\frac{2H{\Gamma}(\frac{3}{2}-H)}{{\Gamma}(H+\frac{1}{2}){\Gamma}(2-2H)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It was proved in
[
3
]
that for singular case
(H<\frac{1}{2})
and regular case
(H>\frac{1}{2})
we have
(\alpha=|H-\frac{1}{2}|)

\displaystyle\text{Singular case}\quad\big|\frac{\partial K^{H}}{\partial t}(t,s)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}\ \text{and}\int_{s}^{t}K^{H}(t,u)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{1-2\alpha},\ s<t.

and

\displaystyle\text{Regular case}\quad\big|\frac{\partial K^{H}}{\partial t}(t,s)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{-\alpha}\ \text{and}\int_{s}^{t}K^{H}(t,u)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\leq C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{1+2\alpha},\ s<t.

So for the singular case and regular case we have that
(t<t^{\prime})

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}(K^{H}(t,s))^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}-t)^{1-2\alpha}=C_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}-t)^{2H},

and

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}(K^{H}(t,s))^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}-t)^{1+2\alpha}=C_{\alpha}(t^{\prime}-t)^{2H}.

Moreover, it was proved in
[
34
]
that for
H\in(0,1)

\int_{0}^{t}\big|K^{H}(t^{\prime},s)-K^{H}(t,s)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq|t^{\prime}-t|^{2H}.

Therefore, it is clear to verify
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
hold with
\gamma=2H
.

\bullet
Convolutional kernel, for the details see Example
2.3

1
]

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
X_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},P)
,
p>2\vee\frac{2}{\gamma}
and assumptions
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
,
\mathbf{(H2)}
-
\mathbf{(H4)}
hold. Then
t\mapsto X(t)
admits a
{\alpha}
-order H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder continuous modification for any
\delta\in[0,\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
.

Proof.

For any
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq T
, we first rewrite
X_{t^{\prime}}-X_{t}
as

\displaystyle X_{t^{\prime}}-X_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}b(t^{\prime},t,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(b(t^{\prime},s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})-b(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\sigma(t^{\prime},t,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\sigma(t^{\prime},s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})-\sigma(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(t^{\prime},t)+I_{2}(t^{\prime},t)+I_{3}(t^{\prime},t)+I_{4}(t^{\prime},t).

As for the term
I_{1}(t^{\prime},t)
, applying H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
,
\mathbf{(H3)}
and (
3.2

\displaystyle E|I_{1}(t^{\prime},t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq E\Big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq E\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot\Big|1+|X_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{3}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma(p-1)}\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot\big(1+E|X_{s}|^{p}\big){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\big(1+E|X_{0}|^{p}\big)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma p}.

As for the term
I_{2}(t^{\prime},t)
, by H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
,
\mathbf{(H4)}
and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle E|I_{2}(t^{\prime},t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq E\Big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq E\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\Big|1+|X_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma(p-1)}\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}F_{1}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\big(1+E|X_{s}|^{p}\big){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\big(1+E|X_{0}|^{p}\big)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma p}.

As for the term
I_{3}(t^{\prime},t)
, by BDG’s inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
,
\mathbf{(H3)}
and (
3.2
), for
p>2
we have

\displaystyle E|I_{3}(t^{\prime},t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big(\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\|\sigma(t^{\prime},s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big(\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{4}(t^{\prime},s)\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{4}(t^{\prime},s)\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big)^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{4}(t^{\prime},s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}|t-t^{\prime}|^{p-2}\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}K_{4}(t^{\prime},s)\cdot\Big(1+E|X_{s}|^{p}\Big){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\big(1+E|X_{0}|^{p}\big)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\gamma p}.

As for the term
I_{4}(t^{\prime},t)
, by BDG’s inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}

1.30
in
[
14
]
),
\mathbf{(H4)}
and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle E|I_{4}(t^{\prime},t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(t^{\prime},s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})-\sigma(t,s,X_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}})\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\big(E\big[1+|X_{s}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}},\delta_{0})\big]\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s)\cdot\big(1+(E|X_{s}|^{p})^{\frac{2}{p}}\big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\big(1+E|X_{0}|^{p}\big)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t^{\prime},t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

Hence, for all

Existence of a continuous version as well as the bouud now follows from the Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, see Theorem I.
2.1
in

]
.

4
Propagation of chaos

In this section, let us now introduce the interacting particle system of (
3.1
) in order to study propagation of chaos property (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
). More precisely, we consider the following interacting particle system with singular kernels:

\displaystyle X_{t}^{i,N}=X_{0}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{i,N},\mu^{X,N}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i,N},\mu^{X,N}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s},

(4.1)

almost surely, where the empirical measures for any
t\in[0,T]
is defined by

\mu^{X,N}_{t}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i,N}}(\cdot),

and
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure at point
x
. Also, we consider the following Volterra system of non-interacting particles,

\displaystyle X_{t}^{i}=X_{0}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s},

(4.2)

almost surely for any
t\in[0,T]
and
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
X_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},P)
with some
q>p
and assumptions
\mathbf{(H1)}
-
\mathbf{(H3)}
hold, then there exists a constant depending only on
q,T,X_{0}^{i}
such that for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

E|X^{i}_{t}|^{q}\leq C_{q,T}\big(E|X_{0}|^{q}+1\big).

Proof.

Applying BDG’s inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1)}
and
\mathbf{(H3)}
yields

\displaystyle E|X_{t}^{i}|^{q}

\displaystyle=E\Big|X_{0}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{q-1}E|X_{0}^{i}|^{q}+3^{q-1}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{q}+3^{q-1}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{q-1}E|X_{0}^{i}|^{q}+3^{q-1}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot(1+|X_{s}^{i}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\delta_{0})){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{q}+C_{q}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{q-1}E|X^{i}_{0}|^{q}+C_{q}\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(1+|X^{i}_{s}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{q}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{q-1}

\displaystyle+C_{q}\int_{0}^{t}K_{4}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(1+|X^{i}_{s}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{q}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}},\delta_{0})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{q-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{3}(t,s)\cdot E(1+|X_{s}^{i}|^{q}){\mathrm{d}}s+C_{q,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{4}(t,s)\cdot E(1+|X_{s}^{i}|^{q}){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq 3^{q-1}E|X_{0}|^{p}+C_{q,T}+C_{q,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i}|^{q}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{q,T}\big(E|X_{0}|^{q}+1\big)+C_{q,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{3}(t,s)+K_{4}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i}|^{q}{\mathrm{d}}s,

then by Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\displaystyle E|X^{i}_{t}|^{q}\leq C_{q,T}\big(E|X_{0}|^{q}+1\big).

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
X_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},P)
with some
q>p
and assumptions
\mathbf{(H1)}
-
\mathbf{(H3)}
hold, then there exists a constant C depending only on
d,p,q,T,X_{0}^{i}
such that for all
N\geq 1
,

\sup_{i=1,\cdots,N}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E|X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{i}|^{p}\leq C_{p,d,q,T,X_{0}^{i}}\times\begin{cases}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p>\frac{d}{2}\ \text{and}\ q\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N)+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p=\frac{d}{2}\ \text{and}\ q\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{p}{d}}+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in(0,\frac{d}{2})\ \text{and}\ q\neq\frac{d}{d-p},\end{cases}

Proof.

By BDG’s inequality, H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1)}
and
\mathbf{(H2)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E|X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{i}|^{p}

\displaystyle=E\big|\int_{0}^{t}\big(b(t,s,X_{s}^{i,N},\mu^{X,N}_{s})-b(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})\big){\mathrm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i,N},\mu^{X,N}_{s})-\sigma(t,s,X_{s}^{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})\big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}|b(t,s,X^{i,N}_{s},\mu^{X,N}_{s})-b(t,s,X^{i}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})|{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle+2^{p-1}E\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big(\sigma(t,s,X^{i,N}_{s},\mu^{X,N}_{s})-\sigma(t,s,X^{i}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\big(\big|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{i}_{s}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p}E\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\sigma(t,s,X^{i,N}_{s},\mu^{X,N}_{s})-\sigma(t,s,X^{i}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\big\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(\big|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{i}_{s}\big|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{p-1}

\displaystyle+C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E\Big(\big|X^{i,N}_{s}-X^{i}_{s}\big|^{p}+\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big){\mathrm{d}}s\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathbb{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{2}+2\mathbb{W}^{2}_{2}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C_{p}\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}}).

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C_{p}E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}}).

As a consequence, by extended Minkowski’s inequality and H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle E|X_{t}^{i,N}-X_{t}^{i}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\mu^{X,N}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot\Big[E\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(E|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{2}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\Big]{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E|X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{i}|^{p}{\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p,T}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(K_{1}(t,s)+K_{2}(t,s)\Big)\cdot E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{p}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}}){\mathrm{d}}s,

then applying Theorem
1
of
[
8
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle E\mathbb{W}^{p}_{p}(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{i}_{s}})\leq C_{p,d,q}M_{q}^{\frac{q}{p}}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})\times\begin{cases}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p>\frac{d}{2}\ \text{and}\ q\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N)+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p=\frac{d}{2}\ \text{and}\ q\neq 2p,\\
N^{-\frac{p}{d}}+N^{-\frac{p-q}{q}}&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in(0,\frac{d}{2})\ \text{and}\ q\neq\frac{d}{d-p},\end{cases}

where
M_{q}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})
is defined by

M_{q}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|X_{s}^{i}|^{q}\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}}({\mathrm{d}}X_{s}^{i}).

By Lemma
4.1
,

, using Lemma

5.1
Discretized scheme for interacting particle system

In this subsection, we construct a Euler schemes for the interacting particle system (

) associated with (
4.2
). When the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
are independent of the law of
X_{t}
, Zhang
[

]
proposed Euler schemes for classsical stochastic Volterra equations with singular kernels, where the author made a delicate design to avoid touching the singular points. We now propose the Euler schemes for the interacting particle system (

) associated with (

). Without loss of generality, we assume

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s,\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\Big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s,\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\big\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)\cdot\Big(|X_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n}-X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{X,N,n}_{s,\xi},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\Big)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E|X_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n}-X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{p}+E\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n}-X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big){\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})|s-s_{n}|^{\theta p}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-n\theta p},

where the last step we have used Lemma
5.2
.

For the term
\Pi_{7}
, by Lemma
5.1
, BDG’s inequality, extended Minkowski’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
and
\mathbf{(H4^{\prime})}
, we have

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{7}|^{p}

\displaystyle=E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\Big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\big\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\sigma^{i,N,n}(t,s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\big\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t,\tilde{t},s)\cdot\big(1+|X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi},\delta_{0})\big){\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t,\tilde{t},s)\cdot\big(E\big[1+|X^{i,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi}|^{p}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{i,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi}},\delta_{0})\big]\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}{\mathrm{d}}s\Bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{2}(t,\tilde{t},s){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})|t^{\prime}-t|^{\frac{\gamma p}{2}}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-\frac{n\gamma p}{2}}.

For the term
\Pi_{8}
, by Lemma
5.1
, BDG’s inequality, extended Minkowski’s inequality,
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime})}
and
\mathbf{(H5)}
, we have

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{8}|^{p}

\displaystyle=E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s_{n},X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\Big){\mathrm{d}}W_{s}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s,X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})-\sigma^{i,N,n}(\tilde{t},s_{n},X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n},\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi})\big\|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\Big|\int_{0}^{t}F_{4}(\tilde{t},s,s_{n})\cdot\Big(1+|X_{s_{n},\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{X,N,n}_{s_{n},\xi},\delta_{0})\Big)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}s\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\Big(\int_{0}^{t}F_{4}(\tilde{t},s,s_{n}){\mathrm{d}}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-\frac{n\delta p}{2}}.

\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2},\Pi_{3},\Pi_{4}
can be similarly dealt with, i.e.

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{1}|^{p}\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E|Z_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n}|^{p}+E\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Z^{i,N,n}_{s,\xi}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big){\mathrm{d}}s,

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{2}|^{p}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-n\theta p},

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{3}|^{p}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-n\gamma p},

\displaystyle E|\Pi_{4}|^{p}\leq C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-n\delta p}.

Therefore, if we define

g(t):=\sup_{i=1,\cdots,N}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}EZ_{s,\xi}^{i,N,n},

then we easily obtain

g(t)\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}g(s){\mathrm{d}}s+C_{p}(1+|\xi|^{p})\cdot 2^{-n\eta p},

where
\eta>0
depends only
{\alpha},\gamma,\delta
. Gronwall’s inequality gives the desired result.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{(H1^{\prime\prime})}
,
\mathbf{(H2)}
,
\mathbf{(H3)}
,
\mathbf{(H4^{\prime})}
and
\mathbf{(H5)}
hold, there exists
\lambda>0
such that for any
R>0
and
p>2
sufficiently large,

E\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T],|\xi|\leq R}\big|X_{t,\xi}^{i,N}-X_{t,\xi}^{i,N,n}\big|^{p}\Big)\leq E|A(\omega)|^{p}2^{-np\lambda}.

In particular,

P\Big\{\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T],|\xi|\leq R}\big|X_{t,\xi}^{i,N}-X_{t,\xi}^{i,N,n}\big|=0\Big\}=1.

Proof.

One constructs the following process:

Z(r,t,\xi)=\begin{cases}X^{i,N}_{t,\xi},\ r=0,\\
Z^{i,N,n}_{t,\xi}+2^{n+1}(r-2^{-n})\big(X^{i,N,n+1}_{t,\xi}-X^{i,N,n}_{t,\xi}\big),\ 2^{-(n+1)}<r\leq 2^{-n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Fix
R>0
, by Lemmas
5.1
,
5.2
and Theorem
5.1
, there exists a constant
\beta:=\beta(\gamma)>0
such that for all
p>2
,
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
,
\xi,\eta\in D_{R}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},|\xi|\leq R\}
, we have

E\Big|Z(r,t,\xi)-Z(r^{\prime},t^{\prime},\eta)\Big|^{p}\leq C_{p,T,R,L_{3},L_{4}}\Big(|r-r^{\prime}|^{\beta p}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\beta p}+|\xi-\eta|^{\beta p}\Big).

Thus, by Kolmogorov’s continuity criterium, there is a
p
-order integrable random variable
A(\omega)
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T],|\xi|\leq R}\Big|Z(r,t,\xi)-Z(r^{\prime},t,\xi)\Big|\leq A(\omega)|r-r^{\prime}|^{\lambda},\quad a.s.,

where
\lambda\in(0,\beta-\frac{d+2}{p})
. In particular, taking
r=0,r^{\prime}=2^{-n}
, we have

E\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T],|\xi|\leq R}\big|X_{t,\xi}^{i,N}-X_{t,\xi}^{i,N,n}\big|\Big)^{p}\leq E|A(\omega)|^{p}2^{-np\lambda},

which yields the desired convergence. Borel-Cantelli’s Lemma gives the second conclusion.
∎

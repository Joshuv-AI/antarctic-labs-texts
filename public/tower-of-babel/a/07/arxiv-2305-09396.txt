On Chern classes of Lagrangian fibered hyper-Kähler manifolds
Claire Voisin

Abstract
We study the rank stratification for the differential of a Lagrangian fibration over a smooth basis. We also introduce and study the notion of Lagrangian morphism of vector bundles. As a consequence, we prove some of the vanishing, in the Chow groups of a Lagrangian fibered hyper-Kähler variety X X , of certain polynomials in the Chern classes of X X and the Lagrangian divisor, predicted by the Beauville-Voisin conjecture. Under some natural assumptions on the dimensions of the rank strata, we also establish nonnegativity and positivity results for Chern classes.

Introduction

In the paper
[
4
]
, Beauville and myself proved that for any projective

1.

For any elements

c_{2}(S)=24o_{S}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
.

Although we can work with integral coefficients in the statement above,

will denote from now on the Chow groups of a variety

with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients.
Beauville subsequently proposed in
[

a generalization of property

to any projective hyper-Kähler manifold

, whose weak version is called “Beauville weak splitting conjecture”, stating that any cycle

on

which is a polynomial in divisor classes and is cohomologous to

is rationally equivalent to

modulo torsion. I proposed in turn in
[
41

to generalize the combination of

and
2
to any projective hyper-Kähler manifold

in the following form:

Conjecture 1.1

Let

be a hyper-Kähler manifold and let

be a cycle on

This conjecture is known to hold for Hilbert schemes of
K3

[
32
]
and for generalized Kummer varieties by
[
17
]
. It is also proved in
[
41
]
for the Fano variety of lines in a smooth cubic fourfold.
Riess proved in
[
37
]
a very nice result concerning the weak splitting conjecture. We know the polynomial cohomological relations between divisor classes on a hyper-Kähler manifold

, or equivalently

, where

is the Beauville-Bogomolov quadratic form, the ideal of these relations is generated by

\displaystyle l^{n+1}=0\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{2n+2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\,\,{\rm if}\,\,q(l)=0.

(1)

(In the absence of a rational isotropic class, the relations do not admit such a concrete description and only have a representation-theoretic characterization, unless we pass to real coefficients.) Riess noticed that the relation (
1
) obviously holds in
{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X)
when

and

is a Lagrangian line bundle, that is, there exist a Lagrangian fibration

f:X\rightarrow B
, an integer

\displaystyle L^{n+1}=0\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X).

(2)

If an isotropic line bundle

is in particular nef. The SYZ conjecture states conversely that a nef isotropic line bundle on a hyper-Kähler manifold is Lagrangian. When the Picard number of

is

has an isotropic class,

has two isotropic classes (up to a scalar), and there are many instances when only one class is nef, the other ray of the positive cone not even belonging to the birational Kähler cone (see for example

on the existence of self-correspondences inducing automorphisms of the ring

, could nevertheless extend to the nonnef ray the relation (

) and finally prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Riess
[
37
]
) If

is a projective hyper-Kähler manifold which has an isotropic class in

and whose deformations satisfy the SYZ conjecture,

satisfies Beauville’s weak splitting conjecture.

One of the purposes in this paper is to study Conjecture
1.1
for a certain type of polynomial cohomological relations involving both Chern classes of

and divisor classes.
More precisely, as proved in

, the following generalized Verbitsky relations hold in

\displaystyle l^{n+1-i}c_{I}=0\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{2n+2-2i+4j}(X,\mathbb{Q})\,\,{\rm if}\,\,q(l)=0\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,{\rm deg}\,c_{I}=4j\geq 4i,

where

is any polynomial in the topological Chern classes

(2,0)
-form on a deformation

Remark 1.3
.

The odd Chern classes of a hyper-Kähler manifold

vanish in
{\rm CH}(X)
since its cotangent bundle is isomorphic to its dual. This is why we discuss only even Chern classes.

Remark 1.4
.

The relations (
3

4n

Conjecture

Conjecture 1.5

Let

\displaystyle L^{n-i+1}c_{I}=0\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,{\rm CH}(X).

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow f_{b}^{*}\Omega_{B,b}\rightarrow\Omega_{X\mid X_{b}}\rightarrow\Omega_{X_{b}}\rightarrow 0,

Note that we have more generally an exact sequence

Assume

Lagrangian morphisms of vector bundles

We study in this section general properties of Lagrangian morphisms of vector bundles introduced in Definition
1.10
. We do not know if the notion has been classically studied.
Our motivation for introducing this notion and its relevance for the subject of this paper come from the following

Example 3.1
.

Let
X
be hyper-Kähler and let
f:X\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Then denoting by
B^{\prime}\subset B
the smooth locus of

and
X^{\prime}\subset X
its inverse image in
X
, the morphism

\displaystyle\phi:=f^{*}:f^{*}\Omega_{B^{\prime}}\rightarrow\Omega_{X^{\prime}}

(17)

is Lagrangian on
X^{\prime}
.

Note that it is conjectured that, if

is normal (which we will always assume)

is smooth (and if it is smooth, it is known to be isomorphic to

by
[
22
]
). In any case, as
B
is normal, the codimension of
B\setminus B^{\prime}
in
B
is at least
2
, while the discriminant locus, over which
\phi
is not everywhere of maximal rank, has codimension
1
in
B
, so that the notion is already interesting over

.

To study the rank loci for a general Lagrangian morphism, we consider the universal situation, where the basis is the set

which are Lagrangian, in the sense that the morphism
M:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}

Lemma 3.2

(i) The subvariety

is an irreducible, local complete intersection subvariety of codimension

, hence of dimension
2n^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.

(ii)
M_{\rm lag}
is smooth at a matrix

which is of rank
\geq n-1
, but it is singular at a matrix which is of rank
\leq n-2
. Along the set
M_{\rm lag,n-2}
of matrices of rank

has an ordinary quadratic singularity of codimension

(iii) The locus

of isotropic matrices of rank

. In particular, the locus

is smooth of codimension

Proof.

(i) Denoting

M_{\rm lag}
in
M_{2n,n}
are

\displaystyle\langle m_{k},\,m_{l}\rangle=0

(18)

for
n\geq l>k\geq 1
. This locus is thus defined by

equations. In order to prove that

is a local complete intersection, it suffices to show that its dimension is

, which is done by proving (iii) since

is stratified by the rank

of the matrix. The locus of rank

isotropic matrices is homogeneous under the symplectic group

and of dimension

(n-i)(n+i)-(n-i)(n-i-1)/2+n(n-i)=2n^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-\frac{3i^{2}+i}{2},

since a rank
n-i

determines its image

which is an isotropic vector subspace of

of dimension
n-i
, and a matrix

of size

and rank

. This dimension count proves the codimension statement in (iii). The irreducibility follows from the above dimension count and the fact that the rank

stratum is homogeneous, hence irreducible.

(ii) and (iii) Denote by

the entries of a matrix

, where
i\in\{1,\ldots,2n\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and by

its

-th column vector. Let

be any Lagrangian matrix. As the image of

is isotropic, we can assume by choosing an adequate basis of

in which the intersection form

is the standard one,
that for

, the vector

is the basis vector

of

, and that it is

for

.
For a matrix

and the vector

vanishes at the point

.
In the coordinates

on

centered at

\displaystyle h_{k+n,l}-h_{l+n,k}+\langle h_{k},\,h_{l}\rangle=0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,k,\,l\leq{\rm rk}\,M

(19)

\displaystyle h_{l,k+n}+\langle h_{k},\,h_{l}\rangle=0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,k\leq{\rm rk}\,M,\,l>{\rm rk}\,M

(20)

\displaystyle\langle h_{k},\,h_{l}\rangle=0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,k>{\rm rk}\,M,\,l>{\rm rk}\,M.

where the terms

When the rank of

∎

Lemma 3.7

Let

be a smooth variety and

The obvious case is when

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow M\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}\rightarrow 0.

The case of Lagrangian fibrations

3.2
Non-reduced fibers in codimension 1

We show in this section how to get rid of the assumption that there are no non-reduced fibers in codimension
1
. Our goal is to show how, in the case of a Lagrangian fibration of a hyper-Kähler manifold of Picard number
2
, we can modify the morphism (
17
) over the complement of a closed algebraic subset of codimension
\geq 2
of the base so as to get a Lagrangian morphism of vector bundles having the property that its locus
X_{n-1}
of jumping rank has codimension at least
2
, and to which the arguments of the previous section apply.
Note that the existence of a divisor in the base parameterizing non-reduced fibers can occur, as in the following

Example 3.12
.

Consider an elliptic
K3
surface
f:S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with elliptic general fiber
E_{t},\,t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then for any
n\geq 2
, we have a composed morphism

\displaystyle f_{n}:S^{[n]}\rightarrow S^{(n)}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(n)}=\mathbb{P}^{n},

(28)

which gives a Lagrangian fibration with non-reduced fibers over the big diagonal of
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(n)}
. More precisely, for
n=2
, the fiber of
f_{2}
over
t+t^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(2)},\,t\not=t^{\prime}
, is isomorphic to
E_{t}\times E_{t^{\prime}}
, and for
t=t^{\prime}
, the fiber is as a set the union of
E_{t}^{(2)}
and another component which is also a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
E_{t}
, namely the set of length
2
subschemes of
S
supported at one point of
E_{t}
. The first component is not reduced since near a general point
(x,y),\,x\not=y
of
E_{t}^{(2)}
, the morphism
f_{2}
locally factors through the morphism
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(2)}
which ramifies over the diagonal. The second component is reduced because the morphism
f_{2}:S^{[2]}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(2)}
is of rank
2
at a general point of the divisor parameterizing nonreduced schemes of length
2
. Indeed, considering a general curve
C\subset S
, the morphism
f_{2\mid C^{(2)}}:C^{(2)}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(2)}
is a local analytic isomorphism at a point
2c\in C^{(2)}
, once
f_{\mid C}:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a local analytic isomorphism at
c
.

In this example, there are non-reduced fibers in codimension
1
which are not multiple fibers. This however cannot occur when
X
has Picard number
2
by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration with
X
projective of Picard number
2
. Then the general non-reduced fibers appearing in codimension
1
are multiple fibers.

Proof.

As
B
is normal, hence smooth in codimension
1
, the general non-reduced fibers appearing in codimension
1
appear over the smooth locus
B^{\prime}
, over which the Lagrangian fibration is flat by Matsushita’s theorem
[
31
]
. Assume that over a generic point
t\in B^{1}\subset B^{\prime}
of a divisor in
B^{\prime}
, the fiber
X_{t}
is not reduced but not a multiple fiber. This implies that it has several irreducible components
X_{t,l}
appearing with different multiplicities
l
. The inverse image
D:=f^{-1}(B^{1})
thus has several irreducible components
D_{k}
, where
D_{k}
is defined as the union of components of the general fiber of
f
over
B^{1}
with multiplicity
k
. As the fiber
X_{t}
is a local complete intersection, its irreducible components meet in codimension
1
and as it is connected, it follows that
D_{k}
is effective and non-trivial when restricted to a component
X_{t,l}
for some
l\not=k
. As the divisor
D_{k}
is trivial on the general fiber
X_{t^{\prime}},\,t^{\prime}\in B
, we conclude that
\rho(X)\geq 3
, since
{\rm Pic}(X)
already contains the Lagrangian line bundle, which is trivial on
X_{t,l}
and
X_{t^{\prime}}
, and an ample divisor, which is nontrivial on both
X_{t,l}
and
X_{t^{\prime}}
. This contradiction concludes the proof.
∎

Recall that, by Matsushita
[
30
]
, the Lagrangian fibrations deform over a codimension
1
locally closed analytic subspace of the Kuranishi family of
X
, so that, for the general projective deformation
(X_{t},f_{t})
of
(X,f)
,
X_{t}
has Picard number
2
and Lemma
3.13
applies.
We now consider the case of a Lagrangian fibration
f:X\rightarrow B

\geq n-1

4

We study in this section the stratification by the rank of a Lagrangian fibration with smooth base.
Let
f:X\rightarrow B

Lemma 4.1
.

The relative dimension of

over

Proof.

Let

and let

. Let

be

are independent near

defined on

by the formula

\chi_{i}\lrcorner\sigma_{X}=f^{*}df_{i}

are independent near

and commute, since the fibration

of

and a free action

) and for any

. As these orbits are contained in the fibers of

, the lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 4.2

In the present situation, we have

For each connected component

Proof of Theorem

We just have to check that the assumptions of Proposition

be a smooth cubic fourfold. The base of the associated LSV manifold is the dual 5-dimensional projective space

is the dual of the cubic

of the compactified intermediate Jacobian fibration is the same as the generic behaviour, along the discriminant divisor, of the family of intermediate Jacobians of all cubic threefolds. This is studied in

[

. However, since we are considering a Lagrangian family of abelian fourfolds, it is observed in
[

that the infinitesimal condition

By this argument, if condition

, these abelian fourfolds are the Jacobians of complete intersection curves of type

Theorem 5.7

which proves Theorem

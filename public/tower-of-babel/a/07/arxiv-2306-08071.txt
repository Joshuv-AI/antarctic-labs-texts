Some combinatorial interpretations of the Macdonald identities for affine root systems and Nekrasov–Okounkov type formulas
David Wahiche

Abstract
. We explore some connections between vectors of integers and integer partitions seen as bi-infinite words. This methodology enables us on the one hand to obtain enumerations connecting products of hook lengths and vectors of integers. This yields on the other hand a combinatorial interpretation of the Macdonald identities for affine root systems of the 7 7 infinite families in terms of Schur functions, symplectic and special orthogonal Schur functions. From these results, we are able to derive q q -Nekrasov–Okounkov formulas associated to each type. The latter for limit cases of q q yield Nekrasov–Okounkov type formulas corresponding to all the specializations given by Macdonald. Keywords. Integer partitions, Macdonald identities for affine root systems, Littlewood decomposition, Nekrasov–Okounkov formulas.

Formulas involving hook lengths abound in combinatorics and representation theory. One illustrative example is the hook-length formula discovered in 1954 by Frame, Robinson and Thrall

(1.1)

f^{\lambda}=\frac{n!}{\displaystyle\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}h}\cdot

Hook lengths formulas such as (

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}T^{\lvert\lambda\rvert}\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}\left(1-\frac{z}{h^{2}}\right)=\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(1-T^{k}\right)^{z-1},

This identity was later obtained independently by Han

Let

(1.11)

If one thinks of the Schur polynomials as invariant by the action of any element in

5.
Macdonald identities for affine root systems,
q
-Nekrasov–Okounkov formulas and applications

Motivated by the computations from Section
3
, the last section detailed some enumerative results about subsets of partitions linked to the quadratic forms that appear in the Macdonald identities of each infinite affine root system. In this section, the goal is to show how these enumerations enable one to transcript the Macdonald identities within the language of integer partitions and Schur functions. The
V_{g,t}
-codings introduced in Definition
4.2
(that are some specific decreasingly ordered vectors of integers) are going to be the key tool for this purpose. These are heavily linked to the bijection
\sigma:\{1,\dots,g\}\rightarrow\{0,\dots,g-1\}
involved in their definition and which can be thought of as a shifted permutation
\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{g}
, i.e.
\sigma^{\prime}(i):=g-\sigma(i)
. In Section
5.1
, we detail how the sign of these
\sigma
, denoted by
{\rm sgn}(\sigma):=-{\rm sgn}(\sigma^{\prime})
and which can be thought of as a signature of a permutation sorting in decreasing order, is characterized by both the cardinal of some subsets of hook lengths of the partitions introduced in Section
4.1
and some statistics attached to the partitions (such as
\Delta
or the size of the Durfee square).

The purpose of Section
5.2
is then to provide the desired rewriting of Macdonald identities for all infinite affine types in the language of partition. These formulas are of type “sum = product”, and recall that in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), the sum is computed over
\mathbf{m}
in some subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
without any order on its components. However, partitions come with an order, hence when sending the vector
\mathbf{m}
to the vector
\mathbf{r}
from Theorems
1.2
-
1.3
and the theorems from Section
4.2
we have chosen a specific order: that is the translation by a constant of a
V_{g,t}
-coding. We apply this formalism to rewrite the sum part of Macdonald identities for all
7
infinite root systems as a sum over Schur functions, and over symplectic, special orthogonal and even orthogonal Schur functions.

5.1.

5.3.
q

In this section, we combine the results obtained in Sections
4.2
and
5.2
to derive
q
-Nekrasov–Okounkov type formulas for all infinite types except for the type
\tilde{A}
already given in (
1.4

(1)

Specialize all the Macdonald identities stated in Section
5.2
either taking
x_{i}=q^{i}
or
x_{i}=q^{2i-1}
. Then

(a)

reinterpret the specialized symplectic, special orthogonal Schur functions as a product over hook lengths using one of the theorems in Section
4.2
with an appropriate choice of the function
\tau
on the sum part,

(b)

and rewrite the products with some technical lemmas.

(2)

Then setting
u=q^{g}
or
u=q^{g/2}
, verify that both sides of the equalities derived at the end of the previous step can be seen as Laurent polynomials in
u
.

For instance, to derive Theorem
1.6
, we will use a polynomiality argument by remarking that for any positive integer
n
, the coefficients of
T^{n}
on both sides of (
1.23
) can be seen as Laurent polynomials in
u
with coefficient in
\mathbb{C}(q)
. To see that, note that for any
\lambda\in\mathcal{DD}
we have:

\prod_{s\in\lambda}\frac{1-u^{-2\varepsilon_{s}}q^{h_{s}}}{1-q^{h_{s}}}\prod_{s\in\Delta}\frac{1+uq^{h_{s}/2}}{1+u^{-1}q^{h_{s}/2}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\lambda\\
s\notin\Delta\end{subarray}}\frac{1-u^{-2\varepsilon_{s}}q^{h_{s}}}{1-q^{h_{s}}}\prod_{s\in\Delta}\frac{(1+uq^{h_{s}/2})(1+u^{-1}q^{h_{s}/2})}{1-q^{h_{s}}},

therefore the left-hand side belongs to
\mathbb{C}(q)[u;u^{-1}]
. By expanding the quotient on the right-hand side of (
1.23
) and extracting the coefficients in
T^{n}
, we obtain the Laurent polynomiality in
u
of the coefficients. Hence it is enough to prove that both sides coincide at
u=q^{t}
for infinitely many positive integers
t
.

The following Lemma is the key ingredient in step
(1).(b)
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
x
be a formal variable and
t
a positive integer. Then we have:

(5.23)

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-q^{i}x\right)=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{r}x}{1-q^{r+t}x},

(5.24)

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-q^{-i}x\right)=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{r-1-t}x}{1-q^{r-1}x},

(5.25)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\left(1-q^{i+j}x\right)=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left(1-q^{r+2}x\right)^{\lfloor(r+1)/2\rfloor}\left(1-q^{r+1+2t}x\right)^{r-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}{\left(1-q^{r+1+t}x\right)^{r}},

(5.26)

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\left(1-q^{-i-j}x\right)=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left(1-q^{r-1}x\right)^{\lfloor(r+1)/2\rfloor}\left(1-q^{r-2t}x\right)^{r-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}{\left(1-q^{r-1-t}x\right)^{r}},

and

(5.27)

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\left(1-q^{j-i}x\right)\left(1-q^{i-j}x\right)=\frac{1}{\left(1-x\right)^{t-1}}\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left(1-q^{r-t}x\right)^{r}\left(1-q^{r+t}x\right)^{r}}{\left(1-q^{r+1}x\right)^{r}\left(1-q^{r-1}x\right)^{r}}.

Proof.

Equalities (
5.23
) and (
5.24
) are immediately derived noting that their right-hand sides are telescopic products.
In order to prove (
5.25
), we will need to gather
i+j
by values. Hence if we set
3\leq k\leq 2t-1
, we need to count how many
(i,j)\in\{1,\dots,t\}^{2}
such that
i<j
and
i+j=k
there are. For
i+j=k
, we have:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\begin{cases}1\leq i\leq t-1\\
i+1\leq j\leq t\end{cases}&\iff&\begin{cases}1\leq i\leq t-1\\
2i+1\leq k\leq i+t.\end{cases}\end{array}

Hence it is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\begin{cases}t+1\leq k\leq 2t-1\\
k-t\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor\end{cases}&\text{ or }&\begin{cases}3\leq k\leq t\\
1\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor.\end{cases}\end{array}

Therefore gathering by values of
k
in the left-hand side of (
5.25
) yields:

\prod_{k=3}^{t}\left(1-q^{k}x\right)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\prod_{k=t+1}^{2t-1}\left(1-q^{k}x\right)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor-k+t+1}\\
=\prod_{k=1}^{2t-1}\left(1-q^{k}x\right)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\prod_{k=t+1}^{2t-1}\left(1-q^{k}x\right)^{t+1-k}

Shifting
k\to k+t
in the second product, the above equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{2t-1}\left(1-q^{k}x\right)^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\prod_{k=1}^{t-1}\left(1-q^{k+t}x\right)^{1-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{r\geq 1}\left(1-q^{r}x\right)^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}}{\prod_{r\geq 2t}\left(1-q^{r}x\right)^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}}\frac{\prod_{r\geq t}\left(1-q^{r+t}x\right)^{r-1}}{\prod_{r\geq 1}\left(1-q^{r+t}x\right)^{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left(1-q^{r}x\right)^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}}{\left(1-q^{r-1+2t}x\right)^{t-1+\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}\frac{\left(1-q^{r-1+2t}x\right)^{t+r-2}}{\left(1-q^{r+t}x\right)^{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left(1-q^{r}x\right)^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}\left(1-q^{r-1+2t}x\right)^{r-1-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}{\left(1-q^{r+t}x\right)^{r-1}},

which is the desired result by shifting
r\to r+2
at the numerator and
r\to r+1
at the denominator.
The other equalities (
5.26
) and (
5.27
) are proved similarly.

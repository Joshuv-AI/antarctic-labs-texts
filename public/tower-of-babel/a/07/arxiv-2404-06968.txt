Transcendence properties of the Artin-Hasse exponential modulo p p
Joe Kramer-Miller

Abstract
Let E p ​ ( x ) E_{p}(x) denote the Artin-Hasse exponential and let E ¯ p ​ ( x ) \overline{E}_{p}(x) denote its reduction modulo p p in 𝔽 p ​ [ [ x ] ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!] . In this article we study transcendence properties of E ¯ p ​ ( x ) \overline{E}_{p}(x) over 𝔽 p ​ [ x ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[x] . We give two proofs that E ¯ p ​ ( x ) \overline{E}_{p}(x) is transcendental, affirmatively answering a question of Thakur. We also prove algebraic independence results: i) for f 1 , … , f r ∈ x ​ 𝔽 p ​ [ x ] f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in x\mathbb{F}_{p}[x] satisfying certain linear independence properties, we show that the E ¯ p ​ ( f 1 ) , … , E ¯ p ​ ( f r ) \overline{E}_{p}(f_{1}),\dots,\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r}) are algebraically independent over 𝔽 p ​ [ x ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[x] and ii) we determine the algebraic relations between E ¯ p ​ ( c ​ x ) \overline{E}_{p}(cx) , where c ∈ 𝔽 p × c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times} . Our proof studies the higher derivatives of E ¯ p ​ ( x ) \overline{E}_{p}(x) and makes use of iterative differential Galois theory.



1
Introduction

The Artin-Hasse exponential is defined by

\displaystyle E_{p}(x):=\exp\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p^{n}}}{p^{n}}\right)

and is known to have coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
.
This series plays an enormous role in
p
-adic analysis, showing up
in the construction of Witt vectors (see e.g.
[
7
]
) and the
p
-adic
approach to exponential sums (see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
). From the relation
\frac{E_{p}(x^{p})}{E_{p}(x)^{p}}=\exp(-xp)

it is immediate that
E_{p}(x)
is a transcendental function over
\mathbb{C}(x)
.
However, we may also consider the reduction of
E_{p}(x)
modulo
p
, which we denote by
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
. Then
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is a power series in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]

that in some sense plays the role of the exponential function in characteristic
p
.
For example, one can use
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
to translate Witt vector addition into power
series multiplication as explained in
[
7
]
. In this article
we investigate the transcendence properties of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
. We give two proofs that
\overline{E}_{p}(x)

is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
. This answers a question posed by Dinesh Thakur (see e.g.
[
9
]
or the open questions in chapter 12 of
[
3
]
). We also prove algebraic independence results for
the evaluations of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
at different values in
x\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
.

1.1
Main results and outline

Our first result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The series
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

We give two proofs Theorem
1.1
. The first
proof is given in §
2
. It uses
Christol’s automatic criterion for algebraicity of power series together with
the functional equation: for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
we have
\overline{E}_{p}(cx)=\overline{E}_{p}(x)^{[c]}
for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

where
[c]
denotes the Teichmuller lift of
c
. This proof is relatively elementary and short, but has the disadvantage of requiring
p
to be at least
5
. The second proof
uses iterative differential Galois theory and iterative Frobenius equations. These
are the same methods we use for the stronger algebraic independence results, which we
describe in detail below.

To state our first algebraic independence result we need the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
f(x)=\sum c_{i}x^{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
. We define the
primitive

part of
f
to be

\displaystyle f^{*}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0\\
p\nmid i\end{subarray}}c^{*}_{i}(f)x^{i},

\displaystyle c^{*}_{i}(f):=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{ip^{k}}^{1/p^{k}}.

Note that
f^{*}(x)=0
if and only if
f(x)=g(x)^{p}-g(x)
for some
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in x\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
be nonzero. Assume that one of the two conditions holds:

1.

The elements
f_{1}^{*},\dots,f_{r}^{*}
are linearly independent over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

2.

The
f_{i}^{*}
’s are not all zero and no non-trivial power product of the
f_{i}^{\prime}s
are contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)^{p}
. In other words, if
f_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots f_{r}^{n_{r}}
is a
p
-th power then
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in p\mathbb{Z}
.

Then
\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1}),\dots,\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})
are algebraically independent.

Our second algebraic independence result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)}\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)(\overline{E}_{p}(cx))_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}

\displaystyle=\phi(p-1),

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function.

The proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem

1.4
follow the same strategy. This strategy can be broken into
three steps, which we outline below. For simplicity, we restrict our attention
to the case where the
f_{i}
in Theorem
1.3

have coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Iterated Frobenius equations.

Let
\sigma
be the lifted Frobenius endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![x]\!]
that fixes
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and sends
x
to
x^{p}
.
An iterated Frobenius (abbreviated
IF
) equation with depth
k
is the system of equations:

y_{1}^{\sigma}-y_{1}=x^{b_{1}},~~y_{2}^{\sigma}-y_{2}=x^{b_{2}}y_{1},~~\dots,~~y_{k}^{\sigma}-y_{k}=x^{b_{k}}y_{k-1}.

where the
b_{i}\geq 1
. We say an
IF
-equation is primitive if each
b_{i}
is prime to
p
.
Let
M_{k}
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(x)
-module space generated by all solutions to all
IF
-equations of depth at most
k
and let
M=\cup M_{k}
. In §
3
we study
M
. We prove the following:

1.

The solutions to primitive
IF
-equations form a basis of
M
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(x)
.

2.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(x)
-module
M
is closed under multiplication and
M_{k_{1}}M_{k_{2}}\subset M_{k_{1}+k_{2}}
.

Higher derivatives

Let
\partial^{(k)}
denote the differential operator
\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{d^{k}x}

and let
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}
be the operator sending
f
to
\frac{\partial^{(k)}f}{f}
. We regard

\partial^{(k)}
and
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}
as operators on
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]
and on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![x]\!]
. Note that if
f
is contained in a finite extension
L

of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
, then so is
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(f)
for all
k\geq 0
. This gives a differential
transcendence criterion: if the
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(f)
generate an infinite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
then
f
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
. In §
4
we study the iterated differential equations satisfied by
\overline{E}_{p}(x)

and power products of the
\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})
. We prove that
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(\overline{E}_{p}(x))
is contained in
M_{k}
,
but not in
M_{k-1}
. For example, when
k=1
a quick calculation shows
that
\frac{d}{dx}\overline{E}_{p}(x)=\frac{y}{x}\overline{E}_{p}(x)
, where
y^{\sigma}-y=x
.
More precisely, we determine
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(\overline{E}_{p}(x))
modulo
M_{k-1}
. This is
enough to show
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is transcendental. Next,
using standard properties of higher derivatives and results from §
3
, we are able to determine
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}\left(\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1})^{a_{1}}\dots\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})^{a_{r}}\right)

modulo
M_{k-1}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

Iterative differential Galois groups

Let
K
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(x)\!)
. Note
that
M
is contained in
K
. An
ID
-module over
K
is
a vector space over
K
with compatible actions of
\partial^{(k)}
. For any
f\in x\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
we let
N_{f}
denote the
ID
-module associated to the differential equation satisfied by

\overline{E}_{p}(f)
(see §
5.1
for a precise
description of this
ID
-module). Following Matzat and van der Put (see
[
8
]
) we may define an
ID
-Galois group

\textnormal{Gal}(N_{f})
. Then
\textnormal{Gal}(N_{f})
is a closed subgroup of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and is equal to all of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
precisely when
\overline{E}_{p}(f)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
. More generally,
the algebraic independence of
\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1}),\dots,\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})
is equivalent
to the
ID
-Galois group
\textnormal{Gal}(N_{f_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus N_{f_{r}})
being isomorphic
to
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
. Using the Tannakian interpretation of
ID
-Galois groups, it is enough
to prove
\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1})^{a_{1}}\dots\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})^{a_{r}}
is transcendental
for any
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We then address this transcendentce question under the conditions
of Theorem
1.4
and Theorme
1.3

using the results from §
4
.

1.2
Further questions

It would be interesting to completely determine the algebraic relations between
values
\overline{E}_{p}(f)
for
f\in x\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
. More generally, it would be
interesting to determine the algebraic relations between values of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
at
any algebraic power series. At the moment it is even unclear to the author
what the algebraic relations are between the
\overline{E}_{p}(\xi x)
where
\xi
ranges over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The reader may observe the influence of Ax’s work on Schanuel’s conjecture in
[
4
]
on the final steps of the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Thus, it is natural to ask if an analogue of Ax-Schanuel hold for the
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
.

2
An automatic approach to the transcendence of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)

In this section we give a short proof of the transcendence of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
using
the functional equation satisfied by
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
and automatic methods.

2.1
A transcendental criterion

We recall Christol’s well-known automatic criterion for algebraicity of series in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]
. See
[
6
]
or
[
5
]
for
proofs.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f=\sum f_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]
. The following are equivalent.

1.

The series
f
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

2.

There is a
p
-automaton that gives output
f_{n}
on input
n
.

3.

There are only finitely many subsequence of
(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of the form

(f_{p^{k}n+r})_{n\geq 0}
, where
k
is positive and
r
is in the range
0\leq r<p^{k}
.

This allows us to establish the following transcendence criterion:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
f\in 1+x\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]
be algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Then
f^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![x]\!]
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
if and only if
\lambda
is rational.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
2
]
. We will give a proof in the interest of having a quick and self-contained automatic proof that
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is transcendental. We easily reduce to the case where
f=1+x
. If
\lambda
is rational it is clear that
(1+x)^{\lambda}
is algebraic. For the other direction, assume
\lambda
is irrational. Write
\lambda=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}p^{n}
. For
1\leq c\leq p-1
we define sets

S_{c}:=\left\{n~|~a_{n}=c\right\}~~\text{and}~~S_{c}^{>k}:=S_{c}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>k}.

We then set
\lambda_{c}=\sum_{n\in S_{c}}a_{n}p^{n}.

If
\lambda_{c}
is nonzero and rational, we may replace
\lambda
with
\lambda-\lambda_{c}
. In particular, we may assume each
\lambda_{c}
is either
zero
or non-rational. Let
c_{0}
be the largest number such that
\lambda_{c_{0}}\neq 0
. As

\lambda_{c_{0}}
is irrational for each
k,m>0
we have

\displaystyle m+S_{c_{0}}^{k}

\displaystyle\neq S_{c_{0}}^{k+m},

(1)

as otherwise the digits of
\lambda_{c_{0}}
would eventually be periodic.

Next, write
(1+x)^{\lambda}=\sum_{n=0}b_{n}x^{n}
. By using the product expansion

(1+x)^{\lambda}=\prod(1+x^{p^{n}})^{a_{n}}
we see that

\displaystyle b_{c_{0}p^{k}}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&k\in S_{c_{0}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Consider the sequences
B_{k}=(b_{np^{k}})_{n\geq 1}
. By Theorem
2.1

if we prove that the
B_{k}
are all distinct, we will know
(1+x)^{\lambda}
is transcendental. Note that the
c_{0}p^{n}
-th element in
B_{k}
is
1
if
n+k\in S_{c_{0}}^{k}
and zero otherwise. In particular, if
B_{k}=B_{k+m}
, then we must have

m+S_{c_{0}}^{k}=S_{c_{0}}^{k+m}
. However, by (
1
) we know this can never happen, which proves the lemma.
∎

2.2
A short proof of the transcendence of
\overline{E}_{p}(x)

The first step is to prove that
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
satisfies a functional equation.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
we have
\overline{E}_{p}(\xi x)=\overline{E}_{p}(x)^{[\xi]}
, where
[\xi]\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

denotes the Teichmuller lift of
\xi
.

Proof.

For
f_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![x]\!]
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in x\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![x]\!]
we may take the composition
f_{0}\circ\dots\circ f_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![x]\!]
and this operation is associative. For
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we define

g(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p^{n}}}{p^{n}},~~~L_{c}(x)=cx,~~\text{and}~~P_{c}(x)=(1+x)^{c}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{c}{n}x^{n}.

Note that
E_{p}(x)=\exp\circ g
. Also, for
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
we have the relations:

g\circ L_{[\xi]}=L_{[\xi]}\circ g~~\text{and}~~\exp\circ L_{[\xi]}=P_{[\xi]}\circ(\exp(x)-1).

The first relation comes from the fact that
[\xi]^{p}=[\xi]
.
Thus, we compute

\displaystyle E_{p}([\xi]x)

\displaystyle=\exp\circ g\circ L_{[\xi]}

\displaystyle=\exp\circ L_{[\xi]}\circ g

\displaystyle=P_{[\xi]}\circ(\exp(x)-1)\circ g

\displaystyle=P_{[\xi]}\circ(E_{p}(x)-1)=\overline{E}_{p}(x)^{[\xi]}.

The lemma follows by reducing modulo
p
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

The series
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
if
p\geq 5
.

Proof.

Take
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Since
p\geq 5
we can pick
c
so that
[c]
is not an integer and thus not rational. Assume
\overline{E}_{p}(x)
is algebraic. This implies
\overline{E}_{p}(cx)
is algebraic. Thus, by Lemma
2.3
we know that
\overline{E}_{p}(x)^{[c]}
is algebraic. However, we know
[c]
is not rational, so Lemma
2.2
tells us that
\overline{E}_{p}(x)^{[c]}
is transcendental. This is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

One can get algebraic independence results using
the full result of
[
2
]
. In particular, the argument above
can be modified to show
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)}(\overline{E}_{p}(cx))_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}\geq\phi(p-1)-1
. As we prove in
5
that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)}(\overline{E}_{p}(cx))_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}=\phi(p-1)
, we omit the proof of this weaker result.

3
Solutions to iterated Frobenius equations

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the field with
q=p^{f}
elements and fix
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\zeta,\zeta^{p},\dots,\zeta^{p^{f-1}}
is a basis
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
denote the
p
-typical Witt vectors
W(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
By abuse of notation we regard the
\zeta
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
by identifying
\zeta
with its Teichmuller lift. It will be convenient to define
x_{j}^{i}=\zeta^{p^{j}}x^{i}
and the set

\displaystyle X:=\left\{x_{j}^{i}~\middle|~\begin{array}[]{c}1\leq j\leq f\\
i\geq 1,p\nmid i\end{array}\right\}.

3.1
Iterated Frobenius equations

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(x)\!)\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear endomorphism sending
x
to
x^{p}
and acting as the Frobenius endomorphism on
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Let
f\in x\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\![x]\!]
. We define
\tau(f)
to be the unique solution to
y^{\sigma}-y=f
in
x\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
. Concretely, we have

\tau(f)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f^{\sigma^{n}}.

We regard
\tau
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear map. Note that
\tau
satisfies these two fundamental equations:

\displaystyle\tau(f)\tau(g)

\displaystyle=\tau(f\tau(g))+\tau(g\tau(f))-\tau(fg),

(2)

\displaystyle\tau(f^{\sigma}\tau(g))

\displaystyle=\tau(f\tau(g))+\tau(f^{\sigma}g)-f\tau(g).

(3)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
[k]
denote the set
\{1,\dots,k\}
and let
[0]
denote the empty set. An
iterated Frobenius datum

b
(abbreviated IF-datum) of depth
k
is
a function

b:[k]\to x\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\![x]\!].

If the image of
b
is in
X
we say
b
is a
p
-primary
IF
-datum.
We denote the depth of
b
by
\text{dep}(b)
. We remark that there is exactly one

IF
-datum of depth
0
. We define
D
to be the set of all
IF
-data.

Definition 3.2
.

We let
b^{tr}
denote
the restriction of
b
to
[k-1]
(we think of
b
as a list of elements and
b^{tr}
as
the truncation of the last element, hence the
tr
in the superscript). Also, for any

z\in x\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\![x]\!]
we let
\textnormal{cat}(b,z)
to be the
IF
-datum of depth
k+1
defined by:

\displaystyle\textnormal{cat}(b,z)(i)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}b(i)&i\in[k]\\
z&i=k+1\end{cases}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
b
be an IF-datum of depth
k
. We define

w_{b}\in x\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\![x]\!]
by the recursive definition:

\displaystyle w_{b}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{dep}(b)=0\\
\tau(b(k)w_{b^{tr}})&\text{dep}(b)=k>0\end{cases}.

We refer to
w_{b}
as an iterative Frobenius solution (abbreviated
IF
-solution).

For an
IF
-datum
b
we may consider the iterative Frobenius equation:

y_{1}^{\sigma}-y_{1}=b(1),~~y_{2}^{\sigma}-y_{2}=b(2)y_{1},~~\dots,~~y_{k}^{\sigma}-y_{k}=b(k)y_{k-1}.

There is a unique solution vector
(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
such that

z_{i}\in x^{i}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
. Then
w_{b}
is
z_{k}
.

3.2
Spaces generated by
IF
-solutions

We make the following definitions:

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in D\\
\text{dep}(b)\leq k\end{subarray}}a_{b}w_{b}~|~a_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\text{$p$-adic completion of $M_{k}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\![x]\!]\langle x^{-1}\rangle$},

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in D\\
\text{dep}(b)\leq k\end{subarray}}a_{b}w_{b}~|~a_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}[x]\right\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
k\geq 0
we have
\tau(xA_{k})\subset A_{k+1}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=0
we are reduced to showing
\tau(x_{j}^{i})\in A_{1}
.
Write
i=p^{t}h
where
h
is coprime to
p
. Then

\tau(x_{j}^{i})=\tau(x_{j-t}^{i-t})-\sum_{m=1}^{t}x_{j-m}^{p^{t-m}h},

so
\tau(x_{j}^{i})\in A_{1}
.
For
k>0
it suffices to show
\tau(x_{j}^{i}w_{b})\in A_{k+1}
for any
w_{b}\in W
with
\text{dep}(w)=k
and
k\geq 1
.
Again we write
i=p^{t}h
.
By repeatedly using (
3
)
we obtain

\tau(x_{j}^{i}w_{b})=\tau(x_{j-t}^{h}w_{b})+\sum_{m=1}^{t}\tau(x_{j-m}^{p^{t-m}h}b(k)w_{b^{tr}})-\sum_{m=1}^{t}x_{j-m}^{p^{t-m}h}w_{b}.

Note that
\tau(x_{j-t}^{h}w_{b})
is an
IF
-solution of degree
k+1
, so it is
contained in
A_{k+1}
. The second summation is clearly contained in
A_{k}
. Finally,
by our inductive hypothesis
\tau(x_{j-m}^{p^{t-m}h}b(k)w_{b^{tr}})
is contained in
A_{k}
.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For any
IF
-datum
b
of depth
k
we have
w_{b}\in M_{k}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

We have
\sigma(\widehat{M}_{k})\subset\widehat{M}_{k}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove
w_{b}^{\sigma}\in M_{k}
for any
IF
-datum with
\text{dep}(b)=k
. We have

w_{b}^{\sigma}=w_{b}+b(k)w_{b^{tr}}
, which is in
M_{k}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

We have

Proof.

We induct on

the ring

From the definitions and the Leibnitz rule, we reduce to showing

We deduce that

\displaystyle w_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{n}x^{p^{n+1}-1}(b(k)^{\prime}w_{b^{tr}}+b(k)w_{b^{tr}}^{\prime})^{\sigma^{n}}.

(4)

∎

(5)

4
Higher derivatives and the Artin-Hasse exponential

4.1
Higher derivatives and a transcendental criterion

We define the
higher derivatives
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
and
\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
:

\partial^{(k)}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{d^{k}x}.

We define the
higher logarithmic derivatives
of
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
or
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(x)\!)
by

\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(f)=\frac{\partial^{(k)}(f)}{f}.

Note that
\partial^{(k)}
and
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}
restrict to maps on
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
. Furthermore, if
K
is a finite separable extension
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
, then each
\partial^{(k)}
and
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}
extends uniquely to
K
by a theorem of Schmidt (see
[
8
, §2]
).
We begin with the following general transcendence result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots
be a sequence of power series in
\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
. Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
be a power series satisfying the iterative differential equation

\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(f)=\alpha_{k}.

If
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)[\alpha_{i}]_{i\geq 1}
is an infinite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
, then
f(x)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

Proof.

Assume
f(x)
is algebraic (necessarily separable) and contained in a field
K
.
As
\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}
extends to
K
, we see that
\alpha_{n}
is contained in
K
as well. This contradicts our assumption that
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots)
is an infinite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots
be a sequence of power series contained in
\overline{M}
with
\text{dep}(\alpha_{k})=k
for each
k
.
Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
be a power series satisfying the iterative differential equation

\textnormal{dLog}^{(k)}(f)=\alpha_{k}.

Then
f(x)
is transcendental.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.13
we know that the
\alpha_{k}
’s are linearly independent over

\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
, so they cannot be contained in a finite extension. Then use Theorem
4.1
.
∎

5
Algebraic independence results

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
.

5.1
Iterative differential Galois theory and transcendence

We will make use of iterative differential (
ID
-for short) Galois theory, as expounded on by Matzat and van der Put in
[
8
]
(in particular §3 and §4 in this article.) Let
K
be the algebraic closure
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(x)\!)
. The higher derivatives
\partial^{(k)}
make
K
an
ID
-field. For any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
we recursively define a sequence
A_{f,0},A_{f,1},\dots\in K
by

A_{f,0}=1~\text{ and }A_{f,k}=-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}A_{f,i}\textnormal{dLog}^{(k-i)}(\overline{E}_{p}(f)).

We then define an
ID
-module
N_{f}=Ke_{f}
over
K
of rank one by the rule:

\partial^{(k)}(e_{f})=A_{f,k}e_{f}.

Note that
N_{f}
trivializes over the ring
K\left(\overline{E}_{p}(f),\frac{1}{\overline{E}_{p}(f)}\right)
.
For
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in x\overline{F}_{p}(\!(x)\!)
we define the rank
r

ID
-module:

\displaystyle N:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}N_{f_{1}}.

Then
N
has a full system of solutions over the ring

\displaystyle R_{0}:=K\left(\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1}),\dots,\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r}),\frac{1}{\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1})\dots\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})}\right).

The Picard-Vessiot ring of
N
is
R=R_{0}/I
, where
I
is a maximal
ID
-ideal (i.e.
I
is a maximal element in the ordered set of ideals of
R_{0}
satisfying
\partial^{(k)}(I)\subset I
.) The
ID
-Galois group
\mathscr{G}(N)
is the group of
ID
-automorphisms of
R
that fix
K
. A key fact is that
\mathscr{G}(N)
is reduced algebraic group over the field of constants
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and that
\text{Spec}(R)
is an
\mathscr{G}(N)
-torsor over
K
. In particular we have

\dim_{K}(\textnormal{Frac}(R_{0}))\geq\dim_{K}(\textnormal{Frac}(R))=\dim_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\mathscr{G}(N)).

Thus, it suffices to show
\mathscr{G}(N)
has dimension
r
.

On the other hand, there is a Tannakian interpretation of the
ID
-Galois group. Let
\textnormal{ID}_{K}
denote the category of
ID
-modules over
K
. Then
\textnormal{ID}_{K}
is a
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear tensor category. For any object
N
in
\textnormal{ID}_{K}
we let
[N]
be the full subcategory of
\textnormal{ID}_{K}
generated by tensor powers of
N
and its dual. Then
[N]
is again a
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear tensor category and thus isomorphic to the category
of representations for an affine algebraic group
\textnormal{Gal}(N)
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then by standard arguments (see
[
10
]
for the characteristic
0
case and see
[
8
]
for our precise situation) we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(N)\cong\textnormal{Gal}(N).

Note that since
N_{f_{i}}
has rank one we know
\textnormal{Gal}(N_{f_{i}})
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. In particular, we see that
\textnormal{Gal}(N)
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
. To prove that
\textnormal{Gal}(N)\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}
it is enough to show that for any
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
that are not all zero, the
ID
-module
N_{f_{1}}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes N_{f_{r}}^{\otimes n_{r}}
is nontrivial. This amounts to proving
\overline{E}_{p}(f_{1})^{n_{1}}\dots\overline{E}_{p}(f_{r})^{n_{r}}
is transcendental over
K
. Furthermore, we are easily reduced
to the case where not all the
n_{i}
’s are divisible by
p
.

5.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
satisfy one of the two conditions from Theorem
1.3
. From the discussion in §
5.1

we must show

’s are divisible by
p
.
From Corollary
4.2

(14)

The case where the

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}c_{z}^{*}(f_{i})\textnormal{dLog}^{(1)}(f_{i})

. The theorem follows by observing that

The case where

Without loss of generality assume

.
We may also assume that none of the

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}c_{z}^{*}(f_{i})\textnormal{dLog}^{(1)}(f_{i})

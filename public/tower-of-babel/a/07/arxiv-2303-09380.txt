A small remark on Bernstein’s theorem
Michael Bildhauer, Bernhard Farquhar, Martin Fuchs

Abstract
We investigate splitting-type variational problems with some linear growth conditions. For balanced solutions of the associated Euler-Lagrange equation we receive a result analogous to Bernstein’s theorem on non-parametric minimal surfaces. Without assumptions of this type, Bernstein’s theorem cannot be carried over to the splitting case, which follows from an elementary counterexample. We also include some modifications of our main theorem. 1 1 1 AMS Classification 49Q20, 49Q05, 53A10, 35J20 , 2 2 2 Keywords: Bernstein’s theorem, non-parametric minimal surfaces, two-dimensional variational problems, splitting-type functionals

equation
associated to the area functional

J_{0}[u,\Omega]\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}f_{0}(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2)

We ask the following question: does Bernstein’s theorem extend to the case when the area integrand

is
replaced by the energy density

More generally we let for

f(P):=f_{1}(p_{1})+f_{2}(p_{2})

(3)

with functions
f_{i}\in\mathit{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),i=1,2
, satisfying

0<f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq C_{i}(1+t^{2})^{-\frac{\mu_{i}}{2}},t\in\mathbb{R},

(4)

for numbers
C_{i}>0
and with exponents

\mu_{i}>1.

(5)

Note that (

and on account of (

|f_{i}(t)|\leq a|t|+b,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

for some constants

satisfying (
3
)-(

[
8
]

We then replace (
1

\mathrm{div}\left(Df(\nabla u)\right)=0

(6)

and observe that the non-affine function (
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
)

w(x_{1},x_{2}):=\alpha x_{1}x_{2}+\beta x_{1}+\gamma x_{2}+\delta

(7)

is an entire solution of equation (
6
), in other words: the classical version of Bernstein’s theorem does not extend
to the splitting case. The behaviour of the function
w
defined in (
7
) is characterized in

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in\mathit{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),u=u(x_{1},x_{2})
, is called unbalanced, if and only if both of the following conditions hold

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|\partial_{1}u(x)|}{1+|\partial_{2}u(x)|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\infty,

(8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|\partial_{2}u(x)|}{1+|\partial_{1}u(x)|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\infty.

(9)

Otherwise we say that
u
is of balanced form.

Remark 1.2
.

Condition (
8
) for example means that there exists a sequence of points
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

|x_{n}|\to\infty
and for which

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\partial_{1}u(x_{n})|}{1+|\partial_{2}u(x_{n})|}=\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

If for instance (
8
) is violated, no such sequence exists. Thus we can find constants
R,M>0
such that

|\partial_{1}u(x)|\leq M\left(1+|\partial_{2}u(x)|\right)
for all
|x|\geq R

u
is of class

\mathit{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, this just shows

|\partial_{1}u(x)|\leq m|\partial_{2}u(x)|+M,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(10)

with suitable new constants
m,M>0
.

.

Let (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}|\partial_{1}w_{1}|^{2}\eta^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{2R}}\Theta|\partial_{2}w_{1}|^{2}\eta^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}\eta\partial_{1}w_{1}\,\partial_{1}\eta\,w_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}\Theta\,\eta\,\partial_{2}w_{1}\,\partial_{2}\eta\,w_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Applying Young’s inequality and using
w_{1}
,
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(|\partial_{1}w_{1}|^{2}+\Theta|\partial_{2}w_{1}|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(47)

We then return to (
46
) and apply the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}|\partial_{1}w_{1}|^{2}\eta^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{2R}}\Theta|\partial_{2}w_{1}|^{2}\eta^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\Bigg[\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}\eta^{2}|\partial_{1}w_{1}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg[\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}|\partial_{1}\eta|^{2}w_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+2\Bigg[\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}\Theta\eta^{2}|\partial_{2}w_{1}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg[\int_{B_{2R}-B_{R}}\Theta|\partial_{2}\eta|^{2}w_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the right-hand side of (
48
) we observe that for both parts the first integral is vanishing when passing to the limit
R\to\infty

since we have (
47
), while the remaining integrals stay uniformly bounded.

This gives
\partial_{1}w_{1}=0
and
\partial_{2}w_{1}=0
since we have
\Theta>0
. Hence we obtain
w_{1}\equiv c_{1}

for some constant
c_{1}
. The monotonicity of
f_{1}^{\prime}
then implies
\partial_{1}u\equiv\tilde{c}_{1}

for some different constant
\tilde{c}_{1}
.

By (
43
) we then also have
\partial_{2}w_{2}=0
. Since we have already observed above that
\partial_{1}w_{2}=\Theta\partial_{2}w_{1}
, we
deduce
\partial_{2}u\equiv\tilde{c}_{2}
for some other real number
\tilde{c}_{2}
and
in conclusion
u
must be an affine function which completes the proof of
Theorem 1.9. ∎

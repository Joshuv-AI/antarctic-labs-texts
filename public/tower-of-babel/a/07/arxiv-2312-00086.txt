Shalu M. A, Cyriac Antony

Abstract
Dvořák, Mohar and Šámal (J. Graph Theory, 2013) proved that for every 3-regular graph G G , the line graph of G G is 4-star colourable if and only if G G admits a locally bijective homomorphism to the cube Q 3 Q_{3} . We generalise this result as follows: for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , a K 1 , p + 1 K_{1,p+1} -free 2 ​ p 2p -regular graph G G admits a ( p + 2 ) (p+2) -star colouring if and only if G G admits a locally bijective homomorphism to a fixed 2 ​ p 2p -regular graph named G 2 ​ p G_{2p} . We also prove the following: (i) for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , a 2 ​ p 2p -regular graph G G admits a ( p + 2 ) (p+2) -star colouring if and only if G G has an orientation G → \vec{G} that admits an out-neighbourhood bijective homomorphism to a fixed orientation G → 2 ​ p \vec{G}_{2p} of G 2 ​ p G_{2p} ; (ii) for every 3-regular graph G G , the line graph of G G is 4-star colourable if and only if G G is bipartite and distance-two 4-colourable; and (iii) it is NP-complete to check whether a planar 4-regular 3-connected graph is 4-star colourable.



Keywords:
Star coloring, Graph homomorphism, Locally constrained graph homomorphism, Regular graphs, Locally bijective homomorphism, Graph orientation.

3.1

In this subsection, we prove that locally bijective homomorphisms between 3-regular graphs, in a sense, behave well with respect to the line graph operation.

Theorem 4

Consider ar arbitrary edge

To prove the observation, it suffices to establish the following claim (since

Claim 1:

the restriction of

to the set

is a bijection from

Let

in

, and let

be the neighbours of

in

, where

in

.

to

to

is a bijection from

onto

.

are pairwise distinct.
Similarly,
\psi(u_{1}),\psi(u_{2}),\dots,\psi(u_{k})
are pairwise distinct.

Since
\psi
is a homomorphism from
G
to
H
, the vertices
\psi(u_{1}),\psi(u_{2}),\dots,\psi(u_{k})
are neighbours of
\psi(v_{1})
in

.
Similarly,
\psi(v_{1}),\psi(v_{2}),\dots,\psi(v_{\ell})
are neighbours of
\psi(u_{1})
in

.
Hence,
\psi(u_{2})\psi(v_{1}),\dots\psi(u_{k})\psi(v_{1}),\psi(u_{1})\psi(v_{2}),\dots,\psi(u_{1})\psi(v_{\ell})
are neighbours of
\psi(u_{1})\psi(v_{1})
in

.
Since
\psi^{\prime}(u_{i}v_{1})=\psi(u_{i})\psi(v_{1})
for

\psi^{\prime}(u_{1}v_{j})=\psi(u_{1})\psi(v_{j})

maps each member of

to a member of

.
Hence, to prove Claim

, it suffices to show that no two members of

by

To produce a contradiction, assume on the contrary that one of the following holds:

(Case 1)
\psi^{\prime}(u_{i}v_{1})=\psi^{\prime}(u_{j}v_{1})

(Case 2)
\psi^{\prime}(u_{i}v_{1})=\psi^{\prime}(u_{1}v_{j})

(Case 3)

\psi^{\prime}(u_{i}v_{1})=\psi^{\prime}(u_{j}v_{1})

\psi(u_{2})\psi(v_{1})=\psi(u_{3})\psi(v_{1})
(as edges in

).
That is,
\{\psi(u_{2}),\psi(v_{1})\}=\{\psi(u_{3}),\psi(v_{1})\}

\psi(u_{2})=\psi(u_{3})

Case 2:

\psi^{\prime}(u_{i}v_{1})=\psi^{\prime}(u_{1}v_{j})

By symmetry, Case 3 leads to a contradiction like Case 1.
Since all three cases lead to contradictions, Claim

is proved.

Observation 5
.

Let

, and let

and

be two graphs such that all maximal cliques in

and

are

-cliques.

to

, then there is an LBH from

to

Proof.

Suppose that
\psi
is an LBH from

to

.
Recall that for each

.
Since

is a homomorphism from

to

of

is a clique of

Since maximal cliques of

are

of

is a maximal clique of

Define

for each maximal clique

of

(here,
\psi^{*}(K)
is the image of
\psi^{*}
at

is a set-image).
Observe that

is well-defined.

Claim 1:

\psi^{*}
is a homomorphism from

to

.

Consider an arbitrary edge

of

, where

and

are maximal cliques in

.
We need to show that

\big(

\psi(c_{1})\psi(c_{2})\in E(K(H))
\big)
.
Since

is an edge in

, we have

.
Let

(where

).
Clearly,
\psi(v)\in\psi(c_{1})
and
\psi(v)\in\psi(c_{2})
.
Since
\psi(v)\in\psi(c_{1})\cap\psi(c_{2})

.
Since
\psi(c_{1})
and
\psi(c_{2})
are maximal cliques in

that intersect,
\psi(c_{1})\psi(c_{2})
is an edge in
K(H)
.
This proves Claim
1
; that is,
\psi^{*}
is a homomorphism from
K(G)
to

.

Claim 2:

\psi^{*}
is an LBH from

to
K(H)
.

Consider an arbitrary vertex

of

(i.e.,

is a maximal clique in

).
Since

is arbitrary, to prove Claim

, it suffices to show that the restriction of
\psi^{*}
to

onto

(i.e,

).
Neighbours of

in

are maximal cliques of

that intersect with

.
Each such clique

of

satisfies

\psi(v)\in\psi(K)\cap\psi(\tilde{K})

.
Hence, for each neighbour

of

in

, its image

) is a neighbour of

in

.
That is,
\psi^{*}
maps each member of

to a member of

.
Hence to prove Claim

, it suffices to show that
\psi^{*}
maps distinct members of
N_{K(G)}(K)
to distinct members of

On the contrary, assume that
\psi^{*}
maps distinct members

of

to the same member of

Let

.

Case 1:

.

Since

and

are distinct

-cliques in

, there exists a vertex

.
Note that

Case 2:

.

Since

such that

.
Since

and

.
Since

restricted to

onto

and

of

\psi(y)
.

Since we have a contradiction in both cases,

maps distinct members of

to distinct members of

.
This proves Claim

.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem

now.

Theorem
4
(Restated)
.

Let

be a 3-regular graph, and let

to

.

Conversely, suppose that there is an LBH from

to

.
Since

is

-free and

.
Since

(

.

Claim 1:

contains diamond by Observation

.
But,

.

-CEO of

Proof.

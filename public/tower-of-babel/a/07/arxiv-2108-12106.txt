Sharp embedding between Besov-Triebel-Sobolev spaces and modulation spaces
Yufeng Lu

Abstract
The embedding relations between Besov-Triebel-Sobolev spaces and modulation spaces are determined explicitly. We extend the results in [ 1 ] , [ 2 ] , [ 3 ] to the most general cases. And we give the sharp embedding between Fourier L p L^{p} spaces and modulation spaces.



1
Introduction

The modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
are one of the function spaces introduced by Feichtinger
[
4
]
in the 1980s using the short-time Fourier transform to measure the decay and the regularity of the function in a different way from the usual
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces or Besov-Triebel spaces. The precise definitions will be given in Section
2
. Roughly speaking, the Besov-Triebel spaces mostly use the dyadic decompositions of the frequency space while the modulation spaces using the uniform decompositions of the frequency space. Therefore, these spaces may have many different properties in some cases. Also, they have some properties similarly. So, the relationships between the modulation spaces and Besov-Triebel spaces are important.

Many basic properties of the modulation spaces had been studies in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
such as the Banach spaces properties and the dual spaces of modulation spaces. And the modulation spaces also have some applications in pseudo-differential operators in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
,
[
13
]
. Modulation spaces also have many applications in the analysis of partial differential equations. For example, the Schrodinger and wave semigroups which are not bouned on neither
L^{p}
or
B^{s}_{p,q}
for
p\neq 2
, are bounded on
M_{p,q}^{s}
(see
[
14
]
). So, the modulation space is a good space for the initial data of the Cauchy problem for nonlinear disperse equations(see
[
15
,
2
,
16
,
17
]
) .

As for embedding properties of modulation spaces, Toft in
[
18
]
discussed some of the sufficient conditions of the embedding between modulation spaces and Besov spaces, also, Toft in
[
13
]
discussed the embedding between the
L^{p}
spaces and the Fourier transform of
L^{p}
space with the modulation spaces. Then, Okoudjou in
[
19
]
gave some sufficient conditions for some cases of embedding between Sobolev-Besov spaces and modulation spaces by the means of the short-time Fourier transform. Later, Sugmito and Tomita in
[
1
]
gave the sharp embedding between modulation spaces and Besov spaces when
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
by means of the scaling of modulation spaces, then Wang and Huang in
[
2
]
extended the condition to
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
by means of the uniform decomposition of frequency space. But they all discuss the case when the indexes of physics space and frequency space of modulation spaces and Besov spaces are the same. Later, Kobayashi and Sugimoto in
[
3
]
studied the sharp embedding between
L^{p}
-Sobolev space and modulation spaces using the method similar to
[
20
]
. As for Triebel spaces, Guo, Wu and Zhao in
[
21
]
gave the sharp condition of the embedding
F_{p,r}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s}
when
0<p\leqslant 1
.

In this article, we study the sharp conditions of the embedding between Besov-Triebel-Sobolev spaces and modulation spaces. Let us take Besov spaces for example, in Theorem
4.1
, we get the sufficient and necessary conditions of the embedding
B_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
, which is the most general case of
0<p,q,p_{0},q_{0}\leqslant\infty
, also in Theorem
4.2
, we get the sufficient and necessary conditions of the embedding of the opposite side. In the case of
0<p,q<1
, the dual of Besov spaces and modulation spaces are different, so we cannot just get the opposite side by the method of duality. Instead, we use the method of the uniform decompositions of frequency space to get the result as desired.

We explain the organization of this paper. We give some basic notations and properties of the spaces we need in the next preliminary section. In section 3, we give some lemmas we need in the proof, especially for the case of
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
we discuss in section 4. The embedding between Besov spaces and modulation spaces is considered in section 4. We mainly use the method of uniform decomposition in
[
2
]
. Then we study the embedding between
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces and modulation spaces in section 5, as we can see, there are four conditions totally. In section 6, we consider the embedding Triebel spaces and modulation spaces which is sometimes equivalent to the embedding of Sobolev spaces by Littlewood-Paley theory. In the last section, we give the sharp embedding relationship between the Fourier
L^{r}
spaces and modulation spaces.

2
Perliminaries

2.1
Basic natation

The following notation will be used throughout this article. For
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
, we denote

\displaystyle\sigma(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\wedge(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\wedge(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right);

\displaystyle\tau(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\vee(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\vee(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right),

where
a\wedge b=\min\left\{a,b\right\},a\vee b=\max\left\{a,b\right\}
.

We write
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the Schwartz space of all complex-valued rapidly decreasing infinity differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the dual space of
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, all called the space of all tempered distributions. For simplification, we omit
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
without causing ambiguity. The Fourier transform is defined by
{\mathscr{F}}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}d\xi
, and the inverse Fourier transform by
{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi
. For
1\leqslant p<\infty
, we define the
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

and
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=ess\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}}\left|f(x)\right|
. We also define the
L^{p}
Sobolev norm :

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W^{s,p}}=\left\|(I-\triangle)^{s/2}f\right\|_{p}.

Recall that the Sobolev spaces is defined by
W^{s,p}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{W^{s,p}}<\infty\right\}
. And the Fourier
L^{p}
is defined by
{\mathscr{F}}L^{p}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{p}<\infty\right\}
.

We use the notation
I\lesssim J
if there is an independently constant C such that
I\leqslant CJ
. Also we denote
I\approx J
if
I\lesssim J
and
J\lesssim I
. For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we denote
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, for
0<p<1
, denote
p^{\prime}=\infty
.

2.2
Modulation spaces

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
, the short time Fourier tranform of
f
respect to a window function
g\in\mathscr{S}
is defined as (see
[
4
]
):

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-it\xi}dt.

And then for
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
, we denote

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\|V_{g}f(x,\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{q}L_{x}^{p}},

where
\left\langle\xi\right\rangle=1+\left|\xi\right|
.
Modulation space
M_{p,q}^{s}
are defined as the space of all tempered distribution
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
for which
\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}
is finite.

Also, we know another equivalent definition of modulation by uniform decomposition of frequency space (see
[
6
]
).

Let
\sigma
be a smooth cut-off function adapted to the unit cube
[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
and
\sigma=0
outside the cube
[-3/4,3/4]^{d}
, we write
\sigma_{k}=\sigma(\cdot-k)
, and assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\sigma_{k}(\xi)\equiv 1,\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Denote
\sigma_{k}(\xi)=\sigma(\xi-k)
, and
\Box_{k}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma_{k}{\mathscr{F}}
, then we have the following equivalent norm of modulation space:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}.

We simply write
M_{p,q}
instead of
M_{p,q}^{0}
. One can prove the
M_{p,q}^{s}
norm is independent of the choice of cut-off function
\sigma
. Also
M_{p,q}^{s}
is a quasi Banach space and when
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
,
M_{p,q}^{s}
is a Banach space. When
p,q<\infty
, then
\mathscr{S}
is dense in
M_{p,q}^{s}
. Also,
M_{p,q}^{s}
has some basic properties, we list them in the following lemma(see
[
2
,
22
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s,s_{0},s_{1},\in{\mathbb{R}},0<p,p_{0},p_{1},q,q_{0},q_{1}\leqslant\infty
,

(1)

if
s_{0}\leqslant s_{1},p_{1}\leqslant p_{0},q_{1}\leqslant q_{0}
, we have
M_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow M_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}}
;

(2)

when
p,q<\infty
, the dual space of
M_{p,q}^{s}
is
M_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{-s}
;

(3)

the interpolation spaces theorem is true for
M_{p,q}^{s}
, i.e. for
0<\theta<1

when

s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

we have
\left(M_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}},M_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\right)_{\theta}=M_{p,q}^{s}
;

(4)

when
q_{1}<q,s+d/q>s_{1}+d/q_{1}
, then we have
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q_{1}}^{s_{1}}
.

2.3
Besov-Triebel spaces

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},
choose
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth radial bump function adapted to the ball
B(0,2)
:
\psi(\xi)=1
as
\left|\xi\right|\leqslant 1
and
\psi(\xi)=0
as
\left|\xi\right|\geqslant 2
. We denote
\varphi(\xi)=\psi(\xi)-\psi(2\xi)
, and
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)
for
1\leqslant j,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varphi_{0}(\xi)=1-\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\varphi_{j}(\xi)
. We say that
\triangle_{j}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\varphi_{j}{\mathscr{F}}
are the dyadic decomposition operators. The Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
and the Triebel spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}
are definied in the following way :

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,q}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|2^{js}\left\|\triangle_{j}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{j\geqslant 0}^{q}}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle F^{s}_{p,q}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\left\|f\right\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|\left\|2^{js}\triangle_{j}f\right\|_{\ell_{j\geqslant 0}^{q}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}<\infty\right\}.

One can prove that the Besov-Triebel norms defined by different dyadic decompositions are all equivalent(see
[
23
]
), so without loss of generality, we can assume that when
1\leqslant j
,
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=1
on
D_{j}:=\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}2^{j}\leqslant\left|\xi\right|\leqslant\frac{5}{4}2^{j}\right\}
for convenience. Also, Besov-Triebel spaces have some basic properties known already(see
[
23
,
7
,
22
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s,s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}},0<p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2}\leqslant\infty
,

(1)

if
q_{1}\leqslant q_{2}
, we have
B_{p,q_{1}}^{s}\hookrightarrow B_{p,q_{2}}^{s}
,
F_{p,q_{1}}^{s}\hookrightarrow F_{p,q_{2}}^{s}
;

(2)

\forall\varepsilon>0
, we have
B_{p,q_{1}}^{s+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow B_{p,q_{2}}^{s}
,
F_{p,q_{1}}^{s+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow F_{p,q_{2}}^{s}
;

(3)

B_{p,p\wedge q}^{s}\hookrightarrow F^{s}_{p,q}\hookrightarrow B_{p,p\vee q}^{s}
;

(4)

if
p_{1}\leqslant p_{2},s_{1}-d/p_{1}=s_{2}-d/p_{2}
, we have
B_{p_{1},q}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow B_{p_{2},q}^{s_{2}}
;

(5)

if
p_{1}<p_{2},s_{1}-d/p_{1}=s_{2}-d/p_{2}
, we have
F_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow F_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{s_{2}}
;

(6)

when
1\leqslant p,q<\infty
, the dual space of
B^{s}_{p,q}
is
B_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{-s}
, the dual space of
F^{s}_{p,q}
is
F_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{-s}
;

(7)

the interpolation spaces theorem is true for
B^{s}_{p,q}
and
F^{s}_{p,q}
, i.e. for
0<\theta<1
when

s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

we have
\left(B_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}},B_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\right)_{\theta}=B_{p,q}^{s}
,
\left(F_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}},F_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\right)_{\theta}=F_{p,q}^{s}
;

(8)

when
1<p<\infty
,
F_{p,2}^{s}=W^{s,p}
,
F_{1,2}^{s}\hookrightarrow W^{s,1},W^{s,\infty}\hookrightarrow F_{\infty,2}^{s}
.

3
Some lemmas

In this section, we give some useful lemmas and propositions proved already, which will be used in our proof.

The following Bernstein’s inequality is very useful in time-frequency analysis (see
[
22
]
) :

Lemma 3.1
.

[Bernstein’s inequality]
Let
0<p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty,b>0,\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Denote
L_{B(\xi,b)}^{p}=\left\{f\in L^{p}:\mbox{ supp }\hat{f}\subseteq B(\xi,R)\right\}
. Then there exists
C(d,p,q)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{q}\leqslant C(d,p,q)R^{d(1/p-1/q)}\left\|f\right\|_{p}

holds for all
f\in L_{B(\xi,b)}^{p}
and
C(d,p,q)
is independent of
b>0
and
\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Also, by using the Bernstein’s inequality, we can get the following Young type inequality for
0<p<1
:

Lemma 3.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
0<p<1,R_{1},R_{2}>0,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then there exists
C(d,p)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\left|f\right|*\left|g\right|\right\|_{p}\leqslant C(d,p)(R_{1}+R_{2})^{d(1/p-1)}\left\|f\right\|\left\|g\right\|

holds for all
f\in L_{B(\xi_{1},R_{1})}^{p},g\in L_{B(\xi_{2},R_{2})}^{p}
.

By the definition of
\Box_{k}
and
\triangle_{j}
operators, we know that they are all convolution type operators, so by the Lemma above and the usual Young’s inequality, we have:

Corollary 3.3
.

For
0<p\leqslant\infty,1\leqslant j,\ell,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, there exists
C>0
independent of
k,j
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\triangle_{j}\Box_{k}f\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{p},

\displaystyle\left\|\triangle_{j}\triangle_{\ell}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left\|\triangle_{j}f\right\|_{p}.

Finally we recall a result known already, which is the special case of Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
2
]
)
.

For
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(1)

B^{s}_{p,q}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
if and only if
s\geqslant\tau(p,q)
;

(2)

M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p,q}
if and only if
s\leqslant\sigma(p,q)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

(
[
3
]
)

Let
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
W^{s,p}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
if and only if one of the following cases is satisfied:

(1)

1<p\leqslant q,s\geqslant\tau(p,q)
;

(2)

p>q,s>\tau(p,q)
;

(3)

p=1,q=\infty,s\geqslant\tau(1,\infty)
;

(4)

p=1,q\neq\infty,s>\tau(1,q)
.

4
Embedding between Besov spaces and modulation spaces

In this section, we consider the embedding with the form
B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
. If we got the sufficient and necessary condition of this embedding, then the embedding
B_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s}
can be done in the same way.

Our main result is:

Theorem 4.1
.

For
0<p,q,p_{0},q_{0}\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the embedding
B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
is true if and only if one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)

p_{0}\leqslant p,q_{0}\leqslant q,s\geqslant\tau(p_{0},q)
;

(2)

p_{0}\leqslant p,q_{0}>q,s>\tau(p_{0},q)
.

As for the other side of the embedding between Besov spaces and modulation spaces, we also have:

Theorem 4.2
.

For
0<p,q,p_{1},q_{1}\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the embedding
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p_{1},q_{1}}
is true if and only if one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)

p_{1}\geqslant p,q_{1}\geqslant q,s\leqslant\sigma(p_{1},q)
;

(2)

p_{1}\geqslant p,q_{1}<q,s<\sigma(p_{1},q)
.

Remark 4.3
.

If we regard the Besov space as a special
\alpha
-modulation space when
\alpha=1
as stated in
[
24
]
, the result above is coincident with the Theorem 1.2 in
[
24
]
, where the authors got the full characterization of embedding between
\alpha
-modulation spaces.

For a special case when
p_{0}=q_{0}=2
, which means that
B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}=H^{s}
, the classical Sobolev spaces, then we have

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(1)

the embedding
H^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
is true if and only if one of the following two cases is true:

1.

2\leqslant p,2\leqslant q,s\geqslant 0
;

2.

2\leqslant p,2>q,s>d(1/q-1/2)
.

(2)

The embedding
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow H^{s}
is true if and only if the following two cases is true:

1.

p\leqslant 2,q\leqslant 2,s\leqslant 0
;

2.

p\leqslant 2,q>2,s<d(1/q-1/2)
.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
4.1

In this subsection, we prove the Theorem
4.1
. For sufficiency, by using the lemmas above, we can easily prove it. As for necessity, it may be much more difficult. Here, we mostly use the way in [
[
2
]
] to prove it.

4.1.1
Sufficiency

Use Lemma
2.2
, we know that for any case in
4.1
, we have
B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\hookrightarrow B_{p_{0},q}^{\tau(p_{0},q)}
; then by Proposition
3.4
, we know that
B_{p_{0},q}^{\tau(p_{0},q)}\hookrightarrow M_{p_{0},q}
; also by Lemma
2.1
, we have
M_{p_{0},q}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
.

4.1.2
Necessity

If we have
B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
, then we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}},\ \forall f\in B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}.

Then we can take some different kinds of
f
into (
4.1
) to get some restrictions of indexes.

(1)

Choose
\eta\in\mathscr{S}
, such that supp
\eta\subset[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
, denote
f={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta
, for
\forall\lambda\leqslant 1
, take
f_{\lambda}(x)=f(\lambda x)
into (
4.1
). By the choose of
\eta
, we know that supp
{\mathscr{F}}f_{\lambda}\subset\lambda[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
, which means that

\displaystyle\Box_{k}f_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}f_{\lambda},&$k=0$;\\
0,&$k\neq 0$;\end{cases}\triangle_{j}f_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}f_{\lambda},&$k=0$;\\
0,&$k\neq 0$.\end{cases}

So, take
f_{\lambda}
into (
4.1
), we have
\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{p_{0}}
, which means that
\lambda^{-d/p}\lesssim\lambda^{-d/p_{0}}
. Take
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we have
p_{0}\leqslant p
.

(2)

Denote
A_{j}:=\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:k+[-3/4,3/4]^{d}\subseteq D_{j}\right\}
, for
\ell\geqslant 10
, choose
k_{\ell}\in A_{\ell}
, take
f_{\ell}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta(\cdot-k_{\ell})
,
\eta
is just the function in (1). Then we know that supp
{\mathscr{F}}f_{\ell}\subseteq k_{\ell}+[-1/8,1/8]^{d}\subseteq D_{\ell}
, so we have

\displaystyle\Box_{k}f_{\ell}=\begin{cases}f_{\ell},&$k=k_{\ell}$;\\
0,&$k\neq k_{\ell};$\end{cases}\triangle_{j}f_{\ell}=\begin{cases}f_{\ell},&$j=\ell$;\\
\triangle_{j}f_{\ell},&$0<\left\|j-\ell\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant 3$;\\
0,&$\left\|j-\ell\right\|_{\infty}>3.$\end{cases}

Then by Corollary
3.3
,we have

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{M_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{p}\approx 1

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|2^{js}\left\|\triangle_{j}f_{\ell}\right\|_{p_{0}}\right\|_{\ell_{\left\|j-\ell\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant 3}^{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\ell s}\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{p_{0}}\approx 2^{\ell s}.

Then take the estimate above into (
4.1
), we have
1\lesssim 2^{\ell s},\forall\ell\geqslant 10
, so we get
s\geqslant 0
.

(3)

Take
f_{\ell}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\varphi_{\ell}
, then by Corollary
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{M_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}f_{\ell}\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\geqslant\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}f_{\ell}\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k\in A_{\ell}}^{q}}=\left\|\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma_{k}\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k\in A_{\ell}}^{q}}\approx 2^{\ell d/q};

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|2^{js}\left\|\triangle_{j}f_{\ell}\right\|_{p_{0}}\right\|_{\ell_{j}^{q_{0}}}=\left\|2^{js}\left\|\triangle_{j}f_{\ell}\right\|_{p_{0}}\right\|_{\ell_{\left\|j-\ell\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant 3}^{q_{0}}}\leqslant 2^{\ell s}\left\|f_{\ell}\right\|_{p_{0}}\approx 2^{\ell(s+d(1-1/p_{0}))}.

Take the estimates above into (
4.1
), let
\ell\rightarrow\infty
, we have
s\geqslant d(1/p_{0}+1/q-1)
.

(4)

When
p_{0}\geqslant 2
, choose
\eta\in\mathscr{S}
, such that supp

By the same discussion in the proof of Theorem

p=\infty
. Also, when

Sufficiency: by Lemma

Proof of Theorem
6.3

6.3.1
Sufficiency

For condition (1): by Lemma

For condition (2): by Lemma

6.3.2
Necessity

If we have
F_{p_{0},q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
, then we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{F_{p_{0},q}^{s}}.

(i)

By the same method as in subsection
5.1.2
, we can get
p_{0}\leqslant p
.

(ii)

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
B_{p_{0},p_{0}\wedge q}^{s}\hookrightarrow F_{p_{0},q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
, then by Theorem
4.1
, we have
s\geqslant\tau(p_{0},q)

(iii)

When
p_{0}>q\geqslant 2
, by Lemma
2.2
, we have
W^{s,p_{0}}\approx F_{p_{0},2}^{s}\hookrightarrow F_{p_{0},q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
, then by Theorem

(iv)

When

(v)

We prove that

7
Embedding between Fourier
L^{p}
spaces and modulation spaces

In this section, we consider the embedding
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
. Because of the orthogonality of
\Box_{k}(\sigma_{k})
, we can calculate the
{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
norm easily. Then, we can get the result as follow. Recall that when
0<p<1
, we denote
p^{\prime}=\infty
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
0<p,q,r\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then we have
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

p\leqslant 2,q\leqslant r\leqslant p^{\prime},s\geqslant 0
;

(2)

p\leqslant 2,r\leqslant p^{\prime},r<q,s>d(1/r-1/q)
.

As for the other side of this kind of embedding, we also have:

Theorem 7.2
.

Let Let
0<p,q,r\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then we have
{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s}
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

p\geqslant 2,p^{\prime}\leqslant r\leqslant q,s\leqslant 0
;

(2)

p\geqslant 2,p^{\prime}\leqslant r,r>q,s<d(1/r-1/q)
.

7.1
Proof of Theorem
7.1

7.1.1
Sufficiency

If we have condition (1), when
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, by the orthogonality of
\sigma_{k}
, H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality of
L^{p}
spaces and Hausdorff-Young’s inequality of Fourier transform, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{r}\approx\left\|\left\|\sigma_{k}\hat{f}\right\|_{r}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{r}}\leqslant\left\|\left\|\sigma_{k}\hat{f}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{r}}\leqslant\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{r}}\leqslant\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}=\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}.

When
0<p<1
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have
\left\|\sigma_{k}\hat{f}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{1}\leqslant C\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{p}
, then by the same estimates we can get the result as desired.

If we have condition (2), by Lemma
2.1
, we have
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,r}
, then by the sufficiency of condition(1), we have
M_{p,r}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
.

7.1.2
Necessity

If we have
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
, then we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}.

The conditions we need are contained the following cases.

(i)

For any
f\in\mathscr{S}
with supp
\hat{f}\subseteq[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
, in this case we have
\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}\approx\left\|f\right\|_{p}
. So, (
7.1
) means that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Then by Hausdorff-Young’s inequality on compact set (see Tao’s note of Math 254B, one can extend the result to the case of
0<p<1
with the same proof), we have
r\leqslant p^{\prime},p\leqslant 2
.

(ii)

For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, take
f(x)=e^{ikx}\eta(x)
, where
\eta\in\mathscr{S},
supp
\hat{\eta}\subseteq[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
, then we know that supp
\hat{f}\subseteq k+[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
. So, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\left\|\eta\right\|_{p}\approx\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s};\ \ \left\|f\right\|_{{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}}=\left\|\hat{\eta}\right\|_{r}\approx 1.

Take the estimates into (
7.1
), we have
1\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}
, which means that
s\geqslant 0
, which is the result in condition (1).

(iii)

When
r<q
, take
f(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{k}e^{ikx}\eta(x)
,
\eta
is the same as case (ii). Then we have
\hat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{k}\hat{\eta}(\xi-k)
.
So, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}\approx\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}};\ \ \left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{r}\approx\left\|a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{r}}.

Take the estimates above into (
7.1
), we have
\left\|a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{r}}\lesssim\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}
, then we have
\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-sr}b_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{1}}\lesssim\left\|b_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q/r}}
, which means that
\left\{\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-sr}\right\}\in\ell^{(q/r)^{\prime}}
. So, we have
sr(q/r)^{\prime}>d
, i.e.
s>d(1/r-1/q)
, which is the result in condition (2).

Remark 7.3
.

The proof of Theorem
7.2
is similar to the proof above, we omit it here.

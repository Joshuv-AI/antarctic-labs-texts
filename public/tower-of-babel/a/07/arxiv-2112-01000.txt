A remark on uniform Strichartz estimate for quantum walks on 1D lattice
Takumi Aso, Masaya Maeda

Abstract
In this short note, we study quantum walks (QWs) on one dimensional lattice δ ​ ℤ \delta\mathbb{Z} . Following Hong-Yang [ 3 ] , we prove Strichartz estimates for QWs independent of the lattice width δ \delta .

Introduction

Discrete time quantum walks, or quantum walks (QWs) in short, are space-time discrete unitary dynamical systems first appeared as the space-time discretization of
1+1
dimensional Dirac equation related to Feynman path integral
[
2
]
.
From the end of 20th century, QWs started to attract interest of researchers in many fields of mathematics and physics, see
[
5
,
11
]
and reference therein.
Also, for interesting discussion about the generalization of Quantum walks, see
[
12
,
13
]
.

The purpose of this note is to combine the the study of QWs as dispersive equation
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and the study of the continuous limit of QWs, see
[
9
]
and reference therein.
In particular, we study the Strichartz estimate of QWs in the continuous limit setting and obtain “uniform” Strichartz estimates, where “uniform” means that the inequality is independent of lattice size
\delta>0
.

To state our result precisely, we prepare several notations.
First, Pauli matrices are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \sigma_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\ \sigma_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-{\rm i}\\
{\rm i}&0\end{pmatrix},\ \sigma_{3}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(1.1)

The “shift operator”
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}_{\delta}u\right)(x):=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}(x-\delta)\\
u_{2}(x+\delta)\end{pmatrix},\ \text{for}\ u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix},

and “coin operator”
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{C}_{\delta}u\right)(x):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\delta m)&-{\rm i}\sin(\delta m)\\
-{\rm i}\sin(\delta m)&\cos(\delta m)\end{pmatrix}u(x)=e^{-{\rm i}\delta m\sigma_{1}}u(x).

where
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant and
u={}^{t}\!(u_{1}\ u_{2}):\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Given the shift and coin operators, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{S}_{\delta}\mathcal{C}_{\delta},\quad U_{\delta}(t)u:=\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{t/\delta}u,\ t\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}.

(1.2)

Remark 1.1
.

We can consider more general families of coin operators.
However, for the simplicity of exposition, we chose to consider only the above family of coin operators.

Remark 1.2
.

The definition of
U_{\delta}(t)
, in particular the reason we take
t\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}
and
U_{\delta}(t):=\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{t/\delta}
instead of taking
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
U_{\delta}(t):=\mathcal{U}_{\delta}^{t}
,
is inspired by the continuous limit.
Indeed, formally
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}\sim 1-\delta\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}
and
C_{\delta}\sim 1-{\rm i}\delta m\sigma_{1}
, we see
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}\sim 1-\delta(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+{\rm i}m\sigma_{1})
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(t)\sim{\rm i}\delta^{-1}(u(t+\delta)-u(t))={\rm i}\delta^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}u(t)-u(t))\sim-{\rm i}\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}u(t)+m\sigma_{1}u(t),

which is a
1+1
dimensional (free) Dirac equation up to
O(\delta)
error.

We further define several notations to state our result.

•

\left\langle a\right\rangle:=(1+|a|^{2})^{1/2}
.

•

For
u={}^{t}\!(u_{1}\ u_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we set
\|u\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}:=\left(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

•

For
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, we set

\displaystyle l^{p}_{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\left\{u:\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\ |\ \|u\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}}:=\left(\delta\sum_{x\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}\|u(x)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f:\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\times\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\ |\ \|f\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}}:=\left(\sum_{t\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta\left(\sum_{x\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta\|f(t,x)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}^{q}\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}\right\},

with the standard modification for the cases
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.

•

We define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}
and the inverse Fourier transform by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\delta}u=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{x\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{-{\rm i}x\xi}u(x),\quad\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{-1}v=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\pi/\delta}^{\pi/\delta}e^{{\rm i}x\xi}v(\xi)\,d\xi.

•

Given
p:[-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta]\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
p(\mathcal{D}_{\delta})
by

\left(p(\mathcal{D}_{\delta})u\right)(x):=\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{-1}\left(p(\xi)\left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta}u\right)(\xi)\right)(x).

•

We say
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty]
is an admissible pair if
3p^{-1}+q^{-1}=2^{-1}
.
We say
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty]
is a continuous admissible pair if
2p^{-1}+q^{-1}=2^{-1}
.

•

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote the
p^{\prime}
will mean the Hölder conjugate of
p
, i.e.
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

•

We write
a\lesssim b
by meaning
a\leq Cb
where
C>0
is a constant independent of quantities we are concerning.
In particular, in this note, the implicit constant never depends on
\delta
.
If
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
then we write
a\sim b
.

We are now in the position to state our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
u:\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
f:\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\times\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Let
(p,q)
and
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
be admissible pairs.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)u\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\mathcal{D}_{\delta}|^{1/p}\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\right\rangle^{3/p}u\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|\sum_{s\in[0,t]\cap\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}U_{\delta}(t-s)f\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\mathcal{D}_{\delta}|^{1/p+1/\tilde{p}}\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\right\rangle^{3/p+3/\tilde{p}}f\|_{l^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}_{\delta}l^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{\delta}}.

(1.4)

We compare our result with the known results by Hong-Yang
[
3
]
who proved similar result for discrete Schrödinger equations and discrete Klein-Gordon equations.
Here, we briefly recall the results for discrete Schrödinger equations.
First, discrete Schrödinger equations on
1
D lattice
\delta{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(x)=-\Delta_{\delta}u(x):=\delta^{-2}(2u(x)-u(x-\delta)-u(x+\delta)),\ u:{\mathbb{R}}\times\delta{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}.

For fixed
\delta
, the Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{{\rm i}t\Delta_{\delta}}u\|_{L^{p}l^{q}_{\delta}}\leq C_{\delta}\|u_{0}\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}},\ (p,q)\text{ is admissible},

was proved by Stefanov-Kevrekidis
[
14
]
where the constant
C_{\delta}=C\delta^{-1/p}
blows up as
\delta\to 0
.
In general, one expect that solutions of discretized equation converge to the solutions of the original equation.
This is true for Schrödinger equation for fixed time interval.
However, as we saw above, the Strichartz estimate for discrete Schrödinger equation, which control the global behavior of solutions, do not converge to the Strichartz estimate of the continous Schrödinger equation.
This is because of the lattice resonance, which occurs because the dispersive relation for a equation of lattice always have degeneracy.
Indeed, since the dispersive relation for an equation on
\delta{\mathbb{Z}}
is a function
p_{\delta}(\xi)
on a torus, if
p_{\delta}
is smooth, then there exists a point such that
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=0
.
Also, we can observe the difference between discrete and continous Strichartz estimate from the difference of admissible pair.

Hong-Yang observed that the difference of the scale can be absorbed by fractal difference operator
|\mathcal{D}_{\delta}|^{1/p}
and obtained the result:

\displaystyle\|e^{{\rm i}t\Delta_{\delta}}u\|_{L^{p}l^{q}_{\delta}}\lesssim\||\mathcal{D}_{\delta}|^{1/p}u_{0}\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}},\ (p,q)\text{ is admissible}.

This estimate is compatible with the estimate for the continuous Schrödinger equations.
Hong-Yang also prove similar estimates for discrete Klein-Gordon equations.

We now discuss QWs.
In
[
7
]
, the Strichartz estimate for QWs was given by

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)u\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}}\lesssim C_{\delta}\|u\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}},\quad\ (p,q)\text{ is admissible},

(1.5)

where
C_{\delta}\to\infty
as
\delta\to 0
.
Since QWs are space-time discrete Dirac equations, we compare (
1.5
) with the the Strichartz estimates of the spacetime continuous Dirac equations:

\displaystyle\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\|\left\langle\partial_{x}\right\rangle^{3/p}u\|_{L^{2}},\quad(p,q)\text{ is continous admissible},,

where
H=-{\rm i}\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+m\sigma_{1}
(for the proof see
[
10
]
and reference therein).
To make
(p,q)
to be admissible, using Sobolev inequality we have

\displaystyle\|e^{{\rm i}tH}u\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim\||\partial_{x}|^{1/p}\left\langle\partial_{x}\right\rangle^{3/p}u\|_{L^{2}},\quad\ (p,q)\text{ is admissible}.

We now see that estimate (
1.3
) is compatible with the above estimate of Dirac equation.
For the inhomogeneous estimate (
1.4
) there is also the similar correspondence.

In the next section, we prove Theorem
1.3
following Hong-Yang
[
3
]
.
There are two difference between the discrete Schrödinger equation treated in
[
3
]
and QWs.
The first is that discrete Schrödinger/Klein-Gordon equations are discretized only in space, while QWs are discretized in spacetime.
The second difference is that QWs have more complicated dispersive relation than discrete Schrödinger/Klein-Gordon equations.
For the first problem we follow
[
7
]
.
For the 2nd problem, we carefully estimate the dispersive relation for low and high frequency region separately to get the optimal result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
satisfy
\chi_{[-1,1]}(x)\leq\phi(x)\leq\chi_{[-2,2]}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
\lambda>0
, we set
\psi_{\delta,\lambda}\in C^{\infty}([-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta])
by
\psi_{\delta,\lambda}(\xi):=\psi(\xi/\lambda)
where
\psi(x):=\phi(x)-\phi(2x)
.
We note that since
\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\delta,\lambda}\subset\left([-2\lambda,-\lambda/2]\cup[\lambda/2,2\lambda]\right)\cap[-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta]
, we have
\psi_{\delta,\lambda}=0
a.e. if
\lambda\geq 2\pi/\delta
and for
\lambda<2\pi/\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\delta,\lambda}\ \Rightarrow\ |\xi|\sim\lambda.

Using this
\psi_{\delta,\lambda}
, we define the Littlewood-Paley projection operators by

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}:=P_{\delta,\lambda}:=\psi_{\delta,\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\delta}).

(2.1)

We further set
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
to satisfy
0\not\in\mathrm{supp}\tilde{\psi}
and
\tilde{\psi}(\xi)=1
for
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\psi
and set
\tilde{\psi}_{\delta,\lambda}
and
\tilde{P}_{N}
as
\tilde{\psi}_{\delta,\lambda}(\xi)=\psi(\xi/\lambda)
and (
2.1
).
In particular, we have
P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\tilde{P}_{\lambda}
.

The main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
\lambda\in(0,2\pi/\delta)
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{\infty}_{\delta}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/3}\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle t^{-1/3}\|u\|_{l^{1}_{\delta}}.

(2.2)

Proof.

As in
[
7
]
, by Fourier transformation, we have

\displaystyle U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u_{0}(x)=\sum_{\mathfrak{s}\in\{\pm\}}\left(I_{\delta,\lambda,\mathfrak{s}}*u\right)(x),

(2.3)

where
I*u(x):=h\sum_{x\in\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}I(x-y)u(y)
and

\displaystyle I_{\delta,\lambda,\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/\delta}^{\pi/\delta}e^{{\rm i}\left(\mathfrak{s}p_{\delta}(\xi)t+x\xi\right)}Q_{\delta,\mathfrak{s}}(\xi)\psi_{\delta,\lambda}(\xi)\,d\xi,

(2.4)

\displaystyle p_{\delta}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\delta^{-1}\mathrm{arccos}\left(\cos(\delta m)\cos(\delta\xi)\right),

(2.5)

where
Q_{\delta,\pm}
are
2\times 2
matrices, which can be computed explicitly.

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that

p_{\delta}(\xi)=\sqrt{m^{2}+\xi^{2}}+O(\delta)

for fixed
\xi
, which tells us that QWs have the dispersion relation similar to Dirac equations (and Klein-Gordon equations) in the continuous limit.

For
\mathfrak{s}=\pm
,
i,j=1,2
, since
Q_{\delta,\pm}
depends on
\xi
only through
\delta\xi
,
by
\|f(\delta\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}([-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta])}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi])}
and
\|\left(f(\delta\xi)\right)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}([-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta])}=\|\delta f^{\prime}(\delta\xi)\|_{L^{1}([-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta])}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])}
, we see that

\displaystyle\|Q_{\delta,\mathfrak{s},i,j}\|_{L^{\infty}[-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta]}+\|Q_{\delta,\mathfrak{s},i,j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}[-\pi/\delta,\pi/\delta]}\lesssim 1,

(2.6)

where
Q_{\delta,\mathfrak{s},i,j}
is the
i,j
component of
Q_{\delta,\mathfrak{s}}
.
Since we are intending to use van der Corput Lemma, we record the derivatives of
p_{\delta}
:

\displaystyle p_{\delta}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos(\delta m)\sin(\delta\xi)}{(1-\cos^{2}(\delta m)\cos^{2}(\delta\xi))^{1/2}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\cos(\delta m)\sin^{2}(\delta m)\cos(\delta\xi)}{(1-\cos^{2}(\delta m)\cos^{2}(\delta\xi))^{3/2}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{2}\cos(\delta m)\sin^{2}(\delta m)(1+2\cos^{2}(\delta m)\cos^{2}(\delta\xi))\sin(\delta\xi)}{(1-\cos^{2}(\delta m)\cos^{2}(\delta\xi))^{5/2}},.

(2.9)

To prove (
2.2
), we consider two cases
\lambda\leq 1/2\delta
and
1/2\delta<\lambda
.
First, if
\lambda\leq 1/2\delta
, then by the elementary inequality
\sqrt{1-a^{2}}\leq\cos a
for
|a|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle 1-\cos^{2}(\delta m)\cos^{2}(\delta\xi)\leq\delta^{2}m^{2}+\delta^{2}\xi^{2}\lesssim\delta^{2}\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{2},

because
|\delta\xi|\leq 1
for
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\delta,\lambda}
due to the constraint of
\lambda
.
Thus, we have
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\gtrsim\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{-3}
for
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\delta,\lambda}
and by Young’s convolution inequality, van der Corput lemma and the estimate (
2.6
) combined with the expression (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{3/2}t^{-1/2}\|u\|_{l^{1}_{\delta}}.

(2.10)

Combining (
2.10
) with the trivial
l^{2}
conservation

\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{2}}=\|P_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}}\lesssim\|u\|_{l^{2}_{\delta}},

we have by Riesz-Thorin interpolation (see e.g.
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{q}}\lesssim\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{3(1/2-1/q)}t^{-(1/2-1/q)}\|u\|_{l_{\delta}^{q^{\prime}}},

(2.11)

for
q\geq 2
.
Thus, by Bernstein’s inequality (see Lemma 2.3 of
[
3
]
) and (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{\infty}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{P}_{\lambda}U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}\tilde{P}_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{\infty}_{\delta}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/6}\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}\tilde{P}_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{6}_{\delta}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/6}\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle t^{-1/3}\|\tilde{P}_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/3}\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle t^{-1/3}\|u\|_{l^{1}_{\delta}}.

(2.12)

This gives (
2.2
) for the first case.

Next, we consider the case
1/2\delta<\lambda<2\pi/\delta
.
In this case, since
\lambda\sim\delta^{-1}
, it suffices to prove (
2.2
) with
\lambda
replaced by
\delta^{-1}
.
Since the
\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\delta,\lambda}
can contain both
\pm\pi/2\delta
(where
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}
degenerates) and
\pm\pi/\delta
(where
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime\prime}
degenerates), we split the integral (
2.4
) into two regions containing only one of
\pm\pi/\delta
and
\pm\pi/2\delta
by smooth cutoff.
In the region which do not contain
\pm\pi/\delta
(and note that because of the constraint of
\lambda
), it will not contain neither
0
, we have
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi)\gtrsim\delta^{4}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left(\delta^{4}t\right)^{-1/3},

(2.13)

by van der Corput lemma, which gives us (
2.2
) in this case.
In the region which contains
\pm\pi/\delta
but not
\pm\pi/2\delta
, we have
p_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\gtrsim\delta^{3}
so again by van der Corput lemma, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l_{\delta}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left(\delta^{3}t\right)^{-1/2}.

Repeating the argument of (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) replacing
\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle
by
\delta^{-1}
we also have (
2.13
).
This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By standard
TT^{*}
argument (see e.g.
[
4
]
for general cases and
[
7
]
for discrete setting) we obtain

\displaystyle\|U_{\delta}(t)P_{\lambda}u\|_{l^{p}_{\delta}l^{q}_{\delta}}\lesssim\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{3/p}\lambda^{1/p}\|u\|_{l_{\delta}^{2}},\quad\|\sum_{s\in[0,t]\cap\delta{\mathbb{Z}}}U_{\delta}(t-s)P_{\lambda}f(s)\|_{l^{p}l^{q}}\lesssim\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle^{3/p+3/\tilde{p}}\lambda^{1/p+1/\tilde{p}}\|f\|_{l_{\delta}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}l_{\delta}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}},

for any admissible pairs
(p,q)
and
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
.
Here, we note that the implicit constants are independent of
\lambda
and
\delta
.
Finally, arguing as in Proof of Theorem 1.3 of
[
3
]
, we have the desired estimates.

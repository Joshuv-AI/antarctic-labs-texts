Abstract
. In this work we study the existence of surjective Nash maps between two given semialgebraic sets 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} and 𝒯 {\mathcal{T}} . Some key ingredients are: the irreducible components 𝒮 i ∗ {\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*} of 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} (and their intersections), the analytic-path connected components 𝒯 j {\mathcal{T}}_{j} of 𝒯 {\mathcal{T}} (and their intersections) and the relations between dimensions of the semialgebraic sets 𝒮 i ∗ {\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*} and 𝒯 j {\mathcal{T}}_{j} . A first step to approach the previous problem is the former characterization done by the second author of the images of affine spaces under Nash maps. The core result of this article to obtain a criterion to decide about the existence of surjective Nash maps between two semialgebraic sets is: a full characterization of the semialgebraic subsets 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} that are the image of the closed unit ball ℬ ¯ m \overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{m} of ℝ m {\mathbb{R}}^{m} centered at the origin under a Nash map f : ℝ m → ℝ n f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n} . The necessary and sufficient conditions that must satisfy such a semialgebraic set 𝒮 {\mathcal{S}} are: it is compact, connected by analytic paths and has dimension d ≤ m d\leq m . Two remarkable consequences of the latter result are the following: (1) pure dimensional compact irreducible arc-symmetric semialgebraic sets of dimension d d are Nash images of ℬ ¯ d \overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d} , and (2) compact semialgebraic sets of dimension d d are projections of non-singular algebraic sets of dimension d d whose connected components are Nash diffeomorphic to spheres (maybe of different dimensions) .

1.
Introduction

Although it is usually said that the first work in Real Geometry is due to Harnack
[
Hr
]
, who obtained an upper bound for the number of connected components of a non-singular real algebraic curve in terms of its genus, modern Real Algebraic Geometry was born with Tarski’s article
[
T
]
, where it is proved that a projection of a semialgebraic set is a semialgebraic set.

A subset
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
semialgebraic when it admits a description in terms of a finite boolean combination of polynomial equalities and inequalities, which we will call a semialgebraic description. A Nash manifold
M\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a semialgebraic set that is a smooth submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The category of semialgebraic sets is closed under basic boolean operations but also under usual topological operations: taking closures (denoted by
\operatorname{Cl}(\cdot)
), interiors (denoted by
\operatorname{Int}(\cdot)
), connected components, etc. If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are semialgebraic sets, a map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
is semialgebraic if its graph is a semialgebraic set.

A relevant class of (continuous) semialgebraic maps is that of Nash maps, that is, smooth maps on a semialgebraic neighborhood of
{\mathcal{S}}
that are in addition semialgebraic. More precisely, a Nash map on an open semialgebraic set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a semialgebraic smooth map on
f:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Given a semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, a Nash map on
{\mathcal{S}}
is the restriction to
{\mathcal{S}}
of a Nash map on an open semialgebraic neighborhood
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
. These Nash maps include: polynomial and regular maps. A map
f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is polynomial if its components
f_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]:={\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{m}]
are polynomials. Analogously,
f
is regular if its components can be represented as quotients
f_{k}=\frac{g_{k}}{h_{k}}
of two polynomials
g_{k},h_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}]
such that
h_{k}
never vanishes on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

By Tarski’s Theorem the image of a semialgebraic set under a semialgebraic map is a semialgebraic set, because it is a projection of a semialgebraic set. We are interested in studying ‘inverse type problems’ to Tarski’s result. A first enlightening example is the following:

Problem 1.1

(Bärchen-Schäfchen Problem)
.

Is it possible to transform a semialgebraic Teddy bear
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
onto a semialgebraic sheep
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
by means of a Nash map and/or viceversa?

Figure 1.1
.
Semialgebraic Teddy bear and semialgebraic Sheep (figures borrowed from
[
FU6
, Fig.1.3]
).

The general problem is the following:

Problem 1.2

(Tarski’s inverse problem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be semialgebraic sets. Under which conditions does a surjective Nash map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
exist?

1.1.
State of the art

In the 1990 Oberwolfach reelle algebraische Geometrie week

Problem 1.3

Characterize the (semialgebraic) subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are either polynomial or regular images of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

The previous problem pretends to characterize the semialgebraic sets that admit affine spaces as a kind of ‘algebraic models’. During the last two decades we have attempted to better understand polynomial and regular images of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

•

To find obstructions to be either polynomial or regular images.

•

To (constructively) prove that large families of semialgebraic sets with piecewise linear boundary (convex polyhedra, their interiors, complements and the interiors of their complements) are either polynomial or regular images of affine spaces.

]

[

1.1.1.
First alternative approach.

The rigidity of polynomial and regular maps on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
makes it difficult to approach Problem
1.3
in its full generality. A first possibility to overcome this is to change the domain of definition (using, for instance, closed unit balls or unit spheres). When considering compact domains (and of course compact images), one has more tools and Weierstrass’ polynomial approximation plays an important role. In
[
KPS
, §5.Prob.1]
the following concrete related problem was proposed:

Problem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an arbitrary (compact) convex polygon in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Construct explicit polynomials
f
and
g
in
{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt u},{\tt v},{\tt w}]
such that
{\mathcal{P}}=(f,g)(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{3})
.

A first main result in
[
FU6
]
provides a positive answer to a natural strong generalization to arbitrary dimension of Problem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
FU6
, Thm.1.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be the union of a finite family of
n
-dimensional convex (compact) polyhedra. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths.

(ii)

There exists a polynomial map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{n})={\mathcal{S}}
.

The techniques involved to prove Theorem
1.5
are generalized in
[
FU4
]
to show Theorem
1.6
below. A set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is strictly radially convex (with respect to a point
p\in\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
) if for each ray
\ell
with origin at
p
, the intersection
\ell\cap{\mathcal{S}}
is a segment whose relative interior is contained in
\operatorname{Int}({\mathcal{S}})
. Convex sets are particular examples of strictly radially convex sets (with respect to any of its interior points
[
Be
, Lem.11.2.4]
).

Theorem 1.6

(
[
FU6
, Thm.1.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be the union of a finite family of strictly radially convex semialgebraic sets that are polynomial images of the closed unit ball
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{m}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths.

(ii)

There exists a polynomial map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{m+1})={\mathcal{S}}

1.1.2.
Second alternative approach.

Another possibility is to change the polynomial and regular maps by more flexible maps like Nash maps (smooth semialgebraic maps)
[
Fe2
]
or regulous maps (continuous rational maps)
[
FFQU
]
. Gamboa and Shiota proposed in 1990 to approach the following variant of Problem
1.3
in the same line as Problem
1.1
.

Problem 1.7
.

Characterize the (semialgebraic) subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are Nash images of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

The set
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
of regular points of a semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined as follows. Let
X
be the Zariski closure of
{\mathcal{S}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\widetilde{X}
the complexification of
X
, that is, the smallest complex algebraic subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
that contains
X
. Define
\operatorname{Reg}(X):=X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{X})
and let
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
be the interior of
{\mathcal{S}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde{X})
in
\operatorname{Reg}(X)
. Observe that
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
is a finite union of disjoint Nash manifolds maybe of different dimensions. We refer the reader to
[
Fe2
, §2.A]
for further details concerning the set of regular points of a semialgebraic set. In 1990 Shiota proposed the following conjecture in order to provide a satisfactory answer to Problem
1.7
.

Conjecture 1.8

(Shiota)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set of dimension
d
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if and only if
{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional and there exists an analytic path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all connected components of the set of regular points of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

In
[
Fe2
]
we provided a proof for Shiota’s conjecture as a particular case of the following characterization of the semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of dimension
d
that are images of affine spaces under Nash maps.

Theorem 1.9

(Nash images
[
Fe2
, Main Thm.1.4]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set of dimension
d
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
for some
m\geq d
.

(iii)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by Nash paths.

(iv)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths.

(v)

{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional and there exists a Nash path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all the connected components of the set of regular points of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

(vi)

{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional and there exists an analytic path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all the connected components of the set of regular points of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

1.1.3.
Analytic path-connected components of a semialgebraic set

In order to present the main results of this article, we recall the concept of analytic path-connected components of a semialgebraic set introduced in
[
Fe2
, §9]
.

Definition 1.10
.

A semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
admits a decomposition into
analytic path-connected components
if there exist semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
such that:

(i)

Each
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
is connected by analytic paths.

(ii)

If
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
is a semialgebraic set connected by analytic paths that contains
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}={\mathcal{T}}
.

(iii)

{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\not\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}S_{j}
.

(iv)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
.

In
[
Fe2
, Thm.9.2]
the existence and uniqueness of the analytic path-connected components of a semialgebraic set is shown.

Theorem 1.11

(
[
Fe2
, Thm.9.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semialgebraic set. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
admits a decomposition into analytic path-connected components and this decomposition is unique. In addition, the analytic path-connected components of a semialgebraic set are closed in
{\mathcal{S}}
.

1.1.4.
Irreducibility and irreducible components of a semialgebraic set

The ring
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{S}})
of Nash functions on a semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a noetherian ring
[
FG3
, Thm.2.9]
and we say that
{\mathcal{S}}
is irreducible if and only if
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{S}})
is an integral domain
[
FG3
]
. We next recall the concept of irreducible components of a semi-algebraic set.

Definition 1.12
.

A semi-algebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
admits a decomposition into
irreducible components
if there exist semi-algebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
such that:

(i)

Each
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
is irreducible.

(ii)

If
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
is an irreducible semi-algebraic set that contains
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}={\mathcal{T}}
.

(iii)

{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\not\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}S_{j}
.

(iv)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
.

In
[
FG3
, Thm.4.3, Rmk.4.4]
the following result is proposed concerning the irreducible components of a semi-algebraic set.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a semi-algebraic set. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
admits a decomposition into irreducible components and this decomposition is unique. In addition, the irreducible components of a semi-algebraic set are closed in
{\mathcal{S}}
.

1.2.
Main results

Our purpose in this work is to approach Problem
1.2
(combining somehow the alternative approaches to Problem
1.3
suggested above). The image of a semialgebraic set connected by analytic paths under a Nash map is connected by analytic paths as a consequence of Theorem
1.9
. In addition, the image of an irreducible semialgebraic set under a Nash map is an irreducible semialgebraic set
[
FG3
, §3.1]
. Consequently, as we will explain in detail later, obstructions to construct a surjective Nash map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
between semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
concentrate on the configuration of the intersections of pairwise different analytic path-connected components
\{{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
(resp. irreducible components
\{{\mathcal{S}}_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{\ell}
) of
{\mathcal{S}}
and the configuration of their images, which are semialgebraic subsets
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}:=f({\mathcal{S}}_{i})
of
{\mathcal{T}}
connected by analytic paths (resp. irreducible semialgebraic subsets
{\mathcal{T}}_{j}^{*}:=f({\mathcal{S}}_{j}^{*})
of
{\mathcal{T}}
).

1.2.1.
General surjective Nash maps

If
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a semialgebraic set of dimension
d
, the set
{\mathcal{S}}^{(e)}
of points of
{\mathcal{S}}
of dimension
e
is a semialgebraic subset of
{\mathcal{S}}
, which is closed (in
{\mathcal{S}}
) if
d=e
. In order to soften the quoted obstructions above, we assume that each irreducible component
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
is mapped onto a semialgebraic subset
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
connected by analytic paths and that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{T}}_{i}\neq\varnothing
. Under these assumptions we propose the following solution to Problem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.14

(Surjective Nash maps)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be semialgebraic sets,
\{{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{r}
the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
\{{\mathcal{T}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
a family of (non-necessarily distinct) semialgebraic subsets of
{\mathcal{T}}
connected by analytic paths such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{T}}_{i}\neq\varnothing
and
{\mathcal{T}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
. Denote
d_{i}:=\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
and assume that the set
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
of points of
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
of dimension
d_{i}
is non-compact if
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
is non-compact for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then there exists a surjective Nash map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
such that
f({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})={\mathcal{T}}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
if and only if
e_{i}:=\dim({\mathcal{T}}_{i})\leq\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})=:d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

1.2.2.
Key results

The proof of Theorem
1.14
strongly relies on the following two results. The first of them provides a positive answer to Problem
1.1
, whereas the second is the counterpart for the non-compact case.

Theorem 1.15

(Bärchen-Schäfchen’s Theorem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic set of dimension
d
and
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
a compact semialgebraic set connected by analytic paths of dimension
e\leq d
. Then there exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{T}}
.

The previous result shows that any semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of dimension
d\geq e
is a model to represent any compact semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
connected by analytic paths of dimension
e
as a Nash image. In particular, a semialgebraic sheep is a Nash image of a semialgebraic Teddy bear and viceversa (see Problem
1.1
).

Theorem 1.16

(Non-compact case)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic set such that
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}^{(d)})\cap{\mathcal{S}}
is non-compact for some
d\geq 2
and let
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be semialgebraic set connected by analytic paths. If
{\mathcal{T}}
has dimension
e\leq d
, there exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{T}}
.

Remark 1.17
.

The converse of the previous result is also true. Suppose that
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}^{(d)})\cap{\mathcal{S}}
is compact for each
d\geq e
. Define
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}:=\bigcup_{d\geq e}(\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}^{(d)})\cap{\mathcal{S}})
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}:=\bigcup_{d<e}(\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}^{(d)})\cap{\mathcal{S}})
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
is compact,
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{2})<e
and
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
. Suppose there exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{T}}
. Then
{\mathcal{T}}=f({\mathcal{S}}_{1})\cup f({\mathcal{S}}_{2})
, where
f({\mathcal{S}}_{1})
is compact and
f({\mathcal{S}}_{2})
has dimension
e^{\prime}<e
. This contradicts the fact that
{\mathcal{T}}
is a non-compact semialgebraic set of dimension
d
.

The previous result (together with the corresponding remark) determines the semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of dimension
d\geq e
that are models to represent a non-compact semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
connected by analytic paths of dimension
e
as a Nash image.

1.2.3.
Nash images of the closed unit ball

The core results to prove Theorems
1.15
and
1.16
are Theorem
1.9
already proved in
[
Fe2
]
and Theorem
1.19
where we characterize the compact semialgebraic sets
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are images of closed unit balls under Nash maps. This result provides a full answer to the natural counterpart to Problem
1.7
for the compact case:

Problem 1.18
.

Characterize the (compact semialgebraic) subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that are Nash images of the closed unit ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

The statement of Theorem
1.9
does not take into account whether
{\mathcal{S}}
is compact or not and the involved Nash maps are rarely proper if
d\geq 2
. As closed unit balls
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
are compact, the restrictions to
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
of the involved Nash maps are always proper maps.

Theorem 1.19

(Compact Nash images)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a
d
-dimensional compact semialgebraic set. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d})={\mathcal{S}}
.

(ii)

There exists a Nash map
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
f(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{m})={\mathcal{S}}
for some
m\geq d
.

(iii)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by Nash paths.

(iv)

{\mathcal{S}}
is connected by analytic paths.

(v)

{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional and there exists a Nash path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all the connected components of
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
.

(vi)

{\mathcal{S}}
is pure dimensional and there exists an analytic path
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}
whose image meets all the connected components of
\operatorname{Reg}({\mathcal{S}})
.

In Subsection
2.1
we will show that we can take ‘equivalent’ compact models to the closed unit ball to represent compact semialgebraic sets as their images under Nash maps. The technicalities of the constructions we develop in this article make the simplicial prism the suitable model to squeeze. In Subsection
3.1
we separately treat the
1
-dimensional case and we characterize
1
-dimensional Nash images of affine spaces in terms of their irreducibility.

Proposition 1.20

(The
1
-dimensional case)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a
1
-dimensional compact semialgebraic set. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of some
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{m}
if and only if
{\mathcal{S}}
is irreducible. In addition, if such is the case,
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of the compact interval
[-1,1]
.

1.3.
Two consequences

We next present two additional consequences of Theorem
1.19
.

1.3.1.
Representation of arc-symmetric compact semialgebraic sets

Arc-symmetric semialgebraic sets were introduced by Kurdyka in
[
K
]
and subsequently studied by many authors. Recall that a semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is arc-symmetric if for each analytic arc
\gamma:(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\gamma((-1,0))\subset{\mathcal{S}}
it holds that
\gamma((-1,1))\subset{\mathcal{S}}
. In particular arc-symmetric semialgebraic sets are closed subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. An arc-symmetric semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is irreducible if it cannot be written as the union of two proper arc-symmetric semialgebraic subsets
[
K
, §2]
. It follows from Theorem
1.19
and
[
K
, Cor.2.8]
that a pure dimensional irreducible compact arc-symmetric semialgebraic set is a Nash image of
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
where
d:=\dim({\mathcal{S}})
.

Corollary 1.21
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a pure dimensional irreducible compact arc-symmetric semialgebraic set of dimension
d
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Nash image of
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
.

1.3.2.
Elimination of inequalities

Another converse problem to Tarski’s Theorem is to find an algebraic set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
whose projection is a given semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. This is known as the problem of eliminating inequalities. Motzkin proved in
[
Mo
]
that this problem always has a solution for
k=1
. However, his solution is rather complicated and in general is a reducible algebraic set. Andradas–Gamboa proved in
[
AG1
,
AG2
]
that if
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a closed semialgebraic set whose Zariski closure is irreducible, then
{\mathcal{S}}
is the projection of an irreducible algebraic set in some
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
. Pecker
[
P
]
provides some improvements on both results: for the first one by finding a construction of an algebraic set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
that projects onto the given semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, far simpler than the original construction of Motzkin; for the second one by proving that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is a locally closed semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with an interior point, then
{\mathcal{S}}
is the projection of an irreducible algebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.

In
[
Fe2
]
it is proved that each semialgebraic set
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the projection of a non-singular algebraic set
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
whose connected components are Nash diffeomorphic to affine spaces (maybe of different dimensions). In this article we improve the previous result when
{\mathcal{S}}
is compact and we prove that there exists a non-singular compact algebraic set
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+k}
that is Nash diffeomorphic to a finite pairwise disjoint union of spheres (maybe of different dimensions) and projects onto
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Corollary 1.22
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a compact semialgebraic set of dimension

. We have:

(i)

If

(for some

(2)

(ii)

Even for dimension

Lemma 2.2
.

The
d
-dimensional closed ball
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
is a polynomial image of the
d
-dimensional simplex
\Delta_{d}

Proof.

As
\Delta_{1}=[0,1]
and
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}=[-1,1]

h({\tt t})=2{\tt t}-1
satisfies
h(\Delta_{1})=[-1,1]

h({\tt t}):={\tt t}^{2}\frac{({\tt t}-2d^{2})^{2(2d^{2}-1)}}{(2d^{2}-1)^{2(2d^{2}-1)}}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt t}]\quad\leadsto\quad h^{\prime}({\tt t})=\frac{4d^{2}{\tt t}({\tt t}-2d^{2})^{2(2d^{2}-1)-1}}{(2d^{2}-1)^{2(2d^{2}-1)}}({\tt t}-1).

It satisfies
h(0)=h(2d^{2})=0
and
h(1)=1
. In addition,

is positive on
(0,1)
and negative on
(1,2d^{2})
, so
h
has a global maximum at
t=1
and it satisfies

on the interval
[0,2d^{2}]
.

Consider the simplex
\Delta^{\prime}_{d}:=\{{\tt x}_{1}\geq-1,\ldots,{\tt x}_{d}\geq-1,{\tt x}_{1}+\cdots+{\tt x}_{d}\leq\sqrt{d}\}
. A tangent hyperplane to
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
(which is the boundary of the closed unit ball
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}

{\tt x}_{1}+\cdots+{\tt x}_{d}=0
if and only if the tangent point
p\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
has all its coordinates equal. As
\Delta^{\prime}_{d}\subset\{{\tt x}_{1}\geq-1,\ldots,{\tt x}_{d}\geq-1\}
, we pick the point

and the tangent hyperplane
\{{\tt x}_{1}+\cdots+{\tt x}_{d}=\sqrt{d}\}
, so

. In addition, we claim:
\Delta^{\prime}_{d}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}(0,\sqrt{2}d)
.

As

is the convex hull of its vertices and
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}(0,\sqrt{2}d)
is convex, it is enough to check that the vertices of
\Delta^{\prime}_{d}
belong to
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}(0,\sqrt{2}d)

are

We have

\|v_{i}\|^{2}=d-1+(\sqrt{d}+d-1)^{2}=d^{2}+2\sqrt{d}(d-1)<2d^{2},

so
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}\subset\Delta^{\prime}_{d}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}(0,\sqrt{2}d)

Observe that
g(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d})=g(\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}(0,\sqrt{2}d))=\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}
, so
g(\Delta^{\prime}_{d})=\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{d}

h(\Delta_{d})=\Delta^{\prime}_{d}

satisfies

, as required.

2.3.
Checkerboard sets

Let
X\subset Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be algebraic sets such that

is non-singular and has dimension

X

{\tt x}_{1}\cdots{\tt x}_{k}=0

. In particular, the irreducible components of

are non-singular and have codimension
1
in

. If

is a semialgebraic set, we write

A pure dimensional semialgebraic set

•

is a non-singular algebraic set.

•

\overline{\partial{\mathcal{T}}}^{\operatorname{zar}}

•

is connected.

Each checkerboard set is connected by analytic paths

)

2.4.
Polynomial paths inside semialgebraic sets

We recall next a smart polynomial curve selection lemma (
[
Fe3
, Thm.1.6]
and
[
FU5
, Lem.3.1]
). It allows to approximate continuous semialgebraic paths inside the closure of an open semialgebraic set by polynomial paths, with strong control on the derivatives. This lemma will be one of the main ingredients in our proof of Theorem
1.19
. In
[
Fe3
, Thm.1.6]
and
[
FU5
, Lem.3.1]
an extended study of polynomial and Nash paths inside the closure of open semialgebraic sets is made. We only need a simplified version of the results obtained in
[
Fe3
, Thm.1.6]
and
[
FU5
, Lem.3.1]
that we state in Lemma
2.8
.

We endow the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
of differentiable functions of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}
on the interval
[a,b]
with the
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}
compact-open topology. Recall that a basis of open neighborhoods of
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
in this topology is constituted by the sets of the type:

{\mathcal{U}}^{\nu}_{g,\varepsilon}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}):\ \|f^{(\ell)}-g^{(\ell)}\|_{[a,b]}<\varepsilon:\ \ell=0,\ldots,\nu\}

where
\varepsilon>0
and
\|h\|_{[a,b]}:=\max\{h(x):\,x\in[a,b]\}
. One has
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}}^{n})={\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}([a,b],{\mathbb{R}})
and we endow this space with the product topology. If
X\subset[a,b]
, one analogously defines the
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}
compact-open topology of the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\nu}(X,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

If
\alpha:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a continuous semialgebraic path, recall that by
[
BCR
, Prop.2.9.10]
there exists a finite set
\eta(\alpha)\subset[a,b]
such that
\alpha
is not Nash at the points of
\eta(\alpha)
, but
\alpha|_{[a,b]\setminus\eta(\alpha)}
is a Nash map. We denote the Taylor expansion of degree
\ell\geq 1
of
\alpha
at
t_{0}\in[a,b]\setminus\eta(\alpha)
with
T_{t_{0}}^{\ell}\alpha:=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{1}{\ell!}\alpha^{(k)}(t_{0})({\tt t}-t_{0})^{k}
.

Lemma 2.8

(Smart polynomial curve selection lemma)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open semialgebraic set and
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}\subset\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}})
a finite set of points, not necessarily distinct. Let
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{r}<1
and
\alpha:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
be a continuous semialgebraic path such that
\alpha(t_{i})=p_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
that satisfies
\eta(\alpha)\cap\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\}=\varnothing
and
\alpha([0,1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\})\subset{\mathcal{S}}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
and each
m\geq 0
there exists a polynomial path
\beta:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
such that:
\|\alpha^{(k)}-\beta^{(k)}\|<\varepsilon
for
k=0,\ldots,m
,
T^{m}_{t_{i}}\beta=T^{m}_{t_{i}}\alpha
for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\beta([0,1]\setminus\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\})\subset{\mathcal{S}}
.

3.
Building Nash images of the simplicial prism

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
2.7
, which provides a complete characterization of the Nash images of the closed ball. The proof is quite involved and intricate and we begin with some preliminary results to lighten the proof.

We will start with the 1-dimensional case, that requires a different proof. Then we will focus on the
d
-dimensional case for
d\geq 2
. For the general case we will take advantage of the fact that each checkerboard set
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
admits ‘nice’ triangulations. Roughly speaking, we ‘build’
{\mathcal{T}}
as Nash image of the prism
\Delta_{d-1}\times[0,1]
‘simplex by simplex’.

We consider a suitable subset of the space of Nash maps
\mathcal{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and we will parameterize it (of course not in an injective way) using an open semialgebraic set
\Theta_{0}
of a large affine space. In this space, a continuous semialgebraic path
\sigma:[0,1]\to\Theta_{0}
provides a continuous semialgebraic map
\Delta_{d-1}\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that is Nash on the horizontal slices
\Delta_{d-1}\times\{t\}
. Using Lemma
2.8
, we approximate the path
\sigma
by a polynomial path in order to obtain a Nash map
\Delta_{d-1}\times[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A difficult point is to guarantee that the obtained Nash map has
{\mathcal{T}}
as its target space and that it is surjective.

3.1.
The
1
-dimensional case.

Nash images of closed balls contained in the real line are its compact intervals and all of them are affinely equivalent to the interval
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}:=[-1,1]
. Nash images of closed balls contained in a circumference are its connected compact subsets and all of them are Nash images of
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}
.

Examples 3.1
.

(i) The circumference
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:=\{{\tt x}^{2}+{\tt y}^{2}=1\}
is a Nash image of

As

, then
\rho(x)=x
. Define

. Let

be polynomials of degree
1
such that the hyperplanes

The following result provides sufficient conditions to guarantee that the high order derivatives of two continuous semialgebraic maps on

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a non-empty semialgebraic set. Let
F,G:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be two continuous semialgebraic maps that are Nash on a neighborhood of
{\mathcal{S}}\times\{0\}
and suppose that there exists a Nash function
\lambda:[-1,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\|F-G\|_{{\mathcal{S}}\times[-1,1]}<|\lambda|_{[-1,1]}
and that
\lambda({\tt t})=a_{k+1}{\tt t}^{k+1}u^{2}({\tt t})
where
a_{k+1}\neq 0
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
is a Nash series such that
u(0)=1
. Then, for each
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
we have

\frac{\partial^{\ell}F}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)=\frac{\partial^{\ell}G}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)

for
\ell=0,\ldots,k
.

Proof.

Pick
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
and write

\displaystyle F(x,{\tt t})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{1}{\ell!}\frac{\partial^{\ell}F}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0){\tt t}^{\ell},

\displaystyle G(x,{\tt t})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{1}{\ell!}\frac{\partial^{\ell}G}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0){\tt t}^{\ell}.

Thus, we have the following inequalities in the ring
{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
of algebraic power series with respect to any of its two orders (one characterized by
{\tt t}>0
and the other one by
{\tt t}<0
):

\Big\|\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{1}{\ell!}\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)-\frac{\partial G}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)\Big){\tt t}^{\ell}\Big\|\leq\|F(x,{\tt t})-G(x,{\tt t})\|\leq|a_{k+1}||{\tt t}^{k+1}|u^{2}.

Consequently, the series

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{1}{\ell!}\Big(\frac{\partial^{\ell}F}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)-\frac{\partial^{\ell}G}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)\Big){\tt t}^{\ell}

is a series of order

, so

\frac{\partial^{\ell}F}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)-\frac{\partial^{\ell}G}{\partial{\tt t}^{\ell}}(x,0)=0

for
\ell=0,\ldots,k

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a semialgebraic set and
\{{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{s}
the family of the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{S}}
that are non-compact. Denote

and with

the set of points of
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
of dimension

, which we assume to be non-compact for each
i=1,\ldots,s
. Let
U
be an open semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
that contains
{\mathcal{S}}
and let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{s}
be Nash subsets of
U
such that
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus X_{i}\neq\varnothing
for each
i
. Up to shrinking
U
if necessary, there exist:

•

a Nash manifold
M\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
,

•

a Nash diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to U
and

•

a Nash function
g_{i}:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
whose zero set contains
\varphi^{-1}(X_{i})

such that the corresponding Nash map
G_{i}:M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p+1},\ x\mapsto(x\cdot g_{i}(x),g_{i}(x))
satisfies

and
G_{i}(\varphi^{-1}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}))
is pure dimensional of dimension
d_{i}
and non-compact for

.

Proof.

We may apply the Nash diffeomorphism

\psi_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(0,1),\ x\mapsto\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+\|x\|^{2}}}

to
{\mathcal{S}}
and assume that
{\mathcal{S}}
is bounded. As
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus X_{i}\neq\varnothing
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
is irreducible and
X_{i}
is the zero-set of a Nash function on
U
, we deduce by
[
FG3
, Lem.3.6]
that
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\cap X_{i})<\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})=\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. Pick a point
q_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
. Let
Z_{i}
be the Zariski closure of
(\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i}))\cup\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\cup\{q_{i}\}
. We claim:
Z_{i}
has dimension strictly smaller than
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
.

As
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
is closed in
{\mathcal{S}}
,
X_{i}
is closed in
U
and
{\mathcal{S}}\subset U
, we deduce

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i})\cap{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\cap X_{i},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i})\cap(\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}})\setminus{\mathcal{S}})=\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i})\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\subset\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}.

Both semialgebraic sets have dimensions strictly smaller than
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
, so
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(X_{i}^{*})
has dimension strictly smaller than
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
. In addition,
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})
has dimension strictly smaller than
\dim({\mathcal{S}}^{*}_{i})
, because
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})<d_{i}=\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
. Thus,
Z_{i}
is a real algebraic set of dimension strictly smaller than
\dim({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*})
.

As
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
is bounded and non-compact and
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
is closed in
{\mathcal{S}}
(because it is a closed subset of
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}
, which is a closed subset of
{\mathcal{S}}
), there exists
p_{i}\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\setminus{\mathcal{S}}
(because otherwise
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\subset{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}=\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\cap{\mathcal{S}}=\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})
would be compact). As
p_{i}\not\in{\mathcal{S}}
, up to replace
U
by

and
X_{i}
by

if necessary, we may assume
p_{i}\not\in X_{i}
. As
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus Z_{i}
is dense in
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
(because
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
is pure dimensional and
Z_{i}
has strictly smaller dimension), there exists by the Nash curve selection lemma
[
BCR
, 8.1.13]
a Nash curve
\alpha_{i}:(-1,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
\alpha_{i}((0,1))\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus Z_{i}
and

. Let

be a polynomial whose zero set is
Z_{i}
.

Case 1.
If
Q_{i}(p_{i})\neq 0
, we take a bounded Nash function
g_{i}
on
U
whose zero set is the union of
X_{i}
and the smallest Nash subset of
U
that contains
\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})
. Observe that the limit
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}g_{i}\circ\alpha_{i}(t)
exists and it is non-zero, because otherwise either

belongs to the Zariski closure of

or

, which is a contradiction.

Consider the Nash map
G_{i}:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1},\ x\mapsto(x\cdot g_{i}(x),g_{i}(x))
, whose restriction to
U\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}
is a Nash diffeomorphism between
U\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}
and
G_{i}(U)\setminus\{0\}
, whose inverse is
H_{i}:G_{i}(U)\setminus\{0\}\to U\setminus\{g_{i}=0\},\ (y,t)\mapsto\frac{y}{t}
. If

. As

G_{i}|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}}:{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}\to G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\setminus\{0\}

(because

), which is a contradiction because
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(g_{i}\circ\alpha_{i})(t)
exists and it is non-zero. Consequently,
G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})
is non-compact. Again, as the restriction
G_{i}|_{{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}}
is a Nash diffeomorphism,

G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\setminus\{0\}=G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\})

is pure dimensional of dimension
d_{i}
. As
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}
is pure dimensional of dimension
d_{i}
and
\dim(\{g_{i}=0\})\leq d_{i}-1
, we deduce
q_{i}\in\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\})
. As

, we conclude
0\in G_{i}(\operatorname{Cl}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}))\subset\operatorname{Cl}(G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\setminus\{g_{i}=0\}))
, so

is pure dimensional of dimension
d_{i}
. In addition,

0\in G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})=G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})})\cup\{0\}=G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*,(d_{i})}\cup({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}\cap\{g_{i}=0\}))=G_{i}({\mathcal{S}}_{i}^{*}).

Case 2.
If
Q_{i}(p_{i})=0
, we have
Q_{i}\circ\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}[[{\tt t}]]_{\rm alg}
is a non-zero series. Let
(Y_{i},\phi_{i})
be the blow-up of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
at
p_{i}
. The restriction
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\setminus\{\phi_{i}^{-1}(p_{i})\}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\setminus\{p_{i}\}

, so

is Nash diffeomorphic to

and

is Nash diffeomorphic to

. The series

and let
(Q_{i}\circ\phi_{i})^{*}
be the strict transform of
(Q_{i}\circ\phi_{i})
. The order of the series

, because we have eliminated from

. If

. In each step the order of the strict transform of the corresponding polynomial substituted in the corresponding curve has strictly smaller order, so in finitely many steps we achieve order

to
Case 1

After composing all the involved blow-ups (corresponding to all the indices

that are under the assumptions of

and

and non-compact for

6.
Two applications of the main results

In this section we present two remarkable consequences of Theorem
1.19
. The first one about representation of pure dimensional compact irreducible arc-symmetric semialgebraic sets as Nash images of closed balls. As a second consequence we show that a compact semialgebraic set is the projection of a non-singular compact algebraic set with the simplest possible topology (a disjoint union of spheres).

\pi|_{M_{1}}:M_{1}\to C_{1}
is bijective. Thus, the composition
\pi\circ f:{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

is bijective, but this contradicts Lemma

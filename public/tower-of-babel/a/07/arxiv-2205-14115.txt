On series and integral representations of some NRQCD master integrals


Abstract
We consider new ways of obtaining series and integral representations for master integrals arising in the process of matching of QCD to NRQCD. The latter results are exact in space-time dimension d d . In addition, we discuss series expansion of the obtained results at fixed values of d d .

Appendix A
Notation for iterated integrals

In this Appendix we define our notation used for iterated integrals with algebraic kernels. Throughout the paper we have two types of such integrals. The first one is of polylogarithmic type, which we define as

\text{II}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{n}}=\text{II}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{n}}(z)=\text{II}(\omega_{a_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{a_{n}};z)=\int_{0}^{z}\omega_{a_{1}}(z_{1})\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\omega_{a_{2}}(z_{2})\ldots\int_{0}^{z_{n-1}}\omega_{a_{n}}(z_{n})\,,

(69)

where
\omega_{a_{i}}
are algebraic 1-forms. Our results contain only 8 types of such 1-forms, which are given by

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{z_{1}^{2}}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{z_{1}}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{3}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{1}}{z}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{4}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{1}}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{5}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{2}^{2}}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{6}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{2}}{z}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{7}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{2}}dz,

\displaystyle\omega_{8}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}dz.

(70)

Here are
z_{1}=\sqrt{1+4z^{2}}
and
z_{2}=\sqrt{1-4z}
. In particular we have

\text{II}_{1,4}=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{z^{\prime}dz^{\prime}}{1+4{z^{\prime}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{z^{\prime}}\frac{dz^{\prime\prime}}{\sqrt{1+4{z^{\prime\prime}}^{2}}}\,,\quad\text{II}_{3}=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\sqrt{1+4{z^{\prime}}^{2}}}{z^{\prime}}dz^{\prime}\,.

(71)

The second type of iterated integrals is of elliptic type. We define them as

\text{J}_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}=\text{J}_{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}(x)=\text{J}(\omega_{\bar{a}_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{a_{n}};x)=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{\bar{a}_{1}}(t)\text{II}(\omega_{a_{2}},\ldots,\omega_{a_{n}};\frac{xt}{4})\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{\bar{a}_{1}}(t)\int_{0}^{\frac{xt}{4}}\omega_{a_{2}}(z_{2})\int_{0}^{z_{2}}\omega_{a_{3}}(z_{3})\ldots\int_{0}^{z_{n-1}}\omega_{a_{n}}(z_{n})

(72)

Here all 1-forms
\omega_{a_{i}}
except the first one are the same as in Eq. (
70
). The first 1-forms are different and take the following expressions

\displaystyle\omega_{\bar{1}}(t)=\Omega_{1,0}^{1,0,1}(t),\quad\omega_{\bar{2}}(t)=\Omega_{1,0}^{2,0,0}(t),\quad\omega_{\bar{3}}(t)=\Omega_{1,0}^{2,0,1}(t),\quad\omega_{\bar{4}}(t)=\Omega_{1,0}^{2,1,0}(t),

\displaystyle\omega_{\bar{5}}(t)=\Omega_{1,1}^{1,0,1}(t),\quad\omega_{\bar{6}}(t)=\Omega_{1,1}^{2,0,1}(t),\quad\omega_{\bar{7}}(t)=\Omega_{1,1}^{2,1,0}(t),

(73)

where

\Omega_{de}^{abc}(t)=\frac{t^{d}\log^{e}t~dt}{z^{a}z_{1}^{b}z_{2}^{c}}\Big|_{z=\frac{xt}{4}}

(74)

In particular we have

\displaystyle\text{J}_{\bar{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{16dt}{tx^{2}\sqrt{1-tx}},\quad\text{J}_{\bar{5},6}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{4\log(t)dt}{x\sqrt{1-tx}}\int_{0}^{\frac{xt}{4}}\frac{\sqrt{1-4z^{\prime}}}{z^{\prime}}dz^{\prime},

\displaystyle\text{J}_{\bar{4},3,8}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{16dt}{tx^{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{t^{2}x^{2}}{4}}}\int_{0}^{\frac{xt}{4}}\frac{\sqrt{1+4{z^{\prime}}^{2}}}{z^{\prime}}dz^{\prime}\int_{0}^{z^{\prime}}\frac{dz^{\prime\prime}}{z^{\prime\prime}}\,.

(75)

It may turn out that the last integration over
t
is divergent, such as the integral
\text{J}_{\bar{3}}
in our previous example. Therefore, this definition must be supplemented with an appropriate regularization rule. We define it as follows. If the integral
\int_{0}^{1}f(t)dt
diverges at the lower limit of integration then the function
f
has an expansion
f(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}t^{-i}+\dots
and it is natural to define the regularized version of integral with the substitution
f(t)\to f^{\text{reg}}(t)
, where
f^{\text{reg}}(t)=f(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}t^{-i}
. This way for our previous example we get the following regularization prescription

\text{J}_{\bar{3}}\rightarrow\text{J}_{\bar{3}}^{\text{reg}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{16}{tx^{2}\sqrt{1-tx}}-\frac{16}{tx^{2}}\right]dt.

(76)

Note, that the final results for our master integrals do not depend on regularization prescription due to cancellation of divergences between different
J
-functions.

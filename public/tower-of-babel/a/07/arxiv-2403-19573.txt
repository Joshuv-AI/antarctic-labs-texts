Abstract
. We introduce and study a q q -version of the chromatic polynomial of a given graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , namely, χ G λ ( q , n ) := ∑ proper colorings c : V → [ n ] q ∑ v ∈ V λ v ​ c ​ ( v ) , \chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ :=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\ c\,:\,V\to[n]\end{subarray}}q^{\sum_{v\in V}\lambda_{v}c(v)}, where λ ∈ ℤ V \lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{V} is a fixed linear form. Via work of Chapoton (2016) on q q -Ehrhart polynomials, χ G λ ​ ( q , n ) \chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n) turns out to be a polynomial in the q q -integer [ n ] q [n]_{q} , with coefficients that are rational functions in q q . Additionally, we prove structural results for χ G λ ​ ( q , n ) \chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n) and exhibit connections to neighboring concepts, e.g., chromatic symmetric functions and the arithmetic of order polytopes. We offer a strengthened version of Stanley’s conjecture that the chromatic symmetric function distinguishes trees, which leads to an analogue of P P -partitions for graphs.



1.
Introduction

The
chromatic polynomial
of a graph
G=(V,E)
,

\chi_{G}(n)\ :=\ \#\{c:V\to[n]\,:\,c(v)\neq c(w)\text{ if }vw\in E\}\,,

where
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, is a famous and much-studied enumerative invariant of
G
.
We introduce and study the following refinement: given
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{|V|})\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
, let

\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ :=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c\,:\,V\to[n]\end{subarray}}q^{\sum_{v\in V}\lambda_{v}c(v)}.

Naturally,
\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(1,n)=\chi_{G}(n)
.
On the other hand, consider Stanley’s
chromatic symmetric function

[
21
]

X_{G}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\ :=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:V\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\end{subarray}}x_{1}^{\#c^{-1}(1)}x_{2}^{\#c^{-1}(2)}\cdots

(so that
X_{G}(1,1,\dots,1,0,0,\dots)=\chi_{G}(n)
).
Its
principal evaluation
(sometimes referred to as the
principal specialization
)

(1)

X_{G}(q,q^{2},\ldots,q^{n},0,0,\ldots)\ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:V\to[n]\end{subarray}}q^{\sum_{v\in V}c(v)}\ =\ \chi_{G}^{\mathbf{1}}(q,n)

is the special case
\lambda=\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
, i.e.,
\lambda
is a vector whose entries
are all 1. In fact,
\chi_{G}^{\mathbf{1}}(q,n)
was also the subject of
[
13
]
.
We think of

and
\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)
as (quite) different generalizations of the chromatic polynomial, which meet in (
1
) and still generalize
\chi_{G}(n)
.

Our first result says that
\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)
has a polynomial structure whose coefficients are rational functions in
q
, in the following sense:

Theorem 1
.

There exists a unique polynomial
\widetilde{\chi}_{G}^{\lambda}(q,x)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)[x]
such that

\widetilde{\chi}_{G}^{\lambda}(q,[n]_{q})\ =\ \chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n),

where
[n]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}
.

We thus call
\widetilde{\chi}_{G}^{\lambda}(q,x)
(and sometimes, by a slight abuse of nomenclature,
\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)
) the
q
-chromatic polynomial
of
G
with respect to
\lambda
.
Our main goal is to initiate the study of this polynomial.

2.
q
-Ehrhart Polynomials

Chapoton
[
4
]
introduced a weighted generalization of the Ehrhart polynomial of a
lattice polytope

P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(i.e.,
P
is the convex hull of finitely many integer lattice points in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
).
We briefly sketch this theory and its application to order polytopes, which in turn allows us to exhibit a connection to
q
-chromatic polynomials.

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a linear form, and define

\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ :=\sum_{m\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q^{\lambda(m)}.

The classical Ehrhart polynomial
[
7
]
is the specialization
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(1,n)
.
Chapoton proved that there is a polynomial
\widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}_{P}^{\lambda}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)[x]
, such that

(2)

\widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}_{P}^{\lambda}([n]_{q})\ =\ \operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(q,n)\,.

We refer to
\widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}_{P}^{\lambda}(x)
as the
q
-Ehrhart polynomial
with respect to
\lambda
.
We often denote the linear form
\lambda
as a vector
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
\lambda_{j}=\lambda(e_{j})
.

Parallel to the classical case, structural results for
\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(q,n)
follow from studying the
q
-Ehrhart series

(3)

\operatorname{Ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(q,z)\ :=\ \sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{ehr}_{P}^{\lambda}(q,n)\,z^{n}.

Chapoton
[
4
]
showed that (
3
) can be written as a rational function whose denominator consists of factors
1-q^{j}z
, where
j=\lambda(v)
for a vertex
v
of
P
.
Furthermore, Chapoton proved the reciprocity theorem

(4)

(-1)^{\dim(P)}\,\widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}^{\lambda}_{P}(q,[-n]_{q})\ =\ \widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}^{\lambda}_{P^{\circ}}\left(\tfrac{1}{q},[n]_{\frac{1}{q}}\right),

where
P^{\circ}
denotes the (relative) interior of
P
.
The case
q=1
in (
4
) recovers the classical Ehrhart–Macdonald reciprocity theorem
[
15
,
2
]
.

Given a poset
\Pi=([d],\preceq)
, the
order polytope

\mathcal{O}(\Pi)
is the lattice polytope

\mathcal{O}(\Pi)\ :=\ \left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}:\,x_{i}\leq x_{j}\text{ if }i\preceq j\right\}.

Order polytopes were introduced by Stanley
[
20
]
; they contain much information about a given poset and have provided important examples in polyhedral geometry.

Since all vertices of
\mathcal{O}(\Pi)
are
0/1
-vectors,
\operatorname{Ehr}^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi)}(q,z)
can be written as a rational function with factors
1-q^{j}z
in the denominator where
j
is a sum of some of the entries of
\lambda
.
This yields the following corollary, which we record for future purposes.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Lambda:=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{d}
.
The coefficients of
[\Lambda]_{q}!\,\widetilde{\operatorname{ehr}}^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi)}(x)
are polynomials in
q
.

Kim and Stanton
[
12
, Corollary 9.7]
gave the following (equivalent) formulas for the case when
\lambda=\mathbf{1}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ehr}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi)}(q,z)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\Pi)}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(\sigma)}z^{\operatorname{des}(\sigma)}}{(1-z)(1-qz)\cdots(1-q^{d}z)}

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ehr}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi)}(q,n)\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\Pi)}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(\sigma)}\left[{n+d-\operatorname{des}(\sigma)\atop d}\right]_{q},

where
\mathcal{L}(\Pi)
is the set of linear extensions of
\Pi
and, writing a given linear extension
\sigma
as a permutation of
[d]
, and
1
1

1

Here we fix a natural labeling of
\Pi
, i.e., an order-preserving bijection
\Pi\to[d]
.
The permutation corresponding to a given linear extension
\sigma
can be read off from this labeling.
Unfortunately, there are two different (and conflicting) definitions of the comajor index in the literature: the one we use here, and the sum of the ascent positions.

\operatorname{Des}(\sigma):=\{j:\,\sigma(j+1)<\sigma(j)\},

\operatorname{des}(\sigma):=|\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)|,\text{ and}

\operatorname{comaj}(\sigma):=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}(d-j).

See
[
2
, Chapter 6]
for details on the interplay of linear extensions of a poset, their descent statistics, and the arithmetic of order polytopes.

Given a graph
G
, let
\mathcal{A}(G)
denote the set of acyclic orientations of
G
; each acyclic orientation
\rho
naturally induces a poset, which we denote
\Pi_{\rho}
.
There is a well-known connection (essentially going back to
[
19
]
) between the chromatic polynomial of a given graph
G
and the Ehrhart polynomials of the order polytopes of the acyclic orientations of
G
.
In the language of
q
-chromatic polynomials and
q
-Ehrhart polynomials, it reads as follows.

Lemma 3
.

The
q
-chromatic polynomial with respect to
\lambda
equals

\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ =\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{A}(G)}\operatorname{ehr}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})^{\circ}}^{\lambda}(q,n+1)\,.

Proof.

We follow the philosophy of inside-out polytopes
[
3
]
.
Let
d=|V|
.
We may interpret each
n
-coloring of the vertices of
G
is a lattice point in the
(n+1)
st dilate of the open unit cube
(0,1)^{d}
(where the
j
th coordinate is the color of vertex
j
).
Furthermore, every proper
n
-coloring of
[d]
is a lattice point that is not contained in the graphical hyperplane arrangement

(6)

\mathcal{H}_{G}\ :=\ \left\{x_{i}=x_{j}:\,ij\in E\right\}.

The regions of
(0,1)^{d}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{G}
are precisely the open order polytopes
\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})^{\circ}
for
\rho
an acyclic orientation of
G
.
That is, each proper coloring
c
of
G
induces an acyclic orientation
\rho_{c}
of
G
, where the edge
ij
is oriented from
i
to
j
if
c(i)<c(j)
and from
j
to
i
if
c(j)<c(i)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:[d]\to[n]\end{subarray}}q^{\lambda_{1}c(1)+\cdots+\lambda_{d}c(d)}\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{A}(G)}\ \sum_{\mathbf{c}\in(n+1)\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})^{\circ}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q^{\lambda_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}c_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{A}(G)}\operatorname{ehr}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})^{\circ}}^{\lambda}(q,n+1)\,.\qed

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
f(x)
and
g(x)
are polynomials with coefficients that are rational functions in
q
such that

f([n]_{q})\ =\ g([n]_{q})\qquad\text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\,.

Then
f(x)=g(x)
.

Proof.

By our assumptions, the polynomial
f(x)-g(x)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)[x]
has infinitely many zeros and so must be the zero polynomial.
∎

Together with Chapoton’s result (
2
), Lemmas
3
and
4
prove Theorem
1
.
In fact, we can see more, namely, that the analogue of Lemma
2
holds also for
q
-chromatic polynomials.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\Lambda:=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{d}
.
The coefficients of
[\Lambda]_{q}!\,\widetilde{\chi}^{\lambda}_{G}(x)
are polynomials in
q
.

The case
\lambda=\mathbf{1}
is particularly nice because we can employ (
5
).

Corollary 6
.

For any graph
G=(V,E)
,

\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,n)\ =\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{A}(G)}\ \sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\Pi_{\rho})}q^{\binom{d+1}{2}-\operatorname{comaj}{\sigma}}\left[n+\operatorname{des}{\sigma}\atop d\right]_{q}\,.

Proof.

We apply (
4
) and (
5
):

\operatorname{ehr}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})^{\circ}}(q,n)\ =\ (-1)^{d}\operatorname{ehr}^{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{O}(\Pi_{\rho})}(\tfrac{1}{q},-n)\ =\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}(\Pi_{\rho})}q^{\binom{d+1}{2}-\operatorname{comaj}{\sigma}}\left[n+\operatorname{des}{\sigma}-1\atop d\right]_{q},

and so Lemma
3
finishes the proof.
∎

We record a few consequences of the last corollary.

Corollary 7
.

Let
G=([d],E)
and express
\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,n)
in the form

\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(n)\ =\ \sum_{j\geq 0}\beta_{j}(q)\left[n+j\atop d\right]_{q}\,.

(1)

Each
\beta_{i}(q)
is a polynomial in
q
with nonnegative coefficients.

(2)

\beta_{0}(q)=|\mathcal{A}(G)|\,q^{\binom{d+1}{2}}
; in particular, if
G
is a tree then
\beta_{0}(q)=2^{d-1}q^{\binom{d+1}{2}}
.

(3)

The largest value
i
for which
\beta_{i}(q)\neq 0
is
d-\xi
where
\xi
is the chromatic number of
G
.
Moreover,

\beta_{d-\xi}(q)\ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:V\to\xi\end{subarray}}q^{\sum_{v\in V}c(v)}.

Remark 1
.

Corollary
6
gives another way of realizing the largest value
j
for which
\beta_{j}(q)\neq 0
, namely, as the maximal number
m
of descents in a linear extension of a poset induced by an acyclic orientation of
G
.
Therefore, the chromatic number of
G
is equal to
d-m
, which is one more than the minimal number of ascents in a linear extension of a poset induced by an acyclic orientation of
G
.
This fact is known as the
Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 7.17]
).

We also remark that
\beta_{d-\xi}(q)
distinguishes between some trees as the next example illustrates.

Example 2
.

Let
T_{1}
be the path of length 3 and let
T_{2}
be the star with degree sequence
(3,1,1,1)
.
We compute

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{1}}(q,n)\

\displaystyle=\ 8q^{10}\left[{n\atop 4}\right]_{q}+(4q^{9}+6q^{8}+4q^{7})\left[{n+1\atop 4}\right]_{q}+2q^{6}\left[{n+2\atop 4}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{2}}(q,n)\

\displaystyle=\ 8q^{10}\left[{n\atop 4}\right]_{q}+(5q^{9}+4q^{8}+5q^{7})\left[{n+1\atop 4}\right]_{q}+(q^{7}+q^{5})\left[{n+2\atop 4}\right]_{q}.

In particular,
\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{1}}(q,2)=2q^{6}
while
\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{2}}(q,2)=q^{7}+q^{5}
.
However, the coefficient
\beta_{d-2}(q)
is not enough to distinguish all non-isomorphic trees on
d
vertices.

3.
The structure of
q
-chromatic polynomials

As with the classic chromatic polynomial, the
q
-chromatic polynomial satisfies a
deletion–contraction relation. Naturally, this strongly relates to the deletion–contraction
formula for Crew–Spirkl’s weighted version of the chromatic symmetric
function
[
6
, Lemma 2]
.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
G=([d],E)
is a graph,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
e=12\in E
. Then

\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ =\ \chi_{G\setminus e}^{\lambda}(q,n)-\chi_{G/e}^{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{d})}(q,n)\,.

Proof.

As usual, we observe that the proper
n
-colorings of
G
are precisely the proper
n
-colorings
c
of
G\setminus e
that satisfy the additional condition
c(1)\neq c(2)
.
Therefore, we may count them by counting all proper
n
-colorings
c
of
G\setminus e
and then removing all such colorings
c
for which
c(1)=c(2)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:[d]\to[n]\text{ of }G\end{subarray}}q^{\lambda_{1}c(1)+\cdots+\lambda_{d}c(d)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:[d]\to[n]\text{ of }G\setminus e\end{subarray}}q^{\lambda_{1}c(1)+\cdots+\lambda_{d}c(d)}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper colorings}\\
c:[d]\to[n]\text{ of }G\setminus e\\
\text{where }c(1)=c(2)\end{subarray}}q^{\lambda_{1}c(1)+\cdots+\lambda_{d}c(d)}

\displaystyle=\ \chi_{G\setminus e}^{\lambda}(q,n)-\chi_{G/e}^{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{d})}(q,n)\,.\qed

We observe that a similar computation enables us to express any
q
-chromatic polynomial (for general
\lambda
with positive entries) as a linear combination of
q
-chromatic polynomials with
\lambda=\mathbf{1}
, via a repeated expansion–addition process as follows.
If
G=([d],E)
is a graph and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
with
\lambda_{1}\geq 2
, split the vertex
1
into two vertices
1^{\prime}
and
1^{\prime\prime}
with weights
\lambda_{1}-1
and
1
, respectively.
Create the
expansion graph
\exp(G,e)
of
G
at
1
with vertex set
\{1^{\prime},1^{\prime\prime},2,\ldots,d\}
and edge set

\{1^{\prime}i,1^{\prime\prime}i:\,i\in\{2,\ldots,d\}\text{ such that }1i\in E\}\cup\{ij:\,i,j\in\{2,\ldots,d\}\text{ such that }ij\in E\}\,,

and let the
addition graph
\operatorname{add}(G,e)
of
G
at
1
be
\text{exp}(G,e)
with an edge added between the new vertices
1^{\prime}
and
1^{\prime\prime}
.
Then

\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ =\ \chi_{\text{exp}(G,e)}^{(\lambda_{1}-1,1,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d})}(q,n)-\chi_{\text{add}(G,e)}^{(\lambda_{1}-1,1,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d})}(q,n)\,.

By repeatedly applying this process, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 9
.

If
G=([d],E)
is a graph and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
, then there exist graphs
H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell}
on
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}
vertices and integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
such that

\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}k_{i}\,\chi^{\mathbf{1}}_{H_{i}}(q,n)\,.

Our next result extends Stanley’s famous reciprocity theorem for the chromatic polynomial to the
q
-setting.
A (not necessarily proper) coloring
c
of a graph
G
is
compatible
with an acyclic orientation
\rho
of
G
if
c
(weakly) increases along oriented edges.
Stanley
[
19
]
proved that
|\chi_{G}(-n)|
equals the number of pairs of an
n
-coloring and a compatible acyclic orientation of
G
.
In particular,
|\chi_{G}(-1)|
equals the number of acyclic orientations of
G
.
This generalizes as follows.

Theorem 10
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
, let
\Lambda:=\sum_{v\in V}\lambda_{v}
.
Then

(-1)^{|V|}q^{\Lambda}\,\widetilde{\chi}^{\lambda}_{G}\left(\tfrac{1}{q},[-n]_{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\ =\sum_{(c,\rho)}q^{\sum_{v\in V(G)}\lambda_{v}c(v)},

where the sum is over all pairs of an
n
-coloring
c
and a compatible acyclic orientation
\rho
.

Example 3
.

For
\lambda=\mathbf{1}
, the path on 2 vertices has
q

\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{P_{2}}(q,x)\ =\ \frac{2q^{2}x^{2}-2q^{2}x}{1+q}\,.

Therefore,

(-q)^{2}\,\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{P_{2}}(\tfrac{1}{q},x)\ =\ q^{2}\,\frac{2q^{-2}x^{2}-2q^{-2}x}{1+q^{-1}}\ =\ \frac{2qx^{2}-2qx}{1+q}

and so, e.g.,

(-q)^{2}\,\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{P_{2}}(\tfrac{1}{q},-q-q^{2})\ =\ \frac{2q(-q-q^{2})^{2}-2q(-q-q^{2})}{1+q}\ =\ 2q^{4}+2q^{3}+2q^{2}.

Indeed, this sums
q^{\sum c(v)}
for the six pairs of 2-colorings and compatible acyclic orientations.

6.
Open Questions

From our construction of
\widetilde{\chi}^{\lambda}_{T}(q,n)
for general
\lambda
, a natural weakening of Conjecture
1
(that is perhaps easier to prove) arises.

Conjecture 3
.

For any pair of non-isomorphic trees
S
and
T
on
d
vertices, there exists a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\chi_{S}^{\lambda}(q,n)\neq\chi_{T}^{\lambda}(q,n)
.

Another line of open questions emerges concerning the coefficients of the
q
-chromatic polynomial. The classical chromatic polynomial
\chi_{G}(n)
is very well studied, and many of its coefficients have nice combinatorial interpretations. Can we generalize these to
\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,n)
? For example:

(1)

The second coefficient of
\chi_{G}(n)
is (negative) the number of edges of
G
. Can we refine this to a
q
version, i.e., does the second coefficient of
\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,n)
count the number of edges of
G
, but graded by some property of the edges?

(2)

Can the same be done for the linear coefficient which, in the classical case, counts the number of acyclic orientations with a unique sink at one fixed vertex? (This is not interesting for trees by Remark
2
, but could be interesting for general graphs.)

(3)

The coefficients of
\chi_{G}(n)
are alternating. Can we show that the coefficients of
\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,n)
are “strongly alternating,” in the sense that the coefficient of
x^{j}
in
[d]_{q}!\cdot\widetilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{1}}_{G}(q,x)
is a polynomial in
q
with either all positive or all negative coefficients (depending on the parity of
d-j
)?

Finally, as we mentioned in the introduction, there are further structural results
and questions that stem from viewing
\chi_{G}^{\lambda}(q,n)
as an evaluation of a (weighted) chromatic symmetric function.
It is then natural to ask if there is anything to be gained by zeroing in on the polynomial
\widetilde{\chi}^{\lambda}_{G}(q,x)

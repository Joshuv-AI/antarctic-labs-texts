Improved Analysis of the Breakup Corrections to the High Energy 3 He Beam Polarization measurements with HJET
A. A. Poblaguev

Abstract
The requirements for hadron polarimetry at the future Electron Ion Collider (EIC) include measurements of the absolute helion ( 3 He, h h ) beam polarization with systematic uncertainties better than σ P syst / P ≤ 1 % \sigma^{\text{syst}}_{P}/P\leq 1\% . Recently, it was suggested to utilize the Polarized Atomic Hydrogen Gas Jet Target (HJET) for precision measurement of the polarization of the ∼ \sim 100 GeV/n helion beam. At the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider, HJET serves to determine absolute proton beam polarization with low systematic uncertainties of about δ syst ​ P / P ≲ 0.5 % \delta^{\text{syst}}P/P\lesssim 0.5\% . To adapt the HJET method for the EIC helion beam, the experimentally determined ratio of the beam and target (jet) spin correlated asymmetries should be adjusted by the ratio of p ↑ ​ h p^{\uparrow}{h} and h ↑ ​ p h^{\uparrow}{p} analyzing powers A N p ​ h ​ ( t ) / A N h ​ p ​ ( t ) A_{\text{N}}^{ph}(t)/A_{\text{N}}^{hp}(t) which, in the leading order approximation, is predefined by magnetic moments of the proton and helion, ( μ p − 1 ) / ( μ h / 2 − 1 / 3 ) (\mu_{p}-1)/(\mu_{h}/2-1/3) . However, to achieve the required accuracy in the measured polarization, the corrections due to hadronic spin-flip amplitudes and due to possible beam 3 He breakup should be considered. Here a more accurate analysis of the possible breakup corrections to the measured 3 He beam polarization is provided. The results confirm that the breakup corrections are negligible for the EIC helion beam absolute polarization measurement by HJET.

III
Hadronic Spin-Flip Amplitudes in High Energy Proton-Nucleus Scattering

In Ref.
Kopeliovich and Trueman 2001
, it was shown that, at high energy, to a very good approximation, the ratio of the spin-flip to the non-flip parts of the elastic proton-nucleus amplitude is the same as for proton-nucleon scattering. In terms of the hadronic spin-flip amplitude parameter
r_{5}
, the result can be written as

r_{5}^{pA}=\frac{i+\rho^{pA}}{i+\rho}r_{5}\approx r_{5},

(11)

where
\rho^{pA}
is the real-to-imaginary ratio for the elastic
pA
scattering. It may be noted that Eq. (
11
) can be readily derived considering the polarized proton scattering off an unpolarized nucleus in Glauber (diffraction) approximation
Glauber 1955
;
*Glauber:1959
.

In this approach, elastic (
f\!=\!i
) and/or breakup (
f\!\neq\!i
) proton-nucleus amplitude can be presented
Glauber and Matthiae 1970
using the following integral over the impact vector
\bm{b}
:

\displaystyle F_{fi}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{ik}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}\bm{b}\,e^{i\bm{qb}}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{A}{d^{3}\bm{r}_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Psi_{f}^{*}(\{r_{j}\})\Gamma(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{1}...\bm{s}_{A})\Psi_{i}(\{r_{j}\})

(12)

where
\bm{k}
is the momentum of the incident proton,
\Psi_{i}
and
\Psi_{f}
are the nucleus’s initial and final state wave functions, and
\Gamma(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{1}...\bm{s}_{A})
is the profile function. The positions of the
A
nucleons in the nucleus were defined by the vectors
\bm{r}_{j},~j\!=\!1,\dots,A
, and
\bm{s}_{j}
are the projections of these vectors on the plane perpendicular to
\bm{k}
.

For a proton-deuteron small angle scattering, the elastic
pd
amplitude
F_{ii}
can be approximated
Franco and Glauber 1966
via proton-proton
f_{p}
and proton-neutron
f_{n}
ones as

\displaystyle F_{ii}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=S(\bm{q}/2)f_{n}(\bm{q})+S(\bm{q}/2)f_{p}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2\pi k}\int{S(\bm{q}^{\prime})f_{n}(\bm{q}/2\!+\!\bm{q}^{\prime})f_{p}(\bm{q}/2\!-\!\bm{q}^{\prime})d^{2}\bf{q}^{\prime}},

(13)

where
S(\bm{q})
can be interpreted as a deuteron form factor.

To calculate the
p^{\uparrow}d
spin-flip amplitude
F_{ii}^{\text{sf}}
, one can utilize the proton-nucleon one,
f_{N}^{\text{sf}}(\bm{q})
, which, according to the definition of
r_{5}

Buttimore et al. 1999
, is

f_{N}^{\text{sf}}(\bm{q})=\frac{\bm{qn}}{m_{p}}\,\frac{r_{5}}{i+\rho}\,f_{N}(\bm{q})=\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}f(\bm{q}).

(14)

Since in the HJET measurements
|r_{5}|\,q/m_{p}\lesssim 0.003
, one and only one non-flip amplitude
f_{N}
in each term of sum (
13
) should be replaced by
f_{N}^{\text{sf}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle f_{p}f_{n}

\displaystyle\to\left[(\bm{q}/2\!+\!\bm{q}^{\prime})\bm{n}f_{p}\,f_{n}+f_{p}\,(\bm{q}/2\!-\!\bm{q}^{\prime})\bm{n}f_{n}\right]\,\hat{r}_{5}

\displaystyle=\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}\,f_{p}f_{n}.

(15)

Since each term on the right side of Eq. (
13

\bm{qn}\,\hat{r}_{5}

11

Generally, elastic
pA

\displaystyle F_{ii}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}{\{\mathcal{S}_{i}f_{i}\}}+\sum_{i,j}{\{\mathcal{S}_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}\}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j,k}{\{\mathcal{S}_{ijk}f_{i}f_{j}f_{k}\}}+\dots,

(16)

where, e.g., for the 3-amplitude terms,

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{S}_{ijk}f_{i}f_{j}f_{k}\}

\displaystyle=\int{\mathcal{S}_{ijk}(\bm{q}_{i}^{\prime},\bm{q}_{j}^{\prime},\bm{q}_{k}^{\prime})\,f_{i}(\bm{q}_{i}^{\prime})f_{j}(\bm{q}_{j}^{\prime})f_{j}(\bm{q}_{k}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times\delta(\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{q}_{j}^{\prime}-\bm{q}_{k}^{\prime})d^{2}\bm{q}_{i}^{\prime}d^{2}\bm{q}_{j}^{\prime}d^{2}\bm{q}_{k}^{\prime}

In the case of unpolarized proton scattering of a fully polarized nucleus with all nucleons having the same polarization

IV
Inelastic Scattering in HJET

Although the scattered beam particles are not observed at HJET, the polarimeter has the capability for the identification of inelastic events
Poblaguev et al. 2020a
. For a scattered beam particle with mass
M
, the recoil proton coordinate
z_{R}
in the detector has the following dependence on the kinetic energy
T_{R}
and the effective scattered (missing) mass
M_{X}
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\frac{z_{R}-z_{\text{jet}}}{L}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{T_{R}}{2m_{p}}}\times\left[1+\frac{m_{p}^{2}}{ME_{\text{beam}}}+\frac{m_{p}\Delta}{T_{R}E_{\text{beam}}}\right]\!\!,

(23)

where
E_{\text{beam}}
is the beam energy per nucleon,
\Delta\!=\!M_{X}\!-\!M
,
z_{\text{jet}}
(
\big\langle{z_{\text{jet}}}\big\rangle\!=\!0
,
\langle{z_{\text{jet}}^{2}}\rangle^{1/2}\!\approx\!2.5\,\text{mm}
) is the coordinate of the scattering point, and
L\!=\!77\,\text{cm}
is the distance to the detectors.

For elastic scattering,
z_{R}
linearly depends on
T_{R}^{1/2}
. The possibility to isolate inelastic events in HJET depends on the value of the
\Delta/E_{\text{beam}}
ratio
Poblaguev 2022b
. If, for a given
T_{R}
, the corresponding correction to
z_{R}
exceeds the smearing of the measured
z_{R}
due to the jet thickness, the breakup and elastic events can be separated. However, if
\Delta
is large enough, the breakup event cannot be detected in HJET.

As it was illustrated in Ref.
Poblaguev et al.
, inelastic
pp
scattering (
\Delta\geq m_{\pi}
) is almost invisible in HJET for
E_{\text{beam}}\!=\!100\,\text{GeV}
, but may be detected and well separated from the elastic events if
E_{\text{beam}}\!=\!255\,\text{GeV}
.

In numerous gold beam (3.9 ​– ​31 GeV/nucleon energy range) measurements at HJET
Poblaguev et al.
, pion production events could not be detected, but a high rate of the breakup events (
\Delta_{\text{Au}}\gtrsim 4\,\text{MeV}
) was anticipated. However, no breakup events were reliably identified in these data
Poblaguev 2022b
.

The result can be readily explained, if the breakup is going via incoherent scattering of the beam (jet) proton off a nucleon in the nucleus. In this case
Poblaguev 2022b
,

\Delta=\left(1-\frac{m_{p}}{m_{A}}\right)T_{R}+p_{x}\sqrt{\frac{2T_{R}}{m_{p}}},

(24)

where
p_{x}
is the internal motion momentum of the nucleon (in the direction of the detector). Since
T_{R}\lesssim 10\,\text{MeV}
for recoil protons detected at HJET, the scattered mass excess is much smaller than the nucleus mass,
\Delta\ll M
. Consequently, the breakup event rate should be strongly suppressed by the phase space factor.

IV.1
A Model Used to Isolate Breakup Events in HJET

To experimentally evaluate the breakup fraction in the elastic data in the deuteron beam measurements at HJET, the following model for the breakup event distribution was suggested in Ref.
Poblaguev 2022b
.

Assuming that the
p_{x}
distribution in a deuteron is given by a unity integral normalized Breit ​– ​Wigner function

f_{\text{BW}}(p_{x},\sigma_{p})=\frac{\pi^{-1}\,\sqrt{2}\sigma_{p}}{p_{x}^{2}+2\sigma_{p}^{2}},

(25)

the
d\to pn
breakup fraction
\omega(T_{R},\Delta)
can be found
Poblaguev 2022b
as a convolution of the
\Delta
distribution, calculated in accordance with Eq. (
24
), and the phase space integral (calculated as a function of
\Delta

\displaystyle\omega(T_{R},\Delta)=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\text{brk}}(T_{R},\Delta)}{d\sigma_{\text{el}}(T_{R})\,d\Delta}=\frac{\sqrt{2m_{p}m_{n}}}{4\pi m_{d}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times|\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)|^{2}f_{\text{BW}}(\Delta\!-\!\Delta_{0},\sigma_{\Delta})\sqrt{\frac{\Delta\!-\!\Delta^{d}_{\text{thr}}}{m_{d}}},

(26)

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}=(1-m_{p}/m_{d})T_{R},\qquad\sigma^{2}_{\Delta}=2\sigma_{p}^{2}T_{R}/m_{p}.

(27)

Since the key dependence of the breakup amplitude on
T_{R}
and
\Delta
is allocated in the function
f_{\text{BW}}
,
\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)
should be interpreted as a reduced ratio of the breakup to elastic amplitudes. Therefore, in HJET measurements, i.e., for low
T_{R}
and
\Delta
,
\bar{\psi}(T_{R},\Delta)
can be substituted by a constant
\bar{\psi}\!\equiv\!\bar{\psi}(0,0)
. For the

breakup, the threshold is
\Delta^{d}_{\text{thr}}\!=\!m_{p}\!+\!m_{n}\!-\!m_{d}\!=\!2.2\,\text{MeV}
.

Introducing

\omega_{\Phi}(T_{R})=\int_{\Delta^{d}_{\text{thr}}}^{\infty}{\omega(T_{R},\Delta)_{|\bar{\psi}|=1}\,d\Delta},

(28)

which can be calculated within the model used, one can express the breakup fraction dependence on
T_{R}
as

\omega(T_{R})=\frac{d\sigma_{\text{brk}}(T_{R})}{d\sigma_{\text{el}}(T_{R})}=|\bar{\psi}|^{2}\omega_{\Phi}(T_{R}).

(29)

To search for breakup events in the HJET data, the recoil proton displacement,

\zeta=z_{R}^{\text{brk}}(T_{R},\Delta)-\big\langle{z_{R}^{\text{el}}(T_{R})}\big\rangle\approx\sqrt{\frac{T_{R}}{2m_{p}}}\,\frac{L\Delta}{E_{\text{beam}}},

relative to the mean elastic coordinate for the same

can be considered. Using Eqs. (
24
) and (
26

\displaystyle\omega_{z}(T_{R},\zeta)

\displaystyle\propto f_{\text{BW}}(\zeta-\zeta_{0},\sigma_{z})\sqrt{\zeta-\zeta_{\text{thr}}^{d}},

(31)

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}

\displaystyle=L\sigma_{p}/E_{\text{beam}},

(32)

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{m_{p}}{m_{d}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{T_{R}m_{p}/2}}{\sigma_{p}}\sigma_{z}\approx 0.62\sigma_{z},

(33)

\displaystyle\zeta_{\text{thr}}^{d}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{m_{p}}{2T_{R}}}\frac{\Delta_{\text{thr}}^{d}}{\sigma_{p}}\sigma_{z}\approx 0.69\sigma_{z}.

(34)

Numerical estimates in Eqs. (

V
Evaluation of the Breakup Corrections in the Helion Beam Polarization Measurements

A basic assumption of the Glauber theory approach that a high energy beam proton crosses a target nucleus before any changes in the nucleus structure caused by the proton occur suggests that an electromagnetic interaction is the same for elastic and breakup scattering. However, to compare the electromagnetic amplitudes for the elastic and breakup scattering, the interaction, in accordance with Eq. (
12
), should be projected to the final state wave functions
\Psi_{h}
and
\Psi_{pd}
, respectively.
For
3
He in the ground state, the function
f_{\text{BW}}(\Delta\!-\!\Delta_{0},\sigma_{\Delta})
[Eq. (
26
)], which is defined by the nucleon momentum distribution, is expected to be about the same for hadronic and electromagnetic scattering. Therefore, assuming that the disturbances of
3
He ,
{}^{3}\text{He}\to{{}^{3}\text{He}}^{*}(\bm{q})
, in the scatterings are small, it can be approximated that the reduced breakup to the elastic ratio (
26
) is

\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}^{*}(\bm{q})\rangle_{\text{em}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}^{*}(\bm{q})\rangle_{\text{em}}}\approx\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{em}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{em}}}=\frac{\langle\bar{\Psi}_{pd}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{had}}}{\langle{\Psi}_{h}|{{}^{3}\text{He}}\rangle_{\text{had}}}=\bar{\psi}.

(45)

Consequently, the ratio for the electromagnetic amplitude (either spin-flip
\phi_{5}^{\text{em}}
or non-flip
\phi_{+}^{\text{em}}
) must be nearly the same as for hadronic non-flip
\phi_{+}^{\text{had}}
, which leads to the following breakup corrections in the interference terms in Eq. (
6
):

\displaystyle\phi_{5}^{\text{em}}\phi_{+}^{\text{had}}:\quad

\displaystyle\kappa\to\kappa+\kappa\times\omega_{h}(T_{R}),

(46)

\displaystyle\phi_{5}^{\text{had}}\phi_{+}^{\text{em}}:\quad

\displaystyle I_{5}\to I_{5}+\widetilde{I}_{5}\times\omega_{h}(T_{R}),

(47)

\displaystyle\phi_{5}^{\text{had}}\phi_{+}^{\text{had}}:\quad

\displaystyle R_{5}\to R_{5}+\widetilde{R}_{5}\times\omega_{h}(T_{R}).

(48)

Consequently, correction (
9
) to the measured beam polarization can be evaluated as

\xi(T_{R})=\frac{2\delta_{pd}}{\kappa_{h}}\left(I_{5}+R_{5}T_{R}/T_{c}\right)\omega_{h}(T_{R}).

(49)

Assuming a linear fit of the measured polarization
P_{\text{beam}}(T_{R})
[see Eq. (
8
)] in the
2\!<\!T_{R}\!<\!10\,\text{GeV}

Poblaguev et al. 2020b
kinetic energy range, the breakup fraction functions can be replaced by

\displaystyle\omega_{h}(T_{R})

\displaystyle\approx\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}\frac{T_{R}}{T_{c}}=1.5\%+0.3\%\,\frac{T_{R}}{T_{c}},

(50)

\displaystyle\omega_{h}(T_{R})\frac{T_{R}}{T_{c}}

\displaystyle\approx\omega_{0}^{\prime}+\omega_{1}^{\prime}\frac{T_{R}}{T_{c}}=-9.6\%+6.4\%\,\frac{T_{R}}{T_{c}}.

(51)

Thus, the breakup related systematic error in the beam polarization measurements can be evaluated as

|\delta^{\text{brk}}P_{h}/P_{h}|=\left|\frac{2\delta_{pd}}{\kappa_{h}}\,(I_{5}\omega_{0}+R_{5}\omega_{0}^{\prime})\right|<0.3\%,

(52)

in consistency with the EIC requirement (
1
).

The relatively loose constraint on the systematic uncertainty can be explained by the essential non-linearity of the
\omega_{h}(T_{R})T_{R}/T_{c}
function. To improve, a parabolic function

\xi(T_{R})\approx\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}\>\!T_{R}/T_{c}+\xi_{2}(T_{R}/T_{c})^{2}

(53)

can be considered to fit (assuming
\xi_{0}\!\equiv\!0
)

in the
2\!<\!T_{R}\!<\!7\,\text{GeV}
energy range. In this case

\omega_{0}=0.28\%,\qquad\omega_{0}^{\prime}=-0.21\%

(54)

and the breakup related uncertainty (
52
) is diluted to a negligible value.

To provide better control of the possible breakup corrections, one can consider the following function

\xi(T_{R})\approx\xi_{1}{T_{R}}/{T_{c}}+\left[\xi_{0}^{\omega}+\xi_{1}^{\omega}{T_{R}}/{T_{c}}\right]\times\omega_{h}(T_{R})

(55)

to fit the measured polarization. A more accurate estimate of
\omega_{h}(T_{R})
can be done, e.g., in (relatively) low energy, 5 ​– ​20 GeV,
3
He beam measurements at HJET or by tagging the
3

VI
Summary

In this paper, evaluation
Poblaguev 2022b
of the breakup corrections in the EIC
3
He beam polarization measurements with HJET was reviewed.

For proton-deuteron scattering, the breakup fraction evaluated at HJET
Poblaguev 2022b
was found to be consistent with a value obtained in the hydrogen bubble chamber experiment
Aladashvili et al. 1977
. This result proves, that within the experimental accuracy of the measurements
Poblaguev 2022b
,
(i)
the model used to describe the breakup event rate,
dN/dT_{R}d\Delta
, adequately simulates the experimental data and
(ii)
the breakup event fraction can be reliably monitored in the HJET measurements using 5 ​– ​20 GeV
3
He beams.

In Ref.
Poblaguev 2022b
, the breakup fraction in the future
3
He beam polarization measurement at EIC was estimated by extrapolating the
d\!\to\!pn
results to the two-body
h\!\to\!pd
breakup and neglecting the three-body
h\!\to\!ppn
one. Analyzing experimental distributions displayed in Refs.
Stepaniak
;
Watanabe et al. 2021
, it was confirmed that the
h\!\to\!ppn
rate is very small in the HJET measurements.

The evaluated
Poblaguev 2022b

3
He breakup rate was compared with the results of the
3
He beam scattering in the hydrogen bubble chamber
Glagolev et al. 1993
;
Stepaniak
. It was found that
\omega_{h}(T_{R})
displayed in Fig.
6
should be interpreted as an upper limit for the breakup fraction in the EIC
3
He beam scattering in HJET.

To estimate breakup effects in the
3
He beam polarization measurements, corrections to the interference terms in Eq. (
6
) are needed to be known for both
p^{\uparrow}h
and
h^{\uparrow}p
scattering.

It was shown that, within the applicability of the Glauber theory, the ratio of the proton-nucleus breakup spin-flip and non-flip amplitudes is the same as for elastic proton-proton scattering.

Regardless the breakup corrections to the
\phi_{5}^{\text{em}}\phi_{+}^{\text{had}}
and
\phi_{5}^{\text{had}}\phi_{+}^{\text{em}}
terms in Eq. (
6
) were underestimated in Ref.
Poblaguev 2022b
, this did not lead to a wrong conclusion, as the miscalculation of up to an order of magnitude was being tolerated in the analysis done.

Although the corrections to the interference terms, found here, are relatively large, up to 4%, they cancel to a negligible level in the analyzing power ratio in Eq. (
7
).

To recognize a residual breakup correction (if any) in experimental data analysis, the measured polarization
P_{\text{meas}}(T_{R})
(
8
) should be interpolated using
\xi(T_{R})
given by Eq. (
55
) or, in simplified consideration, by Eq. (
53
).

Deformation cohomology of morphisms of Lie-Yamaguti algebras
Bibhash Mondal, Ripan Saha 1. Department of Mathematics, Behala CollegeBehala, 700060, Kolkata, IndiaEmail: mondaliiser@gmail.com2. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj UniversityRaiganj 733134, West Bengal, India

Abstract




ABSTRACT

We study cohomology of morphisms of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. As an application, we establish that this cohomology ‘controls’ the formal deformations. Additionally, we demonstrate its connection to the abelian extension of morphisms of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Key words
: Lie-Yamaguti algebra, morphism, cohomohology, deformation, rigidity.

17A30, 17A40, 17B56, 17D99.

Introduction

Lie-Yamaguti algebras, a pivotal concept in non-associative algebra theory, find their roots in the pioneering work of Jacobson
[
10
]
, who formally introduced the notion of a Lie triple system. This algebraic structure emerged from the context of quantum mechanics
[
2
]
, demonstrating its profound relevance in this domain. Nomizu
[
16
]
further extended this theory, establishing a compelling connection between affine connections with parallel torsion and curvature, and invariant connections on reductive homogeneous spaces. Notably, each such space possesses a canonical connection that aligns geodesic parallel translation with the natural group action.

Building on these foundational ideas, K. Yamaguti
[
17
]
introduced the concept of a general Lie triple system: a vector space equipped with both bilinear and trilinear operations, satisfying intriguing relations. This innovation was crucial in characterizing the torsion and curvature tensors associated with Nomizu’s canonical connection. The subsequent work of M. Kikkawa
[
11
]
added a crucial insight, recognizing the intimate relationship between this problem, the canonical connection, and the general Lie triple system defined on the tangent space.

In a transformative contribution, Kikkawa renamed the notion of a general Lie system as a Lie triple algebra, establishing a nomenclature that resonates within the mathematical community. Kinyon and Weinstein
[
12
]
expanded this framework, identifying that Lie triple algebras, which they later termed Lie-Yamaguti algebras, could be constructed from Leibniz algebras. This insight demonstrated the broad applicability of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which arose initially from Cartan’s investigations into Riemannian geometry.

In
[
20
]
, the author studied infinitesimal deformations of a Lie triple system using the cohomology groups as introduced by K. Yamaguti in
[
19
]
. In
[
21
]
, the authors studied
(2,3)
-cohomology groups and related deformation theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. In
[
13
]
, the authors discussed the one-paramater formal deformation theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and recently, in
[
9
]
the author studied correct obstruction theory to integrate a finite order deformation to a full-blown deformation.

In 1983, Gerstenhaber and Schack
[
8
]
initiated the study of deformation of morphism of associative algebras. In
[
15
]
, the author studied deformations of Leibniz algebra morphisms. Recently, Das
[
1
]
studied cohomology and deformation theory of Lie algebra morphisms. As Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a generalization (see Example
2.2
and
2.3
) of both Lie and Leibniz algebra
[
14
]
, it is very natural to study morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra from cohomological point of view. The aim of this paper is to study an algebraic deformation theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphisms. We study the deformation theory of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra following the idea of Gerstenhaber’s
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
algebraic deformation theory. We define a suitable deformation cohomology for such formal deformation. We also show that isomorphism classes of abelian extension of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra has one to one correspondence with the ‘simple’
(2,3)
- cohomology group.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
, we recall the definition and cohomology of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. In Section
3
, we discuss representation of morphisms of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. In Section
4
, we introduce cohomology of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra which involves both Lie-Yamaguti part and the morphism part. In Section
5
, we discuss one-parameter formal deformation theory of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra and show that the deformation is ‘controlled’ by our cohomology defined in Section
4
. In Section
6
, we study abelian extension of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra and its relationship with the cohomology. Finally, in Section
7
, we conclude our paper by pointing out that the work of this paper is a special case of a more general problem of diagram of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Lie-Yamaguti algebra and its cohomology

In this section, we recall the cohomology of Lie-Yamaguti algebra as in

Let
(L,[~,~],\{~,~,~\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with representation
V
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a triple
(L,[~,~],\{~,~,~\})
, where
L
is a
k
-vector space,
[~,~]
is a binary operation and
\{~,~,~\}
is
a ternary operation on
L
such that

\displaystyle(1)~~[x,y]=-[y,x],

\displaystyle(2)~~\{x,y,z\}=-\{y,x,z\},

\displaystyle(3)~~\circlearrowleft_{(x,y,z)}([[x,y],z]+\{x,y,z\})=0,

\displaystyle(4)~~\circlearrowleft_{(x,y,z)}(\{[x,y],z,u\})=0,

\displaystyle(5)~~\{x,y,[u,v]\}=[\{x,y,u\},v]+([u,\{x,y,v\}],

\displaystyle(6)~~\{x,y,\{u,v,w\}\}=\{\{x,y,u\},v,w\}+\{u,\{x,y,v\},w\}+\{u,v,\{x,y,w\}\},

for all
x,y,z,u,v,w\in L
and where
\circlearrowleft_{(x,y,z)}
denotes the sum over cyclic permutation of
x,y,z
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
be a Lie algebra over
k
. Then,
\mathfrak{g}
has a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure induced by the given Lie bracket, the trilinear operation being:

\{a,b,c\}=[[a,b],c]

for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a Leibniz algebra. Consider a bilinear operation and a trilinear operation as follows:

[~,~]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},\quad[a,b]:=a\cdot b-b\cdot a,~~a,b\in\mathfrak{g};

\{~,~,~\}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},\quad\{a,b,c\}:=-(a\cdot b)\cdot c,~~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then,
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\{~,~,~\})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(L,[~,~],\{~,~,~\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
V
be a vector space. A representation of

L
on
V
consists of a linear map
\rho:L\rightarrow
End(
V
) and two bilinear maps
D,\theta:L\times L\rightarrow
End(
V
)
satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle(1)~D(x,y)-\theta(y,x)+\theta(x,y)+\rho([x,y])-\rho(x)\rho(y)+\rho(y)\rho(x)=0,

\displaystyle(2)~D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0,

\displaystyle(3)~\theta([x,y],z)=\theta(x,z)\rho(y)-\theta(y,z)\rho(x),

\displaystyle(4)~D(x,y)\rho(z)=\rho(z)D(x,y)+\rho(\{x,y,z\}),

\displaystyle(5)~\theta(x,[y,z])=\rho(y)\theta(x,z)-\rho(z)\theta(x,y),

\displaystyle(6)~D(x,y)\theta(u,v)=~\theta(u,v)D(x,y)+\theta(\{x,y,u\},v)+\theta(u,\{x,y,v\}),

\displaystyle(7)~\theta(x,\{y,z,u\})=\theta(z,u)\theta(x,y)-\theta(y,u)\theta(x,z)+D(y,z)\theta(x,u),

for any
x,y,z,u,v\in L
. Here we say the
(\rho,D,\theta)
is a representation of
L
on
V.

Let us define the cohomology groups of
L
with coefficients
in
V
. Let
f:L\times L\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\times L
be an
n
-linear map of
L
into
V
such that the following conditions are
satisfied:

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2i-1},x_{2i},\ldots,x_{n})=0,~~~~\mbox{if}~~~x_{2i-1}=x_{2i}.

The vector space spanned by such linear maps is called an
n
-cochain of
L
, which is denoted by

C^{n}(L,V)
for
n\geq 1
.

Definition 2.5
.

For any
(f,g)\in C^{2n}(L,V)\times C^{2n+1}(L,V)
,
n\geq 1
the coboundary operator
\delta:(f,g)\rightarrow(\delta_{I}f,\delta_{II}g)
is a mapping from
C^{2n}(L,V)\times C^{2n+1}(L,V)
into
C^{2n+2}(L,V)\times C^{2n+3}(L,V)
defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\delta_{I}f)(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x_{2n+1})g(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+2})-\rho(x_{2n+2})g(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle-g(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},[x_{2n+1},x_{2n+2}])

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,x_{2n+2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+2}(-1)^{n+k}f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,\{x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}\},\ldots,x_{2n+2}),

\displaystyle(\delta_{II}g)(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n+3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x_{2n+2},x_{2n+3})g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+1})-\theta(x_{2n+1},x_{2n+3})g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})g(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,x_{2n+3})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+3}(-1)^{n+k}g(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,\{x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}\},\ldots,x_{2n+3}).

We define
C^{(2n,2n+1)}(L,V):=C^{2n}(L,V)\times C^{2n+1}(L,V)
. Then
\{C^{(\bullet,\bullet)}(L,V),\delta\}
is a cochain complex.

Let
C^{1}(L,V)
denote the space of all linear maps from
L
into
V
. We define the subspace
C(L,V)
by the diagonal elements
(f,f)\in C^{1}(L,V)\times C^{1}(L,V.)
.
The map
\delta:C(L,V)\rightarrow C^{(2,3)}(L,V)
is defined by
\delta(f,f)=(\delta_{I}f,\delta_{II}f)
, where

\displaystyle\delta_{I}f(a,b)=\rho(a)f(b)-\rho(b)f(a)-f([a,b]),

\displaystyle\delta_{II}(a,b,c)=\theta(b,c)f(a)-\theta(a,c)f(b)+D(a,b)f(c)-f(\{a,b,c\})

for all
a,b,c\in L.

From
[
19
]
, we have for any
f\in C^{1}(L,V)
,
\delta_{I}\delta_{I}f=\delta_{II}\delta_{II}f=0
and In general for any
(f,g)\in C^{(2p,2p+1)}(L,V)~\mbox{we have}~~(\delta\circ\delta)(f,g)=(\delta_{I}\circ\delta(f),\delta_{II}\circ\delta_{II}(g))=0.

Morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra and its representation

In this section, we discuss momorphism between Lie-Yamaguti algebras and representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra as defined in
[
17
,
19
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A momorphism between two Lie-Yamaguti algebras
(L,[~,~],\{~,~,~\})
and
(L^{\prime},[~,~]^{\prime},\{~,~,~\}^{\prime})
is a linear map
\varphi:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y])=[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]^{\prime},~~~~~~~~~\varphi(\{x,y,z\})=\{\varphi(x),\varphi(y),\varphi(z)\}^{\prime}

for all
x,y,z\in L.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
and
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}:L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
be two morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A homomorphism between these two morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, where
\alpha:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\beta:L_{2}\rightarrow L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
are two Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism satisfying the condition
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\phi
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
be a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A representation of it is a ordered triple
(V,W,\psi)
, where
V
,
W
are representations of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
respectively, and
\psi:V\rightarrow W
is a linear operator satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle\psi(\rho_{V}(x)v)=\rho_{W}(\phi(x))\psi(v)

\displaystyle\psi(D_{V}(x,y)v)=D_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\psi(v)

\displaystyle\psi(\theta_{V}(x,y)v)=\theta_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\psi(v)

for all
x,y\in L_{1}
and
v\in V.

The following proposition gives an example of representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra induced by representation of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
be a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V,W,\psi)
be a representation of it. Now the maps

\displaystyle\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}:L_{1}\rightarrow End(W)

\displaystyle D_{W}^{L_{1}}:L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow End(W)

\displaystyle\theta_{W}^{L_{1}}:L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow End(W)

defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(x)v=\rho_{W}(\phi(x))v

\displaystyle\theta_{W}^{L_{1}}(x,y)v=\theta_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))v

\displaystyle D_{W}^{L_{1}}(x,y)v=D_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))v

respectively for all
x,y\in L_{1}
and
v\in V
makes

(\rho_{W}^{L_{1}},D_{W}^{L_{1}},\theta_{W}^{L_{1}})
is representation of
L_{1}
on
W.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in L_{1}
and
w\in W.
Observe that

\displaystyle D_{W}^{L_{1}}(x,y)w-\theta_{W}^{L_{1}}(y,x)w+\theta_{W}^{L_{1}}(x,y)w+\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}([x,y])w

\displaystyle-\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(x)\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(y)w+\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(y)\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(x)w

\displaystyle=D_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))w-\theta_{W}(\phi(y),\phi(x))w+\theta_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))w+\rho_{W}([\phi(x),\phi(y)])w

\displaystyle-\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(x)\rho_{W}(\phi(y))w+\rho_{W}^{L_{1}}(y)\rho_{W}(\phi(x))w

\displaystyle=D_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))w-\theta_{W}(\phi(y),\phi(x))w+\theta_{W}(\phi(x),\phi(y))w+\rho_{W}([\phi(x),\phi(y)])w

\displaystyle-\rho_{W}(\phi(x))\rho_{W}(\phi(y))w+\rho(\phi(y))\rho_{W}(\phi(x))w

\displaystyle=0,~~~\mbox{as}~W~\mbox{is a representation of}~L_{2}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle D_{W}^{L_{1}}([x,y],z)w+D_{W}^{L_{1}}([y,z],x)w+D_{W}^{L_{1}}([z,x],y)

\displaystyle=D_{W}(\phi([x,y]),\phi(z))w+D_{W}(\phi([y,z]),\phi(x))w+D_{W}(\phi([z,x]),\phi(y))w

\displaystyle=D_{W}([\phi(x),\phi(y)],\phi(z))w+D_{W}([\phi(y),\phi(z)],\phi(x))w+D_{W}([\phi(z),\phi(x)],\phi(y))w

\displaystyle=0,~~~\mbox{as}~W~\mbox{is a representation of}~L_{2}.

The other axioms of representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra can be proved similarly as above. Hence,
(\rho_{W}^{L_{1}},D_{W}^{L_{1}},\theta_{W}^{L_{1}})
is a representation of
L_{1}
on
W.

∎

We denote the above representation by
W_{\phi}.
The following proposition shows that given a homomorphism between two morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra induces a representation of a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
,
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}:L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
be two morphism of Lie Yamaguti algebras and
(\alpha,\beta)
be a homomorphism between them. Then,
(L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}},\phi^{{}^{\prime}})
is a representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
, where
\rho_{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x):L_{1}\rightarrow End(L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}),~D_{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x,y):L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow End(L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}),~\theta_{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x,y):L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow End(L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})
are defined by

\displaystyle\rho(x)x^{{}^{\prime}}=[\alpha(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle D(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}=\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}=\{x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\}

respectively for all
x,y\in L_{1}
and
x^{{}^{\prime}}\in L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
. Also,
\rho_{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x):L_{2}\rightarrow End(L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}),~D_{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x,y):L_{2}\times L_{2}\rightarrow End(L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}),~\theta_{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x,y):L_{2}\times L_{2}\rightarrow End(L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})
are defined by

\displaystyle\rho(x)x^{{}^{\prime}}=[\beta(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle D(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}=\{\beta(x),\beta(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}=\{x^{{}^{\prime}},\beta(x),\beta(y)\}

respectively for all
x,y\in L_{2}
and
x^{{}^{\prime}}\in L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

For any
x,y,z\in L_{1}
and
x^{{}^{\prime}}\in L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle D(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}-\theta(y,x)x^{{}^{\prime}}+\theta(x,y)x^{{}^{\prime}}+\rho([x,y])x^{{}^{\prime}}-\rho(x)\rho(y)x^{{}^{\prime}}+\rho(y)\rho(x)x^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}-\{x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(y),\alpha(x)\}+\{x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\}

\displaystyle+[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],x^{{}^{\prime}}]-[\alpha(x),[\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}]]+[\alpha(y),[\alpha(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}]]

\displaystyle=\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}+\{\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(x)\}+\{x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\}

\displaystyle+[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],x^{{}^{\prime}}]+[[\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}],\alpha(x)]+[[x^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha(x)],\alpha(y)]

\displaystyle=0.

Again,

\displaystyle D([x,y],z)x^{{}^{\prime}}+D([y,z],x)x^{{}^{\prime}}+D([z,x],y)x^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha([x,y]),\alpha(z),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}+\{\alpha([y,z]),\alpha(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}+\{\alpha([z,x]),\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle=\{[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha(z),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}+\{[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)],\alpha(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}+\{[\alpha(z),\alpha(x)],\alpha(y),x^{{}^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle=0.

In a similar way other axioms of representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra can be shown.
Therefore,
L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
is a representation of
L_{1}
. In a similar way as above
L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
is a representation of
L_{2}.

Now,

\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho_{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}(x)x^{{}^{\prime}})=\phi^{{}^{\prime}}([\alpha(x),x^{{}^{\prime}}])=[\phi^{{}^{\prime}}\circ\alpha(x),\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{{}^{\prime}})]\\
=[\beta\circ\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{{}^{\prime}}),\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{{}^{\prime}})]=\rho_{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}(\phi(x))\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{{}^{\prime}})

The other two conditions for representation for morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra can be shown same way as above.
Hence,
(L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}},\phi)
is a representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra morphism
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}.

∎

4
Cohomology of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we define the cohomology of morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra using cohomolgy theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebra motivated by the cohomolgoy theory of morphism of different algebras in
[
8
,
15
,
1
]
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
be a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V,W,\psi)
be a representation of it. Now we have the following three cochain complexes

•

\{C^{(\bullet,\bullet)}(L_{1},V),\delta^{{}^{\prime}}\}
, the cochain complex of
L_{1}
with coefficients in
V.

•

\{C^{(\bullet,\bullet)}(L_{2},W),\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\}
, the cochain complex of
L_{2}
with coefficients in
W.

•

\{C^{(\bullet,\bullet)}(L_{1},W_{\phi}),\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\}
, the cochain complex of
L_{1}
with coefficients in
W_{\phi}.

Now, using the above three cochain complex we define the cohomology of the morphism of Lie Yamaguti algebra
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
with coefficients in
(V,W,\psi).

For each
n\geq 1
, we define the
(2n,2n+1)
-th cochain group
C^{(2n,2n+1)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
by

C^{(2n,2n+1)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)=\begin{cases}&C^{(2,3)}(L_{1},V)\oplus C^{(2,3)}(L_{2},W)\oplus C(L_{1},W_{\phi})~~\mbox{if}~~n=1\\
&C^{(2n,2n+1)}(L_{1},V)\oplus C^{(2n,2n+1)}(L_{2},W)\oplus C^{(2n-2,2n-1)}(L_{1},W_{\phi}),~~~\mbox{if}~~n\geq 2.\end{cases}

We define the coboundary map

\delta_{mLYA}:C^{(2n,2n+1)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)\rightarrow C^{(2n+2,2n+3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
by

\delta_{mLYA}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\begin{cases}(\delta(\alpha),\delta(\beta),\phi\circ\alpha-\beta\phi-\delta(\gamma))&\text{for }(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in C^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi),n=1\\
(\delta^{{}^{\prime}}(\alpha),\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\beta),\psi\circ\alpha-\beta\circ\wedge^{n}\phi-\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\gamma))&\text{for }(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in C^{(2n,2n+1)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi),n\geq 2,\end{cases}

where

\delta:C(L_{1},W_{\phi})\rightarrow C^{(2,3)}(L_{1},W_{\phi})
is defined by

\delta(\gamma)=(\delta_{I}\gamma,\delta_{II}\gamma)
such that

\displaystyle\delta_{I}\gamma(x,y)=[\phi(x),\gamma(y)]+[\gamma(x),\phi(y)]-\gamma([x,y])

and

\displaystyle\delta_{II}\gamma(x,y,z)=\{\gamma(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)\}+\{\phi(x),\gamma(y),\phi(z)\}+\{\phi(x),\phi(y),\gamma(z)\}-\gamma(\{x,y,z\})

for all
x,y,z\in L_{1}.
Also, note that
\beta\phi:L_{1}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow W_{\phi}
is a linear function defined by

\beta\phi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\beta(\phi(x_{1}),\phi(x_{2}),\ldots,\phi(x_{n}))

for all
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\in L_{1}.

Proposition 4.1
.

The map
\delta_{mLYA}
defined above satisfies
\delta_{mLYA}^{2}=0.

Proof.

Case-I:

For
n=1,
and
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in C^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
, we have

Also by the same argument as above we get
(\rho_{W},D_{W},\theta_{W})
is a representation of
L_{2}
on
W.

∎

Proposition 6.5
.

The representation of
L_{1}
on
V
and the representation of
L_{2}
on
W
induced from a section of an abelian extension is independent of the choice of section.

Proof.

Let
(s,\bar{s})
and
(s^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{s^{{}^{\prime}}})
be two section.
Now
\rho_{V}(x)-\rho_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=[s(x)-s^{{}^{\prime}}(x),i(v)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}=0
and
\rho_{W}(a)-\rho_{W}^{{}^{\prime}}(a)=[s(a)-s^{{}^{\prime}}(a),i(w)]_{\hat{L_{2}}}=0,
since
s(x)-s^{{}^{\prime}}(x)\in\mbox{ker}~p=\mbox{im}~i
and
\bar{s}(a)-\bar{s^{{}^{\prime}}}(a)\in\mbox{ker}~\bar{p}=\mbox{im}~\bar{i}
for all
x\in L_{1},~a\in L_{2},~v\in V,~w\in W.

Similarly,
D_{V}(x,y)v-D_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)v=0
,
D_{W}(a,b)w-D_{W}^{{}^{\prime}}(a,b)w=0
and
\theta(x,y)v-\theta^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)v=0
,
\theta_{W}(a,b)w-\theta_{W}^{{}^{\prime}}(a,b)w=0
for all
x,y\in L_{1},~a,b\in L_{2},~v\in V,~w\in W.
Therefore, the representation induced by the sections
(s,\bar{s})
and
(s^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{s^{{}^{\prime}}})
are same.
∎

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
be a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a representation
(V,W,\psi)
. Now define
Ext(\phi,\psi)
be the isomorphism classes of the abelian extensions of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by the representation
(V,W,\psi)
. It is clear from the Proposition (
6.4
)
Ext(\phi,\psi)
is non empty.

Lemma 6.6
.

Every abelian extension of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
gives a cohomology class in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi).

Proof.

Let
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
be an abelian extension of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
. Let
(s,\bar{s})
be a section. Now define the maps

\alpha_{I}:L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow V,~\beta:L_{2}\times L_{2}\rightarrow W,~\gamma:L_{1}\rightarrow W
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{I}(x,y):=[s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-s([x,y]_{L_{1}}),

\displaystyle\beta_{I}(a,b):=[\bar{s}(a),\bar{s}(b)]_{\hat{L_{2}}}-\bar{s}([a,b]_{L_{2}})~~~\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\gamma(x):=\hat{\phi}(s(x))-\bar{s}(\phi(s)),~~~\mbox{for all }~x,y\in L_{1},~a,b\in L_{2}.

and

\alpha_{II}:L_{1}\times L_{1}\times L_{1}\rightarrow V,~\beta:L_{2}\times L_{2}\times L_{2}\rightarrow W
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{II}(x,y,z):=\{s(x),s(y),s(z)\}_{\hat{L_{1}}}-s(\{x,y,z\}_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle\beta_{II}(a,b,c):=\{\bar{s}(a),\bar{s}(b),\bar{s}(c)\}_{\hat{L_{2}}}-\bar{s}(\{a,b,c\}_{L_{2}})~~~

Therefore,
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{II}),(\gamma,\gamma))\in C^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi).
By the theory of abelian extension of Lie Yamaguti algebra (
[
21
]
) we have
\delta(\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II})=0,\delta(\beta_{I},\beta_{II})=0
and a direct computation shows that
\phi\circ(\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II})-(\beta_{I},\beta_{II})\phi-\delta(\gamma,\gamma)=0.
Hence,
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{II}),(\gamma,\gamma))\in Z^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
. Therefore,
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{II}),(\gamma,\gamma))
corresponds to a cohomology class in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi).

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

The cohomology class induced from an abelian extension of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
does not depend on the choice of section.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
be an abelian extension of a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi).
Let
(s,\bar{s})
and
(s^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{s^{{}^{\prime}}})
be two section of it. Now define
\eta:L_{1}\rightarrow V
by
\eta(x)=s(x)-s^{{}^{\prime}}(x)
for all
x\in L_{1}
and
\eta^{{}^{\prime}}:L_{2}\rightarrow W
by
\eta^{{}^{\prime}}(a)=\bar{s}(a)-\bar{s^{{}^{\prime}}}(a)
for all
a\in L_{2}.

Now,

\displaystyle\alpha_{I}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-s([x,y]_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle=[s^{{}^{\prime}}(x)+\eta(x),s^{{}^{\prime}}(y)+\eta(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-(s^{{}^{\prime}}([x,y])+\eta([x,y]))

\displaystyle=[s^{{}^{\prime}}(x),s^{{}^{\prime}}(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-s^{{}^{\prime}}([x,y]_{L_{1}})+[\eta(x),s^{{}^{\prime}}(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}+[s^{{}^{\prime}}(x),\eta(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-\eta([x,y]_{L_{1}})~(\mbox{as}~[\eta(x),\eta(y)]=0)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)+\delta_{I}(\eta)(x,y)

Similarly we have,
\alpha_{II}(x,y,z)=\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y,z)+\eta_{II}(\eta)(x,y,z)

Therefore,
(\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II})-(\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{I},\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{II})=(\delta_{I}(\eta),\delta_{II}(\eta))\in Z^{(2,3)}(L_{1},V).

Similarly using
\eta^{{}^{\prime}}
we will get
(\beta_{I},\beta_{II})-(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta_{II}^{{}^{\prime}})\in Z^{(2,3)}(L_{2},W)
and
(\gamma,\gamma)-(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}})\in Z(L_{1},W).

Therefore,
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{I}I),(\gamma,\gamma))
and
((\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}),(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{I}I^{{}^{\prime}}),(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}))
are in the same cohomology class in
H_{mLYA,s}^{(2,3)}(\phi,\psi).

∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Isomorphic abelian extensions of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
gives rise to the same element in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
and
\hat{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}:\hat{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}
be two isomorphic abelian extensions and let
(\alpha,\beta)
be the isomorphism between them. Now suppose
(s,\bar{s})
be a section of of the abelian extension
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}.
Therefore,

p^{{}^{\prime}}\circ(\alpha\circ s)=p\circ s=id_{L_{1}}
and
\bar{p^{{}^{\prime}}}\circ(\beta\circ\bar{s})=\bar{p}\circ\bar{s}=id_{L_{2}}.
Thus,
(\alpha\circ s,\beta\circ\bar{s})
is a section of
\hat{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}:\hat{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}
. Now let
((\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}),(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{I}I^{{}^{\prime}}),(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}))\in Z^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}
be the
(2,3)
- cocycle corresponding to the abelian extension
\hat{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}:\hat{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}
with section
(\alpha\circ s,\beta\circ\bar{s}).
Then we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[\alpha\circ s(x),\alpha\circ s(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-\alpha\circ s([x,y]_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle=\alpha([s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{L_{1}}}-s([x,y]_{L_{1}}))

\displaystyle=\alpha_{I}(x,y)~~(\mbox{as}~\alpha_{I}|_{V}=id_{V}),~\mbox{for all}~x,y\in L_{1}.

Similarly, we will get
\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y,z)=\alpha_{II}(x,y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in L_{1}
;

\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}}(a,b)=\beta_{I}(a,b)
and
\beta_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}(a,b,c)=\beta_{II}(a,b,c)
for all
a,b,c\in L_{2}
and
\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=\gamma(x)
for all
x\in L_{1}.
Hence,
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{I}I),(\gamma,\gamma))=((\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}),(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{I}I^{{}^{\prime}}),(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}})).

Thus, isomorphic abelian extension gives rise to the same element in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi)
.

∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Every element in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi)
induces an abelian extension of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{I}I),(\gamma,\gamma))\in Z^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
be a
(2,3)
- cocycle. Now define
\hat{L_{1}}:=L_{1}\oplus V,~\hat{L_{2}}=L_{2}\oplus W
and define
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
by
\hat{\phi}(x,v)=(\phi(x),\psi(v)+\gamma(x))
, for all
(x,v)\in\hat{L_{1}}.
Now define the bilinear and trilinear brackets on
\hat{L_{1}}
and
\hat{L_{2}}
by

\displaystyle[(x,v_{1}),(y,v_{2})]_{\hat{L_{1}}}:=([x,y]_{L_{1}},\rho_{V}(x)v_{2}-\rho_{V}(y)v_{1}+\delta_{I}(x,y))

\displaystyle\{(x,v_{1}),(y,v_{2}),(z,v_{3})\}_{\hat{L_{1}}}:=(\{x,y,z\}_{L_{1}},\theta_{V}(y,z)v_{1}-\theta_{V}(x,z)v_{2}+D_{V}(x,y)v_{3}+\delta_{II}(x,y,z))

\displaystyle\mbox{for all}~x,y,z\in L_{1},~v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V.

and

\displaystyle[(a,w_{1}),(b,w_{2})]_{\hat{L_{2}}}:=([a,b]_{L_{2}},\rho_{V}(a)w_{2}-\rho_{V}(b)w_{1}+\delta_{I}(a,b))

\displaystyle\{(a,w_{1}),(b,w_{2}),(c,w_{3})\}_{\hat{L_{2}}}:=(\{a,b,c\}_{L_{2}},\theta_{V}(b,c)w_{1}-\theta_{V}(a,c)w_{2}+D_{V}(a,b)w_{3}+\delta_{II}(a,b,c))

\displaystyle\mbox{for all}~a,b,c\in L_{2},~w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\in W.

Then
\hat{L_{1}}
,
\hat{L_{2}}
are Lie-Yamaguti algebra with the above defined brackets (for proof see
[
21
]
).
It is easy to show that

\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
is a morphisms of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
and
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
is an abelian extension of
(L_{1},L_{2},\phi)
by
(V,W,\psi)
(For details see
[
21
]
).
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Cohomologous elements in
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi)
gives isomorphic abelian extensions of
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
by
(V,W,\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{I}I),(\gamma,\gamma))
,
((\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}),(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{I}I^{{}^{\prime}}),(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}))\in Z^{(2,3)}_{mLYA}(\phi,\psi)
such that

\displaystyle((\alpha_{I},\alpha_{II}),(\beta_{I},\beta_{I}I),(\gamma,\gamma))-((\alpha_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha_{II}^{{}^{\prime}}),(\beta_{I}^{{}^{\prime}},\beta_{I}I^{{}^{\prime}}),(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}},\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle=d_{mLYA}((\xi,\xi),(\xi^{{}^{\prime}},\xi^{{}^{\prime}}),(0,0))~\mbox{for some}~\xi\in C^{1}(L_{1},V),~\xi^{{}^{\prime}}\in C^{2}(L_{2},W).

Then the corresponding induced abelian extensions
\hat{\phi}:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}}
and
\hat{\phi^{{}^{\prime}}}:\hat{L_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}
respectively are isomorphic via the map
(\alpha,\beta)
where
\alpha:\hat{L_{1}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{1}{{}^{\prime}}}
and
\beta:\hat{L_{2}}\rightarrow\hat{L_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}
defined by
\alpha(x,v)=(x,v+\xi(x))
,
\beta(a,w)=(a,w+\xi^{{}^{\prime}}(a))
,
x\in L_{1},~a\in L_{2},~v\in V,~w\in W
respectively.

∎

From the above Lemmas and Theorems, we have the following main result of this section:

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
\phi:L_{1}\rightarrow L_{2}
be a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra with a representation

. Then there exists a one-to-one correspondence between
Ext(\phi,\psi)
and the
(2,3)
-cohomology group
H^{(2,3)}_{mLYA,s}(\phi,\psi).

Sharp Hardy inequalities involving distance functions from submanifolds of Riemannian manifolds Thanks: A. Kristály is supported by the Excellence Researcher Program ÓE-KP-2-2022 of Óbuda University, Hungary. W. Zhao is supported by Natural Science Foundation of Shanghai (No. 21ZR1418300).
Ningwei Cui, Alexandru Kristály, Wei Zhao

Abstract
. We establish various Hardy inequalities involving the distance function from submanifolds of Riemannian manifolds, where the natural weights are expressed in terms of bounds of the mean curvature of the submanifold and sectional/Ricci curvature of the ambient Riemannian manifold. Our approach is based on subtle Heintze–Karcher-type Laplace comparisons of the distance function and on a D’Ambrosio–Dipierro-type weak divergence formula for suitable vector fields, providing Barbatis–Filippas–Tertikas-type Hardy inequalities in the curved setting. Under very mild assumptions, we also establish the sharpness and non-existence of extremal functions within the Hardy inequalities and – depending on the geometry of the ambient manifold – their extensibility to various function spaces. Several examples are provided by showing the applicability of our approach; in particular, well-known Hardy inequalities appear as limit cases of our new inequalities.

1.
Introduction

The theory of Hardy inequalities still hide interesting and unexplored questions, despite the fact that the first versions of such inequalities were formulated more than a hundred years ago, see Hardy
[
18
]
. The development of Hardy inequalities is strongly motivated by describing a large class of nonlinear phenomena involving certain singularities. Up-to-date expositions of this still flourishing theory can be found in the monographs by Ghoussoub
and Moradifam
[
14
]
, and Ruzhansky and Suragan
[
32
]
.

Given
p>1
, an open domain
\Omega
embedded into an ambient measure space
(M,{\rm d}\mathfrak{m})
, and two non-negative potentials
V,W
on
\Omega
with
W(x)\to\infty
(and perhaps
V(x)\to\infty
as well) as
x\to x_{0}
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega,
a general Hardy inequality can be phrased as

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}V|\nabla u|^{p}{\rm d}\mathfrak{m}\geq\int_{\Omega}W|u|^{p}{\rm d}\mathfrak{m},\ \ \forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

The most familiar form of (
1.1
) is obtained when
V\equiv 1
and
W(x)=\left(\frac{m-p}{p}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}
with
p<m
, where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is any domain containing the origin; moreover, the constant
\left(\frac{m-p}{p}\right)^{p}
is sharp and never achieved.

A prominent setting for Hardy inequalities is when (
1.1
) has a geometric flavor that is reflected in the geometry of the ambient space. The first key contribution in this direction is provided by Carron
[
9
]
, who extended (
1.1
) to
n(\geq 3)
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifolds (simply connected complete Riemannian manifolds with nonpositive sectional curvature) in the case when
V\equiv 1
and
W(x)=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}
, where
r(x)
is the Riemannian-distance of the point
x
to a fixed point
x_{0}
. Carron’s work generated a huge wave of research on Hardy inequalities for Cartan-Hadamard manifolds; without completeness, we mention the works by Berchio, Ganguly and Grillo
[
2
]
, Berchio, Ganguly, Grillo and Pinchover
[
3
]
,
D’Ambrosio and Dipierro
[
7
]
, Kombe and Özaydin
[
23
,
24
]
, Kristály
[
25
]
, Nguyen
[
29
]
, Yang, Su
and Kong
[
33
]
, etc.

An abstract approach to (
1.1
) in the Euclidean setting is developed by Ghoussoub and Moradifam
[
15
,
16
]
, who stated that (
1.1
) holds if and only if
(V,W)
forms a Bessel pair, which is based on the solvability of a linear second-order Bessel-type ordinary differetial equation involving the potentials
V
and
W
. Bessel pairs are extended to Cartan-Hadamard manifolds by Flynn, Lam and Lu
[
12
]
, Flynn, Lam, Lu and Mazumdar
[
13
]
. In the same curved setting, a very recent paper by Kajántó, Kristály, Peter and Zhao
[
21
]
provides an alternative approach to investigate (
1.1
) by using Riccati pairs.

The efficient development of Hardy inequalities on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds is not surprising, since they deeply exploit fundamental properties of such geometric objects, e.g. the emptiness of the cut locus of any point (in particular, the exponential map is a diffeomorphism), the availability of sharp Laplace/volume comparison principles with respect to the model Euclidean space, etc.

The present paper is (at least) two-fold. First, we intend to establish Hardy inequalities on Riemannian manifolds by handling not only the case when the sectional curvature is bounded from above, but also the setting when the Ricci curvature is controlled from below. We notice that such bounds are not necessarily zeros; in particular, in the latter case the manifold might be even compact. Second, we treat Hardy inequalities where the singularities of the potentials
V
and
W
may occur within a whole submanifold of the ambient space; as expected, a careful control on the mean curvature of the submanifold is needed. In this way, the potentials
V
and
W
encode the bounds of both the Ricci/sectional curvature of the ambient space and the mean curvature of the submanifold. As far as we know, these are the first natural Hardy inequalities involving nonvanishing ‘double-curvature’ in terms of the bounds of the Ricci or sectional curvature of the ambient manifold as well as the mean curvature of the submanifold. In limit cases, we recover classical Hardy inequalities.

In the sequel, we shall provide a more detailed picture about our results; to do so, let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold.

In §
2
we present some preliminary notions and results which are indispensable to establish sharp Hardy inequalities. First, in §
2.1
, some important aspects of the submanifold geometry together with the Fermi coordinates on
\Sigma
are presented. Then, in §
2.2
, two Heintze–Karcher-type Laplacian estimates for the distance function
r(x):=r_{\Sigma}(x)=d(\Sigma,x)
are provided, where
d
is the natural metric function on
(M,g)
: Theorem
2.1
is a slightly finer version than the original Heintze–Karcher theorem which is valid when the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded from below, see Heintze and Karcher
[
20
]
, while Theorem
2.2
– which is new in the Riemannian geometry – handles the counterpart case when the sectional curvature is bounded from above. Finally, in §
2.3
, we recall and adapt to our setting the weak divergence notion of D’Ambrosio and Dipierro
[
7
]
.

In §
3
, we state a generic inequality of the form (
1.1
) on any open domain
\Omega\subset M
which verifies a Barbatis–Filippas–Tertikas-type geometric condition, see
[
1
]
, i.e.,
\Sigma={\partial}\Omega
if
n=m-1
or
\Sigma\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
if
0\leq n\leq m-2
. Under this geometric condition and a mild set of assumptions formulated in terms of joint-integrability of the pair of functions
(\phi,\psi)
, see Assumption
1
, we prove in §
3.1
that

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}|{\nabla}u|^{p}\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\dvol_{g}\geq p^{-p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|\dvol_{g},

for every
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
, where
\Omega_{\Sigma}=\Omega\setminus\Sigma
and
\dvol_{g}
is the canonical measure in
(M,g)
, see Theorem
3.1
. The proof of (
1.2
) relies on the weak divergence of D’Ambrosio and Dipierro
[
7
]
. In addition, in §
3.2
and §
3.3
we study the sharpness and the nonexistence of extremal functions in (
1.2
), respectively.

Since (
1.2
) is expected to hold not only on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
but also on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, §
4
is devoted to extend inequality (
1.2
) to some larger function spaces. First, in §
4.1
, we can perform such an extension to the expected space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
by requiring a slightly
stronger assumption than in §
3
, see Assumption
2
and Theorem
4.1
; the sharpness and nonexistence of extremal functions are also investigated in
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, see Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
. In addition, we also observe that (
1.2
) cannot be always extended to
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, for instance, when
\Omega=M
is compact; indeed, by using constant functions as test functions in (
1.2
) – which in such particular case clearly belong to
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
– gives a contradiction. A natural choice to extend (
1.2
) from
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
is the space
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Sigma):=\{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega):u(\Sigma)=0\}.
In this way, in §
4.2
, we establish the extension of (
1.2
) to
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Sigma)
under the Assumption
3
, see Theorem
4.4
.

In §
5
, – as a direct application of the previous results – we establish sharp Hardy inequalities on manifolds with upper curvature bounds. In fact, we work under the ‘double-curvature’ condition in the sense that
(M,\Sigma,\Omega)
satisfies the upper curvature bound condition
(\lambda,\kappa)
, i.e. the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
and the mean curvature of
\Sigma
are controlled from above by
\lambda
and
\kappa
, respectively. Thus, if
(M,\Sigma,\Omega)
satisfies the upper curvature bound condition
(\lambda,\kappa)
, and
\Sigma
is a totally umbilical submanifold of
(M,g)
, in §
5.1

we prove the following Hardy inequality

(1.3)

\int_{\Omega}|{\nabla}u|^{p}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)^{1-p}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r)^{p+\beta}}{(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r))^{n}}\dvol_{g}\geq\left({\beta+m-n\over p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r)^{\beta}}{(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r))^{n}}\dvol_{g},

for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, see Theorem
5.1
, where
\beta>-(m-n)
,
\lambda\leq\Lambda\leq 0
and
\kappa\leq K
with either
K\leq 0
or

K\geq 0
and
K^{2}\leq-\Lambda
, while
\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}
are solutions to the ordinary differential equation
f^{\prime\prime}(t)+\Lambda f(t)=0
,
t>0
, see (
2.1
).
The proof of (
1.3
) is based on the verification of Assumption
2
(see Theorem
4.1
) for the pair of functions
(\phi,\psi)
defined by

(1.4)

\phi(t):=(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-\frac{n}{p-1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{-\frac{m-n-1}{p-1}},\quad\psi(t):=\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{\beta+m-n},\quad\forall\,t\in(0,\min\{\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda},\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}\}),

where the constants
\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}
are related to the first positive zeros of
\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}
, respectively, if they do exist. In addition, it follows that (
1.3
) is sharp if either
\Sigma
is compact with
\Sigma\subset\Omega
or
\Omega
is bounded with
\partial\Omega=\Sigma,
and there is no extremal in either case. In particular, when
\beta=-p
,
\lambda=\Lambda=\kappa=K=0
and
\Sigma=\{x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
(thus
n=0
), inequality (
1.3
) reduces to the sharp Hardy inequality of Carron
[
9
]
; other choices of parameters provide further particular forms of the Hardy inequality. In §
5.2
, sharp Hardy inequalities involving logarithmic double-curvature weights are established, see Theorem
5.2
.

In §
6
, we study Hardy inequalities on manifolds with lower curvature bounds. Although such inequalities seem to be simple counterparts of the ones from §
5
, it turns out that genuine differences appear between the two settings. For instance, Bonnet–Myers theorem implies the compactness of the manifold whenever the Ricci curvature is bounded from below by a positive constant, thus Hardy inequalities may yield undesirable phenomena, see §
4.2
. In a similar manner as in §
5
,
we consider the ‘double-curvature’ condition in the sense that
(M,\Sigma,\Omega)
satisfies the lower curvature bound condition
(\lambda,\kappa)
, i.e. the Ricci curvature of
(M,g)
and the mean curvature of
\Sigma
are controlled from below by
\lambda
and
\kappa
, respectively. Under such curvature assumptions, we establish in §
6.1
the counterpart of (
1.3
) for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\Sigma)
(instead of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
),
see Theorem
6.1
/(i)&(ii), respectively. The sharpness and nonexistence of extremal functions are also discussed in both spaces
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
and
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\Sigma)
. The proof of Theorem
6.1
relies on Theorem
4.4
by checking Assumption
3
.
If
\lambda=\Lambda=\kappa=K=0
, Theorem
6.1
reduces to the sharp Hardy inequality of Chen, Leung and Zhao
[
10
]
and Meng, Wang and Zhao
[
28
]
.
Finally, a sharp Hardy inequality is stated with logarithmic double-curvature weights in §
6.2
, see Theorem
6.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Submanifold geometry & Fermi coordinates

We recall some definitions and properties of submanifolds, focusing mainly on Fermi coordinates; see Chavel
[
5
, Sections 3.6 & 7.3]
, Heintze and Karcher
[
20
]
, and Dajczer and Tojeiro
[
11
]

for details. We first introduce the following notations.

Given a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}t)}{\sqrt{\lambda}},&&\text{ if }\lambda>0,\\
\ \ \ \ t,&&\text{ if }\lambda=0,\\
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-\lambda}t)}{\sqrt{-\lambda}},&&\text{ if }\lambda<0,\end{array}\right.\qquad\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}t)},&&\text{ if }\lambda>0,\\
\ \ \ \ \ 1,&&\text{ if }\lambda=0,\\
{\cosh(\sqrt{-\lambda}t)},&&\text{ if }\lambda<0,\end{array}\right.

where
0\leq t\leq 2\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}
with

(2.8)

\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\pi\over 2\sqrt{\lambda},&&\text{ if }\lambda>0,\\
\\
+\infty,&&\text{ if }\lambda\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

It is easy to check that
\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing on
[0,\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}]
and
both
\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}
are solutions to the ODE
f^{\prime\prime}(t)+\lambda f(t)=0
with additional properties

(2.9)

\mathbf{s}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(t)=\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t),\quad\mathbf{c}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(t)=-\lambda\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t),\quad\lambda\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)^{2}+\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)^{2}=1.

Given
\lambda,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa}
be the first positive zero of
\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}
(if it exists), i.e.,

(2.10)

\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa}:=\sup\left\{t>0\,:\,\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(s)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(s)>0\text{ for $s\in(0,t)$}\right\}.

A direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{\kappa}<\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ if $\lambda=0$ and $\kappa>0$},\\
\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\arctan\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{|\kappa|}<\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ if $\lambda>0$ and $\kappa>0$},\\
\\
\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}=\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ if $\lambda>0$ and $\kappa=0$},\\
\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\left(\pi-\arctan\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{|\kappa|}\right)\in(\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},2\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}),&&\text{ if $\lambda>0$ and $\kappa<0$},\\
\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\lambda}}\text{artanh}\frac{\sqrt{-\lambda}}{|\kappa|}<\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ if $\lambda<0$ and $\kappa>0$ with $\kappa^{2}>-\lambda$},\\
\\
+\infty=\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

In particular, it follows that

(2.23)

\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa}\leq 2\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}.

Moreover, it is immediate that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\left[\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)}{\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)}-\kappa\right]<0,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\kappa}\left[\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)}{\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)}-\kappa\right]<0,\quad\forall\,t\in(0,2\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda}),

which implies

(2.24)

\mathfrak{c}_{\Lambda,K}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa}\quad\text{ for any }\lambda\leq\Lambda,\ \kappa\leq K.

The following result plays an important role in Sections
5
–
6
; for completeness, the proof is given in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(2.25)

\mathfrak{t}_{\lambda,\kappa}:=\mathfrak{t}_{n,\lambda,\kappa}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa},&&\text{ if }n>0,\\
\\
2\mathfrak{r}_{\lambda},&&\text{ if }n=0.\end{array}\right.

Define
a function
G_{\lambda,\kappa}:(0,\mathfrak{t}_{\lambda,\kappa})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(2.26)

G_{\lambda,\kappa}(t):=G_{m,n,\lambda,\kappa}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-n{\lambda\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)+\kappa\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)}+(m-n-1){\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)},&&\text{ if }n>0,\\
\\
(m-1){\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)},&&\text{ if }n=0.\end{array}\right.

If
m\geq n+1
, then
G_{\lambda,\kappa}
is decreasing in both
\lambda
and
\kappa
, i.e., for any
\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
\kappa\leq K
,

G_{\Lambda,K}(t)\leq G_{\lambda,\kappa}(t),\quad\forall\,t\in(0,\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}).

Given

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and let

(i)

\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}(t)

is a minimal geodesic from
\Sigma

;

(iii)

is the largest starlike domain, i.e.,

(iv)

\F:[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{V}S\Sigma\rightarrow M,\quad(t,{\bf n})\mapsto\text{Exp}(t{\bf n}).

Moreover, we have the following result; see Chavel

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\F(t,\mathbf{n})
is well-defined, then

(i)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}:={\dd}{\F}_{(t,\mathbf{n})}\mathbf{n}={\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)
, i.e., the tangent vector of
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}(t)
at
t;

(ii)

given
X\in T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma
,
J_{X}(t):={\dd}{\F}_{(t,\mathbf{n})}X
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
, orthogonal to
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
, with

J_{X}(0)=X,\quad(\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(0)}J_{X})(0)=-\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}(X);

(iii)

given
Y\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma\cap\mathbf{n}^{\bot}
,
J_{Y}(t):={\dd}{\F}_{(t,\mathbf{n})}Y
is a Jacobi field along
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
, orthogonal to
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
, with

J_{Y}(0)=0,\quad(\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(0)}J_{Y})(0)=Y,

where
\mathbf{n}^{\bot}:=\{Y\in T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}M:\,g(\mathbf{n},Y)=0\}
.

Now we study the construction of such Jacobi fields.
Let
R
be the
curvature tensor
of
(M,g)
, i.e.,

R(U,V)W=\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}W-\nabla_{V}\nabla_{U}W-\nabla_{[U,V]}W,

where
U,V,W
are three smooth vector fields on
M
. Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
,
denote by
P_{t;\mathbf{n}}
the
parallel transport
along

\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}
from
T_{\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}(0)}M
to
T_{\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}(t)}M
for all
t\geq 0
. Set

R_{{\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)}:=R\left(\,\cdot\,,{\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)\right){\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)
and

\mathcal{R}(t,\mathbf{n}):=P^{-1}_{t;\mathbf{n}}\circ R_{{\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)}\circ P_{t;\mathbf{n}}:\mathbf{n}^{\bot}\rightarrow\mathbf{n}^{\bot}.

Let
\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})
be the solution of the matrix (or linear
transformation) ordinary differential equation on
\mathbf{n}^{\bot}
:

(2.32)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{A}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t,\mathbf{n})+\mathcal{R}(t,\mathbf{n})\,\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})=0,\\
\\
\mathcal{A}(0,\mathbf{n})|_{T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma}=\id|_{T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma},\ \mathcal{A}^{\prime}(0,\mathbf{n})|_{T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma}=-\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}},\\
\\
\mathcal{A}(0,\mathbf{n})|_{\mathcal{V}_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma\cap\mathbf{n}^{\bot}}=0,\ \mathcal{A}^{\prime}(0,\mathbf{n})|_{\mathcal{V}_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma\cap\mathbf{n}^{\bot}}=\id|_{\mathcal{V}_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma\cap\mathbf{n}^{\bot}},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\frac{\dd}{{\dd}t}\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{{\dd}^{2}}{{\dd}t^{2}}\mathcal{A}
. On account of Lemma
2.2
, we have

(2.33)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}:={\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)=P_{t;\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{n},\\
\\
J_{X}(t)={\dd}{\F}_{(t,\mathbf{n})}X=P_{t;\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})X,\quad\forall\,X\in T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma,\\
\\
J_{Y}(t)={\dd}{\F}_{(t,\mathbf{n})}Y=P_{t;\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})Y,\quad\forall\,Y\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma\cap\mathbf{n}^{\bot}.\end{array}\right.

It should be emphasized that
\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})>0
for any
t\in(0,c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n}))
.
Moreover, (
2.32
) yields

(2.34)

\displaystyle\underset{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})}{t^{m-n-1}}=1,\quad\frac{(\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n}))^{\prime}}{\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})}=\frac{(m-n-1)}{t}-\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}+O(t)\ \ {\rm as}\ \ t\to 0.

Using the Fermi coordinate system
(t,{\bf n})
, the
Riemannian measure

\dvol_{g}
of
(M,g)
can be represented as

(2.35)

\displaystyle{\dvol}_{g}(t,\mathbf{n})=\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n}){\dd}t\cdot{\dvol}_{i^{*}g}(x)\cdot d\nu_{x}(\mathbf{n}),

where
{\dvol}_{i^{*}g}
is the
induced Riemannian measure
of
(\Sigma,i^{*}g)
and
{\dd}\nu_{x}
is the Riemannian measure of
\mathcal{V}S_{x}\Sigma
with
x=\pi(\mathbf{n})
.
Thus, for
f\in L^{1}(M)
, the change of variables formula reads as

\int_{M}f\,{\dvol}_{g}=\int_{\Sigma}{\dvol}_{i^{*}g}(x)\int_{\mathcal{V}S_{x}\Sigma}d\nu_{x}(\mathbf{n})\int_{0}^{c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n})}f(\Exp(t\mathbf{n}))\,\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n}){\dd}t.

Throughout the paper, we always use
\T_{s}
to denote the
s
-tuber neighborhood of
\Sigma
, i.e.,

(2.36)

\T_{s}:=\{x\in M\,:\,r(x)<s\}.

2.2.
Heintze–Karcher-type Laplacian estimates

In this subsection we establish two Heintze–Karcher-type Laplacian estimates for the distance function
r(x):=d(\Sigma,x)
. Given
x\in\mathcal{V}\mathscr{D}\Sigma\backslash\Sigma
, due to Lemma
2.1
/(
iv
) and (
2.31
), there is a unique
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
and
t=r(x)\in(0,c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n}))
such that
x=\F(t,\mathbf{n})
. A direct calculation (cf. Chen et al.
[
10
, (2.6) & (2.9)]
) yields

(2.37)

\nabla r|_{(t,\mathbf{n})}={\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{(t,\mathbf{n})},\quad\Delta r|_{(t,{\bf n})}=\Delta t={(\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n}))^{\prime}\over\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n})}.

In the sequel, we provide a slightly finer version of the Heintze–Karcher theorem when the curvature is controlled from below; compare with
[
20
, Section 3.2]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
\sec_{M}\geq\lambda
, and
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional closed submanifold. Then, for every
{\bf n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
, one has

(2.38)

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})\leq\left[\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\frac{\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}}{n}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)\right]^{n}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)^{m-n-1},\quad\forall\,t\in[0,c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n})],

(2.39)

\displaystyle\Delta r|_{(t,\mathbf{n})}\leq G_{\lambda,\frac{\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}}{n}}(t)=-n{\lambda\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)+\frac{\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}}{n}\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\frac{\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}}{n}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)}+(m-n-1){\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)},\quad\forall\,t\in(0,c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n})).

Moreover, provided either
n=0

(
i.e.,
\Sigma
is a point
)
or
n=m-1

(
i.e.,
\Sigma
is a hypersurface
),
then both (
2.38
) and (
2.39
) remain valid when
\Ric_{M}\geq(n-1)\lambda
instead of
\sec_{M}\geq\lambda
.

Proof.

If
n=0
, the statement is nothing but the well-known Bishop comparison theorem; see Chavel
[
5
, Theorem 3.8]
. When
n=m-1
, the statement is exactly the result of Chen et al.
[
10
, Theorem 2.5]
; see also
[
5
, Theorem 3.14]
. In the sequel, we show the general case, whose proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1.
We first show that under the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}=n\kappa
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(2.40)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}{(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})(t)}\leq n{(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})(t)},\quad 0<t<\min_{i}\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa_{i}},

with equality if and only if
\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}=\kappa
; here,
\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa_{i}}
is defined by (
2.10
).

Let
0<t<\min_{i}\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa_{i}}
be fixed; we prove (
2.40
) by using Lagrange multipliers. Let us consider the Lagrange function

\Phi(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n},\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})(t)}+\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}-n\kappa\right),

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the Lagrange multiplier. By using (
2.9
), we obtain for any
1\leq j\leq n
that

\displaystyle{{\partial}\Phi\over{\partial}\kappa_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa_{j}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t))^{2}}+\mu.

Therefore, the unique critical point of
\Phi
is
\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}=\kappa
and
\mu=(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t))^{-2}
. Moreover, the Hessian of
\Phi
at this critical point is

\left({{\partial}^{2}\Phi\over{\partial}\kappa_{i}{\partial}\kappa_{j}}\right)(\kappa,\ldots,\kappa,(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t))^{-2})=\left({-2\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)\delta_{ij}\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t))^{3}}\right)_{i,j=1,...,n},

which is negative definite. Accordingly, this critical point is the maximum of
\Phi
under the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}=n\kappa
; hence (
2.40
) follows.

Step 2.
We now complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
, it follows from the proof the Heintze–Karcher comparison theorem, see
[
5
, Theorem 7.5]
or
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
, that
c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n})\leq\min_{i}\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\kappa_{i}}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{\lambda,\frac{\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}}{n}}
and

(2.41)

\displaystyle{(\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n}))^{\prime}\over\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}{(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)\over(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}-\kappa_{i}\mathbf{s}_{\lambda})(t)}+(m-n-1){\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)\over\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)},\quad\forall\,t\in(0,c_{\mathcal{V}}({\bf n})),

where
\{\kappa_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to some orthonormal basis of
T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma
.
Therefore, (
2.38
) and (
2.39
) follow by (
2.40
), (
2.41
), (
2.34
) and (
2.37
)
2
; here, (
2.37
)
2
denotes the second relation in (
2.37
).
∎

.

In order to prove Theorem

, we need some auxiliary notions and results.
Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma

be a geodesic with

along

The standard Jacobi criteria reads as follows, see Chavel
[

Lemma 2.3

(Jacobi Criteria)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold and
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional closed submanifold. Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
,
let
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}:[0,b]\rightarrow M
be a geodesic satisfying
{\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(0)=\mathbf{n}
,
b\in(0,c_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{n}))
and
let

be a transverse Jacobi field along

. Then

I_{b}(X,X)\geq I_{b}(J,J),\quad\forall\,X\in\Upsilon_{\mathbf{n}}\text{ with }X(b)=J(b),

with equality if and only if
X=J
.

We are also going to apply the following result by Dajczer and Tojeiro
[
11

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\MM}_{\lambda}
be an
m
-dimensional simply connected complete Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature
\lambda
and let
\widetilde{o}\in{\MM}_{\lambda}
be a fixed point. Then for every
n(\geq 1)
-dimensional proper vector subspace
\VV\subset T_{\widetilde{o}}{\MM}_{\lambda}
and every vector
\HH\in T_{\widetilde{o}}{\MM}_{\lambda}
orthogonal to
\VV
, there exists a unique
n
-dimensional complete totally umbilical submanifold
\widetilde{\Sigma}
through
\widetilde{o}
satisfying

•

T_{\widetilde{o}}\widetilde{\Sigma}=\VV
and
\HH
is the mean curvature vector of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
at
\widetilde{o};

•

\widetilde{\Sigma}
is extrinsic sphere, i.e., its mean curvature is parallel
;

•

the sectional curvature
\sec_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\equiv\lambda+|\HH|^{2}
and particularly,
{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
is totally geodesic if and only if
\HH=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\widetilde{H}
denote the mean curvature of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Since
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is an extrinsic sphere, we have
|\widetilde{H}|(\tilde{x})\equiv|\HH|
for any
\tilde{x}\in\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Moreover, for any
\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathcal{V}S_{\tilde{x}}\widetilde{\Sigma}
, it follows by (
2.27

\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}=\langle\widetilde{H},\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\rangle\id_{T_{\tilde{x}}\widetilde{\Sigma}}.

In particular, given
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\kappa|=|\widetilde{H}|(\tilde{x})
, we have
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}=\kappa\id_{T_{\tilde{x}}\widetilde{\Sigma}}
by choosing

(2.46)

\tilde{\mathbf{n}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{sgn}(\kappa)\frac{\widetilde{H}(\tilde{x})}{|\widetilde{H}(\tilde{x})|},&\text{ if }\kappa\neq 0,\\
\\
\text{any unit normal vector in $\mathcal{V}S_{\tilde{x}}\widetilde{\Sigma}$},&\text{ if }\kappa=0.\end{array}\right.

2.2
.
Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma

•

•

For convenience, let

Step 1
. Given

’s are transverse Jacobi fields, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0),\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0)+\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0))\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}J^{i}_{\alpha}(0)(({J}^{i}_{\alpha})^{\prime}(0)+\kappa J^{i}_{\alpha}(0))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}J^{i}_{\alpha}(0)(({J}^{i}_{\alpha})^{\prime}(0)+\kappa_{i}J^{i}_{\alpha}(0))

\displaystyle=\langle J_{\alpha}(0),{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}J_{\alpha}(0)+{\mathfrak{A}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}(J_{\alpha}(0))\rangle=0.

A similar calculation combined with the construction of
\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}
and
\sec_{M}({\gamma}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(t),\cdot)\leq\lambda
furnishes

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{t_{0}}(\widetilde{X}_{\alpha},\widetilde{X}_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(t_{0}),\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(t_{0})\rangle-\langle\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0),\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0)+\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}^{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}(\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}(0))\rangle

\displaystyle-\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\left(\langle\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\widetilde{X}_{\alpha},\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}\rangle+\lambda|{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\wedge\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}|^{2}\right){\dd}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0}),{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0})\rangle-\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\left(\langle{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}{J_{\alpha}},J_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle R_{\gamma^{\prime}}(J_{\alpha}),J_{\alpha}\rangle\right){\dd}t=\langle{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0}),{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0})\rangle,

which together with (
2.52
) and a standard calculation, see Chavel
[
5
, p. 325]
, yields

(2.53)

\left.\frac{1}{2}{(\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n}))^{\prime}\over\det\mathcal{A}(t,{\bf n})}\right|_{t=t_{0}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m-1}\langle{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0}),{\nabla}_{{{\gamma}^{\prime}}}{J_{\alpha}}(t_{0})\rangle\geq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m-1}\langle\tilde{J}_{\alpha}(t_{0}),\widetilde{\nabla}_{{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\tilde{J}_{\alpha}(t_{0})\rangle=\left.\frac{1}{2}{(\det\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t,\tilde{{\bf n}}))^{\prime}\over\det\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t,\tilde{{\bf n}})}\right|_{t=t_{0}},

where
\det\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t,\tilde{{\bf n}}):=(\mathbf{c}_{\lambda}(t)-\kappa\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t))^{n}\,\mathbf{s}_{\lambda}(t)^{m-n-1}
. Therefore, (
2.43
) follows by (
2.37
) and (
2.53
), while (
2.34
) combined with (
2.53
) implies (

).

\square

2.3.
Weak divergence

In our approach, weak divergence plays an important role in establishing Hardy inequalities; first we recall its definition, see D’Ambrosio
[
6
]
and D’Ambrosio and Dipierro
[
7
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

Given an open subset
U\subset M
, let
L^{1}_{\lo}(TU)
and
L^{1}_{\lo}(U)
denote the locally integrable vector fields and functions on
U
(w.r.t.
\vol_{g}
), respectively. That is, for any compact set
\mathscr{K}\subset U
,

\int_{\mathscr{K}}|X|\dvol_{g}<\infty,\ \forall\,X\in L^{1}_{\lo}(TU);\quad\int_{\mathscr{K}}|f|\dvol_{g}<\infty,\ \forall\,f\in L^{1}_{\lo}(U).

Given a vector field

X\in L^{1}_{\lo}(TU)
and a non-negative function
f_{X}\in L^{1}_{\lo}(U)
, we say
f_{X}\leq\di X

in the weak sense
if

\int_{U}uf_{X}{\dvol}_{g}\leq-\int_{U}\langle\nabla u,X\rangle{\dvol}_{g},\quad\forall\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\text{ with }u\geq 0.

Proceeding as in D’Ambrosio
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
or D’Ambrosio and Dipierro
[
7
, Lemma 2.10]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
U
be an open set of
M
.
Given
p>1
, if a vector field
X\in L^{1}_{\lo}(T{U})
and a non-negative function
f_{X}\in L^{1}_{\lo}({U})
satisfy
|X|^{p}/f_{X}^{p-1}\in L^{1}_{\lo}({U})
and
f_{X}\leq\di X
in the weak sense, then

\displaystyle p^{p}\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{p}\frac{|X|^{p}}{f_{X}^{p-1}}{\dvol}_{g}\geq\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{U}|u|^{p}f_{X}{\dvol}_{g},\quad\forall\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}({U}).

In order to adapt to our setting, we extend the weak divergence to Lipschitz functions, see Proposition
2.2
.
For convenience, we use
\mathsf{Lip}(V)
(resp.,
\mathsf{Lip}_{\lo}(V))
to denote the collection of Lipschitz (resp., locally Lipschitz) functions on
V
. Moreover, let
\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(V):=C_{0}(V)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(V)
, i.e., the collection of Lipschitz functions on
V
with compact support. Given a Lipschitz function
f
,
the minimal
C>0
satisfying the inequality

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq Cd(x,y),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\text{ the domain of $f$},

is called the
dilatation
of
f
, denoted by
\mathsf{dil}(f)
, see Burago, Burago and Ivanov
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
U\subset M
be an open set. Then
C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)
and
hu\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)
for every
h\in\mathsf{Lip}_{\lo}(U)
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)
.

The proof of Lemma
2.6
directly follows from the definitions, thus we omit it. By Rademacher’s theorem and local coordinate systems, we know that every Lipschitz function is differentiable
\vol_{g}
-a.e. Moreover, we have the following approximation result:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
U\subset M
be an open set and let
w:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function with compact support. Then there exists a sequence of Lipschitz functions
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)
such that

\supp w_{n}\subset\mathscr{K},\quad|w_{n}|\leq C,\quad|\nabla w_{n}|\leq\mathsf{dil}(w_{n})\leq C,\quad w_{n}\rightrightarrows w,\quad\|w_{n}-w\|_{W^{1,1}(U)}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{K}
is a compact set in
U
and
C>0
is a constant. In particular, if
w
is nonnegative, so are
w_{n}
’s.

The proof of Lemma
2.7
is based on standard approximation arguments, combining convolutions of suitable mollifiers with smooth partition of unity; therefore, we omit its proof.

The following proposition plays a crucial role in the proof of our main general result, see Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f_{X}\in L^{1}_{\loc}(U)
and
X\in L^{1}_{\loc}(TU)
satisfying
f_{X}\leq\dii X
in the weak sense. Then
for every nonnegative

h\in\mathsf{Lip}_{\lo}(U)

we have

(2.56)

\int_{U}(hu)\,f_{X}\dvol_{g}\leq-\int_{U}\langle\nabla(hu),X\rangle\dvol_{g},\quad\forall\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\text{ with }u\geq 0.

Proof.

Lemma
2.6
implies that
w=hu\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U);
thus, it suffices to show

(2.57)

\int_{U}wf_{X}{\dvol}_{g}\leq-\int_{U}\langle\nabla w,X\rangle{\dvol}_{g},\quad\forall\,w\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)\text{ with }w\geq 0.

If
w\in\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)
is a nonnegative function, Lemma

2.7
yields a sequence of functions
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{0}(U)
satisfying

(2.58)

\supp w_{n}\subset\mathscr{K},\quad 0\leq w_{n}\leq C,\quad|\nabla w_{n}|\leq\mathsf{dil}(w_{n})\leq C,\quad w_{n}\rightrightarrows w,\quad\|w_{n}-w\|_{W^{1,1}(U)}\rightarrow 0,

for some compact
\mathscr{K}\subset U
and constant
C\geq 0
.
Thus, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
w_{n}\rightarrow w
and
\nabla w_{n}\rightarrow\nabla w
point-wisely for
\vol_{g}
-a.e. Since
f_{X}\leq\dii X
in the weak sense, we have

(2.59)

\int_{U}w_{n}f_{X}\dvol_{g}\leq-\int_{U}\langle\nabla w_{n},X\rangle\dvol_{g}.

Note that (
2.58
) and the assumption imply that

|w_{n}f_{X}|\leq\chi_{\mathscr{K}}C|f_{X}|\in L^{1}(U),\quad|\langle\nabla w_{n},X\rangle|\leq\chi_{\mathscr{K}}C|X|\in L^{1}(U),

where
\chi_{\mathscr{K}}
stands for the characteristic function of
\mathscr{K}
.
Therefore (
2.57
) follows by (
2.59
) and the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

3.
A general Hardy inequality

In this section, we will establish a Hardy inequality involving a pair of parametric functions.

3.1.
Abstract construction

Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold,
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional closed submanifold, and
r
be the distance function from
\Sigma
. Inspired by the seminal paper of Barbatis, Filippas and Tertikas
[
1
]
– where the ambient space is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
– throughout the present paper,
\Omega\subset M
is always a non-empty domain (i.e., a connected open set) satisfying

(3.1)

(a)
\Sigma={\partial}\Omega
if
n=m-1
;   (b)
\Sigma\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
if
0\leq n\leq m-2
.

For convenience, set
\Omega_{\Sigma}:=\Omega\setminus\Sigma
.
Now
we introduce the following structural assumption.

Assumption 1
.

Given
p>1
, let
(\phi,\psi)
be a pair of two nonnegative
C^{1}
-functions defined on some subinterval of
(0,+\infty)
such that

(a)

\phi(r),\psi(r)
are well-defined in
\Omega_{\Sigma}
;

(b)

\psi
is monotone and
\psi(r)
is locally Lipschitz on
\Omega_{\Sigma}
;

(c)

\displaystyle\dii(\sgn(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)\geq 0
in the weak sense in
\Omega_{\Sigma}
;

(d)

\phi(r)^{p-1},\phi(r)^{p-1}\psi(r),\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|,\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
;

(e)

\{x\in\Omega_{\Sigma}\,:\,\phi(r(x))\,\psi(r(x))\,\psi^{\prime}(r(x))=0\}
is a
\vol_{g}
-negligible set.

Remark 3.1
.

Assumption (
a
) implies that
\phi^{\prime}(r),\psi^{\prime}(r)
are well-defined in
\Omega_{\Sigma}
. We notice that there is a close relation between
\dii(\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)
and
p
-Laplacian. In fact, let
\Phi
be the primitive function of
\phi
(i.e.,
\Phi^{\prime}=\phi
), which is usually locally defined. Then
\dii(\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)
is exactly the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}\Phi(r):=\dii(|{\nabla}\Phi(r)|^{p-2}{\nabla}\Phi(r))
.
Since
\psi
is monotone, then
(
c
) implies that
\Delta_{p}\Phi(r)\geq 0
or
\Delta_{p}\Phi(r)\leq 0
in the weak sense.
In particular, a direct calculation yields

(3.2)

\dii(\sgn(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)=\sgn(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,\phi(r)^{p-1}\left(\Delta r+(p-1)\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)}\right)\quad\text{ on }\mathcal{V}\mathscr{D}\Sigma\backslash\Sigma.

Since
M\backslash\mathcal{V}\mathscr{D}\Sigma=\Sigma\cup\mathcal{V}\mathscr{C}\Sigma
is a
\vol_{g}
-negligible set, one may use the Laplacian comparison (Theorems
2.1
–
2.2
) to determine the sign of
\dii(\sgn(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)
directly.

We notice that the structural assumptions (
a
)–(
d
) are enough to establish Hardy inequalities, see e.g. (
3.5
) below; the role of (
e
) is to make (
3.5
) nontrivial. Moreover, (
e
) is useful to investigate the non-existence of extremal functions in Hardy-type inequalities, see Section
3.3
.

Infinitely many pairs of
(\phi,\psi)
can be produced on space forms; the following example provides such constructions.

.

be the space form and

Note that if

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an

be an

-dimensional closed submanifold and

be the distance function from

for all

. Then, by Assumption

)(

4.2.
Extension to the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\Sigma)

Although expected, in some cases it is impossible to extend (
3.5
) to
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. Such phenomenon occurs for instance when
\Omega=M
is compact; indeed, in this case the constant functions belong to
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, and (
4.2
) provides a contradiction. A possible way to surpass this problem is to extend (
3.5
) from
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
to the space

(4.16)

C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Sigma):=\{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\,:\,u(\Sigma)=0\}.

In this subsection, we discuss the validity of
(
3.5
) on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Sigma)
under the following assumption.

Assumption 3
.

Given
p>1
, let
(\phi,\psi)
be a pair of two nonnegative
C^{2}
-functions defined on
some subinterval of
(0,+\infty)
such that

(a)

\phi(r),\psi(r)
are well-defined in
M\backslash\Sigma
;

(b)

\psi
is monotone and
\psi(r)
is locally Lipschitz on
\Omega_{\Sigma}
;

(c)

\displaystyle\dii(\sgn(\psi^{\prime}(r))\,\phi(r)^{p-1}{\nabla}r)\geq 0
in the weak sense in
\Omega_{\Sigma}
;

(d)

\phi(r)^{p-1},\phi(r)^{p-1}\psi(r),\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega_{\Sigma})
and
\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\in L^{1}_{\loc}(M)

(e)

\{x\in{\Omega_{\Sigma}}\,:\,\phi(r(x))\,\psi(r(x))\,\psi^{\prime}(r(x))=0\}
is a
\vol_{g}
-negligible set;

(f)

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log\left(\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\right)
is locally bounded (i.e., bounded on every compact set) in

.

Remark 4.4
.

Obviously, (

)–(

) are enough to obtain the Hardy inequality (
3.5
); in particular, Assumption

is stronger than Assumption

More precisely, (

) means that

are well-defined on

, which is necessary to define
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\right)
. Furthermore,
the local boundedness of
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\right)
, i.e., (

) and Corollary

We now introduce a new Sobolev space.

Definition 4.1

In Appendix

we provide further properties of the spaces

and

. In view of (

), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

5.
Application I: Sharp Hardy inequalities on manifolds with upper curvature bounds

As we pointed out, classical Hardy inequalities have been extended to Cartan–Hadamard manifolds, see Carron
[
9
]
, Kristály
[
25
]
, Yang et al.
[
33
]
. In this section, we consider a more general case when the curvature is bounded from above; more precisely, by recalling (
2.29
), we consider the following setting:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional closed
totally umbilical
submanifold and
\Omega
be a non-empty domain in
M
satisfying (
3.1
). Given
\lambda,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, the triple

(M,\Sigma,\Omega)
satisfies the
upper curvature bound condition
(\lambda,\kappa)
if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

{\sec}_{M}\leq\lambda
and
n=0
;

(b)

{\sec}_{M}\leq\lambda
,
n=m-1
and
\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}\leq n\kappa
for any inward normal vector
\mathbf{n}
w.r.t.
\Omega
;

(c)

\sec_{M}\leq\lambda
,
1\leq n\leq m-2
and
\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}\leq n\kappa
for any
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
.

Remark 5.1
.

Since
\Sigma
is a totally umbilical submanifold, for a given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{V}S\Sigma
the eigenvalues
\{\kappa_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to every orthonormal basis of
T_{\pi(\mathbf{n})}\Sigma
are equal, i.e.,

\kappa_{1}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}
.
Thus,
\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}\leq n\kappa
implies each
\kappa_{i}\leq\kappa
. Moreover,

•

(
a
) means that
\Sigma
is a point and hence, the Weingarten map always vanishes, in which case
\kappa
can be chosen as any real number (because it is meaningless);

•

(
c
) implies
\kappa\geq 0
because
-n\kappa\leq-\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}=\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{-n}}\leq n\kappa
. In particular, if
\kappa=0
then
0=\tr\mathfrak{A}^{\mathbf{n}}=n\kappa_{i}
for each
\kappa_{i}
, i.e.,
\kappa_{i}=0
and therefore,
\Sigma
is actually totally geodesic.

According to the last statement – although in the sequel we will not point out explicitly the sign of
\kappa
– we should keep in mind that
\kappa\geq 0
whenever
1\leq n\leq m-2
.

5.1.
Hardy inequalities involving double-curvature weights

In the sequel we use
(\lambda,\kappa)\leq(\Lambda,K)
to denote
\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
\kappa\leq K
, respectively.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold,
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
be an
n
-dimensional closed totally umbilical submanifold and
\Omega
be a noncompact domain in
M
satisfying (
3.1
). Denote
r(x):=d(\Sigma,x)
by the distance function from
\Sigma
.
Suppose that
(M,\Sigma,\Omega)
satisfies the upper curvature bound condition
(\lambda,\kappa)
.
Then for any
p>1
,
\beta>-(m-n)
and any pair
(\Lambda,K)
satisfying one of the following conditions

(i)

(\lambda,\kappa)\leq(\Lambda,K)\leq(0,0);

(ii)

(\lambda,\kappa)\leq(\Lambda,K)
,
\Lambda\leq 0
,
K\geq 0
and
K^{2}\leq-\Lambda
,

there holds

(5.1)

\int_{\Omega}|{\nabla}u|^{p}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)^{1-p}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r)^{p+\beta}}{(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r))^{n}}\dvol_{g}\geq\left({\beta+m-n\over p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r)^{\beta}}{(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(r)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(r))^{n}}\dvol_{g},

for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, (
5.1
) is sharp if either
\Sigma
is compact with
\Sigma\subset\Omega
or
\Omega
is bounded with
\partial\Omega=\Sigma,
and there is no extremal in either case.

Proof.

Let
(\phi,\psi)
be the pair of functions defined as

(5.2)

\phi(t):=(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-\frac{n}{p-1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{-\frac{m-n-1}{p-1}},\quad\psi(t):=\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{\beta+m-n},\quad\forall\,t\in(0,\min\{\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda},\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}\}).

It follows from (
2.1
), (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) that

\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}=+\infty=\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}
.

We claim that
(\phi,\psi)
satisfies Assumption
2
.
Indeed, Assumption
2
/(
a
)(
b
)(
f
) are obviously true.
Besides, (
2.43
) together with Proposition
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\Delta r\geq G_{\lambda,\kappa}(r)\geq G_{\Lambda,K}(r)=-(p-1)\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathcal{V}\mathscr{D}\Sigma\backslash\Sigma,

which combined with (
3.2
) and
\psi^{\prime}>0
furnishes Assumption
2
/(
d
).
Moreover, if
\alpha:=\beta+m-n>0
, a direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\phi(t)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\psi(t)^{s}\sim t^{-(m-n-1)+s\alpha},\quad\phi(t)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|=\alpha(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-n}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{\beta}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)\sim\alpha t^{\beta},

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(t)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(t)|}{|(\log\psi(t))^{\prime}|^{p}}=\alpha^{1-p}(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-n}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{p+\beta}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)^{1-p}\sim\alpha^{1-p}t^{p+\beta},

as

. Thus, (
2.34
)
1
combined with
\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}=\mathfrak{t}_{\Lambda,K}=+\infty
furnishes Assumption
2
/(
c
)(
e
). Therefore, the claim is true and (
5.1
) follows by Theorem
4.1
directly.
We now discuss the sharpness.

(a)
Suppose that
\Sigma
is compact with
\Sigma\subset\Omega
.
Thus, owing to Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
, it suffices to check (
4.13
). By choosing a small

we obtain that for any

\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\phi(t)^{p-1}|(\log\psi(t))^{\prime}|\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)^{n}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{m-n-1}{\dd}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\lim_{{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-n}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{-1}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)^{n+1}{\dd}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\lim_{{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{-1}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t){\dd}t=+\infty,

i.e., (
4.13
) holds.

(b)
Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega=\Sigma
, in which case
n=m-1
, see (
3.1
). Let
D:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}r(x)<+\infty
. The completeness of

implies the compactness of
\Sigma

. Owing to (
2.34
)

, there exists

\det\mathcal{A}(t,\mathbf{n})\leq 2\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{m-n-1}=2,\text{ for }(t,\mathbf{n})\in[0,s_{0}]\times\mathcal{V}S\Sigma,

which together with (

\displaystyle\int_{{\T_{s_{0}}}\cap\,\Omega}\psi(r)^{1+\varepsilon}\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|\dvol_{g}\leq 2\alpha\vol_{i^{*}g}(\Sigma)\int^{s_{0}}_{0}(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-n}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{(2+\varepsilon)\alpha-1}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t){\dd}t

\displaystyle 2\alpha\vol_{i^{*}g}(\Sigma)\max_{t\in[0,s_{0}]}(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-n}\int^{s_{0}}_{0}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{(2+\varepsilon)\alpha-1}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t){\dd}t<+\infty.

The non-existence of extremal function is a direct consequence of Theorem

.
∎

5.2.
Hardy inequalities involving logarithmic double-curvature weights

In this subsection, we will establish a Hardy inequality whose weight involves logarithmic terms.
First, we need the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Given
\Lambda,s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
D\in(0,\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}]\cap\mathbb{R}
,
l_{1}\in(0,D)
and
l_{2}\in(l_{1},D]
, let

\displaystyle H_{1}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\int^{l_{1}}_{0}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)^{s_{2}}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(\tau){\dd}\tau,\quad H_{2}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\int^{l_{2}}_{l_{1}}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)^{s_{2}}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(\tau){\dd}\tau.

Then we have

•

H_{1}
is well-defined if either
s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
s_{2}>-1
or
s_{1}<-1
,
s_{2}=-1;

•

H_{2}
is well-defined if either
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
l_{2}<D
or
s_{1}>-1
,
s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
l_{2}=D
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}]\cap\mathbb{R}
, by changing variables
\sigma:=\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)
we obtain

H_{1}(s_{1},s_{2})=\int^{\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(l_{1})}_{0}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\sigma}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\sigma^{s_{2}}{\dd}\sigma,\quad H_{2}(s_{1},s_{2})=\int^{\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(l_{2})}_{\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(l_{1})}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\sigma}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\sigma^{s_{2}}{\dd}\sigma,

which together with Chen et al.
[
10
, Lemma 3.12]
concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Note that
\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}<+\infty
if

\mathfrak{r}_{\Lambda}=+\infty

Given

\phi(t):=(\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)-K\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t))^{-{n\over p-1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{-{m-n-1\over p-1}},\quad\psi(t):=\psi(t,s_{1},s_{2}):=\int^{t}_{0}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(\tau)^{s_{2}}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(\tau){\dd}\tau.

Basic properties of the function
\psi
are collected in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

The function
\psi
in (

) is well-defined and strictly increasing in
[0,D)
if
s_{1}<-1
and
s_{2}\geq-1
.
In addition, if

,
then

(5.7)

\psi(t)\leq s^{-1}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}+1}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{s_{2}+1}=s^{-1}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)}\right)\right]{\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)\over\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)}\psi^{\prime}(t),\ \ t\in[0,D),

with equality if

and
s_{2}=-1

Proof.

Set

F(t):=\psi(t)-s^{-1}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}+1}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{s_{2}+1},\quad\forall\,t\in(0,D).

Clearly,
F(0):=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(t)=0
.
Since
\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)\leq\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)
for

, we have

F^{\prime}(t)=s^{-1}\left[\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)}\right)\right]^{s_{1}}\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)^{s_{2}}\mathbf{c}_{\Lambda}(t)\left[s_{1}+1+s-(s_{2}+1)\log\left({\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(D)\over\mathbf{s}_{\Lambda}(t)}\right)\right]\leq 0,

which implies

for any

and

6.
Application II: Sharp Hardy inequalities on manifolds with lower curvature bounds

In this section we study Hardy inequalities on manifolds with curvature bounded from below. The generic setting of the present section is described in the following definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m

n

We use

to denote the lower bound of

•

(

•

As in the previous section, although we do not emphasize the sign of

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\T_{\epsilon}}\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\dvol_{g}\leq 4\vol(\mathbb{S}^{m-n-1})\vol_{i^{*}g}(\Sigma)\int^{\epsilon}_{0}t^{p+\beta+m-n-1}{\dd}t<+\infty,

It remains to show the nonexistence of extremal functions. Owing to the compactness of

∎

Appendix A
Monotonicity of
G_{\lambda,\kappa}

In this section, we provide the proof of Proposition
2.1
that states some important monotonicity properties of the function
G_{\lambda,\kappa}
defined by (
2.26
), i.e.,

\displaystyle G_{\lambda,\kappa}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\sqrt{\lambda}\left[-n{\sqrt{\lambda}+\kappa\cot(\sqrt{\lambda}t)\over\sqrt{\lambda}\cot(\sqrt{\lambda}t)-\kappa}+(m-n-1)\cot(\sqrt{\lambda}t)\right],&&\text{ if }\lambda>0,\\
\\
-{n\kappa\over 1-\kappa t}+{m-n-1\over t},&&\text{ if }\lambda=0,\\
\\
\sqrt{-\lambda}\left[-n{-\sqrt{-\lambda}+\kappa\coth(\sqrt{-\lambda}t)\over\sqrt{-\lambda}\coth(\sqrt{-\lambda}t)-\kappa}+(m-n-1)\coth(\sqrt{-\lambda}t)\right],&&\text{ if }\lambda<0.\end{array}\right.

Although the proof is elementary, for the sake of completeness, we provide its proof.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The monotonicity in
\kappa
follows by a similar calculation as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
(see Step 1). Hence, we focus on the monotonicity in
\lambda
.

Case 1
:
\lambda>0
. For convenience, set
z:=\sqrt{\lambda}
. Thus, in view of (
A
),
we study the monotonicity of

z\mapsto z\left[-n{z+\kappa\cot(zt)\over z\cot(zt)-\kappa}+(m-n-1)\cot(zt)\right]=:\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}(z,t).

A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}}{\partial z}(z,t)=

\displaystyle-n{z^{2}[\cot(zt)+zt\text{csc}^{2}(zt)]+\kappa^{2}[zt\text{csc}^{2}(zt)-\cot(zt)]-2\kappa z\over(z\cot(zt)-\kappa)^{2}}

(A.6)

\displaystyle+(m-n-1)[\cot(zt)-zt\csc^{2}(zt)].

On the other hand, we have the identities

\displaystyle A(x):=x\csc^{2}(x)+\cot(x)=\frac{x+\sin(x)\cos(x)}{\sin^{2}(x)}=\frac{x+\frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)}{\sin^{2}(x)}>0,\quad\forall\,x>0.

(A.7)

\displaystyle B(x):=x\csc^{2}(x)-\cot(x)=\frac{x-\sin(x)\cos(x)}{\sin^{2}(x)}=\frac{x-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)}{\sin^{2}(x)}>0,\quad\forall\,x>0.

Since
\sin^{2}x\leq x^{2}
, we have
x^{2}\csc^{2}x-1\geq 0
and hence,

\displaystyle z^{2}A(x)+\kappa^{2}B(x)

\displaystyle\geq 2z|\kappa|A(x)B(x)=2z|\kappa|\left(x^{2}\csc^{4}x-\cot^{2}x\right)

\displaystyle=2z|\kappa|\left(\csc^{2}x(x^{2}\csc^{2}x-1)+1\right)\geq 2z|\kappa|\geq 2\kappa z,

which together with (
A.6
) and (
A.7
) implies
\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}}{\partial z}(z,t)<0
, i.e.,
G_{\lambda,\kappa}(t)
is decreasing in
\lambda
.
Furthermore, by using
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sqrt{\lambda}\cot(\sqrt{\lambda}t)=1/t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}}G_{\lambda,\kappa}(t)=-{n\kappa\over 1-\kappa t}+{m-n-1\over t}=G_{0,\kappa}(t).

Case 2
:
\lambda<0
.
By setting
w:=\sqrt{-\lambda}
in (
A
), we need to study the monotonicity of

w\mapsto w\left[-n{-w+\kappa\coth(wt)\over w\coth(wt)-\kappa}+(m-n-1)\coth(wt)\right]=:\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}(w,t).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}}{\partial w}(w,t)=

\displaystyle n{w^{2}[wt\text{csch}^{2}(wt)+\coth(wt)]+\kappa^{2}[\coth(wt)-wt\text{csch}^{2}(wt)]-2\kappa w\over(w\coth(wt)-\kappa)^{2}}

(A.8)

\displaystyle+(m-n-1)[\coth(wt)-wt\text{csch}^{2}(wt)].

Let

(A.9)

\displaystyle A(x):=\coth(x)+x\text{csch}^{2}(x)>0,\quad B(x):=\coth(x)-x\text{csch}^{2}(x)>0,\quad\forall\,x>0.

Since
1+\text{csch}^{2}x=\coth^{2}x
and
\sinh^{2}x\geq x^{2}
for any
x\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle w^{2}A(x)+\kappa^{2}B(x)

\displaystyle\geq 2w|\kappa|A(x)B(x)=2w|\kappa|(\coth^{2}x-x^{2}\text{csch}^{4}x)

\displaystyle=2w|\kappa|(\text{csch}^{2}x(1-x^{2}\text{csch}^{2}x)+1)\geq 2w|\kappa|\geq 2w\kappa.

which together with (
A.8
) and (
A.9
) yields
\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}}{\partial w}(w,t)>0
, which concludes the proof. ∎

{U}
) under the norm

\displaystyle[u]_{s,\mu}:=\left(\int_{{{U}}}|u|^{s}{\dmu}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}},\ \ \ \left(\text{ resp., }[X]_{s,\mu}:=\left(\int_{{{U}}}|X|^{s}{\dmu}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\right).

The standard theory of Lebesgue spaces yields the following result.

Theorem B.1
.

Both
L^{s}({U},\phi,\psi)
and
L^{s}(T{U},\phi,\psi)
are reflexive. Hence, if a sequence
\{u_{i}\}

(
resp.,
\{X_{i}\}
)
is bounded in

L^{s}({U},\phi,\psi)

(
resp.,
L^{s}(T{U},\phi,\psi)
),
then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{i_{k}}\}

(
resp.,
\{X_{i_{k}}\}
)
and
u\in L^{s}({U},\phi,\psi)

(
resp.,
X\in L^{s}(T{U},\phi,\psi)
)
such that
u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow u

weakly in
L^{s}({U},\phi,\psi)

(
resp.,
X_{i_{k}}\rightarrow X

weakly in
L^{s}(T{U},\phi,\psi)
)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Let
W^{1,p}(U,\phi,\psi)
be as in Definition
4.1
; thus, for any
u\in C^{\infty}({U})
, we have
\|u\|^{p}_{p}=[u]^{p}_{p,\mu}+[\nabla u]^{p}_{p,\mu}
.

Remark B.1
.

It is easy to check that the weak gradient in the sense of
W^{1,p}({U},\phi,\psi)
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\nabla(\lambda u+\zeta v)=\lambda\nabla u+\zeta\nabla v
for any
\lambda,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u,v\in W^{1,p}({U},\phi,\psi)
;

(b)

For any smooth vector field
X
with compact support in
{U}_{\Sigma}
, one has

(B.1)

\int_{{{U}}}g(\nabla u,X){\dmu}=-\int_{{{U}}}u\di_{\mu}X{\dmu},\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}({U},\phi,\psi),

where

\di_{\mu}(X){\dd}\mu:={\dd}(X\rfloor{\dd}\mu)
, i.e.,

(B.2)

\di_{\mu}(X)=\di(X)+\langle X,\nabla r\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log\left(\frac{\phi(r)^{p-1}|\psi^{\prime}(r)|}{|\nabla(\log\psi(r))|^{p}}\right).

In particular,
|\di_{\mu}(X)|<+\infty
follows by Assumption
3
/(
f
) and the compactness of
\supp X
.

In the sequel,
U
is either
M
or
\Omega_{\Sigma}
.
The same argument as in Corollary
3.2
yields the following result.

Lemma B.2
.

For
any globally Lipschitz function
u
with compact support in
U
, we have

u\in W_{0}^{1,p}({U},\phi,\psi)
.

Due to Lemma
B.2
, the following result can be proved in the same way as in Hebey
[
19
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Theorem B.2
.

W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)=W_{0}^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
.

Theorem
B.2
combined with the same proof of Zhao
[
34
, Lemma B.4]
produces the following result.

Corollary B.3
.

If
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
with compact support in
{\Omega_{\Sigma}}
, then
u|_{\Omega_{\Sigma}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\Sigma},\phi,\psi)
.

In order to show the reflexivity of
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, we
recall Mazur’s lemma (cf. Renardy and Rogers
[
31
, Lemma 10.19]
).

Lemma B.3

(Mazur’s lemma)
.

Assume that
X
is a Banach space and that

x_{i}\rightarrow x
weakly in
X
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Then there exists a sequence of convex
combinations

\tilde{x}_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{m_{i}}a_{i,j}x_{j},\ a_{i,j}\geq 0,\ \sum_{j=i}^{m_{i}}a_{i,j}=1,

such that
\tilde{x}_{i}\rightarrow x
strongly in
X
as
i\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem B.4
.

W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
is reflexive, i.e., given a bounded sequence
\{u_{i}\}
in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
and
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
such that
u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow u
weakly in
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow\nabla u
weakly in
L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\{u_{i}\}
is a bounded sequence in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, Theorem
B.1
yields a subsequence
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
and
u\in L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and a vector field
X\in L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
such that
u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow u
weakly in
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow X
weakly in
L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Consider the Banach space
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)\times L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
endowed with the product metric. Obviously,

(u_{i_{k}},\nabla u_{i_{k}})\rightarrow(u,X)\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)\times L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi).

Thus, by Lemma
B.3
we have a sequence of convex combinations such that

\sum_{k=l}^{m_{l}}a_{l,k}(u_{i_{k}},\nabla u_{i_{k}})\rightarrow(u,X)\text{ strongly in }L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)\times L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi),\text{ as }l\rightarrow\infty.

Set
v_{l}:=\sum_{k=l}^{m_{l}}a_{l,k}u_{i_{k}}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
. Then both
v_{l}\rightarrow u
strongly and
\nabla v_{l}\rightarrow X
strongly in the corresponding
[\cdot]_{p,\mu}
-norms. Hence,
(v_{l})
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, which implies
\nabla u=X
and
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
.
∎

Theorem
B.4
together with the dominated convergence theorem implies the following two consequences; we omit their proofs because they are standard.

Corollary B.5
.

If
u\in{W^{1,p}}(M,\phi,\psi)
, then
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
,
u_{-}:=-\min\{u,0\}
and
|u|=u_{+}-u_{-}
are all in
{W^{1,p}}(M,\phi,\psi)
. In particular,
\nabla|u|=\sgn(u)\nabla u
.

Corollary B.6
.

Given
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, we have
\max\{0,\min\{u,1\}\}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
. Moreover,
for any
\lambda>0
, set
u_{\lambda}:=\max\{-\lambda,\min\{u,\lambda\}\}
. Then
u_{\lambda}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proposition B.1
.

Given
u,v\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, the following statements hold
:

(i)

\max\{u,v\}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
|\nabla\max\{u,v\}|\leq\max\{|\nabla u|,|\nabla v|\}
;

(ii)

if
\|u\|_{\infty}+\|v\|_{\infty}<+\infty
, then
uv\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla(uv)=u\nabla v+v\nabla u
.

Proof.

Since
\max\{a,b\}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b+|a-b|)
, (
i
) immediately follows by Corollary
B.5
and Remark
B.1
/(
a
).
In order to prove (
ii
), by Theorem
B.2
we have two sequences
\{u_{i}\},\{v_{i}\}
in
C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
such that
u_{i}\rightarrow u
and
v_{i}\rightarrow v
under
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. Let
u^{*}_{i}:=\max\{-\lambda,\min\{u_{i},\lambda\}\}
and
v^{*}_{i}:=\max\{-\lambda,\min\{v_{i},\lambda\}\}
, where
\lambda
is a constant satisfying
\|u\|_{\infty}+\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda
. Thus,
u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i}
is a Lipschitz function with compact support, which together with Lemma
B.2
and Theorem
B.2
implies
u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
.
Moreover,
it follows from Corollary
B.6
that
u^{*}_{i},v^{*}_{i}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
u^{*}_{i}\rightarrow u
,
v^{*}_{i}\rightarrow v
under
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. Since
\|u^{*}_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|v^{*}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\lambda
,
it is easy to check
u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i}\rightarrow uv
and
\nabla(u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i})\rightarrow v\nabla u+u\nabla v
under the corresponding
[\cdot]_{p,\mu}
-norms.
Hence,
\{u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
. We conclude the proof by
u^{*}_{i}v^{*}_{i}\rightarrow uv
in
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.
∎

Theorem B.7
.

Suppose that
\{u_{i}\}
is a bounded sequence in
W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
u_{i}\rightarrow u
pointwise a.e. in
{M}
. Then
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
,
u_{i}\rightarrow u
weakly in
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla u_{i}\rightarrow\nabla u
weakly in
L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
.

Proof.

Choose an arbitrary subsequence
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
of
\{u_{i}\}
.
According to Theorem
B.4
, there is a subsequence of
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
, still denoted by
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
, and a function
v\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
such that
u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow v
weakly in
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow\nabla v
weakly in
L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
. We now show that
v=u
a.e. in
{M}
.

Lemma
B.3
together with the proof of Theorem
B.4
provides a sequence of convex combinations with

u^{*}_{k}:=\sum_{j=k}^{m_{k}}a_{k,j}u_{i_{j}}\rightarrow v,\ \ \nabla u^{*}_{k}=\sum_{j=k}^{m_{k}}a_{k,j}\nabla u_{i_{j}}\rightarrow\nabla v,\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty,

in the corresponding
[\cdot]_{p,\mu}
-norms. Thus, the standard theory of
L^{p}
-space together with Lemma
B.1
yields a subsequence of
\{u^{*}_{k}\}
converging pointwise to
v
a.e. in
{M}
. On the other hand, since
\{u_{j}\}
converges to
u
point-wisely, we see that
\{u^{*}_{k}\}
converges to
u
point-wisely as well. Consequently,

v=u
a.e. and therefore
\nabla u=\nabla v
a.e. in
{M}
.

Therefore, we proved that for every subsequence of
\{u_{i}\}
, there is another subsequence
\{u_{i_{k}}\}
such that
u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow u
weakly in
L^{p}(M,\phi,\psi)
and
\nabla u_{i_{k}}\rightarrow\nabla u
weakly in
L^{p}(TM,\phi,\psi)
, which indicates that the original sequence

\{u_{i}\}
also satisfies such a property.
∎

Corollary B.8
.

Given
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
, we have
\max\{u-\epsilon,0\}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
for any
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

Due to Theorem
B.2
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{i}\}
in
C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
such that
u_{i}\rightarrow u
under
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
\|u_{i}\|_{p}\leq\|u\|_{p}+1
. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\{u_{i}\}
converges to
u
point-wisely. Let
v_{i}:=\max\{u_{i}-\epsilon,0\}
. Since
v_{i}
is a Lipschitz function with compact support, Lemma
B.2
yields
v_{i}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
. Moreover, note that
\|v_{i}\|_{p}\leq\|u_{i}\|_{p}\leq\|u\|_{p}+1
and

\{v_{i}\}
converges to
\max\{u-\epsilon,0\}
point-wisely, which implies
\max\{u-\epsilon,0\}\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
through Theorem
B.7
.
∎

Now we introduce the capacity with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

Definition B.1
.

The
p
-capacity
of a set
E\subset M
is defined by

\displaystyle\Ca_{p}(E):=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{A}(E)}\|u\|^{p}_{p},

where
\mathscr{A}(E)=\{u\in{W^{1,p}}(M,\phi,\psi)\,:\,u\geq 1\text{ on a neighborhood of }E\}
.
In particular, set
\Ca_{p}(E):=\infty
if
\mathscr{A}(E)=\emptyset
.

Proceeding as in the proof of Kinnunen and Martio
[
22
, Remark 3.1]
and using Corollary
B.6
, we have the following result.

Proposition B.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(E):=\{u\in{W^{1,p}}(M,\phi,\psi)\,:\,0\leq u\leq 1\text{ and }u=1\text{ on a neighborhood of }E\}
. Then

\Ca_{p}(E)=\inf_{u\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(E)}\|u\|^{p}_{p}.

The following result collects the basic properties of
\Ca_{p}
; since the proofs are standard, based on Theorem
B.7
, Proposition
B.1
and Lemma
B.3
, we omit them.

Theorem B.9
.

The
p
-capacity is an outer measure, that is

(i)

\Ca_{p}(\emptyset)=0
;

(ii)

\Ca_{p}(E_{1})\leq\Ca_{p}(E_{2})
if
E_{1}\subset E_{2}
;

(iii)

\Ca_{p}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Ca_{p}(E_{i})
;

(iv)

\Ca_{p}(\cdot)
is outer regular, i.e.,
\Ca_{p}(E)=\inf\left\{\Ca_{p}(O)\,:\,O\text{ is open with }E\subset O\right\}
.

Definition B.2
.

We say that a property holds
p
-quasieverywhere in a set
E
, if it holds except for a subset of
p
-capacity zero in
E
.
In addition,
a function
u:M\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty]
is
p
-quasicontinuous
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a set
E
such that
\Ca_{p}(E)<\epsilon
and the restriction
u|_{M\backslash E}
is continuous.

A suitable modification of the argument from Chen et al.
[
10
, Theorem A.16]
, based on the the above notions, provides the following result:

Theorem B.10
.

If
u\in W^{1,p}(M,\phi,\psi)
is
p
-quasicontinuous and
u=0

p
-quasieverywhere in
M\backslash{\Omega_{\Sigma}}
, then
{u|_{{\Omega_{\Sigma}}}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{\Sigma},\phi,\psi)
.

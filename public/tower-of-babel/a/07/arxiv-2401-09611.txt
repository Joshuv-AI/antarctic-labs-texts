Abstract
. We investigate new pointwise bounds for a class of rough integral operators, T Ω , α T_{\Omega,{\alpha}} , for a parameter 0 < α < n 0<{\alpha}<n that includes classical rough singular integrals of Calderón and Zygmund, rough hypersingular integrals, and rough fractional integral operators. We prove that the rough integral operators are bounded by a sparse potential operator that depends on the size of the symbol Ω \Omega . As a result of our pointwise inequalities, we obtain several new Sobolev mappings of the form T Ω , α : W ˙ 1 , p → L q T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}:\dot{W}^{1,p}\rightarrow L^{q} .

1.
Introduction

Let
I_{\alpha}
be the Riesz potential of order
0<{\alpha}<n
,

I_{\alpha}f(x)={c_{n,{\alpha}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,dy

where the constant
c_{n,{\alpha}}
is chosen so that

\widehat{I_{\alpha}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-{\alpha}}\hat{f}(\xi).

The standard mapping properties of the Riesz potential are

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(1)

when
1<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
. At the endpoint
p=1
the weak-type inequality

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(2)

holds.
One of the main applications of the Riesz potential is the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality,

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(3)

for
1\leq p<n
and
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
. If we define the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the closure of smooth functions with compact support with respect to the seminorm
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then we see that inequality (
3
) gives rise to the Sobolev embedding

\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

When
1<p<n
, inequality (
3
) follows from mapping properties of the Riesz potential
I_{1}
and the pointwise inequality

|f(x)|\leq c_{n}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4)

Inequality (
3
) also holds when
p=1
and
1^{*}=\frac{n}{n-1}=n^{\prime}
despite the fact that
I_{1}
only satisfies the weak-type boundedness
I_{1}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

1.1.
New pointwise bounds

Recently, in
[
10
]
, the authors investigated an extension of inequality (
4
) to operators. More precisely, the inequality

|Tf(x)|\leq c_{T}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(5)

holds for several prominent operators in harmonic analysis. We now survey these results and their consequences.

•

The Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is given by

Mf(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\fint_{Q}|f|

where the supremum is over all cubes
Q
. The operator and its iterates
M^{k}=M\circ\cdots\circ M
, satisfy

M^{k}f(x)\leq c_{k}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad k=1,2,\ldots

(6)

Inequality (
6
) follows from the fact that
I_{1}(|\nabla f|)
is an
A_{1}
weight. The corresponding inequality with the smaller operator
M_{1}(|\nabla f|)
on the right-hand side does not hold, even when
k=0
, despite the fact
M_{1}(|\nabla f|)
is also an
A_{1}
weight (see Section
4
).

•

The
L^{r}
-maximal operators are given by

M_{L^{r}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\left(\fint_{Q}|f|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}=[M(|f|^{r})(x)]^{\frac{1}{r}}.

These operators satisfy

M^{k}f(x)\leq c_{k,r}M_{L^{r}}f(x),

for all
k=1,2,\ldots,
and
r>1
. If
1\leq r\leq n^{\prime}
then

M_{L^{r}}f(x)\leq cI_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),

(7)

which improves (
6
).
The value
r=n^{\prime}
is a critical index in the sense that
M_{L^{n^{\prime}+{\epsilon}}}f
is not bounded by
I_{1}(|\nabla f|)
when
{\epsilon}>0
.

•

We can improve (
6
) and (
7
) by considering maximal functions associated with Lorentz spaces. Let
L^{p,q}
be the Lorentz space normed by

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left(p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}|\{x:|f(x)|>t\}|^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

when
1\leq q<\infty
and

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sup_{t>0}t|\{x:|f(x)|>t\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Define the normalized Lorentz average on a cube by

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(Q)}=\frac{1}{|Q|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\mathbf{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(8)

and the Lorentz maximal function

M_{L^{p,q}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(Q)}.

The following pointwise bound holds

M_{L^{n^{\prime},1}}f(x)\leq cI_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x).

(9)

Inequality (
9
) further improves (
7
) because

M_{L^{n^{\prime}}}f(x)\leq M_{L^{n^{\prime},1}}f(x)

by the well-known containments of Lorentz spaces
L^{n^{\prime},1}\subseteq L^{n^{\prime}}
.

•

Given
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
consider the rough maximal operators

M_{\Omega}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})f(x-y)|\,dy

where
y^{\prime}=y/|y|
. When
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
we have

M_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{L^{n^{\prime},1}}f(x).

and so by inequality (
9
) we have

M_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x).

•

The spherical maximal operator is defined to be

\mathcal{S}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\fint_{\partial B(x,r)}|f(y)|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

where
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is surface measure on
\partial B(x,r)
. In
[
10
]
it is shown that

\mathcal{S}f(x)\leq I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(10)

This inequality is of interest because it is related to the Sobolev mapping properties of
\mathcal{S}
. More general maximal operators of the form

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}(x)=\sup_{r>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+ry)|\,d\mu

can also be considered. Here
\mu
is a spherical-like measure, i.e.,
\mathsf{supp}\,\mu\subseteq B(0,R)
for some
R>0
and

\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{n-1}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0.

In fact, Hajłasz and Liu
[
9
]
show that

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}f(x)\leq cI_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which generalizes (
10
) since
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
when
\mu
is the normalized Hausdorff measure on the unit sphere.

•

The main results from
[
10
]
state that the pointwise bound (
5
) holds for rough singular integral operators

T_{\Omega}f(x)=\mathsf{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)\,dy

(11)

where
y^{\prime}=y/|y|
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega=0
. It was shown that if
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
then

|T_{\Omega}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x).

This pointwise bound was extended to more general potential operators on the right-hand side in
[
11
]
.

1.2.
Consequences

By the mapping properties of
I_{1}
, any operator that satisfies the pointwise bound (
5
) will satisfy several Sobolev mapping properties. First, the operator will be bounded from
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
1<p<n
, namely,

\|Tf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

When
p=1
,
I_{1}
only satisfies a weak type bound and the corresponding mapping is a
\dot{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
inequality

\|Tf\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(12)

The mapping
T:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is less difficult to prove than
T:W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, but is still of interest, especially at the endpoint. Indeed, the endpoint inequality (
12
) is particularly significant for the operators
M_{L^{n^{\prime},1}}
and
\mathcal{S}
which are
not
bounded at the weak endpoint. When
p=n
, we have
I_{1}:L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow BMO
. The space
BMO
does not preserve pointwise inequalities, however,
I_{1}
does map into a local exponential space. Namely, for any cube
Q
containing the support of
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\|Tf\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(Q)}\leq C\|I_{1}(|\nabla f|)\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(Q)}\leq C\left(\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
[
19
]
showed that
I_{1}
satisfies the weighted inequality

\|wI_{1}g\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
1<p<n
precisely when
w\in A_{p,p^{*}}:

[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}w^{p^{*}}\right)\left(\fint_{Q}w^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

When
p=1
, they showed weak type inequality

\|I_{1}g\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{1}(w)}

holds if and only if

[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}=\left\|\frac{M(w^{n^{\prime}})}{w^{n^{\prime}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

The sharp quantitative bounds were later found in
[
14
]
to be

\|wI_{1}g\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\|wg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and

\|I_{1}g\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq C[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|g\|_{L^{1}(w)}.

It follows, that any operator which satisfies
|Tf|\leq cI_{1}(|\nabla f|)
, will automatically satisfy Sobolev type mappings

\|wTf\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\max\{1,\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{*}}\}}\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and

\|Tf\|_{L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(w^{n^{\prime}})}\leq C[w]_{A_{1,n^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(w)}.

Any operator satisfying (
5
) will also satisfy the two weight Sobolev inequality

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}(u)}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(v)}

whenever
I_{1}:L^{p}(v)\rightarrow L^{q}(u)
. The two weight inequality for
I_{\alpha}
, that is,

\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(u)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}

has a long history and we refer readers to
[
10
]
for a discussion of the conditions that imply it.

Lastly, it was shown in
[
10
]
that the pointwise bound (
5
) implies a certain self-improvement of the form

M_{L^{r}}(Tf)(x)\leq c_{r}I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad 0\leq r<n^{\prime}.

In this sense, we see that there is always “room” in the inequality
|Tf|\leq CI_{1}(|\nabla f|)
for a bigger operator on the left side.

1.3.
The main operators

In the present work, we investigate a more general class of operators and their pointwise bounds by potential operators applied to the gradient. In particular, we extend the results from
[
10
]
twofold. First, we consider operators with a different singularity which can be more singular than the classical case. Second, we address the case when
\Omega
belongs to a class below the critical index
r=n
.

Given
0<{\alpha}<n
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
with mean zero, define the rough fractional singular integral operator

T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)=\mathsf{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}f(x-y)\,dy.

Notice that for
\lambda>0
if we let
f_{\lambda}(x)=f(\lambda x)
, then we have the following homogeneity:

T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}(f_{\lambda})(x)=\lambda^{1-{\alpha}}T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(\lambda x).

The operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
behave differently as
{\alpha}
varies.

•

When
{\alpha}=1
, the operator
T_{\Omega,1}=T_{\Omega}
is a classical rough singular integral operator as defined in (
11
).

•

When
0<{\alpha}<1
the operator
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
has a greater singularity than the classical Calderón-Zygmund operators and is known in the literature as a hypersingular integral operator, in this case, a rough version. We refer readers to the work of Wheeden
[
21
,
22
]
(see also
[
2
]
) for more on hypersingular integrals. In this case, operator
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
is related to the nonlinear fractional differential operator

\mathcal{D}^{1-{\alpha}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{|x-y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}\,dy,\qquad{\alpha}\in(0,1)

introduced in
[
20
]
. In fact, if
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega~=~0
then the equality

\int_{{\epsilon}<|y|<N}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}f(x-y)\,dy\\
=\int_{{\epsilon}<|y|<N}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}(f(x-y)-f(x))\,dy

leads to

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\mathcal{D}^{1-{\alpha}}f(x).

•

When
1<{\alpha}<n
the operator
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
is a rough fractional integral operator. Such operators were used by Hajłasz and Liu
[
8
]
in the study of Sobolev mappings for the spherical maximal operator
\mathcal{S}
.

As motivation, consider the case when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
1<{\alpha}<n
. In this case, we have

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{{\alpha}-1}(|f|)(x)

where the assumption
1<{\alpha}<n
guarantees that
I_{{\alpha}-1}
is well-defined. For smooth functions, by inequality (
4
) we obtain the pointwise bound:

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq cI_{{\alpha}-1}(|f|)(x)\leq cI_{{\alpha}-1}\big(I_{1}(|\nabla f|)\big)(x)=cI_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x).

We have used the classical convolution identity for the Riesz potential operators

I_{\gamma}\circ I_{\beta}=I_{\gamma+\beta},\qquad\gamma+\beta<n,

which can be easily seen on Fourier transform side. The operator
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)
has the same homogeneity as
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
since

I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f_{\lambda}|)(x)=\lambda I_{\alpha}(|(\nabla f)_{\lambda}|)(x)=\lambda^{1-{\alpha}}I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(\lambda x).

Therefore, the operator
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)
serves a natural upper bound to consider for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. Our main results will be new pointwise bounds for the operator
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
depending on the size of
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for
1<r<\infty
and for the full range
0<{\alpha}<n
.

For classical rough singular integral operators
T_{\Omega}
, it is well documented that the size of
\Omega
dictates the behavior of the associated rough operator. In particular, the assumption
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is not enough to guarantee
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness. The weakest assumption that is sufficient for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness is
\Omega\in L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. In our setting, the size will break into three cases:

Critical space:

When
r=n
and
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the critical space that appeared in
[
10
]
. In this case, we extend our results to the operator
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
and show it is bounded by
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla\cdot|)
. As in the case
\alpha=1
, this implies several Sobolev mappings for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
, whenever
I_{\alpha}
satisfies Lebesgue spaces bounds.

Subcritical space:

When
1<r<n
, and
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
we are no longer able to obtain pointwise bounds by the Riesz potential of the gradient. Instead, we obtain bounds by suitable sparse operators. We derive new weighted estimates from these pointwise bounds, which we discuss in later sections.

Endpoint space:

At the endpoint
r=1
, we prove bounds by a sparse operator when
\Omega\in L^{1}(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, but only in the hypersingular case
0<{\alpha}<1
. These lead to a variety of new Sobolev-type bounds. Moreover, this is a larger class than the usual logarithmic endpoint since

L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\subsetneq L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

We begin with the critical space where we have the following pointwise bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose,
0<{\alpha}<n
,
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and has mean zero. Then

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(13)

Inequality (
13
) leads to several Sobolev mapping properties for
T_{\Omega,\alpha}
. In particular, one obtains

\|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(u)}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(v)},

whenever the Riesz potential satisfies
I_{\alpha}:L^{p}(v)\to L^{q}(u)
. Remarkably, a weak-type endpoint Sobolev inequality for measures completely characterizes when general operators admit a pointwise bound by
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla\cdot|)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<n
and
T
is an operator defined pointwise on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The pointwise inequality

|Tf(x)|\leq cI_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x),

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(2)

The weak endpoint Sobolev inequality

\|Tf\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f|(M\mu)^{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}}

(14)

holds for every locally finite Borel measure
\mu
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We note that inequality (
14
) arises naturally in this context. Indeed, by letting
d\mu=w\,dx
with
w\in A_{1}
(i.e.,
Mw\leq cw
), inequality (
14
) yields

\|Tf\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\infty}(w)}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f|\,w^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

(15)

Through off-diagonal extrapolation and appropriate renormalization of the weights, inequality (
15
) in turn implies the weighted Sobolev inequality

\|wTf\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for
1<p<\frac{n}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
, provided that
w\in A_{p,q}
(see Section
5
). Further details may be found in
[
14
]
.

To state our results for
1<r<n
we need to introduce some common machinery concerning dyadic cubes and sparse families. Recall a dyadic grid
\mathscr{D}
is a collection of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that every cube has sidelength
2^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, for each fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the cubes of length
2^{k}
partition
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the entire collection
\mathscr{D}
satisfies the “nested or disjoint” property that
Q\cap P\in\{\varnothing,P,Q\}
for all
Q,P\in\mathscr{D}
. We say a subfamily of dyadic cubes
\mathscr{S}\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}
is sparse if for each
Q\in\mathscr{S}
there exists
E_{Q}\subseteq Q
such that
|Q|\leq 2|E_{Q}|
and the family
\{E_{Q}:Q\in\mathscr{S}\}
is pairwise disjoint.

Given,
0<{\alpha}<n
, a sparse family
\mathscr{S}
, we define the sparse fractional integral operator

I_{{\alpha}}^{\mathscr{S}}f(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\,\fint_{Q}f\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x).

In
[
4
]
it is shown that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded by finitely many sparse operators. If
f\in L_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\geq 0
, then there exists sparse families of cubes
\mathscr{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{S}_{N}
such that

I_{\alpha}f(x)\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{N}I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}f(x).

(16)

The sparse families depend on
f
, but the implicit constants in the inequality do not depend on
f
. The sparse operators are often simpler to work with and several norm inequalities can be gleaned from them.

If we combine Theorem
1.1
with inequality (
16
) we obtain the following corollary, which provides a preview of our forthcoming results.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose,
0<{\alpha}<n
,
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, and has mean zero. Then given
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists finitely many sparse families of cubes
\mathscr{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{S}_{N}
such that

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k=1}^{N}I^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x).

In the subcritical setting for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
we do not obtain bounds by the sparse operator
I^{\mathscr{S}}_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)
, instead, we obtain a bigger operator on the right-hand side. Given
0<{\alpha}<n
and an exponent
1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
we define the
L^{s}
sparse fractional operator

I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\,\fint_{Q}|f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x).

When
s=1
we simply write
I_{{\alpha},L^{1}}^{\mathscr{S}}=I^{\mathscr{S}}_{\alpha}.
The restriction
s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
is necessary, because the operator
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
may not be well-defined when
s\geq\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
. Indeed, consider the sparse family
\mathscr{S}=\{[0,2^{k})^{n}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}.
If
s\geq\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
then for
x\in[0,1]^{n}
we have

I^{\mathscr{S}}_{{\alpha},L^{s}}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]^{n}})(x)=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k({\alpha}-\frac{n}{s})}=\infty.

The operators
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
govern the rough operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
in the subcritical case. Our scope extends beyond the class
L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
– specifically, we are able to consider the more refined class where
\Omega
belongs to the Lorentz space
L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. This space is normed by

\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=\left(r\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r^{*}}\sigma\big(\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:|\Omega(y^{\prime})|>t\})^{\frac{r^{*}}{r}}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{*}}},

where
r^{*}=\frac{nr}{n-r}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
1<r<n
,
0<{\alpha}<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}
, and
\Omega\in L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has mean zero. Then there exists finitely many sparse families
\mathscr{S}_{k}\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,N
such that

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k=1}^{N}I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}(|\nabla f|)(x)

where
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}
.

Since
r^{*}>r
we have

L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=L^{r,r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\subseteq L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

and hence the statement of the theorems holds under the less general assumption
\Omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. If
r
is a given exponent satisfying
1<r<n
, then the condition
s=\frac{r^{\prime}n}{n+r^{\prime}}<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
places a restriction on
{\alpha}
, namely
{\alpha}<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}
.

We will derive the weighted estimates for the operators
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
in Section
5
. These weighted estimates will imply new Sobolev type bounds for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. Before moving on, let us make a few quick observations. The operator,
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
is related to operator

f\mapsto\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}.

The sparse bounds from inequality (
16
) imply the pointwise relationship

\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}\simeq\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}I_{{\alpha}s}^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}(f^{s})\right]^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}I^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f

(17)

where we have used convexity to pull the power
1/s
into the sums. We also have the following norm bounds, which will be used later to prove weighted estimates.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
,
0<p<\infty
, and
w\in A_{\infty}
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any sparse family of cubes
\mathscr{S}

\|I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|[I_{{\alpha}s}(f^{s})]^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}(w)},

and

\|I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(w)}\leq C\big\|[I_{{\alpha}s}(f^{s})]^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(w)},

for measurable functions
f\geq 0
.

By a simple rescaling argument we see that the operator
f\mapsto[I_{{\alpha}s}(f^{s})]^{\frac{1}{s}}
satisfies
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
bounds when
s<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
and the weak type bounds
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\frac{sn}{n-{\alpha}s},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
at the endpoint
p=s
. When
1<r<n
and
\Omega\in{L^{r,r^{*}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has mean zero, by Theorem
1.4
we have

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{N}I^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}_{{\alpha},L^{\frac{r^{\prime}n}{r^{\prime}+n}}}(|\nabla f|)(x).

The resulting weighted Sobolev estimates for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
are more complicated in general because the class of weights depends on
r^{\prime}
and
n
. Instead, we state the following power weight result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
1<r<n
and
\Omega\in L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has mean value zero and
0<{\alpha}<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}
. Then the following inequality holds

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|x|^{\lambda}|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\big)^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|x|^{\lambda}|\nabla f(x)|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
\frac{r^{\prime}n}{r^{\prime}+n}<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
and

{\alpha}-\frac{n}{p}<\lambda<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{n}{p}.

The operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
become more difficult to analyze when
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. For an example of this phenomenon see the example of Honzík
[
12
]
for
T_{\Omega}=T_{\Omega,1}
. In fact most results assume
\Omega\in L^{1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
(see
[
7
]
). In our context we notice that when
r=1
the restriction
{\alpha}<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}
means we can only consider the hypersingular case
0<{\alpha}<1
. In this case, we can work with the class
L^{1}(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<1
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has mean zero. Then there exist sparse families
\mathscr{S}_{k}\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,N
such that

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k=1}^{N}I^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}_{{\alpha},L^{n}}(|\nabla f|)(x).

The operators
I^{\mathscr{S}}_{{\alpha},L^{n}}
satisfy
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
mappings for
n<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
, where note that
p=n
corresponds to the weak endpoint. The following mapping properties for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
now follow, which seem completely new.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<1
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
has mean zero. Then

T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
n<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
and

T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}:\dot{W}^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\frac{n}{1-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Finally, we may use Theorem
1.7
to obtain the weighted norm bounds from Section
5
to derive power weighted norm estimates for the hypersingular rough integral
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
for
\Omega\in L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<1
and
\Omega\in L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
with mean value zero. Then the following inequality holds

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|x|^{\lambda}|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\big)^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(|x|^{\lambda}|\nabla f(x)|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
n<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
and

{\alpha}-\frac{n}{p}<\lambda<1-\frac{n}{p}.

1.4.
Plan of paper

In the next section, Section
2
, we will collect some of the necessary background needed to prove our results. In Section
3
, we will prove all our pointwise bounds from Theorems
1.1
,
1.4
, and
1.7
along with Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4
we study bounds for maximal functions which include
M_{\Omega}
and other maximal functions with cancellation. Section
5
will be devoted to the study of the mapping properties of the dominating operators. While bounds for
I_{\alpha}
and
M_{\alpha}

2.
Preliminaries

A key tool in our proofs of the pointwise bounds is the local Poincaré-Sobolev inequality

\left(\fint_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\ell(Q)\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(18)

which holds for
1\leq p<n
,
1\leq q\leq p^{*}
, and
f\in C^{1}(Q)
. We will use an improved version in the scale of Lorentz spaces

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{*},p}(Q)}\leq C\ell(Q)\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(19)

for the same values
1\leq p<n
. Here the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p^{*},p}(Q)}
is the Lorentz average over the cube
Q
as defined in (
8
). The improvement is due to O’Neill
[
17
]
and Peetre
[
18
]
, but a simpler proof can be found in
[
16
]
.

When
p=n
inequality (
18
) does not hold, but the so-called Trudinger inequality holds, namely,

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(Q)}\leq C\left(\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(20)

where again
\|\cdot\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(Q)}
is the the
\exp L^{n^{\prime}}
average on
Q
. We also remark that any of the inequalities (
18
), (
19
), or (
20
) hold for balls instead of cubes with the proper adjustment of using the radius instead of the sidelength. We refer to
[
15
]
for a proof of (
20
) in a general framework that does not use smoothness.

We will also use the dyadic machinery, particularly, Lemma
2.1
. Given a dyadic grid
{\mathscr{D}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
let

{\mathscr{D}}^{k}=\{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}:\ell(Q)=2^{k}\}.

We will use the well-known collection of dyadic grids for
t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}
defined by

{\mathscr{D}}_{t}=\{2^{k}([0,1)^{n}+m+(-1)^{k}t):k\in\mathbb{Z},m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}.

(21)

These dyadic grids are important because every cube can be “approximated" by a dyadic cube from one of these grids. Namely, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given any cube
Q
, there exists
t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}
and
Q_{t}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{t}
and
\ell(Q_{t})\leq 6\ell(Q).

Recall that the cubes in
{\mathscr{D}}^{k}
for a fixed
k
form a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We have the following variant of Lemma
2.1
which will be needed later.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
Q
is a cube with
\ell(Q)=2^{k}
for some
k
. Then there exists
t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}
and
Q_{t}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+3}
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{t}
.

Proof.

Given the cube
Q
with
\ell(Q)=2^{k}
, by Lemma
2.1
there exists
P_{t}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}
such that
Q\subseteq P_{t}
and
\ell(P_{t})\leq 6\ell(Q)\leq 2^{k+3}
. Let
Q_{t}
be the unique cube in
{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+3}
containing
P_{t}
and hence also
Q
.
∎

We will also make use of the dyadic fractional integral operator associated with a dyadic grid
{\mathscr{D}}

I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{D}}f=\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}f\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}.

Notice the difference between
I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{D}}
and
I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{S}}
is that the sum is over all dyadic cubes from
{\mathscr{D}}
, not just a sparse family
\mathscr{S}
. In
[
4
]
it is shown that the discrete operator
I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{D}}
and the integral operator
I_{\alpha}
are pointwise equivalent, this is in contrast to the singular integral case. We have the following proposition from
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<n
. If
{\mathscr{D}}
is any dyadic grid and
f\geq 0
, then we have

I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{D}}f(x)\leq cI_{\alpha}f(x),

and if
\big\{{\mathscr{D}}_{t}:t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}\big\}
are the dyadic grids defined in (
21
), then

I_{\alpha}f(x)\leq c\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}}I_{\alpha}^{{\mathscr{D}}_{t}}f(x).

If
f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\geq 0
, then given any dyadic grid
{\mathscr{D}}
, there exists a sparse subset
\mathscr{S}\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}
such that

I_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{D}}f(x)\leq cI_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{S}}f(x).

We will need the following lemma which is similar to Proposition
2.3
but for the
L^{s}
averages. Given a dyadic grid
{\mathscr{D}}
define the
L^{s}
dyadic fractional operator for
f\geq 0
by

I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{D}}f=\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}.

The proof is similar to the sparse bound proof in Proposition
2.3
, but we include it here for completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
f\geq 0
. Given any dyadic grid
{\mathscr{D}}
, there exists a sparse family
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{S}(f)\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}
such that

I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{D}}f(x)\leq cI_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f(x).

Proof.

Let
a>1
be a number to be chosen later. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
let

\mathscr{C}^{k}=\left\{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}:a^{k}<\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq a^{k+1}\right\}.

Then every cube
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}
that contributes to the sum of
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{D}}f
belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{k}
for some
k
and hence

I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{D}}f=\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}\leq a\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{C}^{k}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{Q}.

Now let
\mathscr{S}^{k}
be the collection of all maximal dyadic cubes such that

\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}>a^{k}.

If
\mathscr{C}^{k}
is not empty, then such maximal cubes exist because
f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The collection of cubes in
\mathscr{S}^{k}
is pairwise disjoint (for a fixed
k
) and satisfies

\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq 2^{\frac{n}{s}}a^{k}

by maximality. Moreover, every cube
P\in\mathscr{C}^{k}
is a subset of a unique
Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}
. Hence

\displaystyle a\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{C}^{k}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{Q}

\displaystyle\leq a\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}}\sum_{{P\in\mathscr{C}^{k}\atop P\subseteq Q}}\ell(P)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{P}

\displaystyle\leq a\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{{P\in{\mathscr{D}}(Q)\atop\ell(P)=2^{-j}\ell(Q)}}\ell(P)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{P}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{1-2^{-{\alpha}}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{Q}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\Big(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}.

If we let
\mathscr{S}=\bigcup_{k}\mathscr{S}^{k}
then the desired result will follow provided we can prove that
\mathscr{S}
is a sparse family. To see this fix
Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}
, then the sets are nested in the following way: every
P\in\mathscr{S}^{k+1}
satisfies
P\subseteq Q
for some
Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}
. Hence if we let
\Lambda^{k}=\bigcup\{Q:Q\in\mathscr{S}^{k}\}
then we have

|Q\cap\Lambda^{k+1}|=\sum_{{P\in\mathscr{S}^{k+1}\atop P\subseteq Q}}|P|<\frac{1}{a^{(k+1)s}}\sum_{{P\in\mathscr{S}^{k+1}\atop P\subseteq Q}}\int_{P}f^{s}\leq\frac{1}{a^{(k+1)s}}\int_{Q}f^{s}\leq\frac{2^{n}}{a^{s}}|Q|.

By setting
a=2^{\frac{n+1}{s}}
we arrive at
|Q\cap\Lambda^{k+1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|Q|
and
the sparse condition now follows by setting
E_{Q}=Q\backslash\Lambda^{k+1}
.
∎

We will also need some basic facts about Orlicz spaces. Given a Young function
\Phi(t)
we define the Orlicz average of a measurable set
E
(usually a cube or a ball),

\|u\|_{L^{\Phi}(E)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\fint_{E}\Phi\Big(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

The associate space is defined by the Young function

\widebar{\Phi}(t)=\sup_{s>0}(st-\Phi(s)),

which satisfies
\Phi^{-1}(t)\widebar{\Phi}^{-1}(t)\simeq t
. We also have the following Hölder inequality for Young functions

\fint_{E}|fg|\,\leq c\|f\|_{L^{\Phi}(E)}\|g\|_{L^{\widebar{\Phi}}(E)}.

(22)

We will be particularly interested in exponential Young functions of the form
\Phi(t)=\exp(t^{q})-1
for some
q>1
. In this case we have

\widebar{\Phi}(t)\simeq t\log(1+t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and inequality (
22
) becomes

\fint_{E}|fg|\,\leq c\|f\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{q}}(E)}\|g\|_{\exp L^{q}(E)}.

(23)

We will also need the following Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces,

\fint_{E}|fg|\leq c\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(E)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E)}

(24)

which holds for all
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

3.
Pointwise bounds

In this section we prove our pointwise bounds for the integral operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. To prove this, as mentioned above, we will use the local Poincaré-Sobolev inequality. The proofs of the bounds in Theorems
1.1
,
1.4
, and
1.7
all begin with the same decomposition.

We split
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
into dyadic annuli

T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-1}<|y|\leq 2^{k}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}f(x-y)\,dy\\
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-1}<|y|\leq 2^{k}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}(f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})})\,dy

where we have used the cancellation
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega=0
to subtract the constant

f_{B(x,2^{k})}=\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}f.

The decomposition leads to

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-1}<|y|\leq 2^{k}}\frac{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}{|y|^{n+1-{\alpha}}}|f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy\\
\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k({\alpha}-1)}\fint_{|y|\leq 2^{k}}{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}|f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy.

We now analyze the term

\fint_{|y|\leq 2^{k}}{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}|f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy.

We now divide this into to the three cases

Case 1:

the critical case:
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Case 2:

the subcritical case:
\Omega\in{L^{r,r^{*}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for
1<r<n
.

Case 3:

the endpoint case:
\Omega\in L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.

Case 1:
We first use the Lorentz Hölder’s inequality (
24
) and then the local Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (
19
) with
p=1
and
1^{*}=n^{\prime}
to get

\displaystyle\fint_{|y|\leq 2^{k}}{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}|f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\|_{L^{n,\infty}(B(0,2^{k}))}\|f(x-\cdot)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{L^{n^{\prime},1}(B(0,2^{k}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\simeq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|f-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{L^{n^{\prime},1}(B(x,2^{k}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}2^{k}\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|.

In the above estimates we have used that

|\{y\in B(0,2^{k}):|\Omega(y/|y|)|>t\}|=\frac{2^{kn}}{n}\,\sigma(\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:|\Omega(y^{\prime})|>t\})

(25)

where
\sigma=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
to conclude that the averages satisfy

\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\|_{L^{n,\infty}(B(0,2^{k}))}=c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

This leads to the bound,

|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{{\alpha}k}\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|.

(26)

From here we switch our quantities from balls to cubes, namely, let
Q_{k}
be the cube centered at
x
, with sidelength
2^{k+1}
so that
B(x,2^{k})\subseteq Q_{k}
. Fix such a
Q_{k}
. Since
\ell(Q_{k})=2^{k+1}
, using Lemma
2.2
we have that

Q_{k}\subseteq Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+4}

for some
t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}
. Since
{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+4}
forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we may use this estimate to get

\displaystyle 2^{{\alpha}k}\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq c_{{\alpha},n}\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha}\fint_{Q_{k}}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+4}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+4}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x).

Using this estimate in inequality (
26
), we have,

\displaystyle|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{{\alpha}k}\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}^{k+4}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{n,{\alpha}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x)

where we have used the pointwise bound from Proposition
2.3
in our last estimate.

Case 2:

Let
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}
then
s^{*}=r^{\prime}
and
r^{*}=s^{\prime}
. By the Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces, we have

\displaystyle\fint_{|y|\leq 2^{k}}

\displaystyle{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}|f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\big\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(B(0,2^{k}))}\|f(x-\cdot)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{L^{s^{*},s}(B(0,2^{k}))}

\displaystyle=c\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,\|f-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{L^{s^{*},s}(B(x,2^{k}))}

where we have again used the level set equality from (
25
) and the translation invariant property of the Lebesgue measure. We now use the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (
19
) for the oscillation term

\|f-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{L^{s^{*},s}(B(x,2^{k}))}\leq C\,2^{k}\left(\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{{\alpha}k}\left(\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\Omega\|_{L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}^{n}}I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}_{t}}(|\nabla f|)(x)

where the sparse families
\mathscr{S}_{t}\subseteq{\mathscr{D}}_{t}
come from Lemma
2.4
.

Case 3:
In this case we use the Orlicz Hölder inequality (
23
) to estimate

\displaystyle\fint_{B(0,2^{k})}|\Omega(y^{\prime})||f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\|_{L(\log L)^{1/n^{\prime}}(B(0,2^{k}))}\|f(x-\cdot)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(B(0,2^{k}))}.

We now compute the
\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(B(0,2^{k}))}
norm. Let

\Psi(t)=t\log(1+t)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

and suppose
\lambda>0
is such that

\fint_{B(0,2^{k})}\Psi\Big(\frac{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}{\lambda}\Big)\,dy\leq 1.

Let
v_{n}
be the measure of the unit ball, then we have

\displaystyle\fint_{B(0,2^{k})}\Psi\Big(\frac{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}{\lambda}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v_{n}2^{kn}}\int_{0}^{2^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Psi\Big(\frac{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}{\lambda}\Big)r^{n-1}\,drdy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nv_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Psi\Big(\frac{|\Omega(y^{\prime})|}{\lambda}\Big)\,dy^{\prime}.

It follows that

\|\Omega(\cdot/|\cdot|)\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(B(0,2^{k}))}=c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Continuing the estimates and using the Trudinger inequality (
20
), we have

\displaystyle\fint_{B(0,2^{k})}|\Omega(y^{\prime})||f(x-y)-f_{B(x,2^{k})}|\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\|f-f_{B(x,2^{k})}\|_{\exp L^{n^{\prime}}(B(x,2^{k}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\left(\int_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

By similar arguments to the first case, we may replace the sum with the sparse operators to obtain

\displaystyle|T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{({\alpha}-1)k}\left(\int_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{{\alpha}k}\left(\fint_{B(x,2^{k})}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{t}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|\nabla f|^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}}I^{{\mathscr{D}}_{t}}_{{\alpha},n}(|\nabla f|)(x)

\displaystyle\leq c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{t\in\{0,\frac{1}{3}\}}I^{\mathscr{S}_{t}}_{{\alpha},n}(|\nabla f|)(x),

where again we have used Lemma
2.4
to obtain the sparse bounds.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose that
T
is an operator that satisfies

|Tf(x)|\leq cI_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x),

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Let
\mu
be a locally finite Borel measure and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By the pointwise inequality and Minkowski’s integral inequality we have

\|Tf\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\leq C\|I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\\
\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(y)|\big\||\cdot-y|^{{\alpha}-n}\big\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\,dy.

A calculation shows that

\big\||\cdot-y|^{{\alpha}-n}\big\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\mu(\{x:|x-y|^{{\alpha}-n}>\lambda\})^{\frac{n-{\alpha}}{n}}\\
=\sup_{t>0}\Big(\frac{\mu(B(y,t))}{t^{n}}\Big)^{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}}\leq C(M\mu)(y)^{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}}.

Now suppose the weak Sobolev inequality

\|Tf\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f|(M\mu)^{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}}

holds for all locally finite Borel measures and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\mu
be the pointmass measure at
x
. Then

(M\mu)(y)\simeq\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Hence

|Tf(x)|=\|Tf\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mu)}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(y)|(M\mu)(y)^{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}}\,dy\\
\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,dy.

∎

4.
Pointwise bounds for maximal operators

We now turn our attention to some results concerning the related maximal functions. As motivation, let us begin by addressing a natural question that arose in
[
10
]
. Recall the following pointwise bound for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator:

Mf(x)\leq cI_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(27)

The Fefferman–Stein sharp maximal operator,

M^{\#}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\fint_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|,

satisfies a better bound:

M^{\#}f(x)\leq cM_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(28)

where
M_{1}
is the fractional maximal operator associated with the Riesz potential
I_{1}
. More generally, for
0<\alpha<n
, the fractional maximal operator is defined by

M_{\alpha}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|f|\right).

Inequality (
28
) follows from the Poincaré–Sobolev inequality (
18
) in the case
p=q=1
. Notably, the right-hand side of (
28
) improves upon that of (
27
), owing to the well-known pointwise inequality

M_{\alpha}f(x)\leq cI_{\alpha}f(x),\qquad f\geq 0.

(29)

In light of (
29
), we see that the fractional maximal function
M_{\alpha}
satisfies the same
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
mapping properties as the Riesz potential operator, namely those in (
1
) and (
2
). However,
M_{\alpha}
is better behaved at the endpoint
p=\frac{n}{\alpha}
:

\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(In fact, a sharper mapping holds:
M_{\alpha}:L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, but this is not needed for the present discussion.) In particular we have that
M^{\#}:\dot{W}^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since

\|M^{\#}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\|M_{1}(|\nabla f|)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(30)

Since
M^{\#}
characterizes the space
BMO
, namely,

\|f\|_{BMO}=\|M^{\#}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

we see that inequality (
30
) is the well-known embedding

\dot{W}^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow BMO.

The space
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
actually embeds into the smaller space
VMO
(see
[
1
]
).

One might inquire about the potential for obtaining a better estimate for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Namely, is the following inequality

Mf(x)\leq cM_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(31)

true? Inequality (
31
) is, in fact, false. Indeed, if (
31
) held, then we would have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|Mf\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|M_{1}(|\nabla f|)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(32)

Inequality (
32
) is well-known to be false, for example, consider
f
to be a smooth truncation of the function
f(x)=\log\big(\frac{e}{|x|}\big)^{1-\frac{2}{n-1}}
for
|x|\leq 1
. Put another way, the reason that (
31
) cannot hold is because

W^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\not\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Considering the homogeneity,

M_{{\alpha}-1}(f_{\lambda})=\lambda^{1-{\alpha}}\big(M_{{\alpha}-1}f\big)_{\lambda}

the operator
M_{{\alpha}-1}
defined for
1\leq{\alpha}<n
is the natural maximal function associated to
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)
. The operator
M_{{\alpha}-1}
also plays a role in the study of the Sobolev mappings of maximal operators. Indeed, Kinnunen and Saksman
[
13
]

show that when
{\alpha}\geq 1
,
M_{\alpha}
has smoothing properties in the sense that

|\nabla M_{\alpha}f|\leq cM_{{\alpha}-1}f.

Observe that for
1\leq{\alpha}<n,

M_{{\alpha}-1}f(x)\leq cI_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(33)

Indeed, when
{\alpha}=1
this is just inequality (
6
) with
k=1
. For
1<{\alpha}<n
, we may use inequality (
4
) and the fact that
M_{{\alpha}-1}
is also controlled by
I_{{\alpha}-1}
(
29
) to see that

M_{{\alpha}-1}f(x)\leq cI_{{\alpha}-1}(|f|)(x)\leq cI_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x).

In the remainder of this section, we will consider more exotic maximal operators with the same homogeneity as
M_{{\alpha}-1}
and their bounds by potential operators of the gradient.

4.1.
Rough Maximal Operators

The reason that
M^{\#}f
is bounded by
M_{1}(|\nabla f|)
but
M
is not, stems from the presence of cancellation in
M^{\#}
. Namely
M^{\#}(\mathbf{1})=0
, whereas
M
lacks this property. With this in mind, we now consider the companion rough maximal operators to our integral operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. Consider the rough maximal operator

M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})f(x-y)|\,dy,\qquad 1\leq{\alpha}<n.

The operator
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
has the same homogeneity as
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
and
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla\cdot|)
. When
{\alpha}=1
this is the rough maximal operator associated with rough singular integrals (see
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
). The operator
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
does not have cancellation, and because of this,
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
may not be well-defined in the hypersingular range
0<{\alpha}<1
. Indeed, if
\Omega=\mathbf{1}
, then
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}(\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)})\equiv\infty
, near the origin if
{\alpha}<1
.

We can consider a smaller maximal operator to introduce necessary cancellation, namely, consider the
natural
rough fractional maximal operator

M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}t^{{\alpha}-1}\left|\fint_{|y|<t}\Omega(y^{\prime})f(x-y)\,dy\right|,\qquad 0<{\alpha}<n.

Clearly
M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
is smaller than
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. If
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega=0
, then
M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
has cancellation in the sense that
M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}(\mathbf{1})=0
. Finally we introduce a third maximal operator, the sharp rough fractional maximal operator

M^{\#}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})||f(x-y)-f_{B(x,t)}|\,dy,\quad 0<{\alpha}<n.

In the definition of
M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
we will assume that
\Omega
has mean zero, but this assumption is not needed for the other two maximal operators. We also notice that

M^{\natural}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)\leq M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}f(x)

(34)

where we have used the zero average of
\Omega
to subtract the constant
f_{B(x,t)}
. In addition, the following pointwise bounds between
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}
and
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
hold.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
1\leq{\alpha}<n
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, then the following pointwise bound holds

|M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)-M^{\#}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{{\alpha}-1}f(x)

(35)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\omega_{n-1}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the surface area of the unit sphere. In particular, if
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

|M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)-M^{\#}_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}I_{{\alpha}}(|\nabla f|)(x).

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
. Notice that

\displaystyle t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})||f(x-y)-f_{B(x,t)}|\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91692pt\leq t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})||f(x-y)|dy+t^{{\alpha}-1}|f_{B(x,t)}|\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})|\,dy.

If
v_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\omega_{n-1}=nv_{n}
is the surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then

\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})|\,dy=\frac{1}{v_{n}t^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(y^{\prime})|r^{n-1}d\sigma(y^{\prime})dr=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

By taking the supremum over
t>0
, we have

M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}f(x)\leq M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)+\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{{\alpha}-1}f(x).

On the other hand, again for a fixed
t>0

{t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})f(x-y)|\,dy}\\
\qquad\leq{t^{{\alpha}-1}}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})(f(x-y)-f_{B(x,t)})|dy\\
+t^{{\alpha}-1}|f_{B(x,t)}|\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})|\,dy

leads to

M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f(x)\leq M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}f(x)+\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{{\alpha}-1}f(x).

which completes inequality (
35
). If
f
is a smooth function, the bound by
I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)
follows from inequality (
33
).
∎

By these calculations and inequalities (
34
) and (
35
) we only need to investigate bounds for
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}
. Our results for
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}
now mirror those for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
, except we can have a smaller maximal function on the right-hand side. We need an
L^{s}
fractional maximal function,

M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\Big(\fint_{Q}|f|^{s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}},\qquad 1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}.

(36)

Notice that
M_{{\alpha},L^{1}}=M_{\alpha}
and

M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f=\big[M_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
0<{\alpha}<n
and
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Then for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have the following bounds

M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}^{\#}f(x)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c\|\Omega\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x),\\
c\|\Omega\|_{{L^{r,r^{*}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{{\alpha},L^{\frac{r^{\prime}n}{r^{\prime}+n}}}(|\nabla f|)(x),\\
c\|\Omega\|_{L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}M_{{\alpha},L^{n}}(|\nabla f|)(x),\end{array}\right.

where
0<{\alpha}<1+\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}
in the second inequality and
0<{\alpha}<1
in the third.

We will not prove Theorem
4.2
, rather, we will only give a sketch since its proof uses similar calculations to those found in Section
3
. All that is needed is the calculation at a single scale. Indeed, for a fixed
t>0
by Hölder’s inequality in the appropriate space
\mathcal{X}

{t^{{\alpha}-1}}\fint_{|y|<t}|\Omega(y^{\prime})(f(x-y)-f_{B(x,t)})|dy\\
\leq c\,t^{{\alpha}-1}\|\Omega\|_{\mathcal{X}(B(0,t))}\|f-f_{B(x,t)}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(B(x,t))}

where
\mathcal{X}=L^{n,\infty}
,
L^{r,r^{*}}
for
1<r<n
, or
L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}
. The associate spaces now become
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=L^{n^{\prime},1}
,
L^{r^{\prime},(r^{*})^{\prime}}
for
1<r<n
, or
\exp(L^{n^{\prime}})
and we may use the Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities (
19
) or Trudinger’s inequality (
20
) to obtain the desired pointwise bounds.

While
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
have the same
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
mapping properties when
1<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and same weak endpoint behavior when
p=1
, they behave differently at the other endpoint
p=\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
. Likewise the same phenomenon holds for
M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
and
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
when
s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
. As mentioned above
M_{\alpha}:L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and it is a simple consequence of Hölder’s inequality that

M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}:L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

An interesting consequence is the following
L^{\infty}
Sobolev bounds, which we state when
{\alpha}=1
for simplicity.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose
\Omega\in L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
for some
1<r<\infty
. If
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then

\|M^{\natural}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|M^{\#}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

4.2.
The Fractional Spherical Maximal Operator

In this section, we extend the pointwise bounds to fractional spherical maximal functions. Kinnunen and Saksman
[
13
]
defined the fractional spherical maximal function for
0\leq\beta<n-1
by

\mathcal{S}_{\beta}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}r^{\beta}\fint_{\partial B(x,r)}|f(y)|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

In our context, we will work with
\beta={\alpha}-1
, where
1\leq{\alpha}<n
. This way the operators
\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}
match the homogeneity of the operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
and
M_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. We also point out that
\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}
arises in the study of the smoothness of the fractional maximal function. Indeed, it was shown in
[
13
]
that

|\nabla M_{\alpha}f(x)|\leq c_{n}\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}f(x).

We have the following pointwise bounds.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
1\leq{\alpha}<n
, then the following pointwise bound holds

\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}f(x)\leq c_{n}I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x).

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and given
{\epsilon}>0
define the positive and bounded
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function

g_{\epsilon}(x)=(f(x)^{2}+{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The gradient of
g_{\epsilon}
is given by

\nabla g_{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{f(x)\nabla f(x)}{(f(x)^{2}+{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and satisfies

|\nabla g_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq|\nabla f(x)|.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
t>0
and let
\sigma=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
be the surface measure on
\partial B(0,1)=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
where again
\omega_{n-1}=\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\fint_{\partial B(x,t)}g_{\epsilon}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}t^{n-1}}\int_{\partial B(0,t)}g_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g_{\epsilon}(x-t\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta).

Let
N>t
be sufficiently large so that
f(x-N\theta)=0
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. This implies
g_{\epsilon}(x-N\theta)=\sqrt{{\epsilon}}
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. A similar calculation shows

\fint_{\partial B(x,N)}g_{\epsilon}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g_{\epsilon}(x-N\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)=\sqrt{{\epsilon}}

and hence

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\fint_{\partial B(x,N)}g_{\epsilon}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\sqrt{{\epsilon}}.

We now have

\displaystyle\fint_{\partial B(x,t)}g_{\epsilon}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dr}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g_{\epsilon}(x-r\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)dr+\sqrt{{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nabla g_{\epsilon}(x-r\theta)\cdot\theta\,d\sigma(\theta)dr+\sqrt{{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\nabla g_{\epsilon}(x-r\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)dr+\sqrt{{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{|y|>t}\frac{|\nabla g_{\epsilon}(x-y)|}{|y|^{n-1}}\,dy+\sqrt{{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{|y|>t}\frac{|\nabla f(x-y)|}{|y|^{n-1}}\,dy+\sqrt{{\epsilon}}.

Letting
{\epsilon}\rightarrow 0^{+}
we obtain

\fint_{\partial B(x,t)}|f(y)|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\leq c_{n}\int_{|y|>t}\frac{|\nabla f(x-y)|}{|y|^{n-1}}\,dy

and hence

t^{{\alpha}-1}\fint_{\partial B(x,t)}|f(y)|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\leq c_{n}t^{{\alpha}-1}\int_{|y|>t}\frac{|\nabla f(x-y)|}{|y|^{n-1}}\,dy\\
\leq c_{n}\int_{|y|>t}\frac{|\nabla f(x-y)||y|^{{\alpha}-1}}{|y|^{n-1}}\,dy\leq c_{n}I_{\alpha}(|\nabla f|)(x).

∎

As a consequence, we have the following Sobolev bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}
, which seem to be new. Corollary
4.5
will follow from the mapping properties of
I_{\alpha}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose
1\leq{\alpha}<n
and
1<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}:\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\|\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

At the endpoint
p=1
we have the following weak-type estimate

\|\mathcal{S}_{{\alpha}-1}f\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

5.
Weighted estimates

In this section, we prove new weighted estimates for our sparse operators

I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}f^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\mathbf{1}_{Q},\qquad f\geq 0,

where
0<{\alpha}<n
and
1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}.

Since these operators dominate our rough integral operators
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
(Theorems
1.1
,
1.4
, and
1.7
) we will also obtain new weighted Sobolev estimates of the form

\|wT_{\Omega,{\alpha}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|w\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(37)

We will refer to inequality (
37
) as a weighted Sobolev inequality. The parameters and class of weights will depend on the size of
\Omega
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the operator
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
is intimately related to the operator
f\mapsto\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}
(see Theorem
1.5
). We can work with the smaller maximal function
M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
defined in (
36
). Notice that

M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f=\big[M_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq c\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}.

By inequality (
17
) there exists finitely many sparse families
\mathscr{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{S}_{N}
such that

M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{N}I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}_{k}}|f|.

The following theorem shows that we may obtain the opposite inequalities in norm. It also yields Theorem
1.5
as a corollary.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
1\leq s<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
,
w\in A_{\infty}
, and
0<p<\infty
. Then given a sparse family
\mathscr{S}
of cubes there exists
C>0
, such that

\|I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

and

\|I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(w)}\leq C\|M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(w)}.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, we will first prove it in the case
p=1
for any
w\in A_{\infty}
. Once this is shown the conclusion will then follow from the
A_{\infty}
version of the Rubio de Francia extrapolation (see
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
). Fix a sparse subset
\mathscr{S}
and
w\in A_{\infty}
.
Let
E_{Q}\subseteq Q\in\mathscr{S}
be pairwise disjoint majorizing sets. Since
w\in A_{\infty}
and
|Q|\leq 2|E_{Q}|
we have

w(Q)\leq Cw(E_{Q}).

We can now estimate the
L^{1}(w)
norm

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f|w

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}w(Q)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\fint_{Q}|f|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}w(E_{Q})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{S}}\int_{E_{Q}}\big(M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\big)w

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\big)w.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Recall that the weighted estimates for
I_{\alpha}
and
M_{\alpha}
are known and were discovered by Muckenhoupt and Wheeden
[
19
]
. They showed the following equivalences for
1<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
:

•

M_{\alpha}
is bounded:

\|wM_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})};

•

I_{\alpha}
is bounded:

\|wI_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})};

•

w\in A_{p,q}

\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}w^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\fint_{Q}w^{-p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

The weak-type boundedness at the endpoint
p=1
,
q=\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}}

M_{\alpha},I_{\alpha}:L^{1}(w)\rightarrow L^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}},\infty}(w)

is equivalent to the
w\in A_{1,\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}}}
condition:

\left(\fint_{Q}w^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}}}\right)\leq C\inf_{x\in Q}w(x)^{\frac{n}{n-{\alpha}}}.

The
A_{p,q}
classes of weights are related to the usual Muckenhoupt class of weights via the following realization:

w\in A_{p,q}\Leftrightarrow w^{q}\in A_{1+\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}},

(38)

in particular
A_{p,q}
weights raised to the power
q
belong to
A_{\infty}
. Using this rescaling we can easily obtain the weighted norm inequalities for
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
and
M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
by realizing that it is just a rescaling of the
L^{p}
norm:

\|wM_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[M_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{q}{s}}w^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\|w^{s}M_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\|_{L^{\frac{q}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Since

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}}{n}\Leftrightarrow\frac{s}{q}=\frac{s}{p}-\frac{{\alpha}s}{n}

we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
1\leq s<p<\frac{n}{{\alpha}}
and
q
is defined by
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{{\alpha}}{n}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

w^{s}\in A_{\frac{p}{s},\frac{q}{s}}
:

\sup_{Q}\left(\,\fint_{Q}w^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\,\fint_{Q}w^{-\frac{ps}{p-s}}\right)^{\frac{p-s}{ps}}<\infty;

(2)

the operator
M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
satisfies

\|wM_{{\alpha},L^{s}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})};

(3)

for any sparse family of cubes
\mathscr{S}
, we have

\|wI_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})};

(4)

the operator
f\mapsto\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}
satisfies

\big\|w\big[I_{{\alpha}s}(|f|^{s})\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

When
p=s
and
q=\frac{ns}{n-{\alpha}s}
any of the operators
I_{{\alpha},L^{s}}^{\mathscr{S}}
,
M_{{\alpha},L^{s}}
, or
[I_{{\alpha}s}(|\cdot|^{s})]^{\frac{1}{s}}
are bounded from
L^{s}(w^{s})\rightarrow L^{q,\infty}(w^{q})
when
w^{s}\in A_{1,\frac{q}{s}}
i.e.,

\left(\fint_{Q}w^{\frac{ns}{n-{\alpha}s}}\right)\leq C\inf_{x\in Q}w(x)^{\frac{ns}{n-{\alpha}s}}

for all cubes
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

When
\Omega\in L^{r,r^{*}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
or
\Omega\in L(\log L)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, the pointwise bounds from Theorems
1.4
and
1.7
combined with Theorem
5.2
will yield new weighted Sobolev estimates for
T_{\Omega,{\alpha}}
. The class of weights will depend on the exponent
s=\frac{r^{\prime}n}{r^{\prime}+n}
. While this exponent can be used to compute the explicit class of weights, we find it more instructive to consider the power weights. It is well-known that the power weight
w(x)=|x|^{\lambda}
belongs to
A_{p}
if and only if
-n<\lambda<n(p-1)
. Using the equivalence (
38
) we see that
w(x)=|x|^{\lambda}
belongs to
A_{p,q}
if and only if

-\frac{n}{q}<\lambda<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}.

Such power weights satisfy
w^{s}\in A_{\frac{p}{s},\frac{q}{s}}
if and only if

-\frac{n}{q}<\lambda<\frac{n}{s}-\frac{n}{p}.

When
s=\frac{r^{\prime}n}{r^{\prime}+n}
, by our pointwise bounds from Theorem
1.4
we obtain Theorems
1.6
and
1.9
.

Abstract
. We obtain a forcing construction that shows that it is consistent that the torsion-free Abelian group ℚ ( λ ) \mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)} admits a Hausdorff group topology which is also 𝒰 \mathcal{U} -compact and contains no non-trivial convergent sequences, where λ \lambda is a cardinal whose cofinality is ω \omega and 𝒰 \mathcal{U} is a selective ultrafilter. This answers a question posed in [ 1 ] .



1.
Introduction

The study of compactness properties in topological groups has been carried on for more than 70 years. In a seminal paper by Halmos,
[
2
]
, it was shown that the real line can be endowed with a compact group topology. We note that algebraically the real line is the sum of
\mathfrak{c}
copies of the rationals, not only as an Abelian group but also as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space (a fact that is used in Halmos’s result and in much of the subsequent work in the matter).

We recall that, given an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and a topological space
X
, a
\mathcal{U}
-
limit
of a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
in
X
is a
z\in X
such that for every neighbourhood
A
of
z
,
\{n\in\omega:x_{n}\in A\}\in\mathcal{U}
. A topological space
X
is called
\mathcal{U}
-
compact
if every sequence has a
\mathcal{U}
-limit.

In the paper
[
3
]
, Castro and Tomita obtained a consistent classification – assuming some cardinal arithmetic and the existence of a selective ultrafilter
p
– of the Abelian torsion groups which admit a
p
-compact group topology. This proved the consistency of large countably compact torsion groups without non-trivial convergent sequences.

A more recent result,
[
1
]
, showed that given a selective ultrafilter
p
,
\mathbb{Q}^{(\kappa)}
can be endowed with a
p
-compact Hausdorff group topology without non-trivial convergent sequences, whenever
\kappa=\kappa^{\omega}
; this hypothesis implies that
\cf(\kappa)>\omega
. It remained open then whether such a result could consistently hold for cardinals of countable cofinality.

This article answers that question, using forcing to obtain a model where
\lambda
is a cardinal whose cofinality is
\omega
and such that
\mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)}
has a
\mathcal{U}
-compact Hausdorff group topology without non-trivial convergent sequences, where
\mathcal{U}
is a given selective ultrafilter.

2.
Notation

We shall fix throughout this article a cardinal
\lambda
and a selective ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
.

As usual, given
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}
, its
support
is
\supp a=\{\xi\in\lambda:a_{\xi}\neq 0\}
.

Let
G
be the Abelian group
\mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)}=\{a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}:\supp a\text{ is finite}\}
(considering coordinatewise addition as its operation).

If
E\subseteq\lambda
, we do a standard abuse of notation and consider
\mathbb{Q}^{(E)}=\{a\in G:\supp a\subseteq E\}
.

Given
\mu\in\lambda
, we define
\chi_{\mu}\in G
by
\chi_{\mu}(\mu)=1
and
\chi_{\mu}(\beta)=0
for all
\beta\in\lambda
,
\beta\neq\mu
. Now, given
\zeta:\omega\to\lambda
, we define
\chi_{\zeta}:\omega\to G
by
\chi_{\zeta}(n)=\chi_{\zeta(n)}
for each
n\in\omega
. And given a
\mu\in\lambda
, we define
\vec{\mu}
as the constant sequence of value
\mu
. Thus,
\chi_{\vec{\mu}}
is the constant sequence of value
\chi_{\mu}
(this will appear often in our work).

Since
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
\omega
, the
ultrapower
of
G
by
\mathcal{U}
, denoted
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
, is the quotient of the set
G^{\omega}
by the following equivalence relation:
g\sim h
if and only if
\{n\in\omega:g(n)=h(n)\}\in\mathcal{U}
. We will make frequent use of the fact that
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with all operations defined naturally (that is via representatives). If
g\in G^{\omega}
, we will denote its class in
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
by
[g]_{\mathcal{U}}
.

We now fix
\mathcal{H}\subseteq G^{\omega}
such that
([g]_{\mathcal{U}}:g\in\mathcal{H})\cup([\chi_{\vec{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{U}}:\mu<\lambda)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis for
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
.

3.
Forcing poset

Definition 3.1
.

We define
\mathcal{P}
as the set of the tuples of the form
(E,\alpha,\mathcal{G},\xi,\phi)
such that:

•

E
is a countable subset of
\lambda
containing
\omega
,

•

\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
,

•

\mathcal{G}
is a countable subset of
\mathcal{H}
,

•

\xi=(\xi_{g}:g\in\mathcal{G}
) is a family of elements of
\mathfrak{c}\cap E
,

•

\phi:\,\mathbb{Q}^{(E)}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha}
is a homomorphism,

•

\mathcal{U}-\lim(\phi\circ g)=\phi(\chi_{\xi_{g}})
for each
g\in\mathcal{G}
,

We define
(E,\alpha,\mathcal{G},\xi,\phi)\leq(E^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\xi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

if:

(1)

E\supseteq E^{\prime}
,

(2)

\alpha\geq\alpha^{\prime}
,

(3)

\mathcal{G}\supseteq\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
,

(4)

\xi_{g}=\xi^{\prime}_{g}
for each
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, and

(5)

for every
\xi<\alpha^{\prime}
and
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{\prime})}
,
\phi(a)(\xi)=\phi^{\prime}(a)(\xi)
.

Given
p\in\mathcal{P}
, we may denote its components by
E^{p}
,
\alpha^{p}
,
\mathcal{G}^{p}
,
\xi^{p}
and
\phi^{p}
.

If
H
is a generic filter over
\mathcal{P}
then the generic homomorphism defined by
H
is the mapping
\Phi
of domain
\bigcup\{\dom(\phi^{p}):p\in H\}
into
\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
defined by
\Phi(\cdot)(\xi)=\bigcup\{\phi^{p}(\cdot)(\xi):p\in H,\xi<\alpha^{p}\}
. In other words, if
p\in H
,
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}
and
\xi<\alpha_{p}
, then
\Phi(a)(\xi)=\phi^{p}(a)(\xi)
.

Naturally, we must assure that such generic homomorphisms are well-defined and into
\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
. We will do so by showing that, assuming CH in the ground model, this forcing notion is
\omega_{1}
-closed and has the
\omega_{2}
-chain condition, and thus preserves cardinals and
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
e\in G\backslash\{0\}
. Then the set
\mathcal{C}_{e}=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}\text{ and }\phi^{p}(e)\neq 0\}
is open and dense in
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Openness: suppose
p\in\mathcal{C}_{e}
and
q\leq p
. Then since
\phi^{p}(e)\neq 0
, for some
\beta<\alpha^{p}
,
\phi^{p}(e)(\beta)\neq 0
. By (2),
\beta<\alpha^{p}\leq\alpha^{q}
, and by (1)
E^{p}\subseteq E^{q}
. It follows that
e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q})}
and, by (6),
\phi^{q}(e)(\beta)=\phi^{p}(e)(\beta)\neq 0
. Thus,
q\in\mathcal{C}_{e}
as well.

Denseness: now let
p\in\mathcal{P}
be arbitrary. We shall produce a
q\leq p
such that
q\in\mathcal{C}_{e}
. First, take any
d_{0},d_{1}\in G\backslash\{0\}
such that
\supp e
,
\supp d_{0}
and
\supp d_{1}
are pariwise disjoint. Now take
C
a countable subset of
\lambda
such that
\omega\cup\supp e\cup\supp d_{0}\cup\supp d_{1}\cup\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal{G},n\in\omega}\supp g(n)\subseteq C
.

The Main Lemma of
[
1
]
(Lemma 3.4) tells us that there exists a homomorphism
\rho:\mathbb{Q}^{(C)}\to\mathbb{T}
such that:

•

\rho(e)\neq 0
and
\rho(d_{0})\neq\rho(d_{1})
;

•

\mathcal{U}-\lim(\rho\circ(\frac{1}{N}{g}))=\rho(\frac{1}{N}\chi_{\xi_{g}})
, for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
N\in\omega
.

Define now
E^{q}:=E^{p}\cup C
and extend
\phi^{p}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha^{p}}
to a
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q})}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha^{p}}
using the divisibility of the codomain. Also extend
\rho
to a
\psi:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q})}\to\mathbb{T}
. Define then
\alpha^{q}=\alpha^{p}+1
,
\mathcal{G}^{q}=\mathcal{G}^{p}
,
\xi^{q}=\xi^{p}
, and
\phi^{q}=\varphi^{\frown}\psi
.

It follows that
q\in\mathcal{C}_{e}
and
q\leq p
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
. Then the set
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:\alpha^{p}>\alpha\}
is open and dense in
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Openness: suppose
p\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
and
q\leq p
. We have that
\alpha^{p}>\alpha
and
\alpha^{q}\geq\alpha^{p}
, and so
q\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
.

Denseness: let
p\in\mathcal{P}
. If
\alpha^{p}>\alpha
, then
p\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
. Suppose now that
\alpha^{p}\leq\alpha
. We define

as follows:
E^{q}=E^{p}
,
\alpha^{q}=\alpha+1
,
\mathcal{G}^{q}=\mathcal{G}^{p}
,
\xi^{q}=\xi^{p}
, and
\phi^{q}={\phi^{p}}^{\frown}\psi
, where
\psi:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q})}\to\mathbb{T}^{[\alpha^{p},\alpha]}

It follows that

and
q\leq p
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{H}
. Then the set
\mathcal{S}_{g}:=\{p\in\mathcal{P}:g\in\mathcal{G}^{p}\}
is open and dense in
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Openness: suppose
p\in\mathcal{S}_{g}
and
q\leq p
. Since
g\in\mathcal{G}^{p}
and
\mathcal{G}^{p}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{q}
, it follows that

and thus

.

Denseness: Let
p\in\mathcal{P}
be given. First, take
E
a countable subset of
\lambda

\omega\cup E^{p}\subseteq E
and
\bigcup_{k\in\omega}\supp g(k)\subseteq E
. Take any

Define now:
E^{q}=E\cup\{\mu\}
,
\alpha^{q}=\alpha^{p}
,
\mathcal{G}^{q}=\mathcal{G}^{p}\cup\{g\}
, and
\xi^{q}=\xi^{p}\cup\{(g,\mu)\}
.

\phi^{p}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha^{p}}

\psi:\mathbb{Q}^{(E)}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha^{p}}

\phi^{q}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q})}\to\mathbb{T}^{\alpha^{q}}

It follows that

.
∎

.

\mathcal{P}

.

|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume CH. Then the partial order
\mathcal{P}

\omega_{2}
-chain condition.

Proof.

Since under CH,
\mathfrak{c}^{+}=\omega_{2}

\mathcal{P}
has the
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
-c.c.. So let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}

\mathfrak{c}^{+}

\mathcal{Q}
has a subset consisting of
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
pairwise compatible elements.

First, take a
\mathcal{Q}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
and an
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
such that
\alpha_{q}=\alpha
for each
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.

Using the
\Delta
-system Lemma, take a
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{0}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
such that
\{E^{q}:q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}\}
is a
\Delta
-system of root
\tilde{E}
. Now, using CH, we have that
\left|{(\mathbb{T}^{\alpha})}^{\mathbb{Q}^{(\tilde{E})}}\right|=\mathfrak{c}
, and thus we may take a
\mathcal{Q}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{1}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
such that
{\phi^{q}}|_{\mathbb{Q}^{(\tilde{E})}}={\phi^{p}}|_{\mathbb{Q}^{(\tilde{E})}}
for all
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}
.

Using the
\Delta
-system Lemma again, take
\mathcal{Q}_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{2}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
such that
\{\mathcal{G}^{q}:q\in\mathcal{Q}_{3}\}
is a
\Delta
-system of root
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
. Now since for each
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{3}
,
(\xi^{q}_{g}:g\in\tilde{\mathcal{G}})\in\mathfrak{c}^{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}
and
|\mathfrak{c}^{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}|=\mathfrak{c}
, take
\mathcal{Q}_{4}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{3}
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
such that for all
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}_{4}
and each
g\in\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
\xi^{q}_{g}=\xi^{p}_{g}
.

A common extension to
q,p\in\mathcal{Q}_{4}
is an
r
defined as follows:
E^{r}=E^{q}\cup E^{p}
;
\alpha^{r}=\alpha^{q}=\alpha^{p}
;
\mathcal{G}^{r}=\mathcal{G}^{q}\cup\mathcal{G}^{p}
;
\xi^{r}=\xi^{q}\cup\xi^{p}
.

To define
\phi^{r}
, notice that
\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{r})}=\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q}\backslash\tilde{E})}\oplus\mathbb{Q}^{(\tilde{E})}\oplus\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p}\backslash\tilde{E})}
. Then, let
\pi_{0}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{r})}\to\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q}\backslash\tilde{E})}
,
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{r})}\to\mathbb{Q}^{(\tilde{E})}
and
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{r})}\to\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p}\backslash\tilde{E})}
be the projections. Define
\phi^{r}=\phi^{q}\circ\pi_{0}+\phi^{q}\circ\pi_{1}+\phi^{p}\circ\pi_{2}=\phi^{q}\circ\pi_{0}+\phi^{p}\circ\pi_{1}+\phi^{p}\circ\pi_{2}
and we are done.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume CH. Then the forcing notion
\mathcal{P}
preserves cofinalities and cardinals, preserves
\mathfrak{c}
and does not add reals. Given
H
a
\mathcal{P}
-generic filter, the associated
\Phi
(as in
3.1
) is a well-defined monomorphism from
G
to
\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
. Also, given any
g\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a
\xi\in\mathfrak{c}
such that
\mathcal{U}-\lim(\Phi\circ g)=\Phi(\chi_{\xi})
. Furthermore, given any one-to-one sequence
h\in G^{\omega}
,
\Phi\circ h
does not converge.

Proof.

By Propositions
3.5
and
3.9
,
\mathcal{P}
preserves cofinalities (and therefore cardinals), does not add reals and preserves
\mathfrak{c}
. Note that since being a basis for
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
is absolute for transitive models of ZFC,
\mathcal{H}
is still a basis for
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
in the extension.

Let
H
be a
\mathcal{P}
-generic filter and let
\Phi
be its associated homomorphism.

First, let us see that
\Phi:G\to\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is well-defined. Let
q,p\in H
and suppose
\xi<\min\{\alpha^{q},\alpha^{p}\}
and
e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{q}\cap E^{q})}
. We must see that
\phi^{q}(e)(\xi)=\phi^{p}(e)(\xi)
. Take
r\in H
such that
r\leq q,p
. Then
\xi<\alpha^{r}
and
e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{r})}
, and by item (5) of Definition
3.1
,
\phi^{q}(e)(\xi)=\phi^{r}(e)(\xi)=\phi^{p}(e)(\xi)
.

Now let
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
and
e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)}
such that
e\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{e}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
are open and dense, let
p\in H
such that
e\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}
,
\phi^{p}(e)\neq 0
and
\alpha^{p}>\alpha
. Since
\phi^{p}(e)\neq 0
, there is a
\xi\leq\alpha^{p}
such that
\phi^{p}(e)(\xi)\neq 0
, and therefore
\Phi(e)(\xi)\neq 0
, so that
\Phi(e)\neq 0
. And since
\alpha\subseteq\alpha^{p}\subseteq\dom\Phi(e)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}
, and
\alpha
was arbitrary, it follows that
\dom\Phi(e)=\mathfrak{c}
.

We have thus seen that the domain of
\Phi
is
\mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)}
, the codomain is
\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
and that
\Phi
is injective.

Now we see that
\Phi
is a homomorphism. Let
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{(\lambda)}
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{e}
,
\mathcal{C}_{e^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{e+e^{\prime}}
are dense and open, take
p\in H
such that
e,e^{\prime},e+e^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{(E^{p})}
. We know that
\phi^{p}
is a homomorphism, and so
\Phi(e+e^{\prime})=\phi^{p}(e+e^{\prime})=\phi^{p}(e)+\phi^{p}(e^{\prime})=\Phi(e)+\Phi(e^{\prime})
.

Let now
g\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is open and dense, let
p\in H
such that
g\in\mathcal{G}^{p}
. We have then that
\mathcal{U}-\lim(\phi^{p}\circ g)=\phi^{p}(\xi_{g})
. Let us see that
\mathcal{U}-\lim(\Phi\circ g)=\Phi(\xi_{g})
. Let
F
be a finite subset of
\mathfrak{c}
and let
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
such that
F\subseteq\alpha
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is open and dense, and
p\in H
, let
q\in H
such that
q\leq p
and
\alpha^{q}>\alpha
. We have then that
\mathcal{U}-\lim(\pi_{F}\circ\phi^{q}\circ g)=(\pi_{F}\circ\phi^{q})(\xi_{g})
. Since
\pi_{F}\circ\phi^{q}=\pi_{F}\circ\Phi
(due to
\alpha^{q}\supseteq F
), it follows that
\mathcal{U}-\lim(\pi_{F}\circ\Phi\circ g)=(\pi_{F}\circ\Phi)(\xi_{g})
, as we sought for.

Finally, let
h\in G^{\omega}
be a one-to-one sequence. Since
\mathcal{E}_{h}
is open and dense, let
p\in H\cap\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Take then a
\beta<\alpha^{p}
such that
(\phi^{p}(h(n))(\beta):n\in\omega)
does not converge. Since
\Phi(h(n))(\beta)=\phi^{p}(h(n))(\beta)
for each
n\in\omega
, it follows that
(\Phi(h(n))(\beta):n\in\omega)
does not converge, which in turn implies that
(\Phi(h(n)):n\in\omega)=\Phi\circ h
does not converge.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

It is consistent with ZFC that given
\lambda
a countably cofinal cardinal and
\mathcal{U}
a selective ultrafilter,
G
can be endowed with a
\mathcal{U}
-compact Hausdorff group topology without non-trivial convergent sequences.

Proof.

Consider the forcing model obtained via forcing with
\mathcal{P}
. We fix a generic monomorphism
\Phi
as in
3.10
. Since we have that
\Phi
is a monomorphism from
G
to
\mathbb{T}^{\mathfrak{c}}
, then
\Phi
induces a Hausdorff group topology in
G
such that for any
g\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a
\xi\in\mathfrak{c}
such that
\mathcal{U}-\lim g=\chi_{\xi}
. Fix one such
\xi_{g}
for each
g\in\mathcal{H}
.

Now let us see that such topology is indeed
\mathcal{U}
-compact. Let
f\in G^{\omega}
. Since
([g]_{\mathcal{U}}:g\in\mathcal{H})\cup([\chi_{\vec{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{U}}:\mu<\lambda)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis for
\Ult_{\mathcal{U}}(G)
, there exist families
(r_{g}:g\in\mathcal{H})
and
(s_{\mu}:\mu<\lambda)
of rational numbers, all but finitely many of which are 0, such that
[f]_{\mathcal{U}}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{H}}r_{g}\cdot[g]_{\mathcal{U}}+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}s_{\mu}\cdot[\chi_{\vec{\mu}}]_{\mathcal{U}}
. It follows then that
\mathcal{U}-\lim f=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{H}}r_{g}\cdot(\mathcal{U}-\lim g)+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}s_{\mu}\cdot(\mathcal{U}-\lim\chi_{\vec{\mu}})=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{H}}r_{g}\cdot\chi_{\xi_{g}}+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}s_{\mu}\cdot\chi_{\mu}
and
f
has a
\mathcal{U}
-limit.

Finally, there are no non-trivial convergent sequences since for each
h\in G^{\omega}
one-to-one,
\Phi\circ h
does not converge, which means that in the induced topology on
G
,
h
does not converge.
∎

4.
Questions

The recent breakthrough by Hrušák, Ramos, Shelah and van Mill (
[
4
]
) obtained a countably compact group without non-trivial convergent sequences in ZFC. Their construction is over a
\mathfrak{c}
-sized group of order 2. Shortly after Tomita and Trianon-Fraga (
[
5
]
) expanded this construction to obtain a countably compact group without non-trivial convergent sequences which has cardinality
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
and order 2. It is noteworthy that these constructions did not yield so far a
\mathcal{U}
-compact group for any ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and do not seem to be suitable for non-torsion groups. (We recall that such results also imply the existence of a countably compact group whose square is not countably compact; therefore, not all countably compact groups are
\mathcal{U}
-compact for some
\mathcal{U}
).

As such, the following questions remain open:

Question 4.1
.

Is there a countably compact non-torsion group without non-trivial convergent sequences in ZFC?

Question 4.2
.

How large can countably compact groups without non-trivial convergent sequences be in ZFC?

Question 4.3
.

Is there a
\mathcal{U}
-compact group without non-trivial convergent sequences in ZFC, for some ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
?

It should be noted that a positive answer to the first question implies the negation of Wallace’s conjecture, that is, it implies the existence of a countably compact semigroup with both-sided cancellation which is not a group.

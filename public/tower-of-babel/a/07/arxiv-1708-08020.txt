Abstract
. We propose an analogy of splitting principle in genus- 0 0 Gromov–Witten theory. More precisely, we show how the Gromov–Witten theory of a variety X X can be embedded into the theory of the projectivization of a vector bundle over X X . An application is also given.

Introduction

In geometry and topology, it is common to apply base-change in order to reduce a problem. For example, splitting principle in algebraic topology reduces a vector bundle to a split one by applying a base-change. More specifically, let
X
be a smooth manifold and
V
a vector bundle over
X
. The pull-back of
V
along the projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{X}(V)\rightarrow X
splits off a line bundle. Applying it multiple times eventually reduces
V
to a split bundle. But to make it work, facts like the following are crucial.

\text{The pull-back~}\pi^{*}:H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*}(\mathbb{P}_{X}(V);\mathbb{Q})\text{~is injective}.

By using this, a lot of cohomological computations that is done on the pull-back of
V
can be related back to the ones on
V
.

In this paper, our focus will be on Gromov–Witten theory. But the above technique cannot be applied easily because Gromov–Witten theory lacks a nice ”pull-back” operation. Let
f:Y\rightarrow X
be a map between smooth projective varieties. The complexity already appears when
f
is an embedding. In this case, one might naïvely hope for some statement in the spirit of the following.

\text{(a part of) GWT}\text{($Y$) is a sub-theory of GWT}\text{($X$)},

where GWT stands for Gromov–Witten theory. But this is not quite true. The
closest theorem might be the quantum Lefschetz theorem (
[
3
,
8
]
), which
only works in genus
0
and has a restriction on the embedding.

Another common situation is when
f:Y\rightarrow X
is a smooth morphism. When the fibration is nice (e.g. projectivization of vector bundles in splitting principle), one might wonder whether something like the following could happen.

\text{GWT}\text{($X$) is a sub-theory of GWT}\text{($Y$)}.

If a theorem with the above idea can be established, we might be able to imitate splitting principle to simplify some problems via base-changes. But again, the relation between GWT(
X
) and GWT(
Y
) seems complicated, and very few work has been done along these lines.

We will work on the case when
Y
is the projectivization of a vector bundle
V
over
X
. In this paper, Corollary
1
realizes genus-
0
GWT(
X
) as a sub-theory of genus-
0
GWT(
\mathbb{P}(V)
) in a reasonably simple statement. To show an application, we generalize the main theorem in
[
5
]
to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
-fibrations in genus
0
.

It’s worth noting that splitting principle has already inspired some of the techniques in
[
11
]
. Roughly speaking, they apply base-changes along blow-ups to turn a vector bundle into a split one, and relate back via degeneration formula. But the relate-back process is very complicated, mainly due to the complexity of degeneration formula.

Statements of results

Let

be a smooth projective variety,
V
be a vector bundle over

and

\beta\in NE(X)

We first prove the following theorem.

{theo}

If

is globally generated, then the following holds.

\langle\pi^{*}\sigma_{1},\ldots,\pi^{*}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\tilde{\beta}}^{\mathbb{P}_{X}(V),\mathcal{O}(-1)}=\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\beta}^{X,V},

where
\tilde{\beta}\in NE(\mathbb{P}(V))
is an effective class such that
\pi_{*}\tilde{\beta}=\beta
and
(\tilde{\beta},c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1)))=0
.

Here both sides of the equation are twisted Gromov–Witten invariants defined in
Section
2.1
equation (
4

{rmk}

If we put descendants into the invariants and match them up like in Theorem

not
hold. But it holds if we use the pull-back of
\psi_{i}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
on the left-hand side. The difference between
\psi
-classes and the pull-back of
\psi
-classes can be made explicit, but it’s irrelevant in our paper.

Let
\lambda
be the equivariant parameter in the twisted theory. We also use the notation
[\dotsb]_{\lambda^{-N}}
for taking the
\lambda^{-N}
coefficient. We have the following corollary.

Corollary 1

If

according to the following.

\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\beta}^{X}=\left[\langle\pi^{*}\sigma_{1},\ldots,\pi^{*}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\tilde{\beta}}^{\mathbb{P}_{X}(V),\mathcal{O}(-1)}\right]_{\lambda^{-N}},

where
N=rank(V)+(\tilde{\beta},det(T_{\pi}))
with

the relative tangent bundle,

As an application, we prove the following generalization of
[
5

in genus

.

{theo}

Let
\pi_{1}:P_{1}\rightarrow X
,
\pi_{2}:P_{2}\rightarrow X
be two projective smooth morphisms between smooth projective varieties whose fibers are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Suppose their Brauer classes are equal in
H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. Furthermore, suppose there exists a ring isomorphism

\mathscr{F}:H^{*}(P_{1};\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*}(P_{2};\mathbb{Q}),

such that

(a)

\mathscr{F}
sends the subgroup

into
H^{*}(P_{2};\mathbb{Z})/tor(H^{*}(P_{2};\mathbb{Z}))
. Here

denotes the torsion subgroup.

(b)

\mathscr{F}
restricts to identity on
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. Here we identify
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Q})
as subrings under pull-backs of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}

(c)

\mathscr{F}(c_{1}(\omega_{\pi_{1}}))=c_{1}(\omega_{\pi_{2}})

Then we have

\langle\psi^{k_{1}}\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\psi^{k_{n}}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\beta}^{P_{1}}=\langle\psi^{k_{1}}\mathscr{F}\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\psi^{k_{n}}\mathscr{F}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\Psi(\beta)}^{P_{2}},

\mathscr{F}
under Poincaré duality.

{rmk}

When one of
P_{i}
is the projectivization of vector bundle, all these conditions are equivalent to the existence of vector bundles
V_{1},V_{2}
such that
c(V_{1})=c(V_{2})
and
P_{1}=\mathbb{P}(V_{1}),P_{2}=\mathbb{P}(V_{2})

{rmk}

The reduction technique in
[
11

by blow-ups does not seem to work in our situation, as the Brauer group is a birational invariant.

0.2.
Some further questions

Our result is based on Theorem
1.1
. Note that this method seems to fail in high genus. Therefore a further question is the following.

Question 1
.

Are there any similar results as Theorem
0.1
or Corollary
1
in high genus?

Although the proof of Theorem
0.1
is to mainly demonstrate the use of “splitting principle” type of idea in Gromov–Witten theory, we are still interested in the following question.

Question 2
.

Is it essential to put the requirement that
P_{1},P_{2}
have the same Brauer class?

In the meantime, we are wondering to what extent the topology can determine the GWT of a smooth fibration. In particular, one way to phrase our questions is the following.

Question 3
.

Let
P_{1}\rightarrow X,P_{2}\rightarrow X
be two smooth projective morphisms between smooth projective varieties.
Suppose all of their fibers are isomorphic to a certain variety
F
. Can we further impose a suitable isomorphism between
H^{*}(P_{1},\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(P_{2},\mathbb{Q})
, and conclude GWT(
P_{1}
)=GWT(
P_{2}
)?

Finally, we want to remark that Theorem
1.1
yields a number of new identities besides Thereom
0.1
. They are summarized in section
2.2
. They are interesting to us for a few reasons. For example they are relatively simple, especially because they are closed formulas and there is no generating series or mirror map involved (compare
[
2
]
). This seems to show us that the Gromov–Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}(V)
(maybe twisted by
\mathcal{O}(-1)
) and the Gromov–Witten theory of
X
twisted by
V
are closely related. Note that another evidence that already exists in literature is the striking similarity between their
I
-functions when
V
splits. We suspect that these identities are a tip of the iceberg, and there might be a general relationship underneath. Therefore we would like to ask the following (vague) question.

Question 4
.

Is there a reasonably nice relationship between GWT(
\mathbb{P}(V)
) and the GWT(X) twisted by
V
, that includes our identities as special cases?

1.
The naturality of virtual class under certain blow-up

In this section, we take a detour to study the naturality of virtual class under blow-up. Keep in mind that our ultimate goal is still Theorem
0.1
, and it will be proven in section
2.2
when the tools are ready.

Recall a vector bundle
V
over a variety
X
is
convex
if for any morphism
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow X
,
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},f^{*}V)=0
. A smooth projective variety is called
convex
if its tangent bundle is a convex vector bundle. Note that a globally generated vector bundle is always convex.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Let
V
be a vector bundle of rank
\geq 2
over
X
. We want to understand the Gromov–Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}(V)
. For our purpose, we will assume the following.

Convention 1
.

Assume
V
is a globally generated vector bundle.

This assumption can be realized by tensoring with a sufficiently ample line bundle. Define
Y=\mathbb{P}(V\oplus\mathcal{O})
. This projective bundle can be viewed as a compactification of the total space of
V
. The zero section induces an embedding
X\hookrightarrow Y
. We denote its image by
Y_{0}
. There is also an embedding
\mathbb{P}(V)\hookrightarrow Y
whose image is denoted by
Y_{\infty}
(the divisor at infinity).

\mathbb{C}^{*}
can act on
V
by fixing the base and scaling the fibers (direct sum of weight
1
representations). This will induce a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
Y
. Consider
\tilde{Y}=Bl_{Y_{0}}Y
. We denote the contraction by

\pi:\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Y.

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action uniquely lifts to
\tilde{Y}
. On the other hand, it is not hard to see

\tilde{Y}\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}).

Again, it can be seen as the compactification of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)
over
\mathbb{P}(V)
. Denote the zero section by
\tilde{Y}_{0}
and the infinity section by
\tilde{Y}_{\infty}
. One can check that

\pi(\tilde{Y}_{0})=Y_{0}\quad,\quad\pi(\tilde{Y}_{\infty})=Y_{\infty}.

This
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action induces actions on moduli of stable maps. There is a stabilization morphism
\tau:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\tilde{Y},\pi^{!}\beta)\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y,\beta)
. Because
V
is globally generated, by
[
10
]
we have (nonequivariantly)

\tau_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\tilde{Y},\pi^{!}\beta)]^{vir}=[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y,\beta)]^{vir}.

In this

Note 1.1
.

, we would like to partially extend the above identity to equivariant setting under the given
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action. Let
Z\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y,\beta)
be the locus consisting of the stable maps at least one of whose components map nontrivially to the closed subvariety
Y_{\infty}
.
Z
is a closed substack. Denote
\mathscr{U}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y,\beta)\backslash Z
the complement. Let
[\mathscr{U}]^{vir,eq}
denote the equivariant virtual class induced by the perfect obstruction theory coming from the moduli of stable map. Also denote
\tau^{-1}(\mathscr{U})
by
\tilde{\mathscr{U}}
. We will show the following.

{theo}

The following holds for equivariant virtual classes.

\tau_{*}[\tilde{\mathscr{U}}]^{vir,eq}=[\mathscr{U}]^{vir,eq}.

Once this is established, we can compare the localization residues, and eventually prove the following by virtual localization.

{theo}

The following holds for equivariant virtual classes.

\tau_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\tilde{Y},\pi^{!}\beta)]^{vir,eq}=[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y,\beta)]^{vir,eq}.

We prove Theorem
1.1
for the sake of completeness. But Theorem
1.1
is already strong enough to deduce Theorem
0.1
.

.

\tilde{\mathscr{U}}_{\mathbb{G}}=\tau_{\mathbb{G}}^{-1}(\mathscr{U}_{\mathbb{G}})

,

Lemma 3

Theorem

The case when

Lemma 4

The open substack
\mathscr{U}_{\mathbb{G}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(Y_{\mathbb{G}},\beta)

Proof 1.3
.

It suffices to prove that for any stable map

0\rightarrow T_{p_{\mathbb{G}}}\rightarrow T_{Y_{\mathbb{G}}}\rightarrow(p_{\mathbb{G}})^{*}T_{\mathbb{G}}\rightarrow 0.

Since they are vector bundles, this short exact sequence pulls back to

\mathbb{P}^{1}

We choose the point

. Since twisted Gromov–Witten invariant can be computed by descendant Gromov–Witten invariants, we also have

\langle\psi^{k_{1}}\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\psi^{k_{n}}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\beta}^{\mathbb{P}_{P_{1}}(V_{1}),\mathcal{O}(-1)}=\langle\psi^{k_{1}}\mathscr{F}\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\psi^{k_{n}}\mathscr{F}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\Psi(\beta)}^{\mathbb{P}_{P_{2}}(V_{2}),\mathcal{O}(-1)}.

Immediately applying Corollary
1
, we have

\langle\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\beta}^{P_{1}}=\langle\mathscr{F}\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\mathscr{F}\sigma_{n}\rangle_{0,n,\Psi(\beta)}^{P_{2}}.

This is almost the form of Theorem
0.1
except we don’t have descendants here. However, since we only work on genus-
0
Gromov–Witten theory, descendants invariants can be computed via the topological recursion relation from the absolute invariants. Since the cohomology rings of
H^{*}(P_{1};\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{*}(P_{2};\mathbb{Q})
are identified, each step in the computation can be matched. This concludes our proof for Theorem
0.1
.

Appendix A
Correspondence of residues

Let’s put everything into a general setting. Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a
proper map between Deligne–Mumford stacks. Suppose
T=\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts on
both
X
and
Y
and
f
is equivariant. Let the connected components of the fixed loci of
X
and
Y
be

X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}
and
Y_{1},\dotsc,Y_{m}
, respectively. Denote

i_{X_{j}}:X_{j}\hookrightarrow X
,
i_{Y_{i}}:Y_{i}\hookrightarrow Y
as the
corresponding embeddings. Suppose that there are
equivariant perfect obstruction theories of
X
and
Y
. They induce natural
perfect obstruction theories on components
X_{j}
,
Y_{i}
(
[
6
]
). We want to prove the following theorem.

Proposition 14
.

Assume
X
and
Y
admit equivariant embeddings into smooth Delign-Mumford
stacks with
\mathbb{C}^{*}
actions. Suppose
f_{*}
preserves equivariant virtual classes, i.e.,
f_{*}[X]^{vir,eq}=[Y]^{vir,eq}
.
Fixing a connected component
Y_{i}
of the fixed locus in
Y
. We have
the following equality in
A_{*}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(Y_{i})
.

f_{*}\left(\sum\limits_{f(X_{j})\subset Y_{i}}\displaystyle\frac{[X_{j}]^{vir}}{e_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(N^{vir}_{X_{j}/X})}\right)=\displaystyle\frac{[Y_{i}]^{vir}}{e_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(N^{vir}_{Y_{i}/Y})},

where
N^{vir}_{X_{j}/X}
and
N^{vir}_{Y_{i}/Y}
are the corresponding virtual
normal bundles, and we slightly abuse the notation by writing
f|_{f^{-1}(Y_{i})}

simply as
f
.

Proof A.1
.

This is basically a direct consequence of the decomposition of equivariant
Chow groups in
[
9
, Theorem 5.3.5]
and the explicit localization formula
in
[
6
]
. More precisely, by
[
9
, Theorem 5.3.5]
, the equivariant Chow groups of
X
and
Y
admit
the following decompositions.

A^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{*}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}]=\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{n}A^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{*}(X_{j})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[\lambda]}\mathbb{Q}[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}],

A^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{*}(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}]=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}A^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{*}(Y_{i})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[\lambda]}\mathbb{Q}[\lambda,\lambda^{-1}].

Since
f
maps fixed locus into fixed locus,
f_{*}
respects these
decompostions. Now
[
6
]
tells us that under the above decompositions, the

X_{j}
component of
[X]^{vir,eq}
can be explicitly written as

\dfrac{[X_{j}]^{vir}}{e_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(N^{vir}_{X_{j}/X})}
. Since
f_{*}
respects the
decomposition, the
Y_{i}
component of

f_{*}[X]^{vir,eq}
is
f_{*}\left(\sum\limits_{f(X_{j})\subset Y_{i}}\displaystyle\frac{[X_{j}]^{vir}}{e_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(N^{vir}_{X_{j}/X})}\right)
. On
the other hand, the
Y_{i}
component of
[Y]^{vir,eq}
is

\dfrac{[Y_{i}]^{vir}}{e_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(N^{vir}_{Y_{i}/Y})}
. Since

f_{*}[X]^{vir,eq}=[Y]^{vir,eq}
, the result follows.

{rmk}

Conversely, if all localization residues match, one can conclude that

f_{*}[X]^{vir,eq}=[Y]^{vir,eq}
by similar argument. Again, the
decomposition
[
9
, Theorem 5.3.5]
is the key.

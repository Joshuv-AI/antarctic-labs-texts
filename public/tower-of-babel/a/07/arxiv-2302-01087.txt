1.
Introduction

Let
{B}(t)
be a standard Brownian motion with respect to a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\bm{\mathsf{P}})
, and let

{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be smooth continuous real function
[1]
. A classical problem in stochastic analysis is to prove that the Dolean-Dades stochastic exponential

(1.1)

\displaystyle{Z}(t)=\exp\left(\int_{o}^{t}{\psi}(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u)|^{2}du\right)

(1.2)

\displaystyle{Z}(0)=0

is a true martingale
[2]
. The DDSE is the exact solution of the geometric Brownian motion
[1]

(1.3)

\displaystyle{d{Z}(t)}={\psi}(t){Z}(t)~\xi(t)dt\equiv{\psi}(t){Z}(t)~d{B}(t)

where
d{B}(t)=\xi(t)dt
and
\xi(t)
is a Gaussian white noise. The solution has the Ito stochastic integral representation

(1.4)

\displaystyle{Z}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u){Z}(u)d{B}(u)

The explicit solution (1.1) is obtained
[1]
via the Ito expansion of
\log{Z}(t)
. Proving that
{Z}(t)
is a true martingale is actually a somewhat
difficult and subtle problem and is relevant to other theorems and results
[2-7]
. Ito integrals of the form (1.4) are not always martingales. The well-known necessary and sufficient conditions for
{Z}(t)
to be a true martingale are due to Novikov
[2]
and also Kazamaki
[5]
. The Novikov criteria are

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{o}^{t}{\psi}(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u)|^{2}du\right)=1

and the bound

(1.6)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u)|^{2}du\right)<\infty,~~\forall t>0

or equivalently
\frac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u)|^{2}du<\infty
. The function
{\psi}
can also be a random process
{\psi}(t,\omega)
with respect to
\omega\in\Omega
with Novikov criteria
[6]

(1.7)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{o}^{t}{\psi}(u,\omega)d{B}(u,\omega)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u,\omega)|^{2}du\right)=1

and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}|{\psi}(u,\omega)|^{2}du\right)\right\}<\infty,~~\forall t>0

In this note we only consider (1.5), and a short new proof is given to establish that
{Z}(t)
is a martingale. The exact criteria (1.5) and (1.6) are established, via a cluster-expansion method and utilising moment and cumulant-generating functions.

Geometric Brownian motion arises naturally in problems of stochastic exponential growth, which occurs when a quantity of interest grows exponentially but with a random multiplier and/or random waiting time between spurts of growth. Such processes are ubiquitous in both Nature and in human-created systems: bacterial growth in a sustaining medium; in nuclear and cellular fission; tissue growth in embryonic and cancer biology; in viral epidemics; in financial markets and bubbles, and the Black-Scholes option pricing model; internet growth and propagation of viral social media posts; population and extinction dynamics; Moore’s law for computer processing power; and inflationary expansion of the very early Universe with random fluctuations
[8-17]
. Noise can either boost stochastic exponential growth or drive a population to extinction
[8,11,12]
.

Most such models on random growth have focussed on the SDE for geometric Brownian motion since it can be solved exactly with a strong solution. Suppose a system evolves exponentially via the simple linear ODE
dX(t)=\alpha X(t)dt
then
X(t)=X(0)\exp(\alpha t)
. If
\alpha>0
then the system undergoes exponential growth and
X(t)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and if
\alpha<0
then the system exponentially decays or collapses so that
X(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. The system is stable or static for
\alpha=0
. If the system is randomly perturbed by white noise (additively) then

(1.9)

\displaystyle d{X}(t)=\alpha{X}(t)dt+\psi(t){X}(t)\xi(t)dt\equiv\alpha{X}(t)dt+\psi(t){X}(t)d{B}(t)

A strong solution exists for this SDE
[1]
. Given any
C^{2}
-functional
f({X}(t))
, the Ito Lemma is

\displaystyle df({X}(t))=\nabla_{X}f({X}(t))dX(t)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}_{X}f({X}(t))d[{X},{X}](t)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}f({X}(t))d{X}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}_{X}f({X}(t))|\psi(t)|^{2}dt

(1.10)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}f({X}(t))\big\{\alpha{X}(t)dt+\psi(t){X}(t)d{B}(t)\big\}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}_{X}f({X}(t))|\psi(t)|^{2}dt

where
\nabla_{X}=d/d{X}(t)
and
\nabla^{2}_{X}=d^{2}/d{X}(t)^{2}
. Using
f({X}(t))=\log{X}(t)

\displaystyle d{U}(t)\equiv d\log{X}(t)=\frac{1}{{X}(t)}d{X}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{|{X}(t)|^{2}}\right)|\psi(t)|^{2}|{X}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{X}(t)}\big\{\alpha{X}(t)dt+\psi(t){X}(t)d{B}(t)\big\}-\frac{1}{2}\psi(t)|^{2}dt

(1.11)

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}|\psi(t)|^{2}\right)dt+\psi(t)d{B}(t)

Then the solution is

(1.12)

\displaystyle X(t)=X(0)\exp(\alpha t)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)=\exp(\alpha t){Z}(t)

The expectation is

(1.13)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{X}(t)=X(0)\exp(\alpha t)\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)=\exp(\alpha t)\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)

which is
\bm{\mathsf{E}}{X}(t)=\exp(\alpha t)
if
\bm{\mathsf{E}}Z(t)=1
. The martingale property of
{Z}(t)
is therefore desirable and ensures there is no blow up at any finite time
t>0
.

2.
Main theorem and proof utilising a cluster expansion method

2.1.
Preliminary definitions and lemmas

Prior to the proof, we first establish the following preliminary definitions and lemmas. The proof utilises a cluster expansion method
[18-21]
.

Definition 2.1
.

White noise is (informally) defined as a zero-centred Gaussian process
\xi
with covariance
\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t)\xi(s)=\delta(t-s)
and
expectation
\bm{\mathsf{E}}\xi(t)=0
. A scalar or inner product
\langle\bullet,\bullet\rangle
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\phi\rangle=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\big\langle\psi,\xi\big\rangle\big\langle\phi,\xi\big\rangle=\int\!\!\!\!\int\psi(s)\phi(t)\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(s)\xi(t)dsdt=\int\!\!\!\!\int\psi(s)\phi(t)\delta(t-s)dsdt

Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\psi\rangle=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\big\langle\psi,\xi\big\rangle\big\langle\psi,\xi\big\rangle=\int\!\!\!\!\int\psi(s)\psi(t)\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(s)\xi(t)dsdt=\int\!\!\!\!\int\psi(s)\psi(t)\delta(t-s)dsdt

which is

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\psi\rangle=\int|\psi(s)|^{2}ds=\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

The noise
\xi
can be formulated as a Gaussian random process on any space of distributions containing
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Stochastic
integrals of the form
\int\psi(s)\xi(s)ds
are well defined if
\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
\psi
is an indicator function on
[0,t]
then
the process
B(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\xi(s)ds
is a Brownian motion and
dB(t)=\xi(t)dt
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a time-ordered set
(t_{1},...t_{m})\in(0,T)
, with
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<...<t_{m-1}<t_{m}
, the mth-order moments and cumulants for the white noise
\xi(t)
are given by

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{1})\otimes...\otimes\xi(t_{m})=\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

(2.4)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}~\xi(t_{1})\otimes...\otimes\xi(t_{m})=\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

where
\mathbb{T}
is a ’time-ordering operator’. For example if
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
and
f(t)
is a function of t then
\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}f(t_{2})f(t_{1})f(t_{3}=f(t_{1})f(t_{2})f(t_{3})
. Since
\xi(t)
is a Gaussian, it is defined entirely by its first two moments and all cumulants or order
m\geq 3
vanish so that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})\right\}_{m\geq 3}=0

For example, at second order for a Gaussian process the binary cumulant is equivalent to the binary moment

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})=\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})-\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{1})\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{2})=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})=\delta(t_{2}-t_{1})

Definition 2.3
.

The moment generating function (MGF)
\mathscr{M}[\xi(t)]
and the cumulant-generating function [CGF]

\mathscr{C}[\xi(t)]
of the white noise
\xi(t)
are given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{t}...\int_{0}^{t_{m-1}}dt_{1}...dt_{m}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}~\xi(t_{q})

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int_{0}^{t}...\int_{0}^{t_{m-1}}dt_{1}...dt_{m}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

where
{\beta}
is an arbitrary real constant. It is important to note that the summation in (2.8) begins from
m=1
and not
m=0
and that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})\neq\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\xi(t_{q})

(2.10)

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})\neq\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi(t_{q})

Equations (2.7) and (2.8) can also be written as

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int{\mathbf{D}}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{m}}{m!}\int{\mathbf{D}}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

where
\int\bm{\mathrm{D}}_{m}[t]=\int...\int dt_{1}...dt_{m}
is a ’path integral’. Choosing
{\beta}=+1

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}[\xi(t)]=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

Lemma 2.4
.

The relation between the MGF and the CGF is

(2.15)

\displaystyle{\log}{\mathscr{M}}[\xi(t)]={\mathscr{C}}[\xi(t)]

so that

(2.16)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\xi(t)]=\exp\big({\mathscr{C}}[\xi(t)]\big)

Hence

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})

(2.18)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\bm{\mathsf{C}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\xi(t_{q})\right)

Proposition 2.5
.

Given
\psi\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and the white noise
\xi(t)
, then for all
t>0
define the Gaussian noise or random function

(2.19)

\displaystyle\Xi(t)=\psi(t)\xi(t)

Then
\bm{\mathsf{E}}\Xi(t)=0
and

(2.20)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\Xi(t)\Xi(s)=\psi(t)\psi(s)\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t)\xi(s)=\psi(t)\psi(s)\delta(t-s)

Lemma 2.6
.

Given
\Xi(t)=\psi(t)\xi(t)
then for any
t_{2}>t_{1}
and
(t_{1},t_{2})\in(0,t)

(2.21)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\psi\rangle\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\Xi(t_{1})\Xi(t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(t_{1})|^{2}dt_{1}=\|\psi\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}

Proof.

Using the sifting property of the delta function

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\Xi(t_{1})\Xi(t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{2})\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\Xi(t_{1})\Xi(t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t_{1})\bigg|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\psi(t_{2})\delta(t_{2}-t_{1})dt_{2}\bigg|dt_{1}

(2.22)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{1})dt_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(t_{1})|^{2}dt_{1}\equiv\|\psi\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R^{+}})}

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Given
\Xi(t)=\beta(t)\xi(t)
then the Dolean-Dades stochastic exponential
{Z}(t)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{Z}(t)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)\xi(u)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

(2.23)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\Xi(u)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

2.2.
Main theorem

The main theorem and proof are now as follows:

Theorem 2.8
.

The stochastic exponential
{Z}(t)
is a true martingale iff

(2.24)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)\big\}=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)=1

and requiring

(2.25)

\displaystyle\exp(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2})=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)<\infty,~~\forall t>0

Proof.

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)d{B}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi(u)\xi(u)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\Xi(u)du-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\bm{\mathsf{E}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\Xi(u)du\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\bm{\mathsf{E}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}...\int_{0}^{t_{m-1}}dt_{m}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\bm{\mathsf{E}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})

(2.26)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})

However, from (2.11), the MGF is

(2.27)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\Xi(t)]=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\bm{\mathsf{E}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})

so (2.26) becomes

(2.28)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right){\mathscr{M}}[\Xi(t)]

Now using Lemma (2.4)

(2.29)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}[\Xi(t)]=\exp({\mathscr{C}}[\Xi(t)])

giving

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{E}}{Z}(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right){\mathscr{M}}[\Xi(t)]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left({\mathscr{C}}[\Xi(t)]\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\bm{\mathsf{C}}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})\right)

(2.30)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}...t_{m}]\bm{\mathsf{C}}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\prod_{q=1}^{m}\psi(t_{q})\xi(t_{q})\right)

Now since
\xi(t)
is a Gaussian process, all cumulants of order
m\geq 3
vanish so that

(2.31)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{C}}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}\Xi(t_{q})\right\}_{m\geq 3}=\bm{\mathsf{C}}\overrightarrow{\mathbb{T}}\left\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}\psi(t_{q})\xi(t_{q})\right\}_{m\geq 3}=0

This leaves

\displaystyle\bm{\mathbf{E}}{Z}(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\int\mathbf{D}_{m}[t_{1}]\psi(t_{1})\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi(t_{1})\big\}+\frac{1}{2}\int\mathbf{D}[t_{1},t_{2}]\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{2})\bm{\mathsf{C}}\big\{\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\psi(t_{1})\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi(t_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{2})\bm{\mathsf{C}}\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\psi(t_{1})\underbrace{\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\{\xi(t_{1})\big\}}_{=0}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{2})\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{1}dt_{2}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{2})\bm{\mathsf{E}}~\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{1}dt_{2}{\psi}(t_{1}){\psi}(t_{2}){\delta}(t_{2}-t_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}{\psi}(t_{1})\bigg|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{2}{\psi}(t_{2}){\delta}(t_{2}-t_{1})\bigg|\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\psi(t_{1})\psi(t_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(t_{1})|^{2}dt_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\psi(u)|^{2}du\right)

(2.32)

Introduction

In this article we design and analyze arbitrarily high-order quadrature rules for a class of weakly singular integrals in two dimensions, with an isolated singularity at the origin. Such weakly singular integrals arises naturally in non-local partial differential equations involving the fractional Laplacian.

There are different methods of numerical integration for weakly singular integrals, such as singularity subtraction and singularity removing by change of co-ordinates, see for example
[
6
]

[

]

[

[

[

[

This method is efficient as it introduces correction weights for the trapezoidal rule near the singularity. The correction weights are pre-computed. Once being stored, the modified trapezoidal rule can be easily set up. It is well-known that the trapezoidal rule is spectrally accurate for smooth functions with compact support or periodic functions, while it is second order accurate for other functions. There are two main sources of errors: one comes from singularities and the other is from the boundaries. We focus on the error caused by singularity in this paper.

Rokhlin

The method we used for constructing increasingly accurate modified trapezoidal rule is to require the modified rule exactly evaluate the integral of the product of some monomials with increasing degree and the singular kernel. This gives rise to a linear system of equations for the correction weights. The more weights one is using, the larger the linear system is. In general, the weights obtained from solving the linear systems depend on the mesh size

They also gave a rigorous proof for the rate of convergence of the quadrature rule in 1D and conditional proof for the two dimensional counterpart. In fact, the difficulty for proving the linear systems of the correction weights has a well-defined solution when

For any compactly supported function

\displaystyle T_{h}^{0}[f]\coloneqq h^{n}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f(\beta h).

Let

\displaystyle s(x)\coloneqq\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{|x|^{n+\alpha}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \text{with}\ 0<\alpha<2.

(10)

where
\bar{\omega}_{\beta}

\mathcal{L}_{p}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:|\beta|_{1}\leq p\}

\displaystyle\int_{(-h,h)^{2}}|\phi(x)|s(x)\ \operatorname{d}\!{x}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(0,h)}s(x)\ \operatorname{d}\!{x}\lesssim\int_{0}^{h}r^{1-\alpha}\ \operatorname{d}\!{r}\sim h^{2-\alpha},

We now elaborate on how to determine the correction weights

10

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x)s(x)x^{2\xi}\operatorname{d}\!{x}

\displaystyle=T_{h}^{0}[g\cdot s\cdot x^{2\xi}]

\displaystyle+a(h)\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{L}_{p}}\omega_{\beta}(h)(\beta h)^{2\xi}g(\beta h),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}:|\xi|_{1}\leq p,

(11)

\displaystyle|\omega(h)-\bar{\omega}|_{2}=O(h^{2p+2}).

We will introduce some definitions, lemmas and a theorem before we prove the main theorem

Definition 3.1

Given

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\exp{(-2\pi i\xi\cdot x)}\operatorname{d}\!{x}.

We state the Poisson Summation formula for later use, for a proof, see

Lemma 3.2

.

Let

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{n+\delta}}\quad\text{for some}\ C,\delta>0\ \text{and for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(36)

and whose Fourier transform

\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(m)|<\infty,

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{f}(m)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(k).

(38)

It is not hard to verify that if

, then

satisfies the hypotheses of Poisson Summation formula, in fact,

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\left(\frac{1+|R|}{1+|x|}\right)^{n+1}\|f\|_{\infty},\ \text{where}\ supp(f)\subset B_{R}(0)\ \text{for some}\ R>0,

(39)

and for fixed

, then by using integration by part with respect to

(n+1)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(m)=(-1)^{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial^{n+1}_{j}f)(x)\frac{\exp\{-2\pi ix\cdot m\}}{(-2\pi im_{j})^{n+1}}\ \operatorname{d}\!{x},

(40)

note that

, we immediately have

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(m)|\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2\pi}\right)^{n+1}\frac{\max_{|l|_{1}=n+1}\|\partial^{l}f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}}{|m|^{n+1}}.

(41)

The right hand side of above inequality is summable over

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, the partial derivatives of order

(\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n})
of

\displaystyle\partial^{k}\frac{x^{\eta}x_{1}^{2}}{|x|^{n+\alpha}}=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}}}P_{k,\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right),

(49)

where

is a polynomial in

.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\partial^{0}\frac{x^{\eta}x_{1}^{2}}{|x|^{n+\alpha}}=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)^{\eta+2e_{1}},

(50)

which shows

with
j=1,\cdots,n
. Note that
P_{k,\eta}^{(j)}
is a polynomial with one degree less than
P_{k,\eta}
. Now,

\displaystyle\partial^{k+e_{i}}\left\{\frac{x^{\eta+2e_{1}}}{|x|^{n+\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\left\{\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}}}P_{k,\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=-(n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1})\frac{1}{|x|^{n-1+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}}}\frac{x_{i}}{|x|}P_{k,\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|x|^{n-1+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}}}\left[P_{k,\eta}^{(i)}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\left(1-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)-\sum_{j\not=i}P_{k,\eta}^{(j)}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|^{n-1+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}}}\Bigg[-(n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1})\frac{x_{i}}{|x|}P_{k,\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)P_{k,\eta}^{(i)}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)-\sum_{j\not=i}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}P^{(j)}_{k,\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2+\alpha-|\eta|_{1}+|k|_{1}+1}}P_{k+e_{i},\eta}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right).

(51)

One can check that

\partial^{m}_{j}g(x)&,&|x|>h\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle|I_{m}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{0\leq l\leq m}\frac{1}{h^{m-l}}\left|\int_{\frac{h}{2}\leq|x|\leq h}\partial^{m-l}_{j}\Psi\left(\frac{x}{h}\right)\partial^{l}_{j}g(x)\partial^{N-m}_{j}s(x)\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi i}{h}k\cdot x\right)\operatorname{d}\!{x}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{h<|x|<R}\partial^{m}_{j}g(x)\partial^{N-m}_{j}s(x)\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi i}{h}k\cdot x\right)\operatorname{d}\!{x}\right|,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},k\not=0}|I(h,k)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2p+4-\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},k\not=0,\\
k_{j}=\max_{l}|k_{l}|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|k_{j}|^{n+1}}\leq Ch^{2p+4-\alpha}.

(83)

Combining

\displaystyle=\{(s,0):1\leq s\leq p\}=\{(1,0),(2,0),\dots,(p,0)\}.

(138)

The arrangement for

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccccc}(1,1)&(1,2)&\dots&(1,k)&\cdots&(1,p-1)\\
\downarrow&(k-1,2)\nearrow&\vdots&\nearrow\\
(k,1)\nearrow&\dots&(k,p-k)&\\
\vdots&\nearrow&&\\
(p-1,1)&&&\end{array}\

\mathcal{C}_{3}
from

, followed by diagonal
(2,1),(1,2)

\dots
, followed by diagonal

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}=\{(1,1),(2,1),(1,2),\dots,(p-1,1),(p-2,2),\dots,(1,p-1)\}.

(144)

We also arrange
\mathcal{G}
in the same way. We first show matrix
\boldsymbol{K}
organized under this arrangement has a special structure.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
, there exist an ordering on
\mathcal{I}_{p}
such that

1.

K_{1,1}=1
while
K_{j,1}=0
for all
2\leq j\leq N_{p}
.

2.

\boldsymbol{K}
can be expressed as a block matrix of the form

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{K}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}&\boldsymbol{B}\\
\boldsymbol{C}&\boldsymbol{D}\end{pmatrix},

(145)

such that
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{(2p+1)\times(2p+1)}
,
\boldsymbol{C}=\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\times(2p+1)}
,
\boldsymbol{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\times\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle\det\boldsymbol{K}=\det\boldsymbol{A}\cdot\det\boldsymbol{D}.

(146)

Moreover, the block
\boldsymbol{A}
is non-singular.

Proof.

We use the ordering on
\mathcal{I}_{p}
introduced in the beginning of this subsection. It is easy to verify

\displaystyle K_{1,1}=0^{2\cdot 0}\cdot 0^{2\cdot 0}=1,

and for all
\xi_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, either
\xi_{j,1}\not=0
or
\xi_{j,2}\not=0

\displaystyle K_{j,1}=0^{2\xi_{j,1}}\cdot 0^{2\xi_{j,2}}=0,\quad\forall\ 2\leq j\leq N_{p}.

(147)

The first part of the lemma is proved.

It is obvious that
|\mathcal{C}_{1}|=p+1
,
|\mathcal{C}_{2}|=p
and
|\mathcal{C}_{3}|=N_{p}-2p-1=\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
.
\boldsymbol{A}
is generated by all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}
, thus it has size

. Similarly,
\boldsymbol{D}
is generated by all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{3}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{3}

\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\times\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
. To show
\boldsymbol{C}

,

If

, then

for some

and

We are left to show

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*\\
0_{p\times 1}&2\boldsymbol{A}_{1}&\boldsymbol{A}_{2}=\boldsymbol{0}_{p\times p}\\

where both

can be expressed by

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}}\beta^{2\xi},\quad\text{where}\ \xi,\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\setminus\{(0,0)\},

(149)

let

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}}\beta^{2\xi},\quad\text{where}\ \xi,\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{2},

and

(152)

We now focus on showing the sub-matrix

is non-zero. To do this, let

,

, we define matrix
\boldsymbol{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\times\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}
as follows: for all

\displaystyle E_{i,j}=x_{\xi_{j,1}}^{\xi_{i,1}}\cdot x_{\xi_{j,2}}^{\xi_{i,2}}=(x_{\xi_{j,1}},x_{\xi_{j,2}})^{(\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2})},

(154)

where
\xi_{i}=(\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2})
and

are the

-th and

\mathcal{C}_{3}
, respectively. We now prove

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\det\boldsymbol{E}=C\prod_{1\leq j\leq p-1}x_{j}^{2(p-j)}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p-1}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2(p-j)},

(155)

where
C
is a non-zero constant.

Proof.

Denote
\det\boldsymbol{E}\eqqcolon F(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})
. We observe that the multivariate polynomial
F
is homogeneous, i.e., all terms in
F
have the same degree. To see this, apply Leibniz’s formula, we have

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{1\leq i\leq\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}E_{i,\sigma(i)},

(156)

where
S_{m}
is the permutation group of the set
\{1,\dots,m\}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By construction of
\boldsymbol{E}
, every elements in
i
-th row of
\boldsymbol{E}
share the same degree, namely
\xi_{i,1}+\xi_{i,2}
, for any
1\leq i\leq\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
, so
\prod_{1\leq i\leq\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}K_{i,\sigma(i)}
has same degree for all
\sigma\in S_{\frac{p(p-1)}{2}}
and this implies homogeneity of
F
. We can then compute the degree of

by

\displaystyle\deg F=\sum_{q=2}^{p}q(q-1)=\frac{p^{3}-p}{3}.

(157)

Consider
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leq j\leq p-1
, we claim that
x_{j}^{2(p-j)}
divides
F
. By construction of
\boldsymbol{E}
, there are
(p-j)

x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
and
(p-j)
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(\cdot,x_{j})
, so there are
2(p-j)
’s
x_{j}
in total. Hence the algebraic multiplicity of
x_{j}
is at least
2(p-j)
.

For arbitrary pair of integers
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j\leq p-1
, if we substitute
x_{j}
with
x_{i}
, then there would be at least two identical columns in
\boldsymbol{E}
and hence
F
will vanish. Furthermore, there are
(p-j)
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
and
(p-j)
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(\cdot,x_{j})
. As a result, there are
2(p-j)
instances. Therefore,
(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2(p-j)}
divides
F
.

Now, by unique factorization property of multivariate polynomial ring, there exists a multivariate polynomial
C(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})
such that

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})

\displaystyle=C(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})\prod_{1\leq j\leq p-1}x_{j}^{2(p-j)}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p-1}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2(p-j)},

(158)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon C(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-1})H(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1}).

(159)

C(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})
will be a constant polynomial if

\displaystyle\deg H=\deg F.

(160)

We compute

\displaystyle\deg H=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}2(p-j)+\sum_{j=2}^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}2(p-j)=p(p-1)+\frac{1}{3}p(p-1)(p-2)=\frac{p^{3}-p}{3}=\deg F.

(161)

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

4.2
.
∎

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathcal{G}_{1,1},\mathcal{G}_{2,1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{p-1,1},\mathcal{G}_{p-2,2},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{\lceil\frac{p}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}\right\}\eqqcolon\{\mathcal{G}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N_{p}}.

The following lemma reveals the structure of the coefficient matrix

For all

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{H},

(168)

where

is a diagonal matrix with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}_{jj}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nicefrac{{2}}{{|\xi_{j,1}\xi_{j,2}|}},&\text{if}&|\xi_{j,1}|=|\xi_{j,2}|,\ \forall\xi_{j}=(\xi_{j,1},\xi_{j,2})\in\mathcal{G}_{j}\\
\nicefrac{{4}}{{|\xi_{j,1}\xi_{j,2}|}},&\text{if}&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

is given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}_{i,j}=|\xi_{j,1}|^{2\xi_{i,1}}|\xi_{j,2}|^{2\xi_{i,2}}+|\xi_{j,2}|^{2\xi_{i,1}}|\xi_{j,1}|^{2\xi_{i,2}},

. Here,

is

, respectively.

From the definition of coefficient matrix

in

\displaystyle K_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}}\text{sgn}(\xi_{j,1}\xi_{j,2})\xi_{j}^{2\xi_{i}-(1,1)}

The lemma follows immediately.

From

\boldsymbol{T}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times N_{p}}
by

\displaystyle T_{i,j}=x_{\xi_{j,1}}^{\xi_{i,1}}x_{\xi_{j,2}}^{\xi_{i,2}}+x_{\xi_{j,1}}^{\xi_{i,2}}x_{\xi_{j,2}}^{\xi_{i,1}}=(x_{\xi_{j,1}},x_{\xi_{j,2}})^{(\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2})}+(x_{\xi_{j,2}},x_{\xi_{j,1}})^{(\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2})},

(174)

where
\xi_{i}=(\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2})
and
\xi_{j}=(\xi_{j,1},\xi_{j,2})
are the
i
-th and
j
-th element in
\mathcal{I}_{p}
, respectively.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N},\ p\geq 2

\displaystyle\det\boldsymbol{T}=C\prod_{1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}x_{j}^{p+1-j}\prod_{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor<j\leq p-1}x_{j}^{p-j}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{p+1-j}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j,\\
\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor<j\leq p-1\end{subarray}}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{p-j},

(175)

where
C
is a non-zero constant.

Proof.

Let us prove when
p=2k
,
k\geq 1
, the proof for the
p
odd is very similar. From
Section

4.2
we note that
N_{2k}=k^{2}
. Denote
\det\boldsymbol{T}\eqqcolon F(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-1})
. We observe that the multivariate polynomial
F
is homogeneous. To see this, apply Leibniz’s formula, we have

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-1})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k^{2}}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{1\leq j\leq k^{2}}T_{j,\sigma(j)},

(176)

where
S_{n}
is the permutation group of the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By the construction of
\boldsymbol{T}
, all elements in
i
-th row of
\boldsymbol{T}
share the degree
|\xi_{i}|_{1}
, where
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}
for any
1\leq i\leq k^{2}
. Thus,
\prod_{1\leq j\leq k^{2}}\boldsymbol{T}_{j,\sigma(j)}
has same degree for all
\sigma\in S_{k^{2}}
and this implies homogeneity of
T
. We can then compute the degree of
F
by

\displaystyle\deg F

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=2}^{2k}q\lfloor\nicefrac{{q}}{{2}}\rfloor=\frac{4}{3}k^{3}+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+\frac{1}{6}k.

(177)

Consider
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leq j\leq 2k-1
, suppose we substitute
0
into
x_{j}
, then there would be at least one
zero
-column in
\boldsymbol{T}
and hence
F
would be zero. Therefore, each
x_{j}
divides
F
. We further prove that, if
1\leq j\leq k
, then
x_{j}^{2k+1-j}
divides
F
and if
k<j\leq 2k-1
, then
x_{j}^{2k-j}
divides
F
. By construction of
\boldsymbol{T}
, for all
1\leq j\leq k
, there are
j
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
and
2k-2j+1
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(\cdot,x_{j})
, thus there are
2k-j+1
’s
x_{j}
in total. On the other hand, for all
k<j\leq 2k-1
, there are
2k-j
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
and there is no
(\cdot,x_{j})
, so there are
2k-j
’s
x_{j}
in total. Hence the algebraic multiplicity of
x_{j}
is at least
2k+1-j
for all
1\leq j\leq k
and
2k-j
for all
k<j\leq 2k-1
.

For arbitrary pair of integers
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j\leq 2k-1
, if we substituted
x_{j}
with
x_{i}
, then there would be at least two identical columns in
\boldsymbol{T}
and hence
F
would vanish. Furthermore, for all integer pair
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j\leq k
, there are
j
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
and
2k-2j+1
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(\cdot,x_{j})
, and for all integer pair
(i,j)
such that
1\leq i<j,k<j\leq 2k-1
, there are
2k-j
’s
x_{j}
appearing in
(x_{j},\cdot)
. As a result, there are
2k-j+1
’s
x_{j}
for all
2\leq j\leq k
and
2k-j
’s
x_{j}
for all
k<j\leq 2k-1
. Therefore,
(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2k-j+1}
divides
F
if
2\leq j\leq k
and
(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2k-j}
divides
F
if
k<j\leq 2k-1
.

Now, by the unique factorization property of multivariate polynomial ring, there exists a multivariate polynomial
C(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-1})
such that

\displaystyle F(x_{1}\dots,x_{2k-1})

\displaystyle=C(x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-1})\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}x_{j}^{2k-j+1}\prod_{k<j\leq 2k-1}x_{j}^{2k-j}

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2k-j+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j,\\
k<j\leq 2k-1\end{subarray}}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2k-j},

(178)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon C(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2k-1})H(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2k-1}).

C(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2k-1})
will be a constant if we can prove

\displaystyle\deg H=\deg F.

(179)

We compute

\displaystyle\deg H

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(2k-j+1)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k-1}(2k-j)+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(2k-j+1)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(2k-j)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3k^{2}+k}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{k^{2}-k}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{2}{3}k^{3}-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}-\frac{1}{6}k\right)+\left(\frac{2}{3}k^{3}-k^{2}+\frac{1}{3}k\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}k^{3}+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+\frac{1}{6}k=\deg F.

(180)

This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the non-singularity of the coefficient matrix
\boldsymbol{K}
as in
Eq.

30
.

6
Conclusion

We proposed an arbitrarily high-order modified trapezoidal rules for both on-diagonal and off-diagonal weakly singular integrals
I_{i,j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(x)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2+\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!{x}
,
i,j\in\{1,2\}
with some sufficient smooth function
\phi
with compact support. The quadrature rule is a punctured-hole trapezoidal rule with correction terms. We proved the order of accuracy of the modified quadrature given the number of correction layers. We focused on error due to singularity in this paper. The quadrature rule can be combined with any boundary error correction for regular functions
\phi
without compact support to attain high-order convergence. The correction weights can be pre-computed and stored for future use. We tabulated correction weights in more-than-17 digits in scientific form for
\alpha=0.5,1.5
and for both on-diagonal and off-diagonal case. We provided theoretical guarantee that the quadrature rule works with arbitrary
p
, the number associated with correction layers, though we do not recommend to use large
p
since numerical evidence suggested that linear systems
Eq.

21
and
Eq.

31
become increasingly ill-conditioned for large
p
. By using the method suggested in
Remark

Remark
, one can prove the non-singularity left in the work of
[
14
]
. For future works, one can further relax the smoothness criteria for
\phi
in
Theorems

3.4
and
3.11
. Alternatively, the modified trapezoidal rule is of course not confined to singular kernels described here. With proper modification, one can easily adapt this rule to other singular kernels in 2D such as
x_{1}\log{(|x|)}
or
\frac{P(x_{1},x_{2})}{|x|^{\nicefrac{{(2k+1)}}{{2}}}}
where
P(x_{1},x_{2})
is a irreducible polynomial in
x_{1},x_{2}
with degree
k
. Another possible direction is to generalize the quadrature rule to arbitrary
n
dimensions, as some results in this paper are already set up in
n
dimensions.

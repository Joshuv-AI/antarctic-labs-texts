Abstract
We present an error analysis of weak convergence of one-step numerical schemes for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with super-linearly growing coefficients. Following Milstein’s weak error analysis on the one-step approximation of SDEs, we prove a general conclusion on weak convergence of the one-step discretization of the SDEs mentioned above. As applications, we show the weak convergence rates for several numerical schemes of half-order strong convergence, such as tamed and balanced schemes. Numerical examples are presented to verify our theoretical analysis. AMS subject classification: 60H35, 60H15, 65C30. Keywords: SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients, weak convergence theorem, modified Euler method, backward Euler method, weak convergence rate

2.1
Notations

Throughout this paper, the following notation
is frequently used.
Let
|\cdot|
and

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle

be the Euclidean norm and the inner product of vectors
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
By
A^{T}
we denote the transpose of vector or
matrix
A
.
Given a matrix
A
, we use

\|A\|:=\sqrt{\text{trace}(A^{T}A)}

to denote the trace norm of
A
.
On a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, we use
\mathbb{E}
to mean expectation and

L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
to denote the family of

\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
-valued variables with
the norm defined by

\|\xi\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})}=(\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{r}])^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty
.
Next, we give some notation for partial derivatives of functions, frequently used throughout this paper.
A vector
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)

is called a multiindex of order

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d},

where each component
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
is a nonnegative integer.
For a multiindex
\alpha
, we define the partial derivatives of
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
as

D^{\alpha}v(x):=\tfrac{\partial^{|\alpha|}v(x)}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}v(x).

(2.1)

For a nonnegative integer
k
, we use

D^{k}v(x):=\left\{D^{\alpha}v(x):|\alpha|=k\right\}

to denote the set of all partial derivatives of order
k
. Moreover, we define

\left|D^{k}v(x)\right|=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left|D^{\alpha}v(x)\right|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(2.2)

We also use

Dv:=(\tfrac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}},\tfrac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}},\cdots\tfrac{\partial v}{\partial x_{d}})
(gradient vector) and
D^{2}v:=\big(\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\big)_{d\times d}
(Hessian matrix).

Next, we introduce a class of functions with superlinear growth.

Definition 2.1

(polynomial growth)
.

A function
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is said to belong to the class
\mathbb{H}
, written as

\psi\in\mathbb{H}
,
if there exist constants
L>0,l>0
such that the following inequality holds

|\psi(x)|\leq L(1+|x|^{l}).

(2.3)

For
k\in\mathbb{N}

we also use
\mathbb{H}^{k}
to denote a subset of
\mathbb{H}
, consisting of
k
-times continuously
differentiable functions which, together with its partial derivatives
up to and including order
k
,
belong to
\mathbb{H}
.

The above definition is quoted from
[
39
, Definition 1.1 in Chapter 2]
.
For random functions, we introduce a concept of mean-square
differentiability as follows.

Definition 2.2

(mean-square differentiable)
.

Let

\Psi\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and

\phi_{i}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be random functions satisfying

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{\tau}\big[\Psi(x+\tau e_{i})-\Psi(x)\big]-\phi_{i}(x)\Big|^{2}\Big]=0,\quad\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,d\},

(2.4)

where
e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with the
i
-th element being

Then
\Psi
is called to be mean-square differentiable, with

\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{d})

being the derivative (in the mean-square differentiable sense)
of
\Psi
and we also write
\partial_{(i)}\Psi=\phi_{i}
.

The above two definitions can be generalized to vector-valued functions in a component-wise manner.

2.2
Stochastic differential equations

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

be a complete probability space with a
filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
(
T>0
),
satisfying the usual conditions.
Let

d,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and

W:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

stands for
standard Brownian motions
with respect to

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
.
We consider the following autonomous SDEs in the Itô form of

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{d}X(t)&=f(X(t))\,\text{d}t+g(X(t))\,\text{d}W(t),\quad t\in(0,T],\\
X(0)&=X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

where
f=(f^{1},f^{2},\cdots,f^{d})^{T}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the drift coefficient and
f^{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and

g=(g^{i,j})_{d\times m}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}

is the diffusion coefficient. The function
g
is also written as

g=(g^{1},g^{2},\cdots,g^{m})=(g_{1}^{T},g_{2}^{T},\cdots,g_{d}^{T})^{T}
, where

g^{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
d\times 1
vector function
and

g_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a
1\times{m}
vector function.
The initial data

X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is assumed to be deterministic for simplicity.

Assumption 2.3

(Well-posedness of a strong solution)
.

For any
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the SDE (
2.5
) admits
a unique pathwise solution

X:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, given by

X(t)=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(X(s))\,\text{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(X(s))\,\text{d}W(s),\quad a.s..

(2.6)

Let
X(t,x;s)
denotes the solution to (
2.5
) at
s
, starting from the initial value
x
at
t
, namely,

X(t,x;s)=x+\int_{t}^{s}f(X(t,x;r))\,\text{d}r+\int_{t}^{s}g(X(t,x;r))\,\text{d}W(r),\quad 0\leq t\leq s\leq T.

(2.7)

2.3
The general weak convergence theorem

To approximate solutions to
(
2.5
), we construct a uniform mesh on

[0,T]
with
h=\frac{T}{N}
being the
step size,
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Here we use
X_{t,x}(s)
or
X(t,x;s)
for
0\leq s\leq T
to denote the solution to (
2.5
) at the time instant
s
with the initial condition

X_{t,x}(t)=X(t,x;t)=x
.
Also,
X(t):=X(0,X_{0};t)
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
h>0
,

0<t+h\leq T
,
we introduce the one-step approximation

Y(t,x;t+h)
in the form of

Y(t,x;t+h)=x+\Phi(t,x,h;\xi_{t}),

(2.8)

where
\xi_{t}
is a random variable (in general, a vector) defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
,
having moments of a sufficiently high order,
and
\Phi
is a function from

[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Similarly, by
Y(t,x;t+h)
or

Y_{t,x}(t+h)
we denote an approximation of
the solution at
t+h

with initial value

Y(t,x;t)=Y_{t,x}(t)=x
.
Then we use

Y_{n+1}=Y(t_{n},Y_{n};t_{n+1})

to recurrently construct numerical approximations

\{Y_{n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}

on the uniform mesh grid

\{t_{n}=nh,n=0,1,\cdots,N\}
,
given by

Y_{0}=X_{0},\quad Y_{n+1}=Y_{n}+\Phi(t_{n},Y_{n},h;\xi_{n}),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

(2.9)

where the
\xi_{n}
for
n\geq 0
is independent of

Y_{{}_{0}},Y_{{}_{1}},\ldots,Y_{{}_{n}}
,

\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}
.
Alternatively, one can write

Y_{n+1}=Y(t_{n},Y_{n};t_{n+1})=Y(t_{0},Y_{0};t_{n+1}),\quad Y_{0}=X_{0},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N-1.

(2.10)

We aim to analyze the weak error of the one-step approximation in terms of

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(X(t_{0},X_{0};T)\big)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(Y(t_{0},Y_{0};t_{N})\big)\big]\big|,

(2.11)

where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called the test function.
To carry out the weak error analysis,
we introduce the function
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

defined by

u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(X(t,x;T)\big)\big],

(2.12)

which is the unique classical solution of the associated Kolmogorov partial differential equation (PDE) under certain regularity assumptions on
f,g,\varphi
(see
[
14
, Theorem 1.6.2]
for more details).
Boundedness and differentiability properties of such
solutions
u
to the Kolmogorov PDE play an essential role
in the analysis of weak errors of numerical schemes.
In
[
38
]
(see also
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
),
Milstein establishes a well-known weak convergence theorem
for general one-step approximation schemes of SDEs with sufficiently smooth, globally Lipschitz continuous coefficients and sufficiently smooth test functions with polynomial growth.
In
[
18
]
, the author discusses the classes of test functions
\varphi
that can be allowed and also addresses how to interpret
\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(X(t,x;T)\big)\big]
for such cases and has to assume strong conditions on
f
and
g
.
Another approach to prove weak convergence and, if necessary, reduce assumptions on
\varphi
uses Malliavin calculus, see, e.g.
[
28
]
. A further viewpoint on the topic is provided by the concept of cubature on Wiener space, see [6], in particular for conditions on
\varphi
. Overall, the relations among the conditions on
\varphi
, the definition of weak convergence, and the existence and properties of

are standard practices in the literature.

[

,
39
,
40

we aim to reformulate a general weak convergence theorem for one-step approximations of SDEs

, we assume on the one-step approximation error as follows.

(One-step approximation error)
.

For any

,

Let Assumptions

Then the numerical solution produced by (

Before closing this section,

Essentially all articles studying weak convergence of various numerical methods for SDEs

A practical corollary on weak convergence of one-step approximation schemes

3.1
On Assumption

In this subsection, we reveal how Assumption

\varphi
obey certain smoothness and

]

Assumption 3.1
.

There exist a measurable function

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{s}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{j}}\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{s}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{j}}\Big]\bigg|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}K(x)}h^{p+1},\,\,\,s=1,\ldots,2p+1,

3.2
Explicit conditions on coefficients of SDEs to fulfill Assumption
3.2

In Assumptions
3.2
, it is assumed that the underlying SDE (

is well-posed with bounded moments and that the solution is mean-square differentiable with respect to the initial data.
It is a classical result that the solution

of the SDE (

2p+2
times differentiable with respect to

.

To fulfill all conditions of Assumptions
3.2
in a non-globally Lipschitz setting, we
recall the framework of Cerrai
[
14
]
(see Hypotheses 1.1, 1.2 therein), where the drift and diffusion coefficients are assumed to be sufficiently smooth with polynomial growth and satisfy certain monotonicity conditions.

Assumption 3.6
.

(A1)

The drift coefficient function

f\in C^{2p+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

and there exists
r\geq 0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|D^{\alpha}f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2r+1-j}}<\infty,\quad|\alpha|=j,\,\,\,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,2p+2\};

(3.9)

(A2)

The diffusion coefficient function
g\in C^{2p+2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})

and there exists
\rho\leq r
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\|D^{\alpha}g(x)\|}{1+|x|^{\rho-j}}<\infty,\quad|\alpha|=j,\,\,\,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,2p+2\};

(3.10)

(A3)

For all
\lambda_{0}>0
, there exists

C_{\lambda_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}

such that

\langle Df(x)y,y\rangle+\lambda_{0}\|Dg(x)y\|^{2}\leq C_{\lambda_{0}}|y|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};

(3.11)

(A4)

There exist
a_{1}>0
and
\gamma,c_{1}\geq 0
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds

\langle f(x+y)-f(x),y\rangle\leq-a_{1}|y|^{2r+2}+c_{1}(|x|^{\gamma}+1).

(3.12)

Examples of nonlinearity satisfying Assumption
3.6
can be found in

[
14
, Remark 1.1.1]
.
In the proofs which follow we will need some implications of Assumption
3.6
.
The Assumption (
A1
) immediately implies

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2r}+|y|^{2r})|x-y|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.13)

which gives the polynomial growth

|f(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2r+1}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.14)

Similarly, it follows from Assumption (
A2
) that

\|g(x)-g(y)\|\leq C(1+|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1})|x-y|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.15)

and thus

\|g(x)\|\leq C(1+|x|^{\rho}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.16)

In addition, Assumption
3.6
further ensures Khasminskii-type and one-sided Lipschitz property on

and

, which are employed in strong approximation schemes for SDEs such as tamed and semi-implicit schemes (see, e.g.,
[
19
,
23
,
51
]
and references therein).
More precisely, Assumption (
A3
)
implies one-sided Lipschitz property:

\langle x-y,f(x)-f(y)\rangle+\lambda_{0}\|g(x)-g(y)\|^{2}\leq C_{\lambda_{0}}|x-y|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.17)

Under Assumption
3.6
, Theorem 1.3.6 of
[

asserts that the SDE (

6
Weak convergence rate of the backward Euler method

As another application of the corollary on weak convergence,
we analyze the weak convergence rate of the well-known backward Euler method (BEM) applied to SDEs (
2.5
):

Y_{n+1}=Y_{n}+f(Y_{n+1})h+g(Y_{n})\Delta W_{n},\quad Y_{0}=X_{0},

(6.1)

where

\Delta W_{n}:=W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n})
,

n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,N-1\}.

Then the one-step approximation of
(
6.1
) is

Y(t,x;t+h)=x+\int_{t}^{t+h}f(Y(t,x;t+h))\,\text{d}s+\int_{t}^{t+h}g(x)\,\text{d}W(s).

(6.2)

Denote

\delta_{X,x}:=X(t,x;t+h)-x,\,\delta_{Y,x}:=Y(t,x;t+h)-x.

Then by
(
4.1
),

\begin{split}Y(t,x;t+h)=Y_{E}(t,x;t+h)+R_{f},\quad R_{f}:=\int_{t}^{t+h}f(Y(t,x;t+h))-f(x)\,\text{d}s.\end{split}

(6.3)

Before proceeding further, we shall establish the well-posedness of the implicit scheme (
6.1
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let the condition (A3) in
Assumption
3.6
be satisfied and let
hC_{\lambda_{0}}<1
. Then the implicit scheme (
6.1
)
has a unique adapted solution
\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}

with probability one.

The proof can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

As already established in

[
21
, Corollary 2.27]
,

q
-th moments of the BEM are uniformly bounded under Assumption (
A3
).

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose Assumption (A3) holds. Then there exists

C>0,

independent of h such that for all

N\in\mathbb{N}

and

n=0,1,\ldots,N,

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{n}|^{q}]\leq C\big(1+|X_{0}|^{q}\big),\,\,\,\forall\,q\geq 0,

(6.4)

where

\{Y_{n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}

is given in
(
6.1
).

Equipped with the moment bound, we continue to analyze the weak convergence rate of the BEM (
6.1
)
by means of Corollary
3.8
.

The local weak errors for the BEM is similar to that for the modified Euler schemes. At first, we prove the following lemma on one-step strong error bounds of the BEM.

Lemma 6.3
.

Under the same condition of Proposition
6.2
, letting Assumptions (A1) and (A2) hold with
p=1,
then for any
q\geq 1
the following inequalities hold
for the backward Euler scheme
(
6.1
):

\displaystyle\|\delta_{Y,x}\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{2r+1})h^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|R_{f}\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{4r+1})h^{\frac{3}{2}},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\|\delta_{X,x}-\delta_{Y,x}\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{4r+1})h,

(6.7)

where
R_{f}
is defined in (
6.3
).

Proof of Lemma

6.3
.

Repeating the same lines
in the proof of
(
C.2
)
and
(
6.4
)
results in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|(\delta_{Y,x})|^{2q}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{t}^{t+h}f\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t}^{t+h}g(x)\,\text{d}W(s)\Big|^{2q}\Big]

(6.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|f\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s+Ch^{q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|g(x)\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{2q(2r+1)})h^{q},

which confirms
(
6.5
).
To prove (
6.6
),
we use the Hölder inequality,
(
3.21
)
and
(
6.5
)
to derive that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\big|R_{f}\big|^{2q}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|f(Y(t,x;t+h))-f(x)\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s

(6.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{2q(4r+1)})h^{3q}.

Also, one can follow the same lines in the proof
of (
4.3
)
to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\delta_{X,x}-\delta_{Y,x}|^{2q}\big]

(6.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{t}^{t+h}f\big(X(s)\big)-f\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)\,\text{d}s+\int_{t}^{t+h}g\big(X(s)\big)-g(x)\,\text{d}W(s)\Big|^{2q}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|f(X(s))-f(x)\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s+Ch^{2q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|f(x)-f(Y(t,x;t+h))\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{q-1}\int_{t}^{t+h}\big\|g(X(s))-g(x)\big\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})}^{2q}\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{2q(4r+1)})h^{2q},

as required. This thus finishes
the proof of the lemma.
∎

Based on Taylor’s expansion and Lemma
6.3
, we are able to obtain the following local errors of the BEM (
6.1
).

Lemma 6.4
.

Under the same conditions of Lemma
6.3
, one can obtain the local errors of the BEM (
6.2
)

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{s}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{j}}-\prod_{j=1}^{s}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{j}}\Big]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{8r+3})h^{2},\,\,s=1,2,3,

(6.11)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{4}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{j}}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{8r+4})h^{2}.

(6.12)

Proof of Lemma
6.4
.

For
s=1,
we first note that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}}\big]\big|=\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{1}}-R_{f}^{i_{1}}\big]

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{1}}\big]\big|+\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{t+h}f^{i_{1}}\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)-f^{i_{1}}(x)\,\text{d}s\bigg]\bigg|.

The estimate for the first term has been done in
(
C.3
),
namely,

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{1}}\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{4r+1})h^{2}.

(6.14)

Next we bound the second term.
Employing Taylor’s expansion in the form of

\displaystyle f^{i_{1}}\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{i_{1}}(x)+\sum^{d}_{j_{1}=1}\frac{\partial f^{i_{1}}(x)}{\partial x_{j_{1}}}\big(Y^{j_{1}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{1}}\big)

(6.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{d}_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{2}f^{i_{1}}\big(x+\theta\big(Y(t,x;t+h)-x\big)\big)}{\partial x_{j_{1}}\partial x_{j_{2}}}(1-\theta)\,\text{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\big(Y^{j_{1}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{1}}\big)\big(Y^{j_{2}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{2}}\big),

and using the assumption on the function
f
in (
3.9
) and the Hölder inequality, one can infer

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}f^{i_{1}}\big(Y(t,x;t+h)\big)-f^{i_{1}}(x)\,\text{d}s\Big]\Big|

(6.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{d}_{j_{1}=1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}\frac{\partial f^{i_{1}}(x)}{\partial x_{j_{1}}}\big(Y^{j_{1}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{1}}\big)\,\text{d}s\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{d}_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{2}f^{i_{1}}(x+\theta(Y(t,x;t+h)-x))}{\partial x_{j_{1}}\partial x_{j_{2}}}(1-\theta)\,\text{d}\theta\,\text{d}s

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\big(Y^{j_{1}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{1}}\big)\big(Y^{j_{2}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{2}}\big)\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{2r})\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+h}\big(Y^{j_{1}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{j_{1}}\big)\,\text{d}s\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+C(1+|x|^{2r-1})h\,\|(\delta_{Y,x})^{j_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\|(\delta_{Y,x})^{j_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{6r+1})h^{2}.

This together with (
6.14
)
implies

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}}\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{6r+1})h^{2}.

(6.17)

For
s=2
, we first note that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|

(6.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}})\big]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}})(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{2}}-R_{f}^{i_{2}})\big]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{1}}-R_{f}^{i_{1}})(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{2}})\big]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{1}})(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}R_{f}^{i_{2}}\big]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[R_{f}^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|.

By (
C.22
),
it follows that

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y_{E},x})^{i_{2}})\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{6r+2})h^{2}.

(6.19)

The estimate for the second is obtained in the same way.
Utilizing the Hölder inequality,
(
4.5
)
and
(
6.6
)
yields

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}R_{f}^{i_{2}}\big]\big|\leq\|(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\,\|R^{i_{2}}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\leq C(1+|x|^{6r+2})h^{2}.

(6.20)

Following a similar way as (
6.20
),
we acquire

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[R_{f}^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{6r+2})h^{2}.

(6.21)

Collecting

6.19
),
(
6.20
),
and
(
6.21
)
enables us to arrive at

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{6r+2})h^{2}.

(6.22)

Finally, for
s=3,

we make a decomposition as follows:

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{3}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{3}}\big]\big|

(6.23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{3}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{3}})\big]\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}\big[(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{1}}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{3}}((\delta_{X,x})^{i_{2}}-(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{2}})\big]\big|

and

Putting (

Proof
.

times differentiable with respect to

(B.3)

By the Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,X(t,x;t+h))\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,Y(t,x;t+h))\big]

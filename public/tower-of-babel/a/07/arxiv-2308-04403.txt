Properties of sequence of linear functionals on B ​ V BV with applications
L-E. Persson, V. Tsagareishvili and G. Tutberidze

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to investigating the sequence of some linear functionals in the space B ​ V BV of finite variation functions. We prove that under certain conditions this sequence is bounded. We also prove that this result is sharp. In particular, the obtained results can be used to study convergence of some general Fourier series. Moreover, the obtained conditions seem to be new and useful also for classical orthonormal systems.

2.
PRELIMINARIES

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}
be an orthonormal system (ONS) on
\left[0,1\right].

We denote by
BV
the class of all functions of bounded variation on
(0,1)
and write
V\left(f\right)
for the total variation of a function
f
on
\left[0,1\right]
.

By
A
we denote the Banach space of absolutely convergent functions with the norm
{{\left\|f\right\|}_{A}}
defined by

{{\left\|f\right\|}_{A}}\,:=\,{{\left\|f\right\|}_{C}}\,+\,\int\limits_{0}^{1}{\left|\frac{df}{dx}\right|dx.}

(1)

We will investigate the linear functionals
\{U_{n}(f)\}
defined by

{U}_{n}(f):=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx,

(2)

where
f\in L_{2},

a=\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}
is an arbitrary sequence of numbers and

{{Q}_{n}}(d,a,x):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{{d}_{k}}{{a}_{k}}{\log k}{{\varphi}_{k}}(x).}

(3)

Here
\{d_{n}\}
denote a sequence of real number such that

\displaystyle d_{n}=O\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\log^{2}(n+1)}\right).

(4)

For this investigation of the functionals
\{{U}_{n}(f)\}
we need the following important Lemma (see
[
9
]
).

Lemma 1
.

If
f\in L_{2}\left(0,1\right)
takes only finite values on
\left[0,1\right]
and
h\in L_{2}\left(0,1\right)
is an arbitrary function, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)h\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)-f\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)\right)\int_{0}^{i/n}h\left(x\right)dx

(5)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left(f\left(x\right)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right)h\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f\left(1\right)\int_{0}^{1}h\left(x\right)dx.

We denote

\displaystyle B_{n}\left(d,a\right)=\max_{1\leq i<n}\left|\int_{0}^{i/n}Q_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)dx\right|.

(6)

We say that the sequence of functionals
\{U_{n}(f)\}
is bounded on the space
V,
if, for any
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2},

\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathop{\limsup}}\left|{{U}_{n}}(f)\right|<+\infty.

We also need the following result of S.Banach (see e.g.
[
2
]
):

Theorem A
.

Let
f\in L_{2}
be an arbytrary (non-zero) function. Then there exists an ONS
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}
such that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{k}(f)\varphi_{k}(x)\right|=+\infty\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ \ [0,1],

where
C_{k}(f)
are the Fourier coefficients of the function
f\in L_{2}
with respect to the system
\{{\varphi}_{k}\}
and defined as follows

{{C}_{k}}(f):=\int\limits_{0}^{1}{f(x){{\varphi}_{k}}(x)dx.}

(7)

Moreover, we recall the following well-known result of Menshov and Rademacher (see e.g.
[
11
]
Ch.9, p.332).

Theorem B
.

If
\{\varphi_{n}\}
is an ONS on
\left[0,1\right]
and a number sequence
\{c_{n}\}
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{2}\log_{2}^{2}n<+\infty,

then the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\varphi_{n}\left(x\right)

converges a.e. on
\left[0,1\right]
.

3.
The Main Results

Our first main result reads:

Theorem 1
.

If, for any
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2},

\displaystyle\qquad B_{n}\left(d,a\right)=O\left(1\right),

(8)

then the sequence of functionals
\{U_{n}(f)\}
is bounded on the space
BV
for every
f\in BV.

Proof.

By using Lemma
1
, when
h\left(x\right)=Q_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(x)Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)-f\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)\right)\int_{0}^{i/n}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx

(9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left(f\left(x\right)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right)Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx

\displaystyle+f\left(1\right)\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx:=A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}.

Let
f\in BV
, then we get (see (
6
))

\displaystyle\left|A_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i<n}\left|\int_{0}^{i/n}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx\right|\ \cdot\ \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)-f\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq V(f)B_{n}(d,a).

Hence, from condition (
8
) it follows that

\left|A_{1}\right|=O(1)V(f).

By applying Hölder’s inequality and (
4
) , we get (since
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}
)

\displaystyle\left|A_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}\right]}\left|f(x)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right|\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left|Q_{n}(d,a,x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq V(f)\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left|Q_{n}(d,a,x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq V(f)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}^{2}(d,a,x)dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{V(f)}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log k\varphi_{k}(x)\right)^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{V(f)}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}a_{k}^{2}\log^{2}k\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=V(f)\cdot\frac{\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq n}|d_{k}|}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\log n\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=O(1)V(f).

By using (
6
) and Cauchy’s inequality, for any
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}
we find that

\displaystyle\left|A_{3}\right|

\displaystyle=O(1)\left|\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\left(\left|\int_{0}^{1-1/n}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{1-1/n}^{1}Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\left(B_{n}(d,a)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}^{2}(d,a,x)dx\right)^{1/2}\right)=O(1).

Taking into consideration in (
9
) the above estimates of
|A_{1}|,

|A_{2}|
and
|A_{3}|
we have that

\left|\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log k\varphi_{k}(x)\right|=O(1).

It follows that

\left|U_{n}(f)\right|\leq M(f),

(10)

where
M(f)
is a constant which does not depend on
n
and the proof is complete.

∎

Next we state a result which, in particular, show that the statement in Theorem
1
is, in a sense, sharp.

Theorem 2
.

If for some
\{b_{n}\}\in l_{2}

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}B_{n}(d,b)=+\infty,

then there exists a function
g\in A
, such that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|U_{n}(g)\right|=+\infty.

Proof.

First we suppose that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|=+\infty.

Then, if
g(x)=1,\text{ }x\in[0,1],
we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1}g(x)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|=+\infty.

Obviously
g\in A.
Theorem
2
holds in this case.

Next we suppose that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|=O(1).

(11)

Let
1\leq i_{n}<n
be an integer, such that

B_{n}\left(d,b\right)=\max_{1\leq i<n}\left|\int_{0}^{i/n}Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{i_{n}/n}Q_{n}\left(d,c,x\right)dx\right|.

Suppose that for some sequence
b=\{b_{k}\}\in l_{2}

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}B_{n}(d,b)=+\infty.

(12)

Consider the following sequence of test functions:

f_{n}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0,&\text{when}&x\in\left[0,\frac{i_{n}}{n}\right]\\
1,&\text{when}&x\in\left[\frac{i_{n}+1}{n},1\right]\\
\text{continuous and linear, }&\text{when}&x\in\left[\frac{i_{n}}{n},\frac{i_{n}+1}{n}\right].\end{array}\right.

Then (see (
1
))

\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{A}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|f_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(x\right)\right|dx+\left\|f_{n}\left(x\right)\right\|_{C}\leq 2.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(f_{n}\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)-f_{n}\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)\right)\int_{0}^{i/n}Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|

(13)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{{i_{n}}/n}Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|=B_{n}\left(d,b\right).

Then, if
x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}\right]
we find that

\left|f_{n}\left(x\right)-f_{n}\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right|\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\leq 1,&\text{if}&i=i_{n}+1,\\
0,&\text{if}&i\neq i_{n}+1,\\
\end{array}\right.

and it implies that (since
\{b_{n}\}\in l_{2}
)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left(f\left(x\right)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{n},\frac{i}{n}\right]}\left|f\left(x\right)-f\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right|\int_{\left(i-1\right)/n}^{i/n}\left|Q_{n}(d,b,x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq V(f)\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\mathop{\max}}\,\int\limits_{{(i-1)}/{n}}^{{i}/{n}}{\left|Q_{n}(d,b,x)\right|dx}

\displaystyle=O(1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}Q_{n}^{2}(d,b,x)dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=O(1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}d_{k}\log n{{\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{b_{k}^{2}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}=O(1).

Consequently, by using (
5
) when
f\left(x\right)=f_{n}\left(x\right)
and
Q_{n}\left(d,a,x\right)=Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right),
and combining (
11
), (
13
), (
14
), we get that

\left|\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}\left(x\right)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)\right|dx\geq B_{n}(d,b)-O\left(1\right)-O(1).

From here and from (
12
) we have, that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}\left(x\right)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|}=+\infty.

Finally, we note that since

U_{n}\left(f\right)=\int_{0}^{1}f\left(x\right)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx

is a sequence of linear bounded functionals on
A
, then by the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, there exists a function
g\in A
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|U_{n}(g)|=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\int_{0}^{1}g\left(x\right)Q_{n}\left(d,b,x\right)dx\right|}=+\infty.

(15)

The proof is complete.

4.
Applications concerning convergence of general Fourier series

Our first application reads:

Theorem 3
.

If condition (
8
) of Theorem
1
holds then, for any function
f\in BV,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}^{2}C_{k}^{2}(f)\log^{2}k<+\infty.

Proof.

By using condition (
8
) of Theorem
1
, and using equation (
10
) and (
7
) we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}C_{k}(f)\log k

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}\log k\varphi_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)Q_{n}(d,a,x)dx.

Hence,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}C_{k}(f)\log k=U_{n}(f).

Since

\left|U_{n}(f)\right|=O(1)

it follows that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log kC_{k}(f)=O(1).

Now, if we suppose that for any
\{a_{k}\}\in l_{2},

\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathop{\limsup}}\,\,\left|{{U}_{n}}(f)\right|<+\infty,

then the following series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}a_{k}\log kC_{k}(f)

(16)

is convergent.

This means that
\{d_{k}C_{k}(f)\log k\}\in l_{2},
or

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}^{2}C_{k}^{2}(f)\log^{2}k<+\infty.

The proof is complete.

If condition (

) of Theorem

f\in BV,
then the following series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}C_{k}(f)\varphi_{k}(x)

is convergent a.e. on
[0,1].

.

If condition (
8
) of Theorem
Theorem B
is fulfilled and
d_{k}=1,

k=1,2,\dots,
then, for any
f\in BV
the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{k}(f)\varphi_{k}(x)

is convergent a.e. on
[0,1].

Next, we state the following result showing that Theorem
3
is, in a sense, sharp.

Theorem 4
.

For any function
g\in A

(g\neq 0)
there exists an ONS
\{\varphi_{n}\}
such that for some
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}
and
d_{k}=1,

k=1,2,\dots

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{k}^{2}(f)\log^{2}k=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|U_{n}(g)\right|=+\infty.

(17)

Proof.

Let
g
be an arbitrary function. According to the Banach Theorem there exists an ONS
\{\varphi_{n}\}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{k}(g)\varphi_{k}(x)\right|=+\infty\ \ \text{a.e. on}\ [0,1].

(18)

Consequently, by using (
18
) and Theorem A, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{k}^{2}(g)\log^{2}k=+\infty.

(19)

Indeed, suppose the contrary to (
17
) namely that for arbitrary
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|U_{n}(g)\right|<+\infty.

Then as it follows from (
16
) when
d_{k}=1,

k=1,2,\dots
for any
\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2}
that the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\log kC_{k}(g)

is convergent. Thus,
\{C_{k}(g)\log k\}\in l_{2}
or

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{k}^{2}(g)\log^{2}k<+\infty,

which contradicts (
19
).

This contradiction shows that (
17
) holds so the proof is complete.

Finally, we state the following efficiency result:

Theorem 5
.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}
be an ONS such that uniformly with respect to
x\in[0,1]
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\varphi_{n}(x)dx=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

(20)

Then for any
a=\{a_{n}\}\in l_{2},

\displaystyle B_{n}(d,a)\leq\max_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{0}^{x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log k\varphi_{k}(u)du\right|=O(1).

(21)

Proof.

According to (
20
) and by the Cauchy inequality we get (see (
4
))

\displaystyle B_{n}(d,a)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{0}^{x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log k\varphi_{k}(u)du\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}a_{k}\log k\int_{0}^{x}\varphi_{k}(u)du\right|=O(1)\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}d_{k}\log k\frac{1}{k}\right|

\displaystyle=O(1)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}^{2}\log^{2}k\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=O(1)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{\log^{4}(k+1)}\frac{\log^{2}k}{k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}=O(1).

Hence, (
21
) is proved so the proof is complete.

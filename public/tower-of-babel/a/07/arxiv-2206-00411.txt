Deformations of modified r r -matrices and cohomologies of related algebraic structures
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. Modified r r -matrices are solutions of the modified classical Yang-Baxter equation, introduced by Semenov-Tian-Shansky, and play important roles in mathematical physics. In this paper, first we introduce a cohomology theory for modified r r -matrices. Then we study three kinds of deformations of modified r r -matrices using the established cohomology theory, including algebraic deformations, geometric deformations and linear deformations. We give the differential graded Lie algebra that governs algebraic deformations of modified r r -matrices. For geometric deformations, we prove the rigidity theorem and study when is a neighborhood of a modified r r -matrix smooth in the space of all modified r r -matrix structures. In the study of trivial linear deformations, we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis element for a modified r r -matrix. Finally, applications are given to study deformations of complement of the diagonal Lie algebra and compatible Poisson structures.

Introduction

In the seminal work
[

25
,

the corresponding Lie algebras and Lie groups. This factorization problem was considered by Reshetikhin and Semenov-Tian-Shansky in the framework of the enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra with a modified

-matrix to study quantum integrable systems
[

. Any modified

-matrix induces a post-Lie algebra
[
1

, and a factorization theorem for group-like elements of the completion of the Lie enveloping algebra
of a post-Lie algebra was established by Ebrahimi-Fard, Mencattini and Munthe-Kaas in
[
9
,
10

. Recently, the global factorization theorem for a Rota-Baxter Lie group was given in
[
15

. Moreover, modified
r
-matrices are also useful for the construction of flat metrics and Frobenius manifolds
[
27
]
, and compatible Poisson structures
[
18
]
. Note that in the associative algebra context, such objects are called modified Rota-Baxter algebras by Zhang, Gao and Guo
[
30
,
31
]
.

A classical approach to study a mathematical structure is to associate to it invariants. Among these, cohomology theories occupy a central position as they enable for example to control deformations or extension problems.
Note that the cohomology theory for a skew-symmetric classical
r
-matrix was studied in
[
29
]
under the general framework of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators
[
17
]
). The first purpose of this paper is to study the cohomology theory for a modified
r
-matrix. In
[
25
]
, Semenov-Tian-Shansky showed that a modified

-matrix

on a Lie algebra

induces a new Lie algebra

in which the Lie bracket

is given by

In
[
2
]
, Bordemann showed that the induced Lie algebra

represents on
\mathfrak{g}
. We use the corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology
[
4
]

with coefficients in

r
-matrix

. It is well known that there is a one-to-one correspondence between modified

-matrix

The concept of a formal deformation of an algebraic structure began with the seminal
work of Gerstenhaber
[

,
14
]

algebras. Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this study to Lie algebras

[

[

,

]

It is also meaningful to deform

[
3
,
11
,
12
]

{\mathcal{O}}

[

,
6
]
. The second purpose of the paper is to study deformation theories of modified

-matrices. We study three kinds of deformations of a modified
r
-matrix
R
:

•

(algebraic deformations) first we consider algebraic deformation
R+R^{\prime}
for certain linear map

, and show that this kind of deformations are governed by a differential graded Lie algebra. This fulfill the general slogan for the deformation theory proposed by Deligne, Drinfeld and Kontsevich;

•

using the approach developed by Crainic, Schatz and Struchiner in
[
5

of the orbit

will imply certain rigidity theorem, and the condition

-matrices on the Lie algebra

(linear deformation) next we study linear deformation

Note that certain particular deformation of classical

2.
Cohomologies of modified

In this section, we establish the cohomology theory of a modified

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
25
]
)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is called a
modified

-matrix
if it is a solution of the following
modified classical Yang-Baxter equation

(1)

[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
R
and
R^{\prime}
be modified
r
-matrices on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. A
homomorphism
from
R
to
R^{\prime}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that

\varphi\circ R=R^{\prime}\circ\varphi.

Remark 2.3
.

The notion of a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
on an associative algebra was introduced in
[
8
]
. More precisely, it is a linear map
P:A\,\rightarrow\,A
on an associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
satisfying

P(u)\cdot_{A}P(v)=P(P(u)\cdot_{A}v+u\cdot_{A}P(v))-u\cdot_{A}v,\quad\forall u,v\in A.

It is straightforward to see that if a linear map
P:A\,\rightarrow\,A
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
on an associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
, then
P
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A})
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
is the commutator Lie bracket.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
R:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

be a modified

-matrix on a Lie algebra

Proof.

For all

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\rho(y)]z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)\rho(y)z-\rho(y)\rho(x)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)([R(y),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}))-\rho(y)([R(x),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[R(x),[R(y),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[R(x),R([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([x,[R(y),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+R([x,R([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle-[R(y),[R(x),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(y),R([x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})]_{\mathfrak{g}}+R([y,[R(x),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})-R([y,R([x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([R(x),[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})-R([x,[R(y),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

R:\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})

R(x+u)=x-u,\quad\forall x\in B(\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})),u\in A.

By Remark
2.4
, we obtain that
R
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
a=x+u\in\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R})
where

and

, such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}a=0
, that is

\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}a(y)=[R(y),a]-R([y,a])=0,\quad\forall y\in\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{R}).

•

For any

.

•

For any

.

Thus
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}a=0
if and only if

H^{1}(R)\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Example 2.11
.

Consider the Lie algebra

\displaystyle~0

3.
Algebraic deformations of modified
r
-matrices

In this section, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra that governs algebraic deformations of a modified
r
-matrix.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. We consider the graded vector space
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Define a skew-symmetric bracket operation

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{p}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\times\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{p+q}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

by

(7)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket f,g\right\rrbracket(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{p+q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S(q,1,p-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}f([g(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(q)}),x_{\sigma(q+1)}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{\sigma(q+2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q)})

\displaystyle-(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in S(p,1,q-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}g([f(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p)}),x_{\sigma(p+1)}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{\sigma(p+2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q)})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in S(p,q)}(-1)^{\sigma}[f(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p)}),g(x_{\sigma(p+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q)})]_{\mathfrak{g}},

for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{p}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.

Then we have the following theorem characterizing modified
r
-matrices.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. Then
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie algebra and its Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely Rota-Baxter operators of weight
0
.

Moreover, a linear map
R\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
R
satisfies the equation

(8)

\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket=2\pi.

where denote
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
by
\pi
.

Proof.

By
[
29
, Corollary 6.1]
,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie algebra.

For
R\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket(x,y)=2(R([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-R([R(y),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

By this equality, we can deduce that on the one hand
R
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
if and only if
\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket=0
, i.e.
R
is a Maurer-Cartan element. On the other hand,
R
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
R
satisfies (
8
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then
\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket
is in the center of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Proof.

Denote the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
by
\pi
. Since
R
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have
\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket=2\pi
via Theorem
3.1
. For all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, by (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket 2\pi,f\right\rrbracket(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k},x_{k+1},x_{k+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in S(k,1,1)}(-1)^{|\sigma|}\pi(\pi(f(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k)}),x_{\sigma(k+1)}),x_{\sigma(k+2)})

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sigma\in S(2,1,k-1)}(-1)^{|\sigma|}f(\pi(\pi(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)}),x_{\sigma(3)}),x_{\sigma(4)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+2)})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in S(2,k)}(-1)^{|\sigma|}\pi(\pi(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)}),f(x_{\sigma(3)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+2)}))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket
is in the center of
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
.
∎

We denote
\left\llbracket R,\cdot\right\rrbracket
by
\mathrm{d}_{R}
. Now we obtain the differential graded Lie algebra that governs algebraic deformations of a modified
r
-matrix.

Theorem 3.3
.

With the above notations,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d}_{R})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.

Furthermore,
R+R^{\prime}
is still a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if
R^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d}_{R})
.

Proof.

It follows from the graded Jacobi identity that
\mathrm{d}_{R}
is a graded derivation on the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. By Proposition
3.2
, we have

\mathrm{d}_{R}^{2}f=\left\llbracket R,\left\llbracket R,f\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket,f\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket R,\left\llbracket R,f\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket,

which implies that

\mathrm{d}_{R}^{2}f=\left\llbracket R,\left\llbracket R,f\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket\left\llbracket R,R\right\rrbracket,f\right\rrbracket=0.

Therefore,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d}_{R})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.

Let
R^{\prime}
be a linear map from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
R+R^{\prime}
is a modified
r
-matrix if and only if

\left\llbracket R+R^{\prime},R+R^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=2\pi,

that is

0=\left\llbracket R,R^{\prime}\right\rrbracket+\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket R^{\prime},R^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=\mathrm{d}_{R}R^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket R^{\prime},R^{\prime}\right\rrbracket.

Thus
R+R^{\prime}
is still a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if
R^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d}_{R})
.
∎

At the end of this section, we establish the relationship between the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}
and the differential
\mathrm{d}_{R}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}(f)=(-1)^{n-1}\left\llbracket R,f\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

For any
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
x_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, by (
7
), we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\left\llbracket R,f\right\rrbracket(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\Big(\sum_{\sigma\in S(n-1,1)}(-1)^{\sigma}R([f(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n-1)}),x_{\sigma(n)}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle-(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S(1,1,n-2)}(-1)^{\sigma}f([R(x_{\sigma(1)}),x_{\sigma(2)}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{\sigma(3)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S(1,n-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}[R(x_{\sigma(1)}),f(x_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}R([f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n}),x_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\Big(f([R(x_{i}),x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle-f([R(x_{j}),x_{i}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[R(x_{i}),f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{R}(f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}).

We finish the proof.
∎

4.
Geometric deformations of modified
r
-matrices

In this section, we study geometric deformations of modified
r
-matrices following the approach developed by Crainic, Schatz and Struchiner. We show that the condition
H^{2}(R)=0
will imply certain rigidity theorem, and the condition
H^{3}(R)=0
will imply the space of modified
r
-matrices on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a manifold in a neighborhood of
R
. We also give the necessary and sufficient condition on a 2-cocycle giving a geometric deformation using the Kuranishi map.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. A
geometric deformation
of
R
is a smooth one parameter family of modified
r
-matrices
R_{t}
on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
such that
R_{0}=R
.

Definition 4.2
.

Two geometric deformations
R_{t}
and
R_{t}^{\prime}
of
R
are called
equivalent
if there exists a smooth family of modified
r
-matrices isomorphism
\varphi_{t}:R_{t}\,\rightarrow\,R_{t}^{\prime}
such that
\varphi_{0}={\rm{Id}}
, where
\varphi_{t}
are inner automorphisms of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
R_{t}
be a geometric deformation of
R
. Denote
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}R_{t}
by
\dot{R_{0}}
. Then there is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

With the above notations,
\dot{R_{0}}
is a
2
-cocycle in
C^{2}(R)
. Moreover if
R_{t}
and
R_{t}^{\prime}
are equivalent geometric deformations of
R
, then
[\dot{R_{0}}]=[\dot{R_{0}^{\prime}}]
in
H^{2}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
R_{t}
is a geometric deformation of
R
, for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle[R(x),\dot{R_{0}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\dot{R_{0}}(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}[R_{t}(x),R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}(R_{t}([R_{t}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{R_{0}}([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})+R([\dot{R_{0}}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\dot{R_{0}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Thus by (
6
) and (
9
), we have
\mathrm{d}^{R}_{\mathsf{CE}}(\dot{R_{0}})=0
.

Assume that
\varphi_{t}
is an isomorphism from
R_{t}
to
R^{\prime}_{t}
, that is

\varphi_{t}(R_{t}(x))=R^{\prime}_{t}(\varphi_{t}(x)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Denote
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\varphi_{t}
by
\dot{\varphi_{0}}
. Then we have
\dot{\varphi_{0}}(R(x))+\dot{R_{0}}(x)=R(\dot{\varphi_{0}}(x))+\dot{R^{\prime}_{0}}(x)
.
Since
\varphi_{t}
are inner automorphisms of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, it follows that
\dot{\varphi_{0}}
is an inner derivation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus there exists
y\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\dot{\varphi_{0}}=\mathrm{ad}_{y}
. Therefore, we have

[y,R(x)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\dot{R_{0}}(x)=R([y,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\dot{R^{\prime}_{0}}(x),

which implies
\dot{R_{0}}-\dot{R^{\prime}_{0}}=\mathrm{d}^{R}_{\mathsf{CE}}(y)
. Thus
[\dot{R_{0}}]=[\dot{R_{0}^{\prime}}]
in
H^{2}(R)
.
∎

Next, we consider under which conditions does a cocycle
f\in Z^{2}(R)
determine a geometric deformation
R_{t}
. Define the Kuranishi map
K:Z^{2}(R)\,\rightarrow\,H^{3}(R)
by

K(f)=[\left\llbracket f,f\right\rrbracket],\quad\forall f\in Z^{2}(R).

Now we give a necessary condition of the above question. The sufficient condition need some preparations and will be given at the end of this section.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that there exists a geometric deformation
R_{t}
of
R
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
such that
\dot{R_{0}}=f\in Z^{2}(R)
, then
K(f)=0
.

Proof.

Consider the Taylor expansion of
R_{t}
around
t=0
, then we have

R_{t}(x)=R(x)+tf(x)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}g(x)+o(t^{3}).

Since
[R_{t}(x),R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R_{t}([R_{t}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
f\in Z^{2}(R)
, we have

(10)

\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big(\mathrm{d}^{R}_{\mathsf{CE}}(g)(x,y)+2[f(x),f(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-2f([f(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,f(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})\Big)+o(t^{3})=0.

Thus by (
7
) and (
10
), we obtain
\left\llbracket f,f\right\rrbracket=\mathrm{d}^{R}_{\mathsf{CE}}g
, which implies that
K(f)=0
.
∎

Let

\displaystyle AS(f)\circ A^{-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A([f(A^{-1}x),f(A^{-1}y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-f([f(A^{-1}x),A^{-1}y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[A^{-1}x,f(A^{-1}y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})+[A^{-1}x,A^{-1}y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle[Af(A^{-1}x),Af(A^{-1}y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-AfA^{-1}([Af(A^{-1}x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Af(A^{-1}y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(AfA^{-1})(x,y).

Thus we have

which implies that

is an equivariant section.

Since

is a modified

-matrix on the Lie algebra

. Moreover, since

(11)

\displaystyle D(S)_{R}(g)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}S(R+tg)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\Big([R(x)+tg(x),R(y)+tg(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-(R+tg)([R(x)+tg(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)+tg(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle[g(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),g(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([g(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,g(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-g([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

By Proposition

∎

Theorem 4.10

5.

r
-matrices

In this section, we study linear deformations of a modified
r
-matrix using the established cohomology theory. In particular, a trivial linear deformation leads to a Nijenhuis element for a modified
r
-matrix

Definition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
\hat{R}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}

\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}

-matrix on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})

generates a

-matrix

.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
R:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a modified r-matrix on
\mathfrak{g}
. Two linear deformations
R_{t}^{1}=R+t\hat{R}_{1}
and
R_{t}^{2}=R+t\hat{R}_{2}
are said to be

such that

\varphi_{t}={\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x},

satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\varphi_{t}([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\varphi_{t}(y),\varphi_{t}(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

(ii)

R^{2}_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{t}\circ R^{1}_{t}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\hat{R}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
generate a linear deformation of the modified
r
-matrix
R
. Then
\hat{R}
is a
2

and

generated by
\hat{R_{1}}
and
\hat{R_{2}}
respectively. Then
[\hat{R_{1}}]=[\hat{R_{2}}]
in

.

Proof.

Since
R_{t}=R+t\hat{R}
is a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we have

[R_{t}(x),R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R_{t}([R_{t}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Consider the coefficients of
t
and
t^{2}
respectively, we have

(12)

\displaystyle[\hat{R}(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),\hat{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R([\hat{R}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\hat{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\hat{R}([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

and

(13)

\displaystyle[\hat{R}(x),\hat{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\hat{R}([\hat{R}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\hat{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

By (
12

\hat{R}
is a 2-cocycle of the modified
r
-matrix
R
.

If
R^{1}_{t}
and
R^{2}_{t}
are equivalent linear deformations of

, then there exists
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that

({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(R+t\hat{R}_{1})(u)=(R+t\hat{R}_{2})({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(u),\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{g},

\hat{R}_{1}(u)-\hat{R}_{2}(u)=[R(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}-R([u,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{g}.

By (

is given by (

[\hat{R_{1}}]=[\hat{R_{2}}]
in

∎

Definition 5.4
.

A linear deformation of a modified

-matrix

such that
{\rm{Id}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x}
is an isomorphism from

.

.

Let

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle[x,[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,R([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})]_{\mathfrak{g}},

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\oplus}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
, where

[(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})]_{\oplus}=([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[y_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},i=1,2.

Define the subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
by
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}=\{(x,x)|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
and the subspace
\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}=\{(x,-x)|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
. It is obvious that
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
, while
\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}
is not a Lie subalgebra. To find a complement of
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
which is also a Lie subalgebra, it is natural to consider the graph of certain linear map from
\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
. It is known that a complement of
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
is isomorphic to a graph of a linear map from
\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
. Let
R\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
be a linear map. Define a linear map
\hat{R}:\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
by

\hat{R}(x,-x)=(-R(x),-R(x)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 6.1
.

With the above notations, the graph
\mathcal{G}(\hat{R}):=\{\hat{R}u+u|u\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\Delta}\}
is a Lie subalgebra of
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\oplus})
if and only if
R
is a modified
r
-matrix.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle[(-R(x),-R(x))+(x,-x),(-R(y),-R(y))+(y,-y)]_{\oplus}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})+([R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+(-[R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}})+(-[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+(-[R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Thus
\mathcal{G}(\hat{R})
is a Lie subalgebra if and only if

R([R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},

i.e.
R
is a modified
r
-matrix.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix. Then
(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta},\mathcal{G}(\hat{R}))
is a matched pair of Lie algebras.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}\oplus\mathcal{G}(\hat{R})
since
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}\bigcap\mathcal{G}(\hat{R})=0
. Then the conclusion follows from the fact that both
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}
and
\mathcal{G}(\hat{R})
are Lie subalgebras.
∎

Summarizing the above studies, we have the following conclusion.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
R_{t}
be a geometric deformation of a modified
r
-matrix
R
. Then
\mathcal{G}(\hat{R_{t}})
is a deformation of the complement
\mathcal{G}(\hat{R})
. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta},\mathcal{G}(\hat{R_{t}}))
are matched pairs of Lie algebras.

6.2.
Compatible Poisson structures

A
compatible Poisson structure
consists of two Poisson structures
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
on a manifold
M
such that
\pi+\pi^{\prime}
is also a Poisson structure on the manifold
M
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is a Lie algebra and we denote by
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{R})
the corresponding linear Poisson manifold.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
R
be a modified
r
-matrix on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
R_{t}=R+t\hat{R}
be a linear deformation of
R
. For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{R_{t_{1}}}
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{R_{t_{2}}}
are compatible Poisson structures on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Proof.

By the fact that
R+\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\hat{R}
is also a modified
r
-matrix on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have

[x,y]_{R_{t_{1}}}+[x,y]_{R_{t_{2}}}=2([R(x)+\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\hat{R}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,R(y)+\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}\hat{R}(y)]),

which implies that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{R_{t_{1}}}+[\cdot,\cdot]_{R_{t_{2}}}
is also a Lie bracket on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by (
2
). Thus, for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{R_{t_{1}}}
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{R_{t_{2}}}
are compatible Poisson structures on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
∎

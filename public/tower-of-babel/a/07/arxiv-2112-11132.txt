2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and
I
be an ideal of
R
.

•

A vector

v=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in R^{r}
is unimodular if there exist
w=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in R^{r}
with

\langle v,w\rangle=\Sigma_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}b_{i}=1
. The set
of unimodular rows in
R^{r}
is denoted as
\mbox{\rm Um}_{r}(R)
.
The set
\mbox{\rm Um}_{r}(R,I)
consist of unimodular vectors
v\in\mbox{\rm Um}(R)
which are congruent to
e_{1}
modulo
I
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
.

•

The group
\mbox{\rm E}_{r}(R)
is generated by elementary matrices
e_{ij}(\lambda)=\mbox{Id}_{r}+\lambda{\mbox{\rm E}}_{ij}\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{r}(R)
, where
\lambda\in R
and for
i\neq j
,
{\mbox{\rm E}}_{ij}\in\mbox{M}_{r}(R)
whose
(i,j)^{th}
entry is
1
and all other entries are zero.
The group

{\mbox{\rm E}}_{r}(R)
acts on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{r}(R)
by right multiplication, i.e.
v\cdot\varepsilon:=v\varepsilon.

We say
v\sim w
if there exists an
\varepsilon\in E_{r}(R)
such that
v=w\varepsilon.

Let
{\mbox{\rm Um}_{r}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{r}(R)}=\{[v]:v\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{r}(R)\}
denote the elementary orbit space.

•

The group
\mbox{E}_{n}(I)
is the subgroup of
\mbox{E}_{n}(R)
generated
by elements
e_{ij}(x)
with
x\in I.
The relative elementary group
\mbox{E}_{n}(R,I)
is the normal closure of
\mbox{E}_{n}(I)
in
\mbox{E}_{n}(R).

•

We say stable range condition
sr_{n}(R)
holds for
R
if
given
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n+1}(R),
there exist
c_{i}\in R
such that

(a_{1}+c_{1}a_{n+1},a_{2}+c_{2}a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n}+c_{n}a_{n+1})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R).

•

The stable range
of
R,
denoted by
sr(R),
is the least integer
n
such that
sr_{n}(R)
holds. The stable dimension of
R
is

\mbox{sdim}(R)=sr(R)-1.

•

The excision ring
R\oplus I
has coordinatewise addition. The multiplication
is given as

(r,i).(s,j)=(rs,rj+si+ij)
, where
r,s\in R
and
i,j\in I.

The multiplicative identity is
(1,0).

•

A Mennicke symbol of length
n\geq 3
is a pair
(\phi,G)
, where
G
is a group and
\phi:\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R)\to G
is a map satisfying

(1)

\phi(e_{1})=1
and
\phi(v)=\phi(v\sigma)
for
\sigma\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)
.

(2)

\phi(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot\phi(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})=\phi(ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
.

A universal Mennicke symbol exist and is denoted as
(ms,MS_{n}(R))
.

We will state the part of Swan’s Bertini needed in our proof
[
16
, Theorems 1.3, 1.4]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
R
be a geometrically reduced affine ring of dimension
d
over an infinite field
k
and
(a,a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{r+1}(R)
. Then there exist
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in R
such that if
a^{\prime}=a+a_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+a_{r}b_{r}
, then
R/(a^{\prime})
is a reduced affine algebra of dimension
d-1
which is smooth at all smooth points of
R
.

The following result is due to Garge, Gupta, Rao in
[
4
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R\oplus I
be the excision ring of
R
with respect to an ideal
I
in
R
and
n\geq 3.
Suppose both
\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R,I)
and
\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R\oplus I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R\oplus I)
have group structure given by van der Kallen’s rule. Then the group structure on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R,I)
is nice whenever it is nice for
\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R\oplus I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R\oplus I).

The following local-global principle for unimodular rows is due to Rao
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
R
be a ring,
v\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n}(R[X])
and
n\geq 3.
Suppose that
v\equiv v(0)~(\mbox{mod}~\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R_{\mathfrak{m}}[X])
for all maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. Then
v\equiv v(0)~(\mbox{mod}~\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R[X]).

3.
Main Theorems

3.1.
A nice group structure on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R,I)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R
be an affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over a perfect field
k
of char
\neq 2
with c.d.
{}_{2}(k)\leq 2
. Let
J
be an ideal of
R
. Then the group structures on
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{d+1}(R)/{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R)

and
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{d+1}(R,J)/{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R,J)
are nice.

{{\bf Proof:}}
We first prove the absolute case
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{d+1}(R)/{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R)
. When
k
is a finite field, the result follows from
[
3
, Theorem 3.9]
. Hence we assume that
k
is infinite. Further by
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
, we may assume
R
is reduced. Since
k
is perfect, we get
R
is geometrically reduced.

Case I:
If
I
denotes the ideal defining the singular locus of
R,
then ht
(I)\geq 1
. Let
v,w\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R)
. In view of
[
3
, Proposition 3.8]
, we may assume that

v=(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1})
and
w=(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1})
with
v,w\equiv e_{d+1}

(\text{mod}~I)
. Applying Swan’s Bertini Theorem
2.2
, we can add a linear combination of
ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}
to
a_{d+1}
to get

a_{d+1}^{\prime}=a_{d+1}+\lambda ab+\sum_{i=2}^{d}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
for some
\lambda,\lambda_{i}\in R
and
{R}/{(a_{d+1}^{\prime})}
is smooth outside the singular set of
R.
Since
a_{d+1}^{\prime}\equiv 1~(\mbox{mod}~I)
,
{R}/{(a_{d+1}^{\prime})}

is smooth of dimension
d.

Let
\overline{R}=R/(a_{d+1}^{\prime}).
We have
[v]=[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})],[w]=[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})].
Choose
p\in R
such that
ap\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~(a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime}).
Thus
[v]\ast[w]=[(a(b+p)-1,a_{2}(b+p),a_{3},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})].
Thus

[(\overline{a}(\overline{b}+\overline{p})-1,\overline{a_{2}}(\overline{b}+\overline{p}),\overline{a_{3}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})]=[(\overline{a},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})]\ast[(\overline{b},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})]\\
=[(\overline{ab},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})]

by Fasel
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Upon lifting the elementary matrix, we have

[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})]\ast[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})]=[(a(b+p)-1,a_{2}(b+p),a_{3},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})]=[(ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}^{\prime})].

Thus we have

[v]*[w]=[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1})]\ast[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1})]=[(ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1})].

Case II :
By
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
,
R\oplus J
is an affine
k
-algebra of dimension
d
. By case I,
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{d+1}(R\oplus I)/{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R\oplus I)
has nice group structure. Therefore by Lemma
2.3
, the group structure on
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{d+1}(R,J)/{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R,J)
is nice.

\hfill\square

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
R
be an affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over a perfect field
k
of char
\neq 2
with c.d.
{}_{2}(k)\leq 2
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
.
If
\sigma\in{\mbox{\rm SL}}_{d+1}(R,I)\cap{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+2}(R,I)
, then

e_{1}\sigma
can be completed to a relative elementary matrix
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R,I).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Follows from the proof of
[
9
, Corollary 3.5]
.

\hfill\square

3.2.
A nice group structure on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
R
be a reduced affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over an algebraically closed field
k.
Assume that either (i)
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
or (ii)
R
is a normal. Let
I
be the ideal defining singular locus of
R
and
(v,w)
be an admissible pair of size
d
over
R.
Then there exists another admissible pair

(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
of size
d
over
R
such that both
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\equiv e_{d}~(\mbox{mod}~I)
and
(v,w)\rightarrow(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
is an admissible sequence.

{{\bf Proof:}}
The proof is similar to
[
3
, Proposition 3.8]
and we refer to
[
3
]
for the definition of admissible pair and admissible sequence.

Case 1:

k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.
Let
v=(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}),w=(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R).
Since
R
is a reduced affine algebra, we have
\mbox{ht}(I)\geq 1.
Thus
\overline{R}=R/I
has
\mbox{dim}(\overline{R})\leq d-1
and
d-1\geq 3.
By
[
17
, Corollary 17.3]
,
\mbox{sr}(\overline{R})\leq d-1.
Since
\overline{v}=(\overline{a},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}}),\overline{w}=(\overline{b},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(\overline{R}),
we have
(\overline{ab},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(\overline{R}).
Since
\mbox{sr}(\overline{R})\leq d-1,
there exist
\overline{\lambda_{2}},\ldots,\overline{\lambda_{d}}\in\overline{R}
such that
(\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R})
where
\overline{a_{i}^{\prime}}=\overline{a_{i}}+\overline{\lambda_{i}ab},
for
2\leq i\leq d.
Thus we can modify
\overline{v},\overline{w}
to
\overline{v_{1}}=(\overline{a},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}}),\overline{w_{1}}=(\overline{b},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}})
such that
(\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R}).
Since
(\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R}),
after making suitable admissible transformations we may assume that
\overline{a}=\overline{1}
and
\overline{b}=\overline{1}.
Next we transform
\overline{v_{1}},\overline{w_{1}}
to
\overline{v_{2}}=(\overline{a},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}),\overline{w_{2}}=(\overline{b},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}})
where
\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}}+\overline{ab}(1-a_{d}^{\prime})
and

\overline{a_{j}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{a_{j}^{\prime}}+\overline{ab}(-a_{j}^{\prime})
for
2\leq j\leq d-1.
Thus we have

\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{1}
and
\overline{a_{j}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{0}.
Further by modifying
\overline{v_{2}},\overline{w_{2}}
to
\overline{v_{3}}=(\overline{a}-\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}),\overline{w_{3}}=(\overline{b}-\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}},\overline{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}})
, we may assume that
\overline{a},\overline{b}=\overline{0}.
Thus upon lifting the elementary transformations, we can modify
v,w
to
v_{3}=({a},{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,a_{d-1}^{\prime\prime},{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}),w_{3}=({b},{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,a_{d-1}^{\prime\prime},{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}})
respectively such that
v_{3},w_{3}\equiv e_{d}~\mbox{mod}~I
and
(v,w)\rightarrow(v_{1},w_{1})\rightarrow(v_{2},w_{2})\rightarrow(v_{3},w_{3})
is an admissible sequence.

Case 2:

R
is normal. In this case, we have
\mbox{ht}(I)\geq 2.
Thus
\mbox{dim}(\overline{R})\leq d-2.
Since
(\overline{ab},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(\overline{R}),
by

[
17
, Corollary 9.4]
, there exist
\overline{c_{2}},\ldots,\overline{c_{d}}\in\overline{R}
such that
\mbox{ht}(\sum_{i=2}^{d}\overline{R}(\overline{a_{i}}+\overline{c_{i}ab}))\geq d-1.
Thus
(\overline{a_{2}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}^{\prime}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R})
where
\overline{a_{i}^{\prime}}=\overline{a_{i}}+\overline{c_{i}ab},
for
2\leq i\leq d.
Now as done in the previous paragraph, we can modify
v,w
to
v_{3}=({a},{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,a_{d-1}^{\prime\prime},{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}}),w_{3}=({a},{a_{2}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,a_{d-1}^{\prime\prime},{a_{d}^{\prime\prime}})
respectively such that
v_{3},w_{3}\equiv e_{d}~\mbox{mod}~I
and
(v,w)\rightarrow(v_{3},w_{3})
is an admissible sequence.
\hfill\square

\neq 2,3.

{{\bf Proof:}}
In view of
[

, Lemma 3.5]
, we may assume that

is a reduced ring. Let

and
I
denotes the ideal defining the singular locus of
R.
In view of Proposition
3.3
, we may assume
(v,w)
to be an admissible pair such that
v,w\equiv e_{d}~(\mbox{mod}~I).
Let
v=(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}),w=(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}).
Applying Swan’s Bertini Theorem
2.2
, we can add a linear combination of
ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1}
to
a_{d}
to get

a_{d}^{\prime}=a_{d}+\lambda ab+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
for some
\lambda,\lambda_{i}\in R
and
{R}/{(a_{d}^{\prime})}
is smooth outside the singular set of
R.
Since
a_{d}^{\prime}\equiv 1~(\mbox{mod}~I)
,
{R}/{(a_{d}^{\prime})}

is smooth of dimension atmost
d-1.

Let
\overline{R}=R/(a_{d}^{\prime}).
We have
[v]=[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime})],[w]=[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d^{\prime}})].
Choose
p\in R
such that
ap\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~(a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime}).
Thus
[v]\ast[w]=[(a(b+p)-1,a_{2}(b+p),a_{3},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime})].
Thus

[(\overline{a}(\overline{b}+\overline{p})-1,\overline{a_{2}}(\overline{b}+\overline{p}),\overline{a_{3}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}})]=[(\overline{a},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}})]\ast[(\overline{b},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}})]=[(\overline{ab},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}})]

by
[
4
, Theorem 6.8]
.
Upon lifting the elementary matrix, we have

[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime})]\ast[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d^{\prime}})]=[(a(b+p)-1,a_{2}(b+p),a_{3},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime})]=[(ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}^{\prime})].

Thus we have

[(a,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})]\ast[(b,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})]=[(ab,a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})].

\hfill\square

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
R
be an affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
p\neq 2,3
and
I
an ideal of
R.
Then the group structure on
{\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)}
is nice.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Since
R\oplus I
is an affine
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra of dimension
d

[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
, it has nice group structure by Theorem
3.4
. Now the result follows from Lemma
2.3
.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Remark 3.6
.

Let
R
be a normal affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over an algebraically closed field
k
of char
\neq 2,3.
If
I
is an ideal of
R
, then
R\oplus I
is not normal. Therefore we do not have Theorem
3.4
to conclude that
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)
has nice group structure.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d\geq 4
and
J
be an ideal of
R
such that
\mbox{dim}(R/J)\leq d-2.
Let
v\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)
for some ideal
I\subset R.
Then there exists
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)
such that

v\varepsilon=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{d})
with
u_{1}\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~(I\cap J).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
v=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)
and
\overline{R}=R/J.
We have
\overline{v}=(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(\overline{R},\overline{I}).
By stable range condition
[
1
]
, upon adding multiples of
\overline{a_{d}}
to
\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}}
, we may assume that
(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R},\overline{I}).
Let
(\overline{b_{1}},\overline{b_{2}},\ldots,\overline{b_{d-1}})\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d-1}(\overline{R},\overline{I})
be such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\overline{a_{i}}\overline{b_{i}}=\overline{1}.
Let

\overline{\varepsilon_{1}}=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{1}&\overline{0}&\ldots&(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{1}}\\
\overline{0}&\overline{1}&\ldots&(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{2}}\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
\overline{0}&\overline{0}&\ldots&(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{d-1}}\\
\overline{0}&\overline{0}&\ldots&\overline{1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(\overline{R}).

Then
(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d}})\overline{\varepsilon_{1}}=(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}},\overline{1}).
There exists
\overline{\varepsilon_{2}}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(\overline{R},\overline{I})
such that
(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}},\overline{1})\overline{\varepsilon_{2}}=(\overline{1},\overline{0},\ldots,\overline{0},\overline{1}).
Now
(\overline{1},\overline{0},\ldots,\overline{0},\overline{1})\overline{\varepsilon_{1}}^{-1}=(\overline{1},\overline{0},\ldots,\overline{0},\overline{1}-(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{1}}).
Since

1-(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{1}})=\overline{0}~\mbox{mod}~{\overline{I}},
there exists
\overline{\varepsilon_{3}}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(\overline{R},\overline{I})
such that
(\overline{1},\overline{0},\ldots,\overline{0},1-(1-\overline{a_{d}})\overline{b_{1}})\overline{\varepsilon_{3}}=(\overline{1},\overline{0},\ldots,\overline{0},\overline{0}).
Let
\overline{\varepsilon}=\overline{\varepsilon_{1}}\overline{\varepsilon_{2}}\overline{\varepsilon_{1}}^{-1}\overline{\varepsilon_{3}}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(\overline{R},\overline{I}).
Let
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)
be a lift of
\overline{\varepsilon}.
Then we have
v\varepsilon=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{d})
with
u_{1}\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~(I\cap J).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
R
be a normal affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over an algebraically closed field
k
of char
\neq 2,3
and
I
be an ideal of
R.
Then the group structure on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)/\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I)
is nice.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
v=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},a),w=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},b)
be such that
v,w\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I).
Then
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},ab)\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I).
Let
J
be the ideal defining singular locus of
R.
Since
R
is a normal affine algebra,
\mbox{ht}(J)\geq 2.
In view of Lemma
3.7
we may assume that
a_{1}\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~(I\cap J).
Let
a_{1}=1-\lambda
for some
\lambda\in I\cap J.
Then
(a_{1},\lambda a_{2},\ldots,\lambda a_{d-1},\lambda ab)\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I).
By Swan’s Bertini Theorem
2.2
, we can add a linear combination of
\lambda ab,\lambda a_{d-1},\ldots,\lambda a_{2}
to
a_{1}
which transforms it to
a_{1}^{\prime}
such that
R/(a_{1}^{\prime})
is smooth outside the singular set of
R
and
\mbox{dim}(R/(a_{1}^{\prime}))=d-1.
Since
a_{1}^{\prime}\equiv 1~\mbox{mod}~J,

R/(a_{1}^{\prime})
is a smooth affine algebra. Let
a_{1}^{\prime}=1-\eta.
Note that in
\overline{R}=R/(a_{1}^{\prime})
we have
\overline{\eta}=\overline{1}.
Now choose
(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{d-1},p)\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{d}(R,I)
such that
a_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}a_{i}b_{i}+ap=1.
Then

[w]\ast[v]=[(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},b)]\ast[(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},a)]=[(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-2},a_{d-1}(b+p),a(b+p)-\eta)]

Upon going modulo
a_{1}^{\prime}
, we have

[(\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}}(\overline{b}+\overline{p}),\overline{a}(\overline{b}+\overline{p})-\overline{\eta})]=[(\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}},\overline{b})]\ast[(\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}},\overline{a})]=[(\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,\overline{a_{d-1}},\overline{ab})]

by
[
4
, Theorem 6.8]
.
Since
\overline{\eta}=\overline{1},
we have

(a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-2},a_{d-1}(b+p),a(b+p)-\eta)\alpha\equiv(a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},ab)~(\mbox{mod}~(Ia_{1}^{\prime}))

for some
\alpha\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d-1}(\eta)\subset\mbox{\rm E}_{d-1}(I).
Thus

[(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-2},a_{d-1}(b+p),a(b+p)-\eta)]=[(a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},ab)]=[(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d-1},ab)].

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
R
be an affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over an algebraically closed field
k
of char
\neq 2,3.
Let
I
be an ideal of
R.
Assume that either (i)
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
or (ii)
R
is normal. If
\sigma\in{\mbox{\rm SL}}_{d}(R,I)\cap{\mbox{\rm E}}_{d+1}(R,I)
, then

e_{1}\sigma
can be completed to a relative elementary matrix
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{d}(R,I).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Follows from the proof of
[
9
, Corollary 3.5]
.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

3.3.
Divisibility of
MS_{d+1}(R)

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
R
be a reduced affine algebra of dimension
d\geq 4
over an infinite perfect field
k
of char
\neq 2
with
c.d.(k)\leq 1
. Then
MS_{d+1}(R)
is uniquely divisible prime to the characteristic of
k
.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
n\geq 1
be prime to the characteristic of
k
. If
I
is the ideal defining the singular locus of
R
, then
ht(I)\geq 1
. Let
[v]=[(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d+1})]\in MS_{d+1}(R)
. Since
dim(R/I)\leq d-1
, we may assume that
v=e_{1}
mod
I
.
By Swan’s Bertini Theorem
2.2
, we can add multiples of
a_{2},\ldots,a_{d+1}
to
a_{1}
and assume that
R/(a_{1})
is of dimension
d-1\geq 3
and is smooth outside the singular set of
R
. Since
a_{1}=1
mod
I
, we get
\overline{R}=R/(a_{1})
is smooth affine algebra over
k
of dimension
d-1
. Applying Fasel’s result in the smooth case

[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
, we get that
[\overline{v}]=[(\overline{a}_{2},\ldots,\overline{a}_{d+1})]\in MS_{d}(\overline{R})
is uniquely divisible by
n
. So there exist a unique
[\overline{w}]=[(\overline{b}_{2},\ldots,\overline{b}_{d+1})]\in MS_{d}(\overline{R})
such that
[\overline{v}]=[\overline{w}]^{n}=[(\overline{b}_{2}^{n},\overline{b}_{3},\ldots,\overline{b}_{d+1})]\in MS_{d}(\overline{R})
. Therefore,
[v]=[(a_{1},b_{2}^{n},\ldots,b_{d+1})]=[(a_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{d+1})]^{n}\in MS_{d+1}(R)
. Thus
[v]
is divisible by
n
in
MS_{d+1}(R)
.

To prove uniqueness, assume there exist
[w_{1}]=[(c,c_{2},\ldots,c_{d+1})]
and
[w_{2}]=[(d,c_{2},\ldots,c_{d+1})]\in MS_{d+1}(R)
such that
[v]=[w_{1}]^{n}=[w_{2}]^{n}
. In view of
[
3
, Proposition 3.8]
, we may assume that
w_{1},w_{2}=e_{1}
mod
I
. By Swan’s version of Bertini
[
16
]
, adding multiples of
cd,c_{2},\ldots,c_{d}
to
c_{d+1}
, as in Theorem
3.1
, we may assume that
\overline{R}=R/(c_{d+1})
is smooth affine of dimension
d-1
. Since
[\overline{v}]=[\overline{c},\overline{c}_{2},\ldots,\overline{c}_{d}]^{n}=[\overline{d},\overline{c}_{2},\ldots,\overline{c}_{d}]^{n}\in MS_{d}(\overline{R})
, by uniqueness part in
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
, we get
[\overline{c},\overline{c}_{2},\ldots,\overline{c}_{d}]=[\overline{d},\overline{c}_{2},\ldots,\overline{c}_{d}]\in MS_{d}(\overline{R})
. Therefore, lifting elementary automorphism gives us
[w_{1}]=[w_{2}]\in MS_{d+1}(R)
.

\hfill\square

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d\geq 2
such that
\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R)
acts transitively on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R).
Then the first row map

row_{1}:\mbox{\rm GL}_{d+1}(R[X])\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}
is a group homomorphism.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
\sigma,\tau\in\mbox{\rm GL}_{d+1}(R[X])
and
[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]\ast[e_{1}\tau]\ast[e_{1}\sigma\tau]^{-1}.
For every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset R,
by
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[v]_{\mathfrak{m}}=[e_{1}].
Therefore by Theorem
2.4
,

[v]=[v({0})].
Since
\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R)
acts transitively on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R),
we have
[v]=[e_{1}].
Thus
[e_{1}\sigma]\ast[e_{1}\tau]=[e_{1}\sigma\tau].

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

In
[
14
, Corollary 3.8]
, the second author proved that if
R
is a local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
with
1/d!\in R,
then the group
{\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}
has no
m
-torsion, provided
mR=R.
Using this result we prove the following :

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d\geq 2
with
1/d!\in R
such that
\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R)
acts transitively on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R).
Then the group
{\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}
has no
m
-torsion, provided
mR=R.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
[v]\in{\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}
such that
[v]^{m}=[e_{1}].
For every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset R,
by
[
14
, Corollary 3.8]
,
[v]_{\mathfrak{m}}=[e_{1}].
Therefore by Theorem
2.4
,

[v]=[v({0})].
Since
\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R)
acts transitively on
\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R),
we have
[v]=[e_{1}].

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
d\geq 2
. Assume that either (i)
R
is an affine
C
-algebra with

or

\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

(1)

The first row map

\mbox{\rm GL}_{d+1}(R[X])\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}

(2)

The group
{\mbox{\rm Um}_{d+1}(R[X])}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{d+1}(R[X])}

-torsion, provided

{{\bf Proof:}}
Using
[

.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

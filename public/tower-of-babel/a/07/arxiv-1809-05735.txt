We survey some recent developments in the asymptotic geometry of the Hitchin moduli space, starting with an introduction to the Hitchin moduli space and hyperkähler geometry.

A tour of the Hitchin moduli space

Given the data of

•

C
, a compact Riemann surface of genus
\gamma_{C}\geq 2
(unless indicated otherwise), and

•

E\rightarrow C
, a complex vector bundle of rank

\mathcal{M}

In one avatar, the Hitchin moduli space is the moduli space of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

In this section, we define these objects and explain the correspondence between Higgs bundles (a holomorphic object) and flat connections and their associated representations (a representation theoretic object). We survey many of the results appearing in Nigel Hitchin’s seminal paper for the complex Lie group

1.1
Motivation: Narasimhan–Seshadri correspondence

For the sake of motivation, there is an earlier example of a correspondence between holomorphic objects and representations. In 1965, Narasimhan and Seshadri proved the equivalence between stable holomorphic vector bundles
on a compact Riemann surface
C
and irreducible projective unitary representations of the fundamental group
[
31
]
. In 1983, Donaldson gave a more direct proof of this fact using the differential geometry of connections on holomorphic bundles
[
10
]
. We specialize to the degree
0
case for simplicity, so that projective unitary representations are simply unitary representations.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a indecomposable holomorphic bundle of
\deg{\mathcal{E}}=0
and rank
n
over a Riemann surface
C
. The holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is stable if, and only if, there is an irreducible flat unitary connection on
{\mathcal{E}}
. Taking the holonomy representation of this connection, we have the following equivalence:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\mbox{stable holomorphic bundles}\\
{\mathcal{E}}\end{matrix}\right\}_{\!\mbox{$\!/\!\!\sim$}}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{matrix}\mbox{flat $\mathrm{U}(n)$-connections}\\
\nabla\end{matrix}\right\}_{\!\mbox{$\!/\!\!\sim$}}

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{matrix}\mbox{irreducible representations}\\
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{U}(n)\end{matrix}\right\}_{\!\mbox{$\!/\!\!\sim$}}.

This map from a holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
to a flat connection
\nabla
features a distinguished hermitian metric
h
on
{\mathcal{E}}
. First, note that given any hermitian metric
h
on a holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
, there is a unique connection
D(\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{E}},h)
called the Chern connection characterized by the property that (1)
D^{0,1}=\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{E}}
and (2)
D
is unitary with the respect to
h
, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{h}=\langle Ds_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{h}+\langle s_{1},Ds_{2}\rangle_{h}
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the following fact: given a stable holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
of degree
0
, there is a hermitian metric
h
– known as a Hermitian–Einstein metric – such that the Chern connection is flat. Consequently, the flat connection associated to
{\mathcal{E}}
is
\nabla=D(\overline{\partial}_{E},h)
, where
h
is the Hermitian–Einstein metric.

The nonabelian Hodge correspondence, which interpolates between the two avatars of the Hitchin moduli space, also features a distinguished hermitian metric.

1.2
Definition of the Hitchin moduli space

Higgs bundles and the Hitchin moduli space first appeared in Nigel Hitchin’s beautiful paper “The self-duality equations on a Riemann surface”

Definition 1.2
.

Fix a complex vector bundle

.

Fix a complex vector bundle

1.3
Nonabelian Hodge correspondence

The Hitchin moduli space
\mathcal{M}
is hyperkähler. As a consequence, it has a
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-worth of complex structures, labeled by parameter
\zeta\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Two avatars of the Hitchin moduli space are

•

the Higgs bundle moduli space (
\zeta=0
), and

•

the moduli space of flat
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-connections
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

Starting with the triple
[(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi,h)]
in
\mathcal{M}
, the associated Higgs bundle
\big[\big(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi\big)\big]
is obtained by forgetting the harmonic metric
h
. Starting with the triple
\big[\big(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi,h\big)\big]
, for each
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, the associated flat connection is
[\nabla_{\zeta}]
where

\displaystyle\nabla_{\zeta}=\zeta\varphi+D_{(\overline{\partial}_{E},h)}+\zeta^{-1}\varphi^{*_{h}}.

(1.2)

The nonabelian Hodge correspondence describes the correspondence between solutions of Hitchin’s equations, Higgs bundles, and flat connections. It answers questions that include “What Higgs bundles admit harmonic metrics?” and “Can any flat connection be produced in this way?”

What Higgs bundles
({\mathcal{E}},\varphi)
admit harmonic metrics
h
? The following algebraic stability condition guarantees the
existence
of a harmonic metric. Moreover, any harmonic metric on an indecomposable Higgs bundle is
unique
up to rescaling by a constant. We define stability for holomorphic bundles, before generalizing it to Higgs bundles.

Definition 1.9
.

A holomorphic bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is
stable
if for every proper holomorphic subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
, the slopes
\mu(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{\deg\mathcal{F}}{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{F}}

satisfy

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{E}).

Definition 1.10
.

A Higgs bundle
({\mathcal{E}},\varphi)
is
stable
if for every
\varphi
-invariant proper holomorphic subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
, the slopes satisfy

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{E}).

A Higgs bundle
({\mathcal{E}},\varphi)
is
polystable
if it is the direct sum of stable Higgs bundles of the same slope.

Theorem 1.11

(
[
22
,
35
]
)
.

A Higgs bundle admits a harmonic metric if, and only if, it is polystable.

The nonabelian Hodge correspondence gives an equivalence between Higgs bundles, solutions of Hitchin’s equations, and flat connections. Admittedly, our presentation in this paper focuses on the equivalence between Higgs bundles and solutions of Hitchin’s equations, while neglecting flat connections. For more on the equivalence between flat connections and solutions of Hitchin’s equations, see, for example,

The hyperkähler structure of the Hitchin moduli space

•

There are many recent results about “branes” in Hitchin moduli space.

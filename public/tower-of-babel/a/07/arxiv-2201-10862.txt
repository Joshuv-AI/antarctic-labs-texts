Hopf-Galois Realizability of ℤ n ⋊ ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2} Thanks: The first named author is partially supported by the IISER Pune research fellowship and the second author has been partially supported by supported by NBHM fellowship.
Namrata Arvind and Saikat Panja

Abstract
. Let G G and N N be finite groups of order 2 ​ n 2n where n n is odd. We say the pair ( G , N ) (G,N) is Hopf-Galois realizable if G G is a regular subgroup of Hol ​ ( N ) = N ⋊ Aut ​ ( N ) \textup{Hol}(N)=N\rtimes\textup{Aut}(N) . In this article we give necessary conditions on G G (similarly N N ) when N N (similarly G G ) is a group of the form ℤ n ⋊ ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2} . Further we show that this condition is also sufficient if radical of n n is a Burnside number. This classifies all the skew braces which has the additive group (or the multiplicative group) to be isomorphic to ℤ n ⋊ ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2} , in this case.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hopf-Galois structures

Let
K/F
be a finite Galois field extension. An
F
-Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
, with an action on
K
such that
K
is an
H
-module algebra
and the action makes
K
into an
\mathcal{H}
-Galois extension, will be called a
Hopf-Galois structure

on
K/F
.
From
[
8
, Theorem 6.8]
, we have that if
K/F
be a Galois extension of fields and
G=\textup{Gal}(K/F)
, then there is a bijection between Hopf-Galois structures on
K/F
and regular subgroups
N
of
\textup{Perm}(G)
normalized by
\lambda(G)
where
\lambda
is the left regular representation.
In the proof of this theorem, given a regular subgroup
N\leq\textup{Perm}(G)
normalized by
\lambda(G)
, the Hopf-Galois structure on
K/F
corresponding to
N
is
K[N]^{G}
.
Here
G
acts on
N
by conjugation inside
\textup{Perm}(G)
and it acts on
K
by field automorphism, which induces an action of
G
on
K[N]
. This was further simplified by Nigel Byott in

[
2
, Proposition 1]
, which states that this is equivalent to having
G
as a regular subgroup of
\textup{Hol}(N)=N\rtimes\textup{Aut}(N)
. We are now ready to define Hopf-Galois realizability.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G,N
be two finite groups such that
|G|=|N|
. Then the pair
(G,N)
is called Hopf-Galois realizable if
G
is a regular subgroup of
\textup{Hol}(N)
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
be the cyclic group of order
2k
and
N=\mathcal{D}_{2k}
be the dihedral group of order
2k
. Then the pair
(G,N)
is Hopf-Galois realizable.

1.2.
Skew-braces

Definition 1.3
.

A left skew brace is a triple
(\Gamma,+,\circ)
where
(\Gamma,+),(\Gamma,\circ)
are groups and satisfy

a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)+a^{-1}+(a\circ c),

for all
a,b,c\in\Gamma
. The groups
(\Gamma,+),(\Gamma,\circ)
will be called the additive group and the multiplicative group respectively.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
G,N
be two finite groups such that
|G|=|N|
. Then the pair
(G,N)
is called skew brace realizable if the exists a skew brace

(\Gamma,+,\circ)
such that
(\Gamma,+)\cong N
and
(\Gamma,\circ)\cong G
.

Now given a skew brace
(\Gamma,+,\circ)
, we have two left regular representations:

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\textup{Perm}(\Gamma)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{\circ}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\textup{Perm}(\Gamma)

where
\lambda_{+}(g)(x)=g+x
and
\lambda_{\circ}(g)(x)=g\circ x
.
We will need
the following result which connects skew braces and regular subgroups.

Proposition 1.5
.

[
7
, Proposition 1.2]

(\Gamma,+,\circ)
is a skew brace if and only if the homomorphism

\lambda_{\circ}:(\Gamma,\circ)\longrightarrow\textup{Perm}(\Gamma)

has image in
\textup{Hol}(\Gamma,+)
.

Remark 1.6
.

The above proposition tells us that a pair of groups
(G,H)
(of the same order) is Hopf-Galois realizable if and only if it is skew brace realizable.
Thus from now on we will use the phrase ”realizability of a pair of groups” instead of Hopf-Galois (or skew brace) realizability.

1.3.
Results

It is known from
[
3
]
that if
G
is a finite simple group and
N
is finite group of same order as
G
, then the pair
(G,N)
is realizable if and only if
G\cong N
. This result was extended to a quasisimple groups in
[
15
]
. Also in
[
9
]
all Hopf-Galois structures on groups of order
p^{2}q
, with cyclic sylow
p
subgroup were classified. In
[
11
]
, the author gives a necessary condition on
G
if
(G,N)
is realizable for a cyclic
N
.
Fixing
G
to be cyclic, in
[
13
]
a complete characterization of
N
is given whenever
(G,N)
is realizable.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
be odd. In this article we first fix
G
to
be a group of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
classify all such
N
for which
(G,N)
is realizable. Turns out in
this case
N\cong H_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
H_{n}
is a
C-group (see section 2 for a definition of a C-group). Next we fix
N
to be a group of the form

\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and show that

G\cong H_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for a C-group
H_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}
, whenever
(G,N)
is
realizable. Further the converse of the above statements is also
true if radical of
n
is assumed to be a Burnside number. The
number of Hopf-Galois structures in these cases has been calculated in
[
1
]
.

We prove a group theoretic result and collect few known results in first part of section
2
.
In the second part, we mention the results for realizability of pairs
(G,N)
where one of the group is a Cyclic. Section
3
is concerned with proof of all the main results.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Group Theoretic Results

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a groups of order
2n
such that
n
is odd. Then
G
has a unique subgroup of order
n
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
be an element of order
2
(this exists by Cauchy’s theorem). Consider the following compositions of the maps:

\displaystyle G\xrightarrow{\lambda}\textup{Perm}(G)\xrightarrow{\text{sgn}}\{\pm 1\},

where
\lambda
represents the left regular representation and
sgn
is the sign representation of symmetric group.
Note that
\lambda(g)=(a_{1},ga_{1})(a_{2},ga_{2})\ldots(a_{n},ga_{n})
for
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in G
such that

g^{k}a_{l}\neq a_{m}
for all
k,l,m
. Since
n
is odd, we have that
\text{sgn}(\lambda(g))=-1
and hence
\text{sgn}\circ\lambda
is surjective. Thus

H=\ker(\text{sgn}\circ\lambda)
is a subgroup of order
n
.

To prove that
H
is unique subgroup of order
n
, on contrary assume that there is another subgroup
H^{\prime}\neq H
of order
n
. Then consider the following group homomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:G\longrightarrow(G/H)\oplus(G/H^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Since
H\neq H^{\prime}
, we have that
G
surjects into
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then
4
divides the order of the group, which is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group of order
2^{k}n
such that
2\not|n
. If the Sylow-2-subgroup of
G
is cyclic then
G
has unique subgroups of order
2^{l}n
for all
0\leq l\leq k
.

Proof.

Follows by induction, Proposition
2.1
and observing that subgroups of cyclic groups are cyclic.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

A finite group
G
is called a
C-group
if all the Sylow subgroups are cyclic. The group
G
is called
almost Sylow-cyclic
if
its Sylow subgroups of odd order are cyclic, while either the Sylow-2-subgroup is trivial or they contain a cyclic subgroup of index
2
.

We have the following theorem from Burnside’s work.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
5
]

Let
G
be a finite group. Then all the Sylow subgroups are cyclic if and only if
G
is a semidirect product of two cyclic groups of coprime order.

2.2.
Realizability results in case of Cyclic groups

The notion of bijective crossed homomorphism to study Hopf-Galois module was introduced by Cindy Tsang in the paper
[
14
]
.
For a map
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(G,\textup{Aut}(N))
, a map
\mathfrak{g}\in\text{Map}(G,N)
is said to be a crossed homomorphism with respect to
\mathfrak{f}
if

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(ab)=\mathfrak{g}(a)\mathfrak{f}(a)(\mathfrak{g}(b))\text{ for all }a,b\in G.

We set

\displaystyle Z_{\mathfrak{f}}^{1}(G,N)=\{\mathfrak{g}:\mathfrak{g}\text{ is bijective crossed homomorphism w.r.t }\mathfrak{f}\}.

Then we have the following.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
14
, Proposition 2.1]

The regular subgroups of
\textup{Hol}(N)
isomorphic to
G
are precisely the subsets of
\textup{Hol}(N)
of the form

\displaystyle\{(\mathfrak{g}(a),\mathfrak{f}(a)):a\in G\},

where
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(G,\textup{Aut}(N)),\mathfrak{g}\in Z_{\mathfrak{f}}^{1}(G,N)
.

Using this it has been proved that if
(G,N)
is realizable, then

(1)

if
G
is simple then
N
is simple,

(2)

if
G
is quasisimple then
N
is quasisimple,

(3)

if
G
is almost simple then
N
is almost simple.

To study the realizability of pair of groups
(G,N)
it is often useful to use charcteristic subgroups of
N
. A useful result in this direction is given by the following.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
G,N
be two groups such that
|G|=|N|
. Let
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(G,\textup{Aut}(N))
and
\mathfrak{g}\in Z_{\mathfrak{f}}^{1}
be a bijective crossed homomorphism (i.e.
(G,N)
is realizable). Then if
M
is a characteristic subgroup of
N
and
H=\mathfrak{g}^{-1}(M)
, we have
that the pair
(H,M)
is realizable.

In recent work realizability of Cyclic groups has been characterized by the following two results. We mention them here as this will be the key ingredient to classify the same in case of Dihedral groups.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
N
be a group of odd order
n
such that the pair
(\mathbb{Z}_{n},N)
is realizable. Then
N
is a
C
-group.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
11
, Theorem 1]

Let
G
be a group of order
n
such that
(G,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is realizable. Then
G
is solvable and almost Sylow-cyclic.

3.
Main Results

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
N=\mathcal{D}_{2n}
and
(G,N)
is realizable. Then
G
is solvable.

Proof.

The non-trivial proper characteristic subgroups of
\mathcal{D}_{2n}=\langle r,s:r^{n}=s^{2}=srsr=1\rangle
are given by

\displaystyle\langle r^{d}\rangle,\text{ where }d|n.

Then for all
\alpha|n
the group
G
has a subgroup of order
\alpha
, say
H_{\alpha}
such that
(H_{\alpha},\langle r^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}\rangle)
is realizable. Since
G
has order
2n
and
H_{n}
is a subgroup of index
2
, then
H_{n}
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Let
n=p_{1}^{\beta_{1}}p_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\ldots p_{k}^{\beta_{k}}
. Now consider the series

\displaystyle G\geq H_{n}\geq H_{n/p_{1}}\geq H_{n/p_{1}^{2}}\geq\ldots\geq H_{n/p_{1}^{\beta_{1}}p_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\ldots p_{k}^{\beta_{k}-1}}\geq 1.

Then each of
H_{\alpha}/H_{\alpha/p_{j}}
is cyclic, hence abelian.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
N
be a group of order
2n
, where
n
is odd and the pair
(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2},N)
is realizable. Then
N\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{l})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
where
(k,l)=1,lk=n
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
,
N
has a unique and hence characteristic subgroup
H_{n}
of order
n
. Then by Proposition
2.5
there exists a bijective crossed homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\in Z^{1}_{\mathfrak{f}}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2},N)
for some
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2},\textup{Aut}(N))
.
Hence by Theorem
2.6
the pair
(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}H_{n},H_{n})
is realizable. Note that
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has unique subgroup of order
n
, which is cyclic.
It follows that
\mathfrak{g}^{-1}H_{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. This implies that
(\mathbb{Z}_{n},H_{n})
is realizable. Hence by Proposition
2.7
we get that
H_{n}
is a
C
-group, whence it follows from
[
5
]
that
H_{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{l}
for
(k,l)=1,kl=n
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group of order
2n
such that the pair
(G,\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is realizable. Then
G=(\mathbb{Z}_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{l})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{l}
for some
(k,l)=1,kl=n
.

Proof.

Given that the pair
(G,\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is realizable, by Proposition
2.5
there exists a bijective
crossed homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\in Z^{1}_{\mathfrak{f}}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2})
for some
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(G,\textup{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}))
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a characteristic subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we get that
\mathfrak{g}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is a subgroup
of
G
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is realizable. Then by Proposition
2.8
, we have that

\mathfrak{g}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is almost Sylow-cylic. Hence by
[
5
]

\mathfrak{g}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\mathbb{Z}_{k}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{l}
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
n
be an odd number such that
\mathfrak{R}(n)
is a Burnside number. Assume that
|G|=|N|=2n
and
(G,N)
is realizable. Then
G=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if and only if
N=\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
be two numbers such that
(r_{1},r_{2})=1
and
\mathfrak{R}(r_{1}r_{2})
is a Burnside number. Take
i\neq j
and
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. Then the group homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\mathbb{Z}_{r_{i}}\rightarrow\textup{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{r_{j}})

is trivial, which shows that
\mathbb{Z}_{r_{i}}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{r_{j}}=\mathbb{Z}_{r_{i}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{r_{j}}=\mathbb{Z}_{r_{1}r_{2}}
. Hence the result follows from previous two Theorems.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

It was shown in
[
1
]
, that if
G=\mathcal{D}_{2n}
then for any
\phi\in\textup{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\textup{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}))
the pair

(\mathcal{D}_{2n},\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is realizable. This along with
the previous theorem implies that when
\mathfrak{R}(n)
is a Burnside
number, these are the all possible realizable pairs.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension with Galois group isomorphic to

\mathcal{D}_{2n}
where
n
is odd such that
\mathfrak{R}(n)
is a Burnside number. Then the
number of Hopf-Galois structures on
L/K
is

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{D}_{2n})=\displaystyle{\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}2^{m}\chi(n-m)},

where
\chi(w)
is the coefficient of
x^{w}
in the polynomial
\prod\limits_{p_{u}\in\pi(n)}(x+p_{u}^{\alpha_{u}})
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
3.4
and
[
1
, Corollary 4.1]
. Then the result follows from Theorem
3.4
and Remark
3.5
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\equiv 2\pmod{4}
. Suppose
(G,\mathcal{D}_{2n})
is realizable. Then there exists a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{l}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{k}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\longrightarrow 0,

for some
(k,l)=1,kl=2n
.

Proof.

Given that the pair
(G,\mathcal{D}_{2n})
is realizable, we get that there exists
\mathfrak{f}\in\textup{Hom}(G,\textup{Aut}(\mathcal{D}_{2n}))
and
\mathfrak{g}\in Z_{\mathfrak{f}}^{1}(G,\mathcal{D}_{2n})

corresponding to the regular embedding of
G
in
\textup{Hol}(\mathcal{D}_{2n})
. Take
M=\langle r\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2n}
, which is a characteristic subgroup of
\mathcal{D}_{2n}
.
Then the pair
(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is realizable. Hence we have that
\mathfrak{g}^{-1}M\cong\mathbb{Z}_{l}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{k}
for some
(k,l)=1,kl=2n
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}^{-1}M
is a subgroup of index
2
, the result follows.
∎

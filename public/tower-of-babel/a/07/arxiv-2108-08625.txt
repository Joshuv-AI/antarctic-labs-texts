Inner functions, invariant subspaces and cyclicity in 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) -spaces
Adem Limani and Bartosz Malman

Abstract
We study the invariant subspaces generated by inner functions for a class of 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) -spaces which can be identified as spaces of analytic functions in the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , where μ \mu is a measure supported in the closed unit disk and 𝒫 t ​ ( μ ) \mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu) is the span of analytic polynomials in the usual Lebesgue space L t ​ ( μ ) L^{t}(\mu) . Our measures define a range of spaces somewhere in between the Hardy and the Bergman spaces, and our results are thus a mixture of results from these two theories. For a large class of measures μ \mu we characterize the cyclic inner functions, and exhibit some interesting properties of invariant subspaces generated by non-cyclic inner functions. Our study is motivated by a connection with the problem of smooth approximations in de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.



1 Introduction

Let
t>0
and
\mu
be a compactly supported positive finite Borel measure in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
the closure of the analytic polynomials in the usual Lebesgue space
L^{t}(\mu)
. The classical Hardy spaces
H^{t}
can be defined as the closure of analytic polynomials in
L^{t}(d\textit{m})
, where
d\textit{m}
is the Lebesgue measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. The Bergman spaces can likewise be defined as the closures of analytic polynomials in spaces
L^{t}(dA)
, where
dA
is the area measure on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In both cases, it so happens that these closures can be identified with spaces of analytic functions, but for general measures
\mu
this is no longer true. For instance, if
\mu
is a measure supported on
\mathbb{T}
and contains a singular part
\mu_{s}
, then
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
contains
L^{2}(\mu_{s})
as a direct summand, which is certainly not a space of analytic functions. For absolutely continuous measures supported on
\mathbb{T}
this phenomenon is explained by a famous theorem of Szegő. Namely, if
\omega
is a positive integrable function on
\mathbb{T}
, then the space
L^{2}(\omega d\textit{m})
can be identified with a space of analytic functions if and only if
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(\omega)d\textit{m}>-\infty
. Moreover, if the log-integrability condition fails, then we actually have that
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\omega dm)=L^{2}(\omega dm)
.

In the present paper, we shall introduce a class of measures
\mu
supported on the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
for which the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can be identified with a sufficiently well-behaved space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. We will stay within the realm of Bergman-type spaces, but we will be working with spaces considerably larger than the usual Hardy spaces. Our purpose is to study subspaces invariant for the forward shift operator
\mathcal{M}_{z}:f(z)\to zf(z)
which are generated by inner functions. Our work is motivated by a connection with certain approximation problems in the theory of de Branges-Rovnyak spaces which we will briefly explain below.

In the case
t=2
, the spaces
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
and the shift operator
\mathcal{M}_{z}
are universal models for the class of subnormal operators (see
[
5
]
). A subnormal operator is the restriction to an invariant subspace of a normal operator on a Hilbert space, and any such operator which also has a cyclic vector is unitarily equivalent to
\mathcal{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
for some measure
\mu
supported in the complex plane. The spaces
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
which will fall into our framework here will be the ones on which
M_{z}
acts as a
completely non-isometric
contraction, in the sense that the ability to identify our spaces as spaces of analytic functions is equivalent to non-existence of invariant subspaces of
M_{z}
on which it acts as an isometry.

We will now survey a number of results on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-spaces as a way of justifying our choice of the class of measures
\mu
which will be presented below. If
\mu
is supported on the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then a necessary condition for
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
to be a space of analytic functions is that the space contains no characteristic functions
1_{F}
for any measurable subset
F\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. This property is sometimes called
irreducibility
, as it was for instance in
[
3
]
. The case we will be studying is when the restriction
\mu|\mathbb{D}
of
\mu
to the open disk
\mathbb{D}
already makes
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})
into a space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the possibility to identify
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
with a space of analytic functions is equivalent to non-existence of a sequence of analytic polynomials
p_{n}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|p_{n}|^{t}d\mu|\mathbb{D}=0

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|p_{n}-1_{F}|^{t}d\mu|\mathbb{T}=0

for some measurable subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{T}
,
\mu|\mathbb{T}
being the restriction of
\mu
to
\mathbb{T}
. We will see that this condition is equivalent to
M_{z}:\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)\to\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
admitting no invariant subspace on which it acts isometrically. In the literature, the phenomenon above is referred to as
splitting
(at least when
F=\mathbb{T}
). Vast amount of research has been carried out investigating when splitting can occur, but the exact mechanism is still not understood. For instance, splitting has been shown to occur if the measure
\mu|\mathbb{D}
decays extremely fast close to the boundary and
\mu|\mathbb{T}=\omega d\textit{m}
with
\log(\omega)\not\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, see for instance
[
14
]
. However, there exists an example in
[
11
]
which produces a compact subset
F
of
\mathbb{T}
such that
\mathcal{P}^{2}(dA+1_{F}d\textit{m})
splits, even though, clearly, the measure
dA
does not decay at all. An important result is
[
14
, Theorem D]
: if
\mu|\mathbb{D}
does not decay too fast and if
\mu|\mathbb{T}=\omega d\textit{m}
with
\int_{I}\log(\omega)d\textit{m}>-\infty
for some circular arc
I
, then the situation above, with the two limits simultaneously being zero, cannot occur for any Borel set
F
contained in
I
. Thus in this case, if
\omega
is supported on
I
, then
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mu)
becomes a space of analytic functions.

The weight
\omega
will be living on sets more general than intervals, as in the already cited result from
[
14
]
. For a closed set
E
on
\mathbb{T}
, we consider the complement
\mathbb{T}\setminus E=\cup_{k}A_{k}
, where
A_{k}
are disjoint open circular arcs. The set
E
is a
Beurling-Carleson set
if the following condition is satisfied:

\displaystyle\sum_{k}|A_{k}|\log\left(\frac{1}{|A_{k}|}\right)<\infty.

(1)

Notation
|A_{k}|
stands for the Lebesgue measure of the arc
A_{k}
.
The sets satisfying (
1
) will appear in two contexts below, and we will need to consider both Beurling-Carleson of positive Lebesgue measure, and of zero Lebesgue measure. Based on the discussion and results stated above, we will introduce the following conditions on the positive finite measure
\mu
supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

(A)

The measure
d\mu|\mathbb{T}=\omega d\textit{m}
is absolutely continuous and non-zero only on a set
E
which can be written as a countable union
E=\cup_{k}E_{k}
of Beurling-Carleson sets of positive measure for which we have that

\int_{E_{k}}\log(\omega)d\textit{m}>-\infty.

(B)

The measure
\mu|\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson and a reverse Carleson measure for some standard weighted Bergman spaces, and thus satisfies the following Carleson embeddings:

\mathcal{P}^{t}\left((1-|z|)^{\beta}dA\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{t}\left((1-|z|)^{\alpha}dA\right)

for some
-1<\beta\leq\alpha
.

The condition (A) is present primarily to make
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
into a space of analytic functions, but is alone not sufficient without some assumption on
\mu|\mathbb{D}
, and indeed it is an interplay between the assumption (A) and (B) that ensures that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a space of analytic functions. On the other hand, a remarkable result by Khrushchev says that if
\omega
is concentrated on a set which contains no Beurling-Carleson set of positive measure, then
\mathcal{P}^{t}(d\mu|\mathbb{D}+\omega dm)
splits for any standard weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})
(see
[
12
]
, Theorem 4.1). This indicates that Beurling-Carleson sets of positive measure are the appropriate carriers of weights on the unit circle in order to ensure
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
being a space of analytic functions contained in the standard weighted Bergman spaces.
Beyond this first condition, we shall also implicitly assume that
\log(\omega)
is not integrable on the entire unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, since integrability of this quantity leads to a space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
which is essentially a Hardy space. The key point of assumption (B) is that it is readily satisfied by any standard Bergman weight
d\mu|\mathbb{D}=(1-|z|)^{\alpha}dA
, for
\alpha>-1
, and the reader can think of
\mu|\mathbb{D}
as such a weight. Our techniques allow however to replace the standard Bergman weight by such a two-sided Carleson measure, which is a somewhat more general condition.

We move on to the presentation of our main results. As a starting point, we must of course establish that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can genuinely be identified as a space of analytic functions. This is a consequence of ?THM?
which is proved in Section
2
. In fact, as soon as this is established, this result can be used in combination with the work of Aleman, Richter and Sundberg in
[
3
]
to infer further properties of the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. This combination establishes the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu=\mu|\mathbb{D}+\mu|\mathbb{T}
be a positive finite measure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
satisfying the assumptions
(A)
and
(B)
. For any
t\in(0,\infty)
, the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is a genuine space of analytic functions: the restriction
f|\mathbb{D}
of any element
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
and
f|\mathbb{D}
extends uniquely to the element
f
in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
. The space satisfies the following additional properties:

(i)

Each
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
has non-tangential limits
\mu|\mathbb{T}
-almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.

(ii)

The index of each
M_{z}
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is equal to 1.

The
index
notion in the theorem refers to the co-dimension of the image of
M_{z}
when restricted to an invariant subspace. Our main motivation for this work was the study of the closed
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-spaces generated by inner functions. For a positive singular measure
\nu
supported on
\mathbb{T}
, the
singular inner function

S_{\nu}
is defined by the formula

S_{\nu}(z)=\exp\Big(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}d\mu(\zeta)\Big).

(2)

A general inner function
\theta
is the product
\theta=BS_{\nu}
of a singular inner function
S_{\nu}
and a convergent Blaschke product
B
of the form

B(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{a_{n}}\frac{a_{n}-z}{1-\overline{a_{n}}z}.

One of the main questions we want to answer is: for which inner functions
\theta
are the polynomial multiples of
\theta
dense in the space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
? We compare to the situation in Hardy and Bergman spaces. A classical result of operator theory characterizes the
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces of the Hardy space
H^{p}
with
0<p<\infty
as subspaces of the form
\theta H^{2}
. In particular, every inner function
\theta
generates an invariant subspace properly contained in
H^{p}
. A description of invariant subspaces generated by inner functions exists also for the Bergman spaces, and it involves the notion Beurling-Carleson sets of zero measure, defined as above in (
1
). Every positive singular measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{T}
can be uniquely decomposed into a sum

\nu=\nu_{\mathcal{C}}+\nu_{\mathcal{K}}

(3)

of mutually orthogonal measures where
\nu_{\mathcal{C}}
is concentrated on a countable union of Beurling-Carlesons sets of measure zero, while
\nu_{\mathcal{K}}
vanishes on every such Beurling-Carleson set. For a measure
\nu
, by
concentrated on a set
F
, we mean that
\mu(B)=\mu(B\cap F)
for every Borel set
B
. The decomposition in (
3
) corresponds to a factorization
S_{\nu}=S_{\nu_{\mathcal{C}}}S_{\nu_{\mathcal{K}}}
. We will call
\nu_{\mathcal{C}}
for the
Beurling-Carleson
part of
\nu
and
\nu_{\mathcal{K}}
for the
Korenblum-Roberts
part of
\nu
, with similar designations for the singular inner functions constructed from these measures. The works of Roberts in
[
17
]
and Korenblum in
[
13
]
independently establish that the singular inner function
S_{\nu_{\mathcal{K}}}
is a cyclic vector for the
M_{z}
-operator on the standard weighted Bergman spaces, while
S_{\nu_{\mathcal{C}}}
generates a proper invariant subspace. The subspace generated by a non-cyclic inner function satisfies a very special property which we will below denote by (P) and describe next.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{+}
denote the Smirnov class
f=u/v
of quotients of bounded analytic functions, with
v
being outer (see
[
7
]
). Consider a pair
(X,\theta)
, where

is a space of analytic function on

which contains bounded functions and on which the

operator acts continuously, and

be an inner function. Let
[\theta]_{X}
be the smallest closed

-invariant subspace of

.

(P)

The invariant subspace
[\theta]_{X}
satisfies
[\theta]_{X}\cap\mathcal{N}^{+}\subseteq\theta\mathcal{N}^{+}
.

In other words, if
\theta h_{n}
converges to

in the topology of

h_{n}
are polynomials and

is contained in

, then
f/\theta\in\mathcal{N}^{+}

of the inner factor has appeared implicitly in less general setting in the work of Roberts in
[

]
. When

is a standard weighted Bergman space, then then property (P) for

is actually equivalent to density of classes of functions with some degree of regularity on

in the classical model spaces
K_{\theta}:=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
. Indeed, in ?THM?
below, this permanence property for measures

satisfying (B) and Beurling-Carleson inner functions is deduced from the density result of

]

To further illustrate the importance of the concept, we note that the fundamental and useful fact that the functions with continuous extensions to

are dense in model spaces, established by Aleksandrov in
[
1
]
, can be related to the permanence of the inner factor under weak-star convergence in the space of Cauchy transforms. The density of the same class of functions in de Branges-Rovnyak spaces

can be tied to a similar phenomenon. Establishing such corresponding properties for

-spaces is related instead to approximations by functions with certain degree of smoothness in

Our results are of the kind discussed in the above paragraph. Before stating them we will need to set some further notations and conventions. The symbols

The following theorem establishes the inner factor permanence property (P) for a large class of inner functions in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and
\mu

be an inner function. Let

The difference from the Bergman space case is that in our setting, the singular inner function constructed from the measure

Next, we present our result on cyclicity of inner functions in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

Combining ?THM?

Corollary 1.4
.

Let

and

. Let

(i)

If

(ii)

Note that the result allows us to decompose every inner function into two factors, one satisfying the property (P), and one being cyclic. In particular, one easily deduces from the above that the invariant subspace generated by a bounded analytic function

2 Assumption (A), (B), and irreducibility of the shift

The below proposition is a version of a deep result on
simultaneous approximation
by Khrushchev, found in
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
. We shall introduce an alternate and minimal version of the result which is suitable for our further applications. We also give a sketch of the proof, which is essentially a minor modification of the original argument in
[
12
]
. The proposition will be our principal technical tool in establishing that
M_{z}:\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)\to\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is completely non-isometric if
\mu
satisfies assumptions (A) and (B). In this section, we allow
t
to be any finite positive number.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mu|\mathbb{T}=\omega d\textit{m}
be a measure satisfying condition (A) and which is concentrated on a single Beurling-Carleson set
E
of positive measure. Let
\mu|\mathbb{D}
be a measure satisfying the condition (B). Assume that there exists a sequence of analytic polynomials
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, an analytic function
f\in\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})
and
g\in L^{t}(\omega d\textit{m})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|p_{n}-f|^{t}d\mu|\mathbb{D}=0

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|p_{n}-g|^{t}\omega d\textit{m}=0.

If
g
is non-zero, then
f
is non-zero.

Proof.

As indicated above, we will follow closely the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
. There, an auxiliary domain
G
is constructed. If
\mathbb{T}\setminus E=\cup_{n}A_{n}
are the complementary arcs to
E
, we associate to each arc
A_{n}
its usual Carleson square
C_{n}
, and then the domain
G
is obtained by removing the squares
C_{n}
from
\mathbb{D}
. It is not difficult to see that the boundary
\partial G
is a rectifiable curve, and that
\partial G\cap\mathbb{T}=E
. Let
\nu
be the harmonic measure on
\partial G
associated with the point
0\in G
. Since assumption (B) is satisfied and thus
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})
is continuously contained in a standard weighted Bergman space, it follows that we can find constants
C,K>0
, independent of
n
, such that

|p_{n}(z)|\leq C\|p_{n}\|_{\mu_{\mathbb{D}},t}(1-|z|)^{-K}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\partial G}\log^{+}|p_{n}|d\nu=\int_{\partial G\cap\mathbb{D}}\log^{+}(|p_{n}|)d\nu+\int_{E}\log^{+}(|p_{n}|)d\nu

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{\partial G\cap\mathbb{D}}\log^{+}\Big(\frac{1}{1-|z|}\Big)d\nu+\int_{E}\log^{+}(|p_{n}|)d\nu.

(4)

The main technical difficulty of the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
is using the assumption that

is a Beurling-Carleson in order to show that the integral over
\partial G\cap\mathbb{D}
is finite. For the proof of this fact, we refer the reader to the cited paper. Proceeding, assumption (A) ensures that there exists an outer function

which satisfies
|h|^{t}=\omega
on

, and from this we obtain

\int_{E}\log^{+}(|p_{n}|)d\nu\lesssim\int_{E}\log^{+}(|p_{n}h|)d\nu+\int_{E}\log^{+}(|h|^{-1})d\nu.

(5)

We will now show that the Lebesgue measure
dm
dominates
d\nu
on
E
. For any positive continuous function
\psi
on
\mathbb{T}
, let
P\psi
denote its Poisson extension to
\mathbb{D}
, and note that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi d\textit{m}=P\psi(0)=\int_{\partial G}P\psi d\nu\geq\int_{E}P\psi d\nu=\int_{E}\psi d\nu.

Thus
d\textit{m}-d\nu|E
is a non-negative measure, and so (
5

\int_{E}\log^{+}(|p_{n}|)d\nu\lesssim 1+\int_{E}|p_{n}|^{t}\omega d\textit{m}+\int_{E}|\log\omega|d\textit{m}

(6)

3 Cauchy duals of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-spaces

Let
\mu
be a positive measure supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
G\in L^{1}(\mu)
. The Cauchy transform
C_{G\mu}
is the analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
given by the formula

C_{G\mu}(z)=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{G(\zeta)d\mu(\zeta)}{1-\overline{\zeta}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The following realization of the dual space of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
is particularly well-suited for our purposes. Here and throughout this section
t\in[1,\infty)
and
t^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate exponent such that
1/t+1/{t^{\prime}}=1
, of course interpreting
t^{\prime}
as infinity if

.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
g\in Hol(\mathbb{D})
and

be a polynomial. Then

\Lambda_{g}(f):=\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(r\zeta)\overline{g(r\zeta)}d\textit{m}(\zeta)

(8)

extends to a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
if and only if
g
is of the form
g=C_{G\mu}
for some
G\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mu)
. If this is the case, then we have that

\left\lvert\Lambda_{g}(f)\right\rvert=\inf\{\|G\|_{\mu,t^{\prime}}:g=C_{G\mu}\}.

(9)

Moreover, every bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can be represented in this way.

Proof.

First assume that
g
equals the Cauchy transform
C_{G\mu}
with
G\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mu)
. If
f
is a polynomial, then in particular
f(rz)\to f(z)
uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
as
r\to 1-
, and from this it follows that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{g}(f)=\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(r\zeta)\overline{g(r\zeta)}d\textit{m}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}f(r\zeta)\frac{\overline{G(z)}}{1-r\overline{\zeta}z}d\mu(z)d\textit{m}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\overline{G(z)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(r\zeta)}{1-r\overline{\zeta}z}dm(\zeta)\Big)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\overline{G(z)}f(r^{2}z)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\overline{G(z)}f(z)d\mu(z).

This shows that any
g=C_{G\mu}
with

induces a bounded linear functional
\Lambda_{g}
, with

\left\lvert\Lambda_{g}(f)\right\rvert\leq\|G\|_{\mu,t^{\prime}}\|f\|_{\mu,t}.

Conversely, given any bounded linear functional
\Lambda
on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, the Hahn-Banach theorem and the usual duality between

and
L^{t^{\prime}}(\mu)
implies that

can be represented in the form

\Lambda(f)=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}f(z)\overline{G(z)}d\mu(z)

for some
G\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mu)
. Following the steps backward in the above computation, we easily deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}f(z)\overline{G(z)}d\mu(z)=\lim_{r\to 1-}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(r\zeta)\overline{\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{G(z)}{1-r\overline{z}\zeta}d\mu(z)}\,d\textit{m}(\zeta).

Thus
\Lambda(f)=\Lambda_{g}(f)
, with

. The norm equality in (
9
) follows in a standard way.
∎

Our assumption (B) on the measure
\mu|\mathbb{D}
implies that functions with sufficiently smooth extensions to the boundary correspond to bounded linear functionals on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})
under Cauchy duality. Consequently, they are also bounded on the smaller space
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
(if the latter is a space of analytic functions). This observation is the content of our next corollary. For
\tau>0
, let
\mathcal{H}^{\tau}
denote the so-called analytic Sobolev spaces:

\mathcal{H}^{\tau}=\{f\in Hol(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\tau}}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)}^{\tau}|f_{n}|^{2}<\infty\}.

(10)

It is easy to see that given any positive integer
N
, there exists a
\tau>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{\tau}
will consist entirely of functions for which the first
N
derivatives extend continuously to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
.

Recall the quantity

defined in property (B).

Corollary 3.2
.

If

satisfies the second of the embeddings in (B), then for all

with

is bounded on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu|\mathbb{D})

there exists a solution

to the equation

Proof.

If

, then a straightforward computation gives the following estimate for the Taylor coefficients of

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{f_{n}}{(n+1)^{1+\alpha}}\Big|\simeq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{D}}f\overline{z^{n}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\Big|\leq C\|f\|_{\mu|\mathbb{D},t},

where

is independent of

and

\frac{|f_{n}|^{2}}{(n+1)^{2+2\alpha}}\leq C^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{\mu|\mathbb{D},t}.

In particular, this implies

Consequently, the limit in

The existence of a solution

is now an immediate consequence of ?THM?
.

F_{p}\in K_{\theta}
, so

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\theta vp_{n}\overline{F_{p}}d\textit{m}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{F_{p}}(\theta vp_{n})=\Lambda_{F_{p}}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}u\overline{F_{p}}d\textit{m}.

By density of the functions
F_{p}
inside
K_{\theta}
, we deduce that
u\in(K_{\theta})^{\perp}=\theta H^{2}
, and consequently
f/\theta\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
.
∎

It follows easily from ?THM?
that the permanence property holds for general measure
\mu|\mathbb{T}
concentrated on a countable union
\cup_{k}E_{k}
of Beurling-Carleson sets
E_{k}
of positive measure, and
\theta
being an inner function with corresponding singular measure concentrated on
\cup_{k}E_{k}
. Indeed, if
\theta=BS_{\nu}
, then we simply apply the above proposition to
\theta_{E_{k}}=BS_{\nu|E_{k}}
, where
\nu|E_{k}
is the restriction of
\nu
to the set
E_{k}
, for each
k
. This observation completes the proof of ?THM?
of Section
1
.

5 Cyclic inner functions in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)

In this final section, we will establish our results on cyclicity. Here we may assume that
t\in(0,\infty)
. The notation
\|f\|_{\mu,t}
should in case
t<1
be interpreted in the usual way which makes it into a translation-invariant metric on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
, thus for any
t>0
, we have

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mu,t}=\left(\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left\lvert f(z)\right\rvert^{t}d\mu(z)\right)^{\min\{1,1/t\}}.

The relevant assumptions on
\mu
that we shall need in this section are that
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
can be identified as a space of analytic functions and that condition (B) holds. In fact, we may actually relax condition (B) slightly, by requiring that the measure
\mu|\mathbb{D}
is such that the norms of monomials do not decrease too slowly: There exists a constant
\beta>0
, such that

(B’)

\left\lVert z^{n}\right\rVert_{\mu|\mathbb{D},t}\lesssim n^{-\beta}

for all positive integers
n
. Our main task in this section is to prove ?THM?
.
The idea behind the proof is very much inspired by the beautiful techniques of Roberts
[
17
]
, which establishes cyclicity of Korenblum-Roberts inner functions in the case of Bergman-type spaces. However, due to the fact that our measure
\mu
lives on
\mathbb{T}
, these techniques require certain adaptations to our setting. In this section, we shall denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of analytic polynomials and fix
\rho
to be the distance between the closed sets
\text{supp}({\nu})
and
F:=\text{supp}({\mu|\mathbb{T}})
, where
\nu
is a Korenblum-Roberts measure concentrated on the complement of
F
. The following observation will allow us to assume that
\rho>0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
S_{\nu}
be a singular inner function as in the statement of ?THM?
. Let
\nu_{n}
be the restriction of
\nu
to the set
\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}:\text{dist}(\zeta,F)>1/n\}
and
S_{\nu_{n}}
be the corresponding inner function. If
S_{\nu_{n}}
is a cyclic vector in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
for all sufficiently large
n\geq 1
, then so is
S_{\nu}
.

Proof.

For any analytic polynomial
p
and integer
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{\nu}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{\nu}p-S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}\right\rVert_{\mu,t}+\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert S_{\nu_{n}}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}+\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}.

By our assumption, we can for any sufficiently large
n\geq 1
find an analytic polynomial
p_{n}
making the quantity
\left\lVert S_{\nu_{n}}p_{n}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}
arbitrarily small, thus

\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left\lVert S_{\nu}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}.

Now since
\nu
is a singular continuous measure, we have that
\nu_{n}\to\nu
in total variation norm, as
n\to\infty
. From this it follows that
S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}(z)\to 1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, we also have that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert 1-S_{(\nu-\nu_{n})}(\zeta)\right\rvert^{2}dm(\zeta)=2\left(1-e^{-(\nu-\nu_{n})(\mathbb{T})}\right)\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. This means that we can extract a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
with
n_{k}\to\infty
, such that
S_{(\nu-\nu_{n_{k}})}(\zeta)\to 1
for
m
-a.e every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. Now combining these observations and using the fact that
d\mu=d\mu\lvert\mathbb{D}+\omega dm
is a finite measure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu_{n_{k}})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}=0.

It follows now that
S_{\nu}
is a cyclic vector in
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.
∎

With ?THM?
at hand, we may fix
\rho>0
. Next we shall use a decomposition result for singular measures which implicitly appears in the work of Roberts in
[
17
]
. This result is also carefully treated in
[
10
]
, where Ivrii clarifies some technical points in the construction. Before we present this result we recall the notion of modulus of continuity of a positive measure
\nu
, defined by

\omega_{\nu}(\delta)=\sup_{|I|<\delta}\nu(I),

where the supremum is taken over all arcs
I\subset\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure
|I|<\delta
.

Proposition 5.2

(Roberts,
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a Korenblum-Roberts measure and let
N>1
be a positive integer and
c>0
be given. Then there exists a sequence of positive measures
\{\nu_{k}\}_{k}
, satisfying the following conditions on their modulus of continuity

\omega_{\nu_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{n_{k}}\right)\leq\frac{c\log(n_{k})}{n_{k}},\qquad n_{k}=2^{2^{N+k}},\,k=0,1,2,\dots,

such that
\nu
decomposes as

\nu=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu_{k}.

In the similar spirit as
[
17
]
, we will utilize the following quantitative form of the Corona theorem.

Lemma 5.3

(The Corona Theorem)
.

There exists a universal constant
K>0
, such that whenever
f_{1},f_{2}\in H^{\infty}
with
\|f_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, for
j=1,2
, and

\left\lvert f_{1}(z)\right\rvert+\left\lvert f_{2}(z)\right\rvert\geq\delta,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
0<\delta\leq 1/2
, then there exists functions
g_{1},g_{2}\in H^{\infty}
with
\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta^{-K}
, so that

f_{1}g_{1}+f_{2}g_{2}=1.

The point of using the Corona theorem is to find bounded analytic functions
g_{k}
, such that
S_{\nu_{k}}g_{k}-1
has very small
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-norm, where
\nu_{k}
is a singular measure appearing in the decomposition of Proposition
.

Our next quest is to establish that singular inner functions associated to singular measures
\nu_{k}
are bounded below on considerably large parts of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. To this end, we shall now fix
\nu
to be a Korenblum-Roberts measure with the property that the distance between
\text{supp}({\nu})
and
F:=\text{supp}({\mu|\mathbb{T}})
is
\rho>0
. Associated to
\nu
, we introduce the following sets

\Omega_{n}:=D_{n}\cup\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\text{dist}(z,\text{supp}({\nu}))\geq\rho/2\},

where
D_{n}
denotes the disc centered at origin of radius
1-1/n
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a constant
c_{1}>0
, such that whenever
\nu_{0}
is a proper restriction of the Korenblum-Roberts measure
\nu
and
n_{0}
is a sufficiently large positive integer such that
\omega_{\nu_{0}}(1/n_{0})\leq c\log(n_{0})/n_{0}
, for some
c>0
, then the associated singular inner function
S_{\nu_{0}}
satisfies

\left\lvert S_{\nu_{0}}(z)\right\rvert\geq n_{0}^{-cc_{1}},\quad z\in\Omega_{n_{0}}.

Proof.

The bound from below on
D_{n_{0}}
, for some
c_{1}>0
, follows from the assumption on the modulus of continuity of
\nu_{0}
and holds for any
n_{0}>2
, see
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
). The function
S_{\nu}
extends analytically across
\{z\in\mathbb{T}:\text{dist}(z,\text{supp}({\nu}))\geq\rho/2\}
with
|S_{\nu}|=1
there, and since
\nu_{0}
is a restriction of the measure
\nu
, we have
|S_{\nu_{0}}|\geq|S_{\nu}|
on
\mathbb{D}
. Thus if
n_{0}
is large enough, then
|S_{\nu_{0}}|>1/2
on
\Omega_{n_{0}}\setminus D_{n_{0}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

We shall now proceed by constructing an appropriate companion to the singular inner function
S_{\nu_{0}}
appearing in ?THM?
, which will allow us to apply the Corona theorem. Indeed, we shall construct a sequence of analytic functions in the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}
, which are bounded from below on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n}
, but have comparatively smaller
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
-norms. The lemma below is technical and revolves around choosing specific parameters in a careful way.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
c_{1}>0
denote the constant in ?THM?
,
K>0
denote the constant in the Corona theorem above and fix a small
c>0
, so that
3cc_{1}K<\beta
, where
\beta>0
is as in (B’). Then there exists a positive integer
N_{0}
and a sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq N_{0}}
inside the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}
, satisfying the estimates

(i)

\left\lvert f_{n}(z)\right\rvert\geq n^{-cc_{1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n},

(ii)

\left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq n^{-3cc_{1}K}.

Proof.

Note that according to assumption (B’) and
3cc_{1}K<\beta
, we see that for all sufficiently large
n
, the monomials
z^{n}
will satisfy the estimate

\left\lVert z^{n}\right\rVert_{\mu|\mathbb{D},t}\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-3cc_{1}K}.

(13)

Let
\sigma>0
be a constant to be specified later and define the outer function

h_{n}(z)=\exp\left(-\sigma n\log(n)\int_{F}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}dm(\zeta)\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
\left\lvert h_{n}\right\rvert=n^{-\sigma n}

m
-a.e on
F
and
\left\lvert h_{n}\right\rvert=1
on
\mathbb{T}\setminus F
. We now set
f_{n}(z):=z^{n}h_{n}(z)
and claim that
\{f_{n}\}
is our candidate, that will eventually satisfy both
(i)
and
(ii)
. In light of (
13
), we note that in order for
\{f_{n}\}
to satisfy
(ii)
, it suffices to find a positive integer
N_{0}
, such that

\left(\int_{F}\left\lvert h_{n}(\zeta)\right\rvert^{t}\omega(\zeta)dm(\zeta)\right)^{\min(1,1/t)}\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-3cc_{1}K},\quad n\geq N_{0}.

(14)

However, since
\left\lvert h_{n}\right\rvert=n^{-\sigma n}

m
-a.e on
F
and
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T},m)
, it readily follows that for any fixed
\sigma>0
, we can choose
N_{0}>0
(depending on
\sigma
) such that (
14
) holds for all
n\geq N_{0}
. It now remains to check that
\{f_{n}\}
also eventually satisfies
(i)
. Recall that by definition of
\rho>0
, every
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n}
must necessarily satisfy
\text{dist}(z,F)\geq\rho/2
, hence

-\log\left\lvert h_{n}(z)\right\rvert=\sigma n\log(n)\int_{F}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{\left\lvert\zeta-z\right\rvert^{2}}dm(\zeta)\leq\frac{8\sigma}{\rho^{2}}\log(n),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n}.

This implies that

\left\lvert f_{n}(z)\right\rvert=\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n}\left\lvert h_{n}(z)\right\rvert\geq(1-1/n)^{n}n^{-8\sigma/\rho^{2}}\geq 4^{-1}n^{-8\sigma/\rho^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n}.

In the last step, we used the elementary inequality
(1-1/n)^{n}\geq 1/4
, for all
n\geq 1
. Thus in order to satisfy condition
(i)
, we only need the simple inequality

n^{cc_{1}}\geq 4n^{8\sigma/\rho^{2}}

to hold for all large
n
. However, choosing a fixed
0<\sigma<cc_{1}\rho^{2}/8
, it follows that the inequality above is easily met, thus
(i)
holds for all sufficiently large
n
. Combining with our previous observations about
\{h_{n}\}
eventually satisfying (
14
), we conclude that there exists an integer
N_{0}>0
, such that the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq N_{0}}
belongs to the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}
and satisfies both conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
.
∎

We now turn to the final proof of ?THM?
. We shall follow the ideas in
[
17
]
rather closely, but there are some minor differences, mainly attributed to the presence of ?THM?
and the fact that a part of our measure
\mu
lives on
\mathbb{T}
. For the reader’s convenience, we shall include a proof.

Proof of ?THM?
.

Recall the definition of
\rho
as the distance between
\text{supp}({\nu})
and
F
, which according to ?THM?
can be assumed positive and fixed. Let
c>0
according to ?THM?
and let
n_{0}
and
N_{0}
denote the constants from ?THM?
and ?THM?
, respectively. We now pick an arbitrary integer
N>\max(n_{0},N_{0})
and apply ?THM?
with parameters
c
and
N
as above, in order to decompose our Korenblum-Roberts measure
\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\nu_{j}
, where each measure
\nu_{j}
satisfies

\omega_{\nu_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{n_{j}}\right)\leq\frac{c\,\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}

with
n_{j}=2^{2^{N+j}}
. Note that according to ?THM?
, we can find a constant
c_{1}>0
, such that for any
j
, we have

\left\lvert S_{\nu_{j}}(z)\right\rvert\geq n_{j}^{-cc_{1}},\quad z\in\Omega_{n_{j}}.

We may also apply ?THM?
, in order to obtain a sequence of functions
\{f_{n_{j}}\}_{j}\subset H^{\infty}
with
\left\lVert f_{n_{j}}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
, such that

\left\lvert f_{n_{j}}(z)\right\rvert\geq n_{j}^{-cc_{1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\Omega_{n_{j}}.

Combining, we have that
\left\lvert S_{\nu_{j}}(z)\right\rvert+\left\lvert f_{n_{j}}(z)\right\rvert\geq n_{j}^{-cc_{1}}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Invoking the Corona theorem for each
j
, we obtain functions
g_{j},h_{j}\in H^{\infty}
with
\left\lVert g_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert h_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n_{j}^{cc_{1}K}
, that solve the equation
S_{\nu_{j}}g_{j}+f_{n_{j}}h_{j}=1
on
\mathbb{D}
. Using this and
(ii)
of ?THM?
, we get

\left\lVert S_{\nu_{j}}g_{j}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq\left\lVert f_{n_{j}}\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\cdot\left\lVert h_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n_{j}^{-3cc_{1}K}\cdot n_{j}^{cc_{1}K}=n_{j}^{-2cc_{1}K}.

(15)

With this observation at hand, we now combine:

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}g_{1}g_{2}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq\left\lVert S_{\nu_{1}}g_{1}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert S_{\nu_{2}}g_{2}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}+\left\lVert S_{\nu_{1}}g_{1}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(n_{1}/n^{2}_{2}\right)^{cc_{1}K}+n_{1}^{-2cc_{1}K}.

Setting
\nu^{M}:=\sum_{1\leq j\leq M}\nu_{j}
and iterating this procedure, we actually get for any positive integer
M>1
:

\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\,\left\lVert S_{\nu^{M}}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq\frac{1}{n_{1}^{2cc_{1}K}}+\sum_{j=2}^{M}\left(\frac{n_{1}\dots n_{j-1}}{n^{2}_{j}}\right)^{cc_{1}K}\leq C2^{-Ncc_{1}K}.

Here
C>0
is an absolute constant, independent of
M,N
. With this at hand, we deduce that for any
M>1
:

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left\lVert S_{\nu}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu^{M})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}+\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left\lVert S_{\nu^{M}}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu^{M})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}+C2^{-Ncc_{1}K}.

(16)

An argument similar to that of the proof of ?THM?
, allows us to extract a subsequence
\{M_{j}\}_{j}
, such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\lVert S_{(\nu-\nu^{M_{j}})}-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}=0.

Returning back to (
16
) with
M=M_{j}
and taking
j\to\infty
, we obtain

\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left\lVert S_{\nu}p-1\right\rVert_{\mu,t}\leq C2^{-Ncc_{1}K}.

Since the parameter
N>1
in the decomposition of ?THM?
can be chosen arbitrary large, we finally conclude that
S_{\nu}
is a cyclic vector for the shift operator on
\mathcal{P}^{t}(\mu)
.

∎

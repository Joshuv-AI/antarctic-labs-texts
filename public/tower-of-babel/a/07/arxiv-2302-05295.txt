Identifiability and singular locus of secant varieties to spinor varieties
Vincenzo Galgano

Abstract
In this work we analyze the S ​ p ​ i ​ n Spin –structure of the secant variety of lines σ 2 ​ ( 𝕊 ) \sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}) to a Spinor variety 𝕊 \mathbb{S} minimally embedded in its spin representation. Introduction

Given an irreducible non-degenerate projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
and a point

, the
X
–rank
of

X
whose linear span contains

. In particular, point of
X

–rank

one defines the

\sigma_{r}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
as the Zariski closure of points of
\mathbb{P}^{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
of

Secant varieties have been studied for decades, but several aspects of their geometry are still mysterious and difficult to handle with. Even computing their dimensions is a hard task and a current topic of research. Two crucial aspects of secant varieties which are fundamental for applications and are still unknown in general are the
identifiability
and the
singularity
of their points. Namely, a given point
f\in\mathbb{P}^{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
X
–rank
r
is identifiable if it admits a unique decomposition as sum of
r
elements of
X
. On the other hand, singular points in
\sigma_{r}(X)
are those such that the dimension of the tangent spaces at these points overcome the dimension of the secant variety: conversely, the points for which such dimensions coincide are smooth. Of course, identifiability and singularity are of great impact to both theory and applications. Results having the smoothness (ie. non-singularity) of the variety among the hypotheses are a dense in geometry: for instance, in
[
COV14
, Prop. 5.1]
the authors give a criterion for identifiability of specific tensors in a secant variety under the assumption that these tensors are smooth. In fact, identifiability and smoothness are quite related each other and often one notion suggests the other (as in this work), although in general both implications admit counterexamples.

Owned to the above, determining the
singular locus

\Sing(\sigma_{r}(X))
of a secant variety
\sigma_{r}(X)
is a central problem. Classically, if
\sigma_{r}(X)
is not a linear space, it is known that the singular locus
\Sing(\sigma_{r}(X))
contains the secant variety
\sigma_{r-1}(X)
but only in few cases
\Sing(\sigma_{r}(X))
is actually determined. For instance, the case
r=2
for

is solved in
[
MOZ15
]
; for
Veronese varieties
the cases

and

[

]
and
[
Han18
]
respectively, while partial results for higher cases

are obtained in
[
FH21
]
; partial results for the
2

[
MM15
]
.

Segre varieties, Veronese varieties and Grassmannians are example of projective rational homogeneous varieties (RHV). They are described as quotients

The aim of this paper is to describe the secant variety of lines to a Spinor variety. Due to the fact that Spinor varieties of type

Hamming distance of varieties.

Given
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{M}
a projective variety, the
Hamming distance

between two points
p,q\in X
, already considered in
[

,
AOP12
,
GS23
]
, is the minimum number of lines of
\mathbb{P}^{M}
lying in

and connecting

to

d(p,q):=\min\left\{r\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ \exists\text{ distinct }p_{1},\ldots,p_{r-1}\in X,\ L(p_{i},p_{i+1})\subset X\ \forall i=0:r-1\right\}

(1.6)

where we set
p_{0}=p
,
p_{r}=q
and
L(p_{i},p_{i+1})
is the line passing through
p_{i}
and
p_{i+1}
. In particular,
d(p,q)=0
if and only if
p=q
, while
d(p,q)=1
if and only if

and

. The
diameter
of

is
\diam(X):=\max_{p,q\in X}\left\{d(p,q)\right\}
.

.

for any
N

[
Kaj99
,
Ang11
]
, that is

\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=\min\left\{2\dim\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}+1,\dim\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{ev}E\right)\right\}=\begin{cases}2^{N-1}-1&\text{for }N\leq 6\\
N(N-1)+1&\text{for }N\geq 6\end{cases}\ .

(1.7)

For
N\leq 5
the secant variety
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
overfills the ambient space
\mathbb{P}^{2^{N-1}-1}
(i.e.
\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=2^{N-1}-1\lneq 2\dim\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}+1
), for
N\geq 7
it is strictly contained, while for
N=6
it perfectly fills
\mathbb{P}^{31}
(in the sense
\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=2\dim(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})+1=2^{N-1}-1
): the latter case is said to be
perfect
.

Identifiability.

For any
[p]\in\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
, the
decomposition locus
of
p
is the set of all pairs of pure spinors in
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
giving a decomposition of
[p]
, that is

Dec_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}([p]):=\left\{([a],[b])\ |\ [a],[b]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\ [p]\in\langle[a],[b]\rangle\right\}\subset(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})^{2}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{2}}\ ,

where
(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})^{2}_{/\mathfrak{S}_{2}}
denotes the symmetric quotient of
(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})^{2}
by the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{2}
. An element
([a],[b])\in Dec_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}([p])
is called a
decomposition
of
[p]
.

A point
[p]\in\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
is
identifiable
if
Dec_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}([p])
is a singleton. Geometrically, this means that
[p]
lies on a unique bisecant line to
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. Otherwise one says that
[p]
is
unidentifiable
. For any subset
Y\subset\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
, we say that
Y
is (un)identifiable if any point of
Y
is so. In particular, an orbit is (un)identifiable if and only if a representative of it is so.

Tangential-identifiability

The
tangential variety
of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
in
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{ev}E)
is the union of all lines in
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{ev}E)
which are tangent to
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, i.e.
\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}):=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}T_{p}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\ \subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{ev}E)
. In particular, it holds
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\subset\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\subset\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
, and
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\cup\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
(the union may be non-disjoint). Moreover, from Zak’s key result
[
Zak93
, Theorem 1.4]
it holds that either
\dim\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=2\dim\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
and
\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=2\dim\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}+1
, or
\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
.

Definition 1.5
.

A tangent point
[q]\in\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
is
tangential-identifiable
if it lies on a unique tangent line to
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, or equivalently if there exists a unique
[p]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
such that
[q]\in T_{[p]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. Otherwise it is
tangential-unidentifiable
.

We say that an orbit is tangential-(un)identifiable if all of its elements are so.

Definition 1.6
.

Given a tangent point
[q]\in\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
, its
tangential-locus
is the set of points
p\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
such that
q\in T_{p}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
.

Clearly, if
q
is tangential-identifiable, then its tangential-locus is given by a single point at
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
.

Nonabelian apolarity.

The classical apolarity action
[
IK99
]
and the skew-apolarity action
[
Arr+21
, Definition 4]
are particular cases of a more general apolarity, namely the
nonabelian apolarity
, introduced at first in
[
LO13
, Section 1.3]
. Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a projective variety and let
\mathcal{L}
be a very ample line bundle on
X
giving the embedding
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee})
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank–
e
vector bundle on
X
such that
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})
is not trivial, where
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
denotes the bundle dual to
\mathcal{E}
. The natural contraction map
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\otimes H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
leads to the morphism
A:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\otimes H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
and, after fixing a given
f\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
, one gets the linear map

A_{f}:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})^{\vee}

defined as
A_{f}(s):=A(s\otimes f)
for any
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})
, and called
nonabelian apolarity action
.
Let
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E})
and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})
be the spaces of global sections vanishing on a
0
–dimensional subscheme
Z\subset X
, and let
\langle Z\rangle\subset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
be the linear span of
Z
. For a proof of the following result we refer to
[
LO13
, Proposition 5.4.1]
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
f\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
and let
Z\subset X
be a
0
–dimensional subscheme such that
f\in\langle Z\rangle
. Then it holds
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E})\subseteq\Ker A_{f}
and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})\subseteq\im A_{f}^{\perp}
.

It is worth remarking that the above result holds for non-reduced subschemes
Z\subset X
too. The schematic non-reduced version of the nonabelian apolarity has already been considered in
[
OR20
, Theorem 6.10]
for Veronese varieties, and more generally in
[
Gał17
, Proposition 7]
. In the classical apolarity, the case of minimal subschemes is considered with respect to cactus varieties and cactus rank
[
BB14
,
BR13
]
, while for non-minimal subschemes see
[
BT20
]
. Finally, for reduced subschemes we recall the following result
[
OO13
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
f\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}
and let
Z\subset X
be a
0
–dimensional reduced subscheme of length
r
, minimal with respect to the property
f\in\langle Z\rangle
. Assume that
\rk A_{f}=r\cdot\rk\mathcal{E}
. Then it holds
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E})=\Ker A_{f}
and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})=\im A_{f}^{\perp}
. In particular,
Z
is contained in the common zero locus of
\Ker A_{f}
and
\im A_{f}^{\perp}
.

2
Clifford apolarity

In this section we analyze the nonabelian apolarity in the case of Spinor varieties, obtaining what we call
Clifford apolarity
. We also exhibit the vanishing condition of the global sections of a certain vector bundle on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. We assume the setting in Remark
1.1
.

Consider the half-spin representations
V_{\omega_{N-1}^{D_{N}}}=\bigwedge^{od}E
and
V_{\omega_{N}}^{D_{N}}=\bigwedge^{ev}E
, and consider the standard representation
V_{\omega_{1}}^{D_{N}}=V
. Let
\mathcal{O}(1)
be the very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. Via the identification with
\OG^{+}(N,V)
, let
\mathcal{U}
be the rank–
N
universal bundle on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
obtained as the pullback of the universal bundle on
\Gr(N,V)
. The bundles
\mathcal{O}(1)
,
\mathcal{U}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{U}(1):=\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
are homogeneous and the corresponding representations of the parabolic subgroup
P_{\omega_{N}}
are irreducible with heighest weights
\omega_{N}
,
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{N-1}
respectively: from Borel–Weil’s Theorem one gets

H^{0}\big(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{O}(1)\big)\simeq\left(\bigwedge^{ev}E\right)^{\vee}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ H^{0}\big(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1)\big)\simeq\left(\bigwedge^{od}E\right)^{\vee}\ \ \ \,\ \ \ H^{0}\big(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}^{\vee}\big)\simeq V^{\vee}\ .

The contraction map
H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}^{\vee})\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{O}(1))
is equivalent to

\left(V_{\omega_{1}}^{D_{N}}\right)^{\vee}\otimes\left(V_{\omega_{N-1}}^{D_{N}}\right)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\left(V_{\omega_{N}}^{D_{N}}\right)^{\vee}\ ,

and, from the duality of half-spin representations (as
N
is even), we get

\left(E\oplus E^{\vee}\right)\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{ev}E\ .

(2.1)

Such map is uniquely determined as
\Spin(V)
-equivariant morphism (up to scalars): this is a consequence of Schur’s lemma applied to the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

The irreducible
\Spin(V)
-module
\bigwedge^{ev}E
(resp.
\bigwedge^{od}E
) appears with multiplicity
1
in the
\Spin(V)
-module
V\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E
(resp.
V\otimes\bigwedge^{ev}E
).

Proof.

In light of the natural inclusion
\SL(E)\subset\Spin(V)
, for any
\Spin(V)
-module
W
among the above ones we can consider its restriction as
\SL(E)
-module
\Res_{\SL(E)}^{\Spin(V)}(W)
: we lighten up the notation by simply writing
W
and specifying its module structure.
Since
N
is even, one can rewrite the
\SL(E)
–module
(E\oplus E^{\vee})\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E
as

(E\oplus E^{\vee})\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(E\otimes\bigwedge^{2k+1}E\right)\oplus\left(E^{\vee}\otimes\bigwedge^{2k+1}E\right)\ .

From Pieri’s formula
[
Pro07
, Sec. 9.10.2]
,
(E\oplus E^{\vee})\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E
decomposes into the
\SL(E)
-modules

V\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E=\mathbb{C}\oplus 2\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\bigwedge^{2k}E\right)\oplus\bigwedge^{N}E\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\frac{N-2}{2}}S^{\scriptsize\overbrace{(2,1,\ldots,1)}^{2k+1}}\oplus S^{\scriptsize(\overbrace{2,\ldots,2}^{2k+1},\overbrace{1,\ldots,1}^{N-2-2k})}\right)\ ,

among which there is only one copy of
\bigwedge^{ev}E
. Thus there has to be only one copy in the
\Spin(V)
-module decomposition as well.
∎

By fixing
q\in\bigwedge^{ev}E
, from the map (
2.1
) we get a
\Spin(V)
-equivariant map
\psi_{q}:(E\oplus E^{\vee})\rightarrow\bigwedge^{od}E
which is unique (up to scalars) by Theorem
2.1
, thus it coincides with the map
\psi_{q}:V\rightarrow\bigwedge E
defined in (
1.3
). Moreover, the natural contraction (
2.1
) is equivalent to a map

\Phi:\bigwedge^{ev}E\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow E\oplus E^{\vee}

which again by Theorem
2.1
is uniquely determined as
\Spin(V)
-equivariant morphism: more precisely, it is the projection onto the unique copy of the irreducible
\Spin(V)
-submodule
E\oplus E^{\vee}
.

Remark 2.2
.

By uniqueness, the map
\Phi
is intrinsically related to the skew-apolarity map defined in
[
Arr+21
, Definition 4]
. Consider the splitting in
\SL(E)
-modules

\bigwedge^{ev}E\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{\frac{N}{2}}\bigoplus_{s=0}^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(\bigwedge^{2r}E\otimes\bigwedge^{2s+1}E^{\vee}\right)\ .

The map
\Phi
is
\Spin(V)
-equivariant, hence
\SL(E)
-equivariant, so are its restrictions

\Phi_{2r,2s+1}:\bigwedge^{2r}E\otimes\bigwedge^{2s+1}E^{\vee}\longrightarrow E\oplus E^{\vee}\ .

By Schur’s lemma, each restriction
\Phi_{2r,2s+1}
is non-zero if and only if either
E
or
E^{\vee}
appears as irreducible
\SL(E)
-summand in the tensor product on the left-hand side: this happens if and only if
|2r-(2s+1)|=1
. In particular, it holds
\im\left(\Phi_{2r,2s+1}\right)=E
if
2r=(2s+1)+1
,
\im\left(\Phi_{2r,2s+1}\right)=E^{\vee}
if
2r=(2s+1)-1
, and
\im\left(\Phi_{2r,2s+1}\right)=0
otherwise.

For
2r=(2s+1)+1
(resp.
2r=(2s+1)-1
), the module
\bigwedge^{2r}E\otimes\bigwedge^{2s+1}E^{\vee}
has a unique copy of
E
(resp.
E^{\vee}
) as irreducible
\SL(E)
-submodule, thus (up to scalars) there exists a unique
\SL(E)
-equivariant morphism from the tensor product onto
E
(resp.
E^{\vee}
). By uniqueness, the restrictions
\Phi_{2r,2s+1}
are given (up to composing with a projection
\pi_{E\oplus E^{\vee}}
onto
E\oplus E^{\vee}
) by generalizing the skew-catalecticant maps
\mathcal{C}^{s,d-s}_{t}
in
[
Arr+21
, Definition 4]
as follows:

\begin{matrix}\Phi_{2r,2s+1}:&\bigwedge^{2r}E\otimes\bigwedge^{2s+1}E^{\vee}&\rightarrow&\bigwedge^{2r-(2s+1)}E\oplus\bigwedge^{2s+1-2r}E^{\vee}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{E\oplus E^{\vee}}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}&E\oplus E^{\vee}\\
&e\otimes f&\mapsto&\mathcal{C}^{2s+1,2r-(2s+1)}_{e}(f)+\mathcal{C}^{2r,2s+1-2r}_{f}(e)\end{matrix}

where
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{s,d-s}=0
for
s>d
: roughly, depending on the sign of
2r-(2s+1)
, one looks at vectors in
E^{\vee}
as derivations on
E
, or viceversa.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Clifford apolarity action
is the
\Spin(E\oplus E^{\vee})
-equivariant map

\begin{matrix}\Phi:&\bigwedge^{ev}E\otimes\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}&\longrightarrow&E\oplus E^{\vee}\\
&e\otimes f&\mapsto&C_{e}(f)_{|_{E}}+C_{f}(e)_{|_{E^{\vee}}}\end{matrix}

(2.2)

where
C_{e}(f)_{|_{E}}
(resp.
C_{f}(e)_{|_{E^{\vee}}}
) is the projection onto
E
(resp.
E^{\vee}
) of the contraction
f\cdot e\in\bigwedge E
(resp.
e\cdot f\in\bigwedge E^{\vee}
) obtained via Clifford multiplication.

Vanishing of sections in
H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))
.

Via the isomorphism
H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))\simeq(V_{\omega_{N-1}}^{D_{N}})^{\vee}=\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}
, any section
j_{f}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))
corresponds to a spinor
f\in\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}
: we describe the zero locus of a section
j_{f}
by generalizing an argument from
[
Man21
, proof of Prop. 2]
.

For any
[a]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, one has the fiber
(\mathcal{U}(1))_{[a]}\simeq\Hom((\mathcal{O}(1))^{\vee}_{[a]},\mathcal{U}_{[a]})
. In particular, it holds
(\mathcal{O}(1))^{\vee}_{[a]}=\langle a\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\subset\bigwedge^{ev}E
and
\mathcal{U}_{[a]}=H_{a}:=\{v\in E\oplus E^{\vee}\ |\ v\cdot a=0\}
. Thus we can identify
j_{f}([a])\in(\mathcal{U}(1))_{[a]}
with a homomorphism
j_{f}([a]):\langle a\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow H_{a}
. We want to determine
j_{f}([a])(\lambda a)\in H_{a}
as the scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
varies.

Remark 2.4
.

The scalar product
q(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V=E\oplus E^{\vee}
extends to a scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\bigwedge V
defined on decomposable elements
x=x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{k}\in\bigwedge^{k}V
and
y=y_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge y_{h}\in\bigwedge^{h}V
as
\langle x,y\rangle=\det(q(x_{i},y_{j}))
if
k=h
, and zero otherwise. The isomorphism of vector spaces
\bigwedge V\simeq\Cl(V,q)
defines such scalar product on the Clifford algebra too. The well-known adjointness between exterior product and contraction
\langle v\wedge x,y\rangle=\langle x,v\neg y\rangle
for
v\in V
,
x\in\bigwedge^{h-1}V
and
y\in\bigwedge^{h}V
, together with the Clifford multiplication (
1.1
), implies
\langle x,v\cdot y\rangle=\langle v\cdot x,y\rangle
for any
x,y\in\bigwedge V
and any
v\in V
: in the Clifford algebra, this property extends to any element
z\in\Cl(V,q)
as
\langle x,zy\rangle=\langle z^{*}x,y\rangle
, where
z^{*}
is as in (
1.2
).

The function
\psi_{f,a}(v)=\langle f,v\cdot a\rangle
is linear in
v\in V
and vanishes on
H_{a}
, thus it belongs to
H_{a}^{\perp}=\{\psi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ |\ \psi(H_{a})=0\}
. But
H_{a}=H_{a}^{\perp}
(since
a
is a pure spinor), thus
\psi_{f,\bullet}\in\Hom(\langle a\rangle_{\mathbb{C}},H_{a})
. It follows that we have two maps

\begin{matrix}&\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}&\longrightarrow&H^{0}\left(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1)\right)\\
j:&f&\mapsto&j_{f}\\
\Psi:&f&\mapsto&\psi_{f,\bullet}\end{matrix}

where
j
is the
\Spin(V)
-equivariant isomorphism given by Borel–Weil Theorem. Next remark shows that the maps
j
and
\Psi
coincide up to scalars.

Remark 2.5
.

The map
\Psi
is
\Spin(V)
-equivariant too. Indeed, for any
G
-homogeneous bundle on a variety
G/P
, the
G
-action on a global section
s
is given by
(g\cdot s)(a)=s(g^{-1}\cdot a)
. Since for any
x\in\Spin(V)
it holds
x^{-1}=x^{*}
, one gets
\left(x\cdot\psi_{f,\bullet}\right)(a)=\psi_{f,\bullet}(x^{*}a)=\psi_{f,x^{*}a}\in\Hom\left(\langle x^{*}a\rangle,H_{x^{*}a}\right)
where
\psi_{f,x^{*}a}(\lambda x^{*}a)\in H_{x*a}^{\perp}=H_{x^{*}a}
is the functional on
V
given by
\psi_{f,x^{*}a}(\lambda x^{*}a)(v)=\langle f,v\cdot\lambda x^{*}a\rangle
. But
x^{*}\in\Spin(V)
acts by conjugacy on
V
, thus
V=x^{*}Vx
and by adjointness one concludes
\langle f,v\cdot\lambda x^{*}a\rangle=\langle f,(x^{*}wx)\cdot\lambda x^{*}a\rangle=\langle f,x^{*}w\cdot\lambda a\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle adj.}}{{=}}\langle x\cdot f,w\cdot\lambda a\rangle
, that is
x\cdot\psi_{f,\bullet}=\psi_{x\cdot f,\bullet}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

In the previous notation, the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}(j_{f})
of a global section
j_{f}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))
corresponding to a spinor
f\in\bigwedge^{od}E^{\vee}
is

\mathcal{Z}(j_{f})=\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap(V\cdot f)^{\perp}\ .

(2.3)

Proof.

By Schur’s lemma, the equivariant maps
j
and
\Psi
coincide up to scalar, hence the section
j_{f}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{U}(1))
is such that
j_{f}([a])(\lambda a)=\langle f,\bullet\cdot(\lambda a)\rangle\in H_{a}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. We conclude that the zero locus
\mathcal{Z}(j_{f})
of the global section
j_{f}
is given by the pure spinors
[a]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
such that
0=\langle f,v\cdot a\rangle=\langle v\cdot f,a\rangle
for any
v\in V
, that is the thesis.
∎

3
The poset of
\Spin_{2N}
–orbits in
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})

We assume the setting in Remark
1.1
. The secant variety of lines
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}^{+}_{N})
is invariant under
\Spin_{2N}
–action, as well as its subsets
\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
and
\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
. We refer to the orbits lying in
\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})

, and to the orbits lying in
\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})

. Recall that the Spinor variety has diameter

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}\ =\ e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N-2l}\ \in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\ ,\\
\\
E_{N-2l}:=H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{N-2l},f_{N-2l+1},\ldots,f_{N}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\ \in\OG^{+}(N,V)\ .\end{matrix}

(3.1)

By convention, for

\mathbb{e}_{[0]}=\ell_{\omega_{N}}=\mathbbm{1}
. On the other hand, for

\mathbb{e}_{[N]}=v_{\omega_{N}}

Spin_{2N}
–orbits in
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

The action of
\Spin_{2N}

\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

g\cdot([a],[b])=([g\cdot a],[g\cdot b])

on

via Clifford multiplication (

).

Remark 3.1
.

Via the bijection (
1.4
), the action on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

\Spin^{q}(V)
on

g\cdot(H_{a},H_{b})=(g\cdot H_{a},g\cdot H_{b})
, where

is the

action: this follows from the inclusion
\Spin^{q}(V)\subset N_{\Cl_{q}(V)^{\times}}(V)
.

From Remark
1.3
, the Hamming distance in
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is invariant under
\Spin^{q}(V)
-action, as well as the dimensions of subspaces in

\Spin^{q}(V)
on
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\times\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
and on

restrict to actions on the subsets

Secant orbits in

We deduce the orbit partition of the dense subset

Lemma 3.4
.

In the above notation, it holds
H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=E\cap E_{N-2l}
if and only if
l\neq 1
. In particular, for any
l\geq 2
, it holds
\dim H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=N-2l
.

Proof.

We already know that
\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{N-2l}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=E\cap E_{N-2l}\subset H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}
holds. Assume that there exists
v\in H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}
such that
v\notin(E\cap E_{N-2l})\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, since
H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}
is fully isotropic, up to linear combinations we can consider a decomposition of
v
with respect to the standard hyperbolic basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{N},f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})
of the form
v=\alpha_{1}e_{N-2l+1}+\ldots+\alpha_{2l}e_{N}+\beta_{1}f_{N-2l+1}+\ldots+\beta_{2l}f_{N}
for some
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=\Ker(\psi_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}})
, it holds

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=v\cdot(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{multiplication}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{2l}\alpha_{i}(e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N-2l}\wedge e_{N-2l+i})+\sum_{j=1}^{2l}(-1)^{j+1}\beta_{j}(e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\hat{e}_{N-2l+j}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N})\ ,

where
\hat{e}_{N-2l+j}
denotes that the vector is missing in the wedge product.

Now, the summands above may simplify one to each other if and only if
l=1
, otherwise they do not since they are all independent vectors in the standard basis of
\bigwedge E
. It follows that, for
l\geq 2
, it holds
v\cdot(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]})=0
if and only if
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{j}=0
for all
i,j\in[2l]
, that is
H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=E\cap E_{l}
. On the other hand, for
l=1
, the conditions
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{2}
and
\alpha_{2}=-\beta_{1}
give a non-zero vector
0\neq v\in H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}\setminus(E\cap E_{l})
, thus
E\cap E_{1}\subsetneq H_{\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}}=\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{N-2},e_{N-1}+f_{N},e_{N}-f_{N-1}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

For any

Let

Lemma 3.6
.

For any two distinct pure spinors
[a],[b]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, the spin group
\Spin^{q}(V)
acts transitively on

Proof.

Since the lines defined by pairs of pure spinors having the same Hamming distance are all conjugated, it is enough to prove the transitivity on the line
L([\mathbb{e}_{[N]}],[\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}])
. Moreover, given a point
[\lambda\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mu\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}]=[\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}]\in L([\mathbb{e}_{[N]}],[\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}])
, we can rewrite it as
\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbb{e}_{[N-2l]}=e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N-2l}\wedge\left(e_{N-2l+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N}+z\mathbbm{1}\right)
. Since
[e_{N-2l+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N}]
and
[z\mathbbm{1}]
are pure spinors in
\mathbb{S}_{2l}^{+}
, we can restrict to consider the line
L([\mathbb{e}_{[N]}],[\mathbbm{1}])
.

Given
\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbbm{1}
, we look for a spin element
g\in\Spin^{q}(V)
such that
\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbbm{1}=k(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbbm{1})
for some
k\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We consider the element
\tilde{g}=(a_{1}e_{1}+b_{1}f_{1})\cdots(a_{N}e_{N}+b_{N}f_{N})\in\Cl_{q}^{+}(V)
for certain
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
: it is product of an even number of vectors in
V
by the assumption as
N
is even. Then
\tilde{g}\in\Spin^{q}(V)
if and only if it is invertible and it has unitary spinor norm, that is if and only if
\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}b_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\clubsuit}}{{=}}1
. Via Clifford multiplication (
1.1
) it holds
(a_{1}e_{1}+b_{1}f_{1})\cdot(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbbm{1})=a_{1}ze_{1}+b_{1}e_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{N}
,
and by iterating for

one gets
\tilde{g}\cdot(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbbm{1})=a_{1}\cdots a_{N}z\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+b_{1}\cdots b_{N}\mathbbm{1}
. In particular, the condition
\tilde{g}\cdot(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+z\mathbbm{1})=k(\mathbb{e}_{[N]}+\mathbbm{1})
is equivalent to the condition
z\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\spadesuit}}{{=}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}
. By putting together the conditions
(\clubsuit)
and
(\spadesuit)

and

∎

\Phi_{q_{l}}(\bigwedge^{2k+1}E^{\vee})
are linearly independent one to each other, we get

\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})=\bigwedge^{od\geq 5}E^{\vee}\oplus\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\cap\bigwedge^{3}E^{\vee}\right)\ ,

thus
\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)
is the intersection of the common zero loci of the two summands above.

Consider
[a]\in\mathcal{Z}\big(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\big)
such that
a=\sum_{s=0}^{\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{J\in\binom{[N]}{2s}}\alpha_{J}\mathbb{e}_{J}
. From Lemma
2.6
it holds

\langle V\cdot f,a\rangle=0\ \ \ \ \ ,\forall f\in\bigwedge^{od\geq 5}E^{\vee}\oplus\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\cap\bigwedge^{3}E^{\vee}\right)\ .

The same computation in (
4.1
) shows that for any index subset
I\subset[N]
such that
|I|=2k+1\geq 5
and for any basis vector
e_{\lambda}\in E
it holds
0=\langle e_{\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{f}_{I},a\rangle=(-1)^{pos(\lambda,I)+1}\alpha_{I\setminus\{\lambda\}}
where again
pos(\lambda,I)
denotes the position of
\lambda
in the ordered index subset
I
. We deduce that in
a
there is no summand indexed by a subset
J
such that
J\subset I
and
|J|=|I|-1
for any
|I|\geq 5
, hence
a\in\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigwedge^{2}E\oplus\bigwedge^{n+1}E
. On the other hand, the same computation as in (
4.3
) shows that for any
I\subset[N]
such that
|I|=3
and any basis vector
f_{\nu}\in E^{\vee}
such that
\nu\notin I
, it holds
0=\langle f_{\nu}\cdot\mathbb{f}_{I},a\rangle=(-1)^{pos(\nu,I\cup\{\nu\})+1}\alpha_{I\cup\{\nu\}}
, hence
a
has no summand in
\bigwedge^{N}E
either. It follows

\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigwedge^{2}E\right)\cap\mathcal{Z}\left(\bigwedge^{3}E^{\vee}\cap\Ker\big(\Phi_{q_{l}}\big)\right)\ .

Clearly,
[\mathbbm{1}]\in\mathcal{Z}(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}}))
since for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and any
f\in\bigwedge^{3}E^{\vee}
one has
\langle f,v\cdot\lambda\rangle=\lambda\langle f,v\rangle=0
. Moreover, for any
b\in\bigwedge^{2}E
it holds that
b\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)
if and only if
\lambda+b\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
: in particular, it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)\cap\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}E)=\emptyset
in order to conclude.

Let
[a]\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)
be such that
a=\sum_{\{s\lneq t\}\subset[N]}\alpha_{st}e_{s}\wedge e_{t}
. First, consider an index subset
I\subset[N]
such that
|I|=3
and
\{2k-1,2k\}\nsubseteq I
for any
k\in[l]
: the condition
C_{\mathbb{f}_{I}}(q_{l})_{|_{E^{\vee}}}=0
implies
\mathbb{f}_{I}\in\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})
. Then, for any
e_{\lambda}\in E
one gets

0=\langle e_{\lambda}\cdot\mathbb{f}_{I},a\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2},i_{3}}-\delta_{\lambda,i_{2}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{3}}+\delta_{\lambda,i_{3}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2}}\ ,

for
\delta_{xy}
being the Kronecker symbol. Since
N\geq 6
, given any two distinct indices
\{i\lneq j\}\subset[N]
such that
\{i,j\}\neq\{2k-1,2k\}
for any
k\in[l]
, one can always find a third index
r\in[N]\setminus\{i,j\}
such that
\{2k-1,2k\}\nsubseteq I=\{i,j,r\}
for any
k\in[l]
. Thus for any
\{i,j\}\neq\{2k-1,2k\}
it holds
\alpha_{ij}=0
and

\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)\cap\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{2}E\right)\subset\left\{[a]=\left[\sum_{k=1}^{l}\alpha_{2k-1,2k}e_{2k-1}\wedge e_{2k}\right]\right\}\ .

Now, for any
\{k\lneq h\}\subset[l]
and any
r\in[N]\setminus\{2k-1,2k,2h-1,2h\}
consider
\mathbb{f}_{2k-1,2k,r}-\mathbb{f}_{2h-1,2h,r}\in\bigwedge^{3}E^{\vee}
: it is a straightforward count that
\mathbb{f}_{2k-1,2k,r}-\mathbb{f}_{2h-1,2h,r}\in\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})
. In particular,
0=\langle e_{r}\cdot(\mathbb{f}_{2k-1,2k,r}-\mathbb{f}_{2h-1,2h,r}),e_{r}\cdot a\rangle=\alpha_{2k-1,2k}-\alpha_{2h-1,2h}
, hence it holds

\alpha_{2k-1,2k}=\alpha_{2h-1,2h}\ \ \ \ \ ,\forall\{h\lneq k\}\subset[l]\ .

It follows that
a=\alpha\cdot q_{l}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. But
[q_{l}]\notin\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
(since
l\geq 3
), thus it has to be
\alpha=0
and
\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)\cap\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}E)=\emptyset
. We conclude that
\mathcal{Z}\left(\Ker(\Phi_{q_{l}})\right)=\{[\mathbbm{1}]\}
.
∎

From

Recall that for

Lemma 6.1

For any

\begin{matrix}\xi:&\Sigma_{l,N}&\longrightarrow&\OG(N-2l,V)\\

Consider the distance-

and the above fibration

Let

Proposition 6.2
.

For any
N\geq 6
and
l=2:\frac{N}{2}-1
, the secant orbit
\Sigma_{l,N}\subset\sigma_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
has dimension

\dim\Sigma_{l,N}=\begin{cases}\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+4N-15&\text{if }l=2\\
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+l(2N-1)-2l^{2}+1&\text{if }l\geq 3\ .\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the fibration
\xi
in Lemma
6.1
. From the fiber dimension theorem we get

\dim\Sigma_{l,N}=\dim\OG(N-2l,V)+\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{2l}^{+})\ .

In general, the orthogonal Grassmannian
\OG(r,M)
coincides with the kernel of the global section
s_{q}\in H^{0}(\Gr(r,M),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U})
induced by the quadratic form
q
on
\mathbb{C}^{M}

\mathcal{U}
is the rank-

\dim\OG(r,M)=\dim\Gr(r,M)-\rk\left(\Sym^{2}(\mathcal{U})\right)=r(M-r)-\binom{r+1}{2}\ .

The thesis follows by substituting

and

∎

7
The
2
–nd Terracini locus of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

In the following we determine the
Terracini locus
, introduced in
[
BC21
,
BBS20
]
. Its importance relies in the fact that it gives information on the singularities of points lying on bisecant lines.

The
2
–nd Terracini locus
of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is

\Terr_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}):=\overline{\left\{\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\in Hilb_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ |\ \dim\langle T_{p_{1}}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},T_{p_{2}}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\rangle\lneq\dim\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\right\}}\ .

We also recall the definition of the
2
–nd abstract secant variety
of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}):=\left\{(\mathcal{Z},p)\in Hilb_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\times\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{ed}E\right)\ |\ p\in\langle\mathcal{Z}\rangle\right\}\ ,

together with the natural projections onto the two factors
\pi_{1}:Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\rightarrow Hilb_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
and
\pi_{2}:Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\rightarrow\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{ev}E)
. Notice that, with this definition, the
2
–nd abstract secant variety is smooth.

The
2
–nd Terracini locus tells us where the differential of the projection from the abstract secant variety onto the secant variety drops rank: this information, combined with the identifiability, allows to deduce the smoothness on the locus of points which are both identifiable and outside the Terracini locus. In the following we consider
N\geq 7
as for
N\leq 6
the secant variety of lines coincides with the ambient space.

Theorem 7.1
.

For any
N\geq 7
, the second Terracini locus
\Terr_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
of the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
corresponds to the distance–
2
orbit closure
\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}
. More precisely, in the above notation, it holds

\Terr_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})=\left(\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2}^{-1}\right)\left(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}\right)\ .

Proof.

Given a point
[a+b]\in\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
for certain
[a],[b]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, we show that
\dim\langle T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},T_{[b]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\rangle
drops if and only if
[a+b]\in\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}=\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\sqcup\Sigma_{2,N}
, that is if and only if
d([a],[b])\leq 2
.

If
d([a],[b])=1
, then any point on the line
L([a],[b])\subset\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is direction of the curve defined by such line, that is
L([a],[b])\subset T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap T_{[b]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
.

If
d([a],[b])=2
, then there exists
[c]\in\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
such that
L([a],[c]),L([c],[b])\subset\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. In particular, from the previous case we deduce
[c]\in T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap T_{[b]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
.

On the other hand, if the dimension drops, then there exists a common non-zero tangent point
[x]\in T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap T_{[b]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. In particular,
[x]
is not tangential-identifiable, hence
[x]\in\Theta_{2,N}
. From the definition (
3.6
) of
\Theta_{2,N}\cap T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
as the set of rank–
4
skew-symmetric matrices in
T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, we know that
[x]=[y+z]
for
[y],[z]\in\Theta_{1,N}\cap T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}=\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap T_{[a]}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
skew-symmetric matrices of rank
2
. In particular,
[a+y]
and
[a+z]
lie in
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, that is
d([a],[y])=d([a],[z])=1
. The same argument shows that also
d([b],[y])=d([b],[z])=1
. We conclude that
d([a],[b])\leq 2
, hence the thesis.

∎

Remark
.

As defective cases, the secant varieties

According to the above remark, we assume

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
N\geq 7
, the distance–
2

lies in the singular locus

of the secant variety of lines to the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. In particular,

\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}\subset\Sing\left(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\right)\ .

Proof.

Clearly, the Spinor variety is singular in its secant variety of lines, hence we just have to prove the singularity of the distance–
2
orbit
\Sigma_{2,N}
. We assume by contradiction that
\Sigma_{2,N}
is smooth: then the poset in Theorem
3.12
implies that all the open subset
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is smooth. Consider the projection from the abstract secant variety
onto the second factor

\begin{matrix}\pi:&Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})&\longrightarrow&\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\\
&([a],[b],[q])&\mapsto&[q]\end{matrix}\ .

The (tangential-)identifiability of the orbits
\Sigma_{l,N}
and
\Theta_{l,N}
for
l\geq 3
(cf. Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
5.2
) implies that the restriction of
\pi
to the open subset
\pi^{-1}(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})
is a bijection of smooth open subsets, hence it is an isomorphism.
On the other hand, the orbit
\Sigma_{2,N}
is unidentifiable and any of its points has
6
–dimensional decomposition locus (cf. Proposition
4.3
), thus the differential
d(\pi)_{([a],[b],[q])}
drops rank exactly at the points in the preimage
\pi^{-1}(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})
.

It follows that the restriction

{\pi}_{|}:Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\longrightarrow\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}

is a morphism of smooth varieties of the same dimension: in particular, the locus of points where the rank of the differential drops is a (determinantal) divisor. We show that the preimage

\left\{([a],[b],[q])\in Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ |\ \rk d(\pi)_{([a],[b],[q])}<N(N-1)\right\}={\pi_{|}}^{-1}\left(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}\right)

cannot be a divisor (leading to a contradiction). Indeed, from Proposition
6.2
we know that
\dim\Sigma_{2,N}=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+4N-15
, and from Proposition
4.3
that the decomposition locus of any point in
\Sigma_{2,N}
is
6
–dimensional, thus from the fiber dimension theorem we get
\dim\pi_{|}^{-1}(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})=\dim\Sigma_{2,N}+6=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+4N-9
and

\dim Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})-\dim\pi_{|}^{-1}(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}-4N+10\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\geq 7}}{{\gneq}}1\ .

Thus
{\pi}_{|}^{-1}(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})
cannot be a divisor for
N\geq 7
, giving the contradiction. We deduce the inclusion
\Sigma_{2,N}\subset\Sing(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}))
.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

For any
N\geq 7
and any
l=3:\frac{N}{2}
, the secant orbit
\Sigma_{l,N}
is smooth in the secant variety of lines
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
. In particular, the singular locus of the secant variety of lines to the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
lies in the tangential variety:

\Sing\left(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\right)\subset\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ .

Proof.

From Proposition
4.5
we know that the secant orbits
\Sigma_{l,N}
for
l=3:N
are identifiable. Moreover, from the poset in Figure
1
it holds

\Sigma_{3,N}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\Sigma_{\frac{N}{2},N}=\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ .

Then the projection
\pi:Ab\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\rightarrow\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
from the abstract secant variety onto the second factor restricts to a bijection

\pi^{\prime}:\pi^{-1}\bigg(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\bigg)\rightarrow\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ .

Finally, the points in
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
are outside the Terracini locus
\Terr_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
(cf. Theorem
7.1
), thus for any point
[p+q]\in\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
it holds
T_{p}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\cap T_{q}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}=\{0\}
. In particular, for such points the differential
d\pi^{\prime}_{([p],[q],[p+q])}
maps
T_{p}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\times T_{q}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
to
T_{p}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\oplus T_{q}\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, hence it is injective. It follows that
\pi^{\prime}
is an isomorphism and the open subset
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
is smooth in the secant variety of lines.
∎

Collecting Lemma
8.1
and Lemma
8.2
we get the following bound on the singular locus.

Corollary 8.3
.

For any
N\geq 7
, the singular locus of the secant variety of lines
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
to the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is such that

\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}\subset\Sing\left(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\right)\subset\tau(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\ .

We conclude this work with a conjecture on what the singular locus of
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
actually is, motivated by the arguments apprearing in the appendix.

Conjecture 8.4
.

For any
N\geq 7
, the singular locus of the secant variety of lines to the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is the closure of the distance–
2
orbit, i.e.

\Sing\left(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\right)=\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}=\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}\sqcup\Sigma_{2,N}\ .

Appendix

The arguments in this section are inspired from
[
Ver01
,
Ver09
,
Ull16
]
: the author thanks L. Manivel for suggesting these references. They have been investigated in the attempt of proving the smoothness of both secant and tangent orbits of distance greater or equal than
3
in the secant variety of lines to a Spinor variety. The idea was to prove that the secant bundle gives a desingularization of the secant variety, but we have just been able to exhibit a bijection (as sets) between dense subsets.

We recall that a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on a smooth projective variety
X
is
k
–very ample
if every
0
–dimensional subscheme
\mathcal{Z}\subset X
of length
k+1
imposes independent conditions on
\mathcal{L}
, or equivalently if the restriction map
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})
is surjective: in particular,
\mathcal{L}
is
1
–very ample if and only if it is very ample, and
\mathcal{L}
is
k
–very ample if and only if there does not exist a
0
–dimensional subscheme
\mathcal{Z}\subset X
of length
k+1
lying on a linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee})
.

Remark 8.5
.

The line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}(1)
on the Spinor variety
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
is very ample, giving the embedding in
\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}}(1))^{\vee}\right)\simeq\mathbb{P}\left(\bigwedge^{ev}E\right)
, but not
2
–very ample. Indeed, it would be
2
–very ample if and only if there would not exist a
0
–dimensional subscheme of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
of length
3
lying on a line
\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{ev}E)
. But we know that the Spinor variety contains lines, defined by pairs of pure spinors having Hamming distance
1
.

Secant bundle.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(1)
be the very ample line bundle on
X:=\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
, and let
Hilb_{2}(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of
0
-dimensional subschemes of
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
of length
2
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}
the locally free sheaf of rank
2
on
Hilb_{2}(X)
having fibers
\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})
: formally,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}:=(\pi_{2})_{\ast}(\pi_{1}^{\ast})(\mathcal{L})
where
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are the natural projections from the universal family
\Phi:=\{(x,\mathcal{Z})\in X\times Hilb_{2}(X)\ |\ x\in\mathcal{Z}\}
onto
X
and
Hilb_{2}(X)
respectively.
From the projection formula, one gets the following global sections of sheaves:

\displaystyle H^{0}(Hilb_{2}(X),\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(\Phi,(\pi_{X})^{\ast}\mathcal{L})=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes(\pi_{X})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\Phi})=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\ ,

where the last equality follows since
\pi_{X}
is a proper projection and
(\pi_{X})_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

By pushing forward via
\pi_{H}
the morphism
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}\rightarrow(\pi_{X})^{\ast}\mathcal{L}
(of sheaves on
\Phi
) one gets the
evaluation
morphism of sheaves on
Hilb_{2}(X)

ev:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Hilb_{2}(X)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\ ,

defined on the fibre as the restriction: for any
\mathcal{Z}\in Hilb_{2}(X)
it holds

\begin{matrix}ev_{\mathcal{Z}}:&H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})&\longrightarrow&\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}&=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})\\
&s&\mapsto&s_{|_{\mathcal{Z}}}\end{matrix}\ .

As
\mathcal{L}
is very ample, the evaluation

ev_{\mathcal{Z}}:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})

is a surjection for any
\mathcal{Z}\in Hilb_{2}(X)
, hence the morphism of sheaves
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Hilb_{2}(X)}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}
is surjective. Moreover, again by very ampleness of
\mathcal{L}
, one can get the injective morphism

\begin{matrix}\psi:&Hilb_{2}(X)&\hookrightarrow&\Gr\big(2,H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\big)\\
&\mathcal{Z}&\mapsto&\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})\right]\end{matrix}\ .

Now, we may think at
\mathbb{P}^{M}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee})
as to the
1
-dimensional quotients of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
, i.e.

\mathbb{P}^{M}=\left\{H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow Q\ |\ \dim Q=1\right\}\ .

We denote by
Q^{1}
a
1
-dimensional vector space. Then one defines the (first)
secant bundle

\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}
as the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle on
Hilb_{2}(X)
with fibers
(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}})_{\mathcal{Z}}=\left\{\left(\mathcal{Z},\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{1}\right]\right)\right\}
. The following morphism is well-defined:

\begin{matrix}f:&\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee}\right)\\
&\left(\mathcal{Z},\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})\twoheadrightarrow Q^{1}\right]\right)&\mapsto&\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow Q^{1}\right]\end{matrix}\ .

Notice that
\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow Q^{1}\right]\subset\im(f)
if and only if it factors through a certain
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})
for some
\mathcal{Z}\in Hilb_{2}(X)
: in particular,

f\left(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)=\sigma_{2}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})^{\vee})\ .

For any
\mathcal{Z}\in Hilb_{2}(X)
, the fibre
\left(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}=\left\{(\mathcal{Z},\left[H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}})\twoheadrightarrow Q^{1}\right])\right\}
is bijectively mapped via
f
to the secant line
L(\mathcal{Z})\subset\sigma_{2}(X)
intersecting
X
in the points of
\mathcal{Z}
: if
\mathcal{Z}=\{p,q\}
is a reduced subscheme, then the fibre at
\mathcal{Z}
gives the bisecant line
\left(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1:1}}{{\mapsto}}L(p,q)
, while if
\mathcal{Z}
is a non-reduced subscheme corresponding to
\{p,v\}
, where
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
, then the fibre at
\mathcal{Z}
gives the tangent line
\left(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1:1}}{{\mapsto}}L_{v}(p)=p+tv
. Finally, in our setting, for any two distinct subschemes
\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y}\in Hilb_{2}(X)
the lines
L(\mathcal{Z})
and
L(\mathcal{Y})
coincide if and only if they lie in
X
, as the Spinor variety is intersection of quadrics.

Lemma 8.6
.

The restriction of
f
to
f^{-1}(\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}})
is a bijection.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
, the points in the secant orbits
\Sigma_{l,N}
for
l\geq 3
are identifiable, thus any two bisecant lines (given by points in
\Sigma_{l,N}
for
l\geq 3
, except for the two points in which they intersect
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
) do not intersect away from
\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+}
. By Theorem
5.2
, the points in the tangent orbits
\Theta_{l,N}
for
l\geq 3
are tangential-identifiable, liying on a unique tangent line, hence any two tangent lines do not intersect away from
\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}
. Finally, by the orbit partition of
\sigma_{2}(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
in Theorem
3.12
, since
\Theta_{l,N}\neq\Sigma_{l,N}
for any
l\geq 3
, a tangent line and a bisecant line never intersect away from
\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}
. It follows that
f:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}\rightarrow\sigma(\mathbb{S}_{N}^{+})
is a bijection away from
f^{-1}\left(\overline{\Sigma_{2,N}}\right)
.
∎

Remark
.

We point out that Vermeire in

Ver09

and Ullery in

Ull16

Prescribed Riemannian Symmetries
Alexandru CHIRVASITU

Abstract
Given a smooth free action of a compact connected Lie group G G on a smooth compact manifold M M , we show that the space of G G -invariant Riemannian metrics on M M whose automorphism group is precisely G G is open dense in the space of all G G -invariant metrics, provided the dimension of M M is “sufficiently large” compared to that of G G . As a consequence, it follows that every compact connected Lie group can be realized as the automorphism group of some compact connected Riemannian manifold; this recovers prior work by Bedford–Dadok and Saerens–Zame under less stringent dimension conditions. Along the way we also show, under less restrictive conditions on both dimensions and actions, that the space of G G -invariant metrics whose automorphism groups preserve the G G -orbits is dense G δ G_{\delta} in the space of all G G -invariant metrics.

Introduction

The present paper fits into the general theme of realizing a predetermined group as the symmetry group of a structure (combinatorial, topological, geometric, etc.) of given type. Variations on the theme abound in the literature, mostly (but by no means exclusively) in the context of

(2)

(3)

Question 1.1

Is it the case that every compact Lie group is the isometry group of some compact Riemannian manifold?

With this question as the initial motivating driver, one can then pose the natural follow-up problem (to be made precise) of “how large” the set of desirable Riemannian metrics is, given a manifold equipped with an action by a compact Lie group. Recall:

Definition 2.2

A subset of a topological space is

•

•

According to the Baire category theorem

complete metric spaces are Baire. Since

As usual (e.g.,

)

of a topological space are countable union of closed subsets, while

are countable intersections of open subsets. Their relevance here stems from the fact that in a Baire space a countable intersection of open dense subsets is residual and hence dense.

Theorem

Definition 3.1
.

Let

(

An orbit

•

(

)

The action is

if all of its orbits are.

(

)

In general (i.e., for possibly-disconnected

), the

of the action are the actions of

(

Similarly, a general action is principal if its components are.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\dim M_{i}\geq 3+\dim G

Remark 3.3

As observed in the statement of

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathcal{F}_{i,n}:=\lx@text@lbrace}\text{ out of }U_{n}\}.

We now have

(i)

Each

To sketch this briefly, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i,n}\ni g_{\alpha}\underset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}g\in\mathcal{M}_{G}(M)

(ii)

The proof of (the rest of) Theorem
3.2
will require some preparation, in part to recall, somewhat informally, the proof strategy for
[
12
, Proposition 8.3]
. That proof proceeds as follows.

(a)

An arbitrary Riemannian metric
g_{ij}
on
M
is first perturbed slightly so that the maximum over
p\in M
of the largest eigenvalue

\displaystyle\max~\textsc{spec}(\textsc{ric}(p))

of the symmetric operator

\displaystyle\textsc{ric}(p):=R^{i}_{j}(p)\colon T_{p}M\to T_{p}M

is achieved at a unique point
p
, and the perturbation is confined to an arbitrarily small neighborhood
U
of
p
.

(b)

With this in hand, every isometry of
M
with respect to the new metric will fix that unique point
p
.

(c)

The procedure is repeated on small spheres around
p
avoiding
U
, ensuring that the maximal eigenvalue of
R^{i}_{j}
on such a sphere is achieved at a unique point, which will then again be fixed by every Riemannian isometry.

(d)

Repeating the procedure a large (but finite) number of times, one obtains a metric whose isometry group fixes at least
\dim M+1
“independent” points of

. It then follows that the isometry group must be trivial (e.g.,
[

The proof of Theorem
3.2
appearing below follows essentially the same plan, with some modifications. For one thing, in place of the maximal eigenvalue we consider other numerical invariants of a self-adjoint operator on a (real) Hilbert space:

Notation 3.4
.

Let

•

•

We will be interested in maximizing the norm or spread of the operators

\displaystyle\max_{p\in M}~\textsc{spr}(\textsc{ric}(p))=0

\displaystyle\forall\,p\in M,\qquad R_{ij}(p)=f(p)g_{ij}(p)

Notation 3.5
.

For a smooth manifold

•

\mathcal{NSR}_{G}(M)\subset\mathcal{M}_{G}(M)

\displaystyle R(p)=\mathrm{tr}(\textsc{ric}(p))\neq 0\qquad\text{over a dense set of}\quad p\in M.

For a subset

•

Finally, we set

4
Maximal rigidity

As indicated in the Introduction, the initial motivation for the results above was to produce

Notation 4.1

Given a faithful isometric action of a Lie group

The same notation (and terminology) applies to arbitrary (non-faithful) actions: if

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{G}^{\max}(M)=\mathcal{M}_{G/H}^{\max}(M).

Since we can harmlessly pass to faithful actions by passing to the quotient by the kernel of the action, we typically assume faithfulness throughout.

One cannot hope for metrics produced as in Theorem

Example 4.2

Let

connected components

, acting in the obvious fashion on

Now, if

\displaystyle S(G/G_{0})\subset\textsc{aut}(g)\quad\text{but}\quad S(G/G_{0})\not\subseteq G\subset\textsc{aut}(g).

Abstract
. This is the second installment of a series of papers aimed at developing a theory of Hrushovski-Kazhdan style motivic integration for certain types of nonarchimedean o o -minimal fields, namely power-bounded T T -convex valued fields, and closely related structures. The main result in the first installment is a canonical isomorphism between the Grothendieck rings of certain categories of definable sets, which is understood as a universal additive invariant or a generalized Euler characteristic because the categories do not carry volume forms. Here we introduce two types of volume forms into each of the relevant categories, one takes values in the value group and the other in the finer RV \RV -sort. The resulting isomorphisms respect Jacobian transformations — that is, the change of variables formula holds — and hence are regarded as motivic integrals. As in the classical theory of integration, one is often led to consider locally constant functions with bounded support in various situations. For the space of such functions, the construction may be fine-tuned so as to become more amenable to applications. The modifications are nevertheless substantial and constitute the bulk of the technical work.

Suppose for contradiction that

Lemma 2.36
.

Let
f:A\longrightarrow\VF^{m}
be a definable continuous function. Suppose that

is closed and bounded. Then

is closed and bounded.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
f(A)
is not bounded. Then, by compactness, there is a definable function
g:\VF^{+}\longrightarrow A
such that
\lvert f(g(x))\rvert>x
for every

. Since

is closed and bounded, applying monotonicity (Corollary

) to each coordinate of

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}g(x)
exists and belongs to

. But this implies that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(g(x))
also exists and belongs to

, which is impossible.

The argument for closedness is similar: Otherwise, we would have a point

and a definable function

such that
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}f(g(x))=b
, which is impossible since
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}g(x)
exists and belongs to

.
∎

Lemma 2.41

In classical analysis, a function

is called a Darboux function if it has the intermediate value property, that is, for any

and any

with
f(c)=x
. By the intermediate value theorem, every continuous function on a real interval is a Darboux function. There are discontinuous Darboux functions, but every discontinuity of such a function is essential, that is, at least one of the one-sided limits does not exist (in
\mathds{R}\cup\pm\infty
). Darboux’s theorem states that if

is a real-valued differentiable function on an open interval then
\tfrac{d}{dx}f
is a Darboux function. This theorem is an easy consequence of the extreme value theorem and hence also holds in any
o
-minimal field.

The following easy lemma must have been observed before, but we are unable to find a reference in the literature on

-minimality.

Lemma 2.43

Let

Proof.

By

Let

4.
Special covariant bijections

Below we shall primarily concentrate on the finer categories with
\RV
-volume forms. It is a routine matter to translate the results and the arguments for the categories with
\Gamma
-volume forms, often simply by forgetting the conditions that involve the
\RV
-Jacobian operator

. Nevertheless, there are shortcuts through forgetful functors, and we will supply more details after the key propositions in Remarks
4.29
and

Our task in this section is to connect
\gsk\mVF[*]
with
\gsk\mRV[*]
, or more precisely, to establish a surjective homomorphism
\gsk\mRV[*]\longrightarrow\gsk\mVF[*]
and a specialized version of it. Notice the direction of the arrow. The main instrument in this endeavor is special covariant transformations.

4.1.
Covariance and invariance

Definition 4.1
.

For each element
\gamma\in\absG_{\infty}
, let

\pi_{\gamma}:\VF\longrightarrow\VF/\MM_{\gamma}

be the natural map. If
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})\in\absG_{\infty}^{n}

\pi_{\gamma}
denotes the product of the maps
\pi_{\gamma_{i}}
. Let

and

and

is
(\alpha,\beta)
-covariant
if it
(\pi_{\alpha},\pi_{\beta})
-contracts to a function

, that is,
\pi_{\beta}\circ f=f_{\downarrow}\circ\pi_{\alpha}
. For simplicity, we shall often suppress mention of parameters and refer to
(\alpha,\beta)
-covariant functions as
(\alpha,-)
-covariant or
(-,\beta)
-covariant or just covariant functions. A set

is
\alpha
-invariant
if its characteristic function is
(\alpha,0)
-covariant.

More generally, for sets
A
,
B
with
\RV
-coordinates, the function

is

if every one of its
\VF
-fibers

is
(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})
-covariant for some
(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})\in\absG_{\infty}
(this is in line with Terminology
2.22
). Accordingly, a set is

if (every
\VF
-fiber of) its characteristic function is
(-,0)
-covariant.

Terminology 4.2
.

Of course
(\infty,\infty)
-covariance is a vacuous requirement on a definable function, as
\infty
-invariance on a definable set; we shall call them
pseudo
covariance and
pseudo
invariance. On the other hand, we say that a definable function

on

is

if

•

the sets

,

are bounded and the sets
A_{\RV}
,
f(A)_{\RV}
are doubly bounded,

•

for each
\VF
-fiber

of

there is a

-definable tuple

such that

is

is
\alpha_{t}
-invariant, and
\ran(f_{t})
is
\beta_{t}
-invariant.

Actually, if

is closed for every

then there is no need to demand that

be doubly bounded, since it is implied by the other conditions: There is an
\mathfrak{o}
-partition

such that, for each open polydisc

in question,

lies in the range of some

-fiber of

. This, by Lemma

that satisfies the assumption of Lemma

is doubly bounded.

Observe also that if

is proper covariant then, since

is doubly bounded, by Lemma

, there is a definable element

such that every
\VF
-fiber

of

is

-covariant (but not necessarily

-covariant) and

We are only interested in proper and pseudo covariant functions, and no covariant functions other than these shall be considered. Accordingly, an invariant set

is either pseudo or proper, where

is

if its projection into the
\RV
-coordinates is proper covariant, in particular,

is bounded and

is doubly bounded (there are various ways to interpret properness when the range of a function has no

-coordinates, and the task of choosing one is left to the reader). It follows from the discussion above that if

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A
be a definable set such that

is bounded and

is doubly bounded. Then

is proper invariant if and only of
A
is clopen (recall Terminology

Proof.

The “only if” direction is clear. For the “if” direction, since every
\VF
-fiber of

is open, there is an
\mathfrak{o}
-partition

. Since every
\VF
-fiber of

is also closed, by Lemma

is doubly bounded. The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

,

be proper invariant subsets of

. Then

,
\RVH(A)\smallsetminus A
, and

Clearly

Note that, however, a restriction of a proper covariant function is not necessarily a proper covariant function, even if the restricted domain and range are both proper invariant. In a sense, this behavior requires continuity.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be a definable continuous surjection between two proper invariant sets. Suppose that the range of every
\VF
-fiber of

For each

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{o}((b,s),\beta_{t})\subseteq\ran(f_{(t,s)}),&\text{if }(b,s)\in\ran(f_{(t,s)}),\\
\mathfrak{o}((b,s),\beta_{t})\cap\ran(f_{(t,s)})=\emptyset,&\text{if }(b,s)\notin\ran(f_{(t,s)}).\end{cases}

Since

is doubly bounded, by Lemma

-definable

For the second claim, since

of

.

Let

be a continuous proper covariant bijection. If

(Blowups)

Theorem 5.32

Let

\mVF^{\diamond}[n]

Let

\int_{+A}(\bm{F},\bm{\omega})=\int_{+B}\phi^{\sigma}_{*}(\bm{F},\bm{\omega})

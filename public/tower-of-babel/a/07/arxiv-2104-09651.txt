Signatures of topological phase transitions in the s-wave superconductor at finite temperature
Stefan Gorol, Florian Loder, Daniel Braak, Arno P. Kampf, Thilo Kopp

Abstract
In two dimensions, an s-wave superconductor in the presence of Rashba spin-orbit coupling possesses distinct topologically non-trivial ground state phases controlled by Zeeman splitting and band filling. These phases can be characterized in terms of spin textures in momentum space. Although the spin texture becomes topologically trivial at finite temperatures, we identify thermodynamic signatures that are directly related to the topological phase transitions of the ground state. In particular, relative maxima in the entropy as a function of the magnetic field in the vicinity of topological phase transitions emerge and are attributed to a sign change in the derivative of the magnetization with respect to temperature.



I
Introduction

Topologically non-trivial electronic systems in two dimensions (2d) are often characterized by a non-zero Chern number
Bernevig 2013
;
Berry 1984
;
Xiao et al. 2010

which reflects a property of the ground state wave function.
This topological invariant is essential for the quantum Hall effect
Qi and Zhang 2011
and determines the quantized Hall conductivity in the zero temperature limit, or the thermal Hall effect
Sumiyoshi and Fujimoto 2013
;
Imai et al. 2016
;
Hasan and Kane 2010
for which the finite Chern number dictates the term in the thermal Hall conductivity linear in temperature.
The 2d s-wave superconductor can be topological—in the sense that the Chern number may differ from zero—if spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and Zeeman spin splitting are present
Sau et al. 2010
;
Loder et al. 2017
;
Alicea 2010
;
Zyuzin et al. 2013
.

In these systems, thermodynamic properties of
Lifshitz transitions concomitant with topological phase transitions have been investigated
Quelle et al. 2016
;
Kempkes et al. 2016
;
He and Huang 2013
;
Dong et al. 2015
. Those transitions are
accompanied by
a kink in the first derivative of the thermodynamic potential with respect to the chemical potential at zero temperature
Lifshitz 1960
;
Volovik 2017
. At finite temperatures, the kink is broadened
but may still be detected
as a peak in the third derivative of the thermodynamic potential with respect to the chemical potential

Varlamov and Pantsulaya 1985
;
He and Huang 2013
;
Seo et al. 2013
;
Zheng et al. 2014
.
On the other hand the bulk topology can be related to topological edge states by the bulk-boundary correspondence
Bernevig 2013
;
Thouless et al. 1982
;
Mong and Shivamoggi 2011
. By using Hill thermodynamics the presence of the edge states in finite-size systems can be taken into account
Quelle et al. 2016
;
Kempkes et al. 2016
,
resulting in a linear contribution to the heat capacity.

In addition to the Chern number, non-trivial bulk topology may be characterized by a non-zero skyrmion number for spin textures in reciprocal space
Bogdanov and Rößler 2001
;
Loder et al. 2017
;
Björnson and Black-Schaffer 2014
;
Sau et al. 2010
;
Qi et al. 2006
.
At zero temperature, the skyrmion number in a topological s-wave superconductor is related to the Chern number
Loder et al. 2017
, but any finite temperature destroys its topological character as will be discussed in more detail in section
II
.
While the Lifshitz transition is related to the Fermi surface structure of the corresponding normal conducting phase, we study here thermodynamic signatures of the spin texture and predict a maximum of the entropy as function of magnetic field at constant temperature in the vicinity of the topological phase transition. This entropy maximum at non-zero temperatures may serve as an experimentally accessible way to detect the transition into a topologically non-trivial ground state of the system.

The realization of a topological 2d s-wave superconductor is, however, not straightforward, because the necessary magnetic fields—if applied perpendicular to the plane—are usually larger than the upper critical magnetic field
h_{\mathrm{c2}}
. It has been argued that topologically non-trivial phases in the s-wave superconductor may be accessible by magnetic field rotation towards in-plane orientations
Loder et al. 2015
, which preserves the non-trivial topology and avoids exceeding
h_{\mathrm{c2}}

Klemm et al. 1975
. In this way, the topological phase transition may be observed in spin-orbit coupled s-wave superconductors with sufficiently strong
Zeeman splitting
Sau et al. 2010
.

II
Topological Characterization

First, we briefly review the model of an topological s-wave superconductor and its topological phases. We exclusively concentrate on the situation of intra-band pairing discussed in Reference
Loder et al. 2013
.
The Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian for the 2D superconducting system
under consideration is diagonal in momentum space and given by the 4x4 matrix

\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{0}(\boldsymbol{k})&\mathbb{D}\\
\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}&-\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{k})\end{array}\right),

(1)

where the Nambu spinor basis
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\left(\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}\right)^{\dagger}
is used with
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{\mathrm{x}},k_{\mathrm{y}})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
The 2x2 matrix
\mathcal{H}_{0}
reads

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon(\boldsymbol{k})-\mu+h_{\mathrm{z}}&\alpha(\boldsymbol{k})+h_{\mathrm{x}}-\mathrm{i}h_{\mathrm{y}}\\
{\alpha}^{*}(\boldsymbol{k})+h_{\mathrm{x}}+\mathrm{i}h_{\mathrm{y}}&\epsilon(\boldsymbol{k})-\mu-h_{\mathrm{z}}\end{array}\right)

(2)

while
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{k})=-2t\,(\cos k_{\mathrm{x}}+\cos k_{\mathrm{y}})
with
t
and
\mu
denoting the usual tight binding hopping energy and the chemical potential, respectively. The Rashba SOC is
\alpha(\boldsymbol{k})=\alpha\left(\sin k_{\mathrm{y}}+i\sin k_{\mathrm{x}}\right)
. The coupling of the magnetic field to the spin by the Zeeman term is included as
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\boldsymbol{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
where
h_{\mathrm{x}}
,
h_{\mathrm{y}}
and
h_{\mathrm{z}}
are the
magnetic field components in x-,y- and z-direction, respectively. We use units such that
k_{\mathrm{B}},\mu_{\mathrm{B}},\hbar=1
.
Regular s-wave pairing is assumed

\mathbb{D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\Delta\\
-\Delta&0\end{array}\right)

(3)

with
\Delta
being the order parameter. The grand canonical potential reads

\Omega=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{\nu}\ln\left[2\cosh\left(\frac{\beta\lambda_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)}{2}\right)\right]-2N\mu+\frac{N|\Delta|^{2}}{V}.

(4)

where
\lambda_{\nu}
denotes the eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian (
1
) and
N
is the number of lattice points. The attractive on-site interaction is
V>0
and
\beta=1/T
. From the gap equation
\partial\Omega/\partial\Delta=0
the
\boldsymbol{k}
-independent mean-field order parameter
\Delta_{\mathrm{OP}}
is obtained and
the particle number density is determined from
n=-\frac{1}{N}{\partial\Omega}/{\partial\mu}
.

In this system, a relevant topological invariant is the Chern number

N_{\mathrm{C}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\nu,\mathrm{occupied}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\Omega^{\nu}_{\mathrm{B}}(\boldsymbol{k});\qquad N_{\mathrm{C}}\in\mathbb{Z}

(5)

with the Berry curvature
\Omega^{\nu}_{\mathrm{B}}(\boldsymbol{k})
given by

\Omega^{\nu}_{\mathrm{B}}(\boldsymbol{k})=\left.\mathrm{i}\nabla\times\langle\nu,\boldsymbol{k}|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{k}}|\nu,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle\right|_{z},

(6)

where
\nu
runs over all occupied bands and
|\nu,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle
refers to an eigenstate of the band
\nu
. For
\boldsymbol{h}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
the four eigenbands
\lambda_{\nu}
with
\nu\in\{1,2,3,4\}
are non-degenerate. The bands are in the following indexed by increasing energy. The Nambu-space bands are related by
\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)=-\lambda_{4}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)
and
\lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)=-\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)
.

A further topological invariant is the skyrmion number
N_{\mathrm{S}}
, defined by

N_{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^{2}k\,\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{k})\cdot\left(\partial_{k_{x}}\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{k})\times\partial_{k_{y}}\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{k})\right),

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{k})
is the normalized spin vector

\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{k})=\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})/|\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})|

(8)

with
\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})=\left(\langle\hat{s}_{x}(\boldsymbol{k})\rangle,\langle\hat{s}_{y}(\boldsymbol{k})\rangle,\langle\hat{s}_{z}(\boldsymbol{k})\rangle\right)
.
We obtain the spin expectation values at each
\boldsymbol{k}
-point for a given temperature
T
with

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=1,2}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\lambda_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)}{2}\right)\langle\nu,\boldsymbol{k}|\left(c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}\right)\boldsymbol{\sigma}\left(c_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}c_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}|\nu,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle.

(9)

It was shown previously
Loder et al. 2017
that in a system described by (
1
) the skyrmion number and the Chern number are related by
N_{\mathrm{C}}=-2N_{\mathrm{S}}
at zero temperature. The skyrmion number takes half-integer values because the spin texture is actually of meron character
Nych et al. 2017
;
Woo 2018
;
Guo et al. 2020
. In the following, for simplicity, we will omit expectation-value brackets and the hat identifying operators.

In order to enter any non-trivial topological phase it is necessary to apply magnetic field energies larger than
\Delta_{\mathrm{OP}}
but the upper critical magnetic field energy set by
h^{2}_{\mathrm{c}2}
is usually smaller than
\Delta_{\mathrm{OP}}
.
If the magnetic field is rotated into an in-plane orientation, the topological transition fields
h_{\mathrm{t},1}
and
h_{\mathrm{t},2}
are decreased.

For
h_{\mathrm{x}}
,
h_{\mathrm{y}}\ll\alpha

h_{\mathrm{t},1,2}(h_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\neq 0)\approx h_{\mathrm{t},1,2}(h_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}=0)-t\frac{h_{\mathrm{x}}^{2}+h_{\mathrm{y}}^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}

(10)

is obtained.
Concomitantly, orbital depairing is
suppressed in the rotated field setup such that

is increased in layered superconductors
Klemm et al. 1975
;
Werthamer et al. 1966
.
It may hence be possible to realize the situation where
h_{\mathrm{t},1}<h_{\mathrm{c,2}}
.
The required field rotation into an in-plane orientation leads to pairing with finite center-of-mass momentum
Kaur et al. 2005
;
Barzykin and Gor’kov 2002
;
Michaeli et al. 2012
;
Fulde and Ferrell 1964
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1965
;
Barzykin and Gor’kov 2002
;
Michaeli et al. 2012
, however, this does not destroy the inherent topological character of
the considered phases
Loder et al. 2017
and, consequently, the finite temperature
signatures addressed in the following are not affected qualitatively. Therefore, without loss of generality, we discuss here the situation for
h_{\mathrm{x}}=h_{\mathrm{y}}=0
.
The topological phase-transition fields are defined by two magnetic fields

at which the band gap closes,

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left({\Delta_{\mathrm{OP}}}\right)^{2}+(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{0})-\mu(n))^{2}},

(11)

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{t},2}(h_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}=0)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left({\Delta_{\mathrm{OP}}}\right)^{2}+\mu^{2}(n)}.

(12)

III
Thermodynamics

The values of
s_{\mathrm{z}}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathrm{VC},i})
at isolated points of the BZ
are not accessible by measurements of thermodynamic quantities.
However, the modification of the spin-structure in a sizable region of the Brillouin zone can affect thermodynamic properties and thereby exhibit signatures of the topological ground-state transition even at
non-zero temperatures.

The energy gap
\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}
depends on
\boldsymbol{k}
due to the Rashba SOC which is discussed in more detail in Reference
Loder et al. 2013
. At low temperatures
T\ll\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}
thermal excitations are restricted to a small neighborhood of the energy gap minima. Thus, information about
variations in the
s_{\mathrm{z}}(\boldsymbol{k})
-values in the vicinity of the gap closing points can be obtained. Taking the partial derivative of the magnetization in z-direction with respect to
temperature, and neglecting
contributions further away from the Fermi-level yields

-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\left.\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial h_{\mathrm{z}}}\right|_{T,\mu}\right)\right|_{h_{\mathrm{z}},n}=\left.\frac{\partial M_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial T}\right|_{n,h_{\mathrm{z}}}\hskip-14.22636pt=\left.\frac{\partial S}{\partial h_{\mathrm{z}}}\right|_{n,T}\hskip-14.22636pt=\frac{1}{N}\!\!\int\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\frac{\partial\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)}{\partial h_{\mathrm{z}}}\frac{\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)}{T^{2}}\;\mathrm{sech}^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)}{2T}\right).

(16)

We omitted the contributions of two eigenbands
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{4}
which are far away from the Fermi level and used the symmetry
\lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)=-\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)
.

T\ll\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}
is reflected in a peak in
T^{-2}\mathrm{sech}^{2}(\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)/2T)
at the gap minimum
\boldsymbol{k}_{\mathrm{min}}
where
\lambda_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\mu)
is minimal. In Equation (
16
),
S
is the entropy and a Maxwell relation is used to relate the derivate of the magnetization with respect to
T
to the derivative of the entropy with respect to the magnetic field.

IV
Conclusion and final remarks

Topologically non-trivial states can
be realized in s-wave superconductors in the presence of Zeeman splitting
and Rashba spin-orbit coupling
Loder et al. 2015
.
The topologically non-trivial phases in the system may be described by either the Chern number or the skyrmion number for the spin-texture in momentum space. The Chern number description is valid only for the ground state while the skyrmion number
characterization yields trivial topology at any finite temperature. Nevertheless, there exist signatures of the topological phase transition at finite temperature related to the profound change in the spin texture signified in certain thermodynamic quantities.

A topological superconductor with spin-orbit coupling shows a characteristic vortex structure of the in-plane spin components at the time-reversal invariant momenta in the Brillouin zone. The out-of-plane spin component at distinct vortex centers flips from zero to one at a critical Zeeman splitting for
T=0
and attains a maximum in the topologically non-trivial phase even for
T>0
. This sudden change manifests itself in a maximum of the entropy as function of magnetic field at constant temperature. Equivalently, it corresponds to a sign change of the derivative of the magnetization with respect to temperature. We propose a suitable generalization of the skyrmion number,
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}
, which measures the singular character of the spin texture and is not confined to the ground state. In this way,
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}
captures the topological nature of the phase transition even at non-zero temperature although it is not a topological invariant.

Other two-dimensional systems with broken time-reversal symmetry, whose topological ground state depends on the magnetic field e. g. the topological mixed-parity superconductor
Yoshida and Yanase 2016
;
Daido and Yanase 2016
, are candidates for similar investigations close to topological phase transitions.
Thermodynamic signatures of topological phase transitions are not restricted to topological superconductors but expected as well for topological insulators.
Replacing the magnetization by a pseudospin polarization, the Chern insulators described in terms of topologically non-trivial pseudospin textures may be analyzed at finite temperatures in a similar fashion
Qi et al. 2006
.

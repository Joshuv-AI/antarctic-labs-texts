Weighted L p L_{p} Markov factors with doubling weights on the ball
Jiansong Li, Heping Wang, Kai Wang

Abstract
. Let L p , w , 1 ≤ p < ∞ , L_{p,w},\ 1\leq p<\infty, denote the weighted L p L_{p} space of functions on the unit ball 𝔹 d \mathbb{B}^{d} with a doubling weight w w on 𝔹 d \mathbb{B}^{d} . The Markov factor for L p , w L_{p,w} on a polynomial P P is defined by ‖ | ∇ P | ‖ p , w ‖ P ‖ p , w \frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{p,w}}{\|P\|_{p,w}} , where ∇ P \nabla P is the gradient of P P . We investigate the worst case Markov factors for L p , w ​ ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ ) L_{p,w}\ (1\leq p<\infty) and obtain that the degree of these factors are at most 2 2 . 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{B}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x|\leq 1\}
denote
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{S}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\,|x|=1\}
denote the unit sphere of

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, where
x\cdot y
is the usual inner product
and
|x|=(x\cdot x)^{1/2}
is the usual Euclidean norm. Given a weight function
w
, we
denote by
L_{p,w}:=L_{p}({B}^{d},w(x)dx)\ (1\leq p\leq\infty)

the Lesbegue space on
{B}^{d}
endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{p,w}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\Big)^{1/p},\ \ &1\leq p<\infty,\\
&{\rm ess}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x)|,\ \ \ &p=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

and we set, for a measurable
subset
E
of
{B}^{d}
,

|\textbf{E}|:=\int_{\textbf{E}}dx\ \ {\rm and}\ \ w(\textbf{E}):=\int_{\textbf{E}}w(x)dx.

For simplicity, we write
L_{p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for
L_{p,w}
and

\|\cdot\|_{p,w}
when
w(x)=1
.

We introduce a distance on
{B}^{d}
:

d(x,y):=\arccos\{x\cdot y+\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}\},\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{d},

and write

\mathbf{B}(x,r):=\{y\in{B}^{d}:\,d(x,y)\leq r\}.

It also can be seen that the distance
d
is
equivalent to the distance
\tilde{d}
on
{B}^{d}
, which is
defined by

\tilde{d}(x,y):=\sqrt{|x-y|^{2}+(\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}-\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}})^{2}},\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{d}.

We also define the
geodesic distance on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by

\rho(x,y):=\arccos(x\cdot y),\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

A weight function
w
is a doubling weight if there exists a constant

L>0
, depending only on
d
and
w
, such that

w(\mathbf{B}(x,2r))\leq L\,w(\mathbf{B}(x,r)),\ {\rm\ for\ all}\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{d},\ r>0\ ,

where the least constant
L_{w}
is called the doubling constant of

w
. In particular, the Jacobi weight

w_{\mu}(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2}\ (\mu\geq 0)
is the doubling
weight on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
. This follows from the fact (see

[
20
, Lemma 5.3]
) that for
r>0
,
x\in{B}^{d}
,

(1.1)

w_{\mu}(\textbf{B}(x,r)):=\int_{\textbf{B}(x,r)}w_{\mu}(y)dy\asymp r^{d}(r+\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}})^{2\mu}.

Here we use the notion
A_{n}\asymp B_{n}
to express

A_{n}\ll B_{n}
and
A_{n}\gg B_{n}
, and
A_{n}\ll B_{n}
(
A_{n}\gg B_{n}
)
means that there exists that a constant
c>0
independent of
n

such that
A_{n}\leq cB_{n}
(
A_{n}\geq cB_{n}
).
According to (
1.1
), we also have for a positive integer
n
,

(1.2)

w_{\mu}(\textbf{B}(x,1/n))\asymp n^{-d}\mathcal{W}_{\mu}(n;x):=n^{-d}(\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}+1/n)^{2\mu}.

Let
\Pi_{n}^{d}
be the space of all polynomials in
d
variables
of total degrees at most
n
. For
\Omega\subset{R}^{d}
, we use

\Pi_{n}(\Omega)
to denote the restriction to
\Omega
of
\Pi_{n}^{d}
.

In this paper we study Markov factors. Suppose that
X
is a
Banach space consisting of functions defined on a domain of
{R}^{d}
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. The Markov factor for
X
on a
polynomial
P
is defined by

\frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{X}}{\|P\|_{X}},

where

\nabla=(\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{d}):=(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d}})
is the gradient operator. The worst case Markov
factor for
X
is defined by

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(X):=\sup_{0\neq P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}}\frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{X}}{\|P\|_{X}}.

If
X
is a Hilbert space, then we can discuss the average case
Markov factor first introduced in
[
1
]
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{N_{n}^{d}}\}
be an orthonormal basis for

\Pi_{n}^{d}
in
X
, where

N_{n}^{d}
denotes the dimension of
\Pi_{n}^{d}
. A random polynomial in
\Pi_{n}^{d}
is defined by

P_{{\mathbf{a}}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}a_{j}P_{j}(x),

where
{{\mathbf{a}}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N_{n}^{d}})^{T}\in{R}^{N_{n}^{d}}
, the coefficients

a_{j}
are independent
N(0,\sigma^{2})
random variables with the
common normal density functions

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-\frac{a_{j}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},\ \ 1\leq j\leq N_{n}^{d}.

The average case Markov factor for
X
is defined by

\displaystyle M_{n}^{\rm ave}(X)

\displaystyle:={E}\Big(\frac{\|\,|\nabla P_{\bf a}|\,\|_{X}}{\|P_{\bf a}\|_{X}}\Big)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{N_{n}^{d}/2}}\int_{{R}^{N_{n}^{d}}}\frac{\|\,|\nabla P_{{\mathbf{a}}}|\,\|_{X}}{\|P_{{\mathbf{a}}}\|_{X}}\,{e^{-\frac{\|{\bf a}\|_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}}d{{\mathbf{a}}},

where

\|{\mathbf{a}}\|_{2}^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}|a_{j}|^{2},\ \ \ d{{\mathbf{a}}}:=da_{1}da_{2}\dots da_{N_{n}^{d}}.

This paper is devoted to investigating the worst case Markov
factors for
L_{p,w},\ 1\leq p<\infty
and the average case
Markov factors for
L_{2,w}
, where

is a doubling weight on

{B}^{d}
. We recall previous results about the worst and average
case Markov factors.

For
X=L_{p}([-1,1]),\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
, the classical Markov
inequality states that

(1.3)

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p}([-1,1])):=\sup_{0\neq P\in\Pi_{n}^{1}}\frac{\|P^{\prime}\|_{p}}{\|P\|_{p}}\asymp n^{2}.

In fact, (
1.3
) was proved by A. A. Markov for

in

[
18
]
and by Hille, Szegö and Tamarkin for

in

[
10
]
.

For

with convex body

{\bf K}
in
{R}^{d}
, Wilhelmsen obtained in
[
22
]
for

p=\infty
, and Kroó and S. Révész in
[
16
]
for

that

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p}({\bf K},dx))\ll n^{2}.

Suppose that
1\leq p<\infty
,

w_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}
is the Jacobi weight
on
[-1,1]
, and
{\alpha},\beta>-1
. It is proved that (see

[
9
]
)

(1.4)

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p}([-1,1],w_{{\alpha},\beta}(x)dx)\asymp n^{2},

and (see
[

]
)

This means that the worst and average

are
different.

For

with doubling
weight
w
on
[-1,1]
, Mastroianni and Totik proved in

[

, Theorem 7.4]
that

(1.5)

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p}([-1,1],w(x)dx))\ll n^{2}.

Wang, Ye and Zhai obtained in
[

]
that

n^{-1/2}M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{2}([-1,1],w(x)dx))\ll M_{n}^{\rm ave}(L_{2}([-1,1],w(x)dx))\ll n^{3/2}.

In general, for a doubling weight
w
on
[-1,1]
, the worst case
Markov factor for

is

.
In this case, the average case Markov factor

.

There are many other paper devoted to discussing the Benstein or
(and) Markov type inequalities, see for example,

,

,

,

,

. We remark that there are few

.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend (

) to the spaces

and (

) to the spaces

, where

Our results can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

,

, and

be a doubling weight on

. Then we have

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p,w}):=\sup_{0\neq P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}}\frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{p,w}}{\|P\|_{p,w}}\ll n^{2}.

In particular, for the Jacobi weight
w_{\mu}(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2}\ (\mu\geq 0)
, the exponent

is sharp, that is

M_{n}^{\rm wor}(L_{p,w_{\mu}}):=\sup_{0\neq P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}}\frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}{\|P\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}\asymp n^{2}.

.

Let

and

be a doubling weight on

. Then we have

M_{n}^{\rm ave}(L_{2,w}):={E}\Big(\frac{\|\,|\nabla P_{\bf a}|\,\|_{2,w}}{\|P_{\bf a}\|_{2,w}}\Big)\ll n^{3/2}.

We organize this paper as follows. In Section 2, we give the

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Integration representation of partial
derivatives of polynomials

We denote by
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}
the space of all polynomials of degree
n

which are orthogonal to lower degree polynomials in
L_{2,w_{\mu}}.

It is well known (see
[
8
]
, p. 38 or p. 229) that the spaces

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}
are just the eigenspaces corresponding to the
eigenvalues
-n(n+2\mu+d-1)
of the second-order differential
operator

D_{\mu}:=\triangle-(x\cdot\nabla)^{2}-(2\mu+d-1)x\cdot\nabla,

where the
\triangle
is the Laplace operator. Also, the spaces

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}
are mutually orthogonal in
L_{2,w_{\mu}}
and

\Pi_{n}^{d}=\ \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{d},\ \ \ \ \ L_{2,w_{\mu}}=\ \bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}.

The orthogonal projector

Proj_{n}:\,L_{2,w_{\mu}}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}

can be written
as

(Proj_{n}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f(y)P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)w_{\mu}(y)dy,

where
P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)

is the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{d}
. For
\mu>0
, we
have (see
[
25
]
)

P_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)=b_{d}^{\mu}b_{1}^{\mu-1/2}\frac{\lambda+n}{\lambda}\int_{-1}^{1}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x\cdot y+u\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}})(1-u^{2})^{\mu-1}du,

where
C_{n}^{\lambda}
is the
n
-th degree Gegenbauer polynomial,

{\lambda}=\mu+\frac{d-1}{2}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad b_{d}^{\mu}:=\Big(\int_{{B}^{d}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\mu-1/2}dx\Big)^{-1}.

The
case
\mu=0
is a limit case and we have

P_{n}(w_{0};x,y)=b_{d}^{0}\frac{\lambda+n}{2\lambda}[C_{n}^{\lambda}(x\cdot y+\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}})+C_{n}^{\lambda}(x\cdot y-\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}})].

Let
\eta
be a

C^{\infty}
-function on
[0,\infty)
with
\eta(t)=1
for
t\in[0,1]
and
\eta(t)=0
for
t\geq 2
. We define

(2.1)

L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\eta(\frac{j}{n})P_{j}(w_{\mu};x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}\eta(\frac{j}{n})P_{j}(w_{\mu};x,y).

It is well known that

L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)=L_{n}(w_{\mu};y,x),\ \ x,y\in{B}^{d},

L_{n}(w_{\mu};\cdot,y)\in\Pi_{2n}^{d}\ \ {\rm for\ fixed}\ y\in{B}^{d},

and for any
P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}
,

(2.2)

P(x)=\int_{{B}^{d}}P(y)L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)w_{\mu}(y)dy,\ \ x\in{B}^{d},

which deduces that

(2.3)

\partial_{i}P(x)=\int_{{B}^{d}}P(y)\partial_{x_{i}}L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)w_{\mu}(y)dy,\ \ 1\leq i\leq d,

where
\partial_{x_{i}}L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)
means the partial derivative about
x_{i}
.
So in order to estimate the norm of
\partial_{i}P(x)
, it is
vital to estimate
\partial_{x_{i}}L_{n}(w_{\mu};x,y)
.

The following lemma will be used in the following sections.

Lemma 2.1
.

(See
[
20
, Lemma 4.6 ]
.) Let
0<p<\infty,\ \mu\geq 0,
and

\sigma>\frac{d}{p}+2\mu|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|
.
Then

J_{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{w_{\mu}(y)dy}{\mathcal{W_{\mu}}(n;y)^{\frac{p}{2}}(1+nd(x,y))^{\sigma p}}\ll n^{-d}{\mathcal{W_{\mu}}(n;x)}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

(2.4)

\displaystyle\asymp\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}{E}\frac{\|{D}_{i}{\mathbf{a}}\|_{2}}{\|{\mathbf{a}}\|_{2}}\asymp n^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\sqrt{{\rm tr}({{D}}_{i}^{T}{{D}}_{i})}\ ,

\bar{P}(x)=\bar{P}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{d})=f(x_{i})\in\Pi_{n}^{d}.

By
[
4
, Corollary A.5.3]
we have

\|\partial_{i}\bar{P}\|_{p,w_{\mu}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f^{\prime}(x_{i})|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx=c_{\mu,d}\int_{-1}^{1}|f^{\prime}(x_{i})|^{p}w_{\lambda}(x_{i})dx_{i},

and

\|\bar{P}\|_{p,w_{\mu}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|f(x_{i})|^{p}w_{\mu}(x)dx=c_{\mu,d}\int_{-1}^{1}|f(x_{i})|^{p}w_{\lambda}(x_{i})dx_{i},

where
w_{\lambda}(t)=(1-t^{2})^{{\lambda}-1/2},\ t\in[-1,1]
,

\lambda=\mu+\frac{d-1}{2}
, and
c_{\mu,d}
is a positive
constant depending only on
\mu
and
d.

Since
\partial_{j}\bar{P}=0
, for
j\neq i,\ 1\leq j\leq d
, it follows that

\frac{\|\,|\nabla\bar{P}|\,\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}{\|\bar{P}\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}=\frac{\|\partial_{i}\bar{P}\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}{\|\bar{P}\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}=\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{p,w_{\lambda}}}{\|f\|_{p,w_{\lambda}}}.

Combining with (
1.4
), we get

\sup\limits_{0\neq P\in\Pi_{n}^{d}}\frac{\|\,|\nabla P|\,\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}{\|P\|_{p,w_{\mu}}}\gg\sup\limits_{0\neq f\in\Pi_{n}^{1}}\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{p,w_{\lambda}}}{\|f\|_{p,w_{\lambda}}}\asymp n^{2}.

Theorem 1.1 is proved.

\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem 1.2.

According to (
2.2
), (
2.5
), and Corollary 4.4, it
suffices to show that for
1\leq i\leq d
,

(5.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}\|\partial_{i}P_{j}\|_{2,w_{n}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}|\partial_{i}P_{j}(x)|^{2}\Big)w_{n}(x)dx\ll n^{d+3}.

Choose
k>s_{w}+d+1
. It follows from (
2.3
), Remark 3.7, the
Hölder inequality, and Lemma 2.1 that

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}P_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|P_{j}(y)||\partial_{x_{i}}L_{n}(w_{1/2};x,y)|dy\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|P_{j}(y)|\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x)})^{3}}\frac{n^{d+1}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;y)}(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{|P_{j}(y)|^{2}}{(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{n^{d}}{\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;y)(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{|P_{j}(y)|^{2}}{(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy.

By (
4.4
), (
4.8
), and Lemma 2.1, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}|\partial_{i}P_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}|P_{j}(y)|^{2}}{(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}\int_{{B}^{d}}\frac{1}{w({\bf B}(y,\frac{1}{n}))(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}\int_{{B}^{d}}\frac{1}{n^{-d}w_{n}(y)\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;y)(1+nd(x,y))^{k}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}w_{n}(x)}\int_{{B}^{d}}\frac{n^{d}}{\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;y)(1+nd(x,y))^{k-s_{w}-1}}dy

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}w_{n}(x)}.

Then, according to
[
24
, Lemma 4]
we have

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{1}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}dx\asymp n,

which leads that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}^{d}}|\partial_{i}P_{j}(x)|^{2}\Big)w_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{n^{d+2}}{(\mathcal{W}_{1/2}(n;x))^{3}}dx\ll n^{d+3},

proving (
5.2
). Theorem 1.2 is proved.

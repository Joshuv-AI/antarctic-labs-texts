Keywords: Choquard equation; Riesz potential; Pohožaev identity; Existence; Nonexistence.

Abstract.
In this paper, we establish the existence of ground state solutions for Choquard equations

-\Delta u+u=q\,(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q-2}u+p\,(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1)

where
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,

I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential,
p,\,q>0
satisfying that

\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}<p+q<\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}.

(2)

Moreover, we prove a Pohožaev type identity for problem (
1
), which implies the non-existence result for the problem when
(p,q)
does not satisfy the condition (
2
).

1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of existence results for nonnegative solutions of Choquard equations

-\Delta u+u=q(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q-2}u+p(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.1)

where
p,\,q>0
,
N\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,N)

and

I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential defined by

I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})|x|^{\alpha-N}}{\pi^{N/2}2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}

with
\Gamma
being the Gamma function, see
[
20
]
.

As early as in 1954, the Choquard equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+u=(I_{2}\ast|u|^{2})u&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0\end{array}\right.

has appeared in the context of various physical models. It seems to originate
from H. Fröhlich and S. Pekar¡¯s model of the polaron, where free electrons
in an ionic lattice interact with phonons associated to deformations of the
lattice or with the polarisation that it creates on the medium (interaction
of an electron with its own hole)
[
5
,
6
,
19
]
. The Choquard equation was
also introduced by Ph. Choquard in 1976 in the modelling of a one-component
plasma.

The existence and qualitative properties of solutions of Choquard equations
have been widely studied in the last decades. In
[
10
]
, Lieb proved the
existence and uniqueness, up to translations, of the ground state. Later on,
in
[
11
]
, Lions showed the existence of a sequence of radially symmetric solutions.
In
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
]
the authors considered the regularity, positivity and radial symmetry of
the ground states and derived decay property at infinity as well.
Especially, Moroz and Van Schaftingen in
[
15
]
studied the generalized Choquard equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+u=(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

they showed that solutions of problem (
1.2
) are, at least formally, critical points of the functional

F
defined for a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

F(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,dx-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{p}\,dx.

In the present paper, we are interested in studying the existence of ground states solutions for Choquard problem (
1.1
).
We note that problem (
1.1
) has a variational structure: the critical points of the function

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{q}\,dx

are solutions of (
1.1
). We state the existence results as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3,\,\alpha\in(0,N)
and
p,\,q>0
satisfying that

\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}<p+q<\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}.

(1.3)

Then problem (
1.1
) admits a positive ground state solution.

To prove the existence result in Theorem
1.1
, we apply the critical points theory to the associated minimizing problem

M_{p}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,dx:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{q}\,dx=1\right\}.

(1.4)

By Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, which states that
if
t,r>1
and
\frac{1}{t}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}+\frac{1}{r}=2
,

f\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists a sharp constant
C(t,N,\alpha,r)
, independent of
f,h
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdy\leq C(t,N,\alpha,r)\|f\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

see for instance
[
8
,
15
]
, we see that
M_{p}>0
.
Then we use the concentration compactness argument and a nonlocal version of Brezis-Lieb lemma to prove that
M_{p}
can be achieved.
The minimization of
M_{p}
is a nontrivial solution of
(
1.1
).

The second aim of this paper is to establish the Pohožaev type identity for (
1.1
) and obtain the non-existence results as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
)
with
p,\,q>0
satisfying that

{p+q}\geq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}\quad{\rm or}\quad{p+q}\leq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}.

(1.5)

Assume that
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|\nabla u|\in H_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then
u
is a trivial solution of (
1.1
).

The content of the paper is the following: in Section 2 we provide some technical preliminaries;
in Section 3 we prove the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.1
)
in Theorem
1.1
by the critical points theory; in Section 4 we show the Pohožaev type identity and then
prove the non-existence results in Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

The purpose of this section is to introduce some preliminaries.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
22
]

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
t>1
and
\{w_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{s}(\Omega)
.
If
w_{m}\to w
almost everywhere on
\Omega
as
m\to\infty
, then for every
r\in[1,s]
, we have that

\lim_{m\to\infty}{\int_{\Omega}|{|w_{m}|^{r}-|w_{m}-w|^{r}-|w|^{r}}|^{\frac{t}{r}}}\,dx=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}<p+q<\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}
and
\{w_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in

L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Assume that

(i)
{w_{m}}
weakly converges to
{w}
in
L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(ii)
{w_{m}}\to w
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx.

Proof.
By direct computation,
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}))(x)(|w_{m}(x)|^{q}-|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q})\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)(|w_{m}(x)|^{q}-|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q})\,dx

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}.

We look at each of these integrals separately. First, we use the Hölder inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}-|w|^{p}))(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left({\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\left(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}-|w|^{p})\right)|^{\frac{N(p+q)}{Np-\alpha q}}}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{Np-\alpha q}{N(p+q)}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q})^{\frac{N(p+q)}{(N+\alpha)q}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{(N+\alpha)q}{N(p+q)}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx.

Using Lemma
2.1
with
r=p
and
t=\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}
, we know that
|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p}\to|w|^{p}
,
strongly in
L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{(N+\alpha)p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
. By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, this implies that

I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}|^{p}-|w_{m}-w|^{p})\to I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p}
in
L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{Np-\alpha q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
. Since
|w_{m}-w|^{q}\rightharpoonup 0

in
L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{(N+\alpha)q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
, then
A_{2}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
. We next deal with the term
A_{3}
. We observe that

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)(|w_{m}(x)|^{q}-|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}-|w(x)|^{q})\,dx

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left({\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\left(I_{\alpha}*(|w_{m}-w|^{p}\right)|^{\frac{N(p+q)}{Np-\alpha q}}}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{Np-\alpha q}{N(p+q)}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|w_{m}(x)|^{q}-|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}-|w(x)|^{q})^{\frac{N(p+q)}{(N+\alpha)q}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{(N+\alpha)q}{N(p+q)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx,

which implies
A_{3}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
by Lemma
2.1
.
Finally, we note that

A_{1}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx

as
m\to\infty
.
The proof ends.
\Box

3.
Ground state solutions

In this section, we establish the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.1
). Let us consider the minimizing problem

M_{p}=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,dx:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{q}\,dx=1\right\},

(3.1)

defined on
{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. By Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we note that
M_{p}
is well defined.

Proposition 3.1
.

The minimizing problem
M_{p}
is achieved by a function
v\in{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, which is a solution of (
1.1
)
up to a translation.

We will use the concentration-compactness principle
[
12
]
to prove Proposition
3.1
.
To this end, we introduce the following vanishing type lemma. Let
B_{r}(x)
denote the ball centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with radius r.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq s<2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
and
r>0
. Suppose that
\{v_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}\int_{B_{r}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{s}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
.
Then for
\frac{(N+\alpha)s}{N}<p+q<\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
l=\frac{p+q}{q}\frac{N}{N+\alpha}
and
t=\frac{p+q}{p}\frac{N}{N+\alpha}
, then
lq=pt
, by Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx\ =\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v_{m}(x)|^{q}|v_{m}(z)|^{p}}{|x-z|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdz

\displaystyle\leq C\|{|v_{m}|^{q}}\|_{L^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|{|v_{m}|^{p}}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\ =C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{m}(x)|^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}.

Since
s<\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}<2^{*}
, using the classical Vanishing Lemma (see Lemma 1.21 in
[
22
]
), it is true that
v_{m}\to 0
in

L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

m\to\infty
.
Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx\to 0

as
m\to\infty
.
The proof is complete.
\Box

We now prove proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1

Let
\{v_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence of
M_{p}
and satisfy that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|v_{m}|^{p})(x)|v_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx=1

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla v_{m}(x)|^{2}+|v_{m}(x)|^{2})\,dx\to M_{p}

as
m\to\infty
.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\delta=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{1}(z)}|v_{m}(x)|^{2}\,dx>0.

Going if necessary to a subsequence, we may assume the existence of
\{z_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\int_{B_{1}(z_{m})}|v_{m}(x)|^{2}\,dx>\frac{\delta}{2}.

Let us denote
w_{m}(x)=v_{m}(x-z_{m})
, then we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}|^{p})(x)|w_{m}(x)|^{q}\,dx=1,\quad\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla w_{m}(x)|^{2}+|w_{m}(x)|^{2})\,dx\to M_{p}

and

\int_{B_{1}(0)}|w_{m}(x)|^{2}\,dx>\frac{\delta}{2}.

(3.2)

Since
\{w_{m}\}_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
w
such that

w_{m}\rightharpoonup w
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
w_{m}\to w
in
L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
w_{m}\to w
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Combining with (
3.2
), we have that
w\neq 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx\neq 0
.

Using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle 1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx+\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx

and

\displaystyle M_{p}=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\|w_{m}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}={\|w\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}+\lim_{m\to\infty}{\|w_{m}-w\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq M_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}

\displaystyle\ \quad+M_{p}\left(\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w_{m}-w|^{p})(x)|(w_{m}-w)(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}

\displaystyle=M_{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}+M_{p}\left(1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p+q}}

Then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|w|^{p})(x)|w(x)|^{q}\,dx=1
. As a consequent, we get that
M_{p}=\|w\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}
.

The proof is completed.
\Box

4.
Nonexistence

In this section, we prove a Pohožaev type identity for (
1.1
), then we obtain the non-existence result of (
1.1
) by this Pohožaev type identity.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\frac{N(p+q)}{N+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a solution of (
1.1
) and
|\nabla u|\in H_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx=(N+\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}*|u|^{p})(x)|u(x)|^{q}\,dx.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi=1
in
B_{1}(0)
, we denote

v_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla u(x).

(4.2)

Using
v_{\lambda}
as a test function in the equation (
1.1
), we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{\lambda}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,v_{\lambda}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(q(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q-2}u\,v_{\lambda}+p(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\,v_{\lambda})\,dx.

We look at each of these integrals separately.
Since
|\nabla u|\in H_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, combining with (
4.2
),
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{\lambda}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left((N-2)\varphi(\lambda x)+\lambda x\cdot\nabla\varphi(\lambda x)\right)\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{2}}{2}\,dx,

then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{\lambda}\,dx=-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

By the definition of
v_{\lambda}
, we also can get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,v_{\lambda}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla u(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla(\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(N\varphi(\lambda x)+\lambda x\cdot\nabla\varphi(\lambda x)\right)(\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{2})\,dx,

by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, it holds

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,v_{\lambda}\,dx=-\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,dx.

Finally, by direct compute, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[q(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q-2}uv_{\lambda}+p(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{q})|u|^{p-2}wv_{\lambda}]\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(I_{\alpha}(x-y)\varphi(\lambda x)x\right)\left[|u(y)|^{p}\nabla(|u(x)|^{q})+|u(y)|^{q}\nabla(|u(x)|^{p})\right]\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}(x-y)\left(|u(y)|^{p}\varphi(\lambda x)x\cdot\nabla(|u(x)|^{q})+|u(x)|^{q}\varphi(\lambda y)y\cdot\nabla(|u(y)|^{p})\right)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(y)|^{p}|u(x)|^{q}\left[I_{\alpha}(x-y)\left(\lambda\nabla\varphi(\lambda x)x+N\varphi(\lambda x)\right)-\frac{(x-y)\cdot x\varphi(\lambda x)(N-\alpha)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha+2}}\right.

\displaystyle\ \left.+I_{\alpha}(x-y)\left(\lambda\nabla\varphi(\lambda y)y+N\varphi(\lambda y)\right)+\frac{(x-y)\cdot y\varphi(\lambda y)(N-\alpha)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha+2}}\right]\,dxdy

and then

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}q(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q-2}u\,v_{\lambda}+p(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{q})|u|^{p-2}u\,v_{\lambda}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(y)|^{p}|u(x)|^{q}\left[2N\cdot I_{\alpha}(x-y)-(N-\alpha)\frac{(x-y)\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha+2}}\right]\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}2N\cdot\frac{|u(y)|^{p}|u(x)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(N-\alpha)\cdot\frac{|u(y)|^{p}|u(x)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(N+\alpha)\cdot\frac{|u(y)|^{p}|u(x)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(N+\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q}\,dx.

The proof ends.
\Box

We now prove the nonexistence result in Theorem
1.2
by Lemma
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
u
is a solution of problem (
1.1
), then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,dx=(p+q)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{q}\,dx,

combining with the Pohožaev type identity (
4.1
), we have that

(\frac{N-2}{2}-\frac{N+\alpha}{p+q})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+(\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N+\alpha}{p+q})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,dx=0.

When

{p+q}\geq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N-2}\qquad{\rm or}\qquad{p+q}\leq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N},

it holds that
u=0
.

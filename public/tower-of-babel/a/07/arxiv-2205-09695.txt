Local Hölder regularity for nonlocal parabolic p \displaystyle p -Laplace equations
Karthik Adimurthi, Harsh Prasad, Vivek Tewary

Abstract
We prove local Hölder regularity for nonlocal parabolic equations of the form ∂ t u + P.V. ​ ∫ ℝ N | u ⁡ ( x , t ) − u ⁡ ( y , t ) | p − 2 ​ ( u ⁡ ( x , t ) − u ⁡ ( y , t ) ) | x − y | N + p ​ s ​ 𝑑 y = 0 , \displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy=0, for p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) \displaystyle p\in(1,\infty) and s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \displaystyle s\in(0,1) . Our contribution extends the fundamental work of Caffarelli, Chan and Vasseur on linear nonlocal parabolic equations to equations modeled on the fractional p \displaystyle p -Laplacian.

Introduction

In this article, we prove local Hölder regularity for a nonlocal parabolic equation whose prototype equation is the parabolic fractional
\displaystyle p
-Laplacian of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy=0,

with
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
and
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. The main tools in our investigation are

•

the exponential change of variable in time discovered by E.DiBenedetto, U.Gianazza and V.Vespri, which they used to prove Harnack’s inequality for the parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations. The standard references for these works are
[
22
,
23
]
. The exponential change of variable in time leads to an expansion of positivity result which is suitable for proving Hölder regularity results. N.Liao in
[
32
]
has effected a direct proof of Hölder regularity for parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equation by using the expansion of positivity. It has also been used to give the first proofs of Hölder regularity for sign-changing solutions of doubly nonlinear equations of porous medium type in
[
6
,
7
,
33
]
. A recent work that employs the exponential change of variable to deduce regularity for elliptic anisotropic equations is
[
34
]
.

•

the energy estimate for fractional parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations containing the “good term” which substitutes the De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality in the so-called “shrinking lemma”. The “good term” has been used to great effect in the works
[
11
,
12
]
. It has been used to define a novel De Giorgi class by Cozzi in
[
14
]
to prove Hölder regularity for fractional
\displaystyle p
-minimizers. Earlier, the absence of De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality was tackled by Di Castro et. al. through a logarithmic estimate
[
19
]
. See
[
15
,
1
]
for discussions on fractional versions of De Giorgi isoperimetric inequalities.

We demonstrate two purely nonlocal phenomenon - one at a technical level which has implications for what the corresponding De Giorgi conjectures related to the "best" Holder exponent in the nonlocal case should be (see
Remark

3.6
) and another at the level of regularity which says that in certain cases, time regularity can beat space regularity when we are working with nonlocal equations (see
Remark

8.3
).

Previously, local boundedness for fractional parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations is proved in the papers
[
39
,
24
,
36
]
. Regarding Hölder regularity for fractional parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations in the spirit of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser, the only result that we are aware of is for the case
\displaystyle 2\leq p<\infty
which was studied in
[
24
]
, though we were unable to verify some of the calculations, particularly (5.23), pertaining to the logarithmic estimates in their paper. Explicit exponents for Hölder regularity for fractional parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations with no coefficients appears in
[
10
]
.

1.2
On historical development of intrinsic scaling

The method of intrinsic scaling was developed by E.DiBenedetto in
[
20
]
to prove Hölder regularity for degenerate quasilinear parabolic equations modelled on the
\displaystyle p
-Laplace operator. A technical requirement of the proof was a novel logarithmic estimate which aids in the expansion of positivity. Subsequently, the proof of Hölder regularity for the singular case was given in
[
40
]
by switching the scaling from time variable to the space variable. These results were collected in E.DiBenedetto’s treatise
[
21
]
. After a gap of several years, E.DiBenedetto, U.Gianazza and V.Vespri
[
22
,
23
]
were able to prove Harnack’s inequality for the parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations by a new technique involving an exponential change of variable in time. This proof relies on expansion of positivity estimates and does not involve logarithmic test functions. Then, a new proof of Hölder regularity was given with a more geometric flavour in
[
26
]
. This theory was extended to generalized parabolic
\displaystyle p
-Laplace equations with Orlicz growth in
[
27
,
28
]
.

In this paper, we assume that solutions to the nonlocal equation are locally bounded. Local boundedness for the nonlocal elliptic equations is proved in
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 6.2]
. In the parabolic case, local boundedness is proved in
[

,

,

]
.

2.2
Structure of the equation

Let
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle p>1
be fixed, and
\displaystyle\Lambda\geq 1
be a given constant. For almost every
\displaystyle x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
\displaystyle K:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a symmetric measurable function satisfying

\frac{(1-s)}{\Lambda|x-y|^{N+ps}}\leq K(x,y,t)\leq\frac{(1-s)\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

(2.1)

In this paper, we are interested in the regularity theory for the equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u=0,

(2.2)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
is the operator formally defined by

\mathcal{L}u=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

2.3
Function spaces

Let
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
, we denote by
\displaystyle p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
the conjugate exponent of
\displaystyle p
. Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be an open subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define the
Sobolev-Slobodekiĭ
space, which is the fractional analogue of Sobolev spaces as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega):[\psi]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\},\qquad\text{for}\ s\in(0,1),

where the seminorm
\displaystyle[\cdot]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
is defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle[\psi]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The space when endowed with the norm
\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[\psi]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
becomes a Banach space. The space
\displaystyle W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is the completion of
\displaystyle C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in the norm of
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We will use the notation
\displaystyle W^{s,p}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
to denote the space of functions in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\displaystyle u-u_{0}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let
\displaystyle I
be an interval and let
\displaystyle V
be a separable, reflexive Banach space, endowed with a norm
\displaystyle\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{V}
. We denote by
\displaystyle V^{*}
to be its topological dual space. Let
\displaystyle v
be a mapping such that for a.e.
\displaystyle t\in I
,
\displaystyle v(t)\in V
. If the function
\displaystyle t\mapsto\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{V}
is measurable on
\displaystyle I
, then
\displaystyle v
is said to belong to the Banach space
\displaystyle L^{p}(I;V)
provided
\displaystyle\int_{I}\left\lVert v(t)\right\rVert_{V}^{p}\,dt<\infty
. It is well known that the dual space
\displaystyle L^{p}(I;V)^{*}
can be characterized as
\displaystyle L^{p^{\prime}}(I;V^{*})
.

Since the boundedness result requires some finiteness condition on the nonlocal tails, we define the tail space for some
\displaystyle m>0
and
\displaystyle s>0
as follows:

L^{m}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L^{m}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{m}}{1+|x|^{N+s}}\,dx<+\infty\right\}.

Then a nonlocal tail is defined by

\text{Tail}_{m,s,\infty}(v;x_{0},R,I):=\text{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},R,t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}):=\sup_{t\in(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0})}\left(R^{sm}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{m-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sm}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}},

where
\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-T,T)
and the interval
\displaystyle I=(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0})\subseteq(-T,T)
. From this definition, it follows that for any
\displaystyle v\in L^{\infty}(-T,T;L^{m-1}_{sm}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, there holds
\displaystyle\text{Tail}_{m,s,\infty}(v;x_{0},R,I)<\infty
.

2.4
Definition of weak solution

Now, we are ready to state the definition of a weak sub(super)-solution.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\displaystyle u\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(I;W^{s,p}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega))\cap C_{\text{loc}}(I;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(I;L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

\displaystyle\Omega\times I
if for any closed interval
\displaystyle[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset I
and any compact set
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(x,t_{2})\phi(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(x,t_{1})\phi(x,t_{1})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))\,dy\,dx\,dt\leq(\geq)0,

for all
\displaystyle\phi\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(I,W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap W^{1,2}_{\text{loc}}(I,L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}))

3.2
Shrinking Lemma

One of the main difficulties we face when dealing with regularity issues for nonlocal equations is the lack of a corresponding isoperimetric inequality for
\displaystyle W^{s,p}
functions. Indeed, since such functions can have jumps, a generic isoperimetric inequality seems out of reach at this time (see
[
15
,
1
]
). One way around this issue is to note that since we are working with solutions of an equation, which we expect to be continuous and hence have no jumps, we could try and cook up an isoperimetric inequality for solutions. Such a strategy turns out to be feasible due to the presence of the “good term” or the isoperimetric term in the Caccioppoli inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle k<l<m
be arbitrary levels and
\displaystyle A\geq 1
. Then,

(l-k)(m-l)^{p-1}\left|[u>m]\cap B_{\rho}\right|\left|[u<k]\cap B_{\rho}\right|\leq C\rho^{N+sp}\int_{B_{\rho}}(u-l)_{-}(x)\int_{B_{A\rho}}\frac{(u-l)_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\,dx,

where
\displaystyle C=C(N,s,p,A)>0
.

Proof.

We estimate from below

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}(u-l)_{-}(x)\int_{B_{A\rho}}\frac{(u-l)_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{\rho^{N+sp}}\int_{B_{\rho}}(u-l)_{-}(x)\int_{B_{\rho}}(u-l)_{+}^{p-1}(y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{\rho^{N+sp}}\int_{B_{\rho}\cap\{u<k\}}(u-l)_{-}(x)\int_{B_{\rho}\cap\{u>m\}}(u-l)_{+}^{p-1}(y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{\rho^{N+sp}}\int_{B_{\rho}\cap\{u<k\}}(l-k)\int_{B_{\rho}\cap\{u>m\}}(m-l)^{p-1}\,dy\,dx.

∎

We now prove the shrinking lemma using
Lemma

. It is usually proved in the local case using the De Giorgi isoperimetric inequality.

3.3
Tail Estimates

In this section, we shall outline how the estimate for the tail term is made and we shall refer to this section whenever a similar calculation is required in subsequent sections.

Given a function
\displaystyle\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\rho})
and any level
\displaystyle k
, we want to estimate

\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in J}}{{x\in\spt\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy,

where
\displaystyle J
is some time interval. In order to do this, we will typically choose
\displaystyle\zeta
to be supported in
\displaystyle B_{\vartheta\rho}
for some
\displaystyle\vartheta\in(0,1)
. With such a choice, we have

|y-y_{0}|\leq|x-y|\left(1+\frac{|x-y_{0}|}{|x-y|}\right)\leq|x-y|\left(1+\frac{\vartheta}{(1-\vartheta)}\right),

so we can make a first estimate

\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in J}}{{x\in\spt\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{(1-\vartheta)^{N+sp}}\underset{t\in J}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|y-y_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dy.

In the local case, we could always estimate
\displaystyle(u-k)_{-}\leq k
because we take
\displaystyle u\geq 0
locally. However, in the Tail term we have no information regarding the solution outside the ball and so unless we make a global boundedness assumption (for e.g.
\displaystyle u\geq 0
in full space), the best we can do is

(u-k)_{-}\leq u_{-}+k,

which leads us to the next estimate

\underset{t\in J}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|y-y_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dy\leq C(p)\frac{k^{p-1}}{\rho^{sp}}+C(p)\,\underset{t\in J}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|y-y_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dy.

Putting together the above estimates yield

\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in J}}{{x\in\spt\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\leq\frac{C(p)}{\rho^{sp}}\frac{1}{(1-\vartheta)^{N+sp}}\left[k^{p-1}+\tll_{\infty}^{p-1}(u_{-};y_{0},\rho,J)\right].

Finally we usually want an estimate of the form

\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\in J}}{{x\in\spt\zeta}}}{\esssup}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(y_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{-}^{p-1}(y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\leq\frac{C(p)}{(1-\vartheta)^{N+sp}}\frac{k^{p-1}}{\rho^{sp}},

and so we impose the condition that

\tll_{\infty}^{p-1}(u_{-};y_{0},\rho,J)\leq k^{p-1}.

This is the origin of the various Tail alternatives.

Remark 3.7
.

We will see that it is the Tail that captures the nonlocality of our equation in the sense that if it is small then the proofs become local. In fact, when dealing with nonnegative supersolutions, we only need the Tail of the negative part of the solution to be small and so if we assume that it is nonnegative in full space then the Tail alternatives are automatically verified and the proofs of the constituent lemmas become “local proofs”.

Lemma 4.1

\displaystyle u

2.2

3.1

\displaystyle(u-M)_{-}

\displaystyle B_{\rho}\times(0,\theta\rho^{sp}]

\displaystyle\theta>0

4.2

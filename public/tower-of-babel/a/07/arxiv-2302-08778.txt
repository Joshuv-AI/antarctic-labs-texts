Sukmoon Huh, Min Gyo Jeong

Abstract
. We study the logarithmic vector bundles associated to arrangements of smooth irreducible curves with small degree on the blow-up of the projective plane at one point. We then investigate whether they are Torelli arrangements, that is, they can be recovered from the attached logarithmic vector bundles.

1.
introduction

In this paper, we study logarithmic vector bundles on the blow-up of a smooth projective surface, mostly in the case when the surface is the projective plane. For a given arrangement
\cal{D}
of reduced and effective divisors on a smooth projective variety
X
, one can consider a sheaf
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
of differential
1
-forms with logarithmic poles along
\cal{D}
, called the
logarithmic sheaf
associated to
\cal{D}
. It is first introduced by P. Deligne in
[
7
]
to define the mixed Hodge structure on the complement of
\cal{D}
. When the associated divisors are arranged with simple normal crossings, the sheaf turns out to be locally free and we call it
logarithmic vector bundle
. In this article, we assume that the divisors in consideration are arranged with simple normal crossings, unless otherwise specified.

In
[
9
]
, I. Dolgachev and M. Kapranov studied the logarithmic vector bundles associated to arrangements of hyperplanes
\cal{H}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\}
in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. They are interested in the question when
\cal{H}
can be recovered from
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log\cal{H})
; this kind of problem is called the
Torelli problem
. The same problem can be considered for more general setting with arrangements
\cal{D}
of divisors, and if
\cal{D}
can be recovered from
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
, then we call it a
Torelli arrangement
.

For example, in
[
9
]
it was proven that a general hyperplane arrangement
\cal{H}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a Torelli arrangement if
m\geq 2n+1
. More specifically,
\cal{H}
is a Torelli arrangement if and only if the hyperplanes in
\cal{H}
does not osculate any rational normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Later the result is extended to the case
m\geq n+3
by J. Vallès in
[
15
]
. Moreover, the associated logarithmic vector bundle
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log\cal{H})
is stable with Chern polynomial
(1-t)^{n-m+1}
. Thus the Torelli arrangements on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
define a generically injective map from the family of arrangements to a moduli space of stable vector bundles, e.g. if
n=2
, then the induced map

\displaystyle\Phi:~~~\mathbf{F}

\displaystyle~~\longrightarrow~~\mathbf{M}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle\cal{D}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt\mapsto~~\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{1}(\log\cal{D})

is generically injective, where
\mathbf{F}
is the family of arrangements of
m\geq 6
lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbf{M}(c_{1},c_{2})
is the moduli space of stable vector bundles with appropriate Chern classes.

After that, many studies follow with more generalized and different settings. In
[
13
]
, K. Ueda and M. Yosingwa suggestesd an equivalent condition for a single smooth hypersurface of degree
d\geq 3
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
to be a Torelli arrangement, that is, the defining polynomial of the hypersurface is not of Sebastiani-Thom type. In
[
1
]
, E. Angelini considered arrangements of
\ell
hypersurfaces
\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell}\}
of the same degree
d\geq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. She showed that if
\ell\geq N+4=\binom{n+d}{d}+3
, then it is a Torelli arrangement unless the hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
corresponding to
D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell}
through the
Veronese map
of degree
d
do not osculate a rational normal curve of degree
N
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
In
[
4
]
E. Ballico, S. Huh and F. Malaspina studied the arrangements of hyperplane sections in an
n
-dimensional smooth quadric hypersurface
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, to show that an arrangement of sufficiently many hyperplane sections is a Torelli arrangement. Recently in
[
11
]

a generalized version of logarithmic vector bundle is introduced and its Torelli problem is investigated.

In this article, as another attempt with the same direction, we focus on the logarithmic vector bundles on the blow-up of a smooth projective surface. In Section
2
we provide our main theorem asserting the relation between the logarithmic vector bundles on a smooth projective surface
X
and its blow-up
\widetilde{X}
; see Theorem
2.2
. Using this main theorem, we obtain a positive answer to the Torelli problem for logarithmic vector bundles on the Del Pezzo surface
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{P}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1))
of degree
8
associated to arrangements of rational normal curves of degree
3
. Then in Section
3
we consider arrangements of lower degree curves on
\mathbb{F}
, intersecting the exceptional divisor
E
.

2.
Logarithmic vector bundle on blown-up surfaces

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geq 2
over the field of complex numbers. Let us denote by
\cal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\}
be an arragement of irreducible divisors
D_{i}
’s on
X
. Then one can define the sheaf of differential
1
-forms with logarithmic poles along
\cal{D}
, denoted by
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
and called the
logarithmic sheaf
; refer to
[
7
]
. In general, the sheaf
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
is reflexive. When
\cal{D}
has simple normal crossings, then the logarithmic sheaf turns out to be locally free and it fits into an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log\cal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\cal{O}_{D_{i}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

called the
Poincaré residue sequence
. In this case, if
z_{1}\cdots z_{\ell}=0
is the local defining equation of
\cal{D}
near
x\in X
, then the section of
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
are meromorphic differential forms

\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{l}u_{i}d\log z_{i}

with a
1
-form
\omega
and functions
u_{i}
, where
\langle z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\rangle
is the local coordinates.

Remark 2.1
.

For a sub-arrangement
\cal{D}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{k}\}\subset\cal{D}
with
k<m
, one can also have a generalized version of the Poincaré residue sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D}^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\bigoplus_{i=k+1}^{m}\cal{O}_{D_{i}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

From now on we assume
n=2
, i.e.,
X
is a smooth projective surface. For a fixed finite set of distinct points
Z=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}\subset X
, denote by
\eta:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
the blow-up of
X
along
Z
with the exceptional divisor
E_{j}:=\eta^{-1}(p_{j})
for each

, we have an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\eta^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{E}\cong\cal{O}_{E}(-2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0;

refer to

10

. Tensoring the sequence (

Since
\left(\eta^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}\right)_{|E}\cong\cal{O}_{E}\oplus\cal{O}_{E}
on

, we obtain the following from the sequence in the above

\left(\Omega^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}\right)_{|E}\cong\cal{O}_{E}(1)\oplus\cal{O}_{E}(-2).

\cal{D}=\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{m}\}
of smooth irreducible curves on
X
, we denote by

\widetilde{\cal{D}}=\{\widetilde{C}_{1},\widetilde{C}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{m}\}

an arrangement of smooth irreducible curves with
\widetilde{C}_{i}
the strict transform of
C_{i}
along
\eta

i
. We also set

\widetilde{\cal{D}}^{E}=\widetilde{\cal{D}}\cup\{E_{1},\dots,E_{k}\}.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
\cal{D}
is a Torelli arrangement on
X
with
Z\cap C_{i}=\emptyset
, then so are
\widetilde{\cal{D}}
and
\widetilde{\cal{D}}^{E}
on
\widetilde{X}
.

Proof.

Applying the push-forward functor
\eta_{*}
to the second horizontal sequence of (
5
), one can get an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})\cong\eta_{*}\eta^{*}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\eta_{*}\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\eta_{*}\cal{O}_{E}(-2)\cong 0,

by the projection formula together with
\eta_{*}\cal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\cal{O}_{X}
. The last vanishing comes from the Theorem on Formal Functions in
[
10
, Section III.11]
. Thus we get
\eta_{*}\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}})\cong\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
and so
\widetilde{\cal{D}}
is a Torelli arrangement. On the other hand, by Theorem
2.2
we get

\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})\cong\eta_{*}\left(\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}}^{E})(-E)\right)

and so
\widetilde{\cal{D}}^{E}
is also a Torelli arrangement.
∎

Example 2.4
.

Let

\cal{L}=\left\{L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{m}\right\}
of

with simple normal crossings, we let

be the strict transform of

for each

\widetilde{\cal{L}}=\{\widetilde{L}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{L}_{m}\}
. Assume now that

. Setting

as before, we have

. In particular, by

.

Proposition 2.6

If
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})

Clearly, this map has full rank and so it is injective. Thus we get

\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\widetilde{\cal{C}}^{E})_{|f}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cal{O}_{f}\left(\frac{\delta-1}{2}\right)^{\oplus 2}&\hbox{($\delta$ : odd)}\\
\cal{O}_{f}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}\left(\frac{\delta-2}{2}\right)&\hbox{($\delta$ : even)}\end{array}\right.

for a generic fibre
f
. In particular, the restriction of the map (
7
) to
f
gives
a+b\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
in either case. Consequently, we have

3a+b=2a+(a+b)\leq\frac{3\delta-8}{2}<\frac{3\delta-7}{2},

a contradiction.
∎

For the stability of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\cal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\}
be an arrangement of
m
smooth irreducible curves with simple normal crossings and
C_{i}\in|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d_{i})|
for each
i
. We may assume that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{m}
without loss of generality. Then
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})
is stable if and only if the corresponding
(m+1)
-tuple
(m;d_{1},\dots,d_{m})
is not contained in the set

\mathbb{S}=\left\{~(1;1),~(2;1,1),~(2;2,1),~(3;1,1,1)~\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\cal{F}:=\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})(k)
be a
normalized bundle
of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})
with

k=\begin{cases}\quad-\cfrac{c_{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C}))}{2}&\quad\text{if $c_{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C}))$ is even}\\
\quad-\cfrac{c_{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C}))+1}{2}&\quad\text{if $c_{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C}))$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Then using the sequence (
6
), we get a long exact sequence of cohomology

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-d_{i}+k)\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(k)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1+k)^{\oplus 3}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\cal{F}\right)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

Note that the first term
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-d_{i}+k))
vanishes except the case
(1;1)
, while the midterm
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(k)^{\oplus(m-1)})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1+k)^{\oplus 3})
vanishes except all the cases in
\mathbb{S}
. Conversely,
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{F})
does not vanish for all the cases in
\mathbb{S}
. Therefore, the arrangement
\cal{C}
does not correspond to one of the cases in
\mathbb{S}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\cal{F})=0
, which is again equivalent to the stability of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})
by
[
14
, Ch.2, Lemma 1.2.5]
. Moreover, we remark that
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\log\cal{C})
is semistable for the case
(2;2,1)
, while the bundle is a direct sum of line bundles in the other cases of
\mathbb{S}
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\cal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\}
be an arrangement of
m
smooth irreducible curves with simple normal crossings and
C_{i}\in|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d)|
for each
i
. Set
\mathbf{F}
the family of arrangements
\widetilde{\cal{C}}^{E}
and assume that
m\geq\binom{2+d}{d}+3
. Then we get a generically injective map

\Phi:\quad\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{F}}(c_{1},c_{2}),

where
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{F}}(c_{1},c_{2})
is the moduli space of stable sheaves of rank two with Chern classes

c_{1}=(md-3)H+2E\quad\text{and}\quad c_{2}=\frac{d^{2}m^{2}+d(d-6)m+4}{2}

with respect to the anticanonical polarization
-K_{\mathbb{F}}=3H-E
.

Proof.

The generic injectivity comes from
[
15
, Corollary 3.1]
for
d=1
, and
[
1
, Corollary 5.6]
for
d\geq 2
. Then the assertion follows from Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

In
[
11
]
a general philosophy about the Torelli property on logarithmic vector bundle was suggested: one can get a positive answer to the Torelli property, after sufficiently many blow-ups. This point of view can be reformulated as follows, using Theorem
2.2
. Let
\mathbf{F}
be the set of arrangements
\cal{D}^{\prime}
of divisors in the system
|\cal{O}_{X}(\cal{D})|
with
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D}^{\prime})\cong\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
. Setting
\eta:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
the blow-up of
X
along a set
Z=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
of
k
points in
X
, we get an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}^{\prime}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}^{\prime}}^{E})\cong\eta^{*}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D}^{\prime})(E)\cong\eta^{*}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})(E)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{E}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

with
E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{k}
. In particular, the isomorphism class of
\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\widetilde{\cal{D}^{\prime}})

is determined by the surjection from
\eta^{*}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})(E)
to
\cal{O}_{E}
, and so it corresponds to a point in

\underbrace{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{k\text{ copies}}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Hom}(\eta^{*}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\cal{D})(E)_{p_{i}},\cal{O}_{p_{i}}).

Hence, we get a map

\Psi:\quad\mathbf{F}\dashrightarrow\underbrace{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{k\text{ copies}},

and the general philosophy can be rephrased as follows: the map
\Psi
can be expected to be generically injective for general
Z
with sufficiently large
k
.

3.
Hirzebruch surfaces

In this section we will focus on the blown-up plane
\mathbb{F}=\mathrm{Bl}_{p}\mathbb{P}^{2}
of Example
2.4
and consider it as a rational ruled surface, namely the Hirzebruch surface. From this view, we will introduce a methodology to find the canonical exact sequence for a logarithmic vector bundle on
\mathbb{F}
, introduced in
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))
for
e\geq 0
be the Hirzebruch surface with minimal self-intersection number
-e
and its ruling

\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We have
\mathrm{h}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}})=g(\mathbb{P}^{1})=0
, and for
e>0
we have

\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}})=\dim\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{e})=\dim\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})+\dim\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))-1=e+5.

We also have
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{F}_{e})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 2}\cong\mathbb{Z}\langle h,f\rangle
, where
f
is a fiber of a ruling of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
and
h
is the section with
h^{2}=-e
,
h\cdot f=1
and
f^{2}=0
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is well known that for a divisor
D=ah+bf
a line bundle
\cal{L}=\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(D)
on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
is very ample if and only if it is ample, which is also equivalent to the condition that
a>0
and
b>ae
. The line bundle
\cal{L}
is effective if and only if
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 0
. There is also a similar equivalent condition for the linear system
|\cal{L}|
to contain an irreducible (smooth) curve, that is either

\mathrm{(i)}~D=h,\quad\mathrm{(ii)}~D=f,\quad\mathrm{(iii)}~a>0,~b>ae,\quad\text{or}\quad\mathrm{(iv)}~e>0,~a>0,~b=ae.

Since
\omega_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(-2h-(e+2)f)
, there is a subcanonical polarization of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
if and only if
e=0,1
. Since the ruling
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a submersion, it induces a surjective map
\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}\rightarrow\pi^{\ast}\cal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(2f)
. So from
\omega_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}^{\vee}\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(2h+(e+2)f)
we get that
\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}
fits into an exact sequence

(8)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(2h+ef)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(2f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Remark 3.2
.

The Chern classes of
\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}
is
(c_{1},c_{2})=(2h+(e+2)f,4)
. By restricting the sequence (
8
) to
f
, we get that the splitting of
\cal{T}_{\cal{F}_{e}}
over each fibre
f
is

\left(\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}\right)_{|f}\cong\cal{O}_{f}(2)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}.

On the other hand, from the normal exact sequence for
h\subset\mathbb{F}_{e}
, we get

\left(\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}\right)_{|h}\cong\cal{O}_{h}(2)\oplus\cal{O}_{h}(-e).

Remark 3.3
.

In the case
e=1
the surface
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{F}_{1}
is isomorphic to the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at one point
p
with
\eta:\mathbb{F}\cong\mathrm{Bl}_{p}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Recall that we already used this notation for the single point blown-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the previous section. The surface
\mathbb{F}
is the del Pezzo surface of degree
8
, and so
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{T}_{\mathbb{F}})=6
. From this point of view, note that a system of curves
\left|\eta^{*}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)\right|
is equal to the system
\left|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h+f)\right|
.

Let
\cal{D}=\{D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{m}\}
be an arrangement of smooth irreducible curves
D_{i}\in|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(a_{i}h+b_{i}f)|
with simple normal crossings on
\mathbb{F}
so that
\cal{D}\in|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha h+\beta f)|
with
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}
and
\beta=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}
. Then from the Poincaré residue sequence for
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})
we get

c_{1}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D}))=(\alpha-2)h+(\beta-3)f

and

c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D}))=4-\alpha-2\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(2a_{i}b_{i}-a_{i}^{2})+\sum_{i<j}(a_{i}b_{j}+a_{j}b_{i}-a_{i}a_{j}).

Ever since Brosius showed in
[
6
]
that any vector bundle of rank two on ruled surface can be described by a canonical extension, there have been many studies using similar tools to study vector bundles of rank two on Hirzebruch surface; see
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
. Throughout this section, we use notations and terminologies of the survey article
[
3
]
. The following result is one of main methodologies that we keep using throughout this section; see
[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\cal{F}
be a vector bundle of rank two on
\mathbb{F}
.

(i)

Set
(d,d^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus 2}
with
d\geq d^{\prime}
the splitting type of
\cal{F}
over a generic fiber
f
. Then
\pi_{*}\cal{F}(-dh)
is a vector bundle of rank
1
(resp. rank
2
) if
d>d^{\prime}
(resp.
d=d^{\prime}
).

(ii)

If
d>d^{\prime}
, set
r:=\deg\pi_{*}\cal{F}(-dh)
. If
d=d^{\prime}
, set
\pi_{*}\cal{F}(-dh)\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(r)\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(s)
with
r\geq s
. Then we have

-r=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\;\big|\;\exists\cal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\;\textrm{with }\;\mathrm{h}^{0}(\cal{F}(-dh)\otimes\pi^{*}\cal{L})\neq 0\right\}.

In Proposition
3.4
we may associate to
\cal{F}
a pair of integers
(d,r)
for which there exists an exact sequence

(9)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(dh+rf)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{F}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z,\mathbb{F}}(d^{\prime}h+r^{\prime}f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

for some zero-dimensional subscheme
Z\subset\mathbb{F}
. We call it a
canonical extension
of
\cal{F}
. We have

\deg(Z)=c_{2}(\cal{F})+a(d-r)-bd+2dr-d^{2},

together with
c_{1}(\cal{F})=ah+bf
and
r+r^{\prime}=b
. We notice that

\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\cal{I}_{Z,\mathbb{F}}(d^{\prime}h+r^{\prime}f),\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(dh+rf))=\deg(Z)+\mathrm{h}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}((d-d^{\prime})h+(r-r^{\prime})f)).

Remark 3.5
.

In the sequence (
9
), the length of
Z
measures the
uniformity
of
\cal{F}
. That is,
\cal{F}
has the same splitting type over all the fibers of
\pi
if and only if
Z=\emptyset
. We say that such a bundle is
\pi
-uniform
; see
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
. For example, as we shall see in Example
3.7
, the logarithmic bundle
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)
associated to a fibre
f\subset\mathbb{F}
is
\pi
-uniform with the splitting type
(0,-2)
, and it admits a canonical extension (
11
).

Remark 3.6
.

As studied in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
, any vector bundle
\cal{F}
of rank two on
\mathbb{F}
is described canonically the extension (
9
). If we call the twisted bundle
\cal{F}(-dh-rf)
the
normalization
of
\cal{F}
, then the extension
(
9
)
is unique if and only if the space of global sections of the normalization of
\cal{F}
is a 1-dimensional. Thus the extension
(
9
)
is unique if
d>d^{\prime}
, or
d=d^{\prime}
and
r>s
.

Let us consider logarithmic vector bundles attached to some arrangements of lower degree curves on
\mathbb{F}
, using its canonical extension.

Example 3.7
.

Set
\cal{D}=\{f\}
for a fibre of
\pi:\mathbb{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We have an exact sequence

(10)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

and the Chern classes of
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)
is
(c_{1},c_{2})=(-2h-2f,2)
. Then we have
\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log f)\right)_{|g}\cong\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}\right)_{|g}\cong\cal{O}_{g}\oplus\cal{O}_{g}(-2)
for any fibre
g\neq f
. Now
tensor the sequence (
10
) with
\cal{O}_{f}
to obtain

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{T}or_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{f},\cal{O}_{f})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

From
\mathcal{T}or_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{f},\cal{O}_{f})\cong\cal{O}_{f}
and Remark
3.2
, the restriction
\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)\right)_{|f}
fits into the following exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}(-2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}^{1}(\log f)\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

This implies that
\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}^{1}(\log f)\right)_{|f}\cong\cal{O}_{f}(a)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}(-2-a)
for
a\in\{-1,0\}
. But the function
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined by
p\mapsto a-b
, where
\left(\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)\right)_{|\pi^{-1}(p)}
has splitting
(a,b)
with
a\geq b
, is upper semi-continuous. Thus we have
a=0
and so the bundle
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)
is
\pi
-uniform with
(d,r)=(0,-1)
. In particular, we get an exact sequence

(11)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

where
\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f),\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-f))=\mathrm{h}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(2h))=1
. Now, from the twisted standard exact sequence for
h\subset\mathbb{F}
, we get a long exact sequence of cohomology

0\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h))\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(2h))\cong\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(h,\cal{O}_{h}(-2))\cong\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{F},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h))\cong 0.

Here, we have
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(2h))\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-h),\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h))
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(h,\cal{O}_{h}(-2))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{h}^{1}(\cal{O}_{h}(1),\cal{O}_{h}(-1))
so that the map
\alpha
sends an extension

(12)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{F}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-h)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

to its restriction to
h
. The unique nontrivial extension class
\xi\in\operatorname{Ext}_{h}^{1}(\cal{O}_{h}(1),\cal{O}_{h}(-1))
corresponds to an extension

(13)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{h}(-1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{h}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{h}(1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

and let
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F},\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(2h))
be an element with
\xi^{\prime}_{|h}=\xi
. The class
\xi^{\prime}
corresponds to an extension (
12
) whose restriction to
h
is (
13
). Returning to the sequence (
11
), we have

\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log f)\otimes\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(h+f)\right)_{|h}\cong\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log f)\right)_{|_{h}}\cong\cal{O}_{h}(1)\oplus\cal{O}_{h}(-1).

In particular, the bundle
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log f)
does not correspond to the extension class
\xi^{\prime}
so that it corresponds to the trivial extension of (
11
). Therefore, we have
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log f)\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-f)\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f)
.

Extending the consequence of the above example, we give the following lemma for arrangement of many fibres on
\mathbb{F}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\cal{D}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
with
m
distinct fibers of
\pi:\mathbb{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then we have

\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\cong\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}((m-2)f)\oplus\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f).

Proof.

Note that the case when
m=1
is already dealt in Example
3.7
and so we assume that
m\geq 2
. Tensoring
\cal{O}_{f}
to the exact sequnce

(14)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\cal{O}_{f_{i}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

we get

(15)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\oplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}or^{1}(\cal{O}_{f_{i}},\cal{O}_{f})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}(-2)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\cal{O}_{f_{i}}\right)\otimes\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

If
f\neq f_{i}
for any
i
, the sequence (
15
) yields

\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\right)_{|f}\cong\cal{O}_{f}(-2)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}.

On the otherhand, if

for some

, we get an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}(-2)\oplus\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

so that

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}(-2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\right)_{|f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{f}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Thus we may take
\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\log\cal{D})\right)_{|f}
among two different types of
(0,-2)
and
(-1,-1)
. In fact, it must be
(0,-2)
because of the semicontinuity that we have mentioned in Example

. Thus
\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log\cal{D})
is

-uniform with

and so we have an exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}((m-2)f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\Omega_{\mathbb{F}}^{1}(\log\cal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Then
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(-2h-f),\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}((m-2)f))\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(2h+(m-1)f))\cong 0
for
m\geq 2
, and the assertion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.10

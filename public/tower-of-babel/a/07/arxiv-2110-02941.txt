Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p
dividing the order of
G

) of the group algebra

[

HH^{1}(B)\neq\{0\}
for a
p

In this paper we settle this problem for the blocks of the sporadic Mathieu groups, in particular showing the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a sporadic Mathieu group. Then for all primes
p
dividing
|G|
and for all
p
-blocks
B
of
kG
with a non-trivial defect group,
HH^{1}(B)\neq\{0\}
. Moreover, for each
B
we determine the dimensions
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(B))
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the process of proving Theorem

p

40
such

-blocks with non-zero defect, we determine that
37
have

that is a solvable Lie algebra, and
1
has
HH^{1}
that is a simple Lie algebra. Of the
18

p
-blocks whose

is a
2

Remark 1.3

By Farrell and Kessar

Moreover, for

3.

The theory of blocks with cyclic defect group is well understood (see for example

,

]
) and the

of such blocks has a Lie algebra structure that is particularly simple to describe.

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
dividing
|G|
. If
B=kGb
is a block of
kG
with block idempotent
b
and a defect group
P
, and if
C=kN_{G}(P)c
is the Brauer correspondent of
B
for some idempotent
c
, then we form the inertial quotient of
B
, the group
E=N_{G}(P,f)/PC_{G}(P)
where
f
is a block of
kC_{G}(P)
satisfying
fc=f
. The inertial quotient is unique up to isomorphism, and so independent of choice of
f
. If
B=B_{0}
is the principal block, then
\mathrm{Br}_{P}(b)
is the principal block of of
kC_{G}(P)
, so that

and
E=N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P)
. Note also that if
P
is abelian,
PC_{G}(P)=C_{G}(P)
.

The inertial quotient appears frequently in the context of blocks with cyclic defect groups, in view of the results stated below. Throughout, unless otherwise stated
P
will denote a nontrivial cyclic group of order
p
and
E
a
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(P)
, of order
e
. Let
x
and
y
be generators of
P
and
E
respectively, then
E
acts on
P
and we will write the action as

Theorem 3.2

Let

Proposition 4.1

Let

Appendix A
The dimension of the
HH^{1}
of a finite group algebra

The much celebrated centraliser decomposition allows us to determine the dimensions of the
HH^{1}
of a finite group algebra with relative ease. It’s heavy use throughout the calculations and proofs in this paper confirms its utility, and so we explain briefly where these dimensions come from, as well as detail the
GAP
code used to efficiently implement the centraliser decomposition and compute these dimensions.

Proposition A.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 2.11.2(ii)]

As a
k
-vector space, the additive structure of the Hochschild cohomology of
kG
is given by

HH^{*}(kG)\cong\bigoplus_{x\in G/\sim}H^{*}(C_{G}(x);k)

where
G/\sim
is the set of conjugacy class representatives of
G
and
C_{G}(x)
is the centraliser of
x\in G
.

The first cohomology group of
G
with coefficients in
k^{\times}
,
H^{1}(G;k^{\times})
, will always be considered with trivial action of
G
on
k^{\times}
. One therefore sees that

HH^{1}(kG)\cong\bigoplus_{x\in G/\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k)

where each summand is a finite abelian group of order dividing
|C_{G}(x)|
, and in particular when viewed as
k
-modules,
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))=\sum_{x\in G/\sim}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k))
.

There exists a twisted group algebra analogue to the centraliser decomposition

Proposition A.2
.

[
39
, Lemma 3.5]

There is an additive decomposition

HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)\cong\bigoplus_{x\in G/\sim}H^{1}(C_{G}(x);V_{x,\alpha}),

where
V_{x,\alpha}
is a
1
-dimensional
C_{G}(x)
-module spanned by the image
\hat{x}\in k_{\alpha}G
of
x\in G
, with action given by
g\cdot\hat{x}=\alpha(g,x)\alpha(x,g)^{-1}\hat{x}
for all
g\in C_{G}(x)
.

To calculate the individual dimensions of
\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k)
in Proposition
A.1
we use the next result, which then forms the rest of the
GAP
code.

Lemma A.3
.

The
k
-vector space
\mathrm{Hom}(G,k)
is nonzero if and only if
G
has a quotient isomorphic to a nontrivial
p
-group. In particular,
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\mathrm{Hom}(G,k))=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\mathrm{Hom}(R/\Phi(R),k))
, where
R=G/O^{p}(G)
.

We detail below the simple
GAP
commands to determine the dimensions of the
HH^{1}
of a finite group algebra (in particular a group on which
GAP
is able to perform calculations), using the centraliser decomposition and Lemma
A.3
.

Once a group
G
is defined in
GAP
, the command
div
below gives the set of prime divisors of
|G|
,
\pi(G)
. The functions following
div
then take as input either
G
, or
G
and a prime
p\in\pi(G)
, and output the following lists;

1.

The centralisers of a complete set of conjugacy class representatives of
G
,

2.

For each conjugacy class representative
x\in G/\sim
, the groups
C_{G}(x)/O^{p}(C_{G}(x))=R
,

3.

The elementary abelian
p
-groups
R/\Phi(R)
,

4.

The rank of
R/\Phi(R)
,

respectively. The final function then produces a list of dimensions
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))
, with one entry for each
p\in\pi(G)
.

div:=PrimeDivisors(Size(G));

ListFpCentralisers:=function(X);

> return List(ConjugacyClasses(X),i->

Image(IsomorphismFpGroup(Centralizer(X,Representative(i)))));

> end;;

ListMaxPQuot:=function(X,p);

> return List(ListFpCentralisers(X),i -> Image(EpimorphismPGroup(i,p)));

> end;;

ListElAb:=function(X,p);

> return List(ListMaxPQuot(X,p), i -> i/FrattiniSubgroup(i));

> end;;

ListPRank:=function(X,p);

> return List(ListElAb(X,p), i -> RankPGroup(i));

> end;;

dimHH1:=function(X);

> return List(div,i->Sum(ListPRank(X,i)));

> end;

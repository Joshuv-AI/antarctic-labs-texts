Computing fusion products of MV cycles using the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism
Roger Bai, Anne Dranowski, Joel Kamnitzer

Abstract
The fusion of two Mirković–Vilonen cycles is a degeneration of their product, defined using the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian. In this paper, we put in place a conceptually elementary approach to computing this product in type A A . We do so by transferring the problem to a fusion of generalized orbital varieties using the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism. As an application, we explicitly compute all cluster exchange relations in the coordinate ring of the upper-triangular subgroup of 𝐆𝐋 4 {\mathbf{GL}}_{4} , confirming that all the cluster variables are contained in the Mirković–Vilonen basis.



1
Introduction

1.1
Geometric Satake and Mirković–Vilonen cycles

The geometric Satake equivalence of Mirković and Vilonen
[
MV07a
]
is an equivalence of categories between the category of spherical perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian of a reductive group
G
and the category of representations of its Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
. This foundational result provides a powerful tool to study representation theory using geometry. In particular, under this equivalence, the MV cycles (short for Mirković–Vilonen) in the affine Grassmannian of
G
index bases for irreducible representations of
G^{\vee}
. In this paper we work with
G={\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
and identify
G^{\vee}={\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
as well.

In
[
MV07b
]
(see also the recent sequel
[
MV19
]
) Mirković and Vybornov supply a geometric version of symmetric and skew Howe
({\mathbf{GL}}_{m},{\mathbf{GL}}_{N})
dualities. They relate Kazhdan–Lusztig slices in the affine Grassmannian of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
to slices in
N\times N
nilpotent orbits on the one hand, and to Nakajima quiver varieties on the other hand. The second author
[
Dra21
]
showed that under the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism, the MV cycles are identified with certain varieties of matrices, called generalized orbital varieties.

Essential to the geometric Satake equivalence, the BD Grassmannian (short for Beilinson–Drinfeld) is used to define a fusion product on the category of spherical perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. In
[
And03
]
, Anderson used the BD Grassmannian to define a fusion of MV cycles. He constructed a family where the general fibre is a Cartesian product of two MV cycles, and the special fibre, called the fusion, is a (non-reduced) union of MV cycles. Anderson conjectured that this fusion product matches the multiplication in the coordinate ring of the unipotent subgroup
N\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
. This conjecture was proven in
[
BKK19
]
, where it was also established that the MV cycles give a basis for
\mathbb{C}[N]
.

1.2
Fusion using generalized orbital varieties

Computing the fusion product of MV cycles using the BD Grassmannian is quite difficult, both computationally and conceptually. A few such computations were done by Anderson and Kogan in
[
AK06
]
in a somewhat ad-hoc fashion. Another perspective on these computations (using convolution Grassmannians) was recently studied by Baumann, Gaussent and Littelmann
[
BGL20
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to give a conceptually elementary way to compute this product by transferring it to a fusion product of generalized orbital varieties. To do so, we use that the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism extends to an isomorphism between families of slices in the BD Grassmannian and families of slices of matrices with two eigenvalues. Our main result is the following (see
Corollary

6.15
for a more precise statement).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}
be MV cycles. The structure constants for the of the corresponding MV basis vectors in
\mathbb{C}[N]
are equal to the intersection multiplicities of generalized orbital varieties in the fusion of the generalized orbital varieties corresponding to
Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime\prime}
.

Since these generalized orbital varieties are subschemes of affine spaces of matrices defined by certain rank conditions, the fusion of generalized orbital varieties can be computed by elementary commutative algebra.

1.3
Cluster algebras and MV cycles

One motivation for computing products of MV basis elements is to relate the cluster algebra structure on
\mathbb{C}[N]
to the MV basis. Cluster algebras were introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky in
[
FZ02
]
and it was shown in
[
GLS07
]
, using a construction of
[
BFZ05
]
, that
\mathbb{C}[N]
has a natural cluster algebra structure.

This structure starts with an initial seed
\Sigma=(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\},B)
, where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[N]
are a cluster of variables,
B
is a certain matrix, and
r=m(m-1)/2
.
We obtain new variables through a process called mutation, where for a mutable variable
x_{i}
, its mutation is the unique element
x_{i}^{*}\in\mathbb{C}[N]
such that

x_{i}x_{i}^{*}=x_{+}+x_{-}\,.

This equation is called an exchange relation, and the terms
x_{+}
and
x_{-}
are certain monomials in the
x_{j}
,
j\neq i
, determined by
B
.
It gives us the new cluster
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i}^{*},\dots,x_{r}\}
.

The initial cluster
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
as well as all possible variables that are obtained through successive mutations are known as cluster variables.
The cluster monomials are products of cluster variables that are supported on a single cluster.

There are two well-known bases of
\mathbb{C}[N]
whose relationship with the cluster structure has been heavily studied: the dual canonical basis of
[
Lus90
]
and the dual semicanonical basis of
[
Lus00
]
.
In
[
KKKO18
]
, Kang, Kashiwara, Kim and Oh showed that the cluster monomials are contained in the dual canonical basis, while, in
[
GLS06
]
, Geiß, Leclerc and Schröer proved that they are all contained in the dual semicanonical basis.

The MV basis, the dual canonical basis, and the dual semicanonical basis are all examples of biperfect bases of
\mathbb{C}[N]

[
BKK19
, Section 2]
. In particular, they share a crystal structure.
In
[
BKK19
, Appendix]
, we showed that the MV basis differs from the dual semicanonical basis at the same point in the underlying crystal as where the dual semicanonical basis differs from the dual canonical basis.
The fact that this point at which each of these bases differ is not a cluster monomial in any of these bases, gives evidence for the following conjecture of Anderson and Kogan
[
AK06
, Conjecture 5.1]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

The cluster monomials are contained in the MV basis.

The conjecture is true in type
A_{n}
for
n\leq 3
since in these cases
\mathbb{C}[N]
has a unique biperfect basis
[
BKK19
, Section 2.3]
.
More generally, cluster variables called flag minors, coming from initial seeds constructed in
[
BFZ05
, Definitions 2.2–2.3]
, belong to each biperfect basis of
\mathbb{C}[N]

[
BKK19
, Remark 2.10]
.
And recently in
[
BGL20
, Proposition 7.2]
cluster monomials supported on clusters of flag minors satisfying a certain combinatorial condition where shown to be in the MV basis.

Our hope is that each exchange relation can be realized using the fusion of MV cycles. This would imply that all cluster variables are in the MV basis. In
Section

7.2
we verify that this is indeed the case in type
A_{3}
by directly computing each fusion product corresponding to an exchange relation.

Rings and discs

Set
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.19995pt[{t}]\hskip-1.19995pt]
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathbb{C}(\!({t})\!)

We will also consider

\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.19995pt[{t^{-1}}]\hskip-1.19995pt]
and
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}=\mathbb{C}(\!({t^{-1}})\!)
. For any point

is the completion of the local ring

is the formal neighbourhood

of

(also called the formal disc centered at

\mathcal{K}_{s}
is the deleted formal neighbourhood (or punctured disc), denoted

.

Note that for
s\in\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}^{1}
, we have an obvious isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{s}\cong\mathcal{O}
and

taking

.

2.2
Groups

Let
H
be an algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. We will be interested in
H(R)
where
R
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, for example
R=\mathbb{C}[t],\mathcal{O}_{s}
, etc.
Note that evaluation at
t=s
provides a group homomorphism
H(\mathcal{O}_{s})\rightarrow H
. We denote the kernel of this map by
H_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{s})
, often called the first congruence subgroup. We will be particularly interested in this construction in the case
s=\infty
, which gives us the group
H_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
.

Throughout the paper, we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We let
G={\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
and we let
T\subset G
be the maximal torus of diagonal matrices. Let
N,N_{-}\subset G
denote the subgroups of upper and lower triangular matrices with 1s on the diagonal.
We identify
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with the coweight lattice of
G
and say that a coweight
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{m})
is
dominant
if
\nu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{m}
and
effective
if
\nu_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
.
If
\nu
is both effective and dominant, then it is a partition of size
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
Q_{+}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m}
denote the positive root cone, explicitly we have

Q_{+}=\{(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{m}):\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{j}\geq 0\text{ for }j=1,\dots,m-1\text{ and }\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{m}=0\}\,.

We define a partial order on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
by
\lambda\geq\mu
if and only if
\lambda-\mu\in Q_{+}
.

Given
s\in\mathbb{C}
we define
(t-s)^{\nu}
to be the diagonal matrix

\begin{bmatrix}(t-s)^{\nu_{1}}\\
&(t-s)^{\nu_{2}}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&(t-s)^{\nu_{m}}\end{bmatrix}

which we can view in
G(K)
for any ring
K
containing
(t-s)^{-1}
and
\mathbb{C}[t]
. For example,
(t-s)^{\nu}\in G(K)
for
K=\mathcal{K}_{s}
or
\mathbb{C}(t)
.

We will also be interested in the affine space
M_{m}
of
m\times m
matrices. Note that for any
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R
,
G(R)
consists of those matrices
g\in M_{m}(R)
whose determinant is invertible in
R
. Thus for example
(t-s)^{\mu}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C}[t])
for all effective
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
but
(t-s)^{\mu}\in G(\mathbb{C}[t])
if and only if
\mu=0
.

2.3
Lattices

We will use the lattice model for the affine Grassmannian, so it is useful to recall the following definition. Let
R\subset K
be two
\mathbb{C}
-algebras (usually, but not always,
K
will be a field).
Consider
K^{m}
as a
K
-module. By restriction
K^{m}
can be viewed as an
R
-module. An
R
-lattice
in
K^{m}
is an
R
-submodule
L\subset K^{m}
which is a free
R
-module of rank
m
and satisfies
L\otimes_{R}K=K^{m}
. Equivalently,
L=\Sp_{R}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
where
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
are free generators of
K^{m}
.

R^{m}\subset K^{m}
is called the standard lattice. The group
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(K)
acts transitively on the set of
R
-lattices in
K^{m}
, thus giving a bijection between this set and
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(K)/{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(R)
, since
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(R)
is the stabilizer of the standard lattice.

We will be particularly interested in
\mathbb{C}[t]
-lattices in
\mathbb{C}(t)^{m}
. Given such a lattice
L
and a point
a\in\mathbb{C}
, the
specialization
of
L
at
a
is the lattice in
\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}
defined as
L(a):=L\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}
.
If
L(a)=\mathcal{O}_{a}^{m}
then
L
is said to be
trivial at
a
.
For example, the lattice
(t-s)^{-1}\mathbb{C}[t]\subset\mathbb{C}(t)
is trivial at any
a\neq s
, since
t-s
is invertible in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
.

3
Affine Grassmannians

We now define various versions of the affine Grassmannian which will play important roles in this paper.
Each definition is made group-theoretically and then restated as a moduli space of vector bundles and as a moduli space of lattices.
We also sketch how to pass between descriptions.

In these definitions,
V_{\mathrm{triv}}
denotes the trivial rank
m
vector bundle.

Definition 3.1
.

The
ordinary affine Grassmannian

\mathcal{G}\!r=G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})
.

It is the moduli space of vector bundles with trivializations,

\mathcal{G}\!r=\left\{(V,\varphi):\text{$V$ is a rank $m$ vector bundle on $D_{0}$, $\varphi:V\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\mathrm{triv}}$ on $D_{0}^{\times}$}\right\}\,.

It is also a moduli space of lattices,

\mathcal{G}\!r=\left\{L\subset\mathcal{K}^{m}:\text{ $L$ is a $\mathcal{O}$-lattice}\right\}\,.

We obtain a lattice from a pair
(V,\varphi)
by setting
L=\Gamma(D_{0},V)
which is embedded into
\mathcal{K}^{m}=\Gamma(D_{0}^{\times},V_{\mathrm{triv}})
using
\varphi
.
On the other hand, to get a lattice from the group-theoretic description
G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})
we set
L=g\mathcal{O}^{m}
for
g\in G(\mathcal{K})
.

Definition 3.2
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, the
ordinary affine Grassmannian

\mathcal{G}\!r_{s}=G(\mathcal{K}_{s})/G(\mathcal{O}_{s})

at

s
.

As above, we have modular and lattice descriptions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\!r_{s}=\left\{(V,\varphi):\text{$V$ is a rank $m$ vector bundle on $D_{s}$, $\varphi:V\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\mathrm{triv}}$ on $D^{\times}_{s}$}\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\!r_{s}=\left\{L\subset\mathcal{K}_{s}^{m}:\text{ $L$ is a $\mathcal{O}_{s}$-lattice}\right\}\,.

Definition 3.3
.

The
thick affine Grassmannian

{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}=G(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})/G(\mathbb{C}[t])
.

Again, we have the following modular and lattice descriptions:

\displaystyle{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}=\left\{(V,\varphi):\text{$V$ is a rank $m$ vector bundle on $\mathbb{P}$, $\varphi:V\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\mathrm{triv}}$ on $D_{\infty}$ }\right\}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}=\left\{L:L\subset\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{m}\text{ is a $\mathbb{C}[t]$-lattice}\right\}\,.

Definition 3.4
.

The two point
Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian

\pi:{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\to\mathbb{A}

with one point fixed at 0, and the second point
s\in\mathbb{A}
varying.

It is described in modular terms by

{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}=\left\{(V,\varphi,s):V\text{ is a rank $m$ vector bundle on }\mathbb{P},\varphi:V\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\mathrm{triv}}\text{ on }\mathbb{P}\setminus\{0,s\}\right\}\,.

The fibre of
{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\to\mathbb{A}
over
s\in\mathbb{A}
will be denoted
\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}
and is given by

G(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},{(t-s)}^{-1}]/G(\mathbb{C}[t])\,.

We also have the lattice descriptions:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}=\left\{(L,s):L\subset\mathbb{C}(t)^{m}\text{ is a $\mathbb{C}[t]$-lattice trivial at any }a\neq 0,s\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}=\left\{L:L\subset{\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},{(t-s)}^{-1}]}^{m}\text{ is a $\mathbb{C}[t]$-lattice}\right\}\,.

Definition 3.5
.

The
positive part
of
\mathcal{G}\!r
, resp.
{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}
, is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\!r^{+}=\left(M_{m}(\mathcal{O})\cap G(\mathcal{K})\right)/G(\mathcal{O})\,,\,\,\text{resp.\ }{{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}}^{+}=\left(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}[t])\cap G(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})\right)/G(\mathbb{C}[t])\,.

In modular terms,
\mathcal{G}\!r^{+}
(resp.
{{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}}^{+}
) is the set of those
(V,\varphi)
where
\varphi:V\rightarrow V_{\mathrm{triv}}
extends to an inclusion of coherent sheaves over
D_{0}
(resp. over
\mathbb{P}
).

In lattice terms,
\mathcal{G}\!r^{+}
(resp.
{{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}}^{+}
) contains those lattices
L
which are contained in
\mathcal{O}^{m}
(resp.
\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
).

3.1
Relations between different affine Grassmannians

These different versions of the affine Grassmannian are related as follows.

Proposition 3.6
.

There is a map
{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}
defined in the following equivalent ways:

1.

in modular terms as

(V,\varphi,s)\mapsto(V,\varphi\big|_{D_{\infty}})\,;

2.

in the fibre over
s\in\mathbb{A}
as the inclusion

G(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},(t-s)^{-1}])/G(\mathbb{C}[t])\rightarrow G(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})/G(\mathbb{C}[t])\,;

3.

in terms of lattices as

(L,s)\mapsto L\,,

using the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},(t-s)^{-1}]^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{m}
on ambient spaces.

The following result is the factorization property of the BD Grassmannian (see
[
Zhu16
, Prop. 3.13]
).

Proposition 3.7
.

The fibres
\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}
of
{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
can be described as

\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}\cong\begin{cases}\mathcal{G}\!r\times\mathcal{G}\!r_{s}&s\neq 0\\
\mathcal{G}\!r&s=0\,.\end{cases}

In the modular realization, this isomorphism is given by restricting the vector bundle and trivialization.

Suppose that
s\neq 0
. In the lattice realization, this is given by forming the specializations

L\mapsto(L(0),L(s))\,.

Note that if
L=g\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
for
g\in G([t,t^{-1},(t-s)^{-1}])
, then

(L(0),L(s))=(g\mathcal{O}^{m},g\mathcal{O}^{m}_{s})\,.

In the
s=0
case, the isomorphism is described in the same way, except that we just need to form
L(0)
.

For
s\neq 0
, there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\!r_{s}\cong\mathcal{G}\!r
coming from the isomorphism
\mathcal{K}_{s}\cong\mathcal{K}
.
Combining with
Proposition

3.7
, we obtain an isomorphism

\theta_{s}:\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\!r\times\mathcal{G}\!r

Similarly, we write
\theta_{0}
for the isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{G}\!r
.

3.2
The fusion construction

The following construction will be very important in this paper.

Let
\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}^{\times}}

{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}=\mathbb{A}\setminus\{0\}

The isomorphisms

\theta:\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}^{\times}}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\!r\times\mathcal{G}\!r\,.

(1)

(This

Let

Some subschemes of affine Grassmannians

The map

Definition 3.11

The

In modular terms,

at

Definition 3.12
.

The
semi-infinite orbit

S^{\mu}=N_{-}(\mathcal{K})t^{\mu}\subset\mathcal{G}\!r
.

Definition 3.13
.

The
family of two semi-infinite orbits

which we define to have fibres

(this uses that

Lemma 3.14
.

Under the map
{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}
, the image of

Proof.

Let

be a lattice in the image of

4

We now consider some subvarieties of the space of
N\times N
matrices, again using the coweights
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}
fixed in the previous section.

4.1
Adjoint orbits and their deformations

Recall that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
is a partition of
N
.
Given a point
s\in\mathbb{A}
we write
J_{s,\lambda}
for the Jordan form matrix with eigenvalue
s
and Jordan blocks of sizes
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The nilpotent (adjoint) orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\lambda}\subset M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
of matrices conjugate to
J_{0,\lambda}
. These matrices and the linear operators they represent will be said to have Jordan type
\lambda
.

Definition 4.2
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
, the (adjoint) orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}
of matrices conjugate to
J_{0,\lambda^{\prime}}\oplus J_{s,\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
.
These matrices and the linear operators they represent will be said to have Jordan type
((0,\lambda^{\prime}),(s,\lambda^{\prime\prime}))
.

We recall that these orbits have closures which are given by rank conditions.
More precisely, we have

\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda}=\{A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C}):\rk A^{c}\leq N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda,\text{ for $c\in\mathbb{N}$}\}

and

\begin{split}\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}=\{A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C}):\rk A^{c}&\leq N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda^{\prime},\text{ for $c\in\mathbb{N}$}\\
\rk(A-s)^{c}&\leq N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda^{\prime\prime},\text{ for $c\in\mathbb{N}$}\}\,.\end{split}

(2)

The following fact seems to be well-known but we could not find this exact statement in the literature.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists a flat family
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
whose fibre over
s\in\mathbb{A}
is reduced and given by
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}
if
s\neq 0
and
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda}
if
s=0
.

In order to prove this proposition, we will need to recall some results from Eisenbud–Saltman
[
ES89
]
.

Let
r
be a decreasing, non-negative function of
\mathbb{N}
with
r(0)=N
, called a
rank function
. Let
k
be maximal such that
r(k)\neq 0
.
Let
W
be a subspace of
\mathbb{A}^{k}
. Eisenbud and Saltman define and study the flat family
X_{r,W}\rightarrow W
whose fibres are reduced and defined by rank conditions. We will now describe these fibres.

Let
z\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
be a point. Some of the coordinates of
z
may be equal, so we introduce the following data to keep track of these equalities. Let
\ell
denote the number of distinct coordinates of
z
. There exist a sequence of integers
\underline{k}=(k_{0},\dots,k_{\ell})
such that
0=k_{0}<k_{1}<\dots<k_{\ell}=k
, a point
\underline{s}\in\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
, such that
s_{a}\neq s_{b}
for any
a\neq b
, and a permutation
p
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
such that

s_{a}=z_{p(k_{a-1}+1)}=z_{p(k_{a-1}+2)}=\cdots=z_{p(k_{a})}

for each
a=1,\dots,\ell
.
Given the choice of
\underline{s}
we require that
p
is of minimal length.
Given
z
, the data
\underline{k},\underline{s},p
is unique up to a permutation of
\{1,\dots,\ell\}
.

Next for
a=1,\dots,\ell
and
c=1,\dots,k_{a+1}-k_{a}
, we define

r_{z,a}(c):=N+\sum_{d=1}^{c}r(p(k_{a}+d))-r(p(k_{a}+d)-1)\,.

By
[
ES89
, Corollary 2.2]
, the fibre of the flat family
X_{r,W}
over
z
is given by

X_{r,z}=\{A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C}):\rk(A-s_{a})^{c}\leq r_{z,a}(c)\text{ for all }c,a\text{ as above}\}.

(3)

We will now apply these ideas to prove our
Proposition

4.3
.

Proof.

For
c\in\mathbb{N}
, let

r(c)=N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda\,.

It is easy to see that this is a rank function, with
k=\lambda_{1}
, the number of columns of
\lambda
.

Since
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}
, there exists a permutation
p
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
(the columns of
\lambda
) such that
p(1),\dots,p(k_{1})
are the columns of
\lambda^{\prime}
and
p(k_{1}+1),\dots,p(k)
are the columns of
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. Here
k_{1}=\lambda^{\prime}_{1}
the number of columns of
\lambda^{\prime}
.

For
s\in\mathbb{A}
, we define
z(s)\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
by

\begin{cases}z(s)_{p(c)}=0&c=1,\dots,k_{1}\\
z(s)_{p(c)}=s&c=k_{1}+1,\dots,k\,.\end{cases}

For
s\neq 0
, we see that the equalities in
z(s)
give rise to the data of
\ell=2
,
\underline{k}=(0,\lambda_{1}^{\prime})
,
\underline{s}=(0,s)
and the permutation
p
.
We also see that

\begin{split}r_{z(s),1}(c)&=N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda^{\prime}\\
r_{z(s),2}(c)&=N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda^{\prime\prime}\,.\end{split}

(4)

On the other hand, for
z(0)
, we get that
\ell=1
and that

r_{z(0),1}(c)=r(c)=N-\#\text{~boxes in first $c$ columns of }\lambda\,.

(5)

Let
W=\{z(s):s\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Combining
Equations

2
,
3
,
4
and
5
, we see that the Eisenbud–Saltman family
X_{r,W}\rightarrow W
gives our family
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
.
∎

4.2
The Mirković–Vybornov slice

Recall that
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})
is a partition of
N
. The
Mirković–Vybornov slice

\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
is the affine space of
N\times N
matrices of the form
A=J_{0,\mu}+X
where
X
is a
\mu\times\mu
block matrix with possibly nonzero entries
A_{ij}^{1},\dots,A_{ij}^{\min(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})}
in the first
\min(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})
columns of the last row of each
\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}
block.

By example, if
\mu=(3,2)
then
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
looks like

\left[\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\hbox{\hbox{}\vrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}}}}\hss}\vbox{}}}\right]

for some
A_{ij}^{k}\in\mathbb{C}
.

To
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
we will associate the
m\times m
matrix of polynomials
g(A)
in
M_{m}(\mathbb{C}[t])
whose
(i,j)
th entry is defined as follows.

g(A)_{ij}=\begin{cases}t^{\mu_{i}}-\sum_{k=1}^{\mu_{i}}A^{k}_{ji}t^{k-1}&i=j\,,\\
-\sum_{k=1}^{\mu_{i}}A^{k}_{ji}t^{k-1}&i\neq j\,.\end{cases}

(6)

Continuing with the
\mu=(3,2)
example,

g(A)=\begin{bmatrix}t^{3}-A_{11}^{3}t^{2}-A_{11}^{2}t-A_{11}^{1}&-A_{21}^{2}t-A_{21}^{1}\\
-A_{12}^{2}t-A_{12}^{1}&t^{2}-A_{22}^{2}t-A_{22}^{1}\end{bmatrix}\,.

In other words, the
\mu_{i}\times\mu_{j}
block of
A
is used to produce a polynomial which is inserted in the
(j,i)
entry of the
m\times m
matrix
g(A)
.

In
\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
we will be interested in a certain family of block upper-triangular matrices.

Definition 4.4
.

The
upper-triangular
Mirković–Vybornov slice
\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
is defined by

\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}:=\{(A,s)\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\times\mathbb{A}:g(A)_{ii}=t^{\mu^{\prime}_{i}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}_{i}},g(A)_{ij}=0\text{ for $j<i$ }\}\,.

So a matrix in
\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
is weakly block upper-triangular and its diagonal blocks are given by the companion matrices for the polynomials
\{t^{\mu^{\prime}_{i}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}_{i}}:i=1,\dots,m\}
.

For example, elements of
\mathbb{U}_{0,s}^{(1,1,0),(2,1,1)}
look like

\left[\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}}}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\hbox{\hbox{}\vrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\vbox{}}}}}\hss}\vbox{}}}\right]\,.

Note that the fibre
\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,0}
is the same as the intersection
\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}
, where
\mathfrak{n}
denotes the set of strictly upper-triangular matrices.

Throughout this section, we continue our notation of the previous sections. So

denote dominant effective coweights, with
\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}=\lambda
. Also

5.1
Linear Operators from lattices

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
L\in{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}^{+}
. Let
s\in{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

L
is in the image of the map
\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}\rightarrow{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}^{+}
.

(ii)

The linear operator
t
on
\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}/L
has Jordan type
((0,\lambda^{\prime}),(s,\lambda^{\prime\prime}))
.

(iii)

L\in G(\mathbb{C}[t])t^{\lambda^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
.

Proof.

First we recall that for
L\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{+}
,
L\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}=G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda}
if and only if
t|_{\mathcal{O}^{m}/L}
has Jordan type
\lambda
.

Now assume that
(L,s)\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}
. By definition,
L(0)\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
L(s)\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
. This means that
t
acting on
\mathcal{O}^{m}/L(0)
has Jordan type
\lambda^{\prime}
and
t
acting on
\mathcal{O}_{s}^{m}/L(s)
has Jordan type
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. For
a=0,s
, we see that

\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}/L\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}\cong\mathcal{O}_{a}^{m}/L(a).

Lemma

5.2
shows that the map
\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}/L\to\mathcal{O}^{m}/L(0)
induces an isomorphism between the
0
-generalized eigenspace of
t
and
\mathcal{O}^{m}/L(0)
. The same thing holds for the
s
-generalized eigenspace of
t
and
\mathcal{O}_{s}^{m}/L(s)
. This shows that
item

(i)
implies
item

(ii)
and the logic can be reversed to see that
item

(ii)
implies
item

(i)
.

On the other hand, if
L=gt^{\lambda^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
for some
g\in G(\mathbb{C}[t])
, then
L(0)=gt^{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}^{m}
since
(t-s)^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\in G(\mathcal{O})
. In this way, we see that
item

(iii)
implies
item

(ii)
and the logic can be reversed to get equivalence.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}[t]
-module which is finite-dimensional as a complex vector space. For any
a\in\mathbb{A}
, the map

V\rightarrow V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}

restricts to an isomorphism between the generalized
a
-eigenspace of
t
and
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}

Proof.

For any
b\in\mathbb{A}
, let
E_{b}
denote the generalized
b
-eigenspace of
t
. Then
V=\oplus_{b\in\mathbb{C}}E_{b}
. Since
t-b
is invertible in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
and
t-b
acts nilpotently on
E_{b}
, we see that
E_{b}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}=0
.

So it suffices to show that
E_{a}\rightarrow E_{a}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathcal{O}_{a}
is an isomorphism. By the classification of modules over
\mathbb{C}[t]
, it suffices to check this when
E_{a}=\mathbb{C}[t]/(t-a)^{k}
, where it is clearly true.
∎

5.2
Matrices from lattices

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
L\in{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}^{+}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

L\in\mathcal{W}_{\mu}
.

(ii)

L=\Sp_{\mathbb{C}[t]}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
for some
v_{i}
of the form
v_{i}=t^{\mu_{i}}e_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{ij}e_{j}
where
p_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}[t]
has degree less than
\min(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})
.

(iii)

For all
i
,

t^{\mu_{i}}e_{i}\in\Sp_{\mathbb{C}}(\{t^{k}e_{j}:0\leq k<\min(\mu_{i},\mu_{j}),1\leq j\leq m\})+L.

Moreover, for such
L
,
\beta_{\mu}:=\{[t^{k}e_{i}]:0\leq k<\mu_{i},1\leq i\leq m\}
forms a basis for
\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}/L
.

Proof.

Let
L\in\mathcal{W}_{\mu}
. Then
L=\Sp_{\mathbb{C}[t]}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
for some
v_{i}
with

and

. Since

, we see that

which means that

lies in
\mathbb{C}[t]
. By construction, the polynomial
p_{ij}
has degree less than
\mu_{i}
.

Fix
i
and suppose that for some
j
,
\mu_{j}<\mu_{i}
. In this case, we can alter our basis to
v^{\prime}_{i}=v_{i}-qv_{j}
for some polynomial

.
This gives us new polynomials

. In this way, we can ensure that

has degree less than

.
Thus

(i)
implies
item

(ii)
.

Suppose that

as in

(ii)
. Then

t^{\mu_{i}}e_{i}-v_{i}\in\Sp_{\mathbb{C}}(\{t^{k}e_{j}:k<\min(\mu_{i},\mu_{j}),1\leq j\leq m\})\,.

Hence

(ii)
implies
item

(iii)
.

Finally, given
item

(iii)
, then we can see
v_{i}:=t^{\mu_{i}}e_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{ij}e_{j}\in L
for some

of degree less than

and so

.
To show that
\beta_{\mu}
forms a basis for

, it suffices to show that for each

This follows immediately from
item

(iii)
.
∎

Given
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
, recall the definition of
g(A)
given in
Equation

6
. Note that
g(A)t^{-\mu}\in G_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
.
Since

as giving an element of

5.3
Upper-triangularity and the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism

For the next result, we will consider the “intersection” of
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
with
\mathcal{W}_{\mu}
. As
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
is not a subscheme of
{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}
, by this intersection, we really mean the preimage of
\mathcal{W}_{\mu}
under the composition

\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}\,.

This is not a very serious abuse of notation, since the map
{\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}
is almost injective. In a similar way, we will write
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
using the “almost injective” map
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\rightarrow M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

The following refinement of the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism is a special case of
[
MV07b
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

There is an isomorphism

\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cong\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathcal{W}_{\mu}

given by
(A,s)\mapsto(g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m},s)
.

Proof.

Since we already have the isomorphism from
Theorem

5.4
, it suffices to show that for any
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
,

(A,s)\in\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\text{ if and only if }(g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m},s)\in\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathcal{W}_{\mu}\,.

This follows immediately from
Lemma

5.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

The isomorphism from
Theorem

5.5
restricts to an isomorphism

\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cong\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\cap S^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}\,.

Proof.

We could prove this by observing that both sides are the attracting locus of an appropriate
{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
action. However, we will give the following more algebraic proof.

Let
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
. We must show that
(A,s)\in\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
if and only if
(g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m},s)\in S^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
.

On the one hand, if
(A,s)\in\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
, then
g(A)
is lower-triangular with diagonal
t^{\mu^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}
, and so
g(A)\in N_{-}[t,t^{-1},(t-s)^{-1}]t^{\mu^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}
.

On the other hand, if
(g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m},s)\in S^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
, then we can write

gt^{\mu}r=nt^{\mu^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}

for some
r\in G(\mathbb{C}[t]),n\in N_{-}(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},(t-s)^{-1}])
and
g=g(A)t^{-\mu}
. Let
h=(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}t^{-\mu^{\prime\prime}}
which lies in
T_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
.
Note that
h^{-1}nh\in N_{-}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
, so we can factor it as
h^{-1}nh=n_{1}n_{2}
, where
n_{1}\in N_{-,1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty}),n_{2}\in N_{-}(\mathbb{C}[t])
. So then after doing a bit of algebra, we reach

t^{\mu}r(t^{-\mu}n_{2}^{-1}t^{\mu})t^{-\mu}=g^{-1}hn_{1}.

Since
g,h,n_{1}\in G_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
, the right hand side
g^{-1}hn_{1}
lies in
G_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
. Since
\mu
is dominant,
t^{-\mu}n_{2}t^{\mu}\in N_{-}(\mathbb{C}[t])
, and so the left hand side lies in
t^{\mu}G(\mathbb{C}[t])t^{-\mu}
.

Moreover, since
\mu
is dominant, we know that

t^{\mu}G(\mathbb{C}[t])t^{-\mu}\cap G_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})=N_{-,1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})\,.

Thus, we deduce that
g^{-1}hn_{1}\in N_{-,1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
and hence
g(A)\in t^{\mu^{\prime}}(t-s)^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}N_{-,1}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
. Since
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
, this implies that
(A,s)\in\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
as desired.
∎

Restricting to the zero fibre and applying
\theta_{0}
together with
Proposition

4.3
, we recover a result of the second author.

Corollary 5.7
.

(
[
Dra21
, Corollary 5.2.2]
)
The map
A\mapsto g(A)\mathcal{O}^{m}
gives an isomorphism

\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}\cong\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda}\cap S^{\mu}_{-}\,.

Climax

6.1
MV cycles and tableaux

Continuing now with the notation of the previous sections, we add the assumption that
\mu\leq\lambda
and forget the assumption that
\mu
is dominant.

Definition 6.1
.

An irreducible component of
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}\cap S^{\mu}}
is called an
Mirković–Vilonen cycle
in
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda}
of coweight
\mu
.

By
[
MV07a
, Theorem 3.2(b)]
, each MV cycle in
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda}
of coweight
\mu
has dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
where
\rho=(m,m-1,\dots,1)
and we use the ordinary dot product on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. We denote by
\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)
the collection of all MV cycles in
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda}
of coweight
\mu\leq\lambda
.

In
[
Kam10
]
, the third author gave a combinatorial description of MV cycles for any reductive group, using Mirković–Vilonen polytopes, or, equivalently, Lusztig data, which are sequences in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
that depend on a choice of reduced expression for the longest element of the Weyl group of
G
. We will now explain how to use these results to index MV cycles for
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
using Young tableaux. (For any
G
, we use
r
for the number of positive roots; in the case of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
,
r=m(m-1)/2
.)

Denote by
[a,b]
the interval
\{a,a+1,\dots,b-1,b\}\subset\mathbb{N}
. We begin with the following definitions.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
L\subset\mathcal{O}^{m}
be a point in
\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}
. We define the relative dimension of
L
by

\operatorname{rdim}L:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}^{m}/L\,.

If
\gamma\subset[1,m]
, we define two lattices
L^{\gamma},L_{\gamma}
in
\mathcal{O}^{\gamma}:=\Sp_{\mathcal{O}}(e_{i}:i\in\gamma)
by

\displaystyle L^{\gamma}:=L\cap\mathcal{O}^{\gamma}\qquad L_{\gamma}:=L/L^{\gamma^{c}}

where
\gamma^{c}:=[1,m]\setminus\gamma
.

We will also need the analogous definitions when
\mathcal{O}
is replaced by
\mathbb{C}[t]
and
L\in{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}_{+}
.
We record the following easy observations.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
L\in\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}
or
{\mathbcal{G}\!{{\boldsymbol{r}}}}_{+}
.

1.

For any
\gamma\subset[m]
, we have

\operatorname{rdim}L_{\gamma}+\operatorname{rdim}L^{\gamma^{c}}=\operatorname{rdim}L\,.

2.

For any
\gamma_{1}\subset\gamma_{2}\subset[m]
, we have

(L_{\gamma_{2}})_{\gamma_{1}}=L_{\gamma_{1}}\,.

For any
L\in\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}
and
\gamma\subset[m]
, we define
D_{\gamma}(L):=\operatorname{rdim}L^{\gamma}=\operatorname{rdim}L-\operatorname{rdim}L_{\gamma^{c}}
. (By
[
Kam10
, Proposition 9.3]
, this coincides with the general definition of
D_{\gamma}
given in that paper; in
loc. cit.
these functions are used to compute the hyperplanes of the associated MV polytopes.)

From now on, we will write
S^{\mu}_{-}=N_{-}(\mathcal{K})t^{\mu}
(with a subscript), because we will also need the opposite semi-infinite cell
S^{\mu}_{+}:=N(\mathcal{K})t^{\mu}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
L\in\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

L\in S_{+}^{\mu}

2.

\operatorname{rdim}L^{[1,i]}=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

3.

\operatorname{rdim}L_{[i+1,m]}=\mu_{i+1}+\dots+\mu_{m}
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

Similarly, the following are equivalent:

1’.

L\in S_{-}^{\mu}

2’.

\operatorname{rdim}L^{[i+1,m]}=\mu_{i+1}+\dots+\mu_{m}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

3’.

\operatorname{rdim}L_{[1,i]}=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

We prove the first statement as the second is similar. Let
L=nt^{\mu}\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
for some
n\in N_{+}(\mathcal{K})
and let
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
denote the columns of the matrix
nt^{\mu}
. Then
L=\Sp_{\mathcal{O}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
. Fix
i\leq m
. Then
L^{[1,i]}=\Sp_{\mathcal{O}}(w_{1},\dots,w_{i})
where
w_{j}
denotes the first
i
entries of
v_{j}
(by upper-triangularity, the rest of the entries are 0, in any case).

Assume that
L\subset\mathcal{O}^{m}
. Then
L^{[1,i]}\subset\mathcal{O}^{i}
and
\operatorname{rdim}L^{[1,i]}
is the valuation of the determinant of the matrix whose columns are
w_{1},\dots,w_{i}
. Since this matrix is upper-triangular with diagonal entries
t^{\mu_{1}},\dots,t^{\mu_{i}}
, this determinant is
t^{\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i}}
and hence
\operatorname{rdim}L^{[1,i]}=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i}
.

Thus
item

1
implies
item

2
. The converse follows from the fact that every
L
lies in some
S_{+}^{\nu}
and by the above reasoning,
\nu
is determined by the values of
\dim L^{[1,i]}
.

The equivalence of
item

2
and
item

3
follows from
Lemma

6.3
.
∎

We now introduce some notation related to Young tableaux. Denote by
YT(\lambda)
the set of (possibly semi-standard) Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
and by
YT(\lambda)_{\mu}
the subset of
YT(\lambda)
of tableaux having weight
\mu
. Let
YT=\bigcup_{\lambda}YT(\lambda)
and denote by
YT_{+}\subset YT
the subset of those tableaux having dominant weight.

Given
\tau\in YT(\lambda)_{\mu}
and
i\in\{1,\dots m\}
, denote by
\lambda(i)
(resp.
\mu(i)
) the shape (resp. the weight) of
\tau(i)
, the tableau got from
\tau
by discarding all boxes of weight exceeding
i
. (Note that
\mu(i)
only depends on
\mu
, while
\lambda(i)
depends on the tableau
\tau
.) We will regard
\lambda(i)
(resp.
\mu(i)
) as an effective dominant coweight (resp. effective coweight) for
{\mathbf{GL}}_{i}
.

The Lusztig datum
n_{\bullet}(\tau)
of the tableau
\tau
is a list of
r=m(m-1)/2
non-negative integers defined from its
Gelfand–Tsetlin pattern
(see
[
BZ88
, Sect. 4]
)
\gt(\tau)=(\lambda(i)_{j})_{1\leq j\leq i\leq m}
by the formula

\begin{gathered}n_{\bullet}(\tau)_{(a,b)}=\lambda(b)_{a}-\lambda(b-1)_{a}=\text{ \# of boxes on row $a$ of $\tau$ of weight $b$}\\
(a,b)=(1,2),\dots,(1,m),(2,3),\dots,(2,m),\dots,(m-1,m)\,.\end{gathered}

Note that

\lambda-\mu=\displaystyle{\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq m}n_{\bullet}(\tau)_{(a,b)}\beta_{a,b}}

where
\beta_{a,b}
denotes the positive root of
G
with a
1
in the
a
slot and a
-1
in the
b
slot. The pattern
\gt(\tau)
is recorded as a lower-triangular matrix (the array of shapes of subtableaux
\tau(i)
) and the datum
n_{\bullet}(\tau)
is recorded as a sequence, unless noted otherwise.

Below is an example with
\lambda=(4,2)
and
\mu=(3,2,1)
.

\tau=\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\vbox{\vbox{\moveright 0.0pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$1$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$1$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$1$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$2$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt\moveright 0.0pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$2$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt\hbox to10.93333pt{\hfil$3$\hfil}\vrule height=8.74669pt,width=0.3pt,depth=2.18663pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt\qquad\gt(\tau)=\begin{matrix}3\\
4&1\\
4&2&0\end{matrix}\qquad n_{\bullet}(\tau)=(1,0,1)

We can associate to
\tau
the locus

\mathring{Z}(\tau)=\{L\in S^{\mu}_{-}:L_{[1,i]}\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda(i)}\text{ for }i=1,\dots m\}\,.

Remark 6.5
.

Theorem 4.2 of
[
CT15
]
shows that the map
YT(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r}
is the canonical crystal embedding
B(\lambda)\rightarrow B(\infty)
.

The following result is closely related to Theorem 5.4.3 from
[
Dra21
]
. It is also closely related to the description of MV cycles in terms of Kostant data obtained by Anderson–Kogan
[
AK04
]
(see
[
Kam10
, Section 9]
).
We remark that a different map from Young tableaux to MV cycles was obtained by Gaussent, Littelmann and Nguyen
[
GLN13
, Theorem 2]
; we are not certain of the relation with our construction.

Proposition 6.6
.

\mathring{Z}(\tau)
has a unique irreducible component of dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
. Let
Z(\tau)
denote the closure of this component. Then,

Z(\tau)
is the MV cycle whose Lusztig datum (with respect to the standard reduced word) is
n_{\bullet}(\tau)
.

Remark 6.7
.

We believe that
\mathring{Z}(\tau)
is irreducible, so that in fact
Z(\tau)=\overline{\mathring{Z}(\tau)}
.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition follows the same strategy as in
[
Dra21
]
.
First, we consider

\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}:=\{L\in S^{\mu}_{-}\cap\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}:L_{[1,i]}\in S_{+}^{\lambda(i)}\text{ for }i=1,\dots m\}\,.

Using Lemma
6.3
(2) and Lemma
6.4
, we see that

\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}=\{L\in\mathcal{G}\!r_{+}:\operatorname{rdim}L_{[a,b]}=\lambda(b)_{a}+\dots+\lambda(b)_{b}\text{ for all $1\leq a\leq b\leq m$}\}

where note that
\lambda(b)_{a}+\dots+\lambda(b)_{b}
is the number of boxes on rows
a,\dots,b
of weight
1,\dots,b
.

From the proof of
[
Kam10
, Proposition 9.6]
, we see that
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
is equal to
t^{\mu}A(n_{\bullet}(\tau))
, where
A(n_{\bullet})
is defined in
[
Kam10
, Sect. 4.3]
. In particular, it is irreducible and of dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
. Moreover, its closure is the MV cycle whose Lusztig datum is
n_{\bullet}(\tau)
. (The reader is warned that in
[
Kam10
]
the third author worked with
G(\mathcal{O})\backslash G(\mathcal{K})
, which we identify with
\mathcal{G}\!r
using the map
G(\mathcal{O})g\mapsto g^{-1}G(\mathcal{O})
.)
Now, by results of
[
Kam08
]
(see especially the proof of Theorem 1.4), we note that
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}\cap\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}
is dense in
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
. (This can also be deduced by combining
Remark

6.5
with
[
And03
, Proposition 6]
.)

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. If
L\in S^{\mu}_{-}
, then
L_{[1,i]}\in S^{\mu(i)}_{-}
so we get a map

f_{i}:\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}\rightarrow S^{\mu(i)}_{-}\cap S_{+}^{\lambda(i)}\,.

From the definition of
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
, we see that
f_{i}(\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1})=\mathring{Z}(\tau(i))_{1}
.
From above,
f_{i}(\mathring{Z}(\tau(i))_{1})\cap\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda(i)}
is a dense constructible subset of
\mathring{Z}(\tau(i))_{1}
.
Since
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
is irreducible, this implies that
f_{i}^{-1}(S^{\mu(i)}_{-}\cap S_{+}^{\lambda(i)}\cap\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda(i)})
is a dense constructible subset of
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
.

Working with all
i
at once, we conclude that

\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{2}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}^{-1}(S^{\mu(i)}_{-}\cap S_{+}^{\lambda(i)}\cap\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda(i)})

is a dense constructible subset of
\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}
. Thus
\overline{\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{2}}=\overline{\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{1}}
.

On the other hand, by definition

\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{2}\subset\mathring{Z}(\tau).

Since
\dim\mathring{Z}(\tau)_{2}=\rho(\lambda-\mu)
, we see that
\mathring{Z}(\tau)
must have at least one component of the maximal dimension. To see that it cannot have any other components of maximal dimension, we note that

\bigsqcup_{\tau\in YT(\lambda)_{\mu}}\mathring{Z}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}\cap S_{-}^{\mu}

and the number of irreducible components of the right hand side equals
|YT(\lambda)_{\mu}|
, thus on the left hand side, each
\mathring{Z}(\tau)
can only have one irreducible component of dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
.
∎

6.2
Fusion of MV cycles via fusion of generalized orbital varieties

Given
A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
we denote by
A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}
the restriction of
A
to the subspace spanned by the first
p
standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. If
A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}(\mathbb{C}^{p})\subset\mathbb{C}^{p}
then we identify it with the
p\times p
upper-left submatrix of
A
.

Let
\tau\in YT(\lambda)_{\mu}
with
\mu
dominant. We define

\mathring{X}(\tau)=\{A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}:A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{|\mu(i)|}}\in\mathbb{O}^{\lambda(i)}\text{ for each }i=1,\dots,m\}\,.

Lemma 6.8
.

Under the Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism
\mathring{X}(\tau)
is mapped isomorphically onto
\mathring{Z}(\tau)
.

Proof.

Fix
A\in\mathring{X}(\tau)
and
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Let
\mathcal{O}^{i}\subset\mathcal{O}^{m}
denote the submodule generated by the first
i
standard basis vectors. Let
l=g(A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{\lvert\mu(i)\rvert}})\mathcal{O}^{i}
and
L=g(A)\mathcal{O}^{m}
. By definition of the map
A\mapsto g(A)
, since
A
is upper-triangular we have that
l=L_{[1,i]}
.
Moreover, since elements of
\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}
are upper triangular
A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{\lvert\mu(i)\rvert}}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu(i)}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{i}
where
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
denotes the subalgebra of upper-triangular matrices in
M_{\lvert\mu(i)\rvert}(\mathbb{C})
.
By definition of
\mathring{X}(\tau)
this principal submatrix has Jordan type
\lambda(i)
. It follows that
l=L_{[1,i]}\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda(i)}\cap S^{\mu(i)}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
so that
L\in\mathring{Z}(\tau)
.

Conversely, let
L\in\mathring{Z}(\tau)
. By
Corollary

5.7
,
L=g(A)\mathcal{O}^{m}
for some
A\in\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}
. Running the above argument in reverse, we see that
A\in\mathring{X}(\tau)
.
∎

By
[
Dra21
, Prop. 4.5.4]
(or
Proposition

6.6
above),
\mathring{X}(\tau)
has a unique irreducible component of dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
. We write
X(\tau)
for the closure of this component. It is an irreducible component of
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}
and will be called a
generalized orbital variety of type
\lambda
.
The collection of all possible generalized orbital varieties of type
\lambda
will be denoted
\mathcal{X}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 6.9
.

(
[
Dra21
, Theorem 4.8.2]
)

\{X(\tau):\tau\in YT(\lambda)_{\mu}\}
is a complete set of irreducible components of
\overline{\mathbb{O}}^{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\mu}\cap\mathfrak{n}
.

Our next goal is to describe the fusion of two MV cycles using a “fusion” of generalized orbital varieties, which we will now define.
Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}
be as in previous sections, once again assuming that
\mu
is dominant.

Given a point
s\in{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
and a pair of tableaux
\tau^{\prime}\in YT(\lambda^{\prime})_{\mu^{\prime}}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in YT(\lambda^{\prime\prime})_{\mu^{\prime\prime}}
we define

\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,s}=\left\{A\in\mathbb{U}_{0,s}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}:A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{|\mu(i)|}}\text{ has Jordan type }((\lambda^{\prime}(i),0),(\lambda^{\prime\prime}(i),s))\text{ for }i=1,\dots,m\right\}

and

\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,\mathbb{A}^{\times}}=\left\{(A,s)\in M_{N}\times\mathbb{A}^{\times}:A\in\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,s}\right\}\,.

Note that elements
A\in\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})
do not in general correspond to pairs
(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime})\in\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime})\times X(\tau^{\prime\prime})
because if
\mu^{\prime}
(resp.
\mu^{\prime\prime}
) is not dominant then
X(\tau^{\prime})
(resp.
X(\tau^{\prime\prime})
) is not well-defined.

Proposition 6.10
.

The image of
\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
under the isomorphism of
Theorem

5.6
is
\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime})\ast_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

Fix
A\in\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,s}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Let
\mathbb{C}[t]^{i}\subset\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}=\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t]
denote the
\mathbb{C}[t]
-submodule generated by the first
i
standard basis vectors.

The lattices
l=g(A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{|\mu(i)|}})\mathbb{C}[t]^{i}
and
L=g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
are related by the equation
l=L_{[1,i]}
where recall
L_{[1,i]}=(L+\mathbb{C}[t]^{[i+1,m]})/\mathbb{C}[t]^{[i+1,m]}\subset\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}/\mathbb{C}[t]^{[i+1,m]}=\mathbb{C}[t]^{i}
.

By definition of
\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,\mathbb{A}}
we have
A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{|\mu(i)|}}\in\mathbb{U}^{\mu^{\prime}(i),\mu^{\prime\prime}(i)}_{0,s}
and by definition of
\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,s}
, the Jordan type of
A\big|_{\mathbb{C}^{|\mu(i)|}}
is
((\lambda^{\prime}(i),0),(\lambda^{\prime\prime}(i),s))
. So,
l\in\mathcal{G}\!r_{0,s}^{\lambda^{\prime}(i),\lambda^{\prime\prime}(i)}
by
Theorem

5.6
.
On the other hand, since
L\in S^{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}}_{0,s}
,
l\in S^{\mu^{\prime}(i),\mu^{\prime\prime}(i)}_{0,s}
.

Therefore, as
i
varies, we see that the pair
(L(0),L(s))\in S^{\mu^{\prime}}\times S^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}
is such that

(L_{[1,i]}(0),L_{[1,i]}(s))=(L(0)_{[1,i]},L(s)_{[1,i]})\in\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime}(i)}\times\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}(i)}

and so
(L(0),L(s))\in\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime})\times\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime\prime})
.

Conversely, given
L\in\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime})\ast_{s}\mathring{Z}(\tau^{\prime\prime})
, we can write
L=g(A)\mathbb{C}[t]^{m}
by
Theorem

5.5
. Then reversing the above logic, we conclude that
A\in\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,s}
.
∎

Now, in analogy with the fusion of
Section

3.2
we define
X(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,\mathbb{A}}
to be the Zariski closure of the unique top-dimensional component of
\mathring{X}(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})_{0,\mathbb{A}^{\times}}
in
M_{N}\times\mathbb{A}
. (As noted in
remark

6.7
, we expect that all these varieties

6.3

\mathbb{C}[N]

In this section we will need to recall that representations of the Langlands dual group

of an arbitrary complex reductive group

G
, and studied in
\mathbb{C}[N^{\vee}]
, the ring of functions on the maximal unipotent subgroup
N^{\vee}
of

.

We use the convenient fact that
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}^{\vee}={\mathbf{GL}}_{m}
(so also
N^{\vee}=N
) and ignore the distinction between weights and coweights.

Let

be the unipotent subgroup of upper-triangular matrices with 1s on the diagonal. In
[
BKK19

the third author along with Baumann and Knutson show that the MV cycles yield a basis of the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[N]
called the MV basis. Note that with respect to the action of the maximal torus

by conjugation on

\mathbb{C}[N]
acquires a homogeneous grading by

Given weights
\mu\leq\lambda

\lambda

the irreducible representation of

of highest weight
\lambda

\mu

There is an injective map
\Psi_{\lambda}:V(\lambda)\to\mathbb{C}[N]
realizing

as a subspace of
\mathbb{C}[N]
and sending

to
\mathbb{C}[N]_{\mu-\lambda}

The geometric Satake correspondence says that
V(\lambda)_{\mu}=H_{2\rho(\lambda-\mu)}(\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}\cap S^{\mu}_{-}})
. In particular, the set
\{[Z]:Z\in\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)\}
gives a basis of

with

denoting the class of
Z
in the appropriate homology group.

By a theorem of Anderson, MV cycles in
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r^{\lambda}\cap S^{\nu+\lambda}_{-}}
consist of those irreducible components of
\overline{S^{\lambda}_{+}\cap S^{\lambda+\nu}_{-}}
that are contained in
\overline{\mathcal{G}\!r}^{\lambda}

[
And03

. The
stable
MV cycles of coweight
\nu\in Q_{+}
are defined to be irreducible components of
\overline{S^{0}_{+}\cap S^{\nu}_{-}}
. Multiplication by
t^{\lambda}
defines an isomorphism
\overline{S^{0}_{+}\cap S^{\nu}_{-}}\cong\overline{S^{\lambda}_{+}\cap S^{\lambda+\nu}_{-}}
. We denote the set of all stable MV cycles
\mathcal{Z}(\infty)
.
By a theorem of the third author, given a choice a reduced word for

, there is a bijection
\mathcal{Z}(\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r}
called the Lusztig datum. For our purposes we will always use the standard reduced word.

One can string together the maps
\Psi_{\lambda}
and the geometric Satake isomoprhisms to show that the stable MV cycles yield a basis of
\mathbb{C}[N]
.

Theorem 6.12
.

(
[
BKK19
, Proposition 6.1]
)
For each

there is a unique element

such that for any dominant weight

then
\Psi_{\lambda}([t^{\lambda}Z])=b_{Z}
.

The set
\{b_{Z}:Z\in\mathcal{Z}(\infty)\}
is called the

of
\mathbb{C}[N]
. The structure constants of multiplication in

with respect to the MV basis are given by intersection multiplicities.

Theorem 6.13
.

(
[

b_{Z^{\prime}}\cdot b_{Z^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{Z}(\infty)}i\left(Z,Z^{\prime}\ast_{0}Z^{\prime\prime}\right)b_{Z}

in

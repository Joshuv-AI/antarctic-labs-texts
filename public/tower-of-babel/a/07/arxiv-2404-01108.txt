Norms of wave functions for FQHE models on a torus
Igor Burban, Semyon Klevtsov

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to give an explicit computation of the curvature of the magnetic vector bundle of the multi-layer model of the fractional quantum Hall effect on a torus. We also obtain concrete formulae for the norms of the corresponding wave functions arising in such models.



1.
Introduction

The quantum-mechanical behaviour of a non-relativistic charged particle moving in a plane in presence of a perpendicular uniform magnetic field is described by the magnetic Schrödinger operator

(1.1)

H:=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+i\frac{b}{2}v\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}-i\frac{b}{2}u\right)^{2}:\;D(H)\longrightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

where
(u,v)
are the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a parameter describing the strength of the magnetic field. The operator
H
is essentially self-adjoint and its definition domain
D(H)
is the entire Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, the spectrum of
H
is the set

\left\{(2l+1)\,\big|\,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
and its ground state is the Segal–Bargmann–Fock space

(1.2)

\left\{\left.e^{-\frac{b}{4}|z|^{2}}f(z)\,\right|\,\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\;\mbox{is analytic}\right\}\cap L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

where
z=u+iv
; see for instance
[
17
]
for details.

The magnetic Schrödinger operator, describing the behaviour of
n
charged particles moving in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, is given by the expression

(1.3)

H_{n,w}:=-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}+i\frac{b}{2}v_{k}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{k}}-i\frac{b}{2}u_{k}\right)^{2}\right)+\sum\limits_{k<l}w\bigl(|z_{k}-z_{l}|),

where
z_{k}=(u_{k},v_{k})
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
w
is some potential function, which describes the mutual interactions between the particles.
For
w\neq 0
, explicit expressions for wave functions of
H_{n,w}
are rarely known.

The fractional quantum Hall effect (FQHE) is a celebrated physical phenomenon in which the Hall conductance of a two-dimensional electron system in a perpendicular magnetic field shows precisely quantized plateaus at fractional values of
e^{2}/h
, where
e
is the electron charge and
h
is the Planck constant. In order to explain this phenomenon, Laughlin
[
18
]
suggested to replace the ground state of
H_{n,w}

by the function

(1.4)

\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=e^{-\frac{b}{4}\left(|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{n}|^{2}\right)}\biggl(\prod\limits_{k<l}(z_{k}-z_{l})\biggr)^{m},

where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is a positive integer. Note that
\Phi
is anti-symmetric if
m
is odd (realizing the fermionic statistics in the case of electrons) and symmetric if
m
is even (corresponding to the bosonic statistics) and belongs to the ground state of the operator
H_{n,0}
.

Investigations of many-body systems, which include effects of the boundary conditions, are very hard to perform. A convenient way to circumvent such difficulties is provided by replacing the plane
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
by a compact Riemann surface
[
8
,
24
,
2
,
14
]
. A complex torus
E=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
is a particularly convenient choice
[
9
]
, see
[
1
]
and also
[
13
]
for a survey.
Here,
\Lambda=\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbbm{Z}}\subset\mathbb{C}
is a lattice determined by a modular parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
with
t=\mathfrak{Im}(\tau)>0
.
We equip
E
with a Riemannian metric induced by the quotient
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow E
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a holomorphic line bundle of degree
k
. The moduli space of such line bundles can be identified with the torus
E
itself, hence we use the notation
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
with
\xi=a\tau+b
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. There is a convenient way to define
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
using the so-called automorphy factors. It turns out that
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
can be equipped with a natural hermitian metric
h=h_{k,\xi}
, whose curvature form is a constant two-form on
E
corresponding to the uniform magnetic field.

The space
\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
of smooth global sections of
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
carries a natural hermitian inner product given for any
f,g\in\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
by the rule

\langle f,g\rangle:=\iint\limits_{E}h(f,g)\,\omega,

where
h(f,g):E\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a smooth function assigning to a point
p\in E
the scalar product of sections
f(p),g(p)\in\mathcal{L}\big|_{p}
with respect to the metric
h
and
\omega
is a volume form on
E
. One can define the corresponding Hilbert space
\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}
of square integrable global section of
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
in a standard way.

The quantum-mechanical behaviour of a charged particle moving on
E
in presence of a uniform magnetic field is described by the so-called holomorphic Bochner Laplacian
D(H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle H}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}
of the hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
. The operator
H
is essentially self-adjoint and has a discrete spectrum with finite dimensional eigenspaces, which can be realized as subspaces of
\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
. As in the case of a plane, we have
D(H)=\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}
. Under the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
has positive degree, the ground state of
H
coincides with the space
\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}
of
holomorphic
global sections of
\mathcal{L}
; see for instance
[
19
,
22
]
for details and proofs.

For any
k>0
, the space
\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}
is
k
-dimensional and admits a distinguished basis
(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
where

s_{j}(z)=\vartheta\Bigl[\frac{j-1}{k},0\Bigr](kz+\xi|k\tau)\;\mbox{\rm for}\;1\leq j\leq k,

where
\vartheta[a,b](z\,\tau)
is the theta-function with characteristics
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
20
]
).
Following the Ansatz of Laughlin (
1.4
), Haldane any Rezayi suggested in
[
9
]
to consider the
m
-dimensional vector space
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
spanned by the functions

\Phi_{j}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\vartheta\left[\dfrac{j-1}{m},0\right](mw+\xi\,|\,m\tau)\left(\prod\limits_{p<q}\vartheta(z_{p}-z_{q})\right)^{m}

for
1\leq j\leq m
. Here,
w=z_{1}+\dots+z_{n}
and
\vartheta(z)=\vartheta\bigl[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr](z|\tau)
(an important feature is that
\vartheta(z)
is an odd function). Note that all functions
\Phi_{j}
are anti-symmetric in the case
m
is odd and symmetric if
m
is even.

In our previous work
[
4
]
, we gave an axiomatic characterization of the space
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
. For simplicity assume that
m
is even. Then
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
is precisely the space of global sections of the line bundle
\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{mn,\xi}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{mn,\xi}}_{n\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}
on
X:=\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{n\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}
, which vanish with order at least
m
on each partial diagonal of
X
. In particular,
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
is a subspace of the ground state
\underbrace{\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}}_{n\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}
of an appropriate
n
-body magnetic Schrödinger operator and inherits the corresponding hermitian inner product.

Based on this fact, we constructed in
[
4
]
the so-called magnetic vector bundle
\mathcal{B}
on the torus
E
. It is a holomorphic hermitian vector bundle of rank
m
such that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
the fiber
\mathcal{B}\big|_{[\xi]}
is isomorphic (as a hermitian complex vector space) to
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
. Using the technique of Fourier–Mukai transforms, we showed that
\mathcal{B}
is simple and has degree
-1
.

More generally, one can define multi-layer torus models of FQHE
[
10
,
11
,
23
]
starting with a so-called Wen datum
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
. Here,
K\in\mathsf{Mat}_{g\times g}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is a symmetric positive definite matrix, whose diagonal elements are simultaneously all even or odd, and
\vec{\mathsf{n}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
\vdots\\
n_{g}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{g}
is such that

K\vec{\mathsf{n}}=d\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{array}\right)

for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
\vec{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
, one can consider a natural vector space
\mathsf{V}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\vec{\zeta}}
of wave functions of Keski-Vakkuri and Wen
[
11
,
23
]
having dimension
\delta=\det(K)
(coinciding with vector the space
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\zeta}
if
g=1
,
K=(m)
and
\vec{\mathsf{n}}=n
). In
[
4
]
, we constructed a holomorphic hermitian vector bundle on the abelian variety
\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}+\tau\mathbb{Z}^{g})
such that such that for any
\vec{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}
its fiber over
[\vec{\zeta}]\in\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}+\tau\mathbb{Z}^{g})
is isometric to the hermitian complex vector space
\mathsf{V}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\vec{\zeta}}
. Considering only

\vec{\zeta}=(\xi,\dots,\xi)
with
\xi\in E
, we get a hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{B}
on the torus
E
.

Any holomorphic hermitian vector bundle on a complex manifold carries a canonical hermitian connection
\nabla
(the Bott–Chern connection
[
3
]
). In
[
4
]
is was shown that the Bott–Chern connection of
\mathcal{B}
(under appropriate additional assumptions on
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
) is projectively flat.
Elaborating this result one step further, we compute the curvature form of
\nabla
, which can be identified with the Hall conductance of the corresponding many-particle system on
E
, as was first explained in a geometric context by Avron-Seiler-Zograf
[
2
]
, see also
[
14
]
.
It turns out, the degree of the magnetic vector bundle
\mathcal{B}
is
-\dfrac{n\delta}{d}
, where
n=n_{1}+\dots+n_{g}
is the total number particles of the system. Hence, the absolute value of the slope of
\mathcal{B}
is
\dfrac{n}{d}
, see Theorem
6.1
. In
[
4
]
is was proven that
\mathcal{B}
is a simple vector bundle.

Whereas our previous work
[
4
]
was essentially based on the theory of derived categories and Fourier–Mukai transforms, the approach of this paper is more elementary. The core results of this work are explicit formulae for norms of many-body wave functions of FQHE models on a torus (see Lemma
3.2
, Proposition
3.3
, Lemma
4.7
and Proposition
5.1
), based on direct computations with theta-functions.

2.
Elliptic curve: one-particle case

In this work,
E:=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
is a complex torus,
\Lambda=\langle 1,\tau\rangle_{\mathbbm{Z}}\subset\mathbb{C}
is a lattice determined by the modular parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
with
t=\mathfrak{Im}(\tau)>0
.
Recall (see
[
20
, Chapter I]
) that the theta-function with characteristics
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
is defined as:

(2.1)

\vartheta[a,b](z|\tau):=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\bigl(\pi i\tau(n+a)^{2}+2\pi i(n+a)(z+b)\bigr).

As a function of
z
, the theta-series satisfies the following quasi-periodic conditions:

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\vartheta[a,b](z+1|\tau)&=&e^{2\pi ia}\vartheta[a,b](z|\tau)\\
\vartheta[a,b](z+\tau|\tau)&=&e^{-2\pi i(z+b)-\pi i\tau}\vartheta[a,b](z|\tau).\end{array}\right.

Considering
\tau
as a constant, we put:

(2.3)

\vartheta(z):=\vartheta\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right](z|\tau).

Then
\vartheta(-z)=-\vartheta(z)
and
\vartheta
has a unique simple zero at
z=0
modulo the lattice
\Lambda
.

For any holomorphic function
\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
satisfying
\psi(z+1)=\psi(z)
and
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, we define the holomorphic line bundle

\mathcal{L}(c,\psi)
on
E
via the commutative diagram of holomorphic complex manifolds

\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\times V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{pr}_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(c,\psi):=\mathbb{C}\times V/\sim\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{E}

Here,
V=\mathbb{C}
and
(z,v)\sim(z+1,cv)\sim(z+\tau,\psi(z)v)
.

Let
\varphi(z):=\exp(-\pi i\tau-2\pi iz)
.
Then any holomorphic line bundle on
E
of degree
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}:=\mathcal{L}\bigl(1,e^{-2\pi i\xi}\varphi(z)^{k}\bigr)
, with
\xi=a\tau+b
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}\cong\mathcal{L}_{k,\zeta}
if and only if
[\xi]=[\zeta]
in
\mathbb{C}/\Lambda=E
.
Next, the space
\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
of smooth global sections of
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
admits the following description:

(2.4)

\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}:=\left\{\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}f\;\mbox{\rm is smooth},f(z+1)=f(z)\\
f(z+\tau)=e^{-2\pi i\xi}\varphi(z)^{k}f(z)\end{array}\right.\right\}

For
k>0
, the space
\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}:=\Gamma\bigl(E,\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi})
of holomorphic sections of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
has dimension
k
and has a distinguished basis
(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
, where

(2.5)

s_{j}(z)=\vartheta\Bigl[\frac{j-1}{k},0\Bigr](kz+\xi|k\tau)\;\mbox{\rm for}\;1\leq j\leq k.

We set
z=x+\tau y
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the following function

(2.6)

h(z)=h_{k,\xi}(x,y):=\exp(-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty)=\exp\bigl(2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}\bigr)\exp\left(-2\pi kt\left(y+\frac{a}{k}\right)^{2}\right).

This function defines a hermitian metric on the holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
, so that
for any
f\in\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
, we have a smooth function

(2.7)

E\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}},\;[z]\mapsto|f(z)|^{2}\,h(z)

assigning to each point
p=[z]\in E
the square of the length of the value of the section
f
at
p
.
This follows from the transformation laws

h(x+1,y)=h(x,y)\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;h(x,y+1)=e^{-2\pi t(2ky+2a+k)}h(x,y)

combined with the quasi-periodicity properties of
f
given by (
2.4
).

The above choice of a hermitian metric
h
combined with a choice of a volume form
\omega=dx\wedge dy=\frac{i}{2t}dz\wedge d{\bar{z}}
on
E
allows to define the Hilbert space of square integrable global sections of the hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}
:

(2.8)

\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}:=\left\{\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}f(z+1)=f(z),f(z+\tau)=\exp(-2\pi i\xi)\varphi(z)^{k}f(z)\\
\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2}}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty}\big|f(x,y)\big|^{2}\omega<\infty\end{array}\right.\right\}.

In particular, for any
f,g\in\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}
, their scalar product is given by the formula

(2.9)

\langle f,g\rangle=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2}}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty}f(z)\overline{g(z)}dx\wedge dy.

Let
z=u+iv
with
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{u}+i\partial_{v})
. Then we have a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\partial}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{B}_{k,\xi}
, which admits an adjoint operator given by the formula

\bar{\partial}^{\ast}=-\left(\partial+\dfrac{\partial(h)}{h}\right)
. It can be shown that

(2.10)

H^{tor}:=\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\bar{\partial}=-\dfrac{1}{4}\bigl(\partial_{u}^{2}+\partial_{v}^{2}\bigr)-2\pi i\dfrac{kv}{t}\bar{\partial}.

is
an essentially self-adjoint operator
acting
on the Hilbert space
\mathsf{H}_{k,\xi}
, which can be identified with the holomorphic Bochner Laplacian of
\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi},h\bigr)
. The magnetic Schrödinger operator
H^{tor}
given by (
2.10
) is an analogue of the plane operator (
1.1
): it describes quantum mechanical motion of a charged particle on a torus
E
in presence of a constant magnetic field, see
[
6
,
21
,
22
,
5
]
and references therein.

It follows that for
k>0
, the ground state of the operator
H^{tor}
is the vector space

\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}
and the functions
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
form its basis.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
1\leq p,q\leq k
we have:

(2.11)

\langle s_{p},s_{q}\rangle=\delta_{pq}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2kt}}e^{2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}}.

Proof.

Since the theta-series (
2.1
) converge absolutely and uniformly in
z
on compact sets of
\mathbb{C}
, the integration and summation can be interchanged in the series expansions for the scalar product:

\displaystyle\langle s_{p},s_{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2}}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty}\vartheta\Bigl[\frac{p-1}{k},0\Bigr](kz+\xi|k\tau)\overline{\vartheta\Bigl[\frac{q-1}{k},0\Bigr](kz+\xi|k\tau)}dxdy

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{{\pi ik\tau\left(m+\frac{p-1}{k}\right)^{2}-\pi ik\bar{\tau}\left(n+\frac{q-1}{k}\right)^{2}}}S_{m,n},

where

(2.12)

S_{m,n}=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2}}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty}F_{n,m}(x,y)dxdy

with

\displaystyle F_{m,n}(x,y)=e^{2\pi i\bigl((k(n-m)+(p-q)\bigr)x}\cdot e^{2\pi i\Bigl(\bigl(n+\frac{p-1}{k}\bigr)\tau-\bigl(m+\frac{q-1}{k}\bigr)\bar{\tau}\Bigr)ky}\cdot

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\Bigl(\bigl(n+\frac{p-1}{k}\bigr)(b+a\tau)-\bigl(m+\frac{q-1}{k}\bigr)(b+a\bar{\tau})\Bigr)}.

Since
|p-q|<k
, we have:

\int\limits_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi i\bigl(k(n-m)+(p-q)\bigr)x}dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\mbox{\rm if}\;n=m\;\mbox{and}\;p=q\\
0&\mbox{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Suppose first that
p\neq q
.
We can separate the variables
x
and
y
in the integral (
2.12
) to conclude that
S_{m,n}=0
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\langle s_{p},s_{q}\rangle=0
for
p\neq q
.

From now on, assume that
p=q
. Since
S_{m,n}=0
for all
m\neq n
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle s_{p},s_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2\pi kt\left(m+\frac{p-1}{k}\right)^{2}}\int\limits_{[0,1]}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}-4\pi aty-4\pi t\left(m+\frac{p-1}{k}\right)(ky+a)}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\;\;\int\limits_{[0,1]}e^{-2\pi kt\left(y+m+\frac{p-1+a}{k}\right)^{2}+2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}}dy=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\;\;\int\limits_{[m,m+1]}e^{-2\pi kt\left(y+\frac{p-1+a}{k}\right)^{2}+2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi kty^{2}+2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}}dy=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2kt}}\cdot e^{2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{k}}.

Proposition is proven.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The fact that
\langle s_{p},s_{q}\rangle=0
and
\langle s_{p},s_{p}\rangle=\langle s_{q},s_{q}\rangle
for all for
1\leq p\neq q\leq k

can be also shown using an appropriate unitary action of a finite Heisenberg group on the space
\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}
; see
[
4
, Section 2]
.

3.
Laughlin states on a complex torus

In this section, we compute scalar products of many-body wave functions on a torus. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be the number of particles,
m\in\mathbb{N}
another parameter (whose meaning will become clear later) and
\xi=a\tau+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. We need the following notation.

Let
X:=\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{n\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}
and

\epsilon_{m,n}:=(-1)^{m(n-1)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\,1&\mbox{\rm if}\,m\in 2\mathbb{N}\;\mbox{\rm or}\;m,n\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1\\
-1&\mbox{\rm if}\;m\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1\;\mbox{\rm and}\;n\in 2\mathbb{N}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we put
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}:=\mathcal{L}(-1,-e^{-2\pi i\xi}\varphi(z)^{k}\bigr)
. It can be shown that
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}\cong\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi^{\sharp}}
, where
\xi^{\sharp}=\xi+\dfrac{1+\tau}{2}
. Moreover, the function
h_{k,\xi}
given by (
2.6
), defines a hermitian metric on the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}
by the same formula (
2.7
). For
k>0
, we denote by
\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}
the space of global holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{L}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}
:

\mathsf{W}_{k,\xi}^{\sharp}:=\left\{\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}f\;\mbox{\rm is holomorphic},f(z+1)=-f(z)\\
f(z+\tau)=-e^{-2\pi i\xi}\varphi(z)^{k}f(z)\end{array}\right.\right\}.

Next, we define the following line bundle on
X
:

\mathcal{W}_{m,n,\xi}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{L}_{mn,_{\xi}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{mn,\xi}&\mbox{\rm if}\;\epsilon_{m,n}=\;1\\
\mathcal{L}_{mn,_{\xi}}^{\sharp}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}^{\sharp}_{mn,\xi}&\mbox{\rm if}\;\epsilon_{m,n}=-1.\end{array}\right.

By Künneth formula, we have:

\mathsf{W}_{m,n,\xi}:=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{W}_{m,n,\xi})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}&\mbox{\rm if}\;\epsilon_{m,n}=\;1\\
\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}^{\sharp}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathsf{W}_{mn,\xi}^{\sharp}&\mbox{\rm if}\;\epsilon_{m,n}=-1.\end{array}\right.

In other terms,
\mathsf{W}_{m,n,\xi}
can be identified with the vector space

\left\{\mathbb{C}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi\;\mbox{\rm is holomorphic}\\
\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}+1,\dots,z_{n})=\epsilon_{m,n}\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}+1,\dots,z_{n})\\

for all

The made choice (

on the abelian variety

\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle:=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2n}}\widehat{h}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\overline{\Psi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})}dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dy_{n}.

where

Theorem 3.1
.

For any

and

•

We have:

\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}\subset\mathsf{W}_{m,n,\xi}.

In particular,

(3.4)

(here,

for all

is a generalization of the operator

given by (

•

Moreover,
\mathsf{V}_{m,n,\xi}
is precisely the space of those holomorphic sections of the line bundle

, which have at least order

vanishing along of each partial diagonal

•

We have:
\langle\Phi_{p},\Phi_{q}\rangle=0

Proofs of the above results can be found in
[

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
1\leq k,l\leq m
we have:

(3.5)

\langle\Phi_{k},\Phi_{l}\rangle=\delta_{kl}e^{\frac{2\pi}{m}ta^{2}}\cdot\gamma,

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant, which depends on
m,n
and
\tau
and is independent of
\xi
and
l
.

Proof.

Due to Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to compute
\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{1}\rangle
. By definition, we have:

(3.6)

\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{1}\rangle=\int_{X}\big|\vartheta\left[0,0\right](mw+\xi\,|\,m\tau)\big|^{2}\prod\limits_{p<q}\left|\vartheta(z_{p}-z_{q})\right|^{2m}\widehat{h}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,\omega,

where
\omega=dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dy_{n}
.
Consider the shift

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}X,\bigl(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\bigr)\mapsto\left(z_{1}-\frac{1}{mn}\xi,\dots,z_{n}-\frac{1}{mn}\xi\right).

It is clear that
\iota^{\ast}(\omega)=\omega
. Moreover,

\int_{X}f\mathcal{A}=\int_{X}\iota^{\ast}(f)\omega

for any smooth function
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2\pi}{m}ta^{2}}\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2n}}\big|\vartheta\left[0,0\right](mw\,|\,m\tau)\big|^{2}\prod\limits_{p<q}\left|\vartheta(z_{p}-z_{q})\right|^{2m}\prod_{p=1}^{n}h_{mn,0}(x_{p},y_{p})\prod_{p=1}^{n}dx_{p}dy_{p}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2\pi}{m}ta^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{\gamma},

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
depends only on
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Unfortunately, for

we do not have an explicit formula for the constant

appearing in (

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi=a\tau+b
, consider the following function:

(3.7)

\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}s_{1}(z_{1})&\dots&s_{1}(z_{n})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
s_{n}(z_{1})&\dots&s_{n}(z_{n})\\
\end{array}\right|

where
s_{j}(z)=\vartheta\bigl[\frac{j-1}{n},0\bigr](nz+\xi|n\tau)
for
1\leq j\leq n
. Then the following statements are true.

•

\Phi
is a global holomorphic section of the line bundle
\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{L}_{n,\xi}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{n,\xi}
on the abelian variety
X
.

•

There exists a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
such that
\Phi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\mu\cdot\widetilde{\Phi}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
, where

(3.8)

\widetilde{\Phi}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\vartheta\left[\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right]\left((z_{1}+\dots+z_{n})+\xi\,|\,\tau\right)\cdot\prod\limits_{p<q}\vartheta(z_{p}-z_{q}).

•

Finally, we have:

(3.9)

\lVert\Phi\rVert=\left(\frac{1}{2nt}\right)^{\frac{n}{4}}\cdot e^{\pi ta^{2}},

where the scalar product is given by the formula (
3.2
) with respect to the metric
\widehat{h}
given by the expression (
3.1
) for
m=1
.

Proof.

For any
1\leq j\leq n
,
s_{j}
is a global holomorphic section of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{n,\xi}
. Hence,
\Phi
is indeed a global holomorphic section of the line bundle
\mathcal{W}
on
X
. Computing the automorphy factors of the function

\widetilde{\Phi}
with respect to the shifts

and
z_{k}\mapsto z_{k}+\tau
for all

, we conclude that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is also a global holomorphic section of
\mathcal{W}
. As a consequence, the quotient
\mu:=\dfrac{\Phi}{\widetilde{\Phi}}
is a meromorpic function on

having at most simple poles. The residue theorem implies that

is constant (alternatively, one can use the fact that the vector space

is one-dimensional; see Theorem

Finally, the Leibniz formula for the determinant implies that

\langle\Phi,\Phi\rangle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\\
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\varepsilon_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}\int_{X}s_{i_{1}}(z_{1})\dots s_{i_{n}}(z_{n})\overline{s_{j_{1}}(z_{1})}\dots\overline{s_{j_{n}}(z_{n})}h(z_{1})\dots h(z_{n})\omega,

where the sum is taken over all permutations

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\dots&n\\
i_{1}&\dots&i_{n}\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\dots&n\\
j_{1}&\dots&j_{n}\end{array}\right)

and

are the corresponding signatures. It follows that

(3.10)

\langle\Phi,\Phi\rangle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\\
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\varepsilon_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}\langle s_{i_{1}},s_{j_{1}}\rangle\dots\langle s_{i_{n}},s_{j_{n}}\rangle.

By Proposition
2.1
,

is an orthonormal basis of the space

with respect to the hermitian product (

\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2nt}}e^{2\pi t\frac{a^{2}}{n}}\right)^{n}=\left(\frac{1}{2nt}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot e^{2\pi ta^{2}}

and there are

∎

Definition 4.1

Let

•

K

•

All entries of the vector
\vec{u}:=K^{-1}\vec{e}\in\mathbb{Q}^{g}

\vec{e}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\vdots\\

For any symmetric matrix
\Omega\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Mat}_{g\times g}(\mathbb{C})

\mathfrak{Im}(\Omega)

\vec{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}

(4.1)

\Theta[\vec{a},\vec{b}](\vec{z}\,|\,\Omega):=\sum\limits_{\vec{k}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\bigl(\pi i(\vec{k}+\vec{a})^{t}\Omega(\vec{k}+\vec{a})+2\pi i(\vec{k}+\vec{a})^{t}(\vec{z}+\vec{b})\bigr).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
K\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Mat}_{g\times g}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
be a matrix satisfying the constraints of Definition
4.1
. Next, let

\vec{\mathsf{n}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
\vdots\\
n_{g}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{g}

Then we put:

•

X=\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{n_{1}\,\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}\times\dots\times\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{n_{g}\,\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}

D_{K}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{g}\left(\prod\limits_{1\leq p<q\leq n_{k}}\vartheta\left(z_{p}^{(k)}-z_{q}^{(k)}\right)\right)^{K_{kk}}\cdot\prod\limits_{1\leq k<l\leq g}\left(\prod\limits_{p=1}^{n_{k}}\prod\limits_{q=1}^{n_{l}}\vartheta\left(z_{p}^{(k)}-z_{q}^{(l)}\right)\right)^{K_{kl}},

where
\left(z_{1}^{(1)},\dots,z_{n_{1}}^{(1)};\dots;z_{1}^{(g)},\dots,z_{n_{g}}^{(g)}\right)
are the standard local coordinates on
X
.

•

For any
1\leq k\leq g
we put:
w_{k}=z_{1}^{(k)}+\dots+z_{n_{k}}^{(k)}
and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{g})
.

•

For any
\vec{c}\in\Pi

\vec{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}^{g}

\Phi_{\vec{c}}\Bigl(z_{1}^{(1)},\dots,z_{n_{1}}^{(1)};\dots;z_{1}^{(g)},\dots,z_{n_{g}}^{(g)}\Bigr)=\Theta\bigl[\vec{c},\vec{0}\bigr](K\vec{w}+\vec{\zeta}|\Omega)\cdot D_{K},

where
\Omega=\tau K
.

Following
[
23
]
, we call the pair
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
a

, which specifies the multi-layer torus model of FQHE
and
\mathsf{V}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\vec{\zeta}}=\bigl\langle\Phi_{\vec{c}}\,|\;\vec{c}\in\Pi\bigr\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}

Next, we put

(4.3)

\mathcal{W}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\vec{\zeta}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{d,\zeta_{1}}\boxtimes\dots\mathcal{L}_{d,\zeta_{1}}}_{n_{1}\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{d,\zeta_{g}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{d,\zeta_{g}}}_{n_{g}\;\mbox{\scriptsize{\sl times}}}&\mbox{\rm if}\,\epsilon(K)+d\in 2\mathbb{N}\\

and set

As before for the one-layer Laughlin states, the hermitian metric on

is the point-wise product metric

\widehat{h}\bigl(\{z^{k}_{p}\}\bigr):=\prod_{k=1}^{g}\prod_{p=1}^{n_{k}}\otimes h_{d,\zeta_{k}}(z_{p}^{(k)})
induced by the metric (
2.6
).
Again, on each copy of

in
X

\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\int_{X}\Phi\big(\{z_{p}^{(k)}\}\big)\overline{\Psi\big(\{z_{p}^{(k)}\}\big)}\prod_{k=1}^{g}\prod_{p=1}^{n_{k}}h(z_{p}^{(k)})\prod_{k=1}^{g}\prod_{p=1}^{n_{k}}dx_{p}^{(k)}dy_{p}^{(k)}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any Wen datum
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
we have:
\frac{n\delta}{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\vec{u}\in K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Moreover, if
\mathsf{gcd}\bigl(\delta,\frac{n\delta}{d}\bigr)=1
then the class of
\vec{u}
generates the group
\Pi
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that

\vec{u}=\frac{1}{d}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
\vdots\\
n_{g}\end{array}\right)=K^{-1}\vec{e}=\frac{1}{\delta}K^{\sharp}\vec{e},

where
K^{\sharp}
is the adjunct matrix of
K
. Since
K^{\sharp}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Mat}_{g\times g}(\mathbb{Z})
, we see that
\vec{u}\in K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}
and
\delta\vec{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Hence,

\frac{n\delta}{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
, as asserted.

Assume now that
\mathsf{gcd}\bigl(\delta,\frac{n\delta}{d}\bigr)=1
. Since
|\Pi|=\delta
, the order of
[\vec{u}]\in\Pi
is a divisor of
\delta
. If
\delta=s\bar{\delta}
with

and
\bar{\delta}\vec{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
then
\frac{n_{i}\bar{\delta}}{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all

. It follows that

\frac{n\bar{\delta}}{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

, yielding a contradiction.
∎

\mathsf{W}_{K,\vec{\xi}}:=\left\{\mathbb{C}^{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle H}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}\;\mbox{\rm holomorphic}\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{array}[]{l}H(\vec{z}+\vec{l})=H(\vec{z})\\
H(\vec{z}+\tau\vec{l})=U_{\vec{l}}(\vec{z},\vec{\xi})H(\vec{z})\end{array}\mbox{\rm for all}\;\vec{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}\\
\end{array}\right.\right\},

where
U_{\vec{l}}(\vec{z},\vec{\xi})=\exp\bigl(-\pi i(\vec{l},2\vec{\xi}+2K\vec{z}+\Omega\vec{l})\bigr)
and
\Omega=\tau K
.
By
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
, we know that the dimension of
\mathsf{W}_{K,\vec{\xi}}
is
\delta=\det(K)
. Let
\Pi=K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Then
\big|\Pi\big|=\delta
and
\mathsf{W}_{K,\vec{\xi}}
has a distinguished basis
\bigl(H_{\vec{c}}\bigr)_{\vec{c}\in\Pi}
, where

(5.2)

H_{\vec{c}}(\vec{z}):=\Theta\bigl[\vec{c},\vec{0}\bigr](K\vec{z}+\vec{\xi}|\Omega).

In what follows, we identify
\vec{c}\in K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}
with its class in
\Pi
.
The vector space
\mathsf{W}_{K,\vec{\xi}}
is equipped with natural hermitian inner product,

(5.3)

\langle\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\rangle:=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2g}}\exp\bigl(-2\pi t(\vec{y},K\vec{y}+2\vec{a})\bigr)\Phi_{1}(\vec{x},\vec{y})\overline{\Phi_{2}(\vec{x},\vec{y})}d\vec{x}\wedge d\vec{y}.

and by
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
, for any any
\vec{c}_{1}\neq\vec{c}_{2}\in\Pi
we have:

\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{1}},H_{\vec{c}_{2}}\bigr\rangle=0
and
\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{1}},H_{\vec{c}_{1}}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{2}},H_{\vec{c}_{2}}\bigr\rangle.

This means that the Gram matrix for the basis (
5.2
) is a scalar matrix.

Proposition 5.1
.

We have the following formula for the Gram matrix of (
5.2
):

(5.4)

\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{1}},H_{\vec{c}_{2}}\bigr\rangle=\kappa(\vec{\xi})\cdot\delta_{\vec{c}_{1},\vec{c}_{2}},

where
\kappa(\vec{\xi})=\left(2t\delta\right)^{-\frac{g}{2}}\cdot e^{2\pi t(\vec{a},K^{-1}\vec{a})}

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward computation analogous to Proposition
2.1
.

\displaystyle\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{1}},H_{\vec{c}_{2}}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2g}}e^{-2\pi t(\vec{y},K\vec{y}+2\vec{a})}\Theta\Bigl[\vec{c}_{1},\vec{0}\Bigr](K\vec{z}+\vec{\xi}|K\tau)\overline{\Theta\Bigl[\vec{c}_{2},\vec{0}\Bigr](K\vec{z}+\vec{\xi}|K\tau)}d\vec{x}\wedge d\vec{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{m},\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}}e^{\pi i\big(\vec{m}+\vec{c}_{1},K\tau(\vec{m}+\vec{c}_{1})\big)-\pi i\big(\vec{n}+\vec{c}_{2},K\bar{\tau}(\vec{n}+\vec{c}_{2})\big)}S_{\vec{m},\vec{n}},

where

S_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}=\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{2g}}G_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}(\vec{x})H_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}(\vec{y})d\vec{x}\wedge d\vec{y}

with

\begin{array}[]{l}G_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}(\vec{x})=e^{2\pi i\bigl(K\vec{x},(\vec{m}-\vec{n})+(\vec{c_{1}}-\vec{c_{2}})\bigr)}\\
H_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}(\vec{y})=e^{-2\pi t\bigl(\vec{y},K\vec{y}+2\vec{a}\bigr)}e^{2\pi i\bigl(\vec{m}+\vec{c}_{1},K\tau\vec{y}+\vec{\xi}\bigr)-2\pi i\bigl(\vec{n}+\vec{c}_{2},K\bar{\tau}\vec{y}+\vec{\xi}\bigr)}.\end{array}

Since
K=K^{t}
, we get:
\bigl(K\vec{x},(\vec{m}-\vec{n})+(\vec{c_{1}}-\vec{c_{2}})\bigr)=(\vec{x},\vec{l})
, where
\vec{l}=K\left((\vec{m}-\vec{n})+(\vec{c_{1}}-\vec{c_{2}})\right)
. As
\vec{c}_{1},\vec{c}_{2}\in K^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}
, we have:
\vec{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
. Moreover,
\vec{l}=\vec{0}
if and only if

\vec{m}=\vec{n}
and
\vec{c_{1}}=\vec{c_{2}}\;\mbox{\rm in}\;\Pi
.
It follows that

\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{g}}G_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}d\vec{x}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{g}\int\limits_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi il_{k}x_{k}}dx_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\mbox{\rm if}\;\vec{m}=\vec{n}\;\mbox{and}\;\vec{c_{1}}=\vec{c_{2}}\;\mbox{\rm in}\;\Pi\\
0&\mbox{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

As a consequence, we have:
\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}_{1}},H_{\vec{c}_{2}}\bigr\rangle=0
if
\vec{c_{1}}\neq\vec{c_{2}}
in
\Pi
.

Now, assume that
\vec{c_{1}}=\vec{c}_{2}=\vec{c}
. Since
S_{\vec{m},\vec{n}}=0
for all
\vec{m}\neq\vec{n}
, we have:

\displaystyle\bigl\langle H_{\vec{c}},H_{\vec{c}}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}}e^{-2\pi t\big(\vec{n}+\vec{c},K(\vec{n}+\vec{c})\big)}\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{g}}e^{-2\pi t(\vec{y},K\vec{y}+2\vec{a})-4\pi t(\vec{n}+\vec{c},K\vec{y}+\vec{a})}d\vec{y}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi t(\vec{a},K^{-1}\vec{a})}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}}\iint\limits_{[0,1]^{g}}e^{-2\pi t\big(\vec{y}+\vec{n}+\vec{c}+K^{-1}\vec{a},K(\vec{y}+\vec{n}+\vec{c})+\vec{a}\big)}d\vec{y}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi t(\vec{a},K^{-1}\vec{a})}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{g}}e^{-2\pi t\bigl(\vec{y}+\vec{c}+K^{-1}\vec{a},K(\vec{y}+\vec{c}+K^{-1}\vec{a})\bigr)}d\vec{y}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi t(\vec{a},K^{-1}\vec{a})}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{g}}e^{-2\pi t(\vec{y},K\vec{y})}d\vec{y}=(2t\delta)^{\frac{g}{2}}\cdot e^{2\pi t(\vec{a},K^{-1}\vec{a})}.

Proposition is proven.
∎

6.
Hermitian connections and adiabatic curvature in the fractional quantum Hall effect

We first recall the definition of the canonical Bott–Chern connection of a holomorphic hermitian vector bundle on a complex manifold; see
[
3
,
16
]
.

Let
M
be a smooth real manifold,
\mathcal{O}
the corresponding structure sheaf,
\mathcal{A}^{p}
the sheaf of differential
p
-forms on
M
and
\mathcal{E}
a smooth complex vector bundle of rank
n
on
M
. Recall that a connection on
\mathcal{E}
is a morphism of sheaves of complex vector spaces
\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}
such that for any
open subset
U\subset M
,
f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O})
and
s\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{E})
we have:

\nabla(f\cdot s)=f\cdot\nabla(s)+df\otimes s.

We get an induced morphism of sheaves
\mathcal{A}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\nabla}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{E}
given locally by the formula

\widetilde{\nabla}(\theta\otimes s)=d\theta\otimes s-\theta\wedge\nabla(s),

where
\theta\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{A}^{1})
,
s\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{E})
and
\theta\wedge\nabla(s)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}(\theta\wedge\theta_{k})\otimes s_{k}
if
\nabla(s)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\theta_{k}\otimes s_{k}
. It can be checked that the composition
\widetilde{\nabla}\nabla:\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{E}
is a morphism of complex vector bundles. Using the canonical isomorphism

\Hom_{M}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{A}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow\Gamma\bigl(M,\mathcal{A}^{2}\otimes\mathit{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{E})\bigr)
, we get a distinguished section
K_{\nabla}\in\Gamma\bigl(M,\mathcal{A}^{2}\otimes\mathit{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{E})\bigr)
called curvature of
\mathcal{E}
. A connection
\nabla
is called flat if
K_{\nabla}=0
and
projectively flat
if there exists

\mathcal{A}\in\Gamma(M,\mathcal{A}^{2})
such that
K_{\nabla}=\omega\cdot\mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Let
U\subset M
be an open subset and
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{E})
. Then

F=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n})
is a local frame of
\mathcal{E}
if
\psi_{1}(p),\dots,\psi_{n}(p)\in\mathcal{E}\big|_{p}
form a basis of
\mathcal{E}\big|_{p}
for all
p\in U
. A choice of a frame
F
is equivalent to a choice of a local trivialization
\mathcal{O}_{U}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\big|_{U}
.

Given such a frame
F
, we can find for any
1\leq k,l\leq n
a unique
\theta_{kl}\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{A}^{1})
such that

\nabla(\psi_{k})=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}\theta_{kl}\psi_{l}.

Next, we put:

\mathcal{A}_{kl}:=d\theta_{kl}-\sum\limits_{p=1}^{n}\theta_{kp}\wedge\theta_{pl}.

The
(n\times n)
-matrix
K^{F}_{\nabla}
gives a local description of the curvature

K_{\nabla}
with respect to the frame
F
. Its trace

(6.1)

\omega_{\nabla}=\mathsf{tr}(K^{F}_{\nabla})=d\Bigl(\sum\limits_{p=1}^{n}\theta_{pp}\Bigr)\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{A}^{2})

does not depend on the choice of a frame
F
. Because of this invariance, we actually get a global closed
2
-form
\omega_{\nabla}\in\Gamma(M,\mathcal{A}^{2})
, whose local description is given by (
6.1
).

Next, assume that
h=\langle-\,,\,-\rangle
is a hermitian metric on
\mathcal{E}
. Then a connection
\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}
is hermitian (or metric) with respect to
h
if

d\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle=\bigl\langle\nabla(s_{1}),s_{2}\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle s_{1},\nabla(s_{2})\bigr\rangle

for any open subset
U\subseteq M
and any local sections
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{E})
.

Now assume that
M
is a complex manifold of complex dimension
d
and
\mathcal{E}
is a holomorphic vector bundle on
M
. For any
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have the corresponding sheaf of differential
(p,q)
-forms
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}
as well as sheaf morphisms
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{p+1,q}
and
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\partial}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{p,q+1}
given by the formulae

\partial(w)=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial z_{l}}dz_{l}\wedge dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J}\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\bar{\partial}(w)=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial\bar{z}_{l}}d\bar{z}_{l}\wedge dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J},

where

w=\sum\limits_{I,J}f_{IJ}dz_{I}\wedge d\bar{z}_{J}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\dots<i_{p}\\
j_{1}<\dots<j_{q}\end{subarray}}f_{i_{1},\dots,i_{p};j_{1},\dots j_{q}}dz_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dz_{k_{p}}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\bar{z}_{j_{q}}\in\Gamma\bigl(U,\mathcal{A}^{p,q}\bigr).

In particular, there is a direct sum decomposition
\mathcal{A}^{1}=\mathcal{A}^{0,1}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{1,0}
. A holomorphic structure on
\mathcal{E}
defines a morphism of sheaves
\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{E}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{0,1}\otimes\mathcal{E}
.
By a result of Bott and Chern
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
for any complex manifold
M
and a holomorphic hermitian vector bundle
(\mathcal{E},h)
there exists a
unique
hermitian connection

\mathcal{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{E}
such that

\nabla^{(0,1)}=\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

The curvature
K_{\nabla}
of this canonical connection
\nabla
admits the following local description. For an open subset
U\subseteq M
, let
F=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n})
be a
holomorphic
frame of
\mathcal{E}
. Consider the corresponding Gram matrix

C=(\langle\psi_{k},\psi_{l}\rangle)\in\mathsf{Mat}_{n\times n}\bigl(\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O})\bigr)
.
Then the curvature matrix of
\nabla
with respect to the frame
F
is given by the following expression:

(6.2)

K^{F}_{\nabla}=\bar{\partial}\bigl(\partial(C)\cdot C^{-1}\bigr).

Now, let
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
be a Wen datum. In
[
4
]
, we constructed a holomorphic hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{B}
on the torus
E=\mathbb{C}/\langle 1,\tau\rangle

having the following property: for any
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
there exists an isomorphisms of
hermitian
complex vector spaces
\mathcal{B}\Big|_{[\xi]}\cong\mathsf{V}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\xi}
, where the hermitian product on
\mathsf{V}_{K,\vec{\mathsf{n}},\xi}
is the restriction of the inner product (
4.4
) on the Hilbert space of the corresponding many-body system. In other words,
\mathcal{B}
has rank
\delta
and
F:=\bigl(\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{\delta})
is a
holomorphic
frame of
\mathcal{B}
in a neighbourhood of the point
[\xi]\in\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(K,\vec{\mathsf{n}})
be a Wen datum satisfying the conditions of Lemma
4.3
.
Then the Bott–Chern connection
\nabla
of the hermitian vector bundle
\mathcal{B}
is projectively flat. Moreover, the trace of the curvature of
\nabla
is
\omega_{\nabla}=-\dfrac{\pi}{t}\cdot\dfrac{n\delta}{d}d\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}
(recall that
\xi=a\tau+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
) and
\mathsf{deg}(\mathcal{B})=-\dfrac{n\delta}{d}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
4.7
, the Gram matrix
C
of
\nabla
with respect to the frame
F
is a scalar matrix. The formula (
6.2
) implies that

K^{F}_{\nabla}=\omega I_{g\times g},

where

\omega=\bar{\partial}\partial\log\left(e^{\frac{2\pi n}{d}ta^{2}}\cdot\gamma\right)=\bar{\partial}\partial\left(\frac{2\pi n}{d}ta^{2}\right).

In particular,

\nabla
is projectively flat, as asserted. Since

a=\dfrac{\xi-\bar{\xi}}{2it}
, we have:

\dfrac{2\pi n}{d}ta^{2}=-\dfrac{\pi n}{2td}(\xi^{2}-2\xi\bar{\xi}+\bar{\xi}^{2})
. It follows that

(6.3)

\omega_{\nabla}=\delta\cdot\omega=-\frac{\pi}{t}\cdot\frac{n\delta}{d}d\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}.

In particular,
c_{1}(\mathcal{B})=\dfrac{i}{2\pi}\omega_{\nabla}=-\dfrac{i}{2t}\cdot\dfrac{n\delta}{d}d\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}
and
\mathsf{deg}(\mathcal{B})=-\dfrac{n\delta}{d}
.
∎

Remark 6.2

It follows that the absolute value of the slope of
\mathcal{B}

\left|\dfrac{\mathsf{deg}(\mathcal{B})}{\mathsf{rk(\mathcal{B})}}\right|=\frac{n}{d}.

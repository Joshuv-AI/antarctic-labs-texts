Bipartite Euler Systems for certain Galois representations
Chandrakant Aribam, Pronay Kumar Karmakar

Abstract
. Let E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} be an elliptic curve with ordinary reduction at a prime p p , and let K K be an imaginary quadratic field. The anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture, depending upon the sign of the functional equation of L ⁡ ( E / K , s ) L(E/K,s) , predicts the behavior of Selmer group of E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} along the anticyclotomic tower of K K . Some of the crucial ideas of Bertolini and Darmon on this conjecture have been abstracted by Howard into an axiomatic set-up through a notion of Bipartite Euler systems, assuming that E ⁡ [ p ] E[p] is an irreducible representation of G K G_{K} . We generalize this work by assuming only ( E ⁡ [ p ] ) G K = 0 (E[p])^{G_{K}}=0 . We use the results of Howard, Nekovář and Castella et al ., along with those of Mazur and Rubin on Kolyvagin systems to show one divisibility of the anticyclotomic main conjecture, for both the signs. The other divisibility can be reduced to proving the nonvanishing of sufficiently many p p -adic L L -functions attached to a family of congruent modular forms.

Introduction

Let

be an elliptic curve defined over

. Now let

be a rational prime such that

has good ordinary reduction at

be the anticyclotomic

. The

-Selmer group

-cotorsion with characteristic ideal related to the
p
-adic

-function.

Beginning with the monumental and fundamental work of Bertolini and Darmon on this conjecture
[
BD
]
in the definite case, much is known when the residual representation
\overline{\rho}_{E}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
of

is

, with surjective image, and the modular form

attached to

is
p
-isolated.
Some aspects of the indefinite case are also treated in
[
Be
]
. When the residual representation

is
reducible
, some cases have been proved by Castella
et al

[
CGLS
]
in the indefinite case using the theory of Kolyvagin systems, under an assumption of the Heegner hypothesis where

is required to be equal to 1. Each of these works depend on the work of many authors, but they all have been inspired by the work of Bertolini and Darmon
[
BD
]
.

Adapting the work of Mazur and Rubin (
[
MR
]
) on Kolyvagin systems, the argument of Bertolini and Darmon has been axiomatized by Howard in
[
H3
]

]

Euler system, which we briefly explain. Let

be the modular form attached to

(by the work of Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, Diamond). For a choice of positive integer

, all of which are inert in

. One then considers a graph whose vertices are the elements in

with edges connecting
\mathfrak{n}
to
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}
for each
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{k}

\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}

is

In the axiomatic set-up in
[
H3
]
, Howard, as in
[
MR
]
, considers, a rank two module

, with maximal ideal

, on which there is a continuous action of

, and a perfect

-equivariant alternating pairing into

of

The set of squarefree products of

. Then to each

Further,

[

]
, Howard defines the sheaf of Euler systems by attaching to an even vertex

For each

Howard shows how a sufficiently non-trivial bipartite Euler system can be used to bound the lengths of

In this manuscript, we extend the work of Howard in

Theorem 3.5

(
[
CGLS
, Prop. 3.3.6]
)
.

Suppose
\alpha\neq 1
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in H^{1}(K,T_{\alpha})
are cocyles such that

Ac_{1}+Ac_{2}
contains a submodule isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{m}}^{d_{1}}A\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}^{d_{2}}A
for some

. Then for any

there exist infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}{\in}{\mathcal{L}_{k}}
such that

\operatorname{ord}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c_{3})){\geq}\operatorname{ord}(c_{3})-\varepsilon_{0}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
k>\varepsilon_{0}
and
\alpha\neq 1
.
Then for any cyclic free
A
-submodule
C=Ac
of rank one contained in
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T_{\alpha})
, there exists infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\neq 0
.

Proof.

For brevity, we write
T
for
T_{\alpha}
.
Since

\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T){\cong}A^{e(\mathfrak{n})}\oplus M_{\mathfrak{n}}{\oplus}M_{\mathfrak{n}}
, and
it contains a non-zero submodule
C
, we have
e_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
or
M_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. It is enough to
prove the statement in the following cases.

Case I:
Let
M_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. Then
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T){\supseteq}M_{\mathfrak{n}}{\oplus}M_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. Let
x\in M_{\mathfrak{n}}
be a non-zero cohomology class.
Let
x
be not torsion. Then

Ax
is a free
A
-submodule, and
Ax\oplus Ax
contains a
submodule isomorphic to
A\oplus A
.

Let
x
be
A
-torsion, and let
t=\operatorname{ord}(x)
. Then
t<k
,
\pi^{t}x=0
, and the natural map

A\longrightarrow Ax
has kernel generated by
\pi^{t}
, and the
natural surjective map
A\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{k-t}A
factors through
\pi^{t}A
. Therefore,
\pi^{k-t}A\cong A/\pi^{t}A\cong Ax
.

Taking
c_{1}
to be equal to
x
coming from
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
c_{2}
from
the other summand
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
, we see that the submodule
xA\oplus xA
has a submodule isomorphic to
\mathfrak{m}^{t}A\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{t}A
.
Then we get two cohomology classes
c_{1},c_{2}\in\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}}(K,T)
such that

c_{1}A\oplus c_{2}A\supset\mathfrak{m}^{t}A\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{t}A
.
Now by Theorem
3.5
we have

\operatorname{ord}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)){\geq}\operatorname{ord}(c)-r(C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{\alpha})\geq k-\varepsilon_{0}>0

Therefore, by Theorem
3.5
, we have infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\neq 0
.

Case II:

Let
e_{\mathfrak{n}}=1,M_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
. Then
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=Ax\cong A
, for some generator
x
.
We first show that either
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\neq H^{1}(K,T)
or
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T))\neq 0

for all
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}\backslash\{\mbox{ primes dividing }\mathfrak{n}\}
.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that, both the equalities hold simultaneously, i.e.,

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=H^{1}(K,T),\mbox{ and }\\
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T))=0\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}\backslash\{\mbox{ primes dividing }\mathfrak{n}\}.\end{cases}

Here
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{N}
.
By definition,

\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\cap\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)=H^{1}(K,T)\cap\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)=\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T).

Then

\operatorname{length}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)))=0\mbox{ and }\operatorname{length}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)))=0.

This is a contradiction as these lengths add up to
\operatorname{length}(A)=k
by
[
H3
, Prop 2.2.9]
.
This proves our claim as
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\neq 0
.

In case,
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\neq H^{1}(K,T)
, let
c_{2}\in H^{1}(K,T)
such that it is not
zero in the quotient
H^{1}(K,T)/\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
. Then the submodule generated
by
x
and
c_{2}
contains a submodule isomorphic to
A\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{b}
for some
a,b\geq 0
. Here again, for the free submodule

, by Theorem
3.5
we get infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\neq 0
.

In the other case,
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=H^{1}(K,T)\cong A
, so

, it is clear that we have infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\neq 0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now have the following key lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\operatorname{length}(A)>\varepsilon_{0}
. Then for any
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}
and any cyclic free rank one
A
-submodule

\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(C)\cong H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)
.

Proof.

Let
C
be generated by
c
, i.e.,
C=Ac\cong A
.
Suppose
k=\operatorname{length}(A)
, then
\operatorname{ord}(c)=k
.
By the previous Proposition, there exists infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that

\operatorname{ord}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c))\geq\operatorname{ord}(c)-\varepsilon_{0}\geq k-\varepsilon_{0}>0.

Therefore,
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}
takes
C
injectively into

, which by equation (
2.1
) is isomorphic to
A
. Hence
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(C)\cong H^{1}_{unr}(K,T)
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\operatorname{length}(A)>\varepsilon_{0}
. Then for any
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}
and any free
A
-submodule
C\subset\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
of rank one, there exists infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
C/\mathfrak{m}\cong H^{1}_{unr}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T)
. In particular,

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T))\neq 0
, for infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

By the lemma above, for infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
, we have an isomorphism:

C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{unr}(K,T)\cong A,

which induces the following isomorphism going modulo
\mathfrak{m}
:

C/\mathfrak{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{unr}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T)\cong A/\mathfrak{m}.

For each of these primes
\mathfrak{l}
, as
C/\mathfrak{m}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)/{\mathfrak{m}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\cong\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T)
, we have

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T))\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T))\neq 0
for some
\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}
and
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
,
\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
. Then, we have an isomorphism
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T))\cong A
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)\cong A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T/\mathfrak{m})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T/\mathfrak{m})\cong A/\mathfrak{m}.}

Suppose
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T))\ncong A
. Since
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T))\neq 0
, we have
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T/{\mathfrak{m}}T))\neq 0

4.
Bipartite Euler System over Artinian Rings

We continue with the notations introduced in Sections
2
and
3
, along with the Hypothesis
2.8
. We still continue to denote
T_{\alpha}
by
T
. Following Howard
[
H3
, Def 2.3.2]
, we define a bipartite Euler system as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

A
bipartite Euler system of odd type
for
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})
is a pair of families

\{\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}{\in}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\mid\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}^{odd}}\}\mbox{ and }\{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}{\in}A\mid\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}^{even}}\}

related by the following reciprocity laws:

(i)

for any
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}{\in}{\mathcal{N}^{odd}}
, there exists an isomorphism of
A
-modules

A/(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\cong H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)/A.\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}),

(ii)

for any
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}{\in}\mathcal{N}^{even}
, there exists an isomorphism of
A
-modules

A/(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})\cong H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)/A.\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}).

A
bipartite Euler system of even type
is defined in the same way, but with the even and odd term interchanged everywhere in the definition. An Euler system is said to be
non-trivial
if
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
for some
\mathfrak{n}
. By the reciprocity law this is equivalent to saying
\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}{\neq}0
for some
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\operatorname{length}(A)>\varepsilon_{0}
. Then there are no nontrivial Euler systems for
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})
of even type.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a non-trivial Euler system of
even
type. Then for some
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
.
As
e_{\mathfrak{n}}=1
,
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}
contains a free
A
-module of rank 1, and
by Lemma
3.7
,
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T))\neq 0
for some
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
.
It follows that the injective map

\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)/\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)\cong A

is an isomorphism. By Proposition
(ii)
, we get
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})=0
, which is not possible by the reciprocity law.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\operatorname{length}(A)=k>\varepsilon_{0}
, and consider a non-trivial Euler system of odd type for
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})
. Let
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\cong A^{e_{\mathfrak{n}}}\oplus M_{\mathfrak{n}}\oplus M_{\mathfrak{n}}
be as in the Proposition

2.10
(i)
. We have

(i)

for
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}
, if
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
, then
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}M_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
,

(ii)

for
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
,
if
\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
and is contained in a cyclic
A
-free rank one submodule
C
of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
,
then
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}M_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{k-1}M_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. This implies
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
contains a free submodule of rank one, say
C
. Let
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
and

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(C)\cong H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)
(using Lemma
3.7
). By Proposition
2.10

(ii)
,
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T))=0
, from which we get
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})=0
. By the first reciprocity laws,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
, which gives a contradiction.

(ii) Let
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{k-1}M_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. By Proposition
2.10
(i)
we know
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
contains a free submodule of rank two, say
D
. Thus by equation (
2.1
), the kernel of the following map,

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}:\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T){\longrightarrow}H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)

which is
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
contains a free submodule of rank one for any
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
. As
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)\subset\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}(K,T)
, and
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}
, so
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}\neq 0
, otherwise
\mathfrak{m}^{k-1}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)=0
.
By part (i),
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}=0
.
By the second reciprocity law, we have,
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L},\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
. For
\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
, this gives a contradiction as there are infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
, such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(C)\cong A
( by Lemma
3.7
).
∎

Definition 4.4
.

A non-trivial Euler system of
odd
type is said to be
free
if for every
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
,
\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}
is contained in a cyclic submodule of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
which is
A
-free of rank one.

Definition 4.5
.

[
H3
, Def 2.2.8]

For
\mathfrak{n}{\in}{\mathcal{N}}
, we recall the definition of the
stub module

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})}A,&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}^{even}\\
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})},&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}^{odd}.\end{cases}

The stub modules for
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}
are related to each other in the following manner.

Proposition 4.6

(
[
H3
, Cor 2.2.13]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}{\in}{\mathcal{N}}
. Then there is an isomorphism of
A
-modules

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}){\cong}\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}},\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}^{odd}}

and

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}){\cong}\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}},\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}^{even}}.

Let
\rho_{0}:=\operatorname{max}\{\rho(\mathfrak{n}):\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}\}
.
For
S
a finite set of primes, let
G_{S}
denotes the Galois group of the maximal extension of
K
unramified outside
S
over
K
. Then, it follows from the
Hermite-Minkowski Theorem
that
\dim_{A/\mathfrak{m}}H^{1}(G_{S},T/{\mathfrak{m}}T)
is finite. So
\rho_{0}
is bounded above by
\dim_{A/\mathfrak{m}}H^{1}(G_{S},T/{\mathfrak{m}}T)
. We define
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon_{0}{\rho_{0}}
. Then
\varepsilon
is a non-negative integer independent of the length of
A
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\operatorname{length}(A)>\varepsilon_{0}
.
For any free Euler system of odd type for
(T,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})
,
{\pi}^{\varepsilon}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\in\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}
for every
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}
, and
{\pi}^{\varepsilon}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}\in\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}
for every
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
. Equivalently, in terms of the
A
-module
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
in the decomposition

\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}\cong A^{e_{\mathfrak{n}}}\oplus M_{\mathfrak{n}}\oplus M_{\mathfrak{n}}
, we have

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})\leq\begin{cases}\operatorname{ind}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}},A)+\varepsilon&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}\\
\operatorname{ind}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}},\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T))+\varepsilon&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
\rho(\mathfrak{n})
. If
\mathfrak{n}
is even and
\rho(\mathfrak{n})=0
, then
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=0
, so
M_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
. Hence
\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}=A
. Similarly if
\rho(\mathfrak{n})=1
and
\mathfrak{n}
is odd, then again we have
M_{\mathfrak{n}}=0
. So the result follows. We now assume that
\rho(\mathfrak{n})\geq 2
, and so
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})\neq 0
.

First suppose that
\operatorname{exp}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Then by the structure of
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
as an
A
-module, we have,
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})\leq{\rho_{0}}.\varepsilon_{0}
.

Now let us assume that
\operatorname{exp}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})>\varepsilon_{0}
, and
\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}^{even}
. Then
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T)
contains an element, say
c
, such that
\operatorname{ord}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(c))>\operatorname{ord}(c)-\varepsilon_{0}>0
for some
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
, fix any
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
prime to
\mathfrak{n}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}(K,T/\mathfrak{m}T))\neq 0
. Also we suppose that
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\neq 0
. By Proposition
2.10
(iii)
and the induction hypothesis,
\pi^{\varepsilon}\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}\in\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}
, and so we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})+a

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}},\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})})+a+{\rho_{0}}\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}),\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}))+a+{\rho_{0}}\varepsilon_{0}

where
a
is as in the Proposition
2.10
(ii)
.
By the first reciprocity law we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}},A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}),H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}),\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}))+\operatorname{length}(H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)/\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}}),\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})})+\operatorname{length}(A)-b.

Since by Proposition
2.10
(ii)
,
a+b=\operatorname{length}(A)
, so
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})\leq\operatorname{ind}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}},A)+\varepsilon
.

Now let us assume that
\exp(M_{\mathfrak{n}})>\varepsilon_{0}
and
\mathfrak{n}
is odd. Let
C_{\mathfrak{n}}
be a free
A
-module in
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}
. Consider a prime
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
prime to
\mathfrak{n}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}
takes
C_{\mathfrak{n}}
isomorphically into
H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)
( using Lemma
3.7
). By Proposition
2.10
(iii)
we have
\rho(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})=\rho(\mathfrak{n})-1<\rho(\mathfrak{n})
. So, by induction hypothesis we have

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})\leq\operatorname{ind}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}},A)+\varepsilon
.
By Proposition
2.12
and second reciprocity law we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}},A)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}}),H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T))+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{n}},\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})})+\varepsilon.

∎

Remark 4.8
.

(i)

A proof of the above length bound is obtained in
[
H3
, Theorem 2.3.7]
, assuming that
T/\mathfrak{m}T
is an irreducible representation of
G_{K}
. There the error term
\varepsilon
does not occur. The above theorem subsumes the result of Howard by taking
\varepsilon=0
if
T/\mathfrak{m}T
is irreducible.

(ii)

The above theorem can be compared with
[
CGLS
, Theorem 3.2.1]
. An error term is also present there.

5.
Stub Modules

We briefly recall some facts about Stub modules from
[
H3
]
. As in the previous section, we denote the twist
T_{\alpha}
by
T
, and retain the notations in the previous section. Our definition of a
core
vertex ( Def
5.1
) is different from that of Howard or Mazur-Rubin’s in
[
MR
]
and we call them absolute core vertices.
Let
\mathcal{X}:=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a graph with the set of vertices
\mathcal{V}:=\{v(\mathfrak{n})\mid\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}\}
and the set of edges
\mathcal{E}:=\{e(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})\mid\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}\}
.
A vertex
v(\mathfrak{n})
is called even ( resp. odd) if
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}
(resp.
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}
). We often write
\mathfrak{n}
is an even or odd vertex accordingly as
v(\mathfrak{n})
is even or odd vertex. Attached to this graph is an
Euler System Sheaf of
A
-modules
, which is defined for a vertex
v=v(\mathfrak{n})
and an edge
e=e(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})
as:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ES}(v)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F(\mathfrak{n})}}&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}\\
A&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\operatorname{ES}(e)=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}\\
H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}.\end{cases}

If
e=e(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})
is an edge with end point
v
, we recall the
vertex-to-edge
map

{\psi}^{e}_{v}:\operatorname{ES}(v){\longrightarrow}\operatorname{ES}(e)

defined as follows. If
v
is odd then

{\psi}^{e}_{v}=\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}:\begin{cases}\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})}{\longrightarrow}H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }v=v(\mathfrak{n})\\
\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})}{\longrightarrow}H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }v=v(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}).\end{cases}

If
v
is even then we fix, using equation (
2.1
), an isomorphism

{\psi}^{e}_{v}:A{\cong}\begin{cases}H^{1}_{unr}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }v=v(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})\\
H^{1}_{ord}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T)&\mbox{ if }v=v(\mathfrak{n}).\end{cases}

Here we fix a choice of this isomorphism for each edge
e
and even vertex

v
.

Over each vertex
v=v(\mathfrak{n})
and edge
e=e(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})
, the
stub sheaf

\operatorname{Stub}(\mathcal{X})
is defined, as follows

\operatorname{Stub}(v):=\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}{\subset}\operatorname{ES}(v)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\operatorname{Stub}(e):=\begin{cases}\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}})&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{odd}\\
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}^{even}.\end{cases}

If
v
is an even vertex, and
v^{\prime}
odd with edge
e=e(v,v^{\prime})
, then the vertex-to-edge map

{\psi}^{e}_{v^{\prime}}:\operatorname{Stub}(v^{\prime}){\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Stub}(e)
is surjective. By Corollary
4.6
, it can be seen that the map
{\psi}^{e}_{v}
gives an isomorphism

\operatorname{Stub}(v){\cong}\operatorname{Stub}(e)
.

Definition 5.1
.

(i)

Let
u=\min\{\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})|\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}\}
. Then a vertex
v
of
\mathcal{X}
is called an
absolute core vertex
if
\operatorname{Stub}(v){\cong}{\mathfrak{m}}^{u}A
.

(ii)

The
absolute core subgraph

\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{abs}}\subset\mathcal{X}
is the graph whose vertices are the absolute core vertices of
\mathcal{X}
, with two vertices in
\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{abs}}
connected by an edge in
\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{abs}}
if and only if they are connected by an edge in
\mathcal{X}
. We let
\operatorname{Stub}(\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{abs}})
be the restriction of
\operatorname{Stub}(\mathcal{X})
to
\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{abs}}
.

Remark 5.2
.

In the special case when
T/\mathfrak{m}T
irreducible, then by
[
H3
, Lemma 2.4.9]
, the minimal length

, and we recover the definition of a
core
vertex in
[
H3
, Def 2.4.2]
and
[
MR
, Def 4.1.8]
. In other words, in the irreducible case

vertices are those vertices
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\operatorname{Stub}_{\mathfrak{n}}\cong A
(see
[
H3
]
).

Definition 5.3
.

(i)

A
path
from a vertex
v
to a vertex
v^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{X}
is a finite sequence of vertices
v=v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{r}=v^{\prime}
such that
v_{i}
is connected to

by an edge

. A path is surjective (for the locally cyclic sheaf

) if the vertex-to-edge map

{\psi}^{e_{i}}_{v_{i+1}}:\operatorname{Stub}(v_{i+1}){\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Stub}(e_{i})

isomorphism
for every

. We define a

in

in the same way.

(ii)

The graph is said to be

if for any two vertices in the graph, there exists a

path between them.
By

, a

v_{0},.....,v_{r}
in
\mathcal{X}
is

if and only if for every
i

\operatorname{length}(\operatorname{Stub}(v_{i+1}))\leq\operatorname{length}(\operatorname{Stub}(v_{i})).

Definition 5.4
.

(i)

Let
\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}^{even}
, then we say that the vertex
v(\mathfrak{n})
is
universally trivial
if

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})})\cong A\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}_{1}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\implies\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}_{2}\nmid\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\quad,

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-2})})\cong A\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\cdots\mathfrak{l}_{2k-2}\implies\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2k}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}_{2k}\nmid\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\quad

and the process continues indefinitely.

(ii)

Let
\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathcal{N}}^{odd}
, then we say that the vertex
v(\mathfrak{n})
is

universally trivial
if

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})})\cong A\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}_{1}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\implies\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}_{2}\nmid\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2})})\cong A\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}_{3}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\implies\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{4}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}\mathfrak{l}_{3})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}_{4}\nmid\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\,

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-2})})\cong A\mbox{ for some }\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1}\nmid\mathfrak{n},\implies\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2r}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1})})=0\,\forall\,\mathfrak{l}_{2r}\nmid\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\quad

and the process continues indefinitely.

Remark 5.5
.

By Lemma
3.7
, for any
odd
vertex
v(\mathfrak{a})
, there exists infinitely many
\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{a})})\cong A
, so the above process continues indefinitely and both the notions are indeed well-defined.

Proposition 5.6
.

(i)

If
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=u
, then
\mathfrak{n}
is universally trivial.

(ii)

Let
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{a}
be any two vertices in
\mathcal{N}
which are universally trivial. Then

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{a}}).

Proof.

(i) First, let
\mathfrak{n}
be even, such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=u
, and
\mathfrak{n}
is not universally trivial. Then, there are primes
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{l}_{2k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})})=0\quad\forall\quad\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{l}_{1}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mbox{ with }\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l})})\cong A\mbox{ then }\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1})})=0\quad\forall\quad\mathfrak{l}_{2}\nmid\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots\quad\cdots

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}\nmid\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-2}\mbox{ with }\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-2})})\cong A\mbox{ but}\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2k}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1})})\neq 0\mbox{ with }\mathfrak{l}_{2k}\nmid\mathfrak{n}.

Then it is easy to see from Proposition
2.12
that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}})=...=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}})
, and

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k-1}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}...\mathfrak{l}_{2k}})+a_{2k}

where
a_{2k}
is as in the Proposition
2.12
and
a_{2k}\neq 0
(by the above assumption).
This contradicts that
u
is minimal. Hence
\mathfrak{n}
is universally trivial.

When
\mathfrak{n}
is odd the proof is similar to the above one. Indeed, let us consider
\mathfrak{n}
is odd,
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=u
, but not universally trivial. Then there exists primes
\mathfrak{l}_{1},...,\mathfrak{l}_{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n})})\cong A,\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}{\mathfrak{l}_{1}})})=0,...,\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-2})})\cong A

but

\operatorname{loc}_{\mathfrak{l}_{2r}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1})})\neq 0.

By Proposition
2.12
, we have

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}})=...=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1}})

and

\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r-1}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}...\mathfrak{l}_{2r}})+a_{2r}

where
a_{2r}
is a non zero positive integer. This contradicts that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=u
is minimal. This completes the proof of the first part of the Proposition.

Before we proceed to prove the second part of this Proposition, we prove the lemma below.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}...\mathfrak{l}_{s}
be a universally trivial vertex. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be any vertex such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=u
. Then there exists a universally trivial vertex
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

Case-I:
Let
\mathfrak{n}
be
even
. Then by Proposition
2.12
,
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}})
and
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}
is odd and universally trivial. By Lemma
3.7
there exists a prime
\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}})
(Proposition
2.12
) and
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}
is even and universally trivial. Proceeding like this and adding primes
\mathfrak{l}_{2},\mathfrak{l}_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,\mathfrak{l}_{s}
, we get a universally trivial vertex
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})
for some
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
.

Case-II:
Let
\mathfrak{n}
be
odd
. Then by Lemma
3.7
, there exists a prime
\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}})
( by Proposition
2.12
). Here as
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}
is even, universally trivial, by using Proposition
2.12
again, we have
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}\mathfrak{l}_{1}})
. Continuing like this we get
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})
for some
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
as
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{1}^{\prime}
is again a universally trivial vertex.
∎

(ii) We now continue with the proof of Proposition
5.6
. Recall that
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
is of minimal length
u
, hence
M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}
is minimal by the previous lemma.
We show the second part of the proposition by induction on the number of prime factors of
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
by constructing a
surjective
path from
\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})=\operatorname{length}(M_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Let the number of prime factors of
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
be greater than one.

Case-Even:
Suppose
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is
even
. Then by Proposition
2.12
,

n(j)\in\mathcal{N}^{definite}_{j}
be such that
{\lambda}_{n(j)}
has nonzero image in
\Lambda/(\mathfrak{P}_{m},p^{k_{0}})
. Then
{\lambda}_{n(j)}
has nontrivial image in
\Lambda/(\mathfrak{P}_{m},p^{k_{0}})
for all
m\geq k_{0}
.
Define

C_{m}=\operatorname{coker}[\Lambda/\mathfrak{P}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}].

Note that
C_{m}
are finite and up to isomorphism do not depend on
m
. If
k_{1}
is large enough that
p^{k_{1}-k_{0}}
kills
C_{m}
, then we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{C_{m}[p^{k_{1}}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda/(\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}},p^{k_{1}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}}/p^{k_{1}}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{m}[p^{k_{0}}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda/(\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}},p^{k_{0}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}}/p^{k_{0}}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}}}.}

It follows that
\lambda_{n(j)}
has nontrivial image in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}/p^{k_{1}}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}}
, so

\pi^{\varepsilon}\lambda_{n(j)}
has non-trivial image in

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}/p^{k_{1}+\varepsilon}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}}
. By Theorem
5.14
,
\pi^{\varepsilon}\lambda_{n(j)}\in\mathfrak{m}^{u_{k_{1}+\varepsilon}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}/p^{k_{1}+\varepsilon}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}}
, and by the observation in equation (
3.1
), for
j\geq k+\varepsilon\geq k_{1}+\varepsilon
we have

\delta_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}(k+\varepsilon,j)\leq\operatorname{ind}({\pi}^{\varepsilon}\lambda_{n(j)},{\mathfrak{m}}^{u_{k+\varepsilon}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}/p^{k+\varepsilon}{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}})<2e(k_{1}+\varepsilon).

This implies
\delta_{\mathfrak{P}_{m}}(k)<2e(k_{1}+\varepsilon)
for all
k\geq k_{1}
and any
m\geq k_{0}
.
∎

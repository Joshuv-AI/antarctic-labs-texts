Abstract
A mixed graph G G is a graph that consists of both undirected and directed edges. An orientation of G G is formed by orienting all the undirected edges of G G , i.e., converting each undirected edge { u , v } \{u,v\} into a directed edge that is either ( u , v ) (u,v) or ( v , u ) (v,u) . The problem of finding an orientation of a mixed graph that makes it strongly connected is well understood and can be solved in linear time. Here we introduce the following orientation problem in mixed graphs. Given a mixed graph G G , we wish to compute its maximal sets of vertices C 1 , C 2 , … , C k C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{k} with the property that by removing any edge e e from G G (directed or undirected), there is an orientation R i R_{i} of G ∖ e G\setminus{e} such that all vertices in C i C_{i} are strongly connected in R i R_{i} . We discuss properties of those sets, and show how to solve this problem in linear time by reducing it to the computation of the 2 2 -edge twinless strongly connected components of a directed graph. A directed graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) is twinless strongly connected if it contains a strongly connected spanning subgraph without any pair of antiparallel (or twin ) edges. The twinless strongly connected components (TSCCs) of a directed graph G G are its maximal twinless strongly connected subgraphs. A 2 2 -edge twinless strongly connected component (2eTSCC) of G G is a maximal subset of vertices C C such that any two vertices u , v ∈ C u,v\in C are in the same twinless strongly connected component of G ∖ e G\setminus e , for any edge e e . These concepts are motivated by several diverse applications, such as the design of road and telecommunication networks, and the structural stability of buildings. Our algorithm is based on two notions: (i) a collection of auxiliary graphs ℋ \mathcal{H} that preserve the 2eTSCCs of G G and, for any H ∈ ℋ H\in\mathcal{H} , the strongly connected components of H H after the deletion of any edge have a very simple structure, and (ii) a reduction to the problem of computing the connected components of an undirected graph after the deletion of certain vertex-edge cuts.

The notions of twinless strong connectivity and mixed graph orientations are indeed related, and are motivated by several diverse applications, such as the design of road and telecommunication networks, the structural stability of buildings

Given a mixed graph

[

,

]

Raghavan

We can also observe that the computation of the twinless strongly connected components is equivalent to the following generalization of strong orientations of a mixed graph. Given a mixed graph
G
, we wish to compute its maximal sets of vertices

-edge strong orientations of mixed graphs in the following sense.
A mixed graph

More efficient algorithms exist for computing a

Concluding remarks

In this paper we studied the notion of edge-resilient strongly orientable blocks of a mixed graph

. Each such block

has the property that for any (directed or undirected) edge

C

We note that if we change in the definition of edge-resilient strongly orientable blocks the assumption that the edges that we allow to fail are both directed and undirected, and we demand that they are only directed, or only undirected, then we can easily modify Algorithm

so that we can compute those blocks in linear time (using again the reduction to computing

-edge twinless strongly connected components).

We may also introduce the similar concept of the

-edge strongly orientable blocks
of a mixed graph. These are the maximal sets of vertices

of

are

-edge strongly connected in

There are some relations between the

-edge strongly orientable blocks and the edge-resilient strongly orientable blocks.
First, we can easily see that every

Moreover, both these concepts coincide with the

-edge strongly connected components in directed graphs. However, Figure

shows that these concepts do not coincide in general.
Despite these connections, the efficient computation of the

-edge strongly orientable blocks seems to be an even more challenging problem than computing the edge-resilient strongly orientable blocks, since the former generalizes the notion of

-edge strong orientations of a mixed graph.

Finally, we note that our techniques may be useful for solving other connectivity problems in mixed graphs, such as related connectivity augmentation problems

x,y\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
.
∎

Now let
G_{s}
be a strongly connected digraph, and let
r
be either a marked vertex of
G_{s}
or
s
. We define the
auxiliary
graph
H(G_{s},r)
as follows. In
G_{s}
we shrink every
D(z)
, where
z
is a marked vertex such that
d(z)\in T(r)
into
z
. Also, if
r\neq s
, we shrink
D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
into
d(r)
. During those shrinkings we maintain all edges, except for self-loops; also, multiple edges are converted into double edges. The resulting graph is
H(G_{s},r)
. Notice that it consists of the subgraph of
G_{s}
induced by
T(r)
, plus some extra vertices and edges. To be specific, the vertex set of
H(G_{s},r)
consists of the vertices of
T(r)
, plus all marked vertices
z
of
G_{s}
such that
d(z)\in T(r)
, plus
d(r)
if
r\neq s
. The vertices of
T(r)
are called
ordinary
in
H(G_{s},r)
. The vertices of
H(G_{s},r)\setminus{T(r)}
are called
auxiliary
in
H(G_{s},r)
. In particular, if
r\neq s
,
d(r)
is called the
critical
vertex of
H(G_{s},r)
, and
(d(r),r)
is called the
critical
edge of
H(G_{s},r)
. (Thus,
H(G_{s},s)
is the only auxiliary graph of
G_{s}
that has no critical vertex and no critical edge.)

Now we will give a more precise description of the edges of
H(G_{s},r)
. First, let
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}
be the collection of all marked vertices of
G_{s}
such that
d(z_{i})\in T(r)
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then we can partition the vertex set of
G_{s}
into
T(r)
,
D(z_{1}),\dots,D(z_{k})
, and
D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
, if
r\neq s
. Now, due to Lemma
A.4
, an edge
(x,y)
of
G_{s}
satisfies one of:

(i)

x\in T(r)
and
y\in T(r)

(ii)

x\in T(r)
and
y\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
(assuming that
r\neq s
)

(iii)

x\in D(z)
and
y\in D(z)
, for some
z\in\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}

(iv)

x\in D(z)
and
y\in T(r)
, for some
z\in\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}

(v)

x\in D(z)
and
y\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
, for some
z\in\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}
(assuming that
r\neq s
)

(vi)

(x,y)=(d(z),z)
, for some
z\in\{z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\}

(vii)

x\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
and
y\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
(assuming that
r\neq s
)

(viii)

(x,y)=(d(r),r)
(assuming that
r\neq s
)

Each of those cases provides the
corresponding
edge of
H(G_{s},r)
as follows.
(i)\mapsto(x,y)
,
(ii)\mapsto(x,d(r))
,
(iii)\mapsto\mathit{none}
.
(iv)\mapsto(z,y)
.
(v)\mapsto(z,d(r))
.
(vi)\mapsto(d(z),z)
.
(vii)\mapsto\mathit{none}
.
(viii)\mapsto(d(r),r)
. For every edge
\tilde{e}
of
H(G_{s},r)
we say that an edge
e
of
G_{s}

corresponds
to
\tilde{e}
if
\tilde{e}
is the edge corresponding to
e
. (Notice that the correspondences
(i)
,
(vi)
and
(viii)
between edges of
G_{s}
and edges of
H(G_{s},r)
are bijective.) Using this correspondence, for every path
P
in
G_{s}
whose endpoints are vertices in
H(G_{s},r)
, there is a
corresponding
path
P_{H}
in
H(G_{s},r)
that has the same endpoints. The following Lemma summarizes the properties of the corresponding path, and establishes a kind of inverse to it.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
G_{s}
be a strongly connected digraph, and let
r
be either a marked vertex of
G_{s}
or
s
. Let
P
be a path in
G_{s}
whose endpoints lie in
H(G_{s},r)
. Then there is a corresponding path
P_{H}
in
H(G_{s},r)
with the same endpoints. In particular, every occurrence of an edge
(x,y)\in P
, such that either
(1)

x
and
y
are ordinary vertices in
H(G_{s},r)
, or
(2)
or
(x,y)=(d(z),z)
, where
z
is a marked vertex of
G_{s}
with
d(z)\in T(r)
, or
(3)

(x,y)=(d(r),r)
(if
r\neq s
), is maintained in
P_{H}
. Conversely, for every path
\tilde{P}
in
H(G_{s},r)
, there is a path
P
in
G_{s}
such that
P_{H}=\tilde{P}
.

Proof.

To construct
P_{H}
we simply apply the above correspondence, that maps edges of
G_{s}
to edges of
H(G_{s},r)
, to every edge of
P
. (Notice that some edges of
P
may be nullified.) It is a simple matter to verify that
P_{H}
is indeed a path, and it also satisfies the claimed property. Now, given a path
\tilde{P}
, we will construct a path
P
in
G_{s}
such that
P_{H}=\tilde{P}
. This is done essentially by maintaining some of the edges of
\tilde{P}
and by expanding some of its edges to paths of
G_{s}
. In particular, every edge
(x,y)
of
\tilde{P}
satisfying
(1)
,
(2)
, or
(3)
, is maintained in
P
. Now, suppose that
\tilde{P}
contains
(d(z),z)
such that
z
is a marked vertex of
G_{s}
with
d(z)\in T(r)
. Then, the successor of
(d(z),z)
in
\tilde{P}
, if it exists, is either an edge of the form
(z,y)
, where
y\in T(r)
, or
(z,d(r))
(assuming that
r\neq s
). In the first case, let
(x,y)
be an edge of
G_{s}
that corresponds to
(z,y)
in
H(G_{s},r)
. Then we have that
x
is a descendant of
z
in
D(G_{s})
, and so, by Lemma
A.5
, there is a path in
G_{s}
from
z
to
x
that uses only vertices from
D(z)
. Thus we replace
(z,y)
in
\tilde{P}
with precisely this path. In the second case, let
(x,y)
be an edge of
G_{s}
that corresponds to
(z,d(r))
in
H(G_{s},r)
. Then we have
x\in D(z)
and
y\in D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
. Now, by Lemma
A.5
, there is a path
P_{1}
from
z
to
x
that uses only vertices from
D(z)
. Furthermore, by the same Lemma, there is a path
P_{2}
from
y
to
d(r)
that uses only vertices from
D(s)\setminus{D(r)}
. Thus we replace
(z,d(r))
in
\tilde{P}
with
P_{1}+(x,y)+P_{2}
. Now suppose that
r\neq s
and
\tilde{P}
contains
(d(r),r)
. Then, the immediate predecessor of
(d(r),r)
in
\tilde{P}
, if it exists, is either an edge of the form
(x,d(r))
, where
x\in T(r)
, or
(z,d(r))
, where
z
in a marked vertex of
G_{s}
with
d(z)\in T(r)
. Now we can treat those cases as before, using Lemma
A.5
in order to expand the corresponding edges of
(x,d(r))
or
(z,d(r))
in
G_{s}
into paths of
G_{s}
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
A.6
is the following.

Corollary A.7
.

Let
G_{s}
be a strongly connected digraph, and let

be either a marked vertex of

or
s
. Then

is strongly connected.

Furthermore,
[
GILP16
]
provided the following two results.

Proposition A.8
.

Let
x,y
be two vertices of a strongly connected digraph
G_{s}
. Then
x\leftrightarrow_{2e}y
in
G_{s}
if and only if there is a vertex
r
(a marked vertex of
G_{s}
or

), such that

and

are both ordinary vertices of

and

.

Theorem A.9
.

Let

be a strongly connected digraph, and let

Now we will prove the analogous result to Proposition
A.8
for
2
-edge twinless strong connectivity.

Proposition A.10
.

Let
x,y
be two vertices of a strongly connected digraph
G_{s}
. Then
x\leftrightarrow_{2et}y
in
G_{s}
if and only if there is a vertex
r
(a marked vertex of
G_{s}
or
s
), such that
x
and
y
are both ordinary vertices in
H(G_{s},r)
and
x\leftrightarrow_{2et}y
in
H(G_{s},r)
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
x\leftrightarrow_{2et}y
in
G_{s}
. Then
x
and
y
are not separated by any bridge of
G_{s}
, and so they both belong to the same graph
H(G_{s},r)
, for some
r
(a marked vertex of
G_{s}
or
s
), as ordinary vertices. Now let
e=(u,v)
be an edge of
H(G_{s},r)
. We will prove that
x\leftrightarrow_{t}y
in
H(G_{s},r)\setminus{e}
. We distinguish five different cases for

.

(1)

u
and
v
are both ordinary vertices of
H(G_{s},r)
.

(2)

v
is an auxiliary vertex of
H(G_{s},r)
, but not
d(r)
(if
r\neq s
).

(3)

u
is an auxiliary vertex of
H(G_{s},r)
, but not
d(r)
(if
r\neq s
).

(4)

(u,v)=(d(r),r)
(if
r\neq s
).

(5)

v=d(r)
(if
r\neq s
).

We will now show how to handle cases
(1)
to
(5)
.

(1)
Let
u
and
v
be ordinary vertices of
H(G_{s},r)
. Then
e=(u,v)
is an edge of
G_{s}
. Since
x\leftrightarrow_{2et}y
in
G_{s}
, there exist two paths
P
and
Q
in
G_{s}\setminus{e}
, such that
P
starts from
x
and ends in
y
,
Q
starts from
y
and ends in
x
, and for every edge
(z,w)\in P
we have that
(w,z)\notin Q
. Now consider the induced paths
P_{H}
and
Q_{H}
of
P
and
Q
, respectively, in
H(G_{s},r)
. These have the property that
P_{H}
starts from
x
and ends in
y
, and
Q_{H}
starts from
y
and ends in
x
. Now we will show that there are subpaths
P^{\prime}_{H}
and
Q^{\prime}_{H}
of
P_{H}
and
Q_{H}
, respectively, that have the same endpoints as
P_{H}
and
Q_{H}
, respectively, and the property that for every
(z,w)\in P^{\prime}_{H}
we have that
(w,z)\notin Q^{\prime}_{H}
. We will construct
P^{\prime}_{H}
and
Q^{\prime}_{H}
by eliminating some segments of

and
Q_{H}
, respectively, that form loops. So let
(z,w)
be an edge of

with the property that
(w,z)\in Q_{H}
. Then we have that

and
w
cannot both be ordinary vertices of

(for otherwise we would have that
(z,w)\in P
and
(w,z)\in Q
). Thus, let us take first the case that
(z,w)=(d(h),h)
, where

is an auxiliary vertex of

. Then we have that
(h,d(h))\in Q_{H}
. But, in order for

to use the edge
(h,d(h))
, it must first pass from the bridge
(d(h),h)
. Thus we can simply discard the segment
(d(h),h),(h,d(h))
from

, as this contributes nothing for
Q_{H}
in order to reach
y
from
x
. Now let us take the case that
z
is an auxiliary vertex of

, but
(z,w)\neq(d(r),r)
(if

). If

we work as before. But this supposition exhausts this case, since

is an edge of

must necessarily be

. Now let us take the case that

). Then we have that

. But then, in order for

to end at an ordinary vertex, it must immediately afterwards use the bridge

. Thus we can eliminate the segment
(r,d(r)),(d(r),r)
from

. Finally, the case
(z,w)=(r,d(r))
is treated similarly as

. This shows that
x\leftrightarrow_{t}y
in
H(G_{s},r)\setminus{e}
.

In cases
(2)
and
(4)
, the challenge in proving

in

is the same as that in
(1)
, since

happens to be an edge of
G_{s}
. Thus, cases
(1)
,
(2)
and
(4)
have essentially the same proof. So we are left to consider cases
(3)
and
(5)
.

(3)
Let’s assume that
u
is an auxiliary vertex of
H(G_{s},r)
with

(if

). Then, since

in
G_{s}
, there exist two paths
P
and

in
G_{s}\setminus{(d(u),u)}
, such that
P
starts from
x
and ends in
y
,

starts from
y
and ends in

, and for every edge
(z,w)\in P
we have that
(w,z)\notin Q
. Now consider the induced paths

and

of

and

, respectively, in

. By Lemma

, these paths do not pass from the bridge
(d(u),u)
. Therefore, they also cannot use the edge
(u,v)
. Now we can reason as in the proof for case
(1)
, to show that there are subpaths

and

of

and

, respectively, with the same endpoints, respectively, and the property that for every

we have that

. This shows that

in
H(G_{s},r)\setminus{(x,y)}
.

(5)
Let’s assume that
r\neq s
and

. Then, since
x\leftrightarrow_{2et}y
in
G_{s}
, there exist two paths
P
and
Q
in
G_{s}\setminus{(d(r),r)}
, such that
P
starts from
x
and ends in
y
,
Q
starts from
y
and ends in
x
, and for every edge
(z,w)\in P
we have that
(w,z)\notin Q
. Now consider the induced paths
P_{H}
and
Q_{H}
of
P
and
Q
, respectively, in
H(G_{s},r)
. By Lemma
A.6
, these paths do not pass from the bridge
(d(r),r)
. Therefore, they also cannot use the edge
(v,u)
. Now we can reason as in the proof for case
(1)
, to show that there are subpaths

and

of
P_{H}
and

, respectively, with the same endpoints, respectively, and the property that for every

we have that

. This shows that

in

.

Now, since cases

to
(5)
exhaust all possibilities for

, we infer that

in

. Due to the generality of

in

.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that

in

. If there is no

and

. Then we may assume, without loss of generality, that there is an edge

cannot reach

. Now we distinguish three cases, depending on the location of the endpoints of

.

, but also that one endpoint of

of an auxiliary vertex

.
(3)

in

. Since

in

, this implies that every path from

to

cannot reach

in

. Any of those cases implies that

.

Now let’s assume that

in

. Then

. We will strengthen this by showing that there exists an edge

, it must also pass from the bridge

. But this means that

, and so we can replace the part of

.

Now let’s assume that

. Thus we can simply skip this part of

(u,v)\neq(d(r),r)
. Then, by Lemma

Now let

(x,y)
be a strong bridge of

. We will define the

-operation on

and
(x,y)
, which produces a set of digraphs as follows. Let

be the strongly connected components of

. Now let

. We will construct a graph

as follows. First, notice that either

and
y\in C
, or
y\notin C
and
x\in C
, or

. Then we set

, or
V(C^{\prime})=V(C)\cup\{y\}
, or

, respectively.
Now, every edge of
G
with both endpoints in

is included in

. Furthermore, for every edge
(u,v)
of

such that

and

, we add the edge

to

. Also, for every edge
(u,v)
of

and

to

. Finally, we also add the edge

to

. Now we define

.

Intuitively, we can describe the idea of the

-operation on

and
(x,y)
as follows. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the DAG of the strongly connected components of

. Then we have that
\mathcal{D}
has a single source that contains

, and a single sink that contains

. Now, for every node

of
\mathcal{D}
, we merge all nodes of
\mathcal{D}
that are reachable from

into

, and all nodes of
\mathcal{D}
that reach

into

. (We ignore all the other nodes of

.) We also reconnect this graph by adding the edge
(x,y)
. We perform this operation for every node of

, and thus we get the collection of graphs

.

Speaking intuitively again, the purpose of this operation is to maintain the connectivity information within every strongly connected component of

. In particular, we have the following correspondence between paths of

and paths in the graphs of

. Let

be two vertices of the same strongly connected component

of

and let

be a path in

from

to

. Then we construct the

of

as follows. First, every edge of

with both endpoints in

is maintained in

. If

lies entirely within

be a maximal segment of

. (Thus we have

and

.) Notice that this segment is unique, because if a path leaves

. Similarly, if

from

. The construction of the compressed path, together with a kind of converse to it, is summarized in the following.

Lemma A.11
.

Let
G
be a strongly connected digraph and let
(x,y)
be a strong bridge of

. Let

(2)

The first part was discussed above. For the converse, we construct the path

The following two lemmas are useful strengthenings of Lemma

Lemma A.13
.

Let
G
be a strongly connected digraph and let
(x,y)
be a strong bridge of
G
. Let
C
be a strongly connected component of
G\setminus(x,y)
, and let
C^{\prime}
be the corresponding digraph in
S(G,(x,y))
. Let
P^{\prime}
be a path in
C^{\prime}
with both endpoints in

. Then there is a path
P
in
G
with
\tilde{P}=P^{\prime}
such that for every pair of twin edges
(z,w)\in P
and
(w,z)\in P
we have that both
(z,w)
and
(w,z)
are in
C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.11
we have that there is a path
P
in
G
such that
\tilde{P}=P^{\prime}
. In particular, the endpoints of
P
lie in
C
. Now let
(z,w)
be an edge such that
(z,w)\in P
,
(w,z)\in P
, and
(z,w)\notin C^{\prime}
. (If no such edge exists, then we are done.) We will show that we can eliminate all those instances from

, and maintain the property that
\tilde{P}=P^{\prime}
. First let us suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
(x,y)\in\{(z,w),(w,z)\}
. Since
(z,w)\notin C^{\prime}
, we have
(z,w)\neq(x,y)
. Therefore,
(w,z)=(x,y)
and
(z,w)=(y,x)
. Since
(z,w)=(y,x)\notin C^{\prime}
, we have that neither
x
nor
y
is in
C
. Thus, since
P
starts from
C
, in order to reach
y
it must pass from
(x,y)
. Then, since it uses
(y,x)
, in order to reach
C
it must again use
(x,y)
. But this contradicts the fact that

may use an edge only once. This shows that
(x,y)\notin\{(z,w),(w,z)\}
.

Now, since
(x,y)\notin\{(z,w),(w,z)\}
, the existence of the edges
(z,w)
and
(w,z)
in
G
implies that

and

belong to the same strongly connected component

of

. Notice that

must be different from

, because otherwise we would have
(z,w)\in C
. Thus, due to the fact that

may use
(x,y)
only once, we have that

is accessed by

only once (because we need to pass from
(x,y)
in order to either reach

from

, or reach

from

). Now, if

first uses
(z,w)
and afterwards

, we have a circuit that starts with
(z,w)
and ends with
(w,z)
that can be eliminated from

. Similarly, if
P
first uses
(w,z)
and then
(z,w)
, we have a circuit that starts with
(w,z)
and ends with
(z,w)
that can be eliminated from

. In both cases,
\tilde{P}=P^{\prime}
is maintained.

Thus, after all those eliminations, we have that, if
(z,w)
and
(w,z)
are edges of

, then both of them must be in
C^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma A.14
.

be a strongly connected digraph and let
(x,y)
be a strong bridge of

. Let

be a strongly connected component of

, and let

be the corresponding digraph in

and

are in

.

By Lemma

we have that there is a path

in

with

such that for every pair of twin edges

and

(w,z)\in Q

(z,w)
and

are in

.

Now let us suppose that there is an edge
(z,w)\in P
, such that
(w,z)\in Q
, and at least one of
(z,w)
and
(w,z)
is not in

. (If no such edge exists, then we are done.) Let us suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that

, or

, nor

Lemma A.24

Now let

be two
\mathit{oo}

, and

to

, and a path

in

to

and

, then they are paths in
\widetilde{H}_{rr}
, and this implies that

are twinless strongly connected in

. Otherwise, at least one of

. If the outgoing edge of

in

is not

, then we still have that

and

are twinless strongly connected in

. Otherwise, since

are twinless strongly connected in
\widetilde{H}_{rr}\setminus{e}
. Now let

. Since

, we have that

are twinless strongly connected in

. This means that there is a path

to

, and a path

to

is the only incoming edge to

in

, this means that

do not pass from

. Also,

do not use the edge
(r,x)
. This means that

are twinless strongly connected in

. Thus we have

.

Conversely, suppose that

are not

. Then there is an edge

and a pair of antiparallel edges

. Let

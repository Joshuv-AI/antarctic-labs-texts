Abhishek Oswal, Ananth N. Shankar, Xinwen Zhu

Abstract

We prove every rigid-analytic map defined over a discretely valued p p -adic field 𝖣 × × X → S ​ ( G , ℋ ) 𝖪 an \mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}} extends to an analytic map 𝖣 × X → ( S ​ ( G , ℋ ) 𝖪 BB ) an \mathsf{D}\times X\rightarrow(S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{BB}})^{\mathrm{an}} , where S ​ ( G , ℋ ) 𝖪 BB S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{BB}} is the Baily-Borel compactification of S ​ ( G , ℋ ) 𝖪 S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}} . 1
Introduction

In 1972, Borel proved what is now known as the Borel extension property for Shimura varieties.

Theorem

(Borel, Theorem A of
[
Bor72
]
)
.

Let
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
be a Shimura variety with torsion-free level structure. Let
D
be the complex open unit disc, and let
D^{\times}=D\setminus\{0\}
be the punctured open unit disc. Let
a,b
be non-negative integers. Then, every holomorphic map
(D^{\times})^{a}\times D^{b}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{hol}}
extends to a holomorphic map
D^{a+b}\rightarrow(S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{BB}})^{\textrm{hol}}
, where
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{BB}}
denotes the Baily–Borel compactification of
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
.

By combining this extension property with resolution of singularities and GAGA, Borel proves the following important algebraicity theorem in the theory of Shimura varieties.

Theorem

(Borel, Theorem 3.10 of
[
Bor72
]
)
.

Let
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
be as above, and let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. Then, every holomorphic map
X^{\textrm{hol}}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{hol}}
is automatically an algebraic map.

The main purpose of this paper is to prove analogous results in the
p
-adic setting. To that end, let
F
denote a discretely valued
p
-adic field
1
1

1

The reader may assume that
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or of
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})[1/p]
.
. Let
\mathsf{D}
denote the rigid-analytic closed unit disc over
F
and set
\mathsf{D}^{\times}=\mathsf{D}\setminus\{0\}
. The main theorem of this paper is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
denote a Shimura variety of abelian type with torsion-free level structure defined over a number field contained in
F
. Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic variety over
F
. Then every rigid-analytic map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}
defined over
F
extends to an analytic map
\mathsf{D}\times X\rightarrow(S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\textrm{BB}}_{\mathsf{K}})^{\mathrm{an}}
, where
S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\textrm{BB}}_{\mathsf{K}}
is the Baily-Borel compactification of
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
.

Along the way, we also prove an extension theorem for Rapoport-Zink spaces
\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}^{\rig}
(see
Section 2.1
for a review) – a result interesting in its own right:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be as in
Theorem 1.1
. Then every rigid-analytic map
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}^{\rig}
defined over
F
extends analytically to a map
\mathsf{D}\times X\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}^{\rig}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that the extension property for maps out of
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X
as in the statement of
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
immediately implies the extension property for maps out of
(\mathsf{D}^{\times})^{a}\times\mathsf{D}^{b}
as in the statement of Borel’s theorem.
The inverse implication, however, is not automatic in non-archimedean situation. Namely, although
every boundary point
z\in\mathsf{D}\times X\setminus\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X
admits a neighbourhood

, such that
U\cap(\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X)\subset U
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times\mathsf{D}^{b}\subset\mathsf{D}^{1+b}
, these neighbourhoods (together with
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X
) need not give a(n admissible) cover of
\mathsf{D}\times X
. Nevertheless, we shall see in

that the inverse implication still holds when the target is quasi-projective.

Our main theorems have several corollaries. The first is the
p
-adic analogue of Borel’s algebraicity theorem.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
V/F
be an algebraic variety. Let
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
denote a Shimura variety of abelian type with torsion-free level structure. Then, every rigid-analytic map
V^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}

is algebraic.

The analytification
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}
, of the moduli space of polarized abelian varieties
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, solves the moduli problem of polarized abeloids (see
[
Con18
]
for this fact and the definition of abeloids). Therefore,
1.4
has the following consequence to the alegbraicity of polarized families of abeloids.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
V/F
be an algebraic variety. Then every family
B/V^{\mathrm{an}}
of polarized abeloids is the analytification of a polarized abelian scheme
A/V
.

For toroidal compactifications of Shimura varieties, the extension property for maps from
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X
as in
Theorem 1.1
cannot hold in general. But the valuative criterion of properness implies the following weaker statement still holds.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
be as above and let
S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\textrm{tor}}_{\mathsf{K}}
be some toroidal compactification. Then, every analytic map
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\mathrm{an}}
defined over
F
extends to an analytic map
\mathsf{D}\rightarrow(S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\textrm{tor}}_{\mathsf{K}})^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Consequently, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let

denote a family of polarized abeloids defined over
F
. Suppose that

is constant on

extends to a polarized family of semi-abeloids

is actually a family of abeloids.

and

also have similar implications to the algebraicity of analytic families of K3 surfaces over algebraic bases, and to degenerations of families of K3 surfaces – where at least one fiber has good reduction – over
\mathsf{D}^{\times}
.
We will prove all these corollaries in Section
6
.

1.1
Applications to
p
-adic hyperbolicity

Our main theorems also have applications to
p
-adic notions of hyperbolicity. In
[
Bru22
]
, Brunebarbe discusses different notions of hyperbolicity over the complex numbers and amongst other things, shows that Shimura varieties satisfy these notions. In the
p
-adic setting, there have been several attempts at formulating analogues of hyperbolicity (see for example
[
JV21
]
and
[
Mor21
]
). Consider the following definition of Brody hyperbolicity over a discretely valued field:

Definition 1.8
.

Let
S
denote an algebraic variety over the discretely valued
p
-adic field
F
. We say that
S
is
discretely Brody hyperbolic
if:

1.

For every abelian variety
A
defined over a finite extension
F^{\prime}
of
F
, every map
A\rightarrow S\times_{F}F^{\prime}
is constant.

2.

Every rigid-analytic map
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow(S\times_{F}F^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
defined over a finite extension
F^{\prime}
of
F
is constant.

1.4
implies that Shimura varieties of abelian type are discretely Brody hyperbolic! Indeed,
1.4
implies that any such map to a Shimura variety of abelian type
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathsf{K}}
must be algebraic. There are no non-constant algebraic maps from
\mathbb{G}_{m}
to a Shimura variety with torsion-free level structure (such a map would give a non-constant holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}^{\textrm{hol}}
, but it is known that there does not exist such map). In fact,
1.4
implies that every rigid-analytic map
U^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H}))_{\mathsf{K}}
defined over a finite extension of
F
must also be constant, where
U
is a smooth simply connected algebraic variety! Indeed, Deligne proves (in
[
Del71a
]
) that every algebraic map
U\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})_{\mathsf{K}}
from a smooth simply connected variety to a Shimura variety must be constant. The result in
loc. cit.
proves that any map from a smooth variety with finite fundamental group must be constant. This can be leveraged to obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
denote an algebraic group defined over the discretely valued non-archimedean field
F
. Then, every rigid-analytic map
\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow S(G,\mathcal{H})^{\mathrm{an}}_{\mathsf{K}}
defined over a finite extension of
F
is constant.

Deducing this corollary from
1.4
should be well known to experts. Nonetheless, we include a brief sketch in Section
6
for completeness.

Using work of Nadel
[
Nad89
, Theorem 0.1]
, our algebraization result
1.4
implies the following corollary:

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
N\geq\max\{28,g(g+1)/2\}
and let
\mathcal{A}_{g,1,N}
denote the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties with full level
N
structure. Then, there is no non-constant rigid-analytic morphism
C^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,1,N}^{\mathrm{an}}
defined over
F
, where
C/F
is a smooth algebraic curve whose (smooth) compactification has genus
\leq 1
.

1.2
Outline of proof

Our proof does not parallel Borel’s proof in the archimedean case. The proof of
Theorem 1.1
blends
p
-adic and
\ell
-adic methods together with techniques from rigid-analytic geometry. To ease exposition, we will stick to the case of
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is the moduli space of polarized abelian varieties with suitably fine level structure. We will remark on the setting of
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times X
when appropriate. We write
\mathsf{D}^{\times}=\cup_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{A}_{n}
, where
\mathsf{A}_{n}
is the closed annulus with outer radius 1 and inner radius
\frac{1}{p^{n}}
. The standing assumption will be that all spaces and maps are defined over the discretely valued field
F
. The outline goes as follows:

Reducing to the good reduction locus

Working backwards, one should expect that any map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}
should have the property that
f(\mathsf{D}^{\times}_{\epsilon})
is entirely contained inside the good-reduction locus, or entirely contained inside the bad reduction locus. Here,
\mathsf{D}_{\epsilon}\subset\mathsf{D}
is the closed unit disk of radius
\epsilon
, and
\mathsf{D}^{\times}_{\epsilon}=\mathsf{D}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathsf{D}^{\times}
. Using the
\ell
-adic monodromy of abelian varieties in conjunction with the interplay between formal schemes and rigid-analytic varieties, we in fact are able to prove that every
x\in f(\mathsf{D}^{\times})
has the same reduction type. It is striking that we do not have to shrink
\mathsf{D}^{\times}
! By analyzing the geometric structure of
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\textrm{BB}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\textrm{tor}}
around the boundary, we reduce to prove
Theorem 1.1
in the case when
f(\mathsf{D}^{\times})
is entirely contained in the good reduction locus
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\good}
of
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Lifting to Rapoport-Zink space

In the appendix, Patel proves that the special fiber of a formal model
\mathfrak{A}
of a closed rigid-analytic annulus must have a very specific form. Using this classification, we prove that the mod
p
special fiber of a formal abelian scheme defined over
\mathfrak{A}
is quasi-isogenous to some abelian variety defined over a finite field. Together with the fact that the map from a Rapoport-Zink space to
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a topological cover onto its image, we show that every map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}
whose image is contained in the good reduction locus lifts to a map
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}
! This reduces
Theorem 1.1
to
Theorem 1.2
, i.e. to proving that Rapoport-Zink spaces satisfy the Borel-extension property.

Extension for Rapoport-Zink spaces

We thus reduce
Theorem 1.1
to
Theorem 1.2
.
As above, our exposition will be in the setting of a map
\tilde{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}
. There are two steps in proving that
\tilde{f}
extends to an analytic map from
\mathsf{D}
, and both steps make crucial use of the family of (fiberwise crystalline) Galois representations paremeterized by Rapoport-Zink spaces. First, we use inputs related to the
p
-adic Riemann-Hilbert correspondence
[
DLLZ18
]
and Shimizu’s work
[
Shi22
]
on
p
-adic local systems to prove that the family of (fiberwise crystalline) Galois representations on
\mathsf{D}^{\times}
extends to a family of crystalline Galois representations on
\mathsf{D}
. The second step involves a careful analysis of the period map
\check{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
where
\mathcal{F}
is an appropriate flag variety. We use Hartl’s description of the image of the period map to prove that the map
\check{\pi}\circ\tilde{f}
extends to a rigid-analytic map
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
with
\textrm{Im}(g)\subset\textrm{Im}(\check{\pi})
. Finally, we use De Jong’s analysis of
\check{\pi}
in
[
dJ95
]
to extend
\tilde{f}
.

1.3
Prior work

In
[
Che02
]
, Cherry proves that for a smooth, projective algebraic curve over
\mathbb{C}_{p}
of genus at least 2, any rigid-analytic map
\mathsf{D}^{\times}\rightarrow C^{\mathrm{an}}
must extend analytically to a map
\mathsf{D}\rightarrow C^{\mathrm{an}}
. Cherry and Ru in
[

]
prove a non-archimedean big Picard style result, using which Sun, in

Organization of the paper

Preliminaries and reduction to
\mathcal{A}_{g}

2.1
Moduli of abelian varieties and
p
-divisible groups

Let
(V,\psi)
denote the unique non-degenerate symplectic
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with dimension
2g
. Let
\GSp
denote the group of symplectic similitudes over
\mathbb{Q}
defined by
(V,\psi)
. Fix a lattice
L\subset V\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which satisfies
L\subset L^{\vee}\subset\frac{1}{p}L
, where
L^{\vee}\subset V\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denotes the lattice dual to
L
under
\psi
. The subset of
\GSp(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
stabilizing the lattice chain
L\subset L^{\vee}
is a maximal parahoric subgroup of
\GSp(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and every maximal parahoric arises this way (see
[
RZ96
, Chapter 3]
). We note that every compact open subgroup of
\GSp(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is contained in a maximal parahoric subgroup. Recall that
\GSp
admits a natural Shimura data. For
\mathsf{K}\subset\GSp(\mathbb{A}_{f})
a compact open subgroup, we let
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
denote the associated Shimura variety viewed over
\mathbb{Q}
with level structure defined by
\mathsf{K}
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
can be identified with the moduli space of
g
-dimensional polarized abelian varieties. Whether or not the moduli problem is fine and indeed the precise moduli problem itself depends on the choice of level structure. We will now define various notions associated to level structure.

Let
\mathsf{K}_{p}=\mathsf{K}\cap\GSp(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and let
(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}})_{p}
denote a maximal parahoric subgroup containing
\mathsf{K}_{p}
.

Definition 2.1
.

1.

We say that the level structure is
maximal parahoric at
p
if
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{K}^{p}\mathsf{K}_{p}
with
\mathsf{K}_{p}=(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}})_{p}
for some maximal parahoric subgroup, where
\mathsf{K}^{p}
is the prime-to-
p
part of
\mathsf{K}
. We denote the Shimura variety by
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
if the level structure is maximal parahoric at
p
.

2.

Let
\ell
be a prime. We say
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
has full level-
\ell
structure if
\mathsf{K}_{\ell}
is contained in the principal congruence subgroup at
\ell
.

Suppose now that
\ell\neq p
is an odd prime, and that
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
has full level structure at
\ell
. Then, the level structure is torsion free and
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
is a fine moduli space. We let
\underline{\mathscr{A}}_{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
denote the universal polarized abelian scheme over
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
. For brevity we will refer to the universal family over
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
as
\underline{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
for all choices of level structure as above. The family
\underline{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
admits a canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-model, which we also denote by
\underline{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
. The abelian scheme
\underline{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
is a polarized abelian scheme with prime-to-
p
level structure, such that the
p
-power part of the kernel of the polarization is a
p
-torsion subgroup.

Definition 2.2
.

We let
\Tate/\mathcal{A}_{g}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the relative
p
-adic étale cohomology and
\Vdr/\mathcal{A}_{g}
denote the relative de Rham cohomology with Gauss-Manin connection of the universal abelian scheme over
\mathcal{A}_{g}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

We recall the definition of Rapoport-Zink spaces, which are local analogues of moduli of abelian varieties.
Let
\mathrm{Nilp}_{W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})}
denote the category of
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
-algebras in which
p
is nilpotent. We fix
L\subset L^{\vee}\subset\frac{1}{p}L\subset V\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as before.

We fix a pair
\mathbb{X}=(\mathscr{G}_{0},\lambda_{0})
where
\mathscr{G}_{0}
is a
p
-divisible group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
\lambda_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{G}_{0}\to\mathscr{G}_{0}^{\vee}
is a polarization with
\ker(\lambda_{0})\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{0}[p]
and the degree of
\deg\lambda_{0}
equals the index of
L
in

.

Definition 2.3
.

We let

\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathbb{X}}
be the functor from
\mathrm{Nilp}_{W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})}
to sets, such that
R\mapsto\{(\mathscr{G},\lambda,\rho)\}/\sim
where
(\mathscr{G},\lambda)
is a polarized

-divisible group over

, satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{G}_{0}\times S_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}\times_{S}S_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
is a quasi-isogeny of

-divisible groups.

2.

\ker(\lambda)\subseteq\mathscr{G}_{0}[p]
.

3.

The degree of the polarization

equals the index of
L

.

4.

The polarizations are compatible under pullback up to a scalar, i.e.
\rho^{*}(\lambda)=c\lambda_{x}

Two data

whose mod

which is realized as the quotient of a connected component of an appropriate Shimura variety of Hodge type (for example, see
[
Kis10
, Page 970]
) by a finite group that acts freely (and at the cost of replacing
\mathsf{K}_{G_{1}}
by a finite-index subgroup we may assume that the Hodge type Shimura variety has torsion-free level). Therefore, the extension theorem for this connected component of

reduces to the case of Hodge type by again applying

by étale correspondences. Therefore, the case of abelian type Shimura varieties reduces to the case of
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
with torsion-free level structure
\mathsf{K}
.

By another application of
2.9
, we may assume that
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
has full level-
\ell
structure. Finally, let
\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}=\mathsf{K}^{p}(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}})_{p}
with
\mathsf{K}^{p}
as above, and
(\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}})_{p}
some maximal parahoric subgroup containing

. The level structure of
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}
is still torsion-free (because of the condition imposed on
\ell
), and the map

is a finite étale map. Therefore,

reduces to the case of
\mathcal{A}_{g,{\mathsf{K}_{\mathrm{par}}}}

∎

3
The bad reduction locus

Let
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\good}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that for every

, every rigid-analytic map

\mathsf{D}^{1+b}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\good}

is true.

3.1
Constancy of reduction type

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A/F
be an abelian variety with semistable reduction. The abelian rank of
A
is defined to be the dimension of the maximal abelian quotient of the the identity component of the special fiber of the Néron model of
A
over the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of
F
.

We prove the following result in this subsection.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times\mathsf{D}^{b}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}
denote a rigid-analytic map defined over
F
. Then, the abelian rank of
x^{*}\underline{\mathscr{A}}
is constant for all classical (rigid) points
x\in\mathsf{D}^{\times}\times\mathsf{D}^{b}
.
In particular, the image of
f
is either entirely contained in the good-reduction locus, or entirely contained in the bad-reduction locus.

This result easily reduces to the one-dimensional case, that is to the case where
b=0
. Furthermore, we can reduce further to the case where the source of the rigid-analytic map
f
is a closed rigid-analytic annulus
\mathsf{A}
defined over
F
. The above result then follows immediately from
Theorem 3.4
below.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathsf{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{an}}
be a rigid-analytic map defined over
F
. Then, the abelian rank of
x^{*}\underline{\mathscr{A}}
is constant for all classical points
x\in\mathsf{A}(L)
for all finite extensions
L
of
F
.
In particular, the image of
f
is either entirely contained in the good reduction locus, or entirely contained in the bad reduction locus.

Before proving of these results, we will first describe the Baily-Borel compactification
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
. The boundary,
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
, has a natural stratification, and each stratum is canonically isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{g^{\prime}\mathsf{K}^{\prime}}
, for
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
. The level structure
\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
inherits our initial assumptions on the level structure on
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}
(namely, maximal parahoric at
p
, and full level structure at an odd prime
\ell\neq p
). For brevity, we denote this stratum by
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}(g^{\prime})
. We also have that each
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}(g^{\prime})
is locally closed in
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}
, its closure is the union of strata
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}(g^{\prime\prime})
for each

. The above stratification exists integrally over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Finally, the abelian rank of the mod
\pi
reduction of an abelian variety over

can be easily read off using the mod
p
Baily-Borel compactification. Indeed, let
x\in\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}(F)
denote a point which specializes a point in
\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}^{\mathrm{BB}}(g^{\prime})(\mathbb{F})
. Then, the reduction of the abelian variety parameterized by

has abelian rank
g^{\prime}
. We make the following definition for brevity:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{A}_{g,\mathsf{K}}(F)
be a point, and let

denote the abelian variety parameterized by

. We define the abelian rank of

to be the abelian rank of the mod

reduction of

.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathsf{A}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\rig}

denote the thin annulus over
F
. Let
\mathbb{L}
be an étale
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-local system on
\mathsf{A}_{0}
for a prime

. Suppose that there exists a classical
F
-point

such that the Galois representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{F}
induced by

is unramified. Then
\mathbb{L}
is unramified everywhere; i.e.
given any finite extension

of

and any classical

-point

, the
\ell
-adic Galois representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{L}
induced by
\mathbb{L}_{\bar{y}}
is unramified.

Firstly, in order to prove

we may assume that

, the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
inside
\overline{F}
.

Any
\ell
-adic local system on

can be pulled back to give a local system on
\mathsf{A}_{0}
. The main step in the proof of

is the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathbb{L}
denote an étale
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-local system on
\mathsf{A}_{0}
with prime-to-
p
monodromy. Suppose there exists a classical point

such that
\mathbb{L}_{x}
is trivial. Then,
\mathbb{L}
is pulled back from a local system on
\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

The proofs of
3.6
and
3.7
will be entirely

-adic, so we first start by recalling some results about the prime-to-
p
étale fundamental groups of thin annuli. Let

denote the maximal prime-to-

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x\in\mathsf{A}_{0}(F)
be a classical point. Let

also denote the induced algebraic point of the variety
{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{F}
. The canonical map
\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathsf{A}_{0},\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{F},\bar{x})

Proof.

The canonical map
\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathsf{A}_{0},\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{F},\bar{x})
factors as

\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathsf{A}_{0},\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{an}},\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{1}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{F},\bar{x}).

It suffices to prove that both the above maps are isomorphisms. By
[

, Theorem 3.1]
, we have that every finite étale cover of

is uniquely algebraizable, and therefore the second map is an isomorphism. It suffices to prove that the first is an isomorphism.

By

, Theorem 2.11 (b)]
, the geometric prime-to-

étale covers of
\mathsf{A}_{0}
are given by adjoining prime-to-
p
roots of

. This is true for

, and therefore also for

(again by

, Theorem 3.1]
).
Therefore, the canonical map
\pi^{\prime}_{1,\geom}(\mathsf{A}_{0},\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{1,\geom}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{an}},\bar{x})
is an isomorphism. As the arithmetic-geometric exact sequences of fundamental groups are functorial for maps between rigid-analytic varieties, the map
\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathsf{A}_{0},\bar{x})\simeq\pi^{\prime}_{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{an}},\bar{x})

∎

We are now ready to prove

and

3.7
.

By

,

is induced by a local system on

(which we shall also denote by

). In order to prove that

is induced by a

Proof of

By

, we may work in the setting of

is constant for a point

Proof of

We will again assume that

A/F
denote an abelian variety with semistable reduction. By

Let

Remark 3.9

Lutkebohmert also proves that the prime-to-

Throughout this appendix, let

-scheme

The purpose of this appendix is to prove

of a
\overline{k}
-curve

Definition A.1
.

The

of a
\overline{k}
-curve

is the graph whose vertices are those points of

lying on at least
2
irreducible components, with one edge connecting distinct vertices
p
and
q
for each irreducible component of

p
and

.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
X/R
is an integral, regular
R
-scheme proper and flat over
\Spec R
, and suppose
X_{F}
is a smooth genus
0
curve with
H^{0}(X_{F},\mathcal{O}_{X_{F}})=F
. Then the curve
(X_{\overline{k}})_{\red}
is connected, its irreducible components are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{k}}
, and its intersection graph is a tree.

Proof.

Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
denote the irreducible components (with reduced scheme structure) of the Cartier divisor
X_{k}\subset X
, and for each
i=1,\dots,n
let
m_{i}
denote the multiplicity of
C_{i}
in
X_{k}
. As
X
is regular, each
C_{i}
is also a Cartier divisor in
X
. Setting
d=\gcd(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
, we let
D\subset X
denote the Cartier divisor
\sum_{i}(\frac{m_{i}}{d})C_{i}
. By
[
Sta18
,
Tag 0C69
]
, we get:

\displaystyle 1=d[\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k](1-g_{D}),

where
\kappa=H^{0}(D,\mathcal{O}_{D})
, and
g_{D}=\dim H^{1}(D,\mathcal{O}_{D})
. Thus
g_{D}=0
. As
\dim X_{k}=1
, it follows that the map

H^{1}(D,\mathcal{O}_{D})\to H^{1}((X_{k})_{\red},\mathcal{O}_{(X_{k})_{\red}})

induced by the natural quotient map
\mathcal{O}_{D}\to\mathcal{O}_{(X_{k})_{\red}}
is surjective, and hence

H^{1}\left((X_{k})_{\red},\mathcal{O}_{(X_{k})_{\red}}\right)=0.

Flat base change then gives

\displaystyle H^{1}\left((X_{\overline{k}})_{\red},\mathcal{O}_{(X_{\overline{k}})_{\red}}\right)=0.

(3)

For the rest of the proof, let
\Gamma
denote the reduced, proper
\overline{k}
-curve
\left(X_{\overline{k}}\right)_{\red}
. By
[
Sta18
,
Tag 0AY8
]
we see that
\Gamma
is connected. If
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
is any sub-curve of
\Gamma
(meaning a
1
-dimensional closed subscheme), then the map

H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma})\to H^{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma^{\prime}})

induced by the natural quotient map of sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is surjective (using the long exact sequence and that
\Gamma
is
1
-dimensional) and therefore (
3
) implies
H^{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma^{\prime}})=0
. An easy argument using the finiteness of normalization shows that if
E
is an integral and proper curve over an algebraically closed field, then the vanishing of

implies

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Applying this fact to each irreducible component of
\Gamma
it follows that every irreducible component of
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{k}}
.

Finally, for sake of contradiction, suppose
\Gamma_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
,

are irreducible components of
\Gamma
such that there exist points
p_{j}\in\Gamma_{j}\cap\Gamma_{j+1}
for all

with the further property that
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
whenever

. (This is negating the property that the intersection graph is a tree.) Let

Z=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma_{j}\subset\Gamma,

and let

Z^{\prime}=\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma_{j}

denote the normalization of
Z
with normalization map
\eta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Z^{\prime}\to Z
. From the previous paragraph, we know that
H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})=0
. Consider the short exact sequence of sheaves

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to\eta_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{C}\to 0

(4)

where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the cokernel, supported on the set
S\subset\Gamma
of intersections among the
\Gamma_{j}
’s, of the map
\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to\eta_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}
. As
H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})=0
, the long exact sequence implies that the induced map on global sections

\displaystyle H^{0}(Z,\eta_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})\to H^{0}(Z,\mathcal{C})

(5)

and hence is
m

\mathcal{C}
is supported on

Abstract
This letter provides query-age-optimal joint sampling and transmission scheduling policies for a heterogeneous status update system, consisting of a stochastic arrival and a generate-at-will source, with an unreliable channel. Our main goal is to minimize the average query age of information (QAoI) subject to average sampling, average transmission, and per-slot transmission constraints. To this end, an optimization problem is formulated and solved by casting it into a linear program. We also provide a low-complexity near-optimal policy using the notion of weakly-coupled constrained Markov decision processes. The numerical results show up to 32 % 32\% performance improvement by the proposed policies compared with a benchmark policy.

System Model and Problem Formulation

We consider a heterogeneous status update system consisting of two independent sources:
one random arrival (Source 1) and one generate-at-will (Source 2), a buffer-aided transmitter, and a monitor, as depicted in Fig.

A discrete-time system with unit time slots

The sources are indexed

The status update packets of Source 1 are generated according to the Bernoulli distribution with parameter

To maintain the freshest information currently available at the transmitter,

Notice that considering a buffer of size one packet for each source is sufficient in our system, because storing and transmitting the outdated packets does not improve the AoI.

In this section, we develop a low-complexity policy to the main problem (

We relax the per-slot constraint (

\bar{\lambda}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\{\textstyle\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(t)\}\leq 1

This constraint then becomes inactive due to constraint (

Then, the main problem (
3

can be cast as a weakly coupled CMDP problem.
This is because the transition probabilities, the action space, and the immediate cost functions become independent among the sources;
now, the average transmission constraint (
3b

To proceed, we define the state, action, transition probabilities, and cost functions associated with each source
i
. The state of source
i
in slot
t
is
{s_{i}(t)\triangleq\big(r_{i}(t),\theta_{i}(t),\delta_{i}(t)\big)}
, with state space
\mathcal{S}_{i}
, which is a finite set.
The action of source
i
in slot
t
is denoted by
a_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
with action spaces
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{0,1\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\{0,2_{\mathrm{r}},2_{\mathrm{n}}\}
.
The state transition probabilities of source
i
, denoted
as
{\mathcal{\tilde{P}}_{s_{i}s_{i}^{\prime}}^{i}(a),\penalty\ \forall\,s_{i},\,s_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}
,
is given by

\Pr\{r_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,r_{i}\}\Pr\{\mathbb{\underline{s}}_{i}^{\prime}\,\big|\,\mathbb{\underline{s}}_{i},a\}
, where
\Pr\{r_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,r_{i}\}
and
\Pr\{\mathbb{\underline{s}}_{i}^{\prime}\,\big|\,\mathbb{\underline{s}}_{i},a\}
were defined in
(
18
) and (
4
). The QAoI cost of source
i
is
{c_{i}({s}_{i}(t))=r_{i}(t)\delta_{i}(t)}
. The transmission cost of source
i
is
{d_{i}^{\mathrm{tr}}(a_{i}(t))=\mathds{1}_{\{a_{i}(t)\neq 0\}}}
. The sampling cost of Source 2 is
{d^{\mathrm{sm}}(a_{2}(t))=\mathds{1}_{\{a_{2}(t)=2_{n}\}}}
.

First, for a presentation simplicity, we remove the subscript
i
from
s_{i}(t)
and
a_{i}(t)
.
Then, for each source
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
x_{i}(s,a)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}_{i}||\mathcal{A}_{i}|},\penalty\ \forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}_{i},\penalty\ \forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}_{i},
be defined as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{i}(s,a)&\triangleq\bar{\lambda}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{t-1}\Pr_{i}^{\eta_{i}}{\{s(t)=s,\,a(t)=a\}},\end{array}

where
\Pr_{i}^{\eta_{i}}{\{s(t)=s,\,a(t)=a\}}
denotes the probability of taking action
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
at state
s\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
in slot
t
given the initial distribution
\eta_{i}
.
Then, the corresponding LP of the weakly coupled CMDP problem is given by

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbb{X}_{i}}{\mbox{minimize}}\penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i}\textstyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}x_{i}(s,a)c_{i}(s)

(24a)

subject to

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i}\textstyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}x_{i}(s,a)d_{i}^{\mathrm{tr}}(a)\leq\Gamma^{\mathrm{tr}},

(24b)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}x_{2}(s,a)d^{\mathrm{sm}}(a)\leq\Gamma^{\mathrm{sm}},

(24c)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}x_{i}(s^{\prime},a)\big(\mathds{1}_{\{s=s^{\prime}\}}-\lambda\mathcal{\tilde{P}}^{i}_{s^{\prime}s}(a)\big)

\displaystyle=\bar{\lambda}\eta_{i}(s),\forall\,i,\penalty\ \forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}_{i},

(24d)

\displaystyle x_{i}(s,a)\geq 0,\forall\,i,\penalty\ \forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}_{i},\penalty\ \forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}_{i},

(24e)

with variables
\mathbb{X}_{i}=[x_{i}(s,a)],\penalty\ i\in\{1,2\}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{i}^{*}=[x_{i}^{*}(s,a)]
be a solution of the LP (
24
).
By results of
[

]
,
an optimal stationary randomized policy for each source
i
,
\pi^{*}_{i}\triangleq\{f_{i}(s,a)\}
, is given by

\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{i}(s,a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{i}^{*}(s,a)/\bar{x}_{i,s},&\text{if}\penalty\ \bar{x}_{i,s}>0,\\
\text{arbitrary},&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(25)

where
{\bar{x}_{i,s}=\textstyle\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}x_{i}^{*}(s,a^{\prime})}
and
{f_{i}(s,a)}
,
{\forall\,s\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}
,
{\forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}
, is the probability that an action
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
is chosen at state
s\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
for source

.

Notably, the per-source policies
\{\pi_{i}^{*}\}
construct
a
lower-bound

3
);
this lower bound is tight as empirically shown in Fig.

Having constructed the above weakly coupled CMDP and derived its solution,
we now propose a near-optimal low-complexity policy for the main problem (
3
)
that operates as follows:
(i) it determines an action of each source
i
in each slot
t
according to
\pi^{*}_{i}
, and
(ii) having the actions determined, if there is more than one packet to be transmitted,
the packet of source
\underline{i}
will be transmitted, where

{\underline{i}=\argmax_{i\in\{1,2\}}\{h_{i}(t)\}}
in which

h_{1}(t)
and
h_{2}(t)
are given as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{1}(t)\triangleq r_{1}(t)(\delta_{1}(t)-\theta_{1}(t)),\penalty\ h_{2}(t)\triangleq r_{2}(t)\delta_{2}(t).\end{array}

We term the above Step (ii) as a
dynamic truncation algorithm
, which still couples the sources.

The main idea of the truncation algorithm is to transmit the packet that is more fresh from the monitor’s perspective, as it has greater potential to decrease the AoI at the monitor.
To capture such relative freshness, we have used the AoI difference between the monitor and the transmitter for Source 1, and the AoI at the monitor itself for Source 2.

Complexity analysis:

The main complexity of finding the low-complexity policy comes from solving the LP
(
24
), which is (only)
linear
in the number of sources.
This is because
its number of variables and constraints vary with the
state space size
of the weakly-coupled CMDP problem, which
grows linearly with the number of sources.

Thus, the proposed policy has low complexity,
and as numerically shown in the next section, obtains
near-optimal performance.

VI
Conclusion

We provided a query-age-optimal joint sampling and scheduling policy for a heterogeneous status update system under average sampling, average transmission, and per-slot transmission constraints.
To this end, a CMDP problem with two average constraints was cast and solved via its equivalent LP.
We also provided a low-complexity policy by casting a weakly-coupled CMDP problem and solving it via its equivalent LP.
Numerical results showed the effectiveness of
the proposed policies, revealing that age-optimal sampling and scheduling is crucial for
resource-constrained status update systems, where
a greedy-based policy is inefficient.
Moreover, the near-optimal performance of the proposed low-complexity policy substantiates that the LP approach provides a viable solution for practical systems having many sources/sensors.

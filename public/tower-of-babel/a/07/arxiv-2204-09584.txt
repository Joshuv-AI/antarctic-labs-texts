Abstract
. We describe an equivalent formulation of algebraic weak factorisation systems, not involving monads and comonads, but involving double categories of morphisms equipped with a lifting operation satisfying lifting and factorisation axioms.

Weak factorisation systems were first considered by Quillen

As understood by Quillen from the outset, under mild completeness conditions such a notion of homotopy is intrinsically encoded within the weak factorisation system itself.

Dropping uniqueness in orthogonality has, however, some drawbacks. One loses, for example, the functoriality of factorisations. This is at odds with the vast majority of examples of weak factorisation systems, which can be made functorial by Quillen’s small object argument. 2.1.
Double categories of maps

In the context of classical orthogonal factorisation systems, we have two classes of morphisms

Examples 2

Definition 3
.

A lifting structure
(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},\varphi,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
is

•

a pre-awfs if it satisfies the axiom of lifting;

•

a lifting awfs if it satisfies the axioms of lifting and factorisation.

The definition of a pre-awfs is motivated by the notion of a pre-factorisation system
[
8
]
, which consists of two classes of maps
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
{\mathcal{E}}={}^{\perp}{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{E}}^{\perp}
. We remark that the usual definition of awfs, presented in Section
3.1
, does not easily lead to a definition of pre-awfs, since it has the factorisations built in as primitive structure.

Lifting awfs are the central notion of this paper, and we will prove their equivalence with awfs in Theorem
14
. We note that, in fact, there is some redundancy in the definition of a lifting awfs. Indeed it suffices to ask that the factorisation has
either
universal property — the other one then following automatically. See Proposition
17
.

Examples 6
.

In practice, the most common examples of lifting structures (resp. lifting awfs) are the
cofibrantly generated ones
, which combine the two cases of Examples
5
. These are the lifting structures (resp. lifting awfs) of the form
(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}({\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))},can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
for
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}
a

{\mathcal{C}}

For instance, let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Quillen model category and

{\mathcal{C}}
. We can view

as a double category
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}
over
\mathbb{S}\mathbf{q}({\mathcal{C}})
with one vertical morphism for each member of

, and only identity horizontal morphisms and squares. Then the double category of right maps
\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})
consists of the
algebraic trivial fibrations

[
20
]
— that is, morphisms of
{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with a choice of lifting against each generating cofibration of

.

There are strong results about when such lifting structures are lifting awfs — for instance, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is locally presentable and
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}
a small double category over
{\mathcal{C}}
, then
(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}({\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))},can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
is always a lifting awfs. See Proposition
18
of Section
4
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
combinatorial model category
— that is, locally presentable and cofibrantly generated — this is the case. We refer the reader to
[
20
]
for further homotopical examples of this kind, expressed as awfs rather than as lifting awfs (of course our main result Theorem

will establish the equivalence of the two notions.)

Let us remark that in such cofibrantly generated settings, it is quite rare that one has a complete understanding of the double category
\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
of left maps. One knows that the left maps include
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}
via the counit
\epsilon_{\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}}\colon\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}\to\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
and that they inherit various constructions from
{\mathcal{C}}
, such as coproducts and pushout along a map, but often we only have partial information of this nature. Obtaining a more complete understanding of
\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
in terms of

would be useful, with a starting point being Athorne’s

— see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.

We define the categories of pre-awfs and lifting awfs on
{\mathcal{C}}
as the full subcategories
{\mathbf{PAWFS}({\mathcal{C}})}
and
{\mathbf{LAWFS}({\mathcal{C}})}
of
{\mathbf{LIFT}({\mathcal{C}})}
containing the pre-awfs and lifting awfs respectively. Accordingly, we have inclusions of full subcategories

and
{\mathbf{PAWFS}({\mathcal{C}})}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{LIFT}({\mathcal{C}})}
. We will frequently use that being a pre-awfs or lifting awfs is an

of lifting structures. In other words,
{\mathbf{LAWFS}({\mathcal{C}})}
and

.

Observe that a lifting structure
(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},\varphi,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
is a pre-awfs if and only if both

and

The forgetful functors (

and
\mathbf{Right}\mathsf{}

\mathbf{Left}\mathsf{}
and
\mathbf{Right}\mathsf{}

(\varphi^{\prime}_{l},1)\colon(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},\varphi^{\prime},\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})\to(\mathbf{LLP}({\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})},can,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
is an isomorphism of lifting structures; hence
(\mathbf{LLP}({\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})},can,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
is a lifting awfs too.

The final claim about essential uniqueness follows from the fully faithfulness of
\mathbf{Right}\mathsf{}
, established in Proposition
11
.
∎

The dual conditions characterise the essential image of
\mathbf{Left}\mathsf{\colon}{\mathbf{LAWFS}({\mathcal{C}})}\to{\mathbf{DBL}}/\mathbb{S}\mathbf{q}({\mathcal{C}})
. We also give the lifting awfs variant of Proposition
13
and again there is a corresponding dual version.

Proposition 16
.

Consider a double category
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}
over
\mathbb{S}\mathbf{q}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then the lifting structure
(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})),can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
is a lifting awfs if and only if
V_{1}\colon\mathbf{RLP}{(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathbf{2}}}
has a left adjoint.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
13
, if
V_{1}
has a left adjoint then
V\colon\mathbf{RLP}{(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})}\to\mathbb{S}\mathbf{q}({\mathcal{C}})
satisfies the conditions of Proposition
15
, proving the result.
∎

Proposition
16
implies the slight redundancy in the axiom of factorisation.

Proposition 17
.

A lifting awfs is a pre-awfs such that each morphism
f
in
{\mathcal{C}}
admits a factorisation
f=U_{1}g\circ V_{1}h
such that either
(1_{A},V_{1}f)\colon U_{1}g\to f
is
U_{1}
-couniversal
or

(U_{1}g,1_{B})\colon f\to V_{1}h
is
V_{1}
-universal.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case that the factorisations are
V_{1}
-universal — the other case is dual. So let
(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},\varphi,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
be a pre-awfs admitting factorisations
f=U_{1}g\circ V_{1}h
such that
(U_{1}g,1_{B})\colon f\to V_{1}h
is
V_{1}
-universal. As a pre-awfs, it follows by Remark
7
that the induced map
(1,\varphi_{r})\colon(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))\to(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},\varphi,\mathbf{\mathbb{R}})
is invertible, so the lhs is also a pre-awfs. By Remark
7
again,
(\epsilon_{\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}},1)\colon(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}},can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))\to(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})),can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
is also invertible. Therefore it suffices to prove that
(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})),can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
is a lifting awfs. Now
V_{1}\colon{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathbf{2}}}
has a left adjoint by assumption, hence so too does the isomorphic
V_{1}\colon\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathbf{2}}}
so that
(\mathbf{LLP}(\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}})),can,\mathbf{RLP}(\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}))
is a lifting awfs by Proposition
16
.
∎

Proposition 18

Suppose that
{\mathcal{C}}
is locally presentable and
\mathbf{\mathbb{L}}

exactly as in the proof of Proposition 23 of
[
4
]
.
∎

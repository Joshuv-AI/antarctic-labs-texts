I
Introduction

Charmless hadronic
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
decays provide a rich ground for studying the mechanisms
of
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
meson decays and the phenomenon of CP violation.
In particular, the decay
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
is a rare charmless hadronic

\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
decay, mediated via hadronic penguin diagram, which is particularly
sensitive to new physics in the hadronic loop.
The measurements of CP violation parameters using time dependent CP violation
techniques are the most precise for this kind of decay, thanks to the relatively
large branching fraction.
These measurements are also very clean from the theoretical point of view, thanks to the very
limited tree pollution
Beneke 2005
.

The
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
decay was initially discovered by
CLEO
Behrens et al. 1998
;
Richichi et al. 2000
.
The current best measurements of branching ratio
\mathcal{B}
were obtained by Belle
Schumann et al. 2006
and
BaBar
Aubert et al. 2009
, using
386
and
467
million
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs,
respectively.
The current Belle II integrated luminosity, collected at the
{\mathit{{\mathchar 28935\relax}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({4S}\right)}}}
resonance, does not allow, yet, to improve these measurements, but the rediscovery
of these final states is an important benchmark to demonstrate the capability
of the Belle II detector.
These
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
decays are characterized by complicated final states, with charged and
neutral particles, and intermediate resonances. Moreover, they are affected by
a large contamination due to background coming both from
continuum

\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{q}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{q}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
(
\mathit{{q}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}=\mathit{{u}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{d}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{s}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{c}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
) events
as well as from misreconstructed signal events (
self
cross feed
(SxF)). The continuum suppression is achieved by a
multivariate discriminator
\text{CS}_{var}
, which is validated on off-resonance data.
The signal yield is extracted with a multidimensional maximum
likelihood fit, using as input variables :

M_{\text{b}c}=\sqrt{E^{*2}_{beam}c^{4}-p^{*2}_{B}c^{2}}
,
\Delta{E}=E^{*}_{B}-E_{beam}

(where

are momentum and energy of the candidate
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
computed
in the center of mass system, and

),
and the output of the continuum suppression discriminator.

Both charged and neutral decays are measured.
Two decay modes for
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
are considered:

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}(\rightarrow\gamma\gamma)\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
and

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\rho}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}(\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}

\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{S}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{0}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}

II
The Belle II detector and dataset

The Belle II detector is described in detail in Ref.
Abe et al. 2010
.
The detector has a cylindrical structure around the beam pipe, placed partially
inside a solenoidal superconducting magnet providing a 1.5T magnetic field.
The innermost sub-detector is the vertex detector (VXD), formed by two layers of
silicon pixel sensors and four layers of silicon strips, devoted to tracking
and vertexing. It is surrounded by a large central drift chamber (CDC), with
small cells and filled with a helium ethane mixture, which provide precise
measurement of momenta of charged tracks as well as particle identification via
energy loss measurement (
dE/dx
).
Two Cherenkov detectors provide additional particle identification: the Time of
Propagation (TOP) counter in the barrel region, and the Aerogel Ring Imaging
Cherenkov (ARICH) in the forward region.
The last detector inside the solenoid is the electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL),
based on CsI(Tl) crystals, dedicated to photon and electron identification and measurement.
The return yoke of the magnet is instrumented with scintillator strips and
resistive plate chambers, to provide measurements for
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{L}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{0}}}
mesons and muons (KLM).
The coordinate system is defined by the
z
axis, corresponding to the solenoid
axis, and roughly oriented with the electron beam, the polar angle
\theta

defined with respect the
z
axis, and the azimuthal angle
\phi
.

The dataset used for this analysis was collected by Belle II in 2019 and
2020 at the SuperKEKB asymmetric energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider
Akai et al. 2018
.
The integrated luminosity collected at a centre-of-mass (CM) energy
corresponding to the
{\mathit{{\mathchar 28935\relax}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({4S}\right)}}}
resonance is
62.8\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, with an
additional
9.2\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
collected about 60
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
below the resonance
(off-resonance dataset). This corresponds to
n(\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}})=68.21\times{}10^{6}
produced
pairs of
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}

The Belle II
Collaboration 2020a
.

.

The candidate multiplicity is around two candidates per event for the

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
decay channel, and about six for

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\rho}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
.
Only the candidate with the best
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
vertex probability is retained: the simulation
shows that this selects the correct candidate in about 95% of the cases.
The overall efficiency for this selection is about
31\%
and
24\%
for the

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
and
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\rho}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
decay channels,
respectively. The self cross feed is about 2-3% of the signal.

To reduce the dominant background from random combinations of particles in
continuum events, we use a multivariate approach, combining a set of variables
which are sensitive to the event shape. These includes Kakuno-Super-Fox-Wolfram
moments
Bevan et al. 2014
,
CLEO
cones
Asner et al. 1996
, as well
as the angles of the thrust axis of signal
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
with respect to that the rest of event and that with respect the beam
axis. All variables that exhibit a correlation greater than 10% with
M_{\text{b}c}
and

\Delta{\text{E}}
are excluded. The classifier (
\text{CS}_{var}
) used is based on the FastBDT algorithm
Keck 2017
.
The output of the discriminator is validated using
off-resonance data, as shown in Fig.
1
, where the
discrimination against signal is also visible.
The discrepancy in shape between off-resonance data and simulation is covered
by a dedicated systematic uncertainty.
No selection is applied to this variable, but it is directly used in the
fit to extract the signal yield, as described below.

IV
Maximum likelihood fit

The signal extraction is performed via an extended, unbinned multivariate
maximum likelihood (ML) fit. The likelihood
\mathcal{L}
is defined, for the

i^{th}
events, with a set of observables
\vec{x}_{i}
, as:

\mathcal{L}_{i}(\vec{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j}(\vec{x}_{i})\quad,

(1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{j}
is the probability density for the component
j
computed for
input variables (observables)
\vec{x}_{i}
, and
n_{j}
is the number of events in
the dataset for component
j
.
The input observables
\vec{x}_{i}
are
M_{\text{b}c}
,
\Delta{E}
, and the continuum
suppression discriminator
\text{CS}_{var}
.

The probabilities
\mathcal{P}_{j}
are assumed to be the product of 1-dimensional
probability density functions (
pdf
s) for each input variable, neglecting
correlations.
The correlation of the observables has been tested on simulation, where a small
(anti-)correlation (
\sim 10\%
) between
M_{\text{b}c}
and
\Delta{\text{E}}
is observed, while the continuum
suppression discriminator shows no correlation with the other two variables.

For a dataset of
N
events, where
N
is expected to fluctuate according to
Poisson statistics, the likelihood
\mathcal{L}
is:

\mathcal{L}(N;\vec{x})=\frac{e^{-\sum n_{j}}}{N!}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{i}\quad,

(2)

The components considered for the fit are:

•

signal
: correctly reconstructed signal;

•

signal cross feed (SxF)
: incorrectly reconstructed signal, namely
events where a signal is present but where the reconstruction fails to assign
the correct particles to form a
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
;

•

continuum
: background from

\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{q}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{q}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
(
\mathit{{q}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}=\mathit{{u}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{d}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{s}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}},\mathit{{c}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
) and
\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\tau}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\tau}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
;

•

peaking
: background from
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, both charged and neutral.

As the signal cross feed component is expected to be small and the observables
are not sensitive to it, the fit is performed considering signal and signal
cross feed as a single component, and the relative fraction (about 9%) is
fixed from simulation.

The functional form of the
pdf
s for all components are modelled from simulation.
We use a double Gaussian for
M_{\text{b}c}
and
\Delta{\text{E}}
for signal, for SxF a Crystal Ball
Oreglia
(CB) for
M_{\text{b}c}
and CB or Gaussian plus polynomial for
\Delta{\text{E}}
.
For continuum, an
ARGUS
function
Albrecht et al. 1990
is used for
M_{\text{b}c}
, and a polynomial for
\Delta{\text{E}}
.
For peaking background, the sum of an
ARGUS
and a Gaussian is used for
M_{\text{b}c}
and a polynomial is used for
\Delta{\text{E}}
.
Finally, a bifurcated Gaussian is used for
\text{CS}_{var}
.

The fit to data is performed by varying the yield of signal,
continuum, and peaking background, as well as the mean and width of the
Gaussian used to model
M_{\text{b}c}
for signal, the slope of the
ARGUS

for continuum for
M_{\text{b}c}
, the mean and width of the Gaussian for
\Delta{\text{E}}
for signal,
and the parameters for the polynomial model for continuum
\Delta{\text{E}}
.

The minimization is performed using
MINUT

James
, and the
uncertainty computation uses the
MINOS
algorithm.
The fit was validated and confirmed to be unbiased with
pseudo-experiments.
The events in the signal region (defined as
M_{\text{b}c}>5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and

-0.07<\Delta{E}<0.05\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
) were not examined until the fit procedure
was defined and validated, using simulation, side bands, and off-resonance
data.

Systematics uncertainties

The systematic uncertainties considered for this analysis are the following:

•

tracking efficiency
: we add 0.69% for each charged track in the signal final state
The Belle II
Collaboration 2020b
;

•

photon efficiency
: from a sample of

\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\mu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{\mu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
events, the systematic uncertainties have been
evaluated as a function of photon energy and polar angle
\theta
;

•

\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{S}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{0}}}
reconstruction efficiency
: comparing data and simulation,
we observed that the ratio of
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{S}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{0}}}
reconstruction efficiency changes linearly as a
function of the flight distance, so we applied an uncertainty of 0.31% per
cm of the average flight length, plus a 15% uncertainty for the mis-modeling of
material between second and third layer of SVD (10% of candidates);

•

PID
: a sample of
\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\ast}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{D}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}(\rightarrow\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}_{soft}
is used to compute
the difference in efficiency for data and simulation as a function of
particle momentum and
\cos\theta
for pions and kaons
The Belle II
Collaboration 2020c
;

•

Continuum suppression modelling
: we repeat the ML fit using
off-resonance data to obtain the pdf parameters for the continuum suppression
discriminator, and use the difference in yield using the pdf from simulation as the uncertainty;

•

SxF fraction
: we vary the fraction of SxF to truth matched
signal found in simulation by
\pm 50\%
and assign the difference in yield
as systematic uncertainty;

•

N(
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
) yield
: an overall uncertainty of 1.4% is taken as a systematic uncertainty, and
includes the uncertainty on the cross section, integrated luminosity, and
possible shift of collision energy
The Belle II
Collaboration 2020a
;

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we described the rediscovery of the
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
hadronic-penguin mediated
decays, both for charged and neutral
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
mesons.
The full Belle II luminosity collected up to summer 2020 has been used,
corresponding to
L=62.8\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
and
68.2\times 10^{6}

B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs.
The signal yield is extracted via a maximum likelihood fit to the data, and has been validated
on simulation, as well as on off-resonance data.
The results, reported in Table
3
, are in good agreement with world averages
Zyla et al. 2020
.
The signal yield per
10^{6}~B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
is similar to that reported by
BaBar
Aubert et al. 2009
, and almost a factor two larger that that of
Belle
Schumann et al. 2006
, partially thanks to the absence of selection on
continuum suppression variable.
The next step will be to use the future large data sample collected at Belle II for a full time
dependent CP violation analysis.

Fourier–Mukai partners and generalized Kummer structures on generalized Kummer surfaces of order 3 3
Xavier Roulleau, Alessandra Sarti

Abstract
. A generalized Kummer surface X X of order 3 3 is the minimal resolution of the quotient of an abelian surface A A by an order 3 3 symplectic automorphism. We study a generalization of a problem of Shioda for classical Kummer surfaces, which is to understand to what extend X X is determined by A A and conversely. The surface X X posses a big and nef divisor L X L_{X} such that L X 2 = 0 L_{X}^{2}=0 or 2 2 mod 6 6 ; we suppose it has Picard number 19 19 . We show that for surfaces with L X 2 = 6 ​ k L_{X}^{2}=6k with k ≠ 0 , 6 ​ mod ​ 9 k\neq 0,6{\rm\,mod\,}9 , the surface X X determines the transcendental lattice T ⁡ ( A ) T(A) of A A and the Hodge structure on T ⁡ ( A ) T(A) . Conversely if A A and B B are Fourier-Mukai partners (i.e. if the Hodge structures of their transcendental lattices are isomorphic) and Y Y is the generalized Kummer surface which is the minimal resolution of the quotient of B B by an order 3 3 symplectic automorphism, we obtain that X X and Y Y are isomorphic. These results are also known to hold for surfaces with L X 2 = 2 ​ mod ​ 6 L_{X}^{2}=2{\rm\,mod\,}6 from a previous work [ 11 ] . When k = 0 ​ or ​ 6 ​ mod ​ 9 , k=0\text{ or }6{\rm\,mod\,}9, we show that X X determines T ⁡ ( A ) T(A) and its Hodge structure, but the converse does not hold in general.



1.
Introduction

An (algebraic and complex) generalized Kummer surface
X={\rm Km}_{3}(A)

of order
3
is a K3 surface which is the minimal resolution of the
quotient
A/G_{A}
of an abelian surface
A
by an order
3
symplectic
automorphism group
G_{A}
. In
[
11
,
6
,
10
]
we study when
there exists another Abelian surface
B
and order
3
automorphism
group
G_{B}
such that
(B,G_{B})
is not isomorphic to
(A,G_{A})

but the generalized Kummer surfaces
{\rm Km}_{3}(A)
and
{\rm Km}_{3}(B)

are. That question is a natural extension to generalized Kummer surfaces
of a problem of Shioda on classical Kummer surfaces.

Recall that the quotient surface
A/G_{A}
has
9
cups singularities,
each of these being resolved by two
{\rm(-2)}
-curves on
X
. For a very
general abelian surface
A
, the K3 surface
X
has Picard number

19
. In the present paper, we will always suppose that the surface

X
has Picard number
19
. The positive generator
L_{X}
of the
orthogonal complement in the Néron-Severi group
{\rm NS}(X)
of the
18

exceptional curves of the resolution
X\to A/G_{A}
satisfies
L_{X}^{2}=0

or
2{\rm\,mod\,}6
.

Let us also recall that for an abelian surface (respectively a K3
surface)
Y
, a Fourier-Mukai partner of
Y
is an abelian surface
(respectively K3 surface)
Y^{\prime}
such that there is an isomorphism
of Hodge structures

(T(Y),\mathbb{C}\omega_{Y})\simeq(T(Y^{\prime}),\mathbb{C}\omega_{Y^{\prime}}).

Here the transcendental lattice

is the orthogonal complement
in
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
of the Néron-Severi lattice
{\rm NS}(Y)\subset H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})

and
\omega_{Y}
is a generator of the space of holomorphic
2
-forms.

Let
(A,G_{A})
and
(B,G_{B})
be two abelian surfaces with an
order
3
symplectic automorphism group. Suppose that
X={\rm Km}_{3}(A)

is a generalized Kummer surface with Picard number
19
. Consider
the following two assertions:

(I) The surface

is a Fourier–Mukai partner of
A
.

(II) The surfaces
{\rm Km}_{3}(B)
and
{\rm Km}_{3}(A)
are isomorphic.

As shown in
[
11
]
, for generalized Kummer surfaces

with

L_{X}^{2}=2{\rm\,mod\,}6
, the assertions (I) and (II) are equivalent. The
aim of this paper is to prove the following result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a generalized Kummer surface with Picard
number
19
and such that
L_{X}^{2}=6k
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

a) Suppose that
k\neq 0\text{ and }6{\rm\,mod\,}9
. Then (I) is equivalent
to (II).

b) Suppose
k=0
or
6{\rm\,mod\,}9
. Then (II) implies (I), but (I) does
not implies (II) in general: there exist abelian surfaces with order

3
symplectic automorphisms that are Fourier-Mukai partners and
such that the associated generalized Kummer surfaces are not isomorphic.

By
[
5
]
, since
X={\rm Km}_{3}(A)
has Picard number
19>2+\ell

(here
\ell
is the length of the discriminant group of
{\rm NS}(X)
,
which is also the length of
T(X)
, and therefore is
\leq 3
), if
a K3 surface is a Fourier-Mukai partner of
{\rm Km}_{3}(A)
, then it
is isomorphic to
{\rm Km}_{3}(A)
. The assertion (II) is therefore equivalent
to :

(II’) The surfaces
{\rm Km}_{3}(A)
and
{\rm Km}_{3}(B)
are Fourier-Mukai
partners, i.e. there exists a Hodge isometry

(T({\rm Km}_{3}(A)),\mathbb{C}\omega_{{\rm Km}_{3}(A)})\simeq(T({\rm Km}_{3}(B)),\mathbb{C}\omega_{{\rm Km}_{3}(B)}).

In case
L_{X}^{2}=2{\rm\,mod\,}3
, the equivalence between (I) and (II’)
(and therefore with (II)) is much simpler. Indeed, it is easy to compare
the Hodge structures, since in that case
T({\rm Km}_{3}(A))
is isometric
to
T(A)(3)
, (where the
3
means that the quadratic intersection
form of the lattice is multiplied by
3
). In case
L_{X}^{2}=0{\rm\,mod\,}6
,
one only knows that
T({\rm Km}_{3}(A))
contains an index
3
sub-lattice
isometric to
T(A)(3)
and that subtlety makes the proof much more
involved.

A generalized Kummer structure on the generalized Kummer surface
X

is an isomorphism class of pairs
(B,G_{B})
such that the associated
generalized Kummer surface
{\rm Km}_{3}(B)
is isomorphic to
X={\rm Km}_{3}(A)
.
The generalized problem of Shioda can be rephrased as the problem
to understand if there is a unique generalized Kummer structure. Theorem

1
shows that a generalized Kummer structure
\{(B,G_{B})\}

on
X
is such that
B
is a Fourier-Mukai partner of
A
, for

X
such that
L_{X}^{2}\neq 0,6{\rm\,mod\,}9
. Theorem
1

is a key-result in order to compute the number of generalized Kummer
structures in
[
10
]
according to the value of
L_{X}^{2}
,
for
L_{X}^{2}\neq 0,6{\rm\,mod\,}9
.

In
[
4
,
5
]
, Hosono, Lian, Oguiso and Yau study the analogous
problem for the classical Kummer surfaces (and compute the number
of Kummer structures of some Kummer surfaces). For these surfaces,
one always has
T({\rm Km}(A))=T(A)(2)
, and the equivalence between the
analog of (I) and (II) for Kummer surfaces is immediate. The fact
that for generalized Kummer surfaces (I) does not imply (II) is all

The paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we give preliminaries
and notations on lattice theory. In Section 3, we explain how we proceed
to prove Theorem
1
. One wants in particular to know
the over-lattices of

which are isomorphic to
T(X)
. Sections

, and section
7 deals with the remaining cases.

2.1.
The discriminant group of a
lattice as a torsion quadratic module

Let
L
be an even lattice ; the intersection of two elements
v,v^{\prime}\in L

is denoted by
vv^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The bilinear pairing extends to
L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
,
and the dual of
L
is

\check{L}:=\{v\in L\otimes\mathbb{Q}\,|\,\forall w\in L,\,vw\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

The discriminant group of
L
is a torsion quadratic module, denoted
by
\text{A}_{L}
: its subjacent group is the finite abelian group

\check{L}/L
, and its quadratic form
q_{L}:\check{L}/L\to\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}

is defined for
\frac{1}{n}w\in\check{L}
(for
n\in\mathbb{Z},n\neq 0

and
w\in L
such that
\frac{1}{n}w\in\check{L}
) by

q_{L}(\frac{1}{n}w)=\frac{1}{n^{2}}w^{2}{\rm\,mod\,}2\mathbb{Z}.

If
v\in L
, one has

(\frac{1}{n}w+v)^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}w^{2}+2\frac{1}{n}wv+v^{2}.

Since
\frac{1}{n}w\in\check{L}
,
2\frac{1}{n}wv
is an even integer
and since
L
is even, one has
v^{2}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, thus the quadratic
form
q_{L}
is a well defined function on the finite group
\check{L}/L
.

Let
L
be the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with Gram matrix
G
for the canonical
basis. The columns
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
of
G^{-1}
considered as
elements of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
generate the group
\text{A}_{L}
,
moreover the quadratic form
q_{L}
on these generators is given
by the matrix
G^{-1}=(^{t}c_{i}Gc_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, where
the diagonal entries of
G^{-1}
are taken modulo
2\mathbb{Z}
, and the
other entries are taken modulo
\mathbb{Z}
(and respecting the symmetry).

2.2.
Genus of lattice, discriminant group
and over-lattices

By definition, two lattices
L_{1},L_{2}
are in the same genus if

L_{1}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\simeq L_{2}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for all primes
p

and
L_{1}\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq L_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}
. One has

Theorem 2
.

(
[
8
, Corollary 1.9.4]
) The lattices
L_{1},L_{2}
are
in the same genus if and only if they have the same signature and
have isometric discriminant groups.

Any over-lattice
L_{H}
of
L
is (up to isometry) the pull-back

L_{H}=\pi^{-1}(H)
by the quotient map
\pi:\check{L}\to\check{L}/L

of an isotropic subgroup
H
contained in
\text{A}_{L}=\check{L}/L
.

An isometry
g\in O(L)
induces an isometry
\bar{g}
of
\text{A}_{L}
.
If
H_{1}\subset\text{A}_{L}
is an isotropic sub-group, so is
H_{2}=\bar{g}(H_{1})

and
g
induces an isometry between the over-lattices
L_{H_{1}}

and
L_{H_{2}}
preserving the lattice
L
. More generally, two
over-lattices
\iota_{1}:L\hookrightarrow L_{1},\iota_{2}:L\hookrightarrow L_{2}

are said isomorphic if there exists an isometry
g
of
L
extending
to an isometry
\tilde{g}
between
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. Then the
following diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}L&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{1}}}{{\to}}&L_{1}\\
\downarrow g&&\downarrow\tilde{g}\\
L&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{2}}}{{\to}}&L_{2}\end{array}

is commutative. Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be the isotropic sub-groups of

\text{A}_{L}
corresponding to over-lattices
L_{1},L_{2}
. One
has:

Proposition 3
.

(
[
8
, Proposition 1.4.3]
).
The over-lattices
\iota_{1}:L\hookrightarrow L_{1},\iota_{2}:L\hookrightarrow L_{2}

are isomorphic if and only if there exist an isometry
g
of
L

such
\bar{g}(H_{1})=H_{2}
.

Let
M
be a lattice containing
L
with finite index, let
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}

be the set of all isotropic sub-groups of the (finite) group
\text{A}_{L}

such that their associated over-lattices
L_{1},\dots,L_{n}
are
isometric to
M
. Let
\iota_{j}:L\hookrightarrow L_{j}
be the
inclusion of
L
in
L_{j}
.

Corollary 4
.

Under the setting above, suppose
that the over-lattices
\iota_{j}:L\hookrightarrow L_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n

are isomorphic. Then there exist a unique embedding of
L
in
M

up to isometries of
L
and
M
.

Proof.

Let
\iota:L\hookrightarrow M
,
\iota^{\prime}:L\hookrightarrow M
be two
embeddings, let
H,H^{\prime}
be the two isotropic sub-groups of
L
corresponding
to these over-lattices. There exist two indices
s,t\in\{1,\dots,n\}

such that (up to isometries)
\iota:L\hookrightarrow M
is isomorphic
to
\iota_{s}:L\hookrightarrow L_{s}
and
\iota^{\prime}:L\hookrightarrow M

is isomorphic to
\iota_{t}:L\hookrightarrow L_{t}
, the over-lattices

L_{s},L_{t}
corresponding to
H_{s}
and
H_{t}
respectively.
Since by hypothesis
\iota_{s}:L\hookrightarrow L_{s}
and
\iota_{t}:L\hookrightarrow L_{t}

are isomorphic, the over-lattices
\iota:L\hookrightarrow M
,
\iota^{\prime}:L\hookrightarrow M

are isomorphic, thus there exist isometries
g
of
L
and
\tilde{g}

of
M
such that
\tilde{g}\circ\iota=g\circ\iota^{\prime}
.
∎

2.3.
Examples of quadratic torsion
modules

In this section, we give some examples of quadratic torsion modules
which we will later use to compute the discriminant groups of various
lattices.

Consider
\frac{u}{v}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
with
u,v
coprime such
that either
u
or
v
is even. Let us denote by
(\frac{u}{v})

the torsion quadratic module
(\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z},q)
with quadratic form
q:\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}

defined by

q(x)=\frac{ux^{2}}{v}\in(\frac{1}{v}\mathbb{Z})/2\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}.

If
x^{\prime}=x+tv
with
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

q(x^{\prime})=\frac{u(x^{2}+2xtv+t^{2}v^{2})}{v}=\frac{ux^{2}}{v}+u(2xt+t^{2}v)=\frac{ux^{2}}{v}=q(x),

thus
q
is well-defined; here we use that
u
or
v
is even at
the third equality (if both were odd, the form
q
would be well-defined
only modulo
\mathbb{Z}
). For
u,u^{\prime}
coprime to
v
, one has

(\frac{u}{v})=(\frac{u^{\prime}}{v})

if and only if
u=u^{\prime}{\rm\,mod\,}2v
.

Suppose that
v=ab
with
a,b
coprime integers, and let
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}

such that
as+bt=1
. If
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
is another solution, one has
s^{\prime}=s+mb

and
t^{\prime}=t-ma
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

\bullet
When
u
is even, (thus
a
and
b
are odd). Then,
since
u
is even, one has
us=us^{\prime}{\rm\,mod\,}2b
,
ut=ut^{\prime}{\rm\,mod\,}2a
and the
torsion quadratic modules

(\frac{us}{b}),\,\,(\frac{ut}{a})

are independent of the choice of the solution
(s,t)
.

\bullet
When
u
is odd and
a
, say, is even,
b
is odd. The
relation
as+bt=1
implies that
t
is odd. Moreover, since
b

is odd, up to changing
s
by
s+mb
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, one can choose

s
to be even, and in fact one must do it in order for the quadratic
form of
(\frac{us}{b})
to have values in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. That choice
of
s
is unique modulo
2b
. The choice of
t
is then unique
modulo
2a
, and in the notations:
(\frac{us}{b}),(\frac{ut}{a})
,
it is always implicitly supposed that
s
is even.

In both cases
u
even or odd, the relation

\frac{u}{ab}=\frac{tu}{a}+\frac{su}{b}

implies that
(\frac{u}{v})
decomposes as a direct sum

(\frac{u}{v})=(\frac{tu}{a})+(\frac{su}{b})

where we use the canonical isomorphism
x{\rm\,mod\,}ab\to(x\,{\rm\,mod\,}a,x{\rm\,mod\,}b)

between
\mathbb{Z}/ab\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z}
(we recall that

a,b
are coprime integers). For
v=ab
with
ab
coprime, we denote
by
(\frac{u}{v})_{a}=(\frac{tu}{a})
the restriction of
(\frac{u}{v})

to
\mathbb{Z}/a\mathbb{Z}
. Since
s
is even, in
\mathbb{Z}/2a\mathbb{Z}
one has
\bar{b}\bar{t}=\bar{1}
,
thus we sometimes write
(\frac{u}{v})_{a}=(\frac{u/b}{a})
.

The decomposition
(\frac{u}{ab})=(\frac{tu}{a})+(\frac{su}{b})

implies that if
v=p_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots p_{k}^{n_{k}}
is the factorization
of
v
into a product of distinct primes, the torsion quadratic module

(\frac{u}{v})
is isometric to a direct sum

(\frac{u_{1}}{p_{1}^{n_{1}}})+\dots+(\frac{u_{k}}{p_{k}^{n_{k}}})

for some integers
u_{i}
coprime to
p_{i}
and such that
u_{i}

or
p_{i}
is even. More generally, using that a finite abelian group
is the direct sum of its
p
-sub-groups, a torsion quadratic module

M
is a direct sum
M=\oplus_{p\,\text{prime}}M_{p}
of
p
-sub-groups.
For
p\geq 3
,
M_{p}
is a direct sum of torsion quadratic modules
of the form
(\frac{u}{p^{n}})
(for
u

p
and

). For

, the situation is more complicated, see

[
3
]
.

Let be
w\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\bar{w}\in\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}
is a unit. For any

n\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

u(w+nv)^{2}=uw^{2}+2nuwv+un^{2}v^{2}=uw^{2}{\rm\,mod\,}2v,

(where in the last equality we use that
u
or
v
is even), thus
group of units
(\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z})^{*}
acts on the set of torsion quadratic
modules
(\frac{u}{v})
by
\bar{w}.(\frac{u}{v})=(\frac{uw^{2}}{v})
.
One has

\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z},\,\,\frac{uw^{2}}{v}x^{2}=\frac{u}{v}(\bar{w}x)^{2},

therefore the automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}
defined by

is an isometry between the torsion quadratic modules
\bar{w}.(\frac{u}{v})

and
(\frac{u}{v})
. Conversely, if there is an isometry between

(\frac{u}{v})
and
(\frac{u^{\prime}}{v})
, the subjacent automorphism

\psi
of
\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}
being of the form

for some

\bar{w}\in(\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, one has

, thus

and

is a square.

Suppose that
v
is an odd prime. The set
S_{q}
of square elements
in
(\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z})^{*}
is a sub-group of index
2
. Let
m_{2}:\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}\to 2\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}2v\mathbb{Z}

be the set

The set

is in bijection with the set of quadratic torsion modules
by the map

. There is a natural faithful
action of

on

given by

That action has two orbits, corresponding to the invertible square,
and invertible non-square in

. Thus there are only two isometry

,

.
For example, the only torsion quadratic modules on

(\frac{2}{3^{a}})

(\frac{4}{3^{a}})
(for

3.

.

Suppose that there exists in

, where

Theorem 7
.

Under the above hypothesis, there exists a Hodge

(3.2)

Proposition 23

for which a reference is

. A Hodge structure
on a rank

of signature
(2,1)
is the data of a

such that

for

decomposes as

where
\mathbb{C}t
is the orthogonal complement of the space
\mathbb{C}w\oplus\mathbb{C}\bar{w}
;
this is a real subspace:
\overline{\mathbb{C}t}=\mathbb{C}t
. The set of Hodge
structures is an (euclidian) open subset in the smooth quadric
Q_{T}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}

defined by
\omega^{2}=0
. In fact, this is the complement of the real
axis in
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}=Q_{T}

to

, where
\mathcal{H}

is the complex upper-plane (see
[

). Two Hodge
structures
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
on

are isomorphic if and only if there exists
an isometry

such that

where

is the complexification of

(an isometry of

acts as an homography on
\mathcal{H}\cup\overline{\mathcal{H}}\subset Q_{T}
).

The fixed point set of a projective automorphism

acting on
\mathbb{P}(T\otimes\mathbb{C})

is a union of linear subspaces, thus the stabilizer group (in the
projective automorphism group) of the smooth quadric
Q_{T}
acts
faithfully on
Q_{T}
.

Since we suppose that the over-lattices
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
are
isomorphic, there exist isometries

and

such that the following diagram is commutative

\begin{array}[]{ccc}T(A)(3)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\hookrightarrow}}&T_{i}\\
\downarrow h_{0}&&\downarrow h\\
T(A)(3)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\hookrightarrow}}&T_{j}\end{array}.

We consider
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
contained in
T(A)(3)\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
The complexification

of the lattice isometry

preserves
the quadric

containing
the Hodge structures. That quadric is the same for

and

. For

, the isometry

induces an isomorphism
between the Hodge structures
(T_{i},\omega)
and
(T_{j},h_{\mathbb{C}}(\omega))
.

Suppose that for a general period
\omega\in Q

(T_{j},\omega)

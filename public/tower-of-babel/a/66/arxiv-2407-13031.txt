Power operations preserve Thom classes in twisted equivariant Real K-theory
Daniel Berwick-Evans and Meng Guo

Abstract
. We construct power operations for twisted KR-theory of topological stacks. Standard algebraic properties of Clifford algebras imply that these power operations preserve universal Thom classes. As a consequence, we show that the twisted Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro orientation commutes with power operations.

Introduction

In this paper we construct universal Thom classes in twisted equivariant Real K-theory,

Remark 1.6
.

We recall that the K-theory of quotient stacks is constructed to coincide with (the non-completed) equivariant K-theory of

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau}_{G}(X)={\rm KR}^{\tau}(X/\!\!/G),\quad{\rm KO}^{\tau}_{G}(X)={\rm KO}^{\tau}(X/\!\!/G),\quad{\rm KU}^{\tau}_{G}(X)={\rm KU}^{\tau}(X/\!\!/G)

Remark 1.7
.

The pullback along the quotient map
q\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}/\!\!/{\rm O}_{p,q}

(7)

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau_{p,q}}({\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}/\!\!/{\rm O}_{p,q})\xrightarrow{q^{*}}{\rm KR}^{q^{*}\tau_{p,q}}({\mathbb{R}}^{p,q})\simeq{\rm KR}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}),\qquad{\rm Th}_{p,q}\mapsto{{u}}_{p,q},

sends the Thom class (
1
) to the
{\rm KR}
-suspension class
{{u}}_{p,q}
of the
C_{2}
-representation sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}
. Equivalently, (
7
) restricts the Thom class of the vector bundle
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}/\!\!/{\rm O}_{p,q}\to{*}/\!\!/{\rm O}_{p,q}
to the fiber at the basepoint

-equivariance, (

: a Thom class is defined as a class that restricts to the suspension class on each fiber. Turning (
7

to descend to an
{\rm O}_{p,q}
-equivariant class.

Remark 1.8
.

In Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.3
, Thom twistings are essential to even parse a compatibility between power operations and Thom classes. Algebraically, this stems from the fact that the wreath products
{\rm Spin}_{n}\wr\Sigma_{k}
are not natural subgroups of
{\rm Spin}(nk)
; this stands in contrast to the wreath products of orthogonal groups as subgroups of larger orthogonal groups. Geometrically, twistings arise from the fact that the action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{k}
on the
k^{\rm th}
power
({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\times k}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{nk}
need not preserve a spin structure, or even an orientation. Hence, power operations applied to Thom classes are unavoidably twisted.

Remark 1.9
.

Building on the previous remark, twistings are also inevitable when applying power operations to classes in nonzero cohomological degree. To summarize, the
k^{\rm th}
power operation on a
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}
-linear Fredholm operator yields a
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}^{\otimes k}
-linear
\Sigma_{k}
-equivariant Fredholm operator. However, the
\Sigma_{k}
-equivariance involves the
\Sigma_{k}
-action on
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}^{\otimes k}
that permutes the tensor factors, which introduces a twisting. Indeed, this twisting is a specialization of the Thom twisting for the
\Sigma_{k}
-action on
({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\times k}
. The twisting trivializes when the Clifford algebra is Morita trivial: in degrees
0
mod
2
for
{\rm KU}
and degrees
0
mod
8
for
{\rm KO}
.

Remark 1.10
.

When
G
is trivial, Theorems
1.4
and
6.8
generalize the classical compatibility between power operations and orientations of K-theory as maps of
H_{\infty}
-spectra
[
BMMS86
, Proposition VII.7.2]
. These previous results restrict the Spin
c
orientation of
{\rm KU}
to degrees 0 mod 2 and the spin orientation of
{\rm KO}
to degrees 0 mod 8. This restriction is explained by the previous remark: power operations in other degrees necessitate twistings.

1.1.
Representing stacks for twisted KR-theory

A reoccurring strategy in this paper is to formulate definitions and constructions using universal objects. This begins with the following stacky distillation of twisted Real K-theory that is somewhat implicit in the literature, e.g., see preprint versions of
[
FHT11b
]
and
[
SS21

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a complex Hilbert space (typically infinite rank) with the structure of a graded, unitary, Real right
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}
-module where
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}

2.3

]

[
Ati66a
]

Real stack
is a topological stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
with (weak)
C_{2}
-action, and a
Real map
is a
C_{2}
-equivariant map of topological stacks. Consider the Real fibration

(8)

\displaystyle{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})

whose source is the quotient stack for the conjugation action of the projective unitary group on
{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})

\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}
-linear Fredholm operators.

Definition 1.11

(Definitions
3.7
and
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
)
.

For a Real local quotient stack

All tensor products below are

2.1.
The category of Real graded groups

Definition 2.1

(
[
Ati66a
, §1]
)
.

The category of
Real spaces
has objects spaces
X
with involution
(\overline{\phantom{g}})\colon X\to X
and morphisms continuous maps
f\colon X\to Y
compatible with the involutions,
f(\overline{x})=\overline{f(x)}
.

Example 2.2
.

The most basic examples of Real spaces are
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with involution given by the usual complex conjugation
z\mapsto\overline{z}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{p,q}
denote the Real space given by
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+q}={\mathbb{R}}^{p}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
with involution

(17)

\displaystyle(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p},\ y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}.

We note the isomorphisms of Real spaces
{\mathbb{R}}^{n,n}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
given by
(x,y)\mapsto x+iy=z
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
Ati68
, §5]
)
.

A
Real group
is a group object in Real spaces, i.e., a group
G
with an involution homomorphism
(\overline{\phantom{g}})\colon G\to G
. A
Real homomorphism

(G,(\overline{\phantom{g}}))\to(H,(\overline{\phantom{g}}))
is a homomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to H
compatible with the involutions,
\varphi(\overline{g})=\overline{\varphi(g)}
. A
Real action
of a Real group
G
on a Real space
X
is an action compatible with the Real structure,
\overline{g\cdot x}=\overline{g}\cdot\overline{x}
for
g\in G
and
x\in X
.

Example 2.5
.

The Real structure on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
promotes the unitary group
{\rm U}_{n}
to a Real group with involution corresponding to complex conjugation of matrix entries. Equivalently,

(18)

\displaystyle\overline{u}=(\overline{\phantom{g}})\circ u\circ(\overline{\phantom{g}}),\qquad u\in{\rm U}_{n}

for pre- and post-composition by the involution on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
from Example

. The standard

-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
determines a Real action.

Example 2.6
.

Let
{\rm O}_{p,q}
denote the Real group defined by the orthogonal group

with Real structure given by the same formula as (

. The standard

-action on

Example 2.7
.

A Real action of a Real group

in the sense of Definition

Definition 2.8

A

is a group

Definition 2.9

A

\epsilon(\overline{g})=\overline{\epsilon(g)}.
Real graded groups are the objects of a category whose morphisms are Real, graded homomorphisms.

Example 2.10

The determinant homomorphism

Remark 2.11
.

The involution

generates an action on

by a group of order 2. This group plays a distinct role from the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
involved in the grading. To disambiguate these notationally, throughout the paper

Remark 2.12

There are various ways to pass between combinations of Real and graded groups. For example, any group

has the trivial grading

and the trivial Real structure given by the identity involution. As another example, for a Real graded group

the fixed point subgroup

For a Real group, the

Definition 4.1
.

For
{\mathcal{H}}
a Real
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}
-module, let
{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})
denote the Real stack associated with the
C_{2}
-equivariant map (
72
).

Remark 4.2
.

The above is the Real generalization of a Fredholm operator construction of twisted complex K-theory from
[
AS04
, §A.2]
and
[
FHT11a
, §A.5]
. A comprehensive reference for this Real generalization is
[
Gom23
, §B.2 and Remark 4.12]
. An early reference for Fredholm operators as a representing space for KR-theory is
[
Mat71
, Theorem II]
. Similar ideas using representing stacks for
{\rm KR}
-theory are in
[
SS21
, Example 1.3.19]
.

For an isometric inclusion
{\mathcal{H}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
of right
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}
-modules, there is a 2-commutative square of Real stacks

{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})
{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})
{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})

determined by strictly commuting squares of topological groupoids. The lower horizontal arrows are determined by the homomorphism (
48
), while the upper arrows are given by

(73)

\displaystyle{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}),\qquad F\mapsto F\oplus\epsilon,\quad{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\simeq{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
is the orthogonal complements to the embedding and
\epsilon\in{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\perp})
is a basepoint, i.e., an invertible Fredholm operator. The space of invertible Fredholm operators is contractible, so the map (
73
) is unique up to a contractible space of choices.

The tensor product of Clifford linear Fredholm operators

(74)

\displaystyle{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})\times{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(F,F^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle F\otimes{{\rm id}}+{{\rm id}}\otimes F^{\prime}

is equivariant relative to the homomorphism (
46
), giving 2-commutative diagrams in stacks

{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})\times{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})/\!\!/({\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})\times{\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}))
{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
{*}/\!\!/({\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})\times{\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}))
{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}).

Remark 4.3
.

A finite rank Real (super) vector bundle
V\to X
determines a map
X\to{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})
that is unique up to a contractible space of choices. Indeed, there is a contractible space of Real embeddings
V\hookrightarrow X\times{\mathcal{H}}
over
X
, realizing
V
as a summand in the trivial Real Hilbert bundle over
X
, so
X\times{\mathcal{H}}\simeq V\oplus V^{\perp}
. With respect to this decomposition, define a Real Fredholm operator
F=0_{V}\oplus{{\rm id}}_{V^{\perp}}
whose kernel is
V
.

4.2.
Twisted KR-theory

Definition 4.4

(Twisted KR, KO, and KU)
.

For a Real local quotient stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
and a Real map
\tau\colon{\mathfrak{X}}\to{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})
, define the
\tau
-twisted KR-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
as the homotopy classes of Real (i.e.,
C_{2}
-invariant) sections

(75)

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}}):=\pi_{0}(\Gamma({\mathfrak{X}};\tau^{*}{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}))^{C_{2}}).

The
\tau
-twisted
{\rm KO}
-theory
of a local quotient stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
is the
{\rm KR}
-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
for the trivial Real structure, i.e., trivial
C_{2}
-action,

{\rm KO}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}}):={\rm KR}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}})=\pi_{0}(\Gamma({\mathfrak{X}};\tau^{*}{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}))^{C_{2}}).

For a local quotient stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
and a map
\tau\colon{\mathfrak{X}}\to{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})
, define the
\tau
-twisted KU-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
as the homotopy classes of sections

{\rm KU}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}}):=\pi_{0}(\Gamma({\mathfrak{X}};\tau^{*}{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}))),

i.e., the same as (
75
) but without taking Real (
C_{2}
-invariant) sections.

Remark 4.5
.

We remind that spaces of sections as in (
75
) can be computed relative to any groupoid presentation of
{\mathfrak{X}}
, see (
79
) below.

Lemma 4.6
.

A class in the
\tau
-twisted KR-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
is represented by a Real map,

(76)

\displaystyle F\colon{\mathfrak{X}}\to{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})

where the twist
\tau
is the post-composition with the projection to
{*}/\!\!/{\rm PU}^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})
. For a stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
(without specified Real structure), a class in the
\tau
-twisted KU-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a map (
76
) (without
C_{2}
-equivariance data). Similarly, a class in the
\tau
-twisted KO-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
is represented by a Real map (
76
) for the trivial
C_{2}
-action on
{\mathfrak{X}}
, which is equivalent to a map of stacks

(77)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{X}}\to{\rm Fred}_{{\rm Cl}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}})/\!\!/{\rm PO}^{\pm}_{{\rm Cl}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}).

for
{\rm Fred}_{{\rm Cl}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}})
the set of Fredholm operators on the real
{\rm Cl}_{p,q}
-module
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}:={\mathcal{H}}^{C_{2}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

The claims that identify a section of a bundle of Fredholm operators with a map of stacks follow directly from the universal property of pullbacks. It remains to verify that (
76
) reduces to (
77
) for the trivial
C_{2}
-action on
{\mathfrak{X}}
. Consider the diagram

(78)

{\mathfrak{X}}^{C_{2}}
({\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}))^{C_{2}}
{\mathfrak{X}}
{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})
\simeq

that includes the
C_{2}
-fixed points. The vertical equivalence on the left follows from the
C_{2}
-action on
{\mathfrak{X}}
being trivial. The claim then follows after identifying the (homotopy)
C_{2}
-fixed points in the upper right of (

) with the stack underlying the (strict)
C_{2}
-fixed points in the presenting groupoid
[

({\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}))^{C_{2}}\simeq{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}})^{C_{2}}/\!\!/{\rm PU}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}})^{C_{2}}\simeq{\rm Fred}_{\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{p,q}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}})/\!\!/{\rm PO}_{{\rm Cl}_{p,q}}^{\pm}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}).

By the direct computation above we obtain the claimed stack: the

Definition 4.7
.

The
external product
of twisted K-groups

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}})\times{\rm KR}^{\tau^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\boxtimes}

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau\boxtimes\tau^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{X}}\times{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime})

is induced by the tensor product of Fredholm operators (

\displaystyle{\rm KR}^{\tau}({\mathfrak{X}})\times{\rm KR}^{\tau^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{X}})\xrightarrow{\boxtimes}{\rm KR}^{\tau\boxtimes\tau^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{X}}\times{\mathfrak{X}})\xrightarrow{\Delta^{*}}{\rm KR}^{\tau\otimes\tau^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{X}})

the diagonal.

Remark 4.8
.

The twisted K-groups in Definition
4.4
are defined relative to a specific twisted Hilbert bundle specified by
\tau
. This differs from the formalism from
[
FHT11a
]
where the twist is labeled by a graded gerbe. These approaches are equivalent if one always chooses a locally absorbing twisted Hilbert bundle from Proposition

, see also
[
FHT11a
, Remark 3.17]
.

Notation 4.9
.

For a Real stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
, let
{\rm KR}^{p,q}({\mathfrak{X}})
denote the twisted KR-theory of
{\mathfrak{X}}
for the locally absorbing twist classified by the map

Remark 4.10
.

In the existing literature, it is perhaps more common to use additive notation for the tensor product of twists (
68
) in Definition
4.7
. This is consistent with the binary operation on the group

that classifies gerbes and their corresponding twists. For our purposes, we prefer the tensor product notation, as it emphasizes that the product of twists is inherited from the tensor product of Hilbert bundles and Fredholm operators. Alas, our choice leads to the somewhat awkward formula

for the degree twistings above inherited from the isomorphism (

A.3.
Symmetric powers of
G
-stacks

Definition A.15
.

The
k^{\rm th}
symmetric power
of a topological stack

is

{\rm Sym}^{k}({\mathfrak{X}}):={\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k}.

This extends to a functor
{\rm Sym}^{k}\colon{\sf TopStack}\to{\sf TopStack}/({*}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k})
from stacks to over
{*}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k}
.

Lemma A.16
.

For
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map of stacks

{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times j}/\!\!/\Sigma_{j}\times{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k}\simeq({\mathfrak{X}}^{\times(j+k)})/\!\!/(\Sigma_{j}\times\Sigma_{k})\to({\mathfrak{X}}^{\times(j+k)})/\!\!/\Sigma_{j+k}

induced by the inclusion of groups
\Sigma_{j}\times\Sigma_{k}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{j+k}
determines a natural transformation

(128)

\displaystyle\alpha_{j,k}\colon{\rm Sym}^{j}(-)\times{\rm Sym}^{k}(-)\to{\rm Sym}^{j+k}(-)

between functors from stacks to stacks over
{*}/\!\!/\Sigma_{j+k}
.

Lemma A.17
.

For
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the map of stacks

({\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k})^{\times j}/\!\!/\Sigma_{j}\simeq{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times jk}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k}\wr\Sigma_{j}\to{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times jk}/\!\!/\Sigma_{jk}

induced by the inclusion of groups
\Sigma_{k}\wr\Sigma_{j}=(\Sigma_{k}^{\times j})\rtimes\Sigma_{j}\subseteq\Sigma_{jk}
determines a natural transformation

(129)

\displaystyle\beta_{j,k}\colon{\rm Sym}^{j}({\rm Sym}^{k}(-))\to{\rm Sym}^{jk}(-)

between functors from stacks to stacks over
{*}/\!\!/\Sigma_{jk}
.

We omit the (straightforward) proofs of the above.

Example A.18
.

For a global quotient stack
X/\!\!/G
, there is an equivalence of topological stacks
{\rm Sym}^{k}(X/\!\!/G)\simeq X^{\times k}/\!\!/G\wr\Sigma_{k}
for the action of the wreath product

on

. We recall the homomorphism of groups

G\times\Sigma_{k}\xhookrightarrow{\Delta\times{{\rm id}}_{\Sigma_{k}}}G^{\times k}\rtimes\Sigma_{k}\simeq G\wr\Sigma_{k}

induced by the diagonal
G\hookrightarrow G^{\times k}
. This gives a map

(X^{\times k}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k})/\!\!/G\simeq X^{\times k}/\!\!/(\Sigma_{k}\times G)\to{\rm Sym}^{k}(X/\!\!/G)

Example A.19
.

More generally, for a
G
-stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
, there is a map

({\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/\Sigma_{k})/\!\!/G\simeq{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/(\Sigma_{k}\times G)\to{\mathfrak{X}}^{\times k}/\!\!/G\wr\Sigma_{k}\simeq{\rm Sym}^{k}({\mathfrak{X}}/\!\!/G).

The quotient on the left gives

the structure of a
G
-stack, which maps to the symmetric power

of the

-quotient.

Definition A.20
.

The
k^{\rm th}

is the stack

with the diagonal

A.4.
Real graded gerbes

There are several equivalent approaches to gerbes on topological stacks, all tracing back to
[
Gir71
]
. Perhaps the most practical point of view treats gerbes as central extensions of topological groupoids, e.g., see
[
FHT11a
, §2.2]
. On the other hand, the global geometry of gerbes and the larger bicategory in which they reside is most transparent in the language of higher categorical principal bundles, e.g., see
[
Gaj97
, §A]
and
[
Moe02
]
. The literature on this subject is vast, and we do not give exhaustive references below.
However, for the reader’s convenience we indicate some of the proofs.

We begin with the basic notions. Multiplication on
{\rm U}(1)
makes
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
an abelian group object in stacks, yielding a bicategory of stacks with
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-action
[
SP11
, §3.3]
.

Definition A.21
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a topological stack. A (bundle)
gerbe
over
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-principal bundle
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\to{\mathfrak{X}}
, i.e.,
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}
is equipped with a
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-action and there exists an atlas
u\colon X\to{\mathfrak{X}}
with a
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-equivariant equivalence
u^{*}\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\simeq X\times{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
over
X
.

For a local quotient stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
and a gerbe
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\to{\mathfrak{X}}
, the stack
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}
is again a local quotient stack
[
Hei05
, Corollary 6.3]
. Gerbes are the objects of a symmetric monoidal 2-stack
[
NS11
, §4.1]
; in particular for each stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
there is a symmetric monoidal 2-groupoid
{\rm Gerbe}_{\mathfrak{X}}
and a map of stacks
{\mathfrak{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
determines a (weak) pullback 2-functor
{\rm Gerbe}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\rm Gerbe}_{\mathfrak{X}}
.

Next we observe that
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
is an abelian group object in Real stacks for the Real structure on
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
generated by complex conjugation on
{\rm U}(1)
.

Definition A.22
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a Real stack. A
Real gerbe
over
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a Real stack
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}
and a Real map
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\to{\mathfrak{X}}
that (after forgetting the Real structure) is a gerbe over
{\mathfrak{X}}
and with the property that on a Real atlas
X\to{\mathfrak{X}}
, the
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-equivariant isomorphism
u^{*}\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\simeq X\times{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
is furthermore a Real isomorphism for the Real structure on
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}
, the Real structure on
{\mathfrak{X}}
, and the Real structure on
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
described above.

Definition A.23
.

A
grading
for a topological stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a map of stacks
{\mathfrak{X}}\to{*}/\!\!/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)
.

We observe that a grading is the same data as a metrized real line bundle on
{\mathfrak{X}}
.

Definition A.24
.

A
graded gerbe
over a stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a grading on
{\mathfrak{X}}
and a
{\rm U}(1)
-bundle gerbe

(130)

\displaystyle{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)\to\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\to{\mathfrak{X}},\qquad{\mathfrak{X}}\to{*}/\!\!/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)

A
Real graded gerbe
over a
C_{2}
-stack

is a graded gerbe (
130
) in
C_{2}
-stacks:
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
carries a
C_{2}
-action from complex conjugation,
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}
is endowed with a
C_{2}
-action, the maps in (

C_{2}
-equviariant, and the grading
{\mathfrak{X}}\to{*}/\!\!/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)
is
C_{2}
-equivariant for the trivial
C_{2}
-action on
{*}/\!\!/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)

Real graded gerbes are the objects of 2-stack with a forgetful functor to stacks. The 1-morphisms are Real
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)
-equivariant maps
\widehat{{\mathfrak{X}}}\to\widehat{{\mathcal{Y}}}
covering a Real map
{\mathfrak{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
of stacks and compatible with the gradings.

Definition A.25
.

The
trivial
Real graded gerbe over a Real stack

is the Real stack
{*}/\!\!/{\rm U}(1)\times{\mathfrak{X}}
with diagonal Real structure and grading given by the trivial map to

Given a Real gerbe

be a Real atlas determining a groupoid presentation
{\mathbb{X}}
of a Real topological stack
{\mathfrak{X}}
. There is a natural equivalence of the following 2-groupoids:

(1)

Real graded central extensions of
{\mathbb{X}}
; and

(2)

Real graded gerbes that trivialize on
{\mathbb{X}}_{0}\to{\mathfrak{X}}
.

Furthermore, the monoidal structure on Real graded gerbes is compatible with the tensor product of Real graded central extensions.

Proof sketch.

The statement without gradings is well-known, e.g.,
[
Hei05
, Remark 5.5]
or
[
BX06
, Proposition 4.12]
; adding Real and graded structures presents no new difficulties.
∎

Remark A.31
.

We note that complex conjugation on
{\rm U}(1)
sends a graded central extension to its dual, in the sense that the
{\rm U}(1)
-principal bundles are dual to each other. Hence, a Real structure on a central extension (or gerbe) can be described in terms of an involutive isomorphism with its dual. This gives the description of Real gerbes as
Jandl gerbes
, e.g., see
[
GSW11
, §2.3]
,
[
Wal07
, §3.3]
and
[
NS11
, §4.2]
.

Remark A.32
.

Definitions of Real (or Jandl, or orientifold) gerbes in the literature vary in two essentially superficial ways. First, the 2-category of
C_{2}
-objects in topological stacks (where actions are weak) is equivalent a localization of the 2-category of topological groupoids with strict
C_{2}
-actions
[
Rom05
, Proposition 1.5]
. This allows one to specify either weak or strict equivariance data in the definition of a Real gerbe over a topological groupoid. Unless stated otherwise, we assume that
C_{2}
-actions are weak. The second variation in the definitions of Real gerbes uses the equivalence of 2-categories between
C_{2}
-stacks and stacks over
{*}/\!\!/C_{2}
, where the latter leads to the definition of an orientifold, see §
A.2
and Remarks
3.5
and
A.26
. Jandl gerbes and orientifolds were shown to be equivalent as 2-stacks in
[
NS11
, Corollary A.2.4]
.

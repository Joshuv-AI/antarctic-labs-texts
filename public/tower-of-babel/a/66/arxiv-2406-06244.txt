Abstract
. This paper proposes hybrid high-order eigensolvers for the computation of guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds. These bounds display higher order convergence rates and are accessible to adaptive mesh-refining algorithms. The involved constants arise from local embeddings and are available for all polynomial degrees. A wide range of applications is possible including the linear elasticity and Steklov eigenvalue problem.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Guaranteed error control for the approximation of eigenvalues associated with positive definite
partial differential equations requires upper and lower bounds of the exact eigenvalue
\lambda(j)
. While upper bounds can be achieved by conforming methods using the Rayleigh-Ritz principle, lower bounds are more challenging to obtain.
Lower bounds with nonconforming methods under sufficiently small mesh-size conditions can be derived from the asymptotic analysis of, e.g.,
[
36
,
32
]
.
This paper derives guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds (GLB) without any condition on the mesh.

Using the nonconforming Crouzeix-Raviart finite element method (FEM),
[
8
]
derived GLB for the Laplace eigenvalue problem, with extension to the Morley-FEM for the biharmonic eigenvalue problem in
[
7
]
. Both contributions
[
8
,
7
]
required a separation condition for higher eigenvalues, which is, in fact, not necessary as observed in
[
30
]
.
At a similar time to
[
8
]
,
[
31
]
derived GLB for the Laplace eigenvalue problem using conforming finite elements via the hyper-circle principle.
While additional computational effort is required to compute the explicit constants in the GLB, this approach is more general and can be applied to, e.g., the Maxwell and Steklov eigenvalue problems
[
37
,
28
]
.
Recently, mixed methods
[
27
]
provide GLB for a large range of examples, where the involved constants are independent of the explicit discretization.
The bounds of all aforementioned methods are postprocessings of the computed eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}(j)
of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{h}(j)}{1+\delta\lambda_{h}(j)}\leq\lambda(j)

for the
j
-th exact eigenvalue.
They are restricted to lowest-order convergence because, in practice, the parameter
\delta
has a fixed scaling in terms of maximal mesh-size arising from some local embeddings. Thus, higher-order methods cannot provide any improvement and uniform mesh-refining algorithms may even outperform adaptive computations in certain examples on nonconvex domains
[
12
]
.

A remedy is
[
14
]
with a hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) eigensolver plus the Lehrenfeld-Schöberl stabilization, where the computed eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
is a direct GLB of
\lambda(j)
(without further postprocessing) under an explicit assumption on the maximal mesh-size.
This makes the bound accessible to higher-order discretizations and adaptivity.
The method of
[
14
]
, the hybridizable methods
[
6
,
10
]
mentioned below, and the proposed method of this paper can also be interpreted as an HDG
[
18
]
, a weak Galerkin (WG)
[
35
]
, or an hybrid high-order (HHO) method
[
23
,
22
]
.
In fact, WG methods can be embedded into the HDG methodology
[
16
, Section 6.6]
and the reconstructions therein have close relationship to the HHO methodology. We also refer to

[
17
]
for explicit links between HDG and HHO methods and the monograph
[
15
]
for further discussions on the connection between HHO and WG methods.

The HHO methods of
[
6
,
10
]
utilize multiple discrete spaces to reconstruct the gradient. As a result, an additional selectable parameter allows for more flexibility and lead, in numerical experiments, to improved GLB
[
9
]
with
p
-robust constants in
[
10
]
.
Optimal convergence rates of adaptive mesh-refining algorithms can be proven for a related lowest-order extra-stabilized Crouzeix-Raviart FEM
[
11
]
.
However,
[
14
,
6
,
10
]
are only concerned with the Laplace eigenvalue problem and the involved constants therein are
merely
guaranteed for lowest-order discretizations.

This paper exploits the fact that the Poincaré constant is explicitly known in convex sets. The degrees of freedom associated with the kernel of
\nabla
can be locally eliminated in the definition of the potential reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}
in the HHO methodology.
Therefore, the HHO methods of this paper only utilize the single reconstruction operator
\mathcal{R}_{h}
. This allows for guaranteed constants independent of the polynomial degree in the GLB below.
As an extra benefit, hanging nodes are allowed as long as the mesh is partitioned into convex cells for additional flexibility in the mesh design.
Since the presented approach relies on intrinsic features of the HHO methodology, it applies to a wide range of examples including linear elasticity and Steklov eigenvalue problems.
To be precise, we identify
three abstract conditions for the GLB

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GLB}(j)\coloneqq\min\{1,1/(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\}\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)

from the observations of
[
14
]

with the convention
\mathrm{GLB}(j)\coloneqq 0
for
\lambda_{h}(j)=\infty
.
In practice,
\alpha<1
is a chosen parameter and
\beta
is an explicit constant with a positive scaling of the maximal mesh-size. Therefore,
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
eventually holds whenever
\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)<1
.

While discretizations into nonconvex cells are certainly allowed, the embedding constants required in GLB are rather inaccurate. Accurate guaranteed upper bounds can be computed using the HHO eigensolvers of this paper with additional computational effort.

As the focus is on GLB, the convergence of the eigensolver is not explicitly proven. However, we mention that a priori error estimates plus elliptic regularity for the source problem allow for the spectral correctness of HHO eigensolvers
[
5
]
. The arguments of
[
5
]
are applicable to almost all examples of this paper as briefly outlined in the respective sections.

1.2.
Setting

For the sake of brevity, we first consider the Laplace eigenvalue problem but mention that the extension to other eigenvalue problems follow in later sections.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain in two or three space dimensions
n=2,3
.
The Laplace eigenvalue problem seeks eigenpairs
(\lambda,u)
with

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

The variational formulation of this reads

(1.3)

\displaystyle a(u,v)=\lambda b(u,v)\quad\text{for any }v\in V

with
V\coloneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
a(u,v)=(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, and
b(u,v)=(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
The bilinear forms
a,b
induce the norms
\|\bullet\|_{\mathrm{a}}
and
\|\bullet\|_{\mathrm{b}}
in
V
.
The finite eigenvalues
0<\lambda(1)\leq\lambda(2)\leq\dots<\infty
of (
1.3
) are given by the Rayleigh-Ritz principle

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda(j)=\min_{W\subset V,\mathrm{dim}(W)=j}\max_{v\in W\setminus\{0\}}\|v\|_{a}^{2}/\|v\|_{b}^{2}

with
the corresponding eigenvectors
u(1),u(2),\dots
, that are subject to the normalization
\|u(j)\|_{b}=1
and satisfy the orthogonality

(1.5)

\displaystyle a(u(j),u(k))=b(u(j),u(k))=0\quad\text{for any }j\neq k.

2.
HHO eigensolver

This section introduces an HHO eigensolver for the Laplace eigenvalue problem of
Section

1.2
.

2.1.
Polytopal mesh

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite collection of closed
convex
nonempty polyhedra with overlap of volume measure zero that cover
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}K
.
A side
S
of the mesh
\mathcal{M}
is a closed connected polyhedral subset of a hyperplane
H_{S}
with positive
(n-1)
-dimensional surface measure such that either (a) there exist
K_{+},K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap K_{-}
(interior side) or (b) there exists
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap\partial\Omega
(boundary sides).
Let
\Sigma
be a finite collection of sides with overlap of
(n-1)
-dimensional surface measure zero that covers the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\partial K=\cup_{S\in\Sigma}S
.
The normal vector
\nu_{S}
of an interior side
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
is fixed beforehand and set
\nu_{S}\coloneqq\nu|_{S}
for boundary sides
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
.
For
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
,
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
(resp.
K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
) denotes the unique cell with
S\subset\partial K_{+}
(resp.
S\subset\partial K_{-}
) and
\nu_{K_{+}}|_{S}=\nu_{S}
(resp.
\nu_{K_{-}}|_{S}=-\nu_{S}
).
The jump
[v]_{S}
of any function
v\in W^{1,1}(\mathrm{int}(T_{+}\cup T_{-});\mathbb{R}^{m})
along
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
is defined by
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{K_{+}}-v|_{K_{-}}\in L^{1}(S;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
If
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
, then
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{S}
.

The set
V_{\mathrm{nc}}\coloneqq H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
is the space of all piecewise
H^{1}
function with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{M}
.
The notation
\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}
denotes the piecewise application of
\nabla
without explicit reference to
\mathcal{M}
and analogously applies to other differential operators as well.
The seminorm
\|\bullet\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}
in
V_{\mathrm{nc}}
is induced by the positive semidefinite bilinear form
a_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{\mathrm{nc}},v_{\mathrm{nc}})\coloneqq(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}u_{\mathrm{nc}},\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{\mathrm{nc}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in
V_{\mathrm{nc}}
.

2.2.
Finite element spaces

Given a subset
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of diameter
h_{M}
, let
P_{k}(M)
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
.
For any
v\in L^{1}(M)
,
\Pi_{M}^{k}v\in P_{k}(M)
denotes the
L^{2}
projection of
v
onto
P_{k}(M)
. The space of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k
with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{M}
or the sides
\Sigma
is denoted by
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
or
P_{k}(\Sigma)
.
Given
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
(resp.
v\in L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{M})
), define the
L^{2}
projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v
(resp.
\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}v
) of
v
onto
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
(resp.
P_{k}(\Sigma)
) by
(\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v)|_{K}=\Pi_{K}^{k}v|_{K}
in any cell
K\in\mathcal{M}
(resp.
(\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}v)|_{S}=\Pi_{S}^{k}v
along any side
S\in\Sigma
).
The mesh
\mathcal{M}
gives rise to the piecewise constant function
h_{\mathcal{M}}\in P_{0}(\mathcal{M})
with
h_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}=h_{K}
for any
K\in\mathcal{M}
;
h_{\max}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}h_{K}
is the maximal mesh-size of
\mathcal{M}
.

2.3.
Discretization

This subsection presents the HHO eigensolver for the computation of GLB.

Discrete ansatz spaces.

Given a fixed
k\geq 0
, let
V_{h}\coloneqq P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))
denote the discrete ansatz space for
V
.
In this definition,
P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))
is the subspace of
P_{k}(\Sigma)
with the convention
v_{\Sigma}\in P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))
if
v_{\Sigma}|_{S}\equiv 0
on boundary sides
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
to model homogenous Dirichlet boundary condition.
For any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
, we use the notation
v_{K}\coloneqq v_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}
and
v_{S}\coloneqq v_{\Sigma}|_{S}
to abbreviate the restriction of
v_{\mathcal{M}}
in a cell
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
v_{\Sigma}
along a side
S\in\Sigma
.
The interpolation operator
\mathrm{I}_{h}:V\to V_{h}
maps
v\in V
to
\mathrm{I}_{h}v\coloneqq(\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k+1}v,\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k}v)\in V_{h}
.

Potential reconstruction.

Given
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
, the potential reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\in W_{\mathrm{nc}}
satisfies, for any
\varphi_{k+1}\in W_{\mathrm{nc}}
, that

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h},\varphi_{k+1})=-(v_{\mathcal{M}},\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k+1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{S\in\Sigma}(v_{S},[\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k+1}]_{S}\cdot\nu_{S})_{L^{2}(S)}.

This defines
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}
uniquely up to piecewise constants, which is fixed by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{K}v_{K}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any }K\in\mathcal{M}.

Stabilization.

Given a convex polyhedra
K\in\mathcal{M}
of diameter
h_{K}
, let
x_{K}
denote the midpoint of
K
. For any side
S\in\Sigma(K)
of
K
,
K_{S}\coloneqq\mathrm{conv}\{x_{K},S\}\subset K
is the convex hull of
S
and
x_{K}
.
We define the weight

(2.3)

\displaystyle\ell(S,K)\coloneqq|S|h_{K}^{2}/|K_{S}|.

Notice the scaling
\ell(S,K)\approx h_{K}
for admissible meshes (cf.
[
21
, Definition 1.38]
for a precise definition).
For a positive parameter
\sigma>0
, the local stabilization
\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h},v_{h})
reads,
for any
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma}),v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sigma h_{K}^{-2}(u_{K}-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h},v_{K}-\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(K)}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}(\Pi_{S}^{k}(u_{S}-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}|_{K}),v_{S}-\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}|_{K})_{L^{2}(S)}

with
\ell(S,K)
from (
2.3
) and the global version

\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h},v_{h}).

A similar stabilization has been proposed (and analysed) in
[
20
, Example 2.8]
for the discrete ansatz space
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma(\Omega))
, however we note that it is not sufficient for stability for the present ansatz space.

Discrete eigenvalue problem.

We seek discrete eigenpairs
(\lambda_{h},u_{h})
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

with the bilinear forms

(2.6)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h},\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h})\quad\text{and}\quad b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(u_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\mathcal{M}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for any
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma}),v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
.
Similar to the continuous level, the discrete eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{h}(1)\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{h}(N)\leq\infty
with
N\coloneqq\mathrm{dim}\,V_{h}
satisfy the Rayleigh-Ritz principle

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(j)=\min_{W_{h}\subset V_{h},\mathrm{dim}(W_{h})=j}\max_{v_{h}\in W_{h}\setminus\{0\}}(\|v_{h}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2})/\|v_{h}\|_{b_{h}}^{2}.

Lemma 2.1

(coercivity)
.

The bilinear form
a_{h}+\mathrm{s}_{h}
is a scalar product in
V_{h}
.
In particular, there are
\mathrm{dim}(P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M}))
finite discrete eigenvalues of (
2.5
).∎

Proof.

Let
\|v_{h}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}=0
for some
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
.
From
\|v_{h}\|_{a_{h}}=0
, we infer

\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}=0
and so,
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}
is piecewise constant.
Since
\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}=0
, the traces of
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}
coincide with
v_{\Sigma}
and so,
v_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}
is a constant function with homogenous boundary condition. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}=0
and
v_{h}=0
.
∎

3.
Guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds

This section establishes the GLB (
1.1
).

3.1.
Key properties

First, three key properties essential for GLB are highlighted. They also hold for other eigenvalue problems as well.

Let
\mathrm{G}_{h}:V_{\mathrm{nc}}\to W_{\mathrm{nc}}
denote the Galerkin projection, defined, for given
v\in V_{\mathrm{nc}}
, as the unique solution to

(3.1)

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathrm{G}_{h}v,\varphi_{\mathrm{nc}})=a_{\mathrm{pw}}(v,\varphi_{\mathrm{nc}})\quad\text{for any }\varphi_{\mathrm{nc}}\in W_{\mathrm{nc}}

with the integral mean property

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathrm{G}_{h}v\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{K}v\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any }K\in\mathcal{M}.

A characteristic feature of the HHO methodology is the connection

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h}\circ\mathrm{I}_{h}=\mathrm{G}_{h}\quad\text{in }V

between
\mathcal{R}_{h}
and
\mathrm{G}_{h}
.
Indeed, (
3.3
) follows from the orthogonality
v-\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}v\perp_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}W_{\mathrm{nc}}

[
23
, Eq. (18)]
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{h}v=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}v
in (
2.2
).
From (
3.1
), we infer the orthogonality
v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\perp_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}W_{\mathrm{nc}}
. Together with (
3.3
), this shows

(A)
a
-orthogonality

\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{a_{h}}^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{a}-\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}

for any
v\in V
(even with equality for the Laplace eigenvalue problem at hand).
In particular,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}\leq\|v\|_{a}.

The quasi-best approximation property

(B)
scaling of stabilization

\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}\leq\alpha\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}

of the stabilization with a positive constant
\alpha
is utilized in many contributions
[
25
,
2
,
13
]
.
An explicit upper bound for
\alpha
can be computed using the following result.

Lemma 3.1

(trace inequality)
.

Any
v\in H^{1}(\mathrm{int}(K))
with
\int_{K}v\,\mathrm{d}x=0
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\leq c_{\mathrm{tr}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

with the constant
c_{\mathrm{tr}}\coloneqq 1/\pi^{2}+2/(n\pi)
.

Proof.

Given
S\in\Sigma(K)
, the classical trace identity implies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S)}\leq\frac{|S|}{|K_{S}|}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K_{S})}^{2}+\frac{2h_{K_{S}}|S|}{n|K_{S}|}\int_{K_{S}}|v||\nabla v|\,\mathrm{d}x

cf., e.g.,
[
27
, proof of Lemma 7.2]
.
The sum of this over all
S\in\Sigma(K)
with the notation
\ell(S,K)
from (
2.3
) and
h_{K_{S}}/h_{K}\leq 1
establish

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\leq h_{K}^{-2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+2h_{K}^{-1}n^{-1}\int_{K}|v||\nabla v|\,\mathrm{d}x.

This, the Hölder, and Poincaré inequality conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2

(simplicial cells)
.

If
K
is a simplex, then
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
provides an improved version of
Lemma

3.1
.

For any
v\in V
,
the best approximation property of
L^{2}
projections and (
3.3
) provide the upper bound

\displaystyle\sigma h_{K}^{-2}\|\Pi_{K}^{k+1}(v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\sigma\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}\|\Pi_{S}^{k}(v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v|_{K})\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma h_{K}^{-2}\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\sigma\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v|_{K}\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}

for the stabilization
\mathrm{s}_{K}(\mathrm{I}_{h}v,\mathrm{I}_{h}v)
. This, the Poincaré, and trace inequality from
Lemma

3.1
establish
(B)
with the constant

\displaystyle\alpha\coloneqq\sigma/\pi^{2}+\sigma c_{\mathrm{tr}}.

Note that
\alpha
is independent of the polynomial degree
k
.
The final ingredient towards GLB involves the bound
\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}
with
\beta\leq h_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}/\pi^{2}
from a Poincaré inequality
[
1
]
.
This, the Pythagoras theorem
\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|^{2}_{b_{h}}=\|v\|_{b}^{2}-\|v-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k+1}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, and
\|v-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k+1}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
allow for

(C)
b
-orthogonality

\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{b_{h}}^{2}\geq\|v\|_{b}^{2}-\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}
.

3.2.
Main result

We recall the result mentioned in the introduction.

Theorem 3.3

(GLB for Laplace)
.

The bound (
1.1
) holds for any
j=1,\dots,N
with the constants
\alpha\coloneqq\sigma/\pi^{2}+\sigma c_{\mathrm{tr}}
and
\beta\coloneqq h_{\max}^{2}/\pi^{2}
. In particular,

\mathrm{GLB}(j)\coloneqq\min\{1,1/(\sigma/\pi^{2}+\sigma c_{\mathrm{tr}}+h_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\lambda_{h}(j)/\pi^{2})\}\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j).

The proofs in this section follow
[
14
]
and are outlined for the sake of completeness. The following result is required for the proof of
Theorem

3.3
.
Define the finite dimensional subspace
W\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\{u(1),\dots,u(j)\}
of
V
.

Lemma 3.4

(linear independency)
.

If
\beta\lambda(j)<1
, then (a)–(b) hold.

(a)

The interpolations
\mathrm{I}_{h}u(1)
,
\dots
,
\mathrm{I}_{h}u(j)
of the exact eigenvectors
u(1),\dots,u(j)
are linearly independent in
V_{h}
.

(b)

Suppose additionally
\lambda_{h}(j)<\infty
, then there exists
v\in W
with
\|v\|_{b}=1
and

\displaystyle(1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j).

Proof of (a).

Suppose otherwise, then there are real numbers
t_{1},\dots,t_{j}
such that
v\coloneqq t_{1}u(1)+\dots+t_{j}u(j)
satisfies
\|v\|_{b}=1
but
\mathrm{I}_{h}v=0
.
Elementary algebra with the orthogonality in (
1.5
) proves

1=\|v\|_{b}^{2}=t_{1}^{2}\|u(1)\|_{b}^{2}+\dots t_{j}^{2}\|u(j)\|_{b}=t_{1}^{2}+\dots+t_{j}^{2}
and therefore,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{a}^{2}=t_{1}^{2}\|u(1)\|^{2}_{a}+\dots+t_{j}^{2}\|u(j)\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{2}\lambda(1)+\dots+t_{j}^{2}\lambda(j)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq(t_{1}^{2}+\dots+t_{j}^{2})\lambda(j)=\lambda(j).

The combination of this with
(C)
and (
3.4
) results in

(3.8)

\displaystyle 1=\|v\|_{b}^{2}\leq\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\beta\|v\|^{2}_{a}\leq\beta\lambda(j);

a contradiction to
\beta\lambda(j)<1
.

Proof of (b)

Since the interpolations
\mathrm{I}_{h}u(1),\dots,\mathrm{I}_{h}u(j)
are linearly independent from (a),
\mathrm{I}_{h}W\subset V_{h}
is a
j
-dimensional subspace of
V_{h}
.
The Rayleigh-Ritz principle (
2.7
) on the discrete level shows

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\max_{v_{h}\in\mathrm{I}_{h}W\setminus\{0\}}(\|v_{h}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2})/\|v_{h}\|_{b_{h}}^{2}.

Let
v\in W\setminus\{0\}
with
\|v\|_{b}=1
be given such that
\mathrm{I}_{h}v
is a maximizer of this Rayleigh quotient (which can attain the value
+\infty
).
Then

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(j)\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{b_{h}}^{2}\leq\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}.

From
(A)
and
\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)
in (
3.7
), we deduce that

\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{a_{h}}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)-\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}
. This and
(B)
imply that the right-hand side of (
3.9
) is bounded by
\lambda(j)-(1-\alpha)\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}
, while
\lambda_{h}(j)(1-\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2})
is a lower bound for the left-hand side of (
3.9
) thanks to
(C)
and
\|v\|_{b}=1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(j)(1-\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2})\leq\lambda(j)-(1-\alpha)\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}.

Reorganizing these terms concludes the proof of (b).
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Fix
1\leq j\leq N
.
Recall the convention
\mathrm{GLB}(j)\coloneqq 0
for
\lambda_{h}(j)=\infty
, which is a trivial bound for
\lambda(j)
. Hence, throughout this proof, we assume that
\lambda_{h}(j)<\infty
.
The proof
consists of the two cases (a)
\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\leq 1
and (b)
\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)>1
.

(a)
Suppose that
\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\leq 1
, then the assertion reads

\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
.
If
\beta\lambda(j)\geq 1
, then
\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\leq 1\leq\beta\lambda(j)
, whence
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
.
If
\beta\lambda(j)<1
, then
Lemma

3.4
(b) and
1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\geq 0
imply
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
.

(b)
Suppose that
\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j)>1
, then the claim reads
\lambda_{h}(j)/(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\leq\lambda(j)
.
If
\beta\lambda(j)<1
, then
Lemma

3.4
(b) implies the existence of a
v\in W
with
\|v\|_{b}=1
and
(1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)
. The normalization
\|v\|_{b}=1
provides
\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)
in (
3.7
) and so,
\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)
from (
3.4
). This and
1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j)<0
yield

\displaystyle(1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\lambda(j)\leq(1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j),

whence
\lambda_{h}(j)/(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\leq\lambda(j)
. If
1\leq\beta\lambda(j)
, then
\lambda_{h}(j)/(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{h}(j))\leq 1/\beta\leq\lambda(j)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

As in
[
14
,
6
,
2
]
, the conditions
(A)
–
(C)
allow for GLB from a priori information on the exact eigenvalue.

Corollary 3.5

(a priori GLB)
.

If
\alpha+\beta\lambda(j)\leq 1
and
\lambda_{h}(j)<\infty
, then
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
.

Proof.

The bound
\alpha+\beta\lambda(j)\leq 1
implies

1-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\geq\alpha+\beta\lambda(j)-\alpha-\beta\lambda_{h}(j)\geq\beta(\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j))
.
Since
\beta\lambda(j)<1
,
Lemma

3.4
(b) holds and therefore,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\beta(\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j))\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)

for some
v\in V
with
\|v\|_{b}=1
.
Recall
\beta\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}\leq\beta\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq\beta\lambda(j)

from (
3.4
) and (
3.7
).
The combination of this with (
3.10
) results in

\displaystyle\beta\lambda(j)(\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j))\leq\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j).

This inequality can only hold if
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda(j)
because
\beta\lambda(j)<1
.
∎

3.3

. Thus, an extension of

3.3
to other eigenvalue problems is straightforward by verifying these conditions in

3.3.
Numerical example

The computer experiments throughout this paper are carried out on regular triangulation into simplices.
Some general remarks on the realization precede the numerical results.

3.3.1.
Displayed quantities

The convergence history plots display the error
\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathrm{GLB}(j)
against the number of degrees of freedom
\mathrm{ndof}
in a log-log plot, where the upper bound
\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
is the Rayleigh quotient of some conforming function
u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\in P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M})\cap V
.
If
j=1
, then
u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
is the scaled nodal average of
\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)
with
\|u_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{b}=1

and for
j\geq 1
,
u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
can be obtained from
the conforming Galerkin
P_{k+1}
FEM on the same mesh.
At least on simplicial meshes (without hanging nodes),
\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}=\|v_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{a}^{2}
and
\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{b_{h}}^{2}=\|v_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{b}^{2}
holds for any
v_{\mathrm{C}}\in P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M})\cap V
. Therefore, the discrete Rayleigh-Ritz principle (
2.7
) shows
\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
.
In combination with
Theorem

3.3
,
\mathrm{GLB}(j)\leq\{\lambda,\lambda_{h}(j)\}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
and so,
|\lambda(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)|\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathrm{GLB}(j)
.

3.3.2.
Adaptive refinement indicator

If
\mathrm{GLB}(j)=\lambda_{h}(j)
, the error estimator
\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)
can be localized following
[
34
, Lemma 6.3]
. Let
u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)
be given such that
\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)=\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\|_{a}^{2}
with the normalization
\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\|_{b}^{2}=1
and
(u_{\mathrm{C}}(j),u_{\mathcal{M}}(j))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>0
, where
u_{h}(j)=(u_{\mathcal{M}}(j),u_{\Sigma}(j))\in V_{h}
is the computed eigenvector of
\lambda_{h}(j)
.
Elementary algebra with
\|\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}=\lambda_{h}(j)-\|u_{h}(j)\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}
shows

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)=\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\|_{a}^{2}-\lambda_{h}(j)

\displaystyle\quad=\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}+2a_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{\mathrm{C}}(j),\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j))-\|\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}-\lambda_{h}(j)

\displaystyle\quad=\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}+2a_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{\mathrm{C}}(j),\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j))-2\lambda_{h}(j)+\|u_{h}(j)\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}.

The discrete variational formulation (
2.5
) and the commuting property (
3.3
) show

\displaystyle 2a_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{\mathrm{C}}(j),\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j))

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{h}(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\mathrm{C}}(j))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{h}\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-u_{\mathcal{M}}(j)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lambda_{h}(\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\|^{2}_{b}+\|u_{h}(j)\|_{b_{h}}^{2}).

The two previously displayed formula with
\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)\|^{2}_{b}+\|u_{h}(j)\|_{b_{h}}^{2}=2
conclude

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\lambda_{h}(j)\leq\eta\coloneqq\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}+\|u_{h}(j)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}.

Notice that the term
-\lambda_{h}\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-u_{\mathcal{M}}(j)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0
is neglected in the last formula. However, it is expected that
\|u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-u_{\mathcal{M}}(j)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is of higher order due to additional smoothness of the exact eigenvector.
A generalization of (
3.11
) to general polyhedral meshes is possible with the addition of
\|\mathrm{I}_{h}u_{\mathrm{C}}\|_{\mathrm{s}_{h}}^{2}
on the right-hand side of (
3.11
).
The bound (
3.11
)
motivates the local refinement indicator

\displaystyle\eta(K)\coloneqq\|\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}(u_{\mathrm{C}}(j)-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}(j))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h}(j),u_{h}(j)).

This localization is relevant for simple eigenvalues. For the approximation of eigenvalue clusters, we suggest the use of residual-based error estimators, e.g., from
[
10
]
, as in
[
26
]
.

4.
Steklov eigenvalue problem

The Steklov eigenvalue problem seeks eigenpairs
(\lambda,u)
with

\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=0\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\partial u/\partial\nu=\lambda u\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

The weak formulation of this is (
1.3
) with
V\coloneqq H^{1}(\Omega)
,
a(u,v)\coloneqq(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, and
b(u,v)\coloneqq(u,v)_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
.

4.1.
HHO eigensolver

Let
V_{\mathrm{nc}}\coloneqq H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
,
a_{\mathrm{pw}}\coloneqq(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\bullet,\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\bullet)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, and
W_{\mathrm{nc}}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M})
with
k\geq 0
.
We utilize the discrete ansatz space
V_{h}\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M})\times P_{k+1}(\Sigma)
with the interpolation
\mathrm{I}_{h}v\coloneqq(\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v,\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k+1}v)\in V_{h}
for any
v\in V
.
The discrete problem (
2.5
) is defined with the bilinear forms

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h},\nabla_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(u_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\mathcal{M}})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle b_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(u_{\Sigma},v_{\Sigma})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

and the stabilization
\mathrm{s}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h},v_{h})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{K}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sigma h_{K}^{-2}(\Pi_{K}^{k}(u_{K}-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}),v_{K}-\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma\sum_{S\in\Sigma(K)}\ell(S,K)^{-1}(u_{S}-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h}|_{K},v_{S}-\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}|_{K})_{L^{2}(S)},

for any
u_{h}=(u_{\mathcal{M}},u_{\Sigma}),v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{h}
, where the potential reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}
is defined verbatim as in (
2.1
)–(
2.2
). It is straightforward to verify the coercivity of
a_{h}+\mathrm{s}_{h}
as in the proof of
Lemma

2.1
.

Lemma 4.1

(coercivity)
.

The bilinear form
a_{h}+\mathrm{s}_{h}
is a scalar product in
V_{h}
.
In particular, there are
\mathrm{dim}(P_{k+1}(\Sigma(\partial\Omega)))
finite discrete eigenvalues of (
2.5
).∎

Remark 4.2

(spectral correctness)
.

An a priori error analysis of HHO eigensolvers for the Steklov eigenvalue problem has been established in
[
4
]
. The arguments therein apply to the present HHO eigensolver and lead to its spectral correctness.

4.2.
Lower eigenvalue bounds

This subsection verifies
(A)
–
(C)
and derives GLB for the Steklov eigenvalue problem.
The Galerkin projection is defined by (
3.1
)–(
3.2
).
We utilize the following trace inequality. Given a boundary side
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
,
there exists a unique cell
K\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\in\Sigma(K)
.
The convex hull of
S
and a point
x\in K
of largest volume is denoted by
K^{\mathrm{m}}_{S}
.
(Notice that
K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}=K
for simplicial meshes.)
The trace inequality (
3.5
) in
K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}
and a Hölder inequality provide, for any
v\in H^{1}(K)
, that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\leq\frac{|S|}{|K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}|}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{2h_{K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}}|S|}{n|K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}|}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

This motivates the definition of the constant

(4.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{St}}\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{0}}\max_{S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)}\frac{h_{K}|S|}{|K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}|}(h_{K}/\pi^{2}+2h_{K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}}/(n\pi))

utilized in
Corollary

4.3
below, where
c_{0}
is the maximal number of boundary sides belonging to the same cell.

Corollary 4.3

(GLB for Steklov)
.

It holds

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{GLB}(j)\coloneqq\min\{1,1/(\sigma/\pi^{2}+\sigma c_{\mathrm{tr}}+\beta_{\mathrm{St}}\lambda_{h}(j))\}\leq\lambda(j).

Proof.

The property (
3.3
) is independent of boundary data and holds here as well. This, the Pythagoras theorem with the orthogonality
v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\perp_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}W_{\mathrm{nc}}
, and the best approximation property of
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}
imply, for any
v\in V
, that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{a_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}-\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v\|^{2}\leq\|v\|_{a}^{2}-\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2},

which is
(A)
. The same arguments as in (
3.6
) provide
(B)
with
\alpha\coloneqq\sigma/\pi^{2}+\sigma c_{\mathrm{tr}}
.
For any boundary side
S\in\partial\Omega
, let
K
denote the unique cell with
S\in\Sigma(K)
. The Pythagoras theorem and the best approximation property of
L^{2}
projections imply

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{S}^{k+1}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S)}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}-\|v-\Pi_{S}^{k+1}v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}-\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}.

Invoking the trace inequality (
4.1
) and the Poincaré inequality, we infer that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\leq\frac{h_{K}|S|}{|K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}|}(h_{K}/\pi^{2}+2h_{K_{S}^{\mathrm{m}}}/(n\pi))\|\nabla(v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

The combination of the two previously displayed formula with an overlap argument results
in

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{I}_{h}v\|_{b_{h}}^{2}=\|\Pi_{\Sigma}^{k+1}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{b}^{2}-\beta_{\mathrm{St}}\|v-\mathrm{G}_{h}v\|_{a_{\mathrm{pw}}}^{2},

whence
(C)
holds with
\beta\coloneqq\beta_{\mathrm{St}}
.
Note that, in the proof of
Lemma

3.4
, the normalization
\|v\|_{b}=1
is still possible even if
b
is merely a seminorm because
b
is a norm in
\mathrm{span}\{u(1),\dots,u(j)\}
. Therefore, the proof of
Theorem

3.3
applies verbatim and (
1.1
) holds with the aforementioned constants.
∎

5.
Linear elasticity

Let the boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
be split into a closed connected nonempty Dirichlet part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\subset\partial\Omega
and a Neumann part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
.
The linear elasticity eigenvalue problem seeks eigenpairs
(\lambda,u)
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma=\lambda u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad\sigma=\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(u),\quad u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}},\quad\text{and }\sigma\nu=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}.

Here,
\varepsilon(v)\coloneqq\mathrm{sym}(\mathrm{D}v)
is the symmetric part of the gradient
\mathrm{D}v
of
v
and
\mathbb{C}A\coloneqq 2\mu A+\kappa\mathrm{tr}(A)\mathrm{I}_{n}
for any
A\in\mathbb{S}
denotes the linearized strain tensor with Lamé parameters

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{h})\rightharpoonup\varepsilon(v)\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)^{n\times n},

\displaystyle u_{\mathcal{M}}\to v\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)^{n},\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\Sigma}|_{\partial\Omega}\to v\text{ on }L^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{n}

up to a not relabelled subsequence,
whence
\|v\|_{b}=1
. As a convex functional,
\|\bullet\|_{a_{h}}^{2}
is weakly lower semi-continuous and so,

\displaystyle\gamma(\Omega)\leq\|v\|_{a}^{2}\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}\|u_{h}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}\lambda_{h}(1).

On the other hand, let
u\in V
denote the minimizer of (
6.1
). For sufficiently small mesh-sizes

\|\mathrm{I}_{h}u\|_{b_{h}}\neq 0
and the discrete function

v_{h}\coloneqq\mathrm{I}_{h}u/\|\mathrm{I}_{h}u\|_{b_{h}}\in V_{h}
is well defined.
It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to 0}\lambda_{h}(1)\leq\limsup_{h\to 0}\big(\|v_{h}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{s_{h}}^{2}\big)=\|u\|_{a}^{2}=\gamma(\Omega).

The combination of the two previously displayed formula conclude
\lim_{h\to 0}\lambda_{h}(1)=\gamma(\Omega)
.

6.5.
Conclusions

In all examples, adaptive computation recovers optimal convergence rates of the lower eigenvalue bounds towards the exact eigenvalue.
Accurate upper bounds of constants related to local embeddings can be computed and improve the quality of the lower eigenvalue bounds.
Additional applications beyond those presented in this paper include general scalar elliptic operators (instead of Laplace) and the biharmonic eigenvalue problem
[
24
,
29
]
.
Another application is the Stokes eigenvalue problem in primal form, where the vanishing divergence is enforced by using the reconstruction
\mathrm{div}_{h}
from
Remark

5.7
on the discrete level.
Since the conditions
(B)
–
(C)
arise from local estimates in practice, the analysis extends to piecewise constant
\sigma
for improved handling of variable material parameters, e.g., in linear elasticity.

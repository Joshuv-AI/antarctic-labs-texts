Absorbing ideals: a survey on ω \omega -stable groups
Bruno Moreira Fernandes

Abstract
We introduce a new concept in the Absorbing Ideal Theory in commutative rings, that is, the ω \omega -stable groups. We will provide examples and non-examples of these groups, and establish their relationship with H H -congruence. Ultimately, we will study the action of these groups considering the transitivity condition.

Absorbing ideals in commutative rings were studied, systematically, by D. Anderson and A. Badawi in [
??

(we only write

One of the results obtained in this theory that interests us is the following:

Claim 1.1

([

Let

be a surjective ring homomorphism and

-absorbing ideal of

We consider this theorem as the most natural starting point for investigating subgroups of permutations of sets of ideals that are “
\omega
-stable”, concept that we will define later. In this work, we define this notion and provide some examples and non-examples of this property in certain rings.

In the follow section, we present the definitions and basic notions about the
Absorbing Ideal Theory
in commutative rings, some basic results of this theory and also some theoretical constructions.

After, we define the
\omega
-stable groups over sets of ideals of a ring

, providing some examples and non-examples of these groups in different rings. We will also define the
\omega
-congruence and establish their relationship to

Finally, we will study

).

Basic definitions and some ring-theoretic constructions with absorbing ideals

Let

The most trivial example is a proper ideal

On
\omega
-stable groups

The following result shows that there is a certain type of “stability” in relation to absorbing ideals.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:R\longrightarrow T
be a surjective ring homomorphism and
I
be an absorbing ideal of
R
containing
ker(f)
. Then
f(I)
is an
n
-absorbing ideal of
T
if and only if
I
is an
n
-absorbing ideal of
R
. Moreover,
\omega_{T}(f(I))=\omega_{R}(I)
. In particular, this holds if
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
I
be an absorbing ideal of
R
containing
ker(f)
. Suppose that
f(I)
is an
n
-absorbing ideal of
T
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in R
such that
x_{1}\cdots x_{n+1}\in I
. Then
f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n+1})\in T
and
f(x_{1})\cdots f(x_{n+1})\in f(I)
. Since
f(I)
is an
n
-absorbing ideal, then there are
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq n+1
such that
f(x_{i_{1}})\cdots f(x_{i_{n}})\in f(I)
. Hence, there exist
i\in I
such that
f(x_{i_{1}})\cdots f(x_{i_{n}})=f(i)
. Since
f
is a ring homomorphism, then
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}-i\in ker(f)\subseteq I
and hence,
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}\in I
. Thus
I
is an
n
-absorbing ideal.

Reciprocally, suppose that
I
is an
n
-absorbing ideal of
R
. Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1}\in T
such that
y_{1}\cdots y_{n+1}\in f(I)
. Then there exist
i\in I
such that
y_{1}\cdots y_{n+1}=f(i)
. Since
f
is surjective, there are
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in R
such that
y_{i}=f(x_{i})
for every
i=1,\ldots,n+1
. Hence, we obtain
f(x_{1})\cdots f(x_{n+1})=f(i)
. Since
f
is a ring homomorphism, then
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}-i\in ker(f)\subseteq I
and hence,
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}\in I
. Being
I
an
n
-absorbing ideal, there are
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq n+1
such that
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}\in I
. So,
y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n}}=f(x_{i_{1}})\cdots f(x_{i_{n}})=f(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}})\in f(I)
. Therefore,
f(I)
is an
n
-absorbing ideal of
T
.

The “moreover” statement is true because if it were
\omega_{T}(f(I))\neq\omega_{R}(I)
, then we would obtain a contradiction with what we have just proved.

The “particular” statement is obvious.

∎

. Furthermore, there is a natural left group action of

Note that the group

The equality

Example 3.1
.

Recall that an automorphism

4
Transitive actions of
\omega
-stable groups

We say that
G
is
transitive
on
X
if, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, there exist
g\in G
such that
x^{\prime}=g\cdot x
. Equivalently,
G
is transitive over
X
iff
X
has only one
G
-orbit, i.e,
X=\texttt{Orb}_{G}(x)
, for some (and hence all)
x\in X
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a set of proper ideals of
R
and
H
be a subgroup of
\texttt{Perm}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
H
is
\omega
-stable and transitive over
\mathcal{I}
, then
\Omega(R,\mathcal{I})=\{\omega(I)\}
for some ideal
I\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

If
H
is
\omega
-stable on
\mathcal{I}
then every
H
-orbit is contained in only one
\omega
-orbit of
\mathcal{I}
. Hence, since
H
is transitive over
\mathcal{I}
, i.e,
\mathcal{I}=\texttt{Orb}_{H}(I)
, for some
I\in\mathcal{I}
, then by the Remark
2.2
the result it is proven.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
R
be an integral domain and suppose that
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{I}
. If
H
is
\omega
-stable and transitive over
\mathcal{I}
, then all ideals in
\mathcal{I}
are prime.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
is
\omega
-stable and transitive over
\mathcal{I}
. By Proposition
4.1
,
\Omega(R,\mathcal{I})=\{\omega_{R}(I)\}
for some (and all) ideal

. Now, just choose

.
∎

The next result tells us that the search for
\omega
-stable groups over the set of all proper ideals of integral domains that are not fields can and must be done by eliminating the transitivity condition.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be an integral domain. Then
H
is
\omega
-stable and transitive over
\texttt{Prp}(R)
iff
R
is a field.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
is
\omega
-stable and transitive over
\texttt{Prp}(R)
. Since
\mathbf{0}\in\texttt{Prp}(R)
so, by Corollary
4.1
, all ideals of
R
are prime. Being all proper ideals of
R
primes, let any
x\in R
,
x\neq 0
. Since
R
is an integral domain, then
x^{2}\neq 0
. Take the non-zero ideal
\left\langle x^{2}\right\rangle
. Since
\left\langle x^{2}\right\rangle
is prime (by hypothesis) and
x^{2}\in\left\langle x^{2}\right\rangle
, then
x\in\left\langle x^{2}\right\rangle
. Hence,
x=x^{2}y
, for some

. Thus, we have

x(xy-1)=0

Since
R
is an integral domain and
x\neq 0
, we obtain
xy=1
. Thus,
x
is invertible. Therefore,
R
is a field. The reciprocal is obvious.
∎

Best proximity point results for almost contraction and application to nonlinear differential equation
Azhar Hussain Mujahid Abbas, Muhammad Adeel Tanzeela Kanwal

Abstract




Abstract

Brinde [Approximating fixed points of weak
contractions using the Picard itration, Nonlinear Anal. Forum
9

(2004), 43-53] introduced almost contraction mappings and proved Banach
contraction principle for such mappings. The aim of this paper is to
introduce the notion of multivalued almost
\Theta
-contraction mappings and
present some best proximity point results for this new class of mappings. As
applications, best proximity point and fixed point results for weak single
valued
\Theta
-contraction mappings are obtained. An example is presented
to support the results presented herein. An application to a nonlinear
differential equation is also provided.

y_{1}\in Fx_{2}

such that

Since

\Theta

\displaystyle\Theta(d(x_{1},x_{2}))\leq\Theta(d(y_{0},y_{1}))

\displaystyle\Theta(H(Fx_{1},Fx_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha(x_{1},x_{2})\Theta(H(Fx_{1},Fx_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[\Theta(d(x_{1},x_{2})+\lambda D(x_{2},Fx_{1}))]^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Theta(d(x_{1},x_{2})))^{k}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\Theta(d(x_{1},x_{2})),

a contradiction. Hence
d(x_{1},x_{2})=0
, and
x_{1}=x_{2}.

If the pair

satisfies the weak

-property, then it satisfies the
P
-property, we have the following corollaries:

Application to Nonlinear Differential Equations

Let
C([0,1])
be the set of all continuous functions defined on
[0,1]
and

d:C([0,1])\times C([0,1])\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be the metric defined by

d(x,y)=||x-y||_{\infty}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}|x(t)-y(t)|.

(42)

It is known that
(C([0,1]),d)
is a complete metric space.

Let us consider the two-point boundary value problem of the second-order
differential equation:

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{d^{2}x}{dt^{2}}=f(t,x(t))&t\in[0,1];\\
x(0)=x(1)=0&\end{array}\right\}

(43)

where
f:[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous mapping.

The Green function associated with (
43
) is defined by

G(t,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}t(1-s)&if~~0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\\
s(1-t)&if~~0\leq s\leq t\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

(44)

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a given function.

Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

|f(t,a)-f(t,b)|\leq\max\limits_{a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}}|a-b|
for all

t\in[0,1]
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\phi(a,b)\geq 0
;

(ii)

there exists
x_{0}\in C[0,1]
such that
\phi(x_{0}(t),Fx_{0}(t))\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,1]
where
F:C[0,1]\rightarrow C[0,1]
;

(iii)

for each
t\in[0,1]
and
x,y\in C[0,1]
,
\phi(x(t),y(t))\geq 0
implies
\phi(Fx(t),Fy(t))\geq 0
;

(iv)

for each
t\in[0,1]
, if
\{x_{n}\}
is a sequence in
C[0,1]
such that
x_{n}\rightarrow x
in
C[0,1]
and
\phi(x_{n}(t),x_{n+1}(t))\geq 0
for all
n\in N
, then
\phi(x_{n}(t),x(t))\geq 0
for all
n\in N
.

We now prove the existence of a solution of the second order differential
equation (
43
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the assumptions (i)-(iv), (
43
) has a
solution in
C^{2}([0,1])
.

Proof.

It is well known that
x\in C^{2}([0,1])
is a
solution of (
43
) is equivalent to
x\in C([0,1])
is a solution of the
integral equation

x(t)=\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)f(s,x(s))ds,~t\in[0,1].

(45)

Let
F:C[0,1]\rightarrow C[0,1]
be a mapping defined by

Fx(t)=\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)f(s,x(s))ds.

(46)

Suppose that
x,y\in C([0,1])
such that
\phi(x(t),y(t))\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,1]
. By applying (i), we obtain that

\displaystyle|Fu(x)-Fv(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)f(s,x(s))ds-\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)f(s,y(s))ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)[f(s,x(s))-f(s,y(s))]ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)|f(s,x(s))-f(s,y(s))|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)\cdot(\max|x(s)-y(s)|)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||x-y||_{\infty}\cdot\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)ds\Bigg).

Since
\int\limits_{0}^{1}G(t,s)ds=-(t^{2}/2)+(t/2)
, for all
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}G(t,s)ds\Bigg)=\frac{1}{8}.

It follows that

||Fx-Fy||_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{8}(||x-y||_{\infty}

(47)

Taking exponential on the both sides, we have

\displaystyle e^{||Fx-Fy||_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{8}(||x-y||_{\infty})}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[e^{(||x-y||_{\infty})}]^{\frac{1}{8}},

for all
x,y\in C[0,1]
. Now consider a function
\Theta:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(1,\infty)
by
\Theta(t)=e^{t}
. Define

\alpha(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&if~~\phi(x(t),y(t))\geq 0,t\in[0,1],\\
0&otherwise.\end{array}\right.

Then from (
48
) with
k=\frac{1}{8},
we obtain that

\alpha(x,y)\Theta(||Fx-Fy||_{\infty})\leq[\Theta(d(x,y))]^{k}\leq[\Theta(d(x,y)+\lambda d(y,Fx))]^{k}.

Therefore the mapping
F
is multivalued almost
\Theta
-contraction.

From (ii) there exists
x_{0}\in C[0,1]
such that
\alpha(x_{0},Fx_{0})\geq 1
. Next, for any
x,y\in C[0,1]
with
\alpha(x,y)\geq 1,
we have

\displaystyle\phi(x(t),y(t))\geq 0~~~~\text{for all}~~t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\phi(Fx(t),Fy(t))\geq 0~~~~\text{for all}~~t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha(Fx,Fy)\geq 1,

and hence
F
is
\alpha
-admissible. It follows from Theorem
4.3

that
F
has a fixed point
x
in
C([0,1])
which in turns is the solution
of (
43
).

6
Conclusion

This paper is concerned with the existence and uniqueness of the best proximity point results for Brinde type contractive conditions via auxiliary function
\Theta\in\Omega
in the framework of complete metric spaces. Also, some fixed point results as a special cases of our best proximity point results in the relevant contractive conditions are studied. Moreover, the corresponding fixed point results are obtained. An example is discussed to show the significance of the investigation of this paper. An application to a nonlinear differential equation is presented to illustrate the usability of the new theory.

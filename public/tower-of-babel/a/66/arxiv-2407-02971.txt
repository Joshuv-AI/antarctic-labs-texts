Automorphisms, cohomology and extensions of symmetric quandles
Biswadeep Karmakar, Deepanshi Saraf, Mahender Singh

Abstract
. It is well-known that the cohomology of symmetric quandles generates robust cocycle invariants for unoriented classical and surface links. Expanding on the recently introduced module-theoretic generalized cohomology for symmetric quandles, we derive a four-term exact sequence that relates 1-cocycles, second cohomology, and a specific group of automorphisms associated with the extensions of symmetric quandles. This exact sequence shows that the obstruction to lifting and extending automorphisms is found in the second symmetric quandle cohomology. Additionally, some general aspects of dynamical cocycles and extensions are discussed.

1.
Introduction

Quandles are algebraic structures that systematically represent the three Reidemeister moves in planar diagrams of oriented links within the 3-sphere. When extended to racks, they become strong algebraic tools for addressing codimension two embeddings in any
n
-sphere
[
5
]
. Their study gained significant momentum following the seminal works of Joyce
[
9
]
and Matveev
[
14
]
. They showed that link quandles are complete invariants for non-split links, considering the orientation of the ambient space.

In classical knot theory, having an orientation is often crucial, especially when dealing with quandle cocycle invariants. Kamada and Oshiro
[
10
,
12
]
addressed the challenge of eliminating the need for orientation by introducing symmetric quandles and their homology groups. They defined a quandle cocycle invariant for an unoriented link using a given symmetric quandle 2-cocycle, and showed that aligns with the quandle cocycle invariant of an oriented link when an orientation is arbitrarily assigned. Similarly, an invariant for unoriented surface links has been defined using symmetric quandle 3-cocycles, yielding comparable results. Using 4-fold symmetric quandles, an invariant for 3-manifolds has been defined
[
6
,
15
]
, and it has been proved that the Chern–Simons invariant of closed 3-manifolds can be interpreted as a quandle cocycle invariant. In
[
8
]
, symmetric quandles have also been employed to define invariants for spatial graphs and handlebody-links. These results, along with further refinements (see, for example
[
4
,
16
,
17
]
), underscore the significance of symmetric quandles in low-dimensional topology.

Motivated by
[
1
,
7
]
, in
[
13
]
, we examined these objects from a categorical perspective and determined the Beck modules
[
3
]
in the category of symmetric racks (respectively, quandles). Beck modules provide a quite general concept of a coefficient module for an associated (co)homology theory. They generalize the notion of coefficient modules from group cohomology, Lie algebra cohomology, and Hochschild cohomology of associative algebras. We formulated a generalised (co)homology theory for symmetric racks (respectively, quandles) with homogeneous coefficients, extending the cohomolgy theory introduced in
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we develop this extension theory further, and establish an exact sequence connecting automorphisms of symmetric quandles with their extensions and the generalised second cohomology. This work builds upon the paper
[
2
]
by extending the results to symmetric racks and quandles, and utilizing more general module-theoretic coefficients developed in
[
13
]
. The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, some necessary preliminaries have been recalled. In Section
3
, we introduce dynamical cocycles and extensions of symmetric racks and quandles (Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.5
). In Section
4
, we relate dynamical cocycles and ordinary cocycles of symmetric racks and quandles (Theorem
4.1
). In Section
5
, we prove that group extensions lead to some natural dynamical extensions of symmetric racks and quandles (Corollary
5.2
and Corollary
5.3
). Finally, in Section
6
, we prove our main result (Theorem
6.7
). More precisely, we prove that if
(X,\rho_{X})
is a symmetric rack,
\mathcal{F}=(A,\phi,\psi,\eta)
is an
(X,\rho_{X})
-module,
\alpha\in Z_{SR}^{2}((X,\rho_{X});A)
is a 2-cocycle and
E(\mathcal{F},\alpha)
is the abelian extension of
(X,\rho_{X})
by
\mathcal{F}
through
\alpha
, then there is an exact sequence

0\to Z_{SR}^{1}\big((X,\rho_{X});A\big)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{A}\big(E(\mathcal{F},\alpha),\rho_{E(\mathcal{F},\alpha)}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma}}{{\to}}\operatorname{Aut}(X,\rho_{X})\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Lambda_{[\alpha]}}}{{\to}}H_{SR}^{2}\big((X,\rho_{X});A\big)

of groups. The exact sequence shows that the obstruction to lifting and extension of automorphisms lies in the second symmetric rack cohomology. There is a similar exact sequence for symmetric quandles as well.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a rack (respectively, quandle). A map
\rho_{X}:X\rightarrow X

(S1)

\rho_{X}^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{X}

(S2)

\rho_{X}(x\ast y)=\rho_{X}(x)\ast y

(S3)

x\ast\rho_{X}(y)=x\ast^{-1}y
,

for all
x,y\in X
. The pair
(X,\rho_{X})
is referred as a
symmetric rack (respectively, quandle)
.

We refer the reader to
[

11
,
12
,
17
]
for more details on these structures and their applications in knot theory.

Example 2.2
.

Below we list some elementary examples:

(1)

Every involution on a trivial quandle is a good involution.

(2)

For each

X=\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(G)
is the
n

\rho_{X}:X\rightarrow X

(3)

If

Definition 2.3
.

A
symmetric rack (respectively, quandle) homomorphism
is a map
f:(X,\rho_{X})\rightarrow(Y,\rho_{Y})

f(x\ast y)=f(x)\ast f(y)\quad\textrm{and}\quad f\big(\rho_{X}(x)\big)=\rho_{Y}\big(f(x)\big)

for all

. A bijective symmetric rack (respectively, quandle) homomorphism is called a

3.
Dynamical cocycles for symmetric quandles

A far-reaching generalization of quandle extensions using dynamical 2-cocycles has been provided in

Proposition 3.1

Let
(X,\rho)

and
\beta(x)\in\mathrm{Map}\big(S_{x},S_{\rho(x)}\big)

and
\beta(x)(s)
by
\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)
and
\beta_{x}(s)

Since

is dynamical cocycle, by condition
(2)
in Proposition
3.1
, we see that
\alpha^{\prime}_{x*y,z}(\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t),w)=\alpha^{\prime}_{x*z,y*z}(\alpha^{\prime}_{x,z}(s,w),\alpha^{\prime}_{y,z}(t,w))
, which is desired.

(3)

For all
x,y\in X
and
s\in S_{x},t\in S_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{\rho(x),y}\big(\beta^{\prime}_{x}(s),t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{\rho(x),y}\big(\gamma_{\rho(x)}(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s))),t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rho(x)*y}\big(\alpha_{\rho(x),y}\big(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)),\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)\big)

and

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{x*y}\big(\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{x*y}\big(\gamma_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s),\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t))\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rho(x*y)}\big(\beta_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s),\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t))\big)\big).

Since
(\alpha,\beta)
is dynamical cocycle, by condition
(3)
in Proposition
3.1
, we get

\alpha^{\prime}_{\rho(x),y}(\beta^{\prime}_{x}(s),t)=\beta^{\prime}_{x*y}(\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t))
.

(4)

For all
x\in X
and
s\in S_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{\rho(x)}\beta^{\prime}_{x}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{\rho(x)}\big(\gamma_{\rho(x)}(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rho^{2}(x)}\big(\beta_{\rho(x)}(\gamma_{\rho(x)}^{-1}(\gamma_{\rho(x)}(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)))))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}\big(\beta_{\rho(x)}(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)))\big).

Since
(\alpha,\beta)
is dynamical cocycle, by condition
(4)
in Proposition
3.1
, we have
\beta^{\prime}_{\rho(x)}\beta^{\prime}_{x}(s)=\gamma_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s))=s.

(5)

Note that
\big(\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(t)\big)^{-1}(s)=\gamma_{x}\big((\alpha_{x,y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)))^{-1}(\gamma_{x*y}^{-1}(s))\big)
. Thus, for
x,y\in X
and
s\in S_{x},t\in S_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{x,\rho(y)}(\beta^{\prime}_{y}(t))(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{x,\rho(y)}\big(\gamma_{\rho(y)}(\beta_{y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)))\big)(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*\rho(y)}\big(\alpha_{x,\rho(y)}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s),\gamma_{\rho(y)}^{-1}(\gamma_{\rho(y)}(\beta_{y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t))))\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*\rho(y)}\big(\alpha_{x,\rho(y)}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s),(\beta_{y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)))\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*\rho(y)}\big(\alpha_{x,\rho(y)}(\beta_{y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)))(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*\rho(y)}\big((\alpha_{x*^{-1}y,y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)))^{-1}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s))\big),\quad\textrm{since}~\alpha~\textrm{satisfies $(5)$ in Proposition \ref{Dynamical cocycles for symmetric racks}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{\prime}_{x*^{-1}y,y}(t))^{-1}(s).

(6)

If
(X,\rho)
is a symmetric quandle, then
\alpha
satisfies condition
(6)
in Proposition
3.1
. Thus,

\alpha^{\prime}_{x,x}(s,s)=\gamma_{x*x}\big(\alpha_{x,x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s),\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s))\big)=\gamma_{x}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)\big)=s

for all
x\in X
and
s\in S_{x}
.

Suppose that
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
and
(\alpha,\beta)
are cohomologous. Then, there exists
\gamma
such that (
3.0.1
) holds. Define

T:(X\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}S,\rho_{\alpha,\beta})\rightarrow(X\times_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}S,\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}})
by
T(x,s)=(x,\gamma_{x}(s)).
Clearly,
T
is a bijection. Further,

T\big((x,s)*(y,t)\big)=T\big((x*y,\alpha_{x,y}(s,t))\big)=\big(x*y,\gamma_{x*y}(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t))\big)

and

T\big((x,s)\big)*T\big((y,t)\big)=\big(x,\gamma_{x}(s)\big)*\big(y,\gamma_{y}(t)\big)=\big(x*y,\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(\gamma_{x}(s),\gamma_{y}(t))\big).

Thus, by the definition of
\alpha^{\prime}
, we have
T((x,s)*(y,t))=T((x,s))*T((y,t)).
Similarly,

T\big(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(x,s)\big)=T\big(\rho(x),\beta_{x}(s)\big)=\big(\rho(x),\gamma_{\rho(x)}(\beta_{x}(s))\big)

and

\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\big(T(x,s)\big)=\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\big(x,\gamma_{x}(s)\big)=\big(\rho(x),\beta_{x}^{\prime}(\gamma_{x}(s))\big).

Thus, by the definition of
\beta^{\prime}
, we obtain
T(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(x,s))=\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(T(x,s)).
Since
p\,T=p
, we obtain
(X\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}S,\rho_{\alpha,\beta})\cong(X\times_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}S,\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}})
as extensions of
(X,\rho)
by
S
. Conversely, suppose that
R:\big(X\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}S,\rho_{\alpha,\beta}\big)\to\big(X\times_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}S,\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\big)
is an isomorphism of extensions. Then, there exists a map
\gamma:X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}\Sigma_{S_{x}}
such that
R(x,s)=(x,\gamma_{x}(s))
. The fact that
R
is a symmetric rack homomorphism implies that
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
are related as in (
3.0.1
), and hence are cohomologous.
∎

4.
Dynamical cocycles and second cohomology

Let
(X,\rho)
be a symmetric rack. Recall from Remark
2.6
that a map
\sigma:X\times X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}A_{x}
is a
symmetric rack 2-cocycle if and only if
\sigma
satisfies:

\mathrm{(F1)}

\sigma_{x\ast y,z}+\phi_{x\ast y,z}(\sigma_{x,y})=\phi_{x\ast z,y\ast z}(\sigma_{x,z})+\sigma_{x\ast z,y\ast z}+\psi_{x\ast z,y\ast z}(\sigma_{y,z})
,

\mathrm{(F2)}

\sigma_{\rho_{X}(x),y}=\eta_{x\ast y}(\sigma_{x,y})
,

\mathrm{(F3)}

\phi_{x\ast\rho_{X}(y),y}(\sigma_{x,\rho_{X}(y)})=-\sigma_{x\ast\rho_{X}(y),y}

for all
x,y,z\in X
, where
\sigma(x,y)=\sigma_{x,y}
and
\sigma_{x,y}\in A_{x*y}
. Also if
(X,\rho)
is a symmetric quandle, then
\sigma
is a symmetric quandle 2-cocycle if in addition, we have
\sigma_{x,x}=0
for all
x\in X
. The following theorem extends
[
1
, Proposition 2.25]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a symmetric rack and
A=\{A_{x}\mid x\in X\}
be a collection of abelian groups. Consider isomorphisms
\phi_{x,y}:A_{x}\rightarrow A_{x*y}
, homomorphisms
\psi_{x,y}:A_{y}\rightarrow A_{x*y}
and
\eta_{x}:A_{x}\rightarrow A_{\rho(x)}
, and a map
\sigma:X\times X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}A_{x}
such that
\sigma_{x,y}\in A_{x*y}
for all
x,y\in X
. Define

\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow\cup_{x,y\in X}\mathrm{Map}\big(A_{x}\times A_{y},A_{x*y}\big)~~\textrm{by}~~\alpha_{x,y}(a,b)=\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma_{x,y}

and

\beta:X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}\mathrm{Map}\big(A_{x},A_{\rho(x)}\big)~\textrm{by}~\beta_{x}(a)=\eta_{x}(a)

for all
x,y\in X
,
a\in A_{x}
and
b\in A_{y}
. Then the following assertions hold:

(A)

The pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is a dynamical cocycle for the symmetric rack
(X,\rho)
over
A
if and only if
(A,\phi_{x,y},\psi_{x,y},\eta_{x})
is an
(X,\rho)
-module and
\sigma
is a symmetric rack 2-cocycle.

Furthermore, if
(X,\rho)
is a symmetric quandle, then the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is a dynamical cocycle for
(X,\rho)
over
A
if and only if
(A,\phi_{x,y},\psi_{x,y},\eta_{x})
is an
(X,\rho)
-module and
\sigma
is a symmetric quandle 2-cocycle.

(B)

Let
\sigma^{\prime}
be another symmetric rack (respectively, quandle)
2
-cocycle and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
its corresponding dynamical cocycle as defined above. If
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are cohomologous, then
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
are cohomologous.

Proof.

(A)

We proceed case by case as follows:

(1)

It is easy to see that
\alpha_{x,y}(b)
is a bijection, with inverse defined as
\alpha_{x,y}(b)^{-1}(a)=\phi_{x,y}^{-1}(a)-\phi_{x,y}^{-1}\psi_{x,y}(b)-\phi_{x,y}^{-1}\sigma_{x,y}
for all
x,y\in X
.

(2)

For all
x,y,z\in X
and
a\in A_{x},b\in A_{y},c\in A_{z},
we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{x*y,z}(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b),c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{x*y,z}(\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma_{x,y},c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{x*y,z}(\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma_{x,y})+\psi_{x*y,z}(c)+\sigma_{x*y,z}

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{x*z,y*z}(\alpha_{x,z}(a,c),\alpha_{y,z}(b,c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{x*z,y*z}(\phi_{x,z}(a)+\psi_{x,z}(c)+\sigma_{x,z},\phi_{y,z}(b)+\psi_{y,z}(c)+\sigma_{y,z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{x*z,y*z}(\phi_{x,z}(a)+\psi_{x,z}(c)+\sigma_{x,z})+\psi_{x*z,y*z}(\phi_{y,z}(b)+\psi_{y,z}(c)+\sigma_{y,z})+\sigma_{x*z,y*z}.

Let
0_{x}
denote the identity of the group
A_{x}
for each
x\in X
. If
\alpha
is a dynamical cocycle, then
\alpha_{x*y,z}(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b),c)=\alpha_{x*z,y*z}(\alpha_{x,z}(a,c),\alpha_{y,z}(b,c))
. Putting
a=0_{x}
,
b=0_{y}
and
c=0_{z}
in above equations gives

\phi_{x*y,z}(\sigma_{x,y})+\sigma_{x*y,z}=\phi_{x*z,y*z}(\sigma_{x,z})+\psi_{x*z,y*z}(\sigma_{y,z})+\sigma_{x*z,y*z}.

Thus,
\alpha_{x*y,z}(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b),c)=\alpha_{x*z,y*z}(\alpha_{x,z}(a,c),\alpha_{y,z}(b,c))
implies that

\phi_{x*y,z}(\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b))+\psi_{x*y,z}(c)=\phi_{x*z,y*z}(\phi_{x,z}(a)+\psi_{x,z}(c))+\psi_{x*z,y*z}(\phi_{y,z}(b)+\psi_{y,z}(c)).

Putting
b=0_{y}
and
c=0_{z}
, we get
(M1)
. Similarly, taking
a=0_{x}
and
c=0_{z}
gives
(M2)
, whereas taking
a=0_{x}
and
b=0_{y}
gives
(M7)
. Conversely, if
(A,\phi_{x,y},\psi_{x,y},\eta_{x})
is an
(X,\rho)
-module and
\sigma
is a symmetric rack 2-cocycle, then using
(X,\rho)
-module axioms
(M1)
,
(M2)
,
(M7)
and the cocycle condition
(F1)
, we get

\alpha_{x*y,z}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b),c\big)=\alpha_{x*z,y*z}\big(\alpha_{x,z}(a,c),\alpha_{y,z}(b,c)\big),

which is condition (2) for
(\alpha,\beta)
to be a dynamical cocycle.

(3)

For all
x,y\in X
and
a\in A_{x},b\in A_{y},
we have

\alpha_{\rho(x),y}\big(\beta_{x}(a),b\big)=\phi_{\rho(x),y}(\eta_{x}(a))+\psi_{\rho(x),y}(b)+\sigma_{\rho(x),y}

and

\beta_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b)\big)=\eta_{x*y}\big(\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma_{x,y}\big).

Thus, putting appropriate identities of abelian groups as in the argument of
(2)
above, we see that
\alpha_{\rho(x),y}(\beta_{x}(a),b)=\beta_{x*y}(\alpha_{x,y}(a,b))
if and only if
(X,\rho)
-module axioms
(M4)
,
(M5)
and cocycle condition
(F2)
holds.

(4)

The condition (4) for
(\alpha,\beta)
to be a dynamical cocycle is
\beta_{\rho(x)}\beta_{x}(a)=a
, which holds if and only if
\eta_{\rho(x)}\eta_{x}(a)=a
and
(X,\rho)
-module axiom
(M3)
holds for all
x\in X
and
a\in A_{x}.

(5)

For all
x,y\in X
and
a\in A_{x},b\in A_{y},
we have

\alpha_{x,\rho(y)}\big(\beta_{y}(b)\big)(a)=\phi_{x,\rho(y)}(a)+\psi_{x,\rho(y)}(\eta_{y}(b))+\sigma_{x,\rho(y)}

and

\big(\alpha_{x*^{-1}y,y}(b)\big)^{-1}(a)=\phi^{-1}_{x*^{-1}y,y}(a)-\phi^{-1}_{x*^{-1}y,y}\psi_{x*^{-1}y,y}(b)-\phi^{-1}_{x*^{-1}y,y}\sigma_{x*^{-1}y,y}.

The condition (5) for
(\alpha,\beta)
to be a dynamical cocycle is
\alpha_{x,\rho(y)}(\beta_{y}(b))(a)=\alpha_{x*^{-1}y,y}(b))^{-1}(a)
, which holds if and only if
(M7)
,
(M8)
and cocycle condition
(F3)
holds.

In addition if
(X,\rho)
is a quandle, then
\alpha_{x,x}(s,s)=s
if and only if
(M9)
holds and
\sigma_{x,x}=0_{x}.
This completes the proof of assertion (A).

(B)

If
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are cohomologous, then there exists
\tau:X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}A_{x}
such that
\tau_{x}\in A_{x}
,
\eta_{x}(\tau(x))=\tau(\rho(x))
and
\sigma_{x,y}-\sigma^{\prime}_{x,y}=\phi_{x,y}\tau(x)-\tau(x*y)+\psi_{x,y}(\tau(y))
. For each
x\in X,~a\in A_{x}
, we define

\gamma:X\rightarrow\cup_{x\in X}\Sigma_{A_{x}}~\textrm{by}~\gamma_{x}(a)=\tau(x)+a.

Clearly,
\gamma_{x}\in\Sigma_{A_{x}}
with its inverse defined as
\gamma_{x}^{-1}(a)=a-\tau(x)
for all
a\in A_{x}.

Now,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma^{\prime}_{x,y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\phi_{x,y}(a)+\psi_{x,y}(b)+\sigma^{\prime}_{x,y}-\tau(x*y)\big),~\textrm{by definition of}~\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\phi_{x,y}(a-\tau(x))+\psi_{x,y}(b-\tau(y))+\sigma^{\prime}_{x,y}+\phi_{x,y}\tau(x)-\tau(x*y)+\psi_{x,y}(\tau(y))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\phi_{x,y}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(a))+\psi_{x,y}(\gamma_{y}^{-1}(b))+\sigma_{x,y}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(a),\gamma_{y}^{-1}(b))\big)

and

\beta^{\prime}_{x}(a)=\eta_{x}(a)=\tau(\rho(x))+\eta_{x}(a)-\eta_{x}(\tau(x))=\gamma_{\rho(x)}\big(\eta_{x}(a-\tau(x))\big)=\gamma_{\rho(x)}\big(\beta_{x}(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(a))\big).

Hence,
(\alpha,\beta)
is cohomologous to
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
, and the proof is complete.

∎

5.
From group extensions to dynamical extensions

In this section, we explore connection between group extensions and dynamical extensions. The following result is inspired from
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\rho)
and
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\rho})
be symmetric racks (respectively, quandles). Let
f:(\tilde{X},\tilde{\rho})\rightarrow(X,\rho)
be a surjective symmetric rack (respectively, quandle) homomorphism and
T=\{f^{-1}(x)\mid x\in X\}
. Let
S
be a collection of sets
\{S_{x}\mid x\in X\}
such that there is a bijection
\mu_{x}:f^{-1}(x)\rightarrow S_{x}
for each
x\in X
. Then
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\rho})\cong(X\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}S,\rho_{\alpha,\beta})
for some dynamical cocycle
(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

Let
X\times S:=\{(x,s)\mid x\in X~\textrm{and}~s\in S_{x}\}.
Clearly, the map
\Phi:X\times S\to\tilde{X}
given by
\Phi(x,s)=\mu_{x}^{-1}(s)
, is a bijection. Define the maps

\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow\cup_{x,y\in X}\mathrm{Map}(S_{x}\times S_{y},S_{x*y})~\textrm{by}~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\mu_{x*y}\big(\mu_{x}^{-1}(s)\tilde{*}\mu_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)

and

\beta:X\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}\big(S_{x},S_{\rho(x)}\big)~\textrm{by}~\beta_{x}(s)=\mu_{\rho(x)}\big(\tilde{\rho}(\mu_{x}^{-1}(s))\big),

where
\tilde{*}
is the rack operation on
\tilde{X}.
Note that
\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\in S_{x*y}
and
\beta_{x}(s)\in S_{\rho(x)}.
Since
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\rho})
is a symmetric rack, it follows that
(\alpha,\beta)
is a dynamical cocycle of
(X,\rho)
over
S
, and the bijection
\Phi
becomes an isomorphism of symmetric racks.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
1\rightarrow A\rightarrow E\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
be an extension of groups and
SQ(E)=(\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(E),\mathrm{inv})
or
(\operatorname{Core}(E),\mathrm{id})
. Then
SQ(E)\cong SQ(G)\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}SQ(A)
for some dynamical cocycle
(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

Let
\pi:E\rightarrow G
be the quotient homomorphism and
\kappa:G\rightarrow E
a set-theoretic section such that
\kappa(1)=1
. By functoriality, we have a surjective symmetric quandle homomorphism
SQ(\pi):SQ(E)\rightarrow SQ(G)
such that

SQ(\pi)\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)=x*y=SQ(\pi)\big(\kappa(x*y)\big)

for all
x,y\in G
. Thus, there exists a unique element
\theta(x,y)\in A
such that

\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)=\kappa(x*y)\theta(x,y).

Note that, every element of
E
can be written uniquely in the form
\kappa(x)s
for some
x\in G
and
s\in A
. For each
x\in G,
there is a bijection
g_{x}:SQ(\pi)^{-1}(x)\rightarrow A
given by
g_{x}(\kappa(x)s)=s.
In the notation of Proposition
5.1
, we identify each
S_{x}
with
A
. Hence, by Proposition
5.1
, we obtain
SQ(E)\cong SQ(G)\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}A
, where
(\alpha,\beta)
is the dynamical cocycle given by

\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=g_{x*y}\big(g_{x}^{-1}(s)\tilde{*}g_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)~\textrm{and}~\beta_{x}(s)=g_{\rho(x)}\big(\tilde{\rho}(g_{x}^{-1}(s))\big)

for
x,y\in G
and
s,t\in A
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
1\rightarrow A\rightarrow E\xrightarrow{\pi}G\rightarrow 1
be an extension of groups,
z\in A
be a central element of
E
and
\bar{z}:=\pi(z)
. Then
(\operatorname{Core}(E),z)\cong(\operatorname{Core}(G),\bar{z})\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\operatorname{Core}(A),z)
for some dynamical cocycle
(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

As above, let
\pi:E\rightarrow G
be the quotient map and
\kappa:G\rightarrow E
a set-theoretic section such that
\kappa(1)=1
. We have a surjective symmetric quandle homomorphism
\operatorname{Core}(\pi):(\operatorname{Core}(E),z)\rightarrow(\operatorname{Core}(G),\bar{z}))
. Employing the arguments similar to corollary
5.2
, we obtain
(\operatorname{Core}(E),z)\cong(\operatorname{Core}(G),\bar{z})\times_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\operatorname{Core}(A),z)
.
∎

6.
Wells type exact sequence for symmetric racks

Let
(X,\rho_{X})
be a symmetric rack and
A
be a left
\mathbb{Z}(X,\rho_{X})
-module. Let
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\rho_{X})
denote the group of symmetric rack automorphisms of
(X,\rho_{X})
, and
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
denote the group of
\mathbb{Z}(X,\rho_{X})
-module automorphisms of
A
. For investigations in this section, we will restrict ourselves to the case where
\phi_{x,y}=\phi
,
\psi_{x,y}=\psi
and
\eta_{x}=\eta
, i.e. when
\phi,\psi,\eta
do not depend on
x,y\in X
. Note that, even with this restriction, the coefficient system is quite general (see
[
13
, Example 3.3]
).

For
(\zeta,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,\rho_{X})\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and a 2-cocycle
\alpha\in Z_{SR}^{2}((X,\rho_{X});A)
, we define

{}^{(\zeta,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}\coloneqq\theta\big(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(y)}\big).

Lemma 6.1
.

The map
\sigma:X\times X\longrightarrow A
given by
\sigma(x,y)=\theta\big(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(y)}\big)
is a symmetric rack 2-cocycle.

Proof.

We verify the symmetric rack 2-cocycle conditions as given in Remark
2.6
. For any
x,y,z\in X
, we have

\displaystyle-\phi_{x*z,y*z}\sigma(x,z)+\phi_{x*y,z}\sigma(x,y)+\sigma(x*y,z)-\sigma(x*z,y*z)-\psi_{x*z,y*z}\sigma(y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi_{x*z,y*z}\theta(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(z)})+\phi_{x*y,z}\theta(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(y)})+\theta(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x*y),\zeta^{-1}(z)})

\displaystyle-\theta(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x*z),\zeta^{-1}(y*z)})-\psi_{x*z,y*z}\theta(\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(y),\zeta^{-1}(z)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(-\phi_{x*z,y*z}\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(z)}+\phi_{x*y,z}\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x),\zeta^{-1}(y)}+\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x*y),\zeta^{-1}(z)}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(x*z),\zeta^{-1}(y*z)}-\psi_{x*z,y*z}\alpha_{\zeta^{-1}(y),\zeta^{-1}(z)}\Big),

since
\theta
is a
\mathbb{Z}(X,\rho_{X})
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle=

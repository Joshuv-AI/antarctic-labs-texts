Thin accretion disk images of rotating hairy Horndeski black holes
Mohaddese Heydari-Fard 1 , Malihe Heydari-Fard 2 and Nematollah Riazi 1 1 Department of Physics, Shahid Beheshti University, Evin 19839, Tehran, Iran 2 Department of Physics, The University of Qom, 3716146611, Qom, Iran

Abstract
By considering the steady-state Novikov-Thorne model, we study thin accretion disk processes for rotating hairy black holes in the framework of the Horndeski gravity. We obtain the electromagnetic properties of accretion disk around such black holes and investigate the effects of the hair parameter h h on them. We find that by decreasing the hair parameter from the Kerr limit, h → 0 h\rightarrow 0 , the radius of the innermost stable circular orbit decreases which makes thin accretion disks around rotating hairy black holes in Horndeski gravity more efficient than that for the Kerr black hole in general relativity. Furthermore, using the numerical ray-tracing method, we plot thin accretion disk images around these black holes and investigate the effects of hair parameter on the central shadow area of accretion disk. PACS numbers : 97.10.Gz, 04.70.–s, 04.50.Kd Keywords : Accretion and accretion disks, Physics of black holes, Modified theories of gravity

2
Thin accretion disk model

In order to study the electromagnetic properties of accretion disk, we need to obtain the equations of motion of test particles in the space-time of the central compact object. Therefore, we take into account the Lagrangian
{\cal L}
of a test particle in the vicinity of the central BH as follows

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x^{\mu}}\dot{x^{\nu}},

(1)

where a dot denotes derivative with respect to the affine parameter and
g_{\mu\nu}
is the line element of a generic stationary and axisymmetric
space-time

ds^{2}=g_{tt}dt^{2}+2g_{t\phi}dtd\phi+g_{rr}dr^{2}+g_{\theta\theta}d\theta^{2}+g_{\phi\phi}d\phi^{2},

(2)

for which the metric components
g_{tt},g_{rr},g_{\theta\theta}
,
g_{\phi\phi}
and
g_{t\phi}
are assumed to be only functions of
r
and
\theta
coordinates. Thus, there are two constants of motion, the energy and the angular momentum per unit rest-mass,
\tilde{E}
and
\tilde{L}
, which are given by

g_{tt}\dot{t}+g_{t\phi}\dot{\phi}=-\tilde{E},

(3)

g_{t\phi}\dot{t}+g_{\phi\phi}\dot{\phi}=\tilde{L}.

(4)

Using above equations and the normalization condition for massive particles,
g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=-1
, we obtain

g_{rr}\dot{r}^{2}+g_{\theta\theta}\dot{\theta}^{2}=V_{\rm eff}(r,\theta),

(5)

where the effective potential is

V_{\rm eff}(r,\theta)=-1+\frac{\tilde{E}^{2}g_{\phi\phi}+2\tilde{E}\tilde{L}g_{t\phi}+\tilde{L}^{2}g_{tt}}{g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}}.

(6)

Then, from the radial component of the geodesic equation with conditions
\dot{r}=\dot{\theta}=\ddot{r}=0
for equatorial circular orbits, we find the following relation for the angular velocity,
\Omega=\dot{\phi}/\dot{t}
,
[
59
]

\Omega_{\pm}=\frac{-g_{t\phi,r}\pm\sqrt{(g_{t\phi,r})^{2}-g_{tt,r}g_{\phi\phi,r}}}{g_{\phi\phi,r}},

(7)

where the upper and lower signs refer to co-rotating and counter-rotating orbits, respectively. Now, using equations (
3
)-(
4
) and from condition
g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=-1
, the specific energy
{\tilde{E}}
and the specific angular momentum
{\tilde{L}}
for a particle on a circular orbit can be expresed as

{\tilde{E}}=-\frac{g_{tt}+g_{t\phi}\Omega}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}-2g_{t\phi}\Omega-g_{\phi\phi}\Omega^{2}}},

(8)

{\tilde{L}}=\frac{g_{t\phi}+g_{\phi\phi}\Omega}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}-2g_{t\phi}\Omega-g_{\phi\phi}\Omega^{2}}}.

(9)

The innermost stable circular orbit,
r_{\rm isco}
, can be obtained from the condition
V_{\rm eff,rr}=0
, which leads to the following equation

V_{\rm eff,rr}\mid_{r=r_{\rm isco}}=\frac{1}{g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{tt}g_{t\phi}}\left[{\tilde{E}^{2}g_{\phi\phi,rr}}+2\tilde{E}\tilde{L}g_{t\phi,rr}+{\tilde{L}^{2}g_{tt,rr}}-\left(g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}\right)_{,rr}\right]\mid_{r=r_{\rm isco}}=0.

(10)

The standard framework to describe thin accretion disk processes is the Novikov-Thorne model
[
58
]
, which is a generalization of the Newtonian approach of Shakura-Sunyaev
[
60
]
. The model is based on some typical assumptions such as the vertical size of disk,
H
, is negligible compared to its horizontal size,
H\ll r
. Also, it is assumed that the disk is in the equatorial plane of the central compact object and its inner edge is located at the ISCO radius. The disk is considered in a steady-state which means that the mass accretion rate,
\dot{M}
, is constant in time. In this steady-state model the accreting gas is assumed to be in thermodynamical equilibrium and thus the disk temperature is related to the energy flux via the equation

F(r)=\sigma_{\rm SB}T^{4}(r),

(11)

where
\sigma_{\rm SB}=5.67\times 10^{-5}\rm erg

\rm s^{-1}cm^{-2}K^{-4}
is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant and the energy flux emitted from the disk surface is given by
[
58
,
61
]

F(r)=-\frac{\dot{M}_{0}\Omega_{,r}}{4\pi\sqrt{-g}\left(\tilde{E}-\Omega\tilde{L}\right)^{2}}\int^{r}_{r_{\rm isco}}\left(\tilde{E}-\Omega\tilde{L}\right)\tilde{L}_{,r}dr.

(12)

Moreover, the luminosity of disk can be found as
[
62
]

L(\nu)=4\pi d^{2}I(\nu)=\frac{16\pi^{2}\hbar\cos\gamma}{c^{2}}\int_{r_{\rm in}}^{r_{\rm out}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\nu_{\rm e}^{3}rdrd\phi}{\exp{[\frac{h\nu_{\rm e}}{k_{\rm B}T}]}-1},

(13)

where
d
is the distance from the disk center,
\gamma
is the disk inclination angle, and
r_{\rm in}
and
r_{\rm out}
denote the inner and outer radii of the edge of disk, respectively. Also,
\hbar
and
k_{\rm B}
are respectively the reduced Planck constant and Boltzmann constant, while
\nu_{\rm e}=\nu(1+z)
is the emitted frequency with the redshift factor
z
given by

1+z=\frac{1+\Omega r\sin\phi\sin\gamma}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}-2g_{t\phi}\Omega-g_{\phi\phi}\Omega^{2}}}.

(14)

Finally, we define the accretion efficiency as

\epsilon=1-\tilde{E}_{\rm isco}.

(15)

which denotes the capability of central massive object to convert rest mass into radiation.

3
Rotating BHs in Horndeski gravity

The action of Horndeski gravity is described by

{\cal S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[Q_{2}(\chi)+Q_{3}(\chi)\Box\phi+Q_{4}(\chi)R+Q_{4,\chi}\left((\Box\phi)^{2}-(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi)(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)\right)\right],

(16)

where
g
,
R
and
\phi
are the metric determinant, Ricci scalar and a scalar field, respectively. Also,
Q_{i=2,..4}(\chi)
are arbitrary functions of the kinetic term
\chi=-1/2\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi
. Note the above action is a quartic Horndeski scalar field model in which the terms containing
Q_{5}
are absent
[
47
]
. From the definition of the four-current

j^{\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta{\cal S}}{\delta(\phi_{,\nu})},

(17)

it follows that

\displaystyle j^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Q_{2,\chi}\phi^{,\nu}-Q_{3,\chi}(\phi^{,\nu}\Box\phi+\chi^{,\nu})-Q_{4,\chi}(\phi^{,\nu}R-2R^{\nu\sigma}\phi_{,\sigma})

(18)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle Q_{4,\chi,\chi}[\phi^{,\nu}((\Box\phi)^{2}-(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\phi)(\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}\phi))+2(\chi^{,\nu}\Box\phi-\chi_{,\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi)],

where we have used the usual convention of the Riemann tensor

\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi-\nabla{\beta}\nabla{\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi=-R^{\sigma}_{\alpha\rho\beta}\nabla_{\sigma}\phi.

(19)

Varying action (
16
) with respect to metric
g^{\mu\nu}
we find the field equations

Q_{4}G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu},

(20)

where
T_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(Q_{2,\chi}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}+Q_{2}g_{\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{2}Q_{3,\chi}(\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\Box\phi-g_{\mu\nu}\chi_{,\alpha}\phi^{,\alpha}+\chi_{,\mu}\phi_{\nu}+\chi_{,\nu}\phi_{\mu})

(21)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle Q_{4,\chi}\left(\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}[(\Box\phi)^{2}-(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\phi)(\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}\phi)-2R_{\sigma\gamma}\phi^{,\sigma}\phi^{,\gamma}]-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\Box\phi\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\nabla_{\gamma}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\gamma}\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}R+R_{\sigma\mu}\phi^{,\sigma}\phi_{,\nu}+R_{\sigma\nu}\phi^{,\sigma}\phi_{,\mu}+R_{\sigma\nu\gamma\mu}\phi^{,\sigma}\phi^{,\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle Q_{4,\chi,\chi}\left(g_{\mu\nu}(\chi_{\alpha}\phi^{,\alpha}\Box\phi+\chi_{\alpha}\chi^{,\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\phi\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}\phi-(\Box\phi)^{2})\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\chi_{,\mu}\chi_{,\nu}-\Box\phi(\chi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}+\chi_{,\nu}\phi_{,\mu})-\chi_{,\gamma}[\phi^{,\gamma}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi-(\nabla^{\gamma}\nabla_{\mu}\phi)\phi_{,\nu}-(\nabla^{\gamma}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)\phi_{,\mu}]\right).

By considering the canonical action for the scalar field
\phi=\phi(r)
, and also imposing conditions of the finite energy of
\phi
, i.e.,
E=\int_{V}\sqrt{-g}T^{0}_{0}d^{3}x
and a vanishing radial four-current at infinity
j^{r}=0
, one can solve the field equations (
20
) and obtain the static and spherically symmetric solution as

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}),

(22)

with

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{h}{r}\ln\left(\frac{r}{2M}\right),

(23)

where
M
is the BH mass and
h
is referred to as the hair parameter with the dimension of length. When
h/M>0
there is only one horizon at
r=2M
, while for the values of
h
in the interval
-2<h/M<0
, there are two horizons; one is the outer event horizon at
r_{+}=2M
and other is the inner Cauchy horizon,
r_{-}
. These two horizons coincide at
r=2M
and we get an extremal BH
[
47
]
. Also, when
h\rightarrow 0
the metric (
23
) reduces to the Schwarzschild solution, and it is asymptotically flat. Thus, here we restrict our analysis to the case of
-2<h/M<0
as considered in
[
48
]
–
[
57
]
. Using the revised Newman-Janis algorithm, Walia et al., obtained the rotating BH in Horndeski gravity which in the Boyer–Lindqist coordinates is given by
[
48
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}{dr^{2}}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2a\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\left(\Delta-(r^{2}+a^{2})\right)dtd\varphi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]d\varphi^{2},

with

\Delta=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr+hr\ln\left(\frac{r}{2M}\right),\quad\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

(25)

In the absence of hair,
h\rightarrow 0
, the above metric reduces to the Kerr metric and for
a=0
it reduces to the line element of a static hairy Horndeski BH in equation (
22
). From the condition
g^{rr}=\Delta=0
one can obtain the location of horizons. We have plotted
\Delta(r)
as a function of radial coordinate for different values of
h
, and for
a=0.6M
and
a=0.9M
in Fig.

. As is clear, in each panel for some values of the hair parameter
h
, there are two horizons, namely the inner Cauchy horizon,
r_{-}
, and the outer event horizon,
r_{+}
, so that
r_{-}<r_{+}
. However for a critical value of
h=h_{\rm c}
, the two horizons coincide,
r_{-}=r_{+}
, corresponding to an extremal BH. Also, for
h<h_{\rm c}
there are no BH space-time
[
50
]
. Also, we see that the radius of the event horizon decrease with increasing
|h|
.

5.1
Photon initial conditions

For the first step we write the photon initial conditions in the image plane of the distant observer. For this purpose we consider the photon places at the image plane at the position
(X_{0},Y_{0},0)
with 4-momentum
{k}^{\alpha}=(k_{0},0,0,-k_{0})
so that the photon 3-momentum
\mathbf{k_{0}}=-k_{0}\hat{Z}
is perpendicular to the image plane. The initial conditions for the photon position are given as

\displaystyle t_{0}=0,

\displaystyle r_{0}=\sqrt{D^{2}+X_{0}^{2}+Y_{0}^{2}},

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\arccos\frac{D\cos i+Y_{0}\sin i}{r_{0}},

\displaystyle\phi_{0}=\arctan\frac{X_{0}}{D\sin i-Y_{0}\cos i}.

(32)

Using the relation
k^{\mu}=(\partial x^{\mu}/\partial\tilde{x}^{\alpha})\tilde{k}^{\alpha}
, where
\tilde{k}^{\mu}
is the photon 4-momentum in Cartesian coordinates, we obtain the photon 4-momentum in the spherical coordinates system

\displaystyle k_{0}^{r}=-\frac{D}{r_{0}}|\mathbf{k_{0}}|,

\displaystyle k_{0}^{\theta}=\frac{\cos i-(Y_{0}\sin i+D\cos i)\frac{D}{r_{0}^{2}}}{\sqrt{X_{0}^{2}+(D\sin i-Y_{0}\cos i)^{2}}}|\mathbf{k_{0}}|,

\displaystyle k_{0}^{\phi}=\frac{X_{0}\sin i}{X_{0}^{2}+(D\sin i-Y_{0}\cos i)^{2}}|\mathbf{k_{0}}|,

(33)

where
k_{0}^{t}
cab be obtained from the condition
g_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=0
as follows

\displaystyle k_{0}^{t}=\sqrt{(k_{0}^{r})^{2}+r_{0}^{2}(k_{0}^{\theta})^{2}+r_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}(k_{0}^{\phi})^{2}}.

(34)

5.2
Photon trajectory

Now by using the initial conditions (

)-(

It is believed that the large luminosity of active galactic nuclei (AGN) and quasars is the result of gas accreted by supersessive BH. The quasar accretion disk emits X-rays. This emission is lensed by the central BH before it arrives at distant observer. The radiation intensity profile emitted from the disk surface has been observed to follow a power law. 6
Conclusions

In the present work, we considered thin accretion disks around rotating hairy BHs in Horndeski gravity. First, we studied the geodesic motion of test particles in the space-time of these BHs and obtained the relevant quantities, namely the specific angular momentum
\tilde{L}
, specific energy
\tilde{E}
, angular velocity
\Omega
and the ISCO radius for particles moving in circular orbits. We numerically obtained the ISCO radius for both spin,
a
, and the hair parameter,
h
. It is shown that by increasing both the spin parameter and the absolute value of the hair parameter, the ISCO radius decreases. Then, using the steady-state Novikov-Thorne model, we obtained the physical properties of accretion disks including the energy flux
F(r)
, temperature distribution
T(r)
, and the emission spectra
L(\nu)
for some values of the hair parameter and plotted their profiles. The decrease of ISCO radius shows that the presence of scalar filed weakens the strength of gravitational field and thus the inner edge of disk shifts to lower and lower radii. Therefore, the disks around rotating BHs in Horndeski gravity are hotter and more luminous than the Kerr BH in the absence of hair.

Furthermore, we investigated the images of thin accretion disks around rotating hairy Horndeski BHs using a ray-tracing technique. We made the ray-traced redshifted images, intensity and polarization profiles of disks for both hairy Horndeski BH and the Kerr BH in the absence of hair. It is shown that with increasing the absolute value of the hair parameter
h
, the central shadow area decreases and the size and orientation of polarization vectors also decrease. Moreover, the results show that the inclination angle has a pronounced effect on the disk images.

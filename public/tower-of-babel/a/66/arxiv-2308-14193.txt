Local Maximal Monotonicity in Variational Analysis and Optimization
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu Vinh Huy Khoa, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat

Abstract




Abstract
. The paper is devoted to a systematic study and characterizations of notions of local maximal monotonicity and their strong counterparts for set-valued operators that appear in variational analysis, optimization, and their applications. We obtain novel resolvent characterizations of these notions together with efficient conditions for their preservation under summation in broad infinite-dimensional settings. Further characterizations of these notions are derived by using generalized differentiation of variational analysis in the framework of Hilbert spaces.

Keywords
. variational analysis and optimization, locally monotone operators, generalized differentiation, characterizations of local maximal monotonicity

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
49J52, 49J53, 90C99, 47H05

1
Introduction

It is difficult to overstate the importance of monotonicity and especially
(
global
)
maximal monotonicity
notions and results for set-valued mappings/operators/multifunctions with applications to various areas of operations research, optimization, numerical analysis, systems control, etc. The theory of monotone operators has started in the early 1960s by Minty who obtained in
[
9
]
his seminal theorem characterizing maximal monotonicity of set-valued operators in Hilbert spaces via the single-valuedness and surjectivity of resolvents. An extension of Minty’s theorem to operators in reflexive Banach spaces and many other fundamental results on global maximal monotonicity were established by Rockafellar (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
19
]
) who applied them, in particular, to developing the
proximal point method
(PPM) in numerical optimization. The reader is referred to the books by Bauschke and Combettes
[
1
]
and by Simons
[
24
]
, which largely reflect the current stage of the theory of monotone operators and their important applications in Hilbert and general Banach spaces, respectively.

There are two different motivations and formalizations of
local maximal monotonicity
, which came from the requirements of variational analysis and optimization. The first notion goes back to Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
17
]
who used it for the study of
tilt stability
of local minimizers, the notion which by now plays a highly important role in both theoretical and numerical aspects of optimization; see
[
7
,
6
,
8
,
14
,
21
,
22
]
and the references therein for the most recent developments and applications. The second notion of local maximal monotonicity was introduced and further developed by Pennanen
[
15
,
16
]
motivated by applications to local convergence of PPM and related
numerical algorithms
of optimization. To the best of our knowledge, both notions of local maximal monotonicity have been studied and applied only in finite dimensions.

In this paper, we address the above notions of local maximal monotonicity for set-valued mappings defined on general
Banach spaces
and prove that they are
equivalent
if the space in question is
reflexive
. Note that all the obtained results are
new in finite dimensions
.

Our major theorem provides an appropriate resolvent characterization of these equivalent notions in reflexive Banach spaces that can be viewed as a
local counterpart
of Minty’s surjectivity theorem applied to
graphical localizations
. We also study and characterize the corresponding
strong versions
of local maximal monotonicity. The obtained characterizations allow us to establish verifiable conditions ensuring the
preservation
of local maximal monotonicity of set-valued mappings under
summation
in reflexive Banach spaces. Our further characterizations of local maximal monotonicity and its strong counterpart are established for set-valued mappings defined on
Hilbert spaces
, where we employ powerful tools of
variational analysis
and
generalized differentiation
that revolve around
coderivatives
of multifunctions. Coderivatives have been already used by Mordukhovich and Nghia
[
13
]
to characterize
strong
local maximal monotonicity and by Chieu et al.
[
2
]
to characterize
global
maximal monotonicity of set-valued mappings in Hilbert and finite-dimensional spaces. Now we are able to derive
coderivative characterizations
of
local maximal monotonicity
(not just its strong version) for arbitrary set-valued operators in Hilbert spaces by using the newly established resolvent characterization of this notion married to the advanced machinery of generalized differentiation. Among strong advantages of the obtained coderivative characterizations are extensive
calculus rules
available for coderivatives in the books
[
10
,
11
,
23
]
in both finite and infinite dimensions, which allow us to deal with various structured problems and open the door for subsequent developments and applications. This is done in our forthcoming papers.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls and discusses the definitions of
globally monotone
and globally
maximal monotone
set-valued mappings in arbitrary Banach spaces together with their
strong
counterparts. We present here important monotonicity properties needed in what follows, including some new observations and proofs.

Section
3
is devoted to the study of two versions of
local maximal monotonicity
for set-valued mappings in Banach spaces. The main result here is a
resolvent characterization
of both versions of local maximal monotonicity in reflexive Banach spaces, which yields the equivalence of these notions in such a framework. We also prove in this section that local maximal monotonicity is
preserved
under the operator
summation
in reflexive spaces provided the fulfillment of a certain
inner semicontinuity
property, which is shown to be essential for the preservation.

Section
4
concerns
local strong monotonicity
of multifunctions in Banach spaces admitting a Gâteaux smooth renorming. Among other results, we reveal here geometric properties of the space in question ensuring the equivalence between two versions of local strong maximal monotonicity of set-valued operators with a
prescribed modulus
.

Section
5
briefly discusses
generalized differential tools
of variational analysis in Hilbert spaces that are needed for subsequent characterizations of local maximal and strong maximal monotonicity. Reviewing the major
coderivative
constructions for set-valued mappings employed in such characterizations, we present some of their properties used in what follows.

The main result of Section
6
provides two equivalent characterizations of local maximal monotonicity of set-valued operators in Hilbert spaces in terms of the
positive-semidefiniteness
of appropriate
coderivatives
combined with the property of local
hypomonotonicity
, which is well understood in variational analysis. The concluding Section
7
summarizes the major contributions of the paper and discusses some topics for future research.

Finally in this section, we recall some notation used throughout the paper. Unless otherwise stated, all the spaces under consideration are
Banach
, and we use the
sum norm

\displaystyle\|(x,y)\|:=\|x\|+\|y\|\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in X,\;y\in Y

on the product spaces
X\times Y
. The symbols
x_{k}\to x
and
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}x
indicate the strong/norm and weak convergence on
X
. Given a set-valued mapping
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
, denote by

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm dom}\,F:=\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;F(x)\neq\emptyset\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\mbox{\rm gph}\,F:=\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;\big|\;y\in F(x)\big\}

its
domain
and
graph
, respectively. For a nonempty set
\Omega\subset X
, we use the notation
x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\to}}\bar{x}
meaning that
x\to\bar{x}
with
x\in\Omega
. As always,
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.

2
Global Monotonicity and Maximality in Banach Spaces

Let
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping acting between a Banach space
X
and its topological dual
X^{*}
. Recall that the operator
T
is (globally)
monotone
if

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

A monotone operator
T
is
maximal monotone
if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
for any monotone operator
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
.

To proceed with strong counterparts of global monotonicity, we first define the
duality mapping

J:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
between
(X,\|\cdot\|)
and
(X^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
given by

J(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;\big|\;\langle x^{*},x\rangle=\|x\|^{2}=\|x^{*}\|_{*}^{2}\big\},\quad x\in X.

(2.1)

Since
J
is the subgradient mapping of the convex quadratic function
\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
, it is maximal monotone on
X
with
\mbox{\rm dom}\,J=X
. It is well known that the
single-valuedness
of the duality mapping
J
is equivalent to the space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
being
(
Gâteaux
)
smooth
, i.e., its norm
\|\cdot\|
is Gâteaux differentiability on
X\setminus\{0\}
. The latter class is sufficiently broad including, in particular, all separable and all reflexive Banach spaces; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
]
.

Having (
2.1
) in hand, we say that
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is (globally)
strongly monotone
on
X
with modulus
\sigma>0
(or
\sigma
-strongly monotone) if the shift
T-\sigma J
is monotone on
X
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\sigma\langle j(x_{1})-j(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle

whenever
(x_{1},x_{1}^{*}),(x_{2},x_{2}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\text{ and }(x_{1},j(x_{1})),(x_{2},j(x_{2}))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,J
. A
\sigma
-strongly monotone mapping
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone
if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
for any
\sigma
-strongly monotone operator
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
.

The following simple observation is useful in what follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T,S,R:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be set-valued mappings satisfying the inclusion

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-S)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,R.

(2.2)

Then we have the equivalent properties:

(i)

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(R+S)
.

(ii)

\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm dom}\,S
.

[
Proof.]
To verify first that (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), we deduce from
\mbox{\rm dom}\,(R+S)=\mbox{\rm dom}\,R\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,S
and
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(R+S)
that
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm dom}\,R\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,S\subset\mbox{\rm dom}\,S
, which gives us (ii). To justify the reverse implication, pick
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
giving us
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
and hence
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,S
. This allows us to find
y^{*}\in S(x)
such that
(x,x^{*}-y^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-S)
. It follows from (
2.2
) that
(x,x^{*}-y^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,R
, which yields
x^{*}-y^{*}\in R(x)
and
x^{*}\in R(x)+y^{*}\subset(R+S)(x)
. Therefore, we get
(x,x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(R+S)
, which verifies
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(R+S)
and thus completes the proof.

The next proposition presents some results about the
preservation of maximality
of set-valued mappings under their addition/subtraction with the duality mapping
J
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, and let
J:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be the duality mapping (
2.1
). For any fixed
\sigma>0
, we have the following statements:

(i)
If
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone, then
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone on
X
.

(ii)
If
X
is reflexive and
T
is maximal monotone, then the shifted operator
T+\sigma J
is also maximal monotone.

(iii)
If
T
is maximal monotone with the
closed and convex domain
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
, then
T+\sigma J
is maximal monotone.

(iv)
If in addition to the assumptions in either
(ii)
or
(iii)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|
is Gâteaux differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
, then
T+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i), assume that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
for some monotone mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
. As
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
and
\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\sigma J)=X
, Proposition
2.1
implies that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)
. The maximality of
T
yields the equality
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)
. It follows from Proposition
2.1
that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)
, and hence the equality
\mbox{\rm gph}\,S=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)
holds. Therefore,
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone.

Next we justify assertion (ii). Since
T
is maximal monotone, we get
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\neq\emptyset
. Moreover,
\sigma J
is maximal monotone with
\mbox{\rm int}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\sigma J)=X
. Thus
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\cap\mbox{\rm int}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\sigma J)\neq\emptyset
, and it follows from
[
18
, Theorem 1]
that
T+\sigma J
is maximal monotone under the reflexivity of
X
.

For (iii), we have that the set
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\sigma J)=\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
is closed, that the set
\mathrm{cl}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T)=\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
is convex, and that
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\cap\mathrm{int}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\sigma J)=\mbox{\rm dom}\,T\neq\emptyset
. All of the above ensure the maximal monotonicity of
T+\sigma J
by using the result of
[
25
, Theorem 5.10(
\eta
)]
.

If for (iv) the norm
\|\cdot\|
is Gâteaux smooth on
X\setminus\{0\}
in (ii) and (iii), then
J
is single-valued on the whole space
X
, which ensures that
T+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone by the monotonicity of
T=(T+\sigma J)-\sigma J
. Combining this with the maximality justified above, we arrive at the
\sigma
-strong maximal monotonicity of
T+\sigma J
and thus complete the proof.

In fact, the assumptions of Proposition
2.2
allow us to get the following necessary and sufficient condition for the
\sigma
-strong monotonicity of set-valued mappings.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Gâteaux smooth Banach space, and let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a
\sigma
-strongly monotone operator for some
\sigma>0
. Assume that either
X
is reflexive, or the set
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
is closed and convex. Then
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone if and only if

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\text{ for any $\sigma$-strongly monotone }S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}\text{ with }\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S.

(2.3)

[
Proof.]
It is clear that the
\sigma
-strong maximal monotonicity of
T
yields (
2.3
). Conversely, suppose that
T
satisfies (
2.3
) and show that
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone. First check that the
\sigma
-strong monotonicity of
T
and (
2.3
) imply that
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone. Indeed, taking into account that
T-\sigma J
is monotone on
X
and considering any monotone mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
tell us by Proposition
2.1
that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)
. Since the operator
S+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone on
X
, we deduce from (
2.3
) that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)
, and hence
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
. This verifies the maximal monotonicity of
T-\sigma J
. To complete the proof of the proposition, observe that the assumptions on either the reflexivity of
X
or on the closedness and convexity of
\mbox{\rm dom}\,T
together with the Gâteaux smoothness of
\|\cdot\|
ensure by Proposition
2.2
that the operator
T=(T-\sigma J)+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone.

We conclude this section with the following proposition, which shows that the study of monotone (resp. strongly monotone) mappings can be largely reduced to that of their maximal (resp. strongly maximal monotone) versions.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, where
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is an arbitrary Banach space. We have:

(i)
If
T
is monotone, then there exists a maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
extending
T
in the sense that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}
.

(ii)
If
X
is reflexive and Gâteaux smooth, and if
T
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with some
\sigma>0
, then there exists a
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
extending
T
.

[
Proof.]
Assertion (i) is proved by employing Zorn’s lemma; see, e.g.,
[
23
, Proposition 12.6]
. Let us now derive (ii) from (i) by the usage of the reflexivity of
X
and the Gâteaux smoothness of the norm
\|\cdot\|
. If
T
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with some
\sigma>0
, then the shift
T-\sigma J
is monotone, and thus we get by (i) a maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}
. From Proposition
2.1
and
\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\sigma J)=\mbox{\rm dom}\,J=X
, it follows that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(\bar{T}+\sigma J)
. Since the extension
\bar{T}
is maximal monotone and since the space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is reflexive and Gâteaux smooth, Proposition
2.2
(ii) ensures that
\bar{T}+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone. This shows that the operator
\bar{T}
satisfies all the requirements in (ii), which thus completes the proof.

3
Local Monotonicity of Set-Valued Mappings

This section addresses
local
monotonicity of set-valued mappings defined on Banach spaces and study the two versions of
local maximal
monotonicity discussed in Section
1
. The proof of the main result here uses some notions and facts from
geometry theory
of Banach spaces, which we overview first based on the books
[
3
,
4
,
12
]
, where the reader can find more details.

Similarly to the case of Gâteaux smoothness, the norm
\|\cdot\|
on
X
is
Fréchet smooth
if it is Fréchet differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
. This is equivalent to saying that the duality mapping (
2.1
) is single-valued and continuous on
X
. Note that in this case, the function
\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is continuously differentiable on
X
with
\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\right)=J
. It is well known that if either
X
is reflexive or
X^{*}
is separable, then
(X,\|\cdot\|)
admits a Fréchet smooth
renorming
, i.e., an equivalent norm that is Fréchet differentiable at all nonzero points.

Further, a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
strictly convex
if for all
x,y\in S_{X}:=\{x\in X\mid\|x\|=1\}
,
x\neq y
, we have

\|\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\|<1
for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Recall finally that
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
Kadec-Klee
if whenever
x_{k}\xrightarrow{w}x
and
\|x_{k}\|\rightarrow\|x\|
,
the strong convergence
x_{k}\rightarrow x
as
k\to\infty
is guaranteed. The fundamental achievements in geometric theory of Banach spaces tell us that in
any reflexive
space
X
, there exists an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|
such that
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is

Fréchet smooth, strictly convex, and Kadec-Klee.

(3.1)

Now we are ready to proceed with the study of locally monotone multifunctions with paying the main attention to the maximality issues. The notions of local monotonicity is defined in the following natural way as a localization of global monotonicity. A set-valued mapping
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
on a Banach space
X
is
locally monotone
around a point
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
if there is an (open) neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a globally monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(3.2)

The mapping
T
is called
monotone with respect to

W\subset X\times X^{*}
(
W
is not necessarily open) if (
3.2
) holds with replacing
U\times V
by
W
.

For local
maximal
monotonicity, we have the following two versions labeled as types
(A)
and
(B)
, which were originated in
[
17
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, where
X
is a Banach space. Then:

(i)

T
is
locally maximal monotone of type (A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there is a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
such that
T
is monotone with respect to
U\times V
and that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
for any mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, which is monotone with respect to
U\times V
and satisfies the inclusion
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
.

(ii)

T
is
locally maximal monotone of type (B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
if there are a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}
such that (
3.2
) holds.

Similarly to the above, we understand the monotonicity of types (A) and (B)
with respect to some
W\subset X\times X^{*}
. The following proposition easily follows from the definitions.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is locally maximal monotone of type
(A)
with respect to
W\subset X\times X^{*}
, then this mapping is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
with respect to
W
.

[
Proof.]
Assuming that
T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(A)
with respect to
W
, we find a globally maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W
. Let
F:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,F:=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W
, which yields the global monotonicity of
F
. It follows from Proposition
2.4
(i) that there exists a maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
satisfying
\mbox{\rm gph}\,F\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}
. Therefore, we arrive at the inclusion

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}.

(3.3)

The monotonicity of
\bar{T}
and local monotonicity (A) of
T
with respect to
W
imply by (
3.3
) that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap W
. This gives us a maximal monotone operator satisfying
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap W
. Thus
T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
with respect to
W
.

A natural question arises on whether we have the
equivalence
between the two types of local maximal monotonicity in Definition
3.1
. In what follows, we answer this question in the
affirmative
in the case of
reflexive
Banach spaces. In fact, we deduce this equivalence from the
resolvent characterization
of both notions in our major theorem given below.

To proceed, recall that given a set-valued mapping
T:X\rightrightarrows Y
and a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, it is said that
\widehat{T}:X\rightrightarrows Y
is a
(
graphical
)
localization
of
T
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there is a neighborhood
U\times V
of this point such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\widehat{T}=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)
. Furthermore,
T
has a
single-valued localization
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if the mapping
\widehat{T}
is single-valued on
U
with
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\widehat{T}=U
. If in addition
\widehat{T}
is continuous on
U
then we say that
T
admits a
continuous single-valued localization
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. It is obvious that if
T
has a (continuous) single-valued localization around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
\sigma\neq 0
, then the mapping
\sigma T
has a (continuous) single-valued localization around
(\bar{x},\sigma\bar{y})
, and also the inverse mapping
T^{-1}
has a (continuous) single-valued localization around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
.

The following crucial result provides a characterization of both notions of local maximal monotonicity of set-valued operators
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
in terms of continuous single-valued localizations of their
resolvents

(J+\lambda T)^{-1}
, where
J
is the duality mapping (
2.1
) associated with the
\|\cdot\|
which satisfies the properties in (
3.1
).
Recall that such an equivalent norm is available in any reflexive Banach space. The obtained characterization can be viewed as a
local counterpart
of the celebrated Minty theorem on global monotonicity.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a reflexive space endowed with an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|
satisfying the properties in (
3.1
), and let
J
be the associated duality mapping (
2.1
). Given a multifunction
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
and a point
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
.

(ii)

T
is locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and the resolvent
(J+\lambda T)^{-1}
has a continuous single-valued localization around
(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})
for any
\lambda>0
.

(iii)

T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), let
T
be locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and then find a maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}
on
X
and a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with

\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(3.4)

Since
J
is continuous, the mapping
\Psi_{\lambda}:X\times X^{*}\rightarrow X^{*}\times X
defined by

\Psi_{\lambda}(x,x^{*}):=\big(J(x)+\lambda x^{*},x\big)\ \text{ for all }\;(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*},\quad\lambda>0

is a homeomorphism. By taking the image of
\Psi_{\lambda}
on both sides of (
3.4
), we have

\mbox{\rm gph}\,(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}\cap\Psi_{\lambda}(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(J+\lambda T)^{-1}\cap\Psi_{\lambda}(U\times V).

(3.5)

The reflexivity of
X
allows us to apply to
\bar{T}
the fundamental Rockafellar’s characterization of global maximal monotonicity telling us that
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
is a single-valued operator from
X^{*}
to
X
which is
demicontinuous
, i.e., continuous from the strong topology on
X^{*}
to the weak topology of
X
. Let us show that
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
is actually
continuous
from the strong topology of
X^{*}
to the strong topology of
X
. Indeed, fix
x^{*}\in X^{*}
and consider a sequence
\{x_{k}^{*}\}\subset X^{*}
strongly converging to
x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
. For each
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, define

x_{k}:=(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}(x_{k}^{*})\;\mbox{ and }\;x:=(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}(x^{*}).

(3.6)

We know by the demicontinuity of
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
that
x_{k}\xrightarrow{w}x
as
k\to\infty
. It follows from (
3.6
) that there exist
y_{k}^{*}\in\bar{T}(x_{k})
as
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and
y^{*}\in\bar{T}(x)
such that

x_{k}^{*}=J(x_{k})+\lambda y_{k}^{*}\;\mbox{ and }\;x^{*}=J(x)+\lambda y^{*}.

(3.7)

Since
x_{k}^{*}\rightarrow x^{*}
and
x_{k}\xrightarrow{w}x
, we clearly have the convergence

\langle x_{k}^{*}-x^{*},x_{k}-x\rangle\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty.

(3.8)

Using (
3.7
) allows us to rewrite (
3.8
) as

\langle J(x_{k})-J(x),x_{k}-x\rangle+\lambda\langle y_{k}^{*}-y^{*},x_{k}-x\rangle\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty.

(3.9)

By the global monotonicity of the operators
J
and
\bar{T}
, both terms on the left-hand side of (
3.9
) are nonnegative, and hence
\langle J(x_{k})-J(x),x_{k}-x\rangle\searrow 0
as
k\to\infty
. Combining the latter with the well-known estimate

\langle J(x_{k})-J(x),x_{k}-x\rangle\geq\big(\|x_{k}\|-\|x\|\big)^{2},\quad k\in{\rm I\!N},

gives us
\|x_{k}\|\rightarrow\|x\|
as
k\to\infty
. Having
x_{k}\xrightarrow{w}x
and
\|x_{k}\|\rightarrow\|x\|
, we arrive at the strong convergence
x_{k}\rightarrow x
, since the norm
\|\cdot\|
under consideration possesses the Kadec-Klee property. This justifies the norm-to-norm continuity of
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
.

Remembering that
\Psi_{\lambda}
is a homeomorphism and that
U\times V
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, we get that the image
\Psi_{\lambda}(U\times V)
is a neighborhood of
(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})=\Psi_{\lambda}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
. This allows us to choose neighborhoods
W
of
J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}
and
Q
of
\bar{x}
such that
W\times Q\subset\Psi_{\lambda}(U\times V)
. Since
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
is continuous at
J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*}
and
Q
is a neighborhood of
\bar{x}=(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*})
, we may shrink
W
if necessary so that
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}(W)\subset Q
. Combining the above with (
3.5
) brings us to the equality

\mbox{\rm gph}\,(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}\cap(W\times Q)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(J+\lambda T)^{-1}\cap(W\times Q),

(3.10)

which ensures that
(J+\lambda T)^{-1}
has a single-valued localization around
(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})
. The continuity of this localization on
W
follows from that of
(J+\lambda\bar{T})^{-1}
, and thus we get (ii).

Let us now verify implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii). Supposing that (ii) holds gives us a neighborhood
V\times U
of
(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})
such that the mapping
S:V\rightrightarrows U
defined by
\mbox{\rm gph}\,S:=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(J+\lambda T)^{-1}\cap(V\times U)
is single-valued on
V
with
\mbox{\rm dom}\,S=V
. We aim at proving that
T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(A)
with respect to the neighborhood
\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U)
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})=\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(J(\bar{x})+\lambda\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})
. Indeed, fix a pair
(x,x^{*})\in\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U)
satisfying the conditions

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ for all }\ (y,y^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U).

(3.11)

It follows from
(x,x^{*})\in\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U)
that

(J(x)+\lambda x^{*},x)=\Psi_{\lambda}(x,x^{*})\in\Psi_{\lambda}\big(\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U)\big)=V\times U,

and hence
x\in U
and
J(x)+\lambda x^{*}\in V
. By
\mbox{\rm dom}\,S=V
and
\mbox{\rm dom}\,S^{-1}=U
, the vector
y:=S(J(x)+\lambda x^{*})
belongs to
U
. From the definition of
S
, we also have that
y\in(J+\lambda T)^{-1}(J(x)+\lambda x^{*})
and
J(x)+\lambda x^{*}\in(J+\lambda T)(y)
, which yield

\lambda^{-1}\big(J(x)+\lambda x^{*}-J(y)\big)\in T(y).

(3.12)

Observe also the relationships

\Psi_{\lambda}\big(y,\lambda^{-1}(J(x)+\lambda x^{*}-J(y))\big)=(J(x)+\lambda x^{*},y)\in V\times U.

(3.13)

It follows from (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) that
\big(y,\lambda^{-1}(J(x)+\lambda x^{*}-J(y))\big)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap\Psi_{\lambda}^{-1}(V\times U)
. Therefore, we deduce from (
3.11
) that

\lambda^{-1}\langle J(y)-J(x),x-y\rangle\geq 0

giving us
\langle J(y)-J(x),y-x\rangle=0
due to the global monotonicity of
J
. By the strict convexity of
\|\cdot\|
, the latter implies that
x=y
. Hence
x^{*}\in T(x)
, this means that
T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
of type
(A)
, which justifies (iii). The last implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) was verified in Proposition
3.2
for general Banach spaces. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Having in mind the equivalence between types (A) and (B) in Theorem
3.3
, we use in what follows the term “
local maximal monotonicity
” for set-valued mappings on reflexive spaces.

Next we address the question about
preservation
of local maximal monotonicity under summation of two set-valued operators in reflexive Banach spaces. Rockafellar’s result in
[
18
, Theorem 1]
establishes the preservation of
global maximal monotonicity
for the sum
T_{1}+T_{2}
of two maximal monotone operators in reflexive spaces under
qualifications condition

\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{1}\cap\mathrm{int}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{2})\neq\emptyset.

(3.14)

We are not familiar with any result concerning preservation of
local maximal monotonicity
under summation. Such a local preservation theorem is derived below in reflexive spaces under a certain inner semicontinuity condition, which is shown to be essential for the fulfillment of the local preservation result. The needed
graphical
inner semicontinuity condition is taken from
[
10
, Definition 1.63(i)]
being different from the standard inner/lower semicontinuity of multifunctions at domain points; see
[
10
,
23
]
. We say that
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
between two Banach spaces is (graphically)
inner semicontinuous
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
if

\forall x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}\ \ \ \exists\,N\subset{\rm I\!N}\;\mbox{ such that }\;{\rm I\!N}\setminus N\;\text{ is finite and }\;y_{k}\overset{N}{\rightarrow}\bar{y}\;\text{ with }\;y_{k}\in F(x_{k}).

(3.15)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space, and let
T_{1},T_{2}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be set-valued mappings. Take
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{int}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{2}
,
\bar{x}^{*}_{1}\in T_{1}(\bar{x})
, and
\bar{x}^{*}_{2}\in T_{2}(\bar{x})
. If both mappings
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1})
and
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{2})
, respectively, then the sum
T:=T_{1}+T_{2}
is also locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1}+\bar{x}_{2}^{*})
provided that the mapping
T_{1}
is graphically inner semicontinuous at
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1})
.

[
Proof.]
By the assumptions made, we find neighborhoods
U\times V_{1}
and
U\times V_{2}
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1})
and
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{2})
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}_{1}\cap(U\times V_{1})=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{1}\cap(U\times V_{1}),\quad\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}_{2}\cap(U\times V_{2})=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{2}\cap(U\times V_{2})

(3.16)

with the globally maximal monotone operators
\bar{T}_{1}
and
\bar{T}_{2}
. We have
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{int}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\bar{T}_{1})
since
T_{1}
(and hence
\bar{T}_{1}
) is graphically inner semicontinuous at
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1})
. It follows from
[
18
, Theorem 1]
discussed above that the sum
\bar{T}:=\bar{T}_{1}+\bar{T}_{2}
is maximal monotone on
X
. Select
r>0
so that
B_{r}(\bar{x}^{*}_{2})\subset V_{2}
for the ball centered at
\bar{x}^{*}_{2}
with radius
r
. Consider neighborhoods
W_{1}\subset V_{1}
of
\bar{x}^{*}_{1}
and
W_{2}
of
\bar{x}^{*}_{2}
satisfying the condition

\max\{\mathrm{diam}(W_{1}),\;\mathrm{diam}(W_{2})\}<r/2,

where
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega):=\sup\{\|x-y\|\mid x,y\in\Omega\}
is the diameter of a given set. Due to the inner semicontinuity of
T_{1}
and hence of
\bar{T}_{1}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1})
, there is a neighborhood
Q\subset U
of
\bar{x}
for which

T_{1}(Q)\cup\bar{T}_{1}(Q)\subset W_{1}.

With
W:=W_{1}+W_{2}
, observe that
Q\times W
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}_{1}+\bar{x}_{2}^{*})
. To complete the proof of the theorem, it remains to show that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(Q\times W)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(Q\times W)
. Indeed, picking
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(Q\times W)
gives us

\displaystyle y=y_{1}+y_{2}\in W\;\mbox{ with }\;y_{1}\in\bar{T}_{1}(x),\;y_{2}\in\bar{T}_{2}(x),\;x\in Q.

(3.17)

Since
x\in Q
, we have
y_{1}\in\bar{T}_{1}(x)\subset W_{1}\subset V_{1}
, and hence it follows from (
3.16
) that
(x,y_{1})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{1}
. Using
y\in W=W_{1}+W_{2}
allows us to find
z_{1}\in W_{1}
with
y-z_{1}\in W_{2}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left\|y-y_{1}-\bar{x}^{*}_{2}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(y-z_{1}-\bar{x}^{*}_{2})+(z_{1}-y_{1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(y-z_{1})-\bar{x}^{*}_{2}\right\|+\left\|z_{1}-y_{1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(W_{2})+\mathrm{diam}(W_{1})<r,

and thus
y_{2}=y-y_{1}\in B_{r}(\bar{x}^{*}_{2})\subset V_{2}
, which yields
(x,y_{2})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}_{2}\cap(U\times V_{2})=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{2}\cap(U\times V_{2})
. Since
(x,y_{2})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T_{2}
and
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, we arrive at
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(Q\times W)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(Q\times W)
. The reverse inclusion can be checked similarly. Therefore,

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(Q\times W)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(Q\times W)
, which verifies the local maximal monotonicity of
T
relative to
Q\times W
.

To conclude this section, we present an example showing that the inner semicontinuity assumption is
essential
for the fulfillment of the preservation result in Theorem
3.4
.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the two multifunctions
T_{1},T_{2}\colon{\rm I\!R}^{2}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{2}
generated by the
normal cone mappings
to convex closed sets as follows

T_{i}(x,y):=N\big((x,y);\Omega_{i}\big),\;i=1,2,\;\mbox{ for }\;\Omega_{1}:=\big\{(x,y)\;|\;y\geq x^{2}\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\Omega_{2}:={\rm I\!R}\times\{0\}.

It is well known from convex analysis that
T_{1},T_{2}
are maximal monotone operators on
{\rm I\!R}^{2}
. Their sum
T:=T_{1}+T_{2}
is calculated by

T(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(0,t)\in{\rm I\!R}^{2}\;\big|\;t\in{\rm I\!R}\}&\mbox{for }\;(x,y)=(0,0),\\
\emptyset&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We can easily see that
T
is not maximally monotone on
{\rm I\!R}^{2}
. Indeed, by adding any point
\big((\alpha,0),(0,0)\big)
with
\alpha>0
to the graph of
T
, we get another monotone operator, which contradicts the maximal monotonicity of
T
. Observe that the qualification condition
(
3.14
) clearly fails in this case since
\mbox{\rm int}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{2})=\emptyset
.

Let us now check whether the sum operator
T
is
locally
maximal monotone around the point
(0,0)\in T_{1}(0,0)\cap T_{2}(0,0)
. Take any neighborhood
U\times V
of
(0,0)\in{\rm I\!R}^{2}\times{\rm I\!R}^{2}
and show that
T
is
not
maximal monotone with respect to
U\times V
, i.e., there exists a monotone operator
S:{\rm I\!R}^{2}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{2}
with respect to
U\times V
such that the set
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)
is properly contained in
\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
. Indeed, constructing
S
by

S(x,y):=\begin{cases}T(x,y)&\mbox{ for }\;x=y=0,\\
\{(0,0)\}&\mbox{ for }\;x\neq 0,\;y=0,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

we see that this mapping satisfies all the required conditions. Observe that
(0,0)\notin\mbox{\rm int}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{1})\cup\mbox{\rm int}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,T_{2})
, and that neither
T_{1}
nor
T_{2}
in inner semicontinuous at
(0,0)
.

4
Local Strong Monotonicity in Smooth Banach Spaces

This section is devoted to the study of
strong local monotonicity
and its
maximality
versions for set-valued mappings defined on Banach spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping on a Banach space, let
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, and let
\sigma>0
. Then we say that:

(i)

T
is
locally strongly monotone
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
if there are a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a globally
\sigma
-strongly monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

(4.1)

(ii)

T
is
locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
if there is a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
such that
T
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
U\times V
, and such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
for any mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, which is monotone with respect to
U\times V
and satisfies the inclusion
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
.

(iii)

T
is
locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
if there are a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone mapping
\bar{T}
such that (
4.1
) holds.

As we see, the strong monotonicity notions involve the duality mapping
J
from (
2.1
), which is
single-valued
if and only if the space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
Gâteaux smooth
. For simplicity and definiteness, this property is
assumed
without further mentioning in the reminder of this section.

For any
\sigma\neq 0
, consider the
vertical shear mapping/vertical transvection

\Phi_{\sigma}:X\times X^{*}\rightarrow X\times X^{*}
defined by

\Phi_{\sigma}(x,x^{*}):=\big(x,x^{*}+\sigma J(x)\big)\ \text{ whenever }\;(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*},

(4.2)

which is a
bijection
(actually, a homeomorphism if
\|\cdot\|
is Fréchet smooth) from
X\times X^{*}
to itself. It is easy to check that for any set-valued mapping
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, we have

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\overset{\Phi_{\sigma}}{\longmapsto}\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma J)\overset{\Phi_{-\sigma}}{\longmapsto}\mbox{\rm gph}\,T.

(4.3)

To illustrate (
4.2
), look at the simplest case when
X={\rm I\!R}
. Here the vertical shear mapping takes the point
(x,y)
to the point
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, where

\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\sigma&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}.

The vertical shear displaces points to the right of the
y
-axis up or down, depending on the sign of
\sigma
. It leaves vertical lines invariant, but tilts all other lines around the point where they meet the
y
-axis. Horizontal lines, in particular, get tilted to become lines with slope
\sigma
.

To proceed further, observe that the (global and local)
monotonicity and strong monotonicity notions for set-valued mappings can be applied to
sets
by just considering mappings whose graphs are exactly the sets. Having this in mind, we now present useful discussions involving set monotonicity via the vertical shear mapping (
4.2
).

Remark 4.2
.

It follows from (
4.3
) that whenever
\sigma>0
, we have:

(i)

\Phi_{\sigma}
carries monotone sets into
\sigma
-strongly monotone sets. Moreover, we know from Proposition
2.2
that
\Phi_{\sigma}
also maps maximal monotone sets into
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone sets provided that
X
is reflexive.

(ii)
If
W\subset X\times X^{*}
is monotone with respect to
U\times V
, then
\Phi_{\sigma}(W)
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V)
.

(iii)

\Phi_{-\sigma}
carries
\sigma
-strongly monotone sets into monotone sets. Furthermore, Proposition
2.2
says that it also maps
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone sets into maximal monotone sets.

(iv)
If
W\subset X\times X^{*}
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
U\times V
, then
\Phi_{-\sigma}(W)
is monotone with respect to
\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
.

(v)
For any
U\times V\subset X\times X^{*}
and for any
\sigma\neq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V)=\left\{(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*}\;\big|\;x\in U,\ x^{*}-\sigma J(x)\in V\right\}.

If furthermore
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is Fréchet smooth and
U\times V
is open, then
\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V)
is open as well.

Using the above definitions, properties of the vertical shear transformation
\Phi_{\sigma}
, and Proposition
2.2
allows us to verify the preservation of local maximal monotonicity of type
(B)
under shifts.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Fréchet smooth space. Consider a set-valued mapping
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
and a graph point
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then for any
\sigma>0
, we have:

(i)
If
X
is reflexive and
T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, then
T+\sigma J
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}+\sigma J(\bar{x}))
with modulus
\sigma
.

(ii)
If
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
, then
T-\sigma J
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i), we deduce from the assumptions in (i) that there exist a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V).

Then
\Phi_{\sigma}\big[\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)\big]=\Phi_{\sigma}\big[\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\big]
while giving us

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,\big(\bar{T}+\sigma J\big)\cap\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma J)\cap\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V),

(4.4)

where
\Phi_{\sigma}(U\times V)
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}+\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. It follows from Proposition
2.2
(ii) that
\bar{T}+\sigma J
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone on
X
. Thus we get from (
4.4
) that
T+\sigma J
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}+\sigma J(\bar{x}))
with modulus
\sigma
. Assertion (ii) is verified in the same way by using the shear mapping
\Phi_{-\sigma}
and the result of Proposition
2.2
(i).

Now we are in a position to reveal close relationships
between both type
(A)
and
(B)
of local strong maximal monotonicity of operators and single-valued continuous localizations of their inverses. Recall that the spaces in question are supposed to be Gâteaux smooth.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a reflexive space, and let

T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping with
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. For any
\sigma>0
, consider the following assertions:

(i)

T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
.

(ii)

T
is locally strongly monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
and its inverse
T^{-1}
has a continuous single-valued localization around
(\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x})
.

(iii)

T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
.

Then we always have the equivalence
(i)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii)
. The other equivalence
(i)
\Longleftrightarrow
(ii)
holds provided that the norm
\|\cdot\|
has the properties listed in (
3.1
).

[
Proof.]
Let us first verify that (i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii), where the given norm
\|\cdot\|
is Gâteaux differentiable on
X\setminus\{0\}
.
Supposing that
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma>0
tells us that
T
is locally
\sigma
-strongly monotone and also locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around this point. We know from Theorem
3.3
that the latter is equivalent to the local maximal monotonicity of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
. It follows from the definition that
T
is in fact locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
around the reference point.

To prove the reverse implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i), suppose that
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
with modulus
\sigma>0
relative to a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
. Obviously, the set
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone. Using Proposition
2.4
(ii) under the imposed Gâteaux smoothness and reflexivity of the space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
, we find a
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
such that

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}.

(4.5)

Since
\bar{T}
is a monotone mapping and
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
relative to
U\times V
, it follows from (
4.5
) that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)
. Therefore, we have a
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone operator
\bar{T}
on
X
satisfying the condition
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V)
, which means that
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(B)
with modulus
\sigma
relative to
U\times V
. This completes the proof of the equivalence (i)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii).

Assuming now that the norm
\|\cdot\|
satisfies all the geometric properties in (
3.1
), let us verify that (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Indeed, it follows from Proposition
4.3
(ii) that the local strong maximal monotonicity of type
(B)
in (i) ensures that the shifted operator
T-\sigma J
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. Choosing
\lambda:=\sigma^{-1}
in implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) of Theorem
3.3
allows us to conclude that the mapping

(\sigma^{-1}T)^{-1}=\big(J+\sigma^{-1}(T-\sigma J)\big)^{-1}

admits a single-valued continuous localization around
(\bar{x}^{*}/\sigma,\bar{x})
. Therefore, the inverse mapping
T^{-1}
has a single-valued continuous localization around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, which justifies assertion (ii).

It remains to show that (ii) yields (i). Using (ii) and the discussions in Remark
4.2
tells us that the shifted operator
T-\sigma J
is locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. We also have that
(\sigma^{-1}T)^{-1}
admits a single-valued localization around
(\bar{x}^{*}/\sigma,\bar{x})
. This ensures that the mapping

\big(J+\sigma^{-1}(T-\sigma J)\big)^{-1}=(\sigma^{-1}T)^{-1}

admits a single-valued localization around that point as well. Employing finally implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) in Theorem
3.3
verifies that
T-\sigma J
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. Then assertion (i) follows from
Proposition
4.3
(i), which completes the proof.

Due to the equivalence of Theorem
4.4
between the two types of local strong maximal monotonicity, we use the term “
local strong maximal monotonicity
” when the space
X
is reflexive.

The next proposition, which is a useful consequence of Theorem
4.4
and some other results above, concerns relationships between local strong monotonicity and local maximal monotonicity of operators and their shifts in Fréchet smooth (reflexive and nonreflexive) Banach spaces.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Fréchet smooth Banach space, and let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping with
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then for any
\sigma>0
the following hold:

(i)
If
X
is reflexive and
T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, then the mapping
T+\sigma J
is locally strongly maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}+\sigma J(\bar{x}))
with modulus
\sigma
.

(ii)
If
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
, then
T-\sigma J
is locally maximal monotone of the same type around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
.

[
Proof.]
Assertion (i) is a direct consequence of Proposition
4.3
(i), and Theorem
4.4
. To verify (ii), suppose that
T
is strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
on a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
and then prove that
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone of the same type with respect to the neighborhood

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)=\left\{(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*}\;\big|\;x\in U,\ x^{*}+\sigma J(x)\in V\right\}

of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. First we deduce from the
\sigma
-strong monotonicity of
T
with respect to
U\times V
and the discussions in Remark
4.2
that
T-\sigma J
is monotone with respect to
\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
. To justify its local maximality of type
(A)
, consider an operator
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
, which is monotone with respect to
\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
and such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
. Taking the image of
\Phi_{\sigma}
on both sides of this inclusion brings us to

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)\cap(U\times V).

(4.6)

Since
S+\sigma J
is globally monotone and
T
is maximal monotone with respect to
U\times V
, we get from (
4.6
) that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(S+\sigma J)\cap(U\times V)
. Taking now the image of
\Phi_{-\sigma}
on both sides of the latter equality yields

\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma J)\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V),

which therefore completes the proof of the proposition.

Finally in this section, we present a consequence of Proposition
4.5
on the
\sigma
-strong local maximal monotonicity of operators without involving the shift mappings in the formulation while using them in the proof. This corollary is a local counterpart to Proposition
2.3
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a reflexive and Fréchet smooth Banach space, and let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
with
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then for any
\sigma>0
we have that
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
with modulus
\sigma
if and only if there is a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
such that
T
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
U\times V
, and that

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)

(4.7)

for any
\sigma
-strongly monotone mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
on
U\times V
satisfying the inclusion
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
.

[
Proof.]
The “only if” part is trivial. To verify the “if” part, assume the existence of a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
such that
T
is
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
U\times V
while satisfying the equality in (
4.7
) for any
\sigma
-strongly monotone operator
S:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
on
U\times V
with
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,S\cap(U\times V)
. We intend to check that
T
is
\sigma
-strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
with respect to
U\times V
, which gives us the claimed result due to the equivalence in Theorem
4.4
. Indeed, utilizing the vertical shear transformation
\Phi_{-\sigma}
tells us that the shifted operator
T-\sigma J
is maximal monotone of type
(A)
with respect to the set
\Phi_{-\sigma}(U\times V)
, which is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*}-\sigma J(\bar{x}))
. Then it follows from Proposition
4.5
(i) that
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone of type
(A)
around
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
, and we are done with the proof.

5
Generalized Differentiation

Our further plans in this paper include the usage of appropriate
generalized differential tools
of variational analysis to
characterize
local maximal monotonicity of set-valued mappings. This will be done in Section
6
in the framework of Hilbert spaces. In what follows, all the spaces under consideration are assumed to be
Hilbert
without special mentioning.

This section overviews the constructions and preliminaries of generalized differentiation in Hilbert spaces that are needed for local maximal monotonicity characterizations in Section
6
. We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for the corresponding extensions and further material in more generality, and to
[
11
,
23
]
for rich variational theory and applications in finite dimensions.

Our main generalized differential characterizations of local maximal monotonicity and its strong counterparts are obtained in terms of
coderivatives
of set-valued mappings. To begin with, we define the (Fréchet)
regular normal cone
to a set
\Omega\subset X
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
by

\widehat{N}(x;\Omega):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X\;\Big|\;\limsup_{u\overset{\Omega}{\rightarrow}x}\frac{\langle x^{*},u-x\rangle}{\|u-x\|}\leq 0\Big\}.

(5.1)

Among various generalized differential constructions for mappings, in this paper we use the following two coderivatives. Given a set-valued mapping
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
between Hilbert spaces, the
regular coderivative
of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
is defined by

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*}):=\big\{x^{*}\in X\;\big|\;(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F)\big\},\quad y^{*}\in Y,

(5.2)

via the regular normal cone (
5.1
) to the graph of
F
.
The other coderivative construction for
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
, known as the (Mordukhovich)
mixed coderivative
, is given by:

\begin{array}[]{ll}D^{*}_{M}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\bar{y}^{*}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X\;\Big|&\exists\,(x_{k},y_{k},y_{k}^{*})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{y}^{*}),\;x_{k}^{*}\xrightarrow{w}\bar{x}^{*}\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty\\
&\mbox{with }\;(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F,\;x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{D}^{*}F(x_{k},y_{k})(y_{k}^{*})\Big\},\quad\bar{y}^{*}\in Y.\end{array}

(5.3)

Observe that the limiting construction in (
5.3
) employs the
mixed
convergence of
(x^{*}_{k},y^{*}_{k})
: weak for
x^{*}_{k}
and strong for
y^{*}_{k}
. This differs (
5.3
) from the
normal coderivative

D^{*}_{N}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\bar{y}^{*})
in infinite dimensions (which is not used in this paper), where both
x^{*}_{k}
and
y^{*}_{k}
converge weakly on
X\times Y
. Despite the nonconvexity of
D^{*}_{M}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\bar{y}^{*})
and
D^{*}_{N}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\bar{y}^{*})
, both coderivatives enjoy
full calculus
based on the fundamental
extremal principle
of variational analysis. They both play a prominent role in variational analysis and numerous applications; see
[
10
]
. Note that when
F
is single-valued and continuously differentiable around
\bar{x}
, then we have

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x})(y^{*})=D^{*}_{M}F(\bar{x})(y^{*})=D^{*}_{N}F(\bar{x})(y^{*})=\big\{\nabla F(\bar{x})^{*}y^{*}\big\},\quad y^{*}\in Y,

where
\nabla F(\bar{x})^{*}
stands for the adjoint derivative operator, and where
\bar{y}=F(\bar{x})
is omitted.

To conclude this section, we formulate the following simple observation used in Section
6
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
F,G:X\rightrightarrows Y
be set-valued mappings with a common point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
of their graphs. Assume that there exists a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that

\mbox{\rm gph}\,F\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\cap W.

Then for all
y^{*}\in Y
we have the equalities

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})=\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})\;\mbox{ and }\;D^{*}_{M}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})=D^{*}_{M}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*}).

[
Proof.]
It suffices to check the first equality while the second one follows from definition (
5.3
). To proceed, pick
x^{*}\in\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})
, i.e.,
(y^{*},x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. We get from (
5.2
) that

\displaystyle(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F\big)=\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,F\cap W\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\cap W\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\big),

where the first and last equalities are due to definition (
5.1
) and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in W
, where the neighborhood
W
is an open set. This gives us
(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}((\bar{x},\bar{y});\mbox{\rm gph}\,G)
, which means that
(y^{*},x^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})
as claimed.

6
Coderivative Criteria for Local Maximal Monotonicity

The main results of this section provide
necessary and sufficient conditions
for
local maximal monotonicity
of set-valued mappings on Hilbert spaces obtained in terms of their regular and mixed
coderivatives
. As a consequence, we derive also coderivative characterizations of
local strong
maximal monotonicity. The resolvent (Minty-type) characterizations of local maximal monotonicity obtained in Theorem
3.3
, being married to
coderivative calculus
and criteria for
Lipschitzian stability
, plays a crucial role in our device.

In our characterizations below, we use certain hypomonotonicity properties of set-valued mappings, which are recalled now for mappings in Hilbert spaces. It is said that
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X
is (globally)
hypomonotone
with modulus
r>0
if the mapping
T+rI
is monotone on
X
, i.e.,

\langle x^{*}_{1}-x^{*}_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq-r\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;(x_{1},x^{*}_{1}),(x_{2},x^{*}_{2})\in X\times X.

The
local hypomonotonicity
of
T
around a point
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
with modulus
r>0
is defined via the existence of a neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{*})
and a globally hypomonotone operator
\bar{T}\colon X\rightrightarrows X
with modulus
r>0
such that

\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap(U\times V)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,\bar{T}\cap(U\times V).

It has been realized in variational analysis that the hypomonotonicity properties hold for broad classes of mappings that appear in various problems of optimization, control, and numerous applications. The reader is referred to
[
13
,
15
,
23
]
and the bibliographies therein for more details.

We begin with formulating the following characterization of
global
maximal monotonicity, which is taken from
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
and is used in the proof of the main result below.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X
be a set-valued mapping with closed graph. Then we have the equivalent assertions:

(i)

T
is maximal monotone on
X
.

(ii)

T
is hypomonotone on
X
with some modulus
r>0
, and the mixed coderivative
D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)
is positive-semidefinite on
X
meaning that

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ for any }\;z\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w),\;(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T,\;\mbox{ and }\;w\in X.

To derive coderivative characterizations of
local
maximal monotonicity, we employ a novel
vertical transvection
technique of its own interest. Taking any real number
\sigma\neq 0
, define the transformation
\Delta_{\sigma}:X\times X\rightarrow X\times X
by

\Delta_{\sigma}(x,v):=(v+\sigma x,x)\;\mbox{ for any }\;(x,v)\in X\times X.

(6.1)

Observe that
\Delta_{\sigma}=\Theta\circ\Phi_{\sigma}
is the composition of the vertical shear mapping
\Phi_{\sigma}
from (
4.2
) and the reflection
\Theta
over the diagonal
\{(u,u)\in X\times X\mid u\in X\}
in the scheme

(x,v)\overset{\Phi_{\sigma}}{\mapsto}(x,v+\sigma x)\overset{\Theta}{\mapsto}(v+\sigma x,x).

It is easy to check the following properties of
\Delta_{\sigma}
:

(a)

\Delta_{\sigma}
is a homeomorphism from
X\times X
to itself as the composition of two homeomorphisms.

(b)
The inverse map
\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}=\Phi_{\sigma}^{-1}\circ\Theta^{-1}=\Phi_{-\sigma}\circ\Theta
is specified as

(v,x)\overset{\Theta}{\mapsto}(x,v)\overset{\Phi_{-\sigma}}{\mapsto}(x,v-\sigma x).

(6.2)

(c)

\Delta_{\sigma}
maps the graph of any multifunction
T:X\rightrightarrows X
into the graph of
(T+\sigma I)^{-1}
, i.e.,

\Delta_{\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,T)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1},

(6.3)

while
\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}\big(\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\big)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
.

Utilizing the transformations
\Delta_{\sigma}
and its inverse, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X
be a set-valued mapping with
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
and such that
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
is locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
. Suppose that
T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with modulus
r>0
. Then for all
\sigma>r
, the mapping
(T+\sigma I)^{-1}
has a localization
R_{\sigma}
around
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
, which is single-valued on its domain satisfying the implication

\big[w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(v)(z)\Longrightarrow-z+\sigma w\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(v),v-\sigma R_{\sigma}(v))(-w)\big]\ \text{ for all }\ v\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,R_{\sigma}.

(6.4)

[
Proof.]
As
T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with modulus
r>0
, there exists a neighborhood
W\subset X\times X
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
such that

\langle v_{1}-v_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq-r\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\;(x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2})\in\text{\rm gph}T\cap W.

(6.5)

Pick any
\sigma>r
. Since
\Delta_{\sigma}
is continuous with
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})=\Delta_{\sigma}(\bar{x},\bar{v})
, we have from (
6.3
) that
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}
, and
\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
as
W
is open. Let
R_{\sigma}
be the graphical localization of
(T+\sigma I)^{-1}
with respect to
\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
given by

\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}(W).

(6.6)

We claim that
R_{\sigma}
is single-valued on its domain. For any
(v_{i},x_{i})\in\text{\rm gph}R_{\sigma}
,
i=1,2
, it follows from (
6.2
), (
6.3
), and (
6.6
) that

(x_{i},v_{i}-\sigma x_{i})=\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}(v_{i},x_{i})\in\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma})=\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}\big(\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\big)\cap\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}\big(\Delta_{\sigma}(W)\big)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

Combining the latter with (
6.5
) tells us that

\langle v_{1}-\sigma x_{1}-(v_{2}-\sigma x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq-r\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},

which implies in turn that

\langle v_{1}-v_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq(\sigma-r)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields the estimate

\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|\geq(\sigma-r)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,

which implies
R_{\sigma}(v)
has merely one element for all
v\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,R_{\sigma}
.

Next we justify (
6.4
). Picking
v\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,R_{\sigma}
and
w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(v)(z)
gives us by (
5.2
) that

(w,-z)\in\widehat{N}\big((v,R_{\sigma}(v));\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}\big).

It is easy to deduce from definition (
5.1
) of the regular normal cone that

\displaystyle(w,-z)\in\widehat{N}\big((v,R_{\sigma}(v));\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}\big)

\displaystyle=\widehat{N}\big((v,R_{\sigma}(v));\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}(W)\big)

\displaystyle=\widehat{N}\big((v,R_{\sigma}(v));\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\big)

since
\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
is an open set containing
(v,R_{\sigma}(v))
. Therefore,

w\in\widehat{D}^{*}(T+\sigma I)^{-1}(v,R_{\sigma}(v))(z),

which can be clearly rewritten as

-z\in\widehat{D}^{*}(T+\sigma I)(R_{\sigma}(v),v)(-w).

(6.7)

Employing the coderivative sum rule from
[
10
, Theorem 1.62(i)]
) says that

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}(T+\sigma I)(R_{\sigma}(v),v)(-w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(v),v-\sigma R_{\sigma}(v))(-w)+\nabla(\sigma I)(R_{\sigma}(v))^{*}(-w)

(6.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(v),v-\sigma R_{\sigma}(v))(-w)-\sigma w.

Finally, it follows from (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) that
-z+\sigma w\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(v),v-\sigma R_{\sigma}(v))(-w)
, which completes the proof of the proposition.

Before establishing the main result of this section, we need to recall one more property of multifunction. Namely,
F:X\rightrightarrows X
is
Lipschitz-like
(or has the Aubin property) around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
with modulus
\ell\geq 0
if there are neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{v}
such that

F(x)\cap V\subset F(u)+\ell\|x-u\|\mathbb{B}\;\text{ for all }\;x,u\in U,

(6.9)

where
\mathbb{B}
stands for the unit ball in
X
. Variational analysis achieves complete characterizations of the Lipschitz-like property in both finite and infinite dimensions known as the coderivative/Mordukhovich criteria; see
[
10
,
23
]
and the reference therein.

Now we are ready to derive the aforementioned coderivative characterizations of local maximal monotonicity of set-valued mappings in Hilbert spaces.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X
be of locally closed graph around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
.

(ii)

T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with some modulus
r>0
, and there exists a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
such that

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(6.10)

(iii)

T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with some modulus
r>0
, and there exists a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
such that

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(u,v)(w)\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(6.11)

[
Proof.]
To verify (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), we can suppose by the equivalence of Theorem
3.3
that
T
is locally maximal monotone of type
(B)
relative to a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
. Thus Definition
3.1
(ii) gives us a globally maximal monotone mapping
G:X\rightrightarrows X
of closed graph such that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,G\cap W=\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(6.12)

Pick
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W
and
(z,w)\in X\times X
with
z\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)
and get by (
6.12
) and Proposition
5.1
that
z\in D^{*}_{M}G(u,v)(w)
. Combining the latter with
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,G
and the maximal monotonicity of
G
on
X
, we deduce from Lemma
6.1
that
\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0
, which justifies the claimed implication. The next implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) immediately follows from the fact that the regular coderivative is always contained in the mixed one.

Let us now prove the most challenging implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Assuming that
T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with modulus
r>0
and the fulfillment of condition (
6.11
), we deduce from Proposition
6.2
that for all
\sigma>r
the set-valued mapping
R_{\sigma}:X\rightrightarrows X
defined in (
6.6
) is single-valued on
\mbox{\rm dom}\,R_{\sigma}
, and that for all
y\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,R_{\sigma}
we have the implication

w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(y)(z)\Longrightarrow-z+\sigma w\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(y),y-\sigma R_{\sigma}(y))(-w),

(6.13)

where
\Delta_{\sigma}(u,v)
is taken from (
6.1
). We split the rest of the proof into three claims.

Claim 1:
R_{\sigma}
is Lipschitz-like (
6.9
) around
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
with modulus
\sigma^{-1}
. To justify this claim, pick
(y,R_{\sigma}(y))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
and
w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(y)(z)
remembering that
\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
. It follows from (
6.13
) that

-z+\sigma w\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(R_{\sigma}(y),y-\sigma R_{\sigma}(y))(-w).

(6.14)

By
(y,R_{\sigma}(y))\in\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
, we also observe that

(R_{\sigma}(y),y-\sigma R_{\sigma}(y))=\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}(y,R_{\sigma}(y))\in\Delta^{-1}_{\sigma}(\Delta_{\sigma}(W))=W.

(6.15)

Combining (
6.11
), (
6.14
), and (
6.15
) gives us
\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}
, and hence

\|w\|\leq\sigma^{-1}\|z\|\;\text{ whenever }\;w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(y)(z).

(6.16)

Since (
6.16
) holds for all
(y,R_{\sigma}(y))\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}\cap\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
and all
z\in X
with
w\in\widehat{D}^{*}R_{\sigma}(y)(z)
, it follows from the regular coderivative criterion in
[
10
, Theorem 4.7]
that
R_{\sigma}
is Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
with modulus
\sigma^{-1}
as claimed.

Claim 2:
T
is locally monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
.
Indeed, it follows from Claim 1 that there exist neighborhoods
V
of
\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x}
and
U
of
\bar{x}
for which

R_{\sigma}(y_{1})\cap U\subset R_{\sigma}(y_{2})+\sigma^{-1}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\mathbb{B}\;\text{ whenever }\;y_{1},y_{2}\in V.

(6.17)

Furthermore, we have the condition

R_{\sigma}(y)\cap U\neq\emptyset\;\text{ for all }\;y\in V.

(6.18)

As
\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})=(\bar{x},\bar{v})
and
W
is an open set containing
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
, we may shrink
U
and
V
if necessary such that
\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(V\times U)\subset W
. Picking two pairs
(u_{i},v_{i})\in\text{\rm gph}T\cap\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(V\times U)
,
i=1,2
, brings us to the equalities

\displaystyle(v_{i}+\sigma u_{i},u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sigma}(u_{i},v_{i})\in\Delta_{\sigma}\big(\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(V\times U)\big)=\Delta_{\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,T)\cap\Delta_{\sigma}\big(\Delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(V\times U)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\cap(V\times U)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T+\sigma I)^{-1}\cap(V\times U)\cap\Delta_{\sigma}(W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gph}\,R_{\sigma}\cap(V\times U).

Since
R_{\sigma}(y)
has at most one element for each
y\in X
, it follows from (
6.17
) that

u_{1}\in u_{2}+\sigma^{-1}\|(v_{1}+\sigma u_{1})-(v_{2}+\sigma u_{2})\|\mathbb{B}

which implies in turn that

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|\leq\sigma^{-1}\|v_{1}-v_{2}+\sigma(u_{1}-u_{2})\|.

Therefore, we arrive at the estimate

\sigma^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}\leq\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|^{2}+2\sigma\langle v_{1}-v_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle+\sigma^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2},

which consequently gives us the condition

\langle v_{1}-v_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle\geq-(2\sigma)^{-1}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|^{2}.

By the arbitrary choice of
\sigma\in(r,\infty)
, we can let
\sigma\to\infty
in the above inequality and get

\langle v_{1}-v_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle\geq 0,

which justifies the local monotonicity of
T
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
.

Claim 3:
The mapping
(T+\sigma I)^{-1}
admits a single-valued localization around
(\bar{v}+\sigma\bar{x},\bar{x})
.

Since
V\times U\subset\Delta_{\sigma}(W)
and the mapping
R_{\sigma}
defined in (
6.6
) is single-valued on its domain, we conclude that
R_{\sigma}|_{V}
is also single-valued due to (
6.18
), which justifies Claim 3. Employing finally Theorem
3.3
ensures that
T
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
6.3
, we now derive the following complete coderivative characterizations of
local strong maximal monotonicity
of set-valued operators in Hilbert spaces. Note that the results below have been obtained in
[
13
]
independently of the characterizations of local maximal monotonicity in Theorem
6.3
by using an essentially more involved device.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X
be a set-valued mapping of closed graph around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
. Then for any
\sigma>0
, we have the equivalent assertions:

(i)

T
is locally strongly maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with modulus
\sigma
.

(ii)

T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
, and there is a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(iii)

T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
, and there is a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(u,v)(w)\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W.

(6.19)

[
Proof.]
Assume (i) and show that (ii) holds. Indeed, Proposition
4.3
(ii) implies that
T-\sigma I
is locally maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
. Applying Theorem
6.3
gives us a neighborhood
Q
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
such that

\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ for all }\ x\in D^{*}_{M}(T-\sigma I)(s,t)(y)\ \text{ and }\ (s,t)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma I)\cap Q.

(6.20)

Since
\Phi_{\sigma}(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})=(\bar{x},\bar{v})
and the set
Q\ni(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
is open, its image
W:=\Phi_{\sigma}(Q)
is a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
. Picking
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W
and
z\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)
, let us check that
\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}
by using (
6.20
). To proceed, for
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W
we have

\displaystyle(u,v-\sigma u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\sigma}(u,v)\in\Phi_{-\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{-\sigma}(\mbox{\rm gph}\,T)\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(W)=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma I)\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(\Phi_{\sigma}(Q))=\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma I)\cap Q.

Employing the coderivative sum rule from
[
11
, Theorem 3.10]
with
F_{1}:=T-\sigma I
,
F_{2}:=I
,
(u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T
, and
w\in X
ensures that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)&\subset D^{*}_{M}(T-\sigma I)(u,v-\sigma u)(w)+D^{*}_{M}(\sigma I)(u,\sigma u)(w)\\
&=D^{*}_{M}(T-\sigma I)(u,v-\sigma u)(w)+\sigma w,\end{array}

which results in the inclusions

z-\sigma w\in D^{*}_{M}T(u,v)(w)-\sigma w\subset D^{*}_{M}(T-\sigma I)(u,v-\sigma u)(w).

As
(u,v-\sigma u)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma I)\cap Q
and
z-\sigma w\in D^{*}_{M}(T-\sigma I)(u,v-\sigma u)(w)
, (
6.20
) yields

\displaystyle\langle z-\sigma w,w\rangle\geq 0,\;\mbox{ i.e.,}\;\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2},

and thus justifies (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Since (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is trivial, it remains to verify
(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i).

To this end, suppose that
T
is locally hypomonotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
and there exists a neighborhood
W
such that (
6.19
) holds. The hypomonotonicity of
T
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
ensures the one for
T-\sigma I
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
. By the regular coderivative sum rule from
[
10
, Theorem 1.62(i)]
and elementary calculations, we check that

\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ whenever }\ x\in\widehat{D}^{*}(T-\sigma I)(s,t)(y)\ \text{ and }\ (s,t)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,(T-\sigma I)\cap\Phi_{-\sigma}(W).

(6.22)

Due to (
6.22
) and the local hypomonotonicity of
T-\sigma I
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
, Theorem
6.3
gives us the local maximal monotonicity of
T-\sigma I
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v}-\sigma\bar{x})
. Finally, we deduce from Proposition
4.5
(i) that the original mapping
T
is locally strongly maximal monotone around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with modulus
\sigma
, which therefore completes the proof.

7
Concluding Remarks

The paper provides a systematic study of local maximal monotonicity and its strong counterparts for set-valued mappings in infinite-dimensional spaces, while all the established characterizations of these properties are new even in finite dimension. The study of local maximal monotonicity, besides being of its own interest, is highly motivated by applications to important problems of optimization and operations research by using powerful methods of variational analysis. A large portion of our future research will aim at applications to recent concepts of variational and strong variational convexity in both finite and infinite dimensions that have drawn a great interest in various aspects of optimization theory and numerical algorithms; see, e.g.,
[
5
,
7
,
21
,
22
]
.

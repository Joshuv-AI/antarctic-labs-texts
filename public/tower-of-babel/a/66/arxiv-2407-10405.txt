Abstract
. In this paper we describe the geodesics on the Kähler cone of the Heisenberg group. Furthermore we also prove that this is not a complete manifold.



1.
Introduction

The Heisenberg group appears as one of the most important objects in the study of not only many areas of mathematics but also in other fields such as physics and quantum mechanics. In particular, and for what concerns the purposes of this article, the one point compactification of the Heisenberg group is identified with the boundary of the complex hyperbolic plane and therefore it plays a vital role in the study of complex hyperbolic geometry.

The complex hyperbolic plane
{\bf H}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, endowed with the Bergman metric, is a Kähler manifold with constant holomorphic sectional curvature -1 and real sectional curvature pinched between -1 and -1/4. The group
{\rm SU}(2,1)
is a triple cover of the group
{\rm PU}(2,1)
of the holomorphic isometries of
{\bf H}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
. A full description of the geodesics of complex hyperbolic plane may be found in the book of Goldman,
[
3
]
, or, alternatively, in the notes of Parker,
[
8
]
.

Besides the Bergman metric, there is another natural metric which can be defined on the underlying manifold of complex hyperbolic plane. The full discussion about this metric may be found for instance in
[
5
]
; we repeat here in brief its main features. The starting point of the construction of this metric is the Heisenberg group
\mathfrak{H}
. The Heisenberg group is also a contact manifold and a CR-manifold. It turns out that from these structures it is defined a contact metric structure which is Sasakian. This is equivalent to say that the manifold
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H})=\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathfrak{H}
, that is, the horospherical parametrisation of complex hyperbolic plane, endowed with the warped product metric and a complex structure which is an extension of the almost complex structure of the horizontal space of
\mathfrak{H}
is a Kähler manifold. In contrast to the Bergman metric, it is of non constant negative holomorphic curvature. The manifold
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H})
is the Kähler cone of the Heisenberg group and the goal of this paper is to describe its geodesics; incidentally, we do the same for the geodesics of the Heisenberg group as a Sasakian manifold. We also prove that the Kähler cone of the Heisenberg group is not complete.

The paper is organised as follows: in next section, we review the definition and properties of the Heisenberg group and its Riemannian cone constructed in
[
5
]
. In section
3
, we review the geodesics of
\mathfrak{H}
in
[
6
]
. Section
4
is intended to describe the geodesics on the Riemannian cone of the Heisenberg group. Finally,
we briefly prove that the Riemannian cone of the Heisenberg group is not geodesically complete.

2.1.
Contact metric structure of the Heisenberg group

The (first) Heisenberg group
\mathfrak{H}
is the set
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
with multiplication
*
given by

(z,t)*(w,s)=(z+w,t+s+2\Im(z\overline{w})).

The Heisenberg group
\mathfrak{H}
is a 2-step nilpotent Lie group; a left invariant basis for its Lie algebra comprises the following vector fields:

\displaystyle X=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2y\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad Y=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-2x\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

The only nontrivial Lie bracket relation between them is

[X,Y]=-4T.

A left-invariant 1-form
\omega
is defined in
\mathfrak{H}
by

\omega=dt+2xdy-2ydx=dt+2\Im(\overline{z}dz).

\omega
is a contact form for
\mathfrak{H}
:
\omega\wedge d\omega\neq 0
and in fact
dm=-(1/4)\;\omega\wedge d\omega
, where
dm
is the Haar measure for
\mathfrak{H}
.
The Reeb vector field for
\omega
is
T
.
The kernel
\ker\omega=\mathcal{H}
is spanned by the vector fields
X,Y
.
Consider the almost complex structure
J
defined on
\mathcal{H}
by

JX=Y
,
JY=-X.

Then
J
is compatible with
d\omega
and moreover,
\mathcal{H}
is a strictly pseudoconvex CR structure; that is,
d\omega
is positively oriented on
\mathcal{H}
.

A sub-Riemannian metric
g_{cc}
in
\mathfrak{H}
is defined in
\mathcal{H}
by the relations

g_{cc}(X,X)=g_{cc}(Y,Y)=1,\quad g_{cc}(X,Y)=0.

The corresponding sub-Riemannian tensor is then given by

g_{cc}=dx^{2}+dy^{2}.

Given
L>0
we are able to define a contact Riemannian structures in
\mathfrak{H}
such that the frame

\{X,Y,T_{L}=T/\sqrt{L}\}

is orthonormal. In this way, we have that the Riemannian tensor is

g_{L}=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+L\omega^{2}=ds^{2}_{cc}+L\omega^{2}.

Recall that if
(M,\omega)
is a 3-dimensional contact manifold and
(\mathcal{H}=\ker(\eta),J)
is its CR-structure, then the almost complex structure
J
on
\mathcal{H}
can be extended to an endomorphism
\phi
of the whole tangent bundle
{\rm T}(M)
by setting
\phi(\xi)=0
, where
\xi
is the Reeb field of
\omega
. Subsequently, a canonical Riemannian metric
g
is defined in
M
from the relations

\omega(X)=g(X,\xi),\quad\frac{1}{2}d\omega(X,Y)=g(\phi X,Y),\quad\phi^{2}(X)=-X+\omega(X)\xi,

for all vector fields
X,Y
in
{\mathfrak{X}}(M)
.
We then call
(M;\omega,\xi,\phi,g)
the contact Riemannian structure on
M
associated to the contact structure
(M,\omega)
.

The complex structure
J
on
\mathfrak{H}
can be extended to an endomorphism
\phi
of the whole tangent bundle by setting
\phi(T_{L})=0
.
Then it is proved in
[
5
]
that the group
\mathfrak{H}
with contact form
\sqrt{L}\omega
, Reeb vector field
T_{L},
endomorphism
\phi
and Riemannian metric
g_{L}
is a contact Riemannian manifold if and only if
L=1/4
.
Being in accordance with the notation in
[
5
]
, we will hereafter write
g
instead of
g_{1/4}
,
\widetilde{T}
instead of
2T
and
\widetilde{\omega}
instead of
(1/2)\omega
.

2.2.
Kähler cone

With the contact metric structure described above, it is proved in
[
5
]
that
\mathfrak{H}
is a Sasakian manifold. That is equivalent to say that the manifold

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H})=\mathfrak{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
endowed with the metric

g_{r}=dr^{2}+r^{2}g

is Kähler. Explicitely:

•

An orthonormal basis for the metric
g_{r}
comprises the vector fields

X_{r}=(1/r)X,\quad Y_{r}=(1/r)Y,\quad T_{r}=(1/r)\widetilde{T},\quad R_{r}=d/dr.

Note that all Lie brackets vanish besides

[X_{r},Y_{r}]=-(2/r)T_{r},\quad[X_{r},R_{r}]=(1/r)X_{r},\quad[Y_{r},R_{r}]=(1/r)Y_{r},\quad[T_{r},R_{r}]=(1/r)T_{r}.

•

The endomorphism
\mathbb{J}
defined by

\mathbb{J}X_{r}=Y_{r},\quad\mathbb{J}Y_{r}=-X_{r},\quad\mathbb{J}T_{r}=-R_{r},\quad\mathbb{J}R_{r}=T_{r},

is a complex structure for
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H})
which preserves the metric
g_{r}
.

•

The 2-form
\Omega_{r}
defined by

\Omega_{r}=d\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\widetilde{\omega}\right)

is the fundamental form for the Hermitian manifold
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H}),g_{r},\mathbb{J})
and it is closed. Thus the manifold
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H}),g_{r},\mathbb{J},\Omega_{r})
is Kähler.

3.
Geodesics of the Heisenberg group

An exhaustive treatment of the geodesics of
(\mathfrak{H},g)
may be found for instance in
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
. We repeat in brief the discussion below. Let
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s),t(s))
be a smooth curve defined in an interval
I=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, where
\epsilon>0
and is sufficiently small, and suppose that
\gamma(0)=(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=p_{0}
. The tangent vector
\dot{\gamma}(s)
at a point
\gamma(s)
is then

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(s)=\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{x}\partial_{x}+\dot{y}\partial_{y}+\dot{t}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{x}X+\dot{y}Y+(1/2)(\dot{t}+2x\dot{y}-2y\dot{x})\widetilde{T}.

We set

f(s)=\dot{x}(s),\quad g(s)=\dot{y}(s),\quad h(s)=(1/2)(\dot{t}(s)+2x(s)\dot{y}(s)-2y(s)\dot{x}(s)).

We may assume that
\gamma
is of unit speed:
f^{2}+g^{2}+h^{2}=1
.
Recall from
[
5
]
that if
\nabla
is the Riemannian connection then the following hold:

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}X=0,\quad\nabla_{X}Y=-\widetilde{T},\quad\nabla_{X}\widetilde{T}=Y,

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}X=\widetilde{T},\quad\nabla_{Y}Y=0,\quad\nabla_{Y}\widetilde{T}=-X,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}X=Y,\quad\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}Y=-X,\quad\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}\widetilde{T}=0.

Recall again from
[
5
]
that
the covariant derivative of
\dot{\gamma}
is

\frac{D\dot{\gamma}}{ds}=\dot{f}X+\dot{g}Y+\dot{h}\widetilde{T}+f\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+g\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}Y+h\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\widetilde{T}.

Since

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X=f\nabla_{X}X+g\nabla_{Y}X+h\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}X=g\widetilde{T}+hY,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}Y=f\nabla_{X}Y+g\nabla_{Y}Y+h\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}Y=-f\widetilde{T}-hX,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}{\widetilde{T}}=f\nabla_{X}\widetilde{T}+g\nabla_{Y}\widetilde{T}+h\nabla_{\widetilde{T}}\widetilde{T}=fY-gX,

we deduce

\frac{D\dot{\gamma}}{ds}=(\dot{f}-2gh)X+(\dot{g}+2fh)Y+\dot{h}\widetilde{T}.

Therefore, the geodesic equations are

(1)

\dot{f}=2gh,\quad\dot{g}=-2fh,\quad\dot{h}=0,\quad f^{2}+g^{2}+h^{2}=1.

In the special case
h=0
, that is,
\gamma
is horizontal, we obtain the straight lines

\gamma(s)=(as+x_{0},\;bs+y_{0},\;2(ay_{0}-bx_{0})s+t_{0}),

where
a,b
are real constants and
a^{2}+b^{2}=1
. Those are all
g_{cc}
-geodesics.

In the case
h\neq 0,
one can get
h=c,
where
c\neq 0
is a constant. We now write
F=f+ig,

z(s)=x(s)+iy(s)
and
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}
. Then the above system (
1
) is written equivalently as

\dot{F}=-2ic\;F,

and
has general solution

F(s)=ke^{-2ics},\quad k\in\mathbb{C},\quad|k|^{2}+c^{2}=1.

We therefore have
|c|\leq 1
. If
|c|=1
, then
k=0
and

\gamma(s)=(x_{0},\;y_{0},\;2cs+t_{0}),

is a vertical geodesics through
p_{0}
.
If now
|c|<1
and
\gamma(s)=(z(s),t(s))
, then

(2)

\displaystyle z(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ik(e^{-2ics}-1)}{2c}+z_{0},

(3)

\displaystyle t(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\left((1+c^{2})s-\frac{(1-c^{2})\sin(2cs)}{2c}-\Re(\overline{z_{0}}k(e^{-2ics}-1))\right)+t_{0}.

We observe that the curves in (
2
) are Euclidean circles, which are the same as the projection of non linear
g_{cc}
-geodesics on the complex plane. However, Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) do not give any non linear
g_{cc}
-geodesic. In fact, in order this to happen we should have
\dot{t}(s)+2\Im(\overline{z(s)}\dot{z}(s)=0
, that is, the geodesic must be horizontal.
This condition leads to
h=0,
i.e.,

c=0
which is impossible.

4.
Geodesics of the Riemannian cone

Let
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s),t(s),r(s))
be a smooth curve in the Kähler cone and suppose that
\gamma(0)=(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0},r_{0})=q_{0}
. Its tangent vector is

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(s)=\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{x}\partial_{x}+\dot{y}\partial_{y}+\dot{t}\partial_{t}+\dot{r}R_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\dot{x}X_{r}+r\dot{y}Y_{r}+(r/2)(\dot{t}+2x\dot{y}-2y\dot{x})T_{r}+\dot{r}R_{r}.

We set

\displaystyle f(s)=r(s)\dot{x}(s),\quad g(s)=r(s)\dot{y}(s),

\displaystyle h(s)=(1/2)r(s)(\dot{t}(s)+2x(s)\dot{y}(s)-2y(s)\dot{x}(s)),\quad k(s)=\dot{r}(s),

and we may suppose that
f^{2}+g^{2}+h^{2}+k^{2}=1
. Recall again from
[
5
]
that if
\nabla^{r}
is the Riemannian connection, then

\displaystyle\nabla^{r}_{X_{r}}X_{r}=-(1/r)R_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{Y_{r}}X_{r}=(1/r)T_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{T_{r}}X_{r}=(1/r)Y_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{R_{r}}X_{r}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla^{r}_{X_{r}}Y_{r}=-(1/r)T_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{Y_{r}}Y_{r}=-(1/r)R_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{T_{r}}Y_{r}=-(1/r)X_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{R_{r}}Y_{r}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla^{r}_{X_{r}}T_{r}=(1/r)Y_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{Y_{r}}T_{r}=-(1/r)X_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{T_{r}}T_{r}=-(1/r)R_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{R_{r}}T_{r}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla^{r}_{X_{r}}R_{r}=(1/r)X_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{Y_{r}}R_{r}=(1/r)Y_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{T_{r}}R_{r}=(1/r)T_{r},\quad\nabla^{r}_{R_{r}}R_{r}=0.

The covariant derivative of
\dot{\gamma}
is

\displaystyle\frac{D\dot{\gamma}}{ds}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{f}X_{r}+\dot{g}Y_{r}+\dot{h}T_{r}+\dot{k}R_{r}

\displaystyle+f\nabla^{r}_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{r}+g\nabla^{r}_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{r}+h\nabla^{r}_{\dot{\gamma}}T_{r}+k\nabla^{r}_{\dot{\gamma}}R_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{f}X_{r}+\dot{g}Y_{r}+\dot{h}T_{r}+\dot{k}R_{r}

\displaystyle+(f/r)(-fR_{r}+gT_{r}+hY_{r})

\displaystyle+(g/r)(-fT_{r}-gR_{r}-hX_{r})

\displaystyle+(h/r)(fY_{r}-gX_{r}-hR_{r})

\displaystyle+(k/r)(fX_{r}+gY_{r}+hT_{r}).

From the vanishing of the covariant derivative and the unit speed assumption we obtain the following geodesic equations:

(4)

\displaystyle\dot{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/r)(2gh-kf),

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/r)(-2fh-kg),

(6)

\displaystyle\dot{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/r)(-kh),

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/r)(1-k^{2}).

Equation (
7
) also reads as

r\ddot{r}+(\dot{r})^{2}=1.

The positive solutions to this ODE are of the form

r(s)=\sqrt{(s+c_{1})^{2}+c_{2}},\quad c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\;c_{2}\geq 0.

From the initial condition
r(0)=r_{0}
, we also have
c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}=r_{0}^{2}
; thus

r(s)=\sqrt{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}},\;c_{1}\in\mathbb{R},\;r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}\geq 0.

From Equation (
6
) we obtain

h(s)=\frac{c_{3}}{r(s)}=\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}}},\quad c_{3}\in\mathbb{R}.

We now have

f^{2}+g^{2}=1-h^{2}-k^{2}=\frac{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}}{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}}\geq 0.

On the other hand, from

c_{3}/r(s)=(1/2)r(s)(\dot{t}(s)+2x(s)\dot{y}(s)-2y(s)\dot{x}(s))

we obtain that

(8)

\dot{t}(s)+2x(s)\dot{y}(s)-2y(s)\dot{x}(s)=2c_{3}/(s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}).

In the case where
r_{0}^{2}=c_{1}^{2}+c_{3}^{2},
one can get that
f\equiv g\equiv 0
which yields to

x(s)=x_{0},\quad y(s)=y_{0}.

Also, from
\dot{t}(s)=2\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}/(s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2})
we have

t(s)=2\arctan\left(\frac{s\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}}{r_{0}^{2}+c_{1}s}\right)+t_{0},\quad r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}>0.

In the case where
r_{0}^{2}=c_{1}^{2}
, it is easy to know that
r(s)=\pm s+r_{0}
and
t(s)=t_{0}
.
Hence the resulting geodesics in this case are of the form:

\gamma_{c}(s)=\left(x_{0},\;y_{0},\;2\arctan\left(\frac{s\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}}{r_{0}^{2}+c_{1}s}\right)+t_{0},\sqrt{s^{2}+2cs+r_{0}^{2}}\right),\quad c\in\mathbb{R}

or straight lines of the form

\gamma(s)=(x_{0},\;y_{0},\;t_{0},\;s+r_{0}).

In the case where
r_{0}^{2}>c_{1}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}
, by plugging
r(s),h(s)
and
k(s)
into Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2c_{3}g-(s+c_{1})f}{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}},

\displaystyle\dot{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2c_{3}f+(s+c_{1})g}{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}}.

We set
F=f+ig
and the system of geodesic equations becomes the following complex ODE of the first order:

\dot{F}=-\frac{s+c_{1}+2ic_{3}}{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}}F,

where

|F|^{2}=\frac{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}}{s^{2}+2c_{1}s+r_{0}^{2}}>0.

Then we get

F(s)=\frac{Ce^{i\Phi(s)}}{r(s)},

where
C\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
|C|^{2}=r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}-c_{3}^{2}
and

(9)

\Phi(s)=-\frac{2c_{3}}{\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}}\arctan\left(\frac{s+c_{1}}{\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}}}\right).

Since
F(s)=r(s)\dot{z}(s)
, we have the complex ODE of the first order

\dot{z}(s)=\frac{Ce^{i\Phi(s)}}{r^{2}(s)},

which gives

z(s)=\frac{iCe^{i\Phi(s)}}{2c_{3}}+D,\quad D\in\mathbb{C}.

From the initial conditions we then obtain

D=z_{0}-\frac{iCe^{i\Phi(0)}}{2c_{3}}.

Hence, we have

z(s)=\frac{iC\left(e^{i\Phi(s)}-e^{i\Phi(0)}\right)}{2c_{3}}+z_{0}.

Now from Eq. (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\dot{t}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{c_{3}}{r^{2}(s)}-\Im(\overline{z}(s)\dot{z}(s))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2c_{3}^{2}+|C|^{2}}{c_{3}r^{2}(s)}-\frac{|C|^{2}}{c_{3}r^{2}(s)}\cos\left(\Phi(s)-\Phi(0)\right)-\frac{2}{r^{2}(s)}\Im\left(\overline{z_{0}}Ce^{i\Phi(s)}\right).

By integrating and taking under account the initial conditions we obtain

\displaystyle t(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1+\frac{|C|^{2}}{2c_{3}^{2}}\right)(\Phi(s)-\Phi(0))+\frac{|C|^{2}}{2c_{3}^{2}}\sin(\Phi(s)-\Phi(0))

\displaystyle-\Re\left(\overline{z_{0}}C\left(e^{i\Phi(s)}-e^{i\Phi(0)}\right)\right)+t_{0},

where
\Phi(s)
is given by (
9
).

4.1.
Non-completeness

In
[
9
]
, the authors considered the non-completeness of the geodesics under the metric
g_{\mathcal{U}}=dr^{2}+r^{2}dt^{2}
on
\mathcal{U}
, where
\mathcal{U}=\{(t,r):t\in\mathbb{R},\;r>0\}
is the half plane.
It is also proved in
[
5
]
that
\mathcal{U}
can be embedded into
C(\mathfrak{H})
as a submanifold by setting

\iota_{\mathcal{U}}(t,r)=(0,2t,r),

which is totally geodesic with
g_{\mathcal{U}}=\iota_{\mathcal{U}}^{\ast}g_{r}.

We now obtain the following:

Corollary 4.1
.

The totally geodesic submanifold
\mathcal{U}
of
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H}),g_{r})
is not complete. Hence the same holds for
(\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{H}),g_{r})
.

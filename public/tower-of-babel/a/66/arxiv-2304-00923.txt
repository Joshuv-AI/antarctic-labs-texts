Planar site percolation via tree embeddings
Zhongyang Li

Abstract
. We prove that for any any infinite, connected, planar graph G G properly embedded in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} with minimal vertex degree at least 7, the i.i.d. Bernoulli( p p ) site percolation on G G a.s. has infinitely many infinite 1-clusters and for any p ∈ ( p c s ​ i ​ t ​ e , 1 − p c s ​ i ​ t ​ e ) p\in(p_{c}^{site},1-p_{c}^{site}) . Moreover, p c s ​ i ​ t ​ e < 1 2 p_{c}^{site}<\frac{1}{2} , so the above interval is non-empty. This confirms a conjecture of Benjamini and Schramm in 1996 (Conjecture 7 in [ 5 ] ). The proof is based on a novel construction of embedded trees on such graphs, which not only proves the existence of infinitely many infinite clusters when p p is in a neighborhood of 1 2 \frac{1}{2} , but also proves the exponential decay of point-to-point connection probabilities by constructing infinitely many trees separating two vertices as the distance of the two vertices goes to infinity.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview and Main Results

Introduced by Broadbent and Hammersley in 1957 (see
[
9
]
) to study the random spread of a fluid through a medium, percolation has been a celebrated model illustrating the phase transition, magnetization, or the spread of pandemic diseases; see
[
14
,
12
]
for recent accounts of the theory.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. We write
e=\langle u,v\rangle
for an edge with endpoints
u
and
v
; where
u,v\in V
and
e\in E
.
The (
vertex
-)
degree
of a vertex
v\in V
is the number
of edges incident to
v
; i.e. edges one of whose endpoints is
v
. We say a graph is locally finite if each vertex has finite degree.

Assume
G=(V,E)
is an infinite, locally finite, connected graph.
A site percolation configuration
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{V}
is a an assignment to each vertex in
G
of either state 0 or state 1. A cluster in
\omega
is a maximal connected set of vertices in which each vertex has the same state in
\omega
. A cluster may be a 0-cluster or a 1-cluster depending on the common state of vertices in the cluster. A cluster may be finite or infinite depending on the total number of vertices in it. We say that percolation occurs in
\omega
if there exists an infinite 1-cluster in
\omega
.

In this paper we focus on site percolation on planar graphs; i.e. graphs that can be “nicely drawn” in the plane. More precisely, we have the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A planar graph
G
is a graph that can be drawn in the plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, with vertices represented
by points and edges represented by curves, such that edges can intersect only at vertices.

We call a drawing of the graph into the plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a proper embedding in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
if any compact subset
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
intersects at most finitely many edges and vertices.

See also Section 2 of
[
7
]
for the definition of proper embeddings. From Definition
1.1
we see that if a graph
G
can be properly embedded into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, then it is locally finite. Since
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is homeomorphic to any simply-connected open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, a graph can be properly embedded into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
if and only if it can be properly embedded into any simply-connected open subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, in particular the unit disk (with appropriate metric this gives the hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
); see
[
11
,
7
,
26
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
for graphs embedded into the hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
and statistical mechanical models on such graphs. However, in general a graph cannot be circle packed in both
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
(
[
21
,
22
]
).

Of particular interest is the i.i.d. Bernoulli site percolation on a graph. In such a model, an independent Bernoulli random variable, which takes value 1 with probability
p\in[0,1]
, is associated to each vertex. We use
{\mathbb{P}}_{p}
to denote the probability measure for i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) site percolation on
G
.

For the i.i.d. Bernoulli site percolation, define

(1.1)

\displaystyle p_{c}^{site}(G):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{p\in[0,1]:\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\ \mathrm{site\ percolation\ on}\ G\ \mathrm{has\ an\ infinite\ 1-cluster\ a.s.}\}

(1.2)

\displaystyle p_{u}^{site}(G):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{p\in[0,1]:\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)\ \mathrm{site\ percolation\ on}\ G\ \mathrm{has\ a\ unique\ infinite\ 1-cluster\ a.s.}\}

It follows immediately that
p_{c}^{site}(G)\leq p_{u}^{site}(G)
. If strict inequalities hold, then for certain
p
, there is a strictly positive probability that in the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) percolation at least two infinite 1-clusters exist.
A number of problems related to the uniqueness and non-uniqueness of infinite percolation clusters
were formulated by Benjamini and Schramm in
their influential paper
[
5
]
, including the following one.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(Conjecture 7 in
[
5
]
). Consider site percolation on an infinite, connected,
planar graph G with minimal degree at least 7. Then, for any
p\in(p_{c}^{site},1-p_{c}^{site})
, we
have
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters. in the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) site percolation on
G
. Moreover, it is the case that
p_{c}^{site}<\frac{1}{2}
, so the above interval is
invariably non-empty.

A graph is called vertex-transitive (resp. quasi-transitive) when there is a unique orbit (at most finitely many orbits) of vertices under the action of its automorphism group. Invariant percolation processes on quasi-transitive graphs have been studied extensively, in which the symmetry property associated with the quasi-transitivity makes the analysis more convenient, and interesting techniques were developed, for example, and the mass-transport principle (MTP), see
[
3
,
4
]
. Bernoulli percolation on vertex-transitive graphs embedded into the hyperbolic plane was studied in
[
25
,
7
]
, where
p_{c}<p_{u}
were proved.
Certain critical exponents were proved to exist and to take their mean-field values for independent percolation on infinite, locally finite, connected transitive, non-amenable, planar, one-ended graphs
[
37
]
.
Using the result
p_{c}^{bond}(G)+p_{u}^{bond}(G^{+})=1
in
[
7
,
29
]
and an explicit correspondence between bond configurations on the dual pairs of graphs
(G,G^{+})
and site configurations on the matching pairs of graphs
(G,G_{*})
,
Conjecture
1.2
was proved in
[
16
]
when the graph
G
is infinite, locally finite, planar, 2-connected, simple and quasi-transitive;
see also
[
26
]
for a proof of Conjecture
1.2
in the special case when the graph is a vertex-transitive triangular tiling of the hyperbolic plane with miminal vertex degree at least 7.
Furthermore, it is proved in
[
17
]
that if the graph
G
is infinite, locally finite, planar, 2-connected, simple, transitive and not a planar triangulation, then
\mathbb{P}_{1-p_{c}^{site}}
a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters. Without the quasi-transitivity assumption, many existing techniques, e.g. 0-1 laws of events invariant under group actions such as the a.s. constant of the number of infinite clusters(
[
2
,
10
]
), MTP, etc., do not work any more.

One approach to overcome this difficulty is based on the “uniform percolation” defined in
[
35
]
. In the absence of quasi-transitivity, the “uniform percolation” gives “similarities” to different vertices which make the analysis work. One important consequence of the “uniform percolation” is the “stability of infinite clusters”, which states that for any
0\leq p_{1}<p_{2}\leq 1
, if there is uniform percolation at level
p_{1}
, then every infinite 1-cluster in the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p_{2}
) percolation a.s. contains at least one infinite 1-cluster in the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p_{1}
) percolation. See also Theorem 1.10 in
[
23
]
.

One class of graphs satisfying the uniform percolation condition are the“semi-transitive graphs” defined in
[
18
]
, which are more general than quasi-transitive graphs but still quite restrictive. Moreover, there are examples of bounded-degree planar graphs which do not have uniform percolation near
p_{c}^{site}(G)
yet the conclusions of Conjecture
1.2
still hold. We shall discuss more on the approach of uniform percolation in a companion paper
[
27
]
.

In general, the uniform percolation assumption is not straightforward to be verified, especially when
p
is close to
p_{c}^{site}
.
The main goal of this paper is to investigate the conjecture for planar graphs without the quasi-transitive assumption or the uniform percolation assumption when
p
is close to
p_{c}^{site}
. In the meanwhile, we hope to develop new techniques to study percolation on general locally finite graphs.

The
p_{c}^{site}<\frac{1}{2}
part of Conjecture
1.2
was proved in
[
20
]
. As we shall see, the techniques developed in this paper lead to a different proof of the fact that
p_{c}^{site}<\frac{1}{2}
.

Here is the main result proved in this paper:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be an infinite, connected graph properly embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that the minimal vertex degree at least 7. Then, for any
p\in(p_{c}^{site}(G),1-p_{c}^{site}(G))
, a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters in the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) site percolation on
G
. Moreover, it is the case that
p_{c}^{site}(G)<\frac{1}{2}
, so the above interval is
invariably non-empty.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on a novel construction of trees with vertex degrees 3 or 4 embedded into a graph satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
. It is known from
[
6
]
that every locally finite graph with Cheeger constant at least
n
(where
n
is a positive integer) has a spanning forest in which every tree is isomorphic to
T_{n+1}
; where
T_{n+1}
is the tree whose root has degree
n
, and other vertices have degree
n+2
. In
[
6
]
such spanning forests are constructed by flows on graphs, and the geometries of the embedded trees are not immediately clear from the construction. For planar graphs satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, we construct embedded trees in a somewhat explicit way, which can be used to prove the exponential decay of the two point connection probabilities as the distance of the the two points goes to infinity.

Matching graphs were introduced by Sykes and Essam
[
38
]

and explored further by Kesten
[
24
]
. Matching pairs of graphs may be considered as site-percolation analogs of dual pairs of graphs in bond-percolation, and play an essential role in the proofs of the main results of the paper.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite, simple, planar graph. Fix an embedding of
G
into the plane. The
matching graph

G_{*}=(V,E_{*})
is the graph whose vertex set is the same as that of
G
; and for
u,v\in V
and
u\neq v
,
\langle u,v\rangle\in E_{*}
if and only if
one of the following two conditions holds

(1)

\langle u,v\rangle\in E
; or

(2)

\langle u,v\rangle\notin E
, but
u
and
v
share a finite face in
G
.

Definition 1.5
.

The number of ends of a connected graph is the supreme over its finite subgraphs of the number of infinite components that remain after removing the subgraph.

The graphs considered in this paper may have more than one end; therefore we allow infinite faces in our graphs. In Definition
1.4
,
E_{*}\setminus E
contains only edges joining two vertices sharing finite faces; so that
G_{*}
is locally finite. If the proper embedding of graph
G
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
has only finite faces, then Definition
1.4
coincides with the usual definition of matching graphs. It is known from Lemma 4.1 of
[
30
]
that any one-ended, locally finite planar graph in which every face is finite can be properly embedded into the plane.

Here we sketch the main steps to prove Theorem
1.3
as well as the organization of the paper.

(1)

Explicitly construct a tree
T
embedded in
G
as subgraph, and show that
p_{c}^{site}(G)\leq p_{c}^{site}(T)<\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{2}}
a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters; see Section

.

(2)

Show that when

, the two point connection probabilities in the matching graph

(hence in

4
.

(3)

With the help of step (2), show that when
p_{c}^{site}(G_{*})<p<1-p_{c}^{site}(T)
(resp.
p_{c}^{site}(G)<p<1-p_{c}^{site}(T)
),
\mathbb{P}_{p}
a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-*-clusters (resp. infinitely many infinite 1-clusters).

(4)

It follows from step (3) and planar duality that when
p_{c}^{site}(T)<p<1-p_{c}^{site}(G_{*})
,
\mathbb{P}_{p}
a.s. infinitely 1-clusters have infinitely many ends;
see Section
3
.

(5)

Show that when
p_{c}^{site}(T)<p<1-p_{c}^{site}(G)
,
\mathbb{P}_{p}
a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters by

(a)

Since
p_{c}^{site}(T)<p<1-p_{c}^{site}(G)
,
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 0-*-clusters, if one infinite 0-*-cluster has infinitely many ends, or infinitely many infinite 0-*-clusters have at least 2 ends; then by planar duality there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters.

(b)

Consider the case that with strictly positive probability at most finitely many infinite 0-*-clusters have at least 2 ends and no infinite 0-*-clusters have infinitely many ends, then with strictly positive probability every infinite 0-*-cluster is 1-ended. In this case we show that when the growth rates of non-self-touching polygons on
G_{*}
the graph satisfy certain conditions, each infinite 0-*-cluster

. This is a contradiction to the assumption that

. See Section
5
.

(c)

We verify the condition on growth rates of non-self-touching polygons for graphs of which each finite face is a triangle. It follows that for all such graphs
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters when
p\in(1-p_{c}^{site}(G)-\delta,1-p_{c}^{site}(G))
, for some

. See Section
6
.

(d)

We use a version of “uniform percolation” with respect to binary trees to show the “stability of infinite clusters” regarding to adding edges to graphs, which, together with “stability of infinite clusters” regarding to increasing
p
’s, implies
that for each infinite, connected graph properly embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with minimal vertex degree at least 7, and for each
p\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1-p_{c}^{site}(G)\right)
,
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters. See section
7
.

Step (3) is based on a characterization of

for all the infinite, connected, locally finite graphs without the transitive assumption generalizing arguments by
[

,
13
]
, which may be of independent interest.
The advantage of the uniform percolation with respect to binary trees, in step (5d), compared to the traditional uniform percolation with respect to balls in
[

lies in the fact that the former does not need the bounded degree assumption to prove stability of infinite clusters. This technique of uniform percolation with respect to binary trees is possible for the class of graphs considered in this paper because of our explicitly constructed tree embeddings.

1.2.
Background and Notation

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected graph. Let
\hat{G}=(V,\hat{E})
be the graph obtained from
G
by

•

removing any edge of
G
joining the same vertex;

•

if multiple edges joining the same pair of vertices exist, keeping exactly such edge for each pair of vertices but removing all the others.

It is straightforward to check that
p_{c}^{site}(G)=p_{c}^{site}(\hat{G})
. Hence without loss of generality, all graphs in this paper are assumed simple in the sense that

•

each edge has two distinct endpoints; and

•

there exists at most one edge joining two distinct vertices.

A walk, or a path of
G
is an alternating, finite or infinite sequence
(\dots,v_{0},e_{0},v_{1},e_{1},\dots)
with

e_{i}=\langle v_{i},v_{i+1}\rangle
for all
i
. Since our graphs are assumed simple, we may refer
to a walk by its vertex-sequence only.

A walk is
closed
if it can be written as
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
with
v_{0}=v_{n}
. In this case we say the total number of steps in this closed walk is
n
.
A walk is
self-avoiding
if it visits no vertex twice or more.
A
cycle
, or a
simple cycle

C=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n},v_{0})
is a self-avoiding walk
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n})

such that
v_{0}\sim v_{n}
together with the edge
\langle v_{0},v_{n}\rangle
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. Once
G
is suitably embedded in the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
one defines a
face
of
G
to be a maximal connected subset of

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus G
. Note that faces are open sets, and may be either bounded or unbounded.
While it may be helpful to think of
a face as being bounded by a cycle of
G
, the reality can be more complicated in that
faces are not invariably simply connected (if
G
is disconnected) and their boundaries
are not generally self-avoiding cycles or paths (if
G
is not 2-connected).

The boundary of a face is a closed walk; the
degree
of a face
f
, denoted by
|f|
, is the total number of steps of the closed walk representing its boundary. The degree
|f|
of a face is bounded below by the number of distinct vertices on its boundary and by the number of distinct edges on its boundary; since a vertex or an edge on the boundary may be visited multiple times by the walk. Although in general, the boundary of a face can be more complicated than a cycle, as we shall show in Lemma
2.10
, for graphs under the assumptions of the paper (especially the negative curvature assumption of each vertex), the boundary of each face is a cycle.

For
u,w\in V
, we write
u\sim w
if
u
and
w
are joined by an edge.

For
\omega\in\Omega
, we write
u\leftrightarrow v
if there exists an open path of
G
with endpoints
u
and
v
,
and
x{\mathrel{\to}^{A}}v
if such a path exists within the set
A\subseteq V
. A similar notation
is used for the existence of infinite open paths. When such open paths exist in the matching graph
G_{*}
, we
use the relation
{\mathrel{\to}^{\ast}}
.

2.
Embedded Trees

The goal of this section is to show that
every infinite, connected, planar graph with sufficiently large vertex/face degrees
possesses a subgraph that is a tree in which every vertex other than the root vertex has degree
3
or
4
. We start with geometric properties of planar graphs.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a planar graph. Let
f
be a face of

. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(f):=(|f|-2)\pi-\sum_{i=1}^{|f|}\mathrm{internal\ angle\ of}\ f\ \mathrm{at\ }v_{i}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(R_{C})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{A}(f_{i}).

Definition 2.2

Let

be a locally finite planar graph. Let

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{A}(f_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[(|f_{i}|-2)\pi-\sum_{k=1}^{|f_{i}|}\mathrm{internal\ angle\ of}\ \mathcal{A}(f_{i})\ {at\ }w_{j}\right];

Then the lemma follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in R_{C}}\mathcal{A}(f)+\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathrm{internal\ angle\ of\ }C\ \mathrm{at\ }v_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{z\in R_{C}^{\circ}}\sum_{f\in P_{0}:f\sim z}\left(\frac{[|f|-2]\pi}{|f|}-\mathrm{internal\ angle\ of\ }f\ \mathrm{at}\ z\right)

An

It is straightforward to see that an

Assumption 5.3

6.
Non-self-touching polygons

In this section, we verify Assumption
5.3
for graphs of which each finite face is a triangle, by enumerating non-self-touching polygons with the help of an isoperimetric inequality. The idea is inspried by
[
31
]
, where the growth rate for self-avoiding polygons on vertex transitive planar graphs in which every face has the same degree was upper bounded. It follows that for all the infinite, connected graphs properly embedded into
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that each face is a triangle and the minimal vertex degree is at least 7,
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-a.s. there are infinitely many infinite 1-clusters when

, for some

Definition 6.1
.

Given a locally finite, simple graph

and

, let

be the number of

\displaystyle\gamma_{G}(e):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}[c_{n}(e)]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that

is an infinite, locally finite, connected
graph. Let

. Then for any

,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{p}(u{\mathrel{\to}^{nstp,\ \geq m}}v)=0;

where
\mathbb{P}_{p}(u{\mathrel{\to}^{nstp,\ \geq m}}v)
is the probability that there exists a a non-self-touching 1-polygon with length at least
m
containing
e=\langle u,v\rangle
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by Proposition 8.3 of
[
18
]
, where self-avoiding walks are used instead of non-self-touching polygons. Since we consider site percolation, it is convenient to use non-self-touching polygons instead of self-avoiding walks to obtain more precise results.

From Definition
6.1
we infer that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists

, such that for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}(u{\mathrel{\to}^{nstp,\ \geq m}}v)\leq\sum_{n\geq m}c_{n}(e)p^{n}\leq\sum_{n\geq m}[(\gamma_{G}(e)+\epsilon)p]^{n}=\frac{(p(\gamma_{G}(e)+\epsilon))^{m}}{1-p(\gamma_{G}(e)+\epsilon)}.

Then the lemma follows.
∎

7.

In this section, we discuss the existence of infinitely many infinite clusters when

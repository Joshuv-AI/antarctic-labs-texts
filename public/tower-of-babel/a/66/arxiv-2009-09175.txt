On the Boundary Value Problems of Ψ \Psi -Hilfer Fractional Differential Equations


Abstract
In the current paper, we derive the comparison results for the homogeneous and non-homogeneous linear initial value problem (IVP) for Ψ \Psi -Hilfer fractional differential equations. In the presence of upper and lower solutions, the obtained comparison results and the location of roots theorem utilized to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution for the linear Ψ \Psi -Hilfer boundary value problem (BVP) through the linear non-homogeneous Ψ \Psi -Hilfer IVP. Assuming the existence of lower solution w 0 w_{0} and upper solution z 0 z_{0} , we establish the existence of minimal and maximal solutions for the nonlinear Ψ \Psi -Hilfer BVP in the line segment [ w 0 , z 0 ] [w_{0},\,z_{0}] of the weighted space C 1 − γ ; Ψ ​ ( J , ℝ ) C_{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right) . Further, it demonstrated that the iterative Picard type sequences that began with lower and upper solutions respectively converges to a minimal and maximal solutions, and that started with any point on a line segment converge to the exact solution of nonlinear Ψ \Psi -Hilfer BVP. Finally, an example is provided in support of the main results we acquired.

Theorem 3.1

Assume that
y\in C_{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
and satisfies

{}^{H}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\,\beta\,;\,\Psi}_{0+}y(t)-My(t)\leq 0,~t\in(0,\,T],~

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}y(0)\leq 0.

(3.2)

Then,
y(t)\leq 0
,

\,t\in(0,\,T]
.

Proof:

Consider the following linear IVP

{}^{H}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\,\beta\,;\,\Psi}_{0+}y(t)-My(t)=\sigma(t),~t\in(0,\,T],~

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}y(0)=y_{0},

(3.4)

where

By Lemma
2.4

)-(
3.4

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=y_{0}(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{\gamma-1}E_{\alpha,\,\gamma}\left(M(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{\prime}(s)(\Psi(t)-\Psi(s))^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\,\alpha}\left(M(\Psi(t)-\Psi(s))^{\alpha}\right)\sigma(s)ds,~t\in(0,\,T].

(3.5)

From equations (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we have
y_{0}\leq 0
. Since
\Psi
is an increasing function, we have
(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{\gamma-1}\geq 0,\,t\in J
and
(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{\alpha}\geq 0,\,t\in J
.
This gives,
E_{\alpha,\,\gamma}\left(M(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{\alpha}\right)>0,\,t\in J

3

y(t)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{\prime}(s)(\Psi(t)-\Psi(s))^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\,\alpha}\left(M(\Psi(t)-\Psi(s))^{\alpha}\right)\sigma(s)ds,~t\in(0,\,T].

(3.6)

From equations (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), it follows that
\sigma(t)\leq 0,\,t\in J
. Since

is an increasing continuous function, we have

for
\,t\in J

for

(3.7)

From (

4
Existence and uniqueness for the linear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP

In this section, using the method of upper and lower solutions, we derive the existence and uniqueness results for the linear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.2
)-(
1.3

Theorem 4.1

Assume that there exist upper and lower solutions

v,u\in C_{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
respectively of the linear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) such that
u\preceq v
. Then, the linear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) has a unique solution
y\in C_{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
that satisfy
u\preceq y\preceq v
.

Proof:

We give the proof in following two parts.

Part I:
In this part we prove that the linear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) has a unique solution. Consider the functions
p,q\in C_{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
defined by

p(t)=\begin{cases}r\,u(t),~\text{if}\,\,r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)\\
r\,u(t)+\xi(t)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right),~\text{if}\,\,r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)<\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0),\end{cases}

(4.1)

and

q(t)=\begin{cases}r\,v(t),~\text{if}\,\,r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(T)\leq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(0)\\
r\,v(t)-\xi(t)\left(r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(T)-\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(0)\right),~\text{if}\,\,r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(T)>\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(0),\end{cases}

(4.2)

where
\xi(t)
is defined as in the equation (
2.3
).

Case 1: If
r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)

then
p(t)=r\,u(t),\,t\in(0,\,T]
. Therefore,

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0).

Further,

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)\\
=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)}{r}.

Thus,

r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0).

Case 2: If
r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)<\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)

then
p(t)=r\,u(t)+\xi(t)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right),

\,t\in(0,\,T]
.
Therefore,

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)+\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\xi(0)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right).

(4.3)

But from (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.1
(b), we have

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\xi(t)=\frac{(\Psi(t)-\Psi(0))^{1-\,\gamma+\delta}}{(\Psi(T)-\Psi(0))^{1-\,\gamma+\delta}},\,t\in J.

(4.4)

From (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0).

Further,

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)+\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}\xi(T)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right).

(4.5)

Again from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), it follows that

\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)=\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)}{r}.

Thus,

r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)=\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0).

From Case 1 and Case 2, we have

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0),

(4.6)

r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(T)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}p(0).

(4.7)

On the similar line for the function
q
one can obtain the following relations

\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}q(0)=r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}v(0),

(4.8)

r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}q(T)\leq\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}q(0).

(4.9)

Next, our aim is to prove that

{}^{H}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\,\beta\,;\,\Psi}_{0+}p(t)-Mp(t)

\displaystyle=\,^{H}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\,\beta\,;\,\Psi}_{0+}\left[r\,u(t)+\xi(t)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-M\left[r\,u(t)+\xi(t)\left(\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(0)-r\,\mathcal{I}_{0+}^{1-\,\gamma;\,\Psi}u(T)\right)\right]

\Psi

)-(
1.3
) from above equation, we obtain

) satisfies the fractional differential inequality (

for any

From equations (

) gives the error bound with respect to the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C_{1-\,\gamma;\Psi}\left(J,\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)}
between approximation
y_{n}
and the exact solution
y^{*}
of the nonlinear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.4
)-(
1.5
).

\Box

6
Example

We consider a specific case of the problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) to illustrate the main results that we acquired.

Example 6.1

Consider the following BVP for the nonlinear Caputo FDEs

{}^{C}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0+}y(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{10}-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{25}+\frac{1}{25}\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5}\right)+\frac{1}{25}\left(5y(t)-\sin\left(y(t)\right)\right),~t\in(0,\,1],~

(6.1)

\displaystyle y(0)=\frac{1}{2}\,y(1).

(6.2)

One can verify that
y^{*}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5},~t\in[0,\,1]
is an exact solution of the problem (
6.1
)-(
6.2
).
Comparing the above problem with the nonlinear
\Psi
-Hilfer BVP (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), we obtain

\alpha=\frac{1}{2},\beta=1,\gamma=\alpha+\beta(1-\alpha)=1,M=0,r=\frac{1}{2},T=1~\text{and}~\Psi(t)=t,t\in[0,\,1].

(6.3)

In this case the weighted space
C_{1-\,\gamma;\Psi}\left([0,\,1],\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
reduces to the space of continuous functions
C\left([0,\,1],\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)
endowed with the supremum norm.

Define the function
f:[0,\,1]\times\mbox{\Bbb R}\rightarrow\mbox{\Bbb R}
by

f(t,y)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{10}-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{25}+\frac{1}{25}\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5}\right)+\frac{1}{25}\left(5y-\sin y\right).

(6.4)

(1) For any
k\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
consider the function
\tilde{f}:\mbox{\Bbb R}\rightarrow\mbox{\Bbb R}
defined by

\tilde{f}(y)=k+\frac{1}{25}\left(5y-\sin y\right),\,y\in\mbox{\Bbb R}.

Then,

\tilde{f}^{\prime}(y)=\frac{1}{25}\left(5-\cos y\right)\geq 0,~\text{for all }~y\in\mbox{\Bbb R}.
Therefore the function
\tilde{f}
is increasing on
ℝ
for any real
k
. This implies the function
f:[0,\,1]\times\mbox{\Bbb R}\rightarrow\mbox{\Bbb R}
defined in (
6.4
) is increasing in
y\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
for each
t\in[0,1]
.

(2) Let any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
with
y_{1}\leq y_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle f(t,y_{2})-f(t,y_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{25}\left\{5(y_{2}-y_{1})-\left(\sin y_{2}-\sin y_{1}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{25}\left\{5|y_{2}-y_{1}|+|\sin y_{2}-\sin y_{1}|\right\}.

(6.5)

Since
\sin y
is continuous and differentiable on the interval
[y_{1},y_{2}]
, applying mean value theorem, there exists
\tilde{y}\in[y_{1},y_{2}]
such that

\frac{\sin y_{2}-\sin y_{1}}{y_{2}-y_{1}}=\cos\tilde{y}.

This implies
|\sin y_{2}-\sin y_{1}|\leq|y_{2}-y_{1}|
. Therefore, the inequality (
6
) reduces to

\displaystyle f(t,y_{2})-f(t,y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{25}\left\{5|y_{2}-y_{1}|+|y_{2}-y_{1}|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{25}\left(y_{2}-y_{1}\right),~\text{for any}~y_{1},y_{2}\in\mbox{\Bbb R}~\text{with}~y_{1}\leq y_{2}.

(6.6)

Comparing the above inequality with (
5.12
), we have
\tilde{L}=\frac{6}{25}
.

(3) Next, we prove that
\tilde{L}\in\left[0,\,\Omega^{-1}\right)
where
\Omega
is defied in (
5.2
). Using the values given in (
6.3
), the equation (
5.2
) reduces to

\Omega=B\left(\frac{1}{2}-1+1,1\right)\frac{\frac{1}{2}\,E_{\frac{1}{2},\,1}\left(0\right)E_{\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2}+1-\,1}\left(0\right)}{\left[1-\frac{1}{2}\,E_{\frac{1}{2},\,1}\left(0\right)\right]}+B\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)E_{\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2}}\left(0\right).

Using Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

E_{n_{1},\,n_{2}}(0)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(n_{2}\right)},~n_{1},~n_{2}>0.

(6.7)

Therefore

\Omega=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+1\right)}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1\right)}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}}{\left[1-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(1\right)}\right]}+\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+1\right)}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

Note that, for
\tilde{L}=\frac{6}{25}~\mbox{and}~\Omega=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}},
we have
\tilde{L}\in\left[0,\,\Omega^{-1}\right)
.

(4) Define
z_{0}(t)=\sqrt{t}+1,\,t\in[0,1]
and
w_{0}(t)=\frac{-(\sqrt{t}+1)}{6},\,t\in[0,1]
. Then,

\displaystyle h_{1}(t):

\displaystyle=\,^{C}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0+}z_{0}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2},

\displaystyle h_{2}(t):

\displaystyle=f(t,z_{0}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{10}-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{25}+\frac{1}{25}\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5}\right)+\frac{1}{25}\left(5\left(\sqrt{t}+1\right)-\sin\left(\sqrt{t}+1\right)\right),

\displaystyle h_{3}(t):

\displaystyle=\,^{C}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0+}w_{0}(t)=\frac{-\sqrt{\pi}}{12},

\displaystyle h_{4}(t):

\displaystyle=f(t,w_{0}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{10}-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{25}+\frac{1}{25}\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5}\right)+\frac{1}{25}\left(5\left(\frac{-(\sqrt{t}+1)}{6}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{-(\sqrt{t}+1)}{6}\right)\right).

Figure 1:
z_{0}
is an upper solution

Figure 2:
w_{0}
is a lower solution

From Figure 1, it follows that

{}^{C}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0+}z_{0}(t)\geq f(t,z_{0}(t)),\,t\in[0,1].

Further, using the definition of
z_{0}
, we have
z_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{2}z_{0}(1)
and hence
b_{z_{0}}(t)=0
. This proves that
z_{0}(t)=\sqrt{t}+1,\,t\in[0,1]
is an upper solution of the problem (
6.1
)-(
6.2
). Again, from Figure 2, it follows that

{}^{C}\mathcal{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0+}w_{0}(t)\leq f(t,w_{0}(t)),\,t\in[0,1].

Further, by definition of
w_{0}
, we have

w_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{2}w_{0}(1)
and
a_{w_{0}}(t)=0
. This implies
w_{0}(t)=-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{6},\,t\in[0,1]
is a lower solution of the problem (
6.1
)-(
6.2
). Since all the assumptions of Theorem
5.2
are satisfied, it guarantee the existence of a unique solution
y^{*}
in

of the problem (
6.1
)-(
6.2
). Indeed,

-\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{6}\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}+1}{5}\leq\sqrt{t}+1,~\text{for all }~t\in[0,1],

w_{0}(t)\leq y^{*}(t)\leq z_{0}(t),~t\in[0,1].

This implies

6.1
)-(
6.2
).

(5) Using (

6.7
), the sequence

y_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-s\right)^{\frac{-1}{2}}f(s,y_{n-1}(s))ds+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{-1}{2}}f(s,y_{n-1}(s))ds

,

(6.8)

Using (
6.3

\Omega
and

\rho=\Omega\tilde{L}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{6}{25}=\frac{24}{25\sqrt{\pi}}.

Thus, from the inequality (

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n}-y^{*}\right\|_{C\left([0,1],\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)}

\left\|y_{n}-y^{*}\right\|_{C\left([0,1],\,\mbox{\Bbb R}\right)}\leq\frac{7}{3}\left(\frac{24}{25\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{n}.

Locally analytic vectors and rings of periods
Léo Poyeton

Abstract
Résumé Let K K be a finite extension of 𝐐 p {{\bf Q}_{p}} . In this paper, we try to extend Berger’s and Colmez’s point of view, using locally analytic vectors in order to generalize classical cyclotomic theory, in higher rings of periods. We also provide a construction of analogs of the ring 𝐁 S ​ e ​ n {\bf B}_{Sen} of Colmez, one of which computes Sen theory in the de Rham case, and one which computes classical ( φ , Γ ) (\varphi,\Gamma) -modules theory. We explain what happens when we try to generalize constructions of ( φ , Γ ) (\varphi,\Gamma) -modules to arbitrary infinitely ramified p-adic Lie extensions, and provide a conjecture on the structure of the locally analytic vectors in the corresponding rings. In particular, we highlight the fact that the situation should be very different, depending on wether the p p -adic Lie extension contains a cyclotomic extension or not. Finally, we explain how some of these constructions may be related to the construction of a potential ring of trianguline periods.

Introduction

Let
p
be a prime, and let
K
be a finite extension of
{\bf Q}_{p}
. We fix
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}=\overline{K}
an algebraic closure of
K
, and we let
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/K)
be its absolute Galois group.

A classical idea in
p
-adic Hodge theory in order to study
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
is to use an intermediate extension
K_{\infty}/K
such that
K_{\infty}/K
is nice enough but such that it contains “most of the ramification” of
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K
, so that
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty}
is almost étale in the sense of Faltings (which is the same as saying that the
p
-adic completion of
K_{\infty}
is perfectoid). The main example of such an extension is the cyclotomic extension
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
of
K
, which has been thoroughly used in
p
-adic Hodge theory, notably in Sen theory and
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory.

In some sense, Kummer extensions are simpler than the cyclotomic extension, and work from Breuil
[
Bre98
]
and Kisin
[
Kis06
]
show that Kummer extensions are very useful in order to study semistable representations. However, Kummer extensions are never Galois and this implies that we usually have to replace them by their Galois closure which increases the difficulty of the situation. Lubin-Tate extensions attached to uniformizers of
K
, of which the cyclotomic extension when
K={{\bf Q}_{p}}
is a particular case, trivialize local class field theory and thus seem particularly useful in order to extend the
p
-adic Langlands correspondence to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(K)
(see for example
[
KR09
,
FX14
,
Ber16b
]
for work in this direction). More generally, the interesting framework should be the one of infinitely ramified Galois extensions whose Galois group is a
p
-adic Lie group, with potential applications in Iwasawa theory
[
Ven03
]
.

Let
V
be a
p
-adic Lie extension of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, and let
K_{\infty}=K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
,
H_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K\infty)
and
\Gamma_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\infty}/K)
. Recall that the cyclotomic character
\chi_{\mathrm{cycl}}:\Gamma_{K}\to{\bf Z}_{p}^{\times}
identifies
\Gamma_{K}
with an open subgroup of
{\bf Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Since
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty}
is almost étale,
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}\otimes_{\widehat{K_{\infty}}}{{\bf C}_{p}}\simeq V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}}
, so that the study of the
{{\bf C}_{p}}
-representation
V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}}
is reduced to the one of
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}
. The idea of Sen to study such a representation
[
Sen80
]
is to consider the subspace
D_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}(V)
of
K
-finite vectors, which are elements of
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}
which belong to finite dimensional sub-
K
-vector spaces stable by
\Gamma_{K}
. This is a sub-
K_{\infty}
-vector space of
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}
, and Sen proved that
D_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}(V)\otimes_{K_{\infty}}\widehat{K_{\infty}}\simeq V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}
.

If
K_{\infty}
is any infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension
K_{\infty}/K
, and if
V
is a
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, then since
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty}
is almost étale, we still have an isomorphism
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{H_{K}}\otimes_{\widehat{K_{\infty}}}{{\bf C}_{p}}\simeq V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}}
, but if the dimension of
\Gamma_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\infty}/K)
as a
p
-adic Lie group is greater or equal to
2
, then the space of
K
-finite vectors of this semilinear
\widehat{K_{\infty}}
-representation of
\Gamma_{K}
is no longer suitable, as shown by
[
BC16
, Prop. 1.5]
.

In order to generalize Sen theory to any infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension
K_{\infty}/K
, Berger and Colmez suggest to replace the space of
K
-finite vectors and the use of normalized Tate’s traces maps (which no longer exist in general
[
Fou09
]
) by the space of locally analytic vectors, which are elements
x
such that the orbit map
g\mapsto g(x)
is a locally analytic function on
\Gamma_{K}
. This gives a decompletion of
(V\otimes_{{\bf Q}_{p}}{{\bf C}_{p}})^{{\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty})}
into a
\widehat{K_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
-vector space of dimension
\dim_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
, but in general
\widehat{K_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
strictly contains
K_{\infty}
.

Recall that the strategy developped by Fontaine (see
[
Fon94b
]
) to study
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
is to construct some
p
-adic rings of periods
B
, which are topological
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-algebras endowed with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
and additional structures such that if
V
is a
p
-adic representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, then the
B^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K}}
-module
D_{B}(V):=(B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K}}
is endowed with the structures coming from those on
B
, and such that the functor
B\mapsto D_{B}(V)
gives some interesting invariants attached to
V
. For Fontaine’s strategy to work, one requires that these rings of periods
B
are
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-regular in the sense of
[
Fon94b
, 1.4.1]
(this implies in particular that
B^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K}}
is a field). We then say that a
p
-adic representation
V
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
of dimension
d
is
B
-admissible if
B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V\simeq B^{d}
as
B
-representations. The strategy of Fontaine then consists of classifying
p
-adic representations according to the rings of periods for which they are admissible. In the case where
V
is admissible,
D_{B}(V)
can usually be used to recover
V
, or at least
V_{|{\mathcal{G}}_{L}}
for some finite extension
L
of
K
.

Fontaine has constructed several
p
-adic rings of periods, and in particular the rings
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{cris}}
,
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}
and
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
. Recall that
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}
is endowed with a Frobenius
\varphi
,
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}
contains
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}
, is endowed with a Frobenius
\varphi
and a monodromy operator
N
such that
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}={\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}^{N=0}
, and
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
is a field endowed with a filtration
\{{\mathrm{Fil}}^{i}{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}\}_{i\in{\bf Z}}
and such that there is an injective map
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{st}}}\rightarrow{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
. Moreover, these rings all contain an element
t
which is “Fontaine’s
p
-adic
2i\pi
”, and there exists rings
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}^{+}
,
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}^{+}
and
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
such that
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}={\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}^{+}[1/t]
,
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}={\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}^{+}[1/t]
and
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}={\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}[1/t]
. Representations that are
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{crys}}
-admissible,
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{st}}
-admissible and
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
-admissible are respectively called crystalline, semi-stable and de Rham. The relation between those rings imply that crystalline representations are semi-stable and that semi-stable representations are de Rham.

Colmez has constructed in
[
Col94
]
a ring of periods
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}
which recovers Sen’s theory in the cyclotomic setting. Precisely, he defines
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}^{n}
as the set of power series in the variable
u
over
{{\bf C}_{p}}
, with radius of convergence
\geq p^{-n}
, and endows it with an action of
{\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K(\mu_{p^{n}}))
by
g(u)=u+\log\chi_{\mathrm{cycl}}(g)
(this makes sense since
\log\chi_{{\mathrm{cycl}}}(g)\in p^{n}\mathcal{O}_{K}
if
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
). He then shows that
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}^{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K(\mu_{p^{n}})}}=K(\mu_{p^{n}})
and that
K_{\infty}\otimes_{K_{n}}({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}^{n}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K(\mu_{p^{n}})}}
is isomorphic to
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}(V)
.

One other key ingredient in the study of
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
is the theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules, which provides an equivalence of categories
V\mapsto D(V)
between the category of all
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
and the category of étale
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules. In Fontaine’s theory,
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules are finite dimensional vector spaces, defined over a dimension 2 local ring
{\bf B}_{K}
and endowed with semilinear actions of a Frobenius
\varphi
and of
\Gamma_{K}
which commutes one to another. The ring
{\bf B}_{K}
is isomorphic to the ring of power series
\sum_{k\in{\bf Z}}a_{k}T^{k}
where the sequence
(a_{k})
is a bounded sequence of elements of
K_{0}=K_{\infty}\cap{\bf Q}_{p}^{\mathrm{unr}}
such that
a_{-k}\rightarrow 0
when
k{\rightarrow}+\infty
, and the actions of
\varphi
and
\Gamma_{K}
on
T
are constructed through the theory of the field of norms
[
Win83
]
.

One variant of the theory, which has been used with many useful applications, is the theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules over the Robba ring
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K}^{\dagger}
, which consists of the power series
\sum_{k\in{\bf Z}}a_{k}T^{k}
where
a_{k}\in K_{0}
and for which there exists
\rho
such that the series converges on the
p
-adic annulus
\rho\leq|T|_{p}<1
. The theorem of Cherbonnier-Colmez
[
CC98
]
shows that the category of étale
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules over
{\bf B}_{K}
actually embedds into the category of
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules over
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K}^{\dagger}
of slope
0
, and the slope filtration theorem of Kedlaya
[
Ked05
]
shows that this is an equivalence of categories.

One interesting feature of the Robba ring is that it can be used as a bridge between the classical theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules and
p
-adic Hodge theory, as its elements can be embedded inside
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
. In particular, Berger has shown
[
Ber02
]
how to recover the invariants attached to a
p
-adic representation
V
in
p
-adic Hodge theory from its
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-module on the Robba ring.

In particular, the overconvergence of cyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules is a really important component for their application. Kisin and Ren have defined Lubin-Tate
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules
[
KR09
]
and proved that the category of Lubin-Tate étale
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules is equivalent to the one of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-representations, but unfortunately a result from Fourquaux and Xie
[
FX14
]
shows that those
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules are usually not overconvergent. Results from Berger
[
Ber13
]

[
Ber16b
]
suggest that the right objects to consider are once again the locally analytic vectors inside some higher rings of periods.

In this paper, we try to understand what happens if we use the point of view of Berger-Colmez of locally analytic vectors in “higher rings of periods”.

Our first result, which should be well known to the experts, is that Colmez’s construction of
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}
can be generalized to construct rings of periods which “compute the cyclotomic theory”. More precisely, if
B
is a
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-Banach (or Fréchet) ring endowed with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, such that the functor
V\mapsto D_{B}^{{\mathrm{la}}}(V):=(B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K(\mu_{p^{\infty}}),{\mathrm{la}})}
gives interesting invariants of
V
, then there exists a ring
{\bf B}{\{\{u\}\}}
that “computes” the functor
V\mapsto D_{B}^{{\mathrm{la}}}(V)
: Let
u
be a variable and
B
be a
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-algebra endowed with a topology for which it is complete, and equipped with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. We denote by
B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}
the set of power series
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}u^{k}
with coefficients in
B
such that the series
\sum_{k\geq 0}(p^{n})^{k}a_{k}
converges in
B
and we equip it with the natural topology and with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
by setting

g(\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}u^{k})=\sum_{k\geq 0}g(a_{k})(u+\log\chi_{{\mathrm{cycl}}}(g))^{k}.

We let
B{\{\{u\}\}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}
, endowed with the inductive limit topology.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm

Let

In particular, this allows us to provide constructions recovering cyclotomic

In order to generalize

-modules theory to any infinitely ramified

The fact that we expect the need to contain a cyclotomic extension follows from the following, which shows that for

If

Finally, we highlight the fact that the constructions of the rings of periods
{\bf B}{\{\{u\}\}}
could have applications in order to define rings of periods for trianguline representations : A trianguline representation is a representation such that its attached

-module on the Robba ring is a successive extension of rank
1

(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-modules, but as stated above, that does not mean that the corresponding representation itself is a successive extension of rank

-modules of rank

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

There is no reasonable ring of periods

,

, since every rank

with coefficients in
{{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}
and “radius of convergence

If we let

-adic representations

]

]

Structure of the paper

The first section of the paper recalls the theory of classical rings of periods and the theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules and the rings it involves. The second section recalls the theory of locally and pro-analytic vectors. In §3, we recall the main results from
[
BC16
]
and develop the framework of the rings
{\bf B}{\{\{u\}\}}
, proving theorem
Introduction
. We explain in §4 how this framework recovers classical Sen theory for
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
-representations, and we compute what
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}^{+})^{H_{K},{\mathrm{la}}}
looks like in some particular cases mainly the Lubin-Tate one). Section 5 is dedicated to how we recover
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory in our framework, and we explain what happens in the anticyclotomic case. In §6, we explain what we expect to happen in general when trying to generalize
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory by using locally analytic vectors, and prove the particular case of the conjecture. The computations of locally analytic vectors in the Robba rings is done in §7. Finally, §8 is devoted to the applications to trianguline representations and towards a construction of rings of trianguline periods.

2.
Locally and pro-analytic vectors

Here, we recall some of the theory of locally- and pro-analytic vectors, following the presentation of Emerton in
[
Eme17
]
and of Berger in
[
Ber16b
]
.

Let
G
be a
p
-adic Lie group, and let
W
be a
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-Banach representation of
G
. Let
H
be an open subgroup of
G
such that there exists coordinates
c_{1},\cdots,c_{d}:H\to{{\bf Z}_{p}}
giving rise to an analytic bijection
{\bf{c}}:H\to{\bf Z}_{p}^{d}
. We say that
w\in W
is an
H
-analytic vector if there exists a sequence
\left\{w_{{\bf{k}}}\right\}_{{\bf{k}}\in{\bf N}^{d}}
such that
w_{{\bf{k}}}\rightarrow 0
in
W
and such that
g(w)=\sum_{{\bf{k}}\in{\bf N}^{d}}{\bf{c}}(g)^{{\bf{k}}}w_{{\bf{k}}}
for all
g\in H
. We let
W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
be the space of
H
-analytic vectors. This space injects into
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(H,W)
, the space of all analytic functions
f:H\to W
. Note that
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(H,W)
is a Banach space equipped with its usual Banach norm, so that we can endow
W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
with the induced norm, that we will denote by
||\cdot||_{H}
. With this definition, we have
||w||_{H}=\sup_{{\bf{k}}\in{\bf N}^{d}}||w_{{\bf{k}}}||
and
(W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}},||\cdot||_{H})
is a Banach space.

The space
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(H,W)
is endowed by an action of
H\times H\times H
, given by

((g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\cdot f)(g)=g_{1}\cdot f(g_{2}^{-1}gg_{3})

and one can recover
W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
as the closed subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(H,W)
of its
\Delta_{1,2}(H)
-invariants, where
\Delta_{1,2}:H\to H\times H\times H
denotes the map
g\mapsto(g,g,1)
(we refer the reader to
[
Eme17
, §3.3]
for more details).

We say that a vector
w
of
W
is locally analytic if there exists an open subgroup
H
as above such that
w\in W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
. Let
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
be the space of such vectors, so that
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}=\bigcup_{H}W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
, where
H
runs through a sequence of open subgroups of
G
. The space
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
is naturally endowed with the inductive limit topology, so that it is an LB space.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

If
W
is a ring such that
||xy||\leq||x||\cdot||y||
for
x,y\in W
, then

(1)

W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
is a ring, and
||xy||_{H}\leq||x||_{H}\cdot||y||_{H}
if
x,y\in W^{H-{\mathrm{an}}}
;

(2)

if

, then
1/w\in W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
. In particular, if
W
is a field, then
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
is also a field.

Démonstration.

See
[
BC16
, Lemm. 2.5]
.
∎

3.
Sen theory by Berger-Colmez

Recall that to a
p
-adic representation
V
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, one can attach to it the
K_{\infty}
-vector space
D_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}(V)
which is the set of elements of
W=({{\bf C}_{p}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}}
which belong to some finite dimensional
K
-vector subspace of
W
which is stable by
\Gamma_{K}
. The
K_{\infty}
-vector space
D_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}
comes equipped with an action of the Lie algebra of
\Gamma_{K}
and admits a canonical generator
\nabla=\lim_{\gamma\to 1}\frac{\gamma-1}{\chi_{\mathrm{cycl}}(\gamma)-1}
which is the operator of Sen, usually denoted by
\Theta_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}
and whose eigenvalues are called the generalized Hodge-Tate weights of the representation
V
.

Colmez has constructed in
[
Col94
]
a ring
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{Sen}}
as follows :

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
u
be a variable and
{\bf B}_{Sen}^{n}={{\bf C}_{p}}{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}
be the set of power series
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}u^{k}
with coefficients in
{{\bf C}_{p}}
such that the series
\sum_{k\geq 0}(p^{n})^{k}a_{k}
converges in
{{\bf C}_{p}}
and equip it with the natural topology and with an action of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/K(\mu_{p^{n}}))
by setting

g(\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}u^{k})=\sum_{k\geq 0}g(a_{k})(u+\log\chi_{{\mathrm{cycl}}}(g))^{k}.

Note that this makes sense since
\log\chi_{{\mathrm{cycl}}}(g)\in p^{n}{\bf Z}_{p}
if
g\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/K(\mu_{p^{n}}))
. Let
{\bf B}_{Sen}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}{\bf B}_{Sen}^{n}
, endowed with the inductive limit topology.

We now recall the following properties (for more details, see
[
Col94
]
and
[
BC16
, §2.2]
) :

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

(1)

;

(2)

V
is a
p
-adic representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
and if

is an integer, let

equipped with the operator

induced by the operator
\nabla_{u}
on
{\bf B}_{\mathrm{Sen}}^{n}
(meaning that

p
-adic Banach ring, endowed with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. We have an isomorphism

(B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}\simeq(B^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}.

Démonstration.

Let
x\in(B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
. We can write
x=\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{i}u^{i}
. The fact that
x\in(B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
shows that
x\in((B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}}=((B^{H_{K}}){\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{\Gamma_{n}}
. Moreover, it implies that for all
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
, we have
g(x_{i})=\sum_{j\geq 0}x_{i+j}\binom{i+j}{j}(-\log\chi_{{\mathrm{cycl}}}(g))^{j}
. Therefore, the
x_{i}
are all locally analytic vectors of
B^{H_{K}}
. We can define an operator
\nabla
on
(B^{H_{K}})^{{\mathrm{la}}}{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}
by
\nabla=\frac{\log g}{\log\chi_{\pi}(g)}
for
g
close enough to
1
and which does not depend on such a choice of
g
. A quick computation shows that

\nabla(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\nabla(x_{i})u^{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}ix_{i}u^{i-1}.

Since
x\in((B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}}
, it is killed by
\nabla
, and thus we get that for all
i\geq 0
, we have
x_{i+1}=-\frac{\nabla(x_{i})}{i+1}
. Therefore, we have
x_{i}=(-1)^{i}\frac{\nabla^{i}(x_{0})}{i!}
for all
i\geq 0
.

The fact that
x\in B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}
then implies that the sequence
\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\frac{\nabla^{i}(x_{0})}{i!}p^{ni}
converges in
B
, so that by definition
x_{0}
is a
\Gamma_{n}
-analytic vector of
B
. It follows that the map
x\mapsto x_{0}
is an injective morphism of rings
(B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}{\rightarrow}(B^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}
, whose inverse is given by
z\mapsto\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\frac{\nabla^{i}(z)}{i!}u^{i}
, so that it is an isomorphism.
∎

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
B
be a
p
-adic Banach ring, endowed with an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. Let
V
be a
p
-adic representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. Then we have an isomorphism

(B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}\simeq((B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}.

Démonstration.

The proof is exactly the same as above, replacing
B
by
B{\{\{u\}\}}_{n}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
.
∎

We can also define an
F
-analytic generalization of these constructions. In the following definitions, the cyclotomic setting is replaced by the Lubin-Tate one.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

Let

be a variable and

be a
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-algebra endowed with a topology for which it is complete, and equipped with an action of

\the\smf@thm

Let

. Let

4.
de Rham computations

In this section we compute locally analytic vectors and pro-analytic vectors in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
, both in the cyclotomic case and in the Lubin-Tate case, and we explain how to recover the module
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
attached to a
p
-adic representation
V
thanks to the use of the locally analytic vectors. The fact that locally analytic vectors are able to recover
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
has already been proven in
[
Por22
, §6.1]
but here we will also use proposition
3
to produce a ring of periods which “computes” the functor
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}
.

4.1.
Computations in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}

We let
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+}
and
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}
denote respectively
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}^{H_{F}}
and
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}^{H_{F}}
. Recall that there is a natural injective,
{\mathcal{G}}_{F}
-equivariant map
{{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}{\rightarrow}{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
, which sends
t_{\pi}
to a generator of
\ker(\theta)
in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
and we still denote the image of
t_{\pi}
through this map by
t_{\pi}
. The image of
t_{\tau}
,
\tau\in\Sigma
through this map is still denoted by
t_{\tau}
, and note that
t_{\tau}\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\times}
if
\tau\neq{\mathrm{id}}
(see for example item 2 of
[
BDM19
, Prop. 3.4]
). We let
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}=\frac{1}{t_{\pi}}\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}(({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}})\subset({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
.

Démonstration.

Let
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
. Then
\theta(x)\in\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
. Since
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}=0
on
\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
, we get that
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}\circ\theta(x)=0=\theta\circ\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}(x)
so that
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}(x)\in t_{\pi}{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
. Therefore,
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}(x)\in{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
. Since
t_{\pi}
is a pro-analytic vector of
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}
and since

\partial_{\mathrm{id}}(x)\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
.
∎

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F})^{F-{\mathrm{pa}}}=F_{\infty}(\!(t_{\pi})\!)
and
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{F-{\mathrm{pa}}}=F_{\infty}[\![t_{\pi}]\!]
.

Démonstration.

See
[
Por20
, Prop. 2.6]
.
∎

We let
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}
denote the set of pro-analytic vectors of
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+}
which are killed by
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}
.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}=\left\{\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}t_{\pi}^{k},a_{k}\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}\right\}
.

Démonstration.

Let
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
. For
i\geq 0
, we let
x_{i}=\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial_{\mathrm{id}}^{i+k}(x)}{k!}t_{\pi}^{k}
. By lemma
4.1
, we have that for any
i,k\geq 0
,
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}^{i+k}(x)
belongs to
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
so that the sum
\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial_{\mathrm{id}}^{i+k}(x)}{k!}t_{\pi}^{k}
converges in
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
to an element
x_{i}
such that
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}(x_{i})=0
.

The sum
\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{i}t_{\pi}^{i}
converges in
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
and a simple computation shows that
x=\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{i}t_{\pi}^{i}
.

Conversely, it is easy to check that if
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence of elements of
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}
, the sum
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}t_{\pi}^{k}
converges to an element of
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
.
∎

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}
such that
t_{\pi}|x
in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
. Then
x=0
.

Démonstration.

Let
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}
such that
t_{\pi}|x
, and assume that
x\neq 0
. We can therefore write
x=t_{\pi}^{k}\alpha
with
k\geq 1
,
\alpha\in{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
and
t_{\pi}
does not divide
\alpha
in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
. Moreover, since
t_{\pi}
is pro-analytic for the action of
\Gamma_{F}
, we get that
\alpha
is pro-analytic for the action of
\Gamma_{K}
.

By proposition
4.1
, we can write
\alpha=\sum_{j\geq 0}a_{j}t_{\pi}^{j}
where the
a_{j}
are elements of
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
killed by
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}
. The fact that
x
is killed by
\nabla_{\mathrm{id}}
translates into

\sum_{j\geq 0}(k+j)a_{j}t_{\pi}^{k+j}=0.

Applying
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}^{k}
to this equality and reducing mod

, we obtain that
a_{0}=0\mod t_{\pi}
and thus
t_{\pi}|\alpha
, which is not possible.
∎

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

For any
N\geq 1
, the map
\theta_{N}:({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}{\rightarrow}({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+}/t_{\pi}^{N}{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}
is injective.

Note that
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+}/t_{\pi}{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}=\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{la}}}
and that
\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{la}}}=\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
by
[
Ber16b
, Prop. 2.10]
. Note that this also implies that for any
m\geq 0
, the natural map
\theta:({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{\Gamma_{m}-{\mathrm{an}},\Sigma_{0}-{\mathrm{pa}}}{\rightarrow}\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{\Gamma_{m}-{\mathrm{an}}}
is injective. By
[
Por22
]
, the map
\theta:({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}{\rightarrow}\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
is surjective. In particular, using proposition
4.1
, we get the following “description” of
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
:

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

The natural map
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}\mapsto\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta(x_{i})t_{\pi}^{i}
, where
x_{i}=\frac{1}{i!}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\partial_{\mathrm{id}}^{i+k}(x)}{k!}t_{\pi}^{k}
, induces a
\Gamma_{F}
-equivariant isomorphism from
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
to
\widehat{F_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}[\![t_{\pi}]\!]
.

Démonstration.

We already know from the above that the map
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}\mapsto\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta(x_{i})t_{\pi}^{i}
is injective. To prove that it is surjective, recall that the map
\theta:({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}{\rightarrow}\widehat{K_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
is surjective. If
y\in\widehat{K_{\infty}}^{{\mathrm{la}}}
, let
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
such that
\theta(x)=y
. One can write
x=\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{i}t_{\pi}^{i}
with
\partial_{\mathrm{id}}(x_{i})=0
for all
i
, and thus
x_{0}
satisfies
\theta(x_{0})=\theta(x)=y
and
\partial(x_{0})=0
, so that the map above is injective.
∎

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},F}^{+}\simeq\widehat{F_{\infty}}[\![t_{\pi}]\!]
noncanonically but this isomorphism is not
\Gamma_{F}
-equivariant. However, taking only the pro-analytic vectors gives us a canonical isomorphism which is
\Gamma_{F}
-equivariant.

4.2.
The modules
{\bf D}_{\mathrm{Dif}}^{+}(V)

When
K_{\infty}/K
is the cyclotomic extension of
K
, Fontaine has proven in
[
Fon04
]
that the set of sub-
K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]
-modules free of finite type of
({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}}
and stable by the action of
\Gamma_{K}
admits a maximal element, usually denoted by
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
, and which is such that
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)={\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma_{K}
is close enough to
1
, then the power series defining
\log(\gamma)
converges as a power series of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-linear operators of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
, and the operator
\nabla_{V}=\frac{\log(\gamma)}{\log(\chi_{\mathrm{cycl}}(\gamma))}
does not depend on the choice of
\gamma
and satisfies the Leibniz rule
\nabla_{V}(\lambda\cdot x)=\lambda\nabla_{V}(x)+\nabla(\lambda)x
. The map
\theta:{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\to{{\bf C}_{p}}
induces an isomorphism of modules with connexions
({\bf D}_{\mathrm{Dif}}^{+}(V),\nabla_{V}){\rightarrow}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Sen}}}(V),\Theta_{V})
(see for example
[
Ber02
, §5.3]
).

The map
\iota_{n}:{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger,r_{n}}{\rightarrow}{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
sends
{\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger,r_{n}}
into
K_{n}[\![t]\!]\subset{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
and
{\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{n}}(V)
in a sub-
K_{n}[\![t]\!]
-module of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
, and we let
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V):=K_{n}[\![t]\!]\otimes_{\iota_{n}({\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger,r_{n}})}\iota_{n}({\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{n}}(V))
. Proposition 5.7 of
[
Ber02
]
shows that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)=K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]\otimes_{K_{n}[\![t]\!]}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V)
.

The fact that one could retrieve the modules
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
and
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V)
using the theory of locally analytic vectors had already been noticed by Berger and Colmez
[
BC16
, Rem. 3.3]
and proven by Porat in
[
Por20
, Prop. 3.3]
and
[
Por22
, Thm. 6.2]
but we now explain how this incorporates into the setting laid out at the end of §3.

Note that
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
, endowed with its natural topology, is not a Banach ring but a Fréchet ring, and as Berger points out in
[
Ber16b
]
, locally analytic vectors in the setting of Fréchet spaces usually have to be replaced with the weaker notion of pro-analytic vectors, because the resulting objects are too small in general. However, in the setting of
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
and
{\bf D}_{\mathrm{Dif}}^{+}(V)
, locally analytic vectors are actually sufficient to recover the theory.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}=K_{n}[\![t]\!]
,
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{{\mathrm{la}}}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}[\![t]\!]
and
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\mathrm{pa}}=K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]
.

Démonstration.

The second equality follows directly from the first one. For the first equality, take
x\in({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}
. We have
\theta(x)\in\widehat{K_{\infty}}^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}=K_{n}
, so that we can write
x=x_{0}+ty
, with
x_{0}\in K_{n}
and
y\in{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+}
, and one checks that
y
is
\Gamma_{n}
-analytic because
x
,
x_{0}
and
t
are. By induction,
x\in K_{n}[\![t]\!]
. Because
K_{n}\subset({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}
and because
t
is
\Gamma_{0}
-analytic, we have
K_{n}[\![t]\!]\subset({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

For
n\gg 0
, we have
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V)=(({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}

Démonstration.

Since
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{dR}},K}^{+})^{\Gamma_{n}-{\mathrm{an}}}=K_{n}[\![t]\!]
, it suffices to prove that the elements of
\iota_{n}({\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{n}}(V)
are
\Gamma_{n}
-analytic for
n\gg 0
.

Let
m\geq 0
be such that
{\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{m}}(V)
has the right dimension, and let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{d}
be a basis of
{\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{m}}(V)
. We can see the elements of
{\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{m}}(V)
as elements of
{\bf D}^{[r_{m};r_{m}]}(V)
. By §2.1 of
[
KR09
]
, these elements are
\Gamma_{n}
-analytic for
n\gg m
big enough. A direct consequence of lemma 2.2 of
[
BC16
]
shows that if we let
u_{i}=\varphi^{n-m}(e_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq d
, then the
u_{i}
are
\Gamma_{m}
-analytic as elements of
{\bf D}^{[r_{n};r_{n}]}(V)
, and we know that it is a basis of
{\bf D}^{\dagger,r_{n}}(V)
(since
\varphi^{*}({\bf D}^{\dagger}(V))\simeq{\bf D}^{\dagger}(V)
) and thus of
{\bf D}^{[r_{n};r_{n}]}(V)
. Therefore,
(\iota_{n}(u_{1}),\cdots,\iota_{n}(u_{d}))
generates
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V)
, and forms a basis of
\Gamma_{n}
-analytic elements of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},n}^{+}(V)
.
∎

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)=(({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}})^{{\mathrm{pa}}}
.

Démonstration.

This is proposition 3.3 of
[
Por20
]
and also follows from the previous proposition.
∎

In the Lubin-Tate case, one can also define a variant of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
, which we will denote by
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},F,{\mathrm{LT}}}^{+}(V)
, and which retrieves the
K
-Hodge Tate weights of

. We let
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},F,{\mathrm{LT}}}^{+}(V):=(({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}})^{F-{\mathrm{pa}}}

shows that this seems to be the good version for a generalization of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}}}^{+}(V)
in the

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{Dif}},F,{\mathrm{LT}},n}^{+}(V):=(({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{H_{K}})^{\Gamma_{n}-F-{\mathrm{an}}}
.

We now study the case of definitions
3
and
3
applied to
B={\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
, endowed with its Fréchet topology.

Recall that by the discussion following lemma 3.4 of
[
Ber16b
]
, there exists an element
t_{\pi}\in{{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}
such that

and

(in the cyclotomic case, we have

). Therefore, the element

and all its powers are invariants under the action of

. We have an inclusion
{{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}\rightarrow{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
defined in
[
Ber02
, §2]
and we still denote by

the image of

through this map.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}=K_{n}[\![x_{K}]\!]
and
({\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}=K_{n}(\!(x_{K})\!)
.

Démonstration.

Since both
K_{n}
and
x_{K}
are invariant under the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
, it suffices to show the direct inclusions.

Recall that that there is a map
\theta:{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}\rightarrow{{\bf C}_{p}}
which is a surjective,
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-equivariant ring morphism and such that
{\mathrm{ker}}\theta
is the maximal ideal of
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
and is generated by

. This induces a collection of maps
\theta:{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n}\rightarrow{{\bf C}_{p}}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n}
given by

and which are
{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
-equivariant surjective ring morphisms.

The same argument as in
[
BC16
, Prop. 4.7]
and theorem 2.7 of ibid. show that

so that we can write

with
y_{0}\in K_{n}
and

. Using the fact that both

and
t_{\pi}e^{-u_{K}}
are invariant under the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
and that

is invertible in
{\bf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n}
, we get that

We recall the notion of a weak Fréchet-Stein structure on a locally convex

\definame

5.3.
The anticyclotomic case

Berger and Colmez have proven in
[
BC16
]
that the theory of locally analytic vectors is the right object to consider in order to generalize classical Sen theory to arbitrary
p
-adic Lie extensions. With that in mind, and considering the results above that show that in the cyclotomic (and in the
F
-analytic Lubin-Tate) case one recovers classical
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory, it seems reasonable to assume that the theory of locally analytic vectors is the right object to consider in order to generalize
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules to arbitrary
p
-adic Lie extensions.

It has already been noticed that, even in the Lubin-Tate case, “one dimensional
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules” do not behave well
[
FX14
]
and that the kind of objects one should consider are multivariable Lubin-Tate
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules
[
Ber13
]
which arise from locally analytic vectors
[
Ber16b
]
.

Therefore, in general, one should expect to use that theory for arbitrary
p
-adic Lie extensions to get a theory of
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules over
({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{pa}}

is a faithfully exact functor. We now give some insight as to why such a generalization does not seem to be true in general, using the anticyclotomic extension as a potential counterexample.

Let
F/{{\bf Q}_{p}}
be the unramified extension of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
of degree
2

to be equal to
p
in our Lubin-Tate setting. We let

{{\bf Q}_{p}}

p

7.

In this section we give an example of why locally analytic vectors are usually not the right object to consider when working with Fréchet rings. is injective since
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{I}(V)
is flat over
({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{\nabla=0}
(it is torsion free over
({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{\nabla=0}
which is a PID by corollary
7.1
).

In order to prove that
\alpha^{\prime}
is injective, we are reduced to check that, given a family of elements
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d})
linearly independent over
({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{\nabla=0}
, they are linearly independent over
{\mathrm{Frac}}({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})
. We prove the result by induction on

. The result is trivial when
d=1
because
({\mathrm{Frac}}({\bf B}_{K}^{I})\otimes_{{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger,r}}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger,r}(V)){\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n}
is free over
{\mathrm{Frac}}({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})
. Assume now that

is such that the result holds for

. Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d}
in
{\mathrm{Frac}}({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}u_{i}=0
. By induction, we may assume that
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
. Dividing by
\lambda_{1}
, we obtain that
u_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}u_{i}=0
where
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}
. Applying
\nabla
, we get that
\sum_{i=2}^{d}\nabla(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})u_{i}=0
so that the
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
are all zero by induction.

This now implies that
\alpha
is injective, so that
{\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n}\otimes_{({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{\nabla=0}}\mathcal{D}_{n}^{I}(V)
is finitely generated. By lemma
7.1
and descent for faitfully flat modules, we deduce that

is finitely generated, and thus free since it is torsion free over
({\bf B}_{K}^{I}{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{\nabla=0}
which is a PID.
∎

8.

We now explain how some of the rings previously introduced provide some results towards the question of the existence of a ring of periods for trianguline representations. We will start by recalling the notions of trianguline representations and refinements.

8.2.
Discussion on a ring of periods for trianguline representations

By proposition
8.1
, we know that the category of (split) trianguline representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
is a Tannakian category. Because of this and because of proposition
8.1
, it appears reasonable to look for a ring
B
such that trianguline representations are exactly the representations which are
B
-admissible in the sense of Fontaine.

Recall that the notion of admissibility in the sense of Fontaine is defined for what he called regular rings and is as follows (we only recall the definitions of
[
Fon94b
]
in the particular case of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-representations because that’s all we need here).

Let
B
be a topological
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-algebra endowed with an action of a group
G
. For any
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-representation of
G
, we let
D_{B}(V):=(B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{G}
. We let
\alpha_{B}(V)
denote the
B
-linear map
B\otimes_{B^{G}}D_{B}(V)\longrightarrow B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
deduced from the inclusion
D_{B}(V)\subset B\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
by extending the scalars to
B
. The ring
B
is said to be
G
-regular if the following hold :

(1)

B
is reduced ;

(2)

for any
p
-adic representation
V
of
G
, the map
\alpha_{V}
is injective ;

(3)

any element
b
of
B
which is nonzero and is such that the
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
-line generated by
B
is
G
-stable is invertible.

The last condition implies in particular that
B^{G}
is a field. If
B
is
G
-regular, a representation
V
of
G
is said to be
B
-admissible if
\alpha_{B}(V)
is an isomorphism, which is equivalent as saying that
\dim_{B^{G}}D_{B}(V)=\dim_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V
.

Unfortunately, it seems to us that in the case we consider, the last condition is too strong and thus we extend the notion of
G
-regularity as follows : we say that
B
is
G
-regular if the following conditions are met :

(1)

B
is reduced ;

(2)

for any
p
-adic representation
V
of
G
,
D_{B}(V)
is a free
B^{G}
-module ;

(3)

the map
\alpha_{V}
is injective.

It is clear that
G
-regular rings in the sense of Fontaine are
G
-regular for us, but that the converse does not hold.

In the rest of the paper,
G
-regularity and admissibility are to be understood in our sense.

We now explain exactly what we mean by a ring of trianguline periods.

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

A
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-regular ring

if trianguline representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}

-admissible, and if

-admissible representations of

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-regular ring and let

{\mathcal{G}}_{K}

is

-admissible if and only if there exists a finite extension

Démonstration.

It’s clear that if

-admissible. To show the reverse, first note that the admissibility of an

-representation or as an

∎

Unlike in the crystalline or semistable case, if such a ring exists, it has to depend on

\propname

\the\smf@thm

There is no ring

Let

By the results of §5, the periods of any representation live in

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}({\mathrm{Fil}}_{d-1}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)))
, the matrices of
\nabla_{u}
and
\varphi
are respectively of the form :

\begin{pmatrix}w(\delta_{1})&*&\cdots&*\\
0&w(\delta_{2})&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&w(\delta_{d-1})\end{pmatrix}\quad\textrm{and }\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{1}(\pi)&*&\cdots&*\\
0&\delta_{2}(\pi)&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\delta_{d-1}(\pi)\end{pmatrix}.

Since our constructions are also stable by quotients by saturated sub-objects and using the proof in the rank
1
case, we know that the matrices of
\nabla_{u}
and
\varphi_{q}
in a basis of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)/{\mathrm{Fil}}_{d-1}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V))\simeq E\otimes_{K}{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K}(\delta_{d})
are respectively of the form
(w(\delta_{d}))
and
(\delta_{d}(\pi))
. Therefore, in an adapted basis for the refinement of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
corresponding to
\delta
, the matrices of
\varphi_{q}
and
\nabla_{u}
are as we wanted.
∎

In particular, as in the crystalline case, a refinement defines an ordering on both the eigenvalues of
\varphi_{q}
and on the set of Hodge-Tate weights of
V
, and encodes the data of the Hodge-Tate weights of its parameter.

8.4.
Crystabelian representations

Recall that to any
F
-analytic representation
V
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, we can attach a filtered
\varphi_{q}
-module over
K

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
, which is a finite dimensional
K
-vector space (whose dimension is
\leq\dim_{K}(V)
), endowed with a Frobenius map
\varphi_{q}
such that
\varphi_{q}^{*}D\simeq D
, and with a filtration by
K
subspaces, indexed by
{\bf Z}
, which is decreasing and separated.

Given the construction of our rings of periods, it is quite obvious that the modules
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
attached to
F
-analytic
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
contain its crystalline periods :

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
V
be an
F
-analytic
p
-adic representation. Then

(1)

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V)\subset{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)^{\nabla_{u}=0}
;

(2)

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)\subset({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)[1/x_{K}])^{\nabla_{u}=0}
.

Démonstration.

Item 1 just follows from the facts that
\nabla_{u}
and the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
commute, that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V)=({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{+}\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K}}
and that
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}})^{\nabla_{u}=0}={{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}
.

Item 2 follows from the fact that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)=({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{+}[1/t]\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}V)^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K}}
by lemma 3.8 of
[
Por20
]
and that
({\bf B}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}[1/x_{K}])^{\nabla_{u}=0}={{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{\mathrm{rig}}^{+}}[1/t_{\pi}]
.
∎

A crystabelian representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
is a representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
which becomes crystalline over an abelian extension of
K
. In what follows, we will assume that
V
is a crystabelian
F
-analytic representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. Since
K^{\mathrm{ab}}=K^{\mathrm{unr}}\cdot K_{\infty}
, and by the same argument as in the proof of proposition
8.2
, it means that there exists
n\geq 0
such that
V_{|G_{K_{n}}}
is crystalline. We still let
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
stand for the corresponding filtered
\varphi_{q}
-module, even though the filtration is defined over
K_{n}
.

Let
k\geq 0
be such that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V(\chi_{\pi}^{-k}))={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V(\chi_{\pi}^{-k}))
. A quick computation shows that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V(\chi_{\pi}^{-k}))=e^{ku_{K}}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
and that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V(\chi_{\pi}^{-k}))=t_{\pi}^{k}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
. In particular, this shows that

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)=e^{-ku_{K}}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)\supset{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V(\chi_{\pi}^{-k}))=t_{\pi}^{k}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)

so that
x_{K}^{k}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)\subset{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
. Since we have
\varphi(x_{K})=\pi\cdot x_{K}
, this shows that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
contains a copy of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
, but with the filtration and Frobenius shifted by some power. Note that we can also recover the filtration on
K_{\infty}\otimes_{K}{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V)
as it was done for
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{dR}}}^{+}(V)
in §4.

Assume for now that
V
is a crystalline positive
p
-adic representation, which means that
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}^{+}(V)
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}=({\mathcal{F}}_{i})_{i=0,\dots,d}
be a full
\varphi
- and
\nabla_{u}
-stable
E{\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}
-filtration of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
. Since the filtration is stable by
\nabla_{u}
, the filtration
{\mathcal{F}}^{\nabla_{u}=0}=({\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\nabla_{u}=0})_{i=0,\dots,d}
is also a full
\varphi
-stable filtration of
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(V)^{\nabla_{u}=0}={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
. In particular, refinements in our sense for trianguline representations give rise to refinements of positive crystalline representations. In the other direction, we can for example use proposition
8.1
in order to construct a filtration on
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)
starting from a
\varphi
-stable filtration on
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
and then use proposition
8.3
to recover the filtration on
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
. By compatibility of the constructions, we see that our definition of refinements is an extension of the one of
[
BC09a
, §2.4]
.

We now explain how to recover our module
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
from
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
V
be an
F
-analytic crystabelian representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. Then

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[1/x_{K}]={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)[1/x_{K}].

Démonstration.

By
[
KR09
, Prop. 2.2.6]
, we have
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)=({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)[1/t_{\pi}])^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
, and by proposition
7.1
, we have
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)=({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V){\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
. In particular, if we let
D=({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)[1/t_{\pi}]{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
, then
D
contains both
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
and
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
. Moreover, since
x_{K}=t_{\pi}e^{-u_{K}}
and since
e^{-u_{K}}
is invertible in
{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}
, we have that
D=({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)[1/x_{K}]{\{\{u_{K}\}\}}_{n})^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}
. Since
\frac{1}{x_{K}}
is invariant under the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}
, this implies that
D={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)[1/x_{K}]
. But since
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
has the right dimension, we also know that
D=({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)\otimes_{K}{\bf B}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}[1/x_{K}])^{{\mathcal{G}}_{K_{n}}}={\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)\otimes_{K}K{\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[1/x_{K}]
.
∎

It remains to see how one can recover
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
inside
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)[1/x_{K}]
. First note that we can define a filtration on
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[1/x_{K}]
by

{\mathrm{Fil}}^{k}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[1/x_{K}])=\bigcup_{i+j=k}x^{i}\left({\mathrm{Fil}}^{j}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)\right){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have

{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{n,{\mathrm{an}}}(V)=\left\{\begin{aligned} z\in{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[\frac{1}{x_{K}}]\textrm{ such that }\varphi_{q}^{-n}(z)\in{\mathrm{Fil}}^{0}({\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V){\langle\langle x_{K}\rangle\rangle}[\frac{1}{x_{K}}])\\
\textrm{ for }n\gg 0\end{aligned}\right\}.

Démonstration.

This follows from the usual way one recovers
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\dagger}(V)
from
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{crys}}}(V)
(see for example
[
Ber08
, §V.3]
) and from the previous lemma.
∎

Because of proposition
8.2
, one could be weary of the fact that our modules
{\bf D}_{{\mathrm{tri}},K}^{{\mathrm{an}}}(V)
do not take into account what happens at a finite level inside the tower
K_{\infty}=\cup_{n\geq 0}K_{n}
. Regarding potential applications to trianguline representatons, this is already solved by theorem
8.3
, but we also mention the following result, which adds some insight for why this is not a problem in the case we consider :

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
V
be a potentially crystalline representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
. If
V
is trianguline then
V
is crystabelian.

Démonstration.

We prove the result by induction. First note that any
p
-adic representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
of rank
1
which is potentially crystalline is crystabelian because such a representation factors through
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)
.

The same arguments as in the proof of lemma 6.4 of
[
Ber02
]
shows that is
W
is a potentially crystalline
B
-pair which is an extension of
E
by
E^{\prime}
, where
V
and
V^{\prime}
are two crystalline
B
-pairs, then
W
is crystalline.

Now if
W=W(V)
is the
B
-pair attached to
V
, since
V
is trianguline there exists a finite extension
L
of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
such that
L\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}W
is an extension of
E
by
E^{\prime}
, where
E
is a rank
1

{\bf B}_{|K}^{\otimes L}
-pair and
E^{\prime}
is a rank
d-1

{\bf B}_{|K}^{\otimes L}
-pair. Since
V
is potentially crystalline, so are
E
and
E^{\prime}
, and by induction
E
and
E^{\prime}
are both crystabelian, so that there exists
n\geq 0
such that
E
and
E^{\prime}
are crystalline
{\bf B}_{|K_{n}}^{\otimes L}
-pairs. Therefore
L\otimes_{{{\bf Q}_{p}}}W
is a crystalline
{\bf B}_{|K_{n}}^{\otimes L}
-pair, so that
V
is crystabelian.
∎

Do you know what q-means?
Joao F. Doriguello, Alessandro Luongo, Ewin Tang

Abstract
Clustering is one of the most important tools for analysis of large datasets, and perhaps the most popular clustering algorithm is Lloyd’s iteration for k k -means. This iteration takes n n vectors V = [ v 1 , … , v n ] ∈ ℝ n × d V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d} and outputs k k centroids c 1 , … , c k ∈ ℝ d c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} ; these partition the vectors into clusters based on which centroid is closest to a particular vector. We present an overall improved version of the “ q q -means” algorithm, the quantum algorithm originally proposed by Kerenidis, Landman, Luongo, and Prakash (NeurIPS’19) which performs ε \varepsilon - k k -means, an approximate version of k k -means clustering. Our algorithm does not rely on quantum linear algebra primitives of prior work, but instead only uses QRAM to prepare simple states based on the current iteration’s clusters and multivariate quantum amplitude estimation. The time complexity is O ~ ​ ( ‖ V ‖ F n ​ k 5 / 2 ​ d ε ​ ( k + log ⁡ n ) ) \widetilde{O}\big(\frac{\|V\|_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{k^{5/2}d}{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{k}+\log{n})\big) and maintains the logarithmic dependence on n n while improving the dependence on most of the other parameters. We also present a “dequantized” algorithm for ε \varepsilon - k k -means which runs in O ⁡ ( ‖ V ‖ F 2 n ​ k 2 ε 2 ​ ( k ​ d + log ⁡ n ) ) O\big(\frac{\|V\|_{F}^{2}}{n}\frac{k^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(kd+\log{n})\big) time. Notably, this classical algorithm matches the logarithmic dependence on n n attained by the quantum algorithm.

Introduction

Among machine learning problems, data clustering and the
k
-means problem are of particular relevance and have attracted much attention in the past
[
HW79
,
KM99
,
LVV03
]
. Here the task is to find an assignment of each vector from a dataset of size
n
to one of
k
labels (for a given
k
assumed to be known) such that similar vectors are assigned to the same cluster. To be more precise, in the
k
-means problem we are given
n
vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as columns in a matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, and a positive integer
k
, and the task is to find
k
centroids
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the cost function, called
residual sum of squares

\displaystyle\text{RSS}:=\sum_{i\in[n]}\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}\|^{2}

is minimized, where
\|v_{i}-c_{j}\|
is the Euclidean distance between
v_{i}
and
c_{j}
and
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since the
k
-means problem is known to be NP-hard
[
Das08
,
Vat09
,
MNV12
]
, several polynomial-time algorithms
[
KMN+02
,
JKS14
,
ANFSW19
,
BGJK20
]
have been developed to obtain
approximate
solutions. One such algorithm is the
k
-means algorithm, or Lloyd’s iterations, introduced by Lloyd
[
Llo82
]
. The
k
-means algorithm is a heuristic algorithm that iteratively updates the centroids
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}
until some desired precision is reached. At each time step
t
, the algorithm clusters the data in
k
clusters denoted by the sets
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}\subseteq[n]
,
j\in[k]
, each with centroid
c_{j}^{t}
, and then updates the centroids based on such clustering. More precisely, the
k
-means algorithm starts with initial centroids
c_{1}^{0},\dots,c_{k}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, picked either randomly or through some pre-processing routine as in the
k
-means++ algorithm
[
AV07
]
, and alternates between two steps:

•

Each vector
v_{i}
,
i\in[n]
, is assigned a cluster
\mathcal{C}_{\ell_{i}^{t}}^{t}
where
\ell^{t}_{i}=\arg\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
;

•

The new centroids
\{c_{j}^{t+1}\}_{j=1}^{k}
are updated based on the new clusters,
c_{j}^{t+1}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}v_{i}
.

The above steps are repeated until
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j\in[k]}\|c_{j}^{t}-c_{j}^{t+1}\|\leq\tau
for a given threshold
\tau>0
, in which case we say that the algorithm has converged.

On the other hand, quantum computation is a fast-paced field that promises a new paradigm to solve certain tasks more efficiently, well-known examples being Shor’s algorithm
[
Sho94
,
Sho99
]
and Grover’s search
[
Gro96
,
Gro97
]
. The subfield of quantum machine learning aims to offer computational advantages in machine learning, with many algorithms proposed to attain these advantages
[
LMR14
,
KP17
,
KP20a
,
CGJ19
,
LMR13
,
AHKZ20
]
. A notable line of work in this subfield is that of quantum versions of the
k
-means algorithm, to improve the classical running time of
O(knd)
per-iteration. Aimeur, Brassard, and Gambs
[
ABG13
]
proposed a
O(n^{3/2}/\sqrt{k})
-time quantum algorithm based on quantum minimum finding
[
DH96
]
for executing a single Lloyd’s iteration for the
k
-median clustering problem (which is related to the
k
-means problem). Lloyd, Mohseni, and Rebentrost
[
LMR13
]
gave a
O\big(\frac{kn\log{d}}{\varepsilon}\big)
-time quantum algorithm for executing a single Lloyd’s iteration from the
k
-means problem. The authors used an efficient subroutine for quantum distance estimation with error
\varepsilon
assuming quantum access to the dataset via a QRAM (Quantum Random Access Memory)
[
GLM08a
,
GLM08b
]
.

More recently, Kerenidis, Landman, Luongo, and Prakash
[
KLLP19
]
proposed a quantum version of Lloyd’s iterations and
k
-means++, which they named
q
-means, using quantum linear algebra subroutines and assuming the input vectors be stored in a QRAM. Interestingly enough, their runtime only depends polylogarithmically on the size
n
of the dataset. On the other hand, however, their quantum algorithm only performs the iteration approximately and does not give provable approximation guarantees, since Lloyd’s iterations also do not. Instead, the authors show that
q
-means performs a robust version of
k
-means which they name
\varepsilon
-
k
-means, and then show through experiments that the approximation does not worsen the quality of the centroids. In the
\varepsilon
-
k
-means algorithm, a noise parameter
\varepsilon>0
is introduced both in the distance estimation and centroid update steps. It alternates now between the steps:

1.

Each vector
v_{i}
is assigned a cluster
\mathcal{C}_{\ell_{i}^{t}}^{t}
such that
\ell_{i}^{t}\in\{l\in[k]:|\|v_{i}-c_{i}^{\ast}\|-\|v_{i}-c_{l}^{t}\||\leq\varepsilon\}
, where
c^{\ast}_{i}
is the closet centroid to
v_{i}
;

2.

The new centroids
\{c_{j}^{t+1}\}_{j\in[n]}
are updated such that
\big\|c_{j}^{t+1}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}v_{i}\big\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Again, it is iterated until convergence.

The overall idea behind
q
-means as proposed in
[
KLLP19
]
is to first create the quantum state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i\in[n]}|i\rangle|\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle

using distance estimation and quantum minimum finding
[
DH96
]
, followed by measuring the label register
|\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle
to obtain

\displaystyle|\chi_{j}^{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}|i\rangle

for some random
j\in[k]
. Here
|\chi_{j}^{t}\rangle
is the quantum state of the characteristic vector
\chi_{j}^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as
(\chi_{j}^{t})_{i}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}
if
i\in\mathcal{C}^{t}_{j}
and
0
if
i\not\in\mathcal{C}^{t}_{j}
. The
q
-means algorithm then proceeds to perform a matrix multiplication with matrix
V^{\top}
and the quantum state
|\chi_{j}^{t}\rangle
by using quantum linear algebra techniques. This leads to

\displaystyle|c_{j}^{t+1}\rangle=\sum_{i\in[d]}\frac{(c_{j}^{t+1})_{i}}{\|c_{j}^{t+1}\|}|i\rangle

by the crucial fact that
c_{j}^{t+1}=V^{\top}\chi_{j}^{t}
. Finally, quantum tomography
[
KP20b
]
is performed on
|c_{j}^{t+1}\rangle
in order to retrieve a classical description. Since quantum linear algebra techniques are used to obtain
|c_{j}^{t+1}\rangle
from
|\chi_{j}^{t}\rangle
, the final runtime depends on quantities like the condition number
\kappa(V)
of the matrix
V
and a matrix dependent parameter
\mu(V)
which is upper bounded by the Frobenius norm
\|V\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{j\in[d]}V_{ij}^{2}}
of
V
.
Since the runtime for estimating the norm is dominated by the runtime of the tomography, the final runtime is the following.

Fact 1

(
[
KLLP19
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

For a parameter
\varepsilon>0
and data matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
with
1\leq\|v_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta
and condition number
\kappa(V)
, the
q
-means algorithm outputs centroids consistent with the classical
\varepsilon
-
k
-means algorithm in
\widetilde{O}\big(kd\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\kappa(V)(\mu(V)+k\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon})+k^{2}\frac{\eta^{3/2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\kappa(V)\mu(V)\big)
time per iteration.

1.1
Our results

In this work, we provide an improved
q
-means algorithm that avoids the need for quantum linear algebra subroutines and still keeps the polylogarithmically dependence on the size
n
of the dataset. Just like
[
KLLP19
]
, we construct states of the form
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i\in[n]}|i,\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle
, but instead of performing quantum linear algebra subroutines and quantum tomography, we input such states (or more precisely the unitaries behind them) into a quantum amplitude estimation subroutine, in this case, the multivariate quantum Monte Carlo subroutine from Cornelissen, Hamoudi, and Jerbi
[
CHJ22
]
. The outcome is several vectors that approximate
c_{1}^{t+1},\dots,c_{k}^{t+1}
up to an additive error
\varepsilon
in
\ell_{\infty}
-norm. In order to construct the states
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i\in[n]}|i,\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle
we require
V
to be stored in a KP-tree
[
Pra14
,
KP17
]
and to be accessible via a QRAM
[
GLM08a
,
GLM08b
]
. Furthermore, in the same spirit of previous “dequantization” results
[
Tan19
,
Tan21
,
GLT18
,
GST22
]
, we provide a classical algorithm for
\varepsilon
-
k
-means based on
\ell_{2}
-sampling that has a very similar runtime compared to our
q
-means, which includes the logarithmic dependence on
n
. Here we assume access to a data structure that allows efficient
\ell_{2}
-sampling from the rows of
V
and from the vector of its row norms.

Theorem 2
.

Let
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
with
\min_{i\in[n]}\|v_{i}\|\geq 1
and a parameter
\varepsilon>0
. Assume that all clusters satisfy
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|=\Omega(n/k)
. Then the
\varepsilon
-
k
-means algorithm can be executed with probability at least
1-\delta
and in time per iteration

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{\|V\|_{F}^{2}}{n}\frac{k^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(kd+\log{n}\right)\log\frac{k}{\delta}\right).

Moreover, there is a quantum algorithm that outputs centroids consistent with the classical
\varepsilon
-
k
-means with probability at least
1-\delta
and in time per iteration

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{\|V\|_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{k^{3/2}d}{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{k}+\log{n})(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{d})\log^{2}\frac{1}{\delta}\right),

where
\widetilde{O}()
hides
polylog
factors in
k
,
d
,
1/\varepsilon
, and
\|V\|_{F}/\sqrt{n}
.

Related work

Here we briefly mention related works that have appeared online around the same time as ours. First, the independent work of Jaiswal
[
Jai23
]
has recently quantized the highly parallel, sampling-based approximation scheme of
[
BGJK20
]
and thus obtained a quantum algorithm for the

-means problem with
provable

[

[

, who introduced a quantum algorithm based on the original q-means algorithm from Kerenidis

Preliminaries

Define
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
. In the rest of the paper, our dataset is a matrix
V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
comprised of row vectors
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i\in[n]
, such that
\min_{i\in[n]}\|v_{i}\|\geq 1
, where
n
is the number of samples and
d
is the number of features. Given a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\|x\|:=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in[d]}x_{i}^{2}}
and
\|x\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in[d]}\{x_{i}\}
, and by
\mathcal{D}_{x}
we denote the distribution over
[d]
with probability density function
\mathcal{D}_{x}(i)=x_{i}^{2}/\|x\|^{2}
. For
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, let
\|V\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{j\in[d]}V_{ij}^{2}}
be its Frobenius norm and
\|V\|_{2,\infty}=\max_{i\in[n]}\|v_{i}\|
its maximum row
2
-norm. We use
|\bar{0}\rangle
to denote the state
|0\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|0\rangle
where the number of qubits is clear from context.

In the next sections, we will make use of the quantum minimum finding subroutine.

Fact 3

(Quantum minimum finding
[
DH96
]
)
.

Given
\delta\in(0,1)
and oracle access to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there is a quantum algorithm that outputs
|\psi_{x}\rangle
in
O(\sqrt{N}\log(1/\delta))
time such that, upon measuring
|\psi_{x}\rangle
in the computational basis, the outcome equals
\arg\min_{i\in[N]}\{x_{i}\}
with probability at least
1-\delta
.

We shall also employ the multivariate quantum Monte Carlo subroutine from Cornelissen, Hamoudi, and Jerbi
[
CHJ22
]
.

Fact 4

(
[
CHJ22
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Consider a bounded
N
-dimensional random variable
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
over a probability space
(\Omega,2^{\Omega},\mathbb{P})
with mean
\mu=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathbb{P}(\omega)X(\omega)
and such that
\|X\|\leq 1
. Assume access to a unitary that prepares a superposition over the probability space
U_{\mathbb{P}}:|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(\omega)}|\omega\rangle
and to a unitary that evaluates the random variable over the sample set
\mathcal{B}_{X}:|\omega\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|\omega\rangle|X(\omega)\rangle
. Given
\delta\in(0,1)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and an upper bound
L_{2}\geq\mathbb{E}[\|X\|]
, there is a quantum algorithm that outputs
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\|\mu-\widetilde{\mu}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\sqrt{L_{2}}\log(N/\delta)}{m}
with success probability at least
1-\delta
and using
O(m\poly\log{m})
queries to the oracles
U_{\mathbb{P}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{X}
.

Finally, the following concentration inequalities and approximation lemma will be useful.

Fact 5

(Chernoff’s bound)
.

Let
X:=\sum_{i\in[N]}X_{i}
where
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
are independently distributed in
[0,1]
. Then
\operatorname{Pr}[|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\geq\epsilon\mathbb{E}[X]]\leq 2e^{-\epsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}[X]/3}
for
\epsilon>0
.

Fact 6

(McDiarmid’s Inequality (Bernstein form))
.

Let
f(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})
be a function of independent random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
such that, for all
i\in[N]
and choices of
X_{1},\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{N}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\max}_{X_{i},X^{\prime}_{i}}|f(X_{1},\dots,X_{i},\dots,X_{N})-f(X_{1},\dots,X^{\prime}_{i},\dots,X_{N})|\leq\delta,

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{X_{i}}[f(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})^{2}]-\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{X_{i}}[f(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})]^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}.

Then, for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pr}[f(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})\geq t]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}/2}{N\sigma^{2}+\delta t/3}\right).

Lemma 7
.

Let
\widetilde{a},a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be such that
|a-\widetilde{a}|\leq\epsilon
, where
\epsilon\in[0,\frac{a}{2}]
. Then
\left|\frac{1}{\widetilde{a}}-\frac{1}{a}\right|\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{a^{2}}
.

Proof.

Since
a-\widetilde{a}\leq\epsilon\implies\frac{1}{\widetilde{a}}\leq\frac{1}{a-\epsilon}\leq\frac{2}{a}

\left|\frac{1}{\widetilde{a}}-\frac{1}{a}\right|=\left|\frac{\widetilde{a}-a}{a\widetilde{a}}\right|\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{a^{2}}
.

Computational models and data structures

In our classical algorithm, we shall assume the existence of a low-overhead data structure storing the dataset matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.

.

We say we have

V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

V
is stored in a data structure that supports the following operations:

1.

Reading and updating an entry of
V
in
O(\log(nd))

2.

Finding
\|v_{i}\|
and
\|V\|_{F}
in
O(\log{n})

3.

Sampling from
\mathcal{D}_{(\|v_{i}\|)_{i=1}^{n}}

in
O(\log{n})
and

In our quantum algorithm, we shall assume quantum access to a data structure that stores the dataset matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and the centroid matrix

.

Definition 9

(Quantum query access)
.

We say we have

V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

V

2.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and assume quantum query access to a matrix
V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
with
\min_{i\in[n]}\|v_{i}\|\geq 1
. If all clusters satisfy
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|=\Omega(n/k)
, then
Algorithm

2
outputs centroids consistent with the
\varepsilon
-
k
-means with probability at least
1-\delta
and in time per iteration

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{\|V\|_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{k^{3/2}d}{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{k}+\log{n})(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{d})\log^{2}\frac{1}{\delta}\right),

where
\widetilde{O}()
hides
polylog
factors in
k
,
d
,
1/\varepsilon
, and
\|V\|_{F}/\sqrt{n}
.

Proof.

We start with the error analysis. Consider the unitaries

\displaystyle U_{V}:|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto\sum_{i\in[n]}\sqrt{\frac{\|v_{i}\|}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}}|i,\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{V}:|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes k}\mapsto|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes(j-1)}|v_{i}/\|v_{i}\|\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes(k-j)},

\displaystyle U_{I}:|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto\sum_{i\in[n]}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}|i,\ell_{i}^{t}\rangle,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{I}:|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes k}\mapsto|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes(j-1)}|1\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle^{\otimes(k-j)}.

The unitaries
U_{V}
and
U_{I}
can be thought of as preparing a superposition over probability spaces with distributions
\mathbb{P}_{V}
and
\mathbb{P}_{I}
, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{V}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\frac{\|v_{i}\|}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}&\text{if}~j=\ell_{i}^{t},\\
0&\text{if}~j\neq\ell_{i}^{t},\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{P}_{I}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}&\text{if}~j=\ell_{i}^{t},\\
0&\text{if}~j\neq\ell_{i}^{t},\end{cases}

while the unitaries
\mathcal{B}_{V}
and
\mathcal{B}_{I}
can be thought of as binary encoding the random variables
X_{V}:[n]\times[k]\to(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
and
X_{I}:[n]\times[k]\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, respectively, given by
X_{V}(i,j)=(0,\dots,0,\frac{v_{i}}{\|v_{i}\|},0,\dots,0)
and
X_{I}(i,j)=(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)
, where the non-zero entry is the
j
-th entry. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in[n]\times[k]}\mathbb{P}_{V}(i,j)X_{V}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{t}}\frac{v_{i}}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}},\dots,\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{t}}\frac{v_{i}}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in[n]\times[k]}\mathbb{P}_{I}(i,j)X_{I}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{1}^{t}|}{n},\dots,\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{k}^{t}|}{n}\right).

Therefore, the multivariate quantum Monte Carlo subroutine (
4
) returns
P\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
Q\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{k}
such that, with probability at least
1-\delta
and for some
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
to be determined,

\displaystyle\left|P_{j}-\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}{n}\right|\leq\varepsilon_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Bigg\|Q_{j}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}\frac{v_{i}}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}\Bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{2}\qquad\forall j\in[k].

This means that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{c}_{j}^{t+1}-c_{j}^{t+1}\|=\Bigg\|\frac{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}{n}\frac{Q_{j}}{P_{j}}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}v_{i}\Bigg\|\leq\left|\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}{nP_{j}}-1\right|\Bigg\|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}v_{i}\Bigg\|+\frac{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}{nP_{j}}\Bigg\|Q_{j}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}\frac{v_{i}}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}\Bigg\|.

For
\varepsilon_{1}
sufficiently small such that
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\max_{j\in[k]}\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}{2n}
, then
\left|P_{j}-\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}{n}\right|\leq\varepsilon_{1}\implies\frac{1}{nP_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|-n\varepsilon_{1}}\leq\frac{2}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}
and
\left|\frac{1}{P_{j}}-\frac{n}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\right|\leq\frac{2n^{2}}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|^{2}}\varepsilon_{1}
according to
Lemma

7
. Moreover,
\left\|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}}v_{i}\right\|=\|V^{\top}\chi_{j}^{t}\|\leq\|V\|\|\chi_{j}^{t}\|\leq\|V\|_{F}/\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{c}_{j}^{t+1}-c_{j}^{t+1}\|\leq\frac{\|V\|_{F}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}}\frac{2n}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\varepsilon_{1}+\frac{2\sqrt{d}\|V\|_{2,\infty}}{|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|}\varepsilon_{2}.

It suffices to take
\varepsilon_{1}=O\big(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\|V\|_{F}}\frac{\varepsilon}{k^{3/2}}\big)
and
\varepsilon_{2}=O\big(\frac{n}{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}\frac{\varepsilon}{k\sqrt{d}}\big)
in order to obtain
\|\widetilde{c}_{j}^{t+1}-c_{j}^{t+1}\|\leq\varepsilon
, where we used that
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{t}|=\Omega(n/k)
. In order to obtain

and

in the multivariate quantum Monte Carlo subroutine a number of times equal to (note that

On the other hand, in order to obtain

, we must query the unitaries
U_{V}
and
\mathcal{B}_{V}
in the multivariate quantum Monte Carlo subroutine a number of times equal to (note that
\|X_{V}\|=1
and
\mathbb{E}[\|X_{V}\|]=1

\displaystyle q=\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{\|V\|_{2,\infty}}{n}\frac{k\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\log(kd/\delta)\right)=\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{\|V\|_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{k\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\log(kd/\delta)\right).

Finally, we must show how to perform the unitaries

,

Lemma 12

(Exact cluster assignment)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and assume quantum query access to matrices
V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
[c_{1}^{t},\dots,c_{k}^{t}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
.
There is a quantum algorithm that performs the mapping
\ket{i}|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i\rangle|L^{t}_{i}\rangle
in
O(\sqrt{k}d\log(1/\delta)\log{n})
time such that, upon measuring
|L^{t}_{i}\rangle
on the computational basis, the outcome equals
\arg\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Proof.

First we describe how to perform the mapping
|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i,j\rangle|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle
. Starting from
|i,j\rangle|\bar{0},\bar{0}\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle
, we query
2d
times the QRAM oracles used in
Definition

9

\displaystyle|i,j\rangle|\bar{0},\bar{0}\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i,j\rangle|v_{i},c_{j}^{t}\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle.

This operation is followed by computing the distance
\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
between the vectors
v_{i}
and
c_{j}^{t}
in
O(d)
size and
O(\log d)
depth by using a classical circuit, which leads to

\displaystyle|i,j\rangle|v_{i},c_{j}^{t}\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i,j\rangle|v_{i},c_{j}^{t}\rangle|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle.

Uncomputing the first step leads to the desire state. Overall, the map
|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i,j\rangle|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle
uses
O(d)
queries to the matrices
V
and
[c_{1}^{t},\dots,c_{k}^{t}]
.

Fix
i\in[n]
. The mapping
|j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|j\rangle|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle
can be viewed as quantum access to the vector
(\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|)_{j\in[k]}
. Therefore, we can assign a cluster
\ell^{t}_{i}:=\arg\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
to the vector

) quantum minimum finding subroutine (

), which leads to the map

\displaystyle|i\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i\rangle|L^{t}_{i}\rangle,

where upon measuring
|L^{t}_{i}\rangle
on the computational basis, the outcome equals
\ell^{t}_{i}=\arg\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
with probability at least
1-\delta
.
The time cost of finding the minimum is
O(\sqrt{k}\log(1/\delta))
queries to the unitary performing the mapping
|i,j\rangle|\bar{0}\rangle\mapsto|i,j\rangle|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle
, to a total time complexity of
O(\sqrt{k}d\log(1/\delta)\log{n})
.

Remark 13
.

It is possible to avoid quantum query access to the centroid matrix
[c_{1}^{t},\dots,c_{k}^{t}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
by accessing it through the fixed registers
\bigotimes_{j\in[k]}|c_{j}^{t}\rangle
. This, however, hinders the use of quantum minimum finding. The index
\ell_{i}^{t}=\arg\min_{j\in[k]}\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|
can be found, instead, through a classical circuit on the registers
\bigotimes_{j\in[k]}|\|v_{i}-c_{j}^{t}\|\rangle
, which modifies the time complexity of
Lemma

12
to
O(d(k+\log{n}))
.

Conclusions and future work

In this work, we improved the runtime from the original
q
-means
[
KLLP19
]
by avoiding quantum linear algorithm subroutines and quantum tomography. We further dequantised our

, while the dependence on

We mention a few possible future directions. One is to consider a quantum version of

]
, in order to obtain tighter runtimes. Another interesting direction is bridge both our and Jaiswal
[

]
works and obtain improved time complexities in
k
and
1/\varepsilon
together with provable guarantees.

Behaviour of n n -point scattering amplitudes at high energies
Shreya Shrivastava

Abstract
We study the on-shell scattering amplitudes in quantum gravity for high-energy collisions in the eikonal approximation. We first evaluate the n n -loop 2-particle scattering amplitude in the high energy and low momentum transfer limit. We do so in a symmetrized manner by finding the contributions of each of the particle worldines to the scattering amplitude and gluing them together via the n n intermediate particle exchanges. In this limit on applying the eikonal approximation and summing over all n n -loop Feynman diagrams we obtain a closed form for the 2 particle scattering amplitude. Finally, we extend this approach to obtain a generalized eikonal approximation for N N -particle scattering at high energies and small momentum transfers. The generalised form of the scattering amplitude can then be used to evaluate the bound states of the system.

Introduction

In light of ’t Hooft’s work which draws a relation between Veneziano amplitudes in string theory and high energy two-particle scattering in quantum gravity and string theory
tHooft:1987vrq
, several calculations have been done to establish a similar connection between the two pictures. One such approach has been done by Muzinich and Soldate
PhysRevD.37.359
where four-point string amplitudes at large center-of-mass energy
\sqrt{s}

and fixed momentum transfer
q=-t
is related to 2-particle scattering amplitude obtained by summing multiple Reggeized graviton exchange in the eikonal approximation in D space-time
dimensions. Additionally, the expression obtained for 2-2 scattering in QED in the eikonal approximation has been used to determine the relativistic Balmer formula in
PhysRevD.1.2349
. However the aforementioned works have been limited to 2-particle scattering amplitudes, due to the more involved calculations required for N-particle scattering. In this paper we develop a symmetric approach to relativistic eikonal calculations resulting in a straightforward generalization to higher point amplitudes. We further confirm the result obtained by H. and E. Verlinde
Verlinde_1992
stating that N-particle amplitudes describing the gravitational
scattering of the matter particles factorize into the product of
two-particle amplitudes.

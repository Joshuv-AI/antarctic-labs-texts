The influence of adsorbed atoms concentration on the temperature coefficient of resonant frequency of the quasi-Rayleigh wave
M.Ya. Seneta ? , ?, R.M. Peleshchak ? , ?, A.I. Nesterivskyi ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, N.I. Lazurchak ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, S.K. Guba ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}

Abstract
Within the model of self-consistent connection of quasi-Rayleigh wave with adsorbed atoms, a method of constructing a new class of radiometric sensors of the temperature and concentration of adsorbed atoms on the surface acoustic waves is proposed. Based on the developed theory of the dispersion and acoustic mode width of a quasi-Rayleigh wave on the adsorbed surface of monocrystals with a Zinc blende structure, the temperature coefficient of the resonant frequency of the surface acoustic wave is calculated depending on the temperature and on the concentration of adsorbed atoms. Key words: surface acoustic wave (SAW), adsorbed atoms, temperature coefficient of resonant frequency



1
Introduction

Currently, the surface acoustic wave (SAW) devices are widely used in acoustoelectronics. In order to create a new class of microelectronic and nanoelectronic SAW devices with controlled parameters, such as sensors of temperature, sensors of the concentration of adsorbed atoms, pressure sensors, gas sensors, biosensors, filters, signal processing units, automated actuators, etc., and to develop radio measuring remote systems for control and measurement of physical values, for control of technological processes and the parameters of the operation of various mechanisms for identification of objects, it is necessary to investigate the regularities of the changes of the dispersion of surface acoustic waves, the width of the surface elastic acoustic mode on the surface of semiconductors, the mechanisms of the electron states excitation on the dynamically deformed adsorbed surface and on the border of semiconductors, depending on the concentration and on the type of adsorbed atoms. These studies will be used in SAW technologies for the production of wireless, passive, and hybrid multifunctional SAW sensors.

3
The influence of the concentration of adsorbed atoms on the temperature coefficient of the resonant frequency

SAW-sensors measuring the temperature on the surface of a monocrystal without adsorbed atoms are based on the change of the frequency of the quasi-Rayleigh wave depending on the temperature.
This effect is described by the temperature coefficient of the resonance frequency (TCF), which is defined as the relative change in the resonance frequency with temperature
[
1
]
:

\alpha_{TCF}^{0}=\frac{1}{{{f}_{0}}}\frac{\text{d}{{f}_{0}}}{\operatorname{d}T}=\frac{1}{v}\frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}T}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{\text{d}T},

(3.1)

where
{{f}_{0}}
is the resonance SAW frequency;
v
is the velocity propagation of SAW;
\lambda
is the SAW length;
T
is the temperature of SAW-device.

The interaction between adatoms and a surface acoustic wave affects the value of its resonant frequency, which is expressed by the temperature coefficient of frequency
\alpha_{TCF}
. However, in expression (
3.1
) presented in
[
1
]
, there is no component for the temperature coefficient of frequency that describes the influence of the adsorbed atoms concentration on the value of this coefficient.

In the case where a monocrystal surface has got adsorbed atoms, the change of the resonant frequency occurs not only due to the changes of temperature but also due to the presence of adatoms:

f({N}_{0d},T)=f_{0}(T)+\Delta f({N}_{0d},T).

(3.2)

Let us perform logarithmic operations on two parts of the expression:

\ln f({N}_{0d},T)=\ln f_{0}(T)+\ln\left[1+\frac{\Delta f({N}_{0d},T)}{f_{0}(T)}\right].

(3.3)

Taking into account that
{\left[\frac{\Delta f({N}_{0d},T)}{f_{0}(T)}\right]}^{2}\ll 1
, we receive:

\ln f({N}_{0d},T)=\ln f_{0}(T)+\frac{\Delta f({N}_{0d},T)}{f_{0}(T)}.

(3.4)

We take the derivative of two parts of expression(
3.4
). Taking into account the ratio
f=\frac{v}{\lambda}
, we obtain:

\frac{1}{f}\frac{\text{d}f({N}_{0d},T)}{\text{d}T}=\frac{1}{v}\frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}T}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{\text{d}T}+\left(\frac{1}{v}\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{\text{d}T}-\frac{\lambda}{v^{2}}\frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}T}\right)\Delta f({N}_{0d},T)+\frac{\lambda}{v}\frac{\text{d}\Delta f({N}_{0d},T)}{\text{d}T}.

(3.5)

Taking into account the concentration component, the dependence of the coefficient
\alpha_{TCF}
on temperature and on the concentration of the adsorbed atoms is described by the following functional expression:

\alpha_{TCF}({N}_{0d},T)=\alpha_{TCF}^{0}(T)+\Delta\alpha_{TCF}({N}_{0d},T);

(3.6)

\Delta\alpha_{\text{TCF}}({{N}_{0d}})=\frac{1}{v}\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{\text{d}T}\Delta f({{N}_{0d}})-\frac{\lambda}{{{v}^{2}}}\frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}T}\Delta f({{N}_{0d}})+\frac{\lambda}{v}\frac{\text{d}\Delta f({{N}_{0d}})}{\text{d}T}.

{\omega}^{\prime}(q,{{N}_{0d}})-{\omega}^{\prime}(q,0)=\Delta{\omega}^{\prime}(q,{{N}_{0d}})=2\piup\Delta f(q,{{N}_{0d}})
we get:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[q\sqrt{1-{{\xi}_{0}}^{2}}\frac{\partial\tilde{F}}{\partial{{N}_{1d}}}+(2-{{\xi}_{0}}^{2})\frac{{{\theta}_{d}}}{2a}\right].

4
Conclusion

1.

There was proposed a method for determining the concentration of adsorbed atoms using the radiometric sensors on surface acoustic waves on the basis of a model of a self-consistent connection of a quasi-Rayleigh wave with adsorbed atoms.

2.

There was calculated the temperature-concentration coefficient of the resonant frequency of the quasi-Rayleigh wave depending on the temperature and concentration of the adsorbed atoms, taking into account the theory of dispersion of the surface acoustic wave on the adsorbed surface of monocrystals having a Zinc blende structure.

3.

It was shown that the functional dependence
\alpha_{TCF}=f(N{{}_{0d}})
is of a non-monotonous character with a maximum corresponding to the adatoms concentration of
{{N}_{0d}}=1.87\cdot{{10}^{13}}
cm
-2
, which corresponds to the highest sensitivity of the radiometric sensor.

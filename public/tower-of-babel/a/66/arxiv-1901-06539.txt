W W -Types in Categories of Coalgebras
Taichi Uemura

Abstract
We construct W W -types in the category of coalgebras for a cartesian comonad. It generalizes the constructions of W W -types in presheaf toposes and gluing toposes.



1
Introduction

W
-type is an important feature of Martin-Löf’s dependent type
theory
[
Mar82
,
NPS90
]
and
allows us to define a wide range of inductive types. Its categorical
counterpart is the initial algebra for a polynomial endofunctor

[
MP00
,
GH04
]
.

In this paper we construct
W
-types in the category
of coalgebras for a cartesian comonad. This is a generalization of the
constructions of
W
-types in presheaf toposes and Freyd cover given
by Moerdijk and Palmgren
[
MP00
]
and
gluing
\Pi W
-pretoposes given by van den Berg

[
Ber06
]
.

In Section
2
we review the notion of

W
-type. In Section
3
we describe pushforwards in
the category of coalgebras for a cartesian comonad. In Section

4
we show the main theorem (Theorem

35
) and give some
applications. In Section
5
we show a
technical lemma used in the proof of the main theorem.

2
W
-Types in Categories

We review the notions of polynomial, algebra and
W
-type. First we
recall the notion of algebra for an endofunctor.

Definition 1
.

Let
P:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
be an endofunctor on a category
\mathcal{E}
. A

P
-algebra
consists of an object
X\in\mathcal{E}
and a
morphism
s_{X}:PX\to X
. A
morphism
of
P
-algebras

(X,s_{X})\to(Y,s_{Y})
is a morphism
h:X\to Y
in

\mathcal{E}
such that
h\circ s_{X}=s_{Y}\circ Ph
. We shall denote
by
\mathrm{Alg}(P)
the category of
P
-algebras and morphisms of

P
-algebras, and by
U_{P}:\mathrm{Alg}(P)\to\mathcal{E}
the obvious
forgetful functor. An
initial
P
-algebra
is an initial
object in the category
\mathrm{Alg}(P)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
P:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
and
Q:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
be
endofunctors. A
lax morphism
P\to Q
is a pair
(H,\sigma)
consisting of a functor
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
and a
natural transformation
\sigma:QH\Rightarrow HP
. A
strong
morphism
P\to Q
is a lax morphism
(H,\sigma):P\to Q

such that
\sigma
is an natural isomorphism.

Proposition 3
.

Let
P:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
and
Q:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
be
endofunctors. A lax morphism
(H,\sigma):P\to Q
induces a
functor
\tilde{H}:\mathrm{Alg}(P)\to\mathrm{Alg}(Q)
such that

U_{Q}\tilde{H}=HU_{P}
defined by

\tilde{H}(X,s)=(HX,Hs\circ\sigma(X))
.

The following is analogous to the adjoint lifting theorem for
(co)algebras for (co)monads by Johnstone
[
Joh75
]

and Keigher
[
Kei75
]
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
P:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
and
Q:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
be
endofunctors and
(H,\sigma):P\to Q
a strong
morphism. Suppose that
H
has a right adjoint
R
. Then the
induced functor
\tilde{H}:\mathrm{Alg}(P)\to\mathrm{Alg}(Q)
has a right
adjoint
\tilde{R}:\mathrm{Alg}(Q)\to\mathrm{Alg}(P)
such that

U_{P}\tilde{R}=RU_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\eta
and
\varepsilon
denote the unit and counit,
respectively, of the adjunction
H\dashv R
. The functor
R
is
part of a lax morphism
Q\to P
with the mate of
\sigma^{-1}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces PR}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RHPR}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RQHR}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RQ.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta PR}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\sigma^{-1}R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RQ\varepsilon}

Hence we have a functor
\tilde{R}:\mathrm{Alg}(Q)\to\mathrm{Alg}(P)
such
that
U_{P}\tilde{R}=RU_{Q}
. One can show that
\eta
and

\varepsilon
defines natural transformations
1\Rightarrow\tilde{R}\tilde{H}
and
\tilde{H}\tilde{R}\Rightarrow 1
respectively, so

\tilde{R}
is the right adjoint to
\tilde{H}
.
∎

W
-types are defined as the initial algebras for endofunctors
represented by polynomials.

.

\mathcal{E}

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category. For objects
I,J\in\mathcal{E}
, a
polynomial
from
I
to
J
is a triple
(f,g,h)
of morphisms of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

For a polynomial
(f,g,h)
from
I
to
J
, we define a
functor
P_{f,g,h}:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
to be

f_{!}g_{*}h^{*}
. We say a functor
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
is
represented by a polynomial
(f,g,h)
from
I
to
J
when

P
is isomorphic to
P_{f,g,h}
. We shall denote by

\mathbf{Poly}_{\mathcal{E}}(I,J)
the class of functors
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J

that are represented by some polynomial from
I
to
J
. We
often omit the subscript and simply write
\mathbf{Poly}(I,J)
when the
category
\mathcal{E}
is clear from the context.

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category. For an object

I\in\mathcal{E}
, an
endopolynomial
on
I
is a polynomial of
the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

We say
\mathcal{E}

has
W
-types
or is a locally cartesian
closed category
with
W
-types
if, for any endopolynomial

(f,g,h)
on any object
I
, there exists an initial

P_{f,g,h}
-algebra.

Remark 8
.

W
-types in this paper are what are called dependent
W
-types
or indexed
W
-types in the literature. Gambino and Hyland

[
GH04
]
showed that a locally cartesian closed
category has all non-dependent
W
-types if and only if it has all
dependent
W
-types. So our terminology “having
W
-types”
coincides with that of Moerdijk and Palmgren

[
MP00
]
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
be locally cartesian closed categories
and
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
a locally cartesian closed functor,
namely a functor that preserves finite limits and pushforwards. By
definition, for an endopolynomial
(f,g,h)
on an object

I\in\mathcal{E}
, the functor
H/I:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{F}/HI

is part of a strong morphism
P_{f,g,h}\to P_{Hf,Hg,Hh}
. Suppose
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
have
W
-types. We say

H

preserves
W
-types
if the induced functor

\mathrm{Alg}(P_{f,g,h})\to\mathrm{Alg}(P_{Hf,Hg,Hh})
preserves initial
objects for any endopolynomial
(f,g,h)
in
\mathcal{E}
.

By Proposition
4
we have the
following.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
be locally cartesian closed
categories with
W
-types and
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
a
locally cartesian closed functor. If
H
has a right adjoint, then

H
preserves
W
-types.

The class of functors represented by polynomials is closed under
several constructions.

Proposition 11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category.

1.

For any object
I\in\mathcal{E}
, the identity functor
\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/I
belongs to
\mathbf{Poly}(I,I)
.

2.

For functors
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
and
Q:\mathcal{E}/J\to\mathcal{E}/K
,
if
P
belongs to
\mathbf{Poly}(I,J)
and
Q
belongs to

\mathbf{Poly}(J,K)
, then
QP
belongs to
\mathbf{Poly}(I,K)
.

Proof.

The identity functor
\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/I
is represented by the
polynomial
(1_{I},1_{I},1_{I})
. For the composition, see

[
GK13
]
.
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category and

P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
a functor. If
P
belongs to

\mathbf{Poly}(I,J)
, then
P/K
belongs to
\mathbf{Poly}(K,PK)
for any
object
K\in\mathcal{E}/I
.

Proof.

Suppose that

is represented by a polynomial

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J.}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{*}K}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}

We introduce the notion of
class of polynomial functors
which
is a generalization of the family of classes of functors

(\mathbf{Poly}_{\mathcal{E}}(I,J))_{I,J\in\mathcal{E}}
. This generalization is necessary
to apply Theorem
35
on the existence of

W
-types for a wide range of categorical constructions (see Section

4.1
and
4.2
).

Definition 13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category with

W
-types. A
class of polynomial functors
consists of a class

\mathscr{P}(I,J)
of functors
\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
for each pair of
objects
I,J\in\mathcal{E}
satisfying the following conditions.

1.

If
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
belongs to
\mathbf{Poly}(I,J)
, then it
also belongs to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
.

2.

If
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
and
Q:\mathcal{E}/J\to\mathcal{E}/K
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(J,K)
, then
QP
belongs to

\mathscr{P}(I,K)
.

3.

If
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
and
P\cong Q
, then
Q
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
.

4.

If
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/J
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
, then
P/K:\mathcal{E}/K\to\mathcal{E}/PK
belongs to
\mathscr{P}(K,PK)
for any object
K\in\mathcal{E}/I
.

5.

For any endofunctor
P:\mathcal{E}/I\to\mathcal{E}/I
that belongs to

\mathscr{P}(I,I)
, there exists an initial
P
-algebra.

Example 14
.

For a locally cartesian closed category
\mathcal{E}
with
W
-types,
the classes
(\mathbf{Poly}(I,J))_{I,J\in\mathcal{E}}
form a class of
polynomial functors by Proposition
11

and
12
.

Example 15
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a class of polynomial functors on a locally cartesian
closed category
\mathcal{E}
with
W
-types. For an object

K\in\mathcal{E}
and objects
I,J\in\mathcal{E}/K
, we define a class

\mathscr{P}/K(I,J)
of functors
(\mathcal{E}/K)/I\to(\mathcal{E}/K)/J
to be the
class of functors that belong to
\mathscr{P}(I,J)
under the
identification
(\mathcal{E}/K)/I\cong\mathcal{E}/I
and
(\mathcal{E}/K)/J\cong\mathcal{E}/J
. Then
(\mathscr{P}/K(I,J))_{I,J\in\mathcal{E}/K}
form a class of
polynomial functors on
\mathcal{E}/K
.

Remark 16
.

Clearly
\mathbf{Poly}_{\mathcal{E}/K}\subset\mathbf{Poly}_{\mathcal{E}}/K

2.1
The Characterization Theorem

In some nice category
\mathcal{E}
,
W
-types have a nice characterization
due to van den Berg
[
Ber05
]
. To achieve this
characterization, the category
\mathcal{E}
should be extensive

[
CLW93
]
.

Definition 17
.

An
extensive category
is a category
\mathcal{E}
with finite
coproduct such that, for any objects
A,B\in\mathcal{E}
, the coproduct
functor

+:\mathcal{E}/A\times\mathcal{E}/B\to\mathcal{E}/(A+B)

is an equivalence of categories.

When
\mathcal{E}
is locally cartesian closed, the extensivity of
\mathcal{E}
is
equivalent to much simpler conditions.

Definition 18
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a category with finite coproducts. We say a binary
coproduct
A+B
in
\mathcal{E}
is
disjoint
if the inclusions

A\to A+B
and
B\to A+B
are monic and the pullback of
these inclusions is the initial object.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally cartesian closed category with finite
coproducts. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

\mathcal{E}
is extensive.

2.

Binary coproducts in
\mathcal{E}
are disjoint.

3.

The binary coproduct
1+1
is disjoint.

Proof.

It is known that a category with finite coproducts is extensive if
and only if binary coproducts are universal and disjoint

[
CLW93
, Proposition 2.14]
. Binary
coproducts in a locally cartesian closed category
\mathcal{E}
are always
universal because pullback functors have right adjoints. Hence the
conditions
1
and
2
are equivalent. Trivially

2
implies
3
. Suppose that the coproduct
1+1
is disjoint. To show that any binary coproduct
A+B
is
disjoint, it suffices to show that the pullback of the inclusions

A\to A+B
and
B\to A+B
is the initial object

[
CLW93
, Proposition 2.13]
because
binary coproducts are universal. Now we have a morphism
A\times_{A+B}B\to 1\times_{1+1}1\cong 0
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is
cartesian closed, the initial object is strict

[
Joh02
, A1.5.12]
. Hence
A\times_{A+B}B

is the initial object.
∎

Definition 20
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a category. We say an object
A\in\mathcal{E}
is

well-founded
if any monomorphism
X\rightarrowtail A
into

A
is an isomorphism.

Definition 21
.

Let
P:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
be an endofunctor on a category. A

fixed point of
P
is a
P
-algebra
(X,s)
such that

s:PX\to X
is an isomorphism.

(Characterization Theorem)
.

Let

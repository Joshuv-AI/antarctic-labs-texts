Confined run-and-tumble model with boundary aggregation: long time behavior and convergence to the confined Fokker-Planck model
Jingyi Fu, Jiuyang Liang, Benoit Perthame, Min Tang

Abstract
The motile micro-organisms such as E. coli , sperm, or some seaweed are usually modelled by self-propelled particles that move with the run-and-tumble process. Individual-based stochastic models are usually employed to model the aggregation phenomenon at the boundary, which is an active research field that has attracted a lot of biologists and biophysicists. Self-propelled particles at the microscale have complex behaviors, while characteristics at the population level are more important for practical applications but rely on individual behaviors. Kinetic PDE models that describe the time evolution of the probability density distribution of the motile micro-organisms are widely used. However, how to impose the appropriate boundary conditions that take into account the boundary aggregation phenomena is rarely studied. In this paper, we propose the boundary conditions for a 2D confined run-and-tumble model (CRTM) for self-propelled particle populations moving between two parallel plates with a run-and-tumble process. The proposed model satisfies the relative entropy inequality and thus long-time convergence. We establish the relation between CRTM and the confined Fokker-Planck model (CFPM) studied in [ 22 ] . We prove theoretically that when the tumble is highly forward peaked and frequent enough, CRTM converges asymptotically to the CFPM. A numerical comparison of the CRTM with aggregation and CFPM is given. The time evolution of both the deterministic PDE model and individual-based stochastic simulations are displayed, which match each other well.

Introduction

The run-and-tumble process is now standard to describe the movement of motile organisms, in which the bacteria like
E. coli
move straight forward and take reorientation after some time, then continue moving in a new direction
[
7

When the self-propelled micro-organisms move in a confined environment, they may aggregate at the boundary

This phenomenon has important influences on several biological processes. For example, the aggregation of sperm at the border affects mammalian reproduction
[

[
40
,
25

[
39

. Both sterical and hydrodynamical forces play important roles in boundary aggregations. In
[
9
]
, the authors measured the detailed density distribution between two parallel plates and pointed out that non-tumbling cells moving with hydrodynamical forces will reorient to the direction parallel to the surfaces and hence be attracted by the wall. On the other hand, the work of Guanglai Li et al.
[
30
,
29
]
proposed a model of self-propelled particles to explain the boundary aggregation by taking into account the steric force between the swimming cells and the surface and the rotational Brownian motion. More recently, observing by 3D holographic imaging, Bianchi et al. found that reorientation of cells occurs when they are in contact with the surface, and the orientations of cells always point into the surface, no matter the boundary is no-slip or free-slip

One simple stochastic microscopic model that can take into account the boundary aggregations is to assume that micro-organisms are confined in a 2-dimensional space between two horizontal boundaries
\{y=\pm L\}

(V\cos\theta,V\sin\theta)

When the micro-organisms are away from the boundaries, they follow the classical run-and-tumble movement; when moving toward the boundary, they touch it, and then stay at the boundary until their orientations direct them away from the boundary. For simplicity, we ignore any possible external fields and the rotational/translational diffusion here. More precisely, for each cell, let

0,~~&\text{for}~~|Y_{t}|=L~~\text{and}~~Y_{t}\Theta_{t}\geq 0,\end{cases}\\[12.0pt]
\Theta_{t}={\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{P_{t}}\Delta\Theta_{t}^{j},\end{cases}

(1.1)

Stochastic simulations can only obtain the macroscale characteristics after the whole simulation. It is desired to propose simpler mesoscopic kinetic models for the probability density distribution of the micro-organisms. The kinetic run-and-tumble model is developed and analyzed in

However, most previous works of the kinetic run-and-tumble model consider only the internal domain, and the boundary effects are rarely considered. Boundary aggregations are not considered in the imposed boundary conditions in some theoretical or numerical works. For example, reflective boundary conditions are considered in

We propose a confined run-and-tumble model (CRTM) here that describes the time evolution the probability density function of the stochastic process in (

Inside

In

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In

\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}+k_{-}(\theta)\right]n_{-}(t,\theta)-\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{0}\!K_{-}\left(\theta^{\prime},{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}\right)n_{-}(t,\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}=-V\sin\theta n(t,-L,\theta),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{0}\!V\sin\theta n^{\varepsilon}(t,L,\theta)\Phi(t,L,\theta)d\theta dt

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\eqref{5.3b}}}

\displaystyle-\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{0}\!\Phi(t,L,\theta)\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\!\pi}\!\!\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\theta^{\prime},\dfrac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}d\theta dt

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{Assumption(A1)}}

\displaystyle-\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi+\varepsilon}\Phi(t,L,\theta)\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\theta+2\pi-\varepsilon}^{\!\pi}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\theta^{\prime},\dfrac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}d\theta dt

\displaystyle-\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\Phi(t,L,\theta)\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta+\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\theta^{\prime},\dfrac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}d\theta dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,-\pi)\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi+\varepsilon}\!\!\int_{\theta+2\pi-\varepsilon}^{\!\pi}\!\!\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\pi)+\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}-\pi}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,\pi)\right]K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\pi,\dfrac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)d\theta^{\prime}d\theta dt

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,0)\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\int_{0}^{\theta+\varepsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\ \varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,0)+\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,0)\right]K_{\varepsilon+}\left(0,\dfrac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)d\theta^{\prime}d\theta dt+O(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{z=\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi+\varepsilon}\!\!\int_{\frac{\theta-\pi}{\varepsilon}}^{-\frac{2\pi}{\varepsilon}+1}\!\!-K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\pi,z\right)dzd\theta\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,-\pi)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\pi)dt

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi+\varepsilon}\!\!\int_{\frac{\theta-\pi}{\varepsilon}}^{-\frac{2\pi}{\varepsilon}+1}\!\!-\left(\frac{\theta-\pi}{\varepsilon}-z\right)K_{\varepsilon+}\left(\pi,z\right)dzd\theta\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,-\pi)\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\pi)}{\partial\theta}dt

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\int_{-1}^{\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}}-K_{\varepsilon+}\left(0,z\right)dzd\theta\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,0)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,0)dt

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\int_{-1}^{\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}}-\left(\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon}-z\right)K_{\varepsilon+}\left(0,z\right)dzd\theta\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,0)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,0)}{\partial\theta}dt+O(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\!\!\int_{-1}^{0}-zK_{\varepsilon+}(\pi,z)dz\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,-\pi)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\pi)dt-\!\!\int_{-1}^{0}-zK_{\varepsilon+}(0,z)dz\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,0)\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,0)dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}z^{2}K_{\varepsilon+}(\pi,z)dz\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,-\pi)\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,\pi)}{\partial\theta}dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}z^{2}K_{\varepsilon+}(0,z)dz\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,0)\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}(t,0)}{\partial\theta}dt+O(\varepsilon).

Using (
4.12
), (
4.21
) and periodicity of
\Phi(t,L,\theta)
in
\theta
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{0}\!V\sin\theta n^{\varepsilon}(t,L,\theta)\Phi(t,L,\theta)d\theta dt=D_{+}(\theta)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,\theta)\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,\theta)dt\bigg|_{0}^{\!\pi}.

(4.22)

Similarly, on
y=-L
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\!\pi}\!\!V\sin\theta n^{\varepsilon}(t,-L,\theta)\Phi(t,-L,\theta)d\theta dt=D_{-}(\theta)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,-L,\theta)\frac{\partial n_{-}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,\theta)dt\bigg|_{-\pi}^{0}.

(4.23)

We can take
\Phi(t,L,0)=\Phi(t,L,\pi)=\Phi(t,-L,-\pi)=\Phi(t,-L,0)=0
, then we pass to the limit and obtain the weak form of (
4.2b
).

On the other hand, when we take
\Phi(t,L,\theta)=0
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
\Phi(t,-L,\theta)=0
for
\theta\in(-\pi,0)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-L}^{L}\!\int_{-\pi}^{\!\pi}

\displaystyle\!-n^{\varepsilon}\left[\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}+V\sin\theta\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}-D(y,\theta)\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right]d\theta dydt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\!\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{0}\!V\sin\theta n^{\varepsilon}(t,L,\theta)\Phi(t,L,\theta)d\theta dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\!\pi}\!\!V\sin\theta n^{\varepsilon}(t,-L,\theta)\Phi(t,-L,\theta)d\theta dt

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\eqref{eq3.21},\eqref{eq3.22}}}

\displaystyle-D_{+}(\theta)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,L,\theta)\frac{\partial n_{+}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,\theta)dt\bigg|_{0}^{\!\pi}+D_{-}(\theta)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Phi(t,-L,\theta)\frac{\partial n_{-}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\theta}(t,\theta)dt\bigg|_{-\pi}^{0},

then we pass to the limit and get the weak form of (
4.2d
) and (
4.2e
). Hence we have completed the proof.

5.1
Description of numerical scheme

We consider an uniform mesh in both
y
and
\theta
in a rectangular domain
\Omega=[-L,L]\times[-\pi,\pi]
. The two integers
N_{y}
and
N_{\theta}
define the mesh sizes of
\Omega
,
\Delta y=L/N_{y}
and
\Delta\theta=\pi/N_{\theta}
along the
y
and
\theta
axes, respectively. The index sets are defined as

I_{y}=\{1,2,\cdots,2N_{y}\},~~I_{\theta+}=\{N_{\theta+1},\cdots,2N_{\theta}\},~~I_{\theta-}=\{1,\cdots,N_{\theta}\},~~I_{\theta}=I_{\theta+}\cup I_{\theta-}.

(5.1)

The grids inside the computational domain are

(y_{i},\theta_{j})=((i-N_{y}-1/2)\Delta y,~(j-N_{\theta}-1/2)\Delta\theta),~~i\in I_{y},~~j\in I_{\theta},

(5.2)

and the nodes at the boundaries are

(\pm L,~\theta_{j}),~~j\in I_{\theta}.

(5.3)

The unknowns are
n_{\pm,j}\approx n_{\pm}^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta_{j})
and

n_{i,j}\approx\dfrac{1}{\Delta y}\int_{y_{i}-\Delta y/2}^{y_{i}+\Delta y/2}n^{\varepsilon}(t,y_{i},\theta_{j})dy.

(5.4)

We use upwind discretization for
\frac{\partial n}{\partial y}
which has first-order convergence w.r.t
y
, and forward Euler method for
\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}
which has first-order convergence w.r.t
t
. The semi-discretized scheme for the scaled model (
4.3
) writes

\begin{cases}\dfrac{dn_{i,j}}{dt}+V\sin\theta_{j}\dfrac{n_{i,j}-n_{i-1,j}}{\Delta y}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{i,j}n_{i,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta}}K_{i,j^{\prime},j}n_{i,j^{\prime}}\right)=0,~~i\in I_{y},~~j\in I_{\theta+},\\[12.0pt]
\dfrac{dn_{i,j}}{dt}+V\sin\theta_{j}\dfrac{n_{i+1,j}-n_{i,j}}{\Delta y}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{i,j}n_{i,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta}}K_{i,j^{\prime},j}n_{i,j^{\prime}}\right)=0,~~i\in I_{y},~~j\in I_{\theta-},\\[12.0pt]
\dfrac{dn_{+,j}}{dt}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{2N_{y},j}n_{+,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta+}}K_{+,j^{\prime},j}n_{+,j^{\prime}}\right)=V\sin\theta_{j}n_{2N_{y},j},~~j\in I_{\theta+},\\[12.0pt]
\dfrac{dn_{-,j}}{dt}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{1,j}n_{-,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta-}}K_{-,j^{\prime},j}n_{-,j^{\prime}}\right)=-V\sin\theta_{j}n_{1,j},~~j\in I_{\theta-},\end{cases}

(5.5)

where

\begin{split}&k_{i,j}=k(y_{i},\theta_{j}),\qquad K_{i,j,j^{\prime}}=\int_{\theta_{j^{\prime}}-\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}^{\theta_{j^{\prime}}+\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}K\left(y_{i},\theta_{j},\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}-\theta_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right){\rm d}\theta^{\prime},\\
&k_{\pm,j}=k_{\pm}(\theta_{j}),\qquad K_{\pm,j,j^{\prime}}=\int_{\theta_{j^{\prime}}-\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}^{\theta_{j^{\prime}}+\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}K_{\pm}\left(\theta_{j},\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}-\theta_{j}}{\varepsilon}\right){\rm d}\theta^{\prime}.\end{split}

(5.6)

Since both
K
and
n^{\prime}
have no singularity, the integrals in (
5.6
) are discretized using either the adaptive Gauss-Legendre integral
quadrature
[
41
]
(function ”quadgk” in MATLAB) or the trapezoid rule
[
45
]
for their efficiency. The boundary conditions in (
4.6b
) and (
4.6c
) are discretized as

\begin{cases}-V\sin\theta_{j}n_{2N_{y}+1,j}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta+}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{+,j^{\prime},j}n_{+,j^{\prime}},~\quad j\in I_{\theta-},\\[12.0pt]
V\sin\theta_{j}n_{0,j}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta-}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{-,j^{\prime},j}n_{-,j^{\prime}},~\quad j\in I_{\theta+}.\end{cases}

(5.7)

Non-negative initial data
n_{i,j}(0)
and
n_{\pm,j}(0)
are chosen such that the condition

\Delta y\Delta\theta\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{y}\times I_{\theta}}n_{i,j}(0)+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta+}}n_{+,j}(0)+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta-}}n_{-,j}(0)=1

(5.8)

is satisfied and it is easy to verify that the numerical scheme conserves the discrete total mass
defined by

\Delta y\Delta\theta\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{y}\times I_{\theta}}n_{i,j}(t)+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta+}}n_{+,j}(t)+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta-}}n_{-,j}(t).

(5.9)

Remark 5.1
.

Here we have used

k_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\Delta\theta\Delta y}\int_{y_{i}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}}^{y_{i}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}}\int_{\theta_{j}-\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}^{\theta_{j}+\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}k(y,\theta){\rm d}\theta{\rm d}y,

(5.10)

K_{i,j,j^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\Delta\theta^{2}\Delta y}\int_{y_{i}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}}^{y_{i}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}}\int_{\theta_{j}-\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}^{\theta_{j}+\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}\int_{\theta_{j^{\prime}}-\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}^{\theta_{j^{\prime}}+\frac{\Delta\theta}{2}}K\left(y,\theta,\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}-\theta}{\varepsilon}\right){\rm d}\theta^{\prime}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}y,

(5.11)

to approximate

,

. The key point for mass conservation is to keep

(5.12)

When

\begin{cases}V\sin\theta_{j}\dfrac{m_{i,j}-m_{i-1,j}}{\Delta y}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{i,j}m_{i,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta}}K_{i,j^{\prime},j}m_{i,j^{\prime}}\right)=0,~~i\in I_{y},~~j\in I_{\theta+},\\
V\sin\theta_{j}\dfrac{m_{i+1,j}-m_{i,j}}{\Delta y}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{i,j}m_{i,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta}}K_{i,j^{\prime},j}m_{i,j^{\prime}}\right)=0,~~i\in I_{y},~~j\in I_{\theta-},\\
\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{2N_{y},j}m_{+,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta+}}K_{+,j^{\prime},j}m_{+,j^{\prime}}\right)=V\sin\theta_{j}m_{2N_{y},j},~~j\in I_{\theta+},\\
\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(k_{1,j}m_{-,j}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta-}}K_{-,j^{\prime},j}m_{-,j^{\prime}}\right)=-V\sin\theta_{j}m_{1,j},~~j\in I_{\theta-},\end{cases}

(5.13)

with boundary conditions

\begin{cases}-V\sin\theta_{j}m_{2N_{y}+1,j}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\in I_{\theta+}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}K_{+,j^{\prime},j}m_{+,j^{\prime}},~~j\in I_{\theta-},\\

(5.14)

whose total mass satisfies

\Delta y\Delta\theta\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{y}\times I_{\theta}}m_{i,j}+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta+}}m_{+,j}+\Delta\theta\sum_{j\in I_{\theta-}}m_{-,j}=1.

(5.15)

Algorithm 1

Monte Carlo simulation

1:

For each cell, generate a number of jump
P_{i}^{n}

\Gamma_{i}^{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &k(Y_{i}^{n-1},\Theta_{i}^{n-1}),&&\lvert Y_{i}^{n-1}\rvert<L,\\
&k_{\pm}(\Theta_{i}^{n-1}),&&Y_{i}^{n-1}=\pm L,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.17)

at time
\Delta t
, i.e.

\mathbb{P}(P_{i}^{n}=J)=\frac{(\Gamma_{i}^{n}\Delta t)^{J}}{J!}e^{-\Gamma_{i}^{n}\Delta t},\quad J\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.18)

2:

Sample
P_{i}^{n}
random increments
\{\Delta\Theta_{i,j}^{n}\}_{j=1,\cdots,P_{i}^{n}}
, which are independent identically distributed from the distribution

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{K_{\varepsilon}\left(Y_{i}^{n-1},\Theta_{i}^{n-1},\dfrac{\Delta\Theta}{\varepsilon}\right)}{k(Y_{i}^{n-1},\Theta_{i}^{n-1})},&&\lvert Y_{i}^{n-1}\rvert<L,\\
&\frac{K_{\varepsilon\pm}\left(\Theta_{i}^{n-1},\dfrac{\Delta\Theta}{\varepsilon}\right)}{k_{\pm}(\Theta_{i}^{n-1})},&&Y_{i}^{n-1}=\pm L.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.19)

3:

Sample
P_{i}^{n}
i.i.d random numbers
\{T_{j}\}_{j=1,\cdots,P_{i}^{n}}
which are uniformly distributed over
[0,1]
. Let

and
T_{P_{i}^{n}+1}=1
.

4:

Initialize

,
\Theta_{i}^{n}=\Theta_{i}^{n-1}
.

5:

for

j=1,2,\cdots P_{i}^{n}

do

6:

Compute
\tilde{Y}_{i}^{n}=Y_{i}^{n}+V\sin(\Theta_{i}^{n})(T_{j}-T_{j-1})\Delta t
. Evolve the position
Y_{i}^{n}
by

Y_{i}^{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &sign(\tilde{Y}_{i}^{n})L,&&\lvert\tilde{Y}_{i}^{n}\rvert>L,\\

(5.20)

7:

Evolve the orientation
\Theta_{i}^{n}

\Theta_{i}^{n}=\Theta_{i}^{n}+\Delta\Theta_{i,j}^{n}

8:

end

Motivated by recent biophysical experiments

We build a run-and-tumble model with boundary conditions that are more biologically relevant. The relative entropy inequality is established and it is theoretically proved that the system will converge to a steady state. Using similar scaling as in the derivation of the Fokker-Planck limit from the radiative transport equation, we can recover the CFPM studied in

The asymptotics allows us to compute the coefficients of the CFPM based on the individual rate of tumbling.
Several numerical comparisons between the CRTM and the CFPM are presented.

A field guide to categories with A n A_{n} fusion rules
Cain Edie-Michell, Scott Morrison

Abstract
. We collate information about the fusion categories with A n A_{n} fusion rules. This note includes the classification of these categories, a realisation via the Temperley-Lieb categories, the auto-equivalence groups (both braided and tensor), identifications of the subcategories of invertible objects, and explicit descriptions of the Drinfeld centres. The first section describes the classification of these categories (as monoidal, dagger, pivotal, and braided categories). The second section describes the properties of these categories.



1.
Classification

We say that a fusion category has fusion rules
A_{k+1}
if there are
k+1
isomorphism classes of simple objects, which we may index
X_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,k
, such that:

•

X_{0}
is the tensor identity,

•

X_{i}\otimes X_{1}\cong X_{i-1}\oplus X_{i+1}
when
1\leq i<k
,

•

X_{k}\otimes X_{1}\cong X_{k-1}
.

The fusion graph for tensoring by
X_{1}
is thus the
A_{k+1}
Dynkin diagram:

X_{0}
X_{1}
X_{2}
X_{k-1}
X_{k}

\cmdGR@edge@label
\cmdGR@edge@label
\cmdGR@edge@label

These hypotheses then ensure that the fusion rules are commutative, and in particular that

X_{i}\otimes X_{j}\cong\begin{cases}X_{|i-j|}\oplus X_{|i-j|+2}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{i+j}&i+j\leq k\\
X_{|i-j|}\oplus X_{|i-j|+2}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{2k-(i+j)}&i+j>k.\end{cases}

The only category with
A_{1}
fusion rules is
{\mathsf{Vec}}
. For all
k\geq 1
, the monoidal categories with fusion rules
A_{k+1}
are classified by
m\in\mathbb{Z}/(k+2)\mathbb{Z}
with
(m,k+2)=1
. See
[
FK93
]
for details. We name these monoidal categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
. The monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
has minimal field of definition
\mathbb{Q}(q)
where
q=e^{\frac{m\pi i}{k+2}}
. The value
q+q^{-1}
is a complete invariant of the monoidal categories with
A_{k+1}
fusion rules.

Dagger structures

The monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
admits a dagger structure
\dagger
given by the conjugate linear extension of reflection in a horizontal line. All other dagger structures are of the form
\phi\mapsto\lambda^{(n-m)/2}\phi^{\dagger}
(where
\phi:\Hom(X^{\otimes n}\to X^{\otimes m})
) for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
. We denote this dagger category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m,\lambda}^{\dagger}
.

Braidings

We recall that for
k>1
, the braided fusion categories with fusion rules
A_{k+1}
are classified by
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}/4(k+2){\mathbb{Z}}
with
(\ell,k+2)=1
. See
[
FK93
, Proposition 8.2.6]
for details. We name these braided categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,l}^{\text{br}}
. We can recover the underlying monoidal category of
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\ell}^{\text{br}}
by taking
m:=\ell\pmod{k+2}
. The braided category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,l}^{\text{br}}
has a split rational form over
{\mathbb{Q}}(s)
, where
s=\exp\left(2\pi i\frac{\ell+k+2}{4(k+2)}\right)=i\exp\left(\pi i\frac{\ell}{2(k+2)}\right)
. We have
s^{2}=\pm q
, and this
s
is a complete invariant of the braided category.

The above classification does not hold for
k=1
as it double counts the braided fusion categories. The
4
braided fusion categories with the
A_{2}
fusion rules are usually called
{\mathsf{Rep}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
,
{\mathsf{sVec}}
,
{\mathsf{Sem}}
, and
\overline{{\mathsf{Sem}}}
. (The category
{\mathsf{Sem}}
is the “semions”, and
\overline{{\mathsf{Sem}}}
its Galois conjugate.) The
A_{2}
categories can be identified as:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Rep}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{1,7}^{\text{br}}={\mathcal{C}}_{1,11}^{\text{br}}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{sVec}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{1,1}^{\text{br}}={\mathcal{C}}_{1,5}^{\text{br}}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Sem}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{1,4}^{\text{br}}={\mathcal{C}}_{1,8}^{\text{br}}

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathsf{Sem}}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{C}}_{1,2}^{\text{br}}={\mathcal{C}}_{1,10}^{\text{br}}.

The braided category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\ell}^{\text{br}}
can be realised by equipping the monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\ell\pmod{k+2}}
with a braiding that depends of

Galois conjugates

The monoidal categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
form two Galois equivalence classes when
k
is odd, depending on the parity of
m
. When
k
is even the monoidal categories lie in a single Galois orbit.
The braided categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\ell}^{\text{br}}
form three Galois equivalence classes when
k
is even, depending on whether
\ell\pmod{4}
lies in
\{0\}
,
\{2\}
, or
\{1,3\}
. When
k
is even the braided categories lie in a single Galois orbit.

Sphericality

The pivotal categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m,\pm}
are always spherical.

Drinfeld centre

The monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
can be equiped with a braiding to give the braided category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}^{\text{br}}
. This braided category is modular when
k
is even or
2\mid m
. For these cases we can use the results of
[
M“”ug03
]
to see that the Drinfeld centre of the monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}
is the braided category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}^{\text{br}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}^{\text{br op}}
.

When
k
is odd and
2\nmid m
the monoidal category
C_{k,m}
does not admit a modular braiding, so we have to work harder to compute the centre. As monoidal categories we have an equivalence
C_{k,m}\cong{\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m})\boxtimes{\mathcal{C}}_{1,1}
. Thus
Z(C_{k,m})\cong Z({\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m}))\boxtimes Z({\mathcal{C}}_{1,1})
as the Drinfeld centre respects products of categories
[
KZ15
]
.

The monoidal category
{\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m})
admits a modular braiding, that is the braiding inherited from the braided category
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,m}^{\text{br}}
. Thus
Z({\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m}))\cong{\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m}^{\text{br}})\boxtimes{\operatorname{Ad}}(C_{k,m}^{\text{br op}})
.

The monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,1}
is better known as
{\mathsf{Vec}}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. The Drinfeld centre of this monoidal category is described in
[
Ost03a
]
and a skein theoretic presentation is given in
[
EM17
]
.

Inverse scattering transform for the Tzitzéica equation
Linlin Wang, Junyi Zhu

Abstract
The inverse scattering transform is extended to investigate the Tzitzéica equation. A set of sectionally analytic eigenfunctions and auxiliary eigenfunctions are introduced. We note that in this procedure, the auxiliary eigenfunctions play an important role. Besides, the symmetries of the analytic eigenfunctions and scattering data are discussed. The asymptotic behaviors of the Jost eigenfunctions are derived systematically. A Riemann-Hilbert problem is constructed to study the inverse scattering problem. Lastly, some novel exact solutions are obtained for reflectionless potentials. Keywords : The Tzitzéica equation; Lax pair; The inverse spectral transform; Vanishing boundary condition; Explicit solutions.

equation through inverse scattering transforms. To this end, a Riemann-Hilbert (RH) problem with the corresponding six jump matrices and six jump conditions is constructed. 3.1
Discrete spectrum

In the following, we switch our attention to discuss the discrete spectrum
for the Tzitzéica equation. To this end, we introduce the following six
3\times 3
matrices

\mathbf{G}_{1}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\psi_{-1}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{+2}(x,t,\nu),\chi_{1}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{1},

(3.1a)

\mathbf{G}_{2}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\chi_{2}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{+2}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{-3}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{2},

(3.1b)

\mathbf{G}_{3}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\psi_{+1}(x,t,\nu),\chi_{3}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{-3}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{3},

(3.1c)

\mathbf{G}_{4}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\psi_{+1}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{-2}(x,t,\nu),\chi_{4}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{4},

(3.1d)

\mathbf{G}_{5}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\chi_{5}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{-2}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{+3}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{5},

(3.1e)

\mathbf{G}_{6}(x,t,\nu)=\bigg(\psi_{-1}(x,t,\nu),\chi_{6}(x,t,\nu),\psi_{+3}(x,t,\nu)\bigg),\quad\nu\in D_{6}.

(3.1f)

We note that the above matrix function are analytic in their respective domain.
From (2.31), we know that

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{1}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=s_{11}(\nu)r_{22}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{1},

(3.2a)

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{2}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=s_{33}(\nu)r_{22}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{2},

(3.2b)

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{3}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=r_{11}(\nu)s_{33}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{3},

(3.2c)

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{4}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=r_{11}(\nu)s_{22}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{4},

(3.2d)

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{5}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=r_{33}(\nu)s_{22}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{5},

(3.2e)

\det\Big(\mathbf{G}_{6}(x,t,\nu)\Big)=s_{11}(\nu)s_{33}(\nu)\gamma,\quad\nu\in D_{6}.

(3.2f)

Thus, we can easily observe that the columns of

\mathbf{G}_{1}(x,t,\nu)
become linearly dependent at the zeros of

s_{11}(\nu)
and
r_{22}(\nu)
.

According to the symmetry conditions about the scattering matrices discussed in section 2.4, we can locate the distribution of the discrete spectrum. For convenience, we let
\nu_{0}\in D_{2}
be a discrete eigenvalue of the scattering problem. It is easy to see that
\nu_{0}
can be divided into two types of eigenvalue.

1. The first type is that

\nu_{0}=-\nu_{0}^{\ast}
.

2. The second type is that
\nu_{0}\neq-\nu_{0}^{\ast}
.

When
\nu_{0}=-\nu_{0}^{\ast}
, the symmetry conditions about the scattering matrices imply that

\displaystyle s_{33}(\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{22}(\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{11}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow s_{11}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{22}(\alpha\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{33}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r_{11}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{11}(\alpha\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{22}(\nu_{0})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r_{22}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{33}(\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{33}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=0.

(3.3)

When
\nu_{0}\neq-\nu_{0}^{\ast}
, we can derive

\displaystyle s_{33}(\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{22}(\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{11}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow s_{11}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{22}(\alpha\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow s_{33}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r_{11}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{11}(-\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{22}(-\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r_{22}(-\alpha\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{33}(-\nu_{0})=0\Leftrightarrow r_{33}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=0.

(3.4)

In addition, for
\nu_{0}=-\nu_{0}^{\ast},\nu_{0}\in D_{2}
, Jost functions and the auxiliary eigenfunctions
admit the following statements

(1):
\chi_{2}(\nu_{0})=\textbf{0},

(2):
\chi_{4}(\alpha\nu_{0})=\textbf{0},

(3):
\chi_{6}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=\textbf{0},

(4): There exists a constant
b_{2}
such that
\psi_{+2}(-\nu_{0}^{\ast})=b_{2}\psi_{-3}(-\nu_{0}^{\ast}),

(5): There exists a constant
b_{4}
such that
\psi_{+3}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=b_{4}\psi_{-1}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast}),

(6): There exists a constant
b_{6}
such that
\psi_{+1}(-\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=b_{6}\psi_{-2}(-\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast}).

Similarly, when
\nu_{0}\neq-\nu_{0}^{\ast},\nu_{0}\in D_{2}
, the following statements are equivalent

(1):
\chi_{1}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\textbf{0},

(2):
\chi_{3}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\textbf{0},

(3):
\chi_{5}(\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\textbf{0},

(4): There exists a constant
b_{1}
such that
\psi_{+1}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=b_{1}\psi_{-3}(-\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}),

(5): There exists a constant
b_{3}
such that
\psi_{+2}(-\alpha\nu_{0})=b_{3}\psi_{-1}(-\alpha\nu_{0}),

(6): There exists a constant
b_{5}
such that
\psi_{+3}(-\nu_{0})=b_{5}\psi_{-2}(-\nu_{j})

In what follows, we assume that the zeros of

and

(i) If
\nu_{0}
is an eigenvalue of the first type, we can
directly get

result, we observe that

\psi_{+2}(\nu_{0})=f_{0}\psi_{-3}(\nu_{0}),\quad\psi_{+1}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\hat{f}_{0}\psi_{-3}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}^{\ast}),

(3.5a)

\psi_{+2}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\check{f}_{0}\psi_{-1}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast}),\quad\psi_{+3}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0})=\breve{f}_{0}\psi_{-1}(\alpha^{2}\nu_{0}),

(3.5b)

\psi_{+3}(\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\acute{f}_{0}\psi_{-2}(\nu_{0}^{\ast}),\quad\psi_{+1}(\alpha\nu_{0})=\grave{f}_{0}\psi_{-2}(\alpha\nu_{0}),

(3.5c)

where
f_{0},\hat{f}_{0},\check{f}_{0},\breve{f}_{0},\acute{f}_{0}
, and
\grave{f}_{0}
are the associated
proportionality constants.

(ii) Suppose
\nu_{0}
is an
eigenvalue of the second type. In this case, we find that

\chi_{2}(\nu_{0})=\hat{c}_{0}\psi_{-3}(\nu_{0}),\quad\psi_{+2}(-\nu_{0}^{\ast})=\hat{d}_{0}\chi_{2}(-\nu_{0}^{\ast}),

(3.6a)

\chi_{1}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast})=c_{0}\psi_{-1}(\alpha\nu_{0}^{\ast}),\quad\psi_{+2}(-\alpha\nu_{0})=d_{0}\chi_{1}(-\alpha\nu_{0}),

(3.6b)

(3.6e)

(3.7f)

In order to simplify the calculation, we introduce the modified

m_{n}(x,t,\nu)=e^{-\alpha^{2}\nu x-\alpha\nu^{-1}t}\chi_{n}(\nu),\quad n=1,4,

(3.8a)

(3.8c)

Abstract
. Let λ \lambda the Barban–Vehov weights, defined in ( 1 ). Let X ≥ z 1 ≥ 100 X\geq z_{1}\geq 100 and z 2 = z 1 τ z_{2}=z_{1}^{\tau} for some τ > 1 \tau>1 . We prove that ∑ n ≤ X 1 n ​ ( ∑ d | n λ d ) 2 ≤ f ⁡ ( τ ) ​ log ⁡ X log ⁡ ( z 2 / z 1 ) , \sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\Bigr)^{2}\leq f(\tau)\frac{\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}, for a completely determined function f : ( 1 , ∞ ) → ℝ > 0 f:(1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0} . In particular, we may take f ⁡ ( 2 ) = 30 f(2)=30 , saving more than a factor of 5 5 on what was the best known result for τ = 2 \tau=2 . Two related estimates are also provided for general τ > 1 \tau>1 .



1.
Introduction and results

In
[
1
]
, M. Barban and P. Vehov introduced the weights

(1)

\lambda_{d}=\lambda_{d}(z_{2},z_{1})=\mu(d)\frac{\log^{+}(z_{2}/d)-\log^{+}(z_{1}/d)}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})},

defined for fixed
z_{2}>z_{1}>0
, where
\log^{+}=\max\{\log,0\}
. They discovered that the
L^{2}
-mean of
\sum_{d|n}\lambda_{d}
is
remarkably small, even when
n
is smaller than
z_{2}^{2}
.

The Barban–Vehov weights were further studied for instance by
S. Graham
[
2
]
, Y. Motohashi
[
5
,
\lx@sectionsign

1.3]
and M. Haye Betah
[
4
]
.

In particular, Y. Motohashi found that, in order to use such weights
to obtain log-free density estimates, it is enough to upper bound
their harmonic
L^{2}
-average and that, in fact, it is sufficient to
bound

\sum_{n\geq 1}\bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\bigr)^{2}/n^{1+\varepsilon}
,
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Here is our first result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
z_{1}\geq 100
. Consider
z_{2}=z_{1}^{\tau}
for some
\tau>1
. Then,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{1+\varepsilon}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\biggr)^{2}\leq\frac{e^{\gamma\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{A(\tau+1)+[B-\tfrac{C}{z_{1}^{\varepsilon}}+(B-\tfrac{C}{z_{2}^{\varepsilon}})\tau^{2}]\varepsilon\log z_{1}}{\tau-1},

where
A=1.084
,
B=1.301
,
C=0.318
.

The next is a result that enables one to compare Theorem
1.1
to previous work.

Corollary 1.2
.

When
X\geq z_{1}\geq 100
and
z_{2}=z_{1}^{\tau}
, for some
\tau>1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\biggr)^{2}\leq 3.09\frac{\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{1.084(\tau+1)+1.301(1+\tau^{2})-0.116}{\tau-1}.

In particular, when
\tau=2
, the right hand side bound reads
30\log X/\log z_{1}
.

The above estimate not only generalises the result of
[
4
]
,
but also saves more than a factor of
5
on the special case considered
therein, namely when
\tau=2
. Note that a more precise explicit study
of these weights when
X\geq z_{2}^{2}
has been the subject of

[
9
]
by the second author.

On the other hand, while studying zeros of the Riemann zeta function,
S. Graham also used the Barban–Vehov weights. Here is a corollary
that may be compared to
[
3
, Lemma 9]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X\geq z_{1}\geq 100
and
z_{2}=z_{1}^{\tau}
, for some
\tau>1
. Then, for
any
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha-1}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\biggr)^{2}\leq 3.09X^{2-2\alpha}\frac{\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{1.084(\tau+1)+1.301(1+\tau^{2})-0.116}{\tau-1}.

As the reader will see, the proofs of the above results are remarkably simple, a fact that is
reflected thanks to the very moderate constants that appear throughout our bounds. It is noteworthy that, giving our historical remarks, it has taken quite a while to reach the reasonable bounds we present in Theorem
1.1
, Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.

Notation.

We shall use throughout the notation
\sigma=1+\varepsilon
— we will use both notations interchangeably. Further, and in accordance with
earlier work on the subject, we use the family of functions

(2)

\check{m}_{q}(X,\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\log\biggl(\frac{X}{n}\biggr).

We shall also require the
Euler

\varphi_{s}
-function
defined, for any complex number
s
, by

\varphi_{s}(q)=q^{s}\prod_{p|q}\left(1-{1}/{p^{s}}\right)
. Finally, the variable
p
always corresponds to a prime number.

2.
Auxiliary results

The following result may be found in
[
6
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then
\zeta(1+\varepsilon)\leq e^{\gamma\varepsilon}/\varepsilon
.

On the other hand, we have

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
Y\geq 100
. Then

\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p}\log\biggl(\frac{Y}{p}\biggr)\geq 0.318\log^{2}Y.

The above sum is in fact asymptotic to
\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}Y
, converging very slowly.

Proof.

We verify the claimed inequality for
Y\in[100,10^{8}]
by using the
Pari-GP script
GP/MertensAux.gp
.

Suppose now that
Y\geq 10^{8}
. By summation by parts,
\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p}\log(\tfrac{Y}{p})
equals

\displaystyle\log Y\sum_{p\leq 100}\frac{\log p}{p}-\sum_{p\leq 100}\frac{\log^{2}(p)}{p}+\int_{100}^{Y}(\vartheta(t)-\vartheta(100))\frac{1+\log(Y/t)}{t^{2}}dt

(3)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 3.369\log Y-8.739+\int_{100}^{Y}(\vartheta(t)-\vartheta(100))\frac{1+\log(Y/t)}{t^{2}}dt,

where
\vartheta(t)=\sum_{p\leq t}\log p
is the Chebyshev function.
Now, by
[
8
, Thm. 6]
, we have that
\vartheta(t)>0.998697\ t
for any
t\geq 1155901
.
On the other hand, one can verify that
\vartheta(t)>0.835\ t
provided that
100\leq t\leq 1155901
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{100}^{Y}(\vartheta(t)-\vartheta(100))\frac{1+\log(Y/t)}{t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{100}^{Y}(0.835\ t-\vartheta(100))\frac{1+\log(Y/t)}{t^{2}}dt

(4)

\displaystyle\geq\log^{2}(Y)\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{0.845}{\log Y}-\frac{5.058}{\log^{2}Y}\right].

By combining (
3
) and (
4
), and then using that
Y\geq 10^{8}
, we arrive at

\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p}\log\biggl(\frac{Y}{p}\biggr)\geq\log^{2}(Y)\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{13.8}{\log^{2}(10^{8})}\right]\geq 0.376\log^{2}Y.

As
0.376>0.318
, we derive the inequality of the statement.

Finally, thanks to the prime number theorem,
\vartheta(t)\sim t
, so we indeed have that
\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p}\log(\frac{Y}{p})\sim\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}Y
.
∎

3.
Some lemmas involving
\check{m}_{q}(X,\sigma)

The following result is crucial for our improvement on
[
4
]
. It can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X>0
. When
k\geq 1
and
\sigma\geq 1
, we have

0\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\log^{k}\biggl(\frac{X}{n}\biggr)\leq 1.00303\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\bigl(k+(\sigma-1)\log X\bigr)\log^{k-1}X.

When
k\geq 2
, we may replace
1.00303
by 1.

We also have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Y\geq 100
. Then

U(Y)=\sum_{\delta\leq Y}\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\check{m}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{Y}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr)^{2}\leq\left(A+(\sigma-1)\left(B-\tfrac{C}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\log Y\right)\right)\log Y.

where
A=1.084
,
B=1.301
,
C=0.318
.

Proof.

Each
\check{m}_{\delta}(y/\delta;\sigma)
is non-negative, thanks to
Lemma
3.1
with
k=1
. Thus, we have the following estimation

(5)

U(Y)\leq 1.00303\sum_{\delta\leq Y}\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\frac{\delta}{\varphi(\delta)}\check{m}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{Y}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr)\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{\delta}\bigg)\biggr).

Let us define the auxiliary non-negative multiplicative function
f
on primes
p
by

f(p)=\frac{1-p^{-\sigma}}{1-1/p}>0
and on prime powers by

f(p^{k})=0
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
. We observe that

(6)

\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{\sigma}}\frac{\delta}{\varphi(\delta)}=\mu^{2}(\delta)\sum_{a|\delta}f(a).

On using identity (
6
) on estimation (
5
), and recalling definition (
2
)
, we derive

\displaystyle U(Y)

\displaystyle\leq 1.00303\sum_{a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\leq Y/a\\
(b,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{\sigma}}\check{m}_{ab}\Bigl(\frac{Y}{ab};\sigma\Bigr)\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{ab}\bigg)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq 1.00303\sum_{a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{a}\bigg)\biggr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq Y/a\\
(d,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d^{\sigma}}\log\biggl(\frac{Y/a}{d}\biggr)\sum_{bc=d}\mu(c)

\displaystyle\qquad-1.00303\varepsilon\sum_{a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq Y/a\\
(d,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{d^{\sigma}}\log\biggl(\frac{Y/a}{d}\biggr)\sum_{bc=d}\mu(c)\log b.

By Möbius inversion, the innermost sum of the above first term reduces to
d=1
. Moreover, as
\sum_{bc=d}\mu(c)\log(b)=\Lambda(d)
, where
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt function, the innermost sum of the above second term is supported on prime numbers. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{U(Y)}{1.00303}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{a}\bigg)\biggr)\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{a}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon\,\sum_{a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y/a\\
(p,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}}\log\bigg(\frac{Y/a}{p}\bigg).

In the above inequality, we consider the two terms coming from
a=1
. Then, by using the inequality
p^{-\sigma}\geq y^{-\varepsilon}p^{-1}
, valid whenever
p\leq y
, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{U(Y)}{1.00303}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon\log Y)\log Y+\sum_{2\leq a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{a}\bigg)\biggr)\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{a}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}}\log\bigg(\frac{Y}{p}\bigg)-\varepsilon\,\sum_{2\leq a\leq Y}\mu^{2}(a)\frac{f(a)}{a^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y/a\\
(p,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}}\log\bigg(\frac{Y/a}{p}\bigg).

Next, by trivially bounding the above last term and using Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{U(Y)}{1.00303}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon\log Y)\log Y-\frac{0.318\varepsilon}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\log^{2}Y

\displaystyle\quad+\biggl[-1+\prod_{p\geq 2}\biggl(1+\frac{p^{-\varepsilon}(1-p^{-\varepsilon})}{p(p-1)}\biggr)\biggr]\biggl(1+\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{Y}{2}\right)\biggr)\log\left(\frac{Y}{2}\right).

Furthermore, by using that
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mapsto t^{-1}(1-t^{-1})
has a maximum
1/4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle-1+\prod_{p\geq 2}\biggl(1+\frac{p^{-\varepsilon}(1-p^{-\varepsilon})}{p(p-1)}\biggr)\leq-1+\biggl(1+\frac{2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon})}{2}\biggr)\prod_{p\geq 3}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{4p(p-1)}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-1+\biggl(1+\frac{2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon})}{2}\biggr)1.08\leq 0.08+0.54\cdot 2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon}),

so that

\displaystyle\bigl(0.08

\displaystyle+0.54\cdot 2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon})\bigr)(1+\varepsilon\log Y)

\displaystyle\leq 0.08+\varepsilon\biggl(0.08+0.54\cdot 2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon})+0.54\frac{2^{-\varepsilon}(1-2^{-\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon\log Y}\biggr)\log Y

\displaystyle\leq 0.08+0.297\,\varepsilon\log Y,

where we have used that
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mapsto(1-2^{-t})/t
is decreasing and has as maximum
\log 2
at
t=0
.
We conclude the result by observing that

\displaystyle\frac{U(Y)}{1.00303}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon\log Y)\log Y-\frac{0.318\varepsilon}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\log^{2}Y+\biggl(0.08+0.297\varepsilon\log Y\biggr)\log\left(\frac{Y}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1.08\log Y+\varepsilon\log^{2}Y\bigg(1.297-\tfrac{0.318}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\bigg).

∎

4.
Main result

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall definition (
1
). Let us define

(7)

\Sigma(\sigma)=\Sigma(\sigma,z_{2},z_{1})=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\Bigr)^{2}.

By expanding the square, we observe that

(8)

\Sigma(\sigma)=\sum_{d,e}\lambda_{d}\lambda_{e}\sum_{[d,e]|n}\frac{1}{n^{\sigma}}=\zeta(\sigma)\sum_{d,e}\frac{\lambda_{d}\lambda_{e}}{[d,e]^{\sigma}},

where the variables
d
and
e
may be restricted to
d,e\leq z_{2}
.

Let us use now the Selberg diagonalisation process. For any pair of square-free numbers
d,e
, we have

(9)

\frac{d^{\sigma}e^{\sigma}}{[d,e]^{\sigma}}=(d,e)^{\sigma}=\sum_{\delta|(d,e)}\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta).

As the support of
\lambda
is contained in the set of square-free
numbers, we may derive from (
9
) the following expression

(10)

\sum_{d,e}\frac{\lambda_{d}\lambda_{e}}{[d,e]^{\sigma}}=\sum_{\delta}\frac{\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\\
(\ell,\delta)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\delta\ell}}{\ell^{\sigma}}\biggr)^{2}.

Thus, by definition (
1
) and equation (
8
), we
obtain

(11)

\Sigma(\sigma)\leq\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\log^{2}(z_{2}/z_{1})}\sum_{\delta\leq z_{2}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\biggl[\check{m}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{z_{2}}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr)-\check{m}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{z_{1}}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr)\biggr]^{2},

where we have used the definition (
2
).
Furthermore, as
\check{m}_{q}(X;\sigma)\geq 0
, thanks to Lemma
3.1
, we find that

\bigg[\check{m}_{\delta}\bigg(\frac{z_{2}}{\delta};\sigma\bigg)-\check{m}_{\delta}\bigg(\frac{z_{1}}{\delta};\sigma\bigg)\bigg]^{2}\leq\check{m}^{2}_{\delta}\bigg(\frac{z_{2}}{\delta};\sigma\bigg)+\check{m}^{2}_{\delta}\bigg(\frac{z_{1}}{\delta};\sigma\bigg)

with equality if and only if

\check{m}_{\delta}({z_{2}}/{\delta};\sigma)\check{m}_{\delta}({z_{1}}/{\delta};\sigma)=0
.
Therefore, we see from (
11
) that

\frac{\log^{2}(z_{2}/z_{1})}{\zeta(\sigma)}\Sigma(\sigma)\leq\sum_{\delta\leq z_{2}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\check{m}^{2}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{z_{2}}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr)+\sum_{\delta\leq z_{1}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\delta)\varphi_{\sigma}(\delta)}{\delta^{2\sigma}}\check{m}^{2}_{\delta}\Bigl(\frac{z_{1}}{\delta};\sigma\Bigr),

so that

\Sigma(\sigma)\leq\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\log^{2}(z_{2}/z_{1})}(U(z_{2})+U(z_{1}))=\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{U(z_{2})+U(z_{1})}{(\tau-1)\log z_{1}}.

Theorem
1.1
follows by applying Lemma
3.2
twice, recalling that
z_{2}=z_{1}^{\tau}
and using
then Lemma
2.1
.

\square

With the help of (
10
), it is worth noticing that, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any weight
\lambda
, the sum
\sum_{d,e}\frac{\lambda_{d}\lambda_{e}}{[d,e]^{\sigma}}
is always non-negative.

5.
Corollaries

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

By using Rankin’s trick, we have

\Sigma(0)=\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\biggr)^{2}\leq\sum_{n}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{n^{1+\varepsilon}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\Bigr)^{2}

for any parameter
\varepsilon
that we shall soon choose.
Now, by using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the bound

(12)

\Sigma(0)\leq\frac{X^{\varepsilon}e^{\gamma\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{A(\tau+1)+[B-\tfrac{C}{z_{1}^{\varepsilon}}+(B-\tfrac{C}{z_{2}^{\varepsilon}})\tau^{2}]\varepsilon\log z_{1}}{\tau-1},

where
A=1.084
,
B=1.301
,
C=0.318
.

Set
\varepsilon=1/\log X
. Since there is no further assumptions on
z_{2}
, we use the bound
-Cz_{2}^{-\varepsilon}\leq 0
. Thus, by writing
u=\varepsilon\log z_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma(0)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{1+\gamma/\log X}\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{A(\tau+1)+[B(1+\tau^{2})-\tfrac{C}{e^{u}}]u}{\tau-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{1+\gamma/\log 100}\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\frac{A(\tau+1)+B(1+\tau^{2})-\tfrac{C}{e}}{\tau-1}

where we have used that
X\geq 100
and
0<u\leq 1
.

In the specific and common situation
when
\tau=2
, unlike the general case, we may take advantage of the factor
Cz_{2}^{-\varepsilon}=Ce^{-2u}
appearing in (
12
). Hence, by using again that
X\geq 100
and
0<u\leq 1
, we finally obtain

\Sigma(0,z_{1}^{2},z_{1})\leq e^{1+\gamma/\log 100}[A(\tau+1)+B(1+\tau^{2})-\tfrac{C}{e}-\tfrac{C\tau^{2}}{e}]\frac{\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}\leq\frac{29.18\log X}{\log(z_{2}/z_{1})}.

\square

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

We simply write

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha-1}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d})^{2}}{n}n^{2-2\alpha}\leq X^{2-2\alpha}\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{1}{n}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda_{d}\bigg)^{2},

since
2-2\alpha\geq 0
. Then the result follows by using Corollary
1.2
.

\square

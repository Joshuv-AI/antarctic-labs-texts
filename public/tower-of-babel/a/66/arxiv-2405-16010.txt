The Torsion Ideal Lattice of the Endomorphism Ring of an Abelian p p –Group
Phill Schultz

Abstract
. The lattice of ideals of the torsion ideal of the endomorphism ring of an abelian p p –group is classified by a system of cardinal invariants.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20K10, 20K30

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to classify the lattice of ideals of the torsion ideal of the endomorphism ring of a reduced abelian
p
–group. Let
G
be an unbounded reduced abelian
p
–group and
\mathbf{t}
its ring of endomorphisms of finite order. I show in Theorem
6.6
that the lattice of ideals of
\mathbf{t}
is isomorphic to the lattice under the pointwise ordering of pairs
(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{m})
where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a
G
–admissible indicator with finite entries and
\mathbf{m}
is a decreasing sequence of infinite cardinals.
This result generalises the classification of the lattice of ideals of the endomorphism ring of a bounded abelian
p
–group described in
[
AvS, 2000
]
and
[
ASZ, 2020
]
.

After recalling basic properties of
p
–groups in §2,
in §3 I describe the known properties of the endomorphism ring of
G
and its torsion ideal
\mathbf{t}
. In §4 I construct a Galois Correspondence between the
\mathbf{t}
–invariant subgroups of
G
and the ideals of
\mathbf{t}
. In §5 I define the lattice of ranked admissible indicators of
\mathbf{t}
. Finally in §6 I use ranked admissible indicators to describe the lattice of ideals of
\mathbf{t}
.

2.3.
Indicators

The following notation and properties of indicators are found in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10, §1]
:

(1)

An
indicator

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i}\colon i\in{\mathbb{N}})
is a strictly increasing finite sequence of ordinals
\sigma_{i},\ i\leqslant n
, followed by a countably infinite sequence of symbols
\infty
, or a strictly increasing sequence of ordinals.
In the former case, we abbreviate the indicator
(\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n},\infty,\dots,\infty)
by
(\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n},\infty)
.
The set of indicators is denoted
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
.

(2)

An indicator
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
has
length
n
in the former case and
infinite length
in the latter case. An indicator
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
has
finite entries
if each ordinal entry
\sigma_{i}
is finite. The
zero indicator
, denoted
(\infty)
, has length 0 and is considered to have finite entries.

(3)

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
is ordered pointwise:
\boldsymbol{\sigma}

precedes

\boldsymbol{\tau}
, denoted
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\preceq\boldsymbol{\tau}
if and only if for all
i<\omega,\ \sigma_{i}\leqslant\tau_{i}
, where we regard an ordinal
\sigma_{i}<\infty
and
\infty\leqslant\infty
.

(4)

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
is a well–ordered set with minimum
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{min}=(i\colon i<\omega)
and maximum
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{max}=(\infty)
.
If
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
then
\bigwedge\mathcal{S}=(\sigma_{i})
where
\sigma_{i}=\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{S}}\tau_{i}
and
\bigvee\mathcal{S}=(\rho_{i})
where
\rho_{i}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{S}}\tau_{i}
, so
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
is a complete lattice.

(5)

The set of indicators with finite entries is denoted
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}
.

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}
is a sublattice of
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
, closed under arbitrary meets and finite joins.

(6)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d

has a gap at
i
if
\sigma_{i}+1<\sigma_{i+1}<\infty
.

Indicators are used to define certain invariants of groups. Let
G
be a group and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
.
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is
G
–admissible
if

(1)

For all
i<\omega
, if
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
has a gap at
i
and
\sigma_{i}=\kappa
, then the Ulm invariant
u_{\kappa}\neq 0
;

(2)

If
\exp(G)=e
then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
has length
e
.

For example, for any group
G
,
(i\colon i\in[0,n],\infty),\,(i\colon i<\omega)
and
(\infty)
are
G
–admissible.

Let
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
be the set of
G
–admissible indicators and
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)=\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)\cap\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}
. The following properties of
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
are either proved in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10]
or are straightforward consequences of the definitions:

(1)

If
G
is
\Sigma
–cyclic, then every
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
has finite entries. Conversely, if
G
has basic subgroup
B
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)
, then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(B)
.

(2)

Let
a\in G
with
\exp(a)=n+1
. Let
\sigma_{i}=0pt(p^{i}a)
. The
indicator of
a
is the
G
–admissible indicator
\operatorname{ind}(a)=(\sigma_{i}\colon i\in[0,n],\infty)
.
Conversely, if
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
is finite of length

, then there exists

(3)

For all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)

.

.

(4)

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\ \boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
. Then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\preceq\boldsymbol{\tau}
if and only if

.

(5)

Let
a\in G
and
f\in\mathcal{E}
. Then
\operatorname{ind}(a)\preceq\operatorname{ind}(af)
.

G
is

such that

.
Simply presented and separable groups are fully transitive.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and let
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\ \boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)

(1)

For all
i<\omega,\ \sigma_{i}=\min\{0pt(p^{i}a)\colon a\in G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\}
.

(2)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\tau}
if and only if
G(\boldsymbol{\tau})=G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
.

(3)

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
is a lattice anti–isomorphic to
G(\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d)
;

(4)

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)
is a sublattice anti–isomorphic to
G(\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega})
.

Proof.

(1) Let
a\in G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. Then for all
i\leqslant\exp(a),\ \sigma_{i}\leqslant 0pt(p^{i}a)
. Conversely, if for all
i,\ \sigma_{i}\leqslant 0pt(p^{i}a)
then
\sigma\preceq\operatorname{ind}(a)
so
a\in G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. Hence
for all
i<\omega,\ \min_{a\in G}\{0pt(p^{i}a)\}=\sigma_{i}
.

(2) Necessity is obvious. Suppose
G(\boldsymbol{\tau})=G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. Then by (1), for all
i,\tau_{i}=\min\{0pt(p^{i}a)\colon a\in G(\boldsymbol{\tau})\}=\min\{0pt(p^{i}a)\colon a\in G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\}=\sigma_{i}
.

(3) It is straightforward to verify that if
S
is a finite set of
G
–admissible indicators, then
\bigvee S
and
\bigwedge S
are
G
–admissible, and
G(\bigvee S)
and
G(\bigwedge S)
are fully invariant in
G
. The map
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)\to G(\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d)
defined by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mapsto G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
reverses order, and by (2) it is a bijection. Hence the map is a a lattice anti–tsomorphism.

(4) The anti–isomorphism (3) maps
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)
to
G(\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega})

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group. The mapping
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mapsto G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})

is a lattice anti–isomorphism of
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
to

, mapping finite length indicators to bounded groups and

∎

.

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i})\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
and for each

let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(n)}
be the truncation

(1)

is

(2)

.

Proof.

(1)
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
has a gap at

if and only if whenever

.

(2) For all

. Conversely, if for all

Corollary 2.4
.

If
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G
be unbounded with basic subgroup
B
.

(1)

G/p^{\omega}G
has a basic subgroup isomorphic to
B
;

(2)

\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)=\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G/p^{\omega}G)=\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(B)
.

Proof.

(1) For all

. Hence for all

. Thus

contains for all

so

has

has a basic subgroup isomorphic to

.

(2)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a

2.4.
Separable Groups

A group of length
\leqslant\omega
, i.e., either bounded or unbounded with no elements of infinite height, is called
separable
.
Let
G
be a separable group with basic subgroup
B=B(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})=\bigoplus_{i<\omega}B_{n_{i}}
. It is shown in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10, §3]
that
if
G
is unbounded, the
p
–adic completion of
B
is a subgroup of
\prod_{i<\omega}B_{n_{i}}
whose torsion subgroup
\overline{B}
is a
p
–group consisting of all bounded sequences in
\prod_{i<\omega}B_{n_{i}}
.
\overline{B}
is called the
torsion completion of
B
. If
|B|=\mu
, then
|\overline{B}|=2^{\mu}
and
\overline{B}/B
is divisible of rank
2^{\mu}
.

Throughout this section, we represent elements of
\overline{B}
as bounded sequences
\mathbf{b}=(b_{i}\colon i\in{\mathbb{N}})\in\prod_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{n_{i}}
where
b_{i}\in B_{n_{i}}
.

Fuchs
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10]
shows that the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

G
is separable;

(2)

every finite subset of
G
is contained in a finite direct summand;

(3)

B\leqslant G\leqslant\overline{B}
,
G
is pure in
\overline{B}
and
\overline{B}/G
is divisible.

Consequently, a separable
G
with basic subgroup
B
is simply presented if and only if
G=B
.

On the other hand, separable groups are fully transitive (
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter b10, Corollary 1.5]
).

Consequently, by Kaplansky’s Theorem,
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10, Theorem 2.2]
, every fully invariant subgroup
H=G(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
for some
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)=\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d^{<\omega}(G)
.

Countable separable groups are
\Sigma
–cyclic, but uncountable separable groups are notoriously complicated; as Fuchs says,
[
Fuchs, 2015
, page 316]
, no general structure theorem is available, and none is expected.
In particular, Corner
[
Corner, 1969
]
shows that even in the simplest case when each
B_{n}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{n})
there exists a family of
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
non–isomorphic pure subgroups
G
of
\overline{B}
containing
B
.

This result holds for any
|B|\leqslant 2^{\aleph_{0}}
; for larger
|B|
, Shelah has shown,
[
Fuchs, 2015
, p. 332]
, that for every infinite cardinal
\kappa
there are
2^{\kappa}
non–isomorphic separable groups of cardinal
\kappa
with the same basic subgroup.

Lemma 2.6
.

For every group
G
,

G/p^{\omega}G
is separable.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, let
0\neq a\in G\setminus p^{\omega}G
such that the height of
a+p^{\omega}G
in
G/p^{\omega}G
is infinite.
Then for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exist
b_{n}
and
c_{n}
in
G
such that
a-p^{n}b=c_{n}\in p^{\omega}G
. Hence there exist
d_{n}\in G
such that
p^{n}d_{n}=c_{n}
, so for all
n,\ a=p^{n}(b_{n}+d_{n})\in p^{n}G
, so
a\in p^{\omega}G
, a contradiction.
∎

3.
The endomorphism ring of
G

Our current knowledge of the structure of the endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
of an abelian
p
–group
G
is presented in
[
Krylov, Mikhalev and Tuganbaev, 2003
, Chapter 4, §20]
which mainly draws on the results presented in
[
Fuchs, 2015
]
and
[
Pierce, 1963
]
.
For the particular case of separable
G
, see
[
Corner, 1969
]
and
[
Stringall, 1967
]
.
These references are mainly concerned with identifying the Jacobson radical
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathcal{E}
and the factor ring
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{J}
. The main structure theorem states that if
G
has non–zero Ulm invariants
(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa<\lambda)
then
\mathcal{E}
is the split extension of the Jacobson radical
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathcal{E}
by a subring of the direct product
\prod_{i<\lambda}\mathcal{L}(u_{i})
where
\mathcal{L}(u_{i})
is the ring of linear transformations of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–space of dimension
u_{i}
.
\mathcal{J}
is charatacterised as the
p
–adic closure of the ideal of endomorphisms which raise heights in the socle, or alternatively as the ideal of endomorphisms
f
for which the restriction of
1-f
to the socle
G[p]
is monic and epic.

The article
[
AvS, 2000
]
contains an explicit description, in terms of cardinal invariants, of the ideal lattice of
\mathcal{E}
in the bounded case. Let
G=\bigoplus_{i\in[s]}B_{i}
where
B_{i}
is homocyclic of exponent
n_{i}
and rank
m_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is represented as the matrix ring
(\mathcal{E}_{ij})
where
\mathcal{E}_{ij}=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(B_{i},B_{j})
. The main theorem
[
AvS, 2000
, Theorem 4.5]
shows that an ideal
I
of
\mathcal{E}
is a matrix ring
(I_{ij})
where
I_{ij}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{E}_{ij}

of the form
I_{ij}=\mathcal{E}_{i,j}(\mathbf{k}_{ij},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{ij})=\sum_{u\in[0.t]}p^{k_{ij}(u)}\mathcal{E}_{ij}^{\mu_{ij}(u)}
. Here
\mathcal{E}_{ij}^{\mu_{ij}(u)}=\{f\in\mathcal{E}_{ij}\colon\operatorname{rank}(f)\leqslant\mu_{ij}(u)\},\ \mathbf{k}_{ij}=(k_{ij}(0),\dots,k_{ij}(t_{ij}))
is an increasing sequence of positive integers and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{ij}=(\mu_{ij}(0),\dots,\mu_{ij}(t_{ij}))
a non–increasing sequence of cardinals. The sequences
\mathbf{k}_{ij}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{ij}
satisfy a system of inequalitites determined by the parameters
n_{i}
and
m_{i}
of the subgroups
B_{i}
.

Thus the ideals of
\mathcal{E}
are characterised by an
s\times s
matrix whose terms are homomorphism groups parametrised by three variables: height, exponent and rank.
This result is extended in
[
AbS, 2004
]
to separable reduced
p
–groups by using row convergent
\omega\times\omega
matrices
(I_{ij})
with similar parameters.

The aim of the rest of this paper is to classify the ideals of the torsion ideal
\mathbf{t}
of
\mathcal{E}
for arbitrary unbounded
G
. Instead of representing
\mathbf{t}
by matrices
(\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(B_{i},B_{j})
and its ideals by matrices
(I_{ij})
as in
[
AvS, 2000
]
and
[
AbS, 2004
]
, we characterise ideals of
\mathbf{t}
by indicators and ranks. When applied to bounded groups, this is a new, co–ordinate free version of the results of
[
AvS, 2000
]
and
[
AbS, 2004
]
.

4.
The torsion ideal of
\mathcal{E}

Let
G
be an unbounded group with endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
. It is well known (
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 1, Lemma 8.3]
) that
G
is a
p
–adic module under the natural action of the
p
–adic integers and
\mathcal{E}
is its
p
–adic endomorphism ring. Let
\mathbf{t}

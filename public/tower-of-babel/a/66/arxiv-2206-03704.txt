Quasi-forest simplicial complexes and almost Cohen-Macaulay
Chwas Ahmed, Amir Mafi* and Mohammed Rafiq Namiq

Abstract
. In this paper we study the quasi-forest simplicial complexes and we define the concept of simplicial k k -cycle (denoted by 𝒮 k \s_{k} ) and simplicial k k -point (denoted by 𝒫 k \p_{k} ). We show that a simplicial complex Δ \Delta is quasi-forest if and only if it does not have any 𝒫 k \p_{k} and any 𝒮 k \s_{k} for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 . Furthermore we characterize almost Cohen-Macaulay quasi-forest simplicial complexes. In the end we show that the cycle graph G = C n G=C_{n} is almost Cohen-Macaulay if and only if n = 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 11 n=3,4,5,6,7,8,9,11 .

1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and known results which are used in this paper.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
V=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a collection of subsets of
V
such that (i)
\{x_{i}\}\in\Delta
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, and (ii) if

and

, then

. Each element

of
\Delta
is called a face of
\Delta
and it is called an
i

|F|=i+1
. The dimension of a face

is
|F|-1
and the dimension of
\Delta
is defined to be
\dim\triangle=d-1
, where
d=\max\{|F|\mid F\in\Delta\}
. A facet of
\Delta
is maximal face (with respect to inclusion). The set
\mathcal{F}(\Delta):=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}\}
is the set of all facets of
\Delta
. A simplicial complex
\Delta
with the facets
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
is denoted by
\Delta=\left\langle F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\right\rangle
. The Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is
I_{\Delta}:=\left\langle\prod_{x_{i}\in F}x_{i}\mid F\notin\Delta\right\rangle
and the quotient ring
K[\Delta]=R/I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
over a field
K
where
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. A simplicial complex
\Delta
is pure if every facet has the same cardinality. A simplicial complex
\overline{\Delta}=\left\langle\overline{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\right\rangle
is the complement of
\Delta
. The Alexander dual of
\Delta
, denoted by
\Delta^{\vee}
, is defined as
\Delta^{\vee}=\{V\setminus F\mid F\not\in\Delta\}
. The subcomplex
\Delta(i)=\{F\in\Delta\mid\dim F=i\}
is called the pure
i
-skeleton of
\Delta
.
Terai
[
22
]
proved that if
I
is a square-free monomial ideal of

\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta^{\vee}})=\operatorname{proj\,dim}(R/I_{\Delta})
.

Herzog, Hibi and Zheng

Proposition 1.1

A simplicial complex

Now, since

Recall that a simplicial complex is called flag, if all minimal nonfaces consist of two elements, equivalently,
I_{\Delta}
is generated by quadratic monomials. By
[

, a quasi-forest simplicial complex is flag. A monomial ideal

proved that the edge ideal

is chordal. Recall that a graph

is called chordal if each cycle of length

As before the independent simplicial complex of a graph

is the clique complex of

and vice versa. One can rephrase

Theorem 1.2

Let

A Ferrers graph is a bipartite graph on two distinct vertex sets

The equality holds if

[
1
, P. 153]
). In particular, if
\Delta
is a quasi-forest, then the equality holds.

Proposition 3.1
.

(Compare with
[
10
, Proposition 2.3]
)
Let
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial quasi-forest with
h(\Delta)=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
. Then
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
h_{2},\dots,h_{d}
are zero.

Proof.

Since
\Delta
is a quasi-forest, we have

\deg h_{K[\Delta]}(t)=\dim(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])+1.

If
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\deg h_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta]}(t)=1
. Therefore
h_{2},\dots,h_{d}
are zero.
Conversely, let
h_{2},\dots,h_{d}
be zero. Then

1=\deg h_{K[\Delta]}(t)=\dim(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])+1.

Thus
\dim(K[\Delta])=\dep(K[\Delta])
and so
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial quasi-forest with
h(\Delta)=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
. Then
\Delta
is almost Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
h_{2}
is non-positive, and
h_{3},\dots,h_{d}
are zero.

Proof.

Since
\Delta
is a quasi-forest, we have

\deg h_{K[\Delta]}(t)=\dim(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])+1.

If
\Delta
is almost Cohen-Macaulay, then
\deg h_{K[\Delta]}(t)\leq 2
. Therefore

h_{3},\dots,h_{d}
are zero. By
[
26
, Theorem 6.7.6]

h_{0}=1
and
h_{1}=f_{0}-d=n-d
which is not negative. Now, we consider the following cases:

(i)

: let
h_{1}=0
. Then
f_{0}=d
and so
h_{2}
is zero;

(ii)

: let
h_{1}
be positive. By Theorem
1.4
, we have
f_{d-1}\leq n-d+1
. Since
f_{d-1}=h_{0}+h_{1}+\dots+h_{d}
, it follows that
h_{0}+h_{1}+\dots+h_{d}\leq n-d+1
. Thus
h_{2}+\dots+h_{d}\leq 0
. It therefore follows that
h_{2}\leq 0
.

Conversely, suppose that
h_{2}\leq 0
and
h_{3}=\dots=h_{d}=0
.
Since
\dim(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])+1=\deg h_{K[\Delta]}(t)\leq 2,

it follows that
\dim(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])\leq 1
.
Hence
\Delta
is almost Cohen-Macaulay, as required.
∎

As before if
K[\Delta]
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
a(K[\Delta])=\operatorname{reg}(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])
. It is natural to ask whether if
K[\Delta]
is almost Cohen-Macaulay, then
a(K[\Delta])=\operatorname{reg}(K[\Delta])-\dep(K[\Delta])-1
is it true in general.
In the following we give a counter example for this question.

Example 3.3
.

Let
n=5
and
I=(x_{4}x_{5},x_{1}x_{3}x_{5},x_{1}x_{2}x_{5},x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})
be an ideal of
R
. Then by using Macaulay 2 we have
\dim(R/I)=3
and
\dep(R/I)=2
and so
I
is almost Cohen-Macaulay. On the other hand,
\operatorname{reg}(R/I)=3
and
H_{R/I}(t)=\frac{1+2t+2t^{2}}{(1-t)^{3}}
. Thus
a(R/I)=-1
and
\operatorname{reg}(R/I)-\dep(R/I)-1\neq a(R/I)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

(Compare with
[
5
, Corollary 2.8]
) Let
G
be a Ferrers graph on two distinct vertex sets
V_{1}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
V_{2}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
and
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal in the ring
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Then
I
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if the Hilbert series
H_{S/I}(t)=\frac{1+nt}{(1-t)^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
I
be Cohen-Macaulay. Then by Proposition
3.1

h_{2},\dots,h_{d}
are zero and
d=\dim R/I=n
. Since
h_{1}=f_{0}-d=2n-n=n
, it follows that

H_{S/I}(t)=\frac{1+nt}{(1-t)^{n}}
. Conversely, suppose
H_{S/I}(t)=\frac{1+nt}{(1-t)^{n}}
. Since
I
has a linear resolution, we have

\deg h_{S/I}(t)-\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=\dim(S/I)-\dep(S/I)
and
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=1
. By hypothesis
\deg h_{S/I}(t)=1
and it therefore follows
\dim(S/I)=\dep(S/I)

Thus
I
is Cohen-Macaulay, as required.
∎

The following result proved by Cimpoeas in
[
4
, Proposition 1.3]
:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G=C_{n}
be a cycle graph and
I=I(G)
. Then
\operatorname{depth}R/I=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G=C_{n}
be a cycle graph and
I=I(G)
. Then
I
is almost Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
n=3,4,5,6,7,8,9,11
.

Proof.

(\Longleftarrow)
. By using Macaulay 2 one can easily obtain the result.

(\Longrightarrow)
. Let
J=I(P_{n})
, where
P_{n}
is a path of length
n-1
with
n-1
edges
x_{i}x_{i+1}
such that
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Now by induction on
n
, we prove that
\dim(R/J)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. If
n=3
, then by using Macaulay 2 there is nothing to prove. Suppose
n\geq 4
and the result has been proved for smaller values of
n
. Consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R/(J:x_{n})\overset{x_{n}}{\longrightarrow}R/J\longrightarrow R/(J,x_{n})\longrightarrow 0.\ \ (\ast)

Since
(J:x_{n})=(I(P_{n-2}),x_{n-1})
, it follows that
\dim R/(J:x_{n})=\dim R^{{}^{\prime}}[x_{n}]/I(P_{n-2})\\
=\dim R^{{}^{\prime}}/I(P_{n-2})+1
, where
R^{{}^{\prime}}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2}]
. Now, by induction hypothesis, we obtain
\dim R/(J:x_{n})=\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor+1=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
Similarly,
(J,x_{n})=(I(P_{n-1}),x_{n})
and again by using induction hypothesis
\dim R/(J,x_{n})=\dim K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]/(I(P_{n-1}))=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
.
Since
\dim R/J=\max\{\dim R/(J:x_{n}),\dim R/(J,x_{n})\}
, it follows that
\dim R/J=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Since
(I:x_{n})=(I(P_{n-3}),x_{n-1},x_{1})
, similarly by using
I
in stead of
J
in the exact sequence
(\ast)
, we get
\dim R/I=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. Also, by Proposition
3.5
we have

\operatorname{depth}R/I=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
. Hence by comparing
\operatorname{depth}R/I
and
\dim R/I
we conclude that
n=3,4,5,6,7,8,9,11
.
∎

A canonical realization of the alt ν \nu -associahedron Thanks: The author was supported by the Austrian Science Fund FWF, grants P 33278 and I 5788.
Cesar Ceballos

Abstract
. Given a lattice path ν \nu , the alt ν \nu -Tamari lattice is a partial order recently introduced by Ceballos and Chenevière, which generalizes the ν \nu -Tamari lattice and the ν \nu -Dyck lattice. All these posets are defined on the set of lattice paths that lie weakly above ν \nu , and posses a rich combinatorial structure. In this paper, we study the geometric structure of these posets. We show that their Hasse diagram is the edge graph of a polytopal complex induced by a tropical hyperplane arrangement, which we call the alt ν \nu -associahedron. This generalizes the realization of ν \nu -associahedra by Ceballos, Padrol and Sarmiento. Our approach leads to an elegant construction, in terms of areas below lattice paths, which we call the canonical realization. Surprisingly, in the case of the classical associahedron, our canonical realization magically recovers Loday’s ubiquitous realization, via a simple affine transformation.

Introduction

The
n

[
21
]
.
Its vertices correspond to plane binary trees with
n+1
internal nodes, and two trees are connected by an edge if they are related by a tree rotation.
The associahedron appeared for the first time in Dov Tamari’s doctoral thesis in 1951, where illustrations of the associahedron up to dimension 3 are shown.
However, general geometric realizations of the associahedron in higher dimensions were discovered only until the mid and late 1980’s, via explicit constructions by Mark Haiman
[
15
]
and Carl Lee
[
19
]
.
Since then, many different constructions methods emerged. We refer to
[
9

for more on the history of some constructions.

One of the most ubiquitous constructions of the associahedron is the one commonly known as the Loday’s associahedron
[
20
]
.
The history of this construction goes back to Steven Shnider and Shlomo Sternberg
[

Then, Jean-Louis Loday

In this work, we unexpectedly discovered a new appearance of Loday’s associahedron (via a simple affine transformation) in a much wider generality. Before going into details about the general framework, we would like to explain our construction in the special case of the classical associahedron to highlight the beauty of both constructions and their relation.

Our next goal is to describe another elegant way to provide coordinates to plane binary trees, providing another beautiful realization of the associahedron.

\nu

]

Motivated by all these connections,

A further generalization of the

2.1.
The alt

Let
\nu

Throughout the paper, we assume that this path ends at the coordinate

Sometimes, we represent a lattice path

For instance, the path

A

\nu

Alternatively,

3.
The

-Tamari complex

The description of alt

-Tamari lattices in terms on

Definition 3.1
.

Let

-compatible subsets of

Lemma 3.2

The

-Tamari complex

is a pure simplicial complex of dimension

, where

and

Proof.

Assume that
U
fits in an

rectangle. By Remark
2.8
, the set

corresponds to a pair
(\delta,\nu)
where
\delta
is an increment vector with respect to a lattice path
\nu
that starts at
(0,0)
and ends at
(a,b)
.
By Proposition

,
U
-trees correspond to
\nu
-paths under the right flushing bijection. Since every

is pure of dimension

.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(
[

, Lemma 12]
)
.

Two

-trees differ by a single element (i.e. the two facets intersect in a codimension 1 face) if and only if they are related by a rotation.

Proof.

This statement was proven in
[

, Lemma 12]
in the case where

is left aligned (which corresponds the classical
\nu
-Tamari case). Assume

is not left aligned. Let
\check{U}\supseteq U
be the smallest left aligned set containing

in the

U
-trees are exactly
\check{U}
-trees
that are contained in

. Since the result holds for

-trees, it holds for

-trees.
∎

The
boundary
of
\mathcal{TC}({U})
is the simplicial complex induced by codimension 1 faces that are contained in exactly one facet. The faces that are not contained in the boundary are called

.

The following result characterizes the interior faces of the
U
-Tamari complex.

Proposition 3.4

(Cf.
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

The interior faces of
\mathcal{TC}({U})
are subsets of pairwise
U
-compatible elements in
U
that contain the top left corner in
U
(the root) and at least one element per row and column.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{TC}({U})
be a facet (a
U
-tree). We say that an element

flippable if there is another element

such that

is also a facet.

By Lemma

, flips correspond to tree
U
-rotations.
In other words,

is flippable if and only if

or

, for some

different than

.

We claim that

is not flippable if and only if

is the root of

or

is the unique element of

in its row or column.
The backward direction follows from the previous paragraph.
The forward direction can be shown by a case analysis.

The codimension 1 boundary faces of
\mathcal{TC}({U})
are obtained by removing a non-flippable element from a facet. Therefore,

is an interior face if and only if it contains the root and at least one element per row and column.
∎

Definition 5.1
.

Let

\mathcal{H}^{h}
be the arrangement of inverted tropical hyperplanes described in Equation (

is the polyhedral complex of bounded cells induced by

For an increment vector
\delta

as

By Proposition

, recall that

-Tamari complex

are subsets of pairwise

The following is an alternative definition of the

Theorem 5.2

(Cf.
[

, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

For a non-crossing height function

-associahedron

-covering faces.

(2)

U
-trees.

(3)

On isosupremic vectorial minimisation problems in L ∞ L^{\infty} with general nonlinear constraints Thanks: E.C. has been financially supported through the UK EPSRC scholarship GS19-055
Ed Clark, Nikos Katzourakis

Abstract
. We study minimisation problems in L ∞ L^{\infty} for general quasiconvex first order functionals, where the class of admissible mappings is constrained by the sublevel sets of another supremal functional and by the zero set of a nonlinear operator. Examples of admissible operators include those expressing pointwise, unilateral, integral isoperimetric, elliptic quasilinear differential, jacobian and null Lagrangian constraints. Via the method of L p L^{p} approximations as p → ∞ p\to\infty , we illustrate the existence of a special L ∞ L^{\infty} minimiser which solves a divergence PDE system involving certain auxiliary measures as coefficients. This system can be seen as a divergence form counterpart of the Aronsson PDE system which is associated with the constrained L ∞ L^{\infty} variational problem.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
and let also
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Consider two functions

(1.1)

f,\,g\ :\ \ \Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

which will be assumed to satisfy certain natural structural assumptions. Additionally, let
\bar{p}>n
be fixed and consider a given nonlinear operator

(1.2)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)\longrightarrow\textbf{E},\end{split}

where
(\textbf{E},\|\cdot\|)
is an arbitrary Banach space. In this paper we are interested in the following variational problem: given
G\geq 0
and the supremal functionals

\mathrm{F}_{\infty},\mathrm{G}_{\infty}\ :\ \ W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

defined by

(1.3)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u):&=\,\underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,f(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u),\phantom{\Big|}\\
\mathrm{G}_{\infty}(u):&=\,\underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,g(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u),\end{split}\right.\ \

find
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(1.4)

\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})\,=\,\inf\Big\{\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)\ :\ u\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \mathrm{G}_{\infty}(u)\leq G\ \,\&\,\ \mathrm{Q}(u)=0\Big\}.\\

We are also interested in deriving appropriate differential equations as necessary conditions that such constrained minimisers
u_{\infty}
might satisfy. Hence, the problem under consideration is that of minimising the supremal functional
\textrm{F}_{\infty}
within the
G
-sublevel set of the supremal functional
\textrm{G}_{\infty}
, constrained also by the zero level set of the mapping
\mathrm{Q}
. Minimisation problems in
L^{\infty}
with an
L^{\infty}
sublevel-set constraints are called
isosupremic
, a terminology introduced by Aronsson-Barron in
[
2
]
, by analogy to the terminology of
isoperimetric
constraints utilised for integral functionals.

The assumptions required for our mapping
\mathrm{Q}
expressing the nonlinear level-set constraint are very general, allowing for the solvability of a very large class of problems. In Section
4
we provide a fairly comprehensive list of explicit examples of operators
\mathrm{Q}
to which our results apply. Indeed,
\mathrm{Q}
may manifest itself as a pointwise, unilateral, or inclusion constraint (Subsection
4.1
), as an integral isoperimetric constraint (Subsection
4.2
), or as a nonlinear PDE constraint, including second order quasilinear divergence systems (Subsection
4.3
), jacobian equations and other PDEs driven by null Lagrangians (Subsection
4.4
).

Minimisation of supremal functionals poses several important challenges.
Firstly, the
L^{\infty}
norm is neither Gateaux differentiable nor strictly convex, and the space
L^{\infty}
is neither reflexive nor separable. A related additional complication is that the
L^{\infty}
norm is not additive but only sub-additive with respect to the domain. As a result, if one solves the
L^{\infty}
minimisation existence problem via the direct method, then the analogue of the Euler-Lagrange equations for the
L^{\infty}
problem cannot be derived directly by considering variations, due to the lack of smoothness of the
L^{\infty}
norm.

In this paper, we transcend the difficulties illustrated above, by following the relatively standard strategy of using appropriate approximations by
L^{p}
functionals as
p\to\infty
. We solve the desired
L^{\infty}
variational problem by solving approximating
L^{p}
variational problems for all
p
, and obtaining the necessary compactness estimates that allow us to pass to the limit as
p\to\infty
. The case of finite
p
, which is of independent interest and a byproduct of our analysis, for us is just the mechanism to solve the desired
L^{\infty}
problem. The intuition behind the use of
L^{p}
approximations is based on the fact that for a fixed essentially bounded function on a set of finite measure, the
L^{p}
norm tends to the
L^{\infty}
norm as
p\to\infty
.

Problems involving constraints are relatively new in the Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
and previous work has been relatively sparse, even more so in the vectorial case. To the best of our knowledge, the only work which directly studies isosupremic problems is
[
2
]
by Aronsson-Barron. Among other questions answered therein, it considers some aspects of the one-dimensional case for
n=1
, but with no additional constraints of any type (which amounts to
\mathrm{Q}\equiv 0
in our setting).

More broadly, very few previous works involve vectorial problems with general constraints in
L^{\infty}
. Certain vectorial and higher order problems involving eigenvalues in
L^{\infty}
have been considered in
[
26
,
28
]
. Examples of problems with PDE and other constraints are considered in
[
12
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
. In the paper
[
6
]
of Barron-Jensen, a scalar
L^{\infty}
constrained problem was considered, but the constraint was integral. With the exception of the paper
[
2
]
, it appears that vectorial variational problems in
L^{\infty}
involving isosupremic constraints have not been studied before, especially including additional nonlinear constraints which cover numerous different cases, as in this work. For assorted interesting works within the wider area of Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
we refer to
[
1
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
.

Let us note that, in this work, we refrain from discussing the question of defining and studying localised versions of
L^{\infty}
minimisers on subdomains. Such minimisers are commonly called
absolute
in the Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
, a concept first introduced and studied by Aronsson in his seminal papers in the 1960s. As already noted in
[
2
]
, the definition of constrained absolute minimisers on subdomains is quite problematic in many respects, even for specific choices of constraints in the scalar case
N=1
, which has a well developed
L^{\infty}
theory. In any case, the “non-intrinsic” method of
L^{p}
approximations, employed herein, is widely believed to select always the “best”
L^{\infty}
minimiser, which in the scalar unconstrained case can be shown to be indeed an absolute minimiser. In the vectorial case of
N\geq 2
, the situation is trickier and the “correct” localised minimality notion is still under discussion, even without any constraints being involved (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
for work in this direction).

We now state our main results. Our notation is generally standard as e.g. in
[
30
]
, or otherwise self-explanatory. As we will only be working with finite measures, we will be using the normalised
L^{p}
norms in which we replace the integral over the domain with the respective average. For our density functions
f
and
g
appearing in (
1.1
) we will assume the following:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{Both $f$ and $g$ are Carath\'{e}odory functions, namely $f(\cdot,\eta,P)$, $g(\cdot,\eta,P)$ are}\\
&\text{measurable for all $(\eta,P)$, and $f(x,\cdot,\cdot)$, $g(x,\cdot,\cdot)$ are continuous for a.e.\ $x$.}\end{split}\right.\!\!\!\!

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{Exists a continuous $\mathrm{C}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)$ and an $\alpha>1$:}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt&\hskip 40.0pt\left\{\ \ \begin{split}0\leq f(x,\eta,P)\leq\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)\big(|P|^{\alpha}+1\big),&\\
0\leq g(x,\eta,P)\leq\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)\big(|P|^{\alpha}+1\big),\end{split}\right.\\
&\text{for a.e.\ $x$ and all $(\eta,P)$.}\end{split}\right.

(1.7)

For a.e.
x
and all
\eta
,
f(x,\eta,\cdot)
and
g(x,\eta,\cdot)
are quasiconvex on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{{Either} $f$ {or} $g$ is coercive, namely exist $c,C>0$ such that either}\\
&\hskip 80.0ptf(x,\eta,P)\,\geq\,c|P|^{\alpha}-C,\\
&\text{or}\\
&\hskip 80.0ptg(x,\eta,P)\,\geq\,c|P|^{\alpha}-C,\\
&\text{for a.e.\ $x$ and all $(\eta,P)$.}\end{split}\right.

These assumptions are relatively standard in the Calculus of Variations. We note only that (
1.7
) is meant in the sense of Morrey quasiconvexity for integral functionals (as e.g. in
[
14
]
), not in the sense of level-convexity or of the
L^{\infty}
notion of “BJW-quasiconvexity” of Barron-Jensen-Wang in
[
8
]
, nor in the sense of “
\mathcal{A}
-Young quasiconvexity” of Ansini-Prinari in
[
1
]
. This stronger notion of quasiconvexity is not necessary if one is interested in merely solving (
1.4
) by applying the direct method in
W^{1,\infty}

without deriving any PDEs
, and can indeed be weakened substantially, but it is needed for Theorems
2
and
3
, so we simplify the exposition by assuming it at the outset. For our operator
\mathrm{Q}
in (
1.2
) we will assume the following:

(1.9)

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}\big(\{0\}\big)\ \text{is weakly closed in}\ W^{1,\overline{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

This is a very feeble requirement for
\mathrm{Q}
and there exist numerous explicit examples of interest that satisfy (
1.9
), see Section
4
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the approximating
L^{p}
functionals
\mathrm{F}_{p},\mathrm{G}_{p}:W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

(1.10)

\left\{\ \ \ \ \begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u):&=\left(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\right)^{\!\!1/p},\\
\mathrm{G}_{p}(u):&=\left(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\right)^{\!\!1/p}.\end{split}\right.

For
p=\infty
,
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
and
\mathrm{G}_{\infty}
are given by (
1.3
). For each
p\in[1,\infty]
and
G\geq 0
fixed, we define the admissible minimisation class
\mathfrak{X}^{p}({\Omega})
on which
\mathrm{F}_{p}
is to be minimised, by setting

(1.11)

\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)\,:=\,\Big\{v\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \mathrm{G}_{p}(v)\leq G\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{Q}(v)=0\Big\}.

Our first main result concerns the existence of
\mathrm{F}_{p}
-minimisers in
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)
and the existence of
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
-minimisers in
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, obtained as subsequential limits as
p\to\infty
.

Theorem 1

(
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
-minimisers,
\mathrm{F}_{p}
-minimisers & convergence as
p\to\infty
)
.

Suppose that the mappings
f,g
and
\mathrm{Q}
satisfy the assumptions (
1.5
) through (
1.9
). If the next compatibility condition is satisfied

(1.12)

\inf\Big\{\mathrm{G}_{\infty}:\mathrm{Q}^{-1}\big(\{0\}\big)\cap W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big\}\,<\,G,

then, for any
p\in[\bar{p},\infty]
, the functional
\mathrm{F}_{p}
has a constrained minimiser
u_{p}
in the admissible class
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)
, namely

(1.13)

\mathrm{F}_{p}\big(u_{p})\,=\,\inf\Big\{\mathrm{F}_{p}\big(v)\,:\ v\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)\Big\}.

Additionally, there exists a subsequence of indices
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
such that, the sequence of respective
\mathrm{F}_{p_{j}}
-minimisers satisfies
u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
, and
u_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
weakly in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for all
q\in(1,\infty)
fixed, as
p_{j}\to\infty
. Finally, we have the convergence of minimum values
\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})\longrightarrow\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})
as
p_{j}\to\infty
.

Note that, in view of (
1.9
), the compatibility condition (
1.12
) guarantees that
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset
. Given the existence of constrained minimisers provided from Theorem
1
above, the next natural question concerns the deduction of necessary conditions satisfied by constrained minimisers in the form of PDEs. Firstly, we examine the case of
p<\infty
. The nonlinear constraint expressed by the zero level-set of the mapping
\mathrm{Q}
in (
1.11
) gives rise to a Lagrange multiplier in the Euler-Lagrange equations. This can be inferred by employing well-known results on Lagrange multipliers, see for instance
[
37
]
. For this to be possible, though, one needs improved regularity of the mappings
f,g
and
\mathrm{Q}
involved. We will suppose additionally that

(1.14)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\text{The partial derivatives $f_{\eta},f_{P},g_{\eta},g_{P}$ of $f$ and $g$ are continuous on \ \ \ }\\
&\text{$\smash{\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}}$, and for $\mathrm{C},\alpha$ as in \eqref{1.6}, we have the bounds}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \left\{\ \ \begin{split}&|f_{\eta}|(x,\eta,P)+|f_{P}|(x,\eta,P)\leq\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)\big(|P|^{\alpha-1}+1\big),\\
&|g_{\eta}|(x,\eta,P)+|g_{P}|(x,\eta,P)\leq\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)\big(|P|^{\alpha-1}+1\big),\end{split}\right.\\
&\text{for all }(x,\eta,P).\end{split}\right.

Further, we will assume that:

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{$\mathrm{Q}$ is continuously differentiable, and its Fr\'{e}chet derivative}\\
&\hskip 50.0pt(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{\overline{u}}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\textbf{E}\\
&\text{has closed range in $\textbf{E}$, for any $\overline{u}\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\subseteq W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})$. }\end{split}\right.

Recall that no regularity was assumed for
\mathrm{Q}
to obtain the existence of minimisers in Theorem
1
. Finally, for the sake of brevity, for any
u\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
, we will employ the following notation

f[u]\,\equiv\,f(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u),\ \ \ g[u]\,\equiv\,g(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u),

and similar notation will be used for the compositions of
f_{\eta},f_{P},g_{\eta},g_{P}
of
f
and
g
respectively, namely
f_{\eta}[u],g_{\eta}[u]
, etc. Further, “
\cdot
” and “
:
” will denote the standard inner products on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
respectively.

Theorem 2

(The equations in
L^{p}
)
.

Suppose we are in the setting of Theorem
1
and assumptions (
1.5
) through (
1.9
) are satisfied. Suppose additionally that (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) are satisfied. Then, for any
p\in(\bar{p},\infty)
, there exists Lagrange multipliers

(1.16)

\lambda_{p}\geq 0,\ \ \mu_{p}\geq 0,\ \ \ \psi_{p}\,\in\,\emph{\textbf{E}}^{*},

where
(\emph{\textbf{E}}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
is the dual space of
E
, such that not all vanish simultaneously:

(1.17)

|\lambda_{p}|+|\mu_{p}|+\|\psi_{p}\|_{*}\neq 0.

Then, the minimiser
u_{p}\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)
satisfies the equation

(1.18)

\left\{\begin{split}&\lambda_{p}\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f[u_{p}]^{p-1}\Big(f_{\eta}[u_{p}]\cdot\phi\,+\,f_{P}[u_{p}]:\mathrm{D}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
+\ &\mu_{p}\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g[u_{p}]^{p-1}\Big(g_{\eta}[u_{p}]\cdot\phi\,+\,g_{P}[u_{p}]:\mathrm{D}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\,=\,\big\langle\psi_{p},(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{p}}(\phi)\big\rangle,\end{split}\right.

for all test mappings
\phi\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)
, coupled by the additional condition

(1.19)

\mu_{p}\big(\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})-G\big)=0.

Note that condition (
1.19
) implies that if
\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})<G
, namely if the
L^{\infty}
-energy constraint is not realised (i.e. the minimiser
u_{p}
lies in the interior of the sublevel set
\{\mathrm{G}_{p}\leq G\}
), then
\mu_{p}=0
and hence the associated multiplier vanishes.

Now we consider the case of
p=\infty
. In this case, there is not a simple analogue of the divergence structure Euler Lagrange equations. The equations are derived by an appropriate passage to limits as
p\to\infty
in Theorem
2
. A standard approach in Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
has been to derive Aronsson-type PDE systems, which are non-divergence counterparts to the Euler-Lagrange equations, as e.g. done in
[
2
]
for the case of
n=1
and
\mathrm{Q}\equiv 0
. However, Aronsson-type systems are always non-divergence and far less tractable than their divergence counterparts. In fact, in the vectorial case they have discontinuous coefficients and are fully nonlinear in the higher order case (see for instance
[
13
]
and
[
21
,
22
,
29
]
for this evolving line of development regarding the direct study of generalised solutions to Aronsson systems).

Nevertheless, there exists an alternative approach which allows to derive
divergence structure
PDE systems as necessary conditions. The starting point of this idea is based on the use of a different scaling in the Euler-Lagrange equations in
L^{p}
and has already born substantial fruit in
[
12
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. There is however a toll to be paid for this “forcing” of divergence structure: certain non-uniquely determined measures arise as auxiliary parameters in the coefficients of the PDE system, which depend nonlinearly on the minimisers. For more details on the historical origins of this alternative approach to deriving
L^{\infty}
equations for variational problems we refer to
[
26
]
.

Our final main result therefore concerns the satisfaction of necessary conditions for the constrained minimiser in
L^{\infty}
constructed in Theorem
1
. For this result we will need to impose some natural additional hypotheses. These hypotheses, although they restrict considerably the classes of
f,g,\mathrm{Q}
that were utilised in order to prove existence of minimisers, they do nonetheless include the interesting case of
F_{\infty}
being the
L^{\infty}
norm of the gradient. Firstly, let us introduce some convenient notation and rewrite (
1.18
) in a way which will be more appropriate for the statement and the subsequent proof. By introducing for each
p\in(\bar{p},\infty)
the non-negative Radon measures
\sigma_{p},\tau_{p}\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})
given by

(1.20)

\begin{split}\sigma_{p}\,&:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\bigg(\dfrac{f[u_{p}]}{\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})}\bigg)^{\!p-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega},&\text{ if }\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})>0,\\
0,&\text{ if }\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})=0,\end{array}\right.\\
\tau_{p}\,&:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\bigg(\dfrac{g[u_{p}]}{\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})}\bigg)^{\!p-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega},&\text{ if }\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})>0,\\
0,&\text{ if }\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})=0,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

and the scaled multipliers

(1.21)

\begin{split}\hat{\lambda}_{p}\,&:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{p}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})^{p-1},&\text{ if }\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})>0,\\
\lambda_{p},&\text{ if }\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})=0,\end{array}\right.\\
\hat{\mu}_{p}\,&:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{p}\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})^{p-1},&\text{ if }\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})>0,\\
\mu_{p},&\text{ if }\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{p})=0,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

we can rewrite (
1.18
) as

(1.22)

\left\{\begin{split}&\hat{\lambda}_{p}\int_{\Omega}\Big(f_{\eta}[u_{p}]\cdot\phi\,+\,f_{P}[u_{p}]:\mathrm{D}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{p}\\
+\ &\hat{\mu}_{p}\int_{\Omega}\Big(g_{\eta}[u_{p}]\cdot\phi\,+\,g_{P}[u_{p}]:\mathrm{D}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{p}\,=\,\big\langle\psi_{p},(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{p}}(\phi)\big\rangle,\end{split}\right.

Further, let us set

(1.23)

R_{p}\,:=\,\hat{\lambda}_{p}+\hat{\mu}_{p}+\|\psi_{p}\|_{*}

and note that, by virtue of Theorem
2
and Definition (
1.21
), we have that
R_{p}>0
. We may then define the new rescaled multipliers

(1.24)

\Lambda_{p}\,:=\,\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{p}}{R_{p}}\in[0,1],\ \ \ \mathrm{M}_{p}\,:=\,\frac{\hat{\mu}_{p}}{R_{p}}\in[0,1],\ \ \ \Psi_{p}\,:=\,\frac{\psi_{p}}{R_{p}}\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\textbf{E}^{*}}_{1}\!(0),

where
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\textbf{E}^{*}}_{1}\!(0)
is the closed unit ball in
\textbf{E}^{*}
. Let us finally set

C^{1}_{0}\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)\,:=\,W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigcap C^{1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{N}\big).

Now we state the additional assumptions which we need to impose:

(1.25)

\textbf{E}\text{ is a separable Banach space}.

(1.26)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\text{The restriction of the differential $(u,v)\mapsto(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(v)$, considered as}\\
&\hskip 60.0pt\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ \mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\times W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\textbf{E},\\
&\text{satisfies the following conditions:}\\
&\text{If }u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u\text{ in }\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\text{ as }m\to\infty,\text{ and }\phi\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\text{ then }\\
&\hskip 60.0pt\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(u_{m})\longrightarrow(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(u),\\
&(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(\phi)\longrightarrow(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi),\end{split}\right.\\
&\text{ as }m\to\infty.\end{split}\right.

The above assumption requires that
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q}
be weakly-strongly continuous on the diagonal of
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\times\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
and on subsets of the form
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\times\{\phi\}
, when
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is endowed with its weak topology and
E
with its norm topology. We assume further that:

(1.27)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\text{(i) $g$ does not depend on $P$, namely $g(x,\eta,P)=g(x,\eta)$,}\\
&\text{(ii) $f$ is quadratic in $P$ and independent of $\eta$, namely}\\
&\text{\hskip 80.0pt $f(x,\eta,P)=\textbf{A}(x):P\otimes P$,}\\
&\text{for some continuous positive symmetric fourth order tensor}\\
&\text{$\smash{\textbf{A}:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}}$, which satisfies}\\
&\text{\hskip 30.0pt$\textbf{A}(x):P\otimes P>0$\ , \ \ $\textbf{A}(x):P\otimes Q=\textbf{A}(x):Q\otimes P$,}\\
&\text{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega}$ and all $P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\setminus\{0\}$.}\end{split}\right.

The above requirements are compatible with the previous assumptions on
f
. In fact, by
[
26
, Lemma 4, p. 8]
and our earlier assumptions, the positivity and symmetry requirements for
A
are superfluous and can be deduced by merely assuming that
f
is quadratic in
P
(up to a replacement of
A
by its symmetrisation), but we have added them to (
1.27

Theorem 3

(The equations in

Proposition 4
.

In the setting of Theorem
1

Proof of Proposition

4
. Fix

. We begin by illustrating that

1.12

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}\big(\{0\}\big)\cap W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\neq\emptyset

\begin{split}\mathrm{G}_{p}(u_{0})\,=\,\left(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g(\cdot,u_{0},\mathrm{D}u_{0})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\right)^{\!\!1/p}\leq\,\big\|g(\cdot,u_{0},\mathrm{D}u_{0})\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,=\,\mathrm{G}_{\infty}(u_{0})\,<\,G.\end{split}

Consequently, in view of (
1.11

u_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset

1.7

open and

Hence, by Jensen’s inequality and the convexity of

, we conclude that

h(P)^{p}\leq\bigg(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!U}h(P+\mathrm{D}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\bigg)^{\!\!p}\,\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!U}h(P+\mathrm{D}\phi)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.

We now proceed to bound

1.6

\begin{split}&0\leq f(x,\eta,P)^{p}\leq\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)^{p}\big(1+|P|^{\alpha}\big)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\mathrm{C}(x,\eta)^{p}\big(1+|P|^{\alpha p}\big).\end{split}

By standard results (see
[
14

\mathrm{F}_{p}
is weakly lower semicontinuous on
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

\begin{split}\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u^{(i)})&\leq\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{0})\\

&\leq\bigg(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\Big(\mathrm{C}(\cdot,u_{0})(1+|\mathrm{D}u_{0}|^{\alpha})\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\bigg)^{\!\!1/p},\end{split}

\begin{split}\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u^{(i)})&\leq\Big\|\mathrm{C}(\cdot,u_{0})\big(1+|\mathrm{D}u_{0}|^{\alpha}\big)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\|\mathrm{C}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|(1+|\mathrm{D}u_{0}|^{\alpha})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\

&<\infty.\end{split}

Hence, the infimum is indeed finite. Now we show that under assumption (
1.8
), the minimising sequence
(u^{(i)})_{1}^{\infty}
is bounded in

h
symbolise either
f
or
g

{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\big|h(\cdot,u^{(i)},\mathrm{D}u^{(i)})+C\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\geq\,c\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}|\mathrm{D}u^{(i)}|^{\alpha p}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.

By using the Poincaré and Hölder inequalities, we infer that

\begin{split}&C\,+\,\bigg(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}|h(\cdot,u^{(i)},\mathrm{D}u^{(i)})|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\bigg)^{\!\!\frac{1}{p}}\,\geq\,c^{\prime}\,\|u^{(i)}\|^{\alpha}_{W^{1,\alpha p}(\Omega)}\end{split}

for some new constant

which is independent of
i\in\mathbb{N}
. If
h=f
, then by the previously derived estimates we have the uniform bound

\|u^{(i)}\|^{\alpha}_{W^{1,\alpha p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\Big(C\,+\,\mathrm{F}_{p}(u^{(i)})\Big)\,\leq\,\frac{C}{c^{\prime}}\,+\,\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\|\mathrm{C}(\cdot,u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big(1+\|\mathrm{D}u_{0}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big),

and if

In either case, we have that
(u^{(i)})_{1}^{\infty}
is weakly precompact in
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By passing to a subsequence if necessary, standard strong and weak compactness arguments imply that there exists a map
u_{p}\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a subsequence denoted again by

such that

\left\{\ \begin{split}&u^{(i)}\longrightarrow u_{p},\ \ \ \ \ \ \,\text{in}\ L^{\alpha p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

\end{split}\right.

as
i\to\infty
. Further, since

, by the Morrey estimate we have that

is also bounded in

for
\gamma<1-n/(\alpha p)
. By the compact embedding of Hölder spaces, we conclude that

u^{(i)}\longrightarrow u_{p}\ \ \ \text{in}\ C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}),

as

1.5

is weakly lower-continuous. Since

in

Hence,

is a weakly closed subset of

3.
The equations for constrained minimisers in

and in

In this section we establish the proofs of Theorem
2

. The proof of the former is a relatively simple consequence of deep results in the generalised Kuhn-Tucker theory from

Proof of Theorem

2
. Fix
p\in[\bar{p},\infty)
. We begin with the simplifying observation that, the minimisation problem (
1.13
) can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u_{p})^{p}\,=\,\bigg\{\frac{1}{p}\mathrm{F}_{p}(u)^{p}\ :\ u\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \frac{1}{p}\mathrm{G}_{p}(u)^{p}-\frac{G^{p}}{p}\leq 0\ \&\ \mathrm{Q}(u)=0\bigg\}.

This reformulation is a labour-saving device, drastically shortening the proof of this result. In view of assumption (
1.14
), first we will show that the following functionals are Fréchet differentiable

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{F}_{p})^{p}\ &:\ \ W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\\
\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{G}_{p})^{p}-\frac{G^{p}}{p}\ &:\ \ W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}.\end{split}

A direct computation gives the next formal expressions for their Gateaux derivatives

\begin{split}\bigg(\mathrm{d}\Big[\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{F}_{p})^{p}\Big]\bigg)_{\!\!u}(v)\,&=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f[u]^{p-1}\big(f_{\eta}[u]\cdot v+f_{P}[u]:\mathrm{D}v\big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n},\\
\bigg(\mathrm{d}\Big[\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{G}_{p})^{p}-\frac{G^{p}}{p}\Big]\bigg)_{\!\!u}(v)\,&=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g[u]^{p-1}\big(g_{\eta}[u]\cdot v+g_{P}[u]:\mathrm{D}v\big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n},\end{split}

for all
u,v\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We will now show the above formal expressions indeed define Fréchet derivatives, by employing relatively standard estimates through the Hölder inequality. We argue only for
\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{F}_{p})^{p}
, as the estimates for
\frac{1}{p}\big((\mathrm{G}_{p})^{p}-{G^{p}}\big)
are identical. Since
\alpha>1
and
p\geq\bar{p}>n
, by Morrey’s estimate we have

\begin{split}\bigg|\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f[u]&{}^{p-1}\big(f_{\eta}[u]\cdot v+f_{P}[u]:\mathrm{D}v\big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\bigg|\\
&\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}|f[u]|^{p-1}\Big(|f_{\eta}[u]||v|+|f_{P}[u]||\mathrm{D}v|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\\
&\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}C(\cdot,u)^{p}\big(1+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha}\big)^{p-1}\big(1+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha-1}\big)(|v|+|\mathrm{D}v|)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\\
&\leq\,2^{p}\big\||v|C(\cdot,u)^{p}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\Big(1+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha-1}+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha p-\alpha}+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha p-1}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\\
&\ \ +\,2^{p}\big\|C(\cdot,u)^{p}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\Big(1+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha-1}+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha p-\alpha}+|\mathrm{D}u|^{\alpha p-1}\Big)|\mathrm{D}v|\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\\
&\leq\,2^{p}\big\||v|C(\cdot,u)^{p}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big(1+\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega)}^{\alpha-1}+\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega)}^{\alpha p-\alpha}+\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega)}^{\alpha p-1}\Big)\\
&\ \ +\,2^{p}\big\|C(\cdot,u)^{p}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\bigg(\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \ +\sum_{t\in\{\alpha-1,\alpha p-\alpha,\alpha p-1\}}\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\frac{t\alpha p}{\alpha p-1}}(\Omega)}^{t}\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\alpha p}(\Omega)}\bigg).\end{split}

We now show that the equations that the constrained minimiser satisfies take the form as given in (
1.18
) and (
1.19
). Given the Fréchet derivatives and our assumption (
1.15
) on the range of
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q}
, we can invoke the generalised Kuhn-Tucker theory. By applying
[
37
, Theorem 48.B, p.p. 416-417]
with (in the book’s notation)

\begin{split}F_{0}&:=\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{F}_{p})^{p},\ \ \ F_{1}:=\frac{1}{p}(\mathrm{G}_{p})^{p}-\frac{G^{p}}{p},\ \ \ F_{3}:=\mathrm{Q},\\
X&=U=N_{2}:=W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big),\ \ \ Y:=\textbf{E},\ \ \ n=2,\end{split}

and by noting that
N_{2}
herein is the entire (vector) space
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)
, we readily infer the claims made in (
1.16
)-(
1.19
). The result ensues.
∎

, Prop. 12, p. 18]
, for any
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
:

\bullet
We have that
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)
, and the identity

\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)\,=\,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)\,+\,\varepsilon\ell(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u))(\mathrm{D}\xi)^{\top},

everywhere on
\overline{\Omega}
.

\bullet
We have
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u\longrightarrow u
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
, and in
C^{\gamma}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Further,
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u\,\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\,u
in
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

\bullet
For any
\Theta\in C\big(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\big)
, satisfying for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
that
\Theta(x,\cdot)
is convex on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
with
\Theta(x,\cdot)\geq\Theta(x,0)=0
, and also that the partial derivative
\Theta_{P}
exists and is continuous on
\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, we will show that there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega\in C\big([0,\infty);[0,\infty)\big)
with
\omega(0)=0
which is independent of
\varepsilon
and
x
, such that

\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)(x)\big)\,\leq\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon),

for any
x\in\Omega
. In particular, the above estimate implies that

\big\|\Theta\big(\cdot,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon).

(This was already established in
[
26
]
, but without
x
-dependence for
\Theta
.) As a result,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\big\|\Theta\big(\cdot,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Let us now establish the claimed estimate. For any fixed
R>0
such that

R\,>\,\big(\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+1\big)\big(\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1\big),

we set

\omega(t)\,:=\,t\ell R\|\Theta_{P}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R}(0))}\,+\underset{|z-x|\leq t\ell R}{\sup_{|Q|\leq R}}\Big|\Theta\big(x,Q\big)-\Theta\big(z,Q\big)\Big|,\ \ \ t\geq 0.

Then, we have

\begin{split}\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)(x)\big)&=\Theta\Big(x,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)\,+\,\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)(\mathrm{D}\xi(x))^{\top}\Big)\\
&\leq\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)\big)+\|\Theta_{P}(x,\cdot)\|_{C(\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R}(0))}\big\|\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(\mathrm{D}\xi)^{\top}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)\big)+\varepsilon\ell\|\Theta_{P}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R}(0))}\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)\big)\,+\,\varepsilon\ell R\|\Theta_{P}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R}(0))},\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. Further, since
\varrho\mathcal{L}^{n}
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}u)(x)\big)\,&=\,\Theta\left(x,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}u\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\,\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\\
&\leq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Theta\Big(x,\mathrm{D}u\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\Big)\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\,\underset{y\in\mathbb{B}_{1}(0)}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta\Big(x,\mathrm{D}u\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\Big)\\
&=\,\underset{z\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{D}u(z)\big)\\
&\leq\,\underset{z\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta\big(z,\mathrm{D}u(z)\big)\\
&\ \ \ +\underset{|z-x|\leq\varepsilon\ell\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\sup_{|Q|\leq\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}}\Big|\Theta\big(x,Q\big)-\Theta\big(z,Q\big)\Big|,\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. By using that
\mathrm{D}u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and our assumptions on
\Theta
, the previous two estimates yield that

\begin{split}\Theta\big(x,\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u)(x)\big)\,&\leq\,\underset{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon)\\
&=\,\max\bigg\{\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u),\ \underset{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))\setminus\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\bigg\}\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon)\\
&=\,\max\bigg\{\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u),\ \underset{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))\setminus\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,0)\bigg\}\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon)\\
&=\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Theta(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u)\,+\,\omega(\varepsilon),\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
.

In conclusion, the above establish the existence of an approximating sequence as in (
1.32
), by taking

\ \ \ \ v_{j}\,:=\,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon_{j}}u_{\infty}\in\,C^{1}_{0}\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}\big),\ \ j\in\mathbb{N},

along any infinitesimal sequence
(\varepsilon_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
satisfying
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, for the choice
\Theta:=f
, which is admissible because of our hypotheses (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.27
).

Step 12.
By putting together all the previous, the proof of Theorem
3
has now been completed.
∎

,

\mathrm{Q}
, in order to obtain the corresponding result (assuming that

\mathrm{Q}
solely).

4.1.
Pointwise constraints, unilateral constraints and inclusions

The nonlinear operator of (
1.2
) we are using in the admissible class of (
1.4
), can include the following model cases:

Case 1.

\Pi(x,u(x))=0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where
\Pi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M}
is given.

Case 2.

\Pi(x,u(x))\leq 0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where
\Pi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given.

Case 3.

u(x)\in\mathcal{K}
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a given closed set.

Constraints as in Case 1 are sometimes called
holonomic
(see for instance
[
2
]
). We now elaborate on the assumptions required to be fulfilled in each of these cases.

Proposition 6

(Case 1)
.

Let
\Pi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
. By defining

(4.1)

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Q}(u)\,:=\,\Pi(\cdot,u),

and setting
\emph{\textbf{E}}:=L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
, we have the following:

(i)
The zero set of
\mathrm{Q}
equals

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\Big\{v\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \Pi(x,v(x))=0,\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\Big\},

and assumption (
1.9
) is always satisfied.

(ii)
If for any
x\in\Omega
we have

\big\{\Pi(x,\cdot)=0\big\}\,\subseteq\,\big\{\Pi_{\eta}(x,\cdot)=0\big\},

namely when all points in the zero set are critical points, then
\mathrm{Q}
satisfies (
1.15
).

(iii)
Assumptions (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are always satisfied.

The choice of
E
is deliberately made “as large as possible”, as then the Lagrange multipliers of Theorems
2
and
3
are valued in the smaller space
\textbf{E}^{*}=L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.

Proof of Proposition

6
. (i) Follows directly from the definitions, by the continuity of
\Pi
and by Morrey’s estimate, because
\bar{p}>n
.

(ii) Indeed, since

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)\,=\,\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot,u)\cdot\phi,

if
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, then
\Pi(\cdot,u)=0
a.e. on
\Omega
and therefore
\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot,u)=0
a.e. on
\Omega
, which implies that
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}=0
, hence its image is the closed trivial subspace
\{0\}\subseteq L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.

(iii) Note first that
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
is separable. Also, if we have
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
and
\phi_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\phi
in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
, then by Morrey’s theorem and the compactness of the imbedding of Hölder spaces we have
u_{m}\longrightarrow u
and also
\phi_{m}\longrightarrow\phi
in
C\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)
as
m\to\infty
. Hence, we have as
m\to\infty
that

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(\phi_{m})=\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot,u_{m})\cdot\phi_{m}\,\longrightarrow\,\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot,u)\cdot\phi=(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi),

in
C\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{M}\big)
, which a fortiori implies strong convergence in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
. ∎

We note that the proof of (iii) above is immediate if one assumes the additional hypothesis of (ii), since then
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}=0
for any sequence
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
.

Proposition 7

(Case 2)
.

Let
\Pi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let us define
\pi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(4.2)

\pi(t)\,:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&t\leq 0,\\
t^{2},&t>0.\end{array}\right.

By defining the operator

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega),\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Q}(u)\,:=\,\pi(\Pi(\cdot,u)),

for
\emph{\textbf{E}}:=L^{1}(\Omega)
, we have the following:

(i)
The zero set of
\mathrm{Q}
equals

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\Big\{v\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \Pi(x,v(x))\leq 0,\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\Big\},

and assumption (
1.9
) is always satisfied.

(ii)
If for any
x\in\Omega
it holds that

\big\{\Pi(x,\cdot)=0\big\}\,\subseteq\,\big\{\Pi_{\eta}(x,\cdot)=0\big\},

then
\mathrm{Q}
satisfies assumption (
1.15
).

(iii)
Assumptions (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are always satisfied.

Proof of Proposition

7
. (i) Follows as in the proof of Proposition
6
(i), upon noting that
\{\pi\leq 0\}=(-\infty,0]
.

(ii) Since

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)\,=\,\pi^{\prime}\big(\Pi(\cdot,u)\big)\Pi_{\eta}(\cdot,u)\cdot\phi,

if
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, then
\Pi(\cdot,u)\leq 0
a.e. on
\Omega
and therefore
\pi^{\prime}(\Pi(\cdot,u))=0
a.e. on
\Omega
because
\{\pi^{\prime}=0\}=(-\infty,0]
, which implies that
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}=0
, hence its image is the closed trivial subspace
\{0\}\subseteq L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
and (
1.15
) is satisfied.

(iii) Similar to the proof of Proposition
6
(iii), using the different expression for the differential
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q}
as above.
∎

Proposition 8

(Case 3)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a closed set with
\mathcal{K}\neq\emptyset
. Then, there exists
\Pi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\mathcal{K}=\{\Pi=0\}\subseteq\{\Pi_{\eta}=0\}
. Further, if one defines

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega),\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Q}(u)\,:=\,\Pi(u),

and
\emph{\textbf{E}}:=L^{1}(\Omega)
, then we have

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\Big\{v\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ v(x)\in\mathcal{K},\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\Big\},

and
\mathrm{Q}
satisfies (
1.9
), (
1.15
), (
1.25
) and (
1.26
).

Proof of Proposition

8
. It is well-known that for every such set
\mathcal{K}
, there exists a function
\Pi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the claimed properties. A proof of this fact can be found e.g. in
[
34
, Sec. 1.1.13, p. 25]
(the claimed inclusion is not explicitly stated, but follows from the method of proof by the smooth Urysohn lemma). The rest follows from Proposition
6
.
∎

4.2.
Integral and isoperimetric constraints

The nonlinear operator of (
1.2
) can also cover the following important case of constraint:

\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq H,

when
h:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
H\in\mathbb{R}
are given.

Proposition 9
.

Let
h:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the assumptions (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) and (
1.14
) that
f,g
are assumed to satisfy, with
\alpha\leq\bar{p}
. Let also
H\in\mathbb{R}
be given and let
\pi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be as in (
4.2
). Then, by defining the operator

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \ \ \ \mathrm{Q}(u)\,:=\,\pi\left(\,\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\!\right),

and setting
\emph{\textbf{E}}:=\mathbb{R}
, we have the following:

(i)
The zero set of
\mathrm{Q}
equals

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\left\{v\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,v,\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq H\right\}

and assumption (
1.9
) is satisfied.

(ii)

\mathrm{Q}
satisfies (
1.15
), (
1.25
) and (
1.26
).

Proof of Proposition

9
. (i) If
\mathrm{Q}(u_{m})=0
and
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
, then since
\{\pi\leq 0\}=(-\infty,0]
, we have

\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u_{m},\mathrm{D}u_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\,\leq\,0.

Since
h
satisfies (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) for
\alpha\leq\bar{p}
, by standard results (see e.g.
[
14
]
), the functional
u\mapsto\|h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
is weakly lower-semicontinuous in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence

\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\,\leq\,\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u_{m},\mathrm{D}u_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\,\leq\,0.

Therefore,
\mathrm{Q}(u)=0
, yielding that
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
is weakly closed and hence (
1.9
) is satisfied.

(ii) By a computation, the Gateaux derivative of
\mathrm{Q}
is given by

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)=\pi^{\prime}\!\left(\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\!\right)\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Big[h_{\eta}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\phi\,+\,h_{P}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},

and assumption (
1.14
) for
h
implies that
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q}
is (jointly) continuous on
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\times W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Further, if
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, then by part (i) we have

\int_{\Omega}h(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-H\,\leq\,0,

and therefore the first factor of
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)
vanishes because
\{\pi^{\prime}=0\}=(-\infty,0]
. Thus,
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}=0
when
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, and hence its image is the closed trivial subspace
\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, yielding that (
1.15
) is satisfied.

(iii) For any sequences
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
in
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\subseteq W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\phi_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\phi
in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty
, by part (ii) we have

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(\phi_{m})\,=\,0\longrightarrow 0\,=\,(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)

as
m\to\infty
, hence (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are satisfied.
∎

4.3.
Quasilinear second order differential constraints

The operator
\mathrm{Q}
of (
1.2
) can also cover the case of various types of nontrivial PDE constraints. As an example, we discuss the case of quasilinear divergence second order systems of PDE of the form

(4.3)

\mathrm{div}\big(A(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\big)\,=\,B(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\ \ \text{ in }\Omega,

where the coefficients maps
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and
B:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
are given. Given the plethora of possibilities on the assumptions for such systems, the discussion in this subsection is less formal and is only aimed as a general indication of the admissible choices for
\mathrm{Q}
.

Suppose that
A,B
are
C^{1}
and satisfy appropriate growth bounds, and also that
P\mapsto A(\cdot,\cdot,P)
a monotone map, and that the set of weak solutions to the system (
4.3
) is strongly precompact in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. A sufficient conditions for strong precompactness in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for the set of weak solutions is for example a global
C^{1,\gamma}
or a
W^{2,1+\gamma}
a priori uniform bound on the set of solutions, for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Appropriate assumptions on the coefficients
A,B
that allow the derivation of such a priori bounds can be found e.g. in
[
17
]
for
N=1
and in
[
16
]
for
N\geq 2
. Then, by defining the operator

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow W^{-1,\bar{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

as

\langle\mathrm{Q}(u),\psi\rangle\,:=\,\int_{\Omega}\Big[A(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\psi\,+\,B(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\psi\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},

and setting also
\textbf{E}:=W^{-1,\bar{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, assumptions (
1.9
), (
1.15
), (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are satisfied, with

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\bigg\{u\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ \mathrm{div}\big(A(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\big)=B(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\text{ weakly in }\Omega\bigg\}.

Note first that the expression of
\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
is immediate by the definition of the differential operator
\mathrm{Q}
. Next, note that by assumption, for any sequence of weak solutions
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
to (
4.3
), there exists
u\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u_{m}\longrightarrow u
strongly along a subsequence
m_{j}\to\infty
. By applying this to any sequence
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
(namely sequence of solutions) for which
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
as
m\to\infty
, by passing to the limit in the weak formulation for fixed
\psi\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which reads

\int_{\Omega}\Big[A(\cdot,u_{m},\mathrm{D}u_{m}):\mathrm{D}\psi\,+\,B(\cdot,u_{m},\mathrm{D}u_{m})\cdot\psi\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,0,

we get that
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, as the convergence is in fact strong. Hence, (
1.9
) is satisfied. Further, under appropriate bounds, the operator
\mathrm{Q}
is Fréchet differentiable and

\begin{split}\big\langle(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi),\psi\big\rangle\,&=\,\int_{\Omega}\Big[A_{\eta}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\phi\,+\,A_{P}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi\Big]:\mathrm{D}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&+\,\int_{\Omega}\Big[B_{\eta}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\phi\,+\,B_{P}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi\Big]\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.\end{split}

To see that the image of
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}:W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\longrightarrow\textbf{E}
is closed for any fixed
u\in\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
, let
(T_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{Rg}\big((\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}\big)\subseteq\textbf{E}
be a sequence in the range with
T_{m}\longrightarrow T
strongly in
E
as
m\to\infty
. Since
T_{m}\in\mathrm{Rg}\big((\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}\big)
, exists
\phi_{m}\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
solving the following linear second order system

\begin{split}-\mathrm{div}\Big(A_{\eta}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\phi_{m}\,&+\,A_{P}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi_{m}\Big)\\
&+\,B_{P}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi_{m}\,+\,B_{\eta}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\cdot\phi_{m}\,=\,T_{m}.\end{split}

By the monotonicity of the above system (due to our earlier assumption), under appropriate conditions one has a uniform bound in
W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, yielding the weak compactness of the sequence of solutions
(\phi_{m})_{1}^{\infty}
, which establishes the closedness of
\mathrm{Rg}\big((\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}\big)\subseteq\textbf{E}
and (
1.15
) ensues.

Finally, for any sequence
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
satisfying
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
as
m\to\infty
and any
\phi\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
m_{j}\to\infty
such that
u_{m}\longrightarrow u
as
m_{j}\to\infty
. These facts imply that
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(u_{m})\longrightarrow(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(u)
and also
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(\phi)\longrightarrow(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)
, both strongly in
E
as
m\to\infty
. Hence, (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are satisfied.

4.4.
Null Lagrangians and determinant constraints

We close this paper with the observation that Theorem
1
holds true even when
\mathrm{Q}
expresses a fully nonlinear pointwise jacobian determinant constraint, or even a more general pointwise PDE constraint driven by a null Lagrangian. As an explicit example, let
n=N
and consider the differential operator

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow W^{-1,(\bar{p}/n)^{\prime}}(\Omega),

by setting

\mathrm{Q}(u)\,:=\,\det(\mathrm{D}u)-h,

for a fixed
h\in L^{\bar{p}/n}(\Omega)
, satisfying the necessary compatibility condition

\int_{\Omega}h\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,0.

We also take

\textbf{E}\,:=\,W^{-1,(\bar{p}/n)^{\prime}}(\Omega)\,=\,\big(W_{0}^{1,\bar{p}/n}(\Omega)\big)^{*}.

Then, we have

\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\Big\{u\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\ :\ \det(\mathrm{D}u)=h\text{ a.e.\ in }\Omega\Big\}.

It follows that (
1.9
) is satisfied by the well-known property of weak continuity for jacobian determinants (see e.g.
[
14
, Th. 8.20, p. 395]
). However, the situation is more complicated regarding the satisfaction of the remaining assumptions.
If additionally
n=2
, then (
1.25
) and (
1.26
) are also satisfied. Indeed, since

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)\,=\,\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}\phi,

and since for
u=\phi
we have the identity

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(u)\,=\,\mathrm{cof}(\mathrm{D}u):\mathrm{D}u\,=\,n\det(\mathrm{D}u),

for any
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}^{-1}(\{0\})
with
u_{m}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u
as
m\to\infty
, we have

\begin{split}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(u_{m})\,=\,n\det(\mathrm{D}u_{m})\,\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\,n\det(\mathrm{D}u)\,=\,(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(u)\end{split}

in
L^{\bar{p}/2}(\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
, whilst for any
\phi\in W^{1,\bar{p}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
we have

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u_{m}}(\phi)\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Q})_{u}(\phi)

in
L^{\bar{p}/2}(\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
, by the linearity of the cofactor operator when
n=2
. Then, the compactness of the imbedding

L^{\bar{p}/2}(\Omega)\,\Subset\,W^{-1,(\bar{p}/2)^{\prime}}(\Omega)

implies that the above modes of convergence are in fact strong in
\textbf{E}=W^{-1,(\bar{p}/2)^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. However, it is not clear when assumption (
1.15
) is satisfied, or when (
1.26
) is satisfied in the case that
n\geq 3
. This means Theorems
2
and
3
as they stand do not apply to the case of jacobian constraints. This does
not
mean that it is impossible to derive the associated equations, it merely means that in this case of such a highly nonlinear constraint a different specialised method of proof is required.

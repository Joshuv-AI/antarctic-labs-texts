Ro-vibrational energy analysis of Manning-Rosen and Pöschl-Teller potentials with a new improved approximation in the centrifugal term
Debraj Nath, Amlan K. Roy

Abstract
Two physically important potentials (Manning-Rosen and Pöschl-Teller) are considered for the ro-vibrational energy in diatomic molecules. An improved new approximation is invoked for the centrifugal term, which is then used for their solution within the Nikiforov-Uvarov framework. This employs a recently proposed scheme, which combines the two widely used Greene-Aldrich and Pekeris-type approximations. Thus, approximate analytical expressions are derived for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. The energies are examined with respect to two approximation parameters, λ \lambda and ν \nu . The original approximations are recovered for certain specials values of these two parameters. This offers a simple effective scheme for these and other relevant potentials in quantum mechanics.

Introduction

The construction of a general, universal potential energy function for molecules has been at the forefront of research
activity in chemical, molecular and solid-state physics, as it carries the relevant information for a given molecule.
They are needed as input in various areas including spectroscopy, molecule-molecule collision, molecular simulation,
dynamical situation, thermodynamic properties etc.
There are non-trivial challenges faced towards the development of energy-distance relationship, due to which, its general
form still remains elusive. The publication of famous exponential Morse potential about 90 years ago, has inspired a broad
range of works along this direction having varying degrees of sophistication, accuracy and flexibility. A vast number of
such functions have been generated over the years by a number of researchers. Generally speaking, as the number of parameters
in the analytic potential function increases, the greater it fits with the experimental data.

Exact solution of the Schrödinger equation by Nikiforov-Uvarov Method

Let us consider the Schrödinger equation of a diatomic molecule,

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\nabla^{2}\psi+V({\bf r})\psi=E\psi,

(3)

in presence of MR (

potential of Eq. (

\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}},

(6)

where
\widetilde{\tau}(s)
,
\sigma(s)
,
\widetilde{\sigma}(s)
are polynomials in

If we let,

(9)

be the solution of Eq. (

[

\sigma(s)\phi_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(s)+\tau(s)\phi^{\prime}_{2}(s)+\nu\,\phi_{2}(s)=0,

(10)

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{1}(s)=\displaystyle e^{\int\frac{\pi(s)}{\sigma(s)}ds},\end{array}

(11)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi(s)&=\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{\tau}(s)}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{\tau}(s)}{2}\right)^{2}-\widetilde{\sigma}(s)+k\,\sigma(s)},\\
\tau(s)&=\widetilde{\tau}(s)+2\pi(s),~\bar{\nu}=k+\pi^{\prime}(s),\end{array}

(12)

\bar{\nu}
and

are real constants. Since
\pi(s)
is a polynomial in

\left(\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{\tau}(s)}{2}\right)^{2}-\widetilde{\sigma}(s)+k\,\sigma(s)
is a square of a polynomial in

Eq. (

\phi_{2,n_{r}}(s)=\frac{1}{\rho(s)}\frac{d^{n_{r}}}{ds^{n_{r}}}\left[\sigma^{n_{r}}(s)\rho(s)\right],

\nu_{n_{r}}=-{n_{r}}\,\tau^{\prime}(s)-\frac{{n_{r}}({n_{r}}-1)}{2}\sigma^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(s),~{n_{r}}=0,1,2,\dots,

\begin{array}[]{l}\rho(s)=\displaystyle\left[\sigma(s)\right]^{-1}\,e^{\int\frac{\tau(s)}{\sigma(s)}ds}.\end{array}

x_{3}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\nu\lambda\\
\nu(1-\lambda)\\
1-\nu\end{array}\right),

(47)

and
\lambda,\nu
are real constants. In Fig.
2
, the centrifugal term under different approximations, defined in
Eqs. (
40
), (
42
),
(
44
) and (
46
) are displayed. It is quite clear that, the approximation of Eq. (
46
) is the best of all. When
(\lambda,\nu)=(1,1)
,
Eq. (
46
) implies Eq. (
40
);
(\lambda,\nu)=(0,1)
means (
42
);
(\lambda,\nu)=(\lambda,0)
, leads to (
44
). It may be noted that,
Eq. (
46
) is good for negative
\nu
in the large-
r
region. The advantage is that, for any arbitrary values of
r
, this approximation is
easily seen to provide the best representation amongst all, for select values of
\lambda
and
\nu
.

4.5
Solution of the PT potential

Under the transformation,
s=\tanh^{2}\frac{\alpha r}{2}
, the radial Schrödinger equation in (
6
) becomes,

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d^{2}R}{ds^{2}}+\frac{1-3s}{s(1-s)}\frac{dR}{ds}+\frac{-As^{2}+Bs-C}{s^{2}(1-s)^{2}}R=0,\end{array}

(48)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}A&=\frac{\mu}{2\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})+\frac{l(l+1)}{4}(x_{3}-x_{2}),\\
B&=\frac{2\mu}{\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}\left(E+\frac{\xi_{1}}{2}\right)+\frac{l(l+1)}{2}(x_{2}-2x_{1}),\\
C&=\frac{\mu}{2\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})+\frac{l(l+1)}{4}(x_{3}+x_{2}).\\
\end{array}

(49)

Similarly, we obtain a pair of
k
, which can be defined by,

k_{\pm}=B-2C\pm a\sqrt{C},~a=\sqrt{1+4(A-B+C)}.

(50)

This produces,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi(s)&=\frac{s}{2}\pm\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left|\sqrt{C}-\frac{a}{2}\right|s+\sqrt{C},&k=k_{+},&k_{+}-B>0\\
\left(\sqrt{C}+\frac{a}{2}\right)s+\sqrt{C},&k=k_{-},&k_{-}-B>0\\
\left|\sqrt{C}-\frac{a}{2}\right|s-\sqrt{C},&k=k_{+},&k_{+}-B<0\\
\left(\sqrt{C}+\frac{a}{2}\right)s-\sqrt{C},&k=k_{-},&k_{-}-B<0\end{array}\right\}.\end{array}

(51)

Since
\tau^{\prime}(s)<0
, for PT potential, we choose
k=k_{-}=B-2C-a\sqrt{C}
, where
k_{-}-B<0
, and select
\pi(s)
as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi(s)&=\left(\frac{1-a}{2}-\sqrt{C}\right)s+\sqrt{C}.\end{array}

(52)

Then, we find,

\begin{array}[]{l}\tau(s)=1+2\sqrt{C}-\left(2+a+2\sqrt{C}\right)s,\\
\rho(s)=s^{2\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{a},\\
\phi_{1}(s)=s^{\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{\frac{a-1}{2}},\end{array}

(53)

along with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{2,n_{r}}(s)&=s^{-2\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{-a}\frac{d^{n_{r}}}{ds^{n_{r}}}\left[s^{{n_{r}}+2\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{{n_{r}}+a}\right]=({n_{r}})!\,P_{n_{r}}^{(2\sqrt{C},a)}(1-2s).\end{array}

(54)

Then the ro-vibrational energy
E_{n_{r},l}^{PT}
of PT potential can be obtained from the following relation,

k+(2{n_{r}}+1)\pi^{\prime}(s)={n_{r}}^{2}+2{n_{r}}.

(55)

Accordingly, one gets the ro-vibrational energy spectrum, as function of
(\hbar,\mu),(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\alpha),(\lambda,\nu),(n_{r},l)
, as below,

E_{n_{r},l}^{PT}=x_{4}-\frac{\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}(n_{r}+L)^{2},

(56)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{4}&=-\frac{\xi_{1}}{2}+\frac{l(l+1)\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}{4\mu}\left(2x_{1}-x_{2}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\left[\frac{1}{4}+A+C\right],\\
L&=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{C}-\sqrt{1+A}.\end{array}

(57)

Therefore, the explicit form of radial wave function can be found to be
[
38
]
,

R_{n_{r}}^{PT}(r)=N_{n_{r},l}\,s^{\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{\frac{a-1}{2}}P_{n_{r}}^{(2\sqrt{C},a)}(1-2s),

(58)

where

\begin{array}[]{r}N_{n_{r},l}=\sqrt{\alpha}\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n_{r}}(-1)^{n_{r}-m}\binom{n_{r}+\gamma}{m}\binom{n_{r}+\delta}{n_{r}-m}\frac{\Gamma(n_{r}-m+\gamma+1)\Gamma(m+\delta+1)\Gamma(n_{r}+\gamma+\frac{3}{2})}{n_{r}!\Gamma(n_{r}+\gamma+\delta+2)\Gamma(n_{r}-m+\gamma+1)}\\
\displaystyle\times{}_{3}F_{2}(-n_{r},2n_{r}+1+\gamma+\delta,m+1+\delta;n_{r}-m+\gamma+1,n_{r}+\gamma+\delta+2;1)\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}

(59)

is the normalization constant determined from the following condition,

\displaystyle\int\left[R_{n_{r}}^{PT}(r)\right]^{2}\,dr=1,

(60)

where

\gamma=2\sqrt{C}-\frac{1}{2},~\delta=a-2.

(61)

The required eigenfunctions of PT potential are finally expressed as,

\displaystyle\psi_{n_{r},l,m}^{PT}({\bf r})=\left[\frac{(2l+1)(l-|m|)!}{4\pi(l+|m|)!}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{N_{n_{r},l}}{r}\,s^{\sqrt{C}}(1-s)^{\frac{a-1}{2}}P_{n_{r}}^{(2\sqrt{C},a)}(1-2s)\,P_{l}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,e^{im\phi},~s=\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha r}{2}\right).

(62)

It is to be noted that the approximation (
46
) is well defined
for
\lambda,\nu
satisfying,

\begin{array}[]{l}2\mu(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})+\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l(l+1)\left[(x_{21}+x_{31})\nu\lambda+(x_{22}+x_{32})\nu(1-\lambda)+(x_{23}+x_{33})(1-\nu)\right]>0,\\
(2x_{11}-x_{21}+x_{31})\nu\lambda+(2x_{12}-x_{22}+x_{32})\nu(1-\lambda)+(2x_{13}-x_{23}+x_{33})(1-\nu)>0\end{array}

(63)

6
Conclusions

In this article, we have introduced a new simple novel approximations to the centrifugal term for both MR and PT potentials. These are intuitively derived from a
linear combination of the commonly used Greene-Aldrich and Pekeris-type approximations. From this, the original approximations are recovered
for certain special values of the two approximating parameters
\lambda
and
\nu
. Approximate analytical expressions are then presented for these two
potentials by the NU method. It is gratifying to note that, the approximation perform quite nicely throughout the whole range of
r
,
whereas, Greene-Aldrich and Pekeris provide superior approximations near the origin
r=0
, and
r=r_{0}
(where the potential is minimum) respectively. Analytical expressions are
presented for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions.

An investigation of the controlling parameters,
\lambda
and
\nu
on energy spectra shows that,
E_{n_{r},l}^{MR}
is an increasing function of
\nu
subject to the conditions (
39
).
Whereas
E_{n_{r},l}^{PT}
is a increasing function of
\lambda
for
\nu<0
and a decreasing function of
\lambda
for
\nu>0
subject to the conditions (
63
).
For some special cases
(\lambda,\nu)=(1,1)
,
(\lambda,\nu)=(0,1)
, and
(\lambda,\nu)=(\lambda,0)
, energies of MR and PT potentials compare quite favorably
with available literature results. It may be worthwhile to study the performance and efficacy of this approach for other related potentials of
physical and chemical interest. Also its relevance in the thermodynamic studies may be pursued.

On subgeometric ergodicity of regime-switching diffusion processes
Petra Lazić, Nikola Sandrić

Abstract
. In this article, we discuss subgeometric ergodicity of a class of regime-switching diffusion processes. We derive conditions on the drift and diffusion coefficients, and the switching mechanism which result in subgeometric ergodicity of the corresponding semigroup with respect to the total variation distance as well as a class of Wasserstein distances. At the end, subgeometric ergodicity of certain classes of regime-switching Markov processes with jumps is also discussed.

In this article, we discuss subgeometric ergodicity of a regime-switching diffusion process

1.1.
Ergodicity of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

We recall some definitions and results from the ergodic theory of Markov processes. Our main references are
[
14
]
and
[
24
]
.
The process
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),

\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be

(i)

\upphi
-irreducible if there exists a
\sigma
-finite measure
\upphi
on

\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) such that whenever
\upphi(B)>0
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),B\times\{j\})\,\mathrm{d}t>0
for all
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
and
j\in\mathbb{S}

(ii)

transient if it is
\upphi
-irreducible, and if there exists a countable
covering of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sets

\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and for each

k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a finite constant
c_{k}\geq 0
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),B_{k}\times\{j\})\,\mathrm{d}{t}\leq c_{k}
holds for all
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
and
j\in\mathbb{S}

(iii)

recurrent if it is
\upphi
-irreducible, and
\upphi(B)>0
implies

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),B\times\{j\})\,\mathrm{d}{t}=\infty
for all
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
and
j\in\mathbb{S}
.

Let us remark that if
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is
\upphi
-irreducible, then the irreducibility measure
\upphi
can be
maximized, that is, there exists a unique “maximal” irreducibility
measure
\uppsi
such that for any measure
\bar{\upphi}
,

\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is
\bar{\upphi}
-irreducible if, and only if,

\bar{\upphi}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\uppsi
(see
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
).
In view to this, when we refer to an irreducibility
measure we actually refer to the maximal irreducibility measure.
It is also well known that every
\uppsi
-irreducible Markov
process is either transient or recurrent (see
[
24
, Theorem
2.3]
).
Further,
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be

(i)

open-set irreducible
if the support of its maximal irreducibility measure
\uppsi
,

{\rm supp}\,\uppsi\,=\,\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\uppsi(O)>0\ \text{for every open neighborhood}\ O\ \text{of}\ x\}\,,

has a non-empty interior

(ii)

aperiodic if it admits an irreducible skeleton chain, that is,
there exist
t_{0}>0
and a
\sigma
-finite measure
\upphi
on

\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
\upphi(B)>0
implies

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{p}(nt_{0},(x,i),B\times\{j\})>0
for all
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
and
j\in\mathbb{S}
.

For
t\geq 0
and a measure
\upmu
on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})

\upmu\mathcal{P}_{t}
stands for
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),\mathrm{d}y\times\{j\})\,\upmu(\mathrm{d}x\times\{i\}).
Observe that
\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t}=\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),\mathrm{d}y\times\{j\})
, where
\updelta_{(x,i)}
denotes the Dirac measure at
(x,i).

A
(not necessarily finite) measure
\uppi

\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})
is called invariant for

\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

\upmu\mathcal{P}_{t}=\upmu

for all
t\geq 0
.
It is well known that if
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is
recurrent, then it possesses a unique (up to constant
multiples) invariant measure
\uppi

(see
[
24
, Theorem 2.6]
).
If the
invariant measure is
finite, then it may be normalized to a probability measure. If

\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),
\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is recurrent with finite invariant measure, then it is called
positive recurrent; otherwise it is called null recurrent. It is easy to see that a transient
process cannot have a finite invariant measure.
Finally,
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be ergodic
if it possesses an invariant probability
measure
\uppi
and there exists a nondecreasing function

r:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}r(t)\lVert\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t}-\uppi\rVert_{{\rm TV}}\,=\,0

for all
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
, where
\lVert\upmu\rVert_{{\rm TV}}:=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})}|\upmu(B)|
is the total variation norm of a signed measure
\upmu
(on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})
).
We say that
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is sub-geometrically ergodic if it is ergodic and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\ln r(t)/t=0
, and
that it is geometrically ergodic if it is ergodic and

r(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa t}
for some
\kappa>0
.
Let us remark that (under the assumptions of
\mathcal{C}_{b}
-Feller property, open-set irreducibility
and aperiodicity) ergodicity is equivalent
to positive recurrence (see

[
14
, Theorem 6.1]
,
and
[
24
, Theorems 4.1, 4.2 and 7.1]
).

We now recall the notion and some general facts about Wasserstein distances (on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
). Let
\uprho
be a distance on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{B}_{\uprho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
induced by
\uprho
.
For
p\geq 0
let
\mathcal{P}_{\uprho,p}
be the space of all probability measures
\upmu
on
\mathfrak{B}_{\uprho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})
having finite
p
-th moment, that is,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}}\uprho((x,i),(y,j))^{p}\upmu(\mathrm{d}y\times\{j\})<\infty
for some (and then any)
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
.
For
p\geq 1
and
\upmu,\upnu\in\mathcal{P}_{\uprho,p}
, the
\mathcal{L}^{p}
-Wasserstein distance between
\upmu
and
\upnu
is defined as

\mathcal{W}_{\uprho,p}(\upmu,\upnu)\,\coloneqq\,\inf_{\Pi\in\mathcal{C}(\upmu,\upnu)}\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})}\uprho\bigl((x,i),(y,j)\bigr)^{p}\,\Pi\bigl(\mathrm{d}x\times\{i\},\mathrm{d}y\times\{j\}\bigr)\right)^{1/p}\,,

where
\mathcal{C}(\upmu,\upnu)
is the family of couplings of
\upmu
and
\upnu
, that is,
\Pi\in\mathcal{C}(\upmu,\upnu)
if, and only if,
\Pi
is a probability measure on
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S})
having
\upmu
and
\upnu
as its marginals. It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{W}_{\uprho,p}
satisfies the axioms of a (not necessarily finite) distance on
\mathcal{P}_{\uprho,p}
. The restriction of
\mathcal{W}_{\uprho,p}
to
\mathcal{P}_{\uprho,p}
defines a finite distance.
If
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S},\uprho)
is a complete (separable) metric space space, then it is well known that
(\mathcal{P}_{\rho,p},\mathcal{W}_{\uprho,p})
is also a complete (separable) metric space (see
[
25
, Theorem 6.18]
). Of our special interest will be the situation when
\uprho
takes the form

(1.2)

\uprho\bigl((x,i),(y,j)\bigr)\,=\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\neq j\}}(i,j)+f\bigl(|x-y|\bigr)

for some
non-decreasing concave
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

f(u)=0
if, and only if,
u=0
. In this situation, the corresponding Wasserstein space is denoted by
(\mathcal{P}_{f,p},\mathcal{W}_{f,p})
(which is always a complete metric space).
Observe that if
f(t)=\mathbb{1}_{(0,\infty)}(t)
, then
\mathcal{W}_{f,p}(\upmu,\upnu)=\rVert\upmu-\upnu\lVert_{{\rm TV}}
for all
p\geq 1
.
For more on Wasserstein distances we refer the readers to
[
25
]
.

1.2.
Main results

We now state the main results of this article. We first discuss subgeometric ergodicity of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
with respect to the total variation distance. Recall, a right-continuous temporally-homogeneous Markov chain
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{S}
given by state-independent generator
\mathcal{Q}
is irreducible if for any
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
,
i\neq j
, there are
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
k_{0},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{S}
with
k_{0}=i
,
k_{m}=j
and
k_{l}\neq k_{l+1}
for
l=0,\dots,m-1
, such that

\mathrm{q}_{k_{l}k_{l+1}}>0
for all
l=0,\dots,m-1
. Due to finiteness of
\mathbb{S}
, it is well known that
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is then geometrically ergodic. Let
\{\bar{\Lambda}(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be an independent copy of
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Put

\tau_{ij}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\{t>0\colon\Lambda(i;t)\,=\,\bar{\Lambda}(j;t)\}\,,&i\neq j\,,\\
0\,,&i=j\,,\end{array}\right.

and
\zeta\coloneqq\inf_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=j)
. Observe that
0<\zeta<1
(recall that
\mathbb{S}
is finite and
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is irreducible).
Define
\vartheta\coloneqq-\log(1-\zeta)
. It holds that

(1.3)

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t)\,\leq\,\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t\rfloor}

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
and
t\geq 0
(see

4.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

The method itself consists of finding an appropriate recurrent (petite) set

Standard and crucial assumption ensuring open-set irreducibility and aperiodicity of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
used in the literature is uniform ellipticity of the matrix
\upsigma(x,i)\upsigma(x,i)^{T}
(see
[
11
]
and the references therein). In

we relax this assumption and show that
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
will be open-set irreducible and aperiodic if
\upsigma(x,i)\upsigma(x,i)^{T}
is uniformly elliptic on an open ball only, while on the rest of the state space it can degenerate.
In the case when
\upsigma(x,i)\upsigma(x,i)^{T}
is highly degenerated (for example, it completely vanishes), the topology induced by the total variation distance becomes too “rough”, that is, it cannot completely capture the singular behavior of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
, and
\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),\mathrm{d}y\times\{j\})
cannot converge to the underlying invariant probability measure (if it exists) in this topology, but in a weaker sense. Therefore, in this situation, we naturally resort to Wasserstein distances which, in a certain sense, induce a finer topology (see
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
).

\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

1.3.
Literature review

Our work relates to the active research on ergodicity properties of Markov processes, with focus on regime-switching diffusion processes. This type of processes have a significant role in modeling of many phenomena arising in nature and engineering (for instance, see
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
).
At first glance one may think that these processes behave like the standard diffusion processes. However, the underlying regime-switching component can considerably influence some of their properties. In
[
17
]
it is shown
that even if in every fixed regime
i\in\mathbb{S}
the corresponding diffusion process is positive recurrent (transient), the overall regime-switching diffusion can be transient (positive recurrent).
This shows that the behavior of these type of processes
depends on an (sometime very nontrivial) interplay between both (continuous-state and regime-switching) components.

By using the classical Foster-Lyapunov method for geometric ergodicity of Markov processes,
in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
29
]

geometric ergodicity with respect to the total variation distance of regime-switching diffusions is established. In this article, we employ the Foster-Lyapunov method for subgeometric ergodicity of Markov processes developed in
[
6
]
and obtain conditions ensuring subgeometric ergodicity of this class of processes. Furthermore, we adapt these results and also discuss subgeometric ergodicity of a class of regime-switching Markov processes obtained through the Bochner’s subordination.

As mentioned above, the studies on ergodicity properties with respect to the total variation distance assume that the underlying process is irreducible and aperiodic. This is satisfied if the process does not show a singular behavior in its motion, for example, if its diffusion part is non-singular. For Markov processes that do not converge in total variation, ergodic properties under Wasserstein distances are studied since this distance function, in a certain sense, induces a finner topology (see
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
).
In
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
23
]
the coupling approach together with the Foster-Lyapunov method is employed to establish geometric contractivity and ergodicity of the semigroup of a regime-switching diffusion with respect to a Wasserstein distance.
In this article, by using the ideas developed in
[
12
]
, in the context of diffusion processes, and combining the asymptotic flatness conditions in
eqs.

1.4
and
1.7
and the coupling method, we obtain subgeometric contractivity and ergodicity of the semigroup of a regime-switching diffusion, with possibly singular diffusion coefficient, with respect to a Wasserstein distance. At the end, we again discuss subgeometric ergodicity, but with respect to Wasserstein distances, of a class of regime-switching diffusions with jumps and a class of regime-switching Markov processes obtained through the Bochner’s subordination.
These results are related to
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
where geometric ergodicity of regime-switching diffusions with jumps is established with respect to the total variation distance.

i\in\mathbb{S}_{>}^{c}.

Define

\mathrm{b}(x,i)\,\coloneqq\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-b_{1}(x,i)x\,,&i\in\mathbb{S}_{>}\,,\\
b_{2}(x,i)\,,&i\in\mathbb{S}_{>}^{c}\,,\end{array}\right.

and
\upsigma(x,i)\coloneqq\sigma(x,i)\,\mathbb{I}_{d}
, where
\mathbb{I}_{d}
stands for the
d\times d
identity matrix. It is clear that such a regime-switching diffusion process satisfies
(
\overline{\textbf{A4}}
)
with
x_{0}=0
and any
0<r_{0}<\min_{i\in\mathbb{S}_{>}}r_{i}.

3.
Ergodicity with respect to the total variation distance

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
\beta\coloneqq-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}c_{i}\uplambda_{i}>0
. Clearly,

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}(c_{i}+\beta)\,\uplambda_{i}\,=\,0\,.

From
[
29
, Lemma A.12]
it then follows that the system

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{q}_{ij}\gamma_{j}\,=\,-c_{i}-\beta\,,\qquad i\in\mathbb{S}\,,

admits a solution
(\gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}
.
Let
m>\max\{\beta,2\}
and let
\mathcal{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}\to[1,\infty)
be twice continuously differentiable (in the first coordinate) and such that

\mathcal{V}(x,i)\,=\,\frac{m}{\beta}\bigl(\mathsf{V}(x)+\gamma_{i}\,\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)\bigr)

for all
i\in\mathbb{S}
and all
|x|
large enough. Observe that the assumptions in (i) ensure existence of such a function.
For all
i\in\mathbb{S}
and all
|x|
large enough, we now have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{V}(x,i)

\displaystyle\,=\,\frac{m}{\beta}\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathsf{V}(x)+\frac{m\gamma_{i}}{\beta}\mathcal{L}_{i}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)+\frac{m}{\beta}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)\,\mathcal{Q}(x)\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\frac{mc_{i}}{\beta}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)+\frac{m\gamma_{i}}{\beta}\mathcal{L}_{i}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)+\frac{m}{\beta}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{q}_{ij}\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{i}\mathrm{o}(1)\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,\left(c_{i}+\gamma_{i}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{i}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}{\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}-c_{i}-\beta+\gamma_{i}\mathrm{o}(1)\right)\frac{m}{\beta}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)

\displaystyle\,=\,\left(\gamma_{i}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{i}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}{\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}-\beta+\gamma_{i}\mathrm{o}(1)\right)\frac{m}{\beta}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)\,,

where in the second line we use assumption (ii).
By assumption, for all
|x|
large enough it holds that

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\left|\gamma_{i}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{i}\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}{\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)}+\gamma_{i}\mathrm{o}(1)\right|\,<\,\frac{\beta}{m}\,.

Thus, for all
i\in\mathbb{S}
and all
|x|
large enough,

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{V}(x,i)\,\leq\,-(m-1)\,\theta\circ\mathsf{V}(x)\,\leq\,-(m-1)\,\theta\bigl(\mathcal{V}(x,i)/(m-1)\bigr)\,\leq\,-\theta\circ\mathcal{V}(x,i)\,,

where in the last step we employed the subadditivity property of
\theta(u)
. The assertion of the theorem now follows from

[
24
, Theorems 5.1 and 7.1]
(which ensure that every compact set is petite set for
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
) and
[
6
, Theorems 3.2 and 3.4]
.
∎

In the next proposition we show that if
\theta(u)
is linear, then
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;u))\}_{t\geq 0}
is geometrically ergodic.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

be an open-set irreducible and aperiodic regime-switching diffusion process satisfying

(A1)
-
(A5)
. Assume

(i)

there are
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\subset\mathbb{R}
and twice continuously differentiable
\mathsf{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(1,\infty)
,
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathsf{V}(x)}{\mathsf{V}(x)}\,<\,c_{i}\,,

(ii)

\mathcal{Q}(x)=\mathcal{Q}+\mathrm{o}(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
,

and either one of the following two conditions

(iii)

\mathcal{Q}=(\mathrm{q}_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}
is the infinitesimal generator of an irreducible right-continuous temporally-homogeneous Markov chain on
\mathbb{S}
with invariant probability measure by
\uplambda=(\uplambda_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}c_{i}\uplambda_{i}<0
.

(iii’)

the matrix
-(\mathcal{Q}+\mathrm{diag}\,c)
is a nonsingular
\mathcal{M}
-matrix, where
c=(c_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}.

Then,
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

is geometrically ergodic.

Proof.

Assume first (i)-(iii). Analogously as in
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
we conclude that there are
\zeta\in(0,1)
,
\eta>0
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}
with strictly positive components, such that

(\mathcal{Q}+\zeta\,\mathrm{diag}\,c)\gamma\,=\,-\eta\,\gamma\,.

Define
\mathcal{V}(x,i)\coloneqq\gamma_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x).
Since
\zeta\in(0,1)
, it is straightforward to check that

\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)\,\leq\,\zeta\,\mathsf{V}^{\zeta-1}(x)\,\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathsf{V}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i\in\mathbb{S}
. Thus, for all
i\in\mathbb{S}
and
|x|
large enough, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{V}(x,i)

\displaystyle=\,\gamma_{i}\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)+\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)\,\mathcal{Q}(x)\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\,\zeta\,c_{i}\gamma_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)+\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{q}_{ij}\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)\mathrm{o}(1)

\displaystyle=\,-\eta\,\gamma_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)+\gamma_{i}\mathsf{V}^{\zeta}(x)\mathrm{o}(1)

\displaystyle\leq\,-\eta\,\mathcal{V}(x,i)+\mathcal{V}(x,i)\mathrm{o}(1)\,,

which is exactly the Lyapunov equation on

[
15
, page 529]

with
c=\eta-\epsilon
(
0<\epsilon<\eta
is such that
|\mathrm{o}(1)|<\epsilon
for all
|x|
large enough),
f(x,i)=\mathcal{V}(x,i)
,
C=\overline{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{S}
for
r>0
large enough and
d=\sup_{(x,i)\in C}|\mathcal{L}\mathcal{V}(x,i)|
. According to
[
24

, together with open-set irreducibility and
\mathcal{C}_{b}
-Feller property of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
, it follows that
C
is a petite set for
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
.
Consequently, from

[
14
, Proposition 6.1]
,
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
and
aperiodicity it follows now that there are a petite set
\mathcal{C}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})
,
T>0
and a non-trivial measure
\upnu_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S})
, such that
\upnu_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C})>0
and

\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),B)\,\geq\,\upnu_{\mathcal{C}}(B)

for all
(x,i)\in\mathcal{C}
,

and
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{S}).
In particular,

\mathsf{p}(t,(x,i),\mathcal{C})\,>\,0

for all
(x,i)\in\mathcal{C}
and
t\geq T
, which is exactly the definition of aperiodicity used on
[
7
, page 1675]
. Finally, observe that
eq.

3.1
is also the Lyapunov equation used on
[
7
, page 1679]
with
c=\eta-\epsilon
(
\epsilon
is defined as above),
\tilde{V}(x,i)=\mathcal{V}(x,i)
,

.

Assume now (i), (ii) and (iii’).
Since
-(\mathcal{Q}+\mathrm{diag}\,c)
is a nonsingular

-matrix, there is a vector
\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}

Define

. Analogously as above we see that for all

,

4.
Ergodicity with respect to Wasserstein distances

In this section, we discuss subgeometric ergodicity of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

with respect to a class of Wasserstein distances. In addition to
(A1)
-
(A5)
, throughout the section we assume

•

\{\Lambda(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\upsigma(x,i)
are state independent, that is,
\Lambda(x,i;t)=\Lambda(i;t)
and
\upsigma(x,i)=\upsigma(i)
for all
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
and
t\geq 0
;

•

\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is irreducible.

We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{S}
,
\tau\geq 0
,
\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\subset(-\infty,0]
,
F:[0,T)\to[0,\infty)
with
0<T\leq\infty
, and
\psi:[0,+\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be such that

(i)

F(t)
is absolutely continuous on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
for any
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<T
;

(ii)

F^{\prime}(t)\leq\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+t)}\psi(F(t))
a.e. on
[0,T)
;

(iii)

\Psi_{F(0)}(t)\coloneqq\int_{t}^{F(0)}\mathrm{d}s/\psi(s)<\infty
for all
t\in(0,F(0)]
.

Then

F(t)\,\leq\,\Psi_{F(0)}^{-1}\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)

for all
t\in[0,T)
such that
-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+s)}\mathrm{d}s<\Psi_{F(0)}(0).
In addition, if
there is
\gamma\in[F(0),\infty]
such that

\Psi_{\gamma}(t)=\int_{t}^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}s/\psi(s)<\infty
for all
t\in(0,\gamma]
, then

F(t)\,\leq\,\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)

for all
t\in[0,T)
such that
0\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+s)}\mathrm{d}s<\Psi_{\gamma}(0).

Furthermore, if
\psi(t)
is convex and vanishes at zero, then
\Psi_{F(0)}(0)=\infty.
In particular, the previous relations hold for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

We have,

-\Psi_{F(0)}(F(t))\,=\,\int_{F(0)}^{f(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\psi(s)}\,=\,\int_{0}^{t}\frac{F^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s}{\psi(F(s))}\,\leq\,\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau+s)}\mathrm{d}s

for all
t\in[0,T)
,
which proves the first assertion.
The second claim follows from the fact that
\Psi_{F(0)}(t)\leq\Psi_{\gamma}(t)
for all
t\in(0,F(0)]
, while
the last part follows from the convexity of
\psi(t)
:

\psi(t)\,=\,\psi(t+(1-t)0)\,\leq\,t\,\psi(1)+(1-t)\,\psi(0)\,=\,t\,\psi(1)

for all
t\in[0,1]
.
∎

We next prove
eq.

1.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Recall that
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is irreducible,
\{\bar{\Lambda}(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is its independent copy,

\tau_{ij}\,=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\{t>0\colon\Lambda(i;t)\,=\,\bar{\Lambda}(j;t)\}\,,&i\neq j\,,\\
0\,,&i=j\,,\end{array}\right.

\zeta=\inf_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=j)
and
\vartheta=-\log(1-\zeta)
. Then,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t)\,\leq\,\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t\rfloor}.

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
and
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, observe that for any initial distribution
\upmu=(\upmu_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{S}}
of
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and all
j\in\mathbb{S}
it holds that

(4.1)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=j)\,\upmu_{i}\,\geq\,\zeta\,.

Thus, for any initial distribution
\Pi=(\Pi_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}
of
\{(\Lambda(i;t),\bar{\Lambda}(j;t))\}_{t\geq 0}

we have that

(4.2)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;1))\,\Pi_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i,1)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;1),\,\Lambda(i;1)=k)\,\Pi_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}}\big(\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;1),\,\Lambda(i;1)=k)+\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=\bar{\Lambda}(j;1),\,\Lambda(i;1)=k)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=\bar{\Lambda}(j;1),\,\Lambda(i;1)=k)\big)\,\Pi_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=k)\,\Pi_{i,j}-\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=k,\,\Lambda(j;1)=k)\,\Pi_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\,1-\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{S}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;1)=k)\mathbb{P}(\bar{\Lambda}(j;1)=k)\,\Pi_{i,j}

\displaystyle\leq\,1-\zeta\,.

Let

t\geq 1
be arbitrary. Then,
t=\lfloor t\rfloor+s
for some
s\in[0,1)
. We have that

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t)\,=\,\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t,\,\tau_{ij}>t-1)\,\leq\,\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;t)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;t),\,\tau_{ij}>t-1)\,.

Observe that
\{\tau_{ij}>t-1\}\in\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1}
. Here,
\{\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
stands for the natural filtration of the process
\{(\Lambda(i;t),\bar{\Lambda}(j;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
. This and
eq.

4.2
imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;t)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;t),\,\tau_{ij}>t-1)

\displaystyle\,=\,\int_{\{\tau_{ij}>t-1\}}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(i;t)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(j;t)\mid\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{t-1})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\,=\,\int_{\{\tau_{ij}>t-1\}}\mathbb{P}\left(\Lambda(\Lambda(i;t-1);1)\neq\bar{\Lambda}(\bar{\Lambda}(j;t-1);1)\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,(1-\zeta)\,\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t-1)\,.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t)\,\leq\,(1-\zeta)\,\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t-1)\,.

Iterating this procedure we arrive at

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{ij}>t)\,\leq\,(1-\zeta)^{\lfloor t\rfloor}\,=\,\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t\rfloor}

for all
t\geq 1
.
However, it is clear that the relation holds for all
t\geq 0.

∎

We now prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\{\bar{\Lambda}(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be an independent copy of
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
(which is also independent of
\{\mathsf{B}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
). Define

\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t)\,\coloneqq\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{\Lambda}(j;t)\,,&t<\tau_{ij}\,,\\
\Lambda(i;t)\,,&t\geq\tau_{ij}\,,\end{array}\right.

for
t\geq 0
.
By employing Markov property, it is easy to see that
\{\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t)\}_{t\geq 0}

is a Markov chain with the same law as
\{\Lambda(j;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Fix now
(x,i),(y,j)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}
, and let
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{(\mathsf{X}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
be the corresponding solutions to
eq.

1.1
. Define

\tau\,\coloneqq\,\inf\{t>0\colon(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t))=(\mathsf{X}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\}\,.

Clearly,
\tau\geq\tau_{ij}.

Put

\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)\,\coloneqq\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathsf{X}(y,j;t)\,,&t<\tau\,,\\
\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)\,,&t\geq\tau\,,\end{array}\right.

for
t\geq 0
.
The marginals of the process

\{(\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
have the same law as the marginals of
\{(\mathsf{X}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
. Namely, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\in B\times\{k\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\in B\times\{k\},\,t<\tau\bigr)+\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\in B\times\{k\},\,t\geq\tau\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\in B\times\{k\},t<\tau\bigr)+\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t))\in B\times\{k\},\,t\geq\tau\bigr)\,.

Further, by the strong Markov property we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t))\in B\times\{k\},\,t\geq\tau\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t-\tau+\tau\geq\tau\}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{B\times\{k\}}((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t-\tau+\tau),\Lambda(i;t-\tau+\tau))\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq\tau\}}\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau),\Lambda(i;\tau);t-\tau),\Lambda(\Lambda(i;\tau);t-\tau))\in B\times\{k\},\,t-\tau\geq 0\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{t\geq\tau\}}\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{X}(y,i;\tau),\tilde{\Lambda}(i;\tau);t-\tau),\tilde{\Lambda}(\tilde{\Lambda}(i;\tau);t-\tau))\in B\times\{k\},\,t-\tau\geq 0\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{P}\bigl((\mathsf{X}(y,i;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(i;t))\in B\times\{k\},\,t\geq\tau\bigr)\,,

which proves the assertion.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{p}_{f,p}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)\,\leq\,\mathbb{E}\left[\uprho\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t)),(\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\bigr)^{p}\right]\,.

Now, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{p}_{f,p}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\mathbb{E}\left[\uprho\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t)),(\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\bigr)^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}>t/2\}}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\mathbb{E}\left[\uprho\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t)),(\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\bigr)^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/2\}}\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,(1+\gamma)^{p}\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t/2\rfloor}+\mathbb{E}\left[f\bigl(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|\bigr)^{p}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/2\}}\right]\,,

where in the last step
\gamma\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}f(t)
and we employed
Lemma

4.2
.
After time
\tau_{ij}
the processes
\{\Lambda(i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
move together. Hence, by
eq.

1.4
it holds that (here we also use the fact that
\upsigma(x,i)=\upsigma(i)
)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f\bigl(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|\bigr)\,\leq\,0

a.e. on
[\tau_{ij},+\infty)
. Thus,
t\mapsto f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|)
is non-increasing on
[\tau_{ij},\infty)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{f,p}^{p}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,(1+\gamma)^{p}\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t/2\rfloor}+\mathbb{E}\left[f\bigl(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij}+t/2)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij}+t/2)|\bigr)^{p}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/2\}}\right]\,.

Define

F(t)\,\coloneqq\,f\bigl(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij}+t)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij}+t)|\bigr)

for
t\geq 0
.
By employing
eq.

1.4
again, it follows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F(t)\,\leq\,\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau_{ij}+t)}\psi\bigl(F(t)\bigr)

a.e. on
[0,\tau-\tau_{ij})
.
By
Lemma

4.1
we have that

F(t)\,\leq\,\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau_{ij}+s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\,=\,\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)

on
[0,\infty)
.
For
t\geq\tau
the term on the left-hand side vanishes, and the term on the right-hand side is well defined and strictly positive (
\psi(u)
is convex and
\psi(u)=0
if, and only if,
u=0
). Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{f,p}^{p}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)\,\leq\,(1+\gamma)^{p}\mathrm{e}^{-\vartheta\lfloor t/2\rfloor}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{p}\right]\,.

Birkhoff ergodic theorem implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{2}{t}\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\lambda_{i}\,<\,0\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Hence, since
\psi(u)
is convex and
\psi(u)=0
if, and only if,
u=0
,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\,=\,0

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. This, together with dominated convergence theorem, shows the first assertion.

Assume now that
\psi(u)=u^{q}
for
q>1
. Then,

\Psi_{\gamma}(t)\,=\,\frac{1}{q-1}\bigl(t^{1-q}-\gamma^{1-q}\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}(t)\,=\,\bigl(\gamma^{1-q}+(q-1)t\bigr)^{1/(1-q)}\,.

By employing Birkhoff ergodic theorem and Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{p}\right]}{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(1-q)}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\gamma^{1-q}+(1-q)\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/(1-q)}}{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(1-q)}}\right]

\displaystyle\,\geq\,1\,.

On the other side,
Birkhoff ergodic theorem, Fatou’s lemma
and Jensen’s inequality imply

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(1-q)}}{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{p}\right]}

\displaystyle\,=\,\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\gamma^{1-q}+(1-q)\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/(1-q)}}{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(1-q)}}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\liminf_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left(\gamma^{q-1}+(1-q)\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p/(1-q)}}{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(q-1)}}\right]

\displaystyle\,=\,1\,.

Thus,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{p}\right]}{\left(\frac{(1-q)t}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\right)^{p/(1-q)}}\,=\,1\,,

which proves the second relation.

If
\psi(u)=\kappa u
for
\kappa>0
, then

\Psi_{\gamma}(t)\,=\,\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\left(\frac{\gamma}{t}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}(t)\,=\,\gamma\,\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t}\,.

Arguing as above, it holds that for any
0<\beta<-\kappa\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}
,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Psi_{\gamma}^{-1}\left(-\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{t/2+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{p}\right]}{\mathrm{e}^{-p\beta t/2}}\,=\,0\,.

Hence, by taking
0<\alpha<\min\{\vartheta/p,-\kappa\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\uplambda_{i}\}
the assertion follows.
∎

We next prove
Theorem

1.3
. First, we discuss conditions under which the process
\{\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
has second moment.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
eq.

1.6
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\bigr]\,\leq\ \bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{Kt}\,.

Furthermore, for any
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-stopping time
\tau
such that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\bigr]<\infty
it follows that

\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau)|^{2}\right]\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Proof.

Recall that
\{\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{b}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)\,.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\tau_{n}\,\coloneqq\,\inf\{t\geq 0:|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|\geq n\}\,.

By employing Itô’s formula we conclude that

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle\,=\,|x|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\bigl\langle\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\mathrm{b}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\bigr\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)^{T}\bigr)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(x,i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\tau_{n}]}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(x,i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\leq|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s\wedge\tau_{n})|^{2}\bigr)\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(x,i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)\,.

Since the last term on the right side is a martingale, we conclude that

1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau_{n})|^{2}\right]\,\leq\,1+|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s\wedge\tau_{n})|^{2}\right]\bigr)\mathrm{d}s\,.

The first assertion now follows by employing Grönwall’s inequality and Fatou’s lemma (observe that since
\{\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is nonexplosive,
\mathbb{P}(\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}=\infty)=1
).

Assume now that
\tau
is a stopping time such that
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}]<\infty
. By employing Itô’s lemma again we have that

|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+Kt+K\int_{0}^{t}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)\,.

Grönwall’s inequality then gives

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+Kt+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t}K\left(|x|^{2}+Ks+2\int_{0}^{s}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;u)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right)\mathrm{e}^{K(t-s)}\mathrm{d}s\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau)|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+K(t\wedge\tau)+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}K\left(|x|^{2}+Ks+2\int_{0}^{s}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;u)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right)\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,=\,|x|^{2}+K(t\wedge\tau)+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +|x|^{2}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{Kt\wedge\tau}-1\bigr)+\mathrm{e}^{Kt\wedge\tau}-K(t\wedge\tau)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +2K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\int_{0}^{s}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;u)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +2K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\int_{0}^{s}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;u)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Taking expectation (and the previous result) we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau)|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +2K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\int_{0}^{s}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;u)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +2K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{2K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)^{T}\upsigma(\Lambda(i;u))\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(u)\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+2K\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-Ks}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t\wedge\tau]}(s)\,|\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+2K\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-Ks}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+2K\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-Ks/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,=\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\,,

where in the third step we used Itô’s isometry and in the fifth step we used the first assertion of the lemma.
∎

.

Proof of

1.3
.

Theorem

1.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{f,1}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)\,\leq\,\mathbb{E}\left[\uprho\bigl((\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t)),(\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t),\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t))\bigr)\right]\,.

Further, for

(such

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{\{\Lambda(i;t)\neq\tilde{\Lambda}(j;t)\}}+f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|)\bigr)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}>t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\mathbb{E}\left[\bigl(1+f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|)+f(|\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|)\bigr)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-(\vartheta/2)\lfloor t/(1+\varepsilon)\rfloor}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,3^{1/2}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[f(|\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-(\vartheta/2)\lfloor t/(1+\varepsilon)\rfloor}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,C_{1}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathrm{Y}(y,j;t)|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-(\vartheta/2)\lfloor t/(1+\varepsilon)\rfloor}

for some

. Here, in the third step we used subadditivity property of concave functions and

, in the fifth step we used the fact that

for some

(

is concave), and in the last step we used

4.3
. Clearly, the first term on the right-hand side will converge to zero (as

goes to infinity) due to the choice of

.

1.2
, by employing

1.7

a.e. on

. Thus,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{f,1}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathbb{E}\left[F(\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\right]\,,

where

)
by

for

.
We now have that

(4.3)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{f,1}\bigl(\updelta_{(x,i)}\mathcal{P}_{t},\updelta_{(y,j)}\mathcal{P}_{t}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\,C_{2}\left(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{Kt/2-(\vartheta/2)\lfloor t/(1+\varepsilon)\rfloor}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[F(\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|)\leq\eta\}}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[F(\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|)>\eta\}}\right]\,.

On the event

we have the following. By employing

1.7
again, it follows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F(t)\,\leq\,\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;\tau_{ij}+t)}\psi\bigl(F(t)\bigr)

a.e. on

.

now implies that

For

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|)\leq\eta\}}\right]

Birkhoff ergodic theorem implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon t}\int_{\tau_{ij}}^{\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon)+\tau_{ij}}\Gamma_{\Lambda(i;s)}\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}}\Gamma_{i}\lambda_{i}\,<\,0

\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}
on

.

,

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. This, together with dominated convergence theorem, shows that

(4.4)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[F(\varepsilon t/(1+\varepsilon))\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{ij}\leq t/(1+\varepsilon)\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|)\leq\eta\}}\right]\,=\,0\,.

On the event
\{f(|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|)>\eta\}
we proceed as follows.
Recall that
\delta=\inf\{t\geq 0\colon f(1/t)\leq\eta\}
. It clearly must hold that
\delta>0
. Thus, since for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\lceil\delta|x-y|\rceil\geq\delta|x-y|
, we have that

f\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\lceil\delta|x-y|\rceil}\right)\,\leq\,f(1/\delta)\,\leq\,\eta\,.

Let
z_{0},\dots,z_{\lceil\delta|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})-\mathrm{Y}(y,j;\tau_{ij})|\rceil}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
z_{0}\coloneqq\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau_{ij})
and

5.
Ergodicity of some regime-switching Markov processes with jumps

In this section, we briefly discuss ergodicity properties of a class of regime-switching Markov processes with jumps.

One of the most common approaches in obtaining Markov processes with jumps (from a given Markov process) is through the Bochner’s subordination method.
Among the most interesting examples are the rotationally invariant stable Lévy processes, which can be viewed as subordinate Brownian motions.
Recall, a subordinator
\{\mathsf{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a non-decreasing Lévy process on
\left[0,\infty\right)
with Laplace transform

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-u\mathsf{S}(t)}\right]\,=\,\mathrm{e}^{-t\phi(u)}\,.

The characteristic (Laplace) exponent
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a Bernstein function, that is, it is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and
(-1)^{n}\phi^{(n)}(u)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is well known that every Bernstein function admits a unique (Lévy-Khintchine) representation

\phi(u)\,=\,\beta u+\int_{(0,\infty)}\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-uy}\bigr)\,\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\,,

where
\beta\geq 0
is the drift parameter and
\upnu
is a Lévy measure, that is, a measure on
\mathfrak{B}((0,\infty))
satisfying
\int_{(0,\infty)}(1\wedge y)\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
.
For more on subordinators and Bernstein functions we refer the readers to the monograph
[
19
]
.
Let now
\{\mathsf{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinator with characteristic exponent
\phi(u)
, independent of
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
.
The process
(\mathsf{X}^{\phi}(x,i;t),\Lambda^{\phi}(x,i;t))\coloneqq(\mathsf{X}(x,i;\mathsf{S}(t)),\Lambda(x,i;\mathsf{S}(t)))
,
t\geq 0

\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
by
a random time change through
\{\mathsf{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, is referred to as the subordinate process
\{(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t),\Lambda(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
with subordinator
\{\mathsf{S}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in the sense of Bochner. It is known that many fine properties of Markov processes (and the corresponding semigroups) are preserved under subordination. It is easy to see that
\{(\mathsf{X}^{\phi}(x,i;t),\Lambda^{\phi}(x,i;t))\}_{t\geq 0}
is again a Markov process with transition kernel

is the transition probability of
\mathsf{S}(t)
,
t\geq 0

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(i)\gamma\upsigma(i)^{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)\bigr)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(f(x+y)-f(x)-\langle y,\nabla f(x)\rangle\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(0)}(y)\bigr)\upnu_{i}(\mathrm{d}y)

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|y|^{2}\vee|y|^{4})\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
(or, equivalentely,
\mathbb{E}[|\mathsf{L}_{t}|^{4}]<\infty
for all
t\geq 0
) and

(5.2)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\bigl\langle x,\mathrm{b}(x,i)+\upsigma(i)\beta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\upsigma(i)y\bigl(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\upsigma(i)y\bigr)-\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(0)}(y)\bigr)\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\bigl\rangle

\displaystyle+{\rm Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(i)\gamma\upsigma(i)^{T}\bigr)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{2}\upnu_{i}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\leq\,K(1+|x|^{2})\,.

Then,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\bigr]\,\leq\ \bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{Kt}\,.

Furthermore, for any
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
-stopping time
\tau
such that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\bigr]<\infty
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;\tau)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\Bigg(4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)+\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{4}\nu(\mathrm{d}y)/K

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)+4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{3}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\Bigg)^{1/2}\,.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
be such that
f_{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(the space of bounded and twice continuously differentiable functions with bounded first and second order derivatives),
f_{n}(x)=|x|^{2}
on
\mathscr{B}_{n+1}(0)
and
f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n+1}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\tau_{n}\,\coloneqq\,\inf\{t\geq 0:|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|\geq n\}\,.

Further, for
t>0
and
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote

\mathrm{N}((0,t],B)\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{0<s\leq t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(\mathsf{L}(s)-\mathsf{L}(s-))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}y)\,\coloneqq\,\mathrm{N}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)-\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}s\,.

By employing Itô’s formula and the assumption that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|y|^{2}\vee|y|^{4})\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
we conclude that for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau_{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\,=\,f_{n}(x)+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\Bigg(\Big\langle\nabla f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr),\mathrm{b}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)+\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\beta

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)y\bigl(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)y\bigr)-\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(0)}(y)\bigr)\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\bigl(\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\gamma\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)^{T}\nabla^{2}f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)\bigr)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)+y\bigr)-f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)-\bigl\langle y,\nabla f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)\bigr\rangle\right)\upnu_{\Lambda(i;s)}(\mathrm{d}y)\Bigg)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\nabla f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigr(f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)+\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y\bigr)-f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)\bigr)\bigl)\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\,=\,|x|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\Bigg(\Big\langle\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\mathrm{b}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)+\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\beta

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)y\bigl(\mathbb{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)y\bigr)-\mathbb{1}_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(0)}(y)\bigr)\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +{\rm Tr}\bigl(\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\gamma\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)^{T}\bigr)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{2}\upnu_{\Lambda(i;s)}(\mathrm{d}y)\Bigg)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\nabla f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigr(f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)+\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y\bigr)-f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)\bigr)\bigl)\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{[0,\tau_{n}]}(s)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\nabla f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)\bigr)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigr(f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)+\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y\bigr)-f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)\bigr)\bigl)\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}s)\,,

where in the last step we used
eq.

5.2
.
By taking expectation, we have that

\displaystyle 1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{[0,\tau_{n})}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,1+\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}\bigl(\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau_{n})\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,1+|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\right]\mathbb{1}_{[0,\tau_{n}]}(s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,=\,1+|x|^{2}+K\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\mathbb{1}_{[0,\tau_{n})}(s)\right]\bigr)\mathrm{d}s\,.

The first assertion now follows by employing Grönwall’s inequality and Fatou’s lemma.

Let now
\tau
be a stopping time such that
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}]<\infty
. Itô’s lemma then gives

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}\,\leq\,

\displaystyle|x|^{2}+Kt+K\int_{0}^{t}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigr(|\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y|^{2}+2\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y\bigl)\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}s)\,.

Denote

\displaystyle\alpha(t)\,\coloneqq\,

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{X}(x,i;s)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathsf{B}(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigr(|\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y|^{2}+2\mathsf{X}(x,i;s-)^{T}\upsigma\bigl(\Lambda(i;s-)\bigr)y\bigl)\tilde{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}s)\,.

Grönwall’s inequality then gives

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+Kt+\alpha(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K\left(|x|^{2}+Ks+\alpha(s)\right)\mathrm{e}^{K(t-s)}\mathrm{d}s\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau)|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+K(t\wedge\tau)+\alpha(t\wedge\tau)+|x|^{2}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{Kt\wedge\tau}-1\bigr)+\mathrm{e}^{Kt\wedge\tau}-K(t\wedge\tau)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\alpha(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\alpha(t\wedge\tau)+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}+K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\alpha(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Taking expectation we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathsf{X}(x,i;t\wedge\tau)|^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\alpha(s)\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]+K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\tau]}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{2K(t\wedge\tau-s)}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\alpha(s)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +K\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-Ks}\Bigg(4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{2}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{4}\nu(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{3}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s}|\mathsf{X}(x,i;u)|\mathrm{d}u\right]\Bigg)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +K\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-Ks}\Bigg(4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{Ks}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{2}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{4}\nu(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{Ks}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ 4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{3}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)\mathrm{e}^{Ks/2}\Bigg)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\,\leq\,|x|^{2}+\bigl(1+|x|^{2}\bigr)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{K\tau}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2K\tau}\right]^{1/2}\Bigg(4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)^{2}+\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{4}\nu(\mathrm{d}y)/K

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{2}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(j)\upsigma(j)^{T}\bigr)^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{3}\upnu(\mathrm{d}y)\bigr(1+|x|\bigl)\Bigg)^{1/2}\,,

where in the third step we used Itô’s isometry and in the fourth step we used the first assertion of the lemma.
∎

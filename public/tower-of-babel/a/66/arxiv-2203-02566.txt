1.
Introduction

In
[
DL13
]
and
[
DL21
]
, Davis and Lück study groups of the form
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\rho}\mathbb{Z}/p
where
p
is an odd prime and
\rho:\mathbb{Z}/p\rightarrow\operatorname{{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
has no nonzero fixed points.
They compute the topological
K
-theory of the real and complex group
C^{*}
-algebras of
\Gamma
in
[
DL13
]
.
Along the way, they compute
K^{*}(B\Gamma)
and several other
K
-theory groups.
Letting
T^{n}_{\rho}
denote the torus with
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action determined by
\rho
and letting
S^{\ell}
denote a sphere with a free
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action, define
M:=T^{n}_{\rho}\times_{\mathbb{Z}/p}S^{\ell}
.
The manifold
M
is a torus bundle over a lens space and the assumption that
\mathbb{Z}/p
acts freely on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
implies that the action of
\mathbb{Z}/p
on
T^{n}_{\rho}
has discrete fixed points.
In
[
DL21
]
, Davis and Lück use the computations from
[
DL13
]
to determine the
L
-groups of
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
and the structure set of
M
in the sense of surgery theory.

For the
L
-theory computation, Davis and Lück use the Farrell-Jones Conjecture for
\Gamma
to conclude
L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\cong H^{\Gamma}_{m}\left(\underline{E}\Gamma;\mathbf{L}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
.
Then, they compute the homology group by inverting
p
and inverting
2
.
After inverting
p
,
H^{\Gamma}_{m}\left(\underline{E}\Gamma;\mathbf{L}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
becomes part of a split short exact sequence.
After inverting
2
, there is an isomorphism
H^{\Gamma}_{m}\left(\underline{E}\Gamma;\mathbf{L}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\cong KO^{\Gamma}_{m}(\underline{E}\Gamma)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]
and one applies the computations of
[
DL13
]
.

In this paper, we study the case where the action of
\mathbb{Z}/p
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is not necessarily free on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
A free abelian group with a
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action can be written as
\mathbb{Z}^{n}=M\oplus N\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{c}
where
M\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta\right)^{a}
and
N\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Q}[\mathbb{Z}/p]^{b}
.
Here, we use
\zeta
to denote a primitive
p
-th root of unity.
We say that such a module is
of type
(a,b,c)
in which case
n=a(p-1)+bp+c
.
The
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p]
-module
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
will be denoted
L
.
As in
[
DL21
]
define
\Gamma:=L\rtimes_{\rho}\mathbb{Z}/p
and define
M:=T^{n}_{\rho}\times_{\mathbb{Z}/p}S^{\ell}
.
The fixed points of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action on
T^{n}_{\rho}
is a disjoint union of
a(p-1)
many
(b+c)
-dimensional tori rather than a discrete set when

is of type
(a,b,c)
.
Proving that
L_{m}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
is
p
-torsion free is one of the main difficulties in applying the machinery of
[
DL21
]
to our case.
In order to do this, we invert
2
and study topological
K
-theory.
One of our main results is

{restatable*}

If

Using the techniques in

1.1.
Geometric Interpretations of the Structure Set

In the situation of
[
DL21
]
, the computation of the structure set is interpreted as follows.
Suppose
f:N\rightarrow M
is a structure and let
\overline{f}:\overline{N}\rightarrow\overline{M}\cong T^{n}\times S^{\ell}
denote the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-cover.
The proof of
[
DL21
, Theorem 10.6]
applies in our case so we have the following interpretation of the
H_{n}(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\langle 1\rangle)^{\mathbb{Z}/p}
summand of the structure set.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The structure
[f:N\rightarrow M]\in\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,s}(M)
vanishes under projection to
H_{n}(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\langle 1\rangle)^{\mathbb{Z}/p}
;

(2)

\overline{f}:\overline{N}\rightarrow\overline{M}
is homotopic to a homeomorphism;

(3)

For a nonempty
J\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
, let
T^{J}\subseteq T^{n}
denote the obvious subtorus.
After making
\overline{f}
transverse to
T^{J}\times\{pt\}\subseteq T^{n}\times S^{\ell}
, we obtain a surgery problem

\overline{f}:\overline{f}^{-1}(T^{J}\times\{pt\})\rightarrow T^{J}\times\{pt\}.

This has a vanishing surgery obstruction in
L_{\left\lvert J\right\rvert}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\mathbb{Z}^{\left\lvert J\right\rvert}\right]\right)
for all nonempty
J\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Understanding the
\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}L^{s}_{n+\ell+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)/L^{s}_{n+\ell+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)
summand is more difficult.
Shaneson splitting implies that
L^{h}_{2m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\mathbb{Z}/p\right]\right)
will appear as summands of
L^{s}_{n+\ell+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)
.
These groups have
2
-torsion which involves the ideal class groups.
Rationally, there is an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}L^{s}_{n+\ell+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)/L^{s}_{n+\ell+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq b+c\\
k+\ell+1\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\left(R_{\mathbb{C}}^{(-1)^{k+\ell+1/2}}(\mathbb{Z}/p)/\langle\operatorname{{reg}}\rangle\right)^{\binom{b+c}{k}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

In the above expression,
R_{\mathbb{C}}^{\pm}(\mathbb{Z}/p)
denotes the group of virtual complex
\mathbb{Z}/p
-representations whose characters are of the form
\chi\pm\chi^{-1}
and
\operatorname{{reg}}
denotes the regular representation.

We give a heuristic description of this summand in the case
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}/p
(i.e. when
M
is the product of a torus and a lens space
L^{\ell}
).
Note that the inner sum on the right hand side is indexed by the standard subtori
T^{k}\subseteq T^{n}
where
k+\ell+1
is even.
Suppose
f:N\rightarrow M
is a structure and let
T^{k}\subseteq T^{n}
be a subtorus such that
k+\ell+1
is even.
Suppose
f
is transverse to the submanifold
T^{k}\times L^{\ell}
.
Then
f^{-1}\left(T^{k}\times L^{\ell}\right)
has a
\mathbb{Z}/p
-cover so we may take its
\rho
-invariant to obtain an element of
\left(R_{\mathbb{C}}^{(-1)^{k+\ell+1/2}}(\mathbb{Z}/p)/\langle\operatorname{{reg}}\rangle\right)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
This gives an element in the summand corresponding to the torus
T^{k}
.
This description is not technically correct; without some modifications, we do not know how to show it is well-defined.
A rigorous interpretation of these
\rho
-invariants will be the subject of future work.

2.
Group Theoretic Preliminaries

We are interested in groups
\Gamma
of the form
L\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p
where
L
is a finitely generated free abelian group.
Throughout this paper,
p
will always be an odd prime.
Curtis and Reiner classified these groups in
[
CR62
, Theorem 74.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
p
-th root of unity.
If
L
is an indecomposable integral
\mathbb{Z}/p
representation then
L
is either

(1)

B\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
a fractional ideal with action given by multiplication by
\zeta
.

(2)

B\oplus\mathbb{Z}
where
B\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
is a fractional ideal and
t\cdot(b,m)=(\zeta b+mb_{0},m)
where
b_{0}\in B\setminus(1-\zeta)B
.
We will denote this by
B\oplus_{b_{0}}\mathbb{Z}
.
Two such representations of this form are isomorphic if the fractional ideals represent the same element in the ideal class group.

(3)

\mathbb{Z}
with a trivial action.

Example 1
.

Let
\zeta
be a
p
-th root of unity.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]
is an example of a module of the form
1
in Theorem
2.1
.
The group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p]
is an example of a module of the form
2
; it is of the form
\mathbb{Z}\left[\zeta\right]\oplus_{1}\mathbb{Z}
.
When
p=3
, for instance, the assignment

\left(a_{0}+a_{1}\zeta,m\right)\mapsto a_{0}\left(-a_{0}+a_{1}+m\right)t+\left(-a_{1}+m\right)t^{2}

defines an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}\left[\mathbb{Z}/p\right]
-modules.

The group
\Gamma
determines a torus bundle
M
over a lens space with fundamental group
\mathbb{Z}/p
.
Note that the manifold corresponding to
(L\oplus\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p
is a product
M\times S^{1}
.
As taking products with
S^{1}
is a well understood operation in topology, we will sometimes make the simplification that
L
does not have any
\mathbb{Z}
summands.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a
\mathbb{Z}/p
-module
L
is
of type
(a,b,c)
if it is of the form
L=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{a}M_{i}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}\right)\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{c}
where
M_{i}
is in form (
1
) and
N_{j}
is of the form (
2
) in Theorem
2.1
.
We say that
L\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p
is
of type
(a,b,c)
if
L
is of type
(a,b,c)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
\Gamma=L\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p
is of type
(a,b,c)
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The virtually cyclic subgroups of
\Gamma
are isomorphic to either the trivial group,
\mathbb{Z}/p
,
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{{P}}
denote the set of conjugacy classes of maximal finite subgroups of
\Gamma
.
Then,
\left\lvert\mathcal{{P}}\right\rvert=p^{a}
.

(3)

If
P
is a finite subgroup of order
p
, then the normalizer
N_{\Gamma}P
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{b+c}\times P
and the Weyl group
W_{\Gamma}P:=N_{\Gamma}P/P
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{b+c}
.

Proof.

The elements of our group can be written as
xy^{i}
where
x\in L
and
y
is a generator of
\mathbb{Z}/p
.
Let
\rho(-):L\rightarrow L
denote the action of
\mathbb{Z}/p
on
L
.

To show the first statement, if
xy
and
x^{\prime}y
are in a subgroup
H
, then
x(x^{\prime})^{-1}
must be in
H
.
Hence, if
H
is finite,
x=x^{\prime}
.
It follows that the nontrivial finite subgroups are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p
.
Suppose
V
is an infinite virtually cyclic subgroup that is not infinite cyclic.
Then
V
must surject onto
\mathbb{Z}
with kernel
\mathbb{Z}/p
and
V\cap L
is an infinite cyclic group.
Let
xy
be a torsion element of
V
and let
v\in V\cap L
.
Then
xyvy^{-1}x^{-1}=\rho(v)\in V\cap L
.
But if
\rho(v)\neq v
,
V\cap L
would contain a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Therefore,
V\cap L
is fixed by
\rho
.
It follows that
V
is isomorphic
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p
.

For the second statement, observe that
(xy)^{p}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}y^{i}\cdot x
.
Therefore,
xy
is torsion if and only if
x
is in the kernel of the norm map
\text{Norm}:L\rightarrow L
.
Moreover, if
x
and
z
are in the kernel of the norm map, one checks that the group generated by
xy
is conjugate to the group generated by
zy
if an only if
x-z
is in the image of
1-y:L\rightarrow L
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{{P}}
is in bijection with
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/p;L)
.
It follows from Proposition
4.2
, which we prove later, that this is isomorphic to
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/p;\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]^{a}\right)
.
By
[
DL13
, Lemma 1.10(i)]
, this group is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{a}
.

The third statement follows from the beginning of the proof of
[
LR14
, Theorem 1.10]
.
∎

3.
Equivariant Homology and the Farrell-Jones Conjecture

In this section, we introduce some preliminary material on equivariant homology following
[
DL98
]
and on the Farrell-Jones Conjecture, which will allow us to compute
L
and
K
-groups.

3.1.
Equivariant Homology

Let
G
be a discrete group.
Given a covariant functor
\mathbf{E}:Grpd\rightarrow Sp
from the category of small groupoids to spectra, define the equivariant homology groups of a
G
-CW-complex
X
to be

H_{m}^{G}(X;\mathbf{E}):=\pi_{m}\left(X^{-}_{+}\wedge_{\operatorname{{Or}}(G)}\mathbf{E}(\overline{G/-})\right)

where
\operatorname{{Or}}(G)
is the orbit category of
G
and
\overline{G/H}
is the groupoid associated to the
G
-set
G/H
and
-\wedge_{\operatorname{{Or}}(G)}-
denotes a coend.
The functor
X^{-}_{+}
sends an orbit
G/H
to the fixed point set
X^{H}_{+}
.
If
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a map of
G
-CW-complexes, then we write

H_{m}^{G}\left(X\rightarrow Y;\mathbf{E}\right):=H_{m}^{G}\left(\operatorname{{cone}}(f);\mathbf{E}\right).

In this paper, we will take
\mathbf{E}
to be
\mathbf{K}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-\infty}
and
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\langle j\rangle}
for
j=2,1,0,\cdots,-\infty
.
The corresponding homology theories have the property that
H_{m}^{G}\left(G/H;\mathbf{K}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-\infty}\right)\cong K_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[H])
and
H_{m}^{G}\left(G/H;\mathbf{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\langle j\rangle}\right)\cong L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[H])
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We also use equivariant topological
K
-theory, which sends
G/H
to the representation ring
R_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
when
G
is finite.

Equivariant cohomology is defined analogously.
We refer to
[
DL98
]
for more details.

Remark
.

The notation
H_{m}^{G}\left(X;\mathbf{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
denotes the Davis-Lück equivariant homology as mentioned above whereas the notation
H_{m}\left(X;L^{\langle j\rangle}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)\right)
denotes the generalized homology of
X
with coefficients in the spectrum
L^{\langle j\rangle}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)
.

3.2.
Classifying Spaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group.
A
family
of subgroups is a nonempty set
\mathcal{{F}}
of subgroups closed under taking subgroups and conjugation.
A
classifying space for
\mathcal{{F}}
, denoted
E_{\mathcal{{F}}}G
, is a

5.
L
-Theory Computations

For geometric applications, one is typically interested in the groups
L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
and
L^{h}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
.
The group
L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
contains obstructions to obtaining simple homotopy equivalences through surgery and the group
L^{h}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
contains obstructions to obtaining homotopy equivalences through surgery.
There is a map
L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\rightarrow L^{h}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
.
More generally, one can define the lower
L
-groups
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
where
j=2,1,0,\cdots
with the convention

L^{\langle 2\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])=L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\hskip 28.45274ptL^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])=L^{h}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]).

There are maps

L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\rightarrow L^{\langle j-1\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])

and we define
L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]):=\varinjlim L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
.
This theory is developed in
[
Ran92b
]
.
The group
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
is
\pi_{m}L^{\langle j\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
for a spectrum
L^{\langle j\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
.
Also, the functors
\mathbf{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}:Grpd\rightarrow Sp
send a group
G
(regarded as a groupoid) to
L^{\langle j\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
.

We begin by computing
L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
.
This is easier to work with as the Farrell-Jones Conjecture holds for
L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}
.
Using Rothenberg sequences
[
Ran92b
, Section 17]
, we then compute
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
for all
j
.

One of the primary
L
-groups that appear in our computations are the groups
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)
and
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)
so we take some time to discuss these groups here.
Recall that
N_{\Gamma}P\cong\mathbb{Z}^{b+c}\times\mathbb{Z}/p
and
W_{\Gamma}P\cong\mathbb{Z}^{b+c}
.
Shaneson splitting
[
Ran92b
, Theorem 17.2]
gives isomorphisms

L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}L^{\langle j-i\rangle}_{m-i}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])^{\binom{b+c}{i}}\hskip 28.45274ptL^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}L^{\langle j-i\rangle}_{m-i}(\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{b+c}{i}}.

When
j=-\infty
, the Farrell-Jones Conjecture says that the first group can be repackaged as the homology group
H_{m}\left(T^{b};L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\right)
.
The second group can always be rewritten as
H_{m}\left(T^{b+c};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)
since the maps
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow L^{\langle j-1\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
are isomorphisms for all
j
.
Since the map
N_{\Gamma}P\rightarrow W_{\Gamma}P
splits in our case, we have an inclusion
L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\rightarrow L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)
.
The quotient is

L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)/L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}\tilde{L}^{\langle j-i\rangle}_{m-i}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])^{\binom{b+c}{i}}.

So, these groups can be computed in terms of the
L
-groups of the group
\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Finally, we record the
L
-groups of
\mathbb{Z}/p
.
The following theorem can be found in
[
Bak78
]
and
[
Bak75
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

There are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{(p-1)/2}&m\text{ even}\\
0&m\text{ odd}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{h}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{(p-1)/2}\oplus H(\mathbb{Z}/p)&m\text{ even}\\
0&m\text{ odd}\end{cases}

where
H(\mathbb{Z}/p)
is finitely generated abelian group of exponent
2
.
For
j\leq 0
, there is an isomorphism

\tilde{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{(p-1)/2}&m\text{ even}\\
0&m\text{ odd}\end{cases}.

Remark
.

Although
\tilde{L}^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])
and
\tilde{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])
are isomorphic for
j\leq 0
, the map

\tilde{L}^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\rightarrow\tilde{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])

is not an isomorphism.

5.1.
The
L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}
Computation

Theorem 5.2
.

There is an isomorphism

L_{m}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\cong\left(\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}H_{m}\left(\left(T_{\rho}^{n}\right)^{P};\tilde{L}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\right)\right)\oplus H_{m}\left(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)^{\mathbb{Z}/p}.

Proof.

We show that the isomorphism holds when
p
is inverted and when
2
is inverted.
This suffices since the groups are finitely generated.

Using the Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.2
, we have the following long exact sequence.

(4)

\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{{K}}_{m}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{m}}L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\rightarrow H_{m}(\underline{B}\Gamma;L(\mathbb{Z}))\rightarrow\mathcal{{K}}_{m-1}\rightarrow\cdots

3.2
, we can make the identification

\mathcal{{K}}_{m}\cong\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}H_{m}\left(\left(T_{\rho}^{n}\right)^{P};\tilde{L}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\right).

After inverting
p
, the sequence splits into short exact sequences

0\rightarrow\mathcal{{K}}_{m}\rightarrow L_{m}^{\langle-\infty\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])\rightarrow H_{m}(\underline{B}\Gamma;L(\mathbb{Z}))\rightarrow 0.

But when
p

\mathcal{{K}}_{m}

Now it remains to show that the groups are isomorphic after inverting

By

DL21

\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{{K}}_{m}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\xrightarrow{\varphi_{m}}KO^{\Gamma}_{m}(\underline{E}\Gamma)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\rightarrow KO_{m}(\underline{B}\Gamma)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\rightarrow\mathcal{{K}}_{m-1}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\rightarrow\cdots

The map

It remains to check that

But this follows from the fact that

6.
Computation of the Structure Set

6.1.
A Brief Review of Surgery

Let
M
be a simple Poincaré duality complex.
Its
geometric simple structure set
, denoted
\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,s}(M)
, is defined to be the set of equivalence classes of simple homotopy equivalences
f:N\rightarrow M
.
Two simple homotopy equivalences
f:N\rightarrow M
and
f^{\prime}:N^{\prime}\rightarrow M
are equivalent if there is a homeomorphism
g:N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
such that
f
and
f^{\prime}\circ g
are homotopic.
The geometric simple structure set is contained in geometric surgery exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow\mathcal{{N}}(M\times I)\rightarrow L^{s}_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(M)])\rightarrow\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,s}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{{N}}(N)\rightarrow L^{s}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(M)]).

This can be found in
[
Wal99
]
.
It is not clear from this description that there is an abelian group structure on
\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,s}(M)
.

In
[
Ran92a
]
, Ranicki defines an algebraic surgery exact sequence valid for any CW complex
X
.

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow H_{m+1}(X;L(\mathbb{Z}))\xrightarrow{A_{m+1}(X)}

\displaystyle L^{s}_{m+1}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}X])\xrightarrow{\xi_{m+1}(X)}\mathcal{{S}}^{per,s}_{m+1}(X)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\eta_{m+1}(X)}H_{m}(X;L(\mathbb{Z}))\xrightarrow{A_{m}(X)}L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}X])\rightarrow\cdots

The groups
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,s}_{m}(X)
are called the periodic simple structure groups.
One can, for instance, define the assembly map
A_{m}(X)
and define
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,s}_{m}(X)
to be the homotopy groups of the fiber.
When
X
is an
m
-dimensional simple Poincaré complex, elements of
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,s}_{m+1}(X)
can be represented by homotopy equivalences
f:N\rightarrow X
where
N
is a homology manifold (see
[
BFMW96
]
).

If we define

\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(X):=H_{m}\left(X\rightarrow\operatorname{{pt}};\mathbf{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{\mathbb{Z}}\right)

then we have algebraic surgery exact sequences for all decorations.
The techniques used to compute
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,s}_{n+\ell+1}(M)
will also compute
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(M)
so we state our results in this level of generality.

Let
L(\mathbb{Z})\langle 1\rangle
denote the
1
-connective cover of
L(\mathbb{Z})
.
Then, there is an algebraic surgery exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow H_{m+1}(X;L(\mathbb{Z})\langle 1\rangle))\xrightarrow{A^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m+1}(X)}

\displaystyle L_{m+1}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])\xrightarrow{\xi^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m+1}(X)}\mathcal{{S}}^{\langle 1\rangle,s}_{m+1}(X)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\eta^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m+1}(X)}H_{m}(X;L\langle 1\rangle)\xrightarrow{A^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m}(X)}L^{s}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])\rightarrow\cdots.

When
X
is an
m
dimensional closed manifold, Ranicki shows that

\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,s}(X)\cong\mathcal{{S}}^{\langle 1\rangle,s}_{m+1}(X).

Just as we did for the periodic structure sets, we may define structure sets
\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(X)
so that there is an algebraic surgery exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow H_{m}(X;L(\mathbb{Z})\langle 1\rangle)\xrightarrow{A^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m+1}(X)}L_{m}^{\langle j\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])\xrightarrow{\xi^{\langle 1\rangle}_{m+1}(X)}\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(X)\rightarrow\cdots

for all decorations.
We opt to use the notation
\mathcal{{S}}^{geo,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(X)
rather than the more appropriate
\mathcal{{S}}^{\langle 1\rangle,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(X)
in order to avoid confusion.

6.2.
The Periodic Structure Set of
B\Gamma

Here, we will record computations of periodic structure sets found in

DL21
]
and
[

.

The following results are
[
DL21
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
LR14
, Theorem 1.13]
, respectively.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
P
be a finite
p
-group.
Then, the homomorphism
\xi_{*}(BP):L^{\langle j\rangle}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}[P])\rightarrow\mathcal{{S}}_{*}^{per,\langle j\rangle}(BP)
induces a
\frac{1}{p}
-localization

\tilde{\xi}_{*}(BP):\tilde{L}^{\langle j\rangle}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}[P])\rightarrow\mathcal{{S}}_{*}^{per,\langle j\rangle}(BP).

In particular,

\mathcal{{S}}_{m}^{per,\langle j\rangle}(BP)\cong\tilde{L}_{m}^{\langle j\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[P])\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]^{(p-1)/2}&m\text{ even}\\
0&m\text{ odd}\end{cases}

when
j\neq 1
.

Theorem 6.2
.

There is an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j-i\rangle}_{m-i}(BP)^{\binom{b+c}{i}}\cong\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(B\Gamma).

Remark
.

Although
[
DL21
, Theorem 6.1]
is only stated for the decoration
s
, the proof is valid for all decorations.

Remark
.

In the proof of
[
LR14
, Theorem 1.13]
, the authors use an isomorphism
\bigoplus_{P\in\mathcal{{P}}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}\tilde{K}_{j}(\mathbb{Z}[P])^{\binom{b+c}{i}}\cong\tilde{K}_{j}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
rather than the correct isomorphism

\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}\tilde{K}_{j-i}(\mathbb{Z}[P])^{\binom{b+c}{i}}\cong\tilde{K}_{j}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])

and conclude that there is an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b+c}\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n-i}(BP)^{\binom{b+c}{i}}\cong\mathcal{{S}}_{n}^{per,\langle j\rangle}(B\Gamma)

rather than the isomorphism in Theorem
6.2
.
The isomorphism in Theorem
6.2
can be obtained from the proof of
[
LR14
, Theorem 1.13]
after resolving the indexing issue.

Remark
.

The isomorphism in Theorem
6.2
can be rewritten as

\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{m}(B\Gamma)\cong\bigoplus_{(P)\in\mathcal{{P}}}L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[N_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)/L^{\langle j\rangle}_{m}\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[W_{\Gamma}P\right]\right)\left[\frac{1}{p}\right].

6.3.
The Periodic Structure Set of
M

In order to compute the periodic structure sets
\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(M)
, we follow
[
DL21
, §8]
.
Namely, we study a map

\sigma:\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(M)\rightarrow H_{n}\left(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)^{\mathbb{Z}/p}

so that we get a well behaved injection

\sigma\times\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(f):\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(M)\rightarrow H_{n}\left(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)^{\mathbb{Z}/p}\times\mathcal{{S}}^{per,\langle j\rangle}_{n+\ell+1}(B\Gamma)

where
f:M\rightarrow B\Gamma
is the inclusion.

First, we record a consequence of Corollary
4.19
.

Proposition 6.3
.

The differentials in the Atiyah-Hirzebruch-Serre spectral sequences

\displaystyle E^{2}_{i,j}(B\Gamma)=H_{i}\left(\mathbb{Z}/p;H_{j}\left(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H_{i+j}(B\Gamma;L(\mathbb{Z}))

\displaystyle E^{2}_{i,j}(M)=H_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}/p}\left(S^{\ell};H_{j}\left(T_{\rho}^{n};L(\mathbb{Z})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H_{i+j}(M;L(\mathbb{Z}))

vanish.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
DL21
, Lemma 8.3]
so we give an outline.

We first show that the differentials in the first spectral sequence vanish.
It suffices to show that the differentials vanish after inverting
p
and after localizing
p
.
After inverting
p
, the only nonzero terms are in the column
E^{2}_{0,j}
so the differentials must vanish.

After localizing at
p
and applying
[
DL21
, Theorem 4.2]
, it suffices to show that the differentials for the homology theory
KO_{*}(-)_{(p)}
vanish.
But multiplication by
2
in the homology theory
KO_{*}
factors through the map
K_{*}
.
This exhibits a
KO_{*}(B\Gamma)_{(p)}
as a retract of
K_{*}(B\Gamma)_{(p)}
.
The result follows from Corollary
4.19
, which asserts that the differentials in
K_{*}(B\Gamma)_{(p)}
vanish.

To show that the differentials in the second spectral sequence vanishes, we consider the map
f:M\rightarrow B\Gamma
.
This induces a map from the second spectral sequence to the first which is bijective on terms
E^{2}_{i,j}
for

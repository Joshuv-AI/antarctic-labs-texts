Removing scalar curvature assumption for Ricci flow smoothing
Adam Martens

Abstract
. In recent work of Chan-Huang-Lee, it is shown that if a manifold enjoys uniform bounds on (a) the negative part of the scalar curvature, (b) the local entropy, and (c) volume ratios up to a fixed scale, then there exists a Ricci flow for some definite time with estimates on the solution assuming that the local curvature concentration is small enough initially (depending only on these a priori bounds). In this work, we show that the bound on scalar curvature assumption (a) is redundant. We also give some applications of this quantitative short-time existence, including a Ricci flow smoothing result for measure space limits, a Gromov-Hausdorff compactness result, and a topological and geometric rigidity result in the case that the a priori local bounds are strengthened to be global.

1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
, Chan-Huang-Lee prove a quantitative version of short-time Ricci flow existence, with scale-invariant estimates on the solution, starting from a metric
g
that has effective uniform bounds on the negative part of the scalar curvature, volume ratios up to a fixed scale, and local
\bar{\nu}
-entropy, assuming that the local scale-invariant integral curvature (so-called “curvature concentration”) is uniformly dependently small. Although it is not necessary to assume that
g
has uniformly bounded sectional curvature, the authors of
[
3
]
do impose that it should enjoy an
ineffective
lower Ricci bound. To summarize precisely, the initial metric
g
should satisfy

(1)

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{R}}_{g}\geq-\lambda,\\
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)\leq\mathfrak{v}_{0}r^{n}\;\text{ for all }x\in M,r\in(0,1],\\
\bar{\nu}(B_{g}(x,1),g,\tau)\geq-A\;\text{ for all }x\in M,\\
\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,1)}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\sigma\;\text{ for all }x\in M,\text{ and }\\
\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Rc}_{g}(x)>-\infty,\end{cases}

where
\lambda,\mathfrak{v}_{0},\tau,A
can be prescribed arbitrarily, but
\sigma
depends on these choices. We direct the reader to
[
12
]
for the definition and properties of the localized
\nu
- and
\bar{\nu}
-functionals. The ineffective lower Ricci bound in (
1
) is expected not to be necessary as it is only ever used to construct a smooth distance-like function on
M
, which in turn facilitates the approximation of the possibly unbounded curvature metric
g
by a sequence of bounded curvature metrics satisfying the local assumptions (
1
) (with possibly different constants). As commented on in
[
3
]
, this assumption may be weakened to a condition on how quickly
\mathrm{Rc}_{g}^{-}
grows at spatial infinity, though we do not pursue this here. Rather, the purpose of this work is to show that the scalar curvature bound assumption in (
1
) is redundant in the sense that the required smallness of
\sigma
and the estimates of the solution need not depend on
\lambda
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
n\geq 4,A,\tau,\mathfrak{v}_{0}>0
, there are
C_{0},\sigma,T>0
depending only on
n,A,\tau,\mathfrak{v}_{0}
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete noncompact manifold (not necessarily bounded curvature) such that for some
\varepsilon\leq\sigma
and all

, the metric

satisfies:

(i)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g}B_{g}(x,r)\leq\mathfrak{v}_{0}r^{n}
for all

;

(ii)

\bar{\nu}(B_{g}(x,1),g,\tau)\geq-A
;

(iii)

\left(\int_{B_{g}(x,1)}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\varepsilon
; and

(iv)

\inf_{x\in M}\mathrm{Rc}_{g}(x)>-\infty

Then there exists a complete Ricci flow

on

(2)

\begin{cases}&|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g(t)}(x)\leq C_{0}\varepsilon t^{-1},\\
&\mathrm{inj}_{g(t)}(x)\geq C_{0}^{-1}\sqrt{t},\text{ and}\\
&\left(\int_{B_{g(0)}(x,\frac{1}{16})}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Before we provide the applications of Theorem
1.1
, we first make some remarks regarding its hypotheses and statement.

•

We have already mentioned above that the ineffective lower Ricci bound (
iv
) is likely a redundant hypothesis as it is only used to construct a distance-like function on
(M,g)
satisfying some desired properties. Of particular note here, we suggest that the upper volume bound assumption (
i
) (here and throughout the paper) should
also
be redundant. Some evidence for this is discussed after Question 1 below. On the other hand, the
C^{0}
-control in the form of a
\bar{\nu}
-functional bound (
ii
) and the proportional smallness of the curvature concentration (
iii
) are a somewhat natural pairing from a Ricci flow point of view (see
[
9
]
and the references within for more discussion on this).

•

The choice of

in the curvature concentration in (
2
) is for convenience, not necessity; any

would work with appropriate modification. Also, by applying the Shrinking Balls Lemma
[
11
]
, the domain in the integral could be replaced with the ball of radius

with respect to

•

The constants
C_{0},\sigma,T
in Theorem

can be explicitly bounded in terms of

though we do not pursue this here because our methods for bounding these variables would be far from sharp. For example, suppose

and
\mathfrak{v}_{0}=2\omega_{n}
, just for simplicity. Then, since the local curvature concentration ought to be preserved by the flow (up to a dimensional constant perhaps), we can compute that an optimal bound on

, from the parabolic point of view at least, should be

\sigma\geq\frac{1}{C(n)e^{\frac{2A}{n}}}.

This expected bound on

comes from the equivalence between the

-functional and an

-Sobolev inequality, and the relationship between this Sobolev inequality and the curvature concentration via Hölder’s inequality. The reason why we would be unable to achieve a bound of the form (

) with our current machinery is due to the fact that there is not a known sharp relationship between volume ratios and
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequalities in spaces with possibly negative Ricci curvature. We direct the reader to sections 1 and 2 of
[
9
]
for more related discussion.

For any

Remark 1.3
.

Although the convergence of the distance function

The second application we present is for manifolds

Theorem 1.4
.

2.
Modified distance distortion

First, we recall the well-known Shrinking Balls Lemma due to Simon-Topping, which dictates how quickly distances can contract under Ricci flows with scale-invariant curvature bounds:

\sup_{y\in B_{g(0)}(x,r)}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g(t)}(y)\leq c_{0}/t\;\Longrightarrow\;B_{g(t)}(x,r-\beta\sqrt{c_{0}t})\subset B_{g(0)}(x,r)

Here
\beta=\beta(n)
is a dimensional constant, which is now fixed notation in the remainder of the paper. We direct the reader to
[
11
, Corollary 3.3]
for a precise statement.

Obtaining control on how quickly distances can expand under the flow usually requires control on the negative part of the Ricci or scalar curvature along the flow. In our setting, however, we are able to substitute this
L^{\infty}
curvature assumption for control on the local curvature concentration along the flow. We begin with an elementary lemma that allows us to control the rate at which volumes are perturbed, which will be useful in its own right.

Lemma 2.1

(Volume Perturbation)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N},v,\sigma,r>0
, and any pointed (not assumed complete) Ricci flow
(M^{n},g(t),x_{0})
,
t\in[a,b]
for some
a<b
, let
\Omega\subset\subset M
be any precompact set satisfying:

(a)

B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r)\subset\Omega
for each
t\in[a,b]
;

(b)

\left(\int_{\Omega}|{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\sigma
for all
t\in[a,b]
;

(c)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r)\geq vr^{n}
for all
t\in[a,b]
.

Then whenever
a\leq s\leq t\leq b
, there holds

e^{-\sigma v^{-\frac{2}{n}}r^{-2}(t-s)}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega\leq e^{\sigma v^{-\frac{2}{n}}r^{-2}(t-s)}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega.

Remark 2.2
.

Our most common application of Lemma
2.1
will be with
a=s=0
and
\Omega=B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2r)
. In this setting, if we further assume that the flow enjoys an
\alpha/t
curvature bound, then we can see (by SBL) that condition (
a
) can be replaced by the assumption that
B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2r)\subset\Omega
as long as we also restrict
b\leq\frac{r^{2}}{\beta^{2}\alpha}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

By the evolution of the volume form
\partial_{t}dV_{g(t)}=-{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}dV_{g(t)}
, the assumed smallness of integral scalar curvature (
b
) and the lower bound of volumes at time
t
in (
c
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}|^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}[\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega)]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma[\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(x_{0},r)]^{-\frac{2}{n}}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma(vr^{n})^{-\frac{2}{n}}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega).

Therefore by comparison, we obtain
\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega\leq e^{\sigma v^{-\frac{2}{n}}r^{-2}(t-s)}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega
for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. The other inequality follows from an identical argument.
∎

This volume perturbation estimate allows us to remove the assumption of a lower bound on scalar curvature in the version of EBL from
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Expanding Balls)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\alpha,v,\sigma>0
, there exists
\widehat{T}(n,\alpha,v,\sigma)>0
such that the following holds. Let
(M^{n},g(t),x_{0})
,
t\in[0,T]
be a pointed Ricci flow (not assumed complete) such that for some
V>0
there holds:

(a)

B_{g(t)}(x_{0},2^{n+2}Vv^{-1})\subset\subset M
for all
t\in[0,T]
;

(b)

\left(\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2)}|{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\sigma
for all
t\in[0,T]
;

(c)

\sup_{x\in B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2)}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g(t)}(x)\leq\alpha t^{-1}
for all
t\in(0,T]
;

(d)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(0)}B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2)\leq V
; and

(e)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}B_{g(t)}(x,r)\geq vr^{n}
whenever
x\in B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)
,
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, and
t\in(0,T]
.

Then for all
t\in[0,\min\{T,\widehat{T}\}]
, we have
B_{g(0)}(x_{0},1)\subset B_{g(t)}(x_{0},2^{n+2}Vv^{-1})
.

Proof.

The proof is nearly identical to the argument in
[

]
. The only changes are as follows. First, the maximal time interval in our setting will be

\widehat{T}=\min\left\{\frac{v^{\frac{2}{n}}\log 2}{\sigma},\frac{1}{4\beta^{2}\alpha}\right\}.

Now the only setting in which the lower bound of scalar curvature was used in

was to obtain the volume control

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2))\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}_{g(0)}(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},2))

for all

. For us, this implication follows immediately from Lemma

by our restrictions on the size of

and Remark

.
∎

3.2.2.
Existence and properties of

Our technique to establishing the existence of the measure

will be to show that the limits
\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)
both exist and are equal whenever

as a limit of countably many precompact pieces. Before commencing the proof however, we give a reformulation of our Volume Perturbation Lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0})
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
for some choices of variables

, and write

,

to be the corresponding complete Ricci flow. Given any precompact set
\Omega\subset\subset M
such that

for some

, then whenever

C^{-(t-s)}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega\leq C^{(t-s)}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega.

Here
C>0
is some constant that depends only on
n,A,\tau,r
as well as the smallest number

such that

\Omega\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B_{g(0)}\left(x_{j},\frac{1}{16}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows from the properties of such a flow

as laid out in (
10

2.1
. In particular, we can say as long as
t\in[0,T]
satisfies

B_{g(t)}(x,r/2)\subset B_{g(0)}(x,r)\subset\Omega.

Since

has a lower bound of volume ratios

from (

\left(\int_{\Omega}|{\mathcal{R}}_{g(t)}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{j},\frac{1}{16})}n^{\frac{n}{2}}|\mathrm{Rm}|_{g(t)}^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dV_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq(n^{\frac{n}{2}}N\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}})^{\frac{2}{n}}=N^{\frac{2}{n}}n\alpha.

Therefore, we can see that the result holds by applying Lemma

with

there being

, and thus we may take

C=\exp\left(N^{\frac{2}{n}}n\alpha\tilde{v}^{-\frac{2}{n}}(r/2)^{-2}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{N^{\frac{2}{n}}n\tilde{v}^{-\frac{2}{n}}}{(n-1)r^{2}}\right)

where

depends only on

.
∎

We also require the following elementary lemma which will be applied with

being either
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t)}F_{i}(\Omega)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a positive continuous function satisfying

C^{-(t-s)}f(s)\leq f(t)\leq C^{(t-s)}f(s)

whenever

. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

depending only on
\varepsilon
as well as upper bounds of

|f(t)-f(s)|\leq\varepsilon.

In particular,
\lim_{t\to 0}f(t)
exists.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0

t_{\varepsilon}=\min\left\{T,\frac{1}{\log C}\log\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{C^{T}f(T)}\right)\right\}.

Then whenever
0<s\leq t\leq t_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

|f(t)-f(s)|\leq(C^{(t-s)}-1)\sup_{t^{\prime}\in(0,T]}f(t^{\prime})\leq(C^{t_{\varepsilon}}-1)C^{T}f(T)\leq\varepsilon.

∎

Armed with these preliminary lemmas, we are ready to prove the existence and equality of the desired limits. We split our proof is split into three claims: (1) First show that the time limit exists assuming that the interior of the set is not empty (Claim

Claim 3.5

So for any fixed

We can therefore conclude that

Claim 3.6

For any precompact set

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)=\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega.

Proof.

We have already established that the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega
exists. Thus for any
\varepsilon>0
, we may assume
t_{0}>0
is small enough so that whenever
s\in(0,t_{0}]
,

|\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega-\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}\Omega|<\varepsilon.

Without loss of generality, we assume
t_{0}\leq T_{\Omega}
where

is defined in the proof of Claim

for some

which may be increased several times. First, if we restrict

large enough so that

\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t_{0})}F_{i}(\Omega)\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{0})}\Omega,

then we obtain from (

) and Lemma

(here

does not depend on

Then we increase

|\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{\varepsilon})}\Omega-\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t_{\varepsilon})}F_{i}(\Omega)|<\varepsilon.

A direct application of the triangle inequality completes the proof.
∎

Claim 3.7
.

For any precompact Borel set
\Omega\subset M_{\infty}
(no other assumptions), there holds

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)=\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega.

Proof.

We begin by claiming that

(18)

\liminf_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega=\limsup_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r)).

The “
\leq
” inequality is obvious, so assume for the sake of contradiction that there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that

(19)

\varepsilon+\liminf_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega\leq\limsup_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r)).

Define
r_{0}=\sqrt[n]{\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{n+4}\mathfrak{v}_{0}}}
, i.e.,
2^{n+4}\mathfrak{v}_{0}r_{0}^{n}=\varepsilon
. Then select some

small enough so that

\limsup_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r))\leq\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Since the limit on the RHS of (

, we may take
t_{0}>0
small enough so that whenever
s\in(0,t_{0}]
,

(21)

\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g(s)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Similarly, we can find a sequence
t_{j}\to 0
such that for every

,

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}\Omega-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega.

Thus whenever
t_{j}\leq t_{0}
, we can see by (
19
) - (

) that

(23)

\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}\Omega\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}\Omega+\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}(B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1})).

For any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we can assume that whenever
i
is large enough (depending on
j

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}(B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t_{j})}F_{i}(B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r_{1}))\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t_{j})}(B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},2r_{1})).

Restricting

large enough so that
t_{j}\leq\frac{(2r_{1})^{2}}{\beta^{2}}
, we obtain from Lemma
3.3
and (
23
) that

\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(t_{j})}(B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},2r_{1}))\leq 4\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},2r_{1})).

Here we have assumed that

is sufficiently large (depending on

, where

is the constant from Lemma
3.3
. Now a contradiction is immediately apparent because we have assumed

, and we know that
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},2r_{1}))\leq\mathfrak{v}_{0}(2r_{1})^{n}
. Thus we have proved that (
18
) holds. Moreover, using a similar (albeit less involved) approach to the proof of (
18

(24)

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)=\limsup_{r\to 0}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r)).

Not only can we say from (

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)

\displaystyle|\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)-

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\Omega|\leq|\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega)-\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r))|

\displaystyle\quad+|\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r))-\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(\Omega\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},r))|

This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

We are now ready to formally define the measure
\mu
. For the sake of brevity, let us write
{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
to denote the
j
’th annulus centered at
x_{\infty}
with respect to the metric
d_{0}
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{A}}_{j}:=B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},j)\setminus B_{d_{0}}(x_{\infty},j-1).

Also we write
\operatorname{Borel}(\Omega)
to denote the
\sigma
-algebra of all Borel subset of
\Omega
and recall a basic measure theory fact that

\operatorname{Borel}({\mathcal{A}}_{j})=\{E\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j}:E\subset\operatorname{Borel}(M_{\infty})\}.

Thus for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define
\mu_{j}:\operatorname{Borel}({\mathcal{A}}_{j})\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

(25)

\mu_{j}(E)=\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}E\;\;\text{ (or equivalently, }\mu_{j}(E)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}F_{i}(E)\text{)},

which is a well-defined function by Claim
3.7
. Additionally, by an elementary measure theory argument (see
[
10
]
for instance), we may conclude that each
\mu_{j}
is countably additive, or equivalently in this setting, that each tuple
({\mathcal{A}}_{j},\operatorname{Borel}({\mathcal{A}}_{j}),\mu_{j})
is in fact a measure space. Now for any
E\in\operatorname{Borel}(M_{\infty})
, we define

\mu(E):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(E\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j}).

This can be seen to define a measure on
M_{\infty}
because if
E=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}
for some pairwise disjoint sequence of Borel sets
E_{k}
, we have

\mu(E)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(E\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(E_{k}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(E_{k}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(E_{k}),

where we have applied Tonelli’s Theorem and the countable additivity of each
\mu_{j}
. Finally, to show that
\mu
is Radon, we must show that it is outer and inner regular, i.e., that

\mu(E)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subset E\text{ is compact}\}=\inf\{\mu(U):U\supset E\text{ is open}\}

for any
E\in\operatorname{Borel}(M_{\infty})
. Since the proof of inner regularity is very similar to that of the outer regularity, we will only show the latter. To that end, fix some
E\in\operatorname{Borel}(M_{\infty})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Just as in the proof of (
18
) in Claim
3.7
, for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
we can find some
x_{j}\in M_{\infty}
and
r_{j}>0
such that
B_{d_{0}}(x_{j},r_{j})\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
and that

(26)

\mu(B_{d_{0}}(x_{j},r_{j}))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}.

Write
B_{j}=(E\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j})\cup B_{d_{0}}(x_{j},r_{j})
, which is precompact in
M_{\infty}
, so we may choose a finite sequence of points
\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset M_{\infty}
such that

(27)

B_{j}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B_{d_{0}}\left(y_{k},\frac{1}{100}\right).

Now choose
t_{j}^{\prime}>0
small enough such that whenever
t\in(0,t_{j}^{\prime}]
,

(28)

|\mu_{j}(B_{j})-\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{j})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}.

Using the fact that
\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}
is a Riemannian measure (and subsequently, Radon), we may choose some precompact open set
U_{j,t}\subset M_{\infty}
, containing
B_{j}
, that satisfies

(29)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(U_{j,t})-\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(B_{j})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}.

Moreover by intersecting
U_{j,t}
with the finite open cover from (
27
), we may assume

U_{j,t}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B_{d_{0}}\left(y_{k},\frac{1}{100}\right).

Importantly, here
N
is independent of
t\in(0,t_{j}^{\prime}]
. Thus by a similar argument to that of the proof of Claim
3.7
, we obtain that

(30)

|\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}(U_{j,t})-\mu(U_{j,t})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}

whenever
t
is sufficiently small. So for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we select
t_{j}>0
small enough so that all of (
28
), (
29
), (
30
) hold. Then with
U=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}U_{j,t_{j}}
(which clearly is open and contains
E
because each
U_{j,t_{j}}
contains
E\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
), we may apply (
26
) and (
28
) - (
30
) along with the fact that
\mu|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{j}}=\mu_{j}
to see that

\displaystyle\mu(U)-\mu(E)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu(U_{j,t_{j}})-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(B_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}(B_{d_{0}}(x_{j},r_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}(U_{j,t_{j}})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}\right]-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{j})}(B_{j})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j+2}}\right]=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.

on derived equivalence for Abuaf flop: mutation of non-commutative crepant resolutions and spherical twists
wahei hara

Abstract
. In [ 18 ] , Segal constructed a derived equivalence for an interesting 5-fold flop that was provided by Abuaf. The aim of this article is to add some results for the derived equivalence for Abuaf’s flop. Concretely, we study the equivalence for Abuaf’s flop by using Toda-Uehara’s tilting bundles and Iyama-Wemyss’s mutation functors. In addition, we observe a “flop-flop=twist” result and a “multi-mutation=twist” result for Abuaf’s flop.

14E05, 14F05, 14J32

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In
[
18
]
, Segal studied an interesting flop provided by Abuaf, which we call the
Abuaf flop
.
Let
V
be a four dimensional symplectic vector space and
\LGr(V)
the Lagrangian Grassmannian.
Let
Y
be a total space of a rank 2 bundle
\Sub(-1)
on
\LGr(V)
, where
\Sub
is the rank 2 subbundle and
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr(V)}(-1):=\bigwedge^{2}\Sub
.
Then,
Y
is a local Calabi-Yau
5
-fold.
On the other hand, let
\PP(V)
be the projective space and put
\LL:=\mathcal{O}_{\PP(V)}(-1)
.
Then using the symplectic form on
V
gives an injective bundle map
\LL\hookrightarrow\LL^{\bot}
.
Let
Y^{\prime}
be the total space of a bundle
(\LL^{\bot}/\LL)\otimes\LL^{2}
,
then
Y^{\prime}
is another local Calabi-Yau
5
-fold with an isomorphism
H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\simeq H^{0}(Y^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}})=:R
of
\CC
-algebras.
Put
X:=\Spec R
.
Abuaf observed that the correspondence
Y\rightarrow X\leftarrow Y^{\prime}
gives an example of
5
-dimensional flops.
This flop has the nice feature that the contracting loci on either side are not isomorphic.
In
[
15
]
, Li proved that a simple flop of dimension at most five is one of the following

(1)

a (locally trivial deformation of) standard flop,

(2)

a (locally trivial deformation of) Mukai flop,

(3)

the Abuaf flop.

Standard flops and Mukai flops are well-studied.
Thus it is important to study the Abuaf flop from the point of view of Li’s classification.

Based on the famous conjecture by Bondal, Orlov, and Kawamata, it is expected that
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are derived equivalent.
Segal proved that this expectation is true.
The method of his proof is as follows.
He constructed tilting bundles
\Tilt_{\mathrm{S}}
and
\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{S}}
on
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
respectively,
and proved that there is an isomorphism

\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{S}})\simeq\End_{Y^{\prime}}(\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{S}}).

Then, by using a basic theorem for tilting objects, we have a derived equivalence

\Seg^{\prime}:\D(Y^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(Y).

On the other hand, in
[
21
]
, Toda and Uehara established a method to construct a tilting bundle under some assumptions (Assumption
2.10
and Assumption
2.11
).
The difficulties to use Toda-Uehara’s method are the following:

(a)

There are few examples known to satisfy their assumptions.

(b)

Since Toda-Uehara’s construction consists of complicated inductive step, it is difficult to find an explicit description of the resulting tilting bundle in general.

However, in the case of the Abuaf flop, it is possible show the following.

Theorem 1.1

(see Section
3.2
)
.

Y
and
Y^{\prime}
satisfy Toda-Uehara’s assumptions.

Hence the construction by Toda and Uehara gives new tilting bundles
\Tilt_{\mathrm{T}}
and
\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{T}}
on
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
, respectively.
Moreover, fortunately, it is possible to compute the resulting tilting bundles explicitly in this case.
Using this explicit description of the tilting bundle shows that there is another tilting bundle
\Tilt_{\mathrm{U}}
on
Y
that satisfies

\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{U}})\simeq\End_{Y^{\prime}}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{T}}^{\prime}).

Therefore applying the basic theorem for tilting objects again provides a new derived equivalence

\TU^{\prime}:\D(Y^{\prime})\to\D(Y).

Note that a tilting bundle constructed by using Toda-Uehara’s method can be regarded as a canonical one,
because it provides a projective generator of a perverse heart of the derived category.
Thus, it is quite natural to ask the following questions.

Question 1.2
.

(1)

What is the relation among three tilting bundles on
Y
,
\Tilt_{\mathrm{S}}
,
\Tilt_{\mathrm{T}}
, and
\Tilt_{\mathrm{U}}
?

(2)

What is the relation between two tilting bundles on
Y^{\prime}
,
\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{S}}
and
\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{T}}
?

(3)

What is the relation between two equivalences
\Seg^{\prime}
and
\TU^{\prime}
?

The aim of this article is to answer these questions.

1.2.
NCCRs and Iyama-Wemyss’s mutations

Put

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{S}}

\displaystyle:=\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{S}})=\End_{Y^{\prime}}(\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{S}}),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle:=\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{T}}),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{U}}

\displaystyle:=\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{U}})=\End_{Y^{\prime}}(\Tilt^{\prime}_{\mathrm{T}}).

Then these algebras are
non-commutative crepant resolutions
(=NCCRs) of
X=\Spec R
.
The notion of NCCR was first introduced by Van den Bergh as a non-commutative analog of crepant resolutions.
An NCCR of a Gorenstein domain
R
is defined as the endomorphism ring
\Lambda:=\End_{R}(M)
of a reflexive
R
-module
M
such that

\Lambda
is Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module and its global dimension is finite.
As in the commutative case, a Gorenstein domain
R
may have many different NCCRs.
One way to compare different NCCRs is
Iyama-Wemyss’s mutation
(= IW mutation).

Let
A
be a
d
-singular Calabi-Yau algebra and
M
an
A
-module whose endomorphism ring
\End_{A}(M)
is an NCCR of
A
.
Let
N\in\add M
and consider a right
(\add N^{*})
-approximation of
M^{*}

a:N_{0}^{*}\to M^{*}

(see Definition
2.16
).
Then IW mutation of
M
at
N
is defined as
\mu_{N}(M):=N\oplus\Ker(a)^{*}
.
In
[
12
]
, Iyama and Wemyss proved that the endomorphism ring of
\mu_{N}(M)
is also an NCCR of
A
and there is a derived equivalence

\Phi_{N}:\D(\modu\End_{A}(M))\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(\modu\End_{A}(\mu_{N}(M)))

(see Theorem
2.18
for more detail).

In many cases, it is observed that important NCCRs are connected by multiple IW mutations.
For example, Nakajima proved that, in the case of three dimensional Gorenstein toric singularities associated with reflexive polygons, all
splitting
NCCRs are connected by repeating IW mutations
[
16
]
.
In addition, the author studied IW mutations of certain NCCRs of the minimal nilpotent orbit closure of type A
[
9
]
.
Also in the case of the Abuaf flop, we can show the following.

Theorem 1.3

(= Theorem
3.14
, Theorem
3.15
)
.

The above three NCCRs
\Lambda_{\mathrm{S}}
,
\Lambda_{\mathrm{T}}
, and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{U}}
are connected by multiple IW mutations.

This result provides an answer to Question
1.2
(1) and (2).
We prove this theorem by relating IW mutations with
mutations of full exceptional collections
on
\D(\LGr(V))

(see Section
5
).

1.3.
Flop-Flop=Twist result

Recall that
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are
5
-dimensional local Calabi-Yau varieties.
It is known that the derived category of a Calabi-Yau variety normally admits an interesting autoequivalence called a
spherical twist

(see Section
2.5
).
Spherical twists arise naturally in mathematical string theory and homological mirror symmetry.

On the other hand, it is widely observed that spherical twists also appear in the context of birational geometry.
Namely, in many places, it is observed that a spherical twist arises as a difference of two derived equivalences associated to a flop
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
9
,
7
,
6
,
20
]
.
We call this phenomenon “flop-flop=twist”.
The following theorem gives the first example of this phenomenon in the case of the Abuaf flop.

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
4.6
)
.

(1)

Let us consider a spherical twist
\ST_{\Sub[2]}
around a
1
-term complex
\Sub[2]=\Sub|_{\LGr}[2]
on the zero section
\LGr\subset Y
.
Then, we have a functor isomorphism

\Seg^{\prime}\circ\TU^{\prime-1}\simeq\ST_{\Sub[2]}\in\Auteq(\D(Y)).

(2)

An autoequivalence
\TU^{\prime-1}\circ\Seg^{\prime}
of
\D(Y^{\prime})
is isomorphic to a spherical twist
\ST_{\mathcal{O}_{\PP}(-3)}
associated to a sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\PP}(-3)
on the zero-section
\PP\subset Y^{\prime}
:

\TU^{\prime-1}\circ\Seg^{\prime}\simeq\ST_{\mathcal{O}_{\PP}(-3)}\in\Auteq(\D(Y^{\prime})).

To prove the first statement of the theorem, we provide an explicit description of a Fourier-Mukai kernel of an equivalence
\TU^{\prime}
.
Let
\widetilde{Y}
be a blowing-up of
Y
along the zero section
\LGr=\LGr(V)
.
Then, the exceptional divisor
E
of
\widetilde{Y}
is isomorphic to
\PP_{\LGr}(\Sub(-1))
.
Thus we can embed
E
into the product
\LGr(V)\times\PP(V)
via an injective bundle map
\Sub(-1)\subset V\otimes_{\CC}\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)
.
Set
\widehat{Y}:=\widetilde{Y}\cup_{E}(\LGr(V)\times\PP(V))
.
We prove the following.

Theorem 1.5

(= Theorem
4.5
)
.

The Fourier-Mukai kernel of the equivalence
\TU^{\prime}
is given by the structure sheaf of
\widehat{Y}
.

Note that
\widehat{Y}=Y\times_{X}Y^{\prime}
.
This is very close to the case of Mukai flops
[
13
,
17
]
.

1.4.
Multi-mutation=twist result.

We also study a spherical twist from the point of view of NCCRs.
Namely, we can understand a spherical twist as a composition of IW mutations in the following way.
Let us consider a bundle on
Y

\Tilt_{\mathrm{U},1}:=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Y}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)\oplus\Sub(1).

We can show that this bundle is also a tilting bundle on
Y
.
Put

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:=H^{0}(Y,\Tilt_{\mathrm{U},1}),

\displaystyle W^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)\oplus\Sub(1)),~\text{and}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{U},1}

\displaystyle:=\End_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{U},1})\simeq\End_{R}(M).

We show that there is an isomorphism of
R
-modules

\mu_{W^{\prime}}(\mu_{W^{\prime}}(\mu_{W^{\prime}}(\mu_{W^{\prime}}(M))))\simeq M

(Proposition
4.8
).
Furthermore, using Iyama-Wemyss’s theorem gives an autoequivalence of
\D(\modu\Lambda_{\mathrm{U},1})

\nu_{W^{\prime}}:=\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\circ\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\circ\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\circ\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\in\Auteq(\D(\modu\Lambda_{\mathrm{U},1})).

This autoequivalence corresponds to a spherical twist in the following sense:

Theorem 1.6

(= Theorem
4.9
)
.

The autoequivalence
\nu_{W^{\prime}}
of
\D(\modu\Lambda_{\mathrm{U},1})
corresponds to a spherical twist

\ST_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)}\in\Auteq(\D(Y))

under the identification
\RHom_{Y}(\Tilt_{\mathrm{U},1},-):\D(Y)\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(\modu\Lambda_{\mathrm{U},1})
.

Donovan and Wemyss proved that, in the case of
3
-fold flops, a composition of
two
IW mutation functors corresponds to a spherical-like twist
[
7
]
.
In the case of Mukai flops, the author observed that a composition of
many
IW mutation functors corresponds to a P-twist
[
9
]
.
The theorem above provides a new example of this phenomenon for the Abuaf flop.

1.5.
Plan of the article

In Section
2
, we provide some basic definitions and theorems we use in later sections.
In Section

, we give an explicit description of the tilting bundle obtained by Toda-Uehara’s construction.
In addition, we show that NCCRs obtained as the endomorphism rings of Toda-Uehara’s or Segal’s tilting bundle are connected by repeating IW mutations.

, we explain the definition of exceptional collections and its mutation.
As an application of them, we explain how to find a resolution of a sheaf from an exceptional collection.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Abuaf flop

This section explains the geometry of the Abuaf flop briefly.
For more details, see
[
18
]
.
Let
V
be a four dimensional symplectic vector space.
Let
\LGr(V)
be the Lagrangian Grassmannian of
V
and
\Sub\subset V\otimes_{\CC}\mathcal{O}_{\LGr(V)}
the rank two universal subbundle.
Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr(V)}(1):=\bigwedge^{2}\Sub^{*}
is the ample generator of
\Pic(\LGr(V))
,
and this polarisation identifies the Lagrangian Grassmannian
\LGr(V)
with the quadric threefold
Q_{3}\subset\PP^{4}
.
Note that the canonical embedding
\LGr(V)\subset\Gr(2,V)
coincides with a hyperplane section
Q_{3}=Q_{4}\cap H\subset Q_{4}\subset\PP^{5}
.
Let
Y
be the total space of a vector bundle
\Sub(-1)
:

Y:=\Tot(\Sub(-1))\xrightarrow{\pi}\LGr(V).

Since
\bigwedge^{2}(\Sub(-1))\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\LGr(V)}(-3)\simeq\omega_{\LGr(V)}
, the variety
Y
is a five dimensional (local) Calabi-Yau variety.

Let
\LGr\subset Y
be the zero section.
Then, it is possible to contract
\LGr
and this gives a flopping contraction
\phi:Y\to X
.
The algebra
R:=\phi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is a normal Gorenstein algebra such that
X=\Spec R
.

Next, consider the
3
-dimensional projective space
\PP(V)
.
The symplectic form on
V
gives an embedding of the universal line bundle
\LL=\mathcal{O}_{\PP(V)}(-1)
into
\Omega_{\PP(V)}^{1}(1)\simeq\LL^{\bot}
.
Consider the total space

Y^{\prime}:=\Tot((\LL^{\bot}/\LL)\otimes\LL^{2})\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}\PP(V)

of a vector bundle
(\LL^{\bot}/\LL)\otimes\LL^{2}
,
which is another five dimensional (local) Calabi-Yau variety.
The zero section
\PP\subset Y^{\prime}
can be contracted, and gives a flopping contraction
\phi^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\to X
.
Combining
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
provides a diagram of a flop

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}}

The affine variety
X
has a unique singular point
o\in X
.
In contrast to the case of the Atiyah flop or the Mukai flop, two fibers
\phi^{-1}(o)=\LGr
and
\phi^{\prime-1}(o)=\PP
are not isomorphic to each other.
Since this interesting flop was first provided by Abuaf, we call this flop the
Abuaf flop
.

2.2.
Non-commutative crepant resolution and tilting bundle

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a normal Gorenstein (commutative) algebra, and
M
a non-zero reflexive
R
-module.
We set
\Lambda:=\End_{R}(M)
.
We say that the
R
-algebra
\Lambda
is a
non-commutative crepant resolution (=NCCR)
of
R
, or
M
gives an NCCR of
R
,
if
\gldim\Lambda<\infty
and
\Lambda
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module.

The notion of NCCR is a non-commutative analog of the notion of crepant resolutions.
The following conjecture is due to Bondal, Orlov, and Van den Bergh.

Conjecture 2.2

(
[
23
]
, Conjecture 4.6)
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.
Then, all crepant resolutions of
R
and all NCCRs of
R
are derived equivalent.

The theory of NCCRs has strong relationship to the theory of tilting bundles.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a variety. A vector bundle
\Tilt
(of finite rank) on
X
is called a
partial tilting bundle
if

(1)

\Ext_{X}^{i}(\Tilt,\Tilt)=0
for
i\neq 0
.

Further, if a partial tilting bundle
\Tilt
satisfies the following condition,

(2)

\Tilt
generates the category
\mathrm{D}(\Qcoh(X))
, i.e. for
E\in\mathrm{D}(\Qcoh(X))
,
\RHom_{X}(\Tilt,E)=0
implies
E=0

we say that the bundle
\Tilt
is a
tilting bundle
.

Example 2.4
.

In
[
3
]
, Beilinson showed that the following vector bundles on a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}

T=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k),~~~T^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{k}(k+1)

are tilting bundles.
Note that these tilting bundles come from full strong exceptional collections of the derived category
\D(\PP^{n})
of
\PP^{n}
that are called the
Beilinson collections
.

A tilting bundle on a variety gives an equivalence between the derived category of the variety and the derived category of a non-commutative algebra that is given as the endomorphism ring of the tilting bundle.
This is a generalisation of classical Morita theory.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Tilt\in\D(X)
be a tilting bundle on a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
.
If we set
\Lambda:=\End_{X}(\Tilt)
, we have an equivalence of categories

\RHom_{X}(\Tilt,-):\D(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(\modu(\Lambda)),

and the quasi-inverse of this functor is given by

-\otimes_{\Lambda}\Tilt:\D(\modu(\Lambda))\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(X).

For the proof of Theorem
2.5
, see
[
10
, Theorem 7.6]
or
[
21
, Lemma 3.3]
.
A tilting bundle on a crepant resolution provides an NCCR.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\phi:Y\to X=\Spec R
be a crepant resolution of an affine normal Gorenstein variety
X
.
Let
\Tilt
be a tilting bundle on
Y
and assume that
\Tilt
contains a trivial line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
as a direct summand.
Then, we have an isomorphism
\End_{Y}(\Tilt)\simeq\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt)
.
In particular, the
R
-module
\phi_{*}\Tilt
gives an NCCR of
R
.

To prove this proposition, we need the following three propositions.

Proposition 2.7

(c.f.
[
9
]
Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
\phi:Y\to X=\Spec R
be a crepant resolution of a Gorenstein affine scheme
X
and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
Y
.
Assume that

H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{F})=0=\Ext_{Y}^{i}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{Y})

for all
i>0
.
Then, an
R
-module
\phi_{*}\mathcal{F}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proposition 2.8

(see e.g.
[
9
]
Proposition 2.8)
.

Let
R
be a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain and
M
a (maximal) Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module.
Then,
M
is reflexive.

Proposition 2.9

(see e.g.
[
9
]
Proposition 2.9)
.

Let
R
be a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain and
M,N
(maximal) Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules.
Then, the
R
-module
\Hom_{R}(N,M)
is reflexive.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

Since
\Tilt
contains
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
as a direct summand, we have

H^{i}(Y,\Tilt)=0=\Ext_{Y}^{i}(\Tilt,\mathcal{O}_{Y})

for all
i\neq 0
.
Thus
\phi_{*}\Tilt
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module and hence
\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt)
is a reflexive
R
-module.
On the other hand, since
H^{i}(Y,\Tilt^{*}\otimes\Tilt)=0
for
i\neq 0
and
(\Tilt^{*}\otimes\Tilt)^{*}\simeq\Tilt\otimes\Tilt^{*}
,
the
R
-module
\phi_{*}(\Tilt^{*}\otimes\Tilt)=\End_{Y}(\Tilt)
is also Cohen-Macaulay and reflexive.
Since
\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt)
and
\End_{Y}(\Tilt)
are isomorphic to each other in codimension one, we have an isomorphism

\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt)\simeq\End_{Y}(\Tilt).

Since there is an equivalence of categories
\D(Y)\simeq\D(\modu\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt))
, the algebra
\End_{R}(\phi_{*}\Tilt)
has finite global dimension.

2.3.
Toda-Uehara’s construction for tilting bundles and perverse hearts

Van den Bergh showed in
[
22
,
23
]
that if
f:Y\to X
is a projective morphism that has fibers of dimension at most one and satisfies
Rf_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
(e.g.
3
-fold flopping contraction),
then there is a tilting bundle on
Y
that is a projective generator of a perverse heart
{}^{0}\Per(Y/X)
.
As a generalisation of this result, Toda and Uehara provided a method to construct a tilting bundle in higher dimensional cases that satisfy certain assumptions
[
21
]
.
They also provided a perverse heart
{}^{0}\Per(Y/A_{n-1})
that contains the tilting bundle as a projective generator.
In the present subsection, we recall the construction of Toda-Uehara’s tilting bundle.

Let
f:Y\to X=\Spec R
be a projective morphism from a Noetherian scheme
Y
to an affine scheme
X
of finite type.
Assume that
Rf_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\dim f^{-1}(x)\leq n
for all
x\in X

In addition, let us assume the following condition holds for

Definition 2.15

Let

Let

A

A morphism

(\add N)
-approximation

Let
R
be a normal Gorenstein algebra and

a modifying

-module.
For any

(1)

a right
(\add N)
-approximation of

(2)

a right
(\add N^{*})
-approximation of

Put

and
K_{1}:=\Ker(b)
.

Definition 2.17
.

With notations as above, we define the

of

at

and the

of
M
at

Note that, the right and left mutations are well-defined up to additive closure
[
12
, Lemma 6.3]

In
[

]
, Iyama and Wemyss proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.18

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
R
be a normal Gorenstein algebra
and
M
a modifying module.
Assume that

.
Then

(1)

R
-algebras
\End_{R}(M)
,
\End_{R}(\mu_{N}^{R}(M))

(2)

If

\mu^{L}_{N}(M)

and

-module and there is an isomorphism of

\IW_{N}:=\RHom(C\oplus Q,-):\D(\modu(\End_{R}(M)))\to\D(\modu(\End_{R}(\mu_{N}^{L}(M)))).

The following lemmas are useful to find an approximation.

Lemma 2.19

(
[

]

Lemma 2.20
.

Let

be a crepant resolution of an affine Gorenstein normal variety

Let

be a vector bundle on

Assume that

(a)

\EE\in\add(\mathcal{W})
,

(b)

\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{K}
and

are tilting bundles, and

(c)

\mathcal{W}
contains
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
as a direct summand.

Then,

(1)

The sequence

is exact and provides a right
(\add f_{*}\mathcal{W})
-approximation of
f_{*}\mathcal{C}

(2)

The IW mutation functor

\Phi_{f_{*}\mathcal{W}}:\D(\modu\End_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{K}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\D(\modu\End_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{C}))

coincides with the functor
\RHom(\RHom_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{C}),-)

Proof.

First, note that there are isomorphisms of

-algebras

\displaystyle\End_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{K})\simeq\End_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W}\oplus f_{*}\mathcal{K}),~\End_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{C})\simeq\End_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W}\oplus f_{*}\mathcal{C})

The assumptions (b) and (c) imply

Moreover, as in the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle\Hom_{Y}(\mathcal{W},\EE)\simeq\Hom_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W},f_{*}\EE),~\Hom_{Y}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{C})\simeq\Hom_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W},f_{*}\mathcal{C}).

\Hom_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W},f_{*}\EE)\to\Hom_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{W},f_{*}\mathcal{C})

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{Y}(\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{K},\EE)}

∎

is Calabi-Yau, the condition

Remark 4.11

By Theorem

5.
Mutation of exceptional objects

In Section
3
, we construct a resolution of a sheaf by using the mutation of exceptional objects.
In this section, we recall the definition of exceptional objects and mutation of them, and explain how to find resolutions that we used in Section
3
.

5.1.
Definition

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category with finite dimensional
\Hom
spaces.

Definition 5.1
.

(i)

An object
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{D}
is called an
exceptional object
if

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}[i])=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\CC&\mbox{if $i=0$,}\\
0&\mbox{if $i\neq 0$.}\end{array}\right.

(ii)

A sequence of exceptional objects
\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{r}
is called an
exceptional collection
if
\RHom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{E}_{l},\mathcal{E}_{k})=0
for all
1\leq k<l\leq r
.

(iii)

An exceptional collection
\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{r}
is
full
if it generates the whole category
\mathcal{D}
. In such case, we write

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{r}\rangle.

Example 5.2

(
[
3
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

(1)

An
n
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has a full exceptional collection consisting of line bundles called the Beilinson collection

\D(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\langle\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}(1),\mathcal{O}(2),\dots,\mathcal{O}(n)\rangle.

(2)

Let
V
be a four dimensional symplectic vector space and
\LGr(V)
the Lagrangian Grassmannian of
V
.
Kuznetsov found a full exceptional collection

\D(\LGr(V))=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\LGr},\Sub(1),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(1),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(2)\rangle.

For an object
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{D}
, we define subcategories
\mathcal{E}^{\bot},{}^{\bot}\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\bot}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\RHom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0\}

\displaystyle{}^{\bot}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\RHom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})=0\}.

The following lemma is useful.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{r}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{r}\rangle

be two full exceptional collections with the same length.
Let

\mathcal{E}_{j}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{j}

Then, we have

\mathcal{E}_{i}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}

up to shift.

Proof.

This Lemma follows from the fact

{}^{\bot}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{}^{\bot}\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{i+1}^{\bot}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{E}_{r}^{\bot}=\D(\Spec\CC)\otimes_{\CC}\mathcal{E}_{i}.

For this fact, see

]
.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let

in

Similarly, for an object

in

through

5.2.
Application for finding resolutions

Lemma 5.7
.

There exist the following exact sequences on
\LGr(V)

(1)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)\to\Sub(-2)^{\oplus 4}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)^{\oplus 11}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)^{\oplus 5}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}\to 0
.

(2)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)^{\oplus 5}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}^{\oplus 11}\to\Sub(1)^{\oplus 4}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(1)\to 0.

(3)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}^{\oplus 5}\to\Sub(1)^{\oplus 4}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(1)^{\oplus 5}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(2)\to 0
.

Proof.

Let us consider a full exceptional collection

\D(\LGr(V))=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3),\Sub(-2),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2),\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-1)\rangle.

To prove (1), we mutate
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)
over an exceptional collection
\Sub(-2),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2),\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-1)
.
By Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
5.3
, there is an isomorphism

\RRR_{\Sub(-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3))\simeq\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)}\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)\otimes\omega_{\LGr}^{-1})

up to shift.
Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)\otimes\omega_{\LGr}^{-1}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}
.

First, we have

\RHom_{\LGr(V)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3),\Sub(-2))\simeq\CC^{4}

and hence the object
\RRR_{\Sub(-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3))[1]
lies on an exact triangle

\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)\xrightarrow{\text{ev}}\Sub(-2)^{\oplus 4}\to\RRR_{\Sub(-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3))[1]\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)[1].

Since
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)
and
\Sub(-2)^{\oplus 4}
are vector bundles on
\LGr(V)
, the map
ev
should be injective and hence the object

\RRR_{\Sub(-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3))[1]
is a sheaf on
\LGr(V)
.
Thus, we put

\mathcal{F}:=\RRR_{\Sub(-2)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3))[1].

Next, we have
\RHom_{\LGr(V)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr})\simeq\CC^{5}
and hence

\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr})[-1]\simeq\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr}.

Moreover, an easy computation shows that
\RHom_{\LGr(V)}(\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2),\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr})\simeq\CC^{11}
and hence the object

\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)}(\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr})
lies on the exact sequence

\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)^{\oplus 11}\xrightarrow{\text{ev}}\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr}\to\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)}(\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr})\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)^{\oplus 11}[1].

From the above computation, the object
\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)}(\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr})
should be a sheaf on
\LGr(V)
(up to shift)
whose generic rank is equal to
7
.
Thus the map
ev
is surjective and
\LLL_{\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)}(\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr})[-1]\simeq\mathcal{F}
.

Summarising the above arguments, we have three exact sequences:

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-3)\to\Sub(-2)^{\oplus 4}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-2)^{\oplus 11}\to\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr}\to 0,~\text{and}

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega_{\PP^{4}}^{1}|_{\LGr}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)^{\oplus 5}\to\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}\to 0.

Combining these three exact sequences gives the exact sequence (1).

To obtain (2), take the dual of (1), and then apply
\otimes\mathcal{O}(-2)
.

The sequence (3) is proved using a similar argument as in (1).
This exact sequence comes from the right mutation of
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(-1)
over an exceptional collection
\mathcal{O}_{\LGr},\Sub(1),\mathcal{O}_{\LGr}(1)
.
∎

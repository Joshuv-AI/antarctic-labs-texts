Uniqueness of Obstacles in Riemannian Manifolds from Travelling Times
Tal Gurfinkel, Lyle Noakes, Luchezar Stoyanov

Abstract
Suppose that K K and L L are two disjoint unions of strictly convex obstacles with the same set of travelling times, contained in an n n -dimensional Riemannian manifold M M (where n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ). Under some natural curvature conditions on M M , and provided that no geodesic intersects more than two components in K K or L L , we show that K = L K=L .

Boundary rigidity problems have been important in Riemannian geometry for over one hundred years, thanks to their practical applications in tomography and geophysics
[

We also consider an inverse problem related to boundary rigidity, concerning the recovery of the boundary of many disjoint obstacles contained within an exterior body, from scattering data known only along the boundary of the exterior body. Classically boundary rigidity problems make use of data given by the geodesic flow, while our data will be determined by the billiard flow. Note that we aim to determine the boundary of several bodies while in boundary rigidity problems this information is already known and the objective is to recover the metric on the given manifold.

The Propagation of Convex Fronts

Let

h(Y,Z)=\langle\nabla_{Y}Z,N\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{Y}N,Z\rangle,

\langle-sY,Z\rangle=h(Y,Z)

Now define the submanifold

Here

to

along each geodesic normal to

and

of

and

respectively. When we do so, we take the parallel translates of vectors

from

to

along the geodesic. The purpose of this section is to describe, as directly as possible, the evolution of the curvature of

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that
\gamma_{x,N(x)}
does not undergo any reflections for all
x\in X
, and all
t_{0}<t<t_{1}
.
Then for all
t_{0}<t<t_{1}
, the shape operator
s
of
X_{t}
satisfies the following differential equation,

\dot{s}Y=R(N,Y)N-s^{2}Y,

(3)

for all

tangent to
X
. Moreover, if

is a principal curvature of

then it satisfies the following differential equation:

\dot{k}=-sec_{M}(N,V)-k^{2},

(4)

where
V
is the principal eigenvector corresponding to

Proof.

We compute the derivative:

\displaystyle\dot{s}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{N}\nabla_{Y}N

\displaystyle=R(N,Y)N+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{N}N+\nabla_{[N,Y]}N.

Consider the term
[N,Y]
, by the symmetry of the connection we have:

\displaystyle[N,Y]=\nabla_{N}Y-\nabla_{Y}N=-\nabla_{Y}N=-sY.

Now since
\nabla_{N}N=0
we have,

\displaystyle\dot{s}Y

\displaystyle=R(N,Y)N-\nabla_{sY}N

\displaystyle=R(N,Y)N-s^{2}Y.

Note that the eigenvalues of

are the principal curvatures, so given an eigenvector
V
of

such that

and
\langle V,V\rangle=1
, we have

. Thus,

\displaystyle\dot{k}

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{s}V,V\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle R(N,V)N-s^{2}V,V\rangle

\displaystyle=-sec_{M}(N,V)-k^{2}.

Recall that by
sec_{M}(N,V)
, we denote the sectional curvature of

with respect to
N
and
V
.

Proposition 6

Suppose

Proof.

Shrink

We begin by taking Fermi coordinates about

9

Hence, taking inner products with

Now taking the covariant derivative we get

\nabla_{\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{j}}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial x}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial^{2}t}{\partial{x_{i}}\partial{x_{j}}}N+\nabla_{\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{i}}}N.

Therefore we note that

Proposition 7
.

Let
q:[0,\ell]\to X
be an arc-length parameterised curve on a front
X

q

(16)

Where
k_{\min}
is the minimum principal curvature of

d_{X}
and

X

X_{t}

Proof.

We begin by noting that

is a variation through geodesics. We also note that

\displaystyle\frac{D}{\partial t}J_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{\partial t}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}q_{t}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{\partial u}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q_{t}(u)

\displaystyle=s_{t}J_{u}(t).

Here
s_{t}
is the shape operator of
X_{t}
. To bound

J_{s}(t)
, so consider the function

\displaystyle\dot{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|}\left\langle\frac{D}{\partial t}J_{u}(t),J_{u}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|}\left\langle s_{t}J_{u}(t),J_{u}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|\left\langle s_{t}\frac{J_{u}(t)}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|},\frac{J_{u}(t)}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=f(t)\left\langle s_{t}\frac{J_{u}(t)}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|},\frac{J_{u}(t)}{\left|\left|J_{u}(t)\right|\right|}\right\rangle.

It now follows that the solution to

\displaystyle d_{X_{t}}(q_{t}(0),q_{t}(\ell))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\ell}f(t)\ du\geq\int_{0}^{\ell}e^{tk_{\min}}\ du=\ell e^{tk_{\min}}.

This is
eq.

16

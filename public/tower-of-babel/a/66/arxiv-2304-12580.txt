1.
Introduction

In this paper, we would like to study weak solutions of the following equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d}{dt}\left[k*(u-u_{0})\right]=\operatorname{div}\left(|u|^{m}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\right)&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)\,,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{in}~~\mathbb{R}^{N}\,,\end{array}\right.

where
m\geq 1
,
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
, and space dimension
N\geq 2
. The symbol
(-\Delta)^{-s}
denotes by the inverse of the fractional Laplacian operator, and kernel
k\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is of
\mathcal{PC}
type, see Definition
2.1
. Through the paper, we denote

k*u(t)=\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}k(t-\tau)u(\tau)\,d\tau\,.

A typical kernel for our study is
k(t)=g_{1-\alpha}(t)e^{-\nu t}
,
\nu\geq 0
, where
g_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
(the so-called Riemann–Liouville kernel), and
\Gamma(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{\beta-1}e^{-\lambda}\,d\lambda
,
\beta>0
is the usual gamma function. In particular, if we take
\nu=0
, then
\frac{d}{dt}\left[k*(u-u_{0})\right]
becomes the time-fractional derivative of Caputo-type. In this case, we denote it by
\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u(t)
for short.

For a definitive study, we would like to consider
k(t)=g_{1-\alpha}(t)
in this paper, even our proofs below also hold for
k(t)=g_{1-\alpha}(t)e^{-\nu t}
,
\nu\geq 0
. Moreover, we always assume that initial data
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
except Theorem
1.3
.

When
\alpha=1
, and
s=0
, Eq (
1
) is a porous medium equation. This model arises from considering a compressible fluid with a nonnegative density distribution
u(x,t)
, and with Darcy’s law leading to the equation

(1.3)

u_{t}-\operatorname{div}(u\vec{\mu})=0\,,

where
\vec{\mu}=\nabla{\bf p}
is the potential, and
{\bf p}
is the pressure. Many other different relations between the density,
the potential, and the pressure arising in the applications. For example, the model
proposed by Leibenzon and Muskat states a law in which
{\bf p}=h(u)
, where
h
is a
nondecreasing scalar function (see more examples in
[
31
]
). In the paper, we study a potential that takes into account long-range interactions, namely
{\bf p}=(-\Delta)^{-s}u
.
Eq (
1.3
) in terms of nonlocal pressure has been studied by many authors in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
29
]
and the references cited therein. There are many questions, addressed to equations of this type, which are the object of active research, such as those pertaining to existence and uniqueness, regularity, the behavior of solutions in short time and in large time, and so on.

In
[
8
]
, Caputo introduced another model of the porous medium equation

(1.4)

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u-\operatorname{div}\left(\kappa(u)\nabla u\right)=f

by modifying Darcy’s law. Eq (
1.4
) is a time-fractional diffusion equation. A strong motivation for investigating such equation comes from physics and engineering for memory effect, viscoelasticity, porous media, etc (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
15
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
and the references therein).

At the moment, we want to present several mathematical works for the time-fractional equations in order to motivate our research. In
[
35
]
, Zacher proved the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to certain abstract evolutionary integro-differential equations in Hilbert spaces

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\frac{d}{dt}\left<k*\left(u(t)-u_{0}\right),v\right>_{\mathcal{H}}+a\left(t,u(t),v\right)=\left<f(t),v\right>_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}},\quad v\in\mathcal{V},\,\text{a.e. }t\in(0,T)\,,\\
&u(0)=u_{0}\,,\\
&u\in H^{1}_{2}\left([0,T];\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathcal{V}\right)\,,\end{array}\right.

where
a:(0,T)\times\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
coercive bilinear form; and
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}
are real separable Hilbert spaces such that
\mathcal{V}
is densely, and continuously embedded into
\mathcal{H}
, and

\mathcal{V}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. We would like to mention that Allen
[
1
]
also obtained the unique result for a different class of weak solutions that correspond to the Marchaud derivative.

Furthermore, the short-time and long-time behavior of solutions of the time-fractional diffusion equations of non-divergence and divergence form have been studied by the authors in
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
22
,
32
,
33
]
, and the references cited therein. In particular, such behavior have been obtained for mild solutions, represented via Duhamel’s formula. For a simple introduction and for our proof below, let us consider the following equation

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=\Delta u+f,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)\,,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,.\end{array}\right.

It is known that if
u_{0}
and
f
are sufficiently regular, then Eq (
1.6
) possesses a unique mild solution

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Z(x-\xi,t)u_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi+\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Y(x-\xi,t-\tau)f(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi d\tau

(1.7)

\displaystyle:=Z(t)*u_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}Y(t-\tau)*f(\tau)\,d\tau\,,

with

\widehat{Z}(\xi,t)=E_{\alpha,1}(-t^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}),\,\text{ and }\,\widehat{Y}(\xi,t)=t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(-t^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}\right)\,,

where
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
is
the Mittag–Leffler function.

Concerning the pointwise estimates of fundamental solutions
Z(x,t),Y(x,t)
, we refer to
[
12
, Chapter 5]
for details, see also the seminal work by Kochubei
[
19
,
20
]
, and by
Eidelman–Kochubei
[
13
]
. By exploiting the pointwise estimates of the fundamental solutions, one obtains the
L^{r}
-decay estimates of
Z(x,t),Y(x,t)
(see, e.g.,
[
17
,
18
,
22
]
).

Precisely, for
t>0
we have

(1.8)

\|Z(t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)},\text{ if }1\leq r<\kappa_{1}(N),\,\kappa_{1}(N)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{N}{N-2}&\text{ if }N\geq 3\,,\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right..

Note that if
N\leq 2
, then (
1.8
) holds true for all
r\in[1,\infty]
.
And,

(1.9)

\|Z(t)\|_{L^{\kappa_{1}(N),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\alpha}\,,\text{ if }N\geq 3\,,

where
L^{q,\infty}
is the weak-
L^{q}
space.

For
Y(x,t)
, one has

(1.10)

\|Y(t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{\alpha-1-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\,,\text{ if }1\leq r<\kappa_{2}(N)\,,N>4\,,

with
\kappa_{2}(N)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\frac{N}{N-4},\text{ if }N>4,\\
&\infty,\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.
. If
N<4
, then (
1.10
) holds true for all
r\in[1,\infty]
.

In addition,

(1.11)

\|Y(t)\|_{L^{\kappa_{2}(N),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\alpha-1}\,,\text{ if }N>4\,.

For our purpose later, we also present the
L^{r}
-estimate of
\nabla Y
.

Denote
\kappa_{3}(N)=\frac{N}{N-3}
if
N>3
, and
\kappa_{3}(N)=\infty
otherwise. Then, the following statements hold true

(1.12)

\|\nabla Y(t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\,,\text{ if }1\leq r<\kappa_{3}(N),\,N>3\,.

If
N\leq 3
, then (
1.12
) also holds for
r\in[1,\infty]
.

When
N>3
, and
r=\kappa_{3}(N)
,
(
1.12
) only holds in the weak-
L^{r}
. That is

(1.13)

\|\nabla Y(t)\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\alpha-1}\,.

From
\eqref{1.7}-\eqref{1.10d}
, we can deduce the
L^{q}-L^{p}
decay estimates of mild solutions of Eq (
1.6
). For instance, if
f=0
, then it follows from (
1.8
) and the Young inequality that

(1.14)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|Z*u_{0}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|Z(t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

provided that
1\leq r<\kappa_{1}(N)
,
N\geq 3
, and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+1=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}
.

We also note that such decay estimates have been established by the authors in
[
18
]
for the mild solutions to Eq (
1.6
) type with fractional diffusion, and by the authors in
[
22
]
for the mild solutions to Keller–Segel type time-space fractional diffusion equation.

On the other hand, to obtain the above estimates for weak solutions of time-fractional diffusion equations, one uses the energy method. For example, the authors
[
17
]
studied the decay estimates of weak solutions to the nonlocal in-time diffusion equation

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=\operatorname{div}\left(A(x,t)\nabla u\right),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)\,,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,,\end{array}\right.

where
A\in L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\big)
satisfies the elliptic condition. Particularly,
the authors obtained the
L^{2}
decay estimate of weak solutions to Eq (
1.15
):

(1.16)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\alpha N}{N+4}},\quad t>0\,,

provided that
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Note that
constant
C>0
in (
1.16
) depends on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and the parameters involved, see
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Compare (
1.16
) to (
1.14
) when
p=r=2
,
q=1
, one observes that the
L^{2}
decay estimates of mild solution and weak solution are different. This point of view is very different from the fundamental heat equation with the Gaussian kernel
G(x,t)=\displaystyle(4\pi t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
.

Besides, the energy method has been used by Vergara–Zacher
[
32
]
in order to obtain the decay estimates of weak solutions of Eq (
1.15
), of time-fractional
p
-Laplace equation, and of the time-fractional porous medium equation with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. In addition, this method is very useful in studying the regularity of weak solutions. For instance, Zacher
[

obtained a Hölder continuous regularity of weak solutions of the time-fractional diffusion equations.

It is interesting to mention that Giga-Namba
[
14
]
studied the Hamilton–Jacobi equations with Caputo’s
time fractional derivative in
\mathbb{T}^{N}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. By introducing a suitable notion of viscosity solutions, the authors established the unique existence, stability, and regularity results of a viscosity solution.

Now, we discuss some recent results concerning our equation. When
m=1
, and
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, Allen–Caffarelli–Vasseur
[

with the same assumption of exponential decay at infinity on initial data. In addition, they proved the finite speed of propagation of solutions. That is solution is compactly supported for any time

if the initial datum has compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We emphasize that the proofs of the existence of solutions to Eq (
1

in

From our knowledge, Eq (

\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1

possibly changed sign. Thus, we would like to study weak solutions of Eq (

And,

Next, we denote

Through the paper, the constant

Finally, the notation

Main results

We introduce the notion of weak solutions to Eq (
1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in X
. We say that
u
is a weak solution of Eq (
1
) if
u\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;X\big)
,
\Theta(u)\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
for
T>0
, and for every test function
\varphi\in H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
with
\varphi(T)=0
there holds

(1.17)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\varphi_{t}[g_{1-\alpha}*(u-u_{0})]\,dxdt=\int_{Q_{T}}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt\,.

Then, our first result is of the existence of weak solutions to Eq (
1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
,
0<\alpha<1
,
m\geq 1
, and let
u_{0}\in X
. Then, Eq (
1
) has a weak solution

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
. Moreover, for any
q\in[1,\infty]
, there holds true

(1.18)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,\quad\text{for }\,t\in(0,\infty)\,.

Furthermore, for any

we have

(1.19)

\int^{T}_{0}\big\||u|^{\theta_{q}-1}u(t)\big\|^{2}_{H^{1-s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,dt\leq C(\alpha,m,q)T^{1-\alpha}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

with
\theta_{q}=\frac{m+q}{2}
,

.

Remark 1.1

It follows from the regularity of the weak solution that

converges to

in

Next, we point out the

2.
Preliminary results

2.1.
Regularization of the kernel

We first define the kernels of
\mathcal{PC}
type.

Definition 2.1
.

A kernel
k\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is said to be of type
\mathcal{PC}
if it is nonnegative and nonincreasing, and there exists a
kernel
l\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
k*l=1
in
(0,\infty)
. In this case, we say that
(k,l)
is a
\mathcal{PC}
pair and write
(k,l)\in\mathcal{PC}
.

An important example is given by

(2.1)

k(t)=g_{1-\alpha}(t)e^{-\nu t},\,\text{ and }\,l(t)=g_{\alpha}(t)e^{-\nu t}+\nu\big(1*[g_{\alpha}(\cdot)e^{-\nu\cdot}]\big)(t),\quad t>0\,,

with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\nu\geq 0
. Both kernels are strictly positive and decreasing. We refer to
[
17
,
32
]
for the fundamental properties of these kernels.
In particular, if we take
\nu=0
in (
2.1
), then
(k,l)=(g_{1-\alpha},g_{\alpha})
.

Next, we introduce a smoothing effect to
g_{\alpha}
by using the Yosida approximation.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space. Then,
for
p\geq 1
we define the operator
B
by

\displaystyle Bu=\frac{d}{dt}(g_{1-\alpha}*u),\quad D(B)=\left\{u\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H}):g_{1-\alpha}*u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})\right\}\,,

where the zero means the vanishing at
t=0
.
Its Yosida approximations
B_{n}
, defined by
B_{n}=nB(n+B)^{-1}
,
n\geq 1
enjoy the property that for any
u\in D(B)
, one has
B_{n}u\rightarrow Bu
in
L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{n}u=\frac{d}{dt}\left(g_{1-\alpha,n}*u\right),\quad u\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})\,,

where
g_{1-\alpha,n}=ns_{\alpha,n}
, and
s_{\alpha,n}
is the solution of the scalar-valued Volterra equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle s_{\alpha,n}(t)+n\left(s_{\alpha,n}*g_{\alpha}\right)(t)=1,\quad t>0,\,n\geq 1.

Denote
h_{\alpha,n}
by the resolvent kernel, associated with
ng_{\alpha}
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha,n}\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),\quad h_{\alpha,n}(t)+n\left(h_{\alpha,n}*g_{\alpha}\right)(t)=ng_{\alpha}(t),\quad t>0,\,n\geq 1\,,

By taking the convolution (
2.4
) with
g_{1-\alpha}
, and by using the fact
g_{\alpha}*g_{1-\alpha}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(g_{1-\alpha}*h_{\alpha,n}\right)(t)+n\left(\left[g_{1-\alpha}*h_{\alpha,n}\right]*g_{\alpha}\right)(t)=n\,,\quad t>0,

which shows that

(2.5)

\displaystyle g_{1-\alpha,n}=ns_{\alpha,n}=g_{1-\alpha}*h_{\alpha,n}\,,\quad n\geq 1.

In view of (
2.5
), we conclude that the kernels
g_{1-\alpha,n}\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
are nonnegative, and nonincreasing for all
n\geq 1
.

As a consequence, for any function
f\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
, there holds true

(2.6)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha,n}*f\rightarrow f\text{ in }L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})\,,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, defining
u=g_{\alpha}*f
, we have
u\in D(B)
, and

\displaystyle B_{n}u

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left(g_{1-\alpha,n}*u\right)=\frac{d}{dt}\left(g_{1-\alpha}*g_{\alpha}*h_{\alpha,n}*f\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=h_{\alpha,n}*f\rightarrow Bu=f\quad\text{ in }L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{H})\,,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In particular,
g_{1-\alpha,n}\rightarrow g_{1-\alpha}
in
L^{1}(0,T)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Now, we point out a fundamental identity for integro-differential operators of the form

\frac{d}{dt}(k*u)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
k\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
, and
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then a straightforward computation shows that for a sufficiently smooth function
u
on
(0,T)
one has for a.a.
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}\big(u(t)\big)\frac{d}{dt}(k*u)(t)=\frac{d}{dt}k*G(u)(t)+\big[-G\big(u(t)\big)+G^{\prime}\big(u(t)\big)u(t)\big]k(t)

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}\left\{G\big(u(t-s)\big)-G\big(u(t)\big)-G^{\prime}\big(u(t)\big)[u(t-s)-u(t)]\right\}\big(-\dot{k}(s)\big)ds\,.

Remark 2.1
.

An integrated version of (
2.1
) can be found in
[
16
, Lemma 18.4.1]
.

On the other hand, observe that the last term in (
2.1
) is nonnegative in case
G
is convex and
k
is nonincreasing. This fact will be used repeatedly below.

2.2.
Fractional Laplacian and Sobolev spaces

Let
p\geq 1
, and
s\in(0,1)
. For a given domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define the fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

Moreover, we also denote the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space by

\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, endowed with the seminorm

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

In particular, we denote
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which turns out to be a Hilbert space. It is well-known that we have the equivalent characterization

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\mathcal{F}\{u\}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform, and we have

\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\mathcal{F}\{u\}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

In addition, for
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the fractional Laplacian is defined by

(2.9)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(N,s)P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)\}.

Then,

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+C\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Next, for
s>0
we denote

(-\Delta)^{-s}f=\mathcal{I}_{2s}[f]=\frac{1}{C(N,s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{N-2s}}\,dy\,,

the Riesz potential, where
C(N,s)=\frac{\pi^{N/2}2^{2s}\Gamma\left(s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N-2s}{2}\right)}
(see
[
30
, Chapter 5]
).
Moreover, the following inequality holds true

(2.10)

\|\mathcal{I}_{s}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
1<p<\frac{N}{s}
, and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{N}
.

In particular, if
s=2
, then
\mathcal{I}_{2}
is the inverse of the Laplacian operator.
Note that if
N=s=2
, then the above integral is a convolution of
f
and
\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x|
instead of
C|x-y|^{s-N}
.

In addition, it is known that
\mathcal{R}(f)=\nabla\mathcal{I}_{1}(f)
the Riesz transforms, and
\mathcal{R}
maps
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
1<p<\infty
, see
[
30
, Chapter 3]
.

Next, we recall a generalized version of the Stroock–Varopoulos inequality by Hung–Vázquez
[
27
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
s\in(0,1)
, and let
\psi,\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\psi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\geq 0
. Then,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\psi(f)(-\Delta)^{s}\left[\phi(f)\right]dx\geq 0\,.

If we take
\psi(f)=f
, then we obtain

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(-\Delta)^{s}\left[\phi(f)\right]\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Phi(f)\right|^{2}\,dx\,,

where
\phi^{\prime}=(\Phi^{\prime})^{2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

A version of this lemma for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
was obtained by the authors in
[
10
,
29
]
.

Finally, we recall the interpolation inequality.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq r,q,p\leq\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{q},\quad\theta\in(0,1)\,,

and let
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, we have
u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u\|^{\theta}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

In particular, if
u\in X
, then for any
1<r<\infty
, there holds true

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u\|^{\frac{1}{r}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|^{1-\frac{1}{r}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u\|_{X}\,.

3.
Regularizing equations

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we study a regularizing equation to Eq (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u-\varepsilon\Delta u=\operatorname{div}\Theta(u)&\text{in }Q_{T}\,,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\,.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then, we look for the existence of mild solutions of Eq (
3
) as a fixed point of the map

\mathcal{T}(u)(t)=Z_{\varepsilon}(t)*u_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla Y_{\varepsilon}(t-\tau)*\Theta(u)(\tau)\,d\tau\,,

where
Z_{\varepsilon},Y_{\varepsilon}
are the fundamental solutions to Eq (
1.6
) with diffusion
\varepsilon\Delta u
, modified from
Z,Y
in (
1
).

In fact, we show that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction mapping from
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;X\big)\to L^{\infty}\big(0,T;X\big)
for some
T>0
.
Then, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)

m\geq 1
, and
u_{0}\in X
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, then Eq (
3
) has a unique mild solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;X\big)
for some
T>0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The proof follows by way of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume hypotheses as in Theorem
3.1
. Then, there exists a time
T_{0}\in(0,1)
, and a real number
\gamma>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}(u)-\mathcal{T}(v)\big\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}\leq CM_{0}^{m}T_{0}^{\gamma}\big\|u-v\big\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}\,,\quad\forall u,v\in\overline{B(0,M_{0})}\,,

where
\overline{B(0,M_{0})}
is the closed ball in
L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)
with center at
0
, and radius
M_{0}
; and constant
C>0
only depends on the parameters involved such as
\varepsilon,N,m,\alpha,s
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let us drop the dependence on
\varepsilon
of
Z_{\varepsilon}
, and
Y_{\varepsilon}
for short. The reader should keep in mind that the estimates of
Y_{\varepsilon}(x,t)
below depend on
\varepsilon
.

By (
1.12
), applying Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}(u)-\mathcal{T}(v)\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla Y(t-\tau)*\big[\Theta(u)(\tau)-\Theta(v)(\tau)\big]\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\nabla Y(t-\tau)\big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\Theta(u)(\tau)-\Theta(v)(\tau)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,d\tau

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\big\|\Theta(u)(\tau)-\Theta(v)(\tau)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,d\tau\,,

with
\frac{1}{p}+1=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}
,

r\in\big[1,\kappa_{3}(N)\big)
, and
C=C(\varepsilon,N,\alpha,r)>0
.

It is of course that we will consider
p=1
and
p=\infty
alternatively in (
3
), and
r\in\big[1,\kappa_{3}(N)\big)
will be chosen with respect to
p
.

At the moment, we want to estimate (
3
). Observe that

\displaystyle\Theta(u)-\Theta(v)

\displaystyle=(|u|^{m}-|v|^{m})\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}[u]+|v|^{m}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}[u-v]

\displaystyle=(|u|^{m}-|v|^{m})\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u]+|v|^{m}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u-v]\,.

By Hölder’s inequality and the fact that
\mathcal{R}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\Theta(u)(t)-\Theta(v)(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big(|u|^{m}-|v|^{m}\big)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u](t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\big\||v|^{m}(t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u-v](t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\big(|u|^{m}-|v|^{m}\big)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u](t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\big\||v|^{m}(t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u-v](t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

With the last inequality noted, it follows from the Riesz potential estimate in (
2.10
), and the interpolation inequality in Proposition
2.1
that

\displaystyle\big\|\Theta(u)(t)-\Theta(v)(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{0}^{m-1}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2(2s-1)}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v(t)\|^{m}_{L^{2m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left\|(u-v)(t)\right\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2(2s-1)}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{0}^{m}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{X}\,,\quad\forall u,v\in\overline{B(0,M_{0})}\,.

Note that
\frac{2N}{N+2(2s-1)}>1
since
N\geq 2
, and
1/2\leq s<1
.

By choosing
p=r=q=1
in (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}(u)(t)-\mathcal{T}(v)(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\big\|(u-v)(\tau)\big\|_{X}\,d\tau

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{m}T_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}\,,

for any
t\in(0,T_{0})
, with
C=C(\varepsilon,N,\alpha,m,s)
.

Next, we set
q_{s}=\frac{Nq}{N+q(2s-1)}
. Note that
q_{s}>1\Leftrightarrow q>\frac{N}{N+1-2s}
.

By a similar argument as in the proof of (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\big\|\Theta(u)(t)-\Theta(v)(t)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\left(|u|^{m}-|v|^{m}\right)(t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u](t)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\big\||v|^{m}(t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|\mathcal{I}_{2s-1}[u-v](t)\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{0}^{m-1}\big\|(u-v)(t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\big\|u(t)\big\|_{L^{q_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+M_{0}^{m}\big\|(u-v)(t)\big\|_{L^{q_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{0}^{m-1}\left(\|(u-v)(t)\|_{X}\|u(t)\|_{X}+\|v(t)\|_{X}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{X}\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{m}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{X}\,.

Pick
p=\infty
, and
q>N>\frac{N}{N+1-2s}
in (
3
). Then, observe that
r=\frac{q}{q-1}\in\big[1,\frac{N}{N-1}\big)\subset\big[1,\kappa_{3}(N)\big)
.

Inserting (
3
) into (
3
) yields

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}(u)(t)-\mathcal{T}(v)(t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{m}\int^{t}_{0}(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|(u-v)(\tau)\|_{X}\,d\tau

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{m}T_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\alpha N}{2q}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}\,,

for
t\in(0,T_{0})
.

From (
3
) and (
3
), we obtain (
3.3
) with
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\alpha N}{2q}
for

.

Therefore, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Now, it is enough to finish the proof of Theorem
3.1
. By choosing
T_{0}>0
small enough such that

(3.9)

CM_{0}^{m}T_{0}^{\gamma}<1/2\,,

then it is clear that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction mapping on
L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)
.
It remains to show that
\mathcal{T}
maps
\overline{B(0,M_{0})}
to
\overline{B(0,M_{0})}
, with

M_{0}=2\|u_{0}\|_{X}
.

Indeed, let us take

in (
3.3
). Then, for
u\in\overline{B(0,M_{0})}
, and by (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}(0)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}+CM_{0}^{m}T_{0}^{\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}(0)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{0};X\right)}+\frac{1}{2}M_{0}\,.

On the other hand, it is known that

is a probability density function. That is

\|Z(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=1\,,\quad\forall t>0\,.

Thus, it follows from Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(0)(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|Z*u_{0}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|Z(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(0)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|Z(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\quad\text{for }t>0\,.

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(0)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};X)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{X}\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2}\,.

) into (

) yields the conclusion.

This puts an end to proof of Theorem

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\big\|\operatorname{div}\big(\Theta(u)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\big)(t)\big\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\,dt

Now, we apply

to

,

which yields (

Therefore, there is a constant

Proof of Lemma

The proof is just a slight modification of the one in

By a technical problem, we divide the proof of (

i) The case

We apply (
2.1
) to
k=g_{n}
, and

. Note that

Thanks to Remark

, for a.e.

\displaystyle G^{\prime}\big(u(t)\big)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(t)\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*G(u)\right](t)+\big[-G\big(u(t)\big)+G^{\prime}\big(u(t)\big)u(t)\big]g_{n}(t)\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|u|^{q-2}u\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(t)\geq\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*|u|^{q}\right](t)+\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)|u|^{q}(t)g_{n}(t)\,.

Integrating both sides of this inequality on
\Omega
yields

(3.26)

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx\geq\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right](t)+\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)g_{n}(t)\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,.

Next, let us take
\psi=|u|^{q-2}u
,
q\geq 2
as a test function to (
3.24
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|u|^{q-2}u\frac{d}{dt}[g_{n}*(u-u_{0})]+\varepsilon(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u\right)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\,,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\frac{d}{dt}[g_{n}(t)*u_{0}(x)]\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\,.

With the last integral equation noted, it follows from (
3.26

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right](t)+\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}g_{n}(t)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\,.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,.

From the last inequality and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right](t)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\frac{1}{q}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\left(\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)\right](t)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\,.

By (
2.6
) and (
2.1
), we can pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
in (
3
) in order to obtain

(3.29)

\displaystyle F(t)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\leq 0\,,

where

(3.30)

\frac{1}{q}\left(\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)=g_{\alpha}*F(t)\,.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=(q-1)\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\cdot|u|^{m+q-2}\nabla u(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{m+q-1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\cdot\nabla\left(|u|^{m+q-2}u\right)(x,t)\,dx

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{m+q-1}\int_{\Omega}\big(|u|^{m+q-2}u\big)(-\Delta)^{1-s}u(x,t)\,dx\,.

Thanks to the Stroock–Varopoulos inequality in Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(|u|^{m+q-2}u\big)(-\Delta)^{1-s}u(x,t)\,dx\geq\frac{m+q-1}{\theta^{2}_{q}}\big\||u|^{\theta_{q}-1}u(t)\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}\,,

with

.

With this inequality note, it follows from (
3
) that

(3.32)

\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx\geq\frac{q-1}{\theta_{q}^{2}}\big\||u|^{\theta_{q}-1}u(t)\big\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1-s}(\Omega)}\,.

In addition, it is easy to verify that

(3.33)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla|u|^{q-2}u(x,t)\,dx=(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}(x,t)\,dx\geq 0\,.

From (
3.29

3.32
), and (

F(t)\leq 0
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. Since

\frac{1}{q}\left(\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)=g_{\alpha}*F(t)\leq 0\,,

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,
which yields (
3.16
).

iii) The case

.
To prove (
3.16

-estimate
q\geq 2

G=S_{\eta}(u)

\chi_{\eta}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm sign}(r)&\text{ if }|r|\geq\eta\\
\frac{r}{\eta}&\text{ if }|r|<\eta\end{array}\right.,\quad S_{\eta}(r)=\int^{r}_{0}\chi_{\eta}(\tau)\,d\tau

Obviously,

Then, we obtain as in (
3.25

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\eta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\|S_{\eta}(u)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right](t)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big(-S_{\eta}(u)+\chi_{\eta}(u)u\big)(x,t)g_{n}(t)\,dx\,.

A simple calculation shows that

-S_{\eta}(u)+\chi_{\eta}(u)u=\frac{u^{2}}{2\eta}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|<\eta\}}+\frac{\eta}{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|\geq\eta\}}\,.

With this identity noted, it follows from the last inequality that

(3.37)

\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\eta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\|S_{\eta}(u)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right](t)\,.

Next, we pick
\chi_{\eta}(u)
as a test function to (
3.24
). Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\eta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}[g_{n}*(u-u_{0})](x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\eta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\eta}(u(x,t))u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

With the last inequality noted, it follows from (
3.37
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\left(\|S_{\eta}(u)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\right](t)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

At the moment, let us set

\|S_{\eta}(u(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=g_{\alpha}*F^{(\eta)}_{1}(t)\,.

Thanks to (
2.6
) and(
2.1
), we can pass to the limit
as
n\to\infty
in the last inequality in order to get

\displaystyle F^{(\eta)}_{1}(t)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq g_{1-\alpha}(t)\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\,.

Since
\chi_{\eta}(\cdot)
is a nondecreasing function, then we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\chi^{\prime}_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\geq 0\,.

In addition, observe that
\chi^{\prime}_{\eta}(l)=\eta^{m}\chi^{\prime}_{\eta^{m+1}}(|l|^{m}l)
,

. Thus, we have

\displaystyle|u|^{m}\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)=|u|^{m}\chi^{\prime}_{\eta}(u)\nabla u

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{m}}{m+1}\chi^{\prime}_{\eta^{m+1}}(|u|^{m}u)\nabla(|u|^{m}u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{m}}{m+1}\nabla\chi_{\eta^{m+1}}(|u|^{m}u)\,.

With the identities noted, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\cdot|u|^{m}\nabla\chi_{\eta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{m}}{m+1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\cdot\nabla\chi_{\eta^{m+1}}(|u|^{m}u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{m}}{m+1}\int_{\Omega}\big[(-\Delta)^{1-s}u\big]\big[\chi_{\eta^{m+1}}(|u|^{m}u)\big](x,t)\,dx\geq 0,

since the Stroock–Varopoulos inequality, see Lemma
2.2
.

Thus, it follows from (

F^{(\eta)}_{1}(t)\leq g_{1-\alpha}(t)\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big)\,.

Finally, passing to the limit as
\eta\to 0

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\eta\to 0}g_{\alpha}*F^{(\eta)}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\eta\to 0}g_{\alpha}*g_{1-\alpha}(t)\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\|S_{\eta}(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big)=0,

since

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}S_{\eta}(w)=w
in

for

iv) The case

The proof is similar to the one of the case

It is clear that function

Moreover, for
\delta>0
fixed, one can verify easily that
T_{\delta}(u)\in L^{2}\big((0,T);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)
for

. Next, we set

G_{\delta}(r)=\int^{r}_{0}T_{\delta}(\tau)\,d\tau=\left(\frac{|r|^{q}-\delta^{q}}{q}+\frac{\delta^{q}}{4}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{|r|>\delta\}}+\delta^{q-2}\left(r^{2}-\delta|r|+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\frac{\delta}{2}<|r|\leq\delta\}}\,.

Obviously,

with

, and

Observe that

\displaystyle-G_{\delta}\big(u\big)+G^{\prime}_{\delta}\big(u\big)u=

\displaystyle\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)|u|^{q}+\frac{\delta^{q}}{q}-\frac{\delta^{q}}{4}\right]\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}+\delta^{q-2}\left(u^{2}_{R}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\frac{\delta}{2}<|u|\leq\delta\}}\,.

With the identity noted, it follows from the indicated inequality that

\displaystyle T_{\delta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(t)\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*G_{\delta}(u)\right](t)+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{n}(t)|u|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(t)\,.

Integrating the last inequality on
\Omega

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\big\|G_{\delta}(u)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right](t)

(3.39)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(x,t)\,dx\,.

By picking
\psi=T_{\delta}(u)
as a test function to (
3.24
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[T_{\delta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\big(u-u_{0}\big)\right]+\varepsilon(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)\right](x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)\frac{d}{dt}(g_{n}*u)(x,t)\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle=g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

Inserting (
3
) into the last integral identity yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\big\|G_{\delta}(u)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right](t)+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{n}(t)\big\||u|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\,.

We rewrite the inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[g_{n}*\left(\big\|G_{\delta}(u)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\right](t)+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{n}(t)\big\||u|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(h_{n}*\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}[h_{n}*\Theta(u)]\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq g_{n}(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-g_{n}(t)\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,.

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
in (
3
) yields

\displaystyle F^{(\delta)}_{2}(t)+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{1-\alpha}(t)\big\||u|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx

(3.41)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\leq g_{1-\alpha}(t)\int_{\Omega}T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)u_{0}(x)\,dx-g_{1-\alpha}(t)\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,,

with

\big\|G_{\delta}(u(t))\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=g_{\alpha}*F^{(\delta)}_{2}(t)\,.

Again, observe that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx,\,\int_{\Omega}\Theta(u)\cdot\nabla T_{\delta}(u)(x,t)\,dx\geq 0

since
T^{\prime}_{\delta}(\cdot)\geq 0
, and the Stroock–Varopoulos inequality.

Thus, it follows from (
3
) that

\displaystyle F^{(\delta)}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)g_{1-\alpha}(t)\big\||u|^{q}\mathbf{1}_{\{|u|>\delta\}}(t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

(3.42)

\displaystyle\leq g_{1-\alpha}(t)\left(\big\|T_{\delta}(u(t))u_{0}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right).

On the other hand, we deduce from the interpolation inequality that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|u(t)\|^{q/2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u(t)\|^{1-q/2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|^{q/2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{0}\|^{1-q/2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\,\quad 1<q<2\,.

Therefore,
\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t
\varepsilon
, for all
t\in(0,T)
. In particular,
|u(x,t)|^{q}
is integrable on
\Omega
. Since

for all
\delta>0

as

, then it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\big\|G_{\delta}(u)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to\frac{1}{q}\big\||u|^{q}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\,\,\big\|G_{\delta}(u_{0})\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to\frac{1}{q}\big\||u_{0}|^{q}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

as
\delta\to 0
. As a result, we obtain

(3.43)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}g_{\alpha}*F^{(\delta)}_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{q}\left(\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)\,.

Next, observe that

\big|T_{\delta}(v)\big|\leq|v|^{q-1}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{|v|>\delta\right\}}+\delta^{q-1}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{|v|\leq\delta\right\}}\,,\text{ and }T_{\delta}(v)\to|v|^{q-2}v

as
\delta\to 0
. Thus, we deduce from the dominated convergence theorem and the Hölder inequality that

\big\|T_{\delta}(u(t))u_{0}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to\big\||u|^{q-2}u(t)u_{0}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,.

From (
3.43
) and (
3.44
), we can pass to the limit as
\delta\to 0
in (
3
). Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\left(\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)\leq

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}*\left[g_{1-\alpha}(t)\left(\big\||u|^{q-2}u(t)u_{0}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}-\frac{1}{q}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right.\right.

(3.45)

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)\right]\,.

Note that we cannot apply the identity
g_{\alpha}*g_{1-\alpha}(t)=1
in (

) right now since the resulted function in the bracket (multiplying with

) is of

\displaystyle\big\||u|^{q-2}u(t)u_{0}\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u(t)\|^{q-1}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\|u(t)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,.

With the last inequality noted, we deduce from (
3
) that

This puts an end to the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

\displaystyle\big\|u(t)\big\|^{\theta_{q}}_{L^{2^{\star}\theta_{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\big\||u|^{\theta_{q}-1}u(t)\big\|_{L^{2^{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\big\||u|^{\theta_{q}-1}u(t)\big\|_{\dot{H}^{1-s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

Since

for

Hence,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|^{2\theta_{q}}_{L^{2^{\star}\theta_{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\frac{\theta^{2}_{q}}{q(q-1)}(t-\tau)^{-\alpha}\|u(\tau)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\text{for }0<\tau<t<\infty\,.

Since we are going to use an iteration method starting from here, then it is important to note that the constant
C
in (

) will not change step by step.

Put

t_{n}=t(1-2^{-n}),\quad q_{n+1}=2^{\star}\theta_{n},\quad\theta_{n}=\frac{m+q_{n}}{2},\,n\geq 0,

where
q_{0}=q
,

At the moment, we apply (

) to

,

, and

. Then,

\|u(t_{n+1})\|^{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}_{L^{q_{n+1}}}\leq C\frac{\theta^{2}_{q_{n}}}{q_{n}(q_{n}-1)}(t_{n+1}-t_{n})^{-\alpha}\|u(t_{n})\|^{q_{n}}_{L^{q_{n}}}\,,\,\lambda_{0}=\frac{N-2(1-s)}{N}\,.

By induction, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n+1})\|_{L^{q_{n+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C\frac{\theta^{2}_{q_{n}}}{q_{n}(q_{n}-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}\left[C\frac{\theta^{2}_{q_{n-1}}}{q_{n-1}(q_{n-1}-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\dots\left[C\frac{\theta^{2}_{q_{0}}}{q_{0}(q_{0}-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\times\left(t^{-\alpha}2^{n+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}\left(t^{-\alpha}2^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}q_{n+1}}}\dots\left(t^{-\alpha}2\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{n+1}q_{n+1}}}\times\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{q_{0}}{\lambda_{0}^{n+1}q_{n+1}}}_{L^{q_{0}}}\,.

It is not difficult to verify that

(5.5)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{0}^{n+1}q_{n+1}=q_{0}+\frac{m}{1-\lambda_{0}}.

Next, by using (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(t^{-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}\left(t^{-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}q_{n+1}}}\dots\left(t^{-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{n+1}q_{n+1}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=0}\lambda_{0}^{-j}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}\frac{1-\lambda^{-(n+1)}_{0}}{1-\lambda^{-1}_{0}}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\,.

Similarly, we also have

(5.7)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}C^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}^{2}q_{n+1}}}\dots C^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}=C^{\frac{1}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\,,

and

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(2^{n+1})^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}(2^{n})^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\dots 2^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}=2^{\frac{1}{(1-\lambda_{0})\left(q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m\right)}}\,.

Next, we put

Z_{n}=q_{n}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}q_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\dots q_{0}^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\,.

Let us show that
Z_{n}
is convergent as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Indeed, we consider a power series

(5.9)

\displaystyle S_{n}(s)=s^{n}\ln q_{n}+s^{n-1}\ln q_{n-1}+\dots+s^{1}\ln q_{1}+\ln q_{0}\,.

Obviously, the radius of convergence of
S_{n}(s)
is
1
. Thus,
S_{n}(\lambda_{0})
converges absolutely to a real number
S_{0}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
On the other hand, we note that

\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}\ln Z_{n}=S_{n}(\lambda_{0})\,.

Then,

(5.10)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Z_{n}=\exp\left\{\frac{S_{0}}{q_{0}+\frac{m}{1-\lambda_{0}}}\right\}\,.

By an analogue as in the proof of (
5.10
), there are two real numbers
S_{1},S_{2}>0
such that

(5.11)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(q_{n}-1)^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}(q_{n-1}-1)^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\dots(q_{0}-1)^{\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}=S_{1}\,,

and

(5.12)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{q_{n}}^{\frac{2}{\lambda_{0}q_{n+1}}}\theta_{q_{n-1}}^{\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}_{0}q_{n+1}}}\dots\theta_{q_{0}}^{\frac{2}{\lambda^{n+1}_{0}q_{n+1}}}=S_{2}\,.

By inserting (
5
), (
5.7
), (
5.8
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
), and (
5.12
) into (
5
), we find that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\alpha}{q(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{q(1-\lambda_{0})}{q(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

with
C=C(\alpha,s,N,m,q)>0
.
This yields (
1.20
).

Thus, we get the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In the beginning, we can assume that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. As soon as we establish (
1.21
) for such initial data, then we can utilize (
1.21
) as a priori estimate in order to get the existence of the weak solution with initial data
u_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
q_{0}>1
.

Now, we prove the
L^{1}-L^{p}
decay estimate of weak solutions of Eq (
1
) for
1<p<\infty
with regular initial data.
It follows from the interpolation inequality, and (
1.18
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u(t)\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u(t)\|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

Next, applying (
1.20
) in Theorem
1.2
to
q=q_{0}
yields

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim(t-t/2)^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\|u(t/2)\|^{\frac{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

By combining the two indicated inequalities, we obtain

(5.13)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\alpha\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

It remains to prove the existence of weak solutions to Eq (
1
) for
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, let
u_{0,\delta}=u_{0}*\varrho_{\delta}
,
\delta>0
, where
\{\varrho_{\delta}\}
is the standard sequence of mollifier functions. Obviously, we have

u_{0,\delta}\in X
,
\|u_{0,\delta}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

for all
\delta>0
, and
u_{0,\delta}\to u_{0}
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1\leq q\leq q_{0}
as
\delta\to 0
.

Next, for any
\delta>0
, let
u_{\delta}
be the solution of Eq (
1
), constructed as in Theorem
1.1
with initial datum
u_{0,\delta}
.

Then, applying (
1.20
) to
u_{\delta}
yields

\|u_{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad t\in(0,\infty)\,.

This implies that for any
\tau>0
fixed,
\|u_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\|_{X}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(\tau,\infty)
w.r.t
\delta>0
.
Since our proof of the existence in Theorem
1.1
is local in time, then we can mimic it in order to obtain

u_{\delta}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[\tau,T])

1\leq p<\infty
up to a subsequence for
0<\tau<T<\infty
.

As a result, one has
u_{\delta}(x,t)\to u(x,t)
for a.e.
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
by the diagonal argument up to a subsequence (not labeled). Furthermore, it is not difficult to verify that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{Q_{T}}\left(|u_{\delta}|^{m}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u_{\delta}-|u|^{m}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-s}u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt=0,\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{T})\,.

Thus, we conclude that
u
is a weak solution of Eq (
1
). Furthermore,
u
also satisfies (
1.18
) for
q\in[1,q_{0}]
.

This puts an end to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

We can obtain another the
L^{q_{0}}
-estimate by using the comparison result by the authors in
[
32
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Indeed, for any
q>q_{0}
, we deduce from the interpolation inequality in Proposition
2.1
, and (
1.18
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u(t)\|^{\sigma}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u(t)\|^{1-\sigma}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u_{0}\|^{\sigma}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u(t)\|^{1-\sigma}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,,

with
\sigma=\frac{\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{1}{q}}{1-\frac{1}{q}}\in(0,1)
.

Therefore,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\geq\|u_{0}\|^{-\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u(t)\|^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

With the last inequality noted, it follows from (
3.3
), and (
5.2
) with
q=2^{\star}\theta_{q_{0}}
that

\displaystyle g_{1-\alpha}*\left(\|u(t)\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-\|u_{0}\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)+C\left(\|u_{0}\|^{-\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u(t)\|^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)^{2\theta_{q_{0}}}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq g_{1-\alpha}*\left(\|u(t)\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-\|u_{0}\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)+C\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2\theta_{q_{0}}}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 0\,,

where
C=C(q_{0},N,m,s)>0
.

Then, it suffices to apply
[
32
, Theorem 7.1]
to
\|u(t)\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
with

\gamma=\frac{2\theta_{q_{0}}}{q_{0}(1-\sigma)}=\frac{m+q_{0}-\lambda_{0}}{q_{0}-1},\,\text{ and }\nu=C\|u_{0}\|^{-\frac{2\sigma\theta_{q_{0}}}{1-\sigma}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,.

Thus, we get

(5.15)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{1+t^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\alpha}{q_{0}\gamma}},\quad\text{for }t>0\,,

where
C^{\prime}
depends on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and the parameters involved.

A straightforward computation shows that

C^{\prime}=\|u_{0}\|^{q_{0}}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}t^{\alpha/\gamma}_{0},\quad t^{\alpha}_{0}=C^{\prime\prime}(\alpha,\gamma)\frac{\|u_{0}\|^{{q_{0}}(1-\gamma)}_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\nu}\,.

It is clear that the
L^{q_{0}}
-estimate in (
1.21
) is better than its version in (
5.15
) when
t
is large since
\frac{\alpha\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{q_{0}(1-\lambda_{0})+m}>\frac{\alpha}{\gamma q_{0}}
.

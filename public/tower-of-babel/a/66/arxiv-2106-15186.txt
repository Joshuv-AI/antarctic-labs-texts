Modulation and amplitude equations on bounded domains for nonlinear SPDEs driven by cylindrical α \alpha -stable Lévy processes Journal: SIAM
Shenglan Yuan, Dirk Bl o ¨ \rm\ddot{o} mker

Abstract
In the present work, we establish the approximation of nonlinear stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) driven by cylindrical α \alpha -stable Lévy processes via modulation or amplitude equations. We study SPDEs with a cubic nonlinearity, where the deterministic equation is close to a change of stability of the trivial solution. The natural separation of time-scales close to this bifurcation allows us to obtain an amplitude equation describing the essential dynamics of the bifurcating pattern, thus reducing the original infinite dimensional dynamics to a simpler finite-dimensional effective dynamics. In the presence of a multiplicative stable Lévy noise that preserves the constant trivial solution we study the impact of noise on the approximation. In contrast to Gaussian noise, where non-dominant pattern are uniformly small in time due to averaging effects, large jumps in the Lévy noise might lead to large error terms, and thus new estimates are needed to take this into account.



1
Introduction

Models of modulation or amplitude equations
[
4
,
12
,
21
,
38
]
have proven to be rather universal and efficient in describing the dynamics associated with a qualitative change of stability (bifurcation). Such structures emerge in fields ranging from spatially and temporarily oscillating wave packets
[
23
]
to long waves in dispersive media
[
36
]
and spatio-temporal pattern in dissipative systems
[
31
]
. In particular, the Ginzburg-Landau equation plays a prominent role as the effective modulation equation for the description of pattern forming systems close to the first instability since the 1960s; see Newell and Whitehead
[
22
]
. Among these, arguably, the most prototypical one is the Allen-Cahn equation with bistable behavior
[
17
]
, which characterizes interface motion between two stable phases.

The mathematical justification of modulation equation
beyond pure formal calculations
has been started in the early 90th, see for example
[
20
,
15
,
18
,
34
,
33
]
.
All these results and many later treated the case of unbounded domains,
as in the bounded doamin case the theory of
center manifolds is available in order to reduce the dynamics,
which does not help in the stochastic case.

Earlier works in the stochastic case
studied almost all the case of Gaussian additive noise,
and starting from
[
13
]
many articles explored the use of amplitude
approximation in order to qualitatively examine the dynamics of stochastic systems near a change of stability.
The quantitative error estimates are usually done pathwise with high probability or in moments on the natural slow time-scales close to the bifurcation.
Nevertheless modulation or amplitude equations can also give the approximation in the long-time behavior, for example the approximation of the infinite-dimensional invariant measure for a Swift-Hohenberg equation
[
7
]
.
Also ideas presented in
[
3
,
6
]
can be used
in approximating random attractors or random invariant manifolds via amplitude equations.

The case of multiplicative Wiener noise is not that well studied, but also here amplitude equations provide insights into the impact of multiplicative noise in SPDEs close to bifurcation; see
[
5
]
for an example and
[
3
]
for general results on scalar one-dimensional noise.

It is worthwhile to note that here we consider our SPDEs on a bounded domain only,
thus leading to a finite dimensional space of dominant patterns that change their stability. In this setting, the amplitude equation
turns out to be a stochastic ordinary differential equation (SDE) describing the amplitude of these dominant modes.
For the case of SPDEs on an unbounded domain,
the effective equation is no longer an SDE and the amplitude of a dominant mode is slowly modulated in space,
thus the reduced model is still an infinite dimensional SPDE.
Nevertheless, we will not focus on this case here.
See
[
11
]
for the full approximation of Swift-Hohenberg perturbed by space-time white noise on the whole real line,
[
21
]
in the case of a simple one-dimensional noise, and
[
12
]
for large domain.

In this paper, we study the following class of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process of the following form,

du(t)=[\mathcal{A}u(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{L}u(t)+\mathcal{F}(u(t))]dt+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}G(u(t))dL^{\alpha}(t),

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a non-positive self-adjoint operator with finite-dimensional kernel,
\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{L}
represents a small deterministic perturbation with a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
measuring the distance to bifurcation (the change of stability). The nonlinearity
\mathcal{F}
stands for a cubic mapping where one standard example is the cubic nonlinearity
-u^{3}
,
and
G(u)
denotes a Hilbert-Schmidt operator with
G(0)=0
so that the constant
u=0
is a solution to equation (
1.1
).
The prototypical case is the multiplication with
u
combined with a fixed Hilbert-Schmidt operator independent of
u
that regularizes the noise.
The noise
L^{\alpha}(t)
is a cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process on some stochastic basis with the index
\alpha\in(1,2)
of stability.
We will give more details on the setting in our assumptions below.

Our aim in the present work is to explore the asymptotic dynamics
in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
of solutions
u(t)
to equation (
1.1
) on the natural slow time-scale of order
\varepsilon^{-2}
.
Utilizing a separation of time-scales, near a change of stability for the linearized operator
\mathcal{A}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{L}
, the system (
1.1
) can be transformed to the slow dynamics where the dominant
pattern is still coupled to the dynamics on a fast time scale.
A reduced equation eliminating the
fast variable and characterizing the behaviour of dominant modes significantly simplifies the
dynamics to an SDE, which we classify as amplitude equation identifying the essential dynamics of
dominant pattern.

The scaling of the
\alpha
-stable noise is chosen in such a way
that it has an impact on the slow time-scale. If we take a larger exponent in the noise strength
\varepsilon^{2/\alpha}
, then we expect the noise to have no impact on the approximation, while for smaller exponents the noise should dominate it and we loose the impact of
\mathcal{L}
.
Another equivalent point of view is, that with a fixed noise strength we have to choose
exactly the right distance from bifurcation in order to see an impact of the small noise on the bifurcation.

The main advantage of
\alpha
-stable noise is that as in the Gaussian case it is a self-similar process that scales in time, so we can rescale equations to the slow time-scale easily.
The disadvantage are the large jumps. These lead to large error terms and we are not able to use uniform error bounds in time as in the Gaussian case.

Previous approximation results via amplitude equations considered mainly square integrable processes or even Gaussians having all moments.
This rules out the interesting cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process that only has finite
p
th moment for
p\in(0,\alpha)
.
Many tools developed so far are not suitable to treat cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy noises with the loss of the second moment, such as Kunita’s inequality, Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and Da
Prato-Kwapień-Zabczyk’s factorization technique
[
24
,
35
]
.
Therefore, we require new and different techniques to explore the cylindrical
\alpha
-stable noise more carefully. A challenging problem in this paper is how to handle the nonlinear terms, where the techniques of stopping times is used frequently
in order to cut-off the nonlinear terms that get too large.
But in connection with large jumps induced by
the cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise this causes many technical problems
which we had to overcome.

Our presentation is structured as follows. In Section 2, we briefly present the theoretical assumptions and analysis tools of the estimates for main results. While Section 3 provides our main results for considering the amplitude equation of equation (
1.1
). In Section 4, we analyze examples to illustrate applications of our main results.
Finally, Section 5 summarizes our findings and showcase our conclusions, as well as a number of directions for future study.

Assumptions and analysis tools

Throughout the paper, we shall work in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, endowed with the usual
scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and with the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, by using the domain of definition for fractional powers of the operator
\mathcal{A}
,

\mathcal{H}^{\theta}:=D((1-\mathcal{A})^{\theta}):=\Big\{h=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}e_{k}:h_{k}=\langle h,e_{k}\rangle\in\mathbb{R},\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+1)^{2\theta}h_{k}^{2}<\infty\Big\},

where

is an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions
such that
-\mathcal{A}e_{k}=\lambda_{k}e_{k}
and

(1-\mathcal{A})^{\theta}h:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+1)^{\theta}h_{k}e_{k},~~~h\in D((1-\mathcal{A})^{\theta}),

with the associated norm

\|h\|_{\theta}=\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}e_{k}\|_{\theta}:=\|(1-\mathcal{A})^{\theta}h\|:=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+1)^{2\theta}h_{k}^{2}},

where
:=
will be used hereafter to denote definitions. It is straightforward to infer that
\mathcal{H}^{0}=\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}=D((1-\mathcal{A}))
and
\mathcal{H}^{-\theta}
is the dual space of
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}
.

The cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
L^{\alpha}(t)
is defined via

L^{\alpha}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)e_{k},~~~~~~t\geq 0,

where
\{L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are independent one dimensional
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes on stochastic base
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. They are purely jump Lévy processes and have the same characteristic function by Lévy-Khinchine formula
[
10
]
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[e^{i\xi L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)}]=e^{t\psi(\xi)},t\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
,
where
\psi(\xi)
is the Lévy symbol given by

\psi(\xi)=-|\xi|^{\alpha}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\{0\}}}(e^{i\xi y}-1-i\xi y\mathds{1}_{\{|y|<1\}})\nu_{\alpha}(dy).

Here
\nu_{\alpha}
is the Lévy measure satisfying
\int_{{R}\setminus{\{0\}}}1\wedge|y|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)<\infty
, which is determined by

\nu_{\alpha}(dy)=c(1,{\alpha})\frac{1}{|y|^{1+\alpha}}dy,

where
c(1,\alpha)=\frac{\alpha\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}{2^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma{(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}}
and
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. For
t>0
and Borel set
B\in\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\{0\}})
, define the Poisson random measure of
L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)
by

N_{k}(t,B)=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\mathds{1}_{B}(L_{k}^{\alpha}(s)-L_{k}^{\alpha}(s-))=\#\{s\in(0,t]:L_{k}^{\alpha}(s)-L_{k}^{\alpha}(s-)\in B\},

where
L_{k}^{\alpha}(s-)
is the left limit of
L_{k}^{\alpha}(s)
. The function
\nu_{\alpha}(B)=\mathbb{E}(N(1,B))
of the Lévy measure is to describe the expected number of jumps in a certain size at a time interval
(0,1]
.
Furthermore, define the compensated Poisson measure of
L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)
via

\tilde{N}_{k}(t,B)=N_{k}(t,B)-t\nu_{\alpha}(B).

According to the Lévy-Itô decomposition
[
32
]
,
L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)
are able to be expressed as

L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)=\int_{|y|<1}y\tilde{N}_{k}(t,dy)+\int_{|y|\geq 1}yN_{k}(t,dy).

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(s)dL^{\alpha}(s)\|_{\theta}\Big)^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\Psi(s)\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}.

Remark 2.3
.

It should be highlighted here that we can take
\Psi(s)=G(u(s))
to introduce one of our main tools

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\int_{0}^{t}G(u(s))dL^{\alpha}(s)\|_{\theta}\Big)^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|G(u(s))\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}ds\Big)^{\frac{p}{\alpha}},

(2.14)

which is a crucial moment inequality in investigating system (
1.1
). Note that it does not apply to the stochastic convolution in the Definition (
2.1
),
as the integrand there depends on time.

3
Framework and main result

Focusing on investigating the local mild solution
u
such that it is small
of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, we introduce the slow time scaling
T=\varepsilon^{2}t
. Let us split it into

u(t)=\varepsilon a(\varepsilon^{2}t)+\varepsilon b(\varepsilon^{2}t),

with
a\in\mathcal{N}
and
b\in\mathcal{S}
. By projecting and rescaling to the slow time scale, we obtain

da(T)=[\mathcal{L}_{c}a(T)+\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(T)+b(T))]dT+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(T)+\varepsilon b(T))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T)

(3.15)

and

db(T)=[\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{s}b(T)+\mathcal{L}_{s}b(T)+\mathcal{F}_{s}(a(T)+b(T))]dT+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(T)+\varepsilon b(T))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T),

(3.16)

where
\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T):=\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}L^{\alpha}(\varepsilon^{-2}T)
is a rescaled version of the
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. It is based on the fact that
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
L^{\alpha}(t)
is self-similar with Hurst index
1/\alpha
, i.e.,

L^{\alpha}(ct)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}c^{1/\alpha}L^{\alpha}(t),~~~~c>0,

where
``\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}"
denotes equivalence (coincidence) in distribution.

Using the mild formulation, we rewrite the equations (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) into the integral form:

a(T)=a(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{c}a(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau)+b(\tau))d\tau+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle b(T)

\displaystyle=e^{\varepsilon^{-2}T\mathcal{A}_{s}}b(0)+\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{L}_{s}b(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{F}_{s}(a(\tau)+b(\tau))d\tau

(3.18)

\displaystyle~~~~+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau).

Denote the corresponding four terms arising in the right-hand side of system (
3.18
) by
Q(T)
,
I(T)
,
J(T)
and
K(T)
, respectively. That is

b(T)=Q(T)+I(T)+J(T)+K(T).

(3.19)

We shall see later that
b
is bounded and it’s integral is small as long as
a
is of order one (see Remark
3.1
, Lemma
3.1
-
3.2
and Lemma
3.11
for the precise statements). Only
Q(T)
with the initial condition and the term
K(T)
are not
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, but
Q(T)
is only of order one for very small times and the integral of
K(T)
is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
. Thus by neglecting all
b
-dependent terms in (
3.15
) or (
3.17
) and
expanding the
G_{c}
term we obtain the amplitude equation

d\varphi(T)=\mathcal{L}_{c}\varphi(T)dT+\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(T))dT+[G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(T)]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T),~~~~\varphi(0)=a(0).

(3.20)

Integrating (
3.20
) we obtain

\varphi(T)=a(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{c}\varphi(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}[G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau).

(3.21)

Note that the noise in the SDE is still infinite dimensional,
but
G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi=\mathcal{P}_{c}[G^{\prime}(0)\cdot\varphi]
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator that maps
\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)
into the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{N}
.

Define the overall error between
u
and
\varepsilon\varphi
by
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon^{2}t):=u(t)-\varepsilon\varphi(\varepsilon^{2}t)
or

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)

\displaystyle:=u(\varepsilon^{-2}T)-\varepsilon\varphi(T)

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon[a(T)-\varphi(T)+b(T)]

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon[a(T)-\varphi(T)+Q(T)+I(T)+J(T)+K(T)].

(3.22)

With our main assumptions we have the following main result on the approximation by
amplitude equation, which is proved later at the end of this section .

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
hold. Let
u
be the mild solution of (
1.1
)
with initial condition

u(0)=\varepsilon a(0)+\varepsilon b(0),

where
a(0)\in\mathcal{N}
and
b(0)\in\mathcal{S}
. The solution
\varphi
of the amplitude equation (
3.20
) satisfies the initial condition
\varphi(0)=a(0)
. Then for any
p\in(0,\alpha)
,
T_{0}>0
and all small
\kappa\in(0,\frac{2}{19})
,
provided

\|u(0)\|_{\theta}\leq\varepsilon^{1-\kappa/2}

we
obtain for the error
\mathcal{R}
defined in (
3.22
)
that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\mathcal{R}_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}\geq\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa}\Big)\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\mathcal{R}_{s}\|_{L^{p}([0,T_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}\geq\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa}\Big)\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

Let us first remark that in the previous result for the time
\tau_{ex}
of existence
of our local mild solution we also have
\tau_{ex}>\varepsilon^{-2}T_{0}
with high probability.

Now we need to introduce a stopping time in connection with process
(a,b)
. This stopping
time is equivalent to a cut-off in (
1.1
) at order slightly bigger than
\varepsilon
. Also this stopping time is the reason, why we only need local solutions for the SPDE that might not exist for all times.

Definition 3.1
.

For the
\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{S}
-valued stochastic process
(a,b)
satisfying the integral equations (
3.17
) and (
3.18
)
, some time
T_{0}>0
and small exponent
\kappa\in(0,\frac{2}{19})
, we define the stopping time
\tau^{*}
as

\tau^{*}:=T_{0}\wedge\inf\{T>0:~\|a(T)\|_{\theta}>\varepsilon^{-\kappa}~~~or~~~\|b(T)\|_{\theta}>\varepsilon^{-2\kappa}\}.

(3.23)

Remark 3.1
.

In the decomposition of
b(T)
, for
Q(T)=e^{\varepsilon^{-2}T\mathcal{A}_{s}}b(0)
the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|Q(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}Ce^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho pT}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}\|Q(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau\leq\mathbb{E}C\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho p\tau}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}.

Before proving the main result Theorem
3.1
, we need to state some technical lemmas used later in the proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the assumptions
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
hold. For
p\in(0,\alpha)
and
\tau^{*}
from Definition
3.1
,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|I(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-2\kappa p}

(3.24)

and

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|J(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-6\kappa p}.

(3.25)

Proof.

By virtue of the estimate (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|I(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{L}_{s}b(\tau)d\tau\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{L}_{s}b(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\|\mathcal{L}_{s}b(\tau)\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\varepsilon^{-2\kappa}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-2\kappa p}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}\rho T}e^{-r}r^{-\sigma}dr]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-2\kappa p}.

Now, let’s prove inequality (
3.25
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|J(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{F}_{s}(a(\tau)+b(\tau))d\tau\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathcal{F}_{s}(a(\tau)+b(\tau))\|_{\theta}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\|\mathcal{F}_{s}(a(\tau)+b(\tau))\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\|a(\tau)+b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{3}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2\sigma p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\rho(T-\tau)}(T-\tau)^{-\sigma}\varepsilon^{-6\kappa}d\tau]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2p-6\kappa p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}[\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{-2}\rho T}e^{-r}r^{-\sigma}dr]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2p-6\kappa p}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

For
I
and
J
, we have uniform bounds in time that show the smallness of both terms. The bounds of the error term in Lemma
3.3
will thus be determined via the estimate on
K
.
But here we encounter serious problems. By large jumps due to the noise,
we are no longer able to show that
K
is small uniformly in time.
We can only verify bounds for
K
in
L^{p}([0,\tau^{*}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume the setting of Lemma
3.1
. Then it holds for every
p\in(0,\alpha)
that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}\|K(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau\leq C\varepsilon^{(\frac{2}{\alpha}-2\kappa)p+2}.

(3.26)

Proof.

To show the estimate (
3.26
), we use the Riesz-Nagy-trick
[
26
]
here, which embeds a contraction semigroup into a larger Hilbert-space,
where it is a group defined for all times.
Let
\lambda_{0}
be a positive constant less than
\lambda_{n+1}
but close to it. For any
p\in(0,\alpha)
, by using the maximal inequality for stochastic convolutions
[
8
]
based on the Riesz-Nagy theorem (as
\mathcal{A}_{s}+\lambda_{0}\mathcal{I}
generates a contraction semigroup on
\mathcal{S}
), the condition (
2.7
) for
G
, and the definition of the stopping time
\tau^{*}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}\|K(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}}\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(\tau-r)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(r)+\varepsilon b(r))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(\tau-r)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(r)+\varepsilon b(r))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge\tau^{*}}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(\tau-r)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(r)+\varepsilon b(r))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(\tau-r)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(r)+\varepsilon b(r))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{p}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}p\tau}\mathbb{E}\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(\tau-r)(\mathcal{A}_{s}+\lambda_{0}\mathcal{I})}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}r}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(r)+\varepsilon b(r))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}p\tau}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\alpha\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}r}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}\|a(r)+b(r)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}dr\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}p\tau}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau}\varepsilon^{-2\alpha\kappa}e^{\alpha\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}r}dr\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{2p}{\alpha}-2\kappa p}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}e^{-\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}p\tau}\Big[e^{\alpha\varepsilon^{-2}\lambda_{0}\tau}-1\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{(\frac{2}{\alpha}-2\kappa)p+2}.

This confirms that the estimate (
3.26
) holds.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

The expression (
3.19
) for
b
, Hölder’s inequality, triangle inequality, equivalence of norms and Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
provide

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)(\|Q(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}+\|I(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}+\|J(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}+\|K(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha})d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\Big[\varepsilon^{2p}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}+\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau^{*}}\|I(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}+\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau^{*}}\|J(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}+\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau^{*}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|K(r)\|_{\theta}^{p}dr\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,p}\varepsilon^{2p-6\kappa p}.

Note that this
L^{p}
-bound on
b
is not sufficient to obtain the estimate and remove the stopping time.
Here we will need uniform bounds both on
a
and
b
. This will be done in the following results.

Let us rewrite the equation (
3.17
) for
a
as the amplitude equation plus an error term (or residual):

a(T)=a(0)+\int_{0}^{T}[\mathcal{L}_{c}a(\tau)+\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau))]d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)+R(T),

(3.27)

where the error term is given by

\displaystyle R(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}[3\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),b(\tau))+3\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),b(\tau),b(\tau))+\mathcal{F}_{c}(b(\tau))]d\tau

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,~~~+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\Big]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau).

(3.28)

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
p\in(0,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),b(\tau))d\tau\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}p-8\kappa p}+\varepsilon^{2p-2\kappa p}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p})

Proof.

Using
b=Q+I+J+K
from (
3.19
), by brute force expansion of the cubic,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),b(\tau))d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),Q(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),I(\tau))d\tau

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),J(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),K(\tau))d\tau

\displaystyle:=R_{1,1}(T)+R_{1,2}(T)+R_{1,3}(T)+R_{1,4}(T).

(3.29)

Now we estimate each term separately. Since all
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}
-norms are equivalent on
\mathcal{N}
, by Definition
3.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,1}(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,1}(T)\|_{\theta-\sigma}^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),Q(\tau))\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2}\|Q(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau\Big]^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2\kappa p}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|e^{\varepsilon^{-2}\tau\mathcal{A}_{s}}b(0)\|_{\theta}d\tau\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-2\kappa p}\|b(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}.

For
R_{1,2}(T)
, by the estimate (
3.24
), we argue that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,2}(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,2}(T)\|_{\theta-\sigma}^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),I(\tau))\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2}\|I(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau\Big]^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2\kappa p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\int_{0}^{T}\|I(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-4\kappa p}.

Due to the estimate (
3.25
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,3}(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,3}(T)\|_{\theta-\sigma}^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),J(\tau))\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2}\|J(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau\Big]^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2\kappa p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\int_{0}^{T}\|J(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2p-8\kappa p}.

Based on the estimate (
3.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,4}(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R_{1,4}(T)\|_{\theta-\sigma}^{p}\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{F}_{c}(a(\tau),a(\tau),K(\tau))\|_{\theta-\sigma}d\tau\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2}\|K(\tau)\|_{\theta}d\tau\Big]^{p}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2\kappa p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\int_{0}^{T}\|K(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{(\frac{2}{\alpha}-4\kappa)p+2}.

Thus we finished the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\Big]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

(3.30)

Utilizing the Taylor formula and
G(0)=0
, we can check

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[G(0)+G^{\prime}(0)(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))+\frac{1}{2}G^{\prime\prime}(z(\tau))\cdot(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau),\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))\Big]-G^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}(0)\cdot b(\tau)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}G^{\prime\prime}(z(\tau))\cdot(a(\tau)+b(\tau),a(\tau)+b(\tau)),

where
z(\tau)
is a vector on the line segment connecting
0
and
\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau)
. With the conditions (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\|G^{\prime}(0)\cdot b(\tau)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}G^{\prime\prime}(z(\tau))\cdot(a(\tau)+b(\tau),a(\tau)+b(\tau))\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2\alpha}).

After substituting the above estimate back into (
3.30
), we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\Big]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)(\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{2\alpha})d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-4\kappa p}+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}},

where the last estimate is obtained via the definition of
\tau^{*}
.
Hence, using Corollary
3.1
leads to

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}G_{c}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))-G_{c}^{\prime}(0)\cdot a(\tau)\Big]d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-6\kappa p}.

∎

The following uniform bound depending on the initial condition

Lemma 3.8
.

Under assumptions
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
, for any
p\in(1,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\varphi(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\big(\|a(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}+1\big).

(3.32)

Proof.

Define a smooth function
f
on
\mathcal{H}
by

f(\cdot)=\big(\|\cdot\|^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(3.33)

As a result, for any
x,h\in\mathcal{H}
,

f^{\prime}(x)h=\frac{p}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle x,h\rangle

(3.34)

and

f^{\prime\prime}(x)(h,h)=\frac{p}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle h,h\rangle+\frac{p(p-2)}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{2-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle x,h\rangle\langle x,h\rangle\leq\frac{p(p-1)}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\|h\|^{2}.

(3.35)

Moreover,

\|f^{\prime}(x)\|\leq\frac{C_{p}\|x\|}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\leq C_{p}\|x\|^{p-1},\quad\|f^{\prime\prime}(x)\|\leq\frac{C_{p}}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\leq C_{p}.

It follows from Itô’s formula (
[
2
, Theorem 4.4.7]
) that

\displaystyle f(\varphi(T))

\displaystyle=f(a(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{c}\varphi(\tau),p\varphi(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}+\frac{\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau)),p\varphi(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big]d\tau

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|\geq 1}[f(\varphi(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau-))]N_{k}(d\tau,dy)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|<1}[f(\varphi(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau-))]\tilde{N}_{k}(d\tau,dy)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|<1}\Big[f(\varphi(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau-))-\frac{\langle G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k},p\varphi(\tau-)\rangle}{\big(\|\varphi(\tau-)\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big]\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau

\displaystyle:=\big(\|a(0)\|^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+I_{1}(T)+I_{2}(T)+I_{3}(T)+I_{4}(T).

(3.36)

Define a stopping time

\mathcal{T}:=T_{0}\wedge\inf\{t>0:\|\varphi(t)\|>n\}\leq T_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that we need to use a stopping time
\mathcal{T}
in order to have
\varphi
bounded.
A-priori we do not know that the moments of
\varphi
are finite, thus we consider the process only up to the stopping time
\mathcal{T}

which ensures this.

Taking account of (
3.34
), assumption
(\textbf{A2})
and the bound on
\mathcal{F}
from (
2.4
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\mathcal{T}}\|I_{1}(T)\|\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau.

(3.37)

Using (
3.34
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\mathcal{T}}\|I_{2}(T)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|f(\varphi(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau))|N_{k}(d\tau,dy)\Big)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|f(\varphi(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau))|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{\prime}(\varphi(\tau)+\xi G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})\|d\xi\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p-1}+\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|^{p-1})\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|y|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{p}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|y|^{p}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\Big)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau.

(3.38)

Since the compensated compound Poisson process
\tilde{N}
is a martingale, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
14
]
and (
3.34
), we figure out

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\mathcal{T}}\|I_{3}(T)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|<1}|f(\varphi(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau))|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{\prime}(\varphi(\tau)+\xi G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})\|^{2}d\xi\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|<1}(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2p-2}+\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|^{2p-2})\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\Big[\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{p}\Big[\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2p}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\sup_{0\leq s\leq\tau}\|\varphi(s)\|^{p}d\tau,

(3.39)

where we used Young’s inequality in the last inequality.
The Taylor’s expansion and (
3.34
)-(
3.35
) imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\mathcal{T}}\|I_{4}(T)\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\Big|f(\varphi(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k})-f(\varphi(\tau))-\frac{\langle G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k},p\varphi(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\frac{\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(\tau)ye_{k}\|^{2}}{\big(\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}+1\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau.

(3.40)

Combining estimates (
3.36
)-(
3.40
) yields

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(t)\|^{p}\leq C_{p}\big(\|a(0)\|^{p}+1\big)+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\varphi(s)\|^{p}dt.

By an application of Gronwall’s lemma, we thus derive

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\|\varphi(t)\|^{p}\leq C_{p}\big(\|a(0)\|^{p}+1\big)e^{C_{p}\mathcal{T}}\leq C_{p}\big(\|a(0)\|^{p}+1\big)e^{C_{p}T_{0}}.

As the equation above holds for any radius
n\in\mathcal{N}
in the definition of the stopping time
\mathcal{T}
we can pass to the monotone limit to obtain

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\varphi(T)\|^{p}\leq C_{p}\big(\|a(0)\|^{p}+1\big).

Finally, recall that the norm in
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}

and the norm in
\mathcal{H}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{N}
.
∎

The next step now is to remove the error from the equation for
a
to obtain the amplitude equation. We show an error estimate between

Lemma 3.9
.

Thanks to the use of
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
and
\|b(0)\|_{\theta}\leq\varepsilon^{-\kappa}
, for any
p\in(1,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)-\varphi(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-18\kappa p}.

Proof.

For the proof we derive an equation for the error
a-\varphi
and proceed similarly that for the bound on
\varphi
. But as
R

(defined in (
3.28
)) is not differentiable in the Itô-sense, we first substitute
g:=a-R
. Clearly, we have

g(T)=a(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{c}(g(\tau)+R(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(g(\tau)+R(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(g(\tau)+R(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau).

Defining the error

, we get

e(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{c}e(\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}_{c}R(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau))d\tau-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau)-e(\tau)+R(\tau))d\tau+\int_{0}^{T}G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau).

Let
f
be the smooth function on
\mathcal{H}
given by

f(\cdot)=\big(\|\cdot\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}},\quad\text{where}~~\delta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{2}.

For any
x,h\in\mathcal{H}
,

f^{\prime}(x)h=\frac{p}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle x,h\rangle

and

f^{\prime\prime}(x)(h,h)=\frac{p}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle h,h\rangle+\frac{p(p-2)}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{2-\frac{p}{2}}}\langle x,h\rangle\langle x,h\rangle\leq\frac{p(p-1)}{\big(\|x\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\|h\|^{2}.

Applying Itô’s formula to compute

\displaystyle f(e(T))

\displaystyle=\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p}{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{c}e(\tau),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{c}R(\tau),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle~~~+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau))-\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau)-e(\tau)+R(\tau)),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|\geq 1}[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))]N_{k}(d\tau,dy)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|<1}[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))]\tilde{N}_{k}(d\tau,dy)

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|y|<1}\Big[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))-\frac{\langle G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k},pe(\tau-)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau-)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big]\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau.

We derive

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{c}e(\tau),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|e(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau,

(3.41)

and

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{c}R(\tau),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|R(\tau)\|\|e(\tau)\|}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|e(\tau)\|^{p-1}\|R(\tau)\|d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|e(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau+C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|R(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau.

(3.42)

By condition (
2.6
), Young’s inequality makes sure that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau))-\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(\tau)-e(\tau)+R(\tau)),pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|R(\tau)\|^{4}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau+C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}\|R(\tau)\|^{2}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|R(\tau)\|^{4}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d\tau+C_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}\|R(\tau)\|^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d\tau.

(3.43)

The stochastic term is bounded as follows. We examine

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|y|\geq 1}[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))]N_{k}(d\tau,dy)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|f(e(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau))|N_{k}(d\tau,dy)\Big)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|f(e(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau))|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{\prime}(e(\tau)+\xi G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))(\tau)ye_{k})\|d\xi\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|\geq 1}(\|e(\tau)\|^{p-1}+\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|^{p-1})\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|y|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)\|^{p-1}\|e(\tau)-R(\tau)\|d\tau+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{p}\int_{|y|\geq 1}|y|^{p}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)-R(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}[\|e(\tau)\|^{p}+\|e(\tau)\|^{p-1}\|R(\tau)\|]d\tau+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}[\|e(\tau)\|^{p}+\|R(\tau)\|^{p}]d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|R(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau,

(3.44)

where we used Young’s inequality in the last inequality.
By Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|y|<1}[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))]\tilde{N}_{k}(d\tau,dy)\Big|

(3.45)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|<1}|f(e(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau))|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{\prime}(e(\tau)+\xi G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})\|^{2}d\xi\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|<1}(\|e(\tau)\|^{2p-2}+\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|^{2p-2})\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\Big[\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)\|^{2p-2}\|e(\tau)-R(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{p}\Big[\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2p}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)-R(\tau)\|^{2p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}[\|e(\tau)\|^{2p}+\|e(\tau)\|^{2p-2}\|R(\tau)\|^{2}]d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}[\|e(\tau)\|^{2p}+\|R(\tau)\|^{2p}]d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)\|^{2p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|R(\tau)\|^{2p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(T)\|^{p}+C_{p}\int_{0}^{{T}}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq r\leq\tau^{*}\wedge\tau}\|e(r)\|^{p}d\tau+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|R(\tau)\|^{2p}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The Taylor’s expansion has been successfully used to monitor

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|y|<1}\Big[f(e(\tau-)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau-))-\frac{\langle G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k},pe(\tau-)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big]\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\Big|f(e(\tau)+G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k})-f(e(\tau))-\frac{\langle G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k},pe(\tau)\rangle}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\Big|\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\int_{|y|<1}\frac{\|G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot(e(\tau)-R(\tau))ye_{k}\|^{2}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{2}\nu_{\alpha}(dy)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\frac{\|e(\tau)-R(\tau)\|^{2}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\frac{\|e(\tau)\|^{2}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\frac{\|R(\tau)\|^{2}}{\big(\|e(\tau)\|^{2}+\delta_{\varepsilon}\big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(\tau)\|^{p}d\tau+C_{p}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|R(\tau)\|^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p}{2}-1}d\tau.

(3.46)

Therefore, it should be evident from collecting together (
3.31
), (
3.32
) and (
3.41
)-(
3.46
) that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau^{*}\wedge{T}}\|e(t)\|^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-18\kappa p}+C_{p}\int_{0}^{{T}}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq r\leq\tau^{*}\wedge\tau}\|e(r)\|^{p}d\tau.

The use of Gronwall’s lemma allows us to identify

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|e(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-18\kappa p},

where we used that the norm in
\mathcal{H}^{\theta}

and the norm in
\mathcal{H}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{N}
.
Moreover, because of (
3.31
),

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)-\varphi(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|e(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|R(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{p-18\kappa p}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

It is worthwhile to note that due to Lemma
3.9
and (
3.32
), for any
p\in(0,\alpha)
and
\kappa\in(0,\frac{1}{18})

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)-\varphi(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\varphi(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}(\|a(0)\|_{\theta}^{p}+1).

(3.47)

A remarkable find of Lemma
3.9
is the following bound on
\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)=\varepsilon[a(T)-\varphi(T)]
:

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
p\in(1,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{2p-18\kappa p}.

(3.48)

As Lemma
3.1
indicates that
I
and
J
are uniformly small, but up to now we only verified an
L^{p}
-bound on
K
. But in order to show that the stopping time
\tau^{*}
is large, it is important to bound

K(T)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}G_{s}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)

uniformly in time.
The moment inequality (
2.14
) is unfortunately not available here, as the integrand in

depends on

. We will use again the Riesz-Nagy-trick.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume the setting of Lemma
3.9
. For any
p\in(0,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|K(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{-\kappa p}.

(3.49)

Proof.

There exists a Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
and a unitary strongly continuous group
\{U(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mathcal{H}
embeds isometrically into
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
and the contraction semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{A}}
on
\mathcal{H}
is a projection of
U(t)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}U(t)=e^{t\mathcal{A}}
on
\mathcal{H}
for all
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{P}
being the orthogonal projection from
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
onto
\mathcal{H}
. The moment inequality (
2.14
) and Riesz-Nagy theorem recognize that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|K(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon^{-2}(T-\tau)\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)G_{s}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{P}U_{\varepsilon}(T)U_{\varepsilon}(-\tau)\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)G_{s}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))d\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|U_{\varepsilon}(-\tau)\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)G_{s}(\varepsilon a(\tau)+\varepsilon b(\tau))\|_{L_{HS}}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|a(\tau)+b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|a(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}+C_{\alpha,p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\tau^{*}]}(\tau)\|b(\tau)\|_{\theta}^{\alpha}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}.

Notice that when
\tau\leq\tau^{*}
, the first term on the right-hand side is bounded by
C\varepsilon^{-\kappa p}
uniformly in time. Consequently, we observe from Corollary
3.1
that

\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|K(T)\|_{\theta}^{p}\leq C_{p}\varepsilon^{-\kappa p}.

∎

Furthermore, by the definition
b(T)=Q(T)+I(T)+J(T)+K(T)
, Remark
3.1
, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma

enable us to gain a detailed understanding of the bound on

.

Lemma 3.11
.

With assumptions
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
and
\|b(0)\|_{\theta}\leq\varepsilon^{-\kappa}
, for any

, there exists a constant

such that

(3.50)

Before continuing, we construct a subset of
\Omega
, which enjoys nearly full probability.

Definition 3.2
.

For
\kappa\in(0,\frac{1}{18})
, from the definition of
\tau^{*}
as in (
3.23
), define the set
\Omega^{*}\subset\Omega
of all
\omega\in\Omega
such that all these estimates

\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{-\kappa},~~~\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|b(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{-2\kappa},~~~\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa}~~~\text{and}~~~\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,\tau^{*}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}<\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa}

hold.

Lemma 3.12
.

The set
\Omega^{*}
in Definition
3.2
has approximately probability
1
.

Proof.

It is natural to consider

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{*})\geq

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)\|_{\theta}\geq\varepsilon^{-\kappa})-\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|b(T)\|_{\theta}\geq\varepsilon^{-2\kappa})

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}\geq\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa})-\mathbb{P}(\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,\tau^{*}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}>\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa}).

Admittedly, Chebychev’s inequality, Corollary
3.1
and (
3.47
)-(
3.50
) illustrate

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{*})\geq

\displaystyle 1-(\varepsilon^{-\kappa})^{-q}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)\|_{\theta}^{q}-(\varepsilon^{-2\kappa})^{-q}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|b(T)\|_{\theta}^{q}

\displaystyle-(\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa})^{-q}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}^{q}-(\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa})^{-q}\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,\tau^{*}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-C\varepsilon^{\kappa q}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}\kappa q}-C\varepsilon^{2\kappa q}\varepsilon^{-\kappa q}-C\varepsilon^{(19\kappa-2)q}\varepsilon^{(2-18\kappa)q}-C\varepsilon^{(7\kappa-3)q}\varepsilon^{(3-6\kappa)q}

\displaystyle\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}1.

∎

Lastly, at the level of the present considerations it is relevant to point out that
\|a\|<\varepsilon^{-\kappa}
on
[0,T_{0}]
with probability almost
1
. Let us finally prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As the definition of
\Omega^{*}
and
\tau^{*}
suggest

\Omega^{*}\subseteq\Big\{\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|a(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{-\kappa},~~\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|b(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{-2\kappa}\Big\}\subseteq\{\tau^{*}=T_{0}\}\subseteq\Omega.

This permits us to obtain on
\Omega^{*}
that

\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}=\sup_{0\leq T\leq\tau^{*}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}<\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa}~~~\text{and}~~~\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,T_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}=\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,\tau^{*}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}<\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa},

such that

\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{0}}\|\mathcal{R}_{c}(T)\|_{\theta}\geq\varepsilon^{2-19\kappa}\big)\leq 1-\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{*})\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0~~~\text{and}~~~\mathbb{P}\big(\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(T)\|_{L^{p}([0,T_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\theta})}\geq\varepsilon^{3-7\kappa}\big)\leq 1-\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{*})\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0,

respectively. Recalling representation (
3.22
) of
\mathcal{R}
, the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The uniqueness in the previous theorem should be understood by choosing a version of the solution, i.e., by changing it on null sets.

4
Examples and applications

In this section, we provide two examples to corroborate our analytical results.
The first example is the Ginzburg-Landau equation,
which is an effective amplitude equation for the description of pattern forming systems close to the first instability.
Consider the following stochastic Allen-Cahn equation (real Ginzburg-Landau equation), subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions,
with linear multiplicative noise on the domain
D=[0,\pi]
of the type

\partial_{t}u(t)=(\partial_{x}^{2}+1)u(t)+\gamma\varepsilon^{2}u(t)-u^{3}(t)+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}u(t)\partial_{t}Q^{1/2}L^{\alpha}(t),

(4.51)

where
\mathcal{A}:=\partial_{x}^{2}+1
,
\mathcal{L}:=\gamma\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{F}(u):=-u^{3}
, and
G(u)=uQ^{1/2}
, the multiplication operator combined with the covariance operator
Q
defined below.

We scale the linear term to be close to bifurcation by choosing
\gamma\varepsilon^{2}
and choose a noise strength of order
\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
with the index of stability
\alpha\in(1,2)
. In this case both the noise and the linear (in)stability will survive in the amplitude equation.

The noise
L^{\alpha}(t)
is a cylidrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process on some stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{P})
defined via

L^{\alpha}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}L_{k}^{\alpha}(t)e_{k},~~~~~~t\geq 0.

and the covariance operator
Q
is defined by
Qe_{k}=\beta^{2}_{k}e_{k}
where
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is one given sequence of positive numbers satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}k^{2}<\infty.

Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}([0,\pi])
be the Hilbert space of all square integrable real-valued functions defined on the interval
[0,\pi]
we are going to work in.
Denote
H^{1}_{0}([0,\pi])
as the Sobolev space of functions with square integrable derivatives that satisfy Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The existence and uniqueness of global mild solutions (i.e.,
\tau_{ex}=\infty
) for equation (
4.51
) based on a Galerkin approximation is standard, so we won’t go into detail here.

The eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{A}=-\partial_{x}^{2}-1
are calculated accurately to be
\lambda_{k}=k^{2}-1
,
k=1,2,...
, and then
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
for
k\to\infty
. The associated eigenvectors are
e_{k}(x)=({2}{\pi}^{-1})^{1/2}\sin(kx)

and the dominant space is
\mathcal{N}=\text{span}\{e_{1}\}
.
Hence assumptions
(\textbf{A1})
is valid.

Arguably, assumption
(\textbf{A2})
is true for example for any
\theta>\frac{1}{2}

and
\sigma=0
. As for the norm in
\mathcal{H}^{\theta/2}=H_{0}^{\theta}
, we then have

\|uv\|_{H_{0}^{\theta}}\leq C\|u\|_{\theta}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{\theta}}
.
For simplicity, we will fix
\theta=1
.

It should be noted, that on the one-dimensional space
\mathcal{N}

the
H_{0}^{\theta}
-norm is just a multiple of the
\mathcal{H}
-norm.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a standard cubic nonlinearity, for
u,w\in\mathcal{N}
,

\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(u),u\rangle=-\int_{0}^{\pi}u^{4}(x)dx\leq 0,\,\,\,\,\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(u,u,w),w\rangle=-\int_{0}^{\pi}u^{2}(x)\omega^{2}(x)dx\leq 0,

and condition (
2.6
) holds for some positive constants
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, and thus assumption
(\textbf{A3})
is true.

The Hilbert-Schmidt operator

G:\mathcal{H}^{1}\rightarrow L_{HS}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{1})

satisfies
G(0)=0

and

\|G(u)\|_{L_{HS}}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|u\cdot Q^{1/2}e_{k}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}\|e_{k}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}<\infty.

In addition,
G^{\prime}(u)\cdot v=Q^{1/2}v
and
G^{\prime\prime}(u)=0
.
So assumptions
(\textbf{A4})
and
(\textbf{A5})
follow immediately.

Under our main assumptions, the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation (
4.51
) is well approximated by the amplitude equation

\partial_{T}\varphi(T)=\mathcal{L}_{c}\varphi(T)+\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(T))+[G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(T)]\partial_{T}\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T)\\
=\mathcal{P}_{c}[\gamma\varphi(T)-\varphi^{3}(T)]+\mathcal{P}_{c}\varphi(T)Q^{1/2}\partial_{T}\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T),

where
\varphi\in\mathcal{N}

is determined by the rescaled solution
u(t,x)\approx\varepsilon\varphi(\varepsilon^{2}t,x)
of (
4.51
).

To be more specific, we calculate the amplitude equation for the actual amplitude of

\varphi=\phi\sin(\cdot)
.
We have

\mathcal{P}_{c}\varphi=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\phi\sin^{2}(y)dy\sin(\cdot)=\phi\sin(\cdot)

and

\mathcal{P}_{c}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=-\phi^{3}\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin(y)\sin^{3}(y)dy\sin(\cdot)=-\frac{3}{4}\phi^{3}\sin(\cdot)
.
Moreover,

\mathcal{P}_{c}\varphi(T)Q^{1/2}\partial_{T}\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T)=\phi(T)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\mathcal{P}_{c}[\sin(\cdot)e_{k}]\partial_{T}\tilde{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(T)=\phi(T)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}\beta_{k}\partial_{T}\tilde{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(T)\sin(\cdot),

where

\mathcal{P}_{c}[\sin(x)e_{k}(x)]=\mathcal{P}_{c}[\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sin(x)\sin(kx)]=(\frac{2}{\pi})^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}(y)\sin(ky)dy\sin(\cdot)=\delta_{k}\sin(\cdot)

with

\displaystyle\delta_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\frac{2}{\pi})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{2(\cos(k\pi)-1)}{k(k^{2}-4)},\,\,\,k\neq 2,\\
0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,k=2.\end{array}\right.

Hence the amplitude equation for (
4.51
) is

\partial_{T}\phi(T)=[\gamma\phi(T)-\frac{3}{4}\phi^{3}(T)]+\phi(T)\partial_{T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\delta_{k}\tilde{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(T),

(4.53)

where the deterministic part describes a forward-pitchfork bifurcation. The deterministic counterpart
\dot{\phi}=\gamma\phi-\frac{3}{4}\phi^{3}
has either one or three fixed points depending on the value of the parameter
\gamma
. When
\gamma\leq 0
, there is one stable fixed point at
\phi=0
.
When
\gamma>0
, there are three fixed points at
\phi=0
,
2\sqrt{\gamma/3}
and
-2\sqrt{\gamma/3}
.

It is remarkable that in the driving Lévy process all infinitely many one-dimensional
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes
L_{k}^{\alpha}
contribute to the noise in the amplitude equation.
By means of Monte Carlo Simulation, several trajectories of stochastic system (
4.53
) approach the unique stable equilibrium state
\phi=0
with the negative bifurcation parameter
\gamma=-0.05
and the noise intensity
\delta=0.1
. The decrease of the index of stability from

. When the index of stability varies from

The second example, which we will discuss very briefly,
is the following surface growth model

\partial_{t}u(t)=-\Delta^{2}u(t)-\mu\Delta u(t)+\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\nabla u(t))+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}u(t)\partial_{t}Q^{1/2}L^{\alpha}(t),

(4.54)

subject to periodic boundary conditions on the interval
[0,2\pi]
. In order to get close to the change of stability, we consider
\mu=1+\varepsilon^{2}\gamma
. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}=-\Delta^{2}-\Delta,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{L}=-\gamma\Delta\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\mathcal{F}(u)=\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla u).

One can check that assumptions
(\textbf{A1})
-
(\textbf{A5})
are satisfied.
The eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{A}=\Delta^{2}+\Delta
are
\lambda_{k}=k^{4}-k^{2}
,
k=1,2,...
, and then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=\infty
.
Consider for
k\in\mathbb{N}
the eigenfunctions

e_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sin(kx)
and
e_{-k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cos(kx)
,
and
e_{0}(x)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}
.
We obtain
\mathcal{N}=\text{span}\{e_{1},\ e_{-1}\}

and we will work in the space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}([0,2\pi])
. And the space
\mathcal{H}^{\theta/4}
is the standard Sobolev space
H^{\theta}_{per}
.

Furthermore, if
u=\gamma_{1}\sin+\gamma_{2}\cos\in\mathcal{N}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{c}(u)=-\frac{3}{4}|\gamma|^{2}(\gamma_{1}\sin,\gamma_{2}\cos)

and
\langle\mathcal{F}_{c}(u),u\rangle=-\frac{3\pi}{4}|\gamma|^{4}\leq 0
.
Moreover, for
\theta=\sigma
,

\|\mathcal{F}(u)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}u)^{3}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{x}u)^{3}\|_{1}\leq C\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{1}}^{3}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{3}.

As before,
L^{\alpha}
is a cylindrical Lévy process and the covariance operator is

Q^{1/2}
with
Qe_{k}=\beta_{k}^{2}e_{k}
.
We suppose
\sum_{k}\beta_{k}<\infty
and
\sum_{k}\beta_{k}^{2}k^{4}<\infty
, so that the assumptions on
G
are satisfied.

Utilizing the approximation
u(t,x)\approx\varepsilon\varphi(\varepsilon^{2}t,x)

of (
4.54
), the amplitude equation takes the form

\partial_{T}\varphi(T)=\mathcal{L}_{c}\varphi(T)+\mathcal{F}_{c}(\varphi(T))+[G^{\prime}_{c}(0)\cdot\varphi(T)]\partial_{T}\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T)\\
=\mathcal{P}_{c}[-\mu\Delta\varphi(T)+\nabla\cdot(|\nabla\varphi(T)|^{2}\nabla\varphi(T))]+\mathcal{P}_{c}\varphi(T)\partial_{T}Q^{1/2}\tilde{L}^{\alpha}(T).

Suppose that
\phi(T)=(\gamma_{1}(T)\sin,\gamma_{2}(T)\cos)

we can reduce the previous system to (
j=1,2
)

d\gamma_{j}=[\mu\gamma_{j}-\frac{3}{4}|\gamma|^{2}\gamma_{j}]dT+\gamma_{j}d\hat{Z}^{\alpha}_{j}

where the driving
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
\hat{Z}^{\alpha}
depends only on
L^{\alpha}_{0},\ L^{\alpha}_{2},\ L^{\alpha}_{-2}
.

5
Conclusions and future challenges

In this work, we analysed a class of stochastic partial differential equations of the form (
1.1
)
driven by cylindrical
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes with
\alpha\in(1,2)
in fractional Sobolev spaces. By utilizing a separation of time-scales,
we explored the dynamics of the solution
u(t)
to equation (
1.1
) on the natural slow time-scale of order
\varepsilon^{-2}
in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Here
(\ref{GinzburgLandau})
was reduced to slow dynamics
on a dominant pattern coupled to dynamics on a fast time scale, which provided an effective tool for the qualitative analysis of the dynamical behaviors.
Our main result in Theorem
3.1
stated that near the change of stability, i.e., for small
\varepsilon>0
, the dynamics of (
1.1
) is well approximated by the amplitude equation (
3.20
) under appropriate conditions.
In order to obtain the error estimates of the approximation result, we introduced the moment inequality (
2.14
). The accuracy for those estimations were quantified by
p
-moment with
p\in(0,\alpha)
. The amplitude equation offered a benefit of dimension reduction in characterizing the qualitative properties of stochastic dynamics and detecting rigorously the stochastic bifurcation.

Let us comment here briefly on possible extensions of those results. We focused on the case of infinite dimensional multiplicative noise with
G(0)=0
. It should be a straightforward modification of our
analysis to treat additive noise
or noise with
G(0)\not=0
.
We have a slighly different scaling of the noise in that case,
but the general result will be similar.
The Lévy noise
L^{\alpha}
was assumed to be
\alpha
-stable and symmetric in this paper. Quantifying SPDEs driven by general Lévy processes and examining the impact of noise on system’s dynamics would be of particular useful for scientific computation and further analysis. Moreover, it would be interesting to extend the present considerations to SPDEs on unbounded domains that are intensely studied over the past few years, cf.
[
4
,
11
]
. Such studies are currently in progress and will be reported in future publications.

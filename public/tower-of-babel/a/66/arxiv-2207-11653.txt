Rationally AF algebras and KMS states of 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -absorbing C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras
George A. Elliott, Yasuhiko Sato

Abstract
In order to realize all possible KMS-bundles on the Jiang-Su algebra, we introduce a class of C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras which we call rationally approximately finite dimensional (RAF). Using these, we show that for a given proper simplex bundle ( S , π ) (S,\pi) with a singleton π − 1 ​ ( { 0 } ) \pi^{-1}(\{0\}) and a unital separable monotracial C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra A A absorbing the Jiang-Su algebra tensorially (for instance, the irrational rotation algebra), there exists a flow on A A whose KMS-bundle is isomorphic to ( S , π ) (S,\pi) .

Preliminaries

Let us start with some basic terminology and a fact for ordered abelian groups with the Riesz interpolation property.

In this paper, a
partially ordered abelian group
(
G
,
G^{+}
) will mean an abelian group
G
with a positive cone
G^{+}
satisfying
G^{+}+G^{+}\subset G^{+}
and
G^{+}\cap-G^{+}=\{0\}
. If just the first condition is satisfied,
(G,G^{+})
will be called a
pre-ordered abelian group
(see
[
43
]
). If, in addition to both conditions,
G=G^{+}-G^{+}
, then
(G,G^{+})
will be called an
ordered abelian group
(see
[
69
, Definition 5.1.3]
). For a partially ordered (or pre-ordered) abelian group
(G,G^{+})
, we shall denote by
\leq
the order relation defined by
g\leq h
if
h-g\in G^{+}
.

A partially ordered abelian group
(G,G^{+})
will be said to have the
Riesz (or Birkhoff-Riesz) interpolation property
(RIP) if for
g_{0}
,
g_{1}
,
h_{0}
,
h_{1}\in G
satisfying
g_{i}\leq h_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
there exists
a\in G
such that
g_{i}\leq a\leq h_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.
(See
[
7
]
.)

For a supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
(i.e., a formal infinite product of finite or infinite powers of prime numbers; see
[
17
]
,
[
66
]
), denote by
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
the set of all rational numbers
p/q
given by
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
q|{\mathfrak{n}}
, and fix the positive cone
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}=\{d\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}\ :\ d\geq 0\}
. Denote by
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]
the group of Laurent polynomials on
\mathbb{R}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
. For a closed subset
F\subset\mathbb{R}
, equip
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]
with the strict pointwise order, making it an ordered abelian group:

\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]_{F}^{+}=\{0\}\cup\{f\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]\ :\ f(x)>0\text{ for any }x\in F\}.

We will have occasion to use the following fundamental lemma in the sequel (in Lemma
3.5
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}
an infinite supernatural number. Then the ordered abelian group
(\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}],\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]_{F}^{+})
has the RIP if and only if
F
is semi-bounded (i.e., either bounded below or bounded above).

Proof.

The “if” part of the statement is a variant of the argument of
[
78
, Section 5]
. We include a proof for the reader’s convenience. Suppose that
F
is semi-bounded, and set
-F=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\ :\ -x\in F\}
. In the case that
F
is bounded, the Stone-Weierstrass theorem can be applied directly to show the RIP. So we may assume that
F
is unbounded. To simplify notation, set
G=\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}[e^{x},e^{-x}]
and
G_{F}^{+}=\mathbb{D}[e^{x},e^{-x}]_{F}^{+}
. It is immediate that
(G,G_{F}^{+})
has the RIP if and only if
(G,G_{-F}^{+})
has. Thus, we may assume that
F
is bounded below.

Let
p_{i}
,
q_{j}\in G
,
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, be such that
p_{i}\leq q_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
. If
p_{i_{0}}=q_{j_{0}}
for some
i_{0},j_{0}\in\{0,1\}
, then
a=p_{i_{0}}=q_{j_{0}}
satisfies
p_{i}\leq a\leq q_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
. Therefore, we may suppose that
p_{i}(x)<q_{j}(x)
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
and
x\in F
. As the first step in the argument which follows, note that for sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
R>0
, there exists
a_{0}\in G
such that

p_{i}(x)+e^{-Nx}<a_{0}(x)<q_{j}(x)-e^{-Nx},

for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
and
x\in F\cap[R,\infty)
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
denote the infinite direct sum
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
of copies of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
over
\mathbb{Z}
, and equip
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
with the reverse lexicographic order
\leq_{\rm lex}
, which means that
for
g=(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
,

if
g_{n}=h_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
or
g_{n_{0}}<h_{n_{0}}
for
n_{0}=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\ :\ g_{n}\neq h_{n}\}
. If
g\leq_{\rm lex}h
and
g\neq h
, write
g<_{\rm lex}h
. Since
F
is unbounded (above), it follows that
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}<_{\rm lex}(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
if, and only if, there exists
r>0
such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{n}e^{nx}<\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{n}e^{nx}
for any
x\in F\cap[r,\infty)
. Let
p_{i}^{\infty}=(p_{i,n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
q_{j}^{\infty}=(q_{j,n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
denote the coefficient sequences of
p_{i}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{i,n}e^{nx}
and
q_{j}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{j,n}e^{nx}
,
i,j\in\{0,1\}
. Since the reverse lexicographic order is a total order, reindexing, we may assume that
p_{0}^{\infty}\leq_{\rm lex}p_{1}^{\infty}<_{\rm lex}q_{0}^{\infty}\leq_{\rm lex}q_{1}^{\infty}
. Set
n_{0}=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\ :\ p_{1,n}\neq q_{0,n}\}
and note that
p_{1,n_{0}}<q_{0,n_{0}}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
\delta_{k}=(\delta_{k,n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
where
\delta_{k,n}
is the Kronecker delta, and set
a_{0}^{\infty}=p_{1}^{\infty}+\delta_{n_{0}-1}
. Then we have
p_{1}^{\infty}<_{\rm lex}a_{0}^{\infty}<_{\rm lex}q_{0}^{\infty}
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
chosen above be chosen sufficiently large that also
p_{0}^{\infty}+\delta_{-N}\leq_{\rm lex}p_{1}^{\infty}+\delta_{-N}<_{\rm lex}a_{0}^{\infty}<_{\rm lex}q_{0}^{\infty}-\delta_{-N}\leq q_{1}^{\infty}-\delta_{-N}
, which is the analogue in
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\infty}
of the required condition.

Since by the choice of
a_{0}
,
e^{Nx}(p_{i}(x)-a_{0}(x))<-1<1<e^{Nx}(q_{j}(x)-a_{0}(x))
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
and
x\in F\cap[R,\infty)
, there exist

and a continuous function

with compact support such that
e^{Nx}(p_{i}(x)-a_{0}(x))+d<f(x)<e^{Nx}(q_{j}(x)-a_{0}(x))-d
for any
x\in F
. By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem (applied to the compact space

and the subalgebra of Laurent polynomials bounded on this space), we obtain

such that
\sup_{x\in F}|f(x)-a_{1}(x)|<d
. Defining
a\in G
by
a(x)=e^{-Nx}a_{1}(x)+a_{0}(x)
, we have
p_{i}\leq a\leq q_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.

To show the “only if ” part, assume that
F
is not semi-bounded. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in F
be such that
N<e^{\beta}<2N
. Define
p_{i}
,
q_{j}\in G
,
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, by
p_{0}(x)=-1
,
p_{1}(x)=(N-e^{x})(e^{x}-2N)
,
q_{0}(x)=e^{2x}
, and
q_{1}(x)=N^{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then it follows that

p_{i}\leq q_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
. If there existed
a\in G
with
p_{i}\leq a\leq q_{j}
for all
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, then, as
p_{0}\leq a\leq q_{1}
,
a
would be a bounded function on
F
. Since
F
is not semi-bounded,
a
must be a constant function, in contradiction with
p_{1}\leq a\leq q_{0}
.
∎

3
Rational dimension groups and simplex bundles

The notion of rationally Riesz group was introduced in
[
56
]
, and it was shown that a weakly unperforated simple ordered abelian group
G
is a rationally Riesz group if and only if
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
has the Riesz interpolation property for every infinite supernatural number
{\mathfrak{p}}
. (See
[
66
]
for the definition of the supernatural numbers, which extend the natural numbers, which we shall refer to as finite.) In this section, we shall consider a modified definition for non-simple ordered abelian groups (Definition
3.2
), and construct an example which gives rise in a natural way to a given proper simplex bundle (see below), in Proposition
3.6
. In the sequel (see Theorem
6.5
), proper simplex bundles will be shown to give rise to
KMS-bundles on the Jiang-Su algebra.

Recall that the positive cone
(G\otimes H)^{+}
of the tensor product of two partially ordered (or pre-ordered) abelian groups
(G,G^{+})
and
(H,H^{+})
is defined as the set of all finite sums of
\{g\otimes h\ :\ g\in G^{+},h\in H^{+}\}
, and note that
(G\otimes H,(G\otimes H)^{+})
is then a partially ordered (or pre-ordered) abelian group; see
[
45
]
. For convenience, we shall just write
{G\otimes H}^{+}
. For an abelian group
G
we shall denote by
G\otimes 1_{{\bf k}}
the subgroup of
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{{\bf k}}
consisting of
\{g\otimes 1_{{\bf k}}\ :\ g\in G\}
, where
1_{{\bf k}}
denotes the number
1\in\mathbb{D}_{{\bf k}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}
be relatively prime supernatural numbers. For an abelian group
G
, the following fundamental statements hold.

(i)

If
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}
are torsion-free abelian groups, then so also is
G
.

(ii)

If
G
is torsion free, then the subgroup
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}})\cap(G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}})
of
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is isomorphic to
G
as an abelian group.

(iii)

If a partially ordered abelian group
(G,G^{+})
is such that
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+})
and
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{+})
are torsion-free ordered abelian groups, then
(G,G^{+})
is an ordered abelian group (i.e.,
G=G^{+}-G^{+}
).

(iv)

If
(G,G^{+})
is an unperforated ordered abelian group (i.e.,
ng\geq 0
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
implies
g\geq 0
; see
[
2
, Definition 6.7.1]
), then so also is
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
for any supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
.

(v)

If
(G,G^{+})
is an unperforated partially ordered abelian group such that
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+})
and
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{+})
are dimension groups, then
(G,G^{+})
is an ordered abelian group and
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
is a dimension group for any infinite supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
.

Proof.

(i). Assume that
g\in G
is a torsion element and
n\in\mathbb{N}
is the first number such that
ng=0
. Since
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is torsion free, we have
g\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
in
\langle g\rangle\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since
\langle g\rangle\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}/n\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, it follows that
1\in n\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Similarly, we have
1\in n\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Because
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\mathfrak{q}
are relatively prime, this implies that

and so
g=ng=0
.

(ii). Since
G
is torsion free, the canonical embedding
\varphi:G\rightarrow G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}}
defined by
\varphi(g)=g\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}}
for

is a group isomorphism. Therefore, it suffices to show that
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}})\cap(G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}})=G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}}
. If
x\in(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}})\cap(G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}})
, then there exist
k
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1/k\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
1/l\in\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, and
kx,lx\in G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Since
k
and

are relatively prime, there exist
a
,

such that
1=ak+bl
. It follows that

. The converse inclusion

is trivial.

(iii). Fix
x\in G
. For
{\mathfrak{i}}={\mathfrak{p}}
,
{\mathfrak{q}}

and
n_{\mathfrak{i}}a_{\mathfrak{i}}
,
n_{\mathfrak{i}}b_{\mathfrak{i}}\in G\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{i}}
for
{\mathfrak{i}}={\mathfrak{p}}
,
{\mathfrak{q}}
. Since
a_{\mathfrak{i}}
,
b_{\mathfrak{i}}\in{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{i}}}^{+}
, there exist
g_{a_{\mathfrak{i}}}
,
g_{b_{\mathfrak{i}}}\in G^{+}
,

, such that
n_{\mathfrak{i}}a_{\mathfrak{i}}=g_{a_{\mathfrak{i}}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{i}}
and
n_{\mathfrak{i}}b_{\mathfrak{i}}=g_{b_{\mathfrak{i}}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{i}}
. By (i),
G
is torsion free, and so from

satisfy

. Let

,

. Since

, and

Definition 3.2
.

We shall call a partially ordered abelian group
(G,G^{+})
a
rational dimension group
if
(G,G^{+})
is unperforated and, further,
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+})
and
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{+})
are dimension groups for some pair of relatively prime supernatural numbers
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}
. Note that a partially ordered abelian group is not necessarily unperforated even if
G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
is a dimension group for every infinite supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
. For example, the ordered abelian group
(\mathbb{Z},S_{n})
with
S_{n}=\{0,n,n+1,...\}
is not unperforated for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
; however,
(\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{S_{n}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
as an ordered abelian group for any infinite supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

For simple ordered abelian groups, it is enough for the tensor product of an unperforated ordered group with
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}
for a single supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
to be a dimension group in order for it to be a rational dimension group (see
[
56
, Proposition 5.7]
). However, for non-simple ordered abelian groups, the following example indicates the necessity of the two tensor products in the definition above.

Let
p
and
q
be two relatively prime numbers, and let
{\mathfrak{u}}
denote the universal supernatural number, with
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathbb{Q}
. Set
H=\mathbb{Q}^{2}
,
H^{+}=\{(x,y)\in H\ :\ x\geq 0,y\geq 0\}
,
a_{0}=(0,0)
,
b_{0}=(1,0)
,
a_{1}=(1/p,1/p-1)
, and
b_{1}=(1/p,1/p)
in
H
. Define
(G,G^{+})
as the ordered subgroup of
(H,H^{+})
generated by
\{a_{1},b_{1}\}
, which is unperforated and non-simple. Note that, for any supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
, the ordered abelian group
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
is isomorphic to

(\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}a_{1}+\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}b_{1},(\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}a_{1}+\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}b_{1})\cap H^{+})
, and that
a_{0}
,
b_{0}\in G
.
Then the tensor product
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{u}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{u}}}^{+})

\cong(H,H^{+})
is a dimension group. However, if there exists
x\in G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{q^{\infty}}
such that
a_{i}\otimes 1_{q^{\infty}}\leq x\leq b_{j}\otimes 1_{q^{\infty}}
, for

, then

must correspond to
(1/p,0)
via the identification of

with
\mathbb{D}_{q^{\infty}}a_{1}+\mathbb{D}_{q^{\infty}}b_{1}
. Since
p
and
q
are relatively prime,

cannot be contained in

. This shows that
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{q^{\infty}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{q^{\infty}}}^{+})
does not have the RIP.

Let us recall a few notions concerning simplex bundles, introduced in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
(see also
[
32
]
and
[
31
]
). A pair
(S,\pi)
consisting of a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space

and a continuous map
\pi:S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a
simplex bundle
if
\pi^{-1}(\{\beta\})
is compact and has a structure of Choquet simplex for every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. To simplify notation, we shall write
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
for
\pi^{-1}(\{\beta\})
. Two simplex bundles
(S,\pi)
and
(S^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists a homeomorphism
\Phi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\circ\Phi=\pi
and the restriction of
\Phi
to
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
is affine for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

For a simplex bundle
(S,\pi)
, we shall denote by
A(S)
the set of all continuous functions
f
from
S
to
\mathbb{R}
such that each restriction of
f
to
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
is affine for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We also set

\displaystyle A_{0}(S)

\displaystyle=\{f\in A(S)\ :\ f\text{ vanishes at infinity}\},

\displaystyle A_{00}(S)

\displaystyle=\{f\in A(S)\ :\supp(f)\text{ is compact and }\supp(f)\cap\pi^{-1}(0)=\O\}.

A simplex bundle
(S,\pi)
will be called
proper
if
\pi
is a proper map (i.e.
\pi^{-1}(K)
is compact for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
), and

separates points of
S
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(S,\pi)
be a simplex bundle,
S^{\prime}
a locally compact second countable Hausdorff space, and
\pi^{\prime}:S^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a continuous map. Suppose that
\pi
is proper and
\Phi
is a bijective continuous map from
S
to
S^{\prime}
such that
\pi=\pi^{\prime}\circ\Phi
. Then
\Phi
is a homeomorphism. In particular, if
(S,\pi)
is a proper simplex bundle and the restriction of
\Phi
to
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
is affine for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, then
(S^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
is also a proper simplex bundle.

Proof.

For any compact subset
K
of
S^{\prime}
, since
\pi^{\prime}
is continuous, there exists a compact interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\pi^{\prime}(K)\subset I
, which implies that
\Phi^{-1}(K)\subset\pi^{-1}(I)
. Since
\pi^{-1}(I)
is compact, so also is
\Phi^{-1}(K)
. This shows that
\Phi
is a proper map. Let
F
be a closed subset of
S
. To show
\Phi^{-1}
is continuous, it suffices to show that
\Phi(F)
is closed. For any compact subset
K
of
S^{\prime}
, it follows that
\Phi(F)\cap K=\Phi(F\cap\Phi^{-1}(K))
is compact. Since
S^{\prime}
is locally compact and Hausdorff, this shows that
\Phi(F)
is closed.

As
\pi=\pi^{\prime}\circ\Phi
, it follows that
{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(B)=\Phi(\pi^{-1}(B))
for any subset

of
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\pi^{\prime}
is also a proper map. If each restriction of
\Phi
to
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
is affine, then we see that
A(S^{\prime})=\{f\circ\Phi^{-1}\ :\ f\in A(S)\}
separates points of
S^{\prime}
.
∎

, it follows that
\iota_{{\mathfrak{u}}\sigma}^{*}
is a bijective continuous map such that
\pi_{\mathbb{Z}}\circ\iota_{{\mathfrak{u}}\sigma}^{*}=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\iota_{{\mathfrak{u}}\sigma}^{*}|_{\pi_{\mathbb{Q}}^{-1}(\beta)}
is affine for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Because of Lemma
3.4
,
\iota_{{\mathfrak{u}}\sigma}^{*}
is an isomorphism of simplex bundles and
(S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}}),\pi_{\mathbb{Z}})
is also proper.

(iii).
As
\Psi_{\mathbb{Z}}=\iota_{{\mathfrak{u}}\sigma}^{*}\circ\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}
, it suffices to show that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}:S\rightarrow S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is an isomorphism of simplex bundles. From the definition, it is straightforward to check that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}
is injective and continuous, and the restriction of
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}
to
\pi^{-1}(\beta)
is affine for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
3.4
, it is enough to show that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}
is surjective. However, this is done in the proof of
[
32
, Lemma 4.12]
. We sketch a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Let
(\varphi,\beta)
be in
S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
g=\xi\oplus f\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
f=0\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Q})
, then it follows that
ng\leq u
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies
\varphi(g)=0
. Thus we obtain a positive group homomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}:\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}\circ\rho=\varphi
. Denote by
A_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
the real Banach space, with the supremum norm, consisting of functions in
A(S)
which have a limit at infinity. Since
\rho(G_{\mathbb{Q}}^{u})\cap A_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
is uniformly dense in
A_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
(see
[
32
, Lemma 4.11]
), we can extend
\varphi^{\prime}
to
A_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
in such a way that
|\varphi^{\prime}(f)|\leq\sup_{s\in S}|f(s)|
for any
f\in A_{\mathbb{R}}(S)
. By the Hahn-Banach theorem, we can further extend
\varphi^{\prime}
to the Banach space
C^{u}(S)=\{f\in C(S,\mathbb{R}):\text{ there exists }\lim_{s\to\pm\infty}f(s)\}
. Since
\varphi^{\prime}(1)=1
, the extension of
\varphi^{\prime}
is also a positive bounded linear functional on
C^{u}(S)
. Thus we obtain a Borel measure
m_{\varphi}
on
S
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(f)=\int_{S}f(x)\ dm_{\varphi}(x)\quad\text{for any }f\in C_{0}(S).

For
y\in\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
such that
y>0
, by
[
32
, Lemma 4.4 (2) and Lemma 4.11]
, we obtain a sequence
g_{yn}=(\xi_{yn}\oplus f_{yn})\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\supp(f_{yn})
is compact,
f_{yn}\geq 0
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{s\in S}|f_{yn}(s)|<\infty
, and
f_{yn}|_{\pi^{-1}(\beta)}
converges to
y
uniformly. Let
\omega
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. For
y\in\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
with
y>0
, we define
\displaystyle{\overline{\varphi}}(y)=\lim_{n\to\omega}\varphi^{\prime}(f_{yn})
. Then
{\overline{\varphi}}
is independent of the choice of
\xi_{yn}
and
f_{yn}
. Indeed, if we suppose that
g_{yn}^{\prime}=(\xi_{yn}^{\prime}\oplus f_{yn}^{\prime})\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
is another choice, then for
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sup_{s\in\pi^{-1}(\beta)}|f_{yn}(s)-f_{yn}^{\prime}(s)|<\varepsilon
, for any
n>N
. Set
X_{n}=\supp(f_{yn})\cup\supp(f_{yn}^{\prime})
, which is compact for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
q_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}[e^{x},e^{-x}]
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence such that
q_{m}|_{X_{n}}
converges to the characteristic function
\chi_{\beta}

at
\{\beta\}
and such that
\supp_{s\in X_{n}}|q_{m}(s)|
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, is a bounded sequence.
Then, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}((q_{m}\circ\pi)f_{yn})

\displaystyle=\int_{S}(\chi_{\beta}\circ\pi)f_{yn}\ dm_{\varphi}\approx_{\varepsilon}\int_{S}(\chi_{\beta}\circ\pi)f_{yn}^{\prime}\ dm_{\varphi}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}((q_{m}\circ\pi)f_{yn}^{\prime}),

for any
n\geq N
.
It follows that

|\varphi^{\prime}(f_{yn}-f_{yn}^{\prime})|\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\left|\varphi^{\prime}(q_{m}\circ\pi(f_{yn}-f_{yn}^{\prime}))\right|+\left|(1-q_{m}(\beta))\varphi^{\prime}(f_{yn}-f_{yn}^{\prime})\right|\right)<\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have
\lim_{n\to\omega}\varphi^{\prime}(f_{yn})=\lim_{n\to\omega}\varphi^{\prime}(f_{yn}^{\prime})
.

Since
{\overline{\varphi}}
is well defined, we see that
{\overline{\varphi}}(x+y)={\overline{\varphi}}(x)+{\overline{\varphi}}(y)
for any
x,y\in\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
with
x,y>0
. Because
\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
is an ordered abelian group in the strict ordering, we can extend
{\overline{\varphi}}
to a positive group homomorphism from
\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
to
\mathbb{R}
. Thus there exists
s\in\pi^{-1}(\beta)
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
such that
{\overline{\varphi}}(y)=\lambda y(s)
for any
y\in\Aff(\pi^{-1}(\beta))
; see
[
43
, Theorem 7.1]
, for example. From the definition of
{\overline{\varphi}}
it follows that

\lambda f(s)={\overline{\varphi}}(f|_{\pi^{-1}(\beta)})=\varphi(\xi\oplus f),

for any
g=\xi\oplus f\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\supp(f)
is compact. For
g=\xi\oplus f\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
with
f\in A_{0}(S)
and
\xi=0
, by the property (1) of
\mathcal{G}_{00}
, there exists a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{00}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which converges to
f
uniformly. Then it follows that
\varphi(0\oplus f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(0\oplus f_{n})=\lambda f(s)
. For
f\in Q
, there exists a large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(fe^{\mp Nx}\psi_{k\pm})\circ\pi\in A_{0}(S)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have that
\varphi(0\oplus(f\psi_{k\pm})\circ\pi)=e^{\pm N\beta}\varphi(0\oplus(e^{\mp Nx}f\psi_{k\pm})\circ\pi)=\lambda f\psi_{k\pm}(\beta)
, which implies that
\varphi(g)=\lambda f(s)
for any

. Since
\varphi(u)=1
, it is clear that
\lambda=1
, and thus we conclude that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)=(\varphi,\beta)
.
∎

4
Rationally AF algebras

In order to realize a given rational dimension group at the level of
K_{0}
-groups for operator algebras, it seems plausible that a corresponding
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra should become an AF-algebra after tensoring
with a UHF-algebra. This tensoring procedure is called rationalization in
[
3
]
,
[
84
]
. While the rationalization was determined by a single UHF-algebra in these previous works, we would like to use the term “rationally” to refer to the tensor products with two UHF-algebras, that are relatively prime in the natural sense. (cf. Remark
3.3
.)

We prepare some facts and notation concerning
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
For a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C
, we let
C^{\sim}
denote the unitization of
C
, and
C^{1}
denote the closed unit ball of
C
. We denote by
\id_{C}
the identity automorphism of
C
. For a subset
F\subset C
, let
P(F)
denote the set of all projections in
F
and
F^{+}
the set of all positive elements in
F
. When
C
is a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, the symbol
1_{C}
means the unit of
C
. For two elements
x
,
y\in C
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we use the notation
x\approx_{\varepsilon}y
if
\|x-y\|<\varepsilon
. For subsets
F
,
G
in
C
, and
\varepsilon>0
, the notation
F\subset_{\varepsilon}G
means that for any
x\in F
there exists
y\in G
with
y\approx_{\varepsilon}x
.

For a supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
, let
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
denote the uniformly hyperfinite (UHF) algebra of type
{\mathfrak{n}}
(i.e.,
(K_{0}(M_{\mathfrak{n}}),K_{0}(M_{\mathfrak{n}})^{+},[1_{M_{\mathfrak{n}}}]_{0})\cong(\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{+},1_{\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}})
), and
\mathcal{K}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of all compact operators on a separable Hilbert space. For a natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\{e_{ij}^{(n)}\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
or
\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
denote the set of canonical matrix units of
M_{n}
.

For two
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
C
,
D
, and a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi
from
C
to
D
, denote the induced map from
K_{0}(C)
to
K_{0}(D)
, a homomorphism of pre-ordered abelian groups (the positive cone of
K_{0}
being the canonical image of the Murray-von Neumann semigroup—see
[
69
]
), by
\varphi_{*}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, we shall say that
A
is
rationally AF
(RAF) if
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{q}}
are AF-algebras for some relatively prime pair of supernatural numbers
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

(i)

If
A
is a RAF-algebra, then
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}
is approximately finite dimensional for every infinite supernatural number
{\mathfrak{n}}
.

(ii)

Any RAF-algebra has trivial
K_{1}
-group.

Proof.

(i). Since
A\otimes M_{n}
is also a RAF-algebra for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, to show
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}
is approximately finite dimensional, it suffices to show that for any finite subset
F
of
A
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
B
of
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}
such that
F\otimes 1_{M_{\mathfrak{n}}}\subset_{\varepsilon}B
.

Taking large natural numbers
m_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{q}}
with
m_{\mathfrak{p}}|{\mathfrak{p}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{q}}|{\mathfrak{q}}
, we have finite dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebras
A_{\mathfrak{i}}
of
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{i}}
for
{\mathfrak{i}}={\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}
such that
F\otimes 1_{M_{\mathfrak{i}}}\subset_{\varepsilon}A_{\mathfrak{i}}
for both
\mathfrak{i}={\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}
. Since
m_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{q}}
are relatively prime, there exist natural numbers
a
,
b
, and
N
such that
am_{\mathfrak{p}}+bm_{\mathfrak{q}}=N
and
N|{\mathfrak{n}}
(cf. proof of Lemma
3.1
). Therefore there exists a unital embedding
\Phi
of
M_{m_{\mathfrak{p}}}\oplus M_{m_{\mathfrak{q}}}
into
M_{\mathfrak{n}}
. Identifying
A\otimes(M_{m_{\mathfrak{p}}}\oplus M_{m_{\mathfrak{q}}})
with
(A\otimes M_{m_{\mathfrak{p}}})\oplus(A\otimes M_{m_{\mathfrak{q}}})
, we obtain a finite dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
B=(\id_{A}\otimes\Phi)(A_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus A_{\mathfrak{q}})
of
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}
which satisfies the above condition.

(ii). Because of the Künneth theorem
[
74
, Theorem 2.14]
(see also
[
2
, Theorem 23.1.3]
), we see that
K_{1}(A)\otimes K_{0}(M_{\mathfrak{i}})\cong K_{1}(A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{i}})=0
for both
{\mathfrak{i}}={\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}
. From (i) of Lemma
3.1
, it follows that
K_{1}(A)
is torsion free. Therefore the canonical embedding of
K_{1}(A)
into
K_{1}(A)\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
is injective, and so
K_{1}(A)=0
.
∎

Note that any RAF-algebra is AF-embeddable, and so it is stably finite. Hence the ordered
K_{0}
-group of any RAF-algebra is a partially ordered abelian group. In combination with Lemma
3.1
(iii), the following results show that the ordered
K_{0}
-group of a
\mathcal{Z}
-absorbing RAF-algebra is an ordered abelian group.

A typical example of a RAF-algebra is the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
, which is constructed as a unital separable simple monotracial RAF-algebra whose ordered
K_{0}
-group is
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. In
[
38
, Theorem 1]
, Gong, Jiang, and Su showed that the ordered
K_{0}
-group of a unital simple
\mathcal{Z}
-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra is weakly unperforated. Part (ii) of the following Proposition
4.3
is a variant of their argument for RAF-algebras in the absence of simplicity.

Proposition 4.3
.

(i)

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
{\mathfrak{n}}
a supernatural number. Then the pre-ordered abelian groups
(K_{0}(A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}),

K_{0}(A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}})^{+})
and
(K_{0}(A)\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}},{K_{0}(A)\otimes\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{+})
are isomorphic (in a natural way).

(ii)

Let
A
be a
\mathcal{Z}
-absorbing RAF-algebra. Then the partially ordered abelian group
(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)^{+})
is unperforated, and, furthermore, is a rational dimension group.

Proof.

(i).
It is enough to note that, for any natural number
n
, the Murray-von Neumann semigroup of
A\otimes M_{n}
is the same (by definition—with respect to the map
A\rightarrow A\otimes e_{11}\subset A\otimes M_{n}
) as that of
A
(the image of which in
K_{0}(A)
is the positive cone), and the semigroup endomorphism corresponding to the map
A\rightarrow A\otimes 1_{M_{n}}\subset A\otimes M_{n}
is just multiplication by
n
. (Recall that both the Murray-von Neumann semigroup functor and the
K_{0}
functor preserve inductive limits.)

(ii). If
A
is a RAF-algebra, then so also is
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
. Since the ordered
K_{0}
-groups
(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)^{+})
and
(K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{K}),K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{K})^{+})
are isomorphic, without loss of generality we may assume that
A
is stable.

Let
\iota
denote the canonical embedding of
A
into
A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
defined by
\iota(a)=a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}
for
a\in A
, and
\iota_{*}
the induced map by
\iota
from
K_{0}(A)
to
K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})
. As in the proof of
[
38
, Theorem 1]
, for
g\in K_{0}(A)
it is enough to show that
\iota_{*}(g)\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}
if and only if
ng\in K_{0}(A)^{+}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, if
x\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})
satisfies
nx\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then, since (by
[
74
]
)
\iota_{*}
is a group isomorphism,
there exists
g_{x}\in K_{0}(A)
such that
\iota_{*}(g_{x})=x
. Then we obtain
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
mng_{x}\in K_{0}(A)^{+}
which implies
x\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}
.

Suppose that
g\in K_{0}(A)
satisfies
\iota_{*}(g)\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}
. Recall that the Jiang-Su algebra is constructed as the inductive limit
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}(\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}},\varphi_{n})
of prime dimension drop algebras
\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the connecting maps
\varphi_{n}:\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}}

\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n+1}q_{n+1}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
are relatively prime numbers; see
[
46
]
. Note that
A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}}
is stably finite for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
: then
(K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}}),K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}})^{+})
is a partially ordered abelian group. By the continuity of the functor
K_{0}
(see
[
69
, Theorem 6.3.2]
, for example), there exist embeddings
\psi_{n}:A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}}\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
such that
K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n*}(K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{n}q_{n}})^{+})
. Then there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}})^{+}
such that
\iota_{*}(g)=\psi_{N*}(x)
. Let
\iota_{N}:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}}
denote the canonical embedding defined by
\iota_{N}(a)=a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}}}
for
a\in A
and
\ev_{0}:A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}}\rightarrow A\otimes M_{p_{N}}
the evaluation map determined by
\ev_{0}(f)\otimes 1_{M_{q_{N}}}=f(0)
for
f\in A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}}
.
Since
\ev_{0}\circ\iota_{N}(a)=a\otimes 1_{M_{p_{N}}}
for any
a\in A
, it follows that
\ev_{0*}\circ\iota_{N*}(g)=p_{N}g
.
Since
\iota_{N*}(g)=x\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{p_{N}q_{N}})^{+}
, we have
p_{N}g=\ev_{0*}\circ\iota_{N*}(g)\in K_{0}(A)^{+}
.

Conversely, suppose that
g\in K_{0}(A)
satisfies
ng\in K_{0}(A)^{+}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
p
and
q\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
be relatively prime natural numbers, and denote by
\iota_{i}:A\rightarrow A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}}
,
i=p,q
, the canonical embeddings defined by
\iota_{i}(a)=a\otimes 1_{M_{i^{\infty}}}
for
a\in A
. Since
K_{0}(A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}})
,
i=p,q
, are ordered abelian groups, there exist
a_{i}
,
b_{i}\in K_{0}(A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}})^{+}
such that
\iota_{i*}(g)=a_{i}-b_{i}
for both

. Since

is unperforated and

a_{i}-b_{i}\in K_{0}(A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}})^{+}
. Because
A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}}
,
i=p,q

y_{i}\in A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}}
,
i=p,q

[y_{i}]_{0}=a_{i}-b_{i}
. Identifying
A\otimes M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}}

canonically, we have
[y_{p}\otimes 1_{M_{q^{\infty}}}]_{0}=[y_{q}\otimes 1_{M_{p^{\infty}}}]_{0}
in
K_{0}(A\otimes M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}})
. Since
A\otimes M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}}
is an AF-algebra, there exists a projection

such that

and
z(1)=y_{q}\otimes 1_{M_{p^{\infty}}}

-algebra

\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}=\{f\in C([0,1])\otimes M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}}\ :\ f(0)\in M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes 1_{M_{q^{\infty}}},f(1)\in 1_{M_{p^{\infty}}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}}\},

\iota_{\infty}:A\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}

\iota_{\infty}(a)=a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}}
for

, and by
\Ev_{i}:A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}\rightarrow A\otimes M_{i^{\infty}}
,

and

. Since
K_{1}(A\otimes M_{p^{\infty}}\otimes M_{q^{\infty}})=0

(\Ev_{p}\oplus\Ev_{q})_{*}:K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{\infty})\rightarrow K_{0}(A\otimes M_{p^{\infty}})\oplus K_{0}(A\otimes M_{q^{\infty}})

(\Ev_{p}\oplus\Ev_{q})_{*}([z]_{0}-\iota_{\infty*}(g))=([y_{p}]_{0}\oplus[y_{q}]_{0})-(\iota_{p*}(g)\oplus\iota_{q*}(g))=0,

considering the embedding
\iota_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}}:A\otimes\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}\rightarrow A\otimes\mathcal{Z}

\iota_{*}(g)=\iota_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}*}\circ\iota_{\infty*}(g)\in K_{0}(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})^{+}
.

This shows that
(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)^{+})
is an unperforated partially ordered abelian group. On using once more that

is RAF, the second statement follows from (i).
∎

Our goal in this section is to provide a construction of RAF-algebras which exhausts all countable rational dimension groups as
K_{0}
-groups. Here we emphasize that the non-simple cases of RAF-algebras are required in order to realize the rational dimension group
(G_{\mathbb{Z}},G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})
of Section 3. The construction is somewhat analogous to the construction of simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in
[
25
]
and
[
46
]
.

As a corollary, we also show that a natural addition to the invariant in the non-stable case is exhausted (Corollary
4.8
). In Section 5, we shall show that the augmented invariant (
K_{0}
alone in the stable case) is complete.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any countable rational dimension group
(G,G^{+})
, there exists a separable stable RAF-algebra
A_{G}
such that
A_{G}\otimes\mathcal{Z}\cong A_{G}
and
(K_{0}(A_{G}),K_{0}(A_{G})^{+})
is isomorphic to
(G,G^{+})
as an ordered abelian group.

Let
(G,G^{+})
be a countable rational dimension group. For
i=0,1
, we obtain a stable AF-algebra
{\it A}\hskip-3.00003pt\raisebox{0.23499pt}{\sf F}_{i}
such that
(K_{0}({\it A}\hskip-3.00003pt\raisebox{0.23499pt}{\sf F}_{i}),K_{0}({\it A}\hskip-3.00003pt\raisebox{0.23499pt}{\sf F}_{i})^{+})
is isomorphic to
(G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{(2+i)^{\infty}},{G\otimes\mathbb{D}_{(2+i)^{\infty}}}^{+})
as an ordered abelian group. Because of the Elliott classification theorem
[
21
]
and Proposition

\tau\in\mathcal{T}(A)
.
Then it follows that the net
\tau_{\lambda*}\otimes\tau_{{\mathfrak{n}}*}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
in
S_{0}(K_{0}(A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}))
converges to
\tau_{*}\otimes\tau_{{\mathfrak{n}}*}
in the topology of pointwise convergence. Applying Lemma
6.3
(ii) to
A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}}
, we have that
\tau_{\lambda}\otimes\tau_{\mathfrak{n}}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
converges to
\tau\otimes\tau_{\mathfrak{n}}
in
\mathcal{T}(A\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}})
. Since
(A\otimes M_{N})_{0}\otimes 1_{M_{\mathfrak{n}}}\subset(A\otimes M_{N}\otimes M_{\mathfrak{n}})_{0}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we conclude that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\tau_{\lambda}(a)=\tau(a)
for any
a\in(A\otimes M_{N})_{0}
.

∎

We are now ready to give our application of the RAF-algebra classification.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(S,\pi)
be a proper simplex bundle such that
\pi^{-1}(0)
is a singleton. Then there exists a
2\pi
-periodic flow
\theta
on the Jiang-Su algebra whose KMS-bundle is isomorphic to
(S,\pi)
.

For a given proper simplex bundle
(S,\pi)
with
\pi^{-1}(0)=\{\tau_{S}\}
, in Section 3 we have constructed the rational dimension group
(G_{\mathbb{Z}},G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})
and its shift automorphism
\sigma
such that
(S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}}),\pi_{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
(S,\pi)
as a simplex bundle (Proposition
3.6
(iii)).

We define a positive group homomorphism
\Sigma_{0}:G_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
by
\Sigma_{0}((z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\oplus g)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z_{n}=g(\tau_{S})
for
(z_{n})_{n}\oplus g\in G_{\mathbb{Z}}
. By the same arguments as in the proofs of
[
32
, Lemma 4.10]
,
[
31
, Lemma 3.7]
, and
[
32
, Lemma 4.14]
, the rational dimension group
(G_{\mathbb{Z}},G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})
has the following properties.

Lemma 6.6
.

(i)

(G_{\mathbb{Z}},G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})
is
\sigma
-simple, and

(ii)

\displaystyle(\id-\sigma)(G_{\mathbb{Z}})=\ker(\Sigma_{0})
.

From Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
5.3
, we obtain a stable
{\mathcal{Z}}
-absorbing RAF-algebra
A
and an automorphism
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}
on
A
such that

(K_{0}(A),K_{0}(A)^{+})\cong(G_{\mathbb{Z}},G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}*}=\sigma\text{ on }K_{0}(A).

By Proposition
6.2
and the same argument in
[
57
, Remark 2.8]
(see also
[
31
, Lemma 3.6]
),
A
and
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}
can be chosen to have the following properties.

Lemma 6.7
.

(i)

A\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{Z}
is a simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra,

(ii)
The restriction map
\tau\mapsto\tau|_{A}
from
\mathcal{T}(A\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{Z})
onto the
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant traces in
\mathcal{T}(A)
is bijective, and

(iii)

A\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{Z}
absorbs the Jiang-Su algebra tensorially.

Set
C=A\rtimes_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{Z}
, and denote by
P
the conditional expectation from
C
onto
A
. Since
A
is a stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, for
u\in G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}\cong K_{0}(A)^{+}
defined as
u=(1_{0},1)
in Section 3, there exists a projection
p
in
A
such that
[p]_{0}=u
. Then the unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
pCp
has the following properties.

Proposition 6.8
.

(i)

pCp
has a unique tracial state,

(ii)

(K_{0}(pCp),K_{0}(pCp)^{+})\cong(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{+})
,
[p]_{0}=1\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(iii)

K_{1}(pCp)\cong 0
, and

(iv)
therefore
pCp
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Proof.

(i). Set
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{0}(A)=\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(A)\ :\ \tau\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}=\tau,\text{ and }\tau(p)=1\}
. By Proposition
6.4
,
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{0}(A)
corresponds to
S_{\sigma}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Z}})=\{\tau_{S}\}
via
{\Phi_{\mathcal{T}}}
. Because of
[
15
, Proposition 4.7]
, the set of tracial states
T(pCp)
of
pCp
corresponds to
\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(C)\ :\ \tau(p)=1\}
via the restriction on
pCp
. By Lemma
6.7
(ii),
we know that
\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(C)\ :\ \tau(p)=1\}
is a singleton, which implies that
T(pCp)
is also singleton.

(ii). By Lemma
4.2
(ii), we see that
K_{1}(A)=0
. Then the Pimsner-Voiculescu six-term exact sequence allows us to see that

K_{0}(C)\cong G_{\mathbb{Z}}/(\id-\sigma)(G_{\mathbb{Z}})=G_{\mathbb{Z}}/\ker(\Sigma_{0})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

Consider the quotient map
q:G_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{Z}}/(\id-\sigma)(G_{\mathbb{Z}})
and denote by
\iota_{*}:K_{0}(A)\rightarrow K_{0}(C)
the map induced by the inclusion
\iota:A\rightarrow C
. In the exact sequence, precisely,
\iota_{*}
corresponds to
q
and the above group isomorphism
\Sigma:K_{0}(C)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is determined by
\Sigma\circ q=\Sigma_{0}
; see also the paragraph after
[
31
, Lemma 3.7]
. Set
\tau^{0}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{0}(A)
which corresponds to
\tau_{S}\in S_{\sigma}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Z}})
, (determined by
\tau^{0}_{*}=\widehat{\tau_{S}}
). For
x\in K_{0}(C)^{+}
, now we obtain
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\oplus f\in G_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
q((y_{n})_{n}\oplus f))=x
. Then it follows that

0\leq(\tau^{0}\circ P)_{*}(x)=\tau_{*}^{0}((y_{n})_{n}\oplus f)=f(\tau_{S})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}y_{n}\in\mathbb{Z},

which implies that
\Sigma(x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. The converse inclusion
\Sigma(K_{0}(C)^{+})\supset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
follows from the properties
nu\in K_{0}(A)^{+}
and
n=\Sigma\circ q(nu)\in\Sigma(K_{0}(C)^{+})
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then we have that
(K_{0}(pCp),K_{0}(pCp)^{+})\cong(K_{0}(C),K_{0}(C)^{+})\cong(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. In particular, since
1=\Sigma\circ q(u)
,
[p]_{0}
corresponds to
1\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii).
For the same reason as in
[
31
, Lemma 3.11]
, we see that the map
\id-\sigma
is injective on
K_{0}(A)\cong G_{\mathbb{Z}}
. From the Pimsner-Voiculescu exact sequence, we see that
K_{1}(C)=0
.

(iv).
By Lemma
6.7
, we have now shown that
pCp
is a unital separable simple nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra which absorbs the Jiang-Su algebra tensorially and satisfies the universal coefficient theorem. By (i), (ii), (iii) above, we see that
pCp
is monotracial and the Elliott invariant of
pCp
is same as for
{\mathcal{Z}}
. From
[
67
, Corollary 4.6]
, we also see that
pCp
has strict comparison. Then by
[
59
, Corollary 6.2]
, we conclude that
pCp\cong{\mathcal{Z}}
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

Denote by
\widetilde{\theta}
the dual action of
\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}
on
C
and by
\theta
the restriction of
\widetilde{\theta}
to
pCp\cong{\mathcal{Z}}
. Denote by
(S_{\theta},\pi_{\theta})
the KMS-bundle of the dynamical system
({\mathcal{Z}},\theta)
.
By Proposition
3.6
(iii),
(S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}}),\pi_{\mathbb{Z}})
is a proper simplex bundle isomorphic to
(S,\pi)
. It remains to show that
(S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}}),\pi_{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
(S_{\theta},\pi_{\theta})
. Set

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)=\{(\tau,\beta)\in\mathcal{T}(A)\times\mathbb{R}\ :\ \tau\circ\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}=e^{-\beta}\tau,\quad\tau(p)=1\},

equipped with the product topology on
\mathcal{T}(A)\times\mathbb{R}
, and denote by
\pi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}:\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the projection. Define a continuous map
\Phi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}:\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)\rightarrow S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}})
by
\Phi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tau,\beta)=({\Phi_{\mathcal{T}}}(\tau),\beta)
for
(\tau,\beta)\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
. By Proposition
6.4
,
\Phi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a homeomorphism, and
\Phi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{-1}|_{\pi_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-1}(\beta)}
is affine for each
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

It follows that the projection
p\in A
(of Proposition
6.8
) is full in
C
, i.e.,
\overline{CpC}=C
, by the simplicity of
C
.
Define a map

\Psi:\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)\rightarrow S_{\theta}
by
\Psi((\tau,\beta))=(\tau\circ P|_{pCp},\beta)

for
(\tau,\beta)\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}(A)
. By
[
32
, Lemma 4.1]
, we see that
\Psi
is bijective and
\Psi|_{\pi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{-1}(\beta)}
is an affine homeomorphism from
\pi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{-1}(\beta)
onto
\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(\beta)
. Since
pAp\subset A_{0}
, if a sequence
\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(A)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges to
\tau\in\mathcal{T}(A)
then
\tau_{n}\circ P|_{pCp}\rightarrow\tau\circ P|_{pCp}
in the topology of pointwise convergence. Thus,
\Psi
is continuous. We define a bijective continuous map
\Phi:S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow S_{\theta}
by
\Phi=\Psi\circ\Phi_{\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}}}^{-1}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we conclude that
(S_{\sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Z}}),\pi_{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
(S_{\theta},\pi_{\theta})
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
A
be a unital separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with a unique tracial state. Suppose that

A
absorbs the Jiang-Su algebra tensorially.
Then for any proper simplex bundle
(S,\pi)
such that
\pi^{-1}(0)
is singleton,
there exists a
2\pi
-periodic flow on
A
whose KMS-bundle is isomorphic to
(S,\pi)
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\iota}_{t}=\id_{A}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, denote the trivial flow on
A
and
\tau
the unique tracial state of
A
. For a given
(S,\pi)
, by Theorem
6.5
we obtain a flow
\theta
on
\mathcal{Z}
whose KMS-bundle is isomorphic to
(S,\pi)
. We denote by
A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra tensor product and define a flow
\alpha
on
A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
as the tensor product action which is defined by
\alpha_{t}(a\otimes b)=\widetilde{\iota}_{t}(a)\otimes\theta_{t}(b)
for
a\in A
and
b\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Let
(S_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha})
denote the KMS-bundle of
\alpha
and
S_{\theta}^{\beta}({\mathcal{Z}})
(resp.
S_{\alpha}^{\beta}(A\otimes{\mathcal{Z}})
) the set of
\beta
-KMS states for
\theta
(resp.
\alpha
).
Define an affine map
\Phi
from
S(\mathcal{Z})
to
S(A\otimes\mathcal{Z})
by
\Phi(\varphi)=\tau\otimes\varphi
. Then it is straightforward to check that
\Phi(\varphi)
is a
\beta
-KMS state for
\alpha
for any
\varphi\in S_{\theta}^{\beta}(\mathcal{Z})
, and
\Phi|_{S_{\theta}^{\beta}({\mathcal{Z}})}

:S_{\theta}^{\beta}({\mathcal{Z}})\rightarrow S_{\alpha}^{\beta}(A\otimes{\mathcal{Z}})
is affine continuous and injective for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Define a continuous map
\Phi_{A}

:S_{\theta}\rightarrow S_{\alpha}
by
\Phi_{A}((\varphi,\beta))=(\Phi(\varphi),\beta)
for
(\varphi,\beta)\in S_{\theta}
.

In order to apply Lemma
3.4
to
\Phi_{A}
, it remains to show surjectivity of
\Phi|_{S_{\theta}^{\beta}({\mathcal{Z}})}
. Given
\psi\in S_{\alpha}^{\beta}(A\otimes{\mathcal{Z}})
,
x\in\mathcal{Z}
, and
a,b\in A
, note that
a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}
and
b\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}
are analytic elements for
\alpha
and
\psi(ab\otimes x)=\psi((b\otimes x)\alpha_{i\beta}(a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}}))=\psi(ba\otimes x)
.
Since
\tau
is a unique tracial state, we have
\tau(a)\psi(1_{A}\otimes x)=\psi(a\otimes x)
, for any
a\in A
and
x\in\mathcal{Z}
. Set
\psi_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)=\psi(1_{A}\otimes x)
, for
x\in\mathcal{Z}
. Then
\psi_{\mathcal{Z}}
is contained in
S_{\theta}^{\beta}(\mathcal{Z})
, as
\psi(1_{A}\otimes xy)=\psi((1_{A}\otimes y)\alpha_{i\beta}(1_{A}\otimes x))

=\psi(1_{A}\otimes y(\theta_{i\beta}(x)))
for any analytic elements
x,y\in\mathcal{Z}
for
\theta
. Thus we see that
\Phi(\psi_{\mathcal{Z}})(a\otimes x)=\psi(a\otimes x)
for any
a\in A
and
x\in\mathcal{Z}
.
∎

From
[
63
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
51
, Proposition 2.1]
, and
[
58
, Theorem 1.1]
, we obtain uncountably many flows which are not approximately inner on the following classifiable class of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Corollary 6.10

(cf. Corollary 4.2 of

]
)
.

Any unital separable simple amenable monotracial

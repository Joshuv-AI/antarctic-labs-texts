Unique Solutions to Hyperbolic Conservation Laws with a Strictly Convex Entropy
Alberto Bressan, Graziano Guerra (*) Department of Mathematics, Penn State University, (**) Department of Mathematics and Applications, University of Milano - Bicocca. E-mails: axb62@psu.edu, graziano.guerra@unimib.it

Abstract
Consider a strictly hyperbolic n × n n\times n system of conservation laws, where each characteristic field is either genuinely nonlinear or linearly degenerate. In this standard setting, it is well known that there exists a Lipschitz semigroup of weak solutions, defined on a domain of functions with small total variation. If the system admits a strictly convex entropy, we give a short proof that every entropy weak solution taking values within the domain of the semigroup coincides with a semigroup trajectory. The result shows that the assumptions of “Tame Variation” or “Tame Oscillation”, previously used to achieve uniqueness, can be removed in the presence of a strictly convex entropy.



1
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for a strictly hyperbolic
n\times n
system of conservation laws in one space dimension:

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~0,

(1.1)

u(0,x)~=~\bar{u}(x).

(1.2)

As usual,
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the flux, defined on some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We assume that each characteristic family is either genuinely nonlinear or linearly degenerate.
In this setting, it is well known that there exists a Lipschitz continuous semigroup
S:{\cal D}\times[0,+\infty[\,\mapsto{\cal D}
of entropy weak solutions, defined on a domain
{\cal D}\subset{\bf L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}};\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
containing all functions
with sufficiently small total variation
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
20
,
23
]
. The trajectories of this
semigroup are the unique limits of front tracking approximations, and also of Glimm
approximations
[
7
]
and of
vanishing viscosity approximations
[
6
]
.
We recall that the semigroup is globally Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the
{\bf L}^{1}
distance. Namely,
there exists a constant
L
such that

\bigl\|S_{t}\bar{u}-S_{s}\bar{v}\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~L\Big(|t-s|+\|\bar{u}-\bar{v}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\Big)\qquad\hbox{for all}~s,t\geq 0,~~\bar{u},\bar{v}\in{\cal D}.

(1.3)

Given any weak solution
u=u(t,x)
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
various conditions have been derived in
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
which guarantee
the identity

u(t)~=~S_{t}\bar{u}\qquad\hbox{for all}~t\geq 0.

(1.4)

Since the semigroup
S
is unique, the identity
(
1.4
) yields the uniqueness of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
In addition to the standard assumptions, earlier results required some additional
regularity conditions, such as “Tame Variation” or “Tame Oscillation”, controlling
the behavior of the solution near a point where the variation is locally small.

Aim of the present note is to show that, if the
n\times n
system (
1.1
) is endowed with a strictly convex entropy
\eta(\cdot)
, then every entropy-weak solution
t\mapsto u(t)

taking values within the domain
{\cal D}

of the semigroup satisfies (
1.4
). In other words, uniqueness is guaranteed without
any further regularity assumption.

As in
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
,
the proof relies on the elementary error estimate

\bigl\|u(t)-S_{t}\bar{u}\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~L\cdot\int_{0}^{t}\liminf_{h\to 0+}\,{\bigl\|u(\tau+h)-S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\over h}\,d\tau\,.

(1.5)

Assuming that the system is endowed with a strictly convex entropy,
we will prove that the integrand is zero for a.e. time
\tau\geq 0
.
Following an argument introduced in
[
7
]
, this is achieved by two estimates:

(i)

In a neighborhood of a point
(\tau,y)
where
u(\tau,\cdot)
has a large jump,
the weak solution
u
is compared with the solution to a Riemann problem.

(ii)

In a region where the total variation is small,
the weak solution
u
is compared with the solution to a linear system with constant
coefficients.

The main difference is that here we estimate the lim-inf in (
1.5
) only at times

\tau
which are Lebesgue points for a countable family of total variation functions

W^{\xi,\zeta}(\cdot)
, defined at (
3.5
).

To precisely state the result, we begin by collecting the main assumptions.

(A1)

(Conservation equations)

The function
u=u(t,x)

is a weak solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) taking values within the domain of the semigroup.

More precisely,
u:[0,T]\mapsto{\cal D}
is continuous w.r.t. the
{\bf L}^{1}
distance.
The identity
u(0,\cdot)=\bar{u}
holds in
{\bf L}^{1}
, and moreover

\int\!\!\int\big(u\varphi_{t}+f(u)\varphi_{x}\big)~dxdt=0

(1.6)

for every
{\cal C}^{1}
function
\varphi
with compact support contained
inside the open strip
\,]0,T[\,\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Regarding the entropy conditions,
we assume that the system (
1.1
)
admits
a
{\cal C}^{2}
entropy function
\eta:\Omega\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
with entropy flux
q
,
so that the equality
\nabla q(\omega)=\nabla\eta(\omega)Df(\omega)
holds for all
\omega\in\Omega
. We also assume that the entropy
\eta
satisfies the strict convexity condition

\eta(\omega)~\geq~\eta(\overline{\omega})+\nabla\eta(\overline{\omega})\cdot(\omega-\overline{\omega})+c_{0}|\omega-\overline{\omega}|^{2},

(1.7)

for some
c_{0}>0
and every couple of states
\omega,\;\overline{\omega}\in\Omega
.
As usual, we say that a weak solution
u
is entropy-admissible if it satisfies:

(A2)

(Entropy admissibility condition)

For every
{\cal C}^{1}
function
\varphi\geq 0
with compact support contained
inside the open strip
\,]0,T[\,\times{\mathbb{R}}
, one has

\int\!\!\int\big(\eta(u)\varphi_{t}+q(u)\varphi_{x}\big)~dxdt~\geq~0.

(1.8)

Our result can be simply stated as:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let (
1.1
) be a strictly hyperbolic
n\times n
system, where
each characteristic field is either genuinely nonlinear or linearly degenerate, and which
admits a strictly convex entropy
\eta(\cdot)
as in (
1.7
).
Then every entropy-weak solution
u:[0,T]\mapsto{\cal D}
, taking values within the
domain of the semigroup, coincides with a semigroup trajectory.

The theorem will be proved in Section
3
. We remark that, restricted to a class of

2\times 2
systems, a more elaborate
proof of this result was recently given in
[
19
]
.

In our view, the main interest in the above uniqueness theorem is that it opens the door to the
study of uniform convergence rates for a very wide class of approximation algorithms.
This will be better explained in the concluding remarks, in Section
4
.

2
Preliminary lemmas

Let
M
be an upper bound on the total variation of all functions in the domain

\mathcal{D}
of the semigroup:

\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\left\{u;\mathbb{R}\right\}\leq M,\quad\text{ for all }u\in\mathcal{D}.

(2.1)

Since by assumption our solution
u(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{D}
,
for sake of definiteness we shall assume that it is right continuous, namely

u(t,x)=\lim_{y\to x+}u(t,y)
.
By
[
20
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, we have
the Lipschitz bound

\bigl\|u(t_{2},\cdot)-u(t_{1},\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}~\leq~C_{M}\,(t_{2}-t_{1})\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\,,

(2.2)

for some constant
C_{M}>0
depending only on
M
and on the flux

f
.

We begin by reviewing the well known fact that the
entropy has finite propagation speed.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u=u(t,x)
be a function satisfying
(A1)
and
(A2)
.
Then there exists two constants
\widehat{C},\;\hat{\lambda}>0
such
that the following holds. For any constant state
u^{*}\in\Omega
, any

a<b
, and any
0\leq\tau<\tau^{\prime}
with

2\hat{\lambda}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)<b-a
,
one has

\int_{a+\hat{\lambda}\left(\tau^{\prime}-\tau\right)}^{b-\hat{\lambda}\left(\tau^{\prime}-\tau\right)}\left|u(\tau^{\prime},x)-u^{*}\right|^{2}\;dx~\leq~\widehat{C}\int_{a}^{b}\left|u(\tau,x)-u^{*}\right|^{2}\;dx.

(2.3)

Proof.

Given the constant state
u^{*}\in\Omega
, for all
\omega\in\Omega

define the relative entropy

\eta\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)
and the corresponding entropy flux

q\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)
as

\begin{array}[]{rl}\eta\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)&=~\eta\left(\omega\right)-\eta\left(u^{*}\right)-\nabla\eta\left(u^{*}\right)\left(\omega-u^{*}\right),\\[5.69054pt]
q\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)&=~q\left(\omega\right)-q\left(u^{*}\right)-\nabla\eta\left(u^{*}\right)\bigl(f\left(\omega\right)-f\left(u^{*}\right)\bigr).\end{array}

(2.4)

The equations (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) yield

\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)_{t}+q\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)_{x}~\leq~0,

(2.5)

while (
1.7
) implies

\eta\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)~\geq~c_{0}\left|\omega-u^{*}\right|^{2},\qquad\text{ for all }~\omega,u^{*}\in\Omega.

(2.6)

By the
{\cal C}^{2}
regularity of the functions
\eta,q
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}

such that

\eta\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)~\leq~C^{\prime}\left|\omega-u^{*}\right|^{2},\qquad q\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)~\leq~C^{\prime}\left|\omega-u^{*}\right|^{2},\quad\text{ for all }~\omega,u^{*}\in\Omega.

(2.7)

In view of (
2.6
), there exists a sufficiently large constant

\hat{\lambda}>0
such that

-\hat{\lambda}\eta\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)\pm q\left(\omega\mid u^{*}\right)~\leq~0,\qquad\text{ for all }~\omega,u^{*}\in\Omega.

(2.8)

Using (
2.5
), in connection with
test functions that approximate the
characteristic function of the trapezoid

\Gamma~=~\bigl\{(t,x)\,;~\tau<t<\tau^{\prime},~~a+\hat{\lambda}(t-\tau)<x<b-\hat{\lambda}(t-\tau)\bigr\},

and
recalling (
2.8
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{a+\hat{\lambda}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}^{b-\hat{\lambda}(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)}\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)\left(\tau^{\prime},x\right)\;dx&~\leq~\int_{a}^{b}\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)\left(\tau,x\right)\;dx\\
~&\quad+\int_{\tau}^{\tau^{\prime}}\left(-\hat{\lambda}\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)+q\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)\right)\left(t,a+\hat{\lambda}\left(t-\tau\right)\right)\;dt\\
&\quad+\int_{\tau}^{\tau^{\prime}}\left(-\hat{\lambda}\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)-q\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)\right)\left(t,b-\hat{\lambda}\left(t-\tau\right)\right)\;dt\\
&~\leq~\int_{a}^{b}\eta\left(u\mid u^{*}\right)\left(\tau,x\right)\;dx.\end{split}

Together with (
2.6
)-(
2.7
), this proves the lemma.

Throughout the following, without loss of generality we shall always assume
\hat{\lambda}=1
.
We observe that this can always be achieved by a suitable rescaling of the time variable:

\tilde{t}~=~\kappa t.

Similarly, to simplify notation, we also assume that all wave speeds lie in the interval
[-1,1]
.

Given any
\tau\geq 0
and any bounded interval
]a,b[
with
-\infty\leq a<b\leq+\infty
, we consider the open intervals

J(t)~=~\bigl]a+{}(t-\tau)\,,~b-{}(t-\tau)\bigr[\,,\qquad\qquad\tau\leq t<\tau+\frac{b-a}{2}\,.

(2.9)

Toward the proof of Theorem
1.1
, in order to replace the “Tame Variation” condition,
the main tool is provided by the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, for some constant
C>0
the following holds.
Let
u=u(t,x)
be any entropy weak solution to (
1.1
). Then

\int_{J(t)}\bigl|u(t,x)-u(\tau,x)\bigr|\,dx~\leq~C(t-\tau)\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~]a,b[\bigr\}.

(2.10)

The covering of the interval

Proof.

1.

We first consider the case where
-\infty<a<b<+\infty
.
For notational simplicity, w.l.o.g. we assume that
\tau=0
. Given a time

, as shown in Fig.
1

x_{k}

For

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{k}\bigr|^{2}\,dx&\leq\displaystyle~\widehat{C}\,\int_{x_{k-1}}^{x_{k+2}}\bigl|u(0,x)-u_{k}\bigr|^{2}\,dx\\[11.38109pt]

(2.12)

\int_{x_{N-1}}^{b-t}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{N-1}\bigr|^{2}\,dx~\leq\displaystyle~\widehat{C}\cdot 3t\cdot\Big(\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]x_{N-2},b[\bigr\}\Big)^{2}.

(2.13)

2.
Define the piecewise constant approximation
\bar{u}:[a+t,b-t[\,\mapsto\Omega

Using Cauchy’s inequality and the bounds (

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{a+t}^{b-t}\bigl|u(t,x)-\bar{u}(x)\bigl|\,dx~=~\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{k}\bigr|\,dx+\int_{x_{N-1}}^{b-t}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{N-1}\bigr|\,dx\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad\displaystyle\leq~\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}\sqrt{t}\left(\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{k-1}\bigr|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\sqrt{t}\left(\int_{x_{N-1}}^{b-t}\bigl|u(t,x)-u_{N-1}\bigr|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad\displaystyle\leq~\sqrt{t}\cdot\sqrt{\widehat{C}\cdot 3t}\cdot\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]x_{k-1},x_{k+2}[\bigr\}+\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]x_{N-2},b[\bigr\}\right).\end{array}

(2.15)

Observing that every point

is contained in at most three

]x_{k-1},x_{k+2}[\,
, from (

) we conclude

\int_{a+t}^{b-t}\bigl|u(t,x)-\bar{u}(x)\bigl|\,dx~\leq~\sqrt{3\widehat{C}}\cdot 3t\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]a,b[\bigr\}.

(2.16)

3.
Next, we compute

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{a+t}^{b-t}\bigl|u(0,x)-\bar{u}(x)\bigl|\,dx~=\displaystyle~\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\bigl|u(0,x)-u_{k}\bigr|\,dx+\int_{x_{N-1}}^{b-t}\bigl|u(0,x)-u_{N-1}\bigr|\,dx\\[11.38109pt]
\quad\leq\displaystyle~\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}t\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]x_{k},x_{k+1}[\,\bigr\}+t\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]x_{N-1},b-t[\,\bigr\}\\[11.38109pt]
\quad\displaystyle\leq~t\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(0,\cdot)\,;~]a,b[\bigr\}.\end{array}

(2.17)

Combining (
2.16
) with (
2.17
) we obtain a proof of the

Letting
a\to-\infty
or

the same conclusion remains valid also for unbounded intervals,
such as
]-\infty,b[\,
or

3
Proof of the theorem

We are now ready to give a
proof of Theorem
1.1
, in several steps.

1.
By the structure theorem for BV functions
[
1
,
22
]
, there is a null set of times

{\cal N}\subset[0,T]
such that the following holds.

Every point
(\tau,\xi)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
with
\tau\notin{\cal N}
is either a point of approximate continuity, or a point of approximate jump of the function
u
.
In this second case, there exists states

u^{-},u^{+}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a speed
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that, calling

U(t,x)~\doteq~\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}u^{-}&\hbox{if}\qquad\left(x-\xi\right)<\lambda\left(t-\tau\right),\\[5.69054pt]
u^{+}&\hbox{if}\qquad\left(x-\xi\right)>\lambda\left(t-\tau\right),\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

there holds

\lim_{r\to 0+}~{1\over r^{2}}\int_{-r}^{r}\int_{-r}^{r}\Big|u(\tau+t,~\xi+x)-U(\tau+t,\xi+x)\Big|~dxdt~=~0.

(3.2)

The conservation equations (
1.6
) imply that
the piecewise constant function
U
must be a
weak solution to the system of conservation laws (see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
),
satisfying the Rankine-Hugoniot equations:

f(u^{+})-f(u^{-})~=~\lambda(u^{+}-u^{-}).

(3.3)

Moreover, the entropy condition (
1.8
) implies

q(u^{+})-q(u^{-})~\leq~\lambda\bigl(\eta(u^{+})-\eta(u^{-})\bigr).

(3.4)

Next, we observe that, for every couple of rational points
\xi,\zeta\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, the
scalar function

W^{\xi,\zeta}(t)~\doteq~\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\big\{u(t)\,;~]\xi+t\,,~\zeta-t[\,\bigr\}&\hbox{if}\quad\xi+t<\zeta-t\,,\\[5.69054pt]
0&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

is bounded and measurable (indeed, it is lower semicontinuous). Therefore a.e.
t\in[0,T]
is a Lebesgue point.
We denote by
{\cal N}^{\prime}\subset[0,T]
the set of all times
t
which are NOT
Lebesgue for at least one of the countably many functions
W^{\xi,\zeta}
.
Of course,
{\cal N}^{\prime}
has zero Lebesgue measure.

In view of (
1.5
), we will prove the theorem by establishing the following claim.

(C)

For every
\tau\in[0,T]\setminus({\cal N\cup\cal N^{\prime}})
and
\varepsilon>0
,
one has

\limsup_{h\to 0+}{1\over h}\Big\|u(\tau+h)-S_{h}u(\tau)\Big\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~\varepsilon.

(3.6)

2.
Assume
\tau\notin\cal N\cup\cal N^{\prime}
.
Since
u(\tau,\cdot)
has bounded variation,
we define points

-\infty=y_{-1}<y_{0}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{N}<y_{N+1}=+\infty,

y_{k+1}~=~\sup~\Big\{x>y_{k}\,;~~\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~~]y_{k},x[\,\bigr\}\leq\varepsilon\Big\}.

Since
u(\tau,\cdot)
is right continuous, we have

\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~]y_{k-1},y_{k}[\,\bigr\}~\leq~\varepsilon,&\text{ for }k=0,\ldots,N+1,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~]y_{k-1},y_{k}]\,\bigr\}~\geq~\varepsilon,&\text{ for }k=0,\ldots,N,\\
N~\leq~{M\over\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

(3.7)

Where
M
is an upper bound for the total variation of all functions
\bar{u}\in{\cal D}
, as in
(
2.1
).

Then we choose points
y_{k}^{\prime},y_{k}^{\prime\prime}
such that

-\infty<y_{0}<y_{0}^{\prime\prime}~\leq~y_{1}^{\prime}<y_{1}<y_{1}^{\prime\prime}~\leq~y_{2}^{\prime}<y_{2}<y_{2}^{\prime\prime}~<~\cdots~\leq~y_{N}^{\prime}<y_{N}<+\infty

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~]y^{\prime}_{k},y_{k}[\,\bigr\}\,\leq\,\varepsilon^{2},\quad k=1,\ldots N,\\[8.53581pt]
\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(\tau,\cdot)\,;~]y_{k},y^{\prime\prime}_{k}[\,\bigr\}\leq\,\varepsilon^{2},\,\quad k=0,\ldots N-1,\\[8.53581pt]
\text{ all values $y_{k}^{\prime}-\tau$, $y^{\prime\prime}_{k}-\tau$ are rational}.\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

(I)

The integral of

(III)

The integral of
\bigl|u(t,x)-u(\tau,x)\bigr|
over the intervals

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}J_{k}^{\prime}(t)&=~\bigl]y_{k}^{\prime}+(t-\tau)\,,~y_{k}-(t-\tau)\bigr[,\quad k=1,\ldots,N,\\[5.69054pt]
J^{\prime\prime}_{k}(t)&=~\bigl]y_{k}+(t-\tau)\,,~y^{\prime\prime}_{k}-(t-\tau)\bigr[,\quad k=0,\ldots,N-1,\\[5.69054pt]
J_{0}(t)&\dot{=}~\left]-\infty,y_{0}-\left(t-\tau\right)\right[,\\[5.69054pt]
J_{N+1}&\dot{=}~\left]y_{N}+\left(t-\tau\right),+\infty\right[.\end{array}\right.

4.
To estimate integrals of type (I), assuming that
(\tau,y)
is either
a Lebesgue point or a point of approximate jump of the
function
u
, we obtain

\lim_{h\to 0+}{1\over h}\int_{y-h}^{y+h}\Big|u(\tau+h,~x)-U^{\sharp}_{(u;\tau,y)}(\tau+h,x)\Big|~dx~=~0.

(3.12)

Indeed, by
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
, setting

u^{\pm}=u\left(\tau,y\pm\right)
, the function
U

defined in (
3.1
) satisfies (
3.2
) and consequently
u^{\pm}

satisfy (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). It implies that

u\left(\tau,y\pm\right)
are connected by a single entropic shock whose
speed is
\lambda
. Consequently
U^{\sharp}_{\left(u;\tau,y\right)}
coincides with
the piecewise constant
function
U
defined
in (
3.1
) so that (
3.12
) follows
from
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
.

5.
We now estimate the integrals of type (II).
By construction, both values
y_{k-1}^{\prime\prime}-\tau
and
y^{\prime}_{k}+\tau
are rational. Hence

y_{k-1}^{\prime\prime}\,=\,\xi+\tau,\qquad\qquad y_{k}^{\prime}\,=\,\zeta-\tau,

for some
\xi,\zeta\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.
This implies that
\tau
is a Lebesgue point of the map

t~\mapsto~V(t)~=~W^{\xi,\zeta}(t).

(3.13)

Let
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\lambda_{i}\left(\tilde{u}_{k}\right)
,
\tilde{l}_{i}=l_{i}\left(\tilde{u}_{k}\right)
,

\tilde{r}_{i}=r_{i}\left(\tilde{u}_{k}\right)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, be
respectively the
i
-th eigenvalues and left and right eigenvectors of
the matrix
A\doteq Df\big(\widetilde{u}_{k}\big)
.
We thus have

\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot U^{\flat}(t,x)~=~\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot U^{\flat}\big(\tau,x-(t-\tau)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\big)~=~\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot u\big(\tau,x-(t-\tau)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\big).

Following the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 9.4]
, fix any two points

\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}\in J_{k}(t)
,
\zeta^{\prime}<\zeta^{\prime\prime}
and consider the quantity

\begin{array}[]{rl}E_{i}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})&\displaystyle\doteq~\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot\int_{\zeta^{\prime}}^{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}\big(u(t,x)-U^{\flat}(t,x)\big)~dx\\[8.53581pt]
&=~\displaystyle\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot\int_{\zeta^{\prime}}^{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}\Big(u(t,x)-u\big(\tau,~x-(t-\tau)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\big)\Big)~dx.\end{array}

(3.14)

Figure 3:
The domain
D_{i}
considered at (
3.15
).

We apply the divergence theorem to the vector

\big(u,\,f(u)\big)

on the domain

D_{i}~\doteq~\Big\{(s,x);~~s\in[\tau,~t],~~\zeta^{\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\leq x\leq\zeta^{\prime\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\Big\},

(3.15)

shown in Fig.
3
.
Since
u
satisfies the conservation equation (
1.1
),
the difference between the integral of
u
at the top and at the
bottom of the domain
D_{i}
is thus
measured by the inflow from the left side minus
the outflow from the right side of
D_{i}
.
From (
3.14
) it thus follows

\begin{array}[]{rl}E_{i}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})=&~\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot\Big(\big(f(u)-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}u\big)(s,~\zeta^{\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\,ds\cr&\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot\Big(\big(f(u)-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}u\big)(s,~\zeta^{\prime\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\,ds\cr=&\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}l_{i}(\tilde{u}_{k})\cdot\Big(\big(f(u^{\prime}(s))-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\left(\tilde{u}_{k}\right)u^{\prime}(s)\big)-\big(f(u^{\prime\prime}(s))-\lambda_{i}(\tilde{u}_{k})u^{\prime\prime}(s)\big)\Big)\,ds\cr=&\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}H\left(\widetilde{u}_{k},u^{\prime}(s),u^{\prime\prime}(s)\right)\,ds,\end{array}

(3.16)

where we set

u^{\prime}(s)\doteq u\big(s,~\zeta^{\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\big),\qquad u^{\prime\prime}(s)\doteq u\big(s,~\zeta^{\prime\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\big),

H\left(u,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\,\doteq\,l_{i}(u)\cdot\Big(\big(f(u_{1})-\lambda_{i}(u)u_{1}\big)-\big(f(u_{2})-\lambda_{i}(u)u_{2}\big)\Big).

Observing that

•

H\left(u,u_{2},u_{2}\right)=0
,

•

D_{u_{1}}H\left(u,u_{1},u_{2}\right)=l_{i}(u)\cdot\left(Df(u_{1})-\lambda_{i}(u)I\right)
,

•

D_{u_{1}}H\left(u,u,u_{2}\right)=l_{i}(u)\cdot\left(Df(u)-\lambda_{i}(u)I\right)=0
,

we estimate

\begin{array}[]{rl}H\left(u,u_{1},u_{2}\right)&=~H\left(u,u_{1},u_{2}\right)-H\left(u,u_{2},u_{2}\right)\\[11.38109pt]
&=~\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}D_{u_{1}}H\left(u,u_{2}+\sigma\left(u_{1}-u_{2}\right),u_{2}\right)\;d\sigma\cdot\left(u_{1}-u_{2}\right)\\[11.38109pt]
&=~\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[D_{u_{1}}H\left(u,u_{2}+\sigma\left(u_{1}-u_{2}\right),u_{2}\right)-D_{u_{1}}H\left(u,u,u_{2}\right)\right]\;d\sigma\cdot\left(u_{1}-u_{2}\right)\\[11.38109pt]
&=~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\bigl(\left|u_{1}-u\right|+\left|u_{2}-u\right|\bigr)\cdot\left|u_{1}-u_{2}\right|.\end{array}

Therefore,

E_{i}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{\tau}^{t}\left|u^{\prime}(s)-u^{\prime\prime}(s)\right|\cdot\Big(\left|u^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|+\left|u^{\prime\prime}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|\Big)\,ds.

Recalling (
3.11
) and (
3.13
), for any
x\in J_{k}(s)
we now compute

\left|u^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|\leq V(s)+\left|u\big(s,x\big)-u\left(\tau,x\right)\right|+\left|u\left(\tau,x\right)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|\leq V(s)+\left|u\big(s,x\big)-u\left(\tau,x\right)\right|+V(\tau).

Integrating w.r.t.
x
over the interval

J_{k}(s)
, dividing by its length and using (
2.2
)
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rl}\left|u^{\prime}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|&\displaystyle\leq~V(s)+V(\tau)+\frac{1}{\hbox{meas}\bigl(J_{k}(s)\bigr)}\int_{J_{k}(s)}\bigl|u(s,x)-u(\tau,x)\bigr|\,dx\\[11.38109pt]
&\displaystyle=~V(s)+\varepsilon+{\cal O}(1)\cdot\frac{C_{M}(s-\tau)}{\hbox{meas}\bigl(J_{k}(s)\bigr)}~\dot{=}~g(s).\end{array}

(3.17)

An entirely similar estimate clearly holds for
\left|u^{\prime\prime}(s)-\widetilde{u}_{k}\right|
.
Hence

\begin{array}[]{rl}E_{i}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})&\displaystyle=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{\tau}^{t}\left|u^{\prime}(s)-u^{\prime\prime}(s)\right|\cdot g(s)\,ds\\[11.38109pt]
&=~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\Big\{u(s)\,;~\bigl]\zeta^{\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i},~\zeta^{\prime\prime}-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\bigr]\Big\}\cdot g(s)\,ds\\[11.38109pt]
&=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\mu_{i}\bigl(]\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}]\bigr).\end{array}

Here
\mu_{i}
is the Borel measure defined by

\mu_{i}\bigl(]a,b[\bigr)~=~\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\Big\{u(s)\,;~\bigl]a-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i},~b-(t-s)\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\bigr[\Big\}\cdot g(s)\,ds,

for any open interval
]a,b[\,\subset J_{k}(t)
.

According to Lemma 9.3 in
[
8
]
, we now have

\begin{split}\int_{J_{k}(t)}\left|u(t,x)-U^{\flat}\left(t,x\right)\right|\;dx&=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{J_{k}(t)}\left|\tilde{l}_{i}\cdot\left(u(t,x)-U^{\flat}\left(t,x\right)\right)\right|\;dx\\
&=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\left(J_{k}(t)\right)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{\tau}^{t}V(s)\cdot g(s)\;ds.\end{split}

In turn, this implies

\frac{1}{t-\tau}\int_{J_{k}(t)}\left|u(t,x)-U^{\flat}\left(t,x\right)\right|\;dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left(\frac{\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}}{t-\tau}\int_{\tau}^{t}\left|V(s)-V(\tau)\right|\;ds+\frac{V(\tau)}{t-\tau}\int_{\tau}^{t}g(s)\;ds\right).

(3.18)

We now observe that, for all
s>\tau
sufficiently close to
\tau
,
the function
g
introduced at (
3.17
) satisfies

g(s)~\leq~V(s)+\varepsilon+{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left(t-\tau\right).

(3.19)

Since
t=\tau
is a
Lebesgue point for
V
, taking the limit of (
3.18
) as
t\to\tau+
we thus obtain

\limsup_{t\to\tau+}\frac{1}{t-\tau}\int_{J_{k}(t)}\left|u(t,x)-U^{\flat}\left(t,x\right)\right|\;dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot V(\tau)\left(V(\tau)+\varepsilon\right)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.20)

6.
Finally, regarding integrals of type (III), using Lemma
2.2

we obtain the bounds

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y^{\prime}_{k}+h}^{y_{k}-h}\Big|u(\tau+h,~x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2},

(3.21)

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y_{k}+h}^{y^{\prime\prime}_{k}-h}\Big|u(\tau+h,~x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2},

(3.22)

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}-h}\Big|u(\tau+h,~x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon,

(3.23)

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y_{N}+h}^{+\infty}\Big|u(\tau+h,~x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon.

(3.24)

7.
On the other hand, it is well known
[
7
,
8
]
that semigroup trajectories
satisfy entirely similar estimates. Indeed, at every point
y
the difference
between the semigroup solution and the solution to a Riemann problem
satisfies

\lim_{h\to 0+}{1\over h}\int_{y-h}^{y+h}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-U^{\sharp}_{(u;\tau,y)}(\tau+h,x)\Big|~dx~=~0.

(3.25)

Since the total variation of
u(\tau,\cdot)
on the open interval
]y^{\prime\prime}_{k-1},y_{k}^{\prime}[
is
\leq\varepsilon
,
we have

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y_{k-1}^{\prime\prime}+h}^{y_{k}^{\prime}-h}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-U^{\flat}_{(u,\tau,k)}(\tau+h,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2}.

(3.26)

Moreover, since the total variation of
u(\tau,\cdot)
on the open intervals

]y^{\prime}_{k},y_{k}[
and
]y_{k},y^{\prime\prime}_{k}[
is
\leq\varepsilon^{2}
,
we have

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y^{\prime}_{k}+h}^{y_{k}-h}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2},

(3.27)

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y_{k}+h}^{y^{\prime\prime}_{k}-h}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon^{2},

(3.28)

and similarly

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{-\infty}^{y_{0}-h}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon,

(3.29)

\limsup_{h\to 0}~{1\over h}\int_{y_{N}+h}^{\infty}\Big|\bigl(S_{h}u(\tau)\bigr)(x)-u(\tau,x)\Big|~dx~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\varepsilon.

(3.30)

8.
Combining all the previous estimates, and recalling that the total number of
intervals is
N\leq M\varepsilon^{-1}
, we establish the limit (
3.6
), proving the theorem.

4
Concluding remarks

The present analysis opens the door to the study of
convergence and a posteriori error estimates
for a wide variety of approximation schemes.

Following
[
9
]
, we say that
u=u(t,x)
is an
\varepsilon
-approximate solution to (
1.1
) if,
given the time step
\varepsilon=\Delta t
, the following holds.

(AL)

Approximate Lipschitz continuity.
For every
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\geq 0

one has

\|u(\tau,\cdot)-u(\tau^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~M\,\bigl(|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|+\varepsilon\bigr)\cdot\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}.

(P
ε
)

Approximate conservation law, and approximate entropy inequality.

For every strip
[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]\times{\mathbb{R}}
with
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{N}}
, and every test function
\varphi\in{\cal C}^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, there holds

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\left|\int u(\tau,x)\varphi(\tau,x)\,dx-\int u(\tau^{\prime},x)\varphi(\tau^{\prime},x)\,dx+\int_{\tau}^{\tau^{\prime}}\!\!\int\bigl\{u\varphi_{t}+f(u)\varphi_{x}\bigr\}\,dx\,dt\right|\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad\qquad\displaystyle\leq~C\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\cdot(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\cdot\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}.\end{array}

(4.1)

Moreover, given a uniformly convex entropy
\eta
with flux
q
, assuming
\varphi\geq 0

one has the entropy inequality

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int\eta(u(\tau,x))\varphi(\tau,x)\,dx-\int\eta(u(\tau^{\prime},x))\varphi(\tau^{\prime},x)\,dx+\int_{\tau}^{\tau^{\prime}}\!\!\int\bigl\{\eta(u)\varphi_{t}+q(u)\varphi_{x}\bigr\}\,dxdt\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad\qquad\displaystyle\geq~-C\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\cdot(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\cdot\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]}\mathop{\mathrm{Tot.Var.}}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}.\end{array}

(4.2)

In the above setting, the recent paper
[
9
]
has established
a posteriori

error estimates,
assuming that the total variation of
u(t,\cdot)
remains small, so that
u(t,\cdot)
remains
inside the domain of the semigroup. However, the estimates in
[
9
]
also required a
“post processing algorithm”, tracing the location of the large shocks in the approximate solution.
We would like to achieve error estimates based solely on an a posteriori
bound of the total variation.
The possibility of such estimates is the content of the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let (
1.1
) be an
n\times n
strictly hyperbolic system, generating
a Lipschitz semigroup of entropy-weak solutions on a domain
{\cal D}
of functions with small total variation.
Then, given
T,R>0
, there exists a function
\varepsilon\mapsto\varrho(\varepsilon)

with the following properties.

(i)

\varrho
is continuous, nondecreasing, with
\varrho(0)=0
.

(ii)

Let
t\mapsto u(t)\in{\cal D}
be an
\varepsilon
-approximate solution to
(
1.1
), satisfying
(AL)-(P
ε
)
and supported inside the interval
[-R,R]
.
Then, calling
\bar{u}=u(0)
, one has

\bigl\|u(t)-S_{t}\bar{u}\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~\varrho(\varepsilon)\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~t\in[0,T].

(4.3)

Proof.
If the conclusion fails, there exists a sequence of
\varepsilon_{n}
-approximate solutions
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, all supported inside
[-R,R]
, with
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
but

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}~\bigl\|u_{n}(t)-S_{t}u_{n}(0)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\geq~\delta_{0}~>~0\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~n\geq 1.

(4.4)

By compactness, taking a subsequence we achieve the
{\bf L}^{1}
-convergence

u_{n}(t)\to u(t)
, uniformly for
t\in[0,T]
.
Setting
\bar{u}(x)\doteq u(0,x)
, the limit function
u
is thus an entropy weak
solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), distinct from the semigroup trajectory
S_{t}\bar{u}
. This contradicts the uniqueness stated in
Theorem
1.1
.

MM

We regard the function
\varrho(\cdot)
as a
universal convergence rate

for approximate BV solutions
to the hyperbolic system (
1.1
).
Having proved the existence of such a function, the major open problem is now to
provide an asymptotic estimate on
\varrho(\varepsilon)
,
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
In some sense,
starting from a uniqueness theorem and deriving a uniform convergence rate
is a task analogous to the derivation of quantitative compactness estimates
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
21
]
.
Based on the convergence estimates already available
for the Glimm scheme
[
5
,
17
]

and for vanishing viscosity approximations
[
13
,
18
]
,
one might guess that

\varrho(\varepsilon)\approx\sqrt{\varepsilon}|\ln\varepsilon|
.
We leave this as an open question for future investigation.

The value distribution of the Gauss maps of minimal surfaces was initially studied by Osserman in a series of his papers

, M. Ru

and others. In 1988, H. Fujimoto
[

showed that the Gauss map of a complete non-flat minimal surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
can omit at most four points, and this number four is the best possible number. For the case of higher dimension, in 1991, based on an earlier result of H. Fujimoto, M. Ru
[
20

showed that the generalized Gauss map of a complete non-flat minimal surface
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
cannot omit more than
\frac{m(m+1)}{2}
hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position. In the same year, S. J. Kao
[
12
]
extended H. Fujimoto’s method and showed that the Gauss map of an end of a non-flat complete minimal surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
that is conformally an annulus
\{z;0<1/r<|z|<r\}
must also assume every value, with at most four exceptions. Later on, L. Jin and M. Ru in
[
21
]
extended the result of Kao to the case of higher dimension by showing that the generalized Gauss map on an annular end of a complete minimal surface
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
cannot omit more than
\frac{m(m+1)}{2}
hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}({\mathbb{C}})

[

have improved the result of L. Jin and M. Ru by considering the ramification of the generalized Gauss map on an annular end over a family of hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in subgeneral position.

In this paper, we will give a modified defect relation for the generalized Gauss map on an annular end of a complete minimal surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
into a projective subvariety

for hypersurfaces of

. Our result extends and generalizes all above mentioned results. In order to state the main result of this paper, we will introduce the notion of modified defect for hypersurfaces in a projective subvariety

from an open Riemann surface into

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
introduced by H. Fujimoto

,

,

), as follows.

Let
S
be an open complete Riemann surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. By a divisor on
S
we mean a map
\nu
of
S
into
\mathbb{Z}
whose support
\mathrm{Supp}\,(\nu):=\{p\in S;\nu(p)\neq 0\}
has no accumulation points in

. Let
A
be an annular end on

and let
f
be a holomorphic curve from
A
to a
k
-dimension projective subvariety
V
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and let
Q
be a hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of degree
d
. By
\nu_{Q(f)}
we denote the pull-back of the divisor
Q
by
f
. Let
F=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
be a global reduced representation of
f
on
A
. Assume that, the hypersurface
Q
has a defining polynomial, denoted again by the same notation
Q
(throughout this paper) if there is no confusion, as follows

Q(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}x^{I},

where
\mathcal{T}_{d}=\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{+};i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=d\}
,

are not all zero for

and

for each

. We set

Q(F)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}f^{I},

where
f^{I}=f_{0}^{i_{0}}\ldots f_{n}^{i_{n}}
for each

. Throughout this paper, for each given hypersurface

we assume that

. The

for

\delta^{H,m}_{f,A}(Q):=1-\mathrm{inf}\{\eta;\eta\text{ satisfies Condition }(*)_{H}\}.

Here, Condition
(*)_{H}

-valued continuous subharmonic function

on

and satisfying conditions:

(D_{1})

e^{u}\leq\|F\|^{d\eta}

(D_{2})

for each

\underset{z\rightarrow\xi}{\lim}(u(z)-\min(\nu_{Q(f)}(z),m)\log|z-\xi|)\in[-\infty,\infty).

Denote by

the ideal of homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},...,x_{n}]

the vector space of all homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},...,x_{n}]

I_{d}(V):=\dfrac{H_{d}}{I(V)\cap H_{d}}\text{ and }H_{V}(d):=\dim I_{d}(V).

For an element

we denote by

If

For

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an annular end of a non-flat complete minimal surface
S
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Let
V
be a
k
-dimension projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})\ (n=m-1)
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be hypersurfaces of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
and
d
the least common multiple of
\deg Q_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq q).
Assume that the generalized Gauss map
g:S\rightarrow V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{H,M}_{g,A}(Q_{j})\leq\dfrac{(2N-k+1)(M+1)}{k+1}+\dfrac{(2N-k+1)M(M+1)}{2d(k+1)},

where
M=H_{V}(d)-1
.

From Theorem
1.1
we immediately have the following corollary about the ramification of
f
over a family of hypersurfaces on an annular end.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
S,A,V,\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q},d,M
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Assume that the generalized Gauss map
g:S\rightarrow V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
and ramified over
Q_{j}
on
A
with multiplicity at least
m_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq q)
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left(1-\frac{M}{m_{j}}\right)\leq\dfrac{(2N-k+1)(M+1)}{k+1}+\dfrac{(2N-k+1)M(M+1)}{2d(k+1)}.

In particular,
g
cannot omit
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
on
A
if
q>\frac{(2N-k+1)(M+1)}{k+1}+\frac{(2N-k+1)M(M+1)}{2d(k+1)}.

Proof.

Indeed, since
g
is ramified over
Q_{j}
with multiplicity at least
m_{j}
on
A
for each
j
, we have

\delta^{H,M}_{g,S}(Q_{j})\geq 1-\dfrac{M}{m_{j}}\ \ (1\leq j\leq q).

Then by Theorem
1.1
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left(1-\frac{M}{m_{j}}\right)\leq\dfrac{(2N-k+1)(M+1)}{k+1}+\dfrac{(2N-k+1)M(M+1)}{2d(k+1)}.

The corollary is proved.
∎

Let

The Ricci of

The continuous pseudo-metric

P=\{X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M+1};E\wedge X=0\}

and set

\phi_{p}(P)=\underset{R\in\mathcal{R}_{p}}{\max}\min\left\{\dfrac{\left|E\vee a_{j}\right|^{2}}{|E|^{2}};\ j\not\in R\right\}.

Then
\phi_{p}(P)
depends only on

and may be regarded as a function on the Grassmannian manifold
G(M,p)
. For each nonzero
(k+1)
-vector

we set

R=\{j\in Q;\ E\vee a_{j}=0\}.

Note that,
E\vee a_{j}=0
if and only if
a_{j}
is contained in the orthogonal complement of the vector space
\mathrm{Span}(E_{0},\ldots,E_{k})
. Then we see

\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{a_{j};\ j\in R\}=\dim\mathrm{Span}(a_{j};\ j\in R)\leq M-p,

namely,
R\in\mathcal{R}_{p}
. Hence
\phi_{p}
is positive everywhere on
G(M,p)
.
Since
\phi_{p}
is obviously continuous and
G(M,p)
is compact, we can take a positive constant
\delta
such that
\phi_{p}(P)>\delta
for each
P\in G(M,p)
.

Take a point

with
F_{\mathcal{V},p}(z)\neq 0
. The vector space generated
by
F^{(0)}_{\mathcal{V}}(z),F^{(1)}_{\mathcal{V}}(z)
,…,
F^{(p)}_{\mathcal{V}}(z)
determines a point in
G(M,p)
. Therefore, there is a set
R
in
\mathcal{R}_{p}
with

\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{a_{j};j\in R\}\leq M-p

such that
\varphi_{\mathcal{V},p}(Q_{j})(z)\geq\delta
for all
j\not\in R
. Then, we can choose a finite positive constant

depending only on
\mathcal{V},Q_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq q)
such that

for all

. Set

We distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1:
Assume that
T
is an empty set. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q}\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}^{\omega_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sum_{j}\omega_{j}}}

\displaystyle\geq(p+1)K\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(\dfrac{\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}}{K}\right)^{\frac{\omega_{j}}{M-p}}\right)^{\frac{M-p}{\sum_{j}\omega_{j}}}

\displaystyle\geq(p+1)K\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}}{K}\right)^{\omega_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{M-p}},

because
\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}=\tilde{\omega}(q-2N+k-1)+k+1\geq\dfrac{q(k+1)}{2N-k+1}\geq M+1>M-p
.

Case 2:
Assume that
T\neq\emptyset
. We see that
T\subset R
and so
\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{a_{j};\ j\in T\}\leq M-p
holds. Since the intersection of
V
and arbitrary
N+1
hyperplanes
Q_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq q)
is empty, there are at most
N
indexes
j
satisfying
\langle F^{(0)}_{\mathcal{V}}(z),a_{j}\rangle=0
(i.e.,
Q_{j}(F)(z)=0
). It yields that
\sharp T<N+1
, and hence
l\leq\mathrm{rank}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{a_{j};\ j\in T\}\leq M-p
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in T}\omega_{j}\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}\geq Kl\prod_{j\in T}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}}{K}\right)^{\frac{\omega_{j}}{l}}

\displaystyle\geq Kl\prod_{j\in T}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}}{K}\right)^{\omega_{j}/(M-p)}\geq C\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathcal{V},jp}}{K}\right)^{\omega_{j}/(M-p)},

for some positive constant
C>0
, which depends only on
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
.

From the above two cases, we get the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.7

3.

d\tau^{2}=\left(\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|Q_{j}(G)\|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}|^{\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{2\rho^{*}}|dz|^{2}.

Here and throughout this section, for simplicity we will write
\prod_{j,p}
for
\prod_{j=1}^{q}\prod_{p=1}^{M-1}
.

Since
Q_{j}(G),G_{\mathcal{V},M},\psi(G)_{jp}
are all holomorphic functions and
u_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq q)
are harmonic functions on
A_{1}
,
d\tau
is flat on
A_{1}
.

Claim
3.3
.

d\tau
is complete on the set
\{z;\ |z|=r\}\cup\{z|\psi(G)_{jp}(z)=0\}\cup\{z|\ Q_{j}(G)(z)=0\}
, i.e., set
\{z;\ |z|=r\}\cup\{z|\ \psi(G)_{jp}(z)=0\}\cup\{z|\ Q_{j}(G)(z)=0\}
is at infinite distance from any interior point.

Indeed, if
a\in A
such that
\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(Q_{j}(G)(a)\cdot\prod_{p=0}^{M}\psi(G)_{jp}(a)\right)=0
then by Theorem
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{d\tau}(a)

\displaystyle\leq-\biggl[\nu_{G_{\mathcal{V},M}}(a)-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}

\displaystyle\ \ +\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}\nu_{e^{u_{j}|z-z_{0}|^{-\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}}}}(a)+\epsilon\nu_{G_{\mathcal{V},M}}(a)

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(\dfrac{\epsilon}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\big(\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}+\sum_{p=0}^{M-1}\nu_{\psi(G)_{jp}}(a)\big)\right)\biggl]\rho^{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon\rho^{*}\nu_{G_{\mathcal{V},M}}(a)-\dfrac{\epsilon\rho^{*}}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\big(\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}+\sum_{p=0}^{M-1}\nu_{\psi(G)_{jp}}(a)\big)\leq-\dfrac{\epsilon\rho^{*}}{q}

(since
\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}>1
on
\mathrm{Supp}\,\min\{\nu_{Q_{j}(G)}(a),M\}
). Then, there is a positive constant
C
such that

|d\tau|\geq\dfrac{C}{|z-z(a)|^{\frac{\epsilon\rho^{*}}{q}}}|dz|

in a neighborhood of
a
, where
\frac{\epsilon\rho^{*}}{q}>1
. Then
d\tau
is complete on the set

\{z|\psi(G)_{jp}(z)=0\}\cup\{z|\ Q_{j}(G)(z)=0\}.

Now suppose that
d\tau^{2}
is not complete on the set
\{z;\ |z|=r\}
. Then, there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1)\rightarrow A_{1}
, where
\gamma(1)\in\{z;\ |z|=r\}
so that the length of
\gamma
in the metric
d\tau
satisfying
l_{d\tau}(\gamma)<\infty
. Furthermore, we may also assume that
dist(\gamma(0),\{z;\ |z|=1/r\})>3l_{d\tau}(\gamma)
.

Take a disk
\Delta
(in the metric induced by
d\tau
) with centered at
\gamma(0)
. Since
d\tau
is flat, by Lemma
3.1
,
\Delta
is isometric to an ordinary disk in the plane. Let
\Phi:\Delta(r)=\{|\omega|<r\}\rightarrow\Delta
be this isometric with
\Phi(0)=\gamma(0)
. Extend
\Phi
as a local isometric into
A_{1}
to a the largest disk possible
\Delta(R)=\{|\omega|<R\}
, and denoted again by
\Phi
this extension (for simplicity, we may consider
\Phi
as the exponential map). Since
\int_{\gamma|_{[0,1)}}d\tau<{\rm dist}(\gamma(0),\{z;\ |z|=1/r\})/3
, we have
R\leq{\rm dist}(\gamma(0),\{z;\ |z|=1/r\})/3
. Hence, the image of
\Phi
is bounded away from
\{z;\ |z|=1/r\}
by a distance at least
{\rm dist}(\gamma(0),\{z;\ |z|=1/r\})/3
. Since
\Phi
cannot be extended to a larger disk, it must be hold that the image of
\Phi
goes to the boundary
A_{1}
. But, this image cannot go to points
a
of
A
with
\prod_{j=1}^{q}\left(Q_{j}(G)(a)\prod_{p=0}^{M}\psi(G)_{jp}(a)\right)=0
, since we have already shown that
\gamma(0)
is infinitely far away in the metric with respect to these points. Then the image of
\Phi
must go to the set
\{z;\ |z|=r\}
. Hence, by again Lemma
3.1
, there exists a point
w_{0}
with
|w_{0}|=R
so that
\Gamma=\Phi(\overline{0,w_{0}})
is a divergent curve on
S
.

We now show that
\Gamma
has finite length in the original metric
ds^{2}
on
S
, contradicting the completeness of
S
. Let
f:=g\circ\Phi:\Delta(R)\rightarrow V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
,
\omega\in\Delta(R)\mapsto f(\omega)\in V
, be a holomorphic curve which is nondegenerate over
I_{d}(V)
. Then
f
have a reduced representation

F=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}),

where
f_{i}=G\circ\Phi\ (0\leq i\leq n).

Hence, we have:

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}ds^{2}

\displaystyle=2\|G\circ\Phi\|^{2}|\Phi^{*}dz|^{2}=2\|F\|^{2}\left|\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right|^{2}|dw|^{2},

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{V},M}

\displaystyle=(G\circ\Phi)_{\mathcal{V},M}=G_{\mathcal{V},M}\circ\Phi\cdot\left(\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right)^{\sigma_{M}},

\displaystyle\psi(F)_{jp}

\displaystyle=\psi(G\circ\Phi)_{jp}=\psi(G)_{jp}\cdot\left(\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right)^{\sigma_{p}},(0\leq p\leq M).

On the other hand, since
\Phi
is locally isometric,

\displaystyle|dw|=|\Phi^{*}d\tau|

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(G)\circ\Phi|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}\circ\Phi|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}\circ\Phi|^{\epsilon/q}}\right)^{\rho^{*}}\left|\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right|\cdot|dw|

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(F)_{jp}|^{\epsilon/q}}\right)^{\rho^{*}}\left|\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right|^{1+h\rho\rho^{*}}\cdot|dw|

(because
1+\rho^{*}(\sigma_{M}+\epsilon\tau_{M})=1+h\rho\rho^{*}
).
This implies that

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{d(z\circ\Phi)}{dw}\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(F)_{jp}|^{\epsilon/q}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{\rho^{*}}{1+h\rho\rho^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|F_{\mathcal{V},p}(Q_{j})|^{\epsilon/q}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{\rho^{*}}{1+h\rho\rho^{*}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|F_{\mathcal{V},p}(Q_{j})|^{\epsilon/q}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{1/h}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|F\|\left(\dfrac{|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|F_{\mathcal{V},p}(Q_{j})|^{\epsilon/q}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{1/h}|dw|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\left(\dfrac{|F_{\mathcal{V},0}|^{\frac{h}{d}}|F_{\mathcal{V},M}|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}\circ\Phi}\prod_{j,p}|F_{\mathcal{V},p}(Q_{j})|^{\epsilon/q}}{\prod_{j=1}^{q}|Q_{j}(F)|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{1/h}|dw|.

Here, note that
\frac{h}{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\omega_{j}(1-\eta_{j})-M-1-\epsilon(\sigma_{M+1}+1)
. Then the inequality (
3.2
) yields that the conditions of Lemma
2.5
are satisfied. Applying Lemma
2.5
we have

\Phi^{*}ds\leq C\left(\dfrac{2R}{R^{2}-|w|^{2}}\right)^{\rho}|dw|,

for some positive constant
C
. Also, since
0<\rho<1
,

l_{ds^{2}}(\Gamma)\leq\int_{\Gamma}ds=\int_{\overline{0,w_{0}}}\Phi^{*}ds\leq C\cdot\int_{0}^{R}\left(\dfrac{2R}{R^{2}-|w|^{2}}\right)^{\rho}|dw|<+\infty.

This contradicts the assumption of the completeness of
S
with respect to
ds^{2}
. Thus, Claim
3.3
is proved.

Now, we set
d\tilde{\tau}^{2}=\lambda|dz|^{2}
where

\displaystyle\lambda(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|Q_{j}(G)(z)\|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}(z)|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}(z)}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}(z)|^{\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{2\rho^{*}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|Q_{j}(G)(\frac{1}{z})\|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}(\frac{1}{z})|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}(\frac{1}{z})}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}(\frac{1}{z})|^{\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}\right)^{2\rho^{*}}.

Also, we note that
\{u_{j}=-\infty\}=Q_{j}(G)^{-1}\{0\}
. This yields that the function

\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|Q_{j}(G)(z)\|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}(z))|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}(z)}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}(z)|^{\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}

is continuous and non-vanishing on
A_{1}
. Similarly the function

\dfrac{\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|Q_{j}(G)(\frac{1}{z})\|^{\omega_{j}-\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}{|G_{\mathcal{V},M}(\frac{1}{z}))|^{1+\epsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{\omega_{j}u_{j}(\frac{1}{z})}\prod_{j,p}|\psi(G)_{jp}(\frac{1}{z})|^{\frac{\epsilon}{q}}}

is continuous and non-vanishing on
A_{1}^{\prime}
, where

A_{1}^{\prime}=\left\{a\in A;\psi(G_{z})_{jp}\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)\neq 0,u_{j}\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)\neq-\infty\ \forall j=1,\ldots,q;p=0,\ldots,M\right\}.

We set
A^{\prime}=A_{1}\cap A_{1}^{\prime}
. These facts yield that the metric
d\tilde{\tau}^{2}
is complete and flat on
A^{\prime}
.

From Lemma
3.1
, there exist a local isometric
\Phi:\Delta(R_{0})\rightarrow A^{\prime}
. Since
(A^{\prime},d\tilde{\tau}^{2})
is a complete Riemann surface, it is necessary that
R_{0}=\infty
. So
\Phi:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow A^{\prime}\subset\{z;\ |z|<r\}

is a non-constant holomorphic map, which contradicts to Liouville’s theorem. Then, the supposition was wrong. Hence, Theorem
1.1
is proved.
∎

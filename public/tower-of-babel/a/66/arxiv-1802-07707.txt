. In this paper we prove and discuss a new divergence result of Nörlund logarithmic means with respect to Vilenkin system in Hardy space H 1 . H_{1}.

42C10.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin system, Nörlund logarithmic means,

1.
Introduction

It is well-known that (see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
) Vilenkin systems
do not form bases in the space
L_{1}.
Moreover, there exists a function in
the dyadic Hardy space
H_{1},
such that the partial sums of
f
are not
bounded in
L_{1}
-norm.

In
[
10
]
(see also
[
13
]
) it was proved that the following
is true:

Theorem T1:
The

maximal operator
\widetilde{S}^{\ast}

defined by

\widetilde{S}^{\ast}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|S_{n}\right|}{\log\left(n+1\right)}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1}
to the space
L_{1}.
Here
S_{n}

denotes the
n
-th partial sum with respect to the Vilenkin system.
Moreover, it was proved that the rate of the factor
log(n+1)
is in a sense
sharp.

Móricz and Siddiqi
[
6
]
investigate the approximation properties of
some special Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series of
L_{p}
functions in
norm. Fridli, Manchanda and Siddiqi
[
3
]
improved and extended the
results of Móricz and Siddiqi
[
6
]
to Martingale Hardy spaces. However, the
case when
\left\{q_{k}=1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}
was excluded, since
the methods are not applicable to Nörlund logarithmic means. In
[
4
]

Gát and Goginava proved some convergence and divergence properties of
Walsh-Fourier series of the Nörlund logarithmic means of functions in the
Lebesgue space
L_{1}.
In particular, they proved that there exists an
function in the space
L_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

Analogical result for some unbounded Vilenkin systems was proved in
[
2
]
.

In
[
8
]
(see also
[
13
]
) it was proved that there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p},\ \ (0<p<1)
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{p}=\infty,

Here
L_{n}
is
n
-th Nörlund logarithmic means with respect to
Vilenkin system.

In this paper we prove an analogical result for the bounded Vilenkin systems in the
case when
p=1.
Moreover, we discuss boundedness of weighted maximal
operators on the Hardy space
H_{1}.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.

Let
m:=(m_{0},m_{1},\dots)
denote a sequence of the positive numbers not
less than 2.

Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,\dots,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k},

k\in\mathbb{N}.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{k}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{k}}`
s.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k},(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m},
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup\limits_{n}m_{n}<\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded Vilenkin group. If the generating sequence
m
is not bounded, then
G_{m}
is said to be an unbounded Vilenkin group.
In this paper we discuss bounded Vilenkin groups only.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots),\ \left(x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighbourhood of
G_{m}:

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},

I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\},(x\in G_{m},n\in\mathbb{N}).

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right),
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overset{-}{I_{n}}:=G_{m}\backslash I_{n}
.

The norm (or quasi-norm) of the spaces
L_{p}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\ \ \ \left(0<p<\infty\right).

If we define the so-called generalized powers system based on
m
in the
following way:

M_{0}:=1,\ M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k}\ \qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}M_{k},
where
n_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\ (k\in\mathbb{N})
and only a finite number of
n_{k}`
s differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\ n_{k}\neq 0\}.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an orthonormal system, which is called the
Vilenkin system. First define the complex valued functions
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the generalized Rademacher functions,
by

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi\imath x_{k}/m_{k}\right),\ \left(\imath^{2}=-1,\ x\in G_{m},k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now, define the Vilenkin systems
\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}(x):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right),\ \left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Specifically, we call this system the Walsh-Paley one if
m\equiv 2
.

The Vilenkin systems are orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)
(see e.g.
[
1
,
14
]
).

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
we can establish Fourier coefficients,
partial sums, Dirichlet kernels, with respect to Vilenkin systems in the
usual manner:

\widehat{f}\left(n\right):=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{n}d\mu,\ \left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

S_{n}f:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k},\text{ \ }\ \ D_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k},\ \ \ \left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Let
\left\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\right\}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers.
The
n
-th Nörlund mean for the Fourier series of
f
is defined by

t_{n}f=\frac{1}{{l_{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{n-k}S_{k}f.

If
q_{k}={1}/{k}
, then we get the Nörlund logarithmic means:

L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k},\ \ l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k},

The kernel of the Nörlund logarithmic means is defined by

F_{n}:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{D_{k}}{n-k}.

In the special case when
\{q_{k}=1:k\in N\},
we get the Fejér means

\sigma_{n}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f\,.

We define
n
-th Fejér kernel

K_{n}f:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k}.

Let
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}).
Then the maximal function is given by

f^{\ast}(x)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)\mu\left(u\right)\right|.

The Hardy martingale spaces
H_{1}
consist of all martingales for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}:=\left\|f^{\ast}\right\|_{1}<\infty.

It it well-known (see e.g.
[
9
]
and
[
16
]
) that if
f\in L_{1},

then

(1)

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\sim\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|S_{M_{n}}f\right|\right\|_{1}.

A bounded measurable function
a
is a 1-atom if either
a=1
or

\int_{I}ad\mu=0,\ \left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right),\text{ \ supp}\left(a\right)\subset I.

3.
Auxiliary propositions

The Hardy martingale space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
has an atomic
characterization (see
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
):

Lemma 1
.

A function
f\in H_{1}
if and only if there exist a
sequence
\left(a_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of 1-atoms and a sequence
\left(\mu_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of real numbers such that

(2)

\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}a_{k}=f,\text{ \ a.e.,}

where

\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|<\infty.

Moreover,

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\backsim\inf\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|\right),

where the infimum is taken over all decomposition of
f
of the form (
2
).

In Blahota, Gát
[
2
]
the following lemma (see Lemma 5) was proved for unbounded Vilenkin systems:

Lemma 2
.

Let
q_{A}=M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}.
If
\log m_{n}=O(n^{\beta}),
for some
0<\delta<1/2,
then

\left\|F_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}\geq c(\log q_{A})^{\beta},

for some
0<\beta<1-\delta.

For the proof of main result we also need the following new Lemma of
independent interest:

Lemma 3
.

Let
G_{m}
be a bounded Vilenkin system and
q_{A}=M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}.
Then

\left\|F_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}\geq c\log q_{A}.

Proof.

During the proof we use some method of Blahota, Gát
[
2
]
(see Lemma 5) and Gát, Goginava
[
4
]
(see Lemma 2).

Set

\theta_{n}:=l_{n}F_{n}=\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{D_{n-k}}{k}.

Then we have that

\displaystyle\theta_{q_{A}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{1}{k}D_{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}(x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\overset{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}{\underset{k=M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}}{\sum}}\frac{1}{k}D_{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I+II.

We first discuss
I.
Since
k<M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0},
then

\displaystyle D_{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-k}(x)=D_{M_{2A}}(x)+r_{2A}D_{M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}(x).

This gives that

(3)

\displaystyle I=l_{q_{A-1}}D_{M_{2A}}(x)+r_{2A}G_{M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}}(x).

Moreover, by using Abel transformation, we get that

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}{\underset{k=M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}}{\sum}}\frac{1}{k}D_{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{1}}{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}-\frac{\left(M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-2\right)K_{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-2}}{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\overset{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-2}{\underset{k=M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}}{\sum}}\frac{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}{k(k+1)}K_{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}.

Since (for details see e.g.
[
1
]
)
\left\|K_{n}\right\|_{1}\leq 2,
for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we obtain that

(4)

\displaystyle\left\|II\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{2}{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}+\frac{2\left(M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-2\right)}{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\overset{M_{2A}+...+M_{0}-2}{\underset{k=M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}}{\sum}}\frac{M_{2A}+M_{2A-2}+...+M_{0}-k}{k(k+1)}<c<\infty.

Hence, by (
3
) and (
4
), we get that

\left\|\theta_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}\geq\left\|l_{q_{A-1}}D_{M_{2A}}+r_{2A}\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}-c.

We now discuss the right-hand side of this inequality, more exactly we give
a lower bound for it. First, we consider the integral on the set
G_{m}\setminus I_{2A}
:

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}\setminus I_{2A}}{\left|l_{q_{A-1}}D_{M_{2A}}(x)+r_{2A}\theta_{q_{A-1}}(x)\right|}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}\setminus I_{2A}}{\left|\theta_{q_{A-1}}(x)\right|}d\mu(x)=\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}-\int_{I_{2A}}{\left|\theta_{q_{A-1}}(x)\right|}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}-\frac{\theta_{q_{A-1}}(0)}{M_{2A}}.

Next, we note that on the set
I_{2A}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{2A}}{\left|l_{q_{A-1}}D_{M_{2A}}(x)+r_{2A}\theta_{q_{A-1}}(x)\right|}d\mu(x)=l_{q_{A-1}}-\frac{\theta_{q_{A-1}}(0)}{M_{2A}}.

It follows that

(5)

\left\|\theta_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}\geq\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+l_{q_{A-1}}-\frac{2\theta_{q_{A-1}}(0)}{M_{2A}}-c.

Moreover, according to simple estimation,

\theta_{n}(0)=\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{n-k}{k}=n\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{1}{k}-n+1=nl_{n}+O(n).

Therefore, since

q_{A-1}\leq M_{2A-2}(1+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{16}+...)=\frac{4}{3}M_{2A-2}\leq\frac{1}{3}M_{2A}

we obtain that

(6)

\frac{2}{l_{q_{A}}M_{2A}}\theta_{q_{A-1}}(0)=\frac{2q_{A-1}}{M_{2A}}\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}+o(1)\leq\frac{2}{3}\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}+o(1).

Finally, by using (
5
) and (
6
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|F_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|\theta_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}-o(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}-o(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|\theta_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{6}-o(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|F_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{6}-o(1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{l_{q_{A-1}}}{l_{q_{A}}}\left\|F_{q_{A-1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{8}

By iterating this estimate we get

\left\|F_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{8}\overset{A-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{l_{q_{k}}}{l_{q_{A}}}\geq\frac{c}{8A}\overset{A-1}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}k\geq cA\geq c\log q_{A}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Formulation of the Main Results

Our main results read:

Theorem 1
.

Let
G_{m}
be a bounded Vilenkin system. Then there exist a martingale
f\in H_{1}
such that

(7)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=+\infty.

Remark 1
.

In one point of view Theorem 1 of Blahota and Gát
[
2
]
is better then our resut (see Theorem 1) because in their result boundedness of the group
G_{m}
is not necessary and in the other point of view theorem of Blahota and Gát is a slightly weaker since they construct function in the Lebesgue space
L_{1}
.

The next result can be found in
[
13
]
and
[
8
]
, respectively:

Corollary 1
.

Let
G_{m}
be a bounded Vilenkin system. Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{1}
such that

\left\|L^{\ast}f\right\|_{1}=+\infty.

Corollary 2
.

Let
0<p<1
and
G_{m}
be a bounded Vilenkin system. Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}
such that

\left\|L^{\ast}f\right\|_{p}=+\infty.

Theorem 2
.

a) Let
G_{m}
be a bounded Vilenkin system. Then the maximal operator

\overset{\sim}{L}^{\ast}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\log\left(n+1\right)}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).

b) Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}_{+}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be a
nondecreasing function satisfying the condition

(8)

\overline{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\log\left(n+1\right)}{\varphi\left(n\right)}=+\infty.

Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that the maximal operator

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\varphi\left(n\right)}

is not bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the
Lebesgue space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).

Remark 2
.

Note that b) shows that the statement in part a) of Theorem
2 is in the sense sharp with respect to the logarithmic factor in (
2
).

5.
Proofs of the Main Results

Proof of Theorem 1.

Let
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be an increasing sequence
of positive integers such that

(9)

\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{\eta}}<\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{k}},

(10)

\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{k-1}}<\alpha_{k}.

Since

\alpha_{k}\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{\eta}}\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \frac{M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{k}}\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \text{as}\ \ k\rightarrow\infty

and all of three sequences are strightly increasing, we can construct lacunar increasing sequence
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
of natural number
\mathbb{N}
, for which

\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{\eta}}<\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\alpha^{1/2}_{k}}<\alpha_{k+1}.

We also note that under condition (
10
) we get that

M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}<\alpha_{k}\alpha^{1/2}_{k-1}\leq\alpha_{k}^{3/2}

and

(11)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{-1/2}<\alpha_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}^{1/3}}<c<\infty.

Similar consructions of martingales can be found in
[
10
]
-
[
13
]
(see also
[
7
]
).

Let

f\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where

\lambda_{k}=\frac{m_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\alpha_{k}},\ \ a_{k}=\frac{1}{m_{2\alpha_{k}}}\left(D_{M_{2\alpha_{k}+1}}-D_{M_{{}_{2\alpha_{k}}}}\right).

From (
11
) and Lemma 1 we can conclude that
f\in H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).

It is easy to show that

(12)

\widehat{f}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{\alpha^{1/2}_{k}},\,\,\text{ if \thinspace\thinspace}j\in\left\{M_{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}M_{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\},\text{ }k=0,1,2...,\\
0,\text{ \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}j\notin\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\{M_{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}M_{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\}\text{ .}\end{array}\right.

We can write that

(13)

\displaystyle L_{q_{\alpha_{k}}}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{j=1}{\overset{q_{\alpha_{k}}}{\sum}}\frac{S_{j}f}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{j=1}{\overset{M_{2\alpha_{k}}-1}{\sum}}\frac{S_{j}f}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}+\frac{1}{q_{\alpha_{k}}}\underset{j=M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\overset{q_{\alpha_{k}}}{\sum}}\frac{S_{j}f}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{q_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{j=1}{\overset{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}+1}{\sum}}\frac{S_{j}f}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}+\frac{1}{q_{\alpha_{k}}}\underset{j=M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{\overset{q_{\alpha_{k}}}{\sum}}\frac{S_{j}f}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I+II.

Let
j<M_{2\alpha_{k-1}+1}.
By combining (
9
) and (
12
) we get that

\displaystyle\left|S_{j}f\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\left|\widehat{f}(v)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}\leq c\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-2}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}+\frac{cM_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2cM_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}.

Since

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}\frac{1}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=M_{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{M_{2\alpha_{k}}}\frac{1}{j}\leq\log M_{2\alpha_{k}}-\log M_{2\alpha_{k}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k}}}{M_{2\alpha_{k}-1}}<c<\infty

according to (
10
) and (
14
) we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|I\right|\leq\frac{2c}{\alpha_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}\frac{1}{q_{\alpha_{k}}-j}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\alpha_{k}}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k-1}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k-1}}}<c<\infty.

Hence,

\left\|I\right\|_{1}<c<\infty.

Let
M_{2\alpha_{k}}\leq j\leq q_{\alpha_{k}}.
Then we have that

\displaystyle S_{j}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\sum_{v=M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}^{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}-1}\widehat{f}(v)\psi_{v}+\sum_{v=M_{2\alpha_{k}}}^{j-1}\widehat{f}(v)\psi_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{M_{2\alpha_{\eta}}^{1/p-1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\eta}}}\left(D_{M_{{}_{2\alpha_{\eta}+1}}}-D_{M_{{}_{2\alpha_{\eta}}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{M_{2\alpha_{k}}^{1/p-1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}}\left(D_{j}-D_{M_{{}_{2\alpha_{k}}}}\right).

This gives that

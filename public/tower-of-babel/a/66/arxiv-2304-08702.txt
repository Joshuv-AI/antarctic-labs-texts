Abstract
. Let 𝒢 α ​ ( X , G ) \mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(X,G) be the G G -gauge group over a space X X corresponding to a map α : X → B ​ G \alpha\colon X\to BG . We compute the integral cohomology of B ​ 𝒢 1 ​ ( S 2 , S ​ O ​ ( n ) ) B\mathcal{G}_{1}(S^{2},SO(n)) for n = 3 , 4 n=3,4 . 1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a topological group, and let
P
be a principal
G
-bundle over a base space
X
. Then automorphisms of
P
are, by definition,
G
-equivariant self-maps of
P
covering the identity map of
X
. The
gauge group
of
P
is defined as the topological group of automorphisms of
P
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(X,G)
denote the gauge group of the principal
G
-bundle over
X
corresponding to a map
\alpha\colon X\to BG
.

Gauge groups have been studied in several contexts of geometry, topology, and physics. Here, we briefly recall the homotopy theory of gauge groups. Given
G
and
X
, we have a family of principal
G
-bundles over
X
, and so we get a family of their gauge groups. There are many previous studies about the classification of the homotopy types of gauge groups in this family. As shown by Gottlieb
[
6
]
, there is a natural homotopy equivalence

(1.1)

B\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(X,G)\simeq\operatorname{Map}(X,BG;\alpha)

where
\operatorname{Map}(X,Y;f)
denotes the path-component of the space of maps from
X
to
Y
containing a map
f\colon X\to Y
(see also
[
1
]
). Then the classification of the homotopy types of gauge groups is closely related to the classical problem in algebraic topology, the classification of components of a mapping space
[
20
]
. There are some general results on the classification of the homotopy types of gauge groups
[
10
,
12
,
14
,
22
,
25
]
. Remarkably, Crabb and Sutherland
[
4
]
showed that there are only finitely many homotopy types of
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(X,G)
as
\alpha
ranges over all maps
X\to BG
whenever
G
is a compact connected Lie group and
X
is a connected finite complex, while there are possibly infinitely many principal
G
-bundles over
X
.

The classification of the homotopy types of gauge groups is generalized to the classification of the
A_{n}
-types: Tsutaya
[
27
]
showed that there are only finitely many
A_{n}
-types for
n<\infty
whenever
G
is a compact connected Lie group and
X
is a finite complex. On the other hand, Kishimoto and Tsutaya
[
17
]
showed that there are infinitely many
A_{\infty}
-types, i.e. the homotopy types of the classifying spaces, of gauge groups whenever
G
is a compact connected simple Lie group and
X=S^{d}
with
\pi_{d-1}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\neq 0
. In addition, there are many results on the classification for specific
G
and
X

[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
13
,
16
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
.

Although there are many results on the classification of the homotopy types of gauge groups, not much is known about their (co)homology. In particular, there are only a few results determining the (co)homology of the classifying spaces of gauge groups. Takeda
[
21
]
computed the integral cohomology ring of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
. Kishimoto and Theriault
[
15
]
proved that, for a compact connected simple Lie group
G
of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{l})
and a prime
p
larger than
n_{l}+1
, there is an isomorphism

H_{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{4},G);\mathbb{Z}/p)\cong H_{*}(BG;\mathbb{Z}/p)\otimes H_{*}(\Omega^{3}G\langle 3\rangle;\mathbb{Z}/p)

as
\mathbb{Z}/p
-vector spaces and so the mod
p
homology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{4},G)
does not depend on the classifying map
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{3}(G)
. Partial results on the mod
p
(co)homology of
B\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(X,G)
for specific
G
and
X
are given by Choi
[
3
]
, Masbaum
[
19
]
and Tsukuda
[
26
]
. As of today, Tsukuda’s result
[
26
]
is the only nontrivial result on the integral or mod
p
(co)homology of
B\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(S^{d},G)
for a Lie group
G
that has
p
-torsion in its homology.

In this paper, we study the cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},SO(n))
for
n\geq 3
, where
k=0,1\in\mathbb{Z}/2\cong\pi_{1}(SO(n))
. We will always assume
n\geq 3
as long as we consider
SO(n)
. First, we will apply the result of Takeda
[
21
]
on the integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
for determining the integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{1}(S^{2},SO(n))
for
n\leq 4
, where the result of Takeda includes a minor mistake and we will correct it. To state the result, we set notation. Let
A
be a graded abelian group of finite type. The
rational closure
of a subgroup
B
of
A
is defined by

B_{\mathcal{R}}=\{a\in A\mid na\in B\text{ for some }0\neq n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We also define a polynomial
s_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
inductively by

(1.2)

s_{0}=1,\quad s_{1}=x_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{n}=(-1)nx_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+i+1}x_{n-i}s_{i}.

So if

is the

, then for

, we have

s_{k}=t_{1}^{k}+\cdots+t_{n}^{k}.

Now we are ready to state the first result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

There is an isomorphism

H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{1}(S^{2},SO(3));\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/((h_{2},h_{3},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}}+(x_{1}))

where
|c_{i}|=|x_{i}|=2i
and
h_{i}=s_{i}-s_{i-1}c_{1}+s_{i-2}c_{2}
.

(2)

There is an isomorphism

H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{1}(S^{2},SO(4));\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]\otimes\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/I

where
|c_{i}|=|x_{i}|=2i
,
h_{i}=s_{i}-s_{i-1}c_{1}+s_{i-2}c_{2}
and

I=(h_{2}\otimes 1,1\otimes h_{2},h_{3}\otimes 1,1\otimes h_{3},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}}+(c_{1}\otimes 1-1\otimes c_{1},x_{1}\otimes 1).

Next, as an application of Theorem

[

Theorem 1.2
.

The homology of

is torsion free if and only if

.

Finally, as another application of Theorem

, we will compute

for

. Then by combining with Theriault’s homotopy decomposition of a gauge group over a Riemann surface

as follows, where

denotes a Riemann surface of genus

. Note that the homotopy set

is isomorphic to

.

Theorem 1.3

For

, there are isomorphisms

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{k}(\Sigma_{g},SO(n)))\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2&(k=0)\\
\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}&(k=1).\end{cases}

For

,
\mathcal{G}_{k}(\Sigma_{g},SO(n))\simeq\mathcal{G}_{l}(\Sigma_{g},SO(n))

2.
Cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},Spin^{c}(n))
for
n=3,4

This section computes the integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},Spin^{c}(n))
for
n=3,4
by correcting the result of Takeda
[
21
]
on the integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
. Recall that
Spin^{c}(n)
is defined as the nontrivial extension of
SO(n)
by
S^{1}
:

1\to S^{1}\xrightarrow{\alpha}Spin^{c}(n)\xrightarrow{\pi}SO(n)\to 1.

This short exact sequence of Lie groups is natural with respect to the inclusions
i^{\prime}_{n}\colon Spin^{c}(n)\to Spin^{c}(n+1)
and
i_{n}\colon SO(n)\to SO(n+1)
. Then, in particular, there are exceptional isomorphisms:

Spin^{c}(3)\cong U(2)\quad\text{and}\quad Spin^{c}(4)\cong\left\{(A,B)\in U(2)\times U(2)\mid\det A=\det B\right\}

where
\alpha\colon S^{1}\to Spin^{c}(3)
and
i^{\prime}_{3}\colon Spin^{c}(3)\to Spin^{c}(4)
are given by

\alpha(e^{i\theta})=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta}&0\\
0&e^{i\theta}\end{pmatrix},\quad i^{\prime}_{3}(A)=(A,A).

So we can get the cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},Spin^{c}(n))
for
n=3,4
from that of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(2))
.

Takeda
[
21
]
described the integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
, but it includes a mistake. Then we correct it. Bott
[
2
]
studied the homology of the loop space of a Lie group, and in particular, we have

Proposition 2.1
.

There is an isomorphism

H^{*}(\Omega_{0}U(n);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(s_{n},s_{n+1},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}},\quad|x_{i}|=2i

where
s_{n}
is as in (
1.2
).

By (
1.1
), we have
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))\simeq\operatorname{Map}(S^{2},BU(n);k)
. Then there is a homotopy fibration

(2.1)

\Omega_{0}U(n)\xrightarrow{\iota}B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))\xrightarrow{e}BU(n)

given by the evaluation at the basepoint of
S^{2}
. Recall that the cohomology of
BU(n)
is given by

H^{*}(BU(n);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}],\quad|c_{i}|=2i

where
c_{i}
is the universal
i
-th Chern class. Then

H^{{\operatorname{odd}}}(BU(n);\mathbb{Z})=0\quad\text{and}\quad H^{{\operatorname{odd}}}(\Omega_{0}U(n);\mathbb{Z})=0

by Proposition
2.1
. Thus the integral cohomology Serre spectral sequence for the above homotopy fibration collapses at the
E_{2}
-term and we get

Lemma 2.2
.

The integral cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
is a free abelian group and generated by
c_{i}=e^{*}(c_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
x_{j}
for
j\geq 1
as an algebra, where
\iota^{*}(x_{j})=x_{j}
in Proposition
2.1
.

Let
c_{0}=1
and
c_{i}=0
for
i>n
. We define

h_{i}=kc_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{j}s_{j}c_{i-j}\quad\text{for}\quad i\geq 1.

Takeda
[
21
]
showed that
h_{i}=0
in
H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n));\mathbb{Z})
for
i\geq n
by constructing the element
x_{j}\in H^{2j}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n));\mathbb{Z})
explicitly. Thus by Lemma
2.2
, there is a surjection

\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)\to H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n));\mathbb{Z}).

Takeda
[
21
]
claimed that the surjection above is an isomorphism, which is false. To see this, let
n=2
and
k=1
. Then

h_{4}-c_{1}h_{3}-(c_{1}x_{1}+x_{1}^{2})h_{2}=2y

where

y=c_{1}^{2}x_{2}+c_{1}x_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-c_{2}x_{2}+x_{2}^{2}+2x_{1}x_{3}-2x_{4}.

However, since the coefficient of
x_{4}
in
f\in(h_{2},h_{3},\ldots)
is a multiple of 4, we have
y\notin(h_{2},h_{3},\ldots)
. Thus the image of
y
in
\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{2},h_{3},\ldots)
is of order
2
, while by Lemma
2.2
,
H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{1}(S^{2},U(2));\mathbb{Z})
is a free abelian group.

Note that, for a free abelian group
A
and its subgroup
B
,
B_{\mathcal{R}}
is the minimal direct summand of
A
containing
B
. Then
\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}}
is the maximal free abelian subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)
. Thus by Lemma
2.2
, we have a surjection

(2.2)

\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}}\to H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n));\mathbb{Z}).

Theorem 2.3
.

There is an isomorphism

H^{*}(B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n));\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}}.

Proof.

Since the rational cohomology Serre spectral sequence for the homotopy fibration (
2.1
) collapses at the
E_{2}
-term as above, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that the Poincaré series of the rational cohomology of
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
is given by

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-t^{2i}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-t^{2i}}.

On the other hand, one can easily see that
h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots
is a regular sequence in
\mathbb{Q}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]
and so
(\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots]/(h_{n},h_{n+1},\ldots)_{\mathcal{R}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
has the same Poincaré series as
B\mathcal{G}_{k}(S^{2},U(n))
. Thus the map (
2.2
) is injective, hence an isomorphism.
∎

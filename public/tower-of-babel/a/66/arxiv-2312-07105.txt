A coarse geometric approach to graph layout problems
Wanying Huang, David Hume, Samuel J. Kelly, Ryan Lam

Abstract
We define a range of new coarse geometric invariants based on various graph-theoretic measures of complexity for finite graphs, including: treewidth, pathwidth, cutwidth, search number, topological bandwidth, bandwidth, minimal linear arrangment, sumcut, profile, vertex and edge separation. We prove that, for bounded degree graphs, these invariants can be used to define functions which satisfy a strong monotonicity property, namely they are monotonically non-decreasing with respect to regular maps, and as such have potential applications in coarse geometry and geometric group theory. On the graph-theoretic side, we prove asymptotically optimal upper bounds on the treewidth, pathwidth, cutwidth, search number, topological bandwidth, vertex separation, edge separation, minimal linear arrangement, sumcut and profile for the family of all finite subgraphs of any bounded degree graph whose separation profile is known to be of the form r a ​ log ⁡ ( r ) b r^{a}\log(r)^{b} for some a > 0 a>0 . This large class includes the Diestel-Leader graph, all Cayley graphs of non-virtually cyclic polycyclic groups, uniform lattices in almost all connected unimodular Lie groups, and certain hyperbolic groups.

Introduction

The primary objects of study in this paper are
graph layout problems
: finding linear orderings or decompositions of graphs which minimise a specified cost function. Examples of such invariants include treewidth, cutwidth and bandwidth. There are many motivations for finding upper bounds on these invariants coming from a variety of areas including: optimization of networks for parallel computer architectures, VLSI circuit design, information retrieval, numerical analysis, computational biology, graph theory, scheduling and archaeology (cf.
[
DPS02
]
,
[
Bod06
]
and references therein). Typically, realising the minimal possible value of these invariants is NP-hard (see, for example
[
GJ79
]
for cutwidth and
[
ACP87
]
for treewidth). Despite this, there are asymptotically optimal upper bounds for large classes of graphs. One noteworthy result in the context of this paper is that for every
m
-vertex graph
H
, any
n
-vertex graph which does not contain
H
as a minor has treewidth at most
m^{3/2}n^{1/2}

[
AST90
, (1.8)]
. The goal of this paper is to take a “large-scale” viewpoint on these invariants which will enable us to give asymptotically optimal upper and lower bounds on the growth of some of these graph layout parameters for subgraphs of particular infinite graphs of bounded degree. The motivation for working in this level of generality comes from the study of (obstructions to) regular maps.

Until recently, there were very few methods to obstruct regular maps between graphs, but in the last decade the number and variety of tools has greatly increased
[
BST12
,
HMT20
,
HMT22
,
BH21
,
HMT23
]
. This paper is another part of this proliferation of new techniques.

A map between the vertex sets of two bounded degree graphs is
regular
if it is Lipschitz (with respect to the shortest path metrics) and preimages of vertices have uniformly bounded cardinality. The inclusion of one graph as a subgraph of another is always a regular map; also, given two finitely generated groups
H,G
, there is a regular map from a Cayley graph of
H
to a Cayley graph of
G
whenever
H
admits a finite-index subgroup which is isomorphic to a subgroup of
G
. Thus, the statement that no regular map exists between two graphs is a strong geometric version of the statement
X
is not (similar to) a subgraph of
Y
, or
H
is not (similar to) a subgroup of
G
. For bounded degree graphs, regular maps generalise both quasi-isometric and coarse embeddings. These observations give combinatorial and algebraic motivations for determining obstructions to the existence of regular maps.

To fix notation, the vertex and edge sets of a graph
\Gamma
will be denoted by
V\Gamma
and
E\Gamma
respectively, and we write
\Gamma\leq\Gamma^{\prime}
to denote that
\Gamma
is a subgraph of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. The maximal vertex degree of
\Gamma
(when it exists) will be denoted by
\Delta(\Gamma)
or just
\Delta
when there is no ambiguity.

1.1
Invariants and monotonicity under regular maps

Our main result is that many invariants related to graph layout problems can be used as obstructions to the existence of regular maps. Specifically, we will define four spectra of invariants. The first three are denoted

\vc^{p}

•

1

•

\vc^{\infty}
is vertex separation (
\vs

\vc^{1}
is sumcut (
\sumcut

•

\ell^{\infty}
is bandwidth.

The final class of invariants are denoted

equals the treewidth (

\pw

and any

We now define our set of invariants

\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{p\in[1,\infty]}\left(\left\{\ec^{p},\vc^{p},\ell^{p}\right\}\cup\bigcup_{G}\left\{\wid^{G,p}\right\}\right).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

and

be bounded degree graphs and assume

has infinitely many edges. If there is a regular map

, then for every

, there is a constant

such that for all

\displaystyle I_{X}(r)\leq CI_{Y}(Cr+C)+C

where
I_{X}(r)=\max\left\{I(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}
.

The motivation behind the definition of

comes from the separation profile
\sep_{X}
of Benjamini-Schramm-Timár (where

is the cutsize cf. Definition

)

]
. We will refer to

1.2
Comparing Invariants

Our next goal is to compare these new invariants with each other and to other cost functions associated to graph layout problems. Firstly, we present some elementary comparisons of
\ell^{p}
norms on functions.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any bounded degree graph
X
and any

\displaystyle\ec^{p}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\simeq\vc^{p}_{X}(r).

Moreover, for any

p\leq q
and any graph

\displaystyle\ec^{q}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\ec^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\ec^{q}_{X}(r),

\displaystyle\ell^{q}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\ell^{q}_{X}(r),

\displaystyle\wid^{G,q}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\wid^{G,p}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\wid^{G,q}_{X}.

Throughout the paper we make the convention that
\frac{1}{\infty}=0

For any bounded degree graph

\displaystyle\sep_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tw_{X}\lesssim\pw_{X}

1.3
Calculations

Our next result states that when the separation profile is a “nice” function and not too small, then all of the invariants mentioned in Theorem
1.3
(except bandwidth) are
\simeq
-equivalent.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph such that
\sep_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
with
a>0
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\cw_{X}(r)\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}r^{a}\log(r)^{b}&\textrm{if}&a>0\\
\log(r)^{b+1}&\textrm{if}&a=0\end{array}\right.

In particular, if
\sep_{X}(r)\simeq r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
with
a>0
, then

\sep_{X}(r)\simeq\tw_{X}(r)\simeq\pw_{X}(r)\simeq\cw_{X}(r)\simeq\sn_{X}(r)\simeq\tbw_{X}(r).

Moving to lower bounds, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.5
.

For every bounded degree graph
X
and every
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\vc^{p}_{X}(r)\gtrsim r^{\frac{1}{p}}\sep_{X}(r).

Thus, if
\sep_{X}(r)\simeq r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
with
a>0
, then for every
p\in[1,\infty]

\vc^{p}_{X}(r)\simeq r^{\frac{1}{p}+a}\log(r)^{b}.

The class of graphs whose separation profile is known to be of the form

Moreover, we obtain the following bounds for sumcut and minimal linear arrangement. For each

such that for every

•

each
\Gamma\leq X
with
|\Gamma|\leq r

,

•

some
\Gamma\leq X
with

Examples of such graphs include products

2
Defining and comparing invariants

2.1
Graph decompositions

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
and
\Gamma
be graphs. A
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
is a collection
\left\{X_{g}\ \left|\ g\in VG\right.\right\}
of subsets of
V\Gamma
satisfying the following three conditions:

1.

\bigcup_{g\in VG}X_{g}=V\Gamma
,

2.

for every edge
vw\in E\Gamma
there is some
g\in VG
such that
\{v,w\}\subseteq X_{g}
,

3.

for each
v\in V\Gamma
, the full subgraph of
\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ v\in X_{g}\right.\right\}
is connected.

We now introduce a cost associated to
G
-decompositions.

Definition 2.2
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
p
-width
of a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
is the
\ell^{p}
norm of the function
VG\to\mathbb{R}
given by
g\mapsto|X_{g}|
, i.e. for
p\in[1,\infty)
, the
p
-width is

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{g\in VG}|X_{g}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and the
\infty
-width is
\max_{g\in VG}|X_{g}|
.

Remark 2.3
.

It would be more consistent with the literature to consider the cost function
g\mapsto|X_{g}|-1
. Our reasons for not doing this are that in the
\infty
-case the difference of
1
does not change the cost up to
\simeq
, and in the case
p\in[1,\infty)
we do not need to exclude “small” decompositions (where
|X_{g}|=1
occurs for “most”
g\in VG
) in the monotonicity result.

Now we present the first collection of invariants in
\mathcal{I}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be any graph. The
(G,p)
-width of a graph
\Gamma
is the minimal
p
-width over the set of all
G
-decompositions of
\Gamma
. We denote this by
\wid^{G,p}(\Gamma)
.

Commonly studied examples include treewidth
\tw(\Gamma)=\wid^{T,\infty}(\Gamma)-1
and pathwidth
\pw(\Gamma)=\wid^{P,\infty}(\Gamma)-1
, where
T
is the disjoint union of all finite trees, and
P
is the disjoint union of all finite paths.

Remark 2.5
.

We note that for any
G
and any
\Gamma
,
\wid^{G,1}(\Gamma)=|V\Gamma|
. The lower bound is a consequence of the requirement that
\bigcup_{g\in VG}X_{g}=V\Gamma
, and the upper bound is obtained from any decomposition where
X_{g}=V\Gamma
for some
g\in VG
and
X_{g^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for all other
g^{\prime}\in VG
. For other finite values of
p
the invariants are more interesting. For instance, if
\Gamma
is the subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with vertex set
\{1,\ldots,k\}^{3}
and edges
(a,b,c)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
whenever
|a-a^{\prime}|+|b-b^{\prime}|+|c-c^{\prime}|=1
, then

X_{(x,y)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{(a,b,c)\ \left|\ |a-x|+|b-y|\leq 1\right.\right\}&\textup{if}\ 1\leq x,y\leq k,\\
\emptyset&\textup{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

defines a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-decomposition of
\Gamma
such that

\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|X_{(x,y)}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq 5k^{1+2/p}=5|V\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3p}}.

2.2
Linear arrangements

Definition 2.6
.

A linear arrangement of a finite graph
\Gamma
is a bijective function
f:V\Gamma\to\{1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\}
.

We will associate three cost functions to a linear arrangement.

Definition 2.7
.

We define the following functions
\{1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\}\to\mathbb{R}
:

•

edge cut
:
i\mapsto\ec_{f}(i)=|\left\{vw\in E\Gamma\ \left|\ f(v)\leq i<f(w)\right.\right\}|
,

•

vertex cut
:
i\mapsto\vc_{f}(i)=|\left\{v\in V\Gamma\ \left|\ \exists vw\in E\Gamma,\ f(v)\leq i<f(w)\right.\right\}|
.

We also define a function
E\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
called
length
:
vw\mapsto|f(v)-f(w)|
.

Note that edge cut and vertex cut are related in the following way.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph with maximal degree
\Delta
. For every linear arrangement
f
and every
i
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta}\vc_{f}(i)\leq\ec_{f}(i)\leq\Delta\vc_{f}(i)

As with graph decompositions, for each finite graph
\Gamma
and each
p\in[1,\infty]
we define the
p
-edge cut
\ec^{p}(\Gamma)
and
p
-length
\ell^{p}(\Gamma)
to be the minimal
\ell^{p}
norm of the respective function over all linear arrangements of
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X
be a graph and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. We define

\displaystyle\ec^{p}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\ec^{p}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}

\displaystyle\vc^{p}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\vc^{p}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}

\displaystyle\ell^{p}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\ell^{p}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}.

and call these the
p
-edge cut,
p
-vertex cut and
p
-length profiles of

Remark 2.10
.

As noted in the introduction, the
\ell^{1}
and

versions of the above invariants are typically known by other names.
\infty

,

\infty

,
\infty

Another cost associated to a linear arrangement is modified cutwidth (

Lemma 2.11
.

For any finite graph

\ec^{1}(\Gamma)=\mcw(\Gamma)+|E\Gamma|

where

is the modified cutwidth - the minimal

3.1
Width of graph decompositions

We begin with a useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\left\{X_{g}\ \left|\ g\in VG\right.\right\}
be a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
. For every subset
A\subseteq V\Gamma
which induces a connected subgraph of
\Gamma
,

\bigcup_{a\in A}\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ a\in X_{g}\right.\right\}

induces a connected subgraph of
G
.

Proof.

Set
G_{a}=\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ a\in X_{g}\right.\right\}
. As
\left\{X_{g}\ \left|\ g\in VG\right.\right\}
be a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
, each
G_{a}
induces a connected subgraph of
G
. Now for each distinct pair
b,b^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{a\in A}\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ a\in X_{g}\right.\right\}
there exist
a,a^{\prime}\in A
such that
b\in G_{a}
and
b^{\prime}\in G_{a^{\prime}}
. Let
a=a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}=a^{\prime}
be the vertex set of a path in
A
. Since
a_{i-1}a_{i}\in E\Gamma
for all
i
, there is some
g\in VG
such that
\{a_{i-1},a_{i}\}\subseteq X_{g}
. Thus,
g\in G_{a_{i-1}}\cap G_{a_{i}}
. Therefore,

\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}G_{a_{i}}\subseteq\bigcup_{a\in A}\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ a\in X_{g}\right.\right\}

induces a connected subgraph of
G
which contains
b,b^{\prime}
. As this holds for each pair
b,b^{\prime}
, the lemma follows.
∎

Let

\wid^{G,p}_{X}(r)\lesssim\wid^{G,p}_{Y}(r).

Let

be a path of length at most

connecting
\phi(x)
to
\phi(y)
. Define

\Gamma^{\prime}=\phi(V\Gamma)\cup\bigcup_{xy\in E\Gamma}P_{xy}\leq Y

Given
x\in V\Gamma
, we denote by
\overline{\phi}(x)
the set of all vertices in

\left\{\phi(x)\right\}\cup\bigcup_{\left\{vw\in E\Gamma\ \left|\ x\in\{v,w\}\right.\right\}}P_{xy}.

We have
|V\Gamma^{\prime}|\leq|V\Gamma|+2\kappa|E(\Gamma)|\leq|V\Gamma|(1+\kappa\Delta(X))

Fix a
G
-decomposition
\left\{X^{\prime}_{g}\ \left|\ g\in G\right.\right\}
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
with
p
-width at most
\wid^{G,p}_{Y}((1+\kappa\Delta(X))r)
.

Now define
X_{g}=\left\{x\in V\Gamma\ \left|\ \overline{\phi}(x)\cap X^{\prime}_{g}\neq\emptyset\right.\right\}
. We now prove that
\{X_{g}\}
is a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
. For every
x\in V\Gamma

\phi(x)\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
, so there is some
g\in G
such that
\phi(x)\in X^{\prime}_{g}
. Therefore,
x\in X^{\prime}_{g}
, so condition (i) holds. Now let
xy\in V\Gamma
and let
w
be any vertex on the path
P_{xy}
. There is some
g\in G
such that
w\in X^{\prime}_{g}
and
w\in\overline{\phi}(x)\cap\overline{\phi}(y)
, so
x,y\in X_{g}
for the same
g
. Thus
(ii)
holds.
By construction, for each
x\in V\Gamma
,

\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ x\in X_{g}\right.\right\}=\bigcup_{y\in\overline{\phi}(x)}\left\{g\in VG\ \left|\ y\in X^{\prime}_{g}\right.\right\}.

(3.3)

As
\overline{\phi}(v)
is connected, by Lemma
3.1
, the full subgraph of
G
with vertex set (
3.3
) is connected, so
\{X_{g}\}
is a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
.

Finally, for a fixed
y\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
, if
y\in\overline{\phi}(x)
then
\phi(x)
is contained in the ball of radius
2\kappa
centred at
y
. As
|\phi^{-1}(z)|\leq\kappa
for every
z\in VY
, we deduce that there are at most
\kappa(1+\Delta(X))^{2\kappa}
possibilities for
x\in V\Gamma
. Thus, for each
g\in G
,

|X_{g}|\leq\kappa(1+\Delta(X))^{2\kappa}|X^{\prime}_{g}|

so, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\wid^{G,p}(\Gamma)\leq\kappa(1+\Delta(X))^{2\kappa}\wid^{G,p}(\Gamma^{\prime}).

As this holds for all finite
\Gamma\leq X
, we have

\wid^{G,p}_{X}(r)\leq K\wid^{G,p}_{Y}(Kr)

where
K=\kappa(1+\Delta(X))^{2\kappa}
.
∎

3.2
Linear arrangement costs

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X,Y
be bounded degree graphs where
Y
has infinitely many edges. If there is a regular map
VX\to VY
then for every
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\ell^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim\ell^{p}_{Y}(r)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\ec^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim\ec^{p}_{Y}(r).

(3.6)

Proof.

Note that as
Y
has infinitely many edges, then for every
r
there is a subgraph
\Gamma_{r}\leq Y
with at most
r
vertices and at least
\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor
edges. Thus,

\displaystyle\min\{\ell^{p}_{Y}(r),\ec^{p}_{Y}(r)\}\geq\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor^{1/p}.

(3.7)

Therefore, we may assume that
\ell^{p}_{Y}(r)\gtrsim r^{1/p}
and
\ec^{p}_{Y}(r)\gtrsim r^{1/p}
.

Since there is a regular map
\phi:VX\to VY
, there is some
\kappa\geq 1
such that

•

d_{Y}(\phi(x),\phi(x^{\prime}))\leq\kappa(1+d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}))
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in VX
, and

•

|\phi^{-1}(y)|\leq\kappa
for all
y\in VY
.

Let
\Gamma\leq X
with
|V\Gamma|\leq r
. Define a subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq Y
which contains
\phi(V\Gamma)
and such that whenever
xy\in E\Gamma
, there is a path of length at most
2\kappa
connecting
\phi(x)
to
\phi(y)
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
. We may ensure that
\frac{1}{\kappa}|V\Gamma|\leq|V\Gamma^{\prime}|\leq 2\kappa|E\Gamma|\leq\kappa\Delta(X)|V\Gamma|
.

Given a proper numbering
g:V\Gamma^{\prime}\to\{1,\ldots,|V\Gamma^{\prime}|\}
, choose a proper numbering
f:V\Gamma\to\{1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\}
with the property that
f(v)\leq f(v^{\prime})
implies
g(\phi(v))\leq g(\phi(v^{\prime}))
. In particular, this means that the sequence
a_{i}=g(\phi(f^{-1}(i)))
is monotonically non-decreasing.

We claim that for every edge
vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma
such that
|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|>2\kappa^{2}
, there is some edge
ww^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfying

•

d_{Y}(\phi(v),w))\leq 2\kappa
, and

•

|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|\leq 2\kappa^{2}|g(w)-g(w^{\prime})|
.

Given the claim we complete the proof as follows. By the first condition above there is a uniform bound (call it
C
) on the number of edges
vv^{\prime}
which yield the same edge
ww^{\prime}
.
Thus for
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\ell^{p}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\min_{f}\left(\sum_{vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma}|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{g}\left(C\sum_{ww^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}}(2\kappa^{2}|g(w)-g(w^{\prime})|)^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma}(2\kappa^{2})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa^{2}\left(C^{1/p}\ell^{p}(\Gamma^{\prime})+|E\Gamma|^{1/p}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell^{p}_{Y}(\max\{|V\Gamma^{\prime}|,|E\Gamma|\}).

Note that the last step uses the assumption that
\ell^{p}_{Y}(r)\gtrsim r^{1/p}
. For
p=\infty
,

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\min_{f}\max_{vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma}|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\min_{g}\max_{ww^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}}2\kappa^{2}|g(w)-g(w^{\prime})|+2\kappa^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa^{2}\left(\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\prime})+1\right).

It remains to prove the claim. Suppose
|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|>2\kappa^{2}
. Without loss of generality, assume
k=f(v)<f(v^{\prime})=k+l
. Since
f
and
g
are injective, we have

\left\lvert g(\phi(\{f^{-1}(k),\ldots,f^{-1}(k+\ell)\}))\right\rvert\geq\ell/\kappa

and
g(\phi(f^{-1}(k)))\leq\ldots\leq g(\phi(f^{-1}(k+\ell)))
, so
g(\phi(f^{-1}(k+\ell)))-g(\phi(f^{-1}(k)))\geq\ell/\kappa
. In particular,
\phi(v)\neq\phi(v^{\prime})
.

Since
vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma
,
d_{Y}(\phi(v),\phi(v^{\prime}))\leq 2\kappa
. Let
\phi(v)=w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}=\phi(v^{\prime})
be the vertices of a path in
\Gamma^{\prime}
with
m\leq 2\kappa
. Since
g(w_{m})-g(w_{0})\geq\ell/\kappa
, there is some
i
such that
|g(w_{i+1})-g(w_{i})|\geq\ell/(2\kappa^{2})
. We choose
w=w_{i}
and
w^{\prime}=w_{i+1}
. By construction
ww^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
,
d_{Y}(\phi(v),w)\leq 2\kappa
and
|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})|\leq 2\kappa^{2}|g(w)-g(w^{\prime})|
completing the claim.

For edge cut we use the same proper numbering
f
. Fix
1<k\leq|V\Gamma|
. For convenience define

\displaystyle F_{k}=\left\{vv^{\prime}\in E\Gamma\ \left|\ f(v)\leq k<f(v^{\prime})\right.\right\}

\displaystyle G_{l}=\left\{ww^{\prime}\in E\Gamma^{\prime}\ \left|\ g(w)\leq l<g(w^{\prime})\right.\right\}

and write
f_{k}=|F_{k}|
,
g_{l}=|G_{l}|
. We claim that
f_{k}\leq Cg_{a_{k}}+\kappa\Delta(X)
for some constant
C
which depends only on
\kappa
and
\Delta(Y)
. We recall that
a_{k}=g(\phi(f^{-1}(k)))
.

Given the claim, we see that for
p=\infty
,

\displaystyle\ec^{\infty}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\min_{f}\max_{k}f_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{g}\max_{l}Cg_{l}+\kappa\Delta(X)

\displaystyle\leq C\ec^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\prime})+\kappa\Delta(X).

When
p\in[1,\infty)
, using the fact that for any
l
there are at most
\kappa
values of
k
such that
a_{k}=l
, we have

\displaystyle\ec^{p}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\min_{f}\left(\sum_{k}f_{k}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{g}\left(\sum_{l}\kappa\left(Cg_{l}+\kappa\Delta(X)\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{1/p}\min_{g}\left(\sum_{l}\left(g_{l}\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}+C\kappa^{1+1/p}\Delta(X)|V\Gamma^{\prime}|^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\kappa^{1/p}\max\left\{\min_{g}\left(\sum_{l}\left(g_{l}\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p},\kappa\Delta(X)|V\Gamma^{\prime}|^{1/p}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ec^{p}_{Y}(|V\Gamma^{\prime}|).

Note that the final step uses the assumption made at the start of the proof that
\ec^{p}_{Y}(r)\gtrsim r^{1/p}
.

Now we prove the claim. For each edge in the set
F_{k}
, we have
g(\phi(v))\leq a_{k}\leq g(\phi(v^{\prime}))
. Since
\phi
has preimages of cardinality at most
\kappa
there are at most
\kappa\Delta(X)
edges in
E\Gamma
with an end vertex
w
such that
g(\phi(w))=a_{k}
.

If
vv^{\prime}\in F_{k}
is not one of these edges, then
g(\phi(v))\leq a_{k}<g(\phi(v^{\prime}))
. There is a path in
\Gamma^{\prime}
connecting
\phi(v)
to
\phi(v^{\prime})
with length at most
2\kappa
, and there is some edge
ww^{\prime}
on this path such that
g(w)\leq a_{k}<g(w^{\prime})
.

As in the previous argument, since
d_{Y}(\phi(v),w))\leq 2\kappa
there is a uniform bound
C
on the number of edges
vv^{\prime}
which yield the same edge
vv^{\prime}
via this process. Thus,

g_{a_{k}}\geq\frac{f_{k}-\kappa\Delta(X)}{C},

as required.
∎

4
Calculations

4.1
Proof of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5

We briefly recall the definition of the separation profile.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. A subset
C\subseteq V\Gamma
is a
\varepsilon
-cutset
if every connected component of
\Gamma\setminus C
contains at most
\varepsilon|V\Gamma|
vertices. The
\varepsilon
-cutsize
of a finite graph
\Gamma
,
(\cut^{\varepsilon}(\Gamma))
is the minimal cardinality of a
\varepsilon
-cutset.

The
\varepsilon
-separation profile
of a graph
X
is the function

\sep_{X}^{\varepsilon}(r)=\max\left\{\cut^{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}

One important result from
[
BST12
]
is that for all
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,

\ \sep_{X}^{\varepsilon}(r)\simeq\sep_{X}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(r).

The key observation here is the following. A similar argument appears in
[
DV03
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\leq\ec^{\infty}_{X}(\varepsilon r)+\Delta(X)\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r).

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\leq X
with
|\Gamma|\leq r
. Let
C
be a
\varepsilon
-cutset of
\Gamma
satisfying
|\Gamma|\leq\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r)
, and let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be the connected components of
\Gamma-C
. Choose bijections
\sigma_{i}:A_{i}^{0}\to\{1,\ldots,|A_{i}|\}
such that
\max_{i}|\{vw\in EA_{i}:\ \sigma_{i}(v)\leq i<\sigma_{i}(w)\}|=\ec^{\infty}(A_{i})
. Now we define a linear arrangement
\sigma
of
V\Gamma^{\prime}
. For each
v\in A_{i}
, define

\sigma(v)=|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{i-1}|+\sigma_{i}(v)

and map the vertices in
C
to
\{\sum_{i=0}^{k}|A_{i}|+1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\}
in any bijective way.

For all
|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{i-1}|\leq j<|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{i}|
, we have

\displaystyle|\{vw\in E\Gamma:\ \sigma(v)\leq j<\sigma(w)\}|

\displaystyle\leq\ec^{\infty}(A_{i})+|\{vw\in E\Gamma:\{v,w\}\cap C\neq\emptyset\}|

\displaystyle\leq\ec^{\infty}(A_{i})+\Delta(X)\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r).

Moreover, for
j>|A_{1}|+\ldots+|A_{k}|
,

|\{vw\in E\Gamma:\ \sigma(v)\leq j<\sigma(w)\}|\leq\Delta(X)\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r).

As this holds for all
\Gamma
, and
|A_{i}|\leq\varepsilon r
for all
i
, we see that

\cw_{X}(r)\leq\ec^{\infty}_{X}(\varepsilon r)+\Delta(X)\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r).\qed

Repeatedly applying the above lemma (with
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
) we get a strong control on
\ec^{\infty}
in terms of separation.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph. Then

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\leq\Delta(X)\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}\sep(2^{-i}r)\leq\Delta(X)(1+\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil)\sep_{X}(r).

Proof.

Repeatedly applying Lemma
4.2
, we see that

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\leq\ec^{\infty}_{X}\left(\frac{r}{2^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}}\right)+\Delta(X)\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}\sep(2^{-i}r).

As
2^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}\geq r
and the cutwidth of a graph with at most
1
vertex is
0
, the result follows.
∎

When the separation profile is known to be sufficiently nice, the above bound can be improved.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph such that
\sep_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
where
a\in[0,1]
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}r^{a}\log(r)^{b}&\textup{if}&a>0,\\
\log(r)^{b+1}&\textup{if}&a=0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.3

\displaystyle\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(X)\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}\sep(2^{-i}r)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(X)\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}C(C2^{-i}r)^{a}\log(C2^{-i}r)^{b}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(X)C^{1+a}\log(Cr)^{b}\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}(2^{-i}r)^{a}.

If
a>0
, then
\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}(2^{-i}r)^{a}\leq\frac{r^{a}}{1-2^{-a}}
, so

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\leq C^{\prime}r^{a}\log(Cr)^{b}.

If
a=0
, then
\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil}(2^{-i}r)^{a}=1+\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil
, so

\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\leq\Delta(X)C\log(Cr)^{b}(1+\lceil\log_{2}(r)\rceil)\lesssim\log(r)^{b+1}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Corollary
4.4
, if
\sep_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
, then

\cw_{X}(r)\simeq\ec^{\infty}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}.

If, in addition
\sep_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}
, then by Theorem
1.3

r^{a}\log(r)^{b}\lesssim\sep_{X}(r)\lesssim\cw_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{a}\log(r)^{b}.\qed

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.5
, which immediately follows from the following:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. We have

\displaystyle\vc^{p}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\left\lfloor\frac{|V\Gamma|}{3}\right\rfloor^{\frac{1}{p}}\cut^{\frac{2}{3}}(\Gamma).

Proof.

Choose a bijection
f:V\Gamma\to\{1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\}
, and note that for each
\frac{|V\Gamma|}{3}\leq i\leq\frac{2|V\Gamma|}{3}
,

C_{i}:=\left\{v\in V\Gamma\ \left|\ \exists vw\in E\Gamma,\ f(v)\leq i<f(w)\right.\right\}

is a
\frac{2}{3}
cutset of
\Gamma
, since no connected component of
\Gamma\setminus C_{i}
can contain a vertex in both
f^{-1}(\{1,\ldots,i\})
and
f^{-1}(\{i+1,\ldots,|V\Gamma|\})
. As there are at least
\left\lfloor\frac{|V\Gamma|}{3}\right\rfloor
such values of
i
, we have

\vc^{p}(\Gamma)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{|V\Gamma|}{3}\right\rfloor^{\frac{1}{p}}\cut^{\frac{2}{3}}(\Gamma).\qed

4.2
Coarse wirings and graph width

We recall the definition of coarse wirings and the coarse wiring profile from
[
BH21
]
. The definition given here is slightly different to allow situations where the image graph does not have bounded degree. In the bounded degree case, the two definitions are equivalent up to a multiplicative error depending only on maximal degree.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}
be graphs. A
wiring
of
\Gamma
into
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a continuous map
f:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
which maps vertices to vertices and each edge
xy
to a walk
W_{xy}
which starts at
f(x)
and ends at
f(y)
.

A wiring
f
is a
coarse
k
-wiring
if the preimage of each vertex of
V\Gamma^{\prime}
contains at most
k
vertices in
V\Gamma
, and each vertex
v\in V\Gamma^{\prime}
is contained in at most
k
of the walks in
\mathcal{W}=\left\{W_{xy}\ \left|\ xy\in E\Gamma\right.\right\}
.

We consider the
image
of a wiring
\im(f)
to be the graph

\phi(V\Gamma)\cup\bigcup_{xy\in E\Gamma}W_{xy}\leq\Gamma^{\prime}.

The
volume
of a wiring
\vol(f)
is the number of vertices in its image.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph and let
Y
be a graph. We denote by
\wir^{k}(\Gamma\to Y)
the minimal volume of a coarse
k
-wiring of
\Gamma
into
Y
. If no such coarse
k
-wiring exists, we say
\wir^{k}(\Gamma\to Y)=+\infty
.

Remark 4.7
.

The definition of a coarse
k
-wiring in
[
BH21
]
requires that each
edge

e\in E\Gamma^{\prime}
is contained in at most
k
of the walks in
\mathcal{W}=\left\{W_{xy}\ \left|\ xy\in E\Gamma\right.\right\}
. Any
k
-coarse wiring in the sense of
[
BH21
]
is a
2k
-coarse wiring as defined here. When
\Gamma^{\prime}
has bounded degree, any
k
-coarse wiring as defined here is a
k\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})
-coarse wiring in the sense of
[
BH21
]
. We write the definition in this way here as we will want to allow
\Gamma^{\prime}
to have unbounded degree.

We associate two cost functions to the difficulty of coarse wiring one graph into another.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
X
and
Y
be graphs.

We define
\para_{X\to Y}(r)
to be the minimal
k
such that every
\Gamma\leq X
with
|V\Gamma|\leq r
admits a
k
-coarse wiring into
Y
.

For each
k
, we define
\wir^{k}_{X\to Y}(r)=\max\left\{\wir^{k}(\Gamma\to Y)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}
.

Note that when
X
has bounded degree, the upper bound
\para_{X\to Y}(r)\leq\Delta(X)r
is obtained by mapping all of
\Gamma
to a single vertex.

Given a graph
G
, coarse wiring costs can be compared with
\wid^{G,\infty}
in the following way:

Proposition 4.9
.

For any bounded degree graph
X
and any graph
G
,

\wid^{G,\infty}_{X}(r)\leq 2\para_{X\to G}(r).

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\leq X
with
|V\Gamma|\leq r
and let
f:\Gamma\to G
be a
k
-coarse wiring with
k\leq\para_{X\to G}(r)
.

For each
x\in V\Gamma
, set
\overline{f}(x)
to be the set of all vertices in
\bigcup_{xy\in E\Gamma}W_{x,y}
. We claim that
X_{g}=\left\{x\in V\Gamma\ \left|\ g\in\overline{f}(x)\right.\right\}
defines a
G
-decomposition of
\Gamma
.

For each edge
xy\in E\Gamma
,
\{x,y\}\subset X_{g}
for any
g\in VW_{xy}\neq\emptyset
. Next, by construction, for each
x\in V\Gamma
,

\left\{g\in G\ \left|\ x\in X_{g}\right.\right\}=\overline{f}(x)

which induces a connected subgraph (
\bigcup_{xy\in E\Gamma}W_{x,y}
) of
G
.

Finally, for each
g
, there are at most
k
edges
xy\in E\Gamma
such that
g\in W_{x,y}
and therefore at most
2k
vertices
x\in E\Gamma
, such that
g\in\overline{f}(x)
.
∎

We define one more informative variation of
\wid^{G,p}
which is more closely related to the volume of coarse wirings.

Definition 4.10
.

Fix
\ell
. Given a graph
X
and a graph
G
, we define
\wid^{G,p,\ell}(\Gamma)
to be the minimal
p
-norm of a
G
-decomposition
(X_{g})
of
\Gamma
with
\max_{g}|X_{g}|\leq\ell
.

Now, given a graph
X
and a function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, we define

\wid^{G,p,f}_{X}(r)=\max\left\{\wid^{G,p,f(r)}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |V\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}.

Repeating the proof of Proposition
3.2
we get the following:

Proposition 4.11
.

If there is a
\kappa
-regular map
VX\to VY
then there is a constant
C=C(\kappa,\Delta(X))
such that for every graph
G
and function
f
,

\wid^{G,p,f}_{X}(r)\lesssim\wid^{G,p,Cf}_{Y}(r).

We can also compare this variant of
1
-width with the coarse wiring profile by repeating the argument of Proposition
4.9
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph and let
G
be a graph. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\wid^{G,1,\ell}_{X}(r)\lesssim\wir^{k}_{X\to G}(r).

We note that the
\simeq
-class of
\wir^{k}_{X\to G}(r)
can vary depending on the parameter

, as demonstrated in
[

]
.

Question 5.1

Abstract
. We consider a certain class of infinitary rules of inference, called here restriction rules, using of which allows us to deduce complete theories of given models. The first instance of such rules was the ω \omega -rule introduced by Hilbert, and generalizations of the ω \omega -rule were first considered by Henkin. Later on Barwise showed that within countable languages, for any countable model 𝔐 \mathfrak{M} , first-order logic expanded by the corresponding 𝔐 \mathfrak{M} -rule deduces from the diagram of 𝔐 \mathfrak{M} all formulas that are true in all models that include 𝔐 \mathfrak{M} , in particular, it deduces the (relativized) complete theory of 𝔐 \mathfrak{M} . We show that, if the aim is only deducing the complete theory of a given model, these countability assumptions can be omitted. Moreover, similar facts hold for infinitary and higher-order logics, even if these logics are highly incomplete. Finally, we show that Barwise’s theorem in its stronger form, for vocabularies of arbitrary cardinality, holds for infinitary logics ℒ κ , λ \mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda} whenever κ \kappa is supercompact. The note also contains brief historical remarks.

Introduction

True arithmetic

{\mathrm{TA}}
is the complete
first-order theory of
(\omega,0,s,+,\,\cdot\,,<)
,
the standard model of Peano arithmetic
{\mathrm{PA}}

By the classical undefinability theorem of Tarski,

{\mathrm{TA}}
is not arithmetically definable. A sharper

the
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
-fragment of
{\mathrm{TA}}
is arithmetically
definable but only by a formula of complexity higher
than
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
.

Since by Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem,
no recursively enumerable extension

of
{\mathrm{PA}}

proves even its own consistency

, which
is a
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-formula, a fortiori there is no
effective way to describe
{\mathrm{TA}}
. Among non-effective
ways to do this, one approach is to expand the usual
first-order finitary language
\mathscr{L}_{\omega,\omega}
;
the second-order language, or even the first-order
infinitary language
\mathscr{L}_{\omega_{1},\omega}

with countable connectives, suffices to describe
{\mathrm{TA}}

by a single formula (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).
Another approach is, remaining within the language

\mathscr{L}_{\omega,\omega}
, to extend the usual set
of inference rules with a schema of infinite rules,
called the

Recall that, for each formula
\varphi(x)

\omega

\forall x\,\varphi(x)

with

applied

(interpreted by the successor operation):

The

This note provides a small contribution to the area.
The main observation here is that, if our aim is
only to deduce the complete theory of a given model,
the countability assumptions of
[
2
]

can be omitted. Moreover, analogous facts hold for
infinitary and higher-order logics as well, even if
these logics are highly incomplete.

The deductive systems considered here differ from those
that were used by Barwise result only in minor details.
As said, we consider rules with arbitrarily many
premises and use them in languages of arbitrary
cardinalities. We call them
restriction rules
since
they restrict the class of models of a given theory in
the same way as the
\omega
-rule restricts the class
of models of
{\mathrm{PA}}
to the models elementarily equivalent
to the standard model; namely, the resulting complete
theories describe pointwise definable models.
Unlike Barwise, instead of using constants
for elements of a model under consideration, we use terms
(as in the original
\omega
-rule) or even, most generally,
formulas that uniquely characterize the elements; the
notion of the diagram of a model generalizes accordingly.
Also unlike Barwise, we do not treat the generalized diagram
as new logical axioms but rather as axioms of a basic theory
designed to derive the complete theory of the model from it.

For simplicity, we start with the definition of restriction
rules in the term version, which gives us the
\omega
-rule
as an obvious instance. We show that, for a set
S
of terms,
whenever a theory decides whether an atomic formula with
terms in
S
substituting its free variables is true or not,
then the theory becomes complete if we add the corresponding

S
-rule to the usual rules of first-order logic
(Theorem
1
). Based on this, we establish
a semantic completeness result stating that a suitable

S
-rule allows one to deduce the complete theory

\mathrm{Th}(\mathfrak{A})
of a given model
\mathfrak{A}

from an appropriate, diagram-like fragment of the theory
(Theorem
2
).

Further, we provide the general version of restriction
rules using uniqueness formulas and note that the term
version and the general version are essentially equivalent.
Then we formulate the general relativized versions of
two previous completeness results, syntactic
(Theorem
3
) and semantic
(Theorem
4
), where the latter
relates to the theories of pointwise definable models.
As an example, we consider an extension of
{\mathrm{ZFC}}
,
Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory, endowed with the
corresponding restriction rule and show that it gives
the complete theory of
L
, Gödel’s universe of
constructible sets (Proposition
1
).
Finally, we point out that restriction rules naturally
lead to certain polymodal logics in the same way as
the
\omega
-rule leads to the well-known logic
{\mathrm{GLP}}
.

In the last section, we extend this apparatus to
higher logics. Mainly we consider first-order infinitary
languages
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
. As the
corresponding restriction rules deal with formulas
in
<\lambda
parameters, the universal quantifiers
in their conclusions are also
({<}\lambda)
-ary.
We show that the basic deductive system
(consisting of immediate generalizations of the usual
logical axioms and rules) strengthened with a suitable
restriction rule provides analogous syntactic and
semantic completeness results as in the finitary case
(Theorems
5

and
6
), even if this logic
without the restriction rule is highly incomplete.
Moreover, we consider the deductive system that includes
additional axioms and rules (related to distributivity
and choice) and show that if
\kappa
is supercompact
then Barwise’s theorem holds in

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}

in a stronger form, with vocabularies of arbitrary
cardinality (Theorem
7
).
Finally, we briefly discuss the case of second-order
(finitary) languages and their appropriate restriction
rules, consider the derivation of true second-order
arithmetic as an example, and point out that this
machinery extends to higher-order (even infinitary)
languages.

The note also contains short overview on earlier
results and poses some questions.

Let us remark that mathematical side of this note
is quite easy and standard; our purpose is rather
to present the idea of restriction rules allowing us
to get the complete theories of arbitrary models, with
the expectation that its further development may lead
to finer constructions.

2.
Restriction rules, the term form

The

\mathrm{T}\vdash\neg\,\varphi

(iv)

Theorem 1

(Syntactic Completeness)
.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be a theory and
S

Assume that for every atomic formula
\varphi

\mathrm{T}\vdash\bar{\varphi}\;\text{ or }\;\mathrm{T}\vdash\neg\,\bar{\varphi}

whenever
\bar{\varphi}
is the sentence obtained from

\varphi
by a substitution of some terms of
S

instead of all its free variables.
Then for every sentence
\sigma

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\sigma\;\text{ or }\;\ \mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\sigma,

i.e., the theory
[\mathrm{T},\vdash_{S}]
is complete.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to verify that
for every formula
\psi
,

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\psi}\;\text{ or }\;\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\bar{\psi}

whenever
\bar{\psi}
is obtained from
\psi

in the described way. Let us prove this
by induction on construction of
\psi
.

Atomic formulas
.

By assumption (and since
\vdash_{S}
includes
\vdash
).

Negation
.

If the claim holds for
\varphi

then it trivially holds for
\neg\,\varphi
.

Conjunction
.

Let
\psi
be
\varphi_{0}\wedge\varphi_{1}
.
Fix some
\bar{\psi}
; then
\bar{\psi}
is

\bar{\varphi_{0}}\wedge\bar{\varphi_{1}}
with the
inherited
\bar{\varphi_{0}}
and
\bar{\varphi_{1}}
.
We have two cases:

Case 1

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\varphi_{0}}
and

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\varphi_{1}}
. Then

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\psi}
by the conjunction rule.

Case 2
.

\mathrm{T}\not\vdash_{S}\bar{\varphi}

for some
\varphi\in\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}
. Then

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\bar{\varphi}
(for this
\bar{\varphi}
)
by inductive hypothesis. Therefore, since

\neg\,\bar{\varphi}\to\neg\,\bar{\psi}
is an instance
of the conjunction axiom in contraposition,

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\bar{\psi}
by modus ponens.

Universal quantifier
.

Let
\psi
be
\forall x\,\varphi(x,v,\ldots)
.
Fix some
\bar{\psi}
; then
\bar{\psi}
is

\forall x\,\varphi(x,t,\ldots)

\varphi

has the free variable
x
and the inherited

. Denote this

Again we have two cases:

Case 1
.

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\chi}

Then
\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\bar{\psi}
by the

-rule.

Case 2
.

\mathrm{T}\not\vdash_{S}\bar{\chi}
for some
\bar{\chi}
.
Then
\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\bar{\chi}
(for this
\bar{\chi}
)
by inductive hypothesis. Therefore,

\mathrm{T}\vdash_{S}\neg\,\bar{\psi}
by the generalization rule.

The theorem is proved.

Relativized restriction rules

Restriction rules can be given in a more general,
relativized form. In the case, the deduced complete
theory turns out to be “localized”, i.e., relativized;
this method allows one to work within a larger theory.

If
S
is a set of closed terms and
U
a unary predicate
symbol, the
S
-rule relativized to
U
is the
following schema of rules, for each formula
\varphi

in one free variable:

\dfrac{\{\varphi^{U}(t):t\in S\}}{(\forall x\,\varphi(x))^{U}}

where, as usual,
\psi^{U}
denotes the relativization
of
\psi
to
U
. The provability generated by
\vdash

and the
S
-rule relativized to
U
will be denoted
by
\vdash^{U}_{S}
. Note that the former
S
-rule can
be obtained from this relativized form by adding

\forall x\,U(x)
.

A slight modification of the previous arguments proves
the analogs of the above results; we do not write up
them here because in the next section we shall give an
even more general definition of restriction rules.

3.
Restriction rules, the general form

Now we formulate restriction rules in a general form,
without assuming terms in the language under consideration.

If
\theta(x)
is a formula in one free variable
x
,
let us say that
\theta
is a
uniqueness formula
in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)

iff the system proves that
there exists exactly one
x
satisfying
\theta(x)
:

\mathrm{T}\;\vdash\;\exists x\,(\theta(x)\wedge\,\forall y\,(\theta(y)\to x=y)).

Let
\Theta
be a set of uniqueness formulas in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
.
The
restriction rule given by
\Theta
,
or the
\Theta
-
rule
for short, is the schema
of rules that states, for each formula
\varphi
in
a free variable
x
:

\frac{\bigl\{\exists x\,(\varphi(x)\wedge\theta(x)):\theta\in\Theta\bigr\}}{\forall x\,\varphi(x)}.

(Note that, since
\Theta
consists of uniqueness
formulas, we can alternatively define the
\Theta
-rule
by changing the formulas

\exists x\,(\varphi(x)\wedge\theta(x))

in the premise to the formulas

\forall x\,(\theta(x)\to\varphi(x))

without changing the resulting deductive system.)

Likewise, the
restriction rule given by
\Theta

relativized to
U
, shortly, the
(\Theta,U)
-rule
, is the schema

\frac{\bigl\{(\exists x\,(\varphi(x)\wedge\theta(x)))^{U}:\theta\in\Theta\bigr\}}{(\forall x\,\varphi(x))^{U}}.

The symbols
\vdash_{\Theta}
and
\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}

denote the provability generated by
\vdash
and the

\Theta
-rule, respectively, the
(\Theta,U)
-rule.

As it is easy to see, both proposed versions of restriction
rules, one using terms and another using uniqueness
formulas, express essentially the same and are mutually
changeable in the following sense.

Let

(i)

(ii)

For any set

[T,\vdash_{\Theta}]=[\mathrm{T},\vdash_{S(\Theta)}]

Proof.

(i).
If

The

To distinguish between the syntax of modal

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\omega}

is strongly complete if
\kappa
is strongly compact,

(vi)

the standard system of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}

is strongly complete if
\kappa
is strongly compact.

In contrast to these completeness results,
Scott’s undefinability theorem states that
the set
\mathrm{Val}(\mathscr{L}_{\kappa^{+},\kappa^{+}}(\in))

is not definable over
(H_{\kappa^{+}},\in)

even by any formula of the language

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa^{+},\kappa^{+}}(\in)
itself,
thus showing the language does not admit
any reasonably definable complete deductive system
(see
[
24
]
or

[
11
]
, App. C, II).

In order to formulate variants of restriction rules
that would be appropriate for languages

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
, note first that
if
\lambda>\omega
, such rules must take into account
infinitely many parameters in the formulas.
As a simple particular case, the (non-relativized) term
form of restriction rule schema given by a set
S
of
terms would state:

\frac{\bigl\{\varphi(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}:(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}\in S^{\gamma}\bigr\}}{\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\varphi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}},

for all formulas
\varphi
in an arbitrary number

\gamma<\lambda
of parameters.
In the general setting, if
U
is a unary predicate
and
\Theta
a set of uniqueness formulas in a system

(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
under consideration,
the
(\Theta,U)
-restriction rule
is the schema of
rules that, for each formula
\varphi
in free variables

(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}
,
\gamma<\lambda
, states:

\frac{\bigl\{\bigl(\bigexists_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\theta_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\wedge\varphi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}\bigr)\bigr)^{U}:(\theta_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}\in\Theta^{\gamma}\bigr\}}{\bigl(\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\varphi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}\bigr)^{U}}.

Now we are going to generalize the results above,
Theorems
3

and
4
, allowing to obtain, for
a given pointwise
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
-definable
model, its complete
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
-theory.
Surprisingly, such a generalization is very immediate,
holds for any infinitary language even it is highly
incomplete like
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa^{+},\kappa^{+}}
,
and does not requires nothing but the basic deductive
system of the language under consideration, to which
we add the corresponding restriction rule.

For this, first observe the following fact.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be a theory and
\vdash
the basic provability
of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
, and let

\gamma<\kappa
and
\delta<\lambda
.
If
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
proves that

(\theta_{\beta})_{\beta<\delta}

consists of uniqueness formulas, then
it proves the following equivalences,
for all formulas
\varphi,\varphi_{\alpha}
:

(i)

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\neg\,\varphi\bigr)\leftrightarrow\neg\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\varphi\bigr),

(ii)

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\varphi_{\alpha}\bigr)\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\varphi_{\alpha}\bigr),

(iii)

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}\!y_{\alpha}\,\varphi\bigr)\leftrightarrow\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}\!y_{\alpha}\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\varphi\bigr).

Consequently, for any formula
\psi
, the part

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\ldots)

of the formula
\bar{\psi}
can be
pushed down inside, or brought out it.

Proof.

The (
\to
) parts of each of (i)–(iii) use that

\forall x\forall y\,(\theta_{\beta}(x)\wedge\theta_{\beta}(y)\to x=y)

while their (
\leftarrow
) parts use that

\exists x\,\theta_{\beta}(x)

whenever
\theta_{\beta}
is a uniqueness formula.
∎

Theorem 5

(Syntactic Completeness)
.

Let
\kappa\geq\lambda\geq\omega
be cardinals,

\mathrm{T}
a theory and
\vdash
the basic provability
of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
,

\Theta
a set of uniqueness formulas in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
,
and
U
a unary predicate symbol. Assume that

(i)

\mathrm{T}\vdash\forall x\,(\theta(x)\to U(x))

for all
\theta\in\Theta
, and

(ii)

for every atomic formula
\varphi
in
n+1
variables
and
\theta_{0},\ldots,\theta_{n}\in\Theta
,

\mathrm{T}\vdash\bar{\varphi}\;\text{ or }\;\mathrm{T}\vdash\neg\,\bar{\varphi}

where
\bar{\varphi}
is the sentence

\exists x_{0}\ldots\exists x_{n}\,(\varphi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\wedge\theta_{0}(x_{0})\wedge\ldots\wedge\theta_{n}(x_{n})).

Then for every sentence
\sigma
,

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma^{U}\;\text{ or }\;\ \mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\sigma^{U},

i.e., the theory
\{\sigma^{U}:\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma\}

is a complete theory relativized to
U
.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to verify that
for every formula
\psi(x_{\beta})_{\beta<\delta}

of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}

in
\delta<\lambda
variables
x_{\beta}
, we have

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\psi}^{\,U}\;\text{ or }\;\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bar{\psi}^{\,U}

whenever
\bar{\psi}
is the sentence

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}x_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(x_{\beta})\wedge\psi(x_{\beta})_{\beta<\delta}\bigr)

for some
\delta
-sequence

(\theta_{\beta})_{\beta<\delta}
in
\Theta
.
Let us prove this by induction on construction of
\psi
.

Atomic formulas
.

By assumption (and since
\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}

includes
\vdash
).

Negation
.

Let
\psi
be
\neg\,\varphi
.
Fix some
\bar{\psi}
; then by
Lemma
5
(i),
in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
,
\bar{\psi}
is equivalent to

\neg\,\bar{\varphi}
with the inherited
\bar{\varphi}
.
By induction hypothesis, the claim holds for
\bar{\varphi}
,
and then it trivially holds for
\neg\,\bar{\varphi}
,
and so for
\bar{\psi}
.

Conjunction
.

Let
\psi
be
\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\varphi_{\alpha}
.
Fix some
\bar{\psi}
; then by
Lemma
5
(ii),
in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
,
\bar{\psi}
is equivalent to

\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}
with
the inherited
\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}
.
We have two cases:

Case 1
.

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

for all
\alpha<\gamma
.
Then

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

by the conjunction rule, and so

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\psi}
.

Case 2
.

\mathrm{T}\not\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

for some
\alpha<\gamma
. Then

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

(for this
\alpha
) by inductive hypothesis.
Therefore, since

\neg\,\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}\to\neg\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

is an instance of the conjunction axiom in contraposition,

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\bigwedge_{\alpha<\gamma}\bar{\varphi}_{\alpha}

by modus ponens, and so

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bar{\psi}
.

Universal quantifier
.

Let
\psi
be

\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\varphi(x_{\alpha},y_{\beta})_{\alpha<\gamma,\beta<\delta}.

Fix some
\bar{\psi}
; then by
Lemma
5
(iii),
in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
,
\bar{\psi}
is equivalent to

\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\chi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}

where
\chi
is the formula

\bigexists_{\beta<\delta}\!y_{\beta}\,\bigl(\bigwedge_{\beta<\delta}\theta_{\beta}(y_{\beta})\wedge\varphi(x_{\alpha},y_{\beta})_{\alpha<\gamma,\beta<\delta}\bigr)

with the inherited
(\theta_{\beta})_{\beta<\delta}
.
Again we have two cases:

Case 1
.

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\chi}
for all
\bar{\chi}
.
Then

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\chi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}

by the
(\Theta,U)
-rule,
and so
\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\psi}
.

Case 2
.

\mathrm{T}\not\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\bar{\chi}
for some
\bar{\chi}
.
Then
\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bar{\chi}

(for this
\bar{\chi}
) by inductive hypothesis.
Therefore,

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bigforall_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}\,\chi(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}

by the generalization rule,
and so
\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\neg\,\bar{\psi}
.

The theorem is proved.
∎

Noting that
(\Theta,U)
-rule preserves validity
in every model in which the submodel definable by
U

is pointwise definable by formulas in
\Theta
,
we get a straightforward generalization of
Theorem
4

(or
2
):

Theorem 6

(Semantic Completeness)
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a model pointwise definable
by formulas of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}

in
\Theta=\{\theta_{a}:a\in A\}
,

\mathrm{T}
a theory,
\vdash
the standard system,
and
U
a unary predicate symbol.
Assume that

(i)

\mathrm{T}\vdash\forall x\,(\theta_{a}(x)\to U(x))

for all

, and

(ii)

\mathrm{Dg}^{U}_{\Theta}(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq\{\sigma^{U}:\mathrm{T}\vdash\sigma\}\subseteq\mathrm{Th}^{U}(\mathfrak{A}).

Then

\{\sigma^{U}:\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma\}=\mathrm{Th}^{U}(\mathfrak{A}).

Proof.

Modify the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Although we do not discuss here possible examples or
applications,
concerning analogs of the model
L
in infinitary and
second-order languages, let us shortly mention the standard
result in
[
9
]
,
[
30
]
, and the recent
article
[
25
]
.

Now we consider a special case of

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
in which an analog
of above mentioned Barwise’s result holds
even in a stronger form.

Benda proved
[
4
]
that

\kappa
is supercompact iff the language

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}
has a kind of
“compactness for omitting type” in the following sense:
for every theory
\mathrm{T}
and type
\Gamma(x,y)

in two variables, if each subtype

\Gamma_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{\kappa}(\Gamma)
has
a model of
\mathrm{T}
that realizes
x
and omits
y
,
then the whole
\Gamma
has such a model.
Using this fact, we can generalize Barwise’s
completeness result to
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}

with supercompact
\kappa
.

Theorem 7

(Strong Semantic Completeness)
.

Let
\lambda\leq\kappa\leq\mu
,

\kappa
a
\mu
-supercompact cardinal,

\tau
a vocabulary containing a unary predicate
symbol
U
,
|\tau|\leq\mu
,

\mathrm{T}
a theory of
\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}(\tau)
,

\mathfrak{A}
a model of
\tau
pointwise definable by

\Theta
, and let
\vdash
be the standard system.
Assume that

\mathrm{T}\vdash\forall x\,(\theta(x)\to U(x))

for all
\theta\in\Theta
, and

\mathrm{Dg}^{U}_{\Theta}(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq\{\sigma^{U}:\mathrm{T}\vdash\sigma\}\subseteq\mathrm{Th}^{U}(\mathfrak{A}).

Then for every sentence
\sigma
of the language

\mathscr{L}_{\kappa,\lambda}(\tau)
we have:

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma\;\;\text{iff}\;\;\;\mathrm{T}\vDash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma.

Proof.

Only if.

Assume
\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma
. If

\mathfrak{B}\vDash\mathrm{T}
and
\mathfrak{A}
is
the submodel of
\mathfrak{B}
defined by
U
, prove

\mathfrak{B}\vDash\sigma
by induction on complexity
of
\sigma
starting from
\mathrm{Dg}^{U}_{\Theta}(\mathfrak{A})

by using that the
(\Theta,U)
-rule preserves validity
in such models
\mathfrak{B}
.

If.

Assume that for all models
\mathfrak{B}
such that

\mathfrak{B}\vDash\mathrm{T}
and
\mathfrak{A}
is
the submodel of
\mathfrak{B}
defined by
U
,
we have
\mathfrak{B}\vDash\sigma
, and prove

\mathrm{T}\vdash^{U}_{\Theta}\sigma
. Thus in contraposition,
assume that
\neg\,\sigma
is consistent with

(\mathrm{T},\vdash^{U}_{\Theta})
and prove that
there exists a model
\mathfrak{B}
such that

\mathfrak{B}\vDash\mathrm{T}+\neg\,\sigma
in which

U
defines the submodel
\mathfrak{A}
, thus

\mathfrak{B}
omits the
1
-type

\Gamma(x):=\{U(x)\wedge\neg\,\theta(x):\theta\in\Theta\},

in other words, satisfies the sentence

\forall x\,(U(x)\to\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta(x))

of the language
\mathscr{L}_{\mu,\omega}
).

Let
\Theta_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{\kappa}(\Theta)
,

U_{0}
a unary predicate symbol, and
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}

the expansion of
\mathrm{T}
by the formula

\forall x\,(U_{0}(x)\;\leftrightarrow\;\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta_{0}}\theta(x)).

Then
\neg\,\sigma
is consistent with

(\mathrm{T}^{\prime},\vdash^{U}_{\Theta})
and a fortiori with

(\mathrm{T}^{\prime},\vdash)
. Therefore, since
\kappa
is

\mu
-strongly compact, there exists a model
of
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}+\neg\,\sigma
omitting the type

\Gamma_{0}(x):=\{U(x)\wedge\neg\,\theta(x):\theta\in\Theta_{0}\}.

As
\Gamma_{0}
is an arbitrary small subtype of
\Gamma
,
by the compactness for omitting type we conclude that
there exists a model of
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}+\neg\,\sigma
omitting
the whole type
\Gamma
, as required.
∎

Second-order logics

A second-order formula
\theta(X)
in
a single
(n+1)
-ary relation variable
X
is
a
uniqueness formula
in
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
iff

\mathrm{T}\;\vdash\;\forall X\forall Y\,(\theta(X)\wedge\theta(Y)\to\forall x_{0}\ldots\forall x_{n}(X(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\,\leftrightarrow\,Y(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})),

i.e., the system
(\mathrm{T},\vdash)
proves that the formula

\theta
defines a single
(n+1)
-ary relation.
Clearly, this happens iff there exists
a (second-order) formula
\psi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})

having first-order parameters
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}

and no other parameters and such that

\mathrm{T}\;\vdash\;\forall X(\theta(X)\,\leftrightarrow\,\forall x_{0}\ldots\forall x_{n}(X(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\,\leftrightarrow\,\psi(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}))).

Uniqueness formulas in function variables are
defined similarly.

To simplify the notation, below we formulate only
the non-relativized version of the second-order
restriction rules.

Given a set
\Theta
of uniqueness formulas in
a second-order variable
X
of a fixed arity
n
,
the
\Theta
-rule of arity
n
is the schema
consisting of the following rules:

\frac{\{\varphi(X)\wedge\theta(X):\theta\in\Theta\}}{\forall X\,\varphi(X)}.

By the
\Theta
-rule
, we mean
the union of these schemas for all finite
n
.

Second-order provability involves, besides the usual
first-order axioms and rules, the comprehension axioms
and the generalization rule for second-order quantifiers.
Considering this, it is easy to obtain a second-order
syntactic completeness theorem along with a proof
essentially repeating that of Theorem
1
,
where second-order uniqueness formulas act like additional
predicate symbols; we leave the exact formulation and
the proof to the reader.

Regarding semantics, recall that along with
the
full
semantics, where the second-order
quantifiers involve all relations and functions,
there is the
general
or
Henkin
semantics
which uses only relations and functions in a given class
(see, e.g.,
[
45
]
).
Obviously, if the universe of a model
\mathfrak{A}
has the
cardinality
\kappa
while the set
\Theta
of second-order
uniqueness formulas the cardinality
<2^{\kappa}

(e.g., if both the model and the language are countable),
we have no guarantee to deduce the second-order theory
of
\mathfrak{A}
in the full semantics by means of
the
\Theta
-rule.
The correct version of Lemma
2
is:

The second-order
\Theta
-rule preserves validity
in every model in the Henkin semantics based on
\Theta
.

With this in mind, a second-order semantic completeness
theorem analogous to Theorem
2
can be
obtained in the similar way. We leave the exact formulation
and its proof to the reader and give here only two examples
related to arithmetic.

An example in arithmetic.

Below
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}
denotes, as usual, the extension of

{\mathrm{PA}}
(or of the Robinson arithmetic
\mathrm{Q}
) by
the second-order induction and comprehension schemas.
The set is
analytical
iff it is definable by
a formula of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}
.
{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}
is
second-order
true arithmetic
, i.e., the complete second-order
theory of the standard model of arithmetic.

Let also
\tau
be the vocabulary of arithmetic expanded
by symbols for all relations and operations on
\omega
,
and let
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}(\tau)
be the theory in
\tau

extending
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}
by all the atomic and negated
atomic sentences true in
\omega
, i.e., if
R_{S}
is
the relation symbols for
S\subseteq\omega^{m}
and
t_{n}

is the term for
n\in\omega
, then
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}(\tau)

contains
R_{S}(t_{n_{0}},\ldots,t_{n_{m-1}})

if
(n_{0},\ldots,n_{m-1})\in S
, and

\neg\,R_{S}(t_{n_{0}},\ldots,t_{n_{m-1}})
otherwise.
Let
{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}(\tau)
denote the complete second-order
theory of the standard model of arithmetic in
\tau
.
By
true theory of analytical sets
we mean
the theory of second-order arithmetic with
the Henkin semantic based on analytical sets.

Proposition 2
.

(i)

If
\Theta
consists of uniqueness formulas
of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}
, then
[{\mathrm{Z}}_{2},\vdash_{\Theta}]
is
true theory of analytical sets.

(ii)

If
\Theta
consists of uniqueness formulas
of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}(\tau)
, then

[{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}(\tau),\vdash_{\Theta}]={\mathrm{TA}}_{2}(\tau),

and a fortiori,
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}(\tau)\vdash_{\Theta}{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}
.

Remark 5
.

Let us notice a few things in this connection.

1.
The generalization of Post’s theorem to
the analytical hierarchy shows that
{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}
is not
analytical, i.e., not definable by any single
second-order formula of arithmetic.

2.

{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}
and even the theory of analytical sets depends on
the meta-theory. E.g., the projective determinacy
{\mathrm{PD}}
is
expressible as a schema in the language of
\mathrm{Z}_{2}
;
the continuum hypothesis
{\mathrm{CH}}
also is expressible by
a second-order sentence, in fact, by a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-sentence
in the empty vocabulary.

3.
Even validity in second-order logic is not second-order
definable over
\omega
nor over any second-order
characterizable structure (the latters include

\mathbb{R}
,
\omega_{1}
, the first inaccessible
cardinal, etc.), see
[
46
]
.
This follows from the two facts:
the set of (Gödel’s numbers of) valid second-order
sentences is a complete
\Pi_{2}
-set
[
44
]

while every class of models that is definable by
a second-order formula is a
\Delta_{2}
-set

[
45
]
(where
\Pi_{2}
and
\Delta_{2}

relate to the Lévy hierarchy in
{\mathrm{ZFC}}
).

4.
As shown in
[
28
]
(see also

[
45
,
46
]
),
third-order logic, as well as much higher-order
logics, is expressible in second-order logic.

5.
By
[
40
]
, the recursively restricted

\omega
-rule suffices to deduce
{\mathrm{TA}}
from
{\mathrm{PA}}
.
Of course, the
\omega
-rule does not deduce
{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}

from
{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}
, and Shoenfield asked whether the recursively
restricted
\omega
-rule deduces the same fragment of

{\mathrm{TA}}_{2}
as the full
\omega
-rule; the affirmative
answer was given in
[
43
]
.

Further extensions of this machinery to higher-order
infinitary logics are possible.

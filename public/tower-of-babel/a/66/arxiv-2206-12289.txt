Mathematical modeling of multilane vehicular traffic by discrete kinetic theory approach
Mohamed Zagour

Abstract
This paper deals with the modeling and numerical simulations of multilane vehicular traffic according to the discrete kinetic theory approach. The nonlinear additive interactions and external actions such as tollgates as well traffic signs are considered and modeled. The well-posedness of the related Cauchy problem for the spatially homogeneous case has been proved by using Banach fixed-point theory. Numerical simulations are carried out to validate the improved model in the cases of spatially homogeneous and inhomogeneous problems.

Multilane vehicular traffic, Discrete kinetic theory, Nonlinearity, Banach fixed-point theory, Finite volume method.

Introduction

As it is known, the mathematical approach to vehicular traffic modeling is developed on three representation scales: microscopic, macroscopic, and kinetic. However, the critical analysis proposed in
[BDS11]
states that none of those scale approaches is totally satisfactory. Precisely, each one presents technical and conceptual advantages and disadvantages. Consequently, a multiscale approach is a necessity to obtain a detailed description of the dynamics of vehicles on the road. The author in
[Do2]
compliments this point of view, where a hybrid model is proposed with detailed modeling of the dynamics of the micro-systems which is implemented into a macroscopic hyperbolic equation. An important reference to clarify research activity in the field is the survey of
[HEL01]
on the physics and modeling of multi-particle systems. While, the critical paper by
[DAG95]
provides some drawbacks of the driver-vehicle micro-system, where interactions can even modify the behavior of the driver and his/her ability is conditioned by the local flow conditions. Next, this paper has generated various discussions and reactions to account for the aforementioned criticisms
[BDF12]
;
[HJ09]
;
[PSTV15]
;
[PSTV17]
. We mention that the approach of
[BDF12]
has been further developed by various papers, for instance by a multiscale continuous velocity and activity variables for vehicular traffic flow
[CNZ19]
.

This paper deals with the mathematical modeling of vehicular traffic flow along a multilane road on the basis of the kinetic theory. Precisely, we refer to Reference
[BG09]
, where the authors proposed a discrete kinetic model with the main features:

1.

The approach is developed at the kinetic scale where the modeling of the interacting vehicles is considered at the microscopic scale with a binary interactions;

2.

The velocity variable is assumed to be discrete to overtake the drawback of vehicles number might not large enough to assure continuity of the probability distributions over the micro-states;

3.

The quality of the road conditions is taken into account by an additional parameter which takes values in the interval
\displaystyle[0,1]
, where the extremes of the interval correspond to worst and best conditions respectively.

The proposing discrete model can be considered as a deep revisiting of the proposed model in
[BG09]
. Indeed, the aforesaid paper consider only the numerical simulations of the spatially homogeneous problem where the asymptotic property in time has been shown. In this paper, we consider both the spatially homogeneous and inhomogeneous problems. In the first case, the well-posedness of the related Cauchy problem has been proved according to Banach fixed-point theory, and the Kerner’s fundamental diagrams has been reproduced. In the second problem, we show the numerical results of the emerging clusters phenomena. In the modeling part, we deal with the following topics:
\displaystyle i)
nonlinear additive interactions (rather than binary interactions) between vehicles accounting on
\displaystyle ii)
perceived density (rather than real one),
\displaystyle iii)
function depending on the space variable modeling the road conditions (rather than a parameter), and
\displaystyle iv)
dynamics under external actions such as the presence of tollgates. The modeling is inspired by the books of
[KER04]
;
[PH71]
which reports a detailed interpretation of the physics traffic.

Let us comment now on some references in the literature about the modeling of multilane traffic flow. One can find the macroscopic approach in
[MBY84]
while
[MSR10]
reports an interesting survey of different models for lane changing. Also, a multilane model is analyzed in
[GKR03]
focusing on the total vehicle density across all lanes, while a modeling traffic vehicular with conservation laws is proposed in
[GR20]
. On the basis of kinetic theory, microscopic models are studied in
[KW99a]
;
[KW99b]
and a macroscopic model derived from individual behavior is given in
[HG97]
. Recently, the authors in
[HMV18]
extended the analysis to the second-order Aw-Rascle model and a hybrid stochastic kinetic model, respectively. Note that the approach in
[CC06]
is more similar to the analysis presented in
[HN19]
but with a different source term.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
deals with a concise description of multilane traffic flow and derives a new mathematical structure suitable to include the aforementioned features in addition to those already included in
[BDF12]
. Section
3
is devoted to the derivation of a specific model by inserting a possible form of the terms that appeared in the proposed mathematical structure, that is to say, the perceived density, the encounter rate, table of games, the weight function and the external force. Section
4
presents a qualitative analysis of the spatially homogeneous problem. Moreover, Kerner’s fundamental diagrams
[KER04]
are reproduced and the trend to equilibrium (asymptotic in time) is shown. Finally, in Section
5
the numerical simulations of the spatially inhomogeneous problem are provided to capture emerging behaviors.

2
Mathematical representation and structure

This section is organized through two subsections: the first one deals with the description of traffic flow along a multilane and the second one is devoted to the design of mathematical frameworks for nonlinearly additive interactions along with multilane traffic flow.

2.1
Representation of traffic flow along a multilane

This subsection deals with a discrete kinetic description of traffic flow along a multilane road and introduces the classical macroscopic quantities.

Herein, all the dependent variables describing the traffic flow are dimensionless and normalized to values in the interval
\displaystyle[0,1]
.

•

\displaystyle X
is the length of the road;

•

\displaystyle V_{M}
is the maximum velocity attained by an isolated fast vehicle when moving in free flow conditions;

•

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{M}
is the maximum density of vehicles corresponding to bumper to bumper vehicle distance.

Using the above quantities, the following dimensionless variables are identified:

•

\displaystyle x
is the position referred to length of road
\displaystyle X
;

•

\displaystyle t
is the time normalized by means of
\displaystyle\frac{X}{V_{M}}
;

•

\displaystyle v=\frac{V}{V_{M}}
is the velocity referred to
\displaystyle V_{M}
;

•

\displaystyle\rho=\tilde{\rho}/\tilde{\rho}_{M}
is the density of vehicles referred to the maximum one.

In this paper, we adopt the discrete kinetic theory method by introducing the uniform grids of the velocity variable taking the following forms

0=v_{1}<v_{2}<\dots<v_{i}<\dots<v_{n}=1,\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

(1)

Under these hypotheses, the evolution in time and space of the multilane traffic flow is described by introducing in each lane a statistical distribution function as a linear combination of Dirac distributions in variables
\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle u
as follows:

f^{\ell}(t,x,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)\otimes\delta_{i}(v-v_{i}):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},

where
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)=f^{\ell}(t,x,v_{i})
for
\displaystyle\ell=1,\cdots,L
with
\displaystyle L
is the total number of lanes. Thus, macroscopic quantities can derived in each lane as follow

•

The density in the
\displaystyle\ell
-lane is

\rho_{\ell}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x);

•

The flux in the
\displaystyle\ell
-lane is

q^{\ell}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x);

•

The average velocity in the
\displaystyle\ell
-lane is

U^{\ell}(t,x)=\frac{q^{\ell}(t,x)}{\rho^{\ell}(t,x)}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}(t,x)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x);

•

The variance of the velocity in the
\displaystyle\ell
-lane is

\Theta^{\ell}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}(t,x)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(v_{i}-U^{\ell}(t,x)\big)^{2}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x).

The global density and the global flux are obtained by summing the contributions of all lanes

\rho(t,x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\rho_{\ell}(t,x),\qquad q(t,x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}q^{\ell}(t,x).

Moreover, the global average velocity and variance are given by

U(t,x)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}U^{\ell}(t,x)\qquad\Theta(t,x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Theta^{\ell}(t,x).

2.2
A mathematical structure toward modeling

This subsection reports about the derivation of a specific structure in the case of nonlinearly additive interactions involving the micro-systems, generating the evolution of the distribution function
\displaystyle f^{\ell}_{i}
. In general, the kinetic approach is such that interactions are modeled by table of games. Thus, three types of vehicles are involved:

•
Test particle
: It represents the whole system. The related distribution function is
\displaystyle f^{\ell}_{i}=f^{\ell}(t,x,v_{i})
;

•
Candidate particle
: It changes its current state to that of the test particle as a consequence of an interaction. The related distribution function is

\displaystyle f^{r}_{h}=f^{r}(t,x,v_{h})
;

•
Field particle
: It interacts with test and candidate vehicles. The related distribution function is
\displaystyle f^{r}_{p}=f^{r}(t,x^{*},v_{p})
.

Our structure is obtained by a balance of vehicles in the elementary volume of the space of the microscopic state which includes position and velocity. This balance of vehicles includes the free transport term, the dynamics of nonlinear additive interactions, and the trend to the velocity imposed by the external actions. We mention that the dynamics of nonlinear additive interactions include a gain term of vehicles that enter in the
aforementioned elementary volume and a loss term of vehicles that leave it. Thus, the resulting structure can be written, at a formal level, as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)+v_{i}\,\partial_{x}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)=\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\ell}[\textbf{f},\textbf{f}](t,x)+\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\ell}[\textbf{f}](t,x),

(2)

where
\displaystyle f^{\ell}_{i}=f^{\ell}(t,x,v_{i}),\;\textbf{f}=(f_{1},\cdots,n)
and
\displaystyle v_{i}\,\partial_{x}f^{\ell}_{i}
is the free flow transport term, while
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\ell}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\ell}_{i}
correspond, respectively, to nonlinear additive interactions and interaction with external actions. Thus, the evolution of the distribution function in each lane is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)+v_{i}\,\partial_{x}f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)=\mathcal{G}_{i}^{\ell}[\textbf{f},\textbf{f}](t,x)-f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)\,\mathcal{L}^{\ell}[\textbf{f}](t,x)+\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\ell}[\textbf{f}](t,x)\\
\\
\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544pt=\sum_{r=1}^{L}\sum_{h,p=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{v}}\eta^{r}[\rho^{\star}_{r}(t,x^{*})]\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}[\rho_{1}^{\star},\dots,\rho_{L}^{\star};\alpha](t,x^{*})f_{h}^{r}(t,x)f_{p}^{r}(t,x^{*})\omega^{r}(x,x^{*})dx^{*}\\
\\
\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)\int_{\Omega_{v}}\eta^{\ell}[\rho^{\star}_{\ell}(t,x^{*})]\rho_{\ell}(t,x^{*})\,\omega^{\ell}(x,x^{*})dx^{*}+\mu^{\ell}[\rho^{\star}_{\ell}]\Big(f_{e}^{\ell}(x;v_{e}(x))-f_{i}^{\ell}(t,x)\Big).\end{array}

In model (
2.2
),
\displaystyle\Omega_{v}=[x,x+\xi_{v}]
represents the visibility zone where
\displaystyle\xi_{v}
is the visibility length on front of the vehicle, that depends on the quality of the road-environment, that is to say on
\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha(x)
;
\displaystyle\eta^{\ell}[\rho_{\ell}(t,x^{*}),x]
is the encounter rate, which depends on the probability distributions by means of the perceived density
\displaystyle\rho^{\star}_{\ell}
in the
\displaystyle\ell-
lane;
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h,p,r}^{i,j,\ell}
defines the table of games, which denotes the probability density that the candidate particle falls into the state of the test particle after an interaction with a field particle;
\displaystyle\omega^{\ell}(x,x^{*})
represents the weight function in each lane
\displaystyle\ell
; and
\displaystyle\mu^{\ell}[\rho^{\star}_{\ell}]
models the intensity of the action, which increases with
\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}^{\star}
, while
\displaystyle v_{e}(x)
is the speed imposed by the external action in the
\displaystyle\ell-
lane.

Herein, we shall consider admissible interactions that generate transitions only in continuous lanes. In details, the sets of admissible lane transitions are as follows

I_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{r}=\{1,2\},&r=1;\\
I_{r}=\{r-1,r,r+1\},&r\neq 1,L;\\
I_{r}=\{L-1,L\},&r=L.\end{array}\right.

(9)

3
From mathematical structure to model

This section deals with the derivation of a specific model of multilane vehicular traffic by inserting into the aforesaid mathematical structure (
2.2
) models of interactions at the microscopic scale. This goal is pursued by looking at the modeling of the interaction terms that characterize such structure, that is to say,
\displaystyle\eta^{\ell},\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell},\omega^{\ell},\mu^{\ell}
and
\displaystyle f_{e}^{\ell}
, such that a good agreement with empirical data, concerning both the Kerner’s fundamental diagram and the emerging behaviors in unsteady flow conditions, is given.

3.1
Modeling of interaction domain and perceived density

The vehicles have a visibility zone denoted by
\displaystyle\Omega_{v}=\Omega_{v}(x)=[x,x+\xi]
, where
\displaystyle\xi
is the visibility length on front of the particle, that depends on the quality of the road-environment conditions modeled by
\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha(x)
. In more detail, we assume that
\displaystyle\xi=\alpha\,X
, where
\displaystyle X<<\xi
is the visibility length in the case of best quality of the road (
\displaystyle\alpha=1
). Note that one can consider a sensitive zone
\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=[x,x+\xi_{s}]
, necessary to perceive the flow conditions in
\displaystyle\Omega_{s}
. We refer the interested reader to the paper
[CNZ19]
for more details.

The concept of perceived density was introduced in
[DEA99]
, where it was suggested that this quantity is greater (smaller) than the real one whenever positive (negative) density gradients appear. The following expression is considered

\rho^{\star}_{\ell}[f]=\rho_{\ell}+\frac{\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell}}{\sqrt{1+(\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell})^{2}}}\,\Big((\frac{1}{L}-\rho_{\ell})\,H(\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell})+\rho_{\ell}\,H(-\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell})\Big),

(10)

where
\displaystyle H(\cdot)
is the Heaviside function
\displaystyle H(\cdot\geq 0)=1
, while
\displaystyle H(\cdot<0)=0
. Thus, the perceived density, positive gradients increase the value of
\displaystyle\rho^{\star}_{\ell}
from
\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}
to the maximum admissible value
\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}=\frac{1}{L}
, while negative gradients decrease it from
\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}
to the lowest admissible value
\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}=0
such that

\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell}\to+\infty\Rightarrow\rho^{\star}_{\ell}\to\frac{1}{L},\quad\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell}=0\Rightarrow\rho^{\star}_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell},\quad\partial_{x}\rho_{\ell}\to-\infty\Rightarrow\rho^{\star}_{\ell}\to 0.

3.2
Modeling of the encounter rate and the weight function

The encounter rate
\displaystyle\eta^{\ell}[\rho^{\star}_{\ell}(t,x)]
gives the number of interactions per unit time among the micro-systems in each lane. We assume that this term grows with the local perceived density starting from a minimum value corresponding to driving in vacuum conditions
\displaystyle\eta^{0}
. The following expression is considered

\eta^{\ell}[\rho^{\star}_{\ell}(t,x)]=\eta^{0}\big(1+\gamma_{\eta}^{\ell}L\rho^{\star}_{\ell}(t,x)\big),

where
\displaystyle\gamma_{\eta}^{\ell}
is the growth coefficient and
\displaystyle\rho^{\star}_{\ell}
is the perceived density in each lane.

The weight function
\displaystyle\omega^{\ell}(x,x^{*})
is assumed to be the same in all lanes and it satisfies the following requirement:

\omega^{\ell}(x,x^{*})\geq 0,\qquad\int_{\Omega_{v}}\omega^{\ell}(x,x^{*})dx^{*}=1,\qquad\forall x^{*}\in{\Omega_{v}}.

3.3
Modeling of the table of games

We present and discuss a possible form for the table of games
\displaystyle A_{h,p,r}^{i,\ell}
which gives the probability that a micro-system in the lane
\displaystyle r
with velocity
\displaystyle v_{h}
reaches the velocity
\displaystyle v_{i}
in the lane
\displaystyle\ell
, after an interaction with a micro-system traveling at velocity
\displaystyle v_{p}
in the lane
\displaystyle r
. It satisfies the following

\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}=1,\quad\forall h,p=1,\cdots,n,\quad\forall r=1,\cdots,L

(11)

Herein, we improve the table of games proposed by
[BG09]
by taking into account the nonlinear additive interactions rather than binary ones, perceived density rather than the real one, and space function modeling the road conditions rather than a parameter.

We consider that the candidate particle with velocity
\displaystyle v_{h}
and assume that it interacts with a field particle with velocity
\displaystyle v_{p}
in the same lane. Thus, the cases
\displaystyle v_{h}>v_{p}
,
\displaystyle v_{h}<v_{p}
,
\displaystyle v_{h}=v_{p}
, are analysed separately. Herein, we omit the dependence of the road conditions and the perceived density functions on the space variable
\displaystyle x
.

I. Interaction with a faster particle
\displaystyle(v_{h}<v_{p})
.

In this case, the candidate particle is encountering a faster field particle in the
\displaystyle r
-lane. Then the candidate particle either maintains its lane or it changes it to the right continuous one, which means
\displaystyle I_{r}=\{r-1,r\}
. In the following we distinguish the case
\displaystyle r=\{2,\cdots,L\}
from the case
\displaystyle r=1
.

a)
Interaction in the case
\displaystyle h\neq 1,

\displaystyle r\neq 1

As a result of the above assumptions, it follows that
\displaystyle I_{r}=\{r-1,r\}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell=r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\cdots,h-1,\\
(1-\alpha)\big(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r-1})\big),&i=h,\\
\displaystyle\alpha\frac{1}{(i-h)}\frac{1}{\sum_{z=h+1}^{p}(z-h)^{-1}}\big(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r-1})\big),&i=h+1,\cdots,p.\end{array}\right.

(12)

\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell=r-1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle(1-\alpha)(h-i)\frac{1}{\sum_{z=1}^{h-1}(h-z)}L\rho^{\star}_{r}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r-1}),&i=1,\cdots,h-1,\\
\displaystyle\alpha L\rho^{\star}_{r}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r-1}),&i=h,\\
0,&i=h+1,\cdots,p.\end{array}\right.

(13)

Note that Eq. (
12
) gives the probability that the candidate particle maintains its lane. It can maintain its current speeds or accelerate. In the latter case, it can reach a new velocity
\displaystyle v_{i}\in\{v_{h+1},\cdots,v_{p}\}
with a probability that depends not only on the road conditions and on the perceived density but also on the distance between the velocity classes involved. Eq. (
13
) yields the probability that the candidate particle changes its lane to the right lane.

b)
Interaction in the case
\displaystyle h=1
and
\displaystyle r\neq 1

In this case,
\displaystyle I_{r}=\{r-1,r\}
and the field particle stops or the candidate particle accelerates in the same lane. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{1p,r}^{i,\ell=r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L\rho^{\star}_{r},&i=1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(i-1)}\frac{1}{\sum_{z=2}^{p}(z-1)^{-1}}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r}),&i=2,\cdots,p.\end{array}\right.

(14)

c)
Interaction in the case
\displaystyle h=1,\cdots,p-1
and
\displaystyle r=1

In this case, the candidate particle is in the slowest lane
\displaystyle(I_{r}=1)
. We assume that when it interacts with a faster field particle, it can only maintain its current lane with the same velocity or accelerating. In the latter case, it can reach a new velocity
\displaystyle v_{i}\in\{v_{h+1},\cdots,v_{p}\}
with a probability that depends not only on the road conditions and on the perceived density but also on the distance between the velocity classes involved. Consequently,

\mathcal{A}_{hp,1}^{i,\ell=1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\cdots,h-1,\\
\displaystyle 1-\alpha(1-L\rho^{\star}_{1}),&i=h,\\
\displaystyle\alpha\frac{1}{(i-h)}\frac{1}{\sum_{i=h+1}^{p-1}(i-h)^{-1}}\big(1-L\rho^{\star}_{1}\big),&i=h+1,\cdots,p.\end{array}\right.

(15)

\displaystyle(v_{h}>v_{p})

In this case, the candidate particle is encountering a slower field particle in the
\displaystyle r-

\displaystyle I_{r}=\{r,r+1\}

\displaystyle r=\{1,\cdots,L-1\}

\displaystyle r=L

a)
Interaction in the case
\displaystyle h\neq n

\displaystyle r\neq L

In this case
\displaystyle I_{c}=\{r,r+1\}

\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell=r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L\rho^{\star}_{r+1},&i=p,\\

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\rho^{\star}_{r+1}

b)
Interaction in the case
\displaystyle h=n
and
\displaystyle r\neq L

As a consequence of the above assumptions, it follows that
\displaystyle I_{r}=\{r,r+1\}

\mathcal{A}_{np,r}^{i,\ell=r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L\rho^{\star}_{r+1},&i=p,\\

\mathcal{A}_{np,r}^{i,\ell=r+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{n-i}{\sum_{z=p+1}^{n}(n-z)}(1-L\rho^{\star}_{r+1}),&i=p+1,\cdots,n,\\

Interaction in the case

and
\displaystyle r=L

The candidate particle is in the fastest lane. When it interacts with a slower field particle, we assume that it is obligated to travel with the velocity

Interaction in the case

\displaystyle r=1

In this case, the interacting vehicles are in the slowest lane and

4
The spatially homogeneous problem

In this section, we address the theoretical and computational analysis of the spatially homogeneous problem in which the distribution function
\displaystyle f^{\ell}
is independent of the space variable
\displaystyle x
. We define it as follows

f^{\ell}=f^{\ell}(t,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{\ell}(t)\otimes\delta_{i}(v-v_{i}):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Consequently, it results that

\partial_{x}f_{i}^{\ell}=0,\qquad\forall i=1,\cdots,n,\quad\forall\ell=1,\cdots,L.

This implies
\displaystyle\rho^{\star}_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell}
for
\displaystyle\;\ell=1,\cdots,L
.
Taking into account the above hypotheses, the final form of model (
2.2
) in the spatially homogeneous case is reduced to the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{df_{i}^{\ell}}{dt}=\sum_{r\in I_{c}}\eta^{r}[\rho_{r}(t)]\sum_{h,p=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}f_{h}^{r}f_{p}^{r}-\eta^{\ell}[\rho_{\ell}(t)]f_{i}^{\ell}\rho_{\ell}(t),\\
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\ell}(0)=f_{i,0}^{\ell},\end{array}\right.

(43)

where

\mathcal{A}_{h,p,r}^{i,\ell}=\mathcal{A}_{hk,pq,r}^{i,\ell}[v_{h}\rightarrow v_{i},I_{r}\rightarrow I_{\ell}\mid,v_{h},v_{p},I_{r},\rho_{1}(t),\dots,\rho_{L}(t)].

Recalling the probability density propriety (
11
), the above model (
43
) satisfies the mass conservation hypothesis, i.e.
\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}=0
, as it is required in spatially homogeneous conditions.

4.1
Well-posedness of the related Cauchy problem

Let denote

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{Ln}
a set of matrix endowed with the
\displaystyle 1-
norm

\parallel f(t)\parallel_{1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid f_{i}(t)\mid,\quad f=(f_{i}^{\ell})\in\mathbb{M}_{Ln}.

We introduce the linear space
\displaystyle X_{T}=C([0,T];\mathbb{M}_{Ln})
of the continuous functions
\displaystyle f=f(t):[0,T]\to\mathbb{M}_{Ln}
for
\displaystyle T>0
equipped
with the infinity norm

\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}=\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\parallel f(t)\parallel_{1}.

Note that
\displaystyle(X_{T},\parallel.\parallel_{\infty})
is a real Banach space.

Well-posedness of the spatially homogeneous problem means global in time existence and uniqueness of a solution
\displaystyle f=f(t)
to the Cauchy problem (
43
). These results pass through two steps. Firstly, we prove the local existence and uniqueness in time of the solution
\displaystyle f
in
\displaystyle X_{T}
for a certain
\displaystyle T>0
. Secondly, we extend the obtained result to a global solution defined for all
\displaystyle t>0
.

We consider the following assumptions to prove the well-posedness of the spatially homogeneous problem (
43
):

(\textbf{{H1}})\qquad{\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}\geq 0},\qquad\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}=1\quad\forall r=1,\dots,L\quad\forall h,p=1,\dots,n

whenever
\displaystyle 0\leq\rho_{\ell}{\leq}\frac{1}{L}
and

(\textbf{{H2}})\qquad\exists C_{\eta}>0\quad/\quad 0<\eta^{\ell}[\rho_{\ell}]\leq C_{\eta}^{\ell},\quad\hbox{when}\quad 0\leq\rho_{\ell}{\leq}\frac{1}{L},\quad\ell=1,\dots,L.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
\displaystyle(\textbf{H1})
-
\displaystyle(\textbf{H2})
are satisfied and let
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\ell}_{ij}:=\rho_{0}\in[0,1]
, then

\displaystyle\exists\hskip 5.69046ptT>0
such that problem (
43
) admits unique local non-negative solution
\displaystyle f\in X_{T}
. Moreover, it satisfies the mass conservation

\parallel f(t)\parallel_{1}=\rho_{0}.

(44)

We start by giving some estimates on the nonlinear operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}
given by:

\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{i}^{\ell}:=\sum_{r\in I_{c}}\eta^{r}[\rho_{r}(t)]\sum_{h,p=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}f_{h}^{r}f_{p}^{r}-\eta^{\ell}[\rho^{\ell}(t)]f_{i}^{\ell}(t)\rho_{\ell}(t).

Lemma 1
.

Assume
\displaystyle(\textbf{{H1}})
and
\displaystyle(\textbf{{H2}})
are satisfied, then
\displaystyle\exists\;C_{1}>0
such that

\parallel\mathcal{J}(f)\parallel_{1}\leq C_{1}\parallel f\parallel_{1}^{2},

\parallel\mathcal{J}(f)-\mathcal{J}(g)\parallel_{1}\leq C_{1}(\parallel f\parallel_{1}+\parallel g\parallel_{1})\parallel f-g\parallel_{1}.

Next, we define the following operator

\phi(f)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{J}(f)(s)ds.

We have the following estimates:

Lemma 2
.

Assume
\displaystyle(\textbf{{H1}})
and
\displaystyle(\textbf{{H2}})
are satisfied, then
\displaystyle\exists\,C_{2}(T)>0
such that

\parallel\phi(f)\parallel_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}^{2},

\parallel\phi(f)-\phi(g)\parallel_{\infty}\leq C_{2}(\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}+\parallel g\parallel_{\infty})\parallel f-g\parallel_{\infty}.

Proof of Theorem 1
First, we rewrite problem (
43
) as an integral form

f=\mathcal{N}(f)

where the mild equation is as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}(f))_{i}^{\ell}=f_{i,0}^{\ell}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{L}\eta^{r}[\rho_{r}]\sum_{h,p=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}f_{h}^{r}(s)f^{r}_{p}(s)-f^{\ell}_{i}(s)\eta^{\ell}[\rho_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}(s)\Big)ds.

(45)

Proving the uniqueness and existence of solution of the above mild equation in
\displaystyle X_{T}
implies to finding a fixed point of
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
. This is given by the following lemma

Lemma 3
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
maps the ball
\displaystyle B_{X_{T}}(0,2\,\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1})
into itself.

Let
\displaystyle f\in X_{T},\quad R>0
such that
\displaystyle\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}\leq R
. From Lemma
2
we obtain

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)\parallel_{\infty}\leq\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}+TC_{1}\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}^{2},

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)\parallel_{\infty}\leq R+TC_{1}R^{2}.

We chose
\displaystyle T
such that

TC_{1}R^{2}<\frac{R}{4},

this implies

T<\frac{1}{4C_{1}\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}}.

Then,

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)\parallel_{\infty}\leq\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}+\frac{1}{4C_{1}\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}}C_{1}\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}^{2},

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)\parallel_{\infty}\leq\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}+\frac{1}{4\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}}R^{2}.

where
\displaystyle R
is a solution of
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}R^{2}-\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}R+\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}^{2}=0.

Then,
\displaystyle R=2\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}
.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
is strictly contraction on a ball
\displaystyle B_{X_{T}}\big(0,2\parallel f_{0}\parallel_{1}\big)
.

Let
\displaystyle f,g\in X_{T},\;R>0
such that
\displaystyle\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}\leq R
and
\displaystyle\parallel g\parallel_{\infty}\leq R
. Thanks to Lemma
2
, we have the following estimate

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)-\mathcal{N}(g)\parallel_{\infty}\leq TC_{1}\big(\parallel f\parallel_{\infty}+\parallel g\parallel_{\infty}\big)\parallel f-g\parallel_{\infty}.

It follows that

\parallel\mathcal{N}(f)-\mathcal{N}(g)\parallel_{\infty}\leq 2RTC_{1}\parallel f-g\parallel_{\infty}.

We chose
\displaystyle T
such that

T<\frac{1}{2RC_{1}}.

Then, Lemma
4
is achieved.

Now, by summing over
\displaystyle\ell,i,j
and using Assumption (
H1
), one gets the conservation of mass given by equation (
44
). Notice that we have the non-negativity of the solution thanks to Lemma
3
under the non-negativity of the initial condition
\displaystyle f_{i,0}^{\ell}>0
. Indeed,
Eq. (
43
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f_{i}^{\ell}(t)+f_{i}^{\ell}(t)L(f)(t)=G_{i}(f,f)(t):=\sum_{r\in I_{c}}\eta^{r}[\rho_{r}(t)]\sum_{h,p=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{hp,r}^{i,\ell}f_{h}^{r}f_{p}^{r},

(46)

where
\displaystyle L(f)(t)=\eta^{\ell}[\rho_{\ell}(t)]\rho_{\ell}(t)
.
Let denote
\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\int_{0}^{t}L(f)(s)ds
.
If
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\ell}
is a solution of Eq. (
46
), then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\exp(\lambda(t))f_{i}^{\ell}(t)\Big)=\exp(\lambda(t))G_{i}(f,f)(t),

which implies

f_{i}^{\ell}(t)=\exp(-\lambda(t))f_{i,0}^{\ell}+\int_{0}^{t}\exp(\lambda(s))G_{i}(f,f)(s)ds.

(47)

Consequently, eq. (
47
) allows to conclude that, given
\displaystyle f_{i,0}^{\ell}>0
and the positivity of the integral function, the solution
\displaystyle f_{i}^{\ell}(t)
satisfies non-negativity condition in its domain of existence.

\hskip 441.01772pt\square

The following theorem guaranties the existence of global solution of the spatially homogeneous problem (
43
):

Theorem 2
.

Under the same hypotheses of Theorem
1
, Problem (
43
) admits a unique non-negative global solution
\displaystyle f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{M}_{Ln})
satisfying estimate (
44
).

Proof.
It suffices to apply the same reasoning developed in the proof of Theorem

1
on the interval
\displaystyle(T^{*},2T^{*}]
, taking
\displaystyle f(T^{*})
as new initial condition. Since
\displaystyle f^{\ell}_{i}(T)\geq 0
for all

\displaystyle\ell=1,\dots,L,\;i=1,\dots,n
, and
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\ell}_{i}=\rho_{0}\in[0,1],
we are in the same hypotheses of Theorem
1
. Hence, we conclude the existence and uniqueness of a local in time continuous solution on
\displaystyle[T^{*},2T^{*}]
satisfying

\parallel f(t)\parallel_{1}=\rho_{0},\qquad\forall t\in[T^{*},2T^{*}].

(48)

Iterating this procedure on all intervals of the form
\displaystyle(kT^{*},(k+1)T^{*}],k\in\mathbb{N},
we can construct the global solution on real positive axis
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

6
Conclusions and perspectives

In this paper we have developed a new discrete kinetic model for multilane traffic flow on the basis of the kinetic theory approach. After a detailed description of a multilane road and the discrete method adopted, we have presented our proposed discrete model. Next, we have modeled each term appearing in the proposed model. We have proposed an improved table of games by taking into account further features of the complexity, namely the nonlinear additive interactions, perceived density rather than real one, and the road conditions as a function depending on the space variable which can used to depict the road maintenance areas. Moreover, we have modeled the external actions such as tollgates as well traffic signs. The well-posedness of the related Cauchy problem for the spatially homogeneous case has been proved by using Banach fixed-point theory. In order to validate the proposed improved model, in both the spatially homogeneous and inhomogeneous cases, and in fact to show its ability to reproduce certain realistic phenomena, we have reproduced the numerical simulations for Kerner’s fundamental diagrams, and the asymptotic in time property along three lanes. Finally, we have obtained numerical simulations of the emerging of clusters with closed speed along two lanes by using the higher resolution finite volume method. We mention that all numerical simulations are provided with particular focus on the road environment conditions modeled by a parameter, which has been shown to have a great influence on the dynamic of vehicles.

Looking ahead, the obtained numerical simulations in the case of the spatially inhomogeneous problem are just one of the several emerging behaviors that can be depicted by our proposed modeling. One can also reproduce many realistic phenomena such as stop and go waves, and the external action by tollgates (see
[Do1]
for one lane). In addition, it is interesting to develop an approach of asymptotic limits towards macroscopic models known in the literature, for example, the recent models
[GR20]
;
[HMV18]
. One can draw inspiration from the ideas developed in
[BD11]
.

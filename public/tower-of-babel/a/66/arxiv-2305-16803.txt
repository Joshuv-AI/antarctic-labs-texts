Quantum work extraction efficiency for noisy quantum batteries: the role of coherence
Salvatore Tirone, Raffaele Salvia, Stefano Chessa, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
Quantum work capacitances and maximal asymptotic work/energy ratios are figures of merit characterizing the robustness against noise of work extraction processes in quantum batteries formed by collections of quantum systems. In this paper we establish a direct connection between these functionals and, exploiting this result, we analyze different types of noise models mimicking self-discharging (amplitude damping channels), thermalization (generalized amplitude damping channels) and dephasing effects (simultaneous phase-amplitude damping channels). In this context we show that input quantum coherence can significantly improve the storage performance of noisy quantum batteries and that the maximum output ergotropy is not always achieved by the maximum available input energy.

II.1
Quantum Work Capacitances

The first example of quantum work capacitance introduced in
Tirone et al. 2024

(2)

(3)

where, given

the QB Hamiltonian, we set

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\rho}^{(n)}\in\overline{\mathfrak{S}}^{(n)}_{E}}\mathcal{E}(\Lambda^{\otimes n}(\hat{\rho}^{(n)});\hat{H}^{(n)})\;,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq E^{\prime}\leq E}\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E^{\prime})

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\rho}^{(n)}\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{(n)}_{E}}\mathcal{E}(\Lambda^{\otimes n}(\hat{\rho}^{(n)});\hat{H}^{(n)})\;,

II.2
Maximal asymptotic Work/Energy ratios

While the quantum work capacitances are defined for a finite ratio of the input energy
E
with respect to the number
n
of q-cells (i.e.,
E/n
is fixed), MAWERs are computed instead assuming
n
to be a free resource, independent of
E
.
Formally, computing the MAWER corresponding to

C_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\Lambda,\mathfrak{e}\right)

accounts to solving the following limit:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda):=\limsup_{E\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup_{n\geq\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}_{\max}\rceil}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)}{E}\right)\;,

(14)

with
\mathfrak{e}_{\max}
being the maximum eigenvalue of the single-site Hamiltonian
\hat{h}

14

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)=\lim_{E\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)}{E}\;.

(15)

This latter characterization gives a physical meaning to the quantity above: it shows that in principle this measure of efficiency can be practically achieved by using a large number of q-cells. The proof of Eq. (
15
) can be found in appendix
B
.
Analogously to what was done for the quantum work capacitances, also for the MAWER one can introduce a hierarchy of constrained versions of such quantity differing on the resources dedicated to the work extraction process. Specifically,
replacing
\mathcal{E}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)
appearing in the r.h.s. of Eq. (
14
) with

\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)
,
\mathcal{E}_{\rm sep}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc,sep}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)
, we can speak of
local MAWER
{\cal J}_{\rm loc}(\Lambda)
, separable-input MAWER
{\cal J}_{\rm sep}(\Lambda)
, and separable-input local MAWER
{\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}(\Lambda)
, respectively. For all of the constrained versions of the MAWER defined above holds a characterization analogous to Eq. (
15
).

We now prove an important relation allowing us to establish a direct connection between each one of such functionals and their associated quantum work-capacitance:

Theorem 1
.

For any given CPTP map
\Lambda
, the following identity holds

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)=\lim_{\mathfrak{e}\rightarrow 0}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;,

(16)

where hereafter we will use the symbol
\diamond
as a placeholder variable taking values on the set
\{{\cal E};{\rm sep};{\rm loc};{\rm loc,sep}\}
.

Proof.

Let us start by noticing that
{\cal J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)
can be always lower bounded as

{\cal J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)\geq\sup_{\mathfrak{e}\in(0,\mathfrak{e}_{\max}]}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;.

(17)

Indeed since for
\mathfrak{e}\in(0,\mathfrak{e}_{\max}]
one has

\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil\geq\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}_{\max}\rceil
, we can write

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{E\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil)}(\Lambda;E)}{E}

(18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{E\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)}(\Lambda;(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)\mathfrak{e})}{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{E\rightarrow\infty}\left(\tfrac{\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1}{E}\right)\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)}(\Lambda;(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)\mathfrak{e})}{\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;,

where the second inequality follows from

E\geq(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)\mathfrak{e}
and from the fact that

is monotonically increasing

Evaluated for

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)\geq\lim_{\mathfrak{e}\rightarrow 0}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;.

(19)

Accordingly to prove the thesis we only need to show that also the reverse inequality holds true.
For this purpose observe that, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)=\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(n)}(\Lambda;nE/n)\leq nC_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E/n)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{E\rightarrow\infty}\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E/n)}{E/n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathfrak{e}\rightarrow 0}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;.

III.1
Amplitude Damping Channel

Expressed in the energy basis
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}
, the action of the amplitude damping channel
\Phi_{\gamma}
on a generic density matrix
\hat{\rho}
of a qubit system is defined as
Giovannetti and Fazio 2005
:

\Phi_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}+\gamma\rho_{11}&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{01}\\
\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{10}&(1-\gamma)\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(30)

with
\gamma\in[0,1]
being the damping parameter of the model, and
\rho_{ij}
being the matrix elements of
\hat{\rho}
.
We recall that
\Phi_{\gamma}
commutes with the Hamiltonian evolution of the system, i.e.

\displaystyle\Phi_{\gamma}(e^{-i\hat{h}t}\hat{\rho}e^{i\hat{h}t})=e^{-i\hat{h}t}\Phi_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho})e^{i\hat{h}t}\;,

(31)

and that the output energy and input
energy of any state are connected via the identity

\mathfrak{E}(\Phi_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho}))=(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho})\;.

(32)

Notice also that
\Phi_{\gamma}
maps the ground state into itself, i.e.

\displaystyle\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0})=\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}\;.

(33)

As proven in
Tirone et al. 2021
, the output ergotropy
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda(\hat{\rho});\hat{h})
of a generic quantum
channel
\Lambda
is always maximized by a pure state. Therefore, to compute the single-use fixed-energy maximum output
ergotropy
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};E=\mathfrak{e})
, it is sufficient to consider the sets of pure states with energy
\mathfrak{e}\in[0,1]
,
which can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}}^{(\phi)}}:=\sqrt{1-{\mathfrak{e}}}\ket{0}+e^{i\phi}\sqrt{{\mathfrak{e}}}\ket{1}\;,

(34)

with the phase
\phi
that can be taken equal to zero thanks to the covariance property (
31
)
Tirone et al. 2024
.
By direct computation one can observe that
\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}}^{(\phi)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}}^{(\phi)}})
admits as eigenvalues

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\gamma}({\mathfrak{e}}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}\gamma(1-\gamma)}\right)\;,

(35)

which, using (
96
), leads to the following formula for the the single-shot (
n=1
), fixed energy, maximum output ergotropy of the model,

\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})=(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-4{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}\gamma(1-\gamma)}\right)\;.

(36)

As shown in Fig.
2
, for fixed
\gamma
, this is a concave and in general non-monotonic function of the energy
{\mathfrak{e}}
. In the interval
[0,1]
, it reaches its maximum value for

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}:=\min\left\{1,1/({2\sqrt{\gamma}})\right\}\;,

(37)

meaning that using all the available input energy is not always the best option to get a configuration that is more resilient to the noise.
An inspection of Eq. (
36

\displaystyle{E}^{\star}_{\gamma}({\mathfrak{e}}):=\min\{{\mathfrak{e}},E_{\gamma}\}=\min\{{\mathfrak{e}},1/(2\sqrt{\gamma})\}\;,

(38)

which yields

\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};{E}^{\star}_{\gamma}({\mathfrak{e}}))\;,

(39)

that is also concave w.r.t.
{\mathfrak{e}}
– see Fig.
2
. When replaced in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) we can thus arrive to the following expression for the local ergotropic capacitances,

C_{\rm loc}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)=C_{\rm loc,sep}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)=\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})\;,

(40)

which we plot in Fig.
2
as functions of
\gamma
for fixed
\mathfrak{e}
.
Notably for the ADC
\Phi_{\gamma}
the quantity
\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})
also provides the value of the separable-input ergotropy capacitance, i.e.

\displaystyle C_{\rm sep}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)=\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})\;.

(41)

The proof of this important identity is postponed to Sec.
III.2
, where it will be derived for the larger class of GADC’s which includes the
\Phi_{\gamma}
’s as special instances.

We conclude by noticing that in view of the ordering (
6
),
\chi(\Phi_{\gamma},\mathfrak{e})
represents a lower bound for
the last of the work capacitances introduced in Sec.

, i.e.
C_{\cal E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma},\mathfrak{e}\right)
.
An upper bound for such term can be computed exploiting the fact that the mean output energy of a state is always larger than or equal to its ergotropy. Thus, from (
32
), we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(n)}(\Phi_{\gamma};E)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)E\;,

(42)

that leads to the inequality

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{e}\geq C_{\cal E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)\geq\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})\;.

(43)

Notice that Eq. (
42

determines the value of
C_{\cal E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)
at least
for small enough
\mathfrak{e}
: indeed in this regime

\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})
reduces to

(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{e}
, so we can write

\displaystyle C_{\cal E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}\right)=(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{e}+O(\mathfrak{e}^{2})\;.

(44)

III.1.1
Estimating MAWERs for ADCs

We can exactly compute the value of the MAWER functional
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})
for ADC’s.
To begin with, observe that from Eq. (
42
) it is easy verified that such quantity cannot be larger than
1-\gamma
, i.e.
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})\leq 1-\gamma
.
We will now show a family of states which achieves this upper bound, proving therefore that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})=1-\gamma\;.

(45)

To this aim let’s consider, for any fixed energy
E
and arbitrary
n\geq E
, the vectors

\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}

(see (
34
)).
Observe then that by invoking (
99
) we can write

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma}^{\otimes n}(\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}\!\bra{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n});\hat{H}^{(n)})}{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{n\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}\!\bra{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}});\hat{h})}{E}

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)-O\left(\tfrac{E}{n}\right)\;,

which, taking the sup over
n
, leads to Eq. (
45
).
We observe next that
1-\gamma
provides also the value of the constrained versions of the MAWER, i.e.

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\rm sep}(\Phi_{\gamma})={\cal J}_{\rm loc}(\Phi_{\gamma})={\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}(\Phi_{\gamma})=1-\gamma\;.

(47)

Indeed, while because of (
45
),

1-\gamma
is trivially an upper bound for
{\cal J}_{\rm sep}(\Phi_{\gamma})
,
{\cal J}_{\rm loc}(\Phi_{\gamma})
,
{\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}(\Phi_{\gamma})
, such value can be attained by the smaller of them (i.e.
{\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}(\Phi_{\gamma})
), again using the separable state
\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
as trial input.
This follows by simply noticing that the identity in Eq. (
46
) would trivially apply also if we replaced

\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma}^{\otimes n}(\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}};\hat{H}^{(n)})
in the l.h.s. side with

\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}(\Phi_{\gamma}^{\otimes n}(\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}};\hat{H}^{(n)})
.
It goes without mentioning that, since the function
\chi(\Phi_{\gamma},\mathfrak{e})
verifies the conditions

\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e}=0)=0
and

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial{\chi(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})}}{\partial\mathfrak{e}}\right|_{\mathfrak{e}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial{\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma};\mathfrak{e})}}{\partial\mathfrak{e}}\right|_{\mathfrak{e}=0}=1-\gamma\;,

(48)

for the local, separable-input local and separable MAWER, the identity (
47
)
exactly matches with the prescription of Theorem
1
.
We also notice that (
44
) can be seen as a consequence of Remark
2
and (
45
).

We conclude by observing that the vectors
\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
are not the only ones that allow us to assign the value
1-\gamma
to
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})
.
Interestingly we can also employ more “classical” input states, that at variance with
\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}

exhibit no quantum coherence among the energy eigenstates of the system Hamiltonian. Consider for instance what happens if we take tensor product states of the form

\displaystyle|\phi_{E}^{(n)}\rangle:=\ket{1}^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor}\otimes\ket{0}^{n-\lfloor E\rfloor}\;,

(49)

in which the first
\lfloor E\rfloor
q-cells are initialized into the maximum energy eigenstate of
\hat{h}
while the remaining ones are prepared in the ground state.
In this case it is still possible to realize a work/energy ratio as high as the MAWER. Indeed invoking Eq. (
33
) we can write

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{E}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(|\phi_{E}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{E}^{(n)}|);\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\lfloor E\rfloor(1-\gamma)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(|\phi_{E}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{E}^{(n)}|);\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}\left(\left(\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1})\right)^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}^{\otimes(n-\lfloor E\rfloor)};\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathfrak{E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1})^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor};\hat{H}^{(\lfloor E\rfloor)}\right)-1

\displaystyle\qquad=\lfloor E\rfloor\mathfrak{E}\left(\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1});\hat{h}\right)-1

\displaystyle\qquad=\lfloor E\rfloor(1-\gamma)-1\;,

(50)

where the third passage is justified because the rank of the state
\Phi_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1})^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}^{\otimes(n-\lfloor E\rfloor)}
is at most
2^{\lfloor E\rfloor}
, so for
n\geq 2^{\lfloor E\rfloor}+\lfloor E\rfloor
we can find a suitable work extraction unitary rearranging all the output state eigenvalues into the first excited state of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{(n)}
. Therefore, remembering that the supremum over
n
can always be replaced by a limit (see Eq. (
106
) of Appendix
B
), we can write

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{E\to\infty}\sup\limits_{n\geq\lceil E\rceil}\frac{\mathcal{E}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\bra{1}^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor}\otimes\ket{0}\!\bra{0}^{n-\lfloor E\rfloor});\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)}{E}

\displaystyle=1-\gamma=\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})\;.

(51)

However, while the family
\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
allows to reach the limit (
45
) with local unitaries (see discussion below (
47
)), if we use the “classical” input states
|\phi_{E}^{(n)}\rangle
we necessarily need the power of nonlocal operations to extract the energy at the output of the channel. Indeed using only local operations in this case we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(|\phi_{E}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\phi_{E}^{(n)}|);\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lfloor E\rfloor\mathcal{E}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|);\hat{h}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lfloor E\rfloor\max\left(0,1-2\gamma\right)\;,

which leads to

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{E\to\infty}\sup\limits_{n\geq\lceil E\rceil}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}\left(\Phi^{\otimes n}_{\gamma}(\ket{1}\!\bra{1}^{\otimes\lfloor E\rfloor}\otimes\ket{0}\!\bra{0}^{n-\lfloor E\rfloor});\hat{H}^{(n)}\right)}{E}

\displaystyle=\max\left(0,1-2\gamma\right)<\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(\Phi_{\gamma})\;.

(52)

It is finally worth noting that any state
\hat{\rho}^{(n)}_{d}
which is diagonal in the same basis as
\hat{H}^{(n)}
can be written as a linear convex combination of terms in the form
\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1}^{\otimes k}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}^{n-k}
. Therefore, Eq. (
52
) implies that no diagonal input state can attain the MAWER for this family of channels.

The take home message of all the here presented discussion is that the family of states
\ket{\psi_{E/n}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
is capable of reaching the maximal MAWER efficiency under the effect of the ADC noise using only local operations: this thanks to the explicit
presence of quantum coherence in the superposition (
34
).

The action of a GADC
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}
on a density matrix
\hat{\rho}
is defined in terms of two noise parameters
\gamma
and
\eta
via the mapping

Khatri et al. 2020
:

\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\rho})=\begin{pmatrix}1-\gamma\eta-(1-\gamma)\rho_{11}&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{01}\\

(53)

The channel
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}
is in principle well defined for every
\eta\in[0,1]
. However, it represents a thermalization at finite positive temperature only when
\eta\in\left[0,{1}/{2}\right]

Notice in particular that for
\eta=0

\Phi_{\gamma,0}=\Phi_{\gamma}
. For
\eta=1/2
instead it describes the thermal contact with an infinite temperature. Additionally, for arbitrary
\eta\in\left[0,{1}/{2}\right]

\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}
sends the ground state into a thermal state (
100
) of inverse temperature

\displaystyle\beta(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta})=\beta_{\gamma,\eta}:=-\ln\left(\frac{\gamma\eta}{1-\gamma\eta}\right)\;.

(54)

Similarly to the case of ADCs one can easily verify that GADCs commute with the Hamiltonian evolution
(i.e.
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(e^{-i\hat{h}t}\hat{\rho}e^{i\hat{h}t})=e^{-i\hat{h}t}\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\rho})e^{i\hat{h}t}
) and maintain linear dependence between the input and output energies, i.e.

\mathfrak{E}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\rho}))=(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho})+\gamma\eta\;.

(55)

As for the ADC, we can compute the single-shot fixed-energy maximum output ergotropy
focusing on the input states (
34
).

In this case
the eigenvalues of
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\ket{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}}^{(\phi)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}}^{(\phi)}})
are given by

\lambda^{({\pm})}_{\gamma,\eta}({\mathfrak{e}}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{(1-2\eta\gamma)^{2}+4\gamma(1-\gamma)(2\eta-{\mathfrak{e}}){\mathfrak{e}}}\right),

(56)

which replaced into (
96
) lead to

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};{\mathfrak{e}})=(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}+\eta\gamma

(57)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{(1-2\eta\gamma)^{2}+4\gamma(1-\gamma)(2\eta-{\mathfrak{e}}){\mathfrak{e}}}\right)\;.

The behavior of
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};{\mathfrak{e}})
is depicted in Fig.
3
. Simple algebra reveals that, for fixed
\eta
and
\gamma
, such a function is still concave w.r.t. to
\mathfrak{e}
and that, on the interval
[0,1]
, it achieves its maximum for

\displaystyle E_{\gamma,\eta}:=\min\left\{1,\eta+\tfrac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma\eta(1-\eta)}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\right\}\;.

(58)

Accordingly, introducing

{E}^{\star}_{\gamma,\eta}({\mathfrak{e}}):=\min\{{\mathfrak{e}},E_{\gamma,\eta}\}=\min\left\{{\mathfrak{e}},\eta+\tfrac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma\eta(1-\eta)}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\right\}\;,

(59)

we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};{\mathfrak{e}})=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};{E}^{\star}_{\gamma,\eta}({\mathfrak{e}}))\;,

(60)

which inherits from (
57
) the property of being a concave function of
{\mathfrak{e}}
.
Replaced in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8

\displaystyle C_{\rm loc}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rm loc,sep}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e})=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e})\;,

which we plot in Fig.
3
for
\mathfrak{e}
alongside with the values of the optimal input energy

.
As anticipated in the previous section,
for the GADCs
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}
the quantity
\chi(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta},\mathfrak{e})
also provides the value of the separable-input ergotropy capacitance,
i.e.

\displaystyle C_{\rm sep}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e}\right)=\chi(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{e})\;.

(62)

An explicit proof of this result follows from two facts. The first is simply that, since for qubit systems the total ergotropy and the ergotropy always coincide (see Eq. (
98
) of Appendix
A
), we can claim

\chi_{\rm tot}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta},\mathfrak{e})=\chi(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta},\mathfrak{e})
.
The second is that GADCs fulfill the condition (
12
) which, according to Ref.
Tirone et al. 2023
, enables one to show that the r.h.s. side of Eq. (
11
) coincides with
C_{\rm sep}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta},\mathfrak{e}\right)
.
To verify this fact, notice that by definition the single-site states entering (
13
) can be identified as states as in (
34
), i.e.

\displaystyle|\psi_{j}\rangle=\ket{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}^{(\phi_{j})}}\;,

(63)

with
\phi_{j}
arbitrary, and energy terms
{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}
such that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{e}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}/n\leq{\mathfrak{e}}\;.

(64)

Observe next that, by explicit calculation, the von Neumann entropy of these states evaluated at the output of the GADC turns out to be independent of
\phi_{j}
and to be a convex function of
{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}
,

\displaystyle S(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(|\psi_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}^{(\phi_{j})}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}^{(\phi_{j})}|))

(65)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=H_{\gamma,\eta}({\mathfrak{e}_{j}}):=-\sum_{k=\pm}\lambda_{\gamma,\eta}^{(k)}(\mathfrak{e}_{j})\log_{2}\lambda_{\gamma,\eta}^{(k)}(\mathfrak{e}_{j})\;,

(see Fig.
4
).

Thus we can write

\displaystyle S\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}^{\otimes n}(|\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}|)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}S\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\ket{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}^{(\phi_{j})}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}^{(\phi_{j})}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}H_{\gamma,\lambda}({\mathfrak{e}_{j}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle nH_{\gamma,\lambda}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{e}_{j}}/n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nS\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\ket{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\left((\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\ket{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}))^{\otimes n}\right)\;,

which explicitly proves that the state
\ket{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
minimizes the output entropy of the channel
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}^{\otimes n}
over the set of pure states
|\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}\rangle
of fixed input energy
n\overline{\mathfrak{e}}
.
As the entropy and the total ergotropy are related by a bijective relation (see e.g.
Salvia and Giovannetti 2022
) the above result also implies that the equipartite state
\ket{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}^{\otimes n}
is the one that maximizes the output total ergotropy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(|\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}|))
for all such states. Setting
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}}=\ket{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{\overline{\mathfrak{e}}}^{(0)}}
we can hence write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}((\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}}))^{\otimes n};\hat{H}^{(n)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}^{\otimes n}(|\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}|);\hat{H}^{(n)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}^{\otimes n}(|\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}\rangle\!\langle\Psi_{\rm fact}^{(n)}|);\hat{H}^{(n)})

which leads to Eq. (
12
) by the following identities

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}((\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}}))^{\otimes n};\hat{H}^{(n)})=n\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}});\hat{h})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=n\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}});\hat{h})=\mathcal{E}((\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathfrak{e}}))^{\otimes n};\hat{H}^{(n)})\;,

the first being a general property of the total ergotropy, the last two applying instead for the special case of qubits thanks to Eq. (
99
).

We conclude by remarking that as in the case of the ADC’s we cannot provide

(66)

{\cal J}_{\rm sep}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta})={\cal J}_{\rm loc}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta})={\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta})=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-2\eta\gamma}\;.

Two-uses output ergotropy

In this section we investigate possible superadditivity effects of the GADC. We do so by an extensive numerical calculation on two-qubits states, finding no violation of additivity in the two-uses case.
We performed a numerical search looking for two-qubit states
\rho^{(2)}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}^{\otimes 2})\left(\rho^{(2)}\right)>2\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};\mathfrak{E}(\rho^{(2)})/2\right)\;.

(74)

Any pure two-qubit state
\ket{\psi}\!\!\bra{\psi}
can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}c_{i}\ket{i}e^{i\phi_{i}}\;,

(75)

with
c_{i}\in[0,1]
and
\phi_{i}\in[0,2\pi)
.
Since we know that the spectrum of the output state
\Phi_{\gamma,\eta}\left(\ket{\psi}\!\!\bra{\psi}\right)
is independent of the phases
\phi_{i}
, we can set without loss of generality
\phi_{i}=0
.
Using the normalization constraint
\sum_{i}c^{2}_{i}=1
and the average energy constraint
\braket{\phi|E|\phi}
, we can restrict the search to a sample space of two real parameters. Among these states, to test possible additivity violations, we selected separable states and entangled input states of the form
\sqrt{1-E/2}\ket{00}+\sqrt{E/2}\ket{11}
and compared the associated output ergotropy (at fixed average input energy
\mathfrak{e}
). The small number of real parameters involved in the optimization allows a brute-force span of the parameter space. The numerical search failed though to find, for any value of
\gamma
and
\eta
(including the ADC case
\eta=0
), any instance
\rho
verifying the strict inequality (
74
). Therefore, while non exhaustive, numerical evidence seems to suggest that
\mathcal{E}^{(2)}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};E\right)=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};E\right)
. Considering that the same technique was sufficient to find violations for other qubit channels (e.g. dephasing
Tirone et al. 2024
), we conjecture the optimal output ergotropy of the GADC (and ADC) to be additive; i.e. that
\mathcal{E}^{(n)}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};E\right)=n\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\left(\Phi_{\gamma,\eta};E\right)
for every
n
and
E
.

III.3
Composition of dephasing channels and ADCs

Adding extra dephasing to an ADC
\Phi_{\gamma}
leads to the identification of a class of maps defined by the transformations
Jiang and Chen 2012

D_{\kappa,\gamma}(\hat{\rho})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}+\gamma\rho_{11}&\sqrt{1-\kappa}\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{01}\\
\sqrt{1-\kappa}\sqrt{1-\gamma}\rho_{10}&(1-\gamma)\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(76)

with
\kappa\in[0,1]
being the dephasing parameter (
\kappa=0
representing zero extra noise while

\kappa=1
representing complete extra dephasing).
These maps still commute with the Hamiltonian evolution of the system and have the same input-output energy relations of the ADC’s, i.e.

\mathfrak{E}(D_{\kappa,\gamma}(\hat{\rho}))=\mathfrak{E}(\Phi_{\gamma}(\hat{\rho}))=(1-\gamma)\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho})\;.

(77)

The optimization of the
single-shot fixed-energy maximal output ergotropy proceeds along the same lines
of the other classes of maps we have analyzed so far. In particular, also in this case we can restrict the analysis to input states (
34
) whose outputs have eigenvalues

\lambda^{(\pm)}_{\kappa,\gamma}({\mathfrak{e}}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}[{\mathfrak{e}}(\gamma-\kappa)+\kappa)]}\right)\;.

(78)

Eq. (
96
) hence allows us to express the fixed energy single-shot maximal ergotropy as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}-\frac{1}{2}

(79)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}[{\mathfrak{e}}(\gamma-\kappa)+\kappa)]}\;,

which, at variance with what happens for ADCs and GADCs, is not always concave w.r.t. to
{\mathfrak{e}}
.
Indeed computing the second order derivative we observe that

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial^{2}\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})}{\partial^{2}E}

(80)

\displaystyle\quad=\tfrac{-2(1-\gamma)(1-\kappa)(\gamma-\kappa(1-\gamma))}{\left(1-4(1-\gamma){\mathfrak{e}}[{\mathfrak{e}}(\gamma-\kappa)+\kappa)]\right)^{{3/2}}}\geq 0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\kappa\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\;,

which implies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\kappa<\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})\quad\mbox{concave}\\
\kappa=\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})\quad\mbox{linear}\\
\kappa>\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})\quad\mbox{convex.}\end{cases}

(81)

Considering that
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
is always positive semidefinite (with

\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};0)=0
), this means that for
\kappa\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}
, the function (
79
) is monotonically increasing, achieving its maximum value

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left(0,1-2\gamma\right)\;,

(82)

at the extreme of the allowed domain (i.e. for
{\mathfrak{e}}=1
). Here,
\kappa\geq\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}
is possible for
\gamma\leq 1/2
, as it can be seen in Eq. (
80
).
Accordingly we can conclude that

\kappa\in[\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma},1]\;\Longrightarrow\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}E_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\star}({\mathfrak{e}})=E\;,\\
\\
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})\;,\\
\\
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})=\mathfrak{e}\max\left(0,1-2\gamma\right)\;,\end{array}\right.

(83)

where,
E_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\star}
represents the energy value maximizing
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
in the interval
[0,E]
. In the derivation above we used the fact that
\bar{\mathcal{E}}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};\mathfrak{e})
in this parameter region is a monotonically increasing convex function of
\mathfrak{e}
, so
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})
is provided by the linear interpolation between its final value (i.e.
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};1)
) and initial value (i.e.
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};0)=0
).
For
\kappa<\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}
instead,
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
is concave and can achieve its maximum inside the allowed domain
[0,1]
.
A study of the first order derivative of the function allows us to verify that, if
\kappa\geq 4\gamma-1
(a region which incidentally fully includes also the cases
\kappa\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}
analyzed before), the maximum is still achieved for
{\mathfrak{e}}=1
, meaning that
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
remains monotonous w.r.t. to
{\mathfrak{e}}
.
In this case we can hence write

\forall\kappa\in[4\gamma-1,\tfrac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}]\;\Longrightarrow\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}E_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\star}({\mathfrak{e}})={\mathfrak{e}}\;,\\
\\
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})\;,\\
\\
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};\mathfrak{e})\;,\end{array}\right.

(84)

where now the value of
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})
coincides with

\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};\mathfrak{e})
since the latter is a concave function.
Finally, for
\kappa\leq 4\gamma-1
the maximum of
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
is achieved for

\displaystyle E_{\kappa,\gamma}:=\tfrac{-\kappa+\sqrt{\gamma-\kappa(1-\gamma)}}{2(\gamma-\kappa)}\;,

(85)

implying

\kappa\in[0,4\gamma-1]\;\Longrightarrow\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}E_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\star}({\mathfrak{e}})=\min\{{\mathfrak{e}},E_{\kappa,\gamma}\}\;,\\
\\
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};E_{\kappa,\gamma}^{\star}({\mathfrak{e}}))\;,\\
\\
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};\mathfrak{e})\;,\end{array}\right.

(86)

where, to express
\chi(D_{\kappa,\gamma},\mathfrak{e})
, we used the fact that since
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};{\mathfrak{e}})
is concave then

\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(D_{\kappa,\gamma};\mathfrak{e})
is concave too.

IV
Discussion

In this article we examined to what extent a QB composed of an arbitrary number of q-cells can maintain its initial energy after the action of environmental noise. In particular, we focused on the self-discharging process of the battery as a result of contact with a thermal environment and we studied how the loss of coherence due to extra dephasing noise affects the stability of a QB. We did so by adopting the framework of the quantum work capacitances and MAWERs.
More explicitly, among other considerations:

•

In Theorem
1
we show how the capacitance and the MAWER associated with a specific functional (e.g. the ergotropy) are directly and formally related.

•

In Sec.
III.1
, addressing the ADC, we provide an analytical expression for the maximizer input energy of
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}
, which are consequently also found analytically. We show how for the ADC
C_{\rm loc}=C_{\rm loc,sep}=\chi=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and provide upper and lower bounds for
C_{\cal E}
. Remarkably, concerning the MAWER, we prove that
{\cal J}_{\cal E}={\cal J}_{\rm sep}={\cal J}_{\rm loc}={\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}
and give their exact value.

•

In Sec.
III.2
, addressing the GADC, as for the ADC we find the analytical values for the input energy maximizer of
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}
, the analytical expression of
C_{\rm loc}=C_{\rm loc,sep}=\chi=C_{\rm sep}=\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and upper and lower bounds for
C_{\cal E}
.
For MAWERs we show that

{\cal J}_{\cal E}={\cal J}_{\rm sep}={\cal J}_{\rm loc}={\cal J}_{\rm loc,sep}
and these have an analytical expression.

•

In Sec.
III.3
we analyzed the simultaneous action of dephasing and ADC. We found, for various regimes of the channel parameters, analytical expressions for the input energy maximizer of
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}
, the values of
\chi
,
C_{\rm loc}
and
\cal{J}_{\rm loc}
.

Interestingly, in the case of the generalized amplitude damping channel, the analysis shows that quantum coherence can enhance the efficiency of energy storage, while this advantage is lost in the presence of sufficiently large dephasing. Remarkably, in the special case of a QB in contact with a very high temperature environment (i.e. for GADCs in the limit
\eta\to\frac{1}{2}
), the battery is able to preserve all of its input ergotropy.

Building on these considerations and results, this work may serve as a starting point for a more realistic analysis of the performance of quantum batteries. While noisy channels as the ones analyzed in this paper are in general simplified models of those occurring in experimental setups, our findings can be of assistance in experiment design by providing indications of the level of noise resistance and storing strategies required to observe a quantum advantage for energy storage quantum devices.

Appendix A
Ergotropy: a brief review

The maximization on the right-hand-side of Eq. (
2
) admits a closed expression in terms of the passive counterpart of
\hat{\rho}
, i.e. the
density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{\text{pass}}
obtained by operating on the latter via
a unitary rotation that transforms its eigenvectors
\{|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle\}_{\ell}
into the eingenvectors
\{|E_{\ell}\rangle\}_{\ell}
of the system Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
, matching the corresponding eingenvalues in reverse order, i.e.

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\rho}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\lambda_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{\ell}|\\
\\
\hat{H}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}E_{\ell}|E_{\ell}\rangle\!\langle E_{\ell}|\end{array}\right\}\mapsto\hat{\rho}_{\text{pass}}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\lambda_{\ell}|E_{\ell}\rangle\!\langle E_{\ell}|\;,

(94)

where for all
\ell=1,\cdots,d-1
, we set
\lambda_{\ell}\geq\lambda_{\ell+1}
and

E_{\ell}\leq E_{\ell+1}
. Explicitly we can write

\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})-\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{pass}};\hat{H})=\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\lambda_{\ell}E_{\ell}\;.

(95)

Notice that for the special case in which the quantum system is a qubit with Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=|1\rangle\langle 1|
,
Eq. (
95
) gives,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\langle 1|\hat{\rho}|1\rangle-\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\rho})\;,

(96)

with
\lambda_{\min}(\hat{\rho})
the minimum eigenvalue of
\hat{\rho}
.

The total-ergotropy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
is a regularized version of

\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
that emerges when considering scenarios where one has at disposal an arbitrary large number of identical copies of the input state
\hat{\rho}
.
Formally it is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho}^{\otimes N};\hat{H}^{(N)})}{N}\;,

(97)

where for fixed
N
integer,
\hat{H}^{(N)}
is the total Hamiltonian of the
N
copies of the system obtained by assigning to each of them the same
\hat{H}
(no interactions being included).
One can show
Alicki and Fannes 2013
;
Salvia and Giovannetti 2022
that the limit in Eq. (
97
) exists and corresponds to the maximum of
\frac{\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho}^{\otimes N};\hat{H}^{(N)})}{N}
with respect to all possible
N
, implying in particular that
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
is at least as large as
\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\sup_{N\geq 1}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho}^{\otimes N};\hat{H}^{(N)})}{N}\geq\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})\;.

(98)

The case where the system has dimension 2 represents an exception to this rule as in this case one has that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\frac{\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho}^{\otimes N};\hat{H}^{(N)})}{N}=\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})\;,

(99)

for all inputs and for all
N
.
Most notably
\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
can be expressed via a single letter formula that mimics Eq. (
95
), i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{\rm tot}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})-\mathfrak{E}^{({\beta_{\star}})}_{{\small{\rm GIBBS}}}(\hat{H})\;,

(100)

where for
\beta\geq 0
and
Z_{\beta}({\hat{H}}):=\mbox{Tr}[e^{-\beta\hat{H}}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}e^{-\beta E_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}^{({\beta})}_{{\small{\rm GIBBS}}}(\hat{H})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\tau}_{\beta};\hat{H})=-\frac{d}{d\beta}\ln Z_{\beta}({\hat{H}})\;,

(101)

is the mean energy of the thermal Gibbs state

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{\beta}:=e^{-\beta\hat{H}}/Z_{\beta}({\hat{H}})\;,

(102)

with effective inverse temperature
\beta
; while finally
\beta_{\star}
is chosen so that
\hat{\rho}_{\beta_{\star}}

has the same von Neumann entropy of
\hat{\rho}
, i.e.

\displaystyle S_{\beta_{\star}}=S(\hat{\rho}):=-\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\rho}\ln\hat{\rho}]\;,

(103)

with
S_{\beta}:=-\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\tau}_{\beta}\ln\hat{\tau}_{\beta}]=-\beta\frac{d}{d\beta}\ln Z_{\beta}({\hat{H}})+\ln Z_{\beta}({\hat{H}})
.

For many-body quantum systems, local ergotropy is defined by restricting to only local transformations the optimization over the unitary set of Eq. (
2
), i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H}):=\max_{\hat{U}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{\rm loc}(d)}\Big\{\mathfrak{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})-\mathfrak{E}(\hat{U}\hat{\rho}\hat{U}^{\dagger};\hat{H})\Big\}\;.

(104)

By construction it provides a lower bound for
\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})
and in case the system is represented by
k
non-interacting particles (i.e. if
\hat{H}
is given by a sum of local terms
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{1}+\cdots+\hat{H}_{k}
) it reduces to the sum of local
contributions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm loc}(\hat{\rho};\hat{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}(\hat{\rho}_{k};\hat{H}_{k})\;,

(105)

where for
i=\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
the
\hat{\rho}_{i}
represents the reduced density matrix of the
i
-th subsystem of the model.

Appendix B
Alternative characterization of the MAWER

First of all we notice that for any quantum channel
\Lambda
the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(n)}(\Lambda;E)
is non-decreasing in
n
for any fixed input energy
E\in(0,\mathfrak{e}_{max}]
. I.e.
\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathcal{E}^{(n+1)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)\geq\mathcal{E}^{(n)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)
, this property is proven in
Tirone et al. 2024
. Due to this fact it easily follows that

\sup_{n\geq\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}_{max}\rceil}\mathcal{E}^{(n)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}^{(n)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)\;.

(106)

We now define the quantity
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)
as

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\diamond}(\Lambda):=\liminf_{E\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(n)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)}{E}\;,

(107)

clearly for any quantum channel
\Lambda

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)\leq\mathcal{J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)
we now need to prove the converse.

Lemma 1
.

For any quantum channel
\Lambda
the following equation holds true:

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)\geq\sup_{\mathfrak{e}\in(0,\mathfrak{e}_{max}]}\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}=\mathcal{J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)\;.

(108)

Proof.

By definition we have that for any
\mathfrak{e}\in(0,\mathfrak{e}_{max}]

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{E\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)\mathfrak{e})}{E}

(109)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{E\rightarrow\infty}\left(\tfrac{\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1}{E}\right)\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\diamond}^{(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)}(\Lambda;(\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1)\mathfrak{e})}{\lceil E/\mathfrak{e}\rceil-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\diamond}(\Lambda;\mathfrak{e})}{\mathfrak{e}}\;,

in the last passage we used the fact that for all the terms of the expression the limit inferior is equal to the limit, so we conclude
∎

Thanks to Lemma
1
we can now state that the MAWER can be written as

\mathcal{J}_{\diamond}(\Lambda)=\lim_{E\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(n)}_{\diamond}(\Lambda;E)}{E}\;,

(110)

here the limits do not commute.

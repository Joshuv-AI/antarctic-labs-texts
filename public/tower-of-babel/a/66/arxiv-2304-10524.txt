Learning Narrow One-Hidden-Layer ReLU Networks
Sitan Chen, Zehao Dou, Surbhi Goel, Adam Klivans, Raghu Meka

Abstract
We consider the well-studied problem of learning a linear combination of k k ReLU activations with respect to a Gaussian distribution on inputs in d d dimensions. We give the first polynomial-time algorithm that succeeds whenever k k is a constant. All prior polynomial-time learners require additional assumptions on the network, such as positive combining coefficients or the matrix of hidden weight vectors being well-conditioned. Our approach is based on analyzing random contractions of higher-order moment tensors. We use a multi-scale analysis to argue that sufficiently close neurons can be collapsed together, sidestepping the conditioning issues present in prior work. This allows us to design an iterative procedure to discover individual neurons.

Introduction

We study the problem of PAC learning one-hidden-layer ReLU networks from labeled examples. In particular, we consider ReLU networks with
k
neurons:

f_{\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}(x)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}\cdot\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)

(1)

where
\mathsf{relu}(a)=\max(0,a)
is the ReLU activation, and

(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
where

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
and

This problem has inspired a large body of research in the machine learning community and acts as a benchmark for the design and analysis of novel learners.
The goal is to design provably efficient (sample complexity and running time being polynomial in the problem parameters

) algorithms to PAC learn this class of functions under minimal assumptions.
The most common assumption is that

Even under this assumption, no known algorithm achieves runtime and sample complexity

This paper presents the first algorithm for PAC learning one-hidden-layer ReLU networks under Gaussian inputs that succeeds whenever

.

to guarantee recovery of a
k
-dimensional subspace such that for all
i\in[k]
,
|\lambda_{i}|\|u_{i}-v_{i}\|^{2}
is small, which will guarantee small loss. Our first idea is to take
M
to be a suitable linear combination of the moment tensor contractions
M_{\ell}^{g}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}\langle u_{i},g\rangle^{\ell-2}\cdot u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}
. That is, we would like to find coefficients
\{\alpha_{s}\}_{s\in[k]}
such that

\sum_{s=1}^{k}\alpha_{s}M_{2s}^{g}\approx\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}\,.

As long as the entries of
\alpha
are not too large in magnitude, we can use a net-argument to brute-force over the choices of
\alpha
and run
[
DKKZ20
]
for each choice of
\alpha
. Showing that there exists such
\alpha_{s}
for
s\in[k]
reduces to showing that for all
i\in[k]

\sum_{s=1}^{k}\alpha_{s}v_{i}^{2(s-1)}=\textnormal{sgn}(\lambda_{i}).

This is equivalent to showing the existence of a univariate polynomial
p
of degree
k-1
with bounded norm that matches the sign pattern of
\lambda
on inputs
\{v_{1}^{2},\ldots,v_{k}^{2}\}
.

If we had that
|v_{i}^{2}-v_{j}^{2}|
was lower bounded for all
i\neq j
, then using the following condition number bound for the
k\times k
Vandermonde matrix generated by
v_{1}^{2},\ldots,v_{k}^{2}
would give us the desired
\alpha
with bounded norm dependent on the gap:

Lemma 3.1

(E.g., Fact 5.10 from
[
CLLZ22
]
)
.

Given an
m\times m
Vandermonde matrix
V
generated by nodes
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
for which
|a_{i}-a_{j}|\geq\Delta
for all
i\neq j
, and given
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, there exists
\alpha
for which
V\alpha=c
such that
\lVert\alpha\rVert\leq m(1/\Delta)^{2m-2}\lVert c\rVert
.

Unfortunately, as we make no assumptions on the hidden weight vectors, it is not necessarily the case that
v^{2}_{1},\ldots,v^{2}_{k}
are well-separated. Nevertheless, we could try clumping together very close
v_{i}^{2}
’s into a single representative node (and adding their corresponding combining coefficients) so that each clump is well-separated while the approximation error from clumping does not blow up. In order to formally define clumping at scale
\gamma
, consider a graph on the indices with an edge between indices
i,j
if
|v_{i}^{2}-v_{j}^{2}|\leq\gamma
. Then a clumping is specified by a set of disjoint connected subgraphs in this graph. The main challenge is that if we clump things together at scale
\gamma
, then when we use the above result to construct
\{\alpha_{s}\}
, our coefficients are of magnitude
O(1/\gamma^{k})
. This would lead to an
O(1/\gamma^{k})
blow up in the error for the indices within each clump when we approximate them by the representative node for that clump.

If we can find a scale
\gamma
such that the elements in any clump are
\approx\gamma^{k}
-close while the clumps are
\gamma
-separated from each other, then this blow up does not hurt us. It turns out that we can always find a scale
\gamma=\varepsilon^{k^{c}}
for some
c\in[0,k-1]
that satisfies this
\gamma
versus
\gamma^{k}
“gap.” To prove this, suppose we are at a scale
\varepsilon^{k^{c}}
and this property is not satisfied. Then there must be two clumps that are separated by
<\varepsilon^{k^{c}}
, therefore, when we go up a scale
\varepsilon^{k^{c-1}}
then these two clumps would be combined together. This implies that whenever our condition is not satisfied, going up a scale reduces the number of clumps by 1. However, there can be at most
k
clumps at the smallest scale. Thus at some scale, we must either have our desired gap or we can clump everything together. If we can clump everything together, this implies that the original network is well-approximated by at most two neurons, and we can learn these neurons directly.

This attempt would give us a runtime of
(d/\varepsilon)^{k^{O(k)}}
. This argument can be formalized, however we only present the high-level intuition since our main result improves over this significantly. At a high level, the improvement is as follows. So far, we have given an approach that tries to learn the network in “one shot” by looking for a single scale at which there is a gap for all clumps simultaneously. Instead, in our improved algorithm, we learn the network over multiple steps. In each step, we identify disjoint clumps at several different scales such that each clump has a gap
for its corresponding scale
, and use PCA to learn the neurons within these clumps. It turns out that instead of going down to scales as small as
\varepsilon^{k^{k}}
, now it suffices to go down to scale
\varepsilon^{k^{\log k}}
(Lemma
4.6
). We then prove that we can find enough such clumps at each step that after
O(\log k)
steps, we can approximate the entire network, thus yielding an improved runtime of
(d/\varepsilon)^{k^{\log^{2}k}}
.

4
Recursively Learning in Time
d^{k^{O(\log^{2}k)}}

As the algorithmic guarantee in Theorem
1.1
only beats brute force for
k\ll\mathrm{poly}(d)
, throughout we will assume this to simplify some estimates.

For some
S\subseteq[k]
, suppose we have access to
(\widehat{\lambda}_{i},\widehat{u}_{i})_{i\in S}
for which

\lVert f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}-f_{0,\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}}}-f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda}_{S^{c}},\mathbf{u}_{S^{c}}}\rVert\leq\xi\,.

(12)

In other words,
f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}-f_{0,\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}}}
, i.e. the difference between the ground truth and what we have learned so far, is close to

plus the subnetwork of

For notational convenience, we assume without loss of generality that

, where
{k_{\sf res}}\triangleq k-|S|
.
Define the parameters

\varepsilon^{\prime}\triangleq\varepsilon/\mathrm{poly}(d,k,\mathcal{R})\qquad\Lambda\triangleq\mathrm{poly}(d\mathcal{R}/\varepsilon)\cdot\xi^{1/k^{\Theta(\log k)}}\qquad\underline{\gamma}\triangleq\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda\varepsilon}{\mathcal{R}d}\Bigr)^{k^{\Theta(\log k)}}\,.

The parameter

will be sufficiently small that

.

One important case in which we will show it is possible to learn a neuron

is when there is a significant

among the distances from other

to

\displaystyle S^{\sf far}_{i}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\triangleq\{j\in[{k_{\sf res}}]:|v_{j}-v_{i}|\geq\gamma\}

\displaystyle S^{\sf close}_{i}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\triangleq\{j\in[{k_{\sf res}}]:|v_{j}-v_{i}|\leq T(\gamma)\}\,,

where

T(\gamma)\triangleq\frac{\Lambda^{10}}{\mathcal{R}^{2}}\,(\gamma/d)^{\Theta(k)}\,.

(17)

Definition 4.1
.

Given

and

, we say that

is a

if
[{k_{\sf res}}]=S^{\sf close}_{i}(\gamma)\sqcup S^{\sf far}_{i}(\gamma)
and

. Further, we say that

is

.

Figure 1:
Illustration of

and
S^{\sf far}_{i}(\gamma)
. The figure shows that

\gamma
is not a gapped scale for

.

In this section we will show that one of two things can happen which will allow us to learn part of or all of
f_{\sf res}
.

1.

If
\max_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}-\min_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
(this includes the case where
{k_{\sf res}}=0
), then we can find an approximation to
f_{\sf res}
as a linear combination of two ReLUs and terminate.

2.

There exists at least one pair
(i,\gamma)\in[{k_{\sf res}}]\times[\underline{\gamma},1)
such that
\gamma
is a gapped scale for
i
. In this case, let
\mathcal{J}
be any set of such pairs
(i,\gamma)
such that the sets
S^{\sf close}_{i}(\gamma)
are all disjoint (we will specify the precise
\mathcal{J}
that we work with later).
We will show that:

(a)

For those
(i,\gamma)\in\mathcal{J}
for which

is detectable at scale
\gamma
, we can use the different

to construct a net containing a vector close to
u_{j}
for some
j\in S^{\sf close}_{i}(\gamma)
.

(b)

For all other
(i,\gamma)\in\mathcal{J}
, the subnetwork

of

given by the corresponding neurons in

is well-approximated by the zero function.

Suppose we know which
(i,\gamma)\in\mathcal{J}
fall into Case 2a versus Case 2b (recall that our algorithm is nondeterministic, so along some computation path we will have correctly guessed these). Then after brute-forcing over weights
\widehat{\lambda}
to assign to the neurons learned in Case 2a, we can update the set of pairs

to a set of pairs

for some

such that

\lVert f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}-f_{0,\widetilde{\mathbf{\lambda}},\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}}-f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda}_{{S^{\prime}}^{c}},\mathbf{u}_{{S^{\prime}}^{c}}}\rVert\leq\xi^{*}

for some new error

is all of

and we have learned an approximation to the entire original network

4.1
Moment tensor estimations

Here we show how to estimate
T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda}_{[{k_{\sf res}}]},\mathbf{u}_{[{k_{\sf res}}]})
using the parameters
(\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}})
as well as Gaussian samples
\{(x_{a},y_{a})\}_{a\in[N]}
labeled by the original function
f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}
. We use the following standard guarantee for approximately recovering moment tensors from samples, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
B.1
.

Lemma 4.2

(Moment estimation)
.

Let
\ell\in\{1,2,4,6,\ldots\}
. Let

c_{\ell}\triangleq\begin{cases}1/2&\text{if}\ \ell=1\\
\frac{\mathrm{He}_{\ell}(0)+\ell H_{\ell-2}(0)}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\ell!}}&\text{if}\ \ell\ \text{even}\end{cases}

(19)

Given samples
\{(x_{i},f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}(x_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,N}
for
x_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Id)
and
N\geq\ell^{O(\ell)}d^{2\ell}\mathcal{R}^{2}/\xi^{2}
, the tensor
\widehat{T}=\frac{1}{2c_{\ell}N}\sum^{k}_{i=1}f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}(x_{i})\cdot S_{\ell}(x_{i})
, where
S_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-th normalized probabilist’s Hermite tensor, satisfies
\lVert\widehat{T}-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})\rVert_{F}\leq\xi
if
\ell
is even, and otherwise satisfies
\lVert\widehat{T}-w\rVert_{2}\leq\xi
if
\ell=1
.

The following lemma, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
B.2
, shows that we can approximate the Hermite coefficients of
f_{\sf res}
by empirically estimating the Hermite coefficients of
f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}-f_{0,\widehat{\mathbf{\lambda}},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}}
:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

\xi^{\prime}\triangleq\mathrm{poly}(d)\cdot(\mathcal{R}+\lVert\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}}\rVert_{1})\cdot\xi\,.

(20)

Given the parameters
(\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}})
and Gaussian samples
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{N},y_{N})
labeled by the original function
f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}
, we have for all
\ell=2,\ldots,2k+2
that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{a=1}(y_{a}-f_{0,\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}}}(x_{a}))\,x_{a}-w\right\|\leq\xi^{\prime}

(21)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2c_{\ell}N}\sum^{N}_{a=1}(y_{a}-f_{0,\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}}}(x_{a}))\,S_{\ell}(x_{a})-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda}_{[{k_{\sf res}}]},\mathbf{u}_{[{k_{\sf res}}]})\right\|\leq\xi^{\prime}

(22)

provided
N\geq d^{O(k)}\,(\mathcal{R}^{2}+\lVert\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}}\rVert^{2}_{1})/\xi^{\prime 2}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that if instead of getting samples of the form
(x,f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}(x))
, we had samples of the form
(x,f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}(x)+\zeta)
where
\zeta
is independent mean-zero noise, then the estimators for
T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})
and
w
defined above are still unbiased estimators for these quantities. Our algorithm uses its samples solely to form these estimators, so provided
\zeta
has bounded second moment so that the variance of these estimators is not too large, our algorithm will still work in the presence of such label noise.

4.2
Case 1: two-neuron approximation

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
\max_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}-\min_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Then there is an efficient algorithm that takes the parameters
(\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}})
as well as
\mathrm{poly}(d)(\mathcal{R}^{2}+\lVert\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}}\rVert^{2}_{1})/\xi^{\prime 2}
samples from the original function
f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}
and outputs weights
\mu^{+},\mu^{-}
and a vector

such that the network

h\triangleq\mu^{+}\mathsf{relu}(\langle u,\cdot\rangle)+\mu^{-}\mathsf{relu}(\langle-u,\cdot\rangle)

(23)

satisfies
\lVert f_{w,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}-f_{0,\mathbf{\widehat{\lambda}},\mathbf{\widehat{u}}}-h\rVert\lesssim\mathrm{poly}(d,\mathcal{R})(\varepsilon^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime})
.

Sketch, see Appendix
B.3
.

The condition on
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k_{\sf res}}
and the fact that projection along

roughly preserves distances among

implies that all of the weight vectors in
f_{\sf res}
are close in Euclidean distance. As a result,
f_{\sf res}
is well-approximated by a ReLU network which only depends on the projection of the input to a single direction, i.e. a width-2 network whose weight vectors are
u
and
-u
for some vector

. This can be done by considering suitable linear combinations of the first Hermite coefficients of

, namely

, together with a contraction of the second moment matrix, which is simply
\sum_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}\lambda_{i}v_{i}u_{i}
, both of which can be estimated from samples by Lemma

.
∎

4.3
Existence of a gapped scale

As a warmup, here we show that if we are not in Case 1, there exists
i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]
for which there is a gapped scale
\gamma
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
\max_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}-\min_{i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]}v_{i}>\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Then there exists at least one index
i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]
for which there is a gapped scale
\gamma
.

Proof.

In this proof, assume without loss of generality that
v_{1}\leq\cdots\leq v_{k_{\sf res}}
, and define
\delta_{i}\triangleq v_{i}-v_{i-1}
for
1<i\leq{k_{\sf res}}
. By averaging, there exists some
i_{0}
for which
\delta_{i_{0}}\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}}
. Suppose without loss of generality that
i_{0}>{k_{\sf res}}/2
.

If
\delta_{j}\leq T(\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}
for all
i_{0}<j\leq{k_{\sf res}}
, then
\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}}
is a gapped scale for
i_{0}
. Otherwise, there exists
i_{0}<i_{1}\leq{k_{\sf res}}
for which
\delta_{i_{1}}>T(\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}
. Suppose without loss of generality that
i_{1}
is closer to
{k_{\sf res}}
than
i_{0}
.

Again, if
\delta_{j}\leq T(T(\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}
for all
i_{1}<j\leq{k_{\sf res}}
, then
T(\varepsilon^{\prime}/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}
is a gapped scale for
i_{0}
. Otherwise, we can continue this binary search procedure at most
O(\log k)
times.

Finally, we verify that
\underline{\gamma}
is smaller than the result of iterating
z\mapsto T(z/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}
for
O(\log k)
times starting from
\varepsilon^{\prime}
, which will prove that there exists a gapped scale
\gamma
for some
i\in[{k_{\sf res}}]
.

To verify this, note that
T(z/{k_{\sf res}})/{k_{\sf res}}\geq T(z/k)/k\geq(\Lambda^{10}/\mathcal{R}^{2})(z/d)^{\Theta(k)}
. So iterating this

times starting from

gives a quantity which is at least
\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda\varepsilon}{\mathcal{R}d}\Bigr)^{k^{\Theta(\log k)}}\approx\underline{\gamma}
.
∎

\mathcal{S}
contains a vector which is

Proof.

We will denote

Since

\displaystyle 2\eta\lVert r_{i}\rVert^{2}\geq r_{i}^{\top}M_{\ell}r_{i}\geq C_{1}\lVert r_{i}\rVert^{4}-C_{2}.

This gives us that

.
∎

4.5
Case 2b: ignoring undetectable neurons

Here we show that if there are undetectable neurons, then we can approximate them by zero:

Lemma 4.10

4.7
Power sum estimate

In this section we prove a technical claim which is essential to correctness of the PCA-based procedure described in Case 2a from Section
4.4
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k
, and let
q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a vector such that
|\sum^{k^{\prime}}_{i=1}q_{i}|\geq\tau
and
\lVert q\rVert_{\infty}\leq R
. If
v\in[-1,1]^{k}
satisfies

1.

|v_{1}|\geq\alpha

2.

|v_{1}-v_{i}|\leq\beta
for all
1\leq i\leq k^{\prime}
,

3.

|v_{i}-v_{j}|\geq\gamma
for all
1\leq i\leq k^{\prime}<j\leq k
.

for some parameters
0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma<1
, then there exists an even integer
0\leq\ell\leq 2k
for which

|\langle v^{\odot\ell},q\rangle|\gtrsim\frac{\tau}{2k}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4k}\Bigr)^{k}-CRk(k^{\prime}-1)\beta

(50)

for some absolute constant
C>0
.

To interpret this lemma, it is helpful to first consider the special case where
k^{\prime}=1
. In this case, there is a single entry,
v_{1}
, of non-negligible magnitude which is separated from all other entries by a margin of
\gamma
. The claim is that for any vector
q
that with a non-negligible first entry, there is some entrywise power of
v
which has non-negligible correlation with
q
. Importantly, this holds even if the other entries of
v
are not well-separated. As such one can interpret this result as some kind of
robust local inverse
for the Vandermonde matrix: even if the
k\times k
Vandermonde matrix generated by the nodes
v^{2}_{1},\ldots,v^{2}_{k}
is ill-conditioned, it is well-conditioned in directions that place sufficient mass on coordinates corresponding to nodes which are separated from the other entries. We also remark that the
\gamma^{k}
scaling is qualitatively tight by the following example: consider
v^{\odot 2}=(1,1-\gamma,\ldots,1-(k-1)\gamma)
and
q=(\binom{k-1}{0},-\binom{k-1}{1},\binom{k-1}{2},\ldots,(-1)^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{k-1})
. Then one can check that
\langle v^{\odot\ell},q\rangle=0
for all
\ell=0,\ldots,2k-2
, and for
\ell=k
, this equals
k!\gamma^{k}
.

For general
k^{\prime}
, note that the bound in the lemma is non-vacuous provided
\beta
scales with
\gamma^{k}
, and this “gap” between
\gamma
and
\gamma^{k}
is the central motivation behind our definition of
T(\gamma)
and the different scales considered in the analysis of Case 2a in Section
4.4
.

Proof of Lemma
4.15
.

We first reduce to the case where
k^{\prime}=1
. Consider modifying
v,q
follows. Remove from
v
entries
2,\ldots,k^{\prime}
. Also set the first entry of
q
to be
\sum^{k^{\prime}}_{i=1}q_{i}
and remove from
q
entries
2,\ldots,k^{\prime}
. As
\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\lVert q\rVert_{\infty}\leq R
, this changes every
\langle v^{\odot\ell},q\rangle
by at most

\sum^{k^{\prime}}_{i=1}q_{i}(v^{\ell}_{1}-v^{\ell}_{i})=\sum^{k^{\prime}}_{i=2}q_{i}(v^{\ell}_{1}-v^{\ell}_{i})\lesssim R\ell(k^{\prime}-1)\beta\lesssim Rk(k^{\prime}-1)\beta\,.

(51)

It therefore suffices to prove the lemma for
k^{\prime}=1
, so henceforth we specialize to this case.

Suppose to the contrary that for all
\ell=0,2,4,\ldots,2k-2
,
|\langle v^{\odot\ell},q\rangle|\leq\zeta
for

\zeta\triangleq\frac{\tau}{2k}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4k}\Bigr)^{k}\,.

(52)

Next, note that

\langle v^{\odot\ell},q\rangle=\sum^{k}_{i=1}q_{i}v^{\ell}_{i}=v^{\ell}_{1}\sum^{k}_{i=1}q_{i}(v^{2}_{i}/v^{2}_{1})^{\ell/2},

(53)

so if we define
v^{\prime}_{i}\triangleq v^{2}_{i}/v^{2}_{1}
, then we have that
|\langle v^{\prime\odot\ell/2},q\rangle|\leq\zeta/v_{1}^{\ell}\leq\zeta\alpha^{-2k}
. Furthermore, for all
j>1
, we have
|v^{\prime}_{j}-1|\geq\gamma^{2}/v^{2}_{1}\geq\gamma^{2}
. Additionally,
|v^{\prime}_{i}|\leq 1/\alpha^{2}
for all
i\in[k]
.

Define
\varepsilon_{i}=v^{\prime 2}_{i}-1
so that
\varepsilon_{1}=0
and

|\varepsilon_{i}|\geq\gamma^{2}

(54)

for
i>1
. Then for all
\ell=0,2,4,\ldots,2k-2
,

\langle v^{\prime\odot\ell/2}-\mathbf{1},q\rangle=\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}((1+\varepsilon_{i})^{\ell/2}-1)=\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\sum^{\ell/2}_{s=1}\binom{\ell/2}{s}\varepsilon_{i}^{s}=\sum^{\ell/2}_{s=1}\binom{\ell/2}{s}\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{s}.

(55)

As
|\langle v^{\prime\odot\ell/2}-\mathbf{1},q\rangle|\leq 2\zeta\alpha^{-2k}
and

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=1}q_{i}\varepsilon^{\ell/2}_{i}\Bigr|\leq|\langle v^{\prime\odot\ell/2}-\mathbf{1},q\rangle|+\sum^{\ell/2-1}_{s=1}\binom{\ell/2}{s}\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{s}_{i}\Bigr|\leq 2\zeta\alpha^{-2k}+2^{k}\sum^{\ell/2-1}_{s=1}\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{s}_{i}\Bigr|

(56)

by induction we find that for all
1\leq s<k
,

\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{s}_{i}\Bigr|\lesssim(2k/\alpha^{2})^{k}\cdot\zeta

(57)

We also have that

\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\Bigr|\geq|q_{1}|-\zeta\alpha^{-2k}\geq\tau-\zeta\alpha^{-2k}>\tau/2\,.

(58)

We now use Lemma
4.16
to draw a contradiction. Taking
\ell=k-1
and
z=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})
in the lemma, we find that

\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{k-1}_{i}=\sum^{k-2}_{s=0}(-1)^{k-s}p_{k-1-i}(\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{s}_{i}

(59)

where here
p_{s}
denotes the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
s
on
k-1
variables.
We can rearrange to get

-p_{k-1}(\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}=-\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\varepsilon^{k-1}_{i}+\sum^{k-2}_{s=1}(-1)^{k-s}p_{k-1-i}(\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\cdot\sum^{k-2}_{i=1}q_{i}\varepsilon^{s}_{i}

(60)

As
|\varepsilon_{i}|\in[\gamma^{2},1]
,
p_{k-1-s}(\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\leq 2^{k}
for all
s
, and
|p_{k-1}(\varepsilon_{2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})|\geq\gamma^{2k}
. So by triangle inequality,

\Bigl|\sum^{k}_{i=2}q_{i}\Bigr|\leq k\,\Bigl(\frac{4k}{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}}\Bigr)^{k}\cdot\zeta\leq\tau/2,

(61)

which contradicts (
58
).
∎

The above proof used the following basic fact:

Lemma 4.16
.

Given
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{K})
, let
v_{\ell}
denote the vector
(z^{\ell}_{1},\ldots,z^{\ell}_{K})
. Let
p_{\ell}
denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
\ell
on
K
variables. Then

v_{K}=\sum^{K-1}_{s=0}(-1)^{K-s+1}p_{K-s}(z)\cdot v_{s}

(62)

4.8
Validation

Now we have a set of candidate estimators, one of which is guaranteed to be close to the ground truth network in square loss. We will use a validation set of fresh samples to estimate the loss of each of these estimators and pick the best predictor. In order to guarantee that this predictor will have low test loss, we will prove a concentration property of ReLU networks (as in
[
CKM22
]
). Notice that for any estimator function

. For

is at most

, so if

are the neurons remaining after the first step of the algorithm, the vector with entries consisting of

is a valid

in the noisy clumping game. See Figure

6
Conclusion and Future Directions

In this paper, we provided the first PAC learning algorithm that learns narrow (constant
k
) one-hidden-layer neural networks in polynomial time. Our algorithm used random contractions of higher order moment tensors and subsequently performed an iterative procedure to repeatedly learn some clumps of neurons. This allowed us to avoid depending on the condition number of the weight matrix, which was a core assumption in several prior works.

One obvious drawback of our technique is the unsatisfactory dependence on
k
in our runtime
d^{k^{\mathcal{O}(\log^{2}k)}}
. Note that known CSQ hardness results rule out the possibility of improving to
d^{o(k)}
for our algorithm, but we leave as an interesting open question closing the gap between our upper bound and these lower bounds. Furthermore, harnessing the power of non-CSQ style approaches as in
[
CM20
,
CKM22
]
could potentially allow us to get an algorithm that runs in time
\mathrm{poly}(d)\cdot(1/\varepsilon)^{h(k)}
for some function
h
.

Another worthwhile direction is to investigate other applications of the power sum estimate technique proposed in our work to tensor problems in the absence of separation conditions.

Appendix A
Deferred Preliminaries

A.1
Hermite polynomials

Recall the definition of the probabilist’s Hermite polynomials:

H_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\cdot\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}.

Under this definition, the first four Hermite polynomials are

H_{0}(x)=1,~~H_{1}(x)=x,~~H_{2}(x)=x^{2}-1,~~H_{3}(x)=x^{3}-3x.

The Hermite polynomials comprise an orthogonal basis of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega)

where
\omega
is the standard Gaussian measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
In this function space, the inner product is defined as
\langle f,g\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}[f(x)g(x)]
.
Under this inner product, we have

\langle H_{m},H_{n}\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}H_{m}(x)H_{n}(x)=m!\cdot\mathbb{I}[m=n].

The
normalized
probabilist’s Hermite polynomials are given by
\widehat{H}_{\ell}(x)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell!}}H_{\ell}(x)
; these comprise an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\omega)
. Finally, given
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define the
Hermite tensor

S_{\ell}(x)\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{\otimes\ell}
to be the tensor whose
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})
-th entry is given as follows. Suppose that element
j\in[d]
appears among
i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}
a total of
n_{j}
times. Then the
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})
-th entry of
S_{\ell}
is given by
\prod^{d}_{j=1}\widehat{H}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})
.

A.2
Measuring distance between networks

Here, we will show that parameter closeness implies closeness in the square loss, which also implies the closeness between moment tensors.

Lemma A.1

(Lemma 3.3 from
[
CKM21
]
)
.

For any unit vectors
u,u^{\prime}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathsf{relu}(\langle u,x\rangle)-\mathsf{relu}(\langle u^{\prime},x\rangle)\right]^{2}\leq\frac{5}{6}\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}.

(73)

Lemma A.2
.

For two 2-layer ReLU networks
f_{\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}},f_{\mathbf{\lambda}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}
with the same width
k
and for which

\lVert\mathbf{\lambda}\rVert_{1},\lVert\mathbf{\lambda}^{\prime}\rVert_{1}\leq\mathcal{R}
for some
\mathcal{R}>0
, we have

\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}-f_{\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}\|_{2}\lesssim k\max(1,\mathcal{R})\cdot d_{\sf param}((\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}),(\mathbf{\lambda}^{\prime},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})).

Proof.

By AM-GM, for any permutation
\pi
:

\displaystyle\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}-f_{\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\cdot\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)-\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime}\cdot\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{\pi(i)}^{\prime},x\rangle)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime}\cdot\left(\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)-\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{\pi(i)}^{\prime},x\rangle)\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(2k)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime})^{2}\cdot\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)^{2}\right]+(2k)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda_{i}^{\prime 2}\cdot\left(\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{i},x\rangle)-\mathsf{relu}(\langle u_{\pi(i)}^{\prime},x\rangle)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant k\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{5k}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{\pi(i)}^{\prime 2}\cdot\|u_{i}-u_{\pi(i)}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant k^{2}\cdot d_{\sf param}((\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}),(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}}))^{2}+\frac{5k^{2}}{3}\mathcal{R}^{2}\cdot d_{\sf param}((\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}),(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant k^{2}(1+2\mathcal{R}^{2})\cdot d_{\sf param}((\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}),(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}}))^{2},

where in the third step we used Lemma
A.1
. Therefore, we conclude that

\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}-f_{\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}\|_{2}\leqslant k\sqrt{1+2R^{2}}\cdot d_{\sf param}((\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}),(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})),

as claimed.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For the 2-norm of
f_{\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}
and the Frobenius norm of its moment tensor, it holds that:

\lVert T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})\rVert_{F}\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\cdot d^{\ell/2}\cdot\lVert f_{\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u}}\rVert_{2}.

Proof.

To prove this equation, we firstly need to decompose the ReLU activation function
\mathsf{relu}(\cdot)
with the Hermite polynomials. From Lemma A.2 of
[
GGJ+20
]
, we have:

\mathsf{relu}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}H_{k}(x),

where the coefficients are

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}},c_{1}=\frac{1}{2},~c_{2k}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(2k-3)!!}{\sqrt{2\pi}\cdot(2k)!}~(k\geqslant 1)~~\text{and}~~c_{2k+1}=0.

Note that, these coefficients are slightly different from
[
GGJ+20
]
since our definition of
H_{n}
is unnormalized (which means their
H_{n}
stands for our
\widehat{H}_{n}
).

Therefore, we can express
T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})
as:

\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}(x)\cdot H_{\ell}(x)\right]=\ell!\cdot c_{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}u_{i}^{\otimes\ell}=\ell!\cdot c_{\ell}T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u}).

Now, we take the Frobenius norm of both sides, we have:

\displaystyle\ell!\cdot\frac{(\ell-3)!!}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell!}\cdot\|T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}(x)\cdot H_{\ell}(x)\right]\right\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{\alpha\in[d]^{l}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}(x)\cdot H_{\ell,\alpha}(x)\right]\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\sum_{\alpha\in[d]^{l}}\mathbb{E}[f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}(x)^{2}]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[H_{\ell,\alpha}(x)^{2}\right]}=\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|H_{\ell}(x)\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle<\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}\cdot\sqrt{d^{\ell}\cdot\ell!}

Since
(\ell-3)!!<\sqrt{\ell!}
, we can conclude that:

\|T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})\|_{F}\leqslant\sqrt{2\pi}\cdot d^{\ell/2}\|f_{\lambda,\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}.

∎

Lemma A.4
.

For any unit vector
g\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, it holds that:

\lVert M^{g}_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})-M^{g}_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\leq\lVert T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})\rVert_{F}.

Proof.

Notice that:

M_{\ell}^{g}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})=T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})[g,\ldots,g,:,:],~~M_{\ell}^{g}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})=T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})[g,\ldots,g,:,:].

Then, for any unit vector

, we have:

\displaystyle v^{\top}\left(M_{\ell}^{g}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-M_{\ell}^{g}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\right)v=\left(T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\right)[g,\ldots,g,v,v]

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}),g\otimes\ldots\otimes g\otimes v\otimes v\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\right\|_{F}\cdot\left\|g\otimes\ldots\otimes g\otimes v\otimes v\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\right\|_{F}\cdot\left(\|g\|_{2}^{l-2}\|v\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leqslant\left\|T_{\ell}(\lambda,\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\lambda^{\prime},\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\right\|_{F},

which leads to the conclusion that

\lVert M^{g}_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})-M^{g}_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\leq\lVert T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{u})-T_{\ell}(\mathbf{\lambda^{\prime}},\mathbf{u^{\prime}})\rVert_{F}.

∎

Higgs-Sector Predictions from Maximally Symmetric multi-Higgs Doublet Models
*

Abstract
Maximally Symmetric n n -Higgs Doublet Models (MS- n n HDMs) define very economic settings that enable sharp Higgs-sector predictions beyond the Standard Model (SM) potentially testable at high-energy colliders. The scalar potential of a MS- n n HDM obeys an Sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{Sp(2}n) symmetry, which is softly broken by bilinear scalar masses and explicitly by hypercharge and Yukawa couplings through renormalisation-group effects. The Sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{Sp(2}n) also ensures natural SM alignment and allows for quartic coupling unification up to the Planck scale. As typical examples, we consider maximally symmetric realisations of the Type-II 2HDM and the Type-V 3HDM. We show how in terms of a few input parameters, definite predictions for the entire scalar mass spectrum of the MS-2HDM and MS-3HDM are obtained, including the SM-like Higgs-boson couplings to the gauge bosons and fermions.

2
The Higgs Spectrum of the Type-II 2HDM

The Higgs sector of the 2HDM is described by two scalar doublets
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
.
Performing the usual linear expansion of these scalar doublets about their VEVs, we may express them as

\Phi_{i}\ =\ \left(\begin{matrix}\phi_{i}^{+}\\
{1\over\sqrt{2}}(v_{i}+\phi_{i}+i\chi_{i})\end{matrix}\right)\;,

(3)

with
i=1,2
. In the case of CP-conserving Type-II 2HDM, both
scalar doublets receive real and nonzero vacuum
expectation values (VEVs). In detail, we have

\langle\Phi_{1}^{0}\rangle=v_{1}/\sqrt{2}
and

\langle\Phi_{2}^{0}\rangle=v_{2}/\sqrt{2}
, where
\tan\beta=v_{2}/v_{1}
and
v\equiv(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})^{1/2}
forms the VEV of the SM Higgs doublet.

Our first step is to transform all scalar fields from a weak basis with generic choice of vacua to the so-called Higgs basis
[
20
,
21
]
, where only one Higgs doublet acquires the SM VEV. This can be achieved by virtue of a common orthogonal transformation that involves
the mixing angles
\beta
, i.e.

\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}\\
H_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{R}(\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}G^{0}\\
a\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{R}(\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}\\
\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}G^{\pm}\\
h^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{R}(\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\pm}\\
\phi_{2}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where the two-dimensional rotational matrix is

\displaystyle R(\beta)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\cos\beta&\sin\beta\\
-\sin\beta&\cos\beta\end{array}\right).

After spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB), the
Z
and
W^{\pm}
gauge bosons become massive after absorbing in the unitary gauge the three would-be Goldstone bosons
G^{0}
and
G^{\pm}
, respectively
[
22
]
. Consequently, the 2HDM can account for only five physical scalar states: two
CP-even scalars (
h
,
H
), one CP-odd scalar
(a)
and two charged
bosons (
h^{\pm}
). The masses of the
a
and
h^{\pm}
scalars with
s_{\beta}\equiv\sin{\beta}
and
c_{\beta}\equiv\cos{\beta}
are given by

\displaystyle M_{a}^{2}\,=\,M_{h^{\pm}}^{2}+{v^{2}\over 2}(\lambda_{4}\,-\,\lambda_{5}),\quad M_{h^{\pm}}^{2}\,=\,{m_{12}^{2}\over c_{\beta}s_{\beta}}\,-\,{v^{2}\over 2}(\lambda_{4}\,+\,\lambda_{5})+{v^{2}\over 2c_{\beta}s_{\beta}}(\lambda_{6}c_{\beta}^{2}+\lambda_{7}s_{\beta}^{2}).

(7)

The masses of the two CP-even scalars,
h
and
H
may be obtained by
diagonalising the CP-even mass matrix
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{S}
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm S}^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{M}_{H}^{2}&0\\
0&{M}_{h}^{2}\end{array}\right)=\ R(\alpha)\,{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm S}^{2}\,{R(\alpha)}^{\sf T}=\ R(\alpha)\,\left(\begin{matrix}{A}\,&\,{C}\\
{C}\,&\,{B}\end{matrix}\right)\,{R(\alpha)}^{\sf T}.

The details of the above matrix are presented in
[
23
]
.
Additionally, the mixing angle
\alpha
may be determined by
\tan 2\alpha\,=\,{2C/A-B}
.

To this extend, one may obtain the SM-normalised couplings of the CP-even scalars,
h
and
H
, to the gauge bosons (
V\,=\,W^{\pm},Z
) as follows:

g_{hVV}=\sin(\beta-\alpha),\qquad g_{HVV}=\cos(\beta-\alpha).

(10)

Therefore, there are two scenarios to realise
the SM alignment limit: (i) SM-like
H
scenario with
\cos(\beta-\alpha)\to 1
, and (ii) SM-like
h
scenario
\sin(\beta-\alpha)\to 1
.
Here, we consider the first scenario with
\cos(\beta-\alpha)\to 1
.

In the Higgs basis, the CP-even mass matrix takes on the form

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{S}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\widehat{A}&\widehat{C}\\
\widehat{C}&\widehat{B}\end{matrix}\right)\,=\,R(\beta){\mathcal{M}_{S}}^{2}R(\beta)^{\sf T}\,,

(11)

with

\displaystyle\widehat{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2v^{2}\left[c_{\beta}^{4}\lambda_{1}+s_{\beta}^{2}c_{\beta}^{2}\lambda_{345}+s_{\beta}^{4}\lambda_{2}+2c_{\beta}s_{\beta}\left(c_{\beta}^{2}\lambda_{6}+s_{\beta}^{2}\lambda_{7}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\widehat{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{a}^{2}+\lambda_{5}v^{2}+2v^{2}\left[s_{\beta}^{2}c_{\beta}^{2}\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{345}\right)-c_{\beta}s_{\beta}\left(c_{\beta}^{2}-s_{\beta}^{2}\right)\left(\lambda_{6}-\lambda_{7}\right)\right],

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{2}\left[s_{\beta}^{3}c_{\beta}\left(2\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{345}\right)-c_{\beta}^{3}s_{\beta}\left(2\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{345}\right)+c_{\beta}^{2}\left(1-4s_{\beta}^{2}\right)\lambda_{6}+s_{\beta}^{2}\left(4c_{\beta}^{2}-1\right)\lambda_{7}\right].

Thereby, in the SM alignment limit
\beta=\alpha

the mass parameter
\widehat{A}
becomes equal to
M_{H}^{2}
, while the parameters
\widehat{C}
vanishes. Thus, in the limit
\widehat{C}\to 0
, we obtain the following conditions:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}={(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{5})/2},\quad\lambda_{6}=\lambda_{7}=0,

(13)

where by imposing symmetries identified in (
2
) with
n=2
the above relationships between the quartic couplings may be fulfilled naturally.

Moreover, the deviation of the
H
-boson couplings from their SM values in terms of the light-to-heavy scalar-mixing parameter
\theta_{\mathcal{S}}\equiv\widehat{C}/\widehat{B}
are given by

\displaystyle g_{HVV}\simeq 1-\theta_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}/2,\qquad g_{hVV}\simeq-{\theta_{\mathcal{S}}}.

(14)

where the mixing parameter
\theta_{\mathcal{S}}

vanishes in the exact SM alignment limit as

\alpha\to\beta
. Accordingly, we may derive approximate analytic expressions for the

h
- and
H
-boson couplings to up- and down-type quarks to leading order in
\theta_{\mathcal{S}}
, as

\displaystyle g_{Huu}

\displaystyle\simeq 1+{t^{-1}_{\beta}}\,{\theta_{\mathcal{S}}},\qquad\qquad g_{Hdd}\simeq 1-{\theta_{\mathcal{S}}}\,{t_{\beta}},

(15)

\displaystyle g_{huu}

\displaystyle\simeq-{\theta_{\mathcal{S}}}+{t^{-1}_{\beta}},\qquad\qquad g_{hdd}\simeq-{\theta_{\mathcal{S}}}-{t_{\beta}}.

In the SM alignment limit, we have
g_{Huu}\,\to\,1
and

g_{Hdd}\to
1. Obviously, any deviation of these couplings from their SM values is governed by quantities
\tan\beta
and
\theta_{\mathcal{S}}
.

The Higgs Spectrum of the Type-V 3HDM

In the case of CP-conserving Type-V 3HDM, the VEVs of scalar doublets,

\Phi_{i}

(i=1,2,3)

\displaystyle v_{1}\,=\,v\cos\beta_{1}\cos\beta_{2},\,\quad v_{2}\,=\,v\sin\beta_{1}\cos\beta_{2},\,\quad v_{3}\,=\,v\sin\beta_{2}.

(16)

Equivalently, the VEVs may be defined by the two ratios,
\tan\beta_{1}=v_{2}/v_{1}
and
\tan\beta_{2}=v_{3}/\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}

v\equiv\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{3}^{2}}

For later convenience, we proceed as in

and define the three-dimensional rotational matrices about the individual axes

\displaystyle R_{12}(\alpha)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha&0\\

In the case of 3HDM, the two mixing angles
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are required to transform all scalar fields to the Higgs basis with

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}\\

\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}G^{0}\\

\eta_{1}^{\pm}\\
\eta_{2}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{O}_{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\pm}\\
\phi_{2}^{\pm}\\
\phi_{3}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}.

(26)

Therefore, after SSB the model exhibits nine scalar mass eigenstates: (i) three CP-even scalars (

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\text{P}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}M^{2}_{\text{P},22}&M^{2}_{\text{P},23}\\

\displaystyle g_{HVV}\ =

\displaystyle\ \cos{\alpha_{2}}\cos{\beta_{2}}\cos({\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}})\,+\,\sin{\alpha_{2}}\sin{\beta_{2}},

\displaystyle g_{h_{1}VV}\ =

In the Higgs basis

, the

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\rm S}^{2}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widehat{A}&\widehat{C}_{1}&\widehat{C}_{2}\\

\displaystyle g_{h_{2}VV}\

\displaystyle g_{Hd\bar{d}}\

\displaystyle\simeq\ 1-(t_{\beta_{1}}+t_{\beta_{2}})\;\theta_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}-(1-t_{\beta_{1}}t_{\beta_{2}})\;\theta_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle g_{Hu\bar{u}}\

4
Maximally Symmetric
n
HDMs

MS-
n
HDMs are the most economic settings that realise naturally SM
alignment from the general class of
n
HDMs. The

\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
-invariant potential has the same form as the SM
potential,

\displaystyle V_{\rm SM}=-m^{2}\left|\Phi\right|^{2}\,+\,\lambda\left|\Phi\right|^{4},

(49)

that contains a single mass term and a
single quartic coupling. In the case of the 2HDM

\mathrm{Sp}(4){/Z_{2}}
-invariant potential, the so called MS-2HDM is
given by

\displaystyle V_{\rm{MS-2HDM}}\,=\,-m^{2}\left(\left|\Phi_{1}\right|^{2}\,+\,\left|\Phi_{2}\right|^{2}\right)+\lambda\left(\left|\Phi_{1}\right|^{2}\,+\,\left|\Phi_{2}\right|^{2}\right)^{2},

(50)

where the non-zero parameters have the following relations,

\displaystyle m^{2}\,=\,m_{11}^{2}\,=\,m_{22}^{2},\qquad\lambda\,=\,\lambda_{1}\,=\,\lambda_{2}=\,\lambda_{3}/2.

Likewise, in the case of
\mathrm{Sp}(6){/Z_{2}}
-invariant potential, the so called MS-3HDM we have

V_{\rm MS-3HDM}\ =\ -\,m^{2}\,\Big(\left|\Phi_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\Phi_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\Phi_{3}\right|^{2}\Big)\>+\>\lambda\,\Big(\left|\Phi_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\Phi_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\Phi_{3}\right|^{2}\Big)^{2},

(51)

with the following relationship between non-zero parameters:

\displaystyle m^{2}\,=\,m_{11}^{2}\,=\,m_{22}^{2}\,=\,m_{33}^{2}\,,\qquad\,\lambda\,=\,\lambda_{11}\,=\,\lambda_{22}\,=\,\lambda_{33}\,=\,\lambda_{1122}/2\,=\,\lambda_{1133}/2\,=\,\lambda_{2233}/2\,,

In particular, in the MS-3HDM one can realize the mixing angles that diagonalise
the heavy sectors of the CP-even, CP-odd and charged- scalar mass matrices are all equal, i.e.
\bm{\alpha\,=\,\rho\,=\,\sigma}
,
that is a distinct and unique feature of the MS-3HDM.

Moreover, after SSB and in the Born approximation, the MS-
n
HDM predicts one CP-even scalar
H
with non-zero squared mass
M^{2}_{H}\,=2\lambda v^{2}
, while all other scalars are massless pseudo-Goldstone bosons with sizeable gauge and Yukawa interactions. Therefore, we need to consider two main sources that break the
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
symmetry of the theory: (i) the RG effects of the gauge and Yukawa couplings on the potential parameters, and (ii) soft symmetry-breaking bilinear masses, rendering all pseudo-Goldstone fields sufficiently heavy in agreement with current LHC data and other low-energy experiments. Hence, the complete MS-
n
HDM potential is

\displaystyle V\

\displaystyle=\ V_{{\rm MS}-n{\rm HDM}}\>+\>\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,m_{ij}^{2}\,(\Phi_{i}^{\dagger}\Phi_{j})\,.

(52)

Henceforth, with this minimal addition, the
scalar-boson masses in MS-2HDM are given by

\displaystyle M_{H}^{2}=2\lambda_{2}v^{2},\qquad M_{h}^{2}\ =\ M_{a}^{2}\ =\ M_{h^{\pm}}^{2}\ =\ {\text{Re}(m_{12}^{2})\over c_{\beta}s_{\beta}},

(53)

where this degeneracy will be broken by RG effect.

In the case of MS-3HDM, the scalar masses are found to be:

\displaystyle M_{H}^{2}\,\simeq\ 2\lambda_{22}v^{2},\quad M_{h_{1},h_{2}}^{2}\,\simeq\,M_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{2}\ \simeq\ M_{{h_{1}^{\pm},h_{2}^{\pm}}}^{2}\ \simeq\ {1\over 2}\bigg[\,S_{1}+S_{2}\mp\big[{(S_{1}-S_{2})^{2}+4S_{3}^{2}}\big]^{1/2}\,\bigg]\;,

(54)

with

\displaystyle S_{1}=\frac{{m_{12}^{2}}+{t_{\beta_{2}}}\big({{m_{13}^{2}\,s_{\beta_{1}}^{3}}+m_{23}^{2}\,c_{\beta_{1}}^{3}}\big)}{c_{\beta_{1}}s_{\beta_{1}}},\quad S_{2}=\frac{m_{13}^{2}\,c_{\beta_{1}}+m_{23}^{2}\,s_{\beta_{1}}}{c_{\beta_{2}}s_{\beta_{2}}}\,\;,\quad S_{3}=\frac{m_{13}^{2}\,s_{\beta_{1}}-m_{23}^{2}\,c_{\beta_{1}}}{c_{\beta_{2}}}\;.

(55)

Therefore, with the introduction of soft symmetry-breaking bilinears, all pseudo-Goldstone bosons,
h_{1,2}
,
a_{1,2}
and
h_{1,2}^{\pm}
, receive appreciable masses at the tree level.

\mu^{(1,2)}_{X}

\mu^{(1)}_{X}\leq\mu^{(2)}_{X}

5
Conclusions

We have analysed the basic low-energy structure of the general 2HDM and 3HDM. Our study was focused on the canonical SM-like Higgs scenario of the Type-II 2HDM and the Type-V 3HDM, for which conditions on these models parameters for achieving exact SM alignment were derived. Interestingly enough, there are three continuous symmetries which, when imposed on the
n
HDM scalar potential, are sufficient to ensure SM alignment: (i)
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
, (ii)
\mathrm{SU}(n)_{\rm HF}
,
and (iii)
\mathrm{SO(n})_{\rm HF}
. Amongst these symmetries, the most economic setting is the Maximally Symmetric multi-Higgs Doublet Model (MS-
n
HDM), whose potential obeys an
\mathrm{Sp(2}n)
symmetry. This symmetry is softly broken by bilinear masses
m^{2}_{ij}
(with
i\neq j
), as well as explicitly by hypercharge and Yukawa couplings through RG effects, whilst the theory exhibits quartic coupling unification up to the Planck scale. In particular, we have shown that all quartic couplings in the MS-3HDM (MS-2HDM) can unify at high-energy scales
\mu_{X}
and vanish simultaneously at two distinct conformal points, that are denoted by
\mu^{(1,2)}_{X}
with
\mu_{X}^{(1)}\lesssim 10^{13}\,\text{GeV}
(
\mu_{X}^{(1)}\lesssim 10^{11}\,\text{GeV}
) and
\mu_{X}^{(2)}\gtrsim 10^{21}\,\text{GeV}
(
\mu_{X}^{(2)}\gtrsim 10^{18}\,\text{GeV}
).

The MS-
n
HDM is a remarkably predictive scenario, as it only depends
on a few theoretical parameters when compared to the large number of
independent parameters that are required in the general
n
HDM. For
example, in the MS-2HDM the only additional parameters are: (i) the
charged Higgs mass
M_{h^{\pm}}
and (ii)
\tan\beta
. In the case of
MS-3HDM, besides the ratios of the Higgs-doublet VEVs,

\tan\beta_{1,2}
, the model is mainly governed by only three input
parameters: the masses of the two charged Higgs bosons,

M_{h_{1,2}^{\pm}}
, and their mixing angle
\sigma
. Most notably,
with the help of these input parameters, we have obtained misalignment
predictions for the entire scalar mass spectrum of the theory,
including the interactions of all Higgs particles to the SM fields.
The present results for the
MS-
n
HDM
[
23
,
19
]
, demonstrate the
high predictive power of maximally symmetric settings in
n
HDMs. Such
settings not only can naturally provide the experimentally favoured SM
alignment, but also allow us to obtain sharp predictions for the
entire scalar mass spectrum of the theory. In conclusion, the MS-
n
HDM
provides a unique framework that can be tested or even falsified at the
Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and at upcoming high-energy colliders, such as
the projected Future Circular Collider (FCC) at CERN.

An obstruction to smoothing stable maps
Fatemeh Rezaee, Mohan Swaminathan

Abstract
. We describe an obstruction to smoothing stable maps in smooth projective varieties, which generalizes some previously known obstructions. Our obstruction comes from the non-existence of certain rational functions on the ghost components, with prescribed simple poles and residues.



1.
Introduction

The present article is a sequel to
[
6
]
and our main result is an obstruction to smoothing stable maps. We work over a fixed algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
and let
X
denote a smooth projective variety. We start by recalling some basic definitions.

Definition 1.1

(Prestable curve, stable map)
.

A
prestable curve

\mathscr{C}
is a projective, connected, reduced curve with at worst ordinary nodal singularities. A
stable map
is a morphism
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
from a prestable curve
\mathscr{C}
such that there are only finitely many automorphisms of
\mathscr{C}
which preserve the morphism
f
.

Definition 1.2

(Ghost, effective component)
.

For a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, the
ghost sub-curve
is the union of the irreducible components of
\mathscr{C}
on which
f
is constant. Each connected component
C
of the ghost sub-curve is called a
ghost component
of
(\mathscr{C},f)
. The
effective sub-curve
of
(\mathscr{C},f)
is the closure of the complement of the ghost sub-curve.

Definition 1.3

(Eventual smoothability)
.

Denote the moduli stack of stable maps to
X
, of all genera and degrees, by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)
. Let
\mathcal{M}(X)
denote the open substack of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)
which parametrizes maps with non-singular domain curves.

We say that a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
is
smoothable
if
(\mathscr{C},f)
, as a point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)
, lies in the closure of
\mathcal{M}(X)
. We say that
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
is
eventually smoothable
if there exists an embedding
\iota:X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
into another smooth projective variety such that the map
\iota\circ f:\mathscr{C}\to X^{\prime}
is smoothable.

Smoothability implies eventual smoothability, but the converse is false in general. In
[
6
, Theorem B]
, we identified a large class of stable maps which we call ‘stable maps with model ghosts’ and showed that these are eventually smoothable.

Definition 1.4

(Residue obstruction, evaluation map)
.

Let
p
be a non-singular closed point on a prestable curve
C
. We denote the connecting map in the cohomology exact sequence associated to the short exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)\to T_{C,p}\to 0
by

\displaystyle\delta_{C,p}:T_{C,p}\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}).

Here, by a slight abuse of notation, we denote the skyscraper sheaf on
C
supported at
p
with stalk
T_{C,p}
by the same symbol as for the stalk. The map
\delta_{C,p}
corresponds to the map

\displaystyle\text{ev}_{C,p}:H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to T_{C,p}^{\vee}

given by evaluation at
p
under Serre duality. Here,
\omega_{C}
is the dualizing line bundle of
C
.

Definition 1.5

(First derivative)
.

For a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, and a non-singular closed point
p\in\mathscr{C}
, we denote the derivative of
f
at
p
by

\displaystyle df_{p}:T_{\mathscr{C},p}\to T_{X,f(p)}.

We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 1.6

(Obstruction to eventual smoothability)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
be a non-constant stable map.
Let
C
be a ghost component of
(\mathscr{C},f)
mapping to a point
q\in X
and let
\mathscr{E}
be the effective sub-curve. If
(\mathscr{C},f)
is eventually smoothable, then the linear map

(1.1)

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in C\cap\mathscr{E}}\delta_{C,p}\otimes d(f|_{\mathscr{E}})_{p}:\bigoplus_{p\in C\cap\mathscr{E}}T_{C,p}\otimes T_{\mathscr{E},p}\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes T_{X,q}

has a non-trivial kernel.

To obstruct eventual smoothability of a stable map
(\mathscr{C},f)
using this theorem, one needs to analyze the map (
1.1
), which involves interactions between
\delta_{C,p}
and
d(f|_{\mathscr{E}})_{p}
for
p\in C\cap\mathscr{E}
. The following corollary is easier to use as it sidesteps these interactions.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
be a non-constant eventually smoothable stable map with effective sub-curve
\mathscr{E}
. If
C
is any ghost component of
(\mathscr{C},f)
, mapping to
q\in X
, then there is a non-empty subset
D\subset C\cap\mathscr{E}
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\rank\left[\bigoplus_{p\in D}T_{\mathscr{E},p}\to T_{X,q}\right]+\rank\left[H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to\bigoplus_{p\in D}T_{C,p}^{\vee}\right]\leq|D|.

Proof.

Use Theorem
1.6
to find a nonzero element
x=(x_{p})_{p\in C\cap\mathscr{E}}
in the kernel of (
1.1
). Define
D\subset C\cap\mathscr{E}
to consist of those
p
for which
x_{p}
is nonzero and therefore provides an isomorphism
x_{p}:T_{C,p}^{\vee}\to T_{\mathscr{E},p}
. Since (
1.1
) maps
x
to zero, the composition

\displaystyle H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to\bigoplus_{p\in D}T_{C,p}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{p\in D}T_{\mathscr{E},p}\to T_{X,q}

must be zero. The result now follows from the rank-nullity theorem.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

The class of examples discussed in §
2.2
demonstrate that the obstruction to eventual smoothability provided by Corollary
1.7
is strictly weaker than the one coming from Theorem
1.6
. See Remark
2.2
for more details.

Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.7
provide obstructions to eventual smoothability which are insensitive to the global geometry of
X
. Rather, they are sensitive only to the behavior of the stable map near its ghost components. This is consistent with the ‘local criterion for eventual smoothability’
[
6
, Theorem A]
.

Relations to previous work

The question of eventual smoothability of stable maps has been studied in many earlier works in algebraic and symplectic geometry. We give a brief overview here and refer the reader to
[
6
, §1.3]
for a more thorough discussion.

Algebraic geometry: The genus
1
case of Theorem
1.6
was obtained in
[
8
, Lemma 5.9]
. When
X
is a surface, obstructions for general genus are given
[
9
, Lemma 2.4.1]
by comparing stable map limits with Hilbert scheme limits. It should also be possible to deduce obstructions for general genus and
X
of general dimension, using the
stable unramified maps
compactification of
\mathcal{M}(X)
, constructed in
[
4
]
. This is because this compactification has a natural non-surjective forgetful map to the stable map compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)
.

Symplectic geometry: Optimal obstructions to eventual smoothability of stable maps are known in genus
1

[
10
]
and genus
2

[
5
]
. Comparing with these two works, we find that Theorem
1.6
recovers the optimal obstruction for genus
1
but not for genus
2
(cf. Remark
2.1
). For the situation where a ghost component of general genus meets the effective sub-curve in at most two points, obstructions to eventual smoothability were obtained in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. For algebraic target manifolds, Corollary
1.7
extends the results of these two works. We point out that our map (
1.1
) is the algebro-geometric analogue of the ‘leading order term of the obstruction on ghost components’ from
[
1
, Lemma 5.47]
.

Structure of the paper

In section
2
, we give examples of stable maps whose eventual smoothability can be ruled out using Theorem
1.6
. The examples we give also show that Theorem
1.6
is stronger than Corollary
1.7
. In section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.6
.

Conventions

All schemes we consider will be quasi-projective over the fixed algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. The word
variety
is reserved for reduced and irreducible schemes. The notation
\mathbb{A}^{n}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}^{n}
) denotes the affine (resp. projective)
n
-space over
k
. Tensor products and dimensions written without subscripts are understood to be over
k
.

For a scheme
S
and a point
p\in S
, its local ring at
p
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{S,p}
and its maximal ideal is denoted by
\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}
. An
étale neighborhood
of
p
in
S
is a scheme
U
along with an étale morphism
U\to S
under which the inverse image of
p
is a single point.

When
p
is a closed point of the scheme
S
, we denote the Zariski tangent space of
S
at
p
by
T_{S,p}=({\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}}/{\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}^{2}})^{\vee}
. When
S
is smooth of dimension
n
at the closed point
p
, then elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{S,p}
are called
local coordinates
for
S
at
p
if they generate the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}
. The images
dz_{1},\ldots,dz_{n}
of the local coordinates
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
in
{\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}}/{\mathfrak{m}_{S,p}^{2}}=T_{S,p}^{\vee}
form a basis, and we denote the corresponding dual basis of
T_{S,p}
by
\partial_{z_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{z_{n}}
.

When
Z
is an local complete intersection subscheme of a smooth variety
Y
defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}
, the
normal bundle
of
Z\subset Y
is the locally free sheaf on
Z
given by
N_{Z/Y}=({\mathscr{I}}/{\mathscr{I}^{2}})^{\vee}
. We often use the fact that when
D
is a reduced effective divisor in the smooth variety
Y
, we have a natural identification
N_{D/Y}^{\otimes m}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mD)|_{D}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, when
p\in C
is a non-singular closed point on a curve, we have
N_{p/C}=T_{C,p}
and therefore, we have
\mathcal{O}_{C}(mp)|_{p}=T_{C,p}^{\otimes m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.
Non-smoothable stable maps to projective space

In this section, we give a concrete class of examples to illustrate Theorem
1.6
. Before discussing the examples, we use an elementary dimension counting argument to show that, in projective space, stable maps with ghost components are usually not smoothable.

2.1.
Dimension counting

Fix integers
N,g,d\geq 1
with
d\geq 2g-1
, and consider the moduli stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d)
of stable genus
g
maps of degree
d
to
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

For any smooth projective curve
\mathscr{C}
of genus
g
, any degree
d
morphism
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
has unobstructed deformations, since we have
H^{1}(\mathscr{C},f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}})=0
. This comes from the Euler exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)^{N+1}\to T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\to 0
, and

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathscr{C},f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1))=H^{0}(\mathscr{C},\omega_{\mathscr{C}}\otimes f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-1))^{\vee}=0,

using Serre duality. Thus, the open substack
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d)
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
parametrizing stable maps with non-singular domain curves is smooth and has dimension

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}=\chi(\mathscr{C},f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}})+\dim\mathcal{M}_{g}=(N-3)(1-g)+d(N+1).

Let us now describe a stratum within
\overline{\mathcal{M}}\setminus\mathcal{M}
. Let
n,h\geq 1
be integers with
h\leq g
. Let
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\geq 1
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\geq 0
be integers such that
d_{i}\geq 2g_{i}
, and we have
d=\sum d_{i}
and
g=h+\sum g_{i}
. For instance, we could take
n=1
and
h=g
. Consider the stack

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{M}_{h,n}\times(\mathcal{M}_{g_{1},1}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d_{1})\times_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\mathcal{M}_{g_{n},1}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d_{n}))

parametrizing the data of an
n
-pointed smooth projective genus
h
curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
and, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, a degree
d_{i}
morphism
f_{i}:\mathscr{E}_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
from a pointed smooth projective genus
g_{i}
curve
(\mathscr{E}_{i},p_{i}^{\prime})
such that
f_{1}(p_{1}^{\prime})=\cdots=f_{n}(p_{n}^{\prime})
. We have a natural morphism

\displaystyle\iota:\mathcal{N}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}},

given by mapping
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}),(\mathscr{E}_{1},p_{1}^{\prime},f_{1}),\ldots,(\mathscr{E}_{n},p_{n}^{\prime},f_{n}^{\prime})
to the stable map
(\mathscr{C},f)
, where
\mathscr{C}
is the prestable curve obtained from
C\sqcup\bigsqcup\mathscr{E}_{i}
by making the identifications
p_{i}\sim p_{i}^{\prime}
, and
f
is the morphism which restricts to
f_{i}
on
\mathscr{E}_{i}
and is constant on
C
. The morphism
\iota
maps
\mathcal{N}
onto its image in a finite-to-one fashion. For
1\leq i\leq n
, the morphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{g_{i},1}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d_{i})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{P}^{N}

\displaystyle(f_{i}:\mathscr{E}_{i}\to X,p_{i}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mapsto f_{i}(p_{i}^{\prime})

given by evaluation at the marked point is smooth since Serre duality gives

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathscr{E}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{E}_{i}}(-p_{i}^{\prime})\otimes f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1))=H^{0}(\mathscr{E}_{i},\omega_{\mathscr{E}_{i}}(p_{i}^{\prime})\otimes f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-1))^{\vee}=0.

Thus,
\mathcal{N}
is smooth and has dimension

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}_{h,n}-N(n-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\chi(\mathscr{E}_{i},f_{i}^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}})+\dim\mathcal{M}_{g_{i},1}\big)

\displaystyle=3h-3+n-N(n-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big((N-3)(1-g_{i})+d_{i}(N+1)+1\big)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{M}+Nh-n.

When
Nh-n\geq 0
, we get
\dim\mathcal{N}\geq\dim\mathcal{M}
, which implies that most points of
\iota(\mathcal{N})
are not contained in the closure of
\mathcal{M}
. More precisely, the pullback of the closure of
\mathcal{M}
under the morphism
\iota
is of codimension
\geq Nh-n+1
in
\mathcal{N}
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is instructive to compare the conclusion obtained above via dimension counting with what our main result gives in this situation.

Theorem
1.6
asserts that (
1.1
), which is a linear map from an
n
-dimensional vector space to an
Nh
-dimensional vector space, must have a non-trivial kernel at all points of
\iota(\mathcal{N})
that lie in the closure of
\mathcal{M}
. When
Nh\geq n
, having a non-trivial null-space is a codimension
Nh-n+1
condition on the space of
Nh\times n
matrices. Thus, the above dimension count is consistent with what our main result predicts.

However, the same dimension count can also be used to show the existence of points in
\iota(\mathcal{N})
whose non-smoothability cannot be detected by our main result. Specifically, consider the case where
n=1
and simplify the notation by writing
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{1}
,
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{1}
and
p=p_{1}
. In this situation, the map
\delta_{C,p}
is always injective and Theorem
1.6
simply says that points of
\iota(\mathcal{N})
lying in the closure of
\mathcal{M}
must satisfy
d(f|_{\mathscr{E}})_{p^{\prime}}=0
. After pulling back via
\iota
, the vanishing of
d(f|_{\mathscr{E}})_{p^{\prime}}
defines a locus in
\mathcal{N}
of codimension
\leq N
. On the other hand, we know that the points of
\mathcal{N}
whose image under
\iota
lies in the closure of
\mathcal{M}
form a locus of codimension
\geq Nh-n+1=Nh
in
\mathcal{N}
. In particular, when
h>1
, we deduce the existence of non-smoothable stable maps in
\iota(\mathcal{N})
which nevertheless satisfy
d(f|_{\mathscr{E}})_{p^{\prime}}=0
.

2.2.
Concrete class of examples

Keeping the above setup, we now take

\displaystyle N,h\geq 2,\quad n=Nh,\quad g_{1}=\cdots=g_{n}=0\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}=\cdots=d_{n}=1.

Let
C
be a smooth projective genus
h

p_{i,j}\in C
for
1\leq i\leq N
and

\{p_{i,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq h}
have linearly independent images under the natural morphism

\displaystyle\iota_{C}:C\to\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})^{\vee},

defined by the canonical bundle

of

. Note that since

is smooth, its canonical bundle is the same as its dualizing line bundle. From Serre duality, it follows that the map

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq h}\delta_{C,p_{i,j}}:\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq h}T_{C,p_{i,j}}\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})

\displaystyle T_{\mathbb{P}^{N},q}=\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq N}T_{L_{i},q}.

For each

3.1.
Reduction from eventual smoothability to smoothability

Since
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
is eventually smoothable, there is an embedding
\iota:X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
into another smooth projective variety such that
\iota\circ f:\mathscr{C}\to X^{\prime}
is smoothable. The answer to whether or not the map (
1.1
) is injective remains unchanged when we replace
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
by
\iota\circ f:\mathscr{C}\to X^{\prime}
. Therefore, replacing
X
by
X^{\prime}
, we assume for the remainder that
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
is smoothable.

3.2.
Smoothing family and its resolution

f:\mathscr{C}\to X

Proposition 3.5

Corollary 3.6

The composite linear map

\displaystyle T_{\Delta,s}^{\otimes m}\xrightarrow{(\Phi_{p_{1}},\ldots,\Phi_{p_{r}})}\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq r}T_{C,p_{i}}\otimes T_{\mathscr{E},p_{i}}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n}T_{C,p_{i}}\otimes T_{\mathscr{E},p_{i}}\xrightarrow{\eqref{eqn:first-order-obstruction-map}}H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes T_{X,q}

Write

\displaystyle H^{0}(C,\mathfrak{F})\to\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq r}T_{C,p_{i}}\otimes W\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\otimes W.

A remark on the Hölder regularity of solutions to the complex Hessian equation
Sławomir Kołodziej and Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We prove that the Dirichlet problem for the complex Hessian equation has the Hölder continuous solution provided it has a subsolution with this property. Compared to the previous result of Benali-Zeriahi and Charabati-Zeriahi we remove the assumption on the finite total mass of the measure on the right hand side.



1.
Introduction

The theory of weak solutions of the complex Monge-Ampère equations started with the fundamental works of Bedford and Taylor
[
2
,
3
]
.
It has found numerous profound applications in complex geometry (see e.g. the survey
[
31
]
).
The Hölder continuous solutions are interesting because such functions often appear in complex dynamic in higher dimension as potentials of Green currents associated to dominating holomorphic maps
[
16
]
and
[
32
]
, as well as in approximation theory
[
29
]
.

Thanks to a series of works
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
8
,
9
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
27
,
28
]
,
[
30
]
and
[
34
]
the Hölder regularity of the solution to the complex Monge-Ampère equation is rather well-understood.
Notably, it was shown in
[
1
]
that if the measure on the right hand side has a density in
L^{p}
,
p>1
and the boundary data is Hölder continuous, then the solution is Hölder continuous. Such a regularity was also proved for the volume form of a generic smooth compact submanifold in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
[
30
]
and
[
34
]
. These measures are singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a Hölder continuous solution via Hölder continuous subsolution was proved in
[
27
,
28
]
, which was inspired by
[
19
]
.

Recently, potential theory has been developed very successfully for the study of weak solutions to complex Hessian equations
(of which the Monge-Ampère equation is a special case) both in domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and on compact Kähler manifolds by Błocki
[
6
]
, Dinew and the first author
[
13
,
14
]
, and Lu
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
The study of Hölder regularity of
m
-subharmonic solutions to the complex Hessian equation
is recently very active
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
and
[
33
]
. Although the statements are often similar to the ones for the complex Monge-Ampère equation, the proofs are substantially different and usually the Hölder exponent of the solutions is
probably far from optimal.

We focus now on the Hölder regularity of
m
-subharmonic solutions to the complex Hessian equation assuming the existence of a Hölder continuous subsolution.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
1\leq m<n
be an integer. Let us denote by
\beta=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
the standard Kähler form and
SH_{m}(\Omega)
the set of all
m
-subharmonic functions in
\Omega
defined in
[
6
]
. For
v\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
its complex Hessian measure is given by

H_{m}(v):=(dd^{c}v)^{m}\wedge\beta^{n-m}.

Let
\varphi\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
with
0<\alpha\leq 1
and let
\mu
be a positive measure in
\Omega
satisfying

(1.1)

\mu\leq H_{m}(\varphi).

To study the Dirichlet problem we assume that
\Omega
is a bounded strictly
m
- pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\rho\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
be a strictly
m
-subharmonic defining function of
\Omega
.
By adding
\varphi
to an
m
-subharmonic envelope whose boundary value is equal to
-\varphi
as in
[
26
, Theorem 4.7]
, we obtain a new Hölder continuous subsolution
(with possibly smaller Hölder exponent)
with zero boundary value. Therefore, without loss of generality we may assume that

(1.2)

\varphi=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Given the boundary data
\phi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
we wish to solve the Hessian equation

(1.3)

u\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}),\quad H_{m}(u)=\mu,\quad\text{and }u=\phi\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

The main result of the paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be the measure satisfying (
1.1
). Then, the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is solvable and there exists
0<\alpha^{\prime}\leq 1
such that

(1.4)

u\in C^{0,\alpha^{\prime}}(\bar{\Omega}).

This result was proved in
[
21
]
for a compactly supported measure
\mu
in
\Omega
. Later, Benali-Zeriahi
[
4
, Theorem B]
(in a special case) and
Charabati and Zeriahi
[
10
, Theorem 4]
solved the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
)
\&
(
1.4
) for any
\mu
having finite total mass, i.e.,
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
.
More precisely, it was a consequence of a general statement
[
10
, Theorem 3]
for the modulus of continuity under such an assumption. In other words, they obtained the Hölder continuous subsolution theorem for finite mass measures. Here, we are able to remove the finite mass assumption. The method to prove the theorem is inspired by
[
4
]
and
[
21
]
. As in
[
21
]
we do not invoke neither the viscosity solutions nor the rooftop envelopes. However, the Hölder exponent of the solution is worse than the one in
[
4
]
and
[
10
]
(see also
[
5
]
).

In general the complex Hessian measure of a Hölder continuous
m
-subharmonic function may have unbounded total mass. Thus, for applications it is desirable to prove the Hölder continuous subsolution theorem for possibly unbounded total mass measures. Let us consider specific examples.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a strictly
m
-pseuconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\rho
is a
C^{2}
strictly
m
-subharmonic defining function of
\Omega
as above.

(a)

Consider the function
-(-\rho)^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then, it is Hölder continuous on
\bar{\Omega}
and
m
-subharmonic in
\Omega
. It is easy to see that for any
x\in\partial\Omega
and
U_{x}
a neighborhood of
x
,

\int_{\Omega\cap U_{x}}H_{m}(-|\rho|^{\alpha})=+\infty.

It follows that the measure
\mu={\bf 1}_{U_{x}}\cdot H_{m}(-|\rho|^{\alpha})
has unbounded mass on
\Omega
and
\mu\leq H_{m}(-|\rho|^{\alpha})
.

(b)

Take
G\subset\Omega
be such that
\mu:={\bf 1}_{G}\cdot H_{m}(-|\rho|^{\alpha})
satisfies
\mu(\Omega)<+\infty
. Then, it is not clear how to find
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{0}_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
such that
\mu\leq H_{m}(\varphi)
, where
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{m}(\Omega)
is the Cegrell class of bounded
m
-subharmonic functions whose boundary values are zero and whose total masses of complex Hessian measures are finite.

Example 1.3
.

Take
\varphi\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
such that
\varphi\neq 0
on
\partial\Omega
. Assume
\mu\leq H_{m}(\varphi)
in
\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}H_{m}(\varphi)<+\infty
. Then, it is not clear if one can find
\varphi^{\prime}
Hölder continuous
m
-subharmonic function such that
\varphi^{\prime}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}H_{m}(\varphi^{\prime})<+\infty
.

The Hölder regularity of solutions to complex Hessian equations or fully non-linear elliptic equations on compact Kähler manifold is also very active field of research see e.g.,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
35
]
. It is worth to point out that the Hölder regularity of quasi
m
-subharmonic solutions to the equation on a compact Kähler manifold,
under similar hypothesis as the ones mentioned above, is widely open. There is a partial progress made by Cheng and Yu
[
11
]
in case nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature of the metric.

2.
Hessian measures dominated by capacity

In this section we consider the positive Radon measure
\mu
which is dominated by the complex Hessian measure associated to a Hölder continuous
m
-subharmonic function
\varphi
satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Our goal is to prove that there exist uniform constants
C,\alpha_{0}>0
depending only on
\varphi
and
\Omega
such that for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
,

\mu(K)\leq C\left[cap_{m}(K,\Omega)\right]^{1+\alpha_{0}},

where the
m
-capacity defined as in
[
13
]
, namely

cap_{m}(K,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}H_{m}(w):w\in SH_{m}(\Omega),\;-1\leq w\leq 0\right\}.

This inequality was proved under the assumption of the compact support of the measure in
[
21
]
. Later it was proved without this extra hypothesis by Benali and Zeriahi
[
4
]
and a more general formulation was given by Charabati and Zeriahi
[
10
]
, where they employed the viscosity solution. Here we give an alternative proof with a smaller exponent but it does not invoke neither viscosity solutions nor the rooftop envelope. This is done by slightly extending the Hölder continuous subsolution to a neighborhood of
\bar{\Omega}
using the defining function
\rho
. The cost is the growth of the Hölder norm of the new subsolution and we need to carefully analyze it.

Since
\rho
is a
C^{2}
defining function of
\Omega
,
we can assume that
\rho
is a strictly
m
-subharmonic function in a neighborhood
\widetilde{\Omega}
of
\bar{\Omega}
.
Let us define for
\varepsilon>0

the sets

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{z\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\},

and

D_{\varepsilon}=\{z\in\Omega:\rho(z)<-\varepsilon\}.

These two domains are comparable as, by the Hopf lemma, there exist constants
c_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

(2.1)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{c_{0}\varepsilon}\quad\text{for every }0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Let us consider the following extension of the subsolution
\varphi

(2.2)

\psi:=\max\left\{\varphi-\varepsilon,\frac{M\rho}{c_{0}\varepsilon}\right\},

where
M=1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Clearly,
\psi
is defined on
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Furthermore,

\psi=\varphi-\varepsilon
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
\psi=M\rho/(c_{0}\varepsilon)
near
\partial\Omega
. Thus,
\psi
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and
m
-subharmonic in the neighborhood
\widetilde{\Omega}
of
\bar{\Omega}
and

(2.3)

H_{m}(\varphi)=H_{m}(\psi)\quad\text{on }\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Let us fix an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending only on
\Omega
such that for every
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

(2.4)

\bar{\Omega}\subset\{z\in\widetilde{\Omega}:{\rm dist}(z,\partial\widetilde{\Omega})>\varepsilon\}.

As
\varphi-\varepsilon\leq\psi\leq 0
,

(2.5)

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq M.

Now we are ready to give a slightly different proof of the following result due to Benali and Zeriahi
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exist constants
A_{1}>0
and
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for any compact set
K\subset\Omega

\int_{K}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq A_{1}\left[cap_{m}(K)\right]^{1+\alpha_{0}}.

Precisely,
A_{1}=C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{m}\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}^{m}
with
C
a uniform constant depending only on
\Omega
and
\alpha_{0}
which is explicitly computable in terms of
\alpha,m
and
n
.

Following the idea in
[
4
]
we divide a compact
K
into two parts
K\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
K\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. To deal with the first part we need

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
1<p<n/m
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is given in (
2.1
). Let
E\subset\Omega_{2\varepsilon}
be a Borel set. Then,

\int_{E}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq A_{2}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-2m}[cap_{m}(E)]^{p-1}\right)cap_{m}(E),

where
A_{2}=C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{m}\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
E
is a regular compact set. This means that the relative extremal function

u_{E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in SH_{m}(\Omega):v\leq 0,\;v\leq-1\text{ on }E\}

is a continuous
m
-subharmonic function on
\bar{\Omega}
. For the general
E
one can use the fact that

\mu
is a Radon measure and
m
-capacity enjoys the inner regularity property. Recall that
\psi
is the extension defined in (
2.2
). Let us denote by
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\psi*\chi_{\varepsilon}
, where
\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is the radial smoothing kernels, the standard regularization of
\psi
in
\widetilde{\Omega}
for
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. We have by (
2.4
) that the domain of
\psi_{\varepsilon}
contains
\bar{\Omega}
. Hence,
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
. Using the Hölder continuity of
\psi=\varphi-\varepsilon
we have

0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}-\psi\leq c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha}:=\delta\quad\text{on }\bar{\Omega}_{2\varepsilon},

where
c_{1}=\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}
.
The compactness of
E
gives
u_{E}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Also
\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\psi
on
\bar{\Omega}
by the subharmonicity of
\psi
. Consider the set
E^{\prime}:=\{3\delta u_{E}+\psi_{\varepsilon}<\psi-2\delta\}
. Then,

E\subset E^{\prime}\subset\{u_{E}<-1/2\}\subset\subset\Omega,

where we used the fact
E\subset\Omega_{2\varepsilon}
for the first inclusion and
\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\psi
for the second one. In what follows we write for any Borel set
G\subset\Omega
,

(2.6)

cap_{m}(G):=cap_{m}(G,\Omega)

Clearly,
cap_{m}(E)\leq cap_{m}(E^{\prime})
. On the other hand, by the comparison principle
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle cap_{m}(E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq cap_{m}\left(\{u_{E}<-1/2\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m}\int_{\{u_{E}<0\}}(dd^{c}u_{E})^{m}\wedge\beta^{n-m}

\displaystyle=2^{m}cap_{m}(E).

Next, notice from (
2.5
) that on
\bar{\Omega}
,

dd^{c}\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\beta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\leq\frac{CM\beta}{\varepsilon^{2}},\quad\|\psi_{\varepsilon}+u_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M.

Since
E\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

\int_{E}H_{m}(\varphi)=\int_{E}H_{m}(\psi)
.
The comparison principle and the above inequalities give us that

\displaystyle\int_{E^{\prime}}H_{m}(\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E^{\prime}}H_{m}(3\delta u_{E}+\psi_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E^{\prime}}3\delta H_{m}(u_{E}+\psi_{\varepsilon})+\int_{E^{\prime}}H_{m}(\psi_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq 3\delta M^{m}cap_{m}(E^{\prime})+CM^{m}\varepsilon^{-2m}\left[cap_{m}(E^{\prime})\right]^{p},

where we also used
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
for the last inequality with
1<p<n/m
.
Combining with the equivalence (
2.7
) of
cap_{m}(E)
and
cap_{m}(E^{\prime})
we get that for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

(2.8)

\int_{E}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq CM^{m}\left(c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-2m}[cap_{m}(E)]^{p-1}\right)cap_{m}(E).

Since we may assume
c_{1}=\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}\geq 1
, the desired inequality follows.
∎

The next lemma is due to Benali and Zeriahi
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
which takes care of the second part in the decomposition.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F\subset\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2\varepsilon}
be a compact set. Then,

(2.9)

\int_{F}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq A_{3}\;\varepsilon^{m\alpha}cap_{m}(F),

where
A_{3}=2^{m\alpha}\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}^{m}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
F
is regular in the sense that the relative extremal function
u_{F}
with respect to
\Omega
is continuous as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
. Since
\varphi
is Hölder continuous on
\bar{\Omega}
and
\varphi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have for
z\in F

-\varphi(z)\leq c_{1}{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)^{\alpha}<2^{\alpha}c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha}=:\delta,

where
c_{1}=\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}
.
Consider the set

F^{\prime}=\{\delta u_{F}<\varphi\}
. Since
u_{F}=-1
on
F
, we have
F\subset F^{\prime}
. Note that
u_{F}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, hence
F^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. By the comparison principle

\displaystyle\int_{F^{\prime}}H_{m}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{F^{\prime}}H_{m}(\delta u_{F})

\displaystyle=\delta^{m}\int_{F^{\prime}}H_{m}(u_{F})

\displaystyle=\delta^{m}cap_{m}(F).

The desired inequality follows.
∎

End of proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Now for any compact set
K\subset\Omega
we write
K=(K\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cup(K\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}).
Let us denote by
E
and
F
the two sets of this union. Then, both of them have
m
-capacity less than
cap_{m}(K)
.
It follows from (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int_{K}H_{m}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}H_{m}(\varphi)+\int_{F}H_{m}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq A_{2}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-2m}\;[cap_{m}(E)]^{p-1}\right)cap_{m}(E)+A_{3}\;\varepsilon^{m\alpha}cap_{m}(F)

\displaystyle\leq A_{4}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-2m}[cap_{m}(K)]^{p-1}+\varepsilon^{m\alpha}\right)cap_{m}(K),

where
A_{4}=\max\{A_{2},A_{3}\}
.
Notice that this inequality holds for every
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is fixed and defined in (
2.4
).

The proof of the theorem will follow by choosing a suitable
\varepsilon>0
. To this end we define
\alpha_{1}>0
by

p-1-2m\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1}\alpha\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{1}=\frac{p-1}{2m+\alpha}.

If
[cap_{m}(K)]^{\alpha_{1}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
, then by (
2.10
)

\displaystyle\int_{K}H_{m}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq A_{4}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2m}_{0}cap_{m}(K)^{p-1}+\varepsilon^{m\alpha}_{0}\right)cap_{m}(K)

\displaystyle\leq A_{4}\left(\left[cap_{m}(K)\right]^{1+\alpha_{1}\alpha}+\varepsilon_{0}^{-2m}[cap_{m}(K)]^{p}+[cap_{m}(K)]^{1+m\alpha_{1}\alpha}\right).

So, we get the desired inequality in this case. Otherwise, if
[cap_{m}(K)]^{\alpha_{1}}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then we can choose
\varepsilon=[cap_{m}(K)]^{\alpha_{1}}
. It follows from (
2.10
) that
\int_{K}H_{m}(\varphi)
is less than

A_{4}\left(\left[cap_{m}(K)\right]^{1+\alpha_{1}\alpha}+\left[cap_{m}(K)\right]^{p-2m\alpha_{1}}+[cap_{m}(K)]^{1+m\alpha_{1}\alpha}\right).

Therefore, the inequality

\int_{K}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq A_{1}\left[cap_{m}(K)\right]^{1+\alpha_{0}}

holds for
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}\alpha>0
and
A_{1}=3A_{4}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

3.
The Hölder regularity of the solution

Let us proceed to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Thanks to the proof in
[
21
, Theorem 1.2]
and Theorem
2.1
we can find
u\in SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying

(3.1)

(dd^{c}u)^{m}\wedge\beta^{n-m}=\mu,\quad u=\phi\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

The continuous solution has been obtained by Charabati and Zeriahi
[
10
]
for a more general family of measures: those which are dominated by a complex Hessian measure associated to a continuous
m
-subharmonic function whose modulus of continuity satisfies the Dini-type condition.

Under the Hölder continuity of the boundary data
\phi\in C^{0,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
we wish to show that

u\in C^{0,\alpha^{\prime}}(\bar{\Omega})\quad\text{for some }\alpha^{\prime}>0.

Here, we may assume that the Hölder exponent of
\phi
is the same as the one of
\varphi
, otherwise we can decrease
\alpha
.
We will see in the proof that the exponent
\alpha^{\prime}
is explicitly computable in terms of
\alpha,m
and
n
.
We follow closely the argument in
[
28
]
which deals with the complex Monge-Ampère equation. Consider the sequence of sets
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
small. Recall that
M=1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and denote

\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}=\max\{\varphi-\varepsilon,M\rho/\varepsilon\}.

This is similar to
\psi
but we need to keep track of the dependence on
\varepsilon
more carefully this time. Notice that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
is
\alpha-
Hölder continuous and
m
-subharmonic in a neighborhood of
\bar{\Omega}
, its value on the boundary
\partial\Omega
is zero. Moreover, for
\varepsilon>0
small

(3.2)

\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{A}{\varepsilon},\quad\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq\frac{A}{\varepsilon},

where
A=M(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}+\|\rho\|_{C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})})
.
Next, we can follow the lines of the proofs of
[
28
, Lemma 4.4, Remark 4.5, Corollary 4.6]
to get the following two lemmata with the constant
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
and
\rho
.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have for
k=1,...,m

\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-m+k}\leq\frac{CM^{k}}{\varepsilon^{k}}.

Moreover,

{\bf 1}_{D_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\mu\leq H_{m}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ on }D_{\varepsilon}.

Since the norms of
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy (
3.2
) we can apply Theorem
2.1
for this Hölder continuous subsolution to get

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha_{0}
be defined in Theorem
2.1
. Then,
for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
,

\int_{K}H_{m}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{CA^{2m}}{\varepsilon^{2m}}[cap_{m}(K)]^{1+\alpha_{0}}.

We are in the position to state an analogue of
[
28
, Proposition 4.7]
for the complex Hessian equation.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u
be the solution in (
3.1
). Let
v\in SH_{m}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
v=u
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Then, there is
0<\alpha_{2}<1
such that

\sup_{\Omega}(v-u)\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2m}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\max\{v-u,0\}d\mu\right)^{\alpha_{2}},

where
C>0
is a uniform constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\sup_{\Omega}(v-u)>0
. Set

(3.3)

s_{0}:=\inf_{\Omega}(u-v).

We know that
for
0<s<|s_{0}|
,

(3.4)

U(s):=\{u<v+s_{0}+s\}\subset\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

The only difference from
[
21
, Proposition 2.5]
is that now the constant
C/\varepsilon^{m}
appeared on the right hand side.
As shown in
[
28
]
for the Monge-Ampère equation the proof of the proposition will follow once we have the following inequality.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha_{0}>0
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Then, for every
0<s,t<\frac{|s_{0}|}{2}
,

(3.5)

t^{m}cap_{m}(U(s))\leq\frac{C(\alpha_{0})}{\varepsilon^{2m}}\left[cap_{m}(U(s+t))\right]^{1+\alpha_{0}}.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
0\leq w\leq 1
be a
m
-subharmonic function in
\Omega
. By the comparison principle
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
,

\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s+tw\}}H_{m}(v+tw)\leq\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s+tw\}}H_{m}(u),

Since
U(s)\subset\{u<v+s_{0}+s+tw\}\subset U(s+t)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{U(s)}H_{m}(v+tw)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s+tw\}}H_{m}(v+tw)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s+tw\}}H_{m}(u),

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U(s+t)}d\mu.

As
t^{m}\int_{U(s)}H_{m}(w)\leq\int_{U(s)}H_{m}(v+tw)
, taking supremum over all such
w
we get

(3.6)

t^{m}cap_{m}(U(s))\leq\int_{U(s+t)}d\mu.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1

{\bf 1}_{D_{c_{0}\varepsilon}}\cdot d\mu\leq H_{m}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{c_{0}\varepsilon})\quad\text{in }\Omega

as two measures, where
c_{0}
is the constant defined in (
2.1
).
Combining this with
U(s+t)\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{c_{0}\varepsilon}
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{U(s+t)}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U(s+t)}H_{m}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{c_{0}\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\alpha_{0})}{(c_{0}\varepsilon)^{2m}}\left[cap_{m}(U(s+t))\right]^{1+\alpha_{0}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.2
. Thus, the proof of the lemma follows from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).
∎

Then, the rest of the proof of the proposition is the same as in
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
with obvious change of notations.
∎

An important step is to derive the
L^{1}-L^{1}
(with respect to the measure
\mu
and the Lebesgue measure) stability estimate of solutions. The one stated below is an analogue of
[
28
, Theorem 4.9]
for the complex Hessian equation. For the sake of completeness we give the detailed proof.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u
be the solution in (
3.1
). Let
v\in SH_{m}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
v=u
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Then, there is
0<\alpha_{3}<1
such that

\int_{\Omega}|v-u|d\mu\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{m+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v-u|dV_{2n}\right)^{\alpha_{3}},

where
C>0
is a uniform constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Put

(3.8)

S_{k,\varepsilon}:=(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-k},\quad k=0,...,m.

where
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}=\max\{\varphi-\varepsilon,M\rho/\varepsilon\}
. Since
\mu\leq S_{m,\varepsilon}
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, it is enough to show that there is
0<\tau\leq 1
satisfying

(3.9)

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)S_{m,\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{m+1}}\|v-u\|_{1}^{\tau}.

for
v\geq u
on
\Omega
, where here and below we denote by

\|\cdot\|_{1}
the
L^{1}
-norm with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
(In the general case we use the identity

|v-u|=(\max\{v,u\}-u)+(\max\{v,u\}-v)

and apply twice the inequality (
3.9
) to get the theorem.)

Now we can use the inductive arguments in
0\leq k\leq m
. Clearly it holds for
k=0
with
\alpha_{2}=1
. Assume the inequality holds for
k<m-1
. Let us denote
\{\chi_{t}\}_{t>0}
the family of standard radial smoothing kernels. To pass from the
k
-th step to the step number
(k+1)\leq m

we need the following inequality (with the notation
S_{\varepsilon}:=(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-k-1}
)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(v-u)dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\wedge S_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}(v-u)dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{t}\wedge S_{\varepsilon}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\Omega}(v-u)dd^{c}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{t}-\varphi_{\varepsilon})\wedge S_{\varepsilon}\right|

\displaystyle=:I_{1,\varepsilon}+I_{2,\varepsilon}.

Notice that
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
is well-defined on the neighborhood
\widetilde{\Omega}
of
\bar{\Omega}
, so is
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{t}
for
t>0
small. Moreover,

\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq C/\varepsilon.

Since for
t>0
,

(3.11)

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{t}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}(z)\right|\leq\frac{C\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{t^{2}}\leq\frac{C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})}{t^{2}},

and by the induction hypothesis at
k
-th step, there exists
0<\tau_{k}\leq 1
such that

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)S_{\varepsilon}\wedge\beta\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}\|v-u\|_{1}^{\tau_{k}},

we conclude that

(3.12)

\displaystyle I_{1,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})}{t^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(v-u)S_{\varepsilon}\wedge\beta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})}{\varepsilon^{k+1}\,t^{2}}\|v-u\|_{1}^{\tau_{k}}.

By integration by parts, using
u=v
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, and

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}*\chi_{t}(z)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon},

it follows that

(3.13)

\displaystyle I_{2,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(dd^{c}v+dd^{c}u)\wedge S_{\varepsilon}.

At this point the total mass of the right hand side may go to infinity as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. However, we can control it uniformly in terms of a negative exponent of
\varepsilon
. The argument is inspired by
[
28
, Lemma 4.3]
. Namely, we obtain

(3.14)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(dd^{c}u+dd^{c}v)\wedge S_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C\|u+v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{k}}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}.

Indeed, we first have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}dd^{c}(u+v)\wedge(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\max\{\rho,-\varepsilon^{\prime}/2\}-\rho\right)\wedge dd^{c}(u+v)\wedge(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|u+v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\rho)\wedge(dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\beta^{n-k-1},

where
\varepsilon^{\prime}=c_{0}\varepsilon
with
c_{0}
defined by (
2.1
).
The desired inequality (
3.14
) follows from Lemma
3.1
.
Now, combining (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we get that

(3.15)

I_{2,\varepsilon}\leq\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon^{k+2}}.

Next, it is easy to see (from Lemma
3.1
) that

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)S_{m,\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(1+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{m}}{\varepsilon^{m}}.

Therefore, we can assume that
0<\|v-u\|_{1}<0.01
.
Thanks to (
3.12
) and (
3.15
) we have

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\wedge S_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{k+1}\,t^{2}}\|v-u\|_{1}^{\tau_{k}}+\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{\varepsilon^{k+2}}.

If we choose

t=\|v-u\|_{1}^{\frac{\tau_{k}}{3}},\quad\tau_{k+1}=\frac{\alpha\tau_{k}}{3},

then

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)S_{\varepsilon}\wedge dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{k+2}}\|v-u\|_{1}^{\tau_{k+1}}.

Thus, the induction argument is completed, and the theorem follows.
∎

The next result is from
[
28
, Lemma 4.10]
and its proof used only the subharmonicity. Therefore, it is also valid for
m
-subharmonic functions.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u
be the solution in (
3.1
) and define
for
z\in\Omega_{\delta}
,

\hat{u}_{\delta}(z):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{2n}\delta^{2n}}\int_{|\zeta|\leq\delta}u(z+\zeta)dV_{2n}(\zeta),

where
\sigma_{2n}
is the volume of the unit ball. Then, for
\delta>0
small,

(3.16)

\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}|\hat{u}_{\delta}-u|dV_{2n}\leq C\delta.

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{\delta}\subset D_{c_{0}\delta}
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}dd^{c}u(z)\wedge\beta^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{c_{0}\delta}(\max\{\rho,-c_{\delta}/2\}-\rho)dd^{c}u\wedge\beta^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}dd^{c}\rho\wedge\beta^{n-1}.

Therefore, using the classical Jensen formula for subharmonic functions (see e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 4.3]
),

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}|\hat{u}_{\delta}-u|dV_{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{2\delta}}|\hat{u}_{\delta}-u|dV_{2n}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}\setminus\Omega_{2\delta}}dV_{2n}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\Delta u+c_{6}\delta

\displaystyle\leq C\delta.

This is the required inequality.
∎

We are ready to prove the Hölder continuity of the solution
u
in (
3.1
).

End of Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us fix
\delta
such that
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
small and let
\varepsilon
be such that
\delta\leq\varepsilon<\delta_{0}
which is to be determined later. Recall that

(3.19)

\Omega_{\delta}:=\{z\in\Omega:dist(z,\partial\Omega)>\delta\};

and for
z\in\Omega_{\delta}
we define

(3.20)

\displaystyle u_{\delta}(z):=\sup_{|\zeta|\leq\delta}u(z+\zeta).

Directly from definitions and the Hölder continuity of the boundary data as in
[
28
, Lemma 4.1]
we have the following inequalities.

Lemma 3.7
.

We have for
z\in\bar{\Omega}_{\delta}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

(3.21)

u_{\delta}(z)\leq u(z)+c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha},

where
c_{5}=\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}+\|\phi\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})}
.
In particular,

(3.22)

\sup_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)\leq\sup_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)+c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

Remark 3.8
.

It is important to keep in mind that the uniform constants
C,c_{i}>0
which appear in the lemma, and several times below are independent of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.

Thanks to Lemma
3.7
and
\hat{u}_{\delta}\leq u_{\delta}
we have
\hat{u}_{\delta}-c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\leq u
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, the function

(3.23)

\tilde{u}:=\begin{cases}\max\{\hat{u}_{\delta}-c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha},u\}\quad&\mbox{ on }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u\quad&\mbox{ on }\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

belongs to
SH_{m}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
. Notice that
\tilde{u}\geq u
in
\Omega
, and

(3.24)

\tilde{u}=u\quad\mbox{ on }\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Again, by the second part of Lemma
3.7
we have that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)+c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\Omega}(\tilde{u}-u)+2c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

By the stability estimate (Proposition
3.3
) there exists
0<\alpha_{2}\leq 1
such that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}(\tilde{u}-u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2m}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\max\{\tilde{u}-u,0\}d\mu\right)^{\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2m}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{u}-u|d\mu\right)^{\alpha_{2}},

where we used the fact that
\tilde{u}=u
outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.
Using Theorem
3.5
, there is
0<\alpha_{3}\leq 1
such that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}(\tilde{u}-u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2m+(m+1)\alpha_{2}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{u}-u|dV_{2n}\right)^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{3m+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}|\hat{u}_{\delta}-u|dV_{2n}\right)^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}},

where we used
0\leq\tilde{u}-u\leq{\bf 1}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\cdot(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)
and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega_{\delta}
for the second inequality. It follows from (
3.25
), (
3.27
) and Lemma
3.6
that

(3.28)

\sup_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\frac{C\delta^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}}{\varepsilon^{3m+1}}.

Now, we choose
\alpha_{4}=\alpha\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}/(3m+1+\alpha)
and

\varepsilon=\delta^{\frac{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}{3m+1+\alpha}}.

Then,

\sup_{\Omega_{\delta}}(\hat{u}_{\delta}-u)\leq C\delta^{\alpha_{4}}.

Finally, thanks to
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
(see also
[
36
]
) we infer that

\sup_{\Omega_{\delta}}(u_{\delta}-u)\leq C\delta^{\alpha_{4}}.

The proof of the theorem is finished.
∎

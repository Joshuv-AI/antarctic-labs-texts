Diameter estimate for planar L p L_{p} dual Minkowski problem
Minhyun Kim, Taehun Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, given a prescribed measure on 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} whose density is bounded and positive, we establish a uniform diameter estimate for solutions to the planar L p L_{p} dual Minkowski problem when 0 < p < 1 0<p<1 and q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 . We also prove the uniqueness and positivity of solutions to the L p L_{p} Minkowski problem when the density of the measure is sufficiently close to a constant in C α C^{\alpha} .

52A10, 52A39, 53A04

1.
Introduction

The Minkowski problem, posed by Minkowski
[
28
]
, is one of the central problems in convex geometry. It asks if a given measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
arises as the surface area measure of a convex body. Here, a
convex body
K
is a compact convex set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a nonempty interior, and its surface area measure
S(K,\cdot)
is determined by the celebrated Aleksandrov variational formula

(1.1)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}(K+tL)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0^{+}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)\,\mathrm{d}S(K,u)\quad\text{for any convex body }L,

where
\mathrm{Vol}
denotes the
n
-dimensional volume and
h_{L}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is the
support function of
L
defined by
h_{L}(u)=\max\{u\cdot x:x\in L\}
.

An important variant of the Minkowski problem is the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem initiated by Lutwak
[
26
]
. To describe the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, we first recall Firey’s
[
17
]

p
-linear combination of convex bodies. Let us denote by
\mathcal{K}^{n}_{o}
the set of all convex bodies containing the origin (not necessarily in its interior). For
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{o}
,
p\geq 1
, and
t,s>0
, the
p
-linear combination
t\cdot_{p}K+_{p}s\cdot_{p}L
of
K
and
L
is defined as the convex body whose support function is given by
(th_{K}^{p}+sh_{L}^{p})^{1/p}
. Then, there exists a Borel measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that

(1.2)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}(K+_{p}t\cdot_{p}L)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0^{+}}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}^{p}(u)\,\mathrm{d}S_{p}(K,u)

for all convex bodies
L
. We call this measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
the
L_{p}
surface area measure
, and it is known
[
26
]
that
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
is related to
S(K,\cdot)
by

(1.3)

S_{p}(K,\cdot)=h_{K}^{1-p}S(K,\cdot)\quad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

In addition, the total measure

(1.4)

S_{p}(K,\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}S_{p}(K,u)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}^{1-p}(u)\,\mathrm{d}S(K,u)

is called the
L_{p}
surface area of
K
.
Note that the
L_{p}
surface area measure can be defined for all
p\in\mathbb{R}
through the relation (
1.3
).
The associated Minkowski type problem is then defined for all
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
and is called the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem. Precisely, it asks if a given measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
arises as the
L_{p}
surface area measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
of a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{o}
. This problem includes the classical Minkowski problem (
p=1
), the logarithmic Minkowski problem (
p=0
), and the centro-affine Minkowski problem (
p=-n
) as important cases.

On the other hand, an important variant of the logarithmic Minkowski problem with a parameter
q
is recently proposed in
[
20
]
which incorporates the Aleksandrov problem (
q=0
) as well as the logarithmic Minkowski problem itself (
q=n
). Therefore, in view of the logarithmic Minkowski problem, there are two parameters
p
and
q
which produce two corresponding Brunn–Minkowski type theory, namely, the
L_{p}
Brunn–Minkowski theory and the dual Brunn–Minkowski theory, respectively. Furthermore, in the very recent work
[
27
]
, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang unify the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem and the dual Minkowski problem by considering two parameters
p
and
q
at the same time. In particular, they introduce the
L_{p}
dual curvature measure
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The associated Minkowski problem is called the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem, and this covers all Minkowski type problems mentioned so far. Since then, the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem has been intensively studied by many authors, e.g.,
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
21
,
25
]
.

To describe the
L_{p}
dual curvature measure
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)
, we recall the
q
-th dual volume of
K
:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}_{q}(K)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho^{q}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\rho
is the
radial function of
K
defined by
\rho(\xi)=\max\{\lambda:\lambda\xi\in K\}
. For
q\not=0
, the
q
-th dual curvature measure
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
of a convex body
K
is determined by the following variational formula

(1.6)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}_{q}(K+tL)}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0^{+}}=q\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}h_{K}^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)\quad\text{for any }L\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{n}.

As in (
1.2
), we involve the
p
-linear combination into (
1.6
) to produce the
L_{p}
dual curvature measure
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)
, and it turns out that
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)
is related to
\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot)
by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)=h_{K}^{-p}\widetilde{C}_{q}(K,\cdot).

In the smooth category, the
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem becomes solving the Monge–Ampère type equation

(1.8)

h_{K}^{1-p}(h_{K}^{2}+|\nabla h_{K}|^{2})^{\frac{q-n}{2}}\det(\nabla^{2}h_{K}+h_{K}I)=f\quad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where
\nabla
is the covariant derivative with respect to an orthonormal frame on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
I
is the identity matrix.
In this paper, we consider uniform diameter estimates of solutions to (
1.8
).
This type of estimate is important in view of compactness results as in other geometric problems such as the Yamabe problem
[
24
]
.

The diameter estimate in the case
p>q
follows from a maximum principle argument. Indeed, if
h_{K}
attains its maximum at a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then the equation (
1.8
) implies that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}=h_{K}(x_{0})\leq(\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}.

However, the situation for
p\leq q
is complicated even in the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, i.e.,
q=n
.
In fact, there are no diameter estimates for (
1.8
) when
-n<p<0
and
q=n
as shown in
[
23
]
, where Jian, Lu, and Wang construct a sequence of bounded positive functions
f_{k}
such that diameters of the corresponding solutions are unbounded.
Note that the affine critical case
p=-n
and
q=n
also does not have diameter estimates since it is invariant under affine transformations. Much less is known about the super-critical case
p<-n
and
q=n
in which even the existence problem in the smooth category is resolved very recently
[
19
]
.

On the other hand, when
p>1
and
q=n
, Chou and Wang
[
15
]
showed the diameter estimates for solutions to (
1.8
).
In that paper, the case
1<p\leq n
is obtained by an approximation argument, and the case
p>n
uses a simple maximum argument as in (
1.9
).
For the logarithmic case
p=0
and
q=n
, there are recent results on the lower dimensional case. Namely, when
n=2
or
3
, diameter estimates are provided by Chen and Li
[
13
]
for
n=2
; by Chen, Feng, and Liu
[
11
]
for
n=3
. In particular, the paper
[
13
]
concerns the diameter estimates in a more general framework of the dual Minkowski problem with
q>0=p
. Finally, the case
0<p<1
and
q=2
in planar
L_{p}
Minkowski problem is treated in
[
16
]
.

The question of diameter estimate remains open widely for general
(p,q)
with
p<q
.
In this paper, we prove the diameter estimate for solutions to the planar
L_{p}
dual Minkowski problem for
p\in(0,1)
and
q\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
,
q\geq 2
,
\Lambda\geq 1
,
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, and suppose that
1/\Lambda\leq f\leq\Lambda
. If a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{o}
solves (
1.8
), then
\|h_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq C
for some
C=C(p,q,\Lambda)>0
.

We stress that a crucial ingredient in
Theorem

1.1
is establishing estimates on the total measure of
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\cdot)
in terms of its volume and surface area (
Lemma

2.1
), which also provides a new proof in the
L_{p}
case with
0<p<1
. Once we have the estimates, we will argue by contradiction assuming that there exists a sequence of convex bodies such that eccentricity of their John ellipse converges to one, i.e., the ratio of the major axis over the minor axis goes to infinity.
As indicated in the equation (
1.8
), the location of the origin is important since the equation becomes degenerate at points where
h_{K}
is zero. Hence we divide the problem into two cases: (1) the origin lies near a tip; (2) the origin lies far from the tip. See details in
Section

3
.

It would be interesting to prove the diameter estimate for higher dimensional cases
n\geq 3
. We also note that positive lower bounds for solutions to (
1.8
) are not available in general even when
f
is positive and smooth since there exists a counterexample, see the discussion for positivity of solutions below.

The diameter estimate can be applied to obtain the uniqueness and positivity of solutions to the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem (
q=2
) when the density of the measure is sufficiently close to a constant in
C^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
,
q=2
, and
f\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Then, there exists a small constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
that only depends on
p
, such that if
\|f-1\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the equation (
1.8
) has a unique solution. Moreover, the solution is positive and of
C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

There are relatively a few known results concerning the uniqueness of solutions for the case
0\leq p<1
. This is because even in
n=2
and
p=0
, the uniqueness does not hold for some positive, smooth function
f
as shown in
[
29
]
. See also
[
14
]
for non-uniqueness result when the given measure is discrete and
0<p<1
.
Hence most of literature assume symmetry on
f

[
12
]
or even strongly
f\equiv 1

[
18
,
1
,
6
]
. For a non-symmetric
f
, the uniqueness of solutions to (
1.8
) is proved in
[
11
]
when
p=0
,
n=3
, and
f
is close enough to a constant function as in
Theorem

1.2
; and in
[
4
]
when
0<p<1
,
n=2
, and
f
is origin symmetric.

It is worth noting that when
q>p+4
and
p>0
, the solution to (
1.8
) is not unique even if
f\equiv 1
, see
[
9
]
; when
p>q
, the solution to (
1.8
) is unique for any
f\in C^{\alpha}
, see
[
21
]
. To the best of our knowledge, in the case
p<q\leq p+4
and
0<p<1
, the uniqueness for the equation (
1.8
) is not known even when
f\equiv 1
.

Let us review some results related to the positivity of solutions for various
p
with
q=2
. In
[
15
]
the authors show that when
p\geq n
or
-n<p\leq-n+2
, solutions of (
1.8
) are positive under the assumption
0<c\leq f\leq C
for some constants
c
and
C
. On the other hand, the remaining case in the sub-critical range,
-n+2<p<n
, allows solutions that have zero at some points. See the examples in
[
5
, Example 1.6]
and
[
2
, Example 4.2]
for
-n+2<p<1
and in
[
22
, Example 4.1]
for
1<p<n
. Especially in the planar case
n=2
, the solutions are not positive in general if
0<p<2
. Therefore, the condition that the function
f
is close enough to a constant function in
C^{\alpha}
is necessary for
Theorem

1.2
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we estimate the total measure of
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}
of convex bodies in terms of an interpolation between the area and length of their John ellipses. This estimate is one of the main ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.
We then prove our first main result,
Theorem

1.1
, in
Section

3
, and finally the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is given in
Section

4
.

2.
L_{p}
dual curvature measure of convex bodies

In this section, we provide upper and lower bounds for the total measures of the
L_{p}
dual curvature measures of convex bodies in terms of principal radii of their John ellipses. Recall from
[
20
,
27
]
that

(2.1)

\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho^{q-2}(\mathscr{A}^{\ast}(u))h_{K}^{1-p}(u)\,\mathrm{d}S(K,u),

where
\mathscr{A}^{\ast}(u)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}:N(\rho(\xi)\xi)=u\}
is the reverse radial Gauss mapping and
N:\partial K\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the Gauss map. For notational convenience, we write
S_{K}=S(K,\cdot)
in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{o}
and let
E
be the John ellipse such that
E\subset K\subset 2E
with principal radii
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. If
p\in[0,1]
and
q\geq 2
, then

(2.2)

c_{1}(r_{1}r_{2})^{1-p}\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p+q-2}{2}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})\leq c_{2}(r_{1}r_{2})^{1-p}\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p+q-2}{2}}

for some
c_{1},c_{2}>0
which only depend on
p
and
q
.

Proof.

Let us first prove the upper bound of
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Since

(2.3)

\rho(\mathscr{A}^{\ast}(u))\leq 4\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we obtain

(2.4)

\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})\leq 4^{q-2}\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}S_{p}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1}).

By using the Hölder inequality, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}S_{p}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})&\leq 2^{1-p}\mathrm{Area}(K)^{1-p}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial K)^{p}\\
&\leq 2^{1-p}\mathrm{Area}(2E)^{1-p}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial(2E))^{p}.\end{split}

In the second inequality we used that the area of a set and the length of the boundary of a convex body are monotone with respect to the set inclusion. Combining (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) and using

(2.6)

\mathrm{Area}(2E)=4\pi r_{1}r_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial(2E))\leq 2\sqrt{2}\pi\left(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

we deduce the upper bound of
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})
in (
2.2
) with
c_{2}=2^{2q-1-3p/2}\pi
.

Let us next prove the lower bound of
\widetilde{C}_{p,q}(K,\mathbb{S}^{1})
. By rotating the coordinates if necessary, we may assume that
E
is given by

(2.7)

E=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{(x_{1}-a_{1})^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{(x_{2}-a_{2})^{2}}{r_{2}^{2}}\leq 1\right\}

with
a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0
.
We consider two lines

and
L_{-}
that pass through the point

at

Then, we may write them as

3.
Diameter estimate

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. We basically follow the ideas given in
[
13
]
and
[
11
]
, but our proof significantly uses
Lemma

2.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
E
be the John ellipse such that

(3.1)

E\subset K\subset 2E,

where
E
is centered at the center of mass of
K
. By rotating the coordinates if necessary, we may assume that
E
is given by

(3.2)

E=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{(x_{1}-a_{1})^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{(x_{2}-a_{2})^{2}}{r_{2}^{2}}\leq 1\right\}

with
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
. Once we prove that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
r_{2}\geq C_{0}r_{1}
, the desired result follows. Indeed, if
r_{2}\geq C_{0}r_{1}
, then it follows from
Lemma

2.1

4.
Uniqueness and positivity of solution

In this section, we prove the uniqueness and positivity of a solution to the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
,
q=2
, and
f\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1/2)
such that if
\|f-1\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and if

,

, are solutions to (
1.8
) with
\|h_{K_{j}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then
K_{1}=K_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there is no such a constant
\varepsilon_{0}
. Then, there exist sequences
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{L_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of convex bodies such that
K_{i}\not=L_{i}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|h_{K_{i}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\frac{1}{i+1},\quad\|h_{L_{i}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\frac{1}{i+1},

and both
K_{i}
and
L_{i}
are solutions to (
1.8
) with
f_{i}
satisfying
\|f_{i}-1\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\frac{1}{i+1}
. By the result of Caffarelli
[
7
]
, we have for large
i
that
h_{K_{i}}\in W^{2,q}
for all
q>0
and hence
h_{K_{i}}\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}
for all
0<\alpha^{\prime}<1
.
Moreover, we have
\|h_{K_{i}}\|_{C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq C
for some constant

independent of
i
. By compactness, we find a subsequence (still denoted by
\{K_{i}\}
) such that

in

for all

as

, and in particular
h_{K_{i}}\rightarrow 1
in
C^{0,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.
Furthermore,
h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}\rightarrow 1
in
C^{0,1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
since

and so for any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-p}\left|h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}(x)-h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}(y)\right|\leq\left|\int_{h_{K_{i}}(y)}^{h_{K_{i}}(x)}t^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq 2^{2-p}|h_{K_{i}}(x)-h_{K_{i}}(y)|.

Thus, it follows from

in

that

\displaystyle h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}f_{i}\rightarrow 1\quad\text{in }C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})\quad\text{as }i\rightarrow\infty.

Note that

satisfies a uniformly elliptic linear equation whose coefficients are independent in

. Indeed, it satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(h_{K_{i}}-1)+(h_{K_{i}}-1)

\displaystyle=h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}f_{i}-1\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{1}.

Applying the Schauder estimate to

, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|h_{K_{i}}-1\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq C\left(\|h_{K_{i}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\left\|h_{K_{i}}^{p-1}f_{i}-1\right\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right),

where the right-hand side goes to zero as

.
By the same argument, we also have

, which implies that the linearized operator

is invertible. Hence, by the inverse function theorem, we conclude that

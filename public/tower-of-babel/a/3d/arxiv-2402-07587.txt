Magnetized strangelets with anomalous magnetic moment and Coulomb interactions
Huai-Min Chen, 1, Xiao-Wei Li, 3, Cheng-Jun Xia 4, Jing-Tao Wang 2, Guang-Xiong Peng 2,5,6

Abstract
We study the magnetized strangelets in the baryon density-dependent quark mass model, including the effects of both confinement and lead-order perturbation interactions. The properties of magnetized strangelets are investigated under the the field strength 2 × 10 17 ​ G 2\times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{G} , where the anisotropy caused by the strong magnetic field is insignificant can be treated approximately as an isotropic system. The consideration of anomalous magnetic moments in the energy spectrum naturally solves the difficulty of infrared divergence encountered in integrating the density of states. The Coulomb interaction is accounted for a self-consistent treatment. The energy per baryon, mechanically stable radius, strangeness and electric charge of magnetized strangelets are presented, where their dependence on the field strength and parameter of confinement and perturbation are investigated.

Thermodynamic treatment in a strong magnetic field

II.1
Thermodynamic framework

The thermodynamic potential density
\Omega_{0}
at zero temperature is given for a free quasiparticle system due to its dependence on
\mu^{*}

\Omega_{0}=\sum_{i}\int\Big(\sqrt{p^{2}+m_{i}^{2}}-\mu_{i}^{*}\Big)\rho_{i}(p)\mathrm{d}^{3}p,

(1)

where index

(2)

where

,

and

are volume, area and extrinsic curvature of strangelets, and

(3)

\displaystyle f_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\Big[1-\frac{3p}{2m_{i}}\mathrm{arctan}\big(\frac{m_{i}}{p}\big)\Big].

Note that the density of states in Eq. (

At the zero temperature, within the framework of an equivalent mass model

(5)

Considering mass as an intermediate variable, the differential of Eq. (

\displaystyle\text{d}\overline{E}

\displaystyle=

(6)

\displaystyle+

II.2
Charged fermion in a uniform magnetic field

In the non-relativistic case, the energy levels of charged particles under a uniform magnetic field were given by Landau in 1930. Transitioning from classical theory to quantum mechanics, taking into account the spin of charged particles, the Hamiltonian operator in electromagnetic fields is

\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2m}\Big(\hat{{\bf p}}-q{\bm{A}}\Big)^{2}-\hat{{\bm{\mu}}}\cdot{\bm{B}}+q\phi,

(17)

where
{\bm{B}}
is the magnetic field strength (in the natural system of units, taking into account that the vacuum permeability is taken to be 1, the magnetic field strength is equal to the magnetic induction strength
{\bm{H}}={\bm{B}}
),
{\bm{A}}
is the magnetic vector potential, and
\phi
is the electric potential. The intrinsic magnetic moment
\hat{{\bm{\mu}}}=\mu\hat{{\bm{s}}}/s
, where
\mu
is the magnetic moment value,
\hat{{\bm{s}}}
is the particle spin operator, and
s
is the spin quantum number. Let the uniform magnetic field
{\bm{B}}=(0,0,B)
, then the vectorial potential can be taken as

A_{x}=-By,\quad A_{y}=A_{z}=0.

(18)

The electric potential is taken to be 0 and the Hamiltonian operator becomes

\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2m}\Big(\hat{p_{x}}+qBy\Big)^{2}+\frac{\hat{p}_{y}^{2}+\hat{p}_{z}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{\mu}{s}\hat{s}_{z}B.

(19)

Noting that
\hat{s}_{z}
is commutative with the Hamiltonian, this operator can be replaced by the eigenvalue
\sigma
, then the spin part of the wave function does not matter, and the Schr
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
dinger equation is taken to be

\frac{1}{2m}\Big[\Big(\hat{p_{x}}+qBy\Big)^{2}+\hat{p}_{y}^{2}+\hat{p}_{z}^{2}\Big]\psi-\frac{\mu}{s}\sigma B\psi=E\psi.

(20)

Further note that the Hamiltonian is commutative with
\hat{p}_{x}
and
\hat{p}_{z}
and
\psi
can be taken as
\psi=\mathrm{exp}(i(p_{x}x+p_{z}z))\chi(y)
. The
\chi
satisfies

\chi^{\prime\prime}+2m\Big[\Big(E+\frac{\mu\sigma B}{s}-\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}(y-y_{0})^{2}\Big]\chi=0,

(21)

where
y_{0}=-p_{x}/qB
,
\omega=|q|B/m
. Let
E^{\prime}=E+\mu\sigma B/s-p_{z}^{2}/2m
, then Eq. (
21
) is equivalent to the equation of a linear harmonic oscillator with energy
E^{\prime}
and frequency
\omega
. The energy eigenvalues of the linear harmonic oscillator are
E^{\prime}=(n+1/2)\omega
,
n=0,1,2,\cdots
.

As a result, the energy level of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field is

E_{n}=\Big(n+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\omega+\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{\mu\sigma B}{s}.

(22)

The first term in the above equation corresponds to the motion in the transverse plane, and these energy levels
n
are known as Landau energy levels. For charged fermions,
\mu/s=\mathrm{sgn}(q)|q|/m
, where
\mathrm{sgn}(q)
is the sign function. Thereby, Eq. (
22
) can be rewritten as

E_{p,q}=\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{e|q|B}{2m}\Big[2n+1-2\sigma\mathrm{sgn}(q)\Big]=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}.

(23)

In the case of relativity, the energy of particles

E_{p}=\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}.

(24)

Taking
\eta=2\sigma\mathrm{sgn}(q)
, it can be seen from (
23
) that the transverse momentum is
p_{\perp}=\sqrt{p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2}}=\sqrt{|q|B(2n+1-\eta)}
. Thus, the energy of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field in the relativistic case should be

E_{p_{z},q}=\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+e|q|B(2n+1-\eta)+m^{2}}.

(25)

For multi-body systems, to determine the state in which the system is in, it is usually necessary to integrate in momentum space. Notice that the transverse momentum is discretized, and the integration in the transverse direction should become a summation. Such that
2\nu=2n+1-\eta
. The rules for substituting integrals for summation given by
Chakrabarty1996_PRD54-1306

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{x}\mathrm{d}p_{y}\longrightarrow 2\pi e|q_{i}|B\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}(2-\delta_{\nu,0}),

(26)

In addition to having an impact on particle energy levels and momentum, a strong magnetic field can also cause the pressure distribution of the system to be anisotropic. The pressure also split in
P_{\parallel}
and
P_{\perp}
, which denote parallel and transverse to the magnetic field direction
Ferrer2010_PRC82-065802
. The expressions of
P_{\parallel}
and
P_{\perp}
for a
magnetized fermion system can be written as

\displaystyle P_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu_{i}n_{i}-E-V\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial V}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle P_{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu_{i}n_{i}-E-V\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial V}\right)+B^{2}-\mathcal{M}B.

(28)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the magnetization of the system, which is given by

\mathcal{M}=-\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial B}.

(29)

Details of the derivation of the parallel and transverse pressures from the energy-momentum tensor are provided in Appendix
A

It can been see that the parallel pressure

satisfies the Hugenholtz-Van Hove (HVH) theorem

Considering a strong magnetic field, the energy density at zero temperature is given by

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}+\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{*}n_{i}+\frac{B^{2}}{2}

We assume the spherical shape for strangelets and

II.3
Anomalous magnetic moment

Strictly speaking, the energy formula under the relativistic case should be given by the Dirac equation, and, in addition to the intrinsic magnetic moments, the anomalous magnetic moments of charged particles should be taken into account. In 1950, Johnson and Lippman first considered the anomalous moments in the Dirac equation
Johnson1950_PR77-702
. However, the formula they gave was not covariant. Bjorken and Drell provided a formula for covariation

\Big[\gamma^{\mu}\Big(\partial_{\mu}+i|q|A_{\mu}\Big)-\frac{i\mu}{2}F_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+m\Big]\psi=0.

(34)

Then, the energy of charged particles containing anomalous magnetic moments can be obtained

E=\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+\Big(\sqrt{e|q|B(2n+1-\eta)+m^{2}}-\eta|Q|B\Big)^{2}}.

(35)

It should be noted that
(n=\nu,\eta=1)
and
(n=\nu-1,\eta=-1)
no longer equivalent due to the existence of anomalous magnetic moment
Q
, then the integral replacement rule Eq. (
26
) becomes

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{x}\mathrm{d}p_{y}\longrightarrow 2\pi e|q_{i}|B\sum_{\eta=\pm 1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}.

(36)

Considering that the components of the quark momentum must be real and that the Fermi momentum
\nu_{q}=\sqrt{\mu_{q}^{*2}-m_{q}^{2}}
is an upper limit to the quark momentum at zero temperature, there must be

\mu_{q}^{*2}-m_{q}^{2}-p_{\perp}^{2}\geq 0.

(37)

By Eq. (
37
), we have

n\leq\frac{\mu_{q}^{*2}-m_{q}^{2}}{2e|q_{q}|B}+\frac{\eta-1}{2}.

(38)

Thus, the upper limit for the sum of Landau energy levels in Eq. (
36
) is

n_{\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{int}\Big[\frac{\mu_{q}^{*2}-m_{q}^{2}}{2e|q_{q}|B}\Big]+\frac{\eta-1}{2},

(39)

where
\mathrm{int}[x]
is an integer function that means taking the integer part of argument
x
.

The anomalous magnetic moments of electrons and quarks are given

\begin{split}Q_{e}=0.0016\mu_{B},\quad&Q_{u}=1.85\mu_{N},\\
Q_{d}=-0.97\mu_{N},\quad&Q_{s}=-0.58\mu_{N},\end{split}

(40)

where
\mu_{B}\simeq 5.79\times 10^{-15}\ \mathrm{MeV/G}
,
\mu_{N}\simeq 3.15\times 10^{-18}\ \mathrm{MeV/G}
.
We can see that these anomalous magnetic moments are very small, and unless in extremely strong magnetic field environments, the contribution of anomalous magnetic moments can be completely ignored. Taking
Q=0
, the Eq. (
35
) returns to Eq. (
25
). The contribution of anomalous magnetic moments is not significant, but they play a crucial role in the study of the properties of strangelets under strong magnetic fields.

If the anomalous magnetic moment is not considered, the integration of the density of states under a magnetic field will encounter difficulties in infrared divergence due to the presence of surface and curvature terms. When
n=0
and
\eta=1
, there are two infrared divergences
\mathrm{ln}(p_{\perp,i})
and
\mathrm{ln}(p_{\perp,i})-1/p_{\perp,i}
for density of states under magnetic field, caused by the second and third term of Eq. (
2
), so called surface term and curvature term, respectively.

To address this issue, we introduce an infrared cutoff for
p_{z}
. The anomalous magnetic moment provides a natural cutoff
(2m-|Q|B)|Q|B

Felipe2008_PRC77-015807
, meaning that when
n=0
and
\eta=1
, the lower limit of the integral for
p_{z}
becomes
(2m-|Q|B)|Q|B
. The natural cutoff arises due to the mass-shell condition

p^{2}=E^{2}-m^{2}\geq 0.

(41)

When
n=0
and
\eta=1
, Eq. (
35
) gives us

E=\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+(m-|Q|B)^{2}}.

(42)

Substituting Eq. (
42
) into Eq. (
41
), we obtain

p_{z}^{2}+m^{2}-2m|Q|B+|Q|^{2}B^{2}-m^{2}\geq 0,

(43)

i.e.,

V
Summary

We have investigated the properties of magnetized strangelets by the baryon density-dependent quark mass model with a quark mass scaling contain confinement and perturbative effects. The contribution of Coulomb interaction has been treated in a thermodynamic self-consistent way, where the contribution of Coulomb interaction to the chemical potential and pressure is accounted for. Considering the anisotropy caused by a strong magnetic field and the anomalous magnetic moment of quarks, the thermodynamic quantities of the magnetized strangelets that satisfy thermodynamic consistency are obtained.

It is found that the dependences of energy and charge per baryon and mechanically stable radius of magnetized strangelets on the baryon number and perturbative parameter are similar to the strangelets in zero magnetic field.
The electric charge per baryon of strangelets and magnetized strangelets become close at a large baryon number, and both tend to electric neutrality.
However, the strong magnetic field leads to larger energy and charge per baryon, larger strangeness, and smaller mechanically stable radii of strangelets for fixed parameters, compared with the zero magnetic field one. For fixed confinement parameters, the energy and charge per baryon and mechanically stable radius increase as perturbative
parameter
C
increases, and the rate of charge per baryon of magnetized strangelets is faster than strangelets.
In addition, the dependence of energy per baryon and mechanically stable radius on magnetic field strength are obtained, where the strong magnetic field lead to larger energy and smaller stable radius of strangelets with same baryon number.

Thermodynamics of quark matter in a uniform magnetic field

In the presence of electromagnetic fields, the energy-momentum tensor can be decomposed into matter and field parts, i.e.,

T^{\mu\nu}=T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm matter}+T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm fields},

(69)

where the field part is fixed by

\displaystyle T^{00}_{\rm fields}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(E^{2}+B^{2}),

(70)

\displaystyle T^{0i}_{\rm fields}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{i0}_{\rm fields}=(\vec{E}\times\vec{B})_{i},

(71)

\displaystyle T^{ij}_{\rm fields}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(E^{2}+B^{2})\delta_{ij}-E_{i}E_{j}-B_{i}B_{j}.

(72)

To obtain the matter part of the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm matter}\equiv{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}
in the local rest frame of the system, we first write the Lagrangian density of the equivparticle model for quark matter, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\left[i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m_{i}(n_{\mathrm{b}})+\frac{1}{2}Q_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right]\psi_{i},

(73)

where
q_{i}
is the charge of quark
i
,
Q_{i}
the anomalous magnetic moment,
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+iq_{i}A_{\mu}
,
m_{i}=m_{0i}+m_{\mathrm{I}}(n_{\mathrm{b}})
, and
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=i[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]/2
. The baryon number density is obtained with
n_{\mathrm{b}}=\sum_{i}\langle\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{0}\psi_{i}\rangle/3
. The equations of motion for
\psi_{i}
can be determined by Euler-Lagrange equation, i.e.,

\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\bar{\psi}_{i}}-\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}_{i})}\right)=0.

(74)

This gives the Dirac equation for quarks, i.e.,

\left[i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}-V_{V}\gamma^{0}-m_{i}(n_{\mathrm{b}})+\frac{1}{2}Q_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right]\psi_{i}=0,

(75)

where the term
V_{V}
arises from the density dependent quark masses, i.e.,

V_{V}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\mbox{d}m_{\mathrm{I}}}{\mbox{d}n_{\mathrm{b}}}\sum_{i}\langle\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}\rangle.

(76)

The energy energy-momentum tensor is then obtained with

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\biggl[i\left(\gamma^{\mu}D^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}D^{\mu}\right)\biggr]\psi_{i}

(77)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\biggl[Q_{i}\left(\sigma^{\mu\alpha}{F^{\nu}}_{\alpha}+\sigma^{\nu\alpha}{F^{\mu}}_{\alpha}\right)\biggr]\psi_{i}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V}g^{\mu\nu}.

Note that we have adopted the relation
{\cal L}=3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V}
during derivation using the Minkowski space metric
g^{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
, which is obtained multiplying Eq. (
75
) by
\bar{\psi}_{i}
.

If the magnetic field is uniform and pointing to the

, which gives

0&\sigma_{3}\end{array}\right)\equiv Q_{i}B{\cal S}_{3}.

(78)

The Dirac equation for stationary states
\psi=e^{-i\varepsilon t}\Psi_{i}(\vec{x})
can then obtained with

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\left(i\vec{\nabla}-q_{i}\vec{A}\right)+q_{i}A_{0}\right]\Psi_{i}

(79)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[q_{i}A_{0}+V_{V}+\beta m_{i}-Q_{i}B\beta{\cal S}_{3}\right]\Psi_{i}.

Here

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\left(i\gamma^{0}D^{0}\right)\psi_{i}-3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V},

(80)

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\left(i\gamma^{x}D^{x}-Q_{i}B\sigma^{xy}\right)\psi_{i}+3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V},

(81)

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\left(i\gamma^{y}D^{y}-Q_{i}B\sigma^{xy}\right)\psi_{i}+3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V},

(82)

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{zz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\bar{\psi}_{i}\left(i\gamma^{z}D^{z}\right)\psi_{i}+3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V}.

(83)

For uniform matter comprised of
u
,
d
, and
s
quarks, the electric field vanish. The energy energy-momentum tensor for a uniform magnetic field
B
in parallel to the
z
-axis is determined by

T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm fields}=\frac{B^{2}}{2}{\rm diag}(1,1,1,-1)

(84)

The Dirac equation (
79
) can be solved by assuming
\Psi_{i}(\vec{x})=e^{ip_{x}x}e^{ip_{z}z}u^{(s)}_{n}(y)
with

u^{(s)}_{n}(y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}c_{1}\phi_{\nu}(y)\\
c_{2}\phi_{\nu-1}(y)\\
c_{3}\phi_{\nu}(y)\\
c_{4}\phi_{\nu-1}(y)\\
\end{array}\right)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \nu=n+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{2}\frac{q_{i}}{|q_{i}|},

(85)

where
n=0,1,2,\cdots
and the spin
s=\pm 1
. The function
\phi_{m}
is determined by

\phi_{m}(y)=N_{m}e^{-\xi^{2}/2}H_{m}(\xi)\ \ {\rm with}\ \ \xi=\sqrt{|q_{i}|B}\left(y+\frac{p_{x}}{q_{i}B}\right),

(86)

where
m\geq 0
is an integer,
H_{m}
is a Hermite polynomial, and
N_{m}=(q_{i}B)^{1/4}(\sqrt{\pi}2^{m}m!)^{-1/2}

is a normalization constant which ensures
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\,\phi_{n}^{2}(y)~=~1
.
Inserting this to the Dirac equation (
79
) gives

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}m-QB&0&p_{z}&p_{\nu}\\
0&m+QB&p_{\nu}&-p_{z}\\
p_{z}&p_{\nu}&-m+QB&0\\
p_{\nu}&-p_{z}&0&-m-QB\\
\end{array}\right)\chi=E\chi,

(87)

where
\chi=(c_{1}\;c_{2}\;c_{3}\;c_{4})^{\rm T}
,
p_{\nu}=\sqrt{2|q_{i}|B\nu}
, and
E=\varepsilon-q_{i}A_{0}-V_{V}
. The eigenstates for particles are

\chi_{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\lambda\alpha_{s}\beta_{s}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}s\alpha_{s}\beta_{s}\\
-p_{z}p_{\nu}\\
s\beta_{s}p_{z}\\
\alpha_{s}p_{\nu}\end{array}\right),

(88)

where
\alpha_{s}\equiv E_{s}-Q_{i}B+s\lambda
and
\beta_{s}\equiv\lambda+sm_{i}
. The corresponding eigenvalues are

E_{s}=\sqrt{p_{z}^{2}+(\lambda-sQ_{i}B)^{2}},

(89)

with
\lambda\equiv\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+p_{\nu}^{2}}
.
The general positive energy states can be constructed by

\psi_{i}(x)=\sum_{s=\pm 1}\frac{|q|B}{2\pi}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{s}}}b_{s}({\bf p})u^{(s)}({\bf p})e^{i\tilde{p}_{\mu}x^{\mu}},

(90)

where
b_{s}({\bf k})
obeys following relation

\{b_{r}({\bf p}),b_{s}^{\dagger}({\bf k})\}=(2\pi)\delta_{rs}\delta_{mn}\delta(p_{z}-k_{z}),

(91)

{\bf p}=(n,k_{z})
with
n=0,1,2,\cdots
,
\tilde{p}=(E_{s},p_{x},0,p_{z})
. For details, see Ref.
Strickland2012PRD86-125032
.

The energy density for infinite quark matter is then

\displaystyle{\cal E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\cal T}^{00}\rangle=\sum_{i}\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}

(92)

\displaystyle\times E_{s}f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu_{i}^{*}),

with the Fermi-Dirac distribution of particles fixed by

\langle b_{s}^{\dagger}({\bf p})b_{s}({\bf p})\rangle=f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu_{i}^{*})=\frac{1}{e^{(E_{s}-\mu_{i}^{*})/T}+1}.

(93)

Note that we have adopted the relation
E_{s}=\varepsilon_{s}-q_{i}A_{0}-V_{V}
in the deriving Eq. (
92
). The number density can be determined by

n_{i}=\langle\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{0}\psi_{i}\rangle=\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu_{i}^{*}),

(94)

while the baryon number density is
n_{\mathrm{b}}=\sum_{i}n_{i}/3
.
According to Eq. (
79
), we have

\displaystyle(\varepsilon-q_{i}A_{0}-V_{V})\Psi_{i}^{\dagger}\Psi_{i}=\Psi_{i}^{\dagger}\left[-\vec{\alpha}\cdot\left(i\vec{\nabla}-q_{i}\vec{A}\right)\right]\Psi_{i}

(95)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{\dagger}\left[\beta m_{i}-Q_{i}B\beta{\cal S}_{3}\right]\Psi_{i}.

Upon taking the derivative of the equation above with respect to mass, we obtain the following result:

\Psi_{i}^{\dagger}\beta\Psi_{i}=\frac{\partial E}{\partial m_{i}}\Psi_{i}^{\dagger}\Psi_{i},

(96)

where
E=\varepsilon-q_{i}A_{0}-V_{V}
.
Note that
\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\psi_{i}
and
\beta=\gamma^{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm}\sum_{n}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial E_{s}}{\partial m_{i}}\langle b_{s}^{\dagger}({\bf p})b_{s}({\bf p})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm}\sum_{n}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial E_{s}}{\partial m_{i}}f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu^{*}_{i}).

Thus, for vanishing temperatures with
T=0
, the expression of
V_{V}
is given by

V_{V}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\text{d}m_{I}}{\text{d}n_{b}}\sum_{i}\langle\bar{\psi}_{i}\psi_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\text{d}m_{I}}{\text{d}n_{b}}\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial m_{i}}.

(98)

The longitudinal pressure along magnetic field lines is

\displaystyle P_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\cal T}^{zz}\rangle

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{E_{s}}f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V}.

The transverse pressure is fixed by

\displaystyle P_{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\cal T}^{yy}\rangle=\langle{\cal T}^{xx}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{|q_{i}|B^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp_{z}\frac{1}{E_{s}}f_{+}(E_{s},T,\mu_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle\times\Biggl[\frac{|q_{i}|\nu\bar{m}_{i}(\nu)}{\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+2\nu|q_{i}|B}}-sQ_{i}\bar{m}_{i}(\nu)\Biggr]+3n_{\mathrm{b}}V_{V},

where
\bar{m}_{i}(\nu)\equiv\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+2\nu|q_{i}|B}-sQ_{i}B
.

For vanishing temperatures with
T=0
, the thermodynamic potential density is then fixed by
\Omega={\cal E}-\mu_{i}n_{i}=-P_{\parallel}
with
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{*}+V_{V}
.
A relationship between
P_{\parallel}
and
P_{\perp}
obtained according to the magnetization
\mathcal{M}\equiv-\partial\Omega/\partial B=\partial P_{\parallel}/\partial B
, which gives
P_{\perp}=P_{\parallel}-\mathcal{M}B
.
These relations can be derived through direct integration, as detailed in Ref.
Strickland2012PRD86-125032
.

If we further include the field contributions to energy-momentum tensor, the energy density, longitudinal and transverse pressures at
T=0
are fixed by

\displaystyle{\cal E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}E_{s}\Theta(\mu_{i}^{*}-E_{s})

(101)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}B^{2},

\displaystyle P_{\parallel}=-\Omega-\frac{1}{2}B^{2},

(102)

\displaystyle P_{\perp}=-\Omega-\mathcal{M}B+\frac{1}{2}B^{2}=P_{\parallel}+B^{2}-\mathcal{M}B,

(103)

where

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{|q_{i}|B}{2\pi}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}(E_{s}-\mu_{i})

(104)

\displaystyle\times\Theta(\mu_{i}^{*}-E_{s}),

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{*}+V_{V},

(105)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=-\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial B}.

(106)

Due to the independence of chemical potential
\mu_{i}
on mass
m_{i}
, combining with Eq. (
98
), we derive the relationship between
V_{V}
and the thermodynamic potential density as follows

\displaystyle V_{V}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}m_{I}}{\mathrm{d}n_{b}}\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial m_{i}}.

(107)

For a finite system, the contributions from surface corrections and Coulomb interactions need to be considered, which introduce additional terms to the energy density and pressures.

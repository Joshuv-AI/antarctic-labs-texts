Asymptotic quantization of measures on Riemannian manifolds via covering growth estimates
Ata Deniz Aydın, Mikaela Iacobelli

Abstract
. The quantization problem looks for best approximations of a probability measure on a given metric space by finitely many points, where the approximation error is measured with respect to the Wasserstein distance. On particular smooth domains, such as ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} or complete Riemannian manifolds, the quantization error is known to decay polynomially as the number of points is taken to infinity, provided the measure satisfies an integral condition which controls the amount of mass outside compact sets. On Riemannian manifolds, the existing integral condition involves a quantity measuring the growth of the exponential map, for which the only available estimates are in terms of lower bounds on sectional curvature. In this paper, we provide a more general integral condition for the asymptotics of the quantization error on Riemannian manifolds, given in terms of the growth of the covering numbers of spheres, which is purely metric in nature and concerns only the large-scale growth of the manifold. We further estimate the covering growth of manifolds in two particular cases, namely lower bounds on the Ricci curvature and geometric group actions by a discrete group of isometries. These estimates can themselves generalize beyond manifolds, and hint at a future treatment of asymptotic quantization also on non-smooth metric measure spaces.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
General overview

The quantization problem for measures on metric spaces involves the approximation of finite measures by finitely many points.
Given a finite Borel measure
\mu
on a complete and separable metric space
X
, the quantization problem of order
p\in[1,\infty]
involves the minimization of the
p
-Wasserstein distance
between
\mu
and a measure supported on at most
N
points:

e_{N,p}(\mu)=\inf_{\#\mathrm{supp}\ \mu_{N}\leq N}W_{p}(\mu,\mu_{N}).

This problem admits various equivalent formulations; in particular, it reduces to a problem over the support of the approximating measure
\mu_{N}
. More specifically, it is equivalent to minimizing the following functional

e_{p}(\mu;S):=\|d(\cdot,S)\|_{L^{p}(X;\mu)}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\sqrt[p]{\int_{X}d(x,S)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)},&p<\infty;\\
\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\ \mu}d(x,S),&p=\infty\end{matrix}\right.

over all sets
S\subseteq X
of cardinality at most
N
:

e_{N,p}(\mu)=\inf_{\#S\leq N}e_{p}(\mu;S).

For example, suppose
X
represents a geographical region, and
\mu
describes the distribution of a population on the region. Then the points in
S
are chosen to be optimally reachable by the population in some sense. For
p=1
,
S
is chosen such that the average distance to
S
is minimized, and for
p=\infty
,
S
is chosen such that the maximal distance to
S
is minimized; other values of
p
interpolate between these problems.

In this paper, we will work only with this set distance formulation and the case
p<\infty
, and denote the
p
th powers of
e_{p}(\mu;S)
and
e_{N,p}(\mu)
by
V_{p}(\mu;S)
and
V_{N,p}(\mu)
respectively. For reference on other equivalent formulations, see e.g. Graf and Luschgy
[
30
, Sec. 3]
.

We will be focusing on the asymptotics of the quantization error as
N\to\infty
. Before reviewing existing results on asymptotic quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and Riemannian manifolds, we first give a general survey of the history and applications of the quantization problem.

1.2.
Historical notes and applications

The origins of the quantization problem lie in the field of signal processing, with the goal of efficiently compressing analog signals (such as sounds or images) into digital ones taking values in a finite set. The problem of
scalar quantization
, quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}
, has been studied since the 1940s in information theory e.g. by Bennett
[
8
]
and by Oliver, Pierce and Shannon
[
55
]
. The quantization problem on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, under the name of
vector quantization
, has been studied in the signal processing community starting from the 1970s, e.g. by Gersho
[
28
]
, and afterwards by Zador
[
62
]
, Bucklew and Wise
[
17
]
and Graf and Luschgy
[
30
]
who completed the proof of Zador’s theorem on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For a detailed survey of quantization from the point of view of signal processing and information theory, we refer to Gray and Neuhoff
[
32
]
. See also Pagès
[
56
]
for an introduction to quantization and its applications in numerics.

Independently, Steinhaus
[
58
]
already in 1956 considered the quantization problem on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in a different formalism, and afterwards in 1959, L. Fejes Tóth
[
26
]
demonstrated the asymptotic optimality of hexagonal lattices for quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. This asymptotic optimality and stability of the hexagonal lattice is also observed in similar geometric problems such as the sphere packing problem.
This result of Fejes Tóth has been the subject of extensive study and generalization, see e.g.
[
34
,
61
,
10
]
, the extension to compact Riemannian 2-manifolds
[
35
]
as well as more recent results by
[
19
,
39
,
14
]
.
There also exist more purely geometric applications of quantization, such as the approximation of convex bodies by polytopes
[
13
,
35
,
36
,
43
]
, and
Alexandrov’s problem
of constructing a convex body or surface with prescribed Gaussian curvature
[
9
,
52
]
. These applications provide further motivation for the study of quantization for Riemannian manifolds.

The quantization problem has also been studied under different names, e.g. the
optimal location problem

[
11
,
15
,
14
]
,
centroidal Voronoi tessellations

[
23
,
24
]
, or the (more restrictive) problem of
k
-means clustering
in statistics
[
50
]
.
A related problem is
empirical quantization
or
uniform quantization
, in which one only considers the
p
-Wasserstein distance between a measure
\mu
and empirical measures (measures of the form
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}
). This problem also admits applications in e.g. image analysis, and has been studied in e.g.
[
21
,
44
,
53
]
in the
deterministic
setting, where one seeks to find the optimal
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, and in e.g.
[
37
,
31
,
22
,
27
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
in the
stochastic
setting (also referred to as
random matching
), where the points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are generated independently from a probability distribution, usually
\mu
itself.

Recent directions in the study of quantization include the calculus of variations/
\Gamma
-convergence approach applied in
[
11
,
14
]
for more general entropy as well as cardinality constraints, and the gradient flow approach taken by
[
18
,
19
,

]
for quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, leading to the consideration of
ultrafast diffusion equations

[
40
,
41
]
.

On Riemannian manifolds, the asymptotics of the quantization problem have been investigated by Gruber
[

,
36
]
and Kloeckner
[

for compactly supported measures, and generalized to the non-compact case by Iacobelli
[

]
. See also the recent work by Le Brigant and Puechmorel
[

,
47

introducing an algorithm for finding optimal quantizers for fixed

We will now focus on existing results for the asymptotics of quantization on Euclidean space and Riemannian manifolds in more detail, and afterwards state our new contributions.

1.3.
Asymptotics of quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and Riemannian manifolds

In particular domains, such as
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, Riemannian manifolds and also fractal domains, the quantization error
V_{N,p}(\mu)
has been shown to decay on the order of
N^{-p/d}
as
N\to\infty
, where
d
represents the dimensionality of the domain.
Heuristically, this means that in order to decrease the error
e_{N,p}(\mu)
by a factor of
k
, one would need to scale
N
by
k^{d}
. This exponential dependence on the dimension is also present in related problems, such as
optimal matching
(see e.g. survey given in
[
43
, §1.2.4]
). More generally, it is related to the so-called
curse of dimensionality
phenomenon in approximation, where the number or complexity of parameters required to achieve a given error threshold grows exponentially with the dimension of the data (cf.
[
49
, Thm. 1.1]
in relation to quantization, and
[
45
, §5.2.1]
for a broader background).

For measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
Zador’s theorem
provides the following precise expression for the asymptotics of the quantization error:

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}},

(1.1)

where
\rho
is the density of the absolutely continuous component of
\mu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and
Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\in(0,\infty)
is a constant referred to as the
quantization coefficient
of the
d
-dimensional unit cube. The most general form of this theorem is due to Graf and Luschgy
[
30
, Thm. 6.2]
, and is first proven for measures of compact support, and then generalized to measures which admit finite moments of higher order:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|x\|^{p+\delta}\mu(x)<\infty\quad\text{for some }\delta>0.

(1.2)

This condition implies also the finiteness of the right-hand side in (
1.1
)
[
30
, Rem. 6.3]
.
The extension from the compact to the noncompact case is facilitated by
Pierce’s lemma
, which provides a universal upper bound on the quantization error:

Lemma 1.1

(Pierce
[
57
, Thm. 1]
, Graf and Luschgy
[
30
, Lem. 6.6]
)
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\delta>0
.
Then there exist constants
C>0
and
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
depending on
d
,
p
and
\delta
such that, for any probability measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+\|x\|^{p+\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad\textup{for all }N\geq N_{0}.

Zador’s theorem extends to measures on Riemannian manifolds by a similar argument to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The analogous statement to (
1.1
), with the Lebesgue measure replaced with the Riemannian volume form, has first been proven by Kloeckner
[
43
]
for compactly supported measures, for which no integral condition is necessary. This result was afterwards extended by Iacobelli
[
38
]
to measures satisfying a more refined integrability condition depending also on the curvature of the manifold. The analogous statement to Pierce’s lemma on manifolds is given as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Iacobelli
[
38
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\delta>0
.
Then there exist constants
C>0
,
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
depending on
M
,
x_{0}
,
p
and
\delta
such that, for any probability measure
\mu
on
M
,

N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq C\int_{M}\left(1+d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad\textup{for all }N\geq N_{0},

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
measures the size of the differential of the exponential map at
x_{0}
, restricted to the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{r}\subset T_{x_{0}}M
of radius
r
centered at
0
:

{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(r):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{r},\\
w\in T_{v}{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{r},\\
\|w\|_{v}=r\end{subarray}}\left\|d_{v}\exp_{x_{0}}[w]\right\|_{\exp_{x_{0}}(v)}.

(1.3)

The generalization of Zador’s theorem for non-compactly supported measures is then deduced from the finiteness of the integral expression:

Theorem 1.3

(Iacobelli
[
38
, Thm. 1.4]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
M
such that

\int_{M}\left(d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty

for some
x_{0}\in M
and
\delta>0
. Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(x)\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}}<\infty,

(1.4)

where
\rho
is the density of the absolutely continuous component of
\mu
with respect to the Riemannian volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{M}
on
M
.

The quantity
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(r)
can be estimated in terms of sectional curvature bounds, yielding the following corollary:

Corollary 1.4

(Iacobelli
[
38
, Cor. 1.6]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with sectional curvature bounded from below by
-\kappa^{2}
for some
\kappa\geq 0
,
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
M
such that

\int_{M}(d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+e^{\kappa pd(x,x_{0}))})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty

for some
x_{0}\in M
and
\delta>0
. Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(x)\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}}.

(1.5)

In particular, Pierce’s lemma and Zador’s theorem hold for manifolds of nonnegative sectional curvature in the same form as for Euclidean space.
However, for manifolds of constant negative curvature this exponential moment condition is sharp:
[
38
, Thm. 1.7]
constructs a (singular) measure
\mu
on the hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
for which all polynomial moments
\int_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}d(x,x_{0})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
are finite but
\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)=\infty
.

1.4.
Definitions and main results

In this paper, we demonstrate that an exponential moment condition is not strictly necessary even for nonpositively curved manifolds when the sectional curvature is not constant. We achieve this by replacing the local quantity
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
with global covering numbers capturing the large-scale growth of the manifold, which can be defined in a general metric space setting:

Definition 1.5
.

Let
X
be a metric space.
Given a compact subset
A\subseteq X
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
N
th
covering radius
of
A
is the minimal
r>0
such that
A
can be covered by
N
open balls of radius
r
:

\displaystyle r_{N}(A):=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{r>0\mid\exists\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq X,A\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{r}(x_{i})\right\}.

Fix
d>0
, and let
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be monotone nondecreasing.
X
is said to have
O(f)
covering growth
(of dimension
d
) around
x_{0}\in X
if

kr_{k^{d-1}}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq f(R),\quad\textup{for all }k\in{\mathbb{N}},R>0.

(1.6)

Remark
.

For Riemannian manifolds,
d
will be the same as the dimension of the manifold. In general, the hypothesis that
\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}kr_{k^{d-1}}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))<\infty
implies that spheres
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
are at most
d-1
-dimensional in the sense of box-counting dimension (see e.g.
[
30
, Sect. 11.2]
).

For our purposes, it suffices for (
1.6
) to be satisfied for
k\geq k_{0}
and
R\geq R_{0}
for constants
k_{0},R_{0}>0
, but not when
k\geq k_{0}(R)
dependent on
R
. Up to a factor of
2
, one can equivalently require that
N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq f(R)
for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, since for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
k^{d-1}\leq N\leq(k+1)^{d-1}\leq 2^{d-1}k^{d-1}
.

As detailed in Section
4.2
, Riemannian manifolds of nonnegative Ricci curvature including Euclidean spaces have
O(R)
covering growth, while e.g. hyperbolic spaces only have
O(e^{\alpha R})
covering growth for some
\alpha>0
. As a special case, Example
4.6
shows that the covering growth of two-dimensional Riemannian manifolds coincides exactly with the growth of the perimeters of spheres, although this correspondence does not extend to higher dimensions.

In general, as demonstrated in Example
4.5
, any complete connected Riemannian manifold has
O({\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}})
covering growth around
x_{0}
.
As such, Theorem
1.2
admits the following generalization, valid in the setting of geodesic spaces:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a complete geodesic space. Fix a point
x_{0}\in X
, and suppose
X
has
O(f)
covering growth of dimension
d
around
x_{0}
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\mu
a finite Borel measure on
X
with finite moments up to order
p+\delta
for some
\delta>0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\mu
, for which

k^{p}V_{k^{d}+1,p}(\mu)\leq C\int_{X}\left(1+d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+f(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad\text{for all }k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Consequently,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq C\int_{X}\left(1+d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+f(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

As a direct consequence, on Riemannian manifolds we deduce the following extension of Zador’s theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M
be a complete connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Fix a point
x_{0}\in M
, and suppose
M
has uniform
O(f)
covering growth (of dimension
d
) around
x_{0}
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
M
such that, for some
\delta>0
,

\int_{M}\left(d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+f(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty.

Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}},

(1.7)

where
\rho
is the density of the absolutely continuous component of
\mu
with respect to the Riemannian volume form on
M
, and
Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\in(0,\infty)
is again the quantization coefficient of the
d
-dimensional unit cube.

This is a strictly more general condition than the one expressed in terms of
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
. To demonstrate this, we consider two special cases: manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below, and manifolds subject to a
geometric action
of a discrete group of isometries. In particular, we show in Section
4.2
that if the manifold has nonnegative
Ricci
curvature, then it has
O(R)
covering growth and thus Pierce’s lemma holds as in Euclidean space. In the latter case, if the group of isometries is finitely generated with polynomial growth, the covering growth of the manifold is also polynomial and hence a polynomial moment condition is sufficient for Zador’s theorem to hold:

Proposition 1.8
.

Suppose
M
is subject to the geometric action of a group
G
of isometries, which is of
polynomial growth
of order
\alpha
.

Then fixing
x_{0}\in M
, there exists
C>0
such that for
R>0
sufficiently large and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
arbitrary, the following bound holds for the covering growth of spheres in
M
:

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq CR^{\alpha+d-1}.

Thus
M
has
O(R^{\frac{\alpha}{d-1}+1})
covering growth around any point
x_{0}\in M
.

These results rely only on metric arguments and the
Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem
for complete Riemannian manifolds, and could thus be extended to non-smooth metric measure spaces which satisfy the same volume comparison property. Specifically, the
curvature-dimension spaces
introduced independently by Sturm
[
59
,
60
]
and by Lott and Villani
[
48
]
are shown to satisfy the same volume comparison property as Riemannian manifolds (cf.
[
60
, Thm. 2.3]
).
While Theorem
1.7
will not extend directly to curvature-dimension spaces, owing to the dependence on the locally Euclidean structure of manifolds, the general upper bound in Theorem
1.6
as well as the estimates given in Section
4
will carry over thanks to the Bishop-Gromov theorem. Though we will only be focusing on Riemannian manifolds in this paper, we nevertheless present Theorem
1.6
in the general setting of geodesic spaces for the sake of future investigations of non-smooth settings.

2.
Preliminaries

We first review a list of preliminary definitions and facts about the quantization error, as well as the covering radii of sets. The material in this section is valid in the setting of general
Polish
metric spaces, i.e., separable metric spaces that admit an equivalent complete metric.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Polish metric space,
p\in[1,\infty)
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
the set of finite positive Borel measures on
X
with
finite
p
th moments
:
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
if
\int_{X}d(x,x_{0})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
for some (
\iff
all)
x_{0}\in X
.

Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
, the
quantization error
of a set
S\subseteq X
of order
p
with respect to
\mu
is

V_{p}(\mu;S):=\int_{X}d(x,S)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

The
N
th
quantization error
of
\mu
of order
p
is the infimum over all subsets of cardinality at most
N
:

V_{N,p}(\mu):=\inf_{\#S\leq N}V_{p}(\mu;S).

The
p
th root of the quantization error is also denoted by
e_{N,p}(\mu)
. If
A\subseteq X
, we also denote
V^{(\mu)}_{p}(A;S):=V_{p}(\mu|_{A};S)
and likewise for
V^{(\mu)}_{N,p}
and
e^{(\mu)}_{N,p}
.

The quantization error of a finite linear combination of measures can be decomposed linearly:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}
be finite Borel measures on a Polish metric space
X
, with finite
p
th moments for
p<\infty
.

Set
\mu:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\mu_{i}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}>0
.
Then for any
N,N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
N\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}N_{i}
,

V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}V_{N_{i},p}(\mu_{i}).

Proof.

For each
i=1,\ldots,k
, let
S_{i}
be an arbitrary subset of
X
with cardinality at most
N_{i}
. Then
S:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}
has cardinality at most
N
, and

\displaystyle V_{N,p}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}d(x,S)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\int_{X}d(x,S)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(x)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\int_{X}d(x,S_{i})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(x).

Taking the infimum over all such
S_{i}
yields the statement.
∎

For every
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
,
V_{N,p}(\mu)\to 0
as
N\to\infty
(see e.g.
[
30
, Lem. 6.1]
). When
\mu
is compactly supported, this follows by taking an
\varepsilon
-cover of
\mathrm{supp}\ \mu
for
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small. The general case follows from
Ulam’s lemma
, valid for Polish spaces, which we cite for later use:

Lemma 2.3

(Ulam’s lemma)
.

Let
X
be a Polish space,
\nu
a finite Borel measure on
X
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K\subset X
compact such that
\nu(X\setminus K)<\varepsilon
.

In particular, for
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
K\subseteq X
compact such that
\int_{X\setminus K}d(x,x_{0})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\varepsilon
. For a proof of Ulam’s lemma, refer to Dudley
[
25
, 7.1.4]
.

The rate of convergence of the quantization error to zero can be further quantified by the following definition:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Polish metric space,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
d>0
,
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
.

The
upper
resp.
lower quantization coefficient
of
\mu
of order
p
and dimension
d
is

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu);\quad\underline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu):=\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu).

When the upper and lower limits coincide, the limit is denoted simply by
Q_{p,d}(\mu)
and called the
quantization coefficient
of
\mu
.

We will only need the following subadditivity property of upper quantization coefficients, which is also used implicitly in proofs of the Zador theorem (cf.
[
30
, Lem. 6.5, 6.8]
):

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{+}(X)
,
p<\infty
. Set
\mu:=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
. Let
d>0
, and fix
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
t_{1}+t_{2}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq t_{1}^{-p/d}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})+t_{2}^{-p/d}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2}).

Proof.

For each
N\geq\max\{t_{1}^{-1},t_{2}^{-1}\}
, set
N_{1}=N_{1}(N):=\lfloor t_{1}N\rfloor\geq 1
and
N_{2}=N_{2}(N):=\lfloor t_{2}N\rfloor\geq 1
. Then by Lemma
2.2
,

N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq N^{p/d}V_{N_{1},p}(\mu_{1})+N^{p/d}V_{N_{2},p}(\mu_{2})=\left(\frac{N_{1}}{N}\right)^{-p/d}N_{1}^{p/d}V_{N_{1},p}(\mu_{1})+\left(\frac{N_{2}}{N}\right)^{-p/d}N_{2}^{p/d}V_{N_{2},p}(\mu_{2}).

Taking the limit supremum of both sides, noting that the limit supremum is subadditive and that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{N_{i}}{N}=t_{i}
, yields the inequality.
∎

In particular, picking the proportions
t_{i}
optimally, we have the following inequality:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}
be finite Borel measures on a complete metric space
X
, with finite
p
th moments for
p<\infty
. Set
\mu:=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
. Let
s>0
, and set
r:=\frac{d}{p+d}
; i.e.,
\frac{1}{rp}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})^{r}+\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})^{r}.

Proof.

If either summand is zero, say
\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})=0
, then by Lemma
2.5
, for arbitrary
t_{1}\in(0,1)
we have that

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)\leq t_{1}^{-p/d}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})

Letting
t_{1}\to 1^{-}
yields
\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)\leq\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})
and the desired inequality holds.

We can then assume both summands are nonzero. Set

t_{i}:=Z^{-1}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{i})^{r};\quad Z=\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})^{r}+\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})^{r}.

Then
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,1)
with
t_{1}+t_{2}=1
, so by Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq Z^{p/d}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})^{1-rp/d}+Z^{p/d}\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})^{1-rp/d}

\displaystyle\leq Z^{p/d}\left[\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})^{r}+\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})^{r}\right]

\displaystyle=Z^{1+p/d}=Z^{1/r}

since
1-\frac{rp}{d}=1-\frac{p}{d+p}=\frac{d}{d+p}=r
. This shows that

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)^{r}\leq Z=\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{1})^{r}+\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu_{2})^{r}.\qed

This bound is an application of the equality case of the generalized Hölder inequality. Analogous statements can be formulated for the quantization problem of order
\infty
, but we omit its treatment here.

2.1.
Covering numbers and radii

In preparation for the upper bound on the quantization error, we also review the covering numbers and radii of sets in general metric spaces.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
A
be a nonempty (pre)compact subset of a complete metric space
X
.

•

The
covering number
of
A
of radius
r
is the smallest number of open
r
-balls that cover
A
:

N(A;r):=\min\left\{N\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\exists\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq X,A\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{r}(x_{i})\right\}.

•

The
packing number
of
A
of radius
r
is the greatest number of disjoint open
r
-balls with centers in
A
:

P(A;r):=\max\left\{N\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\exists\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq A,B_{r}(x_{i})\cap B_{r}(x_{j})=\varnothing\ \forall i\neq j\right\}.

•

The
N
th
covering radius
of
A
is the minimal radius of a cover of
A
by
N
open balls:

\displaystyle r_{N}(A):=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{r>0\mid\exists\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq X,A\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{r}(x_{i})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{r>0\mid N(A;r)\leq N\}.

The covering radius can also be thought of as the
N
th quantization error of
A
of order
p=\infty
; see Graf and Luschgy
[
30
, Sect. 10]
.

Remark 2.8
.

For
A
compact, we have the inequalities
r_{N(A;r)}(A)\leq r
and
N(A;r_{N}(A))>N
for all
r>0
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The former inequality follows by definition. For the latter, note that there exists no
r_{N}(A)
-cover of
A
with
N
elements: given any
r
-cover
S:=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
A
with
N
elements, the compactness of
A
implies that
\sup_{x\in A}d(x,S)=\max_{x\in A}d(x,S)<r
since
A
is contained in the open
r
-neighborhood of
S
.

If
T\colon X\to Y
is a
L
-Lipschitz map between metric spaces with
A\subseteq X
, then
N(T(A);Lr)\leq N(A;r)
and
r_{N}(T(A))\leq Lr_{N}(A)
, since for any
r
-cover
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
A
,
\{T(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an
Lr
-cover of
T(A)
with at most
N
elements. Moreover, equality holds if
T\colon X\to Y
is a similarity transformation; i.e.,
d_{Y}(T(x),T(x^{\prime}))=Ld_{X}(x,x^{\prime})
.

The following basic inequalities show that packing and covering numbers grow at the same rate:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A
be a nonempty precompact subset of a complete metric space
X
. Then for any
r>0
,

N(A;2r)\leq P(A;r)\leq N(A;r).

Proof.

Note firstly that by the total boundedness of
A
,
N(A;r)<\infty
for any
r>0
.

We first prove the second inequality. Let
n:=N(A;r)
and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq X
be an
r
-cover of
A
.
Let
\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq A
be an arbitrary finite subset of
A
with
m>n
. Since
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an
r
-cover, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
y_{j}\in B_{r}(x_{i})
.

Then by the assumption that
m>n
, the pigeonhole principle implies the existence of
j\neq j^{\prime}
such that
y_{j},y_{j^{\prime}}\in B_{r}(x_{i})
. But in that case,
x_{i}\in B_{r}(y_{j})\cap B_{r}(y_{j^{\prime}})
, so
\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
cannot be an
r
-packing on
A
. Thus
P(A;r)\leq n
, in particular
P(A;r)<\infty
. This shows the second inequality.

Now setting
m:=P(A;r)\leq n<\infty
, by the well-ordering principle there exists an
r
-packing
\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq A
. Let
x\in A\setminus\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
. Then by maximality, the set
\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\cup\{x\}
cannot also be an
r
-packing, so there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
B_{r}(y_{j})\cap B_{r}(x)\neq\varnothing
. In that case, we have
d(x,y_{j})<2r
by the triangle inequality. This shows that
\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is a
2r
-cover of
A
, yielding the bound
P(A;r)=m\geq N(A;2r)
. This shows the first inequality.
∎

We will rely on the former inequality in order to bound covering numbers from above.
For reference, see e.g. Mattila
[
51
, §5.3]
; note however the additional factor of
2
arising from the different convention to define coverings and packings in terms of closed instead of open balls.

.

We also cite the following volumetric upper bound on covering/packing numbers:

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
\nu
a Borel measure on
X
.

Let
A
be a nonempty compact subset of
X
, and suppose there exist
\vartheta,d,r>0
such that

\nu(B_{r}(x))\geq\vartheta r^{d},\quad\text{for all }x\in A.

Then

N(A;2r)\leq P(A;r)\leq\vartheta^{-1}\nu(A^{r})r^{-d},

where
A^{r}=\{x\in X\mid d(x,A)<r\}
is the open
r
-neighborhood of
A
.

Proof.

Since
A
is compact,
P(A;r)\leq N(A;r)<\infty
.
Take a maximal
r
-packing
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
A
, with
N=P(A;r)\geq N(A;2r)
. Then the disjoint balls
(B_{r}(x_{i}))_{i=1}^{N}
are contained in
A^{r}
, hence

\nu(A^{r})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu(B_{r}(x_{i}))\geq N\vartheta r^{d}.

Reordering yields the desired inequality.
∎

In Section
4.1
, we will apply this basic volumetric bound in order to control the covering numbers of spheres in Riemannian manifolds.

3.
Proof of main results

We now prove the Pierce-type upper bound on the quantization error of measures on complete Riemannian manifolds, and directly deduce the generalization of Zador’s theorem for non-compactly supported measures.

3.1.
Proof of the upper bound

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We obtain the upper bound by reduction to Pierce’s lemma on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Take the distance function
d_{x_{0}}=d(\cdot,x_{0})\colon X\to[0,\infty)
and the pushforward measure
\mu_{1}:=(d_{x_{0}})_{\#}\mu
. We first quantize
\mu_{1}
with
k
radii
0<R_{1}<\ldots<R_{k}<\infty
, and then cover each sphere
\partial B_{R_{i}}(x_{0})
with
k^{d-1}
points in order to quantize
\mu
with
N:=k^{d}+1
points.

Notation
.

We will denote the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
simply by
B(R)
, the sphere
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
by
S(R)
, and the annulus
B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})
by
A[R_{1},R_{2})
(in particular,
A[0,R)=B(R)
and
A[R,\infty)=B(R)^{c}
).

Let
k\geq 1
. Choose
0=R_{0}<R_{1}<\ldots<R_{k}<R_{k+1}=\infty
arbitrary, so that
X
can be partitioned as

X=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{k}X_{i};\quad X_{i}:=A[R_{i},R_{i+1})=\{x\in X\mid R_{i}\leq d(x,x_{0})<R_{i+1}\}=d_{x_{0}}^{-1}([R_{i},R_{i+1})).

We can quantize
\mu
with
N:=k^{d}+1
points by sending
X_{0}
to a single point (which we will take to be
x_{0}
), and quantizing each
X_{i}
by
k^{d-1}
points (which will be a cover of
S(R_{i})
). By Lemma
2.2
, we have

V_{k^{d}+1,p}(\mu)\leq V_{1,p}^{(\mu)}(X_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{k^{d-1},p}^{(\mu)}(X_{i}).

We first cite the following simple lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x\in X\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and
0\leq R\leq d(x,x_{0})
.
Then
d(x,S(R))=d(x,x_{0})-R
.

Proof.

For any
y\in S(R)
, we have

d(x,x_{0})\leq d(x,y)+d(y,x_{0})=d(x,y)+R,

thus also
d(x,S(R))\geq d(x,x_{0})-R
.
Conversely, taking a constant-speed length-minimizing geodesic from
x_{0}
to
x
, the geodesic intersects
S(R)
at a unique point
z
, which satisfies

d(x,x_{0})=d(x,z)+d(z,x_{0})=d(x,z)+R,

hence
d(x,S(R))=d(x,z)=d(x,x_{0})-R
.
∎

This is the only place where we use the assumption of
X
being a geodesic space.
With this expression, we can bound the quantization cost of each annulus:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\leq R_{1}<R_{2}\leq\infty
. Then for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

V_{m,p}^{(\mu)}(A[R_{1},R_{2}))\leq\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}\left[(R-R_{1})+r_{m}(S(R_{1}))\right]^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R),

where by convention
\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}=\int_{[R_{1},R_{2})}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>r_{m}(S(R_{1}))
, and let
S
be a
\delta
-cover for
S(R)
with at most
m
elements. Then for each
x\in A[R_{1},R_{2})
,

d(x,A)\leq\inf_{y\in S(R_{1})}\left[d(x,y)+d(y,S)\right]\leq\inf_{y\in S(R_{1})}\left[d(x,y)+\delta\right]=d(x,S(R_{1}))+\delta.

Since
d(x,S(R_{1}))=d(x,x_{0})-R_{1}
by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle V_{m,p}^{(\mu)}(A[R_{1},R_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A[R_{1},R_{2})}d(x,S)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A[R_{1},R_{2})}\left[(d_{x_{0}}(x)-R_{1})+\delta\right]^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}\left[(R-R_{1})+\delta\right]^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R).

Taking the infimum of the right-hand side over all
\delta>r_{m}(S(R_{1}))
yields the statement.
∎

Note that for
R_{1}=0
,
S(R_{1})
is the singleton
\{x_{0}\}
and its covering radii are identically
0
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
to each annulus
X_{i}
, we then have

\displaystyle V_{1,p}^{(\mu)}(X_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R_{1}}R^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R);

\displaystyle V_{k^{d-1},p}^{(\mu)}(X_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{i+1}}\left[(R-R_{i})+r_{k^{d-1}}(S(R_{i}))\right]^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R),\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

Therefore, by the identity
(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(a^{p}+b^{p})
for
a,b\geq 0
,

\displaystyle k^{p}V_{k^{d}+1,p}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left[k^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{i+1}}(R-R_{i})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{i+1}}k^{p}r_{k^{d-1}}(S(R_{i}))^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left[k^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{i+1}}(R-R_{i})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)+\int_{0}^{\infty}f(R)^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)\right]

where, since
f
is nondecreasing,
Nr_{N^{d-1}}(S(R_{i}))\leq f(R_{i})\leq f(R)
for
R\in[R_{i},R_{i+1})
.

Finally, by Lemma
B.2
, the radii
(R_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
can be chosen such that

k^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{R_{i}}^{R_{i+1}}(R-R_{i})^{k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+R^{p+\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(R)

for a constant
C=C(p,\delta)
independent of
\mu
and

. This proves the non-asymptotic upper bound.

The upper bound on the limit supremum follows from the observation that, for any
N\geq 2
, taking

such that

, we have

N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)\leq N^{p/d}V_{k^{d}+1}(\mu)=\left(\frac{N}{k^{d}}\right)^{p/d}k^{p}V_{k^{d}+1}(\mu),

so taking the limit supremum and noting that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{N}{k(N)^{d}}=1
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}k^{p}V_{k^{d}+1,p}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq C\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\left(1+d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+f(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

This yields the last statement in Theorem
1.6
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of the asymptotic formula

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

The statement has already been proven by Kloeckner
[
43
]
and Iacobelli
[
38
, Thm. 1.4]
for measures of compact support. Hence for any
K\subseteq M
compact,

Q_{p,d}^{(\mu)}(K)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}^{(\mu)}(K)=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{K}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}}.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, by Ulam’s lemma there exists
K\subseteq M
compact such that

\int_{M\setminus K}\left[1+d(x,x_{0})^{p+\delta}+f(d(x,x_{0}))^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\varepsilon.

Consequently, by Theorem
1.6
,

\overline{Q}^{(\mu)}_{p,d}(M\setminus K)^{p}=\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}^{(\mu)}(M\setminus K)<C\varepsilon.

Lemma
2.6
then implies that, for
r=\frac{p+d}{d}
,

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)^{r}\leq{Q}^{(\mu)}_{p,d}(K)^{r}+(C\varepsilon)^{r/p}.

Taking the supremum of the right-hand side over all
K\subseteq M
compact,

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)^{r}\leq\sup_{K\subseteq M\text{ compact}}{Q}^{(\mu)}_{p,d}(K)^{r}+(C\varepsilon)^{r/p}.

Since this inequality holds for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\overline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)\leq\sup_{K\subseteq M\text{ compact}}{Q}^{(\mu)}_{p,d}(K).

However, by the monotonicity of the quantization error, the right-hand side is also a lower bound on
\underline{Q}_{p,d}(\mu)
. This concludes that
Q_{p,d}(\mu)
exists, and is given by

\displaystyle Q_{p,d}(\mu)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{p/d}V_{N,p}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup_{K\subseteq M\text{ compact}}Q_{p,d}^{(\mu)}(K)

\displaystyle=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\sup_{K\subseteq M\text{ compact}}\left(\int_{K}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}}

\displaystyle=Q_{p}([0,1]^{d})\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)^{\frac{d}{d+p}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d+p}{d}}.\qed

4.
Estimates on covering growth

We now present a few special cases in which the covering growth of manifolds can be estimated. We first formulate a general volumetric upper bound on covering numbers using Lemma
2.10
, and then apply this bound on the specific settings of lower bounded Ricci curvature and geometric group actions.

4.1.
General volumetric estimates

In order to bound the expression
N(A;r)r^{m}
over all
r>0
sufficiently small, we introduce the following definitions:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
\nu
a Borel measure on
X
.

For
A\subseteq X
compact and
m,r_{0}>0
, define

\displaystyle C_{m}(A;r_{0}):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<r\leq r_{0}\end{subarray}}N(A;r)r^{m};\quad\vartheta_{m}(A;r_{0}):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in A\\
0<r\leq r_{0}\end{subarray}}r^{-m}\nu(B_{r}(x)).

The quantity
\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})
can be thought of as the lower density of
A
with respect to the measure
\nu
, while
C_{d}(A;r_{0})
is a coarse quantification of the size of the set
A
. Indeed, as
r\to 0^{+}
, the quantity
N(A;r)r^{m}
becomes comparable to the
m
-dimensional
Minkowski content
of
A
; for more detail, refer to Appendix
A
. We will use
C_{m}(A;r_{0})
to control the covering growth of spheres with
A=\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
.

Example 4.2
.

The
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfies
{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(B_{r}(x))=\omega_{d}r^{d}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
, where
\omega_{d}:={\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(B_{1}(0))
is the volume of the unit
d
-dimensional ball.

Consequently, for the choice
\nu={\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})=\omega_{d}
independently of
A
and
r_{0}
.

With these definitions, we can formulate the following general upper bound on covering numbers:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a metric space,
\nu
a Borel measure on
X
,
A\subseteq X
compact. For each
d\geq m>0
and
r_{0}>0
, we have

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
r_{N}(A)\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}Nr_{N}(A)^{m}\leq C_{m}(A;2r_{0})\leq 2^{m}\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})^{-1}\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{\nu(A^{r})}{r^{d-m}}.

(4.1)

If
\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})=0
, the right-hand side is set to
\infty
and the bound is trivial.

Proof.

For the former inequality, observe that for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{N}(A)\leq 2r_{0}
,
N<N(A;r_{N}(A))
hence

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
r_{N}(A)\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}Nr_{N}(A)^{m}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
r_{N}(A)\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}N(A;r_{N}(A))r_{N}(A)^{m}\leq\sup_{0<r\leq 2r_{0}}N(A;r)r^{m}=:C_{m}(A;2r_{0}).

For the latter inequality, assume wlog
\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})>0
.
Let
0<r\leq 2r_{0}
. For
\vartheta=\vartheta_{d}(A;r_{0})
, the inequality
\nu(B_{r/2}(x))\geq\vartheta(\frac{r}{2})^{d}
holds for all
x\in A
, hence by Lemma
2.10
,

N(A;r)\leq P\left(A;\frac{r}{2}\right)\leq\vartheta^{-1}\nu(A^{r/2})\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-d}=2^{m}r^{-m}\vartheta^{-1}\frac{\nu(A^{r/2})}{(r/2)^{d-m}}.

Multiplying by
r^{m}
and taking the supremum over all
r\in(0,2r_{0}]
yields the latter inequality.
∎

We now restrict to geodesic spheres in order to bound the covering growth of manifolds or geodesic spaces.
Let
(M,g)
be a complete
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and fix
x_{0}\in M
. We apply Proposition
4.3
to
A=\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
with
m=d-1
and
\nu=\mathrm{vol}_{M}
.

For
A=\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, observe that
A^{r}\subseteq A[R-r,R+r)=B_{R+r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R-r}(x_{0})
, thus

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(A^{r})\leq\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))-\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R-r}(x_{0})).

For simplicity of notation, we define the following quantity:

P_{M}(R;r)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))-\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R-r}(x_{0}))}{2r},\quad 0<r\leq R<\infty.

This quantity is akin to an approximate Minkowski content for the sphere
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, and will converge to
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))
as
r\to 0^{+}
for almost every value of
R
.

Figure 4.
An
r
-packing on the sphere
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, contained in the annulus
B_{R+r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R-r}(x_{0})
.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.3
, for each
r_{0}>0
, we have

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq\vartheta_{d}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})^{-1}\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}P_{M}(R;r).

(4.2)

In particular, for
r_{0}=R/2
,
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq R=2r_{0}
thus

\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});R)\leq\vartheta_{d}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});R/2)^{-1}\sup_{0<r\leq R/2}P_{M}(R;r).

(4.3)

Up to taking
(d-1)
th roots, the right-hand side thus provides a general upper bound for the covering growth of
M
.
We will estimate the two quantities on the right-hand side separately in the various special cases we will be considering in this section.

Example 4.4

(Spheres in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
)
.

Take
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R}=\partial B_{R}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For
\nu={\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}
and
m=d-1
, Proposition
4.3
yields

C_{d-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R};2r_{0})\leq 2^{d-1}\vartheta_{d}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R};r_{0})^{-1}\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R})^{r})}{r}.

We have
\vartheta_{d}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R};r_{0})=\omega_{d}
by Example
4.2
, and since
({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R})^{r}=B_{R+r}(0)\setminus\bar{B}_{R-r}(0)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R})^{r})}{r}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{\omega_{d}(R+r)^{d}-\omega_{d}(R-r)^{d}}{r}

\displaystyle=2\omega_{d}\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{R-r}^{R+r}dt^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega_{d}d(R+r_{0})^{d-1},

where in the last inequality we apply the convexity of
t\mapsto t^{d}
.
Therefore

C_{d-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R};2r_{0})\leq 2^{d}d(R+r_{0})^{d-1}.

In particular, for all
r_{0}\leq R/2
, the right-hand side is uniformly bounded by a constant multiple of
R^{d-1}
.

Remark
.

Let
T\colon X\to Y
be an
L
-Lipschitz map between metric spaces,
A\subseteq X
compact.
Then since
N(T(A);Lr)\leq N(A;r)
for
T\colon X\to Y
an
L
-Lipschitz map, given
r_{0}>0
we have

C_{m}(T(A);Lr_{0})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<r\leq r_{0}\end{subarray}}N(T(A);Lr)(Lr)^{m}\leq L^{m}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<r\leq r_{0}\end{subarray}}N(A;r)r^{m}=L^{m}C_{m}(A;r_{0}).

Example 4.5

(
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
bounds covering numbers)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
, and let
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
be defined as in Theorem
1.2
. Then
\exp_{x_{0}}
, restricted to a map from
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R}=\partial B_{R}(0)\subseteq T_{v}M
to
\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})\subseteq M
, is
L
-Lipschitz with

L=\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R}}\|d_{v}\exp_{x_{0}}\|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R},\\
w\in T_{v}{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R},\\
\|w\|_{v}=1\end{subarray}}\left\|d_{v}\exp_{x_{0}}[w]\right\|_{\exp_{x_{0}}(v)}=\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)}{R}.

Consequently,

C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq L^{d-1}C_{d-1}\left({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R};\frac{2r_{0}}{L}\right).

Since
T_{v}M
, equipped with the norm induced by
g_{v}
, is isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d}dL^{d-1}\left(R+\frac{2r_{0}}{L}\right)^{d-1}

\displaystyle=2^{d}d\left(\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)}{R}\right)^{d-1}R^{d-1}\left(1+\frac{2r_{0}}{{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)}\right)^{d-1}

\displaystyle=2^{d}d{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)^{d-1}\left(1+\frac{2r_{0}}{{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)}\right)^{d-1}.

In particular, since
L\geq 1
(as long as
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
contains antipodal points) we have
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)\geq R
thus

C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq 2^{2d-1}d{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}(R)^{d-1}\quad\textup{for all }r_{0}\leq R/2.

Thus
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
always yields a viable upper bound on the covering growth of Riemannian manifolds. The following special case illustrates the difference between the two notions of growth:

Example 4.6

(Geodesic spheres for
d=2
)
.

Let
M
be a
2
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
. For a.e.
R>0
, the sphere
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
is a
1
-dimensional compact
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
submanifold of
M
(e.g. by the implicit function theorem), and is thus the finite disjoint union of closed rectifiable curves. In this case, we can control the covering radii of
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
in terms of its total length.

For simplicity, assume
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
is a closed rectifiable curve of length
L
, and take an arc length parametrization
s\colon[0,L]\to\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, which is a
1
-Lipschitz map. Note also that
r_{k}([0,L])\leq\frac{L}{2k}
(in fact, equality holds), covering
[0,L]
by the
k
points

x_{i}:=\frac{2i-1}{2k}L\in[0,L],\quad i=1,\ldots,k.

Compare
[
30
, Ex. 5.5]
.
Then for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

kr_{k}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))=kr_{k}(s([0,L]))\leq kr_{k}([0,L])=\frac{L}{2}.

The same upper bound will carry over when
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
is a finite union of rectifiable curves. Denoting the total length (equiv.
1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) of
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
by
L_{x_{0}}(R)
, this implies that
M
has
O(L_{x_{0}})
covering growth.

In light of this observation, one can construct examples of
2
-manifolds where
L_{x_{0}}
grows at a much slower rate than
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
, e.g. when the derivative of the exponential map is large on a negligible sliver of each sphere. Indeed, we should expect
L_{x_{0}}
to behave as the
L^{1}
norm of
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\exp_{x_{0}}
over
{\mathbb{S}}_{R}\subset T_{x_{0}}M
instead of the
L^{\infty}
norm. See Figure
5
for an illustration of a surface where
L_{x_{0}}
grows linearly but
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
at least exponentially with respect to the radius.

This correspondence between covering growth and the perimeters of spheres does not necessarily hold in higher dimensions, but it is always true that perimeters yield a
lower bound
on the covering growth of manifolds; we illustrate this in Appendix
A
.

Figure 5.

A parabolic
2
-dimensional surface on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with a fold along one direction. The surface is negatively curved along the fold, and the curvature can be made arbitrarily negative by sharpening the fold, causing
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\exp_{x_{0}}
to blow up along the same direction in the tangent space at
x_{0}
and making
{\mathcal{A}}_{x_{0}}
grow exponentially with
R
. However, the perimeters of spheres remain at most linearly proportional to the radius due to the overall parabolic growth of the surface.

4.2.
Lower bounded Ricci curvature

When the Ricci curvature of the manifold is bounded from below, both expressions in the right-hand side of (
4.2
) can be controlled in terms of the volumes of balls. This allows us to show, in particular, that complete Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature have
O(R)
covering growth.

We first recall the
Bishop-Gromov theorem
, which controls the volumes of balls under a lower bound on the Ricci curvature.

Notation
.

We denote by
M^{d}_{\kappa}
the
d
-dimensional complete, simply connected model Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

M^{d}_{\kappa}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\kappa}{\mathbb{S}}^{d},&\kappa>0;\\
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},&\kappa=0;\\
\sqrt{-\kappa}{\mathbb{H}}^{d},&\kappa<0.\end{matrix}\right.

The diameter of
M^{d}_{\kappa}
is

D_{\kappa}=\diam(M^{d}_{\kappa})=\left\{\begin{matrix}\pi/\sqrt{\kappa},&\kappa>0;\\
\infty,&\kappa\leq 0.\end{matrix}\right.

The volume of a ball of radius
R\leq D_{\kappa}
in
M^{d}_{\kappa}
is given by

\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(R):=d\omega_{d}\int_{0}^{R}\sin_{\kappa}^{d-1}(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

where
\omega_{d}
is the volume of the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and

\sin_{\kappa}(r):=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}r),&\kappa>0;\\
r,&\kappa=0;\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\kappa}}\sinh(\sqrt{-\kappa}r),&\kappa<0.\end{matrix}\right.

The function
\sin_{\kappa}
is exactly the solution to the
Jacobi equation

y^{\prime\prime}(r)+\kappa y(r)=0
with
y(0)=0
for constant sectional curvature
\kappa
.

Theorem 4.7

(Bishop-Gromov theorem
[
33
, §2.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded from below:
\mathrm{Ric}\geq(d-1)\kappa g
for some
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Then for any fixed
x\in M
, the function

r\mapsto\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(r)}

is nonincreasing, and tends to
1
as
r\to 0^{+}
. In other words, for
0<r\leq R\leq D_{\kappa}
,

\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R}(x))}\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(R)}=\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\sin_{\kappa}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{R}\sin_{\kappa}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}.

The Bishop-Gromov theorem thus provides simultaneous lower bounds on the volumes of small balls and upper bounds on those of large balls. This will allow us to estimate both quantities
\vartheta_{d}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})
and
P_{M}(R;r)
in (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.8

(Bound on
\vartheta_{d}
)
.

Suppose
\mathrm{Ric}\geq(d-1)\kappa g
on
M
, for some
\kappa\leq 0
.

Fix
x_{0}\in M
and
R\geq r_{0}>0
. Then for any
r_{1}\geq 0
,

\vartheta_{d}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})\geq\omega_{d}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}+r_{1}}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r_{0}+r_{1}})}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})
,
0<r\leq r_{0}
. Then by Bishop-Gromov,

\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(r)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}+r_{1}}(x))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r_{0}+r_{1}})}\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}+r_{1}}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r_{0}+r_{1}})},

where
B_{r_{0}+r_{1}}(x_{0})\subseteq B_{R+r_{0}+r_{1}}(x)
since
d(x,x_{0})=R
. Thus from the fact that
\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(r)\geq\omega_{d}r^{d}
for
\kappa\leq 0
(again by Bishop-Gromov), we obtain that

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}+r_{1}}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r_{0}+r_{1}})}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(r)\geq\omega_{d}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}+r_{1}}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r_{0}+r_{1}})}r^{d}.\qed

Volume comparison argument for bound on
\vartheta_{d}
.

Lemma 4.9

(Bound on
P_{M}
)
.

Suppose
\mathrm{Ric}\geq(d-1)\kappa g
,
\kappa\leq 0
.

Fix
x_{0}\in M
. Then for all
0<r<R<\infty
,

P_{M}(R;r)\leq\frac{P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0})),

where

P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})-\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R-r})}{2r}=d\omega_{d}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{R-r}^{R+r}\sin_{\kappa}^{d-1}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq d\omega_{d}\sin_{\kappa}^{d-1}(R+r).

Proof.

By Bishop-Gromov, we have

\displaystyle P_{M}(R;r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r}\left[\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})-\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R-r}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R-r})}V^{d}_{\kappa}({R-r})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2r}\left[\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})-\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R-r})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r).\qed

We bound the ratio
\frac{P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)}{V^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}
in Lemma
B.3
, from which we can deduce the following:

Lemma 4.10
.

There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that, for each
\kappa\leq 0
and
R\geq 2r_{0}>0
,

\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\leq\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r_{0})}{R}.

In particular, as
R\to\infty
, the right-hand side will approach a finite positive value for
\kappa<0
and will converge to
0
for
\kappa=0
. We note also that for
\kappa=0
, the constant
C_{1}
can be taken to equal
d
.

Putting the above bounds together, we obtain

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq(d-1)\kappa g
,
\kappa\leq 0
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
R\geq 2r_{0}>0
. Then for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq 2r_{0}
,

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq C\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r_{0})}{R}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({2R+r_{0}}),

where
C,C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants independent of
x_{0}
,
R
and
r_{0}
.

In particular, taking
r_{0}=R/2
, we have that

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C\frac{C_{1}+\frac{3C_{2}}{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}R}{R}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}\left(\frac{5R}{2}\right),\quad\textup{for all }N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq 2r_{0}
.
Applying Proposition
4.3
with
\vartheta=\vartheta_{d}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})
, we have

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq 2^{d}\vartheta^{-1}\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}P_{M}(R;r).

By Lemmas
4.9
and
4.10
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}P_{M}(R;r)\leq\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}\frac{P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({R+r})}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))\leq\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0}))\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r_{0})}{R}.

Applying Lemma
4.8
with the choice
r_{1}=R
also yields

\vartheta^{-1}\leq\omega_{d}^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({2R+r_{0}})}{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0}))}.

We therefore have

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq 2^{d}\omega_{d}^{-1}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}({2R+r_{0}})\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r_{0})}{R}.\qed

In particular, we deduce that every complete connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature has
O(R)
covering growth:

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional complete connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature.
Then
M
has uniform
O(R)
covering growth.

More specifically, for each
x_{0}\in M
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R>0
, the following bound holds:

N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq(5^{d}d)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}R.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
R>0
.
Then applying Proposition
4.11
with
\kappa=0
and
r_{0}:=R/2\geq r_{1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))/2
, for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d}\omega_{d}^{-1}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{0}(B_{5R/2})\sup_{0<r\leq R/2}\frac{d}{R+r}

\displaystyle=2^{d}\omega_{d}^{-1}\left(\omega_{d}(5R/2)^{d}\right)\frac{d}{R}=5^{d}dR^{d-1}.\qed

The constant on the right-hand side can be made more precise when
\vartheta_{d}
admits a universal lower bound, in which case Lemma
4.8
need not be applied. This can be thought of as an assumption of
at least Euclidean volume growth
for balls on
M
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq(d-1)\kappa g
,
\kappa\leq 0
.
Assume moreover that there exists
\vartheta>0
such that
\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\geq\vartheta r^{d}
for all
r>0
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
R\geq 2r_{0}>0
. Then for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq 2r_{0}
,

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq 2^{d}\vartheta^{-1}\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r_{0})}{R}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0})),

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are as in Lemma
B.3
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature.
Assume moreover that there exists
\vartheta>0
such that
\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\geq\vartheta r^{d}
for all
r>0
.

Then for each
x_{0}\in M
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R>0
, the following bound holds:

N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq\left(\frac{3^{d}d\omega_{d}}{\vartheta}\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}R.

Proof.

Set
r_{0}:=R/2
. Then for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq R=2r_{0}
, thus

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});2r_{0})\leq 2^{d}\vartheta^{-1}\frac{d}{R}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0})).

By the assumption of nonnegative Ricci curvature,
\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0}))=\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{3R/2}(x_{0}))\leq\omega_{d}(3R/2)^{d}
, thus

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq 3^{d}\vartheta^{-1}d\omega_{d}R^{d-1}.\qed

The constants on the right-hand side may be reduced further if
N
is taken to be larger, hence
r_{0}
is taken to be close to
0
, but the bounds given above are restricted by the fact that they hold for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

For example, on
M={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\partial B_{R}(0)={\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{R}
intersects the axes at
2^{d}
points, which are separated by at least a distance of
\sqrt{2}R
. This implies that

N(\partial B_{R}(0);\sqrt{2}R)\geq P(\partial B_{R}(0);\sqrt{2}R)\geq 2^{d},

thus for
N=2^{d}-1
,
r_{N}(\partial B_{R}(0))\geq\sqrt{2}R
and

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(0))^{d-1}\geq(2^{d}-1)2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}R^{d-1}.

4.3.
Geometric group actions

In order to further demonstrate the applicability of the covering growth upper bound, we consider Riemannian manifolds subject to the
geometric action
of a discrete group of isometries.
In particular, when the group of isometries is of polynomial growth, as in the case of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
equipped with a periodic metric, we show that a polynomial moment condition is sufficient even though the lower bound on the Ricci curvature might be arbitrarily negative. For reference on geometric group theory, we refer to Bridson and Haefliger
[
16
]
.

Definition 4.15
.

A group action
\Gamma\times X\to X
of a group
\Gamma
on a locally compact metric space
X
is said to be a
geometric
group action if

•

each
\gamma\in\Gamma
acts on
X
by an isometry;

•

(
proper discontinuity
) for each
K\subseteq X
compact, the set
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid\gamma\cdot K\cap K\neq\varnothing\}
is finite;

•

(
cocompactness
) there exists
K\subseteq X
compact such that
\Gamma\cdot K:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\cdot K=X
.

Figure 7.
A sphere covered by translates of a compact set
K
along a geometric group action.

Let
M
be a complete, connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold, on which a discrete group
\Gamma
acts geometrically. We seek to control the covering numbers of spheres in terms of the growth of
\Gamma
. In particular, we will prove the following statement:

Proposition 4.16
.

Suppose
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a complete
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
. Assume further that
\Gamma
is of
polynomial growth
: for any choice of generating set
{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Gamma
, there exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that the
growth function

\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k)\leq Ck^{\alpha}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
large.

Then fixing
x_{0}\in M
, for
R>0
sufficiently large and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
arbitrary, the following bound holds for the covering growth of spheres in
M
:

Nr_{N}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))^{d-1}\leq C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq CR^{\alpha+d-1}.

Thus
M
has
O(R^{\frac{\alpha}{d-1}+1})
covering growth around any point
x_{0}\in M
.

This bound more properly illustrates the coarse or large-scale nature of covering growth, as opposed to the growth of the exponential map. As with the material in the previous section, this estimate can also be extended directly to non-smooth metric measure spaces. Similar estimates can also be obtained for other bounds on the growth of the group, such as sub-exponential growth, using the same arguments given below.

Example 4.17

(Periodic metrics on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
)
.

Let
M=({\mathbb{R}}^{d},g)
, where
g
is a
1
-periodic metric tensor on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. That is, for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
g_{x+a}=g_{x}
(more rigorously, the metric tensor
g
is preserved under pullback along each translation
x\mapsto x+a
).

Then the abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
acts geometrically on
M
by translations
x\mapsto x+a
, with
K=[0,1]^{d}
compact in
M
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\cdot K=M
, and is of polynomial growth of order
d
. Thus any such manifold with a periodic metric tensor, however negatively curved they may be at points, will still have at most
O(R^{\frac{d}{d-1}+1})
covering growth.

A simple example of a manifold of this type is a sinusoidal surface as illustrated in Figure
8
, given by the graph of a function of the form
f(x,y)=A\sin(\omega_{x}x+\phi_{x})\sin(\omega_{y}y+\phi_{y})
. Observe the saddle points occurring periodically on the surface. The larger the amplitude
A
of the sinusoid, the more negatively curved the surface will be at the saddle points. Nevertheless, Proposition
4.16
guarantees polynomial covering growth independently of the curvature lower bound.

Figure 8.

Sinusoidal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with one saddle point highlighted in black.

We now proceed with the proof of Proposition
4.16
.
We first recall some basic background on the growth of groups:

Definition 4.18
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. Given a finite generating set
{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Gamma
, the
word length
of
\gamma\in\Gamma
is the length of the shortest representation of
\gamma
as a product of elements of
{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}
:

|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}:=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\exists\alpha_{0}=e,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{A}}^{-1},\gamma=\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{k}\}

where we set
|e|_{\mathcal{A}}:=0
for the identity element
e\in\Gamma
. The
word metric
induced by
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

d_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma,\eta):=|\gamma^{-1}\eta|_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\gamma,\eta\in\Gamma.

The
growth function
of
\Gamma
induced by
{\mathcal{A}}
measures the cardinality of closed balls centered at
e
with respect to the word metric:

\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k):=\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq k\}=\#\left[({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\cup\{e\})^{k}\right].

If there exist constants
C,\alpha,k_{0}>0
such that
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k)\leq Ck^{\alpha}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
, we say that
\Gamma
is of
polynomial growth
of order
\alpha
.

The word metric indeed defines a metric on
\Gamma
, and any two word metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent: given finite generating sets
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
, setting
\lambda:=\max_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}|\alpha|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}
, we have

d_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}(\gamma,\eta)\leq\lambda d_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma,\eta),\quad\textup{for all }\gamma,\eta\in\Gamma.

In particular,
|\gamma|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}\leq\lambda|\gamma|_{{\mathcal{A}}}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, hence
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k)\leq\beta_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}(\lambda k)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The property of polynomial growth is thus independent of the choice of generating set
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Example 4.19
.

The word metric on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with respect to the canonical generators
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
is exactly the metric induced by the
\ell^{1}
norm.

We also recall the definition of
quasi-isometries
between metric spaces:

Definition 4.20
.

Let
X
,
Y
be metric spaces. A (not necessarily continuous) map
\phi\colon X\to Y
is called a
(\lambda,\varepsilon)
-quasi-isometric embedding
for
\lambda\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
if the following inequalities hold for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
:

\lambda^{-1}d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})-\varepsilon\leq d_{Y}(\phi(x),\phi(x^{\prime}))\leq\lambda d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

If moreover
\sup_{y\in Y}d(y,\phi(X))<\infty
,
\phi
is called a
(\lambda,\varepsilon)
-quasi-isometry
and
X
and
Y
are said to be
quasi-isometric
.

Quasi-isometries can be said to capture the coarse or large-scale geometry of metric spaces, as opposed to the local structure.
The following fundamental result provides a correspondence between the growth of groups and the metric spaces they act on:

Proposition 4.21

(Schwarz-Milnor Lemma)
.

Suppose a group
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a locally compact length space
X
. Then
\Gamma
is finitely generated, and for any choice of word metric
d_{\mathcal{A}}
on
\Gamma
and base point
x_{0}\in X
, the map
\phi\colon\Gamma\to X
given by
\phi(\gamma):=\gamma\cdot x_{0}
is a quasi-isometry.

For a proof and further background, we refer to Bridson and Haefliger
[
16
, Prop. 8.19]
. We use this result to control the sizes of balls and annuli around
x_{0}
:

Notation
.

We denote by
N_{\Gamma}(A;B)
the minimum size of a cover of a set
A\subseteq X
by images of another set
B\subseteq X
along the action of
\Gamma
:

N_{\Gamma}(A;B):=\min\{|{\mathcal{G}}|\mid{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq\Gamma,A\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}\cdot B\}.

Lemma 4.22
.

Suppose
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a proper length space
X
. Fix
x_{0}\in X
and a generating set
{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Gamma
. Then there exist
\lambda\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
\delta>0
such that for any
R>0
and
r_{0}>\delta
,

N_{\Gamma}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\leq\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon)\rfloor\right).

Moreover, for any
R_{2}>R_{1}\geq r_{0}+\varepsilon
,

N_{\Gamma}(\bar{B}_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0});\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\leq\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda(R_{2}+r_{0}+\varepsilon)\rfloor\right)-\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda^{-1}(R_{1}-r_{0}-\varepsilon)\rfloor\right).

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon\Gamma\to X
be the quasi-isometry given by the Schwarz-Milnor lemma, with constants
\lambda\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Set moreover
\delta:=\sup_{x\in X}d(x,\phi(\Gamma))<\infty
.

Let
r_{0}>\delta
.
Then the closed ball
B_{0}=\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
is compact and its images
\gamma\cdot B_{0}=\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(\gamma\cdot x_{0})
(noting that
\Gamma
acts by isometries) cover
X
, since for any
x\in X
, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
d(x,\gamma\cdot x_{0})<\frac{r_{0}}{\delta}d(x,\phi(\Gamma))\leq r_{0}
.

Consequently, for any (relatively) compact subset
A\subseteq X
,
N_{\Gamma}(A;B_{0})
is finite and we have the trivial bound

N_{\Gamma}(A;B_{0})\leq\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\cap A\neq\emptyset\}.

We bound the right-hand side separately for
A=\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})
and
A=\bar{B}_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0})
.

Let
R>0
, and let
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that there exists
x\in\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})\cap\gamma\cdot B_{0}
. Since
\phi
is a
(\lambda,\varepsilon)
-quasi-isometry,

\lambda^{-1}d_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma,e)-\varepsilon\leq d(\gamma\cdot x_{0},x_{0})\leq d(\gamma\cdot x_{0},x)+d(x,x_{0})\leq r_{0}+R.

Thus we must have

|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}=d_{\mathcal{A}}(e,\gamma)\leq\lambda(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon).

Noting that
|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}
is always integer-valued, setting
k:=\lfloor\lambda(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon)\rfloor
we then obtain

\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\cap\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})\neq\emptyset\}\leq\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq k\}=\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k).

This yields the first inequality.

For the second inequality, observe that

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\cap(\bar{B}_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0}))\neq\emptyset\}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\cap\bar{B}_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\neq\emptyset\}-\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\subseteq\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0})\}.

(4.5)

The first term can be upper bounded as above. Now set
k_{1}:=\lfloor\lambda^{-1}(R_{1}-r_{0}-\varepsilon)\rfloor
and take
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq k_{1}
. Given any
x\in\gamma\cdot B_{0}=\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(\gamma\cdot x_{0})
, again by the quasi-isometricity of
\phi
,

d(\gamma\cdot x_{0},x_{0})\leq\lambda|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}+\varepsilon\leq R_{1}-r_{0},

and by the triangle inequality,

d(x,x_{0})\leq d(x,\gamma\cdot x_{0})+d(\gamma\cdot x_{0},x_{0})\leq R_{1}.

Therefore
x\in\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0})
. This shows that
|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq k_{1}
implies
\gamma\cdot B_{0}\subseteq\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0})
, in particular

\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid(\gamma\cdot B_{0})\subseteq\bar{B}_{R_{1}}(x_{0})\}\geq\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq k_{1}\}=\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1}).

Applying this to (
4.4
) yields the second inequality.
∎

We now apply this bound, in combination with (
4.2
), in order to control the covering growth of geodesic spheres on Riemannian manifolds. Henceforth we assume
M
to be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold on which a group
G
acts geometrically.

Fixing
x_{0}\in M
and
r_{0}>\delta
, we bound
N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}
by considering the cases
r<r_{0}
and
r\geq r_{0}
separately.

For
r<r_{0}
, we again apply Proposition
4.3
. The estimate given in Lemma
4.22
is too imprecise to bound the approximate perimeters of balls directly, so we will instead proceed as in the previous section by bounding
P_{M}(R;r)\leq C\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r}(x_{0}))
.

Lemma 4.23
.

Fix
r_{0}>0
.
There exists
\lambda>0
dependent on
r_{0}
such that

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\geq\lambda r^{d},\quad\textup{for all }x\in M,r\leq r_{0}.

Consequently,
\lambda_{d}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})\geq\lambda
independently of
R>0
.

Proof.

Take
K\subseteq M
compact such that
\Gamma\cdot K=M
. By Lemma
A.3
, there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\geq\lambda r^{d},\quad\textup{for all }x\in K,r\leq r_{0}.

Now suppose
x\in M
and
r\leq r_{0}
. Take
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma\cdot x\in K
, hence

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))=\mathrm{vol}_{M}(\gamma\cdot B_{r}(x))=\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(\gamma\cdot x))\geq\lambda r^{d}

since
\Gamma
acts on
M
by isometries.
∎

Lemma 4.24
.

There exists a constant
C
dependent on
M
,
x_{0}
and
r_{0}
such that, for any
0<r\leq r_{0}\leq R/2<\infty
,

P_{M}(R;r)\leq C\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0})).

Proof.

Take
K\subseteq M
compact such that
\Gamma\cdot K=M
. Since the Ricci curvature tensor is bounded on
K
, and preserved under the isometric action of each
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
M
has bounded Ricci curvature. The result then follows from Lemmas
4.9
and
4.10
.
∎

Again if
M
has nonnegative Ricci curvature, one can further divide the right-hand side by
R
, but we ignore this distinction here since the case of nonnegative Ricci curvature has already been treated in the previous section.

Lemma 4.25

(
r<r_{0}
)
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
\lambda
,
\varepsilon
and
r_{0}
as above. Suppose
\Gamma
is of polynomial growth with exponent
\alpha>0
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for
R>r_{0}
,

C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})=\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}\leq CR^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Proposition
4.3
and Lemmas
4.23
and
4.24
yield

C_{d-1}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})\leq C\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0})).

Then since
\Gamma
acts by isometries and in particular preserves volume, we can apply the first inequality in Lemma
4.22
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))N_{\Gamma}(B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0});B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon)\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon)^{\alpha}

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending on
x_{0}
,
r_{0}
,
\varepsilon
and the growth rate of
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}
, but not on
R
. Note lastly that for
R>r_{0}
,
\frac{R+r_{0}+\varepsilon}{R}<\frac{r_{0}+\varepsilon}{r_{0}}
which is likewise a constant.
∎

For
r\geq r_{0}
, we can apply the second inequality in Lemma
4.22
, but it does not affect the asymptotic behavior of the bound obtained unless
\lambda=1
and
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}
is strictly polynomial in the sense that
\frac{\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k)}{k^{\alpha}}
converges to some
C\in(0,\infty)
. We nevertheless include the general bound before restricting to polynomial growth:

Lemma 4.26

(
r\geq r_{0}
)
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
,
{\mathcal{A}}
,
\lambda
,
\varepsilon
and
r_{0}
as above.
Given
R>r_{0}
, the following bound holds:

\sup_{r_{0}\leq r\leq R}N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}\leq CR^{d-1}\left[\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda(R+r_{0}+\varepsilon)\rfloor\right)-\beta_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lfloor\lambda^{-1}(R_{1}-r_{0}-\varepsilon)\rfloor\right)\right]

In particular, if
\Gamma
is of polynomial growth of order
\alpha>0
,

\sup_{r_{0}\leq r\leq R}N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}\leq CR^{\alpha+d-1}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows simply by

N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}\leq N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r_{0})R^{d-1}\leq N_{G}(\bar{B}_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\bar{B}_{R-r_{0}}(x_{0});\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))R^{d-1}.

The second inequality then follows by substituting
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}(k)\leq Ck^{\alpha}
.
∎

Remark
.

One could also partition into cases
kr_{0}\leq r\leq(k+1)r_{0}
, and bound

\sup_{kr_{0}\leq r\leq(k+1)r_{0}}N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});r)r^{d-1}\leq N(\partial B_{R}(x_{0});kr_{0})\left((k+1)r_{0}\right)^{d-1},

but this does not improve the exponent
R^{\alpha+d-1}
: Lemma
4.22
bounds the first term on the right-hand side by an expression on the order of
(R+kr_{0})^{\alpha}
, and

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
kr_{0}\leq R\end{subarray}}(R+kr_{0})^{\alpha}(k+1)^{d-1}\geq CR^{\alpha+d-1},

as can be observed by taking
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
R\approx 2kr_{0}
.

These two lemmas together prove Proposition
4.16
.

Remark
.

The exponent
\frac{\alpha}{d-1}+1
in Proposition
4.16
is not sharp in general; this is because the notion of quasi-isometry is still too coarse to capture the sizes of spheres precisely. The
+1
term, which arises only in the
r\geq r_{0}
case, could be eliminated if
\beta_{\mathcal{A}}
is strictly polynomial and the quasi-isometry constant
\lambda
associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
is equal to
1
.
However, the term
\frac{\alpha}{d-1}
that also appears in Lemma
4.25
cannot be improved further using word metrics.

To illustrate this, consider the abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
acting geometrically on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by translations, as a special case of Example
4.17
.
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is of polynomial growth with exponent
\alpha=d
, so Proposition
4.16
implies
O(R^{\frac{d}{d-1}+1})
covering growth, but
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is simply of
O(R)
covering growth.

The inaccuracy here arises from Lemma
4.22
: while we expect annuli of the form
A:=B_{R+r_{0}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R-r_{0}}(x_{0})
to be covered by
CR^{d-1}
cubes for
R\gg r_{0}
, the upper bound on
N_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(A;B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))
is obtained by sandwiching
A
between balls of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with respect to the word metric, which for the canonical basis is the
\ell^{1}
norm. But even as we let
r_{0}\to 0
, the
\ell^{1}
annulus that contains the
\ell^{2}
sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
will have nonzero volume which will scale on the order of
R^{d}
. This prevents
\alpha
from being replaced with
\alpha-1
in Lemma
4.26
.

In such specific examples, possibly also for periodic metrics in general, one could estimate
N_{\Gamma}(A;B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))
or
N_{\Gamma}(A;K)
more directly, which could lead to a more precise exponent in Lemma
4.26
.

Appendix A
Relation between covering growth and Minkowski contents of spheres

The supremum on the right-hand side of equation (
4.1
) can be thought of as an approximate version of the upper perimeters or
Minkowski contents
of sets:

Definition A.1
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
A\subseteq M
compact. Let
m\leq d
be a natural number. The
m
-dimensional Minkowski content of
A
is given by

{\mathcal{M}}^{m}(A)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(A^{r})}{\omega_{d-m}r^{d-m}},

where the upper resp. lower limits are denoted by
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
resp.
\underline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

For
m
-rectifiable subsets
A\subseteq M
,
{\mathcal{M}}^{m}(A)
is known to exist and coincide with the
m
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Moreover, for geodesic spheres, the Minkowski content of the sphere
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
can also be obtained as the derivative of
\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
with respect to
R
. For reference on Minkowski contents and perimeters in the more general setting of metric measure spaces, see Ambrosio, Marino and Gigli
[
1
]
.

For reference, we mention that on Riemannian manifolds with
\nu=\mathrm{vol}_{M}
, the quantity
N(A;r)r^{m}
becomes comparable to
{\mathcal{M}}^{m}(A)
as
r\to 0^{+}
:

Proposition A.2
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
A\subseteq M
compact,
m\leq d
.

Then there exists
C>0
dependent only on
d
and
m
such that

C^{-1}\underline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{m}(A)\leq\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}N(A;r)r^{m}\leq\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}N(A;r)r^{m}\leq C\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{m}(A).

Lemma A.3
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Then

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}=1

uniformly on compact subsets of
M
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq M
be compact. For each
\varepsilon>0
, cover
A
with finitely many normal coordinate charts
\{(U_{i},\phi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, small enough so that each chart
\phi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\phi_{i}(U_{i})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz.

By the Lebesgue number lemma, there exists
\delta>0
such that any ball of radius
r\leq\delta
with center in
A
is contained in a single chart
U_{i}
.
Let
r\leq\delta
,
x\in A
, and suppose
B_{r}(x)\subseteq U_{i}
. Then

B_{(1-\varepsilon)r}(\phi_{i}(x))\subseteq\phi_{i}(B_{r}(x))\subseteq B_{(1+\varepsilon)r}(\phi_{i}(x))

so that

\omega_{d}(1-\varepsilon)^{d}r^{d}\leq{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(\phi_{i}(B_{r}(x)))\subseteq\omega_{d}(1+\varepsilon)^{d}r^{d}.

Moreover, since the volume form on
M
coincides with the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, the fact that
\phi_{i}
is
(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz implies

(1-\varepsilon)^{d}{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(\phi_{i}(B_{r}(x)))\leq\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{d}{\mbox{\script L}\,}^{d}(\phi_{i}(B_{r}(x))).

Therefore

(1-\varepsilon)^{2d}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2d},

and this inequality holds for all
x\in A
and all
0<r\leq\delta
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
A.2
.

First note that by Proposition
4.3
,

\sup_{r^{\prime}\leq 2r_{0}}N(A;r)r^{m}\leq\lambda_{d}(A;r_{0})^{-1}\sup_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(A^{r})}{r^{d-m}}.

Letting
r_{0}\to 0^{+}
and noting that
\lambda_{d}(A;r_{0})\to\omega_{d}
by Lemma
A.3
yields the latter inequality.

For the former inequality, fix
r_{0}>0
, and note that since
A^{\prime}:=\overline{A^{r_{0}}}
is compact,

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}=1

uniformly on
A^{\prime}
.
For
r\leq r_{0}
arbitrary, let
S
be an
r
-cover of
A
with
\#S=N(A;r)
. Then
S\subseteq A^{r_{0}}
by the minimality of
S
, and
A^{r}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in S}B_{2r}(x)
so that

\mathrm{vol}_{M}(A^{r})\leq\sum_{x\in S}\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{2r}(x))\leq N(A;r)\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in A^{\prime}\\
r^{\prime}\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r^{\prime}}(x))}{\omega_{d}r^{\prime d}}\omega_{d}(2r)^{d}.

Rearranging and taking the infimum over all
r\leq r_{0}
,

\inf_{r\leq r_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(A^{r})}{\omega_{d-m}r^{d-m}}\leq\frac{2^{d}\omega_{d}}{\omega_{d-m}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in A^{\prime}\\
r^{\prime}\leq 2r_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{M}(B_{r^{\prime}}(x))}{\omega_{d}r^{\prime d}}\inf_{r\leq r_{0}}N(A;r)r^{m}.

Letting
r_{0}\to 0^{+}
then yields the former inequality.
∎

Appendix B
Auxiliary lemmas

B.1.
Pierce’s lemma for floor quantization.

In the proof of Theorem
1.6
, radii
r\in[0,\infty)
are not assigned to the closest quantizer but to the largest quantizer below them; i.e., each point
r\in[r_{i},r_{i+1})
is mapped to
r_{i}
. In that case, the Pierce upper bound on the quantization error needs to be proven separately. We follow the random quantizer argument in Graf and Luschgy
[
30
, Lem. 6.6]
.

Definition B.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
[0,\infty)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Given a set of quantizers
S=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq(0,\infty)
, indexed in ascending order, the induced
floor quantizer
on
[0,\infty)
assigns each
r\in[0,\infty)
to the greatest element of
S
bounded by
r
:

F_{S}(r):=\max(\{0\}\cup S\cap[0,r])=\left\{\begin{matrix}0,&r<r_{1};\\
r_{i},&r_{i}\leq r<r_{i+1},\\
r_{N},&r_{N}\leq r.\end{matrix}\right.\quad i=1,\ldots,N-1;

The
floor quantization cost
of
\mu
of order
p
with respect to
S
is given by

V^{F}_{p}(\mu;S):=\int_{0}^{\infty}|r-F_{S}(r)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\int_{r_{i}}^{r_{i+1}}(r-r_{i})^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(r),

where by convention
r_{0}=0
,
r_{N+1}=\infty
, and
\int_{r_{i}}^{r_{i+1}}=\int_{[r_{i},r_{i+1})}
.

The
floor quantization cost
of
\mu
of order
p
with cardinality
N
is given by

V^{F}_{N,p}(\mu):=\inf_{\#S\leq N}V^{F}_{p}(\mu;S).

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\mu
be a finite measure on
[0,\infty)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\delta>0
.
Then for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

N^{p}V^{F}_{N,p}(\mu)\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+r^{p+\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(r),

where
C=C(p,\delta)>0
is a constant independent of
\mu
.

Proof.

Set
\beta:=\delta/p
, and suppose the quantizers
S=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are generated independently according to the same Pareto distribution with cumulative distribution function

G(y):=\left\{\begin{matrix}1-(y+1)^{-\beta},&y\geq 0;\\
0,&y\leq 0.\end{matrix}\right.

Then by Tonelli’s theorem, we can bound the quantization cost as follows:

\displaystyle V^{F}_{N,p}(\mu)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|r-F_{S}(r)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}|r-F_{S}(r)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(r),

where the expectation corresponds to integration over
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}G(r_{1})\ \ldots\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}G(r_{N})
.

Given
r,t\in[0,\infty)
,
|r-F_{S}(r)|=r-F_{S}(r)>t
implies that
r>t
and that for each
i=1,\ldots,N
, either
r<r_{i}
or
r>r_{i}+t
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|r-F_{S}(r)|>t)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbbm{1}}(r>t)\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left({\mathbb{P}}(r_{i}>r)+{\mathbb{P}}(r_{i}<r-t)\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}(r>t)\left(1-G(r)+G(r-t)\right)^{N}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}(r>t)\left(1+(r+1)^{-\beta}-(r-t+1)^{-\beta}\right)^{N}.

Considering
f(t):=(r-t+1)^{-\beta}
, we have
f^{\prime}(t)=\beta(r-t+1)^{-\beta-1}\geq\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}
hence

(r-t+1)^{-\beta}-(r+1)^{-\beta}=f(t)-f(0)\geq\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}t.

Consequently, for
0<t<r
,

\left(1+(r+1)^{-\beta}-(r-t+1)^{-\beta}\right)^{N}\leq\left(1-\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}t\right)^{N}\leq\exp(-N\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}t).

Hence for
r\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|r-F_{S}(r)|^{p}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}{\mathbb{P}}(|r-F_{S}(r)|>t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq p\int_{0}^{r}t^{p-1}\exp(-N\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

By the identity
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-at}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\Gamma(p)a^{-p}
for
a>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|r-F_{S}(r)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\exp(-N\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=p\Gamma(p)\left(N\beta(r+1)^{-\beta-1}\right)^{-p}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(p+1)p^{p+1}}{\delta^{p}}N^{-p}(r+1)^{p+\delta}\leq CN^{-p}(r^{p+\delta}+1)

for
C:=2^{p+\delta-1}\Gamma(p+1)p^{p+1}\delta^{-p}
.
Integrating with respect to
r
yields the statement.
∎

B.2.
Approximate perimeters in manifolds of constant curvature.

For the prototypical manifolds of constant sectional curvature
\kappa\leq 0
, the volumes of geodesic balls and spheres satisfy the following bound:

Lemma B.3
.

Let
\kappa\leq 0
. There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 0
such that, for any
R\geq 2r>0
,

\frac{P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{R+r})}\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R+r})}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{R+r})}\leq\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)}{R+r}.

Proof.

Note firstly that, since
\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R})
is increasing with respect to
R
for
\kappa\leq 0
,
P^{d}_{\kappa}(R;r)\leq\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R+r})
. For
\kappa=0
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R+r})}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{R+r})}=\frac{d\omega_{d}(R+r)^{d-1}}{\omega_{d}(R+r)^{d}}=\frac{d}{R+r}.

Now suppose
\kappa=-1
. Fix
r_{0}>0
. We investigate the cases
R\leq 2r_{0}
and
R\geq 2r_{0}
separately. Suppose
R\leq 2r_{0}
, and let
r\leq R/2
so that
R+r\leq 3r_{0}
. Then

P^{d}_{-1}(R;r)\leq\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(\partial B_{R+r})=d\omega_{d}\sinh^{d-1}(R+r)

and since
\sinh^{\prime}=\cosh
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
,

\sinh(R+r)\leq\sinh(0)+(R+r)\cosh(R+r)\leq(R+r)\cosh(3r_{0})

so

P^{d}_{-1}(R;r)\leq d\omega_{d}\cosh^{d-1}(3r_{0})(R+r)^{d-1},

while
\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{R+r})\geq\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{0}(B_{R+r})=\omega_{d}(R+r)^{d}
, so

\frac{P^{d}_{-1}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{R+r})}\leq\frac{d\cosh^{d-1}(3r_{0})}{R+r}.

Now suppose
R\geq 2r_{0}
. Observe that

e^{-t}\sinh(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2}

is increasing with respect to
t\in[0,\infty)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{R+r})

\displaystyle\geq d\omega_{d}\int_{r_{0}}^{R+r}\sinh^{d-1}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq d\omega_{d}\left(e^{-r_{0}}\sinh(r_{0})\right)^{d-1}\int_{r_{0}}^{R+r}e^{(d-1)t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=d\omega_{d}\left(e^{-r_{0}}\sinh(r_{0})\right)^{d-1}\frac{e^{(d-1)(R+r)}-e^{(d-1)r_{0}}}{d-1}

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{d}\sinh^{d-1}(r_{0})\left(e^{(d-1)(R+r-r_{0})}-1\right),

whereas

P^{d}_{-1}(R;r)\leq d\omega_{d}\sinh^{d-1}(R+r)\leq d\omega_{d}e^{(d-1)r_{0}}e^{(d-1)(R+r-r_{0})}.

Hence

\frac{P^{d}_{-1}(R;r)}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{R+r})}\leq\frac{de^{(d-1)r_{0}}}{\sinh^{d-1}(r_{0})}\frac{e^{(d-1)(R+r-r_{0})}}{e^{(d-1)(R+r-r_{0})}-1},

and the right-hand side is uniformly bounded for
R+r-r_{0}\geq r_{0}
.

We can therefore pick constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 0
such that the inequality

\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R+r})}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{R+r})}\leq\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)}{R+r}

holds for
\kappa=0,-1
. For other
\kappa<0
, we have
\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{r})=\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{\sqrt{-\kappa}r})
and
\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{r})=\sqrt{-\kappa}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(\partial B_{\sqrt{-\kappa}r})
, and therefore

\frac{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(\partial B_{R+r})}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{\kappa}(B_{R+r})}=\frac{\sqrt{-\kappa}\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(\partial B_{\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)})}{\mathrm{vol}^{d}_{-1}(B_{\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)})}\leq\sqrt{-\kappa}\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)}{\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)}=\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}\sqrt{-\kappa}(R+r)}{R+r}.\qed

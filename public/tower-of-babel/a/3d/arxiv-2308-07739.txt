Local well-posedness for incompressible neo-Hookean Elastic equations in almost critical Sobolev spaces
Huali Zhang

Abstract
. Inspired by a pioneer work of Andersson-Kapitanski [ 1 ] , we prove the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem of incompressible neo-Hookean equations if the initial deformation and velocity belong to H s + 1 ​ ( ℝ n ) × H s ​ ( ℝ n ) , s > n + 1 2 H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),s>\frac{n+1}{2} ( n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 ). Moreover, if the initial data is small, then we can lower the regularity to s > n 2 s>\frac{n}{2} , where n + 2 2 \frac{n+2}{2} and n 2 \frac{n}{2} is respectively a scaling-invariant exponent for deformation and velocity in Sobolev spaces. Our new observation relies on two folds: a reduction to a second-order wave-elliptic system of deformation and velocity; and a ”wave-map type” null form intrinsic in this coupled system. In particular, the wave nature with ”wave-map type” null form allows us to prove a bilinear estimate of Klainerman-Machedon type for nonlinear terms. So we can lower 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order regularity in 3D and 3 4 \frac{3}{4} -order regularity in 2D for well-posedness compared with [ 1 ] .

Introduction

This paper considers solutions to the Cauchy problem of incompressible neo-Hookean elastic equations for low regularity initial data in two or three spatial dimensions. To fix the formulation of this system, let us introduce the motion of the neo-Hookean material, which can also be found in
[
1
]
.

1.1.
The motion in Lagrangian settings

We assume that an elastic body occupies the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with coordinates
\mathbf{y}
, and
\mathbf{y}=(y^{1},y^{2},\cdots,y^{n})^{\text{T}}
. A generic point
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
will find itself at the location
\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})
at time
t
, and
\mathbf{x}=(x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{n})^{\text{T}}
. Incompressibility means that

\text{det}\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}}=1.

(1.1)

The action functional

\int\int\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial t}\right|^{2}+p(t,\mathbf{y})\left(\text{det}\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}}-1\right)d\mathbf{y}dt,

gives the Euler-Lagrange equations

\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(1.2)

Above,
p(t,\mathbf{y})
is a Lagrangian multiplier. Equation (
1.2
) describes the ideal fluid (incompressible Euler equations), cf.
[
25
]
. For homogeneous, isotropic and hyperelastic materials, adding the potential energy, the action takes form

\int\int\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial t}\right|^{2}-W\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\right)+p(t,\mathbf{y})\left(\text{det}\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}}-1\right)d\mathbf{y}dt,

(1.3)

Here
W
is the stored energy functions and

p
is a Lagrangian multiplier, cf.
[
29
]
. Let us focus on the most simplest case, i.e., the neo-Hookean case, in which the strain
energy functional is simply given by

W\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{n}_{i,a=1}\left|\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial y^{a}}\right|^{2}.

Clearly, in the neo-Hookean case, the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation of (
1.3
) are
1
1

1

In the summation the repeated indices run from
1
to
n
.

\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial y^{a}\partial y^{a}}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(1.4)

Therefore, Equations (
1.4
) complemented by the constraint (
1.1
) describes the motion of the incompressible neo-Hookean material in the Lagrange cooridnates
(t,\mathbf{y})
:

\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}x^{i}}{\partial y^{a}\partial y^{a}}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n,\\
&\text{det}(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}})=1.\end{cases}

(1.5)

To be simple, we consider

2

Our result also works for

\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{U}(t,\mathbf{y}),

(1.6)

where
\mathbf{U}=(U^{1},U^{2},\cdots,U^{n})

1.5

\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial^{2}U^{i}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}U^{i}}{\partial y^{a}\partial y^{a}}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x^{i}}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n,\\
&\text{det}(I+\frac{\partial\mathbf{U}}{\partial\mathbf{y}})=1.\end{cases}

(1.7)

For we study the Cauchy problem of (
1.7

(\mathbf{U},\frac{\partial\mathbf{U}}{\partial t})|_{t=0}=(\mathbf{U}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\in H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.8)

Seeing (
1.7
), it is invariant under the scaling

(t,\mathbf{y})\mapsto(\lambda t,\lambda\mathbf{y}).

(1.9)

This tells us that

\begin{split}&\|\mathbf{U}_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lambda^{s-\frac{n}{2}}\|\mathbf{U}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\\
&\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbf{U}_{\lambda}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lambda^{s-\frac{n}{2}}\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbf{U}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

Taking

1.7

1.8

1.2.
The dynamics in Euler settings

To distinguish between the derivatives in Lagrange coordinate and Euler picture, we denote by
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{i}
the partial derivatives in the Euler coordinate and by
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}
the derivatives in the Lagrangian coordinate.

In the Euler picture, the independent variables are
(t,\mathbf{x})
. Then the vector

\bar{\mathbf{v}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial t},

(1.11)

is the velocity in the Eulerian coordinates, and we set
\bar{\mathbf{v}}=(\bar{v}^{1},\bar{v}^{2},\cdots,\bar{v}^{n})
. The incompressibility condition (
1.1
) translates into

\text{div}\bar{\mathbf{v}}=\partial_{i}\bar{v}^{i}=0.

(1.12)

To describe the Eulerian form of equation (
1.4
) for the incompressible neo-Hookean material. We introduce the deformation gradient
\bar{\mathbf{F}}
by

\bar{F}^{ia}(t,\mathbf{x})=\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial y^{a}},\quad a=1,2,\cdots,n.

(1.13)

We also define the deformation matrix as

\bar{\mathbf{F}}=(\bar{F}^{ia})_{n\times n}.

(1.14)

Additionally, from the Piola’s identity(cf.
[
29
]
), we have

\text{div}\bar{\mathbf{F}}=0.

(1.15)

In the Euler picture, (
1.4
) is transformed to

\partial_{t}\bar{v}^{i}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{v}^{i}-\bar{F}^{kb}\partial_{k}\bar{F}^{ib}+\partial_{i}\bar{p}=0.

(1.16)

Above,
\bar{p}
is transformed by
p
in Euler picture, and
\bar{p}=\bar{p}(t,\mathbf{x})=\bar{p}(t,\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y}))=p(t,\mathbf{y})
.

On the other hand, we also have

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{F}^{ia}=\partial_{t}\bar{F}^{ia}(t,\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{y}))=\partial_{t}\bar{F}^{ia}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{F}^{ia},

(1.17)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{F}^{ia}=\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}(\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial y^{a}})=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial t})=\frac{\partial{v}^{i}}{\partial y^{a}}=\bar{F}^{ka}\partial_{k}\bar{v}^{i}

(1.18)

Due to (
1.17
) and (
1.18
), it yields

\partial_{t}\bar{F}^{ia}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{F}^{ia}-\bar{F}^{ka}\partial_{k}\bar{v}^{i}=0.

(1.19)

Combining (
1.12
), (
1.15
), (
1.16
), and (
1.19
), the incompressible neo-Hookean equations in Euler coordinates is described by

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\bar{v}^{i}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{v}^{i}-\bar{F}^{kb}\partial_{k}\bar{F}^{ib}+\partial_{i}\bar{p}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\bar{F}^{ia}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{F}^{ia}-\bar{F}^{ka}\partial_{k}\bar{v}^{i}=0,\\
&\text{div}\bar{\mathbf{v}}=\text{div}\bar{\mathbf{F}}=0.\end{cases}

(1.20)

Similarly, in the Euler picture, the incompressible Euler equation (
1.2
) becomes to

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\bar{v}^{i}+\bar{v}^{j}\partial_{j}\bar{v}^{i}+\partial_{i}\bar{p}=0,\\
&\text{div}\bar{\mathbf{v}}=0.\end{cases}

(1.21)

As we known, System (
1.21
) is a fundamental equation in fluid dynamics.

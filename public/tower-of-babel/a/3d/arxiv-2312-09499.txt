Abstract




Abstract:
This paper proves several weak limit theorems for the joint version of extreme order statistics and partial sums of independently and identically distributed random variables. The results are also extended to almost sure limit version.

Key Words:
extreme order statistics; partial sums; point process; almost sure limit theorem

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G70; secondary 60G55

1
Introduction

Since the maxima and partial sums of a sequence of random variables played very important role in theoretical and applied probability,
many authors studied their joint asymptotic properties.
Chow and Teugels (1978) first investigated asymptotic relation between the maxima and partial sums of a sequence of independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
. Denote by
M_{n}
and
S_{n}
the maximum and partial sum of
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}\}
, respectively.
Suppose that there exist constants
a_{n}>0,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
and a non-degenerate distribution
G(x)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P(a_{n}(M_{n}-b_{n})\leq x)=G(x),

where
G
must be one of the following three types of extreme value distributions:

\displaystyle\mbox{Gumbel:}\ \ \Lambda(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-e^{-x}\right),\ \ -\infty<x<+\infty;

\displaystyle\mbox{Frechet:}\ \ \Phi_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ x\leq 0,\\
\exp\left(-x^{-\alpha}\right),\quad x>0,\end{array}\right.\alpha\geq 0;

\displaystyle\mbox{Weibull:}\ \ \Psi_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\exp\left(-(-x)^{\alpha}\right),\quad x\leq 0,\\
1,\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad x>0,\end{array}\right.\alpha>0.

If
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
has finite variance, Chow and Teugels (1978) showed that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}-b_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)=G(x)\Phi(y),

(3)

which indicates the maximum and the partial sum are asymptotically independent.
If
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
has infinite variance, Chow and Teugels (1978) showed that the maximum and the partial sum are asymptotically dependent.

The result of (
3
) has many extensions. A natural extension is to consider the dependent case. Anderson and Turkman (1991a,1991b) extended Chow and Teugels’s result to strong mixing case under a technical condition
and then Hsing (1995) completed their results by deleting the technical condition.
Ho and Hsing (1996), Ho and McCormick (1999), McCormick and Qi (2000) and Peng and Nadarajah (2002) dealt with this problem for some dependent Gaussian cases and
James et al. (2007) considered the multivariate stationary Gaussian cases.

By studying the joint behavior of the point processes of exceedances and the partial sums for Gaussian processes, Hu et al. (2009) derived the
asymptotic distribution of the extreme order statistics and partial sums.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a stationary Gaussian sequence with covariance functions
r(n)=E(X_{1}X_{n+1})
.
Denote by
M_{n}^{(k)}
the
k
-th maximum of
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}\}
.
Suppose that the well known Berman’s condition was satisfied, i.e.,

r(n)\log n\rightarrow 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty,

then for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(c_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-d_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{Var(S_{n})}}\leq y\right)=\exp(-e^{-x})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(e^{-x})^{i}}{i!}\Phi(y),

(4)

where
\Phi(\cdot)
stands for the standard normal distribution function and
c_{n}>0,d_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
are real sequences.
For more studies in this direction, we refer to Peng (1998), Tan and Peng (2011a, b), Peng et al. (2012) and Tan and Yang (2015).

Another interesting extension is to consider the almost sure limit theorem for the maxima and partial sums.
Based on the result of Chow and Teugels (1978), Peng et al. (2009) studied the almost sure limit theorem for the maxima and partial sums
for i.i.d. random variables and showed that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}I\left(a_{n}(M_{n}-b_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)=G(x)\Phi(y)\ \ a.s.

(5)

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
I(\cdot)
denotes the indicator function.
For more studies on almost sure limit theorem for the maxima and partial sums for dependent cases, we refer to Dudziński (2003, 2008), Zang et al. (2010), Zhao et al. (2011), Tan and Wang (2011), Zang (2012), Wu (2015) and Wu and Jiang (2016).

Most of the above mentioned studies focus on the Gaussian cases, especially regarding the joint version between extreme order statistics and partial sums.
We even don’t know the asymptotic relation between extreme order statistics and partial sums for i.i.d cases.
In this paper, we continue to study the joint asymptotic properties of extreme order statistics and partial
sums for i.i.d. random sequence and have two goals. The first one is to derive the joint asymptotic distribution of

k
-th maxima and partial sums, which will extend the classic result (
3
). The second one is to prove the almost sure limit theorem
for the joint version of
k
-th maxima and partial sums, which will extend the result (
5
).
Some related limit results on the joint asymptotic of the
k
largest maxima and partial sums are also given.

2
The weak limit theorems

For any random sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
, define the point process of
exceedances of level
u_{n}
formed by
X_{i},1\leq i\leq n
as

N_{n}^{X}(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i}>u_{n},i/n\in B)

(6)

for any Borel set
B
on
(0,1]
.

In this paper, let
M_{n}^{(k)}
be the
k
-th maximum of
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}\}
and
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
.
Let
u_{n}=u_{n}(x)=a_{n}^{-1}x+b_{n}
, where
a_{n}>0,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
will be chosen in Theorem 2.1.

Now we state our main results.

Theorem 2.1
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with nondegenerate common distribution function
F
and
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
. Suppose that there exist constants
a_{n}>0,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}P(a_{n}(M_{n}-b_{n})\leq x)=G(x).

(7)

holds, where
G
is one of the extreme value distribution
\Lambda
,
\Phi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>2
and
\Psi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
Then
N_{n}^{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}N
and

N_{n}^{X}
and
\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
are asymptotically
independent, where
N
is a Poisson point process on
(0,1]
with intensity
\log G^{-1}(x)
.

Remark 2.2
.
This paper only deals with the case that
F
has finite variance. However, our method also does not hold for the case
that
F
is in the domain attraction of
\Phi_{\alpha}
with
\alpha=2
.

Theorem 2.3
.
Under the conditions of Theorem 2.1, we have for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)=G(x)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-\ln G(x))^{i}}{i!}\Phi(y).

(8)

The above results can be extended to the point process of exceedances of multiple levels.
Let
u_{n}^{(1)}\geq u_{n}^{(2)}\geq,\ldots,u_{n}^{(s)}
be
s
levels, where
u_{n}^{(j)}=u_{n}(x_{j})=a_{n}^{-1}x_{j}+b_{n}
,
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,s
. Define the point process
\widetilde{N}_{n}
of exceedances of levels
u_{n}^{(1)},u_{n}^{(2)},\ldots,u_{n}^{(s)}
as

\widetilde{N}_{n}^{X}(B)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}N_{n}^{(j)}(B)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i}>u_{n}^{j},(i/n,j)\in B)

(9)

for any Borel set
B
on
(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Construct a point process
\widetilde{N}
on
(0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\widetilde{N}(\cdot)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}N^{(j)}(\cdot)
, where
for each
j=1,2,\ldots,s-1
,
N^{(j)}
is a Poisson process independent thinning of the Poisson process
N^{(j+1)}
with deleting probability

1-\frac{\log G^{-1}(x_{j})}{\log G^{-1}(x_{j+1})}
, the initial Poisson process
N^{(s)}
has intensity
\log G^{-1}(x_{s})
.
It is known that
N^{(j)},j=1,2,\ldots,s
are Poisson processes with intensity
\log G^{-1}(x_{j})
respectively, and are independent on disjoint
intervals on the plane (cf. Leadbetter et al. 1983, Section 5.5).

Theorem 2.4
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of standard i.i.d. random variables with nondegenerate common distribution function
F
and
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
. Suppose that there exist constants
a_{n}>0,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
and a non-degenerate distribution
G(x)
such that
(
7
) holds, where
G
is one of the extreme value distribution
\Lambda
,
\Phi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>2
and
\Psi_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
Then
\widetilde{N}_{n}^{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\widetilde{N}
and

\widetilde{N}_{n}^{X}
and
\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
are asymptotically
independent.

It is easy to see from Theorem 2.4 that the
k
largest maxima and the partial sum are asymptotically independent.

Theorem 2.5
.
Under the conditions of Theorem 2.4, we have for any fixed
k\geq 1
and any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(1)}-b_{n})\leq x_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x_{k},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=H(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})\Phi(y),

(10)

where
H(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
is the joint limit distribution of
(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(1)}-b_{n}),\ldots,a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n}))
.

The distribution function
H(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
has a very complicated representation. The following corollary states a two dimensional case.

Corollary 2.6
.
Under the conditions of Theorem 2.4, we have for any fixed
l>k\geq 1
and any
x_{1},x_{2},y\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x_{1},a_{n}(M_{n}^{(l)}-b_{n})\leq x_{2},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i}^{l-1}G(x_{2})\frac{(\log G^{-1}(x_{1}))^{i}(\log G^{-1}(x_{2})-\log G^{-1}(x_{1}))^{j-i}}{i!(j-i)!}\Phi(y).

(11)

We need the following lemmas to prove Theorems 2.1 and 2.3-2.5.

Lemma 2.7
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with nondegenerate common distribution function
F
and
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
. Let
\{Y_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an independent copy of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
. If
(\ref{eq2.1})
holds, then for any
0<a<b\leq 1

\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left|P\left(X_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(Y_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right|\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof:
Note that
G
defined in
(\ref{eq2.1})
is either a Gumbel distribution
\Lambda(x)
or a Frechet distribution
\Phi_{\alpha}(x)
with
\alpha>2
or a Weibull-type distribution
\Psi_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
.
Define
x_{F}=\sup\{x:F(x)<1\}
and
S_{ni}=S_{n}-X_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Integration by parts shows that

\displaystyle P\left(X_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u_{n}}^{x_{F}}P\left(\frac{S_{ni}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)dF(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-F(u_{n}))P\left(\frac{S_{ni}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1-F(u_{n}))\int_{u_{n}}^{x_{F}}\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F(u_{n})}dP\left(\frac{S_{ni}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right).

Thus, the sum in Lemma 2.7 is bounded above by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left|(1-F(u_{n}))P\left(\frac{S_{ni}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-(1-F(u_{n}))P\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}(1-F(u_{n}))\int_{u_{n}}^{x_{F}}\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F(u_{n})}dP\left(\frac{S_{ni}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=([nb]-[na])(1-F(u_{n}))\left|P\left(\frac{S_{n1}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ +([nb]-[na])(1-F(u_{n}))\int_{u_{n}}^{x_{F}}\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F(u_{n})}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=:([nb]-[na])(1-F(u_{n}))[T_{n1}+T_{n2}],

where we used the stationarity of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
in the second step. Taking into account (
7
), we get

\displaystyle n(1-F(u_{n}))\rightarrow\log G^{-1}(x)

(12)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, to prove the lemma, it suffices to prove
T_{ni}\rightarrow 0
,
i=1,2
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
If
G=\Lambda
then
u_{n}
is slowly varying and so
u_{n}/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
(see e.g., Anderson and Turkman (1991a)).
If
G=\Phi_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>2
then
u_{n}
is regularly varying with index
1/\alpha<1/2
and so again
u_{n}/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
(see e.g., Anderson and Turkman (1991a)).
If
G=\Psi_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
then
u_{n}\leq x_{F}<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
and obviously
u_{n}/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, by the central limit theorem for i.i.d random variables,
T_{n1}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
It follows from the above arguments that we can also choose a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

(u_{n})^{1+\varepsilon}/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
For the term
T_{n2}
, we will split it into two cases:
x_{F}=\infty
and
x_{F}<\infty
.
For the case
x_{F}=\infty
,
G
must be either
\Lambda
or
\Phi_{\alpha}
.
Note that

\displaystyle T_{n2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}u_{n}\frac{1-F(tu_{n})}{1-F(u_{n})}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+tu_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right).

For
X
in the domain of attraction of
\Phi_{\alpha}
,
\frac{1-F(tu_{n})}{1-F(u_{n})}\sim t^{-\alpha}
as
u_{n}\rightarrow\infty
.
For
X
in the domain of attraction of
\Lambda
,
\frac{1-F(tu_{n})}{1-F(u_{n})}\leq t^{-\alpha}
for large enough
u_{n}
whatever
\alpha>0
.
We thus have for
\alpha>2

\displaystyle T_{n2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}u_{n}t^{-\alpha}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+tu_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u_{n}}^{\infty}(\frac{x}{u_{n}})^{-\alpha}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u_{n}}^{u_{n}^{1+\varepsilon}}(\frac{x}{u_{n}})^{-\alpha}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)+\int_{u_{n}^{1+\varepsilon}}^{\infty}(\frac{x}{u_{n}})^{-\alpha}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{u_{n}}^{u_{n}^{1+\varepsilon}}1dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)+\int_{u_{n}^{1+\varepsilon}}^{\infty}(u_{n})^{-\varepsilon\alpha}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{S_{n1}+u_{n}^{1+\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(\frac{S_{n1}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)+(u_{n})^{-\varepsilon\alpha},

which tends to
0
, by central limit theorem for i.i.d. random variables again, since
(u_{n})^{1+\varepsilon}/\sqrt{n}\rightarrow 0
and
u_{n}\rightarrow\infty
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
For the case
x_{F}<\infty
,
G
must be either
\Lambda
or
\Psi_{\alpha}
.
We have

\displaystyle T_{n2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u_{n}}^{x_{F}}\frac{1-F(x)}{1-F(u_{n})}dP\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{S_{n1}+x_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(\frac{S_{n1}+u_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by central limit theorem for i.i.d. random variables again, since
u_{n}\leq x_{F}<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
. The proof of the lemma is complete.

Lemma 2.8
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with nondegenerate common distribution function
F
and
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
.
Let
M_{n}^{X}(a,b]=\max(X_{i},i/n\in(a,b])
for any
0<a<b\leq 1
.
If
(\ref{eq2.1})
holds, then for any
0<a_{1}<b_{1}\leq a_{2}<b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}<b_{k}\leq 1

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{X}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\to\prod^{k}_{i=1}[G(x)]^{(b_{i}-a_{i})}\Phi(y),

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof:
Since
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables, by Theorem 5.4.5 of Leadbetter et al. (1983),
we have, as
n\rightarrow\infty

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{X}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\}\right)\to\prod^{k}_{i=1}[G(x)]^{(b_{i}-a_{i})}.

The asymptotic independence between the maxima and the sum can be proved by the same way as the proof of Theorem 1 of Anderson and Turkman (1991a).

Lemma 2.9
.
Let
\{Y_{n},n\geq 1\}
be an independent copy of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
.
For any fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
, constructing a new sequence as

Z_{i}=X_{i}I(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y)+{Y_{i}}I(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y),\ \ 1\leq i\leq n

and define

N_{n}^{Z}(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(Z_{i}>u_{n},i/n\in B)

(13)

for any Borel set
B
on
(0,1]
.
Then
N_{n}^{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}N
, where
N
is a Poisson point process on
(0,1]
with intensity
\ln G^{-1}(x)
.

Proof:
By Theorem A.1 in Leadbetter et al. (1983), it is sufficient to show that

(i)\ \ EN^{Z}_{n}((a,b])\rightarrow EN((a,b])=(b-a)\ln G^{-1}(x),0<a<b\leq 1;

\displaystyle(ii)\ \ P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N^{Z}_{n}((a_{i},b_{i}])=0\}\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N((a_{i},b_{i}])=0\}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-a_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)\right)

where
0<a_{1}<b_{1}\leq a_{2}<b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}<b_{k}\leq 1
.

For (i), we have

\displaystyle EN^{Z}_{n}((a,b])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\sum_{i/n\in(a,b]}I(Z_{i}>u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left\{P\left(Z_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)+P\left(Z_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left\{P\left(X_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)+P\left(Y_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left\{P(X_{i}>u_{n})+P\left(Y_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(X_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([nb]-[na])(1-F(u_{n}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=[na]+1}^{[nb]}\left\{P\left(Y_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)-P\left(X_{i}>u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\right\}.

Thus by Lemma 2.7 and the fact (
12
), we have

EN^{Z}_{n}((a,b])\rightarrow(b-a)\ln G^{-1}(x)=EN((a,b]),

as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Next for (ii), it follows that

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N^{Z}_{n}((a_{i},b_{i}])=0\}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{X}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)+P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{Y}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\}\right)P\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right).

(14)

By Lemma 2.8, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{X}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\rightarrow\prod^{k}_{i=1}\exp(-(b_{i}-a_{i})\log G^{-1}(x))\Phi(y),

(15)

as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Recalling that
\{Y_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of
i.i.d. random variables and noting the fact (
12
) again,
we get

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{M_{n}^{Y}(a_{i},b_{i}]\leq u_{n}\}\right)\rightarrow\prod^{k}_{i=1}\exp(-(b_{i}-a_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)),

(16)

as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Since
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is also a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)\rightarrow 1-\Phi(y),

(17)

as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, plugging (
15
-
17
) into (
2
), we get

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N^{Z}_{n}((a_{i},b_{i}])=0\}\right)\rightarrow\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-a_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)\right).

This completes the proof of Lemma 2.9.

Proof of Theorem 2.1:
Since
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of
standard i.i.d. random variables, by Theorem 5.2.1 in Leadbetter et al. (1983),
N_{n}^{X}
converges in
distribution to a Poisson process on (0,1] with intensity
\log G^{-1}(x)
. Next, we show the asymptotic independence between
N_{n}^{X}

and
S_{n}
.

First noting that for disjoint Borel sets
B_{1},B_{2},\cdots,B_{k}
on
(0,1]

with
m(\partial B_{i})=0,i=1,2,\cdots,k
, we have

\displaystyle P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{X}(B_{i})=l_{i}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)-P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)-P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Y}(B_{i})=l_{i}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)-P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Y}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)P\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}>y\right)

where
l_{1},l_{2},\cdots,l_{k}
are non-negative integer numbers.
Since
\{Y_{n},n\geq 1\}
is an independent copy of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
,

N_{n}^{Y}
converges in distribution to a Poisson process on (0,1] with parameter
\ln G^{-1}(x)
. Thus we have

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Y}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x))^{l_{i}}}{l_{i}!}e^{-m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. By Lemma 2.9

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{Z}(B_{i})=l_{i}\}\right)\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x))^{l_{i}}}{l_{i}!}e^{-m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus,

P\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{N_{n}^{X}(B_{i})=l_{i}\},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\rightarrow\Phi(y)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x))^{l_{i}}}{l_{i}!}e^{-m(B_{i})\log G^{-1}(x)},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which proves the asymptotic independence between
N_{n}^{X}

and
S_{n}
. The proof of Theorem 2.1 is complete.

Proof of Theorem 2.3:

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(N_{n}^{X}((0,1])\leq k-1,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}P(N_{n}^{X}((0,1])=l,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y)

and the result follows by Theorem 2.1.

Proof of Theorem 2.4:
The proof of Theorem 2.4 is essentially the same as that of Theorem 2.1, and the details
are omitted here.

Proof of Theorem 2.5:
By Theorem 2.4, we have as
n\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(1)}-b_{n})\leq x_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x_{k},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(N_{n}^{(1)}((0,1])\leq 0,\ldots,N_{n}^{(k)}((0,1])\leq k-1,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow P\left(N^{(1)}((0,1])\leq 0,\ldots,N^{(k)}((0,1])\leq k-1\right)\Phi(y),

which shows the asymptotic independence of the
k
largest maxima and the partial sum.

Proof of Corollary 2.6:

By Theorem 2.4 again, we have

\displaystyle P\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x_{1},a_{n}(M_{n}^{(l)}-b_{n})\leq x_{2},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(N_{n}^{(k)}((0,1])\leq k-1,N_{n}^{(l)}((0,1])\leq l-1,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow P\left(N^{(k)}((0,1])\leq k-1,N^{(l)}((0,1])\leq l-1\right)\Phi(y),

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Note that

\displaystyle P\left(N^{(k)}((0,1])\leq k-1,N^{(l)}((0,1])\leq l-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i}^{l-1}P\left(N^{(k)}((0,1])=i,N^{(l)}((0,1])=j\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i}^{l-1}P\left(N^{(l)}((0,1])=j\right)P\left(N^{(k)}((0,1])=i\big|N^{(l)}((0,1])=j\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i}^{l-1}\frac{\left(\log G^{-1}(x_{2})\right)^{j}}{j!}e^{-\log G^{-1}(x_{2})}\frac{j!}{i!(j-i)!}\left(\frac{\log G^{-1}(x_{1})}{\log G^{-1}(x_{2})}\right)^{i}\left(1-\frac{\log G^{-1}(x_{1})}{\log G^{-1}(x_{2})}\right)^{j-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=i}^{l-1}G(x_{2})\frac{(\log G^{-1}(x_{1}))^{i}(\log G^{-1}(x_{2})-\log G^{-1}(x_{1}))^{j-i}}{i!(j-i)!},

which completes the proof.

3
The almost sure limit theorem

In this section, we extend Theorems 2.3 and 2.5 to the almost sure version.

Theorem 3.1
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of standard i.i.d. random variables with nondegenerate common distribution function
F
and
E(X_{1})=0
,
E(X_{1}^{2})=1
. Suppose that there exists constants
a_{n}>0,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
and a non-degenerate distribution
G(x)
such that
(
7
)
holds. Then for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and fixed
k\geq 1

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}I\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x,\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)=G(x)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-\ln G(x))^{i}}{i!}\Phi(y)\ \ a.s.

(18)

and for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y\in\mathbb{R}
and fixed
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}I\left(a_{n}(M_{n}^{(1)}-b_{n})\leq x_{1},\ldots,a_{n}(M_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n})\leq x_{k},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=H(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})\Phi(y)\ \ a.s.

(19)

Before giving the proof of Theorem 3.1, we state two lemmas which will be used in the proofs of our main results.
For
n-m\geq k
, let
M_{m,n}^{(k)}
be the
k
-th maximum of
\{X_{m+1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
and
S_{m,n}=\sum_{l=m+1}^{n}X_{l}
.
As usual,
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
means
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|a_{n}/b_{n}|<+\infty
.

Lemma 3.2
.
Let
(\xi_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}
be a sequence
of uniformly bounded random variables, i.e., there exists some

M\in(0,\infty)
such that
|\xi_{k}|\leq M

a.s.
for all
k\in N
. If

\displaystyle Var\left(\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}\xi_{n}\right)\ll(\log N)^{2}(\log\log N)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}

for some
\varepsilon>0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}(\xi_{n}-E\xi_{n})=0\ \ a.s.

Proof
. See Lemma
3.1
of Csáki and
Gonchigdanzan (2002).

Lemma 3.3
.
Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of standard i.i.d. random variables. We have for any fixed
k
and
m\geq k

P\left(M_{m}^{(k)}>M_{m,n}^{(k)}\right)\leq k\frac{m}{n}.

Proof
. See Lemma 1 of Peng et al. (2009).

Proof of Theorem 3.1
. Let

\eta_{n}=I\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)-P\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right).

Notice that
(\eta_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of bounded
random variables with
Var(\eta_{n})\leq 1
.
We first show

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\eta_{n}=0,\ \ a.s.

(20)

Using Lemma 3.2, we only need to show

Var\left(\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}\eta_{n}\right)\ll(\log N)^{2}(\log\log N)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}.

(21)

We have,

\displaystyle Var\left(\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}\eta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n}\eta_{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{E\eta_{n}^{2}}{n^{2}}+2\sum_{1\leq m<n\leq N}\frac{E(\eta_{m}\eta_{n})}{nm}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle L_{N,1}+2L_{N,2}.

Clearly

\displaystyle L_{N,1}=\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}E\eta_{n}^{2}\leq\sum^{N}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}=O(1).

For
L_{N,2}
, for
n\geq m+k
, we have

\displaystyle\left|E(\eta_{m}\eta_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|Cov\left(I\left(M_{m}^{(k)}\leq u_{m},\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\leq y\right),I\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3},

where

\displaystyle R_{1}=\left|Cov\left(I\left(M_{m}^{(k)}\leq u_{m},\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\leq y\right),\left[I\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)-I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right]\right)\right|,

\displaystyle R_{2}=\left|Cov\left(I\left(M_{m}^{(k)}\leq u_{m},\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\leq y\right),\left[I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)-I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{m,n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right]\right)\right|

and

\displaystyle R_{3}=\left|Cov\left(I\left(M_{m}^{(k)}\leq u_{m},\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{m}}\leq y\right),I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{m,n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right)\right|.

For the first term
R_{1}
, by Lemma 3.3, we have

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle E\left|I\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)-I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle P\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n}\right)-P\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle P\left(M_{m}^{(k)}>M_{m,n}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\frac{m}{n}.

For the second term
R_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle E\left|I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)-I\left(M_{m,n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{m,n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle E\left|\frac{S_{m}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(E(S_{m}^{2}))^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{1/2}.

By the independence of
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
, we have

R_{3}=0.

Then we conclude that

\displaystyle L_{N,2}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m<n\leq N\atop n\geq m+k}\frac{1}{mn}\left(\frac{m}{n}+\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{1/2}\right)+\sum_{1\leq m<n\leq N\atop n<m+k}\frac{1}{mn}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}1+\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{m^{1/2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{n=m+1}^{m+k}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n}+2\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\log N.

Thus, (
21
) holds. Note that Theorem 2.3 implies

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}P\left(M_{n}^{(k)}\leq u_{n},\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq y\right)=G(x)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-\ln G(x))^{i}}{i!}\Phi(y)\ \ a.s.,

(22)

and then the first assertion of Theorem 2.1 follows from (
20
) and (
22
).
The second assertion can be proved similarly.

A construction of the polylogarithm motive
Clément Dupont, Javier Fresán

Abstract
. Classical polylogarithms give rise to a variation of mixed Hodge–Tate structures on the punctured projective line S = ℙ 1 ∖ { 0 , 1 , ∞ } S=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\} , which is an extension of the symmetric power of the Kummer variation by a trivial variation. By results of Beilinson–Deligne, Huber–Wildeshaus, and Ayoub, this polylogarithm variation has a lift to the category of mixed Tate motives over S S , whose existence is proved by computing the corresponding space of extensions in both the motivic and the Hodge settings. In this paper, we construct the polylogarithm motive as an explicit relative cohomology motive, namely that of the complement of the hypersurface { 1 − z t 1 ⋯ t n = 0 } \{1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n}=0\} in affine space 𝔸 S n \mathbb{A}^{n}_{S} relative to the union of the hyperplanes { t i = 0 } \{t_{i}=0\} and { t i = 1 } \{t_{i}=1\} .



0.
Introduction

0.1.
The polylogarithm variation of mixed Hodge–Tate structures

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer. The
n
-th
polylogarithm

\mathrm{Li}_{n}
is the function defined on the complex unit disk
\lvert z\rvert<1
by

\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n}}.

That is,
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=-\log(1-z)
and
\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
is, for
n\geqslant 2
, the primitive of
\mathrm{Li}_{n-1}(z)/z
that vanishes at
z=0
. Hence, the vector
\big(1,\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z),\dots,\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)\big)
is a solution of the system of linear differential equations
\mathrm{d}L=L\,\Omega_{n}
on the punctured Riemann sphere
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
, where
\Omega_{n}
is the matrix

(0.1)

\Omega_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}&&&&&\\
&0&\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}&&&0&\\
&&0&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&\ddots&&\\
&0&&&&0&\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\\
&&&&&&0\\
\end{matrix}\right).

A full basis of fundamental solutions is given by the rows of the matrix

(0.2)

\Lambda_{n}(z)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)&\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z)&\mathrm{Li}_{3}(z)&\cdots&\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)\\
&2\pi\mathrm{i}&2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\log(z)&2\pi\mathrm{i}\frac{\log^{2}(z)}{2}&\cdots&2\pi\mathrm{i}\frac{\log^{n-1}(z)}{(n-1)!}\\
&&(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}&(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}\log(z)&\cdots&(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}\frac{\log^{n-2}(z)}{(n-2)!}\\
&&&&&\\
&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
&&0&&&\\
&&&&&(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{n}\end{matrix}\right).

The entries of
\Lambda_{n}(z)
are multivalued functions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
, and analytic continuation along a loop around one of the punctures left-multiplies
\Lambda_{n}(z)
by a monodromy matrix. These monodromy matrices, first computed by Ramakrishnan
[
Ram82
]
, are upper triangular with
1
s along the diagonal, and have rational entries thanks to the normalization by powers of
2\pi\mathrm{i}
.

Deligne
[
Del84
]
realized that
\Lambda_{n}(z)
gives rise to a variation of mixed Hodge–Tate structures on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
, the
n
-th
polylogarithm variation

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
, which is defined as follows.

•

Its underlying holomorphic vector bundle is trivial of rank
n+1
with basis
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}
, equipped with the flat connection
\mathrm{d}+\Omega_{n}
. Its weight and Hodge filtrations are such that
W_{2k}=W_{2k+1}
is spanned by
e_{0},\ldots,e_{k}
and
F^{k}
by
e_{k},\ldots,e_{n}
, for all
k
.

•

Its underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-local system consists of those holomorphic functions

\varphi\colon U\to\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{n}\qquad(U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\})

such that
\Lambda_{n}(z)\varphi(z)
has locally constant rational entries. The weight filtration is such that
W_{2k}=W_{2k+1}
consists of those
\varphi
for which
\Lambda_{n}(z)\varphi(z)
takes values in
\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{k}
, which defines a sub-local system by the special shape of the monodromy matrices.

Concretely, the fiber of
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
at
z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
can also be described as the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of dimension
n+1
with basis
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}
, with weight and Hodge filtrations such that
W_{2k}=W_{2k+1}
is spanned by
e_{0},\ldots,e_{k}
and
F^{k}
is spanned by the
k
-th to
n
-th columns of
\Lambda_{n}(z)
, for all
k
.

The block-triangular shape of (
0.2
) shows that
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
contains the trivial variation with fiber
\mathbb{Q}(0)
as a subobject, and that the quotient is a Tate twist (corresponding to the multiplicative factor
2\pi\mathrm{i}
) of the
(n-1)
-symmetric power of the
Kummer variation

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{H}}
, described by the same procedure as above starting from the matrix

\left(\begin{matrix}1&\log(z)\\
0&2\pi\mathrm{i}\end{matrix}\right).

It is also apparent from the shape of (
0.2
) that
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
contains
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{H}}
as a subobject, hence an inductive system
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}
of variations of mixed Hodge–Tate structures. The symmetric powers
\Sym^{n}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{H}})
also make up an inductive system induced by the inclusion of
\mathbb{Q}(0)
inside
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{H}}
, and we get a short exact sequence of ind-variations of mixed Hodge–Tate structures

(0.3)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(0)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{H}})(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

Remark 0.4
.

What usually appears in the literature
[
Ram89
,
Hai94
]
is the dual variation
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}})^{\vee}
, which is less natural from a cohomological viewpoint because it has non-positive weights. Its underlying holomorphic vector bundle is trivial of rank
n+1
with basis
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
equipped with the flat connection
\mathrm{d}-{}^{\mathrm{t}}\Omega_{n}
, with weight and Hodge filtrations such that
W_{-2k}=W_{-2k+1}
is spanned by
f_{k},\ldots,f_{n}
and
F^{-k}
by
f_{0},\ldots,f_{k}
, for all
k
. Its underlying local system is the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of the rows of
\Lambda_{n}(z)
, with weight filtration such that
W_{-2k}=W_{-2k+1}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of the
k
-th to
n
-th rows. Our descriptions of
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}
and
(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}})^{\vee}
are related by the fact that the rows of
\Lambda_{n}(z)
express the coordinates of the dual basis
e_{0}^{\vee},\ldots,e_{n}^{\vee}
in the basis
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
.

0.2.
The polylogarithm motive

In an attempt to find a motivic interpretation of Zagier’s conjecture
[
Zag91
]
expressing the special values of the Dedekind zeta function of a number field in terms of polylogarithms, Beilinson and Deligne
[
BD94
]
postulated the existence of a lift of the polylogarithm variation to the then-conjectural abelian category
\mathbf{MT}(S)
of
mixed Tate motives
over
S=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. The formalism of motivic polylogarithms was generalized to multiple polylogarithms by Goncharov, leading to progress on Zagier’s conjecture
[
Gon95
]
. We refer the reader to the survey article
[
Dup21
]
for more details on the motivic aspects of Zagier’s conjecture.

By the work of Voevodsky
[
Voe00
]
, we now have access to a triangulated category
\mathbf{DM}(S)
of mixed motives over
S
, from which the abelian category
\mathbf{MT}(S)
can be extracted as in the case where the base is a number field explained by Levine
[
Lev93
]
.
Inspired by constructions of Huber and Wildeshaus in the Hodge and the
\ell
-adic settings
[
HW98
]
, Ayoub
[
Ayo04
]
defined a polylogarithm motive as an ind-object of
\mathbf{MT}(S)
. The key idea is to realize that extensions of the form (
0.3
) are parametrized by a one-dimensional space in the Hodge setting, but also in the motivic setting: if
\mathcal{K}
denotes the
Kummer motive
, lifting the Kummer variation to
\mathbf{MT}(S)
, then there is a canonical isomorphism

(0.5)

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Ind}\left(\mathbf{MT}(S)\right)}\left(\Sym(\mathcal{K})(-1),\mathbb{Q}_{S}(0)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{Q}.

One then defines the polylogarithm motive as the extension corresponding to
1\in\mathbb{Q}
via (
0.5
). More recently, Huber and Kings
[
HK18
]
have generalized this construction to attach a polylogarithm motive to a smooth commutative group scheme over a general base.

In this paper, we give an explicit construction of the polylogarithm motive as the relative cohomology of a pair of varieties over
S
. Our starting point is the integral representation

(0.6)

\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)=\int_{[0,1]^{n}}\frac{z\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n}}{1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n}},

valid for
z
outside the half-line
[1,\infty)
, which suggests to work in the following geometric framework. Let
z
denote the coordinate on
S
, and let
X_{n}=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}
be the affine
n
-space over
S
with coordinates
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
. Consider the closed
S
-subschemes of
X_{n}
defined by the equations

A_{n}=\{1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n}=0\}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 8.5359ptB_{n}=\{t_{1}(1-t_{1})\cdots t_{n}(1-t_{n})=0\},

so that the integrand of (
0.6
) defines an algebraic differential
n
-form on
X_{n}\setminus A_{n}
, the integration domain a singular
n
-chain in
X_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with boundary in
B_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and the polylogarithm
\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
a period function of the family of relative cohomology groups

(0.7)

\mathrm{H}^{n}(X_{n}\setminus A_{n},B_{n}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n}).

Figure
1
below illustrates the case
n=2
.

Figure 1.
The hypersurfaces
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
in the affine plane
X_{2}
.

Definition 0.8
.

The
n
-th
polylogarithm motive
is the relative cohomology motive (see Definition
A.1
for this notion)

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\mathrm{M}\big(X_{n}\setminus A_{n},B_{n}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n}\big)[n].

A priori an object of
\mathbf{DM}(S)
, the
n
-th polylogarithm motive turns out to lie in the abelian subcategory
\mathbf{MT}(S)
of mixed Tate motives over
S
, reflecting the fact that the cohomology groups of the pair
(X_{n}\setminus A_{n},B_{n}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n})
are concentrated in degree
n
.
Besides, a partial boundary morphism (see Definition
A.10
) along the irreducible component
\{t_{n}=1\}
of
B_{n}
gives rise to a morphism of motives
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}_{n}
that makes up an inductive system
\mathcal{L}
in
\mathbf{MT}(S)
.

Our main result is as follows (see Theorems
2.3
,
2.6
,
2.21
below).

Theorem 0.9
.

The ind-motive
\mathcal{L}
fits into a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{S}(0)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\Sym(\mathcal{K})(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

Its Hodge realization is the polylogarithm ind-variation
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{H}}
.

It is easy to show that
\mathcal{L}_{n}
fits in a short exact sequence

(0.10)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{S}(0)\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A_{n},A_{n}\cap B_{n})[n-1](-1)\longrightarrow 0,

and the crux of the proof (Theorem
1.19
below) consists in establishing an isomorphism

(0.11)

\mathrm{M}(A_{n},A_{n}\cap B_{n})[n-1]\simeq\mathrm{Sym}^{n-1}(\mathcal{K}).

The main technical ingredient in the proof is a motivic lift of a spectral sequence originally due to Getzler
[
Get99
]
in the Hodge setting, which computes motives of configuration spaces with coefficients, and is a special case of a general construction of
[
DJ23
]
.

The short exact sequence (
0.10
) was already noticed by Deligne in a letter to Beilinson
[
Del01
]
, where the isomorphism (
0.11
) is conjectured:

[\cdots]
while
\mathrm{H}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{1}^{n}x_{i}=z,\mathrm{rel}\,x_{i}=1\right)
is the
\mathrm{Sym}^{n-1}
of the Kummer extension
[\cdots]
. At least I am convinced it is, but here also I would like to understand why.

The letter was prompted by Ball and Rivoal’s proof
[
Riv00
,
BR01
]
that the Riemann zeta function takes irrational values at infinitely many odd integers, which features integrals of the form

(0.12)

\int_{[0,1]^{n}}\frac{t_{1}^{u_{1}}(1-t_{1})^{v_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{u_{n}}(1-t_{n})^{v_{n}}}{(1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n})^{r}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n}

for integer parameters
u_{i},v_{i},r
. By elementary manipulations, these integrals can be written as linear combinations with polynomial coefficients of
1
and the polylogarithms
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z),\ldots,\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
. As they are period functions of the family of relative cohomology groups (
0.7
), the conceptual explanation for this is that (
0.7
) are incarnations of the polylogarithm motives. Ball and Rivoal were eventually interested in the evaluations of (
0.12
) at
z=1
, for which a geometric interpretation was studied by the first-named author
[
Dup18
]
. The present paper can therefore be thought of as a functional version of
loc. cit.
.

An advantage of identifying the polylogarithm with an explicit relative cohomology motive is that one can then define it in the category of perverse Nori motives over
S

[
IM19
]
, where the computation of extension groups is currently out of reach.

0.3.
Iterated integrals and the motivic fundamental group

Considering instead the more familiar representation of the polylogarithm as the iterated integral

(0.13)

\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)=\int_{0\leqslant x_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant x_{n}\leqslant 1}\frac{z\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{1-zx_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}\cdots\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n}}{x_{n}},

which is related to (
0.6
) through the change of variables

(0.14)

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n},t_{2}\cdots t_{n},\dots,t_{n-1}t_{n},t_{n}),

one is led to work in a slightly different geometric framework. Namely, we consider the closed
S
-subschemes of
X_{n}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{S}
defined by

A_{n}^{\prime}=\{(1-zx_{1})x_{2}\cdots x_{n}=0\}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 8.5359ptB^{\prime}_{n}=\{x_{1}(x_{2}-x_{1})(x_{3}-x_{2})\cdots(x_{n}-x_{n-1})(1-x_{n})=0\}.

However, the integral (
0.13
) is not a period of the relative cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{n}(X_{n}^{\prime}\setminus A_{n}^{\prime},B_{n}^{\prime}\setminus A_{n}^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}_{n}),

since the integration simplex
\Delta_{n}
of (
0.13
) meets the subvariety
A_{n}^{\prime}
on
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}
. The trick to separate them is to resort to a tower of blow-ups: let
\pi_{n}\colon\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n}\to X_{n}^{\prime}
be the composition of

•

the blow-up of
X^{\prime}_{n}
at the origin,

•

the blow-up along the strict transform of the line
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0\}
,

•

the blow-up along the strict transform of the plane
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{n-2}=0\}
,

\cdots

•

the blow-up along the strict transform of the codimension-two subspace
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}
,

and let
\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n}
and
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}
denote the strict transforms of
A^{\prime}_{n}
and
B^{\prime}_{n}
respectively, and
E_{n}
the exceptional divisor of
\pi_{n}
. Then the boundary of the preimage by
\pi_{n}
of the interior of
\Delta_{n}
lies on
\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}\cup E_{n}
and does not meet
\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n}
, as shown in Figure
2
below for
n=2
.

Figure 2.
In
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, the blow-up of the origin separates the boundary of the integration simplex
\{0\leqslant x_{1}\leqslant x_{2}\leqslant 1\}
from the pole divisor
\{(1-zx_{1})x_{2}=0\}
. By removing the strict transform of
\{x_{2}=0\}
one recovers the geometry of Figure
1
.

Therefore,
\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
is a period function of the family of relative cohomology groups

(0.15)

\mathrm{H}^{n}(\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n},(\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}\cup E_{n})\setminus\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n}\cap(\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}\cup E_{n})).

Notice that the change of variables (
0.14
) provides a local chart for the blow-up
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n}
. More precisely, it induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle X_{n}\longrightarrow\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\big(\widetilde{\{x_{2}=0\}}\cup\cdots\cup\widetilde{\{x_{n}=0\}}\big)\ ,\qquad(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

that identifies the pairs
(X_{n}\setminus A_{n},B_{n}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n})
and
(\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n},(\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}\cup E_{n})\setminus\widetilde{A}^{\prime}_{n}\cap(\widetilde{B}^{\prime}_{n}\cup E_{n}))
. Therefore, the cohomology groups (
0.7
) and (
0.15
) are isomorphic. Before this paper, the latter had only been computed for
n=2
by Wang
[
Wan06
]
, who gives a slightly different presentation in the spirit of Goncharov–Manin
[
GM04
]
and proves that it is a motivic lift of the dilogarithm variation. Our definition here can thus be seen as a way to circumvent the blow-up process.

Another approach to constructing the polylogarithm motive is via a quotient of the motivic fundamental groupoid
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\};\overline{0},z)
with a tangential base point at
0
and a
varying
usual base point at
z
, of which the iterated integrals (
0.13
) are naturally periods. At the moment of writing, this motivic fundamental group has been constructed as a system of realizations by Deligne
[
Del89
]
and as mixed Tate motive by Deligne–Goncharov
[
DG05
]
, but only for a
fixed algebraic
value of the parameter
z
. Moreover, the construction is not purely motivic, as it relies on the fact that the Hodge realization functor on mixed Tate motives over a number field is fully faithful and its image is stable under subquotients.

Notation and conventions related to categories of motives

Voevodsky’s triangulated category of mixed motives with rational coefficients over a scheme
S
(also known as
motivic sheaves
on
S
) will be denoted by
\mathbf{DM}(S)
. It is a symmetric monoidal category with unit object
\mathbb{Q}_{S}(0)
. The assignment
S\leadsto\mathbf{DM}(S)
supports a six-functor formalism
[
Ayo07a
,
Ayo07b
,
CD19
]
. Every pair of varieties
(X,Z)
over
S
gives rise to an object
\mathrm{M}(X,Z)
of
\mathbf{DM}(S)
that we call a
relative cohomology motive
. We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for a compendium of results on these objects.

We let
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
denote the multiplicative group scheme over
S
, and
\{1\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m,S}
its unit section. We define the
Lefschetz motive
as the relative cohomology motive

(0.16)

\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-1)=\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{G}_{m,S},\{1\})\,[1].

It is an invertible object of
\mathbf{DM}(S)
, and hence the tensor powers
\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-n)=\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-1)^{\otimes n}
are defined for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Given an object
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbf{DM}(S)
, we set
\mathcal{F}(-n)=\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-n)
.

Let
\mathbf{DMT}(S)
denote the triangulated subcategory of
\mathbf{DM}(S)
generated by the objects
\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By a theorem of Levine
[
Lev93
]
(see also
[
EL07
, §3.3]
), if
S
is the spectrum of a number field
F
or a non-empty open
F
-subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
, then
\mathbf{DMT}(S)
is equipped with a canonical
\mathrm{t}
-structure, whose heart
\mathbf{MT}(S)
is called the category of
mixed Tate motives
over
S
. It is an abelian category, endowed with cohomology functors
\mathrm{H}^{n}\colon\mathbf{DMT}(S)\rightarrow\mathbf{MT}(S)
induced by the
\mathrm{t}
-structure. Every object
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathbf{MT}(S)
is equipped with an increasing
weight filtration

W
indexed by even integers such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the graded piece
\mathrm{Gr}_{2n}^{W}\mathcal{F}=W_{2n}\mathcal{F}/W_{2(n-1)}\mathcal{F}
is a finite direct sum of copies of
\mathbb{Q}_{S}(-n)
. Moreover, the symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathbf{DM}(S)
induces the structure of a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear neutral tannakian category on
\mathbf{MT}(S)
.

1.1.
The Kummer motive and the logarithmic system

Let
Z
denote the union of the closed
S
-subschemes
of
\mathbb{G}_{m,S}

\{t=1\}
and
\{tz=1\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
Kummer motive
is the relative cohomology motive

\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{G}_{m,S},Z)\,[1]\;\;\in\mathbf{DM}(S).

The Kummer motive is usually defined over the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0,\infty\}=\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Q}}
, and
\mathcal{K}
is its restriction to
S
.
The following proposition is classical.

Proposition 1.2
.

The Kummer motive
\mathcal{K}
belongs to the subcategory

Proof.

Applied to the closed subschemes

of

, Proposition

1.3.

For the remainder of this section, we make a change of coordinates in

\{x_{n}=1/z\}

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

by permuting the coordinates

and

\mathcal{C}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}

For

\gamma_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\gamma^{i}_{n}.

The collection of the motives
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, endowed with the transition morphisms

, makes up an inductive system

Lemma 1.9
.

The transition morphism
\gamma_{n}
induces a morphism
\gamma_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\colon\mathcal{C}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
.

Proof.

We have the following identity in the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
. Let
\pi_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma
denote the projector onto the alternating component, and let us define

\xi_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}(i\cdots n).

Then we have
\pi_{n}=\xi_{n}\pi_{n-1}
, which follows immediately from the fact that a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
can be uniquely written as a product
(i\cdots n)\sigma^{\prime}
with
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
.
The functoriality of partial boundary morphisms (Proposition
A.11
) with respect to
(i\cdots n)
implies that we have
\gamma_{n}^{i}=(i\cdots n)\circ\gamma_{n}^{n}
, so that we may write
\gamma_{n}=\xi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}^{n}
. Again by the functoriality of partial boundary morphisms,
\gamma_{n}^{n}
is
\mathfrak{S}_{n-1}
-equivariant and we have

\gamma_{n}\circ\pi_{n-1}=\xi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}^{n}\circ\pi_{n-1}=(\xi_{n}\pi_{n-1})\circ\gamma_{n}^{n}=\pi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}^{n}\ ,

and thus
\gamma_{n}
sends the image of
\pi_{n-1}
to the image of
\pi_{n}
. The claim follows from this.
∎

In the same fashion, we have morphisms
\delta_{n}^{i}\colon\mathcal{D}_{n-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}

\{x_{i}=1/z\}
, and

\mathcal{D}

By the functoriality of partial boundary morphisms (Proposition
A.11
), we have
\varphi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}^{i}=\delta_{n}^{i}\circ\varphi_{n-1}
for all

, and we get morphisms of inductive systems

and
\varphi^{\mathrm{sgn}}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{sgn}}
.

Remark 1.12
.

The topological intuition behind Proposition
1.11
is the following. Let us consider the fiber at a fixed
z\in S(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
. In Betti realization, the transpose of
\varphi_{n}
is the natural map

(1.13)

\mathrm{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{B}}(T_{n},C_{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{B}}(T_{n},D_{n})

which sends the class of a cycle on
T_{n}
with boundary along
C_{n}
to the class of the same cycle, viewed with a boundary along
D_{n}\supset C_{n}
. Let us assume for simplicity that
z
is a real number satisfying
0<z<1
. Then the weight zero quotient of
\mathrm{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{B}}(T_{n},C_{n})
is one-dimensional with basis the class of the hypercube
\square_{n}(z)=[1,1/z]^{n}
. The weight zero quotient of
\mathrm{H}_{n}^{\mathrm{B}}(T_{n},D_{n})
has dimension
n!
and has a basis consisting of the classes of the simplices

\Delta_{n}^{\sigma}(z)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;|\;1\leqslant x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}\leqslant 1/z\}

for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. If we take the action of the symmetric group into account, then the weight zero quotient of the map (
1.13
) is the natural inclusion

\mathrm{sgn}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathrm{sgn}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]

sending the class of the hypercube
\square_{n}(z)
to the alternating sum of the classes of the simplices
\Delta_{n}^{\sigma}(z)
, which reflects the fact that
\square_{n}(z)
is paved by the
\Delta_{n}^{\sigma}(z)
for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(the signs are forced by the orientations). This last map clearly induces an isomorphism on the alternating components.

1.6.

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{sgn}}\simeq\mathcal{T}

By the functoriality of relative cohomology motives, there is a natural morphism
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{n}
. We let

\psi_{n}\colon\mathcal{D}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{n}

denote this morphism rescaled by the factor
n!
, i.e.,
\psi_{n}=n!\;\widetilde{\psi}_{n}
. The next lemma shows that these morphisms induce a morphism of inductive systems
\psi\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 1.14
.

We have
\psi_{n}\circ\delta_{n}=\tau_{n}\circ\psi_{n-1}
.

Proof.

By the functoriality of boundary morphisms (Proposition
A.11
), we have
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\circ\delta_{n}^{i}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and
\widetilde{\psi}_{n}\circ\delta_{n}^{n}=\tau_{n}\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{n-1}
. The claim follows since
n!\times(1/n)=(n-1)!\
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

2.3.
de Rham realization

We explicitly compute the de Rham realization of
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, which is an algebraic vector bundle with flat connection on
S
that we denote by
(\mathcal{V}_{n},\nabla)
. We will first see that
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is the trivial vector bundle of rank
n+1
by exhibiting an explicit basis, and compute the connection. For a smooth variety
Y
over
S
, we denote by
p_{Y}\colon Y\to S
the structure morphism and consider the complex of sheaves
\Omega^{\bullet}(Y/S)=\mathrm{R}^{0}(p_{Y})_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y/S}
on
S
whose local sections are global algebraic forms on the fibers of
Y
over
S
. Since
X_{n}\setminus A_{n}
is affine over
S
, the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is the
n
-th cohomology sheaf of the total complex of the double complex

(2.8)

0\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}((X_{n}\setminus A_{n})/S)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i}\,\Omega^{\bullet}((B_{n}^{i}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n}^{i})/S)\longrightarrow\cdots

which involves global algebraic forms on
X_{n}\setminus A_{n}
, on the irreducible components
B_{n}^{i}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n}^{i}
of
B_{n}\setminus A_{n}\cap B_{n}
, and on their multiple intersections.

The following construction is inspired by
[
Dup18
, §3]
. Recall that the
Eulerian polynomials

E_{r}(x)
are defined, for
r\geqslant 0
, by the relation

(2.9)

\sum_{j\geqslant 0}(j+1)^{r}x^{j}=\frac{E_{r}(x)}{(1-x)^{r+1}}.

The first examples are given by
E_{0}(x)=E_{1}(x)=1
,
E_{2}(x)=1+x
,
E_{3}(x)=1+4x+x^{2}
. They satisfy the recurrence relation

(2.10)

E_{r+1}(x)=x(1-x)E^{\prime}_{r}(x)+(1+rx)E_{r}(x).

For
n\geqslant 0
, we define differential forms

\omega_{n}^{(0)}=\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n}

and, for each
k=1,\ldots,n
,

\omega_{n}^{(k)}=\frac{z\,E_{n-k}(zt_{1}\cdots t_{n})}{(1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n})^{n-k+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n}.

They are global sections of the sheaf
\Omega^{n}((X_{n}\setminus A_{n})/S)
. Since
X_{n}\setminus A_{n}
has relative dimension
n
over
S
, they automatically define global sections of the vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

The classes of
\omega_{n}^{(0)},\ldots,\omega_{n}^{(n)}
form a basis of the algebraic vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{n}
, and the connection
\nabla\colon\mathcal{V}_{n}\to\mathcal{V}_{n}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\Omega^{1}_{S/\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies

\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(0)}])=0,\qquad\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(1)}])=[\omega_{n}^{(0)}]\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z},\qquad\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(k)}])=[\omega_{n}^{(k-1)}]\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z},\quad(k=2,\ldots,n).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The statement is clear for
n=0
. For the induction step, with
n\geqslant 1
, we use the following short exact sequence of algebraic vector bundles with flat connections on
S
, which is the de Rham realization of (
2.7
):

(2.12)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{V}_{n-1},\nabla)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{V}_{n},\nabla)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(A_{n}/S),\nabla)\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{S},\mathrm{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

The morphism
\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}
is induced by the inclusion inside (
2.8
) of the similar complex computing
\mathcal{V}_{n-1}
. Therefore, for each global section
\omega
of
\Omega^{n-1}((X_{n-1}\setminus A_{n-1})/S)
we have

\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}([\omega])=-[\mathrm{d}\eta],

where
\eta
is any global section of
\Omega^{n}((X_{n}\setminus A_{n})/S)
such that
\eta_{|\{t_{n}=1\}}=\omega
and
\eta

\{t_{n}=0\}
and all the

and

for

(1)

For

and we get

\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}([\omega_{n-1}^{(0)}])=(-1)^{n}[\omega_{n}^{(0)}].

Since

commutes with the connections, the induction hypothesis implies that

(2.14)

\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(0)}])=0.

(2)

For
\omega=\omega_{n-1}^{(k)}
with

\eta=\frac{zt_{n}E_{n-k-1}(zt_{1}\ldots t_{n})}{(1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n})^{n-k}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n-1}.

The recurrence relation (
2.10
) readily implies the equality

, hence

\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}([\omega_{n-1}^{(k)}])=(-1)^{n}[\omega_{n}^{(k)}]\quad\mbox{ for }k=1,\ldots,n-1.

\lambda_{n,\mathrm{dR}}

(2.16)

\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(k)}])=[\omega_{n}^{(k-1)}]\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\quad\mbox{ for }k=2,\ldots,n-1.

(3)

The induction hypothesis also implies that

\nabla([\omega_{n}^{(1)}])=[\omega_{n}^{(0)}]\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}

if

by hand. For this, we compute:

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\omega_{1}^{(1)}-\frac{1}{1-z}\omega_{1}^{(0)}=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{(1-zt)^{2}}-\frac{1}{1-z}\mathrm{d}t=\mathrm{d}\nu\qquad\mbox{ with }\quad\nu=-\frac{z}{1-z}\frac{t(1-t)}{1-zt}.

\nabla([\omega_{1}^{(1)}])=[\omega_{1}^{(0)}]\otimes\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}.

\omega_{n}^{(n)}=\frac{z\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{n}}{1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n}}=(-1)^{n-1}\mathrm{d}\log(1-zt_{1}\cdots t_{n})\wedge\mathrm{d}\log(t_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\log(t_{n-1})

\mathrm{Res}([\omega_{n}^{(n)}])=(-1)^{n-1}[\mathrm{d}\log(t_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\log(t_{n-1})],

It follows from an easy computation:

This concludes the induction step, and the proof.

Appendix A
Relative cohomology motives

In this appendix, we collect useful facts on relative cohomology motives. All of these results are well-known to experts but we were not able to find a reference where they are presented in a systematic way.

We fix a base scheme
S
which is assumed to be separated and of finite type over a field, and call
variety
a scheme
X
over
S
which is separated and of finite type, for which we denote by
p_{X}\colon X\rightarrow S
the structure morphism. We use the traditional notation

and
j_{U}^{X}\colon U\hookrightarrow X
for open and closed immersions respectively.

A.1.

We consider pairs
(X,Z)
consisting of a variety
X
and a closed subvariety

of

(X_{1},Z_{1})

such that

.

(X,Z)

By a slight abuse, shifts of

Remark A.2

For

is a complex that computes the relative singular cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(X,Z)=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(X(\mathbb{C}),Z(\mathbb{C});\mathbb{Q})

Relative cohomology motives yield a contravariant functor
(X,Z)\mapsto\mathrm{M}(X,Z)
from the category of pairs of varieties to
\mathbf{DM}(S)
.

In particular, we have morphisms

A.3.
The Künneth formula

The category of pairs of varieties is endowed with the product

(X_{1},Z_{1})\times(X_{2},Z_{2})=(X_{1}\times X_{2},(Z_{1}\times X_{2})\cup(X_{1}\times Z_{2})),

where all products are implicitly taken over the base scheme

A.15

Proposition A.13
.

Let
(X_{1},D_{1})
and
(X_{2},D_{2})
be two pairs consisting of a smooth variety and a strict normal crossing divisor. There is a functorial isomorphism in

Proof.

We denote by

the external tensor product defined as
\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{2}=\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{2}

\pi_{i}\colon X_{1}\times X_{2}\rightarrow X_{i}

Now the base change isomorphism and the projection formula imply, as in
[

\left(j_{X_{1}\setminus D_{1}\times X_{2}\setminus D_{2}}^{X_{1}\times X_{2}}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X_{1}\setminus D_{1}\times X_{2}\setminus D_{2}}(0)\simeq\left(j_{X_{1}\setminus D_{1}}^{X_{1}}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X_{1}\setminus D_{1}}(0)\boxtimes\left(j_{X_{2}\setminus D_{2}}^{X_{2}}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X_{2}\setminus D_{2}}(0).

(X_{1}\times X_{2},Z_{1}\times X_{2},X_{1}\times Z_{2})
, for pairs
(X_{1},Z_{1})
and
(X_{2},Z_{2})
.

If
A
and
B
are unions of irreducible components of a normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
such that no component of
D
is both in
A
and
B
, then
(X,A,B)
is locally of product type.

Proposition A.19
.

Let
(X,A,B)
be a triple that is locally of product type, with
X
smooth. Then there is an isomorphism in
\mathbf{DM}(X)
:

\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)_{!}\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus B}\right)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X\setminus A\cup B}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left(j_{X\setminus A}^{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus A}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X\setminus A\cup B}(0),

and an isomorphism in
\mathbf{DM}(S)
:

\mathrm{M}(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)\simeq\left(p_{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)_{!}\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)^{!}\left(j_{X\setminus A}^{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus A}^{X}\right)^{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}(0).

Proof.

The second isomorphism follows from the first and base change as in the proof of Proposition
A.17
. The first morphism corresponds by adjunction to the base change isomorphism:

\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus B}\right)_{*}\longrightarrow\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus B}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus A}\right)^{!}\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus A}\right)_{!}\simeq\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)^{!}\left(j_{X\setminus A}^{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus A}\right)_{!}.

If the triple
(X,A,B)
is of the type
(X_{1}\times X_{2},Z_{1}\times X_{2},X_{1}\times Z_{2})
for pairs
(X_{1},Z_{1})
and
(X_{2},Z_{2})
, with
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
smooth, then the first morphism is an isomorphism because both sides are isomorphic to the external tensor product
\left(j_{X_{1}\setminus Z_{1}}^{X_{1}}\right)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X_{1}\setminus Z_{1}}(0)\boxtimes\left(j_{X_{2}\setminus Z_{2}}^{X_{2}}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X_{2}\setminus Z_{2}}(0)
, by the same kind of reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
A.13
. The proposition follows by étale descent.
∎

Remark A.20
.

Proposition
A.19
implies, if
X
is smooth and proper of dimension
n
and
(X,A,B)
is a triple that is locally of product type, that we have Poincaré–Verdier duality:

(A.21)

\mathbb{D}_{S}\,\mathrm{M}(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)\simeq\mathrm{M}(X\setminus B,A\setminus A\cap B)\,(n)\,[2n].

A.5.
Residue morphisms

Proposition A.22
.

Let
(X,A,B)
a triple that is locally of product type, with
X
smooth and
A
smooth of pure codimension
c
in
X
. We have a distinguished triangle in
\mathbf{DM}(S)
:

(A.23)

\mathrm{M}(X,B)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A,A\cap B)\,(-c)\,[-2c+1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}.

Proof.

By using the distinguished triangle
1\rightarrow j_{*}j^{*}\rightarrow i_{!}i^{!}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\rightarrow}}
for
i=i_{A\setminus A\cap B}^{X\setminus B}
and
j=j_{X\setminus A\cup B}^{X\setminus B}
, applying
\left(p_{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)_{!}
, and using Proposition
A.19
, we get a distinguished triangle:

\mathrm{M}(X,B)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)\longrightarrow\left(p_{X}\right)_{*}\left(j_{X\setminus B}^{X}\right)_{!}\left(i_{A\setminus A\cap B}^{X\setminus B}\right)_{!}\left(i_{A\setminus A\cap B}^{X\setminus B}\right)^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X\setminus B}(0)[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}.

By purity, we have an isomorphism

\left(i_{A\setminus A\cap B}^{X\setminus B}\right)^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{X\setminus B}(0)\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{A\setminus A\cap B}(-c)\,[-2c]

and the rightmost term of the above triangle is isomorphic to

\left(p_{X}\right)_{*}\left(i_{A}^{X}\right)_{!}\left(j_{A\setminus A\cap B}^{A}\right)_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{A\setminus A\cap B}\,(-c)\,[-2c+1]\simeq\mathrm{M}(A,A\cap B)\,(-c)\,[-2c+1].

The proposition follows.
∎

Definition A.24
.

For
(X,A,B)
a triple that is locally of product type, with
X
smooth and
A
smooth of pure codimension
c
in
X
, the morphism

\mathrm{M}(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(A,A\cap B)\,(-c)\,[-2c+1]

appearing in the triangle (
A.23
) is called a
residue morphism
along
A
.

Remark A.25
.

Under the duality (
A.21
), the residue morphism along
A
is exchanged with the boundary morphism

\mathrm{M}(A\setminus A\cap B)[-1]\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(X\setminus B,A\setminus A\cap B).

There are also
partial
residue morphisms, which we do not need.

Remark A.26
.

The name “residue morphism” comes from the codimension
c=1
case, in which (assuming, for simplicity, that
B
is empty) the de Rham realization of the residue morphism
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(X\setminus A)\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet-1}(A)
can be computed by the standard residue of logarithmic
1
-forms
[
GH94
]
.

Proposition A.27
.

Let
(X,A,Z)
be a triple that is locally of product type, with
X
smooth and
A
smooth of pure codimension
c
in
X
. Let us fix a decomposition
Z=Z^{\prime}\cup Y
with
Y
smooth. Then we have a commutative diagram in
\mathbf{DM}(S)
, where the horizontal arrows are residue morphisms and the vertical arrows are partial boundary morphisms:

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}(Y\setminus A\cap Y,Y\cap Z^{\prime}\setminus A\cap Y\cap Z^{\prime})[-1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}(A\cap Y,A\cap Y\cap Z^{\prime})(-c)[-2c]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Proof.

The assumptions imply that

is locally of product type and that

in

; therefore, the upper residue morphism is well-defined. The commutativity of the diagram is left as an exercise to the reader.
∎

Appendix B
Motives of configuration spaces with coefficients

In this appendix, we present a motivic lift of Getzler’s results on mixed Hodge modules on configuration spaces
[
Get99
]
which is used in the proof of Proposition
1.11
. This is a special case of the main theorem of
[
DJ23
]
.

B.1.
The Arnol’d modules
A_{N}

Let
N
be a finite set of cardinality
n\geqslant 1
, and let
E_{N}
denote the graded-commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by degree
1
elements
e_{i,j}
, for distinct indices
{i,j\in N}
, subject to the relations
e_{i,j}=e_{j,i}
and

e_{i,j}e_{i,k}-e_{i,j}e_{j,k}+e_{i,k}e_{j,k}=0

for pairwise distinct indices
i,j,k\in N
. This algebra was introduced by Arnol’d who proved in
[
Arn69
]
that it is isomorphic to the rational cohomology algebra of the configuration space of distinct points indexed by
N
in
\mathbb{C}
. We will be interested in its top degree component.

Definition B.1
.

The
Arnol’d module

A_{N}
is the component of degree
n-1
of
E_{N}
.

A classical interpretation of the Arnol’d modules, originally due to Cohen (see
[
Coh76
, Theorem 12.3]
and
[
Coh95
, Theorem 6.1, (2)]
), is that
A_{N}^{\vee}
is isomorphic, up to the sign character, to the space
\mathrm{Lie}(N)
of Lie words on variables indexed by
N
that are linear in each variable.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
A_{N}
is functorial in
N
in the sense that a bijection
N\simeq N^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism
A_{N}\simeq A_{N^{\prime}}
; in particular,
A_{N}
is a representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
.

B.2.
The Arnol’d modules as poset homology groups

We will use the following interpretation of the Arnol’d module
A_{N}
in terms of poset topology
[
OS80
,
Bar90
,
HW95
]
. Recall that a
partition
of
N
is a set of disjoint subsets of
N
, called the
blocks
of the partition, whose union is
N
. We denote by
\Pi_{N}
the set of partitions of
N
and view it as a poset, where
\pi\leqslant\pi^{\prime}
if and only if
\pi^{\prime}
is obtained from
\pi
by merging blocks. The smallest element
\hat{0}
of
\Pi_{N}
is the partition whose blocks are all singletons, and the largest element
\hat{1}
is the partition with only one block. Note that the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
acts on
\Pi_{N}
. Then the poset
\Pi_{N}\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
only has homology in top degree
n-3
, and the corresponding homology group is isomorphic to
A_{N}
as a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
. With the notation of
[
DJ23
, §1]
, if
C^{\bullet}_{\Pi_{N}}(\hat{1})
denotes the complex that computes the (shifted) reduced cohomology of
\Pi_{N}\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
, we have an
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-equivariant quasi-isomorphism:

(B.2)

C^{\bullet}_{\Pi_{N}}(\hat{1})\simeq A_{N}^{\vee}[-n+1].

We will need a generalization of this fact to lower intervals in the partition poset. For a partition
\pi\in\Pi_{N}
, let us consider the complex
C^{\bullet}(\pi)
from
[
DJ23
, §1]
which computes the (shifted) reduced cohomology of the open interval
(\hat{0},\pi)
in
\Pi_{N}
. The closed interval
[\hat{0},\pi]
is isomorphic to the product of the partition posets
\Pi_{B}
, for
B\in\pi
, and we therefore get a quasi-isomorphism

C^{\bullet}(\pi)\simeq\bigotimes_{B\in\pi}C^{\bullet}_{\Pi_{B}}(\hat{1}).

Using (
B.2
), we get an
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-equivariant quasi-isomorphism

(B.3)

C^{\bullet}(\pi)\simeq\bigotimes_{B\in\pi}A_{B}^{\vee}[-(|B|-1)]\simeq A(\pi)^{\vee}[-n+|\pi|],

where we have set

A(\pi)=\bigotimes_{B\in\pi}A_{B}.

B.3.
The alternating part of Arnol’d modules

We will need the following crucial fact about the Arnol’d modules: they do not contain the sign representation.

Theorem B.4
.

If
n=|N|\geqslant 2
then
(A_{N}^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sgn}}=0
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the theorem after extending scalars to
\mathbb{C}
. The structure of
A_{N}^{\vee}
as a complex representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is given by the following classical result
[
Kly74
,
Han81
,
Sta82
]
. Let
C_{n}
be a cyclic subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
generated by an
n
-cycle, and let
\xi_{n}
be a primitive character of
C_{n}
. There is an
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-equivariant isomorphism

A_{N}^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{sgn}_{N}\otimes\mathrm{Ind}_{C_{n}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{N}}(\xi_{n}),

where
\mathrm{sgn}_{N}
denotes the sign character. Therefore,
(A_{N}^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sgn}}
is isomorphic to the space of fixed points of the
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
-representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{C_{n}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{N}}(\xi_{n})
, which by Frobenius reciprocity is isomorphic to the space of fixed points of the
C_{n}
-representation
\xi_{n}
. It is zero since
\xi_{n}
is a non-trivial character.
∎

B.4.
Motives of configuration spaces with coefficients

Let
X
be a scheme, and let
C_{N}(X)
denote the configuration space of points of
X
indexed by
N
, i.e. the complement in
X^{N}
of the union of the diagonals
x_{a}=x_{b}
, for distinct

indexed by partitions of
N
. Namely, to each
\pi\in\Pi(N)
corresponds a stratum whose Zariski closure is the closed subscheme

where
x_{a}=x_{b}
if
a
and

\pi
.

We let

denote the natural open immersion and let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{DM}(X^{N})
be a motive. Recall that a Postnikov system in a triangulated category is simply a sequence of distinguished triangles where each triangle has a vertex in common with the next one. The following theorem is a motivic lift of Getzler’s results on mixed Hodge modules on configuration spaces
[
Get99
]
.

Theorem B.5
.

There is a Postnikov system in the triangulated category
\mathbf{DM}(X^{N})
:

\textstyle{0=F^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{0}=j_{!}j^{!}\mathcal{F},\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G^{n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{+1}
\textstyle{G^{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{+1}
\textstyle{G^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

whose graded objects are given by

It is equivariant with respect to the action of

.

Proof.

This is a special case of the main theorem of
[

]
, taking the quasi-isomorphism (

∎

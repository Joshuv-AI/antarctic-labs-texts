Non-perturbative real topological strings
Marcos Mariño and Maximilian Schwick

Abstract
We study the resurgent structure of Walcher’s real topological string on general Calabi–Yau manifolds. We find all-orders trans-series solutions to the corresponding holomorphic anomaly equations, by extending the operator formalism of the closed topological string, and we obtain explicit formulae for multi-instanton amplitudes. We find that the integer invariants counting disks appear as Stokes constants in the resurgent structure, and we provide experimental evidence for our results in the case of the real topological string on local ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} .

Introduction

Topological string theory is defined perturbatively, and it has been a
longstanding problem to understand
its non-perturbative structure. An important clue for this is
the asymptotic behavior of its genus expansion: as in
other string theories
gp
;
shenker
, it grows factorially (see e.g.
mmlargen
).
This usually indicates the existence of exponentially small corrections
in the string coupling constant. In the case of
conventional strings and minimal strings, it is believed that such effects
are due to D-branes
polchinski
.

In
mmopen
;
msw
;
mmnp
it was proposed that a systematic understanding
of the non-perturbative effects associated
to the divergence of the topological string perturbation series can be obtained
by using the theory of resurgence
(see
ss
;
msauzin
;
abs
;
mmlargen
;
mmbook
for introductions and reviews). According to
this theory, one can associate to the perturbative series a collection of exponentially
small corrections, describing non-perturbative amplitudes,
together with numerical invariants called Stokes constants (see e.g.
gm-peacock

for a definition of such a “resurgent
structure”). In addition, there is growing evidence that the Stokes constants characterizing
the resurgent structure of the topological string
free energy are closely related to BPS invariants of the Calabi–Yau (CY) threefold

gm-peacock
;
astt
;
ghn
;
gm-multi
;
rella
;
gkkm
;
gu-res
;
im
(a connection between
Stokes constants and BPS invariants has been also found in complex
Chern–Simons theory and its supersymmetric 3d duals, see
gmp
;
ggm1
;
ggm2
;
ggmw
;
wheeler
).

The resurgent structure of topological string theory is therefore a very rich object,
containing perhaps information about all the BPS
invariants of the theory, and a complete description is still lacking. It is possible
however to obtain explicit expressions for the non-perturbative corrections by
using trans-series solutions to the holomorphic anomaly equations (HAEs) of

bcov-pre
;
bcov
. This idea was put forward in
cesv1
;
cesv2
in the local
CY case. It was further developed in
gm-multi
, where exact trans-series solutions
in closed form were obtained, and extended to compact CY manifolds in
gkkm
.
The results of
gm-multi
;
gkkm
are based on an operator formalism first considered
in
coms
;
codesido-thesis
. One finds in particular that multi-instanton solutions to the HAE are given by a generalization
of the eigenvelue tunneling of matrix models
david
;
shenker
;
msw
;
multi-multi
,
which suggests that flat coordinates are quantized in integer units of the string coupling constant.

All the studies done so far focused on closed topological strings. There is a very interesting and rich generalization
thereof which we will call the real topological string. Its
theory was developed in a series of papers
walcher-dq
;
walcher-bcov
;
walcher-tadpole
;
kw-localp2

by J. Walcher and collaborators. The basic idea is to introduce a real three-dimensional locus
in the CY target which plays two simultaneous roles: on the one hand it is a submanifold wrapped
by a D-brane, giving boundary conditions for open topological strings,
and on the other hand it is an orientifold plane, leading in addition to non-orientable amplitudes.
Originally, the theory was conceived as involving
open and closed strings only
walcher-dq
;
walcher-bcov
, but it was later realized that consistency
requires the non-orientable sector as well
walcher-tadpole
.

The real topological string leads to a perturbative series in the string coupling
constant due to a sum over orientable and non-orientable
surfaces, with and without boundaries. It is then natural to ask what is the resurgent
structure of this series and whether the
results of
gm-multi
;
gkkm
can be generalized to this setting. In this paper we start a systematic
exploration of these issues. First, we generalize the results of
gm-multi
;
gkkm
and we obtain
trans-series solutions to Walcher’s HAE
walcher-bcov
;
walcher-tadpole

for the real topological string. This is achieved by extending the operator formalism
of
gm-multi
;
gkkm
. One consequence of our analysis is that multi-instanton solutions
to the HAE of the real topological string can be again interpreted in terms of shifts of the closed string background
by integer multiples of the string coupling constant. The boundary conditions for the trans-series are provided
by the behavior of the real topological string amplitudes at the conifold and the large radius points.
As usual in resurgence theory, our results make it possible to obtain the asymptotics of the free energies
for large “genus” or Euler characteristic of the worldhseet, which we check successfully against perturbative results
in the case of the real topological string on local
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. In addition, we find indications that
the connection between Stokes constants and BPS invariants extends to the
real topological string, and one can explicitly show that the integer invariants counting disks appear as Stokes constants.

2.1

The real topological string involves a CY manifold

\sigma:X\rightarrow X.

In the A-model, the real

holomorphic maps from open and non-orientable Riemann surfaces to the target space

closed topological string theory, the enumerative information of the Gromov–Witten invariants

{\cal F}(t;g_{s})=\sum_{g\geq 0}{\cal F}_{g}(t)g_{s}^{2g-2}

{\cal F}(t;g_{s})=\sum_{d\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}\sum_{r\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}n_{r,d}{1\over k}\left(2\sin{kg_{s}\over 2}\right)^{2r-2}{\rm e}^{-kd\cdot t},

{\cal G}-{\cal F}=2\sum_{r\geq-1}\sum_{d\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\atop d_{i}\equiv r\,\text{mod}\,2}\sum_{k\,\text{odd}}\tilde{n}_{r,d}{1\over k}\left(2{\rm i}\sin{kg_{s}\over 2}\right)^{r}{\rm e}^{-dk\cdot t/2}.

Here,

In the B-model, the free energies

A more detailed analysis shows that

\Delta_{ij}=D_{i}D_{j}{\cal T}-{\rm i}C_{ijk}{\rm e}^{K}G^{k\bar{m}}D_{\bar{m}}\overline{{\cal T}}.

\partial_{\bar{a}}\Delta_{ij}=-{\rm i}C_{ijp}{\rm e}^{K}G^{p\bar{b}}\Delta_{\bar{a}\bar{b}},

(2.17)

where

\Delta_{\bar{a}\bar{b}}=\overline{\Delta_{ab}}.

(2.18)

The Griffiths’ invariant plays the rôle in the open string sector of the Yukawa coupling in the closed string sector.
For this reason, it is convenient to introduce its anti-holomorphic version as

\Delta_{\bar{a}}^{~b}={\rm i}{\rm e}^{K}G^{b\bar{c}}\Delta_{\bar{a}\bar{c}}.

\partial_{\bar{a}}R^{b}={\rm i}{\rm e}^{K}G^{b\bar{c}}\Delta_{\bar{a}\bar{c}}=\Delta_{\bar{a}}^{~b},

(2.38)

and

R^{a}=G^{a\bar{b}}\partial_{\bar{b}}R.

(2.39)

They are both sections of
{\cal L}^{-1}
. One has also the property

\Delta_{\bar{a}\bar{b}}=-{\rm i}\,{\rm e}^{-K}D_{\bar{a}}D_{\bar{b}}R.

(2.40)

The propagator
R^{k}
is closely related to the Griffiths invariant. To see this, we first note that the HAE (
2.17
) can be written as

\partial_{\bar{a}}\Delta_{ij}=-C_{ijk}\Delta_{\bar{a}}^{~k},

(2.41)

and one has

C_{ijk}R^{k}+\Delta_{ij}=d_{ij},

(2.42)

where
d_{ij}
are holomorphic functions. The relation (
2.42
) is similar to
(
2.29
), and as we will see, it gives a useful expression for the holomorphic limit of the real propagators.
As in the closed string case,
the derivatives of
R^{j}
,
R
can be written as polynomials in the propagators. It is convenient
to introduce the tilded real propagators
al

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{i}

\displaystyle=R^{i},

(2.43)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=R-K_{i}R^{i}.

Since
R^{i}
remains the same, we will omit the tilde. Then, one finds the relations
al

\displaystyle\partial_{i}R^{j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}^{j}\widetilde{R}+S^{jl}\left(C_{ilk}R^{k}-d_{il}\right)-s_{ik}^{j}R^{k}+d_{i}^{j},

(2.44)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\widetilde{R}

\displaystyle=\left(C_{ijk}R^{j}-d_{ik}\right)\widetilde{S}^{k}-h_{ij}R^{j}+d_{i},

where
d_{ij}
,
d_{i}^{j}
and
d_{i}
are holomorphic ambiguities and the

are the same quantities appearing in (

).

It is possible to obtain relations between the real topological string propagators and the closed string propagators. Indeed, by using (
2.16
),
we find

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{a}}R^{b}

\displaystyle={\rm i}{\rm e}^{K}G^{b\bar{c}}D_{\bar{a}}D_{\bar{c}}\overline{{\cal T}}-\overline{C}_{\bar{a}}^{bm}D_{m}{\cal T}=\partial_{\bar{a}}\left({\rm i}{\rm e}^{K}G^{b\bar{c}}D_{\bar{c}}\overline{{\cal T}}\right)-\partial_{\bar{a}}S^{bm}D_{m}{\cal T}

(2.45)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\bar{a}}\left\{{\rm i}{\rm e}^{K}G^{b\bar{c}}D_{\bar{c}}\overline{{\cal T}}-S^{bm}D_{m}{\cal T}+S^{b}{\cal T}\right\},

where we have used that

\partial_{\bar{a}}(D_{m}{\cal T})=G_{\bar{a}m}{\cal T},

(2.46)

since
{\cal T}
is holomorphic. A similar argument can be applied to
R
, and one finds in this way,

\displaystyle R^{k}

\displaystyle=-S^{km}\partial_{m}{\cal T}+\tilde{S}^{k}{\cal T}+{\rm i}\,{\rm e}^{K}G^{k\bar{c}}D_{\bar{c}}\overline{{\cal T}}+r^{k},

(2.47)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{S}^{m}\partial_{m}{\cal T}+2\tilde{S}{\cal T}+{\rm i}\,{\rm e}^{K}\left(\overline{{\cal T}}-K_{b}G^{b\bar{c}}D_{\bar{c}}\overline{{\cal T}}\right)+r.

In these expressions,
r^{k}
,
r
are holomorphic functions which
depend explicitly on the domain wall tension
{\cal T}
. For example, one can show that
the functions
r^{k}
satisfy

C_{ijk}r^{k}=-\partial_{ij}^{2}{\cal T}+s^{k}_{ij}\partial_{k}{\cal T}-h_{ij}{\cal T}+d_{ij}.

(2.48)

A similar connection between the real and the closed string propagators is implicit in the expressions for the former
found in
nw
in the big moduli space.

Let us now consider the holomorphic limit for the real topological string. In order to do this, we have to understand
first the holomorphic limit of Griffiths’ invariant. As explained in
walcher-bcov
, in a given frame
one has to choose
{\cal T}
in such a way that it vanishes at the appropriate base-point, together with all its holomorphic
derivatives. When this is the case, both
\overline{{\cal T}}
and
D_{\bar{a}}\overline{{\cal T}}
vanish in the holomorphic limit.
Therefore, the holomorphic limit of Griffiths’ invariant, which we will denote by
{\cal D}_{ij}
, is simply given by

{\cal D}_{ij}=D_{i}D_{j}{\cal T},

(2.49)

where the covariant derivatives are calculated in the holomorphic limit. In terms of holomorphic propagators, one has

{\cal D}_{ij}=\partial_{ij}^{2}{\cal T}-\left(C_{ijl}{\cal S}^{lk}-s^{k}_{ij}\right)\partial_{k}{\cal T}+\left(C_{ijk}\tilde{\cal S}^{k}-h_{ij}\right){\cal T}.

(2.50)

Let us now consider the holomorphic limit of the real propagators
R^{b}
,
\tilde{R}
in a given frame. We will use again calligraphic letters and
denote these limits by
{\cal R}^{b}
,
\tilde{\cal R}
. They satisfy the holomorphic limits of the various equations that we have considered.
For example, from (
2.45
) we find

\displaystyle{\cal R}^{k}

\displaystyle=-{\cal S}^{km}\partial_{m}{\cal T}+\tilde{\cal S}^{k}{\cal T}+r^{k},

(2.51)

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal R}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\cal S}^{m}\partial_{m}{\cal T}+2\tilde{\cal S}{\cal T}+r.

In adition, by taking the holomorphic limit of (
2.42
) we obtain,

C_{ijk}{\cal R}^{k}-d_{ij}=-{\cal D}_{ij}.

(2.52)

As we have emphasized many times, the original propagators are globally
defined but non-holomorphic. Their holomorphic limit
is frame dependent, and we would like to know how they transform under a change
of frame. This was a crucial ingredient in
gm-multi
;
gkkm

and we will need the corresponding generalization to the real case. We recall that a change of frame is implemented
by a change of symplectic basis in (
2.30
) and is therefore associated to a symplectic matrix

\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix},

(2.53)

where
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
are
(1+s)\times(1+s)
matrices which satisfy

A^{\rm T}D-C^{\rm T}B={\bf 1}_{s+1},\,\,\,\ A^{\rm T}C=C^{\rm T}A,\,\,\,\ B^{\rm T}D=D^{\rm T}B,

(2.54)

and
{\bf 1}_{s+1}
is the identity matrix of rank
s+1
. The matrix
\Gamma
acts on the periods as

\displaystyle X^{J}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=C^{JI}{\cal F}_{I}+D^{J}_{~I}X^{I},

(2.55)

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{J}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=A_{J}^{~I}{\cal F}_{I}+B_{JI}X^{I}.

One can show
hosono
;
gkkm
that the holomorphic shifted propagators in the frame defined by
\Gamma
are
related to the ones in the original frame by the equations,

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{kl\Gamma}

\displaystyle={\cal S}^{kl}-[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{IJ}\chi_{I}^{k}\chi_{J}^{l},

(2.56)

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal S}^{k\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\cal S}^{k}+[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{IJ}\chi_{I}^{k}h_{J},

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal S}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\cal S}-{1\over 2}[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{IJ}h_{I}h_{J},

where
\chi_{I}^{k}
,
h_{I}
were introduced in (
2.33
) and (
2.34
), respectively.

In the case of the real topological string there is an additional ingredient, since
the domain wall tension
{\cal T}
is in general
not
invariant under a change of frame.
The reason is that,
as we change the frame, we want to make sure that the domain wall tension in the new frame,
{\cal T}^{\Gamma}
,
as well as its holomorphic derivatives,
vanish at the appropriate base-point. The original domain wall tension
{\cal T}
(or its analytic continuation)
does not satisfy this property. In general,

{\cal T}^{\Gamma}
differs from
{\cal T}
by a (real) linear combination of periods, i.e.

{\cal T}^{\Gamma}={\cal T}+\alpha_{I}X^{I}+\beta^{I}{\cal F}_{I}.

(2.57)

Note that, since
X^{I}
,
{\cal F}_{I}
satisfy the homogeneous version of the Picard–Fuchs equation, both
{\cal T}
and
{\cal T}^{\Gamma}

solve the defining equation (
2.9
) for the domain wall tension.
We also note that the holomorphic functions
r^{k}
,
r
appearing in (
2.47
) will depend on the choice of frame through their dependence
on
{\cal T}
.

We will need the transformation properties of the real propagators under a change of frame specified by
(
2.55
) and (
2.57
). To derive these, we can first deduce the
transformation properties of the holomorphic Griffiths invariant (
2.50
), and then recall the relation (
2.52
) to derive the transformation
of
{\cal R}^{k}
. By using the first equation in (
2.44
) one finally obtains the transformation of
\tilde{\cal R}
. The final result is,

\displaystyle{\cal R}^{k\Gamma}-{\cal R}^{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{PJ}\partial_{P}{\cal T}^{\Gamma}-\beta^{J}\right\}\chi_{J}^{k},

(2.58)

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal R}^{\Gamma}-\tilde{\cal R}

\displaystyle=-\left\{[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{PJ}\partial_{P}{\cal T}^{\Gamma}-\beta^{J}\right\}h_{J}.

In deriving these equations it is useful to keep in mind that, since
{\cal T}
is a section of
{\cal L}
, it is a homogeneous function of

X^{I}
of degree one, and Euler’s theorem gives,

{\cal T}=X^{I}\partial_{I}{\cal T}.

(2.59)

As a consequence,

\chi^{a}_{I}\partial_{a}{\cal T}+h_{I}{\cal T}=\partial_{I}{\cal T}.

(2.60)

2.3
Direct integration

Let us now rewrite Walcher’s HAE in terms of closed and real propagators,
following
al
. Since the non-holomorphic dependence of the free energies is
contained in the propagators, one obtains the equations

\displaystyle{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial S^{jk}}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\sum\limits_{{\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=\chi-2}\atop{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\geq 0}}D_{j}G_{\chi_{1}}D_{k}G_{\chi_{2}}+D_{j}D_{k}G_{\chi-2},

(2.61)

\displaystyle{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial R^{i}}-K_{i}{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial\tilde{R}}

\displaystyle=-D_{i}G_{\chi-1},

as well as the constraint

{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial K_{i}}=0.

(2.62)

It is sometimes useful to use HAE for the different ingredients of the total free energy. For example,
the oriented open string amplitudes
F_{g,h}

satisfy
walcher-bcov

\partial_{\bar{a}}F_{g,h}={1\over 2}\overline{C}_{\bar{a}}^{jk}\sum_{(g_{1},h_{1})+(g_{2},h_{2})=(g,h)}D_{j}F_{g_{1},h_{1}}D_{k}F_{g_{2},h_{2}}+{1\over 2}\overline{C}_{\bar{a}}^{jk}D_{j}D_{k}F_{g-1,h}-R_{\bar{a}}^{j}D_{j}F_{g,h-1},

(2.63)

which leads to

\displaystyle{\partial F_{g,h}\over\partial S^{jk}}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\sum_{(g_{1},h_{1})+(g_{2},h_{2})=(g,h)}D_{j}F_{g_{1},h_{1}}D_{k}F_{g_{2},h_{2}}+{1\over 2}D_{j}D_{k}F_{g-1,h},

(2.64)

\displaystyle{\partial F_{g,h}\over\partial R^{i}}-K_{i}{\partial F_{g,h}\over\partial\tilde{R}}

\displaystyle=-D_{i}F_{g,h-1},

and the constraint

{\partial F_{g,h}\over\partial K_{i}}=0.

(2.65)

Similarly, one can write HAE for the non-orientable amplitudes separately.

We can now use the propagators to write explicit expressions for the free energies. Let us first consider the
exceptional case with
\chi=0
. In the case of the genus one free energy
F_{1}
, we can use the definition of the propagator to integrate (
2.20
) as

D_{i}F_{1}={1\over 2}C_{ijk}S^{jk}-\left({\chi\over 24}-1\right)K_{i}+f^{(1)}_{i}(z),

(2.66)

where
f^{(1)}_{i}(z)
is a holomorphic ambiguity. A similar formula can be obtained for the Klein bottle amplitude,

D_{i}{\cal K}_{0,0}={1\over 2}C_{ijk}S^{jk}-K_{i}+k^{(1)}_{i}(z),

(2.67)

where
k^{(1)}_{i}(z)
is the corresponding ambiguity.
Finally, for the annulus amplitude one obtains from (
2.21
), in terms of real propagators,

\partial_{j}F_{0,2}={1\over 2}C_{jkl}R^{k}R^{l}-d_{jk}R^{k}+f_{j}^{(0,2)}(z),

(2.68)

where
f_{j}^{(0,2)}(z)
is the holomorphic ambiguity. By adding all these results, we obtain a
useful formula for the derivative of the total
\chi=0
amplitude,

\partial_{j}G_{0}=C_{jlk}S^{kl}-{\chi\over 24}K_{j}+{1\over 2}C_{jkl}R^{l}R^{k}-d_{jk}R^{k}+g_{j}^{(0)}.

(2.69)

Let us note that, when acting on
G_{0}
,
D_{j}=\partial_{j}
. The result (
2.69
)
can be used in the HAE (
2.61
) to calculate all the
G_{\chi}
recursively, as polynomials in the
propagators, and up to holomorphic ambiguities. Let us work out the first case,
G_{1}
. It satisfies the equations

\displaystyle{\partial G_{1}\over\partial S^{ij}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{ij}=-C_{ijk}R^{k}+d_{ij},

(2.70)

\displaystyle{\partial G_{1}\over\partial R^{j}}-K_{j}{\partial G_{1}\over\partial\tilde{R}}

\displaystyle=-D_{j}G_{0}.

By using (
2.69
) the last equation can be split into two,

\displaystyle{\partial G_{1}\over\partial R^{k}}

\displaystyle=-C_{ijk}S^{ij}-{1\over 2}C_{kij}R^{i}R^{j}+d_{kj}R^{j}+g_{k}^{(0)},

(2.71)

\displaystyle{\partial G_{1}\over\partial\tilde{R}}

\displaystyle=-{\chi\over 24}.

By integrating these equations, we obtain

G_{1}=-S^{ij}\left(C_{ijk}R^{k}-d_{ij}\right)-{1\over 6}C_{ijk}R^{i}R^{j}R^{k}+{1\over 2}d_{ij}R^{i}R^{j}-{\chi\over 24}\tilde{R}+g_{k}^{(0)}R^{k}+g_{1},

(2.72)

where
g_{1}
is a new holomorphic ambiguity.

2.4
Fixing the holomorphic ambiguity

Integrating the HAE for the real topological
string requires fixing the holomorphic ambiguity at each
value of
\chi
. As in the closed string case, in order to do this it is helpful to know the behavior
of the real topological string amplitudes at special points in moduli space. Among these, perhaps the
most important one is the conifold locus. It has been known for some time that closed
topological string amplitudes
have a universal behavior at this locus, given by the
c=1
string
gv-conifold
. Let us
assume for simplicity that there is a single
modulus in the geometry, and let
t_{c}
be a vanishing flat coordinate at the conifold locus.
Then, in the conifold frame, the closed
string amplitudes have the following behavior,

{\cal F}_{g}^{c}(t_{c})={B_{2g}\over 2g(2g-2)}t_{c}^{2-2g}+{\cal O}(1),

(2.73)

where
B_{2g}
are Bernoulli numbers, and we have normalized
t_{c}
appropriately
(here and in the following we use inhomogeneous free energies,
which differ from the homogeneous ones in an appropriate power of
X^{0}
).
According to (
2.73
) there are no
subleading poles in the free energy near the conifold point. This gap condition was
emphasized in
hk06

and it was later realized that, in many toric CY manifolds, it makes it possible to fix the
holomorphic ambiguity at all genera
hkr
.

The behavior of the real topological string amplitudes near the conifold point was studied in

walcher-bcov
;
walcher-tadpole
;
kw-localp2
;
kw
.
It was found that all real topological string amplitudes
{\cal F}_{g,h}
,
{\cal K}_{g,h}
with
h\not=0
were regular at the
conifold point, except for
{\cal K}_{g,0}
, which in the conifold frame and near the conifold point behaves as

{\cal K}^{c}_{g-1,0}(t_{c})={\Psi_{g}\over t_{c}^{2g-2}}+{\cal O}(1).

(2.74)

A proposal for the value of the coefficient
\Psi_{g}
was made in
kw
, and
it is given by

\Psi_{g}={1\over 2^{2g+1}g(g-1)}\left\{-(2^{2g}-1)B_{2g}+gE_{2g-2}\right\},

(2.75)

where
B_{2g}
are Bernoulli numbers and
E_{2g-2}
are Euler numbers.
We conclude that
{\cal G}_{\chi}
has a gap behavior at the conifold
for
\chi
even,
and is regular for
\chi
odd. This behavior helps in fixing the holomorphic ambiguity, but in contrast to what happens in the closed
string case, it does not fix it completely, even in the local case, and one has to use further information.
We will discuss this in more detail in section
4
.

3
Trans-series solutions and resurgent structure

Our discussion of the real topological string has so far focused on its perturbative expansion.
Following
cesv1
;
cesv2
;
cms
;
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, we would like
to consider now
trans-series solutions
to Walcher’s HAE. These will be used to obtain information on the
non-perturbative sector of the real topological string, and in particular to find explicit multi-instanton amplitudes.

3.1
Master equation and warm-up example

In order to obtain a trans-series solution we have to re-express first the HAE as a “master equation” for the full free energy
G
. Due to
the special rôle of the
G_{\chi}
with
-2\leq\chi\leq 0
, we have to consider four different generating series, which we define as

\displaystyle G^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\geq-2}G_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi},\qquad\widetilde{G}^{(0)}=\sum_{\chi\geq-1}G_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{G}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\geq 0}G_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi},\qquad\overline{G}^{(0)}=\sum_{\chi\geq 1}G_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi}.

The superscript
(0)
indicates that these are all perturbative amplitudes, and the overline in the last series should not be confused
with complex conjugation. We will first consider the following trans-series ansatz to solve the HAE,

G=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}{\cal C}^{\ell}G^{(\ell)},

(3.2)

where
{\cal C}
is a formal parameter keeping track of the exponential weight, and

G^{(\ell)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\rm e}^{-\ell{\cal A}/g_{s}}G_{n}^{(\ell)}g_{s}^{n},\qquad\ell\geq 1.

(3.3)

We will often refer to
{\cal A}
as an “instanton action” and to the
G^{(\ell)}
as
\ell
-th instanton amplitudes (a more
general ansatz will be considered below, in section
3.4
).
Our goal is to find explicit expressions for the amplitudes
G^{(\ell)}
.
We define the trans-series analogue of
\widetilde{G}^{(0)}
as

\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{G}^{(0)}+\sum_{\ell\geq 1}{\cal C}^{\ell}G^{(\ell)},

(3.4)

and similarly for
\widehat{G}
and
\overline{G}
.

Before working out the general trans-series solution, it is instructive to solve a simple, particular case by hand, as it
was done originally in
cesv1
;
cesv2
. The simplest
situation is the one considered in
gm-multi
for closed topological strings, namely
toric or local CY manifolds with a single modulus.
In the local case, as first noted in
kz
, the HAE simplify substantially:
the holomorphic limit of
K_{a}
vanishes, and the closed string propagators
\tilde{\cal S}^{k}
,
\tilde{S}
can be
set to zero by an appropriate choice of the holomorphic functions appearing in the equations
(
2.28
). Similarly, one
can set to zero the holomorphic real topological string propagator
\tilde{\cal R}
by choosing
h_{ij}=d_{i}=0
, as required by the
second equation in (
2.44
). Therefore, if we are interested in the end in calculating holomorphic quantities, we can
set
\tilde{S}^{k}
,
\tilde{S}
and
\tilde{R}
to zero from the very beginning. Since we want to consider a
single modulus case, there are only two non-zero propagators, namely
S^{zz}
and
R^{z}
. Taking all this into account, the HAE (
2.61
) simplify to

\displaystyle{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial S^{zz}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{{\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}=\chi-2}\atop{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\geq 0}}D_{z}G_{\chi_{1}}D_{z}G_{\chi_{2}}+D^{2}_{z}G_{\chi-2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial R^{z}}

\displaystyle=-D_{z}G_{\chi-1}.

We first write down master equations for the perturbative series, and we then postulate that these equations are valid for
their trans-series counterparts. It is easy to see that one obtains in this way,

\displaystyle{\partial\overline{G}\over\partial S^{zz}}

\displaystyle={g_{s}^{2}\over 2}\left(D_{z}\widehat{G}\right)^{2}+g_{s}^{2}D_{z}^{2}\widetilde{G},

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\partial\overline{G}\over\partial R^{z}}

\displaystyle=-g_{s}D_{z}\widehat{G}.

We will focus in this section on the one-instanton amplitude. It satisfies the linearized master equations

\displaystyle{\partial G^{(1)}\over\partial S^{zz}}

\displaystyle=g_{s}^{2}D_{z}\widehat{G}^{(0)}D_{z}G^{(1)}+g_{s}^{2}D_{z}^{2}G^{(1)},

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\partial G^{(1)}\over\partial R^{z}}

\displaystyle=-g_{s}D_{z}G^{(1)}.

We now plug in these equations the ansatz (
3.3
) and we solve order by order in
g_{s}
.
We find that, as in the closed topological string case
cesv1
;
cesv2
,
{\cal A}
is independent of
the propagators. The next order gives the following equations for
G_{0}^{(1)}
:

\displaystyle{\partial G_{0}^{(1)}\over\partial S^{zz}}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{z}{\cal A})^{2}G_{0}^{(1)},

(3.8)

\displaystyle{\partial G_{0}^{(1)}\over\partial R^{z}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}{\cal A}\,G_{0}^{(1)}.

This can be immediately integrated to obtain

G_{0}^{(1)}=\exp\left((\partial_{z}{\cal A})^{2}S^{zz}+\partial_{z}{\cal A}\,R^{z}\right)g_{0}^{(1)},

(3.9)

where
g_{0}^{(1)}
is independent of the propagators, and it is the trans-series counterpart of the holomorphic ambiguity.
As first explained in
cesv1
;
cesv2
, this ambiguity is fixed by evaluating the trans-series in a special
frame, called the
{\cal A}
-frame. This is a frame in which
{\cal A}
is one of the
A
-periods. In this frame we impose
a fixed, simple form for the multi-instanton amplitude, which we will call, as in
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, a
boundary condition
.
We will discuss different boundary conditions in section
3.5
. In the case at hand, as we will explain below, the boundary condition
is simply

{\cal G}_{0,{\cal A}}^{(1)}=1,

(3.10)

where the subscript
{\cal A}
means evaluation in the
{\cal A}
-frame. On the other hand, from the above expression we find

{\cal G}_{0,{\cal A}}^{(1)}=\exp\left((\partial_{z}{\cal A})^{2}{\cal S}^{zz}_{\cal A}+\partial_{z}{\cal A}\,{\cal R}^{z}_{\cal A}\right)g_{0}^{(1)},

(3.11)

and this fixes
g_{0}^{(1)}
. We obtain in the end

G_{0}^{(1)}=\exp\left((\partial_{z}{\cal A})^{2}(S^{zz}-{\cal S}^{zz}_{\cal A})+\partial_{z}{\cal A}\,(R^{z}-{\cal R}^{z}_{\cal A})\right).

(3.12)

This procedure can be pushed to calculate
G^{(1)}
at higher orders. At next to leading order we find, for example,

\displaystyle G^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[(\partial_{z}\mathcal{A})^{2}(S^{zz}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}^{zz})+\partial_{z}\mathcal{A}(R^{z}-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}^{z})\right]\frac{\partial_{z}\mathcal{A}(S^{zz}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}^{zz})}{6}\Big[4C_{zzz}(\partial_{z}\mathcal{A})^{2}(S^{zz}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}^{zz})^{2}+

\displaystyle+6C_{zzz}(S^{zz}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}^{zz})(1+(\partial_{z}\mathcal{A})R^{z})+3C_{zzz}\left((R^{z}-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}^{z})^{2}+2\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}^{zz}+2(R^{z}-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}^{z})\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}^{z}+(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}^{z})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+6k_{z}^{(1)}(z)\Big].

(3.13)

The above expression is already quite complicated and pushing the calculation to higher orders immediately becomes cumbersome, although there is no conceptual difficulty. In addition,
the meaning of the multi-instanton amplitudes is not clear in this language.
For this reason, we will adopt the operator formalism
first discussed in
coms
;
codesido-thesis
.

3.2
Operator formalism

In order to solve the HAE for the trans-series of the real topological string,
we generalize the operator formalism introduced in
gm-multi
;
gkkm
. We have to
introduce first a generalized
U(1)
covariant derivative:

\mathfrak{d}_{\alpha}=(\mathfrak{d}_{0},\mathfrak{d}_{a}),

(3.14)

where

\mathfrak{d}_{0}=-g_{s}{\partial\over\partial g_{s}},\qquad\mathfrak{d}_{a}=\mathfrak{D}_{a}-K_{a}g_{s}{\partial\over\partial g_{s}}.

(3.15)

Here,
\mathfrak{D}_{a}
acts on a function of
z^{a}
, the closed and real propagators
S^{ab}
,
\tilde{S}^{a}
,
\tilde{S}
,
R^{i}
,
\tilde{R}
, and
K_{a}
, as

\mathfrak{D}_{a}f={\partial f\over\partial z^{a}}+\partial_{a}S^{de}{\partial f\over\partial S^{de}}+\partial_{a}\tilde{S}^{c}{\partial f\over\partial\tilde{S}^{c}}+\partial_{a}\tilde{S}{\partial f\over\partial\tilde{S}}+\partial_{a}R^{c}{\partial f\over\partial R^{c}}+\partial_{a}\tilde{R}{\partial f\over\partial\tilde{R}}+\partial_{a}K_{c}{\partial f\over\partial K_{c}},

(3.16)

where the derivatives of the propagators
are computed with the rules (
2.28
), (
2.44
).
The basic operator of the formalism is defined in such a way that, in the holomorphic limit,
it will become a derivative w.r.t. the
flat projective coordinates
X^{I}
. One defines
gkkm

\displaystyle T^{j}

\displaystyle=g_{s}\left\{-{\cal A}\left(\tilde{S}^{j}-\tilde{\cal S}^{j}_{\cal A}\right)+\partial_{m}{\cal A}(S^{mj}-{\cal S}_{\cal A}^{mj})\right\},

(3.17)

\displaystyle T^{0}

\displaystyle=g_{s}\left\{2{\cal A}(\tilde{S}-\tilde{\cal S}_{\cal A})-\partial_{m}{\cal A}(\tilde{S}^{m}-\tilde{\cal S}^{m}_{\cal A})\right\}-K_{j}T^{j}.

These are used to construct the operator
\mathsf{D}
as

\mathsf{D}=T^{\alpha}\mathfrak{d}_{\alpha}=T^{j}\mathfrak{d}_{j}+T^{0}\mathfrak{d}_{0},

(3.18)

and we will sometimes decompose it as

\mathsf{D}=\mathsf{D}_{0}+\mathsf{D}_{1},

(3.19)

where

\mathsf{D}_{0}=T^{0}\mathfrak{d}_{0},\qquad\mathsf{D}_{1}=T^{j}\mathfrak{d}_{j}.

(3.20)

It is also convenient to introduce

\tilde{T}^{0}=T^{0}+K_{j}T^{j}=g_{s}\left\{2{\cal A}(S-{\cal S}_{\cal A})-\partial_{m}{\cal A}(\tilde{S}^{m}-\tilde{\cal S}^{m}_{\cal A})\right\}.

(3.21)

We have the crucial relations
gkkm

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{i}T^{j}

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{ik}^{j}T^{k}-T^{0}\delta_{i}^{j},

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{i}T^{0}

\displaystyle=0,

and we note that

\mathfrak{d}_{i}\tilde{T}^{0}=-K_{i}\tilde{T}^{0}-\left(C_{ijk}\tilde{S}^{k}-h_{ij}\right)T^{j}.

(3.23)

We also introduce derivative operators w.r.t. the propagators, just as in
gkkm
:

\displaystyle\delta^{S}_{ij}

\displaystyle={1\over g_{s}^{2}}\left({\partial\over\partial S^{ij}}-K_{(i}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{j)}}+{1\over 2}K_{i}K_{j}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}}\right),

(3.24)

\displaystyle\delta^{S}_{0i}

\displaystyle=-{1\over g_{s}^{2}}\left({\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{i}}-K_{i}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}}\right),

\displaystyle\delta^{S}_{00}

\displaystyle={1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}}.

and we define

\omega_{S}=T^{i}T^{j}\delta^{S}_{ij}+T^{0}T^{i}\delta^{S}_{0i}+T_{0}^{2}\delta^{S}_{00},

(3.25)

as well as the operator

\mathsf{W}=\omega_{S}-\mathsf{D}\widehat{G}\,\mathsf{D},

(3.26)

where
\widehat{G}=\widehat{G}^{(0)}
is one of the formal series in (
3.1
).

We now write the HAE in terms of these operators. The equation for the dependence on the closed string propagators is
similar to what is obtained in the closed string case,

\mathsf{W}\overline{G}=\mathsf{D}G_{0}\mathsf{D}\widehat{G}-{1\over 2}\left(\mathsf{D}\widehat{G}\right)^{2}+\mathsf{D}^{2}\widetilde{G}.

(3.27)

In this equation,
\mathsf{D}^{2}\widetilde{G}
is a series in
g_{s}
and its first term has to be understood as

\mathsf{D}^{2}\left({G_{-1}\over g_{s}}\right)={1\over g_{s}}T^{i}T^{j}\mathfrak{D}_{i}\mathfrak{D}_{j}G_{-1}={1\over g_{s}}T^{i}T^{j}\Delta_{ij}.

(3.28)

Let us now consider the second equation in (
2.61
), involving the open string propagators.
By considering different powers of
K_{i}
, we obtain two different equations

\displaystyle{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial R^{i}}=-\mathfrak{D}_{i}G_{\chi-1},\qquad{\partial G_{\chi}\over\partial\tilde{R}}=(1-\chi)G_{\chi-1},

(3.29)

which in terms of generating functions read

{\partial\overline{G}\over\partial R^{i}}=-g_{s}\mathfrak{D}_{i}\widehat{G},\qquad{\partial\overline{G}\over\partial\tilde{R}}=g_{s}\mathfrak{d}_{0}\widehat{G}.

(3.30)

This suggests introducing the operator

\mathsf{R}={1\over g_{s}}\left(T^{j}{\partial\over\partial R^{j}}-\tilde{T}^{0}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}}\right).

(3.31)

This is the new ingredient in the operator formalism that we need in order to study the real
topological string. As in
gkkm
, we will require the operators to have zero charge w.r.t. the
U(1)
connection on
{\cal L}
. This is why we have included a factor of
g_{s}
in the r.h.s. of (
3.31
) (since
R^{i}
,
\tilde{R}
have charge one).
Then, the equations (
3.30
) can be written as

\mathsf{R}\overline{G}=-\mathsf{D}\widehat{G}.

(3.32)

We then have three operators:
\mathsf{R}
,
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{D}
, and we want to understand their commutator algebra.
One can immediately calculate

\displaystyle\left[{1\over g_{s}}{\partial\over\partial R^{k}},\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle={1\over g_{s}}\left(S^{jl}C_{ilk}-s^{j}_{ik}-K_{i}\delta_{k}^{j}\right){\partial\over\partial R^{j}}+{1\over g_{s}}\left(\tilde{S}^{j}C_{ijk}-h_{ik}\right){\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}},

(3.33)

\displaystyle\left[{1\over g_{s}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}},\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle={1\over g_{s}}\left({\partial\over\partial R^{i}}-K_{i}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}}\right),

and from this result one easily verifies that
\mathsf{R}
and
\mathsf{D}
commute:

\left[\mathsf{R},\mathsf{D}\right]=0.

(3.34)

To compute the other commutators some extra work is needed. To obtain the commutator between
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{D}
, we have to
calculate

\displaystyle\left[{1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial S^{ij}},\mathfrak{d}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=-{1\over g_{s}^{2}}\left(\Gamma_{ik}^{m}{\partial\over\partial S^{mj}}+\Gamma_{jk}^{m}{\partial\over\partial S^{mi}}\right)

(3.35)

\displaystyle+{1\over 2}\left(C_{ikb}\tilde{S}^{b}-h_{ik}\right){1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{j}}+{1\over 2}\left(C_{jkb}\tilde{S}^{b}-h_{jk}\right){1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{i}}

\displaystyle-{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}C_{ikm}K_{j}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}-{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}C_{jkm}K_{i}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}

\displaystyle+{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}\left(C_{kjm}R^{m}-d_{kj}\right){\partial\over\partial R^{i}}+{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}\left(C_{kim}R^{m}-d_{ki}\right){\partial\over\partial R^{j}},

as well as

\displaystyle\left[{1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{i}},\mathfrak{d}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle={2\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial S^{ij}}-{1\over g_{s}^{2}}\Gamma_{ij}^{m}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{m}}+\left(C_{ijk}\tilde{S}^{k}-h_{ij}\right){1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}}-{1\over g_{s}^{2}}C_{ijm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}

(3.36)

\displaystyle+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}\left(C_{ijp}R^{p}-d_{ij}\right){\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}},

\displaystyle\left[{1\over g_{s}^{2}}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}},\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle={2\over g_{s}^{2}}\left({\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}^{i}}-K_{i}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{S}}\right).

When the real propagators are set to zero one recovers the results of
gkkm
.
Let us introduce

\delta_{i}^{R}={\partial\over\partial R^{j}}-K_{j}{\partial\over\partial\tilde{R}}.

(3.37)

From here one obtains

\displaystyle\left[\delta^{S}_{ij},\mathfrak{d}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=-\Gamma^{m}_{ik}\delta^{S}_{jm}-\Gamma^{m}_{jk}\delta^{S}_{im}+{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}\left(C_{kim}R^{m}-d_{ki}\right)\delta_{j}^{R}+{1\over 2g_{s}^{2}}\left(C_{kjm}R^{m}-d_{kj}\right)\delta^{R}_{i},

(3.38)

\displaystyle\left[\delta^{S}_{0i},\mathfrak{d}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-2\delta^{S}_{ij}-\Gamma^{m}_{ij}\delta^{S}_{0m}+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}C_{ijm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}},

\displaystyle\left[\delta^{S}_{00},\mathfrak{d}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=-\delta^{S}_{0i},

which leads to

\left[\omega_{S},\mathfrak{d}_{k}\right]={1\over g_{s}}T^{i}\left(C_{ipk}R^{p}-d_{ik}\right)\mathsf{R}+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}T^{0}T^{i}C_{ikm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}.

(3.39)

In addition, we have from
gkkm

\omega_{S}(T^{\alpha})=T^{\alpha}\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right),

(3.40)

and one can also deduce

[\omega_{S},\mathsf{D}]=\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)\mathsf{D}+{1\over g_{s}}T^{i}T_{k}\left(C_{ipk}R^{p}-d_{ik}\right)\mathsf{R}+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}T^{0}T^{i}T^{j}C_{ijm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}.

(3.41)

We conclude that

[\mathsf{W},\mathsf{D}]=\mathsf{D}H_{0}\mathsf{D}+{1\over g_{s}}T^{i}T_{k}\left(C_{ipk}R^{p}-d_{ik}\right)\mathsf{R}+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}T^{0}T^{i}T^{j}C_{ijm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}},

(3.42)

where

H_{0}={{\cal A}\over g_{s}}+\mathsf{D}\widehat{G}.

(3.43)

This is the analogue of the functional
{\cal G}
introduced in
coms
;
gm-multi
;
gkkm
.

It is convenient to write (
3.42
) in a slightly different form. We introduce the quantity,

H_{1}=-\mathfrak{D}_{i}{\cal A}\left(R^{i}-{\cal R}^{i}_{\cal A}\right)+{\cal A}\left(\tilde{R}-\tilde{\cal R}_{\cal A}\right).

(3.44)

By using the result of
gkkm

\partial_{ij}^{2}{\cal A}=-\partial_{l}{\cal A}\left(C_{ijm}{\cal S}^{ml}_{\cal A}-s^{l}_{ij}\right)+{\cal A}\left(C_{ijm}\tilde{\cal S}^{m}_{\cal A}-h_{ij}\right)

(3.45)

one can show that

\mathsf{D}H_{1}=-{1\over g_{s}}T^{i}T^{k}\left(C_{ikp}R^{p}-d_{ik}\right),

(3.46)

therefore we can write (
3.42
) as

[\mathsf{W},\mathsf{D}]=\mathsf{D}H_{0}\,\mathsf{D}-\mathsf{D}H_{1}\,\mathsf{R}+{1\over g_{s}^{2}}T^{0}T^{i}T^{j}C_{ijm}{\partial\over\partial K_{m}}.

(3.47)

There are two other properties of
H_{1}
which will be needed:

\mathsf{D}^{2}\left({G_{-1}\over g_{s}}\right)=\mathsf{D}H_{1},\qquad\mathsf{R}\mathsf{D}G_{0}=-\mathsf{D}H_{1}.

(3.48)

Finally, we want to calculate the commutator of
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{R}
. By using that

[\omega_{S},\mathsf{R}]=\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)\mathsf{R},

(3.49)

one finds,

\left[\mathsf{W},\mathsf{R}\right]=\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)\mathsf{R}-\left(\mathsf{D}H_{1}+\mathsf{D}^{2}\widehat{G}\right)\mathsf{D}.

(3.50)

One of the key aspects of the operator formalism constructed in
coms
;
gm-multi
;
gkkm
is that, in the holomorphic limit,

\mathsf{D}
becomes indeed a derivative operator w.r.t. the flat coordinates of the CY. The more general
case was analyzed in
gkkm
. Let us write the
action
{\cal A}
as an integer linear combination of periods,

\kappa^{-1}{\cal A}=c^{J}{\cal F}_{J}+d_{J}X^{J}.

(3.51)

In this formula,
\kappa
is a universal normalization constant which depends on a choice of normalization for the string coupling constant, see
gkkm
for a detailed discussion. We will set it to one in most of the formulae that follow.
Let
f
be a homogeneous function of degree
n
in the big moduli space coordinates. Then, in the holomorphic limit, one has
gkkm

\mathsf{D}(g_{s}^{-n}f)\rightarrow g_{s}^{-n+1}c^{I}{\partial f\over\partial X^{I}}.

(3.52)

3.3
The one-instanton amplitude

We are now ready to use the formalism above to obtain the one-instanton amplitude in closed form. It satisfies the
linearized HAE, which in the operator language that we have just developed is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}G^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}^{2}G^{(1)},

(3.53)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}G^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{D}G^{(1)}.

To solve this equation we introduce an exponential ansatz, as in
gm-multi
,

G^{(1)}={\rm e}^{-\Phi}.

(3.54)

In terms of
\Phi
, the equations (
3.53
) read

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}\Phi

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}^{2}\Phi-\left(\mathsf{D}\Phi\right)^{2},

(3.55)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}\Phi

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{D}\Phi.

To solve these equations we consider

H=H_{0}+H_{1},

(3.56)

where
H_{0,1}
were defined in (
3.43
), (
3.44
), respectively.
One can easily show that it satisfies the equations

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}H

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}^{2}H,

(3.57)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}H

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{D}H.

In proving the first equation, it is useful to note that

\omega_{S}(\mathsf{D}G_{0})=\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)\mathsf{D}G_{0}+\mathsf{D}^{2}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right).

(3.58)

We now claim that (
3.55
) is solved by

\Phi=\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}H,

(3.59)

where
\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}
is the operator

\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{(-\lambda)^{k-1}\over k!}\mathsf{D}^{k-1}={1\over\lambda\mathsf{D}}\left(1-{\rm e}^{-\lambda\mathsf{D}}\right)={1\over\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}{\rm d}u,

(3.60)

and we will have to set

\lambda=2.

(3.61)

The check that (
3.59
) satisfies the second equation in (
3.55
) follows immediately from the second equation
in (
3.57
) and the commutation relation (
3.34
). To prove the first equation we have to work harder. We first
obtain the commutator of
\mathsf{W}
with
\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}
. As in

gm-multi
;
gkkm
, we calculate the commutator of
\mathsf{W}
with
{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}
with the help of Hadamard’s lemma,

{\rm e}^{A}B{\rm e}^{-A}=\sum_{n\geq 0}{1\over n!}[A,B]_{n},

(3.62)

where the iterated commutator
[A,B]_{n}
is defined by

[A,B]_{n}=[A,[A,B]_{n-1}],\qquad[A,B]_{0}=B.

(3.63)

In our case, we have the simple result that

[\mathsf{D},\mathsf{W}]_{n}=-\left(\mathsf{D}^{n}H_{0}\right)\mathsf{D}+\left(\mathsf{D}^{n}H_{1}\right)\mathsf{R}.

(3.64)

We conclude that

\mathsf{W}\,{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}={\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\left(\mathsf{W}-\sum_{k\geq 1}{u^{k}\over k!}\left(\left(\mathsf{D}^{k}H_{0}\right)\mathsf{D}-\left(\mathsf{D}^{k}H_{1}\right)\mathsf{R}\right)\right).

(3.65)

By using now the integral formula for
\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}
in (
3.60
), we find

\mathsf{W}\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}=\mathsf{O}_{\lambda}\mathsf{W}-{1\over\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm d}u\,{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\left\{\left[({\rm e}^{u\mathsf{D}}-1)H_{0}\right]\mathsf{D}-\left[({\rm e}^{u\mathsf{D}}-1)H_{1}\right]\mathsf{R}\right\}.

(3.66)

From this, and by taking into account the second equation in (
3.57
), one obtains

\mathsf{W}\Phi=\mathsf{D}^{2}\Phi-{1\over\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left[(1-{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}})H\right]\left[{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H\right].

(3.67)

On the other hand, we have that

\mathsf{D}\Phi=\sum_{k\geq 1}{(-\lambda)^{k-1}\over k!}\mathsf{D}^{k}H={1\over\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H{\rm d}u.

(3.68)

Its square can be computed as

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{D}\Phi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={1\over\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H\,{\rm d}u\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-v\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H\,{\rm d}v={2\over\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H\,{\rm d}u\int_{0}^{u}{\rm e}^{-v\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H{\rm d}v

(3.69)

\displaystyle={2\over\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left[{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\mathsf{D}H\right]\left[(1-{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}})H\right]\,{\rm d}u.

In going to the last line, instead of integrating the symmetric function in
v
,
u
over the square
[0,\lambda]^{2}
, we integrated it over the triangle below the diagonal, and multiplied the result by two. We conclude that

{1\over\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-u\mathsf{D}}\left\{\left[({\rm e}^{u\mathsf{D}}-1)H\right]\mathsf{D}H\right\}\,{\rm d}u={\lambda\over 2}\left(\mathsf{D}\Phi\right)^{2}.

(3.70)

Therefore,

\mathsf{W}\Phi=\mathsf{D}^{2}\Phi-{\lambda\over 2}\left(\mathsf{D}\Phi\right)^{2},

(3.71)

and by setting
\lambda=2
we obtain the sought-for result.

Since the solution to the HAE is expressed in terms of
H
, we have to calculate its holomorphic limit
{\cal H}
. To do
this it is convenient to introduce a modified prepotential or
\chi=-2
free energy as in
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, defined by

c^{I}{\partial\widetilde{\cal G}_{-2}\over\partial X^{I}}={\cal A}=c^{I}{\cal F}_{I}+d_{I}X^{I}.

(3.72)

It differs from the usual one by quadratic terms in
X^{I}
. Then, the holomorphic limit of
H_{0}
is

{\cal H}_{0}=g_{s}^{-1}c^{I}\partial_{I}\widetilde{\cal G}_{-2}+\sum_{\chi\geq 0}c^{I}\partial_{I}{\cal G}_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi+1}.

(3.73)

We should also note that, as found in
gkkm
, in the compact CY case, the genus one closed string free energy
{\cal F}_{1}
appearing here is in fact given by

{\cal F}_{1}-\left({\chi\over 24}-1\right)\log X^{0}.

(3.74)

The only thing that remains to do is to calculate the holomorphic limit of
H_{1}
, which will be denoted by
{\cal H}_{1}
. By using
(
2.58
) we obtain

{\cal H}_{1}=[(C\tau+D)^{-1}C]^{PJ}\partial_{P}{\cal T}^{\Gamma}\left(\partial_{i}{\cal A}\chi^{i}_{J}+{\cal A}h_{J}\right)-\beta^{J}\left(\partial_{i}{\cal A}\chi^{i}_{J}+{\cal A}h_{J}\right),

(3.75)

where

c^{I}=C^{1I},\qquad d_{I}=D^{1}_{~I},

(3.76)

since we regard
{\cal A}=X^{1}_{\cal A}
as the first coordinate in the
{\cal A}
-frame. We now use

\partial_{i}{\cal A}\chi^{i}_{J}+{\cal A}h_{J}=c^{P}\tau_{PJ}+d_{J}

(3.77)

to obtain

{\cal H}_{1}=c^{P}\partial_{P}{\cal T}^{\Gamma}-\beta^{J}c^{p}\tau_{PJ}-\beta^{J}d_{J}=c^{P}\partial_{P}\left({\cal T}^{\Gamma}-\beta^{J}{\cal F}_{J}\right)-\beta^{J}d_{J},

(3.78)

or equivalently,

{\cal H}_{1}=c^{P}\partial_{P}\left({\cal T}+\alpha_{J}X^{J}\right)-\beta^{J}d_{J}.

(3.79)

This can be used to define a new
\tilde{\cal G}_{-1}
as

{\cal H}_{1}=c^{P}\partial_{P}\tilde{\cal G}_{-1},

(3.80)

which differs from
{\cal G}_{-1}={\cal T}
in linear terms in the
X^{I}
. We then consider a redefined total free energy,

\tilde{\cal G}={1\over g_{s}^{2}}\tilde{\cal G}_{-2}+{1\over g_{s}}\tilde{\cal G}_{-1}+\sum_{\chi\geq 0}{\cal G}_{\chi}g_{s}^{\chi},

(3.81)

in such a way that

{\cal H}=g_{s}c^{I}\partial_{I}\tilde{\cal G}.

(3.82)

We conclude that the holomorphic limit of
\Phi
is

{1\over 2}\left(\tilde{\cal G}(X^{I})-\tilde{\cal G}(X^{I}-2g_{s}c^{I})\right),

(3.83)

and the holomorphic one-instanton amplitude is given by

{\cal G}^{(1)}=\exp\left[{1\over 2}\left(\tilde{\cal G}(X^{I}-2g_{s}c^{I})-\tilde{\cal G}(X^{I})\right)\right].

(3.84)

This is very similar to the result in
gm-multi
;
gkkm
: the one-instanton amplitude is obtained by
shifting the flat coordinates by an integer multiple of the string coupling constant (up to normalization). To illustrate the above result let us list the first few orders in the
g_{s}
expansion of the one-instanton amplitude (
3.84
). We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(1)}=\text{e}^{-\mathcal{A}/g_{s}}\exp\left[c^{I}c^{J}\tau_{IJ}-\mathcal{H}_{1}\right]\left(1+g_{s}\Upsilon_{1}+g_{s}^{2}\left(\Upsilon_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Upsilon_{1}^{2}\right)+\dots\right)

(3.85)

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}c^{I}c^{J}c^{K}C_{IJK}+c^{I}c^{J}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal T}}{\partial X^{I}\partial X^{J}}-c^{I}\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}_{0}}{\partial X^{I}}+\frac{c^{0}}{X^{0}}\left(\frac{\chi}{24}-1\right),

(3.86)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}c^{I}c^{J}c^{K}c^{L}\frac{\partial^{4}\mathcal{G}_{-2}}{\partial X^{I}\partial X^{J}\partial X^{K}\partial X^{L}}-\frac{2}{3}c^{I}c^{J}c^{K}\frac{\partial^{3}{\cal T}}{\partial X^{I}\partial X^{J}\partial X^{K}}+

\displaystyle+c^{I}c^{J}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{G}_{0}}{\partial X^{I}\partial X^{J}}+\frac{(c^{0})^{2}}{(X^{0})^{2}}\left(\frac{\chi}{24}-1\right),

(3.87)

where we have denoted the second derivative of the modified prepotential as

\tau_{IJ}={\partial^{2}\widetilde{\cal G}_{-2}\over\partial X^{I}\partial X^{J}}.

(3.88)

So far we have just considered one-instanton solutions satisfying the boundary condition
{\cal G}_{\cal A}^{(1)}=1
. We will now consider multi-instanton solutions with more general boundary conditions.

3.4
Multi-instantons

In analogy to the closed topological string, following
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, we can derive HAEs for the
partition function of the real topological string, and use them to compute multi-instanton contributions. For this purpose we introduce

Z=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}\right],\quad Z^{(0)}=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\right],\quad Z_{\text{r}}=\frac{Z}{Z^{(0)}}.

(3.89)

We will call
Z_{\rm r}
the reduced partition function.
Notice the additional factor of
1/2
in comparison to the closed string case discussed in
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, which is necessary in order to find linear equations for the reduced partition function. These equations read,

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}Z_{\text{r}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}^{2}Z_{\text{r}},

(3.90)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}Z_{\text{r}}

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{D}Z_{\text{r}}.

We use a trans-series ansatz for
Z_{\rm r}
with two trans-series parameters
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
,
as in
gm-multi
;

3.5
Boundary conditions

To obtain boundary conditions for the trans-series we follow

;
cesv2
;

;
gkkm
and we consider the
behavior of the theory near special points, like the conifold point and the large radius point. This behavior can be immediately translated
in terms of a large order behavior for the amplitudes at large genus, which leads in turn to multi-instanton amplitudes.
For simplicity, we will assume that we are in a situation with a single modulus.

Let us first consider the conifold point. In the case of the conventional closed topological string, we have the behavior (
2.73

The formula for the Bernoulli numbers

B_{2g}=(-1)^{g-1}{2(2g)!\over(2\pi)^{2g}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\ell^{-2g}

(3.114)

where,

{\cal A}_{c}=2\pi{\rm i}t_{c},\qquad\mu_{0,\ell}={{\cal A}_{c}\over\ell},\qquad\mu_{1,\ell}={1\over\ell^{2}}.

(3.115)

According to the correspondence between large order behavior and exponentially small corrections (see e.g.
mmbook
),
(
3.114
) corresponds to an
\ell
-th instanton amplitude of the Pasquetti–Schiappa form

,

{1\over 2\pi}\left({1\over\ell}{{\cal A}_{c}\over g_{s}}+{1\over\ell^{2}}\right){\rm e}^{-\ell{\cal A}_{c}/g_{s}}.

(3.116)

We recall that, since the original series is even in
g_{s}
, we will have a trans-series with action
-{\cal A}_{c}
as well. They both combine to
give the asymptotic behavior (
3.114
). In general, in the discussion below we have to consider as well the trans-series with opposite action
5
5

5

The asymptotic series for the real topological string free energy is obviously not even in
g_{s}
. However, the boundary conditions associated to the conifold point involve both actions
\pm{\cal A}
.

In the case of the real topological string, we have an additional term in
{\cal G}_{\chi}
when

is even, given by (
2.74

2.75

(3.117)

The second term in (

The analysis of the conifold and large radius behavior indicate that there are two types of trans-series appearing

The first one is associated to the boundary conditions (

Let us consider the trans-series associated to the conifold behavior. They are multi-instanton amplitudes corresponding to the

5
Conclusions

In this paper we have initiated the study of the resurgent structure of Walcher’s real topological string. By generalizing
the ideas of
cesv1
;
cesv2
and the operator formalism of
gm-multi
;
gkkm
, we have obtained multi-instanton amplitudes of the real
topological string as trans-series solutions of Walcher’s HAE. The resulting resurgent
structure is slightly more complicated than in the closed string case, since
the boundary conditions coming from the large radius and the conifold behavior lead to
different trans-series. However, as in the closed string case,
the multi-instanton amplitudes are essentially obtained by an integer shift of the closed
string background, while the D-brane and orientifold background is unaffected.

We find it remarkable that the operator formalism of
gm-multi
;
gkkm

can be extended naturally to the real case. The underlying reason might be that, as shown in

ooguri-real
;
nw
, one can relate the solutions of Walcher’s extended HAE, to solutions of the HAE of
bcov

for the closed topological string. Perhaps
the observations of
ooguri-real
;
nw
lead to a simpler derivation of the operator formalism obtained in this paper.

The full resurgent structure of the real topological string involves additional Stokes constants,
as compared to the closed string case. It is natural to
conjecture that the new Stokes constants are related to the counting of BPS states in the presence
of D-branes and orientifold planes. Concrete evidence for this connection has been obtained
in section
3.5
, where we found that the integer invariants counting disks indeed appear as
Stokes constants in the resurgent structure. More generally, our results indicate that the
theory of Donaldson–Thomas invariants underlying BPS counting has a natural extension to the real case.
It would be very interesting to develop these observations
further, and to find a wall-crossing interpretation for the new Stokes automorphism formula (
3.138
), in the
spirit of
KS
.
Another interesting direction is the study of the real topological string on compact CYs from the point of view of resurgence.
In this endeavour, a better understanding of the free energies at high Euler characteristic
would be very useful, and it is clearly an interesting problem in itself.

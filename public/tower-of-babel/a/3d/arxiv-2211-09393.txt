The ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2} -graded dimensions of the free Jordan superalgebra J ⁡ ( D 1 | D 2 ) J(D_{1}|D_{2})
Shikui Shang

Abstract
Let k k be a field of characteristic 0 0 . For a superspace V = V 0 ¯ ⊕ V 1 ¯ V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}} over k k , we call the vector ( dim k V 0 ¯ , dim k V 1 ¯ ) (\dim_{k}V_{\bar{0}},\dim_{k}V_{\bar{1}}) the ( ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2} -)graded dimension of V V . Let J ⁡ ( D 1 | D 2 ) J(D_{1}|D_{2}) be the free Jordan superalgebra generated by D 1 D_{1} even generators and D 2 D_{2} odd generators. In this paper, we study the graded dimensions of the n n -components of J ⁡ ( D 1 | D 2 ) J(D_{1}|D_{2}) and find the connection between them and the homology of Tits-Allison-Gao Lie superalgebra of J ⁡ ( D 1 | D 2 ) J(D_{1}|D_{2}) following the method given by I.Kashuba and O.Mathieu in [ 14 ] , where they deal with the free Jordan algebra. And, four interesting conjectures of above contents are proposed in our paper.

Introduction

Superspaces and superalgebras appear naturally in theoretical physics and mathematics(
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
). Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
. A
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded vector
space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
over
k
is called a superspace. We define its
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded dimension by
\overline{\dim}V=(\dim V_{\bar{0}},\dim V_{\bar{1}})
if both
V_{\bar{0}}
and
V_{\bar{1}}
are finite-dimensional and also call it the graded dimension for simplicity. Denote the degree or parity of a homogeneous element
v\in V_{\alpha}
by
|v|
for
\alpha\in\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
.

A superalgebra
A
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded algebra
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
. The elements in
A_{\bar{0}}
are called even and the elements in
A_{\bar{1}}
are called odd. It is well-known that if
V
is a superspace, then

becomes a Lie superalgebra.

In the mid-1970s, V.Kac in
[
9

introduced the concept of Jordan superalgebras as the generalization of Jordan algebra for super case. Let
J=J_{\bar{0}}\oplus J_{\bar{1}}
be a superalgebra over
k
with a product
\cdot
.
J
is call a Jordan superalgebra if it satisfies the following two identities for any homogeneous
x,y,z\in J

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle(\text{super commutativity})x\cdot y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\cdot x=0,

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle(\text{super Jordan identity)}

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x||z|}[L_{x\cdot y},L_{z}]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[L_{y\cdot z},L_{x}]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[L_{z\cdot x},L_{y}]=0

where
L_{x}
is an endomorphism of
J
with
L_{x}(y)=x\cdot y
and the bracket is taken in the Lie superalgebra
gl(J)
. Some theories of simple and semisimple finite dimensional Jordan superalgebras are studied in
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
25
]
.

For any

, the free Jordan algebra

has been investigated in many papers by the Novosibirsk school of algebra, e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
24
]
,
[

.

Recently, the dimension of the

is studied using Lie algebra homology theory by I. Kashuba and O. Mathieu in paper

The theory of Jordan algebras is very closed associative with the theory of

]

]

In detail, for

sl_{2}(k)
and

, let

be the simple module of

with dimension

sl_{2}
-module

, denote

the sum of all submodules which are isomorphic to

and

{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
of Lie superalgebras

2
The Tits-Allison-Gao functor and Tits functor for super case

2.1
The TKK-construction

Let
J=J_{\bar{0}}\oplus J_{\bar{1}}
be a Jordan superalgebra over
k
which is not necessarily unital.

gl(J)
is the Lie superalgebra of endomorphisms on
J
. For
\alpha\in\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
,
D\in gl(J)_{\alpha}
is called a (super-)derivation of
J
of degree
|D|=\alpha
, if

D(xy)=D(x)\cdot y+(-1)^{|D||x|}x\cdot D(y)

for any homogeneous
x,y\in J
and denote
D\in\text{Der}(J)_{\alpha}
. Set the Lie superalgebra

\text{Der}(J)=\text{Der}(J)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\text{Der}(J)_{\bar{1}}.

By the definition of Jordan superalgebras,

\partial_{x,y}:=[L_{x},L_{y}]=L_{x}L_{y}-(-1)^{|x||y|}L_{y}L_{x}\in\text{Der}(J)_{|x|+|y|}.

The linear combinations of
\partial_{x,y}
are called the inner derivations of
J
, and denote the subspace
\text{Inn}(J)
of inner derivations. Since for any homogeneous
D\in\text{Der}(J)
and
x,y\in J
,

[D,\partial_{x,y}]=\partial_{D(x),y}+(-1)^{|D||x|}\partial_{x,D(y)},

\text{Inn}(J)
is a graded ideal of
\text{Der}(J)
.

Tits-Kantor-Koecher Construction:

The Tits-Kantor-Koecher algebra
{\text{TKK}}(J)
of a Jordan superalgebra
J
is defined as follows. As a vector space

{\text{TKK}}(J)=\text{Inn}(J)\oplus(sl_{2}(k)\otimes J)

with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading

{\text{TKK}}(J)_{\alpha}=\left\{\sum_{i}\partial_{x_{i},y_{i}}\ |\ |x_{i}|+|y_{i}|=\alpha\right\}\oplus(sl_{2}(k)\otimes J_{\alpha})

for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Let
\kappa:sl_{2}(k)\times sl_{2}(k)\rightarrow k
be the Killing form on
sl_{2}(k)
. The bracket of
{\text{TKK}}(J)
is given such that
\text{Inn}(J)
becomes a subalgebra of
{\text{TKK}}(J)
and for
a,b\in sl_{2}(k),x,y,z\in J,D\in\text{Inn}(J)

\displaystyle 1.\ \ \ \ [a\otimes x,b\otimes y]=\frac{1}{2}\kappa(a,b)\partial_{x,y}+[a,b]\otimes(x\cdot y),

\displaystyle 2.\ \ \ \ [D,a\otimes z]=a\otimes D(z).

It is proved in §4.4 of
[
4
]
that
{\text{TKK}}(J)
is a Lie superalgebra over
k
. However,
TKK
is not functorial since the notion of inner
derivations is not functorial.

2.2
The Lie superalgebra
{\text{TAG}}(J)

Following
[
14
]
, we introduce the Tits-Allison-Gao construction of a Jordan superalgebra
J
, which provides a refinement of the
TKK
-construction and was first studied in the paper
[
1
]
.

Let
J=J_{\bar{0}}\oplus J_{\bar{1}}
be a Jordan superalgebra. Then,
J\otimes_{k}J
is a superspace with the canonical
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading. Set
R^{s}(J)
is the subspace of
J\otimes_{k}J
spanned by all homogeneous elements of forms

\displaystyle I_{x,y}=x\otimes y+(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x,

\displaystyle I_{x,y,z}=(-1)^{|x||z|}(x\cdot y)\otimes z+(-1)^{|y||x|}(y\cdot z)\otimes x+(-1)^{|z||y|}(z\cdot x)\otimes y,

where
x,y,z
run over the homogeneous elements in
J
. And, set the superspace

{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)=(J\otimes_{k}J)/R^{s}(J),

and denote the canonical image of
x\otimes y
in
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
by
\{x\otimes y\}
.

Lemma 2.1

The
k
-linear map

\varphi:{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)\rightarrow\text{Inn}(J),\{x\otimes y\}\mapsto\partial_{x,y}

are surjective.

Proof.
Since
\partial_{x,y}
are bilinear in
x,y
and
\text{Inn}(J)
is spanned by
\partial_{x,y}
,

J\otimes J\rightarrow\text{Inn}(J),x\otimes y\mapsto\partial_{x,y}
are well-defined and surjective.

By the definition of Jordan superalgebras,
R^{s}(J)
is contained in the kernel of the above map. Hence, we have the surjective map

\varphi:{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)\rightarrow\text{Inn}(J),\{x\otimes y\}\mapsto\partial_{x,y}
.
\Box

The superspace
J\otimes_{k}J
is a graded
\text{Inn}(J)
-module induced by the natural action of
\text{Inn}(J)
on
J
, i.e.,

\partial_{x,y}(z\otimes w)=\partial_{x,y}(z)\otimes w+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|}z\otimes\partial_{x,y}(w).

Lemma 2.2

R^{s}(J)
is a graded submodule of
J\otimes_{k}J
and the quotient
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
is also an
\text{Inn}(J)
-module.

Proof.
Using direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x,y}(I_{z,w})=I_{\partial_{x,y}(z),w}+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|}I_{z,\partial_{x,y}(w)},

\displaystyle\partial_{x,y}(I_{u,v,w})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|w|}I_{\partial_{x,y}(u),v,w}+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|u|}I_{u,\partial_{x,y}(v),w}+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|v|}I_{u,v,\partial_{x,y}(w)},

for homogeneous
x,y,z,u,v,w\in J
.

Hence,
R^{s}(J)
is invariant under the action of
\text{Inn}(J)
, and
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)=(J\otimes_{k}J)/R^{s}(J)
is the quotient
\text{Inn}(J)
-module.
\Box

We define a bracket product on
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
by

[\{x\otimes y\},\{z\otimes w\}]=\partial_{x,y}(\{z\otimes w\}).

We claim that
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
is a Lie superalgebra over
k
with such bracket. The following lemma is needed,

Lemma 2.3

Let
M
be a graded module over a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\lambda:M\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism. Then,

A(M)=\text{span}\{\lambda(m).n+(-1)^{|m||n|}\lambda(n).m\ |\ m,n\text{ homogeneous in }M\}

is a submodule of
M
satisfying
\text{Im}(\lambda).\ker(\lambda)\subseteq A(M)\subseteq\ker(\lambda)
. Moreover, the quotient module
Q=M/A(M)
forms a Lie superalgebra relative to the product
[p,q]=\lambda(p).q
for
p,q\in Q
and the map
\mu:M/A(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
induced by
\lambda
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.
The proof is parallel to the Lie algebra version of Lemma 3.1 in
[
22
]
.
\Box

Corollary 2.1

{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
is a Lie superalgebra over
k
and
\varphi:{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)\rightarrow\text{Inn}(J)
is a central extension of Lie superalgebras.

Proof.
Applied Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we only need to show that
A({\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J))=0
.
Its verification is straightforward as follows. For homogeneous
x,y,z,w\in J
, in
J\otimes_{k}J
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x,y}(z\otimes w)+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)(|z|+|w|)}\partial_{z,w}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\cdot z)\otimes w-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\cdot(x\cdot z)\otimes w+

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|}z\otimes x\cdot(y\cdot w)-(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|+|x||y|}z\otimes y\cdot(x\cdot w)+

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)(|z|+|w|)}z\cdot(w\cdot x)\otimes y-(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)(|z|+|w|)+|z||w|}w\cdot(z\cdot x)\otimes y

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-1)^{|y|(|z|+|w|)}x\otimes z\cdot(w\cdot y)-(-1)^{|y||z|+|y||w|+|z||w|}x\otimes w\cdot(z\cdot y)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(|x|+|y|+|z|)|w|}(w\cdot x)\otimes(y\cdot z)+(-1)^{(|x|+|y|+|z|)|w|+|x||y|}(w\cdot y)\otimes(x\cdot z)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|x|(|y|+|z|+|w|)+|z||w|}(y\cdot w)\otimes(z\cdot x)+(-1)^{(|y|+|z|)|w|+|y||z|}(x\cdot w)\otimes(z\cdot y)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0(\mod R^{s}(J)).\ \ \ \ \Box

Tits-Allison-Gao Construction:
For a Jordan superalgebra
J
,
the Tits-Allison-Gao algebra
{\text{TAG}}(J)
is defined as the a vector space

{\text{TAG}}(J)={\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)\oplus(sl_{2}(k)\otimes J)

with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading

{\text{TAG}}(J)_{\alpha}=\left\{\sum_{i}\{x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\}\ |\ |x_{i}|+|y_{i}|=\alpha\right\}\oplus(sl_{2}(k)\otimes J_{\alpha})

for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and the bracket product

\displaystyle 1.\ \ \ \ [a\otimes x,b\otimes y]=\frac{1}{2}\kappa(a,b)\{x\otimes y\}+[a,b]\otimes(x\cdot y),

\displaystyle 2.\ \ \ \ [\{x\otimes y\},a\otimes z]=a\otimes\partial_{x,y}(z),

\displaystyle 3.\ \ \ \ [\{x\otimes y\},\{z\otimes w\}]=\partial_{x,y}(\{z\otimes w\}),

for
a,b\in sl_{2}(k),x,y,z,w\in J
.

Theorem 2.1

{\text{TAG}}(J)
is a Lie superalgebra over
k
for Jordan superalgebra
J
.

Proof.
In Corollary
2.1
, we have seen that
{\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J)
is a Lie superalgebra, which is a subalgebra of
{\text{TAG}}(J)
.
It is routine to check the rest cases for elements in
{\text{TAG}}(J)
satisfy the super commutativity and super Jordan identity.
\Box

2.3
The Category
{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
and Tits functor
T

Let
T
be the category of
sl_{2}(k)
-modules
M
such that
M=M^{sl_{2}}\oplus M^{\text{ad}}
. In this paper, weights of an
sk_{2}(k)
-module
M
always mean the eigenvalues of
h\in sl_{2}(k)
.

Let
{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
be the category of Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
in
T
on which
sl_{2}(k)
acts as even derivations. Particularly, both
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
are
sl_{2}(k)
-submodules.

Let
J
be a Jordan super algebra over
k
. The algebra
{\text{TKK}}(J)
and
{\text{TAG}}(J)
are objects in
{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
, where

{\text{TKK}}(J)^{sl_{2}}=\text{Inn}(J),\ \ \ {\text{TKK}}(J)^{\text{ad}}=sl_{2}(k)\otimes J

and

{\text{TAG}}(J)^{sl_{2}}={\mathcal{B}}^{s}(J),\ \ \ {\text{TAG}}(J)^{\text{ad}}=sl_{2}(k)\otimes J.

Let
\{e,h,f\}
be the usual basis o
sl_{2}(k)
. For a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
, set

T({\mathfrak{g}})=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\ |\ h.x=2x\}.

We define a new product
\cdot
between two elements
x,y\in T({\mathfrak{g}})
by

x\cdot y=\frac{1}{2}[x,f.y]\in T({\mathfrak{g}}).

Theorem 2.2

T({\mathfrak{g}})
is a Jordan superalgebra over
k
with above dot product and
T
is a functor from the category
{\text{\bf Liesup}_{\text{T}}}
to the category
Jorsup
of Jordan superalgebra over
k
.

Proof.
We only need to show
T({\mathfrak{g}})
is a Jordan superalgebra since it is obvious that
T
is functorial. It follows the proof of Proposition 1 in
[
23
]
for Tits functor on Lie algebras.

Since the weights of
\mathfrak{g}
are not greater than
2
and
sl_{2}(k)
acts as even derivations on
\mathfrak{g}
, for
x,y\in T({\mathfrak{g}})
, we have that
[x,y]=0
and

x\cdot y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\cdot x=\frac{1}{2}([x,f.y]+[f.x,y])=f.[x,y]=0.

Hence, the super commutativity holds.

Next, we check the super-Jordan identity. For
x\in J
, using the notations

e(x)=x,\ \ h(x)=-f.e(x),\ \ f(x)=\frac{1}{2}f.h(x),

we have that
a.b(x)=[a,b](x)
and
|a(x)|=|x|
for any
a,b\in sl_{2}(k)
and
x\in J
.

Then, for any
x,y\in J
,

[h(x),e(y)]=-[f.e(x),e(y)]=[e(x),f.e(y)]=2e(x\cdot y)

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle[e(x),f(y)]=\frac{1}{2}[e(x),f.h(y)]=\frac{1}{2}\left(f.[e(x),h(y)]-[f.e(x),h(y)]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-f.[e(x),f.e(y)]-[f.e(x),h(y)]\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-2f.e(x\cdot y)+[h(x),h(y)]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(x\cdot y)+\frac{1}{2}[h(x),h(y)].

(2.2)

Moreover,
e.[e(x),f(y)]=-2e(x\cdot y)+\frac{1}{2}e.[h(x),h(y)]
.

On the other hand, we see that

e.[e(x),f(y)]=[e(x),e.f(y)]=[e(x),h(y)]=-2e(x\cdot y).

Comparing with above equation, one has that
e.[h(x),h(y)]=0
and
[h(J),h(J)]\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{sl_{2}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle[h(x),f(y)]=-[f.e(x),f(y)]=-f.[e(x),f(y)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f.(h(x\cdot y)+\frac{1}{2}[h(x),h(y)])=-f.h(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2f(x\cdot y).

(2.3)

For
x,y,z\in J
, by Equation (2.2) and super-commutativity of
\cdot
,

\displaystyle[[e(x),f(y)],h(z)]=[e(x),[f(y),h(z)]]+(-1)^{|y||z|}[[e(x),h(z)],f(y)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2([e(x),f(y\cdot z)]-(-1)^{|y||z|}[e(x\cdot z),f(y)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h(x\cdot(y\cdot z))-(-1)^{|y||z|}(x\cdot z)\cdot y))

\displaystyle+[h(x),h(y\cdot z)]-(-1)^{|y||z|}[h(x\cdot z),h(y)].

Hence, by Equation (2.2),

\displaystyle[h(x\cdot y),h(z)]=\left[[e(x),f(y)]-\frac{1}{2}[h(x),h(y)],h(z)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[e(x),f(y)],h(z)]-\frac{1}{2}[[h(x),h(y)],h(z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h(x\cdot(y\cdot z))-(-1)^{|y||z|}(x\cdot z)\cdot y))

\displaystyle+[h(x),h(y\cdot z)]-(-1)^{|y||z|}[h(x\cdot z),h(y)]-\frac{1}{2}[[h(x),h(y)],h(z)].

(2.4)

Acting the even permutations in the cyclic group
C_{3}
on
\{x,y,z\}
in Equation (2.4) and using the super commutativity, we obtain that

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x||z|}[h(x\cdot y),h(z)]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[h(y\cdot z),h(x)]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[h(z\cdot x),h(y)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h((-1)^{|x||z|}x\cdot(y\cdot z)-(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|}(x\cdot z)\cdot y

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|y||x|}y\cdot(z\cdot x)-(-1)^{(|y|+|z|)|x|}(y\cdot x)\cdot z

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|z||y|}z\cdot(x\cdot y)-(-1)^{|(z|+|x|)|y|}(z\cdot y)\cdot x)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|x||z|}[h(x),h(y\cdot z)]-(-1)^{(|x|+|y|)|z|}[h(x\cdot z),h(y)]

Abstract
We consider the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian among a Poissonian cloud of hard spherical obstacles of fixed radius in large boxes of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . In a large box of side-length 2 ​ ℓ 2\ell centered at the origin, the lowest eigenvalue is known to be typically of order ( log ℓ ) − 2 / d (\log\ell)^{-2/d} . We show here that with probability arbitrarily close to 1 1 as ℓ \ell goes to infinity, the spectral gap stays bigger than σ ​ ( log ⁡ ℓ ) − ( 1 + 2 / d ) \sigma(\log\ell)^{-(1+2/d)} , where the small positive number σ \sigma depends on how close to 1 1 one wishes the probability. Incidentally, the scale ( log ⁡ ℓ ) − ( 1 + 2 / d ) (\log\ell)^{-(1+2/d)} is expected to capture the correct size of the gap. Our result involves the proof of new deconcentration estimates. Combining this lower bound on the spectral gap with the results of Kerner-Pechmann-Spitzer, we infer a type-I generalized Bose-Einstein condensation in probability for a Kac-Luttinger system of non-interacting bosons among Poissonian spherical impurities, with the sole macroscopic occupation of the one-particle ground state when the density exceeds the critical value.

2
Inputs from the method of enlargement of obstacles

In this section we collect various facts from the method of enlargement of obstacles, see Chapter 4, Sections 1 to 3 of
[
28
]
, which will be employed or underpin some of the results in the next two sections.

We begin with an informal description. In a nutshell the method of enlargement of obstacles is a procedure, which for
\ell
positive real (say bigger than
10
) and a configuration
\omega\in\Omega
attaches in a measurable fashion two disjoint subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the
density set

{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
and the
bad set

{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
, so that
\omega
has no point outside
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)\cup{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
:

(2.1)

\omega\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,\backslash\,({\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)\cup{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega))\big)=0\;\mbox{and}\;{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)\cap{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)=\emptyset.

In the presentation made here
(\log\ell)^{1/d}
corresponds to the unit scale in Chapter 4 of
[
28
]
and
\varepsilon=(\log\ell)^{-1/d}
to the small parameter in the same reference. The statements recalled below will hold in the large
\ell
limit (i.e. small
\varepsilon

\omega
.

In essence, adding Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\overline{{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)}
(the closure of
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
) does not increase too much Dirichlet eigenvalues of type
\lambda_{1,\omega}(U)
when they are below
M(\log\ell)^{-2/d}
(see Theorem
2.1
below), and the bad set
{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
has small relative volume on each box of side-length
(\log\ell)^{1/d}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see Theorem
2.4
. In addition, the sets
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
and
{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
have “low combinatorial complexity”: their restriction to each cube

(2.2)

C_{q}=(\log\ell)^{1/d}\,(q+[0,1)^{d}),\;q\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},

is a union of disjoint cubes in an
L
-adic decomposition of
C_{q}
, of size larger than (and of order)
(\log\ell)^{(1-\gamma)/d}
in the case of the density set
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
, and
(\log\ell)^{(1-\beta)/d}
in the case of the bad set
{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
, where
0<\gamma<\beta<1
. This feature constrains the number of possible shapes of the restriction of
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
and of
{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
to any such cube
C_{q}
, to at most
2^{(\log\ell)^{\gamma}}
in the case of
{\cal D}_{\ell}(\omega)
, and at most
2^{(\log\ell)^{\beta}}
in the case of
{\cal B}_{\ell}(\omega)
, see (
2.12
). This reduced combinatorial complexity of the density set and of the bad set underpins the
coarse graining
aspect of the method, and its power when bounding the probability of events of a large deviation nature.

When in addition

7
Bose-Einstein condensation

In this section we combine the lower bound on the spectral gap obtained in the main Theorem
6.1
with the results of Kerner-Pechmann-Spitzer in
[
16
]
. We prove a so-called type-I generalized Bose-Einstein condensation in probability for a model in the spirit of Kac-Luttinger
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
consisting of a non-interacting Bose gas among a Poisson cloud of hard obstacles made of closed balls of radius
a>0
centered at the points of the cloud, which has intensity
\nu>0
. The dimension of space is
d\geq 2
. The radius
a
although fixed can be arbitrarily small, and the complement in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of the spherical impurities may possibly percolate, see Chapter 4 of
[
21
]
. Our main result is Theorem
7.1
.

Following
[
16
]
, we consider a
thermodynamic limit
in a grand-canonical set-up. Given a fixed particle density (not to be confused with the parameter of Section 2).

(7.1)

\rho>0,

we consider the positive sequence
\ell_{N},N\geq 1
, indexed by the particle number
N
, which tends to infinity and satisfies (see (
1.5
) for notation)

(7.2)

\rho\,|B_{\ell_{N}}|=N,\;\mbox{for $N\geq 1$}

(in the notation of
[
16
]
,
2\ell_{N}=L_{N}
).

The events (see (
1.9
), (
1.4
) for notation)

(7.3)

\Omega_{N}=\{\omega\in\Omega;\lambda_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})<\infty\}=\{\omega\in\Omega;B_{\ell_{N},\omega}\not=\emptyset\},\;N\geq 1

are non-decreasing, have positive probability, and

(7.4)

\mbox{$\bigcup_{N\geq 1}\,\Omega_{N}=\Omega_{\infty}$ (the event of full ${\mathbb{P}}$-measure in (\ref{1.6}))}.

To stay within the framework of
[
16
]
, on
\Omega^{c}_{N}
we replace the variables
\lambda_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}}),j\geq 1
, which are all infinite on
\Omega^{c}_{N}
, by the ordered sequence of Dirichlet eigenvalues of
-\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta+1
in
B_{\ell_{N}}
(any positive constant in place of
1
would do). We thus define the modified single particle eigenvalues (see (
1.9
) for notation)

(7.5)

\overline{\lambda}_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\lambda_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})$, when $j\geq 1$ and $\omega\in\Omega_{N}$},\\[4.30554pt]
\mbox{the $j$-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of $-\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta+1$ in $B_{\ell_{N}}$, when $j\geq 1$ and}\\
\mbox{$\omega\notin\Omega_{N}$}.\end{array}\right.

Note that

(7.6)

\mbox{on $\Omega_{\infty}$, for large $N$, $\lambda_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})=\overline{\lambda}_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})$, for all $j\geq 1$},

i.e. outside a
{\mathbb{P}}
-negligible set, the original sequence of eigenvalues agrees with the modified sequences of eigenvalues, when
N
is large. We denote by
\beta\in(0,\infty)
the inverse temperature. Given a chemical potential
\mu\in(-\infty,\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})
), the quantity
(e^{\beta(\overline{\lambda}_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})-\mu)}-1)^{-1}
is the number of particles occupying the
j
-the eigenstate in the grand-canonical ensemble, see (2.7) of
[
16
]
, and we choose
\mu=\mu^{\omega}_{N}\in(-\infty,\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}}))
so that

(7.7)

\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{\ell_{N}}|}$}\;\sum\limits_{j\geq 1}\;\overline{n}\,^{j,\omega}_{N}=\rho,\;\mbox{with}\;\overline{n}\,^{j,\omega}_{N}=1\,/\,(e^{\beta(\overline{\lambda}_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})-\mu^{\omega}_{N})}-1).

In view of (
7.6
) and setting
n^{j,\omega}_{N}=1\,/\,(e^{\beta(\lambda_{j,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})-\mu^{\omega}_{N})}-1)
on
\Omega_{N}
, we also see that

(7.8)

\mbox{on $\Omega_{\infty}$, for large $N$, $\overline{n}\,^{j,\omega}_{N}=n^{j,\omega}_{N}$, for all $j\geq 1$},

(i.e. the modification (
7.5
) is immaterial on
\Omega_{\infty}
for large
N
).

We recall some known facts. As
N\rightarrow\infty
, the random measures on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

(7.9)

m_{N,\omega}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{\ell_{N}}|}$}\;\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\;\delta_{\lambda_{i,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})},\;\mbox{for $\omega\in\Omega_{N}$},\\[8.61108pt]
0,\;\;\mbox{for $\omega\in\Omega^{c}_{N}$},\end{array}\right.

are known to
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. converge vaguely to a deterministic measure, the
density of states

(7.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$m$ on $[0,\infty)$, characterized by its Laplace transform}\\
\displaystyle\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-t\lambda}dm(\lambda)=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}$}\;E^{t}_{0,0}[\exp\{-\nu\,|W_{t}^{a}|\}],\;\mbox{for $t>0$},\end{array}

where
E^{t}_{0,0}
denotes the expectation with respect to the Brownian bridge in time
t
from
0
to
0
, and
W_{t}^{a}
is the Wiener sausage of radius
a
in time
t
, i.e. the closed
a
-neighborhood of the Brownian bridge trajectory. (The proof is similar to that of Theorem 5.18, p. 99 of
[
22
]
). In view of (
7.6
) we also see that

(7.11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{${\mathbb{P}}$-a.s., the measures $\overline{m}_{N,\omega}=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{\ell_{N}}|}$}\;\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\;\delta_{\overline{\lambda}_{i,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})}$ on $[0,\infty)$ converge vaguely}\\
\mbox{as $N\rightarrow\infty$ to the measure $m$ in (\ref{7.10})}.\end{array}

One also knows that
m
has a so-called Lifshitz tail close to
0
. More precisely, see Corollary 3.5 of
[
26
]
or as in Theorem 10.2, p. 221 of
[
22
]
, one has (see above (
0.2
) for notation):

(7.12)

m([0,\lambda])=\exp\big\{-\nu\omega_{d}(\lambda_{d}/\lambda)^{d/2}\big(1+o(1)\big)\big\},\;\mbox{as $\lambda\rightarrow 0$}.

(The quantity
\exp\{-\nu\omega_{d}(\lambda_{d}/\lambda)^{d/2}\}
is the probability that the Poisson point process places no point in the open ball of radius
(\lambda_{d}/\lambda)^{1/2}
centered at the origin. This ball has a principal Dirichlet eigenvalue for
-\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta
equal to
\lambda
.) We then introduce the critical density for our system (see (2.6) of
[
16
]
), namely

(7.13)

\rho_{c}(\beta)=\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{\beta\lambda}-1}$}\;dm(\lambda)\;(<\infty\;\mbox{by (\ref{7.12}))}.

In our model a
generalized Bose-Einstein condensation
, with a macroscopic occupation of an arbitrary small energy band of one-particle states, is known to occur when
\rho>\rho_{c}(\beta)
, see Theorem 2.5 of
[
16
]
or Theorem 4.1 of
[
19
]
.
The main result of this section is the following theorem, which pins down the nature of the condensation. It shows a type-I generalized Bose-Einstein condensation in probability, when
\rho>\rho_{c}(\beta)
:

Theorem 7.1
.

When
\rho>\rho_{c}(\beta)
, then as
N
tends to infinity,

(7.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm i)}&\mbox{$\overline{n}^{1,\omega}_{N}/N$ tends to $\big(\rho-\rho_{c}(\beta)\big)/\rho$ in ${\mathbb{P}}$-probability, and}\\[8.61108pt]
{\rm ii)}&\mbox{$\overline{n}^{j,\omega}/N$ tends to $0$ in ${\mathbb{P}}$-probability, for any $j\geq 2$.}\end{array}\right.

Equivalently, for every fixed
N_{1}\geq 1
, as
N\geq N_{1}
tends to infinity,

(7.15)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm i)}&\mbox{$n^{1,\omega}_{N}/N$ tends to $\big(\rho-\rho_{c}(\beta)\big)/\rho$ in ${\mathbb{P}}_{N_{1}}$-probability, and}\\[8.61108pt]
{\rm ii)}&\mbox{$n^{j,\omega}_{N}/N$ tends to $0$ in ${\mathbb{P}}_{N_{1}}$-probability,}\end{array}\right.

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{N_{1}}
stands for the conditional probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot\,|\,\Omega_{N_{1}})
, see (
7.3
).

(When
\rho\leq\rho_{c}(\beta)
, the above quantities in (
7.14
), respectively in (
7.15
), are known to converge to
0
in
{\mathbb{P}}
-probability, respectively
{\mathbb{P}}_{N_{1}}
-probability, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, see for instance Theorem 3.2.4, p. 30 of
[
23
]
.)

Proof.

The claim (
7.15
) is a simple restatement of (
7.14
) since
n^{j,\omega}_{N},j\geq 1,N\geq N_{1}
coincide with
\overline{n}^{1,\omega}_{N},j\geq 1,N\geq N_{1}
on
\Omega_{N_{1}}
and
\lim_{N_{1}\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{N_{1}})=1
, see (
7.4
), (
7.5
), (
7.7
). We only need to prove (
7.14
).

We want to apply Theorem 2.9 of
[
16
]
. We first need to check the Assumptions 2.2 of the above reference. We have
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.,
\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})\rightarrow 0
, as
N\rightarrow 0
, by (
0.4
) and (
7.6
), and
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.,
\overline{m}_{N,\omega}
converges vaguely to the deterministic measure
m
, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, by (
7.11
). This shows that i) and ii) of Assumptions 2.2 of
[
16
]
hold. As we now show

(7.16)

{\mathbb{E}}[\overline{m}_{N,\omega}([0,\lambda])]\leq m([0,\lambda])\;\mbox{for $\lambda<1$ and $N\geq 1$}.

This will imply that iii) of Assumptions 2.2 of the above reference holds as well. To prove (
7.16
), we first observe that by Dirichlet bracketing and translation invariance, for
N,k\geq 1,\lambda\geq 0
,
{\mathbb{E}}[m_{N,\omega}([0,\lambda])]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[m_{k^{d}N}([0,\lambda])]
(we recall that by (
7.2
)
\ell_{k^{d}N}=k\ell_{N}
). Then, on a set of full
{\mathbb{P}}
-measure
m_{k^{d}N,\omega}
converges vaguely to
m
as
k
goes to infinity (see (
7.9
), (
7.10
)), and hence on that set for every point
\lambda
of continuity of
m([0,\cdot])
,
\lim_{k}m_{k^{d}N,\omega}([0,\lambda])=m([0,\lambda])
. For any such fixed
\lambda
we also have
m_{k^{d}N,\omega}([0,\lambda])\leq m_{k^{d}N,\omega=0}([0,\lambda])
which is deterministic and bounded in
k
. Thus, by dominated convergence, we see that for any
\lambda
point of continuity of
m([0,\cdot])
, one has

(7.17)

{\mathbb{E}}[m_{N,\omega}([0,\lambda])]\leq m([0,\lambda])

and this inequality extends to any
\lambda\geq 0
by right-continuity and monotonicity. Since
\overline{m}_{N,\omega}([0,\lambda])=m_{N,\omega}([0,\lambda])
for all
\lambda<1
and
\omega\in\Omega
by (
7.5
), the claim (
7.16
) follows.

As for iv) of Assumptions 2.2 in
[
16
]
, by the Lifshitz tail asymptotics (
7.12
), one has, picking
\eta_{1}\in(0,1)
and setting
\widetilde{C}_{1}=\nu\,\omega_{d}\,\lambda_{d}^{d/2}(1+\eta_{1}/2)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{0.3})}}{{=}}d\,c_{0}^{d/2}(1+\eta_{1}/2)
,

\lim\limits_{N}\;N^{1-\eta_{1}}\;m([0,\{\widetilde{C}_{1}/\log(|B_{\ell_{N}}|)\}^{2/d}])=0.

This shows that Assumptions 2.2 of
[
16
]
are satisfied.

Now by the lower bound on the spectral gap in Theorem
6.1
and (
7.6
),

(7.19)

\lim\limits_{N}\;{\mathbb{P}}[\overline{\lambda}_{2,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})-\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})\geq(\log\ell_{N})^{-(1+2/d+\varepsilon)}]=1,\;\mbox{for any $\varepsilon>0$}.

Together with (

) (or (

)) and the fact that

, as

, this is more than enough to show that for
\widetilde{C}_{2}=d\,c_{0}^{d/2}(1+\eta_{1}/4)
(
<\widetilde{C}_{1}
above), one has

(7.20)

\lim\limits_{N}\;{\mathbb{P}}[\overline{\lambda}_{2,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})-\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})\geq N^{-(1-\eta_{1})}\;\mbox{and}\;\overline{\lambda}_{1,\omega}(B_{\ell_{N}})\leq\{\widetilde{C}_{2}/\log(|B_{\ell_{N}}|)\}^{2/d}]=1.

The assumptions of Theorem 2.9 of

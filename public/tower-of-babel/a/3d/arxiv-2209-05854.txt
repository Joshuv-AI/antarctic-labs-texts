1.
Introduction

We study hyperbolic Gaussian analytic functions (GAFs) in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}

f_{L}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\xi_{n}\sqrt{\frac{L\left(L+1\right)\cdots\left(L+n-1\right)}{n!}}z^{n},\quad\text{for}\ L>0,

where
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are independent standard complex Gaussian random variables.
The zero set of this family has a distinguished property, which is the invariance of the distribution of their zero set
\mathcal{Z}_{f}=f^{-1}\{0\}
with respect to Möbius transformations of the unit disk. This property is unique in the sense that these are the only GAFs, up to a multiplication by a deterministic nowhere vanishing analytic function, whose zeros are isometry-invariant under the respective group of isometries
[
9
, Chap. 2]
.
Let
n_{L}\left(r\right)=\#\{z\ :\ f\left(z\right)=0\ \text{and}\ \left|z\right|\leq r\}
be the number of zeros of
f_{L}
in the disk of radius
r\in\left(0,1\right)
.
Because of the invariant distribution of the zero set, the first intensity is equal to a constant multiple of the hyperbolic measure on the unit disk.
In fact, it is not hard to check that

(1)

\mathbb{E}[n_{L}(r)]=\frac{Lr^{2}}{1-r^{2}}

see
[
18
]
. Buckley
[
1
]
studied the asymptotic of the variance
v_{L}\left(r\right)
and proved that there is a transition in the asymptotics of the variance at
L=\frac{1}{2}
.
The case
L=1
is special in the hyperbolic models.
Peres and Virág
[
17
]
discovered that the zero set of the Gaussian Taylor series
f_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}z^{n}
is a
determinantal
point process, which allowed them to directly compute a lot of its characteristics (see
[
9
, Chapter 4]
and also
[
9
, Section 5.1]
).
Peres and Virág also showed that
L=1
is the only instance when the zero set of
f_{L}
is a determinantal point process.
In
[
3
]
Buckley, Nishry, Peled, and Sodin found the rate of decay of the hole probability
p_{H}\left(r\right):=\mathbb{P}\left[\{f_{L}\neq 0\ \text{on}\ r\bar{\mathbb{D}}\}\right]
as
r\uparrow 1
.
Recently, Buckley and Nishry
[

]
proved that the limiting (in law) behavior of the normalized version of

\widehat{n}_{L}\left(r\right):=\frac{n_{L}\left(r\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\right]}{\sqrt{v_{L}\left(r\right)}},\quad r\uparrow 1,

For the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic functions Krishnnapur

The first result is about large deviations in the case

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

1.1.
Related works

1.1.1.
Comparison with the Gaussian entire function (GEF)

An analogue process to
f_{L}
whose zero set distribution is invariant under isometries of the plane is given by

g_{L}\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\xi_{n}\sqrt{\frac{L^{n}}{n!}}z^{n}.

with
L>0
. Note that it is enough to consider the case
L=1
since other values of
L
are just scaled versions of this case (unlike the hyperbolic model).
The mean number of zeros is equal to
\frac{L}{\pi}
, with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dm\left(z\right)
on the plane. The asymptotics of the variance in the planar
v\left(R\right)
was computed by Forrester and Honner in
[
7
]
:

v\left(R\right)=cR+o\left(R\right),\quad R\rightarrow\infty.

We use upper case
R
for the radius to emphasize that the function’s domain is
\mathbb{C}
.
In
[
1
]
, Buckley proved that for the hyperbolic model the asymptotic of the variance is given by:

v_{L}\left(r\right)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{c_{L}}{1-r}&\text{for }\ L>\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{c}{1-r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}&\text{for}\ L=\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{c_{L}}{(1-r)^{2-2L}}&\text{for }0<L<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}r\rightarrow 1^{-},

where the constants are explicitly computed and positive in all three cases.
Note that in the planar case, the expectation
\mathbb{E}\left[n\left(R\right)\right]
is in fact the Lebesgue measure of the disc with radius
R
, which is much larger (by factor of
R
) than the variance
v\left(R\right)
, which is proportional to the length of the boundary of the disc. However, in the hyperbolic case, the hyperbolic area and the length circumference of a disk of radius
r
are approximately the same and contrary to the plane, the variance is greater or equal (up to constants) than the expectation (
1
) (depending on the values of
L
).
It is also quite natural to study the probability of (large) fluctuations. We will restrict ourselves to the case of a disk with large radius
r
with respect to the function’s domain (i.e in the unit disk
r
is close to 1 and in the complex plane
R
tends to infinity) and study the probability:

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|n_{L}\left(r\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\right]\right|\geq v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right],

beyond what is known by asymptotic normality (i.e.
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
). This might be called moderate
\backslash
large
\backslash
huge deviations in probability language, depending on
\alpha
and the model we consider.
For the GEF there is a complete answer in terms of
\alpha
given by:

Theorem 1.4

(
Jancovici-Lebowitz-Manificat Law

[
14
,
11
,
19
]
)
.

As
R\rightarrow\infty

\frac{\log\left(-\log\left(\mathbb{P}\left[\left|n\left(R\right)-R^{2}\right|>R^{\alpha}\right]\right)\right)}{\log R}\rightarrow\begin{cases}2\alpha-1&\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1,\\
3\alpha-2&1\leq\alpha\leq 2,\\
2\alpha&\alpha\geq 2.\end{cases}

Here we can see that the asymptotic behavior changes for different values of the exponent
\alpha
, unlike the case of the hyperbolic GAF
Theorem

1.1
.
i.e. considering the
\log\log
asymptotic of this probability as
r\uparrow 1
, it holds that the power remains
2\alpha-1
.

1.1.2.
Determinantal point processes in the unit disk

Our result for the determinantal process in the hyperbolic model is related to recent work of Fenzl and Lambert (see
[
6
]
), where they compute the precise deviations for disk counting statistic of rotation invariant determinantal point processes in the disk and the plane. In particular they proved that the large deviations principle holds for the determinantal point process (
L=1
) and provided a general formula for the rate of decay in terms of the rate function. Here we provide an explicit expression to the rate function. Since the only case when the hyperbolic GAF form a determinantal point process is when
L=1
, we need to use other techniques for different values of
L
.

1.1.3.
Overcrowding estimates.

In
[
11
]
Krishnapur studied the overcrowding probability for zeros of the hyperbolic GAF. Our lower bound for
\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\geq V\right]
is close to his. Our upper bound is more precise, mainly because we found a more accurate estimate of the asymptotic probability

\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{r\mathbb{T}}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|d\mu\leq-x\right],\qquad\text{as}\ \,x\to\infty.

Krishnapur conjectured that his lower bound gives the correct order of decay of the overcrowding probability, and our
Theorem

1.2
confirms his conjecture.

1.1.4.
Hole probability

Consider the event when
f\left(z\right)\neq 0
in
r\mathbb{D}
, sometimes called the
hole event
.
We denote
p_{H}\left(r\right):=\mathbb{P}\left[\{f_{L}\neq 0\ \text{on}\ r\bar{\mathbb{D}}\}\right]
and are interested in its decay as
R\rightarrow\infty
or
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
.
There are results regarding the rate of decay of the hole probability in both models - the planar and the hyperbolic:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
15
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
19
]
)
.

Suppose
R\rightarrow\infty
. Then,

-\log p_{H}\left(R\right)=\frac{e^{2}}{4}R^{4}+O\left(R^{2}\log^{2}R\right).

Theorem 1.6

(
[
3
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Suppose
r\uparrow 1
. Then,

-\log p_{H}(r)\simeq_{L}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\left(1-r\right)^{L}}\log\frac{1}{1-r}&\text{for }\ 0<L<1,\\
\frac{1}{1-r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}&\text{for}\ L=1,\\

1.1.5.
Loss of the balance between excess of zeros and their deficit.

Consider the event of excess of zeros where
\{n_{L}\left(r\right)>\mu_{r}+v_{L}\left(r\right)\}
and the event of deficit of zeroes
\{n_{L}\left(r\right)<\mu_{r}-v_{L}\left(r\right)\}
where
\mu_{r}=\mathbb{E}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\right]
.
Heuristically, letting
\alpha\downarrow 1
in
Theorem

1.2
would imply

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)>\mu_{r}+v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right]\simeq-\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)>v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq v_{L}\left(r\right)^{2\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\overset{\alpha\downarrow 1}{\sim}\begin{cases}v_{L}\left(r\right)&\frac{1}{2}\leq L<1,\\
v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\frac{3-4L}{2-2L}}&0<L<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

while the hole probability results implies that

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)<\mu_{r}-v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\left(1-r\right)^{L}}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\simeq\begin{cases}v_{L}\left(r\right)^{L},&\frac{1}{2}\leq L<1,\\
v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\frac{L}{2-2L}},&0<L<\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

up to some logarithmic factor which we neglect.
I.e. the exponent is different and we do not get the symmetry between these events (contrary to the planar model), the probability for deficit is bigger.

Proof of Theorem 1.2

Fix

. Recall that we want to prove that there exists
C_{L}>0
such that for all

and all

satisfying

(2)

we have

-\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}(r)\geq V\right]\simeq_{L}\left(1-r\right)V^{2}.

Several remarks on
Theorem

1.2

(1)

Assumption (
2
) is meant to ensure that the number of zeros
V
is large enough (compared with the expectation).

(2)

Recall that

v_{L}\left(r\right)=\textrm{Var}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\right]\sim\begin{cases}\frac{c_{L}}{1-r}&\text{for }\ L>\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{c}{1-r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}&\text{for}\ L=\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{c_{L}}{(1-r)^{2-2L}}&\text{for }0<L<\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}\quad,\quad r\uparrow 1.

By the above,
v_{L}^{\alpha}\left(r\right)
will satisfy assumption (
2
), provided that either

,
\alpha>1
, or
L<\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2-2L},1]
.
In both cases
v_{L}(r)^{\alpha}\gg\mu_{r}=\frac{Lr^{2}}{1-r^{2}}
as
r\uparrow 1
, and hence

\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|n_{L}(r)-\mu_{r}\right|\geq v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right]\simeq\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\geq v_{L}\left(r\right)^{\alpha}\right].

In order to prove

we find matching lower and upper bounds for the

,

,

2.1.
Lower Bound

Claim 2.1
.

For
r\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1)
we have

\log\mathbb{P}\left[n_{L}\left(r\right)\geq V\right]\geq-c_{L}\left(1-r\right)V^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
r
close to 1 and take
m=m\left(r\right)=\left[V\right]+1
.
Consider the following independent events:

•

E_{1}\coloneqq\left\{\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq\sqrt{n}\ ,\ n\geq 2m+1\right\},

•

E_{2}\coloneqq\left\{\left|\xi_{n}\right|\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}\ ,\ m+1\leq n\leq 2m\right\},

•

E_{3}\coloneqq\left\{\left|\xi_{m}\right|\geq 4m\sqrt{1-r}\right\},

•

E_{4}\coloneqq\left\{\left|\xi_{n}\right|\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{n}\leq\sqrt{1-r}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}\ ,\ 0\leq n\leq m-1\right\}
.

Claim 2.2
.

Under the events
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}
, on
|z|=r
we have,

\left|f_{L}(z)-\xi_{m}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{m}\right|<\left|\xi_{m}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{m}\right|

whence ,
n_{L}\left(r\right)=m
.

Claim 2.3
.

For all
1\leq j\leq 4
,

\mathbb{P}\left[E_{j}\right]\geq\exp\left(-c_{L}\left(1-r\right)V^{2}\right).

Overall, using the independence of these events we get the desired lower bound, proving
2.1
.
It remains to prove these two claims.

Proof of
2.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\left|f_{L}\left(z\right)-\xi_{m}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}+\sum_{n=m+1}^{2m}+\sum_{n=2m+1}^{\infty}\right)\left|\xi_{n}\right|\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{n}

\displaystyle\leq m\sqrt{1-r}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}+\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}\cdot\frac{r^{m+1}(1-r^{m})}{1-r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2m+1}^{\infty}\sqrt{n}\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{n}.

So we need to show that the second and the third terms on the right hand side are also bounded by
m\sqrt{1-r}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}
.

–

2nd term:

r^{m}=e^{-m\left|\log r\right|}\ll\sqrt{1-r}\quad\text{(assumption \eqref{assumption})}

whence,

r^{m+1}\frac{1-r^{m}}{1-r}<mr^{m}\ll m\sqrt{1-r}.

–

3rd term:
Let
c_{n}=\sqrt{n}\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{n}
. Then
\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{L+n}{n}}\cdot r\rightarrow r
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Choose
r
sufficiently close to 1 such that
\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+r\right)
. Then,

\sum_{n=2m+1}^{\infty}c_{n}\leq c_{2m+1}\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)^{2m+1}\frac{1}{1-\frac{1+r}{2}}\ll c_{2m+1}\ \text{(assumption \eqref{assumption})}

\lesssim c_{m}r^{m}\ \left(\text{since}\binom{-L}{2m+1}\simeq\binom{-L}{m}\right)

=\sqrt{m}r^{m}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}\ll m\sqrt{1-r}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}.

∎

Proof of
2.3
.

First note that
|\xi_{n}|^{2}\sim\exp(1)
.
We start with the event
E_{1}
.

As
r\uparrow 1:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq\sqrt{n},\forall n\geq 2m+1\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{n=2m+1}^{\infty}e^{-n}

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\left(2m+1\right)}\frac{1}{1-e^{-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

For
E_{2}
, since
\binom{-L}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{n^{L-1}}{\Gamma(L)}
, we get that, for
m+1\leq n\leq 2m
, it holds that
\frac{\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\simeq 1

Therefore,

\frac{\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot r^{m}\ll 1.

Next,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq\frac{\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot r^{m}\ ,\forall\ m+1\leq n\leq 2m\right]

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{n=m+1}^{2m}c_{L}r^{2m}

\displaystyle=c_{L}^{m}r^{2m^{2}}\geq e^{-c_{L}m^{2}\left(1-r\right)}.

For
E_{3}
,
\mathbb{P}\left[|\xi_{m}|\geq 4m\sqrt{1-r}\right]=e^{-16m^{2}\left(1-r\right)}
.
Finally, for
E_{4}
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{0}\right|\leq\sqrt{1-r}\frac{\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot r^{m-n}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{0}\right|\leq\sqrt{1-r}\cdot m^{\frac{L-1}{2}}r^{m}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1-r\right)m^{L-1}r^{2m}\geq e^{-c_{L}m\left(1-r\right)},

and for
1\leq n\leq m-1

, while

hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq\sqrt{1-r}\frac{\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}}r^{m-n}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq A_{L}\sqrt{1-r}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}r^{m-n}\right]

Note that for

\max_{1\leq n\leq m-1}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}r^{m-n}=\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\right)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}r\leq D_{L}

which implies that
A_{L}\sqrt{1-r}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}r^{m-n}\ll 1
, and hence,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\leq A_{L}\sqrt{1-r}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\cdot r^{m-n}\right]\geq c_{L}\left(1-r\right)\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{L-1}r^{2\left(m-n\right)}

So finally we get,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\xi_{n}\right|\binom{-L}{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{n}\leq\sqrt{1-r}\binom{-L}{m}^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{m}\ ,\forall\ 0\leq n\leq m-1\right]

\displaystyle\geq e^{-c_{L}m\left(1-r\right)}\prod_{n=1}^{m-1}\left(d_{L}\left(1-r\right)\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{L-1}r^{2\left(m-n\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-c_{L}m\left(1-r\right)}d_{L}^{m}\left(1-r\right)^{m}r^{b_{L}m^{2}}\prod_{n=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{L-1}\geq e^{-c_{L}m^{2}\left(1-r\right)},

proving
2.3
.
∎

Combining altogether, we get the desired lower bound.
∎

Lemma 2.6

as follows. Consider the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{C}_{\nu}
the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{C}
. Then, we take the countable product measure space
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}_{\nu}
, with the sigma algebra generated by cylinder sets, i.e.
\mathbb{C}^{j-1}\times U_{j}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-j}
where

U_{j}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
,
n\geq 1
.
Denote by
\text{A}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
- the space of analytic functions defined on the unit disk. Endow this space with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets. This makes it a complete separable metric space.
Now,
\beta^{*}:\Omega\mapsto[0,2\pi)
can be viewed as a composition of two measurable functions.
The map
\omega=\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\mapsto f\left(z,\omega\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\left(\omega\right)z^{n}
where
\xi_{n}\left(\omega\right)=\omega_{n}
is measurable as a limit of measurable functions (projections are continuous).
Then, we need to prove that the map
\beta^{*}:A\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\mapsto[0,2\pi]
defined momentarily is measurable.

Consider all
\beta
’s such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\left|f\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{j}}\cdot e^{i\beta}\right)\right|=\int_{r_{0}\mathbb{T}}\log\left|f\right|d\mu=:c_{f}

(such
\beta
exists as we’ve already mentioned by continuity,
Equation

8
). We define

\beta^{*}\left(f\right):\text{A}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\rightarrow\left[0,2\pi\right]
as follows:

\beta^{*}\left(f\right)=\inf\{\beta\in[0,2\pi)\ :\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\left|f\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{j}}\cdot e^{i\beta}\right)\right|=c_{f}\}.

To see that
\beta^{*}
is lower semi-continuous (and hence measurable), we want to show that for all
a\in[0,2\pi)
,
\{f\in\text{A}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\ :\ \beta^{*}\left(f\right)>a\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\text{A}\left(r\bar{\mathbb{D}}\right)}

where
\mathcal{B}_{\text{A}\left(r\bar{\mathbb{D}}\right)}
is the Borel sigma-algebra with respect to the sup metric.
(since
r<1
is fixed, we can restrict our domain to
r\mathbb{D}
.)
Indeed,

\displaystyle\{f\in\text{A}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\ :\ \beta^{*}\left(f\right)>a\}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{f\in\text{A}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\ :\ \forall\beta\in\left[0,a\right]\ ,\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\left|f\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{j}}\cdot e^{i\beta}\right)\right|\neq c_{f}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{n}\,\bigg\{f\in\text{A}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)\ :\ \forall\beta\in\left[0,a\right]\ ,\ \Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\big|f\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{j}}\cdot e^{i\beta}\right)\big|-c_{f}\Big|>\frac{1}{n}\bigg\}

So if
\sup_{r\bar{\mathbb{D}}}\left|f-g\right|<\delta
then (by continuity) the integrals are sufficiently close to each other
\left|c_{f}-c_{g}\right|<\epsilon
hence this set is open.

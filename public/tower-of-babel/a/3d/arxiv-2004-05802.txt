To Be Announced
Hans van Ditmarsch

Abstract
In this survey we review dynamic epistemic logics with modalities for quantification over information change. Of such logics we present complete axiomatizations, focussing on axioms involving the interaction between knowledge and such quantifiers, we report on their relative expressivity, on decidability and on the complexity of model checking and satisfiability, and on applications. We focus on open problems and new directions for research.

Introduction

Truthful public announcement
is the misnomer of the year. Which year? Not the year 1989, the publication of Jan Plaza’s ‘Logics of public communications’
[
114
]
. Plaza does not use ‘announcement’. Not the year 1998 either, the publication of Alexandru Baltag et al.’s ‘The logic of public announcements (…)’
[

, as there are prior occurrences such as in Jelle Gerbrandy’s
[

from 1997. Whereas in 1986, before
[

, Yoram Moses et al.’s
[

Truthful
is a misnomer, because a statement may be truthful (what you
believe
to be true, a subjective criterion) but not true (an objective criterion). Only truth is formally required. The twain meet because announcements are assumed to be made by an omniscient and benevolent agent, for whom being truthful is the same as saying the truth, and who will not say what she does not know to be true. More down to earth: the listener assumes that the speaker wants to inform (is truthful) and is also correct (says the truth).

Announcement
is a misnomer, because the observed information may be heard as well as seen (the light is on, or off). Such forms of observation are also modelled as an announcement in the logics that we deal with in this contribution. So it need not be a real announcement, which is presumably a speech act. There may not be an announcer anyway, in the sense of an agent modelled in a system. The source of the new information is typically assumed to be anonymous, whereas an announcement of a proposition by an agent modelled in the system is formalized as ‘the agent knows the proposition’.

Public
is, after all, not a misnomer. The new information is assumed to be received synchronously. That is a backdoor towards making ‘announcement’ acceptable terminology: ‘announcement’ carries the legal sense that all are supposed to have heard, and liable for the consequences even if they did not. Announcements tend to be public by definition.

We will often omit ‘truthful’ from ‘truthful public announcement’. Untruthful announcements play no role in this survey. Public announcements have also been called
revelations
, a very suitable name but that did not stick (unlike in game theory and mechanism design). It is on the same delightfully pretentious scale as calling a state a
world
. And public announcements have been called
public events
, another suitable name that might or might not stick but so far survives in the community. Or public actions, which carries a notion of agency. Personally, I like
public observation
, precisely as it lacks agency. Either way, it always concerns a true proposition that is simultaneously or synchronously observed by a set of agents and as a consequence of which they adjust their knowledge bases, they update what they know to be true.

A different direction of generalizing PAL is to consider quantification over announcements. Arbitrary public announcement logic APAL

To be announced

To determine what is to be announced in order to make

For all dynamic epistemic logics in this survey we focus on complete axiomatizations, decidability of satisfiability, expressivity, to a lesser extent also on the complexity of model checking and (decidable) satisfiability, and last but not least we focus on applications. Applications can be found in multi-agent system analysis, in security protocols, in gossip protocols, and in epistemic planning on specific domains. All these and more will be discussed in detail. We list many open problems but also completely new directions for research.

In this survey we wish to address both the informed reader in modal logics, such as a master or doctoral student in logic, computer science, or artificial intelligence, as well as the expert in dynamic epistemic logic. For the benefit of the former we start the survey with a self-contained introduction in public announcement logic and action model logic. For the benefit of the latter we carefully list the open problems within well-defined subjects and that build on foundations of established results. Either way, we wish to share with the readers of all backgrounds our enthusiasm for the topic and its further development, and thank them for any moment of their time they choose to spend on the survey.

Axiomatization of PAL

The axiomatization of
minimal modal logic
K contains axiom
\Box_{a}(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow\Box_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\Box_{a}\psi
and derivation rule ‘From
\varphi

\Box_{a}\varphi

\Box_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi
,
\Box_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\Box_{a}\Box_{a}\varphi
, and
\neg\Box_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\Box_{a}\neg\Box_{a}\varphi

The axiomatization of PAL extends that of K or S5 (or yet other modal logics) with so-called
reduction axioms
for the announcement. It follows a pattern that is common in dynamic epistemic logics and that we therefore explain in some detail. The reduction axioms for the announcement are validities that have the shape of equivalences. There is one for each main logical connective of the formula bound by the announcement.

\begin{array}[]{lll}{[\psi]}p&\leftrightarrow&(\psi\rightarrow p)\\
{[\psi]}\neg\varphi&\leftrightarrow&(\psi\rightarrow\neg[\psi]\varphi)\\
{[\psi]}(\varphi\wedge\chi)&\leftrightarrow&([\psi]\varphi\wedge[\psi]\chi)\\
{[\psi]}\Box_{a}\varphi&\leftrightarrow&(\psi\rightarrow\Box_{a}[\psi]\varphi)\\
{[\psi]}[\varphi]\chi&\leftrightarrow&[\psi\wedge[\psi]\varphi]\chi\end{array}

Strictly, expressions such as
{[\psi]}(\varphi\wedge\chi)\leftrightarrow([\psi]\varphi\wedge[\psi]\chi)
are not axioms but are
axiom schemes
, whereas for example the instantiation
{[p]}(q\wedge\Box_{a}p)\leftrightarrow([p]q\wedge[p]\Box_{a}p)
of that schema is an axiom. The difference is relevant because the logic PAL (as well as most other dynamic epistemic logics in this survey) does not have the
substitution property
that we can replace the occurrence of an atom in a validity by any formula. For example,
[p]p
is valid but
[p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p](p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)
is invalid. The shape of the axiom
[\psi]p\leftrightarrow(\psi\rightarrow p)
is a giveaway for this lacking feature: it contains an atom as well as an arbitrary formula. That reduction strictly depends on the bound formula being an atom and not just any formula. Whereas in a logic with the substitution property, such as K and S5, atoms can also be any formula.

In other dynamic epistemic logics, many of these reduction axioms are very similar, except the one for the interaction between the update modality (here the announcement) and knowledge. In the continuation we focus on such interaction between update and knowledge.

Except for the first one, the reduction axioms for the announcement all have the shape
[\psi]\mathsf{this}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{that}
where
this
is not a subformula of
that
, but where
\mathsf{that}
contains a subformula
[\psi]\mathsf{other}
where
\mathsf{other}
is a subformula of
\mathsf{this}
. Roughly, that feature is what makes it possible to
reduce
(rewrite) a formula with announcements to an equivalent formula without announcements. Let us give an example:

\begin{array}[]{ll}{[p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p]}\Box_{a}p,&\text{iff}\\
(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)\rightarrow\Box_{a}[p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p]p,&\text{iff}\\
(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)\rightarrow\Box_{a}(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p\rightarrow p).\end{array}

Note that the last formula is equivalent to
p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p
. In two steps the reduction terminates. The final step is when an announcement binds an atom, as the announcement modality will then have ‘disappeared’ on the righthand side. In general, to show that such a rewrite procedure terminates one needs to associate a weight or measure (often a natural number) to a formula and prove that the weight of
[\psi]\mathsf{this}
is larger than the weight of
\mathsf{that}
. Similar reductions exist for many of the dynamic epistemic logics we present in this survey. Each logic may require a different formula weight in order to prove termination of the reduction.

Expressivity

Given two logics
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
defined over the same class of models,
L_{1}
is
at least as expressive as

L_{2}
, if for every formula
\varphi_{2}
in the language of
L_{2}
there is a formula
\varphi_{1}
in the language of
L_{1}
such that
\varphi_{1}
is satisfied by precisely the class of pointed models that satisfy
\varphi_{2}
.
If
L_{1}
is at least as expressive as
L_{2}
and
L_{2}
is at least as expressive as
L_{1}
then
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are
equally expressive
(or:
L_{1}
is as expressive as
L_{2}
). If
L_{1}
is at least as expressive as
L_{2}
and
L_{2}
is not at least as expressive as
L_{1}
then
L_{1}
is
more expressive than

L_{2}
.

By way of reductions as the above example, it can be shown that every formula with announcements is equivalent to a formula without announcements, and therefore public announcement logic PAL is as expressive as multi-agent epistemic logic K (with respect to model class
\mathcal{K}
) and as expressive as the logic S5 (with respect to model class
\mathcal{S}5
). The announcement modality is pushed ever more inward until it gets eliminated by an application of the first axiom
[\psi]p\leftrightarrow(\psi\rightarrow p)
above, where on the right-hand side of the equation there is one less announcement modality than on the left-hand side. We now proceed to eliminate the next announcement modality by such reductions, and so on, until there are none left. See
[
94
]
for a general approach for dynamic modalities.

Action models

An action model
[
30
]
(or event model) is a structure like a Kripke model but with a precondition function instead of a valuation function. An
action model

E=(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{R},\mathsf{pre})
consists of a
domain

\mathsf{S}
of
actions
, an
accessibility function

\mathsf{R}:A\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(\mathsf{S}\times\mathsf{S})
, where each
\mathsf{R}_{a}
is an accessibility relation, and a
precondition function

\mathsf{pre}:\mathsf{S}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}
, where
{\mathcal{L}}
is a logical language — which in our case would typically be the epistemic language
{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond)
; however, there is also a recursive way to define a language with action model modalities, that we present below. A pointed action model
E_{e}
, in other words a pair
(E,e)
, where
e\in\mathsf{S}
, is an
epistemic action
. Just as for Kripke models, we also permit multi-pointed action models
E_{F}
where
F\subseteq\mathsf{S}
. We will also use ‘epistemic action’ to denote any other information changing update.

Performing an epistemic action that is a pointed action model in a pointed epistemic model involves computing the
restricted modal product
of the action model and the epistemic model. This product (also called the update product) encodes the new state of information. It is defined as follows.

Given an epistemic model
M=(S,R,V)
and an action model
E=(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{R},\mathsf{pre})
, the updated model
(M\otimes E)=(S^{\prime},R^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is such that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}S^{\prime}&\ \ =&\{(t,f)\in S\times\mathsf{S}\mid M_{t}\models\mathsf{pre}(f)\}\\
((t,f),(t^{\prime},f^{\prime}))\in R^{\prime}_{a}&\text{iff}&R_{a}tt^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathsf{R}_{a}ff^{\prime}\\
(t,f)\in V^{\prime}(p)&\text{iff}&t\in V(p)\end{array}

In other words: the domain consists of the product domain, but restricted to (state, action) pairs
(t,f)
such that
M_{t}\models\mathsf{pre}(f)
, i.e., such that the action can be executed in that state; an agent considers a pair
(t,f)
possible in the update model if she considered the previous state
t
possible and the execution of action
f
in that state; and the valuations do not change after action execution.

A truthful public announcement
\varphi
is a singleton action model
(\{e\},\mathsf{R},\mathsf{pre})
, with
\mathsf{R}_{a}=\{(e,e)\}
for all
a\in A
, and
\mathsf{pre}(e)=\varphi
.

Language and semantics of AML

As already noted, the language
{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,\otimes)
of
action model logic
AML contains an additional inductive construct

\langle E_{e}\rangle\varphi

standing for ‘there is an execution of (pointed) action model
E_{e}
after which
\varphi
(is true)’, and where it is required that the domain of
E
is finite. This clause
\langle E_{e}\rangle\varphi
is properly recursive in the formal language definition: it says that, given the (finite number)
|{\mathcal{D}}(E)|
formulas that are the preconditions of
E
(although we do not see them) and the formula
\varphi
(that we see), also
\langle E_{e}\rangle\varphi
is a formula. One should see the action model frame as the operator and all those formulas as its arguments. The set of all action model frames should then be seen as an additional parameter of the logical language, apart from the agents
A
and the atoms
P
. As the finite pointed action model frames can be enumerated, this is a countable set.

The definition of modal depth
d(\varphi)
should be extended to the case
\varphi=\langle E_{e}\rangle\psi
, namely as the sum of
d(\psi)
and the maximum depth of the preconditions in
E
. Formally:
d(\langle E_{e}\rangle\psi)=d(\psi)+\max\{d(\mathsf{pre}(f))\mid f\in\mathsf{S}\}
, where
E=(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{R},\mathsf{pre})
.

The semantics for epistemic actions is as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M_{s}\models\langle E_{e}\rangle\psi&\mbox{ \ \ iff \ \ }&M_{s}\models\mathsf{pre}(e)\text{ and }(M\otimes E)_{(s,e)}\models\psi\end{array}

Axiomatization

Action model logic has a complete Hilbert-style axiomatization with reduction axioms for the action model modalities. This is just as for public announcement logic, which is now a special case. For completeness’ sake, the reduction axioms are as follows, where in the final axiom,

{[E_{e}]}\neg\varphi&\leftrightarrow&(\mathsf{pre}(e)\rightarrow\neg[E_{e}]\varphi)\\

The schema
\langle{!}\rangle\Box_{a}\varphi

[

. Fitch addressed the question whether what is true can become known and in
[

]
he demonstrated that the existence of unknown truths (there is a
\varphi
for which
\varphi\wedge\neg\Box_{a}\varphi
) is inconsistent with the requirement that all truths are knowable (for any
\psi
,
\psi\rightarrow\langle{!}\rangle\Box_{a}\psi
). The inconsistency appears by substituting
p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p
for
\psi
, as follows. Let
M_{s}
be a (
\mathcal{S}5
) model satisfying
p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p
and towards a contradiction assume that
M_{s}
also satisfies
(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)\rightarrow\langle{!}\rangle\Box_{a}(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)
. Then
M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\Box_{a}(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)
. Then there is a formula
\psi
witnessing this
\langle{!}\rangle
quantifier such that
M_{s}\models\psi
and
(M|\psi)_{s}\models\Box_{a}(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)
. Now observe that
\Box_{a}(p\wedge\neg\Box_{a}p)
is inconsistent in S5, as we can derive
\Box_{a}p
and
\neg\Box_{a}p
from it, and therefore
\bot
. It can therefore not be true in
(M|\psi)_{s}
.

An interesting APAL validity for the Fitch setting is
\langle{!}\rangle(\Box_{a}\varphi\vee\Box_{a}\neg\varphi)

[

]
. This states that in APAL ‘everything is knowable’, meaning that for every formula we can get to know whether it is true or false.

That this is valid, is shown by announcing the value of all variables occurring in
\varphi

with a constant valuation for those variables, for any

The result is for
\mathcal{S}5

Such knowability comes at a price: the Moore sentence

is knowable, because after being truthfully announced, agent

knows that it is false, as we saw in Example

3.5
Variations

A fair number of variations of APAL have seen the light. Prior to a more detailed presentation, let us see some of this variation.

\begin{array}[]{lll}M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&\text{there is a quantifier-free}\ \psi\ \text{such that}\ M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\hfill\text{ (APAL) \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{balbianietal:2008}{}{}]}}\\
M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&\text{there is a}\ \psi\ \text{such that}\ M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\hfill\text{(full APAL) \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hvdetal.aiml:2016}{}{}]}}\\
M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&\text{there is a positive}\ \psi\ \text{such that}\ M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\hskip 56.9055pt\hfill\text{(APAL${}^{+}$) \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hvdetal.papal:2020}{}{}]}}\\
M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&\text{there is a Boolean}\ \psi\ \text{such that}\ M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\hfill\text{(BAPAL) \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hvdetal.bapal:2022}{}{}]}}\end{array}

But there are many more, that are also reported below. What is the interest? The logic APAL is undecidable, has a high complexity of model checking, and has no known finitary axiomatization. Taming this into decidability, lower complexities, or a finitary axiomatization (or other proof methods such as sequent or tableaux calculi) is a computational advantage for applications such as epistemic planning and security protocols. Although a nice aim, the reality has so far turned out to be fairly tough, as we will see. However, this is, we think, very informative for the reader. Of independent interest is that some variations may provide a better match to address philosophical logical themes such as knowability or AI themes involving agency. Let us proceed.

3.5.1
Fully arbitrary announcements

In this variation we really quantify over all announcements, without restriction. It therefore seems to have a large intuitive appeal.

M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi\text{ \quad iff \quad there is a }\psi\text{ such that }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi

3.5.3

In this variation we only allow quantification over Booleans. We now get a finitary axiomatization and decidability.

The proof that BAPAL is not at least as expressive as APAL is very similar to the proof that PAL is less expressive than APAL in Section

One part was that (trivially) APAL is at least as expressive as PAL, because the language of PAL is a fragment of the language of APAL. This part no longer holds for BAPAL. The other part consisted in showing that PAL is not at least as expressive as APAL. We can reuse that part for BAPAL. In the version of the proof for BAPAL we again use bounded bisimilarity, but slightly differently. To emphasize the similarity in method we will use the same epistemic models as in Section

3.5.5
Almost Arbitrary Announcements

In this variation we quantify over propositions that are stronger or weaker than a given formula. This permits a notion of dynamic consequence with novel substructural properties.

M_{s}\models\langle\chi^{\downarrow}\rangle\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{there is a quantifier-free $\psi$
\emph{implying}}\ \chi\text{ such that }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi

where
\chi
is also quantifier-free, and where by “
\psi
implies
\chi

\psi\rightarrow\chi
is valid (in PAL). So we then get:

M_{s}\models\langle\chi^{\downarrow}\rangle\varphi
iff  there is a quantifier-free
\psi
such that
\models\psi\rightarrow\chi
and
M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
.

This variation of APAL semantics was initially suggested by Igor Sedlár, for a more general setting of substructural logics. Results have been published in
[
151
]
and subsequently in
[
152
]
(the expanded journal version of the former). The resulting logic is called IPAL
↓
(where the I stands for ‘implying’). As in APAL, the quantification in IPAL
↓
is over quantifier-free formulas, however, only over those formulas
\psi
that are at least as strong as a given formula
\chi
. Structurally, given some model
M
, we quantify over all restrictions of
M
that are
contained in

M|\chi
. In the semantics, the following are interchangeable (see
[
151
, Prop. 4]
):

\begin{array}[]{ll}(i)&\models\psi\rightarrow\chi\text{ and }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\\
(ii)&M\models\psi\rightarrow\chi\text{ and }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\\
(iii)&M_{s}\models\langle\psi\wedge\chi\rangle\varphi\end{array}

where in formulation
(iii)
not
\psi
but
\psi\wedge\chi
is the formula implying
\chi
.

A dual ‘at most as strong as’ quantifier
\langle\chi^{\uparrow}\rangle
has semantics

M_{s}\models\langle\chi^{\uparrow}\rangle\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{there is a quantifier-free $\psi$ \emph{implied by} }\chi\text{ such that }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi

In other words, now we quantify over all restrictions of
M
that
contain

M|\chi
. This begets the logic IPAL
↓
; IPAL is the logic containing both operators.

The axiomatization of IPAL
↓
is straightforward, by replacing the APAL axiom and derivation rule by the following, where
\boldsymbol{\eta}(\sharp)
is a necessity form and where we recall the above paraphrase (iii) of the IPAL
↓
semantics:

\begin{array}[]{ll}{[\chi^{\downarrow}]}\varphi\rightarrow[\psi\wedge\chi]\varphi,\text{ for all quantifier-free }\psi\\
\text{From }\boldsymbol{\eta}([\psi\wedge\chi]\varphi)\text{ for all quantifier-free }\psi,\text{ infer }\boldsymbol{\eta}([\chi^{\downarrow}]\varphi)\hfill\end{array}

The logic is also undecidable, where the proof method of
[
6
]
applies, as
[\top^{\downarrow}]\varphi
is equivalent to
[!]\varphi
. So we can build a tiling of the plane as usual.

One can show that IPAL is less expressive than APAL, by a technically involved argument not resembling anything shown so far in this survey and therefore omitted (see
[
152
]
).

The relation between IPAL
↓
and substructural logic is that we can view

as so-called
dynamic consequence

\psi_{1}\dots\psi_{n}\Rightarrow\varphi
, as an alternative to Van Benthem’s dynamic consequence
[

19

defined as

[

]

\psi_{1}\dots\psi\dots\psi_{n}\Rightarrow\varphi

\psi

\psi_{1}\dots\psi_{n}
). This novel notion of dynamic consequence clearly deserves closer exploration.

A possibly very different logic seems to result if in
\langle\chi^{\downarrow}\rangle\varphi
and
\langle\chi^{\uparrow}\rangle\varphi
the formula

is not required to be quantifier-free but may also contain quantifiers. The resulting logic QIPAL is also succinctly presented in
[

we need to establish the validity of

in QIPAL. However, in QIPAL the paraphrased semantics

3.5.6
Knowability logic

In this variation we directly interpret the APAL quantifier on epistemic models and omit the public announcements from the logical language. So let now
\langle!\rangle\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,\langle!\rangle)
.

M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi\text{ \ iff \ there is a quantifier-free }\psi\text{ such that }M_{s}\models\psi\text{ and }(M|\psi)_{s}\models\varphi

We recall that the APAL semantics of

use public announcement.

M_{s}\models\langle{!}\rangle\varphi\mbox{ \ \ iff \ \ there is a quantifier-free $\psi$ such that }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi

The semantics are equivalent. But for the former we do not need public announcement in the logical language. Given that formulas of shape
\langle{!}\rangle\Box_{a}\varphi
represent “
\varphi

without announcements is called

\langle{!}\rangle
in APAL need to be replaced by something not using announcements.

Another such logic for knowability has been proposed in
[
166
]
, now based on a relation-restricting update, instead of a domain-restricting update.

A logic for knowability called LK (as well as a number of variants) was proposed in

wherein the two modalities

3.5.7
Arbitrary announcement as effort

In this variation we interpret the APAL quantifier on epistemic models with more, topological, structure.

M_{s}\models\langle!\rangle\varphi\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{there is a quantifier-free {\bf observable} $\psi$ such that }M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi

Here, ‘observable’ is a topological notion defined further below. In this setting an arbitrary announcement quantifier is interpreted as a kind of ‘effort’ modality: if you can get to know a proposition after incorporating the informative consequences of an announcement, this is like getting to know a proposition after making some
effort
. Instead of quantifying over submodels reached by executing announcements, we can alternatively quantify over submodels of a given model reached by an alternative semantics for such announcements on models that have more structure than the epistemic models central in this survey: only some subsets of the domain that are denotations of formulas are
permitted

[
49
]
, or more topological, or topologically inspired, structures in publications like
[

36
,
147
,
37
]
. In the above semantics of the quantifier, a formula

is

if the topological interior of its denotation is true: in other words, ‘permitted’ then means ‘open’. We refer to the cited works for details.

3.5.8

Group announcement and coalition announcement

4.1
Announcements by agents

This section is devoted to group announcement logic GAL and coalition announcement logic CAL. These are clearly also variations of APAL, but we deal with them separately in this survey, as they are more targeted towards modeling aspects of agency and therefore have received quite a bit of attention from that community. Extensions of GAL and CAL with common knowledge and distributed knowledge are discussed in Section
8
.

How to model an announcement by an agent

We recall that a truthful public announcement made by an outside observer is a true public announcement. This is information coming into the system from a trusted outside source. A truthful public announcement made by an agent whose knowledge or beliefs are modelled in the system is not necessarily true. Now there is a difference between truthful and true. Announcements made by agents can be truthful but not true, and can be true but not truthful. An announcement made by an agent is truthful if it is believed by that agent. If agent
a
truthfully says
\varphi
we therefore model this as the announcement
\Box_{a}\varphi
.

We now have to distinguish our usual setting in this survey, the treatment of knowledge and of updates preserving
\mathcal{S}5
models, from the treatment of belief and of other updates.

On
\mathcal{S}5
models, the truthful announcement
\varphi
by agent
a
is true.

This is, because
\Box_{a}\varphi
implies
\varphi
. It is standard in dynamic epistemic logics to model ‘agent
a
makes a truthful public announcement
\varphi
’ in this way as ‘a truthful public announcement
\Box_{a}\varphi
is made’, where the first occurrence of truthful implies true, and the second occurrence of truthful means true.

It is often said that modelling an announcement
\varphi
by an agent
a
as
\Box_{a}\varphi
is a way to have a notion of agency in dynamic epistemic logic. This is true to the extent that, for example, a multi-agent systems modeller can informally let
\Box_{a}\varphi
always mean that agent
a
observes (announces)
\varphi
. Formally we cannot distinguish the announcement by agent
a
that
\varphi
, modelled in this way, from the announcement by the outside observer that
a
knows
\varphi
.

On not-
\mathcal{S}5
models, the announcement
\varphi
by agent
a
may be truthful but not true.

When agent
a
says
\varphi
and
\neg\varphi\wedge\Box_{a}\varphi
is true, agent
a
is truthful because
\Box_{a}\varphi
is true. This now represents that agent
a
believes
\varphi
. This belief is mistaken (incorrect), because
\varphi
is false. Formula
\neg\varphi\wedge\Box_{a}\varphi
formalizes that agent
a
has an incorrect belief in
\varphi
.

On not-
\mathcal{S}5
models, the announcement
\varphi
by agent
a
may be true but not truthful.

There are now two options. When agent
a
says
\varphi
and
\varphi\wedge\Box_{a}\neg\varphi
is true, agent
a
is
lying
because she believes
\varphi
to be false. But in fact she is mistakenly saying the truth. Whereas when agent
a
says
\varphi
and
\varphi\wedge\neg(\Box_{a}\varphi\vee\Box_{a}\neg\varphi)
is true, agent
a
is

because she is uncertain whether
\varphi
is true, although confidently announcing
\varphi
. In fact she is unknowingly saying the truth, because
\varphi
is true. See

. Speech acts such as lying and bluffing can easily be modelled as action models

[

.

Belief, lying, and bluffing are outside our scope. In this section we restrict ourselves to truthful public announcements by agents on models for knowledge.

What is a simultaneous announcement?

Let us also elaborate on the simultaneity of announcements made by groups of agents. When three agents
a,b,c
respectively say
\psi_{a}
,
\psi_{b}
, and
\psi_{c}
simultaneously, we model this as the announcement
\Box_{a}\psi_{a}\wedge\Box_{b}\psi_{b}\wedge\Box_{c}\psi_{c}
. This is a simultaneous announcement by
a
,
b
, and
c
consisting of three conjuncts. We do not model this as three successive announcements. The difference matters, because the truth value of
\Box_{b}\psi_{b}
before the announcement of
\Box_{a}\psi_{a}
may be different from the truth value of
\Box_{b}\psi_{b}
after the announcement of
\Box_{a}\psi_{a}
. Just imagine that
a
says that
p
is true and that
b
says that he is uncertain whether
p
;
b
can say it at the same time as
a
but not afterwards. We need not imagine simultaneous announcements as everybody talking at the same time. Public announcement is a misnomer for a
public event
, such as ‘the three children who know that they are muddy now step forward’ in the muddy children problem
[
106
,
150
]
. This can be observed when done simultaneously. Or, back to
a,b,c
talking, we can instead imagine
a,b,c
writing, respectively,
\psi_{a}
,
\psi_{b}
, and
\psi_{c}
on a piece of paper put into three envelopes after which the external observer or authority publicly opens the envelopes and reads their contents aloud in whatever order. It would be more accurate to say that we model such agents’ actions as
independent
events, as usual in concurrency. And do the three muddy children really step forward simultaneously, within a split microsecond? Of course not. It is rather that their decisions to step forward were made independently, pertaining to the same perceived previous state of information.

4.3
Coalition announcement logic

Another variation on APAL with aspects of agency is
coalition announcement logic

G

5
Quantifying over action models

In this section we are moving from quantifying over public announcements to quantifying over arbitrary epistemic actions, such as private announcements. We recall that already for PAL the term ‘announcement’ is somewhat questionable. And even more so for non-public information change. However, a constant throughout dynamic epistemic logics is that a model update is induced by an epistemic action, and that an epistemic action needs to satisfy a precondition formula. We simply continue to call this formula the announcement. But it can then be private instead of public. Whether there is an epistemic action after which a proposition is true, is then still the same as what is
to be announced
in order to make that proposition true.

5.1
Arbitrary action model logic

In the logic APAL we quantify over announcements, i.e., public events. We can also quantify over events that may not be public, where the obvious suspect is to quantify over action models. This was observed in
[
25
, Section 5]
, including the obvious semantics, but only truly realized in various works involving James Hales
[
83
,
69
,
84
]
, where our presentation follows
[
83
]
.

We expand the language
{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,\otimes)
of action model logic with a quantifier
\langle\otimes\rangle
with the following semantics, where
E_{e}
is an epistemic action, and where
M
is a
\mathcal{K}
model (the results in this section are typically not for
\mathcal{S}5
models).

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M_{s}\models\langle\otimes\rangle\varphi&\text{ iff }&\text{there is a }E_{e}\text{ with quantifier-free preconditions such that }M_{s}\models\langle E_{e}\rangle\varphi\end{array}

We call the resulting logic AAML, for
arbitrary action model logic
. We recall that in APAL the quantifier over announcements adds expressivity and causes undecidability of satisfiability. It may therefore come as a surprise that in AAML the quantifier does not add expressivity and does not cause undecidability. The logic AAML is as expressive as action model logic AML (and therefore as expressive as the logic K), because the quantifier
\langle\otimes\rangle
can be eliminated by reduction axioms. We recall from Section
2
that such a reduction axiom has the shape of an equivalence between two formulas
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
such that a subformula of
\varphi_{1}
bound by a quantifier is (strictly) more complex than any subformula of
\varphi_{2}
bound by that quantifier, or such that that quantifier no longer occurs in
\varphi_{2}
. Every formula with quantifiers can then be reduced (rewritten) to an equivalent formula without quantifiers. The technique to reduce AAML is borrowed from another logic, refinement modal logic, and therefore we will only discuss it section
7
devoted to that logic.

Because AAML is as expressive as AML by way of a reduction, this logic AAML also allows
synthesis
. This we will now discuss.

Synthesis

Logics with quantifiers over information change sometimes allow
synthesis
. There are (at least) two kinds of synthesis in a dynamic epistemic logic. The logic need not have quantifiers over information change. In the
local synthesis
problem, given an epistemic model
M_{s}
and a formula
\varphi
, the problem is whether there exists an epistemic action
\alpha
(such as a pointed action model
E_{e}
, but we will later also consider other epistemic actions) such that
M_{s}\models\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi
. In the
global synthesis
problem, given a formula
\varphi
, the problem is whether there exists an epistemic action
\alpha
such that whenever
M_{s}\models\langle\beta\rangle\varphi
for some epistemic action
\beta
, then also
M_{s}\models\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi
. In a logic with a quantifier
\langle\bullet\rangle
for information change due to such
\beta
s, global synthesis amounts to the validity of the schema
\langle\bullet\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi
. From here on, by synthesis we mean global synthesis. Not all dynamic epistemic logics allow synthesis, for example, PAL does not. The somewhat trivial reason for this failure is that we cannot make a typical disjunction of epistemic parts, such as
\Box_{a}p\vee\Box_{a}\neg p
,
always
true: we would have to choose between the announcement
p
and the announcement
\neg p
. Although correct, this is somewhat trivial, because allowing the ‘test’ of the value of
p
, that is, non-deterministic choice between the announcements
p
and
\neg p
, would solve the problem for this example. Such a program would amount to a two-pointed action model, with preconditions
p
respectively
\neg p
, and where all agents can distinguish the two actions.

Clearly, a dynamic epistemic logic with quantifiers can only allow synthesis if the quantifier can be eliminated by a reduction, so that any formula with quantifiers is equivalent to a formula without. So for APAL, we need not even think about synthesis, as APAL is (strictly) more expressive than PAL (Section
3
).

The logic AAML allows synthesis
[
83
]
. More specifically, given
\langle\otimes\rangle\varphi
, where
\varphi
is quantifier-free, there is a multi-pointed action model
E_{T}
(where
T\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}(E)
) such that
\langle\otimes\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle E_{T}\rangle\varphi
is valid: given any formula
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,\otimes)
we can
synthesize
an action model
E_{T}
, that is finite, and with quantifier-free preconditions, such that, whenever there is an action model such that after its execution
\varphi
is true, then
E_{T}
can also be executed to make
\varphi
true. We cannot always synthesize a single-pointed action model
E_{t}
. It may have to be multi-pointed, as for the disjunctive epistemic goal
\varphi=\Box_{a}p\vee\Box_{a}\neg p
above. By an easy induction over the number of quantifiers in a formula we can also lift the restriction that
\varphi
be quantifier-free. It can be any formula in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,\otimes,\langle\otimes\rangle)
.

Because there is synthesis for AAML, and because in an actual state
s
some
e\in T
of the synthesized

In public announcement logic an updated model has a restricted domain, and this is not different in action model logic, where the update is a domain-restricted modal product. Language and semantics of refinement modal logic

The language

of refinement modal logic is the language

of multi-agent modal logic extended with an inductive construct

M_{s}\models\langle\succeq\rangle\varphi\quad\mbox{iff}\quad\text{there is }M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\text{ such that }M_{s}\succeq M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\text{ and }M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\models\varphi

As the refinement relation is transitive, reflexive, and confluent (for all

•

(

•

(

•

\langle\succeq\rangle[\succeq]\varphi\rightarrow[\succeq]\langle\succeq\rangle\varphi
(Church-Rosser) / CR

The refinement quantifier also satisfies:

•

[\succeq]\langle\succeq\rangle\varphi\rightarrow\langle\succeq\rangle[\succeq]\varphi
(McKinsey) / MK

As this has not been observed in the literature, it requires proof. We recall the relational property of

Towards a contradiction assume that MK does not hold. Then there is an epistemic model

The final two sections of this survey are more exploratory in nature. They are less a survey on work done, and more about further research, because little work has been done in dynamic epistemic logic on those subjects involving quantification over information change. In Section

8.1
Iterated updates

Standard program operations on updates

So far, we always considered dynamic modalities
\langle\pi\rangle
, where
\pi
is an
update
, inducing transformation of (‘updating’) an epistemic model, not necessarily deterministically. We can see the update as a program. In public announcement logic the programs are the announcements, in action model logic the programs are the pointed finite action models, and so on. We can then wonder about standard program operations, as in PDL: test, choice, sequential execution, and arbitrary iteration (‘Kleene star’)
[
87
]
. Except for the arbitrary iteration, the main topic of this section, such program operations are syntactically definable in dynamic epistemic logics, and (maybe therefore) not commonly considered.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle?\psi\rangle\varphi&\leftrightarrow&\psi\wedge\varphi\\
\langle\pi\cup\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi&\leftrightarrow&\langle\pi\rangle\varphi\vee\langle\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi\\
\langle\pi\ ;\ \pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi&\leftrightarrow&\langle\pi\rangle\langle\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi\end{array}

Test

The semantics for the modality for a test
?\psi
contrasts well with the semantics for public announcement of
\psi
that we already know:

\begin{array}[]{lll}M_{s}\models\langle?\psi\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&M_{s}\models\psi\text{ and }M_{s}\models\varphi\\
M_{s}\models\langle\psi\rangle\varphi&\text{iff}&M_{s}\models\psi\text{ and }(M|\psi)_{s}\models\varphi\end{array}

If a test
?\psi
can be executed,
\psi
must have been true, and as the resulting model is the same as the original one, any postcondition
\varphi
is true if
\varphi
already was true. This explains that
\langle?\psi\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow(\psi\wedge\varphi)
is valid, and that a test is definable in dynamic epistemic logic. A public announcement is a test on
\psi

that is observed by all agents
and thus resulting in a different model in which to interpret
\varphi
, whereas the test
?\psi
is not observed by any agent, and thus resulting in the same model in which to interpret
\varphi
.

Choice

Non-determistic choice between programs does not commonly feature in dynamic epistemic logics because it is definable as
\langle\pi\cup\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi\ \leftrightarrow\ \langle\pi\rangle\varphi\vee\langle\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi
. The box-version of that equivalence,
[\pi\cup\pi^{\prime}]\varphi\ \leftrightarrow\ [\pi]\varphi\wedge[\pi^{\prime}]\varphi
, is considered less intuitive because the choice
\cup
becomes a conjunction
\wedge
. For a finite set
\Pi
of programs we thus get that
\langle\bigcup_{\Pi}\pi\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\bigvee_{\Pi}\langle\pi\rangle\varphi
. Indirectly we have already seen this with the multi-pointed action models:
[E_{F}]\varphi\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{e\in F}[E_{e}]\varphi
and in particular
[E]\varphi\leftrightarrow\bigwedge_{e\in{\mathcal{D}}(E)}[E_{e}]\varphi
. For an infinite set
\Pi
of programs, such as ‘all announcements’ (
\Pi={\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,!)
) or ‘all finite pointed action models’, we can still consider
\bigcup_{\Pi}\pi
as a semantic object, but
\bigvee_{\Pi}\langle\pi\rangle
would be an infinite disjunction, which is not in the logical language. However, for both finite and countably infinite
\Pi
we can consider
\langle\bigcup_{\Pi}\pi\rangle\varphi
as a quantification
\langle\bullet\rangle\varphi
. If
\Pi={\mathcal{L}}(\Diamond,!)
we get
\langle\bullet\rangle=\langle!\rangle
and if
\Pi
is the set of all finite pointed action models we get
\langle\bullet\rangle=\langle\otimes\rangle
.

Sequential update

Given that
\langle\pi\ ;\ \pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\pi\rangle\langle\pi^{\prime}\rangle\varphi
, if
\pi=\pi^{\prime}
we get
\langle\pi\ ;\ \pi\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\pi\rangle\langle\pi\rangle\varphi
, in other words,
\langle\pi^{2}\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\pi\rangle^{2}\varphi
. Similarly, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\langle\pi^{n}\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\pi\rangle^{n}\varphi
, where
\langle\pi^{0}\rangle\varphi=\langle\pi\rangle^{0}\varphi:=\varphi
. In particular, for iteration of public announcements we have
\langle\varphi\rangle^{n}\psi\leftrightarrow\langle\varphi^{n}\rangle\psi
, and for iteration of epistemic actions
E_{e}
we get that
\langle E_{e}\rangle^{n}\psi\leftrightarrow\langle E_{e}^{n}\rangle\psi
. This brings us to arbitrary iteration.

Arbitrary iteration

Let us now define

\langle\pi^{*}\rangle\varphi
iff   there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle\pi^{n}\rangle\varphi
.

In view of the above we write
\langle\pi^{*}\rangle\varphi
and
\langle\pi\rangle^{*}\varphi
interchangeably. We get a very different form of quantification over information change if we merely consider arbitrary iteration of a given update
\pi

[

\mathcal{K}

Iterated arbitrary updates

Let now
\langle\bullet\rangle

Most of our quantifiers satisfy

Most of our quantifiers also satisfy

And most quantifiers also satisfy

From

Considering program operations on modal quantifiers therefore does not have added value. In contrast, the investigation of dynamic epistemic logics with arbitrary iteration of epistemic actions and with additionally quantifiers over such epistemic actions seems worthwhile to pursue.

8.2
Factual change

Dynamic epistemic logic is typically a logic wherein, in a given unchanging world, we model changes of knowledge due to observations by agents. There is no factual change (also known as ontic change, literally: change of the world). But there can be. Factual change can be public — when a solar eclipse obscures the sun to all alike — but also private — when you change the pincode of your bankcard. 9.1
Sabotage logic and relation-changing logics

We have dwelled a long time within the confinement of dynamic epistemic logics, considering the various ways to quantify over epistemic actions interpreted as model updates with corresponding dynamic modalities, with the intention to model arbitrary information change. There is a different tradition in modal logic with update modalities that are quantifiers and that affect the domain of a model or change the relations of the model, including investigations involving modal
\mu
-calculus and determination of first-order definability of modal logical fragments
[
115
,
129
]
. Such logics are not intended to model change of knowledge and are not considered dynamic epistemic logics. Let us call them
modal update logics
. We do not present such modal update logics in detail, but point out how dynamic epistemic logics can benefit from results obtained in modal update logics.

Modal update logics, with dynamic modalities for updates, are all very different and display a far greater variety than dynamic epistemic logics, but we can still roughly compare them with dynamic epistemic logics on a number of features. These features are not necessarily independent. Note that these features are informal, tentative, and (unless cited) not presented elsewhere.

•

modal update logics permit local updates unlike the global updates in dynamic epistemic logics

•

modal update logics permit semantic updates unlike the syntactic updates in dynamic epistemic logics

•

modal update logics permit particular updates unlike the universal updates in dynamic epistemic logics

Local versus global

The update in a dynamic epistemic logic is a model transformer and not a pointed model transformer. It is a function transforming a model into another model. We call this
global
. This induces a relation between points in the initial model and points in the updated model.

But the update is not a function of a pointed model. If it is, we call it
local
.

For example, a public announcement
\varphi
induces a relation between a model
M
and the restriction
M|\varphi
of that model. This induces a relation between
pointed
epistemic models, for example, between
M_{s}
and
(M|\varphi)_{s}
. But although
s
plays a role in the relation, because after all
\varphi
needs to be satisfied there, the state
s
that is the point of
M
does not determine the relation: any state
t
in the domain of
M
that also satisfies
\varphi
and that is not the designated point is treated the same as
s
.

An update of
M_{s}
removing a state
t
different from
s
, or removing a pair
(s,t)
from the accessibility relation, would be local.

This distinction between local and global was proposed in
[
20
]
.

Semantic versus syntactic

The model transformations in dynamic epistemic logic are almost always with respect to the extension of
formulas
evaluated in the initial model, such as the announcement
\varphi
(or the preconditions of an action model, or the source and target conditions of an arrow update). In that sense the update is
syntactic
(formula dependent).

If instead of a restricted model
M|\varphi
we were to consider a restricted model
M|T
where
T
is a subset of the domain of
M
, that may not be the extension of a formula, we call the update
semantic
.

Syntactic updates are typically less expressive than semantic updates, because the updated model is then a function of logical properties of the initial model. Indeed, public announcement logic is as expressive as epistemic logic.

Instead of restricting the model to
all
states satisfying
\varphi

\varphi
at the source and
\psi
at the target, as in dynamic epistemic logics, we now restrict the model by removing
a

.

9.2
Dynamic epistemic logics and relation-changing logics

Let us now relate the domain-changing or relation-changing modal update logics to the dynamic epistemic logics presented in this survey. In relation-changing modal logics we can consider arbitrary iterations or fixpoints of the structural operations wherein a mere pair of the accessibility relation is changed
[
115
,
129
]
. In this section we investigate to what extent such iterated transformations correspond to dynamic epistemic updates.

First consider refinement modal logic RML. Removing a pair from the accessibility relation of a model is a refinement of that model. Removing any finite number of pairs is also a refinement of that model. As is removing any infinite number of pairs of the relation, or the entire relation, which may be a limit or fixpoint (models need not have countable domains). It may be interesting to investigate on which class of models (all finite models?) we have that

\langle\succeq\rangle\varphi
is equivalent to
\langle-\rangle^{*}\varphi
.

Similarly, the quantifier in simulation modal logic can be seen as arbitrary iteration of the bridge modality, such that it is envisageable for some model class that

\langle\preceq\rangle\varphi
is equivalent to
\langle+\rangle^{*}\varphi
.

Now consider arrow update logics AUL and AUML, wherein pairs of the accessibility relation come with logical conditions: arrows with source or target formulas. In sabotage logic we delete pairs, whereas in AUL we retain pairs, but this is mere perspective. Retaining all pairs
(s,t)
with source condition
\varphi
and target condition

(arrow
\varphi\rightarrow_{a}\psi

all pairs that satisfy
\neg\varphi
at the source or
\neg\psi
at the target (arrows
\neg\varphi\rightarrow_{a}\top
and
\top\rightarrow_{a}\neg\psi
).

Assuming a single agent

[

10
Outlook

We have presented many different dynamic epistemic logics with quantification over information change: arbitrary public announcement logic APAL with quantification over announcements, arbitrary action model logic AAML with quantification over action models, arbitrary arrow update logic AAUL with quantification over arrow updates, and refinement modal logic RML with quantification over refinements. And then the many variations on each of those, with all different logical properties. Some corners of this spectrum are not yet well understood, in particular the various ways to obtain decidable APAL versions, the various simulation modal logics and how they relate to refinement modal logics, and the different complexities of very similar logics quantifying over epistemic actions, whether as action models, arrow updates, or refinements. Promising current developments are the combination of group epistemic modalities with quantifiers, and the investigation of iterated epistemic actions or quantifiers in relation to epistemic planning. Further developments could involve incorporating features of relation-changing modal logics (sabotage, bridge, swap) with dynamic epistemic logics, and there are subjects beyond the scope of this survey such as succinctness, where we can expect logics with quantifiers to be more succinct than the same logic without. Applications to gossip protocols, security protocols, and epistemic planning are to be developed much further. Let a hundred flowers bloom.

Future stability of expanding spatially homogeneous FLRW solutions of the spherically symmetric Einstein–massless Vlasov system with spatial topology ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Martin Taylor

Abstract
Spatially homogeneous FLRW solutions constitute an infinite dimensional family of explicit solutions of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system with vanishing cosmological constant. Each member expands towards the future at a decelerated rate. These solutions are shown to be nonlinearly future stable to compactly supported spherically symmetric perturbations, in the case that the spatial topology is that of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The decay rates of the energy momentum tensor components, with respect to an appropriately normalised double null frame, are compared to those around Minkowski space. When measured with respect to their respective t t coordinates, certain components decay faster around Minkowski space, while others decay faster around FLRW.



1
Introduction

Standard homogeneous isotropic cosmological models in general relativity are described by the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) spacetimes

\mathcal{M}=I\times\Sigma,\qquad g=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}g_{\Sigma},

(1.1)

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval,
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
is a constant curvature manifold, and
a\colon I\to(0,\infty)
is an appropriate
scale factor
. See Section 5.3 of
[
21
]
for more on FLRW spacetimes. This article concerns radiation filled FLRW cosmologies in which the constant curvature manifold is Euclidean space,
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})=(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\mathrm{Eucl}})
, with radiation described by spatially homogenous solutions of the massless Vlasov equation, and their stability properties.

1.1
The Einstein–massless Vlasov system

Consider a
3+1
dimensional Lorentzian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
and let

P=\{(t,x,p)\in T\mathcal{M}\mid g_{(t,x)}(p,p)=0\}\subset T\mathcal{M},

denote the
mass shell
of
(\mathcal{M},g)
.
Consider some local coordinates
\{t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
on
\mathcal{M}
, and let
\{t,x^{i},p^{\mu}\}
denote the corresponding conjugate coordinate system for
T\mathcal{M}
, i.e.
(t,x^{i},p^{\mu})
describes the point
p^{\mu}\partial_{x^{\mu}}|_{(t,x)}\in T\mathcal{M}
. The massless Vlasov equation on
(\mathcal{M},g)
takes the form

p^{0}\partial_{t}f+p^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f-p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}\partial_{p^{i}}f=0,

(1.2)

where
p^{0}
is defined by the mass shell relation

g_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}=0.

(1.3)

The Einstein–massless Vlasov system consists of equation (
1.2
) coupled to the Einstein equations

Ric(g)_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}R(g)g_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu},

(1.4)

where the energy momentum tensor takes the form

T^{\mu\nu}(t,x)=\int_{P_{(t,x)}}f(t,x,p)p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\frac{\sqrt{-\det g}}{-p_{0}}dp^{1}dp^{2}dp^{3},

(1.5)

where indices are raised and lowered with respect to the metric
g
(so that, for example,
p_{0}=g_{0\mu}p^{\mu}
). Here
t
is also denoted
x^{0}
, Greek indices, such as
\mu,\nu
, range over
0,1,2,4
, and lower case Latin indices, such as
i,j,k
range over
1,2,3
.

1.2
The spatially homogeneous FLRW family of solutions

The
spatially homogeneous FLRW
family constitutes an infinite dimensional family of explicit solutions of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system (
1.2
)–(
1.5
).

Define the manifold

\mathcal{M}_{\circ}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.6)

and consider the Euclidean metric

g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}=(dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}+(dx^{3})^{2},

on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p=(p^{1},p^{2},p^{3})\in T_{x}\mathbb{R}^{3}
, define

|p|^{2}=|p|_{g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}}^{2}=(g_{\mathrm{Eucl}})_{ij}p^{i}p^{j}=(p^{1})^{2}+(p^{2})^{2}+(p^{3})^{2}.

For any smooth, sufficiently decaying function
\mu:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\mu\not\equiv 0
, the metric
g_{\circ}
and particle density
f_{\circ}
defined by

g_{\circ}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}\big((dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}+(dx^{3})^{2}\big),\qquad f_{\circ}(t,x,p)=\mu(a(t)^{4}|p|^{2}),

(1.7)

where

a(t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\varrho}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},\qquad\varrho=\int|p|\mu(|p|^{2})dp,

(1.8)

define a solution of (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) on (
1.6
). Such solutions are known as spatially homogeneous FLRW solutions.

Remark 1.1

(Decelerated expansion)
.

Spacetimes with metric of the form (
1.1
) are said to undergo
accelerated expansion
if the scale factor
a(t)
satisfies
a^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
for all
t
,
linear expansion
if
a^{\prime\prime}(t)=0
for all
t
, or
decelerated expansion
if
a^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
for all
t
. Note that the solutions (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) undergo decelerated expansion. Previous stability works in cosmological settings have typically considered perturbations of spacetimes undergoing linear or accelerated expansion. See Section
1.4
below.

Remark 1.2

(Anisotropic spatially homogeneous FLRW solutions)
.

The spatially homogeneous FLRW family of solutions (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) lie inside a more general family of
anisotropic
spatially homogeneous FLRW family. Given any smooth, sufficiently decaying function
F:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to[0,\infty)
such that
F\not\equiv 0
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(p)p^{i}dp=0,\quad i=1,2,3,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(p)\frac{p^{i}p^{j}}{|p|}dp=\frac{\varrho}{3}\delta^{ij},\quad i,j=1,2,3,

the metric and particle density

g_{\circ}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}\big((dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}+(dx^{3})^{2}\big),\qquad f_{\circ}(t,x,p)=F(a(t)^{2}p^{1},a(t)^{2}p^{2},a(t)^{2}p^{3}),

where

a(t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{4\varrho}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},\qquad\varrho=\int|p|F(p)dp,

also defines a solution of (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) on (
1.6
). Since these solutions are, in general, not spherically symmetric (see Section
2.1
), consideration here is restricted to the solutions of the form (
1.7
)–(
1.8
).

Remark 1.3

(
\mathbb{T}^{3}
spatial topology)
.

Each (
1.7
)–(
1.8
), and more generally the solutions of Remark
1.2
, also define a spatially homogeneous FLRW solution of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system on the manifold
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
. This article will only be concerned with solutions on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

3.5

The components of the energy momentum tensor of (

1.8
), with respect to the Cartesian

coordinate system, take the form

(T^{\circ})^{tt}(t,x)=\frac{3}{4t^{2}},\qquad(T^{\circ})_{i}^{j}(t,x)=\frac{1}{4t^{2}}\delta_{i}^{j},\qquad(T^{\circ})_{it}(t,x)=0,

for
i,j=1,2,3
, where
(T^{\circ})_{i}^{j}(t,x)=g_{ik}(t,x)(T^{\circ})^{jk}(t,x)

The spacetimes
(\mathcal{M}_{\circ},g_{\circ})
expand from a spacelike singularity at
t=0
(with Kretchmann scalar
|Rm_{\circ}|^{2}_{g_{\circ}}=\frac{3}{2t^{4}}
) and are future geodesically complete. See the Penrose diagram of Figure
1
.
Such solutions feature in the Ehlers–Geren–Sachs Theorem
[
12
]
, which in particular ensures that all solutions of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system for which
f
is
isotropic
and
irrotational
are either stationary or described by an FLRW metric as above.

1.3
First version of the main theorem

The main result of the present work concerns the future stability of the family (
1.7
)–(
1.8
).

Theorem 1.4

(Future stability of FLRW)
.

Each spatially homogeneous FLRW solution is future nonlinearly stable, as a solution of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system (
1.2
)–(
1.5
), to compactly supported spherically symmetric perturbations.

More precisely, consider an FLRW solution of the form (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) for some smooth, non-identically vanishing
\mu
(decaying suitably so that
\varrho
is defined). For all compactly supported spherically symmetric initial data sufficiently close to that of (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) on
\Sigma_{1}=\{t=1\}
, the resulting solution is future geodesically complete, is isometric to (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) after some retarded time and, in an appropriately normalised double null gauge, the (appropriately normalised) components of the energy momentum tensor decay to zero as (
1.9
) to leading order, and each metric component and Christoffel symbol either remains close or decays to its FLRW value to the past of this retarded time, with quantitative polynomial rates.

A more precise version of Theorem
1.4
is stated below in Section
4
. See also the Penrose diagram in Figure
1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on an understanding of decay properties of solutions of the massless Vlasov equation. As such, in addition to Theorem
1.4
, decay properties of solutions of the massless Vlasov equation on a fixed FLRW background of the form (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) are given. See Theorem
3.2
.

The assumption of compact support can be relaxed, and is made for convenience in order to localise the proof. Indeed, following
[
9
]
, the perturbation of
f
, in the coupled problem, vanishes close to the centre of spherical symmetry at late times. The absence of singularities in future evolution therefore follows from the aforementioned quantitative decay properties, along with comparatively soft arguments including an
extension principle around non-central points
(see
[
11
]
or Theorem
2.3
below).

Remark 1.5

(Comparison with decay rates on Minkowski space)
.

The decay rates of solutions of the massless Vlasov equation on FLRW, obtained in Theorem
3.2
, are later compared with those of the massless Vlasov equation on Minkowski space. When measured with respect to their respective
t
coordinates, certain components of the energy momentum tensor, with respect to an appropriately normalised double null frame, decay faster in Minkowski space, while others decay faster in FLRW. See Remark
3.4
.

Remark 1.6

(Birkhoff-type theorem)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
in particular contains a Birkhoff-type theorem for the system (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) which ensures that any spherically symmetric solution with a regular centre, for which
f
is equal to its FLRW value
f_{\circ}
(defined by (
1.7
)), is locally isometric to the FLRW spacetime
(\mathcal{M}_{\circ},g_{\circ})
. See the final step in the proof of Theorem
5.1
(from which a general theorem can be extracted). Recall also the Ehlers–Geren–Sachs Theorem
[
12
]
.

Remark 1.7

(Einstein–Euler with radiation equation of state)
.

The metric
g_{\circ}
, defined by the former of (
1.7
) with
a(t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}}
, also arises as a solution of the Einstein–Euler system, i.e. equation (
1.4
) with

T^{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u^{\mu}u^{\nu}+pg^{\mu\nu},

for unknowns

, four velocity

— satisfying

, and density

The fluid variables for the FLRW metric take the form, in Cartesian coordinates,

(1.10)

It is known that the solution (
1.10
) is unstable to formation of shock waves for the Euler equations
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
on the fixed FLRW background
(\mathcal{M}_{\circ},g_{\circ})
(see Chapter 9 of
[
34
]
).

Note also the linear
Jeans instability
for the Einstein–Euler system linearised around FLRW
[

]
(see also the discussion in
[
6
]
).

2.1
Spherical symmetry

A solution
(\mathcal{M},g,f)
of (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) is
spherically symmetric
if
SO(3)
acts by isometry on
(\mathcal{M},g)
and preserves
f
. More precisely, it is assumed that there is a smooth isometric action
\mathcal{O}\colon\mathcal{M}\times SO(3)\to\mathcal{M}
on
(\mathcal{M},g)
such that, for each
p\in\mathcal{M}
, the orbit of the action satisfies either
Orb(p)\simeq S^{2}
, or
Orb(p)=\{p\}
and, moreover, for each of the generators
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3}
of the
SO(3)
action, the corresponding flows
\Phi^{i}\colon(0,2\pi)\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
satisfy

f(x,p)=f(\Phi^{i}_{s}(x),(\Phi^{i}_{s})_{*}p),\qquad\text{for all }s\in(0,2\pi),\quad i=1,2,3,

(2.1)

for all
(x,p)\in\mathcal{P}
. It is assumed that the centre

\Gamma=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid\mathcal{O}(x,\mathfrak{s})=x\text{ for all }\mathfrak{s}\in SO(3)\},

consists of a single timelike geodesic, and that
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\Gamma
splits diffeomorphically as

\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\Gamma\simeq\mathcal{Q}\times S^{2},

for a smooth 2-manifold
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\Gamma)/SO(3)
.
The quotient space
\mathcal{M}/SO(3)
, also denoted
\mathcal{Q}
, is viewed a manifold with boundary, with boundary identified with the centre
\Gamma
.

2.2
Double null gauge

A double null gauge consists of functions
u,v\colon\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
such that the level hypersurfaces of
u
foliate
\mathcal{Q}
by outgoing lines which are null with respect to the induced metric on
\mathcal{Q}
, and the level hypersurfaces of
v
foliate
\mathcal{Q}
by ingoing null lines. The level hypersurfaces of
u
and
v
lift to outgoing and incoming null cones of
\mathcal{M}
and such a double null gauge can be complemented with local coordinates
(\theta^{1},\theta^{2})
on
S^{2}
to local coordinates
(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2})
for
\mathcal{M}
.

For a given double null gauge, the metric
g
can be written in double null form

g=-\Omega^{2}dudv+R^{2}\gamma,

(2.2)

where
\gamma
is the unit round metric on
S^{2}
, where
\Omega
is a function on
\mathcal{Q}
and
R\colon\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbb{R}
is the area radius function

R(u,v)=\sqrt{\mathrm{Area}(S_{u,v})/4\pi},

where
S_{u,v}=\{(u,v)\}\times S^{2}\subset\mathcal{M}
.
The area radius
R
extends regularly to
0
on the centre
\Gamma
. Define

\lambda=\partial_{v}R,\qquad\nu=\partial_{u}R.

Remark 2.1

(Residual gauge freedom)
.

There is residual double null gauge freedom present in (
2.2
). If
\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v}\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
are increasing functions, with
I\subset\mathbb{R}
a suitable interval, then the change
u\mapsto\widetilde{u}(u)
,
v\mapsto\widetilde{v}(v)
preserves the double null form (
2.2
). Under such a change, the metric takes the form

g=-\widetilde{\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})d\widetilde{u}d\widetilde{v}+R(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})^{2}\gamma=-\widetilde{\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v))\frac{d\widetilde{u}}{du}(u)\frac{d\widetilde{v}}{dv}(v)dudv+R(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v))^{2}\gamma.

It thus follows that

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v))\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(u)\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(v),

(2.3)

\displaystyle R(u,v)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v)),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nu(u,v)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\nu}(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v))\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(u),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lambda(u,v)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\lambda}(\widetilde{u}(u),\widetilde{v}(v))\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(v).

(2.6)

The inverse metric of (
2.2
) takes the form

g^{-1}=-2\Omega^{-2}(\partial_{u}\otimes\partial_{v}+\partial_{v}\otimes\partial_{u})+R^{-2}\gamma^{AB}\partial_{\theta^{A}}\otimes\partial_{\theta^{B}},

where
A
and
B
range over
1
and
2
. The nonvanishing Christoffel symbols of
g
are

\Gamma^{u}_{AB}=\frac{2\lambda R}{\Omega^{2}}\gamma_{AB},\quad\Gamma^{v}_{AB}=\frac{2\nu R}{\Omega^{2}}\gamma_{AB},\quad\Gamma^{A}_{Bv}=\frac{\lambda}{R}\delta^{A}_{B},\quad\Gamma^{A}_{Bu}=\frac{\nu}{R}\delta^{A}_{B},\quad\Gamma^{u}_{uu}=\partial_{u}\log\Omega^{2},\quad\Gamma^{v}_{vv}=\partial_{u}\log\Omega^{2},

and

\Gamma^{A}_{BC}=\not{\Gamma}^{A}_{BC},

where
\not{\Gamma}^{A}_{BC}
are the Christoffel symbols of
(S^{2},\gamma)
.

For a given spherically symmetric double null gauge
(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2})
for
(\mathcal{M},g)
, one defines a corresponding conjugate coordinate system
(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2},p^{u},p^{v},p^{1},p^{2})
for
T\mathcal{M}
, whereby
(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2},p^{u},p^{v},p^{1},p^{2})
defines the point

\big(p^{u}\partial_{u}+p^{v}\partial_{v}+p^{A}\partial_{\theta^{A}}\big)\big|_{(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2})}\in T\mathcal{M}.

Such a coordinate system induces a coordinate system
(u,v,\theta^{1},\theta^{2},p^{v},p^{1},p^{2})
on the mass shell
P
, with
p^{u}
defined by the mass shell relation (
1.3
), which takes the form

p^{u}=\frac{R^{2}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}}{\Omega^{2}p^{v}}=\frac{L^{2}}{\Omega^{2}R^{2}p^{v}},

(2.7)

where the angular momentum
L\colon P\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

L(x,p)^{2}=R^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}.

(2.8)

The volume form on

P_{x}=\{p\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}\mid g_{x}(p,p)=0\}\subset T_{x}\mathcal{M},

in this induced coordinate system takes the form

\frac{R^{2}\sqrt{\det\gamma}}{p^{v}}dp^{v}\wedge dp^{1}\wedge dp^{2}.

The condition (
2.1
) that
f
is spherically symmetric means that, in a given double null gauge,
f
can be written as a function — which, abusing notation slightly, is also denoted
f\colon\mathcal{Q}\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
— of
u
,
v
,
p^{v}
, and
L=(R^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

f(x,p)=f(u,v,p^{v},L).

(2.9)

The energy momentum tensor
T
on
\mathcal{M}
takes the form

T=T_{uu}(u,v)du^{2}+2T_{uv}(u,v)dudv+T_{vv}(u,v)dv^{2}+T_{AB}(u,v)d\theta^{A}d\theta^{B}.

In double null gauge, the components take the form

\displaystyle T_{uu}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}}{R^{2}}\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u,v,p^{v},L)\,p^{v}\,L\,dL\,dp^{v},

(2.10)

\displaystyle T_{uv}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}}{R^{2}}\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u,v,p^{v},L)\,p^{u}\,L\,dL\,dp^{v},

(2.11)

\displaystyle T_{vv}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}}{R^{2}}\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u,v,p^{v},L)\,\frac{(p^{u})^{2}}{p^{v}}\,L\,dL\,dp^{v},

(2.12)

with
p^{u}=p^{u}(u,v,p^{v},L)
defined by (
2.7
). It moreover follows from the mass shell relation (
2.7
) that

\gamma^{AB}T_{AB}=\frac{R^{2}}{4\Omega^{2}}T_{uv}.

2.3
The spherically symmetric Einstein–massless Vlasov system in double null gauge

The Einstein equations (
1.4
) in spherical symmetry take the form of the following system of equations for
(\Omega,R,f)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\partial_{v}(R^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2}+R^{2}T_{uv},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\log\Omega^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2R^{2}}\big(1+4\Omega^{-2}\partial_{u}R\partial_{v}R\big)-T_{uv}-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{4R^{2}}\gamma^{AB}T_{AB},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{u}(\Omega^{-2}\partial_{u}R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R\Omega^{-2}T_{uu},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{v}(\Omega^{-2}\partial_{v}R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R\Omega^{-2}T_{vv},

(2.16)

where
\not{g}=R^{2}\gamma
. The mass shell relation (
1.3
) takes the form

-\Omega^{2}p^{u}p^{v}+R^{2}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}=0.

(2.17)

In particular,
\Omega^{2}T^{uv}=R^{2}\gamma_{AB}T^{AB}
, or
4\Omega^{-2}T_{uv}=R^{-2}\gamma^{AB}T_{AB}
, and so equation (
2.14

Remark 3.5

(Massless Vlasov equation on FLRW with
\mathbb{T}^{3}

5.1
Cauchy stability and the domain of dependence property

Recall the setting of Theorem
4.1
. Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
denote the unique maximal development of

, and let
(\mathcal{Q},\Omega^{2},R,f)
denote the associated maximal solution of the reduced system (

)–(
2.19
) (see Section
2.3
), in which the residual gauge freedom, discussed in Remark

and

.

Consider some

, where

is to be determined later. Given

,

, define the set

and

, for some appropriate

1.7
)–(
1.8
). In particular,

Let
t_{0}=t(U_{1},v_{0})=\frac{1}{4}(v_{0}+U_{1})^{2}
. Let

be such that

R^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}\leq(L_{0})^{2}\qquad\text{in}\qquad\mathrm{supp}(f_{1}-f_{\circ}|_{\Sigma_{1}}),

and let

be such that

. By Cauchy stability (for both the Einstein–massless Vlasov system (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) and the geodesic equations) the solution exists up to
\{t=t_{0}\}
and, by Proposition
3.3
, for any future-maximal null geodesic
\gamma\colon[1,S)\to\mathcal{M}
such that

, there exists

such that

(\gamma(s_{0}),\dot{\gamma}(s_{0}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0},L_{0},v_{0}},

if
v_{0}
is sufficiently large and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is sufficiently small. Moreover

|\Omega^{2}-4t|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\quad|\partial_{u}\log\Omega^{2}-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\quad|R-t^{\frac{1}{2}}r|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\quad|\lambda-v|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\quad|\nu+u|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\quad\text{on }\{v=v_{0}\}.

(5.2)

The remainder of the proof will then be concerned with the region

.

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{2R^{2}}-\frac{\Omega^{2}_{\circ}}{2R^{2}_{\circ}}+\frac{2}{R^{2}}\nu\lambda-\frac{2}{R^{2}_{\circ}}\nu_{\circ}\lambda_{\circ}-2(T_{uv}-T^{\circ}_{uv}),

5.3
The bootstrap theorem

The main content of the proof of Theorem
4.1
is contained in the following bootstrap theorem.

Given
T>t_{0}
and
U_{1}>0
, define the region

\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1},T}=\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{Q}\mid U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1},v\geq v_{0},\text{and }t_{0}\leq t(u,v)<T\}.

(5.10)

Theorem 5.1

(Bootstrap theorem)
.

Suppose that
T>t_{0}
is such that the maximal development exists up to time
T
— in the sense that
(u(t,r),v(t,r))\in\mathcal{Q}
for all
t_{0}\leq t<T
,
r\geq 0
— the difference
f-f_{\circ}
is supported in the region

\mathrm{supp}(\pi(f-f_{\circ}))\subset\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}\},

(5.11)

and, for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
U_{1}>0
, the solution moreover satisfies, for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1},T}
,

\Big|\Omega^{-4}R^{-2}T_{uu}-\Omega^{-4}_{\circ}R^{-2}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{uu}\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{6}},\quad\Big|\Omega^{-2}R^{-4}T_{uv}-\Omega^{-2}_{\circ}R^{-4}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{uv}\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{8}},\quad\Big|R^{-6}T_{vv}-R^{-6}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{vv}\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{10}},

(5.12)

|\Omega^{2}-4t|<\varepsilon t,\qquad\Big|\partial_{v}\log\Omega^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{t},\qquad\Big|\partial_{u}\log\Omega^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big|<\varepsilon,

(5.13)

|R-t^{\frac{1}{2}}r|<\varepsilon,\qquad|R^{2}-tr^{2}|<\varepsilon t,\qquad|\lambda-v|<\varepsilon,\qquad|\nu+u|<\varepsilon.

(5.14)

Then, if
U_{1}
is suitably large and
\varepsilon
is suitably small, there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
and
U_{1}
) such that the inequalities (
5.12
)–(
5.14
) hold in
\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1},T}
with
\varepsilon
replaced by
C\varepsilon_{0}
and (
5.11
) holds with
U_{1}
replaced by
U_{1}/2
. Moreover, the solution is isometric to the spatially homogeneous FLRW solution (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) in the region
\{t_{0}\leq t(u,v)<T\}\cap\{u\geq U_{1}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps. First, the size of the support of
f-f_{\circ}
is estimated. These estimates are then used to obtain estimates on the components of the energy momentum tensor. The metric quantities are then estimated in the region
\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}\}
. Finally, the solution is shown to be isometric to the spatially homogeneous FLRW solution (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) in the region
\{u\geq U_{1}\}
.

Throughout the proof the notation
A\lesssim B
is used when there exists a constant
K
, which may depend on
U_{1}-U_{0}
, such that
A\leq KB
. Constants
0<c\ll 1\ll C
are always independent of
U_{1}-U_{0}
.

Estimates for the support of
f-f_{\circ}
:

Let
\delta_{0}
,
L_{0}
and
v_{0}
be as in Section
5.1
, and recall that, for each null geodesic
\gamma\colon[1,S)\to\mathcal{M}
emanating from
\mathrm{supp}(f_{1})
, if
S
is sufficiently large there exists a time
s_{0}\in[1,S)
such that
(\gamma(s_{0}),\dot{\gamma}(s_{0}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0},L_{0},v_{0}}
.

For such a null geodesic, write

\dot{\gamma}(s)=p^{u}(s)\partial_{u}+p^{v}(s)\partial_{v}+p^{A}(s)\partial_{\theta^{A}}.

By the bootstrap assumption (
5.11
), the
u
coordinate of
\gamma
satisfies

U_{0}\leq u(s)\leq U_{1},

for all
s_{0}\leq s<S
. Recall moreover that
v_{0}>U_{1}
and so
R(v_{0},U_{1})>0
. It follows in particular that

t^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim r\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim v,\qquad\text{along }\gamma.

(5.15)

There exist constants
0<c\ll 1\ll C
such that the components of the tangent vector
\dot{\gamma}
of any such null geodesic
\gamma\colon[1,S)\to\mathcal{M}
emanating from
\mathrm{supp}(f_{1})
satisfy

\left(\gamma_{AB}p^{A}(s)p^{B}(s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{2L_{0}}{t(s)r(s)^{2}},\qquad 0\leq p^{u}(s)\leq\frac{Cp^{v}(s_{0})}{t(s)r(s)^{2}},\qquad c\frac{p^{v}(s_{0})}{t(s)}\leq p^{v}(s)\leq C\frac{p^{v}(s_{0})}{t(s)},

(5.16)

for all
s\geq s_{0}
. Indeed, let
\gamma\colon[s_{0},S)\to\mathcal{M}
be a null geodesic such that
(\gamma(s_{0}),\dot{\gamma}(s_{0}))\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0},L_{0},v_{0}}
. The first of (
5.16
) follows from the conservation of angular momentum (
2.20
), which implies that

R(s)^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}(s)p^{B}(s)=R(s_{0})^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}(s_{0})p^{B}(s_{0})\leq(L_{0})^{2},

(5.17)

along with the fact that
t^{2}r^{4}\leq 2R^{4}
, which follows from the bootstrap assumption (
5.14
).

It follows from the geodesic equation (
5.9
) that

\frac{d}{ds}\Big(tp^{v}\exp\Big(\int_{s_{0}}^{s}h(s^{\prime})p^{v}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\Big)\Big)=\exp\Big(\int_{s_{0}}^{s}h(s^{\prime})p^{v}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\Big)\frac{2t^{\frac{3}{2}}R}{\Omega^{2}}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B},

(5.18)

where

h(s)=(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\partial_{v}\log\Omega^{2})+\big((R-R_{\circ})-2t^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nu-\nu_{\circ})\big)\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}R}{\Omega^{2}}\frac{\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}}{(p^{v})^{2}}.

In order to obtain the lower bound of the latter of (
5.16
), consider the set

\mathcal{E}=\left\{s\in[s_{0},S)\mid(tp^{v})(s^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}(tp^{v})(s_{0})\text{ for all }s_{0}\leq s^{\prime}\leq s\right\}\subset[s_{0},S).

The set
\mathcal{E}
is manifestly a closed, connected, non-empty subset of
[s_{0},S)
. Moreover, if
s\in\mathcal{E}
, then

\int_{s_{0}}^{s}|h(s^{\prime})|p^{v}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}=\int_{v_{0}}^{v(s)}|h|dv\leq C\int_{v_{0}}^{v(s)}\frac{\varepsilon}{v^{2}}dv\leq C\varepsilon,

(5.19)

by the fact that

R(s)^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}(s)p^{B}(s)=R(s_{0})^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}(s_{0})p^{B}(s_{0})=R(s_{0})^{2}\Omega(s_{0})^{2}p^{u}(s_{0})p^{v}(s_{0}),

(5.20)

along with the bootstrap assumptions (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and the equivalence (
5.15
). Since

\frac{d}{ds}\Big(tp^{v}\exp\Big(\int_{s_{0}}^{s}h(s^{\prime})p^{v}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\Big)\Big)\geq 0,

it follows that

(tp^{v})(s_{0})\leq e^{C\varepsilon}(tp^{v})(s),

and thus, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, the set
\mathcal{E}\subset[s_{0},S)
is open, and hence equal to
[s_{0},S)
.

For the upper bound of the latter of (
5.16
), recall equation (
5.18
) and note that

\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{2t^{\frac{3}{2}}R}{\Omega^{2}}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}ds^{\prime}=\int_{v_{0}}^{v(s)}\frac{2t^{\frac{5}{2}}}{R^{3}\Omega^{2}}\frac{R^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}}{tp^{v}}dv\leq C\int_{v_{0}}^{v(s)}\frac{p^{v}(s_{0})}{v^{3}}dv\leq Cp^{v}(s_{0}),

by (
5.15
), (
5.20
) and the lower bound for
(tp^{v})(s)
. The upper bound for
(tp^{v})(s)
then follows from the estimate (
5.19
), after integrating equation (
5.18
).

The estimate for
p^{u}(s)
in (
5.16
) finally follows from the mass shell relation (
2.17
), which gives

\frac{\Omega(s)^{2}}{t(s)}p^{u}(s)=\frac{\big(R^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}\big)(s)}{t(s)p^{v}(s)},

along with the former and latter of (
5.16
).

It in particular follows from (
5.16
) that

\left(\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{CL_{0}}{t^{2}},\qquad 0\leq p^{u}\leq\frac{Cp^{v}}{t},\qquad 0\leq p^{v}\leq\frac{C}{t},\qquad\text{in }\mathrm{supp}(f-f_{\circ}).

(5.21)

Consider now the support property (
5.11
). For any null geodesic
\gamma\colon[s_{0},S)\to\mathcal{M}
as above, it follows from (
5.16
) that,

u(s)-u(s_{0})=\int_{s_{0}}^{s}p^{u}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\leq\int_{v(s_{0})}^{v(s)}\frac{C}{tv^{2}}\frac{p^{v}(s_{0})}{p^{v}}dv\leq\frac{C}{t(s_{0})v_{0}}.

Thus, since
t(s_{0}),v_{0}\geq 1
,

\mathrm{supp}(\pi(f-f_{\circ}))\subset\{U_{0}\leq u\leq C\},

(5.22)

and so (
5.11
) in fact holds with
U_{1}
replaced by
U_{1}/2
, provided that
U_{1}\geq 2C
.

Estimates for the energy momentum tensor components:

Recall the expressions (
2.10
)–(
2.12
) for the components of the energy momentum tensor, along with the expression (
3.2
) for the components of the energy momentum tensor of
f_{\circ}
. By (

), for any

|f(u,v,p^{v},L)-f_{\circ}(u,v,p^{v},L)|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}.

(5.23)

Define

L_{\circ}^{2}=R_{\circ}^{4}\gamma_{AB}p^{A}p^{B},

and note that

. It follows from the support property (

\displaystyle\Big|\Omega^{-4}R^{-2}T_{uu}-\Omega^{-4}_{\circ}R^{-2}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{uu}\Big|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{L_{0}}\int_{0}^{Ct^{-1}}|f-f_{\circ}|\,p^{v}dp^{v}R^{-4}L\,dL\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{6}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}.

Noting that
\Omega^{2}R^{-2}p^{u}=\Omega^{2}_{\circ}R^{-2}_{\circ}\mathring{p}^{u}
, it similarly follows that

\displaystyle\Big|\Omega^{-2}R^{-4}T_{uv}-\Omega^{-2}_{\circ}R^{-4}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{uv}\Big|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{L_{0}}\int_{0}^{Ct^{-1}}|f-f_{\circ}|\,\frac{\Omega^{2}p^{u}}{R^{2}}dp^{v}R^{-4}L\,dL\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{8}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}},

and

\displaystyle\Big|R^{-6}T_{vv}-R^{-6}_{\circ}T^{\circ}_{vv}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{L_{0}}\int_{0}^{Ct^{-1}}|f-f_{\circ}|\,\frac{\Omega^{4}(p^{u})^{2}}{R^{4}p^{v}}dp^{v}R^{-4}L\,dL\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{10}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}.

\displaystyle\Big|T_{uu}-\frac{1}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}+t^{-2}|R-R_{\circ}|+t^{-2}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|,

\displaystyle\Big|T_{uv}-\frac{1}{2t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{3}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}+t^{-2}|R-R_{\circ}|+t^{-2}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|,

(5.25)

\displaystyle\Big|T_{vv}-\frac{1}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{t^{4}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}/2\}}+t^{-2}|R-R_{\circ}|.

(5.26)

Estimates for the metric quantities in the region
u\leq U_{1}
:

For the metric quantities, consider the region
U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}
and recall that

t^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim r\sim R^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim v,\qquad\text{for }U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1},\quad v\geq v_{0}.

Let
u
and

be such that
U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1}
,
v\geq v_{0}
and
t_{0}\leq t(u,v)<T
.

Note that

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}(R^{2}-R_{\circ}^{2})=R_{\circ}(\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}\big)+(R-R_{\circ})\lambda.

Equation (
5.3
) and the estimate (
5.25
) then imply that

\big|\partial_{u}\big(R_{\circ}(\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}\big)+(R-R_{\circ})\lambda\big|\lesssim|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|+|R-R_{\circ}|+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-2}.

Thus, integrating and using the boundary condition (
5.1
),

|\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}|(u,v)\lesssim v^{-1}|R-R_{\circ}|(u,v)+v^{-2}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u^{\prime},v)+|R-R_{\circ}|(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-4}.

(5.27)

Similarly, since

\nu-\nu_{\circ}=\Omega^{2}\big(\Omega^{-2}\nu-\Omega^{-2}_{\circ}\nu_{\circ}\big)-\nu_{\circ}(1-\Omega^{2}\Omega^{-2}_{\circ}),

it follows from equation (
5.5
) and the estimate (
5.25
) and the boundary condition (
5.1
) that

|\nu-\nu_{\circ}|(u,v)\lesssim v^{-2}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u,v)+v^{-2}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u^{\prime},v)+|R-R_{\circ}|(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-2}.

(5.28)

Moreover,

\partial_{u}(R-R_{\circ})=\nu-\nu_{\circ},

and so (
5.28
) and the Grönwall inequality imply that

|R-R_{\circ}|(u,v)\lesssim v^{-2}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-2}.

(5.29)

Returning to (
5.27
) and (
5.28
), one then has

|\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}|(u,v)\lesssim v^{-2}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-3},

(5.30)

and

|\nu-\nu_{\circ}|(u,v)\lesssim v^{-2}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u,v)+v^{-2}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(u^{\prime},v)du^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-2}.

(5.31)

Now, by equation (
5.4
) and the estimate (
5.25
)

\Big|\partial_{u}\partial_{v}\log\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}_{\circ}}\Big|\lesssim v^{-4}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|+v^{-4}|R-R_{\circ}|+v^{-3}|\nu-\nu_{\circ}|+v^{-4}|\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}|+\varepsilon_{0}v^{-4},

(5.32)

Integrating in
u
and
v
, using the boundary condition (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), and inserting (
5.29
)–(
5.31
), it follows that

\Big|\log\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}_{\circ}}\Big|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}+\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\int_{U_{0}}^{u}(v^{\prime})^{-4}|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})du^{\prime}dv^{\prime}

(5.33)

Now since, for
\varepsilon
suitably small,

|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|\lesssim\Omega^{2}\Big|\log\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}_{\circ}}\Big|\lesssim v^{2}\Big|\log\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}_{\circ}}\Big|\qquad\text{for }U_{0}\leq u\leq U_{1},

it follows from the Grönwall inequality, after integrating (
5.33
) once more in
u
, that

|\Omega^{2}-\Omega_{\circ}^{2}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}v^{2}.

Returning to (
5.29
)–(
5.31
), it then follows that

|R-R_{\circ}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\qquad|\lambda-\lambda_{\circ}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},\qquad|\nu-\nu_{\circ}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

Similarly, returning to (
5.32
),

\Big|\partial_{v}\log\Omega^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}v^{-2},\qquad\Big|\partial_{u}\log\Omega^{2}-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

The region
u\geq U_{1}
:

In order to see that the solution is isometric to the spatially homogeneous FLRW solution (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) in the region
u\geq U_{1}
, recall first (
5.23
), from which it follows that

\big(R^{-6}T_{vv}\big)(U_{1},v)=\big(R_{\circ}^{-6}T^{\circ}_{vv}\big)(U_{1},v)=t(U_{1},v)^{-4}r(U_{1},v)^{-6}\qquad\text{for all }v\geq V_{1},

where
V_{1}
is such that
R(U_{1},V_{1})=0
. Recall the residual gauge freedom of Remark
2.1
and consider the following change of gauge. Define increasing functions
\widetilde{v}(v)
and
\widetilde{u}(u)
as solutions of

\widetilde{v}(V_{1})=U_{1},\qquad\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(v)=\lambda(U_{1},v)\lambda_{\circ}^{-1}(U_{1},\widetilde{v}(v)),\qquad\text{for }v\geq V_{1},

and

\widetilde{u}(u)=u\quad\text{for }u\leq U_{1},\qquad\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(V_{1})}\Omega^{2}(u,V_{1})\Omega_{\circ}^{-2}(\widetilde{u}(u),V_{1})\quad\text{for }u>U_{1},

respectively. It follows that
\{u=U_{1}\}=\{\widetilde{u}=U_{1}\}
, and moreover the relations (
2.3
) and (
2.6
) imply that the solution in the resulting gauge satisfies

R(U_{1},U_{1})=0,\qquad\Omega^{2}(U_{1},U_{1})=\Omega_{\circ}^{2}(U_{1},U_{1})\qquad\partial_{v}R(U_{1},v)=\partial_{v}R_{\circ}(U_{1},v),\qquad\text{for all }v\geq U_{1}.

The Raychaudhuri equation (
2.16
), restricted to
u=U_{1}
, takes the form

\partial_{v}(\Omega^{-2}\partial_{v}R)(U_{1},v)=-\frac{1}{2}R^{7}\Omega^{-2}\big(R_{\circ}^{-6}T^{\circ}_{vv}\big)(U_{1},v).

It then follows that

R(U_{1},v)=R_{\circ}(U_{1},v),\qquad\Omega^{2}(U_{1},v)=\Omega_{\circ}^{2}(U_{1},v)\qquad\text{for all }v\geq U_{1}.

By uniqueness for the characteristic initial value problem, it follows that
R=R_{\circ}
and
\Omega^{2}=\Omega^{2}_{\circ}
for all
u\geq U_{1}
.
∎

The conclusion of the proof of the main theorem

Recall the set
\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1},T}\subset{Q}
defined by (

). Consider the set
\mathcal{A}\subset[t_{0},\infty)
of times

such that the solution exists up to time

— in the sense that
(u(t,r),v(t,r))\in\mathcal{Q}
for all

,
r\geq 0
— and moreover satisfies the estimates (
5.12
)–(
5.14
) for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1},T}
for some
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that the conclusion of Theorem
5.1
holds. The set
\mathcal{A}\subset[t_{0},\infty)
is manifestly connected and open, and is moreover non-empty by local existence and Cauchy stability, provided
\varepsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small. By Theorem
2.3
, the bounds (
5.13
)–(
5.14
) imply that
(u(T,r),v(T,r))\in\mathcal{Q}
for all
r\geq 0
such that
u(T,r)\leq U_{1}
. By Theorem
5.1
, the solution is isometric to the spatially homogeneous FLRW solution (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) in the region
\{t_{0}\leq t(u,v)<T\}\cap\{u\geq U_{1}\}
and thus
(u(T,r),v(T,r))\in\mathcal{Q}
for all
r\geq 0
. Theorem
5.1
moreover guarantees that the bounds (
5.12
)–(
5.14
) cannot be saturated, and thus the set
\mathcal{A}
is moreover closed, and hence equal to
[t_{0},\infty)
.

The future geodesic completeness follows from the bounds on the Christoffel symbols arising from (
5.13
)–(

) (as, for example in Section 16 of

]

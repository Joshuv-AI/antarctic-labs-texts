Gerghaty type results via simulation and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -class functions with application
Azhar Hussain, Muhammad Ishfaq Tanzeela Kanwal Stojan Radenović

Abstract




Abstract
: In this paper we study the notion of Gerghaty type contractive mapping via simulation function along with
\mathcal{C}
-class functions and prove the existence of several fixed point results in ordinary and partially ordered metric spaces. An example is given to show the validity of our results given herein. Moreover, existence of solution of two-point boundary value second order nonlinear differential equation is obtain.

: 54H25, 47H10

Keywords
: Simulation functions,
\mathcal{C}
-class function, partially ordered metric space.

1
Introduction

The Banach contraction principle
[
4
]
is one of the fundamental result
in metric fixed point theory. Because of its importance in nonlinear analysis, number of authors
have improved, generalized and extended this basic result either by defining a new contractive
mapping in the context of a complete metric space or by investigating the existing
contractive mappings in various abstract spaces (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
18
,
26
]
and references therein).

In particularly, Geraghty
[
8
]
consider an auxiliary function and generalized the Banach contraction in the frame work of complete metric space. Later on, Amini-Harandi and Emami
[
26
]
obtained similar results in the setting of partially ordered metric spaces. Using the concept of Samet
[
9
]
, Cho et al.
[
6
]
generalized Geraghty contraction to
\alpha
-Geraghty contraction and prove a fixed point theorem for such contraction.
On the other hand, Khojasteh
et al.

[
13
]
introduced the notion of
\mathcal{Z}
-contraction by using a function called simulation function and proved a version of Banach contraction principle.

\displaystyle<\mathcal{G}(\beta(M(x_{n(k)},x^{*}))M(x_{n(k)},x^{*})),\alpha(x_{n(k)},x^{*})d(Tx_{n(k)},Tx^{*})).

By definition of
\mathcal{G}
, we get that

\alpha(x_{n(k)},x^{*})d(Tx_{n(k)},Tx^{*})<\beta(M(x_{n(k)},x^{*}))M(x_{n(k)},x^{*})).

(13)

From (
12

d(x_{n(k)+1},Tx^{*})<\beta(M(x_{n(k)},x^{*}))M(x_{n(k)},x^{*})),

(14)

where

\displaystyle M(x_{n(k)},x^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{d(x_{n(k)},x^{*}),d(x_{n(k)},Tx_{n(k)}),d(x^{*},Tx^{*})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{d(x_{n(k)},x^{*}),d(x_{n(k)},x_{n(k)+1}),d(x^{*},Tx^{*})\}.

Letting

in the above inequality, we get that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}M(x_{n(k)},x^{*})=d(x^{*},Tx^{*}).

(15)

Suppose

.
By definition of
\beta
and (

), we have

Letting

in the above inequality and using (

∎

For the uniqueness of fixed point, we consider the following hypothesis:

(\mathcal{U})
For all

, there exists

\alpha(x,z)\geq 1
and

Theorem 3.3
.

Adding condition

to the hypothesis of Theorem

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
(resp. Theorem
3.2
), we have a fixed point,

of

T

. Then, by assumption

,
there exists
z\in X
such that

\alpha(x^{*},z)\geq 1~~and~~\alpha(y^{*},z)\geq 1.

Since
T
is a
\alpha
-admissible, we have

\alpha(x^{*},T^{n}z)\geq 1~~and~~\alpha(y^{*},T^{n}z)\geq 1

. Hence we have

\displaystyle d(x^{*},T^{n}z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(Tx^{*},TT^{n-1}z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha(x^{*},T^{n-1}z)d(Tx^{*},TT^{n-1}z).

(17)

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}\leq\zeta(\alpha(x^{*},T^{n-1}z)d(Tx^{*},TT^{n-1}z),\beta(M(x^{*},T^{n-1}z))M(x^{*},T^{n-1}z)

By definition of
\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\alpha(x^{*},T^{n-1}z)d(Tx^{*},TT^{n-1}z)<\beta(M(x^{*},T^{n-1}z))M(x^{*},T^{n-1}z),

\displaystyle d(x^{*},T^{n-1}z).

\displaystyle\alpha(x^{*},T^{n-1}z)d(Tx^{*},TT^{n-1}z)

\displaystyle\beta(d(x^{*},T^{n-1}z))d(x^{*},T^{n-1}z).

(18)

Inequality (

\displaystyle d(x^{*},T^{n}z)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\beta(d(x^{*},T^{n-1}z))d(x^{*},T^{n-1}z).

4
Fixed point results in partially ordered metric space

Let
(X,d,\preceq)
be a partially ordered metric space. Many authors has proved the existence of fixed point results in the frame work of partially order metric spaces (see for example
[
21
,
1

18

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,d,\preceq)
be a complete partially ordered metric space and let
x\preceq y
for all
x,y\in X

T:X\rightarrow X

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{G}}
-Geraghty contraction if there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{F}

\zeta(d(Tx,Ty),\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y))\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}},

where

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,d,\preceq)
be a complete partially ordered metric space with
x\preceq y
for all
x,y\in X
. Let
T:X\rightarrow X
be a continuous mapping satisfying

(1)

T
is
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{G}}
-Geraghty contraction;

(2)

T
is increasing;

(3)

there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
x_{1}\preceq Tx_{1}
.

Then
T
has a fixed point
x^{*}\in X
and
T
is a Picard operator that

{T^{n}x_{1}}
converges to
x^{*}
.

Proof.

Define
\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
by

\alpha(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&~if~x\preceq y,\\
0,&otherwise.\end{cases}

Since
T
is a

-Geraghty contraction, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(d(Tx,Ty),\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y),d(Tx,Ty)).

By definition of
\mathcal{G}
, we get

\displaystyle d(Tx,Ty)<\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y),

so,

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)d(Tx,Ty)\leq d(Tx,Ty)<\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y).

Hence
T
is
\mathcal{Z}_{(\alpha,\mathcal{G})}
-Geraghty contraction. Since
T
is increasing,
\alpha(x,y)=1
implies
\alpha(Tx,Ty)=1
for all
x,y\in X
. Further if
z=Ty
, then
\alpha(y,z)=1
, this implies
\alpha(x,z)=1
. Thus,
T
is triangular

\alpha
-admissible. Condition
(2)
implies that there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that

\alpha(x_{1},Tx_{1})=1
, and so condition
(3)
of Theorem
3.1
is
satisfied.
Thus by Theorem
3.1
,

has a fixed point in
X
.
∎

Denote by

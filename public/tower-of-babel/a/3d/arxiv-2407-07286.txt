Abstract
. We show that a large class of infinite measure preserving dynamical systems that do not admit physical measures nevertheless exhibit strong statistical properties. In particular, we give sufficient conditions for existence of a distinguished natural measure ν \nu such that the pushforwards of any absolutely continuous probability measure converge to ν \nu . Moreover, we obtain a distributional limit law for empirical measures. Both of these are new results for intermittent maps with at least two neutral fixed points preserving an infinite σ \sigma -finite absolutely continuous measure. We also extend existing results on the characterisation of the set of almost sure limit points for empirical measures. This result is new when there are at least three neutral fixed points.

Introduction

Let

2.1.
Maps with Gibbs-Markov first return maps

In this section we recall some definitions and basic properties of maps with Gibbs-Markov first return maps needed to state our main assumptions.

Let
X
be a compact metric space with Borel probability measure
m
and
let
f:X\to X
be a nonsingular measurable map, so
m(f^{-1}E)=0
for every null set
E
.
We suppose that
f
is ergodic, so for every measurable set
E
with
f^{-1}E=E
we have
m(E)=0
or
1
. Also, we suppose that
f
is
conservative, which is equivalent to requiring that
f^{n}x\in E
infinitely often for a.e.
x\in E
for any positive measure set
E
.
(A standard reference for this material is
[
4
, Ch 1]
.)

We say that the map
f
together with a countable partition
{\mathcal{P}}
of
X
into positive measure subsets is a
Markov map
if the restriction of
f
to each partition element is a measurable bijection onto a union of partition elements.
For
\theta\in(0,1)
, we define the symbolic metric

d_{\theta}(x,y)\coloneqq\theta^{s(x,y)}
where

s(x,y)\coloneqq\inf\{n{\geq 0}:f^{n}x,f^{n}y\text{ lie in different elements of }{\mathcal{P}}\}
.
It is assumed that
s(x,y)=\infty
if and only if
x=y
so that
d_{\theta}
is a metric.

A Markov map
f
is said to be
topologically mixing
(w.r.t. the symbolic metric topology) if for every pair of open nonempty subsets

U,V\subset X
, there
exists an
N\geq 1
such that
f^{n}U\cap V\neq\emptyset
for every
n\geq N
.
Equivalently, for every

a,b\in{\mathcal{P}}
there exists an
N\geq 1
such that
f^{n}a\cap b\neq\emptyset
for every
n\geq N
.

A standard approach in dynamical systems is
inducing
, whereby one studies the complex dynamics of
f
on
X
by focusing on the “nicer” return dynamics to a well-chosen inducing domain
Y\subset X
.
We take

Y\subset X
to be a union of partition elements and define the
first return time

\tau:Y\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
first return map

F:Y\to Y
by setting

\tau(y)\coloneqq\inf\{n\geq 1:f^{n}y\in Y\},\qquad F(y)\coloneqq f^{\tau(y)}y.

(Conservativity ensures that
\tau
is finite a.e. and hence that
F
is well-defined a.e.)

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}
be the partition of
Y
consisting of sets

\big(\bigcap_{j=0}^{n-1}f^{-j}a_{i}\big)\cap f^{-n}Y
where

a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
a_{0}\subset Y
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}\subset X\setminus Y
. Then
F
is Markov with partition
{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}
.

For the map
F
, we define the symbolic metric
d_{\theta}=\theta^{s}
w.r.t. the partition
{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}
.
The map
F
is
Gibbs-Markov
if

•

finite images
:
\operatorname{Card}\{Fa:a\in{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}\}<\infty
;

•

bounded distortion
: there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that the function
\log\frac{dm}{dm\circ F}
is
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz on elements of
{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{B}}_{\theta}(Y)
the space of real-valued functions which are Lipschitz continuous on
Y
with respect to the metric
d_{\theta}
. We recall the following classical result about the existence of invariant measures for maps with Gibbs-Markov first return maps.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
f:X\to X
is a conservative ergodic Markov map with Gibbs-Markov first return map
F=f^{\tau}:Y\to Y
. Then
f
preserves a unique (up to scaling) absolutely continuous
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
, and
\mu
is equivalent to

.

Moreover, the density
h=d\mu/dm
is nonzero and bounded on
Y
and

h|_{Y},\,(h|_{Y})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\theta}(Y)
. In particular,
\mu(Y)<\infty
.

Furthermore,
\mu(X)<\infty
if and only if
\tau\in L^{1}(Y,m)
.

Proof.

Standard references include
[
1
]
and
[
4
, Chapter 4]

3.1.
Ergodic theorem

In this subsection, we recall the ergodic theorem for infinite measure systems and derive a consequence for the systems studied in this paper.

Lemma 3.1

Assume that
H1:
holds.

Lemma 3.2

Assume that

Proof.

We provide the details for completeness (cf.

For

By

.
Moreover, (
4.12
) implies
1/[(F^{n})^{\prime}y]\lesssim|a|
for all
y\in a
.
Using the standard pointwise formula for the transfer operator
L_{Y}
for
F
, we obtain

\displaystyle|L_{Y}^{n}1(x)-L_{Y}^{n}1(y)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{\tilde{x}\in F^{-n}x,\,\tilde{y}\in F^{-n}y}\frac{1}{(F^{n})^{\prime}\tilde{x}}-\frac{1}{(F^{n})^{\prime}\tilde{y}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tilde{x}\in F^{-n}x,\,\tilde{y}\in F^{-n}}\left|\frac{(F^{n})^{\prime}\tilde{y}}{(F^{n})^{\prime}\tilde{x}}-1\right|\left|\frac{1}{(F^{n})^{\prime}\tilde{y}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{P}}_{Y}^{(n)}}|x-y||a|\lesssim|x-y|.

Similarly, one can check that
L_{Y}^{n}
is uniformly bounded. The Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem then yields that
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}L_{Y}^{j}1
has a subsequence which is uniformly convergent to some Lipschitz
\tilde{h}:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
. As
\tilde{h}
is necessarily the density of an invariant measure it follows that
h1_{Y}=\tilde{h}
yielding that
h
is Lipschitz (and hence continuous). This establishes our claim and concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Series estimates

In this appendix, we carry out some calculations required in Section
3.4
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)^{\alpha-1}j^{-\alpha}=\tfrac{\pi}{\sin\alpha\pi}
.

Proof.

The function
(n-x)^{\alpha-1}x^{-\alpha}
has one critical point at

x=\alpha n
. Hence, by approximating integrals by Riemann sums on the intervals

[0,\alpha n]
and
[\alpha n,n]
,

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)^{\alpha-1}j^{-\alpha}=\int_{0}^{n}(n-x)^{\alpha-1}x^{-\alpha}\,dx+O(n^{-\alpha})+O(n^{\alpha-1}).

Using standard properties of Beta and Gamma functions, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n}(n-x)^{\alpha-1}x^{-\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{\alpha-1}x^{-\alpha}\,dx=\operatorname{B}(\alpha,1-\alpha)=\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)/\Gamma(1)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)=\tfrac{\pi}{\sin\alpha\pi},

as required.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
g:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)=0
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j^{-\alpha}g(j)\,(n-j)^{\alpha-1}=0

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)^{\alpha-1}g(n-j)\,j^{-\alpha}=0
.

Proof.

Set
C=\max_{n}|g(n)|
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j^{-\alpha}g(j)\,(n-j)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j<\varepsilon n}j^{-\alpha}g(j)(n-j)^{\alpha-1}+\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq j<n}j^{-\alpha}g(j)(n-j)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{1-\alpha}n^{\alpha-1}\sum_{1\leq j<\varepsilon n}j^{-\alpha}+(\varepsilon n)^{-\alpha}\max_{k\geq\varepsilon n}g(k)\sum_{\varepsilon n\leq j<n}(n-j)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\alpha-1}(1+(\varepsilon n)^{1-\alpha})+(\varepsilon n)^{-\alpha}\max_{k\geq\varepsilon n}g(k)n^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}+\varepsilon^{-\alpha}\max_{k\geq\varepsilon n}g(k).

Hence,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}j^{-\alpha}g(j)(n-j)^{\alpha-1}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}.

The first limit follows since
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small.
Replacing
\alpha
by
1-\alpha
, we obtain the second limit.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
g(x)=o(1)
as
x\to\infty
.
Then
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g(j)}{j}=o(\log n)
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, choose
n_{0}\geq 1
such that
|g(x)|<\varepsilon
for
x>n_{0}
.
Then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g(j)}{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}\frac{g(j)}{j}+\varepsilon\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n}\frac{1}{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}\frac{g(j)}{j}+\varepsilon\log n.

Hence

\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\log n)^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g(j)}{j}\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2}:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
\lim_{x\to\infty}g_{i}(x)=1
for
i=1,2
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\frac{g_{2}(n-j)}{\log(n-j)}=1.

Proof.

Write

\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\frac{g_{2}(n-j)}{\log(n-j)}=S_{1}+S_{2}

where

S_{1}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n/2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\frac{g_{2}(n-j)}{\log(n-j)},\qquad S_{2}=\sum_{n/2<j\leq n-2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\frac{g_{2}(n-j)}{\log(n-j)}.

Now,

\displaystyle|S_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|g_{1}|_{\infty}|g_{2}|_{\infty}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leq j\leq n-2}\frac{1}{\log(n-j)}

\displaystyle=|g_{1}|_{\infty}|g_{2}|_{\infty}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{2\leq j\leq n/2}\frac{1}{\log j}\leq|g_{1}|_{\infty}|g_{2}|_{\infty}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{2\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{\log j}.

But

\sum_{2\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{\log j}\lesssim\frac{n}{\log n}+\sum_{n/\log n\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{\log j}\leq\frac{n}{\log n}+\frac{1}{\log(n/\log n)}n\lesssim\frac{n}{\log n},

so
|S_{2}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\log n}
.

By Lemma
A.3
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\sim\log n
. Hence,

S_{1}\leq\sup_{k\geq n/2}g_{2}(k)\frac{1}{\log n/2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n/2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\sim\frac{1}{\log n/2}\log n/2=1.

Similarly,

S_{1}\geq\inf_{k\geq n/2}g_{2}(k)\frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n/2}\frac{g_{1}(j)}{j}\sim\frac{1}{\log n}\log n/2\sim 1.

Hence
S_{1}+S_{2}\sim S_{1}\sim 1
as required.
∎

A New Interference-Alignment Scheme for Wireless MapReduce
Yue Bi 1 2 , Michèle Wigger 2 , Yue Wu 1

Abstract
We consider a full-duplex wireless Distributed Computing (DC) system under the MapReduce framework. New upper and lower bounds on the optimal tradeoff between Normalized Delivery Time (NDT) and computation load are presented. The upper bound strictly improves over the previous reported upper bounds and is based on a novel interference alignment (IA) scheme tailored to the interference cancellation capabilities of MapReduce nodes. The lower bound is proved through information-theoretic converse arguments.



I
Introduction

Distributed Computing (DC) systems
are computer networks that through task-parallelization reduce execution times of complex computing tasks such as data mining or computer vision. MapReduce is a popular such framework and runs in three phases
[
1
,
2
]
. In the first
map phase
, nodes calculate intermediate values (IVA) from their associated input files. In the following
shuffle phase
, nodes exchange these IVAs in a way that each node obtains all IVAs required to compute its assigned output function in the final
reduce phase
. MapReduce is primarily applied to wired systems where it has been noticed that a significant part of the MapReduce execution time stems from the IVA
delivery time
during the shuffle phase
[
2
,
3
]
, and can be reduced through smart coding
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
3
,
7
,
8
]
.

MapReduce systems are becoming important building blocks also in wireless scenarios, such as vehicular networks
[
9
]
or distributed e-health applications
[
10
]
, thus creating a need for good wireless MapReduce coding schemes. Similarly to the wired case
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, delivery time in wireless MapReduce systems can be decreased by sending appropriate linear combinations of the IVAs, from which the receiving nodes can extract their desired IVAs by bootstrapping the IVAs that they can compute from their locally stored input files. Further improvements can however be achieved by exploiting specific wireless communication techniques.

The focus of this paper is on the high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) regime, and on the following two key metrics:

•

Computation load

\mathsf{r}
: This describes the average number of nodes to which each file is assigned. In other words, it is the ratio of the total number of assigned input files (including replications) normalized by
the total number of files.

•

Normalized Delivery Time (NDT)

\Delta
: This is the wireless shuffle duration normalized by the number of reduce functions and input files and by the transmission time of a single IVA over a point-to-point channel.

We are interested in the minimal NDT for given computation load
\mathsf{r}
, which we call the
NDT-computation tradeoff
.

The NDT of full-duplex interference networks was considered in
[
11
,
12
]
, see
[
13
]
for the half-duplex network. In
[
11
]
, the authors proposed to divide the nodes into groups and let each group store a subset of the files and apply one-shot beamforming and zero-forcing during the shuffle communication. As shown in
[
11
]
, their scheme is optimal among this class of communication strategies. In our previous work
[
12
]
, we reduced this NDT by introducing the IA technique to the shuffle communication in
[
11
]
.

In this paper, we obtain yet a further NDT reduction by considering the map procedure in
[
4
]
, where
each set of
\mathsf{r}
nodes
stores a subset of the files, and by proposing a novel IA scheme that is tailored to this file assignment and the interference cancellation capabilities of MapReduce nodes so as to obtain an improved performance compared to standard IA schemes. Our scheme is related to the IA scheme in
[
14
]
, which considers a similar file assignment, and to our previous work
[
12
]
, with which it coincides when each node can only store a single file.

The upper bound on the NDT implied by our new IA-scheme improves over the previously proposed bounds in
[
11
,
12
]
whenever the computation load
\mathsf{r}
satisfies
1<\mathsf{r}<\left\lceil\frac{\mathsf{K}-1}{2}\right\rceil
.
Our results thus show that in this regime, beamforming and zero-forcing cannot achieve minimum NDT. On the contrary, through an information-theoretic lower bound on the minimum NDT, in this manuscript we show that for
\mathsf{r}\geq\frac{\mathsf{K}}{2}
the
zero-forcing and interference cancellation scheme in
[
11
]
is optimal also without any restriction on the utilized coding scheme.

II
Wireless MapReduce Framework

Consider a distributed computing (DC) system with a fixed number of
\mathsf{K}
nodes labelled
1,\ldots,\mathsf{K}
; a large number
\mathsf{N}
of input files
W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{N}}
; and
\mathsf{K}
output functions
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\mathsf{K}}
mapping the input files to the desired computations.

A
MapReduce
System decomposes the output functions as:

\phi_{q}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{N}})=v_{q}(a_{q,1},\ldots,a_{q,\mathsf{N}}),\qquad q\in[\mathsf{K}],

(1)

where
v_{q}
is called
reduce function
and
a_{q,p}
the
intermediate value (IVA)
calculated from file
W_{p}
through a
map function

a_{q,p}=u_{q,p}(W_{p}),\qquad p\in[\mathsf{N}].

(2)

IVAs are independent with
\mathsf{A}
i.i.d. bits.

The MapReduce framework has 3 phases:

Map phase
: A subset of all input files
\mathcal{M}_{k}\subseteq[\mathsf{N}]
is assigned to each node
k\in[\mathsf{K}]
. Node
k
computes all IVAs
\{a_{q,p}\colon p\in\mathcal{M}_{k},\,q\in[\mathsf{K}]\}
associated with these input files. Notice that the set
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}\}_{k\in[\mathsf{K}]}
is a design factor.

Shuffle phase:
Computation of the
k
-th output function is assigned to the
k
-th node.

The
\mathsf{K}
nodes in the system communicate over
\mathsf{T}
uses of a wireless network in a full-duplex mode to exchange the . missing IVAs for the computations of their assigned output functions. So, node
k\in[\mathsf{K}]
produces complex channel inputs of the form

{\mathbf{X}}_{k}\triangleq(X_{k}(1),\ldots,X_{k}(\mathsf{T}))^{{T}}=f_{k}^{(\mathsf{T})}\left(\{a_{1,p},\ldots,a_{\mathsf{K},p}\}_{p\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\right),

(3)

by means of an encoding function
f_{k}^{(\mathsf{T})}
on appropriate domains and so that the inputs satisfy the block-power constraint

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{k}(t)|^{2}\right]\leq\mathsf{P},\qquad k\in[\mathsf{K}].

(4)

Given the full-duplex nature of the network, Node
k
also observes the complex channel outputs

Y_{k}(t)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\{k\}}H_{k,k^{\prime}}(t)X_{k^{\prime}}(t)+Z_{k}(t),\quad t\in[\mathsf{T}],

(5)

where the sequences of complex-valued channel coefficients
\{H_{k,k^{\prime}}(t)\}
and standard circularly symmetric Gaussian noises
\{Z_{k}(t)\}
are both i.i.d. and independent of each other and of all other channel coefficients and noises.

Based on its outputs
{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}\triangleq(Y_{k}(1),\ldots,Y_{k}(\mathsf{T}))^{{T}}
and the IVAs
\{a_{q,p}\colon p\in\mathcal{M}_{k},\;q\in[\mathsf{K}]\}
it computed during the Map phase, Node
k
decodes the missing IVAs
\{a_{k,p}\colon p\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}\}
required to compute its assigned output functions
\phi_{k}
as:

\hat{a}_{k,p}=g_{k,p}^{(\mathsf{T})}\left(\{a_{1,i},\ldots,a_{\mathsf{K},i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{M}_{k}},{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}\right),\quad p\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}.

(6)

Reduce phase:
Each node
k\in[\mathsf{K}]
applies reduce functions
\phi_{k}(\cdot)
to the appropriate IVAs calculated during the Map phase or decoded in the Shuffle phase.

The performance of the distributed computing system is measured in terms of its
computation load

\mathsf{r}\triangleq\sum_{k\in[\mathsf{K}]}\frac{|\mathcal{M}_{k}|}{\mathsf{N}},

(7)

and the
normalized delivery time (NDT)

\mathsf{\Delta}\triangleq\varliminf_{\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\infty}\varliminf_{\mathsf{A}\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{A}\cdot\mathsf{K}\cdot\mathsf{N}}\cdot\log\mathsf{P}.

(8)

We focus on the
fundamental NDT-computation tradeoff

\Delta^{*}(\mathsf{r})
, which is defined as the infimum over all values of
\Delta
satisfying (
8
) for some choice of file assignments
\{\mathcal{M}_{k}\}
and sequence (in
\mathsf{T}
) of encoding and decoding functions
\{f_{k}^{(\mathsf{T})}\}
and
\{g_{k,p}^{(\mathsf{T})}\}
in (
3
) and (
6
), all depending on
\mathsf{A}
so that the probability of IVA decoding error

\textnormal{Pr}\bigg[\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in[{\mathsf{K}}]\end{subarray}}\;\bigcup_{p\notin\mathcal{M}_{k}}\hat{a}_{k,p}\neq a_{k,p}\bigg]\to 0\quad\textnormal{as}\quad\mathsf{A}\to\infty.

(9)

II-A

Sufficiency of Symmetric File Assignments

Our model exhibits a perfect symmetry between the various nodes in the network because the channels from any Tx-node to any Rx-node are independent and have identical statistics. The optimal NDT-computation tradeoff can therefore be achieved by a symmetric file assignment where any subset of nodes
\mathcal{T}\subseteq[\mathsf{K}]
of size
i
is assigned the same number of files to be stored at all nodes in
\mathcal{T}
. In fact, any non-symmetric file assignment can be symmetrized without decreasing the NDT-computation tradeoff. It suffices to time-share
\mathsf{K}!
instances of the original scheme for a IVA size that is also multiplied by
\mathsf{K}!
, where in each instance the
\mathsf{K}
nodes are relabeled according to a different permutation. The resulting scheme has a symmetric file assignment and achieves the same NDT-computation tradeoff as the original scheme because both
\mathsf{T}
and
\mathsf{A}
are multiplied by
\mathsf{K}!
while the other parameters remain unchanged and because the new scheme still satisfies (
9
).

II-B

Relation to the Sum-DoF with
\mathsf{r}
-fold Cooperation

A well-studied property of wireless networks is the
Sum Degrees of Freedom (sum-DoF)
, which characterizes the maximum throughput of a network. In this work we are specifically interested in the sum-DoF that one can achieve over the wireless network described by (
5
), when the inputs are subject to the average power constraints (
4
) and any set of
\mathsf{r}
nodes
\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}}
has a message
M_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}
that it wishes to convey to Node
j
, for any
j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T}
. Each message
M_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}
is uniformly distributed over a set
[2^{nR_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}}]
and a rate-tuple
(R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}\colon\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T})
is called achievable if there exists a sequence of encoding and decoding functions such that the probabilities of error tend to 0 in the asymptotic regime of infinite blocklengths. The sum-DoF is then defined as

\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})\triangleq\sup\varlimsup_{P\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T}}R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}(\mathsf{P})}{\frac{1}{2}\log\mathsf{P}},

(10)

where the supremum is over sequences
\{(R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}(\mathsf{P})\colon\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T})\}_{\mathsf{P}>0}
so that for each
\mathsf{P}>0
each tuple
(R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}(\mathsf{P})\colon\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T})
is achievable under power
\mathsf{P}
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\mathsf{r}\in[\mathsf{K}]
:

\mathsf{\Delta}(\mathsf{r})\geq\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\right)\frac{1}{\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})}.

(11)

while the desired signals will be separable from each other and from this interference space.

has 6 signal spaces for the

to Node

Similar considerations hold for Nodes

4

to Node 1. The SumDoF achieved by the scheme is thus:

\textnormal{Sum-DoF}=22/7
.

IV
New Bounds on the NDT

Define for any integer value
\mathsf{r}\in[\mathsf{K}]
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textnormal{Ub}}(\mathsf{r})\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\right)\cdot\frac{\mathsf{r}(\mathsf{K}-1)+\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}-1}{\mathsf{r}(\mathsf{K}-1)^{2}+\mathsf{r}(\mathsf{K}-2)}&\textrm{if }\mathsf{r}<\mathsf{K}/2\\[5.16663pt]
\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}}\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\right)&\textrm{if }\mathsf{r}\geq\mathsf{K}/2\end{array}\right..

Also, let

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textnormal{Lb}}(\mathsf{r})\triangleq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}}\left(2-\frac{3}{\mathsf{K}}\right)&&\text{if }\mathsf{r}=1,\\
&\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}}\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}+\!\max_{t\in[\lfloor\mathsf{K}/2\rfloor]}\textnormal{lowc}\left(C_{t}(\mathsf{r})\right)\right)&&\text{if }\mathsf{r}\in(1,2),\\
&\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}}\left(1-\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}+\textnormal{lowc}\left({C}_{\lfloor\mathsf{K}/2\rfloor}(\mathsf{r})\right)\right)&&\text{if }\mathsf{r}\in[2,\mathsf{K}],\end{aligned}\right.

where for any

:

\displaystyle C_{t}(i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\binom{\mathsf{K}-i}{t-i}}{\binom{\mathsf{K}}{t}\cdot t}\cdot(\mathsf{K}-t-i),&\textnormal{if }i\in[t],\\
0,&\textnormal{if }i\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash[t]\end{cases}

(48)

and for any function
f
,
\textnormal{lowc}\left(f(\ell)\right)
denotes the lower convex enveloppe of
\{(\ell,f(\ell))\}
.

.

The NDT-computation tradeoff
\Delta^{*}(\mathsf{r})
is upper- and lower-bounded as:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textnormal{Lb}}(\mathsf{r})\leq\Delta^{*}(\mathsf{r})\leq\textnormal{lowc}\left({\Delta}_{\textnormal{Ub}}(\mathsf{r})\right).

(49)

Proof:

For integers
\mathsf{r}\geq\mathsf{K}/2
achievability of
\Delta_{\textnormal{Ub}}(\mathsf{r})
is proved in
[
11
]
. For integers
\mathsf{r}<\mathsf{K}/2
achievability of
\Delta_{\textnormal{Ub}}(\mathsf{r})
holds by Lemma
1
and the scheme in Section

. Achievability of the lower convex enveloppe follows by simple time- and memory-sharing strategies. The lower bound can be proved using MAC-type arguments, see Appendix
C
.
∎

V
Conclusion

This paper presents an improved upper bound and the first information-theoretic lower bound on the NDT tradeoff of full-duplex wireless MapReduce systems. The upper bound is obtained by zero-forcing and a novel IA scheme that is tailored to the information cancellation capabilities of the nodes in a MapReduce system. As a conclusion of this work, we observe that linear beamforming, zero-forcing, and interference cancelation are optimal when each node can store at least half of the file, but they are suboptimal for smaller computation loads.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

We show how to construct a distributed computing scheme achieving the NDT upper bound in (
11
). We shall assume a sequence (in
\mathsf{P}>0
) of rates
(R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}\colon\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T})
that achieves the sum-DoF
\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})
and is completely symmetric with respect to indices
j
and sets
\mathcal{T}
. By the same time-sharing and relabeling arguments as described in Subsection
II
such a sequence must exist.

In the Map Phase we choose a regular file assignment.
Partition the input files
\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{N}}\}
into

\mathsf{K}\choose\mathsf{r}
disjoint bundles and assign each bundle to a size-
\mathsf{r}
subset
\mathcal{T}\in[[\mathsf{K}]]^{\mathsf{r}}
. The bundle associated to subset
\mathcal{T}
is denoted
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{\mathsf{N}}\}
.
Notice that the proposed assignment satisfies the constraint on the computation load, because the number of files stored at Node
k
is:

\binom{\mathsf{K}-1}{\mathsf{r}-1}\frac{\mathsf{N}}{\binom{\mathsf{K}}{\mathsf{r}}}=\frac{\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\mathsf{N}.

(58)

Each node computes all IVAs associated to its stored files.

During the Wireless Shuffle Phase, each transmit set
\mathcal{T}
communicates to any receive node
j\notin\mathcal{T}
all IVAs that can be calculated from bundle
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{T}}
. To this end, all nodes use the encoding and decoding functions achieving
\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})
.

Each transmit group
\mathcal{T}
has to send
\frac{\mathsf{N}}{{\mathsf{K}\choose\mathsf{r}}}
IVAs to each Node
j\notin\mathcal{T}
and in total there are
{\mathsf{K}\choose\mathsf{r}}(\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r})
rates in the symmetric rate vector
(R_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}\colon\mathcal{T}\in[\mathsf{K}]^{\mathsf{r}},\;j\in[\mathsf{K}]\backslash\mathcal{T})
. By definition, the probability of error in reconstructing all missing IVAs tends to 0 as
\mathsf{T}\to\infty
if

\varlimsup_{P\to\infty}\varlimsup_{A\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{A}\cdot\mathsf{N}}{\mathsf{T}\cdot{\mathsf{K}\choose\mathsf{r}}\cdot\log\mathsf{P}}<\frac{\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})}{{\mathsf{K}\choose\mathsf{r}}(\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r})}.

(59)

We thus conclude that

\Delta^{\star}(\mathsf{r})\leq\varliminf_{P\to\infty}\varliminf_{A\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{A}\cdot\mathsf{K}\cdot\mathsf{N}}\cdot\log\mathsf{P}=\frac{\mathsf{K}-\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{K}}\frac{1}{\textnormal{Sum-DoF}(\mathsf{r})},

(60)

which proves the desired achievability result.

\displaystyle 2(\mathsf{K}-i)(t-i+1)(\mathsf{K}-t-i).

and after rearranging the terms:

\displaystyle(\mathsf{K}-t)(\mathsf{K}-i)(\mathsf{K}-t-i)+(\mathsf{K}-i)(\mathsf{K}-i+1)

(112)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\mathsf{K}-t)(t-i+1)(\mathsf{K}-t-i)+(t-i)(t-i+1).

This last inequality is easily verified by noting that
\mathsf{K}\geq t+1
.

Appendix E
Proof of Structure of Minimizer

Start with any feasible vector
b_{1},\ldots,b_{\mathsf{K}}
and consider two indices
i<j
with non-zero masses,
b_{i}>0
and
b_{j}>0
. Updating this vector as

\displaystyle b_{i}^{\prime}=b_{i}-\Delta,\qquad

and

\displaystyle\qquad b_{i+1}^{\prime}=b_{i+1}+\Delta,

(113)

\displaystyle b_{j-1}^{\prime}=b_{j-1}+\Delta,\qquad

and

\displaystyle\qquad b_{j}^{\prime}=b_{j}-\Delta,

(114)

for any
\Delta\in[0,\min\{b_{i},b_{j}\}]
, results again in a feasible solution vector, which has smaller objective function due to the convexity of the coefficients
\{C_{i}^{(t)}\}
.

Applying this argument iteratively, one can conclude that there must exist an optimal solution vector where all entries are zero except for two masses
b_{k}>0
and
b_{k+1}\geq 0
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{\mathsf{K}}ib_{i}\leq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{N}
, the index
k
cannot exceed
\mathsf{r}
. By the decreasing monotonicity of the coefficients
C_{i}^{(t)}
, the optimal solution must then be to choose
b_{\lfloor\mathsf{r}\rfloor}>0
and
b_{\lfloor\mathsf{r}\rfloor+1}\geq 0
and all other masses equal to 0. Since there is a unique such choice satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{\mathsf{K}}ib_{i}\leq\mathsf{r}\mathsf{N}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\mathsf{K}}b_{i}=\mathsf{N}
, this concludes the proof.

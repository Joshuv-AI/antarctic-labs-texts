Symmetry criteria for Hochschild extensions
Yang Han

Abstract
We give two sufficient and necessary conditions for the Hochschild extension of a finite dimensional algebra by its dual bimodule and a Hochschild 2-cocycle to be a symmetric algebra.



Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
:
16G10, 16E40

Keywords
: Hochschild extension, symmetric algebra, Hochschild (co)homology, cyclic (co)homology, Connes boundary operator, contraction operator.

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
k
is a fixed field,
\otimes:=\otimes_{k}
, and
(-)^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(-,k)
.
Let
A
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra. Then
A^{*}
is a natural
A
-bimodule whose
A
-bimodule structure is given by
(cfa)(b):=f(abc)
for all
f\in A^{*}
and
a,b,c\in A
.
The
trivial extension
of
A
is the finite dimensional
k
-algebra
T(A):=A\oplus A^{*}
with multiplication
(a,f)(b,g):=(ab,ag+fb)
, which is always a
symmetric algebra
, i.e.,
T(A)\cong T(A)^{*}
as
T(A)
-bimodules, and plays a quite important role in the representation theory of finite dimensional algebras
[
5
]
.
Let
\alpha:A\otimes A\to A^{*}
be a
Hochschild 2-cocycle
, i.e., a
k
-linear map satisfying
Hochschild 2-cocycle condition

a\alpha(b\otimes c)-\alpha(ab\otimes c)+\alpha(a\otimes bc)-\alpha(a\otimes b)c=0
for all
a,b,c\in A
.
Then the
Hochschild extension

T(A,\alpha):=A\oplus A^{*}
with multiplication

(a,f)(b,g):=(ab,ag+fb+\alpha(a\otimes b))
is always a selfinjective algebra
[
10
, Proposition 1.2]
,
but unlike trivial extension it is unnecessary to be a symmetric algebra
[
8
, Proposition 2.8]
.
In
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
, Ohnuki-Takeda-Yamagata gave a sufficient condition for a Hochschild extension
T(A,\alpha)
to be a symmetric algebra, but the condition is not necessary
[
8
, Proposition 2.9]
.
In
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
, Itagaki gave a more general sufficient condition than Ohnuki-Takeda-Yamagata’s, but the condition is not necessary either.
Moreover, in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
we provided a sufficient condition for a Hochschild extension of a finite dimensional dg
k
-algebra to be an
n
-symmetric
A_{\infty}
-algebra.

In this paper, we will give two sufficient and necessary conditions for a Hochschild extension
T(A,\alpha)
to be a symmetric algebra.
Denote by
Z(A)
the center of a
k
-algebra
A
and
U(A)
the group of units (=invertible elements) in
A
. Let
\widetilde{}:\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A^{\otimes 2},A^{*})\to(A^{\otimes 3})^{*},\alpha\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha}
, be the canonical adjunction isomorphism where
\widetilde{\alpha}
is given by
\widetilde{\alpha}(a\otimes b\otimes c):=\alpha(b\otimes c)(a)
for all
a,b,c\in A
. The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A\otimes A,A^{*})
a Hochschild 2-cocycle. Then the following three statements are equivalent:

(1)
The Hochschild extension
T(A,\alpha)
is a symmetric algebra.

(2)
There exist
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
and
h\in A^{*}
such that

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0

for all
a,b\in A
.

(3)
There exists
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
such that the Hochschild cohomology class
[i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})]\in H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})\cong HH_{2}(A)^{*}\cong HH^{2}(A,A^{*})
can be lifted to a cyclic cohomology class in
HC^{2}(A)\cong HC_{2}(A)^{*}
along the canonical map
I^{2}:HC^{2}(A)\to H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
in the Connes periodicity exact sequence.

Remark
.

Let
A=kQ/I
be a bound quiver algebra where
Q
is a finite quiver and
I
is an admissible ideal of the path algebra
kQ
of
Q
(Ref.
[
1
]
), and
K:=kQ_{0}
where
Q_{0}
is the vertex set of
Q
. Since
K\cong k^{|Q_{0}|}
is separable, the homology of the
K
-relative Hochschild chain complex
of
A

\cdots\xrightarrow{b_{3}}A\otimes_{K^{e}}A^{\otimes_{K}2}\xrightarrow{b_{2}}A\otimes_{K^{e}}A\xrightarrow{b_{1}}A\otimes_{K^{e}}K\to 0

is isomorphic to the Hochschild homology of
A
, and the cohomology of the
K
-relative Hochschild cochain complex of
A
with coefficients in
A
-bimodule
A^{*}

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{K^{e}}(K,A^{*})\xrightarrow{\delta^{0}}\mathrm{Hom}_{K^{e}}(A,A^{*})\xrightarrow{\delta^{1}}\mathrm{Hom}_{K^{e}}(A^{\otimes_{K}2},A^{*})\xrightarrow{\delta^{2}}\cdots

is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
A
-bimodule
A^{*}
(Ref.
[
2
, Section 1]
).
For any Hochschild 2-cocycle
\alpha\in\mathrm{Hom}_{K^{e}}(A^{\otimes_{K}2},A^{*})
and Hochschild extension
T(A,\alpha)
, we can obtain an almost the same theorem as above in which
the condition (2) with
h=0
is just Itagaki’s sufficient condition in
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
and
the condition (2) with
h=0
and
c=1
is just Ohnuki-Takeda-Yamagata’s sufficient condition in
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Contraction operator.
Let
A
be a
k

{\alpha}\in C^{m}(A):=C^{m}(A,A)

i_{\alpha}
on the Hochschild chain complex

is the graded morphism
i_{\alpha}:C_{\bullet}(A)\to C_{\bullet}(A)
of degree

i_{\alpha}(a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}):=a_{0}{\alpha}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})\otimes(a_{m+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}).

Note that

where

is the Hochschild coboundary operator.
Consequently, if

is a Hochschild cocycle then the contraction operator

3
The proof of Theorem

Now we start the proof of Theorem.
Write
T(A,\alpha)
as
T
for short.
Any element in
T^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(A\oplus A^{*},k)\cong A^{*}\oplus A^{**}\cong A^{*}\oplus A
can be written as
(f,a)
where
f\in A^{*},a\in A
, and the
k
-linear form
(f,a):T\to k
is given by
(f,a)((b,g)):=f(b)+g(a)
for all
(b,g)\in T
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):

Assume that
T
is a symmetric algebra. Then there exists a
T
-bimodule isomorphism
\phi:T\to T^{*}
.
Let
\phi(1_{T})=(h,c)\in T^{*}
where
h\in A^{*}
and
c\in A
.

Claim 1.

For all
(a,f),(b,g)\in T
, we have

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0,\ f(bc)=f(cb),\ g(ac)=g(ca).

Indeed, since
\phi
is a
T
-bimodule morphism, for any
(a,f)\in T
, we have

\phi(a,f)=(a,f)\phi(1_{T})=\phi(1_{T})(a,f)

which maps any
(b,g)\in T
to

\phi(a,f)((b,g))=((a,f)\phi(1_{T}))((b,g))=(\phi(1_{T})(a,f))((b,g)).

By the canonical
T
-bimodule structure of
T^{*}
, we get

\phi(a,f)((b,g))=\phi(1_{T})((b,g)(a,f))=\phi(1_{T})((a,f)(b,g)).

According to the multiplication of
T
, we have

\phi(a,f)((b,g))=(h,c)((ba,bf+ga+\alpha(b\otimes a)))=(h,c)((ab,ag+fb+\alpha(a\otimes b))).

From the definition of
(h,c)\in T^{*}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi(a,f)((b,g))&=h(ba)+(bf)(c)+(ga)(c)+\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)\\
&=h(ab)+(ag)(c)+(fb)(c)+\alpha(a\otimes b)(c).\end{array}

By the canonical
T
-bimodule structure of
T^{*}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi(a,f)((b,g))&=h(ba)+f(cb)+g(ac)+\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)\\
&=h(ab)+g(ca)+f(bc)+\alpha(a\otimes b)(c).\end{array}

Taking
a=0
, we obtain
f(bc)=f(cb)
for all
b\in A
and
f\in A^{*}
.
Taking
b=0
, we get
g(ac)=g(ca)
for all
a\in A
and
g\in A^{*}
.
Furthermore, for all
(a,f),(b,g)\in T
, we have

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0.

Claim 2.

c\in Z(A)
.

By Claim 1, we have
f(bc-cb)=0
for all
b\in A
and
f\in A^{*}
.
Thus
bc-cb=0
for all
b\in A
. Hence
c\in Z(A)
.

Claim 3.

c\in U(A)
.

Assume on the contrary
c\notin U(A)
.
It follows from Claim 2 that the two-sided ideal
AcA
of
A
generated by
c
is equal to both
Ac
and
cA
. However, it is not equal to
A
due to
c\notin U(A)
.
Thus the complement space
(Ac)^{\perp}
of
Ac
in
A
is not zero.
Since
A=Ac\oplus(Ac)^{\perp}
, we have
A^{*}=(Ac)^{*}\oplus((Ac)^{\perp})^{*}
.
Thus there exists
f\in((Ac)^{\perp})^{*}\subseteq A^{*}
such that
f\neq 0
and
f(Ac)=0
.
For any
(b,g)\in T
, as in the proof of Claim 1, we have
\phi(0,f)((b,g))=h(0b)+g(c0)+f(bc)+\alpha(0\otimes b)(c)=f(bc)\in f(Ac)=0
. So
\phi(0,f)=0
.
Since
\phi
is a
T
-bimodule isomorphism, we have
(0,f)=(0,0)
, and further
f=0
.
It is a contradiction.

Finally, it follows from Claim 1, 2 and 3 that there exist
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
and
h\in A^{*}

such that
\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3):
Assume that there exists
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
such that

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.
From Hochschild 2-cocycle condition, we obtain
\alpha(1\otimes ab)=\alpha(1\otimes a)b
for all
a,b\in A
.
Due to
c\in Z(A)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&(B_{1}^{*}(i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})))(a\otimes b)\\
=&(\widetilde{\alpha}i_{c}B_{1})(a\otimes b)\\
=&(\widetilde{\alpha}i_{c})(1\otimes a\otimes b-1\otimes b\otimes a-a\otimes 1\otimes b+b\otimes 1\otimes a)\\
=&\widetilde{\alpha}(c\otimes a\otimes b-c\otimes b\otimes a-ac\otimes 1\otimes b+bc\otimes 1\otimes a)\\
=&\widetilde{\alpha}(c\otimes a\otimes b)-\widetilde{\alpha}(c\otimes b\otimes a)-\widetilde{\alpha}(ac\otimes 1\otimes b)+\widetilde{\alpha}(bc\otimes 1\otimes a)\\
=&\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)-\alpha(1\otimes b)(ac)+\alpha(1\otimes a)(bc)\\
=&-h(ab-ba)+(\alpha(1\otimes 1)c)(ab-ba)\\
=&-(h-\alpha(1\otimes 1)c)(ab-ba)\\
=&-((h-\alpha(1\otimes 1)c)b_{1})(a\otimes b)\\
=&-b_{1}^{*}(h-\alpha(1\otimes 1)c)(a\otimes b).\end{array}

Let
h^{\prime}=h-\alpha(1\otimes 1)c\in A^{*}
. Then
B_{1}^{*}(i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha}))+b_{1}^{*}(h^{\prime})=0
.
Thus the Hochschild cohomology class
[i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})]\in H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
can be lifted to
the cyclic cohomology class
[(i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha}),h^{\prime})]\in HC^{2}(A)

along the canonical map
I^{2}:HC^{2}(A)\to H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2):

Assume that there exists
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
such that the Hochschild cohomology class

[i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})]\in H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
can be lifted to a cyclic cohomology class

[(\alpha^{\prime},h^{\prime})]\in HC^{2}(A)
along the canonical map
I^{2}:HC^{2}(A)\to H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
where
\alpha^{\prime}\in(A^{\otimes 3})^{*}
and
h^{\prime}\in A^{*}
, i.e.,
I^{2}([(\alpha^{\prime},h^{\prime})])=[\alpha^{\prime}]=[i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})]
.
Then
b_{3}^{*}(\alpha^{\prime})=0
,
B_{1}^{*}(\alpha^{\prime})+b_{1}^{*}(h^{\prime})=0
, i.e.,
\alpha^{\prime}B_{1}+h^{\prime}b_{1}=0
, and
[\alpha^{\prime}]=[i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})]\in H^{2}(C_{\bullet}(A)^{*})
.
Thus there is
\beta\in(A^{\otimes 2})^{*}
such that
i_{c}^{*}(\widetilde{\alpha})=\alpha^{\prime}+b_{2}^{*}(\beta)
, i.e.,
\widetilde{\alpha}i_{c}=\alpha^{\prime}+\beta b_{2}
.
So
\widetilde{\alpha}i_{c}B_{1}=\alpha^{\prime}B_{1}+\beta b_{2}B_{1}=-h^{\prime}b_{1}-\beta B_{0}b_{1}=-(h^{\prime}+\beta B_{0})b_{1}
.
As in the proof of (2)
\Rightarrow
(3), we have

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+(h^{\prime}+\beta B_{0}+\alpha(1\otimes 1)c)(ab-ba)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.
Let
h=h^{\prime}+\beta B_{0}+\alpha(1\otimes 1)c\in A^{*}
.
Then
\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1):

Assume that there exists
c\in Z(A)\cap U(A)
and
h\in A^{*}
such that

\alpha(a\otimes b)(c)-\alpha(b\otimes a)(c)+h(ab-ba)=0
for all
a,b\in A
.
Define a map
\phi:T\to T^{*}
by
\phi(a,f)((b,g)):=(h,c)((a,f)(b,g))
.
Note that

(h,c)((a,f)(b,g))=h(ab)+g(ca)+f(bc)+\alpha(a,b)(c)

and

(h,c)((b,g)(a,f))=h(ba)+f(cb)+g(ac)+\alpha(b,a)(c).

Due to
c\in Z(A)
, we get
f(bc)=f(cb)
and
g(ac)=g(ca)
.
Furthermore, for all
(a,f),(b,g)\in T
, we have

\phi(a,f)((b,g))=(h,c)((a,f)(b,g))=(h,c)((b,g)(a,f)),

or equivalently,

\phi(a,f)=(h,c)(a,f)=(a,f)(h,c).

Now we prove that
\phi
is a
T
-bimodule morphism.
Indeed, for all
(a^{\prime},f^{\prime}),\linebreak(a,f),(a^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime})\in T
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi((a^{\prime},f^{\prime})(a,f)(a^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime}))&=(h,c)(a^{\prime},f^{\prime})(a,f)(a^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime})\\
&=(a^{\prime},f^{\prime})(h,c)(a,f)(a^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime})\\
&=(a^{\prime},f^{\prime})\phi(a,f)(a^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime}).\end{array}

Next we show that
\phi
is a
T
-bimodule isomorphism.
Since
\mathrm{dim}_{k}T=\mathrm{dim}_{k}T^{*}<\infty
, it suffices to prove that
\phi
is injective.
For this, suppose
\phi(a,f)=0
. We need to prove
(a,f)=0
.
Indeed, on one hand, for any
g\in A^{*}
, we have
0=\phi(a,f)((0,g))=h(a0)+g(ca)+f(0c)+\alpha(a\otimes 0)(c)=g(ca)
. Thus
ca=0
.
Due to
c\in U(A)
, we get
a=0
.
On the other hand, for any
b\in A
, we have
0=\phi(0,f)((b,0))=h(0b)+0(c0)+f(bc)+\alpha(0\otimes b)(c)=f(bc)
.
Due to
c\in U(A)
, we obtain
f(A)=0
. Thus
f=0
.

Now we finish the proof of Theorem.

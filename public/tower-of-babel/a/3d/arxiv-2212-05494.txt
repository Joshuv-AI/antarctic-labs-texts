Spaces of non-resultant systems of bounded multiplicity with real coefficients
Andrzej Kozlowski, Kohhei Yamaguchi

Abstract
For each pair ( m , n ) (m,n) of positive integers with ( m , n ) ≠ ( 1 , 1 ) (m,n)\not=(1,1) and an arbitrary field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} with algebraic closure 𝔽 ¯ \overline{\mathbb{F}} , let Poly n d , m ​ ( 𝔽 ) \mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{F}) denote the space of m m -tuples ( f 1 ​ ( z ) , ⋯ , f m ​ ( z ) ) ∈ 𝔽 ​ [ z ] m (f_{1}(z),\cdots,f_{m}(z))\in\mathbb{F}[z]^{m} of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -coefficients monic polynomials of the same degree d d such that the polynomials { f k ​ ( z ) } k = 1 m \{f_{k}(z)\}_{k=1}^{m} have no common root in 𝔽 ¯ \overline{\mathbb{F}} of multiplicity ≥ n \geq n . -terms, i.e.

E^{1;d}_{**}=E^{\infty;d}_{**}
.
∎

Next we give the proofs of Corollaries

2.13
and
2.14
.

Proof of Corollary
2.13
.

It follows from Theorem
2.11
that
there is a stable homotopy equivalence

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/n\rfloor,mn}_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq_{s}\Big(\bigvee_{i=1}^{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}S^{(mn-2)i}\Big)\vee\Big(\bigvee_{i\geq 0,j\geq 1,i+2j\leq\lfloor d/n\rfloor}\Sigma^{(mn-2)(i+2j)}D_{j}\Big).

Thus, by using Corollary
2.12

we easily obtain the stable homotopy equivalence
(
2.21
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.14
.

The assertion (i) follows from Theorem
5.6
and it remains to show (ii).
Recall from Lemma
3.8
that there is a homeomorphism

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{\rm H}_{+})\cong\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Consider the following homotopy commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{\rm H}_{+})@>{\iota^{d,m}_{n,\mathbf{\rm H}_{+}}}>{\subset}>\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\\
\|@V{}V{\simeq_{s}}V\\
\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{\rm H}_{+})\cong\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})@>{j_{1}}>{\subset}>\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\vee B^{d,m}_{n}\vee\mbox{{\rm Q}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\end{CD}

where
j_{1}
denotes the inclusion to the first factor.
Then the assertion (ii) easily follows from
Theorem
2.11
.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

Let

mn\geq 3
.
Then
it follows from (
2.16
), (
4.26
) and
Theorem
2.11
that
there are stable homotopy equivalences

\displaystyle\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mbox{{\rm Q}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\simeq_{s}\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\vee A^{d,m}_{n}\vee\mbox{{\rm Q}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\quad

\displaystyle\simeq_{s}\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\vee B^{d,m}_{n}\vee\mbox{{\rm Q}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

where

A^{d,m}_{n}
denotes the space defined by

(8.20)

A^{d,m}_{n}=\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq\lfloor d/n\rfloor,1\leq j\leq\lfloor d_{0}/n\rfloor}\Sigma^{(mn-2)(i+2j)}D_{j}.

Since

\displaystyle B^{d,m}_{n}=\bigvee_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{F}^{d,m}_{n}}\Sigma^{(mn-2)(i+2j)}D_{j}\subset A^{d,m}_{n}

(by Lemma
3.10
),
the space

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
can be regarded as
the subspace of

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor,m}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mbox{{\rm Q}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

in the stable homotopy category.
∎

9
Appendix: The case
(m,n)=(1,2)

In this section we consider the homotopy type of the space

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,m}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
for the case
(m,n)=(1,2)
.
In fact, the homology stability follows directly from Segal’s seminal article
[
28
]
.
However, as it does not appear to be stated anywhere, we provide a detailed proof below.

Let
f(z)\in\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then

f(z)\in\mathbb{R}[z]
is a monic polynomial of degree
d
without multiple roots.
If
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
is a complex root of
f(z)
, its conjugate

\overline{\alpha}
is also a root of
f(z)
.
Thus it can be represented as

(9.1)

f(z)=\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{d-2j}(z-x_{i})\Big)\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{j}(z-\alpha_{k})(z-\overline{\alpha}_{k})\Big)

for some
(\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d-2j},\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{j})\in C_{d-2j}(\mathbb{R})\times C_{j}(\mathbf{\rm H}_{+})
.

Definition 9.1
.

For each non-negative integer

0\leq j\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
, let

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})
denote the subspace of
\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

consisting
of all monic polynomials
f(z)\in\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

which have only
2j
non-real roots.

It is easy to see that each polynomial
f(z)\in\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})

can be represented in the form of (
9.1
).
∎

Remark 9.2
.

It is also easy to show that there is a homeomorphism

(9.2)

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\cong C_{d-2j}(\mathbb{R})\times C_{j}(\mathbf{\rm H}_{+}).

Let
\varphi:\mathbf{\rm H}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}
be any homeomorphism (which we now fix), and
let

(9.3)

\overline{\varphi}:C_{j}(\mathbf{\rm H}_{+})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{j}(\mathbb{C})

denote the homeomorphism given by

\overline{\varphi}(\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{j})=\{\varphi(\alpha_{k})\}_{k=1}^{j}.

Since there is a homeomorphism
C_{d-2j}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-2j}

and a homotopy equivalence

C_{j}(\mathbb{C})\simeq K({\rm Br}(j),1)
,
there is a homotopy equivalence

(9.4)

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{j}(\mathbb{C})\simeq K({\rm Br}(j),1),

where
{\rm Br}(j)
denotes the Artin braid group on
j
strands.

Theorem 9.3
.

(i)

The space
\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
consists of

(\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1)
connected components

\{\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R}):0\leq j\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor\},

and
there is a homotopy equivalence

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\simeq K({\rm Br}(j),1).

(ii)

There is a natural map

i^{d,1}_{2,j}:\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{2}_{j}\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1}\simeq\Omega^{2}_{j}S^{2}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}

which
is a homology equivalence up to dimension
\lfloor j/2\rfloor
if
j\geq 3
, and
a homotopy equivalence through dimension
1
if
j=2
.

Proof.

Since (i) is obvious, we only deal with (ii).
We make the usual identification
S^{2}=\mathbb{C}\cup\infty

and assume that
j\geq 2
.
Define the map

i_{2,j}:C_{j}(\mathbb{C})\to\Omega^{2}_{j}\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1}

by

(9.5)

i_{2,j}(\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{j})(\alpha)=\begin{cases}[f(\alpha):f(\alpha)+f^{\prime}(\alpha)]=[F_{2}(f)(\alpha)]&\mbox{ if }\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\\
[1:1]&\mbox{ if }\alpha=\infty\end{cases}

for
\alpha\in S^{2}=\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
and

f(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{j}(z-a_{k})
.
Note that

(9.6)

\frac{f(z)+f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=1+\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{1}{z-a_{k}}

It then follows from
[
28
, page 42]
that the map

i_{2,j}
is a homology equivalence up to dimension

\lfloor j/2\rfloor
.
Now let
2\leq j\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
, and consider the map

(9.7)

i^{d}_{2,j}:\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{2}_{j}\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1}\cong\Omega^{2}_{j}S^{2}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}

given by the composite of maps

(9.8)

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,j}(\mathbb{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{j}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{2,j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{2}_{j}\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1}\simeq\Omega^{2}_{j}S^{2}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}.

Since
i_{2,j}
is a homology equivalence up to dimension

\lfloor j/2\rfloor
, the map

i^{d,1}_{2,j}
is also a homology equivalence up to
dimension
\lfloor j/2\rfloor
.

Finally consider the case
j=2
.
Since
{\rm Br}(2)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, there is a homotopy equivalence

\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})\simeq S^{1}.

Since the map

i^{d,1}_{2,2}
induces an epimorphism

(i^{d,1}_{2,2})_{*}:\mathbb{Z}\cong H_{1}(\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R});\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(\Omega^{2}_{j}S^{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z},

it is indeed an isomorphism.
Moreover, note that there is an isomorphism

\pi_{1}(\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,1}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R}))\cong\pi_{1}(S^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{1}(\Omega^{2}_{j}S^{2}).

Next, by the Hurewicz Theorem we know that the map

i^{d,1}_{2,2}
induces an isomorphism on the fundamental group

\pi_{1}(\ )
. We have proved (ii).
∎

Remark 9.4
.

(i)
It follows from (
9.6
) that
the map
i_{2,j}
is equivalent to

the electric field map

E_{c}:\mathbb{C}\cup\infty\to\mathbb{C}\cup\infty
,
described in
[
27
, page 213]
.

Moreover, we can easily see that

i^{d,1}_{2,j}=i^{d,2}_{1,\mathbb{R}}|\mbox{{\rm Poly}}^{d,2}_{1,j}(\mathbb{R})

(ii)

the homotopy stability does not hold for the map
i^{d,2}_{1,j}
when
j\geq 3
.
∎

1.
Introduction

Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the Laplace eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda>0
on an
n
-dimensional compact smooth Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. Let
Z(\varphi_{\lambda})=\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=0\}
be the nodal set of
\varphi_{\lambda}
.
S.T. Yau conjectured that
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
Z(\varphi_{\lambda})
is comparable to
\sqrt{\lambda}
. The original conjecture is still open, while much progress can be found in, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
8
,
10
,
13
,
7
,
3
,
4
]
. We refer to
[
11
]
for a detailed introduction on the Yau’s conjecture.

In this paper, we study the nodal sets of Laplace eigenfunctions in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
. We are interested in how the regularity or geometry of the boundary affects the size of nodal sets near the boundary. Precisely, let
(\varphi_{\lambda},\lambda)
be the eigenpair satisfying
-\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
and
\varphi_{\lambda}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. If
\partial\Omega=\emptyset
or
\partial\Omega
is real analytic, the Yau’s conjecture was proved by Donnelly-Fefferman
[
1
,
2
]
. We remark that the sharp lower bound estimate, namely
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))\geq c(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda}
, actually holds independent of the boundary geometry, due to the interior density of nodal sets. However, the boundary geometry will play a crucial role in the upper bound estimate as the nodal set may concentrate near the irregular boundary.
Recently, Logunov-Malinnikova-Nadirashvili-Nazarov
[
12
]
proved the sharp upper bound for
C^{1}
domains (or Lipschitz domains with sufficiently small Lipshcitz constant), namely
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))\leq C(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda}
. Following the similar idea and taking advantage of the properties of convex domains, Zhu-Zhuge
[
14
]
generalized the result to quasiconvex domains, which is a class of Lipschitz domains that includes both

C^{1}
and convex domains. As far as we know, all the previous results do not cover (nonconvex) polygonal domains with very simple geometry. Note that the upper bound estimate of nodal sets is a global property and the previous results cannot rule out the possibility of concentration of the nodal sets in the vicinity of nonconvex corners or ridges of polygonal domains.

The contribution of this paper is to prove the sharp estimate of the nodal sets for Dirichlet Laplace eigenfunctions in bounded Lipschitz polytopes, which are higher-dimensional generalization of two-dimensional polygons and three-dimensional polyhedras. The precise mathematical characterization of polytopes will be given in Section
3
. The main result of this paper is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let P be a bounded n-dimensional Lipschitz polytope. Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be a Dirichlet eigenfunction of the Laplace operator corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}&=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}\quad&\text{in }&P,\\
\varphi_{\lambda}&=0\quad&\text{on }&\partial P.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the nodal set of
\varphi_{\lambda}
, denoted by
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))
, satisfies

(1.1)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}\left(Z\left(\varphi_{\lambda}\right)\right)\leq C\sqrt{\lambda},

where
C
depends only on
P
and
n
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a lifting argument to convert the eigenfunctions in
P
to harmonic functions in
P\times\mathbb{R}
. Then the difficulty of estimating the nodal sets for harmonic functions lies in the uniform control of the doubling index centered at any points, particularly when the points are close to the corners or ridges. We develop an approach to control the doubling index which involves the monotonicity and propagation of the doubling index in star-shaped domains, the geometric properties of polytopes (near corners, ridges, etc) and the estimation of maximum star-shape radius (MSR). We remark that our approach may also apply to general second order elliptic equations in piecewise smooth/convex domains. To avoid complexity, we do not pursue this generality in the present paper.

Notations.
Throughout the paper, we denote by
c,c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,
small positive constants and
C,C_{1},C_{0},\cdots,
large positive constants depending only on
n
and
P
, which may change from line to line.
We use
B_{r}(x)
or
B(x,r)
to denote the ball of radius
r
centered at
x
. Denote by
d(A,B)
the Euclidean distance between the objects
A
and
B
(which could be points or sets). Denote by
B(E,r)=\{x:d(x,E)<r\}
the
r
-neighborhood of the set
E
. We write
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Organizations.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the monotonicity of the doubling index in star-shaped domains (the outline of the proof will be given in Appendix) and prove a property on the propagation of doubling index. In Section
3
, we study the useful properties of polytopes. Section
4
is dedicated to the uniform estimate of the doubling index. The main theorem is proved in Section
5
.

2.
Monotonicity of doubling index

An important tool to study the nodal sets or vainshing order of the eigenfunctions of elliptic operators is the Almgren’s frequency function or doubling index.
In this section, we recall the monotonicity of doubling index in star-shaped Lipschitz domains.

Definition 2.1
.

We say a domain
\Omega
is Lipschitz, if there exits
r_{0}>0
such that for every
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the boundary patch
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, after a rigid transformation, can be expressed as a Lipschitz graph
x^{n}=\phi(x^{\prime})
such that

(2.1)

B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\Omega=B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\left\{x=(x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{n})=(x^{\prime},x^{n}):x^{n}>\phi(x^{\prime})\right\}

and we call
r_{0}
the Lipschitz radius of
\Omega
. Moreover, the Lipschitz constants of these graphs are uniformly bounded by some constant
L
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say a connected Lipschitz domain
\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to some
x\in\overline{\Omega}
if for almost every
y\in\partial\Omega
,

(2.2)

(y-x)\cdot n(y)\geq 0,

where
n(y)
is the outer normal to
\partial\Omega
at
y
.
Alternatively, a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to
x\in\overline{\Omega}
if for each
y\in\partial\Omega
, the line segment connecting
x
and
y
is entirely contained in
\overline{\Omega}
.

Let
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
.
Define the doubling index of
u
in
\Omega
centered at
x\in\overline{\Omega}
by

N_{u}(x,r)=\log_{2}\frac{\int_{B(x,2r)\cap\Omega}u^{2}}{\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}u^{2}}.

The following lemma was essentially proved in
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
. We outline the proof in Appendix.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
R>0
. Assume that
\Omega\cap B(x,2R)
is star-shaped with respect to
x
. Suppose
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a non-zero harmonic function in
\Omega\cap B(x,2R)
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B(x,2R).
Then for any
0<s<r<R
, we have

(2.3)

N_{u}(x,s)\leq N_{u}(x,r).

We also need a lemma to quantify the propagation of doubling index in star-shaped domains when the center is shifted.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be an
n
-dimensional Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Let
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega\cap B_{R}(0)
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{R}(0)
. Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
. Suppose that there is an embedded Lipschitz curve

\gamma:[0,1]\to\overline{\Omega}\cap B(0,R)
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\gamma(0)=0
and
\gamma(1)=x
.

(ii)

For some
0<r<\frac{1}{4}R
and all
s\in[0,1]
,
\Omega\cap B(\gamma(s),4r)
is contained in
\Omega\cap B_{R\wedge C_{1}r}(0)
for some
C_{1}>1
and is star-shaped with respect to
\gamma(s)
.

(iii)

For some constant
C_{2}>1
,
|\gamma|\leq C_{2}r
.

(iv)

\Omega\cap B_{R}(0)
is star-shaped with respect to
0
.

Then there exists
C=C(C_{1},C_{2})
such that

(2.4)

N_{u}(x,r)\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}R).

Proof.

Let
R_{1}:=R\wedge C_{1}r
. Without loss of generality, we assume

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{1}}(0)}u^{2}=1.

Let
N_{0}=N_{u}(0,R_{1}/2)
. Since
\Omega\cap B_{R}(0)
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, by the monotonicity of the doubling index, we have
N_{0}\leq N_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}R)
.

By the monotonicity of the doubling index again and the assumption (
2.5
), we can show

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(0)}u^{2}\geq\exp_{2}(-N_{0}(1+\log_{2}\frac{R_{1}}{r}))\geq\exp_{2}(-N_{0}(1+\log_{2}C_{1})),

where
\exp_{2}(a)=2^{a}.

Let
x_{1}=\gamma(s_{1})
for some
s_{1}\in(0,1)
and
|x_{1}-0|=r
. Since
B_{4r}(x_{1})
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{1}
(due to (iii)), using the monotonicity of the doubling index (or the three ball inequality), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega}u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{B_{4r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where we also used the assumptions
B_{4r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega\subset B_{R_{1}}(0)\cap\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{1}}(0)}u^{2}=1
. It follows that

(2.8)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\geq\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{2}.

Now we repeat this process and find a sequence of
x_{j}
connecting
0
and
x
with
1\leq j\leq m
and
x_{m}=x
such that
|x_{j}-x_{j-1}|=r
for
j<m
and
|x_{m}-x_{m-1}|\leq r
. By (ii), we see that
m\leq|\gamma|/r\leq C_{2}
. The previous argument yields

(2.9)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\geq\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(x_{j-1})\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{2},\qquad\text{for any }2\leq j\leq m.

This together with (
2.8
) and (
2.6
) gives

(2.10)

\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}u^{2}\geq\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega}u^{2}\bigg)^{2^{m}}\geq\exp_{2}(-N_{0}(1+\log_{2}C_{1})2^{C_{2}}).

Consequently, by the last estimate and (
2.5
) (note the fact
B_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega\subset B_{R_{1}}(0)\cap\Omega
), we have

(2.11)

N_{u}(x,r)=\log_{2}\frac{\int_{B_{2r}(x)\cap\Omega}u^{2}}{\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}u^{2}}\leq N_{0}(1+\log_{2}C_{1})2^{C_{2}}\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}R).

This ends the proof.
∎

3.
Polytopes

3.1.
Geometry of polytopes

A polytope is a geometric object with flat sides (faces), generalizing the two dimensional polygons and three-dimensional polyhedras to any number of dimensions. An
n
-dimensional polytope (as a connected domain) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, bounded by a finite number of
(n-1)
-dimensional facets, will be called an
n
-polytope. These facets are themselves
(n-1)
-polytopes, whose facets are
(n-2)
-polytopes (called ridges), and so forth. These bounding
k
-polytopes (with
0\leq k<n
) will be called
k
-faces of the original
n
-polytope. Typically,
k
-faces arise as the intersection of
(k+1)
-faces. For example, 0-faces are vertices, 1-faces are edges of line segments and
(n-1)
-faces are facets of the original
n
-polytope.

Let
P
be a given
n
-polytope. For each integer
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
, we define
\mathcal{F}^{n-j}
as the collection of all
(n-j)
-faces of
P
and
\mathscr{F}^{n-j}
as the union of all
(n-j)
-faces of
P
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\cup_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}^{n-j}
. By the previous definition, for each
2\leq j\leq n
, we have

\mathscr{F}^{n-j}\subset\mathscr{F}^{n-j+1},\quad\mathscr{F}^{n-1}=\partial P.

Moreover, for a given
(n-j)
face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{n-j}
, we denote by
\partial F
the boundary of
F
, which consists of
(n-j-1)
-faces contained in
\mathscr{F}^{n-j-1}
.

The following is a Euclidean geometric fact of polytopes that characterizes the distance between points on a given
(n-j)
-face and other
(n-1)
-faces not containing the given face.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let P be a
n
-polytope. Then there exists
c^{*}>0
such that for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1}
satisfying
F\cap G\neq F
(or equivalently
F\nsubseteq G
), we have

(3.1)

d(x,G)\geq c^{*}d(x,\partial F),\quad\text{for any }x\in F.

Proof.

We consider the cases
F\cap G\neq\emptyset
and
F\cap G=\emptyset
separately.
When
F\cap G\neq\emptyset
, we have
F\cap G=\partial F\cap G
. A simple geometric observation yields the existence of a constant
c_{F,G}\in(0,1]
such that for any
x\in F

(3.2)

d(x,G)\geq c_{F,G}d(x,F\cap G)\geq c_{F,G}d(x,\partial F).

On the other hand, we note that the number of faces of
P
is finite. Hence,

(3.3)

c^{\prime}:=\min\{c_{F,G}:F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},F\cap G\neq F,F\cap G\neq\emptyset\}>0.

Thus, we have
d(x,G)\geq c^{\prime}d(x,\partial F)
in this case.

When
F\cap G=\emptyset
, we have
d(F,G)>0
. Since the number of the pairs under this condition is finite, we can define

(3.4)

c^{\prime\prime}:=\min\{d(F,G):F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},F\cap G=\emptyset\}>0.

Consequently, for any
x\in F
, we have

(3.5)

d(x,G)\geq d(F,G)\geq c^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{\text{diam}(P)}d(x,\partial F).

Therefore, combining the above two cases and choosing
c^{*}=c^{\prime}\wedge\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{diam(P)}
, we establish (
3.1
).
∎

3.2.
Maximum star-shape radius (MSR)

The key to estimate the nodal sets is to control the doubling index centered at every point in
\overline{P}
. Due to the monotonicity of doubling index in star-shaped regions, it is natural to introduce the maximum star-shape radius at each point. Precisely, define

(3.6)

\displaystyle R^{*}(x)=\max\{r\geq 0:

\displaystyle\text{the connected component of }B_{r}(x)\cap P

\displaystyle\text{ is star-shaped with respect to }x\}\wedge\text{diam}(P).

The following crucial lemma gives lower bounds of MSR, based essentially on the geometry of polytopes. In particular, it tells us that the MSR can be large on the interior portions of any
j
-faces.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each point
x\in\overline{P}
, we have

(3.7)

R^{*}(x)\geq d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-2}).

In addition, if
x\in\mathscr{F}^{n-i}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}
with
i=2,3,\cdots,n-1.
, then

(3.8)

R^{*}(x)\geq c^{*}d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}),

where
c^{*}>0
is the constant given in Lemma
3.1
.
Moreover, if
x\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
, then

(3.9)

R^{*}(x)\geq\inf_{G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},x\notin G}d(x,G)\geq R_{0}>0,

for some constant
R_{0}
depending on
P
.

Proof.

Let
r_{x}=\text{dist}(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-2})
. To show (
3.7
), it suffices to show that the connected component of
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap P
is star-shaped. In fact, by the definition of
r_{x}
, we see that
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap P
does not intersect with
\mathscr{F}^{n-2}
and thus the boundary of
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap P
consists of a portion of
\partial B_{r_{x}}(x)
and flat faces (interior portions of
(n-1)
-faces). Hence,
B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap P
is convex and thus star-shaped.

Now we consider the particular case
x\in\mathscr{F}^{n-i}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}
and prove (
3.8
). Recall that
\mathcal{F}^{n-i}
is the collection of all
(n-i)
-faces. Let
\mathcal{F}^{n-i}=\{F^{n-i}_{j}:1\leq j\leq N_{n-i}\}
, where
N_{n-i}
is the number of all
(n-i)
-faces. Since
x\notin\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}
,
x
is contained in exactly one
(n-i)
-face. Without loss of generality, assume
x\in F^{n-i}_{1}
. Moreover, due to the fact
\partial F^{n-i}_{1}\subset\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}
,

(3.10)

d(x,\partial F^{n-i}_{1})\geq d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}).

Now, for any
(n-1)
-face
G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1}
not containing
F^{n-i}_{1}
, by Lemma
3.1
,

(3.11)

\text{dist}(x,G)\geq c^{*}d(x,\partial F^{n-i}_{1})\geq c^{*}d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}).

Let
r_{x}^{n-i-1}:=d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1})
. Hence, (
3.11
) implies that
B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})
does not intersect with any
(n-1)
-faces not containing
F^{n-i}_{1}
. This further implies

(3.12)

B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap\partial P=B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},x\in F}F.

Next we claim that
B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x
. Notice that

(3.13)

\partial(B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P)=\Big(\partial B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P\Big)\bigcup\Big(B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap\partial P\Big).

It suffices to show that for any
y\in\partial(B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P)
, we have
n(y)\cdot(y-x)\geq 0
, where
n(y)
is the outward normal of
B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P
at
y
. In view of (
3.13
), we have two cases. If
y\in\partial B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap P
, then
n(y)
has the same direction as
y-x
and thus
n(y)\cdot(y-x)>0
. If
y\in B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap\partial P
, then by (
3.12
),
y\in B(x,c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1})\cap F
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1}
and
x\in F
. Since
F
is a portion of a hyperplane,
y-x
is contained in the hyperplane and
n(y)
(exists a.e.) is perpendicular to the hyperplane. Thus
n(y)\cdot(y-x)=0
. Thus the two cases combined lead to the claim.

The above claim then implies

(3.14)

R^{*}(x)\geq c^{*}r_{x}^{n-i-1}=c^{*}d(x,\mathscr{F}^{n-i-1}),

as desired.

Finally, if
x\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
and
r^{0}_{x}=\inf_{G\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},x\notin G}d(x,G)>0
, then

(3.15)

B(x,r^{0}_{x})\cap\partial P=B(x,r^{0}_{x})\cap\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{n-1},x\in F}F.

Following the same argument as before, we can show that
B(x,r^{0}_{x})\cap P
is star-shaped and
R^{*}(x)\geq r^{0}_{x}
. Since
x\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
is a vertex of
P
and
P
has a finite number of vertices, then
r^{0}_{x}\geq R_{0}
for some constant
R_{0}>0
.
∎

The next lemma shows that the boundary of a bounded
n
-polytope can be covered by a finite number of star-shaped subdomains.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
P
be a bounded
n
-polytope and
R_{0}
given as Proposition
3.2
. Then for a fixed
r_{0}\leq R_{0}
, there exist finite number of balls
\{B(x_{k,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0}):j\in\{1,2,\cdots,M_{k}\}\}_{\{k=0,1,\cdots,n-1\}}
with finite overlaps satisfying

(i)

x_{0,j}\in\mathscr{F}^{0}
and
x_{k,j}\in\mathscr{F}^{k}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{k-1}
for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

(ii)

For each
k
and
j
,
P\cap B(x_{k,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{k,j}
.

(iii)

(3.16)

\partial P\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B(x_{k,j},2(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}).

Proof.

Let
x_{0,j}
(
1\leq j\leq M_{0}
) be the vertices in
\mathscr{F}^{0}
. By (
3.9
),
B(x_{0,j},c^{*}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{0,j}
for each
j
. Let

(3.17)

\mathscr{F}^{1}_{\rm int}=\mathscr{F}^{1}\setminus B(\mathscr{F}^{0},\frac{1}{32}c^{*}r_{0}).

Since
\mathscr{F}^{1}_{\rm int}
is compact, we can find a finite number of balls
B(x_{1,j},\frac{1}{32}(c^{*})^{2}r_{0})
(with finite overlaps) centered on
\mathscr{F}^{1}_{\rm int}\subset\mathscr{F}^{1}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{0}
such that

(3.18)

\mathscr{F}^{1}_{\rm int}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{1}}B(x_{1,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{2}r_{0}).

Combining (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we have

(3.19)

\mathscr{F}^{1}\subset B(\mathscr{F}^{1},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{2}r_{0})\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{1}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B(x_{k,j},2(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}).

We claim that
B(x_{1,j},\frac{1}{32}(c^{*})^{2}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{1,j}
. Indeed, by (
3.17
),
\text{dist}(x_{1,j},\mathscr{F}^{0})\geq\frac{1}{32}c^{*}r_{0}
. Then by (
3.8
), we have
R^{*}(x_{1,j})\geq\frac{1}{32}(c^{*})^{2}r_{0}
. This means that
B(x_{1,j},\frac{1}{32}(c^{*})^{2}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{1,j}
. Hence, we have proved (i) and (ii) for
k=1
.

Now consider
k=2
. Let

(3.20)

\mathscr{F}^{2}_{\rm int}=\mathscr{F}^{2}\setminus B(\mathscr{F}^{1},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{2}r_{0}).

Since
\mathscr{F}^{2}_{\rm int}
is compact, we can find finite number of balls
B(x_{2,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{2}c^{*}r_{0})
centered on
\mathscr{F}^{2}_{\rm int}\subset\mathscr{F}^{2}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{1}
such that

(3.21)

\mathscr{F}^{2}_{\rm int}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{2}}B(x_{2,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{3}r_{0}).

Then, doubling the radius of the balls, we have

(3.22)

\mathscr{F}^{2}\subset B(\mathscr{F}^{2},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{3}r_{0})\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{2}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B(x_{k,j},2(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}).

By the same reason as before, we can show that
B(x_{2,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{2}c^{*}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{2,j}
.

Repeating this process, we find a sequence of balls
B(x_{k,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})
with
x_{k,j}\in\mathscr{F}^{k}\setminus\mathscr{F}^{k-1}
such that
B(x_{k,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x_{k,j}
and

(3.23)

\mathscr{F}^{i}\subset B(\mathscr{F}^{i},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{i+1}r_{0})\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{i}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B(x_{k,j},2(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}).

for every
i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
. The proves (i) and (ii) for every
k\leq n-1
, while (iii) follows from (
3.23
) with
i=n-1
and the fact
\partial P=\mathscr{F}^{n-1}
.
∎

The following lemma shows that the MSR does not decrease rapidly if the center moves nontangentially from a boundary point to the interior points.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B_{r_{0}/2}(0)\cap P
be given as in (
2.1
) (up to a rigid transformation)
and
x\in B_{r_{0}/2}(0)\cap P
. Let
R^{**}(x):=R^{*}(x)\wedge\frac{1}{4}r_{0}
. Then for any
t\in(0,\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)]
, we have

R^{*}(x+te_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)
.

Proof.

Since
R^{**}(x)\leq R^{*}(x)
,
B(x,R^{**}(x))\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x
. This implies for any
y\in B(x,R^{**}(x))\cap\partial P
,

(3.24)

n(y)\cdot(y-x)\geq 0.

Note that for any
t\in(0,\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)]
,
B(x+te_{n},\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x))\subset B(x,R^{**}(x))
. Thus, for any
y\in B(x+te_{n},\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x))\cap\partial P
,

(3.25)

n(y)\cdot(y-(x+te_{n}))=n(y)\cdot(y-x)-tn(y)\cdot e_{n}\geq-tn(y)\cdot e_{n}\geq 0,

where the last estimate uses the fact
n(y)\cdot e_{n}\leq 0
. This implies that
B(x+te_{n},\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x))
is star-shaped with respect to
x+te_{n}
and thus
R^{*}(x+te_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)
.
∎

4.
Uniform bound of doubling index

In this section, we prove a crucial lemma that is used to control the doubling index in a star-shaped subdomain of a polytope.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\Omega=B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
0
. Let
u
be a harmonic function in
\Omega
with
u=0
on
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap\partial P
. Then there exist
c_{1}=c_{1}(L)>0
such that for any
x\in B_{r_{0}/8}(0)\cap P
and any
0<r<c_{1}\{R^{*}(x)\wedge\frac{1}{4}r_{0}\}
,

(4.1)

N_{u}(x,r)\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0}).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
is given as in (
2.1
) above a Lipschitz graph.
Let
c\leq\frac{1}{4}
be a small number. Fix
x\in B_{r_{0}/8}(0)\cap P
. If
cr_{0}\leq R^{**}(x):=R^{*}(x)\wedge\frac{1}{4}r_{0}
, then by Lemma
3.4
, for any
0<t\leq\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)
,

(4.2)

R^{*}(x+te_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x).

Since
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
is star-shaped, then by Lemma
3.4
again, for any
0<t\leq\frac{1}{2}r_{0}
,

(4.3)

R^{*}(0+te_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}r_{0}.

Recall that
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, then the line segment connecting
x
and
0
is contained in
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
(above the boundary graph). As a simple geometric observation, the line segment connecting
x+\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)e_{n}
to
0+\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)e_{n}
has a distance at least
\frac{1}{2}\ell_{0}R^{**}(x)
away from the boundary, where

(4.4)

\ell_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+L^{2}}}.

Combining this line segment with the vertical line segments from
x
to
x+\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)e_{n}
and from
0+\frac{1}{2}R^{**}(x)e_{n}
to
0
, we obtain a Lipschitz curve connecting
x
and
0
. Moreover, the Lipschitz curve is contained in
B_{r_{0}}(0)\cap P
and
B_{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{0}R^{**}(x)}(y)\cap P
is star-shaped for any
y
on the Lipschitz curve. Also the length of the curve is bounded by
Cr_{0}\leq C_{1}R^{**}(x)
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
, we have

(4.5)

N_{u}(x,\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{**}(x))\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0}),

where
C
depends on
c
and
L
. If
cr_{0}\leq R^{*}(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}r_{0}
, then the above argument also implies
N_{u}(x,\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(x))\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0})
. This typical argument will be applied repeatedly below.

Now we consider the case
R^{*}(x)<cr_{0}
with sufficiently small
c>0
.

Let
\pi(x)\in\partial P
be the vertical projection of
x
onto
\partial P
. Thus
x=\pi(x)+e_{n}|\pi(x)-x|
. We set
z_{n-1}=\pi(x)
. Let
z_{n-2}
be the closest point to
z_{n-1}
from
\mathscr{F}^{n-2}\cap B_{r_{0}/2}(0)
.
In general, let
z_{n-j}
be the closest point to
z_{n-j+1}
from
\mathscr{F}^{n-j}\cap B_{r_{0}/2}(0)
, until either one of the following two cases happens.

Case 1: for some
2\leq j_{0}\leq n
,
z_{n-j_{0}}
does not exist in
\mathscr{F}^{n-j_{0}}\cap B_{r_{0}/2}(0)
, i.e.,
\mathscr{F}^{n-j_{0}}\cap B_{r_{0}/2}(0)=\emptyset
.

Case 2: all
z_{n-j}
exist for
2\leq j\leq n
.

Clearly, if Case 1 does not happen, then Case 2 must happen.

For case 1, we claim that there exists
c^{\prime}>0
and some
j
with
2\leq j<j_{0}
such that
R^{*}(z_{n-j})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
.
Actually, if
z_{n-j_{0}+1}\in B_{r_{0}/4}(0)
, then
R^{*}(z_{n-j_{0}+1})>\frac{1}{4}c^{*}r_{0}
due to (
3.8
) and the fact that
z_{n-j_{0}}
does not exist in
B_{r_{0}/2}(0)
. Now we consider the situation
z_{n-j_{0}+1}\notin B_{r_{0}/4}(0)
, then

(4.6)

\frac{1}{8}r_{0}<|z_{n-1}-z_{n-j_{0}+1}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}-2}|z_{n-j}-z_{n-j-1}|.

The pigeonhole principle implies that there exists at least one
j
with
1\leq j\leq j_{0}-2
such that

(4.7)

|z_{n-j}-z_{n-j-1}|\geq\frac{1}{8n}r_{0}.

Consequently, by (
3.8
), we have
R^{*}(z_{n-j})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
.

For Case 2, thanks to (
3.9
), we also have
R^{*}(z_{0})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
for some possibly different
c^{\prime}>0
. Combining both cases, without loss of generality, we may assume that the integer
j_{s}
with
1\leq j_{s}\leq j_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
R^{*}(z_{n-j_{s}})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
.

Next, we will develop a procedure to bound the doubling index through the sequence of points
z_{n-j}
with a stopping time before reaching
j=j_{s}
. Let
1\leq k_{1}\leq j_{s}
be the smallest integer such that

(4.8)

R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}})>32\ell_{0}^{-1}|z_{n-k_{1}}-x|\quad\text{and}\quad R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}})>32\ell_{0}^{-1}R^{*}(x).

We temporarily assume that such
k_{1}
exists.
Then we show that there exists
C>0
such that

(4.9)

N(x,\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(x))\leq CN(z_{n-k_{1}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}})).

In fact, by the selection of
k_{1}
and (
3.8
), for each
1\leq j<k_{1}

(4.10)

\displaystyle c^{*}|z_{n-j-1}-x|-c^{*}|z_{n-j}-x|

\displaystyle\leq c^{*}|z_{n-j}-z_{n-j-1}|

\displaystyle\leq R^{*}(z_{n-j})

\displaystyle\leq 32\ell_{0}^{-1}|z_{n-j}-x|+32\ell_{0}^{-1}R^{*}(x).

Thus

(4.11)

|z_{n-j-1}-x|\leq\frac{32\ell_{0}^{-1}+c^{*}}{c^{*}}|z_{n-j}-x|+\frac{32\ell_{0}^{-1}}{c^{*}}R^{*}(x).

It follows by an iteration that

(4.12)

|z_{n-k_{1}}-x|\leq C(n,L)R^{*}(x).

By this and the assumption (
4.8
), and a similar argument as before, we can find a Lipschitz curve contained in
B(z_{n-k_{1}},|x-z_{n-k_{1}}|+R^{*}(x))\cap P
connecting
x
and
z_{n-k_{1}}
whose length is comparable to
|x-z_{n-k_{1}}|+R^{*}(x)\lesssim R^{*}(x)
(by (
4.12
)). Moreover,
P\cap B(y,\frac{1}{2}\ell_{0}R^{*}(x))
is star-shaped for all
y
on the Lipschitz curve. Thus (
4.9
) follows from Lemma
2.4
, provided that
k_{1}
exists. We point out that the particular constant
32\ell_{0}^{-1}
in (
4.8
) guarantees that the condition (iii) in Lemma
2.4
is satisfied with
R=\frac{1}{4}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}})
and
r=\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(x)
.

On the other hand, if
k_{1}
does not exist, then we can show
R^{*}(x)\geq cr_{0}
for some small
c>0
. Actually, if
k_{1}
does not exist, then for any
1\leq j\leq j_{s}
, we have

(4.13)

R^{*}(z_{n-j})\leq 32\ell_{0}^{-1}|z_{n-j}-x|\quad\text{or}\quad R^{*}(z_{n-j})\leq 32\ell_{0}^{-1}R^{*}(x).

Consider
j=1
. A simple geometric observation implies

(4.14)

|z_{n-1}-x|=|\pi(x)-x|\leq CR^{*}(x).

Thus, either case in (
4.13
) with
j=1
gives

(4.15)

R^{*}(z_{n-1})\leq CR^{*}(x).

Then consider
j=2
in (
4.13
). By (
4.14
), (
4.15
) and (
3.8
), we have

(4.16)

|z_{n-2}-x|\leq|z_{n-1}-x|+|z_{n-2}-z_{n-1}|\leq CR^{*}(x)+\frac{1}{c^{*}}R^{*}(z_{n-1})\leq CR^{*}(x).

Hence, (
4.13
) with
j=2
gives

(4.17)

R^{*}(z_{n-2})\leq CR^{*}(x).

Repeating this argument until
j=j_{s}
, we get
R^{*}(z_{n-j_{s}})\leq CR^{*}(x)
for some large
C>0
. But since we know by our construction that
R^{*}(z_{n-j_{s}})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
. Then we must have
R^{*}(x)\geq cr_{0}
for some small
c>0
. This situation has been solved at the beginning of the proof.

We only need to continue with the case when
k_{1}
exists. If
k_{1}=j_{s}
, we stop since
R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}})\geq c^{\prime}r_{0}
. Otherwise, let
k_{2}
with
k_{1}<k_{2}\leq j_{s}
be the smallest integer such that

(4.18)

R^{*}(z_{n-k_{2}})>32\ell_{0}^{-1}|z_{n-k_{2}}-z_{n-k_{1}}|\quad\text{and}\quad R^{*}(z_{n-k_{2}})>32\ell_{0}^{-1}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}}).

Again, if
k_{2}
does not exist, we can show by a similar argument that
R^{*}(z_{n-k_{2}})\geq cr_{0}
. In this case, we have

(4.19)

N_{u}(z_{n-k_{1}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}}))\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0}),

which together with (
4.9
) gives (
4.1
).

It remains to consider the case when
k_{2}
exists. Applying a similar argument as before, we have

(4.20)

N_{u}(z_{n-k_{1}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{1}}))\leq CN_{u}(z_{n-k_{2}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{2}})).

Repeating this process until
k_{m+1}
does not exist or
k_{m}=j_{s}
, we get a sequence of
k_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, with
R^{*}(z_{n-k_{m}})\geq cr_{0}
. Moreover, it holds for each
1\leq i\leq m-1

(4.21)

N_{u}(z_{n-k_{i}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{i}}))\leq CN_{u}(z_{n-k_{i+1}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{i+1}})),

and

(4.22)

N_{u}(z_{n-k_{m}},\frac{1}{8}\ell_{0}R^{*}(z_{n-k_{m}}))\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0}).

Finally, the last two estimates combined with (
4.9
) imply the desired estimate.
∎

5.
Estimates of nodal sets

5.1.
Nodal sets of harmonic functions

We first recall the well-known interior estimate of nodal sets over a flat boundary
[
1
,
6
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
x\in\{y=(y^{\prime},y^{n}):y^{n}\geq 0\}
.
Let
u
be a non-zero harmonic function in
B_{8r}(x)\cap\{y^{n}>0\}
for some
r>0
and
u=0
on
B_{8r}(x)\cap\{y^{n}=0\}
. There exists
C
depending only on
n
such that

(5.1)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(u)\cap B_{r}(x))\leq C(N_{u}(x,4r)+1)r^{n-1}.

With the above lemma, we can obtain the estimate of nodal sets for harmonic functions in a star-shaped subdomain of a polytope.

Theorem 5.2
.

Under the same assumption of Lemma
4.1
, we have

(5.2)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(z(u)\cap P\cap B(0,\frac{1}{8}r_{0}))\leq C(N_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0})+1)r_{0}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Let us define
E_{k}
as follows:

(5.3)

E_{k}=\Big\{y\in B(0,\frac{1}{8}r_{0})\cap P:2^{-k}r_{0}<d(y,\mathscr{F}^{n-2})\leq 2^{-k+1}r_{0}\Big\},\quad k=1,2,3,\cdots

Then

(5.4)

B(0,\frac{1}{8}r_{0})\cap P=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}.

Fix
k\geq 1
. By (
3.7
), for each
x\in E_{k}
,
R^{*}(x)\geq 2^{-k}r_{0}
. We can cover
E_{k}
by a finite number of balls
B(x_{j},2^{-k-2}r_{0})
with bounded overlaps such that
x_{j}\in E_{k}
and

(5.5)

E_{k}\subset\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-5}r_{0})\cap P,

where
c_{1}
is the constant given in Lemma
4.1
. The number of these balls is bounded by
C2^{(n-2)k}
since
\mathscr{F}^{n-2}
is
(n-2)
-dimensional.
Since
d(x_{j},\mathscr{F}^{n-2})\geq 2^{-k}r_{0}
,
B(x_{j},2^{-k-2}r_{0})
does not intersect with
\mathscr{F}^{n-2}
. Thus
B(x_{j},2^{-k-2}r_{0})\cap\partial P
is an empty set or a portion of a flat plane. Hence, we can apply Lemma
5.1
to get

(5.6)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(u)\cap B(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-5}r_{0})\cap P)\leq C(N_{u}(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-3}r_{0})+1)(2^{-k-5}r_{0})^{n-1}.

Since
R^{*}(x_{j})\geq 2^{-k}r_{0}
, then by Lemma
4.1
,

(5.7)

N_{u}(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-3}r_{0})\leq N_{u}(x_{j},c_{1}(R^{*}(x_{j})\wedge\frac{1}{4}r_{0}))\leq CN_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0}).

It follows that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(u)\cap E_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(Z(u)\cap B(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-5}r_{0})\cap P)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j}(N_{u}(x_{j},c_{1}2^{-k-3}r_{0})+1)2^{-(k+5)(n-1)}r_{0}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{(n-2)k}(N_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0})+1)2^{-(k+5)(n-1)}r_{0}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u}(0,\frac{1}{2}r_{0})+1)2^{-k}r_{0}^{n-1}.

Summing over
k\geq 1
, we obtain (
5.2
).
∎

5.2.
Dirichlet eigenfunctions

Let
P
be a bounded Lipschitz polytope. Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the Dirichlet eigenfunction of
-\Delta
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda>0
, namely,
-\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}
in
P
and
\varphi_{\lambda}=0
on
\partial P
. Let

(5.9)

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=e^{t\sqrt{\lambda}}\varphi_{\lambda}(x).

Then
u_{\lambda}
is harmonic in
\widetilde{P}:=P\times\mathbb{R}
and
u_{\lambda}=0
on
\partial\widetilde{P}=\partial P\times\mathbb{R}
.

The next lemma gives an upper bound of the doubling index for
u_{\lambda}
at large scales.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let P be a bounded Lipschitz polytope as above. Let
u_{\lambda}
be the harmonic extension in
\widetilde{P}
of the Dirichlet eigenfunction
\varphi_{\lambda}
given by (
5.9
). There exists
r_{1}=r_{1}(P)>0
such that if
(x,t)\in\widetilde{P}
and
0<r<r_{1},-1<t<1
, then
N_{u_{\lambda}}((x,t),r)\leq C_{r}\sqrt{\lambda}
, where
C_{r}
depends only on r and P.

The proof of the above lemma is standard. We refer to
[
12
]
for a proof in general Lipschitz domains and to
[
1
,
2
]
for smooth domains. The details are skipped.

Finally , we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

To estimate the nodal set of
\varphi_{\lambda}
in
P
, it suffices to estimate the nodal set of
u_{\lambda}
in
P\times(-\delta,\delta)
for some small
\delta>0
(independent of
\lambda
).

We need the following property: if
B(x,r)\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x
, then
B((x,0),r)\cap\widetilde{P}
is star-shaped with respect to
(x,0)
. Note that
y\in\partial P
if and only if
(y,t)\in\partial\widetilde{P}
.
Assume
B(x,r)\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x
.
To show
B((x,0),r)\cap\widetilde{P}
is star-shaped with respect to
(x,0)
, we consider a point
(y,s)\in B((x,0),r)\cap\partial\widetilde{P}
. Note that this implies
y\in B(x,r)\cap\partial P
. Since
B(x,r)\cap P
is star-shaped with respect to
x
and by the definition, for any
y\in B(x,r)\cap\partial P
, we have

(5.10)

(y-x)\cdot n(y)\geq 0.

Observe that the outer normal of
(y,s)\in\partial\widetilde{P}
is
(n(y),0)
. Then it is easy to verify

(5.11)

((y,s)-(x,0))\cdot(n(y),0)=(y-x)\cdot n(y)\geq 0.

Since
(y,s)\in B((x,0),r)\cap\partial\widetilde{P}
is arbitrary, we see that
B((x,0),r)\cap\widetilde{P}
is star-shaped with respect to
(x,0)
. The property is proved.

Recall the partition of
\partial P
in Proposition
3.3
, i.e., there exists
\{x_{k,j}\}\subset\partial P
such that

(5.12)

\partial P\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B(x_{k,j},2(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}).

Moreover,
B(x_{k,j},(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})\cap P
is star-shaped. Thus by the previous property, the extended subdomains
B((x_{k,j},0),(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})\cap\widetilde{P}
are star-shaped with respect to
(x_{k,j},0)
. In addition, for some
\delta_{1}>0
, we have

(5.13)

B(\partial P\times\{0\},\delta_{1})\cap\widetilde{P}\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigcup_{j=1}^{M_{k}}B((x_{k,j},0),4(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0})\cap\widetilde{P}.

Lemma
5.3
indicates that

(5.14)

N_{u_{\lambda}}((x_{k,j},0),\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c^{*}r_{0})\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

By Theorem
5.2
(with
r_{0}
replaced by
32(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0}=(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k}c_{*}r_{0}
), we have

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}(B((x_{k,j},0),4(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0})\cap\widetilde{P}\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u_{\lambda}}((x_{k,j},0),16(\frac{1}{32}c^{*})^{k+1}r_{0})+1)r_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}r_{0}^{n},

where we also used (
5.14
) in the last inequality.
By (
5.13
) and the fact
M_{k}
is bounded by a constant depending on
P
and
r_{0}
,

(5.16)

\mathbb{H}^{n}(B(\partial P\times\{0\},\delta_{1})\cap\widetilde{P}\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

Let
P_{\delta}=B(\partial P,\delta)\cap P
.
By a simple geometric observation, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(5.17)

P_{\delta}\times(-\delta,\delta)\subset B(\partial P\times\{0\},\delta_{1})\cap\widetilde{P}.

Therefore,

(5.18)

\mathbb{H}^{n}(P_{\delta}\times(-\delta,\delta)\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

On the other hand, since
(P\setminus P_{\delta})\times(-\delta,\delta)
is an interior region away from the boundary
\partial\widetilde{P}
, by the interior estimate of the nodal set in Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.3
(combined with a partition of interior balls),

(5.19)

\mathbb{H}^{n}((P\setminus P_{\delta})\times(-\delta,\delta)\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

It follows that

(5.20)

\mathbb{H}^{n}(P\times(-\delta,\delta)\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

Consequently, due to (
5.9
),

(5.21)

\mathbb{H}^{n-1}(P\cap Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))=(2\delta)^{-1}\mathbb{H}^{n}(P\times(-\delta,\delta)\cap Z(u_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}.

This proves the main theorem.
∎

Appendix A
Outline of the proof of Lemma
2.3

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
x=0\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Consider a harmonic function
u
defined in
B_{2R}(0)\cap\Omega
satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
B_{2R}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
.

Step 1
: Monotonicity of Almgren’s frequency function in star-shaped domains.
For
r\in(0,2R)
, define

\displaystyle H(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}\left(0\right)\cap\Omega}u^{2}d\sigma,

\displaystyle D(r)=\int_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2},

where
d\sigma
denotes the surface measure. The Almgren’s frequency function of
u
centered at
0
is given by

(A.1)

\beta_{u}(r)=\beta_{u}(0,r)=\frac{rD(r)}{H(r)}.

It can be shown that
\beta_{u}(r)
is nondecreasing in
r\in(0,2R)
, provided that
B_{2R}(0)\cap\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to the center
0
. A rigorous proof can be found in
[
5
]
.

Step 2
: Monotonicity of the doubling index.
The relation between
H(r)
and the frequency function is given by

(A.2)

\frac{H^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}-\frac{n-1}{r}=\frac{2}{r}\beta_{u}(r).

Integrating (
A.2
) over
r\in(r_{1},r_{2})\subset(0,2R)
, we obtain

(A.3)

\log\frac{H(r_{2})}{H(r_{1})}-(n-1)\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}=2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{\beta_{u}(r)}{r}dr

Taking
r_{1}=t
and
r_{2}=2t
in the above identity and using the monotonicity of the frequency function in Step 1, we derive from (
A.3
) that
H(2t)/H(t)
is nondecreasing in
t\in(0,R)
. Therefore, for any
0<\tau<t<R
,

(A.4)

\frac{H(2\tau)}{H(\tau)}\leq\frac{H(2t)}{H(t)}.

Equivalently,

(A.5)

H(2\tau)H(t)\leq H(\tau)H(2t).

Fix
s
and
r
such that
0<s<r<R
and let
\tau\in(0,s)
and
t\in(s,r)
. Integrating (
A.5
) in
\tau\in(0,s)
and in
t\in(s,r)
, we have

(A.6)

\int_{B_{2s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\int_{B_{r}\setminus B_{s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\leq\int_{B_{s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\int_{B_{2r}\setminus B_{2s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}.

Adding
\int_{B_{2s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\int_{B_{s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}
on both sides, we get

(A.7)

\int_{B_{2s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\leq\int_{B_{s}\cap\Omega}u^{2}\int_{B_{2r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}.

This implies (
2.3
) as desired.
∎

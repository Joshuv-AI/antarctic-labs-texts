On a class of infinite semipositone problems for (p,q) Laplace operator
R. Dhanya, R. Harish, Sarbani Pramanik

Abstract
. We analyze a non-linear elliptic boundary value problem, that involves ( p , q ) (p,q) Laplace operator, for the existence of its positive solution in an arbitrary smooth bounded domain. The non-linearity here is driven by a continuous function in ( 0 , ∞ ) (0,\infty) which is singular, monotonically increasing and eventually positive. We prove the existence of a positive solution of this problem using a fixed point theorem due to Amann [ 3 ] . In addition, for a specific nonlinearity we derive that the obtained solution is maximal in nature. The main results obtained here are first of its kind for a ( p , q ) (p,q) Laplace operator in an arbitrary bounded domain.

Introduction

We consider the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u

\displaystyle=\lambda\frac{f(u)}{u^{\beta}}\ \text{in}\ \Omega

\displaystyle u

In the next theorem under two more hypotheses on the non-linear function

Definition 2.1
.

We define

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{q-2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(w)}{w^{\beta}}\phi\ dx

(1)

By

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
h\in C(\Omega)
such that
|h(x)|\leq\frac{C}{d(x)^{\beta}},
there exists a unique
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
solving

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}w-\Delta_{q}w

\displaystyle=h\ \text{in}\ \Omega

(2.1)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega

in the weak sense.

Proof.

We will prove the existence of the solution by the direct minimization technique. The energy functional
E:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
corresponding to
-\Delta_{p}w-\Delta_{q}w=h(x)
is :

(2.2)

E(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-\int_{\Omega}hu\ dx

Using Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality we get

E(u_{n})\geq\frac{1}{p}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}-C^{\prime}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

and hence

is coercive. In a standard way, we can also show that
E
is weakly lower semi continuous on the space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).
Thus
E
possesses a global minimizer
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and by weak comparison principle this minimizer

is the unique weak solution of the required problem.
∎

4.

In this section, we are interested in the following boundary value problem:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u

\displaystyle=\lambda(u^{\sigma}-\frac{1}{u^{\beta}})\ \text{in}\ \Omega

Our aim in this section is to prove the existence of a positive maximal (global maximal) solution for the Dirichlet problem (

We can verify that

Definition 4.1

We say that

\Phi_{\lambda}
is a supersolution of (

) and

if

We make the following observation which follows from Amman’s fixed point theorem.

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose there exists a global supersolution to (

\Phi_{\lambda}.
Also assume that
\Psi_{\lambda}\leq\Phi_{\lambda}
where

(

We now prove that the solution of the following BVP

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}z-\Delta_{q}z

\displaystyle=\lambda z^{\sigma}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle>0\mbox{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega

is a global supersolution to (

. Let us denote
{\Phi}_{\lambda}
to be the unique solution of (

Thus,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is a global supersolution of (

).

Proof of Theorem

:
We first show that for large

\Psi_{\lambda}\leq\Phi_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
. Since
\Psi_{\lambda}
is a positive subsolution of (

(4.2)

\Psi_{\lambda}

Now Theorem
1.1
ensures the existence of a positive maximal solution for the problem (

[\Psi_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda}]
. Let us call this maximal solution

. Again if
u
is any other positive solution of (

), then Lemma
5.1
gives

in

Next suppose

for (

is monotone increasing, so there exists some

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=0\ on\ \partial\Omega

Now to get a deeper understanding of the solution of equation (

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}-\gamma\Delta_{q}\tilde{\Phi}_{\lambda}

Lemma 4.3

There exists a positive constant

Proof.

We adapt the idea of De Giorgi - Stampachia iteration method to obtain the uniform

Bounds for the periods of eigenfunctions on arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds over surfaces
Jiaqi Hou

Abstract
. Let ψ \psi be a Hecke-Maass form on a compact congruence arithmetic hyperbolic 3 3 -manifold X X , and let Y Y be a hyperbolic surface in X X that is not necessarily closed. We obtain a power saving result over the local bound for the period of ψ \psi along Y Y , by applying the method of arithmetic amplification developed by Iwaniec and Sarnak.



1.
Introduction

If
X
is a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
and
\psi
is a Laplace eigenfunction on
X
satisfying
\Delta\psi+\lambda^{2}\psi=0
, we are interested in the bound of the integral of
\psi
over a submanifold
Y
of dimension
d
. We normalize
\psi
by
\|\psi\|_{2}=1
. Zelditch
[
20
]
provides the general bounds

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\psi(x)dx\ll\lambda^{(n-d-1)/2},

which is optimal on the sphere.

In this paper, we let
X
be a compact congruence arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-manifold arising from a quaternion division algebra over a number field with one complex place. We let
\psi
be a Hecke-Maass form on
X
, that is an eigenfunction of the Laplacian and Hecke operators. We shall always assume
\psi
to be
L^{2}
-normalized. In this context, it is more natural to let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be the spectral parameter of
\psi
, that is
\Delta\psi+(1+\lambda^{2})\psi=0
. As we are considering large-eigenvalue asymptotics, we will also assume that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
Y
be a hyperbolic surface in
X
, and let
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Y)
be a smooth compactly supported function on
Y
. We define

\displaystyle P_{Y}(\psi,b)=\int_{Y}b(x)\psi(x)dx.

Zelditch’s bound gives
P_{Y}(\psi,b)\ll 1
.
Our main result is improving the exponent in the local bound as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke-Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda
. For any hyperbolic surface
Y
in
X
, we have

\displaystyle P_{Y}(\psi,b)\ll_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1/74+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends on
X
, the support of
b
, and
L^{\infty}
-norms of
b
and finitely many of its derivatives.

We may compare the bounds from (
1.1
) and Theorem
1.1
with the average size of
P_{Y}(\psi,b)
. This may be computed using the relative trace formula, which gives

(1.2)

\sum_{|\lambda_{i}-\lambda|<Q}|P_{Y}(\psi_{i},b)|^{2}\sim 1

for a sufficiently large
Q
depending on
Y
and
b
. Here,
\{\psi_{i}\}
is an orthonormal basis of Hecke–Maass forms on
X
, and
\lambda_{i}
is the spectral parameter of
\psi_{i}
. (Note that this requires the bounds for the off-diagonal terms given in Proposition
3.4
, unless we assume that
Y
is embedded in
X
.) The number of
\lambda_{i}
in the sum is
\sim\lambda^{2}
by the Weyl law, and so the average size of
P_{Y}(\psi,b)
is
\lambda^{-1}
.

When the hyperbolic surface
Y
is closed, and we take the function
b
to be
1
, we expect stronger bounds for the period
P_{Y}(\psi):=P_{Y}(\psi,1)
to hold based on automorphic distinction principles and period formulas. As an illustration, let
E
be an imaginary quadratic field. We now allow
X
to be a noncompact manifold obtained as an adelic quotient of the group
{\rm PGL}(2,E)
. Let
\psi
be a Hecke–Maass cusp form on
X
, and let
\{\psi_{i}\}
be an orthonormal basis of such cusp forms. If
Y
is the surface in
X
arising from the subgroup
{\rm PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
of
{\rm PGL}(2,E)
, then
P_{Y}(\psi)
is the Flicker–Rallis period of
\psi
. If we assume that
\psi
lies in an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
, then it is known (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
19
]
) that
P_{Y}(\psi)
can be nonzero only when
\pi
is the base change of a cuspidal automorphic representation
\sigma
of
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
with central character
\eta
, where
\eta
is the quadratic character associated to
E/\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover, in this case, there will be a vector in
\pi
whose Flicker–Rallis period is nonzero.

Let us assume the asymptotic (
1.2
) for the noncompact quotient
X
. There will be
\sim\lambda
cusp forms
\psi_{i}
with
|\lambda_{i}-\lambda|<Q
that are base changes from
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
(after possibly increasing the level of
X
), and so if we combine (
1.2
) with distinction for the Flicker–Rallis period, it will show the existence of forms in any window
|\lambda_{i}-\lambda|<Q
with periods satisfying
P_{Y}(\psi_{i})\gg\lambda_{i}^{-1/2}
. We expect a similar result to hold in the case where
X
is compact.

Moreover, when
\psi
is obtained by base change from a form
\sigma
on
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
with central character
\eta
, the results of
[
12
]
lead us to expect a special value formula for
P_{Y}(\psi)
of the asymptotic form
P_{Y}(\psi)\sim\lambda^{-1/2}L(1,\sym^{2}\sigma)/L(1,\Ad\,\pi)

1

It is not clear to us why
L(s,\sym^{2}\sigma)
should be holomorphic at 1, when
\sigma
is a dihedral form with central character
\eta
and cuspidal base change to
{\rm PGL}(2,E)
. However, it may be shown to be holomorphic in the case of Maass forms with large spectral parameter considered here.
. The paper
[
12
]
proves a formula of this type in the case of
{\rm PGL}(2)
over a

quadratic field. Moreover, an analogous formula for

over general fields, but with a quadratic twist inserted into the period over

, is proved in
[

. In any case, such a period formula, when combined with the bound

from
[

, and the bound

that should follow from
[

, would give

.

In this paper we apply the arithmetic amplification introduced by Iwaniec and Sarnak in
[
11
]
, which they used to deduce a strong bound for the
L^{\infty}
-norms of Hecke-Maass forms on congruence arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces. Let us now assume that

is a congruence arithmetic hyperbolic surface, that is the quotient of the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by the group of units in a maximal order in an indefinite

\mathbb{Q}
, and assume
\psi
is an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke-Maass cusp form on

with spectral parameter
\lambda
. They then prove that
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\ll_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{5/12+\varepsilon}
.

After Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
11
]
, the method of arithmetic amplification is applied to bound the sup-norms of Hecke-Maass forms in different cases.
For instance, Blomer and Maga proved subconvexity for sup-norms of cusp forms on
\operatorname{PGL}(n)
in
[
6
]
. Blomer and Pohl considered the sup-norm problem of Hecke-Maass forms for
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
in
[
7
]
. Blomer, Harcos, Maga and Milićević solved the sup-norm problem for the
group
\operatorname{GL}(2)
over a general number field in
[
4
]
.
For arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-manifolds,
Koyama proved a similar result in
[
13
]
, and
this result has been improved by Blomer, Harcos and Milićević in
[
5
]
, where a bound simultaneously in the eigenvalue and the level aspect was proved. Milićević showed a lower bound for the sup-norm of Hecke Maass forms on arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-manifolds in
[
17
]
.

Marshall applies amplification to the restriction problems for compact
S
in
[
16
]
. For any geodesic segment
l
of unit length, he shows that
\|\psi|_{l}\|_{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{3/14+\varepsilon}
and improved bounds for Fourier coefficients of
\psi
along
l
.
It is hoped to apply amplification to restriction problems on other groups. For instance, Marshall deals with the case of restricting an
\operatorname{SL}(3)
Hecke-Maass form to a maximal flat subspace in
[
15
]
.
In this paper, we consider the restriction problem of the group
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
to the subgroup
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

1.1.
Outline of the proof

We will prove Theorem
1.1
in a similar way as the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 1.3]
.
We shall rewrite the period integral as a pairing of the eigenfunction with the smooth cutoff function integrated along
\mathbb{H}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi,b\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}b(z)\psi(g_{0}z)dz,

where
g_{0}
is the isometry mapping the standard
\mathbb{H}^{2}
to the surface
Y
, after passing to the quotient.

To estimate such integral, we construct an
\operatorname{SU}(2)
-bi-invariant test function
k_{\lambda}
via the
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
Harish-Chandra inversion of an even real-variable function
h_{\lambda}
that is non-negative on the spectral parameters and concentrates near
\pm\lambda
. We sum the test function over the arithmetic lattice to form the kernel function

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}k_{\lambda}(x^{-1}\gamma y)

on the arithmetic hyperbolic
3
-manifold
X
. The kernel
K
has the spectral expansion

\displaystyle\sum_{i}h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{i})\psi_{i}(x)\overline{\psi_{i}(y)}.

If we integrate the kernel against
b\times\bar{b}
along
g_{0}\mathbb{H}^{2}\times g_{0}\mathbb{H}^{2}
, and apply the amplifier operator
\mathcal{T}
, we obtain the amplification inequality (
3.1
).
Roughly speaking, we can bound
\langle\psi,b\rangle
by bounding the integrals

\displaystyle I(\lambda,g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0})=\iint_{\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}}b(z_{1})\overline{b(z_{2})}k_{\lambda}(z_{1}^{-1}g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0}z_{2})dz_{1}dz_{2},

for
\gamma
running through the translations appearing in
\mathcal{T}
. The serious argument of this part occupies Section
3
.

Let us write
g=g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0}
for simplicity. The analytic problem for bounding
I(\lambda,g)
is the main part of this paper and occupies Section
5
. We unfold the integral
I(\lambda,g)
using the inverse Harish-Chandra transform and Harish-Chandra’s integral expansion formula for the spherical functions. Then the inner integral of the unfolding is an oscillatory integral over
\operatorname{SU}(2)\times\mathbb{H}^{2}\times g\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The estimate of the oscillatory integral is based on the method of stationary phase and we will use a geometric description to determine the critical points of the phase function.

Consequently, the integral
I(\lambda,g)
will behave like the general local bound when
g
is close to
H^{\prime}
, which is the stabilizer of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. But
I(\lambda,g)
has a smaller value when
g
is away from
H^{\prime}
. This leads to solving the diophantine problem of estimating the number of times a Hecke operator maps
\mathbb{H}^{2}
back close to itself. We do this in Section
4
. Finally in Section
3
, we can combine the two bounds on
I(\lambda,g)
with the estimate of Hecke returns to obtain Theorem
1.1
.

4.1.
Vector spaces over a number field

We first need some definitions on adelic norms and normed vector spaces over a number field. Let
X
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
F
.
If
v
is a place of
F
, we let
X_{v}=X\otimes_{F}F_{v}
. We shall work with norms
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on the spaces
X_{v}
which are assumed to satisfy the following conditions. If
v
is an infinite place, we assume that the norm comes from a positive definite quadratic or hermitian form. If
v
is finite we assume that
\|x\|_{v}\in q_{v}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{0\}
for all
x\in X_{v}
, which implies that the unit ball
L
of
\|\cdot\|_{v}
is a lattice in
X
such that
\|x\|_{v}=\operatorname{min}\{|c|_{v}\,|\,x\in cL\}
.

By a norm on
X(\mathbb{A})
, we mean a choice of norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on each
X_{v}
, with a compatibility condition that there is an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset X
such that
\mathcal{L}_{v}=\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}_{v}
is the unit ball of
\|\cdot\|_{v}
for almost all
v
. This ensures that we may define
\|x\|
for
x\in X(\mathbb{A})
by the formula
\|x\|=\prod_{v}\|\cdot\|_{v}
. We note that
\|\cdot\|
does not satisfy the triangle inequality. We define a normed
F
-space to be a finite dimensional vector space
X
over
F
with a norm on
X(\mathbb{A})
.

If
X
and
Y
are two normed
F
-spaces, and
T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(X,Y)
, then we may define
\|T\|_{v}
in the usual way

\displaystyle\|T\|_{v}=\sup_{0\neq x\in X_{v}}\frac{\|Tx\|_{v}}{\|x\|_{v}}

for all places
v
. As
\|T\|_{v}=1
for almost all
v
, we may also define the adelic operator norm
\|T\|=\prod_{v}\|T\|_{v}
. For any finite set of places
S
, we define
\|T\|^{S}=\prod_{v\notin S}\|T\|_{v}
and
\|T\|_{S}=\prod_{v\in S}\|T\|_{v}
.
As with vectors, these operator norms do not satisfy the triangle inequality, but they do satisfy
\|TU\|\leq\|T\|\|U\|
.

If we choose a basis
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for
X
, this allows us to define a standard adelic norm in the following way. At infinite places we define the norm by requiring the basis to be orthonormal, and at finite places we require that the unit ball associated to the norm be the lattice spanned by the basis. We shall refer to these norms as the standard norm with respect to the basis.

We now prove some lemmas that let us show that certain
F
-linear maps have nontrivial kernel, and bound the complexity of the kernel. Let
X
and
Y
be two
F
-spaces, with bases
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}
, and let
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
be the corresponding standard norms. If
T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(X,Y)
, we define
{}^{t}T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(Y,X)
the transpose of
T
with repect to the chosen bases, and we have
\|T\|_{v}=\|^{t}T\|_{v}
for all
v
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T\in\operatorname{GL}(X)
. Let
v
be a place of
F
, and extend the norm
|\cdot|_{v}
on
F_{v}
to the algebraic closure
\overline{F_{v}}
. If
\lambda_{v}
is an eignevalue of
T_{v}
, then

\displaystyle|\lambda_{v}|_{v}\geq\frac{1}{\|T\|^{n-1}\|T\|^{v}}.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda_{v,1}=\lambda_{v}
,
\lambda_{v,2},\dots,\lambda_{v,n}
are the eigenvalues of
T_{v}
repeated with multiplicity. For any place
w
we have
|\det T|_{w}\leq\|T\|_{w}^{n}
, and so the product formula
\prod_{w}|\det T|_{w}=1
implies that
|\det T|_{v}\geq\left(\|T\|^{v}\right)^{-n}
. Since
|\lambda_{v,i}|\leq\|T\|_{v}
for all
i
, we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{v}|\|T\|_{v}^{n-1}\geq\prod_{i}|\lambda_{v,i}|_{v}=|\det T|_{v}\geq\left(\|T\|^{v}\right)^{-n},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(X,Y)
. Let
v
be a real place of
F
. If there exists
x_{v}\in X_{v}
with

\displaystyle\frac{\|Tx_{v}\|_{v}}{\|x_{v}\|_{v}}<\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2n-1}\|T\|^{v}},

then
T
has nontrivial kernel.

Proof.

We assume that
T
is injective. For any
0\neq x\in X
, at the real place
v
, we have

\displaystyle\langle^{t}TTx,x\rangle_{X,v}=\langle Tx,Tx\rangle_{Y,v}\neq 0,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X,v}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Y,v}
are the inner products associated to
\|\cdot\|_{X,v}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y,v}
. This implies that
{}^{t}TT
is also injective, so
{}^{t}TT\in\operatorname{GL}(X)
. We have

\displaystyle\|^{t}TTx_{v}\|_{v}\leq\frac{\|Tx_{v}\|_{v}}{\|x_{v}\|_{v}}\|^{t}T\|_{v}\|x_{v}\|_{v}<\frac{\|T\|_{v}}{\|T\|^{2n-1}\|T\|^{v}}\|x_{v}\|_{v}=\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2n-2}(\|T\|^{v})^{2}}\|x_{v}\|_{v}.

Because
{}^{t}TT
is symmetric and
v
is real, if
\lambda_{v}
is the eigenvalue of
{}^{t}T_{v}T_{v}
with minimal absolute value, then

\displaystyle|\lambda_{v}|_{v}\leq\frac{\|^{t}TTx_{v}\|_{v}}{\|x_{v}\|_{v}}<\frac{1}{\|T\|^{2n-2}(\|T\|^{v})^{2}},

which contradicts the result from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
T\in\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(X,Y)
is nonzero and not injective, then
\ker T
can be spanned by vectors
x\in X
with
\|x\|\ll\|T\|^{n-1}
, where the implied constant depends only on
X
and
Y
.

Proof.

With respect to the bases we think of
T
as an
m
-by-
n
matrix. We may assume that the rows of
T
are linearly independent, because removing a row that is a linear combination of other rows does not change the kernel, and decreases
\|T\|
, and so
m\leq n-1
. We can construct an element in
\ker T
via Cramer’s rule as follows. We augment
T
to an
n
-by-
(n-1)
matrix
\widetilde{T}
, by adding in rows equal to arbitrary basis vectors. We then take a vector
v_{\widetilde{T}}
whose entries are the determinants of
(n-1)
-by-
(n-1)
minors of
\widetilde{T}
, times a suitable sign factor. The vectors
v_{\widetilde{T}}
span
\ker T
as we vary over all possibilities for
\widetilde{T}
. If the rows of
T
are
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
, then we have
\|v_{\widetilde{T}}\|\ll\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|v_{i}\|
and
\|v_{i}\|\leq\|T\|
, which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Bounding

We begin the proof with the following lemma. As we shall use the results in last section, applied to the action of
\mathbf{G}

Since the group

\displaystyle M^{+}(g,\delta,\mathfrak{n})=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbf{G}(F)\cap K(\mathfrak{n})\,|\,d(g^{-1}\gamma g,e)\leq 1,d(g^{-1}\gamma g,H_{0})\leq\delta\right\}

\displaystyle M^{-}(g,\delta,\mathfrak{n})=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbf{G}(F)\cap K(\mathfrak{n})\,|\,d(g^{-1}\gamma g,e)\leq 1,d(g^{-1}\gamma g,H^{\prime}\backslash H_{0})\leq\delta\right\}.

\displaystyle M^{\pm}(g,\delta,X)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{n}\text{ only divisible by primes in }\mathscr{P}\\
\operatorname{N}\mathfrak{n}<X\end{subarray}}M^{\pm}(g,\delta,\mathfrak{n}).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g\in\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})

(resp.

(resp.

Proof.

It suffices to consider

Recall that this representation is given by
\rho(g)A=gA\bar{g}^{-1}

and

\rho(hgh^{-1})-I=\rho(h)(\rho(g)-I)\rho(h)^{-1}

to its Jordan form by taking conjugate in

Suppose

The second case is that

&1\end{pmatrix}
, or

&-1\end{pmatrix}

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

There is a constant

Proof.

Let

for

We first show that for arbitrary three elements
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\in M^{+}(g,\delta,X)
,

\bigcap_{i=1}^{3}\ker(\rho(\gamma_{i})-I)\neq 0
. We consider the operator

given by

for suitably chosen

. For

, we have
\|\rho(\gamma_{i})-I\|_{v}\ll 1
because
\gamma_{i}
’s lie in a fixed bounded set at places in

. If

, an elementary computation gives

, and hence
\|\rho(\gamma_{i})-I\|_{v}\ll\operatorname{N}\mathfrak{n}_{i,v}
.
We choose

be such that

so that it generates the ideal class group of

. It follows that

, and
\|\alpha_{i}(\rho(\gamma_{i})-I)\|_{v_{0}}\ll\operatorname{N}\mathfrak{n}_{i}<X
. The same bounds therefore hold for

, so that

and

. The condition

\displaystyle\delta X\gg\frac{\|T\rho(g)\mathbf{v}_{1}\|_{v_{0}}}{\|\rho(g)\mathbf{v}_{1}\|_{v_{0}}}\geq\frac{1}{\|T\|^{7}\|T\|^{v_{0}}}\gg X^{-7}.

It follows that if we take

By Lemma

, if
\pm I\neq\gamma\in\mathbf{G}(F)
is such that

The proof for

∎

\displaystyle X_{A}=\sigma_{1}(k)\left(2\eta_{1}(k)e^{-\xi_{1}(k)}Y_{1}+e^{-\xi_{1}(k)}Y_{2}-(e^{\xi_{1}(k)}+\eta_{1}(k)^{2}e^{-\xi_{1}(k)})Y_{3}\right)\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\
0&-1/2\end{pmatrix},

so that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A\left(k\exp(tY)n(\eta_{1}(k))a(\xi_{1}(k))\right)\right|_{t=0}=\sigma_{1}(k)\left(2\eta_{1}(k)e^{-\xi_{1}(k)}Y_{1}+e^{-\xi_{1}(k)}Y_{2}-(e^{\xi_{1}(k)}+\eta_{1}(k)^{2}e^{-\xi_{1}(k)})Y_{3}\right).

We now prove that
\partial\psi/\partial t(k,\exp(tY))|_{t=0}
is Morse-Bott if
Y\neq 0
under the assumption that
k
lies in a compact set
B\subset\mathcal{P}_{0}
.
By Proposition
5.14
,
k\mapstochar\rightarrow(\eta_{1}(k),\xi_{1}(k))
from
\mathrm{U}(1)\backslash K_{0}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a local diffeomorphism.
Hence, the problem reduces to showing that

\displaystyle f(x,y)=2xe^{-y}Y_{1}+e^{-y}Y_{2}-(e^{y}+x^{2}e^{-y})Y_{3}\quad\quad(x,y\in(\eta_{1},\xi_{1})(B))

only has nondegenerate critical points if
Y\neq 0
. Suppose there is a critical point
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
of
f
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=0,

\displaystyle 2e^{-y^{\prime}}Y_{1}-2x^{\prime}e^{-y^{\prime}}Y_{3}

\displaystyle=-2x^{\prime}e^{-y^{\prime}}Y_{1}-e^{-y^{\prime}}Y_{2}-(e^{y^{\prime}}-x^{\prime 2}e^{-y^{\prime}})Y_{3}=0,

so
Y_{1}=x^{\prime}Y_{3}
and
Y_{2}=-(e^{2y^{\prime}}+x^{\prime 2})Y_{3}
. We may assume
Y_{3}\neq 0
because otherwise
Y_{3}=0
would imply both
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
0
and then
Y=0
. Moreover, for
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in(\eta_{1},\xi_{1})(B)
,

\displaystyle\|Y\|^{2}=Y_{1}^{2}+Y_{2}^{2}+Y_{3}^{2}=\left(1+x^{\prime 2}+(e^{2y^{\prime}}+x^{\prime 2})^{2}\right)Y_{3}^{2}\sim_{B}Y_{3}^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}{\partial^{2}f}/{\partial x^{2}}&{\partial^{2}f}/{\partial x\partial y}\\
{\partial^{2}f}/{\partial x\partial y}&{\partial^{2}f}/{\partial y^{2}}\end{pmatrix}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=4Y_{3}^{2}\sim\|Y\|^{2}.

∎

5.5.
Bounds for
J(s,g)

We shall use the method of stationary phase to estimate the oscillatory integral.
The following is a known generalization of
the stationary phase approximation to the case of Morse–Bott functions, see e.g.
[
8
]
. Let
\alpha,F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\alpha
of compact support.

Proposition 5.17
.

Suppose that
F
is Morse-Bott and that the set of critical points of
F
contained in the support of
\alpha
form a connected submanifold
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the oscillatory integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\alpha(x)e^{itF(x)}dx

is asymptotic as
t\rightarrow\infty
to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\pi}{t}\right)^{(d-e)/2}e^{itF(W)-\frac{i\pi}{4}\sigma}\int_{W}\alpha(x)\left|\operatorname{det}_{W}F^{\prime\prime}(x)\right|^{-1/2}dx+O(t^{-(d-e)/2-1})

where
e=\dim W
,
F(W)
is the value of
F(x)
at any point
x\in W
, and
\sigma
(resp.
\det_{W}F^{\prime\prime}
) is the signature (resp. determinant) of the Hessian of
F
in the directions transverse to
W
.

We let
\psi
be as in (
5.20
)

Lemma 5.18
.

Given a compact set
B\subset\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{S}
,
there is an open neighborhood
0\in U\subset\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})^{\perp}
, such that for all
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{S})
with
\supp(b)\subset B
,
Y\in U
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K_{0}}b(k,\exp(Y))\exp\left({is{\psi}(k,\exp(Y))}\right)dk\ll(1+s\|Y\|)^{-1},

where the implied constant depends on
B
,
L^{\infty}
-norms of
b
and finitely many of its derivatives.

Proof.

Define the polar coordinate
X:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})^{\perp}
by

\displaystyle X(r,\gamma,\delta)=\begin{pmatrix}ir\cos(\gamma)&ir\sin(\gamma)\cos(\delta)\\
ir\sin(\gamma)\sin(\delta)&-ir\cos(\gamma)\end{pmatrix}=r\begin{pmatrix}i\cos(\gamma)&i\sin(\gamma)\cos(\delta)\\
i\sin(\gamma)\sin(\delta)&-i\cos(\gamma)\end{pmatrix}.

We define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}=K_{0}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}=\left\{(k,r,\gamma,\delta)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}:(k,\exp(X(r,\gamma,\delta)))\in\mathcal{S}\right\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}=\left\{(k,r,\gamma,\delta)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}:(k,\exp(X(r,\gamma,\delta)))\in B\right\}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}.

The set
\widetilde{B}
is still compact.
We define
\widetilde{b}(k,r,\gamma,\delta)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{S}})
and
\widetilde{\psi}(k,r,\gamma,\delta)\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{S}})
to be the pullbacks of
b
and
\psi
under
X
, and then
\supp(b)\subset\widetilde{B}
. We know that
\psi(k,g)
vanishes when
g\in H^{\prime}
so
\widetilde{\psi}
vanishes when
r=0
, and as
\widetilde{\psi}
is smooth (in fact, real analytic) we have that
\widetilde{\psi}/r
extends to a smooth function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
. Proposition
5.16
implies that
\widetilde{\psi}/r
is a Morse-Bott function with respect to the variable
k
as
k
lies in a compact set in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
when
r=0
, and so there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that it is also Morse-Bott on the set
\widetilde{B}\cap(K_{0}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
. If we define
U=X((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
, the result now follows from the stationary phase (Proposition
5.17
).
∎

Proposition 5.19
.

Given a smooth compactly supported function
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{S})
and
g_{0}\in H^{\prime}

g_{0}\in U\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\int_{K_{0}}b(k,g)\exp\left({is{\psi}(k,g)}\right)dk\ll\left(1+s\,d(g,H^{\prime})\right)^{-1}.

The implied constant depends only on

, the support of
b
, and
L^{\infty}
-norms of
b
and finitely many of its derivatives.

Proof.

Let

be an open bounded neighborhood of

be a compact set so that the supports of

for all

are contained in

We apply Lemma

to the set

and let

Corollary 5.20
.

Given a smooth compactly supported function

\displaystyle\int_{K_{0}}b(k,g)\exp\left({is{\psi}(k,g)}\right)dk\ll\left(1+s\,d(g,H^{\prime})\right)^{-1}.

b
, and
L^{\infty}
-norms of
b
and finitely many of its derivatives.

Proof.

The subgroup

is closed in

Lemma 5.21
.

Suppose

\displaystyle\int_{K_{0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}c(x_{1},t_{1},x_{2},t_{2},k,g)\exp\left(is{\phi}(x_{1},t_{1},x_{2},t_{2},k,g)\right)dx_{1}dt_{1}dx_{2}dt_{2}\right)dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-2}\int_{K_{0}}c_{1}(k,g)\exp\left(is{\psi}(k,g)\right)dk+O(s^{-3}),

L^{\infty}

Since
(k,g)\in\mathcal{P}\backslash\mathcal{S}

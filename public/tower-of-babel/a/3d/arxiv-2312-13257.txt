Error estimation and adaptive tuning for unregularized robust M-estimator
Pierre C. Bellec pierre.bellec@rutgers.edu, Takuya Koriyama tkoriyam@uchicago.edu

Abstract
We consider unregularized robust M-estimators for linear models under Gaussian design and heavy-tailed noise, in the proportional asymptotics regime where the sample size n n and the number of features p p are both increasing such that p / n → γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p/n\to\gamma\in(0,1) . An estimator of the out-of-sample error of a robust M-estimator is analyzed and proved to be consistent for a large family of loss functions that includes the Huber loss. As an application of this result, we propose an adaptive tuning procedure of the scale parameter λ > 0 \lambda>0 of a given loss function ρ \rho : choosing λ ^ \hat{\lambda} in a given interval I I that minimizes the out-of-sample error estimate of the M-estimator constructed with loss ρ λ ( ⋅ ) = λ 2 ρ ( ⋅ / λ ) \rho_{\lambda}(\cdot)=\lambda^{2}\rho(\cdot/\lambda) leads to the optimal out-of-sample error over I I . The proof relies on a smoothing argument: the unregularized M-estimation objective function is perturbed, or smoothed, with a Ridge penalty that vanishes as n → + ∞ n\to+\infty , and shows that the unregularized M-estimator of interest inherits properties of its smoothed version.

Introduction

Robust statistics originated with the foundational work of
Huber 1964
, which introduced methods for estimating a location parameter under heavy-tailed noise assumptions. Over time, it has evolved into a crucial area of study, providing tools to address the challenges posed by outliers and non-standard error distributions. The practical applications of robust statistics span diverse fields, including financial modeling (
Lambert-Lacroix and Zwald 2011
) and genomic data analysis (
Sun et al. 2020
), underscoring its broad utility across scientific and applied domains. For a comprehensive overview of the theoretical foundations and their practical implementations in data analysis, see
Loh 2024
,
Maronna et al. 2019
, and references therein.

In this paper, we consider the linear model

\bm{y}=\bm{X}\bm{\beta}^{\star}+\bm{\epsilon}

where the design matrix
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
has i.i.d rows
\bm{x}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{p},\bm{\Sigma})
and the noise
\bm{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
has an i.i.d. marginal
F_{\epsilon}
.
Our assumption (
2
below) allows

F_{\epsilon}
to be heavy-tailed, including distributions
with no finite moments.
We focus on the high-dimensional regime where the sample size
n
and the dimension
p
are both increasing such that
p/n\to\gamma\in(0,1)
.
In this setting, we consider the unregularized robust M-estimator

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{y},\bm{X})\in\argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(y_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\beta}),

(1)

where
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex and differentiable loss such that its derivative
\psi=\rho^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded and Lipschitz. A well-studied example is the Huber loss, which is defined by

\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\quad\rho(x)=\int_{0}^{|x|}\min(1,u)\mathrm{d}u=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{2}/2&|x|\leq 1\\
|x|-1/2&|x|\geq 1\end{array}\right..

(2)

Our goal is to select a robust loss achieving a small out-of-sample error in a data-driven manner, i.e., by only looking at the observation
(\bm{y},\bm{X})
.
Here, the out-of-sample error refers to the random quantity

\displaystyle R:=\bigl\|\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}(\bm{y},\bm{X})-\bm{\beta}^{\star})\bigr\|_{2}^{2}=\E\Bigl[\bigl\{\bm{x}_{0}^{\top}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}-\bm{\beta}^{\star})\bigr\}^{2}|\bm{X},\bm{y}\Bigr],

(3)

where
\bm{x}_{0}
is independent of
(\bm{y},\bm{X})
and has the same law as any row
\bm{x}_{i}
of
\bm{X}
.
In this paper, we occasionally refer to the random quantity (
3
) simply as the
Risk
.
Since the calculation of the out-of-sample error requires the unobservable quantities
(\bm{\beta}^{\star},\bm{\Sigma})
,
we need an observable proxy to the out-of-sample error for such data-driven selection of loss.

Classical statistics for M-estimation, for instance
confidence intervals for the coefficients of
\bm{\beta}^{\star}
,
require that

p/n\to 0
, that is, the dimension is negligible compared to
the sample size.
However, the last decade has identified ample evidence that
such classical asymptotic results fail in moderate dimension
where dimension and sample size are of the same order

(
Donoho and Montanari 2016
;
El Karoui et al. 2013
)
. Beyond linear models, this
phenomenon is also
clearly exposed in logistic regression
Sur and Candès 2019

with clear departure from classical results
as soon as
n\geq 10p
is violated.
From the point of view of classical statistics,
a peculiar property of the proportional asymptotic regime
where
\lim p/n
is a positive constant is that

consistency fails
, and that the error

\|\hat{\bm{\beta}}-\bm{\beta}^{\star}\|_{2}^{2}
does not converge to 0 in probability.

Our contribution is to propose a consistent estimator of the out-of-sample error for unregularized robust M-estimators. For
\psi=\rho^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\psi(\bm{r})=(\psi(r_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

1.2
Related work

Under regularity conditions on the loss
\rho
and noise distribution
F_{\epsilon}
, the out-of-sample error of the unregularized M-estimator (
1
) is known

El Karoui et al. 2013
;
El Karoui 2018
;
El Karoui 2013
;
Donoho and Montanari 2016
;
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018

to converge in probability to a deterministic value
\alpha^{2}
, which is a solution to the following nonlinear system of equations
with positive unknowns
(\alpha,\kappa)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha^{2}\gamma&=\E[((\alpha Z+W)-\text{prox}[\kappa\rho](\alpha Z+W))^{2}]\\
\alpha\gamma&=\E[((\alpha Z+W)-\text{prox}[\kappa\rho](\alpha Z+W))\cdot Z]\end{split}

(7)

where
Z\sim\N(0,1)
,
W\sim F_{\epsilon}
, and

\text{prox}[f](x):=\argmin_{u\in\mathbb{R}}(x-u)^{2}/2+f(u)
.
The convergence in probability of the out-of-sample error of
\hat{\bm{\beta}}
to
\alpha^{2}
is granted by

Donoho and Montanari 2016
;
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018

provided that (
7
) admits a unique solution.
The existence of solutions to (
7
) was established
by
Donoho and Montanari 2016
for strongly convex losses, and in our
recent paper
Bellec and Koriyama 2023
for general Lipschitz losses
on the side of the phase transition where exact recovery (i.e.,
\hat{\bm{\beta}}=\bm{\beta}^{*}
) is not possible. On the other hand,
uniqueness is addressed in

Thrampoulidis et al. 2018
by strict convexity arguments and
in
Bellec and Koriyama 2023
.
The working assumptions of the present paper stated
in
Section

2
ensure that a solution
(\alpha,\kappa)
to (
7
)
exists and is unique, so that the convergence in probability

\|\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}-\bm{\beta}^{*})\|\to^{p}\alpha

as
n,p\to+\infty
with
p/n\to\gamma<1

holds by the
main result of
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018
.

Bean et al. 2013

studied the construction of the optimal loss
that minimizes the solution
\alpha
of (
7
) when
F_{\epsilon}
is log-concave and known. However, constructing the optimal loss in this way requires the knowledge of

F_{\epsilon}
to minimize the corresponding
\alpha
in
(
7
). Since
F_{\epsilon}
is typically unknown,
this construction cannot be implemented in practice.

In this paper, we focus on the underparametrized regime
p/n\to\gamma
with
\gamma\in(0,1)
. In the overparametrized regime with
\gamma>1
,
the out-of-sample error of the unregularized M-estimator (
1
)
explodes
(cf.
(
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018
, Remark 5.1.1)
).
This curse of dimensionality
can be overcome by using a penalty function
g
and computing the corresponding
penalized M-estimator

\hat{\bm{\beta}}\in\argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(y_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\beta})+g(\bm{\beta})
, for which the out-of-sample error will be finite
under suitable assumptions on the penalty
g

Bayati and Montanari 2011
;
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018
;
Loureiro et al. 2021
.
If
\gamma<1
, however, some advantages of the unregularized M-estimator(
1
) include its invariance with respect to
(\bm{\Sigma},\bm{\beta}^{\star})

(
El Karoui et al. 2013
, Lemma 1)
and
that confidence intervals for components
\beta_{j}^{\star}
of
\bm{\beta}^{\star}

using the asymptotic normality of
\hat{\beta}_{j}

do not require knowledge of
\bm{\Sigma}
;
on the other hand,
the de-debiasing correction
\bm{e}_{j}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{X}^{\top}\psi(\bm{y}-\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}})
necessary for asymptotic normality
of a regularized M-estimator with penalty

as above
does require knowledge (or some estimate) of
\bm{\Sigma}

(
Bellec et al. 2022
)
.

Most similar works to (
5
) are
Bellec 2023
;
Bellec and Shen 2022
;
Bellec 2025
; they show that for general pairs of loss
\rho
and penalty
g
,
the out-of-sample error of the penalized M-estimator
\hat{\bm{\beta}}\in\argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(y_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\beta})+g(\bm{\beta})

enjoys the approximation

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{\bm{\beta}}-\bm{\beta}^{\star})\|_{2}^{2}\approx\ \tr[\bm{V}]^{-2}({\|\hat{\bm{\psi}}\|_{2}^{2}(2\hat{\text{df}}-p)+\|\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{X}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\psi}}\|_{2}^{2}}),

(8)

where
\hat{\bm{\psi}}:=\psi(\bm{y}-\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\hat{\text{df}}:=\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{y})(\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}})]
, and
\bm{V}
as in (
4
).
Furthermore, these derivatives have closed forms for a certain choice of
(\rho,g)
; for instance when
\rho
is the Huber loss and

penalty, we have

Bellec 2023
, Propositions 2.2 and 2.3)
.

With the KKT conditions of (
1

\bm{X}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\psi}}=\bm{0}_{p}
,
we may regard (
4
) as a special case of (
8
) by setting
\hat{\text{df}}=p
.
However, the proof techniques in the aforementioned papers

Bellec 2023
;
Bellec and Shen 2022
;
Bellec 2025

rely either on the strong convexity of the penalty
g
(excluding the

or on a sufficiently sparse structure in
\bm{\beta}^{\star}
in which
case the L1 penalty also allows for approximations of the form (
8
)
Celentano et al. 2023
;
Bellec 2023
.

For the unregularized case (
1
) studied here,
the results from the papers

El Karoui et al. 2013
;
El Karoui 2018
;
El Karoui 2013
;
Donoho and Montanari 2016
;
Thrampoulidis et al. 2018

5
),

El Karoui et al. 2013
;
El Karoui 2018
;

;

rely on strong convexity on either the loss or the penalty,
but more importantly because these results do not study the trace of the

of the nonlinear system (

, and reversing the argument

in (

. However,
characterizing a limit in probability for the quantity

.
Recently,
Bellec 2025
proposed an alternative regularization

(
1

\rho

1.3
Precise analysis of a perturbed M-estimator

To overcome these difficulties and prove (
5
)
for the Huber loss and its variants, we use the “
Ridge-smoothing
” technique, which is used in previous works

El Karoui 2013
;
Celentano and Montanari 2022
;
Loureiro et al. 2022
.
By rotational and translation invariance, assume without loss of generality that
\bm{\beta}^{\star}=\bm{0}_{p}
and
\bm{\Sigma}=\bm{I}_{p}
.
Then, (
5
) is reduced to

\|\hat{\bm{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2}=p\|\bm{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot(\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}])^{-2}{+o_{P}(1)}

(9)

where
\bm{\psi}:\bm{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a vector field and
(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}
is the Jacobian matrix. To show (
9
), we instead consider the ridge-regularized M-estimator
\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}
with a diminishing regularization parameter
n^{-c}
for some constant
c>0

\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}=\argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(\epsilon_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\beta})+\frac{n^{-c}}{2}\|{\bm{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2},

(10)

and define the vector field
\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}:\bm{\epsilon}\mapsto\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}})
. We prove the target (
9
) by the following two steps:

(I)

Prove (
9
) for the regularized
\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}
, i.e.,

\|\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}\|_{2}^{2}\approx p\|\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot(\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}])^{-2}
.

(II)

Prove that each quantity in (
9
)
is approximately the same for

\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}
and for
\hat{\bm{\beta}}
,
as the Ridge penalty coefficient in (
10
)
converges to 0 polynomially in
n
:

\|\hat{\bm{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}}\|_{2}^{2}\approx\|\hat{\bm{\beta}}\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\tfrac{1}{n}\|\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}\|_{2}^{2}\approx\tfrac{1}{n}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\tfrac{1}{n}\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}]\approx\tfrac{1}{n}\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}].

We prove
(I)
by a chi-square type moment inequality given in
(
Bellec 2023
, Section 7)
. The more subtle and novel part of the proof is to derive
the three approximations in
(II)
, in particular the challenging approximation

\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}]\approx\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}].

(11)

Indeed, the closeness of the two vector fields
(\bm{\psi},\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}})
in the Euclidean norm does not necessarily imply the closeness of their divergence.
To show
(
11
), we leverage the assumption that the noise
distribution is sufficiently smooth: concretely,
2
below grants

that the noise distribution is a convolution of two probability distributions
(F,\tilde{F})
, that is,

\bm{\epsilon}=\bm{z}+\bm{\delta},\qquad\bm{z}\perp\!\!\!\perp\bm{\delta},\qquad(z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim F,\qquad(\delta_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim\tilde{F},

(12)

where
F
has density
z\mapsto\exp(-\phi(z))
such that
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously differentiable with bounded second derivative
(on the other hand,
\tilde{F}
is unrestricted and may have arbitrarily
fat tails).
Then, using a variant of
the second order Stein’s formula
(
Bellec and Zhang 2021
, Section 2.4)
extended to the distribution with density
z\mapsto\exp(-\phi(z))
(see
Theorem

13
below), we will argue in
Lemma

19
below
that

\E[(\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{z})(\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}-\bm{\psi})]-\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}-\bm{\psi}))^{2}]
is relatively small. As a consequence, using the chain rule for the Jacobian, we have

\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}]-\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}]=\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{z})(\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}-\bm{\psi})]\approx\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}-\bm{\psi}).

(13)

Roughly speaking,

2
below grants that the noise distribution is sufficiently
smooth, and thanks to this assumption,

closeness of the vector fields in the form
\|\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}\|^{2}/n=o_{P}(1)
carries over to the two divergences, and implies

\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}]/n=\tr[(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\bm{\psi}_{\text{ridge}}]/n+o_{P}(1).

(14)

It is not clear at this point if
(
14
) holds without this smoothness
assumption on the noise distribution.

Adaptive tuning of the scale parameter
\lambda
was investigated before in several papers, including
Loh 2021
;
Wang et al. 2021
and references therein.
These works do not assume a proportional asymptotics regime
and do not aim for exact multiplicative constants: the resulting risk is only guaranteed to be less than
C\times(\text{optimal risk})
for a constant
C>1
.

On the other hand, our goal in the present paper is to achieve the optimal
risk with multiplicative constant 1.

5.1
Differentiability of M-estimators

In this section, we introduce a useful differentiable structure of

To begin with, we discuss the differentiability of

\bm{\epsilon}

Proposition 0

(Proposition 4.1 in
Bellec 2023
)
.

Suppose that
\rho
is convex and differentiable and that
\psi=\rho^{\prime}
is
1
-Lipschitz. Then, for any
M
-estimator of the form

\hat{\bm{\beta}}\in\argmin_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(\bm{y}_{i}-\bm{x}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\beta})+g(\bm{\beta})

is a convex penalty, the map
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\psi(\bm{y}-\bm{X}\hat{\bm{\beta}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
1
-Lipschitz for almost every
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
.

Since
\psi=\rho^{\prime}
is 1-Lipschitz,

Proposition

9
implies that the map
\bm{\epsilon}\mapsto\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G}))
is
1
-Lipschitz for all
\mu\geq 0
. As a consequence, Rademacher’s theorem gives the following:

\displaystyle\forall\mu\geq 0,\quad\bm{V}_{\mu}:=(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\text{ exists almost everywhere and }\|\bm{V}_{\mu}\|_{op}\leq 1.

(33)

We emphasize that (
33

\mu=0
, so
the original Jacobian matrix
\bm{V}=(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}})
is also well-defined and
\|\bm{V}\|_{op}\leq 1
.

The next theorem introduces a detailed differentiable structure of
\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}

with respect to
\bm{G}
when
\mu
is strictly positive.

Theorem 10

(Theorem 1 in
Bellec and Shen 2022
)
.

Suppose
\mu>0
. Then,
for almost every
(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})
, the map
(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})\mapsto\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})
defined by (
32

(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})
, and
the derivatives are given by

\displaystyle\forall i\in[n],\ \frac{\partial\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}}{\partial\epsilon_{i}}(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}\bm{G}^{\top}\bm{e}_{i}\psi^{\prime}(r_{i}),

\displaystyle\forall i\in[n],\forall j\in[p],\ \frac{\partial\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}}{\partial g_{ij}}(\bm{\epsilon},\bm{G})

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}\bm{e}_{j}\psi(r_{i})-\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}\bm{G}^{\top}\bm{e}_{i}\psi^{\prime}(r_{i})\bm{e}_{j}^{\top}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu},

where
\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}=(\bm{G}^{\top}\diag\{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{r})\}\bm{G}+n\mu I_{p})^{-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}

and
\bm{r}=\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

See
Bellec and Shen 2022
for the differentiability of regularized M-estimators for general strongly convex penalties.
Thanks to the strict positiveness of

is always well-defined and its operator norm is bounded from above by

By

10
and the chain rule, we find that

when

,
with the derivatives given by

\displaystyle(\partial/\partial g_{ij})\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=-\diag\{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{r})\}\bm{G}\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}\bm{e}_{j}\psi(r_{i})-\bm{V}_{\mu}\bm{e}_{i}\bm{e}_{j}^{\top}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{\mu}=(\partial/\partial\bm{\epsilon})\psi(\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{G}\hat{\bm{h}}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\diag\{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{r})\}-\diag\{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{r})\}\bm{G}\hat{\bm{A}}_{\mu}\bm{G}^{\top}\diag\{\psi^{\prime}(\bm{r})\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

Lemma 0

Theorem 12

\displaystyle\E\Bigl[\Big|p\|\bm{f}\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\bm{f}^{\top}\bm{G}\bm{e}_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial g_{ij}})^{2}\Big|\Bigr]\leq C\Bigl[\sqrt{p}\E[(1+\sum_{ij}\|\frac{\partial\bm{f}}{\partial g_{ij}}\|^{2})^{1/2}]+\E[\sum_{ij}\|\frac{\partial\bm{f}}{\partial g_{ij}}\|^{2}]\Bigr]

(35)

Above, we have used

11

\bm{\epsilon}=\bm{z}+\bm{\delta},\quad\bm{z}\perp\!\!\!\perp\bm{\delta},\quad(z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\iid F,\quad(\delta_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\iid\tilde{F},

where
F
has the density
z\mapsto\exp(-\phi(z))
.
By the chain rule,
\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{\mu}
can be written as

\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{\epsilon}}(\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu})=\frac{\partial\bm{z}}{\partial\bm{\epsilon}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{z}}(\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu})=\bm{I}_{n}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{z}}(\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu})=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{z}}(\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu}).

(39)

Here, by the second order Stein’s formula extended to the density of the form
\exp(-\phi(z))
, we will show that
\tr[\bm{V}]-\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]
concentrates around
\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu})
.

Theorem 13

(Equation (2.15) in
Bellec and Zhang 2021
)
.

Assume that the random vector
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
has density
\bm{z}\mapsto\exp(-\phi(\bm{z}))
where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously differentiable function with Hessian
H(\bm{z})=(\partial/\partial\bm{z})^{2}\phi(\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
Then, for any differentiable function
\bm{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\E\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{\partial\phi(\bm{z})}{\partial\bm{z}}\bm{f}(\bm{z})-\tr[\frac{\partial\bm{f}(\bm{z})}{\partial\bm{z}}]\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]=\E\Bigl[\bm{f}(\bm{z})^{\top}\bm{H}(\bm{z})\bm{f}(\bm{z})+\tr[(\frac{\partial\bm{f}(\bm{z})}{\partial\bm{z}})^{2}]\Bigr],

provided that the expectation on the right-hand side exists.

Applying
Theorem

13
with
\phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi(z_{i})
and
\bm{f}=\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu}
, using the identity (
39
) and
\bm{H}(\bm{z})=\text{diag}\{\phi^{\prime\prime}(z_{i}):i\in[n]\}
, we have

\E[(\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\mu}-\bm{\psi})-\tr[\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{\mu}])^{2}]=\E\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi^{\prime\prime}(z_{i})(\psi_{i}-(\psi_{\mu})_{i})^{2}+\tr[(\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{\mu})^{2}]\Bigr],

and we can easily show that the right-hand side is
O(n)
. Hence, by the Markov inequality, we have

\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]-\tr[\bm{V}]=\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\mu}-\bm{\psi})+O_{P}(n^{1/2}).

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we bound
|\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\mu}-\bm{\psi})|
from above as

|\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})^{\top}(\bm{\psi}_{\mu}-\bm{\psi})|\leq\|\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})\|\|\bm{\psi}_{\mu}-\bm{\psi}\|\overset{(*)}{=}O_{P}(n^{1/2})\cdot O_{P}(n^{\frac{1-c}{2}})=O_{P}(n^{1-\frac{c}{2}}),

where
(*)
follows from
\|\phi^{\prime}(\bm{z})\|=O_{P}(n^{1/2})
(see
Lemma

22
) and

\|\bm{\psi}-\bm{\psi}_{\mu}\|=O_{P}(n^{1/2-c})

by (
37
).
As a consequence, we obtain

\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]-\tr[\bm{V}]=O_{P}(n^{1-\frac{c}{2}})+O_{P}(n^{1/2})=O_{P}(n^{1-\frac{c}{2}})

thanks to
c\leq 1
.
Finally,
\|\bm{V}\|_{op}
and
\|\bm{V}_{\mu}\|_{op}\leq 1
lead to

|\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]^{2}-\tr[\bm{V}]^{2}|\leq|\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]+\tr[\bm{V}]||\tr[\bm{V}_{\mu}]-\tr[\bm{V}]|\leq 2nO_{P}(n^{1-\frac{c}{2}})=O_{P}(n^{2-\frac{c}{2}}),

which is exactly (
38
).

\displaystyle\forall\epsilon>0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\PP(|\alpha^{2}(\hat{\lambda}_{N_{n}})-\alpha^{2}(\lambda_{\opt})|>\epsilon)+\PP(|R(\hat{\lambda}_{N_{n}})-\alpha^{2}(\lambda_{\opt})|>\epsilon)=0.

(53)

By
Lemma

27
with
\delta=1/k
, there exists a function
N:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\quad\underbrace{\PP(|\alpha^{2}(\hat{\lambda}_{{N}(k)})-\alpha^{2}(\lambda_{\opt})|\geq 1/k)+\PP(|R(\hat{\lambda}_{{N}(k)})-\alpha^{2}(\lambda_{\opt})|\geq 1/k)}_{:=p_{n,k}}=0.

(54)

Thus, if we can find some function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(n)\to\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n,\varphi(n)}=0
, the array
\{N_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:=\{N\circ\varphi(n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies (
53
), thereby completing the proof.
Below we construct such
n\mapsto\varphi(n)
.

By (
54
), there exists an array of integers
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\forall n\geq n_{k},\quad|p_{n,k}|\leq 1/k.

Here, we assume without of loss of generality that
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
is strictly increasing; otherwise redefine recursively as
\tilde{n}_{k}:=\max(\tilde{n}_{k-1},n_{k})+1
. For this
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, define the step function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
as
\varphi(n):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\bm{1}\{n_{k}\leq n<n_{k+1}\}
.
Then, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(n)=+\infty
, and

|p_{n,k}|\leq 1/k
holds for
k=\varphi(n)
since
n\geq n_{k}
by construction. Therefore, we obtain

\forall n\geq n_{1},\quad|p_{n,\varphi(n)}|\leq 1/\varphi(n).

Since
\varphi(n)\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n,\varphi(n)}=0
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Computing mixed Schatten norm of completely positive maps
Mohammad ShahverdiKondori Siu On Chan

Abstract




Abstract

Computing
p\rightarrow q
norm for matrices is a classical problem in computational mathematics and power iteration is a well known method for computing
p\rightarrow q
norm for a matrix with nonnegative entries. Here we define an equivalent iteration method for computing
S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}
norm for completely positive maps where
S_{p}
is the Schatten
p
norm. We generalize almost all of definitions, properties, lemmas etc. in the matrix setting to completely positive maps and prove an important theorem in this setting.

Introduction

Given a matix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
we define
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}
as

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}=\max\frac{\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert_{q}}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}}

where the
\left\lVert.\right\rVert_{p}
and
\left\lVert.\right\rVert_{q}
are
\ell^{p}
norms. Computing
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}
is a classical problem in computational mathematics.The best known method for this is a nonlinear power method, introduced by Boyd in
[
1
]
and then further analyzed and extended for instance in
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. However the best known result about this problem is the following

Theorem 1.1

(Theorems 3.2 and 3.3,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a matrix with nonnegative entries
and suppose that
A^{T}A
has at least one positive entry per row. If
1<q\leq p<\infty
, then, every
positive critical point of
{f_{A}(x)={\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert_{q}\big/\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}}}
is a global maximizer. Moreover, if either
p>q
or
A^{T}A
is irreducible,
then
f_{A}
has a unique positive critical point
x^{+}
and the power sequence

x_{0}=\frac{x_{0}}{\hskip 7.22743pt\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert_{p}},\quad x_{k+1}=J_{p^{*}}(A^{T}J_{q}(Ax_{k})),\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots

converges to
x^{+}
for every positive starting point.

We can consider completely positive linear maps between complex matrix spaces (see
[
3
]
) as a generalization for positive matrices so it’s interesting to investigate this results for completely positive maps.That means in this note we are trying to find
S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}
norm of a completely positive map

that defines as follows

\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}}=\max\frac{\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{q}}}{\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}

where the max function is on the set of all
n\times n
Hermitian matrices and
S_{p}
and
S_{q}
are Schatten norms.

We generalize the power method that is defined for positive matrices, to completely positive maps and prove this method converges to the value of
S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}
norm.

H^{n}_{+}

In this section we talk about positive semidefinite cone and completely positive maps properties. Le
H^{n}
be the set of all Hermitian
n\times n
matrices and
H^{n}_{+}
(resp.
H^{n}_{++}
) be the set of positive semidefinite (resp. positive definite) Hermitian
n\times n

Definition 2.1
.

(completely positive map)
Let
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be a function on the set of complex matrices then
\Phi
is a completely positive map

if and only if there exist matrices
V_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
such that

\Phi(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{V_{i}AV_{i}^{\dagger}},\quad k\leq nm

Also represent the transpose of
\Phi
by
\Phi^{*}
as

\Phi^{*}:\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},\quad\Phi^{*}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{V_{i}^{\dagger}AV_{i}}

note.
It’s not the original definition but these are equivalent (see
[
3
]
)

For a completely positive map we define
S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}
norm as

\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}}=\max\frac{\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{q}}}{\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}

where
S_{p}
is the Schatten
p
-norm and defines as

\begin{split}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}&=tr(|A|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma_{i}(A)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

where
|A|=\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
and
\sigma_{i}
-s are singular values of A.

Definition 2.2
.

(proper cone)
A cone K in a vector space on field
\mathbb{R}
is called proper cone if it satisfies these conditions:

•

K is convex.

•

K is closed.

•

K is solid, which means it has nonempty interior.

•

K is pointed, which means that it contains no line (or equally
x\in K,-x\in K\Rightarrow x=0)

By
[
8
]
we know that

is a proper cone in space of complex matrices so we can define a partial ordering on matrices with respect to

(for instance see
[
9
]
,chapter2). So we use the notation
A\succeq 0
for saying
A
is positive semidefinite and
A\succ 0
means
A
is positive definite also

means
A-B
is positive semidefinite or equally
A-B\in H^{n}_{+}
. Also for vectors

and

means

(respectively

).

Definition 2.3
.

For a positive semidefinite matrix
A
let

Lemma 2.1
.

For

and completely positive map

\Phi

1.

If

s eigenvalues respectively then we have
\forall i:\lambda_{i}\geq\gamma_{i}
.

2.

If

then
\Phi(A)\succeq\Phi(B)
.

Proof.

For first part consider
\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}
and
\{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n}\}
are set of eigenvectors of
A,B
respectively such that
Av_{i}=\lambda_{i}v_{i},Bu_{i}=\gamma_{i}u_{i}
so these are orthonormal basis for space of complex vectors. Now for proving
\lambda_{i}\geq\gamma_{i}
consider
P=\{c_{i}v_{i}+c_{i+1}v_{i+1}+\cdots+c_{n}v_{n}:\forall j\hskip 5.05942ptc_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
Q=\{d_{1}u_{1}+d2_{u}2+\cdots+d_{i}u_{i}:\forall j\hskip 5.05942ptd_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
T=P\hskip 1.4457pt\cap\hskip 1.4457ptQ
the intersection of spanned spaces by last
n-i+1
eigenvectors of A and first i eigenvectors of B. Note that
dim(P)=n-i+1
and
dim(Q)=i
so
dim(T)>0
and it means there exist
x\neq 0,\left\lVert x\right\rVert=1
in T, for this x it’s easy to see
x^{\dagger}Ax\leq\lambda_{i}
and
x^{\dagger}Bx\geq\gamma_{i}
but
A\succeq B
implies
x^{\dagger}Ax\geq x^{\dagger}Bx
so
\lambda_{i}\geq\gamma_{i}
.

For the second part note that
\Phi
is linear.

Hilbert projective metric is a metric that is defined on rays in a real Banach space and is introduced by Hilbert in
[
4
]
and first time it defined on positive semidefinite cone by C. Liverani and M. P. Wojtkowski in
[
10
]
. Consider K is a proper cone, for
x,y\in K
we use notation
x\sim y
if there exist positive real numbers c and C such that
cy\preceq_{K}x\preceq_{K}Cy
and that means
x-cy,Cy-x\in K
. It’s easy to see
\sim
is an equivalency relation and the equivalency classes are called parts of K. Also the function
M:K\times K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as
M(x/y)=\inf{\left\{\lambda>0:x\preceq_{K}\lambda y\right\}}
then the Hilbert projective metric
d:K\times K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defines as follows

d(x,y)=\begin{cases}\ln M(x/y)M(y/x)&x\sim y\\
0&x=y=0\\
\infty&otherwise\end{cases}

It’s easy to see that for
K=H^{n}_{+}
if
A,B\succ 0
then

d(A,B)=\ln\left(\left\lVert{B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right\rVert\left\lVert{A^{-\frac{1}{2}}BA^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right\rVert\right)

where
\left\lVert A\right\rVert
equals greatest eigenvalue of A. The Hilbert projective metric is a metric on rays that means
d(A,B)=d(\alpha A,\beta B)
for every positive
\alpha,\beta
you can find more properties about Hilbert metric in
[
4
]
.

The proof of our main theorem is based on the Banach contraction principle. Thus, for a map
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
we consider the Birkhoff contraction ratio
\kappa(\Phi)\in[0,\infty]
of
\Phi
, defined as the
smallest Lipschitz constant of
\Phi
with respect to
d
(see
[
11
]
):

\kappa(\Phi)=\inf\left\{C>0:d(\Phi(A),\Phi(B))\leq Cd(A,B),\quad\forall A,B\in H^{n}_{+}\text{ such that }A\sim B\right\}

where if there exist
A,B\in H^{n}_{+}
such that
A\sim B
and
\Phi(A)\nsim\Phi(B)
then
\kappa(\Phi)=\infty
but we know that this case never happend for completely positive maps because for linear maps we have
\kappa(\Phi)\leq 1
. Moreover one can easily show that if
\Phi
is a completely positive map

then
A\sim B
implies
\Phi(A)\sim\Phi(B)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Birkhoff-Hopf,
[
12
]
)
Let
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be a completely positive map

then we have:

\kappa(\Phi)=\tanh\left(\frac{\Delta(\Phi)}{4}\right)

where
\Delta
is the diameter of
\Phi
and defines as follows

\Delta(\Phi)=\sup\{d(\Phi(A),\Phi(B)):A,B\in H^{n}_{+},A\sim B\}

and with the convention of
\tanh(\infty)=1
.

So the Bikhpff-Hopf theorem tells us the contraction ratio of a completely positive map is always less than or equal to 1 and it equals 1 if and only if
\Delta(\Phi)=\infty
.

Nonlinear Perron Frobenius theorem for
\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}}

In this section we generalize the approach, includes structure, lemmas, theorems, etc, as
[
13
]
chapter 4 for proving an important theorem (Theorem
3.1
) in computing
S_{p}\rightarrow S_{q}
norm for completely positive maps.

For the Schatten
p
-norm, derivative
J_{S_{p}}
can be represented as follows (see
[
14
]
):

J_{S_{p}}(A)=\{B:\left<A,B\right>=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}},\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{S_{p^{*}}}=1\}

where
\left<A,B\right>=tr(A^{\dagger}B)
is the Frobenius inner product on matices. Also we know that because the Schatten norm is Fréchet differentiable so
J_{S_{p}}
is single valued also it’s easy to see that for positive semidefinite matrices,
J_{S_{p}}
satisfies following equation (see
[
5
]
)

J_{S_{p}}(A)=\frac{\hskip-7.22743ptQ\Lambda^{p-1}Q^{\dagger}}{\hskip 7.22743pt\left\lVert Q\Lambda^{p-1}Q^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{S_{p^{*}}}}

where
S_{p^{*}}
is the dual norm such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}=1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given matrix A with
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}=1
,
{f_{\Phi}(A)=\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{q}}/\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}\neq 0}
. A is a critical point of function
f_{\Phi}
if and only if it is a fixed point of
S_{\Phi}(A)=J_{S_{p^{*}}}(\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A)))
.

Proof.

At first assume A is a critical point of
f_{\Phi}
then with differentiation we find

\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))=f_{\Phi}(A)J_{S_{p}}(A)

then by applying the
J_{S_{p^{*}}}
function to above equation we have the following

J_{S_{p^{*}}}(\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A)))=\frac{\hskip-3.61371ptA}{\hskip 7.22743pt\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}

because
J_{S_{p}}(J_{S_{p}*}(A))=A/\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}
and
J_{S_{p}}(\alpha A)=J_{S_{p}}(A)
so by the assumption
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}=1
we conclude A is a fixed point of
S_{\Phi}
. Now assume A is fixed point of
S_{\Phi}
then we have

J_{S_{p^{*}}}(\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A)))=A
so there exist
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))=J_{S_{p}}(A)
so by definition of
J_{S_{p}}(A)
we have

\lambda^{-1}=\left<A,\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))\right>=\left<\Phi(A),J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))\right>=\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{q}}=f_{\Phi}(A)

(1)

where the second equality holds because for linear function
\Phi
we have
\left<\Phi(A),B\right>=\left<A,\Phi^{*}(B)\right>
and the last equality holds because
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}=1
so from (
1
) we conclude A is a critical point of
f_{\Phi}
.
∎

Definition 3.1
.

(see

Let

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be a completely positive map

and P be a part of

\Phi^{*}(\Phi(A))\in P
. If
\kappa(\Phi)\leq\tau<1

has a unique fixed point

in P and the following power method converges to

A_{0}=A,\quad A_{k+1}=S_{\Phi}(A_{k}),\quad k=1,2,\cdots

Note that if

then
J_{S_{p}}(A)\in P
because P is a part of
H^{n}_{+}
and
A\sim J_{S_{p}}(A)
so the assumption
\Phi^{*}(\Phi(A))\in P
implies
S_{\Phi}(A)\in P
so by assumption
\kappa(\Phi)\leq\tau<1
we can use the Banach fixed point theorem for complete metric space
(M,d)
where

(for proof see
[
7
]
) and conclude

has a unique fixed point X in P and the power method converges to X for any starting point.
∎

Note that it’s not enough to say the power method always converges to the maximizer of
f_{\Phi}
because now it’s possible for
f_{\Phi}
to have more than one critical points because we only proved in every part of

it has at most one critical point and for our claim we need some more assumptions that you can see in following lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any completely positive map

\Phi
the global maximum of
f_{\Phi}
is attained in
H^{n}_{+}
.

Proof.

We know that
\left\lVert|A|\right\rVert_{S_{p}}=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}
and also
\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{p}}\leq\left\lVert\Phi(|A|)\right\rVert_{S_{p}}
for any
A\in H^{n}
because for every Hermitian matrix A, eigenvalues of
|A|
are absolute values of eigenvalues of A so
A\preceq|A|
and by Lemma
2.1
we have
\Phi(A)\preceq\Phi(|A|)
and then because of monotonicity of Schatten norms we have
\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{p}}\leq\left\lVert\Phi(|A|)\right\rVert_{S_{p}}
. So we have the following

f_{\Phi}(A)=\frac{\left\lVert\Phi(A)\right\rVert_{S_{q}}}{\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}=\frac{\left\lVert|\Phi(A)|\right\rVert_{S_{q}}}{\left\lVert|A|\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert\Phi(|A|\right\rVert_{S_{q}}}{\left\lVert|A|\right\rVert_{S_{p}}}=f_{\Phi}(|A|)

so if
A
is a maximizer of
f_{\Phi}
then
f_{\Phi}(A)\leq f_{\Phi}(|A|)
which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
A\in H^{n}_{+},p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
we have
S_{\Phi}(A)\sim\Phi^{*}\Phi(A).

Proof.

It’s easy to see

and if

\Phi(A)\sim\Phi(B)
so we have

\Phi(A)\sim J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))\Rightarrow\Phi^{*}\Phi(A)\sim\Phi^{*}J_{S_{q}}(\Phi(A))\Rightarrow\Phi^{*}\Phi(A)\sim S_{\Phi}(A)

∎

.

Let

Definition 3.2
.

Let

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
be a positively improving completely positive map

then
\kappa(\Phi)<1
.

Proof.

From Birkhoff-Hopf theorem it’s enough to prove

and also it’s enough to prove
\left\lVert{\Phi(B)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(A)\Phi(B)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right\rVert<\infty
for every

with

because Hilbert metric is defined on rays and also by definition
\Phi(B),\Phi(A)
are positive definite so
\Phi(B)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
exist. It’s known for every

and

we have
\left\lVert AB\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert B\right\rVert
so
\left\lVert{\Phi(B)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(A)\Phi(B)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right\rVert\leq{\lambda_{max}(\Phi(A))}/{\lambda_{min}(\Phi(B))}
where
\lambda_{max}
and

are the largest and the smallest eigenvalues. So it’s enough to show there exist numbers

such that

and
\lambda_{min}(\Phi(A))>c
for any

with

, for proving this note that
\Phi
is a continuous function on the compact set

so the image of
\Phi
is also compact so
\lambda_{max}(\Phi(A))<C
and also we know the image is a subset of inside of positive semidefinite cone that contains it’s boundary so the image of
\Phi
has a positive distance

with the boundary and it means for every matrix

the matrix

is positive definite and it implies

.

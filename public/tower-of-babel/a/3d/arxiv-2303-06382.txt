Baxter operators in Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system II. Bispectral wave functions


Abstract
In the previous paper we introduced a commuting family of Baxter Q Q -operators for the quantum Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system. In the present work we show that the wave functions of the quantum system found by M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars also diagonalize Baxter operators. Using this property we prove the conjectured duality relation for the wave function. As a corollary, we show that the wave function solves bispectral problems for pairs of dual Macdonald and Baxter operators. Besides, we prove the conjectured symmetry of the wave function with respect to spectral variables and obtain new integral representation for it.

Denote by

The Ruijsenaars system
[

]
is governed by commuting symmetric difference operators

H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}+\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}.

(1.3)

Here and in what follows we denote tuples of
n
variables as

\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

(1.4)

One can also consider gauge equivalent Macdonald operators

M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}.

(1.5)

Both families of operators are parametrized by three constants: periods
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and coupling constant
g
,
which originally are supposed to be real positive. The equivalence is established by means of the measure function

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mu(x):=\mu_{g}(x|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}(\imath x|\bm{\omega})S_{2}^{-1}(\imath x+g|\bm{\omega}).

(1.6)

Here
S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
is the double sine function, see Appendix
A

Namely,

\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n})}\,M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n})}}=H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n},g|\bm{\omega}).

(1.7)

Ruijsenaars operators are symmetric with respect to the sesquilinear scalar product defined by the Lebesque measure
d\bm{x}_{n}
, while the Macdonald operators are symmetric with respect to the scalar product related to the measure

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})\,d\bm{x}_{n},\qquad\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.8)

In this paper, as well as in
[

]

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}>0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}>0,\qquad 0<\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}

(1.9)

and

\nu_{g}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}>0.

(1.10)

Denote by

{K}(x):={K}_{g}(x|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath x+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\Big|\bm{\omega}\Bigr)S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath x+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\Big|\bm{\omega}\Bigr)

(1.11)

g^{\ast}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g.

(1.12)

We also use the products of this function

{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}{K}(x_{i}-y_{j}).

(1.13)

&\qquad+\Bigl(M_{r-1}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})\tau_{y}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\ e^{-{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}}\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\biggr].\end{split}

(3.14)

Then using the symmetricity of Macdonald operators (
3.12
) we rewrite (
3.14
) as

\begin{split}e^{{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+2\pi r\lambda_{n}\omega_{1}}\biggl[\Bigl(\tau_{y}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\ M_{r}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})e^{{-2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}}\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\\
+\Bigl(\tau_{y}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\ M_{r-1}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})e^{{-2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}}\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\biggr]\end{split}

(3.15)

under the assumption that the shifts of the integration contour implemented in the equality (
3.12
) do not spoil separation of the poles (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) property. For (
3.6
) this happens when

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}<\mathrm{Re}\,g^{\ast},

(3.16)

which is equivalent to (
1.21

\imath y_{a}=\imath y_{b}+g
are canceled by zeroes of the coefficients

of Macdonald operators, see
[

, Section 2]
, and we do not get here additional restrictions.
Finally we rewrite (
3.15
) as

\begin{split}&e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\biggl[\Bigl(\tau_{y}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\ e^{-2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}}\,M_{r}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\\
&\qquad+\Bigl(\tau_{y}K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),e^{{-2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}+2\pi\lambda_{n}\omega_{1}}M_{r-1}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\biggr]\\
=\,&e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}}\biggl[\Bigl(K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),\ e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}}\,\tau_{y}M_{r}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\\
&\qquad+\Bigl(K(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}),e^{{2\pi\imath\lambda_{n}}\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}+2\pi\lambda_{n}\omega_{1}}\tau_{y}M_{r-1}(\bm{y}_{n-1};g|\bm{\omega})\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\Bigr)\biggr].\end{split}

By induction assumption,

\begin{split}&M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\\[6.0pt]
&\quad\Bigl[e_{r}\big(e^{2\pi\lambda_{1}\omega_{1}},\ldots,e^{2\pi\lambda_{n-1}\omega_{1}}\big)+e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{n}\omega_{1}}e_{r-1}\big(e^{2\pi\lambda_{1}\omega_{1}},\ldots,e^{2\pi\lambda_{n-1}\omega_{1}}\big)\Bigr]\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\end{split}

(3.19)

which in turn implies the theorem statement (

3.2
Eigenfunctions of
Q
-operators

In this section we show that the iterated integrals (
1.24
) represent as well joint eigenfunctions of the commutative family of operators
Q_{n}(\lambda)
. Modulo convergence of the appearing integrals, it is a formal consequence of Theorem
3
. However, these convergence details are not trivial.
See
4

Proof of this theorem follows from the iterative representation of the wave function

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g)=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(3.20)

and the exchange relation given by Theorem
3

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)=\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\rho)\;\Lambda_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda).

(3.21)

The subtle point is the possibility to switch integration order inside of multiple integrals appearing during this calculation. It is indeed possible due to the absolute convergence of these integrals, which we prove in the following proposition. After it we give a proof of Theorem
4
.

Proposition 1
.

Multiple integrals

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}

\displaystyle=Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}},

(3.22)

\displaystyle J_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}},

(3.23)

\displaystyle\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}.

(3.24)

are absolutely convergent for
\lambda,\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})|<\frac{\nu_{g}}{2}(1-\varepsilon),\qquad|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})|<\theta(\varepsilon),\qquad k,j=1,\dots,n

(3.25)

for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and

\theta(\varepsilon)=\frac{\nu_{g}}{4(n-1)!e}\,\varepsilon.

(3.26)

In particular, the wave function
\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
is given by a multiple integral, converging when all the variables are in small strips around the real lines.

Proof.

We present the proof for
n\geq 2
. For
n=1
it is an elementary check.

Consider the first integral (
3.22
). It has
n
levels of integration variables. Denoting integration variables of
k
-th level by

\bm{y}_{k}=\bigl(y_{1}^{(k)},\dots,y_{k}^{(k)}\bigl),

(3.27)

we write integral in its full form

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=C_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n(n+1)/2}}d\bm{y}_{n}

\displaystyle d\bm{y}_{n-1}\dots d\bm{y}_{1}\;e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\left[\lambda\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+(\lambda_{n}-\lambda)\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}\right]}\,{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})

(3.28)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{k=2}^{n}e^{{2\pi\imath}{}(\lambda_{k-1}-\lambda_{k})\underline{\bm{y}}_{k-1}}\,\mu(\bm{y}_{k})\,{K}(\bm{y}_{k},\bm{y}_{k-1}).

Here
C_{I}
contains all constants
d_{k}
from kernels of operators. Denote the integrand as
F
.
Assume that

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})|\leq\delta_{Q}\frac{\nu_{g}}{2},\qquad|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{k-1}-\lambda_{k})|\leq\delta_{\Lambda}\frac{\nu_{g}}{2},\qquad 2<k\leq n

(3.29)

where
\delta_{Q}
and
\delta_{\Lambda}
are some positive constants. Using bounds (
A.18
)

|\mu(x)|\leq Ce^{\pi\nu_{g}|x|},\qquad|{K}(x)|\leq Ce^{-\pi\nu_{g}|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.30)

together with triangle inequalities
|x_{i}-y_{j}|\geq|y_{j}|-|x_{i}|
we get

|F|\leq C_{1}\exp\pi\nu_{g}\biggl(\bigl[\delta_{Q}-n\bigr]\|\bm{y}_{n}\|+S_{n}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n})+\delta_{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|\biggr),

(3.31)

with some
C_{1}(g,\bm{\omega},\bm{x}_{n})
and the function
S_{n}
in exponent is defined by recurrence relation

\displaystyle S_{n}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\bigl|y_{i}^{(n)}-y_{j}^{(n)}\bigr|

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\,\bigl|y_{i}^{(n)}-y_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr|

(3.32)

\displaystyle+S_{n-1}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n-1})

with
S_{1}=0
. As before,
\|\bm{y}_{k}\|
denotes
L^{1}
-norm. In appendix we prove the inequality (
B.39
)

S_{n}\leq(n-1+\varepsilon)\|\bm{y}_{n}\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|

(3.33)

for any
\varepsilon\in\left[0,2(n-1)\right]
, where
c_{n}
is defined by the recurrence relation

c_{n}=(n-1)(c_{n-1}+1)\qquad\text{with}\qquad c_{1}=0.

(3.34)

Clearly,

(n-1)!\leq c_{n}<(n-1)!e,\qquad n\geq 2.

(3.35)

Inserting it in (
3.31
) we obtain

|F|\leq C_{1}\exp\pi\nu_{g}\biggl(\Bigl[(-1+\delta_{Q}+\varepsilon)\Bigr]\|\bm{y}_{n}\|+\left(\delta_{\Lambda}-\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|\biggr).

(3.36)

We have an exponential decay in the right hand side of (
3.36
), if

\delta_{Q}<1-\varepsilon,\qquad\delta_{\Lambda}<\frac{\varepsilon}{(n-1)!e}<\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}.

(3.37)

This proves the statement of the proposition for the integral (
3.22
).

Next, consider the second integral (
3.23
). It also has
n
levels of integration variables. We will denote by
\bm{t}_{n-1}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1})
variables associated with the operator
\Lambda_{n}
and the rest levels of variables, as before, will be denoted by
\bm{y}_{k}
(
k=1,\dots,n-1
). Full form of the integral

\displaystyle J_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=C_{J}\int d\bm{t}_{n-1}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\ldots d\bm{y}_{1}\,e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\left[\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+(\lambda-\lambda_{n})\underline{\bm{t}}_{n-1}+(\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda)\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}\right]}\,{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{t}_{n-1})

\displaystyle\times\mu(\bm{t}_{n-1})\,{K}(\bm{t}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1})\prod_{k=2}^{n-1}e^{{2\pi\imath}{}(\lambda_{k-1}-\lambda_{k})\underline{\bm{y}}_{k-1}}\,\mu(\bm{y}_{k})\,{K}(\bm{y}_{k},\bm{y}_{k-1}).

(3.38)

Again,
C_{J}
contains all unimportant constants. The integration goes over
\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)(n+2)/2}
. Denote the integrand as
G
. Assuming the conditions (
3.29
), using the bounds (
3.30
) and triangle inequalities we arrive at

|G|\leq C_{2}\exp\pi\nu_{g}\biggl(\delta_{Q}\,\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(t_{j}-y_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)\right|+\delta_{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|-n\|\bm{t}_{n-1}\|+T_{n-1}\biggr),

(3.39)

with some
C_{2}(g,\bm{\omega},\bm{x}_{n})
, where the function
T_{n-1}
is defined as

\displaystyle T_{n-1}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n-1},\bm{t}_{n-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}|t_{i}-t_{j}|

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\bigl|t_{i}-y_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr|

(3.40)

\displaystyle+S_{n-1}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n-1}).

In appendix we prove the inequality (
B.51
) (here we replace
r
by
1-\varepsilon
)

\displaystyle T_{n-1}\leq(n-\varepsilon)\|\bm{t}_{n-1}\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)c_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|-(1-\varepsilon)\,\biggl|\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(t_{j}-y_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)\biggr|

(3.41)

for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Using it we have the bound

\displaystyle|G|\leq C_{2}\exp\pi\nu_{g}\biggl(-\varepsilon\|\bm{t}_{n-1}\|

\displaystyle+\left(\delta_{\Lambda}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)c_{n-1}}\right)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|

\displaystyle+(\delta_{Q}-1+\varepsilon)\biggl|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\bigl(t_{j}-y_{j}^{(n-1)}\bigr)\biggr|\,\biggr).

We thus see that the integral (
3.23
) converges once

\delta_{Q}<1-\varepsilon,\qquad\delta_{\Lambda}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)!e}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)c_{n-1}}.

(3.42)

Finally, the third integral (
3.24
) in its full form

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=C_{\Psi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n(n-1)/2}}

\displaystyle d\bm{y}_{n-1}\dots d\bm{y}_{1}\;e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\left[\lambda_{n}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}+(\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n})\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1}\right]}{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})

(3.43)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{k=2}^{n-1}e^{{2\pi\imath}{}(\lambda_{k-1}-\lambda_{k})\underline{\bm{y}}_{k-1}}\,\mu(\bm{y}_{k})\,{K}(\bm{y}_{k},\bm{y}_{k-1})

almost coincides with the first integral

up to additional functions

that only improve convergence.
Its integrand, denote it by

|H|\leq C_{3}\exp\pi\nu_{g}\biggl(\Bigl[(-2+\delta_{\Lambda}+\varepsilon)\Bigr]\|\bm{y}_{n}\|+\left(\delta_{\Lambda}-\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|\biggr).

(3.45)

It converges if

\delta_{\Lambda}<2-\varepsilon,\qquad\delta_{\Lambda}<\frac{\varepsilon}{(n-1)!e}

(3.46)

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Assuming

the first condition follows from the second one.

So, all three integrals converge under assumptions

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})|<\frac{\nu_{g}}{2}(1-\varepsilon),\qquad|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})|<\frac{\nu_{g}}{2}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)!e}

(3.47)

with any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Since all constants that appear in the bounds are uniform on compact sets of parameters
\bm{\omega},g
preserving the conditions (
1.9
), (
1.10
), the considered multiple integrals are analytic functions of the parameters
\bm{\omega},g
, as well as of the variables
x_{i}
and
\lambda_{j}
. In the case of real periods
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
convergence of the third integral (
3.24
), that is the wave function representation, was first proved in
[
HR1
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

In the notation of the previous proposition the theorem states that

I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}.

(3.48)

The proof goes by induction. In the case
n=1
this integral

I_{\lambda,\lambda_{1}}=d_{1}(g|\bm{\omega})\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy_{1}\,e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\left[\lambda x_{1}+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)y_{1}\right]}{K}(x_{1}-y_{1})=\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\lambda_{1})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(3.49)

coincides with the Fourier transform of the function
{K}
(
A.22
). Next, consider the case of arbitrary
n
. The main ingredient of the proof is the local relation (
1.35
)

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)=\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\rho)\;\Lambda_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda).

(3.50)

Consider the function in question

I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}=Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}=Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}.

(3.51)

This integral has
n
levels of integration variables that we denoted by

\bm{y}_{k}=\bigl(y_{1}^{(k)},\dots,y_{k}^{(k)}\bigr),\qquad k=1,\dots,n.

(3.52)

By Proposition
1
the integral is absolutely convergent. Therefore, we can use Fubini’s theorem and evaluate integrals in any order. Take integral over
\bm{y}_{n}
to be the first in this order. Then we use the local relation (
3.50
) and obtain

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})\;\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}

(3.53)

\displaystyle=\hat{{K}}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})\;J_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}.

Obtained integral coincides with the function
J_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n}}
from Proposition
1
. This integral has
n
levels of integration variables that we denoted by
\bm{t}_{n-1}
(variables associated with the operator
\Lambda_{n}
from the left) and
\bm{y}_{k}
(the rest ones,
k=1,\dots,n-1
). Since by proposition it is also absolutely convergent we can change order of integrals and make the integral over
\bm{t}_{n-1}
to be the last one. Then the multiple integral we should take before it

I_{\lambda,\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}=Q_{n-1}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}

(3.54)

represents the function on the previous step of induction. So, to conclude the proof we use induction assumption and obtain desired formula.
∎

4
Duality

In this section, we prove the equivalence of two integral representations of the wave function

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
encoded in duality relation (
1.48
). This will be proved in two steps. First, using the spectral properties of operators

and
\hat{Q}_{n}(x)
proved in the previous section and the bound on the wave function from
[

]

. Then using analiticity of the wave function with respect to the periods (see Remark

in the previous section) we extend this result to complex periods.
See
5

We note that functions from both sides exist under assumptions (
1.46

iterative with respect to
\lambda_{j}

\bigl(\hat{\Lambda}_{n}(x)f\bigr)(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=d_{n-1}(\hat{g}^{*}|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\,\hat{\Lambda}(\bm{\lambda}_{n},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1};x)f(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})

It is also an eigenfunction of the

-operator acting on functions of

with the kernel

and the corresponding eigenvalue

\hat{Q}_{n}(x)\,\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}{K}(x-x_{j})\,\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n}).

All of this is just a matter of renaming the variables
\lambda_{j}\leftrightarrow x_{j}
and parameters

,

, see the definitions of the functions
\hat{{K}}
(
1.34

1.41

4.5

Thirdly, in the proof we use the bound derived in
[
HR3
, Theorem 2.5]
. This bound holds when

and

, but we only need its specialization for the case of real

. Denote

\mu^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}),\qquad\hat{\mu}^{\prime}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}\hat{\mu}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}),

(4.11)

so that comparing with (

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\mu^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{n})\mu^{\prime}(-\bm{x}_{n}),\qquad\hat{\mu}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})=\hat{\mu}^{\prime}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(-{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}).

(4.12)

Theorem
.

[
HR3
, Specialization of Theorem 2.5]
Assuming
\mathrm{Re}\,g\in(0,\max(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})]
, the wave function admits the bound

\Bigl|\mu^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\Bigr|\leq P(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega}),

(4.13)

where
P
is a polynomial with
\deg P\leq n(n-1)/2
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

At first, assume
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are real positive. We prove the theorem by induction. The case
n=1

\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}(x_{1})=\hat{\Psi}_{x_{1}}(\lambda_{1})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(4.14)

is obvious. Suppose we proved the relation for
n-1
case

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1})=\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{x}_{n-1}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}).

(4.15)

Using formula (
4.3
) we write the wave function recursively with the help of
Q
-operator

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\,\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}{K}(x_{n}-x_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(4.16)

By induction assumption (
4.15
) we can represent
{K}
functions here as action of the operator
\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})
, see eigenvalue identity (
4.9
),

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\,\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(4.17)

Since operators
Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})
and
\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})
act on different types of variables (
\bm{x}_{n-1}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}
correspondingly), they commute: switching their order is equivalent to switching order of the corresponding integrals. Let us postpone convergence arguments to the end of this proof and assume that we can switch their order. Then we arrive at

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\,\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(4.18)

Next step is to use the eigenvalue identity (
1.37
) for the operator
Q_{n-1}(\lambda_{n})

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\,\hat{Q}_{n-1}(x_{n})\,\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\hat{{K}}(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}).

(4.19)

Finally, using induction assumption (
4.15
) and formula (
4.10
) we obtain function
\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{x}_{n}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n})
from the right.

It is left to prove that we can switch integration order in representation (
4.17
). Let us write it explicitly

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}x_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\,\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{n}(\bm{x}_{n-1}-\bm{y}_{n-1})}\,{K}(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1})

(4.20)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\,\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}{}x_{n}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})}\,\hat{{K}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\;\Psi_{\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}}(\bm{y}_{n-1}).

To switch order of integrals over
\bm{y}_{n-1}
and
\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}
we bound integrand and use Fubini’s theorem. First, factor measure functions

\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})=\mu^{\prime}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\mu^{\prime}(-\bm{y}_{n-1}),\qquad\hat{\mu}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})=\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}),

(4.21)

where
\mu^{\prime}
and
\hat{\mu}^{\prime}
are defined in (
4.11
). Then using bounds (
A.18
) and analogous ones for the dual functions

\displaystyle|\mu(y)|\leq Ce^{\pi\nu_{g}|y|},\qquad\;\,|{K}(y)|\leq Ce^{-\pi\nu_{g}|y|},\qquad\;\,y\in\mathbb{R},

(4.22)

\displaystyle|\hat{\mu}(\gamma)|\leq Ce^{\pi\nu_{\hat{g}^{*}}|\gamma|},\qquad|\hat{{K}}(\gamma)|\leq Ce^{-\pi\nu_{\hat{g}^{*}}|\gamma|},\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

(4.23)

where

\nu_{g}=\mathrm{Re}\,\hat{g}>0,\qquad\nu_{\hat{g}^{*}}=\mathrm{Re}\,g^{*}>0,

(4.24)

together with triangle inequalities

|x_{i}-y_{j}|\geq|y_{j}|-|x_{i}|,\qquad|\lambda_{i}-\gamma_{j}|\geq|\gamma_{j}|-|\lambda_{i}|

(4.25)

we bound part of the integrand by exponentially decreasing function

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mu^{\prime}(-\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(-\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})

\displaystyle{\displaystyle K}(\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\hat{{K}}(\bm{\lambda}_{n-1},\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\Bigr|

(4.26)

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\exp\Bigl(-\pi\nu_{g}\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|-\pi\nu_{\hat{g}^{*}}\|\bm{\gamma}_{n-1}\|\Bigr)

with some
C_{q}(g,\bm{\omega},\bm{x}_{n-1},\bm{\lambda}_{n-1})
.

For the rest part of the integrand (besides surely bounded oscillating exponents) we use estimate (
4.13
). If
\mathrm{Re}\,g\in(0,\max(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})]
we use it, as it is written. If
\mathrm{Re}\,g\in(\max(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
, then
{\hat{g}^{\ast}}\in(0,\min(\hat{\omega}_{1},\hat{\omega}_{2}))
. In this case we use an analogous to (
4.13
) estimate for the dual wave function
\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{y}_{n-1}}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})

\Bigl|\mu^{\prime}(\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\,\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{y}_{n-1}}(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1})\Bigr|\leq P(\bm{\gamma}_{n-1};\hat{g}^{*}|\hat{\bm{\omega}}),

(4.27)

since wave functions are equal by induction assumption (
4.15
). In both cases exponent in (
4.26
) suppresses the polynomial growth. Thus, the whole integrand is absolutely integrable. This gives the proof of Theorem
5
for real positive periods.

Since the estimate for multiple integral (
3.24
) presenting the wave function in Proposition
1
is uniform on compact subsets of the periods, the wave function
\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
depends analitically on the periods
\bm{\omega}
. The same is true for the function
\hat{\Psi}_{\bm{x}_{n}}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})
. Hence, their coincidence for real periods implies their coincidence for complex periods. This ends the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

Appendix A
The double sine function

The double sine function
S_{2}(z):=S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
, see
[
Ku
]
and references therein, is a meromorphic function that satisfies two functional relations

\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{1})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},\qquad\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{2})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}

(A.1)

and inversion relation

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(-z)=-4\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},

(A.2)

or equivalently

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z)=1.

(A.3)

The function
S_{2}(z)
has poles at the points

z=m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\geq 1

(A.4)

and zeros at

z=-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\geq 0.

(A.5)

For
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
all poles and zeros are simple.
In the analytic region
\mathrm{Re}\,z\in(0,\mathrm{Re}\,(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}))
we have the following integral representation for the logarithm of
S_{2}(z)

\ln S_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{2t}\left(\frac{\sh\left[(2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t\right]}{\sh(\omega_{1}t)\sh(\omega_{2}t)}-\frac{2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}t}\right).

(A.6)

It is clear from this representation that the double sine function is homogeneous

S_{2}(\gamma z|\gamma\omega_{1},\gamma\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\qquad\gamma\in(0,\infty)

(A.7)

and invariant under permutation of periods

S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{2},\omega_{1}).

(A.8)

The double sine function can be expressed through the Barnes double Gamma function
\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})

[
B
]
,

S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\Gamma_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z|\bm{\omega})\Gamma_{2}^{-1}(z|\bm{\omega}),

(A.9)

and its properties follow from the corresponding properties of the double Gamma function.
It is also connected to the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic Gamma function
G(z|\bm{\omega})

[
R2
]

G(z|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}\Bigl(\imath z+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}\,\Big|\,\bm{\omega}\Bigr)

(A.10)

and to the Faddeev quantum dilogarithm
\gamma(z|\bm{\omega})

[
F
]

\gamma(z|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}\Bigl(-\imath z+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}\,\Big|\,\bm{\omega}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\frac{\imath\pi}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\Bigl[z^{2}+\frac{\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}}{12}\Bigr]\Bigr).

(A.11)

Both
G(z|\bm{\omega})
and
\gamma(z|\bm{\omega})
were investigated independently.

In the paper we deal only with ratios of double sine functions denoted by
\mu(x)
(
1.6
) and
K(x)
(
1.11
)

\begin{split}\mu(x)&=S_{2}(\imath x)S_{2}^{-1}(\imath x+g),\\[6.0pt]
K(x)&=S_{2}\left(\imath x+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}+\frac{g}{2}\right)S_{2}^{-1}\left(\imath x+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}-\frac{g}{2}\right).\end{split}

(A.12)

Now we will give the key asymptotic formulas and bounds for them, which were derived in
[
BDKK
, Appendices A, B]
from the known results for the double Gamma function. In what follows we assume conditions (
1.9
), (
1.10
)

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{j}>0,\qquad 0<\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2},\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\hat{g}>0,

(A.13)

where we denoted

\hat{g}=\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(A.14)

Let
\sigma_{i}
be the arguments of the periods
\omega_{i}
,
|\sigma_{i}|<\pi/2
. Because of the symmetry (
A.8
), we may assume that
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}
. Let
D_{+}
and
D_{-}
be the cones of poles (
A.4
) and zeros (
A.5
) of
S_{2}(z)
:

D_{+}=\{z\colon\sigma_{2}<\arg z<\sigma_{1}\},\qquad D_{-}=\{z\colon\pi+\sigma_{2}<\arg z<\pi+\sigma_{1}\},\qquad D=D_{+}\cup D_{-}.

Denote by
d(z,D)
the distance between a point
z
and the cones
D
. Using Barnes’ Stirling formula for the asymptotic of double Gamma function, for the ratio of double sines one obtains

\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+g)}=e^{\mp\pi\imath\hat{g}\left(z-\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\right)}\Bigl(1+\,O\Bigl(d^{-1}(z,D)\Bigr)\Bigr),

(A.15)

where
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus D
and the sign
-
(or
+
) is taken for
\mathrm{Re}\,z>0
(or
\mathrm{Re}\,z<0
), see
[
BDKK

.
Then from (
A.15
) for the functions
\mu(x)
and
K(x)
(
A.12
) with

we have

\mu(x)\sim e^{\pi\hat{g}|x|\pm\imath\frac{\pi\hat{g}g^{*}}{2}},\qquad K(x)\sim e^{-\pi\hat{g}|x|},\qquad x\rightarrow\pm\infty.

(A.16)

Denote also

\nu_{g}=\mathrm{Re}\,\hat{g}.

(A.17)

Under the conditions (
1.9
), (
1.10
) by using the same Stirling formula we also have bounds

|\mu(x)|\leq Ce^{\pi\nu_{g}|x|},\qquad|K(x)|\leq Ce^{-\pi\nu_{g}|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}

(A.18)

where
C
is a positive constant uniform for compact subsets of parameters
\bm{\omega},g
preserving the mentioned conditions, see
[
BDKK

Appendix B
Inequalities with absolute value

First, we will prove a little lemma that will be crucial for all other estimates.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
,
y_{1},y_{2},y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

|y_{1}-y_{2}|-|y_{1}-y|-|y_{2}-y|\leq\varepsilon\left(|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|-|y|\right).

(B.24)

Proof.

Consider two cases. First, assume that
|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|\geq|y|
. Then using triangle inequality we obtain

|y_{1}-y_{2}|-|y_{1}-y|-|y_{2}-y|\leq 0\leq\varepsilon\left(|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|-|y|\right)

(B.25)

since
\varepsilon\geq 0
.

Second, assume
|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|\leq|y|
. Now using
|y_{j}-y|\geq|y|-|y_{j}|
and
|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|
we arrive at

|y_{1}-y_{2}|-|y_{1}-y|-|y_{2}-y|\leq 2\left(|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|-|y|\right)\leq\varepsilon\left(|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|-|y|\right)

(B.26)

because
\varepsilon\leq 2
.
∎

Remark 5
.

Note that Lemma
1
holds for any norm and points
y_{j}
in the corresponding normed space.

Denote by
\bm{y}_{k}
a vector with
k
components
\bm{y}_{k}=\bigl(y^{(k)}_{1},\dots,y^{(k)}_{k}\bigr)
,
y_{j}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}
. Define a function
S_{n}
by a recurrence relation

\displaystyle S_{n}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n)}_{j}\bigr|

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\,\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|

(B.27)

\displaystyle+S_{n-1}(\bm{y}_{1},\dots,\bm{y}_{n-1})

with
S_{1}=0
. Also by
\|\bm{y}_{k}\|
denote
L^{1}
-norm

\|\bm{y}_{k}\|=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigl|y^{(k)}_{j}\bigr|.

(B.28)

Lemma 2
.

The inequality

S_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n)}_{j}\bigr|+c_{n}\varepsilon\|\bm{y}_{n}\|-\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|,

(B.29)

holds for any
\varepsilon\in\left[0,\frac{2(n-1)}{c_{n}}\right]
, where the number
c_{n}
is defined by recurrence relation
c_{n}=(n-1)(1+c_{n-1})
with
c_{1}=0
.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction. Consider the case
n=2
. Using Lemma
1
we have

\displaystyle S_{2}=2\bigl|y_{1}^{(2)}-y_{2}^{(2)}\bigr|-\bigl|y_{1}^{(2)}-

\displaystyle y_{1}^{(1)}\bigr|-\bigl|y_{2}^{(2)}-y_{1}^{(1)}\bigr|

(B.30)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|y_{1}^{(2)}-y_{2}^{(2)}\bigr|+\varepsilon\left(\bigl|y_{1}^{(2)}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{2}^{(2)}\bigr|\right)-\varepsilon\bigl|y_{1}^{(1)}\bigr|

for any
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
. Thus, we proved the base case.

Now suppose we proved the statement for
S_{n-1}
. Both sides of the stated inequality are symmetric with respect to components of
\bm{y}_{k}
for all
k=1,\dots,n
. Therefore, without loss of generality we can assume the ordering

y^{(k)}_{1}\geq\ldots\geq y^{(k)}_{k},\qquad k=1,\dots,n.

(B.31)

For the vector
\bm{y}_{n}
with ordered components we can write

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n)}_{j}\bigr|=2\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2m+1)\bigl|y_{m}^{(n)}-y_{n-m+1}^{(n)}\bigr|.

(B.32)

Consequently, due to the recurrence relation (
B.27
) and induction assumption

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2m+1)\bigl|y_{m}^{(n)}-y_{n-m+1}^{(n)}\bigr|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\,\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|

(B.33)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}(n-2m)\bigl|y_{m}^{(n-1)}-y_{n-m}^{(n-1)}\bigr|+c_{n-1}\varepsilon\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|-\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|.

Next step it to regroup and estimate terms from the first three sums. For the term with
m=1
from the first sum we use the Lemma
1
and for all other terms we simply use triangle inequalities.

Consider term with
m=1
from the first sum and terms with
i=1,n
from the second double sum and write the estimate

\displaystyle(n-1)\bigl|y_{1}^{(n)}-y_{n}^{(n)}\bigr|-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\left(\bigl|y_{1}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{n}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|\right)

(B.34)

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\,\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(\bigl|y_{1}^{(n)}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{n}^{(n)}\bigr|\right)-\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|,

where we used Lemma
1
with parameter
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in[0,2]
multiple times. Similarly let us estimate the term with
m>1
from the first sum together with the corresponding terms from the second double sum

\displaystyle(n-2m+1)

\displaystyle\bigl|y_{m}^{(n)}-y_{n-m+1}^{(n)}\bigr|

(B.35)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=m}^{n-m}\left(\bigl|y_{m}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{n-m+1}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigr|\right)\leq 0,

where we used triangle inequality multiple times. Remaining from the second double sum terms can be grouped with terms from the third sum

\displaystyle(n-2m)

\displaystyle\bigl|y_{m}^{(n-1)}-y_{n-m}^{(n-1)}\bigr|

(B.36)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=m+1}^{n-m}\left(\bigl|y_{i}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{m}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{i}^{(n)}-y^{(n-1)}_{n-m}\bigr|\right)\leq 0,

where we again used triangle inequalities.

So, starting from (
B.33
) and using inequalities (
B.34
), (
B.35
), (
B.36
) we obtain

\displaystyle S_{n}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2m+1)\bigl|y_{m}^{(n)}-y_{n-m+1}^{(n)}\bigr|+(n-1)\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(\bigl|y_{1}^{(n)}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{n}^{(n)}\bigr|\right)

(B.37)

\displaystyle+(c_{n-1}\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime})\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|-\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|.

Finally, for the first sum we again use equality (
B.32
). For the second sum we have

\bigl|y_{1}^{(n)}\bigr|+\bigl|y_{n}^{(n)}\bigr|\leq\|\bm{y}_{n}\|.

(B.38)

Choosing
\varepsilon^{\prime}=(1+c_{n-1})\varepsilon
we get bound stated in the lemma. Note that since
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq 2
then
\varepsilon\leq 2/(1+c_{n-1})=2(n-1)/c_{n}
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

The inequality

S_{n}\leq(n-1+\varepsilon)\|\bm{y}_{n}\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|\bm{y}_{k}\|

(B.39)

holds for any
\varepsilon\in\left[0,2(n-1)\right]
.

Proof.

Use Lemma
2
rescaling the parameter
\varepsilon\rightarrow\varepsilon/c_{n}
and

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\bigl|y^{(n)}_{i}-y^{(n)}_{j}\bigr|\leq(n-1)\|\bm{y}_{n}\|

(B.40)

to arrive at stated inequality.
∎

In fact, Lemma
2
gives an estimate for the integrand in the integral representation of the wave function
\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
(
1.24
) and, as a consequence, the exponential bound for the wave function. Denote, as before (
4.11
),

\mu^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}).

(B.41)

Corollary 6
.

The wave function admits the bound

\bigl|\mu^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{n})\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})\bigr|\leq C\exp\pi\nu_{g}\left(\varepsilon\|\bm{x}_{n}\|-\frac{2}{\nu_{g}}\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}\operatorname{Im}\lambda_{n}\right)

(B.42)

with some
C(g,\bm{\omega})
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,2(n-1)]
, assuming

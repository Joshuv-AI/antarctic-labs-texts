The e e -positivity and Schur positivity of the chromatic symmetric functions of some trees Thanks: This paper is supported by the General Program of National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12171034) and the Fundamental Research Funds for the Central Universities in China (Grant No. 2021CX11012).
David G.L. Wang, Monica M.Y. Wang

Abstract
. We investigate the e e -positivity and Schur positivity of the chromatic symmetric functions of some spider graphs with three legs. We obtain the positivity classification of all broom graphs and that of most double broom graphs. The methods involve extracting particular e e -coefficients of the chromatic symmetric function of these graphs with the aid of Orellana and Scott’s triple-deletion property, and using the combinatorial formula of Schur coefficients by examining certain special rim hook tabloids. We also propose some conjectures on the e e -positivity and Schur positivity of trees.

Introduction

Stanley 1995
introduced
the
chromatic symmetric function

for a simple graph

as

X_{G}=X_{G}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=\sum_{\kappa}\prod_{v\in V(G)}\mathbf{x}_{\kappa(v)}

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
is a countable set
of indeterminates,
and the sum is over all proper colorings
\kappa

namely
colorings such that every each color class
is an independent set.
The chromatic polynomial
\chi_{G}(k)

counts the number of proper colorings using
k

which is a classical graph invariant dating back to
Birkhoff 1912
.
The chromatic symmetric function satisfies
X_{G}(1^{k})=\chi_{G}(k)
.
The chromatic symmetric function
X_{G}
is a symmetric function.
Common bases for the algebra
\Lambda(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)

of symmetric functions include
the monomial symmetric functions
\{m_{\lambda}\}
,
elementary symmetric functions
\{e_{\lambda}\}
,
Schur symmetric functions
\{s_{\lambda}\}
and so on,
see
[
37
, Chapter 7]
.
For any basis
\{b_{\lambda}\}
of

,
the graph

is said to be

-positive

if the expansion of

has only nonnegative coefficients.

A strong motivation of studying the positivity of chromatic symmetric functions
is Stanley and Stembridge’s conjecture posed in 1993, see
1.1
.
The smallest connected non-
e
-positive graph is the claw,
whose chromatic symmetric function is

X_{\text{claw}}=4e_{4}+5e_{31}-2e_{2^{2}}+e_{21^{2}}=s_{31}-s_{2^{2}}+5s_{21^{2}}+8s_{1^{4}}.

A graph is
claw-free
if it does not contain
an induced subgraph which is isomorphic to the claw.
The
incomparability graph
of a poset

is the graph with vertex set
P
, in which two elements are adjacent if and only if they are incomparable.

Conjecture 1.1

(
Stanley and Stembridge 1993
)
.

Any claw-free incomparability graph is
e
-positive.

such that each induced subgraph

A second motivation of studying the positivity of chromatic symmetric functions
is the close relationship between the Schur postivity and representation theory.
The Schur functions are considered to be

A particular class of trees, the spiders,

A

Preliminaries

Let

A
composition

We write

An

It can be recast as

A

of

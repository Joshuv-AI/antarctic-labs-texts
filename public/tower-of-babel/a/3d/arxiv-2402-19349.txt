Optimal Fermionic Joint Measurements for Estimating Non-Commuting Majorana Observables
Daniel McNulty, Susane Calegari, Michał Oszmaniec

Abstract
An important class of fermionic observables, relevant in tasks such as fermionic partial tomography and estimating energy levels of chemical Hamiltonians, are the binary measurements obtained from the product of anti-commuting Majorana operators. In this work, we investigate efficient estimation strategies of these observables based on a joint measurement which, after classical post-processing, yields all sufficiently unsharp (noisy) Majorana observables of even-degree. By exploiting the symmetry properties of the Majorana observables, as described by the braid group, we show that the incompatibility robustness, i.e., the minimal classical noise necessary for joint measurability, relates to the spectral properties of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model. In particular, we show that for an n n mode fermionic system, the incompatibility robustness of all degree– 2 ​ k 2k Majorana observables satisfies Θ ( n − k / 2 ) \Theta(n^{-k/2}) for k ≤ 5 k\leq 5 . Furthermore, we present a joint measurement scheme achieving the asymptotically optimal noise, implemented by a small number of fermionic Gaussian unitaries and sampling from the set of all Majorana monomials. Our joint measurement, which can be performed via a randomization over projective measurements, provides rigorous performance guarantees for estimating fermionic observables comparable with fermionic classical shadows.



I
Introduction

A fundamental non-classical feature of quantum mechanics, and a key ingredient in practical applications, is the inability to jointly measure non-commuting (Hermitian) observables
Busch et al. 2016
;
Heinosaari et al. 2008
.
In the general setting of positive operator-valued measures (POVMs), incompatibility (or lack of joint measurability) and non-commutativity are related but inequivalent notions. If one adds a sufficient amount of (e.g., depolarising) noise to the set of observables, at some threshold, known as the incompatibility robustness
Heinosaari et al. 2015
, the non-commuting measurements become compatible and can be implemented by a parent POVM and classical post-processing.
This leads naturally to a resource theoretic formulation of incompatibility
Heinosaari et al. 2015
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
, with its characterization and quantification connected to both foundational topics
Wolf et al. 2009
;
Kiukas et al. 2019
;
Xu and Cabello 2019
, and practical tasks in quantum information theory
Quintino et al. 2014
;
Uola et al. 2015
;
Skrzypczyk et al. 2019
.

Incompatibility is also relevant in the performance of estimation tasks on present-day quantum computers
Kandala et al. 2017
.
For example, the inability to simultaneously measure non-commuting observables presents a significant hurdle for quantum variational algorithms
Bharti et al. 2022
;
Preskill 2018
;
McClean et al. 2016
;
García-Pérez et al. 2021
, which require repeated estimations of expectation values of many-body Hamiltonians.
To reduce the cost of performing individual measurements, and to improve scalability, several strategies have been developed.
One method involves finding minimal groupings of the measurements into pairwise commuting subclasses that can be jointly measured
Jena et al. 2019
;
Gokhale et al. 2019
;
Yen et al. 2020
;
Verteletskyi et al. 2020
;
Crawford et al. 2021
;
Izmaylov et al. 2020
;
Zhao et al. 2020
;
Bonet-Monroig et al. 2020
.
Another approach, known as classical shadows, implements a randomized measurement strategy to construct a classical representation of the unknown state
Huang et al. 2020
;
Hadfield et al. 2022
;
Gresch and Kliesch 2023
;
Koh and Grewal 2022
;
Chen et al. 2021
. The classical shadow can be used to simultaneously estimate multiple properties of the system such as expectations of arbitrary observables up to some required precision. Originally formulated for systems of qubits, classical shadows have since been extended to the fermion setting via several methods
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
;
Low 2022
;
O’Gorman 2022
.
Alternatively, one can navigate the incompatibility issue directly by applying a parent measurement that simultaneously measures unsharp versions of the non-commuting observables
McNulty et al. 2023
.
This approach has been used to simultaneously estimate expectation values of
n
–qubit Pauli strings and multi-qubit Hamiltonians, and provides efficiency improvements over current classical shadow techniques in noisy scenarios
Koh and Grewal 2022
;
Chen et al. 2021
.

In this work, we extend the joint measurability framework to the fermionic setting and characterize the measurement incompatibility of assemblages of Majorana observables. In particular, we consider strategies which simultaneously measure unsharp (noisy) versions of the set of
\binom{2n}{2k}
degree–
2k
Majorana observables of an
n
mode fermionic system. The joint measurement, together with classical post-processing of the outcomes, yields single shot estimators for tasks such as partial state tomography, which upon repetition can approximate all
k
–body fermionic marginals to a required precision
Bonet-Monroig et al. 2020
;
Zhao et al. 2021
. Equipped with this partial knowledge of the state, one can determine properties such as multipole moments
Gidofalvi and Mazziotti 2007
, derivatives of energy
O’Brien et al. 2019
;
Overy et al. 2014
, as well as apply error mitigation techniques
McClean et al. 2017
;
Takeshita et al. 2020
and fidelity certification
Gluza et al. 2018
. The strategy also provides single-shot estimators for the energy levels of electronic structure Hamiltonians
McClean et al. 2016
.

The optimality of the joint measurability approach is determined by the incompatibility robustness of the set of observables. While calculating this quantity usually requires semidefinite programming (SDP) techniques that quickly become computationally infeasible for large collections of observables
Designolle et al. 2019a
;
Cavalcanti and Skrzypczyk 2017
;
Gühne et al. 2023
, symmetries between the observables can simplify the problem considerably
Nguyen et al. 2020
. We exploit this simplification by showing that the set of degree–
k
Majorana measurements are uniformly and rigidly symmetric with respect to the braid group
Bravyi 2006
, represented in fermionic Fock space. This reduces the problem of finding the optimal robustness to finding the maximal eigenvalue of a class of operators relevant in the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, which is an important model of strongly interacting quantum systems inspired by high energy physics
Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2014
;
Feng et al. 2019
. In the simplest case of
\binom{2n}{2}
quadratic Majoranas, we derive an analytical upper bound for the robustness which is tight if and only if a real
2n\times 2n
skew-Hadamard matrix exists. More generally, for assemblages of degree–
2k
Majorana observables with
k\leq 5
, we apply a bound derived by Hastings and O’Donnell on the spectrum of the SYK model
Hastings and O’Donnell 2022
, to show that the incompatibility robustness satisfies
\mathcal{O}(n^{-k/2})
.

We also provide a joint measurement scheme for each
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
, which simultaneously measures all degree–
2k
Majorana observables with sharpness
\Theta(n^{-k/2})
, and hence is asymptotically optimal for
k\leq 5
. Importantly, the joint measurement can be implemented on a quantum computer by the randomization of projective measurements
Oszmaniec et al. 2017
. In a circuit implementation, the joint measurement corresponds to transforming the state by conjugating with a randomly sampled Majorana monomial, followed by a fermionic Gaussian unitary, and finally a fermionic computational basis measurement (see Fig.
1
). We show that applying this measurement strategy to simultaneously estimate non-commuting fermionic observables yields the same sample complexity scalings as fermionic shadows but requires implementing significantly fewer fermionic Gaussian unitaries
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
.

This work is accompanied by a complementary paper
Majsak et al. 2024
that focuses on the practical problem of implementing the joint measurement strategy for Majorana pairs and quadruples, as well as the problem of estimating expectation values of molecular quantum chemistry Hamiltonians. In the current manuscript, we focus mainly on the structural and mathematical aspects of the joint measurability properties of Majoranas, establishing connections to graph and design theory, as well as to the SYK model. Some of the structures that arise include perfect matchings of Turán graphs, tournament matrices
Ito 2017
, Hadamard and skew-Hadamard matrices
Koukouvinos and Stylianou 2008
, as well as flat orthogonal matrices
Jaming and Matolcsi 2015
.

The paper is structured as follows. We begin in Sec.
II
with some preliminaries and introduce the relevant notation. In Sec.
III
we describe our general fermionic joint measurement strategy. Sec.
IV
provides lower bounds on the sharpness of the degree–
2k
observables that constitute the marginals of the parent POVM. In Sec.
V
we evaluate the incompatibility robustness of degree–
2k
Majorana observables via a connection to the SYK model. In Sec.
VI
we employ the joint measurement strategy to estimate fermionic observables for partial state tomography and energy estimations of Hamiltonians, and in Sec.
VII
we provide comparisons to other approaches. In particular, we highlight some connections to fermionic classical shadows, as well as a measurement strategy based on fermion-to-qubit transformations. Finally, Sec.
VIII
contains some concluding remarks and open questions.

II
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of
\dim\mathcal{H}=d<\infty
. A measurement is described by a POVM
\mathsf{M}
, with a finite outcome set
\Omega
, and consists of positive semidefinite matrices (effects)
\mathsf{M}(e)\geq 0
, for which
\sum_{e\in\Omega}\mathsf{M}(e)=\mathbbm{1}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity of
\mathcal{H}
. For a quantum state
\rho
, the outcome probability distribution of
\mathsf{M}
is given by
p(e|\rho)=\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}(e)\rho\right]
.

A finite collection of measurements
\mathsf{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{M}_{m}
is
jointly measurable
(compatible) whenever their statistics can be reproduced by classical post-processing of the statistics from a single (parent) POVM. In particular, there exists a measurement
\mathsf{G}
with outcome set
\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}
such that for each measurement
\mathsf{M}_{j}
, its effects
\mathsf{M}_{j}(e_{j})
with outcomes
e_{j}\in\Omega_{j}
, can be obtained from
\mathsf{G}
via the classical post-processing (stochastic transformation),

\mathsf{M}_{j}(e_{j})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}}D(e_{j}|j,\lambda)\mathsf{G}(\lambda)\,,

(1)

where
0\leq D(e_{j}|j,\lambda)\leq 1
and
\sum_{e_{j}\in\Omega_{j}}D(e_{j}|j,\lambda)=1
, for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. Equivalently, a set of POVMs is said to be jointly measurable if there exists a parent whose marginals yield all effects of the individual POVMs. A set of POVMs with no joint measurement is called incompatible
Busch et al. 2016
.

For an
n
mode fermionic system, we label the
2n
Majorana operators,

\gamma_{2j-1}=a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j}\,,\qquad\gamma_{2j}=i(a_{j}^{\dagger}-a_{j})\,,

(2)

where
a_{j}
and
a_{j}^{\dagger}
are the annihilation and creation operators of mode
j\in[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. The operators anti-commute and are Hermitian, i.e.,
\gamma_{j}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}+\gamma_{j^{\prime}}\gamma_{j}=2\delta_{j{j^{\prime}}}\mathbbm{1}
and
\gamma^{\dagger}_{j}=\gamma_{j}
, for all
j,j^{\prime}\in[2n]
.

In this work, we investigate the joint measurability properties of degree–
2k
Majorana observables,

\gamma_{S}:=i^{\binom{2k}{2}}\gamma_{j_{1}}\gamma_{j_{2}}\cdots\gamma_{j_{2k}}\,,

(3)

where
S=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{2k}\}\subseteq[2n]
is a
2k
-element subset, with its elements listed in ascending order
1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{2k}\leq 2n
. The phase associated with the monomial ensures Hermiticity. If
S=\varnothing
, i.e.,
|S|=0
, then
\gamma_{\varnothing}=\mathbbm{1}
. Any pair of observables
\gamma_{S}
and
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}
, with
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq\{1,2\ldots,2n\}
, either commute or anti-commute in accordance to,

\gamma_{S}\gamma_{S^{\prime}}=(-1)^{|S|\cdot|S^{\prime}|-|S\cap S^{\prime}|}\gamma_{S^{\prime}}\gamma_{S}\,.

(4)

We label the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables by the index set

\mathcal{S}_{2k}=\binom{[2n]}{2k}=\{S\subset[2n]\,:\,|S|=2k\}\,,

(5)

where each
2k
–element string
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
is ordered ascendingly.

Our aim is to construct a parent POVM which, after classical post-processing, yields the unsharp (noisy) degree–
2k
Majorana measurements,

\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+e_{S}\eta_{S}\gamma_{S})\,,

(6)

for every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, where
e_{S}\in\{\pm 1\}
labels the outcome, and
\eta_{S}
the sharpness, which satisfies
0\leq\eta_{S}\leq 1
. Here,
\mathsf{M}_{S}
is obtained by deforming the projective measurement
\mathsf{P}_{S}(\pm)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\gamma_{S})
with the depolarising channel, i.e.,
\mathsf{M}_{S}=\eta_{S}\mathsf{P}_{S}+(1-\eta_{S})\mathbbm{1}/2
. For a given parent POVM
\mathsf{G}
, we consider
\eta_{S}
to be the maximal sharpness for which
\mathsf{M}_{S}
is jointly measured by
\mathsf{G}
. Furthermore, we denote

\eta_{2k}:=\min\{\eta_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}\,,

(7)

as the (maximal) sharpness for which
all
degree–
2k
Majorana observables can be measured simultaneously by
\mathsf{G}
.

From the outcome of a joint measurement, it is simple to produce a single shot estimator of
\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\rho\right]
, for every degree–
2k
Majorana observable. The general strategy, originally described for systems of qubits
McNulty et al. 2023
, is to estimate
\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\rho\right]
by sampling from outputs
e_{S}
of the noisy Majorana measurement
\mathsf{M}_{S}
of Eq. (
6
). Importantly, for any input state
\rho
, the expectation value of
e_{S}
equals
\eta_{S}\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\rho\right]
, so it is natural to set the unbiased estimator as
\hat{\gamma}_{S}=\eta_{S}^{-1}e_{S}
. We apply this strategy to simultaneously estimate non-commuting fermionic observables, and provide rigorous bounds on the number of copies of the state
\rho
(i.e., the sample complexity) to approximate the expectations up to a required precision.

We will describe the parent measurement in both the Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures: In the Schrödinger picture, the measurement is encoded in a unitary evolution acting on the quantum state (Fig.
1
); in the Heisenberg picture, the transformation acts on the computational basis measurement while the state remains fixed. Both viewpoints are equivalent since implementing a POVM
\mathsf{M}
on the transformed state
U\rho U^{\dagger}
is equivalent to implementing
U^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}U
on the state
\rho
by the duality
\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}U\rho U^{\dagger}\right]=\textrm{tr}\left[U^{\dagger}\mathsf{M}U\rho\right]
.

To construct and implement the parent measurement we rely on fermionic Gaussian (or linear-optical) unitaries. These are a class of unitary operators which satisfy

\gamma_{j}^{O}:=U_{O}^{\dagger}\gamma_{j}U_{O}=\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{2n}O_{jj^{\prime}}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}\,,

(8)

for each
j\in[2n]
, where
O\in O(2n)
is a real orthogonal
2n\times 2n
matrix. In particular, they transform the
2n
Majorana operators into a rotated set of operators which satisfy the same anti-commutation relations. A fermionic Gaussian unitary is generated from a quadratic Hamiltonian
Jozsa and Miyake 2008
, i.e.,
U_{O}=e^{iH}
where
H=-\frac{i}{4}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=1}^{2n}\alpha_{jj^{\prime}}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}
and
\alpha_{jj^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}
are the entries of a real antisymmetric matrix. Under the action of
U_{O}
, an observable
\gamma_{S}
, with
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, transforms to

\gamma_{S}^{O}:=U_{O}^{\dagger}\gamma_{S}U_{O}=\sum_{S^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}_{2k}}}\text{det}(O_{S,S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S^{\prime}}\,,

(9)

where
O_{S,S^{\prime}}
denotes a
2k\times 2k
submatrix of
O
with rows indexed by
S
and columns by
S^{\prime}

Knill 2001
;
Bravyi and Kitaev 2002
;
Jozsa and Miyake 2008
. The class of fermionic Gaussian unitaries play an important role in various quantum computing applications
Valiant 2001
;
Terhal and DiVincenzo 2002
, e.g., for performing quantum chemistry and many-body simulations
Kivlichan et al. 2018
;
Google AI Quantum and
Collaborators 2020
, fermion sampling
Oszmaniec et al. 2022
;
Hangleiter and Eisert 2023
, and classical shadows
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
;
Low 2022
;
O’Gorman 2022
.

It is often convenient to represent a fermionic system as a system of qubits via a fermion-to-qubit mapping. A transformation of this type maps the
2n
Majorana operators of Eq. (
2
) to multi-qubit Pauli operators while preserving their anti-commutativity relations. One such mapping is the Jordan-Wigner (JW) transformation
Jordan and Wigner 1993
, in which the Majorana operators of mode
j\in[n]
are represented as the
n
-qubit Pauli operators,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2j-1}

\displaystyle=Z^{\otimes j-1}\otimes X\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes n-j}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2j}

\displaystyle=Z^{\otimes j-1}\otimes Y\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\otimes n-j}\,,

(11)

where
X,Y
and
Z
are the qubit Pauli operators, and
\mathbbm{1}
the
2\times 2
identity.

A fermionic Gaussian unitary in the JW representation is described by a matchgate circuit
Valiant 2001
. Matchgates are the class of two-qubit gates generated by the rotations
\exp(i\theta X\otimes X)
,
\exp(i\theta Z\otimes\mathbbm{1})
and
\exp(i\theta\mathbbm{1}\otimes Z)
. A matchgate circuit (arranged in a 1D architecture) is generated from the set of all nearest-neighbor matchgates, together with the Pauli operator
X
on the
n
–th qubit.

To describe the behavior of the joint measurement as the system size increases we use the standard notation
\mathcal{O}
,
\Omega
and
\Theta
for asymptotic bounds. In particular,
f(n)=\Theta(g(n))
implies
\exists\,c,C,n_{0}>0
such that
c\cdot g(n)\leq f(n)\leq C\cdot g(n)
for all
n>n_{0}
, while
\mathcal{O}
and
\Omega
are the upper and lower bounds, respectively.

III.1
Single Majoranas

Consider the set of
2n
Majorana measurements
\mathsf{M}_{j}(\pm)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\eta_{j}\gamma_{j})
, with sharpness
\eta_{j}\in[0,1]
for
j\in[2n]
. Joint measurability has previously been considered in both the uniform
Kunjwal et al. 2014
and biased cases
Bluhm and Nechita 2018
. Here, the uniform case satisfies
\eta_{j}=\eta_{j^{\prime}}
for all
j,j^{\prime}\in[2n]
, while a biased measurement drops this constraint. By exploiting connections between joint measurability and the inclusion of free spectrahedra
Bluhm and Nechita 2018
, it was shown that
\{\mathsf{M}_{j}\,|\,j\in[2n]\}
are jointly measurable if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\eta_{j}^{2}\leq 1
. Note that the criterion generalizes the joint measurability condition for qubit Pauli observables
Busch 1986
;
Brougham and Andersson 2007
, and applies for any set of pairwise anti-commuting unitary matrices which are Hermitian and idempotent (of which there are at most
2n+1
in the Hilbert space of dimension
2^{n}
).

In the uniform case, an optimal joint measurement was presented in
Kunjwal et al. 2014
. This generalizes to the biased parent POVM,

\mathsf{G}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n})=\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}e_{j}\eta_{j}\gamma_{j}\right)\,,

(12)

with outcome string
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}\in\{\pm 1\}
. By taking the marginals, for each
j\in[2n]
, we recover
\mathsf{M}_{j}(e_{j})
.

We now show that the optimal joint measurement, with
\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\eta_{j}^{2}=1
, is projectively simulable, i.e., it can be implemented by a randomization over projective measurements
Oszmaniec et al. 2017
. First, a single Majorana operator, say
\gamma_{1}
, is rotated to a linear combination
\gamma_{1}^{O}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\eta_{j}\gamma_{j}
, by an orthogonal transformation
O\in O(2n)
, as described in Eq. (
8
). Then, by conjugating with a randomly sampled monomial
\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}
,
X\subseteq[2n]
, the operator is mapped to

\gamma_{1}^{O}\mapsto\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\cdot\gamma_{1}^{O}\cdot\gamma_{X}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}x_{j}\eta_{j}\gamma_{j}\,,

(13)

where
x_{j}=(-1)^{|X|-|X\cap\{j\}|}
(cf. Eq. (
4
)).
In particular, if
X
is even,

x_{j}=\begin{cases}-1&\mbox{if}\,\,\,j\in X\\
1&\mbox{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

(14)

and if
X
is odd, the signs in Eq. (
14
) are reversed. It follows that, given any
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2n}
,
\exists!\,X\subseteq[2n]
, such that

\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\cdot(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2n})\cdot\gamma_{X}=(x_{1}\gamma_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}\gamma_{2n})\,.

(15)

Consequently, implementing the projective measurement

\mathsf{P}^{O,X}(q)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+q\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\gamma^{O}_{1}\gamma_{X})\,,

(16)

with
X\in 2^{[2n]}
sampled uniformly at random from the power set
2^{[2n]}
, i.e., the set of all subsets of
[2n]
, is equivalent to performing the parent
\mathsf{G}
. The outcome
(q,X)
from (
16
) yields the string of
2n
outcomes
e_{j}=q\cdot x_{j}
for all
j\in[2n]
in Eq. (
12
).

III.2
Degree–
2k
Majoranas

In the fermionic setting, we denote the computation basis measurements by the index set

\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{\,\{2j-1,2j\}\,\,|\,j\in[n]\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}\,,

(17)

and the corresponding POVMs,

\mathsf{P}_{R}(q_{R})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+q_{R}\gamma_{R})\,,\quad R\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\,,

(18)

with outcomes
q_{R}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Under the Jordan-Wigner transformation,
\{\mathsf{P}_{R}\,|\,R\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\}
map to the standard computational basis measurement on
n
qubits (cf. Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
)). More generally, the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables that commute with the computational basis measurements are denoted by the index set

\mathcal{D}_{2k}=\{\cup_{i=1}^{k}R_{i}\,|\,R_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}\,,R_{i}\neq R_{i^{\prime}}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{2k}\,.

(19)

To simultaneously measure all unsharp versions of the degree–
2k
Majorana monomials
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
, we first take an arbitrary
R\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}
, and apply a fermionic Gaussian unitary
U^{\dagger}_{O}
to
\mathsf{P}_{R}
, as described in Eq. (
9
), such that

\mathsf{P}^{O}_{R}(\pm):=U_{O}^{\dagger}\mathsf{P}_{R}(\pm)U_{O}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\gamma_{R}^{O})\,,

(20)

where

\gamma^{O}_{R}:=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}\,.

(21)

Then, conjugating with a Majorana monomial
\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}
,
X\subseteq[2n]
, transforms
\mathsf{P}^{O}_{R}
to

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{O,X}_{R}(\pm)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\cdot\mathsf{P}^{O}_{R}(\pm)\cdot\gamma_{X}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\gamma_{R}^{O,X})\,,

where

\gamma_{R}^{O,X}:=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}x_{S}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}\,,

(22)

and
x_{S}=(-1)^{|X|\cdot|S|-|S\cap X|}
. Finally, sampling
X\subseteq[2n]
uniformly at random from the power set
2^{[2n]}
, i.e., the set of all subsets of
[2n]
, defines a POVM,

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}^{O}_{R}(q_{R},X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 2^{-2n}\mathsf{P}^{O,X}_{R}(q_{R})

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2n+1}}\left(\mathbbm{1}+q_{R}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}x_{S}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}\right)\,,

with outcomes
q_{R}\in\{\pm 1\}
and
X\in 2^{[2n]}
.

More generally we can define a POVM that is not limited to just one choice of
R\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}
. In particular, by taking the product of

IV
Asymptotically optimal joint measurements

Our task is to find a suitable
O\in O(2n)
for the parent
\mathsf{G}^{O}
such that its relevant minors, i.e., Eq. (
27
), are not too small. In particular, we wish to find
O
such that, for every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, the sharpness

\eta_{S}=\max\{\eta_{R,S}\,|\,R\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}\}\,,

(28)

is sufficiently large.

In a naive strategy, consider
O=H
, where
H
is a
2n\times 2n
real Hadamard matrix with elements
|h_{ij}|=1/\sqrt{2n}
for all
i,j\in[2n]
.
Suppose that we wish to jointly measure all degree–
2
Majorana observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\}
. The sharpness
\eta_{R,S}=|\det(O_{R,S})|
associated with each observable is obtained from a
2\times 2
minor of
O
. For each
S
there are
n
possible minors, depending on the choice of
R\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Since every
2\times 2
minor of a
2n\times 2n
Hadamard is either
0,
or
\pm n^{-1}
, it follows that
\eta_{R,S}\in\{0,n^{-1}\}
. By selecting the optimal
R\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
, i.e., avoiding zero-valued minors, we conclude that the parent POVM jointly measures
\gamma_{S}
with sharpness
\eta_{S}=n^{-1}
. Another strategy, for example, is to sample a Haar-distributed random orthogonal matrix
O
and find the maximum minors.

Instead, to obtain sharper observables, our joint measurement
\mathsf{G}
is implemented by a randomization over a set of
N
POVMs,
\mathsf{G}^{(r)}
,
r=1,\ldots,N
. Each POVM is of the form described in Eq. (
24
) and defined by a matrix
O^{(r)}\in O(2n)
which has a modified block diagonal structure. For example, the degree–
2
parent is implemented by sampling, uniformly at random, one of two POVMs,
\mathsf{G}^{(1)}
and
\mathsf{G}^{(2)}
. For a given
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
, at least one of the two POVMs measures
\gamma_{S}
with sharpness
\eta_{S}=\Omega(n^{-1/2})
. If, in the worst-case scenario, the sampled POVM contains no useful information about
\gamma_{S}
, we apply the trivial measurement (i.e. a coin flip) and draw the outcome at random. After taking the randomization over both POVMs into account, the sharpness
\eta_{S}=\Omega(n^{-1/2})
is reduced by a factor of
1/2
. More generally, provided at least one of the
N
POVMs approximates the observable with sufficient sharpness, the randomization will reduce
\eta_{S}
by a factor of at most
1/N
.

In the following theorem, we place a lower bound on the quantity
\eta_{2k}
defined in Eq. (
7
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a parent POVM that jointly measures, with sharpness
\eta_{2k}
, the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
of an
n
mode system.

(i)

If
\mathsf{G}
is implemented via a randomisation over two POVMs
\mathsf{G}^{(1)}
and
\mathsf{G}^{(2)}
, defined by
O^{(1)},O^{(2)}\in O(2n)
in Eq. (
29
), then all degree–
2
observables are jointly measured with
\eta_{2}=\Omega(n^{-1/2})
.

(ii)

Suppose
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
and
N>4k
. If
\mathsf{G}
is implemented via a randomisation over
N
POVMs
\mathsf{G}^{(r)}
, defined by
O^{(r)}\in O(2n)
in Eq. (
32
), for
r=1,\ldots,N

2k

\eta_{2k}=\Omega(n^{-k/2})
.

In Sec.
V
we will show that the parent from Theorem

has the same asymptotic scaling as the incompatibility robustness.

We summarize the measurement strategies of
(i)
and
(ii)

B

C

\sqrt{2n}

The rows and columns of the orthogonal block diagonal matrices (consisting of lower-flat matrix blocks) are then permuted to ensure, for every

V
Incompatibility robustness and the SYK model

We now investigate the incompatibility robustness of degree–
k
Majorana observables, initially dropping the restriction to even degree observables. In particular, for the measurements
\mathsf{M}_{S}(\pm)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\eta\gamma_{S})
, we evaluate the quantity,

\eta_{k}^{*}=\sup\{\eta\,|\,\mathsf{M}_{S}\,\,\text{are compatible}\,\,\forall S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}\,,

(33)

which, equivalently, is the minimal noise
(1-\eta)
needed to ensure the existence of a parent POVM. Note that
\eta_{2k}^{*}
provides an upper bound on
\eta_{2k}
in Eq. (
7
) for any parent POVM
\mathsf{G}
since
\eta_{2k}^{*}=\max_{\mathsf{G}}\eta_{2k}
.

While incompatibility robustness is fully characterized for pairs of qubit observables
Busch 1986
;
Yu et al. 2010
as well as pairs of mutually unbiased bases
Carmeli et al. 2019
;
Kiukas et al. 2022
, semidefinite programming is usually required for more general measurement assemblages
Cavalcanti and Skrzypczyk 2017
;
Designolle et al. 2019a
;
Designolle et al. 2019b
. However, this approach quickly becomes infeasible for large collections of measurements due to the number of variables involved. Nevertheless, for sets of observables satisfying certain symmetry conditions, finding the incompatibility robustness via an SDP reduces to evaluating the maximum eigenvalue of a particular class of operators
Nguyen et al. 2020
. In particular, given a measurement assemblage and a symmetry group
G
which permutes the outcomes while maintaining the bundle structure (see Appendix
E
), the assemblage is required to be uniformly and rigidly symmetric for such a simplification to apply. Uniformity holds if each outcome is related to every other outcome by a symmetry transformation. Rigidity holds if the stabilizer group of each outcome commutes with a single projection operator and its complement. In particular, a unitary representation
U:G\rightarrow U(d)
, restricted to the stabilizer group, has exactly two irreducible subrepresentations. In Appendix
E
, we show that the assemblage of degree–
k
Majorana observables (with
k
even or odd) is uniformly and rigidly symmetric with respect to the braid group. In the spinor representation (see Appendix
E.1
), a braiding transformation acts, via conjugation, on the Majorana operators as,

B_{i,j}\gamma_{\ell}B_{i,j}^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{\ell}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,{\ell}\notin\{i,j\}\,,\\
\gamma_{j}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,{\ell}=i\,,\\
-\gamma_{i}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,{\ell}=j\,.\end{cases}

(34)

The symmetry properties ensure that calculating the incompatibility robustness (for each
k
) reduces to evaluating the operator norm, i.e., finding the maximum eigenvalue, of

H_{k}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}e_{S}\gamma_{S}\,,

(35)

where
e_{S}\in\{\pm 1\}
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
.
In Appendix
F
we show that the incompatibility robustness can be expressed as follows.

Lemma 1
.

The set
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
of degree–
k
Majorana observables of an
n
mode fermionic system has incompatibility robustness,

\eta_{k}^{*}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{k}|}\max_{e_{S}\in\{\pm 1\}\forall S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}{\left\|H_{k}\right\|}_{\infty}\,.

(36)

Interestingly, the spectrum of the operator
H_{k}
has been extensively studied in relation to the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model
Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2014
;
Feng et al. 2019
;
Hastings and O’Donnell 2022
;
Herasymenko et al. 2023
, which is of independent interest in condensed matter physics and string theory.
We note that an upper bound on the incompatibility robustness for binary observables can be derived without symmetry considerations
Kunjwal et al. 2014
, which for Majorana observables is equivalent to the right-hand side of Eq. (
36
).

We now invoke several results on the spectral behavior of
H_{k}
to derive bounds (sometimes tight) on the incompatibility robustness, as summarized in the next theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
denote the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables of an
n
mode fermionic system.

(i)

For
k=1
, the incompatibility robustness satisfies
\eta_{2}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n-1}}
, and is saturated if and only if a
2n\times 2n
(real) skew-Hadamard matrix exists. Furthermore, the joint measurement in Sec.
IV.1
is asymptotically optimal, i.e.,
\eta_{2}^{*}=\Theta(n^{-1/2})
.

(ii)

For
k\leq 5
, and all
n
sufficiently large, the incompatibility robustness satisfies
\eta_{2k}^{*}\leq\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}/\binom{2n}{2k}}
. Furthermore, the joint measurement in Sec.
IV.2
is asymptotically optimal, i.e.,
\eta_{2k}^{*}=\Theta(n^{-k/2})
.

We sketch the proofs of parts
(i)
and
(ii)
in the next two subsections (with further details in Appendix
G
).

V.1
Degree–
2
Majoranas

The coefficients of
H_{2}=i\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}
define a unique
2n\times 2n
real antisymmetric matrix
E=-E^{T}
, with entries
e_{ij}=-e_{ji}
for
i<j
. As described in Appendix
G
, the operator

can be mapped via an orthogonal matrix

to a free fermion Hamiltonian,
H_{2}=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j-1}\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j}
, where
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and

Lemma 2

VII
A comparison to other estimation strategies

VII.1
Joint measurements via fermion-to-qubit mappings

Given that, for an
n
mode fermionic system, every Majorana operator (of odd or even degree) can be expressed as an
n
–qubit Pauli operator, a possible measurement approach is to consider a joint measurement of Pauli observables
Busch 1986
;
Brougham and Andersson 2007
. As shown in
McNulty et al. 2023
, we can measure any sufficiently unsharp
n
–qubit Pauli operator
P=P_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes P_{n}
, with
P_{i}\in\{\mathbbm{1},X,Y,Z\}
, via a local parent POVM on each qubit, with effects

\mathsf{F}(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})=\frac{1}{8}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(x_{i}X+y_{i}Y+z_{i}Z)\right)\,,

(43)

where
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
label the
i
–th qubit outcome string. Furthermore, this measurement can be implemented by a randomization over projective measurements. To obtain the unsharp measurement,

\mathsf{M}_{P}(e_{P})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+e_{P}\eta_{P}P)\,,

(44)

with outcome
e_{P}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we perform the classical post-processing,

D(e_{P}|P,x_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if}\,\,\,e_{P}=\mu(P)\\
0&\mbox{if}\,\,\,e_{P}=-\mu(P)\,,\end{cases}

(45)

where
\mu(P):=\prod_{i\in\text{supp}(P)}\mu_{i}(P)
is the product of the relevant local outcomes, with
\mu_{i}(P)=x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}
if
P_{i}=X,Y,Z
, respectively, and
\text{supp}(P)=\{i\,|\,P_{i}\neq\mathbbm{1}\}
. After post-processing of the joint measurement outcomes it is easy to verify that the noise in Eq. (
44
) satisfies

\eta_{P}=3^{-w(P)/2}\,,

(46)

where
w(P)=|\text{supp}(P)|
is the weight of the Pauli operator.

The choice of fermion-to-qubit mapping plays a crucial role in determining how sharply the observables can be simultaneously measured. A parent measurement implementing sharper Majorana observables requires a mapping that minimizes the weight of the Pauli operator (in Eq. (
46
)) associated with each
\gamma_{j}
. The Jordan-Wigner transformation, as described in Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
), acts non-trivially on
\mathcal{O}(n)
qubits. Consequently, degree–
k
observables are jointly measured with sharpness exponential in
n
.

In fact, the measurement strategy provided in
Jiang et al. 2020
can be interpreted as the joint measurement of
\mathsf{F}
on each qubit, together with a fermion-to-qubit transformation defined on ternary trees
Vlasov 2019
. The ternary trees mapping aims to minimize the average weight of the Pauli operators and maps each
\gamma_{j}
to an operator
P
with
w(P)=\lceil\log_{3}(2n+1)\rceil
. Thus, any degree–
k
Majorana observable is transformed to a Pauli operator of weight at most
k\lceil\log_{3}(2n+1)\rceil
. It follows that all degree–
k
Majorana observables can be jointly measured with

\eta_{k}\lesssim 3^{-\frac{k}{2}\log_{3}(2n+1)}=(2n+1)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\,,

(47)

where
\eta_{k}
is defined in Eq. (
7
).

Approximating the
k
–body reduced density matrices (RDMs) of a fermionic quantum state requires estimating the expectation values of all degree–
2k
Majorana observables (cf. Sec.
VI
). This is achieved, to the desired
\epsilon
precision, by implementing
\mathcal{O}(\eta_{2k}^{-2}\epsilon^{-2})
repetitions of the joint measurement. Since
\eta_{2k}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-k})
via ternary tree mappings,
\mathcal{O}(n^{2k}\epsilon^{-2})
repetitions of the joint measurement are required. For example,
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}\epsilon^{-2})
repetitions are required to estimate 2-RDMs. This contradicts the erroneous claim in
Jiang et al. 2020
that a joint measurement with the ternary tree mapping requires only
\mathcal{O}(n^{k}\epsilon^{-2})
repetitions to estimate
k
–RDMs.

VII.2
Fermionic classical shadows

Classical shadows—a randomized measurement protocol that yields a classical representation of an unknown quantum state
Huang et al. 2020
—provides a method to simultaneously estimate non-commuting observables. Originally applied to systems of qubits, with unitaries sampled from the local or global
n
–qubit Clifford group, the protocol has since been extended in several ways to fermionic systems
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
;
Low 2022
;
O’Gorman 2022
. In one approach, a fermionic shadow is constructed by sampling unitaries from the intersection of the fermionic Gaussian and Clifford unitaries
Zhao et al. 2021
.

Several connections have been made between joint measurements and classical shadows
McNulty et al. 2023
. In particular, for estimating energies of Hamiltonians composed of linear combinations of
n
–qubit Pauli observables, a single shot estimator based on the outcomes of a joint measurement provides the same performance guarantees as locally biased classical shadows
Hadfield et al. 2022
. In light of Prop.
3
, estimating degree–
k
Majorana observables via the joint measurement defined in Eq. (
24
) shares the same asymptotic bounds on the sample complexity as fermionic shadows
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
. However, in contrast to classical shadows which sample from the intersection of fermionic Gaussian and Clifford unitaries, implementing the joint measurement requires sampling from approximately
4k
fermionic Gaussian unitaries and the set of Majorana monomials (or Pauli operators).

Other connections also appear between the two measurement strategies. For example, classical shadows can be used to define joint measurements and, vice versa, the outcomes of a joint measurement, together with classical post-processing, result in a classical shadow of the quantum state
McNulty et al. 2023
. The former relation provides a sufficient condition for the compatibility of an arbitrary set of measurements. In Appendix
H
, we apply the fermionic shadow of
Zhao et al. 2021
to derive a joint measurability condition for the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables. In particular, we show that the observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
are compatible when
\eta_{2k}\leq\binom{n}{k}/\binom{2n}{2k}
, therefore placing a lower bound on the incompatibility robustness
\eta_{2k}^{*}=\Omega(n^{-k})
. This coincides with the bound provided by the joint measurement in Sec.
VII.1
using a ternary tree fermion-to-qubit mapping. Clearly, this provides only a sufficient condition for joint measurability since the parent POVM in Theorem
1
together with the upper bounds from Theorem
2
, imply
\eta_{2k}^{*}=\Theta(n^{-k/2})
for
k\leq 5
.

The fermionic shadow norm
\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|_{\text{FGU}}
, derived in
Zhao et al. 2021
, is a useful quantity that provides a (state independent) bound on the variance of the classical shadow estimators of
\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\rho\right]
, for all
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
. In particular, the squared shadow norm is given by
\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|_{\text{FGU}}^{2}=\binom{2n}{2k}/\binom{n}{k}
. It follows from Theorem
2
that
\eta_{2k}^{*}\leq\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|^{-1}_{\text{FGU}}
for
k\leq 5
. If
k=1
, and the number of fermionic modes
n
agrees with the existence of a
2n\times 2n
skew-Hadamard matrix, then
\eta_{2}^{*}=\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|^{-1}_{\text{FGU}}
. In the following conjecture, we suggest this bound holds more generally.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
denote the set of degree–
2k
Majorana operators of an
n
mode fermionic system, with
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
. The incompatibility robustness satisfies
\eta_{2k}^{*}\leq\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}/\binom{2n}{2k}}=\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|^{-1}_{\text{FGU}}
. Thus, the joint measurement in Sec.
IV
is asymptotically optimal, and
\eta_{2k}^{*}=\Theta(n^{-k/2})
.

Interestingly, due to Lemma
1
, the conjecture places a bound on the maximum eigenvalue of
H_{2k}
, i.e.,
\left\|H_{2k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\binom{2n}{2k}\binom{n}{k}}
. Similar bounds have been considered when optimizing fermionic Hamiltonians, e.g.,
Feng et al. 2019
;
Hastings and O’Donnell 2022
;
Herasymenko et al. 2023
. For
k\leq 5
, the conjectured bound is confirmed in Theorem
2
. We also note that the joint measurement in Sec.
VII.1
places a lower bound on the maximum eigenvalue, namely,
\max_{e_{S}\in\{\pm 1\}}\left\|H_{k}\right\|_{\infty}=\Omega(n^{k/2})
, for all even and odd
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

VIII
Concluding remarks

We have studied the joint measurability of fermionic observables composed of products of anti-commuting Majorana operators. Our main results are summarised in Theorems
1
and
2
. We have introduced a parent POVM which simultaneously measures, for
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
, all degree–
2k
Majorana observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
with sharpness
\eta_{2k}=\Theta(n^{-k/2})
. By characterizing the incompatibility properties of Majorana observables, we found that the joint measurement is asymptotically optimal for
k\leq 5
. Our measurement is well-suited for various estimation tasks and can be implemented via randomized projective measurements.

There are several open questions to explore. While we provide asymptotically tight bounds on the incompatibility robustness when
k\leq 5
, a general upper bound (Conjecture
1
) is left open. A full characterization of the incompatibility robustness, as well as an optimal joint measurement for every system size, also remains open. Furthermore, it would be interesting to explore an extension of the fermionic joint measurement strategy (Sec.
III
) from even-degree Majorana operators to odd-degree observables. While the odd case is covered by jointly measuring local Pauli observables under a fermion-to-qubit mapping (Sec.
VII.1
), the optimal joint measurement remains unknown (except when
k=1
).

From a practical perspective, it would be useful to compare our joint measurement strategy to other estimation methods, e.g., for approximating energies of fermionic Hamiltonians. One potential advantage lies in the ability to tailor the joint measurement to a particular Hamiltonian, and optimize the variance of the estimator to reduce the sample complexity. Similar techniques have been developed for classical shadows
Hadfield et al. 2022
and joint measurements
McNulty et al. 2023
on qubit systems. For this purpose, it would also be useful to explore feasible implementations of the protocol with simple circuits, low circuit depth, and minimal reconfigurations.

). In Eq. (

and

\gamma_{R}^{O}=U_{O}^{\dagger}\gamma_{R}U_{O}=U_{O}^{\dagger}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{R_{i}}\right)U_{O}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(U_{O}^{\dagger}\gamma_{R_{i}}U_{O}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{R_{i}}^{O}\,,

(54)

and similarly,

∎

Next, we show that the POVM can be rewritten by modifying the outcome space by replacing

with a string
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2n}
.

Proposition 6
.

The POVM
\mathsf{G}^{O}
defined in Eq. (
48

G^{O}({\bf q},{\bf x})=\frac{1}{2^{3n}}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}}q_{R}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}\left(\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}\right)\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}\right)\,,

{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{2n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2n}

Proof.

Recall (cf. Eq. (

\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\cdot\gamma_{j}\cdot\gamma_{X}=x_{j}\gamma_{j}\,,\qquad j=1,\ldots,2n,

(56)

and

\gamma^{\dagger}_{X}\cdot\gamma_{S}\cdot\gamma_{X}=x_{S}\gamma_{S}\,,\quad S\in 2^{[2n]}\,,

(57)

where

It is straightforward to check that each

gives rise to a unique string

Proposition 7
.

For any

and

(58)

Proposition 8

For every

B.1
Flat orthogonal matrices

For simplicity, the joint measurability scheme described in Sec.
IV.1
is initially restricted to system size
2n=\ell(\ell+1)
, where
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. To achieve the lower bound in Theorem
1
, the parent POVM relies on the existence of the following orthogonal matrix.

Definition 1
.

An
\ell\times\ell
orthogonal matrix
F\in O(\ell)
is a lower-flat matrix if its entries satisfy

\min_{i,j\in[\ell]}|f_{ij}|=\Omega(\ell^{-1/2})\,.

(65)

A lower-flat matrix is a less restrictive version of a flat orthogonal matrix
Jaming and Matolcsi 2015
. A flat orthogonal matrix additionally requires
\max_{i,j\in[\ell]}|f_{ij}|=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-1/2})
such that all entries have absolute value close to
1/\sqrt{\ell}
. Clearly, any Hadamard matrix is a (lower-)flat matrix. While it is conjectured that flat orthogonal matrices exist for all
\ell
, only specific constructions are known. For instance, if a Hadamard of order
\ell+1
exists, a flat orthogonal matrix of order
\ell
exists. Such examples occur when
\ell=p^{m}
, with
p=4k-1
prime and
m
odd (e.g.
\ell=3,7,11,19,\ldots
).

Fortunately, one can construct a lower-flat orthogonal matrix for any
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Jaming and Matolcsi 2015
. An example is given by the
N\times N
matrix (
N=2^{m}+q
and
q<2^{m}
),

F=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}H_{s,s}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H_{s,q}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H_{s,q}\\
\hline\cr\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H_{q,s}&\frac{1}{2}(H_{q,q}+\mathbbm{1})&\frac{1}{2}(-H_{q,q}+\mathbbm{1})\\
\hline\cr-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}H_{q,s}&\frac{1}{2}(-H_{q,q}+\mathbbm{1})&\frac{1}{2}(H_{q,q}+\mathbbm{1})\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

(66)

where
H
is any Hadamard matrix of order
2^{m}
, which we assign the block structure,

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}H_{s,s}&H_{s,q}\\
\hline\cr H_{q,s}&H_{q,q}\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

(67)

Here,
H_{s,q}
denotes an
s\times q
block matrix with
s=2^{m}-q
. Orthogonality of the lower-flat matrix follows from the decomposition
F=V^{T}\widetilde{F}V
, where

\widetilde{F}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}H&0\\
\hline\cr 0&I_{q}\end{array}\right)\,\qquad\text{and}\qquad V=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}I_{s}&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{q}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{q}\\
\hline\cr 0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{q}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I_{q}\end{array}\right)\,.

(68)

Note that for
m>1
, the entries of
F
in Eq. (
66
) satisfy
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2^{m}}}\leq|f_{ij}|\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{m}}}
, and therefore
|f_{ij}|\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{N}}
.

Y_{i}^{\sigma}
.

This is no longer possible if (b) holds since the cardinality of
Y_{i}
can be larger than the number of partition subsets. Instead, whenever
|Y_{i}|=\ell+2
, we modify the perfect matching to ensure the two “extra” vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in Y_{i}
are connected by an edge in
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,
M=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in\mathcal{M}
. The corresponding POVM
\mathsf{G}^{D}_{M}
, defined in Eq. (
70
), is not guaranteed to measure all sufficiently unsharp pairs
i\gamma_{j_{1}}\gamma_{j_{2}}
, with
j_{1},j_{2}\in Y_{i}
. In particular, the relevant submatrix
D_{M,\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}
(from rows
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
) is not monomial, and the determinant could be zero (or too small). Nevertheless, due to the orthogonality of
F^{(i)}
, there exists at least one pair
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}\subset Y_{i}
such that
i\gamma_{j_{1}}\gamma_{j_{2}}
is jointly measured by
\mathsf{G}^{D}_{M}
with sufficient sharpness. In particular, for orthogonality of
F
to hold (dropping the index
i
for simplicity), given a pair of rows
(v_{1},v_{2})
there exists a pair of columns
(j_{1},j_{2})
such that exactly three of the four matrix entries of

F_{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{j_{1},j_{2}\}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{v_{1},j_{1}}&F_{v_{1},j_{2}}\\
F_{v_{2},j_{1}}&F_{v_{2},j_{2}}\end{array}\right)\,

(77)

have the same sign. In this case,
|\det(F_{\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\{j_{1},j_{2}\}})|=|F_{v_{1},j_{1}}F_{v_{2},j_{2}}|+|F_{v_{1},j_{2}}F_{v_{2},j_{1}}|=\Omega(\ell^{-1})
, guaranteeing that
i\gamma_{j_{1}}\gamma_{j_{2}}
is jointly measured with sufficient sharpness. Therefore, it is not necessary to ensure the vertices of the given pair
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
lie in disjoint subsets in the second partitioning. Instead,
\sigma
is chosen such that the only vertices of
Y_{i}
that remain together in a single partition subset are those connected by an edge in
\mathcal{M}
. Following a similar argument presented in Appendix
B
, we conclude all degree–
2
Majorana observables of an
n
mode fermionic system are jointly measured with the desired sharpness.

Appendix D
Joint measurement of degree–
2k
Majoranas

To simultaneously measure all degree–
2k
Majorana observables (with the noise specified in Thm.
1
) we randomize over a collection on parent POVMs
\mathsf{G}^{O}
defined in Eq. (
69
). For simplicity, we work with POVMs
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{O}
, described by Eq. (
30
) and a perfect matching
\mathcal{M}
of a Turán graph
T(2n,\ell+1)
. We initially assume
2n=\ell(\ell+1)
, ensuring that the corresponding Turán graph has a vertex set that partitions into
\ell+1
subsets, each of cardinality
\ell
. Furthermore, we assume
\mathcal{M}
is sparsely arranged (cf. Sec.
IV.1
).

Let
\mathcal{Y}
denote the set of all partitions
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell+1})\in\mathcal{Y}
of the Turán graph
T(2n,\ell+1)
, i.e., all partitions of its vertex set
[2n]=\{1,2,\ldots,2n\}
, into
\ell+1
subsets
Y_{i}\in\binom{[2n]}{\ell}
, such that
Y_{i}\cap Y_{i^{\prime}}=\varnothing
. In the following definition, we associate a joint measurement
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{O}
with each partition of a Turán graph.

Definition 3
.

We say that the joint measurement
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{O}
(in Eq. (
69
)) is
defined by the partition

Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell+1})\in\mathcal{Y}
of a Turán graph
T(2n,\ell+1)
if

O=D\cdot P_{\sigma}
, where
\sigma\in S_{2n}
is the permutation associated with the partition
Y
.

Here,
D=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell+1}F^{(i)}
is the usual block diagonal matrix of lower-flat matrices
F^{(i)}\in O(\ell)
, and
\sigma
is the permutation which maps the partition
\{1,\ldots,\ell\},\ldots,\{\ell^{2}+1,\ldots,\ell(\ell+1)\}
to the subsets
Y_{1},\ldots Y_{\ell+1}
.

Definition 4
.

The set
S=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2k}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
is
generated
from a partition
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell+1})\in\mathcal{Y}
if for every pair
\{j,j^{\prime}\}\subseteq S
, then
\{j,j^{\prime}\}\nsubseteq Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell+1}
, i.e., no two elements of
S
are in the same partition subset.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{O}
be the joint measurement defined by the partition
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
of a Turán graph
T(2n,\ell+1)
. If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
is generated from
Y
, then
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{O}
jointly measures
\gamma_{S}
with
\eta_{S}=\Omega(n^{-k/2})

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the sparsely arranged perfect matching (described in Fig.
5
of Appendix
B
) of the Turán graph
T(2n,\ell+1)
and suppose
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
are the
k
distinct edges connecting the
2k
distinct partition subsets
Y_{i_{1}},Y_{i_{2}},\ldots,Y_{i_{2k}}
. For any
S=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2k}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, it follows from Prop.
2
that classical post-processing of the joint measurement yields the unsharp observable
\mathsf{M}_{S}(\pm)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm|\det(O_{M,S})|\gamma_{S})
, where
M=\cup_{i=1}^{k}M_{i}
. If
S
is generated from
Y
, the
2k\times 2k
submatrix
O_{M,S}
is monomial (i.e., each row and column has exactly one non-zero element). Therefore, the determinant is the product of
2k
entries from a set of
\ell\times\ell
lower-flat matrices. Since each entry satisfies Eq. (
65
), we have
\eta_{S}=\Omega(n^{-k/2})
.
∎

It follows that we can jointly measure the subset of unsharp degree–
2k
observables
\gamma_{S}
for which the
2k
vertices of
S
belong to
2k
distinct partition subsets. However, we are not guaranteed to jointly measure observables whose
2k
vertices belong to fewer than
2k
distinct subsets. In this case, after classical post-processing, the relevant
2k\times 2k
submatrix of
O
is no longer monomial, and a non-zero determinant is not assured. We therefore implement
N
POVMs
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{(r)}
,
r=1,\ldots,N
, each defined by a random partition from
\mathcal{Y}
. In the following, we show that
N>4k
random partitions generate the full set
\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, with high probability. Thus, we arrive at the conclusion described in Theorem
1
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
2n=\ell(\ell+1)
and
2k\leq\ell+1
. The probability of failing to generate
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
from a partition
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell+1})\in\mathcal{Y}
of
[2n]
, chosen uniformly at random from the set of all partitions
\mathcal{Y}
, is given by

P(S)=1-\ell^{2k}\binom{\ell+1}{2k}\binom{2n}{2k}^{-1}\,.

(78)

Furthermore, for
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the failure probability satisfies
P(S)=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-1})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)
denote the set of all
2k
–element subsets of
[2n]
that are generated from
Y
, i.e.,

\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)=\{\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2k}\}\,|\,\{j_{u},j_{u^{\prime}}\}\nsubseteq Y_{i}\,,\forall\,u,u^{\prime},i\}\,.

The probability of successfully generating the string
\{1,2,\ldots,2k\}
from a partition
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
, chosen uniformly at random, is given by

\text{Prob}(\{1,2,\ldots,2k\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y))=\frac{|\mathcal{P}(1,2,\ldots,2k)|}{|\mathcal{Y}|}\,,

(79)

where

\mathcal{P}(1,2,\ldots,2k)=\{Y\in\mathcal{Y}\,|\,\{1,2,\ldots,2k\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)\}\,,

is the set of all partitions which generate
\{1,2,\ldots,2k\}
.
To calculate this probability we first count the number of elements in the set of pairings

\mathcal{Q}=\{(S,Y)\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)\,,\,Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}\,.

(80)

Using the invariance
|\mathcal{P}(1,2,\ldots,2k)|=|\mathcal{P}(S)|
for all
S\in\binom{[2n]}{2k}
, and
|\mathcal{S}_{2k}|=\binom{2n}{2k}
, we have

|\mathcal{Q}|=|\mathcal{P}(1,2,\ldots,2k)|\,\binom{2n}{2k}\,.

(81)

Alternatively, we can count the number of all partitions
\mathcal{Y}
, together with number of sets
S
generated by each partition, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}(Y)=\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)\}
, such that

|\mathcal{Q}|=|\mathcal{Y}|\cdot|\mathcal{X}(Y)|\,.

(82)

Equating the two expressions of Eqs. (
81
) and (
82
), allows us to rewrite the success probability of Eq. (
79
) as
|\mathcal{X}(Y)|\binom{2n}{2k}^{-1}
. To calculate
|\mathcal{X}(Y)|
note that there are
\ell+1
subsets to choose the
2k
elements from, and for every subset, we can choose any of the
\ell
elements, hence

|\mathcal{X}(Y)|=\binom{\ell+1}{2k}\ell^{2k}\,.

(83)

The probability of failing to generate a
2k
–element subset
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
is, therefore,

P(S):=\text{Prob}\left(S\notin\mathcal{S}_{2k}(Y)\right)=1-|\mathcal{X}(Y)|\binom{2n}{2k}^{-1}=1-\ell^{2k}\binom{\ell+1}{2k}\binom{2n}{2k}^{-1}\,,

(84)

as stated in the proposition.

To determine the asymptotic behavior of the failure probability for
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
, let
L=\ell(\ell+1)
such that

\displaystyle\ell^{2k}\binom{\ell+1}{2k}{\binom{L}{2k}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\ell+1)\ell(\ell-1)\cdots(\ell-2k+2)\ell^{2k}}{L(L-1)(L-2)\cdots(L-2k+1)}\,.

(85)

Thus,

\displaystyle P(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L(L-1)(L-2)\cdots(L-2k+1)-(\ell+1)\ell(\ell-1)\cdots(\ell-2k+2)\ell^{2k}}{L(L-1)(L-2)\cdots(L-2k+1)}

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(L-1)(L-2)\cdots(L-2k+1)-(\ell-1)\cdots(\ell-2k+2)\ell^{2k}}{(L-1)(L-2)\cdots(L-2k+1)}=:\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{D}}\,.

(87)

The numerator
\mathcal{N}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{2k-1}+\mathcal{O}(L^{2k-2})-\ell^{4k-2}+\alpha\ell^{4k-3}+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4k-4})=\alpha\ell^{4k-3}+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4k-4})\,,

(88)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The coefficient
\alpha
can be found from the simple expansion,

(\ell-1)\cdots(\ell-2k+2)=\ell^{2k-2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(2k-1)(2k-2)\ell^{2k-3}+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{2k-4})\,,

(89)

such that
\alpha=(2k-1)(2k-2)/2
. Similarly, for the denominator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=L^{2k-1}+\mathcal{O}(L^{2k})=\ell^{4k-2}+\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4k-4})\,.

(90)

Therefore, for
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
, we have
P(S)=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-1})
, completing the proof of Prop.
10
.
∎

Now suppose we sample
N
independent partitions of
\mathcal{Y}
. The probability of failing to generate
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
from at least one of the
N
partitions is
P(S|N)=P(S)^{N}
. Taking the union bound over all
2k
–element subsets we find that the probability of failing to generate
all
elements of
\mathcal{S}_{2k}
from
N
partitions is,

P_{N}:=P\left(\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}S\,\big|\,N\right)\leq\binom{2n}{2k}P(S)^{N}\,.

(91)

This leads to the following corollary of Prop.
10
.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
2n=\ell(\ell+1)
and
2k\leq\ell+1
, with
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Given
N
partitions sampled uniformly at random from
\mathcal{Y}
, the probability
P_{N}
of failing to generate all elements of
\mathcal{S}_{2k}
satisfies,

P_{N}=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{4k-N})\,.

(92)

For
k=2
, we observe that since
P_{N}=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{8-N})
, taking
N>8
partitions ensures the probability of not generating all subsets becomes sufficiently small for large
n
. Recall that the
r
–th partition defines a POVM
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{(r)}
, and every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
generated from the partition implies
\gamma_{S}
can be jointly measured by
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{(r)}
with
\eta_{S}=\Omega(n^{-k/2})
. Thus, Cor.
1
implies that we can jointly measure, with high probability, the full set of observables via a randomization over
N>4k
POVMs.

Finally, note that up to a minor modification of Eq. (
78
), we can extend Prop.
10
and Cor.
1
to the more general case of
2n=\ell(\ell+1)+t
, where
0\leq t<2(\ell+1)
. Here, we add at most two elements to each of the
\ell+1
subsets
Y_{j}
of
Y
to ensure all
2n
integers are included in the partition. In the extremal case,
t=2\ell
, (
t
must be even) we add an extra two integers to each subset
Y_{i}
such that
|Y_{i}|=(\ell+2)
for all
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. For simplicity, we can also add two “fictitious” elements to the remaining subset, meaning that we generate more terms than necessary. A simple computation reveals the same scaling behavior of
P_{N}
. Thus, each POVM
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{(r)}
is defined by a random partition
Y
of
[2n]
into
\ell+1
subsets, as equally sized as possible. The perfect matching
\mathcal{M}
is partially constructed by first ignoring the “extra”
t
vertices and following the strategy described in Fig.
5
. Edges are then arbitrarily added to complete the perfect matching. We conclude the proof of Theorem
1
by observing that each POVM
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{(r)}
satisfies Lemma
4
, and, with high probability, we can jointly measure the full set of observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}\}
after
N>4k
partitions.

Appendix E
Symmetries of Majorana observables

In this appendix, by studying the symmetries of the degree–
k
Majorana observables described by the braid group, we show that the measurement assemblage
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
is uniformly and rigidly symmetric (see Definitions
5
and
6
). Importantly, these properties result in a simplified expression for the incompatibility robustness, which we discuss in Appendix
F
.

Using terminology from
Nguyen et al. 2020
, it is useful to describe the degree–
k
measurement assemblage as a
bundle
of outcomes, defined by the triple
(\Omega_{k},\pi,\mathcal{S}_{k})
consisting of the set
\mathcal{S}_{k}
of Majorana measurements, the set
\Omega_{k}=\{e_{S}\,|\,e_{S}=\pm 1\,,S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
of all possible outcomes, and a map
\pi:\Omega_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{k}
such that
\pi(e_{S})=S
. Furthermore,
\pi^{-1}(S)
is called the
fibre
over
S
and denotes the set of outcomes of
\mathsf{M}_{S}
, i.e.,
\sum_{e_{S}\in\pi^{-1}(S)}\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})=\mathbbm{1}
, for each
S
. See Fig.
7
for an illustration.

We now introduce a symmetry group
G
of the measurement assemblage acting on the bundle, together with a unitary representation
U:G\rightarrow U(d)
. The symmetry group
G
permutes the outcomes of the bundle in a way that is compatible with the assignment of the outcomes to the measurements
\mathsf{M}_{S}
:

g[\pi(e_{S})]=\pi[g(e_{S})]\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,g\in G\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}\,.

(93)

For outcomes related by a symmetry transformation
g\in G
, their associated POVM effects are related by the unitary
U_{g}
:

\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})=U_{g}\mathsf{M}_{g^{-1}[S]}({g^{-1}[e_{S}]})U^{-1}_{g}\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,g\in G\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}\,.

(94)

To describe the symmetries of the measurement assemblage we consider the braid group introduced below.

E.1
The braid group

We are interested in studying the symmetries of the measurement assemblage described by the braid group
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Consider a braid as a collection of
n
strands with their ends aligned in two rows of points (one above the other). The standard (or Artin) generators of the braid group are
\{b_{i},b_{i}^{-1}\}
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, which act by crossing the
i
–th strand over the
(i+1)
–th strand (see Fig.
8
). The generators
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{-1}
are the inverse of each other and satisfy the relations,

\displaystyle b_{i}b_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{j}b_{i}\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,|i-j|>1\,,

(95)

\displaystyle b_{i}b_{i+1}b_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{i+1}b_{i}b_{i+1}\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,i=1\ldots,n-2\,.

(96)

The first relation shows that two non-neighboring braids commute, while the second is analogous to the third Reidemeister move in knot theory. Together they are sufficient to fully describe the braid group
Artin 1947
. Multiplication in the group corresponds to the sequential application of the braid operations.

Figure 8:
The generators of the braid group. The braid element
b_{j}
acts on the
n
strands by crossing the
i
–th strand over the
(i+1)
–th strand.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{B}_{2n}\rightarrow U(2^{n})
denote the spinor representation of the braid group, with the associated generators

B_{i}:=\varphi(b_{i})=\exp(-\frac{\pi}{2}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i+1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbbm{1}-\gamma_{i}\gamma_{i+1})\,.

(97)

These nearest-neighbor exchange operators generate the non-local operators,

\displaystyle B_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(-\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j})

(98)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{j-1}\ldots B_{i+1}B_{i}B^{\dagger}_{i+1}\ldots B^{\dagger}_{j-1}\,.

(99)

A braiding transformation acts, via conjugation, on the Majorana operators as,

B_{i,j}\gamma_{\ell}B_{i,j}^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{\ell}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,\ell\notin\{i,j\}\,,\\
\gamma_{j}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,\ell=i\,,\\
-\gamma_{i}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,\ell=j\,.\end{cases}

(100)

For a degree–
k
Majorana operator
\gamma_{S}
, the braid transformations satisfy,

B_{i,j}\gamma_{S}B_{i,j}^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{S}&\mbox{if}\,\,\,i,j\notin S\,\,\text{or}\,\,i,j\in S\,,\\
\gamma_{S}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}&\mbox{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(101)

We will also consider the group generated by the
2n
elementary braid operators
B_{i}
and their inverses, defined in Eq. (
97
), together with a single Majorana operator
\gamma_{j}
, with
j\in[2n]
. While the Braiding transformations obey Eq. (
100
)–(
101
), the single Majorana transformations satisfy
\gamma_{j}\gamma_{S}\gamma_{j}^{\dagger}=\pm\gamma_{S}
, with the sign depending on the parity of
|S|
and whether
j\in S
.

E.2
Uniformity and rigidity

Definition 5

(Uniformity)
.

The set of degree–
k
Majorana measurements, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{k}
, is
uniformly symmetric
if any outcome
e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}
can be related to any other outcome
e_{S^{\prime}}\in\Omega_{k}
by a symmetry transformation
g\in G
, i.e., for any pair
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, there exists
g\in G
such that
g(e_{S})=e_{S^{\prime}}
.

Proposition 11

(Braiding recipe)
.

Consider an
n
mode fermionic system.
For any two Majorana operators
\gamma_{S},\gamma_{S^{\prime}}
, where
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, there exists a set of braidings that maps
\gamma_{S}\mapsto\gamma_{S^{\prime}}
.
In particular, the set of braidings
\{B_{I_{1},J_{1}},\ldots,B_{I_{|I|},J_{|I|}}\}
, where
I:=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{|I|}\}=S\backslash S^{\prime}
and
J:=\{J_{1},\ldots,J_{|I|}\}=S^{\prime}\backslash S
,
acts such that

\gamma_{S^{\prime}}=\pm\left(\prod_{i=1}^{|I|}B_{I_{i},J_{i}}\right)\gamma_{S}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{|I|}B_{I_{j},J_{j}}\right)^{\dagger}.

(102)

Proof.

This follows directly from the transformation described in Eq. (
101
).
∎

As an example, consider the two Majorana observables with indices
S=\{1,2,3,4\}
and
S^{\prime}=\{2,4,6,8\}
.
A set of braids that can map
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}\mapsto\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}\gamma_{6}\gamma_{8}
, up to signs, is
\{B_{1,6},B_{3,8}\}
.
Then, applying these transformations to
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}
give us

B_{1,6}B_{3,8}\cdot\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}\cdot B_{3,8}^{\dagger}B_{1,6}^{\dagger}=-\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}\gamma_{6}\gamma_{8}.

(103)

Theorem 3

(Uniformity of Majorana observables)
.

For an n mode fermionic system, the set of degree–
k
Majorana measurements is uniformly symmetric under braid transformations.

Proof.

For
k=1
, any two outcomes
e_{i}
,
e_{j}
, where
i,j\in[2n]
, are related by the braid transformation
B_{i,j}
(cf. Eq. (
100
)). For
i<j
, the braid
B_{i,j}
applied to the measurement
\mathsf{M}_{i}(e_{i})
gives

\mathsf{M}_{j}(e_{j})=\begin{cases}B_{i,j}\mathsf{M}_{i}(e_{i})B_{i,j}^{\dagger},&\text{ if }e_{j}=e_{i}\\
B_{i,j}B_{i,j}B_{i,j}\mathsf{M}_{i}(e_{i})B_{i,j}^{\dagger}B_{i,j}^{\dagger}B_{i,j}^{\dagger},&\text{ if }e_{j}=-e_{i}.\end{cases}

(104)

A similar transformation holds for
i>j
by applying the braiding
B_{j,i}
.

For
k>1
, Prop.
11
gives us a recipe to map any degree–
k
Majorana operators to any other degree–
k
operator, up to signs.
Applying this transformation to the measurement
\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})
, results in

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{|I|}B_{I_{i},J_{i}}\right)\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})\left(\prod_{j=1}^{|I|}B_{I_{j},J_{j}}\right)^{\dagger}=\mathsf{M}_{S^{\prime}}(\pm e_{S}),

(105)

where the sign
\pm
depends on the sequence of braidings.
To obtain the opposite outcome sign, we can apply a braid
B_{i,j}
twice, where
i\in S^{\prime}
and
j\notin S^{\prime}

B_{i,j}B_{i,j}\mathsf{M}_{S^{\prime}}(\pm e_{S})B_{i,j}^{\dagger}B_{i,j}^{\dagger}=\mathsf{M}_{S^{\prime}}(\mp e_{S}).

(106)

Consequently, the set degree–
k
Majorana measurements are uniformly symmetric.
∎

We now show that the degree–
k
measurement assemblages are rigidly symmetric, as defined below. First, we prove rigidity of the Majorana observables via transformations from the braid group, assuming fixed parity of the system. Then, we prove rigidity without restricting the parity, by considering braid and single Majorana transformations. Note that the parity is measured by the operator
Q:=\gamma_{[2n]}=i^{n}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdots\gamma_{2n}
.

Definition 6

(Rigidity)
.

Consider the symmetry group
G
, and a unitary representation,
U:G\to\text{U}(2N)
. Let
G[e_{S}]=\{g\in G:g(e_{S})=e_{S}\}
be the stabilizer group of outcome
e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}
, and
U(G[e_{S}])=\{U_{g}:g\in G[e_{S}]\}
the corresponding set of unitaries. The set of degree–
k
Majorana measurements is
rigidly symmetric

e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}
, the commutant of
U(G[e_{S}])
is given by
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\Pi_{e_{S}})
, where
\Pi_{e_{S}}
is a projection operator.

We note that degree–
k
Majorana observables are uniformly symmetric (cf. Theorem
3
) and therefore all outcomes can be treated equivalently. We therefore consider the stabiliser group
G[S]=\{g\in G:g(S)=S\}
of a single observable
\gamma_{S}

U(G[S])=\{U_{g}\,|\,U_{g}\gamma_{S}U_{g}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{S},\,U_{g}\in U(G)\}
is given by
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\gamma_{S})
.

Theorem 4

(Rigidity with fixed parity)
.

For an
n

k
observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
is rigidly symmetric under braid transformations.

Proof.

Consider the observable
\gamma_{S}
and its stabilizer group
U(G[S])
under braid transformations.
We know that
[\gamma_{S},B_{i,j}]=0
, if the pair
i,j\in S
or if
i,j\notin S
.
Consequently, a subset of the stabilizer group
U(G[S])
is
\mathcal{P}_{S}=\{B_{i,j}\,|\,i,j\in S\text{ or }i,j\notin S\}
.
To prove rigidity it is enough to consider at first only the commutant of
\mathcal{P}_{S}
.
Let
\gamma_{Y}
be a Majorana observable, such that,
[\gamma_{Y},B_{i,j}]=0,\,\forall B_{i,j}\in\mathcal{P}_{S}
.
This restricts
Y
to equal
S
, its complement or
Y=[2n]
, i.e.
Y\in\{S,[2n]\backslash S,[2n]\}
.
Thus, the commutant of
\mathcal{P}_{S}
is given by
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\gamma_{S},Q\gamma_{S},Q)
, where
Q
is the parity operator.
However, since the fermionic system is restricted to the fixed-parity sector, the parity operator
Q
and the operator
\gamma_{S}
are equivalent to
\mathbbm{1}
and
Q\gamma_{S}

Hence, the commutant of
U(G[S])
is
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\gamma_{S})
.
∎

Theorem 5

(Rigidity without fixed parity)
.

For an
n
mode fermionic system, the set of degree–
k
observables
\{\gamma_{S}\,|\,S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
is rigidly symmetric under braid and single Majorana transformations.

Proof.

Consider the observable
\gamma_{S}
and its stabilizer group
U(G[S])
under braid and single Majorana transformations.
In the proof of Theorem
4
we showed that the commutant of
\mathcal{P}_{S}
is
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\gamma_{S},Q\gamma_{S},Q)
, where
\mathcal{P}_{S}\subset U(G[S])
and the stabilizer group contains only braid transformations.
Additionally, let us consider the stabilizer group which includes single Majorana transformations. First, we assume
|S|
is even and let
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\{\gamma_{j}\,|\,j\in[2n]\backslash S\}\subset U(G[S])
.
The observable

does not commute with any unitary in
\mathcal{O}_{S}

Q
.
Therefore, any operator
\gamma_{Y}
that commutes with all unitaries in
\mathcal{P}_{S}
and

must belong to
\text{Span}(\mathbbm{1},\gamma_{S})
.
Hence, the commutant of

is

and the measurement assemblage is rigidly symmetric. If, on the other hand,

∎

Appendix F
Incompatibility robustness and the SYK model

A
section

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\min_{X_{e_{S}}}\quad 1+\sum_{e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}}\textrm{tr}\left[X_{e_{S}}\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{==}\text{s.t.}\quad 1+\sum_{e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}}\textrm{tr}\left[X_{e_{S}}\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})\right]\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}}\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}_{S}(e_{S})\right]\textrm{tr}\left[X_{e_{S}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{==}\qquad\sum_{e_{S}\in\Omega_{k}}\delta_{\mathbf{e}[\pi(e_{S})],e_{S}}X_{e_{S}}\geq 0\quad\forall\mathbf{e}\in\Xi_{k}\,,

We can rewrite Eq. (

After substituting

H_{2}=i\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}e_{ij}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}\,,

Definition 7

Let

(114)

Since

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}e_{ij}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^{2n}o_{ki}b_{k\ell}o_{\ell j}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{2n}b_{k\ell}\widetilde{\gamma}_{k}\widetilde{\gamma}_{\ell}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j-1}\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j}-\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j}\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j-1})

\displaystyle i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j-1}\widetilde{\gamma}_{2j}\,.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{2n}|\theta_{k}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle 2n\sum_{k=1}^{2n}|\theta_{k}|^{2}=2n\sum_{k=1}^{2n}(-\theta_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle 2n\textrm{tr}\left[-S^{2}\right]\,.

Appendix H
Connections to classical shadows

The classical shadow protocol yields a classical representation of an unknown quantum state via a randomized measurement strategy sampling from a unitary ensemble
\mathcal{U}

Huang et al. 2020
. After classical post-processing of the outcomes of a computational basis measurement, one obtains an operator, known as the classical shadow, which is not positive semidefinite but yields the desired state in expectation. This provides a single-shot estimator for multiple properties of the quantum system, including for simultaneous estimation of expectation values for non-commuting observables.

The original formulation of classical shadows, based on sampling from ensembles of random Clifford
n
–qubit unitaries or single-qubit Clifford unitaries, has been adapted to fermionic systems
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wan et al. 2023
;
Low 2022
;
O’Gorman 2022
. In one instance
Zhao et al. 2021
, a fermionic classical shadow is constructed from sampling
U_{O}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{FGU}}
from the ensemble of fermionic Gaussian unitaries which are also
n
–qubit Clifford unitaries (i.e., the braiding group). For an
n
mode fermionic system, a unitary
U_{O}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{FGU}}
and outcome
z\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
of the computational basis measurement, the fermionic classical shadow is given by

\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{2j}^{-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}_{2j}}\langle z|\gamma_{R}|z\rangle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{2j}}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}\right)\,,

(127)

where
\lambda_{2j}=\binom{n}{j}/\binom{2n}{2j}
, and
O\in\text{Alt}(2n)
, with
\text{Alt}(2n)
the alternating group of
2n\times 2n
permutation matrices of determinant one. The shadow provides a single shot estimator
\hat{\gamma}_{S}=\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]
for the expectation value of any even degree Majorana observable. The variance of this estimator, which controls the sample complexity, is bounded by the shadow norm
\left\|\gamma_{S}\right\|_{\text{FGU}}=\sqrt{\binom{2n}{2k}/\binom{n}{k}}
.

In recent work, several connections were made between joint measurements and classical shadows
McNulty et al. 2023
. For instance, a classical shadow can be used to define a joint measurement and to provide a sufficient condition for the compatibility of an arbitrary set of measurements. We now apply the fermionic classical shadow of Eq. (
127
) to derive a joint measurability condition on the set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables.

The randomized measurement procedure of classical shadows can be described by a single POVM
\mathsf{G}(z,U_{O})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\text{FGU}}|}U_{O}^{\dagger}|z\rangle\langle z|U_{O}
, where
U_{O}\in\mathcal{U}_{\text{FGU}}
and
z\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
. For an arbitrary POVM
\mathsf{M}(e)
, let
q(e|\mathsf{M},z,U_{O})=\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}(e)\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]

\mathsf{M}(e)
. To derive a sufficient condition for joint measurability of a set of degree–
2k
Majorana observables, we require that
q(\pm|S,z,U_{O})
is a classical post-processing function for all unsharp measurements
\mathsf{M}_{S}(\pm)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}\pm\eta_{2k}\gamma_{S})
, with
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
. This imposes the condition
\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}_{S}(\pm)\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]\geq 0
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
, which simplifies to
1\pm\eta_{2k}\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]\geq 0
. From Eq. (
127

\displaystyle\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left(\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{2j}^{-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}_{2j}}\langle z|\gamma_{R}|z\rangle\sum_{S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2j}}\det(O_{R,S^{\prime}})\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\gamma_{S^{\prime}}\right]\right)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{2j}^{-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}_{2j}}\langle z|\gamma_{R}|z\rangle\sum_{S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2j}}\det(O_{R,S^{\prime}})\delta_{S,S^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2k}^{-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}}\langle z|\gamma_{R}|z\rangle\det(O_{R,S})\,,

where we have used
\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\right]=0
and
\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\gamma_{S^{\prime}}\right]=2^{n}\delta_{S,S^{\prime}}
. Consider, for example,
k=1

1\pm\eta_{2}\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\hat{\rho}_{z}^{O}\right]\geq 0

1\pm\eta_{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle z|\gamma_{2j-1}\gamma_{2j}|z\rangle\det(O_{\{2j-1,2j\},S})\geq 0\,,

(128)

for all

\exists!S\in\mathcal{S}_{2}

, as described in Eq. (
28
). For simplicity, we also assume all Majorana observables can be jointly measured by a single implementation of the parent POVM
\mathsf{G}^{O}
, i.e., without a randomization over multiple fermionic Gaussian unitaries. In expectation, the estimator satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\hat{H}]=\sum_{S}\eta_{S}^{-1}\alpha_{S}\mathbb{E}[e_{S}]=\sum_{S}\alpha_{S}\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S}\rho\right]=\textrm{tr}\left[H\rho\right]\,,

where the expectation value
\mathbb{E}
is over the outcome statistics of the POVM
\mathsf{G}^{O}
on the state
\rho
. To calculate the variance,

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}[\hat{H}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{S,S^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{S}\alpha_{S^{\prime}}}{\eta_{S}\eta_{S^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}[e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}]-(\textrm{tr}\left[H\rho\right])^{2}\,,

(131)

we evaluate
\mathbb{E}[e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}]
for all pairs of observables
\gamma_{S}
and
\gamma_{S^{\prime}}
with
|S|=2k
and
|S^{\prime}|=2k^{\prime}
. Thus, we require the two-observable marginal of the parent POVM
\mathsf{G}^{O}
of Eq. (
55
), given by

\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}})=\sum_{{\bf q},\,{\bf x}}D(e_{S}|S,R,{\bf q},{\bf x})D(e_{S^{\prime}}|S^{\prime},R^{\prime},{\bf q},{\bf x})\mathsf{G}^{O}({\bf q},{\bf x})\,.

(132)

Here, and for the remainder of the appendix, we will simplify the notation
R(S)
and
R(S^{\prime})
to
R
and
R^{\prime}
, respectively, when no ambiguity arises.

I.1
Single Majoranas

Before moving to the general setting, we consider observables written as linear combinations of single Majorana operators, such that
H=\sum_{\mu=1}^{2n}\alpha_{j}\gamma_{j}
, where
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. The estimator takes the form

\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\eta^{-1}\alpha_{j}e_{j}=\sqrt{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\,,

(133)

where
\eta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}
is the optimal (uniform) noise achieved by the parent POVM of Eq. (
12
). The noisy joint measurement of
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
, for
i,j\in[2n]
, is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{i,j}(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf x}}D(e_{i}|{\bf x})D(e_{j}|{\bf x})\mathsf{G}({\bf x})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf x}\\
x_{i}=e_{i}\\
x_{j}=e_{j}\end{subarray}}\mathsf{G}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}(e_{i}\gamma_{i}+e_{j}\gamma_{j})\right)\,.

It follows that for
i\neq j
,
\mathbb{E}[e_{i}e_{j}]=\sum_{e_{i},e_{j}}\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}_{i,j}(e_{i},e_{j})\rho\right]e_{i}e_{j}=0
. Since
\mathbb{E}[e_{i}^{2}]=1
, it follows from Eq. (
131
) that,

\textrm{Var}[\hat{H}]=2n\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\alpha_{j}^{2}-(\textrm{tr}\left[H\rho\right])^{2}\leq 2n\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\alpha_{j}^{2}\,.

(134)

I.2
Arbitrary even degree Majoranas

We now construct the two-observable marginal
\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}})
, with outcomes
e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}}\in\{\pm 1\}
, for
S\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}
and
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2k^{\prime}}
. Let
\tau=\text{sgn}(\det(O_{R,S}))
and
\tau^{\prime}=\text{sgn}(\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}}))
and denote
S\triangle S^{\prime}=(S\cup S^{\prime})\setminus(S\cap S^{\prime})
as the symmetric difference. For
R=\cup_{i=1}^{k}R_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}
, with
R_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
, let
q_{R}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}q_{R_{i}}
such that
q_{R}q_{R^{\prime}}=q_{R\triangle R^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, let
x_{S}=\prod_{j\in S}x_{j}
with
x_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
x_{S}x_{S^{\prime}}=x_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}
. From Eq. (
132
) we have,

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf q},\,{\bf x}}D(e_{S}|S,R,{\bf q},{\bf x})D(e_{S^{\prime}}|S^{\prime},R^{\prime},{\bf q},{\bf x})\mathsf{G}^{O}({\bf q},{\bf x})

(135)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{3n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf q},\,{\bf x}\\
e_{S}=\tau x_{S}q_{R}\\
e_{S^{\prime}}=\tau^{\prime}x_{S^{\prime}}q_{R^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{D}_{2k}}q_{Y}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{S}_{2k}}x_{T}\det(O_{Y,T})\gamma_{T}\right)\,.

(136)

Notice that terms which do not contain either
x_{S}q_{R}
,
x_{S^{\prime}}q_{R^{\prime}}
or

q_{R\triangle R^{\prime}}x_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}
are unconstrained (taking values
\pm 1
) and therefore cancel in the summation over
{\bf x}
and
{\bf q}
. Hence, we are left with

\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S})=\frac{1}{2^{3n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf q},\,{\bf x}\\
\tau x_{S}q_{R}=e_{S}\\
\tau^{\prime}x_{S^{\prime}}q_{R^{\prime}}=e_{S^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}(\mathbbm{1}+q_{R}x_{S}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}+q_{R^{\prime}}x_{S^{\prime}}\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S^{\prime}}\\
+q_{R\triangle R^{\prime}}x_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|})\,.

Summing over
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2n}
and
{\bf q}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
, with the constraints
\tau x_{S}q_{R}=e_{S}
and
\tau^{\prime}x_{S^{\prime}}q_{R^{\prime}}=e_{S^{\prime}}
, yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}+q_{R}x_{S}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}+q_{R^{\prime}}x_{S^{\prime}}\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S^{\prime}}+q_{R\triangle R^{\prime}}x_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{e_{S}}{\tau}\det(O_{R,S})\gamma_{S}+\frac{e_{S^{\prime}}}{\tau^{\prime}}\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S^{\prime}}+\frac{e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}}{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}+e_{S}|\det(O_{R,S})|\gamma_{S}+e_{S^{\prime}}|\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}})|\gamma_{S^{\prime}}+\frac{e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}}{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|}\right)\,.

It follows that,

\mathbb{E}[e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}]=\sum_{e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}}}\textrm{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}_{S,S^{\prime}}(e_{S},e_{S^{\prime}})\rho\right]e_{S}e_{S^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\rho\right]\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|}\,.

(138)

Finally, the variance of the estimator is given by

\textrm{Var}[\hat{H}]=\sum_{S}\alpha^{2}_{S}\eta_{S}^{-2}+\sum_{S\neq S^{\prime}}\delta_{|R\triangle R^{\prime}|,|S\triangle S^{\prime}|}\alpha_{S}\alpha_{S^{\prime}}\frac{\det(O_{R\triangle R^{\prime},S\triangle S^{\prime}})}{\det(O_{R,S})\det(O_{R^{\prime},S^{\prime}})}\textrm{tr}\left[\gamma_{S\triangle S^{\prime}}\rho\right]-(\textrm{tr}\left[H\rho\right])^{2}\,,

(139)

in agreement with Prop.
4
in Sec.
VI.2
.
